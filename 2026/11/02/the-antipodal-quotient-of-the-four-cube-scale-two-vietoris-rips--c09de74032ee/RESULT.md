# The antipodal quotient of the four-cube scale-two Vietoris–Rips complex

## Finding
Let \(Q_4=\{0,1\}^4\) have Hamming distance and let \(K=\operatorname{VR}_{\le 2}(Q_4)\) denote the clique complex formed by all finite vertex sets of diameter at most two. Let \(\tau(v)=v\oplus(1,1,1,1)\) be simultaneous complementation of the four binary coordinates. The geometric realization of \(K\) has a free simplicial action by \(\tau\). Its orbit space has the **integral homotopy classification**
\[
\boxed{\;|K|/\langle\tau\rangle\simeq\mathbb{RP}^{3}\vee\bigvee^{4}S^{3}.\;}
\]
In particular its fundamental group is \(\mathbb Z/2\), its first integral homology is \(\mathbb Z/2\), and its third integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^5\); every other positive-degree integral homology group vanishes. The mod-two cohomology contains the projective-space generator \(a\in H^1(-;\mathbf F_2)\) with \(a^3\ne0\).

## Assumptions and scope
All simplices use the **inclusive Hamming threshold two**. A binary vertex is encoded as the integer between 0 and 15 having its four coordinates as binary digits. The involution is integer bitwise complementation with mask 15. Homotopy equivalences are of ordinary geometric realizations, not of an abstract quotient simplicial complex obtained by identifying vertices without recording cell incidences. The result concerns this one four-dimensional cube and the subgroup of order two generated by simultaneous bit complementation; it does not claim a classification for other cube dimensions or scales.

The nonequivariant input, proved by Adamaszek and Adams [1], is
\[
K\simeq\bigvee^9 S^3.
\]
Hence \(K\) is simply connected and has reduced integral homology only in degree three, of rank nine. The quotient classification below is not part of that cited input.

## Proof
**1. Free antipodal action.** Two antipodal cube vertices have Hamming distance four and therefore cannot belong to one simplex of \(K\). Thus no nonempty simplex meets its image under \(\tau\), and every geometric point has a two-element orbit. Consequently \(K\to X:=|K|/\langle\tau\rangle\) is a regular, free double covering. Since \(K\) is simply connected by [1], \(\pi_1(X)\cong C_2\), and \(K\) is its universal cover.

**2. Nine explicit embedded three-spheres.** For each coordinate \(i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) and bit \(b\in\{0,1\}\), let \(F_{i,b}\) be the full subcomplex on the eight vertices with coordinate \(i\) equal to \(b\). Distinct vertices within that set have Hamming distance one, two, or three; only the four pairs at distance three are nonedges. Its clique complex is the join of four zero-spheres, which is the boundary of a four-dimensional cross-polytope and hence is \(S^3\). The involution exchanges \(F_{i,0}\) and \(F_{i,1}\).

Let \(P\) be the full subcomplex on the eight even-parity binary vertices. Two distinct such vertices have Hamming distance two or four, and the four nonedges are precisely the pairs \(\{v,v\oplus15\}\). Thus \(P\) is also the boundary of a four-dimensional cross-polytope. The involution restricts on \(|P|\cong S^3\) to the standard antipodal map on its four opposite vertex pairs, so \(P/\tau\cong\mathbb{RP}^3\).

**3. An integral nine-cycle basis.** Give every simplex the orientation of its increasing binary-encoded vertex sequence. The complex has \(16\) vertices, \(80\) edges, \(160\) triangles, \(120\) tetrahedra, and \(16\) four-simplices. Each of the nine cross-polytope boundaries in step 2 has an explicit oriented fundamental three-cycle: choose one vertex from each of four opposite pairs, with the product-sign orientation corrected by the parity of the permutation needed to sort its four vertices. Call these cycles \(z_{i,b}\) and \(z_P\).

Since the known homotopy type has vanishing fourth homology and there are no simplices above dimension four, the fourth boundary map has rank \(16\). Since third homology has rank nine, the lattice \(\ker\partial_3\subset C_3(K;\mathbb Z)\) has rank \(25\). Form the \(120\times25\) integer matrix whose first 16 columns are the oriented boundaries of all four-simplices in lexicographic order and whose last nine columns are the eight \(z_{i,b}\) (ordered first by \(i\), then by \(b\)), followed by \(z_P\).

On the lexicographically numbered tetrahedron rows (zero-indexed)
\[
0,1,3,5,9,11,15,19,26,30,32,36,40,47,51,55,62,66,73,77,84,91,96,100,104
\]
this matrix has an exact integer determinant \(-1\). The included standalone `VERIFY.py` reconstructs every simplex, orientation, cycle, and matrix entry and evaluates this determinant by fraction-free integer elimination. A unimodular maximal minor makes the 25 columns a primitive rank-25 sublattice of \(C_3(K;\mathbb Z)\). The saturated lattice \(\ker\partial_3\) has the same rank and contains them. Therefore the 25 columns form an integral basis of \(\ker\partial_3\), and the nine cross-polytope cycles descend to a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of \(H_3(K;\mathbb Z)\).

**4. Reconstruct the entire quotient homotopy type.** Inclusion \(P/\tau\to X\) gives a based map from \(\mathbb{RP}^3\); the four inclusions \(F_{i,0}\hookrightarrow K\to X\), with basepoint paths attached, give maps of four three-spheres. Their wedge is a continuous map
\[
f:\mathbb{RP}^{3}\vee\bigvee^4S^3\longrightarrow X.
\]
It induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups because the first summand \(P/\tau\) has universal cover \(P\) with deck involution \(\tau\), so the induced generator is the nontrivial deck transformation of the covering \(K\to X\).

The universal cover of the wedge source is homotopy equivalent to nine copies of \(S^3\): one copy is the universal cover \(S^3\) of \(\mathbb{RP}^3\), and eight more arise from the two lifts of each of the four additional three-spheres. The induced lift of \(f\) to the universal covers sends the resulting nine homology generators to \(z_P\) and the eight \(z_{i,b}\), respectively, up to orientation signs. Step 3 proves that this map induces an integral homology isomorphism. Both covering spaces are simply connected CW complexes, so the homology Whitehead theorem makes the lifted map a homotopy equivalence. The original \(f\) is then a homotopy equivalence, proving the boxed statement.

The checker (i) generates all \(392\) nonempty faces, (ii) verifies the nine oriented cycles are closed, (iii) checks the exact 25-by-25 determinant \(-1\), (iv) verifies the involution has no invariant simplex, and (v) independently computes the mod-two boundary ranks \((7,32,47,8)\) on the quotient's cell-orbit complex. It prints `Q4_ANTIPODAL_RP3_WEDGE_FOUR_S3_UNIMODULAR_BASIS_OK` on success. The finite calculations are completely exhaustive for \(Q_4\); they are not offered as proofs for larger cubes. The homotopy argument relies explicitly on the published nonequivariant theorem [1] and the standard covering-space and Whitehead theorems.

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek and Adams [1] prove the nonequivariant nine-sphere homotopy type used in steps 1 and 3. Adams and Virk [2] construct cross-polytopal homology classes and establish general independence and propagation statements; the eight coordinate-facet spheres themselves are not asserted here as a previously unknown construction. Bendersky, Elia, and Grbić [4] also study cross-polytopal cohomology representatives. Galetto, Montaño, and Wellner [3] go further and determine the full rational hyperoctahedral homology representations for these small scales. Accordingly, **neither the nonequivariant wedge nor the rational symmetry character is being claimed as new**. The present result identifies the integral geometric **antipodal orbit-space homotopy type**, its projective-space summand, torsion fundamental group and cup-product consequence; these are not entailed merely by the previously calculated rational representation. The searched primary full texts did not state the quotient classification.

## Limitations
The proof addresses a single finite metric space and the central antipodal quotient, not arbitrary group quotients or a dimension-uniform formula. The exact integral cycle-basis certificate is an exhaustive finite calculation, not an independent formal verification of the published theorem [1]. No external independent audit has been performed. A bibliographic search cannot rule out equivalent uncatalogued or inaccessible earlier work.
