# Scientific review: the cactus boundary for adaptive shattering

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem explicitly quantifies over every finite simple graph. Its VC equality has the standard forest-extension proof. For cacti, the orientation concept class factors exactly into free bridge-edge bits and cycle factors missing only the two coherently directed cycles; the standard Littlestone product recurrence then proves equality. In the noncactus case, an explicit, machine-checked depth-four tree for the diamond establishes a one-level excess; the proved subdivision-lift lemma preserves this excess, and a rooted spanning-forest extension transports it from any theta subgraph to the whole graph. The proof treats disconnection, isolated vertices, and boundary cases. The verifier checks the critical 16-leaf gadget, six subdivisions, and all 1,088 labeled graphs on four or five vertices. Finite evidence corroborates rather than replaces the unrestricted arguments.

## Originality — PASS, subject to independent historical-priority review
Kozma–Moran, arXiv:1211.1319, already proves the VC/graphic-rank identity for acyclic orientations; this is acknowledged as prior background, not presented as new. Chepoi–Knauer–Philibert, arXiv:2110.15168, treats acyclic orientations as examples of COM concepts and proves proper labeled sample compression of size VC dimension, a logically different property. The checked material does not determine their Littlestone dimension, and in fact the diamond example shows that proper VC-sized compression does not force VC–Littlestone equality. Searches for adaptive orientation shattering, thicket dimension, cactus graphs, theta subdivisions, and graphic arrangements yielded no equivalent all-graph criterion in the accessible corpus. A previously reported finite total-order comparison result determines Littlestone dimension for transitive tournaments on at most seven vertices, not the cactus/noncactus dichotomy for arbitrary graphs. Residual risk: the general theorem might have appeared in older work on oriented-matroid topes, hyperplane-arrangement online prediction, or learning graph orientations using a different terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result resolves a natural structural classification for an entire class of edge-direction concept systems: cactus graphs are exactly those where adaptive querying confers no dimension advantage. The positive side is an exact equality for all cacti; the negative side yields a sharp theta obstruction and a quantitative lower bound for every noncactus. A finite, five-edge four-vertex gadget becomes a general obstruction under arbitrary subdivisions and supergraphs, and disjoint unions produce an unbounded adaptive-minus-static gap. The result is therefore not merely an individual finite table or a rephrasing of the previously known static shattering formula.

## Related literature and limitations
The focal work is Chepoi–Knauer–Philibert, *Labeled sample compression schemes for complexes of oriented matroids*, first publicly posted 2021-10-28 (arXiv:2110.15168). Background is Kozma–Moran, *Shattering, Graph Orientations, and Connectivity*, first publicly posted 2012-11-06 (arXiv:1211.1319). The general query-learning context is Chase–Freitag–Reyzin, arXiv:2310.04812. The new theorem gives a strict lower bound rather than an exact Littlestone formula for arbitrary noncacti. It assumes simple undirected input graphs and binary edge-direction queries. The published literature comparison is evidence of noncoverage in inspected sources, not proof of unique historical priority.
