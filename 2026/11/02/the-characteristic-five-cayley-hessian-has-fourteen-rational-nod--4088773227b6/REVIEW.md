# Scientific review: characteristic-five Cayley Hessian

## Correctness — PASS
The integer Hessian determinant identity fixes normalization. All chartwise Jacobian Gröbner bases reduce every variable's fifth-power Frobenius equation to zero; hence the geometric singularities are among the explicit rational candidates. Exactly fourteen satisfy the equations, and a nonsingular local ternary quadratic at each makes them ordinary nodes. The normal quartic has a K3 resolution. The two exact finite-field counts and the split fourteen exceptional conics yield the resolution counts. The second count saturates the K3 Weil bound and therefore forces all degree-two Frobenius eigenvalues to equal \(\pm5\). The first determines the sign multiplicities. The subsequent trace and zeta formulas are deductions, not experimental extrapolations.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Dardanelli–van Geemen establish the Cayley cubic Hessian's fourteen-node complex geometry and rank-two transcendental lattice, but the inspected characteristic-zero §3 does not state this characteristic-five model's rational singularities or Frobenius zeta. Shimada (2012) analyzes degree-two projective models of a characteristic-five supersingular K3, not the four-degree Cayley Hessian polarization or its \(\mathbb F_5\)-model. A distinct characteristic-five Sylvester–Clebsch Hessian is known to have eleven nodes and a different Frobenius sign pattern, so it does not imply this claim. Searches of the indexed mathematical results and accessible same-subject earlier records found no exact or stronger treatment. Broad singular-K3 modularity theorems can explain inert supersingular phenomena and potentially infer some traces; their implications for this explicit reduction require additional identification and descent data. Unindexed calculations or a stronger CM corollary remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is the precise arithmetic behavior of the classical rank-20 Cayley Hessian K3 at the smallest good inert prime in its quadratic CM field: its fourteen nodes remain geometrically ordinary and rational, and its minimal resolution is maximal over the quadratic field. The natural complete invariant is the zeta function, including Frobenius signs over the base field; it is not inferred merely from the characteristic-zero singularity count. Exact point counts also distinguish it from the related Clebsch Hessian characteristic-five model.

## Limitations
The Artin invariant, explicit minimal resolution equations, and automorphism group are not determined. Novelty is not independently certified, and modularity implications remain a specific historical-priority risk.
