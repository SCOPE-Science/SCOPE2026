# Scientific review

## Correctness (C) — PASS

The theorem has a complete algebraic proof independent of enumeration. Expanding the symmetric cubic around the unique characteristic-five point yields \(sQ+D\) with nondegenerate \(Q\) and \(D\) a product of three lines. An explicit six-element rational basepoint list exhausts the intersection \(Q=D=0\) by Bézout, giving the resolved anticanonical map \(\operatorname{Bl}_B\mathbb P^2\to X\). For the group, every automorphism acts on the complete anticanonical embedding, fixes the unique node, and injects into the stabilizer of \(B=Q(\mathbb F_5)\); the latter has order \(120\), matched by coordinate permutations. Blowing up six rational points and contracting a rational conic proves all extension-field point counts. The standalone verifier checks the actual coordinates, action and point counts. Risk: none found in the stated geometric scope; the local group-scheme infinitesimal structure is not asserted.

## Originality (O) — PASS, with residual priority risk

Keneshlou's arXiv:1909.05554 is about Eckardt singular-locus strata and smooth cubic surfaces; the inspected Sections 1–3 give the usual \(\mathfrak S_5\) action but not the full automorphism group of the singular characteristic-five fibre or its zeta function. Dolgachev–Duncan, Remark 6.2 and Section 12, and Vikulova, Proposition 1, explicitly mention the characteristic-five singularity but concern smooth automorphism classifications. Previous descriptions of the six lines through the node, their exceptional six-point action, and the integral line-Hilbert scheme are treated as established, not as the novelty. The new exact group **upper bound**, the six-rational-point blowup model, and the all-extension zeta function are not obtained as mere identical statements in those antecedents. Related work on classical icosahedral blowups suggests a real residual risk that an equivalent formulation exists in older literature.

## Mathematical value (V) — PASS

The global automorphism group of a singular special fibre is not determined by its smooth generic fibre's symmetry group, because extra automorphisms may occur on reduction. Establishing that **no** such extra automorphisms occur, and identifying the special fibre with the contraction of all six finite-field points of a conic, provides a natural arithmetic/geometric classification. The latter determines the complete zeta function, not merely one experimentally observed point count. Both are intrinsic, reproducible invariants of a standard highly symmetric cubic degeneration. This is neither a free renaming of the old line enumeration nor a small unmotivated numerical slice.
