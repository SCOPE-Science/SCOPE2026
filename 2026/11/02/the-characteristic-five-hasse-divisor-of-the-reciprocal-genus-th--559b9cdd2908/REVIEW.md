# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
An independent reconstruction of the standard hyperelliptic Hasse–Witt coefficient formula gives the symmetric \(3\times3\) matrix shown in the result. The coefficient computation is checked exactly by `verify.py`, and the constant eigenbasis ensures the stable Frobenius rank equals the number of nonzero Hasse factors rather than merely the instantaneous matrix rank. An exact discriminant factorization proves the smoothness boundary. Both elimination identities were recomputed in \(\mathbb F_5[u]\), including the roots at the singular boundary which must be removed before counting intersections. The three remaining points have nonzero gradient determinants, so the three components form a simple-normal-crossings divisor. Direct elliptic quotient substitutions and elliptic Hasse coefficients provide a second structural check. The finite \(\mathbb F_5\) parameter census is corroborative, not the proof over the algebraic closure.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risks
The exact same octavic parameters, smoothness factors and elliptic quotients appear in Ohashi (2021), Section 3. These known ingredients are expressly excluded from the new contribution. Glass–Pries (2004) guarantee broad existence of prescribed \(p\)-torsion and discuss commuting involutions, but do not assert the characteristic-five three-component Hasse arrangement, the rank-one points or rank-zero exclusion in this marked locus. Gutierrez–Sevilla–Shaska (2012) and Shaska–Shor (2013) primarily describe automorphisms, moduli and Weierstrass points; Elkin–Pries analyze general characteristic-five genus-three strata, not this specific two-parameter intersection scheme. Targeted published-record and literature searches found no equivalent or stronger claim. This is best-knowledge originality, not proof that no unindexed calculation exists. A directly relevant later article by Ohashi was read beyond abstract-level snippets and compared against the precise statement. A characteristic-seven calculation of the same marked family has different Hasse factors and does not imply these characteristic-five intersection loci.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The reciprocal octavic is a natural two-dimensional sublocus with three elliptic quotients, not an arbitrary hand-picked family. A complete Hasse stratification gives a geometric answer about the intersection of three elliptic supersingularity conditions: the nonordinary divisor has three reduced smooth components, and exactly three transverse double points, with *no* simultaneous triple supersingularity in the smooth family. The absence of rank-zero points is a structural contrast with characteristic-seven behavior on the same naturally defined locus, not a numerical increment of a previously known table. The explicit simple-normal-crossings geometry can be used to study local moduli and degeneration questions. No general theorem outside the marked locus is claimed.
