# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact polynomial identity \(F=2Q^3+(XYZ)^2\) in characteristic five gives a complete Jacobian-equation solution. There are exactly six transverse axis–conic cusp supports and four sign-pattern nodes; each cusp has completed \(A_2\) equation and each node has nonsquare tangent discriminant \(3\). A Bezout-and-cubic-genus argument excludes geometric reducibility, and the delta-genus equality proves a rational normalization. Rational branch degrees determine point counts, and the local-unit sequence proves the generalized Picard-group decomposition. The standalone checker confirms the polynomial identity, all four discriminants, cusp supports and finite-field counts. No finite enumeration is used to infer a theorem for infinitely many extensions.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty
Inoue–Kato's full preprint and its hypotheses exclude characteristic five, while Giulietti–Kawakita–Lia–Montanucci explicitly state the **known** rationality of the characteristic-five normalization. Rationality alone cannot determine whether its arithmetic-genus defect consists of cusps, split or nonsplit nodes, or more complicated singularities. Neither inspected text states the proposed exact \(6A_2+4A_1\) basket, quadratic Frobenius branches, prime-field generalized Picard group, or full singular-model zeta formula. Indexed semantic and standard literature searches using the equation, characteristic, singularity counts and point-count formulas did not return an equivalent statement. This negative-search evidence is not a proof of priority; an unindexed computation or later specialization is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is the complete singularity and arithmetic classification of a canonical exceptional-characteristic specialization of a highly symmetric genus-six curve. The difference between the rational normalization and the nonsplit torus–unipotent generalized Jacobian is genuinely structural, giving all extension-field counts and a striking absence of smooth \(\mathbb F_5\)-points. It refines a previously known rationality statement rather than repackaging it.
