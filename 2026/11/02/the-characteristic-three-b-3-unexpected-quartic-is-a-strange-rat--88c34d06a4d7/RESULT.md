# The characteristic-three \(B_3\) unexpected quartic is a strange rational curve with a \((3,4)\)-cusp

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\). For \(R=[a:b:c]\in\mathbf P^2(k)\), consider the reduction of the primitive integral unexpected-quartic polynomial of Bauer--Malara--Szemberg--Szpond:
\[
Q_R(x,y,z)=a^3(y^3z-yz^3)+b^3(xz^3-x^3z)+c^3(x^3y-xy^3).
\]
Its **saturated projective Jacobian scheme**, for *every* \(R\), is the length-nine complete intersection
\[
\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathrm{Jac}}(Q_R)=V\big((bz-cy)^3,(cx-az)^3,(ay-bx)^3\big)=V(L_1^3,L_2^3),
\]
where \(L_1,L_2\) is any pair of independent linear forms generating the homogeneous ideal of the point \(R\). In particular, this is **not** the ordinary third fat point defined by \(I_R^3\). Its completed local algebra is \(k[[u,v]]/(u^3,v^3)\), with length \(9\) and Hilbert function \((1,2,3,2,1)\).

On the explicit nonempty open set in the parameter chart \(c=1\) defined by
\[
A=a^3-a,\qquad B=b-b^3,\qquad \Delta=A B(A^2-B^2)\ne0,
\]
\(Q_R\) is geometrically integral and rational and has precisely one singular point, \(R\). Its unique branch at \(R\) has valuation semigroup generated by \(3\) and \(4\) (equivalently one Newton edge of intercepts \((3,4)\)), hence delta invariant \(3\). The singular point has Tjurina number \(9\).

The **strange point** is not the singular point in this open family: it is \(S=[a^3:b^3:1]\), a distinct smooth point of \(Q_R\). Every tangent line at a smooth point goes through \(S\); the Gauss map of the normalization is a purely inseparable map of degree \(3\) onto the pencil of lines through \(S\). For every smooth point \(T\ne S\), its tangent line cuts the quartic as the effective divisor \(S+3T\). Thus all such smooth points are flexes of contact order three.

Finally, this quartic is the **unique**, up to nonzero scalar, member of the six-dimensional vector space of quartics through the standard nine \(B_3\) points that has multiplicity at least three at \(R\) for every \(R\) in the displayed open set. This uniqueness and the nine-point configuration are meant for the fixed integral coordinate presentation; no characteristic-independent projective classification is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field \(k\) is algebraically closed of characteristic exactly \(3\). The nine fixed points are
\[
[1:0:0],\ [0:1:0],\ [0:0:1],\ [1:\pm1:0],\ [1:0:\pm1],\ [0:1:\pm1].
\]
The parameter open \(\Delta\ne0\) is defined only in the chart \(c=1\); the Jacobian scheme assertion is universal in \(R\). The words *strange curve* mean that every smooth-point tangent belongs to a fixed line pencil. All multiplicities and scheme lengths are intrinsic to the specified plane equation over \(k\). The original integral polynomial also has the terms
\[
3a(b^2-c^2)x^2yz+3b(c^2-a^2)xy^2z+3c(a^2-b^2)xyz^2,
\]
which vanish after specialization to characteristic \(3\).

## Proof
**1. The exact Jacobian complete intersection.** Differentiating in characteristic three gives the identities
\[
(Q_x,Q_y,Q_z)=\big((bz-cy)^3,(cx-az)^3,(ay-bx)^3\big).
\]
The three linear forms have span the two-dimensional ideal of the point \(R\). Their cubes generate the cubes of any two independent linear equations of \(R\), since \((u+v)^3=u^3+v^3\). This homogeneous complete-intersection ideal is saturated and has degree \(3\cdot3=9\). Consequently the only singular support is \(R\), and affine coordinates centered there identify the local scheme with \(k[[u,v]]/(u^3,v^3)\). Euler's identity \(4Q=xQ_x+yQ_y+zQ_z\) remains valid (\(4=1\) in characteristic three), so this also computes the local Tjurina algebra wherever \(Q\) is nonzero.

**2. Local form and the cusp.** On \(c=z=1\), translate \(x=a+u\), \(y=b+v\). Direct polynomial expansion, with no omitted terms, yields
\[
Q_R(a+u,b+v,1)=Bu^3+Av^3+u^3v-uv^3.
\]
When \(\Delta\ne0\), let \(s\in k\) be the unique cube root of \(-B/A\), put \(w=v-su\), and rewrite this as
\[
A w^3+(s-s^3)u^4+u^3w-u w^3.
\]
The coefficients \(A\) and \(s-s^3\) are nonzero: \(s=s^3\) would force \(s\in\mathbb F_3\), and then \(B/A\in\{0,1,-1\}\), contrary to \(\Delta\ne0\). The lower Newton edge therefore joins exponents \((0,3)\) and \((4,0)\), with no other term on or below it. Since \(\gcd(3,4)=1\), the Newton-valuation criterion proves analytic irreducibility of this germ and its single branch has semigroup generators \(3,4\). Alternatively, the delta invariant follows from Step 3 and the genus formula, even without using a characteristic-zero Puiseux algorithm.

**3. Irreducibility, normalization, and delta.** A line through \(R\) meets the quartic with multiplicity at least three there; the residual intersection is one point for a general line. Local irreducibility at \(R\), together with the absence of any other geometric singular point by Step 1, makes the entire projective quartic irreducible: distinct global components would meet somewhere by Bézout and give an analytically reducible germ at a singular point, necessarily \(R\), which is impossible. A repeated component would give a positive-dimensional Jacobian singular support, also impossible. Thus projection from \(R\) gives a birational map to the pencil \(\mathbf P^1\). The normalization is \(\mathbf P^1\); the arithmetic genus of a plane quartic is three and \(R\) is its unique singularity, so \(\delta_R=3\). Its Tjurina number \(9\) was determined in Step 1.

**4. The strange point and inseparable Gauss map.** Put \(S=[a^3:b^3:c^3]\). Direct substitution shows \(Q_R(S)=0\). The differential identity from Step 1 gives
\[
a^3Q_x+b^3Q_y+c^3Q_z=0
\]
identically. Hence every smooth-point tangent contains \(S\). The Gauss map is the composition of the projection from \(R\), given by the three linear forms \((bz-cy,cx-az,ay-bx)\), with Frobenius cubing of their homogeneous coordinates. Since projection from \(R\) is birational on the normalization, the induced Gauss map has purely inseparable degree three. Under \(\Delta\ne0\) the point \(S\) is distinct from \(R\) (otherwise \(a^3=a,b^3=b\)), hence is smooth by Step 1. Projection from this smooth point \(S\) has degree three on the normalization. Its differential vanishes because every tangent passes through \(S\); hence it is purely inseparable of degree three. The fiber line through \(S\) and any smooth \(T\ne S\) has intersection divisor \(S+3T\), as stated.

**5. Uniqueness among the unexpected quartics.** Direct substitution at the nine points gives a basis for their degree-four vanishing space:
\[
\langle x^3y-xy^3,\ x^3z-xz^3,\ y^3z-yz^3,\ x^2yz,\ xy^2z,\ xyz^2\rangle_k.
\]
The evaluation matrix of all fifteen degree-four monomials at the nine points has rank nine over \(\mathbb F_3\), so the displayed six independent forms constitute the full space. In the affine chart \(z=1\), imposing the six coefficients of total degrees below three in the expansion at \((a,b)\) gives a six-by-six jet matrix. Its determinant is identically zero and one five-by-five minor equals \(-ab\). Since \(\Delta\ne0\) implies \(ab\ne0\), the kernel has dimension exactly one. The displayed \(Q_R\) lies in the kernel, so spans it.

**6. Nonemptiness of the open.** In \(\mathbb F_{27}=\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^3-t-1)\), choose \(a=t\), \(b=t^2\), \(c=1\). Exact reduction gives \(A=1\), \(B=t+2\), and \(\Delta=2\ne0\). Thus all assertions on the open describe a genuine nonempty family.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify_b3.py` checks, in exact arithmetic modulo three, the reduction from the source's integral quartic; all three Frobenius-cubed derivatives; the point and tangent identities for \(S\); the full local equation; the rank-nine basepoint evaluation matrix; the vanishing jet determinant and the nonzero five-by-five minor; and the nonempty \(\mathbb F_{27}\) witness. It prints `B3_CHAR3_FROBENIUS_GRADIENT_LENGTH9_UNIQUE_JET_AND_WILD_CUSP_OPEN_PASS`. The Newton-valuation, Bézout/genus, and purely inseparable morphism arguments are the symbolic proof steps; finite-field experiments alone do not establish these general assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Bauer--Malara--Szemberg--Szpond exhibit the integral formula and the classical nine-point \(B_3\) configuration, and Farnik--Galuppi--Sodomaco--Trok prove the complex uniqueness theorem. Neither inspected paper identifies the characteristic-three Jacobian length, wild \((3,4)\)-cusp, Frobenius-twisted strange point, or all-flex tangent geometry. The independently documented complex marked-point discriminant of this quartic concerns a distinct characteristic-zero stratification, whereas the published representation-theoretic treatment of the nine-point quartic system concerns \(S_4\) over the complex numbers; neither implies the result. Chen--Contreras study strange rational curves and inseparability in positive characteristic, but not this fixed quartic family and its Jacobian interpolation scheme.

## Limitations
The geometric cusp, integrality, rationality, and uniqueness statements are restricted to \(\Delta\ne0\) in the chart \(c=1\); only the Jacobian-scheme formula holds for arbitrary \(R\). The proof does not classify all excluded parameters or all mixed-characteristic deformations, and does not assert a projective equivalence classification of positive-characteristic strange quartics. A potentially unindexed equivalent calculation is a priority risk. Independent external audit has not been performed.
