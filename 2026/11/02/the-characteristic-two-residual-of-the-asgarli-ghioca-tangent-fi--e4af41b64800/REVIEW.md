# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem concerns one geometrically integral residual factor of the published finite-field polynomial in characteristic two, with explicit quantifiers \(q=2^r\), \(r\ge3\). Divisibility, the three boundary-line intersection restrictions, the complete off-boundary Jacobian equations, and the finite-field counting identities are derived exactly in the proof. The ordinary cusp classification depends on a nonzero cubic term along the unique doubled tangent. That factor is proved nonzero from pairwise distinctness and \(s+a+b=1\), and the universal polynomial identity was independently checked symbolically. The absence of multibranch singularities, together with reducedness and Bézout, proves geometric integrality. The normalization genus follows from the number of \(\delta=1\) cusps. Exact small-field tests verify \(q=8,16,32\) as reproducibility checks, not as proof of the infinite family.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact \((q-2)(q-4)\) rational-point family comes within \(q-7\) of the standard plane point bound while having an explicit large cuspidal basket whose geometric singularities are never rational over the defining field. The full geometric/arithmetic boundary structure supplies a meaningful description of a neglected characteristic-two degeneration of an established construction, and is not simply the fact that the polynomial factors.
