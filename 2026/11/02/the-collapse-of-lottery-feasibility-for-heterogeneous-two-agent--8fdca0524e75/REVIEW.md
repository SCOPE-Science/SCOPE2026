# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The complete argument first proves that fractional Pareto optimality requires each one-sided item be allocated to its zero-cost agent and suffices because aggregate cost reaches its minimum \(c\). The shared-item count \(x\) then gives exact weighted envy inequalities \(pc-(1-p)b\leq x\leq p(c+a)\). In any WEFX+fPO realization, a required asymmetric zero-cost chore forces one of the two **full** WEF inequalities pointwise; the other follows in at least one support point from its expectation, yielding a deterministic witness. Deterministic WEF implies strong WEFX because own-chore removal does not increase cost. Binomial enumeration establishes the exact labeled count. The proof covers \(0<p<1\), nonnegative \(a,b,c,d\), \(a+b\geq1\), empty bundles, and zero-cost chores; it does not infer universal truth from finite testing. Two exact checkers, including an independent full-assignment one, corroborated the formulas without floating point.

## Originality — PASS with publication-priority risk

Section 4 of Lin–Wu–Zhou (arXiv:2609.13970v1) shows a **homogeneous** two-common-chore obstruction under unequal weights and Theorem 4.8 guarantees equal-weight lotteries, neither of which implies the heterogeneous exact criterion here. Its Theorem 3.5 asserts deterministic WEFX+fPO, not exact WEF. Lin–Liu–Tao–Zhou (arXiv:2609.02724v1) guarantees only the weaker ex-post WEF1T; Panchapakesan–Vaish (arXiv:2610.14994v1) characterizes lotteries via subsidy-enabled weighted envy-freeability after rescaling, not the ordinary subsidy-free WEF interval. Recorded searches for one-sided binary chore lottery, WEFX zero-removal, convex-hull feasibility and published records found no equivalent; failed search is not uniqueness proof. An unindexed equivalent or unnoticed specialization remains the main risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The general unequal-weight best-of-both-worlds obstruction becomes a **complete instance-level decision criterion** at a natural two-agent heterogeneous binary boundary, not merely another obstruction or one numerical example. More significantly, any one one-sided chore eliminates the possible benefit of randomization relative to deterministic exact weighted fairness, despite an all-shared single chore admitting nontrivial successful lotteries. The theorem also counts all deterministic witnesses and identifies a guaranteed-feasible subdomain with both asymmetric types. The proof supplies a structural explanation via strong WEFX and zero-cost-item removal. No theorem for all-shared chores, three agents, or alternative EFX conventions is claimed.

## Limitations and closest literature

Most directly related: arXiv:2609.13970v1, arXiv:2609.02724v1, arXiv:2610.14994v1. Exhaustive checks end at eight chores and rational weights of denominator at most nine; generality rests solely on the written proof. Publication-priority certainty would require external verification.
