# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Each non-squarefree monic cubic has exactly one repeated linear root; counting pairs of repeated roots after a nonzero affine shift is therefore exhaustive. In odd characteristic, the algebraic inverse correspondence \(d^3=ut+2v\), with \(d\ne0\), proves the count in both the nonconstant and constant-shift cases, including characteristic three. In characteristic two, the Frobenius bijection reduces simultaneous repeated roots to \(au=v\). Inclusion--exclusion and the cube-map kernel establish the stated counts for every prime power. Exact finite-field gcd enumeration independently tests 293 cases in 11 fields; it is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual risk

The closest directly comparable object is Keating--Rudnick [2], Appendix A: it counts simultaneous squarefreeness under shifts, but its statement is large-degree asymptotic, not an exact formula for all degree-three shifts. Carmon--Rudnick [1] use the Möbius sign instead of its square and give estimates and critical-point constraints, not this exact count. Carmon [3] handles sign correlations in characteristic two. Bary-Soroker--Stix [4] count simultaneous irreducibility, stronger and different from squarefreeness. The exact cubic-residue split and characteristic-two spectrum were not found in these full texts or targeted research-record searches. An implicit consequence of a broader historical theorem or unlocated enumeration remains possible; searches alone never certify novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This settles the first low-degree case in which a nontrivial cube-map obstruction naturally appears in the squarefree two-point correlation. The count covers the complete \(q^2-1\)-shift parameter space for infinitely many finite fields and reveals a sharp arithmetic transition between odd cubic-residue fields and characteristic-two fields. It provides exact finite-field benchmarks for large-degree asymptotic squarefree-pair theory, rather than merely reporting a few computational examples.

## Residual scientific limitations

Only degree three, nonzero affine shifts and squarefreeness are treated. No independent mathematical audit has occurred, and unlocated classical finite-field correlation identities remain a novelty risk.
