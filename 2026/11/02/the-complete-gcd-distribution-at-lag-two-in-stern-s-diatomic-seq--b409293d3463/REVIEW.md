# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The binary trailing-ones decomposition yields the exact identity \(\gcd(s(n),s(n+2))=\gcd(2k+1,s(u)-s(u+1))\). The modular primitive-pair distribution is an explicit published theorem of Reznick (2006, Theorem 4.7), not a conjectured input. Local CRT counting gives the diagonal fraction \(\prod_{p^a\parallel d}(p^{a-1}(p+1))^{-1}\). A geometric run-length sum gives the exact divisor densities, while a uniform exponentially small tail justifies both exact-value densities and Möbius inversion. The finite checker confirms 262144 exact index identities and primitive-pair counts for moduli below 100. The full infinite theorem rests on the cited theorem and analytic tail proof.

**Originality — PASS, with a substantive antecedent risk.** Reznick's Corollary 2.4 and Theorem 4.7 provide both major ingredients; the contribution is the *new derived lag-two gcd classification* rather than those ingredients. The 2017 work of Bettin–Drappeau–Spiegelhofer studies logarithmic distributions of single Stern values, not shifted gcds. A 2021 study by Dilcher–Ericksen concerns polynomials whose coefficients are Stern numbers rather than lag-two gcd frequencies. Searches on exact shifted-gcd expressions, divisibility densities, alternative fusc nomenclature, and the base OEIS record found no equivalent stated classification. Because these ingredients are powerful, an implicit specialization in earlier work remains the main priority risk. Absence of a search match is not proof of priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Adjacent Stern terms are always coprime, whereas the next-nearest gcd has a nondegenerate distribution with *every odd positive integer* occurring at positive density. The exact Euler-factor/short-run product, full probability law, and exponential tail characterize a natural local arithmetic statistic, extending one-dimensional distribution theory in a precise manner rather than merely recomputing a table. No result for arbitrary shifts is claimed.
