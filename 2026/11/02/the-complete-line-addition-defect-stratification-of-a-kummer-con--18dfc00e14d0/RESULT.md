# The complete line-addition defect stratification of a Kummer conic pencil

## Finding
Let \(k\geq2\) be an integer, and over \(\mathbb C\) put \(Q_k=(xy)^k+z^{2k}\). For every projective line \(L_{a,b,c}: ax+by+cz=0\), where \([a:b:c]\in(\mathbb P^2)^\vee\), let \(D_{k,L}\) be the reduced degree-\(2k+1\) curve with equation \(F=L_{a,b,c}Q_k=0\). Write \(\nu(D_{k,L})=\max_j\dim_{\mathbb C} (I_F/J_F)_j\), where \(J_F\) is the homogeneous Jacobian ideal and \(I_F\) its saturation.

**Theorem.** For every \(k\ge2\) and every line \(L_{a,b,c}\), the defect is exactly
\[
\nu(D_{k,L})=\begin{cases}
0,&[a:b:c]=[1:0:0]\text{ or }[0:1:0],\\
1,&(a=0\text{ or }b=0)\text{ at every other point},\\
1,&ab\ne0\text{ and }c^{2k}+(4ab)^k=0,\\
2,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus line addition produces a free curve at exactly two lines, a nearly free curve precisely on the complement of those two points in the union of the dual coordinate lines \(a=0\), \(b=0\) and the degree-\(2k\) dual tangency divisor \(c^{2k}+(4ab)^k=0\), and a neither-free-nor-nearly-free curve everywhere else. The free exponents are \((1,2k-1)\). In the nearly free locus they are \((1,2k)\) at \([0:0:1]\), and \((2,2k-1)\) elsewhere.

## Assumptions and scope
All curves and infinitesimal automorphisms are over \(\mathbb C\). The integer \(k\) is arbitrary and at least two. The polynomial \(Q_k\) factors into \(k\) distinct smooth conics \(xy-\alpha z^2\), with \(\alpha^k=-1\). Its components meet only at \(P_1=[1:0:0]\) and \(P_2=[0:1:0]\), each with common tangency; any added line is a different irreducible component, so \(D_{k,L}\) is reduced. The theorem classifies embedded line additions, rather than projective-isomorphism classes of resulting curves.

## Proof
**1. A uniform quadratic relation and the absence of other linear relations.** Let \(d=2k+1\), \(E=x\partial_x+y\partial_y+z\partial_z\), and \(T=x\partial_x-y\partial_y\). Since \(T(Q_k)=0\), \(T(F)=(ax-by)Q_k\). Therefore
\[
\left(dL_{a,b,c}T-(ax-by)E\right)(F)=0.
\]
The displayed vector field has quadratic polynomial coefficients; hence the minimal Jacobian syzygy degree \(r\) is at most two. To classify linear syzygies, a projective linear vector field preserving the reduced union must preserve each of its distinct conic components and its unique line component: a connected one-parameter subgroup cannot permute a finite collection. Since any two conics meet at exactly \(P_1,P_2\), the connected action fixes these two points. Its matrix is consequently upper triangular with only possible extra entries in its final column. Preservation of a conic \(xy-\alpha z^2\) eliminates those extra entries and imposes the diagonal infinitesimal relation \(\delta_x+\delta_y=2\delta_z\). Modulo the projective Euler vector field, the remaining Lie algebra is generated by \(T\). A line is preserved by \(T\) precisely when it is one of \(x=0,y=0,z=0\). Subtracting a scalar Euler vector field gives a degree-one syzygy for these three lines. Thus \(r=1\) for the three coordinate lines and \(r=2\) for every other line.

**2. Local Tjurina calculation for a transverse line through one base point.** The original singular germ at either \(P_i\) is \(u^k+v^{2k}\) and, being weighted homogeneous, has Tjurina number \((k-1)(2k-1)\). If the new line goes through exactly one \(P_i\) and is not the common conic tangent, its local equation is analytically equivalent, after nonzero rescaling, to
\[
g=(u+v)(u^k+v^{2k}).
\]
We claim that the local Tjurina number of this germ is
\[
\tau_0(g)=2k^2-2k+2\qquad(k\ge2).
\]
Here is an exact all-integer local standard-basis calculation, rather than a numerical extrapolation. Work in \(R=\mathbb C[u,v]_{(u,v)}\) and let \(J=(g,g_u,g_v)R\). For \(k\ge3\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=u^k+2kuv^{2k-1}+(2k+1)v^{2k},\\
B&=u^{k-1}v-(2k+2)uv^{2k-1}-(2k+3)v^{2k},\\
C&=v^{2k-1}(u^2-v^2),\\
D&=v^{2k}(u+v),\qquad H=v^{3k-2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first two generators are \(A=g_v\), \(B=(g_u-(k+1)g_v)/k\). The identities \((u+v)A-g=2kv^{2k-1}(u+v)^2\) and \(vA-uB=(2k+2)v^{2k-1}(u+v)^2-D\) show that \(C,D\in J\). (In the second identity the right-hand side is to be read using \(v^{2k-1}(u+v)^2=C+2D\).) Multiplying \(B\) by \(v^{2k-2}\) and reducing with \(C,D\) gives \(v^{3k-2}(({-1})^{k-1}-v^k)\in J\); the bracket is a unit at the origin, proving \(H\in J\). Conversely, \(g,g_u,g_v\) lie in the localized ideal generated by \(A,B,C,D,H\). Buchberger reduction with lexicographic order \(u>v\) shows these five elements are a standard basis, with leading monomials
\[
 u^k,\quad u^{k-1}v,\quad u^2v^{2k-1},\quad uv^{2k},\quad v^{3k-2}.
\]
For instance, \(vA-uB=(2k+2)C+(4k+3)D\), and the remaining critical pairs reduce with \(C,D,H\). Counting standard monomials gives \(k+(2k-2)(k-1)+2+(k-2)=2k^2-2k+2\). For \(k=2\), a local lexicographic standard basis is \(u^2+5v^4,\;uv-7v^4,\;v^5\), with colength six, the same formula. This proves the claim and also exhibits a positive Tjurina defect \(\mu-\tau=k-2\) for \(k\ge3\).

**3. Global Tjurina number.** For \(L=x\) or \(L=y\), one of the base singularities becomes \(u(u^k+v^{2k})\), whose weighted-homogeneous Tjurina number is \(k(2k+1)\), and the other stays \((k-1)(2k-1)\). Thus \(\tau(D)=4k^2-2k+1\). For \(L=z\), both become \(v(u^k+v^{2k})\), each with Tjurina number \(k(2k-1)\), giving \(\tau(D)=4k^2-2k\).

For a noncoordinate line through exactly one base point, the preceding lemma accounts for the modified singularity. It intersects the \(k\) conics transversely in \(k\) further distinct smooth points, creating \(k\) nodes. Consequently
\[
\tau(D)=(2k^2-2k+2)+(k-1)(2k-1)+k=4k^2-4k+3.
\]
For lines avoiding both base points, the conics meet the line in \(2k\) points counted with multiplicity. The dual of \(xy-\alpha z^2=0\) is \(c^2-4\alpha ab=0\), so a tangency to one (and only one) conic occurs exactly when \(ab\ne0\) and \(c^{2k}+(4ab)^k=0\). With no tangency there are \(2k\) nodes and \(\tau(D)=4k^2-4k+2\). At tangency two nodes coalesce into one ordinary tacnode with Tjurina number three, increasing \(\tau(D)\) to \(4k^2-4k+3\). There are no other intersection possibilities.

**4. From Tjurina to the complete defect phase diagram.** Apply Dimca's theorem for reduced degree-\(d\) plane curves with \(r<d/2\):
\[
\nu(D)=(d-1)^2-r(d-r-1)-\tau(D).
\]
For \(r=1\), this is \(4k^2-2k+1-\tau(D)\). For \(r=2\), it is \(4k^2-4k+4-\tau(D)\). Substitution of the just-computed values yields exactly the four cases of the theorem. The standard free and nearly free exponent identities then give the stated exponents. \(\square\)

## Verification
The included `verify_local_tjurina.py` computes exact saturated local Tjurina ideals for \(2\le k\le14\), checks the closed formula, and independently compares the proposed standard basis for \(k\ge3\). The included `verify_syzygy.py` reconstructs the quadratic syzygy and computes exact linear-syzygy nullities for six line types and \(2\le k\le7\). Both programs use exact rational polynomial arithmetic and completed successfully. The all-\(k\) statement follows from the preceding algebraic proof, not from finite testing.

## Relationship to prior work
Malara, Pokora, and Tutaj-Gasińska introduced this Kummer pencil as a nearly free arrangement; their Proposition 3.1 gives its original exponents and Section 3 discusses the three coordinate line additions. These coordinate cases are explicitly prior, not claimed as new. The contribution here is the **exhaustive stratification for every line in the dual plane**, including the tangent divisor, the nonquasihomogeneous local Tjurina computation for lines passing through one base point, and the generic defect-two stratum. Dimca's addition-deletion results for a *free* starting curve do not themselves furnish these facts for this initially nearly free pencil. Broader plus-one-generated addition-deletion literature is related and warrants priority checking beyond the inspected material.

## Limitations
This is a classification over \(\mathbb C\), not an assertion in small positive characteristic. It classifies the Jacobian defect and freeness types, not the full graded Betti table or projective equivalence classes of all added curves. The two coordinate-axis free cases and coordinate-line near-free case were already recorded in the motivating 2020/2021 work. No independent external mathematical validation is asserted.
