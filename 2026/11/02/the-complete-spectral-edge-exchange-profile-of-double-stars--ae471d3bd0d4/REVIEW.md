# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every integer pair \(k\ge m\ge1\), the claim classifies every admissible new edge after deleting either a pendant edge or the central edge. The central-edge candidates have explicit matching/characteristic-polynomial factors whose corrections are nonnegative beyond the original Perron root. For pendant cuts leaving two populated hubs, explicit diagonal resolvents show that any attachment at an existing leaf has strictly smaller spectral radius than restoring the original hub attachment. Two-hub alternatives are ordered by the exact double-star quadratic and its product term. The one-leaf and path boundaries are covered separately by graph isomorphisms and the star extremum. The embedded verifier independently enumerates 19,565 graph exchanges for 78 parameter pairs and checks 112 characteristic-polynomial instances exactly. The finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk

The closest primary work is Bencs–van Dijk–Guo (arXiv:2610.17078v1). Its Example 3.6 proves the central edge of a proper double star is non-sustainable; its Theorem 1.1 gives a broader sufficient condition for whole-tree non-sustainability; and Proposition 3.8 treats global sustainability of certain starlike trees. Those statements are credited. They do not provide the exact classification of *all individual nondecreasing exchanges*, the strict-versus-neutral distinction, or the two-parameter multiplicity table. Bibliographic searches of spectral sustainability, double stars, and leaf reattachment returned no covering statement; mathematical aliases and implications were compared. Historical priority remains provisional because search completeness and inaccessible older articles cannot be guaranteed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Spectral sustainability was introduced as edgewise robustness, so determining which edges possess witnesses—and how many distinct witnesses each has—is intrinsic to the parameter, not an arbitrary statistic. The theorem supplies the complete local exchange neighborhood of a natural infinite two-parameter tree family, with a sharp qualitative transition between balanced, difference-one, and more imbalanced trees; it also resolves the path/one-leaf boundary. The central-edge obstruction alone does not determine this spectrum. The result gives usable exact test instances for subsequent studies of spectral tree exchange orders without asserting a broader unresolved conjecture.

## Closest literature and limits

Bencs–van Dijk–Guo, *Sustainable trees*, 2026-10-12, arXiv:2610.17078v1, especially Sections 2–3, is the principal comparison. Dalfó–Fiol–Garriga (2011) study general spectral changes under pendant perturbation but do not state the exchange spectrum in accessible materials. Belardo–Brunetti (2024) study equal spectral radii after replacing a central edge by a more extensive caterpillar construction; only the abstract was accessible, and their operation is different. An unindexed equivalent characterization cannot be excluded. Finite floating-point checks have limited precision and are not a proof of all parameters.
