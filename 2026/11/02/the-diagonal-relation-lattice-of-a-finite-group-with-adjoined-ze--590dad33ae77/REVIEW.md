# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For any reflexive compatible relation, its nonzero group-square restriction is a finite subgroup containing the group diagonal, hence a unique normal-subgroup congruence. Multiplication by diagonal units forces each cross-zero side to be full or empty. Every one of the four combinations is closed, providing an exact bijection with a normal subgroup and two Boolean flags. Symmetry and transitivity are assessed separately; the coefficient spectrum follows from cyclic prime-power groups. The standalone checker confirms all cases for the smallest cyclic groups by exhaustive relation enumeration and tests a nonabelian group directly. Finite checks are corroboration, not the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The 2026 coefficient paper proves a Clifford-semigroup inequality and explicitly observes that its general construction does not enumerate all compatible relations, but does not state this absorbing-zero classification. Its identity-adjunction example concerns a different multiplication. The earlier 2025 paper establishes qualitative DSC properties, not the exact counts or sharp range. Searches for absorbing-zero groups, zero-augmented Clifford semigroups, and normal-subgroup counting did not locate an equivalent claim. Normal-subgroup correspondence in the group square is prior knowledge; originality concerns its exact extension and coefficient spectrum. Historical treatments might contain an implicit equivalent; priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete lattice classification over the natural and arbitrarily large family of finite group-with-zero Clifford semigroups, motivated by the published question of quantifying the congruence/diagonal-relation gap. The formula gives an exact DSC spectrum, a strict positive threshold of \(1/4\) within the family, and a sharp test for finite simplicity from the coefficient \(3/8\). It distinguishes two precise mechanisms for failure of a compatible relation to be a congruence, rather than merely reproducing the qualitative observation that finite nongroups are not DSC.
