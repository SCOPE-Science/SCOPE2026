# The doubled-node ideal of a general Kummer quartic and a symmetric symbolic defect

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, let \(K\subset\mathbb P^3_k\) be a **general** quartic Kummer surface with exactly sixteen ordinary nodes, and let \(I\subset R=k[X,Y,Z,W]\) denote their reduced homogeneous ideal. Write \(F\) for the quartic defining \(K\) and \(G=(F_X,F_Y,F_Z,F_W)\) for its homogeneous cubic gradient ideal. Then

\[
I^{(2)}=I^2+(F)=(F)+G^2.
\]

The doubled-node subscheme \(2N\) has length \(64\), Hilbert function
\[
H_{R/I^{(2)}}(d)=(1,4,10,20,34,52,64,64,\ldots),\qquad d=0,1,\ldots,
\]

and Hilbert series
\[
\frac{1+3t+6t^2+10t^3+14t^4+18t^5+12t^6}{1-t}.
\]

For the **specific rational symmetric tetrahedroid displayed below**, the finite-length symbolic defect \(M=I^{(2)}/I^2\) is generated by the class of \(F\), with
\[
\dim_k M_d=\begin{cases}1,4,10,4,&d=4,5,6,7\text{ respectively};\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]

Thus the **explicit witness** has \(\dim_k M=19\); on the nonempty general open set, a minimal homogeneous generating set for \(I^{(2)}\) consists of one quartic and the ten products of the four independent gradient cubics. The generic defect dimensions beyond degree six are not claimed.

## Assumptions and scope

“General” means a point of a **nonempty Zariski-open subset** of the irreducible parameter space of quartic Jacobian Kummer surfaces in characteristic zero (after a finite marking cover if necessary to label nodes). The claim is neither an assertion about every special Kummer quartic nor one about characteristic two or other positive characteristics. The symbolic square means \(I^{(2)}=\bigcap_{P\in N}\mathfrak m_P^2\), the saturated ideal of sixteen first infinitesimal neighbourhoods of points in \(\mathbb P^3\).

An exact rational witness is the tetrahedroid
\[
\begin{split}
& p=W+X+Y+Z,\quad q=W+X-Y-Z,\\
& r=W-X+Y-Z,\quad s=W-X-Y+Z,\\
& F=3(2X^2+2Y^2+2Z^2-3W^2)^2-28pqrs.
\end{split}
\]

Its nodes, all displayed with last coordinate \(4\), are \((a:b:c:4)\) for \(a,b,c\in\{\pm1\}\), \(abc=1\), together with the twelve points formed by choosing a coordinate axis \(i\in\{X,Y,Z\}\), a sign \(\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}\), and \(u\in\{\pm2\}\): on axis \(i\) put \(4\epsilon\), and on the other two axes, in increasing coordinate order, put \(u,u\epsilon\). There are four plus twelve distinct points.

## Proof

**1. Exact nodal model.** Direct substitution makes \(F\) and its four first derivatives vanish at the sixteen points. The determinant of the affine \((X,Y,Z)\)-Hessian evaluated at these points is \(-693633024\) at the four diagonal points and \(-11098128384\) at the other twelve. Hence all are ordinary nodes. After setting \(W=1\), the lexicographic Gröbner basis of the four partials has leading monomials \(X,Y^4,Y^2Z^2,Z^6\); the corresponding standard monomial count is \(4\cdot 6-2\cdot4=16\). On each chart at infinity \(W=0\) and one of \(X,Y,Z=1\), the gradient ideal is the unit ideal. The gradient scheme therefore consists of precisely the displayed sixteen reduced nodes. A quartic with sixteen ordinary nodes is a Kummer quartic; this is a classical characterization, not a novel assertion.

**2. Exact evaluation and first-jet ranks.** Work over \(\mathbb Q\) at the witness, with degree-\(d\) monomials in \(X,Y,Z,W\). Evaluate them at the sixteen points. For the doubled scheme, also evaluate each of the three \(X,Y,Z\) derivatives, obtaining a \(64\)-row jet matrix. The rank of ordinary point evaluation is \(16\) in degrees \(3,4\), so \(\dim I_3=4\), \(\dim I_4=19\), and no equation of degree below \(3\) vanishes at every node. The four partial derivatives span \(I_3\). Their sixteen linear multiples are linearly independent, and three further quartics complement them to a basis of \(I_4\). Since the reduced point Hilbert function stabilizes at degree \(3\), the ideal \(I\) is generated by these four cubics and three quartics.

The first-jet ranks in degrees \(3,4,5,6,7,8\) are exactly \(20,34,52,64,64,64\), respectively. The resulting \(I^{(2)}\) components in degrees \(4,5,6,7,8\) have dimensions \(1,4,20,56,101\). In particular the homogeneous coordinate ring reaches the length \(64\) by degree \(6\); the saturated doubled-point ideal is therefore \(7\)-regular and generated in degrees at most \(7\).

Every displayed rank is proved **exactly** rather than guessed from floating-point numerics. For a rational matrix, reduction modulo a prime of good reduction gives a lower bound on its rational rank. The included verifier computes modular ranks at both \(1009\) and \(1013\); when those reach the obvious row bound, the rank is exact over \(\mathbb Q\). In degrees \(4,5\), the explicit independent multiples of \(F\) already impose the matching upper bounds. In degree \(6\) the twenty vectors \(FR_2\) and the ten pairwise products of the four gradient cubics, counted jointly, have exact rank twenty. In degree \(7\), their linear multiples and \(FR_3\) have rank fifty-six. Their inclusions in \(I^{(2)}\), together with the exact jet dimensions above, show
\[
I^{(2)}_d=((F)+G^2)_d\quad(d\le7).
\]
Since \(I^{(2)}\) is generated in degrees at most seven, this proves equality in all degrees. The ten sextic gradient products are linearly independent modulo \(FR_2\), giving the asserted minimal generating degrees.

**3. Ordinary versus symbolic square.** The ordinary square has no forms below degree \(6\); its degree-six component is the ten-dimensional span of pairwise products of four gradient cubics. Direct rational rank computation gives \(\dim (I^2)_7=52\). In degree eight, the complete generating set consisting of products of the four cubic generators and three additional quartic generators has rank \(101\), already the full \(\dim I^{(2)}_8\). Therefore \((I^{(2)}/I^2)_8=0\). As the symbolic square is generated in degrees at most seven and its degree-seven difference is generated by multiples of the quartic \(F\), equality in degree eight persists in all higher degrees. Hence the defect has graded dimensions \((1,4,10,4)\) in degrees \(4,5,6,7\). Moreover the source vector \(F\) generates the entire quotient.

**4. Extension to the general Kummer quartic.** In a finite marking cover of the irreducible characteristic-zero moduli of smooth principally polarized genus-two Jacobians, the sixteen two-torsion nodes move as disjoint finite étale sections. Degree-bounded evaluation matrices, first-jet matrices, and multiplication matrices are algebraic in their coordinates. Rank-at-least conditions are Zariski open. At the rational witness the ranks determining the doubled-point ideal attain their absolute maximum values \(16,34,52,64,20,56\), hence these ranks persist on a nonempty Zariski-open locus (and the dimension bounds from the explicit products and vanishing remain valid). This proves the displayed ideal equality, its doubled-point Hilbert function and its minimal generators for a general Kummer quartic. The rank \(52\) of the degree-seven ordinary square need not remain constant under generic deformation, so the exact defect Hilbert function and total length \(19\) are asserted only for the certified symmetric witness. The good-prime modular rank computations establish exact rational witness ranks; the open-condition argument, not experimental extrapolation, establishes the generic theorem.

## Verification

Run \(\texttt{python3 kummer_node_square_verifier.py}\) with Python 3, SymPy and NumPy. The program reconstructs the integer quartic, all sixteen rational nodes, Hessians, affine/infinite Gröbner checks, rational kernels and product bases. It computes both independent-prime exact rank lower-bound certificates and verifies the rational degree-seven ordinary-square rank. The file \(\texttt{verification_output.txt}\) records both prime ranks, local tests, Hilbert functions, generator structure and terminal \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\). The code is standalone and uses no third-party mathematical data downloads.

## Relationship to prior work

The quartic Kummer model and its sixteen-node configuration are classical; Garbagnati–Sarti [1], especially their discussion of the quartic with sixteen nodes and tropes, provide modern geometric context. Bocci–Harbourne [2], Section 1 and preliminaries, define symbolic powers for sets of points and discuss the distinct general question of power containment. Neither inspected text computes the second symbolic-square ideal, sixteen fat-node Hilbert function, or the length-nineteen defect for the displayed symmetric Kummer tetrahedroid.

The closest related calculations in prior mathematical records address Kummer node–trope incidence modules and the symbolic square of the ten Segre-cubic nodes and eighteen diagonal-cubic Eckardt points. Those latter identities have similar formal shapes, but their point configurations, ideal degrees, jet ranks and defect modules differ; neither implies the sixteen-node Kummer result. A historical implicit consequence of general theorems or a classical computation remains possible; a negative search is not a proof of priority.

## Limitations

No claim is made for every special Kummer quartic, other surface models, higher symbolic powers, or positive characteristic. The sole certified explicit model is the rational tetrahedroid; genericity of the doubled-point ideal and Hilbert function relies on the openness proof. The degree-seven ordinary-square rank and therefore the length-nineteen defect have not been proved generic. Exact modular rank computations certify characteristic-zero lower bounds, but numerical examples are not evidence of a universal assertion. Historical priority remains qualified, and independent mathematical auditing has not been performed.
