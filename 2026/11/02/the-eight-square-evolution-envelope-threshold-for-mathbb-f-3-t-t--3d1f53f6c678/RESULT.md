# The eight-square evolution-envelope threshold for \(\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^4)\)

## Finding

Let \(A=\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^4)\), a four-dimensional unital commutative associative algebra. Its squaring map requires **exactly eight** simultaneous squares of linear forms over \(\mathbb F_3\). Equivalently, the smallest dimension of an evolution algebra over \(\mathbb F_3\) having an ideal isomorphic to \(A\) is **eight**:
\[
\operatorname{wr}(H_A)=\operatorname{edim}(A)=8.
\]
The answer closes the first explicit finite-field gap in the recent truncated-polynomial evolution-envelope calculation: its general lower bound gives seven, while the paper records that seven summands do not suffice in this case [1].

## Assumptions and scope

All scalar arithmetic is in \(\mathbb F_3\). Write an element of \(A\) as \(a+bt+ct^2+dt^3\), with \((a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb F_3^4\). Its squaring map, in the ordered basis \((1,t,t^2,t^3)\), is
\[
H_A(a,b,c,d)=(a^2,\,2ab,\,b^2+2ac,\,2ad+2bc).
\]
The simultaneous Waring rank is the least \(s\) for which \(H_A=\sum_{i=1}^s\ell_i^2 v_i\), with \(\ell_i\in(\mathbb F_3^4)^*\) and \(v_i\in\mathbb F_3^4\). An evolution algebra is an algebra with a basis \((e_i)\) satisfying \(e_ie_j=0\) for distinct basis indices. An ideal here means an ordinary two-sided ideal; products are commutative, and no assumption of associativity is imposed on the ambient evolution algebra.

## Proof

**Upper bound, with an explicit certificate.** In the following order, take eight linear forms
\[
(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_8)=(d,\ c,\ b,\ b+c+d,\ a,\ a+c,\ a+b,\ a+b+c+2d).
\]
Let \(v_i\in\mathbb F_3^4\) be the successive rows of the matrix
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&2\\
0&0&2&1\\
0&2&1&1\\
0&0&0&2\\
1&2&2&2\\
0&0&1&1\\
0&1&0&1\\
0&0&0&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct expansion modulo three gives \(H_A=\sum_{i=1}^8\ell_i^2v_i\), establishing \(\operatorname{wr}(H_A)\le8\). The squares of these eight forms are linearly independent as degree-two polynomial coefficient vectors.

**Lower bound, by a finite exhaustive linear-algebra certificate.** Let \(S\) be the ten-dimensional \(\mathbb F_3\)-space of quadratic forms in \((a,b,c,d)\), and let \(W\subset S\) be the four-space spanned by the four coordinate forms of \(H_A\). There are exactly \((3^4-1)/(3-1)=40\) projective classes of nonzero linear forms; each class has a unique representative whose first nonzero coefficient is one. Denote the forty square forms by \(s_i\).

Choose any linear quotient \(P:S\to\mathbb F_3^6\) with kernel \(W\), and put \(q_i=P(s_i)\). The forty \(q_i\) span the quotient. If at most seven squares spanned \(W\), and \(U\) were the span of their images under \(P\), then
\[
\dim U\le7-\dim W=3.
\]
Every such \(U\) is contained in a three-space generated by three of the \(q_i\), since the forty \(q_i\) span the quotient. It is therefore enough to examine the distinct three-spaces generated by triples of the forty points. For such a three-space \(U\), let
\[
I(U)=\{i:q_i\in U\},\qquad V(U)=\operatorname{span}\{s_i:i\in I(U)\}.
\]
The exact intersection dimension is
\[
\dim(V(U)\cap W)=\dim V(U)-\dim P(V(U)).
\]
The attached executable certificate enumerates all forty projective forms, all \(\binom{40}{3}=9880\) triples, and the **4420 distinct generated three-spaces**. For each, it computes \(I(U)\) and both ranks by exact elimination over \(\mathbb F_3\). The complete census below is indexed by \((|I(U)|,\dim(V(U)\cap W))\):

| Closure size | Intersection dimension | Three-spaces |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 1 | 3213 |
| 5 | 1 | 243 |
| 5 | 2 | 567 |
| 6 | 2 | 243 |
| 7 | 2 | 45 |
| 7 | 3 | 27 |
| 8 | 3 | 81 |
| 13 | 3 | 1 |

In every case, \(\dim(V(U)\cap W)\le3\), so no span of at most seven squares contains the four-space \(W\). This completes the lower bound \(\operatorname{wr}(H_A)\ge8\). The enumeration is exhaustive over a finite set; no finite-to-infinite extrapolation is used.

**Equivalence with the minimal ideal embedding.** Any algebra embedding \(\varphi:A\hookrightarrow E\) as an ideal of an \(s\)-dimensional evolution algebra with natural basis \((e_i)\) supplies an injective coefficient map \(x\mapsto(\ell_i(x))\). Taking the square of \(\varphi(x)=\sum_i\ell_i(x)e_i\), then projecting linearly onto \(\varphi(A)\) and applying \(\varphi^{-1}\), supplies a simultaneous-square decomposition of \(H_A\) of length \(s\). Conversely, given the above eight-term decomposition \(H_A=DL\), its linear-form map \(L\) is injective: a vector annihilated by all \(\ell_i\) would annihilate every element of \(A\), whereas \(A\) has an identity. On \(\mathbb F_3^8\) define the quadratic map \(\widetilde H=LD\), where \(D(y)=\sum_i y_i^2v_i\); polarization makes this an evolution algebra. It has \(L(A)\) as an ideal, because every product lies in \(L(A)\), and the square identity shows that \(L\) is an algebra embedding. Thus the minimum ambient dimension is exactly the simultaneous Waring rank. This constructive correspondence agrees with [1, Section 4].

## Relationship to prior work

Costoya, Fernández Ouaridi, and Viruel [1] established the general relation between minimal evolution envelopes and simultaneous Waring rank and proved the sharp \(2n-1\) formula for truncated polynomial algebras over sufficiently large fields. They specifically observed that over \(\mathbb F_3\), the case \(n=4\) has Waring rank **greater than seven**. Those general results and the strict lower bound are prior work. The contribution here is the explicit eight-square decomposition together with a stand-alone exhaustive minimality certificate, which establishes the exact exceptional value.

Older research on multiplicative complexity and algorithms for full polynomial products [2] is relevant but does not, from the accessible abstract, settle the symmetric rank of the **truncated** four-dimensional algebra. See the limitations concerning incomplete historical comparison.

## Limitations

This result concerns the one algebra \(\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^4)\), not arbitrary finite fields, arbitrary truncated lengths, or arbitrary commutative algebras. It proves optimality among simultaneous-square decompositions and minimal evolution-ideal embeddings; it does not claim optimality among unrestricted nonlinear arithmetic circuits. The full text of [2] was not available for comparison, so historical priority relative to older polynomial-algorithm literature remains uncertain. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
