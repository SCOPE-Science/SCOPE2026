# The exact involution Jordan spectrum of complete three-level incidence algebras in characteristic two

## Finding

Let \(q\) be a power of two. In the unit group of the incidence algebra of the ordinal sum of three antichains of positive sizes \(a,b,c\), the number of elements \(g\) with \(g^2=1\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(g-1)=r\) is the explicit polynomial \(F_{a,b,c;r}(q)\) given below. The identity is the unique rank-zero solution. For each \(r\geq1\), all these elements have **order exactly two** and their ambient matrix Jordan type is \((2^r,1^{a+b+c-2r})\). The possible ranks form the entire interval
\[
0\leq r\leq R:=\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{a+b+c}{2}\right\rfloor,\ a+b,\ a+c,\ b+c\right\}.
\]

Define the Gaussian binomial \({m\brack j}_q\) to be the number of \(j\)-subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^m\), and define the number of rank-\(j\) rectangular matrices by
\[
\mathcal M_q(m,n;j)={m\brack j}_q\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(q^n-q^i),
\]
with the value zero outside \(0\leq j\leq\min(m,n)\). Then
\[
\boxed{F_{a,b,c;r}(q)=
\sum_{\substack{0\leq\alpha\leq\min(a,b)\\
0\leq\beta\leq\min(b-\alpha,c)\\
0\leq\rho\leq\min(a-\alpha,c-\beta)\\
\alpha+\beta+\rho=r}}
\mathcal M_q(a,b;\alpha)\mathcal M_q(b-\alpha,c;\beta)
q^{\alpha(c-\beta)+a\beta}\mathcal M_q(a-\alpha,c-\beta;\rho).}
\]
The formula is valid as a square-zero radical count over **every** finite field; only its interpretation as a complete unit-group involution count requires characteristic two.

For example, in characteristic two with \((a,b,c)=(2,2,2)\), \(q=2\), the counts for ranks \(0,1,2,3\) are respectively \(1,81,630,216\), so there are \(927\) nonidentity involutions.

## Assumptions and scope

The poset is the ordinal sum \(P=P_a\oplus P_b\oplus P_c\), where each \(P_d\) is a discrete antichain of cardinality \(d\). All comparable pairs of distinct elements are across different levels. Fix a linear extension that groups elements by level, and identify its incidence algebra with the algebra of block-upper-triangular matrices with **diagonal** blocks on each level and arbitrary rectangular strict blocks between different levels. Its Jacobson radical \(J\) consists precisely of matrices
\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&A&C\\0&0&B\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 A\in M_{a\times b}(\mathbb F_q),\quad B\in M_{b\times c}(\mathbb F_q),\quad C\in M_{a\times c}(\mathbb F_q).
\]
This is *not* the full block-upper-triangular matrix algebra (whose diagonal blocks would be arbitrary matrices). Rank and Jordan form refer to the natural \((a+b+c)\)-dimensional representation.

## Proof

**Step 1: reduction to the square-zero radical.** Let \(g\) satisfy \(g^2=1\) in characteristic two. Every diagonal coordinate \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_q\) satisfies \(\lambda^2=1\), hence \(\lambda=1\). Thus \(g=1+X\) for a unique \(X\in J\); the relation is \(X^2=0\). Conversely each \(X\in J\) with \(X^2=0\) yields an involution. Block multiplication shows \(X^2=0\) if and only if \(AB=0\), with no condition on \(C\). Since \(X\) is square-zero, each rank-\(r\) matrix has Jordan form \((2^r,1^{a+b+c-2r})\).

**Step 2: canonical rank refinements.** For a square-zero \(X\), let \(\alpha=\operatorname{rank}A\), \(\beta=\operatorname{rank}B\), and put \(K=\ker B\leq\mathbb F_q^c\). Let \(\rho\) be the rank of the induced map \(K\xrightarrow{C}\mathbb F_q^a/\operatorname{im}A\). The equation \(AB=0\) is precisely \(\operatorname{im}B\subseteq\ker A\). The dimension constraints in the displayed sum are necessary and sufficient. The image of \(X\) has its \(\mathbb F_q^b\)-projection equal to \(\operatorname{im}B\) of dimension \(\beta\). The image vectors with zero \(\mathbb F_q^b\)-projection are generated by \(\operatorname{im}A\) and \(C(K)\), of dimension \(\alpha+\rho\). Hence \(\operatorname{rank}X=\alpha+\beta+\rho\).

**Step 3: count each stratum.** There are \(\mathcal M_q(a,b;\alpha)\) choices for \(A\). For each, \(B\) is a rank-\(\beta\) map from \(\mathbb F_q^c\) into the fixed \((b-\alpha)\)-space \(\ker A\), in \(\mathcal M_q(b-\alpha,c;\beta)\) ways. Fix \(A,B\). Choose a complement of \(K\) in \(\mathbb F_q^c\). The map \(C\) on this \(\beta\)-dimensional complement is arbitrary, contributing \(q^{a\beta}\); its restriction to \(K\) has an induced rank-\(\rho\) map into \(\mathbb F_q^a/\operatorname{im}A\), contributing \(\mathcal M_q(a-\alpha,c-\beta;\rho)\); and each such quotient map has \(q^{\alpha(c-\beta)}\) lifts. Multiplication and summation over all disjoint strata prove the polynomial formula. No exceptional characteristic was used in this count.

**Step 4: the exact feasible interval.** Square-zero implies \(r\leq\lfloor(a+b+c)/2\rfloor\). Moreover \(X\) has only \(a+b\) nonzero rows and only \(b+c\) nonzero columns, and Step 2 yields \(r=\alpha+\beta+\rho\leq a+\beta\leq a+c\). These establish \(r\leq R\). For the reverse inequality, regard the three levels as vertex parts of a complete tripartite graph and choose a matching. Its cardinality can equal \(R=\min(\lfloor(a+b+c)/2\rfloor, (a+b+c)-\max(a,b,c))\): the usual complete-multipartite matching argument pairs across the largest part until its shortage is exhausted, or repeatedly pairs vertices from two currently largest nonempty parts until at most one vertex remains. Orient the matching edges from the earlier to the later level and put entry \(1\) in the corresponding allowed positions of \(X\), all other entries zero. Disjoint endpoints imply \(X^2=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}X=\#\text{edges}\). Taking submatchings of every size from zero to \(R\) proves that all and only these ranks occur.

## Verification

The standalone `verify_involution_rank.py` constructs each admissible matrix, checks the square-zero equation by direct multiplication, computes rank by Gaussian elimination, and independently compares the complete observed rank histogram to the closed stratum sum. It runs twelve *exhaustive* configurations over the prime fields \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_5\), including the \((2,2,2)\) binary example. Run `python3 verify_involution_rank.py` and check for `THREE_LEVEL_INCIDENCE_INVOLUTION_RANK_OK 12`. The code tests finite instances, not an infinite family; the proof above establishes the general statement.

## Relationship to prior work

Ekhad and Zeilberger (1995) proved a formula for the **total** square-zero count in the full upper-triangular matrix algebra, using Kirillov's recurrence; their result does not specify this three-level incidence subalgebra or its complete rank distribution. Yip (2013) gives Jordan-type counts for the **full** strict upper-triangular algebra and explains Haglund's rank counting for arbitrary Ferrers-supported matrices; the latter does not impose the nonlinear square-zero relation \(AB=0\). These are genuine broader-looking and methodologically close precedents, but the formulas alone do not identify the constrained three-level subvariety. Gargate and Gargate (2019) count incidence-algebra involutions under the explicit hypothesis that the finite field has characteristic different from two, whereas the group-theoretic part here treats characteristic two and refines by ambient Jordan type. The present census requires a separate \((\alpha,\beta,\rho)\) fiber decomposition. An equivalent specialization of broader rank-variety or quiver-representation literature cannot be excluded without exhaustive review.

## Limitations

The full involution interpretation applies only to finite fields of characteristic two, although the square-zero radical formula holds for all finite fields. The classification is by ambient matrix Jordan type, **not** by conjugacy under the incidence-algebra unit group. It does not cover incomplete interlevel incidence, more than three antichain levels, or higher nilpotency equations. No claim of an independent audit or definitive global publication priority is made.
