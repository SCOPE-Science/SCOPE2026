# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The incidence-algebra diagonal is a product of fields, so an order-dividing-two unit has all diagonal entries one in characteristic two; the remaining strictly three-layer matrix squares to zero precisely when \(AB=0\). Its rank is \(\alpha+\beta+\rho\) using the residual map on \(\ker B\) modulo \(\operatorname{im}A\). The displayed matrix-rank product independently counts \(A\), then \(B\) with image in \(\ker A\), then the free and constrained parts of \(C\); each tuple belongs to a unique stratum. The maximum-rank boundary follows from row/column bounds, square-zero, and a complete tripartite matching construction. Direct brute-force verification covers 12 finite configurations. Risk: computational tests are finite; the infinite result depends on the supplied argument, not on extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The 1995 Ekhad–Zeilberger paper counts all square-zero upper triangular matrices but does not provide the constrained incidence-radical rank census. Yip's 2013 paper proves full triangular Jordan counts and cites Haglund's unrestricted Ferrers rank enumeration; imposing \(AB=0\) changes the problem. Gargate–Gargate 2019 explicitly assumes characteristic different from two when counting incidence-algebra involutions. A search of mathematical result records for block-algebra rank spectra, triple-level poset involutions and square-zero matrix constraints found no matching statement; that absence is **not** a proof of global priority. The principal remaining risk is that an older general quiver-representation or pattern-group rank formula might subsume the present specialization.

## Value — PASS

Involutions in characteristic-two incidence-algebra unit groups are a natural invariant, and ambient Jordan rank determines their dimensions of fixed spaces and the nilpotent rank of their deviation from identity. Exact multiplicities for all feasible Jordan types answer a complete counting question across all three positive level sizes and all even field orders. The sharp matching-based spectrum boundary additionally classifies which types occur. This is stronger than reproducing the known unconstrained Ferrers-rank or full triangular total counts; it makes no claim to a general higher-level classification.
