# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The full weight-preserving bijection between 0–2 copies of every ternary power and an unlimited count of ones proves the first-difference formula coefficientwise; the reverse map uses unique base-three expansion. The support argument explicitly handles the empty partition, the two exceptional plateau values, singleton witnesses for other indices, and the two-part witnesses at powers of three. The accompanying checker independently calculated 513 values and verified 5,000 ternary conversions. Finite checks are supporting diagnostics, not proof of the infinite identity.

**Originality — PASS within the inspected literature.** Merca's 2024 source defines this unweighted counting function and gives its product generating function, but does not classify its adjacent plateaus or exhibit the present positive first-difference interpretation. Liu's arXiv:2409.19907 treats positivity for truncated theta-type series and explicitly identifies the two different conjectures addressed. Targeted searches for monotonicity, 2-adic multiplicity bounds, ternary-power removal and equivalent generating products found no direct earlier classification. Classical base-carrying bijections are acknowledged; the intrinsic risk is that this application might be implicit in or rediscovered from them. An unindexed exact statement remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Unlike a generic existence inequality, the result provides a complete support/equality classification for a naturally defined, published arithmetic partition function, together with an exact positive representation for *every* first difference. The delicate exception at index three is explained structurally by ternary digit carrying, overcoming the failure of naive add-one injection when a part's multiplicity cap is saturated. The proof is elementary but the classified invariant is natural and infinite.

The claim concerns unweighted counts, not Merca's differently weighted overpartition representation, and has no claim of absolute bibliographic priority.
