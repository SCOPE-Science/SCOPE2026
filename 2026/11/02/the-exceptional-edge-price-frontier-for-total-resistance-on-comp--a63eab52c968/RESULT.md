# The exceptional-edge price frontier for total resistance on complete graphs

## Finding
Let \(n\ge 4\), \(B>0\), and \(\tau>0\). Assign independently chosen, nonnegative conductances to every edge of the complete graph \(K_n\). The edge \(\{1,2\}\) costs \(\tau\) units per unit conductance; every other edge costs one. The cost cannot exceed \(B\). Among connected allocations, minimize the sum \(\mathcal R=\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}\) of unordered-pair electrical effective resistances.

Put
\[
m=n-2,\quad \tau_c=\frac{n}{n-2},\quad
c(\tau)=m+1-\frac{m\tau}{2},\quad
\alpha=\frac1m+\frac1n.
\]
The minimizer is **unique among all independently chosen conductances**, and has three edge classes: \(w\) on \(\{1,2\}\), \(x\) on the \(2m\) edges joining \(\{1,2\}\) to the other vertices, and \(y\) on the \(\binom m2\) remaining edges.

For \(0<\tau\le\tau_c\), set
\[
S(\tau)=\sqrt{\frac\tau2}+\frac{m-1}{\sqrt2}
           +\sqrt{\frac{c(\tau)}n},\qquad t=\frac{B}{S(\tau)}.
\]
Then the unique optimal conductances and value are
\[
\boxed{\;x=\frac{t}{\sqrt{n c(\tau)}},\qquad
w=\frac{t}{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac2\tau}-\frac{m}{\sqrt{n c(\tau)}}\right),\qquad
y=\frac{t}{m}\left(\sqrt2-\frac2{\sqrt{n c(\tau)}}\right)\;},
\]
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_{\min}(B,\tau)=\frac nB S(\tau)^2.}
\]
Here \(x,y>0\), and \(w>0\) precisely when \(\tau<\tau_c\).

For \(\tau\ge\tau_c\), set
\[
T=\sqrt{(m+1)\alpha}+\frac{m-1}{\sqrt2},\qquad t=\frac BT.
\]
Then the unique minimizer and value are
\[
\boxed{\;w=0,\qquad x=t\sqrt{\frac\alpha{m+1}},\qquad
 y=\frac{t}{m}\left(\sqrt2-2\sqrt{\frac\alpha{m+1}}\right)\;},
\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal R_{\min}(B,\tau)=\frac nB T^2.}
\]
The formulas coincide at \(\tau=\tau_c\). Thus the expensive edge disappears **exactly** at the threshold \(\tau_c=1+2/(n-2)\), while every other edge remains positively weighted, and all optimal conductances become independent of \(\tau\) beyond that point.

The optimized resistance is continuously differentiable in \(\tau\), but has a one-sided quadratic transition. More precisely, as \(\tau\uparrow\tau_c\),
\[
\frac nB T^2-\mathcal R_{\min}(B,\tau)
=\frac{m+1}{2B}\sqrt{\frac m{2n}}\,T(\tau_c-\tau)^2
  +o\big((\tau_c-\tau)^2\big).
\]
In particular, any fixed price premium \(\tau>1\) eventually eliminates the distinguished edge as \(n\) increases.

For example, with \(n=4\) and \(B=1\), the threshold is \(\tau_c=2\). At and above it,
\[
w=0,\qquad x=\frac{3-\sqrt2}{7},\qquad
y=\frac{4\sqrt2-5}{7},\qquad
\mathcal R_{\min}=11+6\sqrt2.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Electrical resistances are computed from the conductance-weighted **combinatorial** Laplacian \(L\) with no degree normalization: \(R_{ij}=(e_i-e_j)^\mathsf T L^\dagger(e_i-e_j)\). Disconnected designs have infinite objective. The budget uses the linear cost \(\tau w_{12}+\sum_{e\ne\{1,2\}}w_e\le B\). Zero conductance is allowed. The statement holds for all real \(B,\tau>0\) and all integers \(n\ge4\); no symmetry constraints are imposed on the design variables. It is a conductance-sizing result, not a claim about unweighted graphs or probabilistic transition matrices.

## Proof
**1. Strict convexity and full symmetry reduction.** On the connected feasible domain, restrict \(L\) to the orthogonal complement of the all-ones vector. This restriction is positive definite, and
\(\mathcal R=n\operatorname{tr}(L^\dagger)\). In a nonzero edge-weight direction, the second derivative of its inverse trace is \(2n\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}HM^{-1}HM^{-1})>0\), where \(M\succ0\) is the restricted Laplacian and \(H\ne0\) its variation. The strict inequality follows by writing the trace as the trace of the product of a positive definite matrix with the square of the nonzero symmetric matrix \(M^{-1/2}HM^{-1/2}\). Edge weights injectively determine \(L\) through its off-diagonal entries. Hence \(\mathcal R\) is strictly convex in all edge weights wherever connected. Compactness of the cost simplex and divergence at the disconnected boundary ensure existence, and strict convexity ensures uniqueness. Scaling shows that the full budget is used.

The price-preserving permutation group generated by exchanging vertices \(1,2\) and arbitrarily permuting the remaining \(m\) vertices preserves objective and constraints. By uniqueness, its action fixes the minimizer. Therefore it has exactly the three classes \(w,x,y\) specified above; this conclusion is not an imposed symmetry assumption.

**2. Exact spectrum and transformed budget.** Pair-difference vectors, contrasts among the \(m\) other vertices, and the between-part vector give respectively the nonzero Laplacian eigenvalues
\[
A=2w+mx\quad(\text{once}),\qquad
D=2x+my\quad(m-1\text{ times}),\qquad nx\quad(\text{once}).
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{\mathcal R}n=\frac1A+\frac{m-1}{D}+\frac1{nx}.
\]
The exact cost identity is
\[
B=\tau w+2mx+\frac{m(m-1)}2y
=\frac\tau2 A+\frac{m-1}{2}D+c(\tau)x.
\]
Nonnegativity of \(w,y\) is equivalent to \(A\ge mx\) and \(D\ge2x\). Connectivity forces \(x>0\).

**3. Cheap-edge regime.** For \(0<\tau\le n/m\), \(c(\tau)\ge m/2>0\). Applying weighted Cauchy–Schwarz to the three reciprocal terms gives
\[
\left(\frac1A+\frac{m-1}{D}+\frac1{nx}\right)
\left(\frac\tau2 A+\frac{m-1}{2}D+c(\tau)x\right)
\ge S(\tau)^2.
\]
Equality holds exactly for
\[
A=t\sqrt{\frac2\tau},\qquad D=t\sqrt2,\qquad
x=\frac t{\sqrt{nc(\tau)}},\qquad t=\frac B{S(\tau)}.
\]
Recovering \(w=(A-mx)/2\) and \(y=(D-2x)/m\) gives the displayed optimum. The first is nonnegative if and only if \(\tau\le n/m\): square the positive sides of \(\sqrt{2/\tau}\ge m/\sqrt{nc(\tau)}\) and use \(n+m=2(m+1)\). The second is strictly positive because \(nc(\tau)\ge nm/2\ge4>2\) for \(n\ge4\). Therefore the unconstrained Cauchy–Schwarz equality point is feasible throughout, and only throughout, the asserted regime.

**4. Expensive-edge regime.** At \(\tau_c=n/m\), the equality solution has \(w=0\). On this face,
\[
\frac{\mathcal R}n=\frac\alpha x+\frac{m-1}{D},\qquad
B=(m+1)x+\frac{m-1}{2}D.
\]
The sharp two-term Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields \(\mathcal R\ge nT^2/B\), with equality at
\(x=t\sqrt{\alpha/(m+1)}\), \(D=t\sqrt2\), \(t=B/T\). The recovered \(y=(D-2x)/m\) is positive, since \(2\alpha<m+1\) for \(m\ge2\). These values coincide with the cheap-edge formula at \(\tau_c\).

For \(\tau>\tau_c\), any connected allocation using \(w>0\) has strictly smaller cost when priced at \(\tau_c\) rather than at \(\tau\). Rescaling it upward to the same budget \(B\) at price \(\tau_c\) strictly decreases resistance, so its value at price \(\tau\) is strictly larger than the \(\tau_c\) optimum. The \(w=0\) optimizer is feasible with the same cost and value at every higher price. Consequently it is the unique global optimum for \(\tau\ge\tau_c\).

**5. Quadratic contact.** Direct differentiation of \(S\) gives \(S(\tau_c)=T\), \(S'(\tau_c)=0\), and
\[
S''(\tau_c)=-\frac{m+1}{2n}\sqrt{\frac m{2n}}<0.
\]
Taylor expansion of \((n/B)S(\tau)^2\) proves the stated quadratic asymptotic. On the other side the value is constant. Since \(\tau_c=1+2/(n-2)\), the density-dependent deletion statement follows.

## Relationship to prior work
Ghosh, Boyd and Saberi (2006 conference version; 2008 expanded paper) establish the convex total-resistance conductance-design framework, symmetry reduction, and the equal-price complete-graph optimum. They do not give the one-distinguished-edge heterogeneous-price support frontier or the displayed exact allocation. Their uniform-price result is recovered here when \(\tau=1\). The 2026 paper of Bhoite and coauthors studies heterogeneous edge costs in mixed-integer graph augmentation and gives cost-normalized KKT conditions, but does not state an exact from-scratch complete-graph priced-edge classification. Earlier work on a priced *cycle* concerns a different graph (its threshold for a four-cycle is not \(2\)). These distinctions concern mathematical statements, not merely titles or numerical matches.

## Limitations
This result addresses one exceptional edge price, a complete-graph candidate topology, nonnegative conductances, and sum-of-pairwise-resistances loss. No formula is claimed for multiple exceptional prices, different graph families, constrained positive lower bounds on each edge, or alternative spectral objectives. The comparisons performed did not establish absolute publication priority; specialized historical treatments of one-exceptional-edge A-optimal network design may still exist. Numerical tests are not proofs for all dimensions or parameter values.
