# The exterior resonance in commuting square-zero derivations of four-dimensional quantum complete intersections

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic different from \(2\), let \(\lambda\in k^\times\), and set
\[
A_\lambda=k\langle x,y\rangle/(x^2,y^2,yx-\lambda xy).
\]
Derivations throughout are ordinary \(k\)-linear associative derivations, not graded derivations. For every integer \(m\ge1\), define \(X_m(A_\lambda)\) to be the locus of ordered tuples \((D_1,\ldots,D_m)\) of derivations satisfying \(D_i^2=0\) and \(D_iD_j=D_jD_i\) for all \(i,j\).

**Theorem (complete phase classification).** If \(\lambda\ne-1\), the reduced variety \(X_m(A_\lambda)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is affine \(2m\)-space and every product \(D_iD_j\) vanishes. If \(\lambda=-1\), all commuting square-zero derivations still annihilate each other. For \(m=1\) the reduced variety is geometrically irreducible of dimension \(3\). For every \(m\ge2\) it has precisely two geometrically irreducible components: a linear component of dimension \(2m\) and an incidence component of dimension \(2m+1\). Their reduced intersection is the rank-at-most-one variety of \(2\)-by-\(m\) matrices, of dimension \(m+1\). Each component and their intersection are defined over \(k\).

Every nonzero square-zero derivation in this family has rank exactly \(1\) as a linear map on the four-dimensional algebra. For every odd prime power \(Q=|k|\), write
\[
\Phi_{m,\lambda}(T_1,\ldots,T_m)=\sum_{(D_i)\in X_m(A_\lambda)(k)}\prod_{i=1}^m T_i^{\operatorname{rank}D_i}.
\]
The **complete joint-rank enumerator** is
\[
\Phi_{m,\lambda}=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^m\bigl(1+(Q^2-1)T_i\bigr),&\lambda\ne-1,\\[5pt]
\displaystyle(Q+2)\prod_{i=1}^m\bigl(1+(Q^2-1)T_i\bigr)-(Q+1)\prod_{i=1}^m\bigl(1+(Q-1)T_i\bigr),&\lambda=-1.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the respective total tuple counts are \(Q^{2m}\) and
\[
C_m(Q)=(Q+2)Q^{2m}-(Q+1)Q^m.
\]
At the resonant value \(\lambda=-1\), there are exactly \(Q+2\) maximal \(k\)-linear subspaces of pairwise commuting square-zero derivations, all of dimension \(2\): one isolated plane and a projective-line family of planes (with \(Q+1\) rational members). The same classification is valid for maximal such linear spaces over an arbitrary field \(k\) of characteristic different from \(2\).

For the resonant algebra over \(\mathbb F_Q\), the **commuting-neighborhood sizes** (including the element itself) among square-zero derivations are \(C_1(Q)\) once (at zero), \(2Q^2-Q\) for exactly \(Q^2-1\) nonzero derivations, and \(Q^2\) for the remaining \(Q^3-Q\) derivations.

## Assumptions and scope

The algebra has basis \((1,x,y,z)\), with \(z=xy\), \(yx=\lambda z\), \(x^2=y^2=0\), and all positive-degree products with \(z\) zero. The scalar parameter is nonzero and the characteristic is odd; no assumption of algebraic closure is made. The positive integer \(m\) is arbitrary. A component assertion means a component of the **reduced** affine variety, without asserting radicality of the ideal generated by the original polynomial equations. Square-zero means literal composition square, not nilpotence modulo inner derivations. All finite-field counts are counts of labelled operators or tuples, never similarity classes.

## Proof

**1. All derivations.** Since \(D(1)=0\), the relations \(D(x^2)=D(y^2)=0\) and \(2\ne0\) force the constant parts of \(D(x),D(y)\) to vanish. Write
\[
D(x)=a x+b y+c z,\qquad D(y)=d x+e y+f z.
\]
The two square relations give \((1+\lambda)b=(1+\lambda)d=0\). The relation \(yx=\lambda xy\) imposes nothing further, and \(D(z)=(a+e)z\). Consequently, for \(\lambda\ne-1\) one has \(b=d=0\), while at \(\lambda=-1\) all six parameters are free. These claims can also be checked by applying the Leibniz identity to every pair of basis elements.

**2. The nonresonant case.** On the span of \(x,y,z\), a derivation with \(\lambda\ne-1\) has matrix, using columns for images,
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\0&e&0\\c&f&a+e\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If its square is zero, its first two diagonal entries give \(a=e=0\). The remaining derivations \(D(x)=cz\), \(D(y)=fz\), \(D(z)=0\) form a two-dimensional vector space, and any two of them have zero composite. This proves the affine-space assertion, tuple count and rank-one property for \(\lambda\ne-1\).

**3. The exterior case.** Take \(\lambda=-1\), let \(W=kx\oplus ky\), write \(M=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&d\\b&e\end{psmallmatrix}\), and let \(\gamma=(c,f)\in W^*\). The derivation acts on \(W\oplus kz\) as
\[
D_{M,\gamma}=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\\gamma&\operatorname{tr}M\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its square vanishes precisely when \(M^2=0\) and \(\gamma M=0\). Indeed, \(M^2=0\) over a field of odd characteristic implies \(\operatorname{tr}M=0\), so the remaining off-diagonal condition is exactly \(\gamma M=0\). Every nonzero \(2\)-by-\(2\) square-zero matrix has rank one and its image equals its kernel, a line \(L\subset W\). Thus \(M\in\operatorname{Hom}(W/L,L)\), and \(\gamma M=0\) is equivalent to \(\gamma\in\operatorname{Ann}(L)\). In particular all nonzero \(D_{M,\gamma}\) have rank one: when \(M\ne0\), both \(M\) and \(\gamma\) factor through the same one-dimensional quotient \(W/L\).

**4. Commutativity is mutual annihilation.** Suppose \(D_{M,\gamma}\) and \(D_{N,\delta}\) are square-zero. When a nonzero \(M\) commutes with \(N\), the matrix \(N\) is a scalar multiple of \(M\): the centralizer of a nonzero nilpotent \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix is \(kI+kM\), and a square-zero member of this centralizer has zero scalar part in odd characteristic. For a commuting tuple containing some nonzero matrix, all \(M_i\) are therefore multiples of one nilpotent \(M\), including zero multiples. The lower-block commutator equation \(\gamma_iM_j=\gamma_jM_i\), combined with \(\gamma_iM_i=0\), forces **every** \(\gamma_i\) to annihilate \(L=\operatorname{im}M\), including indices with \(M_i=0\). It follows that \(M_iM_j=0\) and \(\gamma_iM_j=0\) for every pair, so \(D_iD_j=0\). If all \(M_i=0\), the same conclusion is immediate.

**5. Geometry and maximal spaces.** The tuple locus is the union of \(Y_0\), defined by all \(M_i=0\), and \(Y_1\), whose points admit some line \(L\subset W\) with \(M_i\in\operatorname{Hom}(W/L,L)\) and \(\gamma_i\in\operatorname{Ann}(L)\) for all \(i\). The first is affine \(2m\)-space. The second is the image of the total space of a rank-\(2m\) vector bundle over \(\mathbb P(W)\cong\mathbb P^1\) under its proper incidence projection, hence is closed and geometrically irreducible of dimension \(2m+1\). The dimension follows because the line \(L\) is uniquely recovered whenever any \(M_i\ne0\). Their intersection consists exactly of the \(2\)-by-\(m\) covector matrices of rank at most one; it is irreducible of dimension \(m+1\). For \(m=1\), \(Y_0\subset Y_1\). For \(m\ge2\) neither component contains the other (take two independent covectors in \(Y_0\)), giving precisely the stated two components. Any compatible linear subspace containing an operator with \(M\ne0\) is contained in the two-dimensional space \(\{D_{tM,\gamma}:t\in k,\gamma\in\operatorname{Ann}(L)\}\), while any such space with all \(M=0\) is contained in \(Y_0\) with \(m=1\). All these planes are maximal, proving the final classification.

**6. Arithmetic and ranks.** There are \(Q+1\) rational lines \(L\subset\mathbb F_Q^2\). The case where all \(M_i=0\) gives \(Q^{2m}\) tuples. Otherwise the common line \(L\) is unique, the \(m\) scalars specifying the \(M_i\) have \(Q^m-1\) non-all-zero choices, and the \(\gamma_i\in\operatorname{Ann}(L)\) have \(Q^m\) choices. This yields \(C_m(Q)\). Since nonzero operators have rank one, inclusion-exclusion gives, for a prescribed support of size \(s\), exactly \((Q+2)(Q^2-1)^s-(Q+1)(Q-1)^s\) tuples. Summing over supports proves the rank enumerator. For neighborhood counts: when \(M=0\), \(\gamma\ne0\), the compatible partners comprise all \(Q^2\) operators with zero matrix and the \(Q(Q-1)\) with nonzero matrix whose image is \(\ker\gamma\). When \(M\ne0\), there are \(Q^2\) partners with both matrix and covector along the fixed common line. The multiplicities of these cases are \(Q^2-1\) and \((Q^2-1)Q=Q^3-Q\), respectively.

## Verification

Run `python3 check.py` with a standard Python 3 installation. It reconstructs the associative multiplication on the original basis and exhaustively tests all possible derivation images of the generators over six odd-prime-field/parameter configurations. For \(Q=3,5,7\) at \(\lambda=-1\), it independently tests every commuting ordered pair, confirms the neighborhood distribution and prescribed-rank support counts, and checks that commuting square-zero operators compose to zero. For \(Q=9\), it uses \(\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^2+1)\), enumerates all structurally square-zero candidates, and checks their derivation and square-zero identities against the original multiplication. The extension-field case is not a brute-force sweep of every linear endomorphism; its completeness uses the proved normal form. The complete console output is supplied in `verify_output.txt`.

Results at the exterior parameter include \((Q,C_1,C_2)=(3,33,369),(5,145,4225),(7,385,21217)\). The extension-field case has \(C_1(9)=801\). At \(\lambda\ne-1\), the checks recover \(Q^2\) square-zero derivations and \(Q^4\) commuting ordered pairs. The finite tests are checks of the symbolic proof, not substitutes for its arbitrary-field or arbitrary-arity quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work

The algebras are the four-dimensional quantum complete intersections central to the Hochschild (co)homology work of Bergh (2007) and Bergh–Erdmann (2007). Those papers provide the algebraic and homological setting, not this square-zero derivation-tuple classification. Bergh's 2007 quantum-exterior paper assumes a parameter not a root of unity, whereas \(\lambda=-1\) is a root of unity and every unit of a finite field is one. The 2016 four-dimensional associative-algebra derivation tables of Abdulkareem, Fiidow and Rakhimov provide broader classifications of derivation spaces over the complex field. The 2016 work of Grimley studies the Gerstenhaber structure of Hochschild cohomology in characteristic zero. Britnell and Wildon (2010) treat commuting similarity classes of matrices. These are substantial antecedents, but none of their inspected statements gives the joint finite-field derivation-rank enumerator, the all-arity mutual-annihilation classification, or the two unequal-dimensional components at the exterior resonance. The individual derivation description, nilpotent matrix centralizers, and the fact that nilpotent lines are parametrized by \(\mathbb P^1\) are not separately claimed as new. The possible existence of a stronger, unlocated theorem about mutually compatible square-zero derivations remains a historical-priority risk.

## Limitations

The characteristic-two quantum complete intersections behave differently; they are excluded. The parameter is nonzero. The result concerns ordinary associative derivations, not arbitrary linear endomorphisms, inner-derivation quotient classes, higher nilpotence indices, or higher-generator quantum complete intersections. Geometric claims are restricted to reduced varieties and do not establish scheme-theoretic reducedness or singularity schemes. No independent proof audit or exhaustive historical-priority certification has been performed.
