# Review of the closed-Johnson trace-spectrum finding

**Correctness.** The exact finite claim is backed by the full 21-orbit classification, independent enumeration of all 4,845 four-query supports, and a directly checkable depth-four witness tree. The 20-hypothesis upper bound and the rank-complement argument give the claimed extremality. The verified script reconstructs all data from elementary combinations rather than trusting unverified logs. The argument does not extrapolate to larger Johnson ranks.

**Originality.** The closest primary paper (Adler, Benediktsson and Macpherson, arXiv:2007.16042, especially Theorems 3.6–3.7 and Figure 4) explicitly studies *open* Johnson neighborhoods and notes only general boundedness for closed neighborhoods. Its published open VC result does not imply a closed 4-support trace count or a closed adaptive tree. The general Littlestone closure paper of Ghazi and collaborators does not specify this instance. Targeted searches under closed balls, radius-one Johnson neighborhoods, rank-three instances and alternate relation terminology did not identify the present finite spectrum; absence of a search hit is not definitive priority proof.

**Value.** This is a sharp first-size boundary for a standard distance-transitive family, where adding equality to an adjacency formula changes static shattering but not adaptive shattering. The full four-point trace spectrum strengthens a mere nonshattering check and gives a reproducible benchmark for rank-three association schemes. Its significance does not depend on a claimed all-parameter formula.

**Limitations.** The upper VC assertion is certified by exhaustive finite orbit coverage, not a short conceptual formula. Independent audit and global historical-priority verification have not been performed; a specialized association-scheme treatment might already encode the histogram.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
