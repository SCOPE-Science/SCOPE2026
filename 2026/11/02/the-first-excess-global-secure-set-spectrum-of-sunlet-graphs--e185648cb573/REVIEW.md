The claimed object is the one-leaf corona of each simple cycle of length at least three, under the all-attacks definition of global security. The proof independently establishes domination's pairwise occupancy bound and the leaf-removal reduction, then derives the exact security surplus for arbitrary selected-core attack sets. The necessity argument excludes all internal bounded core-only runs and all interior runs of two or more leaf-only states; the sufficiency argument provides a positive neighbor that cancels the sole possible negative contribution, or uses the doubly selected pair's charge of two. The complete word enumeration is disjoint after identifying the two uniform duplicates. The proof covers the triangle boundary. An independent original-graph exhaustive checker confirms every first-two-layer configuration for cycle lengths three through eight, and checks the reduced criterion through eleven. Finite checks are corroboration rather than proof of all lengths.

The 2026 Sierpiński-graph result uses the same all-subsets security definition but different host graphs and asks a minimum-size question. The 2023 corona-operator results provide bounds on minimum secure domination, not an enumeration of the first-excess layer. Related results on ordinary, super, co-secure, and guard-swap domination are not equivalent to this all-subsets security criterion. Targeted searches for sunlet global secure-set enumeration, the proposed exact coefficient, and equivalent names found no directly covering prior statement. This is a best-knowledge comparison, not a guarantee of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For a natural infinite unicyclic leaf-corona family with a known security minimum, the first-excess stability problem asks whether a single extra selected vertex releases arbitrary local changes or only rigid, countable patterns. The theorem proves a nontrivial global rigidity law under security inequalities for **all** attack subsets, yielding a quadratic rather than exponential first-excess count. The charge identity reduces an apparently exponential family of security checks to a structural boundary argument. The exact first-excess coefficient is a motivated graph-security enumeration, not a routine restatement of the known minimum.
