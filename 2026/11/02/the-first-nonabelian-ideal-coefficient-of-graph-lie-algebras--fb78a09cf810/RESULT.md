# The first nonabelian ideal coefficient of graph Lie algebras

## Finding

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be **any finite simple undirected graph** on \(n\ge2\) vertices, and let \(q\) be **any prime power**. Associate the two-step nilpotent graph Lie algebra \(L_G\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) defined below. The exact number \(I_3(G;q)\) of codimension-three Lie ideals is

\[
I_3(G;q)={n\brack3}_q+\frac{q^{n-2}}{(q^2-1)(q^2-q)}\bigl[Q_{\overline G}(q-1,q+1)-(q+1)q^n+q\bigr].
\]

Here \({n\brack3}_q\) denotes the Gaussian binomial coefficient, interpreted as zero when \(n<3\); \(\overline G\) is the complement graph; and \(Q_H(s,t)=\sum_{S\subseteq V(H)}s^{|S|}t^{c(H[S])}\) is the induced-subgraph component polynomial, with \(c(H[\varnothing])=0\).

Exactly \({n\brack3}_q\) of these ideals have abelian three-dimensional quotient. The remaining ideals have quotient isomorphic to the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra. Thus the bracketed graph-polynomial term counts **all and only** the first nonabelian quotient stratum. No restriction on the characteristic is required.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set is identified with \(\{1,\dots,n\}\); loops and multiple edges are excluded. Let \(X=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n\mathbb F_q x_i\) and \(Z=\bigoplus_{\{i,j\}\in E}\mathbb F_q z_{ij}\), with \(i<j\) indexing the latter basis. Define \(L_G=X\oplus Z\) by \([x_i,x_j]=z_{ij}\) when \(i<j\) is an edge, by skew symmetry for the reverse order, and by zero for nonedges and all brackets involving \(Z\). Over characteristic two, these relations define an alternating Lie bracket, so the same proof applies.

The count includes all linear Lie ideals, not merely ideals generated by graph vertices. For the empty graph, \(Z=0\), and the formula specializes to the abelian Gaussian coefficient. For the two-vertex complete graph it counts one codimension-three ideal.

## Proof

**1. Separate the quotient types.** The quotient of any codimension-three ideal is a three-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra of class at most two. Every such Lie algebra is either abelian or isomorphic to the Heisenberg algebra \(H_3(\mathbb F_q)\), with basis \(u,v,w\) and sole nonzero bracket \([u,v]=w\). Indeed, if its derived algebra is nonzero, the derived algebra is central and one-dimensional: a two-dimensional central subspace would leave an at-most-one-dimensional quotient, forcing the bracket to vanish. The induced alternating form on the two-dimensional quotient is then nondegenerate and has the stated normal form, in any characteristic.

An abelian quotient kills all of \([L_G,L_G]=Z\); consequently its kernel is exactly the preimage of a codimension-three subspace of \(L_G/Z\cong\mathbb F_q^n\). This contributes \({n\brack3}_q\).

**2. Describe surjective maps to \(H_3\).** Write the image of each generator uniquely as \(f(x_i)=a_{i1}u+a_{i2}v+b_iw\), with \(a_i=(a_{i1},a_{i2})\in\mathbb F_q^2\) and \(b_i\in\mathbb F_q\). For an edge \(\{i,j\}\), compatibility forces \(f(z_{ij})=\det(a_i,a_j)w\), so the map is well-defined if and only if

\[
\det(a_i,a_j)=0\qquad\text{for every nonedge }\{i,j\}.
\]

The map is surjective if and only if \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) span \(\mathbb F_q^2\). Indeed, a spanning pair must be joined by an edge (otherwise its determinant is forbidden), so its bracket provides \(w\). The \(n\) central coordinates \(b_i\) are arbitrary and contribute a factor \(q^n\).

**3. Count the spanning vector assignments.** Let \(S=\{i:a_i\ne0\}\). On each connected component of the induced complement graph \(\overline G[S]\), the nonzero vectors \(a_i\) must lie on one common projective line in \(\mathbb F_q^2\), since adjacent vertices in \(\overline G\) correspond precisely to determinant-zero constraints. Conversely, every such assignment satisfies all constraints. There are \(q+1\) possible projective lines and \(q-1\) nonzero vectors on each. For nonempty \(S\), the assignments spanning \(\mathbb F_q^2\) are exactly those in which not all components use the same line. Hence their number is

\[
(q-1)^{|S|}\bigl((q+1)^{c(\overline G[S])}-(q+1)\bigr).
\]

For \(S=\varnothing\) there are no spanning assignments. Summing over all supports and using \(\sum_S(q-1)^{|S|}=q^n\) therefore gives

\[
N_2(G;q)=Q_{\overline G}(q-1,q+1)-(q+1)q^n+q.
\]

The final \(+q\) corrects the empty support, for which the naïve subtraction contributes \(1-(q+1)=-q\).

**4. Pass from epimorphisms to kernels.** Every epimorphism with kernel \(I\) identifies the quotient \(L_G/I\) with \(H_3\), and the possible identifications are a free transitive torsor for \(\operatorname{Aut}(H_3)\). Each automorphism is determined by an arbitrary matrix in \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\), an arbitrary central additive adjustment of each of \(u,v\), and the determinant action on \(w\). Thus

\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(H_3)|=q^2(q^2-1)(q^2-q).
\]

The number of nonabelian-quotient ideals is consequently \(q^nN_2(G;q)/|\operatorname{Aut}(H_3)|=q^{n-2}N_2(G;q)/((q^2-1)(q^2-q))\). Adding the abelian contribution gives the formula. This is an equality of actual integral counts, including the case where the graph-polynomial expression vanishes. \(\square\)

## Verification

The bundled standalone Python program `verify.py` independently constructs the bracket table and enumerates **all** codimension-three subspaces by reduced-row-echelon matrices; a subspace is counted only when closed under brackets with every algebra generator. This direct ideal test does not use epimorphisms, Heisenberg classification, or projective-line coloring. It compares the observed counts with a separate implementation of the induced-component formula.

Run `python3 verify.py`. The reproduced result is `VERIFY_OK cases=21`: all graph edge sets on three vertices at field orders two and three, the two-vertex edge case at both orders, and three distinct four-vertex graph cases at field order two. The exhaustive checks include the empty, path, and complete three-vertex graphs; they constitute finite corroboration, not a substitute for the unrestricted proof.

As numerical examples, for three vertices over \(\mathbb F_2\), empty, one-edge, path, and complete graphs give respectively \(1,3,7,15\) codimension-three ideals. For a four-cycle over \(\mathbb F_2\), the formula gives \(51\), of which \(36\) have Heisenberg quotient.

## Relationship to prior work

Godoy Molina and Lagos (2023) construct labeled graph Lie algebras and identify certain ideals associated with induced subgraphs (their Proposition 3.4); the criterion is not an enumeration of every codimension-three ideal. Lee (2020) gives a general finite-field ideal-zeta computation framework with explicit examples, but does not state this all-graph component-polynomial coefficient. The distinct-edge star family has separately accessible complete ideal counts, so the star specialization is **not** claimed as a new independent result; the assertion here concerns arbitrary simple graphs and the global component-polynomial mechanism.

Tittmann, Averbouch, and Makowsky (2008) introduced the induced-subgraph component polynomial and its recurrences. Its graph-theoretic meaning is prior work; the relation to codimension-three graph Lie ideals is the asserted new connection. The graph-Lie construction itself predates the 2023 paper.

## Limitations

The formula counts only codimension-three ideals, not all ideal dimensions or subalgebra counts. It applies to the distinct-edge simple-graph construction, not arbitrary edge-labeled Lie algebras with repeated central labels. Existing general algorithms and specialized star cases remain prior work. A concealed equivalent formula in broader graph-group normal-subgroup counts or ideal-zeta literature has not been ruled out globally. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
