# The first rank-one Newton stratum in a dihedral genus-three pencil

## Finding
Let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_{13}\), and for each \(a\in k\) with \(a^2\ne4\) let \(C_a\) be the smooth projective model of
\[
C_a:\qquad y^2=x(x^6+a x^3+1).
\]
Its Jacobian has a complete characteristic-thirteen Newton and \(a\)-number stratification:

| Parameter | Geometric \(13\)-rank | \(a\)-number | Newton slopes (with multiplicities) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(a=6\) or \(a=-6\) | 1 | 2 | \(0,\frac12,\frac12,\frac12,\frac12,1\) |
| \(a^6+4a^4-a^2-6=0\) | 2 | 1 | \(0,0,\frac12,\frac12,1,1\) |
| Every other smooth parameter | 3 | 0 | \(0,0,0,1,1,1\) |

There are precisely two distinct geometric marked parameters of rank one, both already in \(\mathbb F_{13}\), and six distinct geometric parameters of rank two, all defined over \(\mathbb F_{169}\) but none over \(\mathbb F_{13}\). No smooth member has rank zero. Among the eleven smooth marked coefficients \(a\in\mathbb F_{13}\), the rank distribution is \((N_1,N_2,N_3)=(2,0,9)\); over \(\mathbb F_{169}\), it is \((2,6,159)\). Counts are for the displayed **marked coefficient family**, not for isomorphism classes of curves.

Within the same integral pencil at the tame odd primes \(5,7,11,13\), characteristic \(13\) is the first in which a smooth rank-one member occurs. The two characteristic-thirteen rank-one coefficients lie over the special parameter magnitudes \(6\) appearing in the classical uniformization study of the pencil; no characteristic-zero isomorphism statement is asserted about their reductions.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is algebraically closed of characteristic \(13\) for the main classification, and the projective curve is the smooth completion of the stated affine equation. The polynomial has degree \(7\). For \(13\ne2,3\), its seven roots are simple precisely when \(a^2\ne4\): the root at zero is simple and the remaining roots correspond through \(u=x^3\) to the distinct roots of \(u^2+a u+1\). Thus \(C_a\) has genus \(3\). The \(p\)-rank is the dimension over \(\mathbb F_p\) of geometric \(p\)-torsion; the \(a\)-number is \(\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha_p,J(C_a)[p])\). We use the covariant Frobenius/Hasse--Witt matrix convention \(M_{ij}=[x^{pi-j}]f(x)^{(p-1)/2}\), indexed by \(1\le i,j\le3\).

## Proof
**1. Diagonal Hasse--Witt matrix.** Write \(f(x)=x^7+a x^4+x=x(x^6+a x^3+1)\), and put \(u=x^3\). Since
\[
f(x)^6=x^6(u^2+a u+1)^6,
\]
only powers divisible by \(3\) appear. With \(p=13\), the exponents \(13i-j\) are divisible by \(3\) exactly when \(i=j\) for \(1\le i,j\le3\). Hence the genus-three matrix is diagonal. Its first and third entries are the coefficient of \(u^2\) (respectively \(u^{10}\), equal by reciprocity), while its middle entry is the coefficient of \(u^6\). Exact binomial extraction modulo \(13\) gives
\[
M(a)=\operatorname{diag}(B(a),A(a),B(a)),\qquad
B(a)=2a^2+6,\qquad A(a)=a^6+4a^4-a^2-6.
\]
Since this is a diagonal Frobenius-semilinear operator, the stable Frobenius rank is exactly the number of nonzero diagonal entries; its rank is the same number. Therefore the geometric \(13\)-rank equals this count and the \(a\)-number equals three minus this count.

**2. The exceptional parameters.** In \(\mathbb F_{13}[a]\),
\[
B(a)=2(a-6)(a+6),\qquad
A(a)=(a^2+5)(a^2+6a-2)(a^2-6a-2).
\]
The polynomials are squarefree, coprime to each other, and coprime to \(a^2-4\). The quadratic factors of \(A\) are irreducible over \(\mathbb F_{13}\): their discriminants are \(6\), \(5\), and \(5\), all nonsquares in \(\mathbb F_{13}\). Thus \(B\) has exactly the two rational zeros \(\pm6\), whereas \(A\) has exactly six degree-two geometric roots. There is no common zero, excluding rank zero. The rank and \(a\)-number classifications and the finite-field marked-parameter counts follow immediately.

**3. Newton polygons.** The pencil has a dihedral symmetry generated by \(\sigma:(x,y)\mapsto(\zeta x,\zeta^2 y)\), for \(\zeta^3=1\), and \(\tau:(x,y)\mapsto(1/x,y/x^4)\). Both actions are tame in characteristic \(13\). The quotient by \(\langle\sigma\rangle\) is the elliptic curve
\[
E_0:\quad V^2=u^3+a u^2+u,
\qquad u=x^3,\quad V=xy,
\]
and the quotient by \(\langle\tau\rangle\) is
\[
E_1:\quad v^2=t^3-3t+a,
\qquad t=x+x^{-1},\quad v=y/x^2.
\]
The conjugate involution supplies the second copy of \(E_1\). The pullbacks of invariant differentials span \(H^0(C_a,\Omega^1)\): they are proportional to \(x\,dx/y\), \((x^2-1)dx/y\), and its nonproportional \(\sigma\)-translate. More concretely, let \(\Phi:E_0\times E_1\times E_1\to J(C_a)\) be the sum of the three pullback maps, and let \(\Phi^\vee\) be the product of norm maps. The endomorphism \(\Phi\Phi^\vee\) equals \((1+\sigma+\sigma^2)+(1+\tau)+(1+\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1})\) in the dihedral group ring. On the two-dimensional \(\sigma\)-invariant part of \(H^1\), this acts by \(3\); on each of the two copies of the standard dihedral representation, \(\sigma+\sigma^2=-1\) and \((\tau+\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1})^2=1\), so its eigenvalues are \(1,3\). Thus the determinant on \(H^1\) is \(3^4=81\). Since the norm map is dual to pullback, \(\deg(\Phi)^2=81\), hence \(\deg(\Phi)=9\), prime to \(13\). In particular, this supplies the isogeny \(J(C_a)\sim E_0\times E_1^2\) required below. Its Newton polygon is the union of those of the elliptic factors. The elliptic Hasse invariants, coefficients of the relevant degree-twelve monomials in the sixth powers of their cubic right-hand sides, are exactly \(A(a)\) for \(E_0\) and \(B(a)\) for \(E_1\). An ordinary elliptic factor contributes slopes \(0,1\); a supersingular one contributes \(1/2,1/2\). This proves the three displayed Newton polygons. The separate diagonal genus-three matrix above establishes the \(a\)-numbers without relying on the isogeny.

**4. First occurrence among tame odd primes.** At primes \(5,7,11\), the elliptic Hasse coefficient pairs \((A_p,B_p)\), in the same quotient order, are respectively
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
p&A_p&B_p\\\hline
5&a^2+2&-1\\
7&a^3-a&3a\\
11&a^5-2a^3-3a&-5a
\end{array}.
\]
The condition for rank one would be \(B_p=0\) and \(A_p\ne0\), because the elliptic square contributes either zero or two to the rank. At \(5\), \(B_p\) has no zero; at \(7\) and \(11\), its only zero is \(a=0\), which is also a zero of \(A_p\). Consequently no smooth rank-one member occurs at \(p=5,7,11\), while the two smooth parameters \(a=\pm6\) realize rank one at \(p=13\). This comparison concerns only tame odd primes and this specified marked pencil.

## Relationship to prior work
Fuertes--Streit, *Genus 3 normal coverings of the Riemann sphere branched over 4 points*, Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 22 (2006), Section 6, establishes the complex dihedral pencil and studies its automorphisms and distinguished Belyi parameters. Gutierrez--Sevilla--Shaska, *Hyperelliptic curves of genus 3 with prescribed automorphism group* (arXiv:1209.2938, Section 3.1, Table 1), describes the same classical family and its moduli. Achter--Pries, *The \(p\)-rank strata of the moduli space of hyperelliptic curves* (arXiv:0902.4637), studies global hyperelliptic \(p\)-rank strata, rather than the characteristic-thirteen marked pencil. The genus-three equation, its classical dihedral automorphisms, and general modular \(p\)-rank theory are prior work and are not claimed as discoveries here. The specific new statement assessed here is the full characteristic-thirteen rank/\(a\)-number/Newton classification together with the first-occurrence comparison for smaller tame odd primes. Published calculations for this marked family could exist under other parameterizations; no exhaustive priority claim is made.

## Limitations
The result gives geometric strata for the displayed parameter \(a\), not the number of geometric isomorphism classes or a family-universal formula for arbitrary primes. It does not classify the automorphism groups in exceptional characteristic or assert that complex Belyi isomorphisms specialize faithfully. Only primes \(5,7,11,13\) are included in the minimal-prime comparison. The classification is not asserted for the closure of the smooth parameter space.
