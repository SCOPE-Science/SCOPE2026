# The first six Schenker residues and their six Hensel branches

## Finding

For each positive integer \(n\), define the Schenker sum
\[
 A_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{j!}n^j.
\]
For a prime \(p\), let
\[
 \lambda(p)=\#\{r\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}:p\mid A_r\}.
\]
The **least prime** for which \(\lambda(p)\ge 6\) is exactly \(2339\). In fact,
\[
 \lambda(2339)=6,\qquad
 \{r:1\le r<2339,\ A_r\equiv0\pmod{2339}\}
 =\{156,470,520,589,1092,2170\}.
\]
All six congruence zeros are nondegenerate in the Schenker-sum lifting criterion. Consequently, for **every** integer \(k\ge1\), the congruence \(A_n\equiv0\pmod{2339^k}\), restricted to \(2339\nmid n\), has exactly six solution classes modulo \(2339^k\). Each is a unique lift of one of the displayed residues. The complete six classes modulo \(2339^2\) are
\[
 \{3595199,1015596,3195594,2479929,2054734,2820665\}.
\]
This answers the \(m=6\) existence instance of Miska's 2014 Question 4 on Schenker-prime residue multiplicities, and identifies its least prime witness.

## Assumptions and scope

All indices \(n\) are positive integers. The prime \(p\) varies over **all** rational primes when discussing minimality. The single even prime satisfies \(\lambda(2)=1\), since \(A_1=2\). A nonzero class modulo a prime power means a class not divisible by its underlying prime. All sums, modular operations, and comparison bounds are exact integer arithmetic. The exhaustive primality check uses the elementary sieve of Eratosthenes on \(2\le p\le2339\).

## Proof

**1. Exact finite reduction.** For a prime \(p\) and \(1\le r<p\), the factorial \(r!\) is invertible modulo \(p\). Hence
\[
 p\mid A_r\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{j=0}^{r}r^j(j!)^{-1}\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
The complete list of primes up to \(2339\) is generated by marking composites divisible by each integer \(d\) with \(2\le d\le\lfloor\sqrt{2339}\rfloor\). At each odd prime \(p\) the verifier tests **every** \(1\le r<p\) by the recurrence
\[
 t_0=1,\qquad t_j\equiv t_{j-1}rj^{-1}\pmod p,\qquad
 E_p(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}t_j\pmod p.
\]
All inverses are computed by the valid prime-modulus recursion
\[
 \operatorname{inv}(1)=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{inv}(j)\equiv-\left\lfloor\frac pj\right\rfloor
 \operatorname{inv}(p\bmod j)\pmod p\quad(2\le j<p).
\]
The code independently recomputes \(A_r\bmod p\) without any inverses, by Horner evaluation using \(c_r=1\), \(c_{j-1}\equiv j c_j\pmod p\), and
\[
 A_r\equiv c_0+c_1r+\cdots+c_rr^r\pmod p,
 \qquad c_j=\frac{r!}{j!}.
\]
These algorithms test different polynomial representations of the *same* integer and compare full sets of zero residues for each prime, not merely their cardinalities.

**2. Exhaustive certificate.** There are exactly \(345\) odd primes not exceeding \(2339\). The attached `schenker_census.csv` lists each prime and its **complete** zero-residue set; the attached `verify_schenker.cpp` generates that table from first principles and independently checks the result at every prime. Among the \(345\) primes, the numbers with respective values \(\lambda=0,1,2,3,4,5,6\) are
\[
 (121,139,65,14,4,1,1).
\]
The unique prime in the \(\lambda=6\) class is \(2339\). For every smaller odd prime, \(\lambda\le5\). Together with \(\lambda(2)=1\), this proves the claimed minimality and the complete six-root list. The two exact evaluation methods each perform \(280932418\) modular summand evaluations across \(369924\) candidate indices; this is a finite exhaustive computation with explicitly bounded loops, not an inference from a sample.

**3. Nondegenerate lifting.** For any of the six roots \(r\), define the integer
\[
 Q_{r,p}=A_{r+p}-A_r(r+p)^{r+2}r^{p-r-2},\qquad p=2339.
\]
The exponents are nonnegative for all six \(r\). Direct Horner evaluation modulo \(p^2\) gives the following exact residues; the last column is the unique lifted index modulo \(p^2\):

| \(r\) | \(Q_{r,p}/p\pmod p\) | lift digit \(t\) | \(r+tp\) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 156 | 843 | 1537 | 3595199 |
| 470 | 1662 | 434 | 1015596 |
| 520 | 1732 | 1366 | 3195594 |
| 589 | 24 | 1060 | 2479929 |
| 1092 | 685 | 878 | 2054734 |
| 2170 | 1079 | 1205 | 2820665 |

The computed numerators \(Q_{r,p}\) are all divisible by \(p\), but none is divisible by \(p^2\). Every displayed lifted index was also checked directly to satisfy \(p^2\mid A_{r+tp}\). Miska's 2014 Theorem 1 proves that a nonzero root \(r\) for which \(Q_{r,p}\not\equiv0\pmod{p^2}\) lifts uniquely to a root class modulo each \(p^k\). There are precisely six roots at level one, so every higher-level root reduces to one of them; consequently no further classes exist. This proves exactly six classes at every depth.

## Relationship to prior work

Amdeberhan, Callan and Moll (2013) introduced and studied the valuation of the Schenker sums and their Schenker primes. OEIS A219546 gives the binary property of being a Schenker prime, not the exact count of distinct root classes. Miska (2014), Section 4, defined \(\lambda(p)\), tabulated multiplicities for the first 200 primes, gave the conditional unique-lifting theorem, and explicitly asked in Question 4 whether primes exist with \(\lambda(p)\ge m\) for arbitrary \(m\). His table reaches \(\lambda=5\) at \(p=593\) but does not contain \(p=2339\). The present result settles the next natural instance \(m=6\) with its *first* witness, complete root set and six all-depth branches. Miska (2017) studies generalized \(h\)-Schenker sequences and reports a six-root example for \(h(X)=X^2-1\), which is a different sequence and does not imply the \(h(X)=X\) classification proved here. The 2017 numerical tables concern the three different specializations \(h=-X-1,X^2+1,X^2-1\), not this minimality question.

## Limitations

Minimality is established through an exhaustive, independently repeated finite calculation for primes through \(2339\), with source code and a complete table as the certificate. No bound on \(\lambda(p)\) for primes larger than \(2339\) is asserted, and the argument does not resolve Miska's question for \(m\ge7\) or its infinitude variant. The all-depth conclusion invokes Miska's published conditional lifting theorem after independently checking its six numerical hypotheses. Equivalent prior coverage in unpublished or unindexed computations remains possible.
