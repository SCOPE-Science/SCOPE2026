# Review: five \(2\)-rank-drop divisors of type \((3,4)\)

## Correctness — PASS
The cover is tame of degree \(81\), genus \(55\), and its character-factor genera add to \(55\) with counts \(10,10,15,5\) at branch supports \(2,3,4,5\). The prime-to-characteristic character idempotents preserve \(2\)-torsion invariants. The genus-two ordinary factors are deduced from the existing complete type \((3,3)\) result, not assumed generically. A direct Cartier calculation for every genus-three cyclic quotient gives constant \(a=1\) and stable ranks \(0\) or \(2\), and the five branch-coordinate changes give the stated divisor equations. Pairwise eliminations show that all intersections occur at two points; Jacobian derivatives show the five distinct tangent directions. The replayable verification script confirms the finite algebraic identities. No finite sample is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with scope limitations
The papers on automorphisms (arXiv:1409.3063) and Jacobian decomposition (arXiv:1507.02903) establish the objects and factor framework. Hidalgo (arXiv:1710.01349) gives a general characteristic-two Cartier method, expressly a lower bound not sharp for \(n=4\), and computes type \((3,3)\) invariants. The complete marked type \((3,4)\) locus-by-locus \(2\)-rank classification, constant \(a\)-number and two fivefold crossings were not found in the inspected full texts or searched mathematical record summaries. A sufficiently worked-out earlier equivalent consequence of the general Cartier formula would cover priority, so that risk is acknowledged.

## Value — PASS
The result answers a natural family-wide arithmetic geometry question: determine \(2\)-rank and \(a\)-number of all members of the first five-branch generalized Fermat family with a two-dimensional parameter space. Its five smooth jump curves and exceptional rank-\(30\) intersections provide a geometric description beyond a generic invariant, and the full finite-field tangent pencil is a nontrivial structural feature. It does not claim a general theorem for all curves.
