# The four-size conjugacy spectrum of free class-three algebra groups

## Finding

For every finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\) and integer \(d\ge2\), consider the truncated tensor algebra's positive-degree ideal
\[
J=V\oplus V^{\otimes2}\oplus V^{\otimes3},\qquad V=k^d,
\]
with concatenation and all tensors of degree four or more set to zero. Its full algebra group is \(G=1+J\), with multiplication \((1+u)(1+v)=1+u+v+uv\). It has \(|G|=q^{d+d^2+d^3}\) and group nilpotency class exactly three.

The sizes of the conjugacy classes, the numbers of elements in those classes, and the numbers of distinct classes are exactly as follows (the rows are disjoint):

| Condition on \(u=a+b+c\) | Class size | Number of elements | Number of classes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(a=b=0\) | \(1\) | \(q^{d^3}\) | \(q^{d^3}\) |
| \(a=0\), \(b\) a nonzero pure square | \(q^{d-1}\) | \((q^d-1)q^{d^3}\) | \((q^d-1)q^{d^3-d+1}\) |
| \(a=0\), \(b\ne0\) not a pure square | \(q^d\) | \((q^{d^2}-q^d)q^{d^3}\) | \((q^{d^2}-q^d)q^{d^3-d}\) |
| \(a\ne0\) | \(q^{d^2+d-2}\) | \((q^d-1)q^{d^2+d^3}\) | \((q^d-1)q^{d^3-d+2}\) |

Here a nonzero *pure square* means an element \(\lambda v\otimes v\) with \(\lambda\in k^\times\), \(0\ne v\in V\). In the third row the condition explicitly includes \(b\ne0\): it consists of the degree-two tensors other than zero and nonzero pure squares. The class sizes are pairwise distinct for \(d\ge2\).

In particular the class number and commuting probability are
\[
k(G)=q^{d^3-d}\left(q^{d^2}+(q+q^2)(q^d-1)\right),
\qquad
\Pr_{g,h\in G}(gh=hg)=q^{-d^2-2d}\left(q^{d^2}+(q+q^2)(q^d-1)\right).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

\(k\) is any finite field; no odd-characteristic assumption is made. \(d\ge2\), and tensors have the ordinary noncommutative free word basis. All elements of the entire group \(1+J\) are counted, not only elements of the subgroup generated by \(1+e_i\). Conjugacy means ordinary inner conjugacy in \(G\); different elements within one class are not counted as separate classes.

## Proof

**Step 1: centralizers are linear commutator kernels.** For \(u,v\in J\), \((1+u)(1+v)=(1+v)(1+u)\) is equivalent to \(uv=vu\). Thus
\[
C_G(1+u)=1+\ker(\operatorname{ad}_u),
\qquad |(1+u)^G|=q^{\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{ad}_u},
\]
where \(\operatorname{ad}_u(v)=uv-vu\) is a linear endomorphism of \(J\). Write \(u=a+b+c\) and \(v=x+y+z\) by homogeneous degrees one, two and three. Since degree-four terms vanish,
\[
[u,v]_{(2)}=a\otimes x-x\otimes a,
\qquad
[u,v]_{(3)}=a\otimes y-y\otimes a+b\otimes x-x\otimes b.
\]
The top-degree coordinates \(c,z\) never affect the commutator.

**Step 2: two elementary free-word centralizer lemmas.** For each nonzero \(a\in V\),
\[
\ker\bigl(V\to V^{\otimes2},\ x\mapsto a\otimes x-x\otimes a\bigr)=ka,
\]
and
\[
\ker\bigl(V^{\otimes2}\to V^{\otimes3},\ y\mapsto a\otimes y-y\otimes a\bigr)=k(a\otimes a).
\]
Choose a basis having \(a=e_1\). Comparing coefficients of words whose initial letter differs from \(e_1\) eliminates all words in \(y\) not beginning in \(e_1\); comparing words \(e_1e_j e_1\) with \(e_1e_1 e_j\) eliminates the remaining \(e_1e_j\) for \(j\ne1\). The degree-one statement is the same comparison on two-letter words. This is a coefficient argument valid over *every* field.

A further lemma says that for nonzero \(b\in V^{\otimes2}\), the space
\[
K_b=\{x\in V:b\otimes x=x\otimes b\}
\]
is either zero or one-dimensional, and is nonzero precisely when \(b=\lambda x\otimes x\) for some \(\lambda\in k^\times\), \(x\ne0\). Indeed a nonzero \(x\in K_b\) can be taken as \(e_1\), and the same word comparisons force \(b\in k(e_1\otimes e_1)\). Conversely every such pure square commutes with its defining vector. Distinct independent \(x\)'s cannot both commute with nonzero \(b\), by the preceding argument.

**Step 3: compute every possible rank.** If \(a=b=0\), the commutator vanishes, giving rank zero. If \(a=0\), \(b\ne0\), then the sole condition is \(x\in K_b\), while \(y\) and \(z\) are free. Thus the rank is \(d-1\) for a nonzero pure square and \(d\) otherwise.

If \(a\ne0\), the quadratic condition forces \(x=\lambda a\). The cubic condition becomes \([a,y-\lambda b]=0\), hence \(y=\lambda b+\mu(a\otimes a)\). There are exactly two free scalar parameters \(\lambda,\mu\), as well as \(d^3\) free top-degree coordinates \(z\). Therefore
\[
\dim_k\ker\operatorname{ad}_u=d^3+2,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{ad}_u=d+d^2-2.
\]

**Step 4: count pure squares without double counting.** Each one-dimensional subspace \(L\le V\) contributes exactly \(q-1\) nonzero multiples of \(v\otimes v\), independent of the chosen nonzero \(v\in L\). Tensors associated to distinct lines cannot coincide, since a nonzero pure square determines its underlying line. Therefore the count is \((q^d-1)/(q-1)\cdot(q-1)=q^d-1\). There are \(q^{d^2}-q^d\) remaining non-pure-square degree-two tensors. Multiplying each case by the freely chosen coefficients in degree three and, when \(a\ne0\), degree two, gives the element-count column. Dividing by the respective class size gives the class-count column and its stated sum.

**Step 5: exact group nilpotency class.** Since \(J^4=0\), every iterated group commutator of length four vanishes, so the group is nilpotent of class at most three. For two independent degree-one generators \(x,y\), the leading term of the third-level commutator of \(1+x\), \(1+x\), \(1+y\) is
\[
[x,[x,y]]=x^2y-2xyx+yx^2\ne0.
\]
The coefficients of distinct words \(x^2y\) and \(yx^2\) are both one, including in characteristic two, proving class at least three.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` enumerates all choices of degree-one and degree-two coefficients, constructs the original concatenation commutator matrix in the full word basis, and computes Gaussian ranks over five finite fields and generator counts, including the genuine four-element extension field. The degree-three coefficients are omitted from the enumeration only because Step 1 proves them central; all counts are multiplied by their exact number \(q^{d^3}\). The exhaustive tests for these five specified configurations pass and show the predicted four ranks (for \(d=2\)) and four ranks (for \(d=3\)). They are checks of finite examples, not substitutes for the proof for all prime powers or all \(d\). Run `python3 verify.py` without external packages and compare to `VERIFICATION_OUTPUT.txt`.

For \(d=2,q=2\), the group has \(16{,}384\) elements, of class sizes \(1,2,4,16\), partitioned into \(256,384,768,768\) conjugacy classes respectively. Hence the total is \(2{,}176\) classes.

## Relationship to prior work

General theory of algebra groups and their superclasses is established by Diaconis and Isaacs (2008). Soffer (2014) and Pak–Soffer (2015) study class-number bounds and counting methods for unitriangular or pattern groups. Their matrices are constrained by upper-triangular index patterns, whereas the present object has a freely generated word-tensor algebra and a different multiplication tensor. The present exact four-stratum free-algebra-group formula is not a stated result of the inspected source sections, nor an evident specialization of their group families.

Hansmann (2019, based on a 2015 thesis) directly studies the circle group of the free nilpotent associative algebra, and therefore is an important same-object comparison. Its theorem classifies the **subgroup generated by the free generators** in terms of relatively free groups and basic commutator representations. It does not provide the size distribution of centralizers or conjugacy classes in the entire \(1+J\). The subgroup it studies need not equal the full algebra group. The exact spectrum here concerns the latter and is not implied by that subgroup-structure statement.

A search of indexed mathematical claims and accessible earlier computations also found adjacent results about centralizer ranks in matrix algebras and ideal structures of shorter truncated free algebras, without an implication covering the present full algebra-group conjugacy census. These are best-knowledge originality comparisons, not proof of publication priority.

## Limitations

The count concerns the full finite-field algebra group at truncation degree three and at least two generators. It does not classify individual conjugacy orbits by representatives, describe character degrees or supercharacters, or extend to higher truncation degree and general non-free quotients. The original group may share its class-count formula with unrelated groups. Direct exhaustive computations were limited to the listed small finite fields. Earlier unindexed literature, including extended versions of free nilpotent algebra-group work, might contain overlapping results; independent verification of historical publication priority is outstanding.
