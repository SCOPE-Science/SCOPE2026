# The full carrier spectrum of a six-element nondistributive lattice

## Finding

Let \(H\) denote the six-element lattice with elements \(0,a,b,c,d,1\), whose nontrivial strict order relations among the four interior elements are exactly \(a<d\) and \(b<d\); the element \(c\) is incomparable with \(a,b,d\). This is the lattice \(L_{6,1}^{d}\) in the six-element classification of Dormán, Kátai-Urbán, Horváth and Waldhauser.

For each positive integer \(m\), let \(\operatorname{spec}_2(H)\) consist of those \(m\) for which some unary algebra on an \(m\)-point carrier, all of whose basic operations have image of cardinality **exactly two**, has congruence lattice isomorphic to \(H\). Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{spec}_2(H)=\{m\in\mathbb Z:m\ge4\}.}
\]

A general interval-union realization lemma supplies the construction: for any finite set \(A\) of at least two elements and arbitrary equivalence relations \(\delta,\gamma\) on \(A\), there is a family of two-valued unary operations whose full congruence lattice is exactly

\[
[0_A,\delta]_{\operatorname{Eq}(A)}\;\cup\;[\gamma,1_A]_{\operatorname{Eq}(A)}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

An equivalence relation is ordered by containment of related pairs. An operation is two-valued when its image consists of precisely two distinct points. An algebra may have any finite number of basic unary operations, including zero when permitted, although the construction below supplies an explicit finite nonempty family.

For every integer \(m\ge4\), let \(A=\{0,1,2,\ldots,m-1\}\). Define \(\delta\) by the two nonsingleton blocks \(\{0,1\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\), with every remaining point singleton. Define \(\gamma\) by the two blocks

\[
U=\{0,2,4,5,\ldots,m-1\},\qquad V=\{1,3\}.
\]

The ellipsis denotes the empty tail if \(m=4\). Let \(F\) be **all** surjective maps from \(A\) onto a two-element subset of either \(U\) or \(V\) which are constant on each \(\delta\)-equivalence class. Every operation in \(F\) is two-valued. The number of distinct operations is

\[
|F|=\left(\binom{m-2}{2}+1\right)\left(2^{m-2}-2\right).
\]

## Proof

**Interval-union lemma.** Let \(A\) be finite with at least two points; take any \(\delta,\gamma\in\operatorname{Eq}(A)\). Let \(F_{\delta,\gamma}\) consist of all surjective two-valued maps \(f:A\to\{p,q\}\), where \(p\ne q\) lie in the same \(\gamma\)-class, and \(\delta\subseteq\ker f\). (If none exist, take the empty operation family.) Then

\[
\operatorname{Con}(A;F_{\delta,\gamma})=[0_A,\delta]\cup[\gamma,1_A].
\]

Indeed, if \(\theta\subseteq\delta\), every \(f\in F_{\delta,\gamma}\) is constant on every \(\theta\)-class; hence \(\theta\) is a congruence. If \(\gamma\subseteq\theta\), every \(f\) has its entire image in one \(\theta\)-class, so again \(\theta\) is a congruence. Conversely, suppose \(\theta\not\subseteq\delta\) and \(\gamma\not\subseteq\theta\). Select \(x,y\in A\) with \((x,y)\in\theta\setminus\delta\), and select \(p,q\in A\) with \((p,q)\in\gamma\setminus\theta\). Define \(f\) to be constant on each \(\delta\)-class, mapping the class of \(x\) to \(p\), the distinct class of \(y\) to \(q\), and all other classes arbitrarily to \(p\) or \(q\). Then \(f\in F_{\delta,\gamma}\) and \(f\) fails to preserve \(\theta\). This proves the equality. The proof also covers \(\delta=0_A\), \(\gamma=1_A\), and empty families.

**The chosen lattice.** For the specified \(\delta\), the lower interval \([0_A,\delta]\) has exactly four elements: equality, the relation \(a\) that merges \(0,1\), the relation \(b\) that merges \(2,3\), and \(d=\delta\). For the specified \(\gamma\), since it has two blocks, \([\gamma,1_A]\) has exactly two elements, \(c=\gamma\) and universal equivalence. The two intervals are disjoint: \(\gamma\not\subseteq\delta\). Moreover \(\gamma\cap\delta=0_A\) and the equivalence relation generated by \(\gamma\cup\delta\) is universal; the first assertion follows because neither two-point block of \(\delta\) is contained in either \(\gamma\)-class, and the second follows because each of these two-point blocks connects the two \(\gamma\)-classes. Thus the only strict comparabilities among \(a,b,c,d\) are \(a<d\) and \(b<d\), so the resulting six-element congruence lattice is exactly \(H\). It is nondistributive: \(a\vee(b\wedge c)=a\) whereas \((a\vee b)\wedge(a\vee c)=d\ne a\).

**Optimality.** The chain \(0<a<d<1\) in \(H\) has three proper inclusions. The entire partition lattice of an \(m\)-point set has height \(m-1\), and every congruence lattice is one of its sublattices. Therefore \(m-1\ge3\), i.e. \(m\ge4\). The explicit construction realizes all these sizes. Combining the two directions proves the finding.

## Verification

Run the included Python 3 program `verify_interval_union.py` with no nonstandard dependencies. For carrier sizes \(m=4,5,6,7\), it enumerates **every** operation in the stated family, all equivalence relations, and checks their preservation directly. Independently, for \(m=4,5,6,7,8\), it computes the complete Galois closure using the relation-kernel criterion for two-valued maps, without enumerating the same operation list. Each computation yields precisely six congruences, with exact agreement to the claimed partitions. The program prints `VERIFY_OK`. Finite checking corroborates but does not establish the quantified statement for arbitrary \(m\); the proof above does.

## Relationship to prior work

Dormán, Kátai-Urbán, Horváth and Waldhauser (2026, arXiv:2610.33305v1) list a minimal four-point two-valued representation of \(L_{6,1}^{d}\) in Appendix B.4 (item 6/2), but do not give its entire carrier-size spectrum. Their Problem 40 asks for spectra of all two-representable lattices. Their Proposition 42 supplies spectra of **distributive** lattices, whereas \(H\) is nondistributive; Proposition 41 treats partition lattices, and their ordinal-sum closure theorem does not directly apply because \(H\) has no proper element comparable with all other elements. Their general characterization of two-valued congruence lattices (Lemma 11/Theorem 12), drawing on Bandelt's earlier results, is consistent with the interval-union proof, but does not by itself specify the above parameterized carrier embeddings.

The general interval-union device is used here as a direct construction, not asserted to be historically new independently of those earlier characterizations. The claim of interest is the exact spectrum of \(H\).

## Limitations

This result pertains to one six-element nondistributive lattice, not all six-element lattices. The construction does not minimize the number of two-valued unary operations for \(m>4\), and it does not settle spectra of arbitrary nondistributive lattices. The originality assessment is based on accessible literature, not a guarantee of absolute historical priority.
