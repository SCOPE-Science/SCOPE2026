# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The full claim is reconstructed from the disjoint group-of-units/constant-ideal decomposition. A mixed unit/constant class forces universality. The constant classes give a unique field-linear subspace \(W\); a normal subgroup \(N\) of the scalar-affine unit group describes unit classes. Right multiplication by constants forces \(N\) to act trivially on \(V/W\). When \(W\ne V\), this makes \(N=T\) a translation subspace contained in \(W\). When \(W=V\), the only other possible normal subgroups are \(V\rtimes H\) with \(H\le\mathbb F_q^\times\); the commutator argument proves completeness. The explicit partitions are checked against arbitrary left and right products. Counting, order, height, and least-commutative quotient are derived without an unproved classification lemma. Exhaustive independent partition checks cover the smallest monoids and direct constructed-congruence checks cover larger prime and extension fields.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest primary literature studies (i) least commutative congruences, including those of *full* transformation monoids; (ii) the quiver of the complex algebra of the ordinary one-dimensional affine monoid; and (iii) algorithms for general finite semigroup congruences. None of the inspected claims states or implies an all-field/all-rank lattice description in nested subspaces, the Gaussian-binomial count, or the characteristic-two boundary. A generic algorithm does not itself yield a closed formula, and an unrestricted full-transformation congruence result does not classify congruences of a submonoid. A specialized old theorem about permutation groups with an adjoined constant ideal could subsume the structural part; absolute priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete natural semigroup-congruence classification, not an arbitrary single-instance census. It provides closed formulas for the number and height of a lattice in every rank and finite field, and identifies a qualitative change in the maximal commutative quotient at field size two. The dual subspace/subgroup description isolates the group-action mechanism and offers exact examples for finite congruence algorithms.

## Scope and residual risks

The monoid has only *scalar* linear parts; the theorem neither extends to arbitrary affine transformation monoids nor to arbitrary finite rings. Further historical searching could locate a stronger group-with-constants congruence classification. The verification programs cover representative small cases and do not certify infinite parameter ranges independently of the proof.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
