# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The vertex model, adjacency set, all \(200\) nonempty independent faces, the acyclic \(88\)-pair Morse matching and its \(496\) incidences are regenerated by the standalone program. One critical vertex and \(23\) critical two-cells prove that reduced rational homology is solely a \(23\)-dimensional second group. A direct stabilizer upper bound and twelve independently enumerated invertible neighbor-preserving matrices establish all \(192\) automorphisms. Signed fixed-face Lefschetz sums verify the full character distribution; exact fourth-root-of-unity Fourier inversion verifies the translation decomposition, and character averages give quotient Betti dimensions. The computation is finite and exhaustive; no extrapolation to larger graphs is used.

## Originality — PASS against inspected literature
The closest earlier public result, *Independence complexes of the Shrikhande and \(4\times4\) rook graphs have different sphere-wedge homotopy types*, **already proves** the homotopy classification, which is not claimed as novel here. Its complete RESULT.md statement/proof does not give a character, natural translation action or quotient homology. The full text of Bodnar et al. discusses clique complexes on the original graphs rather than the complement clique/independence complex. The more recent strongly regular clique-complex analysis also has different underlying faces. Direct subject and formula searches of published research records and web literature found no exact equivalent or stronger result subsuming the full \(192\)-character. A possible unindexed equivariant computation remains the main residual novelty risk.

## Value — PASS
For a canonical strongly regular graph whose independence complex is known to be a wedge of \(23\) spheres, the natural \(192\)-automorphism action is an intrinsic finer invariant. The explicit character, its sixteen translation Fourier multiplicities and the sharp difference between two translation-invariant classes and zero full-group invariants answer a precise equivariant topology question; they do not follow from nonequivariant homotopy type. The distinction also determines rational homology of two canonical orbit spaces, so the exact finite calculation has a useful topological interpretation rather than being an arbitrary character sample.

Rational quotient homology is computed; integral torsion of orbit spaces and universal claims about other graphs remain open. Independent external mathematical review is not represented.
