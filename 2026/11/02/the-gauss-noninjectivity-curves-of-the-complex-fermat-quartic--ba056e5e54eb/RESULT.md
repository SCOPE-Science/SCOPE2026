# The Gauss noninjectivity curves of the complex Fermat quartic

## Finding
Let \(X\subset\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}\) be \(x_0^4+x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4=0\), with Gauss morphism \(\gamma([x_i])=[x_i^3]\). Fix a primitive cube root \(\omega\). Define \(E=\{x\in X:\gamma^{-1}(\gamma(x))\text{ contains a point distinct from }x\}\). Then its reduced Zariski closure is the union of **exactly 48 distinct lines and 48 distinct smooth plane quartic curves**. More precisely:

* For each partition \(\{i,j\}\sqcup\{k,l\}=\{0,1,2,3\}\) and each pair of fourth roots \(\alpha^4=\beta^4=-1\), there is a line \(L_{ij|kl;\alpha,\beta}\) defined by \(x_i=\alpha x_j,\ x_k=\beta x_l\). These give \(3\cdot4\cdot4=48\) different lines.
* For each unordered coordinate pair \(\{j,k\}\) and each \(\lambda\) with \(\lambda^4\in\{\omega,\omega^2\}\), there is a smooth plane quartic \(Q_{jk;\lambda}=X\cap\{x_j=\lambda x_k\}\). These give \(6\cdot8=48\) different plane quartics, all of genus three.

The exact pointwise noninjectivity set \(E\) is the union of the 48 quartics with their points having \(x_j=x_k=0\) omitted, together with the 48 lines with their points having fewer than four nonzero coordinates omitted. Equivalently a point of \(X\) has a distinct Gauss mate if and only if either (a) all coordinates are nonzero and its fourth powers split into two zero-sum pairs, or (b) two nonzero coordinates have fourth-power ratio \(\omega\) or \(\omega^2\).

A general point of each line has exactly three points in its Gauss fibre; a general point of each of the 48 smooth plane quartics has exactly two. At intersections, larger fibres can occur: finite-field checks over \(\mathbb F_{73}\) exhibit fibres of cardinality five. These statements concern geometric distinct contact points, not scheme-theoretic multiplicities of fibres.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\) (the proof holds over algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from 2 and 3). The Fermat quartic is smooth; because its partial derivatives are \(4x_i^3\), its Gauss morphism is defined everywhere. The noninjectivity locus \(E\) need not be closed: a curve of double contacts may specialize to a single contact point, hence the distinction between \(E\) and its closure is essential. Every number of components is geometric, with no identification under automorphisms.

## Proof
Write \(f=\sum_{i=0}^3x_i^4\). If \(x,y\in X\) have the same Gauss image, their zero-coordinate sets are identical, and after choosing projective representatives there are \(\zeta_i\in\mu_3\) with \(y_i=\zeta_i x_i\) for each nonzero coordinate. Extend these \(\zeta_i\) arbitrarily to zero coordinates. Conversely, each such diagonal transformation satisfying \(\sum\zeta_i x_i^4=0\) produces a Gauss mate, distinct precisely when the phases on nonzero coordinates are not all equal. Multiplying all phases by a common cube root changes no projective point; therefore normalize \(\zeta_0=1\), leaving 27 patterns.

Classify these patterns by multiplicities of the three cube-root values. One pattern has type \((4)\), eight have \((3,1)\), six have \((2,2)\), and twelve have \((2,1,1)\). For type \((4)\), the transformation is scalar. For type \((3,1)\), subtracting the two equations \(\sum x_i^4=0\) and \(\sum\zeta_i x_i^4=0\) forces the singleton coordinate to vanish, so the transformation acts as a scalar on the point and contributes no distinct mate.

For type \((2,2)\), write \(A=x_i^4+x_j^4\) and \(B=x_k^4+x_l^4\). The equations \(A+B=A+\omega B=0\), or with \(\omega^2\), force \(A=B=0\). Each binary quartic factors into four distinct linear factors over \(\mathbb C\), producing the sixteen specified lines for each of the three partitions. At a point on one such line with all coordinates nonzero, independently multiplying the two coordinate pairs by cube roots produces precisely three Gauss mates within that pattern, including the point itself. Additional mates at an intersection are excluded only generically.

For type \((2,1,1)\), let \(A\) be the sum of the fourth powers in the repeated pair, and let \(B=x_j^4\) and \(C=x_k^4\) be the two singletons. With phases normalized to \((1,\omega,\omega^2)\) the two equations become \(A+B+C=0\) and \(A+\omega B+\omega^2 C=0\). Eliminating \(A\) gives \(B=\omega^2C\). Reversing the singleton phases gives \(B=\omega C\). In either case the locus lies in four linear planes \(x_j=\lambda x_k\), one for each fourth root of the specified root of unity. Restricting \(f\) to the plane gives \(x_i^4+x_l^4+(1+\lambda^4)x_k^4=0\), a smooth irreducible plane quartic because \(1+\omega\) and \(1+\omega^2\) are both nonzero. Conversely every point on this quartic with \(x_j,x_k\ne0\) has the exhibited nontrivial phase transformation. There are six unordered coordinate pairs and eight choices of \(\lambda\), yielding 48 distinct plane quartics. Distinct planes cannot contain the same irreducible non-linear plane quartic.

The 48 lines are pairwise distinct because their defining pair partitions and fourth-root choices determine distinct one-dimensional linear spaces. No smooth plane quartic is a line. Their generic points avoid all other components, as intersections between distinct irreducible curves are finite. On a generic quartic component exactly one nonscalar normalized phase pattern applies, hence exactly one distinct mate and Gauss fibre cardinality two. On a generic line component precisely the two nontrivial relative phases on the two pairs apply, hence fibre cardinality three.

At coordinate-boundary points, type \((2,2)\) yields a genuinely different point only when both pairs have nonzero entries, i.e. when all four coordinates are nonzero. Type \((2,1,1)\) yields a different point exactly when the two singleton coordinates are nonzero. No point with only two nonzero coordinates can have a different mate: the two nonzero fourth powers sum to zero, so the phase factors must be equal. This gives the stated exact description of \(E\) and proves the closure assertion: each component contains a nonempty Zariski-open set of genuine double contacts.

## Relationship to prior work
Corvaja and Zucconi, *The surface of Gauss double points* (first made public 6 May 2021), study double-contact geometry for a **very general** quartic surface and derive a degree-320 double-contact divisor, but their very general hypotheses exclude the Fermat quartic, which contains 48 lines. Veniani, *Symmetries and equations of smooth quartic surfaces with many lines* (2017), and *Lines on K3 quartic surfaces in characteristic 3* (2021), document the Fermat quartic's 48 lines and related symmetries; those 48 lines are not new. The additional 48 smooth genus-three components and the pointwise Gauss-contact classification are the assessed refinement. Published general double-point formulas cannot be specialized without accommodating this highly special surface.

## Limitations
The theorem is set-theoretic and about the closure of distinct Gauss contacts; no scheme structure or multiplicities are claimed. The numbers 48 and 48 are counts of geometric components, not isomorphism classes. Special intersections can have Gauss fibres of cardinality larger than three; no complete stratification of these special fibres is claimed. The primary comparison is based on inspected accessible papers and searches; an earlier equivalent special-Fermat computation remains possible. Independent external audit has not been performed.
