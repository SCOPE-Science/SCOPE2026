# Scientific review of the explicit MacLane generalized Jacobian classification

## Correctness
PASS. The 56 triple determinants in the actual eight-line model have norms zero (8 cases) or three (48 cases), excluding bad primes outside three. The eight ordinary triple points and four double points yield a connected plane curve of arithmetic genus 21, with graph torus rank 13 and local additive dimension eight. The exact spanning-tree and unimodular determinant certificate gives six regular quadratic Frobenius summands and one sign summand, not merely an isogeny. The normalization exact sequence proves the Picard rational-group factors; the verified branch-orbit counts prove both zeta functions.

## Originality
PASS with priority qualification. The lines and their topology are already published by Artal Bartolo–Florens–Guerville-Ballé; their nearly-free deletion-family interpretation is also published by Malara–Pokora–Tutaj-Gasińska. Geisser's theorems describe the affine Picard part in general. These works furnish ingredients, but the inspected material does not tabulate the specific integral quadratic Frobenius module, the two explicit rational-point group structures, and the two evaluated zeta functions. Actual mathematical-record, primary-literature, and accessible historical-claim searches did not yield an equal-or-stronger explicit result. The 1936 original MacLane article was not inspected; the earlier general theory makes implicit derivation possible. The historical priority assessment is therefore provisional.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is a natural arithmetic invariant of the minimal nonreal line arrangement, rather than an arbitrary finite computation. Its Picard group has simultaneous unipotent and toric contributions, and the six regular summands require an integral certificate: rational character eigenvalues alone do not determine the finite abelian point-group structure. The two complete cases distinguish a split thirteen-torus from a norm-one factor and six induced tori, and the curve zeta functions provide independent arithmetic counts. The result is specific and does not claim a universal method.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
