# The generator-line divisor on a Hermitian surface is an explicit reduced complete intersection

## Finding
Let \(q\geq2\) be a prime power and \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_{q^2}\). Set
\[
F=\sum_{i=0}^3X_i^{q+1},\qquad
G=\sum_{i=0}^3X_i^{q^3+1},\qquad
S=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^3_k.
\]
Let \(D\) be the reduced union of all projective lines on \(S\) (equivalently, all \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\)-rational Hermitian generators). Then its homogeneous saturated ideal is exactly
\[
\boxed{I(D)=(F,G).}
\]
There are \((q+1)(q^3+1)\) component lines. Consequently the generator arrangement is a reduced complete intersection of bidegree \((q+1,q^3+1)\), and its Hilbert series is
\[
\frac{(1-t^{q+1})(1-t^{q^3+1})}{(1-t)^4}.
\]
Its arithmetic genus is
\[
p_a(D)=1+\frac{(q+1)(q^3+1)(q^3+q-2)}2.
\]
The support of its singular locus is precisely \(S(\mathbb F_{q^2})\), consisting of \((q^3+1)(q^2+1)\) points. At each such point, \(D\), viewed on the smooth surface \(S\), has an ordinary \((q+1)\)-fold point with \(q+1\) distinct tangent directions.

In particular, among homogeneous equations for the union of generator lines **modulo multiples of** \(F\), none has degree below \(q^3+1\), and the vector space of such classes in degree \(q^3+1\) is one-dimensional, generated by the class of \(G\).

## Assumptions and scope
The field has characteristic \(p\), with \(q\) a positive power of \(p\) and \(q\ge2\). All geometric lines and schemes are considered over the algebraic closure \(k\); the displayed equations and generator lines descend to \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\). A generator is a two-dimensional totally isotropic subspace for the nondegenerate Hermitian form
\[
h(u,v)=\sum_{i=0}^3u_i^qv_i
\]
on \(\mathbb F_{q^2}^4\). An ordinary multiple point on the surface means that its local reduced curve has distinct smooth branches with pairwise distinct tangent directions in the tangent plane of \(S\).

## Proof
**All geometric lines are rational generators.** If \(U\subset k^4\) is any two-dimensional vector subspace whose projectivization lies on \(S\), expanding \(F(su+tv)\) for independent \(u,v\in U\) shows that \(\sum u_i^qv_i=\sum v_i^qu_i=0\), as well as the two self-pairings. Therefore the coefficientwise \(q\)-Frobenius image \(U^{[q]}\) is the ordinary bilinear orthogonal \(U^\perp\). Both spaces have dimension two, hence equality holds. Applying \(q\)-Frobenius and double orthogonality gives \(U^{[q^2]}=U\), so \(U\) is \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\)-rational. Thus every geometric line is a generator of the finite Hermitian polar space.

The standard count for the Hermitian surface is \(\#S(\mathbb F_{q^2})=(q^3+1)(q^2+1)\). For every rational point \(P\), the residual rank-two Hermitian polar space in its tangent plane has precisely \(q+1\) isotropic projective directions; these are the \(q+1\) generators through \(P\). Each generator has \(q^2+1\) rational points. Counting point-line incidences therefore yields precisely
\[
N=\frac{(q^3+1)(q^2+1)(q+1)}{q^2+1}=(q+1)(q^3+1)
\]
distinct generators.

**All generators lie in the two equations.** If a generator is spanned by \(u,v\in\mathbb F_{q^2}^4\), self- and cross-pairings vanish. For any \(s,t\in k\), the identity \(u_i^{q^3}=u_i^q\) for field-rational coordinates implies
\[
G(su+tv)=s^{q^3+1}h(u,u)+s^{q^3}t\,h(u,v)
+s t^{q^3}h(v,u)+t^{q^3+1}h(v,v)=0.
\]
Similarly \(F(su+tv)=0\). Hence the reduced generator union \(D\) is contained in \(Z=V(F,G)\).

**The intersection is proper and reduced.** The partial derivatives of \(F\) and \(G\) are respectively \((X_0^q,\ldots,X_3^q)\) and \((X_0^{q^3},\ldots,X_3^{q^3})\), because \(q+1\) and \(q^3+1\) both equal one in \(k\). Thus \(S\) is smooth. The two gradients are proportional precisely when every nonzero coordinate ratio \(r\) satisfies \(r^{q^3-q}=1\), which in characteristic \(p\) is equivalent to \(r^{q^2-1}=1\), namely \(r\in\mathbb F_{q^2}^*\). Thus their rank drops precisely at \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\)-rational projective points. Since not all geometric points of the positive-dimensional smooth surface \(S\) are rational, \(F\) does not divide \(G\); therefore \(Z\) is a proper complete-intersection curve of degree \((q+1)(q^3+1)=N\).

The closed subscheme \(Z\) contains the \(N\) distinct generator lines, whose degrees already sum to \(N\). Since complete intersections are pure one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay schemes, its cycle therefore has exactly those \(N\) lines, each with generic multiplicity one, and no further components. Thus \(Z\) is generically reduced; being Cohen--Macaulay it has no embedded associated primes, so it is reduced. A complete-intersection ideal in the homogeneous polynomial ring is saturated in this positive-dimensional situation. It follows that \(Z=D\) scheme-theoretically and \(I(D)=(F,G)\).

**Singularities and genus.** The Jacobian criterion for the two equations shows that \(D\) is singular exactly at rational projective points on \(S\). At each such point the \(q+1\) rational generator lines through it are precisely the local branches, and distinct lines give distinct tangent directions on \(S\); the singularity is an ordinary \((q+1)\)-fold point. The complete-intersection Hilbert series and the adjunction formula yield the asserted arithmetic genus. Finally, the two-generator homogeneous ideal has no element outside \((F)\) below degree \(q^3+1\), and its quotient by \((F)\) in that degree is spanned by the class of \(G\). This proves the minimality statement.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` independently enumerates row-reduced rational lines over \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_9\), checks their full Hermitian isotropy and the vanishing of \(G\), counts rational points and incidence multiplicities, and checks the derived genus. It returns:

```
q=2 FIELD=4 RATIONAL_POINTS=45 GENERATOR_LINES=27 GENERATORS_PER_POINT=3 DEGREE=27 ARITHMETIC_GENUS=109
q=3 FIELD=9 RATIONAL_POINTS=280 GENERATOR_LINES=112 GENERATORS_PER_POINT=4 DEGREE=112 ARITHMETIC_GENUS=1569
VERIFY_OK
```

The all-prime-power statement is proved symbolically above; finite enumeration is an independent consistency test, not an extrapolated proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Homma and Kim characterize nonsingular Hermitian surfaces through their rational point count and give the classical point formula. Rams and Schütt establish the characteristic-three extremal count of 112 lines, including the Fermat quartic example. These earlier results give the finite-Hermitian geometry and the line count, not, in the passages compared, the saturated homogeneous ideal of the full line union, its minimal defining degree, or the scheme-theoretic complete-intersection description. The contribution here is the explicit generator divisor and the resulting singularity and genus package. Although searches did not locate an equivalent complete-ideal assertion, related older finite-geometry and projective-configuration literature could contain an unindexed equivalent; absence from the searched records is not a priority proof.

## Limitations
No statement of historical uniqueness is asserted. The result is specific to the nondegenerate Hermitian Fermat surface and the union of *all* its generators, not arbitrary subarrangements or arbitrary hypersurfaces. The singularity statement concerns reduced-curve branches on the smooth ambient surface; it is not an assertion about the full singular subscheme's scheme-theoretic multiplicities. No independent review has been performed.
