# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The ground graph is specified by an explicit edge formula, and the exact finite checker regenerates all \(180\) facets and all \(1,634\) nonempty simplices. Its deterministic face matching leaves one zero-cell, four five-cells and five six-cells, and an exhaustive topological sort of the oriented \(8,081\)-incidence Hasse diagram establishes acyclicity. The signed gradient-path recurrence yields an exactly zero integer Morse boundary. Hurewicz in degree five then makes the five six-cell attaching maps nullhomotopic, proving the claimed integral wedge decomposition. Independent original simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) corroborate both reduced Betti numbers.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
The closest foundational ordinary-cut-complex results are arXiv:2304.13675 and arXiv:2407.08158. Their named families and relevant theorems concern complete multipartite graphs, cycles, squared cycles and paths, grids, and clique prisms; none covers the Mycielski five-cycle. The related arXiv:2209.13503 treats total cut complexes (independent complementary sets), not the ordinary disconnected-set construction for \(k=4\). Targeted indexed-record searches found a Grötzsch *matching* complex, a graph Čech nerve, and ordinary four-cut complexes of different graphs, none with a statement implying this result. The search is not proof of uniqueness. The inaccessible 2018 precursor thesis and unindexed equivalent results remain risks.

## Value — PASS
The Grötzsch graph is the smallest triangle-free four-chromatic graph, making its four-cut topology an intrinsically motivated finite invariant. The calculation gives a full **integral homotopy type**, not merely a face count or rational Euler characteristic. It exhibits a pure ordinary four-cut complex with substantial reduced homology in adjacent degrees, giving a concrete non-shellability witness outside the graph families resolved in the foundational series. It is a useful benchmark for how Mycielski constructions affect cut-complex topology; no unsupported general classification is inferred.
