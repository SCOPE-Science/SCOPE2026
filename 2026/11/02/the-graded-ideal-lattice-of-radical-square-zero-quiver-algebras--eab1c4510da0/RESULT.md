# The graded ideal lattice of radical-square-zero quiver algebras and its large-field limit

## Finding

Let \(Q\) be any finite quiver with a nonempty vertex set \(V\), allowing directed cycles, loops, and multiple arrows. Let \(a_{vw}\) denote the number of arrows from vertex \(v\) to vertex \(w\), and let \(A=\mathbb F_qQ/J^2\), where \(J\) is the ideal generated by all arrows. Write \({m\brack r}_q\) for a Gaussian binomial coefficient and define the subspace enumerator
\[
G_m(z;q)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}{m\brack r}_q z^r,\qquad G_0(z;q)=1.
\]
The quotient-dimension enumerator of **all two-sided ideals** is exactly
\[
\boxed{Z_Q(z;q):=\sum_{I\triangleleft A}z^{\dim_{\mathbb F_q}(A/I)}
=\sum_{S\subseteq V}z^{|S|}\prod_{v,w\in S}G_{a_{vw}}(z;q).}
\]
Every ideal is uniquely specified by its surviving-vertex set \(S\subseteq V\) and, for each ordered pair \((v,w)\in S^2\), a vector subspace \(W_{vw}\leq\mathbb F_q^{a_{vw}}\); its quotient dimension is \(|S|+\sum_{v,w\in S}(a_{vw}-\dim W_{vw})\). The ideals with a fixed survivor set thus form a product of ordinary subspace lattices, and cross-fiber inclusion also has an explicit criterion given below.

For the large-field regime, put \(d_m=\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor\), \(D=\sum_{v,w\in V}d_{a_{vw}}\), and let \(U\subseteq V\) be the set of endpoints of all ordered vertex pairs having at least two arrows. Define
\[
b_m(z)=\begin{cases}
z^{m/2},&m\text{ even},\\
z^{(m-1)/2}(1+z),&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then, coefficientwise for fixed \(Q\) as \(q\to\infty\) through prime powers,
\[
\boxed{q^{-D}Z_Q(z;q)=H_Q(z)+O_Q(q^{-1}),\quad
H_Q(z)=\sum_{S\supseteq U}z^{|S|}\prod_{v,w\in S}b_{a_{vw}}(z).}
\]
For a uniformly random two-sided ideal, the quotient-dimension probability generating function therefore converges to \(H_Q(z)/H_Q(1)\), with error \(O_Q(q^{-1})\) in every coefficient. In particular, the surviving vertex set contains \(U\) with probability tending to one. The enumerator \(Z_Q\) is independent of the field size exactly when there is at most one arrow for every ordered pair of vertices; multiplicity two is the sharp boundary.

## Assumptions and scope

The result applies to every finite nonempty quiver and every finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) of prime-power order. The algebra includes the trivial paths (vertex idempotents); it is unital, associative and has arrow-radical square zero. No assumptions on connectedness, orientation, loops or characteristic are made. The limit means the prime-power cardinality \(q\) grows with the quiver fixed. All ideals are counted as distinct \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear subspaces, not modulo algebra automorphisms or isomorphism of quotient algebras.

The arrow space between vertices is \(E_{vw}=e_vJe_w\) of dimension \(a_{vw}\), using the convention that a path from \(v\) to \(w\) is in \(e_vJe_w\). For \(S\subseteq V\) and \(W_{vw}\leq E_{vw}\) for \(v,w\in S\), let
\[
I(S,W)=\bigoplus_{v\notin S}\mathbb F_qe_v
\;\oplus\!
\bigoplus_{\substack{(v,w)\in V^2\\\{v,w\}\not\subseteq S}}E_{vw}
\;\oplus\!
\bigoplus_{v,w\in S}W_{vw}.
\]

## Proof

**1. Recover vertices in an ideal.** Every element of \(A\) has a unique expansion into vertex-idempotent and arrow components. Suppose an ideal \(I\) contains an element with nonzero \(e_v\)-coefficient \(c\). Multiplying on each side by \(e_v\) produces \(ce_v+u\in I\cap e_vAe_v\), where \(u\in e_vJe_v\) is a linear combination of loops and \(u^2=0\). This element is a unit of the corner algebra \(e_vAe_v\), with inverse \(c^{-1}e_v-c^{-2}u\). Multiplication by the inverse shows \(e_v\in I\). Thus, for every \(v\), either \(e_v\in I\) or the coefficient of \(e_v\) in every element of \(I\) is zero.

**2. Classify the remaining arrow components.** Let \(S=\{v:e_v\notin I\}\). For every \(v\notin S\), the relations \(e_v A\subseteq I\) and \(Ae_v\subseteq I\) force all arrows incident with \(v\) into \(I\). By the preceding step, after removing those vertex and forced-arrow summands, \(I\) lies inside \(J\). Since \(e_vxe_w\in I\) for every \(x\in I\), the residual arrow subspace decomposes uniquely into \(W_{vw}=I\cap E_{vw}\), for \(v,w\in S\). Conversely any \(I(S,W)\) as displayed above is an ideal: vertex multiplication preserves each \(E_{vw}\), all products of two arrows vanish, and the forced-arrow summands absorb multiplication involving killed vertices. These constructions are inverse, and they prove the normal form.

The inclusion order is also recovered. Precisely,
\[
I(S,W)\subseteq I(T,Y)
\iff S\supseteq T\text{ and }W_{vw}\subseteq Y_{vw}\text{ for all }v,w\in T.
\]
This follows because a killed vertex in the smaller ideal must also be killed in the larger, and the only arrow corners left unconstrained in the larger ideal have both endpoints in \(T\).

**3. Count ideals by quotient dimension.** A quotient contains the idempotents corresponding to \(S\), contributing \(|S|\) dimensions. For each \((v,w)\in S^2\), the quotient of \(E_{vw}\) by \(W_{vw}\) contributes dimension \(r=a_{vw}-\dim W_{vw}\). There are precisely \({a_{vw}\brack r}_q\) choices of that subspace. Multiplying independent choices and summing over all \(S\) proves the exact displayed formula.

**4. Identify the large-field leading terms.** For fixed integers \(m,r\), the elementary product expression
\[
{m\brack r}_q=q^{r(m-r)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1-q^{-(m-r+j)}}{1-q^{-j}}
\]
shows \({m\brack r}_q=q^{r(m-r)}(1+O_m(q^{-1}))\), with the coefficient \(1\) also valid at \(r=0,m\). The largest exponent over \(0\leq r\leq m\) is \(d_m\). It is attained by one value \(r=m/2\) for even \(m\), and by the adjacent values \((m-1)/2,(m+1)/2\) for odd \(m\). Thus \(q^{-d_m}G_m(z;q)\) tends coefficientwise to \(b_m(z)\), with errors \(O_m(q^{-1})\).

For a fixed survivor set \(S\), its contribution to \(Z_Q\) has \(q\)-degree \(D(S)=\sum_{v,w\in S}d_{a_{vw}}\). Since \(d_m>0\) precisely for \(m\geq2\), we have \(D(S)=D\) if and only if \(S\supseteq U\). Every other term is at least one degree lower. Factoring \(q^D\) yields exactly \(H_Q\), with coefficientwise error \(O_Q(q^{-1})\). Division by \(Z_Q(1;q)=q^D(H_Q(1)+O_Q(q^{-1}))\), where \(H_Q(1)>0\), proves the probability limit and the stated rate.

When every \(a_{vw}\leq1\), each \(G_{a_{vw}}\) equals either \(1\) or \(1+z\), independent of \(q\). Conversely, when some \(a_{vw}\geq2\), then \(D\geq1\), so the positive leading coefficient \(H_Q(1)\) proves that the total ideal count grows unboundedly with \(q\), and hence depends on the field size. This proves the sharp multiplicity criterion.

**5. An explicit transition.** For three vertices and precisely two parallel arrows \(1\to2\) and one arrow \(2\to3\), the formula simplifies to
\[
Z_Q(z;q)=1+3z+3z^2+(q+3)z^3+(q+3)z^4+(q+2)z^5+z^6,
\quad H_Q(z)=z^3+z^4+z^5.
\]
Thus at \(q=2\) there are \(22\) ideals and the quotient-dimension distribution is \((1,3,3,5,5,4,1)\). As \(q\to\infty\), the quotient has dimension \(3\), \(4\), or \(5\) with limiting probability \(1/3\) each; all other quotient dimensions have limiting probability zero.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` independently enumerates **all** subspaces of the underlying vector space in reduced row-echelon form, checks two-sided closure under multiplication by every algebra basis element, and compares the resulting quotient-dimension counts with the closed formula. Thirteen complete small-field configurations were checked, including loops, double arrows, directed cycles, disconnected quivers and the three-vertex transition example, over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). They agree exactly. The unrestricted theorem follows from the proof, not from these finite enumerations; the code enumerates prime-field cases only, while the proof handles all prime powers.

## Relationship to prior work

Vaso studies the *same algebras* \(KQ/J^2\) in representation theory and classifies their higher cluster-tilting subcategories, not their two-sided ideals or finite-field ideal enumerators [1]. Waweru and Maingi give general two-sided Gröbner algorithms for quiver path algebras; their statements do not contain the all-quiver Gaussian ideal-count polynomial or the large-field limit [2]. Catoiu, Chin, and Krop give a broad general description of ideal lattices of finite-dimensional algebras using balanced collections of invariant submodules [3]. This is an important methodological precursor; the special finite-field count and explicit concentration law are not stated there. For the local one-vertex radical-square-zero specialization, the Gaussian subspace count is elementary and previously known; that specialization by itself is not asserted as novel.

The structural Peirce decomposition is standard. The contribution assessed here is its all-quiver weighted enumerator together with the sharp parallel-arrow threshold, precise concentration support and quotient-dimension limiting law. No global uniqueness of these expressions in the literature is asserted.

## Limitations

The limit fixes the quiver while enlarging the finite field; it is not a uniform statement when the numbers of vertices or arrows vary with \(q\). The enumerator counts ideals as subspaces, not isomorphism classes of quotient algebras. Earlier equivalent formulas could be implicit in the more general theory of ideal lattices and incidence algebras; publication priority is not independently certified. Exhaustive computations cover only the explicitly described small-field cases; independent mathematical validation remains outstanding.
