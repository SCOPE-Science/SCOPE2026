# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every candidate is approved by exactly two voters. Consequently a proposal with \(t\) candidates can strictly improve at most \(2t\) voters. Below the half-budget threshold, the Hare quota rules out all blocks. At equality, saturating that incidence bound forces both approving voters of every proposed candidate to receive zero utility from the incumbent committee, which gives the vacant-triple condition; a vacant triple supplies an explicit two-voter block. Rooted cyclic gap vectors of length \(k\) in \(\{0,1,2\}\) and sum \(k\) give the exact coefficient count. The lattice local central limit theorem and Stirling's formula supply the asymptotic fraction. Direct exhaustive integer enumeration from the included `verify.py` agrees for all \(n=2k\le12\) and for tested sub-half-budget cases through \(n=9\).

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** The primary September 2026 core-existence paper proves that some core committee always exists, but provides no forbidden-run classification or central-trinomial census for the fixed overlapping cyclic two-approval family. Restricted-domain existence, generic integer-programming characterizations, and candidate-disjoint-chain least-core formulas do not imply the exact three-vacancy equivalence and count without new analysis. The coefficient itself is classical; the claimed contribution is its identification as the core-committee census at the quota transition. The accessible primary texts and targeted searches did not reveal matching or dominating coverage; unindexed or otherwise uninspected special-case results remain a genuine risk.

**Value — PASS.** For the natural local-approval circle, the theorem pinpoints the first quota at which a committee can be blocked, identifies every stable committee by a local forbidden pattern, and quantifies how rapidly stable committees become rare among fixed-size committees as the electorate grows. This is more informative than a single witness or a general existence result and is directly relevant when randomly sampling committees or checking policy eligibility.

Limits: the theorem does not address super-half budgets, other core definitions, or noncyclic and weighted preferences. The finite checks corroborate only their stated ranges; the universal statements follow from the complete proof. Possible prior equivalent identities are not ruled out with certainty.
