# The hyperoctahedral homology character of two-point configurations on the four-dimensional cross-polytope graph

## Finding
Let \(G=K_{2,2,2,2}\) be the graph on eight vertices \(\pm e_i\) for \(1\le i\le4\), with two vertices adjacent exactly when their axes differ. Put \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|G|)\), the space of unordered configurations of two distinct points on the geometric graph, and let the 384-element signed-permutation group \(B_4=(C_2)^4\rtimes S_4\) act by permuting the axes and changing their signs.

The rational Betti vector is
\[\bigl(b_0(X),b_1(X),b_2(X)\bigr)=(1,17,56),\qquad H_j(X;\mathbb Q)=0\ (j>2).\]

The complete character of \(B_4\) on both nonzero positive-degree homology groups is **exactly** the twenty-class table in `character_table.txt`. Each row is indexed by the two partitions \((\lambda^+,\lambda^-)\) of a signed-permutation cycle type, and records the group-class size and the five traces on \(C_0,C_1,C_2,H_1,H_2\). In particular the traces of the identity on \(H_1,H_2\) are \(17,56\), and those of full coordinate inversion are \(1,-8\).

The rational Betti vectors of natural finite symmetry quotients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(b_0,b_1,b_2)(X/B_4;\mathbb Q)&=(1,0,0),\\
(b_0,b_1,b_2)(X/S_4;\mathbb Q)&=(1,0,2),\\
(b_0,b_1,b_2)(X/(C_2)^4;\mathbb Q)&=(1,3,3).
\end{aligned}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Only the **one-dimensional graph** formed by the vertices and edges of the four-dimensional cross-polytope is used, not the boundary sphere of the polytope. Vertices are labeled \((i,s)\) with \(i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), \(s\in\{0,1\}\). Edges connect unequal axes. The standard cubical two-particle configuration subcomplex has 28 zero-cells, 144 one-cells, and 156 two-cells. The subdivision conditions for the Abrams two-particle model hold: every embedded loop has at least three edges, and every path between distinct vertices of valence other than two has at least one edge. All homology and representations in the theorem have \(\mathbb Q\) coefficients; no integral torsion claim is made.

## Proof
**1. Signed cubical chains.** Fix vertex labels \(0,\ldots,7\); orient graph edges from smaller to larger labels. The cells are (i) unordered distinct vertex pairs \(\{u,v\}\), (ii) a graph edge \(e=[u,v]\) with a vertex \(w\notin e\), oriented along \(e\), and (iii) unordered pairs of disjoint edges \(\{e,f\}\), oriented by the ordered product of the smaller-indexed edge and larger-indexed edge. The boundaries are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_1(e,w)&=(v,w)-(u,w),\\
\partial_2(e,f)&=(f,v)-(f,u)-(e,y)+(e,x),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(e=[u,v]\), \(f=[x,y]\) and \(e<f\). This is the finite two-particle cubical deformation-retract model of the collision-free configuration space. The attached `crosspolytope4_check.py` constructs the signed matrices and checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\).

**2. Exact rational ranks.** The zero–one incidence map has rank \(27\) over \(\mathbb Q\), since its one-skeleton is connected. Exact sparse Gaussian elimination modulo the prime \(1000003\), with explicit integer boundary columns generated by the attached checker, gives rank \(100\) for \(\partial_2\) modulo that prime, hence rank at least \(100\) over \(\mathbb Q\). For the reverse bound, the map \(X\to \operatorname{SP}^2|G|\) induces a surjection on rational first homology onto \(H_1(G;\mathbb Q)\). Indeed, graph triangles generate its cycle space: four-cycles supported on two axes can be decomposed into triangles through a vertex on a third axis, and any longer cycle can likewise be shortened using an available third-axis vertex. Each such triangle has a vertex outside it, so one particle can run around the triangle while the other is held at that disjoint vertex. Its image in symmetric-square first homology is the triangle class. Thus \(b_1(X)\ge \dim H_1(G;\mathbb Q)=24-8+1=17\), and \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2\le144-27-17=100\). Therefore the exact rational boundary ranks are \((27,100)\), giving \(b_1=17\) and \(b_2=156-100=56\). The two-dimensional cellular model excludes higher degrees.

**3. The full character, not only dimension.** The triangle-movement classes also show that \(H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\to H_1(G;\mathbb Q)\) is a \(B_4\)-equivariant isomorphism. Consequently, for each \(g\in B_4\),
\[\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1)=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_1(G))-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0(G))+1,\]
where oriented graph edges fixed in reverse orientation count negatively. The cellular Lefschetz identity then gives
\[\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_2)=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_2(X))-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_1(X))+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0(X))-1+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1).\]
A one-cell \((e,w)\) has the orientation sign of its moved edge; a two-cell \(\{e,f\}\) has the product of the two edge-orientation signs, *times minus one if the two edges are exchanged*. These prescriptions give each of the twenty exact character values in the table. The checker enumerates all \(384\) group elements, confirms each orientation action commutes with both boundaries, and verifies that the character is constant on each signed cycle class.

**4. Quotient homology.** For any finite subgroup \(H\le B_4\), rational transfer after equivariant subdivision identifies \(H_*(X/H;\mathbb Q)\) with the \(H\)-invariants of \(H_*(X;\mathbb Q)\). Averaging the table over the entire group and over the explicitly described coordinate permutation and flip subgroups gives invariant dimensions \((0,0)\), \((0,2)\), and \((3,3)\) in degrees one and two, respectively. Degree zero remains \(\mathbb Q\). This proves every quotient formula.

The program constructs every graph and configuration cell, checks boundary composition, computes the two finite-field ranks, verifies signed cellular equivariance for **all 384** automorphisms and all cubical cells, enumerates the twenty signed-cycle types, and averages homology characters over the three relevant subgroups. The exact output is embedded as `checker_output.txt`. Successful terminal lines include `CROSSPOLYTOPE4_TWO_POINT_EXACT_OK` and `ALL_384_GROUP_ACTIONS_COMMUTE_WITH_BOTH_BOUNDARIES_OK`.

The rank-100 lower bound from finite-field elimination is converted into an **exact rational rank** only by the independent topological upper bound in Proof 2. No claim about integral torsion, integral symmetry lattices, or all higher-dimensional cross-polytope graphs follows from these checks.

## Relationship to prior work
An and Knudsen's 2020 planar graph-braid second-homology theorem motivates the nonplanar case but has a planarity hypothesis that excludes this graph. González and González compute a discrete gradient and ordered two-point cohomology for **complete graphs**; \(K_{2,2,2,2}\) is not complete. Ramos and He Yun examine stable homology representations for complete graphs, balanced complete-bipartite graphs, crown graphs and a tripartite family; those are not this four-part, opposite-vertex-deleted graph. Previously indexed symmetry computations for the octahedral graph, the cuboctahedral graph and the dodecahedral graph concern different two-point configuration spaces. The claim here is specifically the complete \(B_4\)-character and quotient distinction for the four-cross-polytope **graph**.

## Limitations
This is a complete **single natural graph** calculation, not an infinite-family formula for all cross-polytope dimensions. Its significance lies in all twenty symmetry classes, a rank-56 second-homology module, and the contrast among full, axis-permutation and independent-flip quotient homology. The result does not calculate the integral symmetry module, possible integral homology torsion, or the homotopy types of the quotients. The checked literature and indexed sources cannot rule out an equivalent unindexed computation. A close 2026 stability paper was assessed from its accessible abstract and family description, not its full text, leaving a bounded bibliographic risk.
