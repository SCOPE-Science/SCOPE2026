# The integral cohomology ring of the odd biisotropic Grassmannian of lines

## Finding

For each integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(X_n=\operatorname{BIGr}(2;2n+1)\) be the smooth complex variety of two-dimensional subspaces simultaneously isotropic for a general pencil of alternating forms on a \((2n+1)\)-dimensional vector space. Let \(\mathcal U\) be the tautological rank-two bundle restricted from \(G_n=\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n+1)\), and set \(h=c_1(\mathcal U^\vee)\) and \(c=c_2(\mathcal U^\vee)\). Give these elements cohomological degrees \(2\) and \(4\), respectively.

Define integral polynomials \(q_0=1\), \(q_1=h\), and \(q_j=hq_{j-1}-cq_{j-2}\) for \(j\ge2\), and
\[
R_n(h,c)=\sum_{b=0}^{n-1}(-1)^b(n-b)c^bq_{2n-1-2b}(h,c).
\]
Then the entire integral singular cohomology ring, including its cup product, is
\[
\boxed{\ H^*(X_n;\mathbb Z)\ \cong\ \mathbb Z[h,c]/(q_{2n}(h,c),R_n(h,c)).\ }
\]
The isomorphism is the geometric restriction of tautological Chern classes; there is no cohomological torsion or odd cohomology. Both displayed equations are integral: no rational denominators or saturation are needed. The two relations have Chow degrees \(2n\) and \(2n-1\), and the quotient is a finite free complete intersection of top Chow degree \(4n-4\).

In the smallest case \(n=2\), this gives
\[
H^*(\operatorname{BIGr}(2;5);\mathbb Z)
\cong \mathbb Z[h,c]/(h^4-3h^2c+c^2,\;2h^3-5hc).
\]
Despite having the same additive Betti numbers as \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n)\), the two integral cohomology rings are not isomorphic, since their primitive ample-generator top powers are respectively the unequal Catalan numbers \(C_{2n-1}\) and \(C_{2n-2}\).

## Assumptions and scope

All varieties and cohomology groups are over the complex numbers. The pencil is general so that \(X_n\) is smooth; its biisotropic locus for \(k=2\) is exactly the transverse intersection of two Plücker hyperplanes in \(G_n\). Thus \(\dim_{\mathbb C}G_n=4n-2\), \(\dim_{\mathbb C}X_n=4n-4\), and its fundamental class in \(G_n\) is \(h^2\). The smoothness and additive integral cohomology calculation are inputs from the work cited below; the new assertion concerns the full *multiplicative* integral ring structure for \(k=2\). No claim is made for higher \(k\), equivariant cohomology, or quantum cohomology.

## Proof

**1. Ambient Schubert classes and the candidate relation.** The integral cohomology of the ordinary Grassmannian is
\[
H^*(G_n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[h,c]/(q_{2n},q_{2n+1}).
\]
Its Schubert classes are \(\sigma_{a,b}=c^bq_{a-b}\), for \(2n-1\ge a\ge b\ge0\). Let \(\iota:X_n\hookrightarrow G_n\) be the inclusion. By the integral weak Lefschetz theorem, restriction is an isomorphism in cohomological degrees strictly below \(4n-4\). In particular, \(H^{4n-6}(X_n;\mathbb Z)\) is generated by the restricted ambient classes \(\sigma_{2n-3-j,j}\) for \(0\le j\le n-2\).

By the two-row Pieri rule, the pairing in \(G_n\)
\[
\left\langle\sigma_{2n-3-j,j},\, h^2\sigma_{2n-1-b,b}\right\rangle_{G_n}
=\begin{cases}\binom2{b-j},&0\le b-j\le2,\\0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}
\]
for \(0\le j\le n-2\), \(0\le b\le n-1\). The integer coefficient vector \(v_b=(-1)^b(n-b)\) satisfies \(v_j+2v_{j+1}+v_{j+2}=0\), interpreting \(v_n=0\). Thus the restricted class \(\iota^*R_n\) pairs to zero with every class in complementary Chow degree \(2n-3\). By Poincaré duality on the smooth compact variety \(X_n\), and the preceding weak Lefschetz isomorphism, \(\iota^*R_n=0\) *integrally*. This produces a graded ring map
\[
\phi:Q_n:=\mathbb Z[h,c]/(q_{2n},R_n)\longrightarrow H^*(X_n;\mathbb Z).
\]

**2. The candidate is an integral finite flat complete intersection.** The recurrence gives the polynomial identity
\[
h^2R_n=(n+1)h q_{2n}-q_{2n+1}.
\]
Consequently the already-known ambient relation \(q_{2n+1}=0\) follows from the two displayed generators of \(Q_n\). For every field \(K\), the two equations \(q_{2n}=R_n=0\) in \(K[h,c]\) have no nonzero common solution over an algebraic closure. If \(c=0\), then \(q_{2n}=h^{2n}\), so \(h=0\). If \(c\ne0\), the recurrence gives \(q_{2n}(0,c)=(-c)^n\ne0\); thus any proposed common zero has \(h\ne0\). The identity forces \(q_{2n+1}=0\), and the recurrence then forces \(q_{2n-1}=0\). Successively running the recurrence backwards, using \(c\ne0\), contradicts \(q_0=1\). This works even in positive characteristic.

It follows that \(q_{2n},R_n\) form a weighted homogeneous regular sequence after base change to every field. The Hilbert series in Chow degree is therefore, over any field,
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(Q_n,t)=\frac{(1-t^{2n-1})(1-t^{2n})}{(1-t)(1-t^2)}.
\]
The graded pieces of \(Q_n\) are finitely generated abelian groups. Since their dimensions after reduction modulo every prime equal their ranks over \(\mathbb Q\), no graded piece has torsion. Hence they are all free, and the displayed Hilbert series holds over \(\mathbb Z\). Its coefficients are exactly those of \(H^*(\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n);\mathbb Z)\); in particular \(Q_n\) has rank one in top Chow degree \(d=4n-4\) and is zero above it.

**3. Perfect integral pairings.** On \(X_n\), the top degree of \(c^{2n-2}\) is
\[
\int_{X_n}c^{2n-2}=\int_{G_n}h^2c^{2n-2}=1,
\]
by two applications of the Pieri rule. Therefore the image of \(c^{2n-2}\) is a generator of the top integral cohomology of \(X_n\). It follows that its class generates the top rank-one group of \(Q_n\) as well, hence remains nonzero modulo every prime.

Over every field, \(Q_n\) is an Artinian graded complete intersection, hence a Gorenstein algebra with one-dimensional socle in Chow degree \(d\). Multiplication into the chosen nonzero top element therefore gives perfect pairings of complementary degrees. As all graded pieces of \(Q_n\) are free over \(\mathbb Z\), these pairings have integer matrices with determinant nonzero modulo every prime, hence determinant \(\pm1\). Thus \(Q_n\) itself has integral Poincaré duality.

**4. Identification with the geometric ring.** Fonarev and Kuznetsov prove that \(X_n\) has a cellular decomposition with the same number of cells in each dimension as \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n)\), and in particular that its integral cohomology is torsion-free, concentrated in even degrees, and has the same graded ranks as \(Q_n\). The ring map \(\phi\) is an isomorphism in top degree by Step 3, preserves products, and takes the unit to the unit. Both source and target have unimodular Poincaré pairings and equal ranks in all complementary degrees. For each degree, if \(A\) and \(B\) denote matrices of the two complementary components of \(\phi\), preservation of pairings gives \(P_Q=A^\mathsf T P_XB\); taking determinants shows \(|\det A|\,|\det B|=1\). Each component is consequently an integral isomorphism. This proves the claimed ring presentation.

**5. Nonisomorphism to the comparison Grassmannian.** The primitive degree-two group on both sides is \(\mathbb Z\), so a graded integral ring isomorphism would send the positive ample generator to its positive or negative. Since \(4n-4\) is even, the top power is unaffected by sign. The respective Plücker degrees are \(\deg G_n=C_{2n-1}\) and \(\deg\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n)=C_{2n-2}\), with \(C_j=\frac1{j+1}\binom{2j}{j}\). These are different for every \(n\ge2\). Therefore no graded integral ring isomorphism exists.

## Verification

The accompanying independent integer-arithmetic program `verify.py` checks, for every \(2\le n\le12\), the polynomial identity in Step 2, the exact Pieri pairing matrices and their stated kernel, the unimodularity of the middle intersection matrix, and the coefficient-by-coefficient equality between the complete-intersection Hilbert series and the ordinary Grassmannian Betti numbers. It prints `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks are diagnostic only; the all-\(n\), all-characteristic flatness argument and the integral duality proof are mathematical arguments above, not extrapolations from data.

As an additional closed check, the middle intersection form on the restricted ambient Schubert classes \(\sigma_{2n-2-b,b}\), ordered by \(0\le b\le n-1\), is tridiagonal: diagonal entries are \(2\) except the last \(1\), and both immediate off-diagonals are \(1\). Its determinant is \(1\) by a continuant recurrence.

## Relationship to prior work

Fonarev and Kuznetsov establish the additive cohomology/Białynicki-Birula cell count for every \(k\) and explicitly distinguish the yet-to-be-determined ring structure (Remark 1.2 in the October 2026 preprint). The present result answers this multiplicative question for the natural infinite subfamily \(k=2\), where the pencil condition is a codimension-two Plücker linear section. It depends on their additive Betti-number theorem and on standard integral Schubert calculus, but their result does not provide this explicit two-relation ring presentation. The two-variable Schur presentation of ordinary Grassmannian cohomology and the weak Lefschetz theorem are standard inputs, not claimed as new. A general codimension-two linear-section formula elsewhere could imply the result without naming biisotropic varieties; this remains a literature-priority risk.

## Limitations

Only ordinary integral singular cohomology and its cup product are determined. This does not establish integral Chow-ring isomorphism (although all stated generators are algebraic), an equivariant or quantum presentation, or formulas for \(k\ge3\). No independent mathematical validation has been performed. Earlier works on special linear sections could contain an equivalent result; the available source comparisons do not provide a definitive exhaustive priority guarantee.
