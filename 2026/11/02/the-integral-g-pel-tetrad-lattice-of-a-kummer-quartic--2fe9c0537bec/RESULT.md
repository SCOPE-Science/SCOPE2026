# The integral Göpel-tetrad lattice of a Kummer quartic

## Finding

Let \(C\) be a smooth genus-two curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from \(2\), and let \(X=\operatorname{Kum}(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\) be its singular quartic Kummer surface. Label its sixteen nodes by the symplectic vector space \(V=\operatorname{Jac}(C)[2]\cong\mathbb F_2^4\). A **Göpel tetrad** is an affine translate of a maximal totally isotropic two-dimensional vector subspace for the Weil pairing. There are exactly \(60\) Göpel tetrads.

For every such tetrad \(T\), write \(e_T=\sum_{v\in T}e_v\in\mathbb Z^V\), and set \(L=\sum_T\mathbb Ze_T\subseteq\mathbb Z^V\). Fix any symplectic basis with coordinates \(v=(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3)\), pairing
\[
B(v,w)=v_0w_1+v_1w_0+v_2w_3+v_3w_2 \pmod 2,
\]
and quadratic refinement \(q(v)=v_0v_1+v_2v_3\pmod2\). Then **the entire integral span** is
\[
L=\left\{z\in\mathbb Z^{V}:\ \sum_{v\in V}z_v\equiv0\pmod4,\quad
\sum_{v\in V}z_vv_i\equiv0\pmod2\ (0\le i\le3),\quad
\sum_{v\in V}z_vq(v)\equiv0\pmod2\right\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\mathbb Z^{16}/L\cong\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^5,
\qquad [\mathbb Z^{16}:L]=128.
\]
The \(16\)-by-\(60\) node–Göpel-tetrad incidence matrix has full integral rank \(16\) and nonzero Smith factors \(1\) repeated ten times, \(2\) repeated five times, and \(4\) once. Replacing \(q\) by another quadratic refinement does not change the displayed lattice: two refinements differ by a linear form, already accounted for by the four moment conditions.

## Assumptions and scope

The geometric hypothesis guarantees sixteen ordinary nodes indexed by the full reduced two-torsion group and the standard symplectic Weil pairing. The lattice \(L\) is a sublattice of the free abelian group *formally generated by the nodes*. No assertion is made that \(\mathbb Z^{16}/L\) equals a quotient of the Kummer surface's Néron–Severi lattice or of a group of algebraic cycles modulo equivalence. The claimed invariant depends only on the canonical genus-two node configuration with its alternating pairing; symplectic coordinate changes permute columns and rows and preserve the integral isomorphism type.

## Proof

**1. Enumerating the tetrads.** Every nonzero vector \(a\in V\) belongs to a three-dimensional orthogonal space \(a^\perp\). There are six vectors \(b\in a^\perp\setminus\{0,a\}\), so there are \(15\cdot6/6=15\) distinct two-dimensional totally isotropic linear subspaces: each is counted once per each of its six ordered bases. Each such subspace has four distinct cosets in \(V\). The resulting \(60\) affine subspaces are distinct because their translation directions can be recovered as the set of pairwise differences.

**2. Congruences satisfied by every generator.** Every incidence vector \(e_T\) has total coordinate sum four. Every coordinate linear functional \(v_i\) sums to zero modulo two over a two-dimensional affine \(\mathbb F_2\)-plane. Since the polarization of \(q\) is \(B\), the restriction of \(q\) to any affine translate of an isotropic subspace is affine-linear; its four values also sum to zero modulo two. Therefore \(L\subseteq K\), where \(K\) is the simultaneous kernel of the six congruences in the finding.

**3. Index of the congruence lattice.** Consider the homomorphism
\[
\Psi:\mathbb Z^V\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^5,\qquad
z\longmapsto\left(\sum_vz_v,\sum_vz_vv_0,\ldots,\sum_vz_vv_3,\sum_vz_vq(v)\right).
\]
The basis element at the zero vector maps to \((1,0,0,0,0,0)\). Differences of its basis element from the ones at the four coordinate vectors give the four independent binary linear moments with vanishing total sum modulo four. Finally, the integral vector \(e_{(1,1,0,0)}-e_{(1,0,0,0)}-e_{(0,1,0,0)}+e_{(0,0,0,0)}\) maps to the binary quadratic moment, with all five other coordinates zero. Thus \(\Psi\) is onto and its kernel \(K\) has index \(4\cdot2^5=128\).

**4. One exact maximal minor.** Order the sixteen vectors of \(V\) lexicographically as the binary strings \(0000,0001,\ldots,1111\). Order the sixty tetrads by sorting the four binary strings in each, then ordering these four-element tuples lexicographically. Use zero-based column indices. The following columns, in the displayed order, form a \(16\)-by-\(16\) matrix with determinant exactly \(-128\):

```
column index  node indices (each zero-based, lexicographic)
 11           0,6,9,15
  1           0,1,8,9
 39           3,5,10,12
 49           5,6,13,14
 36           3,4,9,14
  7           0,3,8,11
 43           4,5,12,13
 45           4,6,12,14
 23           1,6,8,15
 51           5,7,13,15
 31           2,4,11,13
 37           3,4,10,13
  3           0,2,4,6
 12           0,6,11,13
  9           0,5,10,15
 17           1,2,13,14
```

The determinant can be evaluated by the exact fraction-free Bareiss algorithm, as reproduced in the standalone verification program. Because \(L\) contains the image of these sixteen vectors, it is full-rank and \([\mathbb Z^V:L]\) divides \(128\). On the other hand \(L\subseteq K\) and \([\mathbb Z^V:K]=128\), so \([\mathbb Z^V:L]\ge128\). Equality forces \(L=K\).

**5. Integral quotient and invariant factors.** Surjectivity of \(\Psi\) and equality \(L=\ker\Psi\) identify the quotient as \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^5\). Its invariant factors together with full row rank give the stated Smith form. This proves the complete membership criterion for arbitrary integer node-weight vectors, not only the order of the quotient.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_gopel.py` with the Python standard library. The program generates all fifteen isotropic directions and sixty affine tetrads directly from \(B\), reconstructs the incidence matrix, verifies the congruences, and evaluates the specified maximal minor using exact integer arithmetic. It also independently checks binary rank \(10\), consistent with precisely six even Smith factors. The exact determinant together with the symbolic index comparison above is a finite, verifiable certificate of the full integral theorem; no random sampling or floating-point computation is involved.

## Relationship to prior work

The sixteen nodes of Jacobian Kummer quartics, their two-torsion symplectic structure, and the sixty Göpel tetrads are classical. Elsenhans and Jahnel study the Picard groups of desingularized Kummer quartics (arXiv:1109.4346, first made public 2011-09-20); their framework does not by itself state a presentation of the **formal node–Göpel tetrad incidence image**. Ren, Sam, Schrader and Sturmfels study a different matrix: the \(63\)-by-\(135\) incidence matrix of nonzero vectors and three-dimensional Lagrangians in \(\mathbb F_2^6\), associated with the genus-three Göpel variety (arXiv:1208.1229, Section 6). Neither its dimensions nor its Smith-rank assertion implies the present \(16\)-by-\(60\) integral congruence classification. He (arXiv:2401.07948, Section 2.4.1) and Dolgachev (2024, Section 5) explicitly describe the sixty genus-two Göpel tetrads and their geometric uses, but the inspected sections do not give this saturation and five-plus-one congruence theorem. The result is a lattice invariant of the Göpel **tetrads**, not the sixteen **tropes**; a Smith calculation for the latter is not a calculation for this different matrix.

## Limitations

This classification concerns the integral span in a free node-label lattice, not divisor classes modulo linear or numerical equivalence. It does not compute the full Picard or Néron–Severi lattice of any specific Kummer surface, nor the intersection form on the minimal resolution. The exact certificate is finite because the genus-two symplectic space has sixteen elements; it is not asserted for higher-dimensional Kummer varieties. Searches and inspected material did not reveal an identical historical formulation, but general code/lattice literature could contain an equivalent result under another name. Independent review of historical priority and of the proof has not been performed.
