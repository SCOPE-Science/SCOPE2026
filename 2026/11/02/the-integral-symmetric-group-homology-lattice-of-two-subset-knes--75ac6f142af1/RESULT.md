# The integral symmetric-group homology lattice of two-subset Kneser independence complexes

## Finding

For every integer \(m\geq5\), let \(G_m=\operatorname{KG}(2,m-4)\) have the two-element subsets of \([m]\) as vertices and disjointness as adjacency. Put \(X_m=\operatorname{Ind}(G_m)\), the simplicial complex of pairwise-intersecting families of two-element subsets. Let \(V_m=\mathbb Z^m\), with the coordinate-permutation action of \(S_m\), and let \(U_m=\ker(\epsilon:V_m\to\mathbb Z)\), where \(\epsilon(e_i)=1\).

The full **integral** homology representation is

\[
\widetilde H_j(X_m;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\bigwedge^3 U_m,&j=2,\\
0,&j\ne2,
\end{cases}
\]

naturally as \(\mathbb Z[S_m]\)-modules. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}H_2=\binom{m-1}{3}\), and over \(\mathbb Q\) the nonzero reduced homology is the irreducible hook Specht module \(S^{(m-3,1,1,1)}\). In particular the full \(S_m\) quotient and the \(A_m\) quotient have vanishing positive-degree **rational** homology.

For the subgroup \(C_m\) generated by the full \(m\)-cycle, the quotient retains an explicit number of two-dimensional rational classes:

\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_2(X_m/C_m;\mathbb Q)
=\frac{(m-1)(m-2)+4\mathbf1_{3\mid m}}{6}
-\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

For \(m=5,6,7,8\) this is respectively \(0,2,2,4\). The quotients are connected and have no other positive-degree rational homology.

More generally, if a permutation \(g\in S_m\) has cycle lengths \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s\), its degree-two rational homology trace is

\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_2(X_m;\mathbb Q))
=[t^3]\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1-(-t)^{\lambda_i})}{1+t}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All simplicial complexes are finite abstract complexes with geometric realizations understood. The action permutes the underlying \(m\) labels, and hence permutes the vertices (two-subsets) of \(X_m\). All exterior powers of \(U_m\) are taken over \(\mathbb Z\); the isomorphism above is an integral lattice isomorphism, not merely a rational character equality. For a finite symmetry quotient, assertions about quotient homology use rational coefficients. The infinite-family claim applies to every \(m\ge5\), without extrapolating from a bounded computation.

The same complex is \(\operatorname{VR}(\mathcal F^m_2,2)\) when \(\mathcal F^m_2\) is the set of two-subsets of \([m]\) with symmetric-difference distance, since two distinct two-subsets have distance at most two exactly when they meet.

## Proof

**1. Classify the maximal simplices.** A family of pairwise-intersecting two-subsets of \([m]\) either has a common element or is contained in the three edges of one triangle on the underlying labels. To see this, take distinct meeting pairs \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{a,c\}\). If another pair avoids \(a\), it must be \(\{b,c\}\); any pair meeting all three is one of these three. Thus every simplex lies either in the full simplex \(\Sigma_a\), whose vertices are the pairs containing \(a\), or in one exceptional two-simplex \(T_{abc}=\{\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{b,c\}\}\).

Let \(A=\bigcup_{a\in[m]}\Sigma_a\). For distinct labels \(a,b\), \(\Sigma_a\cap\Sigma_b\) is the single vertex \(\{a,b\}\), and any triple intersection is empty. The natural Mayer–Vietoris chain complex of this cover has in degrees one and zero the oriented-edge and vertex chains of the complete graph on \([m]\). Every intersection is either a point or empty, so its spectral sequence has only the zeroth homology of intersections and collapses. Naturality under label permutations identifies, integrally and equivariantly,

\[
H_1(A;\mathbb Z)\cong\ker(\partial_1:\bigwedge^2V_m\longrightarrow V_m)
=\bigwedge^2U_m,
\]

where \(\partial_1(e_a\wedge e_b)=e_b-e_a\). Also \(A\) is connected and its homology vanishes above degree one. To see the final equality integrally, choose any vector of augmentation one, so \(V_m\cong\mathbb Z\oplus U_m\) as abelian groups; contraction with \(\epsilon\) kills \(\bigwedge^2U_m\) and is an isomorphism on the complementary copy of \(U_m\).

**2. Identify the actual relative attaching homomorphism.** Every exceptional triangle \(T_{abc}\) has its entire boundary in \(A\), is maximal in \(X_m\), and is not itself in \(A\). There are no other cells of \(X_m\setminus A\). Therefore

\[
H_2(X_m,A;\mathbb Z)\cong\bigwedge^3V_m,
\qquad H_j(X_m,A;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(j\ne2).
\]

Orient \(T_{abc}\) by its vertex order \([\{b,c\},\{a,c\},\{a,b\}]\), corresponding to \(e_a\wedge e_b\wedge e_c\). This convention is equivariant, since a relabeling permutes the three pairs just as it permutes their omitted labels. Following the boundary through the three star simplices gives the oriented triangle cycle in the cover nerve:

\[
\delta(e_a\wedge e_b\wedge e_c)
=e_b\wedge e_c-e_a\wedge e_c+e_a\wedge e_b.
\]

Thus the connecting map \(\delta:H_2(X_m,A)\to H_1(A)\) is exactly contraction by \(\epsilon\), with target \(\bigwedge^2U_m\). The triangle cycles generate the entire integral cycle lattice of the complete graph (use the triangles containing a fixed label and a spanning tree). Hence \(\delta\) is surjective.

**3. Finish the integral calculation.** The long exact sequence of \((X_m,A)\) gives \(H_1(X_m)=0\) and

\[
H_2(X_m;\mathbb Z)=\ker\bigl(\delta:\bigwedge^3V_m\to\bigwedge^2U_m\bigr).
\]

Again split \(V_m\) over \(\mathbb Z\) by a vector of augmentation one. Contraction by \(\epsilon\) maps the complementary summand \(\mathbb Z\wedge\bigwedge^2U_m\) isomorphically to \(\bigwedge^2U_m\), and vanishes precisely on \(\bigwedge^3U_m\). This kernel identification does not depend on the auxiliary splitting, so it is canonical and \(S_m\)-equivariant. Relative homology and the homology of \(A\) also show vanishing in all other reduced degrees. This proves the integral statement and torsion-freeness. The rational exterior power of the standard permutation-augmentation module is the irreducible hook Specht module \(S^{(m-3,1,1,1)}\).

**4. Characters and finite quotients.** The permutation representation \(V_m\otimes\mathbb Q\) contains exactly one trivial summand, and its exterior-power generating determinant is \(\prod_i(1-(-t)^{\lambda_i})\). Dividing by \(1+t\) proves the character formula. Since \(m\ge5\), the hook is neither trivial nor the sign representation. As \(A_m\) has index two in \(S_m\), an \(A_m\)-fixed vector in an irreducible \(S_m\)-module would force a trivial or sign summand. Therefore neither full symmetric nor alternating invariants occur.

For cyclic rotations, take invariants in the two short exact sequences of rational modules

\[
0\to\bigwedge^3U_m\to\bigwedge^3V_m\xrightarrow{\delta}\bigwedge^2U_m\to0
\]

and

\[
0\to\bigwedge^2U_m\to\bigwedge^2V_m\to U_m\to0.
\]

Finite-group invariants are exact over \(\mathbb Q\), and \((U_m\otimes\mathbb Q)^{C_m}=0\). The dimension of \((\bigwedge^2V_m\otimes\mathbb Q)^{C_m}\) is \(\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor\): one contribution for every non-antipodal cyclic orbit of unordered pairs, while an antipodal pair has a sign-reversing stabilizer. Each cyclic orbit of three-subsets contributes one dimension to \((\bigwedge^3V_m\otimes\mathbb Q)^{C_m}\), since any nontrivial set stabilizer is a three-cycle, which preserves orientation. Burnside's lemma counts these orbits as

\[
\frac1m\left(\binom m3+\frac{2m}{3}\mathbf1_{3\mid m}\right).
\]

Taking the difference gives the displayed quotient formula. For a finite group acting simplicially, after barycentric subdivision the rational homology of the orbit space is the invariant rational homology; this completes the quotient statements.

## Verification

A standalone exact finite checker, `kg2_symmetry_check.py`, reconstructs every simplex, computes all simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\), and independently compares signed fixed-simplex Lefschetz traces with the proposed exterior-power character. It checks all \(120\), \(720\), and \(5040\) label permutations for \(m=5,6,7\), respectively, and all rotations for \(m=8\). The verified reduced mod-two Betti vectors have a single nonzero entry of ranks \(4,10,20,35\) in degree two at \(m=5,6,7,8\), and cyclic quotient ranks are \(0,2,2,4\). The precise output marker is `KG2_FULL_CHAIN_AND_CHARACTER_CHECKS_OK`. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the above unrestricted integral proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Barmak (first arXiv version July 2, 2010; Theorem 4.11) already proved the nonequivariant homotopy equivalence \(X_m\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{m-1}{3}}S^2\). Its proof contracts a star cluster based at a selected ground-set label; that nonequivariant wedge count is prior art and is not claimed here.

Feng and Nukala (arXiv:2302.14664, first publicly posted February 28, 2023) place the same complex in the constant-weight, symmetric-difference Vietoris–Rips setting and prove broader scale-two **nonequivariant** sphere-wedge results. Feng and Wang (arXiv:2404.10566, April 16, 2024) investigate larger Kneser-subset sizes. The new statement concerns the natural *integral permutation action on homology* and the exact rational homology of its group quotients; the previously known wedge multiplicity by itself does not identify a symmetry representation. The open-neighborhood complex of a Kneser graph is a different functor and is not interchangeable with its independence complex.

## Limitations

This gives an integral homology representation, not an equivariant homotopy classification or an integral calculation for orbit-space homology. The rational quotient statements do not assert integral acyclicity. No claim is made concerning Kneser graphs whose vertices are subsets of size at least three, nor concerning arbitrary scale parameters. An independently audited originality determination has not been performed; inaccessible or unindexed equivalent literature remains a bibliographic risk.
