For every fixed convex planar base and every fixed horizontal apex position, sufficiently tall pyramids have three distinct negative real Steiner-polynomial roots exactly when the base's isoperimetric quotient \(4\pi A/P^2\) is at most \(3/4\); otherwise they have one real root and a nonreal pair. At the sharp equality threshold, the two bounded roots separate along the real axis by an explicit universal \(h^{-1/2}\) law.
