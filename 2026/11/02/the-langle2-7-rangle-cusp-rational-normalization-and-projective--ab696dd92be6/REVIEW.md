# Review of the characteristic-seven Klein model

**Correctness (C): PASS.** Over algebraically closed characteristic-seven fields, the affine Jacobian Groebner basis has one geometric singular support point. The quartic parametrization is base-point-free, and its six quadratic products are independent, establishing an integral degree-four image and a birational normalization. Exact expansions give valuations \(2\) and \(7\), hence one branch of semigroup \(\langle2,7\rangle\) with delta invariant three. The hyperplane-section subspace has the rigid form \(\langle1,u^2,u^4+6u\rangle\); its affine stabilizer is exactly \(u\mapsto au\), \(a^3=1\). The bundled exact program reproduces critical arithmetic identities. The claim excludes stable reduction and unrestricted abstract automorphisms.

**Originality (O): PASS with residual priority risk.** The motivating 2023-03-28 Dolgachev–Martin work already describes the smooth Klein plane quartic and excludes characteristic seven from its smooth classification. The 2018 mathematical discussion already determines the singular support, and the TauCeti documentation establishes the same smoothness boundary. None of the relevant inspected statements gives this degree-four normalization, complete cusp semigroup, and exact projective automorphism group of the singular reduction. Searches of mathematical records and the accessible collection were compared by implication, including superficially similar \(A_6\) cusps and cyclic projective stabilizers on different curves. Literature indexing is incomplete; no priority guarantee is asserted.

**Value (V): PASS.** This is a complete structural description of the exceptional plane-model degeneration of a canonical highly symmetric genus-three curve. It distinguishes the arithmetic drop in geometric genus and the collapse of the projective symmetry group from the familiar smooth \(168\)-element group, and supplies a reusable normalization and exact conductor. The result is not merely a singularity detection or finite point count.

**Limitations:** The projective group is for the singular embedded special fiber. The generic smooth automorphism group cannot be compared as a canonical specialization homomorphism, and no stable model is constructed. Some historically relevant literature may be unindexed.
