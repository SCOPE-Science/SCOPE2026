# The mod-eight arithmetic Picard diagram of the Fermat quartic and its line-class defect

## Finding

For every odd rational prime \(p\), let \(X_p/\mathbb F_p\) denote the smooth Fermat quartic K3 surface
\[
X_p:\qquad x_0^4+x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4=0\subset\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb F_p}.
\]
Write \(N_p=\operatorname{NS}(X_{p,\overline{\mathbb F}_p})\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\), equipped with arithmetic Galois Frobenius \(F_p\). Let \(L_p\subseteq N_p\) be the rational space generated by the **48 classical lines** described below, even when \(p=3\) and 64 additional geometric lines exist. Then \(\dim L_p=20\). The complete arithmetic diagram is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
p\bmod 8&\dim N_p&\dim N_p^{F_p}&\dim L_p^{F_p}&\dim(N_p/L_p)^{F_p}\\\hline
1&20&20&20&0\\
3&22&12&11&1\\
5&20&8&8&0\\
7&22&12&11&1
\end{array}.
\]
More strongly, the Frobenius characteristic polynomials on \(N_p\) and \(L_p\), using the action on divisor classes (eigenvalues \(\pm1\)), are respectively
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
p\bmod8&\det(t-F_p\mid N_p)&\det(t-F_p\mid L_p)\\\hline
1&(t-1)^{20}&(t-1)^{20}\\
3&(t-1)^{12}(t+1)^{10}&(t-1)^{11}(t+1)^9\\
5&(t-1)^8(t+1)^{12}&(t-1)^8(t+1)^{12}\\
7&(t-1)^{12}(t+1)^{10}&(t-1)^{11}(t+1)^9
\end{array}.
\]
In the supersingular classes \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), the **two-dimensional missing quotient** has one Frobenius-fixed direction and one Frobenius-negated direction: \(N_p/L_p\cong\mathbb Q(+1)\oplus\mathbb Q(-1)\). Consequently the 48 classical lines miss precisely one rational divisor direction over \(\mathbb F_p\), not two. In the ordinary cases they rationally generate the entire geometric Néron–Severi space. All geometric divisor classes descend to \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The prime \(p\) is any odd rational prime, with no upper bound; all ranks are **rational** ranks, not claims of integral lattice saturation. Arithmetic Picard rank means \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}\operatorname{NS}(X_p)\), equivalently \(\dim N_p^{F_p}\) (finite-field descent). Fix a primitive eighth root of unity \(\zeta\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\). For each of the three unordered partitions \((01\mid23),(02\mid13),(03\mid12)\) of the coordinates, choose the displayed ordering and define 16 lines by
\[
x_i=\zeta^{2a+1}x_j,\qquad x_k=\zeta^{2b+1}x_l,\qquad 0\le a,b\le3.
\]
There are exactly 48 distinct classical lines, all defined over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\). Their presence and distinctness persist in characteristic three. Define \(L_p\) using only these lines, never silently replacing them with the full 112-line configuration at \(p=3\).

## Proof

**1. Exact classical-line intersection form.** Index the lines as \((k,a,b)\) with \(k=0,1,2\) and \(0\le a,b\le3\). Write their two root-exponents as \(A=2a+1\) and \(B=2b+1\), both modulo eight. Lines within the same partition meet exactly when one root parameter agrees and the other differs. For lines in different partitions, direct elimination of their four linear equations gives, in order of pairs \((0,1),(0,2),(1,2)\), the conditions
\[
A+D\equiv B+C,\qquad A+B+D\equiv C,\qquad A+B\equiv C+D\pmod8,
\]
where \((C,D)\) denotes the two root-exponents for the second line. Their self-intersections are \(-2\) by adjunction on a K3 surface; distinct meeting lines have intersection one. These explicit rules uniquely specify a symmetric integer \(48\times48\) Gram matrix \(M\), with 14 ones in every row. Exact rational Gaussian elimination gives \(\operatorname{rank}M=20\). Since each family of 16 lines sums to \(4H\), where \(H\) is the hyperplane class, the line span contains an ample class. Hodge index therefore makes its intersection form nondegenerate, and \(\dim L_p=20\) (not merely at least twenty).

**2. Frobenius on line classes.** The arithmetic Galois Frobenius sends \(\zeta^e\mapsto\zeta^{pe}\), and hence permutes line labels by
\[
(k,A,B)\longmapsto(k,pA,pB)\pmod8.
\]
Its square is the identity for every odd \(p\), and each nontrivial residue \(p\equiv3,5,7\pmod8\) has 24 two-cycles on the 48 lines. Form the orbit-sum Gram matrix \(B_p\) by summing \(M\) over pairs of permutation orbits. Rational elimination of these explicitly defined matrices yields
\[
\operatorname{rank}B_p=20,11,8,11\quad\text{for }p\equiv1,3,5,7\pmod8.
\]
Orbit sums span \(L_p^{F_p}\) in characteristic zero. This invariant subspace again contains \(H\), so its intersection pairing is nondegenerate; its dimension equals the displayed Gram rank. By \(\dim L_p=20\) and the involutive Frobenius action, the claimed \(\pm1\) multiplicities on \(L_p\) follow. The executable `verify.py` constructs all 48 labels and these matrices directly from the congruences and computes exact rational ranks. It is an exhaustive finite linear-algebra proof certificate, rather than a sampling of primes.

**3. Ordinary primes, \(p\equiv1\pmod4\).** The Hasse invariant of a smooth degree-four Calabi–Yau hypersurface is the coefficient of \((x_0x_1x_2x_3)^{p-1}\) in \((\sum x_i^4)^{p-1}\). For \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), this coefficient equals
\[
\frac{(p-1)!}{(((p-1)/4)!)^4}\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Thus \(X_p\) is an ordinary K3 surface. Its crystalline second-cohomology slopes are \(0,1^{\times20},2\), so algebraic divisor classes (all of slope one) have dimension at most 20. The classical lines already span dimension 20, hence \(N_p=L_p\), with the claimed Frobenius ranks for residues one and five modulo eight.

**4. Supersingular primes, \(p\equiv3\pmod4\).** Put \(s=(p+1)/4\), a positive integer prime to \(p\). Over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\), the degree-\((p+1)\) Hermitian surface
\[
H_p:\qquad y_0^{p+1}+y_1^{p+1}+y_2^{p+1}+y_3^{p+1}=0
\]
admits the finite dominant power map \(H_p\to X_p\), \([y_i]\mapsto[y_i^s]\), of degree \(s^3\) prime to \(p\). For \(\ell\ne p\), pullback on second \(\ell\)-adic cohomology is injective by the projection formula (push–pull equals multiplication by \(s^3\)). The Hermitian surface has
\[
\#H_p(\mathbb F_{p^2})=(p^3+1)(p^2+1),\qquad b_2(H_p)=p^3-p^2+p+1.
\]
The first number equals \(p^4+1+p^2b_2(H_p)\), saturating the Weil bound for this smooth surface. Purity and equality in the triangle inequality force every second-cohomology eigenvalue of quadratic Frobenius on \(H_p\) to be \(+p^2\). Injectivity therefore shows that every eigenvalue of \(F_p^2\) on the 22-dimensional \(H^2(X_{p,\overline{\mathbb F}_p},\mathbb Q_\ell)\) equals \(+p^2\). Hence \(F_p\) has only eigenvalues \(\pm p\) in degree two.

For \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), fourth powers and squares have identical value distributions in \(\mathbb F_p\), because \(\gcd(4,p-1)=2\). It follows that \(\#X_p(\mathbb F_p)\) equals the number of points on the split quadric \(\sum x_i^2=0\) in \(\mathbb P^3\), namely \((p+1)^2\). The K3 Lefschetz formula yields
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(F_p\mid H^2)=\#X_p(\mathbb F_p)-p^2-1=2p.
\]
The 22 degree-two eigenvalues \(\pm p\) therefore have multiplicities \(12\) and \(10\). The Katsura–Shioda supersingularity criterion for Fermat surfaces ([1], Theorem 5.1) independently gives \(\dim N_p=22\) for every such prime. Thus all degree-two cohomology is spanned by divisor classes, on which Frobenius acts semisimply, with finite order after Tate twist. Its divisor-class eigenspaces have 12 fixed and 10 negated directions. The odd-characteristic K3 Tate theorem [3] also identifies arithmetic divisor invariants with the fixed part of twisted cohomology. Subtraction of the line-span multiplicities from step two gives the announced \(1+1\) quotient. As all geometric divisor-class Frobenius eigenvalues are \(\pm1\), every class is fixed by quadratic Frobenius and descends over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using a standard Python 3 interpreter. The deterministic script constructs all 48 line labels, checks every intersection congruence, checks \(M=M^T\) and its uniform degree 14, computes \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M\), constructs the exact Frobenius orbit sums for all four odd residue classes modulo eight, and computes their exact rational ranks. It also independently enumerates projective points over \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_7\), comparing with \((p+1)^2\), and validates the formal eigenmultiplicity arithmetic. The separately executed output file `verification_output.txt` ends in `VERIFY_OK`. The infinite-prime assertions in steps three and four are mathematical deductions, **not** extrapolations from these finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Schütt–Shioda–van Luijk [1] established generation by the 48 lines for the complex Fermat quartic, explained supersingular reduction in primes congruent to three modulo four, and exhibited extra geometric divisors in characteristic three; they explicitly work throughout with *geometric* Néron–Severi groups. Degtyarev [2] likewise studies integral generation in characteristic zero. Madapusi Pera [3] supplies the general odd-characteristic Tate theorem needed to turn the cohomological Frobenius count into the arithmetic Picard ranks. Veniani [4] establishes that the characteristic-three geometric configuration has 112 lines. Neither the known geometric rank 20 or 22, the existence of 48 classical lines, the general Tate theorem, nor the classical Hermitian point count is presented as new. The assessed increment is the uniform **mod-eight arithmetic descent diagram of the classical-line span**, compared with the full rational geometric Néron–Severi representation, including the explicit one-fixed/one-negated missing quotient. Bouyer [5] gives the complex 48-line Picard lattice and computes Frobenius for other members of a quartic family using characteristic-nineteen reduction, but does not give the Fermat quartic arithmetic line-span comparison. Existing character/Jacobi-sum theory for Fermat surfaces potentially permits alternate derivations, but no identical arithmetic line-span comparison was located in the inspected material.

## Limitations

All ranks and quotient decompositions are rational: no new integral saturation claim or explicit equation for a divisor representing the missing fixed direction is proved. The argument applies only to the specific coefficientwise Fermat model over prime fields of odd characteristic, not arbitrary twists or arbitrary finite base fields. In characteristic three, the additional 64 geometric lines are **not** part of the designated 48-class span. The computation of the exact intersection ranks is finite and fully specified but is presented with an executable exact-arithmetic checker rather than a printed 48-by-48 elimination trace. The relationship to broad classical Fermat zeta/character-sum formulas remains a historical-priority risk; independent scientific validation has not occurred.
