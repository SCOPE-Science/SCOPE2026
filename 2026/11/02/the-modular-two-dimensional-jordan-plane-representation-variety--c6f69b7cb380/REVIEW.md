# Scientific review: modular two-dimensional Jordan-plane representation variety

## Correctness

**PASS.** The theorem uses the literal equation \([X,Y]=Y^2\) and distinguishes characteristic two from other characteristics. For odd characteristic, a nonzero generalized eigenvalue would yield a nonzero trace of \(Y^2\) on a spectral summand of dimension at most two, contradicting the trace of a commutator. Thus \(Y^2=0\) and the remaining solutions are commuting pairs. The rank-one nilpotent cone and its two-dimensional commuting fibers give one irreducible four-dimensional component, while \(Y=0\) gives the other. The discriminant condition identifies their exact intersection. For characteristic two, taking trace and substituting traceless coordinates gives the three complete equations. Their solution set is the union of the zero section and the absolutely irreducible hypersurface defined by the primitive linear polynomial \(a^2+bc+br+ct\). The latter is five-dimensional; its Jacobian gradient gives the singular line. Independent finite-field row reduction of the original equation and exhaustive brute checks match the stated formulas. Geometric/field claims are established by proof rather than finite sampling.

## Originality

**PASS under documented best-knowledge comparison.** Iyudu's 2008 and 2012 works address precisely the Jordan plane; both explicitly assume an algebraically closed characteristic-zero base when establishing their representation-theoretic geometry. The 2008 introduction notes, without classifying, that nonnilpotent images of \(y\) may occur in positive characteristic. Iyudu's 2012 Theorem 13.1 proves equidimensionality and components indexed by partitions in characteristic zero. The present theorem identifies a sharp failure of equidimensionality at the smallest possible modular representation dimension, gives the two components, their intersections and a singular locus, and evaluates point counts over all finite fields. G. Bourgeois, *The matrix equation AX-XA = X^α g(X) over fields or rings* (arXiv:1406.0199), investigates the exact commutator-square relation but its main result assumes characteristic zero or greater than the matrix size, and its §2 excludes characteristic two; it cannot entail the modular component classification or point counts. The published 2020 paper on the restricted Jordan plane explicitly addresses odd characteristic and a different algebraic quotient. A semantic search of publicly indexed mathematical findings returned no equivalent theorem. The conclusions do not imply absolute publication priority; non-indexed modular representation literature remains a residual risk.

## Value

**PASS.** The Jordan plane is an established representation-theoretic test algebra. The characteristic-zero equidimensionality theorem motivates asking where its geometry first changes in positive characteristic. Dimension one is trivial in all characteristics; dimension two in characteristic two is therefore the sharp first case. The new component is one dimension larger and contains generically invertible images of \(y\), reversing the nilpotent-stratum picture. The complete decomposition, singular locus and exact finite-field formula are natural invariants that can inform higher-dimensional modular studies; they are not arbitrary parameter slices. The result does not require claiming a downstream general theorem.

## Closest literature and limits

N. K. Iyudu, *The representation theory of the Jordanian algebra*, arXiv:0802.1545v1 (2008), especially introduction and §2, and N. K. Iyudu, *Representation spaces of the Jordan plane*, arXiv:1209.0746v1 (2012), especially §§2 and 13, are the closest primary sources. The latter's partition-indexed classification is scoped to characteristic zero; the present characteristic-two hypersurface is not a corollary. N. Andruskiewitsch and H. Peña Pollastri, arXiv:2002.02514 (2020), studies a restricted Jordan-plane construction in odd characteristic, not the full characteristic-two matrix equation. G. Bourgeois, arXiv:1406.0199 (2014), directly considers the commutator-square matrix equation but explicitly excludes the critical characteristic-two situation in the comparable field theorems. Searches of related published records and primary literature were not exhaustive. The assertions concern reduced varieties, not their possibly nonreduced defining schemes; classification of representations of dimension at least three remains open here.
