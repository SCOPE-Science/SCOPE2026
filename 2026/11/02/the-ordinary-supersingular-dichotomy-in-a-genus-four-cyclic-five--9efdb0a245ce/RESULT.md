# The ordinary–supersingular dichotomy in a genus-four cyclic-five pencil in characteristic seven

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(7\). For each \(a\in k\setminus\{2,-2\}\), let \(C_a\) be the smooth projective curve with affine equation
\[
y^2=x^{10}+a x^5+1.
\]
It has genus \(4\), an automorphism of order \(5\), and belongs to the hyperelliptic cyclic-five family described by van Geemen and Schütt. Its Jacobian has exactly two possible Newton polygons:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{parameter}&\text{Newton slopes}&7\text{-rank}&a\text{-number}\\\hline
 a(a^2+1)\ne0 &0^{\times4},1^{\times4}&4&0\\
 a(a^2+1)=0 &(1/2)^{\times8}&0&2.
\end{array}
\]
Thus the complete nonordinary locus on the smooth parameter line consists of precisely three geometric points, \(a=0\) and the two solutions of \(a^2=-1\). Every exceptional Jacobian is supersingular but not superspecial. The Hasse determinant in the displayed basis is \(3a^2(a^2+1)^2\), so each exceptional point occurs with multiplicity two in that determinant's zero divisor. The change of variables \(x\mapsto\xi x\) with \(\xi^5=-1\) identifies parameters \(a\) and \(-a\); no assertion that these are all parameter identifications is needed.

## Assumptions and scope

All statements are geometric over an algebraically closed field of characteristic exactly \(7\), for every parameter \(a\) except the two singular values \(\pm2\). The \(7\)-rank is the \(\mathbb F_7\)-dimension of geometric \(7\)-torsion, the \(a\)-number is \(\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha_7,\operatorname{Jac}(C_a)[7])\), and supersingular means that every Newton slope of the Jacobian equals \(1/2\). Supersingular is not synonymous with superspecial. The polynomial \(x^{10}+ax^5+1\) is squarefree exactly when \(a^2\ne4\), since for \(z=x^5\) its double roots require \(z^2+az+1=2z+a=0\); zero is never a root. As the polynomial has degree ten, every allowed curve has genus four.

## Proof

**Cartier matrix.** With the standard basis \(x^{j-1}dx/y\), \(1\le j\le4\), and \(f_a(x)=x^{10}+ax^5+1\), write \(f_a(x)^3=\sum c_nx^n\). The Cartier–Manin formula gives entries \(M_{ij}=c_{7i-j}\), up to the usual semilinear convention. The only contributing degrees in this range are \(5,10,20,25\), yielding
\[
M(a)=\begin{pmatrix}
0&3a&0&0\\
0&0&0&3(a^2+1)\\
3(a^2+1)&0&0&0\\
0&0&3a&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det M(a)=-81a^2(a^2+1)^2=3a^2(a^2+1)^2\ \text{in }k.
\]
For \(a(a^2+1)\ne0\), this matrix is invertible, and the Jacobian is ordinary. At \(a(a^2+1)=0\), the matrix has rank exactly two, hence \(a\)-number two. Moreover, its two nonzero arrows have disjoint sources and targets, so \(M(a)M(a)^{(7)}=0\), where \((7)\) denotes entrywise seventh powers. The semilinear Frobenius stable image is zero, proving \(7\)-rank zero (indeed after two iterations).

**Two genus-two quotients.** The involutions \(r:(x,y)\mapsto(x^{-1},y/x^5)\) and \(jr:(x,y)\mapsto(x^{-1},-y/x^5)\), with \(j\) the hyperelliptic involution, commute. Their invariant function fields are generated by \(u=x+x^{-1}\) together with, respectively, \(v=y(x+1)/x^3\) and \(w=y(x-1)/x^3\). The quotients therefore have equations
\[
D_a^+:\quad v^2=(u+2)(u^5-5u^3+5u+a),\qquad
D_a^-:\quad w^2=(u-2)(u^5-5u^3+5u+a).
\]
The identity \(x^5+x^{-5}=u^5-5u^3+5u\) follows by the recurrence \(T_n=uT_{n-1}-T_{n-2}\). Each involution has exactly two fixed geometric points on \(C_a\): for \(r\), the two points above \(x=1\); for \(jr\), those above \(x=-1\). The restriction \(a\ne\pm2\) guarantees that these points are distinct and that the quotient maps are separable. Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus two for both smooth quotients. Equivalently the displayed sextics have no repeated roots away from \(a=\pm2\).

The quotient pullbacks of regular one-forms have complementary two-dimensional eigenspaces under \(r\). Indeed \(r^*(x^i dx/y)=-x^{3-i}dx/y\), whereas \((jr)^*(x^i dx/y)=x^{3-i}dx/y\), for \(0\le i\le3\). Therefore the sum of the quotient pullbacks induces an isogeny
\[
\operatorname{Jac}(D_a^+)\times\operatorname{Jac}(D_a^-)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Jac}(C_a).
\]
Indeed, if \(\pi_\pm:C_a\to D_a^\pm\) are the quotient maps, the pullback–norm identities give \(\pi_+^*\pi_{+*}=1+r\) and \(\pi_-^*\pi_{-*}=1+jr\) on \(\operatorname{Jac}(C_a)\). Since the hyperelliptic involution acts as \(-1\) on its Jacobian, their sum is \([2]\). Hence the pullback-product map, composed with the product of the two norm maps, equals multiplication by two on \(\operatorname{Jac}(C_a)\). This makes it an isogeny of degree a power of two, in particular prime to \(7\). Thus the Jacobian's Newton polygon and \(7\)-rank are the unions of those of the two genus-two factors.

When \(a(a^2+1)=0\), the computed \(7\)-rank of \(\operatorname{Jac}(C_a)\) is zero, forcing each genus-two factor to have \(7\)-rank zero. For an abelian surface with \(7\)-rank zero, symmetry of Newton slopes and the denominator-multiplicity divisibility property leave only the slope multiset \((1/2)^{\times4}\): any other nonzero/nonunit slope denominator at least three would require too many slopes in a self-dual height-four polygon. Thus each factor is supersingular, and so is their product and \(\operatorname{Jac}(C_a)\). Its \(a\)-number two distinguishes it from a superspecial abelian fourfold. This proves the claimed complete dichotomy.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses only standard-library integer and finite-field arithmetic. It independently expands \(f_a^3\) by multinomial coefficients, constructs all sixteen Cartier matrix entries and its signed determinant, verifies the predicted sparse formula at all \(49\) elements of \(\mathbb F_{49}=\mathbb F_7[i]/(i^2+1)\), computes ranks over that field, and tests the two-step semilinear zero condition. Among the \(47\) smooth parameters in \(\mathbb F_{49}\), exactly \(44\) are ordinary and \(3\) are exceptional. The verifier also derives the Chebyshev recurrence underlying the two quotient equations. The proof above, not enumeration over a finite field, establishes the statement for every geometric parameter.

Run `python3 verify.py`; the final line must read `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

The family is the characteristic-seven reduction of the hyperelliptic order-five genus-four pencil written as \(y^2=(x^5-1)(x^5-\mu)\) by van Geemen and Schütt, *Two moduli spaces of abelian fourfolds with an automorphism of order five*, §3.4 (arXiv:1010.3897). Their work studies characteristic-zero moduli and notes a square \(L\)-function for this family; it does not provide the explicit mod-seven Newton stratification established here. The Cartier matrix coefficient formula is standard, for example as recalled by Harvey and Sutherland in arXiv:1410.5222. Kudo and Harashita, arXiv:1607.01114, prove the nonexistence of superspecial genus-four curves in characteristic seven; this is consistent with the present supersingular but nonsuperspecial specializations, and does not supply their locations or Newton polygons. The novelty asserted is only the complete characteristic-seven ordinary/supersingular locus, Cartier ranks, and geometric quotient explanation on this specified family; it is not a claim that the family or the general methods are new.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns this one-dimensional genus-four family, not all curves with an order-five automorphism or all supersingular genus-four curves. It describes geometric Newton slopes and \(a\)-numbers but not isomorphism classes of all exceptional fibers, their Ekedahl–Oort types beyond the stated invariants, or arithmetic zeta functions of particular field models. An equivalent unpublished or differently indexed calculation of the specializations could affect historical originality. The symbolic proof is self-contained except for standard Cartier and Newton-polygon facts; the finite-field verifier corroborates rather than replaces it. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
