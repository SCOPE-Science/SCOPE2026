# The rank-one Brandt skeleton of finite matrix partial-inner monoids

## Finding

Let \(q\) be a prime power and \(n\geq3\). Write \(S=M_n(\mathbb F_q)\), and let \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) be the inverse monoid generated by the partial inner maps of Araújo–Bentz–Kinyon–Konieczny–Malheiro–Mercier. Its nonempty one-dimensional-domain elements form two orthogonal, minimal nonzero ideals, one for rank-one idempotent lines and another for rank-one square-zero lines. Together with the unit group they form an explicitly enumerable proper submonoid.

Put
\[
 Q_n=\frac{q^n-1}{q-1},\qquad E_n=q^{n-1}Q_n,\qquad Z_n=\frac{q^{n-1}-1}{q-1}Q_n.
\]
Here a *line* means an \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear one-dimensional subspace of \(S\), including its zero matrix. The elements having exactly \(q\) matrices in their domain, together with the common zero partial map, form
\[
 I_E\simeq B_{E_n}(1),\qquad
 I_Z\simeq B_{Z_n}(\mathbb F_q^\times),\qquad I_E I_Z=I_ZI_E=\{0\}.
\]
Here \(B_m(H)\) is the Brandt semigroup of \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrix units over a group \(H\), with one adjoined zero. Each of \(I_E\) and \(I_Z\) is a minimal nonzero ideal of \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\), and there are no others.

The unit group is \(\operatorname{PGL}_n(q)\). Its union with the two ideals is a proper inverse submonoid \(\mathcal K_{n,q}\) of order
\[
 |\mathcal K_{n,q}|=1+\frac{|\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|}{q-1}
                 +E_n^2+(q-1)Z_n^2,
 \qquad |\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(q^n-q^i).
\]
In particular, \(|\mathcal K_{3,2}|=1394\) and \(|\mathcal K_{3,3}|=24714\). The formula counts this canonical submonoid, **not** all of \(\operatorname{Inn}(M_n(\mathbb F_q))\) for \(n\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope

Matrices act on column vectors of \(V=\mathbb F_q^n\). For \(g,h\in S\), define
\[
 D_{g,h}=\{X\in S:(gh)X=X=X(gh)\},\qquad
 \phi_{g,h}:D_{g,h}\longrightarrow D_{h,g},\quad X\longmapsto hXg.
\]
The inverse monoid is generated under ordinary composition of partial maps by all \(\phi_{g,h}\). The empty partial function is *not* the zero of this monoid: its actual zero is the identity map on the singleton subsemigroup \(\{0\}\). For subspaces \(U,W\le V\), write
\[
 \mathcal R(U,W)=\{X\in S:\operatorname{im}X\subseteq U,\ W\subseteq\ker X\}
                 \cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_q}(V/W,U).
\]
A nonzero rank-one line \(L\subset S\) is of *idempotent type* if it contains a nonzero idempotent, and of *square-zero type* if every element of \(L\) squares to zero. Every rank-one line has exactly one of these types.

## Proof

**1. Rectangular-domain lemma.** Put \(P=gh\), \(K=\ker(P-I)\), \(W=\operatorname{im}(P-I)\). The domain equations say precisely that \(\operatorname{im}X\subseteq K\) and \(W\subseteq\ker X\), so \(D_{g,h}=\mathcal R(K,W)\). Rank-nullity gives \(\dim K=\operatorname{codim}W=:d\), hence \(|D_{g,h}|=q^{d^2}\). The map \(hXg\) is a linear bijection from this domain to \(D_{h,g}\), as follows either by direct multiplication using \(ghX=X=Xgh\) or from the inverse map \(Y\mapsto gYh\).

More generally a rectangular-domain partial isomorphism sends \(\mathcal R(U,W)\) to \(\mathcal R(U',W')\) by \(X\mapsto aXb\), where \(a:U\to U'\) and the map induced by \(b:V/W'\to V/W\) are linear isomorphisms. These facts hold for every generator. Indeed, for the generator \(a=h\) is injective on \(K\), since \(ghx=x\) for \(x\in K\); its image is \(\ker(hg-I)\). The induced map of the quotients is likewise an isomorphism, with inverse induced by \(h\). If two such maps are composed, the intermediate rectangles intersect as
\[
 \mathcal R(U_1,W_1)\cap\mathcal R(U_2,W_2)
  =\mathcal R(U_1\cap U_2,W_1+W_2).
\]
Pulling this intersection back through the factor isomorphisms again imposes a subspace condition on the image and another on the kernel; thus the restricted domain and range are rectangles and the restricted outer factors remain isomorphisms. Induction proves this for every element of \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\). In particular every element of domain size \(q\) has domain \(\mathcal R(L,W)=\mathbb F_q A\), where \(L\) is one-dimensional, \(W\) is a hyperplane and \(A\) has rank one. Because the induced tensor-factor maps are invertible, the image is another rank-one matrix line and rank is preserved.

**2. Intrinsic types and counts.** Any nonzero rank-one matrix can be written \(A=u\lambda\), where \(0\ne u\in V\) and \(0\ne\lambda\in V^*\). Then \(A^2=\lambda(u)A\). If \(\lambda(u)\ne0\), the line has the unique nonzero idempotent \(A/\lambda(u)\). Otherwise every matrix in the line is square-zero. Semigroup isomorphisms between these line subsemigroups can only preserve type. A rank-one line is uniquely specified by the image line \(L=\mathbb F_q u\) and kernel hyperplane \(W=\ker\lambda\). There are \(Q_n\) possibilities for \(L\); of the hyperplanes, exactly \(q^{n-1}\) avoid \(L\), and exactly \((q^{n-1}-1)/(q-1)\) contain \(L\). Thus the two line counts are \(E_n\) and \(Z_n\).

**3. All maps between lines are attained.** Suppose the source line contains a rank-one idempotent \(e\). Set \(P=e\). Then \(D_{P,P}=\mathbb F_q e\). For any target rank-one idempotent \(f\), choose \(h\in\operatorname{GL}_n(q)\) with \(heh^{-1}=f\); put \(g=eh^{-1}\). Then \(gh=e\), and \(\phi_{g,h}(X)=hXeh^{-1}=hXh^{-1}\) on \(\mathbb F_qe\). This is the unique semigroup isomorphism between these two idempotent lines, since it must send the unique nonzero idempotent to the other.

For a square-zero source line \(\mathbb F_q A\), take a regular nilpotent matrix \(N\) with \(\ker N=\operatorname{im}A\) and \(\operatorname{im}N=\ker A\). Such an \(N\) exists by completing an adapted Jordan chain, using \(\operatorname{im}A\subseteq\ker A\). Set \(P=I+N\). Since \(\ker N\) is a line and \(\operatorname{im}N\) is a hyperplane, \(D_{P,P}=\mathbb F_q A\). Every pair of nonzero rank-one square-zero matrices is conjugate in \(\operatorname{GL}_n(q)\), since both have Jordan type \((2,1,\dots,1)\). For any target line and prescribed nonzero scalar on its generator, choose such a conjugator \(h\), set \(g=Ph^{-1}\), and obtain \(\phi_{g,h}(X)=hXPh^{-1}=hXh^{-1}\) on \(\mathbb F_q A\). Hence all \(q-1\) possible line isomorphisms occur.

**4. Brandt multiplication and ideals.** The composition of two maps whose domains and ranges are matrix lines is zero unless the middle lines match; if they match, the map is the corresponding unique idempotent-line isomorphism, or the product of the scalar labels in \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) for square-zero lines. Therefore the two line-map families with the common zero have the asserted Brandt multiplication and multiply each other to zero. Any partial inner map composed on either side with a line map remains a line map or becomes zero, by the rectangular-domain lemma and rank preservation. Thus these are ideals. Each Brandt semigroup is 0-simple, hence is a minimal nonzero ideal. Conversely, any nonzero partial inner map has a nonzero matrix in its rectangular domain (unless it is zero), and that rectangle contains a nonzero rank-one matrix. Restricting the map by the identity on that rank-one line produces a nonzero element of one of the two ideals. Every nonzero ideal therefore meets one of them. This excludes further minimal nonzero ideals.

**5. Units, enumeration and properness.** A generator has full domain only when \(gh=I\), which over finite-dimensional \(V\) forces \(h=g^{-1}\), giving ordinary conjugation. A product can have full domain only when every factor has full domain, so the unit group is conjugation by \(\operatorname{GL}_n(q)\), whose scalar kernel gives \(\operatorname{PGL}_n(q)\). The union of that group with the two ideals is closed under composition and inversion, disjoint except for the common zero. Counting the \(E_n^2\) idempotent-line maps and \((q-1)Z_n^2\) square-zero-line maps yields the formula. For \(n\ge3\), a rank-two projection \(P\) gives a generator with domain \(\mathcal R(\operatorname{im}P,\ker P)\) of size \(q^4\); it is neither a unit, a line map, nor zero. The skeleton is thus proper.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify.py` directly enumerates all pairs of conjugating matrices and the corresponding generator domains for \((q,n)=(2,2),(3,2),(2,3)\), comparing the resulting one-line maps with the Brandt cardinalities and counting all rank-one source lines. In the first case the counts are \((E_n,Z_n)=(6,3)\) and \(|\mathcal K|=52\); in the second they are \((12,4)\) and \(201\); in the third they are \((28,21)\) and \(1394\). It reports `VERIFY_OK`. The script handles prime fields in these tests; the proof works over every finite field. Exhausting individual generators is not an exhaustive enumeration of the whole inverse monoid of partial maps, which is not claimed.

## Relationship to prior work

Araújo, Bentz, Kinyon, Konieczny, Malheiro and Mercier define this monoid, prove the generator properties in their Section 3, and in Problem 7.9 request the description for endomorphism monoids of vector spaces. Their paper develops other semigroup families but does not state this rank-one ideal classification. An earlier preprint, arXiv:2301.04252, contained precursor work; the current result makes no claim that the underlying construction or problem originated in 2026. The two-dimensional case has a separate exact enumeration; the present theorem is restricted to every higher dimension. Neither the dimension-two special calculation nor the standard classification of rank-one matrices alone gives the required statements about all compositions of partial maps and minimal ideals.

## Limitations

The theorem describes only the rank-one skeleton and its position among the minimal nonzero ideals. It does not count or classify the higher-rank elements of the full partial inner monoid. The \(n=2\) case is used only as a computational boundary test and is not presented as a new result. Potential consequences from older general treatments of partial automorphism ideals have not been excluded by an exhaustive historical bibliography.
