# Scientific review: the reduced superspecial Hasse divisor at inert primes

## Correctness — PASS

For each prime \(p=6m+5\), the proof reconstructs the Cartier operator on all three holomorphic differentials, including its inverse-Frobenius coefficient normalization. An explicit coefficient differential equation and Lucas congruences give the factorization
\[
[z^{3m+2}](z^2+az+b)^{4m+3} = c_m b^{m+1}(a^2-4b)^{m+1}H_m(a,b).
\]
The smoothness condition makes the two off-diagonal Cartier entries vanish simultaneously. Their stable rank and kernel give exactly the \((p\text{-rank},a\text{-number})\) types \((2,1)\) and \((0,3)\). The coefficient ODE proves \(H_m(a,1)\) is squarefree and disjoint from \(a=\pm2\); the marked scaling symmetry gives \(\lceil m/2\rceil\) classes. The boundary \(p=5\) is included and its Hasse polynomial is one. The accompanying standalone program tests exact identities in 23 inert characteristics through 197; the proof is symbolic for all primes.

## Originality — PASS, with historical risk

The 2023 papers of Laga–Shnidman and Nourozi–Rahmati cover the family and general Cartier setup. The 2015 Kazemifard–Tafazolian paper covers the Fermat specialization \(a=0\) in the superspecial congruence class; that special case is not claimed new. Neither inspected primary source establishes the all-inert-prime reduced Hasse divisor, its discriminant factorization, and its complete marked-class count. Seven targeted published-record queries for family aliases, Hasse, inert primes and the source papers, plus comparisons with related earlier statements, found no dominating equivalent; a separate split-prime result has disjoint characteristic scope. A possible differently parametrized or unindexed historical calculation remains a priority risk, so the assessment is not independent novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A complete reduced Hasse locus is a natural arithmetic stratum of a genus-three family with a quaternionic Prym. The theorem supplies an exact degree, simple-root structure, Cartier ranks, and the complete marked superspecial class census uniformly over all inert characteristics. These are not implied by merely knowing that one Fermat model is superspecial.

## Scope and limitations

The field is algebraically closed, the characteristic is \(5\) modulo \(6\), and the curves must be smooth. Marked classes do not mean unmarked isomorphism classes. No finite-field rational class frequencies or full non-superspecial Newton polygons are asserted. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
