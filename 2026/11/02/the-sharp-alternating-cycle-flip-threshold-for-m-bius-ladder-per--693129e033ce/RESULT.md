# The sharp alternating-cycle flip threshold for Möbius-ladder perfect matchings

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(M_{2n}\) denote the cubic graph with vertices \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), perimeter edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and antipodal chord edges \(\{i,i+n\}\), indices modulo \(2n\). For every even positive integer \(L\), join two perfect matchings in \(\mathcal R_{\le L}(M_{2n})\) when their symmetric difference is a **single** alternating simple cycle of length at most \(L\). The sharp cutoff at which this graph becomes connected is
\[
L_{\mathrm{conn}}(n)=\begin{cases}4,&n\equiv0\pmod2,\\ n+1,&n\equiv1\pmod2.\end{cases}
\]
For odd \(n\ge5\), let \(P_0,P_1\) be the two alternating perfect matchings formed entirely of perimeter edges. Every flip incident to either has cycle length at least \(n+1\). At the first admissible length, each has **exactly** \(n\) neighbours, each containing precisely one antipodal chord. Thus exactly \(2n\) exchange edges first connect these two configurations to the remaining connected configurations.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has \(2n\) distinct labelled vertices and \(3n\) edges, with \(n\ge4\). An alternating-cycle flip replaces the matching edges on one simple even cycle with the complementary cycle edges; no simultaneous flips on disjoint cycles are permitted. The statement concerns length cutoffs, not the number of flips or the diameter. The smallest graph \(M_6\) is excluded: it is \(K_{3,3}\) and has additional short cycles. For \(n\ge4\), cycles of length four are exactly the \(n\) twisted ladder faces.

## Proof
**1. Cyclic-tiling decomposition.** Regard antipodal pairs as \(n\) cyclic columns. In any perfect matching containing at least one chord, an unselected chord forces both of its column vertices to be matched along the two perimeter edges connecting that column to an adjacent column. Propagating from a chosen chord gives a unique tiling of the column cycle into singleton columns (selected chords) and adjacent paired columns (two perimeter edges), with the final paired column wrapping through the twist. Paired columns can be encoded by a subset of edges of \(C_n\) with no adjacent selected edges. Conversely, such an independent edge set produces a unique perfect matching. Each selected cycle edge represents two perimeter edges, and each uncovered column represents one chord. When \(n\) is even, the two alternating all-perimeter configurations correspond to the two complete domino tilings of \(C_n\); when \(n\) is odd, they are two additional configurations not represented by any cyclic domino tiling. These are all the possibilities, because deleting all chord edges leaves exactly the perimeter cycle, which has precisely two perfect matchings.

**2. Connectivity by four-cycle flips in the tiling sector.** Each valid addition or deletion of one selected cycle edge replaces exactly the two chords of adjacent columns by their two connecting perimeter edges, or vice versa. This is exactly an alternating four-cycle. From every nonempty independent set, repeatedly delete a selected edge to reach the empty set (the all-chord matching). Consequently all cyclic-tiling configurations form one connected four-cycle-flip component. For even \(n\), they exhaust the perfect matchings, proving \(L_{\mathrm{conn}}(n)=4\), as no alternating simple cycle has fewer than four edges. For odd \(n\), the two all-perimeter matchings admit no alternating four-cycle flip because each four-cycle contains a chord not selected by them; they are outside this component.

**3. Odd-chord winding obstruction.** Let \(n\) be odd and let \(C\) be any simple cycle of \(M_{2n}\) containing an odd number \(r\) of antipodal chord edges. Orient \(C\) and represent each traversal of a perimeter edge by \(+1\) or \(-1\) in \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), and each traversal of a chord by \(\pm n\). The sum of increments must vanish modulo \(2n\). Since \(r\) is odd, the signed perimeter increment sum is congruent to \(n\) modulo \(2n\); its absolute value is at least \(n\). Therefore \(C\) contains at least \(n\) perimeter edges and has length at least \(n+r\ge n+1\).

For odd \(n\), any matching in the cyclic-tiling component has an odd positive number of chords: a domino covers two columns and the number of singleton columns is \(n-2d\). Each all-perimeter matching contains zero chords. The symmetric difference between an all-perimeter matching and a cyclic-tiling matching therefore contains an odd number of chords. If this difference is one alternating cycle, step 3 forces its length to be at least \(n+1\). This proves the lower-bound cutoff: every flip of length at most \(n\) leaves each all-perimeter matching isolated.

**4. Exactly \(n\) first bridges per extreme matching.** Fix an all-perimeter matching \(P\). For each of the \(n\) antipodal chords \(e=\{u,u+n\}\), there are two perimeter semicircles of length \(n\) from \(u\) to \(u+n\). Because \(n\) is odd, precisely one semicircle, as traversed from \(u\), alternates with \(P\) starting and ending on a matching perimeter edge. Together with \(e\), it forms a \(P\)-alternating simple cycle of length \(n+1\). Flipping it replaces its \((n+1)/2\) matching perimeter edges by \((n-1)/2\) nonmatching perimeter edges and \(e\), and creates a valid matching containing exactly one chord. Different choices of \(e\) create different neighbours. Conversely, an alternating cycle of length exactly \(n+1\) from \(P\) must contain an odd positive number \(r\) of chords by the argument above; since its length is at least \(n+r\), necessarily \(r=1\). It must therefore consist of one chord and a shortest semicircle of length \(n\), for which only one semicircle is alternating. There are exactly \(n\) such flips from each extreme matching. Every destination lies in the connected cyclic-tiling component, proving that \(\mathcal R_{\le n+1}\) is connected. No edge between the two extremes appears at this cutoff because their symmetric difference is the entire perimeter \(2n\)-cycle, longer than \(n+1\) for \(n\ge5\). Therefore these are exactly \(2n\) new bridges incident to the extreme matchings, completing the proof. \(\square\)

## Verification
Two standalone scripts are included. `direct_checker.py` enumerates every perfect matching from the graph adjacency representation for each \(4\le n\le13\), detects single-cycle symmetric differences, computes connected components at the stated cutoffs and counts the first bridges. `structural_checker.py` independently builds matchings from cyclic independent edge sets for \(4\le n\le18\) and tests the alternating one-chord semicircle flips from each odd-width perimeter matching. Both terminate with explicit PASS markers when all assertions hold. These tests corroborate finite instances; the preceding proof establishes all infinitely many orders.

## Relationship to prior work
The Lucas-cube model for the four-cycle exchange component, and the existence of two frozen extra configurations for odd widths, are **background**, not part of the originality assertion. Lucas cubes and their degree enumerations have a separate established literature; matching counts on Möbius ladders were also known. General unrestricted alternating-cycle reconfiguration does not determine the minimum **allowed cycle length** at which connectivity first emerges. The contribution here is the sharp parity-dependent threshold \(4\) versus \(n+1\), the winding obstruction establishing the lower bound, and the exact count of first-length bridges.

## Limitations
No general theorem for other twisted ladder families or non-simple alternating exchanges is asserted. The total number of perfect matchings, Lucas-cube identification and four-cycle connectivity decomposition are not claimed as new. The result does not calculate shortest numbers of flips. A historical equivalent in specialized dimer-resonance or matching-switch literature remains possible, particularly in sources whose full texts were unavailable for inspection. Independent scholarly validation has not been performed.
