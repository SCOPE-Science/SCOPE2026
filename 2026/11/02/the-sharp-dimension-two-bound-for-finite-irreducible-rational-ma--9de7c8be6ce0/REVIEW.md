# Scientific review: sharp dimension-two irreducible matrix-semigroup bound

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces all finite irreducible rational \(2\times2\) semigroups to the rank-two versus rank-one cases of their completely \(0\)-simple minimal ideal. It bounds their full-rank subgroup by \(12\), derives a signed rank-two pairing matrix from the scalar maximal subgroup \(\{\pm1\}\), proves the sharp \(ab\le9\) incidence lemma, and shows every remaining rank-one member uses one of at most \(18\) normalized signed outer products. The explicitly constructed equality semigroup is closed, irreducible and has \(31\) elements. The exact checker verifies all \(961\) products and the finite incidence-pattern enumeration. The universal proof is symbolic and uses established finite-semigroup structure theorems.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

Kiefer--Ryzhikov (arXiv:2601.01236v2, Sections 3, 4, 8 and 9) establishes the general upper bound \(3^{n^2}\), exhibits asymptotic constructions and explicitly leaves the exact maximum question open. Steinberg (arXiv:2601.03206v1, Section 2) proves the same general upper bound using completely \(0\)-simple ideals. Their full accessible texts do not state a sharp dimension-two value. The finite-group and Rees ingredients are standard, but their combination with the signed incidence lemma and equality example resolves an exact natural invariant not implied by those general results. Searches for the exact value, equivalent rank-one construction and aliases in mathematical records disclosed no matching theorem. Conceivably a historical dimension-two classification not located in these sources yields the same bound; no claim of absolute priority is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The motivating 2026 paper singles out exact maximum sizes as an intriguing problem. Determining \(31\) solves its first nontrivial dimension and provides a genuinely sharp numerical refinement of \(81\), with an explicit extremal semigroup and an intrinsic reason for the bound: the signed-incidence product of row and column projective directions is at most \(9\). This is a structural extremal result, not an arbitrary sampled numerical invariant.

## Limits and closest sources

The theorem does not classify all maximizers, higher dimensions, or infinite semigroups. The related published results concern general exponential bounds, finiteness/diameter bounds, and standard minimal-ideal structure; none inspected supplies the exact dimension-two maximum. The historical-priority caveat remains. No external or independent audit is represented.
