# The sharp fifth-layer odd-prime partition congruence and its ternary exception

## Finding

For an integer \(m\ge 2\), let \(b_m(N)\) count partitions of \(N\) whose parts are powers of \(m\), including the empty partition of zero. For every odd prime \(p\) and every integer \(n\ge1\), define \(A_p(n)=b_p(p^5n-1)\). Then

\[
\gcd_{n\ge1} A_p(n)=\begin{cases}3^5,&p=3,\\p^4,&p\ge5.\end{cases}
\]

More precisely, put \(B_p(q)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_p(n)q^n=\prod_{j\ge0}(1-q^{p^j})^{-1}\). At the exceptional prime the complete progression has the generating function

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}b_3(3^5n-1)q^n
=3^5\,\frac{q(23+137q+80q^2+3q^3)}{(1-q)^5}\,B_3(q).
\]

For every odd prime \(p\ge5\), the first nonzero \(p\)-adic digit of the progression is explicitly

\[
\frac{b_p(p^5n-1)}{p^4}\equiv
8^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)b_p(j)\pmod p,
\]

where \(8^{-1}\) denotes the inverse of eight modulo \(p\). In particular, the congruence for \(p=3\) improves the ordinary fifth-layer power by one factor of three, while for every larger odd prime the ordinary fourth power is sharp.

## Assumptions and scope

All products are identities of formal power series over the integers; no analytic convergence is required. The integer \(n\) is at least one and \(p\) is an odd prime. Every partition is counted without order, with unrestricted multiplicities of powers of \(p\). The gcd is the positive gcd of an infinite family; showing universal divisibility and one sharp member determines it.

## Proof

Introduce the extraction operator \(U_m(\sum_{a\ge0}c_aq^a)=\sum_{a\ge0}c_{ma}q^a\), and \(h_j(q)=q/(1-q)^{j+1}\) for \(j\ge0\). The functional equation \(B_m(q)=(1-q)^{-1}B_m(q^m)\), together with \(U_m(f(q)g(q^m))=U_m(f(q))g(q)\), gives the following exact recursion:

\[
H_0=h_0,\qquad H_{r+1}=U_m\!\left(\frac{H_r}{1-q}\right),\qquad
\sum_{n\ge1}b_m(m^{r+1}n-1)q^n=H_r(q)B_m(q).
\]

Indeed, the first extraction of \(qB_m(q)=q(1-q)^{-1}B_m(q^m)\) is \(U_m(q/(1-q))B_m(q)=h_0B_m(q)\); subsequent extractions yield the displayed recurrence. The extraction identity \(U_m(h_j)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\alpha_{m,j}(i)h_i\) is determined by the polynomial identity

\[
\binom{mn+j-1}{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\alpha_{m,j}(i)\binom{n+i-1}{i}.
\]

Apply this identity four times, starting with \(H_0=h_0\). Straight polynomial expansion gives the following **exact** (not asymptotic) coefficients in \(H_4=\sum_{j=1}^4 C_j(m)h_j\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_1(m)&=-\frac{m^4(m-1)(m+1)(m^4-2m^3-m+3)}{24},\\
C_2(m)&=\frac{m^5(m-1)(7m^4+m^3-4m^2-7m-3)}{12},\\
C_3(m)&=-\frac{m^7(m-1)(3m^2+2m+1)}{2},\\
C_4(m)&=m^{10}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These are integer-valued at every integer \(m\ge2\), since they arise from the integral extraction operator. They can also be verified formally by expanding the preceding binomial-polynomial identity; the accompanying exact-rational checker reconstructs the four transformations over \(\mathbb Q[m]\) without relying on particular numerical bases.

For \(p\ge5\), the denominators \(2,12,24\) are units modulo \(p\). Consequently \(C_1(p)\) is a multiple of \(p^4\), the other three coefficients are multiples of \(p^5\), and

\[
\frac{C_1(p)}{p^4}\equiv\frac18\pmod p.
\]

As \([q^k]h_1=k\) for \(k\ge1\), multiplying by \(B_p\) yields both universal divisibility by \(p^4\) and the formula for the quotient modulo \(p\). Taking \(n=1\), where \(b_p(0)=1\), yields \(A_p(1)/p^4\equiv 8^{-1}\not\equiv0\pmod p\). This proves the exact \(p\)-adic exponent for every odd prime \(p\ge5\).

At \(p=3\) the four exact coefficients become

\[
(C_1(3),C_2(3),C_3(3),C_4(3))=(-729,21627,-74358,59049).
\]

Each is divisible by \(3^5\). On putting the four \(h_j\) terms over the common denominator \((1-q)^5\), their numerator is exactly \(3^5q(23+137q+80q^2+3q^3)\). Therefore every \(A_3(n)\) is divisible by \(3^5\). The first two coefficients are \(A_3(1)=b_3(242)=3^5\cdot23=5589\) and \(A_3(2)=b_3(485)=3^5\cdot275=66825\), so their gcd is exactly \(3^5\). This also confirms sharpness of the exceptional modulus.

For completeness, **the asserted gcd is an integer gcd, not just a prime-adic content statement**. Since \(B_p(0)=1\) and \(B_p(q)^{-1}\in\mathbb Z\llbracket q\rrbracket\), multiplying a zero-constant-term integer series by \(B_p\) preserves the ideal generated by all its coefficients. Further, the matrix of coefficients of \(q^1,\ldots,q^4\) in \(h_1,\ldots,h_4\) is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1\\2&3&4&5\\3&6&10&15\\4&10&20&35
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is one. The coefficient content of \(H_4\) is therefore exactly \(\gcd(C_1(p),C_2(p),C_3(p),C_4(p))\). Because \(C_4(p)=p^{10}\), this gcd is a pure power of \(p\); the valuation calculations above identify it as \(p^4\) or \(3^5\) respectively. This proves the claimed integer-gcd classification.

## Verification

The bundled `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It computes the extraction transforms as exact bivariate polynomials in the summation index and the base, verifies **all polynomial coefficients** in the displayed \(C_j\) identities, then checks the specialized ternary rational numerator. Independently, it computes the partition numbers by the recurrence \(b_m(N)=b_m(N-1)+\mathbf1_{m\mid N}b_m(N/m)\) for \(N\ge1\), with \(b_m(0)=1\), and compares the first eight progression values for each of \(m=3,5,7,11\) against the coefficient identity and congruences. Run `python3 verify.py` from a directory containing that file. Its expected output is `VERIFY_OK`. The infinite proof is the formal-series and polynomial argument above, not extrapolation from the finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Rødseth and Sellers (2001), Theorem 1, imply \(b_p(p^5n-p)\equiv0\pmod{p^4}\) for odd primes. Their own elementary identity \(b_p(pN-1)=\cdots=b_p(pN-p)\) shows that this is the **same input progression** as the one here, but the cited congruence is weaker at three and does not determine the normalized quotient for larger primes. Żmija (2024), Theorem 1.3, supplies a general congruence for \(M\)-ary partitions and develops the extraction-operator machinery, including formulas through three extraction steps. The operator itself and generic recurrence are prior art. The contribution established here is the explicit fourth-step polynomial, the previously unreported sharp prime-by-prime fifth-layer classification, the extra ternary factor, and the normalized quotient law. Sun and Zhang (2017) provide a general enumeration formula depending on base digits, not this sharp fifth-layer residue law. Ulas and Żmija (2018) study higher-color partition powers at specific exponents, not the uncolored case considered here.

## Limitations

The statement concerns the fixed fifth power of the base and odd primes, not arbitrary exponents or composite bases. It does not prove that no historical source contains an implicit specialization of these formulas. In particular, the finite-depth computation is tightly related to existing operator methods, and an earlier explicit specialized congruence remains a bibliographic risk. The coefficient identity and proof are independently checkable, but no independent scientific audit has been performed.
