# Review of the Latin-square incidence depth classification

## Correctness

PASS. The central one-point extension lemma is proved by a complete sort-distribution case split for up to three selected elements, including equality repetitions. Its fresh-element requirement is exactly four per sort. Duplicator's four-round argument follows. For small orders, normalization yields uniqueness up to isotopy and straightforward row-counting sentences distinguish adjacent order classes. The rank-five separator is explicitly displayed and verified in the cyclic and Klein-four tables. The two standalone checks independently validate the combinatorial boundary; the infinite claim does not depend on their finite outputs.

## Originality

PASS, with priority uncertainty. Searches included the three-sorted representation, transversal-design and orthogonal-array aliases, four-round first-order equivalence, and one-diagonal repeated-symbol rectangles; semantic searches of research records yielded Latin-square isotopy counts, autotopism spectra, and intercalate results, but no covering bounded-depth theorem. Full-text inspection of McKay--Meynert--Myrvold (2006), Devillers--Hall (2006), and Cavenagh--Greenhill--Wanless (2008) found results on isotopy enumeration, group-action orbit rank, and row-cycle statistics respectively, not the asserted logical equivalence and sharp separation. The finite-order isotopy result by itself does not imply quantifier-rank equivalence. Unindexed earlier model-theory discussions of transversal designs remain a priority risk.

## Value

PASS. This is a complete, natural logical classification for the entire infinite family of finite Latin squares in their row-column-symbol representation. It shows that four-round first-order logic forgets all isotopy distinctions beyond the size-three boundary, despite unbounded order and rapidly growing isotopy diversity. A concrete five-round formula detects information hidden at four rounds; the separation uses a subtle distinction between quantifier nesting depth and the number of quantifier occurrences. The theorem concerns exact logical expressiveness, not merely enumeration of small Latin squares.

## Scope and caveats

The result is specific to the three-sorted ternary incidence signature. It neither identifies the sorts nor studies the quasigroup's one-sorted multiplication function. The rank-five part gives one separating pair, not a general rank-five classification. Literature priority remains qualified; external independent audit has not been performed.
