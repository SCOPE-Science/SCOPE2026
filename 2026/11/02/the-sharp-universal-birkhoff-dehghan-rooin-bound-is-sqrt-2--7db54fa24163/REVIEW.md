# Review of the sharp universal angular/skew-angular comparison

## Correctness — PASS
The definitions and quantifiers agree with the normalized Birkhoff-restricted ratio in Du–Hu–Zhang–Liu, arXiv:2609.27754v1, Definition 1.1 and Corollary 2.4. For every normalized ordered pair \(u\perp_B v\) and all \(s>0\), \(d=\|su-v\|\ge s\) follows immediately by rescaling the defining Birkhoff inequality; reverse triangle yields \(d\ge|s-1|\). The triangle inequality bounds the numerator separately on \(s\le\tfrac12\), \(\tfrac12\le s\le1\), and \(s\ge1\). The large-scale coefficient is certified algebraically by \(32s^3-27(2s-1)^2=(2s-3)^2(8s-3)\). The square-plane witness at \(s=1/2\) exactly saturates \(\sqrt2\), and an independent norm-one supporting functional supplies the lower bound. The standalone exact-rational checker passed 3,510 assertions; the analytical proof, not enumeration, establishes universality.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge review)
The central prior statement is Proposition 2.5 of Du–Hu–Zhang–Liu, which states the valid but nonoptimal upper inequality \(\operatorname{DR}_B(X)\le2\) and says \(2\) can be approached in square-like planes (its Example 4.4). The new bound \(\sqrt2\) **strictly contradicts the asserted sharpness**, rather than following from the stated proposition. Their Corollary 2.4 supplies a general scale identity but not the sharp estimate; our split-scale geometric argument is additional. Fu–Li (2024), DOI 10.1007/s43034-024-00341-0, studies an unrestricted constant, and Qi et al., arXiv:2602.13974, investigate other Birkhoff-skew constants; neither of those statements entails this precise bound. Targeted source-term, normalization, square-plane, and equivalent-formulation searches among mathematical records gave no equivalent or stronger result. The full 23-page Fu–Li 2024 journal text was obtained and inspected; it consistently quantifies over unrestricted nonzero vector pairs rather than Birkhoff-orthogonal pairs. Potential still-older synonymous inequalities remain a priority risk; absence from search cannot establish historical priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The precise sharp universal constant for the newly defined \(\operatorname{DR}_B\) is a central question in the first systematic 2026 treatment. Improving \(2\) to \(\sqrt2\) resolves that question completely in all dimensions and exposes an incorrect published extremal interpretation, not merely a small coefficient improvement or a selected example. Its proof requires only two norm estimates intrinsic to directional Birkhoff orthogonality. A particularly simple isometric two-coordinate witness shows that the same sharp value occurs in all real cubes. This yields a natural sharp structural boundary for later geometric estimates. It does not determine all extremizing spaces or any stability rate.

## Closest literature and limitations
Primary text inspected in its entirety for the definition, bounds, example and linked theoretical discussion: J. Du et al., arXiv:2609.27754v1, especially pp. 5–7, 16–17. Also inspected the relevant definitions of J. Qi et al., arXiv:2602.13974, and the full 23-page Fu–Li (2024) article. The latter consistently treats the unrestricted angular/skew-angular comparison. The result concerns real, ordered Birkhoff-orthogonal pairs and cannot be substituted for the unrestricted Dehghan–Rooin inequality. The finite checker is not a proof of infinite-dimensional quantifiers.
