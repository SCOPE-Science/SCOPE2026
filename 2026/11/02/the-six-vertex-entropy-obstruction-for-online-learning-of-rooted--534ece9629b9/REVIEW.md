# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Exhaustive enumeration proves the smallest-order and uniqueness statements by two complete rooted-tree generators and two independent exact Littlestone algorithms. All 37 rooted isomorphism classes through six vertices are matched. The two exceptional upper bounds also follow from explicit first-query positive-branch counts, and the connected infinite family follows from product additivity plus the one-concept augmentation bound. The program and expected census are embedded and replayable.

## Originality
PASS under inspected accessible comparisons. General computational-hardness work (Manurangsi–Rubinstein 2017), tree-dimension bounds in the binary mistake-tree sense (Walker 2022), and graph-geodesic convex learning bounds (Thiessen–Gärtner 2022) do not state the exact rooted-tree-ideal six-vertex obstruction or its two shapes. Available related records for weak-order ideals and two-chain interleavings concern different posets; neither implies this finite rooted-tree classification. Residual historical priority risk remains from less accessible work on monotone functions and directed cuts, notably Gärtner–Garriga 2007.

## Value
PASS. The entropy upper bound is a natural universal benchmark for the optimal online mistake complexity. The theorem determines the **first** failure on an intrinsic and extensively used family of closure systems, identifies its complete minimal obstruction class, and propagates it to an unbounded entropy deficit even for connected rooted trees. Neither the cutoff nor the infinite-family defect is an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Scope and limitations
The census is exact only through six vertices, and the connected infinite-family dimension is bounded between two adjacent integers. The numerical claims depend on complete finite enumeration with source code, not an unbounded computational extrapolation. This review is not an independent audit or a proof of absolute priority.
