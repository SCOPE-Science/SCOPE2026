# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact characteristic-three polynomial factorization and exhaustive component-intersection calculation give six nodes and four order-two tangencies. The local normalization-unit calculations yield six independent multiplicative and four additive parameters for the identity Picard group. This implies the all-extension point counts and zeta formula. Direct computations over \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_9\), and \(\mathbb F_{27}\) corroborate, but do not replace, the structural proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The 2005 Inoue–Kato preprint presents the same classical equation but explicitly excludes characteristic \(3\). Giulietti et al. (arXiv:1805.06317) identify its reducibility at \(3\) without computing the five-component configuration or generalized Picard group in the inspected passages. Dolgachev–Farb–Looijenga discuss a different characteristic-zero five-conic \(K_5\) degeneration. Closely related published characteristic-seven investigations of the Wiman–Edge pencil concern smooth curves rather than this special fiber. Searches for equivalent formulations, stronger statements, exact tables, and implications did not locate coverage. Older unindexed sources remain a priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This natural bad-prime degeneration of a distinguished genus-six curve has a complete weighted \(K_5\) component graph, precisely identified singularities, and a split generalized Picard group of dimension \(10\). This gives all finite-extension point counts and distinguishes the arithmetic and geometric structure hidden behind the known statement of reducibility.

**Limits.** The group is \(\operatorname{Pic}^0\) of the specified plane-model special fiber, not a Néron-model special fiber or stable-reduction computation. Historic originality is qualified by source access.
