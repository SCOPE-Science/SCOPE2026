# Scientific review: square-zero Heisenberg derivation cone

## Correctness — PASS
Every ordinary derivation is uniquely a conformally symplectic quotient map, a central covector and a scalar central action. Square-zero forces the scalar to vanish and imposes a symplectic square-zero quotient and annihilation of its image by the central covector. Its isotropic image, nonsingular symmetric-form realization, central covector fibers and full-rank stratification give the stated count. Lagrangian incidence gives the reduced irreducible rational variety, and the first case is a cubic rational normal scroll. Independent direct enumeration of Lie derivations over finite fields and symmetric-form matrices corroborated the formulas. The geometry and universal formulas depend on proof, not finite experiments.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual risk
Feldvoss–Siciliano–Weigel, arXiv:1102.2629, study **outer restricted** square-zero derivations; this does not imply the all-ordinary-derivation cone. Barnea–Melnikov, arXiv:1509.06008, study square-zero Borel orbits in a symplectic **nilradical** over the complex numbers, omitting the Heisenberg central-functional fibers. La Rosa–Mancini, arXiv:2301.11058, provide ambient derivation descriptions for two-step nilpotent algebras, not the nonlinear square-zero rank census or cone geometry. Comparable finite Heisenberg invariants and evolution-algebra square-zero-derivation spectra do not imply this claim. The symplectic isotropic-plane and nonsingular symmetric-form factors are classical and not separately novel. A hidden equivalent derivation-cone computation in older representation literature remains possible; no exhaustive priority certification is claimed.

## Value — PASS
The square-zero locus of derivations is an intrinsic and motivated invariant, and this is an infinite-dimensional-parameter family with a complete rank census and geometric classification. The central covector condition distinguishes it from a plain symplectic square-zero matrix problem; the smallest case gives a recognizable smooth cubic scroll and an explicit affine singularity. These conclusions supply natural geometric and arithmetic invariants rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limits and reproducibility
The proof concerns reduced cones and labelled ordinary derivations; it does not prove claims about nonreduced schemes, restricted derivations, conjugacy orbits, or general singular loci. The Python standard-library checker runs complete finite enumerations only for the documented small fields and dimensions. Independent review is outstanding.
