# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. For real exponents different from two, the strict scalar Clarkson sign forces any distinct nonzero isometry columns to have disjoint row supports; conversely unit disjoint columns give norm-preserving maps. With exactly one spare output row, each matrix is either a signed-injection vertex or lies on a unique open edge with one doubled column. The finite graph's endpoint attachment map is a continuous bijection from compact graph to Hausdorff matrix space, hence a homeomorphism. There are \(2^{n-1}n!\) vertices, \(2^{n-1}(n-1)n!\) edges, and the explicitly described signed blank-row swaps connect every vertex. The Betti and fundamental-group formula follows from a spanning tree. The exponent change is an explicit entrywise homeomorphism. The finite checker confirms graph counts and norm equations in sampled dimensions, without being used to infer the universal case.

## Originality

PASS on the accessible evidence, with residual priority risk. The closest source, Dalfollo-Daley et al., arXiv:2610.32615v1, Theorem 3.18 and Proposition 4.5, classifies matrices and fixed-support strata, but does not identify the total space after closures of different strata are glued; consequently it does not prove connectedness or the global Betti-number formula. Phillips, arXiv:1201.4196v1, Lemma 6.22, proves homotopy rigidity for *surjective* isometries, not the one-spare-coordinate case in which rows can split and vanish. Searches for global isometry-space topology and signed-injection graphs did not reveal a covering result. An older result in combinatorial guise may still imply the theorem; absence from searched sources is not proof of novelty.

## Value

PASS. The result determines a natural complete topological invariant (indeed, the entire homotopy type and free fundamental-group rank) for the first nonsurjective dimension gap in real finite-dimensional \(\ell^p\) isometry theory. This global problem is not answered by a census of fixed-support components: the crucial new issue is which of those components become connected when coefficients vanish. It reveals a sharp transition from isolated signed permutations in the equal-dimension case to a connected graph with rapidly growing first Betti number after one extra row.

## Scope and limitations

The proof is confined to real linear maps and exponents \(1\le p<\infty\) other than two; the complex, Hilbertian, supremum-norm, and higher-codimension cases require distinct analysis. The supplementary checker supplies finite corroboration only. A published equivalent theorem under the language of signed partial injections or configuration graphs is possible. Independent review has not been undertaken.
