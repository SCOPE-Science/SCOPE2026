# The triadic Fano scheme of lines on the Fermat quartic

## Finding

Put \(R_0=\mathbb Z_3\), \(R=\mathbb Z_3[i]\) with \(i^2=-1\), and \(k=R/(3)=\mathbb F_9\). Let
\[
X=V(x_0^4+x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4)\subset\mathbf P^3_{R_0},
\]
and let \(\mathcal F=\operatorname{Hilb}^{t+1}(X/R_0)\), the relative Fano scheme of embedded lines. Then the following isomorphisms of finite schemes hold (noncanonically):
\[
\boxed{\mathcal F\times_{R_0}R\cong
 \coprod_{48}\operatorname{Spec}R\;\sqcup\;
 \coprod_{64}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_9,}
\]
\[
\boxed{\mathcal F\cong
 \coprod_{24}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z_3[i]\;\sqcup\;
 \coprod_{8}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_3\;\sqcup\;
 \coprod_{28}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_9.}
\]
The 48 geometric generic-fiber lines extend uniquely over \(R\); the remaining 64 special-fiber lines cannot lift even to \(R/(9)\). All 112 geometric characteristic-three points of \(\mathcal F\) are reduced. Among the 64 additional lines, eight are defined over \(\mathbb F_3\) and 56 have minimal field of definition \(\mathbb F_9\). The generic-fiber count 48 and special-fiber count 112 are classical and are **not** asserted as new; the finding concerns the complete relative scheme, its order-nine lifting obstruction, and Galois descent.

## Assumptions and scope

The equation is the *fixed primitive Fermat equation* over the complete discrete valuation ring \(R_0=\mathbb Z_3\). We use embedded lines of Hilbert polynomial \(t+1\), not arbitrary rational curves on the K3 surface. The unique unramified quadratic extension of \(R_0\) is \(R\); its residue field is \(k=\mathbb F_9\). A line in \(\mathbf P^3\) corresponds to a two-dimensional vector subspace of a four-dimensional vector space. All fiber counts are geometric unless a field of definition is specified.

The surface \(X/R_0\) is smooth: its four partial derivatives are \(4x_j^3\), which cannot vanish simultaneously at a projective point in either residue characteristic three or characteristic zero.

## Proof

**1. Every special-fiber line is defined over \(\mathbb F_9\), and there are 112.** Work first over an algebraic closure \(\bar k\) of \(\mathbb F_3\). If \(W=\langle v,w\rangle\subset\bar k^4\) parametrizes a line on \(X_{\bar k}\), expansion of \(\sum_j(sv_j+tw_j)^4\) shows that
\[
\sum_jv_j^4=\sum_jw_j^4=
\sum_jv_j^3w_j=\sum_jv_jw_j^3=0.
\]
For the standard nondegenerate dot product, this says \(W^{(3)}\subseteq W^\perp\); both sides have dimension two, so equality holds. Cubing once more and using that taking orthogonal complements commutes with Frobenius gives
\[
W^{(9)}=(W^\perp)^{(3)}=(W^{(3)})^\perp
=(W^\perp)^\perp=W.
\]
Thus \(W\) is \(\mathbb F_9\)-rational.

Over \(k\), the surface is the nonsingular Hermitian surface for \(h(v,w)=\sum_jv_j^3w_j\). Its isotropic projective \(k\)-points number \(280\). To check this without invoking a table, observe that the norm \(z\mapsto z^4\) from \(\mathbb F_9\) to \(\mathbb F_3\) has one zero fiber and two nonzero fibers of size four. With \(\chi\) a nontrivial additive character of \(\mathbb F_3\),
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_9}\chi(z^4)=1+4\chi(1)+4\chi(-1)=-3.
\]
Consequently the number of vectors in \(\mathbb F_9^4\) with zero sum of fourth powers, including zero, is
\[
\frac{9^4+2(-3)^4}{3}=2241.
\]
Removing the zero vector and dividing by eight gives \(280\) projective points. Through each isotropic point pass four totally isotropic \(\mathbb F_9\)-lines: they correspond to isotropic points of the two-dimensional nondegenerate Hermitian quotient \(P^\perp/P\). Each line has ten \(\mathbb F_9\)-points, hence the total number of lines is
\[
280\cdot4/10=112.
\]

**2. The two special-fiber orbits have sizes 48 and 64.** For each of the three partitions of the four coordinates into two pairs, impose equations of the form \(x_a=\alpha x_b\) and \(x_c=\beta x_d\), where \(\alpha^4=\beta^4=-1\). All four roots of this last equation belong to \(\mathbb F_9\); the choices give \(3\cdot4^2=48\) distinct pairing lines.

The line
\[
L_*:\quad(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)=(u,v,u+v,u-v)
\]
is also contained in the characteristic-three surface. The projective monomial automorphism group generated by coordinate permutations and fourth-root-of-unity scalings has order \(24\cdot4^3=1536\). The four coordinate linear forms on \(L_*\) represent the four distinct points of \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_3)\). A monomial transformation stabilizing \(L_*\) induces a permutation of these four points, so its stabilizer injects into \(S_4\). Conversely every permutation occurs: \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\cong S_4\) permutes the four points, with the associated scalars lying in \(\mathbb F_3^*=\{\pm1\}\), a subgroup of the allowed fourth roots of unity. Thus the stabilizer has order 24, and the orbit of \(L_*\) has size \(1536/24=64\). None of these lines is a pairing line: all four coordinate linear forms are pairwise nonproportional, whereas a pairing line has two proportional pairs. Since \(48+64=112\), these two classes exhaust the special fiber.

**3. All fiberwise lines are reduced and the relative Fano scheme is finite.** Any line \(L\) on a smooth quartic surface has normal line bundle
\[
N_{L/X}\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(-2).
\]
This follows from the normal sequence in \(\mathbf P^3\) (or adjunction on a smooth K3 quartic). Therefore \(H^0(L,N_{L/X})=0\). The Zariski tangent space of the Hilbert scheme at every line is zero, in either characteristic. The geometric Fano fibers are consequently finite and reduced. Their counts are 48 in characteristic zero (the classical pairing lines) and 112 in characteristic three, by Step 1. Since the relative Hilbert scheme of lines is a closed subscheme of the proper Grassmannian, it is proper; since its fibers are finite, it is quasi-finite, hence finite over \(R_0\).

Over the complete unramified ring \(R\), all 112 closed points of the special fiber are rational. Idempotents therefore lift to a product decomposition of the finite \(R\)-algebra of \(\mathcal F_R\) into 112 local factors \(B_L\). Every such factor satisfies \(B_L/(3)=k\). By Nakayama's lemma, the image of \(1\) generates \(B_L\) as an \(R\)-module; hence \(B_L\) is either \(R\) or \(R/(3^n)\) for some \(n\ge1\). To determine the complete relative Fano scheme it remains only to distinguish these two possibilities at each point.

**4. Pairing lines lift, while the 64 additional lines do not lift modulo nine.** The polynomial \(T^4+1\) has four simple roots modulo three, all in \(k\); Hensel's lemma lifts each root uniquely to \(R\). Thus each of the 48 pairing lines defines a section over \(R\), and its local factor is \(B_L=R\).

For the representative \(L_*\), substitute the displayed parametric equations into the integral quartic:
\[
F(u,v,u+v,u-v)=3(u^4+4u^2v^2+v^4).
\]
Any putative lift of this line to \(R/(9)\), in the Grassmann chart where \(x_0=u\), \(x_1=v\), can be written
\[
x_2=u+v+3(Au+Bv),\qquad
x_3=u-v+3(Cu+Dv),
\]
with \(A,B,C,D\in k\), interpreted via arbitrary lifts to \(R/(9)\). Modulo nine, the coefficient of \(u^2v^2\) in the restricted quartic remains \(12\equiv3\pmod9\). Indeed the first-order corrections are multiples of \(3(u+v)^3\) and \(3(u-v)^3\) times linear forms; since their cubic factors reduce to \(u^3+v^3\) and \(u^3-v^3\) modulo three, they contribute nothing to that middle coefficient modulo nine. Hence **no** lift can make the restricted quartic vanish. This rules out every local factor \(R/(3^n)\) with \(n\ge2\) and \(R\) at \(L_*\), giving \(B_{L_*}=k\).

Every monomial symmetry used in Step 2 lifts over \(R\), because the fourth roots of unity are simple roots of \(T^4-1\). Therefore the same obstruction applies to the entire 64-element orbit. We conclude
\[
\mathcal F_R\cong\coprod_{48}\operatorname{Spec}R\sqcup
\coprod_{64}\operatorname{Spec}k.
\]

**5. Descent to \(\mathbb Z_3\).** The nontrivial automorphism of \(R/R_0\) acts on the special fiber by third-power Frobenius. No pairing line is defined over \(\mathbb F_3\), since \(T^4+1\) has no roots in \(\mathbb F_3\). Thus the 48 horizontal lines form 24 conjugate pairs.

A special-fiber line defined over \(\mathbb F_3\) is a totally isotropic two-space for the split quadratic form \(Q=\sum_jx_j^2\) over \(\mathbb F_3\). Its projective zero locus has 16 points: a nonzero isotropic vector has exactly three nonzero coordinates, so there are \(4\cdot2^3/2=16\) projective vectors. This hyperbolic quadric has two generator lines through each point and four points on each generator, hence exactly eight \(\mathbb F_3\)-rational generator lines. All eight lie in the additional orbit, since no pairing line descends to \(\mathbb F_3\). The other 56 additional lines form 28 Frobenius-conjugate pairs. Taking the finite étale Galois descent of the horizontal summands and the finite-field descent of the vertical summands yields
\[
\mathcal F\cong\coprod_{24}\operatorname{Spec}R\sqcup
\coprod_{8}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_3\sqcup
\coprod_{28}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_9,
\]
as asserted.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone program `verify_fermat_quartic_fano.py` uses only integer and finite-field arithmetic in \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[i]\). It enumerates the 7462 two-dimensional vector subspaces of \(\mathbb F_9^4\) by row-reduced echelon forms and checks exactly 112 Hermitian lines, partitioned into 48 pairing and 64 additional lines. It enumerates all \(1536\) monomial transformations to verify the size-64 additional orbit, checks the 8 rational points of that orbit under third-power Frobenius, checks the 112 special-fiber local tangent ranks, and checks the coefficient obstruction modulo nine. Reproduction command:

`python3 verify_fermat_quartic_fano.py`

Expected terminal line: `FERMAT_QUARTIC_MIXED_CHAR_48_64_FROBENIUS_8_28_AND_MOD9_PASS`.

The finite-field computation confirms the finite classification but is **not** used as a replacement for Steps 1--5, which prove the geometric assertions over algebraic closures and the scheme assertions over complete local rings.

## Relationship to prior work

The classical 48 lines on the complex Fermat quartic, and the 112 lines on its smooth characteristic-three Hermitian reduction, are established results. Rams and Schütt (2014) discuss the 112 lines and their \(\mathbb F_9\)-rationality; Veniani's 2016/2021 study analyzes these quartic line configurations in characteristic three; Veniani (2017) describes the complex Fermat quartic's embedded 48-line configuration and symmetries. These sources motivate the arithmetic comparison but do not supply the explicit relative Hilbert-scheme decomposition or the modulo-nine obstruction proved above.

The novelty asserted here is neither the existence of the 64 additional special lines nor the classical counts, but rather their precise *lack of even first-order mixed-characteristic lifts*, the absence of thicker vertical local rings, and the complete \(\mathbb Z_3\)-scheme descent. Comparisons with published related research records have not identified an equivalent scheme-theoretic specialization; that negative search result is not a uniqueness theorem.

## Limitations

The theorem is local at the prime three and concerns the displayed integral quartic model. It does not classify reductions at other primes, quartic models projectively inequivalent over the base, or relative Hilbert schemes for other curve degrees. The disjoint-union isomorphisms are noncanonical because they require labeling line components. They describe the complete **embedded-line** Hilbert scheme over the stated base rings, not a universal deformation space of K3 surfaces. An unindexed equivalent lifting calculation in earlier literature remains a priority risk; no independent external validation has been performed.
