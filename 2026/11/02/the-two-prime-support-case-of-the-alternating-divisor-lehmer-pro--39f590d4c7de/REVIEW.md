# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is universal for \(\omega(n)\le2\). Multiplicativity and prime-power alternating sums yield strict bounds on \((n-1)/\beta(n)\); the only quotient not eliminated by bounds reduces to \(3^{b+1}-2^{a+1}=5\), contradicted modulo \(8\) under the necessary parity of \(b\). Prime and \(4\) exceptions are checked algebraically. The companion finite verifier uses an independent Liouville convolution and is corroborative, not a substitute for proof.

## Originality — PASS
Tóth's survey arXiv:1111.4842v1 (2011-11-21), including its explicit Problem 4, was inspected in full relevant sections. It poses the composite-solution problem but does not furnish the two-prime-support classification. The superficially related \(\chi(n)-\varphi(n)\) small-fiber results, ordinary Lehmer divisibility, and other divisor variants use different invariants; none logically implies the new theorem. Related public descriptions and indexed mathematical claims were searched under aliases, equivalences, and prime-support parameters. No equivalence was established, but incompleteness of the literature search remains a substantive originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
An explicitly posed 2011 divisibility problem has a sharp and natural prime-support boundary: the entire infinite two-prime-support family is settled, with no bounds on its exponents. The proof isolates the even-prime branch and a nontrivial exponential congruence obstruction. The condition that any further composite example have at least three distinct prime factors is a reusable structural restriction rather than a sampled observation.

## Limits
The result leaves all integers with three or more prime supports open. Searches cannot establish global novelty. Finite computations cannot certify the infinite theorem.
