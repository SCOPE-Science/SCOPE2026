# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact decomposition of an ideal by its projection \(A\), central intersection \(W\), and graph lift is bijective. The wedge quotient is \(\bigwedge^2(V/A)\) in every characteristic. Gaussian-binomial bounds and dominated convergence justify the two theta limits; an explicit cubic-exponent separation bounds projection dimensions at least two. Finite RREF enumeration independently confirms small cases. The theorem is asymptotic at fixed \(q\), and no claim of exhaustive infinite computational verification is made.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)
Closest work: Voll's normal zeta function for free class-two groups, Carnevale–Schein–Voll's full fixed-rank ideal zeta machinery, Bauer–Schein's class-two amalgamated powers, and Siconolfi–Vantomme–Voll's subgroup/subalgebra zeta calculations. These cover powerful exact enumeration machinery and motivate the question, but the inspected results do not give the fixed-field growing-generator-rank random-ideal law, with explicit cubic exponent, theta modulation and conditional one-dimensional projection. Relevant searches for central ideals, random ideals and finite-field growing-rank asymptotics returned no covering theorem. An equivalent asymptotic hidden in earlier enumerative literature remains a residual risk; a database non-match does not certify novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural sampling object is the ideal lattice of free class-two nilpotent Lie algebras, central to quotient and normal-subgroup enumeration. The result quantitatively separates central from noncentral ideals at an exact cubic exponent and resolves the conditional structure of rare exceptions. The parity-sensitive theta prefactor is mathematically substantive: it records whether the dominant Grassmannian dimension is integral or half-integral and varies with \(d\bmod4\). This is not merely recomputation of a low-dimensional table.

## Limitations
Fixed \(q\), growing \(d\) only; no simultaneous large-\(q\) uniform claim. Full publication priority and independent mathematical correctness require independent examination.
