# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The algebra is specified in the signature \((+,\cdot)\); the known flat-semiring Rees correspondence is applied with its exact hypotheses. The proof supplies an exact reduction to a word-factor upset count, an exact directed de Bruijn arc formula, exhaustive classification of two-vertex arc multiplicities for every fixed \(k\ge3\), and a uniform split of the remainder. Its \(k=2\) formula follows from a separate exact binomial sum. A Python checker independently enumerates ideal choices and verifies the pair overlap formula; those finite checks are not used as infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Gao and Zhou (arXiv:2610.20308v1) study equational finite bases and explicitly state the congruence–multiplicative-ideal correspondence; that correspondence is not claimed as new. Gao–Ren–Zhao (arXiv:2608.12284v1) treat the same special family of truncated word semirings for the finite-basis problem. Bailey–Finn-Sell–Snocken (arXiv:1409.2444v1) study infinite free-semigroup ideal and congruence growth under Rees-index regimes, not the present fixed-truncation large-alphabet expansion. Searches of related mathematical records and available earlier findings did not identify the displayed second correction. An unlocated previous word-factor-upset enumeration could imply it; historical priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The count is a natural invariant of a concrete family currently central in flat-semiring research. The theorem separates the dominant freely chosen maximal-word layer from rare lower-word collapses, identifies the exact probability scale of nontrivial lower-level identifications, and detects the first structural overlap correction via de Bruijn two-cycles. It is not merely a textbook congruence–ideal correspondence: the coefficients and error require explicit overlap classification and control of every lower-layer choice. Its fixed-length restriction is explicit.
