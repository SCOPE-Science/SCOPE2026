# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Every relevant condition is a finite algebraic equality in the explicit Cayley graph with \(13\) vertices. The self-contained checker verifies the complete \(26\)-triangle boundary rank \(25\), both cochain cocycle conditions, \(78\) four-cycles, two distinct affine orbits of size \(39\), and three independent translation orbits of size \(13\). The proof supplies structural counting from the strongly regular parameters, explicit nullhomology and dual cocycle witnesses, and a group-theoretic reason why translations preserve mod-two homology classes. Triangles rule out shorter nonzero cycles.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
The nearest source describing the specific Paley torus is Jones–Mačaj, Example 6 (arXiv:2303.11808): the torus, its face vector and affine symmetries are already known, and are excluded from the novelty claim. Delecroix et al. (arXiv:2303.08036) give general length-spectrum algorithms for toroidal graphs without this explicit graph-specific shortest-cycle class census. Closest related records concern other torus triangulations, manifolds, and different complexes of Paley graphs; none of the inspected statements yields the claimed \(39=13+13+13\) classification. An unindexed or classical computation of the same regular map remains a bibliographic risk; lack of an indexed match is not proof of global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
A shortest homologically essential edge cycle is an intrinsic and geometrically motivated invariant of a fixed torus triangulation. This result completely determines its minimum shell, the count of realizing simple loops, three equal nonzero homology-class multiplicities, and the affine orbit decomposition. These data are strictly finer than the known topology and face vector, and give a checkable exact example for combinatorial torus length-spectrum comparisons.

## Limitations
No classification of longer cycles or all integral homology slopes is asserted. The independent audit has not been performed.
