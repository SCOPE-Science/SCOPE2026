# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A complete symbolic proof follows from the three count moments. Stationary Markov two-time and three-time probabilities give \(z=(d-m)r\), \(S=3m^2+mz(2+r)\), and a unique inverse for \(r\ne0\). The positive square-root branch is independently justified by the strictly positive 1-to-1 transition probability. The exact zero-memory binomial family proves genuine nonidentifiability; a continuous two-visit indistinguishability family proves the sharp minimum. The exact-rational checker enumerated latent and observed histories and recovered parameters on 687 admissible sampled triples; those finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the general algebraic proof. Boundary \(d=1\), negative \(r\), and allowable transition constraints are handled.

## Originality — PASS with documented priority risk

The source-relative contribution is the sharp three-versus-two **count-only** Markov-availability identification theorem, explicit globally valid inverse and exact zero-memory exception. Hines et al. (2010) model cluster occupancy and detection with available observation histories, and Hines et al. (2014) study correlated replicates using labeled histories. Allman–Matias–Rhodes (2009) prove generic hidden-Markov identifiability with ordered joint observations, which does not imply identification after compressing those observations into a total count. Dekking–Kong (2011) derive Markov-binomial distributions without a distinct unknown emission/detection parameter. Direct source inspection and published-record searches did not reveal a dominating theorem. This is a best-knowledge, not a definitive historical-priority certification. Broader thinning/inverse-Markov-count theory remains a possible source of stronger prior coverage.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact number of count-only surveys necessary for distinguishing temporary unavailability from imperfect detector sensitivity is a natural design and data-compression question. The theorem supplies a sharp three-visit threshold, a complete nonidentifiability boundary and an explicit inverse; the same algebra reveals poor conditioning near the boundary. It makes the ecological distinction between missingness caused by availability and missed detections testable under transparent assumptions, without demanding that ordered histories be retained.

## Limitations

Population-law rather than finite-sample inference; independently replicated known-occupied sites; stationary homogeneous two-state availability; common sensitivity; three equally spaced visits; no false positives or across-site heterogeneity. The distinction can fail outside these assumptions. Historical novelty needs independent verification, particularly against general thinned Markov-binomial identification papers.
