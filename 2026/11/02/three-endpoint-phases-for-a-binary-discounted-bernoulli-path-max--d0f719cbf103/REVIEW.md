The uniformly convergent dyadic score attains its optimum on compact ray space. Splitting at the root gives an exact quadratic recursion below deficit one, and finite-depth all-success paths identify the exact endpoint atom. The noncritical dyadic amplitudes follow from strictly positive convergent infinite products: their correction sequences are bounded by geometric progressions. At criticality the reciprocal increment tends to one half, proving the exact logarithmic coefficient. A dyadic monotonicity sandwich extends endpoint orders to every real tolerance. The packaged exact-rational checker independently tests the recursion and two-generation enumeration; those finite checks are auxiliary to the proof. Risks: all-scale ratio-one asymptotics and continuity of phase amplitudes have not been proved or claimed.

## Mathematical value: PASS

Near-optimal discounted path scores measure the existence and frequency of nearly all-success genealogical routes, a natural extremal question different from whether the supremum is finite. The model has a canonical binary discount and a genuine supercritical, critical and subcritical transition. The critical exact coefficient and the noncritical dyadic-scale laws specify the distribution's near-ceiling structure, rather than giving only a counterexample or a numerical table. The extension to general offspring laws or arbitrary weights is not claimed.

Skiredj–Berrada (arXiv:2610.33897v1), Zhang (arXiv:2602.18919v1), and Aldous–Bandyopadhyay (arXiv:math/0401388) are the closest identified works. The generic survey and Athreya's 1985 paper may conceal a dominating general endpoint statement; no definitive priority certificate is asserted. The theorem requires independent Bernoulli marks, deterministic two-child branching and fixed weight \(2^{-j}\). Critical small-deficit limits are proved analytically; computational experiments alone are not a proof.
