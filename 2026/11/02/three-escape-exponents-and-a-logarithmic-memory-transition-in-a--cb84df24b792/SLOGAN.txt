In the reversible quintic Liénard slice \(\dot x=y\), \(\dot y=x^5+b x^3y\), every positive-quadrant solution escapes in finite time, but the position blow-up exponent jumps through \(1/3\), \(1/2\), and \(1\) as \(b\) changes from positive through zero to negative. An exact cubic-damping memory transition adds a universal logarithmic velocity correction when \(b>0\): initial-speed differences remain positive at infinity, versus algebraic or Gaussian-in-position erasure at nonpositive \(b\).
