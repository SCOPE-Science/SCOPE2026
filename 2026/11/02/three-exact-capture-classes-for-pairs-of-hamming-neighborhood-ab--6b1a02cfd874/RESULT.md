# Three exact capture classes for pairs of Hamming-neighborhood absorbers on hypercubes

## Finding

For each integer \(d\ge6\), choose two different centers \(a,b\in\{0,1\}^d\), at Hamming separation \(t\). Absorb at every vertex in the union of their *closed radius-one neighborhoods*,
\[
T=B_1(a)\cup B_1(b),\qquad B_1(v)=\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,v)\le1\}.
\]
For a maximally mixed one-excitation initial condition, the exact probability of eventual capture is \(K_d(t)/2^d\), where
\[
K_d(t)=\begin{cases}
2d^2-4d,&3\le t\le d-3,\\
2d^2-6d+8,&t\in\{1,2,d-2,d-1\},\\
d^2-d+2,&t=d.
\end{cases}
\]
The intermediate-distance class is strictly optimal among these two-neighborhood placements: its gain over a near-distance placement is \(2d-8>0\) accessible modes, while antipodal neighborhoods add no modes at all compared with one neighborhood. The total numbers of unordered center pairs in the three classes (intermediate, near, antipodal) are respectively
\[
2^{d-1}\bigl(2^d-d(d+1)-2\bigr),\qquad 2^{d-1}d(d+1),\qquad 2^{d-1}.
\]
These are counts of center pairs, not generally counts of distinct absorber sets.

For example, in dimension \(d=6\), the accessible dimensions are respectively \(48,44,32\) out of \(64\), and in dimension \(d=8\), they are \(96,88,58\) out of \(256\).

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex basis is \(\{|x\rangle:x\in\{0,1\}^d\}\). Let \(A\) be the unweighted symmetric adjacency matrix of \(Q_d\) and \(J>0\). For each detector vertex \(x\in T\) fix an arbitrary \(\gamma_x>0\), not necessarily the same at every vertex, and set \(\Gamma=\sum_{x\in T}\gamma_x|x\rangle\langle x|\). Allow any Hermitian \(V=P_TVP_T\), supported solely on the detector vertices. Conditional no-jump evolution is governed by
\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=JA+V-\tfrac{i}{2}\Gamma,\qquad \rho_0=2^{-d}I.
\]
The capture probability is \(1-\lim_{s\to\infty}\operatorname{tr}(e^{-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}s}\rho_0e^{iH_{\mathrm{eff}}^*s})\). This definition involves an irreversible absorbing loss and is not the same as a finite-time quantum search measurement protocol. The state-space and hopping are homogeneous outside the absorbing support.

## Proof

**1. Absorption equals projection onto the detector-generated invariant space.** Define
\[
\mathcal K_T=\operatorname{span}\{A^j|x\rangle:x\in T,\ 0\le j\le d\},\qquad \mathcal D_T=\mathcal K_T^\perp.
\]
The upper index \(d\) suffices because \(A\) has exactly \(d+1\) different eigenvalues. Both subspaces are \(A\)-invariant. Moreover \(\Gamma\) and \(V\) kill \(\mathcal D_T\), and preserve \(\mathcal K_T\). On \(\mathcal D_T\) the evolution is unitary. If a vector in \(\mathcal K_T\) belonged to a nondecaying eigenspace of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\), taking the imaginary part of its eigenvalue equation would give \(\Gamma\psi=0\), hence \(P_T\psi=0\) by strict positivity. The same eigenvalue equation would then give \(A\psi=\lambda\psi/J\), making \(\psi\in\mathcal D_T\), a contradiction. Finite-dimensional contraction evolution has no growing Jordan block at an eigenvalue with zero imaginary part; therefore the whole \(\mathcal K_T\) component decays. Consequently, for any density matrix \(\rho\),
\[
P_{\mathrm{abs}}(\rho)=\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_{\mathcal K_T}\rho),
\]
and for \(\rho_0=I/2^d\) this is \(\dim\mathcal K_T/2^d\). In particular, arbitrary positive absorber strengths and detector-supported Hermitian perturbations do not change the answer.

**2. Fourier reduction.** For every frequency subset \(S\subseteq[d]\), the normalized Walsh function \(2^{-d/2}(-1)^{|S\cap x|}\) is an eigenvector of \(A\) with eigenvalue \(d-2|S|\). Different frequency weights \(k\) have different energies. Hence the dimension generated by detector positions in the weight-\(k\) eigenspace is the row rank of
\[
W_k(T)=\bigl((-1)^{|S\cap x|}\bigr)_{x\in T,\ |S|=k}.
\]
A coordinate translation reduces to \(a=0\). Write \(Y=\operatorname{supp}(b)\) with \(|Y|=t\). Restricted to the \(k\)-subset frequencies, the row span of \(W_k(B_1(0))\) equals the space \(F_k\) of affine coordinate functions \(S\mapsto c+\sum_i c_i\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}}\): the rows for the origin and unit vectors give the constant function and the functions \(1-2\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}}\). The second neighborhood contributes \(\varepsilon_Y F_k\), where \(\varepsilon_Y(S)=(-1)^{|S\cap Y|}\). Therefore the rank is
\[
r_k=\dim(F_k+\varepsilon_YF_k).
\]
For \(k=0,d\), \(r_k=1\). For \(k=1,d-1\), \(F_k\) is the full \(d\)-dimensional function space, so \(r_k=d\). For \(1\le k\le d-1\), \(\dim F_k=d\): the only relation among its \(d+1\) displayed generators is the constant weight-sum constraint \(\sum_i\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}}=k\).

**3. The critical intersection lemma.** Set \(s=\min(t,d-t)\). Replacing \(Y\) by its complement only multiplies \(\varepsilon_Y\) by \((-1)^k\), and does not change rank. For every \(d\ge6\) and \(2\le k\le d-2\),
\[
\dim(F_k\cap\varepsilon_YF_k)=\begin{cases}
d,&s=0,\\
2,&s=1\text{ or }s=2,\\
1,&s\ge3\text{ and }\min(k,d-k)=2,\\
0,&s\ge3\text{ and }\min(k,d-k)\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
Here is a direct proof, including the small-intersection boundaries. Assume \(|Y|=s\le d/2\) and set \(b=d-s\). A \(k\)-subset \(S\) has \(a=|S\cap Y|\) in the consecutive interval
\[
\max(0,k-b)\le a\le\min(s,k),\qquad |S\cap Y^c|=k-a.
\]
On every fixed-\(a\) layer, an affine function decomposes uniquely into (i) a layer-constant value \(c_0+c_1a\), (ii) mean-zero coordinate contrasts within \(Y\), of dimension \(s-1\), active only when \(1\le a\le s-1\), and (iii) mean-zero coordinate contrasts within \(Y^c\), of dimension \(b-1\), active only when \(1\le k-a\le b-1\). These sectors are separated by averaging and within-group coordinate transpositions. Multiplication by \(\varepsilon_Y=(-1)^a\) changes the sign on alternating layers. A nonzero contrast can belong to both affine-function spaces only when its active layer values have a single parity: on two neighboring active layers its fixed contrast coefficient would have to equal its own negative.

If \(s=1\), the contrast in \(Y\) is absent, the contrast in \(Y^c\) is active on both possible layers and is excluded, while every function on the two layer-constant values is affine both before and after sign multiplication: intersection dimension \(2\). If \(s=2\), the contrast in \(Y\) is active only at \(a=1\) and contributes one dimension; contrasts in \(Y^c\) are excluded. On the three possible layers \(a=0,1,2\), requiring both \(c_0+c_1a\) and \((-1)^a(c_0+c_1a)\) to be affine gives exactly one condition, \(c_0+c_1=0\), so the layer-constant sector also contributes one dimension.

If \(s\ge3\), both groups contain at least three coordinates. For \(2\le k\le d-2\), each group's nontrivial coordinate contrasts are active on at least two neighboring possible layers and hence disappear from the intersection. The number of possible \(a\)-values is at least three, equal to three precisely for \(k=2\) or \(k=d-2\), and at least four otherwise. For three consecutive values, an affine function whose alternating-sign transform is affine has exactly one degree of freedom; for four consecutive values, the two consecutive second-difference equations force both coefficients to vanish. This proves the last two cases. Finally, for \(s=0\), \(\varepsilon_Y\) is constant on each spectral shell and the spaces are identical, giving dimension \(d\).

Thus, since \(\dim F_k=d\), the shell ranks for \(2\le k\le d-2\) are \(d\), \(2d-2\), \(2d-1\), or \(2d\) in the cases just listed.

**4. Sum over distinct adjacency energies.** The sum of all \(r_k\) is \(\dim\mathcal K_T\). For the antipodal class, the endpoints contribute \(2\) and the other \(d-1\) shells each contribute \(d\), giving \(d^2-d+2\). For the near class, the endpoints contribute \(2\), the two next-to-endpoint shells contribute \(2d\), and the remaining \(d-3\) shells each contribute \(2d-2\), giving \(2d^2-6d+8\). For intermediate separation, the two shells \(k=2,d-2\) contribute \(2(2d-1)\), the \(d-5\) central shells contribute \((d-5)2d\), and the other four contributions are \(2+2d\), giving \(2d^2-4d\). All three classes occur for \(d\ge6\), and the inequalities are strict in the stated order. The numbers of center pairs follow by multiplying the number \(\binom dt\) of choices of a second center at distance \(t\) from a fixed center by \(2^{d-1}\).

## Verification

The standalone `verify_two_ball_capture.py` constructs detector sets from bitwise adjacency and checks the spectral shell ranks by integer Krawtchouk Gram matrices modulo the distinct primes \(1000003\) and \(1000033\) for every \(6\le d\le13\), every distance and every energy. It separately computes direct Walsh restriction matrices for dimensions \(6\) through \(9\) and direct graph-adjacency Krylov powers for dimensions \(6,7\). All checks passed: \(152\) two-prime Gram tables, \(204\) direct Walsh rank cases, and \(13\) graph Krylov cases. The checks verify finitely many instances, while the proof above establishes every \(d\ge6\).

Run with `python3 verify_two_ball_capture.py`; only the Python standard library is required. The expected output for selected dimensions and the complete check totals are included in `verification_log.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work

Krovi and Brun (2006) establish symmetry-based dark-subspace obstructions in quantum walks, primarily for coined or measured protocols. Varbanov, Krovi and Brun (2008) study continuous-time monitored hitting times using Poisson-time measurements; this is distinct from the positive local irreversible loss used here. Novo and collaborators (2014, later published in 2015) establish the invariant-subspace method for coherent transport and explicitly compute hypercube transport to **one** marked trap. Those general methods are not claimed as new. De Souza and collaborators (2023) examine multiple marked hypercube vertices in a discrete-time, self-loop quantum-search protocol, not the present two-neighborhood loss law.

The derived contribution is a full all-dimension, pair-distance rank and capture classification for two radius-one neighborhoods, including the strict middle-distance optimum and the antipodal inefficiency. Standard hypercube Fourier diagonalization and affine-function restrictions are ingredients of the proof; no uniqueness-of-discovery claim is made from an unsuccessful literature search alone.

## Limitations

The model is finite, single-excitation, coherent, uses homogeneous real edge hopping, and has strictly positive detector loss. The result concerns **eventual** capture from a maximally mixed initial density matrix; for other initial densities, only the invariant-space projection formula is asserted. No finite-time capture rates, decoherence or generic hopping disorder are treated. The statement of historical priority remains provisional; broader controllability or harmonic-analysis results not identified in the inspected sources might imply special cases. The modest-dimensional modular checks do not substitute for the all-dimension proof.
