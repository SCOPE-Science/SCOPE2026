Two radius-one absorbing neighborhoods on a binary hypercube of dimension \(d\ge6\) have exactly three eventual-capture classes, determined by center separation: the accessible dimensions are \(2d^2-4d\) at intermediate distances, \(2d^2-6d+8\) at near distances, and \(d^2-d+2\) for antipodes. Thus a moderately separated pair is strictly best, whereas the antipodal neighborhood adds no accessible modes; the result holds for arbitrary positive local loss rates and detector-supported Hermitian perturbations.
