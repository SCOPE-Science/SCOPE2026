# Scientific review: optimal Heawood identifying-code exchanges

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns all size-seven closed-neighborhood identifying codes of the 14-vertex Fano incidence graph. Every code of size at most seven is tested against fourteen nonempty, pairwise distinct closed-neighborhood signatures. Both supplied implementations independently obtain minimum size seven and the exact 268-code roster. The first enumerates the entire relevant bitmask domain, constructs every one- and at-most-two-replacement edge and every connected component, then checks all-source graph distances. The second builds the plane independently from orthogonal three-vectors over the two-element field and reproduces the code roster, components and distances, as well as the automorphism orbit census. The two 22-vertex components also have an explicit geometric proof as cones over the Heawood line graph. The all-point-to-all-line count-level argument gives an independent lower bound of five moves; exhaustive all-source distances give the matching universal upper bound. No infinite-family theorem is asserted.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The foundational 1998 definition, incidence geometry and the induced-Johnson-subgraph representation are prior results. The 2015 paper of Honkala, Hudry and Lobstein, read in its complete nine-page accessible text, treats extremal numbers of identifying codes and explicitly refers distance questions to a separate 2016 article. The 2016 article's accessible title, abstract and bibliographic metadata establish a general connection with induced subgraphs of Johnson graphs, which subsumes the generic one-swap representation but does not itself give the claimed exact Heawood component sizes or two-token connectivity threshold. Its full text could not be obtained despite a preprint and open-access search and a bounded institutional-access attempt requiring human intervention. This is the main residual originality risk; neither lack of an indexed match nor inaccessible text proves originality. The Gravier et al. manuscript and the Hudry–Junnila–Lobstein survey were inspected in accessible full text and do not give these particular numerical and geometric conclusions. Compared mathematical results concern other graph classes or other code notions, including Heawood locating domination and metric resolving sets; no recoverable exact duplication was identified.

## Value — PASS
A fixed optimal identifying code permits the smallest possible number of sensors while diagnosing each vertex by a distinct set of reporting sensors. The exchange graph quantifies whether one can relocate those optimal sensors without sacrificing diagnostic coverage. Here one-at-a-time exchanges leave three separate mobility sectors; two-at-a-time exchanges connect all 268 valid optimal placements with diameter five. The explicit geometry of the two small components and the sharp replacement threshold are meaningful structural and operational invariants, rather than merely a new table of counts. The result is deliberately restricted to the Heawood incidence graph; possible historical prior publication remains to be checked independently.
