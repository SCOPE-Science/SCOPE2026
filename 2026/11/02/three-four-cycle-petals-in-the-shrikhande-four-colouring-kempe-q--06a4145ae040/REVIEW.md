# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The Cayley and cyclic-Latin constructions produce the same finite Shrikhande graph up to isomorphism, despite independent edge definitions. The 16 independent four-sets, 10 partitions and all 240 named configurations are enumerated exactly. The first checker generates Kempe swaps from components; the second recognizes them by exhaustive pairwise comparison. They agree on all 1008 moves, the three-four-cycle quotient, eccentricities and the 28,680-pair distance histogram. Complete finite enumeration constitutes a proof of precisely these finite claims, not evidence for any unclaimed infinite family.

## Originality: PASS (with residual risk)
Historical OEIS A158792 already contains the colouring count 240, which is excluded from novelty. Cameron and Brouwer describe the graph and its cyclic Latin square construction. Wanless (2004) constructs Latin-square cycle-switch graphs on isotopy and main classes, distinct objects and transitions; his order-four discussion cannot imply the ten exact graph-colour partitions and their Kempe geometry. Chakraborty, Feghali and Mahmoud (2024) treat a broader definition of Kempe exchange but require list sizes at least six for the relevant general theorem; four named colours do not satisfy it. Targeted public and research-record searches found no stated ten-vertex three-petal quotient or full 240-state metric; an unindexed equivalent remains a genuine risk, so independent historical-priority assessment would be useful.

## Value: PASS
The Shrikhande graph is the unique non-rook strongly regular graph with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\), and four-colouring mobility is a canonical transformation problem at its chromatic threshold. The unexpectedly simple quotient (three quadrangles joined at a common parity-block partition) explains a nontrivial topology of the transformation space and supplies exact distances, central states and move counts absent from the known 240-colouring count. This is a complete sharp classification of a naturally motivated finite system.

## Limits
The finite calculations check only the explicitly defined graph and Kempe move. The literature check cannot prove absolute priority; full independent validation has not occurred. The public source dated 2010-06-01 is the OEIS historical entry, not an assertion about when the graph was first introduced.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
