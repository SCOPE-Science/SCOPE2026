# Review of the three-height reflected-plume inverse theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The statement concerns exactly three unknowns \((A,\sigma,H)\) under the image-source Gaussian model. Direct factorization into \(a-su+\log\cosh(t\sqrt u)\) is exact. The explicit derivative calculation shows strict concavity of \(\sqrt u\tanh(t\sqrt u)\) for all \(u\ge0,t>0\); hence the second-divided-difference function is strictly decreasing from zero to minus infinity. Positivity of reconstructed \(s\) is explicitly required. A continuous family demonstrates two-height nonidentifiability even at \(H=0\). Taylor expansion and a curve fixing the first two observations prove the sharp fourth-root singularity; the argument does not substitute finite computation for the global proof. The provided code cross-checks selected cases, not the universal quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority).** Source papers were compared by actual mathematical implications. Stockie (2011) gives the reflected forward kernel and source-amplitude least-squares inverse with known geometry. Lushi–Stockie (2009) considers existing-model emission-rate inversion, not unique joint identification of source height and dispersion from three colocated-altitude measurements; their citation to a 2005 four-spatial-point source-location method concerns differing assumptions. Kumar et al. (2021) and Newman et al. (2024) treat practical/approximate multilevel and multi-parameter inversion; they do not present the exact three-point divided-difference criterion or its universal fourth-order degeneracy in inspected text. Broad mixture-identification literature based on whole functions does not supply the three-fixed-height theorem as stated. No equivalent published-finding corpus entry or available prior research records claim was found. Residual risk: another analytic inversion or Chebyshev-system result may imply this theorem, and the cited 2005 original report was not inspected in full. Absence of a search result is not treated as a novelty proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This solves a naturally motivated *minimal vertical-array identifiability* problem under the standard reflected Gaussian model, with a global inversion and a necessary-and-sufficient data admissibility test, not merely a sample calculation. The universal quartic order at zero height explains a fundamental sensitivity that ordinary finite-parameter numerical inversions can conceal. The result can guide mathematical design interpretation and warns against inferring robust field identification from ideal uniqueness; no claim is made about noisy numerical performance or broader atmospheric dynamics.

**Scientific limits.** Perfect reflection, one steady point source, identical horizontal station, exact concentration data, unknown aggregate rather than separable emission/meteorology. As above, the strongest historical gap concerns specialized atmospheric source-localization/sensor-design literature and potential stronger inverse-function theorems.
