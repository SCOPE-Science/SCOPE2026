# Review

## Correctness: PASS
The claim is for a nonempty generic locus, not all parameters. Invariance and projective normality give the factorisation of both Wronskians through the 24-sheeted quotient \(t=V/U^3\). Each of the 36 fixed-axis points is a simple Wronskian zero when the residual polynomial avoids the three involution branch fibres. Exact modular calculations prove that the degree-11 residual has eleven simple unbranched fibres; the degree calculation plus Riemann–Hurwitz then proves 264 distinct residual points, not merely a finite sample. The computed coprimality of the order-two and order-three residuals excludes ordinary flexes and sextactic points from this residual. Risks: all smooth exceptional parameters are unclassified.

## Originality: PASS
The closest full primary source is Nawara (arXiv:2610.30992v1): Theorem 4.10 implies involution axes are in the third-order divisor, and Section 6 treats only the special \(a=3\) curve, with 252 distinct points. Kamel–Elshareef (2019) supplies universal theory and Fermat/Picard examples; Kamel–Sakai (2009) treats only second-order points on the pencil. Neither these results nor the checked unrelated nearest research records determine the new generic degree-eleven quotient residual, its squarefreeness and branch avoidance. Risk: an implicit earlier generic computation may have escaped retrieval; generalized-pencil 2025 full text was not inspected.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The generic profile of all 300 simple third-order Weierstrass points of a standard genus-three family is a natural complete classification, not an arbitrary parameter slice. The theorem distinguishes the generic behaviour from published special parameter values, and pins down the exact orbit structure and its relationship with second-order Weierstrass points.

## Scientific limitations
The proof establishes a nonempty Zariski-open parameter set only; exceptional fibres and their full stratification are open. The modular witness is exact, but the extension to infinitely many characteristic-zero members uses the separately proved group invariance and algebraic nonvanishing argument. The result has not been independently audited.
