# Three modular Poisson-cohomology rings on the square-zero coordinate plane

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic \(2\), and write \(A=k[x,y]/(x^2,y^2)\), \(z=xy\). Every alternating Poisson structure is specified by \(g=\{x,y\}=a+bx+cy+dz\). Its Lichnerowicz Poisson cohomology ring, using alternating multiderivations, has **exactly three isomorphism types**, classified by whether \(g=0\), \(g\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(z\), or \(g\notin kz\). The respective cohomology dimensions \((\dim HP^0,\dim HP^1,\dim HP^2)\) are
\[
(4,8,4),\qquad(2,4,2),\qquad(1,2,1).
\]
All cohomology vanishes in degree greater than \(2\). The classification includes cup products, not merely Betti numbers.

For \(g=0\), the ring is \(A\otimes_k\Lambda_k(U,V)\), where \(U,V\) have cohomological degree \(1\), and elements of \(A\) have degree \(0\).

For \(g=dz\ne0\), let \(p=[x\partial_x]\), \(q=[y\partial_x]\), \(r=[x\partial_y]\), \(s=[y\partial_y]\) in degree \(1\), and \(\eta=[\partial_x\wedge\partial_y]\) in degree \(2\). Then \(HP^0=k[z]/(z^2)\), \(HP^1=kp\oplus kq\oplus kr\oplus ks\), \(HP^2=k\eta\oplus k(z\eta)\), and the full ring is determined by
\[
z\,HP^1=0,\qquad ps=qr=z\eta,\qquad z^2=0,
\]
with all other products of distinct degree-one basis elements zero, all their squares zero, and products of total cohomological degree above \(2\) zero. Thus the alternating cup pairing on \(HP^1\) is nondegenerate with one-dimensional image \(k(z\eta)\subset HP^2\).

For \(g\notin kz\), the ring is \(\Lambda_k(P,Q)\), with both generators of degree \(1\): the classes of \(1,P,Q,PQ\) form a basis in degrees \(0,1,1,2\).

Over \(\mathbb F_q\) with \(q\) even, these three ring types occur among labelled brackets with multiplicities \(1\), \(q-1\), and \(q^4-q\), respectively. The cohomology ring therefore collapses the parameter-distinct Poisson isomorphism types to three cohomological types, but distinguishes the zero, punctured-socle, and complementary bracket strata.

## Assumptions and scope

The field has characteristic exactly \(2\); it need not be finite, perfect, or algebraically closed. The algebra is the commutative four-dimensional, unital, local algebra \(A\) specified above. The Poisson bracket is \(k\)-bilinear, alternating and a derivation in each input. Poisson cochains are alternating \(k\)-multiderivations, not the larger Hochschild complex. The cup product is induced by exterior multiplication; degree-one squares vanish even in characteristic \(2\). For the finite-field multiplicities, \(q=2^f\) with \(f\ge1\).

## Proof

**(1) Exhaustive Poisson brackets.** In characteristic \(2\), differentiation of the relations \(x^2=y^2=0\) imposes no constraint on the value \(g=\{x,y\}\). For every \(g\in A\), the formula
\[
\{u,v\}=g(\partial_x u\,\partial_y v+\partial_y u\,\partial_x v)
\]
defines an alternating biderivation. The Jacobi identity holds because its Jacobiator is an alternating triderivation on an algebra generated by two elements; on three generators, two inputs must coincide, giving zero. Conversely, any Poisson bracket is uniquely of this form. Hence precisely \(q^4\) labelled brackets occur over \(\mathbb F_q\).

**(2) Cochain complex.** Characteristic \(2\) makes \(\operatorname{Der}_k(A)=A\partial_x\oplus A\partial_y\). Every alternating biderivation is an \(A\)-multiple of \(\partial_x\wedge\partial_y\), and no nonzero alternating triderivations exist. Thus the full Poisson complex has \(k\)-dimensions \(4\to8\to4\). Its differentials are
\[
\delta_0(h)=g\bigl(h_y\partial_x+h_x\partial_y\bigr),\qquad
\delta_1(X\partial_x+Y\partial_y)=
\bigl[g(X_x+Y_y)+Xg_x+Yg_y\bigr]\partial_x\wedge\partial_y.
\]
For \(g=a+bx+cy+dz\), the images of \(1,x,y,z\) under \(\delta_0\), identified as pairs of coefficients, are \((0,0)\), \((0,g)\), \((g,0)\), and \((ax+cz,ay+bz)\). In the ordered degree-one basis \(\partial_x,x\partial_x,y\partial_x,z\partial_x,\partial_y,x\partial_y,y\partial_y,z\partial_y\), the eight images under \(\delta_1\), identified with their coefficient in \(A\), are
\[
b+dy,\quad a+cy,\quad by,\quad ay,\quad c+dx,\quad cx,\quad a+bx,\quad ax.
\]
Inspection of these explicit columns gives
\[
(\operatorname{rank}\delta_0,\operatorname{rank}\delta_1)=
\begin{cases}(0,0)&g=0,\\(2,2)&g=dz\ne0,\\(3,3)&g\notin kz.\end{cases}
\]
Indeed, when \(a\ne0\) both maps have rank three; when \(a=0\) and \((b,c)\ne(0,0)\) each has rank three; and on the punctured socle line each has rank two. The cohomological dimensions follow by rank-nullity. The displayed formula also proves \(\delta_1\delta_0=0\) directly.

**(3) Multiplication for the punctured-socle stratum.** If \(g=dz\ne0\), then \(\operatorname{im}\delta_0\) is spanned by \(z\partial_x,z\partial_y\); \(\ker\delta_1\) consists of the four indicated linear vector fields together with these two boundaries. Moreover \(\operatorname{im}\delta_1=kx\oplus ky\), so \(\eta\) and \(z\eta\) represent independent degree-two classes. Their exterior products are computed in \(A\): \((x\partial_x)\wedge(y\partial_y)=z\eta\), \((y\partial_x)\wedge(x\partial_y)=z\eta\); all other basis pairings vanish since \(x^2=y^2=0\). Multiplication by \(z\) kills every linear vector field and sends \(\eta\) to \(z\eta\). These calculations determine the stated ring, with the displayed nondegenerate alternating pairing on \(HP^1\).

**(4) Multiplication on the complementary stratum.** A change of algebra generators has form \(x\mapsto ux+vy+wz\), \(y\mapsto rx+sy+tz\), with \(us+vr\ne0\). Substitution into the bracket shows that the coefficient of \(z\) is invariant, the constant term transforms by the inverse determinant, and the pair of linear coefficients transforms affinely. In the nonzero-constant stratum, the free quadratic terms \(w,t\) eliminate the linear coefficients, and determinant rescaling normalizes the constant; in the zero-constant, nonzero-linear stratum, an invertible linear change normalizes the linear part. Consequently every bracket off \(kz\) is isomorphic to \(g=1+dz\) or \(g=x+dz\) for some \(d\in k\). (These elementary normal forms and their orbit counts are background, not the new assertion.)

For \(g=1+dz\), the cocycles \(P=[y\partial_x]\) and \(Q=[x\partial_y]\) are independent modulo boundaries, and \(P\wedge Q=[z\partial_x\wedge\partial_y]\) generates \(HP^2\) because \(\operatorname{im}\delta_1=k\oplus kx\oplus ky\). For \(g=x+dz\), use \(P=[z\partial_x]\) and \(Q=[(1+dy)\partial_y]\); their wedge is again the nonzero class of \(z\partial_x\wedge\partial_y\). All degree-one squares are zero, and higher-degree products vanish. Both cases give precisely the two-generator exterior ring \(\Lambda_k(P,Q)\).

**(5) Finite-field strata.** Among the \(q^4\) possible values of \(g\), one is zero, \(q-1\) are nonzero elements of \(kz\), and \(q^4-q\) lie outside \(kz\). This gives the asserted multiplicities without assigning any new originality to the already-known bracket orbit enumeration.

## Verification

The accompanying independently executable checker constructs \(A\) over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_8\), forms the original Poisson brackets, computes cochain maps on bases, checks \(\delta_1\delta_0=0\), and exhaustively checks all \(16\), \(256\), and \(4096\) brackets respectively. It independently verifies ranks in every case and all displayed degree-one cocycle bases, nonzero cup generators, and socle products for every parameter \(d\) in these fields. The marker is `CHAR2_POISSON_COHOMOLOGY_RINGS_AND_CUPS_OK`. Finite verification is corroboration, not a proof for arbitrary fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Launois and Richard (arXiv:0805.4694, first posted 2008-05-30) computed the Poisson cohomology ring of the specific log-canonical bracket \(\{X,Y\}=XY\) on \(\mathbb C[X,Y]/(X^a,Y^b)\); their full original paper, especially Introduction and Sections 2–3, uses complex coefficients and finds a five-dimensional ring in that setting. Our characteristic-two cochain modules differ already because every derivation of the square-zero algebra is allowed; for the bracket \(\{x,y\}=xy\) the cohomology instead has dimensions \((2,4,2)\), totalling eight. The present result also classifies the full ring simultaneously for every alternating bracket, including constant and linear terms. Known classification of these brackets up to isomorphism and known descriptions of their Poisson centres provide useful background but do not themselves specify positive-degree cohomology or the exterior cup product. No claim is made to the prior normal forms, the elementary bracket census, or the complex-characteristic-zero results.

## Limitations

The classification uses the alternating-multiderivation Poisson complex, not restricted Poisson cohomology, Hochschild cohomology, derived Poisson cohomology, or a nonalternating Lie cochain convention. The statement does not cover odd characteristic or higher nilpotence exponents. A broad unpublished or unlocated general modular Poisson-cohomology theorem could subsume the calculation. Independent audit and exhaustive historical priority checking have not been performed.
