# Three perimeter moments reconstruct every rectangular box from unoriented random shadows

## Finding
Let \(Q(a,b,c)\subset\mathbb R^3\) be any rectangular box with positive side lengths \(a,b,c\). Independently choose a plane with Haar-uniform normal \(U\in S^2\), and let \(P\) be the ordinary planar perimeter of the orthogonal shadow of \(Q\). The first three population moments \(\mathbb EP\), \(\mathbb EP^2\), and \(\mathbb EP^3\) uniquely determine the unordered multiset \(\{a,b,c\}\). This is sharp: the first two moments do not determine the multiset, even among positive boxes with three different side lengths, and there is locally a continuum of noncongruent boxes sharing those two moments.

The reconstruction uses only two universal constants
\[
\alpha=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}\sqrt{(1-u_1^2)(1-u_2^2)}\,d\omega(u),\qquad
\beta=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}\prod_{i=1}^3\sqrt{1-u_i^2}\,d\omega(u).
\]
They are independent of the box. For \(T=P/2\), let \(m_j=\mathbb E T^j\) and \(p_j=a^j+b^j+c^j\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_1&=\frac\pi4p_1,\\
m_2&=\alpha p_1^2+\left(\frac23-\alpha\right)p_2,\\
m_3&=\beta p_1^3+3\left(\frac{5\pi}{32}-\beta\right)p_1p_2
+\left(2\beta-\frac{9\pi}{32}\right)p_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both crucial coefficients are nonzero:
\[
\frac23-\alpha>0,\qquad
2\beta-\frac{9\pi}{32}\le\frac4{\sqrt{21}}-\frac{9\pi}{32}<0.
\]
Hence the three equations successively reconstruct \(p_1,p_2,p_3\). The side lengths, including repeated side lengths, are exactly the positive real roots with multiplicity of
\[
 z^3-p_1z^2+\frac{p_1^2-p_2}{2}z-
 \frac{p_1^3-3p_1p_2+2p_3}{6}=0.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All lengths are strictly positive and the box is Euclidean with three mutually perpendicular generating edges. The viewing plane is chosen uniformly on the unoriented Grassmannian of two-planes in \(\mathbb R^3\), equivalently through a uniformly distributed unit normal \(U\). The observable is planar *perimeter*, not planar area, ambient surface area, or the spatial wireframe length. The moments are exact population moments, rather than finitely sampled estimates. Orientation, translation and side order are intentionally unrecoverable.

## Proof
**1. Perimeter of the shadow.** Write the box as the Minkowski sum of three orthogonal centered segments of lengths \(a,b,c\). Its projection onto \(U^\perp\) is a planar zonotope generated by segments of lengths \(a\sqrt{1-U_1^2}\), \(b\sqrt{1-U_2^2}\), and \(c\sqrt{1-U_3^2}\). Planar perimeter is Minkowski additive by Cauchy's perimeter formula, and a segment of length \(L\) has limiting perimeter \(2L\). Therefore, for every normal,
\[
T=\frac P2=aX_1+bX_2+cX_3,\qquad X_i=\sqrt{1-U_i^2}.
\]
No genericity restriction on the viewing direction is necessary.

**2. One- and two-index spherical moments.** Each coordinate \(U_i\) is uniform on \([-1,1]\); hence
\[
\mathbb EX_i=\frac\pi4,\quad \mathbb EX_i^2=\frac23,\quad
\mathbb EX_i^3=\frac{3\pi}{16}.
\]
For distinct indices \(i,j\), conditioning on \(U_j=t\) gives \(\mathbb E(U_i^2\mid U_j=t)=(1-t^2)/2\). Thus
\[
\mathbb E[X_i^2X_j]=\frac12\mathbb E[(1+U_j^2)\sqrt{1-U_j^2}]
=\frac12\left(\frac\pi4+\frac\pi{16}\right)=\frac{5\pi}{32}.
\]
By rotational and coordinate-permutation symmetry, \(\mathbb EX_iX_j=\alpha\) for \(i\ne j\) and \(\mathbb EX_1X_2X_3=\beta\), with the integrals stated above. Expanding the square and cube of \(T\), using
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}a_i^2a_j=p_1p_2-p_3,\qquad
6abc=p_1^3-3p_1p_2+2p_3,
\]
gives all three displayed moment equations.

**3. Nondegeneracy is strict.** Since \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) differ on a set of positive spherical measure,
\[
0<\mathbb E(X_1-X_2)^2=2\left(\frac23-\alpha\right).
\]
For the third coefficient, Cauchy–Schwarz and the exact uniform-sphere coordinate moments yield
\[
\beta^2\le\mathbb E\prod_i(1-U_i^2)
=1-1+\frac3{15}-\frac1{105}=\frac4{21}.
\]
Here \(\mathbb EU_i^2U_j^2=1/15\) for distinct indices and \(\mathbb EU_1^2U_2^2U_3^2=1/105\); both follow by standard spherical coordinates, or by normalizing three independent Gaussian coordinates. Thus \(\beta\le2/\sqrt{21}\). The elementary strict numerical inequality \(4/\sqrt{21}<9\pi/32\) (e.g. from \(\pi>25/8\)) proves \(2\beta-9\pi/32<0\). Therefore no degeneracy occurs for any positive box, including cubes and square prisms.

**4. Explicit inverse.** Divide the observed moments of \(P\) by \(2,4,8\) to obtain \(m_1,m_2,m_3\). Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_1&=\frac{4m_1}{\pi},\\
p_2&=\frac{m_2-\alpha p_1^2}{2/3-\alpha},\\
p_3&=\frac{m_3-\beta p_1^3-3(5\pi/32-\beta)p_1p_2}{2\beta-9\pi/32}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Newton's identities give the coefficients of the cubic polynomial in the finding. Since the original sides are its three positive roots, their unordered multiset is uniquely determined, with repeated roots permitted.

**5. Two moments do not suffice.** The triples
\[
(1,2,3)\quad\text{and}\quad
\left(2-\frac1{\sqrt3},\,2-\frac1{\sqrt3},\,2+\frac2{\sqrt3}\right)
\]
are noncongruent, positive, and both satisfy \(p_1=6\), \(p_2=14\). Thus their first two perimeter moments coincide, while their third power sums differ by \(2\sqrt3/3\), so their third perimeter moments differ by \((2\beta-9\pi/32)(2\sqrt3/3)\ne0\). For local nonuniqueness even among triples of three distinct positive sides, the intersection of the plane \(p_1=s\) and the sphere \(p_2=t\) is a circle near each positive triple with distinct coordinates and \(t>s^2/3\). A small arc on this circle consists of pairwise noncongruent boxes, up to the finite permutation action, with identical first two moments.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses only the Python 3 standard library. It checks the exact rational spherical-square identity \(\mathbb E\prod_i(1-U_i^2)=4/21\), verifies positivity and negativity of both inversion coefficients, and tests reconstruction of the first three power sums and the three elementary symmetric coefficients on four different boxes including a cube and a square prism. It also checks the explicit equal-two-moment counterexample. Most independently, it constructs the projected convex hulls of the eight box vertices for 320 deterministic random directions and directly compares their polygonal perimeters to the zonotope formula.

Its midpoint-spherical quadrature approximates \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) for diagnostics, not for the analytic sign or uniqueness proofs. Neither 320 finite geometric tests nor numerical quadrature proves the all-box theorem; the complete proof is above.

## Relationship to prior work
Finch's 2012 primary study of random planar shadows (`arXiv:1203.2464v1`, especially its Sections 2.2, 3 and 4) evaluates the first and second perimeter moments for the three-dimensional unit cube, and explicitly distinguishes perimeter from projected area. Those covered lower moments are not claimed new. Kim, Yaskin and Zvavitch (`arXiv:1512.07289v1`) study direction-unlabeled distributions of projected **volume** and show nonuniqueness for general convex bodies; their counterexamples do not negate injectivity inside the three-parameter box family using the different **perimeter** statistic.

Related published results include *Haar-random planar projection perimeter moments of all orthotopes* (record `5f6a2c7bb90c`), which computes the first two perimeter moments in arbitrary dimension, but not the third-moment coefficient or perimeter-only box reconstruction; *Sharp finite-moment tomography of orthotopes in every dimension* (record `cb0a2e3c75db`), which reconstructs box sides from moments of projected **hyperplane volume**, not shadow perimeter; and *All-dimensional area–perimeter covariance of random orthotope shadows* (record `357f1ce92466`), which uses joint area–perimeter data rather than the third *marginal* perimeter moment. The present inference is not implied by those stated results: the third all-distinct edge-length correlation and its provably nonzero coefficient are essential.

Earlier image-based particle-shape work by Vickers and Brown (2001, DOI `10.1098/rspa.2000.0667`) examines joint projected perimeter/area laws for other solids. Its abstract was accessible but the full text was not; no statement about its uninspected interior is asserted.

## Limitations
This is an exact inverse theorem for orthogonal boxes under isotropic viewing, not an inverse theorem for arbitrary convex bodies, arbitrary parallelotopes or finite noisy observations. The universal constants are given by definite spherical integrals; no elementary closed form for \(\beta\) is asserted or needed. First and second moments alone yield only two symmetric constraints, and the inversion can become numerically ill-conditioned near equal side lengths, even though it remains mathematically unique. Historical priority is not exhaustively certified: inaccessible full text or differently indexed older work may contain an equivalent moment-identification result.
