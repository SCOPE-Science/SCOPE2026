# Three prime-specialization strata for Mersenne-cycle evolution algebras

## Finding
Fix an integer \(n\geq3\) for which \(\ell=2^n-1\) is prime. Over \(\mathbb Q\) let \(A_n\) have natural basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_{n-1}\), products \(e_ie_j=0\) for \(i\ne j\), and
\[
e_i^2=e_{i+1}\quad(0\leq i<n-1),\qquad e_{n-1}^2=2e_0.
\]
For every odd prime \(p\), its natural integral reduction \(A_{n,p}\) has the same directed cycle and is perfect. Denote by \(I_p\) the number of **nonzero** \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational idempotents and by \(G_p\) its \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational algebra automorphism group. The complete joint spectrum is
\[
(G_p,I_p)\cong\begin{cases}
(C_n,1),&p\not\equiv1\pmod\ell,\\
(C_\ell\rtimes_{2} C_n,\ell),&p\equiv1\pmod\ell\text{ and }2^{(p-1)/\ell}\equiv1\pmod p,\\
(C_\ell,0),&p\equiv1\pmod\ell\text{ and }2^{(p-1)/\ell}\not\equiv1\pmod p.
\end{cases}
\]
Here \(2\) denotes the order-\(n\) automorphism of \(C_\ell\) in the displayed semidirect product. These three cases have respective natural densities among primes
\[
\boxed{\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1},\quad\frac1{\ell(\ell-1)},\quad\frac1\ell}.
\]
Over \(\mathbb Q\) itself, \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(A_n)\) is trivial and the only idempotent is zero. In particular for \(n=3\), the three prime densities are \(5/6\), \(1/42\), and \(1/7\), with respective group and idempotent pairs \((C_3,1)\), \((C_7\rtimes C_3,7)\), and \((C_7,0)\).

## Assumptions and scope
Only \(n\geq3\) with prime Mersenne number \(\ell=2^n-1\) are asserted. Such \(n\) is itself prime. Characteristic \(2\) is excluded; \(p=\ell\) is included in the first case for \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational points, though the diagonal group scheme \(\mu_\ell\) is nonreduced there. A perfect evolution algebra means \(A^2=A\). The three densities refer to rational primes in the usual natural-density sense and disregard finitely many ramified primes. All group assertions concern ordinary rational automorphisms, not group-scheme isomorphism types.

## Proof
**(1) Perfectness and idempotents.** The structure matrix is a weighted permutation matrix of determinant \(\pm2\). Consequently its integral model over \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\) specializes with the same directed cycle and invertible matrix for each odd \(p\). For \(x=\sum x_ie_i\), the idempotence equations are \(x_{i+1}=x_i^2\) for \(0\leq i<n-1\), and \(x_0=2x_{n-1}^2\). Thus either every coordinate is zero or
\[
(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})=(a,a^2,\ldots,a^{2^{n-1}}),\qquad a^\ell=1/2.
\]
In \(\mathbb F_p^\times\), the power map \(a\mapsto a^\ell\) has kernel of cardinality \(g=\gcd(\ell,p-1)\) and image of index \(g\). Hence \(I_p=g\) if \(2\) is an \(\ell\)-th power and \(I_p=0\) otherwise. Since \(\ell\) is prime, \(g=1\) when \(p\not\equiv1\pmod\ell\), and \(g=\ell\) otherwise. In the latter case Euler's criterion for a subgroup of index \(\ell\) gives the stated modular test. There is no nonzero rational idempotent because \(a^\ell=1/2\) has no rational solution, by the \(2\)-adic valuation.

**(2) Full automorphism group over an arbitrary field.** For any field \(k\) in which \(2\ne0\), the squares \(e_i^2\) form a basis. If \(f\) is an algebra automorphism then \(f(e_i)f(e_j)=0\) for distinct \(i,j\). Writing the coefficient matrix of \(f\), every two columns have disjoint supports: since the squares of the standard basis are linearly independent, each row has at most one nonzero coefficient across the columns. Invertibility forces a monomial matrix. Preservation of the directed cycle then forces the permutation to be a rotation \(i\mapsto i+s\) with \(s\in\mathbb Z/n\).

For a diagonal automorphism \(f(e_i)=t_i e_i\), the relation \(e_i^2=e_{i+1}\) forces \(t_i=t_0^{2^i}\), and the final square forces \(t_0^\ell=1\). Thus the diagonal kernel on \(k\)-points is \(\mu_\ell(k)\).

Choose, over an algebraic closure, \(u\) with \(u^\ell=1/2\), and put \(v_i=u^{2^i}e_i\). Then \(v_i^2=v_{i+1}\) cyclically. Every automorphism is a rotation of the \(v_i\)'s composed with a diagonal map \(v_i\mapsto t^{2^i}v_i\), where \(t^\ell=1\). The coefficient of \(e_s\) in the image of \(e_0\) for the rotation by \(s\) is \(u^{2^s-1}t'\), with \((t')^\ell=1\). Therefore the rotation by \(s\) descends to \(k\) exactly when \(2^{-(2^s-1)}\) is an \(\ell\)-th power in \(k\). For \(0<s<n\), primality of \(\ell\) implies \(\gcd(2^s-1,\ell)=1\), since \(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)=n\); the condition is equivalent to \(2\) being an \(\ell\)-th power in \(k\). (In this equivalence, Bezout powers work even when the characteristic divides \(\ell\).) When such a root is present, the normalized basis lies in \(k\), all rotations exist, and the automorphism group is \(\mu_\ell(k)\rtimes C_n\), with an action generated by raising to \(2\). Otherwise only diagonal automorphisms exist. These statements imply the three \(G_p\) cases. Over \(\mathbb Q\), neither the required \(\ell\)-th root of \(2\) nor a nonidentity rational \(\ell\)-th root of unity exists, proving \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(A_n)=1\).

**(3) Prime densities.** Let \(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\), \(L=F(2^{1/\ell})\). The element \(2\) is not an \(\ell\)-th power in \(F\): if \(2=b^\ell\) with \(b\in F\), then norms to \(\mathbb Q\) would give \(2^{\ell-1}=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(b)^\ell\), contradicting the \(2\)-adic valuation. Kummer theory hence gives \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)=C_\ell\), and \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\cong C_\ell\rtimes(\mathbb Z/\ell)^\times\) has order \(\ell(\ell-1)\). For unramified \(p\), the cyclotomic Frobenius component is multiplication by \(p\) modulo \(\ell\). The set with nonidentity cyclotomic component has \(\ell(\ell-2)\) elements; it is the first stratum. On the identity component, Frobenius is identity precisely when \(p\equiv1\pmod\ell\) and \(2\) is an \(\ell\)-th-power residue, giving one element; its other \(\ell-1\) translations give the third stratum. All three sets are unions of conjugacy classes. Chebotarev's density theorem yields the claimed densities.

## Relationship to prior work
Kaygorodov, Martín González and Viruel pose the arithmetic reduction problem as Question 45 and already record the unweighted directed-cycle algebra and its algebraically closed automorphisms. Elduque and Labra proved the general monomial automorphism and diagonal group-scheme framework; notably their Example 3.5 already treats a weighted two-cycle and its cube-root splitting criterion, so **the case \(n=2\) is deliberately excluded**. Their paper does not state the fixed rational weighted Mersenne-cycle family, its joint finite-field automorphism/idempotent trichotomy, or the Chebotarev prime densities. This specialization uses the earlier structural results and classical Kummer and Chebotarev theory, rather than claiming those ingredients as new. The group-theoretic and arithmetic statements should be compared to possible older formulations of weighted-cycle Kummer torsors.

## Limitations
No statement covers non-Mersenne-prime cycle lengths, characteristic two, arbitrary rational weights, the full classification of prime reductions of evolution algebras, or absolute historical priority. The finite-field group classification concerns \(k\)-rational points; at characteristic \(\ell\) the group scheme has nonreduced diagonal subgroup. Unindexed older arithmetic results could imply the Chebotarev density formula via equivalent Kummer constructions.
