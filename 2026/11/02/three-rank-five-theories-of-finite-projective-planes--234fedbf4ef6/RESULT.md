# Three rank-five theories of finite projective planes

## Finding
Let \(\Pi\) and \(\Pi'\) be arbitrary finite projective planes of orders \(q,r\ge 2\), with no Desarguesian assumption. Regard them as first-order structures with separate point and line sorts, equality within each sort, and binary point-line incidence \(I\); the sorts are named, and collinearity is *not* a primitive relation. Write \(\Pi\equiv_k\Pi'\) for agreement on all first-order sentences of quantifier rank at most \(k\), including sentences with alternating quantifiers.

Then
\[
\Pi\equiv_5\Pi'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (q=r=2)\ \text{or}\ (q=r=3)\ \text{or}\ (q,r\ge4).
\]
Thus precisely three rank-five theories occur; the smallest representatives are \(\mathrm{PG}(2,2)\), \(\mathrm{PG}(2,3)\), and \(\mathrm{PG}(2,4)\), with respectively \(14,26,42\) elements when points and lines are counted together. The largest smallest representative is \(42\), sharply. At rank four the same family has exactly two classes: order two and orders at least three. The additional rank-five separation is therefore genuine.

## Assumptions and scope
A plane of order \(q\) has \(v=q^2+q+1\) points and as many lines, with exactly \(q+1\) points per line and exactly \(q+1\) lines through a point. Two distinct points determine a unique joining line and two distinct lines meet in a unique point. Every plane discussed is finite and nondegenerate, so \(q\ge2\). A projective plane may be non-Desarguesian. No statements about full elementary equivalence, higher quantifier ranks, or unnamed-sort incidence graphs are asserted.

For \(k\ge1\), let \(\Theta_k\) be the sentence
\[
\exists \ell_1\cdots\exists\ell_k\;\forall p\;\bigl(I(p,\ell_1)\vee\cdots\vee I(p,\ell_k)\bigr),
\]
where each \(\ell_i\) ranges over lines and \(p\) over points. Its quantifier rank is \(k+1\), even though the chosen lines need not be distinct. In a plane of order \(q\), \(\Theta_k\) holds exactly when \(k\ge q+1\): a complete pencil of \(q+1\) lines covers all points; conversely, at most \(k(q+1)\) points can be covered by \(k\) lines, which proves noncoverage for the two separating cases \((k,q)=(3,3)\) and \((4,4)\). For the more general stated 'exactly when' assertion, a sharper standard pencil-minimum argument applies: if \(k\le q\), each newly added line covers at most \(q\) points not already on a fixed first line, so a union of at most \(k\) lines has size at most \(1+kq<q^2+q+1\) whenever \(k\le q\). (Alternatively one may use the elementary projective-plane blocking argument.) In the proof only \(\Theta_3\) and \(\Theta_4\) are needed.

## Proof
We give a back-and-forth proof that works uniformly in *every* plane of the required order. For an ordered tuple \(\bar a\) of selected points and lines, its **enhanced diagram** records: (i) sorts, equality, and all point-line incidences among its entries; (ii) whether each triple of *distinct selected points* is collinear; and (iii) whether each triple of *distinct selected lines* is concurrent. These last two predicates are bookkeeping for the game, not symbols of the first-order language.

**Three-parameter extension lemma.** Suppose two tuples of length at most three have the same enhanced diagram. A new point can be selected in either plane so that the four-entry enhanced diagram agrees, provided both orders are at least four. Indeed, the information to preserve is equality with an old point, membership in every *named old line*, and collinearity with each pair of distinct old points. For a point not equal to an old entry, this is just an incidence pattern relative to the family of *distinguished lines* comprising the old named lines and all joins of pairs of old points. If the tuple contains \(p\) distinct old points and \(\ell\) distinct old lines, then \(p+\ell\le3\), and there are at most \(\ell+\binom p2\le3\) distinguished lines.

The incidence/intersection diagram of those distinguished lines, including any old named points on them, depends only on the enhanced diagram of the old tuple. To check this, consider the exhaustive possibilities \((p,\ell)=(3,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,3)\), with smaller tuples simpler. Three joins of three noncollinear points form a triangle; if the points are collinear, the joins coincide. One join plus one named line has coincident lines precisely when the named line contains the two points, with the positions of those points on the intersection determined by old incidences. For one point and two named lines the location of their intersection relative to the point is determined by old incidences. For three named lines their concurrency is explicitly recorded.

Every desired pattern meeting two or more *distinct* distinguished lines has at most one realization and is possible exactly when the corresponding lines concur at the prescribed point, which is fixed by this diagram. A point meeting exactly one distinguished line can be chosen fresh: that line contains \(q+1\ge5\) points; at most two intersections with the other distinguished lines, plus old named points, must be avoided. In the four cases above this removes at most respectively \(3,3,2,2\) points; for \((3,0)\) with three noncollinear points, the two intersection points on a chosen side are precisely its old endpoints. A point meeting no distinguished line can also be chosen fresh, since the plane contains \(q^2+q+1\) points, while at most \(3(q+1)\) lie on those lines and at most three further named points can be excluded; for \(q\ge4\) the remainder is at least \(q^2-2q-5>0\). Equal-to-old choices are matched by equality. By point-line duality the identical argument handles the choice of a new line. Hence enhanced diagrams of triples admit matching enhanced extensions in every plane of order at least four.

**Four-parameter final-move lemma.** Suppose two four-entry tuples have equal enhanced diagrams. A fifth selection can be matched so as to preserve the *atomic* diagram in any two planes of orders at least four. For a fresh point, let \(\ell\le4\) be the number of distinct selected lines and let \(p\le4-\ell\) be the number of distinct selected points. Its atomic extension is given by the subset of named lines containing it. If the subset is empty, a fresh point exists because
\[
(q^2+q+1)-\ell(q+1)-p\ \ge\ q^2-3q-3\ >0\quad(q\ge4).
\]
If the subset has exactly one named line, at least \((q+1)-(\ell-1)-p\ge q-2>0\) candidates survive after removing intersections with the other selected lines and the selected points. If the subset contains two or more named lines, it identifies their unique common point. Whether this point also lies on each other named line is determined by the recorded triple-concurrency flags; whether it equals any selected point is determined by old incidences. Thus the candidate's existence and exact incidence pattern are preserved. An element equal to an earlier selected point is simply matched by equality. The line case is dual. This proves the lemma.

**Five-round strategy.** In the first four moves, Duplicator maintains matching enhanced diagrams, starting with the empty tuple and using the three-parameter extension lemma. On the fifth move Duplicator uses the four-parameter final-move lemma. The resulting selected tuples have identical atomic diagrams, so Duplicator wins the five-round Ehrenfeucht--Fraisse game. It follows that every two planes of orders at least four satisfy the same first-order sentences of rank at most five.

**Four-round strategy.** For orders at least three, Duplicator maintains the enhanced diagram through three moves. For extensions of tuples of length at most two, the above distinguished-line family contains at most two lines; their needed fresh intersection, singleton, and complement configurations exist already for \(q\ge3\). On the fourth and final move, only atomic incidence relative to at most three named lines is required. A point outside those lines exists even for \(q=3\): three distinct lines cover at most \(3q+1=10\) of the \(13\) points (the maximum occurs for three concurrent lines); selected points can also be excluded because when an old point lies outside this union, fewer than three lines have been selected. A point on exactly one named line exists because it can avoid at most two intersections and at most two old selected points; the cases with two old points have at most one named line. Multi-line patterns and equality are matched as above; duality handles new lines. Hence all planes of orders at least three are rank-four equivalent.

Finally \(\Theta_3\), of rank four, holds for order two (three lines through any point cover the plane) and fails for all orders at least three (three lines cover at most \(3q+1<q^2+q+1\)). Likewise \(\Theta_4\), of rank five, holds for order three and fails for all orders at least four (four lines cover at most \(4q+1<q^2+q+1\) for \(q\ge4\)). The inequalities follow because the union of \(k\ge1\) lines has at most \(1+kq\) points: fix one line; every further line intersects it, hence adds at most \(q\) points. These sentences separate the three asserted rank-five classes, and the games show there are no others. The indicated smallest representatives exist as Desarguesian projective planes of orders \(2,3,4\); the last has \(2(4^2+4+1)=42\) elements. No plane of smaller order lies in its rank-five class, so the bound is sharp.

## Verification
The standalone deterministic C++17 program `ef_projective.cpp` constructs \(\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\) for \(q=2,3,4,5\) from normalized triples over the corresponding finite fields. It checks the projective-plane incidence axioms and computes *exact* recursive first-order tuple types with unrestricted quantifier alternation. At rank five the four roots have three distinct type IDs, with orders four and five sharing one ID; at rank four orders three, four, and five share one ID. All type signatures are interned by exact vector equality, not merely by hashes. The optimized rank-one extension enumeration for four parameters uses the same empty/singleton/intersection decomposition proved above. A separate complete atomic enumeration of all fifth elements for order four was compared with that optimized routine on all \(42^4=3{,}111{,}696\) ordered four-tuples, with zero mismatches. Source and recorded output are included alongside this document. The infinite result is derived from the proof, not from extrapolating the finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work
Y. Venema's 1999 *Points, lines and diamonds: a two-sorted modal logic for projective planes* studies a *propositional two-sorted modal language* and proves soundness, completeness, and decidability results (Theorems 4.2 and 5.1). That modal result neither supplies unrestricted first-order tuple games nor yields the present rank-five partition. General bounded-quantifier Ehrenfeucht--Fraisse techniques are classical; the contribution here is the exact order-three/order-four threshold and universal five-round extension lemma in projective incidence geometry. The earlier four-round partition is strictly coarser and does not imply the rank-five stabilization statement.

## Limitations
The proof applies to arbitrary finite projective planes, including non-Desarguesian planes, but makes no assertion about higher quantifier ranks. Numerical verification covers only the displayed Desarguesian examples. Literature and research-index searches found no statement that implies the complete rank-five result, but lack of a hit does not establish priority; unindexed equivalent results remain possible. Independent review has not been performed.
