# Review of the three-level mixed-manna welfare frontier

## Correctness — PASS

The hypotheses force all nontrivial singleton-value differences into one unordered same-sign pair. Equal total values force the directed pair counts to match. Allocating the directed pair types to their better valuers is itemwise utilitarian-optimal and creates the same nonnegative comparison slack for both agents. The remaining items are identically valued across agents, including possibly positive and negative items; a signed discrepancy-greedy rule provides an inductive one-item witness of size at least the absolute common discrepancy. The resulting envy gaps are exactly the signed discrepancies less the comparison slack, so every positive gap is eliminated by the witness. Arbitrary nonzero real magnitudes and all finite lengths are covered by the proof. Independent direct small-profile evaluation checked both EF1 and the unrestricted itemwise utility maximum; a separate exhaustive allocation enumeration confirmed the fair optimum. Potential limitation: the statement deliberately excludes sign disagreements, zero singleton utilities, unequal totals, and larger alphabets.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

Teh (2026, arXiv:2609.01580, Section 3.6, Theorem 3.8) obtains an unbounded price of EF1 under common signs and equal normalized totals using four singleton values; its cited results do not establish the zero-price three-level counterpart. Aleksandrov and Walsh (2020, arXiv:2007.04129, Theorem 4, Section 7) **do prove utilitarian optimality** together with EF1 for the zero-inclusive ternary domain \(\{-a,0,b\}\); their 2019 paper (arXiv:1911.11005, Sections 7–8) establishes EFX/PO and related guarantees. Their domains do not include the present strictly nonzero three-level common-sign values with two different positive or two different negative levels. Pareto optimality alone, including its fractional version, does not imply utilitarian maximum. Ma et al. (2025, arXiv:2509.09641, Section 1) study EF1-constrained social welfare for nonnegative goods, so their guarantees do not imply the present mixed-manna theorem. Mathematically adjacent prior records concern online, irrevocable allocation of goods or ordinal matching fairness rather than this welfare optimum. Searches over equivalent wording, sign-dual formulations, broader welfare guarantees, and fixed-alphabet frontier claims did not reveal a dominating theorem. This is self-review comparative review, not proof of priority; an unindexed or inaccessible equivalent remains the main originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The question is driven directly by the fixed-four-value counterexample showing unbounded fairness welfare loss in the recent mixed-manna literature. The result identifies the **exact alphabet cardinality at which a zero-price guarantee can cease** under the same two-agent normalization and sign-agreement hypotheses. The proof explains the boundary structurally: with three values there is only one heterogeneous signed magnitude pair, and equal totals balance its two orientations, whereas four values can support asymmetric discrepancies of both signs that defeat a fair welfare optimum. It gives an explicit linear-arithmetic-operation construction rather than a small numerical improvement or an arbitrary restricted census. The price guarantee does not extend to general goods-only three-level instances, and no such extension is claimed.

## Verification and limitations

The runnable standalone `verify.py` uses exact integer arithmetic to test 117,995 equal-total exhaustive profiles, 13,555 randomized equal-total profiles, 3,186 independently brute-forced fair optima, and six finite instances of the published four-level obstruction. These computations validate implementation and critical algebra only for sampled or bounded inputs. The unrestricted claim is proved by the analytic witness invariant. Verification of scientific novelty remains limited by search coverage and possible nonindexed results. Neither an independent mathematical referee report nor an external audit has occurred.
