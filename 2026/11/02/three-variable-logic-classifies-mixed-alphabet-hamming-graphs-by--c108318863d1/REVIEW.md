# Review of the mixed Hamming-product classification

**Correctness (PASS).** Each graph edge changes exactly one coordinate, so it is in a triangle if and only if that factor size is at least three. The two colored distances determine equality and adjacency. The complete support of the third vertex over any two anchors decomposes into coordinate-local options: at a differing nonbinary coordinate a third symbol always exists, while at a differing binary coordinate it cannot. Hence support depends only on the number of coordinates in each category. This proves a permanent three-pebble strategy. Reusable three-variable formulas for path reachability in the definable binary and nonbinary edge subgraphs recover the two factor counts; alpha-renaming avoids a fourth variable. The boundary cases with zero binary or nonbinary factors are included.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** The relevant full text of Klavžar–Peterin (2005) concerns Hamming-graph embeddings, not finite-variable equivalence. Grohe–Otto (2012) gives the generic pebble-game principle, not the stated mixed-factor classification. Pikhurko–Verbitsky (2010) supplies general logical-complexity context. Earlier claims comparing distance-regular graphs using distance-intersection support imply the uniform Hamming-graph special case, but unequal-factor products are not generally distance-regular. Fixed-rank analyses of rook and uniform Hamming graphs do not imply unrestricted-depth equivalence. Searches of published mathematical research records found no exact match, but this is not an exhaustive historical-priority certificate.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The result supplies a natural, complete unrestricted-depth finite-variable classification of *all* mixed-alphabet Hamming graphs, not just the homogeneous distance-regular subclass. It reconstructs the two intrinsic dimension counts, identifies exactly \(d+1\) theories among products with \(d\) factors, gives sharp minimal representatives of order \(2^b3^s\), and displays a sharp three-versus-four-variable separation. The intrinsic binary/nonbinary edge decomposition is useful for further model-theoretic study of nonuniform Cartesian products.

**Limitations and closest prior work.** The conclusion applies to finite products with every factor at least two, in the pure noncounting adjacency language. It does not assert historical uniqueness, a classification among arbitrary graphs, or invariance under counting or coordinate-labelled expansions. Closest comparators: Klavžar–Peterin (2005), Grohe–Otto (2012), Pikhurko–Verbitsky (2010), and the narrower distance-regular intersection-support result. Two separate deterministic programs and their exact recorded outputs accompany the proof; the infinite theorem is not inferred from them.
