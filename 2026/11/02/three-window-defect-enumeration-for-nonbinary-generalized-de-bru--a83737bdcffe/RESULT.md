# Three-window-defect enumeration for nonbinary generalized de Bruijn necklaces

## Finding
Fix integers \(q\ge3\) and \(n\ge2\). Let \(T_{q,n}\) denote the number, up to cyclic rotation, of length-\(q^n-3\) words on a labeled \(q\)-letter alphabet whose circular length-\(n\) windows are pairwise distinct. Such a necklace is a generalized de Bruijn necklace: it has all possible shorter factors and the maximum possible number of distinct factors at every length. Define the classical de Bruijn count
\[
B_{q,n}=\frac{(q!)^{q^{n-1}}}{q^n}.
\]
For \(n=2\), the exact answer is
\[
\boxed{T_{q,2}=B_{q,2}\frac{q^2-2q+2}{q(q-1)}.}
\]
For \(n\ge3\), set \(m=n-1\), \(x=q^{-m}\), and define
\[
R_2=\frac{q-1+2(-1)^m x}{q+1},\qquad \Delta=q^2+q+1.
\]
Define the remaining two factors by the following exhaustive table (\(\rho=m\bmod3\)):

| \(\rho\) | \(\Delta R_{3,\mathrm{dist}}\) | \(\Delta R_{3,\mathrm{rep}}\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(0\) | \((q-1)^2+3(q-1)x+3x^2\) | \((q-1)^2-(q-1)(q-2)x-q(q+2)x^2\) |
| \(1\) | \((q-1)^2+3x^2\) | \((q-1)^2+q(q-1)x-q(q+2)x^2\) |
| \(2\) | \((q-1)^2-3(q-1)x+3x^2\) | \((q-1)^2-2(q-1)x-q(q+2)x^2\) |

Then
\[
\boxed{
T_{q,n}=\frac{B_{q,n}}{(q-1)^3}
\left[\binom q3+q\binom q2 R_2+q(q-1)R_{3,\mathrm{rep}}
+2\binom q3 R_{3,\mathrm{dist}}\right].}
\]
The four summands enumerate, respectively, three missing constant windows, a missing constant window together with a primitive period-two pair, a primitive period-three orbit using exactly two symbols, and a primitive period-three orbit using three symbols. All four types occur for \(q\ge3\) and \(n\ge3\), and every admissible necklace belongs to exactly one of them.

For example, \(T_{3,2}=20\), \(T_{4,2}=17280\), \(T_{3,3}=376704\), and \(T_{3,4}=12297692033065156608\).

## Assumptions and scope
Words are over \(\Sigma=\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\), with labels fixed; rotations are identified but alphabet relabeling and reversal are not. All length-\(n\) windows are taken cyclically, including wraparound windows, and must be distinct. Exactly three of the \(q^n\) possible windows are missing. No additional balance assumption is imposed on shorter-factor multiplicities. The result counts all generalized de Bruijn necklaces of this length, in the sense of maximizing the number of *distinct* factors at each length, but it does not claim to count the more restrictive multiplicity-balanced \(P\)-sequences of Nellore and Ward.

## Proof
**1. The missing-edge classification.** Consider the directed de Bruijn graph \(G\) on the \(q^{n-1}\) vertices \(\Sigma^{n-1}\); an \(n\)-word labels an edge from its prefix of length \(n-1\) to its suffix of that length. A necklace with all windows distinct traverses an Euler circuit in \(G\setminus E\), where \(E\) is its three missing labeled edges. Since \(G\) has equal in-degree and out-degree at each vertex, \(E\) must be balanced. A finite balanced edge set decomposes into edge-disjoint directed cycles. Its three edges must therefore comprise (i) three loops, (ii) a loop plus a two-cycle, or (iii) a three-cycle.

For \(n\ge3\), vertices belonging to loops and primitive two-cycles are distinct. A directed cycle of length \(d\le3\) corresponds to a primitive cyclic symbol pattern of period \(d\). There are \(\binom q3\) triples of loops, \(q\binom q2\) loop-plus-two-cycle sets, \(q(q-1)\) primitive three-cycles generated by words with two distinct symbols, and \(2\binom q3\) primitive three-cycles with three distinct symbols. They are pairwise disjoint classes of missing-edge sets. For \(n=2\), loop and two-cycle vertices may coincide, so that boundary case is treated separately below.

**2. Euler circuit weights.** Let \(V=q^{n-1}\), let \(L=qI-A\) be the outdegree Laplacian of \(G\), and let \(\tau\) be its number of spanning in-arborescences rooted at a specified vertex. With \(P=A/q\) and \(\Pi=V^{-1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^\mathsf T\), the de Bruijn shift has \(P^{n-1}=\Pi\). The matrix-tree theorem therefore gives
\[
\tau=\frac{\det(L+\Pi)}{V}=q^{V-n}.
\]
The BEST theorem counts circuits with each surviving labeled edge used once, modulo cyclic shift, as \(\tau_E\prod_v(d_E(v)-1)!\), where \(\tau_E\) is the in-arborescence count after deletion. An Euler circuit has no nontrivial rotational stabilizer because its length-\(n\) windows are pairwise distinct. Thus the base count for the undeleted graph is precisely \(B_{q,n}=\tau((q-1)!)^V\).

For \(n\ge3\), all three deleted edges have distinct tails. Every affected outdegree becomes \(q-1\). Consequently each missing-edge set of type \(C\) contributes
\[
\frac{B_{q,n}}{(q-1)^3}\frac{\tau_E}{\tau}.
\]
A loop changes no entry of the Laplacian; three loops therefore have \(\tau_E/\tau=1\).

**3. Rank-three determinant.** For the nonloop removed edges indexed by \(i\), put \(u_i\) for the tail and \(v_i\) for the head. Deleting these edges changes \(L\) by \(-\sum_i e_{u_i}(e_{u_i}-e_{v_i})^\mathsf T\). The matrix determinant lemma, applied to \(L+\Pi\), yields
\[
\frac{\tau_E}{\tau}
=\det\bigl[\delta_{ij}-(e_{u_i}-e_{v_i})^\mathsf T(L+\Pi)^{-1}e_{u_j}\bigr]_{i,j}.
\]
On the zero-sum subspace, \((L+\Pi)^{-1}\) equals \(q^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}P^k\), where \(m=n-1\); differences of two rows annihilate the remaining rank-one terms. This reduces every tree ratio to a determinant of size at most three, regardless of \(q\) and \(n\).

For a period-\(d\) missing cycle (\(d=2\) or \(3\)), enumerate its vertices as \(u_i\), with \(v_i=u_{i+1}\), subscripts modulo \(d\). Let \(R\) be the \(d\)-by-\(d\) shift matrix with \(R_{i,j}=1\) precisely when \(j\equiv i+1\pmod d\). If all phase-starting symbol blocks of every positive length distinguish the phases, the needed matrix is
\[
C_0=\frac1q\left(I-xR^{m\bmod d}\right)\left(I-\frac Rq\right)^{-1},
\qquad \frac{\tau_E}{\tau}=\det\left[I-(I-R)C_0\right].
\]
Indeed, after \(k<m\) random appended symbols a shift from \(u_i\) reaches \(u_j\) with probability \(q^{-k}\) exactly when their overlap of length \(m-k\) agrees. For the alternating two-cycle and the three-symbol period-three cycle this agreement requires \(j\equiv i+k\pmod d\), even when the overlap is one letter. Expanding the two-by-two and three-by-three determinants gives, respectively, \(R_2\) and \(R_{3,\mathrm{dist}}\) in the stated formulas.

For a primitive period-three pattern with exactly two symbols, choose its cyclic phase pattern as \((a,a,b)\) with \(a\ne b\). All its length-two factors \(aa,ab,ba\) are different. Hence the same unique-phase rule holds when the overlap length is at least two. At overlap length one, the repeated symbol \(a\) permits exactly two additional matches. Write \(s=m\bmod3\) and \(\ell=(a,a,b)\), indexed by \(0,1,2\). Define the three-by-three \(0\)-\(1\) matrix
\[
E_{ij}=\mathbf1_{\{\ell_{i+s-1}=\ell_j\ \mathrm{and}\ j\not\equiv i+s-1\pmod3\}},
\]
with phase subscripts modulo three. The correction to \(C_0\) is precisely \(xE\) (the final overlap has probability \(q^{-(m-1)}\) and the prefactor is \(q^{-1}\)). Thus
\[
\frac{\tau_E}{\tau}=\det\left[I-(I-R)(C_0+xE)\right].
\]
Direct expansion for \(s=0,1,2\) yields the three entries of \(R_{3,\mathrm{rep}}\) in the table. This extra one-letter-overlap term is essential: suppressing it gives the incorrect answer for the two-symbol three-cycle.

All four tree ratios are positive for \(q\ge3\), \(n\ge3\). For example \(0<x\le q^{-2}\); the repeated-symbol three-cycle numerators are bounded below by \((q-1)^2-(q-1)\max\{q-2,2\}q^{-2}-q(q+2)q^{-4}>0\); the distinct-symbol three-cycle numerators are bounded below by \((q-1)^2-3(q-1)q^{-2}>0\). Thus the cofactor is positive and each balanced deletion graph has an in-arborescence. Since it is balanced, it is strongly connected, so the BEST theorem applies. Summing the four class counts gives the asserted formula for \(n\ge3\).

**4. Order two.** When \(n=2\), \(G\) is the complete directed graph with one loop at every vertex. Its three balanced deleted-edge sets consist of: three distinct loops (\(\binom q3\) sets); a loop plus a two-cycle with the loop vertex outside the pair (\((q-2)\binom q2\) sets); a loop plus a two-cycle with the loop vertex inside the pair (\(2\binom q2\) sets); or a three-cycle on distinct vertices (\(2\binom q3\) sets). A deleted loop does not change a tree cofactor. Direct two- and three-edge Laplacian updates give tree ratios \(1-2/q\) for a deleted two-cycle and \(1-3/q+3/q^2\) for a deleted three-cycle. The corresponding factorial ratios against \(((q-1)!)^q\) are, respectively, \((q-1)^{-3}\), \((q-1)^{-3}\), \(((q-1)^2(q-2))^{-1}\), and \((q-1)^{-3}\). Multiplying each type's frequency, tree ratio, and factorial ratio, and simplifying, yields \(T_{q,2}/B_{q,2}=(q^2-2q+2)/(q(q-1))\). For \(q\ge3\) every affected degree remains positive and all relevant tree ratios are positive, justifying BEST in this case too.

**5. Generalized de Bruijn equivalence.** Write \(L_*=q^n-3\). For \(q\ge3,n\ge2\), \(q^{n-1}\le L_*<q^n\). Every surviving de Bruijn vertex has positive outdegree: at least \(q-1\) for \(n\ge3\), and at least \(q-2\) for \(n=2\). Hence all \(q^{n-1}\) shorter length-\(n-1\) windows occur. Length-\(n\) windows are distinct by hypothesis. Gabric--Holub--Shallit's two-length characterization then shows maximal distinct factor complexity at every length. Conversely, maximal distinct factor complexity at length \(n\) is exactly the window-distinctness condition. This proves the asserted equality of classes.

## Relationship to prior work
Gabric, Holub and Shallit introduced this generalized de Bruijn factor-complexity notion and explicitly left general exact necklace enumeration open; their tables cover selected finite parameters, including small previously known counts. Chen, Kitaev and Sun studied the probability that deleting specified windows leaves a universal cycle, with exact results for two deletions and certain small binary instances; existence probabilities do not enumerate *how many* Euler circuits occur for every admissible three-deletion set. Nellore and Ward constructed the more restrictive multiplicity-balanced arbitrary-length analogs and posed their enumeration separately; the present theorem does not resolve that stronger counting problem. The BEST and directed matrix-tree theorems are classical and not claimed as new. The asserted new content is the uniform nonbinary *three*-missing-window necklace formula, its complete four-type decomposition, and the periodic-overlap correction in the leading three-cycle tree ratios.

## Limitations
This is a theorem about labeled alphabets of size at least three and exactly three missing length-\(n\) windows. It makes no assertion for a binary alphabet, four or more missing windows, or arbitrary restrictions on shorter-factor multiplicities. The symbolic identities and matrix-tree approach furnish an infinite proof; finite verifier cases are supplementary checks, not substitutes for it. Literature comparison cannot rule out every unindexed or inaccessible source; no independent expert audit has been performed.
