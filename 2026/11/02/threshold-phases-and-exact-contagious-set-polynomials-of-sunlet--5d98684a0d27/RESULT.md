# Threshold phases and exact contagious-set polynomials of sunlet graphs

## Finding
Let \(S_n=C_n\circ K_1\) be the sunlet graph, \(n\ge3\). In synchronous irreversible degree-proportional bootstrap percolation with \(0<\rho\le1\), an uninfected vertex becomes infected when at least \(\lceil\rho d(v)\rceil\) of its neighbors were infected in the preceding round. A set of initial infected vertices is *contagious* if eventually all vertices are infected. Let \(P_{n,\rho}(z)=\sum_{A\text{ contagious}}z^{|A|}\), counting labelled initial sets.

**Theorem.** All three threshold phases admit exact polynomials, necessary-and-sufficient descriptions of contagious sets, and exact minimum-set counts.

1. If \(0<\rho\le1/3\), every nonempty set is contagious, and
   \[P_{n,\rho}(z)=(1+z)^{2n}-1.\]
2. If \(1/3<\rho\le2/3\), put \(Q_0(z)=1\), \(Q_\ell(z)=z^\ell+\ell z^{\ell-1}\) for \(\ell\ge1\). A source set is contagious if and only if it contains at least one cycle vertex and, in every cyclic gap of \(\ell\) nonselected cycle vertices between successive selected cycle vertices, at most one position has neither its cycle vertex nor its pendant leaf initially infected. Writing \(\mathcal G(A)\) for the set of such cyclic gaps between consecutive indices of a nonempty \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\),
   \[P_{n,\rho}(z)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z}[z(1+z)]^{|A|}\prod_{g\in\mathcal G(A)}Q_{|g|}(z).\]
   Equivalently, with \(Q(x,z)=(1-zx)^{-1}+x(1-zx)^{-2}\),
   \[P_{n,\rho}(z)=n\sum_{r=1}^n\frac{[x^{n-r}]Q(x,z)^r}{r}\,[z(1+z)]^r.\]
3. If \(2/3<\rho\le1\), a set is contagious if and only if every cycle vertex/leaf pair contains at least one source and the positions whose *only* source is the pendant leaf form an independent set of \(C_n\). In particular,
   \[P_{n,\rho}(z)=z^n\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{n}{n-j}\binom{n-j}{j}(1+z)^{n-j}.\]

The minimum number of sources is
\[h_\rho(S_n)=\begin{cases}1,&0<\rho\le1/3,\\\lceil n/2\rceil,&1/3<\rho\le2/3,\\n,&2/3<\rho\le1.\end{cases}\]
The exact numbers of minimum sources sets, respectively, are \(2n\), then \(2\) for even \(n\) and \(3n\) for odd \(n\), and finally \(F_{n-1}+F_{n+1}\), where \(F_0=0,F_1=1\) are Fibonacci numbers. In the middle regime, the two even-order minimizers seed the alternate cycle vertices; for odd order, choose any maximum independent set of unseeded vertex/leaf pairs and choose seeds at all remaining cycle vertices, except that the unique consecutive pair of remaining positions may be seeded at both cycle vertices or at one cycle vertex and the other pendant leaf.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, undirected, simple, with cycle vertices \(c_i\) and pendant leaves \(\ell_i\), for indices \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and \(n\ge3\). Its edges are \(c_i c_{i+1}\) and \(c_i\ell_i\), with all indices modulo \(n\). We count distinct vertex subsets, not colorings or ordered activation sequences; \(0<\rho\le1\) and activation is simultaneous, monotone, and permanent. Cycle-vertex degrees are three, leaf degrees are one. All identities hold for every integer \(n\ge3\); no large-order approximation or asymptotic limit is involved.

## Proof
**Threshold reduction.** Leaves always require one infected neighbor. Each cycle vertex requires \(t=\lceil3\rho\rceil\) infected neighbors, so \(t\in\{1,2,3\}\). If \(t=1\), the connected graph infects from every nonempty source set, giving the first polynomial and its minimum-set count.

**The gap criterion when \(t=2\).** Fix initial sources \(B\) and let \(A=\{i:c_i\in B\}\). If \(A=\varnothing\), initially no cycle vertex is infected, and no cycle vertex can become infected: a leaf-only source supplies at most one neighbor, while an unseeded leaf supplies none. Thus \(B\) is not contagious.

Suppose \(A\ne\varnothing\). Between consecutive indices of \(A\), consider a gap of \(\ell\) initially uninfected cycle vertices. Such a vertex is either *supported*, meaning its pendant leaf was seeded, or *unsupported*, meaning neither vertex in its cycle/leaf pair was seeded. A supported cycle vertex activates as soon as one neighboring cycle vertex activates; an unsupported one needs both neighboring cycle vertices to activate. If two unsupported positions occur in one gap, choose consecutive unsupported positions within that gap. Before either endpoint activates, no supported cycle vertex strictly between them can activate, since that segment has no initially infected cycle vertex, and propagation requiring a neighboring infected cycle vertex cannot start inside it. Neither unsupported endpoint can be first, because its neighbor toward the intervening segment is still uninfected. Hence that segment remains uninfected forever. Conversely, if each gap has zero or one unsupported position, infection spreads through the supported vertices inward from the initially infected cycle vertices at the gap boundaries. If present, the unique unsupported cycle vertex then sees both neighboring cycle vertices infected and activates. All remaining leaves activate after their parent cycle vertex. Thus the gap condition is necessary and sufficient.

Each initially seeded cycle vertex contributes weight \(z(1+z)\), since its own leaf is optional. In a gap of length \(\ell\), seeding all \(\ell\) pendant leaves contributes \(z^\ell\); omitting exactly one gives \(\ell z^{\ell-1}\). These cases are disjoint and exhaustive. Multiplying the weights and summing over nonempty \(A\) proves the middle polynomial. The coefficient-extraction form follows by marking one of the \(r\) cycle seeds as a starting point. Each length-\(n\) indexed cycle configuration is counted \(r\) times among \(n\) starting positions and ordered lists of its \(r\) gaps; division by \(r\) therefore gives the same polynomial.

**Minimum sets when \(t=2\).** Let \(U\) be the indices whose cycle vertex and leaf are both unseeded. Two adjacent indices of \(U\) would form a gap segment containing at least two unsupported positions (or, if no cycle seed exists, infection cannot start). Hence \(U\) is independent on \(C_n\). Every initial source belongs to one of the \(n-|U|\) remaining pairs, so \(|B|\ge n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor=\lceil n/2\rceil\). This is attained by seeding the cycle vertices at the complement of a maximum independent set; then every unseeded cycle vertex has both cycle neighbors seeded. For an attaining set, \(U\) must be a maximum independent set and there is exactly one source in each complementary pair. When \(n\) is even, \(U\) is one of the two alternating sets; all supported positions are isolated between members of \(U\), so they must be seeded at the cycle vertex, yielding two minimizers. When \(n\) is odd, there are exactly \(n\) maximum independent sets \(U\), each leaving one pair of consecutive complementary indices and otherwise isolated complementary indices. All isolated complementary positions must seed their cycle vertex; the consecutive pair allows exactly three choices: both cycle vertices seeded, or exactly one cycle vertex seeded and the other pendant leaf seeded. This gives \(3n\) minimizers and proves the claimed classification.

**The exact criterion when \(t=3\).** If some pair \(\{c_i,\ell_i\}\) contains no initial source, neither can ever activate: \(\ell_i\) needs \(c_i\), while \(c_i\) needs \(\ell_i\). Therefore each pair has a source. A pair with only its leaf initially seeded requires both neighboring cycle vertices active before its cycle vertex can activate. Two adjacent leaf-only pairs would block each other permanently, so their indices must be independent. Conversely, if those indices form an independent set, all their neighboring cycle vertices are seeded initially; each remaining uninfected cycle vertex activates in the first round, and all leaves subsequently activate. Thus the classification is exact.

Choose an independent set \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) of leaf-only indices. Each of its pairs contributes \(z\); every other pair contributes either \(z\) (cycle only) or \(z^2\) (both). The weight is \(z^n(1+z)^{n-|I|}\). A cycle has exactly \(\frac n{n-j}\binom{n-j}{j}\) independent sets of size \(j\), proving the last polynomial. Every seed set has at least \(n\) sources; minimum sets choose exactly one source per pair, with leaf-only positions independent. Their count is the number of independent sets in \(C_n\), namely \(F_{n-1}+F_{n+1}\). This completes the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.cpp` constructs the graph independently from its edge definition, performs exhaustive synchronous activation for every nonempty initial subset of \(2n\) vertices for every \(3\le n\le9\) and \(t\in\{1,2,3\}\), compares all optimal cardinalities and minimizer counts, independently reconstructs the complete middle- and high-threshold polynomial coefficients, and checks the middle-phase gap criterion for every subset. Compile and run using `g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -Wall -Wextra verify.cpp -o verify && ./verify`. All checks end in `VERIFY_OK` when successful. Finite enumeration is a diagnostic cross-check, not the proof for all \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Garbe, McDowell, and Mycroft developed degree-proportional contagious-set bounds for arbitrary connected graphs and provided the uniform-threshold definitions. Their Theorem 1.2 gives a general upper bound, not an exact sunlet-source polynomial or an all-minimizer classification. Bushaw and Clifton's 2026 study establishes exact three-neighbor percolation minima on rectangular grids and near-exact results for toroidal grids. Its accessible introductory theorem statements concern those grid families, not the one-leaf cycle corona studied here. Neither cited result entails the three-phase polynomials or the parity-sensitive optimal-source counts. The usual formulas for independent sets of cycles are standard and are used as an enumerative ingredient rather than claimed as new.

## Limitations
This theorem is specific to the one-leaf corona of an ordinary cycle, with homogeneous positive proportional threshold and permanent synchronous activation. It does not compute contagion polynomials of arbitrary unicyclic graphs or of higher-leaf coronas. Priority is provisional because not every potentially relevant indexed or unindexed manuscript could be inspected in full, including the complete 2026 grid paper; absence of an identified covering statement is not a mathematical proof of global novelty. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
