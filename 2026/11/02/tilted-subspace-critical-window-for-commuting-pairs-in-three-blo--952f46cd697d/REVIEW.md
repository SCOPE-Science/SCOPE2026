# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The block-group multiplication reduces commutativity bijectively to a rectangular zero-product equation \(AB=0\), with independent uniform \(A\) and \(B\). An exact count for each *specified* rowspace gives the conditional law without losing geometry. The finite subspace-lattice normalizer is positive, and the uniform finite-product bound \(0\le1-\prod_{i<r}(1-q^{i-a})\le (q^m-1)q^{-a}/(q-1)\) establishes convergence, while ratios establish both off-critical limits. Direct exhaustive computation verifies nine configurations, including the nonprime field \(\mathbb F_4\).

## Originality
PASS relative to checked accessible literature and mathematical records. Garge–Sharma–Singh's commuting-probability asymptotic treats increasing tuple length for reductive groups rather than fixed-length two and increasing outer dimensions for a unipotent radical. Goodwin–Röhrle's polynomiality theorem covers **total class numbers** for the same groups; Kaur–Sharma–Singh give small-degree commuting-tuple formulas. Previous exact joint element class-size and square-rank counts for these groups imply aggregate commuting probabilities and are **not** represented here as new. A previous theta asymptotic for a three-block family has the middle block growing and does not imply the fixed-middle-width conditional *subspace-valued* critical-window distribution. The assessed new content is the conditional rowspace law (1), tilted subspace measure (2), and the precise zero/full/uniform transition. Generic random-matrix product results may implicitly contain it and are a residual publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Conditioning on commutativity is a natural group statistic and selects a markedly non-generic distribution of linear spaces. The critical window gives a complete, explicit limit law for the actual rowspace (not just its dimension), with a sharp balance threshold and elementary but nontrivial finite-field geometry. The result distinguishes an outer-block large-dimension regime from previously studied growing-middle-block rank asymptotics. The exact scalar commuting probability is included only for calibration.

## Limits of review
Finite enumerations do not substitute for proof, and prior-work inspection is not exhaustive. The uniform limit keeps \(q\) and the middle block size fixed. Scientific priority remains provisional.
