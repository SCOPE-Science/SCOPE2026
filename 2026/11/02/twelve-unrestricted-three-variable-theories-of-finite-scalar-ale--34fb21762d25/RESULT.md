# Twelve unrestricted three-variable theories of finite scalar Alexander quandles

## Finding
For every prime power \(q\), positive dimension \(d\), and scalar \(t\in\mathbb F_q\setminus\{0,1\}\), let \(\mathcal Q(q,d,t)\) be the relational structure with universe \(\mathbb F_q^d\) and a single ternary relation
\[
R(a,b,c)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c=(1-t)a+tb.
\]
Among all these structures there are **exactly twelve unrestricted \(\mathrm{FO}^3\) theories**. Equivalence refers to every first-order sentence employing at most three variable symbols, with no counting quantifiers and no limit on quantifier rank. Every class has a representative of cardinality at most nine, and **nine is the least possible uniform bound**.

The twelve classes are distinguished by the following complete ordered-pair extension signatures; each row gives a smallest representative. A positive mask is the six-bit truth vector of the six permuted atomic relations \(R\) on an ordered triple of distinct points, with bit positions listed in lexicographic order of permutations of \((0,1,2)\). The final column indicates whether a third point realizing none of these six relations exists. All entries in the positive-mask column occur **once** for each ordered pair of distinct elements.

## Assumptions and scope
The signature contains **only** equality and the ternary graph of the indicated quasigroup multiplication. Multiplication is not a function symbol; no field operations, constants, coordinate predicates, or counting quantifiers are included. All fields are finite; all dimensions are positive; \(t\ne0,1\) ensures both input translations are bijective. The twelve classes are semantic equivalence classes **within this stated family**; no claim is made about all finite quandles.

## Proof
**(1) Third-point extension types.** Fix distinct \(a,b\). For any third point \(c\) distinct from them there are six possible permuted atomic formulas \(R(v_i,v_j,v_k)\), each using \(\{a,b,c\}\) exactly once. **Whenever at least one holds**, \(c\) lies on the affine line through \(a,b\), and hence can be written uniquely as \(c=a+\lambda(b-a)\). The six respective equations are equivalent to
\[
\lambda\in H(t)=\left\{t,\ 1-t,\ t^{-1},\ (1-t)^{-1},\ (t-1)t^{-1},\ t(t-1)^{-1}\right\}.
\]
Every displayed scalar belongs to \(\mathbb F_q\) and multiplies the same nonzero vector \(b-a\). Each distinct value in \(H(t)\) determines exactly one \(c\). When two listed values coincide their corresponding atomic bits occur together, producing one combined mask. Any remaining point outside \(\{a,b\}\) and those special values satisfies none of the six permuted atoms. Consequently the complete set of third-point atomic types over the ordered pair is the set of positive masks determined by the equalities among the six transforms, together with the null mask precisely if
\[
q^d>2+|H(t)|.
\]
For a pair of equal points, all relevant atoms reduce to the equality pattern: \(R(a,a,c)\), \(R(a,c,a)\), and \(R(c,a,a)\) each holds exactly when \(a=c\). Thus the preceding description includes all possible one-pebble moves, even in configurations with repeated entries.

**(2) Exactly the claimed signatures.** The six fractional-linear transformations form the classical anharmonic group on \(t\), generated by \(t\mapsto1-t\) and \(t\mapsto t^{-1}\). Their orbit has size one exactly in characteristic three with \(t=-1\); size two when \(t^2-t+1=0\), outside characteristic three; size three at each of \(t=\frac12\), \(t=2\), and \(t=-1\), outside characteristics two and three; and size six otherwise. This is exhaustive because any nontrivial stabilizer in the six-element group is a transposition or a three-cycle; solving its fixed-point equation gives precisely the listed cases. The three distinct harmonic stabilizers have different **labeled** mask sets, so they are not merged by the group orbit. Direct substitution into the six equations yields the masks in the table.

A null type is absent only when \(q^d=2+|H(t)|\). The orbit-size-one, -two, -three and -six cases then force total sizes \(3,4,5,8\), respectively. All occur as exhibited in the table. Larger null-present representatives occur with minimum sizes \(9,7,7,9\), respectively (for the harmonic orbit size three, each of the three separate mask patterns has a seven-point representative). This proves all twelve signatures are realizable and gives the least possible representative sizes.

**(3) Back-and-forth for \(\mathrm{FO}^3\).** Suppose two members have the same signature. In a three-pebble game, whenever a pebble is moved, at most two other positions remain fixed. For two distinct occupied positions, Duplicator chooses a point with exactly the same six-bit triple mask, or the same equality case. The previous two paragraphs guarantee the chosen atomic type always exists. For fewer than two distinct fixed positions, use the equality cases and the fact that all universes have at least three elements. The resulting correspondence preserves equality and every ordered ternary \(R\)-atom involving the three pebbles. It remains a partial isomorphism at **every round**, establishing equivalence at every finite quantifier rank.

Conversely, for each six-bit mask \(m\) the three-variable sentence
\[
\exists x\,\exists y\,\exists z\;\bigl(x\ne y\land y\ne z\land z\ne x\land M_m(x,y,z)\bigr)
\]
asks whether a third-point type exists, where \(M_m\) is the conjunction of each of the six permuted \(R\)-atoms or its negation according to the corresponding bit of \(m\). Distinct rows of the table differ in at least one such sentence. Thus the twelve displayed classes are pairwise distinct already at quantifier rank three.

**(4) Sharp representative bound.** The characteristic-three large class has a nine-element minimum: any three-element realization lacks a null type and the next possible power of three is nine. Independently, the generic large class needs strictly more than eight points and has a nine-point realization. Every other row has a representative of size at most nine. Therefore the sharp uniform bound equals nine.

As an expressive-power contrast, the two generic null-present representatives \(\mathcal Q(9,1,u)\) and \(\mathcal Q(11,1,4)\) are \(\mathrm{FO}^3\)-equivalent, but the four-variable sentence
\[
\exists x\,\exists y\,\exists z\,\exists w\;[x\ne y\land R(x,y,z)\land R(z,y,w)\land R(x,w,y)]
\]
is true in the latter and false in the former. For \(t\ne1\), the three equations for \(x\ne y\) reduce to \(t^2-t-1=0\); this holds for \(t=4\) in \(\mathbb F_{11}\) but not for \(t=u\) in \(\mathbb F_9\). This corollary clarifies why the three-variable bound is the intended scope.

## Verification
Two standalone standard-library Python programs accompany the proof. Running `python check_patterns.py` enumerates all ordered pairs and their exact third-point masks across eighteen finite examples, covering 1,666 ordered-pair extension profiles. Running `python check_logic.py` independently constructs complete atomic three-tuple types and performs four rounds of literal three-variable quantifier-type refinement across sixteen examples. Both programs produce the embedded `patterns_output.txt` and `logic_output.txt` records exactly. Both recover twelve types; the latter also confirms the four-variable separating formula. These computations check finite instances only; the group-orbit classification and arbitrary-depth back-and-forth argument above establish the infinite statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Scalar affine quandles are a classical subfamily of Alexander quandles. Nelson's 2002 preprint classifies finite Alexander quandles up to **isomorphism**, using their module data; this does not state unrestricted three-variable elementary equivalence across different cardinalities. Ferman–Nowik–Teicher's 2008 work determines structural and automorphism properties, including high transitivity in particular prime-order cases, but does not identify the twelve cross-field three-variable theories. Elhamdadi's 2012 survey discusses distributive quasigroup and quandle structure rather than this logical quotient. Grohe–Otto's 2012 pebble-game treatment supplies a general logical framework, not the specific affine-quandle mask census. Existing general quasigroup or relational-Latin-square results might nevertheless imply the result indirectly; this historical-priority possibility is a material limitation.

## Limitations
The classification depends on a **pure relational** ternary signature, finite fields, scalar parameters \(t\ne0,1\), and at most three reusable variables without counting. It does not concern function-symbol quasigroup languages, non-scalar Alexander quandles, arbitrary Latin squares, counting logic, or four-variable logic. The verification scripts cannot certify the unbounded claim without the supplied proof. The originality comparison is based on identified primary literature and database searches, not a proof that no equivalent theorem exists elsewhere. No independent mathematical review has been performed.
