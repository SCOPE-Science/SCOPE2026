# Scientific assessment — twenty-five rank-zero fibers in characteristic eleven

## Correctness — PASS

The genus calculation uses six tamely ramified branch points and the explicitly verified squarefreeness condition \(a^5\ne6\). The differential basis has the appropriate pole orders at infinity. Coefficient extraction from \((x^5+ax^2+1)^3\) and \((x^5+ax^2+1)^7\) gives the displayed Cartier blocks. Keeping the \(11\)-power twist in the semilinear iterate gives precisely \(T(a)=2a^{25}-3a^5-3\); the Cartier matrix has rank exactly two throughout the smooth locus, whereas its stable rank is zero exactly on \(T=0\). The explicit finite-field/Kummer calculation proves the degree-five and degree-twenty factors irreducible and confirms that their twenty-five roots avoid the discriminant locus. The two included verifiers reproduced the identities and checked Frobenius-twisted ranks using separate implementations. No numerical census is used as a substitute for the algebraic-closure proof.

## Originality — PASS, with documented residual risk

Achter–Howe (arXiv:1710.10726) provides semilinear matrix conventions; Sutherland (arXiv:2004.10189) provides general superelliptic Cartier computation methods. Neither accessible statement specifies this pencil's \(T\) or its two Frobenius orbits. Camacho-Navarro (arXiv:2012.08705), inspected in full-text sections on preliminaries, genus-four standard forms, the characteristic-eleven sample, and examples, analyzes genus-four \(p\)-rank distributions rather than the complete geometric rank-zero parameter scheme of this specific pencil. An earlier result on \(y^3=x^5+Ax+B\) is for a different marked subfamily; its rank-zero criterion does not imply the present factorization without an unproved equivalence. The closest incomplete comparison is Sohn (DOI:10.23091/japm.2025.059), which studies superelliptic genus-four \(a\)-numbers. Its full text was not retrievable from available sources, so overlap cannot be excluded from abstract alone. The distinctive content assessed here is the stable \(11\)-rank classification with the irreducible degree-five/degree-twenty splitting; the constant \(a\)-number is supporting data, not asserted as independently new.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete arithmetic degeneration classification of a natural, coefficient-marked genus-four trigonal pencil, not a prime-field table. All eleven base-field parameters have positive \(11\)-rank, yet the geometric family has exactly twenty-five rank-zero fibers, visible first in extensions of degrees five and twenty. The dichotomy is forced by the Frobenius exchange of two character eigenspaces and requires twisted iteration, providing a meaningful contrast to the characteristic-seven behavior of the same branch pencil. The exact separable exceptional locus and Frobenius orbit lengths are natural moduli-arithmetic data. This is more than a generic application of a coefficient algorithm or a small parameter substitution.

## Scope and remaining risks

The result counts *marked parameters* and not geometric isomorphism classes. It makes no assertion of supersingularity or independent validation. The full 2025 Sohn text was unavailable, and earlier unindexed equivalent descriptions of the same branch-point pencil cannot be ruled out. Recoverable comparisons do not constitute an exhaustive search of all published or unpublished mathematics.
