# Scientific review: four-symbol shorthand-cycle symmetry classes

## Correctness — PASS

Each admissible four-letter-alphabet cycle has exactly one cyclic `012` window, giving a unique anchored tour of the twelve-vertex overlap graph. The two complete enumeration methods in `verify.py` agree on all 384 tours: one explores every unused-edge branch, while the other checks all 2048 last-exit choices and recovers each valid tour from a rooted arborescence. Every fixed-point comparison is exact, and the complete canonical table `classes.tsv` is regenerated byte for byte. Burnside gives 20 and 11; the orbit-size and reversal-partner census is independently checked. The result has a finite domain, so this genuinely exhaustive computation is a proof of its finite claims, not a finite experiment supporting an infinite conjecture.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Chang–Xue (arXiv:2111.13814, Theorem 1.3) and OEIS A120061 already give the total of 384 *labeled* rotation classes; this total is not newly claimed. Holroyd–Ruskey–Williams (Algorithmica 64 (2012), §§1–3) establish the same shorthand-cycle construction and Gray-code equivalences. Chang–Wang–Xue (SETA 2024, Theorems 5–6) count labeled three- and four-permutation universal cycles. None of the inspected implications determines the nonfree four-symbol relabeling action or the reversal fixed-point table, complete 20 canonical classes and 11 mirror classes. A similarly named Hurlbert–Isaak equivalence-class construction concerns a different encoding. An unindexed equivalent small-order classification remains a genuine risk; this review does not establish absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural representation-invariant questions are which shorthand cycles differ only by symbol names, which are mirror equivalents, and which have a nontrivial automorphism. Their answers cannot be obtained by dividing 384 by 24: eight classes are stabilized by transpositions. The result completely settles these structural questions for the first nontrivial four-symbol case, rather than merely reproducing a known labeled count. It makes no claim of an infinite-alphabet classification.

## Closest literature and remaining limits

The principal comparison is the exact labeled count of Chang and Xue, followed by the graph and Gray-code constructions of Holroyd, Ruskey and Williams. The 2024 extension has broader alphabet and window parameters for labeled counts, but lacks the relevant fixed-point data. The full `classes.tsv` and `verify.py` are essential reproducibility artifacts. The result remains finite and unaudited independently; a nonindexed earlier symmetry table could affect originality.
