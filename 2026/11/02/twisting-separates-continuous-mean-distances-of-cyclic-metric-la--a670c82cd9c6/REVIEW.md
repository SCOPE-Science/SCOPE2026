# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The uniform edge classes have HH/HV/VV weights 4/9,4/9,1/9. For the prism, exact conditional means follow the n-circle uniform distance and the same-edge rung correction; for the Möbius ladder no shortest path requires two rungs, since such a pair is removed by shifting the intervening arc antipodally. Direct geodesic integrations give μ_HH^M=μ_HV^M=n/4+1/2−1/(12n) and μ_VV^M=c_n+2/3−1/(3n)−I_even/(6n). Aggregation yields the stated two means, and counting vertex distances gives the parity contrast. Independently performed direct exact rational planar clipping/integration of the minimum of four endpoint-route affine distances for every ordered edge pair of n=3,…,8; all 12 full means matched the closed forms exactly, including n=3 triangular prism. Independent BFS checked vertex-distance formulas n=3,…,20. The all-n proof comes from the closed edge-category integration, not finite sampling.

## originality

PASS

Garijo et al established algorithms to compute any finite weighted graph mean and formulae for several classes; generic computability does not logically equal the explicit Möbius/prism all-n parity formula. Baptista et al give bounds and other explicit families; related published topological ladder records have different invariants. However Doyle–Graver original 1982 shapes paper reports six infinite families, so specific historical priority cannot be ruled out from abstracts: the original long paper was sought via arXiv/open-access sources and authorized Oxford but full text was not available (VPN disconnected). This is an explicit meaningful risk, not automatically a FAIL.

## value

PASS

The continuous mean intrinsic distance of a metric graph is a natural Wiener-type invariant distinct from vertex Wiener averages. An infinite natural cubic family has a sharp O(1/n) twist gap and a striking odd-parity phenomenon where vertex statistics conceal a strictly positive metric-graph effect. This general-parameter formula is not just applying a known generic edge-pair algorithm to a single small graph.
