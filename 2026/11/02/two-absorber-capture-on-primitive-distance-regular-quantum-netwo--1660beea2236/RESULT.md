# Two-absorber capture on primitive distance-regular quantum networks

## Finding

Let \(G\) be any finite connected simple distance-regular graph of diameter \(d\geq 1\), on \(v\geq 3\) vertices, such that **each distance graph** \(G_r\), connecting pairs at graph distance \(r\), is connected for \(1\leq r\leq d\). In the continuous-time single-excitation adjacency walk with two distinct irreversible absorbing sites and arbitrary strictly positive loss rates, the capturing (bright) subspace has dimension exactly
\[
\dim\mathcal B=2d+1.
\]
Thus, for the maximally mixed initial state the eventual absorption probability is exactly \((2d+1)/v\), irrespective of absorber separation, hopping magnitude, and positive absorption rates. Furthermore, every site-specific absorption probability is determined only by three graph distances and the graph's spectral cosine sequences, through the formula below. The statement includes neither the imprimitive graphs nor dissipative dephasing or finite-time absorption rates.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(G\) is undirected, connected, finite, simple and distance-regular with \(v\geq3\) and diameter \(d\). Write \(A\) for its real symmetric adjacency matrix. Distance-regularity means, for every ordered vertex pair at a given distance, the numbers of neighbors of either vertex one distance level nearer, equal, or farther depend only on that distance. In particular, \(A\) has \(d+1\) distinct eigenvalues and its spectral projectors are functions of graph distance. *Primitivity* here means that **all** distance graphs \(G_r\) for \(1\le r\le d\) are connected, not merely that the original graph is connected or nonbipartite.

Choose distinct absorbers \(u,w\), any real \(J>0\), and rates \(\kappa_u,\kappa_w>0\). The no-jump evolution is generated by
\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=JA-\frac{i}{2}(\kappa_u|u\rangle\langle u|+\kappa_w|w\rangle\langle w|).
\]
For a normalized initial vector \(|x\rangle\), define eventual capture as \(\eta_x=1-\lim_{t\to\infty}\|e^{-itH_{\mathrm{eff}}}|x\rangle\|^2\). The maximally mixed initial state is \(I_v/v\).

Let \(E_0,\ldots,E_d\) be the real orthogonal spectral projectors of \(A\), with \(E_0=\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}/v\) principal, and put \(m_j=\operatorname{rank}E_j\). Define the normalized cosine sequence \(c_j(s)\), for integer distances \(0\le s\le d\), by
\[
(E_j)_{xy}=\frac{m_j}{v}c_j(\operatorname{dist}(x,y)),\qquad c_j(0)=1.
\]
For \(x\in V(G)\), put \(a=\operatorname{dist}(x,u)\), \(b=\operatorname{dist}(x,w)\), and \(r=\operatorname{dist}(u,w)\). Then every \(j\ge1\) satisfies \(|c_j(r)|<1\), and the exact sitewise law is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle \eta_x=\frac1v+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{m_j}{v}\frac{c_j(a)^2+c_j(b)^2-2c_j(r)c_j(a)c_j(b)}{1-c_j(r)^2}.}
\]
This law needs no vertex-transitivity or distance-transitivity beyond the stated distance-regularity hypotheses. The displayed formula is finite and contains no singular denominators under primitivity.

## Proof

**Spectral reduction.** Distance-regularity implies \(\{E_0,\ldots,E_d\}\) is an orthogonal resolution of the identity and each \(E_j\) belongs to the span of the distance matrices. Hence its diagonal entries are constant, \((E_j)_{zz}=m_j/v>0\), and its off-diagonal entries depend solely on distance. Define
\[
\mathcal B=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\operatorname{span}\{E_j|u\rangle,E_j|w\rangle\}.
\]
The terms for distinct \(j\) are orthogonal. The principal term is one-dimensional. We show each of the other \(d\) terms is two-dimensional.

**Strict cosine separation.** Fix \(j\ge1\), \(r\ge1\). By the equal nonzero column norms of the positive semidefinite projector \(E_j\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|c_j(r)|\le1\). If equality occurred, every pair of columns \(E_j|z\rangle\) with vertices at distance \(r\) would be collinear (with one common sign \(c_j(r)\)). Connectedness of \(G_r\) then makes *all* columns collinear, so \(\operatorname{rank}E_j=1\).

A nonprincipal rank-one projector of this adjacency algebra is impossible. Indeed, writing such a projector as \(hh^{\mathsf T}\), its constant diagonal \(1/v\) forces every coordinate of \(h\) to be \(\pm v^{-1/2}\). Its value on every adjacent pair is constant, so all adjacent signs are either equal or opposite. Equality on edges forces \(h\) constant by connectedness, the principal eigenspace, a contradiction. Opposite signs on edges force \(G\) bipartite. If \(d\ge2\), every edge of its distance-two graph connects vertices in the same bipartition class, so \(G_2\) is disconnected, contrary to the hypothesis. If \(d=1\), \(G=K_v\) has a triangle for \(v\ge3\), so it cannot be bipartite. Consequently \(m_j\ge2\) and \(|c_j(r)|<1\). Therefore \(E_j|u\rangle\) and \(E_j|w\rangle\) are independent, and \(\dim\mathcal B=1+2d\).

**Absorption equals bright-space projection.** The orthogonal complement \(\mathcal D=\mathcal B^\perp\) is invariant under \(A\) and orthogonal to \(|u\rangle,|w\rangle\). It evolves unitarily and is dark. Since \(\mathcal B\) is invariant under both \(A\) and the absorptive projectors, it is also invariant under \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\). For any vector evolving under \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\),
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\|\psi(t)\|^2=-\kappa_u|\langle u|\psi(t)\rangle|^2-\kappa_w|\langle w|\psi(t)\rangle|^2.
\]
An eigenvector having a real eigenvalue must therefore be orthogonal to both absorbers and must be an eigenvector of \(A\), hence belongs to \(\mathcal D\). No eigenvector of the restriction to \(\mathcal B\) can have zero decay rate. All eigenvalues of that finite-dimensional restriction have strictly negative imaginary parts, and its exponential converges to zero, even if it has nontrivial Jordan blocks. Therefore the surviving asymptotic norm is the squared length of the dark-space projection, and \(\eta_x=\langle x|P_{\mathcal B}|x\rangle\). For \(I_v/v\), this is \(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\mathcal B})/v=(2d+1)/v\).

**Site-resolved formula.** In eigenspace \(j\ge1\), the two absorber projections have Gram matrix
\[
\frac{m_j}{v}\begin{pmatrix}1&c_j(r)\\c_j(r)&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is strictly positive. The inner products against \(E_j|x\rangle\) are \(\frac{m_j}{v}(c_j(a),c_j(b))\). Inverting the two-by-two Gram matrix yields the \(j\)-th summand in the displayed formula. The one-dimensional principal term contributes \(1/v\). Orthogonality of the distinct spectral eigenspaces completes the proof.

## Verification

Two self-contained finite checkers accompany this result. `scripts/verify.py` independently constructs adjacency matrices, distance graphs, direct Krylov spaces and spectral Gram projectors on five examples: \(C_7\), the Petersen graph, the Odd graph \(KG(7,3)\), \(J(8,3)\), and \(H(3,3)\). All their distance graphs are confirmed connected. Every selected absorber pair was tested (respectively 21, 45, 595, 1540 and 351 pairs), including all initial vertices and both full-graph mean and bright dimension. The largest discrepancy between spectral and Krylov projectors was \(2.856\times10^{-15}\). The separate `scripts/exact_check.py` verifies with exact rational matrices the primitive spectral idempotents, cosine sequences and positive Gram minors for the Petersen and Odd graphs. Reproduction instructions: run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/verify.py` and `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/exact_check.py` with Python, NumPy and SymPy installed; their recorded outputs are in `verification_output.txt`. These finite tests are supplementary: only the analytic argument proves the universal assertion.

## Relationship to prior work

The standard distance-regular adjacency algebra, cosine-sequence embeddings and definition of primitivity are established in van Dam, Koolen and Tanaka (2014). Coutinho, Godsil, Guo and Vanhove (2014) investigate *unitary* perfect transfer on distance-regular graphs; their result concerns another observable. Novo and collaborators (2014) give a general Krylov-space approach to quantum trapping, with a single-absorber transport calculation; this is methodological precedent, not a universal two-absorber formula. Lugão and collaborators (2022) analyze *Hermitian* multimarked spatial search, including two marked vertices on Johnson graphs, but not the irreversible long-time capture law. Relevant narrower previously established calculations include the primitive strongly regular case (diameter two) and nonbinary Hamming cases; neither states the all-primitive distance-regular classification and its arbitrary-diameter sitewise law. The present theorem combines a strict spectral cosine-separation lemma with the dissipative projector to give a uniform result independent of both sink placement and loss strengths. A specialized, unlocated controllability or Bose–Mesner-algebra result could still establish stronger coverage, so historical priority is provisional.

## Limitations

The connectedness of **every** distance graph is essential to the argument: odd cycles such as \(C_9\) can fail this condition at an intermediate distance despite being nonbipartite. The theorem does not assert an analogous dimension on imprimitive graphs. It concerns uniform Hermitian adjacency hopping, precisely two distinct positive-rate sinks, finite graphs, and infinite-time single-excitation absorption. It does not determine decay time scales, dephasing, disorder or larger sink sets.
