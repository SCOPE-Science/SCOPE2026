# Scientific review: two-adic phases of generalized central trinomial coefficients

## Correctness

PASS. The indexed multinomial formula yields the exact falling-factorial quotient in the proof. If \(C\geq2B\), division by \(b^n\) leaves one plus even two-adic terms. If \(C\leq2B-2\), the factorial valuation \(\nu_2((2j+e)!)=2j-s_2(j)\) shows every normalized nonconstant term is even and every term beyond the first correction is divisible by eight; the central binomial valuation supplies the baseline. Explicit degree-two constructions realize arbitrary extra valuations at the boundary. The coprime histogram follows by an exact binary digit count. Independent finite checks include 157,464 index/parameter valuations and 59,292 normalized-unit congruences. The formula is proved for every index; the tests are corroborative.

## Originality

PASS, with residual historical risk. Noe's 2006 full text develops the same objects, proves the modulo-two parity result in Theorem 8.1 and general prime-divisibility criteria in Section 8, but does not supply the exact coefficient-dependent two-adic phase formula. Wang and Sun (2019) prove weighted sums modulo odd-prime squares, not a per-index dyadic phase law. Sun's 2010 preprint explicitly identifies the already-known \(T_n(2,1)=\binom{2n}{n}\) specialization, along with broader congruence sums; that covered case is not claimed as new. Alekseyev, Amdeberhan, Shallit and Vukusic (2025) give a rational-argument Legendre-polynomial valuation theorem; via the discriminant representation it covers a genuine rational-square-discriminant subclass of the present valuation formula, not the arbitrary nonsquare case or normalized mod-four correction. The new statement concerns all parameter valuations off the critical line, the first mod-four odd-unit correction, and sharp nonuniqueness on the critical line. Searches for equivalent valuation phase descriptions and related mathematical records did not locate a covering result, but a hidden general two-adic theorem or earlier specialization is a residual risk. No claim of absolute uniqueness follows from the searches.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a natural arithmetic classification problem for Noe's generalized central trinomial family: it determines exactly how the relative sizes of the two coefficients govern every two-adic valuation, exhibits the unique valuation-level boundary where such determination fails, refines the dominant coefficient by an explicit unit modulo four, and gives a closed all-depth distribution for primitive pairs. The result supplies an infinite parameter classification and a sharp structural boundary, not merely a sampled sequence or renaming of the central-binomial special case. The critical line beyond the explicit counterfamily remains open.
