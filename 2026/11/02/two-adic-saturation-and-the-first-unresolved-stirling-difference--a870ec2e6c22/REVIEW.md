# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. The explicit Stirling sum splits into odd and even exponential bases. The finite eight-residue modular certificate has sum \(4096\pmod{8192}\), and an exact recurrence makes that certificate valid at all exponents \(n\ge10\). Vandermonde's identity bounds every higher odd term strictly above the leading one. The even contribution has exact valuation \(b2^n+6\) before division by \(15!\); the coefficient sum \(8128=64\cdot127\) controls its unit. Comparison gives both exact noncollision branches and only the stated lower bound on collision. The standalone code was actually executed: 165 modular cases, three separate Stirling-recurrence checks, and the finite coefficient certificate passed. Those finite cases are corroboration, not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality

PASS on currently accessible evidence, with residual risk. Lengyel formulated the two-adic difference prediction. Zhao--Zhao--Hong (arXiv:1407.8443, Introduction after Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.2) explicitly state that their arbitrary-pair conjecture remains open for indices \(2^m-1\ge15\), while their 2014 special-step result has different quantifiers. Their 2012 paper proves arbitrary-pair results for indices *not* of the exceptional form. Adelberg's 2019 full HTML article gives pointwise estimates and simultaneous-scaling limits rather than the fixed-index arbitrary-pair difference. Searches for explicit \(k=15\) valuations, equivalences, and later work found no established implication proving the exact two-regime statement. Unindexed literature and unnoticed implicit corollaries are remaining risks; a negative search is not proof of global novelty.

## Value

PASS. The first fixed index left unresolved in a published difference-valuation conjecture is a motivated natural boundary, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The theorem proves the predicted value for all fixed pairs at sufficiently large exponents and identifies the *precise* competing even-base saturation and critical cancellation locus when parameters vary. The exact competing valuations explain both eventual regularity and failure of fixed-level uniformity. Larger exceptional indices and the equality-boundary exact valuation remain open.
