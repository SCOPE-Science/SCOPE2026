# Two-approval ballots separate the complexity of strong and semi-strong justified representation

## Finding

**Theorem (the two-approval separation).** In approval-based committee elections with nonempty voter set \(N\), candidate set \(C\), and \(1\le k\le |C|\), suppose every approval set has size at most two. Let \(N_c=\{i\in N:c\in A_i\}\), call \(c\) *cohesive* when \(k|N_c|\ge |N|\), and for cohesive \(c\) define \(I_c=\bigcap_{i\in N_c}A_i\). The (unnormalized) strong justified representation score is
\[
F_{\mathrm{SJR}}(W)=\big|\{c\in C:k|N_c|\ge |N|,\ I_c\cap W\ne\varnothing\}\big|,\qquad |W|=k.
\]
Construct an undirected graph \(H\) on \(C\) by adding \(\{c,d\}\) for each cohesive \(c\) with \(I_c=\{c,d\}\); singleton requirements \(I_c=\{c\}\) remain attached to vertex \(c\). Every connected component of \(H\) is a star (isolated vertices and single edges included). Let \(w(T)\) denote the number of cohesive candidates in component \(T\), and order the strictly positive component weights as \(w_1\ge\cdots\ge w_t\). Then
\[
\max_{W\subseteq C,\ |W|=k}F_{\mathrm{SJR}}(W)
=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(k,t)}w_j.
\]
A maximizing committee can be constructed in polynomial time by selecting one appropriate representative of each of the \(\min(k,t)\) largest positive-weight components and filling unused seats arbitrarily. In particular, strong justified representation is feasible exactly when \(t\le k\).

In contrast, **deciding whether any \(k\)-member committee satisfies semi-strong justified representation is NP-complete** under the same two-approval restriction, even on instances in which every ballot has *exactly* two approvals, every candidate has exactly three approving voters, and every candidate is cohesive. With ballots of size at most one, both axioms are always feasible. Thus two approvals constitute a sharp arity-one/two computational and feasibility separation for this pair of axioms.

## Assumptions and scope

The electorate and candidate set are finite, \(|N|\ge1\), and \(1\le k\le |C|\). Empty ballots are permitted for the polynomial-time statement; none occur in the hardness construction. Cohesiveness refers to *one*-cohesive voter groups of size at least \(|N|/k\), not to higher-order cohesiveness parameters. A committee satisfies SJR if each cohesive voter group has a common approved winner. It satisfies SSJR if every member of every cohesive voter group approves some winner. For any candidate \(c\), its full supporter set \(N_c\) witnesses all the requirements involving \(c\): checking the largest group \(N_c\) is sufficient, since a subset has a larger approval intersection and fewer voters to represent.

The normalized SJR score of Chingoma et al. divides \(F_{\mathrm{SJR}}(W)\) by the number of cohesive candidates (when positive); thus the same committee maximizes both scores. If no candidate is cohesive, the axioms are vacuous and any size-\(k\) committee is suitable.

## Proof

**Reduction to maximal supporter groups.** A one-cohesive group \(S\subseteq N\) shares some candidate \(c\), and therefore \(S\subseteq N_c\). Since \(|S|\ge |N|/k\), candidate \(c\) is cohesive. Conversely, \(N_c\) itself is cohesive for every cohesive \(c\). Requiring \(I_c\cap W\ne\varnothing\) for each cohesive \(c\) is therefore equivalent to SJR for every cohesive group. Requiring \(A_i\cap W\ne\varnothing\) for every \(i\in N_c\) and every cohesive \(c\) is equivalent to SSJR.

**Star structure.** For every cohesive \(c\), \(c\in I_c\), and \(|I_c|\le2\). If \(I_c=\{c,d\}\), every supporter of \(c\) must approve exactly \(\{c,d\}\), since the ballot has at most two names. Hence no edge of \(H\) joining \(c\) to a third candidate can exist: any such edge \(\{c,e\}\), with \(e\ne d\), would have to be generated by a cohesive \(e\) with \(I_e=\{e,c\}\), producing a voter whose ballot is \(\{e,c\}\), contrary to the restriction on supporters of \(c\). Thus \(c\) has degree at most one whenever it generates an edge. Every edge has at least one degree-one endpoint. A connected simple graph in which each edge has a degree-one endpoint is a star (including a single edge); in particular no cycles or paths with at least three edges occur.

**One representative covers every requirement in a component.** In a star with at least three vertices, only leaves can generate two-candidate requirements. Each leaf \(d\) then has \(I_d=\{d,h\}\) with \(h\) the center, while the center's own requirement, if present, is \(\{h\}\). Selecting \(h\) satisfies every cohesive candidate's requirement in that component. In a single-edge component \(\{a,b\}\), at least one endpoint has the two-candidate requirement \(\{a,b\}\). The other endpoint, if cohesive, either shares this requirement or has a singleton requirement at itself; select that other endpoint in the latter case, and either endpoint otherwise. In an isolated positive-weight component, select its sole candidate. Consequently, a single candidate can satisfy all cohesive-candidate requirements belonging to any fixed component.

No requirement intersects distinct graph components. A committee of size \(k\) can touch at most \(k\) components; its score is therefore at most the sum of the \(k\) largest positive component weights. Representatives of those components attain this bound. Extra seats cannot decrease the score. The graph and all intersections can be constructed from the length-two ballots in polynomial time; sorting the weights and finding representatives gives a polynomial-time optimum. This also gives the full-SJR feasibility criterion.

**Ballots with at most one approval.** If \(|A_i|\le1\), full supporter sets \(N_c\) for distinct candidates are disjoint. At most \(k\) such sets can contain at least \(|N|/k\) voters. Selecting every cohesive candidate, and padding to size \(k\), gives both SJR and SSJR.

**NP-hardness of SSJR with exactly two approvals.** Reduce from VERTEX COVER on simple cubic graphs, which is NP-complete. For a cubic graph \(G=(V,E)\) and proposed cover bound \(K\), create one candidate per vertex, one voter per edge \(\{u,v\}\), with ballot exactly \(\{u,v\}\), and set \(k=K\). There are \(3|V|/2\) voters. Each candidate has precisely three supporters; whenever \(|V|/2\le K<|V|\), each is cohesive because \(3K\ge3|V|/2\). Every edge-voter belongs to a cohesive candidate's supporter set. Therefore SSJR requires every edge-voter to approve a committee member; this holds exactly when the selected vertices form a vertex cover. Conversely, any vertex cover of size at most \(K\) can be padded to size \(K\). The cases \(K<|V|/2\) (necessarily a NO instance by counting edges), and \(K\ge|V|\) (necessarily YES), can be mapped to fixed NO and YES instances, respectively, from the cubic graph \(K_4\) with committee sizes two and three. These fixed instances also have exactly three approvals per candidate, all candidates cohesive, and exactly two approvals per ballot. Thus the promise is preserved for every input. SSJR verification is polynomial by scanning cohesive candidates and their supporters, proving membership in NP.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses only standard Python and integer arithmetic. Run `python3 verify.py`. It enumerates all \(67{,}028\) ordered profiles with up to four voters and up to five candidates, using nonempty ballots of size at most two, and all admissible committee sizes; the optimum from the star-component formula is compared with direct exhaustive committee enumeration. For the first \(25\) profiles of each size, an independent coalition-by-coalition test checks the groupwise SJR equivalence, totaling \(4{,}822\) committee checks. The program also constructs representatives, verifies that they attain the component bound, checks that every component has a suitable representative, and tests SSJR against vertex cover in the cubic graphs \(K_4\) and \(Q_3\). The output is `PASS exhaustive_profiles=67028 independently_checked_committees=4822 cubic_cases=3`.

This finite enumeration is not the proof of the unrestricted theorem; the combinatorial star lemma and the cubic-graph reduction are the infinite/general arguments.

## Relationship to prior work

Aziz et al. introduced the SJR and SSJR requirements. Chingoma, Grossi, Lindeboom and Maly defined the cohesive-candidate SJR/SSJR optimization scores, established general computational hardness, and gave polynomial-time optimization on candidate-interval and voter-interval approval domains. Their Theorems 15 and 20 and Appendix B/C do not give the ballot-arity-two star decomposition or an arity-two separation. The two-approval domain is not contained in the interval domains: the three ballots \(\{a,b\}\), \(\{b,c\}\), \(\{a,c\}\) cannot simultaneously be candidate intervals, nor can their three candidate-support sets simultaneously be voter intervals. Brill et al. study individual representation and related hardness phenomena, but that more demanding axiom and its constructions are distinct. The cubic-graph VERTEX COVER hardness used in the reduction is established background mathematics, not claimed here.

## Limitations

The polynomial algorithm concerns the SJR score and the one-cohesive-group SJR axiom under ballots of cardinality at most two. It does **not** make SSJR score maximization easy or claim tractability for ballots of size three. The NP-completeness result concerns SSJR *feasibility*, not the exact approximation threshold for maximizing its score. The star-decomposition theorem does not apply to definitions of strong representation requiring a number of approved winners for every higher-order cohesive group. The polynomial algorithm assumes explicitly listed ballots and unweighted voters. Priority over unpublished or unindexed earlier equivalent results is not excluded; independent verification has not been performed.
