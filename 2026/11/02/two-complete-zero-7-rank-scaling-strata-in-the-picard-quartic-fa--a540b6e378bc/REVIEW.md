PASS. Smoothness follows from the displayed quartic discriminant. Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus three and the listed differential basis. Direct coefficient extraction from the square and fourth power of the quartic, as reproduced by two exact implementations in the verifier, gives the displayed Cartier matrix, whose determinant and rank are checked algebraically, including the decisive identity \(\Delta=3R\) eliminating any smooth intermediate-rank stratum. On the only possible zero-rank divisor, the rank-one factorization and the twisted product \(B^{(7)}B\) prove the stated stable-rank criterion for every algebraically closed coefficient choice. The discriminant excludes the extraneous factor. Finite enumeration is explicitly not used for infinity claims. Orbit normalization is restricted to marked weighted coordinate scalings.

## Originality

PASS with residual priority risk. Sutherland's published general coefficient algorithm covers computation of superelliptic Cartier matrices, not the specific complete characteristic-seven rank-zero locus. Lario–Somoza and Kazemifard–Tafazolian focus on the individual CM Picard curve \(y^3=x^4-x\), one point of the first component, and related special Newton polygons. The full three-parameter stable-rank criterion and the second component are not implied by either special-curve description alone. Bouw and collaborators give general Picard models and conductor arithmetic rather than this rank locus. The restricted one-parameter slice \(y^3=x^4+ax^2+1\) does not itself imply the general \(b\ne0\) stable-rank locus. A stronger uninspected specialized formula or thesis might imply the conclusion; no global priority certification is claimed.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The ordinary/nonordinary dichotomy and the \(7\)-rank-zero locus in the natural three-coefficient Picard genus-three moduli chart is an arithmetic-geometric degeneration locus, not an arbitrarily selected finite example.
