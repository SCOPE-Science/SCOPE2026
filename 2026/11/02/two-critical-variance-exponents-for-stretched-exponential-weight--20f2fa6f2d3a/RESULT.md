# Two critical variance exponents for stretched-exponential weighted recursive trees

## Finding

Fix parameters \(c\in(0,1)\) and \(\gamma\in(0,1)\). For each \(j\ge2\), set \(p_j=cj^{-\gamma}\). In the weighted recursive tree defined below, let \(T_n\) be the sum of all root distances. Then
\[
\operatorname{Var}(T_n)\sim
\begin{cases}
 c B(1-\gamma,3)n^{3-\gamma},&0<\gamma<\tfrac12,\\[2pt]
 \dfrac{32c^3}{15(1+2c^2)}n^{5/2},&\gamma=\tfrac12,\\[6pt]
 \dfrac{c^3}{1-\gamma}B(2-3\gamma,3)n^{4-3\gamma},&\tfrac12<\gamma<\tfrac23,\\[6pt]
 3c^3n^2\log n,&\gamma=\tfrac23,\\[2pt]
 \sigma_{c,\gamma}^2n^2,&\tfrac23<\gamma<1,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(B(a,3)=\int_0^1t^{a-1}(1-t)^2\,dt=2/[a(a+1)(a+2)]\) for \(a>0\), and
\[
\sigma_{c,\gamma}^2=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}p_j^2v_j\in(0,\infty),\qquad
v_2=0,\qquad v_{j+1}=(1-p_j^2)v_j+p_j(1-p_j).
\]
For \(\gamma>2/3\), moreover,
\[
\frac{T_n-\mathbb ET_n}{n}\longrightarrow Z
\quad\text{almost surely and in }L^2,
\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z)=\sigma_{c,\gamma}^2>0.
\]
At \(\gamma\le 2/3\), the normalized centered path length has diverging variance and cannot converge in \(L^2\). These statements concern the path length, not the height, and make no claim of a Gaussian limit in the diverging-variance regimes.

## Assumptions and scope

Begin with a rooted tree containing vertex \(1\). Choose a deterministic sequence of strictly positive weights \((w_j)_{j\ge1}\) with \(W_j=\sum_{i=1}^jw_i\), \(w_1=1\), and
\[
\frac{w_j}{W_j}=p_j=cj^{-\gamma},\qquad j\ge2.
\]
Equivalently, \(w_j=W_{j-1}p_j/(1-p_j)\), so these positive weights exist uniquely. For each \(j\ge2\), independently attach vertex \(j\) to vertex \(K_j\in\{1,\ldots,j-1\}\) with
\[
\Pr(K_j=i)=\frac{w_i}{W_{j-1}}.
\]
Write \(D_1=0\), \(D_j=1+D_{K_j}\), and \(T_n=\sum_{j=1}^nD_j\). The parameters \(c\) and \(\gamma\) are fixed as \(n\to\infty\); the finite initial attachment rule is thus explicit. All asymptotics are pointwise in these parameters.

The same model has stretched-exponential total attachment weight,
\[
\log W_n=\sum_{j=2}^n-\log(1-p_j)
\sim\frac{c}{1-\gamma}n^{1-\gamma},
\]
and mean total path length
\[
\mathbb ET_n\sim\frac{c}{(1-\gamma)(2-\gamma)}n^{2-\gamma}.
\]

## Proof

**1. Exact orthogonal-increment decomposition.** Let \(\mathcal F_j\) be the filtration generated by parent choices through step \(j\), and set
\[
M_j=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^jw_iD_i}{W_j},\qquad
\xi_j=D_j-\mathbb E(D_j\mid\mathcal F_{j-1})=D_j-1-M_{j-1}.
\]
Since the new weighted mean is \(M_j=(1-p_j)M_{j-1}+p_jD_j\),
\[
M_j=M_{j-1}+p_j(1+\xi_j),\quad M_1=0.
\]
Thus
\[
D_j=1+\sum_{k=2}^{j-1}p_k+\sum_{k=2}^{j-1}p_k\xi_k+\xi_j,
\]
so the martingale differences \(\xi_j\) give the exact identity
\[
T_n-\mathbb ET_n=\sum_{j=2}^n\bigl[1+(n-j)p_j\bigr]\xi_j. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, writing \(v_j=\mathbb E\xi_j^2\), orthogonality yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}(T_n)=\sum_{j=2}^n\bigl[1+(n-j)p_j\bigr]^2v_j. \tag{2}
\]
These exact identities and the following recurrence are also established for arbitrary deterministic positive weights in Gałązka--Wdowicka (2026); their use here is credited rather than claimed as new.

**2. Variance recurrence.** The conditional weighted variance of previously observed depths is
\[
V_{j-1}=\sum_{i<j}\frac{w_i}{W_{j-1}}(D_i-M_{j-1})^2,
\]
with \(\mathbb EV_{j-1}=v_j\). Adding the new depth changes the weighted variance according to the elementary two-population identity
\[
V_j=(1-p_j)V_{j-1}+p_j(1-p_j)(D_j-M_{j-1})^2.
\]
As \(D_j-M_{j-1}=1+\xi_j\) and \(\mathbb E\xi_j=0\), taking expectations gives
\[
v_{j+1}=(1-p_j^2)v_j+p_j(1-p_j),\qquad v_2=0. \tag{3}
\]
The exact one-vertex probability-generating function also follows by averaging weighted depths:
\[
\mathbb E z^{D_j}=z\prod_{k=2}^{j-1}(1-p_k+p_kz).
\]
Hence \(\mathbb ED_j=1+\sum_{k<j,\,k\ge2}p_k\), which proves the displayed mean asymptotic by power summation.

**3. A three-regime recurrence asymptotic.** Put \(q_j=p_j^2\) and \(b_j=p_j(1-p_j)\). Iterating (3) gives the positive kernel
\[
v_j=\sum_{k=2}^{j-1}b_k\prod_{i=k+1}^{j-1}(1-q_i). \tag{4}
\]
If \(0<\gamma<1/2\), then \(q_j\sim c^2j^{-2\gamma}\), with \(jq_j\to\infty\), while \(b_j/q_j\sim j^\gamma/c\). The factors in (4) suppress terms older than \(j^{2\gamma+\varepsilon}\) for any \(0<\varepsilon<1-2\gamma\), since the omitted product is bounded by \(\exp[-Cj^\varepsilon]\). Within the surviving, sublinear window both \(p_k/p_j\) and \(q_k/q_j\) tend uniformly to one. Summing the asymptotically geometric kernel yields
\[
v_j\sim \frac{b_j}{q_j}\sim\frac{j^\gamma}{c}. \tag{5}
\]
At \(\gamma=1/2\), \(q_j=c^2/j\). For large \(k\le j\),
\[
\prod_{i=k+1}^{j-1}(1-c^2/i)
=(k/j)^{c^2}\bigl(1+O(1/k)\bigr)
\]
(up to an immaterial endpoint factor). Inserting this in (4), bounding a fixed initial segment by \(O(j^{-c^2})\), and comparing the remaining sum with its power integral yields
\[
v_j\sim c j^{-c^2}\sum_{k<j}k^{c^2-1/2}
\sim\frac{c}{c^2+1/2}j^{1/2}
=\frac{2c}{1+2c^2}j^{1/2}. \tag{6}
\]
For \(1/2<\gamma<1\), the series \(\sum_jq_j\) converges. On terms with \(k\) sufficiently large, the product in (4) is uniformly within an arbitrarily small multiplicative error of one; the finitely many earlier terms are negligible compared with the diverging sum of \(b_k\). Thus
\[
v_j\sim\sum_{k<j}b_k\sim\frac{c}{1-\gamma}j^{1-\gamma}. \tag{7}
\]
The three estimates imply
\[
p_j^2v_j\sim
\begin{cases}
 cj^{-\gamma},&0<\gamma<1/2,\\
 \dfrac{2c^3}{1+2c^2}j^{-1/2},&\gamma=1/2,\\
 \dfrac{c^3}{1-\gamma}j^{1-3\gamma},&1/2<\gamma<1.
\end{cases} \tag{8}
\]

**4. Exact variance asymptotics.** Separate (2) into
\[
P_n=\sum_{j=2}^n(n-j)^2p_j^2v_j,
\quad R_n=\sum_{j=2}^nv_j,
\quad\operatorname{Var}(T_n)=P_n+O(\sqrt{P_nR_n}+R_n). \tag{9}
\]
Here the error bound is Cauchy--Schwarz. From (5)--(7), \(R_n=O(n^{3/2})\) throughout \(0<\gamma<1\). If \(p_j^2v_j\sim Cj^\eta\) with \(\eta>-1\), elementary Riemann sums give
\[
P_n\sim Cn^{3+\eta}\int_0^1x^\eta(1-x)^2\,dx
=Cn^{3+\eta}B(\eta+1,3).
\]
For \(0<\gamma<2/3\), inserting (8) produces the first three cases of the theorem. At \(\gamma=2/3\), (8) becomes \(p_j^2v_j\sim3c^3/j\), and
\[
P_n\sim3c^3n^2\sum_{j\le n}\frac{(1-j/n)^2}{j}
\sim3c^3n^2\log n.
\]
In all four cases \(P_n/R_n\to\infty\), so (9) leaves the leading term unchanged.

For \(2/3<\gamma<1\), (8) makes \(\sum_jp_j^2v_j\) convergent. Expanding (2) and using (9),
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(T_n)}{n^2}
=\sum_{j=2}^n\left(p_j(1-j/n)+1/n\right)^2v_j
\longrightarrow \sum_{j=2}^\infty p_j^2v_j,
\]
by dominated convergence for the \(p_j^2v_j\) terms and \(R_n/n^2\to0\) for the remainder. Since \(v_3=p_2(1-p_2)>0\) and \(p_3>0\), the limit is strictly positive.

**5. Almost-sure and \(L^2\) limits.** For \(\gamma>2/3\), \(\sum_jp_j^2v_j<\infty\); therefore the martingale \(\sum_{j\le n}p_j\xi_j\) converges almost surely and in \(L^2\) to \(Z\). Rewriting (1) gives
\[
\frac{T_n-\mathbb ET_n}{n}
=\sum_{j=2}^np_j\xi_j
+\frac1n\sum_{j=2}^n(1-jp_j)\xi_j.
\]
For the remainder, orthogonality gives the sharper variance estimate
\[
\sum_{j=2}^n(1/n-p_jj/n)^2v_j
\le2R_n/n^2+2n^{-2}\sum_{j=2}^nj^2p_j^2v_j\longrightarrow0,
\]
where the last term follows by dominated convergence with weights \((j/n)^2\). For almost-sure convergence, \(\sum_j\mathbb E[((1/j-p_j)\xi_j)^2]\le 2\sum_jv_j/j^2+2\sum_jp_j^2v_j<\infty\). Martingale convergence of the corresponding series followed by Kronecker's lemma proves \(n^{-1}\sum_{j\le n}(1-jp_j)\xi_j\to0\) almost surely. Finally, for \(\gamma\le2/3\), the established variance asymptotics imply \(\operatorname{Var}[(T_n-\mathbb ET_n)/n]\to\infty\), precluding \(L^2\) convergence.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` enumerates every possible labeled parent-choice tree of orders two through seven under a nontrivial rational sequence of attachment fractions, compares exact rational means and variances with (1)--(3), and computes finite-size asymptotic diagnostics at five parameter values. It executes 32 assertions and writes its output to `verification_output.txt`. In particular, slow numerical convergence near \(\gamma=2/3\) is visible and is not disguised. These finite calculations do not prove the infinite-volume results, which rely on the arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Gałązka and Wdowicka (arXiv:2610.00614v1, September 30, 2026) established the exact identities (1)--(3) for general deterministic positive weights and proved the linear-fluctuation martingale theorem when \(p_j=O(1/j)\). The new statement concerns a different, stretched-exponential weight family with \(p_j\asymp j^{-\gamma}\), \(0<\gamma<1\), and derives sharp five-regime variance constants, two transition exponents, and the extended sharp \(L^2\) threshold by asymptotically solving their general recurrence. It does not claim invention of the general recurrence or of martingale convergence itself.

Sénizergues (arXiv:1904.07115v3) investigates geometry and profiles of weighted recursive trees under principally polynomial cumulative-weight assumptions; these do not include the stretched-exponential \(W_n\) here. Aguech and collaborators, *Exponentially preferential trees* (Network Science, 2025), study an alternative geometric-weight regime and different graph statistics. Neither source states the path-length phase diagram in this theorem. This is a literature comparison, not a proof of universal novelty.

## Limitations

The constants are for fixed \(c\in(0,1)\) and fixed \(\gamma\in(0,1)\); there is no uniform theorem in parameter-dependent critical windows. The result gives second-moment asymptotics, not Gaussian or other distributional limits below or at \(\gamma=2/3\). The nondegenerate martingale limit above \(2/3\) is not explicitly distributed. An existing exact general-weight recurrence can imply asymptotics after additional analysis; historical priority for the explicit phase diagram remains open to independent checking. The proof and numerical checks have not undergone independent validation.
