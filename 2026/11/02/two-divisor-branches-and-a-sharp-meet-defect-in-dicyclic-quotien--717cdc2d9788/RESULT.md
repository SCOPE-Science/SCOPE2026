# Two divisor branches and a sharp meet defect in dicyclic quotient-isomorphism posets

## Finding

Let \(m\ge 2\) be an integer, write \(m=2^s u\), with \(s\ge0\) and \(u\) odd, and let
\[
G_m=\langle a,b\mid a^{2m}=1,\ b^2=a^m,\ b^{-1}ab=a^{-1}\rangle
\]
be the dicyclic group of order \(4m\). For a finite group \(G\), let \(\operatorname{QIso}(G)\) comprise the isomorphism types \(G/N\), where \(N\triangleleft G\). Give it the order \(X\le Y\) if and only if \(Y\) embeds as a subgroup of \(X\), as in Halder–Ballal.

Define \(\mathsf T\) as the trivial group, \(\mathsf D_d\) as the dihedral group of order \(2d\), for each \(d\mid m\) (including \(\mathsf D_1\cong C_2\)), and \(\mathsf Q_e\) as the dicyclic group of order \(4\cdot2^s e\), for each \(e\mid u\) (with \(\mathsf Q_1\cong C_4\) when \(s=0\)). These are mutually distinct isomorphism types, and they are precisely the elements of \(\operatorname{QIso}(G_m)\).

The **complete order** is the reflexive, transitive closure of
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf D_d\le\mathsf D_f&\iff f\mid d,\\
\mathsf Q_e\le\mathsf Q_h&\iff h\mid e,\\
\mathsf Q_e&\le\mathsf D_1,\\
\mathsf D_1&\le\mathsf T.
\end{aligned}
\]
There are no other cross-branch comparisons. In particular, this poset is a join-semilattice with exactly two minimal elements \(\mathsf D_m\) and \(\mathsf Q_u\), but not a lattice. Among unordered pairs of **distinct** poset elements, exactly
\[
\boxed{\tau(u)\bigl(\tau(m)-1\bigr)}
\]
lack a meet, and every such pair is \((\mathsf D_d,\mathsf Q_e)\) with \(d\mid m\), \(d>1\), and \(e\mid u\). Here \(\tau\) counts positive divisors. Every cross-branch pair of this form has join \(\mathsf D_1\).

The number of quotient-isomorphism classes is
\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{QIso}(G_m)|=1+\tau(m)+\tau(u)=1+\tau(2m).}
\]
Thus \(G_m\) has the **same number** of quotient-isomorphism classes as the dihedral group \(\mathsf D_{2m}\) of the same order, while the latter poset is a distributive lattice (Halder–Ballal, Theorem 2.3).

## Assumptions and scope

The assertion holds for every integer \(m\ge2\), including odd \(m\) and every power of two. The dihedral convention is \(|\mathsf D_d|=2d\), so \(\mathsf D_2\cong C_2\times C_2\). The dicyclic convention includes the degenerate cyclic case of order four. The order on quotient types is **reverse group embeddability**; replacing it with inclusion of normal subgroups would give a different poset.

## Proof

**Normal-subgroup classification.** Put \(A=\langle a\rangle\cong C_{2m}\). All subgroups \(N_d=\langle a^d\rangle\), for \(d\mid2m\), are normal: each is characteristic in the cyclic normal subgroup \(A\). Let \(N\triangleleft G_m\) not be contained in \(A\). Write \(N\cap A=\langle a^d\rangle\) and choose \(a^r b\in N\). Its square is \(a^m\), hence \(d\mid m\). Conjugating \(a^r b\) by \(a\) produces \(a^{r+2}b\), hence \(a^2\in N\cap A\), so \(d\mid2\). If \(d=1\), then \(N=G_m\). If \(d=2\), necessarily \(m\) is even, and the only possibilities are \(\langle a^2,b\rangle\) and \(\langle a^2,ab\rangle\). Both are normal of index two. This exhausts the normal subgroups. Every subgroup is in fact generated by at most two elements: if it is not contained in \(A\), it is generated by its cyclic intersection with \(A\) and any representative of its outside coset.

**Quotient types.** The quotient \(G_m/N_d\) has order \(2d\) and the presentation
\[
\langle\bar a,\bar b\mid\bar a^d=1,\ \bar b^2=\bar a^m,\ \bar b^{-1}\bar a\bar b=\bar a^{-1}\rangle.
\]
If \(d\mid m\), this is \(\mathsf D_d\). If \(d\nmid m\), then \(d\mid2m\) forces \(d=2^{s+1}e\) with \(e\mid u\) and \(m\equiv d/2\pmod d\); the quotient is \(\mathsf Q_e\). The two exceptional index-two normal subgroups, when present, yield \(C_2\), already represented by \(N_1=A\); \(N=G_m\) gives \(\mathsf T\). Distinct \(d\)'s give distinct quotient orders, and the different normal subgroups of index two introduce no new quotient type.

**Embedding order.** A dihedral group \(\mathsf D_d\) embeds in \(\mathsf D_f\) exactly when \(d\mid f\): the standard rotation-reflection generators give sufficiency, while subgroup structure (including the \(d=1,2\) cases) gives necessity. Likewise, \(\mathsf Q_e\) embeds in \(\mathsf Q_h\) exactly when \(e\mid h\). Necessity follows from group order divisibility; sufficiency follows by taking the generators \(a^{h/e}\) and \(b\) in the target dicyclic group.

Every dicyclic group in the \(\mathsf Q\) branch has a unique involution. Each \(\mathsf D_d\) with \(d>1\) has at least two distinct involutions, so it cannot embed in any \(\mathsf Q_e\); \(\mathsf D_1=C_2\) does embed. Conversely, a noncyclic dicyclic group cannot embed into a dihedral group: every noncyclic subgroup of a dihedral group is dihedral and has multiple involutions. The only possible cyclic dicyclic type is \(\mathsf Q_1=C_4\) when \(s=0\); in that case all \(d\mid m=u\) are odd, and no \(\mathsf D_d\) contains an element of order four. Hence no \(\mathsf Q_e\) embeds into a \(\mathsf D_d\). Reverse these embedding relations to obtain the displayed poset order.

**Meets and joins.** Each branch separately is a reversed divisor lattice, with joins at greatest common divisors and meets at least common multiples. The trivial type \(\mathsf T\) is greatest, and \(\mathsf Q_e\le\mathsf D_1\) for all \(e\mid u\). For \(d>1\), the only quotient types embedding into both \(\mathsf D_d\) and \(\mathsf Q_e\) are \(\mathsf D_1=C_2\) and \(\mathsf T=1\), so the join is \(\mathsf D_1\). No available quotient type contains both as subgroups, so that pair has no lower bound and hence no meet. Every other pair has a meet and a join. There are \(\tau(m)-1\) admissible \(d>1\) and \(\tau(u)\) admissible \(e\), proving the meet-defect formula. The two maximal quotient types under embedding are \(\mathsf D_m\) and \(\mathsf Q_u=G_m\), giving exactly two minimal poset elements. Counting the branches and \(\mathsf T\) gives \(1+\tau(m)+\tau(u)=1+\tau(2m)\).

## Verification

The standalone `CHECK.py` constructs the multiplication table of \(G_m\), enumerates all subgroups from generator pairs, determines normality by conjugation, forms quotient multiplication tables, and tests all proposed quotient-type embeddings by exhaustive images of presentation generators. For every \(2\le m\le12\), it directly checks the complete normal-subgroup list, the quotient types, all pairwise embeddings, existence and uniqueness of joins, the exact number of meetless pairs, and the two minimal elements. `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt` records the completed run ending in `VERIFY_OK`.

These are exhaustive **finite checks only for the listed parameters**. The all-\(m\) assertion is proved symbolically above.

## Relationship to prior work

Halder and Ballal (2026, arXiv:2610.13076, Definition in Section 2, Theorem 2.3, and Remark 2.5) introduce the stated quotient-isomorphism order, prove that the dihedral poset is a distributive lattice with \(\tau(n)+1\) elements, and show that the dicyclic poset is not a lattice. Their result does **not** give the two-branch order or the exact meet-defect count established here. The older work of Tărnăuceanu (2018, arXiv:1805.12159) studies *solitary quotients* and their lattice; that is a different object from the poset of all quotient-isomorphism classes.

## Limitations

This is a complete determination only for cyclic-kernel dicyclic groups, not all generalized dicyclic extensions with arbitrary finite abelian kernels. The proof is elementary once the normal-subgroup and subgroup-embedding structures are derived; it does not establish a new general theorem on arbitrary finite-group quotient posets. Historical priority beyond the inspected literature is not certified. The computational checks cover only orders through \(48\).
