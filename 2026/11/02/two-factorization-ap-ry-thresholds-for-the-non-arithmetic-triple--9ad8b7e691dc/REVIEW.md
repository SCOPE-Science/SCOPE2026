# Scientific review of the two-representation threshold theorem

**Correctness (PASS).** A representation bijection indexed by the surplus wrap \(j\) and the number of highest-denomination coins gives exact possible coin lengths \(f(t)+2s\). Direct remainder checking proves that any wrapped representation first appears at \(j=1\), and the second same-layer representation wins for residues at least three. The exceptional small case \(m=4\) is treated explicitly. Independent unrestricted-denumerant dynamic programming checked every residue threshold for 147 parameters; all cases passed. The symbolic argument, rather than the finite checks, proves the infinite claim.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** The closest 2022 Komatsu–Ying theorem covers arithmetic-progression triples \(\langle a,a+d,a+2d\rangle\), not \(\langle m,m+1,m+3\rangle\). Their general \(p\)-Apéry definitions and the max/sum identities are prior work. O'Neill–Pelayo's theorem gives the ordinary first-representation Apéry set for shifted monoids and does not alone identify the second occurrence of a residue. Comparisons with general \(p\)-numerical semigroup bounds, general algorithms and accessible research records disclosed no identical piecewise 1-Apéry classification. An implicit specialization elsewhere in the literature remains possible; no exhaustive novelty assertion is made.

**Value (PASS).** The \(p=1\) layer of the generalized Frobenius problem is a natural published research target, more informative than the ordinary Frobenius number. The result supplies an exact entire residue-threshold structure for a non-arithmetic infinite family, including explicit Frobenius and genus values with a small-parameter boundary, rather than a numerical table or an arbitrary parameter slice.

The theorem classifies neither higher representation thresholds nor general three-denomination sets. Current independent validation is absent.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
