# Two fractional-widths exactly identify a blended point-lens microlensing event

## Finding

For fixed real fractions \(0<a<b<1\), the noiseless peak excess and two corresponding peak-referenced half-widths uniquely determine the source blend fraction, closest lens-source separation, and Einstein crossing time of a standard positive-blend point-source/point-lens event. The complete observation-feasibility region has an exact strict upper and attained lower boundary. The two-width ratio is strictly monotone in the intrinsic peak excess, so an observed point outside that region cannot be generated by this model. One half-width and the peak excess never suffice for unique identification.

Define, for \(y>0\),
\[
T(y)=2\left(\frac{1+y}{\sqrt{y(y+2)}}-1\right),\qquad
D_c(y)=T(cy)-T(y),\qquad
R_{a,b}(y)=\frac{D_a(y)}{D_b(y)}.
\]
The ratio \(R_{a,b}\) increases strictly from
\[
L_{a,b}=\frac{a^{-1/2}-1}{b^{-1/2}-1}
\quad\text{to}\quad
U_{a,b}=\frac{a^{-2}-1}{b^{-2}-1}
\]
as \(y\) increases from zero to infinity. In particular, \(L_{a,b}<R_{a,b}(y)<U_{a,b}\) for every finite \(y>0\).

Write \(\delta>0\) for the observed dimensionless peak excess (peak flux divided by baseline flux, minus one), and \(w_a,w_b>0\) for the *positive* elapsed times from the peak when the excess falls to \(a\delta,b\delta\), respectively. Then the exact necessary and sufficient condition for an event with source blend fraction \(0<f\le1\) is
\[
\boxed{\delta>0,\quad w_b>0,\quad R_{a,b}(\delta)\le
\left(\frac{w_a}{w_b}\right)^2<U_{a,b}.}
\]
The lower bound is attained precisely by unblended events \(f=1\), whereas the upper bound is a nonattained singular limit. Every feasible triple has exactly one associated \((u_0,t_E,f)\), given in the proof. The event-peak time itself is assumed measured and is not part of this parameter triple.

## Assumptions and scope

The source is a point; the lens is a single point mass moving at constant projected velocity relative to the source; the only additional flux is a nonnegative, time-independent blend, and baseline flux is positive and known. Photometry is noiseless in the theorem. There are no finite-source effects, parallax, finite exposures, acceleration, variable backgrounds, binary caustics, or negative blends. Set \(t_E>0\), \(u_0>0\), \(0<f\le1\), and let the peak occur at \(t_0\). The normalized flux model is
\[
\frac{F(t)}{F_{\mathrm{base}}}=1+f\bigl(A(u(t))-1\bigr),\qquad
A(u)=\frac{u^2+2}{u\sqrt{u^2+4}},\qquad
u(t)=\sqrt{u_0^2+\left(\frac{t-t_0}{t_E}\right)^2}.
\]
Here \(u(t)\), *not* a frequency, is the dimensionless lens-source separation. The two threshold levels \(a,b\) are fixed independently of the observed event and are fractions of its *observed flux excess*, not absolute amplification thresholds. All quantifiers over fractions and physical parameters are universal within these stated open/closed ranges. Time units can be arbitrary but are the same for both widths and \(t_E\).

## Proof

**1. Exact reduction.** Set \(y=A(u_0)-1>0\). The inverse of the strictly decreasing point-lens amplification on \(u>0\) is
\[
u_0^2=T(y)=2\left(\frac{1+y}{\sqrt{y(y+2)}}-1\right).
\]
The observed peak excess is \(\delta=fy\). When the measured excess falls to the fraction \(c\delta\), cancellation of the constant factor \(f\) gives \(A(u(t))-1=cy\). Consequently
\[
w_c=t_E\sqrt{T(cy)-T(y)}=t_E\sqrt{D_c(y)},\qquad
\left(\frac{w_a}{w_b}\right)^2=R_{a,b}(y).
\]
All widths are uniquely defined because \(A\) decreases strictly with \(u\), and \(u\) increases with elapsed time from the peak.

**2. Strict global monotonicity (not a finite experiment).** Exact differentiation gives
\[
T'(z)=-\frac{2}{[z(z+2)]^{3/2}},\qquad
D_c(y)=2y^{-1/2}\int_c^1s^{-3/2}(ys+2)^{-3/2}\,ds.
\]
Put \(h_y(s)=s^{-3/2}(ys+2)^{-3/2}\). Then
\[
R_{a,b}(y)=1+\frac{\int_a^b h_y(s)\,ds}{\int_b^1h_y(s)\,ds}.
\]
For any \(a\le s<b<t\le1\),
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\log\frac{h_y(s)}{h_y(t)}
=\frac32\left(\frac{t}{yt+2}-\frac{s}{ys+2}\right)
=\frac{3(t-s)}{(ys+2)(yt+2)}>0.
\]
To verify the last equality, combine the two fractions: the numerator is \(t(ys+2)-s(yt+2)=2(t-s)\); the factor \(3/2\) therefore yields \(3(t-s)\). Cross-multiplying and integrating this strict inequality over the positive-measure rectangle \([a,b]\times[b,1]\) proves that the ratio of the two integrals increases strictly with \(y\). Therefore \(R'_{a,b}(y)>0\) for every \(y>0\). This argument is valid for arbitrary real \(0<a<b<1\), not just sampled values.

Dominated convergence on the fixed interval \([a,1]\), first for \(y\downarrow0\) (when \(h_y\) is proportional asymptotically to \(s^{-3/2}\)), and then for \(y\to\infty\) after cancelling \(y^{-3/2}\) (when it is proportional to \(s^{-3}\)), gives
\[
\lim_{y\downarrow0}R_{a,b}(y)
=\frac{a^{-1/2}-1}{b^{-1/2}-1}=L_{a,b},\quad
\lim_{y\to\infty}R_{a,b}(y)
=\frac{a^{-2}-1}{b^{-2}-1}=U_{a,b}.
\]
The interval \((L_{a,b},U_{a,b})\) is therefore the exact range of the shape ratio.

**3. Feasible observation region, inverse and boundary.** Since \(0<f\le1\), the relation \(\delta=fy\) is equivalent to \(y\ge\delta\). Strict monotonicity and the open upper limit give the necessary inequality
\[
R_{a,b}(\delta)\le r<U_{a,b},\qquad r=(w_a/w_b)^2.
\]
Conversely, suppose \(\delta>0,w_b>0\) and that inequality holds. By continuity and strict monotonicity there exists a *unique* \(y\in[\delta,\infty)\) satisfying \(R_{a,b}(y)=r\). Define
\[
\boxed{u_0=\sqrt{T(y)},\qquad
 t_E=\frac{w_b}{\sqrt{D_b(y)}},\qquad
 f=\frac{\delta}{y}.}
\]
All these are positive, and \(0<f\le1\); direct substitution gives exactly \(\delta,w_a,w_b\), proving both sufficiency and uniqueness. The lower bound corresponds to \(y=\delta\), equivalently \(f=1\). The upper bound cannot occur at any finite \(y\); at fixed \(\delta\) it is approached as \(y\to\infty\), when \(u_0\to0\) and \(f\to0\). No claim of stable numerical inversion at this limit is made.

**4. Sharp one-width insufficiency.** Fix any \(\delta>0\) and one positive half-width \(w_b\). For each real \(y\in[\delta,\infty)\), the distinct triple
\[
\left(\sqrt{T(y)},\frac{w_b}{\sqrt{D_b(y)}},\frac{\delta}{y}\right)
\]
produces these same two observables. Thus the third observable \(w_a\) really supplies information absent from peak-plus-one-width data. This is an identifiability lower bound for the specified summary statistics, not a bound on the number of individual photometric samples.

## Relationship to prior work

The blended point-source/point-lens model and its practically severe photometric fitting degeneracies are established by Woźniak and Paczyński (first publicly available February 21, 1997). Their analysis considers the near-equivalence of fitted curves under finite measurement precision and in small-/large-impact asymptotic regimes. It does not state the strictly monotone two-fraction width ratio, the global exact inverse, or the precise physical image of the summary-data map.

Han (1999) derives forward analytic relationships for observed impact, duration and Einstein time under varying event-detection magnification thresholds. In particular its equations (2.5)–(2.10) contain known one-threshold forward information. The present theorem does **not** claim novelty for that forward width relation: it compares *two different fractions of each event's peak excess*, proves global strict monotonicity of the resulting ratio, and determines the exact feasible inverse region and the sharp one-width insufficiency. The latter assertions were not found in the inspected paper's §§1–3.

Smith et al. (2007) study blended survey light curves, errors and real detection effects, including parameter correlations. Their full accessible arXiv text, §§2 and 5–6, addresses simulated parameter recovery rather than this exact noiseless two-threshold invariant. The strict theoretical identifiability here neither refutes nor remedies the severe real-world ill-conditioning of heavily blended events.

## Limitations

This is an exact structural-identifiability and geometry-of-observables result for the specified idealized light curve; it is not a noise-robust inference method. It does not establish sufficient observational sampling, uncertainty intervals, uniqueness when finite-source, parallax, binary lens, time-variable blend or systematic photometric errors are allowed, or an inverse for negative-blend fractions. Very large intrinsic peak excess or very small excess may produce weak numerical sensitivity to the parameters despite exact uniqueness. The source comparison supports an originality assessment conditional on inspected literature, not exhaustive proof of first historical discovery or independent certification.
