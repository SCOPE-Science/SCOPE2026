None of these existing results is claimed as novel. Full relevant sections of arXiv:2610.32658v1 and arXiv:2607.02272v2 were compared. The new claim is the two precise Gaussian windows of the decomposed BK gap, the pivotal norm limit and critical-window correct-order consequence for the second bound, the conditional overlap profile, and the sharp rare-defect prefactor. Searches of published mathematical records on those objects, variants and limit profiles identified no equivalent complete finding. A residual priority risk is that the first paper's exact combinatorial identities, combined with classical probability asymptotics, make variants of these conclusions derivable without fundamentally new techniques. That risk is disclosed, not denied.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The two windows identify different mechanisms for failure of disjoint witnesses in one versus two independent configurations and determine the ranges where the strengthened inequality carries nearly the whole BK gap. The sharp pivot profile determines an additional natural tightness regime for the 2026 quantitative bound; the selected-overlap law identifies which configurations cause that failure; the left-tail prefactor resolves the rare-event scale. These form a coherent classification of a motivated threshold family, not an arbitrary parameter sample or a mere finite computation. The result neither improves the universal BK inequality nor resolves general events.
