# Scientific review: exact two-leaf coherent capture

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns ordinary adjacency hopping on the complete finite rooted \(b\)-ary tree, two distinct leaf absorbers, and \(b\geq3\), with no assumption about equal positive absorption rates. The proof independently derives the relevant spectral blocks, counts distinct spectral values through reduced rational angles and totients, and establishes that a single leaf has a nonzero component in every eigenspace. At the lowest common ancestor, the eigenvector restrictions on child subtrees agree outside the smaller subtree spectrum, while an antisymmetric construction across three branches exhibits two independently observed eigenvectors inside it. This precisely proves the extra \(D(h-1)\) bright dimensions. An invariant-subspace argument proves eventual capture of the entire bright component for any strictly positive rates, and no capture of the orthogonal component. A finite-field checker confirms the analytic identities for several sizes and two distinct primes, including 121 sites and noncanonical pairs; it also finds a binary-branch counterexample. Prime-field ranks are supplementary evidence rather than substitutes for the proof.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with priority limits

The coherent-dendrimer paper by Mülken, Bierbaum and Blumen (2006) studies untrapped recurrence and mixing, not two-leaf capture. Their related trapping paper (2007) treats survival laws and chains. The general spectral-Krylov transport principle already appears in Novo et al. (2014), including a single **root** absorber on a **binary** tree; therefore the abstract capture/projector identity is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. The finite branching-tree spectrum and its Jacobi decomposition are known from Scarabotti (2005/2006); the distinct-spectrum count for related level-symmetric trees is also discussed by Zhang (2023). Classical dendrimer trapping-time results by Lin and Zhang (2013) concern a different process. The exact two-**leaf** rank increment selected by common-ancestor depth, the necessity of a third branch, and the strict placement optimization are not stated or logically supplied by the inspected accessible sources without the new eigenvector comparison proved here. The full Scarabotti article could not be examined; historical completeness is not certified. Literature search cannot establish universal uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The all-depth expression converts a nontrivial two-site coherent-trapping design question into a simple number-theoretic dependence on separation. It gives a provably optimal physical placement rule, identifies a sharp branching threshold rather than extrapolating binary examples, and quantifies a quadratic versus exponential gap between the number of coherently accessible modes and the graph size. These are structural and practically interpretable conclusions, not a recomputation of a known single-trap spectral count. The result is limited to idealized coherent finite systems and does not predict actual finite-time capture rates.
