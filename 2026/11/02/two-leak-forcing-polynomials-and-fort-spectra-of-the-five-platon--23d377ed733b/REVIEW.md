# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
A two-leak forcing set intersects every nonempty subset with at most two outside unique-neighbor vertices; the two directions follow by a first-force obstruction and a terminal white-set argument. Increasing-mask induction exactly marks all complements containing a fort and therefore counts **every** candidate initial set. Independent direct propagation tests all leak placements for every initial subset in the four graphs of order at most twelve (all leak placements for successful subsets; one witnessed failure for unsuccessful subsets), and directly checks every optimal initial set plus deterministic additional examples in the dodecahedron. The complete twenty-vertex counts rest on the exhaustive-mask algorithm, not on the sampled second checker. Integer bounds and expected-vs-actual vectors are checked by the supplied verifier.

## Originality — PASS with limitations
The notion of leaky forcing, the fort duality, the counting-polynomial definition, and an exponential general algorithm are already known. The 2025 generalized Petersen paper proves the **one-leak** dodecahedral minimum and tabulates two-leak values only through \(GP(9,2)\), while its two-leak theorem is inapplicable to \(GP(10,2)\). The 2026 general polynomial paper computes different special graph families, not the Platonic exact polynomials or the five-support minimal-fort spectrum. No directly equivalent evaluated Platonic catalogue was found. Mathematical priority is necessarily provisional because not every niche table or alternate graph naming can be ruled out.

## Value — PASS
The five canonical highly symmetric graph skeletons provide a natural, bounded benchmark for fault-tolerant propagation. The exact enumerators distinguish a minimum *size* from the multiplicity of fault-tolerant placements; the \(20\)-vertex dodecahedron has a surprisingly heterogeneous minimal obstruction spectrum, in contrast to the \(12\)-vertex icosahedron. This is a motivated finite classification, not an asymptotic or general-family theorem.
