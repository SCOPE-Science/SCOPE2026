# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The Plücker quadratic form on a four-space detects decomposable bivectors. A two-dimensional anisotropic kernel splits off orthogonally, inducing a four-dimensional minus-type quadratic form on the target. The anisotropic norm-plane fiber counts (one solution over zero; \(q+1\) over nonzero scalars), the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) count for a fixed nonzero wedge, and the minus-type null-cone census yield the exact formula. The group cocycle and central-coordinate factors are checked. Finite verification for prime fields corroborates but does not replace the proof.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the listed residual risk. The centralizer and decomposable-free-kernel construction, including its extremal possible dimension, is covered by Gow–Quinlan and earlier isotropic-subspace literature, and the group order-\(p^8\) classification appears in Vaughan-Lee. Levit–Shwartz's 2026 commutator tensor reduction handles aggregate commuting counts, not this target-stratified word-map distribution. The exact two-valued nonzero fiber spectrum and its elliptic null-cone multiplicities have not been located in these relevant results or targeted mathematical records. Relevant source sections and the precise implication distinctions are documented in AUDIT.json. The remaining risk is an older equivalent calculation under commutator word maps, quadratic-form images, or alternating-tensor quotients.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The result determines the entire fiber spectrum of the natural commutator word map on a previously studied extremal centralizer family, not merely its average probability. In particular it proves nonuniform **surjectivity** with two prescribed nonzero multiplicities, a distinction not visible from the rank of any nonzero contraction or from ordinary commuting probability. It is uniform for every odd prime power and every qualifying anisotropic kernel.

## Limitations
Only four-dimensional quotient and target spaces with two-dimensional anisotropic kernel, and finite fields of odd order, are covered. No uniqueness of group isomorphism type is asserted. Prior results on maximally decomposable-free subspaces are acknowledged as known. Independent review remains outstanding.
