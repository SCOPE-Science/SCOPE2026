# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The equivalence \(x^2\equiv2q-1\pmod{4q}\iff x^2\equiv-1\pmod q\) for odd \(x\), the exact Hensel/CRT root count, the exact least-positive residue formula for \(x^3\), and the generator-independent translation pairing prove all assertions for every odd \(q>1\). The order and involution hypotheses are used explicitly. The finite verifier provides cross-checks only and is not promoted to a universal proof.

**Originality — PASS with specified residual risk.** Ma–Merca–Zhang treat the same invariant for products of two distinct odd primes, not even moduli \(4q\) of unbounded odd-prime support. Pomerance–Ulmer's full-coset half-interval balance is stronger and their classification does not state the exact two-level first-moment sum law. The general distribution-vector literature is relevant but its older full text was inaccessible; consequently the priority comparison is best-knowledge, not a uniqueness certificate. Searches of the accessible mathematical research index for quartic subgroup residue sums, the \(2q-1\) involution, and even moduli produced no implying theorem.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a natural, complete local-fiber classification of the centered-residue problem, not a lone example or arbitrary numeric cutoff. It gives an exact existence criterion, the count for arbitrary repeated-prime factorizations, and a balanced/unbalanced involution pairing. It distinguishes subgroup sum-centering without \(-1\) from the substantially stronger coset-balancing property and supplies a clean boundary relevant to recent odd-semiprime analysis. The result concerns only order-four groups with the stated involution.

**Closest sources.** Ma–Merca–Zhang (2026), arXiv:2610.29608v1; Pomerance–Ulmer (2012), arXiv:1204.6705v1; Girstmair (1997), DOI:10.1007/BF02940820 (abstract only); Girstmair (2025), arXiv:2509.21094v1. The exact unrestricted even-modulus formula is not explicitly present in the inspected material. An older implicit or explicit specialization cannot be ruled out independently.
