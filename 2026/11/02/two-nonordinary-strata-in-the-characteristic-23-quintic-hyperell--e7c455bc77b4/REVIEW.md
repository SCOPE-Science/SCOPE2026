# Mathematical review

## Correctness: PASS
The smoothness resultant is \(3(1-t^4)\) modulo \(23\); the genus is two. Independent multinomial and iterative expansions give the same four coefficient polynomials. The Frobenius matrix, determinant, and rank drop are exact. A one-step rank-one matrix need not imply geometric rank one: the proof uses the nonzero eigenvalue \(13\) of the two-step Frobenius and the fact that the four parameters lie in \(\mathbb F_{23^2}\). Scheme multiplicities and the scaling isomorphisms are independently checked. Residual risk: the known convention ambiguity between Cartier–Manin and Hasse–Witt requires care; both are transposes of the relevant coefficient matrix, and the reported stable-rank conclusion is invariant in this explicit triangular case.

## Originality: PASS, qualified
Zhu (arXiv:2606.31783, Sections 3–4) proves generic ordinarity and states a universal Hasse–Witt coefficient formula but does not determine all \(23\)-rank strata, the nonreduced divisor, or the unique nonzero exceptional geometric orbit. The superspecial central model is known and is explicitly not advanced as new on its own (DOI:10.1515/jmc-2019-0021). General hyperelliptic moduli-stratification results of Achter–Pries are not an explicit calculation for this pencil. Public search and published-record checks found no precise equivalent; catalogues under different models could still overlap. The exact determinant is a specialization of Zhu's coefficient formula, but that specialization by itself does not determine the stable geometric rank or the full classification.

## Mathematical value: PASS
This is a natural complete classification of all geometric fibers by two Jacobian invariants within a standard pencil used in the generic-ordinarity problem. It exhibits a fat superspecial parameter and one reduced orbit of intermediate-rank fibers in the same pencil, distinguishes parameter multiplicities from isomorphism classes, and determines the least extension containing the nonzero exceptional parameters. The precision is needed to understand degeneration inside a generically ordinary family; a generic-ordinarity statement does not supply this behavior. The characteristic is selected for its simultaneous jump patterns, not as an arbitrary tabulation.

## Limits and literature
The result is specific to characteristic \(23\), without a classification of every Newton polygon or endomorphism algebra. The exact coefficient formula and known central superspecial curve are fully acknowledged. Historical priority remains provisional for alternate presentations of the same pencil, and independent validation is not claimed.
