## Originality — PASS, qualified

Patrón, Plata and Prados (arXiv:2407.21110) analyze minimum-time protocols for the same Brownian gyrator, including time-dependent equal-time covariance, but do not state the two-contrast, bath-independent, all-protocol inverse or the exact reachable observation region. Baldassarri, Puglisi and Sesta (arXiv:2009.06989) inverse-engineer traps from equal-time covariance and bath parameters, which is not the same observation problem. Ferreira, Metz and Barucca (arXiv:2409.01262) give the standard lagged Ornstein–Uhlenbeck covariance propagator in a different stationary framework. **That generic propagation formula is acknowledged as prior knowledge, not presented as new.** The specific noise-cancelling contrast compression, complete constrained image, and sharp sensitivity are the scientific claim. Cerasoli and coauthors (arXiv:2011.03124) develop time-dependent effective temperatures rather than the proposed lagged-control inverse. Related mathematical records and accessible earlier findings were compared for direct implication and did not identify an encompassing statement. A broader unindexed system-identification theorem may subsume the calculation, so priority needs independent checking.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The same source model treats how fast a nonequilibrium steady state can be driven to another under a bounded control triangle; deciding what drive actually occurred from observations is a natural complementary mathematical question. The result proves that just two carefully selected lagged second moments, rather than the full covariance path or knowledge of bath temperatures, uniquely identify *integrated* control, and gives a **complete** reachable set with a constant-protocol realization of every point. The exact inverse singular values quantify which physically permitted protocols are poorly distinguishable, while the unequal-bath theorem guarantees a nondegenerate reference in the standard model. These are natural full-parameter structural facts, not a single arbitrary numeric sample or a reformulation of the basic propagator alone.

## Boundaries and priority risks

Only the symmetric, simultaneously diagonalizable deterministic drift is covered. The triangular reachability region assumes bounded nonnegative-semidefinite stiffness, including boundary controls. The result is population-level, not a measurement-noise or finite-sample confidence theorem. General controlled linear-system identification may provide broader unindexed prior implications, and originality has not been independently certified. Instantaneous recovery requires differentiation, while the integrated reconstruction does not. If \(d_s=0\), only the selected two contrasts are noninformative; other measurements may still identify controls.
