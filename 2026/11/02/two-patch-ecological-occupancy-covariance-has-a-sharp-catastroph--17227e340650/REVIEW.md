# Review of the two-patch stationary covariance phase law

## Correctness — PASS

The forward generator on the four states has a unique positive stationary law. The two independent balance equations and normalization determine it exactly; the cross-patch covariance sign is that of the explicit quadratic \(F(d)\), with a positive denominator. At fixed catastrophe rate \(d\), \(F\) is strictly increasing in the conditional colonization rate \(b\). The zero curve has exactly one finite stationary point if and only if \(a>2e\), established by an exact discriminant and verified boundary cases. This proves the full two-reversal region and every equality case. Independently solving the balance equations over rational parameter grids and checking the exact example produced 2103 successful assertions. Finite computation is not used to infer the infinite classification.

Historically equivalent results in inaccessible full papers, older thesis chapters, or reliability theory remain possible.

A closely related additive-neighbour-colonization two-site model uses neighbour-*enhanced* colonization and varies a correlated-shock *fraction* at fixed marginal rate; has the conditions \(b\geq a\), whereas the new double-reversal region has \(b<a/2\). Related monotone mean- and joint-occupancy results do not imply the new covariance sign law. Related three-patch or Gaussian synchrony statements concern different observables and dynamical generators.

## Value — PASS

The theorem gives a sharp structural answer to when a common environmental shock can produce an unexpected intermediate interval of positive cross-site occupancy association despite negative association at both smaller and larger shock rates. The boundary \(a=2e\) and suppression threshold \(b_*\) distinguish mechanisms; standard facilitative recolonization cannot exhibit the phenomenon. This is a natural complete finite-state ecological classification with precise mathematical limits, not an empirical field claim.

## Limitations

All conclusions assume two identical habitats, constant transition hazards, complete shared resets and known occupancy. The theorem is not a statement about large networks, imperfect detection, survival time or actual field ecology. Some highly related earlier articles are accessible only by abstract, leaving material historical-priority uncertainty. The arithmetic checker is independent of the analytic derivation but does not constitute independent scholarly validation.
