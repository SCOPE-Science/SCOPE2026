**Claim reviewed:** identical exact two-rank Frobenius pilot data can conceal opposite optimal allocations between the specific ideal one-probe and mixed-probe log-determinant estimators, yielding an unbounded competitive MSE ratio.

**Correctness — PASS.** The two spectra have exactly the same rank-\(27k\) and rank-\(36k\) squared Frobenius tails; sorting follows from \(0<\varepsilon<1/\sqrt{p}\). Gaussian quadratic-form variance yields the exact diffuse excess factor \((4k+1)(1-1/(192k))\). The concentrated family makes the one-probe MSE vanish while mixed MSE approaches a positive limit, so pilot-identical decisions cannot be uniformly competitive. The source's own ideal switching formula always selects one, as a strict rational inequality. The three-step Lanczos calculation is exact here because the residual matrices have no more than three distinct eigenvalues. The included grouped-spectrum verifier passed 5,575 finite checks. The infinite result follows from explicit formulas, not enumeration.

**Originality — PASS with residual historical-priority risk.** The principal prior paper explicitly gives the one/mixed estimators, Gaussian MSE formulas, and Frobenius proxy rule (Sections 2 and 4.3), so none is represented as a discovery. Section 5 and conclusion report numerical behavior rather than a pilot-data indistinguishability theorem. The two spectra, the incompatibility between their ideal MSE optima, and the unbounded-factor conclusion are the additional results. Prior Hutch++ adaptive trace estimation can access different information and does not imply this lower bound. Searches of the relevant published mathematical record for two-pilot log-determinant oracle indistinguishability, rank-budget allocation and spectral tail constructions found no stronger covering claim; search absence alone is not priority proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result identifies an information bottleneck in an explicitly motivated, recently proposed allocation heuristic: knowing the exact two Frobenius tail energies does not determine how the intervening spectral energy is distributed. The construction shows arbitrarily serious *idealized* allocation loss even with a nearly identity regularized system and with finite-step quadrature error removed. This distinguishes the structural limitation of a two-number diagnostic from implementation noise and suggests why additional information about tail localization may matter.

**Scope and residual risks.** The result does not establish failure probability, bias, or MSE for the paper's actual randomized Nyström/leave-one-out implementation; it covers exact best-rank spectral preconditioners and fixed two-pilot decision information. Oracle cost accounting is an idealization, although all quadratic forms are exact in three Lanczos steps on the constructed spectra. The relevant full text and adaptive trace-estimation reference were inspected for closest prior coverage; broader historical-priority uncertainty remains. Independent review is outstanding.
