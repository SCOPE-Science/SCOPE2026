# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The killed Laplacian is positive definite on every connected graph with two positive trap rates. The exact Poisson equation, vertex-transitive Green diagonal, two-by-two trap flux balance, resistance identity, strict allocation derivative, and AM–GM uniqueness together prove every asserted quantifier. The 3-by-3 torus example has an independently computed rational Green inverse; `verify.py` cross-checks full killed-generator inversions on several transitive families. Finite numerical tests are corroboration, not the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with historical-priority risk.** Beveridge's full 2015 Green-function paper is the closest examined underlying method; its single-target and pseudoinverse statements do not themselves assert a two-reactive-site budget allocation or the global maximum-resistance placement law. Lin–Julaiti–Zhang give a general single-perfect-trap hitting formula; Ghosh–Boyd–Saberi optimize conductances on a fixed graph, not reactive-site placement. A related two-trap cycle computation has a fixed localized initial point and unequal optimum, unlike the present uniform-start all-transitive-graph classification. Further historical priority review is warranted: an older general multiple-target resolvent formula may imply this result.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The universal placement and budget-independence law solves a natural joint spatial and chemical design problem across every finite connected vertex-transitive graph, without enumerating target pairs and solving a killed random-walk inverse for each budget. The exact 3-by-3 torus comparison exhibits a strict, parameter-independent lifetime gain, demonstrating that the placement criterion has quantitative consequences.

The scope is uniform-start, symmetric continuous-time diffusion, two positive local killing rates and first mean capture times. No claim is made for directed transport, nonuniform start or more than two traps.
