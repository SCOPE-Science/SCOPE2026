# Two-row product modal-atom dichotomy and exact occupancy laws

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge1\), let \(q=2^k\) be the number of propositional colours. Take \(m\ge1\) columns of two worlds each. Horizontal accessibility relates all worlds in the same row, and vertical accessibility relates the two worlds in the same column; both relations are reflexive equivalence relations. Give the top and bottom worlds of column \(j\) colours \((a_j,b_j)\in[q]^2\). Write \(E=\{(a_j,b_j):1\le j\le m\}\) and \(E^\top=\{(b,a):(a,b)\in E\}\).

**Transpose-support dichotomy.** Let \(Z\) be the number of atoms of the Boolean subalgebra generated by the original \(k\) propositional truth sets and closed under both modal diamonds. Then
\[
Z=\begin{cases}|E|,&E=E^\top,\\2|E|,&E\ne E^\top.\end{cases}
\]
Every atom is definable at modal depth at most two, and this bound is attained. In particular, the sharp maximum over all valuations on the \(2\times m\) frame is
\[
\max Z=2\min\{m,q^2-1\}.
\]
The atom count is not monotone under adding columns: with \(q=2\), support \(\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}\) yields six atoms, but adjoining \((1,0)\) reduces the count to four.

**Exact random law.** If the \(m\) ordered column pairs are independent and uniform in \([q]^2\), put \(N=q^2\), and for \(0\le t\le N\) define
\[
S_t=[z^t](1+z)^q(1+z^2)^{q(q-1)/2}.
\]
Using Stirling numbers of the second kind, for each integer \(\ell\),
\[
\Pr(Z=\ell)=\frac{1}{N^m}\sum_{t=1}^{\min\{m,N\}}
 t!\left\{\begin{matrix}m\\t\end{matrix}\right\}
 \left[S_t\mathbf1_{\ell=t}+(\tbinom Nt-S_t)\mathbf1_{\ell=2t}\right].
\]
For fixed \(q\ge2\), the random variable \(Z\) tends to \(q^2\) in probability as \(m\to\infty\), while
\[
\mathbb E Z=q^2+q\big((q-1)(q^2-2)-1\big)(1-q^{-2})^m
 +O_q\big((1-2q^{-2})^m\big).
\]
The displayed correction coefficient is positive for every \(q\ge2\), so the expectation eventually approaches its limit from above.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix integers \(k,m\ge1\), with \(q=2^k\). The frame carrier is \(W=\{0,1\}\times\{1,\dots,m\}\). Define \((i,j)H(i',j')\) exactly when \(i=i'\), and \((i,j)V(i',j')\) exactly when \(j=j'\). The horizontal and vertical diamonds are the relational existential images under \(H\) and \(V\). The generated algebra is the smallest Boolean subalgebra of \(\mathcal P(W)\) that contains the truth sets of the \(k\) atomic propositions and is closed under both diamonds. The word *atom* means a nonempty minimal Boolean element, equivalently a class of worlds with exactly the same truth values for all formulas using those variables and modalities. No third or diagonal product operator is assumed. The random-law statement refers to independently and uniformly sampled ordered colour pairs; rows and columns are not identified by frame constants.

## Proof
**1. Naming ordered column types.** For each colour \(a\in[q]\), let \(\chi_a\) be the propositional Boolean conjunction characterizing that colour. For distinct colours \(a\ne b\), the depth-one formula \(\theta_{a,b}=\chi_a\land\Diamond_V\chi_b\) holds at exactly the worlds whose own colour is \(a\) and whose column partner has colour \(b\). For equal colours set \(\theta_{a,a}=\chi_a\land\Box_V\chi_a\). Since every vertical equivalence class contains exactly two worlds, this also selects precisely ordered type \((a,a)\). Consequently, depth one distinguishes all realized ordered type labels, and its row-zero labels form \(E\) while its row-one labels form \(E^\top\).

**2. Asymmetric support.** Suppose \(E\ne E^\top\); choose an ordered pair \((a,b)\in E\setminus E^\top\). Then \(\Diamond_H\theta_{a,b}\) holds at every world in row zero and no world in row one. This depth-two formula defines the row. Its conjunction with the depth-one type formulas therefore distinguishes every occupied ordered type in each row, yielding \(2|E|\) nonempty pairwise disjoint definable classes. Two worlds in the same row having the same ordered column type are related by a colour-preserving bisimulation: their vertical successors have the two types \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\), and their horizontal successor type sets are the same, independently of which column is chosen. Thus no finer definable partition exists.

**3. Symmetric support.** Suppose \(E=E^\top\). Relate any two worlds having the same ordered column type, including worlds from different rows. The relation preserves propositional colours. Vertical successors have matching ordered types \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\). For horizontal successors, both rows exhibit exactly the same set of ordered types, namely \(E\), so the back-and-forth conditions hold. This is a bisimulation and therefore every formula is constant on each ordered type. The formulas \(\theta_{a,b}\) distinguish those \(|E|\) types, proving the other case. Both constructions prove the depth-two upper bound.

**4. Sharp depth, sharp maximum, nonmonotonicity.** For \(q=2\) and \(E=\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}\), row palettes are identical, hence depth-one formulas give four classes, while depth two gives six. Since \(1\le |E|\le\min(m,q^2)\), an asymmetric support has at most \(2\min(m,q^2-1)\) atoms; a symmetric support has at most \(\min(m,q^2)\) atoms, which is bounded by the same maximum because \(q\ge2\). For every \(1\le t\le q^2-1\), an asymmetric support of cardinality \(t\) exists: choose one off-diagonal ordered pair but omit its transpose, then take any \(t-1\) of the other \(q^2-2\) pairs. Repetition fills all remaining columns, realizing the bound. Adjoining the missing pair in the displayed three-column example makes \(E\) transpose-invariant with four types, and the count drops from six to four.

**5. Exact occupancy distribution.** For any fixed support \(E\) of size \(t\), the number of length-\(m\) sequences of \(q^2\) equally likely ordered-pair types that have exactly support \(E\) is \(t!\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}m\\t\end{smallmatrix}\right\}\), by surjective occupancy. A transpose-invariant support consists of a chosen subset of the \(q\) diagonal ordered pairs together with whole transpose-orbits of off-diagonal pairs. There are \(q(q-1)/2\) two-element orbits. Therefore the coefficient \(S_t\) counts all symmetric supports of size \(t\); the other \(\binom{q^2}{t}-S_t\) supports are asymmetric. Substitution into the deterministic dichotomy proves the stated exact law.

**6. Limit and expectation.** The probability some ordered type is absent is at most \(q^2(1-q^{-2})^m\). Hence all ordered types are present with probability tending to one, and \(Z=q^2\) on that event. For the expectation, the all-present support contributes no deviation from \(q^2\). If exactly one type is missing, the deviation is \(-1\) for each of the \(q\) diagonal types, and \(q^2-2\) for each of the \(q(q-1)\) off-diagonal types. The probability of omission of any specified single type, with all others present, is \((1-q^{-2})^m+O_q((1-2q^{-2})^m)\). The total probability of at least two omissions is \(O_q((1-2q^{-2})^m)\). Summing the deviations gives the claimed expansion; its leading coefficient is positive for \(q\ge2\).

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `checker.py` directly refines the propositional colour partition by sets of horizontal and vertical successor classes until stable. It enumerates all \(17{,}208\) assignments for \(q=2\), \(m\le6\); \(q=3\), \(m\le4\); and \(q=4\), \(m\le3\). For each it compares the resulting number and first stable modal depth against the formulas, and independently checks the exact distribution by exhaustive aggregation. Run `python3 checker.py`; the expected terminal line is `ALL_CHECKS_OK complete_coloured_models= 17208`. Additional separate support-level enumeration compared the conclusion on all nonempty supports for \(q=3\) and \(q=4\), respectively \(511\) and \(65{,}535\) supports. Finite enumeration is corroboration; the infinite claims follow from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Aghamov, Kudinov, Nguyen and Piribauer (2026) formulate modal semantics for full products of neighborhood frames and derive modal interaction axioms. That theory motivates precise analysis of generated algebras on small rectangular relational special cases; its inspected statement and product-axiom arguments do not give the present transpose-support atom counts or random occupancy distribution. Shapirovsky and Sliusarev (2024/2026) give general criteria for local tabularity of products and bounded factors. A qualitative bound on distinguishable modal types is not an exact sharp count, modal-depth bound, or support-by-support probability law. The present result concerns one specific complete two-world factor and the normal two-diamond relational reduct, not global local tabularity of arbitrary products.

## Limitations
The classification requires exactly two worlds in one factor and full equivalence accessibility in both factors. It does not extend without further proof to larger first factors, arbitrary neighborhood semantics, diagonal modalities, or infinitely many worlds. The asymptotic expansion fixes the number of propositional colours before the column count tends to infinity. Priority relative to older specialized bisimulation and occupancy literature is not exhaustively settled; searches and inspected papers cannot prove absolute novelty.
