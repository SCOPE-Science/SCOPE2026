For the intersection of a square with a concentric disk, the probability that two uniform points are almost diametrically separated has a sharp \(\varepsilon^{5/2}\) law until the circular diameter arcs collapse into the square's corners, where the power becomes \(\varepsilon^4\). Two explicit, distinct joint-scaling limits describe this collision: a piecewise \(H(z)\) curvature-scale law and a quadratic \(J(t)=1/24+t/3+t^2/2\) corner-scale law.
