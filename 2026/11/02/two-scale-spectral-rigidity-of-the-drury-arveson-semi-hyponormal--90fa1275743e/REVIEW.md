# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The operator-matrix identity \(L_n=(I+nR_n)/(n+1)\) is derived directly from normalized bosonic creation and annihilation actions, not inferred from a test. The Gram factorization \(R_n=V_nV_n^*/n\), together with \(V_n^*V_n=(n+d-1)I\), yields both eigenvalues and their exact multiplicities for each finite homogeneous block. Direct summation of dimensions, strict monotonicity of each spectral branch, and asymptotic inversion of the cumulative counts prove the compactness, sharp Schatten membership and critical logarithmic coefficients for all dimensions. A direct finite-block construction passed 1,012 checks in four dimensions. The calculations were inspected at their actual standalone artifact path. Numerical checks do not replace the proof.

## Originality

PASS on current accessible evidence, with residual bibliographic risk. The most direct primary source, Curto–Yoon Theorem 3.18, only derives one negative determinant in a two-dimensional degree-one compression, not the full all-degree and all-dimension spectrum or split Schatten thresholds. Arveson's \(d\)-shift commutator estimates and the Kennedy–Shalit essential-normality analysis concern first-order commutators rather than the nonlinear difference of square roots in this result. Accessible related research records and earlier claims were searched for exact and reduced statements, including different phrasings of Gram roots, semi-hyponormality, spectral multiplicity, and Schatten behavior. None implied the displayed two-branch law. Searches cannot establish publication priority, and an earlier 2025 publication of the motivating paper precedes its 2026 arXiv upload.

## Mathematical value

PASS. A qualitative nonpositivity witness for a prominent universal commuting shift is upgraded to the entire compact indefinite spectrum. The two distinct decay exponents, differing by a factor four at the level of degreewise spectral orders, expose a structural asymmetry between the positive and negative spectral defects; the associated sharp ideal and logarithmic endpoint laws are natural operator-theoretic invariants rather than an arbitrary finite slice. Their exact dimension dependence offers a benchmark for analogous nonlinear matrix-root defects on quotient Hilbert modules.
