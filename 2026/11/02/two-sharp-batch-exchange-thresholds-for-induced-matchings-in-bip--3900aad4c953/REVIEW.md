# Scientific review: batch exchanges on bipartite subdivisions

**Correctness — PASS.** For the once-subdivided complete bipartite graph with both part sizes at least three, inducedness forces every selected edge from an original vertex on one side to avoid the original vertices used on the other side. This proves an upper bound of \(m+n-2\); equality forces the unique omitted row and column and hence one explicitly described optimum matching per ordered pair. The four exact intersection cases give simultaneous replacement costs \(m\), \(n\), and \(m+n-2\). These imply the complete threshold graph classification, including the sharp connectivity threshold \(\max\{m,n\}\). Both included standard-library programs run successfully and independently verify small parameter instances; their finite scope is distinct from the all-parameter proof.

**Originality — PASS, with stated uncertainty.** The inspected 2013 manuscript by Dabrowski, Demange and Lozin establishes induced-matching optimization and complexity results, including those for hypercubes, but no once-subdivided biclique batch-exchange theorem. The inspected 2024 paper by Křišťan and Svoboda establishes general simultaneous token movement results; its moves have underlying graph-distance restrictions, and its theorems do not identify our exact optimum-state graphs. Targeted source searches, published-result searches, and accessible historical finding claims revealed no statement that implies the present classification. Matching only the maximum matching cardinality or the existence of a generic global reconfiguration route would not imply the sharp thresholds. Undiscovered equivalent work remains a residual risk; novelty searches are not uniqueness proofs.

**Value — PASS.** The main information is not merely a new optimum number: the complete optimum solution space has three sharp local-mobility regimes. On one fixed natural infinite family of bipartite graphs of girth eight and average degree strictly below four, every uniformly bounded simultaneous-edge-exchange rule eventually fails to connect all optima, and the necessary budget is exactly the larger bipartition size. The intermediate disconnected union of cliques and the rook-graph transition pinpoint distinct barriers and give explicit tight local-search obstructions rather than an arbitrary finite enumeration.

**Closest literature and limits.** Dabrowski–Demange–Lozin (2013), *New results on maximum induced matchings in bipartite graphs and beyond* (DOI `10.1016/j.tcs.2013.01.027`); Křišťan–Svoboda (2024), *Reconfiguration Using Generalized Token Jumping* (arXiv:2411.12582). Unrestricted simultaneous edge replacements differ from graph-distance-limited token slides, and no claim is made for intermediate nonoptimal matchings, general subdivided graphs, or smaller part sizes.
