# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Both published implicit randomized schemes reduce to the same scalar stability multiplier. Inversion of its strictly increasing signed value gives the density \((1-q)^{-2}\) on exact endpoint limits. Squaring introduces two inverse branches exactly when \(r>1\); the derived CDF and piecewise quantile agree at each boundary, including the second transition at \(r=\sqrt2\). The layer-cake Fréchet lower bound proves the global infimum among *every* uniform-marginal joint law, and conditional lifting produces an attainable stage coupling. Strict monotonicity proves the unique simple antithetic optimizer for \(r\le1\); a measure-preserving partner swap proves its failure for every \(r>1\). The independent verifier reproduces the quantile distribution and numerical benchmark, but the mathematical proof does not rely on a finite test.

**Originality — PASS, qualified best-knowledge assessment.** The original randomized implicit Runge–Kutta source studies independently sampled stages and does not give a fixed-decay optimal pair coupling. Puccetti and Wang's classical countermonotone extremality supplies the *general probability inequality*, which is credited and not claimed new. The present method-specific piecewise squared-amplification law, the sharp antithetic-to-rank-coupling change at \(r=1\), the distinct tail-branch transition at \(r=\sqrt2\), and the exact optimizable Runge–Kutta contraction invariant were not found in the sources inspected. A related available claim proves antithetic *pathwise, all-step-size* rigidity, not mean-square optimality for a fixed decay. A broader theorem or earlier specialized calculation may still cover the explicit transitions; independent priority verification remains necessary.

**Value — PASS.** Fixed-parameter pair coupling is a natural numerical-design choice because it preserves each stage's required uniform law and changes only their dependence. The theorem provides a sharp exact optimum for a measurable numerical stability invariant, quantifies where familiar stage-antithetic coupling ceases to minimize squared amplification, and shows almost a tenfold gain at \(r=2\). Both thresholds are dictated by genuine sign-geometry changes in the published stability multiplier, not arbitrary parameter cuts. The result is deliberately limited to scalar decay, fixed step and two-step mean-square amplification: it neither preserves the original nonlinear accuracy proof nor certifies long-horizon pathwise robustness for the optimizing coupling.

**Closest work and limitations.** Bochacik–Przybyłowicz (2024 preprint; revised 2025) introduce and analyze the independently randomized method. Puccetti–Wang (2015) supply the general bivariate extremal-dependence principle. Related antithetic stability results concern a different universally quantified pathwise problem; neither explains the sharp fixed-step cost-rank transitions proved here. Numeric \(r=2\) constants are quadrature approximations to the specified exact integral. No general nonlinear, multidimensional, or independent scientific assessment is asserted.
