# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The normalized body is full-dimensional for every stated \(d\ge3\) and \(0<t\le1\). Symmetry and uniqueness reduce both ellipsoid problems to two semiaxes. The John facet-support calculation and the Löwner exhaustive vertex list make the reduced containments necessary and sufficient. Strict univariate and strictly concave bivariate optimization give the explicit semiaxes, with separately checked boundary parameters. Explicit nonnegative isotropic contact measures independently certify global optimality. Differentiating the exact ratio proves one and only one global minimizer. The limiting law follows by substitutions into the same exact equation. The companion checker independently optimized 88 finite cases and checked certificates; those samples corroborate, rather than replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, priority risk remains)
Güler and Gürtuna (arXiv:0709.0707, 2007-09-05) provide symmetry and ellipsoid optimality theory and solve Euclidean ellipsoid slabs and cone/cylinder examples, not the \(\ell_1\) coordinate-slab body or its sharp ratio minimum. Liu and Tkocz (arXiv:1910.06993, 2019-10-15) solve volume extremal problems for noncentral cross-polytope sections and for symmetric slab **intersection volumes**, not extremal ellipsoid problems; their Theorem 5 is a different objective. Searches for coordinate-clipped cross-polytopes, polar opposite-spike cubes, and the exact cubic optimum yielded no prior covering statement. Close mathematical records about a **one-sided** capped hypercube, a cube–cross-polytope **Mahler product**, and ball–box intersections require different constraints and do not imply this theorem. Publication-search incompleteness and possible older special-case formulae remain residual risks. No independent historical-priority assessment is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural one-parameter degeneration of the regular cross-polytope, retaining central symmetry while removing just one antipodal pair of tips. The *two* approximation ellipsoids respond at different, dimension-dependent truncation thresholds, and their volume ratio has a unique best-clipped shape. Its optimal relative improvement grows at the precise rate \(d^{1/4}\), which is not implied by the untruncated body's known John and Löwner balls. The all-dimensional exact profile and high-dimensional optimum are substantive structural properties of a canonical family, beyond a single numeric benchmark.

## Scope and residual risks
The theorem assumes a single symmetric coordinate slab. It makes no assertion about rotated or multiple slabs, and uses a limiting interpretation at \(t=0\). Primary texts were consulted for the most plausible comparable problem classes; historical novelty cannot be certified from an incomplete global literature corpus. The evidence is a self-review, not an independent external audit.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
