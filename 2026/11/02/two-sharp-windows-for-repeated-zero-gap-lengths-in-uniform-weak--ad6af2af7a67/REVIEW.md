# Scientific assessment: Two sharp windows for repeated zero-gap lengths

**Correctness — PASS.** Support conditioning gives the exact uniform zero-gap composition, with the displayed exact formula for its support-size distribution. The conditional geometric representation and the explicitly justified negative-binomial local limit yield asymptotic equality-pattern probabilities; matching graphs give the first Poisson law, and a second-moment bound proves collision persistence for all masses below the linear regime. Exact zero-count factorial moments and a hypergeometric isolated-zero count cover the linear-to-quadratic and terminal regimes. Boundary cases (empty mass, endpoint gaps, zero-length intervals) are distinguished. The standard-library checker passed 602 exact assertions over 12,854 weak compositions. Limits rely on the general proof, not on finite enumeration.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** The initial birthday-scale Poisson spacing law is already substantially covered by Klykova (2002/2003) after a negligible-duplicates transfer; no standalone novelty is claimed for that component. Bevan and Threlfall (2023 preprint/2025 journal article) specifically leave equal-length gap thresholds untreated. Their one-gap-length thresholds do not state or directly imply the uniform-composition *all-intermediate-masses* persistence theorem and terminal pair-count distribution. Relevant prior work on distinct positive-composition part sizes concerns a different sampling scheme. Database comparisons found no direct complete two-window theorem. Klykova's full text remained unavailable; its abstract was sufficient to establish overlap with the first window but not to rule out an unstated stronger theorem. Historical priority remains conditional on that risk.

**Value — PASS.** The classification addresses an explicit literature-motivated gap: when two zero runs have equal lengths, rather than when one prescribed length appears. The property is nonmonotone in the mass, exhibiting a birthday-scale emergence, persistence over every intermediate growth rate, and a second extinction at the loss of zeros. The two transition laws differ structurally (Poisson edge collisions versus pairs from a Poisson number of isolated zeros). Neither correctness alone nor a standalone early birthday calculation constitutes the value claim.
