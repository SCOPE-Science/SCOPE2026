# Two-sided ideal Grassmannians and exact ideal counts for radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Finding
For every finite quiver \(Q\) (including loops and parallel arrows) and every field \(k\), let \(A=kQ/J^2\), where \(J\) is the ideal generated by the arrows. Write \(V=Q_0\), \(a_{uv}\) for the number of arrows from vertex \(u\) to vertex \(v\), and \(E_{uv}=e_uJe_v\), with the path-product convention fixed so that \(\dim_k E_{uv}=a_{uv}\). Let \(\mathcal I_d(A)\) be the closed scheme of \(d\)-dimensional two-sided ideals of \(A\) in the ordinary Grassmannian of \(d\)-planes.

**All-ideal and geometric classification.** Every two-sided ideal is uniquely
\[
 I=\bigoplus_{v\in S}ke_v\ \oplus\!\bigoplus_{u,v\in V}W_{uv},
 \qquad W_{uv}\le E_{uv},
\]
where \(S\subseteq V\) and \(W_{uv}=E_{uv}\) if \(u\in S\) or \(v\in S\). Conversely every such choice is a two-sided ideal. For fixed \(d\), the *reduced* ideal variety is canonically a finite disjoint union
\[
 \big(\mathcal I_d(A)\big)_{\mathrm{red}}\cong
 \coprod_{\substack{S\subseteq V,\ 0\le r_{uv}\le a_{uv}\ (u,v\notin S)\\
 d=|S|+i(S)+\sum_{u,v\notin S}r_{uv}}}
 \prod_{u,v\notin S}\operatorname{Gr}(r_{uv},a_{uv}),
 \qquad i(S)=\sum_{u\in S\ \mathrm{or}\ v\in S}a_{uv}.
\]
Empty products are points. Every connected component of this reduced variety is smooth and geometrically irreducible. The component indexed by \((S,(r_{uv}))\) has dimension \(\sum_{u,v\notin S}r_{uv}(a_{uv}-r_{uv})\).

**Finite-field ideal census.** For every prime power \(q\), the complete polynomial counting distinct two-sided ideals by dimension is
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal Z_Q(q;T)=\sum_{S\subseteq V}T^{|S|+i(S)}
 \prod_{u,v\notin S}\left(\sum_{r=0}^{a_{uv}}{a_{uv}\brack r}_qT^r\right),}
\]
where \({a\brack r}_q\) is the number of \(r\)-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^a\). Thus \([T^d]\mathcal Z_Q(q;T)\) is the exact number of distinct \(d\)-dimensional ideals, including the zero and full ideals.

**Orientation boundary.** If \(Q\) arises by orienting each edge of a simple undirected graph \(G\) exactly once, every \(a_{uv}\) is zero or one, so all the Grassmannian factors are points. Its complete ideal lattice (after identifying the same underlying arrows) and polynomial are independent of orientation and of the field. Explicitly,
\[
 \mathcal Z_G(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq V}T^{|S|+|E(G)|-|E(G[V\setminus S])|}
 (1+T)^{|E(G[V\setminus S])|}.
\]
For the three-vertex path this is \(1+2T+3T^2+3T^3+3T^4+T^5\) for every orientation. Orientation-independence fails if parallel-arrow multiplicities can vary: for two vertices with two parallel arrows in one direction, \(\mathcal Z_Q(q;T)=1+(q+1)T+T^2+2T^3+T^4\); for one arrow in each direction, it is \(1+2T+T^2+2T^3+T^4\). These differ over every finite field.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite quiver is allowed to have any finite numbers of loops and arrows between ordered pairs of vertices. The path algebra is truncated by the ideal of all paths of length two and higher, so \(J^2=0\). The base \(k\) is an arbitrary field, including positive characteristic and non-algebraically-closed fields; point-count statements require \(k=\mathbb F_q\). The theorem classifies *labeled vector subspaces that are two-sided ideals*, not ideal isomorphism classes, one-sided ideals, arbitrary representations, or the possibly nonreduced structure of the scheme defined by closure equations.

## Proof
Let \(E=\bigoplus_{v\in V}ke_v\), so \(A=E\oplus J\), \(E\cong k^V\), \(J^2=0\), and \(J=\bigoplus_{u,v\in V}E_{uv}\) as an \(E\)-bimodule. The multiplication convention has \(e_u\alpha e_v=\alpha\) for an arrow \(\alpha\) from \(u\) to \(v\); using the opposite path convention simply interchanges the ordered labels and leaves all formulas invariant.

Let \(I\) be a two-sided ideal. Its image under \(A\to A/J\cong k^V\) is an ideal of the product of fields, so it has a unique vertex support \(S\). For each \(v\in S\), choose \(x\in I\) whose image has coefficient \(\lambda\ne0\) at \(e_v\). Then \(z=e_vxe_v\in I\) has the form \(z=\lambda e_v+\ell\), where \(\ell\in E_{vv}\subset J\) is a linear combination of loops. The relations \(\ell^2=0\) and \(e_v\ell=\ell e_v=\ell\) give
\[
 z^2-2\lambda z=-\lambda^2e_v.
\]
The left side lies in \(I\), and \(\lambda\) is invertible in \(k\); hence \(e_v\in I\). This identity works without division by two, including in characteristic two. Thus \(I=(\bigoplus_{v\in S}ke_v)\oplus (I\cap J)\) as vector spaces. Because \(I\) is a two-sided ideal, multiplying by the vertex idempotents shows that \(I\cap J\) decomposes as \(\bigoplus_{u,v}W_{uv}\) with \(W_{uv}\le E_{uv}\). Since \(e_v\in I\) for \(v\in S\), all arrows incident to \(S\) belong to \(I\), so their corresponding \(W_{uv}=E_{uv}\). Conversely, a space of this form is closed under multiplying on either side by \(E\) and by \(J\): the first preserves the Peirce blocks and the second is forced into the full incident-arrow spaces, since \(J^2=0\). This proves both existence and uniqueness.

For fixed \(S\) and dimensions \(r_{uv}=\dim W_{uv}\) at the unforced pairs, the choice space is the displayed product of ordinary Grassmannians. Its direct-sum embedding into \(\operatorname{Gr}(d,A)\) is a closed immersion: the vertex and forced-arrow summands are fixed; the variable subspace is required to split along the fixed Peirce decomposition with specified block dimensions. Distinct index choices give disjoint closed subsets, because the support of the quotient ideal and each Peirce-block intersection dimension are intrinsic. The ideal locus is closed in the ambient Grassmannian (closure under finitely many left/right multiplications is a closed condition), and its geometric points are exhausted by these finitely many images. Passing to the reduction gives their disjoint union. Each Grassmannian is smooth, projective, geometrically irreducible of dimension \(r(a-r)\), proving the component and dimension assertions.

Over \(\mathbb F_q\), each product component has exactly \(\prod{a_{uv}\brack r_{uv}}_q\) rational points. Every ideal has exactly one parameter pair \((S,(r_{uv}))\), so summing over the mutually disjoint components with the dimension weight \(T^d\) gives the polynomial formula, with no division by automorphism groups and no finite extrapolation.

If each underlying edge has one orientation and no multiple arrows, every optional \(E_{uv}\) is one-dimensional. It contributes exactly two choices, zero or all, and only edges internal to \(V\setminus S\) are optional; this proves the graph formula and orientation independence. For a pair of vertices with two parallel arrows, the unforced part is a two-dimensional vector space with \(q+1\) distinct lines, whereas putting the two arrows in opposite directions yields two separately selectable one-dimensional Peirce blocks, only two one-dimensional choices. All other ideals have the same dimension counts, establishing the displayed sharp boundary.

## Verification
Run the standalone standard-Python checker \(`verify_ideal_census.py`\). It constructs the original truncated path-basis multiplication, enumerates every subspace in reduced row-echelon form over each tested finite field, and directly checks left and right closure under the entire basis. The observed dimension counts are compared with a *separately implemented* Gaussian-binomial formula. The recorded output \(`verify_output.txt`\) ends in \(`VERIFY_OK_RADICAL_SQUARE_ZERO_ALL_IDEALS 22`\). The 22 exhaustive cases cover loop, parallel-arrow, opposite-arrow, path, fork, mixed-loop and disconnected quivers over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and genuine \(\mathbb F_4\). These are checks of finite instances; the proof above supplies the universal result.

## Relationship to prior work
Hubery's *Irreducible components of quiver Grassmannians* (arXiv:1303.4585, initially public 2013-03-19), inspected in its publicly available complete HTML text, develops general component-decomposition results for submodule Grassmannians. Bleher, Chinburg and Huisgen-Zimmermann's *The geometry of finite dimensional algebras with vanishing radical square* (arXiv:1407.3045, initially public 2014-07-11), inspected through the complete available primary PDF, describes components of representation varieties and left-module Grassmannian constructions. Those frameworks make Grassmannian methods natural, but their relevant theorems address one-sided module parametrizations, not the explicit lattice of two-sided ideals of the regular bimodule with a dimension-by-dimension Gaussian count. Colak's *Two-Sided Ideals in Leavitt Path Algebras* (arXiv:0910.0292) concerns a different graph algebra and cannot be substituted for truncated path-algebra ideals. The Peirce idempotent decomposition and Gaussian subspace enumeration are standard tools and are not claimed as innovations. The asserted contribution is the combined all-quiver complete ideal classification, geometric decomposition, explicit finite-field ideal polynomial and orientation boundary. Earlier unlocated formulations of this elementary decomposition remain a priority risk.

## Limitations
No reducedness claim is made for the original scheme cut out by the two-sided closure equations; only its reduction is classified. The result does not treat algebras with \(J^2\ne0\), arbitrary one-sided ideal spaces or moduli of ideals up to algebra automorphisms. The theorem may be derivable from standard descriptions of radical-square-zero basic algebras, and historical priority remains provisional; a failed literature search is not a uniqueness proof. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
