# Two unrestricted three-variable theories of affine resolvable designs

## Finding
Consider the point–block incidence structures of all finite *simple nontrivial affine resolvable* \(2\)-designs. They have exactly **two** theories in non-counting three-variable first-order logic, with no bound on quantifier rank. Two structures have the same such theory precisely when both have two blocks per parallel class, or both have at least three. Every member shares the same rank-two theory. The two classes have smallest members of orders \(10\) and \(21\), respectively, yielding the sharp uniform representative bound \(21\).

## Assumptions and scope
A \(2\)-\((v,k,\lambda)\) design consists of \(v\) points and distinct \(k\)-element subsets called blocks, with every two distinct points lying together in exactly \(\lambda\ge1\) blocks. Here \(2\le k<v\). A *parallel class* is a set of pairwise disjoint blocks partitioning all points. We assume the set of all blocks is partitioned into \(t\) parallel classes, each of size \(q\ge2\), and every pair of blocks from different classes meets in precisely \(\mu\ge1\) points. A design satisfying these assumptions is termed affine resolvable; a choice of its resolution is used in the proof, but is not named in the logical signature. The incidence structure has disjoint unary point and block predicates \(P,B\), equality, and binary incidence \(I\) between a point and a block. The theorem also holds for the undirected bipartite incidence graph with the bipartition named. No predicate for parallelism or intersection is included.

The resolution identities are \(v=qk=q^{2}\mu\), \(k=q\mu\), \(r=t\), and \(\lambda(v-1)=t(k-1)\), where \(r\) denotes the replication number. The distinct blocks ensure \(t>q\), as follows from \(t=(\lambda(v-1))/(k-1)\), \(\lambda>0\), and \(\lambda<t/q\) when \(k=q\mu\). In particular \(t\ge3\). All stated integer inequalities use only the assumptions above.

## Proof
**Step 1: parameters.** Each point lies in exactly one block of each parallel class; hence \(r=t\). Each block meets every block of any other class in \(\mu\) points; those \(q\) other blocks partition the point set, giving \(k=q\mu\) and \(v=qk\). Counting blocks on a fixed pair gives \(t(k-1)=\lambda(v-1)\). Since \(v-1>q(k-1)\) (their difference is \(q-1\)), \(t>q\lambda\ge q\), hence \(t\ge3\).

**Step 2: nine ordered-pair colours.** For elements \(a,b\) of the disjoint union of points and blocks, colour their ordered pair by: equality or inequality for point–point pairs (\(PP=,PP\ne\)); equality, disjointness, or intersection for block–block pairs (\(BB=,BB\perp,BB\cap\)); and incidence or nonincidence for each orientation of point–block pairs (\(PB_I,PB_N,BP_I,BP_N\)). Each of these nine colours occurs in every admissible design. Importantly, the colours refine the atomic incidence/equality types and can be maintained by a pebble-game strategy even though block disjointness is not a primitive relation.

**Step 3: exhaustive third-element support, uniformly in the parameters.** For a fixed ordered pair \((a,b)\) of any one colour, consider which pairs of colours \((\operatorname{col}(a,z),\operatorname{col}(z,b))\) occur as the third element \(z\) ranges over points and blocks. The following counts exhaust the nontrivial cases.

For distinct points \(a,b\), the number of blocks through both is \(\lambda>0\); the number through exactly either one is \(t-\lambda>0\) for each orientation; and the number through neither is \((q-2)t+\lambda>0\). The number of third points different from both is \(v-2>0\).

For intersecting distinct blocks \(a,b\), the counts of third points in both, in only either one, and in neither are, respectively, \(\mu>0\), \(k-\mu=(q-1)\mu>0\), and \(v-2k+\mu=((q-2)q+1)\mu>0\). For disjoint blocks, the two exclusive point regions each have size \(k>0\), and the outside region has size \(v-2k=(q-2)k\): it is nonempty precisely when \(q\ge3\). An additional third block disjoint from both members of a disjoint pair exists precisely when \(q\ge3\), because the blocks disjoint from a given block are exactly the other members of its parallel class. For two intersecting blocks, blocks disjoint from exactly one and intersecting the other exist (choose an alternative from either of their parallel classes), as do blocks intersecting both (choose any of the remaining \(t-2\ge1\) classes).

For a point \(p\) and a block \(H\), there are \(k-1\ge1\) other points in \(H\) when \(p\in H\), and \(v-k-1\ge1\) points outside \(H\) different from \(p\) when \(p\notin H\); analogous elementary counts handle the remaining point choices. Among third blocks from the class of \(H\), if \(p\notin H\), exactly one other block contains \(p\), while \(q-2\) other blocks contain neither \(p\) nor any point of \(H\); the last option occurs precisely when \(q\ge3\). If \(p\in H\), each of the \(q-1\) other parallel blocks omits \(p\). Each of the \(t-1\ge2\) other classes has exactly one block containing \(p\) and at least one omitting it; all these intersect \(H\). For a repeated point or repeated block the possible third-element colours are determined immediately by the same facts.

The cases just listed determine *all* third-colour pairs: for the nine pair colours, their occurrence depends only on whether \(q=2\) or \(q\ge3\); no count beyond positivity survives. The complete finite tables of colour pairs and independently checked examples are generated by the bundled verification script. The proof above verifies the support statements for **every** admissible design; the finite script is corroborative, not an infinite proof.

**Step 4: three-pebble back-and-forth.** Between two designs in the same \(q\)-class, Duplicator maintains the stronger condition that all ordered pairs of occupied pebble positions have matching colours. On each move, at most two pebbles remain fixed. Their ordered-pair colour is the same in the two designs. By Step 3, a third element realising the exact two required colours can be selected in the other design. This includes moves that repeat an occupied vertex. Every response maintains equality, unary sorts, and incidence. This works for any finite number of rounds, establishing unrestricted-depth \(\mathrm{FO}^{3}\) equivalence without counting.

**Step 5: sharp separation.** The three-variable sentence
\[\exists x\,\exists y\,\bigl(B(x)\land B(y)\land x\ne y\land\forall z\,[P(z)\Rightarrow(I(z,x)\lor I(z,y))]\bigr)\]
asserts that two distinct blocks together cover all points. A parallel class provides such a pair if \(q=2\); if \(q\ge3\), any two blocks have union of size at most \(2k<v=qk\). Thus the two theories differ at quantifier rank three. At quantifier rank two, every atomic two-element pattern over the sorts and incidence relation occurs in every admissible design, so all the structures are rank-two equivalent.

**Step 6: smallest representatives.** The total number of elements is \(v+b=q^{2}\mu+qt\). As \(\mu\ge1\) and \(t\ge q+1\), this is at least \(2q^{2}+q\), hence at least \(10\) when \(q=2\), and at least \(21\) when \(q\ge3\). Both lower bounds are attained by the point–line incidence structures of the affine planes \(\mathrm{AG}(2,2)\) and \(\mathrm{AG}(2,3)\). These give the stated sharp uniform representative bound.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using only the Python standard library. The program checks the design axioms and pairwise block-intersection condition for seven finite structures, then computes directly the full occurrence table of possible third-colour pairs for every ordered pair and confirms that its support is constant within each colour and each \(q\)-class. Examples include \(\mathrm{AG}(2,2)\), \(\mathrm{AG}(3,2)\), \(\mathrm{AG}(4,2)\), \(\mathrm{AG}(2,3)\), \(\mathrm{AG}(3,3)\), \(\mathrm{AG}(2,5)\), and the nongeometric Hadamard \(2\)-\((12,6,5)\) design with two blocks per class. The bundled `verification_output.txt` gives the exact observed third-colour tables. Successful finite checks are not a substitute for the all-parameter Step 3.

## Relationship to prior work
Lam and Tonchev classified \(68\) nonisomorphic affine resolvable \(2\)-\((27,9,4)\) designs in their 1996 paper, including one with trivial automorphism group. Their classification concerns isomorphism and incidence ranks, not the non-counting three-variable theory. The present theorem places all of these designs in one logical equivalence class, including the classical ternary affine-space design. Devillers and Praeger (arXiv:1405.3030) classify designs with strong automorphism-group transitivity across several ordered-pair types; no group action assumption appears here. Dawar and Holm (arXiv:1205.0913) establish broader pebble-game methodology, not the stated affine-resolvable exact two-class invariant. Clark, Jungnickel and Tonchev discuss large families with affine design parameters, but matching parameters or resolvability alone need not imply the uniform cross-parallel-class block-intersection hypothesis imposed here.

## Limitations
The statement requires a finite simple affine resolvable \(2\)-design with constant positive intersection size between blocks from different parallel classes; it does **not** cover arbitrary resolvable \(2\)-designs or all designs merely sharing affine-space parameters. The signature names point and block sorts and incidence, but not parallelism, block intersection, a resolution, or counting operators. Additional relations, counting logic, and four-variable logic can distinguish further examples. Exhaustive historical priority and independent mathematical verification have not been established; related broader results in coherent-configuration theory could in principle imply the equivalence.
