# Review of the affine-plane flat-poset classification

## Correctness
**PASS.** The one-point support lemma is derived from the affine-plane axioms, not from coordinates or a finite enumeration. Its 21 ordered pair profiles and their 148 support patterns for order at least three yield an indefinitely maintainable three-pebble invariant. The exceptional patterns are exactly the positive \(q-2\) and \(q(q-2)\) cases. The independent exact logical-type refinement over complete order-two and order-three planes proves that rank three agrees and rank four separates; a type-refinement induction validates its use as a finite proof, rather than assuming finite experiments prove the infinite claim. All required scripts were executed from their package paths.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical uncertainty.** Guo (2008) treats geometricity of affine-flat lattices, not bounded-variable first-order theories; Grohe and Otto (2012) give a general pebble-game method, not this family-specific dichotomy. A prior bounded-rank comparison of affine and linear flats does not imply unrestricted-depth three-variable equivalence, and a prior unrestricted result for linear subspaces does not imply the result for arbitrary affine planes, especially non-Desarguesian planes. Database and source searches found no identical theorem. Absence from these searches is not a priority certificate. Wang and Feng (2006) was abstract-only, and broad possible implicit coverage remains a residual risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The result settles the exact unrestricted three-variable theory spectrum for every finite affine plane, including planes without field coordinatization. It proves a sharp two-variable/three-variable expressive-power boundary, a first distinguishing rank of four, and exact smallest representatives. Those are motivated invariants of the pure affine-flat incidence order and require a nontrivial all-depth bisimulation argument, not just a computation of small finite examples.

## Closest literature and limitations
- J. Guo, *Lattices Associated with Finite Vector Spaces and Finite Affine Spaces*, Ars Combinatoria 88 (2008), 47–53. The full seven-page text was inspected and concerns geometricity, not the present logical equivalence.
- M. Grohe and M. Otto, *Pebble Games and Linear Equations*, arXiv:1204.1990 (2012), and CSL 2012. The inspected sections concern general finite-variable methods; no affine-plane theorem was found there.
- K. Wang and Y.-Q. Feng, *Lattices Generated by Orbits of Flats Under Finite Affine Groups*, Communications in Algebra 34 (2006), 1691–1697, accessible abstract only; historical coverage remains incompletely resolved.
- Unrestricted historical priority is not certified; the signature excludes counting, operations, and named point or line predicates.
