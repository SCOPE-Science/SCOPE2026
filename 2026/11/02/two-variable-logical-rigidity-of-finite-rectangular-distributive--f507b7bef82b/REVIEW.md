# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The two-variable recursion for downward rank is syntactically valid under variable rebinding. For \(P_{m,n}\) the rank of \((i,j)\) is \(i+j\); the rank-one elements are exactly the available coordinate atoms. Their incomparable points occupy exactly one opposite coordinate axis, so the spectrum is the integer interval from one to \(\max(m,n)-1\) when both factors are nontrivial. This proves full reconstruction and the converse follows from coordinate-swap isomorphism. The one-variable lower bound and nonoptimal depth upper bound were independently checked. Two standalone programs reproduced the finite numerical consequences (324 ordered rectangles, 1,877,922 incomparable ordered pairs; 45 unlabelled rectangles separated by rank six).

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Searches for two-variable logical equivalence of rectangular posets, products of chains, distributive lattices and incomparability rank spectra identified no earlier statement proving the unrestricted single-order classification. The most relevant inspected primary sources, Schwentick–Zeume (arXiv:1110.1439, sections on results and definitions) and Manuel–Zeume (CSL 2013, introduction, definitions, contributions and results), concern satisfiability and complexity for two order relations, often with successors or labels. These neither imply the present one-relation reconstruction nor give its atom-incomparability invariant. The structural product-of-chains literature is not a logical identification theorem. The comparison does not establish universal historical priority: an unlocated stronger theorem may subsume the claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete exact classification for a natural infinite family of finite distributive lattices in a deliberately weak signature. The two-versus-one-variable boundary is sharp, and the positive half reconstructs arbitrarily large coordinate lengths by a concrete order-theoretic spectrum, despite the lack of counting, coordinates, cover predicate, or named lattice operations. It gives a useful benchmark for fixed-variable logic on posets; general products of more chains remain an open direction for this argument. This is not a mere finite sample, parameter substitution, or reformulation of the ordinary isomorphism classification.

## Limits and research relationship
The theorem is relative to products of two chains, does not identify them among all posets, and does not claim optimal quantifier depth or historical uniqueness. Related full primary texts: Schwentick and Zeume (2012), *Two-Variable Logic with Two Order Relations*, and Manuel and Zeume (2013), *Two-Variable Logic on 2-Dimensional Structures*. The finite checks are independent corroboration of the symbolic argument, not infinite certificates.
