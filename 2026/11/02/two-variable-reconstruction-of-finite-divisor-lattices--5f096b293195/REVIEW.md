# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every needed formula is explicitly in counting-free two-variable logic. The recursive chain-height formulas can shadow the opposite variable, but never introduce a third one. For a coordinate atom, the maximum rank of an incomparable element equals total rank minus its coordinate's prime exponent. A two-variable unary formula therefore recognizes all atoms with each exponent; a second sentence's least realizable rank measures the *multiplicity* of that exponent. Chains, singletons, zero multiplicities and the absence of named constants are treated separately. A separate actual finite-model formula evaluator checked the two-variable syntax and representative separating sentences. Both finite programs are corroborative, not infinite proofs.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty.** Searches of the published research database, available historical claims, and primary literature uncovered no exact result that identifies every finite divisor lattice up to isomorphism by counting-free two-variable logic. The closest 2015 study identifies finite relational structures in the *stronger* two-variable counting logic, while the 2016 work on two-variable order invariance and the later paper on two-variable partial orders address satisfiability and expressivity constraints, not divisor-exponent reconstruction. The standard chain-product representation is prior mathematical knowledge and not asserted as new. An unlocated general result about modal types of finite chain products remains a residual originality risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result gives an exact classification of every two-variable theory in a natural, unbounded arithmetic family. The recovery of factor **multiplicities** without counting quantifiers is the structural point: a low-variable order fragment can reconstruct an entire prime-exponent partition. Width two is minimal, and this is not a generic finite-poset phenomenon. Examples of equal-sized, equal-height but inequivalent lattices demonstrate that cardinality and height alone do not explain the result. No quantifier-rank efficiency claim is made.
