# Twofold crossings and embedded singular-scheme points on the Fermat quartic threefold's Fano curve

## Finding

Let \(X\subset\mathbf P^4_{\mathbb C}\) be the smooth Fermat quartic threefold
\[
X:\quad x_0^4+x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4+x_4^4=0,
\]
and let \(F=F_1(X)\subset\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\) be its **full scheme** of lines, not merely its support. Write \(R=F_{\mathrm{red}}\), and let \(\Sigma\subset F\) be the intrinsic singular subscheme defined by \(\operatorname{Fitt}_1\Omega^1_{F/\mathbb C}\).

**Theorem.** The reduced curve \(R\) has exactly \(40\) smooth irreducible components, each a plane Fermat quartic of genus \(3\). Its singular locus consists of **exactly \(240\) ordinary nodes**, each lying on exactly two components. In particular, \(R\) is connected and
\[
p_a(R)=321.
\]
At every geometric point corresponding to one of the \(240\) common lines, the completed local rings are
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{R,L}\cong\mathbb C[[u,v]]/(uv),\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{F,L}\cong\mathbb C[[u,v]]/(u^2v^2),
\]
and the intrinsic singular subscheme of the **nonreduced** curve has the completed local ring
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\Sigma,L}\cong
\mathbb C[[u,v]]/(u^2v,uv^2).
\]
Thus \(\Sigma\) has reduced one-dimensional support \(R\), and it has **exactly \(240\) embedded associated points**, one at each node of \(R\). Each embedded defect has length \(1\), in the precise sense that the saturation quotient
\[
(uv)/(u^2v,uv^2)
\]
is a one-dimensional \(\mathbb C\)-vector space. At every other geometric point of \(F\), there are formal coordinates \(t,w\) in which
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{F,L}\cong\mathbb C[[t,w]]/(w^2),\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\Sigma,L}\cong\mathbb C[[t]].
\]
In particular, the entire nonreduced Fano curve is locally a double smooth curve away from the \(240\) crossings, while its intrinsic singular scheme acquires one isolated embedded infinitesimal at each crossing.

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is over \(\mathbb C\). A line means a closed point of the Hilbert/Fano scheme in \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\). A node means a reduced plane-curve ordinary double point. The singular subscheme \(\Sigma\) is defined intrinsically by the first Fitting ideal of Kähler differentials, not by an arbitrary embedding of \(R\). Multiplicity-two components and the existence of forty conical families were previously known; the precise intersection count, complete local models and the \(240\) embedded singular-scheme points constitute the stated finding.

## Proof

**1. Classification of all lines.** Parametrize a line by five binary linear forms \(\ell_i(s,t)\), not all proportional, with \(\sum_i\ell_i^4=0\). Discard identically zero forms. Group the remaining forms according to projective proportionality. The fourth powers of \(r\le5\) pairwise nonproportional binary forms are linearly independent (Vandermonde determinant for the rational normal quartic). Therefore, the sum of fourth-power coefficients within *each* direction group vanishes. No group may have one member. Since at most five forms occur and the line has two independent directions, exactly two groups occur: either sizes \((2,3)\), or \((2,2)\) with one form zero.

For each unordered coordinate pair \(\{i,j\}\subset\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), and each \(\alpha\) with \(\alpha^4=-1\), let \(C_{ij,\alpha}\) be the one-dimensional family of lines in the cone section \(x_i=\alpha x_j\), generated by its vertex and a point of the Fermat plane quartic in the remaining three coordinates. Explicitly its members are the spans of \([\alpha:1:0:0:0]\) in the paired coordinates and arbitrary points on the remaining plane quartic. This family is itself isomorphic to that smooth genus-three plane quartic. The two-direction classification shows that every line belongs to one of the \(\binom52\cdot4=40\) families; the \((2,3)\)-type line belongs to exactly one, while the \((2,2)\)-type line belongs to exactly two.

Two families meet if and only if their coordinate pairs are disjoint. For disjoint pairs \(\{i,j\}\) and \(\{k,l\}\), and choices \(\alpha^4=\beta^4=-1\), their common line is uniquely defined by \(x_i=\alpha x_j\), \(x_k=\beta x_l\), and the remaining coordinate \(x_m=0\). A pair of families sharing a coordinate cannot meet: the two pair cancellations and the global quartic relation would force all three corresponding coordinates to vanish, leaving no line. Hence there are
\[
5\cdot3\cdot4\cdot4=240
\]
distinct intersection lines (choose the omitted coordinate, partition the remaining four into unordered pairs, choose two fourth-root phases). Each family meets precisely \(3\cdot4=12\) others, at distinct lines. The graph on the forty components is connected: its ten coordinate-pair types have the connected disjointness graph of two-subsets of a five-element set, and each adjacency joins all four phases at one type to all four phases at the other.

**2. Complete local ring at an intersection.** Coordinate permutations and diagonal fourth-root automorphisms act transitively on the \(240\) intersection lines. After rescaling two coordinates by fourth roots of \(-1\), study the line \(U=V=s\), \(W=T=t\), \(Z=0\) on the diagonal quartic
\[
-U^4+V^4-W^4+T^4+Z^4=0.
\]
A Grassmannian chart for moving lines has independent coordinates \(V=s,T=t\) and
\[
U=(1+a)s+bt,\qquad W=cs+(1+d)t,\qquad Z=es+ft.
\]
The five Fano equations are the coefficients \(g_0,\ldots,g_4\) of \(s^4,s^3t,s^2t^2,st^3,t^4\) in
\[
-((1+a)s+bt)^4+s^4-(cs+(1+d)t)^4+t^4+(es+ft)^4.
\]
The Jacobian matrix of \((g_0,g_1,g_3,g_4)\) with respect to \((a,b,c,d)\) at the central line is \(\operatorname{diag}(-4,-4,-4,-4)\). By the formal implicit-function theorem these four variables can be eliminated uniquely as power series in \(e,f\), all starting in homogeneous degree four. The remaining coefficient is
\[
G(e,f)=6e^2f^2+\text{terms of total degree at least }8.
\]
Indeed \(g_2=-6(1+a)^2b^2-6c^2(1+d)^2+6e^2f^2\), and the first two terms have order at least eight after elimination. The two known local families are *exactly* the coordinate axes \(e=0\) and \(f=0\): for the first cone one has \(a=b=c=e=0\) and \(1-(1+d)^4+f^4=0\); for the second one has \(b=c=d=f=0\) and \(1-(1+a)^4+e^4=0\). Their equations uniquely determine \(d(f)\) and \(a(e)\), respectively, and each axis is smooth.

Step 1 excludes any other local branch. Since \(\mathbb C[[e,f]]\) is a unique-factorization domain and the support of \(G\) consists exactly of the two coordinate axes, \(G\) must equal a unit times \(e^r f^s\) for positive integers \(r,s\). Its initial form \(6e^2f^2\) forces \(r=s=2\). Dividing out the unit proves \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{F,L}\cong\mathbb C[[e,f]]/(e^2f^2)\), and reducing gives the ordinary node \(\mathbb C[[e,f]]/(ef)\).

**3. All remaining points.** The Fano scheme is the vanishing locus of the five coefficients of a degree-four section of \(\operatorname{Sym}^4\mathcal U^\vee\) on the smooth six-dimensional Grassmannian. Its support consists of curves by Step 1. Thus the defining ideal has pure height five and is locally a complete intersection; in particular, the scheme has no embedded zero-dimensional components. Each irreducible reduced family is smooth. Away from its twelve intersections, a point lies on exactly one family. Local Cohen–Macaulayness and a smooth unique reduced branch force a local hypersurface equation to be a power of its transverse parameter times a unit. The multiplicity is two: this follows from the local formula of Step 2 in a punctured neighborhood of each intersection on each component, and the same one-branch factorization along a smooth irreducible family. Hence the formal ring is \(\mathbb C[[t,w]]/(w^2)\) at every nonintersection point. (Equivalently, the classical multiplicity-two computation applies.)

**4. The intrinsic singular scheme and arithmetic genus.** For a hypersurface ring \(\mathbb C[[u,v]]/(g)\) of dimension one, \(\operatorname{Fitt}_1\Omega^1\) is generated by the two partial derivatives of \(g\), modulo \(g\). For \(g=u^2v^2\), these derivatives generate \((u^2v,uv^2)\), and the original equation already lies in that ideal. The radical is \((uv)\), but
\[
(u^2v,uv^2)=(uv)\cap(u^2,v^2),
\]
where the second component is primary to the maximal ideal \((u,v)\). The saturation defect is \((uv)/(u^2v,uv^2)\cong\mathbb C\); hence exactly one embedded length-one defect occurs at every crossing. For \(g=w^2\), the singular scheme is reduced \((w)\). No other associated points occur.

The normalization of \(R\) is the disjoint union of the forty smooth genus-three quartics. The normalization exact sequence at each of the 240 ordinary nodes contributes a one-dimensional skyscraper cokernel. Thus
\[
\chi(\mathcal O_R)=40(1-3)-240=-320,
\qquad p_a(R)=1-\chi(\mathcal O_R)=321.
\]
This proves every asserted statement.

## Relationship to prior work

The forty conical families and their generic multiplicity two were described classically by Tennison, and are reproduced in the Fano-threefold analysis of Kuznetsov–Prokhorov–Shramov, in the geometric dichotomy of Ciliberto–Flamini–Knutsen, and in the exercises of Eisenbud–Harris. Those results establish the components and their generic nonreducedness; they do not state the 240-node incidence classification, the analytic crossings \(u^2v^2\), or the embedded associated points of the intrinsic singular scheme. The latter are not formal consequences of a Chow-cycle equality alone, which does not determine the punctual nilpotent structure at intersections. The proof supplies the missing local computations directly.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the complex Fermat **quartic threefold** and the natural scheme of lines, not the Fermat quartic surface, the Fermat quintic threefold, or arbitrary quartic threefolds. The 240 points are singularities of the **reduced** Fano curve; the entire unreduced Fano curve is singular. The embedded length-one contributions belong to the intrinsic singular subscheme \(\Sigma\), not to the Fano scheme \(F\) itself. No statement is made about the Hilbert scheme of conics. The 1974 original of Tennison was not fully available for a direct full-text priority comparison; this leaves a residual historical risk that the exact local structures were previously calculated.
