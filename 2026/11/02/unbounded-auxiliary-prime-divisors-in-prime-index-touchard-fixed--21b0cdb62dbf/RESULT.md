# Unbounded auxiliary prime divisors in prime-index Touchard fixed divisors

## Finding

For integers \(n\ge0\), let \(T_n(X)=\sum_{k=0}^n\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}n\\k\end{smallmatrix}\right\}X^k\) be the Touchard (Bell) polynomial, with \(T_0=1\). For every prime \(p\), define the positive fixed divisor
\[
d_p=\gcd\{T_p(z)-2z:z\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
The following statements hold.

1. For every prime \(p\), \(\nu_p(d_p)=1\).
2. For every auxiliary prime \(q\), set \(M_q=q^q-1\). If \(p\ne q\) is prime and \(p\equiv q\pmod{M_q}\), then \(pq\mid d_p\). For each \(q\) there are infinitely many such \(p\). Consequently the integer ratios \(d_p/p\) are unbounded as \(p\) ranges through the primes.
3. Precisely, \(2\mid d_p\) if and only if \(p\equiv2\pmod3\); and \(3\mid d_p\) if and only if \(p\equiv3\pmod{26}\).

For example \(d_5=10\) and \(d_{29}=174\). The statement concerns divisibility of *every* integer evaluation, not merely \(T_p(1)\), the \(p\)-th Bell number.

## Assumptions and scope

All indices and prime variables are ordinary positive integers, and \(z\) runs through every signed integer. The gcd is the positive generator of the ideal generated by all the displayed integer values, with \(\nu_p\) the usual prime valuation. Congruences between polynomial evaluations are understood as congruences of integer-valued functions on all residue classes, not necessarily coefficientwise congruences. The assertion about \(M_q\) does not claim that this period is least. The unboundedness assertion does not claim that a prescribed integer divides \(d_p/p\) for infinitely many primes.

## Proof

**The fixed prime factor is sharp.** For any prime \(p\), the Stirling recurrence or the set-partition action of a \(p\)-cycle gives the established coefficientwise congruence
\[
T_p(X)\equiv X^p+X\pmod p.
\]
Fermat's congruence \(z^p\equiv z\pmod p\) therefore implies \(p\mid T_p(z)-2z\) for every integer \(z\). The polynomial \(f_p(X)=T_p(X)-2X\) has degree \(p\), leading coefficient \(1\), and integral coefficients. Its order-\(p\) forward difference is identically \(p!\):
\[
\Delta^pf_p(0)=\sum_{j=0}^p(-1)^{p-j}\binom pj f_p(j)=p!.
\]
Every term in this integer linear combination is divisible by \(d_p\), so \(d_p\mid p!\), proving \(\nu_p(d_p)\le1\); together with the first congruence this proves equality.

**A uniform prime-to-\(q\) period.** Fix any prime \(q\). The classical polynomial Touchard congruence is
\[
T_{n+q}(X)\equiv X^qT_n(X)+T_{n+1}(X)\pmod q\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
For a fixed \(x\in\mathbb F_q\), put \(u_n=T_n(x)\bmod q\). By the Frobenius identity \(x^q=x\), this is the constant-coefficient recurrence \(u_{n+q}=u_{n+1}+xu_n\), whose characteristic polynomial is \(H_x(Y)=Y^q-Y-x\). For \(x\ne0\), the polynomial \(H_x\) has derivative \(-1\), so is squarefree, and zero is not a root. If \(\alpha\) is any root in an algebraic closure, then \(\alpha^q=\alpha+x\). Repeating Frobenius gives \(\alpha^{q^j}=\alpha+jx\) for each integer \(j\ge0\), hence \(\alpha^{q^q}=\alpha\). All roots therefore belong to \(\mathbb F_{q^q}^{\times}\), and their orders divide \(M_q=q^q-1\). Since the recurrence companion matrix is diagonalizable over this field, its \(M_q\)-th power is the identity. It follows that
\[
T_{n+M_q}(x)\equiv T_n(x)\pmod q\qquad(n\ge0,\ x\in\mathbb F_q^\times).
\]
For \(x=0\), the evaluations \(T_n(0)=0\) for \(n\ge1\), so the same congruence holds for all \(n\ge1\) and all integer \(x\).

Now \(T_q(X)\equiv X^q+X\pmod q\), hence \(T_q(x)\equiv2x\pmod q\) for every integer \(x\). If \(p\equiv q\pmod{M_q}\), with \(p>q\), the established period gives \(T_p(x)\equiv2x\pmod q\) simultaneously for every integer \(x\). Thus \(q\mid d_p\). Together with \(p\mid d_p\), and \(p\ne q\), this gives \(pq\mid d_p\). Since \(\gcd(q,M_q)=1\), Dirichlet's theorem yields infinitely many primes \(p\equiv q\pmod{M_q}\); only finitely many can equal \(q\). Taking arbitrarily large \(q\) proves \(\sup_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}d_p/p=\infty\).

**Sharp small-auxiliary-prime criteria.** The recurrence \(u_{n+q}=u_{n+1}+xu_n\), initialized by \(u_0=1\) and \(u_1=x\), provides complete finite checks of functions \(x\mapsto T_n(x)\bmod q\). At \(q=2\), over the complete positive-index period \(1\le n\le3\), the function \(T_n(x)\) equals \(2x\) on all \(\mathbb F_2\) precisely at \(n=2\). At \(q=3\), over \(1\le n\le26\), this occurs precisely at \(n=3\). The recurrence and the proved periods propagate these complete finite checks to all positive indices. Hence \(2\mid d_p\iff p\equiv2\pmod3\), and \(3\mid d_p\iff p\equiv3\pmod{26}\). The attached standalone checker recomputes all these finite tables by both Stirling polynomial recursion and modular recurrence, so no unproved infinite extrapolation is being made from a finite sample.

The symbolic recurrence, squarefreeness, Frobenius orbit and Dirichlet argument establish the infinite assertions independently of these checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Sun, Wu and Zhuang (2010, §2, equation (2.8)) explicitly state the polynomial Touchard congruence used above and attribute it to Gertsch and Robert. The present claim does not assert that this congruence is new. Gallardo and Rahavandrainy (2014, §§1–2) analyze the unweighted Bell-number sequence modulo a prime using an Artin–Schreier field and discuss its period. Their inspected statements do not classify the fixed divisors of \(T_p(X)-2X\) or relate infinitely many *different prime indices* by Dirichlet's theorem. The additional fixed-divisor/unboundedness assertion, exact index-prime exponent and small-auxiliary-prime criteria are the assessed contribution. This is a synthesis of known congruence and finite-field inputs; an equivalent observation elsewhere, or an explicit stronger published fixed-divisor theorem, remains a novelty risk.

## Limitations

The exact value of \(d_p\) is not determined for every prime, nor is the least positive prime in any displayed arithmetic progression. The period \(q^q-1\) is an explicit valid period, not a minimality claim. The result gives separate infinite progressions for each auxiliary prime; it does not establish simultaneous divisibility by arbitrary prescribed products.
