# Review of prime-index Touchard fixed divisors

## Correctness — PASS

The coefficientwise prime-index Touchard congruence implies every integer evaluation of the difference is divisible by its index prime. Its leading coefficient forces the order-prime forward difference to equal the factorial, so the index prime occurs with exponent exactly one. The Gertsch–Robert polynomial congruence yields a constant-coefficient modular recurrence. The corresponding Artin–Schreier characteristic polynomial has distinct nonzero roots in the finite extension of degree dividing the auxiliary prime, establishing a uniform period for every nonzero input; zero input is treated separately. Dirichlet's theorem then constructs infinitely many indices for each auxiliary prime. Complete modular function tables at auxiliary primes two and three give the exact small-prime criteria. The standalone reproducibility script verifies representative integer fixed divisors, these finite function tables, and the finite-field identities. The infinite statements rely on the explicit proof, not finite sampling.

## Originality — PASS with limitations

Sun, Wu and Zhuang, arXiv:1009.2929v1, equation (2.8), explicitly give the polynomial recurrence; it is acknowledged as prior art. Gallardo and Rahavandrainy, doi:10.37236/3532, investigate modular periods of the scalar Bell-number sequence, not the fixed divisor across all integer inputs of a polynomial at prime indices. Semantic published-finding searches using fixed divisors, Touchard congruences, auxiliary primes, and prime-index progression aliases found no implication covering the three claims. The result might be implicit in general fixed-divisor and Bell-polynomial literature, so lack of a matching hit is not a proof of uniqueness. The mathematical construction, rather than the known basic congruence itself, is assessed as new under available evidence.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural fixed divisor measures universal modular vanishing of a prime-index Bell polynomial on all integer inputs. Its sharp index-prime valuation, unbounded auxiliary prime factors along infinitely many prime indices, and exact parity/ternary criteria form a coherent structural answer not supplied merely by congruences at a single input. The result does not determine full fixed divisors and relies on standard but nontrivially combined algebraic and arithmetic tools; that narrower scope is explicit.
