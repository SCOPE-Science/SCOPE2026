# Review

**Correctness — PASS.** The poset of prescribed consecutive occurrences is a complete encoding of the intended count (additional occurrences allowed). The induction provides an explicit invariant: the last positional vertex has a unique greatest lower vertex and precisely \(2j\) upper vertices. On adding a copy, the incoming long chain is forced between that lower vertex and the separator, while only one fresh vertex admits free placement in \(2j+1\) slots above it. Base case and induction prove \((2j-1)!!\) for every \(m\ge3,K>2m,j\ge1\). Two independently generated poset descriptions and opposite-direction deletion algorithms agree in sixty exact test cases. Risk: finite checks alone would not establish the infinite claim, so the proof is decisive.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The closest full primary article, Blitvić–Kammoun–Steingrímsson (arXiv:2610.29846v1), specifically requests a uniformly described representative for *every* \(m>2,K>2m\) in Section 7, problem 6. Propositions 5.24–5.28 provide general theory and some enumerated families but not this explicit all-parameter family, and the pre-existing double-factorial sequence is not asserted to be new. The authors' 2023 earlier extended abstract and Dwyer–Elizalde (2018) were examined for an equivalent implication. Similarity searches and literal pattern searches gave no matching prior claim. Risk: unpublished work or a disguised special case of a more general formula could affect priority; absence from search results is not proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** Rather than an arbitrary numerical refinement, the result provides a clean constructive solution to a prominently stated mathematical existence question. It works simultaneously for arbitrary length and overlap in the full previously open regime, reveals a uniform structural reason for a classical double-factorial sequence, and supplies a reproducible infinite proof. Its value does not depend on downstream applications.

**Relationship and scope.** The original paper's poset encoding and earlier enumerative formulas are credited. The new statement is the specific uniform family and its general linear-extension argument. No claim about *exactly* specified occurrences, general Wilf-class classification, or complete historical independence is made.
