# Uniform primitive-pair distribution for Stern polynomials at negative two

## Finding

Let \(B_0(t)=0\), \(B_1(t)=1\), \(B_{2n}(t)=tB_n(t)\) and \(B_{2n+1}(t)=B_n(t)+B_{n+1}(t)\) for \(n\geq1\). Put \(b_n=B_n(-2)\). For each odd integer \(m\geq3\), define \(J_2(m)=m^2\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-2})\), where the product is over distinct primes. Then, for every ordered residue pair \((u,v)\pmod m\), the natural density of \(n\geq1\) with \((b_n,b_{n+1})\equiv(u,v)\pmod m\) exists and equals

\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\#\{1\leq n\leq N:(b_n,b_{n+1})\equiv(u,v)\pmod m\}=\begin{cases}J_2(m)^{-1},&\gcd(u,v,m)=1,\\0,&\gcd(u,v,m)>1.\end{cases}
\]

The nonprimitive pairs never arise, rather than merely having zero density. As a consequence, for each residue \(r\pmod m\),

\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\#\{1\leq n\leq N:b_n\equiv r\pmod m\}
=\frac{m}{J_2(m)}\prod_{p\mid\gcd(r,m)}(1-p^{-1}).
\]

In particular, the zero-residue density is \(\bigl(m\prod_{p\mid m}(1+p^{-1})\bigr)^{-1}\). These conclusions include all odd composite moduli, including those with repeated prime factors.

## Assumptions and scope

The modulus is an arbitrary odd integer \(m\geq3\). No primality or squarefreeness hypothesis is imposed. Residues are taken in \(R=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), and \(\gcd(u,v,m)=1\) means that the coordinates generate the unit ideal of \(R\). The natural density is over all positive indices, not only indices in dyadic intervals. No assertion is made for even moduli.

## Proof

**Binary state matrices.** For a positive integer \(n\), write \(w_n=(b_n,b_{n+1})^T\). The defining recurrences give

\[
w_{2n}=A w_n,\quad w_{2n+1}=B w_n,\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}-2&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&-2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

In particular, \(w_1=(1,-2)^T\), and the binary expansion of \(n\), read after its leading digit \(1\), gives precisely the sequence of matrix updates. Since \(\det A=\det B=-2\) is a unit in \(R\), every \(w_n\) is primitive.

**The whole primitive orbit.** In \(\operatorname{GL}_2(R)\) the semigroup generated by \(A,B\) coincides with the group they generate: each generator has finite order and its inverse is a positive power. Set \(C=AB^{-1}\) and \(P=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\). All the following equalities hold over \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\), hence modulo every odd \(m\):

\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}-2&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P^{-1}C^2P=\begin{pmatrix}1&-4\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P^{-1}AC^2A^{-1}P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Because \(-4\) and \(-2\) are units in \(R\), powers of these two unipotents contain every elementary upper and lower shear. Such shears generate \(\operatorname{SL}_2(R)\): by the Chinese remainder decomposition, it suffices to check each local ring \(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\); if a determinant-one matrix has a unit in its first column, a row swap made from shears and elimination reduce it to a diagonal unit matrix, and \(\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1})\) is itself a product of shears. Thus the group conjugated by \(P\) contains \(\operatorname{SL}_2(R)\).

The group \(\operatorname{SL}_2(R)\) acts transitively on primitive columns. Explicitly, for primitive \((u,v)\), choose \(a,b\in R\) with \(au+bv=1\); then \(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-v&u\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_2(R)\) sends \((u,v)^T\) to \((1,0)^T\). Since \(P\) preserves primitivity, the orbit of \(w_1\) under \(A,B\) is exactly the set \(\mathcal P_m\) of primitive columns, of size \(J_2(m)\) by inclusion-exclusion or the Chinese remainder theorem.

**Mixing and the natural density.** Consider the finite Markov chain on \(\mathcal P_m\) that independently chooses \(A\) or \(B\) with probability \(1/2\). Both matrices permute \(\mathcal P_m\), so the uniform probability measure is stationary. The established group transitivity and finite semigroup/group equality imply irreducibility. Further, \(A(0,1)^T=(0,1)^T\) is a self-loop on an accessible state; therefore the chain is aperiodic. The finite-state convergence theorem gives uniform convergence, over every initial state, of \(k\)-step transition probabilities to \(1/J_2(m)\).

Consequently, the \(2^k\) integers in \([2^k,2^{k+1})\), corresponding to every word of \(k\) appended binary digits, are asymptotically uniformly distributed over \(\mathcal P_m\). To pass from complete binary shells to arbitrary endpoints, decompose any initial segment of a shell into at most one binary suffix cylinder for each free-suffix length \(j=0,\ldots,k\). Fix \(L<k\). Cylinders with \(j\geq L\) contribute total counting discrepancy at most \(\epsilon_L2^k\), where \(\epsilon_L\to0\) by uniform finite-chain mixing. The cylinders with \(j<L\) contain fewer than \(2^L\) indices altogether. Choosing \(L=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) shows that the discrepancy of **every** initial segment of the shell is \(o(2^k)\), uniformly in its endpoint. Combining the complete preceding shells gives a discrepancy \(o(N)\) for all \(N\to\infty\). This proves the claimed natural density for each primitive pair.

**One-coordinate fibers.** For fixed \(r\pmod m\), a second coordinate \(v\) is allowed exactly when \(v\not\equiv0\pmod p\) for each \(p\mid r\) that also divides \(m\). The Chinese remainder theorem counts the allowed \(v\) as \(m\prod_{p\mid\gcd(r,m)}(1-p^{-1})\). Dividing by \(J_2(m)\) proves the marginal formula, including its zero-residue specialization. The formula \(J_2(m)=m^2\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-2})\) follows by counting all primitive ordered pairs modulo prime powers.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library. The checker evaluates the defining Stern recurrence independently of binary matrix iteration for \(50{,}000\) indices; for \(36\) representative odd moduli, it breadth-first enumerates the two-generator orbit, compares it with every primitive pair, checks its size against \(J_2(m)\), and checks every one-coordinate marginal by direct counting. It tests finite cases only; the group, Markov, and endpoint arguments above provide the infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Ulas and Ulas introduced foundational arithmetic and coprimality properties of Stern polynomials (arXiv:1102.5109v1), but their studied identities and specializations do not state this odd-modulus joint distribution at \(t=-2\). Gawron (arXiv:1401.3553v1) used finite-state methods for special rational evaluations \(t=-1/2,-1/3\), chiefly rational-root questions, density bounds and nonperiodicity; the matrices and specialization here differ. Deshouillers and Schinzel (DOI:10.4064/bc118-3) determined the modular distribution of the **ordinary** Stern sequence at \(t=1\). Their Theorem 2.2 gives a general uniform-density theorem for invertible digit automata and can be applied after the reachable state set is established. But their Proposition 3.2 identifies that set only for the \(t=1\) matrices; neither result establishes the necessary full primitive orbit for the \(t=-2\) generators, which generally have distinct eigenvalues \(1,-2\). The specialization-specific contribution here is the construction of two opposite elementary unipotents inside the negative-two generator group, giving the primitive orbit for **every odd modulus**. The Markov-chain proof is provided directly for completeness, but uniformity conditional on full reachability is not claimed to be new.

## Limitations

No claim is made about even moduli, where the determinants of both digit matrices cease to be units. Finite verification does not replace proof. Equivalence to a more general uniformity theorem in specialist modular Stern-polynomial or automata literature cannot be excluded without a fully exhaustive literature audit. The highly relevant 2019 paper was inspected in its publicly available full text, notably Theorems 1.1 and 2.2 and Proposition 3.2; no other direct \(t=-2\) orbit result was found there. No independent validation has been performed.
