# Scientific review — two-refuge lifetime theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed domain is every integer \(n\ge4\) and strictly positive symmetric migration rate \(\gamma\) and killing rate \(\mu\). Death-free refuges affect only the killing diagonal, not migration. The proof uses the killed-chain survival resolvent and its first two moments, the zero-column-sum identity, a rank-two inverse, and a fully explicit periodic Green kernel. The expression for the Green kernel is checked by substitution into its second-order difference equation, including the periodic jump condition. Positive definiteness guarantees the relevant denominators are positive. Since the Green kernel decreases strictly with the smaller cyclic distance, the positive-shift Laplace order, variance order, and alternative-initial-ensemble mean order all follow without numerical extrapolation. Exact-rational direct-inversion checks agree across sizes 4–10 and all separations; these are supplementary evidence, not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority
The scientific claim is not that Markov fundamental matrices, cyclic Green functions, or trap search are new. The potentially original result is the **universal strict ordering of all positive-argument death-time Laplace transforms when two sites are exempt from a common mortality hazard**, together with the contrasting exact conditional-mean conservation and the induced second-moment order. Elkin and Possingham (2008) treat 100-patch occupancy dynamics, mortality disturbance, and dispersal, not this single-walker law. Ben Zion, Yaari and Shnerb (2010) study nonlinear population synchronization and checkerboard persistence, a different model and objective. Mavroforakis, Mathioudakis and Gionis (2015) prove centrality properties for *absorbing* nodes, whereas the protected sites here preserve mobility and exclude killing. Neither their general supermodularity nor perfect-trap mean-time formulas supply the present strict conditional-Laplace comparison as stated. Accessible local scientific records were inspected for near overlaps and compared by hypotheses and conclusions, not titles. Historical priority remains provisional: Evans–Nord (1985), Soler (1982) and other broad cyclic-trap literature may contain an implied special case; their complete text was not available for definitive comparison.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Two protected patches on a ring are the first nontrivial geometry of refuge **separation**. There is a substantive risk-assessment distinction: for individuals initially located outside refuges, **no relocation of the two refuges can improve the mean** under balanced migration, but relocation changes every positive-argument Laplace transform and strictly changes lifetime variance. Equal spacing minimizes that variance, while clustering raises the lifetime mean if individuals may start inside refuge patches. This parameter-free conflict among commonly used survival indicators supplies a structural diagnostic for the limits of simulation-based reserve rankings; it is not merely a numerical six-site special case. The result is exactly scoped to one symmetric migrating individual, fixed mortality contrast, and the stated initial ensembles; no population-level management benefit is asserted.

## Closest literature and limits
Primary comparisons include Hatlee–Kozak (1980), Soler (1982), Evans–Nord (1985), Elkin–Possingham (2008), Ben Zion–Yaari–Shnerb (2010), and Mavroforakis–Mathioudakis–Gionis (2015). The ecological papers were examined at the full-text methods/results level; the 2015 absorbing-centrality preprint was examined through its model and theorems. Earlier trap papers were available only as abstracts or bibliographic material; their possible stronger coverage is an explicit originality risk. Laplace-transform ordering is not pointwise stochastic dominance or a full convex-order statement. Equal mean and a smaller variance do not determine a generally preferable conservation strategy.
