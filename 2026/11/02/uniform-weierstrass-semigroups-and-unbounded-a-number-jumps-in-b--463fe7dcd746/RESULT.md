# Uniform Weierstrass semigroups and unbounded \(a\)-number jumps in biquadratic Artin–Schreier families

## Finding

Let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_2\) and let \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_4\setminus\mathbb F_2\). For odd integers \(M\ge5\) and \(D\) with \(M/2<D<M\), denote by \(C_{M,D,\lambda}\) the smooth projective model of the equations
\[
y^2+y=x^M,\qquad z^2+z=\alpha x^M+\lambda x^D.
\]
For its unique point \(P\) at infinity, the complete pole semigroup is
\[
H(P)=\begin{cases}\langle4,M\rangle,&\lambda=0,\\\langle4,2D,3M-2D\rangle,&\lambda\ne0.\end{cases}
\]
Every member has genus \(g=3(M-1)/2\), and the three quadratic quotient genera and upper ramification breaks are independent of \(\lambda\), all equal to \((M-1)/2\) and \(M\), respectively. Define \(F_g(b)=\max\{0,\lceil(g-b)/4\rceil\}\). The exact Jacobian \(a\)-numbers are
\[
a(C_{M,D,\lambda})=\begin{cases}F_g(0)+F_g(M),&\lambda=0,\\F_g(0)+F_g(2D)+F_g(3M-2D),&\lambda\ne0.\end{cases}
\]
On the arithmetic ray \(M=8s+5\), \(D=6s+3\), \(s\ge0\), these are respectively \(4s+3\) and \(3s+2\). Thus, for \(s\ge1\), there are unbounded \(a\)-number jumps of size \(s+1\) while all three intermediate quadratic coverings retain their genera and upper ramification breaks. The \(s=0\) instance is already known from [1, Proposition 10.4].

## Assumptions and scope

All schemes are over the algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic two. The curve means the smooth, geometrically connected projective normalization of the affine fiber product, not the naive affine scheme at infinity. The function \(x\) gives a separable degree-four covering of the projective line. The \(a\)-number means the dimension of the Cartier kernel on regular differentials, equivalently the \(a\)-number of the Jacobian. The three intermediate covers correspond to the nonzero linear combinations of the two defining Artin–Schreier classes. Upper ramification breaks refer to their quadratic characters. No claim is made that their \(a\)-numbers, Newton polygons, or Ekedahl–Oort types remain constant for general \(M,D\).

## Proof

**Degree and genus.** The three nonzero \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear combinations of the right-hand polynomials have respective leading coefficients \(1\), \(\alpha\), and \(1+\alpha\). None is zero, so every combination is a reduced odd-degree Artin–Schreier polynomial of degree \(M\). The two classes are linearly independent in \(k(x)/(F-1)k(x)\) because a pole of odd positive order cannot be an Artin–Schreier coboundary. Hence the fiber product is connected of degree four, is unramified over \(\mathbb A^1\), and is totally ramified over infinity. The three quadratic characters have unique upper break \(M\). The Artin–Schreier genus formula, or the genus formula for degree-four fiber products, gives
\[
g=\frac{3(M-1)}2.
\]
Each quadratic quotient has genus \((M-1)/2\). Moreover, for the unique point \(P\) above infinity, \(\operatorname{div}(dx)=(2g-2)P\) (the one-point differential formula). All assertions in this paragraph follow also from Proposition 10.1 of [1]. Since the covering degree is four, \(\operatorname{ord}_P(x)=-4\); as \(y^2+y=x^M\), \(\operatorname{ord}_P(y)=-2M\).

**Zero coefficient.** Set \(w=z+\alpha^2y\). Since \(\alpha^2+\alpha+1=0\), direct calculation in characteristic two gives
\[
w^2+w=y+\lambda x^D.
\]
When \(\lambda=0\), eliminating \(y\) yields \(w^4+w=x^M\). Consequently \(\operatorname{ord}_P(w)=-M\) and \(\langle4,M\rangle\subseteq H(P)\). As \(\gcd(4,M)=1\), the numerical semigroup generated by \(4\) and \(M\) has \(3(M-1)/2=g\) gaps. A rational point of a genus-\(g\) curve has exactly \(g\) Weierstrass gaps; therefore \(H(P)=\langle4,M\rangle\).

**Nonzero coefficient and its third pole generator.** Assume \(\lambda\ne0\). The pole orders of the two summands on the right of \(w^2+w=y+\lambda x^D\) are \(2M\) and \(4D\). They are distinct and \(4D>2M\), hence \(\operatorname{ord}_P(w)=-2D\). Put \(t=(M-D)/2\), a positive integer, and take \(c\in k^*\) such that \(c^2\lambda=1\). For \(u=y+cx^tw\), direct substitution gives
\[
u^2=y+c^2x^{2t}w+c^2x^{2t}y.
\]
The three terms on the right have respective pole orders \(2M\), \(4M-2D\), and \(6M-4D\). Because \(D<M\), the last is strictly largest; there is no possible cancellation. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{ord}_P(u)=-(3M-2D).
\]
It follows that \(H'=\langle4,2D,3M-2D\rangle\subseteq H(P)\). Write \(b=2D\) and \(c_*=3M-2D\). The four numbers
\[
0,\qquad b,\qquad c_*,\qquad b+c_*=3M
\]
have pairwise distinct residues modulo four: \(b\equiv2\pmod4\), whereas \(c_*\) is odd. They form the Apéry set of \(H'\) with respect to four. Indeed, \(2b=4D\) is a multiple of four; and \(2c_*\equiv b\pmod4\) with \(2c_*>b\), since \(c_*>D\) is equivalent to \(M>D\). Therefore reducing the coefficients of \(b\) and \(c_*\) to zero or one never increases the smallest element in a residue class. The genus (number of gaps) of \(H'\), by the Apéry formula, is
\[
\frac{0+b+c_*+(b+c_*)}{4}-\frac32
=\frac{3(M-1)}2=g.
\]
Thus \(H'=H(P)\) by equality of the numbers of gaps.

**The Cartier-kernel counts.** The one-point differential identity established above and the characteristic-two Cartier-kernel formula (Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 10.1 of [1]) yield
\[
a(C)=\dim_k L((g-1)P)=\#\{h\in H(P):0\le h<g\}.
\]
If an Apéry set modulo four is \(\{w_0,w_1,w_2,w_3\}\), each residue contributes exactly \(F_g(w_i)=\max\{0,\lceil(g-w_i)/4\rceil\}\) nongaps below \(g\). For the zero coefficient the Apéry representatives are \(0,M,2M,3M\); because \(2M>g\), only \(0,M\) contribute. For nonzero coefficient the representatives are \(0,2D,3M-2D,3M\); because \(3M>g\), only the first three may contribute. This proves the stated exact formulas for all allowed \(M,D,\lambda\).

**Unbounded jump.** Set \(M=8s+5\), \(D=6s+3\). Then \(g=12s+6\), \(2D=g\), and \(3M-2D=g+3\). Hence for \(\lambda\ne0\) only multiples of four are nongaps below \(g\), giving \(3s+2\). At \(\lambda=0\), besides those multiples of four there are \(s+1\) nongaps \(M+4j\) for \(0\le j\le s\), giving \(4s+3\). The jump is \(s+1\), and the quadratic quotient genera and upper breaks remain unchanged because their three reduced defining polynomials all have the same degree \(M\). The genus-six instance \(s=0\) matches the previously published example [1, Proposition 10.4].

## Relationship to prior work

Almeida Filho–Tafazolian–Vieira [1, §§2 and 10, especially Propositions 10.1–10.4] provide the characteristic-two semigroup counting criterion and the isolated genus-six example \((M,D)=(5,3)\). Their common-monomial case treats \(\lambda=0\), while their distinct-monomial case has a different profile of quadratic pole orders. Neither statement includes the full nonzero-coefficient semigroup classification for all \(M,D\) nor an unbounded jump. The present theorem derives the missing third pole generator in the two-exponent family and proves a general Apéry identity. Earlier work of van der Geer and van der Vlugt [2, §2 and §3] treats genus, isogeny and arithmetic of Artin–Schreier fiber products but does not state this \(a\)-number/Weierstrass-semigroup classification.

## Limitations

The result applies only when \(M,D\) are odd and \(M/2<D<M\). It describes \(a\)-numbers and a single pointed semigroup, not full Ekedahl–Oort types or supersingularity in general. The cited genus-six example is prior work; no new priority is claimed for that base case. General character-theoretic and Cartier formulas are also established literature, not new results. No independent mathematical audit has been performed; earlier undiscovered equivalent presentations in the literature remain possible.
