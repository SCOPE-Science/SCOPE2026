# Uniformly bounded rowmotion on the first two nonempty Boolean ranks

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 3\), let \(P_n\) consist of the one-element and two-element subsets of \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), ordered by inclusion. Equivalently, \(P_n\) is the vertex–edge incidence poset of the complete graph \(K_n\). Let \(\rho\) be classical rowmotion on the order ideals \(J(P_n)\): the image of an ideal is generated by the minimal elements outside it. For a positive integer \(r\), write \(c_r(n)\) for the number of rowmotion cycles of exact length \(r\).

Define, for every integer \(m\ge2\),
\[
N_m=2^{\binom m2},\qquad
 d_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^j\binom mj 2^{\binom{m-j}{2}},\qquad
 a_m=2d_m-N_m,\qquad b_m=N_m-d_m.
\]
Then **the complete cycle census** is
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_2(n)&=\frac{a_n}{2}, & c_3(n)&=1, & c_5(n)&=n,\\
c_6(n)&=\frac12\sum_{m=4}^{n-1}\binom nm a_m, &&&
c_8(n)&=\frac12\left(\sum_{m=2}^{n-1}\binom nm b_m-n\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Empty sums are zero. Every other cycle count is zero. Hence
\[
\operatorname{ord}(\rho)=
\begin{cases}15,&n=3,\\120,&n\ge4.\end{cases}
\]
In particular, although \(|J(P_n)|=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk2^{\binom k2}\) grows rapidly, every rowmotion orbit has length at most eight, uniformly in \(n\). All five lengths \(2,3,5,6,8\) occur when \(n\ge5\); the lengths for \(n=4\) are \(2,3,5,8\), and those for \(n=3\) are \(3,5\).

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple and vertex-labelled; all rowmotion cycles are counted on labelled order ideals, not modulo graph automorphisms. The order is exactly the inclusion order on subsets of sizes one and two, **without** the empty subset and without subsets of size three or larger. Statements concern classical combinatorial rowmotion, not piecewise-linear or birational lifts. The result is an exact infinite-family theorem for every integer \(n\ge3\).

Write \(V=[n]\), \(K(W)=\binom W2\) for the edge set of the complete graph on \(W\subseteq V\), and \(\operatorname{supp}(F)\) for the endpoints of the edges in \(F\). Every ideal is uniquely a pair \((A,B)\) with \(A\subseteq V\) and \(B\subseteq K(A)\), where \(A\) and \(B\) specify its selected singleton and pair elements. For \(X\subseteq V\), \(Y=V\setminus X\), and \(H\subseteq K(Y)\), denote by \(J_X(H)=\bigl(K(V)\setminus K(Y)\bigr)\cup H\) the edge set containing all edges incident with \(X\), together with \(H\) on \(Y\). Every occurrence of a graph such as \(H\) in an ideal pair means its edge set.

## Proof

**The rowmotion recurrence.** In an ideal \((A,B)\), all singleton elements outside \(A\) are minimal in the complement. A pair element outside \(B\) is minimal there exactly when both its endpoints are in \(A\). Thus, with \(F=K(A)\setminus B\),
\[
\boxed{\rho(A,B)=\bigl((V\setminus A)\cup\operatorname{supp}(F),\ F\bigr).}\tag{1}
\]
An explicit inverse takes any ideal \(I\) to \(P_n\setminus\uparrow\max(I)\), where \(\max(I)\) denotes its maximal elements and \(\uparrow\) denotes upward closure; hence the map is a permutation of the finite ideal set.

**Every orbit visits a full-singleton state.** Suppose \(A\ne V\), and put \(T=\operatorname{supp}(F)\), \(U=A\setminus T\), \(C=V\setminus A\), so \(C\ne\varnothing\). If \(U=\varnothing\), (1) gives singleton set \(V\) after one step. If \(U\ne\varnothing\) and \(T\ne\varnothing\), the next missing-edge set contains every edge between \(C\) and \(T\); it therefore has support \(C\cup T\), and (1) gives singleton set \(V\) after two steps. Finally, if \(T=\varnothing\), the first image is \((C,\varnothing)\); if \(|C|\ge2\), the second has singleton set \(V\). If \(|C|=1\), the second image is \((A,\varnothing)\), whose successor has singleton set \(V\) because \(|A|=n-1\ge2\). Thus every initial state reaches a state of the form \((V,B)\) within at most three steps. Since rowmotion is bijective, every cycle contains such a state.

**Classification from full-singleton states.** Regard \(B\subseteq K(V)\) as a graph on \(V\). A universal vertex of \(B\) has degree \(n-1\); an isolated vertex has degree zero. A graph on \(n\ge3\) vertices cannot simultaneously possess an isolated and a universal vertex.

If \(B\) has neither isolated nor universal vertices, the rowmotion sequence is precisely \((V,B)\), \((V,K(V)\setminus B)\), and back: an exact two-cycle. If \(B\) has isolated vertices but no universal vertex, one rowmotion step reaches the full-singleton state with edge set \(K(V)\setminus B\), which does have universal vertices. The complete graph generates the unique three-cycle
\[
(V,K(V))\longrightarrow(\varnothing,\varnothing)\longrightarrow(V,\varnothing)\longrightarrow(V,K(V)).
\]

It remains to classify a state \((V,B)\) with a nonempty proper set \(X\) of all universal vertices of \(B\). Put \(Y=V\setminus X\). Then \(|Y|\ge2\), \(B=J_X(H)\), and the graph \(H\) on \(Y\) has **no universal vertices**. Every edge or complement in the following explicit cycles is relative to its stated vertex set.

If \(H\) has **no isolated vertices**, let \(\overline H=K(Y)\setminus H\). Both \(H\) and \(\overline H\) have no isolated or universal vertices. Repeated application of (1) gives the exact six-cycle
\[
\begin{aligned}
(V,J_X(H))&\to(Y,\overline H)\to(V,H)\to(V,J_X(\overline H))\\
&\to(Y,H)\to(V,\overline H)\to(V,J_X(H)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The two full-singleton states in (2) that have universal vertices are the first and fourth displayed states.

Suppose instead that \(H\) has isolated vertices \(Z\ne\varnothing\), but also at least one edge. Set \(T=Y\setminus Z\), so \(T\ne\varnothing\) and \(H\) has no isolates on \(T\). Define \(Q=K(X\cup T)\setminus H\) and \(R=K(Z\cup T)\setminus H\). Then (1) yields the exact eight-cycle
\[
\begin{aligned}
(V,J_X(H))&\to(Y,K(Y)\setminus H)\to(X\cup T,H)\to(V,Q)\\
&\to(V,K(V)\setminus Q)\to(X\cup T,Q)\to(Z\cup T,H)\\
&\to(V,R)\to(V,J_X(H)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The full-singleton states with universal vertices in (3) are exactly the first and fifth; their universal-vertex sets are \(X\) and \(Z\), respectively. The presence of edges between \(Z\) and \(T\) in the fifth edge set, but not in the first, rules out period four; the proper singleton sets rule out periods one and two.

Finally, suppose \(H=\varnothing\). If \(|X|=1\), then
\[
(V,J_X(\varnothing))\to(Y,K(Y))\to(X,\varnothing)\to(Y,\varnothing)\to(V,K(Y))\to(V,J_X(\varnothing))
\tag{4}
\]
is an exact five-cycle, with only one full-singleton state having a universal vertex. If \(|X|\ge2\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
(V,J_X(\varnothing))&\to(Y,K(Y))\to(X,\varnothing)\to(V,K(X))\\
&\to(V,J_Y(\varnothing))\to(X,K(X))\to(Y,\varnothing)\\
&\to(V,K(Y))\to(V,J_X(\varnothing))
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
is an exact eight-cycle; precisely the first and fifth states have universal vertices. All cycles in (2)–(5) have the claimed *exact* lengths, as their displayed intermediate singleton/edge pairs are distinct. These cases exhaust every full-singleton state, and the preceding paragraph exhausts every orbit.

**Counting cycles.** There are \(N_m\) labelled simple graphs on \(m\) vertices. Inclusion–exclusion over vertices of degree zero proves that \(d_m\) is the number with no isolated vertices. Since a graph on \(m\ge2\) vertices cannot contain both an isolated and a universal vertex, complementation gives \(a_m=2d_m-N_m\) graphs with neither and \(b_m=N_m-d_m\) graphs with isolates and no universal vertices.

Every two-cycle contributes two full-singleton graphs with neither isolated nor universal vertices, giving \(c_2(n)=a_n/2\). The complete graph gives one three-cycle. The star-with-universal-center states in (4) give \(n\) distinct five-cycles. For a six-cycle, choose the complement \(Y\) of its nonempty universal-vertex set with \(4\le|Y|\le n-1\), then choose one of the \(a_{|Y|}\) graphs \(H\) with neither isolates nor universals; equation (2) counts every six-cycle twice, proving the stated \(c_6(n)\). All remaining full-singleton graphs with universal vertices have \(H\) with at least one isolated vertex. There are \(\sum_{m=2}^{n-1}\binom nm b_m\) such graphs; \(n\) are the five-cycle stars from (4), and each eight-cycle is counted twice by (3) or (5). This proves the stated \(c_8(n)\) and exhausts all cycles.

For \(n=3\), only lengths three and five occur, giving order \(15\). For each \(n\ge4\), the full/empty three-cycle, a star five-cycle, and the eight-cycle in (5) with \(|X|\ge2\) and \(|Y|\ge2\) all exist. Their least common multiple is \(120\), and every orbit length divides \(120\); the order is therefore exactly \(120\). For \(n\ge5\), a graph \(H\) that is a spanning cycle on \(Y\) with \(|Y|=n-1\) and \(|X|=1\) supplies a six-cycle, and a cycle graph on \(V\) with no isolated or universal vertices supplies a two-cycle. This proves the stated lists of occurring lengths. \(\square\)

It also checks the closed-form census and performs nonexhaustive larger-parameter stress tests. The independent `verify_alt.cpp` enumerates subsets of the full poset ground set, tests ideal closure, implements rowmotion by listing minimal complement elements directly, and reconstructs the same complete cycle histograms for \(3\le n\le6\). Execute with `python3 verify.py` and `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify_alt.cpp -o verify_alt && ./verify_alt`. The all-parameter conclusion is proved symbolically above; the finite checks are corroboration.

## Relationship to prior work

Striker and Williams' *Promotion and Rowmotion* gives the classical rowmotion definition and toggle framework, and Striker's *Rowmotion and generalized toggle groups* develops broader toggle-group results. Trotter and Wang define graph incidence posets and study their order dimension. None of these inspected treatments determines the all-parameter orbit spectrum above for the singleton/pair ranks of the Boolean lattice. A complete graph's incidence poset is also the rank-selected Boolean poset on ranks one and two, and rowmotion is conjugate to the standard antichain complement action; these descriptions have been included in the literature comparison. Earlier exact rowmotion results for star, complete-bipartite, and Fano-incidence posets concern **different incidence relations** and do not imply this theorem. The uniform bound and explicit counts here are not consequences merely of the general bijectivity of rowmotion or of its representation by toggles.

## Limitations

No analogous bound is asserted for arbitrary graph-incidence posets, larger rank truncations, birational rowmotion, or all rank-selected Boolean posets. The originality assessment is based on the located and inspected literature and does not certify publication priority; an equivalent result under different terminology could exist. Random large-parameter stress tests and finite enumerations alone would not establish the infinite-family theorem without the explicit case analysis.
