Three-anchor extension is expressible with four variables. Conversely, when one of four pebbles is moved, at most three distinct anchors remain: reuse the corresponding vertex on an equality move and otherwise apply the extension axiom, padding to three anchors if necessary. This works at every finite quantifier rank in pure adjacency with equality. A mixed row–column–symbol triangle can have its required common neighbor only at the fourth corner of the corresponding rectangle, forcing equality of opposite entries. After symbol normalization, the relative row permutations are involutions; the row permutations then commute and act as a transitive, faithful abelian permutation group, hence regularly. This yields a Boolean-group table up to isotopy. Orders 2 and 4 lack an extension; in dimension at least 4, use anchors (0,0,0), (a,b,a+b), (c,d,c+d) with a,b,c,d independent. Any common neighbor would assign row, column and symbol bijectively to those anchors, producing a nontrivial linear dependence.
