# Scientific review of the Hamiltonian-path edge-shattering theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** Necessity follows from the all-positive trace. For sufficiency, contracting demanded-positive path components leaves a linear forest of forbidden interblock edges. At least four blocks admit a block Hamiltonian path in the complement of that forest, via even-then-odd ordering; two and three blocks are handled by reversing a nontrivial block to avoid its unique forbidden join. The graph has at least four vertices, so the nontrivial block required in small cases exists. This establishes every quantified trace. A positive-edge counting online predictor supplies the Littlestone upper bound and fixed-order VC shattering gives the lower bound. Exhaustive finite tests through seven vertices corroborate the support characterization. No finite test is used as a proof of the universal statement.

**Originality — PASS within inspected material; historical priority uncertain.** The closest full-text comparison is Mofidi's 2022 cycle/spanning-tree study, Theorem 3.3, which concerns all cycles or all spanning trees, not all *spanning paths*. A statement about shattering by a superclass cannot establish shattering by the Hamiltonian-path subclass. A 1997 paper on VC set systems defined by graph paths was found, but its full text was inaccessible; the abstract alone does not settle coverage. Hypercube papers address prescribed forest edges and faults in different host graphs. The known linear-forest enumerator is background combinatorics, not claimed as independently novel.

**Value — PASS.** It resolves all edge-query shattering supports for a natural constrained spanning structure, not just a scalar dimension, and proves a simultaneous positive/negative completion theorem. It also establishes the precise online mistake complexity and maximum-support rigidity for all complete-graph orders. This draws a concrete distinction between the all-path, all-cycle, all-tree, and Hamiltonian-path models.

Mofidi, *Facta Universitatis* (2022), DOI `10.22190/FUMI210301011M`; E. Kranakis et al., *Discrete Applied Mathematics* 77 (1997), 237–257, DOI `10.1016/S0166-218X(96)00137-0`; Y. The result does not extend by the same argument to arbitrary host graphs. Finite enumeration is restricted to at most seven vertices.
