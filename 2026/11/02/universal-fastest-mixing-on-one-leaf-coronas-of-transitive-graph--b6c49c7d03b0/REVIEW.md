# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The unrestricted convex SLEM problem is reduced by exact group averaging to two orbit weights. Every candidate then has exact \(2\times2\) spectral blocks. The inequality \(\beta\le2d\) forces the most negative eigenvalue modulus below the upper mode at \(\alpha\), and strict scalar convexity proves the closed minimizer. The optimizer satisfies the neglected symmetric-mode inequality and uses the full core stochastic budget. A standalone program completed 1768 finite exact/numerical grouped assertions on representative graph families. The all-orders proof is analytic, not an extrapolation from those checks.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The original FMMC convex norm formulation (Boyd–Diaconis–Xiao 2004), symmetry averaging and one-orbit results (Boyd–Diaconis–Parrilo–Xiao 2007/2009) are prior work. The four-vertex path and the previously calculated cyclic sunlet are special cases; those formulas are not presented as novel. The new quantified statement treats every connected vertex- and edge-transitive base, establishes the disappearance of the upper Laplacian spectral endpoint, and determines the universal radical and scale separation. Queries concerning pendant, whiskered, sunlet, corona, attached-leaf and SLEM formulations yielded no stronger implication in the material inspected. The complete full text of a potentially related 2020 clique-lifting paper was not accessed; specialized unindexed results remain a genuine risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Private-leaf attachment is a standard graph transformation introducing a bottleneck at each vertex. The theorem gives a complete optimal transition prescription for this natural family and a structural law: \(a_*\sim\tfrac12\sqrt{\alpha/d}\) even though \(1-\mu_*\sim\alpha/(2d)\). It explains exact saturated core weights and quantifies how a graph-wide spectral invariant controls the optimal allocation. The result is not merely an individual small-graph computation or a renamed basic inequality.
