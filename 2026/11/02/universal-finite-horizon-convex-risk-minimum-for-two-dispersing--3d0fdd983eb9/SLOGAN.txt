In a two-habitat metapopulation facing exchangeable, temporally independent growth shocks, the dispersal fraction \(q_*=1/(1+s)\) uniquely minimizes every strictly convex measure of mean-normalized total-population risk after at least two years, even with migrant survival \(s<1\). The exact two-year variance penalty for any other dispersal rate is a positive multiple of its squared deviation from complete mixing, while no claim is made about absolute persistence or optimal long-run growth.
