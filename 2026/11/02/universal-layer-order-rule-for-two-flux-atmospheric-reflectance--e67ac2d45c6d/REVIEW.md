# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The passive reciprocal adding map preserves \([0,1]\), and the exact projective-matrix commutator produces a strict order sign for every backing reflectance. Applying the sign under arbitrary prefixes and suffixes proves the complete all-layer rearrangement and equality cases. The homogeneous Kubelka–Munk identity \(w=2(1+K/S)\) is checked by algebra and a continuous zero-absorption limit. Reproducible exact-rational tests verify 4,800 swap-sign identities and 10,944 sampled permutation comparisons, not infinite generality.

## Originality: PASS, provisional
Kubelka (1954), DOI 10.1364/JOSA.44.000330, already gives the adding law and the exact index in a **three-sheet transmittance** formula; these facts are not new. The claimed extension concerns the complete all-\(n\) *surface-reflectance* order, independent of thickness and backing albedo, plus equality cases. The physically more general Hébert–Hersch–Becker (2007) framework, DOI 10.1364/JOSAA.24.002628, does not expressly state this specialized ordering in inspected relevant sections. Relevant mathematical research-record searches returned no matching published theorem. A comprehensive historical-priority guarantee is impossible, and the 2015 two-flux survey was available only in abstract form. These limitations are explicit rather than being taken as novelty evidence.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The problem asks which ordering of already-given scattering and absorbing strata maximizes return flux for uncertain underlying surface reflectance. The result gives one top-to-bottom sort for every such surface, characterizes all optimal/minimal arrangements, and proves the order is unaffected by layer thickness in the Kubelka–Munk model. Its index is not equivalent to sorting single-layer reflectance; an exact three-layer case shows the distinction. No claims are made for broadband or nonreciprocal full radiative transfer.

## Limits and closest literature
The transport model and one-layer coefficients are classical. The very close 1954 transmittance comparison means the novelty lies strictly in the all-layer reflectance optimization, not the underlying index or transfer law. Broader optical-composition books may subsume the statement. No empirical atmospheric control or spectral optimum is asserted.
