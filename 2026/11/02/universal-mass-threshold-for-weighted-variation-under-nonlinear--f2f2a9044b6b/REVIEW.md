# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proposed proof reduces the full nonlinear star evolution to independent leaf-to-center differences coupled through one scalar flux. The finite-dimensional mass-weighted monotone gradient system has unique global solutions for every \(p>1\). The directional derivative of the weighted \(\ell^1\) edge norm is the maximum over its subdifferential, including coordinates initially equal to zero. The inequality \(s_j\sum_iw_is_i\ge2w_j-W\) gives the sharp derivative bound; isolated-edge inputs attain each coefficient exactly. This proves the universal time criterion and the reciprocal-mass feasibility threshold. The standalone checker passed 26,460 exact-rational derivative inequalities, 15,120 exact extremizer equalities, 1,260 exact feasible-mass profiles, 5,320 fractional-exponent tests, and 15,000 explicit nonlinear flow steps. The latter finite tests are corroboration only.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The most pertinent available full text is Hua and Mugnolo (arXiv:1410.1584v1), especially its weighted generator and nonlinear semigroup theorems: vertex-space \(\ell^q\) contraction does not imply edge-gradient \(\ell^1\) variation contraction, and the mass-balancing threshold is not stated there. Mugnolo (arXiv:1207.1996v1) studies nonlinear semigroup contractions in vertex sequence spaces and positivity/comparison but does not assert the independently weighted edge variation criterion. Keller and Mugnolo (arXiv:1509.06062v1) study \(p\)-energy spectral/Cheeger estimates. Xin, Mu and Liu (doi:10.1155/2011/937079) treat absorption, comparison and extinction/positivity. The graph-cut article of van Gennip and collaborators was available only at the abstract/reference level for this comparison. Targeted searches and available prior results were checked against implication rather than titles alone. The nearest counting-mass heat classification does not cover the changed-mass all-exponent stability threshold. Residual risk: an equivalent theorem might be implicit in less readily retrieved matrix-measure or dissipativity literature, so this assessment is not an assertion of universal uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The result addresses a natural measurable-network question: exactly when nonlinear graph heat truly diminishes independent weighted edge variation, not just vertex norm. It identifies a complete geometric/mass threshold for every finite star and every nonlinear exponent, produces the attained sharp infinitesimal invariant, and demonstrates stabilization of arbitrarily high degree by increasing only the center mass. The six-leaf transition at center-to-leaf mass ratio four is a motivated structural boundary rather than an arbitrary small-graph statistic.

## Limitations

Finite stars only, strictly positive masses/conductances/variation weights and real-valued inputs; one-input variation contraction only. No two-input Lipschitz claim, no exact long-time nonlinear decay profile, and no classification for general trees. Relevant original research is documented, but some implicit prior coverage is possible. The accessible sources cannot substitute for independent scientific assessment.
