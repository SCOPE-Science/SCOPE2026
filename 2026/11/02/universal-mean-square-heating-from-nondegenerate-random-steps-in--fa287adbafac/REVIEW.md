# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The claim is normalized to the unit-frequency oscillator and an independent common step law with compact support in the strictly elliptic interval. Direct multiplication gives the stated Verlet map and its unique invariant positive quadratic form for each deterministic step. A normalized positive-semidefinite map has an eigenmatrix by Brouwer; absence of real eigenlines makes it positive definite. Strict log-determinant concavity forces a Perron eigenvalue greater than one for every nondegenerate step law, while an adjoint eigenmatrix produces an exact exponential quadratic-energy identity for every nonzero initial vector. The three-dimensional second-moment lift, exact two-point determinant, and analytic small-noise expansion were independently checked by the bundled exact-arithmetic verifier. The theorem does not infer an infinite-time statement merely from finite enumeration.

## Originality (PASS; best-knowledge assessment)
The original random-time-step paper (Abdulle--Garegnani, arXiv:1801.01340, full HTML Sections 7.1--7.2 and 9.7) treats symplecticity and time-bounded energy accuracy under restricted noise scales and trajectory hypotheses, not the present exact fixed-law infinite-step criterion. Calvo--Sanz-Serna's author-hosted 1993 text was inspected for deterministic variable-step and long-time results on the Kepler problem; this does not state the independent random-Verlet second-moment law. Skeel--Gear 1992 was located and its abstract examined, but a full-text comparison was not possible. Targeted published-research-record searches returned results on Langevin/UBU harmonic stability and oscillator covariance rather than the same unforced random-time-step Verlet model. The general positive-cone argument and the classical variable-step caution are established mathematics; the specific universal classification, exact binary determinant, and perturbative growth coefficient are the narrowly assessed contribution. Residual risk: an older paper on random products of elliptic symplectic matrices may subsume the qualitative divergence claim, and an unpublished or inaccessible treatment may already calculate its coefficient. This is not an independent priority certification.

## Value (PASS)
Randomization is used to quantify integration uncertainty while retaining a pathwise symplectic update, so it is mathematically consequential to know whether the accumulated mean-square energy stays bounded. The answer here is a sharp zero-variance-versus-positive-variance dichotomy, not a tiny tuning improvement. The leading constant \(\sigma^2h^4/[2(4-h^2)]\) translates the long-time heating into a concrete step-randomization design parameter. The theorem distinguishes a fixed positive-randomness infinite-iteration limit from bounded-horizon results and identifies exactly which guarantees symplecticity cannot supply.

## Risks and scope
Only one degree of freedom, independent nonadaptive steps strictly between zero and two, fixed law as the number of iterations grows, and exact arithmetic are covered. Almost-sure instability, bounded physical-time error, higher-dimensional systems, and a joint small-step/noise limit are not claimed. The inaccessible Skeel--Gear primary full text and general random-matrix literature remain the principal independent originality risks.
