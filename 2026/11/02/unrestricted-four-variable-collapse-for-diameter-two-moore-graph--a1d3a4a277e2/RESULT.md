# Unrestricted four-variable collapse for diameter-two Moore graphs

## Finding
A **diameter-two Moore graph of degree** \(d\ge 2\) is a finite simple undirected graph of degree \(d\), diameter two, and girth five. The Moore bound forces \(d^2+1\) vertices. Let \(G_d\) denote any such graph, whenever one exists, and regard it in the *pure adjacency and equality* first-order language, without counting quantifiers.

For any two such graphs of degrees \(d,e\ge2\), their equivalence under *unrestricted quantifier depth* and a fixed number of reusable variables is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_d\equiv_{\mathrm{FO}^{2}}G_e&\quad\text{always};\\
G_d\equiv_{\mathrm{FO}^{3}}G_e&\iff \min(d,3)=\min(e,3);\\
G_d\equiv_{\mathrm{FO}^{4}}G_e&\iff \min(d,4)=\min(e,4).
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, every hypothetical Moore graph of degree \(57\) has **exactly the same four-variable first-order sentences, at every quantifier depth**, as the 50-vertex Hoffman–Singleton graph. For the known graphs, the five-cycle, Petersen graph, and Hoffman–Singleton graph realize precisely three distinct four-variable theories, with a sharp 50-vertex within-family representative bound. The first distinguishing quantifier ranks for degree two versus any greater degree and degree three versus any degree at least four are respectively three and four.

## Assumptions and scope
All structures are finite, nonempty, simple loop-free graphs, and every occurrence of a Moore graph refers only to diameter two and girth five, not diameter-one complete graphs or longer odd cycles. No assumption of vertex transitivity is used. The degree-57 case is **conditional**; its existence is not claimed. The Hoffman–Singleton theorem restricts the realizable degrees to \(2,3,7\), and possibly \(57\). The theorem is relative to this class of graphs and does not assert identification among all graphs.

For three *distinct* vertices \(x,y,z\), define their **star flag** \(S(x,y,z)\) to be one precisely when the three are pairwise nonadjacent and possess a common neighbor; otherwise it is zero. This is an auxiliary game invariant, not an additional relation in the language. In a Moore graph, two nonadjacent vertices have exactly one common neighbor, and adjacent vertices have none.

## Proof
**1. Basic Moore counts.** Every vertex has \(d\) neighbors, two adjacent vertices have zero common neighbors, and two nonadjacent vertices have exactly one. Indeed, the neighborhoods in the radius-two Moore tree are disjoint because there are no triangles or quadrangles, and exhaust the graph by diameter two. Therefore the order is \(1+d+d(d-1)=d^2+1\). For two distinct adjacent vertices, the numbers of third vertices adjacent to both, to exactly one in either specified direction, and to neither are respectively \(0,d-1,d-1,(d-1)^2\). For two distinct nonadjacent vertices they are \(1,d-1,d-1,d(d-2)\). All required nonzero supports agree when \(d\ge3\). For one fixed vertex there is always an adjacent and a distinct nonadjacent vertex. Permanent strategies retaining only the atomic type of the remaining one or two pebbles therefore establish the first two positive equivalence assertions (the third-variable case for degrees at least three).

**2. The enhanced invariant.** For four pebbles, retain equality, adjacency for all occupied pairs, and \(S\) for every triple of distinct occupied vertices. To show that this can be maintained indefinitely for degrees at least four, fix three distinct occupied vertices \(x,y,z\). They have exactly four possible unlabeled induced-star types: a two-edge path; one edge and an isolated vertex; an independent triple with common center (a *star triple*); or an independent triple without common center (a *nonstar triple*). A triangle is impossible. For every candidate fourth vertex \(w\), its enhanced profile comprises its three adjacency bits to \(x,y,z\) and the three flags \(S(x,y,w),S(x,z,w),S(y,z,w)\). The following exhaustive multiplicity descriptions show that the **support** of this six-bit profile depends only on the triple type, not the degree or the particular Moore graph, as soon as \(d\ge4\):

| Three-pebble type | Fourth-vertex profile multiplicities, each entry referring to a distinct profile | Number of supported profiles for \(d\ge4\) |
|---|---|---|
| Independent, nonstar | Six profiles of multiplicity \(1\); six of multiplicity \(d-3\); one of multiplicity \((d-3)^2+1\) | 13 |
| Independent, star | One of multiplicity \(1\); one of multiplicity \(d-3\); three of multiplicity \(d-1\); one of multiplicity \((d-1)(d-3)\) | 6 |
| Exactly one edge | Two of multiplicity \(1\); five of multiplicity \(d-2\); one of multiplicity \((d-2)(d-3)\) | 8 |
| Two-edge path | One of multiplicity \(d-2\); two of multiplicity \(d-1\); one of multiplicity \((d-1)(d-2)\) | 4 |

Each row sums to \(d^2-2\), the available vertices outside the chosen triple. No profile is counted twice in a row. Its explicit incidence description is as follows.

For a nonstar independent triple, the three unique pair-centers are distinct and, together with the original triple, induce a six-cycle. The opposite pairs of this cycle have three distinct unique common neighbors outside it; no outside vertex can touch two other cycle vertices because this would make a triangle or quadrangle. These yield six singleton profiles, six groups of \(d-3\) vertices attached to exactly one cycle vertex, and the remaining \((d-3)^2+1\) vertices touching none of the cycle. The profile records which center is touched through the corresponding star bit. For a star triple, its common center is the singleton profile; the other \(d-3\) neighbors of that center, the \(d-1\) neighbors outside the center of each original vertex, and the remaining \((d-1)(d-3)\) vertices give the other five profiles. A one-edge triple extends uniquely to a pentagon through the common neighbors of each of its two nonadjacent pairs. Its other two pentagon vertices contribute the singleton profiles, while the \(d-2\) external neighbors of each pentagon vertex contribute five profiles; \((d-2)(d-3)\) vertices touch none of this pentagon. For a two-edge path, the middle vertex is the unique common neighbor of its endpoints. Its \(d-2\) other neighbors give one profile, the \(d-1\) other neighbors of each endpoint give two, and the remaining \((d-1)(d-2)\) vertices give the last. Each statement follows solely from unique common neighbors and the absence of short cycles.

The support descriptions remain valid under any permutation of the selected triple, since the six bits are permuted accordingly. If the remaining selected pebbles are fewer than three distinct vertices, the same conclusion follows directly from the one- and two-anchor counts of Step 1 together with the unique-center characterization of \(S\). A move onto an occupied vertex is matched by equality. Thus any new fourth-vertex enhanced profile realizable on one side is realizable on the other. Duplicator maintains the enhanced invariant after **every** move, regardless of the number of rounds, and in particular preserves the atomic graph type. The standard four-pebble game characterization proves \(G_d\equiv_{\mathrm{FO}^{4}}G_e\) for all realizable \(d,e\ge4\).

**3. Sharp separation.** The five-cycle has no independent triple. Every Moore graph with \(d\ge3\) has three distinct independent neighbors of a vertex, so a three-variable sentence of rank three separates \(d=2\) from \(d\ge3\). No sentence of rank at most two distinguishes any two of these graphs, by the one-pebble extension count from Step 1.

For degrees three versus at least four, use the four-variable rank-four sentence which says that some three distinct independent vertices have both a **common neighbor** and a **common nonneighbor**. It can be written using variables \(x,y,z,w\) by quantifying \(x,y,z\), requiring pairwise distinct nonadjacency, and conjoining \(\exists w(E(w,x)\land E(w,y)\land E(w,z))\) with \(\exists w(w\ne x\land w\ne y\land w\ne z\land\neg E(w,x)\land\neg E(w,y)\land\neg E(w,z))\). The two occurrences of \(w\) reuse the same variable symbol. In degree three, every star triple comprises all neighbors of its center and every other vertex touches one of these three, so the sentence is false. If \(d\ge4\), choose any three neighbors of a center. The number of vertices touching none of these three is \((d-1)(d-3)>0\), so the sentence is true. Since Step 1 establishes unrestricted three-variable equivalence for all \(d\ge3\), neither a smaller variable width nor a quantifier rank below four can distinguish these degree classes. This completes all three classifications.

## Verification
The standalone, standard-library-only program `verify_graphs.py` constructs the Petersen graph as the disjointness graph of two-element subsets of a five-set and independently constructs the Hoffman–Singleton graph from five pentagons and five pentagrams. It checks exact Moore parameters for all vertex pairs and enumerates the six-bit fourth-vertex support for every ordered triple of distinct vertices (720 and 117,600 triples, respectively), confirming the uniform support counts \(7,4,7,4\) for the degree-three unlabeled types and \(13,6,8,4\) for the degree-seven types. It also tests the rank-four separating condition. The separate `verify_symbolic.py` checks the four polynomial multiplicity rows, sums, nonnegativity, and support cutoffs at degrees \(3,4,5,7,57,127\). Run `python3 verify_graphs.py` and `python3 verify_symbolic.py`; expected outputs are included as corresponding `.out` files.

These are finite checks of key lemmas and graph presentations; the unbounded-depth theorem depends on the general counting and invariant proof above, not on extrapolating finite searches. No degree-57 graph was constructed or assumed to exist.

## Relationship to prior work
The Moore-graph structures and exceptional degrees arise from the classical Hoffman–Singleton theory. Azarija and Klavžar's 2012 preprint studies convex-cycle extremality of Moore graphs, not finite-variable theories. Grohe and Otto's 2012 work supplies the relevant unrestricted pebble-game semantics, not this graph-specific stable four-pebble invariant. A closer bounded-**quantifier-rank-four** classification with the same three degree classes has been compared: equality of all rank-four sentences does **not** entail agreement at arbitrary depth using four variables. The present assertion and proof are specifically the permanent three-point star-profile extension property, which promotes bounded-depth similarity to unrestricted \(\mathrm{FO}^4\)-equivalence. No equivalent unrestricted result was located in the consulted record and literature searches; that absence is not a proof of publication priority.

## Limitations
This classification is relative to finite diameter-two Moore graphs of girth five. It does not claim that five variables fail to distinguish a hypothetical degree-57 graph from Hoffman–Singleton, or that such a graph exists. It excludes counting quantifiers, constants, colored edges, and additional predicates. The theorem's correctness rests on the stated incidence-count arguments; finite scripts alone cannot certify all degrees. Exhaustive historical-priority verification and independent mathematical review remain outstanding.
