# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Excitation-sector block decomposition distinguishes two different bright states, proving the exact leakage identity and its necessary-and-sufficient zero set for every pure charger. Orthogonal postcollision charger sectors remove all battery-phase interference for the classified family. A constructive Bloch-axis argument proves existence of adaptive *local* product measurements with identical conditional energy, and direct concurrence algebra gives the rate–concurrence line and quadratic activation. The attached independent 8×8 matrix-exponential test probes random generic and symmetric parameters and all conditional outcomes. The tested 1,800 assertions are supplementary to the complete analytic argument. Boundary requirements \(g_j>0\), \(0<q<1\), and pure fixed chargers are explicit.

**Originality — PASS, best-knowledge.** The full-text motivating paper (arXiv:2610.31242v1, model, recurrence, conclusions, references) assumes equal charger–battery couplings and reports equal-energy local readout; it does not contain the unequal-coupling weighted-bright/dark fixed-point classification, affine concurrence–rate rigidity or asymmetry-induced recovery of charging from a dark-only state. Earlier studies of collisional batteries and dark-state stabilization differ in charged object or environmental architecture. Searches of published mathematical records across source title, parameter substitutions, dark/bright equivalences, battery stabilization, concurrence and measurement outcomes found no stronger implication. Standard two-state Rabi reduction, pure-state concurrence and local measurement principles are used as established tools, not asserted as independent novelties. Residual risk is broader spin-charger work or an inaccessible companion preprint containing an implicit specialization; no exhaustive priority claim is made.

**Value — PASS.** The fully excited battery is an operationally motivated absorbing target for repeated charging. The theorem gives the *complete* stabilizing pure-charger family, not a single hand-picked example, and resolves a physically sharp singular switch at exact coupling symmetry. It quantitatively trades initial charger entanglement against the per-collision charging rate and shows how strictly unequal couplings enable an otherwise inert state, with deterministic trajectory-level mean energy. This answers protocol-design questions not settled by the equal-coupling model. The inequality is a strict exact law rather than a tiny numerical improvement.

**Scientific limits.** Exact for two charger qubits, identical charger gaps, exchange coupling, ideal resets and adaptive local measurements. It does not remove intrinsic quantum energy-measurement variance, quantify ergotropy, or include noise/cost. The related arXiv:2610.01332 and broad spin-charger literature have incomplete full-text coverage; the scientific priority assessment remains qualified.
