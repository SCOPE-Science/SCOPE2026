# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The parallel-arcs reduction uses arbitrary strictly positive edge resistances and produces the exact periodic metric. Four integrations by parts give the Fourier coefficients of the squared circle resistance, and the resulting sum-of-squares identity is strictly negative on every nonzero zero-sum finite coefficient vector by an invertible Vandermonde matrix. That verifies the distinction between embedding \(R\) and the classical embedding of \(\sqrt R\). On regular cycles, exact cyclic second-difference identities yield every nonconstant Gram eigenvalue, and elementary sine/Chebyshev products give the pseudodeterminant and simplex volume. The three-consecutive-vertex Hilbert triangle inequality proves the finite-bound and limit. The all-parameter proof is independent of finite numerical tests.

The 12 exact grounded-Laplacian reconstructions, Gram-minor positivity checks, seven rational volume identities, and 29 lengths of Fourier replay were executed from the standalone source code. The code is finite corroboration and does not by itself certify continuum strictness.

Direct and alias searches of the published mathematical record and deduplication against accessible earlier claims found no statement covering the combined strict \(2\)-negative type, exact Euclidean simplex spectrum/volume and finite-to-continuous exponent boundary.

The full text of one relevant circulant-resistance study could not be obtained from the available access routes; its abstract alone cannot exclude overlap. This was not treated as a proof of noncoverage.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Electrical effective resistance is ordinarily presented as a **squared** Hilbert distance, which does not imply that resistance itself is Euclidean. This theorem identifies a natural, all-weights graph family with the stronger property and exact full affine rank. The explicit simplex spectrum and volume provide natural, reusable Euclidean invariants. The sharp continuous exponent \(2\) is strict on each finite subset, whereas the finite-cycle supremal exponents are all above \(2\) and converge to it; this demonstrates a nontrivial boundary phenomenon in negative-type theory. A unit three-leaf star shows the all-weights cycle conclusion is not a generic resistance-graph fact. The exact formulas and geometric transition motivate the computation; this is not a generic exercise in substituting parameters into an existing formula.

## Remaining limitations

The finite-\(n\) maximal exponents for \(n\ge4\) are bounded, not exactly evaluated. The triangle bound is not optimal. The work is restricted to positive electrical cycles and the associated continuous resistance circle. A separately indexed statement involving Bernoulli kernels or cycle-resistance simplices could limit originality.
