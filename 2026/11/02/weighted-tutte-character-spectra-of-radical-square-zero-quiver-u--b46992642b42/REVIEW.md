# Scientific review of the weighted Tutte character-spectrum theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The hypotheses identify a square-zero radical and a split semisimple diagonal complement, so the unit group is a semidirect product of two finite abelian groups. The dual of the radical is correctly parametrized by one nontrivial finite-field additive character; unordered arrow classes force exactly equality of the corresponding diagonal torus coordinates, and algebra loops are fixed. The stabilizer has \((q-1)^{c(S)}\) elements, the orbit has \((q-1)^{r(S)}\), and the class of dual supports has \(q^L\prod_{e\in S}(q^{a_e}-1)\) elements. The invariant additive character extends over the inertia group, making all little-group constituents explicit and of degree \((q-1)^{r(S)}\). The displayed coefficients, binary-field boundary, forest and Tutte specializations, and character-degree-squared identity follow without omitted finite-field assumptions. Direct independent group-conjugacy computations confirm 19 cases, but do not replace the proof. The local script and exact command are included in the package.

## Originality — PASS relative to examined evidence
The most directly relevant known tools are Clifford's little-group theory and the standard Tutte subset identity. Halasi's 2006 full text (Theorems 1.2–1.3, Lemma 2.1) proves finite DN-algebra unit-group monomiality, possible character-degree shapes and the diagonal semidirect decomposition, but does not give exact multiplicities for every square-zero quiver and a weighted Tutte evaluation. The inspected 2019 André–Dias paper concerns smooth representations over non-Archimedean local fields and monomiality, not a finite-field full degree-enumerator specialization. Bilen Can (2021) analyzes incidence-monoid automorphisms and complexity, while Khrypchenko–Siciliano (2023) analyzes group identities and derived lengths. The closest accessible prior related work on the same radical-square-zero quiver algebras gives adjoint-rank distributions and commuting-element counts, not ordinary irreducible-character spectra of the unit group. Published results on character degrees of class-two graphical groups count alternating-matrix ranks for a different family and do not imply this torus-orbit polynomial. Exact formula, orientations, loops, multiplicities, all fields, and graph-polynomial equivalence were compared as implications, not titles alone. It remains possible that a general existing torus-by-abelian representation formula, when specialized, subsumes this statement; priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Complex irreducible-character degree multiplicities of finite unit groups are structural invariants relevant to finite algebra representation theory and character growth. The theorem determines the complete invariant for every finite radical-square-zero quiver, including arbitrary parallel/opposite arrows and loops, and yields orientation-independent weighted-graphic structure with a precise Tutte evaluation and closed forest factorization. This is a natural complete family-level classification rather than an arbitrary finite slice or a small numerical improvement. It is genuinely additional information to abelian radical structure, commutation probabilities, and ordinary group order, as evidenced by distinct nontrivial degree strata in the examples.

## Closest prior work and limitations
Halasi (2006) is a closely related finite-field induction result; it gives a broad structural premise but not the explicit quiver multiplicities. André–Dias, arXiv:1910.14639v1, is closest in later ambient unit-group representation theory; its result is a broad induction theorem in a different field setting. Bilen Can, arXiv:2105.09826v1, is closest for incidence-monoid torus structure, but studies geometric complexity rather than finite complex character degrees. Khrypchenko–Siciliano, arXiv:2302.01868v1, studies unit-group derived length. The standard Mackey/Clifford theory itself is prior and is a key premise. The new contribution is the complete weighted graph rank enumeration obtained by resolving torus orbit types and stabilizer multiplicities. No claim for radical cube or higher or explicit character values is made. The full 1996 Szegedy paper could not be read; its theorem quoted in Halasi (2006) gives monomiality and degree shapes, not multiplicities, but an additional implicit specialization in that paper remains a residual originality risk. More generally, a priori coverage by an uninspected character-enumeration theorem remains possible. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
