# Review of the wheel primitive-two-cycle theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The update is exactly \(c\mapsto c-Lf\). A two-step return forces equal firing totals per vertex by connectedness of the Laplacian; the primitive case forces one firing per vertex. Each phase cut determines necessary and sufficient independent chip-height intervals of length equal to the local cut degree. Fixing the unique hub-waiting phase counts each orbit exactly once. The four-state cyclic transfer is an exact finite-dimensional encoding of that weighted cut sum, and direct symbolic evaluation of \(\det(I-tT(z))\) yields the claimed recurrence and closed form. The original code paths in the package were executed, with explicit vertex-level exhaustive checks through six rim vertices and binary word/cut checks through fifteen. The proof, rather than these finite checks, establishes all rim sizes.

## Originality — PASS (bounded historical comparison)
Jiang (arXiv:1003.0943), Sections 1–2 and the inspected relevant statements in Sections 3–4, establishes general firing-count constraints and exact possible-period results for complete multipartite graphs, not the rim-cycle-plus-hub orbit census. Jiang–Scully–Zhang (arXiv:1211.6786), Sections 1–2 and 6–8, addresses allowable periodic firing patterns on arbitrary graphs, not the number of microscopic chip-height configurations of wheel two-cycles. A cut-based height count can be deduced from their general framework and is not itself claimed as novel. Evaluating the resulting weighted cut sum on all wheels gives a specific third-order recurrence, rational generating function, exact exponential base and parity correction not found in the checked primary texts. Previously recorded chip-firing results on cycles, open ladders, cliques, clique windmills and books concern distinct graph families. Searches of relevant research records and public literature did not locate a covering wheel-orbit formula, but lack of a hit does not establish exhaustive novelty. Bitar–Goles (1992) was identifiable by abstract and references but full text was not inspected; an unindexed equivalent is possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The primitive two-cycle orbit count is a natural discrete-dynamical invariant of a nontrivial hub-and-ring network. The result gives the exact labelled recurrent-state population (twice the orbit count), its characteristic exponential growth rate, and a detectable alternating finite-size term. These precise quantities are not implied by known possible-period bounds alone, distinguish spatial topology from cycle, clique and other solved families, and can be used as baseline quantities for further fixed-energy or attraction-basin calculations. No broader all-period classification is asserted.
