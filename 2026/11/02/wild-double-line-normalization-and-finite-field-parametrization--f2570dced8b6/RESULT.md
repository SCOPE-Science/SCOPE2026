# Wild double-line normalization and finite-field parametrization deficit of the Steiner Roman quartic

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\). Define the Steiner Roman quartic \(S\subset\mathbb P^3_k\), with coordinates \([W:X:Y:Z]\), by
\[
F=WXYZ+X^2Y^2+X^2Z^2+Y^2Z^2=0.
\]
The morphism
\[
\nu:\mathbb P^2_k\longrightarrow S,\qquad
[s:t:u]\longmapsto[s^2+t^2+u^2:tu:su:st]
\]
is the finite birational normalization. Its nonnormal locus is the reduced union \(D\) of the three coordinate lines \(X=Y=0\), \(X=Z=0\), and \(Y=Z=0\); this reduced subscheme is also the conductor subscheme. Its reduced inverse image is the coordinate triangle \(T=\{stu=0\}\subset\mathbb P^2\), and the scheme-theoretic inverse image of the conductor is that reduced triangle.

Each component of \(T\) maps to the corresponding component of \(D\) as a *separable* double cover with exactly one wildly ramified point, of ramification index \(2\) and different exponent \(2\). The scheme where the differential \(d\nu\) drops rank is the disjoint union of three length-two subschemes, supported at \([0:1:1]\), \([1:0:1]\), and \([1:1:0]\).

For every \(q=2^m\) with \(m\ge1\), with \(S\) and \(\nu\) defined over \(\mathbb F_q\),
\[
\#S(\mathbb F_q)=q^2+q+2,\qquad
\#\nu(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_q))=q^2-\frac q2+2.
\]
Precisely \(3q/2\) rational points of the *non-normal surface* fail to have rational preimages under the normalization, all on the three double lines. On the line \(Y=Z=0\), they are exactly the points \([w:1:0:0]\) with \(w\in\mathbb F_q^\times\) satisfying \(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_2}(1/w)=1\), and similarly for the other two lines.

## Assumptions and scope
All geometric normalization, conductor, and differential assertions hold over any algebraically closed field of characteristic two. The arithmetic counts and trace characterization hold over each finite field \(\mathbb F_{2^m}\), with the displayed split coordinate model and parametrization; they are not asserted for arbitrary twists of the surface. The differential rank-drop locus refers to the determinantal subscheme defined by all \(2\times2\) minors of \(d\nu:T_{\mathbb P^2}\to\nu^*T_{\mathbb P^3}\). The conductor is the annihilator of \(\nu_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}/\mathcal O_S\), not the potentially thicker Jacobian singularity scheme.

## Proof
**1. Image and normalization.** Direct substitution gives \(F\circ\nu=0\). The four quadratic coordinate sections have no common projective zero. Thus \(\nu\) is a morphism, and \(\nu^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^3}(1)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\) is ample, so \(\nu\) is finite onto its image. On \(XYZ\ne0\) it has inverse \([W:X:Y:Z]\mapsto[YZ:ZX:XY]\); hence its image is an irreducible surface, birational to \(\mathbb P^2\), of degree \(4\). Because the displayed degree-four polynomial vanishes on the image, its reduced hypersurface is exactly \(S\). As \(\mathbb P^2\) is normal, \(\nu\) is the normalization.

**2. Nonnormal double curve.** In characteristic two the four partial derivatives are \(F_W=XYZ\), \(F_X=WYZ\), \(F_Y=WXZ\), \(F_Z=WXY\). Together with \(F=0\), they show set-theoretically that the singular locus is exactly the union \(D\) of three coordinate lines. Their inverse image under \(\nu\) is \(T=\{stu=0\}\); off \(D\), the normalization is an isomorphism. The three preimages of the common point \([1:0:0:0]\) are the three coordinate vertices in \(\mathbb P^2\).

**3. Conductor, including the triple point.** On the affine chart \(X=1\), write surface coordinates \((w,y,z)=(W/X,Y/X,Z/X)\). On the normalization chart \(tu\ne0\), put \(a=s/u\) and \(r=t/u\). Then
\[
y=a/r,\quad z=a,\quad w=(a^2+r^2+1)/r.
\]
Writing \(B=k[y,z,w]/(y^2z^2+y^2+z^2+wyz)\) and \(A=k[a,r,r^{-1}]\), one has \(A=B[r]\): the identities \(ry=z\), \(r^2+wr+z^2+1=0\), and \(r^{-1}=r+w+yz\) give the claim. As a \(B\)-module, \(A/B\) is cyclic on the class of \(r\), killed by \(y,z\). There is no further annihilator because modulo \((y,z)\) one obtains \(k[r,r^{-1}]/k[w]\) with \(w=r+r^{-1}\), a free rank-one \(k[w]\)-module generated by the class of \(r\). Hence \(A/B\cong B/(y,z)\) and the conductor on this chart is exactly \((y,z)\). By symmetry this proves the assertion away from the common point of \(D\). At the common point the normalization has three distinct smooth branches, tangent respectively to \(X=0\), \(Y=0\), and \(Z=0\): on the source chart \(s=1\), for example, the pullbacks of \(Y/W\) and \(Z/W\) are \(u/(1+t^2+u^2)\) and \(t/(1+t^2+u^2)\), with independent differentials. The completed local germ is therefore an ordinary transverse triple crossing, formally equivalent to \(abc=0\). Its conductor is the reduced union of the pairwise branch intersections, proving that the global conductor ideal is \(\mathcal I_D\). The pullback of this ideal to \(\mathbb P^2\) defines \(T\) reduced (at a coordinate vertex, for example, its ideal is \((tu)\)).

**4. Wild double covers.** On the source line \(s=0\), the target line \(Y=Z=0\) is parametrized by
\[
[t:u]\longmapsto[W:X]=[t^2+u^2:tu].
\]
On \(tu\ne0\), put \(r=t/u\), \(w=W/X\). Then \(w=r+r^{-1}\) and the degree-two field extension satisfies \(r^2+wr+1=0\), whose derivative in \(r\) is the generically nonzero element \(w\). After setting \(v=(r+1)/w\), its Artin–Schreier normal form is
\[
v^2+v=1/w.
\]
The unique pole is simple at \(w=0\). Standard Artin–Schreier local ramification theory gives one ramification point of index \(2\), different exponent \(2\), and no other ramification. The point is \(r=1\), over \([W:X]=[0:1]\). Cyclic symmetry yields three such wild points.

**5. Differential scheme.** In the \(X=1\) chart, the three affine coordinate functions \((y,z,w)\) on the normalization are \((a/r,a,(a^2+r^2+1)/r)\). Their differential's \(2\times2\) minors, up to units \(r\), generate \((a,a^2+r^2+1)\), which in characteristic two equals \((a,(r+1)^2)\). Therefore the rank-drop scheme has length two at \((a,r)=(0,1)\). The inverse map on \(XYZ\ne0\) and the independent differentials at the three coordinate vertices exclude additional rank-drop points. Symmetry gives exactly three doubled points, total scheme length six.

**6. Finite-field counts and omitted points.** The triangle \(T\) comprises \(3q\) rational points, whereas the three concurrent lines \(D\) comprise \(3q+1\). Since \(\nu\) is an isomorphism between their complements,
\[
\#S(\mathbb F_q)=\bigl((q^2+q+1)-3q\bigr)+(3q+1)=q^2+q+2.
\]
For \(w\in\mathbb F_q^\times\), the equation \(r^2+wr+1=0\) has a rational root exactly when \(v^2+v=1/w\) does, equivalently when \(\operatorname{Tr}(1/w)=0\). Exactly \(q/2\) nonzero values \(w\) have trace one, and they each yield one missing rational point on each of the three lines. The point \(w=0\) is the unique ramification point, and the common triple point does have rational preimages. Thus precisely \(3q/2\) points have no rational preimage; subtracting from the first formula gives the second.

## Relationship to prior work
The quadratic parametrization and the three double lines of the Steiner Roman quartic are classical, including the equations and ordinary-characteristic six-pinch-point discussion in Coffman, Schwartz and Stanton. El Mazouni, Laytimi and Nagaraj discuss related complex Veronese projections and double curves but not the specified characteristic-two normalization or point-image counts. Kleiman's older discussion of enumerative singularities explicitly mentions nonreduced ramification phenomena for Steiner surfaces in characteristic two; **no novelty is claimed merely for the existence of that degeneration**. The additional combined claim is the exact three local Artin–Schreier covers, the conductor, the length-two scheme distribution, and the all-\(q\) arithmetic image/trace obstruction. A fully exhaustive historical priority claim is not made.

## Limitations
This concerns one specified split quartic model, not all Steiner projections or all finite-field twists. The finite-field image deficit counts rational points absent from this normalization parametrization; it does **not** assert that those points are absent from the surface. A relevant older treatment of Steiner ramification in characteristic two was found only through indexed excerpts, not completely inspected, leaving a priority risk for the geometric ramification description. An independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
