The stated split model and quantifiers were checked. Finite normalization follows from pullback of an ample line bundle and the explicit Cremona inverse on the torus; the equation has degree four. The conductor on a coordinate chart is determined by an explicit two-generator integral extension, with a local ordinary triple-crossing argument completing the global statement. The Artin–Schreier transform has one simple pole and hence one wild branch of different exponent two. The differential minors yield a length-two scheme at each of three points. Point counts follow by comparing the normalization triangle with its three concurrent image lines, and the missing-image condition follows from the finite-field absolute trace. The standalone script was run successfully.

## Originality
The earlier quadratic model and classical pinch-point picture are explicitly acknowledged. The additional all-field Artin–Schreier normalization, conductor, exact non-immersion scheme, and all-finite-field parametrization-image deficit were not found in the actual searches or inspected primary treatments. This is a qualified originality assessment, not a uniqueness guarantee; incomplete access to Kleiman's complete treatment and possible older finite-field parametrization studies remain risks.

## Mathematical value
The intrinsic value is an exact boundary phenomenon of a classical rational quartic: tame paired ramification merges into wild length-two degeneracies, and the same coverings quantify a failure of rational parametrization to cover rational points over every binary extension. The trace criterion resolves not just the cardinality but exactly which points are omitted. These geometric and arithmetic descriptions provide useful test cases for normalization, conductors, and finite-field parametric surfaces; they are not consequences of a mere point-count experiment.
