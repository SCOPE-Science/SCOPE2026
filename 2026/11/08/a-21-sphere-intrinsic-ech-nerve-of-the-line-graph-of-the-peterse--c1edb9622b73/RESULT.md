# A 21-sphere intrinsic Čech nerve of the line graph of the Petersen graph

## Finding

Let \(P=KG(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph and \(G=L(P)\) its line graph. Give every edge of \(G\) length one and take the nerve \(\mathcal N(G,r)\) of the closed metric balls of radius \(r/2\), centered at all 15 graph vertices, in the geometric realization of \(G\). Then for each real \(3\le r<4\),

\[\mathcal N(G,r)\simeq\bigvee^{21}S^3.\]

This is an integral, nonequivariant homotopy equivalence, not just a Betti-number calculation. The nerve is pure six-dimensional with 30 facets, each having seven vertices. Because it has nonzero reduced homology in degree three rather than degree six, it is not shellable as a pure simplicial complex.

## Assumptions and scope

Put \(U=\binom{\{0,1,2,3,4\}}2\). The vertices of \(P\) are the ten elements of \(U\); two are adjacent if and only if they are disjoint. Its 15 edges are the vertices of \(G\). Two vertices of \(G\) are adjacent exactly when their corresponding Petersen edges share an endpoint. Thus \(G\) has 15 vertices and 30 edges and is four-regular. All balls are intrinsic closed balls in the unit-edge realization, **not** Euclidean balls or balls restricted to the discrete vertex metric. The claim concerns the single scale interval \([3,4)\); it does not classify all persistence maps.

## Proof

**Step 1: Exact generating facets.** Denote the closed graph neighborhood of \(u\in V(G)\) by \(N_G[u]\). Lemma 2.2 of Adams–Shukla–Singh [2] says that at odd integer scale three the nerve is generated by

\[F_{uv}=N_G[u]\cup N_G[v],\qquad uv\in E(G).\]

Indeed, all relevant ball intersections can be witnessed at edge midpoints. The standalone checker also derives this from shortest-path distances, without relying on a purely vertex-metric replacement. In this graph every \(F_{uv}\) has seven elements, and the 30 sets are distinct. Consequently they are precisely the facets and the nerve is pure of dimension six. The same reference establishes \(\mathcal N(G,r)=\mathcal N(G,\lfloor r\rfloor)\) for real nonnegative \(r\), so it suffices to analyze the integer scale three.

**Step 2: Exhaustive face reconstruction.** Number the ten Petersen vertices lexicographically as two-subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\); number the 15 Petersen edges lexicographically by their endpoint indices. These latter indices number the vertices of \(G\). Represent a simplex by the 15-bit integer whose set bits are its vertices. The verifier constructs all subsets of the 30 seven-vertex sets \(F_{uv}\), and separately tests every one of the \(2^{15}=32768\) subsets against all vertex and edge-midpoint witnesses obtained using a fresh breadth-first-distance matrix. The sets agree and contain 2141 faces counting the empty face. The face vector in dimensions zero through six is

\[(15,105,430,765,585,210,30).\]

**Step 3: Integral discrete-Morse reduction.** Work on the poset of nonempty simplices, ordered by inclusion. Initially mark all faces available. At each stage, if there is a face with exactly one available immediate coface, pair it with that coface and remove both; otherwise mark an available inclusion-maximal face critical and remove it. For reproducibility choose a free face of greatest cardinality, and break ties by increasing integer mask; choose a critical maximal face of greatest cardinality with the same tie order. This is the finite deterministic procedure implemented in `verify.py`, with a second execution using the opposite integer-mask tie order. It is valid because deleting a critical facet and deleting a free codimension-one pair both leave a downward-closed available face family. The checker additionally constructs **all** original Hasse incidences and reverses each matched arrow; a full topological sort proves the resulting matching acyclic, independently of the greedy argument.

Each of the two tie orders gives exactly 1059 matched pairs and critical-cell counts of one zero-cell and 21 three-cells, with no other critical cells. The directed-acyclicity check covers all 8955 nonempty Hasse incidences. Since Forman's discrete Morse theorem gives a CW model with one zero-cell and 21 three-cells and no one- or two-cells, every three-cell is attached by the unique map from a two-sphere to a point. The resulting CW complex is exactly a wedge of 21 three-spheres. The theorem is therefore an **integral homotopy classification**, not an inference from finite-field ranks.

**Step 4: Independent homological check and consequence.** A separate exact elimination of the ordinary simplicial boundary matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\) gives ranks \((14,91,339,405,180,30)\) in dimensions one through six. They imply only \(\widetilde H_3\) is nonzero, of rank 21 over \(\mathbb F_2\), corroborating the integral Morse proof. A pure shellable six-complex has reduced homology supported only in its top dimension, so the displayed nonzero third homology prevents shellability.

The program reconstructs the Petersen and line graphs, enumerates every subset, generates both deterministic Morse matchings, checks every face incidence and directed acyclicity, and independently eliminates all mod-two boundary matrices. Its expected JSON result is supplied in `verification_output.json`. Reproducing the output verifies the **finite** graph-specific combinatorial certificates. The mathematical implication from the acyclic matching to the homotopy type uses the standard discrete Morse theorem; neither a finite-field rank computation nor an enumeration alone is claimed to establish an integral homotopy equivalence.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams–Shukla–Singh [2, Lemma 2.2] give the general intrinsic Čech nerve's even/odd generating-set rule. Their homotopy computations concern hypercube graphs, not the line graph of the Petersen graph. Goyal–Shukla–Singh [1, Sections 3–4] calculate clique complexes of line graphs; their result for a triangle-free base graph gives a circle wedge for the **different** clique complex \(\operatorname{Cl}(L(P))\), not this scale-three Čech nerve.

The published result [3] proves \(\mathcal N(L(H),2)\searrow\operatorname{Cl}(L(H))\) when \(H\) has girth at least five; it applies to Petersen at **scale two only**, and explicitly does not settle higher scales. The scale-three theorem [4] concerns the line graph of the **once-subdivided** graph \(L(S(H))\), which for Petersen has 30 vertices; our 15-vertex object \(L(P)\) is not that graph. The ordinary open-neighborhood complex of a line graph, treated in other graph-topology calculations, is also a distinct construction. Targeted research-record, literature, and accessible historical-claim comparisons found no statement implying this 21-sphere homotopy classification. That negative result is not a proof of novelty; see the limitations.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns exactly \(G=L(KG(5,2))\) and the scale interval \([3,4)\). No classification of arbitrary line graphs, other scales, equivariant homotopy, or persistence maps is claimed. The discrete-Morse certificate is reproducible finite computation for this graph; its validity is contingent on the specified graph model and the executable algorithm. Equivalent formulations in unindexed or inaccessible literature remain a residual originality risk. No independent external audit has been performed.
