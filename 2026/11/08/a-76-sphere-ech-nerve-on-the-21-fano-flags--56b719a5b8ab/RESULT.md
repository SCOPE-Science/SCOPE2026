# A 76-sphere Čech nerve on the 21 Fano flags

## Finding

Let \(H\) be the Heawood graph, presented as the incidence graph of the Fano projective plane, and let \(L(H)\) be its 21-vertex line graph. Give each graph edge unit length and equip the geometric realization of \(L(H)\) with its intrinsic path metric. Write \(\mathcal N(L(H),r)\) for the nerve of the closed metric balls of radius \(r/2\) centered at the vertices of \(L(H)\). Then the **integral homotopy type** at the first odd scale after the low-scale graph recovery is

\[
\mathcal N(L(H),r)\simeq\bigvee^{76}S^2\qquad(3\le r<4).
\]

The complex is pure of dimension six, with 42 facets on seven vertices apiece, but it has reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z^{76}\) in dimension two only. Consequently it is not shellable.

## Assumptions and scope

Index Fano points and lines by \(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), with line \(\ell_j=\{j,j+1,j+3\}\) modulo seven. The graph \(H\) has one edge for each incidence \(i\in\ell_j\). Thus the 21 flags \((i,j)\) with \(i\in\ell_j\) are vertices of \(L(H)\); two distinct flags are adjacent precisely when they share a point or a line. There are 42 such line-graph edges, and each line-graph vertex has degree four. This fixes the exact object without reliance on graph-isomorphism software.

The metric is defined on the continuous unit-edge graph, *not* only the discrete vertex set, and every ball is closed. The scale \(r\) in the theorem is real, and the two endpoints of the displayed interval follow the inclusive closed-ball convention. The conclusion is a nonequivariant homotopy statement; no automorphism representation or general theorem for arbitrary incidence graphs is claimed.

## Proof

**Step 1 (exact Čech facets).** For a finite simple unit-edge graph \(G\), the vertex-centered intrinsic Čech nerve at integer scale three is generated by \(N_G[u]\cup N_G[v]\) as \(uv\) ranges over its edges, where \(N_G[u]\) is the closed one-neighborhood. This is Lemma 2.2 of Adams–Shukla–Singh [1]. Indeed, an intersection of closed radius-\(3/2\) balls can be witnessed at an edge midpoint, and a center is within radius \(3/2\) of that midpoint exactly when it lies in the closed neighborhood of an endpoint. It is therefore sufficient to work with these finite generating sets.

For \(G=L(H)\), each of the 42 sets \(N_G[u]\cup N_G[v]\) has exactly seven vertices. They are distinct, so each is a maximal six-simplex. All other faces are their subsets. The standalone certificate enumerates all \(2^{21}-1\) nonempty vertex subsets independently of subset generation and finds exactly 3,395 simplices, with dimension-wise face vector

\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6)=(21,210,854,1155,819,294,42).
\]

The Euler characteristic is therefore \(77\).

**Step 2 (fully checked acyclic discrete-Morse matching).** Label the flags lexicographically as in the verifier and represent a simplex by its 21-bit characteristic mask. Beginning with all nonempty faces, visit the 21 vertices in ascending order. At each vertex \(v\), pair every still-unmatched face \(\sigma\) avoiding \(v\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\), when the latter is still unmatched. Each pair is an actual cover relation in the nonempty-face poset. This deterministic recipe creates 1,659 distinct matched pairs and leaves exactly one critical vertex and 76 critical two-simplices, with no other critical cells.

For the directed Hasse graph, orient each unmatched face/coface incidence downward and each matched incidence upward. The verifier constructs all 13,755 incidences and explicitly topologically sorts the directed graph, visiting all 3,395 nodes. Hence there is no directed cycle, so the matching is acyclic. Reversing the vertex-processing order independently gives the same critical-cell distribution and also passes the full directed-cycle test. Both computations are reproduced entirely in the attached standard-library Python program; no unverifiable finite-experiment-to-infinite inference is used.

**Step 3 (homotopy, not only homology).** By discrete Morse theory, the complex is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with one zero-cell and 76 two-cells, and no one-cells or other cells. Every two-cell has its attaching map from \(S^1\) into a point. The resulting CW complex is therefore exactly a wedge of 76 two-spheres. This establishes the integral homotopy claim. The separate mod-two boundary calculation gives ranks \((20,190,588,567,252,42)\) in dimensions one through six, and independently corroborates the sole reduced Betti number 76.

For \(3\le r<4\), the Čech nerve is constant by the integer-threshold property [1, §2], proving the full stated interval.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_cech.py` from the unpacked files. The checker constructs the Fano line-incidence graph directly, enumerates all faces both by generators and by all \(2^{21}\) masks, computes all boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\), constructs two complete discrete-Morse matchings, and tests the acyclicity of each against the full oriented Hasse graph. Its reproducible output is `verification_output.json` and ends with the marker `HEAWOOD_LINE_CECH3_WEDGE76_VERIFIED`.

The neighboring scale-two complex is also checked: a separate acyclic matching leaves one vertex and eight one-cells, i.e. a wedge of eight circles. The theorem does not claim to determine all persistence maps.

## Relationship to prior work

Goyal–Shukla–Singh [2, Lemma 3.2 and Corollary 3.3] determine the clique complex of a line graph, which recovers the homotopy of a triangle-free base graph. Its clique-complex construction is not the scale-three Čech nerve. Adams–Shukla–Singh [1] provide the intrinsic graph Čech definition and facet rule; their classified examples are hypercube graphs, not the 21-vertex line graph of the Fano incidence graph. Relevant indexed results about scale-two Čech complexes of line graphs and about Čech nerves of the Heawood graph itself have different scales or vertex sets. None of these checked statements implies the present 76-sphere classification. A search cannot exclude all unindexed or inaccessible equivalent work.

## Limitations

This result is for the one specified 21-vertex graph at one real-scale interval. It does not classify other line graphs, characterize the automorphism action on \(H_2\), or compute integer persistence maps between adjacent scales. The explicit finite certificate proves the homotopy for this finite object and must not be extrapolated to a general family without further argument. An equivalent unpublished or inaccessible calculation remains possible.
