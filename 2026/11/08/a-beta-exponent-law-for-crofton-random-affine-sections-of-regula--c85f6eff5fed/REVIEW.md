# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The invariant measure on hyperplanes gives width-weighted normal directions. Independent Gaussian polar decomposition transfers expected ordered projection gaps to independent normal sample spacings. The exact binomial integral, crossed-edge simplex combinatorics, and logarithmic beta limit follow by explicit calculations and normal tail estimates; the proof checks hypotheses, range \(n\ge4\), and both endpoints of the limiting CDF. The finite independent checker verifies selected dimensions and provides corroboration only, not infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS with specified priority risk.** Full accessible Dirksen (2015), Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets (2015), and De Loera–Lopez-Campos–Torres (2024) texts do not imply the affine Crofton order-spacing law or beta exponent. Two published central-simplex results treat hyperplanes through the centroid, giving a distinct Gaussian-sign and chi-square regime. The published \(n=4\) regular-tetrahedron Crofton quadrilateral probability is a covered marginal and is not the novelty. Baryshnikov–Vitale (1994) is closely relevant for projection/Gaussian equivalence, but its primary full text could not be inspected: an older implicit or explicit version remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The all-dimensional exact section-combinatorial probability law and its nondegenerate polynomial-growth exponent answer a natural integral-geometric question. The limit \(\operatorname{Beta}(1,1/2)\) distinguishes random translated sections from almost-maximal centroidal slices and controls the probability of every intermediate polynomial complexity threshold. This is a structural asymptotic theorem, not a table recomputation.

The theorem excludes nonregular simplices, centroidal planes and normal-by-normal uniform-offset sampling. It establishes no rate of convergence or external certification.
