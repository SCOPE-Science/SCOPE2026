The known two-sided Mallows displacement law has an exact bilateral Euler-product generating function, and hence a representation as a difference of independent geometric sums. Its parity product proves \(c_o(Q)>\tfrac12>c_e(Q)\) for every \(Q>1\), with strict increase of \(c_o\) and decrease of \(c_e\), settling the fixed-point parity questions raised in the cycle-limit literature.
