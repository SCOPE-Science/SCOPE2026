# A binary-defect census of minimum zero forcing sets on even helm graphs

## Finding
For each integer \(k\ge3\), the even helm \(H_{2k}\) has exactly \(6\) minimum standard zero forcing sets if \(k=3\), and exactly \(k^2 2^{k-4}\) such sets if \(k\ge4\). Every one of these sets consists solely of leaves. The token-slide reconfiguration graph on the minimum sets, in which a token can move along one host-graph edge while preserving the standard zero forcing property and cardinality, is therefore edgeless.

There is also an explicit complete characterization of all these minimum sets: encode a selection of \(k\) leaves by a cyclic binary word \(w\) of length \(2k\). For each cycle edge connecting equal symbols, record a defect of type \(00\) or \(11\). In cyclic order, the defect types must alternate except for exactly one doubled \(00\) and exactly one doubled \(11\); each double must consist of physically consecutive cycle edges, giving a single run \(000\) and a single run \(111\). These conditions are necessary and sufficient.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, undirected and simple. Write \(n=2k\). The helm \(H_n\) has vertices \(c\), rim vertices \(r_i\), and leaves \(p_i\), indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Its edges are \(cr_i\), \(r_ir_{i+1}\), and \(r_ip_i\), for all \(i\). Standard zero forcing means that an initially blue set eventually makes every vertex blue by repeating the rule: a blue vertex with exactly one white neighbor may turn that neighbor blue. We consider only minimum-cardinality initial sets. The statement starts at \(n=6\); in particular it makes no claim that \(H_4\) has zero forcing number two.

For a binary word \(w\) on the rim index set, a *defect edge* at \(i\) is one satisfying \(w_i=w_{i+1}\). It is labeled by that common bit. List all defect edges around the cycle, omitting the intervening unequal-bit edges. Two consecutive entries in this list can be of equal type without the corresponding physical edges being adjacent: the criterion specifically demands physical adjacency for the two equal-type pairs. The number of 00 defects equals the number of 11 defects for any balanced cyclic binary word.

## Proof
**1. The minimum size and leaf rigidity.** Every zero forcing chronology decomposes into vertex-disjoint directed forcing chains, each beginning at an initial blue vertex and ending at a vertex that performs no force. A vertex can force at most once. Each path in a chain contains at most two distinct leaves; hence the \(2k\) leaves of \(H_{2k}\) require at least \(k\) chains, and thus at least \(k\) initial vertices. Equality forces every chain to contain exactly two leaves, one at each endpoint. Every initial vertex is therefore a leaf, every selected leaf forces its adjacent rim vertex, and each chain ends at a different initially white leaf. We will construct successful balanced selections below, proving that the lower bound is attained.

Since every minimum set is leaf-only and every leaf's only neighbor is an internal rim vertex, replacing a selected leaf by a neighbor cannot produce another minimum set. This proves the edgeless token-slide assertion, conditional on attaining \(k\).

**2. The hub and the unique 111.** Initially every selected leaf forces its rim neighbor, so just the rim vertices indexed 1 are blue, in addition to the selected leaves. Before the hub becomes blue no selected rim vertex can force a white rim vertex: it would have both the hub and that white rim vertex as white neighbors. Thus the first subsequent force, if any, must force the hub from the middle rim vertex of three consecutive selected indices. Therefore \(111\) occurs. For each such triple, its middle rim vertex has all neighbors except the hub already blue after the leaf forces, so it can perform no force except possibly to the hub. But every selected leaf must lie on a chain ending at a different unselected leaf; hence each selected rim must perform a force. Only one rim can force the hub. There is consequently exactly one occurrence of \(111\), and a longer 1-run is impossible. In any successful process, the hub must subsequently force a rim vertex: otherwise its forcing chain terminates at the hub rather than at a leaf. The hub can force a rim only when precisely one white rim remains.

**3. A forcing criterion on the zero indices.** Once the hub is blue, restrict attention to unselected indices \(U=\{i:w_i=0\}\). A newly blue rim vertex at a zero index has its pendant leaf still white. It cannot force another rim vertex: while some rim neighbor is white it has at least two white neighbors, the leaf and that rim vertex. Only original 1-index rim vertices can force zero-index rim vertices. A zero index is immediately forceable by a neighboring 1-index rim vertex precisely if the latter has its other rim neighbor also initially selected; in binary notation this is a zero adjacent to \(11\). Call such a zero *seeded*. If \(i,j\in U\) are separated by a single 1 (a \(010\) pattern), forcing one of these two zero rims may allow the intervening selected rim to force the other zero. These are all possible rim-to-rim dependencies. Thus form an auxiliary graph on \(U\), joining two zero indices exactly when they are separated around the cycle by one 1. A component of this graph becomes blue without hub assistance if and only if it contains a seeded zero. The propagation in that component is simply along its auxiliary edges.

The components are consecutive runs of zeros joined across isolated 1s. Each component lies between two consecutive defect edges in the cyclic defect list (or between matching boundary defects in the obvious degenerate case). A component has no seeded zero exactly when both its boundary defects are of type \(00\). In particular, every adjacent pair of 00 defects in the cyclic *defect list* accounts for a distinct unseeded component. If the two physical defect edges are adjacent (a \(000\) run), that component contains exactly the middle zero; otherwise the component contains at least two zero indices. Because the hub can force only one remaining rim vertex, a successful configuration has exactly one unseeded zero component, and that component is one vertex. The preceding chain argument ensures that the hub must actually perform this last rim force.

Combining this with the unique physical 111 pair from Step 2 yields precisely the asserted defect condition. Conversely, suppose a balanced word satisfies that condition. Its physical 111 forces the hub. Every zero component other than the one central zero of the physical 000 is seeded and therefore becomes blue through the rim-to-rim dependencies. The hub then forces the unique remaining white rim. Every still-white pendant leaf is now the only white neighbor of its rim vertex, so all of them are forced. Thus the word is a minimum zero forcing configuration. In particular, the explicit word \(000(10)^{k-3}111\), read cyclically, is successful for all \(k\ge3\), completing Step 1.

**4. Exact enumeration.** Let \(r\) be the common number of 00 and 11 defect edges. The criterion requires \(r\ge2\). Mark the first physical edge of the unique double-11 defect, which can occupy any of the \(2k\) edge positions. Starting with the prescribed pair 11, the remaining defect types alternate except for one adjacent 00 pair. There are exactly \(r-1\) positions in the remaining alternating defect sequence at which to place this double-00 pair. Of the \(2r\) consecutive defect gaps, two are the forced physical adjacencies (intervening unequal-bit edge count zero), while the other \(2r-2\) connect opposite defect types and each must contain an odd number \(1+2a_j\) of unequal-bit edges, where \(a_j\ge0\). Since the total cycle length is \(2k\),
\[
2k=2r+(2r-2)+2\sum_{j=1}^{2r-2} a_j,
\qquad \sum_j a_j=k-2r+1.
\]
By stars and bars, the number of gap choices for each defect order is \(\binom{k-2}{2r-3}\) (zero when the lower argument is out of range). Fixing the marked 11 edge, defect order and gaps determines exactly one labeled cyclic binary word. Every successful word has exactly one marked 11 pair, so no overcount occurs. Consequently
\[
N_k=2k\sum_{r\ge2}(r-1)\binom{k-2}{2r-3}.
\]
For \(k=3\), only \(r=2\) contributes, so \(N_3=6\). For \(k\ge4\), put \(m=k-2\) and \(j=2r-3\). The standard odd-index binomial identities \(\sum_{j\text{ odd}}\binom mj=2^{m-1}\) and \(\sum_{j\text{ odd}}j\binom mj=m2^{m-2}\) give
\[
N_k=2k\sum_{j\ge1\text{ odd}}\frac{j+1}{2}\binom{k-2}{j}=k^2 2^{k-4}.
\]
All claims follow.

## Verification
The included `check_python.py` and `check_cpp.cpp` implement the standard forcing closure independently and also implement the defect condition independently. Both enumerate all balanced pendant selections and compare the mathematical criterion with forcing success for \(n=6,8,10,12,14,16,18,20\). Both recover the count sequence \(6,16,50,144,392,1024,2592,6400\). The Python checker additionally exhausts *all* smaller initial vertex subsets for \(n=6,8,10\), confirming the minimum sizes in those cases. Reproduce with `python3 check_python.py` and `c++ -std=c++17 -O2 check_cpp.cpp -o check_cpp && ./check_cpp`. These finite computations check boundaries and implementation, not the infinite theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Barioli et al. introduced forcing-chain/path-cover arguments and proved the general bound between zero forcing and path covers (arXiv:1003.2028v1, 2010). Anderton et al. (arXiv:2310.18513v1, 2023) already proved the zero forcing number of helm graphs, and established the necessity of a unique run of three chosen consecutive pendant vertices for even helms. These previously published facts are not claimed as new. Geneson, Haas and Hogben (arXiv:2009.00220v1, 2020) treat *unrestricted token jumping* on minimum zero forcing sets, a different move relation. The contribution here is the complete binary-defect classification, the exact number of minimum configurations, and the resulting fixed-host slide-graph order. The slide-isolation implication itself follows quickly from known chain ideas and is not presented as an independent major novelty result.

## Limitations
The formula applies only to even helms with at least six rim vertices, with standard undirected zero forcing and minimum-cardinality initial sets. It does not give the general zero forcing polynomial, counts of nonminimum forcing sets, token-jump distances, or results for odd helms. Although relevant literature and mathematical-record searches did not disclose this exact census, independently unindexed or unpublished equivalents may exist. No independent scholarly audit is claimed.
