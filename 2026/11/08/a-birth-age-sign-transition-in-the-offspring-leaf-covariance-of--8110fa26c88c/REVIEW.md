# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The final claim treats uniform parent choices, nonroot offspring counts and outdegree-zero leaves, for every integer \(n\ge3\) and label \(2\le i\le n\). The leaf survival telescoping product is \((i-1)/(n-1)\); conditioning on one future attachment produces the local covariance kernel \([1/2-(i-1)/(j-2)]/(n-1)\). Summing gives the exact harmonic-number formula. Strict discrete convexity follows from a positive second difference \(1/i\). The finite transition follows from endpoint signs and the first-term threshold at \(n=7\); the asymptotic threshold from a strictly convex continuum profile. The variance identities give the normalized correlation limit. An exact checker verifies all formulas by enumerating parent vectors through eight vertices, and it checks further finite shape identities. The checker is not a substitute for the proof.

Primary-source comparisons include Janson's full mathematical statements about global counts by degree (not fixed labels), the full arXiv HTML of Desmarais–Holmgren–Wagner concerning two-colour leaf and fringe counts, and Saunders' arXiv HTML on expected leaf and root-degree counts in a deletion model. ## Value — PASS

The global leaf count is a classical random-growth observable, whereas a specified vertex's offspring count captures local attachment history. The result completely classifies how those two observables interact across every nonroot birth rank, identifies an exactly localized sign reversal at attachment time \(j=2i\), and establishes a sharp macroscopic age threshold near \(0.284668\). The order-seven first onset and positive second differences are structural, not arbitrary small-case statistics. The effect has vanishing correlation at proportional ages, which is stated rather than concealed.
