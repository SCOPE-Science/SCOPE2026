# A canonical K3 carpet as the Fano-vertex tangent cone of a rank-three quadric

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic, and let
\[
Q=V(x_0x_2-x_1^2)\subset\mathbb P^4_{[x_0:x_1:x_2:s:t]}.
\]
The rank-three quadric threefold has vertex line \(\Lambda=V(x_0,x_1,x_2)\). Let \(F_1(Q)\) be the full Grassmannian Fano scheme of lines on \(Q\), and let \(o=[\Lambda]\). Then the projectivized tangent cone to \(F_1(Q)\) at \(o\) is an embedded canonical K3 carpet \(Y\subset\mathbb P^5\), supported on the balanced degree-four rational normal scroll \(S(2,2)\). Explicitly,
\[
Y=V(a_0a_2-a_1^2,\ b_0b_2-b_1^2,\ a_0b_2+a_2b_0-2a_1b_1)\subset\mathbb P^5.
\]
Its nilradical \(\mathcal N\) is square-zero and obeys
\[
\mathcal N\cong\omega_{S(2,2)}\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1}(-2,-2).
\]
The scheme \(Y\) is a complete intersection of three quadrics, with
\[
\omega_Y\cong\mathcal O_Y,\quad h^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=0,\quad \deg Y=8,\quad P_Y(n)=4n^2+2.
\]
Consequently \(F_1(Q)\) has dimension three, embedding dimension six and Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity exactly eight at \(o\). These assertions hold also in characteristic two.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is algebraically closed in any characteristic. A rank-three quadric means a projectively equivalent hypersurface with equation \(x_0x_2-x_1^2=0\); this equation defines a smooth plane conic in the three transverse coordinates in every characteristic. The vertex is the *linear* singular support \(\Lambda\), not necessarily the scheme-theoretic singular locus in characteristic two. The tangent cone is taken with respect to the maximal ideal at the Grassmannian point \(o\). The statement does not describe the whole Fano scheme or the tangent cones at other lines. The name “K3 carpet” refers to the established scheme-theoretic definition (a square-zero double structure on a rational normal scroll with trivial dualizing sheaf and \(h^1=0\)); smooth K3 surfaces are not claimed as fibers of the Fano scheme.

## Proof
**1. Exact Fano chart.** In the affine chart of \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\) parametrizing two-dimensional vector subspaces projecting isomorphically to the \((s,t)\)-plane, every line is spanned by the vectors
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2,1,0),\qquad(b_0,b_1,b_2,0,1).
\]
Restricting \(x_0x_2-x_1^2\) to their span yields the binary quadratic
\[
(a_0a_2-a_1^2)s^2+(a_0b_2+a_2b_0-2a_1b_1)st+(b_0b_2-b_1^2)t^2.
\]
Coefficientwise vanishing defines the *scheme-theoretic* Fano chart. Its six variables form the maximal ideal at \(o\), and all three equations are homogeneous of degree two. Hence its associated graded ring equals the chart's entire graded ring
\[
A=k[a_0,a_1,a_2,b_0,b_1,b_2]/(q_a,q_{ab},q_b),
\]
and the projectivized tangent cone is \(Y=\operatorname{Proj}A\). No characteristic-dependent division is used.

**2. Reduced support and complete intersection.** A \(k\)-point of \(Y\) is a pair of triples \(a,b\), not both zero, such that the binary quadratic form \(q(\lambda a+\mu b)\) vanishes identically. Because the smooth conic \(q=0\subset\mathbb P^2\) contains no projective line, the two triples are proportional whenever both are nonzero. This remains true in characteristic two, by the coefficientwise condition. Thus the reduced support is precisely the image
\[
([u:v],[r:w])\longmapsto
[ur^2:urw:uw^2:vr^2:vrw:vw^2].
\]
It is the Segre–Veronese embedded \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) via \(\mathcal O(1,2)\), namely \(S(2,2)\), of dimension two and degree four. The ideal \(I=(q_a,q_{ab},q_b)\) therefore has height three in the six-variable polynomial ring. Since it is generated by three elements of this height in a regular ring, these elements form a regular sequence. Consequently \(A\) is a three-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay Gorenstein graded complete intersection, with one minimal prime, and \(Y\) has degree eight.

**3. Exact doubled chart.** Inverting \(a_0\), set \(r=a_1/a_0\) and \(\varepsilon=b_1-rb_0\). Solving the first and middle quadratic equations gives
\[
a_2=a_0r^2,\qquad b_2=r^2b_0+2r\varepsilon.
\]
The last quadratic equation becomes exactly \(-\varepsilon^2=0\). Thus
\[
A[a_0^{-1}]\cong k[a_0^{\pm1},r,b_0,\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]
The analogous calculation holds on \(D(a_2)\), \(D(b_0)\), and \(D(b_2)\) after exchanging the two triples or reversing their coordinates. These four opens cover the reduced projective scroll. Every local ring of \(Y\) is therefore a locally free rank-two infinitesimal double structure over its reduction. In particular its nilradical sheaf \(\mathcal N\) is invertible and squares to zero. Algebraically one can also note that, as \(A\) is Cohen–Macaulay with a unique minimal prime, the nonzero divisor \(a_0\) acts injectively on \(A\); the square-zero assertion on \(D(a_0)\) therefore forces the radical ideal's square to vanish globally.

**4. Canonical nilradical and K3 properties.** The complete-intersection adjunction formula in \(\mathbb P^5\) gives \(\omega_Y\simeq\mathcal O_Y(2+2+2-6)=\mathcal O_Y\). Write \(S=Y_{\mathrm{red}}\). Since \(Y\) and \(S\) are equidimensional Cohen–Macaulay and \(S\subset Y\) is finite, duality gives
\[
\omega_S\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_Y}(\mathcal O_S,\omega_Y)
\cong\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_Y}(\mathcal N).
\]
On the exact doubled chart, \(\operatorname{Ann}(\varepsilon)=(\varepsilon)\); this equality glues and implies \(\operatorname{Ann}(\mathcal N)=\mathcal N\). Thus \(\mathcal N\cong\omega_S\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1}(-2,-2)\). From
\[
0\longrightarrow\omega_S\longrightarrow\mathcal O_Y\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S\longrightarrow0
\]
and \(H^1(S,\omega_S)=H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\), one obtains \(H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=0\). Therefore \(Y\) is a K3 carpet in the usual sense.

**5. Hilbert invariants and local multiplicity.** Because the homogeneous ideal is a quadratic regular sequence,
\[
H_A(z)=\frac{(1-z^2)^3}{(1-z)^6}=\frac{(1+z)^3}{(1-z)^3}.
\]
The coefficient in degree \(n\ge2\) is \(4n^2+2\), so \(P_Y(n)=4n^2+2\) and \(\deg Y=8\). This is also the degree of the tangent cone and hence the Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity \(e(\mathcal O_{F_1(Q),o})=8\). The absence of linear Fano-chart equations gives embedding dimension six, and the regular-sequence presentation gives local dimension three. For the global dimension, any line on \(Q\) different from \(\Lambda\) projects from the vertex to a single point of the smooth conic, since its projective-linear image cannot be a line contained in that conic. It therefore lies in the plane spanned by the vertex line and that conic point. Choosing the conic point contributes one parameter and choosing a line in the corresponding plane contributes two; thus the full Fano scheme also has dimension three.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verification/verify.py` checks the universal restricted-quadric identity, the exact local elimination yielding \(-\varepsilon^2\), and pairwise square-zero products of the six scroll minors modulo the three defining equations in characteristics zero, two, three, five and seven. It also checks Hilbert-function agreement through degree twelve. Its recorded output is `verification/verification_output.txt`, ending with `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED`. The general theorem follows from the preceding scheme-theoretic argument, not from checking finitely many characteristics. The program requires Python 3 and SymPy.

## Relationship to prior work
Gallego and Purnaprajna defined K3 carpets on rational normal scrolls and proved uniqueness in characteristic zero, along with Hilbert-theoretic properties. The existence, general equations and properties of the degree-four K3 carpet are **not** new. The K3Carpets computational documentation additionally describes general carpet equations as conic scroll minors with mixed relations. In the special balanced case, those relations agree with the three explicit quadrics above.

Ilten and Zotine's work on toric Fano schemes gives the structure of their reduced components and complete nonreduced schemes for spaces of dimension one less than the ambient variety. For lines on a threefold the latter nonreduced-structure theorem does not apply; their introduction and Remark 9.9 note the difficulty of this other range. The *connection established here* is the exact occurrence of the classical K3 carpet as the projectivized tangent cone of the line Fano scheme at the vertex line of the rank-three quadric, with the induced multiplicity-eight singularity. These cited works do not themselves state this Fano-vertex identification. Similarity of the known equations limits the strength of any historical-priority claim, which remains provisional.

## Limitations
The assertion is local at one specified Fano point. The scroll and K3 carpet were known before this calculation; novelty is claimed only for their identification with this specific Fano tangent cone and the resulting local singularity invariant. Exact overlap was checked against accessible relevant literature, but unindexed historical literature could imply the identification. No independent scientific audit is claimed.
