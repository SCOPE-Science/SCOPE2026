# A Cayley sector and an irregular sector in Nauru edge-colouring reconfiguration

## Finding
Let \(N=GP(12,5)\) be the 24-vertex Nauru graph. Its named proper three-edge-colourings form a finite graph \(\mathcal K(N)\) under single alternating-cycle edge-Kempe switches. The graph has exactly 504 vertices and 1,674 edges. It has precisely two components, \(\mathcal C\) of order 144 and \(\mathcal I\) of order 360.

The following cycle-type criterion identifies the components intrinsically. For a colouring \(c\), consider the three bichromatic spanning two-factors. Then \(c\in\mathcal C\) if and only if *one* of them has a ten-cycle, which is equivalent in this graph to *all three* consisting of a ten-cycle and a fourteen-cycle. No bichromatic two-factor of a colouring in \(\mathcal I\) has a ten-cycle.

The 144-vertex component \(\mathcal C\) is a bipartite six-regular Cayley graph of a group of order 144, generated by six involutions. It is self-centered of radius and diameter four, with every vertex having distance-sphere vector \((1,6,27,66,44)\). Its 432 edges and unordered shortest-distance multiplicities \((432,1944,4752,3168)\) correspond to distances one through four.

The other component \(\mathcal I\) has 1,242 edges, degrees six, eight, and twelve with multiplicities 210, 144, and six, and odd girth five. It has radius five and diameter six; its eccentricity-five and eccentricity-six multiplicities are 330 and 30. The numbers of unordered vertex pairs at distances one through six are exactly \((1242,5904,18981,31401,6732,360)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has vertices \(u_i,v_i\) indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\) and edges \(\{u_i,u_{i+1}\}\), \(\{u_i,v_i\}\), and \(\{v_i,v_{i+5}\}\), with distinct undirected edges taken once. For the verification programs, \(u_i=i\) and \(v_i=12+i\). A state assigns each of the 36 edges one of the three *named* colours \(0,1,2\), so that the three edges at every vertex have different colours. States differing merely by a global permutation of colour names remain distinct.

Given distinct colours \(a,b\), the edges coloured \(a\) or \(b\) form a spanning disjoint union of even cycles. One legal move swaps those two colours on *one entire such cycle*. The reconfiguration graph is undirected and simple: multiple descriptions producing the same neighbour count once. All statements concern this exact finite graph, not arbitrary generalized Petersen graphs or four-edge-colourings.

## Proof
**Exhaustiveness of states.** In any proper three-edge-colouring of a cubic graph, the colour-zero edges form a perfect matching \(M\). Conversely, once \(M\) is fixed, the other two colours properly colour its complementary spanning two-factor exactly when every cycle of that two-factor is even; each such cycle has precisely two alternating colour assignments. Recursing on the least unmatched vertex enumerates the 120 perfect matchings without duplicates. Enumerating all alternating assignments for every feasible matching produces exactly 504 distinct named colourings, with each colouring recovered uniquely from its colour-zero class and the assignments on complementary cycles.

**Exhaustiveness of switches.** At each enumerated state and for each of the three unordered colour pairs, traverse every connected component of the corresponding two-factor. Exchanging that colour pair along a component generates *all and only* the legal moves. Reciprocal adjacency is checked. The 504 by 504 finite adjacency relation contains 3,348 directed incidences, hence 1,674 undirected edges. Full component search gives exactly 144 and 360 vertices, with 432 and 1,242 edges. Inspecting each two-factor proves the ten-cycle criterion and its converse, including stability under every legal switch.

**Cayley structure.** Work with the numerical vertex indexing above. Let \(r\) increment both twelve-element indices, let \(s\) negate both indices, and let \(a\) be the vertex permutation, displayed as the images of vertices \(0,1,\ldots,23\),

\[
(0,11,23,16,4,3,15,20,8,7,19,12,1,10,18,21,5,2,22,13,9,6,14,17).
\]

Each of these three permutations preserves the 36-edge set. Exact permutation closure of \(\langle r,s,a\rangle\) has order 144. For a representative colouring with the ten/fourteen property, its 144 images under this group are pairwise different and exhaust \(\mathcal C\). Thus the action on \(\mathcal C\) is free and transitive. Because graph automorphisms commute with Kempe switching, identifying each group element with its image of the representative makes \(\mathcal C\) a Cayley graph. The six group elements taking that representative to its six neighbours are involutions, checked by squaring their explicit vertex permutations. This gives an explicit six-involution Cayley presentation. The group coincides with the known 144-element automorphism group of the Nauru graph (see Eppstein's account), but the Cayley claim needs only the constructed subgroup.

**Metrics.** Breadth-first search from *every* vertex in each connected component determines every shortest-path distance, not a sample or an extrapolation. The distance-sphere vector for all 144 Cayley states is \((1,6,27,66,44)\). In the irregular component 330 states have eccentricity five and 30 have eccentricity six. The unordered distance counts are obtained from all ordered BFS distances by division by two. A parity-lift BFS shows that \(\mathcal C\) has no odd closed walk, while the shortest odd closed walk of \(\mathcal I\) has length five; thus the former is bipartite and the latter has odd girth five. Two standalone implementations, using different complete state-enumeration and switching algorithms, reproduce the component and metric claims.

## Verification
From the package directory, execute `python verify_matching.py` and `python verify_colouring.py` using Python 3.9 or later. Both use only the standard library. The first enumerates perfect matchings and complementary even cycles, constructs the reconfiguration graph, checks all-pairs distances, and verifies the 144-element regular permutation action and six involutory generators. The second independently solves the 36-edge three-colour constraint problem directly, extracts two-colour cycles by vertex walking, and recomputes all components and distances. Every finite enumeration range, graph definition, expected count, and critical assertion appears in the scripts. The two independent runs each assert all reported metric and cycle-type data.

## Relationship to prior work
The Nauru graph and its permutation-star model and 144 symmetries are classical; Eppstein's 2007 account gives \(GP(12,5)\) and the symmetric-group description. Edge-Kempe equivalence for cubic graphs was studied by belcastro and Haas (public preprint September 8, 2012). Goedgebeur and Östergård (2021) exhaustively surveyed *numbers of edge-Kempe equivalence classes* of cubic graphs through order 30 and bipartite cubic graphs through order 36; their published tables are aggregates, not this graph's 504-state adjacency, its two sharply different metrics, the ten/fourteen-cycle separation, or the Cayley presentation. The bare two-class count is therefore not claimed independently of that computational census. The novelty claim concerns the full, explicitly reconstructed metric and structural dichotomy. The existence of a known 144-automorphism group alone does not imply its free transitive action on a Kempe component.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite statement for \(GP(12,5)\) only. The results do not assert a family theorem or new general criteria for edge-Kempe equivalence. The published graph-census dataset has not been inspected for possible additional unpublished metric fields. Watkins's relevant 1969 paper on Tait colourings of generalized Petersen graphs was identified but its full text was not obtained; no absence-of-coverage conclusion is drawn from its title. Historical priority is therefore qualified. There has been no independent scholarly audit.
