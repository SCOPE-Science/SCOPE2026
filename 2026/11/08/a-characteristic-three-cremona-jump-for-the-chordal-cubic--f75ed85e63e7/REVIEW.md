# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The five derivative identities factor the gradient through six explicit Pluecker minors and a linear projection of the smooth Klein quadric. The proof gives a rational inverse to the catalecticant-to-Grassmannian map on a nonempty open set, using a directly checked rank-three overlap system. The projection center has quadric value \(-3\), making its generic degree one exactly in characteristic three and two otherwise (separable because characteristic two is excluded). The characteristic-three gradient ideal is reduced on the three indicated affine charts, giving the disjoint reduced union of a rational normal quartic and a point. Exhaustive checks over three finite prime fields independently test all polynomial identities and point counts. Residual limit: finite tests do not replace the characteristic-free proof; the unsaturated ideal is not classified.

## Originality
**PASS, provisionally.** The closest direct prior source is Mostafazadehfard--Simis, *Homaloidal determinants*, arXiv:1407.8089 (2014): its introduction restricts to characteristic zero, and Proposition 3.20 establishes that this determinant is **not** homaloidal in characteristic zero. Thus that theorem covers the contrast case but cannot imply the positive-characteristic-three Cremona result. Earlier work on Pluecker embeddings explains the geometry of the intermediate Grassmannian but not the characteristic-dependent location of this gradient projection's center, or the isolated off-hypersurface base point. Targeted published-record and literature searches did not locate an exact positive-characteristic counterpart. Unindexed or later specialized sources remain a priority risk; absence from a search database is not uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a characteristic-dependent change in generic degree of a natural polar map for the classical chordal cubic, from two to a birational map. The degree jump is determined structurally by membership of a projection center in a smooth quadric, and it simultaneously creates an isolated base point not belonging to the defining hypersurface. Such phenomena are genuinely absent in the classical characteristic-zero treatment; the result is not just a coefficient substitution or a finite special case.
