# A characteristic-two automorphism jump for the four-element dihedral quandle algebra

## Finding
For every field \(k\), let \(A=k[R_4]\) be the four-dimensional, generally nonassociative algebra on symbols \(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3\) with the bilinear product
\[
e_i e_j=e_{2j-i\pmod4}.
\]
The **full group of field-linear algebra automorphisms** is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_k(A)\cong
\begin{cases}
(k^\times\times k^\times)\rtimes C_2,&\operatorname{char}k\ne2,\\
M_2(k)^+\rtimes C_2,&\operatorname{char}k=2.
\end{cases}
\]
In the first case, the involution swaps the scalar factors. In the second, it acts on the additive group of \(2\times2\) matrices by \(T\mapsto STS^{-1}\), where \(S=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}\).
Thus, for any finite field \(\mathbb F_q\),
\[
\left|\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_q}(A)\right|=
\begin{cases}
2(q-1)^2,&q\text{ odd},\\2q^4,&q\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, there are \(32\) such automorphisms over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(512\) over \(\mathbb F_4\). The usual \(8\)-element quandle-automorphism subgroup is strictly smaller than the full algebra automorphism group in these two fields.

## Assumptions and scope
All fields are allowed, including infinite fields and nonprime finite fields. Automorphisms must be invertible **\(k\)-linear** maps preserving the indicated bilinear product. The statement makes no claim about automorphism group schemes or semilinear automorphisms. We denote by \(M_2(k)^+\) the additive group of all two-by-two matrices, not its group of invertible matrices. The subgroup \(C_2\) in both formulas is generated by the algebra automorphism \(e_0\leftrightarrow e_1,\ e_2\leftrightarrow e_3\).

## Proof
**Intrinsic right annihilator.** In every characteristic, direct evaluation of the defining multiplication shows that the right annihilator \(R=\{z\in A:Az=0\}\) is the two-dimensional subspace spanned by \(e_0-e_2\) and \(e_1-e_3\). It is characteristic under every algebra automorphism. The quotient \(A/R\), with the images of \(e_0,e_1\) as basis, is the trivial two-point quandle algebra: \(xy=\varepsilon(y)x\), where \(\varepsilon(x_0\bar e_0+x_1\bar e_1)=x_0+x_1\). Thus the induced quotient matrix \(P\) preserves \(\varepsilon\).

**Fields of characteristic other than two.** Define
\[
u=e_0+e_2,\quad w=e_1+e_3,\quad v=e_0-e_2,\quad t=e_1-e_3.
\]
Then \(A=U\oplus R\) with \(U=\langle u,w\rangle\) and \(R=\langle v,t\rangle\). Define the independent linear forms on \(U\),
\[
\varepsilon(\alpha u+\beta w)=2(\alpha+\beta),\qquad
\delta(\alpha u+\beta w)=2(\alpha-\beta).
\]
The products, extended bilinearly to arbitrary \(x,y\in U\) and \(z\in R\), obey
\[
xy=\varepsilon(y)x,\qquad xz=0,\qquad
vz=tz=0,\qquad
v y=\delta(y)v,\quad t y=-\delta(y)t.
\]
Consequently an automorphism has matrix \(\begin{psmallmatrix}P&0\\T&Q\end{psmallmatrix}\) in the ordered decomposition \(U\oplus R\), with \(\varepsilon P=\varepsilon\). Applying the product identity to \(x,y\in U\) gives
\[
\varepsilon(y)T x=\delta(Py)D T x,
\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).
\]
Since \(\varepsilon\) and \(\delta P\) are independent, choose \(y\) in the kernel of \(\delta P\) but not the kernel of \(\varepsilon\). The displayed equation forces \(T=0\). Applying the identity to \(z\in R,y\in U\) then yields
\[
\delta(Py) DQ=\delta(y)QD \quad\text{for all }y\in U.
\]
As \(Q\) is invertible and \(D\) has two distinct eigenvalues \(1\) and \(-1\), it follows that \(\delta P=\delta\) with \(Q\) invertible diagonal, or \(\delta P=-\delta\) with \(Q\) invertible antidiagonal. Together with \(\varepsilon P=\varepsilon\), these alternatives give \(P=I\) or the swap of \(u,w\), respectively. Every such block matrix preserves all the multiplication rules, yielding exactly \((k^\times)^2\rtimes C_2\).

**Fields of characteristic two.** Put
\[
a=e_0,\quad b=e_1,\quad u=e_0+e_2,\quad w=e_1+e_3,
\]
a basis of \(A\). Here \(R=\langle u,w\rangle\). For \(x=\alpha a+\beta b\), \(y=\gamma a+\eta b\), and \(\rho,\sigma\in R\), the complete multiplication law is
\[
(x+\rho)(y+\sigma)
=(\gamma+\eta)(x+\rho)+\alpha\eta\,u+\beta\gamma\,w.
\tag{*}
\]
Let \(F\) be an automorphism. Its induced quotient map \(P\in\operatorname{GL}_2(k)\) preserves coordinate sum. Since \(a\) and \(b\) are idempotents, so are \(F(a)\) and \(F(b)\). The equation \((*)\) shows that an element with quotient coordinates \((\alpha,\beta)\) and coordinate sum one is idempotent only if \(\alpha\beta=0\). Thus each column of \(P\) equals one of the two standard coordinate vectors. As \(P\) is invertible, \(P=I\) or \(P=S\).
Now use \(ab=a+u\) and \(ba=b+w\). For any \(F\) with quotient block \(P\), these two identities determine the restriction \(Q=F|_R\), giving \(Q=P\) on the bases \((u,w)\) and \((a,b)\). By \((*)\), the remaining block \(T:k^2\to R\) is unrestricted. Therefore all and only the automorphisms are
\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}P&0\\T&P\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P\in\{I,S\},\quad T\in M_2(k).
\]
Writing \(T=LP\), each such matrix factors uniquely into \(\begin{psmallmatrix}I&0\\L&I\end{psmallmatrix}\operatorname{diag}(P,P)\), and conjugation by the swap sends \(L\) to \(SLS^{-1}\). This identifies the group with \(M_2(k)^+\rtimes C_2\). The finite-field orders follow immediately.

## Verification
The accompanying `CHECK.py` independently implements the full multiplication table and direct testing of all sixteen products of basis images. It exhaustively enumerates the \(20,160\) invertible linear transformations of \(\mathbb F_2^4\), finding exactly \(32\) automorphisms and checking equality with the proposed normal forms. It checks every predicted automorphism and thousands of random nonmembers over \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_5\) and \(\mathbb F_4\), the last implemented with a polynomial basis for the quadratic extension. `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt` records the actual replay output. Enumeration in finitely many fields cannot replace the proof over arbitrary fields.

## Relationship to prior work
Bardakov–Passi–Singh (2022, Theorem 6.4) determined \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z[R_4])\cong(C_2\times C_2)\rtimes C_2\). This is a result over the integers, not a determination of all field-linear automorphisms after a change of characteristic. Sangare–Ta (2026, Theorem 4.15) prove a canonical embedding for the even-order dihedral-quandle case **only when the coefficient ring has characteristic different from two**; their classification for the odd-order quotient case does not settle the present four-element algebra. The present result gives an exact field-by-field classification, in particular detecting the four-dimensional additive subgroup of extra automorphisms in characteristic two, which those theorems do not cover. Bardakov–Elhamdadi (2026) studies integral quandle rings and other examples, not this full field-automorphism group.

## Limitations
Originality means no stronger or equivalent full field-characteristic classification was identified in the checked literature and research records; it is not an exhaustive proof of historical priority. The formulas describe ordinary \(k\)-linear algebra automorphisms; neither group schemes nor arbitrary commutative coefficient rings are classified. An independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
