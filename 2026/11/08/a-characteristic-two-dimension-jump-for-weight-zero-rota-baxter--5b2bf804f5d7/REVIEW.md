# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The reduction of the weight-zero operator identity is proved without dividing by two: its scalar components force \(P(V)\subset V\) and \(P(1)\in V\), and the remaining bilinear identity is exactly \(L^2=0\), \(2Lv=0\). This distinguishes characteristic two from other fields, including at dimension one. Both proposed resolutions are explicit vector bundles over the same Grassmannian, with proven surjectivity and a unique flag on the maximum-rank dense open stratum. Their dimensions and the Gaussian-binomial counting formula follow from the fiber ranks. Exhaustive small-field tests corroborate the equations.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** Farooq's 2026 characteristic-zero classification proves the non-characteristic-two branch under its assumptions and supplies the motivating parameterization. Achar–Henderson Proposition 3.3 and Corollary 3.4, along with Bellamy–Boos, already cover the individual Grassmannian incidence geometry as an instance of enhanced nilpotent orbit theory; the new conclusion is the **comparison of two operator loci across characteristic two**, including an unbounded dimension jump and finite-field counts as operator counts, not a bare claim to know the square-zero cone. Searches for characteristic-two Rota–Baxter operators on square-zero local or truncated-polynomial algebras located no direct coverage. Negative searches do not prove global novelty. Residual risk: older descriptions under other operator terminology or a stronger general all-characteristic classification.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The assumption of characteristic zero in the 2026 classification is not merely technical: removing it changes the dimension of the moduli of operators by \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), gives a different vector-fiber geometry, and alters the counting polynomial. The result identifies a natural, growing structural boundary in a freshly classified algebra family, with an exact geometric and arithmetic description, rather than only a small-dimensional anomaly. It does not claim the classical Grassmannian method or generic nilpotent orbit results are new.

**Limitations and closest sources.** The statement is restricted to zero-weight operators on unital square-zero local algebras and to reduced operator varieties for geometry. Farooq, arXiv:2605.15670v1, §3 covers characteristic-zero classification; Achar–Henderson, arXiv:0712.1079, and Bellamy–Boos, arXiv:1609.04525, cover related enhanced nilpotent orbits. The geometric proof is not a computational certificate. Priority under alternative terminology remains uncertain.
