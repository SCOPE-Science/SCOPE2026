# A circle-wedge classification for full strong Morse complexes of complete bipartite graphs

## Finding
For all integers \(m,n\ge2\), let \(K_{m,n}\) be the one-dimensional unfilled complete bipartite graph, with vertex parts \(A\) and \(B\) of sizes \(m\) and \(n\). Form the **full** strong discrete-Morse matching complex \(\mathcal{SM}(K_{m,n})\) generated by every injective vertex filtration. There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal{SM}(K_{m,n})\simeq\bigvee^{2mn-m-n+1}S^1.}
\]
In fact, the complex has an explicit simplicial collapse to \(m+n\) disjoint full simplices connected by \(2mn\) bridge edges. Collapsing the full simplices then gives the connected multigraph \(2K_{m,n}\), where each edge of \(K_{m,n}\) is doubled. Consequently \(\widetilde H_1\cong\mathbb Z^{2mn-m-n+1}\) and all other reduced integral homology vanishes.

## Assumptions and scope
The result applies to every \(m,n\in\mathbb Z\) satisfying \(m,n\ge2\), with no upper bound. The graph \(K_{m,n}\) is regarded as an abstract one-dimensional simplicial complex, not as the clique complex of its one-skeleton or a higher matching complex.

An injective function on the graph vertices determines a total order \(v_1,\ldots,v_{m+n}\). A vertex \(v\) is descending dominated exactly when it has **one** earlier graph neighbor: its descending link is then a nonempty zero-dimensional cone (a singleton). For this vertex the strong gradient contains the pair consisting of \(v\) and the edge joining it to that unique earlier neighbor. Denote this pair by the directed symbol \(v\to u\), pointing from the later to the earlier endpoint. The vertices of \(\mathcal{SM}\) are such symbols, and its faces are the subsets of gradient pairs generated by all total orders, including non-maximal subsets. This is the *full*, not pure, strong complex.

## Proof
**1. All possible generating faces.** Write \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\) and \(B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}\). For each \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\), form the full star simplices
\[
S_a=\{b'\to a:b'\in B\},\qquad S_b=\{a'\to b:a'\in A\}.
\]
Their vertex sets are pairwise disjoint, since each directed symbol has exactly one target. In addition, for each \((a,b)\in A\times B\), form two flap simplices
\[
F^A_{a,b}=\{b\to a\}\cup\{a'\to b:a'\in A\setminus\{a\}\},
\]
\[
F^B_{a,b}=\{a\to b\}\cup\{b'\to a:b'\in B\setminus\{b\}\}.
\]
We show that the full complex is generated by exactly these stars and flaps (some listed generators may be contained in others in small parameters). For any total ordering let \(a_0\) and \(b_0\) be its first vertices in \(A\) and \(B\), respectively. A matched \(A\)-vertex must point toward \(b_0\), and a matched \(B\)-vertex must point toward \(a_0\), since the unique earlier neighbor must be the first vertex in the opposite part. If all matched vertices belong to one part, the gradient lies in one of the star simplices. Otherwise suppose \(a_0\) precedes \(b_0\). For an \(A\)-vertex to be matched there must be a second \(A\)-vertex after \(b_0\) but before a second \(B\)-vertex. No \(B\)-vertex after \(b_0\) can then be matched: it sees at least two earlier \(A\)-vertices. Thus the only possible \(B\)-arrow is \(b_0\to a_0\), and all other arrows point from \(A\setminus\{a_0\}\) to \(b_0\). The gradient lies in \(F^A_{a_0,b_0}\). If \(b_0\) precedes \(a_0\), the symmetric argument gives \(F^B_{a_0,b_0}\). Conversely, each star is realized by ordering its target first, then all opposite-part vertices, then the remaining vertices. Each flap is realized by ordering its two distinguished vertices first, in arrow-compatible order, then all remaining vertices of the same part as the first distinguished vertex, then the rest. All listed simplices therefore occur, completing the classification.

**2. Simplicial elementary collapses.** Fix a flap \(F^A_{a,b}\), set \(q=b\to a\), and let \(T=S_b\setminus\{a\to b\}\). Then \(F^A_{a,b}=\{q\}*T\) with \(T\ne\varnothing\) because \(m\ge2\). Fix any pivot \(p\in T\). For every nonempty subset \(U\subseteq T\setminus\{p\}\), pair the faces \(\{q\}\cup U\) and \(\{q,p\}\cup U\), processing subsets \(U\) in decreasing cardinality. These are free-face elementary collapses: the mixed faces containing \(q\) and at least one vertex of \(T\) occur in this unique flap, and all cofaces other than the indicated one were removed at earlier, larger-cardinality steps. The same construction applies to each \(F^B_{a,b}\). No non-star mixed face lies in two distinct flaps: the two directed arrows of a mixed edge specify the middle vertex and its two distinct same-part endpoints. Thus the collapse pairs from different flaps do not interfere. After all collapses, every full star remains, together with exactly one edge from each flap joining a vertex of \(S_a\) to a vertex of \(S_b\).

**3. Homotopy quotient.** The remaining \(m+n\) star simplices are disjoint, nonempty, and contractible subcomplexes of a finite CW complex. Quotienting a contractible CW subcomplex to a point preserves homotopy type, so we may quotient them one at a time. Each pair \((a,b)\) contributes precisely two distinct edges between the quotient vertices corresponding to \(S_a\) and \(S_b\), one from each of \(F^A_{a,b}\) and \(F^B_{a,b}\). No edge becomes a loop, since the stars corresponding to \(a\) and \(b\) are distinct. The quotient is therefore \(2K_{m,n}\), a connected graph with \(m+n\) vertices and \(2mn\) edges. Its first Betti number is \(2mn-(m+n)+1\), and every connected graph is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of that many circles. This proves the theorem over \(\mathbb Z\) for all stated parameters.

## Verification
The companion `verify.py` is a standalone Python 3 program using only standard-library modules. It independently enumerates all \((m+n)!\) injective vertex orders for the test pairs \((m,n)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,3),(3,4),(4,4),(4,5)\), constructs their strong gradients from unique-earlier-neighbor tests, closes each gradient under taking subsets, and compares the exact face sets with the star-and-flap construction. It then executes every claimed flap collapse as an actual free-face deletion, checking that the target face has exactly one remaining coface, that the remainder is the stars plus one bridge per flap, and that the bridge quotient is exactly the doubled complete bipartite multigraph. Every test prints `OK`; the final line is `ALL_VERIFY_OK`. The execution log is provided in `verify_output.txt`. These finite tests corroborate but do not prove the infinite-family theorem; the proof is the symbolic argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens-White, *Complexes of strong discrete Morse matchings* (arXiv:2609.06844, first posted 2026-09-06), introduced the associated full and pure constructions and described classifications for paths, stars, simplex families and cycle subcomplexes. Their indexed abstract does not state an all-complete-bipartite theorem. Fernández, *Strong discrete Morse theory* (arXiv:2504.15729), provides the vertex-filtration and descending-dominance framework used here. The **ordinary** Morse matching complex \(\mathfrak M(K_{m,n})\), allowing arbitrary acyclic matchings, is a different space and its known homotopy computations cannot imply this full **strong** complex classification without an additional argument. The formula specializes at \(m=2\) to \(3n-1\) circles; the result here is the uniform classification for arbitrary bipartition sizes, together with a direct collapse model valid in every case.

## Limitations
The claim is for the full strong matching complex of unfilled \(K_{m,n}\), \(m,n\ge2\), and does not address the pure strong subcomplex, ordinary discrete-Morse matching complexes, other graph families, the cases with a part of size one, or an equivariant homotopy equivalence. The complete text of the motivating recent preprint was not accessible for a line-by-line originality check; its available abstract delineates a different list of computed families, but equivalent unindexed work cannot be ruled out. No independent mathematical validation is asserted.
