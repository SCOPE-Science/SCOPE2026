For \(n\) strict house-allocation agents with all but one sharing a preference ranking, the complete random-priority lottery has one exact probability \(t_x/n!\) per matching whose dissenter receives object \(x\), where \(t_x\) is a suffix-choice count. Its positive priority multiplicities realize every composition of \(n\), its unordered lottery shapes are counted by integer partitions, and a uniformly random dissenter gives a Stirling-number law for the deterministic support size.
