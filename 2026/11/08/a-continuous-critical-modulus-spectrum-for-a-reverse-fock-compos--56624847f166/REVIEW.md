# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The maximal unbounded weighted composition has dense polynomial domain and is closed, the reverse natural product is closed, and every reproducing kernel lies in its domain. On the dense kernel span its Gram form equals the bounded positive operator derived by conjugating Gaussian multiplication with the unitary Segal--Bargmann transform and a quarter-turn. Closure proves that the natural product is bounded on the entire Fock space, not merely on kernels. The positive multiplication model gives the exact continuous essential spectrum, absence of eigenvalues and norm/essential-norm identities. The critical Fock/Bargmann coefficient identity was checked with 732 exact rational assertions from the supplied standalone program. The proof, not the finite check, establishes the full operator statement.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, residual risk recorded)

Sang–Zhao, arXiv:2610.28279, Theorems 1.1–1.2 already imply the bounded/noncompact status for Gaussian kernels of this form; no novelty is asserted for that status. Their Section 5 addresses compact Schatten membership and Hilbert–Schmidt norms, not the critical positive spectral model. Tian, arXiv:2610.29786, explicitly leaves the reverse natural product under one unbounded factor for further study, whereas its results address the forward product. Dong–Zhu, arXiv:2509.16672, treat a different canonical family with unitary bounded boundary and compact singular values. Hai, arXiv:1804.00414, concerns simpler weighted-composition adjoint representations. Searches of related published research records did not identify an implication covering the precise nonunitary critical Gaussian multiplier model. Oscillator-semigroup literature could nevertheless imply the exact model in another formulation; publication priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural reverse product involving compact and unbounded operators possesses a noncompact but bounded transition whose spectral type is determined exactly: the modulus has a full continuous interval, no eigenvalues, and an explicitly computable essential norm equaling its norm. This is structurally different from compact Gaussian operators with discrete singular values and from canonical Gaussian unitary boundary operators; the specific positive spectral model is motivated by the identified critical boundary, rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. It makes the underexplored reverse-product problem tangible without claiming to solve it generally.

## Limitations and priority considerations

The proof covers real slopes and a strictly positive real quadratic weight with the critical relation only. There is no determination of the complex spectrum of the nonnormal product, nor of higher-dimensional/general affine symbols. An older abstract oscillator-semigroup factorization could imply part or all of the result. These possibilities require further independent scholarly comparison.
