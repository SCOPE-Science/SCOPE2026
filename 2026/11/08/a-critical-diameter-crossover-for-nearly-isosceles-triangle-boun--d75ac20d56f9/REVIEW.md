# Self-review: A critical diameter crossover for nearly isosceles triangle boundaries\n\n## Correctness — PASS\nSix disjoint oriented endpoint arcs have exact multinomial avoidance probabilities and converge to independent rate-\(1/P\) exponentials. Since the third side remains uniformly shorter than the two competing sides, Euclidean distance localization reduces the sample diameter to four endpoint minima and the deterministic \(\lambda/N\) side-length offset. The critical cosine factors are \(a=1-c^2/2\) at the apex and \(c/2\) at the base. The base-endpoint minimum has rate \(\beta=1/c\); apex minima are dependent, with the explicit two-dimensional survivor in the proof. The onset identity integrates a rate-\(\kappa\) exponential over an interval of width \(h\), giving a quadratic coefficient \(\kappa\beta e^{-\beta\lambda}/2\). The singularity and the doubled zero-split coefficient follow. The actual standalone checker ran successfully and crosschecked 9 finite-sample distribution values; simulations alone are not asserted to prove the theorem. Remaining boundary: no uniform \(c\to0\), \(c\to1\), or finite-N rate established.\n\n## Originality — PASS (best knowledge, provisional)\nThe relevant fully inspected source is W. Lao, *Some weak limit laws for the diameter of random point sets in bounded regions* (2010), Chapter 5.2, Theorem 5.2, proof pages 81–85 and Appendix D.2. It treats a fixed equilateral triangle: \(c=1\), with three equal longest sides, not the \(0<c<1\) double-scaling family. Mayer and Molchanov (arxiv:math/0605289, 2006) and Jammalamadaka–Janson (arxiv:1211.0822, 2012) use spherical/ball distributions rather than varying triangular boundaries. Search records also yielded fixed-polygon Hausdorff-error and short-chord results, which do not entail the positive CDF curvature jump at an interior threshold. Accessible earlier related claims on polygonal boundary-area and fixed-shape distance measures are distinct. Residual risk: a general triangular-array point-process theorem or an overlooked nonregular-polygon diameter paper could reproduce this limit or imply it immediately; no independent priority certification is available.\n\n## Value — PASS\nThe central geometric motivation is competition among two diametral sides of almost equal length. The exact splitting scale \(N^{-1}\) separates a two-channel regime from a single-channel regime. The observable second-derivative discontinuity identifies the split, and the doubled lower-tail coefficient at coalescence gives a sharp, nontrivial boundary between geometric phases. The two apex costs remain strongly correlated (explicit coefficient \(a(2-a)\)), so treating the two channels as independent would be wrong. This is a natural structural extreme-geometric classification, not an arbitrary parameter slice or just an algebraic rewrite of Lao's equilateral law.\n\n## Literature and limitations\nSources: Lao (2010), official KIT full text, Theorem 5.2 and Appendix D.2; Mayer–Molchanov (2006), arxiv:math/0605289; Jammalamadaka–Janson (2012), arxiv:1211.0822.
