# Scientific scope of the finite-time fourth-cumulant threshold

## Correctness

The exact Boyer--Majumdar characteristic function gives a slow Kummer branch with small spectral exponent and a fast branch separated by a strictly positive decay exponent on the parameter strip surrounding [3/2,5/2]. Holomorphic parameter control and Cauchy differentiation yield power-small remainders uniformly with one reset-rate derivative. The fourth-cumulant derivative has leading coefficient (rho-4)/rho^3 times log(gamma t), which is uniformly negative on the specified interval. Opposite endpoint signs then prove a unique root and its sign orientation. Expanding at rho=2 produces the logarithmic displacement and the moving-layer excess kurtosis. Exact symbolic and finite-time Kummer checks corroborate coefficients; numerical checks do not establish the universal time assertion.

## Originality and prior implication

Boyer--Majumdar (2023/2024) supplied the full characteristic function and leading scaling. A published theorem from 2 November 2026, *Critical fourth-cumulant cancellation in an active memory-reset walk*, already supplies the fixed-rate fourth-cumulant asymptotic **including** the constant at rho=2, and the inverse-log-squared standardized kurtosis. Those facts must not be credited as new. This narrower result concerns a rate-uniform first derivative, a strict entire-interval finite-time uniqueness theorem, and the moving-rate limit; they are not asserted in the prior fixed-rate theorem. The analytic upgrade is short and based on standard uniform Kummer theory; an implicit corollary or an unindexed derivation is a residual originality risk.

## Mathematical value and restrictions

The finite-observation sign crossover is a natural diagnostic of non-Gaussianity in ultraslow memory-resetting transport. An explicit unique threshold over a full rate interval and its logarithmic displacement sharpen the practical meaning of a fixed-rate sign transition without presuming a global all-rate phase diagram. The result is limited to the stated compact interval, symmetric velocity initialization and simultaneous resetting of position and velocity. It does not provide a numerical minimum observation time or finite-sample inference theorem.
