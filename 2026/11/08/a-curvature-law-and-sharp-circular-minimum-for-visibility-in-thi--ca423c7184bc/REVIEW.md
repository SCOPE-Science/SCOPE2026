# Scientific review: curvature law for thin inner-parallel visibility

## Correctness

**PASS.** Normal coordinates give the exact shell area \(P\varepsilon-\pi\varepsilon^2\), and positive curvature uniformly restricts visible pairs to boundary-footpoint separations of order \(\sqrt\varepsilon\). The local quadratic chord-depth expansion leads to the condition \(|z|\le\sqrt{2/\kappa}(\sqrt{1-A}+\sqrt{1-B})\), whose integrated constant is \(8\sqrt2/3\). Dominated convergence is justified by uniform localization and the measure-zero limiting threshold. Strict Jensen equality gives the unique disk optimizer. Independently evaluated disk formulas and ellipse geometric simulations agree at the predicted scale. Computational samples cannot replace the analytic proof.

## Originality

**PASS, provisional historical priority.** The 1974 Beer paper studies external thickening and continuity; the 2014 Balko–Jelínek–Valtr–Walczak paper establishes Beer-index inequalities; the polygonal computation result of Abrahamsen–Fredslund-Hansen is for finite polygons; Fodor–Papvári establish one-point boundary visibility cap bounds in a different random-polytope setting. A published homothetic-shell facet-moment result proves a zero limit for facetless bodies but gives no smooth-body leading rate, and its inner shell is defined by scaling rather than offsetting. The previously available exact circular-annulus Beer index is a strict special case, not a general-body curvature theorem. Relevant searches and implications were compared; an equivalent older theorem may remain undiscovered.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The result identifies the exact first nonzero geometric-probability invariant in the thin-shell limit, proves its \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) order and its dependence on the complete curvature profile, and solves the sharp fixed-perimeter shape optimization with a unique minimizer. Neither the numerical diagnostics nor the general fact that facetless shells have zero limiting index gives this exact coefficient.

## Limits and scientific comparison

The result assumes a fixed compact \(C^3\) strictly convex planar body with curvature bounded away from zero. It does not cover polygons, dimensions greater than two, or homothetic shells in general. The disk formula is used solely for verification. Historical originality is not independently certified.
