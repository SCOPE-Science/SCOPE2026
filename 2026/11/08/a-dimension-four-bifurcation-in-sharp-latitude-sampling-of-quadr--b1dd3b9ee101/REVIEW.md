# Scientific review of the latitude quadrupole frame result

## Correctness

PASS. The theorem's Hilbert space is the pure complex degree-two harmonic subspace in dimension at least three, with normalized ambient probability measure. The sample measure is uniform on each of two antipodal latitudes and has total mass one; a single latitude is equivalent on this even harmonic subspace. Fourth-order moments yield the complete three-sector eigenvalue decomposition, including multiplicities and all injectivity boundaries. The proof uses monotonicity of the two crossing branches on either side of the exceptional squared height, then a separate continuous-dimension monotonicity comparison to prove the unique dimension-four tie. The independent Gram-builder tests 50 dimension/height combinations and 98 dimension-parameter optimizer comparisons; 834 assertions passed.

Xiao and Zhuang (arXiv:2303.05365) establish a design-and-framelet framework but do not prove this sharp one-latitude theorem. The broad Slepian-wavelet analysis by Roddy and McEwen is not a hypersurface trace spectral classification. Known fourth-moment and associated-Legendre formulas are acknowledged. A classical representation-theoretic equivalent might exist and has not been excluded by exhaustive archival investigation.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a natural exact stability-design problem for continuous latitude-limited sensing of pure spherical quadrupoles. The result gives every singular value, the exact exceptional invisible height, and the complete sharp E-optimal design across all dimensions, including the non-obvious double optimum at dimension four. It is useful for distinguishing effects of band restriction, normalization, and observation geometry. It is not an optimization for discrete samplers, mixed harmonic degrees, arbitrary probability measures, or condition numbers.
