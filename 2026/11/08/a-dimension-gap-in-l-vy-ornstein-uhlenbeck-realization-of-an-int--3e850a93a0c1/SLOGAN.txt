For a Brownian particle in a common, randomly switched on/off harmonic trap, every stationary spatial vector law in dimension \(d\ge2\) is infinitely divisible, but admits a stationary-equivalent scalar-drift Lévy–Ornstein–Uhlenbeck bath exactly for switching rate \(\alpha\ge2\). Throughout \(2(\sqrt2-1)\le\alpha<2\), every scalar projection admits such a bath while the joint vector law does not; above the threshold an explicit isotropic compound-Poisson bath has total jump rate \(\alpha\).
