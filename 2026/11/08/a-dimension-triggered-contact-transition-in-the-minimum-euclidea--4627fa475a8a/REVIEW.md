# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the specified polytope \(P_{d;\alpha,\beta}\) with \(d\ge3\) and integer \(\alpha,\beta\ge1\), an exact orbit-minimax lemma determines the minimum enclosing ball of the putative support set. The omitted vertices are then excluded by two explicit, strict squared-distance inequalities. The cube-distance function is affine in the number of selected coordinates, so no interior cube layer has been overlooked. The quadratic discriminant gives the sharp dimensional contact transition. Direct exact Fraction verification checked all vertices in 392 parameter instances and 107,408 assertions, with 66 independent numerical minimax comparisons. Computation corroborates rather than replaces the unrestricted analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated risk

The primary full-text source (Ferroni, arXiv:2609.10513) defines the box-Cayley family and investigates Ehrhart and smoothness properties; it does not present this enclosing-ball radius or phase transition. Searches for equivalent formulations such as Chebyshev centers, minimal enclosing spheres, and Cayley-box circumradii did not locate a covering result. Closely related inscribed-ball results concern the opposite radius, and generic simplex/cube MEB facts do not imply the exact contact classification here. Potential unindexed geometric studies remain a historical-priority risk.

## Value — PASS

This is an exact all-dimensional metric solution for a natural sparse subfamily singled out by recent work. Its contact support undergoes a genuine dimension-driven phase change: for fixed \(\beta>\alpha\), a formerly screened base vertex rejoins the farthest-point set, while the smaller top endpoint remains screened for all dimensions. The theorem gives an explicit center, radius, complete contacting vertex set, and parameter-dependent threshold; this is more than a routine numerical evaluation.

## Limits and nearest literature

The source's Cayley construction and Ehrhart results are not claimed as new. The standard metric and the integer parameter regime are essential, and the result does not extend automatically to arbitrary matrices or affine embeddings. Historical uniqueness is not proved; independent audit has not occurred.
