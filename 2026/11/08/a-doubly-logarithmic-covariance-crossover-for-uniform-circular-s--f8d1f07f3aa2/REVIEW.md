**Correctness — PASS.** The one-coordinate conditional Dirichlet decomposition gives exact identity (1), with the critical remainder identified as \(\eta_n(t)=n^2\mathbb E[R_n\mathbf1_{\{X_1\le t\}}]\). An exponential representation bounds this remainder by \(n^2[(2/e)^n+\exp\{-ne^{-2nt}\}]\), proving it vanishes throughout every fixed centered crossover window. Substituting \(nt=\log\log n+\log\log\log n+z\) gives the stated \(1-e^{-z}\) profile. A convex conditional-maximum argument independently establishes that the finite covariance has a unique interior zero, allowing the root to be located by bracketing. Exact, bounded and independent integral tests pass in the attached reproducibility code. Numeric checks do not substitute for the proof.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, qualified).** Holst (1980), inspected in full, treats ordered spacings, the maximum, and the *number of sequential break points needed to make every piece short*, not covariance of a fixed-\(n\) maximum with its threshold exceedance count. Schlemm (2014), inspected in full, gives moments and the Gumbel limit for the maximum alone. The accessible historical record already has the finite covariance formula and its unique crossing; these are not the claim. The new comparison is at the level of the sharp two-iterated-log location and the universal scaled covariance function. Targeted indexed-record and mathematical-literature queries revealed no directly equivalent result. A hidden specialization of older multivariate spacing formulas remains a priority risk; lack of a keyword match cannot establish novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The threshold at which an extreme gap changes its association with global long-gap abundance lies at \((\log\log n)/n\), unexpectedly separated from the largest gap's \((\log n)/n\) scale. The additional \(\log\log\log n\) correction is necessary to obtain a nondegenerate signed limiting profile; an \(O(1)\) correction at the wrong centering would miss the transition. This supplies a motivated sharp scale and complete crossover law for a natural statistic, rather than an arbitrary finite parameter slice.

**Limitations and residual risks.** The asymptotics are pointwise in fixed \(z\), convergence is slow, and no independent audit has occurred. The earlier finite crossing law is prior material. Classical multivariate spacings literature may contain an implicit corollary, though the inspected relevant primary papers did not state it.
