# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain, frequency-set equivalence convention, and orthogonality normalization are explicit. The character sum on the affine simplex vanishes exactly at weight four; the seven complementary triples of pairwise admissible differences form the unique Fano plane. This gives all, not merely some, simplex-code frequency spectra (thirty subspaces and sixteen cosets each). The difference between the two support transforms forces the shared-spectrum condition \(u\in L^\perp\). Each dual is a \([7,4,3]\) Hamming code with exact weight enumerator \(1+7z^3+7z^4+z^7\), from which coordinate-permutation double-counting yields six compatible subspaces for each weight-three or weight-four displacement. Projection and independent lifting prove the ambient-dimension factor. The saved, standalone integer checker verifies 860,160 pair tests and the complete displacement profile; computation is supplementary, not the infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to the inspected mathematical record

Kadowaki (arXiv:2610.24022, especially Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 3.1) gives existence of individual spectra and universal spectra for small tiling complements, not the intersection count for the pair here. Ferguson–Iosevich (arXiv:2610.36417, Sections 2–4) proves a stronger dimension-eight spectral/tiling theorem but does not state the pairwise count. The closest published indexed results treat simultaneous four-point binary spectra, or individual eight-point subsets in affine dimension at most four; neither statement implies the presented exact dimension-seven mutation classification without additional Fano-incidence analysis. The classical Fano and Hamming properties are not themselves claimed as new. Published-index access to the two indexed reports was limited to their summaries, so earlier alternative formulations remain a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

A simultaneous common exponential basis is strictly stronger than separate spectrality. This natural one-vertex perturbation is the first affine-independent eight-point binary model; all members remain individually spectral, yet changing a single vertex may destroy every common basis. The complete count, sharp Hamming-weight threshold and ambient lifting formula provide a structural stability/compatibility invariant beyond an arbitrary finite census. It does not purport to settle arbitrary pairs of spectral sets.
