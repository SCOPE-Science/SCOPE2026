# Scientific review: complete characteristic-two idempotent law

## Correctness — PASS

The idempotency equation is exactly \(x=M^{\mathsf T}x^{[2]}\). In characteristic two its fixed points form an \(\mathbb F_2\)-space. The essential nonstandard lemma, that independent fixed points over \(\mathbb F_2\) remain independent over \(\mathbb F_q\), follows by applying Frobenius to a minimal dependence relation. This lemma justifies counting \(\mathbb F_q\)-frames and yields frame moments equal to one. The same moments for uniform \(\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)\), together with triangular invertibility, prove equality of the *entire* distributions. Gaussian inversion and Euler's product identity furnish explicit finite and limiting laws. An independent enumerator directly verified six parameter combinations, totaling 205482 invertible matrices; selected computations are not asserted to prove the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The nearest primary source, Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel (arXiv:2610.22248v1, Remark 23), derives only the universal first moment; the random-algebra question remains broader. The fixed-space formula itself is **previously known**, appearing in Fulman–Stanton (2015), Theorem 2.1, from Rudvalis–Shinoda. The present assertion is the structural and distributional *identification* of characteristic-two idempotents with that known law **for every dimension and every extension field**, not a rediscovery of the classical formula. The existing dimension-two law and variance are covered by narrower enumerations, but do not logically imply all higher-dimensional masses. Wei–Zou (2023) concerns isomorphism-class counts. The full Dempwolff–Fisher–Herman (2000) paper was inspected: it provides semilinear normal forms and a fixed-point theorem over the algebraic closure, but not the present finite-field random-matrix law. Its older cited fixed-subspace literature and unknown probabilistic applications remain residual priority risks. Searches found no source explicitly establishing the exact algebra-to-\(\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)\) identification. Historical priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The question is a natural refinement of a published statistical gap concerning finite-field evolution-algebra idempotents. The result gives a complete support restriction, all exact probabilities, extension-degree universality, and a dimension-asymptotic density of idempotent-free algebras. This is not obtainable merely by knowing a mean, variance, or one low-dimensional case. The connection to a classical fixed-space distribution enables immediate transfer of well-developed finite-linear-group techniques.

## Limits and closest literature

The sample is a uniform invertible *labeled* structure matrix, not a uniform isomorphism class. Characteristic two is essential. The cited finite-general-linear-group distribution is established prior art. The broader semilinear-transformation literature and historical priority still require independent scrutiny.
