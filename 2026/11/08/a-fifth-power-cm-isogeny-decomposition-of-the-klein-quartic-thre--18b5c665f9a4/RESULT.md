# A fifth-power CM isogeny decomposition of the Klein quartic threefold's intermediate Jacobian

## Finding
Let \(X\subset\mathbb P^4_{\mathbb C}\) be the smooth Klein quartic threefold
\[
X=V(x_0^3x_1+x_1^3x_2+x_2^3x_3+x_3^3x_4+x_4^3x_0).
\]
Its principally polarized intermediate Jacobian \(J(X)\) has dimension \(30\). Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{61})\), and let \(H\) be the subgroup of \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/61\mathbb Z)^\times\) generated by multiplication by \(-3\), namely
\[
H=\{1,9,20,34,58\},\qquad L=K^H,\qquad[L:\mathbb Q]=12.
\]
Then there exists a simple complex abelian variety \(A\) of dimension \(6\), with \(\operatorname{End}^0(A)\cong L\), such that
\[
\boxed{J(X)\sim_{\mathbb C} A^5,\qquad
\operatorname{End}^0(J(X))\cong M_5(L).}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{End}^0=\operatorname{End}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\); in particular the rational endomorphism algebra has dimension \(25\cdot12=300\). The isogeny does **not** assert a product decomposition of the principal polarization.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\) with ordinary rational Hodge structures and the unpolarized isogeny category of complex abelian varieties. The smoothness of this quartic and its order-\(61\) and order-\(5\) projective symmetries are established in the cited primary references. The CM field \(L\) is determined by the explicit subgroup \(H\), not merely by its degree. Up to a Galois translate/conjugation, the CM type of \(J(X)\) is determined by the character set calculated below.

## Proof
**1. Symmetry and Griffiths' model.** For a primitive \(61\)-st root \(\zeta\), let \(g\) act on the ambient homogeneous coordinates by
\[
(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\longmapsto
(\zeta x_0,\zeta^{-3}x_1,\zeta^{9}x_2,\zeta^{-27}x_3,\zeta^{81}x_4).
\]
Put \(a=(1,-3,9,-27,81)\bmod61\). Since \(3a_i+a_{i+1}\equiv0\pmod{61}\) for cyclic indices, \(g\) preserves the displayed defining form \(F\). Moreover \(\sum_i a_i=61\), so the determinant twist in the Griffiths residue representation is trivial. Griffiths' Jacobian-ring description identifies \(H^{2,1}(X)\) with the degree-three component
\[
R_3=\bigl(\mathbb C[x_0,\ldots,x_4]/(\partial_0F,\ldots,\partial_4F)\bigr)_3,
\]
where a monomial \(\prod_i x_i^{b_i}\) has character \(\zeta^{\sum_i a_i b_i}\).

**2. Exact Hodge character computation.** The degree-three polynomial space has dimension \(35\). The five independent cubic derivatives satisfy
\[
\partial_iF=3x_i^2x_{i+1}+x_{i-1}^3,
\]
and have pairwise distinct weights \(\{-a_i\}=\{3,27,41,52,60\}\bmod61\). Each derivative eliminates exactly one pure cube, leaving the \(30\) monomials whose exponents lie in \(\{0,1,2\}^5\) and sum to \(3\). An exhaustive finite residue calculation gives the following \(30\) distinct eigencharacters of \(H^{2,1}(X)\):
\[
\Phi=\{2,3,4,7,8,11,13,14,15,16,18,19,22,26,27,28,30,
32,36,37,38,40,41,44,49,51,52,55,56,60\}\pmod{61}.
\]
The other \(30\) nonzero characters are exactly \(-\Phi\). Hence \(g\) has every nontrivial \(61\)-st root eigenvalue with multiplicity one on \(H^3(X,\mathbb C)\). Consequently \(H^3(X,\mathbb Q)\), and its Tate twist \(H^3(X,\mathbb Q)(1)\), which is the rational weight-one Hodge structure underlying \(J(X)\), are one-dimensional over \(K\). In particular \(J(X)\) has CM by \(K\) of degree \(60=2\dim J(X)\).

**3. Stabilizer and primitive field.** Multiplication by \(-3\) has order \(5\) modulo \(61\), and \(\Phi\) is precisely the disjoint union of its six orbits
\[
\begin{gathered}
\{2,7,18,40,55\},\ \{3,27,41,52,60\},\ \{4,14,19,36,49\},\\
\{8,11,28,37,38\},\ \{13,15,16,22,56\},\ \{26,30,32,44,51\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The stabilizer of \(\Phi\) under multiplication by \((\mathbb Z/61\mathbb Z)^\times\) is **exactly** \(H\): it is enough to check the twelve cosets of \(H\), represented by
\(1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,13,23,26\). Each representative except \(1\) sends at least one element of \(\Phi\) outside \(\Phi\): respective witnesses are \(3,2,3,2,2,3,2,2,3,2,3\). The exact verifier reproduces all these finite checks.

Since \(-1\notin H\), the fixed field \(L=K^H\) is a CM field. The six orbits descend to a CM type \(\Phi_L\) on \(L\), containing exactly one of each complex-conjugate pair. The calculated stabilizer says that \((L,\Phi_L)\) is **primitive**: if this type descended further, its extension to \(K\) would have a stabilizer properly larger than \(H\). This is the primitive-pair criterion of Milne's Proposition 1.9 and Corollary 1.10.

**4. Isogeny and complete endomorphism algebra.** View the rank-one \(K\)-Hodge structure of \(J(X)\) instead as a rank-five vector space over \(L\). Because its CM type is pulled back from \(\Phi_L\), each \(L\)-embedding contributes either all five holomorphic directions or none. After choosing an \(L\)-basis, the rational weight-one Hodge structure is therefore the direct sum of five copies of the rank-one \(L\)-Hodge structure of \(\Phi_L\) (or its conjugate, depending on the dual convention for the intermediate Jacobian). Milne's classification of CM abelian varieties up to isogeny (Propositions 3.12 and 3.13) produces a simple abelian sixfold \(A\) with that primitive type and \(\operatorname{End}^0(A)=L\). Rational Hodge equivalence in weight one gives \(J(X)\sim A^5\), and then \(\operatorname{End}^0(J(X))\cong M_5(L)\).

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` enumerates every degree-three exponent vector, checks the five derivative weights, computes the thirty Hodge characters and their conjugates, and tests all sixty multipliers (equivalently twelve cosets). It terminates with `VERIFY_OK`. These are exhaustive finite computations, not experiments inferred to arbitrary size. The Hodge-theoretic and CM classification steps are mathematical deductions from established theorems and are not claimed to have independent software certification.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhu (October 29, 2026, Example 3.1 and the group classification) identifies the Klein quartic threefold and its automorphism group \(C_{61}\rtimes C_5\), but does not give an isogeny or rational endomorphism-algebra classification. González-Aguilera, Liendo, Montero and Villaflor Loyola (initial preprint December 26, 2022; published 2024) prove the more general character formula for extremal Klein-hypersurface Hodge structures in their Theorem 5.5. The thirty-character computation here is a specialization of their method, **not an independent novelty claim**. Their Hodge automorphism result and the present CM isogeny classification concern different invariants. Farb (2024) establishes that \(J(X)\) is not a product of curve Jacobians **as a principally polarized abelian variety**; an unpolarized isogeny to \(A^5\) does not contradict that theorem. Milne's CM classification supplies the general isogeny theory. The new exact step claimed here is the field-of-definition/stabilizer computation plus its consequent simple isogeny factor and full endomorphism algebra. Given the general Hodge spectral formula, this result should be understood as a specific derived structural classification; historical priority may require further specialist review.

## Limitations
No isomorphism of principally polarized abelian varieties is asserted. The principal polarization, integral endomorphism orders, arithmetic descent of \(A\), period matrices, and reductions in positive characteristic are not determined. The rational isogeny classification uses standard CM theory, not an explicit algebraic isogeny map. Any previously unlocated derivation of the same CM type and simple factor would reduce originality; independent validation and full priority assessment have not been performed.
