# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof explicitly computes the fourth iterate of the affine right branch, verifies that the switching formulas coincide on the boundary of the unit square, and shows that outside the square one of four rotational first-coordinate values is negative. This reduces all exterior dynamics to the exact one- or three-step horizontal-axis return maps. Elementary affine iteration gives the finite capture/escape classification and the three closed capture-time formulas, with correct endpoint conventions. The isolated period-three cycle lies in strict branches and has return eigenvalues \(a\) and zero. The exact-rational script checks these identities on finite grids; the proof does not rely on those grids for universal quantifiers.

## Originality

PASS, subject to historical priority risk. Simpson (arXiv:2606.08901v1, full accessible journal text) addresses the same normal form for \(\delta_R>1\), so its main first-return theorem cannot imply a result at the neutral equality \(\delta_R=1\). Its discussion makes the boundary significant, while not classifying the quarter-turn global basin, three-cycle separator, or capture counts. The earlier broad paper arXiv:2408.04790 was checked at the abstract/metadata level but not in full, because lawful full-text retrieval was unavailable; it remains a concrete risk and is not represented as NOT_COVERING. A related published neutral-focus return result concerns irrational, not rational, rotation and does not imply the fixed four-step rotation here. No claim of globally certified novelty is made.

## Value

PASS. The rational quarter-turn is a canonical neutral-focus boundary at which the repelling-focus Farey return theory ceases to apply. The finding gives a *complete full-plane* description, not just an isolated periodic orbit: a positive-area continuum of cycles, a sharp full-plane absorption threshold, a unique exterior repelling three-cycle, and exact transient scales. The change from logarithmic capture for \(a<1\) to linear capture at \(a=1\), the logarithmic separatrix slowdown for \(a>1\), and the exact two-sided critical scaling functions \(\log(1+s)\), \(s\), and \(-\log(1-s)\), provide quantitative geometry of the transition at infinity. The slice is special; this is deliberately not presented as a theorem for arbitrary rotations.
