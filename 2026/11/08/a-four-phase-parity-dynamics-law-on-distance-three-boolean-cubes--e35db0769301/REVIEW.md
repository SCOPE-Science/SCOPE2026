# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The complete theorem is derived from group-ring convolution and a symplectic normal-form calculation for multiplication by an alternating quadratic element. The coefficient expansion for the distance-three adjacency operator is explicitly checked in every residue class of \(n\bmod4\). The square-zero cases reduce all orbits to the zero state in at most two steps, and the involutive case reduces all orbits to period one or two. The nonstandard rank step is reconstructed through a direct tensor decomposition: a pair factor has one acyclic chain and two surviving homology classes. The kernel of \(A_n-I\) in the involutive phase is independently calculated with a two-block unit argument. Exact computer tests check finite cases but are not substituted for the infinite-family proof. Risk: an error in the change of variables would invalidate the rank; independently implemented algebraic and graph computations corroborate the critical identities.

**Originality — PASS (with literature limitation).** Searches of distance-three Hamming graphs, ranks of cubic Hamming-scheme adjacency matrices over characteristic two, parity dynamics and exterior quadratic multiplication found no covering result. Compared statements include Martin–Odlyzko–Wolfram (one-dimensional additive cellular automata), Peeters 2002 (distance-one Hamming-graph modular ranks), Hanaki–Yoshikawa 2005 (association schemes of class two only), Fish–Key–Mwambene 2009 (distance-one Hamming neighborhood codes), Yoshikawa 2002 symposium proceedings (full published modular Hamming-scheme algebra presentation) and 2004 journal abstract, and a published distance-two parity-census record. The directly inspected primary sources do not assert the present distance-three formulas. Yoshikawa's later 2004 journal full text and the full text of the distance-two record were not accessible and remain priority risks; the fully inspected 2003 proceedings result does not supply the standard-module distance-three rank; the available summaries were not used to assert full-document noncoverage. The new contribution is limited to the explicit four-phase distance-three standard-module rank/census, not general rank-nullity, convolution algebra, or small-graph examples.

**Value — PASS.** Hamming-distance shells are canonical finite graph families and modular adjacency ranks control the entire state transition diagram of their parity update. The third-distance shell exhibits sharp residue-class changes: a maximal-rank square-zero phase, a nearly maximal-rank square-zero phase with \(2^{(n+1)/2}\) homology, a quarter-rank phase with large homology, and an involutive phase with exact fixed/two-cycle counts. The nontrivial quadratic multiplication lemma explains the two different rank deficits for all dimensions, beyond a brute-force table. This is a full infinite-family natural invariant with a plausible role in modular Hamming-scheme representation and finite dynamical-system classifications, rather than a single unmotivated parameter calculation.
