# Self-review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The finite-dimensional nodal mass matrix follows exactly by integrating hat-function products on every unit edge. The three invariant symmetry blocks give an explicit inverse; its row coefficients yield the displayed exact positive/negative kernel integrals. For the competing vertex classes, a rigorous sign split at the singleton cross coefficient produces rational inequalities whose numerators and denominators are verified, using exact symbolic arithmetic, to have strictly positive polynomial coefficients after nonnegative-domain substitutions. The equality factor is the difference between class sizes. This proves the global norming-location theorem, not just a sampled instance. Exact coefficient certificates also establish the amplification-two cutoff. Interior edges are excluded by convexity and strict sign opposition when the endpoint maxima tie. The theorem's ranges and normalizations are explicit, and the 28 dense rational checks are independent corroboration, not substitutes for universal algebra.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** The relevant previously accessible graph claims concern complete bipartite projections, the balanced multipartite case, a complete-split family, and a general metric-graph upper bound; none implies the exact norm for \(K_{m,n,1}\) with both nonsingleton parts of size at least two, or its size-four threshold. The full texts of Oswald (arXiv:math/0611573) and Passenbrunner (arXiv:1103.1950) were inspected and treat higher-dimensional triangulations and periodic interval splines respectively. Focused searches of a published mathematical findings index returned related but non-covering results about abstract graph projection constants, simplices, and Lipschitz-free metrics. Boman's 2006 finite-element article was available only at abstract level and remains an explicitly identified risk; unindexed equivalent literature cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The result solves a natural two-parameter, genuinely non-tree, unbalanced metric-network projection problem, classifies all norming locations and extremizing input signs, and identifies the unexpectedly simple discrete threshold \(\max(m,n)=4\) despite a much more complicated rational norm. The result supplies an exact stability/positivity-amplification test for network-affine least squares. It is not a mere relabeling of the complete-bipartite kernel: the added singleton creates new cross-sign regimes and the two-dimensional part-size dependence.

## Limits

The proof uses a reproducing-kernel formula and exact symbolic polynomial certificates; no independent replication beyond the displayed exact computations has been performed. No claim is made for variable lengths, atoms, other multipartite profiles or higher-degree elements. The inaccessible Boman full text is an unresolved but not presently decisive literature risk.
