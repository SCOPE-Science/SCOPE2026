# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact seven-dimensional harmonic basis is orthogonal under the normalized sphere inner product. Tetrahedral sign-change and permutation symmetries annihilate every mixed concentration Gram entry, leaving one scalar sector and two three-dimensional sectors. Direct circular polynomial integration yields the three displayed seventh-degree eigenvalue polynomials. Their complete factored differences determine the top-eigenvalue transition and all equality subspaces. The standalone symbolic checker reconstructs the identities from the original polynomials; an independent seven-by-seven spherical cap quadrature checks all entries at five radii. Numerical results are supplementary to the proof. The scope excludes overlapping caps and mixed harmonic degrees.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
The closest earlier full spherical-cap results are the general Slepian formulation by Simons–Dahlen–Wieczorek (2004), the spectral large-sieve results by Speckbacher–Hrycak (2019) and Jaming–Speckbacher (2020), and the single-cap commuting-operator constructions of Michel–Plattner–Seibert (2021). Their inspected mathematical theorems do not give the exact tetrahedral four-cap cubic spectrum or the fourfold crossing. A prior degree-two simplex-cap classification covers a different harmonic degree, with a different two-branch scalar crossover; it does not imply the cubic one-plus-three-plus-three spectrum or this critical height. Full-sphere cubic curvature, hemispherical concentration, and filled-solid Platonic fourth moments also concern different operators. A specialized earlier tetrahedral-harmonic integration formula cannot be ruled out categorically.

## Value — PASS
This computes a natural complete Slepian spectrum on a disconnected, highly symmetric observation mask. It identifies a sharp competition between a tetrahedrally invariant cubic and an inequivalent three-dimensional cubic family, including an exact radical transition and an unusual four-dimensional top eigenspace. This changes which spatial mode yields greatest concentration and gives a benchmark for symmetry-adapted spherical sampling. The result is a finite-degree structural classification, not an assertion of a general high-bandlimit theorem.
