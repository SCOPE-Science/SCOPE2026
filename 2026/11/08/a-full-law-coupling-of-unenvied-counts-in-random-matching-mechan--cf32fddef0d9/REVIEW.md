# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The RSD unenvied count has an explicit transition \(q_{m,r}(h)=m(r)_h/(m+r)_{h+1}\), derived from uniform relative school ranks. The number of coupon singletons after each new distinct type is discovered obeys the identical transition by first-arrival symmetry. The DA-to-coupon singleton equality is pathwise under independent uniform student preferences and arbitrary fixed strict school priorities. The simultaneous unenvied-event probability has the product \(r!/\prod_s(n-i_s+s)\); summation yields the exact variance and factorial moments with exponential limiting moments. An executable, standalone exact-rational verifier checks finite distributions via separate combinatorial and mechanism algorithms. Finite verification does not replace the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
Ortega et al. (2026), Sections 2.1 and 3, demonstrate equality of the expectations \(H_n\), not equality of distributions, and give no RSD count-law coupling. Myers and Wilf (2003/2006), Section 4, already computed the coupon singleton law; that known formula is properly credited. The new mechanism-to-coupon coupling and distributional identity are not consequences of those two statements without additional analysis. Indexed research comparisons involving RSD, deferred acceptance, unenvied agents, coupon singletons, distributional equivalence, and exponential scaling yielded no matching theorem. Equivalent results in unindexed literature remain possible; no claim of definitive priority or independent review is made.

## Value — PASS
The full-law identity explains a structural coincidence between two canonical assignment procedures that earlier work had observed only at the mean level. It yields exact finite-sample prediction intervals and shows relative nonconcentration: despite a mean growing as \(\log n\), the unenvied count has fluctuations of the same order, with an exponential limiting shape. This is a natural matching-market fairness statistic rather than an arbitrary constructed invariant.

## Scientific limitations and evidence
The theorem requires independent uniform complete strict student preferences, square one-to-one matching and exogenous strict school priorities. It makes no claim for correlated or nonuniform preference profiles. Primary materials inspected include the full 2026 envy-distribution manuscript and the original Myers–Wilf singletons section; additional matching literature was checked for competing implications. Exhaustive tests cover only small markets. The full stochastic limit is proved analytically and awaits independent audit.
