## Originality — PASS, provisional

Abreu, Mouayn, and Voigtlaender (arXiv:2201.11705, Proposition 4.1 and Section 4) already provide the beta-prime eigenvalue formula for radial annulus unions in analytic-wavelet Bergman spaces. This is expressly **not** claimed as new. Their sharp norms concern families of fractal unions of annuli with growing hyperbolic area, not the single centered annulus of *constant* invariant area drifting to the boundary; they do not state the exact maximizing-frequency transition, pointwise gamma spectral law, all Schatten constants, or effective-rank limit. Ramos and Tilli (arXiv:2205.07998) determine the *global* maximizer among measurable sets of prescribed hyperbolic area, rather than the spectrum of this constrained, nonoptimal family. Abreu and Dörfler (arXiv:1202.5841) give inverse-domain information. Maximenko and Pacheco (arXiv:2503.23649) give radial operator diagonalization, not the present moving-annulus limits. A published harmonic Bergman circle-trace frequency staircase has a different trace functional, and a published hyperbolic-distance spectral-filling gap treats an unbounded cost, not a shrinking annular indicator. These are comparisons of mathematical hypotheses and conclusions, rather than title matching. Residual priority risk: an earlier unindexed asymptotic under beta-prime or Toeplitz terminology could cover the result.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The family is canonical for analytic-wavelet concentration and hyperbolic geometry: invariant area is held fixed while position escapes toward the geometric boundary. The result distinguishes two spectral facts that standard area estimates alone do not quantify: a fixed trace spreads over a growing number of frequencies, and the exact peak collapses at a computable rate. It simultaneously classifies every finite-position best monomial, the sharp norm coefficient, the full positive moment law, and the area-independent leading effective-rank coefficient. This is an exact structural description of spectral delocalization, not an arbitrary numerical parameter slice or a restatement of the known beta integral.
