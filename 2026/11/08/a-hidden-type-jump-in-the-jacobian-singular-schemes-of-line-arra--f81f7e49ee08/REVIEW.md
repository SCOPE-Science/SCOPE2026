# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The local unit reduction is intrinsic: the ideal generated by an affine defining equation and its derivatives is independent of multiplication by a unit. The tame calculation is a regular sequence of derivative forms. In the wild case, Euler gives \(F_u=vH\) and \(F_v=-uH\); squarefreeness ensures \(\gcd(F,H)=1\), so the explicit maximal-minor matrix has grade two. Hilbert–Burch yields the minimal resolution, identical tame/wild Hilbert series, and socle type two; the \(m=2\) boundary is separate. Exact finite-field computations reproduce the graded dimensions and the extra defining-equation generator for six nontrivial cases. A finite test is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The closest inspected published source on Jacobian ideals (Burity–Simis–Tohaneanu) restricts its main rank-two arguments to the characteristic-not-dividing-degree regime and explicitly discusses breakdown outside it; it does not supply the intrinsic Tjurina resolution or socle classification claimed here. Migliore–Nagel–Schenck treat an analogous arrangement singular-scheme problem over characteristic zero. Greuel–Pham develops positive-characteristic Tjurina-algebra reconstruction but not these exact line-arrangement local Betti tables. Targeted searches also compared aliases involving the local complete-intersection criterion, bad characteristic, and Cohen–Macaulay type. No direct covering assertion was found, but that is not a proof of historical priority; older singularity-theory accounts may contain an equivalent local calculation.

## Value — PASS
The family ranges over all reduced projective line arrangements and all intersection multiplicities and characteristics. The exact structural boundary distinguishes *Gorenstein vs non-Gorenstein* local singular schemes even though their lengths and every graded Hilbert-function value coincide. It provides complete minimal resolutions and two socle degrees in the wild range; this is scheme-theoretic information needed to understand deformation-theoretic Tjurina algebras and not recoverable from the classical point-multiplicity census alone. It does not rely on an arbitrary numerical slice or a tiny optimized improvement.
