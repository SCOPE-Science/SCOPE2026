# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For every integer \(d\ge2\) and \(0\le r\le1\), rotational invariance and uniqueness of the maximal-volume ellipsoid force a centered transverse ball and one axial radius. Exact support inequalities against the two caps and all lateral halfspaces yield the constraints displayed in the result. The strict one-variable optimization gives the two John regimes separated by \(r=(d-1)/(d+1)\). The reflection inclusion tests reduce to cap constraints and an exact side support bound, proving the global coefficient \(1/(2-r)\), its unique center height \(1/(3-r)\), and the three-piece John-center coefficient. The endpoint cone and cylinder are included. No finite test is used as a proof for an infinite parameter range.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority limitations
Xiong–Freund (2026, arXiv:2608.30876v1) motivate the distinction between local John-center symmetry and optimized Minkowski symmetry but do not give the frustum formula. Güler–Gürtuna (2007, arXiv:0709.0707v1, introduction and Section 8) treat the smallest *enclosing* ellipsoid of truncated second-order cones; the cited introduction expressly excludes their inscribed problem. The published note *Exact John ellipsoid of the Khachiyan half-cone* computes one ratio corresponding to \(r=d/(d+1)\), which lies in the larger-radius phase, and does not imply the global two-phase or local plateau results. The published frustum Santaló-volume-product work addresses a different extremal center. Brandenberg–Grundbacher (2025, arXiv:2501.00653v1) compare global/local asymmetries for other bodies. Claim-level published-finding corpus and literature searches are recorded in the structured audit. Older specialized optimization literature could contain an equivalent unlocated formula.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact John/contact transition on a natural one-parameter family of full-dimensional convex cones and cylinders identifies where a second cap starts constraining the optimal ellipsoid. The flat middle phase of the local symmetry coefficient despite a changing John center is a structural non-obvious phenomenon, and the complete comparison with the globally optimal Minkowski center establishes exact equality loci. This is a classification of a natural family, not a routine isolated parameter substitution.

## Limits
The geometry requires concentric Euclidean balls, height-one normalization up to affine scaling, \(d\ge2\), and \(0\le r\le1\). Finite regression checks do not establish universal implications. Priority beyond inspected sources is not certified.
