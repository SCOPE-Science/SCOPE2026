# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact basis actions are derived from \(W_kz^{kn}=z^n\) with the weighted Bergman monomial norms. The two composite maps select the congruence classes of \(b+ka\) and \(a+kb\) modulo \(k^2\). Their row weights converge to \(k^{\alpha+1}\) for every real \(\alpha>-1\). Hence the two self-Gram operators are scalar modulo compact operators; the cross-Gram operators vanish exactly for disjoint residues or are compact perturbations of a fixed bilateral direction in the Calkin unitary. The full unit-circle spectrum of the unilateral shift's Calkin class yields exactly the asserted singleton or interval and essential norms. The claimed right-invertibility statement follows from the C*-algebraic Gram criterion. At \(\alpha=0\) the strict finite-coordinate inequalities and norm-limit lower bounds give the exact unattained ordinary norms. The finite standalone checks corroborate, rather than replace, those infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)

The 2026 paper by Cui, Li and Lu examines qualitative commutativity, zero products and finite rank, not the explicit Calkin Gram and norm formulas. Liu and Lu (2013) study products and commutativity without the essential-spectrum law. The 2010 Lu–Liu–Yang work proves a qualitative essential-commutativity correspondence, so its stated result does not directly entail the different essential-norm constants or the full Gram spectra; however, only its abstract and cited descriptions were accessible. Singh, Singh and Singh (2025) discuss spectral properties of related Bergman decimators, with a normalization distinction. Relevant scientific-index searches found other operator commutators, not the same decimation-residue theorem. A more general result in earlier work could imply the theorem; priority remains unverified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result completely classifies the Calkin Gram spectrum, right-invertibility and essential norm for all pairs of distinct monomial symbols for every integer slant order and power Bergman weight. The sharp \(k^2\)-divisibility boundary separates invertible and noninvertible Gram elements and changes the essential norm by a fixed factor. The exact, unattained unweighted norm resolves a natural quantitative endpoint. These distinctions are not consequences of qualitative noncommutativity alone.
