# Review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact fiber law follows bijectively by separating odd-valued and even-valued parts, then halving even parts. The local limit follows from the standard asymptotics of unrestricted and odd-part partitions: their exponential saddle is uniquely at equal total odd and even mass, the quadratic coefficient is \(\pi/(4\sqrt6)\), and the admissible signed masses have lattice spacing four. A concavity argument provides Gaussian central tails and exponentially suppressed edge tails, establishing both global convergence and convergence of moments. The variance coefficient and balanced atom follow with no appeal to finite enumeration as infinite evidence. The independently computed exact fiber histograms and direct partition enumeration agree on the specified finite ranges.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk

The exact part-splitting factorization is an elementary step and not treated as a standalone novelty. Andrews–Ghosh Dastidar and Bardhan–Saikia study aggregate signed odd/even mass through identities, modular congruences, and monotonicity, without stating a per-partition lattice-local distribution or sharp fluctuation scale. Craig–Li alternates signs by sorted part **position**, rather than part-value parity. Li–Zhao and Bringmann–Nazaroglu–van Ittersum study **counts** of parts in residue classes, not their signed total size. Rausch–Stumpenhusen treats aggregate signed mass in a restricted family. Indexed mathematical research results with similar titles involve unrelated weighted-representation counts, not the product of unrestricted and odd-part partition numbers here. Prior general limit theorems for linear partition statistics remain a possible implication route requiring historical scrutiny.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A recently studied arithmetic parity-mass statistic is resolved at the **distributional** level, with exact finite fibers, a nontrivial \(n^{3/4}\) fluctuation scale, a lattice-sensitive Gaussian density, and an asymptotic for exact odd/even mass balance. The distinction between near-equal total masses and equality is mathematically meaningful: the latter is arithmetically forbidden on three of four integer classes and is asymptotically rare on the fourth. The result goes beyond arithmetic congruences for the first moment and beyond qualitative central-limit convergence.
