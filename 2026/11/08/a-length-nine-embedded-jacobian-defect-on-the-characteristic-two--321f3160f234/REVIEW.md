# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The derivative ideal must include the defining quartic, and it does. On the \(W=1\) chart it reduces to the radical coordinate-axis ideal. On each of three complementary charts, the explicit quotient ring admits the complete normal form \(f(a)+\alpha b+\beta c+\gamma b^2\), giving a three-dimensional nilradical with graded lengths \((2,1)\) killed by the line parameter. All projective charts are covered, hence no additional defects can occur. The three length-\(3\) defects add to \(9\), and the exact sequence with the three-line reduced curve establishes \(P_J(d)=3d+10\). The accompanying exact-arithmetic checker confirms the generators and finite Hilbert-function values but is not the infinite argument.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The reduced three-line singular set, quartic equation and normalization are already in Hartshorne–Polini, Example 6.4, and are explicitly excluded from the novel claim. Kleiman (1977) already gives multiplicity two for characteristic-two Steiner pinch-point ramification, but the *source ramification cycle* is a different scheme/invariant from the nilradical of the *target surface's Jacobian singular scheme*. Their claims do not state the length-\(9\) image defect, its \((2,1)\) layers, or the Hilbert polynomial. Mathematical-record searches of aliases, characteristic, Hilbert invariants, singular scheme and ramification, together with comparison to accessible scientific literature, did not identify a covering theorem. A potentially related older projection paper could not be inspected in full; priority remains qualified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural scheme-theoretic singular invariant of a classical, geometrically motivated quartic in a difficult characteristic, not a change of notation or a finite numerical coincidence. The exact nilpotent filtration shows where the three classical paired pinch points leave *embedded target-Jacobian* information when the characteristic becomes two. The computed Hilbert polynomial is different from that of the reduced double-curve configuration and informs comparison of schemes in degeneration and polar/Jacobian invariants. The three-chart structural classification, not merely derivative vanishing at one point, is the core value.
