# A length-six generic thickening of Fermat fourfold line families

## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge 5\), consider the smooth Fermat hypersurface
\[
X_d=\bigl\{x_0^d+x_1^d+x_2^d+x_3^d+x_4^d+x_5^d=0\bigr\}\subset\mathbb P^5_{\mathbb C}.
\]
For \(\zeta^d=-1\), let \(Q_d\) be the two-dimensional family of lines joining \([1:\zeta:0:0:0:0]\) to arbitrary points of the Fermat surface \(x_2^d+x_3^d+x_4^d+x_5^d=0\) in the complementary coordinate three-space. Then \(Q_d\) is an irreducible component of the scheme of lines \(F_1(X_d)\), and **its generic scheme-theoretic multiplicity is six**. More precisely, the local Artin ring at its generic point has associated-graded Hilbert function \((1,2,2,1)\). This holds for all \(d\ge5\), not just the Calabi–Yau case.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). The dimension of the Fano scheme of lines is exactly two for \(d\ge5\): the cone family has dimension two, and Yu's dimension theorem [1, Theorem 1.3] bounds all components by two (for \(d=5\), it gives dimension \(2\); for \(d\ge6\), its high-degree bound gives dimension at most \(2\)). The Fermat surface \(Q_d\) is smooth and irreducible. We describe its generic, rather than every closed-point, local scheme structure. Coordinate permutations and diagonal rescalings reduce all choices of the first pair and \(\zeta\) to the displayed one.

## Proof
**Affine Grassmann chart and equations.** At a general point of \(Q_d\), all four coordinates of the complementary Fermat surface are nonzero. Scale one to one, writing the point \([1:v_3:v_4:v_5]\) with \(1+v_3^d+v_4^d+v_5^d=0\). A nearby line has the unique parametrization
\[
(s,t)\longmapsto(s,as+bt,t,c_3s+v_3t,c_4s+v_4t,c_5s+v_5t),
\]
with \(a\) near \(\zeta\), \(b,c_j\) near zero, and \(v_j\) near their fixed values. The coefficient of \(s^kt^{d-k}\) in the Fermat equation, divided by \(\binom dk\) for \(0<k<d\), is
\[
E_k=a^kb^{d-k}+\sum_{j=3}^5c_j^kv_j^{d-k}\qquad (1\le k\le d-1).
\]
The endpoint equations are \(E_0=1+b^d+v_3^d+v_4^d+v_5^d=0\) and \(E_d=1+a^d+c_3^d+c_4^d+c_5^d=0\).

The complex formal implicit-function theorem applies to these four equations, in the following order: \(E_0\) determines \(v_5\), \(E_d\) determines \(a\), \(E_1\) determines \(c_5\), and \(E_{d-1}\) determines \(b\). Their respective leading derivatives in the eliminated variables are \(dv_5^{d-1}\), \(d\zeta^{d-1}\), \(v_5^{d-1}\), and \(\zeta^{d-1}\), all nonzero. Thus the completed local equations reduce to the residual conditions \(E_2,\ldots,E_{d-2}\) in the two transverse variables \(c_3,c_4\), over the smooth base coordinates \(v_3,v_4\). The eliminated \(b\) vanishes to transverse order \(d-1\); the shifts of \(a,v_5\) begin in higher transverse orders. The eliminated \(c_5\) begins linearly.

**Initial forms and a nonvanishing witness.** Put \(r_j=c_j/v_j\) and \(w_j=v_j^d\) for \(j=3,4,5\), so that the first-order coefficient equation gives \(w_3r_3+w_4r_4+w_5r_5=0\). Up to invertible scalar factors, the first nonzero transverse homogeneous parts of \(E_2,E_3\) are
\[
q_2=\sum_{j=3}^5w_jr_j^2,\qquad q_3=\sum_{j=3}^5w_jr_j^3,
\]
with \(r_5\) eliminated using the displayed linear relation. To prove these forms have no common zero in \(\mathbb P^1\) generically, exhibit a point of the Fermat surface with \(v_3^d=v_4^d=1\) and \(v_5^d=-3\) (the scaled coordinate \(x_2\) has \(d\)-th power one). Set \(u=r_3,v=r_4\); then \(r_5=(u+v)/3\), and
\[
q_2=\frac23(u^2-uv+v^2),\qquad
q_3=\frac19(u+v)(8u^2-11uv+8v^2).
\]
Their dehomogenized resultant is exactly \(8/81\ne0\). Hence coprimeness holds on a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the irreducible surface \(Q_d\) for each \(d\ge5\).

**Complete intersection and length.** In two variables, homogeneous coprime forms of degrees two and three are a regular sequence. The initial ideal generated by \(q_2,q_3\) has Hilbert series
\[
\frac{(1-t^2)(1-t^3)}{(1-t)^2}=1+2t+2t^2+t^3,
\]
and contains every homogeneous polynomial of degree at least four. Standard initial-ideal division (or filtered Nakayama) shows that the full power-series ideal \((E_2,E_3)\) has the same associated graded and annihilates the fourth power of the transverse maximal ideal. For \(d=5\), no additional residual equations occur; for \(d\ge6\), each \(E_k\) with \(k\ge4\) has transverse order at least four, so is already in \((E_2,E_3)\). Thus the generic completed transverse local algebra has Hilbert function \((1,2,2,1)\) and length \(6\). Its generic localization along the irreducible component \(Q_d\) has the same length, proving the result.

## Relationship to prior work
Yu [1] proves the sharp global dimension bound and recalls the Fermat cone family as a sharpness example. His full text also gives the normal bundle of a Fermat ruling line and thus predicts excess tangent directions, but not the generic transverse Artin algebra or its length. Ciliberto–Zaidenberg [2, Section 2] review the existence and dimension of Fermat hypersurface line families; their discussion does not calculate the scheme multiplicity for the degree-\(d\) Fermat fourfold in \(\mathbb P^5\). Albano–Katz [3] analyze deformations of lines on the Fermat quintic **threefold in \(\mathbb P^4\)**, a different ambient dimension, and motivate the need to distinguish the geometric family from its scheme structure. A historical priority check beyond the accessible primary texts remains desirable.

## Limitations
This is a generic local statement about designated components, not a classification of all lines, component intersections, special closed-point germs, or embedded structures elsewhere. It uses characteristic zero and the sharp global dimension theorem of [1]. No claim about integral models or characteristic-\(p\) behavior is made. Related older primary text [3] was available in abstract/bibliography form but its complete publisher PDF was access-restricted; this is a residual priority risk, not a claim of a full-text negative search.
