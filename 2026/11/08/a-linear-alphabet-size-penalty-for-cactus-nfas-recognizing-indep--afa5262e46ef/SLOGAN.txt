For the \(k\)-letter language requiring every letter to occur an even number of times, an unrestricted NFA needs exactly \(2^k\) states, whereas any epsilon-free, single-initial-state cactus NFA needs at least \(1+3k2^{k-3}\) states for \(k\ge3\). The forced Boolean-cube edge lifts and the sparse edge structure of bipartite cacti give an unbounded linear-in-alphabet state penalty.
