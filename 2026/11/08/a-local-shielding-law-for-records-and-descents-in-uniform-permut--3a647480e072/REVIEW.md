# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

Adjacent swaps leave every off-pair upper-record indicator unchanged while toggling the paired descent. Commuting swaps on a matching prove the full conditional product law, with all conditioning events of positive probability. Conditioning on a record at the first endpoint gives the maximum-of-uniforms mean \(j/(j+1)\); a record at the second endpoint forces an ascent. These cases prove every entry of the covariance formula and its telescoping sum. The standard record-indicator and descent variance formulas yield the stated correlation asymptotic. The standalone exact enumerator checked every permutation through nine positions and all disjoint-site conditioning patterns through seven.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Gnedin's 2006 arXiv paper concerns record-based random permutations and their coherent laws, not conditional descent independence. Jin's 2022 full-text paper handles global Euler–Stirling generating functions and thus may cover the total record–descent covariance, but does not retain position-indexed records and does not imply the conditional matching-site shielding statement as an aggregate identity. The published fixed-point–descent locality theorem was read in full and concerns fixed points rather than prefix records; its position-wise covariance and boundary cancellation do not imply the matching-site conditional product law. Search cannot establish priority, and a positional multivariate enumeration may dominate the theorem; this is a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

A record indicator depends on the entire prefix, so independence of an adjacent descent from *all* off-pair record indicators jointly is not the usual locality of two coordinate events. The commuting-swap theorem extends this to arbitrary nonoverlapping comparisons, giving a structural conditional product law rather than only a single numerical covariance. Its exact two-diagonal matrix and strong negative total covariance explain how records and descents interact through just a few local coordinates, useful for mixed moment calculations and record-aware permutation sampling.

## Limitations

Uniformity and exchangeability are essential. The global bivariate record–descent distribution is studied in existing literature and is not claimed as wholly new. Historical priority for the conditional position-level statement requires further independent review.
