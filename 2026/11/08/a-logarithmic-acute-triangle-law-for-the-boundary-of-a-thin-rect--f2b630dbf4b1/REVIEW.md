# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The boundary measure is specified and the three-point event is acute only when all scalar products at triangle vertices are strictly positive. The six labeled allocations with a pair on one long edge and a singleton on its opposite yield exactly the integral in the result. The independent verification program confirms both elementary antiderivatives and their high-aspect expansions. Every allocation involving a short edge has an explicit upper bound of lower asymptotic order; no unproved exhaustive numerical assertion is used.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

The closest same-model accessible computation is Joriki's 2012 complete square-boundary calculation, giving the fixed value \(23/96\). It does not give the high-aspect thin-rectangle law. Langford's 1969 arbitrary-rectangle formula is for interior-coordinate-uniform sampling, not boundary arclength; Hall's 1982 result is also for interior sampling. The inspected published-record searches did not reveal an equivalent or stronger boundary-rectangle result. Full texts of some older sources were not available, so historical priority is provisional rather than certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Boundary sampling on degenerating convex bodies is a natural counterpart to classical interior-sampling problems. A sharp \((\log L)/L^2\) law with a determined constant correction is structurally distinct from a fixed-square probability: it arises from a resonant near-collinearity geometry and a unique dominant edge-occupancy class. The finite-aspect error bound makes the asymptotic quantitatively interpretable.

## Limitations

The total finite-aspect probability is bracketed, not evaluated in closed form. The bound applies to Euclidean rectangles, three independent normalized-arclength boundary points, and aspect ratios at least two. Numerical tests are supplementary. No independent historical or mathematical audit has been performed.
