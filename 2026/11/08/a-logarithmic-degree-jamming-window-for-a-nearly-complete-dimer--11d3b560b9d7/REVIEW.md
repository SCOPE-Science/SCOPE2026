# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The graph leaves at most one ordinary site empty; even parity forces zero or two vacancies. An exact finite first-edge recursion and its random-permutation representation are derived without assuming independent hypergeometric stages. For a uniformly chosen neighborhood, the cumulative hazard has expectation \(\alpha H_{(N-2)/2}\), variance bounded by an absolute constant times \(\alpha\), and a logarithm-approximation error with expectation bounded by an absolute constant times \(\alpha+\alpha^2\). These estimates prove the full finite/infinite critical-window trichotomy. The boundary degrees are included. The standalone checker performs 102 exact and illustrative consistency checks. The general proof does not rest on finite extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS on examined material; historical-priority risk remains
The closest primary model framework is Penrose–Sudbury (2005), whose read definitions and main results concern general event-order dynamics, forward equations and regular trees, not the exceptional-vertex logarithmic-degree window. Bennett–Bohman (2012/2019) proves results under global regularity and does not determine a two-vacancy probability here. Dyer–Frieze (1991) covers randomized greedy matching at a general level but was inspectable only through its abstract: the unread paper is a specific residual risk rather than evidence of noncoverage. Exact searches for special-vertex, near-clique and logarithmic degree formulations returned no observed implication of the claim. A prior bounded-degree-deletion result does not cover the forbidden star, whose maximum deletion degree is unbounded; a complete split graph has a different adjacency pattern. No absence-of-search-results is treated as a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The model is a dense adsorbing substrate with one kinetically disadvantaged site, a natural worst-case probe of greedy maximal matching. The degree scale \(N/\log N\) produces a nontrivial limiting success/failure curve, rather than only a vanishing or unit probability. Its significance is sharper because a perfect matching exists for every positive exceptional degree: algorithmic failure and combinatorial feasibility are sharply separated. The proof supplies a mechanism through accumulated survival hazards and an exact finite recurrence.

## Scope and residual risks
The finding is limited to even order, unit edge rates, one exceptional vertex and terminal jamming; broader graph statements and dynamic mixing assertions are not proved. Priority relative to inaccessible parts of older randomized matching literature remains unresolved; the accessible primary full texts and published claim summaries do not establish the same threshold.
