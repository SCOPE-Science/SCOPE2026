# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The exact recurrence follows from a complete partition of currently available edges; a jammed state has zero or two vacant vertices because the core is complete and total order is even. Conditioning on no hub-edge acceptance gives an exact random-permutation product for the probability of failure. The cumulative hazard has mean \(\frac{m}{n-1}H_{(n-2)/2}\), variance \(O(m/n)\), and logarithmic remainder controlled by \(O(m/n+(m/n)^2)\). These facts prove the \(e^{-c}\) crossover; monotonicity treats the supercritical window. An additional telescoping product proves the single-forbidden-edge finite identity. Labeled and lumped exact calculations agree on all checked small sizes.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, provisional

The closest general uniform-edge work is Dyer–Frieze (1991), whose available abstract emphasizes matching-performance worst cases and forests; its unavailable full text leaves historical priority risk. Bennett–Bohman (arXiv:1210.3581) assumes regular hypergraphs and estimates an unmatched fraction, which does not entail this nonregular graph's constant-order terminal defect probability. Besser–Poloczek (arXiv:1505.04198) studies approximation bounds, predominantly MinGreedy rather than the same terminal event. The familiar one-missing-edge limit is not the distinct new claim. Database and scientific literature searches found no equivalent \(n/\log n\) rescue law, though this is not proof of exhaustive novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Every positive hub degree permits a perfect matching, yet much larger than constant degree is required for uniform-edge irreversible adsorption to find it with high probability. The exact finite law and \(m\asymp n/\log n\) scaling resolve the natural kinetic question of when a single defective vertex is rescued by a dense adsorption substrate. The result exposes a rare-event threshold invisible to macroscopic coverage statistics, which always tend to one. This is a structural crossover rather than a routine finite table entry or a claim of bulk criticality.
