# A MacMahon correction and an exact minimal-generator formula for endomorphisms of finite transformation monoids

## Finding

For every integer \(n\geq 5\), let \(\mathcal T_n\) be the monoid of all maps \([n]\to[n]\), and let \(\mathcal E_n=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{sgp}}(\mathcal T_n)\) be its monoid of *semigroup* endomorphisms (not necessarily identity-preserving). Let \(m_n\) be the minimum cardinality of a generating set of \(\mathcal E_n\). Write
\[
P(z)=\prod_{j\geq1}(1-z^j)^{-1},\quad
Q(z)=\prod_{j\geq2}(1-z^j)^{-\lfloor j/2\rfloor},\quad
R(z)=\frac{zP(z)Q(z)}{1-z},\quad R_0(z)=\frac{zP(z)}{1-z}.
\]
For a formal power series \(H(z)\) with zero constant term, define its unlabeled-multiset transform by
\[
\mathcal M(H)(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{H(z^k)}{k}\right).
\]
Then the following **exact all-degree enumeration** holds:
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle m_n=3+[z^n]\left(\mathcal M(R)-\mathcal M(R_0)
+\prod_{j\geq1}(1-z^j)^{-j}-P(z)\right).}
\]
More precisely, the number \(r_3(n)\) of rank-three unit-group orbits in \(\mathcal E_n\), and the number \(r_2(n)\) of essential rank-two unit-group orbits, satisfy
\[
\boxed{r_3(n)=[z^n](\mathcal M(R)-\mathcal M(R_0)),\qquad
r_2(n)=\operatorname{pp}(n)-p(n),}
\]
where \(p(n)\) is the ordinary integer-partition number and \(\operatorname{pp}(n)\) is the plane-partition number, with generating products \(P(z)\) and \(\prod_{j\geq1}(1-z^j)^{-j}\), respectively. In particular, the minimum generator numbers for \(n=5,6,7,8,9,10\) are \(35,86,207,503,1206,2880\).

These formulas resolve the *exact counting* part of the rank-orbit problem posed in Section 8 of Gould–Grau–Johnson (2023). No sharp asymptotic for \(m_n\) is asserted here.

## Assumptions and scope

We use the finite transformation semigroup \(\mathcal T_n\) under composition, and ordinary semigroup endomorphisms. The relevant classification from Gould–Grau–Johnson identifies singular endomorphisms, for \(n\ge5\), with distinct pairs \((t,e)\) of full transformations satisfying
\[
t^3=t,\qquad e^2=e,\qquad te=et=e.
\]
Its unit group is \(S_n\), acting on such pairs by simultaneous conjugation. Endomorphism *rank* means the size of its image as a map \(\mathcal T_n\to\mathcal T_n\), not the rank of \(t\). Rank-three orbits are essential. A rank-two orbit is *essential* if it cannot be generated by elements of strictly larger endomorphism rank. The published Proposition 4.2 gives \(m_n=3+r_3(n)+r_2(n)\). Our new work enumerates both orbit terms explicitly. The case \(n=4\) has additional exceptional endomorphisms and is excluded from the generator formula.

All displayed products and exponentials are interpreted as formal power series, so each fixed coefficient depends on finitely many factors.

## Proof

**1. Functional components and red fibers.** In a map \(t\) satisfying \(t^3=t\), every connected component of the underlying functional digraph is either a fixed vertex with \(a\ge0\) incoming leaves, or a two-cycle whose two vertices have \(a,b\ge0\) incoming leaves. This follows because every point maps into a one- or two-cycle after one application. The two-cycle component has isomorphism type \(\{a,b\}\), independent of which cycle vertex is first, so there are exactly \(\lfloor j/2\rfloor\) such component types on \(j\ge2\) vertices.

For \(e^2=e=te=et\), every image point of \(e\) is a fixed point of \(t\). Moreover \(e\) is constant on each functional component of \(t\). Conversely, choosing at least one fixed point of \(t\) as an \(e\)-root, assigning each functional component to one chosen root, and fixing every root gives exactly such an \(e\).

Partition the colored functional graph into the fibers of \(e\) over its fixed roots. In an individual fiber there is one distinguished fixed-point component of \(t\) (the component containing the root), an arbitrary multiset of *other* fixed-point components, and an arbitrary multiset of two-cycle components. The distinguished component contributes \(z/(1-z)\). The other fixed-point components contribute \(P(z)\), while two-cycle components contribute \(Q(z)\). Thus the ordinary generating function for isomorphism types of a single rooted fiber is precisely \(R(z)\).

The different fibers form an unlabeled multiset, so their full orbit generating series is \(\mathcal M(R)\). If \(t\) has no two-cycle, delete the \(Q(z)\) factor; the orbit series becomes \(\mathcal M(R_0)\). The published rank classification identifies a rank-three singular endomorphism exactly by the presence of a two-cycle. Subtraction proves the formula for \(r_3(n)\).

**2. Characterization of essential rank-two orbits.** Rank-two singular endomorphisms correspond to \((u,e)\) with \(u^2=u\) and \(u\ne e\). The multiplication rule in Gould–Grau–Johnson, Corollary 2.6, shows that a product of two rank-three singular endomorphisms can have rank two only when an even-permutation-type left factor acts on a right factor \((t,e)\) by \((t,e)\mapsto(t^2,e)\). For every \(n\ge5\), an even-permutation-type rank-three left factor exists: take a product of two disjoint transpositions fixing one point, with \(e\) constant onto that fixed point. Therefore \((u,e)\) is nonessential exactly when there is a pair \((t,e)\) with \(t^3=t\), at least one two-cycle, and \(t^2=u\).

Such a square root exists **if and only if** one \(e\)-fiber contains at least two distinct \(u\)-fixed points outside \(\operatorname{im}(e)\). Necessity follows because each two-cycle of \(t\) becomes two fixed points of \(t^2\), both have the same \(e\)-image, and neither is an \(e\)-root. For sufficiency, choose such fixed points \(a,b\) in the same \(e\)-fiber. Define \(t(x)=b\) whenever \(u(x)=a\), define \(t(x)=a\) whenever \(u(x)=b\), and otherwise put \(t(x)=u(x)\). Direct substitution gives \(t^2=u\), \(t^3=t\), \(et=te=e\), and \(a,b\) form a two-cycle. Conjugation does not change the criterion. Intermediate factors or units cannot circumvent it: whenever a product of higher-rank elements first drops to rank two, the rule above exhibits such a square; subsequent rank-preserving multiplication produces another such square or its conjugate.

Thus the essential rank-two pairs are those with no two-cycle in \(u\), with *at most one* extra \(u\)-fixed center in each \(e\)-fiber, and with \(u\ne e\). A fiber of size \(j\ge1\) under this restriction has exactly \(j\) unlabeled types: there is one with no extra center, and \(j-1\) with exactly one extra center, distinguished by how the \(j-2\) remaining leaves are divided between the root center and the extra center. The fiber-type generating series is therefore
\[
\sum_{j\ge1} j z^j=\frac{z}{(1-z)^2}.
\]
Taking unlabeled multisets gives
\[
\mathcal M\left(\frac{z}{(1-z)^2}\right)=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-z^j)^{-j}.
\]
The excluded pairs \(u=e\) have only the root center in each fiber; their generating series is \(\mathcal M(z/(1-z))=P(z)\). Subtracting proves \(r_2(n)=\operatorname{pp}(n)-p(n)\).

**3. Minimum rank.** Substitute the two exact orbit formulas into the previously established identity \(m_n=3+r_3(n)+r_2(n)\) for every \(n\ge5\). This proves the displayed theorem.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library. The checker computes the Euler products from logarithmic-derivative recurrences, independently enumerates all labeled solutions of \(t^3=t\) and \(e^2=e=te=et\) through \(n=6\), constructs all possible squares of two-cycle maps to determine rank-two essentiality, and counts simultaneous-conjugacy classes by direct permutation canonicalization through \(n=5\) and by conjugacy-class Burnside summation at \(n=6\). It checks \((r_3(5),r_2(5))=(15,17)\), \((r_3(6),r_2(6))=(46,37)\), and prints `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-degree combinatorial proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Gould, Grau, and Johnson (arXiv:2307.11596v1, posted July 21, 2023) prove the structural classification, the orbit-to-generator identity \(m_n=3+r_3(n)+r_2(n)\), and describe the relevant colored digraphs. Their Section 8 identifies formulas for the two orbit numbers as an open counting task. The novel component here is the explicit finite-product/multiset enumeration of the rank-three orbits and the plane-partition-minus-partition formula for the essential rank-two orbits, which together determine \(m_n\) at every \(n\ge5\). Standard Euler transforms and MacMahon's generating function for plane partitions are established background, not claimed as new. The *numerical sequence* \(\operatorname{pp}(n)-p(n)\) already occurs as OEIS A097391, described there as a count of two-level hierarchical orderings (Wieder, 2004); neither that previously known sequence nor its original hierarchy interpretation is claimed as new. The new statement identifies it with the **essential rank-two orbits** in this particular endomorphism monoid and combines that identification with an independent rank-three orbit enumeration to solve the minimum-generator problem. Schein–Teclezghi (1998) classifies and counts *individual endomorphisms*, not the minimum generating rank. No assertion of absolute publication priority is made.

## Limitations

The generator formula is proved only for \(n\ge5\), because the published structure theory has exceptional behavior at \(n=4\). It uses semigroup endomorphisms, not the smaller monoid of identity-preserving endomorphisms. The result is an exact enumerative answer, not a sharp growth asymptotic for the generator rank. The 1998 original article and the potentially overlapping 2023 dissertation could not be inspected in full; an undiscovered later analysis or an equivalent orbit enumeration remains a publication-priority risk. Numerical computations through \(n=6\) are finite cross-checks, not infinite proofs.
