# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is quantified over all integers \(a\ge4\), \(b\ge2\), and \(1\le t\le\lfloor a/2\rfloor\). A within-coalition enemy makes an agent's utility negative; therefore positive-product coalitions must be independent sets of the enemy graph. A sharp independent-set cap and convex majorization of \(s\log(s-1)\) give the exact optimum. Matching transversals construct every extremal size pattern; symmetry correction gives the exact labeled optimizer count. Finite exhaustive testing checks 37 instances across 772,452 partitions and is not substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Pagano and Schlenga, *Nash Welfare in Additively Separable Hedonic Games* (arXiv:2605.19030, 18 May 2026), provide the main model, a general positive-welfare criterion, hardness and two/three-coalition results. The accessible description does not imply the present product/optimizer-count formula or first-two-edge plateau. Elkind, Flammini and Varricchio (arXiv:2512.20261, 23 December 2025) optimize egalitarian instead of Nash welfare. Relevant graph-indexed results count different invariants. Related recoverable exact statements address odd cycles and another bipartite family with different cross-part edges. The central paper's full primary text could not be inspected, and unindexed coverage remains a priority risk. An unsuccessful search is not novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The construction is a canonical matching-edge perturbation of a complete bipartite enemy network. It quantifies the price of adding incompatibilities with exact products, demonstrates a sharp first-to-second-edge plateau despite optimizer-multiplicity changes, and classifies every optimum in a natural infinite graph family. It directly probes the three-coalition complexity boundary rather than an arbitrary finite parameter slice.

## Limitations
Only symmetric \(+1\) and \(-N\) pairwise utilities, complete cross-part hostility, and within-part matching perturbations are included. The ratios are of Nash products rather than their geometric-mean roots. The theorem does not address arbitrary edge additions or incentive stability.
