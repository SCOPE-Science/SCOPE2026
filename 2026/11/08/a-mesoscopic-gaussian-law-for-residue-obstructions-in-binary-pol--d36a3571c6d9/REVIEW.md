# Scientific review: mesoscopic polynomial-residue obstruction law

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The theorem defines one observable \(W_d\) on uniformly sampled monic degree-\(d\) binary polynomials, with explicit admissible degrees, prime cutoff, and total-variation bound. The prime-local trace probabilities were reconstructed from finite-field inversion and the principal-unit trace action, and the high-valuation truncation was checked against the dependence on \(f\bmod P^{2e}\). The Chinese remainder degree budget is strict for every \(d\ge2^{16}\). Independent Bernoulli coupling, irreducible polynomial counts, and Berry–Esseen then establish the claimed Gaussian and zero laws. The included exact-arithmetic program corroborates the simple- and double-pole calculations. Numerical approximations are not substituted for infinite proofs.

**Originality (O): PASS, with priority limits.** The central 2026 source proves one-prime residue distributions and globally conjectures an independence model; it does not prove a growing-dimensional, quantified product approximation for the residue obstruction indicators, the resulting Gaussian law, or the explicit zero-event constant. The 2020 survey focuses on perfect linear complexity profiles and related sequences. The relevant 2017 thesis sections discuss orthogonal multiplicity and finite-field continued fractions but do not state the mesoscopic residue law. The present result uses the source's established one-prime theorem and standard tools, and does not claim these ingredients as discoveries. Search-based novelty is not a proof of priority: stronger general polynomial-local sieve theorems may imply the result. The full 1998 Blackburn article was not directly available; its relevant statements were checked in accessible expositions.

**Mathematical value (V): PASS.** The original paper explicitly identifies global pooling of local residue conditions as unresolved. This theorem establishes an unconditional near-linear-dimensional slice of that pooling: all irreducibles below an explicit growing degree cutoff jointly follow a product law with \(O(d^{-2})\) error, despite the global reciprocity constraint. The precise cancellation giving Bernoulli parameters \((|P|^{-1}+|P|^{-2})/2\) yields a new quantitative fluctuation law and a computable sieve survival constant. These facts provide a reproducible finite-degree benchmark for future studies of the full problem, not just a small exceptional example. They do **not** resolve the conjectural criterion connecting all residue constraints to positive orthogonal multiplicity.
