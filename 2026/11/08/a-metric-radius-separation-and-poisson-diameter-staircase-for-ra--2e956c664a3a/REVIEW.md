# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
For the unique enclosing ball, any selected-vertex convex representation of the cube center forces every shifted ball center to incur the additional nonnegative squared displacement. An antipodal halfspace count and a hyperplane sign-pattern bound show center capture in both regimes, because the sample intensity grows faster than the square of dimension. For polynomial-size selections, a Hamming-distance Chernoff bound plus one uniformly random selected pair gives the normalized diameter. At the near-antipodal scale, the full graph of pairs with Hamming deficit at most a fixed integer is regular, and its retained-edge count has Poisson factorial moments; a first- and second-moment sandwich pins the maximal Hamming distance into exactly two layers. The antipodal layer has an exact independent-edge product formula. Assumptions include fixed parameters and asymptotic dimension; finite checks are corroboration only.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority
Actual mathematical-record searches for randomly retained Boolean-cube vertices, Hamming pair extremes, near-antipodal Poisson windows and minimum enclosing spheres returned no covering statement. The closest inspected full texts concern either a *different* random base polytope (Newman, arXiv:2006.07000) or continuous random points on polytope boundaries (Reitzner–Schütt–Werner, arXiv:1911.05917). The Dyer–Füredi–McDiarmid source addresses expected volume, rather than the joint radius/diameter behavior. Existing general concentration, halfspace-count and Poisson methods entail components of the proof but do not themselves state the joint cubical metric separation and layer-by-layer transition. Historical risk remains in inaccessible earlier work on random \(0/1\)-polytopes and coding-theoretic extreme-distance distributions; priority should not be treated as independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The two regimes expose a geometric distinction between the smallest enclosing ball and diametral pairs for a natural infinite family of discrete random polytopes. At only polynomially many retained corners the full cube circumsphere is already uniquely optimal while the diameter is asymptotically smaller by a constant factor. Near a fixed Hamming layer the diameter instead undergoes an exact nondegenerate Poisson-to-two-point critical transition, still with the same sphere. This is a structural geometric phenomenon, not merely a finite census or a numerical invariant.

## Closest literature and limitations
Newman's binomial vertex-thinning is on a *random circumscribed* base polytope, and his metric diameter estimate concerns facets of that object. Reitzner–Schütt–Werner use continuous boundary points and investigate expected face/volume statistics. Classical random cube-polytope volume thresholds cannot determine the exact circumball or metric diameter distribution. Füredi's 1986 full paper could not be examined and remains a priority caveat. The polynomial intensity criterion is sufficient, not sharp; the near-antipodal theorem keeps its deficit parameter fixed; no independent audit is claimed.
