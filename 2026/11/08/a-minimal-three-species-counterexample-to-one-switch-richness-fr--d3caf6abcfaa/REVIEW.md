# Assessment: A minimal three-species counterexample to one-switch richness–frequency covariance

## Correctness
**PASS.** The indicator covariance identity is derived directly from the multinomial distribution with all index and exponent boundaries explicit. An independent exact multinomial census of all 153 possible three-species count vectors for 16 draws matches every one of the 16 covariances and all sign phases. The two-species lower bound follows from the pointwise identities \(KF_r=2F_r\) for \(r<n\) and \(F_n=\mathbf{1}\{K=1\}\). Risks: the finite example proves no uniform higher-alphabet bound.

## Originality
**PASS, with limited historical-priority confidence.** The closest known one-switch result explicitly requires a uniform probability vector. Barbour–Gnedin (arXiv:0809.4387) treat a countably infinite positive probability law and asymptotic CLTs, not this finite three-box sign pattern. Exact nonuniform multinomial probability calculations are classical, and implicit coverage by an unlocated occupancy covariance table remains a risk; a search failure is not proof of uniqueness.

## Value
**PASS.** The example shows that a natural global richness/frequency sign classification is not robust to heterogeneity, and the two-species argument proves that three is the *smallest possible alphabet* for its failure. The exact four-phase sign sequence is more informative than a single numerical sign discrepancy; no arbitrary parameter search is needed to verify the supplied rational witness.
