# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The definition of multipaths is equivalent to the explicitly enumerated in/out-degree and cycle constraints. An exhaustive independently repeated census gives 714 nonempty simplices and two equal face sets. The precisely prescribed 341 matched codimension-one pairs are pairwise disjoint; all 2376 reversed-or-unreversed Hasse incidences have an acyclic orientation. The critical profile is one cell in dimension zero, nineteen in dimension three, and twelve in dimension four. Two signed, integer-only reductions of the complete boundary complex independently give the same zero 19-by-12 critical boundary, and raw mod-two homology agrees. Hurewicz on the two-connected three-skeleton converts zero attaching degrees to nullhomotopic four-cell attaching maps. This yields the claimed integral homotopy wedge, not merely an Euler count.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risk)

The 2026 motivating primary paper addresses bidirected linear and polygonal digraphs, not the complete bipartite bidirected graph; the 2022 primary paper's complete bipartite result is for a one-way, alternating orientation. The published double-star multipath calculation is for a tree, while an ordinary Morse-matching complex of the undirected complete bipartite graph has different vertices and simplices. The available previous research records concern different parameters and do not imply this exact integral classification. Targeted source, alias and stronger-coverage searches found no exact matching formula. Because searches do not prove uniqueness, unindexed or inaccessible equivalent results remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The fully bidirected complete bipartite graph with three vertices per part is the smallest symmetric balanced case with both part sizes at least three and multiple overlapping four-cycles. Its multipath complex is a natural extension of the bidirected polygon examples; identifying a nontrivial two-dimensional range of sphere dimensions and its exact integral attaching maps provides a finite but meaningful classification at the first such balanced branching case. The integers 19 and 12 are not consequences of the one-way chessboard formula, nor merely a recomputation of a tabulated invariant. The explicit discrete-Morse model gives reusable structural information.

## Limitations and closest sources

The result is a finite exact homotopy classification, not a uniform theorem. Acyclic matching and zero attaching degrees are verified by executable exhaustive arithmetic, without external independent audit. The closest full-text comparisons are arXiv:2601.05670v1, introduction and Section 2, and arXiv:2208.04656v1, Definitions 2.1–2.2 and Section 3.2.
