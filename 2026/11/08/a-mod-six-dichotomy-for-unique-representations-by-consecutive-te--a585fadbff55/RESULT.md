# A mod-six dichotomy for unique representations by consecutive tetrahedral numbers

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge4\), let \(a_i=\binom{n+i+2}{3}\), for \(0\le i\le3\), and let \(S_n=\langle a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3\rangle\). Let \(R_n(t)\) count the quadruples of nonnegative integers giving \(t=\sum_{i=0}^{3}x_i a_i\), and let \(b_n\) be the least Betti degree of \(S_n\). The *multiple-representation ideal* is principal, in the precise sense
\[
\{t\ge0:R_n(t)\ge2\}=b_n+S_n,
\]
**if and only if** \(n\equiv0,2,3\pmod6\). Equivalently, exactly in those three residue classes the integers with a unique representation are the Apéry set
\[
\{t\ge0:R_n(t)=1\}=\operatorname{Ap}(S_n,b_n)=\{s\in S_n:s-b_n\notin S_n\}.
\]
For these classes, the exact first generalized Frobenius number \(g_1(n)=\max\{t\ge0:R_n(t)\le1\}\) and first generalized genus \(H_1(n)=\#\{t\ge0:R_n(t)\le1\}\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{g_1(n)=F_n+b_n,\qquad H_1(n)=\frac{F_n+1}{2}+b_n},
\]
where \(F_n=F(S_n)\) is the classical Frobenius number. The shift has the explicit form
\[
 b_n=\begin{cases}
 \frac43\binom{n+3}{4},&n\equiv0,3\pmod6,\\
 \frac43\binom{n+4}{4},&n\equiv2\pmod6.
 \end{cases}
\]
For all \(n\equiv1,4,5\pmod6\), the multiple-representation ideal is not principal and \(g_1(n)<F_n+b_n\). For example, \(n=6\) gives \(S_6=\langle56,84,120,165\rangle\), \(b_6=168\), \(F_6=1243\), hence \(g_1(6)=1411\) and \(H_1(6)=790\). At \(n=7\) one has \(g_1(7)=1991<1571+660\).

## Assumptions and scope
Every \(n\ge4\) is included; no primality or coprimality hypotheses are added. The four tetrahedral generators are pairwise distinct and, as established in the cited source, generate a numerical semigroup and form a minimal generating set. A Betti degree means the common weighted degree of a relation from a minimal factorization presentation (equivalently, a degree whose factorization graph has more than one connected component). The term \(b_n+S_n\) denotes a translate, not the semigroup generated by \(b_n\). The first generalized genus counts nonnegative integers with zero or exactly one representation, including zero (which has one representation). Only the three indicated residue classes receive closed \(g_1\) and \(H_1\) formulas here; the strict inequality for the other classes is a proved limit, not an assertion of their exact generalized invariants.

## Proof
**1. General representation-ideal lemma.** Let \(S=\langle a_0,\ldots,a_e\rangle\) be any finitely generated cancellative numerical semigroup and \(\mathcal B\) its set of Betti degrees. Then
\[
\{t\in S:R(t)\ge2\}=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal B}(B+S).
\]
Indeed, a degree \(B\) admitting two factorizations continues to admit two after adding any factorization of any \(s\in S\), proving the reverse inclusion. Conversely, a minimal presentation connects any two factorizations of \(t\) by a finite sequence of elementary relation moves. At least one move is nontrivial, with common degree \(B\in\mathcal B\), and its application uses a nonnegative residual factorization of degree \(t-B\). Thus \(t-B\in S\). This argument does not assume that the full fiber at \(t\) is disconnected.

**2. Published input and exact comparisons.** Robles-Pérez and Rosales (2017, Proposition 32 and Corollary 34) prove that \(S_n\) is free and give its three Betti degrees. Set \(k=\lfloor n/6\rfloor\), and abbreviate \(C_j=\binom{n+j}{4}\). Their degrees, in their published ordering, are
\[
(B_1,B_2,B_3)=\begin{cases}
(2C_5,4C_4,\tfrac43C_3),&n=6k,\\
(\tfrac43C_5,2C_4,4C_3),&n=6k+1,\\
(2C_5,\tfrac43C_4,4C_3),&n=6k+2,\\
(4C_5,2C_4,\tfrac43C_3),&n=6k+3,\\
(\tfrac43C_5,2C_4,4C_3),&n=6k+4,\\
(4C_5,\tfrac43C_4,2C_3),&n=6k+5.
\end{cases}
\]
Each expression is an integer for its indicated congruence class. Elementary polynomial expansion of \(a_i=\binom{n+i+2}{3}\) proves the following six identities for \(k\ge1\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 n\bmod6&\text{identities between Betti differences and generators}\\\hline
0&B_1-B_3=(k+2)a_1+2a_2,\quad B_2-B_3=4(k+1)a_1\\
2&B_1-B_2=(k+1)a_1+2a_2,\quad B_3-B_2=4ka_1\\
3&B_2-B_3=(k+1)a_0+2a_1,\quad B_1-B_2=3(k+2)a_2.
\end{array}
\]
All right-hand sides belong to \(S_n\). In the three rows, the smallest Betti degree is respectively \(B_3,B_2,B_3\), because the displayed differences are positive. By the representation-ideal lemma, all three Betti translates are contained in \(b_n+S_n\) while the latter is itself one of the Betti translates. Therefore the ideal is exactly \(b_n+S_n\), and its complement in \(S_n\) is exactly \(\operatorname{Ap}(S_n,b_n)\). The explicit formulas for \(b_n\) follow from the table.

**3. The sharp nonprincipal obstruction.** For \(n=6k+1\), with \(k\ge1\), let \(b=B_1\) and \(D=B_3-B_1=4(2k+1)^2(3k-2)(3k+2)>0\). Here \(B_2-B_1=(k-1)a_2\in S_n\). For \(n=6k+4\), with \(k\ge1\), put \(b=B_1\) and \(D=B_2-B_1=(k+1)(2k-1)(3k+4)(6k+7)>0\). For \(n=6k+5\), with \(k\ge1\), put \(b=B_2\) and \(D=B_3-B_2=(k+1)(2k-1)(3k+4)(6k+7)>0\). In each row \(b\) is the minimum of the published degrees. Proposition 12 of the cited source supplies \(F_n\); direct substitution into its formulas yields the following *nonnegative generator certificates*:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n=6k+1:\quad F_n-D&=(3k+1)a_0+a_1+(2k+1)a_2+2ka_3,\\
 n=6k+4:\quad F_n-D&=(6k+6)a_0+(3k+3)a_1+a_2+ka_3,\\
 n=6k+5:\quad F_n-D&=(3k+3)a_0+a_1+ka_2+(6k+6)a_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
These identities follow by multiplying out binomial coefficients and are valid for every integer \(k\ge1\). In particular \(F_n-D\in S_n\). Because \(S_n\) is free, it is symmetric: for each \(0<d\le F_n\), \(d\notin S_n\) if and only if \(F_n-d\in S_n\). Thus \(D\notin S_n\). But \(b+D\) is a Betti degree and hence has two representations while it does not belong to \(b+S_n\). This rules out principality. For the remaining two initial cases, \(n=4\) has Betti degrees \((168,140,140)\), and \(n=5\) has \((840,168,140)\). Their least degree is \(140\), and the difference \(28\) from an additional Betti degree is not in \(S_4\) or \(S_5\): no combination of their generators can equal \(28\). Thus principality fails for both.

**4. Exact generalized invariants and strictness.** For any numerical semigroup \(S\), nonzero \(b\in S\), and Frobenius number \(F\), the largest nonnegative integer not in \(b+S\) is \(F+b\). The number of such nonnegative integers is \(b+g(S)\), where \(g(S)\) is the ordinary genus: the missing numbers consist of the first \(b\) integers and the translate by \(b\) of the gaps of \(S\). Free numerical semigroups are symmetric, hence \(g(S_n)=(F_n+1)/2\), proving both displayed formulas in the principal cases. In each nonprincipal class, the certificates \(F_n-D\in S_n\) imply \(F_n+b_n\in(b_n+D)+S_n\). Thus \(F_n+b_n\) already has at least two representations, while all integers larger than \(F_n+b_n\) also have at least two by inclusion of \(b_n+S_n\) in the multiple-representation ideal. This proves \(g_1(n)<F_n+b_n\). For \(n=4,5\), the same conclusion follows from \(253-28=225=6\cdot20+3\cdot35\in S_4\) and \(853-28=825=3\cdot35+6\cdot120\in S_5\), with the corresponding positive gap difference \(D=28\).

## Verification
The standalone file `verify.py` computes the full representation-count sequence up to \(F_n+b_n\), independently computes membership in \(S_n\), and compares every integer with the published Betti translates for \(4\le n\le30\). It verifies the principal/nonprincipal classification in all six residue classes, confirms the exact first generalized Frobenius and genus formulas in the principal classes, and verifies the strict inequality in the other classes. The run checked 27 parameter values and 2,622,124 integers. The generator identities and the three gap certificates above were also checked by symbolic polynomial simplification; their validity for all \(k\ge1\) rests on the displayed polynomial identities, not on finite testing.

## Relationship to prior work
Robles-Pérez and Rosales [1] supply the tetrahedral Frobenius formulas, freeness, and all three Betti degrees; **those inputs are not new here**. The contribution is the exact mod-six classification of when the multiple-representation ideal collapses to one translate, together with the ensuing complete description of uniquely represented integers and the closed first generalized invariants in the principal classes. Komatsu [2] develops generalized Frobenius numbers for *three triangular* generators, which neither states nor supplies this four-tetrahedral classification. Generalized arithmetic-progression results [3] concern a different generating family. The argument uses standard presentation and symmetric-semigroup facts but requires the listed nontrivial congruence-specific difference identities and gap certificates. No claim of independent verification or literature completeness is made.

## Limitations
No closed formula for \(g_1(n)\) or \(H_1(n)\) is asserted for \(n\equiv1,4,5\pmod6\). The boundary classification is for *ordinary nonnegative coefficient representations*, not for representations up to a permutation or restricted coefficients. The proof uses prior published freeness and Betti tables, so any corrected version of those inputs could affect the result. Originality has been checked against the cited papers, targeted indexed searches, and accessible records, but unindexed or more recent equivalent deductions remain a risk. Computational checks are finite and do not replace the symbolic argument.
