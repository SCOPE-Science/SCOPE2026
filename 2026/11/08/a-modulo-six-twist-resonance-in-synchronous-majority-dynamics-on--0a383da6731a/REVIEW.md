# Review of the cubic cyclic-ladder majority theorem

## Correctness (C): PASS

The claim has precise graph and update definitions for all integer circumferences \(n\ge3\), with a twist in a single seam. The monotone, bounded spin potential proves eventual period at most two. Fixed states follow from the disagreement-propagation dichotomy and an explicit two-bit transfer matrix. Two-periodic states correspond bijectively to fixed states on the canonical bipartite double cover, whose connectedness and isomorphism type are determined by the parity of circumference plus twist. Fully flipping states follow from a second exact transfer matrix with complement-twisted closure. The modulo-six sign formula follows from the exact Lucas doubling identity. Direct full-state enumeration of 20 finite cases and a separate exact two-time transfer across 40 cases reproduce all formula values. There is no claim that these finite tests prove the entire infinite family.

## Originality (O): PASS, historically qualified

The classical 1980 Goles–Olivos period theorem is fully credited. The FULL Agur–Fraenkel–Klein (1988) source was read: its cyclic no-singleton and no-triple enumeration formulas are *already known* and expressly excluded from the originality claim, whereas the paper treats self-inclusive majority on a single cycle, not the cubic Möbius/prism comparison or the associated two-step double-cover count. Benjamini et al. (arXiv:1405.2486v1, introduction and Section 2) restate that general period theorem while studying infinite networks and random-graph behavior. Kaaser et al. (arXiv:1508.03519v1, main theorems and Appendix C) study voting times and bipartite distinctions, not these precise recurrent populations. The existing majority-sunlet formula concerns a different graph and does not give the nonbipartite Möbius/prism sign comparison; a circular-ladder parity/XOR theorem uses a different local map. The canonical graph double cover and classical cyclic Lucas transfer counts are standard components, not independently advertised as new. The new claim consists of the exact coupled-ladder population formulas and seam-twist modulo-six sign law. Actual keyword, alias, semantic-index and implication comparisons found no covering orbit census; an unindexed earlier equivalent is a residual priority risk, and the full 1980 article was not accessible.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

The core contribution is a uniform exact orbit classification for two canonical connected cubic graph families of arbitrary size, with a nontrivial mod-six change of sign in the effect of a seam twist. The count difference is exponentially large but subleading and can switch sign even though the graphs have the same vertex count and degree at every parameter. Both the primitive temporal-cycle numbers and the all-vertex-flipping subpopulation are determined without a full configuration enumeration. This goes beyond merely restating the general period-two theorem or changing notation in a known finite table. No basin or transient timing claims are made.

## Limits

Earlier journal metadata supplies only the year for the 1980 publication, whereas the oldest directly verified day-specific inspected source is arXiv:1405.2486v1 dated 2014-05-11. The exact comparison may not rule out an unindexed older result. Program executions corroborate the mathematics but do not constitute independent mathematical certification.
