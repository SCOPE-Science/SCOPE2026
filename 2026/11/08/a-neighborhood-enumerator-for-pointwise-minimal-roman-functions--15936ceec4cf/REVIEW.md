# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The relevant notion is pointwise minimality, not mere optimal weight. Roman feasibility is upward closed in the coordinatewise order, so single-label decreases suffice for minimality. A private-leaf analysis proves all four possible core–leaf states with the correct neighborhood restrictions; fixed core-\(2\) set \(X\) then has exactly \(2^{|V(H)\setminus N_H[X]|}\) admissible configurations of one weight. The bijection is reversible and the independent-set graph encodes the same admissible choices without an extra restriction. The domination and leading-coefficient statements follow from actual dominating sets and component unions. Complete-bipartite cases are a disjoint four-case partition. The verifier directly recomputes the defining predicate for 1,075,446 labelings on 86 bases, and tests the independent-set bridge and extremal formulas. These finite checks supplement but do not replace the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
The closest foundational source, Abu-Khzam–Fernau–Mann (arXiv:2204.04765, Lemma 2 / their minimal-Roman characterization), gives a general minimality criterion and enumeration algorithms. It does not give the exact corona neighborhood enumerator or the auxiliary bipartite independent-set identity. Their *Roman Census* (arXiv:2208.05261, Sections 4–7) counts minimal Roman functions of paths and analyzes enumerators on graph classes but does not state a full one-leaf-corona polynomial. Siva Parvathi–Maheswari (2013, Section 5; 2014, Theorem 3.1) exhibit special minimal functions on cycle–clique and path–star coronas, respectively; neither claims nor proves an all-function classification for unrestricted one-leaf coronas. The known minimum-weight Roman parameter and the general minimality criterion are not claimed as novel. Searches for leaf-corona, private-leaf, neighborhood-bipartite, and weight-enumerator aliases found no equivalent explicit formula among inspected sources. Bibliographic access to a broad 2013 thesis is incomplete, so publication priority is not independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The all-weights classification applies to arbitrary finite bases, including disconnected and highly nonchordal graphs, and bridges a Roman minimal-function count to an independent-set count in a specified bipartite graph. It refines scalar minimum Roman domination, yields a leading-coefficient product governed by connected-component orders, and specializes to an explicit two-parameter complete-bipartite formula. These are structurally motivated invariants and a reproducible enumeration identity, not an arbitrary isolated numeric computation or a restatement of the general enumeration algorithm. Computational efficiency on arbitrary bases is not asserted.

## Comparison and limitations
The strongest existing coverage is the general minimal-Roman function characterization of Abu-Khzam–Fernau–Mann: it supplies prerequisites but not the new closed enumerator. Existing corona-specific constructions are special exhibited configurations and do not enumerate all minimal functions. A 2013 thesis on corona domination was not fully inspected, and more obscure equivalent formulations could exist. Finite tests do not constitute an infinite proof. No independently conducted audit is claimed.
