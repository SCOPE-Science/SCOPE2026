# A nineteen-sphere perfect-matching complex on the hexagonal antiprism

## Finding
The hexagonal-antiprism graph is the four-regular graph \(G=C_{12}^{2}\), with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\) and undirected edges joining vertices whose cyclic distance is one or two. Its *perfect-matching complex* \(\mathcal P(G)\) is generated by all perfect matchings: a nonempty set of graph edges is a simplex precisely when it is contained in one of those matchings. There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\mathcal P(G)\simeq\bigvee^{19}S^2.
\]
The complex is pure of dimension five, although its reduced homology lies only in dimension two; in particular it is not shellable. Its 36 six-edge perfect-matching facets generate a nonempty face vector \((24,180,476,480,216,36)\), listed in dimensions zero to five.

Under the natural dihedral action generated by \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\), the character on \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal P(G);\mathbb Q)\) is, in the order \(r^k\) and \(r^ks\) for \(0\leq k<12\), respectively,
\[
\chi(r^k)=(19,-1,1,-1,1,-1,-5,-1,1,-1,1,-1),
\]
\[
\chi(r^ks)=(1,-3,1,-3,1,-3,1,-3,1,-3,1,-3).
\]
Hence the rational homology fixed by all 24 dihedral symmetries vanishes, while that fixed by the 12 rotations has dimension one. This is a rational representation statement, not an equivariant homotopy splitting.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple and undirected, with exactly 12 vertices and 24 edges; all simplicial chains are oriented by lexicographic ordering of the graph edges. A *partial matching* is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint edges. The perfect-matching complex includes only those partial matchings extendible to a matching covering all 12 graph vertices. Each facet has six graph edges. We calculate integral nonequivariant homotopy type and rational homology character for this one finite graph; no assertion about other antiprisms is made.

## Proof
**Enumerating the complex.** Order all 24 graph edges lexicographically. In a recursive enumeration of perfect matchings, select the smallest uncovered vertex and pair it in turn with each available neighbor, recursing on the ten remaining vertices. This uniquely enumerates 36 perfect matchings of size six. Taking the nonempty subsets of each perfect matching yields 1,412 distinct faces, with the stated vector. Independently, enumerate all matchings by adding graph edges when endpoints are unused, and test extendibility by the same smallest-uncovered-vertex perfect-completion recursion on unused vertices; the resulting face sets agree exactly. Among matchings that do not extend, the inclusion-minimal ones number 24 of size two, 84 of size three, and 12 of size four. This checks that extendibility, not only pairwise disjointness, is essential.

**Elementary discrete-Morse matching.** Starting from all 1,412 nonempty faces, process the 24 lexicographically ordered graph edges. For each edge \(e\), simultaneously pair every remaining face \(F\) that omits \(e\) with \(F\cup\{e\}\) when that coface remains; remove both from the pool. The verifier implements these exact finite steps and checks the complete resulting directed Hasse diagram. Direct every unmatched cover inclusion downward and each matched cover inclusion upward. The verifier explicitly constructs all 5,004 covers and runs topological sorting; all 1,412 face-nodes are removed, which proves that the matching is acyclic. The resulting 696 pairs leave exactly one critical zero-face and 19 critical two-faces, and no other critical cells. Forman's discrete Morse theorem gives a CW complex having one zero-cell and nineteen two-cells; since there are no critical one-cells, its attaching maps are constant and it is a wedge of 19 two-spheres. This proves the integral homotopy statement (not just a mod-two homology calculation).

**Character.** Each symmetry sends a graph edge to another graph edge, hence acts simplicially on the generated complex. On an oriented face, its signed contribution is the sign of the permutation of that face's ordered edge list. For each of the twelve rotations and twelve reflections, exhaustively sum \((-1)^d\) times the orientation sign over the fixed \(d\)-faces to obtain its simplicial Lefschetz number. The homotopy classification establishes connectedness and reduced homology concentrated in degree two, so subtracting the invariant \(H_0\) trace \(1\) from the Lefschetz number gives precisely the displayed character. Averaging over the 24 symmetries gives zero; averaging over the twelve rotations gives one. No rational character decomposition is assumed in deriving these averages.

**Nonshellability.** A pure shellable simplicial complex of dimension five has the homotopy type of a wedge of five-spheres (or is contractible). The nonzero reduced second homology here excludes that possibility.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3's standard library; the script constructs the graph and face sets from first principles, checks the 696-pair matching and exhaustive Hasse acyclicity, independently repeats the entire matching with the graph-edge order reversed (695 pairs, critical cell counts one, 20, one in degrees zero, two, three), computes ordinary simplicial boundary ranks modulo two, and verifies all 24 signed Lefschetz traces. Its complete expected output is included in `verify_output.txt`. The mod-two boundary ranks in dimensions one through five are \((23,157,300,180,36)\); their reduced Betti numbers agree with the integral homotopy proof. No infinite-family extrapolation is inferred from this finite certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Jelić Milutinović, and Vega develop perfect-matching complexes for honeycomb regions [1]. Their methods and theorems concern the described honeycomb graph families, not the four-regular closed antiprism graph. Chandrakar and Singh classify perfect-matching complexes of polygonal line tilings [2], with open strip and grid families, not this cyclic antiprism. Their definitions motivate tracking minimally nonextendible matchings, but no cited classification implies the present homotopy type. Ordinary matching complexes of graph families, including the cycle and other polyhedral graphs, allow nonextendible matchings and need not share the present homotopy type. The equivariant character is not implied by the homotopy rank alone.

## Limitations
This is a complete nonequivariant integral homotopy classification and an exact rational dihedral character for a single canonical graph. No integral dihedral lattice decomposition, equivariant sphere-wedge equivalence, or general formula for all antiprisms is asserted. Unindexed or inaccessible equivalent work cannot be excluded categorically; the comparison is based on inspected primary papers and indexed records. Independent external review has not occurred.
