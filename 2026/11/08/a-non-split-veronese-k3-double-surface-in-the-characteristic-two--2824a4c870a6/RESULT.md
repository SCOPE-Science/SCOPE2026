# A non-split Veronese K3 double surface in the characteristic-two symmetric cubic discriminant

## Finding
Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic \(2\), and let \(X\subset\mathbb P^5_k\) be the cubic hypersurface of singular symmetric \(3\times3\) matrices. The **full scheme-theoretic singular locus** \(Y\) of \(X\) is a degree-eight complete intersection of three quadrics, supported on a quadratic Veronese surface \(C\), with a globally **non-split square-zero** canonical ideal:
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y&=V(bc+f^2,\ ac+e^2,\ ab+d^2),\\
Y_{\mathrm{red}}&=C\cong\mathbb P^2,\\
\mathcal N^2&=0,\qquad \mathcal N\cong\omega_C\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-3).
\end{aligned}
\]
Its dualizing sheaf is trivial, \(H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=0\), and its Hilbert polynomial under its given embedding is \(P_Y(m)=4m^2+2\). Thus \(Y\) is a non-split K3-type carpet on the Veronese plane (the word *carpet* refers to a square-zero double surface, not necessarily a scroll-supported carpet). More precisely, \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_C(\Omega_C,\omega_C)\cong k\), and \(Y\) realizes the unique nonzero abstract square-zero extension class up to automorphisms of its ideal line bundle.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic exactly \(2\). Use homogeneous coordinates \([a:b:c:d:e:f]\) in \(\mathbb P^5\), and the matrix
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&d&e\\d&b&f\\e&f&c\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here \(X=V(\det M)\), and its *scheme-theoretic singular locus* is \(Y=V(\det M,\partial_a\det M,\ldots,\partial_f\det M)\) in \(\mathbb P^5\). All assertions concern this exact Jacobian scheme, not a reduced rank stratum and not a family of arbitrary cubics. The reduced Veronese is
\[
C=\bigl\{[u^2:v^2:w^2:uv:uw:vw]:[u:v:w]\in\mathbb P^2\bigr\}.
\]

## Proof
**1. The entire Jacobian ideal.** Expanding the determinant in characteristic \(2\) gives
\[
F=abc+af^2+be^2+cd^2.
\]
The three off-diagonal partial derivatives vanish identically; the diagonal derivatives are \(bc+f^2\), \(ac+e^2\), and \(ab+d^2\). Euler's identity in degree \(3\) (where \(3=1\) in \(k\)) places \(F\) in their ideal. Thus the stated three quadrics define the *full* singular scheme.

**2. Support, local rings, and square-zero structure.** The charts \(a\ne0,b\ne0,c\ne0\) cover \(Y\): if all three vanish, its defining quadrics force \(d=e=f=0\), impossible for a projective point. On \(a=1\), the equations eliminate \(b=d^2\) and \(c=e^2\), while the third becomes
\[
(f+de)^2=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathcal O_Y(Y\cap\{a\ne0\})\cong k[d,e,\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),\qquad \varepsilon=f+de.
\]
The other two charts follow by cyclic permutation. Each reduced chart is the expected affine Veronese plane and has exactly one square-zero normal direction. The Veronese ideal is generated by the six symmetric \(2\times2\) minors; the three off-principal minors represent nilpotent classes, and their pairwise products lie in the Jacobian ideal (the identities also appear in the verifier). Therefore \(\mathcal N^2=0\), and \(\mathcal N\) is a line bundle on \(C\).

**3. Complete intersection, canonical ideal, and Hilbert polynomial.** The support \(C\) has projective dimension two, so the Jacobian ideal has codimension three. The three generating quadrics therefore form a regular sequence in the six-variable polynomial ring. Consequently \(Y\) is the complete intersection of type \((2,2,2)\) in \(\mathbb P^5\):
\[
\omega_Y\cong\mathcal O_Y(2+2+2-6)\cong\mathcal O_Y.
\]
Since \(Y\) and \(C\) have equal dimension, finite duality for the closed immersion \(C\hookrightarrow Y\) identifies
\[
\omega_C\cong\mathcal Hom_Y(\mathcal O_C,\omega_Y)
\cong\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_Y}(\mathcal N)=\mathcal N.
\]
The last equality follows from \(\mathcal N^2=0\) and the explicit local dual-number rings. Because \(C\cong\mathbb P^2\), this gives \(\mathcal N\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-3)\). The exact sequence \(0\to\mathcal N\to\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_C\to0\) yields \(H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=0\) by the vanishing of \(H^1\) of line bundles on \(\mathbb P^2\). It also gives
\[
P_Y(m)=\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(2m))+\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(2m-3))=4m^2+2.
\]
The degree is \(8\), consistent with the three-quadric complete intersection.

**4. A global non-splitting obstruction.** The square-zero structure is nonconstant under chart changes. On the overlap between the charts \(a=1\) and \(b=1\), with \(d\ne0\), write \(\varepsilon=f+de\) on the first chart and use \(d'=d/b\), \(f'=f/b\), \(\varepsilon'=e'+d'f'\) on the second. Then
\[
d'=d^{-1},\qquad f'=e/d+\varepsilon/d^2,\qquad \varepsilon'=\varepsilon/d^3.
\]
The middle coordinate has a genuine nilpotent correction; in particular \(\varepsilon\) transforms as the local generator of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-3)\). For a coordinate-free proof of nonsplitting, assume for contradiction that the square-zero double structure admits a global retraction \(\rho:Y\to C\). Because \(H^1(C,\mathcal N)=H^1(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(-3))=0\), the restriction map \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\to\operatorname{Pic}(C)\) is injective. Both \(\mathcal O_Y(1)\) and \(\rho^*\mathcal O_C(1)\) restrict to \(\mathcal O_C(1)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\), so \(\mathcal O_Y(1)\cong\rho^*\mathcal O_C(1)\). Also \(\mathcal N(1)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1)\), whose \(H^0\) and \(H^1\) vanish. Thus restriction is an isomorphism \(H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(1))\cong H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(1))\). Under the above line-bundle isomorphism, every hyperplane coordinate section is a pullback from \(C\). The closed immersion \(Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^5\) would factor through \(\rho:Y\to C\), and would therefore identify its nonzero nilpotent functions with zero; this is impossible for a closed immersion. Hence no such retraction exists.

**5. Uniqueness of the non-split abstract extension.** For a smooth support, square-zero thickenings with fixed ideal \(\omega_C\) are classified by \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_C(\Omega_C,\omega_C)\). Here
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_C(\Omega_C,\omega_C)
=H^1(\mathbb P^2,T_{\mathbb P^2}(-3))\cong H^2(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(-3))\cong k,
\]
using Euler's sequence twisted by \(\mathcal O(-3)\) and vanishing of the cohomology of \(\mathcal O(-2)\). The non-splitting just proved shows that the extension class is nonzero; all nonzero classes are equivalent after scaling the ideal line bundle.

## Relationship to prior work
Gallego and Purnaprajna's 1995 work establishes the classical theory of K3 carpets on rational normal scrolls. That result does not directly describe the Veronese support and Jacobian scheme here. Grenet, Monteil and Thomassé (2012) study determinants of symmetric matrices specifically in characteristic two, supplying the pertinent determinant context but not this global singular-scheme identification. Eisenbud and Sammartano (2018), especially Example 6.9, discuss a Gorenstein double structure on the Veronese surface in a larger projective space. They note that their kind of double structure does not fit inside the reduced Veronese's linear span; this is not an established nonexistence statement for arbitrary characteristic-two square-zero extensions with different embeddings. Their construction does not identify the present cubic-Jacobian scheme. A similarity-record search did not reveal an equivalent claim, but search absence is not priority proof.

## Limitations
This is an exact result for the universal symmetric \(3\times3\) determinant in characteristic \(2\). It does not classify all symmetric determinantal hypersurfaces, all double structures on Veronese surfaces with other conormal bundles, or deformations of this carpet. The word K3 refers to the dualizing and cohomological properties of a nonreduced surface; \(Y\) is not a smooth K3 surface. Historical priority is provisional pending further specialist comparison, in particular of possible non-arXiv early characteristic-two determinantal literature. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
