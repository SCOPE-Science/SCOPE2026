# A nonzero Amitsur obstruction throughout an invariant elliptic blow-up family

## Finding

Let \(B=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}\times\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}\), let \(L=\mathcal O_B(1,1)\), and put \(V=\mathbb P_B(\mathcal O_B\oplus L)\). Let \(S_+\subset V\) be its positive section. Fix complex parameters \([a:b:c]\in\mathbb P^2\) satisfying
\[
 a^2\ne b^2,\qquad (4c^2-2a^2-2b^2)^2\ne4(a^2-b^2)^2.
\]
Let \(C_{a,b,c}\subset S_+\cong B\) be the curve
\[
\begin{split}
 F_{a,b,c}={}&a(u_0^2+u_1^2)(v_0^2+v_1^2)\\
 &+b(u_0^2-u_1^2)(v_0^2-v_1^2)\\
 &+4c\,u_0u_1v_0v_1=0.
\end{split}
\]
Then \(C_{a,b,c}\) is a smooth genus-one curve. The blow-up \(X_{a,b,c}=\operatorname{Bl}_{C_{a,b,c}}(V)\) is a smooth Fano threefold in Mori–Mukai family \(4.2\). There is a faithful action of the Klein four-group \(G\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2\) on every \(X_{a,b,c}\), trivial on its Picard group, with
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Am}(X_{a,b,c},G)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.}
\]
Thus the unknown maximal obstruction in this Fano family is **at least** of order two. This does not establish whether the published order-four upper bound is attainable. The asserted phenomenon holds on a nonempty Zariski-open parameter locus (for example \([a:b:c]=[1:2:3]\)); distinct parameters are not asserted to give pairwise nonisomorphic threefolds.

## Assumptions and scope

All varieties and group actions are over \(\mathbb C\). For a smooth projective \(G\)-variety \(Z\), \(\operatorname{Am}(Z,G)\) is the image of the equivariant linearization obstruction map \(\operatorname{Pic}(Z)^G\to H^2(G,\mathbb C^\times)\). Thus an invariant line bundle has zero obstruction precisely when it admits a compatible \(G\)-linearization. The Fano property and Picard group of the blow-up of \(V\) along a smooth \((2,2)\)-curve on \(S_+\) are the standard Mori–Mukai description, also given in Sharma, §9.2, and in the published Fano-threefold construction by De Biase and collaborators. No claim about arbitrary finite subgroups of \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\) or full sharpness of Sharma's upper bound is made.

## Proof

**1. Smooth invariant elliptic centers.** On each factor \(\mathbb P^1\), consider the projective transformations induced by
\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their projective classes commute and generate \(\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/2\). Let \(G\) act *diagonally* by these transformations on the two factors of \(B\). Each of the three summands of \(F_{a,b,c}\) is invariant under the simultaneous sign change and simultaneous coordinate swap; hence \(C_{a,b,c}\) is \(G\)-stable.

In the affine coordinates \(u=u_0/u_1\), \(v=v_0/v_1\), the equation is
\[
 ((a+b)u^2+a-b)v^2+4cuv+(a-b)u^2+a+b=0.
\]
Its discriminant in \(v\) is
\[
4\bigl[(b^2-a^2)(u^4+1)+(4c^2-2a^2-2b^2)u^2\bigr].
\]
By the two stated parameter inequalities, this homogeneous binary quartic has four distinct zeros on \(\mathbb P^1_u\): its leading and trailing coefficients are nonzero, and the quadratic polynomial in \(u^2\) has two nonzero distinct roots. Furthermore, the three binary-quadratic coefficients of the equation in \(v\) never vanish simultaneously: its linear coefficient can vanish only when \(u_0u_1=0\) if \(c\ne0\), while at either such point the remaining coefficients cannot both vanish because \(a^2\ne b^2\). For \(c=0\), the two remaining coefficient equations also cannot vanish simultaneously, since \((a+b)^2-(a-b)^2=4ab\), and the discriminant inequality when \(c=0\) forces \(ab\ne0\). Consequently projection to \(\mathbb P^1_u\) is a finite degree-two cover with four simple branch points; the curve is smooth, connected and of genus one by Riemann–Hurwitz. In particular, for \((a,b,c)=(1,2,3)\) the discriminant is \(4(3u^4+26u^2+3)\), and \(26^2-4\cdot3^2=640\ne0\).

**2. Lifting the action to the Fano threefold.** Although \(R\) and \(S\) anticommute as matrices, the endomorphisms \(R\otimes R\) and \(S\otimes S\) commute and both square to the identity. They therefore yield an honest \(G\)-linearization of \(L=\mathcal O_B(1,1)\), while each \(\mathcal O_B(1,0)\) and \(\mathcal O_B(0,1)\) remains invariant as a class. The induced action on \(\mathcal O_B\oplus L\) gives an action of \(G\) on \(V\) preserving both distinguished sections. As \(C_{a,b,c}\) is stable, this action lifts faithfully to its blow-up \(X_{a,b,c}\). The canonical tautological bundle on \(V\), the pullback of \(L\), and the exceptional divisor of the blow-up all carry honest \(G\)-linearizations.

**3. Exactly one surviving obstruction.** The Picard group of the blow-up is freely generated by the pullbacks of \(\mathcal O_B(1,0)\), \(\mathcal O_B(0,1)\), the relative tautological class, and the exceptional divisor. These four classes are all \(G\)-invariant. Write \(h_1,h_2\) for the first two classes. Since \(h_1+h_2\) is the pullback of the linearized \(L\), and the other two Picard generators are linearized, the entire obstruction image is generated by the single class \(\partial(h_1)=\partial(h_2)\). It has order dividing two.

This class is nonzero. Indeed the section \(S_-\subset V\) is disjoint from the center and remains a \(G\)-invariant copy of \(B\) inside \(X_{a,b,c}\). A \(G\)-linearization of \(h_1\) on the threefold would restrict to a \(G\)-linearization of \(\mathcal O_B(1,0)\). But such a linearization would yield a genuine two-dimensional representation of the commuting projective generators \([R],[S]\), inducing their given action on the first factor. Every possible lift of these projective transformations has the form \(\lambda R\), \(\mu S\), and these always anticommute because \(RS=-SR\). They cannot represent commuting elements of \(G\). Therefore \(\partial(h_1)\ne0\), so its order is exactly two and the Amitsur group is \(\mathbb Z/2\).

## Verification

The attached standalone `verify.py` checks, with sparse integer-polynomial arithmetic, the simultaneous invariance of the entire three-parameter equation, the anti-commuting two-dimensional lifts, the commuting tensor-product lifts, and the discriminant identity. It also checks the nonempty smooth witness \((a,b,c)=(1,2,3)\). It prints `VERIFY_OK`. The geometric arguments about Picard generators, linearizations and the branched double cover are mathematical proof steps, not consequences of finite experiments.

## Relationship to prior work

Sharma's 2026 treatment of Mori–Mukai family \(4.2\), especially Lemma 9.2, establishes \(\operatorname{Am}(X,G)\subseteq(\mathbb Z/2)^2\) in general; Remark 1.2 explicitly leaves attainability of the order-four bound unresolved. It separately shows that Picard-trivial actions reduce to the toric parent family \(3.31\) and have obstruction at most \(\mathbb Z/2\), but does not construct a smooth invariant \((2,2)\) blow-up center achieving the nonzero obstruction. The toric parent result and equivariant blow-up invariance do not, by themselves, supply such a center. The published Mori–Mukai constructions identify this blow-up as Fano family \(4.2\); they do not calculate this explicit action's Amitsur obstruction.

## Limitations

This construction establishes an order-two lower bound, not the possible order-four maximum. It does not determine the full automorphism group of each threefold, the quotient varieties, or the number of distinct isomorphism classes of threefolds represented by the parameter locus. The absence of an equivalent historical example has been checked against identifiable related results and research records but is not a proof of exhaustive historical priority. Independent mathematical audit remains outstanding.
