# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Conditioning on a fixed tree component makes the remaining graph exactly uniform on the outside vertex set, and the conditional isolated-vertex expectation is independent of tree shape. The displayed covariance identity follows by linearity. A strictly positive cross-product difference certifies the global one-crossing conclusion; the limiting onset cutoff follows from exact hypergeometric products. Exhaustive small-graph and exact algebraic checks support these steps.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The closest identifiable historical source is Pittel (1990), which gives a covariance function for asymptotic tree-component counts in two random-graph ensembles. That does not itself establish a finite, all-size sign classification, especially for component size proportional to graph order. Gunderson (2013) treats component expectations and isolation in the independent-edge ensemble rather than this fixed-edge sign classification. The full original Pittel paper was not accessible for detailed full-text comparison, so implicit historical coverage remains a real risk. Searches are not a proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** The result is a complete finite-parameter sign law for two basic component-census observables, with a universal monotone likelihood ratio and a nontrivial macroscopic component-size boundary. It helps distinguish fixed-edge dependence from independent-edge models. Near the macroscopic cutoff these tree components are rare, which limits direct empirical relevance but does not remove the natural mathematical classification.
