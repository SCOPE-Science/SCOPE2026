# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The free class-two bracket forces each ordinary derivation to be the uniquely determined \((B,f,B^{[2]})\) block map. For a linear two-local map, comparison on \(v\) and \(v+z\), repeated with both factors of a decomposable bivector, makes every decomposable bivector an eigenvector of \(T-B^{[2]}\); linearity makes the eigenvalue constant. The distinguished-vector bivector lemma explicitly constructs each simultaneous witness for the remaining one-dimensional defect in every odd characteristic. The direct finite-field witness checker and independent linear-constraint-rank checker both pass. The rank-three local dimension also follows from the invertibility of the three-dimensional exterior-square differential representation when two is invertible.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The closest archival structural foundation is Bardakov–Singh (2015), which treats free class-two nilpotent Lie algebra automorphisms and exterior-square compatibility. Ayupov–Kudaybergenov (2015) establishes local-vs-global behavior for semisimple and filiform Lie algebras. The general existence theorems of Khudoyberdiyev–Jumaniyozov (2024) and Atajonov–Khudoyberdiyev–Yusupov (2026) establish proper **local** maps for broad nilpotent classes; they do not give this rank-uniform **linear two-local classification**. The 2015 article by Ayupov, Kudaybergenov and Rakhimov discusses existence of nonglobal 2-local derivations for nilpotent Lie algebras, but its full text was unavailable for detailed implication comparison. The previously obtained rank-two Heisenberg boundary is not claimed as new. No equivalent or dominating family theorem was located in the inspected sources; this is not an independence or absolute novelty certification.

## Value — PASS

The result resolves a natural local-to-global rigidity question across every free class-two Lie algebra of generator rank at least three and every odd-characteristic base field. It identifies the precise one-dimensional failure of the derivation law under *two*-point testing, while proving that ordinary one-point locality is strictly weaker. In the first non-Heisenberg case, the exact dimension hierarchy \(18<19<27\) makes the distinction quantitative. This is not merely a construction of one exceptional local map: it classifies the entire linear two-local space.

## Residual scientific risks and limitations

The complete 2015 general two-local derivation paper was not accessible for full-text inspection; a stronger implication in that paper or in differently termed reflexive-closure literature remains possible. The result does not treat nonlinear two-local maps, characteristic two, or all local maps in generator rank at least four. Its computational checks cover only finite prime fields and are corroboration of, not a replacement for, the proof. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
