# A parity-mass phase transition for fastest reversible mixing on even cycles

## Finding
Let \(n\ge3\), and put positive, unnormalized stationary masses \(a\) and \(b\) alternately around the cycle \(C_{2n}\). Among **all** nonnegative row-stochastic kernels supported on the cycle and its holding loops that are reversible with respect to these masses, the minimum second-largest eigenvalue modulus (SLEM) is explicit. Set \(A=\max\{a,b\}\), \(B=\min\{a,b\}\), and
\[
S=\frac1A+\frac1B,\qquad
\Psi_n=\sqrt{\left(\frac1A-\frac1B\right)^2+\frac{4\cos^2(\pi/n)}{AB}},\qquad
\Delta_n=S-\Psi_n.
\]
There is an optimal kernel with the same *edge conductance* \(w_*\) on every cycle edge, where
\[
w_*=\min\left\{\frac B2,\frac{2}{3S-\Psi_n}\right\},
\qquad \rho_*=1-\Delta_n w_*.
\]
Here the transition on an edge from vertex \(i\) to its neighbor \(j\) is \(w_*/\pi_i\), where \(\pi_i\) is its unnormalized stationary mass. The balancing-versus-capacity transition is sharp:
\[
\frac AB\le\frac{2}{1+\cos^2(\pi/n)}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
w_*=\frac{2}{3S-\Psi_n}\le\frac B2.
\]
For a strictly smaller ratio, both parity classes have positive holding probability; at equality and beyond, the smaller-mass class has zero holding probability. For fixed \(a,b\) and \(n\to\infty\),
\[
1-\rho_* =\frac{B}{A+B}\frac{\pi^2}{n^2}+O(n^{-4}).
\]
The theorem guarantees a global optimizer; it does **not** assert uniqueness among all non-automorphism-invariant kernels.

## Assumptions and scope
The simple undirected cycle has vertices \(0,1,\ldots,2n-1\) and edges between consecutive vertices modulo \(2n\). The prescribed strictly positive stationary masses are \(\pi_{2j}=a\) and \(\pi_{2j+1}=b\). Their normalization is immaterial. A feasible \(P\) satisfies \(P_{ij}\ge0\), \(\sum_jP_{ij}=1\), \(\pi_iP_{ij}=\pi_jP_{ji}\), and \(P_{ij}=0\) for distinct nonneighbors. Loops (holding probabilities) are allowed. SLEM is the largest absolute eigenvalue outside the stationary eigenvalue \(1\), or equivalently the weighted centered operator norm. This definition includes reducible feasible kernels with additional eigenvalues equal to \(1\).

## Proof
**1. Global symmetry reduction.** Write \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)\), \(v=D^{1/2}{\bf1}/\sqrt{\sum_i\pi_i}\), and \(H=D^{1/2}PD^{-1/2}\). Detailed balance makes \(H\) symmetric, and the objective equals the convex spectral norm \(\|H-vv^T\|_2\). The subgroup of cycle automorphisms generated by rotation through two vertices and reflection through a vertex preserves the alternating masses and acts transitively on the cycle edges. Averaging any feasible kernel over this group preserves feasibility and cannot increase its norm. Every orbit-averaged edge has the same symmetric conductance \(w=\pi_iP_{ij}=\pi_jP_{ji}\). Conversely, the constant-conductance kernel is feasible exactly for \(0\le w\le B/2\). Thus optimization over one scalar is globally sufficient, not a restriction of the original problem.

**2. Exact spectrum.** On this kernel, the two consecutive parity classes in one translation cell yield for each Fourier mode \(k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) a Hermitian \(2\times2\) block. Its eigenvalues are
\[
\lambda_{k,\pm}=1-w\left[S\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac1A-\frac1B\right)^2+\frac{4\cos^2(\pi k/n)}{AB}}\right].
\]
The \(k=0\) minus-sign branch is \(1\), and the \(k=0\) plus-sign branch is \(1-2Sw\). Since \(\cos^2(\pi k/n)\le\cos^2(\pi/n)\) for all \(k=1,\ldots,n-1\), the second-largest eigenvalue is exactly \(1-\Delta_n w\). The smallest eigenvalue is \(1-2Sw\). Also \(0<\Delta_n\le2/A\), so \(1-\Delta_n w\ge0\) throughout the feasible interval.

**3. Scalar minimax.** It follows that
\[
\operatorname{SLEM}(w)=\max\{1-\Delta_nw,\;2Sw-1\},\qquad0\le w\le B/2.
\]
The first affine function decreases strictly and the second increases strictly. Their unique crossing is \(w_0=2/(2S+\Delta_n)=2/(3S-\Psi_n)\). Consequently the unique optimum **within the constant-conductance class** is \(\min\{B/2,w_0\}\), and its value is \(1-\Delta_nw_*\). Step 1 establishes global optimality.

**4. Sharp capacity threshold.** With \(r=A/B\ge1\) and \(c=\cos(\pi/n)\), the inequality \(w_0\le B/2\) is equivalent to \(3(1+1/r)-\sqrt{(1-1/r)^2+4c^2/r}\ge4\). For \(r\ge1\), the right-hand-side squaring is legitimate whenever the original inequality holds; and direct simplification gives \(r(1+c^2)\le2\), which in turn implies \(r<3\), validating the reverse squaring. Thus the exact threshold is \(r\le2/(1+c^2)\). Since \(P_{ii}=1-2w/\pi_i\), the stated holding transition follows.

**5. Long-cycle consequence.** For fixed \(A,B\), \(\sin^2(\pi/n)=\pi^2n^{-2}+O(n^{-4})\). If \(A>B\), eventually \(w_*=B/2\) and \(\Delta_n=2\sin^2(\pi/n)/(A+B)+O(n^{-4})\). If \(A=B\), \(w_*=A/(3-\cos(\pi/n))\) and the same leading coefficient results. This proves the asserted expansion.

## Relationship to prior work
Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo and Xiao establish convex symmetry reduction and explicitly solve ordinary *uniform-stationary* cycles in their §3.1. Their single-weight cycle formula has the equal-mass situation as a special case, but the alternating prescribed masses give a non-symmetric transition kernel, different Fourier blocks, and a nonconstant threshold ratio. Jafarizadeh's 2010 work studies symmetric \(K\)-partite and cyclic network variants rather than the prescribed alternating stationary masses of a simple cycle; its accessible abstract does not settle this exact formula. Jafarizadeh's later clique-lifted and star/path results concern different graph topologies; the inspected full-text sections do not give the present alternating-cycle classification. An uninspected equivalent specialized result may nonetheless exist.

## Limitations
The solution is for **reversible** kernels with a prescribed alternating stationary distribution and allowed holding loops. Nonreversible kernels, other stationary patterns, and arbitrary graph perturbations are not covered. No assertion is made about uniqueness outside the automorphism-invariant family or about the optimality of the kernel for metrics other than SLEM. The proof is exact; independent validation and a publication-priority certification have not been performed.
