# Review: parity-sensitive weak-link dimer jamming on clique barbells

## Correctness — PASS

The process is specified by independent Poisson edge attempts and permanent endpoint exclusion. Conditioning on the independent bridge clock is exact up to its first attempt; isolated clique symmetry gives the survival factors in the integral. First-edge conditioning supplies a finite rational recurrence. The isolated-clique pure-death chain produces an independent finite Laplace formula. The weak-rate boundary follows from the parity-determined residual unmatched vertex and bounded convergence. The jammed-size law holds under every order of accepted internal dimers. For the macroscopic limit, the scaled pure-death drift is \(-x^2\), its martingale fluctuations vanish, and a derived expectation bound controls the rescaled integral's tail. Exact graph-state and spectral-Laplace programs passed 72 and 588 rational comparisons, respectively. No finite test is used as a substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified by access limits)

The common edge-blocking dynamics and randomized greedy-matching interpretation belong to earlier work. Pippenger's 1989 graph adsorption abstract covers the process in general; Dyer and Frieze's 1991 abstract concerns randomized greedy matching, especially forests and performance bounds. The fully inspected 2006 review by Cadilhe, Araújo and Privman treats lattice, continuum, and pre-patterned Bethe-lattice adsorption, not the two dense cliques with a single bridge. Targeted searches for weighted barbell dimer deposition, bridge occupancy, parity-dependent jammed matching, weak-rate singularity, and logarithmic unequal-community scaling located no directly equivalent theorem. Exact dynamic probability, sharp weak-positive-bridge boundary, two-size logarithmic asymptotic and mesoscopic rate crossover are the claimed contributions; no generic RSA mechanism is claimed novel. Full text of the 1989 and 1991 works could not be obtained; their abstracts alone cannot exclude an equivalent specialized result. Historical priority is therefore provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A single edge between dense communities can change the number of dimers at irreversible jamming by exactly one, with the sign controlled by the parity of both community sizes. The exact bridge-selection probability determines this event rather than merely its possible occurrence. Its asymptotic logarithmic dependence on the relative community sizes and its nontrivial crossover when the bridge attempt rate scales with clique size quantify the competition between internal filling and intercommunity placement. The weak-rate discontinuity for two odd communities is an interpretable singular boundary phenomenon caused by permanent residual vacancies. The statements apply to an infinite family and supply exact benchmarks for inhomogeneous adsorption dynamics on modular networks.
