# Scientific review — A parity-sparsity transition for hyper-ary partitions

## Correctness — PASS
The integer-domain claim is for every base \(b\ge2\), each nonnegative cutoff exponent, and each fixed-base index cutoff. The restricted partition recurrence is shown in RESULT.md and gives an exact three-nonzero-state parity transition matrix. The invariant \(x_k-y_k=1\) and boundary correction \(h_b(n)=s_b(n+1)\) yield the inhomogeneous count recurrence; its initial conditions give the displayed generating function. The roots and the positive leading coefficient give the sharp asymptotic with a bounded second root. The binary periodicity is cited to the original 2004 source. The independent exact-integer script was replayed and checked bounded indices only; no numerical test is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, historically qualified
Courtright–Sellers (2004) already derives the hyper-ary recurrences and binary parity criterion. Defant (2015) treats pointwise maxima, Flowers–Lockard (2016) trees, and Edgar (2018) exact pointwise digit factors. These statements are not presented as newly derived here. Edgar's individual-index theorem is broad and may permit an alternate derivation of the aggregate count, but its inspected statement does not give the rational b-adic level-count series, the sharp support exponent, or the all-base density dichotomy. Eom–Jeong–Sohn (2016) has a highly relevant matrix description, but the full text was not accessible for definitive comparison. No matching explicit result was found in the inspected primary literature and related mathematical records; this does not establish absolute priority. The only originality claim is the quantitative all-base parity prevalence classification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The question whether hyper-ary partition counts are usually even or odd is a natural congruence question about restricted partitions. A complete exact count over every full base-power interval, accompanied by a sharp power-law support exponent, establishes a qualitative boundary between base two and all larger bases. This is a structural density statement, not an arbitrary finite range or an unmotivated parameter choice.

## Scope and residual risks
The result does not evaluate the parity at every index with less work than known pointwise formulas; does not treat higher moduli or uniform-in-base asymptotics; and may have implicit antecedents in pointwise matrix formulas that were not fully accessible. Its mathematical proof and bounded exact checker should be independently scrutinized before external dissemination.
