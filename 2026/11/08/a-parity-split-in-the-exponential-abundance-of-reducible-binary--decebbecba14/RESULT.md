# A parity split in the exponential abundance of reducible binary uniform endomorphisms

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma=\{a,b\}\) and let \(\Sigma^+\) be the free semigroup of nonempty binary words. For each integer \(n\geq2\), let \(R_n\) denote the number of **rank-preserving** endomorphisms \(f:\Sigma^+\to\Sigma^+\) satisfying \(|f(a)|=|f(b)|=n\) which factor as \(f=g\circ h\), where neither \(g\) nor \(h\) is an automorphism. Put \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Then

\[
\boxed{\lim_{m\to\infty}R_{2m}^{1/(2m)}=2^{3/2},\qquad
\lim_{m\to\infty}R_{2m+1}^{1/(2m+1)}=\varphi^2.}
\]

There are precisely \(4^n-2^n\) rank-preserving endomorphisms with both generator images of length \(n\), so a uniformly sampled such endomorphism is irreducible with probability tending to one. The exceptional reducible class has **different sharp exponential rates for even and odd lengths**.

## Assumptions and scope

All words are **positive** (no empty generator images); the alphabet is fixed and binary, and factorization is in the endomorphism monoid of the same free semigroup, with no alphabet reduction. An automorphism is a permutation of the two generators. An endomorphism is rank-preserving when its pair of image words cannot be generated by a single nonempty word; equivalently in this binary case its two images do not commute. A rank-preserving endomorphism is reducible exactly when it has a factorization into two nonautomorphisms. The two factors in such a factorization must themselves be rank-preserving: a rank-one intermediate image remains rank one after composition.

The two limits are limits along the respective infinite subsequences, not asserted asymptotic equivalents with constant prefactors. The numerical small-length census below is corroborative only.

## Proof

**1. Equal weighted length and word rank.** For nonempty words \(u,v\), the equality \(uv=vu\) implies that both are powers of a common nonempty word. A proof proceeds by induction on \(|u|+|v|\): when \(|u|\geq|v|\), the prefix relation forced by commutation writes \(u=vw\), and \(wv=vw\) reduces the total length. If \(u,v\) have the same length measured by **any positive integral letter weights**, then their common-power exponents must agree, hence \(u=v\). Consequently, two distinct words of the same positive weighted length form a rank-two pair. In particular, the only rank-reducing uniform pairs of ordinary length \(n\) are the \(2^n\) identical pairs, proving the denominator \(4^n-2^n\).

**2. Parametrizing all factorizations.** Suppose \(f=g\circ h\) with both factors nonautomorphisms and \(f\) rank preserving. Write \(s=|g(a)|\), \(t=|g(b)|\). Both \(s,t\) are positive and at most \(n\), because the rank-two pair \((h(a),h(b))\) uses both letters. Necessarily \((s,t)\ne(1,1)\), since a rank-preserving endomorphism with images of length one is an automorphism. Define \(A_n(s,t)\) to be the number of words whose total weight is \(n\) when \(a\) has weight \(s\) and \(b\) has weight \(t\). Since both \(h(a)\) and \(h(b)\) have weight \(n\), and distinct equal-weight words are rank preserving, they must be distinct members of this finite set. A factorization with \(h\) an automorphism is discarded. There are at most \(2^{s+t}\) choices for \((g(a),g(b))\), and at most \(A_n(s,t)^2\) choices for \((h(a),h(b))\). Overcounting different decompositions is harmless for upper bounds.

**3. Short outer images.** Let \(T=n^{2/3}\). If \(\max(s,t)<T\), arrange \(s\leq t\). The recurrence

\[
A_j(s,t)=A_{j-s}(s,t)+A_{j-t}(s,t),\quad A_0=1,\quad A_j=0\ (j<0)
\]

and \(\varphi^{-s}+\varphi^{-t}\leq\varphi^{-1}+\varphi^{-2}=1\) show inductively that \(A_n(s,t)\leq\varphi^n\), since \((s,t)\ne(1,1)\). Summing over at most \(n^2\) ordered length pairs, this regime contributes at most

\[
n^2\,2^{2n^{2/3}}\varphi^{2n}.
\]

**4. Long outer images.** Now assume \(t=\max(s,t)\geq T\). Each weighted word has at most \(n/t\leq n^{1/3}\) occurrences of the letter with weight \(t\). For each possible number \(j\) of these occurrences, the number of words is at most \((n+1)^j\), since there are at most \(n\) letters altogether. Thus

\[
A_n(s,t)\leq B_n:=(\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor+1)(n+1)^{\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor},
\qquad \log B_n=o(n).
\]

If any word of weight \(n\) uses both letters, necessarily \(s+t\leq n\). If no word of weight \(n\) uses both letters, the existence of two *distinct* available words (required for rank two) forces \(s\mid n\) and \(t\mid n\): one available word consists solely of \(a\)'s, the other solely of \(b\)'s. The case \(s=t=n\) only permits a permutation \(h\), and is excluded. Therefore at least one of \(s,t\) is a proper divisor of \(n\). Let \(p(n)\) be the least prime factor of \(n\). Then

\[
s+t\leq n+\frac{n}{p(n)}.
\]

The entire long-image regime contributes at most

\[
n^2 B_n^2\,2^{n+n/p(n)}.
\]

Combining the regimes gives the uniform upper bound

\[
R_n\leq n^2\left(2^{2n^{2/3}}\varphi^{2n}
+B_n^2\,2^{n+n/p(n)}\right).
\]

For even \(n\), \(p(n)=2\), giving \(\limsup R_n^{1/n}\leq\max(\varphi^2,2^{3/2})=2^{3/2}\). For odd \(n\), \(p(n)\geq3\), and \(\varphi^2>2^{4/3}\), giving \(\limsup R_n^{1/n}\leq\varphi^2\).

**5. Two matching constructions.** For every \(n\), fix the rank-preserving outer map \(g(a)=a\), \(g(b)=bb\). Its images form a prefix code, so \(g\) is injective. There are exactly \(F_{n+1}\) words of weight \(n\) with weights \((1,2)\), where \(F_1=F_2=1\) are Fibonacci numbers. Choose any two distinct such words as \(h(a),h(b)\). Their equal positive weighted length makes \(h\) rank preserving, and \(h\) is not an automorphism; neither is \(g\). Injectivity makes all resulting uniform maps distinct. Hence

\[
R_n\geq F_{n+1}(F_{n+1}-1),
\]

whose \(n\)-th root tends to \(\varphi^2\).

For even \(n\), choose arbitrary binary words \(u\) of length \(n/2\) and \(v\) of length \(n\), excluding \(v=u^2\). Put \(g(a)=u,\ g(b)=v\), \(h(a)=aa,\ h(b)=b\). The maps \(g,h\) are rank preserving and nonautomorphic; \(f(a)=u^2\), \(f(b)=v\) has uniform length \(n\). The unique positive square root of \(u^2\) shows that these outputs are distinct. Thus

\[
R_n\geq2^{n/2}(2^n-1)
\]

along even lengths, with \(n\)-th root tending to \(2^{3/2}\). Both upper bounds are therefore sharp, completing the proof.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` enumerates all factorizations with both outer and inner maps rank preserving and nonautomorphic for \(2\leq n\leq8\). It generates all positive image words of permitted lengths, all inner weighted words, and deduplicates composite uniform maps. Recorded reducible counts, for \(n=2,3,4,5,6,7,8\), are respectively

\[
10,\ 38,\ 144,\ 374,\ 1648,\ 2970,\ 13316.
\]

They lie below the known numbers \(4^n-2^n\) of rank-preserving pairs and above both applicable explicit construction bounds. The program independently tests the expected number of weighted compositions against Fibonacci numbers and checks that each counted composite consists of two distinct words of the stated length. It prints `VERIFY_OK`. Only the proof above establishes the infinite-subsequence limits.

## Relationship to prior work

Bell, Foster and Reidenbach introduced and characterized reducibility of free-semigroup morphisms, including rank-preserving factorizations and the associated factor-basis decision procedure. Their March 2026 longer preprint and October 2026 proceedings version study individual factorizations, uniqueness and incidence-matrix obstructions. In their accessible texts, no enumeration by *equal generator-image length* or distinct parity-dependent exponential growth law is stated. The present argument counts the entire reducible stratum asymptotically rather than deciding reducibility for one given morphism. Earlier literature on simplification, codes and incidence matrices supplies context but does not give this quantitative conclusion in the inspected material.

## Limitations

The result concerns positive endomorphisms of a fixed binary free semigroup and length-uniform generator images. It does not give the multiplicative leading constants for either subsequence, a full exact formula for \(R_n\), or a corresponding result on larger alphabets or nonuniform image lengths. The recorded finite census is not a proof of asymptotic growth. Historical priority for this particular quantitative counting question has not received independent audit, and additional uncatalogued word-combinatorics literature remains possible.
