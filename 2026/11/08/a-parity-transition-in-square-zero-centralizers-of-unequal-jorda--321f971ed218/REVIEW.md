# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The centralizer decomposition follows from the four homomorphism spaces between the cyclic \(k[t]\)-modules \(k[t]/(t^n)\) and \(k\). The square equation reduces, without dividing by 2, to one truncated polynomial identity. Lowest-degree analysis forces the affine coordinate truncation; the parity of the remaining leading square gives either \(bc=0\) or \(x^2+bc=0\). These geometrically reduced equations imply the claimed components, normality, and singular loci. Rank proof uses a kernel-in-\(tR\) argument plus containment of the off-diagonal column. Direct exhaustive independent matrix tests pass over prime fields and \(\mathbb F_4\). Residual correctness risk: no independent formal proof audit or nonreduced-scheme analysis.

**Originality — PASS, provisionally.** Basili–Iarrobino (arXiv:0709.2304, Sections 1–2) and Košir–Oblak (arXiv:0712.2813, Sections 1–2) concern generic Jordan type and irreducibility of the unrestricted nilpotent commutator. Those conclusions do not identify the reduced square-zero sublocus, its parity-dependent components, or its exact finite-field rank enumerator. Bogdanić et al. (arXiv:2212.08425, Theorem 3 and Question 10) restrict their classification to two equal blocks and explicitly identify unequal blocks as a direction for generalization. An equal-block square-zero census concerns the incompatible fixed Jordan type \((n,n)\); it cannot supply the present \((n,1)\) equations. Multiple searches of algebraic literature and mathematical-result records yielded no exact or dominating formulation. A general module-representation scheme result could still imply this restricted case, so priority is provisional.

**Value — PASS.** Square-zero loci are natural rank-constrained strata inside nilpotent centralizers, whose unrestricted irreducibility is a classical context. Here the simplest unequal-block family exhibits a genuine alternating geometric transition, with a normal singular variety on odd lengths and reducible normal-crossing components on even lengths. Full rank multiplicities over all finite fields quantify the transition and provide data for studying representation varieties of commuting operators with quadratic nilpotence. This is a structural infinite-family classification, not an arbitrary small-order count.

The claim is restricted to the reduced square-zero locus for the fixed Jordan type \((n,1)\) and does not resolve the general unequal two-block centralizer problem. Historical-priority checks and independent mathematical validation remain incomplete.
