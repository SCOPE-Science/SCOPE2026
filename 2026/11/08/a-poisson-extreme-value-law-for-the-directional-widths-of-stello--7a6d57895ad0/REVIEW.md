# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The candidate was reconstructed from the specific unweighted simplex-sum polytope, with uniform intrinsic spherical directions and full widths. The support-function reduction was checked directly by enumeration for small dimensions; radial independence gives the exact expectation. Gaussian two-tail Poisson convergence, Bernoulli-thinning truncation in \(L^2\), and chi-radius concentration justify the limiting law and first two moments. The beta-gamma covariance and power-series summation independently give the dilogarithm constant. The embedded executable verifies independent finite cases and numerical identities, but does not purport to certify the infinite limit by sampling.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

The closest literature supplies the standard star nestohedron and combinatorial structure, but the inspected stellohedron sections of Postnikov–Reiner–Williams (arXiv:math/0609184, §10.4), the generalized Minkowski-sum theory of Postnikov (arXiv:math/0507163), and Bayer et al. (arXiv:2403.07387, §3) do not give the claimed random-direction limit or its variance. Related public mathematical records concern circumballs, inballs, asymmetry, and diameters of simplex-sum stellohedra; those deterministic invariants do not imply the width law. The elementary Gaussian extreme-value ingredients are older and explicitly credited. Residual priority risk: a probability-literature treatment of geometrically smoothed Gaussian upper/lower order statistics or an alternate convex-geometric realization could imply this law; the available comparisons found no such dominating statement.

## Value — PASS

A canonical highly asymmetric polytope family has exponentially many Minkowski summands and a natural random-direction width invariant. The work identifies the correct \(2^m\sqrt{\log m/m}\) size, a non-Gaussian second-order law, a one-dimensional exact mean formula, and a sharp positive variance constant. The distributional regime is not determined by the known face counts, inball, circumball, or diameter and is suitable for comparison with other nestohedra.

## Limits

The theorem does not transfer across arbitrary affine equivalence, does not handle weighted summands, and does not provide a quantified convergence rate. The public-paper comparison is substantial but not an exhaustive priority guarantee; independent audit remains outstanding.
