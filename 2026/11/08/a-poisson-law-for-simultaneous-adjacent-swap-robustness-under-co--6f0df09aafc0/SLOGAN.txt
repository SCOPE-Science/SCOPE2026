When one side of a stable-marriage market has a common priority and the other side independently swaps one uniformly random adjacent preference pair per agent, the probability of retaining the entire unique stable matching is exactly \(\prod_{k=2}^n(1-k/[n(n-1)])\), tending to \(e^{-1/2}\). The baseline-pivotal swaps are independent and their count converges to a Poisson law of mean \(1/2\).
