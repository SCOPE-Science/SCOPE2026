# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For any partition, lexicographic conjugate coordinates determine its lower transfer neighbors. The proof establishes that their induced adjacency graph is the line graph of an explicit simple bipartite graph, and that its clique complex has the homotopy type of that graph by a good-cover nerve. The graph is connected at every noninitial vertex. Coning off these connected graph-like links produces a simply connected two-dimensional CW homotopy model, so its Euler characteristic determines the number of integral two-sphere summands. Counting the bipartite vertices and edges gives the nonnegative local weight with no unproved infinite extrapolation. The generating function follows from a bijective gap/multiplicity parametrization. Independent direct graph and formal-series computations agree over their stated finite ranges.

## Originality — PASS

The core prior article [1] proves the sphere-wedge homotopy type but supplies the rank only as an unevaluated Euler characteristic. The numerical paper [2] develops alternating clique counts and tabulates ranks, and explicitly poses exact rank evaluation as a problem. The full-neighborhood line-graph description [4] concerns local graph structure, not Euler increments or filtration. The later study [3] asks for a partition-theoretic interpretation and analyzes full neighborhoods, not the lexicographically restricted link statistic or the positive partition sum. The claimed new content is the all-integer positive height-gap formula, its exact generating series, and the lower-link route to it—not the previously established sphere-wedge fact. Relevant searches found no statement implying the full formula, but equivalence in unindexed enumerative work remains a risk.

## Value — PASS

The earlier exact homotopy reduction left a numerical invariant defined by an alternating count of cliques; the new theorem transforms it into a manifestly nonnegative partition statistic and a coefficient-computable product–sum identity. This answers explicit published questions, explains rather than merely recomputes the known initial table, and provides coefficients for arbitrary integers, including all higher values. The lexicographic lower-link method also gives a short structural proof independent of the earlier global cover construction.

## Scope and limitations

The result is specific to the unlabelled one-cell-transfer graph. It does not classify symmetry actions, geometric fixed points, or asymptotic coefficient growth. The checks are finite corroboration, not replacements for the unrestricted proof; unindexed equivalent literature cannot be excluded. No independent mathematical audit was conducted.
