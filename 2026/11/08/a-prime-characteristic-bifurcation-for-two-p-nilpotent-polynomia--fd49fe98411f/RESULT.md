# A prime-characteristic bifurcation for two \(p\)-nilpotent polynomial shears

## Finding
Let \(K\) be any field of prime characteristic \(p>0\), let \(A=K[x,y]\), and consider the \(K\)-linear derivations
\[
D=x^{p+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\qquad
E=y^{p+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}.
\]
Composition of endomorphisms is written right-to-left. Let \(\mathfrak L=\langle D,E\rangle_{\mathrm{Lie}}\) be their generated Lie subalgebra of \(\operatorname{Der}_K A\), and let \(\mathcal B=K\langle D,E\rangle_{\mathrm{alg},1}\subseteq\operatorname{End}_K A\) be the **unital** associative subalgebra they generate. Put \(t=x^p y^p\), a polynomial (not an operator), and let \(H=[D,E]\).

**Uniform obstruction.** The generators are globally nilpotent of exponent at most \(p\), yet their associative algebra is infinite-dimensional:
\[
D^p=E^p=0,\qquad (DE)^n(x)=t^n x,\qquad H^n(x)=t^n x\quad(n\ge0).
\]
In particular \(H\) is not locally finite on \(x\), and \(\mathcal B\) contains a polynomial algebra \(K[DE]\cong K[T]\).

**Exact Lie algebra in odd characteristic.** If \(p\ne2\), set \(D_i=t^iD\), \(E_i=t^iE\), and \(H_i=t^iH\), for integers \(i\ge0\). Then these operators form a \(K\)-basis of \(\mathfrak L\), with brackets, for all \(i,j\ge0\),
\[
[D_i,E_j]=H_{i+j},\qquad
[H_i,D_j]=2D_{i+j+1},\qquad
[H_i,E_j]=-2E_{i+j+1},
\]
and all brackets within each of the three individual families vanish. Thus \(\mathfrak L\) is infinite-dimensional and has an explicit current-like presentation.

**Exact characteristic-two associative envelope.** If \(p=2\), then
\[
\mathfrak L=KD\oplus KE\oplus KH,
\qquad [D,E]=H,\quad [H,D]=[H,E]=0.
\]
In the same characteristic, \(H=DE+ED\) is central in \(\mathcal B\), and the natural homomorphism from the algebra
\[
K[T]\langle u,v\rangle/(u^2,v^2,uv+vu-T)
\]
(where \(T\) is central) to \(\mathcal B\), sending \(T\mapsto H\), \(u\mapsto D\), \(v\mapsto E\), is an isomorphism. In particular, \(\mathcal B\) is a free module of rank four over its polynomial subalgebra \(K[H]\), with basis \(\{1,D,E,DE\}\). Thus a **three-dimensional** Lie algebra generated by two square-zero derivations can have an **infinite-dimensional** associative operator algebra.

## Assumptions and scope
The field \(K\) is arbitrary of prime characteristic \(p>0\); it need not be finite, perfect, or algebraically closed. Polynomial derivatives are formal derivatives on \(K[x,y]\). Multiplication by \(t\) in the symbols \(t^iD\), etc., means pointwise multiplication of a derivation by the polynomial \(t^i\). The algebra \(\mathcal B\) includes the identity endomorphism. The claim does not say that all elements of \(\mathfrak L\) are locally finite, nor that \(\mathcal B\) is nil or algebraic over \(K\).

## Proof
**(1) Frobenius constants and brackets.** Every derivation of \(A\) annihilates \(x^p\), \(y^p\), hence \(t\). Set \(X=x^p\), \(Y=y^p\), \(d=x\partial_y\), \(e=y\partial_x\), and \(h=x\partial_x-y\partial_y\). Then \(D=Xd\), \(E=Ye\), and direct calculation on \(x,y\) gives
\[
[d,e]=h,\quad [h,d]=2d,\quad [h,e]=-2e.
\]
Since \(X,Y\) are killed by all three derivations, \(H=[D,E]=XYh=th\). Consequently \([t^iU,t^jV]=t^{i+j}[U,V]\) for derivations \(U,V\) among \(D,E,H\), which proves all displayed bracket formulas. In characteristic two the coefficients \(\pm2\) vanish.

**(2) Nilpotence and infinite powers.** Multiplication by \(x^{p+1}\) commutes with \(\partial_y\); in characteristic \(p\), \(\partial_y^p=0\) on \(A\), because the falling factorial of \(p\) consecutive integers is zero. Thus \(D^p=0\); symmetrically \(E^p=0\). Both \(D\) and \(E\) annihilate \(t\), so their compositions are \(K[t]\)-linear. Direct evaluation gives \(DE(x)=D(y^{p+1})=(p+1)x^{p+1}y^p=tx\), hence \((DE)^n(x)=t^nx\) by induction. Also \(H(x)=tx\) and \(H(t)=0\), giving \(H^n(x)=t^nx\). The polynomials \(t^nx\) are linearly independent, so \(K[DE]\) embeds in \(\mathcal B\) and \(H\) is not locally finite on \(x\).

**(3) Odd-characteristic Lie basis.** The displayed formulas show that the span of all \(D_i,E_i,H_i\) is bracket closed. It contains \(D_0,E_0\). Conversely, it is generated by them: \(H_0=[D_0,E_0]\), and, inductively, \(D_{i+1}=\frac12[H_i,D_0]\), \(E_{i+1}=-\frac12[H_i,E_0]\), \(H_{i+1}=[D_{i+1},E_0]\). To check linear independence of any finite family, apply a putative linear relation first to \(x\). The contributions from \(E_i\) are the distinct monomials \(t^iy^{p+1}\) and those from \(H_i\) are the distinct monomials \(t^{i+1}x\); none coincide. Thus their coefficients vanish. Applying the remaining relation to \(y\), the distinct monomials \(t^ix^{p+1}\) force all coefficients of \(D_i\) to vanish. This proves the stated basis and bracket presentation.

**(4) Characteristic-two Lie basis.** The bracket identities show that the three-dimensional span of \(D,E,H\) is a Lie subalgebra. The three derivations are linearly independent: \(D(x)=0\), \(E(x)=y^3\), \(H(x)=x^3y^2\), while \(D(y)=x^3\). Hence the span is exactly \(\mathfrak L\).

**(5) Characteristic-two associative envelope.** Here \(D^2=E^2=0\), and \(ED=H+DE\). Since \(H\) Lie-commutes with \(D,E\), it also commutes with them in their associative operator algebra. Repeatedly replacing \(ED\) by \(H+DE\), and deleting any adjacent \(DD\) or \(EE\), shows that every word lies in the \(K[H]\)-span of \(1,D,E,DE\). To prove independence, suppose \(a(H)+b(H)D+c(H)E+d(H)DE=0\), where \(a,b,c,d\in K[T]\). On \(y\), the relation becomes \(a(t)y+b(t)x^3=0\); the monomial supports of the two summands are disjoint, so \(a=b=0\). On \(x\), the remaining relation is \(c(t)y^3+d(t)tx=0\), again with disjoint monomial supports, so \(c=d=0\). These steps show both that the abstract presented algebra has the expected free spanning basis and that its realization is faithful. Therefore the claimed isomorphism and rank-four freeness hold.

## Verification
A standalone verifier, `verify.py`, checks the identities on monomial bases over the prime fields with \(p\in\{2,3,5,7\}\). It checks \(D^p=E^p=0\) on 81 monomials per generator and prime, the first 12 powers of \(DE\) acting on \(x\), six bracket families for indices \(0\le i,j\le3\), and in characteristic two the centrality and basic relation of \(H\). The recorded deterministic output is in `verification_output.txt` and reports 1,227 successful checks. These are finite corroborations; the general result follows from the proof above. Run `python verify.py` using Python 3 without additional dependencies.

## Relationship to prior work
Bezushchak and Petravchuk, arXiv:2610.15731v1, Theorem 1, prove that finite generation plus local finiteness of **all iterated commutators** on algebra generators gives finite-dimensionality of the associative algebra of derivations in positive characteristic. The present theorem constructs a sharp obstruction to replacing that condition by even uniform \(p\)-nilpotence of the original derivations, and identifies the resulting Lie/associative structures. The cited paper does not state this pair or these structural formulas. Its conclusions remain intact, since \(H\) is not locally finite.

Belabbas, arXiv:2608.12567, treats Lie algebras consisting of locally finite derivations in characteristic zero. Zaidenberg, arXiv:2604.02864v7, proves positive results for solvable such algebras on the affine plane in characteristic zero; neither theorem covers the positive-characteristic pair here. Arzhantsev, Gaifullin and Lopatkin, arXiv:2408.05627v2, discuss finite-dimensional criteria for homogeneous derivation Lie algebras, with a different focus. Earlier general structure theorems do not by themselves supply this characteristic-two associative envelope or its rank-four free-module model.

## Limitations
The result treats the displayed specific symmetric shear pair, not a classification of all pairs of \(p\)-nilpotent polynomial derivations. No claim of uniqueness among prior equivalent formulations is made. The historical novelty risk is that current-algebra or Clifford-type presentations of this pair could occur under different terminology. The characteristic-two algebra is presented as an operator algebra, not as an assertion of an algebraic-group action or of a free associative algebra.
