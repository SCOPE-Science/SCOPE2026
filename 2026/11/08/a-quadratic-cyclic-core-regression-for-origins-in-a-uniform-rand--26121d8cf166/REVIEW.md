# Scientific review: conditional origins and cyclic core of uniform random maps

## Correctness (PASS)
The root-forest bijection is explicitly invertible, and its parent-label prefix is independent uniform on \([n]\). Counting nonroot labels absent from the prefix proves every displayed conditional factorial moment and joint count. The exact covariance uses the independent restriction to \(n-1\) points conditioned on one origin. The Rayleigh-tail product, moment control and quadratic conditional-regression expansion justify the sharp covariance limit; no finite-check-to-infinite-proof inference is used. The standalone finite verifier passed all 200 exact comparisons for \(n\le6\).

## Originality (PASS, with priority risk)
The classical forest/Prüfer representation, Rayleigh cyclic-point law, and mapping leaf marginals are prior work and are not part of the asserted novelty. Inspected passages of Arney–Bender (1982), Soria (1996), Hansen–Jaworski (2006, 2010) do not provide the precise strictly decreasing mean regression, its quadratic order-one Rayleigh-window profile, or the stated negative finite covariance and coefficient. Searches for joint cyclic-point/zero-indegree dependence, origins and cyclic vertices, random-mapping covariance, and conditional forest leaves located no demonstrably dominating claim. Potential extraction from the general multivariate machinery in older papers, and inaccessible Drmota–Soria (1997), remain explicit residual risks; absence of an indexed match is not proof of novelty.

## Value (PASS)
Origin counts track the transient forest boundary while cyclic vertices track the recurrent core of the same functional graph. The order-one quadratic regression explains a dependence invisible in each marginal limit (one statistic is of order \(n\), the other \(\sqrt n\)). The sharp negative covariance identifies the sign and \(\sqrt n\) scale of cross-fluctuation, and the complete conditional factorial moments offer reproducible finite-size information. The result concerns a natural, historically studied joint observable, not an arbitrarily chosen parameter or a recoding of an existing marginal formula.

## Limits and closest work
The 1982 origin-constrained mapping article is the closest early source, followed by Soria's leaf generating function (1996) and Hansen–Jaworski's forest construction (2010). Full text of Drmota–Soria (1997) could not be inspected. The claim is not external or independent certification. Exact finite checks cover only six-point maps; general statements use mathematical proofs.
