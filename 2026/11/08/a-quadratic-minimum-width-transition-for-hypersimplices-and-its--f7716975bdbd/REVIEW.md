# Scientific review of the hypersimplex minimum-width theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The definition of intrinsic Euclidean width uses tangent unit normals to the affine hyperplane. The proof reconstructs the exact top-minus-bottom support function, decomposes each ordering chamber into normalized step rays, uses the strict Euclidean triangle inequality to prove *all* minimizers are two-level, evaluates each ray under any positive diagonal scaling, and proves the sharp unscaled dichotomy by monotonicity and a factored quadratic comparison. The apparent nonlinearity of the direction optimization is completely resolved by the chamber reduction; no partial enumeration is used as an infinite proof. The embedded rational checker tests 566,297 finite grid profiles plus five transition equalities. One rank-one boundary case agrees with the known regular-simplex result.

## Originality — PASS (within checked evidence; priority provisional)

Exact searches covered synonyms of minimum width, thickness, hypersimplex, uniform-matroid base polytope, order-statistic norm, weighted diagonal scale, and quadratic rank thresholds. In the accessible earlier source, Martínez-Sandoval–Padrol (arXiv:1909.01189), the strict-projection preservation theorems classify different objects of inquiry: preserving faces, rather than minimizing the Euclidean width functional. Har-Peled–Robson (arXiv:2301.02616) proves the rank-one regular-simplex case but not the all-rank formula or quadratic crossing. Closest mathematical research records concerning consecutive-layer hypersimplex *inradii*, matroid base-polytope *circumspheres*, and simplex directional width do not logically dominate this claim: width of a single rank layer differs from both inradius and widths of a simplex. Independent archival priority remains uncertain.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The hypersimplex is the base polytope of the uniform matroid and a standard cube-section building block. The width is a fundamental metric invariant of the entire \(n-1\)-dimensional polytope, not an arbitrary minor statistic. The theorem gives an exact unexpected integer threshold \(n=4q^2-1\), the complete minimizing-direction geometry, and large discrete jumps in contact multiplicities. The weighted formula shows the structural mechanism persists under every positive diagonal scaling, while rank one consistently reduces to the classical regular-simplex case. This provides motivated geometric information not implied by the known projection-skeleton or inradius formulas.

## Sources and limitations

The closest inspected full text is the arXiv HTML transcription of Martínez-Sandoval–Padrol (especially Sections 2 and 3) and Har-Peled–Robson (especially Lemmas 1.2–1.3). The 2024 matroid-polytope survey by Ferroni was inspected in relevant sections. Literature searches did not identify the combined width theorem; absence from search results is not a proof of priority. Previously published metric formulas in an unexpected normalization or an older source could still subsume the result. The checker does not establish all-dimensional uniqueness; the proof does.
