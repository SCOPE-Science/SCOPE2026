# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The frontier is exactly \(b\) edges before the second hub is reached; its first-hit hazard is exactly \(j/b\). Thereafter each remaining opposite-side vertex attaches to either hub with independent fair probability. The displayed finite mixture and generating polynomial are therefore exact, including \(b=1\). A logarithmic product expansion yields Rayleigh scaling for the bridge index; conditional characteristic functions yield an independent Gaussian contribution. A uniform exponential tail bound justifies the mean asymptotic. Uniform spanning trees of \(K_{2,b}\) have exactly one bridge vertex, giving the independent binomial comparison. Exact rational frontier-state dynamics were independently checked against the mixture for \(b=1,\ldots,9\).

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk
The principal 2017 full text [1] analyzes growth-tree heights and percolation bounds, not the degree of a designated root in the unbalanced complete bipartite family or its Rayleigh–Gaussian mixture. The 2015 full text [2] analyzes degree laws in complete graphs and random configuration models, not the deterministic \(K_{2,b}\) source-root law. The source comparison is of statements and hypotheses rather than paper titles. Targeted literature and mathematical-record searches for root-degree, first-passage, birthday/Rayleigh scaling and complete-bipartite aliases found no equivalent; this is not an absolute novelty guarantee. Uniform spanning-tree root degree is recognized as known background and not presented as novel.

## Value — PASS
A canonical growth-tree distribution introduced in [1] has a qualitatively non-Gaussian root-degree fluctuation on the simplest nontrivial fixed-width biclique family, rather than the Gaussian fluctuation of its uniform spanning-tree comparator. The exact finite law, the scale-\(\sqrt b\) bias and stochastic dominance are natural interpretable structural differences between two spanning-tree sampling mechanisms; the invariant and object are fixed by the question, not arbitrary numeric slices.
