# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

Direct projection of a general right prism gives \(W_H=Ht+sw(\phi)\) and \(P_H=At+Hsw(\phi+\pi/2)\). Spherical expectations \(1/2,\pi/4,1/3,2/3,1/3\) yield the exact covariance. Dividing by positive height proves the full sign trichotomy with sharp equality, product-of-roots and reciprocity. Constant-width positivity follows \(\delta>1/147\); exact rectangle moments give the negative-only family. The standalone convex-hull checker confirms finite samples, not the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

Horváth's 2019 complete survey gives width/brightness definitions and brightness-preserving prisms but no same-normal covariance. The closest publicly available complete result covers the covariance of orthotopes in all dimensions, a strict subset expressly credited as prior. Other retrieved prism statements concern marginal width or brightness. The new contribution is the general planar-convex-base mixed moment and its all-heights classification, reciprocity, constant-width consequence and equality phase. The full 2026 broad width–brightness manuscript was inaccessible; equivalence remains a residual literature risk, not established coverage. Sources: Horváth, arXiv:1908.03196v1 (full HTML, Theorem 5); published orthotope result (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/tree/main/2026/10/11/SCOPE-sharp-aspect-ratio-phase-diagram-for-width-brightness-covariance--ef50d44d3e36); Abas, arXiv:2609.26202 (abstract only).

## Mathematical value — PASS

An exact three-statistic decision rule for the sign of a natural joint geometric observation across all planar convex bases is a complete classification, not just a numerical moment. The invariant root product equals base area. Every constant-width base, including Reuleaux-type nonsmooth bodies, has a positive-covariance interval, whereas sufficiently elongated rectangles have none. The threshold equality regime occurs among rectangles. The model has clear limitations: right prisms, Haar normals and only the covariance sign; it does not reconstruct the base or the joint law.
