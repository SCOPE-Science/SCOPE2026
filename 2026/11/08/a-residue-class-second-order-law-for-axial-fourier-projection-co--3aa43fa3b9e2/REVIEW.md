# Scientific review — axial Fourier projection second-order law

**Correctness: PASS.** The claim has three exact sharp norm formulas and three asymptotic expansions. Character orthogonality plus translation averaging establishes minimality over all bounded real-linear projections. The finite formulas follow from exact binomial tail-mass identities, and their second terms from shifted Stirling; the continuous formula follows from the characteristic-function identity and a Bessel saddle calculation with explicit tail domination. The finite-group kernel rows were enumerated independently, and high-precision rational/probability and Bessel checks support the normalizations. The checks do not substitute for the analytic infinite-dimensional asymptotic proof.

**Originality: PASS, subject to bibliographic uncertainty.** Existing general Fourier projection/minimality results and Krawtchouk transforms account for the kernel reduction, and recent Hamming-scheme projection results give first-order Gaussian limits. The assessed statement is the **joint signed second-order comparison**: the ternary axial norm alternates between the two sides of the continuous norm by dimension residue, while the quaternary norm lies below at leading correction order. This is not a change of notation or an asserted new binomial formula. No equivalent three-model second-order comparison was located in the accessible primary texts, research records, and targeted searches. The full manuscript on q-ary Hamming projection constants could not be inspected, so it remains a meaningful unresolved priority risk. Because that work also derives exact formulas, a short unpublished corollary may cover portions of the claimed novelty.

**Value: PASS.** Projection constants measure the worst uniform amplification of natural first-frequency harmonic reconstructions. First-order Gaussian limits erase alphabet dependence; this theorem shows that the first distinguishable error is highly non-universal and even reverses sign periodically on a natural finite alphabet. The exact rates and residue classes are the smallest scale at which this difference can be resolved, and supply benchmark constants for lattice-versus-continuum spectral projection.

**Closest sources and scientific limits.** Feinsilver–Schott (arXiv:1409.4715) treats Bernoulli/Krawtchouk transforms and convolution, not this three-model second-order comparison. Defant–Galicer–Mansilla–Mastyło–Muro (arXiv:2302.00231, Section 2) supplies minimal Fourier projection norm principles and general integral formulas, not the explicit signed second-order corrections. Their later arXiv:2607.05594 gives general Hamming projection limits in the available abstract and accessible secondary account; the complete primary manuscript was unavailable for comparison. The theorem does not address all cyclic alphabets, arbitrary frequency sets or effective finite-dimensional sign thresholds.
