# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** Each claimed edge signature follows directly from the graph's distance geometry. The lower bound uses indistinguishable matching columns. The only possible size-\(n-1\) basis has one landmark in each column except one. An explicit indistinguishable rung/clique pair excludes either layer with fewer than three landmarks; checking rung/rung, same-layer, rung/clique, and cross-layer edge pairs establishes sufficiency. All cases include \(n=2\), \(n=6\), and the first feasible value \(n=7\). Population-preserving three-slide exchanges, along with permitted vertical flips between admissible populations, give a constructive proof of connectedness. Two separately implemented finite checks corroborate all specified boundaries.

**Originality (PASS, subject to search limits).** The closest archived primary article (Kelenc, Tratnik and Yero, arXiv:1602.00291v1) introduces edge metric dimension and proves results for paths, cycles, complete and complete bipartite graphs and Cartesian grids, but the inspected relevant text does not provide a complete-prism edge-basis classification. Zubrilina (arXiv:1611.01904) proves a generic interval bound for products with paths, not the claimed exact classes or mobility. A publicly published *Mixed metric bases of complete prisms* result on precisely the same graph has the stronger **mixed** separation requirement and minimum \(n\), so it does not imply the edge-only minimum of \(n-1\), its count, or connectivity. Targeted searches of research records and literature did not locate an exact edge-only duplicate. Indexing and priority uncertainty remain.

**Value (PASS).** The result identifies a sharp feasibility transition at seven columns within a natural Cartesian-product family, gives a complete classification and exponential closed count of minimum edge-resolving configurations, and proves that restrictive adjacency-only landmark replacement nevertheless connects the entire optimum state space. It yields a concrete separation from the mixed metric dimension on the same graphs; the computation of the scalar minimum alone is not the sole contribution.

**Limitations.** The claims do not extend to general rook graphs, noncomplete layers, or arbitrary metric invariants. The 2017 Cartesian-product interval does not settle the result. Search coverage cannot exclude unpublished or differently indexed coverage; no independent scholarly audit has been conducted.
