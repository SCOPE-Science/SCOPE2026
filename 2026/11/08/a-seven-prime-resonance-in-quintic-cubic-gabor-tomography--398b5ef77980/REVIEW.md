# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem classifies one explicitly normalized family of complex-linear Gabor lifting operators over all prime fields. Weyl conjugation diagonalizes the Gram operator and identifies operator rank with the number of nonzero ambiguity coefficients. The cyclotomic minimal polynomial converts every ambiguity zero into an exact prime-field permutation-polynomial condition. Centering each nonzero translation of the quintic–cubic phase produces a quartic with no cubic term. Two elementary finite-field power-sum tests exclude its being a permutation for every prime at least eleven, independently of its remaining coefficients. At seven, the same tests force the quadratic term to vanish and the linear/quartic ratio to be one of two explicitly verified values; translating back gives the four exceptional Weyl frequencies and the coefficient condition. The cubic, Frobenius and affine cases are checked independently. Exact integer computations enumerate all coefficient choices at primes through nineteen and several larger examples; direct singular-value checks at five and seven confirm normalization. Finite tests do not replace the proof for all primes.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison

The claim's novelty is not the Weyl/Gabor criterion, Hermite power-sum method, Dickson's permutation-polynomial classification, or the established Alltop cubic sequence. The distinguishing statement is the complete all-prime parameter-wise rank and Weyl-kernel law for quintic–cubic phase masks, including an isolated prime-seven resonance. Hall–Rao–Donovan study a different, stronger planarity requirement on derivatives and the cubic Alltop property, not these quintic ambiguity zeros. Bojarovska–Flinth provide the general lifting condition and describe classical cubic Alltop obstructions, but not the quintic–cubic deformation family. Later permutation-polynomial classification literature addresses quartic permutations themselves rather than the resulting mask-dependent operator rank. Historical-priority checks included equivalent formulations in sequence correlations, tomography and permutation polynomials. Lüke's 1996 polynomial-phase correlation paper has a relevant title and abstract, but its complete text was inaccessible; a broad correlation-zero classification could imply some of the present rank results. Absolute priority and independent expert validation are not claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For unit-modulus windows, zero-shift ambiguity produces an unavoidable traceless-diagonal blind subspace. The theorem gives a natural full coefficient-family design criterion attaining this best possible rank at all primes at least eleven, while pinpointing exactly when a low-prime algebraic resonance enlarges the blind space. It supplies an explicit, reproducible all-prime phase-mask classification rather than a single small-instance census. It does not confuse complex-linear lifting rank with nonlinear phase-retrieval uniqueness or assert quantitative conditioning of the positive singular spectrum.
