# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof correctly handles the width-bias that distinguishes invariant hitting-plane sampling from Haar-central sampling. Section area follows from restricted positive-definite quadratic forms; uniform conditional offset gives the survival integral. The change of integration variable to the support-width parameter exposes a unique endpoint collision at the normalized area \(x=1\), and expansion of the square-root endpoint integral yields the nonzero exact coefficient \(r^3/[H_r(r^2-1)]\). Reparameterizing the moving integration boundary for \(x>1\) establishes one-sided regularity; away from that threshold, density is analytic. The scale and support recovery follows exactly. The executable checker independently tests three aspect ratios and verifies its output against numerical quadrature; these finite checks are not the proof.

## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)

The established full size/shape stereology for spheroids, especially Cruz-Orive 1976, is the strongest potential overlap; older DeHoff 1962 also studies ellipsoidal sections. Full text of these plausible predecessors was unavailable. Their general joint data could imply the area marginal, but no accessible source establishes the *sharp one-sided singularity and its coefficient*, including the area-only identification mechanism for fixed prolate bodies. Makarenko–Fletcher–Shukurov (2014) work with *filamentarity* rather than section area; Baaske–Ballani–Illgen (2016) emphasize ellipse profiles and a fixed vertical section design rather than the invariant conditioned plane distribution. Other central-plane section laws do not have the requisite offset weighting. a searchable published mathematics-record collection and accessible earlier research records were compared on implications, not just wording. This is a provisional scientific overlap assessment, not a certification of world-first priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The location and fractional regularity of a density singularity are invariant, sharp signatures of spheroid geometry under a canonical motion-invariant cutting experiment. The full formula specifies what information is present in *area-only* observations; it connects convex section geometry, stereological inverse questions, and a nontrivial endpoint singularity. The coefficient is not an arbitrary numerical table entry, and the recovery theorem is a structural consequence for a natural two-parameter shape family.
