# A sharp attracting-triangle window in a heterogeneous Stuart–Landau network

## Finding

For the globally and complex-diffusively coupled **three-oscillator** Stuart–Landau family specified below, two distinct, reflected, zero-mean triangular rotating states exist for every coupling parameter \(q>0\). Their transverse Floquet spectrum is stable **if and only if** \(q_-<q<q_+\), where \(q_-\in(7/50,3/20)\) and \(q_+\in(29/50,59/100)\) are the two distinct positive zeros below \(2\) of the explicit sextic

\[
\begin{aligned}
S(q)={}&14326308q^6-16456527q^5+10608102q^4\\
&-4917328q^3+1178872q^2-215616q+17280.
\end{aligned}
\]

Numerically, \(q_-\approx0.140052926447152\) and \(q_+\approx0.585884191088309\). Throughout the open interval each of these two periodic orbits is orbitally exponentially attracting, and each has an open basin in the full six-dimensional state space. At the two endpoints the transverse linearization has a nonzero pure-imaginary conjugate pair, so neither endpoint is claimed to be exponentially stable. For every positive \(q\) outside the closed interval, the orbit is not orbitally exponentially attracting. No nonlinear Hopf-bifurcation assertion is made.

## Assumptions and scope

Consider three complex states \(z_j(t)\), with \(j=1,2,3\), satisfying

\[
\dot z_j=(\beta_j+i\omega-|z_j|^2)z_j+(C_r+iC_i)\sum_{\ell\ne j}(z_\ell-z_j),
\quad \beta_j=j+3q,\quad \omega=1,\quad C_r=q,\quad C_i=4q,\quad q>0.
\]

Thus the onsite cubic coefficient is \(1\), the Kerr frequency-shift coefficient is \(0\), the growth rates are positive and equally spaced, and the reactive-to-dissipative coupling ratio is fixed at \(4\). The linear shift \(3q\) in each growth rate maintains a fixed polygon geometry while varying coupling strength. Set \(\Omega=1-12q\), and define

\[
a^+=(1,i\sqrt2,-1-i\sqrt2),\qquad
 a^-=(1,-i\sqrt2,-1+i\sqrt2).
\]

For both signs, \(z_j(t)=a_j^{\pm}e^{i\Omega t}\) is an exact solution with \(\sum_j z_j(t)=0\). Since the stable window satisfies \(q>7/50>1/12\), these solutions are genuine nonstationary periodic orbits throughout that window. The two orbits are not equivalent under uniform rotation.

Orbital exponential attraction means that the only zero Floquet exponent is the common-phase symmetry direction and that all five other real-part Floquet exponents are strictly negative. The theorem classifies these explicit two orbits, not every possible rotating or chaotic state of the full system.

## Proof

**1. Exact rotating states and linearization.** Writing \(w_j=z_je^{-i\Omega t}\), the system becomes

\[
\dot w_j=(j-|w_j|^2)w_j+(q+4iq)\sum_{\ell=1}^3 w_\ell.
\]

The three components of \(a^+\) sum to zero and have squared moduli \(1,2,3\), hence yield a fixed point. Their complex conjugates give a second fixed point. At either state, for an infinitesimal complex perturbation \(v_j\), the real-linear variational equation is

\[
\dot v_j=-2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{a_j}v_j)a_j+(q+4iq)\sum_{\ell=1}^3v_\ell.
\]

On \(\mathbb R^6\), introduce \(u_1=(1,0)^\mathsf T\), \(u_2=(0,\sqrt2)^\mathsf T\), \(u_3=(-1,-\sqrt2)^\mathsf T\) and \(C(q)=q\begin{pmatrix}1&-4\\4&1\end{pmatrix}\). In two-by-two blocks the real Jacobian is exactly

\[
J_{jk}(q)=-2\delta_{jk}u_ju_j^\mathsf T+C(q),\qquad j,k\in\{1,2,3\}.
\]

Its characteristic polynomial, computed by exact determinant expansion, factors as \(\det(\lambda I-J)=\lambda Q_q(\lambda)\), with

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_q(\lambda)={}&\lambda^5+(12-6q)\lambda^4+(44-60q+153q^2)\lambda^3\\
&+(48-176q+1224q^2)\lambda^2+(-144q+2652q^2)\lambda+1632q^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(q>0\), the zero eigenvalue is simple because \(Q_q(0)=1632q^2>0\). It is generated by the infinitesimal common rotation \(v=ia^+\).

**2. Exact Hurwitz determinants.** Let \(\Delta_k\) denote the leading \(k\)-by-\(k\) principal minor of the standard Hurwitz matrix of the monic real quintic \(Q_q\). Exact expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_1={}&12-6q,\\
\Delta_2={}&-2P_3(q),\\
\Delta_3={}&-16P_5(q),\\
\Delta_4={}&-192qS(q),\\
\Delta_5={}&-313344q^3S(q),
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
P_3(q)=459q^3-486q^2+404q-240,
\]

\[
P_5(q)=70227q^5-78489q^4+51678q^3-24584q^2+6408q-1440.
\]

By the Routh–Hurwitz criterion, for any \(q>0\) all five nonzero eigenvalues have strictly negative real parts precisely when all these five determinants are positive. Thus stability is equivalent to \(q<2\), \(P_3(q)<0\), \(P_5(q)<0\), and \(S(q)<0\).

**3. Exact real-root separation.** Sturm's theorem over the rationals gives the following complete root counts in \((0,2)\): \(P_3\) has exactly one root, lying in \((39/50,79/100)\); \(P_5\) has exactly one root, lying in \((59/100,3/5)\); and \(S\) has exactly two roots, one in \((7/50,3/20)\), the other in \((29/50,59/100)\). These disjoint rational brackets prove the needed ordering. Since \(P_3(0)<0\), \(P_5(0)<0\), \(S(0)>0\), and \(S(1/5)<0\), the simultaneous Hurwitz inequalities hold exactly on \((q_-,q_+)\). For \(q\ge2\), \(\Delta_1\le0\), precluding strict stability. The isolated spectral endpoints satisfy \(\Delta_4=0\) with \(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3,\Delta_5\) behaving as above. The first three minors are positive there while \(\Delta_4=0\). More explicitly, let \(a_1,\ldots,a_5\) be the five nonleading coefficients of \(Q_q\), and put \(\eta=a_5\Delta_2/\Delta_3>0\). The zero-row Routh identity then yields

\[
Q_q(\lambda)=(\lambda^2+\eta)(\lambda^3+a_1\lambda^2+c\lambda+d),
\qquad c=a_4/\eta,\quad d=a_5/\eta.
\]

Because both endpoints exceed \(7/50>144/2652\), we have \(c,d>0\); moreover \(a_1c-d=\Delta_2>0\). The cubic factor is therefore Hurwitz, and the only neutral transverse pair is the **simple** pair \(\lambda=\pm i\sqrt\eta\). The sextic \(S\) is square-free, so its two parameter roots are isolated.

**4. Mirror state and nonlinear orbital attraction.** Let \(R\) be block diagonal with each two-by-two block equal to \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\). The Jacobian for \(a^-\) is exactly \(R J(q)^\mathsf T R\), and therefore has the same characteristic polynomial. The two states are distinct modulo common phase because the first nonzero component fixes the rotation and their second components have opposite signs. Standard smooth orbital linearization at a periodic orbit, using the simple symmetry direction and strict decay in its five transverse modes, implies local orbital exponential attraction on the stated interval. Their disjoint compact periodic orbits therefore have distinct nonempty open basins.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact symbolic matrices and integer-polynomial arithmetic to reconstruct the characteristic polynomial, all five Hurwitz determinants, conjugate-mirror equivalence, the Sturm root counts and rational isolating brackets, and a strictly stable witness at \(q=1/5\). Run it with Python and SymPy; `requirements.txt` states the dependency. A successful execution reports `VERIFY_OK`. The root approximations are secondary to the exact rational Sturm calculations. Symbolic checks support, but do not independently certify, the analytical argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Zhao and Uchida's 6 October 2026 preprint introduces the same globally complex-diffusively coupled heterogeneous Stuart–Landau system and derives the zero-order-parameter polygon construction. Its polygon discussion identifies a triangular sector and uses sampled Jacobian eigenvalues to assess transverse stability in a specific eight-node parameter regime. It does **not** furnish the present sharp two-transition Routh–Hurwitz formula for three nodes on a shape-preserving growth-rate path. The general polygon existence and the conversion to a rotating-frame Jacobian are prior work and are not claimed here.

The prior phase-reduction studies of Bick, Böhle, and Kuehn (2024), and the distinct engineered three-oscillator Stuart–Landau phase model discussed in public 2026 research, do not directly imply this six-dimensional, fully amplitude-resolving, complex-diffusive triangle calculation. Historical priority remains provisional; no claim is made to classify arbitrary network sizes, geometry or coupling ratios.

## Limitations

Only the two explicit zero-mean triangle branches and their full-state *local* orbital stability are classified. Neither global attractor uniqueness nor nonlinear bifurcation type at the two spectral endpoints is established. The growth rates vary with coupling to preserve the triangle geometry, and the result does not assert stability for the source's fixed eight-node numerical parameter family. Externally independent scientific validation has not been performed.
