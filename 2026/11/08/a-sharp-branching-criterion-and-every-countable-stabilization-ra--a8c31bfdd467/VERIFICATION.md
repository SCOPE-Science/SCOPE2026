# Proof verification and limitations

The proof's finite-branching direction depends on the elementary finite-pigeonhole step for the finitely many child cones of each node. The converse uses the exact input set of all immediate children of an infinitely branching node. These two arguments prove a necessary and sufficient condition; they do not depend on experiments.

For the ordinal spectrum, verify the locality lemma for each child cone first. Transfinite induction then shows that \(B_\alpha\) retains its root through stage \(\alpha\), loses every strict descendant by that stage, and becomes empty at \(\alpha+1\). For limit ranks, the zero spine remains infinite in every one of its cones, while each disjoint attached branch vanishes strictly before the cofinal limit. A countability argument gives the sharp bound \(\omega_1\) on the supremum, with no actual uncountable witness. The indicated finite examples are consistency checks, not a substitute for the transfinite proof.

All constructs are subsets of the specified fixed countable prefix space; witnesses need not be effectively given. The unproved issue is external historical priority under potentially different tree-pruning nomenclature. This is a self-review, not independent validation.
