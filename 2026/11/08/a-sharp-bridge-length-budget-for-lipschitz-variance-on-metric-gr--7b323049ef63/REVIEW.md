# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS (self-review).** The area formula gives at least two level crossings except on a set of levels of total length at most the bridge-edge budget. The generalized quantile is absolutely continuous with the sharp derivative-excess constraint. The centered-primitive variance is the positive definite Brownian-bridge kernel quadratic form. Its global maximizer is the indicator of a central interval: strict convexity excludes fractional slopes, and the strictly concave first-variation potential excludes noninterval selections. Exact moment integration and the symmetric barbell construction show sharpness, including the two endpoint budgets. Edgewise-affine examples and the quantile optimization have been tested by exact rational arithmetic. Limits: finite experiments alone do not prove arbitrary graphs or functions.

## Originality

**PASS (qualified, self-review).** Addario-Berry–Janson–McDiarmid [1] study vertex-uniform graph spread rather than arclength and total bridge length. Dzindzalieta [2] treats one-sided deviation rather than the fixed-bridge-length variance optimum. Nakajima–Shioya [3] requires an isoperimetric-profile comparison that is not forced by the aggregate bridge budget; Mazón [4] concerns Cheeger cuts, not the sharp bridge-budget variance. A bridgeless two-crossing envelope is contained in the \(B=0\) case, but does not imply the positive-bridge polynomial and centered-bridge optimizer. Search and comparison cannot exclude all unindexed variants of this theorem.

## Mathematical value

**PASS (self-review).** A single geometric network statistic, the total length of disconnection edges, controls the exact worst possible Lipschitz-to-centered-\(L^2\) embedding norm. The bound is sharp for every feasible budget, moves from the cycle to the path constant, singles out the balanced barbell rather than an asymmetric pendant extremal geometry, and gives a unique extremal output-law profile. This is a full optimal envelope across graph topology and edge length, not a numerical special case.
