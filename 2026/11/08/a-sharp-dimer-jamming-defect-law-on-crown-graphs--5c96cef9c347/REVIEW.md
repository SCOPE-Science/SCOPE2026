# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The object and adsorption rule are explicitly specified. The three transition weights are an exhaustive partition of available ordered cross-part edges; their sum is \(m^2-c\). The conditional first and second factorial moments are independently derived, and the nonnegative algebraic comparison yields a rigorously telescoping bound. It establishes both finite-size inequalities and the limit \(nq_n\to2\). Two distinct exact-state implementations reproduce the finite rational benchmarks. The proof covers \(n\ge2\), with no generalization to nonuniform edge rates.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
The nearest directly matching dynamics is edge-uniform random greedy matching in Dyer and Frieze (1991) and Gamarnik and Goldberg (2008). The accessible full preprint of the latter proves high-girth, constant-degree and bounded-degree results; for \(n\ge4\) the crown family has girth four and unbounded degree, and the preprint’s formally applicable error term is too large to determine the defect probability. Pippenger (1989) discusses edge adsorption but its available abstract treats trees, sparse short-cycle restrictions and general packing. Miller and Pritikin (1997) provides generic performance bounds, not an evaluated crown law in the material accessed. Derangement counts for perfect matchings are not the law of the random irreversible greedy output. The inaccessible complete texts of the earlier matching papers remain genuine priority risks; searched material does not justify a claim of historically proven uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
An exactly solved sequence of dense, highly loopy adsorption networks is an interpretable benchmark for kinetic jamming and the loss induced by irreversible local choices. The probability of the only nonperfect terminal phase has a sharp \(2/n\) law and a reproducible exact finite-size recurrence. This answers a natural kinetic observable, rather than merely counting static perfect matchings. It is not a statement about dynamic rates, arbitrary graphs or thermodynamic phase transitions.
