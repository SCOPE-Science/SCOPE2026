# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is proved for every integer rim length at least four and every positive spoke/rim weight ratio. Local unilateral payoff comparisons reduce the rim's hub-agreeing set to maximal independent, independent, or empty, depending on the ratio. The hub deviation is automatically blocked because those sets have size at most half the rim. The exact refined count follows from cyclic gaps of lengths two and three; the established Perrin and Lucas counts, maximum-cut bound, equilibrium welfare extrema, and phase asymptotics then follow. The accompanying standalone verifier checks 98,112 labeled profiles in 54 rational settings and does not substitute for the infinite proof. Ties at the critical ratio are explicitly included.

## Originality — PASS, with known enumerative overlap and residual priority risk

The paper by Deligkas et al. (arXiv:2305.07124) gives the anti-coordination polymatrix model and general welfare-complexity reductions, not this exact weighted-wheel equilibrium and efficiency phase diagram. The related arXiv:2311.03195 concerns the same general complexity landscape. Bisdorff and Marichal (2008) explicitly record the *known* Perrin count of maximal independent sets of cycles; no novelty is asserted for this preexisting combinatorial identity or the classical Lucas enumeration. The claim instead identifies the exact strategic mapping, the threshold-at-two transition, and the welfare/PoA consequences. General Ising local-stability work is broader in method but no accessible covering wheel theorem was identified. Residual risk: an equivalent result may exist in unindexed graph games, max-cut, or statistical-mechanics literature. The comparison is based on mathematical implications, not absence of matching titles.

## Value — PASS

Wheels are a canonical minimal graph model of a universal influencer coupled to local ring interactions. The weight-two boundary is mathematically sharp: infinitesimal parameter changes switch equilibrium entropy between Perrin, Lucas, and constant regimes, while all equilibrium social costs become optimal at the threshold. This is a full infinite-family phase diagram, not a single small-instance computation or a rebranding of the Perrin recurrence. The closed welfare bound and sharp worst-case inefficiency reveal a distinct efficiency-vs-multiplicity tradeoff.

## Scope and source limitations

The result assumes two actions, identical spoke strengths, and exact best responses. Generic Ising metastability techniques and unindexed equivalent wheel studies remain a priority risk. No claim of independent validation is made. Only the primary mathematical result and its openly acknowledged prior enumerative ingredients are assessed here.
