In a three-individual evolutionary birth--death chain, reciprocally opposed environments with identical frozen fixation probabilities yield strictly higher fixation under every positive switching rate. The response changes sharply at a first-to-second count-change rate ratio of \(2\): above it, the fixation probability overshoots neutrality and has one finite optimum, while below it the approach to neutrality is monotone; no two-individual model can beat its best frozen environment.
