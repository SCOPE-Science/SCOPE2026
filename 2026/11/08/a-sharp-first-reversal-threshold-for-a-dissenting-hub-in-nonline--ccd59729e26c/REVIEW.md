# Scientific review — first-reversal law in nonlinear voter stars

## Correctness — PASS

The specified binary update rates agree with the static-network rule in Ramirez et al. (2023, equation (3)). While the initial hub does not flip, a state with \(j\) opposing leaves has only two possible changes: total leaf-conversion rate \(j\) and center-reversal rate \((j/m)^q\). Competing exponential races yield the entire first-reversal distribution as a finite product. Positivity, normalization, and monotonicity of the factors establish the unique sharp finite-size half-probability threshold. The \(q=1\) and \(q=2\) inequalities place it in \((1,2)\) for every \(m\ge2\). An improper Riemann sum for the logarithm proves the atom-containing limit law and threshold convergence; the proof handles \(0<q<1\), for which the integrand is unbounded at zero. The no-reversal event is correctly treated as only a subset of eventual positive fixation. The standalone exact-rational checker passed 666 checks, including a separate full-star absorption calculation for small graph sizes; finite checking supplements rather than replaces the proof.

## Originality — PASS on a best-knowledge basis

The full original version of Ramirez et al., *Ordering dynamics of nonlinear voter models* (arXiv:2310.20548v1; publicly submitted 2023-10-31), was inspected for definitions, complete-network and complex-network analysis, conclusions, and appendices. It gives the local power-rate rule and pair approximations, but does not state the star first-hub-reversal stopping distribution, its mixed large-size limit, or the half-probability thresholds. The original 2009 Castellano–Muñoz–Pastor-Satorras work (arXiv:0907.1775) examines a related \(q\)-voter rule principally on fully connected networks and regular lattices and does not give this star law. Min–San Miguel (arXiv:1707.08111, 2017) considers network rewiring and well-mixed/degree-regular approximations, not the fixed-star event. The linear heterogeneous-graph voter literature studies fixation and mean consensus times, not the present stopping event. A separate earlier result on star *context-dependent* voter adoption has state-independent asymmetric probabilities for changing to each opinion and computes eventual fixation; its generator is not \((j/m)^q\) and its claim does not imply this transient distribution. Search for aliases, parameter reductions, stronger results, and the exact product did not identify a covering theorem. Routine exponential-race algebra and Riemann-sum methods are not claimed as original; the exact social-model stopping law and size-dependent crossing are the subject of the claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The immediate question is whether a uniquely dissenting influential vertex can convert an initially unanimous opposite neighborhood **without ever reversing its own opinion**. This first-reversal event is not captured by aggregate order parameters or the eventual consensus probability, because a hub can reverse and then reverse back. The complete first-reversal law quantifies a concrete pathwise leadership-stability invariant on the canonical heterogeneous contact network, and the sharp large-star threshold \(1/\log2\) separates high- and low-probability uninterrupted takeovers. It also yields the counterintuitive but rigorous consequence that a lone opinion can prevail with probability above one half despite starting against every other vertex. The distribution's limiting atom and continuous part distinguish no reversal from a positive-sized residual opposition at the first reversal; this is useful for testing approximations to heterogeneous nonlinear opinion dynamics. These findings are more than a reparameterized consensus-time estimate or a numerical table.

## Closest prior literature and limitations

- Ramirez et al., arXiv:2310.20548v1 (full original HTML inspected), defines the identical flip rates and treats population-level ordering, including pair approximation rather than this finite-star path law.
- Castellano et al., arXiv:0907.1775 (full public text inspected), addresses closely related nonlinear \(q\)-voter rules on fully connected systems and lattices, not star-hub reversal.
- Min and San Miguel, arXiv:1707.08111 (public full text inspected), studies coevolving voter networks and regular-network approximations.
- Sood and Redner, DOI:10.1103/PhysRevLett.94.178701 (abstract and accessible summary checked), analyzes linear voter consensus on heterogeneous networks.

The result is restricted to a binary, fixed, unweighted star with initially opposing hub and leaves. The half-probability threshold describes *uninterrupted takeover*, not a necessary or sufficient exact threshold for the entire eventual-fixation distribution (although exceeding one half for the event is sufficient for the latter). Similar claims may exist in unindexed literature, especially work specializing nonlinear voting to star graphs. There is no independent external audit.
