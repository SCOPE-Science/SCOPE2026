# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Newton's identities are used without division except where \(2\) or \(3\) is a unit. At powers of two the possible unknown elementary-symmetric coefficient lies in the two-torsion and multiplies an even power-sum expression. At powers of three, the third, fourth and fifth equalities annihilate the remaining coefficient against all moments via their parity classes modulo three. Explicit witnesses prove every local lower bound; Hensel's lemma is applied only at the three simple roots of \(X^3-X\) modulo three. CRT gives the composite classification. A standalone checker independently corroborates finite instances, but does not prove the infinite statement.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Wooley [1] considers power-sum systems over the integers and the diagonal integer-triple case, not sharp pointwise moment cutoffs modulo arbitrary \(N\). De Vries [2] analyzes algebraic generation of symmetric polynomials and rational-function recovery; its statements do not furnish the exact four-case evaluation threshold on finite residue triples. A separate prior two-summand result is not a stronger statement about three summands. Targeted claim searches found no exact classification. The main residual risk is unindexed earlier literature on separating evaluations of modular symmetric functions or modular moment signatures; absence of a hit is not a priority proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact minimum amount of moment data needed to control all higher moments is a natural structural invariant of modular power-sum systems. The complete all-modulus classification isolates an otherwise invisible, sharp \(3\)-adic obstruction at modulus divisible by nine and distinguishes it from the dyadic obstruction. A nontrivial cubic Hensel construction establishes that five, not merely three, moments may be necessary.

**Scientific limits.** This is a theorem about equality of moment sequences, not unique recovery of triples; it does not settle larger-number-of-summands analogues. It uses a finite reproducibility program only as supplementary evidence. Priority remains subject to further external bibliographic checking.
