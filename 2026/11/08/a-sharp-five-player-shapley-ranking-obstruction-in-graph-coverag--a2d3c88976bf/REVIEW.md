# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The payoff normalization, Shapley value formula \(\phi_x=\deg(x)/2\), inclusion-AUC/first-cover equivalence and rearrangement proof have been reconstructed. The disjoint-clique criterion follows from a fixed marginal-gain multiset and its feasible sorted merge. The exact \(kq\) loss and asymptotic optimum are algebraic consequences of the same multiset. The universal five-player threshold is a *finite theorem*, proved by exhaustive enumeration of all 1099 labeled graphs at orders one through five with two independently implemented optimum computations (subset DP and every permutation); the program passes. Computation does not replace the infinite-family proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Li et al. (arXiv:2607.09869) already exhibit unrestricted Shapley-versus-inclusion-AUC ranking failures, including at four players, but do not claim the sharp unit-edge graph-coverage threshold or disjoint-clique characterization. Biniaz et al. (arXiv:2609.27117) provide general minimum-sum vertex cover algorithms rather than this Shapley-restriction analysis. The published complete-multipartite exact result concerns another graph class/objective; complementing a clique union changes a minimum-cover-time question into a maximum-cover-time question. Targeted research-record and literature searches found no equivalent statement. Earlier graph-heuristic literature not accessible in full could contain an analogous observation.

**Value — PASS.** The substantive distinction is that failures of additive Shapley attribution do not require unrestricted or pathological coalition utilities: normalized monotone submodular *unit-feature* coverage games already exhibit a unique minimal obstruction, a necessary-and-sufficient clique-union criterion, and a tie-breaking-resistant asymptotically nonzero ranking loss. The exact five-player boundary and persistent quantitative loss are motivated by the inclusion-area benchmark rather than an arbitrary graph statistic. These results clarify the boundary of Shapley-ranking guarantees without claiming a universal replacement method.

The claim is restricted to simple unit-edge graph coverage games; weighted edges, arbitrary set coverage, and all graphs beyond the five-player first-order census are not classified. The accessible mathematical sources and remaining originality risks are documented in `AUDIT.json` and `RESULT.md`.
