# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The fixed-set event is feasible precisely when the complementary size is even. Under that event, the complement carries a uniform perfect matching; splitting inversion pairs into nonfixed/nonfixed, fixed/nonfixed and fixed/fixed gives the exact formula. The pair inversion probability \(r/[2(r-1)]\) remains valid at the boundary \(r=2\); \(r=0\) is handled separately. Pointwise optimization of \(a_s(r-a_s)\) gives sharp bounds and exact equality configurations, not merely examples. Exhaustive rational checks independently reproduced the formula and extremizers for every feasible fixed set through order twelve.

## Originality — PASS with historical priority risk
The 2004 Dukes paper explicitly records the Desarménien bivariate inversion/fixed-*count* recurrence, which controls aggregate statistics but cannot identify the precise fixed-label set. The 2005 Forrester–Nagao–Rains paper concerns increasing-subsequence point processes of random involutions with specified counts. The 2025 Pinsky study handles all permutations constrained by fixed *count*, not exact fixed positions in the involution subclass. Queries for fixed-location conditional inversions and extremizers did not return a dominating published result. A more detailed historical prescribed-location enumeration not indexed in the search may exist, so novelty is provisional rather than independently certified.

## Value — PASS
The position of fixed labels changes the expected inversion count even if the fixed count and the transposition count are held fixed. The sharp, uniquely centered maximizer and complete class of \(k+1\) minimizers classify a natural finite-parameter extremal problem and show a possible difference of quadratic order in system size. This gives genuinely location-resolved information that aggregate bivariate enumeration loses.

## Limits
The theorem concerns expected inversions, not full laws or higher moments. Exhaustive checks cannot substitute for the proof; the proof applies at every admissible size. Uninspected historically refined fixed-set results remain a citation/priority risk.
