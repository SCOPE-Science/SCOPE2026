# Scientific review: random-start skew Toeplitz GMRES

**Correctness (PASS).** The statement is confined to a fixed iteration count and even-dimensional, nonsingular, real skew Toeplitz matrices. Exact even/odd Krylov orthogonality proves odd-step stagnation. The Chebyshev kernel is the unique limiting degree-\(2q\) constrained least-squares minimizer, and an explicit Dirichlet-kernel fourth moment gives the variance. Gaussian quadratic-form Lindeberg theory, with the sphere-normalization correction and a second-order optimization remainder, yields the limiting normal law. The standalone verifier reports `VERIFY_OK 5540`, including exact rational identities and direct finite GMRES checks. Finite tests are not a substitute for the limit proof.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** Chen–Soodhalter (arXiv:2609.24751) derive constraints on attainable Toeplitz GMRES behavior and use random starting vectors only in numerical constructions. Liesen–Strakoš and Li–Zhang study deterministic Toeplitz convergence and bounds. Titley-Peloquin–Pestana–Wathen give weighted spectral GMRES formulas, while the 2022 CG/MINRES covariance CLT studies a different randomized matrix setting. Published mathematical-record comparisons included the critical-tolerance CG Haar power-law result and a symmetric one-step GMRES error barrier; neither implies the explicit random-start skew Toeplitz CLT. The precise combination of Dirichlet fourth moment, sphere normalization, and optimization perturbation is not stated in those inspected sources. Historical priority remains uncertain because general Haar quadratic-form CLTs and polynomial kernels can yield this specialization if combined.

**Value (PASS).** For a canonical non-Hermitian stencil, the theorem determines the typical fixed-step residual and its nonzero, sharp \(N^{-1/2}\) uncertainty scale for **every fixed** even step, including a repeated odd-step plateau. This materially differentiates typical randomized right-hand sides from deterministic worst-case curves and supplies an exact benchmark for Krylov solvers. No growing-iteration, finite-precision or general-stencil performance assertion is made.

The 2022 CG/MINRES CLT was assessed from its published abstract, not its complete text. Potential prior specialization of a general theorem remains the key risk.
