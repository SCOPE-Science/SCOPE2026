# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Stationarity reduces to a single conserved edge current. An arbitrary critical point with any nonpositive strong-edge cosine cannot have positive-definite transverse Hessian: either the quadratic form is rank deficient or its restriction to a strong-edge difference is nonpositive. Thus every hyperbolic sink is captured by one common strong-edge angle. The Chebyshev square identity gives the exact threshold \(w=4/5\); a five-coordinate Hessian congruence gives the branch indices. The admissible discrete-step bound ensures every positive Hessian eigenvalue produces a contracting map multiplier. Exact symbolic and numerical checks from the standalone code agree with the proof. The result is for all \(w>0\) and admissible \(h\), not an extrapolation of the finite tests.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** Buescu, D'Aniello and Oliveira, arXiv:2610.10993v1 (2026), prove gradient and finite-step structure and note twisted cycle minima, but do not calculate the complete single-defect six-cycle bifurcation. Delabays, Coletta and Jacquod, arXiv:1512.04266v2 (2015/2016), give general winding and stable-count bounds, which permit but do not decide whether there are one or three sinks at a specified link ratio. Searches of other finite-network stability records and literature did not find the specific sharp threshold or branch formulas. Historical priority is provisional; broader uninspected physical ring literature could contain an equivalent formula.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is the smallest even cycle admitting hyperbolically stable nonzero winding in the homogeneous attractive case. The exact \(4/5\) single-link threshold quantifies robustness of its chiral attractors against an edge defect, shows both chirality classes are lost simultaneously, and is uniform over an explicit noninfinitesimal step interval. The explicit complete sink count and saddle-node geometry provide more information than a general existence or generic topology statement.
