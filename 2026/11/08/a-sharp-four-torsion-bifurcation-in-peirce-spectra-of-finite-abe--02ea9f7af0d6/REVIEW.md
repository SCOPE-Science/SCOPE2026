# Scientific review: A sharp four-torsion bifurcation in Peirce spectra of finite abelian core quandles

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is reconstructed from the explicit multiplication \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\) and its Fourier transfer. Idempotency forces inversion-symmetric Fourier support, yielding diagonalizable blocks for every nonzero idempotent, including those with augmentation zero. In the absence of four-torsion, squaring lands in a finite odd-order character group, and the cyclic idempotency recursion restricts every block product to zero or one. Boundary witnesses include basis idempotents, the uniform idempotent, and a precise character-derived one-parameter family when four-torsion exists. The construction covers all finite abelian groups, including the one-element group. ## Originality — qualified PASS

Zhang (arXiv:2610.08204v1, Theorem 5.5) already proves the three-point right Peirce spectrum for nontrivial odd-order finite abelian cores. Elhamdadi–Nunez–Singh (arXiv:2207.09257v1, §7) gives order-three examples and the original odd-dihedral conjecture. Kaur–Singh (arXiv:2303.06948, §6) provides an all-order decomposition into right-simple representation modules; Theorems 6.9 and 6.12 were inspected and neither states the union over all idempotent spectra or the four-torsion threshold. Actual semantic searches for Peirce, even dihedral, Takasaki and Fourier aliases and accessible earlier algebra results found no equivalent classification. The closest records on modular R3 idempotents and Fourier spectral subsets have different coefficient fields or invariants. Full texts of arXiv:2609.03799 and arXiv:2608.09149 were unavailable: they may contain related deductions. This priority limitation does not assert uniqueness or independent novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The right Peirce spectrum is a natural invariant of nonassociative quandle algebras. A complete sharp trichotomy replaces the odd-only theorem with a uniform all-abelian-core classification. The existence of arbitrary complex eigenvalues precisely at four-torsion, alongside universal diagonalizability, pinpoints the structural feature responsible for the change and supplies explicit witnesses for further analysis.

## Closest literature and limits

Source differences are mathematical: Zhang's odd-order hypothesis excludes every even case; the 2023 Takasaki decomposition concerns representation constituents rather than the idempotent-dependent spectral union; a rank-three non-Latin quandle with full spectrum is not a counterexample to the classification within finite abelian cores. The result assumes complex coefficients and right multiplication, does not classify general Alexander quandles or all idempotents, and has no independent expert verification. The two inaccessible but plausible 2026 full texts and alternate nomenclature remain the principal historical risks.
