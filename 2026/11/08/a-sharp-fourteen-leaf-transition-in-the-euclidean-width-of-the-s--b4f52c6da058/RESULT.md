# A sharp fourteen-leaf transition in the Euclidean width of the simplex-sum stellohedron

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge2\), let \(n=m+1\), let \(0\) be the hub of the star with leaves \([m]\), and take its **unit tube-simplex realization**
\[
K_m=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[m]}\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{e_0\}\cup\{e_i:i\in S\}\bigr)\subset\mathbb R^{m+1}.
\]
Widths are measured intrinsically in its Euclidean affine hull, parallel to \(H=\{u:\sum_{i=0}^m u_i=0\}\). Put \(h=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
w_{\min}(K_m)=
\begin{cases}
2^{m-1}\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{m}},&2\le m\le13,\\[4pt]
\bigl(2^m-2^{m-h}\bigr)\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{h(m+1-h)}},&m\ge14.
\end{cases}\quad}
\]
Every minimizing oriented unit normal is of the form
\[
\pm\frac{\mathbf1_B-(|B|/n)\mathbf1}{\sqrt{|B|(n-|B|)/n}},
\qquad B\subseteq[m],
\]
where \(|B|=1\) for \(m\le13\), and \(|B|=\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor\) for \(m\ge14\). There are no additional minimizers. The number of unoriented minimum-width lines jumps from \(m\) to \(\binom{m}{\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor}\) at \(m=14\). There is no tie at the transition.

## Assumptions and scope
The metric is the ordinary ambient Euclidean metric restricted to the dimension-\(m\) affine span of the displayed **specific** Minkowski-sum realization; translating the polytope does not affect width. No assertion is made for arbitrary affine-equivalent or merely combinatorially equivalent stellohedra, or for nonuniform simplex weights. A width in unit direction \(u\in H\) is \(h_{K_m}(u)+h_{K_m}(-u)\), where \(h_K\) is the support function.

## Proof
**1. Cut reduction and all equality directions.** Fix a weak coordinate ordering of an arbitrary nonzero \(u\in H\). The cone of vectors respecting that ordering is generated by the centered indicators \(v_j=\mathbf1_{B_j}-(j/n)\mathbf1\), where \(B_j\) comprises the largest \(j\) coordinates, \(1\le j<n\). Indeed, if the ordered adjacent gaps are \(\delta_j\ge0\), then \(u=\sum_j\delta_jv_j\). For each coordinate simplex \(\Delta_T=\operatorname{conv}\{e_i:i\in T\}\), the directional width is \(\max_{i\in T}u_i-\min_{i\in T}u_i\); this is linear in all \(\delta_j\) in the fixed ordering. Therefore \(w(u)=\sum_j\delta_j w(v_j)\). The triangle inequality gives
\[
\frac{w(u)}{\|u\|}\ge
\min_{j:\delta_j>0}\frac{w(v_j)}{\|v_j\|}.
\]
If two or more gaps are positive, the inequality is strict: the centered indicator rays associated with distinct proper initial segments are not positive collinear, so the Euclidean triangle inequality is strict; every positive step width is nonzero. Thus **every** minimizing direction has exactly two coordinate levels and is a signed centered proper-subset indicator.

**2. Tube count.** For a centered cut indicator \(v_B\) with \(B\subseteq[m]\), \(|B|=b\), \(1\le b\le m\), its width is exactly the number of connected tubes meeting both sides of the cut. Such a tube has the hub and at least one leaf of \(B\). Of the \(2^m\) hub-containing tubes, exactly \(2^{m-b}\) avoid \(B\). Hence
\[
\frac{w(v_B)}{\|v_B\|}
=(2^m-2^{m-b})\sqrt{\frac{n}{b(n-b)}}
=2^m\sqrt n\;f_n(b),\qquad
f_n(b)=\frac{1-2^{-b}}{\sqrt{b(n-b)}}.
\]
For \(b>n/2\), the mirror \(b'=n-b<n/2\) is a valid leaf count, has the same denominator, and has a **strictly smaller** numerator. Therefore every optimizing \(b\) is at most \(h=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\).

**3. Exact one-dimensional minimization.** For \(b\ge2\) define
\[
A_b=\left(\frac{1-2^{-(b+1)}}{1-2^{-b}}\right)^2-1
=\frac{2^{b+2}-3}{4(2^b-1)^2}
=\frac1{2^b-1}+\frac1{4(2^b-1)^2}.
\]
We have \(bA_b<1\) for \(b\ge2\): at \(b=2\) the product is \(13/18\), and thereafter it strictly decreases because \(A_{b+1}<A_b/2\) and \(b+1<2b\). Comparing consecutive squares shows, whenever \(b+1\le h\),
\[
f_n(b+1)<f_n(b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n>T_b:=\frac{2b+1-A_b b^2}{1-A_b b}.
\]
For \(b=2,3,4,5\) the exact values of \(T_b\) are, respectively, \(64/5\), \(1111/109\), \(1781/164\), and \(13053/1073\), all less than \(14\). For every \(b\ge6\),
\[
T_b-(2b+2)=\frac{A_b b(b+2)-1}{1-A_bb}<0.
\]
To justify the last strict sign uniformly: \(A_6\,6\cdot8=1012/1323<1\), and \(A_{b+1}<A_b/2\) while \((b+1)(b+3)<2b(b+2)\), so the product \(A_b b(b+2)\) strictly decreases for \(b\ge6\). Since \(b+1\le h\) implies \(n\ge2b+2\), these bounds prove that for every \(n\ge14\), \(f_n(b)\) decreases **strictly** through all integer \(2\le b\le h\). Hence for \(n\ge14\) the sole candidates are \(b=1\) and \(b=h\).

For \(n\le14\), each fixed \(b\ge2\) has the ratio
\[
\frac{f_n(b)^2}{f_n(1)^2}=
4(1-2^{-b})^2\frac{n-1}{b(n-b)},
\]
which decreases as \(n\) increases (for all admissible \(n\ge2b\)). Thus it suffices to check \(n=14\), \(b=2,\ldots,7\). The six exact ratios, in order, are
\[
\frac{39}{32},\quad\frac{637}{528},\quad\frac{585}{512},\quad
\frac{12493}{11520},\quad\frac{17199}{16384},\quad\frac{209677}{200704},
\]
all strictly greater than one. Consequently \(b=1\) is uniquely optimal for every \(n\le14\).

Finally set \(g_n=f_n(h)/f_n(1)\). We have \(g_{14}^2=209677/200704>1\) and \(g_{15}^2=16129/16384<1\). The sequence \(g_n\) strictly decreases for \(n\ge14\): from \(2h\) to \(2h+1\), the squared ratio is \(2h^2/((h+1)(2h-1))<1\); from \(2h+1\) to \(2h+2\), it is \((1+A_h)(2h+1)/(2h+2)<1\) for \(h\ge7\), since \(A_7(15)=7635/64516<1\) and the products \(A_h(2h+1)\) strictly decrease for \(h\ge7\), using \(A_{h+1}<A_h/2\) and \(2h+3<2(2h+1)\). Thus \(b=h\) is uniquely optimal for \(n\ge15\), and \(b=1\) uniquely optimal for \(n\le14\). Together with step-ray strictness, this proves every assertion.

It prints `STELLOHEDRON_WIDTH_CHECKS_PASSED direct=1154 exact_cut_comparisons=90983`. ## Relationship to prior work
Behrend (2024), Section 3(v), explicitly describes the same standard star-graph associahedron as a tube-simplex Minkowski sum, but studies Ehrhart enumeration of partial permutohedra; it does not give a Euclidean-width phase transition. Dao et al. (2019) study face structures, shellings, and extended nestohedra; Berry et al. (2019, first preprint 2016) study combinatorial and algebraic structures of stellohedra; Ivanović (2019) studies geometric realizations of related permutoassociahedra. These are acknowledged as the sources of the family and methodology, not claimed as new.

The general reduction of simplex-sum widths to two-level directions is a useful standard type of normal-fan argument; by itself it is not the central novelty claim. The claimed contribution is the **exact stellohedron width, exhaustive normal classification, and sharp integer switch at fourteen leaves**. A mean random-direction width law for this very same stellohedron, as well as a vertex-covariance theorem for it, answer different metric questions and imply neither this minimum nor its contact classification. Existing inball and circumball results give different extrema and do not determine the narrowest supporting-hyperplane separation.

## Limitations
The classification depends on the unit coefficient of every hub-containing nontrivial coordinate simplex and on the particular Euclidean embedding. Other weights or affine realizations may have different width phases. No assertion is made about arbitrary graph associahedra or their global width order. Historical priority has not been independently certified, and an equivalent prior theorem stated through generalized permutohedral support functions could remain to be found. The finite tests are not an exhaustive search over all real directions; the proof provides that reduction.
