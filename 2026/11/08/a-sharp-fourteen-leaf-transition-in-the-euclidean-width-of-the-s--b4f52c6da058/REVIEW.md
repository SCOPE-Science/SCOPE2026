# Scientific review of the exact stellohedron minimum-width transition

## Correctness — PASS
The proof explicitly fixes a dimension-\(m\) affine Euclidean hyperplane and the unit tube-simplex realization. In a sorted Weyl chamber, every centered vector is a nonnegative sum of nested centered cut indicators; each summand simplex contributes a support-width linear in those gaps. Positive width plus strict Euclidean triangle inequality excludes all non-step minimizers. The tube count \(2^m-2^{m-b}\), normalization \(\sqrt{b(n-b)/n}\), and symmetry comparison for cuts larger than half the vertices are direct. The remaining integer minimization is fully proved, including exact rational checks at the \(n=14,15\) boundary and a uniform consecutive-ratio comparison for unbounded \(n\). The checker supplies separate exact arithmetic and direct-tube sanity tests; no finite computation substitutes for infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk
The relevant Behrend full text, arXiv:2403.06975, Sections 2–4, contains a direct star tube-simplex realization but studies Ehrhart polynomials and enumerative equivalences, not Euclidean minimum width. Dao et al., arXiv:1912.00273, introductory stellohedron description and Section 7, address combinatorial shelling, partial permutations and face numbers, not the narrowest support direction. The Berry–Forcey–Ronco–Showers 2019 full-text article concerns Hopf structures and tubings; Ivanović's 2019 preprint concerns a different Minkowski realization of related polytopes. The closest searchable published geometric records on this exact body describe inballs, circumballs, random-direction mean widths, Minkowski asymmetry, and vertex covariance; none of their stated extremal claims imply this sharp cut-width transition. A general result under another terminology might provide an older equivalent formula and has not been ruled out absolutely.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Minimum Euclidean width is a canonical metric invariant of a standard simplex-sum graph associahedron. The previously unrelated width expectations and radius formulas do not identify a narrowest direction. Its exact minimum is controlled by a sharp, non-obvious integer switch from \(m\) single-leaf normal lines to \(\binom m{\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor}\) balanced-cut lines precisely at dimension fourteen. The classification exhausts the entire infinite family for this canonical embedding rather than reporting an arbitrary finite calculation.

## Boundaries
This does not extend to all affine realizations, all positive simplex weights, or all graph associahedra. The novelty comparison is a self-review rather than independent historical certification. No independent proof audit has been performed.
