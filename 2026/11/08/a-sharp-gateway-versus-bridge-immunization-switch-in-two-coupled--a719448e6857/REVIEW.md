# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The objective is the uniformly seeded expected size of an independent-bond cluster, equivalent to \(1+\frac{2}{2m}\sum_{u<v}\Pr(u\leftrightarrow v)\). Pair counting yields the two general formulas. A coupling strictly dominates ordinary-vertex immunization. At \(m=3\), direct-versus-two-step connection in a triangle gives \(q_3=p+p^2-p^3\), and subtraction gives the cubic certificate. The fractional substitution \(p=t/(1+t)\) transforms its cubic factor to \(1+4t-t^3\), with exactly one positive zero by Descartes' rule and signs at endpoints. These steps cover the complete parameter interval, including the ties. The standalone standard-library checker reconstructs every bond state in the two smallest family members and passed 32 exact assertions on 412 configurations; it is corroboration, not an infinite proof. The asymptotic part for \(m\ge3\) uses \(q_m=p+O(p^2)\) and the exact \(p=1\) endpoint.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence, provisional

Published targeted-immunization work (Pastor-Satorras–Vespignani, 2001), community-aware strategies (Masuda, 2009; Gupta et al., 2014), and approximate optimization (Altarelli et al., 2013) establish the general importance of bridge nodes and vaccination. The inspected accessible article sections do not state the defined uniform-one-seed, independent-bond exact two-triangle optimizer or its unique algebraic threshold. They address large-network thresholds, component fragmentation, heuristic rankings or general approximate methods. General optimization formulations are broader in model coverage but do not mathematically imply the displayed exact switching classification without carrying out this finite exact calculation. Historical priority remains provisional because further specialized network-immunization examples, appendices and earlier finite-graph analyses could exist. The claim is not that a topology-based reversal is new in principle.

## Value — PASS

Two dense communities joined through a degree-two gateway are a minimal natural modular topology in which degree targeting competes with strategic disconnection. The first switch within this family is exactly classified, while a general-size argument proves the conflict persists for all larger equal cliques. The precise crossover matters for choosing between the two natural vaccine targets under an explicit outbreak-risk objective; it is not a gratuitous parameter slice or a numerical-only counterexample. It complements, rather than replaces, general immunization optimization.
