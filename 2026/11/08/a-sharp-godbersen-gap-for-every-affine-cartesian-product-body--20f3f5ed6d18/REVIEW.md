# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. The coefficient of each mixed-volume polynomial is normalized consistently with \(V_n(K[n])=\operatorname{vol}_n(K)\). The Alexandrov--Fenchel inequality provides the lower coefficient bound; the 2026 Godbersen theorem provides the upper coefficient bound. Volume polynomials factor for Cartesian products. Vandermonde's identity permits merging factors, and the verified, published two-by-two Hankel minor inequality for type-B Narayana polynomials shifts the factor partition until one dimension is one. The coefficient algebra and equality classifications were reconstructed. A standard-library exact verifier passed 1,048,469 coefficient comparisons. No extrapolation from finite tests is required for the analytic theorem.

## Originality

PASS, with a residual historical-priority qualification. The principal 2026 papers prove the universal simplex extremal inequality and equality cases, but do not give the explicit sharp, stronger coefficientwise bound on all affine Cartesian-product bodies. The 2021 polynomial theorem proves coefficientwise Hankel positivity, but not its geometric mixed-volume consequence. Known prism formulas capture the sharp witness family without comparison across arbitrary higher-dimensional factors. No equivalent or dominating assertion was found in targeted mathematical-record, literature and accessible earlier-claim checks. A nonindexed prior combination of the ingredients remains possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The recent universal extremal theorem identifies simplices, but does not quantify how a nontrivial affine-product decomposition forces an index-specific gap. Here the sharp gap is \(2k(n-k)/n^2\), uniformly around one-half at a central index, and the simultaneous equality class is determined. The result applies to arbitrary full-dimensional convex factors, not just polytopes, and gives an explicit structural stability separation. It is a meaningful application of the nontrivial coefficientwise Hankel-total positivity theorem, not a renaming or an arbitrary finite sample.

## Comparison and limitations

The theorem relies on the published all-dimensional Godbersen inequality and published total positivity, and is specific to affine products with at least two positive-dimensional factors. It does not settle stability under approximate product structure; it does not classify equality for every individual index. Priority remains subject to independent verification.
