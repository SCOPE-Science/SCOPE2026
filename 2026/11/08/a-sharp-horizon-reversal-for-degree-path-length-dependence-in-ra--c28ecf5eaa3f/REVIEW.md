# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For every labeled arrival pair, changing one parent edge shifts the depths of exactly the final descendants of that arrival. Their expected count is \(n/j\), independent of the pre-arrival tree and chosen parent. Exact expected-depth calculations yield the local mixed covariance, and a telescoping sum gives the finite global identity. The strict inverse-square tail bound proves the eventual negative sign for every nonroot birth label; the signed increments change sign at most once. Euler–Maclaurin tail bounds and harmonic expansions prove the sharp \(2i^2\log i\) scale. Exact-rational enumeration and two independent closed forms were checked for all recursive trees through nine vertices; numeric threshold checks supplement but do not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** The nearest previous root-attachment/path-length result covers label \(i=1\) only. Its local comparison technique does not state the nonroot reversal or quadratic-logarithmic threshold. Full-source comparison with Dobrow–Fill (1999) found total-path-length moments but no labeled outdegree covariance. Lodewijks (2022) treats joint degree/depth of finitely many vertices, not their covariance with the sum of all vertices' depths. Janson (2004/2005) treats degree-count covariances. Later external-path-length work concerns the sum of leaf depths, not labeled degree. A broader analytic framework may encode the mixed expectation implicitly; this possibility does not establish direct prior coverage of the sharp threshold, but requires independent historical scrutiny.

**Value — PASS.** The degree of a nonroot birth-labeled vertex is a natural local statistic of a growing random tree; total path length is the search-cost statistic. Their covariance is positive shortly after the vertex's arrival yet negative at sufficiently long horizons, in sharp contrast with the root. The unique transition and its explicit quadratic-logarithmic asymptotic identify the natural timescale of the reversal. This is a structural classification, not a routine restatement of the root case or a small numerical improvement.

**Limitations.** Only uniform recursive trees; no nonlinear association or closed expression for the exact integer transition is asserted. Historical implicit-coverage risk remains, and no independent audit is claimed.
