# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The Ewens forced-arrow law is proved by contracting compatible prescribed arrows and retaining the weight of any completed cycles. Summing exactly the descending image pairs of adjacent input labels gives the descent mean. Conditioning on a specified fixed point reduces to an Ewens permutation on the remaining labels; the three possible incidences then give every mixed covariance. Exact rational simplification yields the constant remote term and aggregate polynomial. The denominator is strictly positive on the stated domain. The packaged standard-library checker confirms 225302 exact assertions, including exhaustive permutation enumeration up to seven labels, but the infinite quantifiers rely on the explicit proof.

## Originality (O): PASS, with residual priority risk

The principal new scope is the complete **position-resolved**, nonuniform Ewens fixed-point–descent cross-covariance matrix and the parameter-unique dense/local sign transition. The existing uniform case is explicitly credited, and aggregate mean formulas obtainable from class or plethystic enumerators are not presented as standalone novelty. Kammoun treats limiting descent processes for Ewens permutations; Kim–Lee treat class-conditioned descent numbers; Hultman class-averaged permutation statistics; Gessel–Zhuang total fixed-point/cycle-type and descent generating functions; Paguyo different sampling constraints. A concrete pair of permutations with identical cycle type and descent set but different fixed-point sets demonstrates that these aggregate generating functions do not directly specify the labelled mixed matrix. published-finding corpus semantic searches did not reveal an equivalent nonuniform positional claim, but are not exhaustive. Historical priority is qualified by the possibility of an earlier position-sensitive formula or derivation hidden in broader enumeration work.

## Value (V): PASS

A covariance matrix with \(n(n-1)\) potentially nonzero entries is completely classified by three elementary cases, and a single special sampling parameter \(\theta=1\) separates strictly positive from strictly negative long-range mixed dependence. The sign switch and constancy at all nonincident positions are structural, not simply a new value of a known total-count formula. This also explains how a strictly negative total covariance for \(n\ge4\) coexists with positive remote individual covariances for \(0<\theta<1\), while \(n=3\) admits a sharp additional sign threshold. The precise position-resolved law is useful for local statistics under cycle-biased sampling.
