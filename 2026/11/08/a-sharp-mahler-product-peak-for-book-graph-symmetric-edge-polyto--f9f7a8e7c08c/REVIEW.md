# Scientific review: book-graph Mahler-product profile

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is the unweighted triangular book family for integer page count \(m\ge1\). Choosing a root basis produces an explicit polar defined by \(|t|\le1\), \(|w_i|\le1\), and \(|t-w_i|\le1\). Its sections are boxes of side \(2-|t|\), yielding the polar volume. Exact support maximization gives a primal fibre of length \(2-\sum|y_i|\) over the cross-polytope, so its volume is exact as well. Dual-coordinate Jacobians cancel in the affine-invariant product. One rational difference identity and a separate induction establish the unique peak for *all* integers, rather than relying on finite tests. The included checker corroborates exact geometry for dimensions two to five and rational identities in a wider finite range. The formulas, normalization, and strict endpoint cases were reconstructed directly.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority

The closest structural paper, Ohsugi--Tsuchiya (arXiv:2008.08621), already computes the normalized primal volume of a star suspension; that prior result is excluded from the novelty claim. Alexander--Fradelizi--García-Lirola--Zvavitch (arXiv:1911.10642) investigate finite Lipschitz-free volume products and general extremal/decomposition principles, without the evaluated polar volume and three-page book peak. D'Alì--Delucchi--Michałek (arXiv:1910.05193) treat general symmetric edge polytopes, including volume calculations for other families. Higashitani--Mori (arXiv:2608.02445) single out book graphs in a decomposability problem but not in Mahler-product evaluation. Searches of available mathematical-result records for book/thagomizer terminology, normalized polar-volume formulas, and equivalent Mahler-product expressions did not return a theorem whose hypotheses and conclusion imply the full new claim. This is not a proof of historical uniqueness: a specialized Lipschitz-polytope volume formula may exist under another name.

## Value — PASS

The family is a canonical graph-polytopal construction of many triangles sharing one edge, also appearing as the suspension of a star. The exact affine-invariant product completes the previously known primal-volume calculation with a polar evaluation. Its nonmonotone profile has a sharp, uniquely attained finite-size optimum at three pages, while larger books approach the Hanner benchmark from above with an exact first-order rate. The all-dimensional and endpoint-sharp classification makes the fact more than a numerical census or parameter substitution.

## Limits and closest literature

The result does not extend to weighted books or arbitrary graphs. The polar-volume and extremum combination is novel only to the extent supported by accessible full-text and mathematical-record searches. A source phrased solely in graph-metric Lipschitz terminology could cover it; that residual priority risk is stated in the structured audit. The prior primal-volume result is properly credited.
