The Fourier multiplier is exactly \(2\sin(n\pi/2)/(n\pi)\), yielding the isolated critical mode \(n=\pm3\) at \(D_c=2/(27\pi)\). The complementary spectral gap supports Lyapunov–Schmidt and center-manifold reductions. This proves a nontrivial positive, locally radially attracting resident branch for slightly smaller diffusion. For every positive invader diffusion, a self-adjoint periodic elliptic operator has a simple positive principal eigenfunction. The Rayleigh principle and positivity of \(KU\) prove unique competition cutoff; nonconstancy of \(KU\) gives strictly negative diffusion derivative, while \(U\) itself proves the cutoff equals one at matching diffusion. Endpoint limits, the second-order expansion, and the exact slope follow from variational limits and perturbation theory. The new packaged `verify.py` checks exact Fourier/cubic algebra and cutoff coefficients. Neither finite mode sampling nor numeric collocation was used as an infinite-dimensional existence or uniqueness proof.

Residual scientific limits: only small-amplitude residents and linear invasion are established; no nonlinear two-species coexistence classification. At neutral cutoff, linear spectral neutrality does not settle nonlinear behavior. Expansion in the resident amplitude is for fixed positive invader diffusion, not uniform down to zero.

## Value — PASS

The originating 2022 study reports a landscape of numerical competitive exclusion, coexistence and self-organized patterns but leaves general necessary/sufficient parameter boundaries difficult. This result determines a mathematically sharp necessary-and-sufficient *linear introduction* condition across **every positive invader mobility** for an explicit small patterned resident and resolves its relation to the conventional homogeneous competition threshold. It quantifies the mobile competitor's initial ecological advantage or disadvantage by an exact perturbative coefficient and explicit limiting bounds. The finite-range kernel is the model's natural top-hat interaction, the first patterned wavelength is selected by its discrete Fourier spectrum, and the invariant comparison of invader versus resident movement rate is intrinsically motivated. The conclusion is not merely a numerical example, a renamed classical formula, or a claim that nonlocality can favor coexistence (already known). It supplies a precise reusable invasion classification for this biologically motivated nonlocal competition family, though it is explicitly limited to linear invasibility.
