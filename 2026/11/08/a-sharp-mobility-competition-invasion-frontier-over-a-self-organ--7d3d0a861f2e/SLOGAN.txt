In a two-species finite-range nonlocal competition model, a self-organized positive resident pattern has a unique, strictly decreasing invader diffusion–competition threshold \(a_c(d)\) that crosses the conventional exclusion value \(1\) exactly at the resident's own diffusion rate. On an explicit supercritical three-wave branch, every slower-diffusing rare invader can initially grow despite stronger interspecific competition, and the sharp threshold has an exact small-amplitude expansion; nonlinear coexistence is not asserted.
