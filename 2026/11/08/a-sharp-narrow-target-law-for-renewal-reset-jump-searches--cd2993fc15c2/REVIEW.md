# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The result assumes independently resampled positive competing clocks and bounded continuous jump density. The number of jumps in each reset-delimited excursion has geometric survival; its duration has mean \(m/q\) by the stopping-time version of Wald's identity, without assuming independence of each duration and its event type. A pairwise probability bound controls all multiple landings by \(O(\varepsilon^2)\); dominated convergence identifies success probability \(2\varepsilon G(a)+o(\varepsilon)\). A renewal equation yields the mean law, and its Laplace transform yields the exponential limit. Countability proves exact point polarity, while the first event time lower-bounds the capture time when its mean diverges. Explicit Pareto survival integration proves the sharp exponent threshold. The checker gives supplementary finite numerical bounds and reproduces the explicit Gaussian coefficient.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The full text of Li–Zhang–Liu, arXiv:2609.09738, was read in the model, stationary-state, Gaussian and first-arrival sections. Their (63)–(64) determine the occupation measure; their fixed-target formulas use long-wave/diffusive approximations and do not formulate or prove a small-positive-interval landing asymptotic, an exponential rare-capture limit, or the two-Pareto-clock threshold above exponent sum one. The Brownian narrow-capture literature studies continuous-path absorption with distinct geometry, and lattice random-walk first-arrival results use atomically reachable sites. Indexed-result and accessible historical-claim comparisons found no identified stronger covering theorem. Remaining risk: a broader unindexed rare-set hitting theorem for regenerative processes could imply the asymptotic once this model is instantiated; publisher-only primary articles were examined at abstract level and do not justify whole-document noncoverage claims.

## Value — PASS

The exact distinction between point landing and diffusive crossing is essential for interpreting a physical intermittent jump-search mechanism. The theorem supplies both a leading search-time constant and a universal limiting search-time distribution for arbitrarily small but real detector widths; it also locates the sharp boundary where competition between individually heavy-tailed clocks restores finite mean detection. The exact continuous-density coefficient permits quantitative calibration without making a diffusion approximation, and the proof records the precise detection convention and failure regime. This is more than a normalization check or a finite small-parameter table.
