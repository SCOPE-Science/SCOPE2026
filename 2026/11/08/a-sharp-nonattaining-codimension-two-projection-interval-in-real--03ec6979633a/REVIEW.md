# Scientific review: sharp nonattaining codimension-two projection interval

## Correctness — PASS
For \(0<t\le1\), the parameterized two-dimensional space is the kernel of \((1,1,t)\) inside real \(\ell_1^3\). Every projection onto it has the rank-one-complement form \(I-u(1,1,t)\), and an explicit positive dual combination of three column lower bounds proves that its norm is at least \(1+t/(1+t+t^2)\). One rationally defined projection reaches the bound; equality forces its uniqueness. The three-block measure embedding is isometric, the Borel-mass retraction is contractive, and Daugavet duality identifies the codimension-two constant as one plus this finite-dimensional norm. A weak-star continuous minimizer would have continuous coefficient vectors saturated against two incompatible signed facets at the common interval boundary, which is impossible because the vectors lie in the nontrivial kernel of \((1,1,t)\). The monotonicity derivative gives the entire interval, including the sharp endpoint. The finite rational checker confirms the finite-dimensional identities but is not used to justify the analytical universal statements.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not independently certified)
Kania and Lewicki (arXiv:2604.10771v1, 2026) give the duality theorem and nonattaining codimension-two constants through \((3+\sqrt2)/2\), then ask in Question 6.2 whether the complete fixed-codimension interval up to the maximal absolute projection constant can be realized. Their unrestricted-codimension existence theorem does not imply this fixed-codimension result. The classical real planar Grünbaum value \(4/3\) is not claimed as new: the novel assertion is that **every** value in the full codimension-two interval \((2,7/3]\), including the endpoint, is attained as a projection **infimum without a minimizing projection** in explicit three-block continuous-function subspaces. The argument is not the duplication-stability theorem from the motivating paper: it needs only the unique three-column minimizer and incompatibility of norming faces. Comparison with relevant published mathematical claims did not identify a result implying this sharp nonattainment interval. Alternate formulations in older minimal-projection literature remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result resolves the fixed-dimension \(n=2\) instance of an explicitly posed quantitative existence problem and reaches the largest possible constant \(1+\lambda_2=7/3\), not merely a small improvement of an example. The family involves the natural condition of prescribed averages over three adjacent intervals and exposes a reusable geometric obstruction to weak-star minimality when coordinate norming faces cannot meet. It requires no arbitrary computational cutoff. It does not resolve the corresponding questions in higher codimensions or over complex scalars.
