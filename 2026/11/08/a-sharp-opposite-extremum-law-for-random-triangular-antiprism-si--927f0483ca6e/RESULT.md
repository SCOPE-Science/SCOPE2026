# A sharp opposite-extremum law for random triangular-antiprism silhouettes

## Finding
For every height parameter \(h>0\), let \(K_h\subset\mathbb R^3\) be the centrally symmetric right triangular antiprism
\[
K_h=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm v_1,\pm v_2,\pm v_3\},\qquad
v_j=(\cos(2\pi(j-1)/3),\sin(2\pi(j-1)/3),h),\quad j=1,2,3.
\]
Let \(P(h)\) be the probability that orthogonal projection of \(K_h\) onto a Haar-uniform unoriented plane has six vertices. Then the following **exact formula and sharp extremal classification** hold:
\[
\boxed{P(h)=1+\frac3\pi\arctan(4h)-\frac6\pi\arctan\!\frac{2\sqrt3h}{\sqrt{1+4h^2}}.}
\]
The probability decreases strictly on \((0,1/\sqrt2)\) and increases strictly on \((1/\sqrt2,\infty)\). Consequently the regular octahedron is the **unique global minimizer** of six-vertex shadow probability in the entire right triangular-antiprism family:
\[
\boxed{\min_{h>0}P(h)=P(1/\sqrt2)=\frac4\pi\arctan\frac{\sqrt2}{5}=0.35095931218364\ldots.}
\]
The minimum is quadratically stable, with the exact local expansion
\[
P(h)=P(1/\sqrt2)+\frac{4\sqrt2}{9\pi}(h-1/\sqrt2)^2+O\bigl((h-1/\sqrt2)^3\bigr).
\]
At the degenerate endpoints, \(\lim_{h\downarrow0}P(h)=1\) and \(\lim_{h\to\infty}P(h)=1/2\); neither limit supplies an additional minimizer. In a complementary, previously studied *orthogonal-axis-stretch* family of octahedra, the same regular octahedron instead uniquely **maximizes** this probability. The families meet at their regular member but are geometrically different away from it.

## Assumptions and scope
The Euclidean inner product, the radius-one base circles, and positive half-separation \(h\) are fixed as stated. The two equilateral triangular bases lie at \(z=h\) and \(z=-h\), with the lower base rotated by \(\pi/3\) modulo \(2\pi/3\). Their convex hull has six distinct vertices and eight faces for every \(h>0\). The random plane is distributed according to normalized Haar measure on \(G(3,2)\). Its normal is a uniform vector on \(S^2\), identified up to sign. Null sets of projection degeneracies do not affect the probability. A generic planar shadow of this centrally symmetric six-vertex polytope has either four or six vertices.

The three generating vectors are mutually orthogonal if and only if \(h^2=1/2\), because \(v_i\cdot v_j=h^2-1/2\) for distinct indices. Exactly at this height, equal generating-vector lengths make \(K_h\) a regular Euclidean octahedron. For other positive heights the family is nonorthogonal and therefore is not covered by a theorem restricted to mutually perpendicular octahedral axes.

## Proof
**1. A projection criterion.** Form the invertible matrix \(V=[v_1\ v_2\ v_3]\). Its determinant satisfies \(|\det V|=3\sqrt3 h/2\). Fix a generic unit normal \(u\in S^2\) and let \(q_i\) be the orthogonal projection of \(v_i\) onto \(u^\perp\). Put \(c=V^{-1}u\), so \(\sum_i c_i q_i=0\). For any permutation \((i,j,k)\), the projected pair \(\pm q_i\) fails to be a vertex pair precisely when \(q_i\in\operatorname{conv}\{\pm q_j,\pm q_k\}\). Because \(q_j,q_k\) are linearly independent almost surely, this is equivalent to \((|c_j|+|c_k|)/|c_i|\le1\). Thus all six projected vertices are exposed if and only if the positive triple \((|c_1|,|c_2|,|c_3|)\) obeys the three strict triangle inequalities. Equality sets have spherical measure zero.

**2. Exact spherical cones.** Within any sign orthant for \(c\), the strict triangle-inequality cone is generated by the coordinate vectors \((1,1,0)\), \((1,0,1)\), and \((0,1,1)\), with the orthant's prescribed signs. Applying \(V\), eight simplicial cones in the physical normal \(u\)-space result. Rotational symmetry and simultaneous sign reversal produce two congruent *same-sign* cones and six congruent *mixed-sign* cones.

For any three independent generators \(a,b,c\) of a pointed cone, its solid angle is
\[
\Omega(a,b,c)=2\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(|\det(a,b,c)|,\|a\|\|b\|\|c\|+(a\cdot b)\|c\|+(b\cdot c)\|a\|+(c\cdot a)\|b\|\right).
\]
The two-argument arctangent has value in \((0,\pi)\) here, so this formula handles a negative denominator and hence a spherical angle above \(\pi\) without a branch error.

Write \(A=\sqrt{1+4h^2}\). One same-sign cone has generators \(v_1+v_2,v_1+v_3,v_2+v_3\). Their norms are all \(A\), all mutual dot products equal \(4h^2-1/2\), and the absolute determinant is \(3\sqrt3h\). Therefore, with \(\alpha=\Omega_{\rm same}/2\),
\[
\alpha=\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(3\sqrt3h,\,A(16h^2-1/2)\right).
\]
One mixed-sign cone has generators \(v_1+v_2,v_1-v_3,v_2-v_3\). Their lengths are \(A,\sqrt3,\sqrt3\), all mutual dot products are \(3/2\), and the absolute determinant is again \(3\sqrt3h\). Hence, with \(\beta=\Omega_{\rm mixed}/2\),
\[
\beta=\arctan\!\left(\frac{3\sqrt3h}{\frac92 A+3\sqrt3}\right).
\]
Because the eight cones overlap only on boundaries, Haar normalization gives
\[
P(h)=\frac{2\Omega_{\rm same}+6\Omega_{\rm mixed}}{4\pi}
=\frac{\alpha+3\beta}{\pi}.
\]

**3. Reduction to one elementary formula.** The derivatives of the preceding explicit angular expressions are
\[
\alpha'=-\frac{6\sqrt3}{A(1+16h^2)},\qquad
\beta'=\frac4{1+16h^2}-\frac{2\sqrt3}{A(1+16h^2)}.
\]
Indeed the first is obtained from \(\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)'=(xy'-yx')/(x^2+y^2)\), and the second from the usual derivative of an arctangent; substitution and cancellation give the displayed rational-radical expressions. Put \(\varphi(h)=\arctan(2\sqrt3h/A)\). Direct differentiation gives \(\varphi'=2\sqrt3/[A(1+16h^2)]\). Since \(\alpha(h)\to\pi\) and \(\beta(h)\to0\) as \(h\downarrow0\), integration gives, for every \(h>0\),
\[
\alpha=\pi-3\varphi,\qquad \beta=\arctan(4h)-\varphi.
\]
Substitute into \(P=(\alpha+3\beta)/\pi\) to obtain the asserted formula with correct arctangent branches.

**4. Unique extremum and sharp stability.** Differentiation now yields
\[
\boxed{P'(h)=\frac{12}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{1+4h^2}-\sqrt3}{\sqrt{1+4h^2}(1+16h^2)}.}
\]
The derivative is strictly negative for \(0<h<1/\sqrt2\), zero exactly at \(h=1/\sqrt2\), and strictly positive thereafter. At the critical height the pairwise generator dot products vanish, giving precisely the regular octahedron. Evaluating the formula there, using \(\arctan\)-addition identities, gives the stated known regular-octahedral probability. Differentiating once more gives \(P''(1/\sqrt2)=8\sqrt2/(9\pi)\), proving the local coefficient \(4\sqrt2/(9\pi)\). The two endpoint limits follow directly from the formula, completing the global and strict equality claims.

## Verification
The standalone `check_antiprism.py` requires only Python 3's standard library. It uses independent direct planar convex-hull reconstruction from six explicitly projected three-dimensional vertices, not the triangle-inequality criterion, for 18,000 reproducible random orientations at each of five positive heights. For each height, the empirical six-vertex proportion agrees with the exact formula within a predeclared conservative binomial tolerance. It also reconstructs the same-sign and mixed-sign spherical trihedral angles directly from their three rays, tests the elementary formula against the summed solid angles, compares finite-difference and analytic derivatives, and checks the critical height and limiting values. The completed package check printed `TRIANGULAR_ANTIPRISM_SIX_SHADOW_CHECKS_PASSED`. These numerical tests are corroboration; the theorem for all positive heights follows from the analytical cone partition and derivative sign.

## Relationship to prior work
Feldman and Klain [1] establish a general solid-angle formula for expected random-shadow face counts. Their identity supplies a broad implicit approach to such probabilities but does not establish the exact right triangular-antiprism height profile or optimize it. Finch [2, Section 2.3] gives the regular octahedron's numerical and exact four-versus-six shadow probabilities; the critical probability value is therefore explicitly **not** new. Black and Criado [3] give general bounds on mean shadow complexity of fixed polytopes; their full open-access article establishes neither this one-parameter exact probability nor its sharp minimum.

A published geometric result [4] proves that among the **orthogonal-axis stretched** octahedra \(\operatorname{conv}\{\pm a e_1,\pm b e_2,\pm c e_3\}\), the regular member uniquely **maximizes** the six-vertex shadow probability. This is complementary rather than covering: the vectors \(v_i\) of the present nonregular antiprisms have pairwise scalar product \(h^2-1/2\ne0\). The strict **minimum** along a nonorthogonal rotationally symmetric deformation and its quadratic coefficient are new claims relative to that theorem. Published work [5] about the X-ray number of uniform antiprisms concerns a different invariant and only their uniform geometric height, not the random silhouette probability across positive heights. A related five-vertex projection theorem [7] optimizes a right rectangular pyramid rather than a six-vertex antiprism; its square-base case recovers the same previously known regular-octahedral constant, but not this height-family minimization. General solid-angle and vertex-count identities are tools, not asserted as new mathematics.

## Limitations
The sharp extremal classification applies only to the specified right triangular-antiprism height family and Haar-uniform orthogonal projection. It does not say the regular octahedron minimizes among all affine octahedra, arbitrary centrally symmetric six-vertex polytopes, or non-Haar viewing distributions. Limits at zero and infinite height describe degenerating families but do not extend the theorem to those bodies. The constant for the regular octahedron and generic solid-angle formulas were known; the novelty assessment concerns the exact nonorthogonal family profile, unique global minimum, and local stability. A differently stated prior optimization in historical stochastic geometry or polyhedral angle-sum literature remains possible. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
