# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. The exact cyclic erasure-inclusion identity, exceptional-lag correction, trigonometric expansion and quadratic Gauss-sum evaluation yield the finite-prime formula. A direct elementary Fourier-series proof of an \(O(\sqrt p\log p)\) interval character-sum bound supplies the short-interval nonresidues needed to match the exact lower bound with \(O((\log p)^2/\sqrt p)\) error. The standard-library checker compares independently computed cyclic correlations and Fourier fourth moments, and tests spacing invariance; it supplements rather than proves the theorem. The statements include the small-prime and arbitrary-spacing boundaries.

## Originality

PASS provisionally. A full-text comparison with arXiv:2311.01035v1 finds perfect Zadoff–Chu autocorrelation and quadratic Gauss-sum formulas but no two-entry erasure-energy identity or optimized prime-class asymptotics. The full text of arXiv:2211.05702v1 covers perfect, cyclic and truncated correlations, but not this prime-cyclic two-entry mask. The earlier one-erasure classification is a different objective with different arithmetic values; it does not imply this two-erasure sharp asymptotic. The 2014 Lee–Yang partial-period correlation paper is a concrete bibliographic-priority risk: its abstract discusses first and second moments, but full-text comparison was not possible. The present object masks **both** endpoints of every lag correlation and optimizes across all roots and all nonzero spacings; the interval-restricted partial-period correlator is not the same operation.

## Value

PASS. The exact Parseval-equivalent fourth Fourier moment is a natural measure of spectral flatness and cyclic correlation leakage following loss of two samples. The all-spacing spectrum invariance is an arithmetical rigidity, while the \(p\bmod4\) transition in the sharp \(\sqrt p\)-scale improvement over the base \(4p\) is not a routine finite table or a mere higher-erasure restatement. The absolute \(o(1)\) remainder isolates the best attainable constant term as well as the leading arithmetic defect. No claim is made about aperiodic correlation, peak sidelobes or general multierasure masks.

Gregoratti–Arteaga–Broquetas (2023; arXiv:2311.01035), Andrews (2022; arXiv:2211.05702) and Lee–Yang (2014; doi:10.1109/TIT.2014.2334319) are the closest inspected sources. Earlier specialist priority remains possible, particularly from Lee–Yang's inaccessible full text. The mathematical result is a cyclic prime-length family statement, not a general coding-design optimum. The all-prime energy identity and its asymptotic are analytic; numerical checks do not constitute exhaustive infinite-prime verification.
