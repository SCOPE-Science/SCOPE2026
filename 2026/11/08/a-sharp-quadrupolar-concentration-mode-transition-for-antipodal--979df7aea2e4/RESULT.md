# A sharp quadrupolar concentration-mode transition for antipodal caps in every sphere dimension

## Finding

Let \(d\ge3\), let \(\sigma\) be normalized surface measure on \(S^{d-1}\), and let \(\mathcal H_2\) denote the complex space of homogeneous harmonic quadratic polynomials restricted to this sphere. For \(0<a<1\), set
\[
E_a=\{x\in S^{d-1}:|x_1|\ge a\},\qquad
\mathcal T_a=P_{\mathcal H_2}\mathbf1_{E_a}\big|_{\mathcal H_2}.
\]
The complete spectrum of \(\mathcal T_a\) consists of three positive numbers \(\lambda_Z,\lambda_M,\lambda_Q\), having multiplicities respectively \(1\), \(d-1\), and \((d-2)(d+1)/2\). The eigenspaces are the zonal line generated by \(x_1^2-1/d\), the mixed sector generated by \(x_1x_j\) for \(2\le j\le d\), and the traceless transverse quadratic sector.

Put
\[
c_d=\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((d-1)/2)},\qquad
J_k(a)=2c_d\int_a^1t^k(1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}\,dt \quad(k=0,2,4).
\]
Then the eigenvalues are exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_Z&=\frac{d^2(d+2)}{2(d-1)}\left(J_4-\frac2dJ_2+\frac1{d^2}J_0\right),\\
\lambda_M&=\frac{d(d+2)}{d-1}(J_2-J_4),\\
\lambda_Q&=\frac{d(d+2)}{(d-1)(d+1)}(J_0-2J_2+J_4).
\end{aligned}
\]
All three belong to \((0,1)\). Their sharp global ordering changes **exactly once**:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
0<a<1/\sqrt d:&\lambda_M>\lambda_Z>\lambda_Q,\\
a=1/\sqrt d:&\lambda_M=\lambda_Z>\lambda_Q,\\
1/\sqrt d<a<1:&\lambda_Z>\lambda_M>\lambda_Q.
\end{array}
\]
Thus the maximally concentrated quadratic eigenspace jumps from dimension \(d-1\) (mixed modes) to dimension \(1\) (zonal mode), and at the unique transition it has dimension \(d\). The threshold is the root-mean-square value of a coordinate on the uniform sphere. The sharp restriction condition number is \(\sqrt{\max\{\lambda_M,\lambda_Z\}/\lambda_Q}\).

## Assumptions and scope

Only the **pure degree-two** harmonic space is considered, not the full low-pass band through degree two. Each cap has pole at \(\pm e_1\) and colatitudinal radius \(\arccos a\). The result applies to the round sphere in every integer ambient dimension \(d\ge3\); the cap boundary has surface measure zero. Complex coefficients are allowed, and every Rayleigh quotient and eigenspace statement is in the complex \(L^2\) Hilbert space. Eigenvalues and condition numbers refer to exact, continuous spatial restriction, with no noise model or discretization.

## Proof

**1. Three invariant harmonic sectors.** Write \(x=(t,y)\), where \(t=x_1\), \(y\in\mathbb R^{d-1}\). Every trace-free homogeneous quadratic is uniquely a sum of an axial component \(A(t^2-1/d)\), a mixed component \(t\,v\cdot y\), and a trace-free quadratic in \(y\). The sectors have dimensions \(1\), \(d-1\), and \((d-2)(d+1)/2\); they are mutually orthogonal on the sphere and on \(E_a\) by the orthogonal symmetries of \(y\). Their normalized energy quotients are constant within each sector, by rotational and reflection invariance. Their dimensions sum to \(d(d+1)/2-1=\dim\mathcal H_2\).

**2. Exact eigenvalues.** For uniform \(x\), \(t\) has density \(c_d(1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}\) on \((-1,1)\). Its second and fourth moments are \(1/d\) and \(3/[d(d+2)]\); hence
\[
\|t^2-1/d\|_2^2=\frac{2(d-1)}{d^2(d+2)},\qquad
\|t y_j\|_2^2=\frac1{d(d+2)}.
\]
Conditionally on \(t\), \(y\) is uniformly distributed on the sphere of radius \(\sqrt{1-t^2}\) in \(\mathbb R^{d-1}\). Thus \(\mathbb E[y_j^2\mid t]=(1-t^2)/(d-1)\) and, for distinct transverse indices, \(\mathbb E[y_i^2y_j^2\mid t]=(1-t^2)^2/[(d-1)(d+1)]\). Evaluating the three normalized Rayleigh quotients yields the displayed formulas for \(\lambda_Z,\lambda_M,\lambda_Q\). No further eigenvalues exist.

**3. Exact spectral gap identities.** Put \(w(t)=(1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}\). Differentiation gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\left[t(1-t^2)^{(d-1)/2}\left(\frac1d-t^2\right)\right]
=w(t)\left[(d+2)t^4-4t^2+\frac1d\right],
\]
and
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\left[t(1-t^2)^{(d+1)/2}\right]
=-w(t)(1-t^2)\left[(d+2)t^2-1\right].
\]
The boundary terms at \(t=1\) vanish. Substitution into the eigenvalue differences gives the exact identities
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_Z-\lambda_M&=\frac{d(d+2)c_d}{d-1}\,a(1-a^2)^{(d-1)/2}\left(a^2-\frac1d\right),\\
\lambda_M-\lambda_Q&=\frac{2d(d+2)c_d}{(d-1)(d+1)}\,a(1-a^2)^{(d+1)/2},\\
\lambda_Z-\lambda_Q&=\frac{d(d+2)c_d}{d+1}\,a(1-a^2)^{(d-1)/2}\left(a^2+\frac1d\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every prefactor is positive on the stated range. Therefore \(\lambda_Q\) is always strictly smallest; the only equality between the other branches is \(a=1/\sqrt d\), where they cross with opposite ordering. This also determines precisely the top eigenspaces and their dimensions.

**4. Endpoint exclusions.** For every \(0<a<1\), both the open caps and their open complement have positive measure. No nonzero quadratic polynomial can vanish on either nonempty open spherical set; thus every concentration quotient lies strictly between zero and one. In particular all eigenvalues are strictly positive and strictly less than one, as asserted.

## Verification

The standalone Python checker `check_polar.py` independently integrates the truncated spherical moments using 60-digit `mpmath` quadrature. Across dimensions \(d=3,\ldots,14\) and seven cap thresholds per dimension, it compares all three integrated spectral differences with the closed boundary-term identities, checks positivity and tests both strict ordering regimes. It performs 420 assertions and prints `POLAR_CAP_CHECKS_PASS 420`. The proof of the all-dimensional theorem is analytical and does not rely on the finite checks. Reproduction: install `mpmath` according to `requirements.txt`, then run `python check_polar.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Simons and Dahlen (2006, Section 4.2, equations (64)–(72), and Section 5.2) explicitly formulated the **double-polar-cap** Slepian concentration problem on \(S^2\), decomposed it by spherical-harmonic order and parity, and supplied a general computational approach for complete low-pass harmonic bands. Neither the double-cap geometry nor the general Slepian eigenproblem is claimed as new here. The result above instead isolates the pure quadrupolar band in **every** sphere dimension and determines a sharp, dimension-dependent change of its globally maximizing symmetry sector; the general algorithms do not state this threshold or all-dimensional equality classification. Michel, Plattner, and Seibert (2021) developed a broad spherical-cap Slepian framework. Roddy and McEwen (2021) developed Slepian wavelets on incomplete spheres; these contextual papers do not state the present sharp quadratic crossing.

## Limitations

Historical priority for the all-dimensional exact crossing is provisional. The 2006 general double-cap kernel already covers the corresponding ambient dimension-three operator calculations in principle; the novelty claim does not extend to inventing that kernel or to pretending the dimension-three entries are inaccessible from prior methods. Equivalent explicit low-degree calculations might appear in specialized geophysical literature under different terminology. The theorem does not cover full low-pass degree-two spaces, higher harmonic degrees, nonantipodal caps, nonspherical metrics, noisy data, or optimal finite sampling.
