# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The function-space embedding norm squared is exactly the maximum variance under the Lipschitz unit-ball constraint. A strictly convex quadratic form on functions modulo constants forces all maximizers to extreme anchored functions; connected tight-edge spanning trees force integral coordinates. Complete multipartite diameter two reduces the possibilities to binary and three-level potentials, whose exact variances are (2) and (3). Maximizing the two integer quadratics gives the closed formula and all equality cases, including the otherwise exceptional singleton part and the precise parity tie. The four-residue arithmetic is explicit. The standard-library exact-rational checker tests all 58 unordered multipartite profiles through order eight and 499,497 further integer-threshold comparisons. Those finite checks support, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The 2009 spread paper gives the invariant, the integral-extremizer principle and the clique benchmark, not the multipartite sharp constant. The 2018 symmetric-edge-polytope result is particularly close: Proposition 3.5 already classifies the vertices of the Lipschitz polytope at the complete-multipartite endpoint, so neither their existence nor their abstract shape is novel here. It does not, in the inspected full text, optimize the uniform centered quadratic form, characterize equality for the embedding norm, or identify the exact quarter-part stability plateau. The separate 2006 subgaussian-constant work studies a different constant. Published-index comparisons found no matching formula, but unindexed equivalent computations remain a genuine bibliographic risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The object is the canonical Lipschitz Banach-space-to-centered-Hilbert-space inclusion, whose norm is a central concentration parameter. The theorem resolves the **complete arbitrary multipartite family**, including which of two geometrically distinct potential types actually optimize the norm. The cutoff is structural: deleting the within-part edges of a balanced four-partite graph changes its metric but need not increase its worst-case variance at all. The precise threshold, parity coexistence and extremizer description quantify this nonobvious non-strictness of spread under graph-edge deletion, and supply exact benchmark examples for concentration and metric Banach embeddings.

## Closest comparisons and limits

Higashitani–Jochemko–Michałek, Proposition 3.5 (arXiv:1807.07678v1) is a strong structural antecedent; its classification of all extreme potential shapes materially reduces the originality scope. Addario-Berry–Janson–McDiarmid (arXiv:0902.1156v1) establishes the variance invariant and general extremizer context. These sources do not state the centered norm formula. Uniform vertex masses, complete multipartite edge structure and unit edge lengths are essential. There is no claim of independence or unindexed-literature exhaustiveness.
