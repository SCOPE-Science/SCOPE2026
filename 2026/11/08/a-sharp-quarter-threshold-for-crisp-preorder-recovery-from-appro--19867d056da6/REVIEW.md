# Scientific review: sharp quarter-threshold for modal S4 preorder recovery

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem has exact hypotheses (nonempty finite worlds, real-valued standard Łukasiewicz residuum and tensor, arbitrary fuzzy accessibility, all valuations, the three axiom schemas). The normality defect follows from the pointwise Lipschitz inequality for positive-part truncation and is attained by a single designated world. The reflexivity defect is likewise attained by a one-point zero valuation. The fourth-axiom defect follows from the residuated composition identity and a valuation witnessing each positive discrepancy. Comparison to an arbitrary Boolean preorder yields a global distance lower bound. If the maximum defect is below one quarter, endpoint rounding is unique and remains transitive because two high edges and the defect bound force the third edge into the high band. A three-world example saturates the quarter cutoff and has nearest-preorder distance three quarters. The included checker corroborates the identities and critical boundary cases using exhaustive and seeded finite computations; the analytic argument, not the checker, establishes the unbounded finite-frame quantifier.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result gives an exact quantitative bridge between approximate satisfaction of three natural modal axioms and a unique closest classical S4 accessibility frame. It identifies a sharp and operationally useful stability radius, not a parameter-tuned enumeration or a restatement of qualitative crisp normality. The explicit three-world obstruction rules out extending the same certificate to the endpoint. This supplies a reproducible, general-frame criterion for rounding graded relational models while controlling modal semantics.
