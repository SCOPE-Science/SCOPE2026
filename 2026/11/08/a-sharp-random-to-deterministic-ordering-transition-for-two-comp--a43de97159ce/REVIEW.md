# Scientific assessment

**Correctness: PASS.** The exact proximal maps give an affine epoch recurrence with a common multiplier strictly inside the unit circle. Independent Bernoulli epoch innovations yield closed first and second moments; the exact stationary risk, strictly positive quadratic curvature in the ordering probability, constrained optimizer, unique transition, and leading asymptotic coefficients are proved algebraically in `RESULT.md`. The standalone standard-library verifier checks 15,624 exact-rational assertions; it is not offered as a substitute for the universal proof.

**Originality: PASS, subject to specialist precedence risk.** The central comparator is Das–Schölkopf–Muehlebach (arXiv:2206.02953), which analyzes componentwise implicit proximal random reshuffling with uniform permutations, shuffle-once and adversarial orders. Its stated general convergence bounds do not directly determine the exact stationary risk over biased permutation distributions or its minimax phase transition. Traoré–Apidopoulos–Salzo–Villa (arXiv:2308.09310) use stochastic proximal point and variance reduction, not the exact order-law optimization. Rajput–Lee–Papailiopoulos (arXiv:2102.09718) show order sensitivity for explicit SGD and give different rate statements; Mishchenko–Khaled–Richtárik (arXiv:2102.06704) apply the proximal map to a regularizer once per epoch after component gradient steps, rather than to each quadratic component. Published related optimization findings on scalar two-component SGD risk and capped Polyak steps concern different updates and do not imply this precise proximal classification. Related stochastic affine recursion formulas are classical, so the novelty resides in the complete constrained proximal-order design result, not moment-recursion algebra alone. Exhaustive historical coverage cannot be guaranteed.

**Value: PASS.** Each epoch uses the same two component proximal calls and exactly the same components for every order law, so the ordering distribution is a meaningful zero-extra-oracle-cost design choice. The exact classification identifies (i) an order law that restores the correct stationary mean at all finite stepsizes, (ii) a sharp change to deterministic ordering when minimizing total stationary error, and (iii) an actual small-stepsize order gain over uniform permutations. The result is not claimed to optimize all possible solvers, nor to extend without qualification to matrix components.

**Residual scientific limitations.** The analysis is scalar, strongly convex quadratic, fixed stepsize, independent epochs and asymptotic epoch endpoint only. A related paper on general affine random iterations could contain an equivalent specialized classification; such precedence has not been exhaustively ruled out. No external independent audit has been performed.
