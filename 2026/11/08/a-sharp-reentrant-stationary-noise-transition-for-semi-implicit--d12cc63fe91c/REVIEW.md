# Scientific review: sharp stationary-noise turning-point transition

**Correctness — PASS.** The stationary AR(2) representation is obtained directly from the fixed-damping NAG-GS recurrence, with the exact Brownian-increment normalization. Its Yule–Walker equations produce a rational stationary variance only on the Schur-stable domain. An explicit derivative factorization proves the curvature-ratio threshold, both turning points, and the endpoint cases. Three exact rational derivative evaluations prove the full-system sign reversal for a specified eleven-dimensional SPD matrix. Independent direct state-space Lyapunov computations and seeded simulations are reproducibility diagnostics, not substitutes for the analytic argument. The main limits are the noise model and constant quadratic coefficients.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The source NAG-GS paper already gives the recurrence, stability boundary and existence of the stationary Lyapunov covariance; the broader two-step momentum paper gives a general noise-amplification formula. Those are not claimed as original. The newly assessed statement is the **exact curvature threshold separating zero versus two stationary-noise extrema along the prescribed semi-implicit step path**, plus the stationary total-error reversal in a fixed fully stable SPD problem. Full text of the primary source's Sections 2.3, 3.2, 3.5 and Appendix D, and of the broad momentum source's Section IV-C and Appendix E was inspected; neither states the bifurcation or the witness. Relevant archival-result records concern other algorithms. Residual risk: a broader published parameter-optimization theorem or another equivalent semi-implicit formulation might already imply the special phase diagram; no search is a formal novelty guarantee.

**Value — PASS.** Stability and fastest deterministic contraction are insufficient to predict stationary noise under semi-implicit NAG-GS. The sharp \(1.9065\ldots\) ratio identifies a structural boundary, not an arbitrary test parameter. The eleven-dimensional example demonstrates that nonmonotonic error persists at the aggregate optimization-variable level; this gives a concrete diagnostic for tuning noisy semi-implicit optimizers. It does not promise improved practical training performance, and treats neither dynamic damping nor nonquadratic objectives.
