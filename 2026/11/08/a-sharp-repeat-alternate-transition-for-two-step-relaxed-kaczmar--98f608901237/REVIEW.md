# Scientific review of the two-step relaxed Kaczmarz coupling law

**Correctness — PASS.** The derivation is self-contained: every uniform-marginal binary coupling is parametrized by its switching probability; the expected squared-error matrix is a convex combination of exact products of the two relaxed projection maps. Row-exchange symmetry diagonalizes this matrix, and the exact two affine eigenvalue functions yield the entire argmin, including the nonunique plateau and strict interior region. The algebraic condition for the interior region is solved over the full parameter range, not inferred from a grid. The example is exact rational arithmetic. `verify.py`, run with SymPy 1.14.0, reported 39,517 symbolic identities and exact-rational comparisons. Boundary values and other solvers are excluded; the proof does not imply a multi-sweep guarantee.

**Originality — PASS with explicit precedence risk.** Full or substantial primary-text comparisons concern (a) Yaniv et al., non-repetitive selectable sets for exact row projections, (b) Marshall–Mickelin, scheduling a learning rate and without-replacement sampling for randomized Kaczmarz, (c) Necoara, independent randomized block choice and extrapolated updates, (d) Agaskar–Wang–Lu, exact MSE and optimal independent row probabilities, and (e) Ferreira–Acebrón–Monteiro, empirical and conceptual row-sampling comparisons. None of these inspected statements asserts a global minimization over the correlated joint law at fixed uniform marginals and common overrelaxation. Published two-row cyclic overrelaxation results optimize a different criterion (deterministic transient/asymptotic) and the recorded two-row sweep-order result fixes distinct rows. Derivability through elementary matrix products is not mistaken for prior statement coverage. Specialist earlier studies of Markovian or correlated row choices may nevertheless contain an equivalent result; this is a residual risk and independent precedence review remains appropriate.

**Value — PASS.** The exact two-update minimax design answers a natural algorithmic choice: whether to insist on distinct rows or intentionally repeat under otherwise identical marginal sampling and query cost. It provides a complete answer for all nonparallel two-row geometries and all ordinary positive relaxations, including a nontrivial strict interior phase and an exact rational witness reversing a common heuristic that replacement-free sampling is inherently preferable. It is not claimed to outperform a different relaxation schedule, choose globally best algorithm, or predict longer-run performance. The restricted scope is explicit and scientifically motivated rather than an unmotivated numeric slice.
