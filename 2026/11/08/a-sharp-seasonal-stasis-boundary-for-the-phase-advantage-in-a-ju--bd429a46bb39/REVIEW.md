# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The stated two-season products are multiplied exactly. Their trace difference is \( (F-f)(S-s)\) and their determinant difference is \((S-s)(R_2F-R_1f)\). Evaluating the resulting characteristic polynomial difference at the aligned Perron root is legitimate because the opposite matrix's smaller eigenvalue lies below the aligned Perron root. When \(R_2\le R_1\), a strict Perron/diagonal bound proves \(r_A>K\). When \(R_2>R_1\), \(K\) strictly decreases from infinity to \(R_2\), while \(r_A\) strictly increases from finite value to infinity; therefore the crossing is unique. The complete rational witness has \(a_i+S<1\), exact characteristic values at one \(-59/800\) and \(23/1600\), and all matrix diagonal entries below one. The included verifier checks 4,000 fixed-seed rational profiles and independently replays the exact witness. These finite checks are supporting evidence; they do not substitute for the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to external priority risk
Cushing (2006), first published online 2006-04-24, analyzes periodic juvenile–adult matrix systems and the anti-phase advantage in a zero-juvenile-retention model. The present all-positive-stasis theorem introduces the pair of independently season-varying retention products and derives an exact, unique reversed ordering; the zero-juvenile-retention case is covered as the no-reversal boundary but not as this theorem. Ackleh–Chiquet (2009) treats juvenile retention with continuous versus seasonal reproduction, but not the fixed-margin two-placement comparison. Cushing–Ackleh (2011/12) develops periodic reproduction-number methods, not the sharp two-arrangement phase classification. Targeted literature searches and comparisons with available mathematical results found no statement implying this classification. Similar results in broader nonnegative matrix rearrangement research remain possible and not exhaustively excluded.

## Value — PASS
The known simple model's opposite-phase preference is not structurally robust once juvenile stasis and seasonwise adult stasis coexist. This exact dichotomy supplies the smallest, fully motivated extension identifying when phase preference is stable and when one inevitable switch occurs. The biologically feasible rational example shows that simply changing which season has high maturation can move the annual Floquet multiplier across one, a consequential population-growth threshold rather than a tiny parameter-dependent discrepancy. It is mathematical low-density population projection, not empirical persistence prediction.
