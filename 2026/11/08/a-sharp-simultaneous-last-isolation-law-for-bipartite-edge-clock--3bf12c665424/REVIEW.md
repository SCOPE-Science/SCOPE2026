# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. An intersection-of-exponential-survivals calculation gives exact mixed falling-factorial moments, and inclusion–exclusion gives the finite joint completion law. Conditioning on one distinguished edge being the unique synchronized completion edge yields its exact density. An integrable tail bound supports dominated convergence to the rare coincidence coefficient, and the normalized subprobability density is transformed to a \(\operatorname{Gamma}(2,1)\) law. The finite checker independently exhausts the smallest edge orderings and two-time finite-state histories (51 exact checks); it is corroboration rather than an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS, qualified. Blasiak and Durrett's repeated-draw bipartite coverage work [1] has different sampling and first-order coverage conclusions. Penrose's isolated-count Poisson theorems [2] do not determine an order-\(1/n\) synchronized completion event. Berikkyzy and colleagues [4] study the same edge order and give the total count distribution of edges that first expose both endpoints, but not the event that the terminal coverage edge is such an edge or its rare-event timing. No exact same-claim coverage was found through searches for simultaneous final row/column coverage, two-class coupon collectors, and marked edge clocks. Saltykov [3] is a plausible historical source with only an accessible abstract; implicit coverage by older coupon-collection generating functions remains possible. The literature comparison is not a uniqueness certificate.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The event captures one arriving edge simultaneously resolving the last isolated vertex in each part, a natural rare obstruction in bipartite graph growth. The exact finite law, calibrated critical-imbalance coefficient, and conditional Gamma law distinguish a vanishing-probability event from its coarse independent-Gumbel regime. These predictions cannot be obtained from first-order Poisson convergence alone.
