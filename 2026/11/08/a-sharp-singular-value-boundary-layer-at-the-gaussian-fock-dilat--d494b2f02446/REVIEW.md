# Review of the Gaussian Fock dilation boundary-layer theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The result has a complete analytic proof with controlled quantifiers: real \(0<a<1\), real \(0\le t<1-a^2\), fixed parameter \(a\) for the critical limit, and singular-value index starting at zero. Hilbert–Schmidt summation gives the exact trace of \(T^*T\); the Gaussian integral produces an explicit positive kernel. A fixed-point construction generates an infinite orthogonal family of modulus eigenvectors, and the equality of their eigenvalue sum with the independently evaluated trace excludes a hidden positive residual spectrum. Injectivity excludes a kernel. The boundary norm argument uses a weakly null family of normalized Gaussian eigenvectors, not just limits of compact norms. The unbounded regime is witnessed by normalized coherent kernels. The locally uniform layer and counting constant follow from a first-order expansion of the explicit singular-value ratio. The supplied checker passed 96 finite tests on the actual standalone file; these tests supplement, but never replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit implication boundary
Tian's October 2026 work on products of weighted Fock composition operators is the motivating recent context, not the source of these singular-value formulas. Sang and Zhao's August 2026 paper covers the same quadratic-weight boundedness/compactness threshold and derives eigenvectors for \(T\) and \(T^*\); these statements do **not** determine the eigenvalues of \(T^*T\) or the critical counting layer. The general centered-Gaussian oscillator-semigroup theory of Dereziński and Karczmarczyk does contain a positive-modulus normal form, trace- and operator-norm formulas; consequently geometric Gaussian singular spectra as an abstract phenomenon are prior art. The separately proved sharp essential norm of the noncompact endpoint and the fixed-slope locally uniform \(\sqrt{1-a^2-t}\)-scaled singular-value limit and counting law are outside the strictly positive trace-class semigroup setting directly treated there. Those endpoint and scaling assertions, not Gaussian diagonalization as such, constitute the focused quantitative contribution. A deeper specialization of the general oscillator-semigroup framework might yield related formulas and is a genuine residual priority risk.

The accessible historical comparison contains a critical Gaussian-Fock weighted-composition example modified by an oscillatory multiplier, and a separate reverse-product modulus having continuous spectrum; neither implies the theorem for a single quadratic-weight dilation with a near-critical geometric spectrum. The publicly indexed related Fock and weighted-composition records considered likewise address other operators or qualitative boundaries.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The family is a canonical quadratic-exponential perturbation of a Fock dilation and has an independently published compactness boundary. The exact whole-spectrum formula simultaneously determines every Schatten quasi-norm, while the locally uniform profile quantifies *how many* singular values accumulate at each positive threshold as compactness fails. The spectral width diverges at a sharp square-root rate and coexists with a finite nonzero critical essential norm. That complete quantitative bridge is a natural invariant of a mathematically motivated threshold, not an arbitrary finite computation or a reformulation of compactness.

## Closest literature and scope risks
Dereziński and Karczmarczyk (arXiv:1701.07297, Sections 11–14) develop general Gaussian polar/oscillator methods, so geometric subcritical form is not presented as an independently new principle. Sang and Zhao (arXiv:2608.13832, Proposition 2.6 and Theorem A) already give the compactness boundary and ordinary operator eigenvectors. Tian (arXiv:2610.29786v1) treats product compactness and invertibility. Le (DOI:10.1112/blms/bdu046) is prior boundedness and compactness background. The present proof treats one complex dimension and nonnegative real quadratic coefficient; it does not prove results for arbitrary affine parameters or claim classification of all Gaussian kernels. A broader uninspected literature could imply the specific boundary asymptotics; independent publication-priority review remains desirable.
