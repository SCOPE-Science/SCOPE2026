# Scientific review: stretched-exponential Aluthge singular-value boundary layer

## Correctness — PASS

The operator is a compact positive-weight unilateral shift, so its generalized Aluthge transform remains a positive-weight shift and its singular values are the absolute values of its shift weights. The single-step rule and binomial induction yield the exact law involving \(X_n\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,t)\). The normalization, positivity and strict ordering of weights are explicit. The three limiting cases are proved separately: \(\alpha>1\) by a summable fixed-index majorant; \(\alpha=1\) by exact evaluation; and \(0<\alpha<1\) by a uniform binomial concentration estimate, locally uniform singular-value scaling and a uniform tail bound that justifies the passage from pointwise limits to an infinite sum. The large-index region beyond the binomial scale is explicitly controlled rather than discarded. The coefficient \(t^{1-\alpha}/(pc\alpha)\) is the integral of the limiting exponential profile. The proof handles every fixed \(0<p<\infty\), including the quasi-norm range, and does not infer an infinite statement from finite tests. The standalone checker passed 582 finite assertions independently of the asymptotic argument.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The binomial product formula for Aluthge iterates of shifts is already in Stanković, arXiv:2611.07728, equation (9), and is **not** claimed as new. Its Proposition 5.8 proves unnormalized Schatten quasi-norm convergence to zero for arbitrary Schatten-class weighted shifts, but supplies no normalized mass or sharp distribution for the stretched-exponential family. Bala, arXiv:2602.07916, proves convergence of compact iterates in a different generality. Pal and Chandok, arXiv:2610.11336, derive generalized-Aluthge-related Schatten numerical-radius inequalities, not iterated normalized singular-value asymptotics. Dykema and Schultz, arXiv:math/0512197, give binomial ergodic convergence in a tracial von Neumann setting, rather than a compact unilateral-shift boundary-layer classification. The separate coefficient, transition, and locally uniform profile are therefore not statements of these inspected sources. Accessible semantic research records and related published findings on Schatten transitions, weighted shifts, and Aluthge iteration were compared by their actual conclusions; no direct covering statement was identified. A possible unindexed general result on binomial smoothing or regular variation remains a genuine publication-priority risk. Search absence is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The relevant invariant is not an arbitrarily selected coefficient: it is the fraction of Schatten mass represented by the maximal singular value, an intrinsic measure of how close the normalized iterate lies to rank one. The theorem gives a complete trichotomy across the natural family of stretched, ordinary, and compressed exponential weights. It provides an explicit effective spectral dimension, a sharp scaling exponent and constant, and the full boundary-layer profile, explaining why a common qualitative statement—convergence of all unnormalized singular values to zero—conceals three fundamentally different modes of spectral concentration. For \(\alpha>1\) the normalized shifts become asymptotically rank one in every fixed Schatten class; for \(0<\alpha<1\) the effective rank diverges at the proved exact rate. These are operator-theoretic phenomena not supplied by the qualitative limit alone.
