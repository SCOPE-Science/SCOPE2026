# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The joint density in counterclockwise gaps carries the factor two for the two label orderings. The triangle area is the half-determinant of consecutive chords; decomposing the chords yields an exact double integral, in which strict positive curvature gives the uniform local factor \(\kappa(s)ab(a+b)/4\), including highly unequal gaps. The universal sublevel region has beta-area \(B(1/3,1/3)/2\). There are exactly three simplex corners of this type; the complement contributes \(O_\delta(t)\), because away from corners collinearity requires one collision and produces linear area decay. Combining the three contributions yields the claimed \(3\,4^{2/3}B\) coefficient. Hölder gives the unique optimal affine sampling density, and Jensen the sharp fixed-perimeter circle inequality. The tail also gives the exact negative-moment threshold. Circular-gap quadrature and independent ellipse/direct-area simulations corroborate the normalization. Residual correctness risk: no formal proof assistant or independent human audit has checked the compactness estimates, although the needed bounds are given explicitly in the proof.

## Originality
PASS under best available evidence, provisional historical priority. A full-text examination of Craizer–Teixeira–da Silva, *Affine Properties of Convex Equal-Area Polygons* (arXiv:1103.2722), establishes affine arclength and equal-area deterministic polygonal approximation, not the rare-event law of an independent random triple or the variational minimizing property for this statistic. Weisstein's *Circle Triangle Picking* and the 2018 MathOverflow discussion of full circle moments cover the circle model; hence the circle special case is not claimed as an original theorem. The full text of Jiang–Li–Vitányi (arXiv:math/9902043) concerns the least triangle among many interior-uniform points of a square. Related published findings on small circumradius tails for interior point samples and on minimax sampling of random approximating polygons address different random variables and cannot imply the weighted small-area coefficient without an additional theorem. Searches for the exact curvature integral, affine arclength minimization and \(t^{2/3}\) tail found no matching stronger statement; lack of a search hit is not proof of uniqueness. Residual risk: a general determinant sublevel theorem or unindexed geometric-probability literature could cover the same statement.

## Value
PASS. The geometric problem is natural: the area of an inscribed random triangle measures probabilistic degeneracy. The new coefficient identifies a universal noninteger rare-event exponent and exact curvature functional, not just a restatement of a moment of the circle model. The unique affine-arclength optimizer is a canonical, geometrically motivated sampling rule. The sharp circle extremizer at fixed perimeter and the negative-area-moment cutoff give structural consequences, including a precise statistical difference between smooth curved boundaries and ordinary planar point samples. The elliptical example shows a quantitatively nontrivial improvement from choosing the optimal density.

## Limitations and closest sources
The result requires a fixed \(C^3\) embedded strictly convex closed curve with positive curvature and a fixed positive \(C^1\) sampling density. No convergence rate or nonsmooth extension is claimed. Closest primary sources: arXiv:1103.2722 (full text, affine arclength but deterministic polygonal sampling), arXiv:math/9902043 (full text, many interior samples in a square), and the MathWorld circle-triangle page (full text, circle model and positive moments). The 2018 MathOverflow discussion supplies circle moments, which imply the circle-only law in principle but not the general weighted curvature formula.
