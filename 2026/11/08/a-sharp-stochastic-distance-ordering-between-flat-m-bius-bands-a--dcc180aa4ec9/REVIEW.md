# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The two deck-group distance formulas follow from Euclidean shortest segments in the convex infinite strip. The transform of two independent uniform vertical coordinates has exactly constant density on a diamond; after absolute values, the uniform triangular density yields the conditional CDF identity \(\Pr(S\le u\text{ or }V\le v)=1-(1-(u+v)/(2b))_+^2\). Integrating the triangular horizontal displacement density gives both stated finite-radius distributions. A pointwise inequality gives the global stochastic order; a strict positive-measure neighborhood proves the precise open interval of strictness; saturation proves the remaining equalities. The threshold expansion follows by scaling the overlap interval and integrating \(\sqrt{1-|s|}\). Reproducibility code evaluates the same formulas and separately reconstructs geodesic distances directly from sampled rectangle coordinates. No infinite claim rests solely on simulation.

The closest inspected early source is El Mir–Yassine, arXiv:1209.6202v1, which constructs flat Möbius/Klein quotients in the course of conformal systolic inequalities; it has no probability statement comparing point-pair distance distributions. The closest inspected statistical paper is Granata–Carnevale (2016, PMCID: PMC4980871), which uses approximate graph geodesics on a multiply twisted embedded strip and qualitatively compares plots to a cylinder, but neither derives a flat one-twist exact pair-distance law nor proves its global stochastic ordering and sharp threshold. Accessible related research includes a distinct boundary-to-boundary sampling law on a flat Möbius band and an intrinsic pair-distance inverse law on the **closed** flat Klein bottle; neither entails the present area-uniform matched-surface ordering. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem distinguishes two locally flat surfaces with the same area and total boundary length using the *entire* intrinsic two-point distance order. It shows that changing only the end-identification produces a universal direction of stochastic shift, quantifies the first distance scale at which topology becomes detectable, identifies a sharp nonanalytic onset coefficient for every positive aspect ratio, and gives strict inequalities for all positive distance moments. The result is global in the two geometric parameters and every radius, not a single arbitrary sampled statistic.

## Scope and limitations

All formulas are for intrinsic area-uniform distance on the specific flat quotients of the same rectangle. The result does not extend automatically to nonflat or embedded Euclidean distances.
