# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reconstructs the one-descent involutions as adjacent equal-block swaps, counts exactly \(f+1\) for a class with \(f\) fixed points, and derives the unconditional adjacent-descent probability within a fixed-point stratum from mutually exclusive partner configurations. The class-size quotient, strict mean inequality and reverse lower-tail event establish incomparability for the stated infinite range. Exact finite distributions settle the only smaller even cases. Independent exhaustive involution generation through order \(12\) and rational threshold checks through order \(99\) passed \(4893\) assertions. Finite tests are corroboration; the infinite claim rests on the symbolic proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical-priority qualified)

Dukes (2004) records a fixed-point-refined descent generating function due to Désarménien–Foata and proves separate results on all versus fixed-point-free involutions. Guo–Zeng (2005) show unimodality for those separate classes. Chow (2008) treats descent-set enumeration. Kim–Lee (2018) treat conjugacy-class descents asymptotically. Gessel–Zhuang (2020), Section 5.1, provides an even stronger joint enumerator of peaks, descents, and fixed points. None of their inspected statements makes the first-order stochastic comparison between consecutive fixed-point strata or the all-even-size sharp failure at eight. Full enumerative formulas can imply individual input moments and coefficients without making this stochastic-order theorem an explicit published result. Earlier implicit derivability is a substantial residual risk; bibliographic searches do not prove priority. Comparison of claims and implications, not merely titles, supports the qualified conclusion.

## Value — PASS

A natural monotonicity hypothesis would upgrade the strict decrease in average descents with added fixed points to uniform stochastic improvement. The theorem shows this fails first at exactly eight for the most natural pair, and exhibits a general infinite two-parameter region where the extreme lower tail moves opposite the mean. This exact obstruction distinguishes a full distributional order from an expectation comparison; it is motivated by a structural stochastic question rather than an arbitrary coefficient identity.

## Scope and remaining limits

The quadratic region is a sufficient, not globally necessary, condition for all general fixed-point strata. Fully resolving stochastic comparability outside that region remains open here. Older generating-function literature may contain implicit equivalent information. Independent review has not been performed.
