For random involutions with \(f\) versus \(f+2\) fixed points, a simple quadratic threshold \(n-f>(f+2)(f+3)\) forces their descent-count laws to be stochastically incomparable, despite strictly ordered means. In particular, fixed-point-free involutions stochastically dominate two-fixed-point involutions for \(n=2,4,6\), but not for any even \(n\ge8\), the sharp first failure.
