# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact moment calculation follows from \(D=d(P)+1\) and \(L=2\ell-J(P)\). Internal nodes form an ancestor-closed tree, making their depth sum at most \(\binom{i}{2}\); each internal edge has at least one descendant leaf, giving \(Q\ge\ell+i-1\). Both sharp equalities characterize a unique rooted broom. The factorization \((t-9)(i-3)-(i-5)^2\) proves the cutoff, a finite exact optimization establishes the ten-vertex maximizer, and uniform cubic convergence establishes the asymptotic envelope. A standalone exact enumerator corroborated all non-isomorphic rooted shapes through eleven vertices; finite checks are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

The closest directly relevant primary source is Gopaladesikan–Mahmoud–Ward, *Building Random Trees from Blocks*, DOI 10.1017/S0269964813000338. The inspected model and Sections 3–4 establish leaf updates and separate marginal depth/leaf statements, but do not state the covariance extremal classification. Drmota–Hwang examine covariance of level-profile sizes in different random-tree laws, not this uniformly grafted block problem. Published published-finding corpus searches of second-block covariance, rooted brooms and mean depth imbalance did not find a statement implying the whole theorem. The deterministic inequalities may be known in tree-status optimization; those inequalities alone are not claimed as new. An implicit corollary in unexamined broader literature remains a residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The statistic tests whether deeper graft positions systematically increase or decrease the resulting leaf count—the first nontrivial joint-dependence question in rooted-block network growth. The theorem supplies the exact optimal morphology, the smallest block allowing positive covariance, the unique equality boundary, and an asymptotic sharp envelope. This gives a structural classification, not merely a toy example or an arbitrary finite computation. The two-block scope is a limitation, but it is a natural initial growth step in the cited stochastic model.
