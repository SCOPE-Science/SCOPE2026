For every tree on at most twelve vertices, job-side random-vertex greedy matching has at least the expected size of random-edge greedy matching; the unique first reversal is a four-armed three-edge spider on thirteen vertices, with random-edge advantage \(1/6480\). On the analogous \(m\)-armed spiders, the exact two-point laws reverse stochastic dominance at \(m=4\), giving infinitely many connected-tree counterexamples.
