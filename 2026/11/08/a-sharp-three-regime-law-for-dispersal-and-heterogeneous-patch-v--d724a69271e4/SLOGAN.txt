For two equally recovering habitat patches with unequal and correlated environmental noise, increasing symmetric dispersal can reduce local variance, increase it, or produce exactly one finite optimal rate. The complete phase boundary is explicit: with independent shocks, the less noisy patch has an interior variance minimum if and only if the other patch's noise variance exceeds its own by a factor greater than \(5/3\), while a factor greater than \(3\) is needed for very strong dispersal to become worse than isolation.
