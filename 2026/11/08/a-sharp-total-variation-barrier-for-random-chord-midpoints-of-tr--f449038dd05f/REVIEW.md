# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The all-triangle proof directly reconstructs the distribution from the three same-side and three different-side endpoint allocations. The midpoint map from each pair of adjacent edges has Jacobian \(\Delta/2\), and the overlap of three images is precisely the medial triangle. It follows that the four absolutely continuous cell densities are exact and that the singular boundary mass equals the sum of squared normalized side lengths. Strict triangle inequalities establish the signs of the density differences, so the Hahn-positive region and exact total-variation distance are rigorously justified. Cauchy--Schwarz and a realizable degenerating isosceles family prove sharpness. The embedded standalone checker passes exact rational mass tests and independent finite Monte Carlo tests, but simulation is not used as proof of any general assertion.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

The closest accessible primary paper, Bäsel's arXiv:1204.2707 (2012), calculates the chord-length law induced by motion-invariant line measure, plus Euclidean distances of interior-uniform pairs. Its theorems and examples do not cover the midpoint location of boundary-arclength-endpoint chords. Tong--Ahmadi--Pan's arXiv:1312.2498 (2013) also studies area-uniform interior pairs and line-chord length distributions rather than the required midpoint pushforward measure. Semantic research-record searches return an arbitrary-triangle boundary/interior **second distance moment** result, which does not determine or imply the spatial midpoint law and total variation. No equivalent or dominating result was identified by the actual searches. An older arbitrary-triangle chord-length-distribution source (Duma--Rizzo 2009) lacked accessible full text and remains an historical-priority risk, not evidence of coverage.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact total-variation distance is a natural quantitative measure of how strongly an endpoint-defined random chord biases its midpoint relative to uniform area sampling. The result supplies an entire mixed-dimensional spatial law, an explicit maximizing measurable set, a universal nonzero minimum \(5/12\), sharp unattainable supremum \(1/2\), and rigidity of the equilateral minimizer. These are structural and extremal answers for a canonical geometric-probability sampling procedure, not merely a computed value at one arbitrary triangle. The calculation does not claim a general polygon theorem or sampling-efficiency guarantee.
