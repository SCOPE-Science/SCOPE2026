For quadratic sliced-Wasserstein Monte Carlo integration between near-isotropic planar Gaussian laws, the limiting variance ratio of one orthogonal direction pair to two independent directions is exactly \(2r^2/(r^2+16m^2)\), where \(m\) and \(r\) are the scalar and anisotropic parts of the covariance difference. Away from the leading-order equality boundary, orthogonal sampling is locally worse when \(r>4|m|\), and swapping the axes of two unequal Gaussian covariances attains its exact maximum variance penalty of two at every anisotropy strength.
