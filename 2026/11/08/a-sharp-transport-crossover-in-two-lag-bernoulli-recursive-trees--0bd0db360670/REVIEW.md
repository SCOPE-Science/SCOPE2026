# Review of the transport-crossover theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The statement specifies the independent two-lag attachment process, non-lazy simple walk, graph-distance normalization, and the three distinct scaling regimes. Rooted ancestral-pointer decrements are independent at newly visited labels; bounded-increment concentration makes the maximum deviation from linear depth negligible uniformly over the attachment parameter. A longest-zero-run bound controls the maximal label-neighbor distance when \(Np_N\to\infty\). The deterministic comparison of every pairwise graph distance to its label separation, combined with Jang–Kim–Song's exact Kemeny–Wiener identity, proves the macroscopic limits. An exact edge-cut calculation proves the one-bridge three-arm penalty and its critical conditional limit. The derivative formula is an exact first variation around the all-zero configuration. A standalone exact-rational verifier executed 20,859 checks, including direct rational hitting-system solutions and complete small-tree enumeration. No infinite claim relies only on these computations.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge; historical priority remains open)

The full primary text of Tang–Wang–Wu, arXiv:2607.02916v1 (first posted July 3, 2026), was checked for finite-volume Kemeny constants, Wiener indices, rare-one-step attachment, and sparse-parameter limits. It gives one-endedness and infinite-collision theorems for the same tree, but not this crossover. Jang–Kim–Song, arXiv:2209.11271v1, supplies a general identity for Kemeny constants on arbitrary finite trees; that classical identity and the general three-arm summation are not novelty claims. Ciardo–Dahl–Kirkland, arXiv:2003.08286, treats fixed-diameter extremal trees. Lesny–Neininger–Schmieder, arXiv:2606.09228, treats caterpillar construction with fixed-spine/uniform attachment, and Bhutani et al., arXiv:2609.33636, treats a uniformly recruited \(m\)-ary model. None covers the present two-lag Bernoulli sparse-parameter limit. Search comparisons of nearby mathematical research records also found different random-tree statistics, not stronger coverage. An unpublished or differently named random-comb transport theorem may still establish priority; search absence is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The probability of attachment to the last rather than second-last vertex controls a concrete transport observable: the long-range mean hitting time averaged over a stationary target. The endpoint discontinuity and the sharp \(p_N\) scale \(1/N\) are not consequences of the known generic tree identity alone; deriving them needs the ancestral renewal, run geometry, and rare-bridge mechanism. The exact one-bridge penalty shows mathematically why the critical regime fails to concentrate and quantitatively links the finite and asymptotic descriptions. The theorem does not claim a complete critical-law classification or random-walk mixing theorem.
