# A sharp transverse instability tongue for four-dimensional zonotope volume

## Finding
Let \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\) be the standard coordinate vectors of \(\mathbb R^4\), let \(d_{ij}=e_i-e_j\), and put
\[
G=(e_1,e_2,e_3,d_{24},d_{34}),\qquad
w=d_{14},\quad v=d_{23},\quad h_z=e_1-e_2-e_3+ze_4.
\]
For every real \(z\) and \(t\ge0\), define \(A_{t,z}=Z(G,th_z)\), where \(Z\) denotes the Minkowski sum of the segments generated by the listed vectors. Let \(B=[0,w]\) and \(C=[0,v]\). The zonoid-volume log-submodularity defect is
\[
\Delta(t,z)=|A_{t,z}|\,|A_{t,z}+B+C|-|A_{t,z}+B|\,|A_{t,z}+C|.
\]
Positive defect means the log-submodularity inequality **fails**. Its **complete** positive region is exactly
\[
\boxed{\ -1<z<\tfrac13,\qquad 0<t<T(z)\ },
\]
where the sharp boundary is
\[
T(z)=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{2(1+z)}{2z^2-6z+1},&-1<z\le0,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle\frac{2(1-3z)}{1+7z},&0\le z<1/3.
\end{cases}
\]
At \(t=0\) the defect is always zero. At \(t>0\), it is zero **only** on the displayed curve \(t=T(z)\), and is otherwise negative outside the positive region. In particular, \(T\) increases strictly on \((-1,0]\), decreases strictly on \([0,1/3)\), and has global maximum \(T(0)=2\). Thus every \(t>2\) satisfies the volume inequality strictly for **all** transverse tilts \(z\), while at \(t=2\) the sole nonzero-scale equality occurs at \(z=0\).

At the original unit scale, \(t=1\), the failure survives **precisely** for
\[
\boxed{\quad 2-\frac{3\sqrt2}{2}<z<\frac1{13}.\quad}
\]
Consequently the sharp positive and negative fourth-coordinate perturbation tolerances are unequal. Finally, taking Cartesian products with a unit cube gives these exact sign classifications in every dimension \(d\ge4\).

## Assumptions and scope
All volumes are ordinary full-dimensional Lebesgue volumes. The generator \(h_z\) has its first three coordinates fixed at \((1,-1,-1)\), and the parameter \(z\) changes only the fourth coordinate that vanishes in the published counterexample. The independent parameter \(t\ge0\) scales the entire special segment. The base vectors \(G\), and the two test segments \(B,C\), remain fixed. Since \(G\) spans \(\mathbb R^4\), all four relevant zonotopes are full dimensional. Negative \(t\) could be replaced by \(|t|\) up to a translation, but the stated domain is nonnegative \(t\).

## Proof
**Exact generator-volume identity.** For any spanning list of vectors in \(\mathbb R^4\), the volume of the associated zonotope is the sum of the absolute values of its four-by-four maximal minors. Determinants not using the column \(th_z\) are constant in \(t\); those using it are \(t\) times linear functions of \(z\). Consequently the four volumes are exactly \(a_i+tL_i(z)\), with constant terms
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)=(4,12,8,24),
\]
and slopes
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_0(z)&=6+|z|+2|z-1|+|z-2|,\\
L_1(z)&=12+3|z|+3|z-1|+|z-2|+|z+1|,\\
L_2(z)&=10+3|z|+2|z-1|+3|z-2|,\\
L_3(z)&=18+5|z|+5|z-1|+3|z-2|+3|z+1|.
\end{aligned}
\]
The four volumes, respectively, are those of \(A_{t,z}\), \(A_{t,z}+B\), \(A_{t,z}+C\), and \(A_{t,z}+B+C\). To check these identities directly from the displayed vectors, enumerate all triples from each of the four fixed lists \(G\), \((G,w)\), \((G,v)\), and \((G,w,v)\), and take the determinant after appending \(h_z\). These linear determinants have nonzero coefficient roots only at \(-1,0,1,2\); summing their absolute values gives the four displayed expressions. The accompanying ordinary-Python verifier computes the complete triples independently, checks all breakpoint roots, and certifies the affine identity in every one of the five open chambers using two rational evaluations per chamber. This establishes the slope formulas for **all** real \(z\), rather than interpolating a finite sample without a degree justification.

**Exact defect.** Multiply the four expressions to obtain the following piecewise identities, valid for every \(t\ge0\):
\[
\Delta(t,z)=\begin{cases}
4t^2(2z-7),&z\le-1,\\
-4t\bigl[t(2z^2-6z+1)-2(1+z)\bigr],&-1\le z\le0,\\
4t\bigl[2-6z-t(1+7z)\bigr],&0\le z\le1,\\
4t\bigl[t(2z^2-z-9)+4z-8\bigr],&1\le z\le2,\\
-4t^2(2z-1),&z\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
Each pair of neighboring expressions agrees on the shared boundary. For \(t>0\), both outer branches are strictly negative. The \(1\le z\le2\) branch is also strictly negative since \(2z^2-z-9<0\) and \(4z-8\le0\). For \(-1<z<0\), the coefficient \(2z^2-6z+1\) is positive and the sign is positive precisely below the first displayed \(T(z)\). For \(0\le z<1/3\), the sign is positive precisely below the second \(T(z)\); for \(1/3\le z\le1\), it is negative. Together these observations prove the claimed complete phase diagram and its equality cases.

**Sharp peaks and cross-sections.** Direct differentiation gives
\[
T'(z)=\frac{2(-2z^2-4z+7)}{(2z^2-6z+1)^2}>0
\quad(-1<z<0),
\qquad
T'(z)=\frac{-20}{(1+7z)^2}<0\quad(0<z<1/3).
\]
(For the second derivative, differentiating \(2(1-3z)/(1+7z)\) yields \(-20/(1+7z)^2\).) Hence \(T(0)=2\) is the unique global maximum. Solving \(T(z)=1\) on the negative and positive pieces yields exactly \(z=2-3\sqrt2/2\) and \(z=1/13\), respectively. Their strict inequality bounds characterize the unit-scale violation interval.

**Higher dimensions.** For \(d>4\), replace the common four-dimensional zonotope \(A_{t,z}\) by \(A_{t,z}\times[0,1]^{d-4}\) and embed \(B,C\) in the first four coordinates. Each relevant volume is multiplied by the unit cube's volume one. The defect and its sign are identical.

It enumerates all maximal minors for every generator list, establishes the four constant volumes, computes each linear determinant involving the tilted generator, checks that no slope breakpoint is omitted, and confirms both the piecewise volume formulas and defect identity in 512 independent exact rational parameter cases. The symbolic chamber argument above is the unrestricted proof; the sampled cases are corroboration, not an inference of a continuous theorem from finite sampling. ## Relationship to prior work
Skorupinski's original 2026 counterexample is recovered exactly at \((t,z)=(1,0)\), with four volumes \((14,30,26,56)\) and positive defect four. His argument establishes a counterexample, not this perturbation-phase diagram. The previously published *Exact scale phase diagram for a four-dimensional zonotope log-submodularity defect* resolves the subfamily \(z=0\), where \(\Delta(t,0)=4t(2-t)\). The distinct published *Exact two-parameter directional phase diagram for a zonotope log-submodularity counterexample* varies the second and third coordinates of a **normalized** generator \(e_1-ae_2-be_3\), keeping its fourth coordinate zero. Neither prior subfamily, nor their numerical or title similarity, determines the new fourth-coordinate transverse sign boundary. In particular, the present limiting instability directions \(z=-1\) and \(z=1/3\), and the unequal unit-scale radii, are not special cases of their stated formulas.

Fradelizi and collaborators connect volume log-submodularity to Rayleigh inequalities and establish results at bounded degree or codegree; those restrictions do not settle the present eight-column four-dimensional sum. Averkov, von Dichter, and Soprunov prove a different four-dimensional zonoid inequality, not universal log-submodularity. The finite determinant-volume identity itself is classical and not claimed as new. The result here is a sharp, complete, two-parameter stability boundary for the perturbation of a specified recent counterexample, not a new counterexample to the already-disproved general conjecture.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to the displayed two-parameter family and does not classify the full four-dimensional space of possible special-generator perturbations. The unit-scale tolerance refers to the **fourth-coordinate direction** only, not to an isotropic perturbation ball around the generator. Higher-dimensional extensions are Cartesian-product lifts. The comparison with the recent multi-author Rayleigh paper is limited to its accessible summary rather than a line-by-line material inspection of its complete manuscript. As always, public-search noncoverage cannot certify absolute historical priority. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
