# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is class-level **tunability**, not fixed-step necessity. All quantifiers are stated in `RESULT.md`. Firm nonexpansiveness gives the sharp transfer inequality \(\|(1+\theta)(Qv-Qw)-\theta(v-w)\|\le\sqrt{\theta^2+(1-\theta^2)q^2}\|v-w\|\) for \(0\le\theta\le1\). The two-stage difference decomposition supplies the stated nonnegative gain matrix. Its determinant is exactly \(\alpha\beta qs[\mu_A\mu_B-L((1-q)(1+\theta)+c_\theta(q))]\). Since the bracketed coefficient is bounded by \(3-2q\), sufficiently small positive \(\alpha\) and arbitrary positive \(\beta\) give a Schur gain whenever \(\mu_A\mu_B>L\). A positive Perron-type weight yields a single contracting norm for all \(\theta\), which implies convergence under arbitrary switching and a common fixed point. Conversely, the scalar negative-feedback linear family has \(\det(I-T_\theta)=\alpha\beta qs(\mu_A\mu_B-L)\); below the threshold an eigenvalue exceeds one, at equality an eigenvalue equals one, for *every* fixed step and weight. This establishes necessary and sufficient uniform tunability. All three boundaries \(\theta=0,1\) and \(L=0\) are included. The extracted `verify.py` passed 8,737 exact-rational and numerical diagnostic groups; finite checks do not substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The primary comparison is Qu, Wu and Chaffey, *Splitting Compositions of Monotone Operators*, arXiv:2610.39480v1, Sections 3–5. Their Theorem 15, Proposition 16 and Corollary 17 use a triangle-inequality block matrix requiring \(\mu_A\mu_B>L(1+2\theta)\) for fixed positive \(\theta\). Remark 21 documents cancellation in linear examples without a uniform nonlinear replacement. The present result instead proves a sharper firm-resolvent gain, an explicit strict separation at prescribed positive extrapolation, and exact class-level existence of a *common* contraction under arbitrary switching. Those claims do not follow from the source's stated sufficient theorem. Classical firm nonexpansiveness and reflected-resolvent nonexpansiveness, including Liu, Moursi and Vanderwerff, arXiv:2205.09040v1, Fact 2.5, are acknowledged prior mathematics, not claimed new. Evens et al., arXiv:2305.03605, is related splitting theory; only its abstract and source context, not its whole text, were inspected here, so exact coverage is not excluded on that basis alone. Actual semantic record searches and historical claim comparisons found no directly covering assertion; unsuccessful search is not uniqueness proof. Possible older/unindexed operator-splitting variants remain residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The new condition resolves a motivated parameter-tunability question left open by the recent triangular composition-splitting analysis. A gain-matrix estimate that ignores firm nonexpansiveness forbids all positive extrapolation at coupling ratios between 1 and 3; the improved theorem permits arbitrary positive extrapolation weights in that same region through sufficiently small steps. The exact rational example \(\mu_A=3\), \(\mu_B=1/4\), \(L=29/100\), \(\alpha=\beta=1\), \(\theta=1\) demonstrates an actual difference between certified feasible regions. Common-norm robustness for arbitrarily switching extrapolation is a practical structural guarantee. The improvement need not give fast rates and is not a solution of the fixed-step sharpness problem.
