# Scientific review of the odd-cubic harmonic-Bergman spectral phase

## Correctness (PASS)
The claim is about the entire spectrum, not merely potential roots of an algebraic elimination polynomial. Essential-spectrum equality and index zero for continuous harmonic-Bergman Toeplitz symbols provide the Fredholm reduction. The remaining proof checks each possible number of interior roots of the cubic, with multiplicities. The exact Bergman projection identity gives the stated quadratic interpolation kernel. Zero or three interior roots cannot yield nontrivial kernels; one interior root yields the explicit and exhaustive phase interval; two distinct interior roots contradict the position of the third; two coincident interior roots fail the confluent determinant. The equalities at both phase boundaries place an actual zero on the unit circle, so cannot create an isolated off-curve eigenvalue. Directly substituting the one-root factorization gives the eigenfunctions. The packaged checker confirms 31 exact algebraic instances and symbolic identities; it is corroboration, not the proof of the infinite-dimensional statements.

## Originality (PASS, best knowledge, independent priority not certified)
The motivating arXiv:2610.09638v1 supplies the general upper bound \((n-1)2^{n-1}\) and the generic determinant method; the new claim is a complete closed parameter classification of the odd cubic, an exact two-point phase and eigenfunctions, not that general theorem. The previously published 2021 Wang–Zhao work treats certain low-degree cases, Weyl's theorem, and disconnected spectra. Its abstract and subsequent citation were inspected, but its full text was not accessible; an exact earlier cubic-family computation remains a publication-priority risk. Searches for the polynomial family, the radical threshold, roots, alternate spectral terminology and named authors did not reveal the all-parameter formula. Published research index results on harmonic Bergman circle trace constants or on zeros of harmonic cubics are inapplicable because neither studies point spectra of this Toeplitz family. Failed search is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The source bounds the number of isolated points for a degree-three symbol by eight but does not locate them. A sharp, complete classification for the simplest nonlinear *odd* cubic family resolves where the discrete spectrum actually appears, showing a transition from zero to exactly two points and back to zero, and provides explicit eigenvectors for a natural operator class. Both endpoints and arbitrary complex parameter phases are treated, not selected numerical examples. The result is confined to this family; higher-degree and general cubic problems remain open within the scope of this note.
