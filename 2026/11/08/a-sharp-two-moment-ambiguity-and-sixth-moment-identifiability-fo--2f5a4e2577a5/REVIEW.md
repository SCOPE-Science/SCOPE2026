# Scientific review: inverse moments in a finite-tumble harmonic model

**Correctness (PASS).** The stationary Markov-generator moment recursion is derived directly from the stated three-state process. The first three even moments and the fixed-variance inverse change of variables are exact. A quadratic derivative has opposite endpoint signs, giving exactly one fold. The unique-solution argument for equal second, fourth and sixth moments follows by exact symmetric polynomial elimination; the putative distinct pair would require an excluded boundary value. The executable generator calculation and algebra checker passed 119 assertions.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** The 2024 exact-moment paper already provides the marginal stationary distribution and arbitrary-order moments, and the 2026 conditional-distribution paper supplies the newer joint probabilistic perspective. This work does not claim either premise anew. Inspection of the relevant primary analyses did not identify the all-parameter inverse multiplicity classification or three-even-moment injectivity theorem. Searches for hidden-state-rate identifiability, stationary moments, and finite-tumble harmonic particles were compared with published-finding corpus records and related inference literature. The published all-order formula is a strong prior starting point, so independent priority review remains essential.

**Value (PASS).** The theorem solves a natural global inverse problem rather than computing another sample moment: it gives the exact number of rate pairs compatible with each attainable pair of stationary low-order moments and proves that the next even moment is always sufficient to separate them. The collision is present at every fixed variance, so the identifiability issue is structural. It clarifies which stationary observables suffice to infer microscopic run and rest times without watching individual switching events. The theorem makes no assertion about finite-sample estimation quality.
