# Scientific review of the two-parameter skew GRAAL rate barrier

## Correctness: PASS
The result is derived directly from the fixed-step update, with \(\bar x^{-1}=x^0\). Skew-orthogonality \(J^{\mathsf T}=-J\), \(J^2=-I\) guarantees conjugate \(\pm\mathrm i\) modes of equal multiplicity. The characteristic polynomial is exactly the one in the motivating source. The unit-disc root inequality is re-proved from its roots; no unquoted spectral theorem or test grid is needed. When both roots are in the radius-\(1/\sqrt2\) disk, formula (2) forces both nonnegative terms to vanish, giving uniquely \(c=2\) and \(\lambda L=1\). Substitution gives the repeated root and the explicit compatible trajectory in all even dimensions. The norm formula and finite-horizon maximum follow exactly. The standalone symbolic and numerical tests corroborate these identities but are not the proof.

## Originality: PASS (with historical risk)
The 2026 Soe–Tam–Vetrivel paper's Section 2.1 already treats the *same* skew test operator and derives its characteristic polynomial and instability for \(c\ge3\); those facts are not claimed as original. That paper's main theorem supplies an all-monotone sufficient step bound but does not minimize the skew spectral radius across both \(c\) and step size. The earlier FRB skew-rate result already identifies the \(1/\sqrt2\) factor when the reflection weight is fixed at the conventional value (equivalent to \(c=2\)); it does not, by its stated scope, rule out improvements at different mixing parameters. This work supplies the missing global lower bound and uniqueness certificate and an exact all-dimensional transient amplification law. A public analysis of variable-reflection stability optimizes the stability ceiling on split-skew families, rather than the unsplit-rotation convergence factor. An unindexed equivalent older generalized-FRB optimization remains the principal risk and no independent literature certification is claimed.

## Value: PASS
The mixing parameter \(c\) is a genuine algorithmic tuning decision whose extension beyond the golden ratio motivated the 2026 source. An exact optimum over both \(c\) and step length resolves whether that new freedom accelerates the canonical bilinear-rotation obstruction: it does not. The equality is attained beyond the source's uniform guaranteed step bound, distinguishing model-specific speed from worst-case safety. More importantly, even at that global asymptotic optimum the error initially rises to \(150\%\) of its starting norm; the closed transient law gives a concrete failure of interpreting spectral-rate optimization as finite-time monotonic progress. This is a sharp structural algorithm-design boundary, not a numerical table or an arbitrary matrix slice.

## Scope and priority limitations
The structural conclusion is exact only for skew-isometric linear operators, which encompass arbitrary direct sums of the standard skew plane. The full GRAAL family with general monotone operators, arbitrary proximal terms and finite-precision implementation is not addressed. Published and unindexed earlier reflected-gradient analyses may contain equivalent parameterization-independent optimization; this is the main remaining originality risk.
