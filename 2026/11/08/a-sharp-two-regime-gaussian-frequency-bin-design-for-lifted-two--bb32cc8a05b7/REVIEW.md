# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Gaussian-integral identity gives two swapped diagonal responses and one common oscillatory response. Orthogonality over exactly one beat period reduces the full Hermitian lifting to a two-by-two diagonal block and two identical scalar blocks; their four singular values are checked against the stated input/output norms. The min of the resulting hyperbolic difference and Gaussian cross response is globally optimized by their unique crossing and the unique stationary point of the first branch. The sign comparison uses \(\coth(\operatorname{arsinh}(1/\sqrt2))=\sqrt3\). The one-bin phase-fiber transformation preserves both measured intensity Fourier coefficients and the rank-one determinant identity. Assumptions cover every finite \(a>0\), \(x>0\), including the threshold. Numerical tests verify examples and are not substituted for the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
Alaifari--Wellershoff's 2019 first-public work develops discrete cyclic Gabor stability estimates via ambiguity-function bounds and graph components, but its actual theorems do not determine the best Gaussian frequency-line positions or their continuous two-tone singular spectrum. Wellershoff's 2022 two-bin theorem establishes uniqueness on Paley--Wiener spaces using entire-function zero geometry, but does not optimize a lifted smallest singular value. The 2024 uniqueness-versus-stability paper analyzes general signal classes, not this precise maximin sensor-design transition. Grohs--Liehr (2021) derive Gaussian shift-invariant inversion and stability using infinitely many time-slice lines and Riesz bases, and Bartusel (2023) analyzes full-STFT L2 uniqueness through support geometry; neither gives this two-line frequency optimization. Existing results on finite cyclic two-tap spectrogram condition numbers address different parameter and observation spaces, and their formulas do not imply the arcsinh threshold here. Search-based absence is not priority proof: an equivalent exact two-tone sensor-design calculation under different terminology remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The precise lowest singular value of a PhaseLift-style measurement map is a standard worst-case *linear lifted* noise-gain invariant, directly relevant to selecting spectrogram frequency bins. It is not chosen merely to produce a formula. The sharp switch explains when amplitude balancing or interference visibility limits two-line reconstruction, quantifies the small-separation linear penalty and the large-separation exponential penalty, and establishes necessity and sufficiency of two symmetric lines for this precise two-tone space. These conclusions are not implied by qualitative phase-retrieval uniqueness and are relevant for narrowband sensor design, without requiring any unproved downstream theorem.
