# Review: two-site surface-contact throughput

## Correctness — PASS

The Neumann resolvent for the reflected Brownian diffusion, followed by the Poisson-reset renewal resolvent, gives an exact discounted boundary occupation density. Changing variables from physical time to the retained local-time clock proves the inverse-local-time exponent. A separate first-contact renewal relation supplies the prefactor for a positive starting reset point; differentiating both transforms gives the quoted mean threshold times. The inverse-subordinator strong law yields the physical-time contact throughput. For the two-site model, differentiating the two-exponential throughput reduces its stationary points to a scalar equation. The logarithmic derivative of its profile has an exact minimum, yielding the distance-ratio cusp; a strictly increasing logarithmic difference of the two stationary peak heights proves the unique coexistence probability. These are analytic, all-parameter arguments. `checker.py` passes 706 finite, standard-library assertions; finite tests are not substituted for proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority risk

Bressloff, *Threshold surface reactions and local time resetting* (2022), arXiv:2203.04868, section 3 and Eq. (3.17), already obtains the single-site mean threshold time for retained local time. Colantoni, D'Ovidio and Pagnini, *Time reversal of Reflected Brownian Motion with Poissonian Resetting* (2025), section 6, obtain the inverse-local-time exponent for resets to the reflecting boundary itself. Julián-Salgado, Dagdug and Boyer, *Diffusion with two resetting points* (2023/2024), arXiv:2311.11897, sections III–V, obtain the related two-point absorbing first-arrival problem and show optimal-rate jumps for other control slices. Their first-arrival formula contains the same exponential reset-location mixture in a near-target derivative; that formula and general first-passage renewal identities are established antecedents, not claimed as new. The present claim is the exact all-distance-ratio cusp threshold and one-and-only-one weight-driven coexistence in the boundary-contact throughput problem, with its retained-local-time interpretation. Direct checks of published primary sections and overlap searches did not identify an already-stated classification or a dominating stronger theorem. A broader unindexed two-exponential-mixture optimization theorem remains a material possible antecedent.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For a partially reactive reflecting boundary, the accumulating contact-time budget survives positional relocation: this is physically different from absorption at first hit or resetting reaction progress. The exact long-time throughput gives a natural parameter-optimization target. The sharp universal threshold determines when stochastic relocation to a second site can create first-order switching between optimal reset rates; the unique coexistence probability and distinct physical rate branches make the distinction testable. A complete phase diagram is stronger than numerical optima, a formal inverse Laplace expression, or a single special example. No claim is made of a universal threshold for general reset laws or for optimal mean time at finite local-time demand.
