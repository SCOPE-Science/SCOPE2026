# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every player has a definite cost, including the post-deviation divisor. The same-location pair of Nash inequalities cannot both hold for different facilities because \(C/k+C/l>C/(k+1)+C/(l+1)\). Thus each village is unsplit. Direct inequalities rule out crossed assignment and give exact pooled and segregated stability thresholds, including weak ties. The social optimum is the cheaper of the one-facility and two-facility benchmark. Differentiating the two elementary welfare ratios on their valid intervals gives the sharp fixed-population extremum, including exceptional \(m=1,2\). The result is proved for all positive parameters; exact enumeration is a consistency test, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (with priority risk)

The full accessible initial-version text and appendices of Ma et al. [1] give existence, a no-cross lemma for different-position agents, an algorithm for one PNE, and a guarantee for the PNE selected by that algorithm. Those claims do not state or imply the complete co-located two-village equilibrium census or the maximum inefficiency among *all* PNEs, although they supply the general model and potential-method background. The exact rational boundary pairs and optimal PoA constants were not located in direct primary-source and mathematical-record searches. Hansen and Telelis [2] consider a related but different facility-installation game; only its abstract was inspected, so its detailed special cases remain a priority risk. Previously known generic bad-cost-sharing equilibrium examples are not claimed as new. mathematical-index and accessible local result-ledger comparisons did not reveal an equivalent or dominating statement.

## Value — PASS

The result identifies a naturally motivated consolidation-versus-fragmentation phase diagram for equal-cost facilities, counts the complete labeled equilibrium solution set, and resolves the sharp worst-equilibrium inefficiency constant of a family with arbitrarily many agents. In particular, equal charge, perfect bilateral population symmetry, and exact colocation do not prevent worst-PNE cost from increasing linearly with group size. The exact threshold cases expose coexistence of socially optimal and suboptimal equilibria, which an algorithm finding only one PNE would not reveal.

## Closest sources, limitations, and risks

[1] Ma, Xiao, Bai, Cheng (2024), arXiv:2404.08963, v1 full text including appendices, https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08963v1.

[2] Hansen, Telelis (2008), arXiv:0809.4792, abstract; different strategic facility-opening setting, https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.4792.

The theorem assumes two fixed facilities, exactly two equal-size colocated client groups, equal positive opening costs, positive separation, unit distance cost, and non-cooperative unilateral deviations. Unindexed equivalent literature, especially older facility-game subcase analyses, remains a real publication-priority risk. Computational testing was finite and does not substitute for the universal analytic proof.
