With \(m\) clients at each of two equal-cost facility sites, every pure Nash allocation is one of only three profiles: pooling on either side or home segregation, with sharp coexistence boundaries \(d/C=1/[m(m+1)]\) and \(d/C=1-1/(2m)\). The exact worst-equilibrium price of anarchy is \(4/3\) for \(m=1\), \(3/2\) for \(m=2\), and \((2m+1)/4\) for \(m\ge3\), so symmetric equal-charge markets can have linearly inefficient equilibria.
