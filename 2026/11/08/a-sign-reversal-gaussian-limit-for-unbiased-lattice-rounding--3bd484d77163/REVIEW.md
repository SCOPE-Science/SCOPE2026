# Scientific review — A sign-reversal Gaussian limit for unbiased lattice rounding

## Correctness — PASS
For each \(0<r<1\), synchronous common-uniform rounding is exactly \(r\)-contracting in expected absolute distance, which supplies a unique invariant law. Conditional first and second moments give stationary mean \(\theta\) and the exact variance balance. For positive slopes the closed two-state support determines the invariant Bernoulli law. For negative slopes the fractional part of the pre-rounded update is \(\{2\theta+(1-r)(X-\theta)\}\); the uniform stationary second-moment bound makes this converge to the noninteger phase \(p=\{2\theta\}\). A coupling to independent Bernoulli innovations yields a vanishing *normalized* mean-square discrepancy, and a triangular-array characteristic-function argument proves the Gaussian weak limit. Exact rational checks in `verify.py` and the recorded `check_output.txt` support examples but are not substituted for the infinite proof. The proof does not cover the resonant case \(2\theta\in\mathbb Z\), nonuniform float spacing, overflow, or nonlinear maps.

## Originality — PASS with residual precedence risk
The key comparison is Weiß–Zhu, arXiv:2402.15772, §§2–3 and Appendix A: it uses the same mean-preserving adjacent-integer quantizer and derives conditional moment identities for integer ARMA time series, including negative coefficients. Those identities are acknowledged and not claimed as new. Its defined ARMA recursion adds an integer-valued innovation *outside* rounding; it does not state the equal-absolute-contraction sign reversal or the near-minus-one Gaussian law for a **noninteger intercept inside rounding**. El Arar et al., arXiv:2207.03837, §§2–4 and arXiv:2207.10321, §§2–5 discuss unbiasedness, finite arithmetic errors, and bounds for sums/Horner's scheme rather than a stationary weak-contraction law. Xia et al., arXiv:2202.12276, §§1–4, 5.1, discuss low-precision gradient descent and rounding bias/monotonicity, not this stationary law. The closest related deterministic floor-map study concerns cycles of deterministic quantization, which does not imply an invariant Gaussian limit for unbiased random rounding. Search results and a partial older AR(1) rounding literature comparison leave a precedence risk; independent review is still outstanding.

## Value — PASS
A particularly natural benchmark for low-precision fixed-point iteration holds the exact fixed point and contraction magnitude fixed while reversing only the iteration eigenvalue's sign. The stationary mean stays exact in both cases, yet positive slope keeps a bounded two-point noise floor while negative slope produces a diverging variance with a **sharp constant and universal Gaussian scaling**. This distinguishes small bias from long-run mean-square fidelity near a stability boundary. The result's precise fixed-grid context and limitations are stated; it does not allege a floating-point hardware guarantee or a novel rounding operator.

## Sources inspected and remaining limitations
Full available structured article text was read for arXiv:2402.15772 (operator definition, stationarity, moment relations, examples, and appendix), arXiv:2207.10321 (model, variance bounds, summation and Horner applications), arXiv:2207.03837 (background, randomized rounding definition and numerical integration setting), and arXiv:2202.12276 (rounding schemes, convergence analysis and quadratic numerical examples). Earlier integer autoregression and rounding-error articles were checked at abstract level; their full contents were not all accessible. A published specialist result equivalent to the precise near-critical limit is possible but was not found. None of these access limitations is a substitute for independent verification.
