# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The fixed-path rooted-forest extension and deletion of a marked sibling-leaf pair give an exact cherry-size-biased distance-shell law. Its ratio against the unconditional shell mass is strictly log-concave; the endpoint signs establish a single transition, and the square-root-scale expansion locates it. Discrete integration by parts gives the monotone-distance-functional inequality. Boundary sizes four through seven are handled exactly. Finite Prüfer checks are corroboration, not a replacement for the all-size proof.

**Originality — PASS for the revised shellwise claim, with prior work explicitly credited.** An October 13, 2026 result already proves the same-model strict negative cherry–Wiener covariance, exact scalar formula and asymptotic using marked-cherry deletion. Those conclusions are not new. Its scalar conditional-distance sum does not determine the individual distance-shell covariances, their single-crossing pattern or sharp crossover scale. Prior tree distance-profile limit theorems and the classical cherry expectation do not state those finite mixed shell identities.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result connects a natural local branching count to the entire global distance profile through a sharp structural sign law. The \(\sqrt{2n}\) transition and universal monotone-functional inequality give information not recoverable from the known Wiener scalar covariance alone.

**Scope and risks.** This concerns uniformly labeled unweighted unrooted trees, \(n\ge4\), with a broad cherry count allowing high-degree centers. It neither claims a new Wiener-index scalar formula nor rules out equivalent formulas in older specialized random-tree enumerations. No formal proof-assistant or independent expert attestation is claimed.
