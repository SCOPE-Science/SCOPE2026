# Scientific assessment

## Correctness

PASS for the specified limiting norm and strict witness inequalities. The periodic Gram entries follow by exact local integration, the alternating-sign kernel is guaranteed by the even-cycle Stieltjes conjugation, and the opposite-sign linear integration is exact. Both bi-infinite rows satisfy the exceptional-edge recurrence and all uniform-tail recurrences. Their exact evaluations and positivity factorizations are checked with symbolic algebra. The Neumann series has row contraction factor one half, giving uniform exponential inverse decay; the rank-two resolvent identity controls rows remote from the exceptional cell. This establishes existence of the claimed limit and its strict lower bounds. No exact closed form for the full limiting supremum is asserted.

## Originality

PASS on the accessible comparisons, with residual priority uncertainty. Ciesielski–Kamont's 2004 general inverse-Gram norm identity is acknowledged as prior; their evaluated Franklin result covers a finite-interval two-block dyadic mesh, not a periodic single-cell defect with arbitrary positive ratio. Evaluating the arbitrary-mesh identity still requires the new defect recurrence and sign analysis. Passenbrunner's full periodic spline paper proves boundedness and convergence without sharp local sensitivity. Kayumov's early preprint concerns second-order differential-operator splines and mesh-independent constants; Oswald's result is a higher-dimensional obstruction. Gawlik–Lew address smooth-input supercloseness, which does not imply a worst-case \(L^\infty\) norm. Searches of available research records for exceptional-cell, local Green-perturbation, and inverse-Gram formulations returned no covering statement. Unindexed older spline papers remain a genuine risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Uniform meshes have limiting endpoint norm \(2\), but even one anomalous element among arbitrarily many restores a strictly larger asymptotic norm, for any fixed nonunit interval ratio. The phenomenon matters for stability of localized mesh modifications under worst-case bounded inputs and sharply distinguishes isolated mesh defects from globally alternating nonuniformity. Two explicit algebraic witness functions of the ratio establish the sign transition across the entire parameter range; the result is not merely one numerical example.

## Limits and closest literature

This is a strict-instability theorem, not an exact formula for the full limiting operator norm. Existing related work includes Ciesielski–Kamont (2004), Kayumov (2004), Oswald (2006), Gawlik–Lew (2014), and Passenbrunner (2016). Publication priority beyond the inspected sources is not certified.
