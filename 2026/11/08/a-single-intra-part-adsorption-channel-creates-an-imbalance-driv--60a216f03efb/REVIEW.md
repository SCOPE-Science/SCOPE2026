# Scientific review: one added intra-part adsorption channel

## Correctness — PASS

The independent first-alarm coupling rigorously reduces the rate-weighted adsorption problem to the pure complete-bipartite death chain. Exchangeability supplies the two-marked-vertex survival factor, and exponential stage times yield the finite rational law. The pathwise terminal count and the weak-rate limit follow directly; elementary bounds on the Laplace factors and a bulk Riemann-sum estimate prove the thermodynamic and square-root-window laws for all stated parameters. The separately implemented full-graph residual-state dynamic program agreed with the finite formula in 120 rational parameter comparisons, and numerical larger-size runs agree with the analytical limits. Finite computation is corroboration, not the proof of an all-size assertion.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, provisional

The classical graph-RSA and random greedy-matching literature is clearly related; it is not represented as new. The 12-page full arXiv review by Cadilhe, Araújo and Privman discusses patterned and Bethe-lattice adsorption, not this complete-bipartite single-defect law. The full open-access Arnosti paper addresses an all-bipartite random-edge matching setting, not an intra-part added edge or its strong population asymmetry. Available abstracts for Pippenger (1989) and Dyer--Frieze (1991) document foundational graph-RSA and random greedy matching, not the present complete parametric statement. Direct exact-name, synonym and implication searches found no stronger result that establishes the same finite law or critical window; this absence is not by itself a proof of priority. The older full texts remain unavailable and are recorded as residual risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural, nontrivial perturbation of a completely solvable matching/adsorption substrate. The exactly quantifiable effect of one new channel is not monotone in coverage: it loses a dimer, is neutral, or gains a dimer depending solely on the one-vertex-scale population imbalance. The same event shows a change from order-one probability to order-inverse-size probability and a nontrivial critical window of width \(\sqrt a\). These boundaries make the result a useful rigorous benchmark for competition between heterogeneous reaction channels on dense substrates; its value does not depend on a claim of downstream application.

## Closest literature and limitations

Cadilhe--Araújo--Privman (2006 arXiv); Pippenger (1989); Dyer--Frieze (1991); Arnosti (first online 2021). Pippenger's and Dyer--Frieze's full papers were not accessible in this review, and an equivalent unindexed edge-ordering or random greedy matching calculation cannot be ruled out. The result covers only one defect edge, and all limits are for completed irreversible adsorption at positive fixed defect rate, except the explicitly stated subsequent weak-rate limit.
