# A single possible free degree-nine cubic–line incidence profile

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(D\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\) be a reduced union of \(k\geq1\) distinct smooth plane cubics and \(d\geq1\) distinct lines, with \(3k+d=9\). Suppose all singularities of the reduced divisor \(D\) are ordinary nodes \(A_1\), ordinary transverse triple points \(D_4\), or two-smooth-branch contacts of intersection multiplicity three \(A_5\). If \(D\) is a free divisor, then necessarily \(k=2\), \(d=3\), its exponents are \((4,4)\), and its numbers of singular points are

\[
(n_2,n_3,t_5)=(3,0,9).
\]

In that remaining necessary profile, exactly three \(A_5\) points are intersections of the two cubic components; the other six \(A_5\) points are intersections of a cubic with a line. Each of the three lines is a flex tangent to **both** cubic components, each pair of distinct lines meets in an ordinary node, and all intersections between the two cubics occur at the three order-three contacts.

In particular, no free degree-nine divisor of the specified singularity types consists of one smooth cubic and six lines. This theorem is **necessary, not sufficient**: it asserts neither the existence nor nonexistence of an arrangement with the surviving \((2,3;3,0,9)\) profile.

## Assumptions and scope

We work over \(\mathbb C\), on the projective plane, with no coincident components. For a reduced homogeneous equation \(F\) of degree nine, freeness means its Jacobian-syzygy module is freely generated by two homogeneous relations of degrees \(e_1\le e_2\); then \(e_1+e_2=8\). We write \(n_2\) for the number of ordinary double points, \(n_3\) for transverse triple points, and \(t_5\) for contact-type \(A_5\) double points. An \(A_5\) point has exactly two smooth local branches meeting with intersection multiplicity three, so in our arrangements it cannot belong to three or more components. An ordinary triple point has three distinct tangent directions.

The proof uses the characteristic-zero freeness bound and the cubic–line Hirzebruch-type inequality of Talar [1]; these are externally established mathematical inputs, not independently reproved here. All remaining incidence restrictions are elementary consequences of Bézout and distinct-branch hypotheses.

## Proof

**1. Freeness forces the numerical identities.** The smallest local log canonical threshold among the permitted singularities is at least \(2/3\). The Dimca–Sernesi syzygy bound, as applied in [1, Theorem 3.1], gives \(e_1\geq(2/3)\cdot9-2=4\). Since \(e_1\leq e_2\) and \(e_1+e_2=8\), one has \(e_1=e_2=4\). The free-curve Tjurina formula then gives

\[
\tau(D)=8^2-4(8-4)=48.
\]

The local Tjurina numbers for \(A_1,D_4,A_5\) are respectively \(1,4,5\). Hence

\[
n_2+4n_3+5t_5=48.\tag{1}
\]

Bézout's formula for the sum of pairwise intersection multiplicities is

\[
9\binom{k}{2}+3kd+\binom d2=n_2+3n_3+3t_5.\tag{2}
\]

The relation \(3k+d=9\) with positive integers leaves only \((k,d)=(1,6)\) or \((2,3)\).

**2. The flex-consumption lemma.** If \(E\) is a smooth cubic and a line \(L\) makes an \(A_5\) point with \(E\) at \(P\), then \(I_P(E,L)=3\). Bézout gives \(E\cap L=\{P\}\) set-theoretically, with all intersection multiplicity spent there. In particular, \(L\) cannot meet \(E\) at any transverse triple point or any other singular point. Such an \(L\) is an inflectional tangent to \(E\). Distinct lines account for distinct flex contacts with a fixed cubic, because \(A_5\) has only two branches.

**3. One cubic and six lines are impossible.** Here \(A_5\) contacts occur only between the cubic and a line. Let \(t=t_5\), so exactly \(t\) of the six lines are flex tangents, and \(0\leq t\leq6\). Equation (2) has left side \(18+15=33\). Solving with (1) yields

\[
n_3=15-2t,\qquad n_2=3t-12.
\]

Consequently \(t\in\{4,5,6\}\). Among six lines admitting no point of multiplicity greater than three, each line passes through at most two triple intersections of lines (each uses two of the five other lines). Thus there are at most \(\lfloor6\cdot2/3\rfloor=4\) line-only triple points. A triple point containing the cubic consists of the cubic and two lines that are not its flex tangents. There are only \(6-t\) such lines, giving the sharp necessary bound

\[
n_3\leq4+\binom{6-t}{2}.\tag{3}
\]

For \(t=4\), (1)–(2) require \(n_3=7\), contradicting the bound \(5\). For \(t=5\), they require \(n_3=5\), contradicting the bound \(4\). For \(t=6\), one has \((n_2,n_3,t_5)=(6,3,6)\). But the published inequality [1, Theorem 2.2], valid for this entire class of complex arrangements,

\[
27k+n_2+\tfrac34n_3\geq d+5t_5,\tag{4}
\]

would give \(27+6+9/4\geq6+30\), i.e. \(35.25\geq36\), a contradiction. Thus \((k,d)=(1,6)\) is excluded.

**4. Two cubics and three lines leave a single numerical profile.** Now (2) has left side \(9+18+3=30\). Solving it together with (1) gives

\[
n_3=18-2t_5,\qquad n_2=3t_5-24.
\]

Let \(u\) count \(A_5\) intersections of the two cubics, and \(v\) count \(A_5\) intersections of a cubic and a line. Distinct pairs of smooth cubics meet in total intersection multiplicity nine, so \(u\leq3\). There are six cubic–line pairs, and each can contribute at most one \(A_5\) point by the flex-consumption lemma, so \(v\leq6\). Also \(t_5=u+v\leq9\). Nonnegativity of \(n_2\) forces \(t_5\geq8\). Thus only

\[
(n_2,n_3,t_5)=(0,2,8)\quad\text{or}\quad(3,0,9)
\]

can remain. We exclude the first by incidence, not by the inequalities alone.

If \(t_5=8\), necessarily \((u,v)=(2,6)\) or \((3,5)\). In either case, at least five of the six cubic–line pairs are flex contacts, so at most one cubic–line pair has any additional intersection. An ordinary triple consisting of one cubic and two lines would require two **distinct** nonflex cubic–line pairs. Therefore no such triple exists. Likewise an ordinary triple consisting of two cubics and a line is impossible: in the case \(u=3\), the three cubic–cubic \(A_5\) points consume all nine units of cubic–cubic intersection, leaving no transverse cubic–cubic meeting; in the case \(u=2\), one has \(v=6\), leaving no transverse cubic–line meeting. Thus all ordinary triple points must consist of the three lines alone, and there can be at most one such point. This contradicts \(n_3=2\). Therefore \(t_5=9\), \(n_3=0\), and \(n_2=3\).

Finally \(t_5=9\) forces \(u=3\) and \(v=6\). Every cubic–line pair is an \(A_5\) flex contact, and the three cubic–cubic \(A_5\) points exhaust their mutual Bézout number. No cubic can meet any line at another point. The three remaining nodes must therefore be precisely the pairwise intersections of the three lines. This proves all assertions. \(\square\)

## Verification

A standalone pure-Python file `verify_profiles.py` enumerates the nonnegative integer solutions of (1), (2), and (4) for both possible component counts, then applies (3) and the \((u,v)\) incidence restriction. Run it with `python3 verify_profiles.py` from the directory containing this document. The expected terminal line is `DEGREE9_CUBIC_LINE_NECESSARY_CLASSIFICATION_PASS`. It is a finite check of derived necessary arithmetic restrictions; the proof above establishes the geometric restrictions and the infinite complex-geometric theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Talar [1], originally public on 2023-05-02, established the Bézout and orbifold constraints, the free-exponent bound, and four numerically admissible degree-nine profiles. Section 3 explicitly leaves geometric realizability of all four open. The present theorem eliminates the two one-cubic profiles and the two-cubic profile with two triple points by exploiting the fact that cubic–line \(A_5\) contact uses an entire line's Bézout budget, combined with a bound on three-line incidences. The surviving \((3,0,9)\) profile is not claimed as realized.

Dimca–Ilardi–Pokora–Sticlaru [2] provide broader addition-of-inflectional-tangents constructions and freeness techniques; these do not assert this degree-nine exclusion. Bannai–Guerville-Ballé–Shirane–Tokunaga [3] classify topological behavior of \(k\)-Artal arrangements including six flex lines; their focus is Zariski pairs, not the necessary free profiles in this theorem. Earlier conic–line freeness classifications address different component degrees and singularity classes. The novelty assessment remains subject to possible unindexed or implicit earlier results.

## Limitations

This is a conditional nonexistence/necessary-profile theorem for free arrangements with exactly the three allowed local singularity types. It does not classify arbitrary degree-nine cubic–line arrangements, does not assert that the remaining profile occurs or is free, and does not address higher singularities, non-smooth cubic components, other base fields, or near freeness. The external freeness and orbifold inequalities are used as published theorems, and priority beyond the inspected sources is not independently certified.
