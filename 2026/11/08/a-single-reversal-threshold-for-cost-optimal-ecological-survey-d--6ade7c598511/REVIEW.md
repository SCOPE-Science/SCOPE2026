# Scientific review: unique persistent-Gamma survey-cap reversal

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem fixes occupancy \(0<q<1\), Gamma shape \(k>0\), mean conditional rate \(\mu>0\), effort cost \(c>0\), avoided late-management loss \(\Delta>0\), and a single removal-survey cap. Its objective is derived from survival-tail integration and the occupied-but-missed probability. Differentiating shows that both marginal benefit/cost ratios decrease strictly, with a shared starting value. Consequently the zero-cap threshold is exactly \(\mu\Delta/c=1/q\), while positive optimal caps are uniquely defined by an explicit log equation and a monotone Gamma root. The critical nonstandard step is an entire-shape uniqueness argument: the difference between the two marginal ratios has the sign of a function whose third derivative is positive for every \(k>0\), yielding exactly one positive crossing. This single crossing transfers to the unique optimal-cap reversal. The package includes independently executable arithmetic checks; these diagnose formulas but are not substituted for the continuum proof. Endpoints and model limitations are expressly excluded.

## Originality — PASS within available evidence, provisional

The principal prior comparison is Guillera-Arroita, Hauser and McCarthy (2014), DOI 10.1002/ece3.1056: its complete accessible main text addresses stopping a survey on detection with fixed intensity. Moore et al. (2014), DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0115345, analyze stochastic rate variation across visits and explicitly discuss correlated rates; Moore and McCarthy (2016), DOI 10.1111/2041-210X.12564, optimize visit counts and spatial allocations with Gamma variability. The main text and relevant formulas of these primary sources were inspected. Earlier Hauser and McCarthy (2009), DOI 10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01323.x, supplies the cost-allocation context but does not establish the present persistent-Gamma removal-cap comparison in the accessible abstract. The papers already show that stochastic detectability can change survey effort and that allocation can be nonmonotonic; **neither inspected main text supplies the sharp one-crossing theorem comparing mean-matched cap optimizers for the stated persistent-site Gamma model**. Semantic searches of prior research records for persistent frailty, aliases, optimal caps, and Gamma missed-detection costs returned related but not covering statements. This is an implication-based comparison, not a claim that lack of a keyword result proves global novelty. Remaining risks include uninspected supplemental details on correlated survey conditions and older search-theory literature.

## Value — PASS

Rate heterogeneity is routinely handled through the mean rate when planning wildlife and invasive-species detection, while stopping immediately on first detection changes actual survey expenditure. In this model, the direction of the resulting policy error genuinely reverses as the management stakes increase. The theorem supplies an exact parameter threshold for *every* nondegenerate occupancy prior and Gamma shape, rather than one numerical instance or an unsupported prescription. The result identifies when persistent-rate heterogeneity calls for less versus more planned search time and delineates a testable modeling sensitivity. It does not claim empirical calibration, spatial optimization, or performance under repeated independent visits.

## Source-access and priority limitations

The 2014 removal-surveillance primary article and the 2014 and 2016 stochastic-detection primary main texts were accessible. The 2009 publisher page supplied abstract/bibliography but not the detailed optimization appendix; the 2014 correlation supplement was not materially reviewed. Related earlier search-theory papers may prove broader formulas from which this theorem could follow; the present finding should undergo independent historical-priority review before external publication as a new result.
