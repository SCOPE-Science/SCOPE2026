# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The proof closes all quantifiers for fixed known \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) and every integer \(n\ge3\). The stationary covariance fixed-point equations follow from independent synchronous updates and their contraction by \(q<1\); rotational reduction leads to a two-step resolvent of a cyclic shift. The exact Fourier/Poisson-kernel sum gives \(H_n\), and the global comparison \(H_n>H_{n+1}\), together with \(F'_q>0\), proves the strict order. The asymptotic coefficient is an exact derivative. Independent direct-pair linear systems in rational arithmetic agree with the spectral formula for the packaged finite cases. The calculations do not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Benjamini–Helman Tov–Zhukovskii (arXiv:2207.01224, 2022-07-04) introduce the local-observation question and prove wheel-adjacent results for cycles and complete graphs, but do not state this wheel-family one-lag order. Their complete-bipartite section is not a specialization to \(K_1\vee C_n\). Pymar–Rivera (arXiv:2112.01478) instead study an asynchronous voter variant and global density. Literature and published-result searches did not reveal a statement implying the exact wheel comparison, but absence from the retrieved sources is not a novelty certificate. The wheel of rim size three is a complete graph and is a known special case; it is included only as a boundary of the new uniform family.

## Value — PASS
The question is local-to-global graph identification for a natural, structurally nontrivial infinite family. The solution requires a discounted two-walker Green function and handles odd and even rim sizes and every fixed noise strength simultaneously. It yields a complete family classification from a single lag, plus a quantitative \(n^{-2}\) separation showing how discrimination becomes more delicate for larger wheels. No result beyond this family, no unknown-noise identification, and no finite-sample guarantees are claimed.
