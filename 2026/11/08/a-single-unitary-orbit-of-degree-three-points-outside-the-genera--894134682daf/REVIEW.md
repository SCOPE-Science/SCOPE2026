# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem covers every prime power \(q\ge2\), the base field \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), and its degree-three extension. The binary Hermitian-line restriction proves absence of new degree-two points. Frobenius descent and the nondegenerate cyclic Gram frame prove transitivity. Its normalization uses the surjective \(\mathbb F_{q^6}/\mathbb F_{q^3}\) norm, while the stabilizer is the explicitly calculated quotient of two cyclic norm-one groups by the projective center. Independent counting by smooth Hermitian-plane sections reproduces the orbit size. The full-extension count and zeta function follow from maximality and generator incidence. Exact GF(64) enumeration checks the smallest field but is not a substitute for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk
The 2021 geometric source determines classical generator and rational-point data and the unitary symmetry group; its claims about the base-field configuration do not state transitivity on degree-three points *outside every generator*, nor their cyclic stabilizer. The 2018 Hermitian-curve group-theory paper concerns plane curves and triangle stabilizers rather than this surface. Six targeted mathematical-record searches for generator-complement extensions, equivalent projective-unitary orbits, stabilizers and zeta formulas showed no directly covering statement. The ordinary all-extension point-count formula is logically implied by classical known inputs and is *not* asserted to be the new contribution. A historical orbit classification in the finite-polar-geometry literature could nevertheless cover the main result; historical priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The generator complement is an intrinsic arithmetic open surface. The least extension degree of its closed points and their symmetry orbit are natural invariants, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. A single orbit with a cyclic stabilizer gives a complete classification of the first nonempty rational-point stratum and a canonical geometric fiber decomposition; the result is reusable for point-orbit methods and associated evaluation configurations. The proof applies uniformly in even and odd characteristics. The result is not a claim about all unitary varieties or a new count for the classical closed Hermitian surface.
