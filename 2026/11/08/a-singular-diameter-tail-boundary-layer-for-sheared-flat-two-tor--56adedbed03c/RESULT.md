# A singular diameter-tail boundary layer for sheared flat two-tori

## Finding

For \(0\leq\delta<1/2\), let \(\Lambda_\delta\subset\mathbb R^2\) be the lattice generated by \(a=(1,0)\) and \(b=(\delta,1)\). Give \(T_\delta=\mathbb R^2/\Lambda_\delta\) its intrinsic flat Riemannian metric and normalized area measure (its total area is one). Independently sample \(X_\delta,Y_\delta\) from this measure and write \(D_\delta=d_{T_\delta}(X_\delta,Y_\delta)\). Put
\[
R_\delta=\frac12\sqrt{(1+\delta^2)(2-2\delta+\delta^2)}.
\]
Then \(R_\delta\) is the intrinsic diameter. For every fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\) the **exact quadratic upper-tail coefficient** is
\[
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\frac{\Pr\{D_\delta>R_\delta-\varepsilon\}}{\varepsilon^2}
= C_\delta
=2\left(\frac1\delta+\frac1{1-\delta}+\frac1{1-\delta+\delta^2}\right).
\]
At the rectangular endpoint, \(C_0=4\). Thus \(C_\delta=2/\delta+4+O(\delta)\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\): the upper-tail coefficient diverges despite convergence of the underlying flat metrics and their distance distributions.

A sharper **two-scale crossover law** holds. Define \(u_+=\max\{u,0\}\). For each fixed \(t\geq0\),
\[
\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\frac{\Pr\{D_\delta>R_\delta-t\delta^2\}}{\delta^3}
=H(t):=2\sqrt2\,t-\frac23\left[1-(1-2\sqrt2\,t)_+^{3/2}\right].
\]
In particular, \(H(t)\sim2t^2\) as \(t\downarrow0\), while \(H(t)=2\sqrt2\,t-2/3\) for \(t\geq1/(2\sqrt2)\). The coefficient limit and the zero-shear limit do not commute. The theorem concerns the intrinsic distance between independent uniform **points of a flat torus**, not Euclidean chord distance in an embedding.

## Assumptions and scope

All probabilities refer to independent normalized Haar-area samples from a flat two-torus. The shear parameter is real with \(0\leq\delta<1/2\), the lattice is generated by the explicit vectors above, and the diameter is intrinsic. The first limit takes \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) with \(\delta\) fixed. The second takes \(\delta\downarrow0\) with \(t\) fixed. These orders of limits are essential; no uniform-in-\(\delta\) quadratic-tail statement is asserted.

An exact full distribution follows from the proof. For \(0<\delta<1/2\), put
\[
h_1=\frac12,\qquad h_2=\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta^2}}2,\qquad
h_3=\frac{\sqrt{2-2\delta+\delta^2}}2,
\]
and define the circular-segment area
\[
S(r,h)=\begin{cases}0,&0\leq r\leq h,\\
r^2\arccos(h/r)-h\sqrt{r^2-h^2},&r>h.
\end{cases}
\]
Then the full CDF is \(F_\delta(r)=0\) for \(r<0\),
\[
F_\delta(r)=\pi r^2-2\sum_{j=1}^3S(r,h_j)
\quad(0\leq r<R_\delta),
\]
and \(F_\delta(r)=1\) for \(r\geq R_\delta\). At \(\delta=0\) omit \(h_3\), retain \(h_1=h_2=1/2\), and use \(R_0=1/\sqrt2\). This is a constructive formula; previously established qualitative distance-distribution rigidity is **not** claimed as new.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to a Voronoi cell.** By translation invariance, \(Y_\delta-X_\delta\) is Haar-uniform on \(T_\delta\). Its shortest-vector representative is therefore area-uniform on the centered lattice Voronoi cell \(V_\delta\), which has area one. Consequently \(F_\delta(r)=\operatorname{area}(V_\delta\cap B(0,r))\).

For positive shear set \(c=b-a=(\delta-1,1)\). The cell is the intersection of the six half-planes
\[
|z\cdot a|\leq\frac{|a|^2}{2},\qquad
|z\cdot b|\leq\frac{|b|^2}{2},\qquad
|z\cdot c|\leq\frac{|c|^2}{2}.
\]
Here is an elementary completeness check. Every lattice vector has the form \(m a+n b\). If \(m,n\geq0\), express it as positive copies of \(a,b\), whose dot product is \(\delta>0\). If \(m\geq0,n=-k\leq0\) and \(m\geq k\), express it as \((m-k)a+k(a-b)\); its two building vectors have dot product \(1-\delta>0\). If \(k\geq m\), use \(m(a-b)+(k-m)(-b)\); their dot product is \(1-\delta+\delta^2>0\). Negating handles the remaining sign configurations. Hence the six displayed inequalities imply \(|z\cdot v|\leq|v|^2/2\) for *every* lattice vector \(v\): first use the triangle inequality for dot products, then \(\sum|v_i|^2\leq|\sum v_i|^2\) for the nonnegatively paired decomposition. These are exactly the Voronoi inequalities.

Set \(q=1-\delta+\delta^2\) and \(w=1+\delta-\delta^2\). For \(0<\delta<1/2\), the resulting six genuine vertices are the opposite pairs of
\[
(1/2,q/2),\qquad(1/2,-q/2),\qquad(\delta-1/2,w/2).
\]
Direct substitution into the six constraints verifies the vertex list and positive side lengths. All six vertices have radius
\[
R_\delta=\tfrac12\sqrt{1+q^2}=\tfrac12|b|\,|c|.
\]
At \(\delta=0\) the third pair of opposite facets collapses and \(V_0=[-1/2,1/2]^2\). Thus the Voronoi cells in this family are cyclic quadrilaterals or hexagons about their lattice origin, and the asserted \(R_\delta\) is their farthest radius.

**2. The full CDF.** Inside their common circumdisk of radius \(R_\delta\), the parts lying beyond the side chords of a cyclic convex polygon are pairwise disjoint circular segments (up to boundaries). Each pair of opposite sides has supporting-line distance \(h_j\). Subtracting those segments from \(B(0,r)\), for every \(r<R_\delta\), gives the displayed CDF. The origin lies strictly inside the cell; all \(h_j<R_\delta\) for fixed positive shear.

For \(r<R_\delta\), differentiation of the segment area yields
\[
F_\delta'(r)=2\pi r-4r\sum_j\arccos(h_j/r)\,\mathbf1_{\{r>h_j\}}.
\]
Write \(\theta_j=\arccos(h_j/R_\delta)\). Since the cyclic polygon has two copies of each side class, its central arcs sum to \(2\pi\), whence \(\sum_j\theta_j=\pi/2\). Thus \(F_\delta'(R_\delta-)=0\) and
\[
F_\delta''(R_\delta-)=-4\sum_j\frac{h_j}{\sqrt{R_\delta^2-h_j^2}}.
\]
Taylor's formula from the left at \(R_\delta\), where \(F_\delta(R_\delta)=1\), gives the quadratic tail coefficient \(2\sum_jh_j/\sqrt{R_\delta^2-h_j^2}\).

The three fractions simplify *exactly* to
\[
\frac{h_1}{\sqrt{R_\delta^2-h_1^2}}=\frac1{1-\delta+\delta^2},\quad
\frac{h_2}{\sqrt{R_\delta^2-h_2^2}}=\frac1{1-\delta},\quad
\frac{h_3}{\sqrt{R_\delta^2-h_3^2}}=\frac1\delta.
\]
This proves \(C_\delta\). The square has only two side pairs, each contributing one; hence \(C_0=4\), and the claimed divergence follows.

**3. The singular crossover.** Observe that
\[
R_\delta-h_3=\frac{|c|}{2}\bigl(\sqrt{1+\delta^2}-1\bigr)
=\frac{\delta^2}{2\sqrt2}+O(\delta^3),
\]
so the short, disappearing third facet becomes active in the CDF precisely in a radius window of order \(\delta^2\). Set \(r=R_\delta-s\delta^2\), with fixed \(s\geq0\). Its angular term satisfies
\[
\delta^{-1}\arccos(h_3/r)\,\mathbf1_{\{r>h_3\}}
\longrightarrow\sqrt{(1-2\sqrt2\,s)_+}.
\]
Also \(\arccos(h_3/R_\delta)=\arctan\delta\), and the changes in the other two angular terms between \(r\) and \(R_\delta\) are \(O(\delta^2)\), uniformly for bounded \(s\). Using \(\sum_j\theta_j=\pi/2\) in the derivative expression therefore gives
\[
\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\frac{F_\delta'(R_\delta-s\delta^2)}{\delta}
=2\sqrt2\left[1-\sqrt{(1-2\sqrt2\,s)_+}\right].
\]
The derivative is bounded after this rescaling on every fixed compact \(s\)-interval, so integrate \(1-F_\delta(R_\delta-t\delta^2)=\delta^2\int_0^t F_\delta'(R_\delta-s\delta^2)\,ds\) and pass to the limit. Elementary integration gives \(H(t)\) as stated. This also proves the weak continuity of distances need not imply continuity of their endpoint quadratic-tail coefficient. The two one-sided limits are \(+\infty\) (coefficient first) and \(4\) (shear first).

## Verification

The standalone `check.py` uses only Python 3 standard-library modules. It constructs the actual Voronoi polygons from half-plane intersections at five shear values including zero, checks all vertex radii and unit areas, and compares the analytic CDF with independent angular numerical integration at twenty combinations of shears and radii. It also checks the exact tail coefficient algebraically and verifies convergence to the crossover law at twelve small-shear test points. Executed on the packaged source, it reports `FLAT_TORUS_RADIAL_AREA_CYCLICITY_EXACT_TAIL_AND_CROSSOVER_OK 32`. Numerical examples are checks rather than an infinite proof; the general statement follows from the preceding identities and limits.

## Relationship to prior work

Mémoli and Needham, *Distance distributions and inverse problems for metric measure spaces* (arXiv:1810.09646, Proposition 5.16), already establish **qualitative determination of a planar flat torus by its intrinsic distance distribution** and discuss its initial critical radii. Neither that inverse theorem nor the initial-overlap calculation is claimed as new. The present theorem instead gives the **complete upper-endpoint coefficient and singular two-scale boundary law** at a natural rectangular-to-oblique transition. Bernard and Labrousse (arXiv:1406.0794) investigate a different global characterization of flat metrics by geodesic-flow entropy, not random-distance endpoint asymptotics. Balch, Peterson and Shonkwiler (arXiv:2004.13196) treat distance laws on homogeneous spherical lens spaces, not sheared flat two-tori.

## Limitations

The crossover is established only for the explicitly given unit-area shear family and fixed \(t\). It is not a uniform estimate at arbitrary joint rates, a finite-sample statistical stability theorem, or a claim about arbitrary two-dimensional flat metrics or higher-dimensional tori. The result's historical originality is best-knowledge based on the cited literature and searches; an independent priority audit has not been performed.
