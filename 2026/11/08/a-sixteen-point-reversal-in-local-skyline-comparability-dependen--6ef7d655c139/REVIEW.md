The combinatorial overlap identity yields the remaining covariance; strict convexity of the exact harmonic numerator proves the unique sample-size cutoff, and a positive product inequality proves the disjoint-label sign in every dimension. Exact-rational, label-averaged rank enumeration and the finite-sum formulas agree in all tested cases. The proof is self-contained for all admissible dimensions and sample sizes; enumerations are not substituted for proof.

A primary paper on Euler–Stirling statistics reports aggregate generating functions, which do not specify marked comparable-pair incidence. There remains an unexcluded possibility that an older permutation-code calculation implicitly contains the marked overlap; this is not a proof of absolute priority.

**Value — PASS.** The sharp finite crossover separates local incident comparability from global pair density in a classic Pareto-frontier model. It demonstrates a genuine difference between shared and disjoint-label dependence: the local contribution changes sign while the global dependence stays negative. The dimension-free disjoint-label inequality provides a geometric structural explanation, rather than an arbitrary numeric threshold.
