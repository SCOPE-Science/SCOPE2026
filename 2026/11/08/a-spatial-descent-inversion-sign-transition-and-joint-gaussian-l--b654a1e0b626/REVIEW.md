# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact binary-word representation is the simultaneously valid coupling in Işlak (2013, Lemma 2.2). The five overlap types give the complete site-specific mixed covariance; exact second moments and a centered-digit decomposition establish the covariance matrix. A bounded one-dependent blocking argument with Cramér–Wold proves the all-size bivariate normal limit. The determinant \(q^2(1-12q^2)/12\) is strictly positive for \(0<q\le1/4\). The standalone exact verifier passed **702 assertions**, independently comparing random-word and forward-riffle models for \(n\le7\); these checks are supplementary, not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Işlak (2013, arXiv:1303.2387) derives the inverse-shuffle coupling and **separate** one-dimensional CLTs for descents and inversions. Conger and Viswanath (2005, arXiv:math/0508242) handle separate marginal limits of fixed-composition multiset rearrangements and provide moment-calculation tools, some of which could imply special finite-moment expressions. Fulman (1997, arXiv:math/9712240) treats biased-shuffle enumerative formulas. Dörr and Heiny (2023, arXiv:2309.17314) treat joint limits under uniform random permutations, not the biased two-packet distribution. A full text of Thiel (2013, arXiv:1302.6708) was inaccessible; its stated statistic is the major index, but implicit stronger consequences cannot be excluded. Inspected sources did not already state or entail a verified complete joint biased-shuffle Gaussian covariance matrix together with the exact spatial sign crossover. Priority remains qualified, not certified.

## Value — PASS

For the widely studied two-packet riffle model, the known marginal fluctuation laws leave unanswered whether descent and inversion fluctuations remain associated. The explicit asymptotic dependence matrix resolves that natural question. The exact site-specific covariance law goes further: with sufficiently biased packets, early and late descents predict total inversions in **opposite directions**, with a sharp finite threshold and normalized transition coordinate. This is a structural dependence phenomenon, not a numerical case study.

## Limitations and verification boundary

The bias is fixed in \((0,1)\) for the joint CLT. No uniform endpoint regime, quantitative bivariate convergence rate, or exhaustive literature priority certification is asserted. All finite exact checks are independent of the all-\(n\) proof.
