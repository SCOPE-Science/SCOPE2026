# A strict Crofton line-distance order for every regular polygon

## Finding

Fix \(R>0\). For each integer \(n\ge3\), consider the centered regular \(n\)-gon \(K_n\) with circumradius \(R\). A random unoriented line is sampled from motion-invariant line measure conditioned on intersecting \(K_n\). Write \(D_n\) for its distance from the polygon center, and \(F_n(r)=\Pr(D_n\le r)\). Set \(\beta_n=\pi/n\). The complete distribution is

\[
F_n(r)=\begin{cases}
0,&r\le0,\\
\dfrac{\pi r}{nR\sin\beta_n},&0<r\le R\cos\beta_n,\\
1+\dfrac{(r/R)\arccos(r/R)-\sqrt{1-(r/R)^2}}{\sin\beta_n},&R\cos\beta_n<r<R,\\
1,&r\ge R.
\end{cases}
\]

For every \(0<r<R\), this family is strictly ordered:
\[
F_3(r)>F_4(r)>F_5(r)>\cdots>r/R,
\qquad \lim_{n\to\infty}F_n(r)=r/R.
\]
Thus one exact conditional probability that the line also hits a specified concentric disk identifies the number of sides, among regular polygons of a known circumradius. The polygons compared need not be nested.

The law changes smoothly but nonanalytically at the inradius \(c_n=R\cos\beta_n\): it is continuously differentiable there, while its one-sided second derivatives are \(0\) and \(-1/(R^2\sin^2\beta_n)\). At the circumradius,
\[
1-F_n(r)=\frac{2\sqrt2}{3\sin\beta_n}(1-r/R)^{3/2}+O_n((1-r/R)^{5/2}).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The polygons are nondegenerate, centered and regular; \(n\) is any integer at least three. Lines are *not* generated by independently choosing two endpoints on the polygon. Instead, each unoriented line has normal angle \(\theta\in[0,\pi)\) and signed offset \(p\in\mathbb R\), with kinematic measure \(d\theta\,dp\). Conditioning on intersecting \(K_n\) makes this an ordinary probability law of finite total mass. Tangent lines and all boundary cases have probability zero. Radii and distances are Euclidean.

## Proof

**Step 1: Crofton normalization.** For a convex body \(K\) containing the origin, let \(h_K(u)=\max_{x\in K}x\cdot u\) be its support function. For a normal \(u(\theta)\), an unoriented line with signed offset \(p\) intersects \(K\) exactly when \(-h_K(-u)\le p\le h_K(u)\). By integrating this width in \(\theta\), Cauchy's perimeter identity gives total kinematic mass \(P(K)=\int_0^{2\pi}h_K(\theta)\,d\theta\). If \(B_r\) is a concentric disk of radius \(r>0\), its line-hit interval is \([-r,r]\); because \(K_n\) contains the origin, the intersection interval has length \(\min\{r,h_K(u)\}+\min\{r,h_K(-u)\}\). Consequently
\[
F_n(r)=\frac{\int_0^{2\pi}\min\{r,h_{K_n}(\theta)\}\,d\theta}{P(K_n)}
\quad (r\ge0).
\]

**Step 2: Exact support integral.** In each angular Voronoi cell of a polygon vertex, \(-\beta_n\le t\le\beta_n\), the support is exactly \(h_{K_n}=R\cos t\). The cells tile the angle circle. The perimeter is \(P(K_n)=2nR\sin\beta_n\). When \(r\le R\cos\beta_n\), every support value exceeds \(r\) and the numerator is \(2\pi r\). When \(r\ge R\), every support value is at most \(r\), giving probability one. Between these radii write \(\alpha=\arccos(r/R)\), so \(0<\alpha<\beta_n\). Splitting one cell at \(\pm\alpha\) yields
\[
\int_0^{2\pi}\min\{r,h_{K_n}(\theta)\}\,d\theta
=2n\left[r\alpha+R(\sin\beta_n-\sin\alpha)\right].
\]
Division by \(2nR\sin\beta_n\) proves the displayed piecewise law, including its matching boundary values.

**Step 3: Strict nonnested ordering.** Fix \(\rho=r/R\in(0,1)\), let \(\alpha=\arccos\rho\), and regard the same formula as a function of real \(\beta\in(0,\pi/3]\). If \(\beta\le\alpha\), it equals \(\rho\beta/\sin\beta\), which is strictly increasing because \(\sin\beta-\beta\cos\beta>0\). If \(\beta>\alpha\), it equals \(1+(\rho\alpha-\sin\alpha)/\sin\beta\). Since \(\sin\alpha-\alpha\cos\alpha>0\), this is likewise strictly increasing in \(\beta\). The two expressions agree at \(\beta=\alpha\), so the increase is strict across branches. Since \(\beta_n=\pi/n\) strictly decreases with \(n\), the displayed ordering follows. As \(n\to\infty\), eventually the first branch applies and \(\beta_n/\sin\beta_n\to1\). This also proves the one-probability identification of \(n\).

**Step 4: Contact singularities.** On the first open branch, \(F_n'(r)=\beta_n/(R\sin\beta_n)\) and \(F_n''(r)=0\). On the second open branch, differentiating gives
\[
F_n'(r)=\frac{\arccos(r/R)}{R\sin\beta_n},\qquad
F_n''(r)=-\frac1{R^2\sin\beta_n\sqrt{1-(r/R)^2}}.
\]
At \(r=R\cos\beta_n\), first derivatives coincide and the stated one-sided second derivatives differ. At \(r=R\), both one-sided first derivatives vanish, and \(\sin\alpha-\alpha\cos\alpha=\alpha^3/3+O(\alpha^5)\), together with \(\alpha=\sqrt{2(1-r/R)}+O((1-r/R)^{3/2})\), gives the three-halves boundary expansion.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` checks the closed expression independently against 36 numerical support-function quadratures, five kinematic-line rejection-sampling tests (over 800,000 accepted lines), and the strict ordering for side counts \(3\) through \(100\) at six interior radii. The checker is supplementary: the above argument proves all claims for every integer side count, not just tested instances. Run with `python verify.py` using the Python standard library.

## Relationship to prior work

Harutyunyan and Ohanyan (2009) analyze the random *chord-length* distribution of regular polygons using invariant lines and Pleijel's identity. Bäsel (2012) supplies a different full derivation of that chord-length distribution and derives the distance law of two interior-uniform random points. The present observation conditions on the same invariant line model but measures *offset from the fixed polygon center*, rather than chord length or interior-point separation. Neither inspected full text states the exact concentric-disk dual-hit law, the strict side-count stochastic order across nonnested regular polygons, or the inradius and circumradius contact orders; standard Cauchy perimeter identities and the angular support profile are explicitly used here. The ordering is therefore not a consequence of nesting. Historical priority beyond the inspected sources and indexed records has not been independently certified.

## Limitations

The sampling law must be the motion-invariant Crofton measure on lines conditioned on hitting the polygon: independent boundary endpoints lead to a different distribution. The inversion identifies a discrete side count only when the circumradius and test radius are known, and assumes exact probabilities; no finite-sample robustness or noisy inversion is proved. The theorem does not compare general irregular or equal-area polygons, nor does it claim novelty for the underlying Cauchy-Crofton identity. It applies to full lines, not finite segments or chord-length observations.
