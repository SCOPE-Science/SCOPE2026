# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The domain is a unit-edge cube with a single Haar-normal determining both the plane and its projection width. The face-intersection criterion yields precisely the two combinatorial types, and a spherical-circle coarea parametrization gives the normalized width density. Disjoint positive-octant exclusions give the first branch; a squared-norm inequality excludes hexagons at small width; three disjoint maximal-coordinate arcs give the intermediate joint branch; their disappearance gives the upper phase. The threshold values follow from exact inequalities, not fitting. Independent edge-crossing enumeration and numerical quadrature test the formulas without replacing the proof. Possible limits: conditional probabilities at isolated widths are version-dependent.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** Lonke's 1998 cube-section calculation provides the marginal polygon-type probability; Finch's 2011 width study and the later Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets width moments treat the width marginal. Inspected public published-finding corpus results give, separately, an exact width density and an exact box hexagon probability. Neither inspected scientific statement evaluates the distribution of polygon type **conditional on width**, or gives the two width thresholds and monotone interpolation. Marginal data cannot determine a coupling; even knowing the deterministic geometric indicators, the exact conditional spherical-arc calculation is an additional result. The general rectangular-box study includes the cube and explicitly owns its unconditional type law, which is not claimed here as new. No universal claim of bibliographic uniqueness is made.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Coupled measurements of one random section answer a natural geometric question not resolved by either marginal: which section combinatorics are possible at a prescribed width? The theorem gives an exact classification of all width regimes, a strictly monotone conditional law in the mixed regime, and complete subdensities that can recover any joint probability. This is a substantive exact geometric dependence result, not another moment, restated marginal, or arbitrary parameter slice.
