# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement concerns exactly the nonvanishing-coordinate projective torus for every integer \(n\ge8\) and positive coefficient vector. The logarithmic Jacobian equations force alternating nonzero coefficients; parity and positivity yield \(4\mid n\) and the equal parity products. Conversely the parity-preserving two-step linear recurrence closes cyclically precisely under this balance. It parametrizes a projective line, not a nonlinear curve. The logarithmic Hessian at a singular point is an alternating-edge cycle matrix; its kernel is precisely the two-dimensional space of two-periodic vectors. Upon projectivization the normal Hessian has full rank \(n-2\), yielding the claimed analytic Morse–Bott node. The local Jacobian ideal is reduced by the analytic coordinate normal form. All implications are proved for all sizes; four exact-arithmetic checks are corroborative only.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification
The closest primary source is S. Telen, Toric Amplitudes and Universal Adjoints, arXiv:2504.00897, Theorem 7.11 and Example 7.12 (open-access 2026 JLMS version). It explicitly derives the necessary balanced coefficient condition and constructs a singular line in one balanced octagon, but its Theorem 7.11 is stated for non-balanced coefficients and its Example 7.12 does not compute the normal Hessian. The exact all-size full-torus scheme and the transverse analytic normal form are not stated there. The toric binomial equations in its proof already make sufficiency plausible, so that component is not treated as a conceptually independent discovery. The decisive new comparison is the uniform normal Hessian rank \(n-2\), reduced singular scheme, and corresponding analytic classification. K. Kohn and K. Ranestad, Projective Geometry of Wachspress Coordinates, arXiv:1904.02123, §§1–2 and the uniqueness theorem. addresses adjoint curves on the original polytope plane and Wachspress maps, not the ambient universal-adjoint hypersurface. Searches of relevant published claims found no equivalent transverse analytic classification. This is a best-knowledge novelty judgment, not a historical uniqueness certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exceptional parity-balance locus left open in the motivating polygonal criterion genuinely changes the full-torus singular geometry. The result classifies that change across infinitely many dimensions, for all positive coefficients, including actual regular-polygon fans, and separates set-theoretic existence of a singular line from its scheme and local analytic normal type. It shows that the full-torus singular line is a uniform transverse ordinary-double-point stratum rather than a more degenerate locus. This information is relevant to local deformation, transversal vanishing cycles, and stratified singularity geometry; no unproved downstream theorem is asserted.

## Closest literature and limitations
Theorem 7.11 of Telen is stronger on absence of singularities off the irrelevant locus in the nonbalanced range; the present theorem concerns only the all-coordinate-nonzero region, even at balance. Example 7.12 supplies a balanced octagon singular line but not its Hessian. The \(n=4\) quadrilateral case is already handled by Telen and is excluded here. Other singular components may lie on the coordinate boundary. The two endpoints of the balanced line are not covered by the transverse classification.
