For two forward-Euler substeps on any finite connected regular graph, the sharp endpoint-positivity superstep is determined only by the degree \(d\) and the minimum number \(t\) of triangles containing an edge: the largest normalized duration is \((2d-t)/(d-1-t)\) unless the graph is complete, when it is unbounded. At the finite optimum the two-step map becomes the exact stochastic kernel \((A^2-tA-dI)/(d(d-1-t))\), even though an intermediate Euler stage need not preserve nonnegativity.
