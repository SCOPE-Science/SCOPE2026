# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The four-by-four Boolean relations encode every possible cyclic predecessor through closed paths. The deterministic relation automata count each accepted output exactly once. Both complete finite relation graphs were reconstructed and all 117 scalar polynomial checks passed using exact integers. The Cayley–Hamilton deduction from the first 83 and 34 checks is valid for all larger ring lengths. The recurrences, initial values, rational functions, and Tribonacci–Lucas simplification are mutually consistent; separate direct enumeration checked rings through length 16. No infinite conclusion rests only on that enumeration.

## Originality

PASS, subject to historical priority risks. The closest full texts studied for Rule 172 are Fukś's original short-block preimage-tree and probability study and his later explicit multistep Cauchy solution. Neither inspected treatment states the all-length indexed cyclic one-step output count or parity equality with Rule 184. An accessible Rule-184 image theorem already determines that rule's count; it does not imply the independently established Rule-172 recurrence. A finite-ring Garden-of-Eden census addresses related data but does not itself establish the all-length identity. Searches for equivalent formulations, signed Tribonacci–Lucas forms, complete finite-ring enumerators, and the cross-rule parity relation did not locate a dominating result; unindexed or inaccessible literature may change priority.

## Value

PASS. The exact image size is a natural finite-ring information-loss invariant for a well-studied nonlinear selective-copier rule. The theorem is not a numerical extension of a finite table: it supplies a short all-length recurrence, an exact information rate, and a surprising parity equality with a different, nonidentical traffic-rule output language. The last point is not a formal consequence of either rule's known time-evolution formulas.

## Scope and risks

Only indexed cyclic rings and one update are covered. The method is an exact finite automaton plus Cayley–Hamilton certificate, not an elementary word-by-word bijection. Published Garden-of-Eden work and historical priority should be independently reviewed.
