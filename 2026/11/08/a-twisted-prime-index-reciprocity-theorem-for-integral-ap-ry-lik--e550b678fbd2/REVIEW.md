# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The reflection relation \(Q_r(-n-1)=(-1)^rQ_r(n)\), endpoint recurrence at index \(p-1\), and uniqueness of the reversed recurrence for \(1\le n\le p-2\) prove the coefficient identity with all boundary cases. Fermat's theorem at \(n=p-1\) proves that the endpoint is a unit. The equivalent reciprocal-polynomial relation and its root consequence follow coefficientwise. A standard-library program independently checks eleven sequences and 4,803 modular coefficient comparisons. Crucial assumption: the sequence is integer-valued.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Malik–Straub (2015, §6) explicitly identifies the zero-reflection phenomenon for four Almkvist–Zudilin sequences as an unproved empirical observation, although their Lemma 6.2 already proves the untwisted Apéry case. Earlier Jarvis–Verrill reflection identities cover particular quadratic sequences; no novelty is claimed for those specializations. The new general proof establishes a specific previously open observation and uniform twisted reciprocal polynomial identity. Searches and comparisons found no equivalent universal result in the inspected works of Malik–Straub and Gorodetsky, or in the relevant mathematical-results index. Historical priority is not guaranteed, and the recurrence argument may have appeared in other literature.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result converts a published congruence phenomenon from an empirical premise of a prime-divisibility heuristic into a rigorous theorem, uniformly over all parameters whose recurrences have the required reversal symmetry. The endpoint-unit and reciprocal-polynomial consequences provide more information than the mere zero-set observation. The \(d\ne0\) example marks an essential structural boundary.

The main limitations are restriction to integer-valued sequences, odd primes not dividing \(c\), and the absence of prime-power or prime-density claims. The earlier Apéry and selected quadratic cases are explicitly treated as prior mathematics.
