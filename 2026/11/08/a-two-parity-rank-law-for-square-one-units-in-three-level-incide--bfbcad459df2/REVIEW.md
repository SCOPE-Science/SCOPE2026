# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The incidence-algebra basis consists precisely of three diagonal antichain blocks and three complete cross-level blocks. In characteristic two, the diagonal of any square-one unit is one, and the only nonzero square of its radical part is \(AB\). Hence the square-one condition is exactly \(AB=0\), with \(C\) completely free. Standard Gaussian counts of rank-\(r\) square matrices and the cardinality of \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb F_q^n,\ker A)\) prove the exact formula. Its exponent is \(2n^2+r(n-r)\), with a uniformly bounded finite-product correction. A discrete Laplace sum and a full Gaussian-tail bound establish the sharp theta constant, parity-dependent total-variation limit, and vanishing middle homology. Exhaustive checks over characteristic-two fields independently match the counts for small degrees. The limits are mathematical deductions; finite tests do not replace proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked literature
The most directly comparable source is Catanzaro–Zabka, arXiv:1901.00964, whose full text supplies the traditional rank-matrix enumeration and a model of sequentially sampled random differentials. Their measure is different from the *uniform joint* measure on all pairs \(AB=0\); in particular the first map does not exhibit the same fixed-field half-rank distribution in their sequential model. Gargate–Gargate, arXiv:1907.06805, explicitly excludes characteristic two from its incidence-algebra involution counts, while the 2020 potent-algebra paper likewise excludes characteristic dividing the exponent. Ginzburg–Pasechnik (arXiv:1602.08538) treat ungraded square-zero matrices; Ababneh–Kahle (arXiv:2602.10783) treat real-field generic rank strata. Neither furnishes the parity-dependent uniform-pair theta factors in fixed characteristic two. The Gaussian rank formula itself is classical and is not claimed as new. Relevant general varieties-of-complexes literature remains a residual priority risk; similarity and failed searches are not uniqueness proofs.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exact enumeration of a natural order-two locus in the units of a finite incidence algebra, in precisely the characteristic excluded by earlier explicit involution enumeration. The rank spectrum has a sharp field-dependent two-parity limit and implies quantitative middle-homology vanishing for the natural uniform-joint finite-field complex. Its difference from established sequential sampling is mathematically consequential rather than a change of notation. The result does not purport to classify all posets or replace general finite-matrix counting theory.

## Limitations and references
Scope: three equally sized complete levels, characteristic two, fixed finite field in asymptotics. No global publication-priority certificate; distinct conditional versus uniform-joint sampling must be preserved in comparisons. Primary inspected literature: Catanzaro–Zabka (2019), Gargate–Gargate (2019, 2020); contextual full texts: Ginzburg–Pasechnik (2016), Ababneh–Kahle (2026).
