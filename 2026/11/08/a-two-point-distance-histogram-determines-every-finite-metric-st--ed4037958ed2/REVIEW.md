# Scientific review: metric-star pair-distance inverse theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The result assumes a finite, positively weighted metric star with independent uniform normalized arclength sampling. Directly partitioning ordered pairs into same-arm and different-arm pairs produces an explicitly normalized, continuous piecewise-affine probability density. Its value and right derivative at zero yield total length and arm number; the distributional second derivative on the positive half-line is a signed measure with negative atoms at arm lengths and positive atoms at pair sums. The proof of the greedy recovery identifies the smallest unrecovered length *after subtracting all already known self and pair contributions*, so it is not invalidated by repeated lengths or equalities between arm lengths and pair sums. The interval isometry ambiguity at one or two arms is unavoidable and fully included. Exact rational checking and independent simulation are supporting evidence, not a substitute for this general proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual historical risk
Goldsborough–Rautu–Römer (arXiv:1406.4079, 2014) derive discrete ordered leaf-to-leaf distance formulas on regular trees, not global distances between uniform points on arbitrary continuous star arms. Mémoli–Needham (arXiv:1810.09646, 2018), Section 4, demonstrate failure of general metric-tree identification and recover isometry from *local* distance-profile functions plus a continuous measure-preserving map, not from one globally unlabeled distance histogram. Baptista–Kennedy–Mugnolo (arXiv:2312.04952, 2023), Section 4, calculate only the mean distance on equilateral stars. Li–Özaydın (arXiv:2603.00914, 2026) construct persistence models for separated point configurations rather than the product-arclength pushforward distribution. These results do not logically imply the signed-kink inversion or its complete collision-safe classification. The closest additional research record on unordered separated-particle persistence was read and likewise concerns a different invariant. No reviewed covering result was found, but negative searches alone cannot establish historical priority; papers not retrieved remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A global pair-distance histogram is a weak and natural metric-measure invariant: nonisometric general metric trees can share even stronger local distance information. Establishing injectivity on *every finite metric star*, including all nongeneric collisions, is a substantive exact boundary. The reconstruction handles unknown valence and metric scale, identifies exactly the artificial degree-two ambiguity, and gives an implementable finite inversion based on finitely many kink locations and weights. It complements the known mean-distance theory without claiming statistical or noise stability.

## Limits and related literature
The statement concerns only stars, normalized arclength, exact real distances and a full distribution. General trees, nonuniform sampling, recovery from finitely many noisy samples, and classification across larger graph families are not treated. Historical priority is provisionally assessed, not independently validated. Source comparison includes the full HTML sections cited in the result and a related full research record on two-particle persistence.
