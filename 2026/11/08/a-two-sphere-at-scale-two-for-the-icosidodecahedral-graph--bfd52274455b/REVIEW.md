The object is the line graph of the explicit \(GP(10,2)\) graph, with graph shortest-path metric and inclusive scale convention. A complete enumerator constructs all 1022 nonempty simplices at scale two. The included 468-pair certificate is checked sequentially: each recorded lower simplex is contained in exactly one remaining upper simplex, so all deletions are genuine elementary collapses. The remaining 16-vertex complex has every edge in two triangles, single-cycle links at every vertex, connected one-skeleton and Euler characteristic two. It is therefore a triangulated \(S^2\), yielding the integral claim. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The icosidodecahedron is a canonical Archimedean polyhedron obtained by rectifying a Platonic solid. The finding determines a natural missing graph-metric topological invariant at its first thickening, gives the full integral homotopy type rather than an Euler or field-homology calculation, and exhibits a sharp contrast with the known Čech behavior on the same graph. The finite answer is a motivated structural test case for extensions of polyhedral Rips classifications, and the explicit free-face proof can be independently replayed. No claim is made of a general theorem for all rectified polyhedra.
