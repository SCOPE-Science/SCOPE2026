# A two-sphere reflection quotient of the twelve-vertex Möbius-ladder independence complex

## Finding
Let \(M_{12}\) be the graph on \(\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\), with edges between consecutive residues and between residues differing by six. Let \(X=\operatorname{Ind}(M_{12})\) be its ordinary independence complex, and let \(\sigma\) be the simplicial involution induced by \(\sigma(i)=-i\pmod{12}\). Then the **geometric** orbit space has the integral homotopy type
\[
\boxed{|X|/\langle\sigma\rangle\simeq S^2.}
\]
In particular, its fundamental group is trivial, its integral second homology is \(\mathbb Z\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. The result refers to the geometric quotient, not the Borel homotopy quotient or the quotient obtained by merely identifying original vertex orbits.

## Assumptions and scope
For a simple graph \(G\), \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) is the abstract simplicial complex whose faces are independent vertex sets; its realization is taken with the standard simplicial topology. All graph edges, reflections and vertex labels in the claim are explicitly defined above. The reflection fixes graph vertices 0 and 6 (as graph vertices, these are **not** in one independent pair), and hence fixes vertices of the independence complex. The proof invokes the independently published nonequivariant calculation \(|\operatorname{Ind}(M_{12})|\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^2\).

## Proof
**1. Input homotopy type and the fundamental group.** Agarwal and Basak's homotopy calculation for Möbius-ladder independence complexes gives \(|X|\simeq\bigvee^3 S^2\), in particular \(|X|\) is path-connected and simply connected. The finite group \(\langle\sigma\rangle\) is generated by an involution with a fixed point (the vertex labeled 0). Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem, applied to this finite simplicial action, gives \(\pi_1(|X|/\langle\sigma\rangle)=1\). This step is not an inference from the rational character of the action.

**2. A legitimate finite quotient complex.** A simplex of the barycentric subdivision \(\operatorname{sd}(X)\) is a nonempty strictly nested chain \(F_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_r\) of independent vertex subsets. Since all members have distinct cardinalities, if a group element sends such a chain to itself, it fixes every barycenter individually: the reflection action is without inversions on the barycentric subdivision. Consequently, the quotient has a regular cell decomposition with one \(r\)-cell for each reflection-orbit of \((r+1)\)-chains; each cell boundary is the orbit image of deleting one chain member. This is the correct orbit-cell construction; an unsafely identified original simplicial quotient is not used.

**3. Finite, exact elementary-collapse certificate.** Exhaustively enumerate all nonempty independent subsets (196) and all their strictly increasing chains (11,236). The involution has 5,646 chain orbits with orbit-cell numbers in dimensions 0 through 4 equal to
\[
(106,806,1998,2016,720).
\]
The companion `collapse_pairs.csv` lists exactly 2,768 pairs of orbit cells, using canonical lexicographic orbit indices. The standalone `verify.py` independently reconstructs all cells and the face relation, then checks for *each* pair that (i) the proposed lower cell is in the boundary of the upper cell exactly once, (ii) it has no other surviving immediate coface, and (iii) the upper cell has no surviving higher-dimensional coface. These are the conditions for an elementary collapse in this regular cell structure. The checker replays all pairs without trusting a summary log. Its final 110 cells comprise 20 vertices, 54 edges and 36 triangles; therefore the quotient collapses onto a connected two-dimensional CW complex \(Y\) of Euler characteristic
\[
\chi(Y)=20-54+36=2.
\]
Connectivity is independently checked on the remaining one-skeleton. This proves a genuine dimension-two *homotopy* reduction, not merely a calculation of field homology.

**4. Conclude the integral homotopy type.** The collapse preserves both fundamental group and Euler characteristic, so \(Y\) is a simply connected finite two-dimensional CW complex with \(\chi(Y)=2\). Since \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\) and \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\) is a subgroup of the free abelian cellular group \(C_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\), Euler characteristic forces \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). Hurewicz supplies a homotopy class representing a generator, hence a map \(S^2\to Y\) inducing an isomorphism on all integral homology. Since both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, the homological Whitehead theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence. The asserted \(S^2\) type follows.

The exact output in `verification_output.txt` concludes `ALL_VERIFY_OK`; the latest replay checked every collapse and the final cell counts. The certificate contains all 2,768 ordered integer-index pairs, and the cell-numbering algorithm is fully specified by the executable. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v1 (first public on 2026-08-31), prove that the unquotiented independence complex is a wedge of three two-spheres. Their theorem does not determine this reflection quotient. The existing calculation *Dihedral rational homology representations of Möbius-ladder independence complexes* determines the reflection trace on rational \(H_2\): the trace is \(-1\), predicting only that the quotient's rational second Betti number is one. Such rational information does not imply simply connectivity, integral torsion-freeness, or the sphere homotopy type. Pal and Singh's classification of orbit-space mod-two cohomology for **free** involutions of three-sphere wedges does not apply: \(\sigma\) fixes vertices here. The present result adds the complete integral homotopy type for the first three-summand Möbius-ladder case.

## Limitations
The conclusion concerns the specific reflection \(i\mapsto-i\) of the twelve-vertex Möbius ladder. It does not classify other reflections, larger ladders, all dihedral subgroups, or equivariant homotopy types. The nonequivariant wedge classification is an acknowledged published premise. The finite collapse is exact and exhaustive for this graph but is not a proof of any general-parameter claim. Possible unindexed prior equivalent quotient calculations remain a priority risk.
