# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof covers all integers \(m\ge3\) and all fixed \(B,c>0\) with independent nonnegative edge weights. Strict convexity on connected Laplacians proves a unique unrestricted optimizer; automorphisms force equality within rung and rail classes. Both classes must retain positive weights because either zero class disconnects the graph. Exact tensor-product eigenvalues and the budget give the stated rational first-order equation; strict convexity along its linear budget coordinate gives exactly one positive root. The resolvent Fourier identity and a compactness/uniform-asymptotic argument establish the noninteger \(m^{-2/3}\) law and the minimum-value coefficient. Direct rational Laplacian inversions and resolvent identities reproduce the finite cases. The verifier's finite cases do not replace the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with publication-priority risk

The closest sourced claims are: Ghosh–Boyd–Saberi's general weighted resistance-design convexity and unpriced graph examples (DOI 10.1137/050645452); Cinkir's unweighted prism Kirchhoff index (arXiv:1704.03429); Carmona–Encinas–Mitjana's Green function and resistance formulas for weighted *linear* ladder-like chains (DOI 10.1002/qua.24740); and Zhang–Yang's circulant resistance formula (DOI 10.1002/qua.21068). These results provide methods, fixed-weight values, or a different graph family, but the inspected statements do not imply the unique arbitrary-edge costed prism optimizer or its fractional-power rung scaling. A public result on finite-volume weighted-ladder spanning-tree kernels concerns a different invariant and an open ladder, and does not assert this optimal-allocation claim. Multiple searches for priced-prism allocation, equivalent Laplacian-pseudoinverse trace optimization, and the fractional exponent found no direct coverage. The possibility of an unindexed or differently formulated equivalent result remains.

## Value — PASS

A cyclic two-layer network requires both edge types for connectivity, so the typical edge-deletion threshold picture does not apply. The theorem instead explains how optimal interlayer investment vanishes continuously as the system grows. Its sharp exponent \(2/3\), prefactor, and next-order objective are actionable network-design asymptotics and distinguish the finite-size optimal allocation from a fixed-ratio design. This is not just evaluation of a pre-existing unit-weight Kirchhoff-index table.

## Scope and limitations

The theorem uses positive fixed price ratio \(c\), fixed budget \(B\), the unnormalized Laplacian, and circular prisms \(C_m\square K_2\). It does not claim independent external verification, variable prices, a growing price ratio, or general ladder-optimality. The 2006 conference predecessor to the resistance-design framework has no exact public-release day established in the inspected evidence; the earliest day-verified source used for this topic is Zhang–Yang's September 5, 2006 online record. Numerical checks are supplementary. Independent literature priority cannot be guaranteed.
