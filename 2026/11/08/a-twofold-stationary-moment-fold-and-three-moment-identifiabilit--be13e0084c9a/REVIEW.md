# Scientific scope assessment

## Correctness

The forward moments follow from bounded generator identities. Fixing the variance reduces the rates to one open interval. For a distinct pair in that interval, equal fourth and sixth normalized moments would force a unique unordered pair containing the excluded infinite-tumble-rate boundary. Thus no distinct finite positive pair has all three moments equal. The example and exact checks illustrate this all-rate proof.

## Relationship to earlier results

The finite-rate two-moment fold and its complete multiplicity classification are already established in *An exact two-moment inverse fold in a trapped run-and-tumble particle*, 2026-11-02, linked in `RESULT.md`. That earlier finding supplies an example where a sixth moment separates a pair, and explicitly does not claim that it always does. The present statement is limited to global three-moment uniqueness; the fold is prior context. The forward moments themselves are also known. A broader priority claim is not made.

## Mathematical significance and limits

An exact ambiguity from two natural observables motivates asking if one more observable always suffices. The theorem gives a constructive, globally unique selection whenever the data are compatible, but no guarantee about numerical stability, statistical inference, or zero or infinite rates.
