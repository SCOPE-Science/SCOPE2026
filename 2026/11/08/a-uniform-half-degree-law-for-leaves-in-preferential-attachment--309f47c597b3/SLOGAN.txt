In the classical degree-proportional preferential-attachment tree, every vertex's number of leaf neighbours differs from half its degree by at most \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{1/4}(\log n)^2)\), simultaneously over the entire growing tree. Consequently the maximum leafdegree divided by the maximum ordinary degree converges in probability to \(1/2\), proving an extremal-ratio conjecture from the leafdegree literature.
