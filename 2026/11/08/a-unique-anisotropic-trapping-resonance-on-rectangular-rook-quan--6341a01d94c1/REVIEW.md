# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The rectangular principal claim is a consequence of a proven bright/dark reduction and four explicit orthogonal tensor spectral projectors. The possible eigenvalue collisions are exhaustively enumerated for positive hopping strengths; only the middle two collide, exactly when \(am=bn\). Their merged projection yields a sum-of-squares loss formula and all displayed source-orbit entries. Exact labeled-Hamiltonian Krylov projections and long-time non-Hermitian evolution independently check small instances, but the unbounded and continuous claims rely on the written proof. The rate \(\kappa>0\), source classes, and singular endpoints are explicitly specified.

**Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with residual priority risk.** The 2008 dephasing-assisted transport article motivates the model but has no weighted rook-family single-trap classification. The 2009 work develops the relevant invariant-subspace principle for general networks and applies it particularly to complete graphs; the new formulas do not claim invention of that principle. The full 2018 dark-state study considers complete networks and path–cycle cylinders, not a two-clique Cartesian product with independently tunable row and column rates. The 2020/2021 Razzoli–Paris–Bordone article provides a general transport procedure and **explicitly covers the isotropic square rook case** through its strongly regular graph theorem (Equation (25)): that specialization is acknowledged as known and is not part of the novel assertion. Rectangular rook graphs are not strongly regular for unequal side lengths; this theorem does not imply their tuned anisotropic collision or the exact nonneighbor deficit. Nearby published findings on all-state detection in one fixed rook graph and on two-detector cyclic ladders have different sink counts and observables. No examined statement implies the precise rectangular all-size table or its isolated anisotropic ratio; however, indexed coverage cannot establish absolute historical priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The Cartesian product of two all-to-all communities is a canonical finite network with only two independent physical hopping channels. The exact classification identifies a genuine spectral-collision boundary: for unequal side lengths the precisely tuned anisotropic resonance suppresses capture from every nontrap orbit, including nonneighbors, with a shape-dependent strictly positive loss that is exactly zero in the previously known square case. The result isolates directional geometry and interference, quantifies a discontinuous control effect, and provides a finite-size benchmark for studying nearly dark modes. This is a natural structural claim rather than a selected numerical instance.

Closest published sources: Plenio–Huelga (2008), Caruso et al. (2009), Le et al. (2018), and Razzoli–Paris–Bordone (2021). Remaining limitations: infinite-time, one trap, one-excitation, positive rates, no dephasing, and historical-priority uncertainty. The proof itself is self-contained once the model is defined.
