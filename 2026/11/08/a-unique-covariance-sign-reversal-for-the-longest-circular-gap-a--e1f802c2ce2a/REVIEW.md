# Scientific review: maximal circular gap and long-gap count

**Correctness (PASS).** The claimed covariance follows from an explicit simplex probability identity and a layer-cake integral. The single-crossing theorem relies on the convexity of the conditional maximal-gap mean, the positive deviations from the unconditional mean at both endpoints, and a sign-controlled derivative. The strict semicircle bound for sample sizes at least three follows because at a half-circumference threshold the exceedance count is exactly the indicator of an upper tail of the maximum. These arguments cover every integer sample size at least two. The companion verifier passes 2,635 exact rational comparisons and 48 independent numerical quadrature comparisons. The computations corroborate, but do not replace, the proof.

**Originality (PASS, subject to priority risk).** Holst (1980) proves the broken-stick spacing distribution and moments; Schlemm (2014, arXiv:1405.6114v1) proves moment identities for the maximum. Neither inspected full text asserts the finite sample-size universal one-sign-change law for the covariance with a threshold exceedance count. Hüsler (1982) and Holst–Hüsler (1984) concern the joint asymptotic circle-coverage problem; their abstracts were inspected, but not their complete articles. In particular the inaccessible 1984 source is a genuine historical-priority risk. The closed covariance is an application of classical inclusion-exclusion and is not independently represented as a new foundational distribution formula. The unique all-size crossing theorem is the assessed contribution.

**Value (PASS).** Maximum gap and the number of gaps above the arc length are two directly motivated, distinct circle-coverage observables: complete-covering failure is determined by the first, while the second counts remaining holes. Their covariance is not universally positive merely because both measure incomplete coverage. A complete, sharp sign classification for every number of points identifies when their linear association reverses and demonstrates that negative dependence at small thresholds and positive dependence at large thresholds are both intrinsic.

**Remaining limitations.** No analogous theorem is proved for nonuniform spacings. No closed-form general threshold or threshold asymptotic is provided. Relevant inaccessible historical full texts leave a nonzero priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been carried out.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
