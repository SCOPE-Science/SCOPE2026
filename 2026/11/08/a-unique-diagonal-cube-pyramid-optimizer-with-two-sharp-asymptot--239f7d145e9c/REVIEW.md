# Scientific assessment: diagonal cube-pyramid optimum

## Correctness

PASS. The coarea identity gives precisely \(V_n(s)=s f_n(s)/n\). Convolution log-concavity of the uniform density makes \(\log(s f_n(s))\) strictly concave, and symmetry at the center with vanishing density at the cube corner proves one interior optimum for every \(n\ge3\). The Fourier expansion is differentiated with a controlled uniform remainder on bounded offsets; its coefficients produce the optimizer shift, relative volume correction and absolute-volume correction. The embedded numerical verifier successfully performed 672 corroborating checks, not replacing the infinite proof.

PASS on the accessible primary literature and scientific-record comparisons, subject to historical-priority uncertainty. Nikolov's 2026 paper establishes the section-volume expression, the global dimension-three value, inequalities for global maxima and the leading central asymptotic, but does not give the unique diagonal-family optimum with its off-center \(1/6\) shift or its second-order volume correction. Ambrus–Gárgyán (2025) explicitly report the two central Laplace–Pólya asymptotic coefficients already known in the literature; these are not counted as original, and their fixed-center claims do not imply the shifted optimizer or its gain. The closest related published geometric records treat capped-cube ellipsoids, central section width, or unrelated tetrahedron volume spectra and do not imply the stated asymptotics. ## Mathematical value

PASS. The problem of maximizing the volume of a cube-inscribed pyramid over arbitrary directions is open in general dimensions, while a diagonal section provides an explicit geometrically natural lower-bound family. The theorem completely solves its free-offset problem and quantifies the strict improvement over the already-known central benchmark by two sharp asymptotic orders, including a universal noncentral displacement.

## Limitations

The result neither proves global optimality among all hyperplane orientations nor estimates finite-dimensional remainder constants. The dimension-three global optimum and the classical density formula are credited as prior results. Historical priority and independent verification remain open.
