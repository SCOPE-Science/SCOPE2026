# Review of the lower fourth-moment resonance theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The exact Fourier coefficient identity follows by classifying all fourth-order zero sums among \(\{\pm m,\pm n\}\). Away from \(n=3m\), only paired cancellations occur. At the resonance, the extra coefficient is exactly \(\operatorname{Re}(u^3\overline v)/2\). Dividing by the squared second moment gives the normalized rational function. Its stationary cubic has exactly two positive roots; the smaller is the unique global minimizer. The stated algebraic stability factorization was checked symbolically by coefficient comparison in `checker.py`, with a positive quadratic and an independent nonnegative phase term. Boundaries, all phase choices, and sharp equality conditions are explicitly covered. Numerical quadrature is supplementary, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with stated risk
Targeted bibliographic searches and published-result comparisons used the exact harmonic ratio, algebraic endpoint, reverse norm, frequency-pair equivalence, resonant phase, and general-four-mode aliases. Neuwirth's 2007 full text concerns \(L^\infty\) rather than this lower \(L^4\) norm. Schmidt's 2013 full text concerns asymptotic small fourth moments for fixed unimodular analytic sequences, not a freely weighted real two-harmonic space. Defant–Mastyło's 2015 full text establishes upper polynomial norm inequalities, not this sharp lower pairwise classification. The closely related published four-consecutive-character theorem was read in full: its upper extremal constant does apply to the resonant pair after modulation, but does **not** imply the lower extremal constant or its unique exceptional-frequency and minimizer classification. The three-frequency phase-response theorem was also read in full and has distinct unimodular and analytic assumptions. Unindexed earlier work on flat real waveforms or short-correlation optimization remains a residual originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete sharp classification across *every* pair of distinct positive integer Fourier frequencies, not a handpicked coefficient or numerical example. The isolated three-to-one additive resonance changes the attainable fourth-moment geometry, creating a sharp value strictly below the single-harmonic baseline that every other frequency pair retains. The exact algebraic minimum, all minimizing phases and amplitude ratios, and a positive deficit identity yield a reproducible reference for fourth-moment flattening and inverse norm equivalence in low-dimensional real \(L^p\) subspaces. No extrapolation to higher-dimensional spectra is asserted.
