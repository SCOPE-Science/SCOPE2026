# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact mixed moment follows by conditioning on one isolated vertex, leaving a uniformly sampled fixed-edge graph on \(n-1\) vertices. The ratio of motif probabilities gives the displayed falling-factorial formula. A positive decreasing product proves one-switch behavior for noncomplete motifs; a two-term inclusion–exclusion bound proves a positive initial term for \(n\ge2k-2\). Finite-product expansions give the density boundary. The standalone exact checker passed 5,483 assertions including direct enumeration of all graphs on at most six vertices for specified connected motifs. Exhaustive finite checks corroborate but do not replace the proof. Boundary cases and the distinction between normalized sign and covariance magnitude are explicit.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Direct searches for fixed-edge induced-motif/isolation covariance, normalized conditional-isolation ratios, tree density \(1/k\), and corresponding aliases did not identify a prior exact formula or complete sign classification. The closest inspected full texts establish edge/triangle normal approximation (Reinert–Röllin), directed reachability correlations in \(G(n,m)\) (Alm–Janson–Linusson), or local limit laws for induced-subgraph counts (Sah–Sawhney), not the joint isolation covariance. A related exact tree-**component** covariance law also exhibits one-switch behavior but excludes all edges from the component to its exterior; induced copies allow them and this prior statement does not imply the present formula. Broad hypergeometric overlap identities may permit an unstated derivation. No independent historical priority claim is made.

**Value — PASS.** The question is motivated by the distinction between clustering and isolated vertices when total edges are fixed. The exact result is a shape-independent classification across **every** connected induced motif, with a finite global sign-switch condition and explicit asymptotic density \(p_*=(2h-k)/[k(k-2)]\); the tree specialization is \(1/k\). In stark contrast the independent-edge model has strictly negative covariance. These conclusions give a structural boundary rather than a numerical example or mere first-moment restatement.

The primary residual risk is an older general joint-moment formula for fixed-edge random graphs that could imply the statement without matching terminology. The density threshold is qualitative: at constant density isolation becomes rare, and no claim of macroscopic covariance is made.
