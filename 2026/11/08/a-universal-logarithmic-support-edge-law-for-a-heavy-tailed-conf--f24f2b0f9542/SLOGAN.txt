For a harmonically trapped run-and-tumble particle with regularly varying, finite-mean run durations, the stationary probability of lying within \(\varepsilon\) of the propulsion-supported edge is asymptotically one half of the equilibrium residual-run tail at time \(\kappa^{-1}\log(2B/\varepsilon)\). The half-\(t\) waiting-time law consequently produces the sharp inverse-logarithmic edge mass \(\kappa\tau/[4\log(2B/\varepsilon)]\), allowing the hidden run-time tail exponent to be read from spatial data.
