# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite-dimensional two-boson no-jump generator is explicitly defined with strictly positive hopping and strictly positive site-resolved pair-loss coefficients. The imaginary-part identity shows that undamped states are exactly Hermitian eigenvectors vanishing on all collision configurations; all other eigenvalues have strictly negative imaginary part. In the normalized symmetric basis, each leaf-doublon eigen-equation forces the hub–leaf coefficient to vanish. The remaining off-diagonal leaf-pair amplitudes must then lie at energy zero, and their equations are exactly the weighted signless-incidence kernel \(Bc=0\). A triangle among any three leaf indices proves \(B\) has full row rank for every positive weight vector, and \(I-B^{\mathsf T}(BB^{\mathsf T})^{-1}B\) is consequently the complete orthogonal dark projector. The uniform-coupling formula follows by exact inversion. No finite experiment is used to infer the all-size theorem.

An independently constructed exact integer-coordinate two-body walk verifies the full collision-observability nullity and the weighted-projector identities in thirteen test cases, including a two-leaf boundary check and three-through-eight-leaf instances. The all-size proof covers every \(q\ge3\).

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, provisional
Krovi and Brun (2006) establish the general dark-projector mechanism, not the collision-conditioned weighted-star solution. Gamble et al. (2010) establish bosonic two-body walk and on-site collision modeling, not this non-Hermitian all-site collision-loss classification. Gräfe et al. (2013) treat two-particle correlations under independent one-particle lattice losses; their loss is not conditional on both bosons sharing a vertex. Qin et al. (2014) analyze coherent two-particle lattice interactions. Le et al. (2017) analyze dark states on single-excitation networks. An earlier separate study of the all-vertex collision-loss problem on cycles does not imply the weighted-star incidence rank or its leaf-specific capture formula. A later two-exciton star study (Pouthier 2022) uses attractive coherent binding, not the stated dissipative collision model; only its abstract was accessible. The original element under examination is the first-protected-leaf threshold and full weighted projector, not the general dark-state principle or bosonic Fock-basis equations.

Searches compared equivalence of contact loss, doublon decay, non-Hermitian two-body Hubbard walks, star-graph trapping, and stronger arbitrary-graph coverage. No exact covering statement was found in the inspected material. Unindexed or inaccessible equivalent prior evaluations remain possible. These are limits to historical-priority confidence, not substituted novelty proofs.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Pair collisions in coherently propagating bosons are a natural many-particle loss channel. This result gives a sharp connectivity threshold — three leaves have no persistent state, while four leaves have two — and resolves an infinite family for every positive hopping and loss-rate choice. It computes the entire arbitrary-initial-state capture functional, not merely one eigenmode. A localized two-leaf input on a uniformly coupled high-degree star has loss probability \(2/(q-1)\), going to zero even though every double occupancy is lossy; thus branching protects collision avoidance through interference. The exact weighted formula is useful for comparing asymmetric network designs. The result is not a general claim about interacting Bose–Hubbard models or time-dependent transfer rates.
