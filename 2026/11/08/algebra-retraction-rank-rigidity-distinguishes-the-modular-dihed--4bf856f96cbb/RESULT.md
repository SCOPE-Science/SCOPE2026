# Algebra-retraction rank rigidity distinguishes the modular dihedral and quaternion group algebras of order eight

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) have characteristic two, with \(q=2^e\) and \(e\ge1\). Let
\[
D_8=\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle,
\qquad
Q_8=\langle r,s\mid r^4=1,\ r^2=s^2,\ srs^{-1}=r^{-1}\rangle.
\]
For \(A_G=k[G]\), denote by \(N_G(d)\) the number of unital \(k\)-algebra maps \(P:A_G\to A_G\) satisfying \(P^2=P\) and \(\dim_k\operatorname{im}P=d\). Then
\[
\sum_d N_{D_8}(d)z^d=1+2q^5z^2+z^8,
\qquad
\sum_d N_{Q_8}(d)z^d=1+z^8.
\]
The rank-one map in each case is augmentation followed by the scalar inclusion; the rank-eight map is the identity. Every other map for the dihedral algebra has two-dimensional image isomorphic to \(k[t]/(t^2)\). The dihedral algebra has exactly \(2q^4\) different embedded two-dimensional retract images, each with exactly \(q\) algebra retractions onto it. The quaternion algebra has no proper nontrivial unital algebra retract. The existence/rigidity assertions extend without change to arbitrary characteristic-two fields.

## Assumptions and scope

A group algebra is an associative, unital algebra. Endomorphisms here preserve both the unit and the coefficient field. The operator rank is the vector-space dimension of the image, not the rank of the corresponding group endomorphism. In particular, the only idempotent elements of each local algebra \(A_G\) are zero and one, but this has no direct bearing on idempotent **endomorphisms**. The groups have order eight, and the finite-field parameter \(q\) varies through all powers of two.

Write \(z_0=r^2\) (the unique nonidentity central involution), \(c=1+z_0\), and \(C=cA_G\). The four elements \(c,cr,cs,crs\) form a basis of \(C\); it is a central square-zero ideal, because \(c^2=0\) and commutators in either group algebra lie in \(cA_G\). The augmentation ideal \(J\) is the Jacobson radical because the group is a finite two-group in characteristic two. The radical is generated by \(r-1,s-1\), and \(\dim_k J/J^2=2\). Moreover \(C\subseteq J^2\), since \(c=(r+1)^2\).

## Proof

**Step 1 (square-zero cone, established here as a lemma).** A radical element has a unique expression
\[
w=a+br+ds+frs+cu,
\qquad a+b+d+f=0,
\quad u\in\operatorname{span}_k\{1,r,s,rs\}.
\]
Here \(u\) is unique because the eight displayed group basis elements occur in two disjoint coset layers. The central ideal \(C\) annihilates every commutator and satisfies \(C^2=0\). In the dihedral case, expansion using the group relations gives
\[
w^2=c\bigl(b^2+b d\,rs+b f\,s+d f\,r\bigr).
\]
In the quaternion case the corresponding identity is
\[
w^2=c\bigl(b^2+d^2+f^2+b d\,rs+b f\,s+d f\,r\bigr).
\]
As \(c,cr,cs,crs\) are independent and the coefficient field has no zero divisors, these equations yield, respectively,
\[
\{w\in J:w^2=0\}=
C\ \cup\ \bigl(k(1+s)+C\bigr)\ \cup\ \bigl(k(1+rs)+C\bigr)
\quad\text{for }D_8,
\]
with the union of two five-dimensional vector spaces meeting in \(C\); and
\[
\{w\in J:w^2=0\}=C
\quad\text{for }Q_8.
\]
Indeed the dihedral equations imply \(b=0\) and \(df=0\); the quaternion equations imply \(bd=bf=df=0\), then \(b^2+d^2+f^2=0\), so \(b=d=f=0\). No field-size assumption is used in this step beyond characteristic two.

**Step 2 (possible retract dimensions).** Let \(P:A_G\to A_G\) be an idempotent unital algebra endomorphism, and set \(B=P(A_G)\). Its induced map on \(J/J^2\) is an idempotent linear map. Since \(P(J)=B\cap J\) and \(P(J^2)=P(J)^2\), the cotangent image is naturally the cotangent space of the local image algebra \(B\). It has dimension zero, one or two. Dimension zero forces \(P(J)=0\) by Nakayama's lemma and hence \(P\) equals augmentation. Dimension two makes \(P\) surjective by Nakayama; an invertible idempotent is the identity.

If the dimension is one, the image algebra is generated by one radical element, hence is commutative. Put \(a=P(r)\), \(b_0=P(s)\). In the dihedral case the relation \(b_0ab_0^{-1}=a^{-1}\) implies \(a=a^{-1}\) inside the commutative image, so \(a^2=b_0^2=1\). In the quaternion case the same argument gives \(a^2=1\), and the defining relation \(b_0^2=a^2\) also gives \(b_0^2=1\). Thus in either case the image is generated by square-zero elements \(a+1,b_0+1\), with at least one having a nonzero class in \(J/J^2\).

For the quaternion algebra, Step 1 rules this out, because all radical square-zero elements lie in \(C\subseteq J^2\). Therefore only augmentation and identity remain. For the dihedral algebra, either \(a+1\) or \(b_0+1\) generates a square-zero radical algebra, so every rank-one-cotangent image is exactly \(k\oplus kw\), with \(w^2=0\) and nonzero cotangent class. It has vector-space dimension two.

**Step 3 (counting dihedral retractions).** Every such embedded two-dimensional image \(k\oplus kw\) is uniquely represented by a radical square-zero element \(w\) whose cotangent class equals either \(1+s+J^2\) or \(1+rs+J^2\). By Step 1, each choice has precisely \(q^4\) representatives (a freely chosen \(C\)-component). They represent different images: normalizing the tangent coefficient to one removes the only possible change of generator in a two-dimensional local algebra. Thus there are \(2q^4\) embedded images.

For a fixed \(w\), every homomorphism into \(k\oplus kw\) has the form \(r\mapsto1+\alpha w,\ s\mapsto1+\beta w\) for unique \(\alpha,\beta\in k\), and automatically satisfies the dihedral group relations. The map is a retraction onto \(k\oplus kw\) exactly if it sends \(w\) to itself. For the first normalized tangent direction this means \(\beta=1\); for the second this means \(\alpha+\beta=1\). Each linear equation has exactly \(q\) solutions. Hence there are \(2q^4q=2q^5\) rank-two maps, as asserted.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` performs a direct group-table construction of both group algebras over \(\mathbb F_2\). It enumerates all augmentation-one pairs as candidate generator images; tests all defining group relations; evaluates the endomorphism twice on the original generators; and computes the image-space rank by binary Gaussian elimination. It obtains exactly \((N_{D_8}(1),N_{D_8}(2),N_{D_8}(8))=(1,64,1)\) and \((N_{Q_8}(1),N_{Q_8}(8))=(1,1)\). Separately, over both \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_4\), it exhaustively tests every element of the augmentation ideal for the square-zero condition, confirming the structural lemma. All finite-field arithmetic, including \(\mathbb F_4\), is implemented explicitly without external packages; execution prints `VERIFY_OK`. This finite evidence supplements, but does not replace, the arbitrary-field proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Creedon, Hughes, and Szabo (first online January 19, 2021) establish and compare structural ring-theoretic properties of these closely related characteristic-two group algebras, including their nonisomorphism. The present result concerns **idempotent algebra endomorphisms** and embedded retract images, not the ordinary idempotent *elements* of either algebra. The direct square-zero calculations are not claimed as a new invariant in isolation; their use to classify and count algebra retractions is the claimed contribution. Dai and Li consider primitive decompositions of algebra elements in **semisimple complex** group algebras, so their theorems do not directly enumerate these modular algebra maps. Existing group-algebra square-zero structural descriptions constrain this calculation but do not by themselves enumerate the retraction maps. See the scientific comparison and residual source-access limits in the accompanying review.

## Limitations

The exact finite counts concern only order-eight dihedral and quaternion group algebras, and only characteristic two. They neither classify all algebra homomorphisms nor extend by themselves to higher-order generalized quaternion or dihedral groups. General uniqueness and publication priority beyond inspected sources are not asserted. The most directly comparable 2021 article could be inspected only through its accessible publisher preview, not a complete primary full text. The proof itself is self-contained, including the multiplication identities; the verifier checks only finite fields of order two and four.
