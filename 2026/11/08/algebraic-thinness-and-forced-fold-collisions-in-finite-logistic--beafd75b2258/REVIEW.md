# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The collision argument uses the shared fibers of the proportional logistic maps and cardinality of injective images of a finite set. Equality-invariance forces an interior point, whose separate finite orbits under the generators yield two polynomial equations. Dividing by endpoint factors avoids a spurious universal common root. The parameter value two proves the elimination resultants are genuine nonzero polynomials; degrees of the iterates give an explicit finite vertical-section bound. Algebraic zero sets deliver the measure and category conclusions. Exact code checks low-degree identities and two edge examples. No finite computation is presented as the all-size proof.

## Originality
PASS within inspected literature, with residual priority uncertainty. The 2026 logistic-IFS study provides explicit small toss-and-catch cases and their special parameter relations, not the all-cardinality algebraic-locus theorem or the quantitative finite partner-section bound. The earlier Abbasi–Gharaei–Homburg logistic-IFS work addresses synchronization and intermittency, with an explicit two-point finite carrier. Its full paper was not accessible in this review, so the possibility of uninspected overlapping general results remains a priority risk. Searches for finite invariant sets, common preperiodic logistic points, fiber collisions and generic parameter exclusion revealed no logically dominating statement. The result does not claim the earlier examples or the general polynomial-root method as new.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The source's finite examples and parameter curves motivate the open-ended question whether finite switching structures are exceptional in parameter space. The theorem answers that question uniformly for every finite cardinality, provides a forced fold collision for all finite invariant supports, and supplies a constructive elimination filter and a finite bound on admissible partner parameters for each cardinality. This is a structural obstruction of direct relevance to searching for finite toss-and-catch sets, rather than an arbitrary small instance. The estimate is coarse and does not enumerate actual exceptional sets.
