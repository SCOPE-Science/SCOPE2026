# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For a putative twisted cube of root length \(m\), the two shifted block equalities are equivalent to a run of \(2m\) equal shifted digit-sum differences. Removing the largest power of two dividing \(m\) leaves at least \(2u-1\) consecutive constraints on its odd part \(u\). Two full even–odd pairs force incompatible adjacent evenness when \(u\ge3\), proving the root is dyadic. For dyadic roots the complete start-position list follows from the binary carry identity and a two/three-quotient analysis. The earliest point and infinite existence follow from the same exact classification.

An independent morphic-prefix construction and a direct block comparison corroborated all 3,932,160 tested parameter instances. No finite experiment is treated as proof of the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS, qualified by priority uncertainty

Abdullah–Hamoud (2025), arXiv:2505.11403v1, explicitly excludes the cyclic-step twist \(\delta=\sigma\) from its stated avoidance theorem and identifies that case as further work. This result classifies precisely that exceptional case. Blondin-Massé–Brlek–Glen–Labbé (2007), arXiv:0710.4031v1, analyze ordinary fractional powers and positions of ordinary critical factors. Their results do not assert or imply a twisted three-block classification. Allouche–Shallit (2000) similarly addresses ordinary repetitions and palindromes.

Research-index searches for the exceptional twist, dyadic roots, and binary-valuation positions found no implication-equivalent indexed statement; this is not a uniqueness proof. An equivalent unindexed construction or result in a more general theory remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The excluded cyclic twist was identified as a natural missing boundary case in a recent study. The theorem does not merely construct a single exception: it characterizes exactly which of infinitely many candidate root lengths work and gives all occurrences with an elementary computable certificate. The dyadic rigidity and explicit valuation windows explain the combinatorial interaction between letter shifts and binary carries, making the result useful for studying twisted-repetition avoidance and automatic-word factor languages.

## Limits and risks

The scope is binary digit sums, cyclic single-step twists, three consecutive blocks, and alphabet sizes at least three. General bases, different twist steps, and longer twisted powers are not addressed. Independent validation and definitive priority confirmation remain outstanding.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
