# All inclusion-minimal total dominating sets of two-subset Kneser graphs

## Finding
Let \(KG(n,2)\) have the two-element subsets of \([n]\) as vertices, with adjacency when subsets are disjoint. For every \(n\ge4\), every inclusion-minimal total dominating vertex set corresponds to exactly one of four selected-edge subgraphs of the complete graph on \([n]\), ignoring isolated vertices: \(3K_2\), \(K_3\sqcup K_2\), \(C_5\), and \(K_4\). Each of these four families occurs whenever its support fits in \([n]\).

Their complete cardinality enumerator is
\[
M_n(z)=15\binom{n}{6}z^3+10\binom{n}{5}z^4+12\binom{n}{5}z^5+\binom{n}{4}z^6.
\]
Here \(\binom{n}{k}=0\) whenever \(k>n\). The available sizes are \(\{6\}\) for \(n=4\), \(\{4,5,6\}\) for \(n=5\), and \(\{3,4,5,6\}\) for \(n\ge6\). The largest cardinality of an inclusion-minimal total dominating set is \(6\) for every \(n\ge4\).

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are simple and undirected. A *total dominating set* \(S\) is a set of vertices for which every vertex, including those in \(S\), has a neighbor in \(S\); *inclusion-minimal* means no proper subset of \(S\) is total dominating. The corresponding auxiliary edge graph \(H=([n],S)\) contains an edge \(ij\) for each selected Kneser vertex \(\{i,j\}\). Its vertex-cover number \(\tau(H)\) is the least number of original labels incident to every selected edge. Isolated vertices in \(H\) do not affect \(\tau(H)\). There are no further restrictions on \(n\) beyond \(n\ge4\).

## Proof
**Step 1: exact translation.** A Kneser vertex \(\{a,b\}\) has a neighbor in \(S\) exactly when some selected edge of \(H\) avoids both \(a\) and \(b\). Therefore \(S\) totally dominates \(KG(n,2)\) precisely when no two-element subset covers all edges of \(H\), equivalently \(\tau(H)\ge3\). This includes selected vertices, since adjacency is disjointness, not equality. Inclusion-minimality means \(\tau(H-e)\le2\) for every \(e\in E(H)\). The cover number drops by at most one when an edge is deleted (extend a cover of \(H-e\) by one endpoint of \(e\)), so in fact \(\tau(H)=3\) and \(\tau(H-e)=2\) for every selected edge. Conversely, those two conditions imply inclusion-minimal total domination.

**Step 2: an absolute six-edge bound.** Enumerate the selected edges \(E_i\), for \(1\le i\le q\). For each \(E_i\), choose a two-vertex cover \(C_i\) of \(H-E_i\). Because \(\tau(H)=3\), the set \(C_i\) must be disjoint from \(E_i\). For \(j\ne i\), the cover \(C_i\) meets \(E_j\). Thus the two-element sets \(E_i,C_i\) obey \(E_i\cap C_i=\varnothing\) and \(E_i\cap C_j\ne\varnothing\) for all distinct indices. In a uniformly random ordering of all original labels, consider the event that both members of \(E_i\) precede both members of \(C_i\). This event has probability \(1/\binom{4}{2}=1/6\). Events for distinct indices are disjoint: an element of \(E_i\cap C_j\) must precede an element of \(E_j\cap C_i\) if event \(i\) holds, while event \(j\) forces the reverse. Therefore \(q/6\le1\), so \(q\le6\). This is the elementary permutation proof of the two-families inequality in the present \((2,2)\) case.

**Step 3: exhaustive finite classification, with an explicit exhaustive generator.** The attached standard-library script enumerates, up to exact graph isomorphism, *every* simple graph with no isolated vertices and from one through six edges. At the one-edge layer there is a single graph. To pass from the previous layer to the next, add every possible absent edge among existing vertices, every edge joining an existing vertex and one new vertex, and one edge joining two new vertices; discard isomorphic duplicates by exact backtracking over degree-compatible vertex bijections. This is complete by induction: delete any edge from an isolate-free target graph, delete vertices newly isolated by that deletion (at most two), and obtain a graph in the preceding layer, while the deleted edge is restored by one of the three operations. The complete layer sizes in increasing edge order are \(1,2,5,11,26,68\); each is subjected to an independent exact vertex-cover test over all vertex subsets of size at most two, before and after deleting every edge. The only four isomorphism classes passing \(\tau(H)>2\) and \(\tau(H-e)\le2\) for every edge are \(3K_2\), \(K_3\sqcup K_2\), \(C_5\), and \(K_4\). This certifies the finite classification conditional only on the inspectable finite algorithm; it does not infer an infinite statement from testing only finitely many Kneser graphs. The universal edge bound in Step 2 reduces every instance to the enumerated finite case.

**Step 4: label and count.** On a chosen six-label support, \(3K_2\) has \(6!/(2^3 3!)=15\) distinct matchings and has three edges. On any five-label support, \(K_3\sqcup K_2\) has \(\binom53=10\) choices and four edges; \(C_5\) has \(5!/10=12\) distinct cycles and five edges. On a four-label support \(K_4\) has one configuration and six edges. The supports and cardinalities make the four families disjoint. Choosing the support from \([n]\) gives the stated enumerator. Since every family has at most six selected edges and \(K_4\) exists for all \(n\ge4\), the upper inclusion-minimal cardinality is always six. The stated size supports follow from the nonvanishing binomial coefficients.

## Verification
Run \(\texttt{python3 verify_kneser_min_total.py}\) using only the Python standard library. It checks all \(113\) unlabeled isolate-free graphs with up to six edges via independently computed vertex covers. It separately constructs the disjointness adjacency of \(KG(n,2)\) and exhaustively tests all nonempty selected-vertex subsets for \(n=4,5,6,7\): \(2{,}131{,}004\) subsets, including \(2{,}090{,}859\) total dominating sets and \(792\) inclusion-minimal total dominating sets. Their enumerators agree coefficient by coefficient with the formula. The finite Kneser checks are corroboration, not a substitute for the uniform proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Cornet and Torres, *\(k\)-tuple domination on Kneser graphs*, arXiv:2308.15603 (first submitted 2023-08-29), study **closed-neighborhood** multiple domination and its minimum sets. Brešar, Cornet, Dravec and Henning, *\(k\)-Domination invariants on Kneser graphs*, arXiv:2312.15464 (first submitted 2023-12-24), study domination and **open-neighborhood** multiple total-domination numbers and their minimum configurations. The latter includes the relevant open-neighborhood definition, but the inspected text establishes extremal sizes rather than a classification and enumerator of *all inclusion-minimal* ordinary total dominating sets of \(KG(n,2)\). The known minimum-value information is not asserted as new. The present exact classification is stronger than determining the minimum alone and is not implied by a minimum formula. It also differs from the ordinary total-domination polynomial, which counts every feasible set regardless of inclusion-minimality.

## Limitations
The exhaustive finite obstruction census is a reproducible computational lemma; its generator and exhaustive vertex-cover tests are included in source form and have been executed. No machine-checkable proof independent of that finite census is claimed. The result concerns only two-element Kneser graphs and does not characterize the corresponding families for larger subset sizes. A differently phrased earlier enumeration of the four edge-critical auxiliary graphs could imply the displayed formula, so absolute publication priority is not established. Independent mathematical assessment has not been performed.
