In a two-patch population whose favorable patch alternates every generation, survival losses during migration cause a sharp all-or-nothing optimum: move nobody if \(h<\sqrt{b/a}\), move everybody if \(h>\sqrt{b/a}\), and only the two extremes tie at equality. There is a nonempty range of migrant survival where a small increase in movement initially harms population growth, yet full migration rescues the population from decline.
