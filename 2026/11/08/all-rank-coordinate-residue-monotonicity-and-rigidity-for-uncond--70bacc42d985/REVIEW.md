# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies represented residue classes with centered representatives section by section, then rewrites the normalized count as a constant times the probability that an independent product of centered residue magnitudes lies in an absolute ideal. The exact discrete CDF gives a joint monotone coupling. To prove equality rigidity, a minimal missing point in the larger centered box supplies a strictly different one-dimensional conditional CDF; telescoping the coordinate distributions makes the section difference strictly positive. This covers every positive integer modulus pair, every coordinate subgroup rank, and all nonempty sections.

The standalone verifier checked 2,080 exact modular/centered enumerations and 1,664 independent inequality/equality comparisons. These finite checks are not the proof of universal validity; the analytic argument is.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
Malikiosis introduced DMP. Xue's arXiv:2610.39026v1 Theorem 1.1 proves all convex bodies for subgroups of rank at most two; those lower-rank instances of this theorem are prior work. Remark 5.3 includes the corresponding lower-rank saturation principle. The current conditional theorem supplies all coordinate-subgroup ranks, especially the unavailable rank-three-and-higher regime, plus an explicit centered-box equality criterion. Targeted full-text reading and published-finding corpus searches for coordinate ideals, stochastic couplings, congruence images, and equivalent claims found no encompassing high-rank result. Residual risk: a theorem in older absolute-order-ideal literature could imply the result; absence from a database is not uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The original DMP is motivated by sharp lattice-point bounds and successive minima. Its all-rank behavior is structurally harder than the unconditional rank-two theorem. The present theorem identifies a broad natural geometric family, a nontrivial class of higher-rank subgroups, and the exact rigidity of equality for every modulus, thereby giving an explicit boundary of known generality. It does not claim the unrestricted high-rank problem.
