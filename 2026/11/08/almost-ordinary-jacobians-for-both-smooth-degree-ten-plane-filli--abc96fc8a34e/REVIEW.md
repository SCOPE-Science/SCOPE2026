# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the Asgarli--Ghioca degree-ten pencil, the rational-point singularity criterion leaves exactly parameters \(k=1,3\) over \(\mathbb F_7\), and exact Buchberger reductions certify geometrical smoothness of those two members across all three affine charts. Their genus is \(36\). The standard coefficient-extraction formula on the \(36\) adjoint monomials gives Hasse--Witt matrices \(A\) with \(\operatorname{rank}A=\operatorname{rank}A^2=35\) for each parameter. Explicit nonzero \(35\)-minors together with vanishing \(36\)-determinants certify the ranks. Hence the Jacobians have \(7\)-rank \(35\), Cartier \(a\)-number \(1\), and almost-ordinary Newton polygons. Exhaustive point enumeration in two irreducible quadratic field models gives \(\#C_1(\mathbb F_{49})=103\) and \(\#C_3(\mathbb F_{49})=107\). All finite computations are exact and reproducible using the three embedded standard-library scripts.

## Originality — PASS (independent priority unresolved)

The directly inspected Asgarli--Ghioca 2023 paper [1] gives the exact pencil, rational smoothness criterion, and earlier computational smoothness checks; none of those is claimed new. Its full accessible text was compared by implication, including aliases for Hasse--Witt matrices, Cartier ranks, Newton polygons and extension counts. The new statements about stable \(7\)-rank \(35\), \(a=1\), the two distinct quadratic counts and the resulting different isogeny classes are not implied by the source's results. Adolphson--Sperber [2] provides a general calculation formula, not these ranks or counts. Targeted public mathematical searches did not locate a stronger exact specialization. Residual risk: older unindexed arithmetic calculations might cover the result, and search nonhits do not prove novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Degree-\(q+3\) plane-filling pencils are naturally motivated in the cited source, and \(q=7\) is a particularly natural small case because the field characteristic coincides with the exponent seven in the pencil's singularity polynomial. Exactly the two smooth coefficient parameters have Jacobians of almost maximal possible \(7\)-rank, and their Newton polygons are completely fixed. Their different \(\mathbb F_{49}\) counts additionally distinguish their arithmetic isogeny classes despite identical \(\mathbb F_7\) point sets. This is a complete and usable arithmetic classification for a specific natural pencil, not a claim about arbitrary degree-ten curves.
