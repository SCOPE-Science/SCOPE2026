# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Each generic section vertex is a polytope edge crossing. In the regular octahedron the three positive and three negative vertices yield six crossing edges. In the cuboctahedron a partition of its 24 edges among six squares gives eight crossings for any generic normal. For all other parameters explicit diagonal and near-coordinate planes yield two different stable section types; strict inequalities make both events positive-probability. The pure-standard-library rational checker independently reconstructs finite sections from halfspaces. These finite checks do not prove an infinite statement.

## Originality
**PASS, provisionally.** Full primary studies of the standard truncation family by Gantapara and collaborators (2013, 2014), and the broader family by Chen and collaborators (2013), address packing and particle shape, not the complete central-section vertex rigidity classification. Related research-record results treat other invariants on the same shapes or section laws for different polyhedra. The scope was compared at the implication level, not only through shared nouns. Search results cannot certify that all older results have been found; some relevant research-record full texts were unavailable.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The finding is a complete structural classification over a canonical parameter family, with an isolated interior cuboctahedral rigidity phenomenon and explicit open normal sets proving all other parameters are nonrigid. It does not claim a general rigidity classification across all polyhedra.

## Limitations and closest work
The closest primary works are Gantapara et al., *Physical Review Letters* 111 (2013), 015501; Gantapara et al., arXiv:1412.2597; Chen et al., arXiv:1309.2662. They establish different geometric/physical properties. Exceptional section directions are deliberately excluded from the almost-sure assertion; no full nonrigid distribution is claimed. Historical originality requires independent review.
