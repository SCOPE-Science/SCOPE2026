# An arithmetic sphere law for synchronous avalanche complexes of multiple disjoint directed cycles

## Finding
For every integer \(r\ge3\), integer \(d\ge1\), and pairwise coprime integers \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\ge2\) such that every \(n_i=du_i\ge3\), consider the disjoint union \(G=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{r}\overrightarrow C_{n_i}\). On each consistently oriented cycle place zero chips at one vertex and one chip at each other vertex. Fire all eligible vertices synchronously with the standard outdegree-one threshold. Let \(\mathcal A(G,c_0)\) be the simplicial complex generated by the sets of firing vertices in the successive configurations. With \(N=\sum_i n_i\), the **integral homotopy type**, independently of the initially empty positions, is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(G,c_0)\simeq S^{N-(r-1)d-2}.}
\]
Its only nonzero reduced integral homology is a single infinite cyclic group in this dimension. The exact synchronous orbit period is \(d\prod_i u_i\). The pairwise-coprime condition is equivalent to \(\gcd(n_i,n_j)=d\) for all distinct components.

## Assumptions and scope
The directed cycles are disjoint and each edge is oriented consistently around its cycle. A vertex fires if its chip count is at least its outgoing degree, sending one chip along its unique outgoing edge. The simultaneous firing set at time \(t\) is the set of vertices eligible before the \(t\)-th transition. An avalanche simplex is a subset of some such firing set. The initial hole on each component may occur at any index; reindexing components reduces to all initial holes at zero. The assumptions \(r\ge3\), \(u_i\ge2\), \(n_i\ge3\) avoid empty simplex-boundary factors and distinguish the result from the separately known two-cycle case. Homotopy is nonequivariant and all homology coefficients are integral unless explicitly stated.

## Proof
**Parallel dynamics and arithmetic of holes.** Since the cycle has exactly one vacant vertex, all other vertices fire and the vacancy advances by one cyclic step. Indeed, the vacant vertex receives one chip from its predecessor and the successor of the vacancy loses its chip while receiving none. Thus at time \(t\) the omitted vertex on cycle \(i\) is at \(h_i+t\pmod {n_i}\). The global period is \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(n_1,\ldots,n_r)=d\prod_i u_i\). By generalized Chinese remaindering the possible omitted tuples are exactly the tuples whose phase-corrected indices share a single residue modulo \(d\): existence of a common \(t\) is equivalent to equality modulo each pairwise gcd, which here is always \(d\). This characterizes every avalanche facet, without a finite-sampling assumption.

**Residue blocks.** Split the vertex set into \(d\) disjoint blocks \(B_j\), each collecting phase-corrected residue \(j\) on every cycle. For fixed \(j\), the set of omitted tuples runs through *every* choice of one vertex in each of the \(r\) parts of \(B_j\), each part having \(u_i\) vertices. Let \(D_j\) be the complex on \(B_j\) generated by complements of these transversals. A subset \(F\subseteq B_j\) is in \(D_j\) precisely if it omits at least one vertex of each of the \(r\) parts. Therefore, exactly as simplicial complexes,
\[
D_j=\partial\Delta^{u_1-1}*\cdots*\partial\Delta^{u_r-1}
\cong_{\mathrm{PL}} S^{\sum_i(u_i-1)-1}.
\]
Every boundary factor is a nonempty sphere of dimension \(u_i-2\) because \(u_i\ge2\).

**Complement-of-a-disjoint-union suspension lemma.** For disjoint nonempty finite vertex sets \(U,V\) bearing nonempty sets of forbidden hyperedges, let \(D_U,D_V\) be the complexes generated by complements of their respective forbidden hyperedges. The complex on \(U\sqcup V\) generated by complements of hyperedges contained entirely in either block is exactly
\[
(D_U*\Delta(V))\ \cup\ (\Delta(U)*D_V),
\]
whose intersection is \(D_U*D_V\). Here \(\Delta(W)\) denotes the full simplex. Both terms of this union are contractible, since each is a join with a nonempty simplex. The simplicial inclusions of the intersection are cofibrations; the homotopy pushout of two contractible spaces over their common intersection is a suspension. Hence the union is homotopy equivalent to \(\Sigma(D_U*D_V)\). This applies to the two blocks even if the intersection is disconnected; it is a homotopy assertion, not a claim that arbitrary cones are simplicially collapsible.

**Iteration.** Each global omitted tuple lies entirely in precisely one residue block, so the global avalanche complex is the complex of complements of the union of the \(d\) blockwise transversal hypergraphs. Apply the suspension lemma successively to obtain
\[
\mathcal A(G,c_0)\simeq\Sigma^{d-1}(D_0*\cdots*D_{d-1}).
\]
Each \(D_j\) is a sphere of dimension \(\sum_i u_i-r-1\). Their \(d\)-fold join has dimension \(d(\sum_i u_i-r)-1=N-rd-1\). Suspending \(d-1\) times yields dimension \(N-rd-1+d-1=N-(r-1)d-2\), proving the theorem. The sphere is connected of nonnegative dimension under the stated hypotheses; all integral homology claims follow. The argument also proves independence of hole phases by reindexing.

## Relationship to prior work
Riihimäki and Smith's *Avalanche homology of digraphs via sandpile dynamics* (arXiv:2606.26786, first publicly posted 2026-06-25) introduces the avalanche complex and investigates individual directed cycles and constructions that identify vertices of two directed cycles. Identifying a vertex changes the graph and its firing dynamics; the present disjoint, synchronously coupled multicycle problem is distinct. A separate previously recorded two-component theorem gives the sphere of dimension \(a+b-\gcd(a,b)-2\). This result handles arbitrary \(r\ge3\) within the equal-pairwise-gcd family, and proves it by a new multi-block transversal and iterated homotopy-pushout argument. Standard Chinese remaindering, simplex-boundary joins and homotopy pushouts are classical tools and are not claimed as novel. No equivalent unrestricted multicycle conclusion was located in the searched material; this is not a guarantee of priority.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes at least three cycles, pairwise equal gcd, exactly one initial vacancy per cycle, and parallel firing. It does not determine arbitrary gcd patterns, two or more vacancies per component, nonsynchronous updates, other graph families, or an equivariant homotopy decomposition. The case \(r=2\) is previously covered. Novelty comparisons cannot exclude all unindexed or inaccessible literature; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
