In an intermittently active harmonic trap with unequal switching rates, the stationary position law is infinitely divisible at every rate, but its sharp self-decomposability boundary is \(v\ge4/(u+4)\) in one dimension and \(v\ge2\) in all higher dimensions, where \(u\) is on-to-off and \(v\) off-to-on. Hence every scalar projection may admit a Lévy-driven Ornstein–Uhlenbeck representation even when the joint law cannot; an explicit compound-Poisson driver realizes precisely the admissible region.
