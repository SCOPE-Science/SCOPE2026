# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** Exact endpoint coverings, a forbidden-return inequality, monotone inverse branches, and a two-step derivative bound establish a conjugate uniformly expanding golden-mean Cantor set. The exact logit coordinate recurrence and Parry edge probabilities give the two-term Lyapunov expansion uniformly in the stated parameter strip. The rational exponential bounds and representative computations are independently reproducible from the accompanying checker; no finite experiment is used as proof of the infinite claims.

**Originality (PASS within inspected evidence).** Kryzhevich–Zhang–Chmara (2024), Theorem 4.1, gives the qualitative existence of such a repeller at a sufficiently large unspecified parameter depending on the payoff parameter. Its full proof does not give the certified uniform strip or the maximum-entropy Lyapunov constants. Kryzhevich–Zhang (2026) repeats qualitative asymptotic existence. The 2021 game-theory map paper and Bielawski et al. (2024) concern the underlying game/rotation dynamics. Related symbolic horseshoe constructions in other dynamical families are not statement-equivalent. Historical priority of the evaluated constants has not been independently established.

**Value (PASS).** The parameter-free existence threshold in the motivating paper does not identify where its hyperbolic symbolic dynamics is rigorously guaranteed. A broad explicit, independently checkable two-parameter region makes the entropy lower bound actionable. The two-term Lyapunov law adds a genuinely different quantitative invariant of the natural maximum-entropy state and exposes the special balance at the paper's representative \(b=1/3\), not supplied by general horseshoe existence alone.

**Scope and residual risks.** The numerical cutoff is sufficient, not sharp; the theorem identifies a particular invariant repeller, not the global attractor. Standard Parry measure and expanding Markov map machinery are used, not claimed as original. An inaccessible older full-text source or an earlier evaluated thermodynamic expression could affect historical priority.
