# Scientific review: octahedral eighth Fourier moment

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem follows from a finite *symbolic identity* on all six complex unit-modulus phases, not from fitting a sample. Independent coordinate phase rotations and common phase fix the coefficient gauge. The eighth moment is the squared coefficient norm of the fourth convolution; the 85 coefficients (one of \(\ell_1\)-size zero, eighteen of size two, sixty-six of size four) produce exactly \(6390+3360|S|^2+36|T+4Q|^2\). A standalone dependency-free exact-integer checker reconstructs this identity. The square deficit, equilateral-triple characterization, triangle upper bound, connectedness, factorization counts and finite-grid nonalias threshold cover every theorem clause. The checker does not replace the mathematical equality arguments.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact eighth-moment sum-of-squares identity is a structural invariant of a canonical three-dimensional sparse frequency set. It distinguishes the sharp minimum, exact maximum, every continuous-phase equality orbit and a nontrivial finite-phase divisibility threshold. Its precise phase-stability defect offers mathematical information beyond a single computed optimum. No global claim is made for higher-dimensional supports, arbitrary magnitudes, or phase alphabets not divisible by three.
