# An elliptic \(A_2\) singular curve and a noncanonical point on an order-\(27\) quartic quotient

## Finding

Let \(X=X_{a,b}\) be a smooth member of the quartic-threefold family below. For the order-\(27\) diagonal action \(G\), the quotient \(Y=X/G\) is a \(\mathbb Q\)-Fano threefold with reduced singular locus consisting of precisely one smooth elliptic curve \(\Gamma\) and two isolated points. The generic transverse surface singularity along \(\Gamma\) is \(A_2\), and two marked points on \(\Gamma\) have threefold cyclic types \(\frac19(1,2,6)\). The isolated points have types \(\frac1{27}(17,8,26)\) and \(\frac19(4,7,1)\); the first is noncanonical with minimum age \(\frac13\). The global Cartier index of the canonical divisor is \(9\), the anticanonical volume is \((-K_Y)^3=4\), and \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(Y)=2\). An affine model of the elliptic curve is
\[
 z^2=(1+bu+au^2)^2-4u^3.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is over \(\mathbb C\). Fix \(a,b\in\mathbb C\) for which the hypersurface
\[
X_{a,b}=V(F_{a,b})\subset\mathbb P^4,\qquad
F_{a,b}=x_1^3x_2+x_2^3x_4+x_3^3x_5+x_4^3x_5+x_5^4+a x_3^2x_4^2+b x_3x_4x_5^2
\]
is smooth. Write \(\zeta\) for a primitive \(27\)-th root of unity and set
\[
g=[\operatorname{diag}(\zeta,\zeta^{24},\zeta^{18},\zeta^9,1)],\quad G=\langle g\rangle,\quad Y=X/G.
\]
Every monomial in \(F_{a,b}\) is invariant under \(g\). The relevant fixed subvarieties are the plane quartic \(C=X\cap\{x_1=x_2=0\}\) and the quartic surface \(S=X\cap\{x_1=0\}\). The latter is a smooth K3 surface and \(C\) is a smooth genus-three curve. The smooth parameter locus is nonempty: \((a,b)=(1,0)\) is one example, as checked below. The statement holds for **every** smooth member, not merely generically.

All local quotient types refer to analytic germs over \(\mathbb C\), after removing the local pseudo-reflection subgroup when present. The singular locus is considered with reduced structure, not its Jacobian scheme.

## Proof

**(1) Smoothness and fixed loci.** At \((a,b)=(1,0)\), the plane quartic in the chart \(x_5=1\) is \(f(x,y)=x^3+y^3+1+x^2y^2\). The exact Gröbner calculation \(\langle f,f_x,f_y\rangle=\langle1\rangle\) over \(\mathbb Q\), together with \(x_3^2x_4^2\) at \(x_5=0\) and nonzero \(\partial F/\partial x_5\) at its two zeros, proves that \(C\) is smooth. At any singular point of the corresponding \(X\), \(F_{x_1}=3x_1^2x_2=0\) and \(F_{x_2}=x_1^3+3x_2^2x_4=0\) force \(x_1=0\). If \(x_2\ne0\), then \(x_4=0\) and \(F_{x_4}=x_2^3\ne0\), impossible. Thus a singular point would lie on the smooth curve \(C\), another contradiction. Consequently the smooth family is nonempty. For every smooth member, fixed loci of finite-order automorphisms are smooth; in particular \(S\) is a smooth quartic K3 surface and \(C\) is smooth of genus \(3\).

The exponents of \(g\) on the five homogeneous coordinates are \((1,24,18,9,0)\). For \(\gcd(k,27)=1\), they remain pairwise distinct modulo \(27\), and \(\mathrm{Fix}_X(g^k)=\{e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\}\). For \(\gcd(k,27)=3\), the eigenspace partition is \(\{1\},\{2\},\{3,4,5\}\), hence \(\mathrm{Fix}_X(g^k)=C\sqcup\{e_1,e_2\}\). For \(\gcd(k,27)=9\), the partition is \(\{1\},\{2,3,4,5\}\) and \(\mathrm{Fix}_X(g^k)=S\sqcup\{e_1\}\). These cases contain \(18,6,2\) elements of \(G\), respectively. The fifth coordinate vertex is not on \(X\).

**(2) Singular germs.** The order-three subgroup \(\langle g^9\rangle\) fixes the divisor \(S\) pointwise and acts as a pseudo-reflection at its generic points. Thus \(Y\) is smooth along the image of \(S\setminus(C\cup\{e_2\})\). Outside the images of \(C,e_1,e_2\), all stabilizers are trivial or generated by pseudo-reflections, so no other singular locus is possible.

At a general point of \(C\), the stabilizer is \(\langle g^3\rangle\cong C_9\) acting on the normal directions with weights \((1,6)\) modulo \(9\). After quotienting its order-three pseudo-reflection, the residual action is \(\frac13(1,2)\); this is the transverse \(A_2\) surface singularity. Every point of \(C\) is therefore singular in \(Y\).

At the four coordinate points on \(X\), the tangent representations of the generator \(g\), read in natural local coordinates, have weight triples modulo \(27\):
\[
 e_1:(17,8,26),\quad e_2:(4,21,3),\quad e_3:(10,6,18),\quad e_4:(19,15,9).
\]
The tangent coordinate eliminated by the quartic equation is respectively \(x_2,x_4,x_5,x_5\). At \(e_1\) there is no pseudo-reflection, giving \(\frac1{27}(17,8,26)\). At the other three points the order-three subgroup is a pseudo-reflection in the first local coordinate. Cubing that coordinate and taking the residual order-nine quotient gives, respectively,
\[
 e_2:\tfrac19(4,7,1),\quad e_3:\tfrac19(1,2,6),\quad e_4:\tfrac19(1,5,3).
\]
The last type is equivalent to \(\frac19(1,2,6)\) by changing the generator and permuting coordinates. The order-nine germ at \(e_2\) is isolated; at \(e_3,e_4\), the residual stabilizer of order three fixes a one-dimensional axis, yielding the same singular curve \(\Gamma=q(C)\). Its reduced local image is smooth, since it is the invariant quotient of a one-dimensional linear axis. No further singular points occur.

The minimum nonidentity Reid–Tai ages of these three local types are \(\frac13,1,1\), respectively (with the fourth equivalent to the third). The minimum at \(e_1\) is attained at the \(24\)-th power of its local generator, so the germ is noncanonical. The \(e_2,e_3,e_4\) germs are canonical but not terminal. Their canonical indices, computed from the sums of weights, are \(9,3,1,1\); along the remaining \(A_2\)-curve the index is \(1\). Hence the global canonical Cartier index is \(9\).

**(3) The elliptic singular curve.** On \(C\) the action of \(g\) has effective order \(3\), with exactly two fixed points \(e_3,e_4\). Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
2\cdot3-2=3(2g(\Gamma)-2)+2(3-1),\quad\text{so }g(\Gamma)=1.
\]
In the chart \(x_5=1\), put \(u=x_3x_4\), \(v=x_3^3\), \(w=x_4^3\), and \(z=v-w\). Then \(v+w=-(1+bu+au^2)\), \(vw=u^3\), and therefore \(z^2=(1+bu+au^2)^2-4u^3\). These generators give the invariant function field, proving the stated smooth projective genus-one model.

**(4) Canonical class and Euler number.** The only codimension-one ramification of \(q\) is along \(S\), of index \(3\). Since \(K_X=-H\) and \(S\sim H\), the ramification formula gives
\[
K_X=q^*K_Y+2S,\qquad q^*(-K_Y)=3H.
\]
Thus \(-K_Y\) is ample and \((-K_Y)^3= (3H)^3/27=4\). Finite quotient singularities are klt, so \(Y\) is a \(\mathbb Q\)-Fano variety (without the additional requirement of canonical singularities).

For a smooth quartic threefold, the topological Euler number is \(-56\) (the degree of the third Chern class); for \(S\) it is \(24\), and for \(C\) it is \(-4\). Burnside's Euler formula for the finite group action yields
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(Y)=\frac{-56+18\cdot4+6(-4+2)+2(24+1)}{27}=2.
\]
These prove all assertions.

## Verification

Run `python certificate.py` with Python 3 and SymPy installed. It checks invariance of all seven monomials, all \(26\) nonidentity fixed-locus partitions, all four tangent representations, pseudo-reflection reduction and Reid–Tai ages, the canonical indices, the finite Euler and anticanonical arithmetic, and an exact Gröbner smoothness witness. Its terminal line is `CERTIFICATE_OK`. The code does not replace the general quotient-theoretic and Riemann–Hurwitz arguments given above; in particular numerical experiments are not used to infer the all-parameter theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Zhu's 2026 classification (arXiv:2610.39627v1, Theorem 1.2 and Example 3.2(20)) introduces precisely this family and proves the order-\(27\) group is its full automorphism group. Its stated result classifies group actions rather than the singularities, elliptic curve and birational canonical data of their quotients. Zheng's 2020 work (arXiv:2004.09008) studies possible abelian automorphism groups of hypersurfaces and supplies historical context, but the inspected results do not give this quotient calculation. The methods used here (fixed-locus partitions, cyclic quotient weights, Reid–Tai, ramification and Riemann–Hurwitz) are standard; novelty is asserted only for their combined explicit application to Zhu's family. The possibility of an unlocated earlier equivalent quotient classification remains.

## Limitations

The reduced singular locus, not its scheme structure or a resolution, is classified. The result does not determine a minimal model or stringy invariants, and makes no claim about singular members of the quartic family. Canonical age and the global index use the coarse algebraic quotient rather than a stack. The historical-priority assessment is based on identifiable inspected literature and searches and is not proof of uniqueness; independent verification has not been performed.
