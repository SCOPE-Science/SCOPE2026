# An embedded polar-zero divisor in the characteristic-two quadratic resultant

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic \(2\). In \(\mathbb P^5_k\) with homogeneous coordinates \([a:b:c:d:e:f]\), let \(D\) be the quartic hypersurface defined by the Sylvester resultant of
\[
A=aX^2+bXY+cY^2,\qquad B=dX^2+eXY+fY^2.
\]
Set
\[
P=af-cd,\qquad Q=ae-bd,\qquad T=bf-ce,
\qquad I=(P,Q,T),\qquad H=(b,e,P).
\]
Then \(D=V(P^2-QT)\), and its **full Tjurina singular scheme** \(Z\), defined by the equation and all six coefficient derivatives, has the exact homogeneous ideal
\[
\boxed{J_Z=(P^2-QT,\partial_a R,\ldots,\partial_f R)=I H.}
\]
The reduced support is the Segre threefold \(S=V(I)\simeq\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\). Its unique additional associated subvariety is the smooth quadric surface \(E=V(H)\simeq\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), embedded in \(S\) as the locus of proportional quadratic pairs with zero polar form. In ideal-sheaf notation,
\[
\mathcal I_Z=\mathcal I_S\mathcal I_E
=\mathcal I_S\cap\mathcal I_E^2.
\]
The embedded sheaf \(\mathcal I_S/\mathcal I_Z\) is naturally the rank-two conormal bundle \(N^*_{S/\mathbb P^5}|_E\). At a geometric generic point of \(E\), the fiber of the full singular scheme over a transverse normal slice to \(S\) has algebra \(\kappa(E)[r,s]/(r,s)^2\) of length \(3\); away from \(E\) the singular scheme is reduced along \(S\). In particular \(Z\) is not Cohen–Macaulay.

## Assumptions and scope

All identities hold over **every** field \(k\) of characteristic \(2\), without algebraic-closure or finite-field assumptions. The projective ambient space records a pair of binary quadratics up to one simultaneous scalar; the parameter space includes pairs with a zero quadratic. The singular scheme is the scheme defined by both the resultant \(R\) and the six coefficient derivatives, **not** by derivatives alone (the degree \(4\) Euler identity cannot recover \(R\) in characteristic \(2\)). Associated subvarieties and conormal sheaves are meant on \(\mathbb P^5_k\), rather than in an unsaturated affine cone.

## Proof

**1. Resultant and differentiation.** A direct determinant of the four-by-four Sylvester matrix, valid integrally, gives
\[
R=P^2-QT
=a^2f^2-abef-2acdf+ace^2+b^2df-bcde+c^2d^2.
\]
In characteristic \(2\), the full ordered list of coefficient derivatives is
\[
(\partial_aR,\partial_bR,\partial_cR,
\partial_dR,\partial_eR,\partial_fR)
=(eT,eP,eQ,bT,bP,bQ).
\]
Thus \(J_Z=(R)+(b,e)I\). Since \(Q,T\in(b,e)\), the relation \(R=P^2-QT\) shows \(J_Z=(P^2)+(b,e)I=I(b,e,P)=IH\) exactly in the polynomial ring, without radicalization.

**2. Support and embedded divisor.** The three generators of \(I\) are the maximal minors of the two-by-three coefficient matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&b&c\\d&e&f\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Hence \(S\) is the smooth Segre \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\). On this Segre, the conditions \(b=e=0\) mean the middle coordinate of the \(\mathbb P^2\)-factor vanishes, so \(E\simeq\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) is a smooth Cartier divisor in \(S\). Intrinsically, the polar bilinear form of a binary quadratic in characteristic \(2\) vanishes precisely when its middle coefficient does; therefore this divisor is independent of the chosen binary coordinates. Since \(I\subset H\), the radical of \(IH\) is \(I\).

**3. Exact local structure.** On the dense chart \(a=1\), use coordinates \(t=d,x=c,y=b,r=f-tx,s=e-ty\). Then
\[
P=r,\quad Q=s,\quad T=yr-xs,\quad
R=r^2-yrs+xs^2.
\]
The full Tjurina ideal in characteristic \(2\) is
\[
(r^2,rs,s^2,yr,ys)=(r,s)(y,r,s)
=(r,s)\cap(y,r,s)^2.
\]
Here \(S\) is \((r,s)=0\), while \(E\) is \((r,s,y)=0\). The other charts obtained by choosing a nonzero coefficient in the first or second pair of rows give the same local complete-intersection form: \(S\) is a smooth codimension-two subvariety and \(E\) is a smooth divisor on it. Hence the local intersection identity globalizes to \(\mathcal I_Z=\mathcal I_S\cap\mathcal I_E^2\), and the only associated subvarieties are \(S,E\). The quotient
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal I_S/(\mathcal I_S\mathcal I_E)
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_Z\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S\longrightarrow0
\]
identifies its left term with the restricted conormal bundle, locally freely generated by the classes of \(r,s\) on \(E\). This is a rank-two embedded socle. At \(y=0\) the transverse ideal becomes \((r,s)^2\), whose quotient has basis \(1,r,s\). On \(y\ne0\), the ideal is \((r,s)\). The embedded associated prime rules out Cohen–Macaulayness.

**4. Characteristic boundary.** The same local equation over a field of characteristic different from \(2\) has derivatives \(2r-ys\) and \(-yr+2xs\) in the normal directions. At the generic point of \(E\) (where \(x\ne0\)), these generate \((r,s)\) locally. Thus the embedded transverse length-three phenomenon identified above is genuinely characteristic-dependent; no claim is made that every special locus outside characteristic \(2\) is reduced.

## Verification

The standalone `verification.py` independently constructs the Sylvester determinant from the defining quadratics, differentiates all six coefficients modulo \(2\), and checks the equality of the generated ideals in both directions using Gröbner remainders. It also verifies the affine local equation and product ideal, the three-element transverse basis, and the equality of singular support and matrix-rank-one locus at all \(63\) points of \(\mathbb P^5(\mathbb F_2)\). The recorded output appears in `verification_output.txt`. All infinite-field assertions follow from the displayed algebraic proof; finite point enumeration alone proves none of them.

## Relationship to prior work

D'Andrea and Chipalkatti, *On the Jacobian ideal of the binary discriminant* (arXiv:math/0601705, first posted 2006-01-29), §5, analyze resultant Jacobian ideals over \(\mathbb C\) and prove perfection of a partial-coefficient Jacobian ideal in that setting. Their result is not a characteristic-two computation of the full Tjurina scheme. Statnik, *Singular loci of sparse resultants* (arXiv:2110.13872, first posted 2021-10-26), examines singular loci and transverse types over \(\mathbb C\); its statements do not calculate the embedded polar-zero divisor of the characteristic-two full Jacobian scheme. Standard resultant theory supplies the quartic equation and rank-one Segre support; neither fact is presented as novel. The asserted contribution is the exact positive-characteristic ideal product, its embedded associated divisor, and its conormal/transverse description.

## Limitations

Historical priority has not been established absolutely: an unlocated older modular-resultant calculation might imply the product identity. The result concerns only pairs of binary quadratics, not arbitrary degrees or more general sparse supports. Its embedded structure is for the full Tjurina singular scheme, not for the gradient ideal in isolation. There has been no independent specialist audit. The paper comparisons are limited to the specifically described portions and hypotheses.
