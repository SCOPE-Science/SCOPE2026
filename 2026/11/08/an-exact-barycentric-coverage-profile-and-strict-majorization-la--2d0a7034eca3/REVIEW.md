# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The exact formula is derived from an almost-sure disjoint decomposition of the reference triangle into one central random triangle and three corner triangles. The event in a specified corner is a two-variable inequality whose normalized integral is evaluated with its exact lower endpoint. Affine invariance transports the result to every nondegenerate Euclidean triangle. Differentiation proves the strict pair-balancing inequality, which gives strict Schur-concavity on the interior; boundary extension, optimum, binary-entropy law, and integrated area check follow analytically. Independent verification used exact symbolic identities, high-precision quadrature, and direct simulated geometry in a scalene triangle. Finite experiments were not used to infer the all-target theorem.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

The 2014 public problem and solution established the one-point-per-side probability at the equilateral centroid, namely \(\log4/3\), with the same corner-partition idea. This known value is neither renamed nor claimed as new. A prior available result with an entire-perimeter sampling model calculates centroid probability as side-allocation weights vary; its target never varies. A 2012 perimeter sampling question and a later paper on area-uniform triangles use different sampling measures. The assessed result makes a stronger and distinct assertion in target position: complete exact barycentric profile, strict ordering under majorization, and the associated directional vertex limit. Searches did not identify equivalent or dominating statements, but unpublished or unindexed historical proofs remain possible. Source comparisons and residual risks are itemized in `AUDIT.json`.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem identifies which target points are most likely to be covered by a random inscribed triangle and gives a quantitative order for every pair of target points related by barycentric majorization. Unlike a single center probability, this supplies a full affine-invariant spatial coverage field for arbitrary triangles, and a complete optimization theorem. The corner entropy and average \(1/4\) provide independently motivated structural checks and useful descriptions of the profile's boundary behavior.

## Closest literature and limitations

The classical centroid probability and corner decomposition were already public in November 2014. The exact formula concerns three **independent side-conditioned** points, not three independent uniform points from the entire perimeter or area, and its strict majorization statement requires positive barycentric coordinates. Boundary targets all have zero probability; no global strict comparison there. No confidence intervals, arbitrary polygons, or nonuniform target distributions are treated. Independent priority and mathematical validation remain unperformed.
