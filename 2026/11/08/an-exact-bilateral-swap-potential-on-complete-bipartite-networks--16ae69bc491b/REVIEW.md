# Scientific review: complete-bipartite swap games

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact-change identity is proved by deleting and inserting the two agents from the internal pair sums on each side. The displayed cost formulas account for the fact that a swapped agent sees the other participant in its new neighborhood. Strict bilateral improvements therefore yield strict potential increases; finiteness of all labeled assignments gives the all-size finite improvement property. The seven possible two-type-side multisets of the example are exhaustively enumerated, with their social costs and all swaps checked using exact rational arithmetic. The general proof does not rely on finite experiments. The strongest boundary risk is inadvertent application to graphs that are bipartite but not complete; no such extension is asserted.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest direct full-text comparison is Bilò et al., *Schelling Games with Continuous Types* (2023, arXiv:2305.06819): its Proposition 3 proves finite improvement for regular graphs, while Theorems 17–18 prove price of stability one for regular graphs or paths. Neither theorem implies termination on the irregular family \(K_{2,5}\), nor rules out its exact ratio \(17/15\). Our potential is a different, within-part function. The 2022 single-peaked swap work on bipartite networks has categorical types and different single-peaked utilities; its statements do not dominate the current metric-cost theorem. The 2020 categorical swap work likewise differs in costs. Relevant record and web searches found no equivalent. This is best-knowledge originality, not a claim of exhaustive priority; an unindexed, differently parameterized, or implicit equivalent is possible.

**Value — PASS.** A natural missing structural case in the original model is the complete bipartite topology with unequal part sizes, where degrees are irregular and the published regular-graph potential proof is inapplicable. The result supplies an explicit exact pairwise-change potential and universal convergence guarantee. Its seven-agent counterexample also shows that guaranteed convergence does not ensure price of stability one. These two linked statements provide a meaningful boundary between structural stability and welfare optimality rather than an arbitrary computed example.

**Reproducibility and limits.** The standalone script performs 212,500 exact swap identities over 1,519 multiset/part-size settings and independently certifies all 21 labeled positions of the exact example. Finite tests do not certify the infinite claim; the proof supplies that. No inference is made for incomplete bipartite graphs or jump games. Independent publication-priority or mathematical audit is not claimed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
