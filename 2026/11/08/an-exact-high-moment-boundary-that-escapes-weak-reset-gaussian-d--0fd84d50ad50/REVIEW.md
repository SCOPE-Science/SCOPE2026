# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The stationary common-noise mixture follows by conditioning on the common Poisson clock and using independent driving Brownian motions. Poisson and geometric-Brownian exponential integrals exist almost surely in the stated domain. Generator identities plus an integrable-polynomial stationarity argument prove necessity of each moment threshold; Minkowski bounds prove sufficiency; a monotonicity argument permits the factorial products. The strict gap follows from a third-derivative Taylor remainder. The all-order cutoff follows from an explicit strictly decreasing limit function and a simple-root perturbation estimate. The reproducible rational and numerical tests confirm key identities without replacing the infinite-order arguments.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Galla's original study [1], including the main text and relevant supplemental sections, supplies the weak-reset model, common-noise Gaussian approximation, and approximate pair-square covariance, not the exact finite-reset moment phase diagram or the growing-order discrepancy proved here. Pierce [2] uses a constant-advection partial-reset model; Biroli and collaborators [3] study one freely diffusing particle reset by rescaling. Comparisons with published research-record abstracts and earlier accessible science claims found no directly dominating result. General perpetuity theory might subsume the raw moment recursion; establishing publication priority for the integrated threshold comparison requires further human literature review.

**Value — PASS.** Simultaneous-reset correlations are a central observable of the motivating physical model. Exact stationary cross-square correlation can remain finite where its Gaussian approximation is infinite, making the approximation qualitatively wrong for an experimentally meaningful statistic. The discrepancy persists for a diverging interval of joint even-moment orders as the reset amplitude tends to zero. The result characterizes the observable boundary rather than an arbitrary algebraic special case.

**Scope and risk.** The proof is restricted to linear drift, common Poisson clocks, deterministic proportional reset and independent additive Brownian noise. The exact first two variance moments, high-order window and counterexample must not be conflated with an exact density or a result for general nonlinear dynamics. Equivalence to a broader existing stochastic-affine-moment theorem remains a publication-priority risk.
