# Scientific review of the independent-join rank transfer

**Correctness: PASS.** The block-kernel identity is valid for every alternating form in every characteristic. Counting subspaces first by their intersections with the ambient radical and then by the isotropic radical of their symplectic projection proves the transfer coefficient, including all boundary indices and empty products. The complete tripartite specialization uses the standard exact rank count for rectangular matrices; the character formula follows by the odd-characteristic orbit method. Exhaustive direct matrix-rank tests over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_4\) agree in every tested instance. No extrapolation from those tests is needed for the proof.

**Originality: PASS, provisional.** Mainkar's 2013 graph-to-Lie algebra theorem concerns isomorphism types, not rank populations. O'Brien–Voll gives the general rank-to-character reduction without evaluating the graph-specific counts. Lewis et al. count unrestricted alternating matrices and various restricted ordinary and symmetric matrices. Rossmann's 2021 graph-join formulas count conjugacy classes; Section 1.6 explicitly leaves the corresponding character-degree census as a problem. The closest published graph-rank construction adds **one** universal vertex or treats trees and cycles. Its single-vertex case is not claimed as new: iterating that construction creates a clique, whereas the new theorem adds mutually nonadjacent vertices. The complete tripartite formula is thus assessed for three nontrivial parts, not the already-covered singleton-part boundary. General matrix-counting theorems under different terminology remain a priority risk; this is not a claim of definitive literature exhaustiveness.

**Value: PASS.** Exact character-degree distributions are natural invariants of finite nilpotent groups, and Rossmann explicitly formulates their determination as a problem. The transfer gives all rank strata simultaneously for an arbitrary seed graph rather than requiring a fresh enumeration after each independent join. Its concrete all-parameter application determines character degrees of complete tripartite graphical groups with every part size at least two, a nontrivial class outside the single-universal-vertex boundary. The result supplies a reusable structural enumeration principle, not merely a small isolated example.

The principal scientific limitation is that the method does not immediately handle edges among added vertices, and historical-priority comparison has a residual risk of a more general structured-matrix theorem. Only self-review was performed; no independent audit is asserted.
