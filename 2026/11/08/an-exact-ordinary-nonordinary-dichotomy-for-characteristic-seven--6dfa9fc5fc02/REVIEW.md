# Review: characteristic-seven Picard Frobenius strata

## Correctness — PASS
The projective quartic is smooth exactly off its explicit discriminant hypersurface. The \(H^1(\mathcal O)\) Čech basis gives a three-by-three Hasse–Witt matrix by exact coefficient extraction in the sixth power of the defining quartic. Its determinant equals \(3a^2\Delta\) over \(\mathbb F_7\); the boundary has exact matrix rank one. The Frobenius-semilinear second-iterate factor, not the ordinary linear square, determines the \(7\)-rank and leaves precisely \(b^4=4\) after smoothness exclusion. Two separate symbolic extraction programs completed successfully. Genus, boundary, and every quantifier were checked.

## Originality — PASS (provisional; risk retained)
The literal statement in Nourozi–Rahmati, arXiv:2306.07823, Theorem 3.1, gives no such stratification and conflicts with the smooth \(a=0\) locus when read with its explicitly printed hypotheses; their later coefficient formula provides computational tools but does not state the determinant-discriminant factorization or classify the stable Frobenius ranks. Bouw–Koutsianas–Sijsling–Wewers (2020) develops normal forms, while Cavalieri–Pries (2023/24) treats mass formulas and other families. Targeted mathematical-record and textual searches did not reveal this complete classification. The principal remaining risk is prior implicit derivation from general Hasse–Witt results or an unindexed characteristic-seven Picard calculation; absent hits do not establish universal novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete classification of the ordinary/nonordinary and \(7\)-rank loci for a geometrically natural two-parameter Picard family, not an isolated point count. It isolates a codimension-one jump by two in the \(a\)-number, an exact missing intermediate \(7\)-rank, and a four-point zero-rank locus inside the nonordinary boundary. It also gives a concrete test for the scope of a published uniformity assertion.

## Limits
No full Ekedahl–Oort type or curve-isomorphism census; no claim that classical Hasse–Witt coefficient formulas themselves are new. Independent historical review remains outstanding.
