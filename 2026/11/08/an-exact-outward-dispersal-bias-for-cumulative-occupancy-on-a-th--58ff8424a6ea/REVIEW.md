## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence, priority provisional

The foundational finite presence–absence transition-matrix method is already established by Day and Possingham (1995), whose discrete-time applications do not state the present fixed-budget optimum. A further accessible primary full text is Vuilleumier and Possingham (2006), which studies simulation-based viability in asymmetric metapopulations, rather than the two absorbing-chain rewards and their fixed-budget optimizers. Vuilleumier et al. (2010) is a close dispersal-asymmetry study but only its abstract was available; its uninspected full text is an explicit historical-priority risk. Shtilerman and Stone (2015) concern spectral and degree-based network persistence. The classic contact-process transposition duality already covers the arrow-reversal survival invariance and is acknowledged rather than presented as novel. Accessible previous two-patch results concern different state spaces and either extinction-time ordering or immigration-dependent stationary rewards, and do not imply the three-patch cumulative reward optimum. Semantic mathematical searches produced no identified equivalent result; this is not a claim of exhaustive historical uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

Expected time until regional extinction and expected accumulated occupied-patch time are both natural but different metapopulation objectives. Their optimal dispersal allocations are rigorously and strictly distinct for every budget, despite symmetric initial conditions and local extinction rates. The exact cubic, uniqueness, bounds and asymptotic bias quantify this structural disagreement; they are not merely a renamed formula or one numerical example. The size of the reward improvement can be small, so the finding has theoretical benchmark value rather than an unsubstantiated large ecological effect.
