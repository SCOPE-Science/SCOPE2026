# An exact stationary continuum law for the two-hub contact process with a permanent source

## Finding
For each \(c>0\), consider the continuous-time contact process on \(K_{2,n}\), with a permanently infected first hub, a recovering second hub, and \(n\) recovering ordinary vertices. Directed transmissions along each hub–ordinary edge occur independently at rate \(c/\sqrt n\), and every nonpermanent infected vertex recovers at rate one. Let \(Y_n\in\{0,1\}\) denote infection of the second hub and \(K_n\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\) the number of infected ordinary vertices, both sampled from the unique stationary distribution. Put \(U_n=K_n/(c\sqrt n)-1\) and \(a=c^2\).

Then \((Y_n,U_n)\) converges in distribution to a stationary law on \(\{0,1\}\times(0,1)\) with the following joint probability densities (with respect to Lebesgue measure in the second coordinate):
\[
 p_0(u)=\frac{e^{au}u^{a-1}(1-u)}{Z_a},\qquad
 p_1(u)=\frac{e^{au}u^a}{Z_a},\qquad
 Z_a=\int_0^1 e^{av}v^{a-1}\,dv.
\]
Thus the rescaled infected-ordinary population has a nondegenerate limit \(U\) with density \(Z_a^{-1}e^{au}u^{a-1}\), and the conditional second-hub infection probability is exactly \(\Pr(Y=1\mid U=u)=u\). In particular, when \(c=1\), \(U\) has density \(e^u/(e-1)\) on \((0,1)\), and
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr(Y_n=1)=\frac1{e-1},\qquad
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E K_n}{\sqrt n}=\frac e{e-1}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The integers \(n\) tend to infinity with \(c>0\) fixed; there are exactly two hubs and a single permanently infected vertex. Each directed transmission has rate \(c/\sqrt n\), not a discrete-time occupation probability. The model is symmetric among ordinary vertices, so \((Y_n,K_n)\) is a finite, irreducible, continuous-time Markov chain. The theorem concerns its stationary law and first stationary mean, not its extinction time or arbitrary initial states.

## Proof
**1. Exact finite generator.** For \(y\in\{0,1\}\), \(k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\), set \(\lambda_n=c/\sqrt n\). The nonzero transitions are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(y,k)&\longrightarrow(y,k+1)&&\text{at rate }\lambda_n(1+y)(n-k),\\
(y,k)&\longrightarrow(y,k-1)&&\text{at rate }k,\\
(0,k)&\longrightarrow(1,k)&&\text{at rate }\lambda_n k,\\
(1,k)&\longrightarrow(0,k)&&\text{at rate }1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Transitions with an out-of-range target have zero rate. The permanent hub supplies positive infection into a fully susceptible ordinary population, so the reduced finite chain communicates between all states and has a unique stationary law \(\pi_n\).

**2. Tight stationary laws.** Let \(b_n(y,k)=\lambda_n(1+y)(n-k)\le2c\sqrt n\). Stationary generator balance for the functions \(k\) and \(k^2\) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_n}K_n=\mathbb E_{\pi_n}b_n\le2c\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 2\mathbb E_{\pi_n}K_n^2=\mathbb E_{\pi_n}[(2K_n+1)b_n+K_n].
\]
Consequently \(\mathbb E_{\pi_n}K_n^2\le4c^2n+2c\sqrt n\). The stationary laws of \((Y_n,X_n)\), where \(X_n=K_n/\sqrt n\), are therefore tight; \(X_n\) are uniformly integrable.

**3. Limit dynamics and invariance.** On functions with bounded smooth derivatives in the continuous coordinate, Taylor expansion of the exact generator gives locally uniformly
\[
\mathcal Lf(y,x)=[c(1+y)-x]\partial_x f(y,x)
 +(1-y)cx[f(1,x)-f(0,x)]
 +y[f(0,x)-f(1,x)].
\]
The quadratic variation per unit time of the rescaled ordinary-population birth/death martingale is \(O(n^{-1/2})\) on compact sets. The second-hub jump intensities converge locally uniformly. Stopped martingale-problem convergence and the stationary second-moment bound imply that every weak subsequential stationary limit is invariant for the piecewise-deterministic Markov process generated by \(\mathcal L\). This implication can also be obtained by integrating \(\mathcal L_n f\) against \(\pi_n\), passing to the limit using uniform integrability, and using uniqueness of the well-posed martingale problem for \(\mathcal L\).

**4. Support, uniqueness, and stationary density.** Along any limit trajectory, \(\dot X=c(1+Y)-X\). Therefore the distance from \(X\) to \([c,2c]\) decreases at least exponentially between jumps and never increases at jumps; every invariant law is supported on \([c,2c]\). Set \(u=(x-c)/c\). Then the two drifts are \(\dot u=-u\) (state \(0\)) and \(\dot u=1-u\) (state \(1\)); the jump rates are \(a(1+u)\) from state \(0\) to \(1\) and \(1\) from \(1\) to \(0\). These rates are bounded above and strictly positive on \([0,1]\). Successive random switching times have continuous, everywhere-positive densities; two-switch transition kernels give a common absolutely continuous component on overlapping intervals. Such a switching process is irreducible on the open strip \(\{0,1\}\times(0,1)\) and, by compactness and this smoothing/minorization, has at most one invariant law. Neither endpoint supports a stationary atom: its deterministic approach takes infinite time and the outgoing switch rate is strictly positive.

Write its invariant densities as \(p_y(u)\). The stationary transport equations are
\[
\begin{split}
\frac d{du}[-u p_0(u)]&=-a(1+u)p_0(u)+p_1(u),\\
\frac d{du}[(1-u)p_1(u)]&=a(1+u)p_0(u)-p_1(u).
\end{split}
\]
Their sum makes the total flux \(-up_0+(1-u)p_1\) constant. Integrability and no boundary injection force this constant to vanish, so \(up_0=(1-u)p_1=:q\). Substitution gives
\[
\frac{q'(u)}{q(u)}=\frac a u+a-\frac1{1-u},
\qquad q(u)=C e^{au}u^a(1-u).
\]
Normalizing \(p_0+p_1=C e^{au}u^{a-1}\) determines \(C=Z_a^{-1}\), proving the exact law, its uniqueness, and full-sequence weak convergence.

**5. Means.** The stationary second-moment estimate implies uniform integrability of \(X_n\), so its first moments converge. Since \(X=c(1+U)\), this gives the displayed mean limit. The conditional law follows by division \(p_1/(p_0+p_1)=u\). At \(c=1\), \(Z_1=e-1\) and \(\int_0^1ue^u\,du=1\), proving the explicit constants.

## Verification
`verification.py` implements the exact finite generator, solves its finite stationary linear systems independently, checks normalized stationary residuals, verifies the density and flux identities pointwise, and compares finite-size infection fractions and rescaled means to the predicted limits. Numerical comparisons check finite models and are not used as an infinite-size proof. Run with Python 3 plus NumPy and SciPy: `python verification.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
The star and heterogeneous-complete-graph contact-process literature identifies persistent infection near hubs and phase-dependent extinction, but the examined results do not imply the exact stationary two-hub, permanent-source, \(n^{-1/2}\)-edge-rate law. Two-state stochastic feedback modules can themselves be studied using PDMPs or analytical stationary densities; the novelty claimed here is **not** the general idea of two-state switching or integration of its stationary transport equations. It is the proved stationary limit and exact law for the explicitly specified finite contact process, with its asymptotically random \(\sqrt n\)-scale infected population.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify other numbers of hubs, other scaling regimes, transient mixing, or absorbing SIS systems without a permanent source. The exact limiting density may have analogues in older two-state gene-feedback models; such analogues do not automatically imply the finite-graph convergence, but they remain priority context. The historical search is not an exhaustive proof of novelty.
