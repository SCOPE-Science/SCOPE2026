# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The invariant square follows from the signs of the vector field on its faces. At the depleted equilibrium the invasion eigenvalue has a unique positive zero because \(b+d>2\). For the sustainable regime, the equilibrium equation is strictly decreasing and its explicit integral Lyapunov function has a compact positive-resource sublevel, with exactly negative dissipation away from equilibrium. For the depleted and critical regimes, a scalar comparison bounds the resource, and the critical profile is obtained by a proved convolution-ratio limit before integrating the reciprocal resource. The implicit-function slope and example identities were independently checked algebraically and with numerical integration. Nothing in the proof extrapolates finite simulation to infinite time.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge literature comparison)

The initiating paper arXiv:2401.11269v1 already supplies the model and qualitative low- versus high-sensitivity outcomes. Its Section 3.6 explicitly states that it encounters analytic difficulty and therefore uses numerical simulations, rather than the exact sensitivity cutoff, global integral Lyapunov identity, equality-case \(t^{-1}\) coefficient, or sustainable-branch onset derivative stated here. The related arXiv:2405.01271 instead changes resource growth to include an Allee factor and uses replicator or knowledge-feedback rather than the specific logistic strategy response; its bistability thresholds do not imply the present global theorem. A review of human–environment sensitivity tipping concerns broader phenomena, not these exact equations and coefficients. General planar stability tools alone do not identify the numerical model-specific threshold and critical amplitude. Some unpublished or unindexed equivalent derivations could exist; an independent publication-priority review is still needed.

## Value — PASS

The initiating study explicitly leaves the steepness-dependent logistic response analytically difficult. Replacing a numerical low/high phase picture by one exact global threshold answers a motivated sustainability question. The Lyapunov cancellation is a reusable structural fact within this feedback architecture; at the previously unresolved boundary, the exact amplitude distinguishes critical slow depletion from exponential loss. The nonzero sustainable-branch slope quantifies the onset close to the threshold without claiming a policy prescription or full stochastic ecological prediction.

## Scope and limitations

The result is specific to the deterministic, normalized resource system with \(0<b<1<d\), \(b+d>2\), \(0<c<1\), \(k>0\). The depleted face stays depleted even above threshold. The paper's numerical examples can use other natural-growth coefficients, which require corresponding physical-time normalization for the asymptotic constant.
