# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite generator is determined from the stated clocks. Exchangeability identifies factorial moments with products of distinct leaf bits. The generator applied to a leaf product and to its hub-multiplied counterpart yields the two displayed recursions, including the \(r=k\) boundary without an undefined \(F_{k+1}\). The second denominator is positive, initialization is fixed by global spin-flip symmetry, and binomial inversion reconstructs all \(2(k+1)\) stationary probabilities. The scaling limit follows from stationary polynomial test-function identities and compact moment determinacy, not an assumption that finite modes converge. The independent exact-rational checker confirmed 2,550 assertions across 30 chains, including complete joint-law inversion, but the analytical proof is essential for universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS within the inspected record, provisional

The claim is **the complete exact finite-star joint law via a linear-length triangular hierarchy**. Cox–Peres–Steif (arXiv:1408.5122, Section 4) use the same hub/count reduction to prove mixing-time bounds but do not supply the factorial-moment closure and full stationary inversion. Carro–Toral–San Miguel (arXiv:1602.06935, PMC4837380) give noisy-voter network moment methods under annealed assumptions, not this exact full finite-star law. Pymar–Rivera (arXiv:2112.01478) analyze degree-weighted density and explicitly exclude stars from one regularity criterion. Caligiuri–Galla (arXiv:2305.19096) give a stronger **two-state limiting PDMP stationary-density method**, which in fact implies the asymptotic beta marginal here; accordingly the beta limit is contextual and not offered as an independent novelty. The previous discounted-disagreement star-pair calculation concerns two-point resolvents, not higher factorial moments or complete stationary count laws. General 1995 noisy-voter duality establishes exact correlation representations; inaccessible full text leaves some risk of an existing equivalent all-order finite-star recurrence. No absence-of-search-result is treated as a proof of priority.

## Value — PASS

A complete natural probability law on a standard heterogeneous interaction motif is a useful finite-size benchmark. The recurrence computes all stationary observables depending on hub state and opinion-one leaf count in a forward pass, with full probability inversion in quadratic arithmetic cost rather than exponential enumeration. Its all-order information goes beyond second moments and has a concrete reason to be retained: a small star may have a centrally peaked leaf-count histogram even when its growing-star beta limit is endpoint-peaked. This is an exact structural solution to a finite-state model, not a relabeling of the large-population switching theorem.

## Scientific limitations and residual priority risk

The model assumes a single hub, identically updating leaves and fair independent redraws. General noisy-voter duality might conceal an equivalent recursive specialization; the complete 1995 article was not accessible. Some later general switching-limit work covers the limiting density but not the full finite-size law. No independent verification or historical-priority review has occurred.
