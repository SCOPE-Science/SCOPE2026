For the ordinary Euclidean edge polytope of a wheel, every rim length except four has a unique maximal-ball center and inradius \(1/(\lfloor m/2\rfloor u+v)\), with \(u=\sqrt{m/(m+1)}\) and \(v=\sqrt{\lfloor m/2\rfloor(m+1-\lfloor m/2\rfloor)/(m+1)}\). The four-rim wheel is exceptional: its inradius is \(1/(u+2v)\) and its maximizing centers form a nondegenerate two-dimensional rectangle.
