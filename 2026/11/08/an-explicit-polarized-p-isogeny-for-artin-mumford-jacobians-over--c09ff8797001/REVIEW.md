# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
For odd primes, the two simple-pole Artin–Schreier extensions yield \(g(C)=(p-1)^2\) and \(g(D_t)=p-1\). The norm–pullback diagonal equals \([p]\). Cross terms vanish because distinct diagonal subgroups generate the full translation group and the common quotient is \(\mathbb P^1\). This yields an isogeny; Rosati duality proves the exact pulled-back polarization and \(p^{(p-1)^2}\) degree. Direct square-class point counting proves the arithmetic nonisogeny. The included verifier checks independent finite cases; it is not used to extrapolate the all-prime result.

Exact norm–pullback composition identities identify a specific integral correspondence; the full polarization and isogeny degree plus square-class distinction are the assessed refined claim. ## Mathematical value: PASS
The degree and principal-polarization pullback distinguish a precise characteristic-\(p\) Jacobian isogeny from the usual rational decomposition. They determine an exact maximal-isotropic finite kernel length for infinitely many ordinary Jacobians; the two \(\mathbb F_p\)-inequivalent quotient Jacobians give a concrete arithmetic factorization and different point counts. The result does not claim minimality of degree, simple factors, or complete group-scheme structure.
