# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The construction produces a tree and a well-defined edge switch. The four classes \((\{b\},U,V,Z)\) have distinct degrees and the same equitable quotient in both graphs, so color refinement cannot separate their vertices or the two graphs. The triangle formula follows from the two center cliques, and the switch has a rigorously verified loss of \(4m-4\) triangles. The proof is uniform for every \(m\ge2\); finite checks are supplementary.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** Targeted searches found no published statement presenting this explicit infinite tree-line-graph switching family or its exact triangle deficit. Arvind and collaborators' general characterization of amenability and their forest theorem are closest, and the current result does not claim those as new. The general characterization can detect individual examples, so novelty is specific to the explicit infinite family and uniform indistinguishable companions. Earlier unlocated line-graph closure counterexamples remain a priority risk.

## Value

**PASS.** The theorem isolates a natural failure of stability of graph identification under a basic graph operator. The contrast between amenable trees and their nonamenable line graphs is supported by infinitely many explicit witnesses, exact equitable quotient data, and an unbounded independently identifiable triangle deficit. The construction distinguishes class-relative identification from global amenability and shows how simple edge switching can preserve counting two-variable theory while changing subgraph counts.

## Limitations

No minimum order is asserted, and the switched comparison graphs need not themselves be tree line graphs. Independent historical and mathematical review have not occurred.
