# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The four-spine coloring sum accounts for every proper coloring: a fixed spine assignment has independent leaf choices. Swapping the two variable outer weights reduces the three-color difference to three explicit summands. Equality of \(D_0\) and \(D_2\) kills one, and the identity \(L_0L_1(L_1-L_0)=L_1L_2(L_2-L_1)\) cancels the others. The identical bipartition sizes imply the same \(t^2\) coefficient and therefore equality of the entire three-color \(t\)-refinement. For four colors the coefficient at \(q^8\) is polynomial of degree at most eight in the integer parameter once it is at least eight. Nine exact computations establish a constant two on this range, with separate exact boundary checks. Nonisomorphism follows from the unique spine composition up to reversal. The standalone integer-polynomial verifier replays every finite certificate; independent review remains unperformed.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

The motivating paper (Bingham et al., arXiv:2610.31785v1, Introduction and Sections 3–6) explicitly records the \(h=0\) four-spine collision, which is excluded from the novelty claim. Its four-spine discussion does not provide an unbounded continuation or the coefficient distinguishing the fourth specialization. The five-spine ordinary principal-specialization family of its Remark 3.11, and independent work addressing that family and an unrelated finite uniqueness counterexample, do not imply this one-parameter four-spine \(t\)-refined family. Search and comparisons across the available literature, mathematical finding index, and prior accessible claims found no covering statement. Priority is not certified; an unindexed equivalent formula remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The authors' isolated four-spine failure of third-specialization reconstruction is shown to persist for nonisomorphic caterpillars of arbitrarily large orders \(3h+9\), with a transparent local cancellation identity. Simultaneously an exact low-order coefficient separates every one of these pairs with four available colors, providing a rigorous infinite-family instance of the proposed fourth-specialization reconstruction threshold. This is a structurally motivated infinite distinction, rather than a finite recomputation of the known smallest pair.
