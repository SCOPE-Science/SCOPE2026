# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof derives each eigenspace from the unsigned incidence matrix, checks the coordinate PBH condition in both the kernel and nonkernel sectors, and gives strict cut-size bounds for all but one finite graph. It treats the equality cases of maximum cuts separately and analyzes the exceptional \(K_{2,2,2}\) graph both algebraically and through a complete exact enumeration of 924 candidates. Cauchy–Binet proves the weighted census; the four excluded complements are counted without relying on probabilistic sampling. Limitation: the theorem requires regular balanced multipartite graphs and unit weights.

## Originality

**PASS on inspected evidence, with publication-priority risk.** The published exact-controllability principle of Yuan et al. (2013), signless line-graph polynomial relations of Ramane et al. (2013), and the distinct switchboard edge-control model of Pang et al. (2017) do not state or imply this simultaneous classification of **coordinate leader placements** for balanced multipartite line graphs. Relevant earlier complete-graph line-graph and complete-bipartite line-graph results address different source graph families; neither covers the three-or-more-part family with parts of size at least two. The residual risk is an unlocated specialist classification for these exact graphs. Literature search absence alone is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Controlling a fixed edge-state network with separately addressable inputs is a natural placement problem, and the difference between arbitrary-column controllability and coordinate-input controllability is substantive. The result converts a high-dimensional PBH test to an exact combinatorial criterion across an infinite dense regular family, reveals a sharp six-vertex obstruction, and obtains an exact 496-placement count and component-weighted census. It goes beyond simply restating the known maximum-multiplicity lower bound.

## Closest literature and limits

Full HTML treatments were inspected for Yuan et al. (2013), Ramane et al. (2013), and Pang et al. (2017). The first gives a general exact-controllability framework; the second gives line-graph spectral identities; the third has a different undirected-edge switchboard state (two directional variables per physical edge), not this unit line-graph Laplacian. The classification makes no claims about control energy, unequal part sizes, publication priority across inaccessible specialist material, or independent verification.
