# An orbit of 1,280 multiplicity-twelve points on the dual Fermat quartic threefold

## Finding
Let \(V\subset\mathbf P^4_{\mathbb C}\) be the smooth quartic threefold
\[
F=X_0^4+X_1^4+X_2^4+X_3^4+X_4^4=0.
\]
Choose \(\alpha\in\mathbb C\) with \(\alpha^4=-1\). The hyperplane
\[
H_\alpha:\quad X_0+X_1+X_2+X_3+\alpha X_4=0
\]
has an **irreducible quartic surface section with precisely twelve ordinary double points and no other singularities**. The projective dual \(V^\vee\subset(\mathbf P^4)^\vee\) has multiplicity exactly \(12\) at \([H_\alpha]\), with a product of twelve distinct linear factors as tangent cone.

The natural diagonal–permutation projective automorphism subgroup \((\mu_4^5/\mu_4)\rtimes S_5\) carries \([H_\alpha]\) through an orbit of **exactly \(1280\) distinct hyperplanes**, each with this property. This counts a single orbit, not all hyperplanes of dual multiplicity at least twelve.

In rational coordinates on the section, its singular scheme is reduced and is the disjoint union of six degree-two closed points, each with residue field \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\), where \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\).

## Assumptions and scope
All geometric statements are over \(\mathbb C\), hence in characteristic zero. The quartic threefold \(V\) is smooth because its five partial derivatives never vanish simultaneously at a projective point. A hyperplane is a point of the projective dual space. For an ordinary node on a surface, the quadratic part in three local affine parameters has rank three. We do not assert the \(1280\) hyperplanes exhaust the full high-multiplicity locus.

## Proof
**1. Exact equation and complete singular locus.** On \(H_\alpha\), eliminate \(X_4\) by \(\alpha X_4=-S\), where \(S=x_0+x_1+x_2+x_3\). Since \(\alpha^4=-1\), the surface is
\[
G(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)=\sum_{i=0}^3x_i^4-S^4=0
\]
in \(\mathbf P^3\). The four partial derivatives are \(4(x_i^3-S^3)\). If all vanish, \(S\ne0\), since otherwise each \(x_i=0\), impossible in projective space. Normalize \(S=1\). Every \(x_i\) then belongs to \(\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}\), and their sum is one. Let \(n_0,n_1,n_2\) count these three values; the equation \(n_0+n_1\omega+n_2\omega^2=1\) with \(n_0+n_1+n_2=4\) forces \(n_1=n_2=1\) and \(n_0=2\). Thus the singular points are precisely the twelve permutations
\[
[1:1:\omega:\omega^2],\qquad\frac{4!}{2!}=12.
\]
Euler's identity ensures the vanishing gradient points lie on the quartic (also \(G=\sum x_i-S^4=1-1=0\) there). There are no additional singularities, including at infinity in any affine chart.

**2. Every point is an ordinary node.** The Hessian of \(G\), divided by \(12\), is
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(x_0^2,x_1^2,x_2^2,x_3^2)-S^2\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
At each singular point \(S=1\), each coordinate is nonzero and \(x_i^3=1\). Hence \(D=\operatorname{diag}(x_i^2)\) is invertible, with \(D^{-1}\mathbf1=(x_i)_i\); also \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}\mathbf1=S=1\). The rank-one perturbation \(M=D-\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\) has one-dimensional kernel generated by \((x_i)_i\), so rank three. Its induced quadratic form on the three-dimensional affine/projective tangent chart is nondegenerate. Thus every singularity is an \(A_1\) node. Since the singular locus is finite, the projective quartic cannot have two positive-degree factors (they would meet along a curve, making that curve singular). It is geometrically integral.

Over \(\mathbb Q\), complex conjugation interchanges \(\omega\) and \(\omega^2\), pairing the twelve normalized points without fixed points. Ordinary nodes give reduced Jacobian singular schemes, so the singular subscheme is a disjoint union of six copies of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q(\omega)\). This is a statement about the explicitly displayed rational section model; the chosen hyperplane itself is defined over a field containing \(\alpha\).

**3. Multiplicity of the dual.** The point \([H_\alpha]\) has exactly the twelve points above as tangencies on smooth \(V\). The standard duality formula \(\operatorname{mult}_{H}(V^\vee)=\mu(V\cap H)\) (in characteristic zero, for isolated section singularities) identifies its multiplicity with the total Milnor number. Every node has Milnor number one, giving \(\operatorname{mult}_{[H_\alpha]}(V^\vee)=12\). Equivalently, the Gauss map is immersive at each tangency and each yields a smooth local dual branch. Their twelve distinct tangent hyperplanes in dual tangent space correspond to the twelve distinct points \(p_\sigma\) on \(V\). In local dual coordinates \(\delta h\) modulo the line spanned by \(H_\alpha\), the tangent cone is, up to nonzero scalar,
\[
\prod_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Perm}(1,1,\omega,\omega^2)}
\left(\sum_{i=0}^3\sigma_i\,\delta h_i-\alpha^{-1}\delta h_4\right).
\]
Each factor is well-defined because \(H_\alpha(p_\sigma)=0\).

**4. Orbit cardinality.** The diagonal fourth-root actions modulo common scalar together with coordinate permutations form the displayed projective automorphism group. A vector of coefficients in its orbit has fourth powers in the ratio \((1,1,1,1,-1)\), up to rearrangement and a common scalar. There is one uniquely distinguished coordinate with opposite fourth power, giving five choices. Fixing its coefficient projectively to the chosen fourth root of \(-1\), each of the other four coefficients may independently be any fourth root of unity, giving \(4^4\) choices. Two such normalized vectors cannot define the same hyperplane: their distinguished coordinate fixes the projective scale. Thus the orbit has exactly \(5\cdot4^4=1280\) distinct members. Automorphisms preserve node types, tangencies and dual multiplicities.

## Relationship to prior work
Dimca and Ilardi (first public version, 2023) establish that a general degree-four hypersurface in \(\mathbf P^4\) has a four-nodal section and a multiplicity-four normal-crossing point on its dual, and that an arbitrary smooth hypersurface has a dual singularity of multiplicity at least four. Their result does not specify this Fermat threefold, a twelve-node hyperplane, or the orbit count. Dolgachev and Kondō (2025) recall the classical desmic surfaces with twelve nodes, but do not establish that the displayed hyperplanes of the Fermat threefold produce an orbit of 1280 multiplicity-twelve dual points. The quartic equation \(G=0\) itself is the Jacobi–Madden equation, already studied by Jacobi and Madden (2008) for rational solutions: the equation is not claimed to be new. The accessible abstract of that paper does not resolve priority for the singular scheme; its full text was inaccessible, which leaves a real historical-comparison risk. The new assertion here is the explicit incidence and singularity of the **dual Fermat threefold**, including the orbit size.

## Limitations
The construction gives one orbit/lower bound of 1280, **not a classification of every** dual multiplicity-twelve point or a proof that twelve is maximal. It does not claim that twelve-nodal quartic surfaces are a new class; nor does it classify the isomorphism types, lines or minimal resolutions of the section. The Diophantine equation itself and the general dual-Milnor formula are pre-existing. A historically equivalent construction of the Fermat dual orbit may exist in unindexed classical geometry.
