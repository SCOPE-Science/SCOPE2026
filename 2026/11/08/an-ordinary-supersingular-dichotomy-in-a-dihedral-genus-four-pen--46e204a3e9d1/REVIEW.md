# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The genus-four smoothness criterion follows from the squarefreeness of a reciprocal degree-ten polynomial in odd characteristic other than five. The reflection quotient is explicitly derived by invariant functions, and the differential eigenvectors establish a separable isogeny from the square of a genus-two Jacobian. For primes two or three modulo five, the Cartier–Manin matrix has a single four-cycle of possible nonzero entries, giving a strict ordinary-versus-rank-zero dichotomy. A genus-two Jacobian of rank zero is supersingular, so the squared isogeny supplies the Newton-polygon claim. In characteristic seven, direct coefficient expansion gives four monomial entries, dropping to rank two at exactly the three smooth parameters \(a=0\) or \(a^2=-1\). Both standalone exact-arithmetic checkers passed. Limits: the scripts test finite cases; the universal assertions rest on the proof.

## Originality — PASS with historical-priority risk
The closest structural work is Bruin–Kulkarni (2023), which characterizes the larger locus of hyperelliptic genus-four Jacobians isogenous to squares. It covers the kind of square decomposition in broad form but does not classify the inert-prime Newton polygons or the characteristic-seven degeneration in this explicit parameter pencil. Kudo–Nakagawa–Takagi (2022) study order-three genus-four pencils and Cartier algorithms; their formulas do not imply the order-five arithmetic classification as a direct corollary. Li–Mantovan–Pries–Tang (2018) concern other fivefold branch signatures and special cyclic-cover Newton polygons; the exact branch signature and parameter problem differ. Direct equation and parameter searches, as well as mathematical-record and accessible historical-claim comparisons, identified no stronger dominating arithmetic theorem. An earlier equivalent computation expressed via Chebyshev genus-two quotients is the principal residual priority risk; novelty is not certified by lack of search hits.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a one-parameter family with canonical order-five dihedral automorphisms. The result classifies the entire Newton-polygon type in every inert characteristic and pinpoints the complete exceptional locus over characteristic seven. It distinguishes the geometric rank-zero locus from superspeciality via the Cartier \(a\)-number. Such exact stratification can inform computations of Ekedahl–Oort types and decomposable Jacobian loci. The result does not enumerate isomorphism classes or all primes.
