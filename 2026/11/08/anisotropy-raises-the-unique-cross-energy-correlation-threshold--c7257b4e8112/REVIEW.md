# Scientific review: anisotropic cross-energy correlations

## Correctness

PASS. Exponential contraction defines a unique stationary process and uniformly bounded coordinates. The joint stationary covariance is reconstructed from conditional sphere fourth moments and the common Poisson-clock interval sums, with all factors of dimension, speed, rate, and scaling shown in RESULT.md. Closed first-interval renewal equations give an explicit rational function for the covariance. A second derivation using the stationary generator yields the same symbolic rational function. The denominator is strictly positive on the stated domain, so the polynomial sign test is legitimate. Strict coefficient-ratio ordering proves one crossing exactly when dimension exceeds four; an exact evaluation at the equal-stiffness threshold and a positive partial derivative prove strict anisotropy enhancement. The checker does symbolic identities rather than interpreting finite enumeration as a universal proof.

## Originality

PASS, subject to publication-priority risk. The full accessible primary text of Guéneau, Majumdar and Schehr derives an isotropic harmonic law and explicitly proposes anisotropic quadratic trapping as a natural extension, saying that one-coordinate marginals do not reconstruct the full joint law. The older Sun–Ye–Podgornik full preprint concerns a single isotropic trap stiffness and finite tumble durations, and does not display the unequal-stiffness cross-energy sign threshold. A 2024 anisotropic-turning study concerns anisotropic heading dynamics in free space rather than the same diagonal harmonic confinement. Existing isotropic fourth-moment sign results cover the equal-rate boundary and are excluded from novelty. Searches of indexed related findings and literature did not reveal the same anisotropic classification, but absence of a search hit is not proof of absolute priority. A general multivariate renewal or common-clock moment result might imply part of the explicit formula.

## Value

PASS. The cross-energy covariance detects correlations between two spatial degrees of freedom which their independent coordinate marginals cannot determine. A sharp dimension cutoff, unique noise/persistence threshold in all higher dimensions, and strict monotone shift with trap anisotropy constitute a complete structural sign diagram for this natural extension, not an arbitrary numerical slice. For dimension five, unequal rates \(1,2\) shift the critical redraw rate from \(9\) at equal mean stiffness to about \(9.32549\). This makes a testable qualitative distinction between competition induced by a fixed-speed heading and shared Poisson-clock variability. The result does not claim a singular thermodynamic transition.

## Closest literature and residual risks

- Anisotropic run-and-tumble-turn dynamics, *Soft Matter* (2024): different orientation dynamics; fourth moments without diagonal trapping.
- Prior isotropic fourth-moment and kurtosis identities imply the equal-stiffness boundary; only unequal-stiffness consequences are asserted as new.

The main originality risk is an unindexed multivariate extension of a known exact moment hierarchy. The principal applicability limitation is complete common-clock isotropic redraw with diagonal harmonic confinement and no thermal noise.
