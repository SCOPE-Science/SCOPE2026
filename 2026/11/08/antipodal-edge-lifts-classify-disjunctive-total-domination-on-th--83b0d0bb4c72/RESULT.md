# Antipodal edge lifts classify disjunctive total domination on the dodecahedral graph
## Finding
Let \(D\) be the twenty-vertex dodecahedral graph, isomorphic to the generalized Petersen graph \(GP(10,2)\). A set \(S\subseteq V(D)\) is *disjunctive totally dominating* (DTD) if every vertex, including vertices of \(S\), either has a neighbor in \(S\) or has at least **two distinct** vertices of \(S\) at graph distance exactly two. Let \(\gamma_t^d(D)\) denote the minimum size of a DTD set.

**Theorem.** The minimum is \(\gamma_t^d(D)=4\). There are precisely fifteen minimum DTD sets. If \(\tau\) is the unique antipodal involution of \(D\) and \(P=D/\langle\tau\rangle\) is its Petersen-graph quotient, then they are exactly the sets
\[
S_e=q^{-1}(\{a,b\})\quad(e=ab\in E(P)),
\]
where \(q:D\to P\) maps each antipodal pair onto one vertex. Each \(S_e\) induces two disjoint edges in \(D\).

Let \(\mathcal R_j\) be the reconfiguration graph with these fifteen sets as vertices, joining two distinct minimum sets when their symmetric difference consists of exactly \(j\) removed and \(j\) inserted vertices. Then
\[
\mathcal R_1\cong15K_1,\qquad\mathcal R_2\cong L(P).
\]
The first graph has no edges. The second has fifteen vertices, thirty edges, degree four at every vertex, one connected component, radius three, and diameter three. Thus one-token relocation cannot move any optimum, while two simultaneous relocations connect all optima.

## Assumptions and scope
Define the undirected simple graph \(D=GP(10,2)\) using vertices \(u_i,v_i\), \(i\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\), and edges \(u_iu_{i+1}\), \(u_iv_i\), \(v_iv_{i+2}\), with all indices modulo ten. Set \(\tau(u_i)=u_{i+5}\) and \(\tau(v_i)=v_{i+5}\). Then the quotient graph on the ten antipodal pairs is \(P=GP(5,2)\), the Petersen graph, via \(q(u_i)=U_{i\bmod 5}\) and \(q(v_i)=V_{i\bmod 5}\). The graph is cubic and has girth five, so each vertex has exactly three neighbors and six distinct vertices at distance two. All minima and reconfiguration statements concern this specified graph, not all dodecahedral-type graphs or any infinite family.

The rule is *total*: there is no automatic exemption for vertices in \(S\). The second alternative requires two distinct selected vertices at shortest-path distance exactly two, not two distinct length-two walks to the same selected vertex. A replacement of \(j\) vertices means that two optimum sets have intersection size \(4-j\); no intermediate nonoptimum configurations are permitted. Both moves are unrestricted replacements, not token sliding along graph edges.

## Proof
**Step 1 (universal lower bound).** Suppose \(S\) is a DTD set of size three. Every selected vertex is adjacent to exactly three vertices, so at most nine vertices of \(D\) have a selected neighbor. There remain at least eleven vertices with no selected neighbor. By the DTD property each of these eleven needs at least two *distinct* selected vertices at distance two, producing at least twenty-two incidences \((w,s)\) with \(w\) distance two from \(s\in S\). However every selected vertex has only six distinct distance-two neighbors, so there are at most eighteen such incidences. This contradiction rules out all three-element DTD sets. The DTD condition is monotone under adding vertices, so no smaller set is possible either. Consequently \(\gamma_t^d(D)\ge4\).

**Step 2 (fifteen constructions).** The quotient \(P\) is a Petersen graph, with diameter two and without triangles. Every quotient vertex outside an edge \(e=ab\) is either adjacent to one of its endpoints or lies at distance exactly two from both endpoints. If \(q(x)\) is adjacent to \(a\) or \(b\), the two-sheeted covering has an edge from \(x\) to a selected lift in \(S_e=q^{-1}(\{a,b\})\). Otherwise, shortest quotient paths of length two from \(q(x)\) to \(a\) and \(b\) lift to paths from \(x\) to two distinct selected lifts (distinct because the endpoints lie in different fibers). No shorter route exists in \(D\) in these cases, since one would project to a shorter route in \(P\). Thus every vertex satisfies the DTD condition. The fifteen edges of \(P\) yield fifteen distinct four-element sets. Combined with Step 1, the minimum is four.

**Step 3 (exhaustive upper classification).** The standalone script `verify_gp.py` constructs all thirty edges by the three displayed rules and checks all \(\binom{20}{4}=4845\) four-subsets directly against the exact DTD predicate, calculating adjacency and shortest-path-two masks. It finds exactly fifteen feasible four-subsets. It independently constructs the fifteen distinct edge lifts from \(\tau\) and verifies exact set equality, not merely equality of cardinalities. The second script `verify_icosahedral_dual.py` constructs a combinatorially different graph as the face-dual of the twelve-vertex icosahedron, derives antipodes as the unique vertices at distance five, and independently reconstructs all fifteen feasible sets and the same exact classification. The two finite checks constitute a complete exhaustive argument for the fixed twenty-vertex graph: each possible four-subset appears exactly once and every vertex is checked. This step is finite exhaustive proof, not an inference from sampled experiments.

**Step 4 (relocation geometry).** Distinct quotient edges are either disjoint or share exactly one quotient vertex. Their complete antipodal lifts therefore intersect in either zero or two vertices, never three. Hence \(\mathcal R_1\) is edgeless and \(S_e,S_f\) are adjacent in \(\mathcal R_2\) exactly when \(e,f\) share one endpoint in \(P\). This is the defining adjacency of \(L(P)\), giving the claimed isomorphism. Each edge of the cubic Petersen graph meets four other edges, so \(\mathcal R_2\) is four-regular, with \(15\cdot4/2=30\) edges. The embedded verifier performs breadth-first search from every state; all fifteen states are reached from each root and each eccentricity equals three. This proves both radius and diameter are three. Alternatively these last distances can be checked directly on the explicit fifteen-vertex line graph.

## Verification
The two standalone standard-library Python programs `verify_gp.py` and `verify_icosahedral_dual.py` can be run with `python3 verify_gp.py` and `python3 verify_icosahedral_dual.py`. Their stdout is embedded as `verify_gp.output.txt` and `verify_icosahedral_dual.output.txt`. The first uses a generalized-Petersen edge list and integer bitmasks; the second uses the dual of the icosahedron's twenty triangles, set-based neighborhoods and distances. Both verify all \(4845\) four-subsets, all possible one- and two-replacement relations, and fifteen-state distances. The programs also test all subsets of sizes one through three independently; the analytic lower bound does not depend on those tests. Inputs, graph constructions, predicates and state-space searches are self-contained.

## Relationship to prior work
Henning and Naicker introduced and developed disjunctive total domination, giving broad upper bounds and extremal graph characterizations [1,2]. Those general bounds do not determine the exact minimum-set multiplicity or the two-step reconfiguration graph for \(D\); in particular the previously known parameter definition and upper bounds cannot imply the asserted fifteen-state classification. Related disjunctive-total-bondage work [3] concerns edge deletion sensitivity rather than the present minimum-set classification. Ordinary total domination, disjunctive domination without the *total* requirement, and power domination are distinct parameters, so assertions for those parameters do not imply this theorem. The quotient line-graph correspondence is established here from the complete lifted-set classification, not claimed as a new fact about the abstract Petersen graph itself.

## Limitations
This is a precise finite-graph theorem with a complete finite enumeration certificate. It is not a proof of a family-wide classification, a characterization of all dodecahedral-graph invariants, or an independent peer review. Web-index searches and mathematical-record comparisons found no prior source covering these exact fifteen states or relocation graphs, but absence from searched records is not proof of first discovery. The complete text of [3] was not accessible and could conceivably contain related data; hidden or unindexed computations remain a residual historical-priority risk. The finite verification software is transparent and replayable, not a formal theorem-prover certification. The known general background definition of disjunctive total domination is not claimed as new.
