# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The cubic girth-five counting argument excludes every three-element disjunctive total dominating set (at least twenty-two demanded distance-two incidences versus at most eighteen possible). Lifted quotient edges give fifteen feasible four-element sets. Both self-contained implementations exhaust the \(4845\) four-element candidates, reconstruct exactly those fifteen sets, and calculate all legal replacement pairs and their exact graph distances. The quotient map and intersection characterization give a structural explanation of the state graph. This is finite exhaustive verification of a fixed graph, not a general proof for all related graphs.

## Originality — PASS (with stated priority risks)
The closest founding work by Henning and Naicker (arXiv:1409.1681v1, arXiv:1410.0187v1) defines and bounds disjunctive total domination but does not determine the exact optimal configurations or relocation graphs of the dodecahedron. The available Yi disjunctive-total-bondage abstract concerns removing edges, not full classification of minimum DTD sets. Searches under antipodal quotient, the dodecahedral graph, generalized Petersen aliases, configuration reconfiguration and fixed-cardinality relocation produced no equivalent statement. Earlier exact ordinary total-domination and power-domination invariants are different and do not imply this result. The full Yi article and unindexed tables remain priority risks; the search does not establish global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The dodecahedral graph is a canonical symmetric cubic graph with a natural antipodal covering of the Petersen graph. The theorem determines every cheapest redundancy-providing DTD placement and exposes a sharp mobility threshold: no single replacement preserves feasibility, yet two replacements connect all fifteen configurations in a well-known line graph. Both structure and reconfiguration are mathematically motivated, not arbitrary numerical slices.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
