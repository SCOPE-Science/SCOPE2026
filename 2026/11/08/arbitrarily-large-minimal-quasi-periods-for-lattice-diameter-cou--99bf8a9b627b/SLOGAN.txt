Even a lattice parallelogram with primitive edges can have arbitrarily large minimal quasi-period for the count of its maximal lattice-diameter segments: every integer \(q\ge2\) occurs, with an exact formula valid for all dilations. The largest and smallest residue-class leading coefficients differ by a factor of \(q\), forcing exponentially many entries in an explicit residue table relative to the polygon's binary input length, although a compact modular formula exists.
