# Scientific review: arbitrary-period three-agent SCOD cycles

## Correctness — PASS

The proof constructs, for every integer \(p\ge2\), exact rational orbit sites \(2^k/(2^p-1)\) and a uniform seven-component symmetric confidence set. At every point, the chosen stubborn neighbor is verified by strict even/odd arithmetic gaps; the ordinary opinion follows one \(x\mapsto(x+1)/2\) update and \(p-1\) updates \(x\mapsto x/2\). These distinct points form a prime period-\(p\) cycle. Every membership predicate has positive clearance; fixed itinerary neighborhoods map into each other with multiplier \(2^{-p}\), proving local attraction and small-perturbation persistence. The standalone exact-rational checker verifies the formulas for \(2\le p\le200\); the infinite quantifier rests on the proved inequalities, not enumeration. No stochastic or nonlinear global persistence property is implied.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge comparison

Zabarianska--Proskurnikov, arXiv:2408.01753v1 (2024), Example 4 already explicitly constructs a period-three orbit with two distinct stubborn opinions in symmetric confidence; its Theorem 1 covers convergence when stubborn opinions are identical. These are acknowledged as prior work. The submitted statement adds every prescribed **least** period, strict locally attracting dynamics and a constant upper bound of seven interval components. Targeted equivalent-form, stronger-coverage, exact-table and implication searches found no directly covering claim. The 2025 norm-ball study and a generic switching-graph result treat different hypotheses or observables. Prior-results search cannot prove priority; the 2025 complete manuscript and unindexed general switched-map constructions remain risks.

## Value — PASS

The initiating source names the characterization of stubborn-induced oscillations as an open direction. Arbitrarily large robust periods in the minimal three-agent system, without adding complexity to the confidence-set component count, answer a natural structural limitation question. The exact return multiplier \(2^{-p}\) and nonzero clearance show these are genuine attracting orbits, not isolated boundary-tie artifacts. The construction does not extend this conclusion to convex or norm-ball confidence sets, does not produce every period for a single fixed confidence set and gives no global basin classification.
