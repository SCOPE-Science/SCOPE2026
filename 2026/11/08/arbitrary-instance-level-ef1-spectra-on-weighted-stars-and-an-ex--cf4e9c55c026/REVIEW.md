# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The connected star partition has one hub-containing share, with all other shares singleton leaves. Deleting the largest-valued item is the best EF1 repair, so a candidate is feasible precisely when the minimum outside leaf exceeds the hub bundle's trimmed value. Replacing selected leaves by smaller outside leaves can only improve both sides of that inequality, proving the prefix criterion. When the hub exceeds the combined leaf value and leaves are strictly increasing and positive, every non-prefix selection fails; the unique feasible hub-leaf set yields exactly \(n!\) labeled assignments. A recursive increasing-integer construction realizes every prescribed intermediate feasibility word. A direct-definition verifier checks 320 sampled weighted cases, 510 full pattern words and 3,586 constructed pattern positions. All quantifiers and edge cases are explicit in RESULT.md. Computation is corroboration rather than an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk
The nearest primary paper, Chen and Zwicker, *Cutsets and EF1 Fair Division of Graphs* (arXiv:2402.05884, Section 5), analyzes **universal** profile-independent graph guarantees and conjectures a three-block universal spectrum. Its star calculations do not imply that one **fixed** strictly positive common valuation realizes every intermediate binary word, nor do they give the exact labeled-allocation census. Bei et al., *The Price of Connectivity in Fair Division* (arXiv:1908.05433, Section 4), concern worst-case existence and approximate fairness across all valuations. Hosseini et al., *Non-Monotonicity in Fair Division of Graphs* (arXiv:2511.03629, Introduction), use nonmonotone cut utilities and simultaneous transfer stability. Searches of arXiv-indexed literature, exact phrases, conceptual aliases, and mathematical finding records did not reveal a dominating claim. The mathematically relevant sections of all three primary full-text presentations were inspected. Remaining risk: an equivalent fixed-valuation star calculation may occur in an unindexed paper or an unexplored appendix; absence from search is not proof of priority.

## Value — PASS
The number-of-agents spectrum is a primary object in connected fair division. This theorem identifies a sharp contrast between universal guarantees and a single valuation instance: even a five-vertex star exhibits a feasibility hole, and arbitrary long binary patterns can occur without nonmonotone or heterogeneous valuations. The closed-form prefix criterion is constructive, and the exact \(0\)-or-\(n!\) count describes the full labeled feasible set for an infinite, naturally motivated weighted-star class. The flexibility result is structural, not an arbitrary enumerated numerical slice.

## Scope and residual risks
The theorem concerns ordinary EF1, which permits deletion of the center even if that disconnects the envied bundle. It does not claim an analogous result for outer-good EF1, heterogeneous valuations, or universal graph spectra. Independent external validation has not been conducted.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
