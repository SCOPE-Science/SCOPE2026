# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Over a splitting field, the cyclic translation action on derivations is diagonalizable because the field characteristic does not divide the odd quandle order. Each nonzero weight yields a quadratic polynomial vanishing on at least three distinct roots of unity, so it is zero. The residual weight-zero equation is both necessary and sufficient. Its antisymmetry and halving constraints reduce to one parameter per compatible signed-doubling orbit; freeness of the unit-group action proves the formula. Independent unrestricted matrix-rank calculations and direct Leibniz evaluations corroborate finite cases. The restrictions to odd order and coprime characteristic are explicit.

**Originality — PASS, with historical qualification.** The closest primary result, Elhamdadi et al. (2021), proves the odd-dihedral zero-derivation statement only in characteristic zero and lists positive-characteristic examples for orders at most six. It does not supply a general coprime-characteristic orbit formula, or the explicit nonzero examples at orders eleven and thirteen. Sangare–Ta (2026, primary MSC 17D99) gives automorphism and commutator-width theorems for this quandle-algebra family, not the positive-characteristic derivation Lie-algebra dimension. Bardakov–Elhamdadi (2026) cites the earlier characteristic-zero result, while Elhamdadi–Ta–Virgin (2026) investigates idempotents by Fourier methods. Searches of research records and related literature did not find a theorem implying the present entire cross-characteristic classification. The full 2026 Fourier paper could not be read and is retained as a specific historical-priority risk; missing text is not treated as evidence of noncoverage.

**Value — PASS.** The exact classification isolates the arithmetic mechanism for nontrivial derivations that is invisible to characteristic-zero theory: divisibility of explicitly defined powers of two. It determines the entire Lie-algebra structure and dimension uniformly over all odd quandle orders in characteristics prime to order, not just isolated tables. Its surprising order-eleven/characteristic-three example demonstrates substantive new behavior. This is a natural invariant of the nonassociative algebra, relevant to quandle-ring structural study.

The principal limitations are exclusion of even quandle orders and coefficient characteristics dividing the order, plus qualified historical priority of the recent Fourier paper.
