# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The derivation equations force diagonal operators and give the edge relation \(d_j=2d_i\). On an invertible connected support component, a nonzero solution imposes a cyclic \(h\)-coloring whose color classes have equal cardinality. Connected cyclic-block tuples are counted by a colored exponential formula; dividing by exactly \(h\) rotations gives the eligible connected-block count. Reweighting those components by one variable yields the stated bivariate series. The proof handles characteristic two separately and checks the \(n=0\), \(n<h\), and \(h\leq n<2h\) boundaries. The independent finite-field matrix enumerations, extension-field arithmetic, and formal-coefficient checks succeeded. The finite checks do not replace the full proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

The closest predecessor is Elduque–Labra, arXiv:1902.02191, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.3, which determine derivations for a fixed support graph. This precursor is openly acknowledged; the present new claim is the all-dimensional, all-finite-field enumerator over **invertible labeled structure matrices**. Its extra counting uses balanced cyclic block matrices, their connected decomposition, and a rotation quotient. Bekbaev, arXiv:2403.15104, addresses generic triviality and classifications rather than the stated distribution. The Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel survey, arXiv:2610.22248, discusses derivation theory and other finite-field averages but not this bivariate series. Wei–Zou, arXiv:2310.12368, treats isomorphism classes, not labeled derivation strata; only its abstract could be accessed. No exact covering statement was identified in the performed literature, database, or accessible historical-claim searches. An independently discovered equivalent formula or a broader enumeration of cyclic-partite invertible matrices remains a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Derivation-space dimension is a natural infinitesimal-symmetry invariant. This result gives its full finite-field distribution in arbitrary dimension rather than just a pointwise graph test, with arithmetic changes as the characteristic changes and a sharp nonzero-derivation dimension threshold \(h\). It also shows that every permitted dimension occurs and provides an exact first-window count. The novel enumerative factor is not determined by knowing only the ordinary connected-component or centroid-dimension distribution.

## Limits

Counts are for matrices in a fixed ordered natural basis, not for isomorphism classes. Full publication priority is not certified; complete inspection of Wei–Zou was unavailable, but its stated isomorphism-class target differs from the present invariant.
