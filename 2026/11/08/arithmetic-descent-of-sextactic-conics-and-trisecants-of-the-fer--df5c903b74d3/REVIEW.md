# Review of arithmetic descent of Fermat sextactic conics and trisecants

## Correctness
**PASS.** The theorem applies to every finite field of characteristic at least five. Divisor theory identifies the 27 sextactic points with \(E[6]\setminus E[3]\) and the 81 trisecant lines with unordered zero-sum triples. A six-subset of sextactic points defines one conic precisely when its six group elements sum to zero, and that conic is reducible precisely when the subset partitions into two zero-sum triples. These equivalences are characteristic-independent under the explicit hypotheses. The finite group enumeration checks all \(\binom{27}{6}=296010\) subsets, obtaining exactly 8244 conics, including 2268 reducible. The independent group-count proof derives 36 invariant conics in the three-cycle case and 76 in the involution case; counting Frobenius-stable pairs of disjoint trisecants gives 36 and 54 invariant reducible conics, hence 0 and 22 invariant smooth ones. Exact arithmetic over the characteristic-five, -seven and -31 splitting fields separately checks the point/line Frobenius types and incidences. Full finite-set cycle enumerations, rather than sampling, verify all conic orbit multiplicities. The universal claims rest on a three-case classification of Frobenius on the separable \(2\)- and \(3\)-torsion; finite computational evidence is not extrapolated to untested fields.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical-priority risk.** Szemberg--Szpond (arXiv:2211.00494, pp. 5--6) already obtained the 27 sextactic points, the 81 trisecants, and the geometric totals 8244 conics, 5976 smooth and 2268 reducible. None of those geometric counts is claimed as new. Merta--Zięba (arXiv:2406.13802, pp. 1--5) discuss other tangent and conic arrangements but not rational six-point conics over finite fields. Moe--Toft (arXiv:2412.16993, pp. 8--11) describe the geometric grid splitting of the same 81 lines, also over the complex numbers. In the relevant full-text sections of those papers, no Frobenius classification, rational smooth-conic counts \(0,22,5976\), or rational six-point-conic zeta functions were found. Web searches for '81 sextactic lines finite fields', 'Fermat cubic sextactic 81 lines Frobenius', and 'sextactic Fermat arithmetic finite field' likewise found no matching arithmetic classification. Published mathematical-record queries with the precise sextactic/trisecant/conic and Galois descriptions returned closest records on *Fermat cubic surfaces* or unrelated arithmetic curves, not this plane-cubic configuration. The residual risk is that such a complete Galois classification may be implicit in older explicit equations or unpublished arithmetic computations. This risk does not support a positive overlap claim against an identified existing theorem.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The six-point conics form a substantial, canonical configuration attached to the sextactic divisor of a well-studied elliptic plane curve. The answer distinguishes irreducible (geometrically smooth) from reducible rational conics. Its particularly strong arithmetic distinction—no rational smooth six-point conic when \(q\equiv1\pmod3\) and two is a noncube, but exactly 22 for \(q\equiv2\pmod3\)—is not a restatement of the geometric total 5976, and requires descent of unordered six-point subsets even where no underlying point is rational. The complete Frobenius cycle profile supplies explicit zeta functions and every extension-field count for four natural finite étale incidence configurations. These are motivated arithmetic invariants, rather than an arbitrary finite slice. The result does not classify intersection singularities of the conic arrangement or twists of the Fermat model.

## Sources and limitations
The earliest directly motivating preprint was posted November 1, 2022. Relevant sections of the three primary arXiv documents were inspected, and the complete comparisons above separate prior geometric enumeration from the newly derived arithmetic descent. Characteristics two and three are excluded; the stated Frobenius types cover every other finite field. The literature comparison is not an exhaustive historical census, and no independent mathematical audit has been conducted.
