# Scientific review: arithmetic modular idempotent classification

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof uses a vector-space identification of the nonassociative quandle algebra with a cyclic group algebra, retaining their distinct multiplications. A nilpotent-ideal argument excludes augmentation-zero idempotents. The truncated logarithm turns all augmentation-one idempotents into a linear kernel, and an explicitly diagonalized regular action of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) gives its dimension \(d=(p-1)/\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_p(2))\). The multiplication rule on this kernel gives the claimed core quandle. Every step is valid over any field of odd characteristic \(p\). The enumerations check examples, not universality.

## Originality

**PASS relative to inspected literature.** The known characteristic-three affine-line result is a strict special case and is acknowledged, not claimed as new. The original idempotent literature includes finite coefficient rings and provides some small-support obstruction results for dihedral quandles, but not the all-prime characteristic-equal-order count. Other prior work studies integral coefficient rings or nonmodular Fourier settings, which do not imply the present modular classification. The 2026 full-text study of core quandles investigates different objects and the integer ring of \(R_5\). No general modular formula with the arithmetic jump at \(p=17\) was found in checked sources. Priority remains provisional; unknown broader work is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The object is the naturally defined idempotent set of \(k[R_p]\) in the modular case, which previously exhibited exceptional behavior at \(p=3\). The theorem replaces isolated examples by a complete parameterization, exact finite-field counts, and an unexpected number-theoretic dimension governed by the multiplicative order of \(2\) modulo \(p\). It further identifies the induced quandle, giving information beyond counting. In particular \(p=17\) has a two-dimensional idempotent family and \(272\) nonbasis idempotents over \(\mathbb F_{17}\).

## Closest literature and limitations

Elhamdadi et al., arXiv:2204.11288, Section 3, are close because they explicitly treat finite quandles with coefficient rings in which quandle order may be noninvertible, yet prove only certain support restrictions for odd dihedral cases. Narayanan–Panja, arXiv:2206.10386, and Bardakov–Passi–Singh, arXiv:2001.06843, treat integral and selected small-quandle cases. A known result for \(R_3\) in characteristic three gives the \(d=1\) subcase. Churchill et al., arXiv:2608.09149, Section 4, solve integer-coefficient \(R_5\) questions. The theorem does not address composite-order quandles, nonmatching coefficient characteristics, or isomorphism-orbit enumerations. A broader but unidentified statement could imply the formula.
