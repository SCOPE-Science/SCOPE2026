# Review of the slant-monomial essential-singular-spectrum dichotomy

## Correctness — PASS
The proof uses the orthonormal Bergman basis and the norm-one slant coisometry. Bergman multiplication by a fixed monomial is a compact perturbation of the unilateral coordinate shift; hence the commutator has the same Calkin class as a difference of two explicit residue-class decimations. The source residues of the products coincide exactly when the two monomial exponents agree modulo the square of the slant order. When they differ, the Gram image is twice the identity modulo finite rank; when they agree, the nonzero corner is a difference of nontrivial integer shift powers, whose Calkin absolute spectrum is the entire interval from zero to two. All integers, including zero degrees and different-but-congruent degrees, are covered. The finite exact checks corroborate but do not replace the infinite-dimensional operator argument.

## Originality — PASS (qualified best-knowledge)
Cui–Li–Lu (arXiv:2610.09728v1, first public October 7, 2026) describe qualitative noncommutation and vanishing of finite-rank commutators. Liu–Lu (2013, DOI:10.1155/2013/473916) treat products and commutativity, but neither cited source was found to state the exact essential norm and two distinct essential spectra depending on degree difference modulo the square of the slant order. Singh–Singh–Singh (arXiv:2507.06546) discuss spectra of single slant operators and the normalized decimation shift. The older full open-access article was inspected; the newest paper's abstract and metadata were accessible, but its full text could not be inspected. General Calkin-algebra coverage remains a residual priority risk and independent priority certification has not occurred.

## Mathematical value — PASS
An arithmetic distinction completely changes a natural quantitative invariant for all pairs of analytic monomial slant operators and all slant orders: one phase has a two-point essential singular spectrum, while the other fills an interval. This refines qualitative nonessential commutation into a complete, explicit spectral phase diagram for the foundational test family; it is neither a finite-table fact nor a mere scalar restatement.
