# Arithmetic flux switching of dark states on lossy quantum rings

## Finding

Let \(C_n\), \(n\ge3\), be a finite coherent nearest-neighbor ring. A nonempty set \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) carries independent irreversible absorbers with arbitrary strictly positive loss rates. Every edge has the same nonzero hopping magnitude; Peierls phases may vary around the ring, and their total gauge-invariant flux is \(\Phi\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z\).

Fix any \(a\in S\), and put
\[
 g=\gcd\bigl(n,\{s-a:s\in S\}\bigr),\qquad q=n/g,\qquad h=\gcd(q,2).
\]
Here the gcd includes \(n\) and is positive, even for a singleton trap set. The full indefinitely loss-free invariant subspace \(\mathcal D\) has dimension
\[
\boxed{\dim\mathcal D=\begin{cases}
 (gh-\gcd(n,2))/2,&\Phi\equiv0\pmod{2\pi},\\
 (g-\mathbf1_{\{n\text{ odd}\}})/2,&\Phi\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}\text{ and }q\text{ odd},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]
The result is independent of all positive absorption rates. More strongly, for fixed flux the entire protected projector depends on the absorber locations only through their common residue anchor \(a\) and gcd \(g\): arbitrary absorber sets with the same generated subgroup and anchor have the same protected sector.

For an initially localized excitation \(\lvert r\rangle\), its exact infinite-time survival probability \(p_\infty(r)\) is as follows. Write \(t=r-a\pmod n\). For zero flux,
\[
\boxed{p_\infty(r)=\frac{gh}{2n}\,\mathbf1_{\{gh\nmid 2t\}}.}
\]
For half flux \(\Phi\equiv\pi\), the probability is zero if \(q\) is even. Otherwise it is
\[
\boxed{p_\infty(r)=\begin{cases}
0,&t\equiv0\pmod g,\\
g/n,&g\text{ even and }t\equiv g/2\pmod g,\\
g/(2n),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]
For every other flux it is zero. For any initial density matrix \(\rho\) supported on the ring, the eventual survival is exactly \(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\mathcal D}\rho)\), where \(P_{\mathcal D}\) is the orthogonal projector constructed in the proof. The mean of \(p_\infty(r)\) over initially untrapped sites equals \(\dim\mathcal D/(n-|S|)\) when \(|S|<n\).

In particular, when \(n\) is even and there is one absorber at \(a\), a particle initialized exactly opposite it survives with probability \(0\) at zero flux, with probability \(1\) at half flux, and with probability \(0\) at every other flux. With two antipodal absorbers, by contrast, half flux removes **all** dark states, while zero flux leaves \(n/2-1\) of them. Thus a single flux switch may either create a perfectly protected localized initial state or eliminate a macroscopic protected subspace, depending on absorber geometry.

## Assumptions and scope

There are \(n\ge3\) labeled sites with indices modulo \(n\). The single-excitation hopping Hamiltonian is
\[
H=J\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\bigl(e^{i\theta_j}\lvert j+1\rangle\langle j\rvert+e^{-i\theta_j}\lvert j\rangle\langle j+1\rvert\bigr),
\qquad J>0,\qquad \sum_j\theta_j\equiv\Phi\pmod{2\pi}.
\]
All sites have equal onsite energy, taken as zero since a common scalar shift changes no capture probabilities. The sole dissipation mechanism is a set of jump operators \(L_s=\sqrt{\gamma_s}\lvert\mathrm{vac}\rangle\langle s\rvert\) for \(s\in S\), where \(0<\gamma_s<\infty\); the vacuum is absorbing. No measurement backaction other than these irreversible channels, incoherent hopping, or dephasing is present. On the one-excitation sector the no-capture propagator is generated by
\[
K=H-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{s\in S}\gamma_s\lvert s\rangle\langle s\rvert.
\]
For normalized \(\lvert\psi\rangle\), its no-capture probability at time \(t\ge0\) is \(\|e^{-iKt}\psi\|^2\). The reported survival is its limit as \(t\to\infty\). Gauge transformations redistribute edge phases without changing localized or total survival probabilities.

## Proof

**1. Loss-free subspace is an orthogonal spectral kernel.** Let \(\Gamma=\sum_{s\in S}\gamma_s\lvert s\rangle\langle s\rvert\). Differentiating the squared norm gives \(-\langle\psi(t),\Gamma\psi(t)\rangle\). Any eigenvector of \(K\) with real eigenvalue must therefore have zero amplitude at every absorber and be an eigenvector of the Hermitian \(H\). Conversely, every eigenvector of \(H\) vanishing on \(S\) is unaffected by \(\Gamma\). Their span \(\mathcal D\) is a reducing subspace for both \(H\) and \(\Gamma\), hence also for \(K\). Its orthogonal complement contains no eigenvalue of \(K\) with vanishing decay rate; finite-dimensional spectral theory makes its propagation tend to zero exponentially, up to possible polynomial Jordan factors. Consequently the limiting capture complement is the squared norm of the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal D\), not merely a perturbative approximation. The conclusion uses only strict positivity, not equality, of the rates.

**2. Bloch degeneracies and absorber constraints.** A diagonal unitary gauge transform gives \(\theta_j=\Phi/n\). Relative to absorber \(a\), choose Bloch vectors
\[
 u_k(x)=n^{-1/2}e^{-2\pi i k(x-a)/n},\qquad
 Hu_k=2J\cos\bigl((2\pi k+\Phi)/n\bigr)u_k,
\quad k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}.
\]
If \(\Phi\not\equiv0,\pi\pmod{2\pi}\), these eigenvalues are all distinct. Every Bloch vector has nonzero amplitude at every absorber, so \(\mathcal D=\{0\}\).

At zero flux the degenerate partners are \(k\leftrightarrow-k\pmod n\). At half flux they are \(k\leftrightarrow-k-1\pmod n\). A self-partner spans only one Bloch vector and is never protected. For a distinct partner \(\ell\), the unique normalized linear combination vanishing at \(a\) is \((u_k-u_\ell)/\sqrt2\); it also vanishes at every other absorber precisely when
\[
 n\mid (k-\ell)(s-a)\quad\text{for every }s\in S.
\]
By Bézout's identity applied to \(g=\gcd(n,\{s-a\})\), this is equivalent to \(q\mid k-\ell\). Thus every admissible distinct pair contributes exactly one mutually orthogonal dark eigenvector, and these vectors are a complete basis of \(\mathcal D\).

**3. Counting dark pairs.** At zero flux the admissibility equation is \(q\mid 2k\). It has exactly \(g\gcd(q,2)=gh\) solutions \(k\pmod n\); exactly \(\gcd(n,2)\) are self-partners solving \(2k\equiv0\pmod n\). Dividing the remaining solutions by two gives \((gh-\gcd(n,2))/2\). At half flux, \(q\mid2k+1\) has no solutions for even \(q\) and exactly \(g\) for odd \(q\). There is exactly one self-partner when \(n\) is odd and none when \(n\) is even. The dimension formula follows.

**4. Exact localized probabilities.** For any contributing pair \(k,\ell\), the squared overlap of \(\lvert r\rangle\) with \((u_k-u_\ell)/\sqrt2\) equals
\[
\frac1n\bigl[1-\cos(2\pi(k-\ell)(r-a)/n)\bigr].
\]
At zero flux, summing over both members of each pair equals
\[
\frac1{2n}\sum_{k:\,q\mid2k}\bigl[1-\cos(4\pi k(r-a)/n)\bigr].
\]
The permitted \(k\)'s form the cyclic subgroup generated by \(q/h\) and have order \(gh\). Their character sum is \(gh\) exactly when \(gh\mid2(r-a)\), and zero otherwise. This proves the zero-flux formula, including self-pairs since their summands vanish.

At half flux, if \(q\) is odd, all admissible integers are \(k=(q-1)/2+qj\), with \(0\le j<g\). Thus the doubled-pair character sum is a geometric sum of \(e^{2\pi i(2j+1)(r-a)/g}\). It vanishes unless \(g\mid2(r-a)\); if this divisibility holds, it equals \(g\) for the residue class \(r-a\equiv0\pmod g\), or \(-g\) for the opposite half-residue class when \(g\) is even. Dividing by \(2n\) proves the stated three-case probability. If \(q\) is even, there are no admissible pairs. The special one-trap and antipodal-trap consequences follow by direct substitution. Since dark vectors vanish on every absorber, summing diagonal entries of their projector over all sites, or just nontrapped sites, gives \(\dim\mathcal D\); division by \(n-|S|\) gives the mean survival.

## Verification

The included programs use only standard Python, NumPy, and SciPy. Run from the directory containing the scripts:

```bash
python check_modular.py
python check_spectral.py
python check_flux_gauge.py
```

`check_modular.py` directly enumerates the Bloch partner congruences for every nonempty absorber subset through \(n=11\) and deterministic examples up to \(n=16\), confirming 8,358 dimension cases and 84,598 site-probability cases by an independent character sum. `check_spectral.py` diagonalizes full complex Hermitian hopping matrices and finds the kernel of the absorber restrictions inside each eigenspace by singular-value decomposition: 297 independent matrix-projector cases spanning zero, half and generic flux. `check_flux_gauge.py` independently randomizes the edge phases and positive absorption rates and checks 216 cases, including non-Hermitian finite-time lower bounds. These finite computations check the proof's formulas and convention choices, but the unbounded \(n\) statement rests on the algebraic argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Krovi and Brun (2006) identify the general connection between symmetry, degenerate eigenspaces, and infinite quantum-walk hitting times, but do not give the flux-dependent multi-absorber arithmetic classification of a finite uniform ring. Agliari, Mülken and Blumen (2009), especially their formulas for ring-trap degenerate perturbation and periodically positioned traps, establish important **zero-flux** antecedents. In particular, the zero-flux periodic-site counts and the underlying idea of zero-decay eigenvectors are not novel and are not claimed as such. Their stated ring calculations take even ring size, uniform trapping rates, and no Peierls flux; they do not state the arbitrary-absorber gcd-and-flux classification or exact point-initialized survival law. Xu, Ide and Konno (2012) analyze trapping on a ring with an extra edge and a single trap, which is a different hopping topology. Gou and collaborators (2020) study a dissipative flux-threaded triangle coupled to an extended system, giving a physical flux/loss context but not the stated closed uniform-ring all-size classification.

The new mathematical content assessed here is the combined all-size flux trichotomy, all-pattern arithmetic criterion, complete dark projector, localized exact probabilities, and exact zero-versus-perfect survival switches under unequal positive losses. Its elementary spectral ingredients are known; the novelty assessment is conditional on the inspected literature and is not a priority certification.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes exactly equal magnitudes of ring-edge hopping and equal onsite energies. True hopping-magnitude disorder, site-energy disorder, incoherent scattering, and positive dephasing can destroy the protected states, and no result for those models is claimed. No uniform nonzero decay gap as \(\Phi\) approaches a special flux is asserted: at non-special flux capture may be arbitrarily slow. The flux switch concerns the infinite-time limit on a fixed finite network. It is not a thermodynamic phase transition, nor an estimate of mean first-capture time. An unindexed equivalent flux-resolved absorber calculation remains a historical priority risk.
