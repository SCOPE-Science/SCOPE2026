# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim is a theorem for every integer \(n\ge3\), every nonempty absorber set, positive rates, and any real flux. The proof separates a loss-free invariant reducing subspace from the strictly decaying sector using dissipativity and finite-dimensional spectral theory; Fourier degeneracy pairing then converts every absorber restriction to the divisibility \(n/g\mid k-\ell\). Exact congruence counts give both flux cases. Orthogonal-projector diagonal entries and finite character sums give site-specific probabilities. Three reproducible programs check the dimensional and probability identities, full complex Hamiltonian eigenspaces, nonuniform edge-phase gauges and positive rates. Mathematical risk: the result does not extend to hopping disorder or dephasing.

**Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with residual priority risk.** Krovi–Brun (2006) covers symmetry-induced invariant subspaces broadly. Agliari–Mülken–Blumen (2009) has particularly close zero-flux ring-trap perturbation formulas (its equations 22–26) and exact zero-decay observations for periodic traps; those antecedents are explicitly not claimed new. Neither inspected treatment contains the combined arbitrary-site and nonzero-flux arithmetic classification, point-initialized piecewise probabilities, or rate-independent half-flux opposite-site switch. Xu–Ide–Konno (2012) changes the network by an extra edge. Gou et al. (2020) has a dissipative flux-threaded triangle coupled to leads, not the closed-ring all-size theorem. Database searches and matching nearby published statements did not establish implication of the full result; non-indexed equivalent results remain possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The formulas classify all absorber placements by an integer invariant and identify a practical, finite-network mechanism for turning protected transport on or off with a gauge flux. The exact dichotomy between a single trap and two antipodal traps under half flux is not a generic linear-algebra bookkeeping fact; it gives a sharp and testable interference-based absorber-design criterion. The contribution is a finite coherent network classification and not a phase transition or a claim about disordered transport.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
