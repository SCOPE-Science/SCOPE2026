# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The simplicial domain is explicit for every finite abelian group and nonzero shift with proper generated subgroup. Every face has an unambiguous coset pair; colorful edges cannot belong to two distinct sheets. Translation identifies each sheet with the two-layer complex on the cyclic subgroup. At each vertex of a sheet, the two perfect matchings in its link alternate along a generating translation, forming one \(2m\)-cycle. Opposite triangle orientations prove orientability and Euler's formula gives genus \((m-1)(m-2)/2\). The incidence graph is connected with \(d^2+3N\) nodes and \(3Nd\) edges; the graph-of-spaces lemma gives exactly the displayed wedge. Integral homology and fundamental groups follow without finite-computation extrapolation. The standalone checker independently reconstructs examples and calculates mod-two boundary ranks; its limit is finite testing only.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Ayzenberg–Khoroshavkina's generalized-inflation theorem applies to flabby sheaves; the two-level color-assignment sheaf here is nonflabby by a concrete restriction failure from a simplex to its boundary. A published Latin-array result covers one allowed sum level, whose three-color complex collapses to a graph; it neither treats double layers nor the surface normalization. A separate published two-adjacent-affine-layers theorem computes integral homology for unit difference \(1\) over \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), and **explicitly excludes nonunit differences**. The present theorem resolves precisely that excluded arithmetic regime and strengthens the conclusion to integral homotopy, for arbitrary finite abelian groups. Classical cyclic Latin-square biembeddings classify connected surface sheets (difference generating the cyclic group); they do not imply the singular multi-sheet vertex identifications and free-loop count for proper generated subgroups. The historical literature on singular Latin bitrades may contain unindexed equivalent descriptions; no source search proves global uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The shift's order—not the number of faces—governs a sharp structural change: for the same \(18,108,72\) face vector, shifts \(1,2,3\) on \(\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\) produce a genus-ten surface, four tori wedged with fifteen circles, and nine spheres wedged with twenty-eight circles. Determining the normalized surfaces, the exact graph-of-spaces loop correction, the integral homotopy type and nonfree fundamental groups addresses a natural motivated boundary explicitly left outside the adjacent-affine-layers theorem. The result is not a routine parameter substitution, a restatement of the flabby theorem, or a bare Betti-number calculation.

The theorem does not address nonabelian multiplication, more allowed sums, or canonical equivariance.
