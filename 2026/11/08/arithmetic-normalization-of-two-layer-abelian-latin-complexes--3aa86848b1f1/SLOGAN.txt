Two shifted Latin-sum layers on three copies of a finite abelian group decompose, when the shift generates a proper subgroup, into \(d^2\) closed orientable genus-\((m-1)(m-2)/2\) surface sheets wedged with \(3N(d-1)-d^2+1\) circles, where \(m\) is the shift order and \(d=N/m\). This completely determines integral homotopy and shows why replacing a unit shift by a nonunit shift can create many surface components even though the simplicial face vector stays unchanged.
