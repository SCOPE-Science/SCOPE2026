# Arithmetic rank and image-code spectra of inner derivations of generalized dihedral group algebras

## Finding
Let \(H\) be any finite abelian group of odd order \(n\), with exponent \(e\), and let \(q\) be a prime power coprime to \(n\). Form the generalized dihedral group \(G=H\rtimes C_2\), where the nonidentity element of \(C_2\) acts by inversion, and put \(A=\mathbb F_q[G]\). For \(x\in A\), let \(D_x(y)=xy-yx\). Inner derivations are counted as distinct linear maps, not as choices of \(x\).

For each divisor \(m>1\) of \(e\), define \(a_m=|\{h\in H:\operatorname{ord}(h)=m\}|\), let \(d_m\) be the least positive integer satisfying \(q^{d_m}\equiv 1\) or \(-1\pmod m\), and set \(b_m=a_m/(2d_m)\). The numbers \(b_m\) are nonnegative integers. Define the inner-derivation rank polynomial and distinct-image polynomial by
\[
R_{H,q}(T)=\sum_{D\in\operatorname{InnDer}_{\mathbb F_q}(A)}T^{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q}D},\qquad
I_{H,q}(T)=\sum_{W\in\{\operatorname{im}D:D\in\operatorname{InnDer}_{\mathbb F_q}(A)\}}T^{\dim_{\mathbb F_q}W}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{R_{H,q}(T)=\prod_{\substack{m\mid e\\m>1}}\bigl(1+(q^{3d_m}-1)T^{2d_m}\bigr)^{b_m}},\qquad
\boxed{I_{H,q}(T)=\prod_{\substack{m\mid e\\m>1}}\bigl(1+(q^{2d_m}+q^{d_m}+1)T^{2d_m}\bigr)^{b_m}}.
\]
The empty product is \(1\), including when \(H\) is trivial. Thus there are \(q^{3(n-1)/2}\) distinct inner derivations, and their possible ranks are precisely the subset sums of the multiset having \(b_m\) copies of \(2d_m\) for each \(m\). The largest rank is \(n-1\).

For each nonzero image space arising from a single factor indexed by \(m\), exactly \(q^{d_m}-1\) distinct nonzero inner derivations have that image. More generally, for a prescribed global image subspace whose active factors form a set \(S\), the number of distinct derivations with that image is \(\prod_{j\in S}(q^{d_j}-1)\) (with factor indices counted with multiplicity). In particular the number of rank-two inner derivations is
\[
(q^3-1)\,\frac{|H[q-1]|+|H[q+1]|-2}{2},
\]
where \(H[t]=\{h\in H:h^t=1\}\). A nonzero rank-two inner derivation exists exactly when a nonidentity element of \(H\) has order dividing \(q-1\) or \(q+1\). These assertions include characteristic two.

## Assumptions and scope
The multiplication and ranks are over the ground field \(\mathbb F_q\), not its algebraic closure. The group \(H\) is abelian of odd order; \(\gcd(q,|H|)=1\). These conditions allow characteristic two but exclude modular characteristic on \(H\). All image codes are literal \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear subspaces of the fixed group algebra, counted without identifying them under coordinate changes. An expression such as \(H[t]\) uses the indicated torsion subgroup. The number \(a_m\) can be computed from the group type by \(a_m=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)|H[d]|\). No claim is made about outer derivations or about cases violating the hypotheses.

## Proof
**1. Arithmetic simple factors.** Since the characteristic does not divide \(|H|\), the commutative algebra \(B=\mathbb F_q[H]\) is finite étale. Over an algebraic closure, its primitive idempotents are indexed by characters \(\chi:H\to\overline{\mathbb F}_q^\times\). Field Frobenius sends \(\chi\) to \(\chi^q\), and the inversion automorphism sends it to \(\chi^{-1}\). A nontrivial character of order \(m\) has an orbit of exactly \(2d_m\) under the group generated by these two commuting actions; in particular, its orbit may be a single Frobenius orbit stable under inversion or a pair of Frobenius orbits exchanged by inversion. Character duality gives exactly \(a_m\) characters of order \(m\); hence this orbit count is \(b_m\). Characters are grouped by disjoint such orbits, making all \(b_m\) integers.

**2. Matrix-block decomposition.** Since \(A=B\rtimes C_2\) is the skew group algebra for inversion, the trivial character contributes the commutative algebra \(\mathbb F_q[C_2]\). Consider a nontrivial signed Frobenius orbit with parameter \(d=d_m\). If inversion exchanges the two Frobenius orbits, the corresponding algebra is \((K\times K)\rtimes C_2\cong M_2(K)\), with \(K=\mathbb F_{q^d}\). If inversion stabilizes one Frobenius orbit, the corresponding étale algebra is \(L=\mathbb F_{q^{2d}}\), inversion is its unique involutory \(K\)-automorphism with fixed field \(K=\mathbb F_{q^d}\), and \(L\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\cong\operatorname{End}_K(L)\cong M_2(K)\). The latter isomorphism maps \(a+bs\) to \(v\mapsto av+b\bar v\), and is an isomorphism by independence of field automorphisms and dimension; it also works in characteristic two. Therefore
\[
A\cong\mathbb F_q[C_2]\times\prod_{\substack{m\mid e\\m>1}}M_2(\mathbb F_{q^{d_m}})^{b_m}.
\]
The first factor is commutative, including in characteristic two. The displayed decomposition has dimension \(2+\sum_m4d_mb_m=2n\), as required.

**3. Ranks and multiplicities.** Fix one block \(M_2(K)\) with \(Q=|K|=q^d\). The kernel of \(\operatorname{ad}_x\) is the centralizer of \(x\). When \(x\) is scalar, \(\operatorname{ad}_x=0\). Otherwise \(x\) has minimal polynomial of degree two, and its full matrix centralizer is the two-dimensional \(K\)-space \(K I+Kx\). Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q}\operatorname{ad}_x=2d\). Distinct maps correspond exactly to cosets of the scalar center, so one block supplies one zero derivation and \(Q^3-1\) distinct nonzero derivations. The direct-product decomposition makes the blocks independent and gives the rank polynomial. As \(\sum_m2d_mb_m=n-1\), the total number is \(\prod_m q^{3d_mb_m}=q^{3(n-1)/2}\).

**4. Image subspaces.** The pairing \((u,v)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(uv)\) on \(M_2(K)\) is nondegenerate in all characteristics. Its cyclic trace identity shows that \(\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{ad}_x)=C(x)^\perp\). For nonscalar \(x\), the centralizer \(C(x)=KI+Kx\) is a two-dimensional \(K\)-subspace containing \(KI\). Conversely every such two-plane is the centralizer of any element chosen outside \(KI\), because every nonscalar matrix has exactly this centralizer. Thus distinct nonzero images correspond to the one-dimensional subspaces of \(M_2(K)/KI\), of which there are \((Q^3-1)/(Q-1)=Q^2+Q+1\). For each such image there are precisely \(Q-1\) nonzero inner derivations. Independence across block summands yields the second product and the stated multiplicity per global image.

**5. Arithmetic rank-two threshold.** A rank-two summand requires \(d_m=1\), or equivalently \(q\equiv\pm1\pmod m\). Such nonidentity character elements are counted by the union of the two torsion subgroups \(H[q-1]\) and \(H[q+1]\). Their intersection is trivial, since \(|H|\) is odd and \(\gcd(q-1,q+1)\mid2\). Removing the shared identity and pairing inverse characters gives \((|H[q-1]|+|H[q+1]|-2)/2\) contributing blocks. Each block contributes \(q^3-1\) rank-two maps, and simultaneous activation of two blocks has rank at least four. This proves the count.

## Verification
The included standalone programs implement finite computations without relying on any unproved classification. `verify_binary.py` directly forms the multiplication table of \(H\rtimes C_2\), exhaustively enumerates all group-algebra elements over \(\mathbb F_2\) for \(H=C_3,C_5,C_7,C_3\times C_3\), computes ranks by binary elimination, obtains distinct image spaces by canonical elimination, and matches both products. `verify_fields.py` directly counts all elements for \(H=C_3\) over \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_5\), and samples the \(H=C_5\) case over \(\mathbb F_4\), checking field-matrix ranks. `verify_images.py` separately enumerates canonical images over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_4\), and \(\mathbb F_5\) for small cyclic kernels. Run these with Python 3 from the folder containing all three files. Successful completion prints `VERIFY_OK`, `VERIFY_FIELDS_OK`, and `VERIFY_IMAGES_OK`, respectively. Exhaustive finite checks do not prove the all-groups, all-fields assertion; Steps 1–5 do.

For example \(H=C_5,q=2\) gives \(R(T)=1+63T^4\) and \(I(T)=1+21T^4\). For \(H=C_3\times C_3,q=2\) the polynomials are \(R(T)=(1+7T^2)^4\) and \(I(T)=(1+7T^2)^4\). For \(H=C_3,q=4\) they are \(R(T)=1+63T^2\) and \(I(T)=1+21T^2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Creedon and Hughes (first public preprint August 16, 2018) study derivations on group algebras, including bases for inner derivations in dihedral cases and coding-theoretic constructions of image codes. Manju and Sharma (first public preprint December 19, 2023) describe derivations and bases of inner derivations for dihedral and related group algebras over various fields. These source results are not claimed here. The rank and distinct-image enumerators stated above refine those questions for the natural generalized-dihedral odd-abelian-kernel family, using the arithmetic of Frobenius/inversion character orbits; neither inspected source furnishes these complete enumerators. The simple-factor decomposition and matrix-centralizer method use established algebraic techniques, not new structural claims about semisimple algebra theory.

## Limitations
The result concerns **inner derivations only**, and does not classify all derivations in modular characteristic two, outer derivations, arbitrary group kernels, or fields with characteristic dividing \(|H|\). The image polynomial counts distinct subspaces, not equivalence classes under algebra automorphisms or their code weight enumerators. Although the two displayed products are proved by the argument, a broader literature theorem on inner-derivation rank/image spectra might imply them; publication priority is not independently certified. Small-field computations are consistency checks, not substitutes for the universal proof.
