# Review of the wheel-wave temporal classification

## Correctness

PASS. The proof reduces the rim shift to its one-primary part and invertible complement, identifies the hub's modular adjacency blocks in all two-adic cases, converts each spatial Jordan block to the exact temporal companion block, and computes the resulting fixed dimensions. The odd case accounts for the separate polynomial \(X^2+X+1\) rather than incorrectly treating the hub as nilpotent. Formulas, sharp global orders, allowed periods, and Möbius inversion are all derived for every stipulated parameter with rim size at least five. Small rim lengths three and four are checked but not asserted as the original contribution. Two separately implemented exact finite checks agree on 751 binary fixed-space tests and on direct enumeration of 349440 binary paired states. No finite test is presented as an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS, with historical-priority risk. In Tadaki (1994), the first-order cylindrical rules have a nilpotent behavior at powers of two, but no wheel hub and no reversible paired-time census. Macfarlane (2004) supplies the second-order framework for one-dimensional cellular automata, not the wheel arithmetic phase diagram. Abu-Jeib (2006) resolves real wheel eigenvalues/determinants, not characteristic-two Jordan blocks or reversible fixed-point populations. Targeted public searches and statement/implication comparisons found no exact wheel-wave theorem. Previous first-order wheel nilpotent analyses and reversible ring-wave analyses share constituent algebra but neither independently states the all-rim wheel-wave fixed-point formula, the extra three-resonance for odd rims, or the four distinct global-order regimes. Unindexed work and equivalent modular matrix descriptions remain plausible priority risks; this is not an independent literature certification.

## Mathematical value

PASS. A wheel is a canonical graph perturbation of a cycle by one universal vertex. The exact all-time return counts and complete primitive-orbit populations provide a natural recurrent-state invariant for every rim length, not a selected finite sample. The hub's effect changes discontinuously with the two-adic valuation of rim length and with divisibility by three; the resulting order multipliers (one, two, three or four) are useful structural information unavailable from ordinary wheel determinant formulas or generic reversibility alone. The absence of period two on all odd rims is an additional sharp dynamical restriction. No general claim for arbitrary graph joins is inferred.
