# Scientific review

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The claim defines a continuous infinite-time minimum chord between distinct unitary spectral values. The Paley spectral formula follows from the quadratic Gauss sum or the stated strongly regular identity. For nonsquare orders, the two spectral differences are linearly independent over the rationals and continuous Kronecker–Weyl equidistribution applies to a continuous observable. The Dirichlet minimum-gap law gives the exact universal integral, while the square-order cases reduce to a periodic trigonometric minimum; exact interval bounds certify the strict ordering of the two explicit resonances. Recomputed `verify.py` checks finite-field relations and rational enclosures, but no finite test substitutes for the proof.

**Originality (O): PASS, qualified.** Searches for long-time QAOA unitary eigenvalue-separation, Paley quantum-walk spectral phase averages, arithmetic resonances, and Dirichlet circular spacings found the recently introduced heuristic [1], known Paley spectra [2,3], and classical minimum-gap probability [5]. The distinct all-Paley nonsquare law and the square-order \(q=9\) versus \(q=49\) opposite-side split were not found as prior statements. The source abstract of [1] enumerates complete graphs, hypercubes, cycles, and balanced tori, not Paley graphs. However, its full text was inaccessible, so this is qualified best-knowledge originality, not proof of uniqueness or priority. The closest indexed record about a projective spectral-gap expression concerns a different functional of a unitary, not the time-averaged minimum chord.

**Value (V): PASS.** This is not merely a recomputation of Paley eigenvalues: it shows that a recently proposed dynamical spectral-gap heuristic is generically independent of Paley graph order but can shift above *or below* the generic value at integral-spectrum orders. The explicit small 9- and 49-vertex witnesses establish an arithmetic sensitivity in a natural, infinite, strongly regular graph family, which matters to interpreting spectral-only QAOA mixer rankings. It makes no claim about algorithm performance.

**Closest literature and risk.** [1] introduces the heuristic but its full manuscript could not be inspected; [2,3] supply spectral background and [5] the classical probability ingredient. A possibly broader unindexed result about unitary eigenphase equidistribution or a theorem inside the unavailable manuscript could imply part or all of this classification. These remain publication-priority limitations rather than mathematical proof gaps.
