# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. Identical unit costs reduce WEF1 to the exact linear-fraction inequality \((x_i-1)/w_i\le x_j/w_j\) for a nonempty recipient and reduce ex-ante WEF to \(\mathbb E[X_i]=mw_i\). The zero-coordinate, hypersimplex and single-extra-item arguments establish the sharp first three regimes. The critical all-counts exchange inequality (3) enforces an exact rational weight ratio along any supported exchange and is verified independently using exact fractions. Component-wise conservation of count sums and positive rational edge ratios prove uniqueness and rationality for any fixed finite support; only finitely many support sets exist. The two-person integrality alternative follows from an exact length-one integer interval. The finite checker provides additional corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS under inspected literature and actual semantic-index searches, with a concrete residual risk. Lin–Wu–Zhou (arXiv:2609.13970, §4) prove a two-agent/two-chore unequal-weight obstruction, matching only the \(m=n=2\) boundary, not the all-counts finiteness theorem, the exact \(m=n+1\) weight list, nor the two-agent formula for arbitrary chore counts. Wu–Zhang–Zhou (arXiv:2301.08090) concern deterministic WEF1; Hoefer–Schmalhofer–Varricchio (arXiv:2209.03908) guarantee the weaker ex-post transfer notion; weighted-picking-sequence work is likewise not an ex-ante characterization. One related mixed-manna preprint's full text could not be inspected, and unindexed equivalent apportionment or lottery decompositions remain a risk. The novelty assessment does not infer uniqueness from failed search alone.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The motivation is the concrete incompatibility between exact weighted ex-ante fairness and individually WEF1 realizations in the recent binary-chore literature. A sharp transition from a positive-dimensional feasible simplex to finitely many rational exceptions, including an explicit classification of all \(2^n-1\) exceptions at the first supercritical chore count, is a structural characterization rather than a small numerical increment. The all-population exchange-rigidity mechanism is reusable in related discrete allocation and apportionment questions. The precise two-agent congruence law also quantifies exactly how exceptional feasibility is.

## Limits and closest literature

The theorem covers complete allocations of identical unit-cost chores only. Source priority cannot be assured against inaccessible or unindexed work. Verification uses finite rational-grid enumeration and numerical LP checks in addition to an analytical proof; neither the enumeration nor numerical optimization is represented as universal proof. No external independent scientific validation has been performed.
