For rectangular fixed-cardinality assignment polytopes, Euclidean inball centers undergo two different aspect-ratio transitions: below the maximal matching rank a row-constrained center polytope can have dimension \(m(n-1)\), while at maximal rank the center polytope can be a scaled rectangular transportation polytope of dimension \((m-1)(n-1)\). Exact inradii, phase thresholds, and tangent-facet counts determine when these families collapse to the unique uniform incenter.
