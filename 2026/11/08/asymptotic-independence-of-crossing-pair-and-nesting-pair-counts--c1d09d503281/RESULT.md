# Asymptotic independence of crossing-pair and nesting-pair counts in uniform matchings

## Finding
Let \(M_n\) be a uniformly random perfect matching on the ordered points \(1,\ldots,2n\). For each unordered pair of its \(n\) edges, classify their four endpoints as a **crossing** (alternating), **nesting** (one edge's interval properly contains the other's), or **alignment** (disjoint intervals). Denote their counts by \(X_n,N_n,A_n\), respectively. For all integers \(n\ge2\),
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathbb E X_n=\mathbb E N_n=\mathbb E A_n=\frac13\binom n2,\\
\operatorname{Var}(X_n)=\operatorname{Var}(N_n)=\frac{n(n-1)(n+3)}{45},\\
\operatorname{Cov}(X_n,N_n)=-\frac{n(n-1)}{18},\qquad
\operatorname{Corr}(X_n,N_n)=-\frac{5}{2(n+3)}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Nevertheless, in the joint distribution,
\[
\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\left(X_n-\frac13\binom n2,\ N_n-\frac13\binom n2\right)
\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm d} (G_X,G_N),
\qquad G_X,G_N\text{ independent }\mathcal N(0,1/45).
\]
The complete three-class limit is obtained from \(A_n=\binom n2-X_n-N_n\): its normalized coordinates converge to \((G_X,G_N,-G_X-G_N)\). **The asymptotic joint independence of the pair counts, rather than their finite enumerating function or their separate marginal normal laws, is the assessed statement.**

## Assumptions and scope
The sample space consists of the \((2n-1)!!\) perfect matchings on \(2n\) distinct ordered positions, with equal probability. An alignment uses the linear order; all endpoints are distinct. The quantities count *pairs of edges*, not the largest number of mutually crossing or nested edges. For \(n=1\), all counts are zero and the Pearson correlation is undefined; the central limit assertion concerns \(n\to\infty\).

## Proof
**Uniform independent-chord representation.** Sample \(n\) independent unordered endpoint pairs \(Z_i=(U_i,V_i)\), where \(U_i<V_i\) are the sorted values of two independent uniform \((0,1)\) variables. Sort all \(2n\) endpoints by value and replace them by ranks. The resulting unlabeled matching is uniform: any fixed matching is generated by exactly \(n!2^n\) equally likely endpoint-label assignments. Write \(h_X(Z_i,Z_j)\) and \(h_N(Z_i,Z_j)\) for the crossing and nesting indicators, so \(X_n=\sum_{i<j}h_X(Z_i,Z_j)\) and analogously for \(N_n\). In a comparison of four distinct endpoints there are exactly three pairings, so both kernels have mean \(1/3\).

**A conditional probability calculation.** Given a chord with endpoints \(0<u<v<1\) and length \(\ell=v-u\), the conditional probabilities for a second independent chord are
\[
p_X(u,v)=2\ell(1-\ell),\qquad
p_N(u,v)=\ell^2+2u(1-v).
\]
Indeed, a crossing has exactly one endpoint in the interior; a nesting places both endpoints inside, or straddles the specified chord. The density of \((u,v)\) is \(2\) on \(0<u<v<1\). Elementary polynomial integration gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathbb E p_X=\mathbb E p_N=\frac13,\\
\mathbb E(p_X-1/3)^2=\mathbb E(p_N-1/3)^2=\frac1{45},\\
\mathbb E[(p_X-1/3)(p_N-1/3)]=0.
\end{gathered}
\]
These are exact rational integrals; the reproducibility script evaluates every monomial explicitly.

**Exact finite covariance.** Two distinct chord-pair indicators are independent when their index pairs are disjoint. If they share precisely one chord, condition on that chord and use independence of the other two chords. Their covariance is therefore the covariance of the corresponding conditional probabilities. For crossing with crossing and nesting with nesting this is \(1/45\), whereas crossing with nesting is exactly zero. For identical index pairs, the two alternative relation indicators are mutually exclusive, giving covariance \(-1/9\); a single crossing or nesting indicator has variance \(2/9\). There are \(\binom n2\) identical index pairs and \(3\binom n3\) unordered pairs of distinct index pairs that share one chord. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X_n)&=\frac29\binom n2+\frac{6}{45}\binom n3
=\frac{n(n-1)(n+3)}{45},\\
\operatorname{Cov}(X_n,N_n)&=-\frac19\binom n2
=-\frac{n(n-1)}{18}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Interchanging crossing and nesting gives the same variance for \(N_n\). Division by the variances yields the exact correlation formula.

**Joint central limit theorem.** Define \(g_X(Z)=p_X(Z)-1/3\) and \(g_N(Z)=p_N(Z)-1/3\). The centered two-dimensional Hoeffding decomposition for the complete order-two sums reads
\[
\binom{X_n-\mathbb EX_n}{N_n-\mathbb EN_n}
=(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{g_X(Z_i)}{g_N(Z_i)}+R_n.
\]
Each component of \(R_n\) is a sum of degenerate kernels, meaning its conditional expectation given either argument is zero. Distinct degenerate terms are uncorrelated even if they share one index (condition on the common argument), and are independent if disjoint. Each kernel is bounded. Hence \(\mathbb E\lVert R_n\rVert^2=O(n^2)\), so \(n^{-3/2}R_n\to0\) in \(L^2\). The independent vectors \((g_X(Z_i),g_N(Z_i))\) have covariance matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1/45,1/45)\), by the calculated conditional moments. The two-dimensional i.i.d. central limit theorem followed by Slutsky's theorem proves the claimed limit. A centered bivariate normal with diagonal covariance has independent coordinates. The three-class statement follows by its exact deterministic linear relation.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 and no third-party dependencies. The verifier enumerates all \((2n-1)!!\) perfect matchings for \(2\le n\le7\) (including \(135135\) at \(n=7\)); compares their exact rational means and nine covariance entries to the displayed formulas; enumerates all \(90\) labeled three-chord endpoint words to check the shared-chord relation table; and integrates polynomial first projections exactly. It passed \(105\) assertions. These finite checks corroborate the algebra but do **not** prove the infinite joint central limit theorem; that proof is the Hoeffding-decomposition argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Klazar [1] proved joint crossing/nesting symmetry in matchings. Kasraoui and Zeng [2], especially Proposition 3.8, provide a bivariate continued-fraction generating function that encodes the **entire finite joint distribution**. Thus the finite mean and covariance identities here are consequences of stronger previously published enumeration and are **not separately claimed as original**. Flajolet and Noy [3] established a marginal Gaussian crossing limit, later revisited quantitatively by Paguyo [4], but those results do not state a bivariate independent-Gaussian limit with the number of nested *pairs*. Baik and Jenkins [5] prove asymptotic independence for the **maximum crossing and nesting sizes** in Poissonized random matchings; those are different, extreme-value statistics. Féray [6] proves pattern-count CLTs for uniformly random set partitions; this is a different probability model from uniform perfect matchings and does not supply the displayed joint covariance cancellation. The present contribution is the explicit joint limit and its orthogonal-projection mechanism.

## Limitations
No convergence rate, local limit theorem, finite-sample independence or statement about maximum crossing/nesting sizes is established. The result is specialized to uniform perfect matchings; no assertion is made for uniform set partitions, conditioned matching types or nonuniform chord laws. The known bivariate generating function contains all finite joint moments, and earlier literature could contain an unlocated equivalent bivariate limit; originality is assessed against the sources described here, not proved by search absence. The review is not an independent validation.
