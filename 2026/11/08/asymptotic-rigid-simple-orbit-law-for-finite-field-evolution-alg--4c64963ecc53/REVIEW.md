# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The matrix model and the known monomial-basis and simplicity criteria are stated explicitly. Directed cut counting bounds nonsimple matrices without treating invertibility and connectivity as independent. For every nonidentity monomial transformation, either the diagonal graph-loop obstruction applies on the simple locus or the permutation of ordered matrix coordinates gives a uniform upper bound on fixed-space dimension. The two bounds feed directly into Burnside's formula. The explicit bounds tend to zero for each fixed finite field. A separate exhaustive small-instance program verifies the actual monomial action, graph property, stabilizer counts, and orbit counts. The finite experiments are corroboration, not a substitute for the general proof.

## Originality — qualified PASS

The 2026 survey poses random finite-field evolution-algebra and isomorphism-class asymptotics questions. Wei–Zou 2023 gives the general Burnside framework for perfect algebras, not the stated asymptotic for simple isomorphism classes. A prior binary-field result gives a sharper formula when the field has two elements, so that case is not claimed as new. A prior labeled-matrix result already determines the simplicity probability limit for every finite field; that limit is acknowledged and used, not claimed. For fixed fields with at least three elements, the generic monomial stabilizer estimate and the resulting simple-orbit count require an additional uniform analysis of weighted permutation and diagonal stabilizers. Searches for equivalent formulations were negative, but do not prove world-wide historical uniqueness. An earlier derivation from a broad Burnside formula remains a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result directly determines the leading asymptotic number of simple isomorphism types for every fixed finite-field size above two, addressing a natural restricted part of a recently stated counting question. Unlike a labeled probability alone, the classification asymptotic must control orbit-size bias caused by nontrivial automorphisms. The work proves that these exceptional stabilizers contribute negligibly even when nontrivial scalar changes are allowed; it is a structural bridge from random matrices to isomorphism classes.

## Closest literature and boundaries

Wei–Zou 2023 provides an exact Burnside sum on the larger perfect locus; it does not state this asymptotic. Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel 2026 poses Questions 21–22. Earlier finite-binary-field estimates and labeled simplicity asymptotics cover adjacent, not identical, claims. The theorem does not count all evolution algebras and does not handle field size growing with dimension. Independent validation remains outstanding.
