# Scientific review: attracting three-cycle chirality

## Correctness

**PASS.** All statements are confined to the strict two-saddle region, to first-coordinate itinerary signs, and to hyperbolically attracting prime-three orbits. Affine elimination for the candidate \(LLR\) cycle gives a unique denominator \(S=\det(I-RL^2)\), the explicit three first-coordinate numerators, and three Jury/Schur inequalities. Because \(N_2\) is a strict sum of squares and \(S>0\), the five inequalities are both necessary and sufficient for strict \(LLR\) attraction. For \(LRR\), the left-labelled coordinate equals a positive-square numerator divided by a positive Schur denominator, an admissibility contradiction. The golden-ratio edge construction is proved by explicit expansion, with a limiting edge point used only for perturbative existence; the strict two-saddle condition holds only *after* the indicated positive perturbations. The rational witness and the checker corroborate, but are not an infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS within inspected evidence, with a documented publication-priority risk.** The full accessible text of Ghosh–McLachlan–Simpson, arXiv:2402.05393, treats the same two-saddle domain and stable low-period complications but does not give these exact prime-three inequalities, the \(LRR\) sum-of-squares contradiction, or the explicit golden-ratio saddle-edge strip. The full accessible text of Fatoyinbo–Simpson, arXiv:2210.14445, already reports attracting \(LLR\) orbits for a non-two-saddle parameter choice and provides general periodic-word stability methods, which are credited; its period-three appendices focus on opposite-forcing \(LRR\) tongues. The 2016 Simpson survey documents general formulas but was accessible only at abstract level, despite alternative access attempts. Generic itinerary criteria are acknowledged as the machinery, so no priority is claimed for those criteria or for the existence of an attracting \(LLR\) orbit in unrestricted parameters. A distinct public four-cycle obstruction concerns \(LLLR\), not this length-three classification.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Robust-chaos diagrams with two saddle fixed points can have additional stable low-period windows. An exact complete prime-three test and elimination of the opposite three-cycle itinerary show which windows can actually arise, rather than relying on numerical diagrams. The coexistence of two saddle fixed points with a period-three sink rules out any inference that two-saddle geometry alone forces a chaotic attractor. The near-saddle-edge construction proves a nonempty open set of **two-saddle** maps with a period-three sink, and gives a sharp algebraic golden-ratio boundary for the proposed edge strip. The boundary is not overinterpreted as a global period-three cutoff.

## Limits

No global attractor uniqueness or nonexistence of other periods is inferred. The 2016 survey was not reviewed in full; unindexed equivalent computations might exist. No independent mathematical audit has been carried out.
