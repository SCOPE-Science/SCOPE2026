# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The characterization of idempotent averaging maps via image/kernel is explicitly proved from the operator identity. The nonzero image must project onto the affine generator; the kernel is invariant under the two-eigenvalue adjoint of a lift of that generator and hence lies in the abelian derived ideal. This gives an ideal kernel and turns the map into a Lie retraction. A direct parameterization by a linear projection and a vector in its kernel leads to the exact Gaussian-binomial count. The finite-product formula and controlled discrete-Gaussian tails establish both parity limits. The independent matrix verifier checked seven exhaustive dimension–field instances, including characteristic two and a nonprime field. No finite test is used to prove an unrestricted assertion.

## Originality — PASS with residual risks

The general image-subalgebra/kernel-invariance criterion is prior work in Laraiedh–Silvestrov, arXiv:2304.00662, Proposition 2.24 (2023 preprint). Tang–Sheng–Wagemann, arXiv:2306.13873, investigates relative averaging operators and deformation/cohomology, not embedded rank enumerators. Actual database searches for averaging projections, scalar-affine Lie retractions, and finite-field rank spectra found no exact equivalent. The present claim specializes a known criterion but adds a family-specific rigidity theorem, complete operator multiplicities, and a quantitative two-parity limit. A broader classification of Lie retractions, if found, could imply the formula; uninspected sources remain a publication-priority risk.

## Value — PASS

Idempotent averaging maps are canonical splitting operators associated with induced Leibniz structures; determining when their image/kernel data force genuine Lie retractions is a structural question. The scalar-affine family has arbitrary dimension and every finite-field characteristic. The exact rank polynomial distinguishes labeled operators by the size of their Lie image, and the two parity-dependent theta laws quantify typical splitting dimensions. The Heisenberg counterexample shows that the retraction rigidity is not a general tautology. The rank and limit refinements are natural invariants rather than arbitrary finite computations.

The proof establishes all positive dimensions and all finite field orders; exhaustive checks cover seven small finite cases and are not substitutes for the general proof. Prior generic theorems are acknowledged, and independent scientific audit has not been performed.
