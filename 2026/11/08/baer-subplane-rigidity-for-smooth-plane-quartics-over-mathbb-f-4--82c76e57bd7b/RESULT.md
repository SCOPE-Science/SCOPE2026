# Baer-subplane rigidity for smooth plane quartics over \(\mathbb F_4\)

## Finding
Let \(B\subset\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)\) be any Baer subplane, that is, a projective copy of \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_2)\). Exactly **24** geometrically smooth degree-four curves \(C/\mathbb F_4\), considered up to nonzero scalar multiples of their homogeneous equations, satisfy \(C(\mathbb F_4)=B\). In coordinates taking \(B\) to the standard seven-point \(\mathbb F_2\)-subplane, every such equation is defined over \(\mathbb F_2\) up to scalar; hence the exact rational-point condition forces descent of its plane equation. These 24 curves form one orbit under the stabilizer \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) of \(B\), with stabilizer order \(7\).

Every such curve determines a perfect matching between the seven points and seven lines of its Fano subplane: each subplane line is tangent to the curve at precisely one of its three subplane points, and no point serves twice. Conversely all **24** perfect matchings occur and determine these curves bijectively.

There are \(360\) Baer subplanes in \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)\), hence exactly \(360\cdot24=8640\) geometrically smooth quartic equations up to scalar whose rational point set is *some* Baer subplane. They constitute one orbit under \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_4)\), with projective stabilizer order \(7\). The curves have geometric genus \(3\). No smooth plane curve of degree less than \(4\) has precisely a Baer subplane as its \(\mathbb F_4\)-rational point set.

One representative, over \(\mathbb F_2\), is
\[
X^3Y+X^3Z+X^2Y^2+X^2YZ+X^2Z^2+XYZ^2+Y^3Z+YZ^3=0.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The field is \(k=\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2(\alpha)\), with \(\alpha^2+\alpha+1=0\). Curves are homogeneous projective plane subschemes defined by nonzero quartic forms, identified only when forms differ by \(k^\times\). The word smooth always means geometrically smooth after extension to \(\overline k\). The standard Baer subplane has precisely the seven projective vectors with coordinates in \(\mathbb F_2\). Every Baer subplane over \(k\) is carried to the standard one by a \(k\)-projectivity. The exact condition \(C(k)=B\) is stronger than requiring \(B\subseteq C(k)\).

The group \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) has order \(168\); \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_4)\) has order \(60480\). The former is exactly the projective stabilizer of the standard Baer subplane. The claimed projective equivalences are over \(\mathbb F_4\), not over an algebraic closure; no assertion about full geometric automorphism groups is made.

## Proof
**1. Forced tangencies.** Fix a standard subplane \(B\) and a geometrically smooth quartic \(C\) with \(C(k)=B\). A line \(L\) of the Fano subplane is defined over \(\mathbb F_2\) and contains three points of \(B\) and two further \(k\)-points. The degree-four restriction of the defining form to \(L\cong\mathbb P^1\) has the three distinct \(\mathbb F_2\)-roots. Since it is defined over \(k\), its remaining root also belongs to \(\mathbb P^1(k)\), counting multiplicities. It cannot be either of the two other \(k\)-points because the rational point set is exactly \(B\). Thus one of the three points of \(B\cap L\) is a double zero. Bézout ensures that the restriction is not identically zero because \(C\) is smooth and irreducible. Therefore \(L\) is tangent to \(C\) at one Fano point. Since a smooth point has a unique tangent line, two distinct Fano lines cannot be assigned to the same point. The seven assignments form a bijective point–line incidence matching of the Fano plane.

**2. Every matching yields a unique descended equation.** There are exactly \(24\) perfect matchings of the Fano point–line incidence graph. For each matching \(m\) impose seven conditions \(F(P)=0\) and seven conditions that the directional derivative along the matched line vanishes at \(P\). These are \(14\) homogeneous linear equations in the \(15\) coefficients of a ternary quartic. Exact row reduction over \(\mathbb F_2\) gives rank \(14\) for every matching. Since the matrix is over \(\mathbb F_2\), its kernel after extension to \(k\) is the extension of its one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-kernel. Thus the matched form is unique up to \(k^\times\) and has coefficients in \(\mathbb F_2\). Distinct matchings cannot define the same smooth quartic, by tangent uniqueness.

**3. Existence and geometric smoothness.** The full group \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) acts transitively on the \(24\) matchings. It is therefore enough to verify one representative. For the displayed representative, exact Gröbner-basis calculations in characteristic two on the three charts \(X=1\), \(Y=1\), and \(Z=1\) show that the ideals generated by its dehomogenization and the two affine partial derivatives are the whole polynomial ring. This proves nonsingularity over \(\overline{k}\), not only at rational points. Direct enumeration of the \(21\) points of \(\mathbb P^2(k)\) shows that its zero set comprises exactly the seven standard \(\mathbb F_2\)-points. Projective transformations then provide a smooth exact-point-set curve for all \(24\) matchings. Combining with Steps 1–2 proves the fixed-subplane classification and descent.

**4. Global projective orbit.** A Baer subplane is generated by any projective quadrangle in it, so all Baer subplanes are \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(k)\)-conjugate. The subgroup preserving the standard seven-point subplane is exactly \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), of order \(168\); thus there are \(60480/168=360\) subplanes. There are \(360\cdot24=8640\) curves because their rational point sets distinguish subplanes. Transitivity on subplanes and then matchings gives one \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(k)\)-orbit. Orbit–stabilizer gives \(60480/8640=7\) for each curve. A smooth plane quartic has genus \((4-1)(4-2)/2=3\).

**5. Sharpness in degree.** A nonsingular conic over \(k\) has five \(k\)-points; a line has five, so neither can have rational point set \(B\). A smooth cubic with seven \(k\)-points is an elliptic curve with group of order seven. Multiplication by three is invertible on this group, so it has a \(k\)-rational flex; choosing it as the origin identifies its rational hyperplane section with the group-law zero. Among seven points of a cyclic group of order seven, there are precisely five unordered triples of distinct elements summing to zero. But all seven lines of the Fano subplane each contain three distinct \(k\)-points of the proposed cubic. They would give seven different zero-sum triples, a contradiction. The independent finite-field verifier also confirms directly that no degree-three form has the exact rational zero locus \(B\), whether smooth or not.

## Verification
Run from the directory containing both scripts:

```sh
python3 verify_baer_matching.py
python3 verify_baer_exhaustive.py
```

The first uses Python and SymPy. It enumerates the \(7!\) bijections between Fano points and lines, verifies the \(24\) incidence matchings, computes every matching's \(14\)-equation rank and unique binary quartic, enumerates the \(168\) invertible binary \(3\times3\) matrices and proves orbit transitivity, verifies geometric smoothness of the displayed representative using Gröbner unit ideals in all three charts, and checks its rational zeros in \(\mathbb F_4\). Its required success marker is `BAER_MATCHING_PASS 7 24 24 168 24 7`.

The independently implemented second script uses only the Python standard library, implements \(\mathbb F_4\) arithmetic, and exhausts the \(4^8-1=65535\) nonzero coefficient vectors in the degree-four vanishing space. It finds \(1080\) forms with exact rational zero locus \(B\), of which \(72\) have no singularities at the seven rational points. Normalizing modulo \(\mathbb F_4^\times\) leaves exactly \(24\), all with binary coefficients. The result agrees with the geometric-smoothness classification; it is not alone a proof of geometric smoothness. The success markers are `BAER_EXHAUSTIVE_PASS 6 0 0 7 3 0 7 8 1080 72 24` and `COEFFICIENT_DESCENT_PASS 24`. Both scripts are embedded with the result.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli–Ghioca–Yip (arXiv:2208.13299, initial public version August 28, 2022) motivate rational-point blocking sets on smooth curves, describe the Baer lower bound and finite-field quartic context, and provide low-degree restrictions without this exact \(\mathbb F_4\) Baer-subplane quartic classification. Homma–Kim (arXiv:0903.1918; relating their results to Tallini) classify nonsingular minimum-degree curves through **all** points of \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_q)\), in particular the individual binary filling quartics when \(q=2\). Their previously known descriptions and counts of the binary quartics are not new here. Della Vedova–Pagani–Pianta independently enumerate the **360 Baer subplanes** of \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)\) as point sets; that incidence count, also classical, is not new here. The new statement is that imposing the *exact* seven-point Baer rational locus on quartic curves originally defined over the larger field \(\mathbb F_4\) forces descent, and yields a bijection with incidence matchings and one complete \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_4)\)-orbit of \(8640\) curves. These implications do not follow from a mere classification of forms **a priori** over \(\mathbb F_2\) without ruling out additional coefficients over \(\mathbb F_4\).

## Limitations
This theorem concerns geometrically smooth **plane quartics** and exact \(\mathbb F_4\)-rational zero sets that are Baer subplanes. It does not classify all blocking quartics, quartics with a Baer subplane as only a subset of their points, singular plane curves, curves over larger fields, or geometric automorphism groups. Historical priority beyond the inspected primary literature and searchable mathematical records is not independently certified. The exhaustive finite checks are logically finite, while geometric smoothness is established with exact polynomial ideal certificates rather than only finite-point tests.
