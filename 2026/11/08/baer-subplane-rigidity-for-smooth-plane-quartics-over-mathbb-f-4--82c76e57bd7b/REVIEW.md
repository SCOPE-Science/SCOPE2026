# Scientific review — Baer-subplane rigidity for smooth plane quartics

## Correctness: PASS
The exact claim concerns scalar classes of geometrically smooth ternary quartics over \(\mathbb F_4\) whose complete \(\mathbb F_4\)-rational zero set is a Baer subplane. A Fano line has three prescribed zeros, and its fourth intersection is rational because the residual factor of a degree-four binary form with three rational roots is linear. Exactness forces this fourth intersection to coincide with one of the three prescribed points. Smoothness makes the tangent line unique and converts the seven tangencies into a perfect point–line matching. The provided exhaustive matching verifier checks that there are 24 matchings; every one defines a rank-14 linear system in 15 quartic coefficients over \(\mathbb F_2\), so descent follows after scalar extension. It checks transitivity of all 168 Fano projectivities, verifies the representative's exact seven \(\mathbb F_4\)-points, and proves geometric smoothness by unit Gröbner ideals on three affine charts. The independent standard-library coefficient verifier exhausts the 65535 nonzero vectors of the eight-dimensional \(\mathbb F_4\) quartic vanishing space and reproduces the 24 projective forms. Orbit–stabilizer gives 360 Baer subplanes and 8640 curves; the group orders are independently explicit. The genus and degree-three obstruction follow respectively from the plane-curve genus formula and the cubic group-law collinearity count. Finite rational-point checks are not substituted for geometric smoothness.

## Originality: PASS, subject to historical priority
The motivating treatment by Asgarli–Ghioca–Yip (arXiv:2208.13299, primary MSC including 14H50) treats blocking sets on smooth curves and identifies the Baer case as extremal, but the inspected full-text sections do not classify exact-point-set smooth quartics over \(\mathbb F_4\). Della Vedova–Pagani–Pianta (doi:10.1007/s00022-022-00662-2) explicitly count the 360 Baer subplanes as finite point sets, which is not new. Homma–Kim (arXiv:0903.1918), expanding Tallini's work, already classifies minimum-degree filling quartics over \(\mathbb F_2\); that prior binary classification is expressly excluded from the new contribution. A theorem for forms whose coefficients **start** in \(\mathbb F_2\) does not directly rule out the far larger coefficient space over \(\mathbb F_4\). The new direction is the exact seven-point rational-locus rigidity over the extension field, its derivation from incidence perfect matchings, and a full \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_4)\) orbit enumeration. Targeted mathematical-record and relevant primary-literature searches found no logically stronger covering statement, but older or unindexed equivalent treatments remain a residual risk.

## Mathematical value: PASS
A Baer subplane of seven points is an extremal nontrivial blocking configuration in \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_4)\). The result classifies **all** smooth genus-three quartics whose rational points realize that natural extremum, showing that a field-extension coefficient freedom unexpectedly collapses to the prime subfield. The forced tangencies give a direct, conceptually useful bridge from algebraic plane curves to the Heawood graph, and the result determines the exact arithmetic orbit of 8640 forms and their projective stabilizers. This is a complete natural classification, rather than an arbitrary numerical slice, and it specifies the sharp degree boundary for that exact point configuration.

## Scope, closest literature and risks
The assertion is about exact rational point sets and plane projective equivalence, not all blocking curves, all curves containing seven Baer points, or automorphisms over the algebraic closure. The 2009 Tallini–Homma–Kim binary filling result supplies antecedent mathematics and must not be represented as new. Searches cannot exclude an older unpublished classification; no independent external review has been performed. The embedded finite-field programs and three-chart Gröbner tests provide reproducible same-model evidence of the particular new theorem.
