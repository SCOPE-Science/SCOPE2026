# Balanced four-tap generalized-dihedral convolution: three nullity strata and a sharp quadratic spectral gap

## Finding
Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group of odd order \(N\), and let \(G=A\rtimes C_2\), with the nonidentity involution acting on \(A\) by negation. For any distinct \(a,b\in A\) and distinct \(c,d\in A\), consider the balanced four-element support
\[
S=\{(a,0),(b,0),(c,1),(d,1)\}\subset G,
\qquad (T_Sf)(h)=\sum_{g\in S}f(hg).
\]
Set \(u=b-a\ne0\), \(v=d-c\ne0\), and let \(\widehat A\) denote all unitary characters of \(A\). The **complete singular-value multiset** of the unnormalized right-convolution operator \(T_S\) on \(\ell^2(G)\) is
\[
\bigcup_{\chi\in\widehat A}
\left\{\left|1+\chi(u)\right|+\left|1+\chi(v)\right|,
\ \left|\left|1+\chi(u)\right|-\left|1+\chi(v)\right|\right|\right\},
\]
counting one of each displayed value for each character. In particular,
\[
\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker T_S
=\frac{N}{\operatorname{ord}(u-v)}
+\frac{N}{\operatorname{ord}(u+v)}
-\frac{N}{|\langle u-v,u+v\rangle|}.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{ord}(0)=1\); group orders in the formula are positive integers.

Now specialize to \(A=\mathbb F_p^d\), with odd prime \(p\), dimension \(d\ge2\), and \(N=p^d\). The exact complex nullity takes **precisely three geometric values**:
\[
\nu(S)=\begin{cases}
N,&v=u\text{ or }v=-u,\\
N/p,&v\in\langle u\rangle_{\mathbb F_p}\setminus\{0,u,-u\},\\
2N/p-N/p^2,&u,v\text{ linearly independent}.
\end{cases}
\]
The middle case is absent for \(p=3\). Among all \(\binom N2^2\) supports containing two group elements from each coset, the numbers in the three respective strata are
\[
\frac{N^2(N-1)}2,\qquad
\frac{N^2(N-1)(p-3)}4,\qquad
\frac{N^2(N-1)(N-p)}4.
\]
Every balanced four-point convolution is singular, but its recoverable component has an exact stability law. **Whenever \(u,v\) are independent**, its largest singular value is \(4\), its smallest *positive* singular value is
\[
\sigma_{\min}^+(T_S)=2\bigl(1-\cos(\pi/p)\bigr)
\]
with multiplicity \(4p^{d-2}\), and its nonzero-spectrum condition number is exactly
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{visible}}(T_S)=\frac{2}{1-\cos(\pi/p)}
\sim \frac{4p^2}{\pi^2}\quad(p\to\infty).
\]
The formulas are independent of the positions of the two pairs, depending only on their differences.

## Assumptions and scope
The subgroup \(A\) has odd order in the first theorem, and all four support points are distinct because \(a\ne b\) and \(c\ne d\) lie in different cosets. The group law is \((x,\epsilon)(y,\delta)=(x+(-1)^\epsilon y,\epsilon+\delta\bmod2)\). Convolution is defined with right multiplication; group-counting measure defines the Hilbert-space norm. All ranks, kernels, and singular values are over \(\mathbb C\), without normalizing the four coefficients. The sharp-gap statement requires \(p\) odd, \(d\ge2\), and independent \(u,v\). For a rank-deficient map, \(\kappa_{\mathrm{visible}}\) denotes the ratio of the largest to the smallest strictly positive singular value, equivalently the condition number between \((\ker T_S)^\perp\) and \(\operatorname{im}T_S\).

## Proof
**1. Exact two-coset Fourier blocks.** Write \(f=(f_0,f_1)\) according to the two cosets. By the group law,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(T_Sf)_0(x)&=f_0(x+a)+f_0(x+b)+f_1(x+c)+f_1(x+d),\\
(T_Sf)_1(x)&=f_1(x-a)+f_1(x-b)+f_0(x-c)+f_0(x-d).
\end{aligned}
\]
Under the unitary character transform of \(A\), the \(\chi\)-fiber is represented by
\[
B_\chi=\begin{pmatrix}r_\chi&t_\chi\\\overline{t_\chi}&\overline{r_\chi}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad r_\chi=\chi(a)+\chi(b),\quad t_\chi=\chi(c)+\chi(d).
\]
Consequently \(T_S\) is unitarily equivalent to the direct sum of the \(N\) matrices \(B_\chi\), **including** the reducible trivial-character fiber. Direct multiplication gives
\[
B_\chi^*B_\chi=\begin{pmatrix}
|r_\chi|^2+|t_\chi|^2&2\overline{r_\chi}t_\chi\\
2r_\chi\overline{t_\chi}&|r_\chi|^2+|t_\chi|^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are \((|r_\chi|\pm|t_\chi|)^2\). As \(|r_\chi|=|1+\chi(u)|\) and \(|t_\chi|=|1+\chi(v)|\), the singular-value multiset follows.

**2. Kernel and arithmetic formula.** Since \(A\) has odd order, neither \(\chi(u)\) nor \(\chi(v)\) equals \(-1\), so \(r_\chi,t_\chi\ne0\). Hence a singular block has rank exactly one; its kernel is the line generated by \((t_\chi,-r_\chi)\). For unit complex numbers \(z,w\), \(|1+z|=|1+w|\) if and only if \(z=w\) or \(z=\overline w\). Thus singular characters are exactly
\[
\{\chi:\chi(u-v)=1\}\ \cup\ \{\chi:\chi(u+v)=1\}.
\]
The annihilator of a subgroup \(H\le A\) has cardinality \(N/|H|\). Inclusion–exclusion gives the exact nullity. In particular, the complete kernel consists of Fourier fibers \(\mathbb C(t_\chi,-r_\chi)\) for these characters, with all remaining fibers zero.

**3. Three geometric strata and census.** Over \(\mathbb F_p\), the two annihilator constraints are affine-linear hyperplanes in the character space. If \(v=\pm u\), one condition is identically true. If \(v\) is a nonzero scalar multiple other than \(\pm u\), the two conditions coincide and have \(N/p\) solutions. If \(u,v\) are independent, the hyperplanes are distinct and intersect in exactly \(N/p^2\) characters, giving \(2N/p-N/p^2\). To count supports, first choose the two-point rotation pair in \(\binom N2\) ways. A fixed nonzero difference class \(\{u,-u\}\) has exactly \(N\) unordered translated realizations as a reflection pair. There are respectively \(1\), \((p-3)/2\), and \((N-p)/2\) nonzero difference classes of the prescribed three geometric types. Multiplying these counts proves the census; its sum equals \(\binom N2^2\).

**4. Sharp positive spectral gap.** When \(u,v\) are independent, \(\chi\mapsto(\chi(u),\chi(v))\) is onto the product of the \(p\)-th roots of unity, and each pair occurs \(p^{d-2}\) times. The distinct values of \(|1+\zeta^j|\), where \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}\), are
\[
b_j=2\cos(\pi j/p),\qquad 0\le j\le(p-1)/2,
\]
strictly decreasing and positive. Their successive gaps are
\[
b_j-b_{j+1}=4\sin\!\left(\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2p}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right),
\]
which strictly increase with \(j\) in this range. The smallest distinct-value difference is therefore \(b_0-b_1=4\sin^2(\pi/(2p))=2(1-\cos(\pi/p))\). Every plus singular value is at least \(2b_{(p-1)/2}=4\sin(\pi/(2p))\), which strictly exceeds that gap. Thus the least positive singular value is exactly \(b_0-b_1\). It occurs for precisely the four ordered root pairs in which one root is \(1\) and the other is \(\zeta\) or \(\zeta^{-1}\); each has \(p^{d-2}\) character preimages. The constant function shows \(\|T_S\|\ge4\), while the sum of four unitary right translations gives \(\|T_S\|\le4\). This proves the multiplicity, the sharp condition number, and its Taylor asymptotic.

## Verification
Run the standalone Python 3 file `dihedral_four_tap_check.py` with NumPy installed. It directly constructs integer right-convolution matrices, computes their modular ranks, and independently compares all numerical singular values to the predicted character-block singular values. Cases include \(\mathbb F_3^2\), \(\mathbb F_5^2\) (all three geometric regimes), \(\mathbb F_7^2\), \(\mathbb F_3^3\), and odd cyclic groups of orders \(9,15,21\). It also exhaustively counts every balanced two-pair support at \((p,d)=(3,2)\) and \((5,2)\), yielding the expected stratum counts \((324,0,972)\) and \((7500,7500,75000)\). These finite numerical and modular checks are consistency tests, not an all-parameter proof; the four arguments above establish the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
The unitary decomposition into two-coset Fourier blocks is classical finite-group harmonic analysis and **not** claimed as new. Alagic–Russell (2006) establish compact-group operator Fourier/uncertainty principles. Arezoomand–Taeri (2013) derive representation-theoretic characteristic-polynomial factorizations for general Cayley digraphs, including cyclic-index-two cases. Huang–Huang–Lu (2016) analyze undirected cubic Cayley graphs on cyclic dihedral groups. A published three-tap rank census on elementary generalized-dihedral groups treats a different support size, with no four-tap rank census or singular-value endpoint. The claimed contribution here is the complete *balanced four-tap* arithmetic nullity and geometric support census together with the sharp nonzero singular-value gap and dimension-independent quadratic conditioning in the independent-difference regime. Generic block formulas provide tools but do not themselves state these optimized conclusions.

## Limitations
This concerns precisely two taps in each coset of an inversion semidirect product with odd-order normal abelian subgroup. It does not classify three-one or four-zero support splits, even-order groups, arbitrary complex tap weights, or general semidirect actions. The least-positive singular-value formula is asserted only for linearly independent differences over prime fields; the unrestricted finite-abelian theorem provides its spectrum without optimizing endpoints. A broader unlocated Cayley-spectrum formula could imply the special results; independent literature priority review remains necessary.
