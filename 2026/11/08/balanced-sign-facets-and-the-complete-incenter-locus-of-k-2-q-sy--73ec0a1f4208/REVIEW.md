# Scientific review of the \(K_{2,q}\) incenter-locus theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The claimed orthogonal product is checked directly from the root vertices and full Gram matrix. Its cross-polytope factor has exactly one facet per sign vector. The rank-one matrix inverse yields every facet-normal length. Centrally symmetric averaging proves a centered ball attains the inradius, and the sign vectors attaining the greatest normal norm force all maximizers onto the explicitly parameterized affine subspace. The remaining inequalities are solved exactly, using monotonicity of a displayed elementary scalar function. Every tangency statement is an equality case in the complete facet system. The independent convex-hull/LP computation agrees in dimensions three through six, while the proof is unrestricted.

## Originality
PASS on the accessible comparisons, with provisional historical priority. Higashitani–Mori (arXiv:2608.02445, Sections 1 and 5–6), and Codenotti–Riccardi–Venturello (arXiv:2512.16572, Introduction and main theorems) supply decomposability and graph-edge counts but no Euclidean incenter-locus classification. The nearby 2024 complete-multipartite Ehrhart paper addresses arithmetic rather than Euclidean geometry. Relevant published record summaries about stars, cycles and crowns do not cover \(K_{2,q}\): graph family and supporting-facet Gram matrices differ. An earlier published claim about the lattice polar's magic-basis Ehrhart positivity likewise does not imply intrinsic Euclidean radius/center data. The remaining risk is an unindexed equivalent cross-polytope Chebyshev-center theorem or an inaccessible full related record; no broader covering result was identified.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The class \(K_{2,q}\) is singled out by the recent complete decomposability classification, making exact Euclidean geometry of its exceptional summand structure a natural gap. The result supplies both a full facet-distance distribution and the complete, generally nonunique optimal-center set for every dimension. The parity change in affine dimension from one to two, exact rectangle side lengths, and endpoint tangencies are not consequences of knowing only the Minkowski decomposition, combinatorial face numbers, or a single centered-ball radius. The theorem has an infinite parameter range and explicitly rules out unsupported extensions.
