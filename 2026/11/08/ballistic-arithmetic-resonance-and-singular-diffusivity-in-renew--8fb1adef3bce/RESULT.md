# Ballistic arithmetic resonance and singular diffusivity in renewal-tumbled circular swimmers

## Finding

A constant-speed circular swimmer subject to fixed-angle turns at arbitrary independent renewal times has a complete transport trichotomy: bounded motion, finite diffusive mean-square growth, or nonzero ballistic motion. The key invariant is the first Fourier coefficient of a run duration, and the phase-arithmetic criterion distinguishes all three regimes without imposing higher run-time moments.

Identify the plane with complex numbers and let the displacement be
\[
Z(t)=v\int_0^t\exp\bigl(i[\phi_0+\omega s+\theta N(s)]\bigr)\,ds,
\]
where \(N(s)\) counts renewals by time \(s\). Put \(\mu=\mathbb ET\), \(m=\mathbb E e^{i\omega T}\), \(h=e^{i\theta}\), and \(q=hm\).

**Nonlattice phase case:** When \(|m|<1\), the limit
\[
D_{\mathrm{MSD}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E|Z(t)|^2}{4t}
=\frac{v^2(1-\cos\theta)(1-|m|^2)}{2\mu\omega^2|1-hm|^2}
\]
exists. It is strictly positive if \(h\ne1\), while for \(h=1\) every trajectory is uniformly bounded.

**Phase-lattice case:** When \(|m|=1\), necessarily \(e^{i\omega T}=\zeta\) almost surely for one unit complex number \(\zeta=m\). If \(h\zeta\ne1\), trajectories are uniformly bounded. If \(h\zeta=1\), then
\[
\frac{Z(t)}t\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}
\frac{v e^{i\phi_0}}{i\omega\mu}(\zeta-1).
\]
Consequently motion is ballistic with strictly positive speed exactly when \(h\zeta=1\) and \(\zeta\ne1\); when \(\zeta=1\) the motion is bounded. The criterion \(|m|=1\) is equivalent to the support of \(T\) lying in one congruence class modulo \(2\pi/|\omega|\).

**A sharp noise-induced crossover:** With \(v=|\omega|=1\), \(\theta=\pi\), and \(T\) equally likely to equal \(\pi-\sigma\) or \(\pi+\sigma\), for every \(0<\sigma<\pi\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{MSD}}(\sigma)=\frac1\pi\cot^2(\sigma/2)
\sim\frac4{\pi\sigma^2}\qquad(\sigma\downarrow0).
\]
At \(\sigma=0\), by contrast, the actual displacement is ballistic with speed \(2/\pi\). Thus arbitrarily small renewal-time jitter turns resonant ballistic motion into diffusion with a sharply divergent coefficient.

## Assumptions and scope

Fix a speed \(v>0\), angular velocity \(\omega\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\), fixed turn angle \(\theta\in\mathbb R\), and initial phase \(\phi_0\in\mathbb R\). Let \((T_j)_{j\ge1}\) be independent and identically distributed strictly positive random variables, independent of all deterministic parameters, with \(0<\mu=\mathbb ET_1<\infty\). Write renewal epochs \(S_n=T_1+\dots+T_n\), \(S_0=0\), and \(N(t)=\max\{n:S_n\le t\}\). At a renewal the swimmer instantly rotates its heading through \(\theta\) and does not jump in position. No Brownian angular diffusion or translational noise is present. The process starts at \(Z(0)=0\), so no stationary renewal-age assumption is needed.

For \(|m|<1\), the ``diffusivity'' stated above means only the mean-square growth limit \(\mathbb E|Z(t)|^2/(4t)\); a functional central limit theorem, convergence of rescaled densities and isotropy from a fixed initial heading are not claimed. Positivity of the limit refers to the Euclidean mean square and does not imply any particular central-limit conclusion. The bound for \(h=1\) is deterministic for every renewal sequence.

## Proof

Put \(U_j=e^{i\omega T_j}\), \(Y_j=e^{i\phi_0}\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}hU_\ell\), and \(c=v/(i\omega)\). The complex displacement during a complete \(j\)-th run and heading update are
\[
\Delta Z_j=cY_{j-1}(U_j-1),\qquad Y_j=hU_jY_{j-1}.
\]
Each partial run has displacement at most \(2v/|\omega|\), regardless of its duration.

Suppose first that \(|m|<1\). Then \(1-q\ne0\). Set
\[
\kappa=c\frac{m-1}{1-q},\qquad
M_j=\Delta Z_j+\kappa(Y_j-Y_{j-1})
=c\frac{1-h}{1-hm}Y_{j-1}(U_j-m).
\]
With the filtration generated by the first \(j\) run durations, \((M_j)\) is a complex martingale-difference sequence: \(\mathbb E(M_j\mid\mathcal F_{j-1})=0\) and
\[
\mathbb E(|M_j|^2\mid\mathcal F_{j-1})
=\frac{v^2}{\omega^2}\frac{|1-h|^2(1-|m|^2)}{|1-hm|^2}
=:V_M.
\]
The expression uses \(\mathbb E|U_j-m|^2=1-|m|^2\).

For fixed \(t\), define the crossing index \(\tau(t)=\min\{n\ge1:S_n>t\}=N(t)+1\). It is a stopping time for the run filtration and has finite expectation: for any \(a>0\), the inequality
\[
\Pr(S_n\le t)\le e^{at}(\mathbb Ee^{-aT})^n
\]
provides a geometric bound, since \(T>0\) almost surely. Also \(\{j\le\tau(t)\}=\{S_{j-1}\le t\}\) is predictable. Applying orthogonality of stopped martingale differences and monotone convergence gives
\[
\mathbb E\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\tau(t)}M_j\right|^2
=V_M\,\mathbb E\tau(t).
\]
The actual position differs from this stopped sum by the heading coboundary (bounded by \(2|\kappa|\)) and by the unfinished part of the final circular arc (bounded by \(2v/|\omega|\)). A Cauchy–Schwarz estimate therefore yields
\[
\mathbb E|Z(t)|^2=V_M\,\mathbb E\tau(t)
+O\bigl(\sqrt{\mathbb E\tau(t)}+1\bigr).
\]
By the elementary renewal theorem, \(\mathbb E\tau(t)/t\to1/\mu\). Dividing by \(4t\) gives \(D_{\mathrm{MSD}}=V_M/(4\mu)\). The equality \(|1-h|^2=2(1-\cos\theta)\) completes the formula. If \(h=1\), directly \(Z(t)=c e^{i\phi_0}(e^{i\omega t}-1)\), so every path is bounded.

Now suppose \(|m|=1\). Equality in the unit-disk convexity inequality \(|\mathbb EU|\le\mathbb E|U|=1\) forces \(U=\zeta=m\) almost surely. Consequently \(Y_j=e^{i\phi_0}(h\zeta)^j\) and every complete run contributes \(c e^{i\phi_0}(\zeta-1)(h\zeta)^{j-1}\). If \(h\zeta\ne1\), the geometric sum is uniformly bounded, and the unfinished arc is bounded. If \(h\zeta=1\), the sum is \(N(t)c e^{i\phi_0}(\zeta-1)\) plus a uniformly bounded unfinished arc. The renewal strong law \(N(t)/t\to1/\mu\) yields the stated almost-sure velocity. This velocity vanishes precisely when \(\zeta=1\), in which case complete arc displacements vanish and the unfinished arc is bounded. The support congruence criterion follows from \(e^{i\omega T}=\zeta\) almost surely.

For the two-point example, \(\mu=\pi\), \(m=-\cos\sigma\), and \(h=-1\). Substitution gives \(D_{\mathrm{MSD}}=(1/\pi)\cot^2(\sigma/2)\), and the Taylor expansion of the cotangent yields the asymptotic coefficient. At \(\sigma=0\), \(\zeta=-1\) and \(h\zeta=1\), giving limiting speed \(2/\pi\).

## Verification

Run `python verify.py` using Python's standard library. The checker evaluates complete-run second moments by both exact finite-path enumeration and a separately summed correlation identity, checks the long-run variance slope, verifies the two-point jitter law and its small-jitter coefficient, tests deterministic bounded/ballistic cases, and checks the exponential-run specialization against the known Poisson model. These calculations are finite diagnostics only; the infinite-time statements follow from the proof above.

For exponentially distributed run durations with rate \(\lambda>0\), the formula reduces to
\[
D_{\mathrm{MSD}}=\frac{v^2\lambda(1-\cos\theta)}{2\bigl([\lambda(1-\cos\theta)]^2+[\omega+\lambda\sin\theta]^2\bigr)}.
\]
This uses the convention \(\mathbb E|Z(t)|^2/(4t)\), one-half the convention \(\mathbb E|Z(t)|^2/(2t)\) for the two-dimensional mean-square displacement used in some active-matter literature. Normalizations must be compared before matching formulas.

## Relationship to prior work

Olsen and Löwen (2024), arXiv:2405.14326, derive exact transport properties for Poissonian tumbles and fixed turn angles. Their Secs. II and IV contain the Poissonian transport formula, while Sec. II.2 explicitly mentions a deterministic half-circle non-Poisson turning example as a possible ballistic mechanism without establishing an arbitrary-renewal classification. The present statement includes the Poissonian result as a specialization but distinguishes arbitrary renewal laws with identical angular Fourier means, identifies all phase-lattice resonance cases, and quantifies a singular two-point jitter regularization.

Detcheverry (2017) gives a broad operator formalism for non-Poisson run-and-turn motions and explicitly includes curved trajectories. That work is a materially plausible source of overlapping diffusivity results. Only the accessible abstract and publication metadata were compared; its full detailed derivation was not available for direct examination. Accordingly the exact formula's publication-priority status is less certain than the independent proof, and the arithmetic phase classification/jitter singularity is the more specific contribution. No claim is made that general renewal-walk methods are new.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes fixed angular speed between turns, an instantaneous constant turn angle, and independent strictly positive run durations of finite mean. It does not cover arbitrary random turn angles, angular Brownian noise, infinite-mean renewals, a functional central limit theorem, or an operational limit in which the jitter parameter and observation time approach their limits jointly. A possibly overlapping 2017 general run-and-turn study could contain stronger results; only partial source coverage is presently documented. The Poisson limit is a consistency check, not an independently new theorem.
