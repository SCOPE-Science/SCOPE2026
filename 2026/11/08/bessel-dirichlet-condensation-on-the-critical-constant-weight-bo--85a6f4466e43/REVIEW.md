# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The canonical coefficient identity follows exactly from the binomial expansion of the product generating function. For each fixed number of perturbatively marked sites, the bulk law of large numbers gives the normalized coefficient. A summable factorial majorant permits exchange of the number-of-sites limit with the sum over *all* marked-site counts. The exponentially small unmarked-only term follows from a positive exponential moment. The conditional bulk Radon–Nikodym bound and uniform-composition Riemann sums establish that the *observable* number of macroscopic sites and their ranked occupations have the stated limits; no inference from finite experiments to infinite volume is needed. The asserted topology is the supremum norm, not the false stronger assertion of mass-norm convergence. Reproducibility is supported by an exact-rational checker and an independent finite-volume numerical diagnostic. Neither checker is a proof of the limit.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The motivating full-text 2026 study explicitly leaves its critical line open (Sections 2.2–3.4, in particular the paragraph following Theorem 3); its published off-boundary results do not imply the finite Bessel–Dirichlet law. Its binomial coefficient expansion is prior work and is not claimed as new. The 2024 zero-range study also explicitly identifies the critical gamma-two regime as unresolved; its fixed-volume simplex limit uses a different order of limits. The 2022 Poisson–Dirichlet classification concerns occupation-dependent weights and yields infinitely many positive macroscopic parts, unlike this finite-count law. Semantic indexed searches and accessible prior-claim comparisons found no direct coverage. The main residual risk is earlier unindexed work on singular coefficient asymptotics or related critical condensation models.

## Value — PASS
At the physically motivated boundary separating a guaranteed single macroscopic condensate from mesoscopic clusters, the exact number of macroscopic sites, their probabilities, their entire ranked mass allocation, and the first-order canonical normalization are genuine missing natural invariants. The result distinguishes finite random fragmentation from Poisson–Dirichlet universality and holds for a whole class of bulk laws rather than a single parameter example. It is an equilibrium theorem, not a claim about dynamics or general perturbative exponents.
