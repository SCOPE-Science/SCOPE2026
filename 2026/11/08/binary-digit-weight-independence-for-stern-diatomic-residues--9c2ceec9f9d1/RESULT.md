# Binary-digit-weight independence for Stern diatomic residues

## Finding

Let \(s(0)=0\), \(s(1)=1\), \(s(2n)=s(n)\) for \(n\ge1\), and \(s(2n+1)=s(n)+s(n+1)\) for \(n\ge1\). Write \(w_2(n)\) for the number of ones in the ordinary binary representation of \(n\). For arbitrary fixed integers \(m,h\ge2\), put
\[
J_2(m)=m^2\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-2}),
\]
where the product runs over distinct primes dividing \(m\). For \(a,b\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) and \(t\in\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z\) satisfying \(\gcd(a,b,m)=1\), define
\[
A_{m,h}(X;a,b,t)=\#\{1\le n<X:(s(n),s(n+1))\equiv(a,b)\pmod m,\quad w_2(n)\equiv t\pmod h\}.
\]
Then there exists \(\delta_{m,h}>0\) such that **simultaneously for all primitive residue pairs and digit-weight classes**,
\[
A_{m,h}(X;a,b,t)=\frac{X}{hJ_2(m)}+O_{m,h}(X^{1-\delta_{m,h}})\qquad(X\ge2).
\]
More precisely, there is \(0<\theta_{m,h}<1\) with, uniformly over these classes and all integers \(k\ge0\),
\[
\#\{2^k\le n<2^{k+1}:(s(n),s(n+1))\equiv(a,b)\pmod m,\ w_2(n)\equiv t\pmod h\}
=\frac{2^k}{hJ_2(m)}+O_{m,h}((2\theta_{m,h})^k).
\]
Nonprimitive residue pairs never occur. Thus the joint limiting law is the product of the uniform law on primitive pairs modulo \(m\) and the uniform law on binary-weight residues modulo \(h\), with a power-saving error.

## Assumptions and scope

The modulus \(m\) and the digit-weight modulus \(h\) are fixed positive integers at least two; no primality or coprimality relation between them is assumed. The residues \(a,b,t\) are arbitrary in their indicated rings. For integral representatives \(a,b\), the condition \(\gcd(a,b,m)=1\) is representative-independent. Implied constants and the positive saving exponent may depend on \(m,h\), not on the target residues or \(X\). The binary expansion uses no leading zeros. All counts use \(1\le n<X\).

## Proof

**1. Primitive state space.** Put \(V_m=\{(a,b)\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^2:\gcd(a,b,m)=1\}\), and \(\Omega=V_m\times\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z\). The exact number of primitive vectors is
\[
|V_m|=J_2(m)=m^2\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-2}),
\]
by the Chinese remainder theorem and exclusion of vectors that vanish simultaneously modulo a prime. The Stern adjacency identity \(\gcd(s(n),s(n+1))=1\) follows inductively from the recurrences. In particular, every occupied residue vector belongs to \(V_m\).

**2. Two permutations on the lifted state space.** For \(v=(a,b,t)\in\Omega\), set
\[
L(v)=(a,a+b,t),\qquad R(v)=(a+b,b,t+1),
\]
with the two vector coordinates modulo \(m\) and the last modulo \(h\). Both are permutations. The Stern recurrences and \(w_2(2n)=w_2(n)\), \(w_2(2n+1)=w_2(n)+1\) show that the states of the children \(2n,2n+1\) of an index \(n\ge1\) are \(L(v_n),R(v_n)\). The root \(n=1\) has state \((1,1,1\bmod h)\). Thus each dyadic block \([2^k,2^{k+1})\) consists of all length-\(k\) words in these two permutations applied to that root.

**3. Transitivity of the augmented action.** Regard the vector actions as elementary matrices
\[
\mathbf L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
They generate \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\): the integral elementary matrices generate \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\), and the reduction map onto \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\) is surjective. For completeness, surjectivity can be seen by lifting any primitive modular first column to a coprime integral column, completing it by Bézout to a unimodular integral matrix, and applying elementary column reductions; the second columns differ by an elementary shear. To lift the first column, choose nonzero integral \(A\equiv a\pmod m\) and \(B\equiv b\pmod m\); primes dividing \(A\) and \(m\) cannot divide \(B\), and for every other prime dividing \(A\) one can avoid its unique forbidden residue by replacing \(B\) with \(B+jm\). This yields \(\gcd(A,B+jm)=1\). Therefore \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\) is transitive on \(V_m\).

There is no obstruction from the extra digit-weight coordinate. In the matrix group one has the exact identity
\[
\mathbf L\mathbf R^{-1}\mathbf L
=\mathbf R^{-1}\mathbf L\mathbf R^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The digit-weight increments of the left and right words are \(-1\) and \(-2\), respectively. Taking the quotient of these two words in the finite permutation group generated by \(L,R\) therefore yields the transformation \((a,b,t)\mapsto(a,b,t+1)\). Hence the generated group contains every independent digit-coordinate translation as well as the full group of elementary matrix actions on \(V_m\); it acts transitively on \(\Omega\).

**4. Primitive finite Markov chain.** Let \(P=(P_L+P_R)/2\) act on probability distributions on \(\Omega\), where \(P_L,P_R\) are the permutation matrices. The uniform distribution \(\pi\) on \(\Omega\) is stationary because both moves permute the states. In a finite permutation group, each generator inverse is a positive power of the generator, so group transitivity implies directed reachability using only \(L,R\); consequently, the chain is irreducible. The state \((0,1,t)\) has an \(L\)-self-loop for every \(t\), hence the irreducible chain is aperiodic. The finite-state Perron–Frobenius theorem now provides \(0<\theta<1\) and a constant \(C\), depending only on \(m,h\), such that for every initial state \(v\in\Omega\) and target \(u\in\Omega\),
\[
|P^k(v,u)-1/(hJ_2(m))|\le C\theta^k.
\]
(The value of \(\theta\) can be chosen strictly between the subleading spectral radius and one to absorb possible Jordan factors.) Multiplying by \(2^k\) proves the exact dyadic-block asymptotic.

**5. General index cutoffs.** A binary prefix decomposition of \([1,X)\) includes the complete level sets \([2^j,2^{j+1})\) below the highest level, and decomposes the partial highest level into at most one lexicographic subtree of each depth. Consequently there are at most two sets of descendants of each depth \(j\), each from an allowed root state. Such a set of descendants of depth \(j\) contributes its size times the uniform state mass plus \(O_{m,h}((2\theta)^j)\), uniformly in that root. Summing over \(j\le\log_2 X\), the total error is
\[
O_{m,h}\bigl(1+\log X+X^{\max(0,\log_2(2\theta))}\bigr).
\]
Choosing any fixed \(0<\delta_{m,h}<\min\{1,-\log_2\theta\}\) absorbs this bound into \(O_{m,h}(X^{1-\delta_{m,h}})\). The total cardinality of the disjoint binary subtrees is \(X-1\), and replacing this by \(X\) costs at most a constant. This proves the statement for all \(X\ge2\).

## Verification

The accompanying standalone script `check_stern.py` independently generates Stern values from their arithmetic recurrences and separately generates the finite-state evolution. For each \(2\le m\le12\), \(2\le h\le6\), it checks that the reachable state set equals all \(hJ_2(m)\) primitive states, that the explicit self-loop exists, and that the two counts agree state-by-state in all dyadic blocks of depths \(0\) through \(9\). All 55 modulus pairs passed. These computations do not prove transitivity or the power-saving bound for all moduli; Steps 3–5 supply those proofs.

To reproduce: run `python3 check_stern.py`; the stored output is `check_output.txt`. Both files are self-contained and require only the standard library.

## Relationship to prior work

Reznick, *Regularity Properties of the Stern Enumeration of the Rationals* (2008), proves equidistribution of the adjacent Stern pair modulo a fixed modulus, but does not impose a binary digit-weight congruence; taking the marginal over the digit-weight classes here recovers the corresponding uniform limit. Bettin, Drappeau, and Spiegelhofer, *Statistical distribution of the Stern sequence* (first publicly posted 18 April 2017), study distribution on the logarithmic size scale and transfer operators, rather than the joint finite-state congruence/weight law proved here. Beck and Dilcher (2021) relate Stern-polynomial coefficient matrices to the Prouhet–Thue–Morse sequence, but their identity is not an adjacent-residue joint equidistribution theorem. General finite-state Markov-chain mixing is an ingredient in the proof, not a claim of a new general spectral theorem.

## Limitations

The saving exponent \(\delta_{m,h}\) is existential and may depend on the two moduli; no bound uniform in growing moduli is claimed. The statement concerns binary digit **weight**, not arbitrary fixed-position digit patterns or the length of the binary expansion. The theorem is a mathematical deduction from an explicit finite transitive lift; an unindexed equivalent statement or a known general theorem specializing to precisely this joint statistic remains an originality risk. Independent audit has not been performed.
