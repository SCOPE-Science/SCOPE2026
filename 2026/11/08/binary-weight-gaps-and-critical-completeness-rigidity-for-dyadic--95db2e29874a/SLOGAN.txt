A single dyadic floor ray \(\{\lfloor 2^j\alpha\rfloor:j\ge1\}\) contains every sufficiently large integer as a distinct-index subset sum **only** when \(\alpha\) is a reciprocal power of two. Its canonical missing-gap length is exactly one less than the binary digit weight of the next term (clipped at zero); for a uniformly random scaling this gives an exact binomial law and almost surely \((1/4+o(1))(\log_2 X)^2\) canonically omitted integers below \(X\).
