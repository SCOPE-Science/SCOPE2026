For Brownian searchers in clusters sharing independent frozen \(\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha,1)\) diffusivities, the first-arrival tail has exact exponent \(m\min\{\alpha,k/2\}\) for \(m\) clusters of \(k\) particles, with a sharp critical logarithm. Merely regrouping six otherwise identical particles can change the mean first-arrival time from finite to infinite; at fixed population, larger shared-environment groups are always stochastically slower.
