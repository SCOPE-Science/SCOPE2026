# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For a uniformly chosen first dimer the residual intervals are independent; the proof explicitly reconstructs the two first moments and the mixed moment, solves their formal generating functions, and extracts finite-size coefficients. It proves sign for all lengths via a factorial remainder bound and exact rational evaluation of the finite initial range, and proves the asymptotic constant by cancellation. The standalone script exhaustively checks all pair-priority permutations through nine sites, compares recurrences and closed forms through one hundred sites, and replays 523 exact assertions. The enumeration is corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Semantic comparisons of published mathematical findings on Page--Rényi dimer parking, unfriendly seating, boundary occupancy, and terminal mixed moments did not identify this exact covariance. The directly relevant published full texts by Gerin (arXiv:1411.8002; EJC 2015) and Shneer--van de Ven (2016 preprint) were inspected: the former supplies marginal occupancy and a bound/example for infinite pair correlations; the latter supplies finite one-point occupancy probabilities. Neither contains the finite boundary-vacancy/global-occupancy mixed-moment formula and complete sign classification. The established first-dimer recurrence for total occupancy alone does not determine the joint moment; the new third recurrence is essential. Uninspected general historical multivariate correlation treatments, including older Page parking literature, may still imply a specialization.

## Value — PASS

The canonical one-dimensional random sequential adsorption model has a natural boundary-versus-bulk question: does a terminal vacant endpoint predict a more or less occupied final configuration? The exact mixed-moment law answers this for every path length, including the nontrivial zero exception at five sites, and identifies a persistent negative constant in the long-path limit. This structural dependence statistic is not recoverable from the individual one-point means and helps distinguish finite-boundary effects from global jamming density.

## Limitations

The result concerns ordinary unbiased dimers on finite paths. No extension to other geometries, nonuniform priorities, or other car lengths is proved. Historical-priority verification is same-model and is not a claim of exhaustive novelty search.
