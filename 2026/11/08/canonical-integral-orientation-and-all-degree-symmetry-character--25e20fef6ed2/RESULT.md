# Canonical integral orientation and all-degree symmetry characters of graph flex complexes

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph **without isolated vertices**, with connected components \(G_1,\ldots,G_s\), and let \(A=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Let \(F(G)=\operatorname{Flex}(G)\) be the source/sink-flip cubical complex of graph orientations. Write \(\mathscr B=\pi_0(F(G))\). For \(b\in\mathscr B\), the corresponding connected component is a product \(X_b=\prod_{i=1}^s X_{b,i}\), where \(X_{b,i}\) is a connected component of \(F(G_i)\). By Kozlov's theorem, each \(X_{b,i}\) is either contractible or homotopy equivalent to a circle. Put \(I_b=\{i:X_{b,i}\simeq S^1\}\).

**Theorem.** There are canonical choices of positive integral generators in the first homology of every circular component of \(F(G_i)\), natural under **all graph isomorphisms**. Consequently, for every \(q\ge0\), the entire integral automorphism representation is the following explicitly signed permutation module:
\[
\boxed{H_q(F(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\bigoplus_{b\in\mathscr B}\bigwedge^q\mathbb Z[I_b].}
\]
The graph automorphism \(g\) carries the basis wedge \(e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_q}\) in the \(b\)-summand to \(e_{g(i_1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{g(i_q)}\) in the \(g(b)\)-summand, with exactly the usual reordering sign and **no additional orientation sign**. The zeroth exterior power is \(\mathbb Z\); exterior powers above \(|I_b|\) are zero.

In particular, when \(G\) is connected, let \(\mathcal C(G)\) be the set of components of \(F(G)\) homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\). Then
\[
\boxed{H_1(F(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z[\mathcal C(G)]}
\]
as an integral \(A\)-permutation module, and all higher homology vanishes. Every automorphism has nonnegative integral \(H_1\)-trace equal to its number of fixed circular components.

For every subgroup \(H\le A\) and every integer \(q\ge0\), the **rational Betti numbers of the actual geometric quotient** follow without a homology computation:
\[
\boxed{b_q(F(G)/H;\mathbb Q)=\frac1{|H|}\sum_{g\in H}\sum_{\substack{b\in\mathscr B\\g(b)=b}}
[t^q]\prod_{C\in\operatorname{Cyc}(g|_{I_b})}\bigl(1-(-t)^{|C|}\bigr).}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Cyc}(g|_{I_b})\) means the cycles of the permutation induced by \(g\) on the circular factor indices of an invariant component \(b\). The empty product is \(1\). These formulas determine the complete graded rational character of \(A\) and all subgroup-quotient rational Betti numbers. They do not claim the quotient integral homology is torsion-free.

**Sign-sensitive example.** Take \(G=K_2\sqcup K_2\) and let \(H\cong C_2\) exchange the two graph components. Each factor has a flex circle, hence \(F(G)\simeq S^1\times S^1\). The exchange acts by the permutation matrix on integral first homology but by \(-1\) on integral second homology. The quotient therefore has rational Betti numbers \(b_0=b_1=1\) and \(b_2=0\). This illustrates why the exterior/Koszul sign cannot be discarded even though every individual flex circle has an orientation-preserving automorphism action.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, undirected and have no isolated vertices. This excludes the degenerate isolated-vertex flex convention; the theorem otherwise allows disconnected graphs and arbitrarily many edges, including cycles. An orientation assigns a direction to each graph edge. A vertex is a sink (respectively source) when all incident edges point inward (respectively outward). A legal flex reverses all incident directions at one such vertex. A set of pairwise nonadjacent flippable vertices indexes a cube, as in the source definition. Graph automorphisms act on the cubical complex by relabeling vertices and transporting orientations.

For every connected factor \(G_i\), write \(n_i=|V(G_i)|\ge2\). Nothing in the theorem asserts an equivariant deformation retraction of the full complex onto the circles: only the **integral homology action**, together with the rational homology of finite-group quotients, is classified.

## Proof
**1. A natural closed integer cochain.** For a connected graph \(K\) with at least one edge, orient every cubical one-cell corresponding to a flex at \(v\) **from the orientation in which \(v\) is a sink to the orientation in which it is a source**. This direction is unambiguous, even if different one-cells happen to share endpoints. Define the integer one-cochain \(\alpha_K\) to have value \(+1\) on each such directed one-cell. It is preserved exactly by every graph isomorphism, since an isomorphism preserves the sink/source distinction.

Consider any two-dimensional cube associated to two distinct, nonadjacent flippable vertices \(u,v\). Since their incident edge sets are disjoint, changing directions at one vertex cannot change whether the other is a source or sink. The two opposite \(u\)-edges of this square therefore have equal values under \(\alpha_K\), as do the two opposite \(v\)-edges. The cellular boundary signs cancel them pairwise. Hence \(\delta\alpha_K=0\), and \(\alpha_K\) represents a natural class in \(H^1(F(K);\mathbb Z)\). The square calculation suffices to establish closedness in every dimension.

**2. Exact periods and positive integral generators.** Kozlov's core-cycle theorem (Theorem 6.1, with the explicit sink-flip parametrization in Proposition 6.7) states that each noncontractible component \(X\) of \(F(K)\) collapses to an \(n\)-edge cycle, where \(n=|V(K)|\), traversed by flexing **each vertex exactly once as a sink**. Therefore \(\langle\alpha_K,[\Gamma]\rangle=n\) on that oriented core cycle, while \(\Gamma\) generates \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). Contractible components have trivial first homology. The positive generator \(c_X\) of each circular component is consequently characterized intrinsically by \(\langle[\alpha_K],c_X\rangle=n\), independently of the vertex labeling used to construct a core cycle. Equivalently, \([\alpha_K]\) is divisible by \(n\) as an **integral cohomology class**, and its quotient evaluates to \(+1\) on every positive generator. This is a statement about cohomology, not pointwise division of integral cochains. Uniqueness and naturality give \(g_*(c_X)=c_{gX}\) for every graph isomorphism \(g\).

**3. Disconnected graphs and exterior signs.** For \(G=\coprod_i G_i\), its flex complex is the cubical Cartesian product \(F(G)=\prod_i F(G_i)\) (Kozlov, Definition 2.9). Each component \(X_b\) is a product of point-like factors and circle-like factors. The integral Künneth theorem applies without Tor terms, because all factor homology is free and concentrated in degrees zero and one. Choosing the canonical positive generators in Step 2 yields \(H_q(X_b;\mathbb Z)\cong\bigwedge^q\mathbb Z[I_b]\). The Eilenberg–Zilber shuffle sign when two degree-one factors are exchanged is exactly the antisymmetry of the exterior product. No factor can acquire a minus sign under an isomorphism by Step 2. Therefore graph automorphisms act precisely by the announced signed permutation on wedges, transported between component summands. Summing over \(b\) proves the complete integral module formula.

**4. Rational quotient characters.** For a fixed \(g\in A\), a summand indexed by \(b\) contributes zero to its homology trace unless \(g(b)=b\). In an invariant summand, \(g\) acts on \(\mathbb Z[I_b]\) by an ordinary permutation matrix \(P\), so the graded trace is \(\det(I+tP)\). A cycle of length \(d\) contributes \(1-(-t)^d\). This proves the stated coefficient formula for \(\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_q(F(G);\mathbb Q))\). For a finite group acting cellularly on a finite complex, rational transfer, equivalently equivariant triangulation and coinvariant chains, identifies \(H_q(F(G)/H;\mathbb Q)\) with the invariant rational homology \(H_q(F(G);\mathbb Q)^H\). Averaging character values over \(H\) gives the quotient Betti-number formula. This finishes the proof.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify_flex.py` directly constructs orientation states, source/sink flexes, all cubical cells, and graph automorphisms for every connected labeled graph on two, three, or four vertices, together with five representative five-vertex graphs. It checks the integer cochain condition on every enumerated square and exact invariance under every enumerated graph automorphism. It computes Euler characteristics by **all** cubical dimensions, verifies their point/circle values, and finds positive length-\(n\) cycles in every circular component for graphs of at most four vertices. Exact execution produces `ALL_VERIFY_OK graphs=48 orientations=1798 cells=3158 squares=201 automorphisms=326 core_cycles=85 components=964 circular_components=122 fixed_circular_character_sum=396`.

Run `python3 verify_flex.py` from the directory containing the verifier; Python 3 standard library only. This enumeration is finite corroboration; the general result follows from Steps 1–4 and the fully stated Kozlov core-cycle theorem, not from extrapolation or numerical experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's 2026 full-text paper constructs the flex complex, proves its factor-product property, and classifies the **ordinary homotopy type** as a disjoint union of tori. It moreover gives the essential length-\(n\) positive sink-flip core cycles. These are incorporated as prior results and not asserted as new. The new statement identifies a canonical integral cocycle and its primitive cohomology class, resolves natural orientations simultaneously for every graph, and determines the full graded **integral automorphism representation** and every subgroup-quotient rational Betti number.

A previous published result on *complete-graph* flex complexes computes explicit \(S_n\)-characters and symmetric/alternating quotient topology. It already observes positive sink flips for that special case and is not a new contribution of this theorem. The present result holds for **arbitrary connected and disconnected graphs without isolated vertices**, and determines all homological degrees including the exterior/Koszul signs, although it does not replicate the special-case numerical enumeration or homeomorphisms of quotients. A separate prior result classifies orientation-state connectivity and distances by cycle anchors, not the homology representations or graph-automorphism signs.

## Limitations
The result does not compute the action of automorphisms on the component set \(\mathscr B\), which can still be combinatorially difficult; it supplies the exact representation **conditional on that finite combinatorial action**. It does not supply equivariant homotopy equivalences, integral quotient homology or fundamental groups, or cover the convention-dependent isolated-vertex case. The underlying ordinary torus classification and core-circle property are pre-existing. No claim of exhaustive literature uniqueness or independent validation is made.
