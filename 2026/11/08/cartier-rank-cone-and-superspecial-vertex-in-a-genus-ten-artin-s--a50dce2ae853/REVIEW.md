# Review of the Cartier-rank cone

**Correctness — PASS.** The ten-element basis follows from pole orders and genus. The Cartier calculation lists all possible nonzero entries of the fifth-power matrix; the case split gives ranks exactly \(4,3,0\), and elementary algebra identifies the irreducible jump cone. A verifier that reconstructs rather than transcribes the matrix checks all \(625\) points of the coefficient space over \(\mathbb F_5\) and \(3125\) coordinate translations. The finite checks are not used as an infinite-field proof.

**Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority.** Pries–Zhu address the \(p\)-rank of Artin–Schreier strata; Farnell–Pries's constant-rank theorem assumes \(p\equiv1\pmod L\), which fails for pole order \(6\) in characteristic \(5\). The Hermitian superspecial vertex is known, but the full coefficient-level cone with ranks \(4,3,0\) and absence of intermediate values is not implied. Farnell's thesis contains related algorithms, and implicit historical computations remain a priority risk. Targeted published-record searches did not return an equivalent or stronger explicit classification.

**Value — PASS.** This is a complete, structurally motivated classification of the \(a\)-number across the normalized deformation family of the classical Hermitian curve. The nonlinear irreducible jumping cone and its superspecial singular line explain a change invisible to genus and \(5\)-rank. It is neither a single numerical case nor a routine restatement of bounds.

**Limitations.** Not a classification of unmarked isomorphism classes, Newton polygons or all Ekedahl–Oort types. The earlier thesis's full collection of computed tables is a remaining historical-priority risk; no independent scientific or historical audit has taken place.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
