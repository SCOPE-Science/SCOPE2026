# Scientific review: cyclic genus-four double-elliptic Cartier stratification

## Correctness — PASS

The exact assertion is restricted to the smooth geometric coefficient domain \(t^2\ne1\) over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_5\) or \(\overline{\mathbf F}_7\). The branch divisor is the union of two disjoint three-point transverse hyperplane sections of the fixed smooth elliptic cubic; this proves smooth genus four. The standard complete-intersection Hasse–Witt coefficient lemma yields a full diagonal matrix, and two independent exact symbolic algorithms verify every entry at both primes. Nonzero diagonal entries survive every Frobenius iteration, so stable rank equals ordinary matrix rank and the \(a\)-number is its nullity. The base elliptic Hasse invariants and the prime-to-\(p\) Prym isogeny establish the three-dimensional Prym invariants. The exceptional parameter equations are separable and avoid the singular parameters. The proof extends over every geometric parameter, not only points in the prime field. Residual proof risk: the argument invokes standard Frobenius/Cartier and Prym-isogeny comparison lemmas, which are given with their relevant hypotheses; no independent audit has been carried out.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

The closest primary comparison is Ogasawara–Ohashi–Sakata–Harashita, *Superspecial genus-4 double covers of elliptic curves*, arXiv:2403.08139 (2024-03-12), especially Sections 2.2, 2.4, 4.3 and 5; its entire publicly accessible HTML article was consulted at relevant geometric, formula, classification, and conclusion passages. That work explicitly supplies the general double-elliptic construction, smoothness criterion and complete-intersection Hasse–Witt coefficient formula. It *also* records the known unique superspecial genus-four isomorphism class in characteristic five and known nonexistence of superspecial genus-four curves in characteristic seven. None is new here. Its published conclusions focus on curves with superspecial entire Jacobians; they do not state the two-characteristic full parameter stratification or the characteristic-seven phenomenon of a superspecial Prym over an ordinary elliptic curve with non-superspecial genus-four Jacobian. Its Section 5 discusses precisely the separation of Prym and elliptic superspeciality. The present result requires evaluating and factoring the special family's full matrix and subtracting the elliptic factor; it is not an immediate specialization of the earlier classification table. The older Kudo–Harashita global characteristic-seven theorem excludes superspecial full Jacobians only. Hyperelliptic cyclic genus-four Hasse–Witt computations concern a distinct non-isomorphic class of embedded curves. Search results from the mathematical record and accessible historical claims were compared by implication, not merely by matching titles. A further unindexed computation of this same special family would defeat the priority assertion and remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A naturally cyclic one-parameter locus inside the six-dimensional space of genus-four double covers of elliptic curves has its complete \(p\)-rank and \(a\)-number stratification in the first two relevant odd characteristics. More substantially, its distinguished Prym becomes superspecial in characteristic seven at one exact geometric parameter even though the elliptic base is ordinary, a separation specifically relevant to the motivating paper's Prym discussion and invisible in its global superspecial-Jacobian table. The characteristic-five exceptional two-point locus is acknowledged as lying in an already known isomorphism class; the value is the complete parametrized stratification and the cross-characteristic Prym comparison, not a claim to rediscover that class. No unwarranted full-moduli, geometric isomorphism-class, or Newton-polygon classification is asserted.
