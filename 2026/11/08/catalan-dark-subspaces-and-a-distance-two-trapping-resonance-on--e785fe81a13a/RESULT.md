# Catalan dark subspaces and a distance-two trapping resonance on subset quantum networks

## Finding
Let \(n\ge 2k+1\) and \(k\ge2\) be integers. The network vertices are the \(k\)-subsets of \([n]\). Place a lossy absorber at every subset containing a distinguished point \(*\). Consider separately the nearest-neighbor Johnson network \(J(n,k)\), whose edges exchange one element, and the Kneser network \(KG(n,k)\), whose edges join disjoint subsets. Give every graph edge the same nonzero real hopping amplitude and give each absorbing vertex an arbitrary strictly positive loss rate.

**Theorem A (identical eventual-capture laws).** The two networks have **exactly the same** asymptotic loss-free orthogonal projector \(P\), for all choices of positive loss rates. Let \(U\) be the \(k\)-subsets not containing \(*\), and put \(r=k-|S\cap T|\). Its matrix, indexed by all vertices, vanishes whenever either index is absorbing; for \(S,T\in U\),
\[
 P_{S,T}=\frac{n-2k}{n-k}\,\frac{(-1)^r}{\binom{n-k-1}{r}}.
\]
Its rank is \(\binom{n-1}{k}-\binom{n-1}{k-1}\), which becomes the Catalan number \(\frac1{k+1}\binom{2k}{k}\) when \(n=2k+1\). For every density matrix \(\rho\) of trace one, eventual capture has probability \(1-\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho)\). A localized untrapped vertex is eventually captured with probability \(\frac{k}{n-k}\).

**Theorem B (first intermediate-distance resonance).** On the distance-two relation graph on the \(3\)-subsets of \([7]\) (distinct triples adjacent when they intersect in exactly one element), retain the same 15 star absorbers. Among its 20 untrapped vertices, take the complement involution \(C\) of triples in the six-point unmarked ground set. The loss-free projector is exactly \(P_2=(I-C)/2\), with rank 10. In contrast, the rank in each of the nearest-neighbor and disjointness graphs is 5. For a complementary pair \(S,T\), the state \((|S\rangle-|T\rangle)/\sqrt2\) has eventual capture probability zero in the distance-two network and \(1/2\) in either comparison network; the plus superposition is fully captured in all three. The result holds for all strictly positive, independently specified absorber rates and all nonzero real uniform hopping amplitudes.

## Assumptions and scope
The finite-dimensional no-click evolution is generated by \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}=gA-i\Gamma\), where \(g\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\), \(A\) is the selected unweighted undirected adjacency matrix, and \(\Gamma=\sum_{D\ni *}\gamma_D|D\rangle\langle D|\), with each \(\gamma_D>0\) (in units with \(\hbar=1\)). The survival probability of \(\rho\) at time \(t\) is \(\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-itH_{\mathrm{eff}}}\rho e^{itH_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger})\); its limit is the loss-free population. No arbitrary onsite potentials, edge disorder, finite-time optimum, repeated projective measurements, or zero loss rates are asserted. Vertices and density matrices may carry arbitrary coherent complex amplitudes.

Write \(v=n-1\); the unmarked point set has size \(v\). Absorbers are indexed by \((k-1)\)-subsets \(R\) and unmarked vertices by \(k\)-subsets \(S\). Denote by \(W_{a,b}\) the binary inclusion matrix with entry \(1[I\subseteq S]\) for \(|I|=a\), \(|S|=b\).

## Proof
**1. Inclusion rank lemma.** For integers \(0\le a\le b\le v-a\), \(W_{a,b}\) has full row rank over \(\mathbb R\). For \(a=0\) this is immediate. Suppose a linear combination of its rows vanishes. For two distinct ground elements \(x,y\), subtract the equations belonging to \(T\cup\{x\}\) and \(T\cup\{y\}\) for every \((b-1)\)-subset \(T\) outside \(\{x,y\}\). The differences of the coefficients indexed by \(R\cup\{x\}\) and \(R\cup\{y\}\) then lie in the left kernel of \(W_{a-1,b-1}\) on \(v-2\) points. By induction, every such difference is zero. Thus coefficients at adjacent \(a\)-subsets coincide. Connectedness of their exchange graph makes every coefficient equal; the original equations force this common value to vanish. The induction applies because \(b-1\le(v-2)-(a-1)\).

**2. Identical boundary kernels.** The absorber-to-unmarked adjacency blocks are
\[
 B_J(R,S)=1[R\subset S],\qquad B_K(R,S)=1[R\cap S=\varnothing].
\]
The first is \(W_{k-1,k}\), hence has full row rank \(\binom v{k-1}\), since \(v\ge2k\). The second becomes \(W_{k-1,v-k}\) after complementing the unmarked column index; it has the same full row rank. Its row functions expand as
\[
 1[R\cap S=\varnothing]=\sum_{I\subseteq R}(-1)^{|I|}1[I\subseteq S].
\]
For \(|I|\le k-1\), the function \(1[I\subseteq S]\) lies in the row span of \(B_J\), because
\[
 \sum_{\substack{R\supseteq I\\ |R|=k-1}}1[R\subseteq S]
 =(k-|I|)1[I\subseteq S].
\]
Therefore \(B_K\) and \(B_J\) have identical row spaces and identical kernels \(Q\), of dimension \(d=\binom v k-\binom v{k-1}\).

**3. Complete characterization of dark states.** Every \(f\in Q\) has \(\sum_{S\supseteq I}f(S)=0\) for each \(I\) of cardinality less than \(k\), by the averaging identity just used. The untrapped induced Johnson adjacency obeys \(B_J^\top B_J=kI+A_{J,U}\). Thus \(A_{J,U}f=-kf\). Inclusion–exclusion yields, for the induced Kneser adjacency,
\[
 (A_{K,U}f)(S)=\sum_{T:T\cap S=\varnothing}f(T)
 =\sum_{I\subseteq S}(-1)^{|I|}\sum_{T\supseteq I}f(T)
 =(-1)^k f(S).
\]
The boundary blocks annihilate \(Q\), so all its vectors, extended by zero on the absorber vertices, are full-adjacency eigenvectors annihilated by \(\Gamma\). Conversely, any nondecaying eigenvector of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) must have zero absorber population: taking the imaginary part of its eigenvalue identity forces \(\langle\psi,\Gamma\psi\rangle=0\). Its boundary equations then put it in \(\ker B_J\) or \(\ker B_K\), exactly \(Q\). The subspace \(Q\) reduces both \(A\) and \(\Gamma\). On its orthogonal complement every eigenvalue of the dissipative generator has strictly negative real part; finite-dimensional Jordan theory implies decay to zero. Consequently the full eventual-survival map is orthogonal projection onto \(Q\), and the loss is \(1-\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho)\).

**4. Exact projector entries.** The projector onto \(Q\) commutes with every permutation of the \(v\) unmarked points, so \(P_{S,T}=a_r\) depends only on \(r=k-|S\cap T|\). The equation \(B_JP=0\) at a fixed \((k-1)\)-subset \(R\) and column \(T\), with \(|R\cap T|=k-1-r\), gives
\[
 (r+1)a_r+(v-k-r)a_{r+1}=0\quad(0\le r<k).
\]
Hence \(a_r=(-1)^r a_0/\binom{v-k}{r}\). Since all diagonal entries are equal and \(\operatorname{rank}P=d\),
\[
 a_0=\frac d{\binom vk}=\frac{v-2k+1}{v-k+1}=\frac{n-2k}{n-k}.
\]
This establishes the displayed kernel and localized capture value. With \(n=2k+1\), \(d=\binom{2k}{k}-\binom{2k}{k-1}\) equals the stated Catalan number.

**5. Distance-two resonance.** Specialize to \(n=7,k=3\). The unmarked points form a six-set, with 20 triples grouped into 10 complementary pairs. The absorber-to-unmarked distance-two block is \(B_2(R,S)=1[|R\cap S|=1]\), where \(R\) is a two-set. Its columns for \(S\) and its six-set complement \(S^c\) coincide. Compressing these equal columns to a 15-by-10 matrix \(M\), each column indicates the nine edges of \(K_6\) crossing a balanced three–three partition. Distinct partitions have five crossing edges in common, whereas each partition has nine, so
\[
 M^\top M=4I_{10}+5\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top.
\]
This is positive definite. Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}B_2=10\) and its kernel is exactly the 10-dimensional space antisymmetric under triple complementation. Since complementation preserves intersection cardinalities among triples of six points, it commutes with the unmarked distance-two adjacency. Thus this entire kernel and no larger subspace is loss-free, with projector \((I-C)/2\). For a complementary pair, the projector entries are \(1/2\) and \(-1/2\); comparing with the formula of Theorem A at \(n=7,k=3,r=3\), which gives diagonal \(1/4\) and off-diagonal \(-1/4\), proves the claimed phase-sensitive probabilities.

## Verification
The independent algebraic identities are supplemented by the included `verify.py` file, which uses exact rational SymPy matrices. It checks boundary ranks, row-space equality, full projector idempotence, trace, and both induced adjacency eigenvalue identities for 14 parameter choices with unmarked dimension at most 36; the middle-shell test checks the complete 15-by-20 boundary matrix, complement involution, 10-dimensional kernel, and the exact 10-by-10 partition Gram matrix. Run `python verify.py` in an environment with Python 3 and SymPy. A successful output includes `VERIFY_OK` and `J_2(7,3) resonance: (15, 20, 10)`. The proof above, not this finite testing, establishes the infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work
The non-Hermitian trapping formalism and dark-state mechanism are standard in quantum-walk studies [1–3]. Johnson eigenvalues and association-scheme primitive idempotents are classical [4,5], so the highest Johnson harmonic, its dimension, and a familiar idempotent kernel are not independently claimed as new. Johnson quantum walk search with a **single** marked subset was studied in [6]. The contribution here is the simultaneous physically exact absorption projector for two distinct graph families and arbitrary positive trap strengths, together with the distance-two graph's explicit complement-antisymmetric resonance and coherent discrimination at the same marked star. Neither the general trapping formalism nor the spectral-table formulas directly state this combined comparison.

## Limitations
The result is for finite uniform-edge adjacency hopping and strictly positive loss on every star-marked site. It gives asymptotic absorption, not first-hit distributions, mean times, asymptotic rates, or transport robustness. The exact Johnson projector is a known association-scheme type of object; novelty concerns the paired trapping interpretation and the intermediate-shell resonance rather than rediscovery of a primitive idempotent. Searches of available publications and research records found no direct prior statement of the combined theorem, but an unindexed classical incidence/scheme or quantum-observability formulation may dominate it; independent priority assessment remains outstanding.
