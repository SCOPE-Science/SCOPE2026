# Characteristic-three Wiman lines become the complete secants of a ten-point conic

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[\alpha]/(\alpha^2+1)\). Specialize Harbourne's explicit 45-line Wiman arrangement over the coefficient ring of the root \(a\) of \(a^4-a^2+4\), setting \(a=\alpha\), \(A=\alpha-1\), and \(B=1\). The resulting 45 *distinct* projective lines are exactly the 45 secants joining unordered pairs of the ten \(k\)-points on the nonsingular conic
\[
Q:\quad x^2+y^2+z^2=0.
\]
Their set of crossings is all \(91\) points of \(\mathbb P^2(k)\). More precisely, the multiplicity census is \(t_4=45\), \(t_5=36\), \(t_9=10\), and every other \(t_j=0\).

The reduced union \(D\) of these lines is defined over \(\mathbb F_3\). It comprises nine geometrically distinct lines individually defined over \(\mathbb F_3\), and 18 conjugate pairs of lines defined over \(\mathbb F_9\). Its open complement \(U=\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb F_3}\setminus D\) has, for each integer \(n\ge1\), the exact point count
\[
\#U(\mathbb F_{3^n})=\begin{cases}3^{2n}-8\cdot3^n+15,&n\text{ odd},\\3^{2n}-44\cdot3^n+315,&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]
In particular \(\#U(\mathbb F_3)=\#U(\mathbb F_9)=0\), \(\#U(\mathbb F_{27})=528\), and \(\#U(\mathbb F_{81})=3312\). The zeta functions of the line union and its complement are
\[
Z(D/\mathbb F_3,T)=\frac{(1-T)^{164}(1+T)^{150}}{(1-3T)^{27}(1+3T)^{18}},\qquad
Z(U/\mathbb F_3,T)=\frac{(1-3T)^{26}(1+3T)^{18}}{(1-T)^{165}(1+T)^{150}(1-9T)}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Work in characteristic three. The specialization is specifically the homomorphism from the coefficient ring generated by a root of \(a^4-a^2+4\) and the inverses of \(4\), into \(k\), given by \(a\mapsto\alpha\). This is legitimate because \(a^4-a^2+4=(a^2+1)^2\) modulo three. The source's \(45\) linear forms are those in Example 3.3.4 of Harbourne (2016); the standalone verifier contains their exact coefficient triples. The source definitions \(A=-(a^3-3a-2)/4\), \(B=(a^3+a-2)/4\) reduce to \(A=\alpha-1\), \(B=1\). All statements about singularities refer to the reduced union of distinct lines, not an unexamined nonreduced scheme of a different model.

## Proof

**Step 1: exact identification of the specialized lines.** The field \(k\) has nine elements \(u+v\alpha\) with \(u,v\in\mathbb F_3\). Write each displayed linear form as a coefficient triple in \(k^3\), normalize by the first nonzero coefficient, and discard scalars. Direct reduction of the source's \(45\) triples yields a set \(\mathcal L\) of \(45\) distinct points of the dual projective plane; these computations are explicitly reproduced in the standalone exact-arithmetic program. Independently enumerate the \(91\) normalized nonzero triples in \(k^3\), retain those on \(Q\), and obtain exactly ten distinct conic points. Compute the normalized cross product of every unordered pair of these ten points. The resulting \(45\) distinct dual points are *equal as sets* to \(\mathcal L\); the verifier checks equality in both directions with exact nine-element-field arithmetic. This is a finite exhaustive identity between completely specified algebraic sets, rather than numerical sampling.

**Step 2: all crossing multiplicities.** A smooth conic over \(\mathbb F_q\) has exactly \(q+1\) rational points. For \(q=9\), each conic point lies on the nine joining secants. A point outside a conic lies on either two or zero rational tangent lines. Accordingly it lies on \((q-1)/2=4\) or \((q+1)/2=5\) joining secants. In the finite projective plane there are \(q(q+1)/2=45\) points of the former type and \(q(q-1)/2=36\) of the latter, the familiar external/internal conic split. Thus every one of the \(q^2+q+1=91\) rational plane points is on at least four lines. The multiplicities are \((t_4,t_5,t_9)=(45,36,10)\). As a check, \(45\binom42+36\binom52+10\binom92=990=\binom{45}{2}\). Hence these are *all geometric* crossing points: any two distinct \(k\)-rational lines meet in a \(k\)-rational point, and every geometric crossing is such a pairwise meeting.

**Step 3: descent and Frobenius.** The map \(\alpha\mapsto-\alpha\) acts as absolute Frobenius on \(k\). Exact normalized coefficient comparison gives \(\mathcal L^\sigma=\mathcal L\), with precisely nine individually fixed lines; the other \(36\) form \(18\) two-element orbits. Thus the reduced divisor \(D\) descends to \(\mathbb F_3\). All \(13\) points of \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_3)\) are crossing points and lie on at least one fixed line. The sum of the numbers of *fixed* lines through these points equals \(9(3+1)=36\). The resulting excess-incidence correction is \(36-13=23\).

**Step 4: point counts over all extensions.** For odd \(n\), set \(q=3^n\). Of the \(45\) geometric lines, exactly nine are individually \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational. If an \(\mathbb F_q\)-point lies on a line not fixed by Frobenius, it also lies on its conjugate (since \(n\) is odd), hence equals the unique intersection of the two, which is \(\mathbb F_3\)-rational and already lies on a fixed line. Therefore \(D(\mathbb F_q)\) is exactly the union of the nine fixed lines, giving \(9(q+1)-23=9q-14\). For even \(n\), every geometric line and every crossing point is \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational. The normalization separates \(45\) lines; glueing them at the \(91\) intersections removes an excess of \(45(4-1)+36(5-1)+10(9-1)=359\) points. Consequently \(\#D(\mathbb F_q)=45(q+1)-359=45q-314\). Subtract from \(\#\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_q)=q^2+q+1\) to obtain the asserted formula for \(U\).

**Step 5: zeta function.** Both parity cases combine as \(\#D(\mathbb F_{3^n})=27\cdot3^n+18(-3)^n-164-150(-1)^n\). Exponentiating the defining identity \(\log Z(D,T)=\sum_{n\ge1}\#D(\mathbb F_{3^n})T^n/n\) gives the displayed rational expression for \(Z(D,T)\). The standard closed-open zeta identity \(Z(\mathbb P^2,T)=Z(D,T)Z(U,T)\), together with \(Z(\mathbb P^2,T)=((1-T)(1-3T)(1-9T))^{-1}\), gives \(Z(U,T)\).

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` reproduces every finite exhaustive identity: reduction of all \(45\) source coefficient triples, equality with the ten-point conic secant set, full \(91\)-point incidence census, Frobenius orbit counts, and fixed-line gluing correction. As a separate transcription check, it also evaluates the source list in \(\mathbb F_{19}\) at \(a=3\) and recovers the classical \(201\)-point Wiman histogram \((t_3,t_4,t_5)=(120,45,36)\). Run `python3 verify.py`; the expected output is given verbatim in `verify_output.txt`, terminating in `VERIFY_OK`. The infinite extension formulas are deduced algebraically in the proof, not inferred from the finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Harbourne's 2016 lecture notes supply the explicit integral algebraic coordinates of the classical Wiman \(45\)-line configuration and its characteristic-zero \(201\)-point incidence table. Bauer and collaborators study the complex arrangement and its algebraic invariants. Innamorati studies the Wiman arrangement over \(\mathbb F_{19}\), where the ordinary \(201\)-point incidence geometry persists. Those statements do not imply the ramified-characteristic-three identification with every secant of a ten-point conic, nor its descendant point counts and zeta functions. The external literature and mathematical-record searches identified no explicit equivalent assertion. This is an originality assessment subject to remaining historical-priority risk, not a uniqueness proof.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the indicated coefficientwise specialization at the prime three, not every integral model of the Wiman arrangement, arbitrary good-characteristic reductions, or stable reduction of its invariant covers. The reduced union has ordinary multiple crossings; no claim is made about potentially different nonreduced reductions. The finite verifier certifies the stated explicit coefficient identities, but does not by itself establish historical priority. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
