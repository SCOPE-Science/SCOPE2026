# Characteristic-two rank expansion for idempotent derivations of free class-two Lie algebras

## Finding

Let \(q\) be a prime power, \(n\geq2\), and let
\[
L_n=V\oplus\bigwedge^2 V,\qquad V=\mathbb F_q^n,
\quad [u,v]=u\wedge v,\quad [\bigwedge^2V,L_n]=0.
\]
For every \(r\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\), write \({n\brack r}_q\) for the Gaussian binomial coefficient, \(s=n-r\), and
\[
\beta_n(r)=r\left(\binom r2+\binom s2\right)+rs^2.
\]
Let \(F_{n,q}(z)=\sum_{D\in\operatorname{Der}(L_n),\ D^2=D} z^{\operatorname{rank}D}\), with one term for each distinct linear operator.

In odd characteristic,
\[
\boxed{F_{n,q}(z)=1+{n\brack1}_q\,q^{n-1+\binom{n-1}{2}+(n-1)^2}\,z^n.}
\]
In characteristic two,
\[
\boxed{F_{n,q}(z)=\sum_{r=0}^n {n\brack r}_q\,q^{r(n-r)+\beta_n(r)}\,z^{r(n-r+1)}.}
\]
These are exact formulas over **every** finite field of the indicated characteristic, including nonprime fields. Terms with equal exponents of \(z\) must be combined.

For odd characteristic the only ranks are \(0\) and \(n\). For characteristic two the ranks are precisely
\[
\{0\}\cup\{r(n-r+1):1\le r\le n\},
\]
and the **maximum attainable rank** is \(\lfloor(n+1)^2/4\rfloor\). Thus the maximal rank is linear in the generator dimension in odd characteristic, but quadratic in characteristic two.

A further phenomenon holds for fixed even prime-power \(q\): if \(D_n\) is uniformly selected from all idempotent derivations, then
\[
\Pr\bigl(D_n|_{L_n/[L_n,L_n]}=I\bigr)
=1-O_q\!\left(nq^{-n^2/4}\right).
\]
All derivations in that dominant stratum have rank \(n\), even though characteristic two also permits ranks of order \(n^2\).

## Assumptions and scope

A derivation is a linear map satisfying \(D[x,y]=[Dx,y]+[x,Dy]\). The free class-two Lie algebra is the alternating-bracket algebra displayed above, including in characteristic two; no assertion is made about every general two-step nilpotent Lie algebra. Finite-field order \(q\) is arbitrary for the exact counts, but held fixed in the asymptotic statement. The polynomial counts embedded endomorphisms of the underlying vector space, not conjugacy classes or derivations up to isomorphism.

## Proof

**1. All derivations.** Since the algebra is generated by \(V\), a derivation is uniquely determined by arbitrary linear maps \(A:V\to V\) and \(B:V\to\bigwedge^2V\). Its matrix with respect to the displayed direct sum is
\[
D_{A,B}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\ B&C_A\end{pmatrix},
\qquad C_A(u\wedge v)=Au\wedge v+u\wedge Av.
\]
Indeed the derivation equation determines the displayed central block, which respects antisymmetry and alternating relations in every characteristic. Conversely the formula satisfies the derivation equation on all brackets. Every choice of \(A,B\) gives a distinct derivation.

**2. Idempotence.** Squaring the block matrix gives the three necessary and sufficient conditions
\[
A^2=A,\qquad C_A^2=C_A,\qquad BA+C_AB=B.
\]
For each rank-\(r\) projection \(A\), choose the canonical decomposition \(V=U\oplus W\) with \(U=\operatorname{im}A\) and \(W=\ker A\) of dimensions \(r,s\). The central space decomposes canonically into
\[
\bigwedge^2V=(\bigwedge^2U)\oplus(U\wedge W)\oplus(\bigwedge^2W).
\]
On these summands \(C_A\) acts by the scalars \(2,1,0\), respectively.

If \(q\) is odd, \(C_A^2=C_A\) forces \(\bigwedge^2U=0\), hence \(r\le1\). If \(r=0\), then \(A=C_A=0\), and the third equation gives \(B=0\), yielding just the zero derivation. If \(r=1\), the first two central blocks have dimensions \(0,s\), and the third has dimension \(\binom s2\). The last equation requires \(B(U)\subseteq\bigwedge^2W\) and \(B(W)\subseteq U\wedge W\). Hence the number of valid \(B\)'s is \(q^{\binom s2+s^2}\). There are \({n\brack1}_q q^s\) projections \(A\) of rank one (choose \(U\) and a complementary \(W\)); every associated idempotent has rank \(\dim U+\dim(U\wedge W)=n\). This proves the odd-characteristic polynomial.

If \(q\) is even, \(2=0\), so \(C_A\) is automatically idempotent and has image \(U\wedge W\) of dimension \(rs\), with kernel \(\bigwedge^2U\oplus\bigwedge^2W\). The third equation requires precisely
\[
B(U)\subseteq\ker C_A,
\qquad B(W)\subseteq\operatorname{im}C_A.
\]
There are \(q^{r(\binom r2+\binom s2)+srs}=q^{\beta_n(r)}\) such maps \(B\). Multiplying by the \({n\brack r}_q q^{rs}\) choices of \(A\) proves the coefficient formula. Because an idempotent block matrix has image dimension equal to the sum of the dimensions of the images of its diagonal blocks (conjugate it by a block lower-unitriangular map, or use the eigenspace decomposition from the last displayed inclusion), each of these \(D\)'s has rank \(r+rs=r(s+1)\).

For \(r\ge1\), the quadratic \(r(n-r+1)\) has maximum \(\lfloor(n+1)^2/4\rfloor\) at the nearest integers to \((n+1)/2\). The zero derivation has rank zero; every other stratum is nonempty, so these are all attainable ranks.

**3. Dominant stratum in characteristic two.** The \(r=n\) summand consists of \(A=I\) and arbitrary \(B\), hence has cardinality
\[
M_n=q^{n\binom n2}.
\]
For a fixed \(q\), put \(C_q=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^{-j})^{-1}<\infty\). The standard bound \({n\brack r}_q\le C_q q^{rs}\) shows that the \(r=n-s\) term is at most \(C_q q^{M'_n-\Delta_n(s)}\), where \(M'_n=n\binom n2\) and a direct expansion gives
\[
\Delta_n(s)=\frac{s}{2}\left(3n^2-6ns-5n+4s^2+4s\right).
\]
For every integer \(n\ge20\) and \(1\le s\le n\), completing the square yields
\[
\Delta_n(s)=\frac{s}{2}\left[4\left(s-\frac{3n}{4}\right)^2+\frac{3n^2}{4}-5n+4s\right]\ge\frac{sn^2}{4}.
\]
Summing \(n\) exceptional strata, their total count is at most \(M_n C_q n q^{-n^2/4}\). Divide by the full count, which is at least \(M_n\), to obtain the asserted probability. The constant in the big-O depends only on fixed \(q\).

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` independently builds the entire derivation matrix from the bracket on generators and enumerates every possible matrix over the prime fields \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5\) in generator rank two and \(\mathbb F_2\) in generator rank three. It tests \(D^2=D\) by full-matrix multiplication and calculates each rank by Gaussian elimination, rather than substituting the dimension formula. Its observed histograms are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(n,q)=(2,2):&\quad 1+16z^2,\\
(2,3):&\quad 1+36z^2,\\
(2,5):&\quad 1+150z^2,\\
(3,2):&\quad 1+1408z^3+448z^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Running `python verify.py` gives `VERIFY_OK`. These computations are finite corroboration, not a substitute for the proof above; extension fields are covered algebraically, not independently exhaustively tested.

## Relationship to prior work

Benito and Roldán-López (arXiv:1911.01897v1, first public November 5, 2019), Proposition 1 and Corollary 1, establish the general description of free-nilpotent Lie derivations by maps from generators, primarily in characteristic zero. This gives the structural starting point but does not assert idempotent-operator rank counts over finite fields. La Rosa and Mancini (arXiv:2301.11058v1, January 26, 2023) investigate derivation Lie algebras of related two-step nilpotent and Leibniz algebras, generally with characteristic not two; no equivalent finite-field idempotent rank polynomial appears in the inspected text. Eick and Moede (arXiv:2205.03155v1, May 2022) address computational characteristic-two gradings of simple Lie algebras, not this free nilpotent family. These sources supply context, not an originality guarantee.

The new rank enumeration concerns **idempotent derivations**, not idempotent Lie endomorphisms. The latter induce \(\bigwedge^2 A\) on the commutator ideal, rather than the derivation-induced \(C_A=A\wedge I+I\wedge A\), and therefore have different allowed ranks and multiplicities. Existing rank counts of free class-two Lie algebra endomorphisms do not imply the displayed formulas. Previous studies of idempotent derivations on filiform and graph Lie algebras concern different Lie brackets. Distinct terms such as characteristic-two gradings and semisimple derivations were also searched for implicit equivalent coverage.

## Limitations

The field is finite, \(n\ge2\), and the Lie bracket is the canonical free class-two bracket. The quantitative concentration concerns fixed characteristic-two field order and generator rank growing to infinity. The finite enumerations cover only small prime fields. The literature comparison does not prove global publication priority: a broader result on idempotent elements in derivation Lie algebras might imply the count. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
