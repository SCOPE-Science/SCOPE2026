# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** General linear operators were decomposed by residue and radical components, and the original Rota–Baxter identity forced both residue components to vanish. Direct substitution yields necessary and sufficient \(T^2=0\), \(2Tv=0\), so characteristic two imposes no condition on \(Tv\). The square-zero-matrix count is a unique image/kernel/isomorphism construction, not an extrapolation from finite data. The nilpotence and conjugacy conclusions follow from explicit operator powers and module automorphism normal forms. Exhaustive basis-identity and conjugacy computations independently corroborate the smallest cases.

## Originality
**PASS with residual historical-priority uncertainty.** The most material primary comparison is Farooq, arXiv:2605.15670v1, Theorem 3.4 and Section 3.1, inspected in full available text: same local algebra, **characteristic zero**, forced square-zero operators, no positive-characteristic count or conjugacy classification. The index-three example in characteristic two is outside and incompatible with the characteristic-zero conclusion if it were incorrectly generalized. Guo–Rosenkranz–Zheng, arXiv:1407.5306, concerns the untruncated polynomial algebra and characteristic-zero integral-like operators. Benito–Gubarev–Pozhidaev's 2018 abstract and reference list concern different algebras. A broader as-yet-unidentified theorem may imply the classification, and module-theoretic orbit reductions are classical; no guarantee of priority is made.

## Value
**PASS.** The radical-square-zero local algebra is the natural first infinitesimal neighborhood, and its Rota–Baxter operators provide a sharp characteristic-dependent boundary: the characteristic-zero square-zero law genuinely breaks in characteristic two. The all-rank finite-field enumerator and field-independent complete conjugacy classification determine two natural invariants and distinguish nilpotence-index strata. This is more than an isolated low-dimensional computation or renaming of a known theorem.
