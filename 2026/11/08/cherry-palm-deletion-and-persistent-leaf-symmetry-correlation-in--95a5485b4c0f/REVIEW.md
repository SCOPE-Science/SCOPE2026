# Scientific review: cherry-Palm deletion in uniform labeled trees

**Correctness — PASS.** The marked-cherry deletion/reinsertion bijection supplies an exact conditional law for each marked pair; summing its indicators yields the size-biased distributional statement. Prüfer-word absence probabilities give the covariance. Strict positivity follows from the decreasing sequence of leaf probabilities. Overlap classes of ordered cherry pairs give an independent exact second moment; asymptotic expansions establish the positive limiting correlation. The packaged checker independently enumerates all Prüfer words through eight vertices and verifies 631 exact assertions. The proof treats every integer \(n\ge4\); a finite run is not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS with historical risk.** The closest classical source is Erdős–Rényi (1963), §5, which studies the cherry indicator and its occurrence in uniform labeled trees. Although the probability in its equation (5.1) supplies the counting ingredient, neither that section nor the inspected tree-symmetry preprint reports the cherry-size-biased leaf distribution, the exact leaf/cherry mixed covariance, or the limiting correlation. The latter preprint imposes a different degree-three condition on the center. Disanto and Wiehe (arXiv:1112.1295v1, 2011-12-06) study ranked coalescent binary trees and cherry/pitchfork joints, a different random tree and a different paired statistic. Related accessible historical results on Cayley-tree leaf/distance and leaf/edge-split statistics do not imply this law because neither determines the marked common-neighbor leaf pairing. Search absence cannot establish uniqueness, and an unlocated earlier general forest generating function may yield these formulas implicitly.

**Value — PASS.** The cherry count records local interchangeable leaf pairs responsible for graph automorphisms, while the leaf count measures overall fringe abundance. Their exact distributional interaction is a natural missing joint statistic, and its positive all-size covariance and persistent correlation \(\approx0.617858\) distinguish local symmetry from mere leaf abundance. The Palm identity allows arbitrary leaf-count tests and is more informative than a single covariance computation.

**Limitations.** Uniform labeled unrooted trees only; no claim of independent checking, causal relation, or generalization to different tree laws. A secondary bibliographic index gives 1963-09-01 but the publisher specifies only September 1963, so the true first-publication day is not verified. The archived public preprint arXiv:1112.1295v1 is dated 2011-12-06, but predates neither the 1963 journal work nor all relevant historical results.
