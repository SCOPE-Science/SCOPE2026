It gives a nonzero surjective linear functional precisely at bichromatic edges. Independent edge-coordinate counting establishes both individual fibers and the single-commutator support criterion. Concatenating the vertex labels for \(r\) commutators proves the necessity and sufficiency of \(q^{2r}\) colors, and thereby the exact length and width formulas. The finite replay checks direct ordered-pair enumeration against a separate contracted-graph expression and the binary five-vertex boundary. No unproved extrapolation from finite samples is needed.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources

The closest earlier general quiver computation is the commuting-pair **zero-fiber** Potts/Tutte identity; it is fully acknowledged as covered. The published two-vertex Kronecker result determines nonzero fibers only in that very special underlying graph. Neither earlier statement, including its implications, determines all nonzero support-sensitive fibers or the chromatic-number commutator-length law for general graphs. Green–Schroll's 2023 full text concerns path-identifying commuting algebras, not associative commutator word maps; Shumyatsky–Vannacci's 2023 full text treats the zero-fiber probability and structural bounds. Targeted database and literature searches did not find a dominating general theorem. The novelty determination remains provisional, especially regarding unindexed algebraic word-map literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a structural graph-theoretic classification of the precise additive commutator length of *every* radical element for an infinite, representation-theoretically natural family of finite-dimensional associative algebras. It identifies a sharp field-size/chromatic obstruction, yields an explicit first binary width-two example where exactly one radical target fails to be a commutator, and transfers NP-complete 4-colorability to concrete commutator membership. Unlike the already known commuting-pair count, the full nonzero fibers and exact width answer genuinely different questions and provide tools for comparing finite quiver algebras.
