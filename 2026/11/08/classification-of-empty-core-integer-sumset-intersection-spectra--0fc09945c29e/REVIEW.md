# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The classification has both directions. Necessity follows by choosing persistent integer witnesses for two failure lengths and adding them at every index; the empty-core condition excludes length \(1\). Every submonoid of nonnegative integers is finitely generated by its least positive element and least representatives of the attained residue classes. For the converse, the separated integer scales \(G_{j+1}=b_jG_j\) have unbounded quotients. For each fixed \(h\), reduction of a hypothetical zero-sum relation modulo \(G_{j+1}\) at its least nonzero coefficient forces a contradiction with \(|z_j|<b_j\); the remaining balanced blocks have total length in the designated monoid. Conversely every monoid length is realized by balanced blocks at generator types appearing arbitrarily far in the tail. The only integer divisible by all tail scales is zero. All quantifiers and the \(S=\{0\}\) boundary case are covered. The standalone finite enumerator agrees in 38 small cases but does not establish the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
Theorem 5 of Marques–Nathanson (arXiv:2603.14510v1) supplies only the single-generator failure set \(\{d,2d,3d,\ldots\}\). Here arbitrary finitely generated additive submonoids occur and the necessary closure law makes the classification complete; examples such as \(\langle3,5\rangle\) cannot be derived as an instance of their single generator theorem. Their Theorem 4 has a nonempty core and hence cannot subsume the empty-core converse. Nathanson's semigroup sequel (arXiv:2604.04781v2) poses related fixed-semigroup problems, not this result. The globally universal classification (van Doorn–Monticone–Tang, arXiv:2604.18869v2) permits varying the semigroup and does not imply which sets occur for one fixed additive group with empty core; its unrestricted spectra include shapes excluded by the proved closure law. Earlier foundational work (arXiv:2512.23574v3) formulates the commutation problem without this classification. Focused bibliographic and mathematical research-record searches found no equivalent or stronger covering result. No search guarantees exhaustive indexing; the separated-scale idea is closely related to the single-generator construction and priority for an independently discovered generalization remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete characterization for a natural fixed ambient arithmetic structure under the empty-core regime. It turns an open-ended spectrum problem into the exact invariant 'complement of a positive additive submonoid', proves this obstruction is the only one, and supplies an explicit realization for every allowed monoid. It goes beyond a routine pointwise numerical example: semigroups with multiple generators, noncofinite semigroups and the trivial monoid all occur, while many otherwise plausible spectra are ruled out. The exact dependence on the empty-core hypothesis delineates the boundary of the result. No classification of nonempty-core spectra or all ambient semigroups is claimed.

## Source limitations
The directly relevant statements and proofs of arXiv:2603.14510v1 were inspected, including Theorems 2–5. The arXiv:2604.04781v2 motivating problems and Theorem 14 and the arXiv:2604.18869v2 main classification and construction were compared. The earlier arXiv:2512.23574v3 introduction and sumset-commutation framework were inspected. Remaining risk: unindexed or differently phrased same-ambient classifications. Numerical tests cannot determine novelty or prove infinitude.
