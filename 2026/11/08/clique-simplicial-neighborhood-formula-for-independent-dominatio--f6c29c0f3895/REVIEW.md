# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The classification follows from a two-case examination of each subdivision vertex: it is forced into an independent dominating set exactly when neither base endpoint is selected. Original vertices outside the chosen clique are undominated exactly when their open neighborhood equals the clique. Distinct simplicial neighborhoods are therefore subtracted only once. This gives the full polynomial and, after direct clique enumeration, its triangle-free and multipartite specializations. Original-neighborhood brute force and an independent clique formula agree on the explicitly reported finite test families; the unbounded statement rests on the proof.

Earlier independent-domination polynomial papers supply generic graph-polynomial identities, not the special central-graph structural formula. The nearest public records concerning central-graph iterates or total domination involve different objects or parameters. **Mathematical value — PASS.** The result classifies every independent dominating set for arbitrary finite base graphs, not just minimizers; gives a graph-wide clique/simplicial-neighborhood identity for the total number; and yields an exact degree-weighted polynomial and universal \(1+n+m\) count across all triangle-free graphs with minimum degree at least two. It also gives a uniform part-size formula for all complete multipartite bases, including singleton-boundary corrections. These are natural complete classifications rather than arbitrary finite slices. The general clique sum is not asserted efficient.
