# Closed zero-forcing polynomials and well-forcedness of clique-expanded cycles

## Finding
For integers \(n\ge3\) and \(m\ge2\), replace every vertex of the simple cycle \(C_n\) with an \(m\)-vertex clique, joining consecutive cliques completely. Write \(H_{n,m}=C_n[K_m]\). Define its ordinary zero-forcing polynomial by \(\mathcal Z_{n,m}(z)=\sum_{S\text{ zero forcing}}z^{|S|}\), where all vertex subsets are distinguished by their actual vertices. Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal Z_{n,m}(z)=z^{n(m-1)}\left((z+m)^n-\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}a_{n,k}m^{n-k}z^k\right)},
\qquad a_{n,0}=1,\quad a_{n,k}=\frac{n}{n-2k}\binom{n-2k}{k}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
In particular, \(Z(H_{n,m})=n(m-1)+2\). Every inclusion-minimal zero-forcing set has this cardinality; hence \(H_{n,m}\) is well-forced. The exact number of these minimum sets is
\[
\boxed{\#\min\mathrm{ZF}(H_{n,m})=e_n m^{n-2},\qquad e_3=3,\quad e_4=6,\quad e_n=2n\ (n\ge5).}
\]
For \(n\ge5\) and \(m\ge3\), this value contradicts the formula claimed in Corollary 3.17 of Javaid–Irshad–Batool–Raza (2016), which gives \(n(m-1)+\lceil n/2\rceil\) instead; the gap is \(\lceil n/2\rceil-2\). As a concrete falsifying witness, in \(C_5[K_3]\) select all three vertices in cliques \(0,1\), and select any two vertices in each of cliques \(2,3,4\). These \(12\) initial blue vertices force the missing vertices successively in cliques \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\), whereas the cited formula claims \(13\). The general proof below also establishes the matching lower bound \(12\) in this case. This comparison does not imply every other claim in that paper is false.

There is a set-level characterization: a subset \(S\) is zero forcing if and only if it omits at most one vertex of each replacement clique and the cliques entirely contained in \(S\) include two at cyclic distance at most two. The statement for \(n=3\) uses the simple three-cycle; its three closed radius-one neighbourhoods have distinct indices.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected. The vertices are pairs \((i,a)\), where \(i\) ranges through \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and \(a\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\). Two distinct vertices are adjacent if their first coordinates agree or are adjacent in \(C_n\). A blue vertex forces its unique white neighbour, if exactly one exists. A set is zero forcing if repeated applications ultimately blue all vertices. *Inclusion-minimal* means that removing any one of its vertices destroys that property, not merely minimum cardinality. The cycle-square adjacency relation on distinct indices is cyclic graph distance at most two; it is complete for \(3\le n\le5\).

## Proof
**1. The true-twin obstruction.** Every two vertices in one clique have the same neighbours outside that clique and are adjacent to one another. If two vertices of a clique start white, no first force into that pair can occur: any outside blue vertex sees both, while any blue vertex of their clique also sees both. This remains true until one has been forced, so it is a permanent obstruction. Therefore any zero-forcing initial set has at most one white vertex in each clique. Conversely, this condition alone gives at least one initially blue vertex in every clique because \(m\ge2\).

**2. Forcing on cyclic binary patterns.** For an initial set meeting the preceding condition, call index \(i\) *full* if its entire clique is blue, and *partial* if exactly one vertex is white. In any state obtained by forcing, every clique retains at least one blue vertex, and every partial clique has exactly one white. Thus for each blue vertex in clique \(i\), the number of white neighbours is precisely the number of partial indices in \(\{i-1,i,i+1\}\) (indices modulo \(n\)). A force is possible exactly when one of these cyclic triples has one partial and two full indices.

Suppose that the full indices contain no two at cyclic distance at most two. Then no triple contains two full indices; no force can occur, and the initial set fails unless no white vertices remain. The all-full case already contains the required pair. This proves necessity.

For sufficiency, suppose two full indices have cyclic distance at most two. If they are separated by one index and this middle index is partial, a blue vertex in its clique has exactly one white neighbour (its own clique's white vertex), so it forces that vertex and creates two adjacent full indices; if the middle index is full, an adjacent full pair already exists. Starting with adjacent full indices, take a maximal cyclic run of full indices containing that pair. Unless the entire cycle is full, the first partial clique beyond either boundary of the run has one white vertex, and a blue vertex in the boundary full clique has exactly one white neighbour: the remaining white vertex of that adjacent partial clique. Forcing it extends the run. Iterating at most \(n-2\) times makes every clique full. Thus the initial set is zero forcing. The argument also applies when \(n=3\) or \(4\), with three distinct indices in each triple.

**3. Polynomial enumeration.** Once the true-twin obstruction is avoided, each clique contributes either (i) a full clique, with \(m\) initial blue vertices, or (ii) a partial clique, with \(m-1\) blue vertices and \(m\) choices of its unique white vertex. Factoring \(z^{n(m-1)}\), the generating weight is \((z+m)^n\). Bad configurations are exactly those whose set \(B\) of full indices is independent in the second power \(C_n^2\) (where distinct vertices are adjacent if at cyclic distance at most two). For each such \(B\) of size \(k\), its contribution is \(m^{n-k}z^k\).

For \(k=0\), there is one independent index set. For \(k\ge1\), take a cyclically ordered \(k\)-set of indices with at least two unselected indices between successive selected indices. The \(k\) positive cyclic gaps between selected indices sum to \(n\) and each is at least three. There are \(\binom{n-2k-1}{k-1}\) ordered gap lists and \(n\) choices of initial marked index, but each independent set is marked \(k\) times. Consequently the number is \(\frac nk\binom{n-2k-1}{k-1}=\frac{n}{n-2k}\binom{n-2k}{k}\) for \(3k\le n\); for larger \(k\) it is zero. This proves the asserted polynomial.

**4. Minimum and minimal sets.** The full-index set \(B\) must contain an edge of \(C_n^2\), so the fewest full indices is two. Each contributes one additional initially blue vertex above the baseline \(n(m-1)\), proving \(Z(H_{n,m})=n(m-1)+2\). Every zero-forcing set with at least three full indices contains an edge \(\{i,j\}\) of \(C_n^2\); retain exactly those two full cliques and make every other clique partial by deleting one initially blue vertex when necessary, obtaining a proper zero-forcing subset. A zero-forcing set with exactly two full indices is inclusion-minimal: deleting a blue vertex from a partial clique introduces two whites into that clique and activates the true-twin obstruction, whereas deleting a blue vertex from a full clique leaves only one full index and no admissible first force. Thus the inclusion-minimal sets are exactly the ones with two full cliques forming an edge of \(C_n^2\), one omitted vertex in each other clique. Every inclusion-minimal set is minimum. For \(n=3\) and \(n=4\), the cycle-square has respectively three and six edges; for \(n\ge5\), every vertex has four square-neighbours (also for \(n=5\)), yielding \(2n\) edges. The remaining \(n-2\) partial cliques have \(m^{n-2}\) choices of omitted representatives. This proves the last formula.

## Verification
The supplementary standalone `verify.py` constructs graph adjacency and applies the literal zero-forcing colour-change rule, without invoking the structural characterization inside its forcing procedure. It exhaustively compares the outcome for all \(128{,}832\) vertex subsets across ten graph profiles: \((n,m)=(3,2),(3,3),(3,4),(4,2),(4,3),(5,2),(5,3),(6,2),(7,2),(8,2)\). Every outcome agrees with the characterization, every coefficient agrees with the closed polynomial, and the minimum cardinality and multiplicity agree. Exactly \(6{,}962\) enumerated subsets were zero forcing. Running `python3 verify.py` prints `VERIFY_OK` after all assertions. These calculations are finite corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Boyer and collaborators introduced the general zero-forcing polynomial in 2018, including exact polynomials for other families, notably threshold graphs. Javaid, Irshad, Batool and Raza examined zero-forcing numbers of lexicographic products in 2016; their Corollary 3.17 asserts an exact value for \(C_n[K_m]\), but it disagrees with the explicit initial configurations and the proof here for every \(n\ge5\) and \(m\ge3\). The source is therefore a directly relevant, non-covering contrary statement, not merely a general bound. Grood, Haas, Jacob, King and Nasserasr introduced *well-forced* graph terminology and characterized trees and several elementary families in their December 21, 2023 public preprint; it is the primary motivation for identifying all inclusion-minimal sets here. Wang's 2026 modular-compression theorem establishes an exact general algorithmic profile reduction for true-twin blow-ups and derives explicit formulas for star-type common-clique unions and complete multipartite graphs. Its binary restriction is prior coverage, not claimed as new here. The present result evaluates the cyclic true-twin case to a simple cycle-square independence polynomial and proves its exact all-minimal-set classification; these particular statements were not identified in the inspected relevant texts. These are priority comparisons, not a claim of independently certified novelty.

## Limitations
The result concerns ordinary zero forcing, not positive-semidefinite, skew, leaky, or directed variants. It requires equal clique fibres with size at least two and a cycle of length at least three; it does not claim the corresponding result for arbitrary base graphs or variable fibre sizes. The generic modular-compression theorem implies an exponential-time computation of the present polynomial and is substantial broader prior coverage; assessing whether the short closed evaluation is considered sufficiently distinct requires independent literature review. Some equivalent terminology or unindexed literature could still cover the specific criterion. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
