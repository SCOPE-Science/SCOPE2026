# Codimension-two cyclic quotient strata of the Liu–Zhu toric Fano family

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge3\), let \(X_n\) be the \(n\)-dimensional complex toric \(\mathbf Q\)-Fano variety defined by Liu and Zhu from the face fan of
\[
Q_n=\{(d_0,\ldots,d_n)\in\mathbf R^{n+1}:\ \sum_i d_i=0,\quad -(2n-1)\le d_i\le2\}.
\]
Write \(v_{p,q}\) for its primitive lattice vertex with coordinate \(p\) equal to \(-(2n-1)\), coordinate \(q\) equal to \(1\), and all others \(2\), for distinct indices \(p,q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\). For an unordered pair \(\{p,q\}\), set \(\sigma_{p,q}=\mathbf R_{\ge0}v_{p,q}+\mathbf R_{\ge0}v_{q,p}\).

The **codimension-two irreducible components** of \(\operatorname{Sing}(X_n)_{\mathrm{red}}\) are exactly the \(\binom{n+1}{2}\) invariant orbit closures \(V(\sigma_{p,q})\). At a point of the dense torus orbit of each such component, the analytic germ is a product of a smooth \((n-2)\)-dimensional torus and the two-dimensional cyclic quotient singularity
\[
\mathbf A^2/\mu_{4n}\quad\text{of type}\quad \frac1{4n}(1,2n-1).
\]
Here a primitive \((4n)\)-th root \(\zeta\) acts by \((u,v)\mapsto(\zeta u,\zeta^{2n-1}v)\). The transverse quotient has canonical index \(2\), minimal log discrepancy \(1/2\), and Hirzebruch–Jung resolution string
\[
[3,\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{n-2\ \mathrm{terms}},3].
\]
In particular, these K-polystable varieties are noncanonical in codimension two for every \(n\ge3\). This does not claim a classification of possible additional higher-codimension singular components.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). The cocharacter lattice in the difference coordinates is \(N=\{d\in\mathbb Z^{n+1}:\sum_i d_i=0\}\). The fan is the **face fan** of \(Q_n\), whose rays are generated by the vertices \(v_{p,q}\), as verified in Liu–Zhu, Proposition 2.1 and Section 3. The indices \(n\ge3\) are integers, and all assertions about local singularity type apply on the dense orbit of the specified codimension-two strata. The transverse minimal log discrepancy is the invariant of the two-dimensional quotient factor; it is not asserted to equal the minimal log discrepancy of the full \(n\)-dimensional local germ at a closed point.

## Proof
**1. Classify every two-dimensional cone.** Put \(x_i=2-d_i\). This maps \(Q_n\) to the simplex slice
\[
 x_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_i x_i=2(n+1),\qquad x_i\le2n+1.
\]
Thus the polytope is obtained by truncating every vertex of the standard \(n\)-simplex. The truncation neighborhoods do not meet, since \(2(2n+1)>2(n+1)\). The vertices are the ordered-pair points \(v_{p,q}\): at such a point \(x_p=2n+1\), \(x_q=1\), and other coordinates vanish. Every edge has exactly one of two forms: a **short edge** joining \(v_{p,q}\) and \(v_{p,r}\), where \(p,q,r\) are distinct; or a **long edge** joining \(v_{p,q}\) and \(v_{q,p}\), where \(p\ne q\). There are \((n+1)\binom n2\) short edges and \(\binom{n+1}2\) long edges. Since the two-dimensional cones of the face fan are precisely cones over edges, this exhausts their possibilities.

**2. All short-edge cones are regular.** Fix a short edge, with vertices \(v_{p,q},v_{p,r}\). As \(n\ge3\), choose \(s\) distinct from \(p,q,r\). Use the coordinates excluding \(p\) as a basis of \(N\cong\mathbb Z^n\). The two primitive rays have \((q,r)\)-row minor \(-3\) and \((q,s)\)-row minor \(-2\) (up to orientation). Their \(2\times2\) minors therefore have gcd \(1\). Equivalently, the rays form a basis of the saturated rank-two lattice in their real span, so the corresponding two-dimensional affine cone is smooth.

**3. Identify the long-edge cones.** Fix \(p\ne q\), and set \(r=2n-1\). The saturated lattice of the real plane spanned by \(v_{p,q}\) and \(v_{q,p}\) consists of zero-sum integral vectors whose coordinates other than \(p,q\) are all equal. A basis is \(g_1=e_p-e_q\) and \(g_2\), whose coordinates at \(p,q\) are \(0,-(n-1)\) and whose other coordinates are \(1\). Directly,
\[
 v_{p,q}=-r g_1+2g_2,\qquad v_{q,p}=g_1+2g_2.
\]
The determinant in this saturated lattice has absolute value \(2(r+1)=4n\). Moreover
\[
 g_2=\frac{v_{p,q}+r v_{q,p}}{4n}.
\]
It follows that the quotient of the saturated plane lattice by the sublattice of the two ray generators is cyclic of order \(4n\), generated by \(g_2\). Passing to the smooth affine toric cover of the simplicial cone, the fractional ray coordinates of \(g_2\) show that its deck group acts on \(\mathbf A^2\) with weights \((1,r)\). Thus the transverse singularity is exactly \(\frac1{4n}(1,2n-1)\), nontrivial for every \(n\ge3\).

Every ray lies on at least one maximal cone, and a saturated two-dimensional sublattice splits off from \(N\). Thus the affine toric open set for \(\sigma_{p,q}\) is \((\mathbb C^*)^{n-2}\times(\mathbf A^2/\mu_{4n})\); its closed two-dimensional-cone orbit is the product of the torus with the quotient vertex.

**4. Singular components and local numerical invariants.** The singular locus of a toric variety is torus invariant, so its codimension-two orbit strata correspond exactly to nonregular two-dimensional cones. The preceding two steps show that these are precisely the long-edge cones. Their orbit closures are distinct, and because a normal toric variety is smooth in codimension one, each is an irreducible component of the reduced singular locus.

The cyclic quotient canonical index is
\[
\frac{4n}{\gcd(4n,1+(2n-1))}=2.
\]
The negative continued fraction is
\[
\frac{4n}{2n-1}=[3,\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{n-2},3],
\]
as follows by evaluating \([\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{k},3]=(2k+3)/(2k+1)\) with \(k=n-2\). This gives the stated minimal toric resolution string. Finally, for each \(1\le j<4n\) the quotient age is
\[
 \frac{j+((2n-1)j\bmod 4n)}{4n}.
\]
For even \(j\) it is \(1\); for odd \(j\in\{1,3,\ldots,2n-1\}\) it is \(1/2\); for the remaining odd values it is \(3/2\). The minimum is exactly \(1/2\). The standard quotient/toric log-discrepancy criterion identifies this minimum with the transverse minimal log discrepancy and shows that the quotient is not canonical. All claims follow.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu and Zhu give the polar box-slice, all primitive rays, and K-polystability with \(\alpha(X_n)=2/(2n+1)\), but do not classify its codimension-two quotient singularities. The earlier public note *Local divisor class groups at the torus fixed points of the Liu–Zhu small-alpha examples* determines local class groups only at torus-fixed points of maximal-cone charts. Its statements do not imply the two-dimensional orbit-cone classification or the transverse analytic weights, resolution chains, and discrepancies proved here. Wu's alpha-spectrum theorem studies a broader family of toric Fano examples but does not provide the explicit claim in its accessible abstract and synopsis. Standard toric quotient and resolution theory supplies the general tools, but not the computed all-parameter singularity stratification for these named examples.

## Limitations
The result concerns the codimension-two components only; singular points in higher codimension may be present. The dimension-two boundary \(n=2\) is not covered. The assertion about local quotient type applies on the indicated dense torus orbits, not necessarily at every point of their closures. The independent priority comparison is limited by the inaccessibility of the complete text of Wu's 2026 preprint and by the possibility of unindexed equivalent toric calculations. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
