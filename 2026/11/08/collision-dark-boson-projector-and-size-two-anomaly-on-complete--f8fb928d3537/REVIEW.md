# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The energy-sector argument uses the two nonzero one-particle eigenvalues \(\pm J\sqrt{ab}\) and the \((a+b-2)\)-dimensional zero eigenspace. Nonzero two-particle energy eigenspaces cannot have an identically vanishing collision diagonal. Block multiplication gives necessary and sufficient zero-mode conditions, and their orthogonal solutions generate the displayed projector. Unsigned incidence rank gives the exact anomaly at side size two. Strictly positive collision rates exclude all other nondecaying modes, establishing the arbitrary-state capture law. Two independent exact rational scripts confirm the full projector and independent Krylov ranks for explicitly stated finite examples; the finite tests are not used in place of the general proof.

Krovi–Brun (2006) gives the abstract undetectable-eigenstate mechanism, and Gamble et al. (2010) supplies the bosonic two-body Kronecker-sum Hamiltonian but studies strongly regular graph-isomorphism dynamics, not all-site collision-loss response on bicliques. Thiel et al. (2019) derives bright/dark probabilities for repeated detection of a single target under discrete probing; that result does not explicitly give this continuous two-body loss projector, its biclique dimension jump, or three input-class capture laws. Related geometric configuration-space results on complete bipartite graphs concern topology rather than the spectral absorption problem. Prior star and four-cycle special cases do not logically give the biclique formula, although they share the same underlying interference mechanism. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural biclique family interpolates between highly asymmetric and balanced two-part coupling architectures. The classification predicts when two-particle collision protection is extensive, gives the entire arbitrary-state capture response instead of just isolated dark vectors, and shows an unexpected one-dimension anomaly tied to the rank defect of the two-vertex incidence matrix. At equal total network size, \(K_{2,4}\) and \(K_{3,3}\) have dark dimensions six and five, and different opposite-class capture probabilities \(5/8\) and \(5/9\); thus topology affects detection even when the total dark dimension is otherwise invariant. The result is not claimed as a generic graph theorem or a finite-time dynamics result.

## Limitations and closest literature

The theorem requires homogeneous edge hopping, bosonic exchange symmetry, no coherent interactions, all-site strictly positive collision loss and finite complete bipartite networks. An indexed or unindexed older result combining general spectral projectors with the complete bipartite spectrum could conceivably contain an equivalent explicit classification; the inspected works did not exhibit it.
