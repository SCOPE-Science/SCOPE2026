# Commutative divisors of finite completely zero-simple semigroups collapse to Brandt embeddings

## Finding
Let \(S\) be a nonempty finite **commutative** semigroup. The following are equivalent:

1. \(S\) divides a finite completely \(0\)-simple semigroup; that is, \(S\) is a homomorphic image of a subsemigroup of a finite Rees \(0\)-matrix semigroup over a finite group.
2. \(S\) divides a finite Brandt semigroup.
3. \(S\) embeds into a finite Brandt semigroup.
4. Either \(S\) is a finite abelian group, or it has an absorbing element \(z\) and a disjoint decomposition
   \[S=\{z\}\sqcup H_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup H_t\sqcup X,\]
   where each \(H_i\) is an abelian subgroup with its own identity, products of elements in distinct \(H_i\) equal \(z\), and every \(x\in X\) annihilates the whole semigroup. The index \(t\) may be zero and \(X\) may be empty.

In particular, **finite completely \(0\)-simple semigroups and finite Brandt semigroups have exactly the same finite commutative divisors**, even though their noncommutative divisor classes differ. The theorem explicitly identifies the commutative-target slice of both classes mentioned in Problem 1.2 of Bershadsky--Kublanovsky--Mashevitzky (2026). It does not classify arbitrary, noncommutative divisors.

## Assumptions and scope
All semigroups, groups and index sets are finite and nonempty unless zero is explicitly adjoined. Rees' representation writes a completely \(0\)-simple semigroup as
\[M^0(G;I,\Lambda;P)=\{0\}\cup(I\times G\times\Lambda),\]
with a regular sandwich matrix \(P=(p_{\lambda i})\) over \(G\cup\{0\}\), and multiplication
\[(i,g,\lambda)(j,h,\mu)=\begin{cases}(i,gp_{\lambda j}h,\mu),&p_{\lambda j}\ne0,\\0,&p_{\lambda j}=0.\end{cases}\]
No condition that the nonzero sandwich entries equal the group identity is required. A nonzero element \((i,g,\lambda)\) is in a subgroup if and only if \(p_{\lambda i}\ne0\); otherwise its square is zero. A Brandt semigroup is the special case with the sandwich matrix equal to the identity matrix. A one-element semigroup qualifies via both the group and zero cases, with no uniqueness asserted for that degenerate description.

## Proof
**Step 1: The nonzero-square dichotomy survives passage to a finite subsemigroup.** Fix a subsemigroup \(U\subseteq M^0(G;I,\Lambda;P)\) and a surjective homomorphism \(f:U\to S\), with \(S\) commutative. An element \(u=(i,g,\lambda)\in U\) with \(p_{\lambda i}\ne0\) lies in the ambient maximal subgroup at coordinates \((i,\lambda)\). Since the subgroup is finite, its cyclic subgroup generated by \(u\), including its identity \(e_u\), consists of positive powers of \(u\), hence lies in \(U\). Therefore \(f(u)\) lies in a subgroup of \(S\), with identity \(f(e_u)\). If \(p_{\lambda i}=0\), then \(u^2=0\) and \(0\in U\).

**Step 2: A zero-square image annihilates everything.** First assume \(0\in U\), and write \(z=f(0)\); it is absorbing in \(S\). Take a nonzero \(u=(i,g,\lambda)\in U\) satisfying \(u^2=0\), and any \(v=(j,h,\mu)\in U\). If \(uv=0\), then \(f(uv)=z\). If \(uv\ne0\) but \(uv\) has zero square, then \(p_{\mu i}=0\), so \(vu=0\) and commutativity of \(S\) yields \(f(uv)=f(vu)=z\). Finally, if \(uv\ne0\) belongs to an ambient subgroup, then \(f(uv)\) belongs to a subgroup of \(S\), while
\[f(uv)^2=f(u)^2f(v)^2=z f(v)^2=z.\]
A group element of a semigroup whose square is its absorbing zero must itself equal that zero: its subgroup identity would be the absorbing zero and that subgroup must be a singleton. Thus in every case \(f(u)f(v)=z\). The conclusion also holds when \(v=0\). Consequently all images of source square-zero elements, except possibly \(z\) itself, are elements of a null annihilator set \(X\), and none belongs to a nonzero subgroup.

**Step 3: Different nonzero subgroup images are orthogonal.** For group elements \(u\in U\cap H_{i\lambda}\) and \(v\in U\cap H_{j\mu}\), let \(e_u,e_v\in U\) be the identities of their ambient maximal subgroups and put \(E=f(e_u)\), \(F=f(e_v)\). Both are idempotents in the commutative quotient. If either cross entry \(p_{\lambda j}\) or \(p_{\mu i}\) vanishes, then one of \(e_ue_v,e_ve_u\) is the zero of the Rees semigroup, hence \(EF=z\). Otherwise both cross entries are nonzero, and the sandwich products \(e_ue_ve_u\) and \(e_ve_ue_v\) belong, respectively, to the ambient groups at \((i,\lambda)\) and \((j,\mu)\), with respective identities \(e_u\) and \(e_v\). Their images belong to groups at \(E\) and \(F\), but by commutativity are both the idempotent \(EF\). As the only idempotent of a group is its identity, \(EF=E\) and \(EF=F\), hence \(E=F\).

Thus, in the presence of a source zero, any two distinct nonzero subgroup identities in \(S\) have product \(z\). Indeed, if \(E\ne F\), the previous paragraph forces \(EF=z\). All regular images with a given nonzero identity form one (necessarily abelian) group: products and inverses remain in their common ambient group in \(S\), and all elements in the quotient belonging to that group share the same identity. Elements of groups with distinct identities multiply to \(z\), since their product equals \((EF)xy=z\). Together with Step 2, this gives decomposition (4).

**Step 4: The source-zero-free case.** If \(0\notin U\), no nonzero element of \(U\) can have square zero, and none of the products appearing in Step 3 can vanish. Consequently all subgroup identities in the commutative image coincide. Every image element lies in the corresponding subgroup, so \(S\) is a single finite abelian group. The case \(U=\{0\}\) yields the trivial semigroup.

**Step 5: Explicit Brandt embedding.** Conversely assume (4). Choose a finite group \(K\) into which all \(H_i\) embed, for example the direct product of the \(H_i\) augmented by a sufficiently large cyclic group so that \(|K|\ge |X|\). Choose pairwise distinct indices \(1,\ldots,t,\alpha,\beta\). In the Brandt semigroup \(B(K)\) on these indices, map \(z\) to \(0\), and map \(h\in H_i\) to \((i,\iota_i(h),i)\), where \(\iota_i\) is an embedding into \(K\). Assign distinct coefficients \(k_x\in K\) to \(x\in X\) and map \(x\) to \((\alpha,k_x,\beta)\). Since \(\alpha\ne\beta\) and neither is a group index, each product involving one of these off-diagonal images is zero, exactly as required; group products and cross-group products are preserved. The map is injective. If \(S\) is a group without an external zero, embed it on one diagonal index instead. Hence (4) implies (3); (3) implies (2), and (2) implies (1), since every Brandt semigroup is completely \(0\)-simple. This completes all implications.

## Verification
The proof is symbolic and valid for arbitrary finite Rees sandwich groups and matrices. Independently, `CHECK.py` enumerates every regular zero-one Rees sandwich matrix of sizes \(2\times2\), \(2\times3\), and \(3\times2\), all its zero-containing nonempty subsemigroups, and all congruences with commutative quotients. The checker examines \(1483\) source subsemigroups and \(3616\) commutative quotient instances, and confirms the zero-orthogonality and annihilator classification. Run `python CHECK.py`; the expected terminal record is in `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt`. This finite verification does **not** prove the unrestricted group-and-index theorem, and the proof does not depend on it.

## Relationship to prior work
Problem 1.2 of Bershadsky, Kublanovsky and Mashevitzky (arXiv:2604.04763, initially made public on 2026-04-06) asks for divisors of finite Brandt semigroups and of the zero-one Rees-matrix subclass of completely \(0\)-simple semigroups. Hall, Kublanovskii, Margolis, Sapir and Trotter (1997) study finite Rees-matrix embeddings and pseudovarieties generated by completely \(0\)-simple and Brandt semigroups; their \(HSP\) closure includes finite products and is not the single-factor division relation \(HS\) addressed here. Kublanovsky (1998) is cited by the 2026 notebook for a positive-universal-formula description of divisors of all finite completely \(0\)-simple semigroups. That abstract characterization is relevant background; the present assertion is the concrete **equality of the commutative single-factor divisor classes**, with an explicit normal form and Brandt embedding, not an assertion that general divisor recognition was previously unsolved. Related known Brandt-only classifications do not supply the converse inclusion from arbitrary sandwich matrices without Step 3.

## Limitations
The target must be commutative and finite. Neither equality of unrestricted noncommutative divisor classes nor a uniform bound on the size of the representing Brandt group is claimed. The explicit constructive characterization may be implicitly derivable from older general divisor formulas; historical priority of that formulation requires independent bibliographic confirmation, particularly against Kublanovsky's 1998 chapter. The computational test covers trivial structure groups and small sandwich matrices only; all larger cases rest on the written proof.
