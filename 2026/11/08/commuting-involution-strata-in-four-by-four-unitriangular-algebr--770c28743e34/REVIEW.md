# Review: commuting involution strata in four-by-four unitriangular algebra groups

**Correctness (PASS).** Direct multiplication gives three equations for \(N^2=0\) and three for \(NM=MN\); disjoint cases on the middle superdiagonal, outer superdiagonals, and second superdiagonal give four exact counts. The four populations sum to the classical \(2q^4-q^2\) single-element count, and their weighted centralizer sizes sum to \(q^8+2q^7-2q^5\). Exhaustive direct field arithmetic at \(q=2,4\) is independent corroboration. The proof does not assume genericity, algebraic closure, or an extrapolation from these checks.

**Originality (PASS, provisional).** Ekhad–Zeilberger's 1995 full text counts individual square-zero matrices, not the commutation restriction on pairs. Melnikov counts Borel-conjugacy orbits and their individual sizes but not square-zero commuting-pair fibers; Britnell–Wildon studies commuting similarity classes without a fixed common flag. Basili studies geometry of commuting upper-triangular varieties; the accessible abstract is not an explicit square-zero intersection census. Isaacs–Karagueuzian's 2005 involution-counting article is closely related but its full text was inaccessible. Searches for equivalent involution-pair counts, restricted centralizers, and the exact polynomial did not show a dominating formula. The residual risk is an existing table or theorem under modular group/algebra terminology or in the uninspected sources.

**Value (PASS).** This is a complete natural rank-four boundary case for the joint constraints \(g^2=h^2=1\) and \(gh=hg\) inside the unit group of a familiar finite algebra. It refines the known single-element enumeration to the full restricted-centralizer distribution and explains a nonvanishing limiting commuting probability through two competing maximal-dimensional square-zero components. The result is not asserted as a general upper-triangular enumeration.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
