# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is established for all finite posets, odd prime powers, and positive tuple lengths. The crucial step constructs explicit simultaneous spectral idempotents and the invertible element \(u=\sum p_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}\), conjugating every commuting tuple to its diagonal sign tuple by an element of \(1+J\). Orbit–stabilizer then yields exactly one free field coordinate per comparable pair with different sign vectors. The classical spanning-subgraph expansion follows by expanding edge factors; the forest formula and recovery of the Tutte polynomial are proved algebraically. Direct multiplication checks include nonzero radical-square products. Risks: characteristic two and infinite posets are excluded; no computational check covers all finite fields.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Słowik (2013) addresses individual triangular-group involutions; Gargate–Gargate (arXiv:1907.06805) counts individual involutions in poset incidence algebras. Thus the \(r=1\) formula is already covered, and it is not the new claim. Sokal (arXiv:math/0503607) establishes the general Potts/Tutte transformation, which is credited. The new assessed statement is the simultaneous commuting \(r\)-tuple count over **arbitrary finite posets**, including cases with nonzero radical-square products, and the exact recovery of the comparability-graph Tutte invariant from those algebraic counts. No matching published or published-finding corpus claim was located in the recorded searches. This is provisional: general results on representations of elementary abelian groups into triangular units and idempotent lifting provide a plausible route to the same formula and need separate historical scrutiny.

## Value — PASS

The invariant counts commuting families of order-two units, a natural finite-algebra statistic not determined by the total number of individual involutions. The theorem identifies it uniformly with a classical graph polynomial, gives a closed forest law and exact commuting probabilities, and determines which graph-matroid information can and cannot be recovered from all such counts. Unlike a small-dimensional census, it applies to every finite poset and every positive tuple length. Its contribution is an all-dimensional algebra-to-combinatorics classification, not the known Potts identity by itself.

## Sources and limitations

Primary materials examined include the complete rendered sections of Gargate–Gargate (2019), the relevant incidence-algebra definitions of Quinn (2015), and Sokal's Potts expansion and forest examples (2005). The Słowik full text was not accessible in the current research materials; its abstract and its description in Gargate–Gargate were available. The explicit \(r=1\) specialization and classical idempotent-lifting theorem are not claimed novel. Broader coverage by an unidentified representation-count theorem is an unresolved historical risk, not a proved absence of precedent.
