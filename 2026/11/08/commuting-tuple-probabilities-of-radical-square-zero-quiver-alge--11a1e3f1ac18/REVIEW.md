# Review: quiver commuting probabilities and Tutte reconstruction

## Correctness — PASS

For the radical-square-zero quotient of a finite loopless quiver, write all ordered \(t\)-tuple coefficients as vectors in \(\mathbb F_q^t\). For an arrow, the equations for pairwise commutation are precisely the vanishing of the wedge of its endpoint-vector difference with its arrow-coefficient vector. Given equal endpoints all \(q^t\) arrow vectors work; given different endpoints exactly \(q\) work. Conditional independence of distinct arrow coefficients gives a product of edge agreement weights; expanding it gives the exact Fortuin–Kasteleyn spanning-subgraph polynomial with the stated normalizing factor. This proof includes all prime powers, all \(t\ge2\), every finite loopless quiver and parallel/oppositely oriented arrows. For reconstruction, expansion of \(Z_G(X,Y-1)\) has \(Y\)-degree at most \(m\), and \((i,j)\mapsto t(i+j)-j\) is injective if \(t>m\); values at infinitely many prime powers then determine every coefficient. Tree and cycle evaluations follow by direct graph-specific state sums. The standard-library verifier directly multiplies algebra elements and independently enumerates spanning subgraphs, including a degree-three commuting-tuple test, and prints `VERIFY_OK`. Finite checks corroborate but do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources

The generalized Kronecker two-vertex commuting-pair formula is previously published and is explicitly subsumed. It does **not** establish a general graph-indexed \(t\)-tuple law or the one-arity reconstruction theorem. Vaso's radical-square-zero quiver source proves higher representation-theoretic classification; it neither counts commuting tuples nor connects them to Tutte invariants. Sokal's Fortuin–Kasteleyn state sum and Tutte transformations are classical and not claimed new; the graph polynomial already existed. The new asserted contribution is identifying precisely this graph polynomial from arbitrary loopless quiver algebra commuting probabilities and recovering the entire ordinary Tutte polynomial from field-size variation. Dutta–Basnet–Nath and Shumyatsky–Vannacci establish commuting-pair results for finite rings but not this identity. Green–Schroll's “commuting algebra” is a different quotient construction, not a ring commuting-probability theorem. Targeted searches using quiver, radical-square-zero, Potts, Tutte, commuting pairs, commuting tuples and orientation-independent aliases did not reveal an identical or stronger theorem. There remains priority risk from the very broad literature on bilinear constraints and Potts models, and earlier or unindexed mathematical literature was not exhaustively covered; absence from the searches is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The commutation probability of arbitrary algebra elements is a natural finite-ring invariant. The exact all-graph/all-tuple formula shows that this invariant discards arrow orientations, recognizes cycles beyond trivial vertex and arrow counts, and, when sampled at one sufficiently large arity over varying finite fields, reconstructs the entire ordinary graph Tutte polynomial. This creates a nontrivial invariant bridge between quiver algebras and graph structure, not just a count for selected small quivers. It has explicit structural limits: it does not distinguish Tutte-equivalent graphs, reconstruct graph isomorphism types, or recover individually edge-weighted multivariate Tutte data.

## Closest literature and limitations

The strongest directly overlapping previously disseminated formula counts commuting **pairs** in two-vertex generalized Kronecker path algebras. All claims specialized to that case are treated as known. The Fortuin–Kasteleyn expansion was known decades earlier. Vaso 2021 is a motivating primary source for the specified quiver-algebra family. The exact connection/reconstruction has not been independently audited, and priority is relative to examined accessible sources only.
