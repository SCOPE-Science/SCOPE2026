# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact fixed-vector overlap formula (1) converts a single norm-inflating vector with numerical expectation modulus \(q\) into a strict \(q\)-radius witness. For every compact perturbation of the unilateral shift, a finite-support norm-inflating vector can be mixed with weakly null tail-geometric approximate eigenvectors whose expectations range continuously from zero to near one. Compactness makes all cross terms small uniformly, while the norm inflation persists. The ordinary-radius counterexample follows from the exact discrete-gradient identity and positivity of a two-variable quadratic form. The analytical proof handles every compact operator, without relying on finite tests.

**Originality — PASS, with literature-access risk.** The 2026 scaled-range dilation theorem controls \(q^{-1}W_q(T)\) and does not assert the unscaled threshold \(w_q(S+K)=1\) or its equivalence with contractivity. The 2024 general radius estimates do not classify compact-shift perturbations. Work on periodic weighted shifts concerns a narrower recurrent coefficient setting, whereas arbitrary compact perturbations need not be shifts; work on one-edge ordinary numerical radii covers the endpoint phenomenon, not the interior all-compact criterion. Searches for alternative formulations (\(q\)-range norm detection, essential shifts, weakly null approximate eigenvectors, rank-one edge perturbations) did not find a directly covering result. The complete text of the 2007 periodic-weight paper was not available and remains a specific priority risk; failed retrieval is not evidence of noncoverage.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem is an exact, all-compact-perturbation contractivity test simultaneously valid for every interior overlap, with a sharp, structurally different classical endpoint. It identifies a natural infinite-dimensional rigidity class rather than an isolated numerical example, and it distinguishes the unscaled radius threshold from the current scaled-dilation conditions. The claim is restricted to compact perturbations of the unilateral forward shift and does not address arbitrary operators with essential norm one.
