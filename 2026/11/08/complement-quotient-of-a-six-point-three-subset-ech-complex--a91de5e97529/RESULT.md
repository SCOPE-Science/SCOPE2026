# Complement quotient of a six-point three-subset Čech complex

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal T=\binom{[6]}3\) be the twenty three-element subsets of a six-element set, with symmetric-difference distance \(d(A,B)=|A\triangle B|\). Let \(X\) be the intrinsic, vertex-centered Čech nerve of **closed balls of radius two** in \(\mathcal T\): a nonempty set \(\sigma\subseteq\mathcal T\) is a simplex precisely when there exists \(T\in\mathcal T\) such that \(d(T,A)\le2\) for each \(A\in\sigma\). The same complex is obtained for any ball radius \(\rho\) with \(2\le\rho<4\).

The complement involution \(\tau(A)=[6]\setminus A\) induces a free action on \(|X|\). Its geometric quotient \(Y=|X|/\langle\tau\rangle\) has
\[
\pi_1(Y)\cong\mathbb Z/2,\qquad H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Its reduced rational homology is
\[
\widetilde H_i(Y;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q^6&i=3,\\
\mathbb Q^{15}&i=4,\\
0&i\ne3,4.
\end{cases}
\]
The rational complement involution on \(\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\) has trace \(+1\), and on \(\widetilde H_4(X;\mathbb Q)\) has trace \(0\). The ambient original has rational Betti numbers \(11\) and \(30\) in those degrees, respectively; these two unquotiented ranks are already tabulated in the motivating literature and are not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
Balls are centered **in the same finite set** \(\mathcal T\), not in the full Boolean cube or in Euclidean space. The Čech faces are simultaneous intersections of these finite metric balls, not merely cliques in the Johnson graph. The quotient means the geometric orbit space of the simplicial action, rather than the naive simplicial complex on the ten vertex orbits. Homology uses ordinary singular homology, and the rank computations use rational coefficients unless integral coefficients are displayed explicitly.

## Proof

**1. Facets and the free involution.** For a center \(T\in\mathcal T\), the closed radius-two ball is
\[
N(T)=\{A\in\mathcal T:|A\cap T|\ge2\}.
\]
It has \(1+3\cdot3=10\) elements. Thus \(X=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal T}\Delta(N(T))\), where \(\Delta(N(T))\) is the full simplex on that ball. Since complementation preserves symmetric-difference distance, \(\tau N(T)=N(\tau T)\). A face cannot contain both \(A\) and \(\tau A\): any center at distance at most two from both would violate \(d(A,\tau A)=6\) by the triangle inequality. An invariant nonempty simplex under an involution would contain a complementary pair, so no simplex is invariant setwise. It follows that the induced action on the geometric realization is free.

**2. Simply connected total space.** The 20 vertices are partitioned into ten pairs \(\{A,\tau A\}\). Let \(P\) be the boundary of the ten-dimensional cross-polytope whose antipodal vertex pairs are precisely these ten pairs. Every face of \(X\) contains at most one vertex from each pair, so \(X\subseteq P\). The whole one-skeleton of \(P\) lies in \(X\). More strongly, every three-element face of \(P\) lies in \(X\). Here is a finite, symmetry-reduced certificate: the 960 triples that do not contain a complementary pair form exactly six \(S_6\)-orbits, with orbit sizes \(60,60,120,180,180,360\). One may choose the following representative triples and common centers, written using symbols \(0,1,2,3,4,5\):

| Three vertices (each vertex is a three-subset) | Common center | Orbit size |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 012, 013, 014 | 012 | 60 |
| 012, 013, 023 | 012 | 60 |
| 012, 013, 024 | 012 | 360 |
| 012, 013, 045 | 014 | 180 |
| 012, 013, 234 | 023 | 180 |
| 012, 034, 135 | 013 | 120 |

Every listed center has at least two elements in common with each vertex in its row; the property is preserved by relabelings. Exhaustive enumeration of the \(720\) relabelings confirms that the six orbits are disjoint and cover every admissible triple. Therefore \(X^{(2)}=P^{(2)}\). As \(P\cong S^9\), its two-skeleton is simply connected; attaching cells above dimension two does not affect \(\pi_1\). Hence \(\pi_1(X)=0\). The two-sheeted free covering \(|X|\to Y\) is universal and has deck group \(C_2\); this proves \(\pi_1(Y)\cong C_2\), and abelianization gives the claimed \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\).

**3. Exact rational homology of the quotient.** All simplices of \(X\) are generated by taking all nonempty subsets of each \(N(T)\). This gives the numbers of faces in dimensions \(0\) through \(9\):
\[
(20,180,960,2850,4500,4110,2400,900,200,20).
\]
No simplex is fixed setwise by \(\tau\), so the quotient is a finite \(\Delta\)-complex with half as many cell orbits in each dimension. Use the increasing ordering of the 20 three-subsets to orient each simplex. For a quotient cell, choose the lexicographically first face in its orbit; when one of its boundary faces is expressed as its selected orbit representative, multiply the ordinary alternating boundary sign by the sign of the sorting permutation induced by complementation if needed. The resulting **integral** cellular matrices obey \(\partial^2=0\). The exact ranks of their rationalizations, computed by the exhaustive rational Gaussian elimination in `verify.py`, are:

| Boundary degree \(d\) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_d\) for \(X\) | 19 | 161 | 799 | 2040 | 2430 | 1680 | 720 | 180 | 20 |
| \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_d\) for \(Y\) | 9 | 81 | 399 | 1020 | 1215 | 840 | 360 | 90 | 10 |

The quotient cell dimensions have counts
\[
(10,90,480,1425,2250,2055,1200,450,100,10).
\]
For each \(d\), the formula \(\beta_d=\#\text{cells}_d-\operatorname{rank}\partial_d-\operatorname{rank}\partial_{d+1}\), with \(\partial_0=\partial_{10}=0\), gives ordinary rational Betti vectors
\[
(1,0,0,11,30,0,0,0,0,0)\quad\text{for }X,
\]
\[
(1,0,0,6,15,0,0,0,0,0)\quad\text{for }Y.
\]
These are exact finite calculations, not extrapolations from a sample. The self-contained rational-arithmetic program in `verify.py` generates the boundary matrices directly from the definition, verifies all integral boundary-square identities, and reproduces the ranks without external input data.

**4. Homology character.** For a finite free involution and rational coefficients, transfer identifies \(H_i(Y;\mathbb Q)\) with the \(+1\)-eigenspace of \(\tau_*\) on \(H_i(X;\mathbb Q)\). Thus, in degrees three and four,
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_*|H_i(X;\mathbb Q))=2\beta_i(Y)-\beta_i(X),
\]
which yields \(2\cdot6-11=1\) and \(2\cdot15-30=0\), respectively. This also directly distinguishes the quotient invariant from the nonequivariant Betti numbers of \(X\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with standard Python. It constructs all twenty metric balls, all faces, the full non-antipodal two-skeleton, the six \(S_6\)-orbits, the 10 complement pairs, and both exact cellular chain complexes. It checks integral boundary squares, rational ranks, Euler characteristics and the reported homological traces. The exhaustive check ends with `ALL_VERIFY_OK`; the actual output is in `verification_output.txt`. The computation is finite and exact, using `fractions.Fraction`, not floating-point eigenvalues or randomized tests. The independent covering-space argument, rather than a matrix-based guess, proves the integral fundamental-group and degree-one homology assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Biswas, *Čech complexes for finite sets* (arXiv:2609.32786, first publicly posted 2026-09-26), studies the same symmetric-difference geometry, and its computational examples already give the unquotiented sphere counts \(11\) in degree three and \(30\) in degree four. The complement quotient, its fundamental group, and its rational homology character are the additional claims here. Feng and Nukala (arXiv:2302.14664) treat Vietoris–Rips complexes of subset spaces; their observation that a larger Vietoris–Rips complex is a cross-polytope boundary does not identify the Čech quotient studied here. Related published calculations on Johnson-graph Rips complexes and constant-weight Hamming Rips representations use a different complex, and do not give the stated quotient computations.

## Limitations
The proof determines the integral fundamental group and first homology of \(Y\), but does **not** determine integral higher homology of \(Y\), possible torsion there, its complete homotopy type, or the integral \(C_2\)-representation on higher homology. It is one natural finite middle layer; no all-parameters classification is asserted. The new mathematical contribution is the quotient/symmetry analysis, not the previously tabulated unquotiented sphere counts. An equivalent unpublished or unindexed formulation may exist.
