# Complete aperiodic orientable-word spectra and extremal symmetry at binary spans six and seven

## Finding

A binary word \(w=w_0\cdots w_{L-1}\) is *aperiodic orientable of span* \(n\) when its forward and reversed length-\(n\) factors, taken together over all starting positions, are pairwise distinct. Write \(N_n(L)\) for the number of such labeled length-\(L\) words, with no identification of reversal or bit complement. The following is an exact finite classification for \(n\in\{6,7\}\).

| Span \(n\) | Maximum length \(L_n\) | Labeled maximizers | Reversal/complement orbits | Size-two orbits | Size-four orbits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(6\) | \(26\) | \(22\) | \(6\) | \(1\) | \(5\) |
| \(7\) | \(48\) | \(3294\) | \(829\) | \(11\) | \(818\) |

These are *all* maximizers, not just witnesses. Every maximum-length word is balanced, containing exactly \(L_n/2\) occurrences of each bit. Every maximizer beginning with \(0\) starts in \(0^{n-1}1\) and ends in \(01^{n-1}\); its bit complement starts in \(1^{n-1}0\) and ends in \(10^{n-1}\). Exactly \(2\) of the \(22\) span-six maximizers, and exactly \(22\) of the \(3294\) span-seven maximizers, are invariant under *reverse complement*.

The result also supplies the complete exact word-count spectrum \(N_n(L)\) for every \(n\le L\le L_n+1\), including the zero value at \(L_n+1\). ## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is precisely \(\{0,1\}\). The words are *linear*: factors never wrap around an endpoint. The span parameter is exactly \(n=6\) or \(n=7\). For length \(L\ge n\), the \(L-n+1\) forward windows must have disjoint two-element reversal orbits, so every palindromic length-\(n\) window is forbidden. For any nonpalindromic window \(x\), let \(x^R\) be its reversal and \(\rho(x)=\min\{x,x^R\}\) in binary lexicographic order. There are precisely \(28\) available reversal pairs when \(n=6\) and \(56\) when \(n=7\).

The symmetry group is the four-element group generated by word reversal \(R\) and bitwise complementation \(C\); it does not include arbitrary cyclic rotations, since the words are not circular. Labeled words distinguish their positions and bit labels. The main counts are finite exact integers, not asymptotic estimates.

## Proof

**Reversal-orbit path encoding.** A word is orientable exactly when no length-\(n\) window is palindromic and no two distinct windows lie in the same reversal pair. Indeed, two windows from one reversal pair would repeat either a forward or a reversed observed window; conversely, distinct nonpalindromic reversal pairs make all forward and reverse observations distinct. This is a necessary-and-sufficient equivalence, not an approximation.

For each legal initial length-\(n\) window \(x\), record its final \(n-1\) bits and a bitset \(S=\{\rho(x)\}\). If the current suffix is \(s\) and the next appended bit is \(b\in\{0,1\}\), set \(y=sb\). Append \(b\) if and only if \(y\ne y^R\) and \(\rho(y)\notin S\); then replace the suffix by the final \(n-1\) bits of \(y\) and replace \(S\) by \(S\cup\{\rho(y)\}\). Every legal word extends along exactly one path of this search and every search path is legal. Induction on the number of appended bits proves both statements. Thus exact depth-first enumeration over all initial windows and both outgoing bits is a complete finite proof procedure.

**Symmetry normalization and exact numerical certificate.** Complementation is a fixed-point-free involution on all nonempty binary words and preserves the reversal-orbit condition. Consequently it suffices to start from every nonpalindromic length-\(n\) window whose first bit is zero and double the number at each depth. The programs `verify_bitmask.py` and `verify_direct.py` implement the complete path recurrence in two different representations: numerical reversal-pair masks and literal sets of previously visited window strings. Neither uses random sampling, an incomplete search bound, or a heuristic cutoff. Each checks all depths to \(L_n+1\), and both reproduce the complete exact coefficient sequences in the statement and the attached list of all first-bit-zero maximizers.

Both independent traversals find respectively \(11\) and \(1647\) normalized words at \(L_n=26\) and \(L_n=48\), and **zero** normalized words at lengths \(27\) and \(49\). Since every longer legal word would have a legal prefix of those excluded lengths, these zero counts prove optimality. The recorded first/last windows and Hamming weights of every maximizer directly establish the asserted endpoint patterns and exact balance. This is an exhaustive finite certificate with an inductive coverage argument; no inference from finite tests to an unbounded claim is made.

**Orbit formula.** Reversal and complementation commute. Neither has a fixed maximizer: bit complement fixes no nonempty word, while a word equal to its own reversal would have its first and last length-\(n\) windows in the same reversal orbit at distinct positions (here \(L_n>n\)). The remaining nonidentity symmetry is reverse complement \(RC\). The complete normalized lists contain exactly \(1\) and \(11\) reverse-complement-fixed words, so the full labeled lists contain exactly \(2\) and \(22\). Burnside's lemma now gives

\[
\frac{22+2}{4}=6,\qquad \frac{3294+22}{4}=829
\]

symmetry orbits. A reverse-complement-fixed word has a two-element orbit, and all other words have four-element orbits, yielding the asserted orbit-type counts.

## Relationship to prior work

Mitchell's October 24, 2026 preprint [1] determines optimal aperiodic lengths through span sixteen and gives examples. It discusses uniqueness under reversal and complementation at spans four and five, but does not give the two complete labeled length spectra, maximizer multiplicities, or symmetry-resolved endpoint-and-weight census above for spans six and seven in the inspected text. The established optimal lengths \(26\) and \(48\) themselves are not claimed as new. Earlier orientable-sequence construction results [2,3] address especially the periodic, cyclic problem and construction methods; those are not equivalent to the count of *linear* words at each length. The older position-sensing literature [4] supplies historical context, not the stated tables in the material inspected.

The new contribution is the exact, reproducible **population and symmetry classification** of the first two nonunique extremal binary aperiodic cases, together with all shorter-length counts, an invariant weight and forced endpoint structure. A stored earlier exhaustive-search implementation could in principle output some of these quantities without having published them, and undetected equivalent enumerations remain a priority risk.

## Limitations

The scope is only the two specified spans, not arbitrary \(n\) or nonbinary alphabets. The computation exhausts the stated finite search spaces, but no closed general formula for all spans is asserted. The given counts concern aperiodic linear sequences, not cyclic rotation classes. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed. The priority search is evidence of no identified earlier exact statement, not proof that no equivalent unpublished census exists.
