# Mathematical review: binary four-run cyclic antidictionaries

## Correctness

PASS. The proposed claim includes a complete binary bispecial-factor census and two sharp corollaries, the total number and maximum length of minimal forbidden factors. All boundary cases with positive run lengths, including the nonprimitive situation \(a=c,b=d\), are handled. The proof classifies zero-, one-, two- and at least three-transition factors. The key phase argument shows that two consecutive complete interior runs determine the cyclic phase unless the four-run word is a doubled two-run word. The general missing-extension identity is obtained by counting extensions at every factor length and telescoping the eventually constant complexity of a periodic word. Every mixed bispecial factor has two observed bi-extensions and thus a missing one; constant-letter forbidden factors attain the other possible maxima. A standalone implementation independently verified actual bispecial sets and minimal forbidden counts/maxima in 1,296 complete small profiles and 200 larger deterministic samples. Exhaustive small-profile checks are not the infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS on the evidence examined, with residual priority risk. The closest primary source is Fici–Restivo–Rizzo (arXiv:1707.04450), which defines circular minimal forbidden factors, gives general bounds and studies circular Fibonacci words; its Theorems 4–5 and its special-case classifications do not provide the four-run binary formula. Akagi–Okabe–Mieno–Nakashima–Inenaga (arXiv:2202.13591) provide compressed finite-word MAW algorithms and five run classes, but not this cyclic closed form. Charalampopoulos–Crochemore–Pissis (SPIRE 2018) give extended-bispecial data structures for finite words without a symbolic cyclic four-run classification in the accessible text. Existing general algorithms are not equivalent to an already proved symbolic formula unless the relevant run analysis is also supplied. Searches for periodic four-block binary minimal forbidden words, bispecial cores, exact counts and maximum lengths yielded no covering formulation. Earlier related distinct-letter circular antidictionary work assumes distinct symbols at every run; a four-run binary cycle repeats both symbols and does not satisfy it. Related four-run binary shuffle and five-run scattered-antichain results concern different predicates. No search can establish uniqueness against every unindexed source.

## Value

PASS. The four-run binary family is the first genuinely two-pair alternating binary circular pattern beyond the classical two-run cycle, and is a natural smallest setting in which opposite pairs of run lengths can disagree independently. The exact \(\max(a,c)+\max(b,d)+[a\ne c]+[b\ne d]\) cardinality collapses a potentially long forbidden-word census to two maxima and two equality tests; the bispecial list characterizes why two independent run-length defects contribute one each. The sharp longest-absence formula distinguishes the equal-pair, one-equality and two-inequality regimes. Both are exact, parameter-unbounded benchmarks for circular antidictionary and run-length-compressed factor algorithms, not merely a sample numerical table.

## Closest literature and limitations

The mathematical tools of circular forbidden factors, bispecial extensions and output-sensitive MAW enumeration are established; none of those tools alone certifies this family-specific closed form. The comparison covers arXiv:1707.04450, arXiv:2202.13591 and the author-available SPIRE 2018 manuscript. The last manuscript was inspected through extracted textual content rather than a visually accessible PDF. Exact priority remains open to an equivalent result expressed in other run conventions or absent-word terminology. The theorem does not cover finite-word boundary factors or additional run pairs. An independent audit has not been undertaken.
