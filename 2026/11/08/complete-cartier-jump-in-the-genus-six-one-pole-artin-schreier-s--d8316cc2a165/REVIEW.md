# Scientific review: exact Cartier jump in genus-six Artin–Schreier curves

## Correctness (C): PASS

The single-pole genus-six family is exhaustive by the standard-form theorem of Duque-Rosero et al. The unique break \(7\) gives a different exponent \(16\), genus \(6\), and an explicitly checked six-element regular-differential basis. The Cartier relation \(y=y^3-f(x)\) reduces the operator to a six-column calculation. Images have rank \(2\) exactly at \(a=0\), rank \(3\) otherwise; the second iterate has rank \(1\) and third iterate vanishes without restrictions. The exact script reconstructs these facts independently over all \(729\) parameter triples of \(\mathbb F_9\). Crucially, the algebraic proof, not finite sampling, establishes the infinite quantifiers.

## Originality (O): PASS with residual risk

The closest exact-scope studies are Groen's bounds for iterates, Booher–Cais's bounds and different-parameter jump examples, Farnell–Pries's constant-rank theorem subject to \(p\equiv1\pmod L\), and Duque-Rosero et al.'s complete normal forms and genus-three/four classifications. Groen's bounds do not give the coefficient criterion; the Booher–Cais characteristic-five example has a different genus and pole order; \(3\not\equiv1\pmod7\) excludes the Farnell–Pries constant-rank result; and the genus-three/four classification stops short of genus six. Full texts of these primary sources were inspected at the relevant theorems and examples. Searches of the mathematical-record repository using aliases, parameters, and Cartier-rank formulations did not return an equivalent claim. These checks do not prove absence of an older genus-six calculation in a thesis or a database; that remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

The classification treats the **entire** natural \(3\)-rank-zero genus-six Artin–Schreier stratum, rather than a sample, one arbitrary polynomial slice, or just two examples. Its sharp jump locus, the codimension-one coefficient hyperplane in an exhaustive normal form, distinguishes two regular-differential Cartier structures with the same genus, ramification, and \(3\)-rank. It resolves an invariant stratification left unspecified by general Cartier bounds and by the genus-three/four standard-form classification. The proof is short because the low-genus case admits a complete six-dimensional differential basis; shortness does not replace the structural full-stratum motivation.

## Scope and risks

The result does not determine Newton polygons, entire Ekedahl–Oort types, or the abstract moduli quotient, and it is limited to characteristic \(3\). The full extent of historical thesis computations cannot be excluded by searchable sources alone. Finite replay does not certify generic characteristic-three geometry; the universal symbolic argument bears that burden.
