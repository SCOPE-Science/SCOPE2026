# Complete census of optimal GC-balanced four-base DNA Hamming codes

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma=\{A,C,G,T\}\) and let \(\rho\) reverse a DNA word and complement \(A\leftrightarrow T\) and \(C\leftrightarrow G\). Among the subsets \(C\subseteq\Sigma^4\) in which every word has exactly two \(C/G\) coordinates, distinct words have Hamming distance at least \(3\), and \(\rho(C)=C\), the largest possible size is \(12\). There are **exactly \(16\) distinct labeled maximizers** (ordinary sets of four-letter words, without quotienting by symmetry). Exactly \(8\) contain no reverse-complement fixed words; the remaining \(8\) contain exactly \(4\) such words. Every maximizer has \(48\) unordered word-pairs at Hamming distance \(3\) and \(18\) at distance \(4\).

Let \(G\) be the group generated by independent alphabet permutations commuting with Watson–Crick complementation and coordinate permutations commuting with coordinate reversal. On this constant-GC-two problem the alphabet group has order \(8\) and the coordinate group has order \(8\); all \(64\) transformations preserve the conditions. The \(16\) maximizers form exactly **five \(G\)-orbits, of sizes \(2,2,4,4,4\)**. The palindrome-count/orbit-size pairs are \((4,2),(4,2),(4,4),(0,4),(0,4)\).

In the alternative *orientation-selected* convention, the code is a \(6\)-word set \(B\) satisfying both \(d_H(x,y)\ge3\) for distinct \(x,y\in B\) and \(d_H(x,\rho(y))\ge3\) for **all** \(x,y\in B\), including \(x=y\). There are exactly **\(512\) labeled six-word optima** in that convention, and each yields one of the eight palindrome-free twelve-word codes under \(B\mapsto B\cup\rho(B)\). The known maximum sizes \(12\) and \(6\) are not claimed as new; the exhaustive enumerations and structural classification are the finding.

## Assumptions and scope

All words have length exactly \(4\), the alphabet is ordered only to provide a reproducible lexicographic listing, GC-content is exactly \(2\), and Hamming distance counts unequal coordinates. A *labeled code* is a literal subset of \(\Sigma^4\), not an equivalence class modulo any permutation. A self-reverse-complementary word \(w=\rho(w)\) may belong to a twelve-word closed code; such words cannot belong to an orientation-selected code satisfying the stronger self-separation condition.

The required alphabet permutations may exchange the \(\{A,T\}\) and \(\{C,G\}\) blocks because exactly half the positions in each word are GC. For \(n=4\), the permissible coordinate permutations \(\pi\) satisfy \(\pi(3-i)=3-\pi(i)\) for \(i=0,1,2,3\). These two permutation groups commute as actions and have orders \(8\) and \(8\).

## Proof

**Universal bound.** Each word determines a two-element GC-position mask, one of \(\binom42=6\) masks. Fixing a mask leaves \(2^4=16\) words: each position independently chooses one of the two bases of its AT or GC pair. Among any three such four-bit words, the sum of all three pairwise Hamming distances is at most \(8\), since each coordinate distinguishes at most two of the three unordered pairs. Thus three words cannot have mutual distances at least \(3\). At most two codewords occur under any fixed mask, yielding \(|C|\le12\). Equality requires exactly two words in each mask.

**Exhaustive equality classification by masks.** Each mask has \(40\) unordered compatible pairs of two words: each of its \(16\) words has exactly five same-mask neighbors at Hamming distance \(3\) or \(4\), so \(16\cdot5/2=40\). The reverse-complement involution on masks has two nonfixed pairs, \(0011\leftrightarrow1100\) and \(0101\leftrightarrow1010\), and two fixed masks, \(0110\) and \(1001\). Every fixed mask has exactly \(4\) eligible two-word choices invariant under \(\rho\); the verifier computes these by enumerating all \(40\) choices and applying the involution. Therefore **every** potentially extremal \(\rho\)-closed code is represented, without omission, by choosing \(40\) pair options under mask \(0011\), \(40\) under \(0101\), and one of the \(4\) fixed-mask choices each under \(0110\) and \(1001\). The other two masks are forced by complementation. This is a finite family of exactly \(40^2\cdot4^2=25{,}600\) candidates. The standalone deterministic verifier `verify_masks.py` tests the complete six-mask Hamming condition for every candidate and produces precisely the \(16\) twelve-word codes listed in `optimal_codes.json`.

**Independent exhaustive enumeration.** `verify_graph.py` constructs the \(44\) allowable \(\rho\)-orbits of individual balanced words (discarding two-element orbits with internal Hamming distance less than \(3\)). Two vertices are adjacent precisely when the two orbits are pairwise Hamming-compatible. Its Bron–Kerbosch routine with pivoting exhaustively visits all maximal cliques, finding \(704\) of them, with \(16\) of total word weight \(12\); these are exactly the same listed codes. A maximum closed code corresponds to a clique because the word orbits partition every closed set. Both programs are short enough for direct algorithmic inspection and use Python's standard library.

**Palindromes, symmetries, and orientation selection.** The first verifier counts fixed points of \(\rho\) in each of the \(16\) codes. It enumerates all \(8\) commuting complement-preserving alphabet permutations and all \(8\) reversal-preserving coordinate permutations, computes full orbits, and verifies the stated five orbit sizes and fixed-point counts. A palindrome-free twelve-word code contains exactly six disjoint two-word \(\rho\)-orbits, and there are \(2^6=64\) ways to choose one orientation from each orbit. Every selection satisfies the stronger orientation-selected separation because all distinct words in the closed code already satisfy the Hamming condition. Conversely an orientation-selected six-word code is disjoint from its reverse complement and its union with that image is a closed twelve-word code. Hence all such optima arise uniquely from one of the \(8\) palindrome-free closed codes and one of \(64\) choices, giving \(8\cdot64=512\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_masks.py` followed by `python3 verify_graph.py` alongside `optimal_codes.json` using Python \(3.10\) or later (`int.bit_count` is required). Each verifier uses exhaustive loops over finite, explicitly specified search spaces, rather than statistical sampling. The scripts assert the entire output census, palindrome bifurcation, symmetry classes, oriented selection counts, the weight-twelve upper bound and the complete list agreement. `optimal_codes.json` is the full collection of \(16\) labeled codes and supplementary counts. The graph enumerator is independent of the mask-based search and reconstructs its conflict graph directly from the definitions.

## Relationship to prior work

King (first public arXiv version June 12, 2003; published September 2003) defines and studies the distinct constant-GC Hamming and reverse-complement constraints; its published tables give maximum \(12\) for the ordinary GC-Hamming condition at \((n,d,w)=(4,3,2)\) and maximum \(6\) for the stronger reverse-complement-separation convention. These numerical maxima are **prior results**. The new assertion is the full enumeration of optimal reverse-complement-*closed* twelve-word codes, the fixed-point/symmetry structure and the count of all oriented six-word optima. King's bounds and an exhibited code do not imply these numbers. Bennenni, Guenda and Gulliver (2015) study algebraic/greedy code constructions and bounds, not this full small-parameter census in the material inspected. A pertinent 2012 article by Aboluion, Smith and Perkins was identified, but only its abstract was accessible; an unexamined theorem or appendix there could affect historical priority.

## Limitations

This result is an exact finite classification for length \(4\), GC-content \(2\), minimum Hamming distance \(3\), and the stated two closely distinguished reverse-complement conventions. It does not establish values at greater lengths or other distances, error mechanisms, melting temperatures, or biological performance. The code-based enumeration can be checked directly from the included algorithm and outputs; the result has not undergone independent external scientific or historical-priority validation. The complete text of Aboluion–Smith–Perkins (2012) was inaccessible at the time of the literature comparison.
