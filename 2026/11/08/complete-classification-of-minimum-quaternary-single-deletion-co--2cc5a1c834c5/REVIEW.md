# Scientific review of the quaternary one-deletion covering-codebook census

**Correctness — PASS.** The sixteen ordered pairs require at least six codewords because any three-letter word has at most three distinct one-deletion outputs. The known six-word example attains this bound. An exhaustive lexicographic enumeration over all \(\binom{64}{6}=74{,}974{,}368\) candidate subsets uses only a necessary remaining-capacity pruning rule. An independently structured uncovered-pair branching procedure reaches every possible cover and regenerates exactly the same 644 codebooks. Direct finite group action on the full census yields 21 orbits, with sizes 8, 12, 24 and 48 in multiplicities 1, 3, 9 and 8. Exact deletion-shadow cardinalities yield 120 perfect, 336 one-excess and 188 two-excess covers. The executable checks passed on the actual supplied files.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Chee–Ge–Ling (2010), Section 6 and Table 6.1, already settle possible sizes of perfect codes, including a six-word quaternary witness. That does not imply the numbers of all minimum *covering* codebooks, especially the 524 nonperfect ones, or the 21-orbit classification. The full inspected 2020 conference paper by Lenz et al. concerns general construction and inequalities and reports no relevant exact census. Public research-index searches checked the aliases directed pair covering, deletion-covering code, perfect deletion-correcting code, and four-symbol six-word classification without finding an equivalent enumerator. The 2026 asymptotic paper was accessible only through descriptive excerpts, so unindexed or inaccessible historical enumeration remains a risk; absence of search hits alone is not a proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** Four symbols are the natural nucleotide alphabet; the shortest nontrivial ordered-pair deletion-covering designs have only six words. Complete classification of the minimum solution population distinguishes 21 structural symmetry types and identifies exactly which five classes have disjoint shadows, a property needed for error correction rather than coverage alone. This is a natural entire optimum layer, not an arbitrary subset of parameter choices or a restatement of the known optimum size. It provides a full reproducible design catalog for selecting distinct minimal codebooks.

**Limits.** This is not a new optimal size theorem or a claim about general lengths or alphabets. Priority remains qualified pending independent historical review; no independent audit has occurred.
