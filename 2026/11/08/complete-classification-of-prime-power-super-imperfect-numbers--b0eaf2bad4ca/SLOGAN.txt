The super-imperfect prime powers, defined by \(2\beta(\beta(n))=n\) for the alternating divisor-sum function \(\beta\), are exactly \(2,4,8,128,32768,2147483648\). A Fermat-prime factorization argument excludes every other prime-power exponent, completing the prime-power subcase of a question posed by Tóth while leaving arbitrary composites open.
