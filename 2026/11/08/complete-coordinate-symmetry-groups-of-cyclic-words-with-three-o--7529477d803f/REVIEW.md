# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim follows by four verified implications: (1) the full phase-factor family recovers residue blocks as its coordinate equality classes because the period is primitive; (2) an induced residue permutation preserves every translate of a cyclic run interval; (3) the maximal co-occurrence graph for a proper nonsingleton interval family is the original cycle, so the induced permutation is dihedral and the cyclic order of at least three uniquely named runs excludes reflection; (4) the exact multiplicities of residue classes admit a cyclic block shift only when the length is a multiple of the period. The proof handles the all-singleton-run case separately. These steps show group equality, not just a lower bound. The direct enumerator independently checked 382 finite parameter/length configurations and the two-colour boundary. The finite experiment is not substituted for the argument covering all positive parameters.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest source is Luchinin–Puzynina, arXiv:2112.04848v1 (December 9, 2021), pp. 3, 6–8. Proposition 6 proves the within-period residue-symmetric subgroup and Corollary 2 gives cyclic shifts at multiples of the period. Those statements do not imply the reverse inclusion: for other periodic words, additional reflections or residue permutations can survive. The present run-interval rigidity argument rules them out in a new natural infinite-parameter family and determines the entire group. Charlier–Puzynina–Zamboni (2015) and Launer–Puzynina–Voloshinova (2026) concern orbit-count complexity, not this maximum language-preserving symmetry group. Comparisons with the cited primary scientific statements and searches under multiple mathematical formulations found no stronger equality theorem. Residual risk: an unindexed equivalent result, especially in a graph/hypergraph automorphism formulation, could exist.

## Value — PASS
Proposition 6 provides only a universal lower subgroup for periodic words, while a full group is sensitive to hidden coordinate automorphisms. The explicit wreath/direct-product dichotomy resolves that gap for all cyclic words with distinct run letters, independent of the individual run lengths, and identifies a sharp arithmetical group jump at every multiple of the period. The binary counterexample proves the alphabet threshold is substantive. This is a complete natural family classification, with exact group structure and cardinality, not a sample calculation or a restatement of the lower bound.

## Relationship and limitations
The proof is restricted to at least three distinct run labels and factor lengths at least the full period. The two-letter case can carry reflections; other periodic words may have larger automorphism groups. The literature comparison has residual bibliographic uncertainty and is not an independent audit.
