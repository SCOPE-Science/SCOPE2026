# Complete coset-shattering polynomial for prime dihedral groups

## Finding
For every odd prime \(p\ge3\), write \(D_{2p}=\langle r,s\mid r^p=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\). Let \(\mathcal C_p\) be the family of **all distinct nonempty left cosets of all subgroups** of \(D_{2p}\), with the ground set the \(2p\) group elements. For each integer \(j\ge0\), let \(s_j(p)\) be the number of unordered \(j\)-element supports shattered by the membership concepts \(\mathcal C_p\). Then the entire shattering polynomial is
\[
\Phi_p(z)=\sum_{j\ge0}s_j(p)z^j=1+2pz+\binom{2p}{2}z^2+p^2(p-1)z^3.
\]
In particular, \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal C_p)=3\). More precisely, a triple is shattered exactly when it has two elements in one of the two cosets of the normal rotation subgroup \(\langle r\rangle\) and one in the other; every pair is shattered, and no support of size four is shattered. The family contains \(p^2+2p+3\) distinct concepts: \(2p\) singletons, \(p^2\) cross-coset pairs, two rotation-subgroup cosets and the entire group.

## Assumptions and scope
The symbol \(D_{2p}\) means the group of order \(2p\), not the polygon group of order \(4p\). The prime \(p\) is odd. Membership queries range over group elements; a support is shattered when every subset appears as its intersection with at least one coset. Only a **single coset** is selected for each trace, not an arbitrary union of cosets. The concept family includes the whole group and all singleton cosets, but not the empty concept. The constant coefficient convention is \(s_0=1\), as the family is nonempty. The statement concerns static VC shattering, not online/Littlestone complexity.

## Proof
**1. Subgroup/coset classification.** Since \(p\) is odd prime, Lagrange's theorem and elementary Sylow theory give exactly the trivial subgroup, the unique subgroup \(R=\langle r\rangle\) of order \(p\), the \(p\) reflection subgroups \(\langle r^i s\rangle\) of order two, and the full group. Their distinct left cosets are, respectively: all \(2p\) singletons; the two classes \(R\) and \(sR\); and the whole group. The order-two left cosets each contain one rotation and one reflection. Conversely, each rotation–reflection pair is a left coset of a unique reflection subgroup: for \(a\in R\), \(b\in sR\), the element \(a^{-1}b\) is a reflection and \(a\langle a^{-1}b\rangle=\{a,b\}\). Therefore these cosets are precisely the \(p^2\) cross-part pairs. No additional coset types occur. For \(p\ge3\), all listed types are distinct.

**2. Singletons and pairs.** Every one-point support is shattered by the singleton containing its element and a distinct singleton avoiding it. For any two distinct group elements \(u,v\), both singleton traces are witnessed by \(\{u\}\) and \(\{v\}\), while the empty trace is witnessed by a singleton outside \(\{u,v\}\). The full trace is witnessed by \(R\) or \(sR\) if \(u,v\) lie in the same part, and by the cross-part order-two coset \(\{u,v\}\) if they lie in different parts. Thus all \(\binom{2p}{2}\) pairs are shattered.

**3. Triples: necessity.** Let \(S\) be a three-element support contained entirely in \(R\), or entirely in \(sR\). Any concept containing two of its points must be one of the two whole-part cosets or the full group, and hence contains the third point. Consequently the trace consisting of those two points cannot be realized; \(S\) is not shattered. Every three-element support not confined to one part has a 2-plus-1 distribution.

**4. Triples: sufficiency.** Let \(S=\{u,v,w\}\) with \(u,v\in R\), \(w\in sR\), or with the two parts interchanged. The eight required traces are realized as follows: full support by \(D_{2p}\); \(\{u,v\}\) by its whole-part coset; \(\{u,w\}\) and \(\{v,w\}\) by their cross-part pair cosets; each singleton by its trivial-subgroup coset; and the empty set by any singleton outside \(S\), which exists because \(2p\ge6\). Hence every mixed triple is shattered. Their total number is \(2\binom p2p=p^2(p-1)\).

**5. No four-point supports.** If a four-element support has at least three points in one part, it contains a nonshattered triple by Step 3, so it cannot be shattered (hereditary property of shattering). Otherwise its points are distributed 2-plus-2 between the two parts. Consider the trace consisting of both elements in the first part and one element in the second part. The only concept containing three points from both parts is the entire group; all remaining concepts have at most two points or lie within a single part. The entire group also includes the fourth support point, so the proposed triple trace cannot occur. Every four-point support is unshattered. By heredity, so is every larger support. Summing the established coefficients proves the formula.

## Verification
A standalone standard-library Python checker `verify_dihedral.py` constructs the multiplication of \(D_{2p}\), independently enumerates subgroups generated by one or two elements, their left cosets, and all traces on supports of sizes zero through three for \(p=3,5,7,11\). It compares the computed coefficients and concept counts with the closed formulas. The proof above applies to every odd prime without numerical extrapolation. The checker does not claim exhaustive historical priority or independent verification.

## Relationship to prior work
Conant, Pillay and Terry [1] study bounded-VC translates and subgroup-coset structure in finite nonabelian groups. Their theorem concerns a fixed set and its translates, with global regularity/structure conclusions; it neither classifies all cosets of all subgroups of a prime dihedral group as one concept class nor enumerates shattered supports. General VC/shattering and finite-hypothesis frameworks [2] do not specify these coefficients. By comparison, cyclic groups of the same order admit only their own subgroup cosets; their cyclic coset families are structurally different from the cross-part reflection-coset configuration exploited here. The result is a fully explicit nonabelian benchmark for coset-set-system shattering, not a corollary of the cited global regularity result.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to groups of order \(2p\) for odd prime \(p\). Composite dihedral groups possess further subgroups and are not covered. No claim is made that all subgroup-coset systems of nonabelian groups have VC dimension three. The enumeration is a static shattering result and does not establish an online mistake bound. The earlier general framework is not a novelty certificate; an equivalent special-case classification under another terminology could exist and remains a historical-priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
