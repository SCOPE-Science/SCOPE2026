# Review of the rook-graph dissociation polynomial

## Correctness — PASS
The line-graph edge-degree identity is exact; the permitted selected-edge components are precisely isolated edges and two-edge paths. Component centres, leaves and isolated edges give a unique labelled enumeration and the factorial coefficient with no overcounting. The sharp maximum follows from three vertex-use bounds and an explicit feasible construction, with all residue classes and one-row boundaries handled. The separate finite checker reproduces every polynomial coefficient for eleven small boards, then verifies boundary constructions for 1–39 in both dimensions. Finite verification corroborates the complete proof but is not its basis.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
The 2011 line-graph dissociation theorem already implies a subset of the maximum-size formula for balanced complete bipartite hosts. A PUMaC 2010 problem also gives the precise 6-by-6 maximum of eight rooks and its 8100 optimal placements; both overlaps are credited and not asserted as original. The 2024 rook-graph *domination* polynomial counts a logically different property, while the 2026 sub-quorum manuscript treats path-factor grids and general lower bounds, not this clique-factor enumeration. Published findings on dissociation polynomials of multipartite graphs, windmills, and fans use different graph families. Semantic database searches found no statement giving all coefficients for arbitrary rook-board dimensions. Further older rook-placement literature may contain an equivalent enumerator under alternative terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The full coefficient distribution is a natural refinement of the ubiquitous nonattacking-rook and maximum-dissociation invariants. It counts all placements in which each chosen rook attacks at most one other chosen rook, and yields an exact finite sum and sharp dimension-ratio phase transition for an infinite two-parameter graph family. Neither the 2011 extremal value nor the 2024 domination polynomial implies these coefficients; the formula also supplies explicit lower bounds on the recent sub-quorum parameter.
