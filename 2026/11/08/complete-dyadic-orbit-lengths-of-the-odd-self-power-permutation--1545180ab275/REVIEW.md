# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The input is the unit group of the ring modulo \(2^k\), and the first proof step establishes that \(x\mapsto x^x\) is well defined and bijective on that group. The critical strong-isometry congruence is derived from convergent 2-adic binomial series, not inferred from finite samples. Exact first-displacement valuations are proved separately at the two 2-adic fixed points. The identity \(F^2(x)/x=(-x)^{xF(x)-1}\) on the negative branch yields the decisive cubic valuation \(3\nu_2(x+1)\). The period-doubling lemma then proves the all-exponent valuation law and exact minimal periods. Direct cycle enumeration through \(k=16\) checks an independent implementation; it does not substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with a stated historical risk
The closest verified paper is Holden–Richardson–Robinson, arXiv:1609.06696, whose Corollaries 11 and 19 already count one- and two-step returns for the binary self-power map. Those known counts are correctly treated as consequences, not as new work. Its full accessible HTML discusses the further treatment of longer walks as future work; the present theorem instead provides the least period of **every** odd residue and the complete census for **every** dyadic level. The earlier Holden–Robinson (2012) Section 6 addresses inverse-image fibers for odd primes, not this binary dynamics. The 2010 prime-modulus functional-graph work is more remote. Searches of the cited literature, exact formulation aliases, a public mathematical-result index, and accessible historical research claims did not identify equivalent or stronger coverage; absence of a hit does not establish historical uniqueness. The 2010 paper was inspected at abstract/scope level only, and unlocated historical equivalents remain a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural complete classification for a classical nonlinear arithmetic map, not an arbitrary finite slice. A finite graph of exponentially many vertices is described uniformly by two valuations and a closed census at every exponent. The exact maximum-period law, with precisely two largest cycles, and the striking asymmetry between the neighborhoods of \(+1\) and \(-1\) are structural information not determined by the previously published fixed- and two-cycle counts. This is useful for understanding the non-random functional graphs of self-power maps over binary rings.

## Scope and remaining limitations
The result is restricted to odd inputs and powers of two. The stronger prior literature establishes some low-period consequences, while independent priority and correctness reviews remain outstanding. The finite verifier supports, but cannot establish, the all-\(k\) claim.
