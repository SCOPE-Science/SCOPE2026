# Complete Ehrhart spectra of the five exceptional seven-dimensional binary polytopes

## Finding

Let \(V_1,\ldots,V_5\subset\{0,1\}^7\) be the five explicitly listed vertex sets in Table 3 of Kaibel and Pokutta (2026), in the order printed there, and write \(P_i=\operatorname{conv}(V_i)\). These are the complete five cube-symmetry classes of seven-dimensional simplicial \(0/1\)-polytopes with fourteen vertices that are not centrally symmetric, by the cited classification. The following table determines **their complete Ehrhart series**, not just their first few lattice-point counts. The coefficients list the polynomial \(h_i^*(z)\) in increasing degree, including its zero degree-seven coefficient. Normalized volume is \(7!\operatorname{vol}_7(P_i)\), with respect to \(\mathbb Z^7\).

| Polytope | \((h_{i,0}^*,\ldots,h_{i,7}^*)\) | Normalized volume |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| \(P_1\) | \((1,6,31,132,201,92,1,0)\) | \(464\) |
| \(P_2\) | \((1,6,31,112,171,64,1,0)\) | \(386\) |
| \(P_3\) | \((1,6,31,112,171,64,1,0)\) | \(386\) |
| \(P_4\) | \((1,6,29,134,197,82,1,0)\) | \(450\) |
| \(P_5\) | \((1,6,33,148,231,94,1,0)\) | \(514\) |

Explicitly, for every \(i\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}\) and every integer \(q\ge0\),
\[
\sum_{q\ge0} L_i(q)z^q=\frac{h_i^*(z)}{(1-z)^8},\qquad L_i(q)=\#(qP_i\cap\mathbb Z^7),
\]
with \(h_i^*(z)\) given by the table. Equivalently,
\[
L_i(q)=\sum_{j=0}^7h_{i,j}^*\binom{q+7-j}{7},\qquad
\binom{m}{7}=0\text{ for integer }m<7.
\]

The four distinct Ehrhart polynomials correspond to the four distinct affine-isomorphism types already identified by Kaibel and Pokutta. More concretely, \((L_i(2),L_i(3))\) is respectively \((115,716),(115,696),(115,696),(113,702),(117,748)\), so the two smallest positive dilations beyond the initial vertex count distinguish every pair among the five classes **except** \(P_2,P_3\), whose entire Ehrhart polynomials coincide. This is a lattice-arithmetic distinction despite the shared ordinary face vector of \(P_2,P_3,P_4,P_5\).

## Assumptions and scope

The assertion is exactly for the five seven-dimensional, full-dimensional lattice polytopes with the fourteen binary vertices in the five **columns** of Appendix A, Table 3, arXiv:2606.31640v2. Digits in each printed seven-bit word are the coordinates in their displayed order; coordinates lie in the ordinary lattice \(\mathbb Z^7\). The terms "Ehrhart \(h^*\)" and "ordinary face \(h\)" are distinct. The existing classification into five cube-symmetry classes, two combinatorial types, and four affine types is taken as a premise from the primary paper; this finding supplies the new lattice-counting data. No claim is made about other dimensions or the centrally symmetric members of the maximal-vertex class.

## Proof

The computation is a **finite exhaustive proof**; its self-contained exact verifier is `verify.py`. No unexamined numerical hull oracle is involved in this certificate.

1. Transcribe the five columns of the primary vertex table into five \(14\times7\) integer matrices. For each column, the fourteen binary rows are distinct, and each coordinate has exactly seven ones. The augmented \(8\times8\) matrix formed from the first eight vertices and a terminal column of ones has exact determinant, respectively, \(2,-6,-6,-6,-8\); in particular every \(P_i\) is seven-dimensional. The average of all fourteen vertices is \(c=\tfrac12\mathbf1\), which is strictly interior because the vertices affinely span and their average has all positive barycentric coefficients.

2. Every facet of any full-dimensional seven-polytope contains seven affinely independent vertices. Consequently, every facet of each \(P_i\) is generated by at least one seven-element subset of its fourteen vertices. The verifier visits **all \(\binom{14}{7}=3432\)** subsets for each column. For each subset it computes all seven maximal cofactors of its six-row difference matrix by exact fraction-free integer elimination. When the cofactor vector is nonzero, the resulting hyperplane is tested against *all fourteen vertices*. It is accepted precisely if it is supporting. Every accepted supporting hyperplane contains exactly seven vertices; this certifies that the facets are simplices and gives exactly \(144,136,136,136,136\) facets, independently reproducing the published face counts. The exhaustive subset argument proves that no facet is omitted.

3. All integer points of \(qP_i\) lie in the finite grid \(\{0,\ldots,q\}^7\), since \(P_i\subset[0,1]^7\). Given the **complete** exact facet inequalities from step 2, an integer point belongs to \(qP_i\) if and only if it satisfies each inequality after multiplying the right-hand side by \(q\). The verifier tests each integer grid point for \(q=0,1,\ldots,7\), without sampling or an unproved pruning assumption. The resulting eight-tuples \((L_i(0),\ldots,L_i(7))\) are

   | Polytope | \((L_i(0),\ldots,L_i(7))\) |
   | --- | --- |
   | \(P_1\) | \((1,14,115,716,3423,12944,40511,109280)\) |
   | \(P_2\) | \((1,14,115,696,3233,11956,36807,98072)\) |
   | \(P_3\) | \((1,14,115,696,3233,11956,36807,98072)\) |
   | \(P_4\) | \((1,14,113,702,3363,12734,39867,107516)\) |
   | \(P_5\) | \((1,14,117,748,3653,14002,44187,119816)\) |

4. For an integral seven-polytope, Ehrhart theory gives a polynomial numerator of degree at most seven in \(\sum_{q\ge0}L_i(q)z^q=h_i^*(z)/(1-z)^8\). Therefore its coefficients are **exactly**
\[
h_{i,j}^*=\sum_{q=0}^j(-1)^{j-q}\binom8{j-q}L_i(q),\qquad 0\le j\le7.
\]
   Applying this finite identity to the eight exact counts produces the displayed table and determines the Ehrhart polynomial for **every** dilation \(q\ge0\), not just \(q\le7\).

5. The normalized-volume calculation has a separate exact geometric cross-check. Every \(P_i\) is the union, with disjoint interiors, of pyramids \(\operatorname{conv}(c,F)\) over its simplicial facets \(F\), since \(c\) is interior. If the seven vertices of \(F\) are \(v_1,\ldots,v_7\), the normalized volume contributed by that pyramid is \(|\det(v_1-c,\ldots,v_7-c)|\). The verifier sums \(2^{-7}|\det(2v_1-\mathbf1,\ldots,2v_7-\mathbf1)|\) over its exhaustive facet list, using only integer determinants. This independently gives \(464,386,386,450,514\), agreeing with \(\sum_j h_{i,j}^*\) for every column.

Each of steps 1–5 uses finite integer operations with explicitly exhausted ranges. All boundary cases, including dilations \(q=0\) and \(q=7\), are included. Thus the table is exact and the universal-in-dilation conclusion follows from the standard Ehrhart rationality theorem.

The program contains the complete fourteen-by-five seven-bit source data and expected coefficients; it independently constructs every facet by checking all 3432 candidate subsets per polytope, enumerates lattice points through the required eighth dilation, and verifies an independent center-star volume determinant sum. ## Relationship to prior work

Kaibel and Pokutta, *A Counterexample to Ziegler's Cross-Polytope Conjecture for Simplicial 0/1-Polytopes* (arXiv:2606.31640, first submitted 2026-06-30), proved the sharp extremal vertex bound, found the first noncentrally symmetric example, enumerated the five exceptional cube-symmetry orbits, and provided all five generating vertex columns plus their ordinary combinatorial \(f\)- and \(h\)-vectors. These results and data are **not new here**. Sections 3–4 and Appendix A of the accessible complete HTML text were examined directly. The primary paper does **not** tabulate the five Ehrhart \(h^*\)-polynomials or lattice volumes and makes no such claimed classification. Its ordinary \(h\)-vectors cannot imply the new Ehrhart numbers: the four examples with identical ordinary face vectors here have three different Ehrhart series. Classical Ehrhart theory supplies the finite-difference identity and the volume normalization; it does not supply the particular numerical spectra without the new exact enumeration.

The closest mathematical-record results retrieved on October 2026 polytope geometry concern different seven-dimensional polytopes (vertex-facet assignment obstructions) or unrelated root-polytopes' Ehrhart series; neither covers these five vertex configurations. No historical uniqueness is claimed solely from unsuccessful searches.

## Limitations

The completeness over *all* exceptional seven-dimensional objects relies on the primary paper's published exhaustive classification and is not re-proved here. The independent enumeration covers only the five listed representatives, all their nonnegative dilation counts by Ehrhart's theorem, and no other seven-dimensional families. The equality of the Ehrhart series of \(P_2\) and \(P_3\) does not by itself imply unimodular or affine equivalence; the prior paper independently establishes their affine equivalence. Priority beyond the inspected primary and related records remains provisional. No independent audit or independent validation was performed.
