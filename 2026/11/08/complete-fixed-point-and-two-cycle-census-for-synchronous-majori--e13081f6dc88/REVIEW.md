# Scientific review: prism majority terminal-state census

## Correctness (C) — PASS
The claim is one exact theorem for every integer \(n\ge3\) under simultaneous self-exclusive three-neighbor strict majority. The energy argument proves periods of length at most two without an unverified assumption about convergence. Rung-by-rung fixed-point classification gives \(F_n\) and handles the small triangular prism. A separate time-pair transfer construction represents exactly \(M^2(x)=x\) as a finite graph trace, not merely a sampled approximation. All sixteen column symbols, \(256\) ordered-pair vertices and \(400\) legal transitions are checked. The six cycle-bearing strongly connected components have explicitly reconstructed characteristic polynomials using only integer arithmetic; the recurrent trace simplifies algebraically to the stated six-residue Lucas formulas. The package's own `verify.py` checks these identities and independently exhausts all configurations for \(3\le n\le8\). The source proof and reproducible code agree. The verifier is a checkable finite certificate, not an independent audit.

## Originality (O) — PASS with documented residual risk
The paper arXiv:2302.06760 proves stable-count growth and other diffusion results on one-dimensional cycles, while the present object is the cubic two-ring prism and the main result enumerates every temporal two-cycle. arXiv:2312.13769 supplies finite-tree fixed/pure-cycle algorithms but does not cover cyclic prisms or mixed temporal two-cycles. arXiv:2205.08972 concerns the self-inclusive one-dimensional radius-majority rule and does not imply the two-track self-exclusive prism trace. arXiv:2101.09103 counts static anti-coordination Nash equilibria on circular ladders; potential bipartite relabelings may relate some fixed-state special cases, but its static equilibrium formulas do not establish \(\operatorname{tr}(B^n)\) for two synchronous updates or the exact strict two-cycle count. Searches for synonymous prism/circular-ladder majority orbit formulas and indexed mathematical claims did not identify a dominating result. An unindexed equivalent Boolean-network transfer calculation remains possible, and a specialist literature review would still be needed for publication priority.

## Value (V) — PASS
The problem is the entire terminal-orbit spectrum of a canonical vertex-transitive cubic social network, directly motivated by the source's question about stable configurations beyond simple cycles. The unexpected feature is an explicit exponential separation between blinking terminal states and fixed opinion patterns: \(T_n\sim\varphi^{2n}\) but \(F_n\sim\varphi^n\). The exact six-residue correction and strict two-cycle orbit count provide a natural benchmark for majority dynamics on multilayer structures; this is not a small numerical sample or an arbitrary isolated parameter. It answers a concrete question about dynamic versus static opinion diversity, while deliberately not making claims about attraction probabilities.

## Closest literature and limitations
Primary material compared: Zehmakan (2023, arXiv:2302.06760, introduction, theorem on stable cycle colorings, applications and conclusions); Nakar and Ron (2022, arXiv:2205.08972, definitions, main theorem, temporal periodicity); Turau (2023, arXiv:2312.13769, theorem statements and Sections 2–3, tree and pure-cycle scope); Sánchez Muñoz and Mc Gettrick (2021, arXiv:2101.09103, game definition, both circular-ladder sections, discussion and appendices). These are not claimed to be a complete literature universe. The calculation is for labeled configurations and excludes basin sizes, noisy updates and weighted interactions. Finite code checks are accompanied by a uniform trace identity and a proof of period-two convergence.
