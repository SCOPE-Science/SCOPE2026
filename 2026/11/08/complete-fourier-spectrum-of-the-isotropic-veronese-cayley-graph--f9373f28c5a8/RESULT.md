# Complete Fourier spectrum of the isotropic Veronese Cayley graph over odd finite fields

## Finding
For each odd prime power \(q\ge5\), the trace-zero symmetric-matrix Cayley graph generated by nonzero symmetric rank-one nilpotents has exactly six distinct adjacency eigenvalues. Its degree is \(q^2-1\). The five nonconstant eigenvalues, indexed by \(0\le k\le4\), are
\[
\lambda_k=qk-(q+1),\qquad
M_k=\binom{q+1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{4-k}(-1)^j\binom{q+1-k}{j}\bigl(q^{5-k-j}-1\bigr).
\]
Each \(M_k\) is strictly positive. The degree has multiplicity one. Consequently the convolution operator is invertible with exact spectral condition number \(q^2-1\); its largest absolute eigenvalue away from the constant eigenspace is \(3q-1\), and its sharp normalized spectral gap is \(1-(3q-1)/(q^2-1)\).

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(\mathbb F_q\) have odd order \(q\ge5\), let \(V=\{A\in\operatorname{Sym}_3(\mathbb F_q):\operatorname{tr}A=0\}\), and use its additive group structure. Set
\[
S=\{X\in V:\operatorname{rank}X=1,\ X^2=0\},\qquad
(Cf)(A)=\sum_{X\in S}f(A-X).
\]
The trace condition is over the field, while the spectral operator acts over \(\mathbb C\). The Euclidean condition number is the ratio of the largest to smallest absolute eigenvalues (counted with their multiplicities) of the self-adjoint convolution operator. A fixed nontrivial additive character of \(\mathbb F_q\) is understood.

## Proof
**1. Character parametrization.** The vector space \(V\) has dimension five. Under the pairing \((A,Y)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(AY)\), its dual equals \(\operatorname{Sym}_3(\mathbb F_q)/\mathbb F_q I\): the annihilator of the trace-zero hyperplane consists of scalar matrices. This remains valid in characteristic three because the trace pairing on the full symmetric-matrix space is nondegenerate in odd characteristic.

**2. Generators and the conic.** Every nonzero rank-one symmetric matrix can be written \(\alpha uu^{\mathsf T}\), with \(\alpha\ne0\) and \(u\ne0\). Its square is \(\alpha^2(u\cdot u)uu^{\mathsf T}\), so square-zero is equivalent to \(u\cdot u=0\). The nonsingular ternary quadratic form \(u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2\) defines a nonsingular projective conic \(\mathcal C\), which has exactly \(q+1\) rational points. Each isotropic projective point supplies exactly \(q-1\) distinct generators; different projective points supply disjoint sets. Therefore \(|S|=(q+1)(q-1)=q^2-1\), and \(S=-S\).

**3. Fourier diagonalization.** For a class \([Y]\) in the dual, use the character \(\chi_Y(A)=\psi(\operatorname{tr}(AY))\). The associated eigenvalue is
\[
\sum_{[u]\in\mathcal C(\mathbb F_q)}\ \sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb F_q^\times}\psi(\alpha u^{\mathsf T}Yu)
=q\,k(Y)-(q+1),
\]
where \(k(Y)\) is the number of projective conic points with \(u^{\mathsf T}Yu=0\). Choice of representative for \([Y]\) is harmless because adding a scalar multiple of \(I\) does not change \(u^{\mathsf T}Yu\) on \(\mathcal C\). The zero class yields \(k=q+1\) and eigenvalue \(q^2-1\).

**4. The complete set of quartics.** Identify \(\mathcal C\) with \(\mathbb P^1\) through a conic parametrization by quadratic homogeneous coordinates. Restricting any ternary quadratic \(u^{\mathsf T}Yu\) to \(\mathcal C\) produces a homogeneous binary quartic. Conversely, restriction is surjective onto the five-dimensional space of binary quartics: the kernel is exactly the one-dimensional span of the defining conic equation, since a degree-two homogeneous polynomial vanishing on the nonsingular conic is a multiple of its defining quadratic. This also follows by the standard quadratic Veronese parametrization and explicit coefficient comparison. Hence the quotient of the six-dimensional symmetric coefficient space by scalar \(I\) is linearly identified with all binary quartics. A nonzero quartic has at most four projective roots. Each value \(k=0,1,2,3,4\) occurs: choose a quartic without rational roots for \(k=0\) (an irreducible quartic exists), or choose products of exactly \(k\) distinct rational linear factors, with repetitions to attain degree four, for \(k\ge1\).

**5. Multiplicity count.** Fix \(k\) specified distinct points of \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)\). The space of quartics vanishing at any \(r\le4\) specified points has dimension \(5-r\): evaluation conditions are independent by homogeneous polynomial interpolation. Therefore the number of *nonzero* quartics vanishing exactly at those \(k\) points and no others is, by inclusion-exclusion,
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{4-k}(-1)^j\binom{q+1-k}{j}(q^{5-k-j}-1).
\]
Terms with \(k+j\ge5\) vanish because no nonzero quartic has five distinct roots. Choosing the \(k\) zero points gives \(M_k\). Positivity follows from the explicit examples above. These multiplicities together with the zero-quartic constant class exhaust \(q^5\) characters.

**6. Exact spectral consequences.** All nonconstant eigenvalues are congruent to \(-1\) modulo \(q\) and are thus nonzero. The smallest absolute eigenvalue is \(|\lambda_1|=1\), and the largest is the degree \(q^2-1\), since \(3q-1<q^2-1\) for \(q\ge5\). Among nonconstant characters, the largest modulus is \(\lambda_4=3q-1\), attained with multiplicity \(M_4>0\). Hence the exact condition number and normalized spectral ratio follow. In particular the graph is connected and not bipartite.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` enumerates the entire five-dimensional character dual at \(q=5\), constructs the generator set independently from isotropic projective points, directly evaluates every Fourier character sum, and checks the inclusion-exclusion multiplicity formula. It verifies 3,125 characters, 24 generators, the root multiplicity histogram \((820,1224,780,240,60)\), and the constant eigenspace. These checks corroborate, rather than replace, the all-field proof.

To reproduce: run `python verify.py` using Python 3 standard library; expected terminal line: `ISOTROPIC_VERONESE_FOURIER_CENSUS_PASS`.

## Relationship to prior work
The nilpotent-orbit literature, including McNinch's 2002 preprint and Lehrer's studies of invariant functions and Fourier transforms, motivates the use of rank-one nilpotents, but the inspected material did not state the present trace-zero symmetric-conic spectrum with exact eigenvalue multiplicities. Published finite-field Fourier restriction calculations on rank-one symmetric sets address different restricted character families or operators, not this full additive Cayley convolution law. This is a bounded priority comparison, not an assertion of exhaustive historical uniqueness.

## Limitations
The restriction to odd field order and \(q\ge5\) is essential to this statement; small fields and characteristic two are not classified. The theorem determines the adjacency spectrum, not arbitrary nonlinear matrix-function Fourier transforms. Earlier finite association-scheme or orthogonal-group orbital transform formulas could imply this result indirectly; historical priority remains a genuine risk. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
