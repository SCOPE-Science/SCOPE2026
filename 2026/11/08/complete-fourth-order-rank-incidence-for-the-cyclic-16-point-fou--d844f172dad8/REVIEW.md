# Review

## Correctness — PASS

Every restriction is treated as a matrix over the sixteenth cyclotomic field, with correct quantifiers and no use of floating-point rank. The exact determinant polynomial has at most 24 unit-coefficient terms, giving a degree-seven reduction with integer coefficients of magnitude at most 24. Its vanishing is equivalent to vanishing at all eight primitive roots in characteristic 97. A cyclotomic-integer method and modular Gaussian elimination agree on all 45,500 affine-representative cases. The affine group accounts for every one of the 3,312,400 pairs, with disjoint orbits and exact multiplication by orbit size. The table and global totals are reproduced by the included checker.

## Originality — PASS under bounded evidence

Tao's prime-order theorem does not cover cyclic order 16. Theorem 9 of Alexeev–Cahill–Mixon determines which *row sets* are full spark and implies the 256 all-invertible row sets, not the full rank-defect census or 25-orbit incidence. A previously described near-consecutive four-row classification covers one row orbit and agrees with its 28 singular partners; an order-16 support-gap result concerns different rectangular restrictions. No identified result specifies the four rank totals together with their affine-stratified multiplicities. Unseen tables or stronger general theorems remain possible, so priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The standard 16-point DFT and its fourth-order restrictions form a canonical model of four-sparse time-frequency interpolation. The complete rank data distinguish exact nullity levels, not merely invertibility, and show 25 inequivalent row geometries with markedly different rank profiles. The example is a natural first substantial dyadic FFT test case and distinguishes its group-dependent interpolation geometry from the Walsh transform of the same order. The full-spark theorem by itself does not supply this information.

## Scope and residual risk

The theorem is specific to fourth-order restrictions of the cyclic order-16 Fourier transform. Historical coverage may be broader than the two primary papers and the compared published summaries; independent assessment has not yet been undertaken.
