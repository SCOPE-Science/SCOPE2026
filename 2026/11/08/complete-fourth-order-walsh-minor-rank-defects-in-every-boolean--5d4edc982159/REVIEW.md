# Scientific review: fourth-order Walsh minor rank defects

## Correctness: PASS

The reduction of every normalized fourth-order Walsh restriction matrix to the integer three-by-three incidence block is exact over the real and complex fields. The two row classes are exhaustive. The fixed finite triple sums enumerate all projected distinct column quadruples with their proper lift multiplicities; their degree-three polynomial identities were checked exactly for more than four distinct values. A standalone integer checker also enumerates actual Walsh restrictions in dimensions three, four and five and directly cross-checks raw fourth-order minors in dimensions three and four. The formulas satisfy the general Cauchy–Binet determinant-squared identity, and the previously published order-16 singular total agrees. Integer experiments support the explicit universally quantified finite-case proof; the proof does not infer an infinite statement from selected dimensions.

## Originality: PASS (bounded)

Brent and Osborn's 2012 preprints establish general Hadamard minor identities, zero counts in smaller minor orders, and the finite order-16 fourth-minor zero count; none of those statements gives the dimension-uniform singular **rank-refined** distribution. Previously published classifications of orthogonal four-character spectra determine some nonsingular maximal-determinant cases; they do not give counts for ranks one, two, and three. Previously published four-sparse Walsh support spectra concern one-dimensional nullspace vectors with nonzero coefficients and the number of Fourier zeros, not rank distributions over all four-by-four restrictions. The novelty assessment is limited to the rank-defect census and its dimension-uniform affine-type formulas, not the known zero count, determinant-moment identity, or orthogonal-basis counts. There remains a risk that an unindexed rank-distribution formula or a stronger Hadamard submatrix theorem already implies these formulas. A historical search is not a priority certificate.

## Value: PASS

The rank of a Fourier restriction submatrix is the number of locally identifiable degrees of freedom and the dimension of its kernel measures interpolation failure. Resolving rank-one, rank-two and rank-three restrictions separately is strictly more informative than the known aggregate count of singular minors. The affine-plane/tetrahedron dichotomy yields complete uniform formulas in all dimensions and a sharp asymptotic real-rank law. The object is a canonical family of Fourier sampling matrices rather than an arbitrary unmotivated finite selection.

## Limitations

These statements cover four-by-four restrictions of Walsh matrices on binary vector spaces; they make no assertion about general Hadamard matrices or higher-order restrictions. The finite checker is corroborating evidence for an explicitly enumerated finite core, not an external mathematical certification. An independent audit has not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
