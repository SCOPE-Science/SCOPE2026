# Complete infinity semigroups on maximal curves without geometric automorphisms

## Finding

Let \(p\) be an odd prime, \(n\ge 2\) an integer, and \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p\). Let \(X_{p,n}\) denote the smooth projective model of the curve
\[
 u v^p+v=-1-2\sum_{i=1}^{n}u^{(p^{2i}-1)/(2(p-1))}.
\]
This is the maximal, geometrically automorphism-free family introduced by Bartoli, Borges, Timpanella and Zini [1]. Let \(P_\infty\in X_{p,n}(k)\) be the unique point above \(u=\infty\), and put
\[
 D=2(p-1),\qquad L=\frac{p^{2n}+1-2p}{D}=\frac{p^{2n}-1}{2(p-1)}-1.
\]
The **complete Weierstrass semigroup** of pole orders at \(P_\infty\) is
\[
 \boxed{H(P_\infty)=\langle p,\ p+L,\ p+2L,\ldots,\ p+(p-1)L\rangle.}
\]
Its Apéry set with respect to \(p\) is precisely
\[
 \operatorname{Ap}(H(P_\infty),p)
 =\{0\}\cup\{p+bL:1\le b\le p-1\}.
\]
These \(p\) displayed generators are all minimal, so the semigroup has multiplicity and embedding dimension both equal to \(p\). Its numerical-semigroup genus, Frobenius number, conductor, and type are respectively
\[
 \boxed{g=\frac{p^{2n}-1}{4},\quad F=(p-1)L,\quad c=(p-1)L+1,\quad t=p-1.}
\]
Its pseudo-Frobenius numbers are exactly \(L,2L,\ldots,(p-1)L\), and it is almost symmetric (\(2g=F+t\)). The **classical Weierstrass weight** of the distinguished point is
\[
 \boxed{w(P_\infty)=\frac{(p^2-1)(L^2-1)}{24}.}
\]
In particular, if \(\ell(sP_\infty)=\dim_k H^0(X_{p,n},\mathcal O(sP_\infty))\) for \(s\ge0\), then every one-point Riemann–Roch dimension is explicitly
\[
 \ell(sP_\infty)=1+\left\lfloor\frac{s}{p}\right\rfloor
 +\sum_{b=1}^{p-1}\max\left(0,\ 1+\left\lfloor\frac{s-p-bL}{p}\right\rfloor\right).
\]
All assertions concern the geometric curve over \(k\); the original models and maximality statements are over the finite fields specified in [1].

## Assumptions and scope

The prime \(p\) is odd, \(n\) is any integer at least two, and all fields and function-field valuations in the proof are taken after base change to \(k\). At a point \(P\), \(H(P)\) comprises zero and the positive integers occurring as the exact pole order of a rational function with no other poles. A nonnegative integer not in \(H(P)\) is a gap. The Weierstrass weight is \(\sum_{j=1}^g(\gamma_j-j)\), where \(1\le\gamma_1<\cdots<\gamma_g\) are the gaps. The type is the number of pseudo-Frobenius gaps \(a\), characterized by \(a+h\in H\) for every positive \(h\in H\). The proof does not require coefficients beyond the nonzero lowest and highest additive terms.

## Proof

**1. Tame scalar covering and a unique infinite point.** Define \(m=p^{2n}+1\) and choose \(\lambda\in k^\times\) with \(\lambda^{p-1}=-1\). There is an additive polynomial
\[
 R(x)=\sum_{i=0}^n c_i x^{p^{2i}},\qquad c_0c_n\ne0,
\]
whose coefficients satisfy \(R(x)/x=-P(x^D)/\lambda\), where \(P(u)=-1-2\sum_{i=1}^n u^{(p^{2i}-1)/D}\). Indeed, each exponent \((p^{2i}-1)/D\) is an integer, and the indicated substitution gives precisely powers \(x^{p^{2i}-1}\). Consider the smooth affine Artin–Schreier curve
\[
 W:\quad y^p-y=xR(x).
\]
The cyclic group \(G=\mu_D\) acts by \((x,y)\mapsto(a x,a^2y)\). The relation \(a^{p^{2i}-1}=1\) for \(a\in\mu_D\) shows that this action preserves \(W\). Its fixed subfield is generated by \(u=x^D\) and \(z=y/x^2\), for which \(u z^p-z=R(x)/x\). Replacing \(z\) by \(v=\lambda z\) produces \(u v^p+v=P(u)\). Thus the smooth quotient of \(W\) by \(G\) is \(X_{p,n}\), as also given by the scalar-quotient construction in [1].

Since \(\deg(xR)=m\) and \(\gcd(p,m)=1\), the smooth completion of \(W\) has a unique point \(Q_\infty\) above \(x=\infty\). The standard valuation calculation for an Artin–Schreier extension gives
\[
 -\operatorname{ord}_{Q_\infty}(x)=p,
 \qquad -\operatorname{ord}_{Q_\infty}(y)=m.
\]
The point \(Q_\infty\) is fixed by \(G\). As \(|G|=D\) is prime to \(p\), it is totally tamely ramified, of index \(D\), above the unique quotient point \(P_\infty\). Consequently every invariant function's pole order at \(P_\infty\) is its pole order at \(Q_\infty\) divided by \(D\).

**2. Complete invariant affine ring.** The coordinate ring of \(W\setminus\{Q_\infty\}\) is
\[
 A=k[x,y]/(y^p-y-xR(x))
 =\bigoplus_{b=0}^{p-1} k[x]y^b.
\]
The second equality is a free \(k[x]\)-module decomposition, since the equation is monic of degree \(p\) in \(y\). A monomial \(x^a y^b\) has \(G\)-weight \(a+2b\pmod D\); it is invariant if and only if \(a+2b\equiv0\pmod D\). Therefore, putting
\[
 U=x^D,\qquad \Phi_0=1,\qquad
 \Phi_b=x^{D-2b}y^b\quad(1\le b\le p-1),
\]
we obtain the **entire** invariant ring
\[
 A^G=\bigoplus_{b=0}^{p-1}k[U]\Phi_b.
\]
Here \(D-2b\ge0\), including the endpoint \(b=p-1\). Because the affine quotient is the complement of the single point \(P_\infty\), this invariant ring is exactly the ring of rational functions with no poles outside \(P_\infty\).

The pole orders downstairs are
\[
 -\operatorname{ord}_{P_\infty}(U)=p,
\qquad -\operatorname{ord}_{P_\infty}(\Phi_b)
 =\frac{p(D-2b)+mb}{D}=p+bL\quad(1\le b\le p-1).
\]
The integer \(L\) is prime to \(p\), since \(L\equiv-2^{-1}\pmod p\). Thus \(0,p+L,\ldots,p+(p-1)L\) occupy **distinct residue classes modulo \(p\)**. As a result, the leading poles of terms from different direct summands cannot cancel; within one summand, different powers of \(U\) also have different pole orders. This shows, without any assumption about generic coefficients, that
\[
 H(P_\infty)=p\mathbb N_0\ \sqcup\!
 \bigsqcup_{b=1}^{p-1}\bigl(p+bL+p\mathbb N_0\bigr).
\]
This is equality, not just inclusion: every regular-away-from-infinity function belongs to \(A^G\), and every pole order in the displayed union is realized by a monomial \(U^j\Phi_b\).

**3. Apéry set, minimal generators and type.** The distinct residue classes prove the Apéry-set statement. None of the positive Apéry elements \(w_b=p+bL\) can be decomposed into smaller positive semigroup elements: subtraction of \(p\) leaves the gap \(bL\), while an alleged decomposition into two nonzero Apéry elements would require their subscripts to sum to \(b\) or \(b+p\); in either case the sum exceeds \(w_b\). Hence all \(p\) generators are minimal.

The maximal gap equals \(\max\operatorname{Ap}(H,p)-p=(p-1)L\). Each \(bL=w_b-p\) is a gap, but adding any generator makes it a non-gap: adding \(p\) gives \(w_b\), while adding \(p+cL\) gives \(p+(b+c)L\), which is \(w_{b+c}\) if \(b+c<p\), and is a nonnegative multiple of \(p\) beyond an Apéry representative if \(b+c\ge p\). Thus all \(p-1\) numbers \(bL\) are pseudo-Frobenius gaps. No other such gaps exist, since a numerical semigroup of multiplicity \(p\) has type at most \(p-1\).

The sum of the nonzero Apéry elements is \(p(p-1)+Lp(p-1)/2\). The standard residue-class gap count yields
\[
 g=\frac1p\sum_{w\in\operatorname{Ap}(H,p)}w-\frac{p-1}{2}
  =\frac{(p-1)(L+1)}2=\frac{p^{2n}-1}{4}.
\]
Consequently \(2g=(p-1)L+(p-1)=F+t\), the defining equality for an almost-symmetric numerical semigroup.

**4. Weight and all one-point dimensions.** For a numerical semigroup of multiplicity \(p\) with Apéry representatives \(w_r\) of residues \(r=0,\ldots,p-1\), summing the arithmetic progression of gaps in each residue gives
\[
 \sum_{\gamma\notin H,\,\gamma>0}\gamma
 =\frac{1}{2p}\sum_r w_r^2-\frac12\sum_r w_r+\frac{p^2-1}{12}.
\]
Substitute \(w_0=0\) and \(w_b=p+bL\) for \(1\le b<p\), and subtract \(g(g+1)/2\). Elementary simplification gives exactly \((p^2-1)(L^2-1)/24\). Finally, the functions \(U^j\Phi_b\) form a basis of the pole-bounded subspace, because they form a basis of \(A^G\) and have pairwise distinct pole orders. Counting those of order at most \(s\) gives the asserted formula for \(\ell(sP_\infty)\).

## Verification

The attached standalone Python program `verify_semigroup.py` enumerates invariant monomials in bounded \(x\)-degree, checks the complete residue-minimum Apéry profile, and independently enumerates gaps and pseudo-Frobenius numbers. It checks the genus, conductor, almost-symmetric identity, minimal generators and Weierstrass weight for \((p,n)=(3,2),(3,3),(5,2),(5,3),(7,2),(11,2)\). The printed terminating marker is `VERIFY_OK`. These checks corroborate the formulas in finite cases; the invariant-ring argument above proves the assertions for all allowed parameters.

For a small exact instance \((p,n)=(3,2)\), one has \(L=19\), \(g=20\), \(H(P_\infty)=\langle3,22,41\rangle\), conductor \(39\), and weight \(120\). For \((p,n)=(5,2)\), one gets \(L=77\), \(g=156\), conductor \(309\), type \(4\) and weight \(5928\).

## Relationship to prior work

Bartoli–Borges–Timpanella–Zini [1] construct these maximal curves, prove their absence of geometric automorphisms, and supply the scalar quotient and unique degree-\(p\) pencil. Those established results are not claimed here. The result above determines a distinct **pointwise** invariant: the entire Weierstrass gap semigroup and its weight, which is not determined by genus, maximality, the automorphism group, or gonality. Published work on Weierstrass semigroups of other maximal curves [2] and on tame Kummer covers [3] uses related techniques but concerns different geometric families; work on van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves [4] primarily treats \(L\)-polynomials and other quotient constructions. The numerical-semigroup consequences employ standard Apéry techniques [5], rather than claiming those general techniques to be new.

## Limitations

Only the unique point above \(u=\infty\) is classified; no claim is made about other Weierstrass points or the whole set of gap sequences. The computation is geometric over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\); it does not separately determine the dimensions of arbitrary multipoint spaces or the distance of AG codes. Explicit semigroup formulas can sometimes follow from broad theorems for cyclic covers; exhaustive specialist historical-priority review, including possible implicit coverage in general quotient literature, has not been performed. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
