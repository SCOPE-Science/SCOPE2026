# Scientific review of the icosidodecahedral graph-metric Rips classification

## Correctness — PASS

The graph is constructed by a completely specified \(\operatorname{GP}(10,2)\) adjacency rule and the line-graph operation. The proof independently covers six integer-radius stages using the exact shortest-path distance distribution, twenty disjoint star triangles at radius one, fifteen antipodal nonedges at radius four, and exhaustive acyclic Morse matchings at radii two and three. The finite verifier constructs all \(1022\) and \(15966\) nonempty faces; verifies each matched pair is a genuine codimension-one incidence; checks all \(3330\) and \(83730\) covering relations by topological sorting; and finds only one critical vertex and one critical two-simplex in each of the latter complexes. This proves their integral \(S^2\) homotopy types. For persistence, the explicitly triangulated closed connected orientable surface has a coherent fundamental two-cycle, and the exact integral cochain algorithm verifies the cocycle equations on every three-simplex. Unit pairings \(1\) and \(-1\) prove that the inclusion is an integral homology isomorphism. The claim does not infer an infinite theorem from finite experiments; it concerns this finite metric space.

## Originality — PASS on the inspected comparisons

M. Adamaszek, *Clique complexes and graph powers*, arXiv:1104.0433 (2011), defines the graph-power filtration and proves statements for ordinary line-graph clique complexes, but his Section 5 does not classify the scale-two and scale-three powers of the icosidodecahedral graph. N. Saleh, T. Titz Mite and S. Witzel, *Vietoris–Rips complexes of Platonic solids*, arXiv:2302.14388 (2023), give explicit classifications of the five Platonic-solid vertex sets and suggest Archimedean solids as further investigation; the graph and metric in this claim are outside their stated theorem. Targeted comparisons under graph-power, icosidodecahedron, rectified-dodecahedron and line-graph formulations identified nearby calculations of other polyhedra, dodecahedral \(\operatorname{GP}(10,2)\) complexes, and graph neighborhood/Čech objects, not an implication of this exact Rips persistence claim. Residual risk: unpublished, uncatalogued or differently named work could exist; no absolute publication-priority guarantee is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A paper that classifies Platonic-solid Rips complexes explicitly proposes the Archimedean case. The icosidodecahedron is a canonical, vertex-transitive Archimedean graph and also the line graph of the dodecahedron. Its graph-distance filtration contains a nonobvious two-sphere interval persisting unchanged across the distinct, high-dimensional face complexes at scales two and three, before a sharp jump to the antipodal \(S^{14}\). The integral inclusion degree is essential to distinguish one long persistence interval from two consecutive intervals. A complete exact filtration and persistence classification of this canonical object is mathematically motivated independent of a general family theorem.

## Limitations

The metric is the intrinsic unit-edge graph metric, not a Euclidean chordal or full polyhedral metric. The calculation is limited to this graph. Same-model computation and reasoning are not independent mathematical validation. There is no proof of unique publication priority or of an equivariant homotopy equivalence.
