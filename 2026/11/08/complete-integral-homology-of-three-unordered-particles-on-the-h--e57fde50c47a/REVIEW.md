The integral chain model is supported by the reduced Świątkowski quasi-isomorphism for graph configuration spaces [An, Drummond-Cole, Knudsen, arXiv:1708.02351]. All weight-three basis elements are enumerated; the complete signed matrices satisfy both integer chain identities. Unimodular unit-pivot reductions compute the three Smith invariant multisets as \(1^{1770}\), \(1^{4689},2\), and \(1^{2911}\), with all omitted entries zero. The only non-unit residual has proven rank one and content two; the top matrix reduces to zero after its unit pivots for two distinct pivot strategies. The degree-two homology embeds in a torsion-free cokernel, so there can be no hidden large-prime torsion. Verification code and reproduced output are supplied. No claim about unreconstructed topological structures is needed.

The missing large-prime torsion obstruction cannot be excluded by those fields. Ko–Park's published first-homology formula covers \(H_1\) only. Maciążek–Sawicki's generation theorem is not an integral Smith reduction and does not fix the subgroup relations, while planar-graph generation results exclude the nonplanar Heawood graph. The subsequent complete-bipartite-family torsion theorem of An does not apply as a classification for this graph. Record searches for exact groups, synonyms, field-to-integral lift and graph family coverage found no stronger statement. Global unindexed priority and unforeseen general implications remain risks; they have not been certified absent.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Fano incidence graph is a canonical trivalent nonplanar test case. Its three-point configuration space has high-rank second homology and known characteristic-two anomalies; this makes the exact integral extension a natural and non-obvious question. The new computation resolves whether the natural second homology has *any* torsion, a question left open by all previously computed coefficient fields, and completes the integral groups of this configuration space. This is motivated full natural-invariant classification, not a routine field conversion or an arbitrary parameter adjustment.
