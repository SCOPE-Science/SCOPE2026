# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact claim uses the ten two-subsets of a five-element set, disjointness adjacency, both orientations of each of its fifteen edges, and the augmented arc-set multipath complex. Two different exhaustive enumerators agree on all 59,203 faces. Both lexicographically opposite discrete-Morse matchings pass full Hasse-graph acyclicity, and their integer boundary matrices have unit pivots with zero remainders, yielding the same free integral Betti vector. The independent unreduced mod-two calculation confirms the Betti vector. The proof covers every face of this finite complex; it makes no inference to general graphs.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence

The closest foundational literature is Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith, arXiv:2208.04656, which develops multipath homotopy methods for different graph families; Caputi–Collari–Ramos, arXiv:2401.01248, whose directed-cycle-free matching comparison does not apply; and Caputi–Collari–Smith, arXiv:2601.05670, on bidirected polygons. The earliest path-poset construction, arXiv:2110.11206, provides definitions and general results but not this Petersen calculation. Text searches and section-by-section theorem inspection found no implication. Nearby computations on the same undirected graph concern distinct complexes, not the arc-set complex of vertex-disjoint directed paths. A computation outside indexed or accessible literature remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Petersen graph is the canonical ten-vertex cubic graph of girth five and is not Hamiltonian. Its multipath complex has 240 top-dimensional simplices but trivial degree-eight homology; its nonzero integral homology occurs in four consecutive lower degrees. The complete integral Betti vector, and the absence of torsion, provide a natural benchmark outside the path/polygon families for comparing topological behavior of directed-path complexes. This is a bounded complete invariant, not an arbitrary slice or a proposed infinite-family formula.

## Limits

No homotopy equivalence to a wedge of spheres is inferred from the integral homology. No claim of absolute bibliographic novelty or independently verified proof is made.
