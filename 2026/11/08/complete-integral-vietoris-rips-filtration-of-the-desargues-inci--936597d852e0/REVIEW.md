# Scientific review: Desargues incidence-graph Rips filtration

## Correctness — PASS
The object is the incidence graph of the two- and three-subsets of a five-element set. Pairwise distance formulas are checked against 190 independent breadth-first computations. Its 20 vertices, 30 edges, and graph diameter five give the graph-stage circle wedge, the last full simplex, and an antipodal perfect-matching complement at threshold four. The latter is the join of ten zero-spheres and hence \(S^9\). For thresholds two and three, the standalone verifier uses two complete face-set constructions that agree on all \(2^{20}\) potential subsets at each scale. It validates the complete directed Morse Hasse diagrams, including respectively 660 and 13,980 covering incidences, with a topological sort covering every nonempty face. The matching leaves exactly one critical vertex and nineteen two-cells at threshold two; one critical vertex and one three-cell at threshold three. Standard discrete Morse theory thus supplies integral homotopy equivalences. Mod-two matrix ranks are independent checks, not the basis of an integral conclusion.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
Adamaszek's 2011 graph-power paper proves a girth-at-least-seven scale-two stability theorem and exact results for cycle powers. The Desargues graph has girth six, so the girth theorem does not cover its threshold-two outcome and the cycle classification is inapplicable. The hypercube theorem treats an entire five-cube rather than its two middle layers, and higher homotopy of the subcomplex is not implied by the ambient result. Closest published incidence-graph Rips results concern finite projective or affine planes, not the Desargues configuration; the relevant structural incidence axioms fail. The published edge-disjoint-hexagon classification does not apply because twenty Desargues hexagons overlap, four at each edge. Other published results on the same Desargues graph concern Čech nerves, matching or independence complexes, not its graph-distance Rips filtration. General graph-power persistence bounds and bipartite-square fundamental-group results do not give all asserted integral homotopy types. Literature and research-record searches found no encompassing statement. An unindexed equivalent classification remains possible, and the full text of a general persistence-bounds article was unavailable during this review.

## Value — PASS
The Desargues graph is a canonical twenty-vertex cubic, distance-transitive, antipodal graph and the Levi graph of the \((10_3)\) Desargues configuration. A complete integral vertex-Rips filtration is a natural topological invariant for this specific graph. The precise transition through eleven circles, nineteen two-spheres, a three-sphere and a nine-sphere gives both the full integer persistence barcode and the topology of each stage. The two middle sphere classifications are not accessible from Euler characteristic or the elementary antipodal argument alone, while the interplay of a girth-six boundary and an antipodal high-dimensional boundary offers a motivated benchmark for graph-power topology. The result is a finite exact classification, not an unsupported all-graphs claim.

## Evidence and remaining limitations
The deterministic standard-library verifier and recorded output are included. They constitute a finite, exhaustive mathematical certificate for the only nontrivial matching assertions; no sampling or extrapolation is used. The article and comparison-source evidence do not prove universal publication priority. The theorem makes no statement about the geometric realization including interiors of graph edges, the automorphism representation, or higher middle-level hypercube graphs.
