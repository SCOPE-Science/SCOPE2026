## Correctness — PASS
The proof first supplies a uniform forward bound for the radius and the axial coordinate, then uses the exact meridional divergence and the three equilibrium types to exclude cycles and homoclinic graphics. A bounded planar omega-limit set must therefore be a single equilibrium. The exact phase identity, valid even on the axis through a Cartesian analytic transformation, gives the claimed asymptotic circle phase. Poincaré recurrence and uniqueness of Haar measure on a continuously rotated circle establish the full three-vertex invariant-probability simplex. The standalone verifier reproduces the critical symbolic reductions and numerical conjugacy comparisons. Global completeness is forward only, and no finite experiment is treated as a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
The closest primary work is Castañeda–Rebollo-Perdomo, arXiv:2610.09176v1, sections 1.2, 2, 3 and 4. Its proved statements give the Hurwitz vector field, the two circles, the meridional reduction and nonexistence of other **periodic** orbits; its basin discussion is a numerical illustration. It does not establish that all invariant probabilities are mixtures of exactly three ergodic laws, explicitly exclude nonperiodic recurrent stationary statistics, or identify a globally analytic time-preserving phase gauge. The new result invokes standard planar theorems but adds a global trapping argument and a stationary-measure exhaustion not supplied by the original periodic-orbit census. Related published mathematical records on phase conjugacy and stationary measures concern different vector fields, not this family. The all-initial-state convergence is consistent with the qualitative phase portrait presented by the source and is not advertised as a wholly unanticipated basin pattern.

The restriction to the natural negative-divergence parameter range and the absence of a higher-parameter classification are clearly stated.
