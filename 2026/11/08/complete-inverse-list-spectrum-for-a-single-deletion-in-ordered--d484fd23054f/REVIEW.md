# Scientific review: complete inverse-list spectrum for one overlapping-read deletion

**Correctness — PASS.** Each deleted read is a gap in a path of overlapping full \(\ell\)-mers. Such a gap can create at most one standard-overlap defect; a weakly compatible unique defect can be bridged in exactly one way. If there is no defect, boundary extensions contribute \(2q\) source candidates, with the one constant-source collision, and eligible internal duplications are indexed by long strictly internal constant runs. Counting nonconstant length-\(\ell\) blocks gives the exact number of defective observations. Run-composition generating functions then enumerate the complete compatible inverse-list spectrum. The independent brute-force checker agreed for 49 small-parameter combinations, covering 343,337 feasible read vectors and 631,205 distinct source/output incidences. Exhaustive finite tests corroborate, but the written all-parameter proof is essential.

**Originality — PASS against available evidence, with a material limitation.** Ye and Ge's June 2026 paper uses the same ordered \(\ell\)-mer channel and develops structural deletion decoding and redundancy bounds, so it is the closest comparison. The accessible introductory material does not state the exact all-parameter inverse-degree law, feasible-output count, maximum, or rational distribution; the rest of its manuscript was not completely accessible and may contain an equivalent implicit argument. The Hamming-weight readout of Banerjee et al., the Hamming-radius list decoding of symbol-pair codes, and the earlier two-*trace* ordinary-deletion reconstruction graph are not the same inverse-channel statistic. Search absence is not regarded as a novelty proof. This PASS is a best-knowledge overlap assessment with the explicit access and priority risks recorded in the structured audit, not an assertion of definitive historical uniqueness.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A one-deletion readout can have many candidate original sequences even without read substitution noise. The complete list-size law determines feasibility, every possible exact inverse-list count, and the sharp alphabet- and read-width-dependent worst case, answering a natural decoding-ambiguity question across all source lengths rather than for a short isolated census. It gives exact inputs for adversarial list-decoding guarantees and finite-length ambiguity enumerators. It does not establish new code-rate bounds or extend to multiple read deletions.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Ye–Ge, arXiv:2606.26434v1; Banerjee–Yehezkeally–Wachter-Zeh–Yaakobi, *Correcting a Single Deletion in Reads from a Nanopore Sequencer*; Liu–Xing–Yuan, arXiv:1806.08992. Only the first defines the identical channel, and the principal residual risk is incomplete inspection of its detailed body. Scientific validation by an independent party has not been performed.
