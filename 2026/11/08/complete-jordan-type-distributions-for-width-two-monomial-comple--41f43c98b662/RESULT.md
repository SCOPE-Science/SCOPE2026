# Complete Jordan-type distributions for width-two monomial complete intersections

## Finding
Let \(m\ge2\), let \(k\) be an arbitrary field, and let
\[
A_m=k[x,y]/(x^2,y^m).
\]
Every nilpotent element of this local algebra is in its maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(x,y)\). For \(f\in\mathfrak m\), denote by \(M_f\) multiplication by \(f\) on the \(2m\)-dimensional \(k\)-vector space \(A_m\).

**Complete power-rank theorem.** Write uniquely \(f=a(y)+xb(y)\), where \(a\in yk[y]/(y^m)\) and \(b\in k[y]/(y^m)\). Set
\[
u=\operatorname{ord}_y(a)\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\qquad
v=\operatorname{ord}_y(b)\in\{0,\ldots,m\},
\]
with valuation \(m\) assigned to the zero polynomial. For each integer \(t\ge1\), define
\[
\alpha_t=tu,\qquad
\beta_t=\begin{cases}(t-1)u+v&\text{if }t\cdot1_k\ne0,\\+\infty&\text{if }t\cdot1_k=0,\end{cases}
\]
\[
d_t=\min(\alpha_t,\beta_t),\qquad e_t=2\alpha_t-d_t.
\]
Then the exact power-rank sequence is
\[
\boxed{R_0=2m,\quad R_t=\operatorname{rank}_k(M_f^t)
=\max\{m-d_t,0\}+\max\{m-e_t,0\}\quad(t\ge1).}
\]
In particular \(R_t=0\) for \(t\ge m+1\). The number of Jordan blocks of size exactly \(t\), for every \(1\le t\le m+1\), is
\[
\boxed{b_t=R_{t-1}-2R_t+R_{t+1}.}
\]
Consequently the full nilpotent Jordan partition, denoted \(\lambda_{m,p}(u,v)\) with \(p=\operatorname{char}k\), is determined entirely by the two lowest nonzero \(y\)-degrees of \(a\) and \(b\), even for inhomogeneous \(f\) and in every characteristic.

**Complete finite-field distribution.** For \(k=\mathbb F_q\) of characteristic \(p\), set
\[
w_m(s)=\begin{cases}(q-1)q^{m-s-1}&0\le s<m,\\1&s=m.\end{cases}
\]
Using independent formal variables \(Z_\lambda\), one for each partition \(\lambda\) of \(2m\), the exact Jordan-type enumerator of all elements of \(\mathfrak m\) is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J_{m,q}=\sum_{u=1}^m\sum_{v=0}^m
w_m(u)w_m(v)Z_{\lambda_{m,p}(u,v)}.}
\]
Thus the coefficient of \(Z_\lambda\) is the number of **individual labeled elements** whose multiplication operator has partition \(\lambda\), not the number of ideals or automorphism orbits. The sum of the coefficients is \(q^{2m-1}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The quotient is a commutative, unital, width-two monomial complete intersection, not a general two-generator local algebra. The parameter \(m\) is at least two. The field may have characteristic zero or positive characteristic. An element is nilpotent precisely when its constant coefficient is zero. The zero element has \(u=v=m\) and Jordan partition \((1,\ldots,1)\) with \(2m\) parts. The convention \(+\infty\) is used only when the scalar \(t\) vanishes in \(k\); then \(d_t=e_t=tu\). A zero polynomial has the truncated valuation \(m\), not infinite valuation, because all formulas compute ranks modulo \(y^m\).

## Proof
Put \(R=k[y]/(y^m)\). As an \(R\)-module, \(A_m=R\oplus xR\), and multiplication by \(f=a+xb\) has matrix
\[
M_f=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\b&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The two matrix summands commute, and \(x^2=0\). Therefore, without dividing by any factorial and over every field,
\[
M_f^t=\begin{pmatrix}a^t&0\\t a^{t-1}b&a^t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Lift \(a,b\) from \(R\) to the discrete valuation ring \(k[[y]]\), taking a lift of valuation \(u\) for \(a\), and of valuation \(v\) for \(b\). For a zero polynomial one may use the lift \(y^m\); this does not change the reduction modulo \(y^m\). The resulting \(2\times2\) matrix over \(k[[y]]\) has determinant \(a^{2t}\), of valuation \(2tu\). Its ideal of entries is generated by a uniformizer power with exponent
\[
\min\{tu,(t-1)u+v\}=d_t
\]
when \(t\cdot1_k\ne0\), and exponent \(tu\) when \(t\cdot1_k=0\). These two ideals determine the Smith exponents \(d_t\) and \(2tu-d_t=e_t\). Indeed, elementary row/column operations invertible over the DVR diagonalize the matrix to \(\operatorname{diag}(y^{d_t},y^{e_t})\), up to units. The operations remain invertible after reduction modulo \(y^m\).

Multiplication by \(y^s\) on \(R\) has \(k\)-rank \(\max\{m-s,0\}\), so diagonal reduction proves the displayed formula for \(R_t\). Since \(f^{m+1}=0\) in \(A_m\), all Jordan blocks have size at most \(m+1\). For any nilpotent operator, the difference \(R_{t-1}-R_t\) is the number of blocks of size at least \(t\); a second difference gives exactly \(b_t\), proving the partition formula with all boundary cases included.

For \(0\le s<m\), there are exactly \((q-1)q^{m-s-1}\) polynomials in \(k[y]/(y^m)\) with valuation \(s\), and one zero polynomial with valuation \(m\). The coefficient restrictions on \(a\) amount to \(u\ge1\); the two polynomial choices are independent. Summing the weights over \(u=1,\ldots,m\) and \(v=0,\ldots,m\) gives \(q^{m-1}q^m=q^{2m-1}\). Each summand is assigned its Jordan partition by the previously proved rank formula, completing the exact finite-field distribution.

## Verification
The accompanying dependency-free script `verify.py` independently builds the multiplication matrix directly from the monomial multiplication table in the basis \(1,y,\ldots,y^{m-1},x,xy,\ldots,xy^{m-1}\), computes all power ranks by finite-field Gaussian elimination, and compares their resulting Jordan partitions with the valuation-and-Smith formula for **every** nilpotent element in nine bounded examples:
\[
(m,q)\in\{(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(3,4),(4,2),(4,3),(5,2)\}.
\]
Arithmetic for \(q=4\) is performed in \(\mathbb F_2[z]/(z^2+z+1)\), not by modular arithmetic modulo four. The test output ends with `WIDTH_TWO_JORDAN_SPECTRUM_DIRECT_CHECK_OK`; the independent direct enumeration agrees with the exact distribution in all tested cases. Finite checks corroborate, but do not prove, the arbitrary-field theorem. Its proof is the Smith-normal-form and counting argument above.

For illustration, over \(\mathbb F_3\) with \(m=4\) there are \(3^7=2187\) nilpotent elements distributed over nine Jordan partitions. Among these, 972 have partition \((5,3)\) and 486 have partition \((4,4)\). Over \(\mathbb F_2\) with \(m=4\), 64 of the 128 elements have partition \((4,4)\); the full frequencies are recorded in the direct checker output.

## Relationship to prior work
Basili and Iarrobino [1, Theorem 2.16 and Example 2.18] study Jordan partitions of generic **linear pencils** in Artinian two-variable algebras and explicitly discuss the identical complete intersection \(k[x,y]/(x^m,y^2)\), including the characteristic-dependent partition of a generic linear form. That linear-pencil phenomenon, including the familiar \((m+1,m-1)\) versus \((m,m)\) transition when the characteristic divides \(m\), is **prior work**, not a new result here. The present theorem instead gives the *entire* Jordan partition for every nilpotent, potentially inhomogeneous element, with an exact finite-field multiplicity for each partition at all \(m\) and all field orders. Cook [2] addresses strong Lefschetz phenomena for two-variable monomial complete intersections, and Iarrobino, Macias Marques, and McDaniel [3] give a broad Jordan-type framework; their inspected results do not explicitly give this complete all-element finite-field census.

A previously published index result [4] gives the nilpotency-index distribution of arbitrary dual-number lifts \(J_m+\varepsilon B\), with \(B\) an arbitrary matrix. Although its operator matrices are superficially similar to the present block form, its stated invariant is the *index* of a matrix over a dual-number ring, and its parameter space is an arbitrary matrix fiber, not the algebra's nilpotent multiplier locus. It does not state this joint Jordan-block distribution. Standard Smith theory can of course be used to obtain the rank steps, so unlocated earlier applications remain a legitimate priority risk.

## Limitations
The full formula is for nilpotent multiplication operators in \(A_m=k[x,y]/(x^2,y^m)\), not for arbitrary two-variable complete intersections or arbitrary local Artinian algebras. The enumerator counts elements, not conjugacy classes or principal ideals. It is a theorem about the linear Jordan partition; it does not determine additional gradings or scheme structures on Jordan-type loci. Independent validation and exhaustive historical-priority analysis have not been performed; in particular, existing general Smith-normal-form machinery might contain an equivalent all-element specialization.
