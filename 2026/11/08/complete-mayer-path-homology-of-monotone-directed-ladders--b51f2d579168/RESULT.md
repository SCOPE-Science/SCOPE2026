# Complete Mayer path homology of monotone directed ladders

## Finding

Let \(L_m\), for an integer \(m\ge 2\), be the directed ladder with vertices \(b_0,\ldots,b_{m-1}\) and \(t_0,\ldots,t_{m-1}\), horizontal arrows \(b_i\to b_{i+1}\) and \(t_i\to t_{i+1}\) for \(0\le i<m-1\), and vertical arrows \(b_i\to t_i\) for \(0\le i<m\). For the unreduced, nonregular complex Mayer path homology of Karaguler--Wei, at every primitive \(N\)-th root \(\xi\in\mathbb C\), every integer \(N\ge2\), and every \(1\le q<N\), all homology in degree at least three vanishes.

For \(N=2\) (necessarily \(q=1\)), the Betti numbers in degrees zero, one, and two are \((1,0,0)\). For \(N\ge3\), putting \(\beta_k^{N,q}=\dim_{\mathbb C}H_k^{N,q}(L_m;\mathbb C)\), the **complete** Betti numbers are

\[
\beta_0^{N,q}=\begin{cases}2m&1\le q\le N-3,\\m+1&q=N-2,\\1&q=N-1,\end{cases}\qquad
\beta_1^{N,q}=\begin{cases}m-1&q=1,\\3m-2&2\le q\le N-2,\\2m-1&q=N-1,\end{cases}
\]
\[
\beta_2^{N,q}=\begin{cases}0&q=1,2,\\m-1&3\le q\le N-1.\end{cases}
\]
Empty cases in these ranges are ignored. Thus degree-two Mayer classes appear precisely for \(N\ge4\) and \(3\le q<N\); their rank counts the \(m-1\) directed squares. For example, the cubic-root cases are \((\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2)=(m+1,m-1,0)\) for \(q=1\) and \((1,2m-1,0)\) for \(q=2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ladder arrows are exactly those displayed above: in particular, there are **no** shortcuts, diagonals, or reverse arrows. Let \(A_k\) be the complex vector space generated by allowed directed paths of length \(k\), and set \(A_k=0\) for \(k<0\). The nonregular root-of-unity path boundary on an allowed path is \(\partial e_{v_0\ldots v_k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\xi^j e_{v_0\ldots\widehat v_j\ldots v_k}\) in positive degree, with zero boundary from degree zero. A deleted path not in \(A_{k-1}\) remains a formal ambient path for testing invariance, and is **not** silently projected away.

The invariant subspace \(\Omega_k^N\) consists of allowed-path chains whose boundary powers \(\partial^j\) are allowed for every \(1\le j<N\). The homology is \(H_k^{N,q}=\ker(\partial^q:\Omega_k^N\to\Omega_{k-q}^N)/\operatorname{im}(\partial^{N-q}:\Omega_{k+N-q}^N\to\Omega_k^N)\), with all negative-degree groups zero. This is the unreduced convention used in the motivating theory; it is not the regular quotient boundary and not ordinary graph-cycle homology.

## Proof

**Invariant chain groups.** Write \(S_i=e_{b_i b_{i+1}t_{i+1}}-e_{b_i t_i t_{i+1}}\) for \(0\le i<m-1\). Every allowed path moves to the right horizontally and can move up at most once. In degree two, any path with two horizontal steps has a forbidden two-step horizontal shortcut upon deleting the middle vertex, and hence coefficient zero in any invariant combination. The remaining nonzero allowed two-paths travel across one elementary square, in one of its two two-step routes. Both have the same missing diagonal \(b_i\to t_{i+1}\), with coefficient \(\xi\); cancellation forces their coefficients to sum to zero. Each such diagonal belongs to exactly one square. Hence \(\Omega_2^{N,1}=\operatorname{span}\{S_0,\ldots,S_{m-2}\}\). Deleting a vertex from each \(S_i\) leaves only allowed arrows, so every boundary power of \(S_i\) remains allowed. It follows that \(\Omega_2^N=\operatorname{span}\{S_i\}\), of rank \(m-1\). Also \(\Omega_0^N=A_0\) and \(\Omega_1^N=A_1\), of respective dimensions \(2m\) and \(3m-2\).

For degree \(k\ge3\), a path whose first two arrows are horizontal has coefficient zero in every \(\Omega_k^{N,1}\)-chain: deleting the vertex at position one produces a forbidden, first-position length-two horizontal jump. Such a forbidden face cannot be obtained by deleting a different position, and determines the original path uniquely. The analogous argument at the final two arrows kills paths ending in two horizontal arrows. Since there is at most one vertical arrow, every path of length at least four has one of these horizontal pairs. A surviving path of length three, if any, must be of type horizontal--vertical--horizontal. Deleting its vertex at position one creates a forbidden first-position diagonal. The only other allowable preimage of this forbidden face begins vertical--horizontal--horizontal and has already been killed by the final-horizontal-pair argument. Consequently its coefficient also vanishes. Thus \(\Omega_k^{N,1}=0\) for all \(k\ge3\), and a fortiori \(\Omega_k^N=0\) for all \(k\ge3\). This is an exhaustive argument, not a finite-sample inference.

**Boundary ranks.** In the oriented-arrow basis \(a_i=e_{b_i b_{i+1}}\), \(u_i=e_{t_i t_{i+1}}\), and \(c_i=e_{b_i t_i}\), direct deletion gives
\[
\partial S_i=c_{i+1}-u_i+\xi^2(a_i-c_i),\qquad
\partial^2 S_i=(\xi+\xi^2)(e_{b_{i+1}}-e_{t_i}).
\]
The \(m-1\) columns of \(\partial:\Omega_2^N\to\Omega_1^N\) are independent, since each contains the distinct top arrow \(u_i\) with coefficient \(-1\). For every \(N\ge3\), the scalar \(\xi+\xi^2\) is nonzero, and the \(m-1\) displayed two-step boundaries have disjoint vertex supports; therefore \(\operatorname{rank}(\partial^2|_{\Omega_2^N})=m-1\). When \(N=2\), \(\xi=-1\), so this composite vanishes as required.

On an arrow \(e_{v w}\), the first boundary is \(e_w+\xi e_v\). The left nullspace of the degree-one boundary consists of functions \(\lambda\) satisfying \(\lambda(w)=-\xi\lambda(v)\) on each arrow. The ladder is connected, and its commuting squares impose consistent transport, so this nullspace is exactly one-dimensional; explicitly \(\lambda(b_i)=(-\xi)^i\lambda(b_0)\) and \(\lambda(t_i)=(-\xi)^{i+1}\lambda(b_0)\). Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(\partial|_{\Omega_1^N})=2m-1\).

**Mayer groups.** All higher differentials and all third powers on invariant chains vanish because \(\Omega_k^N=0\) for \(k\ge3\). The only possible nonzero boundary ranks are therefore \(r_1=2m-1\) from degree one, \(r_2=m-1\) from degree two, and \(r_{12}=0\) for \(N=2\) or \(m-1\) for \(N\ge3\) from the square of the boundary. For each permitted \(q\), substitute these ranks into
\[
\dim H_k^{N,q}=\dim\Omega_k^N-\operatorname{rank}(\partial^q|_{\Omega_k^N})-\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{N-q}|_{\Omega_{k+N-q}^N}).
\]
This gives exactly the stated piecewise formulas, including the boundary cases \(N=2\) and \(N=3\). Every chain group above degree two is zero, completing the proof.

The verifier constructs *all* allowed directed paths for each ladder with \(2\le m\le8\), forms the forbidden-face matrix without assuming the invariant generators, and independently computes exact ranks over finite fields carrying roots of orders \(N\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}\). ## Relationship to prior work

Karaguler and Wei introduced Mayer path homology, established the invariant path-chain definitions and generator classifications for degrees two and three, and calculated several small digraph motifs, including a **single biparallel square** at \(N=3\). Their Example 3.4 agrees with the present formulas at \(m=2,N=3\); that small motif calculation is explicitly prior work. The present theorem adds the first closed calculation for the arbitrary-length monotone ladder and every \(N,q\), including its higher-degree vanishing proof, explicit boundary-power ranks, and positive second homology for \(N\ge4\). Their general generator and cycle classifications provide ingredients, but do not state or by themselves determine the exact family-wide Mayer groups. Li and Shen describe degree-three generators for the ordinary alternating-sign differential; that setting does not determine these general-root homology ranks.

The closest indexed result on *complete transitively layered digraphs* assumes every vertex in an earlier level points to every vertex in every later level. The ladder is sparse and lacks those arrows, so its universal augmentation-block result does not imply the present formulas. Other nearby results on cubical ladder homology and ladder Morse complexes use different chain functors. Source and semantic overlap searches found no exact formula for this directed-ladder family, but lack of an indexed match does not establish priority.

## Limitations

The classification requires the exact monotone orientation with vertical arrows all pointing upward, no shortcut arrows, complex coefficients, a primitive root of unity, and unreduced nonregular Mayer path homology. It does not classify arbitrary ladder orientations, multiple rows, or persistent variants. Published motif calculations already include the one-square case for \(N=3\). No independent validation is claimed.
