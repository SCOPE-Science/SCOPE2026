# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The displayed algebra has a two-dimensional square and trivial annihilator. The proof derives every possible natural basis from the two independent multiplication coefficients and real positive-definite orthogonality; its graph must be a join of a clique of size at least two with an independent set. Real orthogonal transitivity realizes every graph. The exact Cheeger expression follows from a cut count and a size-increasing argument that preserves or decreases the boundary ratio, including even/odd endpoints. An integer-basis checker independently reproduces all edge supports and all graph cuts for dimensions 3–10. General validity rests on proof, not tests.

## Originality — qualified PASS
Giacomelli's May 2026 paper, especially Theorem 3.8 and Problem 8.3, covers nonsingular basis invariance and explicitly leaves the singular case open. Elduque–Labra give earlier small basis-dependence examples and connectivity results, but not the exact rank-two complete-split graph and Cheeger spectrum. The October 2026 survey discusses natural-basis classification as an open theme. Earlier rank-one graph-support results do not entail the new rank-two quantitative classification. Search coverage has limits; a general low-dimensional basis-change theorem might simplify cases, and historical priority cannot be certified by negative searches.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem gives an exact answer for a natural nontrivial rank-two, annihilator-free family motivated by an explicit recent quantitative open question, rather than only demonstrating a basis anomaly. It classifies all obtainable graphs, computes every attainable numerical Cheeger constant, and proves sharp parity-dependent minima and an unbounded expansion ratio. These distinctions survive connectedness, which itself is basis-independent in the annihilator-free setting.
