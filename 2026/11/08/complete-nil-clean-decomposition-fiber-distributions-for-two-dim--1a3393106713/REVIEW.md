# Scientific self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The trace of an idempotent equals its rank even in characteristic two. For a prescribed trace-one target, complementary image/kernel lines parameterize rank-one idempotents, and the determinant of the residual matrix imposes one prescribed diagonal entry. Non-eigenlines each determine one complement, eigenlines of the required eigenvalue determine \(q\), and all other eigenlines determine none. The characteristic polynomial at \(1-\det(A)\) evaluates to \(\det(A)^2\), producing the exceptional rank-one-idempotent class. Centralizer sizes and the split/irreducible discriminant census yield the complete histograms, with coincident labels added at \(q=2\). Exhaustive independent matrix-pair counts for \(q=2,3,4,5,7\) passed, including a nonprime field. Finite computations are corroborative, not proofs for all fields.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)

The natural decomposition-count invariant is the nil-clean index of an element. The 2013 field-matrix theorem gives existence, the 2014 paper supplies the general index framework and binary index, and the 2017 paper treats the different weakly nil-clean maximum in size two over \(\mathbb F_3\). Their inspected arguments and statements do not directly provide this complete all-field histogram. The 2019 work treats integral matrix Diophantine problems, not finite-field fibers. Published results on idempotent–nilpotent multiplication and rank-one-idempotent commutators do not imply additive decomposition fibers. Relevant exact searches located no equivalent distribution. The abstract of a 2018 dissertation addresses indices for matrix rings over residue rings; its inaccessible full text remains a specific unresolved literature risk, particularly for the maximum or prime-field cases. A failed search does not prove uniqueness, and independent historical-priority checking is not claimed.

## Value — PASS

Decomposition nonuniqueness is a named ring-theoretic invariant, not an arbitrary slice. The exact maximum alone hides all intermediate multiplicities. The theorem determines **every** fiber over **every** finite field, quantifies the nil-clean locus and its density, and explains the separate characteristic-two behavior with a line-incidence argument. The full histogram contains the arithmetic distinction between split, nonsplit, repeated-root and singular idempotent strata. It therefore provides a useful exact baseline for the nil-clean-index program and for higher-dimensional finite-field enumeration, not a recomputation of the known existence criterion.
