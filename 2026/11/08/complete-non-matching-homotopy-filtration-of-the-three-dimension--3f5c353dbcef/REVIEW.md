# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness

The cube graph has twelve edges, and each subset is a face of the non-matching complex at threshold k exactly when its maximum matching has fewer than k edges. Independently reconstructed exhaustive matching-number values give 45, 601, and 2,665 augmented faces at k=2,3,4. The specified pivot matchings have, on the ordinary nonempty face posets, 19, 295, and 1,331 pairs; exact directed-cover acyclicity and the residual critical cells give one zero-cell together with respectively five one-cells, nine three-cells, and one five-cell. Forman's discrete-Morse theorem gives integral wedges of spheres, not merely homology-group ranks. The initial empty-face-only stage and the final full-simplex stages follow directly from the cube's maximum matching number four. The positive-degree homology degrees differ at adjacent stages, giving the stated finite barcode.

## Relationship to earlier mathematics

Linusson–Shareshian–Welker prove the complete bipartite non-matching complex theorem, with multiplicities (9,9,1) for K4,4; Q3 is K4,4 with a perfect matching deleted and its first rank differs. Holmsen–Lee prove field-homological vanishing bounds for all bipartite graphs, not exact cube ranks or integral attaching maps. Shan–Lu–Wang give edge-addition injectivity and top-degree nonvanishing for graphs containing sufficiently long cycles; this partially yields the k=4 top field rank, not the full cube homotopy filtration. The k=2 rank five also follows from the standard nerve lemma on the eight vertex-star simplices. Petersen non-matching complexes, Heawood complexes, square-bouquet non-matching complexes, and degree-constrained two-matching complexes concern different graph families or different face predicates. The detailed published results inspected do not imply the remaining complete Q3 classification. Equivalent unindexed work may exist.

## Mathematical interest and limits

The non-matching filtration of the canonical three-cube is a natural finite topological invariant. Complete integral types in all nontrivial degrees, including the nine three-spheres at k=3, identify which behavior survives deleting one perfect matching from K4,4. The result is a graph-specific finite classification with executable complete matching certificates, not an asserted theorem for arbitrary crowns, higher cubes, or graph automorphisms. These computations do not constitute formal or expert external attestation.
