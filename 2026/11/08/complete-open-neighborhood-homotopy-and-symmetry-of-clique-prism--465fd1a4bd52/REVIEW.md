# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The defining open neighborhoods give two simplex-boundary pieces with private apex cones. Their intersection is precisely the connected crown graph, whose cycle rank is \(n^2-3n+1\). The homotopy-pushout argument for \(n\ge4\) proves the exact wedge decomposition over the integers; Mayer–Vietoris and oriented incidence chains prove the integral modules and group-character formulas for \(n\ge5\). The standalone verifier reconstructed all simplices and mod-two boundary ranks for \(n=4,5,6,7,8\) and checked all 1,680 signed equivariant Lefschetz identities for \(n=5,6\). Finite computations are a check, not the general proof.

## Originality
PASS on inspected evidence, with priority uncertainty. Kahle's classical neighborhood-complex work and Matsushita's fundamental-group theory do not determine the present homotopy. Shukla's degree-limited classification cannot cover \(n\ge5\); Matsushita's same-graph rook result treats **closed** neighborhoods. The published open rook higher-homology theorem requires both dimensions at least three, excluding this two-row family; the published clique–cycle theorem assumes a genuine cycle factor of length at least five. The related Kneser symmetric-group character does not give the graph-specific homotopy, layer-swap action or quotient theorem. Equivalent unindexed work is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The two-row boundary of a standard graph family has a complete all-parameter, mixed-dimensional integral homotopy classification. The exact symmetry module and rationally acyclic quotient are structural additional results that cannot be inferred from Betti numbers. They identify a natural degree-two cycle lattice and its nontrivial transpose action rather than an arbitrary small computation.
