# Complete orbit census for two-odd-prime cyclotomic recurrences

## Finding
For distinct odd primes \(p<q\) and every integer \(m\ge1\), let \(C\) be the companion matrix of the cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_{pq}(X)\), acting modulo \(m\) on a space of dimension \(d=(p-1)(q-1)\). With \(g_p=\gcd(m,p)\) and \(g_q=\gcd(m,q)\), the number \(N_t\) of distinct cycles of exact length \(t\) is
\[
N_1=1,\qquad N_p=\frac{g_q^{p-1}-1}{p},\qquad
N_q=\frac{g_p^{q-1}-1}{q},\qquad
N_{pq}=\frac{m^d-g_q^{p-1}-g_p^{q-1}+1}{pq}.
\]
There are no cycles of any other lengths. Moreover the Smith normal form over \(\mathbb Z\) of \(C^p-I\) has precisely \(p-1\) nonunit entries, all equal to \(q\); the Smith form of \(C^q-I\) has precisely \(q-1\) nonunit entries, all equal to \(p\).

## Assumptions and scope
The parameters \(p<q\) are **distinct odd primes** and \(m\ge1\). Every initial state in \((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^d\) is counted, including zero. The companion convention is the usual shift of a length-\(d\) block under the monic integer recurrence with characteristic polynomial \(\Phi_{pq}\); transposing the companion has identical Smith forms and orbit census. At modulus \(m=1\), the unique state is fixed and the formulas give \(N_1=1\). The theorem does not assert a formula for composite indices with three or more different prime factors.

## Proof
**1. General fixed-point conversion.** Put \(R=\mathbb Z[X]/(\Phi_{pq}(X))\). As an abelian group \(R\) is free of rank \(d\). Multiplication by \(X\) is represented by an integral matrix conjugate to the chosen companion convention (or its transpose, which has the same Smith form). For any positive \(t\), the number of fixed states of \(C^t\) modulo \(m\) equals the product of \(\gcd(m,s_i)\) over the nonzero Smith entries \(s_i\) of \(C^t-I\), times the usual free factors when present. The cokernel of multiplication by \(X^t-1\) is
\[
R/(X^t-1)=\mathbb Z[X]/(\Phi_{pq}(X),X^t-1).
\]

**2. Period restriction and the fixed state.** The identity \(X^{pq}-1\equiv0\pmod{\Phi_{pq}(X)}\) gives \(C^{pq}=I\). Thus only periods \(1,p,q,pq\) can occur. Furthermore \(\Phi_{pq}(1)=1\), so multiplication by \(X-1\) is unimodular and there is just one fixed state modulo every \(m\): \(F_1=1\).

**3. Smith invariant factors at \(p\).** The standard cyclotomic factorization for distinct primes is
\[
\Phi_{pq}(X)\Phi_q(X)=\Phi_q(X^p)=1+X^p+\cdots+X^{(q-1)p}.
\]
Modulo \(X^p-1\), the right side is \(q\). Consequently the abelian group \(G_p=\mathbb Z[X]/(\Phi_{pq},X^p-1)\) is annihilated by \(q\). To identify it completely, reduce modulo \(q\). In characteristic \(q\) the other cyclotomic factorization yields
\[
\Phi_{pq}(X)=\frac{\Phi_p(X^q)}{\Phi_p(X)}\equiv\Phi_p(X)^{q-1}\pmod q.
\]
Since \(X^p-1=(X-1)\Phi_p(X)\) and \(\Phi_p(1)=p\ne0\pmod q\), the ideal generated by \(X^p-1\) and \(\Phi_p(X)^{q-1}\) in \(\mathbb F_q[X]\) is exactly \((\Phi_p(X))\). Therefore
\[
G_p\cong\mathbb F_q[X]/(\Phi_p(X))\cong(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^{p-1}
\]
as abelian groups. The rational map \(C^p-I\) has no kernel, because primitive \(pq\)-th roots are not \(p\)-th roots. Its integer Smith form therefore consists of \(p-1\) entries \(q\) and \(d-(p-1)\) entries \(1\). Reducing modulo \(m\) yields \(F_p=g_q^{p-1}\).

**4. Smith factors at \(q\).** Interchanging \(p\) and \(q\) in the same argument gives
\[
G_q=\mathbb Z[X]/(\Phi_{pq},X^q-1)\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{q-1},
\]
so \(F_q=g_p^{q-1}\). The full-period fixed count is \(F_{pq}=m^d\).

**5. Exact cycles.** Every length-\(p\) cycle contributes \(p\) points fixed by \(C^p\) but not \(C\); thus \(pN_p=F_p-F_1\). Similarly \(qN_q=F_q-F_1\). Inclusion-exclusion on the two proper-period sets gives \(pqN_{pq}=F_{pq}-F_p-F_q+F_1\). Substituting the explicit fixed counts proves all four formulas and exhaustiveness. The computations are integer counts because they count genuine cycles.

In particular no experiment can establish the all-primes all-moduli assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Pudelko (2025), Section 2, studies periodicity of modular recurrences with various characteristic polynomials and proposes precise cyclotomic prime and prime-power period-count formulas. The previously obtained complete prime-power-index cyclotomic recurrence classification has the same general state-space model, but does **not** cover indices with two distinct odd prime factors. The present argument computes the two distinct torsion cokernels for the first squarefree composite type and gives all four orbit lengths for every modulus. General periodic-orbit inversion principles alone do not supply these integer Smith invariants. A literature and mathematical-record search found no statement implying this entire semiprime-index formula; absence from an index is not a proof of historical priority.

## Limitations
The conclusion is confined to distinct odd prime factors of the cyclotomic index. It does not classify indices with more prime factors or repeated factors. The finite verifier does not prove the infinite result. Earlier unindexed formulas or equivalent integral-cokernel calculations remain a potential historical-priority risk. No independent review or independent validation has been performed.
