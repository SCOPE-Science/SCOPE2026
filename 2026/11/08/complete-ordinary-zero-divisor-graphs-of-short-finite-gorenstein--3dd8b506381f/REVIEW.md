# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The algebraic hypotheses force a one-dimensional square of the maximal ideal equal to the socle. Multiplication gives a nondegenerate rank-two symmetric form on the two-dimensional radical quotient. The ordinary zero-divisor graph decomposes by projective orthogonality: universal nonzero socle, self-perpendicular lines giving full cliques, and paired nonisotropic lines giving complete bicliques. Field-characteristic cases and the self-pairing issue for distinct vertices were explicitly checked. Counts, chromatic polynomial, group decomposition, and spectral factorization follow by counting invariant graph components. Direct finite-field multiplication verification includes every binary and odd-characteristic form type for six finite-field orders; separate mixed-characteristic, direct coloring, and exact characteristic-polynomial checks passed. The proof covers all prime powers and both characteristics; the computations are corroborating evidence only.

## Originality — PASS, with substantial prior structural input

Kadu (2021, Proposition 3.6) **already proves the core multiplication/orthogonality correspondence** and the nondegeneracy criterion; these are not original. Kadu and Tanpure (2026) prove comparisons among Gorenstein-ring graph compressions, but do not classify the *ordinary* zero-divisor graphs within Hilbert function (1,2,1). The new stated object is the complete ordinary graph isomorphism type and its exact chromatic polynomial, full automorphism group, characteristic polynomial, and mixed-characteristic non-reconstruction example. Source and mathematical-record searches did not recover that complete classification. This is an explicit structural refinement, not a new bilinear-form existence theorem. The refinement follows by further finite-projective orthogonality analysis; historical novelty and whether a broader general graph theorem already implies it require independent expert verification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The classification gives the full unlabeled graph type of a natural nonprincipal Artinian Gorenstein family, not just a single numeric graph parameter. It decides precisely which members have isomorphic ordinary graphs, gives tractable whole-family coloring and spectra, and provides nonisomorphic equal-graph examples even across ring characteristic. It also sharply contrasts ordinary and compressed graphs in an area where published work focuses heavily on compression. The formula is elementary once the known bilinear correspondence is available; the mathematical value lies in the simultaneous complete graph classification and explicit invariants, not in novelty of the multiplication lemma.
