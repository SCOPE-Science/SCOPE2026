# Complete \(P_3\)-convexity polynomial of cycle powers

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(n\ge3\) and let \(r\ge2\) be integers. The \(r\)-th power \(G=C_n^r\) of the labelled \(n\)-cycle has vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with distinct vertices joined when their ordinary cyclic distance is at most \(r\). A vertex set \(S\) is \(P_3\)-convex when every vertex outside \(S\) has at most one neighbour in \(S\). Then

\[
 S\text{ is }P_3\text{-convex}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S=V(G)\text{ or }\bigl(d_{C_n}(u,v)\ge2r+1\text{ for all distinct }u,v\in S\bigr).
\]

In particular, the proper convex sets are precisely the independent sets of the \((2r)\)-th power of the original cycle, \(C_n^{2r}\), and the full cardinality enumerator is

\[
 \Phi_{n,r}(z)=\sum_{S\subseteq V(G),\,S\text{ convex}}z^{|S|}
 =1+nz+z^n+\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor n/(2r+1)\rfloor}
 \frac nk\binom{n-2rk-1}{k-1}z^k.
\]

The sum is empty if its upper limit is smaller than two. The maximum size of a *proper* convex set is \(\max\{1,\lfloor n/(2r+1)\rfloor\}\). The case \(r=1\) is excluded and the structural equivalence need not hold there.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and their vertex sets are labelled. Here the cycle \(C_n\) is the usual simple cycle of order \(n\ge3\); \(d_{C_n}\) is distance in this underlying cycle, not in its power. For \(r\ge\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), the cycle power is complete; the formula and the separation criterion remain valid. A pair of selected vertices may be adjacent in the powered graph without being the endpoints of an *induced* three-vertex path. This result uses ordinary \(P_3\)-convexity, generated by **all** three-vertex paths, not induced-path \(P_3^*\)-convexity. All sets including the empty set and the whole vertex set are included exactly once.

## Proof

**Step 1: any close selected pair fills the entire cycle.** Let \(u\ne v\) with \(d_{C_n}(u,v)=d\le2r\). Suppose that a convex set contains both vertices. If \(d\ge2\), fix a shortest cyclic arc between them, of length \(d\), and take an internal vertex \(w\) whose two arc distances to \(u,v\) are at most \(r\); such a vertex exists because \(2\le d\le2r\). The two endpoints \(u,v\) are both neighbours of \(w\) in \(C_n^r\). Therefore convexity forces \(w\). The pair \(u,w\) has cyclic distance at most \(\lceil d/2\rceil<d\). Repeating decreases the distance until the set contains two consecutive vertices of the original cycle.

Now suppose that cyclicly consecutive vertices \(i,i+1\) are already selected. Since \(r\ge2\), each of their adjacent outer vertices \(i-1\) and \(i+2\) (when not yet selected) has both \(i,i+1\) as neighbours in the powered graph, and is forced. This expands a selected consecutive cyclic interval. Inductively the interval grows until every vertex has been selected. The same reasoning handles small cycles where the two outer vertices coincide or one is already selected. Thus a convex set containing any pair of underlying cyclic distance at most \(2r\) must equal the entire vertex set.

**Step 2: the converse.** Suppose no two distinct vertices in \(S\) have underlying cyclic distance at most \(2r\). If a vertex \(w\notin S\) had two neighbours \(u,v\in S\) in \(C_n^r\), the triangle inequality in the cycle metric would give \(d_{C_n}(u,v)\le d_{C_n}(u,w)+d_{C_n}(w,v)\le2r\), a contradiction. Hence every such separated set is convex. The whole vertex set is trivially convex. Because \(n\ge3\), \(r\ge2\), the whole set is not separated, so these cases are disjoint.

**Step 3: count the proper sets.** The empty set and each of the \(n\) singleton sets are convex. Fix \(k\ge2\). Order the selected vertices clockwise and write \(g_1,\ldots,g_k\) for the positive clockwise arc lengths between successive selected vertices, with \(\sum g_i=n\). Pairwise cyclic distance at least \(2r+1\) is equivalent to **all** gaps obeying \(g_i\ge2r+1\). Necessity holds because each gap is itself an arc between selected consecutive vertices; sufficiency holds because any arc between selected vertices is the sum of one or more gaps and thus has length at least \(2r+1\), including the complementary arc.

Subtract \(2r+1\) from every gap. The remaining nonnegative integers sum to \(n-k(2r+1)\), so the number of ordered gap vectors is

\[
 \binom{n-k(2r+1)+k-1}{k-1}
 =\binom{n-2rk-1}{k-1},
\]

provided \(k\le\lfloor n/(2r+1)\rfloor\), and is zero otherwise. Choose a labelled starting vertex in \(n\) ways and an ordered gap vector. Every unordered set of size \(k\) is produced exactly \(k\) times, once for each selected starting vertex. Dividing by \(k\) proves the coefficient formula. The whole vertex set contributes the additional monomial \(z^n\), completing the polynomial and the maximum-proper-size statement.

**Boundary.** For \(r=1\), two consecutive selected vertices in \(C_7\) form a proper convex set: every outside vertex has at most one selected neighbour. They are not separated by distance \(2r+1=3\); thus \(r\ge2\) is essential.

## Relationship to prior work

Dourado, Grippo, Lin, and Protti (2026) introduced a systematic study of counting \(P_3\)-convex sets, proved general counting hardness, and gave counting methods for trees and threshold graphs. Their original full-text treatment of ordinary paths and their general exponential-time algorithms do not state the cycle-power classification or this two-parameter polynomial. Their general algorithms can compute particular instances, which is not a closed-form implication.

González, Grippo, and Safe (2021) study \(P_3\)-hull, interval and percolation-time parameters on caterpillars and unit interval graphs. Their Section 3 quotes a stronger general hull statement for **2-connected chordal graphs**: any pair of vertices with a common neighbour is a hull set. That theorem covers the analogous **path-power** propagation observation; the present claim is deliberately confined to cycle powers, which include nonchordal examples such as \(C_6^2\), and supplies the complete all-set polynomial. The known chordal theorem does not apply to those nonchordal powers. Mathematical-record searches and searches of published graph-convexity sources found no covering cycle-power enumerator; this does not prove historical priority.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns ordinary \(P_3\)-convexity, labelled cycle powers and integer radii \(r\ge2\). It does not count sets up to cyclic or dihedral automorphism, does not treat induced-path convexity or general circular-arc graphs, and makes no claim of a new path-power result. Its originality assessment remains provisional pending independent literature review. Mathematical verification here is a reproducible self-review, not an independent audit.
