# Complete period–weight orbit spectrum of constacyclic Hamming codes

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(q\ge 2\) be any prime power and \(r\ge 2\). Put \(N=(q^r-1)/(q-1)\) and \(M=q^r-1\). Let \(\alpha\) be a primitive element of \(\mathbb F_{q^r}\), let \(\lambda=\alpha^N\in\mathbb F_q^\times\), and consider the \(q\)-ary Hamming code
\[
\mathcal H=\left\{(c_0,\ldots,c_{N-1})\in\mathbb F_q^N:\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_j\alpha^j=0\right\}.
\]
The constacyclic action is
\[
T(c_0,\ldots,c_{N-1})=(\lambda c_{N-1},c_0,\ldots,c_{N-2}).
\]
For every divisor \(d\mid M\), define the *period-dividing weight enumerator*
\[
W_d(z)=\sum_{\substack{c\in\mathcal H\\T^dc=c}}z^{\operatorname{wt}(c)}.
\]
For each proper divisor \(d<M\), write \(g=\gcd(N,d)\). Then
\[
\boxed{W_d(z)=\begin{cases}
\bigl(1+(q-1)z^{N/g}\bigr)^g,&q-1\mid d/g,\\
1,&q-1\nmid d/g.
\end{cases}}
\]
For the full divisor \(M\),
\[
\boxed{W_M(z)=\frac{(1+(q-1)z)^N+(q^r-1)(1-z)^{q^{r-1}}(1+(q-1)z)^{N-q^{r-1}}}{q^r}.}
\]
For \(d\mid M\) and \(0\le w\le N\), the number \(C_{d,w}\) of constacyclic orbits of **shortest period exactly \(d\)** and **Hamming weight exactly \(w\)** is
\[
\boxed{C_{d,w}=\frac1d[z^w]\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)W_e(z),}
\]
where \(\mu\) is the number-theoretic Möbius function. This gives all period/weight pairs and their multiplicities for every permissible alphabet and redundancy, including composite \(N\).

For example, \(q=5,r=2\) gives \(N=6,M=24\). The zero orbit has period \(1\). There are exactly three period-\(8\) orbits: one of weight \(3\) and two of weight \(6\). There are exactly twenty-five period-\(24\) orbits: \(3,5,11,6\) of respective weights \(3,4,5,6\). All other period/weight cells are zero, and the total is \(29\) orbits, including zero.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet must be the finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), not merely a set of \(q\) symbols. The integer \(r\) is at least two; the displayed parity-check columns represent the \(N\) one-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^r\). The code consists of \(q\)-ary vectors, not words over the extension field. The period always refers to \(T\), the constacyclic shift; it is **not** the ordinary cyclic period of the unextended length-\(N\) vector unless \(\lambda=1\). The all-zero word is included as an orbit of period \(1\) and weight \(0\). No orbit is quotiented by arbitrary alphabet relabeling, field automorphisms, or additional scalar actions.

## Proof

**1. The action and the check map.** Since \(\alpha\) has multiplicative order \(M\) and \(N=M/(q-1)\), the element \(\lambda=\alpha^N\) has order \(q-1\) and lies in \(\mathbb F_q\). The map \(L(c)=\sum_j c_j\alpha^j\) is onto \(\mathbb F_{q^r}\): its first \(r\) columns \(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{r-1}\) form an \(\mathbb F_q\)-basis, since a primitive element cannot belong to a proper subfield and has minimal polynomial of degree \(r\). Thus \(\mathcal H=\ker L\) has dimension \(N-r\). Directly,
\[
L(Tc)=\lambda c_{N-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}c_{j-1}\alpha^j
=\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_j\alpha^j=\alpha L(c),
\]
using \(\alpha^N=\lambda\). Hence \(T\) preserves \(\mathcal H\), \(T^N=\lambda I\), and \(T^M=I\).

**2. Proper-divisor fixed words automatically pass the check.** Let \(d\mid M\) and \(d<M\). If \(T^dc=c\) in the entire ambient space, then
\[
L(c)=L(T^dc)=\alpha^d L(c).
\]
Because the order of \(\alpha\) is \(M\), we have \(\alpha^d\ne1\), forcing \(L(c)=0\). Therefore the \(T^d\)-fixed vectors of the *ambient* space and of \(\mathcal H\) coincide. This is an essential special feature of this parity-check realization.

**3. Fixed-word weight polynomial.** The underlying coordinate permutation of \(T^d\) is a shift by \(d\) modulo \(N\), with exactly \(g=\gcd(N,d)\) disjoint cycles of length \(N/g\). Iteration once around any cycle multiplies a coordinate by \(\lambda^{d/g}\): indeed \((T^d)^{N/g}=T^{dN/g}=\lambda^{d/g}I\). If \(\lambda^{d/g}\ne1\), every fixed coordinate is zero. If \(\lambda^{d/g}=1\), a chosen field element on each cycle propagates uniquely around that cycle; zero contributes weight zero and each of \(q-1\) nonzero choices contributes weight \(N/g\). Multiplying the independent cycle polynomials proves the asserted \(W_d\) for \(d<M\), with the divisibility criterion \(q-1\mid d/g\).

**4. The full word polynomial.** Since \(T^M=I\), \(W_M\) is the ordinary weight enumerator of \(\mathcal H\). Its dual is the \(q\)-ary simplex code generated by the columns \(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{N-1}\). Every nonzero dual word has weight \(q^{r-1}\): a nonzero linear functional on \(\mathbb F_q^r\) vanishes on \((q^{r-1}-1)/(q-1)\) projective points, and so is nonzero at the remaining \(q^{r-1}\). Consequently its dual weight enumerator is \(1+(q^r-1)z^{q^{r-1}}\). Applying the standard \(q\)-ary MacWilliams identity to this *explicit* dual enumerator proves the formula for \(W_M\).

**5. Exact shortest periods and weight preservation.** For each \(w\), let \(A_{d,w}\) count codewords of weight \(w\) with shortest \(T\)-period exactly \(d\). Because \(T^M=I\), the possible shortest periods divide \(M\). The finite cyclic-action identity
\[
[z^w]W_d(z)=\sum_{e\mid d}A_{e,w}
\]
holds for every \(d\mid M\). Möbius inversion gives \(A_{d,w}=\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)[z^w]W_e(z)\). Since \(T\) only shifts and multiplies coordinates by nonzero field elements, it preserves Hamming weight. Every shortest-period-\(d\) orbit therefore contains exactly \(d\) words of the same weight, giving \(C_{d,w}=A_{d,w}/d\). No unproved asymptotics or finite enumeration is involved in these identities. This proves the theorem for all prime powers and all redundancies in the stated scope.

Run `python3 verify_orbits.py` using Python 3 standard library. The checker independently constructs the extension fields using explicit irreducible polynomials, selects primitive elements, forms the parity check, enumerates every codeword, computes actual constacyclic orbits, and compares all period/weight cells with the polynomial formulas and with all fixed-point weight enumerators. It covers \((q,r)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(4,2),(5,2)\), including the 59,049-word ternary code and a nonprime repunit example. The checks are finite tests, not the reason the universal theorem is true; the universal proof is above.

## Relationship to prior work

Etzion's July 16, 2026 manuscript [1] supplies this constacyclic representation of the \(q\)-ary Hamming code and establishes that its constacyclic periods divide \(q^r-1\). It discusses repunit-prime cases and specifically asks about possible periods and orbit sizes when the repunit length is composite. The present theorem gives an all-prime-power, all-redundancy refinement by **both** shortest period and weight.

Zhang and Cao [2] already give formulas for the **total number of constacyclic orbits** of general simple-root constacyclic codes; those totals must not be treated as new here. Their orbit-count formulas and weight-count upper bounds do not provide the stated joint period–weight classification. Chen, Fu and Liu [3] study orbit counts under a larger automorphism group and bounds for the number of distinct weights, again different from this specific joint spectrum. The preexisting MacWilliams identity and group-action Möbius inversion are used as standard tools, not claimed as original results. Chee, Etzion, Ta and Vu [4] develop covering-sequence constructions, not these weight-refined orbit counts.

## Limitations

The result concerns constacyclic Hamming codes under the **specific** action \(T\), not arbitrary cyclic codes, other parity-check choices, or the orbit structure after scalar or semilinear quotienting. The general total-orbit count is known independently [2]. The result does not give optimal covering-sequence lengths or an efficient construction of a single covering sequence from all orbits. Definitive historical priority relative to other, possibly unindexed, weight-refined orbit tables is not established; an independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
