# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The pointwise formula is proved by reducing rank-one idempotents to a smooth ternary quadratic level set, conjugating every primitive traceless matrix to a cyclic companion form, then counting a split or anisotropic binary quadratic equation over \(\mathbb Z/p^m\mathbb Z\). Distinct valuation branches are treated, including the singular target norm and zero right-hand side. The exact energy follows from the displayed norm-fiber counts and their exhaustive valuation partition; the sharp minimum and its equality count use the same partition. Integer-only direct convolution verified all targets at moduli \(3,9,27,5,25,7,11,13\). Risk: independent mathematical validation has not occurred; finite testing is not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparisons

The earlier field-depth rank-one idempotent Fourier transform covers the field analogue and is **not reasserted** as new; the statement is restricted to depths at least two. De Seguins Pazzis, arXiv:0907.4436v1, Theorem 3, classifies when matrices over a **field** are sums of two idempotents, not their ordered decomposition numbers over local rings. Evdokimov–Ponomarenko, arXiv:math/0608469v1, develop cyclotomic and translation schemes over rings but investigate their normality and automorphism groups, not these exact projector-pair fibers. Tang, Zhou and Su, arXiv:1712.04607v1, classify the three-summand existence property over rings and do not imply the two-summand counts. Direct equivalence and implication searches, including character-fourth-moment and norm-conic formulations, yielded no confirmed dominating local-ring theorem. The residual priority risk is a broader quadratic representation-density or association-scheme result not located in bounded comparison; this assessment does not certify uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Rank-one projectors form a canonical conjugacy orbit. Their twofold sum distribution is a natural additive harmonic invariant on a matrix ring. The exact all-depth convolution captures singular valuation strata, gives a sharp positive uniform lower bound with a genuine exceptional prime, and yields an explicit fourth Fourier moment, not just a single finite count or a routine field-lifting statement. The result does not claim a sharp arbitrary-weight Fourier restriction constant.
