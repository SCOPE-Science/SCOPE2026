# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The curve polynomial is irreducible for every strictly positive radius because its quadratic-in-conjugate-coordinate discriminant has odd-order poles at the two foci. A resultant argument forces any quadratic-intensity difference that vanishes along an arc to be a multiple of that polynomial. The coefficient Hermitian matrix of this relation has rank two and its range is exactly the even-polynomial plane; the rank-one difference identity then proves the complete ambiguity classification. The explicit circle reflection proves sufficiency, including degenerate linear factors. The Hermitian quotient has dimension eight and real-analytic evaluation determinants establish generic unisolvence of both the plain and antipodal samples. The finite checker corroborates representative instances; it does not certify irreducibility or all-radius quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk.** The full text of Chalendar--Partington (arXiv:2403.16255), particularly Sections 2.1--2.4, treats circles and straight lines and does not imply the Cassini quadratic ambiguity classification: its reflection mechanisms are Möbius/circle-specific. Przybyłek--Siedlecki (arXiv:1907.09371) permits selection of sampling sites in the plane and does not establish constrained quartic-curve geometry. Published mathematical-record searches for Cassini phaseless interpolation, polynomial lemniscates, and bidegree rank-two ambiguity did not identify the same result. The most relevant available earlier findings concerning ellipse/parabolic-arc/spiral sampling are not stronger covering theorems, because their geometric sets do not contain a Cassini oval and their intensity ideals differ. A possible general theorem for algebraic-curve polynomial phase retrieval remains a priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** Phase retrieval on simple curves is a motivated analytic sampling question. The Cassini level set is the first polynomial lemniscate beyond a circle, with a geometric topology change at the lemniscate radius. The result does more than exhibit the obvious nonuniqueness \(|z^2-1|=r\): it **completely classifies all quadratic ambiguities**, proves they form exactly the even subspace, and supplies finite eight-point intensity-profile recovery on any smooth arc, valid even at the singular transition. It separates generic recoverability from universal failure in a natural restricted measurement family. The bound is explicitly limited to linear profile reconstruction; noise stability and higher degrees remain open.
