# Scientific review: exact small-generator Kiselman sphere census

## Correctness — PASS
The claim quantifies **only** \(1\le n\le6\), and the supplied standalone Python program exhausts each finite canonical language. The needed nontrivial theorem (canonical uniqueness) is a known published result [2,3 in RESULT.md]. A word is canonically valid exactly if repeats of letter \(i\) are separated by both a lower and higher letter. The direct prefix enumerator is complete by induction on word length, while an independent breadth-first multiplication enumerator uses the two standard shortening relations, and the code compares their **full sets**. Two separate level computations (greedy right-multiplication, and quotient deletion followed by reduction) agree on all \(85823\) canonical words for sizes 1 through 6. The exact counts also match the known total orders; the computed interior data, and the finite symmetries, are direct consequences of the exhaustive displayed computation. The program was inspected and run from the actual delivered package. Finite output is not extrapolated to all \(n\).

## Originality — PASS, with qualified historical priority
The April 28, 2026 manuscript [1] was read, particularly Sections 5–7. It establishes the ultrametric and general level computation, plus only the two extreme sphere sizes; it does not report the interior full census. The older Kudryavtseva–Mazorchuk canonical theorem and OEIS A125625 report unique words and **total** orders, not radius-resolved counts. Searches in primary literature, published-finding corpus and accessible prior research records did not produce an equivalent table for these radii; search absence is not proof of novelty. The precise distinction from all closest prior work is the **interior distribution** through six generators (not normal forms, total sizes, endpoint counts, or an all-size symmetry theorem). An unindexed or later source with the same table remains a residual historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS, finite scope
The April 2026 introduction of a semigroup ultrametric makes its metric radial sizes an intrinsically defined and motivated invariant. The source leaves intermediate spheres numerically uncomputed. The exact first full census resolves that concrete gap over the entire currently tractable small-generator range: the six-generator algebra has \(83973\) elements, while the next order is \(22263378\). Its nontrivial interior numbers refine information inaccessible from known total orders or extreme sphere formulas, and the exact symmetry across all six cases provides reproducible boundary data for future structural work. This is a finite census, **not** a general theorem; a limited but well-motivated finite cutoff is valuable here because it gives the first complete metric distribution for the natural family. The routine known facts and the cardinality sequence alone carry no novelty/value claim.

## Limits and nearest research
The principal scope limit is \(n\le6\). Computations are exhaustive exactly in that range; no all-size palindromicity, general sphere formula, or pairwise distance distribution has been shown. The principal originality risk is an equivalent unindexed sphere table, especially in combinatorial discussions of canonical Kiselman words.
