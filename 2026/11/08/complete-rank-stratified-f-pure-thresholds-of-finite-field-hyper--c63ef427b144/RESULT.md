# Complete rank-stratified F-pure thresholds of finite-field hyperplane arrangements

## Finding
Fix a prime power \(q=p^a\), an algebraic closure \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_q\), and an integer \(N\ge 3\). Let \(D_{N,q}\) be the reduced union in \(\mathbb P^{N-1}_k\) of *every* hyperplane defined over \(\mathbb F_q\). Its homogeneous defining polynomial, up to a nonzero scalar, is the Moore determinant
\[
M_{N,q}(X)=\det\bigl(X_i^{q^{j-1}}\bigr)_{1\le i,j\le N}.
\]
For a point \(P=[z_1:\cdots:z_N]\) over \(k\), put \(r(P)=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\langle z_1,\ldots,z_N\rangle\). This is independent of the representative. If \(r(P)<N\), then \(P\in D_{N,q}\) and
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{fpt}_P(D_{N,q})=q^{-(N-r(P)-1)}.}
\]
If \(r(P)=N\), then \(P\notin D_{N,q}\). Thus the values on the divisor are exactly \(1,q^{-1},\ldots,q^{-(N-2)}\), indexed by coordinate rank; at the vertex of the affine cone the threshold is \(q^{-(N-1)}\).

The formula is stronger at finite Frobenius levels. Write \(m=N-r(P)\ge1\), and let \(\nu_P(p^s)\) be the greatest integer \(j\ge0\) for which a local equation to the \(j\)-th power does *not* lie in the \(p^s\)-th Frobenius power of the maximal ideal. Then for every integer \(s\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{\nu_P(p^s)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s<a(m-1),\\
p^{s-a(m-1)}-1,&s\ge a(m-1).
\end{cases}}
\]
The same formula holds at the cone vertex with \(m=N\).

For each \(1\le r\le N\), the rank-at-most-\(r\) locus in \(\mathbb P^{N-1}_k\) is the union of all \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational projective \((r-1)\)-planes. It has \({N\brack r}_q\) irreducible components, each of dimension \(r-1\). Moreover, for every extension degree \(s\ge1\), the number of \(\mathbb F_{q^s}\)-rational projective points of coordinate rank exactly \(r\) is
\[
\boxed{A_{N,q,s}(r)={N\brack r}_q\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}(q^s-q^j),\quad 1\le r\le\min(N,s),}
\]
where \({N\brack r}_q=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(q^N-q^i)/(q^r-q^i)\). Therefore this also gives the exact distribution of all local F-pure thresholds on \(D_{N,q}(\mathbb F_{q^s})\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient field \(k\) is perfect of characteristic \(p\) and contains \(\mathbb F_q\); the stated projective geometry and counting are over its algebraic closure. The local invariant refers to the hypersurface equation in the regular local ring of the ambient smooth projective space, not to the F-pure threshold of an abstract nonregular coordinate ring. A unit local equation is excluded from the threshold assertion, rather than assigned a spurious finite threshold. In the affine assertion the vertex means the origin of \(\mathbb A^N\). The binary case \(N=2\) follows by the same elementary calculation but is not proposed as a novel case.

For a formal power-series local equation \(f\) in characteristic \(p\), and maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\), the Frobenius power \(\mathfrak m^{[p^s]}\) is generated by the \(p^s\)-th powers of a regular parameter system. Set \(\nu_f(p^s)=\max\{j\ge0:f^j\notin\mathfrak m^{[p^s]}\}\); then \(\operatorname{fpt}(f)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\nu_f(p^s)/p^s\).

## Proof
**1. Determinantal factorization.** If \(c=(c_i)\in\mathbb F_q^N\) is nonzero, then on the linear hyperplane \(\sum c_iX_i=0\) one has \(\sum c_iX_i^{q^j}=0\) for every \(j\). Hence that linear form divides \(M_{N,q}\). The forms corresponding to different points of the dual finite projective space are pairwise nonassociate. Their number is \((q^N-1)/(q-1)=1+q+\cdots+q^{N-1}\), the degree of the determinant. The determinant has a nonzero permutation monomial, so it is nonzero, and degree comparison proves that it is a nonzero scalar times the product of these distinct linear forms.

**2. Reduction of each local equation to a smaller Moore determinant.** At a vector \(z=(z_i)\), the \(\mathbb F_q\)-space of coefficient vectors for vanishing rational hyperplanes is the kernel of \(\mathbb F_q^N\to k\), \(c\mapsto\sum c_iz_i\). It has dimension \(m=N-r(P)\). Choose a basis of this kernel over \(\mathbb F_q\), extend it to a full linear dual coordinate system, and translate the resulting affine coordinates to \(P\). The factors vanishing at \(P\) become precisely the products of all nonzero \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear forms in \(m\) transverse parameters, modulo scalar. Every other factor is a unit. Therefore the completed local equation is a unit times \(M_{m,q}\) in those \(m\) parameters, with \(N-m\) additional smooth parameters. Unit multiplication and adjoining smooth parameters do not alter membership in Frobenius powers of the maximal ideal.

**3. Exact Frobenius exponent.** For \(m\ge1\), let \(Q=q^{m-1}=p^{a(m-1)}\). Every determinant monomial of \(M_{m,q}\) contains some variable to the \(Q\)-th power; thus \(M_{m,q}\in(y_1^Q,\ldots,y_m^Q)\). If \(s<a(m-1)\), this already lies in \((y_1^{p^s},\ldots,y_m^{p^s})\) and the exponent is zero. If \(s\ge a(m-1)\), put \(H=p^{s-a(m-1)}\). Raising to the \(H\)-th power by Frobenius gives \(M_{m,q}^H\in(y_1^{p^s},\ldots,y_m^{p^s})\). On the other hand, for lexicographic order \(y_1>\cdots>y_m\), the determinant has the unique initial monomial \(\pm y_1^{q^{m-1}}y_2^{q^{m-2}}\cdots y_m\). Its \((H-1)\)-st power has nonzero coefficient and every exponent is strictly smaller than \(p^s\), so \(M_{m,q}^{H-1}\) does not lie in the Frobenius power. This proves the exact exponent formula; dividing by \(p^s\) and taking the limit gives the threshold. For \(m=1\) the same argument says the equation is a regular parameter and has threshold one.

**4. The geometric and arithmetic rank strata.** Rank \(\le r\) means that the space of rational linear equations vanishing at a point has dimension \(\ge N-r\). Consequently the locus is the union of the \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational projective \((r-1)\)-subspaces. In each such subspace, points of rank exactly \(r\) form a nonempty Zariski-open subset, so these subspaces are exactly the irreducible components. To count points over \(\mathbb F_{q^s}\), view an \(N\)-tuple of elements of that field as an \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear map \(\mathbb F_q^N\to\mathbb F_{q^s}\). A rank-\(r\) map is specified by its kernel (\({N\brack r}_q\) choices) and an injection from the quotient into \(\mathbb F_{q^s}\) (\(\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q^s-q^j)\) choices). Dividing by the free action of \(\mathbb F_{q^s}^\times\) yields the asserted product beginning at \(j=1\).

## Verification
An independent Python 3 standard-library program reconstructs the Moore determinant using the signed permutation expansion over prime fields. It then multiplies sparse multivariate polynomials modulo the Frobenius parameter-power ideal, checking exact exponents (not floating-point estimates) for \((N,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(3,4),(4,2)\), at several Frobenius levels. A separate exact matrix-rank enumeration checks the projective point-count identity for five small parameter triples. Run `python verify_moore.py` in the same directory. The expected final messages are `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED` and `ARITHMETIC_CHECKS_PASSED`.

## Relationship to prior work
The Moore determinant factorization is classical. Blickle–Mustaţă–Smith (arXiv:math/0607660; arXiv:0705.1210) supply the general framework of F-thresholds, test ideals, and rationality/discreteness, not this exact local profile for the finite-field arrangement. Hernández–Núñez-Betancourt–Witt–Zhang (arXiv:1404.3772) obtain strong formulas under isolated-singularity hypotheses, which the higher-dimensional arrangement does not satisfy. The 2020 preprint of Kadyrsizova et al. (arXiv:2009.13679) characterizes degree-\(Q+1\) extremal forms and gives the Frobenius-ideal upper-bound criterion; it already subsumes the two-variable base case, but does not determine the higher-dimensional arrangement's exact thresholds or its rank-stratum point counts. Smith–Vraciu (arXiv:2207.11800, 2022) investigate generic forms and possible threshold values for reduced forms, not the Moore arrangement's exact pointwise values. These comparisons leave historical priority subject to specialist literature not identified in the inspected sources.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the *first* positive F-jumping number (F-pure threshold), not all test ideals, further jumping numbers, Hilbert–Kunz multiplicities, or F-signatures. The finite verifications are corroborative; the general theorem rests on the determinant and Frobenius proof. The result is not a calculation of a characteristic-zero log canonical threshold, and there is no claim that finite projective-plane matroids lift to characteristic zero. Independent assessment of historical priority has not been performed.
