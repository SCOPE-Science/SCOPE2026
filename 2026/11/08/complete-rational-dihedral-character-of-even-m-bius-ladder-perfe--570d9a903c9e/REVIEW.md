# Review: even Möbius-ladder perfect-matching dihedral homology

**Correctness — PASS.** The source homotopy theorem identifies a two-dimensional rational homology group and the source's minimal forbidden pairs identify the complex as a graph independence complex. Every fixed simplex corresponds to an independent selection of admissible edge-orbits; the signed Lefschetz factor includes both simplex dimension and orientation. For vertex-axis reflections, an isolated spoke orbit cancels the independence sum; for edge-axis reflections, explicitly proved false-twin deletions leave a chain of triangles. Its independent-set polynomial at \(-1\) has only the two alternating rim covers surviving, yielding the residue-class trace. Rotational traces follow from a complete fixed-orbit list and finite-order eigenvalues. A separate program re-enumerates the original face sets and all group traces for four widths and checks quotient reductions for six widths. No finite extrapolation is used in the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with stated limitations.** Agarwal–Basak supply the nonequivariant classification and forbidden-pair input, but not the reflection character. The previously published one-step-rotation result determines the cyclic module and is incorporated with credit; the antipodal-half-turn result is a smaller restriction, and the complete odd-width dihedral classification is outside the even-width hypotheses. The exact new reflection trace at widths congruent to two modulo four, including its alternating sign, does not follow from the cyclic representation: on its two rotation eigenlines, two inequivalent choices for reflection signs are still possible. Searches of equivalent formulations, homological Lefschetz traces and relevant primary/published findings produced no covering statement. The expanded 2026 source revision could be checked only at abstract level; an unindexed equivalent calculation cannot be excluded.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This finishes the natural even-width dihedral-character question for a canonical symmetric family in which the nonequivariant sphere count and cyclic character were known. It gives the exact reflection eigenline orientations and a nontrivial periodic dichotomy. The distinction between the two reflection classes is mathematically motivated by dihedral representation theory, not an arbitrary finite parameter slice; the result applies to every even width.

**Closest literature and scope.** Agarwal and Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v1, §4; *Rotation has order two or four on Möbius-ladder perfect-matching homology*, 2026-10-11; *The antipodal half-turn representation on Möbius-ladder perfect-matching homology*, 2026-10-11; *Complete dihedral rational homology representations of odd Möbius-ladder perfect-matching complexes*, 2026-10-11. The result does not imply an integral lattice decomposition or equivariant homotopy equivalence, and independent validation is unperformed.
