# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The normalized harmonic Laurent basis and monomial-projection identity yield a bilateral shift formula. Its adjoint produces a diagonal signed self-commutator, with a reflection symmetry reducing every coefficient to the stated positive formula. Two monotone branches give the global norm and a residue-class total-variation calculation gives the trace norm. The paired tail behaves as \(m^2/j^2\), rigorously giving \(n^2s_n\to4m^2\) and the exact quasi-Schatten threshold. The claims include \(m=1\), the crossing-origin branch, zero eigenvalue, spectral multiplicities and the weak endpoint. The packaged checker passed 23,205 exact finite assertions; the mathematical proof is not based on finite enumeration.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified best-knowledge assessment.** Tian–Wang–Zhao (arXiv:2609.14635, Theorem 1.2) prove zero-versus-nonzero commutation, not these quantitative monomial spectra. Dong–Zhou (arXiv:1411.0213v3, Section 4) classify *finite ranks* of quasihomogeneous commutators, not the full infinite singular spectrum, sharp index asymptotic and exact trace norm. Chbichib–Ghiloufi (arXiv:2605.24698) work with ambient projection commutators \([M_u,Q]\), not a Toeplitz–Toeplitz self-commutator. The 2013 Chen–Koo–Lee finite-rank article was checked by abstract and metadata only. Older general monomial-operator formulas may imply some statements by specialization: independent priority assessment is still needed.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The full singular spectrum and two exact norms give a complete quantitative classification of a natural family singled out by the recent qualitative commutativity theorem. The sharp transition between strong and weak Schatten ideals at \(p=1/2\) and the explicit trace norm quantify noncommutativity at every monomial degree, information not contained in qualitative nonzero or finite-rank criteria. The claim is limited to a natural family rather than an arbitrary coefficient slice.
