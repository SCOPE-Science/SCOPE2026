# Complete symmetry classification for a gap-three Pell numerical-semigroup family

## Finding
Let the Pell numbers satisfy \(P_0=0\), \(P_1=1\), and \(P_{n+2}=2P_{n+1}+P_n\). For each integer \(n\ge1\), form the numerical semigroup
\[
S_n=\langle P_n,P_{n+3},P_{n+4}\rangle.
\]
Then the following is an exact classification:
\[
\boxed{S_n\text{ is symmetric}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\in\{1,2,4\}\ \text{or}\ 3\mid n.}
\]
Equivalently, its semigroup algebra over any field is Gorenstein, or equivalently a complete intersection, precisely at these indices. For \(3\mid n\), the symmetric semigroup has the explicit gluing
\[
 S_n=5\left\langle P_n/5,P_{n+3}/5\right\rangle+P_{n+4}\mathbb N_0.
\]
The two exceptional nonmultiples of three with nontrivial generators are \(S_2=\langle2,29\rangle\) and \(S_4=\langle12,169\rangle\); \(S_1=\mathbb N_0\).

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(\mathbb N_0\) denotes the nonnegative integers; \(\langle a,b,c\rangle\) is the additive monoid of nonnegative linear combinations of its generators. A numerical semigroup \(S\) is *symmetric* if for its Frobenius number \(F(S)\) every integer \(z\) satisfies \(z\in S\) if and only if \(F(S)-z\notin S\). For \(S=\mathbb N_0\), use \(F(S)=-1\) so that the same definition applies. The Pell sequence is the ordinary sequence with recurrence coefficient \(2\), not an arbitrary generalized Pell sequence. The claim holds for every positive index \(n\), without a bound or density restriction.

We invoke two standard arithmetic and semigroup facts. First, the Pell strong-divisibility identity is
\[\gcd(P_r,P_s)=P_{\gcd(r,s)}\qquad(r,s\ge1).\]
Second, a numerical semigroup minimally generated by three positive integers \(a,b,c\) is symmetric if and only if some pair, say \(a,b\), has \(d=\gcd(a,b)>1\) and the remaining generator belongs to \(\langle a/d,b/d\rangle\). The latter is the standard three-generator complete-intersection/gluing criterion; when a generator is redundant, the semigroup is two-generated and automatically symmetric. In the criterion the remaining generator is automatically coprime to \(d\) because the three original generators have gcd one.

## Proof
**A uniform Pell relation.** Repeatedly applying the recurrence gives
\[
P_{n+4}=12P_{n+1}+5P_n,\qquad P_{n+3}=5P_{n+1}+2P_n,
\]
and therefore
\[\boxed{5P_{n+4}=P_n+12P_{n+3}.}\tag{1}\]
Write \(a=P_n\), \(b=P_{n+3}\), \(c=P_{n+4}\). Then \(0<a<b<c<3b\) for every \(n\ge1\), since \(c=2b+P_{n+2}\) and \(0<P_{n+2}<b\). The gcd identity gives
\[
\gcd(a,b)=P_{\gcd(n,3)},\quad
\gcd(a,c)=P_{\gcd(n,4)},\quad
\gcd(b,c)=1.
\tag{2}\]
Since \(\gcd(b,c)=1\), each \(S_n\) is numerical.

**The symmetric indices.** At \(n=1\) the first generator is \(1\). At \(n=2\), \(a=2\) divides \(c=70\), hence \(S_2=\langle2,29\rangle\). At \(n=4\), \(a=12\) divides \(c=408\), hence \(S_4=\langle12,169\rangle\). All are symmetric because they have at most two minimal generators.

Suppose now \(3\mid n\). By (2), \(d=\gcd(a,b)=P_3=5\); also \(\gcd(c,5)=1\) because \(\gcd(n+4,3)=1\). Equation (1) becomes
\[c=a/5+12(b/5),\]
so \(c\in\langle a/5,b/5\rangle\) and \(\gcd(a/5,b/5)=1\). Thus \(S_n=5\langle a/5,b/5\rangle+c\mathbb N_0\) is a gluing of two symmetric one-dimensional numerical semigroups; it is symmetric. This also covers \(n=3\), when the generator \(b\) is redundant.

**No further symmetric indices.** Assume \(n\ge5\) and \(3\nmid n\). The gcd formula (2) gives \(\gcd(a,b)=1\). We first show that \(a,b,c\) are all minimal generators. Clearly \(a\) is smallest, and \(b\notin\langle a,c\rangle\), because \(b<c\) and \(a\nmid b\). If \(c=ua+vb\) with \(u,v\in\mathbb N_0\), the inequality \(c<3b\) forces \(0\le v\le2\). Multiplying by five and applying (1) yields
\[(5u-1)a=(12-5v)b.\]
Since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), we would have \(a\mid(12-5v)\), where \(12-5v\) is one of \(12,7,2\). But \(a=P_n\ge P_5=29\), a contradiction. Therefore the generating triple is minimal.

If \(n\) is odd, (2) shows that the three generators are pairwise coprime: \(\gcd(n,4)=1\). The three-generator criterion rules out symmetry.

If \(n\) is even, then \(d=\gcd(a,c)\) is the only possible nontrivial pairwise gcd. There are two cases.

When \(n\equiv2\pmod4\), (2) gives \(d=P_2=2\). Since \(c>2b\), one has \(c/2>b\). Any representation \(b=u(a/2)+v(c/2)\) with \(u,v\in\mathbb N_0\) must have \(v=0\), forcing \(a/2\mid b\). Yet \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), so \(a/2=1\), impossible for \(n\ge5\).

When \(4\mid n\), (2) gives \(d=P_4=12\). Put \(A=a/12\) and \(C=c/12\); then \(\gcd(A,C)=1\). From (1), \(b=5C-A\), so any representation \(b=uA+vC\) would imply
\[(u+1)A=(5-v)C.\]
Because \(u\ge0\) and \(A>0\), necessarily \(0\le v\le4\). Coprimality yields \(A\mid(5-v)\), hence \(A\le5\). But now \(n\ge8\) and \(A\ge P_8/12=34\), contradiction. Thus the three-generator gluing criterion fails in every remaining case. This proves the classification. \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work
Komatsu and Mu, *\(p\)-numerical semigroups of Pell triples* (first publicly posted 2023-07-18, arXiv:2307.08998), obtain Frobenius/genus expressions for the different generator family \(\langle P_i(u),P_{i+2}(u),P_{i+k}(u)\rangle\). Their fixed initial gap is \(2\), whereas the family here has first gap \(3\) and last gap \(1\), so the present index classification is not obtained by choosing one of their parameters. Fel, *Symmetric Numerical Semigroups Generated by Fibonacci and Lucas Triples* (arXiv:0803.1606), treats the Fibonacci and Lucas sequences, not the ordinary Pell sequence with recurrence coefficient two. The classical three-generator gluing theorem supplies a general test, but does not by itself determine the Pell-index set; the relation (1), exact gcds, and exclusion arguments are required. Earlier investigations of different Pell triple patterns, including gaps \(2,2\), are distinct from this family.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to ordinary Pell numbers and to the exact index gaps \((3,1)\). It does not enumerate all gap lengths, provide explicit Frobenius or genus formulas for nonsymmetric indices, or determine factorization invariants. The relevant 2008 Fibonacci/Lucas paper was available by abstract in this review, not fully inspected; an unindexed generalization could overlap. Self-review is not independent validation.
