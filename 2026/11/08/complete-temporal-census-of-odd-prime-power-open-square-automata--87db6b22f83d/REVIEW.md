# Review of the open-square temporal classification

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof starts with the open-path characteristic polynomial and uses Frobenius to split the one-dimensional operator into the two Jordan blocks \(J_m(2)\) and \(J_m(-2)\). The two-dimensional Kronecker sum then has four primary sectors. The nonstandard modular step—the assertion that multiplication by \(x+y\) on the truncated two-variable algebra has every odd Jordan block exactly once—is proved using explicit binomial Toeplitz determinants and Legendre valuation cancellation for every prime power. This recovers *all* Jordan blocks, rather than just eigenvalue multiplicities. Fitting decomposition and a precise computation of \(\ker(J_\ell(\lambda)^d-I)\) derive the entrance, fixed-point, primitive-period, and joint formulas. The scripts check small matrices and separate truncated-algebra ranks; finite checks support the proof but are not its source of universal quantification.

## Originality
**PASS, qualified.** The algebraic ingredients are known in broader contexts: Cook's strong Lefschetz classification directly predicts the relevant truncated-algebra property, Norman's work treats modular Kronecker sums, and Hunziker–Machiavelo–Park treat Chebyshev and square-grid reversibility. These are acknowledged and not represented as original. The final claim is an explicit *complete temporal* census for odd-prime-power open square grids, including all Jordan eigenvalues, exact joint entrance/primitive-period counts, sharp maximum period, and sharp number of maximally delayed states. The accessible full Cook text establishes no such grid dynamical classification. Searches of mathematical records, square-grid automaton literature, and accessible earlier related findings found no direct covering statement. Norman's and Hunziker–Machiavelo–Park's potentially relevant full texts could not be obtained; an earlier derivation of this specialization remains possible. A generic modular Jordan theorem can supply a major proof component, so the originality assessed is the complete and explicit dynamical synthesis, not the underlying Jordan algorithm.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The update is a canonical local linear cellular automaton with open boundary, and its finite-field dynamics is an exact countable family rather than a chosen finite example. Knowing only grid nullity or reversibility cannot determine first-entry distributions, least periods, or primitive-orbit numbers. This result supplies all of those invariants, shows an exact half-dimension zero/invertible split, and exhibits an attained period controlled simultaneously by field multiplicative orders and prime-power unipotent depth. The prime-power side length is motivated by Frobenius collapse of the path characteristic polynomial. These are natural complete dynamical invariants usable as exact benchmarks; no broader theorem about arbitrary grid lengths is asserted.

## Closest literature and limitations
Cook (arXiv:1111.4979v1, Theorem 4.9) proves the relevant modular strong Lefschetz *property*, rather than the temporal grid counts. Norman (DOI 10.1080/03081080701395640) develops modular Kronecker-sum Jordan methods; only accessible bibliographic/abstract material could be checked. Hunziker–Machiavelo–Park (DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2004.03.031) concern square-grid additive automata and Chebyshev reversibility, but their full text remained unavailable. The proof does not extend to characteristic two, periodic boundaries or all square sizes. The possibility of an unlocated equivalent or an uninspected specialization is explicitly retained.
