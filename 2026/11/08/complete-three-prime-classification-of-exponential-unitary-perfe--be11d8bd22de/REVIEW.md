# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
An unrestricted proof reduces to the squarefull core, establishes the sharp local ratio bound \(R_p(a)\le1+1/p\) with equality only at exponent two, and strengthens the estimate at exponents \(a\ge4\) for the prime two. Parity and forced prime divisibility then classify all two- and three-prime cores. The only cases are \(36,1800,2700\), and a singleton coprime prime factor can be reinserted only into the first. Exact computation tests \(184320\) prime-exponent triples and all integers through \(350000\); unrestrictedness rests on the argument, not the tests.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The 2009 preprint by Minculete and Tóth defines the function, gives the three core examples, and poses an e-unitary/e-perfect equivalence problem. It does not present an exhaustive three-prime theorem. The known two-prime classification, \(36\), is subsumed and explicitly credited. A published fixed-support asymptotic for ordinary exponential-perfect integers does not prove the e-unitary restriction or classify powerful three-prime cores. OEIS A391281 contains example data but no proof of three-prime exhaustiveness. The 1978 *On e-perfect numbers* primary article was not accessed in full; its relevance and the risk of older terminology are retained. Targeted search of the statement and equivalent forms did not find dominating coverage, which is not absolute novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete natural next support layer after the classical two-prime case distinguishes two exceptional squarefull cores from infinitely many squarefree extensions of \(36\), without arbitrary parameter cutoffs. It strengthens the necessary condition on a counterexample to the explicit open equivalence question from three distinct prime factors to four. It also fully explains why the first listed primitive examples have exactly these exponents.

## Limits
An overlooked prior classification remains a historical-priority risk. Nothing is claimed at support size four or beyond. Computations corroborate, and independent review is absent.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
