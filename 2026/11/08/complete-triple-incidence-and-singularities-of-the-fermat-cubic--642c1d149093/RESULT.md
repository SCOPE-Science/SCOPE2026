# Complete triple incidence and singularities of the Fermat cubic's 27 hyperosculating conics

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb C\), let \(F\subset\mathbb P^2_k\) be the Fermat cubic \(X^3+Y^3+Z^3=0\), and let \(\mathcal Q\) be the union of its \(27\) distinct hyperosculating conics. Exactly \(27\) of the \(\binom{27}{3}=2925\) unordered triples of these conics have a nonempty common intersection. Of these, \(9\) triples meet at one common point, where all three conics have the same tangent; the other \(18\) triples meet at two common points, and all three conics are pairwise transverse at each point. No point lies on four conics.

Consequently the singular points of the **union of the conics alone**, without adjoining \(F\), consist of exactly \(1242\) ordinary nodes, \(36\) ordinary triple points and \(9\) three-branch common-tangent points of type \(J_{2,0}\). There are \(1287\) distinct singular points, and the total Tjurina number is \(1476\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\), and choose \(\alpha\in\mathbb C\) and \(\omega\in\mathbb C\) with \(\alpha^3=2\), \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\). For \(h,i,j\in\{0,1,2\}\), read coordinate indices cyclically and set
\[
U=\omega^iX_h,\qquad V=\omega^jX_{h+1},\qquad W=X_{h+2},\qquad
Q_{h,i,j}=(U-V)^2-W\bigl(\alpha^2(U+V)+2\alpha W\bigr).
\]
The \(27\) zero loci \(Q_{h,i,j}=0\) are the distinct smooth hyperosculating conics, as identified in Dimca–Ilardi–Malara–Pokora, Section 4, or by the explicit sextactic-point construction there. Multiplication of an equation by a nonzero scalar does not change its conic. All triple counts here concern unordered distinct conics and all point counts concern geometric support, not intersection multiplicity.

Define \(P_{h,k}=\{Q_{h,i,i+k}:i\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\}\) and define two further families \(T^U_{h,i}=\{Q_{h,i,j}:j\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\}\), \(T^V_{h,j}=\{Q_{h,i,j}:i\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\}\). Thus there are nine \(P\)-triples and eighteen \(T\)-triples.

## Proof
**1. The exhibited intersections.** The conics in \(P_{0,0}\) all pass through \([1:1:0]\) with tangent \(Z=0\). Their exact three-equation projective intersection is that singleton: on the chart \(Y=1\) their reduced support is \(Z=0,X=1\), and the other charts add no point. The group generated by coordinate permutations and independent cubic-root coordinate scalings sends this to all nine \(P\)-triples.

For \(T^U_{0,0}=\{Q_{0,0,j}:j=0,1,2\}\), all three equations coincide on \(Y=0\), where they reduce to \(X^2-\alpha^2XZ-2\alpha Z^2=0\). This equation has discriminant \(\alpha^4+8\alpha=10\alpha\ne0\); its two zeros are distinct. Exact Gröbner reduction of the three equations on \(Z=1\) yields the ideal \((Y,X^2-\alpha^2X-2\alpha)\), and the remaining charts introduce no additional projective points. The \(54\)-element symmetry group sends this triple to all eighteen \(T\)-triples. Each \(T\)-triple meets conics from three distinct \(P\)-classes. Proposition 4.7 of Dimca–Ilardi–Malara–Pokora therefore ensures that all pairwise intersections at these points are transverse. For the \(P\)-triples that proposition and their Theorem 4.10 establish the three common tangencies and local type \(J_{2,0}\).

**2. Exhaustiveness by two algebraic reductions.** The symmetry group \(G\cong(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^2\rtimes S_3\) acts on the \(27\) conics, hence on all \(2925\) unordered triples. Exhaustive orbit generation from the four explicit generators \(X\mapsto\omega X\), \(Y\mapsto\omega Y\), \((X,Y,Z)\mapsto(Y,Z,X)\), and \((X,Y,Z)\mapsto(Y,X,Z)\) gives exactly \(65\) triple orbits. Their cardinalities sum to \(2925\). Both the generators and the resulting \(65\) orbit representatives are computed independently of any numerical point enumeration by `verify_fermat_conic_incidence.py`.

For a representative triple and a prime \(p\), reduce its three homogeneous quadratic equations modulo a chosen prime of the integer ring \(\mathbb Z[\alpha,\omega]\). On each of the three standard affine charts, compute the Gröbner basis of the triple ideal in \(\mathbb F_p[u,v]\). If all three bases equal \((1)\), the three reduced conics have **no common projective point even over the algebraic closure**. By properness of projective space, a common complex solution of equations with algebraic-integer coefficients would, after passing to a finite extension and rescaling coordinates in its valuation ring, reduce to a common projective solution at **every** such prime. Therefore one such empty reduction rigorously rules out common points in characteristic zero.

Use the two valid residue specializations
\[
(p,\bar\alpha,\bar\omega)=(109,57,45),\qquad(127,32,19).
\]
In each specialization the listed values satisfy \(\bar\alpha^3=2\) and \(\bar\omega^2+\bar\omega+1=0\), and the \(27\) conics remain distinct. At \(p=109\) five of the \(65\) orbit representatives have nonunit triple ideals on some chart; at \(p=127\) four do. The intersection of these two lists contains **only two orbits**, represented by the index triples \((0,1,2)\) and \((0,4,8)\), indexed lexicographically by \((h,i,j)\). These orbits have sizes \(18\) and \(9\) and are exactly the \(T\)- and \(P\)-families verified in Step 1. The remaining \(63\) orbits are excluded by a unit Gröbner basis at one of the primes. There is no inference from the absence of \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational points: the unit ideals exclude solutions in every extension.

**3. No higher concurrence.** Every unordered pair of conics occurs in at most one of the \(27\) concurrent triples just classified. Indeed, triples of either type use a fixed cyclic index \(h\); inside each fixed \(h\), a pair sharing one of \(i,j\) belongs to a unique \(T\)-triple, and a pair varying both indices belongs to at most one diagonal \(P\)-triple. A fourfold intersection would put the same pair in at least two distinct concurrent triples, contradicting this uniqueness. Similarly, none of the \(9+36\) triple points coincides with another triple point.

**4. The singularity census.** There are \(\binom{27}{2}=351\) pairs. The nine \(P\)-classes account for \(9\binom32=27\) within-class pairs and \(324\) cross-class pairs. By the cited pairwise intersection theorem, each within-class pair has two additional transverse intersection points, and each cross-class pair has four transverse intersection points. Before identifying nodes shared by three branches this gives \(27\cdot2+324\cdot4=1350\) pairwise transverse incidences. The \(36\) ordinary triple points each contribute three incidences but constitute only one point, setting aside all \(3\cdot36=108\) incidences at triple points, so the number of distinct **nodes** is \(1350-3\cdot36=1242\) after reserving the triple incidences. (The remaining \(36\) triple locations are counted separately.) There are \(9\) further common-tangent triple points. Thus the number of singular points is \(1242+36+9=1287\). Ordinary nodes, ordinary triple points and \(J_{2,0}\) points have Tjurina numbers \(1,4,10\), respectively (the last from Dimca–Ilardi–Malara–Pokora, Theorem 4.10); hence \(\tau=1242+4(36)+10(9)=1476\). As a check on multiplicities, \(1242+3(36)+6(9)=1404=4\binom{27}{2}\), as required by Bézout.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_fermat_conic_incidence.py` with a working SymPy installation. Its successful terminating line is

`FERMAT_27_CONICS_COMPLETE_PASS 2925 65 5 4 2 9 18 36 1242`

The program constructs all \(27\) explicit conic coefficient vectors at each prime, checks the generators act as permutations, enumerates \(65\) orbits covering all \(2925\) unordered triples, computes unit/nonunit three-chart Gröbner bases for both primes, verifies that the positive-orbit intersection is precisely the two displayed representatives, verifies the explicit combinatorial list of \(27\) triple sets and pair uniqueness, and checks exact characteristic-zero Gröbner bases for the two positive representatives. Because every triple belongs to an explicitly enumerated symmetry orbit, the finite orbit search is exhaustive for the infinite-field claim; the two reductions are algebraic certificates, not sample-point searches.

## Relationship to prior work
Dimca, Ilardi, Malara and Pokora identify the \(27\) conics, their nine tangency classes and the pairwise intersections (Proposition 4.7), and analyze one three-conic tangency arrangement (Theorem 4.10). They note that the \(\binom{27}{3}\) possibilities are elaborate. Toft and Moe subsequently construct and describe the two kinds of concurrent triples via collinear sextactic-point grids (Theorems 4.10 and 4.13). The positive triple families and their tangencies are therefore **not** claimed as new. The added claim is the **exclusion of every other triple**, absence of fourfold concurrence and resulting exact entire-arrangement singularity census, using the two-prime complete orbit certificate.

## Limitations
The result concerns only the union of the conics over \(\mathbb C\), not the arrangement after adjoining the original cubic. It does not classify freeness of arbitrary subarrangements or provide a closed-form symbolic certificate over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha,\omega)\) for each of the \(63\) excluded orbit types; instead it uses two exact good-prime reductions and the valid projective-specialization argument. Unindexed earlier global incidence calculations could affect priority. The review is not independent mathematical validation.
