# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The main theorem specifies the ring, the odd-prime restriction, the ordered representation count and all valuation conventions. The inverse-sum identity converts the convolution fiber into a quadratic congruence. Its discriminant roots are counted, including zero and even positive valuations, by an explicitly proved Hensel argument. The unit-first-coordinate and nonunit-first-coordinate cases give independent sumset counts. Energy is derived by an ordered collision calculation, using the exact count of factorizations with zero product modulo a prime power. The proof treats all odd primes and every exponent at least two, with elementary Chinese-remainder factorization for odd composite moduli.

The complete stand-alone checker evaluates all target fibers with integer arithmetic for 13 prime-power moduli and evaluates the factorization for nine composites. The checker is corroborative; the general assertion is analytic. The exact fourth-moment normalization is a two-parameter average of **unnormalized** complete Kloosterman sums and is proved by character orthogonality, rather than by numerical Fourier approximation.

## Originality
**PASS relative to inspected prior statements, with remaining priority risk.** Iosevich–Li–Yu, arXiv:2609.25404, Section 8, supplies general Fourier-moment/additive-energy inequalities, not a modular-hyperbola two-sum classification. Iosevich–Murphy–Pakianathan, arXiv:1405.7657, examine the same hyperbola through Fourier decay and the Kloosterman–Salem number, not exact ordered convolution fibers. The published finite-field hyperbola result already gives the exponent-one energy; that specialization is expressly excluded from novelty. The published prime-power hyperbola digit-tree theorem is about orthogonal characters, not pair-sum multiplicities. Gong–Veys–Wan, arXiv:1306.4104, calculate fixed-parameter one-variable Kloosterman moments; those might indirectly imply an energy corollary, but they do not provide the positionwise inverse-graph convolution law or sumset cardinalities. A published finite-field fifth-power graph two-sum classification concerns a different algebraic curve.

Residual risk: an unindexed historical modular-hyperbola sumset calculation, or a reformulation of high Kloosterman moments through convolution, could overlap the energy or some of the fiber results. Search failure is not a proof of publication priority. The decisive positive novelty comparison is the **complete prime-power target-by-target representation law together with exact two-sumset growth**, not the elementary prime-field energy specialization or a generic Fourier fourth-moment identity.

## Value
**PASS.** The modular hyperbola is a natural Fourier test surface for Kloosterman sums, and the additive convolution is a natural quantity for sumset growth and restriction theory. Zero divisors cause new depth-dependent collision fibers that are absent over fields. The theorem gives a complete exact solution across an infinite family, with a precise p-adic square-root mechanism and closed sumset and energy formulas. For example, the distinct sums at modulus \(27\) number \(57\), whereas at modulus \(125\) they number \(3009\); neither count is provided by general Fourier cancellation bounds. The result does not claim a sharp unrestricted weighted extension norm.
