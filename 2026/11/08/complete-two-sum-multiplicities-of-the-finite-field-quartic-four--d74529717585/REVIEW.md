# Scientific review: complete quartic two-sum multiplicities

## Correctness — PASS
The pointwise quadratic-root reduction is an exact bijection. At zero first-coordinate sum, the fourth-power homomorphism has precisely the stated fiber size; at every nonzero first-coordinate sum, evenness forces exactly one potentially odd target. Two exact equations, obtained from total representation mass and a quadratic-character collision count, uniquely determine all remaining positive multiplicity levels. This is a proof over *all* finite fields of characteristic greater than three, independently of the exhaustive finite-field checker. The finite computation corroborates 46 small field cases and cannot establish the universal quantifiers on its own.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources
The strongest relevant prior publication, *Exact additive energy of the finite-field quartic graph*, already contains the quartic collision identity and second-moment aggregate. Both are expressly excluded from the novelty claim. That prior result does not imply the stated pointwise odd-fiber count or the \(\chi(-6)\)-dependent exact multiplicity and sumset formulas without additional analysis. *Complete two-sum spectrum of the finite-field fifth-power graph* uses a very similar strategy for a different curve and is credited for that methodological precedent; its formulas do not specialize to the quartic graph. No directly equivalent quartic two-sum spectrum was found by targeted searches. An older unindexed calculation remains a genuine priority risk, and the contribution is a focused exact classification rather than a fundamentally new technique.

## Value — PASS
The exact multiplicity spectrum is a natural Fourier-convolution invariant, and its character \(\chi(-6)\) is invisible to the already known energy. It supplies exact sumset size and every collision-power moment, distinguishing different arithmetic regimes of the same concrete curve. This is a complete structural classification across an infinite family of finite fields, not a single numerical table or an arbitrary parameter slice. Its modest proof length limits the scale of the advance but does not remove the information supplied by the previously unrecorded full distribution.
