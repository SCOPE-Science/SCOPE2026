# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The main proof is constructive at the level of atomic pair types. In an odd-order group, taking square roots is a permutation; square, inverse, and square root of a nonidentity element are equal precisely at order three, otherwise they are distinct. This gives a complete two-variable pair-type count table indexed by identity, nonidentity \(3\)-torsion, and the rest. The standard counting-formula induction then proves every quantifier rank; explicit two-variable counting sentences recover both invariants. The companion checker verifies thirteen small group examples, including negative cases. Those checks are **not** used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Checked general primary sources characterize \(\mathrm C^2\) via pair types or regular-biregular models. Their statements cover the general methodology but not an explicit odd-group collapse to just order and nonidentity \(3\)-torsion count. The closest group-focused source on finite abelian groups studies **functional** addition/inverse and unrestricted first-order model checking; it does not give this claim. Accessible previous group findings concern counting-free width or narrower classes. Residual risk: a specialist earlier statement or an unindexed equivalence may exist; the general pair-invariant characterization makes priority and nontriviality especially important to check independently.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is an all-orders classification for a natural algebraic class, not an arbitrary finite threshold. The proof identifies the precise effect of odd-order squaring and the relational signature on counting logic. In particular, \(C_{25}\) and \(C_5^2\) become equivalent despite different exponents, and the abelian \(C_3^3\) and nonabelian \(\mathrm{UT}_3(\mathbb F_3)\) become equivalent despite different commutativity. The functional-signature distinction is essential rather than cosmetic.

## Literature and limitations

Primary comparisons: Kopczyński–Tan, arXiv:1304.0829, introduction and §2; Kiefer–Schweitzer–Selman, arXiv:1503.08792, §2 and §5; Bova–Martin, DOI:10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2015.41, introduction and §2. The theorem does not treat even-order groups, multiplication function symbols, or \(\mathrm C^k\) for \(k\ge3\). Limited literature inspection cannot establish global historical novelty.
