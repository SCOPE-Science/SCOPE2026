# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The icosidodecahedral graph is the line graph of the cubic dodecahedron. Its 60 interaction edges partition into 20 edge-disjoint triangles, with each spin in two local triangle fields; this yields an exact local-energy bound with no missing terms. All lower bounds are attainable. The zero-field state characterization is equivalent to a nonmonochromatic binary edge labelling at each cubic vertex. An inclusion-exclusion product over equivalence classes proves an exact finite coefficient identity, evaluated independently by two complete C++17 enumerations with different graph labelings and methods. The 111,376 saturation states are exactly all 20-vertex dodecahedral matchings. The code checks every coefficient, not only totals. No infinite-family, thermodynamic-limit or approximate claim is inferred from finite computation.

## Originality — PASS on the inspected evidence, with stated priority risk

The directly relevant 2007 primary full text supplies 36 dodecahedral perfect matchings for the strict positive-field up-up-down Ising plateau; this is already established and not claimed as new. It does not provide the zero-field 3,251,250-state magnetization polynomial or the 111,376-state saturation-field matching distribution. General finite-cluster Ising methods, the Platonic-polyhedron results and planar triangular-cluster counts do not imply these explicit numerical coefficients without a separate exact graph-specific evaluation. The distinct 3,600,000 proton-ice edge-*orientation* count on the dodecahedron does not enumerate binary edge labels with the same endpoint sign. A specialist unindexed density-of-states table or equivalent binary edge-colouring computation may precede this result; no universal novelty certification is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The spherical kagome nanomagnet has a physically motivated 30-spin geometry, while the exact residual entropy at zero field and the field-singular state counts are natural zero-temperature invariants. The complete magnetization-resolved polynomial quantifies how more than three million equally optimal states collapse to only 36 under an infinitesimal symmetry-breaking field, and the all-matching count identifies the highly degenerate saturation boundary. The ten noncentral polynomial coefficients are not consequences of the known 36 plateau count. This is a nontrivial natural finite enumeration, not an arbitrary subcase or a physical thermodynamic-phase-transition claim.
