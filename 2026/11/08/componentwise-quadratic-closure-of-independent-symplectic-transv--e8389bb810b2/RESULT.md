# Componentwise quadratic closure of independent symplectic transvections

## Finding
Let \((V,\omega)\) be a finite-dimensional nondegenerate alternating space over a field \(K\) with \(\operatorname{char}K\ne2\). Fix linearly independent nonzero vectors \(u_1,\ldots,u_k\) and the rank-one, square-zero symplectic endomorphisms
\[
T_u(v)=\omega(u,v)u.
\]
Let \(\Gamma\) have vertex set \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and an edge \(ij\) precisely when \(\omega(u_i,u_j)\ne0\). For a component \(C\), let \(W_C=\operatorname{span}\{u_i:i\in C\}\) and \(d_C=|C|\). Define
\[
Q(W)=\operatorname{span}_K\{X_{a,b}:a,b\in W\},\qquad
X_{a,b}(v)=\omega(a,v)b+\omega(b,v)a.
\]
**Componentwise closure theorem.** The Lie subalgebra generated by the \(T_{u_i}\) is *exactly* the internal direct sum
\[
\mathfrak l=\bigoplus_{C\in\pi_0(\Gamma)} Q(W_C),
\qquad
\dim_K\mathfrak l=\sum_{C\in\pi_0(\Gamma)}\frac{d_C(d_C+1)}2.
\]
This applies even when the alternating form restricted to some \(W_C\) is degenerate. In particular, connectedness is equivalent to the full quadratic closure \(Q(\operatorname{span}\{u_i\})\).

If \(k=\dim V=2m\), every \(d_C\) is even. The complete possible *dimension spectrum* over all vector bases of \(V\) is
\[
\left\{\sum_{j=1}^r m_j(2m_j+1):
 m_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1},\ \sum_{j=1}^r m_j=m\right\}.
\]
Every value in this set is attained over **every** field of characteristic different from two. The lower and upper extremes are \(3m\) and \(m(2m+1)\); full generation of \(\mathfrak{sp}(V,\omega)\) occurs exactly for connected \(\Gamma\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient space is symplectic of dimension \(2m\ge2\); the field need not be algebraically closed or finite. The \(k\ge1\) selected vectors must be linearly independent. The operators are Lie generators under commutators, not generators of the associated discrete group. The subspaces \(W_C\) may be singular until the full-basis specialization.

## Proof
**1. Symmetric tensors.** Nondegeneracy of \(\omega\) identifies \(V\) with its dual. Because two is invertible in \(K\), the maps \(X_{a,b}\) induce a linear isomorphism
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{sp}(V,\omega).
\]
Indeed, the endomorphism condition \(\omega(Ax,y)+\omega(x,Ay)=0\) identifies \(\omega(A\cdot,\cdot)\) with a symmetric bilinear form. Under this isomorphism \(T_u=\tfrac12 X_{u,u}\), and for arbitrary \(a,b,c\), direct composition gives
\[
[X_{a,b},X_{c,c}]=2\omega(a,c)X_{b,c}+2\omega(b,c)X_{a,c}.
\]
In particular, \([X_{a,a},X_{b,b}]=4\omega(a,b)X_{a,b}\). More generally, the commutator of any two symmetric-tensor maps with all arguments in a subspace \(W\) remains in \(Q(W)\). Thus \(Q(W)\) is a Lie subalgebra, even for degenerate restricted forms, and \(\dim Q(W)=\dim W(\dim W+1)/2\).

**2. Connected-component induction.** Fix a connected component \(C\) and choose a vertex order so each new vertex \(v\) is adjacent to some earlier vertex \(w\). Suppose the generators already contain \(Q(W)\), where \(W\) spans the earlier vertices. Since \(\omega(w,v)\ne0\), the first bracket identity yields \(X_{w,v}\). Then for any \(a\in W\),
\[
[X_{a,w},X_{v,v}]
=2\omega(a,v)X_{w,v}+2\omega(w,v)X_{a,v}.
\]
Hence \(X_{a,v}\) is generated. Together with \(X_{v,v}\) and \(Q(W)\), these span \(Q(W+Kv)\). This inductively yields \(Q(W_C)\), starting with the single generator \(T_{u_i}\).

If \(C\ne D\), every \(\omega(u_i,u_j)=0\) for \(i\in C,j\in D\); by bilinearity \(W_C\perp W_D\). Their corresponding \(Q(W_C)\) and \(Q(W_D)\) commute. Since the original \(u_i\) are independent, \(\sum_CW_C\) is a vector-space direct sum, and the summands \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(W_C)\subset\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)\) are linearly independent. The generated Lie algebra is therefore precisely the stated internal direct sum. The dimension formula follows.

**3. Full-basis dimension spectrum.** If the \(u_i\) form a basis of \(V\), then \(V=\bigoplus_CW_C\) is an orthogonal direct sum. Since \(\omega\) is nondegenerate on \(V\), its restriction to each \(W_C\) is nondegenerate. Hence \(d_C=2m_C\) is even. Conversely, for every partition \(m=m_1+\cdots+m_r\), construct a block-diagonal alternating Gram matrix, each block of size \(2m_j\) with entries \(\omega(e_i,e_{i+1})=1\) between consecutive basis vectors and zero for nonconsecutive pairs. Each block is nondegenerate: its Pfaffian equals \(1\), by the unique consecutive perfect matching. Its pairing graph is a path, so the components have sizes \(2m_j\). All nondegenerate alternating forms in a fixed even dimension over \(K\) are isometric, so this yields a basis of the given symplectic space. The preceding dimension formula proves the spectrum and attainability.

The minimum comes from the partition into \(m\) ones, giving \(3m\). Merging two positive parts strictly increases \(m_j(2m_j+1)\), hence the maximum is attained by the single part \(m\), giving \(m(2m+1)\). The map \(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\to\mathfrak{sp}(V)\) shows that \(\dim\mathfrak{sp}(V)=m(2m+1)\), and equality holds precisely when \(\Gamma\) has one component.

## Verification
The independent standard-library checker `verify.py` constructs each endomorphism \(T_{u_i}\) as a concrete matrix over a finite prime field. It closes their span under *actual matrix commutators*, computes ranks by modular elimination, and compares against graph-component dimensions. It exhausts all nondegenerate alternating four-by-four matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\), samples additional matrices over \(\mathbb F_5\) and dimensions six/eight in characteristics three, five and seven, and tests all initial independent subsets. It also checks all even path Gram forms in sizes two through eight. The recorded result is `verify_output.txt`; the checker is reproducible with `python3 verify.py`. Finite verification is not a proof of the universal theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
In 't Panhuis, Postma and Roozemond (arXiv:0705.2332, Theorem 6.10) already provide a path-shaped set of \(2m\) extremal transvection generators for \(\mathfrak{sp}_{2m}\) over algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from two, and explicitly discuss minimal extremal generating sets. **Neither the path construction nor minimal generator count is claimed here as new.** Cuypers and Fleischmann (arXiv:1707.02095, Section 3, especially Example 3.8) establish the geometric connection to rank-one symplectic extremal elements and treat a degenerate three-space. Draisma and in 't Panhuis (2008) study graph-parametrized abstract extremal Lie algebras. Chistopolskaya (arXiv:1908.04065) studies two nilpotent generators without rank-one restrictions. The 2026 paper by Choudhary, Kishnani and Singh (arXiv:2610.35537) establishes two-generation of Chevalley Lie algebras over finite fields, motivating attention to restricted-generator models.

The present statement concerns **every** independent configuration of rank-one symplectic generators, including singular spans and arbitrary pairing graphs, and gives its complete Lie closure and exact dimension; the full-basis dimension spectrum is a consequence. These general all-graph conclusions were not located in the inspected prior results; possible equivalent descriptions in older extremal-element literature remain an originality risk.

## Limitations
Characteristic two is excluded because the symmetric-tensor identification and bracket isolation use division by two. The theorem does not classify linearly dependent generating families (adding a dependent vector can join pairing components), nor the corresponding discrete transvection groups. A proof of independent research priority is not supplied: the historical literature on extremal Lie generators is broad. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
