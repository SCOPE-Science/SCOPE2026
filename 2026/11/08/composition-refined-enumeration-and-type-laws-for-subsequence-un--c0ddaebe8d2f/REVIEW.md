# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** A complete-alphabet greedy-arch factorization is proved to equal the scattered-subsequence universality index by an explicit absent-subsequence construction. Inclusion-exclusion counts all arches and all incomplete remainders exactly, yielding the multivariate rational identity without convergence assumptions. Independent coefficient differentiation establishes the binary first-moment expression; a positive expansion establishes the maximal-index counts. An exchangeable fixed-type coupling and coupon-collector renewal law prove the positive-frequency limit. Exhaustive integer checks cover 48,069 words and 1,586 coefficient cells.

**Originality: PASS, qualified.** Barker et al. (2020) establish the universality framework; Adamson (2023) gives unweighted length/index counting algorithms. Neither inspected full source states the alphabet-composition-refined rational function or a fixed-type density law. Fazekas et al. (2026) concern existential/universal indices within binary Parikh-matrix classes and approximate pattern-containment probabilities, not the complete distribution across words in a fixed ordinary Parikh class. The closest public research-record match provides the unweighted binary specialization; it does not imply individual composition coefficients. The standard arch theorem itself is attributed, not claimed as new. Potential equivalent formulations in unindexed literature remain a residual risk.

**Value: PASS.** Constant-composition languages arise naturally when sequence inventories are fixed. The theorem supplies the full spectrum of a natural word statistic for every alphabet size, a positive closed formula for binary minority-limited extremals, exact conditional means, and a general type-class law that is not specified by length-only enumeration. It concerns all compositions and indices, not arbitrary isolated parameters.

The positive-frequency assumption is essential for the stated renewal argument; sparse types and distributional fluctuations remain outside scope.
