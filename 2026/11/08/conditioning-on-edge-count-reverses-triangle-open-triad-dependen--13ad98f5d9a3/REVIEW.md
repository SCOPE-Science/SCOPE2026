# Scientific assessment: triangle and induced open-triad counts

## Correctness — PASS
The claim covers all integers \(n\geq4\), all feasible \(m\), and all independent-edge probabilities \(0<p<1\), with induced rather than non-induced open triads. The three ordered-pair overlap types are exhaustive. The fixed-edge joint probabilities are exact finite-population probabilities, and their algebraic factorization has a strictly positive denominator and an explicitly positive residual factor in the nondegenerate regime. The independent-edge formula is a separate overlap calculation, not an unwarranted substitution \(m=p\binom n2\). The derivation includes degenerate boundaries, a unique independent-edge zero, and correctly scoped asymptotics. A standalone standard-library checker made 14,456 exact rational comparisons, exhaustively enumerating the sample space for \(n=4,5,6\). This finite checking is corroboration, not the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty
Searches compared open/closed triads, induced three-vertex path counts, triangle–wedge covariance, and the \(G(n,p)\)-to-\(G(n,m)\) conditioning relationship. Alm–Janson–Linusson, arXiv:0906.0720, study directed reachability events; their conditional-covariance mechanism is an antecedent, but their statements do not imply the asserted induced-triad formula or strict fixed-edge sign. Kunegis, arXiv:1402.5835, supplies a general overlap-counting method and particular \(G(n,1/2)\) covariances for non-induced subgraphs, but not the all-density, all-finite-parameter sign comparison. Goldschmidt–Griffiths–Scott, arXiv:1902.06830, control path and triangle deviations in \(G(n,m)\), rather than proving a finite exact sign classification of induced open triads. Janson's 1990 general limit theorem is relevant but was accessible here only at abstract level. It creates an acknowledged risk that an older formulation could subsume part of the result. No publication-priority or independent novelty certification is asserted.

## Value — PASS
The first nontrivial undirected motif census separates open triads from triangles. The universal strictly negative within-edge-count dependence contrasts sharply with the independent-edge positive-to-negative phase transition, and the variance decomposition explains why fixing the number of edges changes the answer rather than merely the precision of the estimate. Exact finite-size thresholds and a different asymptotic order of covariance matter to motif-based calibration and model comparison. The assertion is a complete natural classification, not an arbitrary numerical slice or a recomputation of a known single-model moment.

## Scientific limitations
The theorem concerns covariance of induced three-vertex motif counts, not independence, negative association, fixed-degree ensembles, or covariance of a clustering ratio. The proof is elementary overlap enumeration plus a consequential all-parameter sign factorization. Generic subgraph covariance frameworks and historically inaccessible full texts leave a genuine originality risk. Numerical checks do not supply an infinite proof or an external audit.
