# Mathematical review: cone-boundary and volume affine least-squares projectors

## Correctness — PASS
The general argument is an explicit covariance-kernel computation followed by Jensen's inequality for the symmetric absolute-value function. The proof of strictness separately establishes the required positive kernel-sign mass at a norming boundary point by a supporting-functional/Cauchy--Schwarz estimate. No regularity of the convex body's boundary is assumed; the cone probability is defined by radial disintegration. The exact cube formulas are established by the sum-of-uniforms positive-part identity, and strict vertex norming follows from a positive-measure sign change on a facet. The asymptotic difference follows from an explicit one-dimensional radial-moment difference, the central limit theorem with uniform integrability, and an \(O(d^{-1})\) control of the kink remainder. The available checker independently performs exact-rational integrations and moment checks. Finite tests are supporting checks, not an infinite-dimensional certificate.

## Originality — PASS, with disclosed scope
Primary literature examined includes Hangelbroek, Narcowich, Sun and Ward, *Kernel Approximation on Manifolds II*, arXiv:1005.2424 (first posted May 13, 2010), whose Theorem 5.1 concerns boundedness for localized kernel spaces on smooth manifolds, and Trefethen, *Multivariate polynomial approximation in the hypercube*, arXiv:1608.02216 (first posted August 7, 2016), whose Theorem 4.2 concerns rates of polynomial approximation for analytic functions. Białas-Cież, Kenne, Sommariva and Vianello, *Evaluating Lebesgue constants by Chebyshev polynomial meshes on cube, simplex and ball*, arXiv:2311.18656 (2023), treat numerical bounds for finite-point projection designs; their Proposition 1 and least-squares examples do not cover the two continuous sampling measures or the sharp cube gap. None of these inspected primary results implies the present radial-cone versus volume operator-norm inequality, nor the exact cube sampling formulas and \(d^{-1/2}\) gap. An earlier indexed exact simplex affine-projector result concerns a noncentrally-symmetric domain, and the prior boundary-simplex calculation likewise does not imply the centrally symmetric cone-measure theorem. Searches included cone-volume, affine-projection, exact hypercube formula and equivalent sampling terms; no dominating result was identified in accessible material. An unindexed equivalent remains possible. The search does not establish absolute uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem compares two canonical data-acquisition measures for the *same affine regression space* over every centrally symmetric convex body, yielding a strict, dimension-independent directional conclusion rather than a catalogue of isolated constants. On cubes, where cone and physical surface sampling coincide, it also computes the exact all-dimensional stability constants, all norming points and a sharp nonzero scaled asymptotic gap. These quantities directly control uniform stability and near-best-approximation factors for boundary versus interior least squares. The general comparison is mathematically stronger than a one-off cube integral and is not simply a normalization substitution: the covariance rescaling and radial randomization compete, requiring the convexity argument.

## Boundaries of the claim
The result does not compare ordinary Hausdorff surface measure on every symmetric convex body, which generally differs from cone probability. It does not apply to noncentrally-symmetric bodies or higher polynomial degrees. Prior work on least-squares stability is substantial; comparison of the primary results listed above and indexed related claims supports, but cannot certify, novelty beyond those sources.
