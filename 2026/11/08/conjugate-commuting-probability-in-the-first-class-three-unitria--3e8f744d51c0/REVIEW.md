# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof checks all three independent matrix-commutation equations and the effect of conjugating only one input. The scalar correction is linear, so its image is either zero or the entire field; the seven exhaustive primary-coordinate cases yield the exact count. The independent commuting-pair count checks the ordinary probability, and the difference factorization is positive for every prime power. Explicit exhaustions over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_4\) agree, including direct group multiplication in characteristic two.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

The 2007 Britnell–Wildon paper proves a matching theorem for commuting conjugacy classes under cyclic quotients, rather than an exact density for unitriangular groups. The 2011 Blackburn–Britnell–Wildon paper defines the exact relaxed probability and treats general properties and symmetric groups, not an explicit \(\operatorname{UT}_4\) formula. The 2020 Kaur–Sharma–Singh computation studies ordinary commuting tuples and their simultaneous conjugacy orbits for unitriangular matrices; those are not the Boolean event that a given pair has *some* commuting conjugates. Direct comparison of these distinct statements, and targeted searches of accessible mathematical records, produced no dominating formula. A broader unidentified group-theoretic enumeration could still imply this result. A conflicting ordinary commuting-probability entry appears in the 2020 paper: its formula gives 53 conjugacy classes for \(q=3\), whereas direct literal multiplication and centralizer enumeration give 57. This is a source-comparison risk explicitly recorded in the finding, not evidence of novelty. The full text of the 2026 paper on the ordinary commuting graph of \(\operatorname{UT}(4;p)\) could not be obtained; the available abstract does not claim the present statistic.

## Value — PASS

The 2011 framework asks for concrete understanding of conjugacy-relaxed commuting probabilities. The \(4\times4\) unitriangular group is the first member of this family with nilpotency class three; smaller nonabelian members have class two and hence no difference between these probabilities. The exact formula establishes a strict difference for every characteristic and reveals a nontrivial asymptotic separation by a factor of order \(q\). This is a natural infinite-family group invariant, not an arbitrary finite table.

## Limits

The theorem does not extend automatically to \(\operatorname{UT}_n\) with \(n\ge5\). It is a self-review only, with provisional priority and no independent audit.
