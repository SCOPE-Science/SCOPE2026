# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The normalization is Parseval, so deleting three atoms subtracts a rank-three positive operator with factor \(3/N\). Rephasing converts each erased three-column Gram matrix into the stated real cosine kernel. The consecutive Gram matrix has the displayed Perron eigenvalue. For \(N\ge8\), cyclic distances and the lower bound \(-1/3\) yield an entrywise nonnegative Perron majorant; equality forces exactly two adjacent edges, hence consecutive erased labels. Orders six and seven are established separately by a complete gap-pattern classification and explicit numerical-independent trigonometric inequalities. Vandermonde independence ensures strict positivity for all remaining frames. The finite checker is supporting evidence, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic limits
The original harmonic-frame source (Marshall–Waldron, arXiv:1611.07121v1) studies construction and equivalence counts; Cheng–Lv–Sun characterize uniform-subframe-bound frames; Batenkov and coauthors give clustered-node singular-value bounds. None of their inspected statements gives the fixed three-row, exactly-three-erasure best lower bound or identifies its unique masks. The closest published exact two-row erasure theorem explicitly excludes three rows; the closest three-row Riesz theorem optimizes the largest/smallest *ratio*, not the largest eigenvalue (and obtains nearly equilateral, not consecutive, triples); the published full-spark classification determines only singularity. These implications were compared at the mathematical-statement level. A historically older specialized cyclic frame stability theorem could still exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem solves a natural adversarial-loss stability problem in a standard three-dimensional tight harmonic frame: it gives an exact all-order quantitative frame bound, a complete extremizer classification, and the nontrivial discrete small-order exceptions needed for its proof. It advances from the two-row erasure formula to the first three-row case, which requires genuinely different three-eigenvalue geometry. The result does not claim an arbitrary-erasures theorem or a universal optimal decoding method.
