# Consecutive mixed-dimensional graph Čech wedges on the Frucht graph

## Finding
Let \(F\) be the standard cubic Frucht graph on twelve vertices, with unit-length edges and the intrinsic shortest-path metric on its geometric realization. Define \(\mathcal N(F,r)\) to be the nerve of the twelve **closed** balls of radius \(r/2\) centered at the graph vertices. There are two consecutive nontrivial homotopy types:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal N(F,r)\simeq (\bigvee^{2}S^2)\vee (\bigvee^{2}S^3)\quad (3\le r<4),}
\]
\[
\boxed{\mathcal N(F,r)\simeq (\bigvee^{2}S^3)\vee S^4\quad (4\le r<5).}
\]
In particular, the former has reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z^2\) in each of degrees two and three, and the latter \(\mathbb Z^2\) in degree three and \(\mathbb Z\) in degree four; every other reduced integral homology group vanishes. Both classifications determine full nonequivariant homotopy types, not only Betti ranks.

## Assumptions and scope
The Frucht graph is the simple undirected graph with vertex set \(\{0,\ldots,11\}\) and edge set given exactly by `EDGES` in `VERIFY.py`; the 18 ordered pairs are interpreted as unordered edges. The graph is cubic and connected and has diameter four. The Čech nerve is formed in the *geometric realization* of the graph; balls are not restricted to its discrete vertex set, and the construction must not be confused with the graph's Vietoris–Rips or independence complex. The scale parameter is real and inclusive. The claim covers \(3\le r<5\), with different types on the two specified half-open intervals. No assertion is made about persistence maps or other ranges of the filtration.

## Proof
**Exact face models.** For an integer scale \(r=2k\), graph-ball Čech faces are exactly all subsets of \(N_k[v]=\{w:d(v,w)\le k\}\), over vertices \(v\). For an odd integer scale \(r=2k+1\), they are exactly all subsets of \(N_k[u]\cup N_k[v]\), over edges \(uv\). This is Adams–Shukla–Singh, Lemma 2.2, based on moving a common-ball intersection to a graph vertex or edge midpoint. The same lemma gives \(\mathcal N(F,r)=\mathcal N(F,\lfloor r\rfloor)\) for real \(r\). For scale three, take the 18 edge-union generating sets with \(k=1\). Exactly 17 of them are inclusion-maximal. Their downset contains 423 nonempty faces, with face vector \((12,64,141,138,59,9)\) in dimensions zero through five. For scale four, take the 12 closed distance-two neighborhoods; exactly ten are inclusion-maximal. Their downset contains 2,084 nonempty faces, with face vector \((12,66,219,459,580,449,219,67,12,1)\) in dimensions zero through nine. The attached standard-library checker independently enumerates all \(2^{12}\) subsets and tests containment in a generating neighborhood to verify both face sets.

**Complete acyclic Morse matchings.** Encode a face by its twelve-bit vertex mask. At successive vertices in descending label order, pair every still-unmatched face \(\sigma\) not containing the current vertex to \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\), when both faces are present and unmatched. Exclude the empty face. The scale-three matching has 209 matched face/coface pairs and exactly one critical zero-cell, two critical two-cells and two critical three-cells. Its 423 nonempty faces have 1,452 codimension-one incidences; orient matched incidences upward and all other incidences downward. An exhaustive topological sort visits every face, proving no closed gradient path exists. The scale-four matching similarly has 1,040 matched pairs and exactly one critical zero-cell, two critical three-cells and one critical four-cell; its 2,084 nonempty faces have 10,406 incidences, and the same exhaustive acyclicity check succeeds. Forman's theorem gives a finite CW model with precisely these critical cells in each case.

**Integral attaching maps.** Orient each simplex by increasing vertex labels. For every matched cover \(\sigma\subset\tau\), the incidence coefficient in the integral simplicial boundary is \(\pm1\). Eliminate each matched lower face by substituting the signed boundary of its matched upper face; a face matched as the upper member in the previous dimension has zero projection. Acyclicity makes this recursion terminate. `VERIFY.py` computes the resulting exact integer Morse boundary of every critical cell: every coefficient is zero at **both** scales. At scale three the one-point CW model has two two-cells forming \(S^2\vee S^2\), followed by two three-cells. Their attaching maps lie in \(\pi_2(S^2\vee S^2)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), where their degrees are precisely the two checked boundary columns. These are zero, so both attachments are nullhomotopic and split off two three-spheres. At scale four the same reasoning starts with two three-cells and attaches a four-cell: \(\pi_3(S^3\vee S^3)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and the complete zero integral boundary makes that attaching map nullhomotopic. This proves both displayed wedge decompositions *integrally*. Nothing is inferred from finite-field homology alone.

Run `python3 VERIFY.py` in Python 3 with only its standard library. It constructs the Frucht graph directly from the embedded complete edge list, obtains all distances by both breadth-first search and Floyd–Warshall, independently checks each vertex subset against Čech neighborhood witnesses, checks the claimed face vectors, all matched cover relations and every oriented Hasse incidence, and topologically sorts each matching. Its integral Morse boundary projection uses recursive signed unit pivots. As an additional cross-check it computes mod-two boundary ranks on the original simplicial complexes, and builds a second, differently ordered matching and its integral boundary for each scale. Successful output ends with `FRUCHT_INTRINSIC_CECH_MIXED_WEDGES_VERIFIED`. The exact output is embedded as `VERIFY_OUTPUT.json`. ## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh (arXiv:2212.05871) define these vertex-centered intrinsic Čech complexes and prove the neighborhood-facet rule; their main homotopy calculations concern hypercube graphs and do not classify the Frucht graph. The Frucht graph contains triangles and is asymmetric, so general high-girth graph-recovery statements do not imply the two higher-scale homotopy types. Recorded work on the *same Frucht graph* determines its independence complex and vertex-deletion links, while an exact calculation for five asymmetric cubic Hamiltonian graphs on twelve vertices determines their **Vietoris–Rips** complexes. Those are different face criteria: an independence face forbids original edges, a Rips face has bounded pairwise graph distances, and a Čech face requires a **single common intersection** of all closed graph balls. The Rips classification does not determine the present two nerve homotopy types. Published Čech calculations for hypercubes, Petersen, Pappus, dodecahedral and other symmetric graphs likewise have different graph hypotheses. Targeted literature and mathematical-record searches did not identify a theorem implying the displayed Frucht Čech classifications; this is not a proof of bibliographic uniqueness.

## Limitations
The two homotopy types concern exactly one graph and two adjacent scale intervals; no general theorem for all asymmetric cubic or Halin graphs is asserted, and the inclusion map between their integral homology groups has not been determined. The finite certificates exhaust the specified twelve-vertex complexes but cannot exclude an unindexed independent calculation. The exact interval endpoints depend on the explicitly stated closed-ball convention. A separate independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
