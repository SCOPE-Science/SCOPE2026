# Contact-refined punctual Hilbert series of the cusp and every added fat line

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field, write \(\mathbb L=[\mathbb A^1_k]\) in the Grothendieck ring of varieties, and let \(g=y^2-x^3\in k[[x,y]]\). For an ideal \(I\) of finite colength, its \(x\)-contact is
\[
j_x(I)=\operatorname{length}_k k[[x,y]]/(I+(x)).
\]
The contact-refined punctual Hilbert series of the ordinary cusp is exactly
\[
\boxed{G_g^x(Q,z)=1+Qz+(Q^2+Q^3)(\mathbb Lz+z^2)+\frac{(1+\mathbb L)Q^4z^2}{1-Q}.}
\]
For every integer \(u\geq0\), put \(C_u=\{x^u(y^2-x^3)=0\}\) as a plane formal germ. Its **full contact-refined motivic Hilbert series**, simultaneously for every colength and every contact, is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{n,j\geq0}[\operatorname{Hilb}_n(C_u,0)_{j_x=j}]Q^nz^j
=\frac{G_g^x(Q,(Q\mathbb L)^uz)}{\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{u-1}(1-Q^{i+1}\mathbb L^iz)}.}
\]
The product is \(1\) for \(u=0\); all equalities lie in \(K_0(\operatorname{Var}_k)[z][[Q]]\), with the punctual Hilbert schemes and strata understood as varieties with their reduced structures. In particular setting \(z=1\) gives an explicit two-parameter family of rational punctual Hilbert zeta functions for the nonreduced curves \(C_u\). No assertion of scheme-theoretic reducedness of their Hilbert schemes is made.

## Assumptions and scope

The ambient complete local ring is \(S=k[[x,y]]\). For any equation \(f\in(x,y)\), \(\operatorname{Hilb}_n(f,0)\) parametrizes ideals \(I\supset(f)\) with \(\dim_k S/I=n\). The constructible contact stratum \(\operatorname{Hilb}_n(f,0)_{j_x=j}\) is defined by the displayed length condition; its class is taken in the Grothendieck ring of \(k\)-varieties. The formula holds for all fields \(k\), all \(u\geq0\), all \(n,j\geq0\). The variable \(Q\) records colength and \(z\) records contact; \(\mathbb L\) is a motivic coefficient, not a numerical specialization. Both components of the finding are parts of one exact contact-refined classification.

## Proof

**1. Classify ideals on the cusp.** Parametrize \(S/(g)\) as
\[
R=k[[t^2,t^3]]\subset B=k[[t]],\qquad x=t^2,\quad y=t^3.
\]
Its nonunit elements are precisely the elements of \(t^2B\). Let \(I\subset R\) be a proper finite-colength ideal and let \(d\ge2\) be its least \(t\)-adic valuation. For a generator \(h=t^d v\in I\) with \(v\in B^\times\), multiplication by \(t^2B\subset R\) yields
\[
t^{d+2}B=h(t^2B)\subset I.
\]
Modulo this conductor tail, \(I\) therefore corresponds to a nonzero subspace of the two-dimensional space \(t^dB/t^{d+2}B\). If that subspace has dimension two, \(I=t^dB\) and \(\operatorname{length}(R/I)=d-1\). If it has dimension one, it is
\[
I_{[a:b]}=k(at^d+bt^{d+1})+t^{d+2}B
\]
for \([a:b]\in\mathbb P^1(k)\), and the colength is \(d\) when \(a\ne0\); its \(a=0\) boundary point is \(t^{d+1}B\), of colength \(d\). Thus at fixed \(n\ge2\) **every** colength-\(n\) ideal has the uniform presentation
\[
I_{[a:b]}=k(at^n+bt^{n+1})+t^{n+2}B,\qquad[a:b]\in\mathbb P^1.
\]
These are ideals because multiplying the displayed generator by any element of the maximal ideal \(t^2B\) lands in the tail. The family is an algebraic \(\mathbb P^1\) in the Grassmannian of one-dimensional subspaces of \(t^nB/t^{n+2}B\), and all geometric points of the reduced punctual Hilbert scheme lie in it. The containment conditions into and out of the two fixed tails are closed and hold on every geometric point, hence on the reduced base. The resulting Grassmannian map is inverse to the displayed universal family, so \(\operatorname{Hilb}_n(g,0)_{\rm red}\simeq\mathbb P^1\) for \(n\ge2\); at \(n=0,1\) it is a point.

**2. Compute the contacts.** The quotient modulo \(x=t^2\) is
\[
R/(x)\cong k[y]/(y^2).
\]
For \(n=0\), the contact is \(0\); for \(n=1\), it is \(1\). For \(n=2\), the image of \(I_{[a:b]}\) in \(R/(x)\) is generated by \(by\), so contact \(1\) occurs exactly on \(\{b\ne0\}\cong\mathbb A^1\), and contact \(2\) at \([1:0]\). For \(n=3\), the image is generated by \(ay\), so contact \(1\) occurs on \(\{a\ne0\}\cong\mathbb A^1\), and contact \(2\) at \([0:1]\). For all \(n\ge4\), both \(t^n\) and \(t^{n+1}\) are divisible by \(t^2\) in \(R\), as is the tail; every ideal has contact \(2\). Therefore the contact classes are precisely
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
n&\text{nonempty contact strata and their classes}\\\hline
0&j=0:\ 1\\
1&j=1:\ 1\\
2,3&j=1:\ \mathbb L,\quad j=2:\ 1\\
n\ge4&j=2:\ 1+\mathbb L.
\end{array}
\]
Summing this disjoint stratification proves the first boxed identity. As an independent boundary check, \(G_g^x(Q,1)=(1+\mathbb L Q^2)/(1-Q)\), the established unrefined punctual cusp Hilbert series.

**3. Attach an arbitrary fat line.** Corollary 6.8 in Huang--Jiang--Oblomkov, *Sheaves on nodal singularities I* (arXiv:2610.34676v1, 2026), proves for every plane germ \(g\) and integer \(u\ge0\) the identity
\[
\sum_{n,j}[\operatorname{Hilb}_n(\{x^ug=0\},0)_{j_x=j}]Q^nz^j
=\frac{G_g^x(Q,(Q\mathbb L)^uz)}{\prod_{i=0}^{u-1}(1-Q^{i+1}\mathbb L^iz)}.
\]
Its proof uses elementary modification \(I\mapsto(I:x)\) and does not require transversality. Applying this published, fully general theorem to the explicitly computed cusp input gives the second boxed identity without an additional assumption or an unproved recurrence. This step is **not** claimed as an independently new general theorem; its new input is the exact cusp contact calculation.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` independently enumerates invariant vector subspaces of finite truncated cusp rings over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), using reduced row-echelon forms rather than the normalization classification. It recovers every contact stratum for \(1\le n\le4\). In each field it obtains the counts \(1\), \((q,1)\), \((q,1)\), and \((q+1)\) for the respective colength/contact distributions. A second enumeration of the truncated plane ring over \(\mathbb F_2\) matches the predicted initial coefficients for \(u=1\), while 21 additional series specializations check the rational formula through degree \(12\). Running `python3 verify.py` produces `CERTIFICATE_OK`.

The finite enumerations are checks and are not used as an infinite proof. The proof is the universal ideal classification and the cited formal modification identity.

## Relationship to prior work

Huang--Jiang--Oblomkov (2026) explicitly posed, in Question 11.4, computation of contact-refined punctual Hilbert series for unibranch germs including \(y^p=x^q\). Their Section 6.4 / Corollary 6.8 gives the transformation for adding a fat branch but leaves the contact-refined input unspecified. The first boxed formula supplies that input in the \((p,q)=(2,3)\) cusp case, and the second answers the corresponding complete fat-branch family.

Huang--Jiang (2023), *Punctual Quot schemes and Cohen--Lenstra series of the cusp singularity* (arXiv:2305.06411), Section 7.2, gives the **unrefined** identity \((1+\mathbb L Q^2)/(1-Q)\), which is strictly weaker: it forgets \(j_x\) and does not determine the mixed fat-branch series via Corollary 6.8. Classical rationality of motivic Hilbert zeta functions for reduced curves also does not specify the contact classes. Exact overlap and implication searches of accessible related literature and research records found no statement giving both boxed formulas; this is evidence for novelty, not proof of historical priority.

## Limitations

The formula concerns the \(A_2\) cusp and its added fat \(x\)-axis branch; it does not compute contact-refined series for arbitrary \(y^p=x^q\), for two independently thickened tangent branches, or for nonpunctual Hilbert schemes. It concerns Grothendieck classes of reduced varieties, not scheme-theoretic multiplicities or embedded nilpotents of the punctual Hilbert schemes. The construction uses the cited arbitrary-germ branch-modification theorem as a premise. Historical-priority checking cannot exhaust all implicit semigroup or knot-theoretic computations; independent mathematical review has not been performed.
