# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem uses full finite exchangeability, hence uniform conditional subsets at each retained count. Its local concentration transfer compares two parent-retention configurations of equal probability; all remaining specialist labels are conditionally exchangeable. The nested-subset hypergeometric coupling ensures the more heavily supported branch has stochastically no fewer retained specialists, and discrete convex increments then establish the all-convex-function inequality. A chain of unit transfers establishes majorization. Separately, exact joint-retention probabilities for single parents and parent-specialist pairs establish the profile-independent mean and \(2\Delta T(q_3-q_4)\) variance gap. Strictness holds precisely when the retention distribution charges \(3\le R<N\). A standalone standard-library program independently enumerates fixed-size subsets and generating-function coefficients and reproduces the exact finite checks. These tests are corroboration, not a substitute for the transfer proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

Dunne, Williams and Martinez (2002) introduce primary-removal/secondary-extinction robustness; their accessible abstract and publication record do not state the mathematical allocation theorem. The full texts of Allesina, Bodini and Pascual (2009) and Allesina and Pascual (2009) were inspected, including methods and results, and address link functionality and species-ranking optimization rather than the all-convex, fixed-count group-allocation claim. The closest mathematical precedent is Bäuerle and Müller (1998), Theorem 3.2, which *does* establish a group-size majorization law for a class of independent-factor risk portfolios. This is strong related coverage, not merely a title resemblance. Its i.i.d. individual and group factors do not cover negatively associated fixed-cardinality initial-retention indicators; for \(0<r<N\) their pairwise covariance is strictly negative, whereas the cited portfolio model's cross-group indicators cannot have such negative covariance. It also does not supply this ecological parent-counting model's strict variance/equality classification. Primary full-text inspection, exact claim-implication comparison, prior work search and ledger-claim deduplication found no inspected proof of the stated extension. Residual historical-priority risk remains from general stochastic-order theorems that may give alternative reductions.

## Value — PASS

Fixed-count primary removal is a natural protocol for controlled food-web robustness comparisons. Two allocations with the same numbers of parent and specialist species have the *same mean survivor count*; nevertheless, one allocation is uniformly preferable for every convex dispersion/loss metric. The majorization theorem covers all group profiles, all subset counts, arbitrary exchangeable mixtures and gives an exact strictness boundary rather than an arbitrary one-off statistic. This supplies a structural risk benchmark, not a claimed ecological field prediction. Its assumptions and limits are stated in the result.

## Literature and limitations

Most pertinent: Bäuerle and Müller (1998), Theorem 3.2; Dunne, Williams and Martinez (2002); Allesina, Bodini and Pascual (2009); Allesina and Pascual (2009). The group-factor majorization result is a genuine conceptual precursor and may reduce the perceived novelty; no claim of historical uniqueness or independent verification is made. Only two trophic levels, an undeletable basal source, exchangeable initial retention and exclusive specialist support are proved.
