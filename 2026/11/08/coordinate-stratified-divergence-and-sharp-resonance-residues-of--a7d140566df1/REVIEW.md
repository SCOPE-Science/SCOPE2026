# Scientific review of the coordinate-stratified cube Fourier mean-body result

**Correctness: PASS.** The normalization is derived from the Fourier transform of the cube and the established radial-integral definition. At a direction with \(k\) nonzero coordinates the exponent is exactly \(p-2k-1\). The constant Fourier term is a positive sum of squares, precluding cancellation of the leading divergence. Oscillatory terms give a bounded critical remainder and a lower-order supercritical remainder. A pointwise arithmetic–geometric mean bound and a strict positive-mean argument prove sharpness and equality, and complete enumeration of the possible two- and three-term signed relations proves the three-coordinate spectrum. The standalone exact-rational and quadrature verifier passed 7,198 checks. Real frequencies and arbitrary dimension rely on the proof, not a finite test.

**Originality: PASS, with historical-priority risk.** Langharst–Manui–Zvavitch (arXiv:2601.20117, Sections 1.3 and 5) introduced this Fourier mean body, proved only the global cube Fourier index \(2\), and displayed the starting cube product formula. These established inputs are expressly excluded from the proposed novelty. The additional result identifies every directional coordinate-support threshold, every critical leading logarithmic coefficient, the sharp coefficient bound and rigid equality, and all five three-frequency resonance cases. Targeted searches did not identify the same geometric classification. Elementary trigonometric-polynomial averaging certainly predates this application; an implicit or explicit earlier directional treatment remains possible.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The result makes the newly studied operator's noncompactness sharply geometric: at each exponent the infinite directions form an exact coordinate-subsphere filtration. Its divergence is not determined by the support count alone; arithmetic resonances can either raise or lower the critical residue, with an exact sharp maximizer and a first complete low-dimensional spectrum. Those precise quantities refine the original paper's coarse global compactness threshold, and give reproducible quantitative information about singular radial directions. No claim is made about unrelated convex bodies or downstream theorems.

**Closest literature and limitations.** The closest primary text is *On the Fourier Mean Bodies of a Convex Body* (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20117v2). Equation (142) already reduces the cube problem to a squared-sine product, so the present contribution is an evaluation of that established representation.
