# Coprime-arm criterion for perfect central capture on quantum spiders

## Finding

Let a spider graph have a central vertex \(o\) and \(q\ge3\) arms. Arm \(i\) has \(L_i-1\) internal-and-leaf vertices at graph distances \(1,\ldots,L_i-1\) from \(o\), where all \(L_i\ge2\) are integers. Consider coherent nearest-neighbor hopping with nonzero real amplitude \(J\), constant on-site shift \(\epsilon\), and a single irreversible absorber at \(o\) of rate \(\gamma>0\). Let \(P_{\mathrm D}\) denote the orthogonal projection onto states never captured in the infinite-time norm limit.

For each integer \(b\ge2\), put \(m_b=|\{i:b\mid L_i\}|\), and let \(\varphi\) be Euler's totient function. Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}P_{\mathrm D}
=\sum_{b=2}^{\max_i L_i}\varphi(b)(m_b-1)_+.
\]

In particular **every initial quantum state is eventually captured if and only if**

\[
\gcd(L_i,L_j)=1\qquad\text{for every }i\ne j.
\]

Thus an arbitrarily large branching graph can be perfectly absorbing despite having only one absorbing vertex, whereas any shared divisor of two augmented arm lengths produces a protected coherent mode. The result is independent of the nonzero hopping magnitude, the uniform energy shift, and the positive absorption rate.

More precisely, for a walker initially at arm \(i\), site \(h\) (with \(1\le h<L_i\)), the exact never-captured probability is

\[
 p_{i,h}=\frac{2}{L_i}
 \sum_{\substack{b\mid L_i\\b\ge2,\ m_b\ge2}}
 \left(1-\frac{L_i^{-1}}{\sum_{j:\,b\mid L_j}L_j^{-1}}\right)
 \sum_{\substack{1\le a<b\\\gcd(a,b)=1}}
 \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi a h}{b}\right).
\]

The central site's survival probability is zero. For a general normalized pure state \(|\psi\rangle\), the probability of eventual survival is \(\langle\psi|P_{\mathrm D}|\psi\rangle\); for a density matrix \(\rho\), it is \(\operatorname{tr}(P_{\mathrm D}\rho)\). A full explicit spectral formula for \(P_{\mathrm D}\) appears below.

As examples, augmented arm lengths \((2,3,5)\) yield complete capture, while \((2,3,6)\) yield exactly three protected modes. For the latter, a walker initially at distance one on the length-six arm survives with probability \(7/12\), whereas at distance three on that arm the probability is \(1/4\). Equal augmented arm lengths \(L_1=\cdots=L_q=L\) yield the familiar symmetric limiting case \(\operatorname{rank}P_{\mathrm D}=(q-1)(L-1)\) and \(p_{i,h}=1-1/q\).

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is finite, simple, and undirected, with arms joined only at their single shared center. The Hilbert space has the orthonormal vertex basis \(|o\rangle,|i,h\rangle\), where \(1\le i\le q\) and \(1\le h<L_i\). Its Hermitian hopping Hamiltonian is \(H_0=JA+\epsilon I\), with \(A\) the adjacency matrix, \(J\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\), and \(\epsilon\in\mathbb R\). The no-click evolution is generated by

\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=H_0-\frac{i\gamma}{2}|o\rangle\langle o|,\qquad\gamma>0.
\]

For a normalized initial state, eventual noncapture means \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\|e^{-itH_{\mathrm{eff}}}|\psi\rangle\|^2\). The theorem concerns this infinite-time, one-excitation, purely coherent network model with local irreversible loss. It does not assert finite-time fast absorption, or robustness to additional dephasing or nonuniform hopping.

## Proof

**1. Exactly the zero-center eigenspaces survive.** The no-click evolution obeys

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\|\psi(t)\|^2=-\gamma|\langle o|\psi(t)\rangle|^2.
\]

Let \(\mathcal D\) be the direct sum, over eigenvalues of \(H_0\), of the eigenvectors having zero amplitude at \(o\). It is invariant under \(H_0\) and annihilated by the absorber. Because \(H_0\) is self-adjoint, its orthogonal complement is also invariant under both terms of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\). Every eigenvector of the restriction to \(\mathcal D^\perp\) has strictly negative imaginary eigenvalue: an eigenvector with real eigenvalue would make the norm derivative vanish and hence would be a forbidden zero-center eigenvector. The finite-dimensional restriction therefore decays as \(t\to\infty\). Consequently the entire infinite-time survivor operator is the orthogonal projector \(P_{\mathrm D}\) onto \(\mathcal D\).

**2. Dirichlet modes on individual arms.** If a vector has zero center amplitude, its restriction to an arm must be an eigenvector of the path on \(L_i-1\) sites with Dirichlet endpoints. Such a nonzero vector is proportional to

\[
\phi_{i,k}(h)=\sqrt{\frac2{L_i}}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi kh}{L_i}\right),\qquad 1\le k<L_i,
\]

with eigenvalue \(\epsilon+2J\cos(\pi k/L_i)\). Since cosine is strictly monotone on \((0,\pi)\), the same eigenvalue on two arms occurs exactly when the fractions \(k/L_i\) agree. Writing this common fraction in lowest terms \(a/b\) shows that the spectral value occurs precisely on the arms whose \(L_i\) are divisible by \(b\). There are \(\varphi(b)\) possible reduced numerators \(a\) for each \(b\ge2\).

**3. The one scalar junction constraint.** For a fixed reduced fraction \(a/b\), let \(S_b=\{i:b\mid L_i\}\), and let \(|\phi_{i,a/b}\rangle\) be the normalized Dirichlet wave on arm \(i\in S_b\), zero elsewhere. Every zero-center eigenvector in this frequency sector has the form \(\sum_{i\in S_b}c_i|\phi_{i,a/b}\rangle\). The eigenvector equation at \(o\) becomes

\[
\sum_{i\in S_b}\frac{c_i}{\sqrt{L_i}}=0,
\]

because the common nonzero factor \(J\sqrt2\sin(\pi a/b)\) cancels. This is one nontrivial linear constraint among \(m_b\) coefficients. Hence the dark subspace in this frequency sector has dimension \(m_b-1\). Summing over all reduced fractions gives the dimension formula, with sectors having \(m_b\le1\) contributing zero.

**4. The complete-capture arithmetic boundary.** A pair of lengths has greatest common divisor exceeding one precisely when there is a denominator \(b\ge2\) dividing both, or equivalently a sector with \(m_b\ge2\). Thus the dimension is zero if and only if the augmented arm lengths are pairwise coprime. With \(\dim\mathcal D=0\), Step 1 gives absorption of every initial state. Conversely, if two lengths share a divisor, the corresponding standing waves cancel at the center and are protected forever.

**5. Explicit survivor projector and localized law.** Define

\[
|v_{a/b}\rangle=\sum_{i\in S_b}L_i^{-1/2}|\phi_{i,a/b}\rangle,
\qquad
s_b=\sum_{i\in S_b}L_i^{-1}.
\]

Different reduced fractions correspond to orthogonal spectral sectors. The full dark projector is therefore

\[
P_{\mathrm D}=\sum_{\substack{b\ge2\\m_b\ge2}}
\sum_{\substack{1\le a<b\\\gcd(a,b)=1}}
\left(\sum_{i\in S_b}|\phi_{i,a/b}\rangle\langle\phi_{i,a/b}|
-\frac{|v_{a/b}\rangle\langle v_{a/b}|}{s_b}\right).
\]

Taking its diagonal matrix element at \(|i,h\rangle\) gives the stated site-resolved probability, and at \(|o\rangle\) gives zero. The formula also proves the general initial-state and density-matrix statements.

## Verification

The included standalone files `verify_arithmetic.py` and `verify_projector.py` use different constructions. The first builds integer adjacency walk matrices and checks their exact rational rank against the divisor formula and pairwise-coprime criterion on 145 spider graphs. The second diagonalizes full adjacency matrices and independently reconstructs the protected projector, comparing the full matrix and its site diagonal against the formulas on all 145 graphs (maximum absolute difference below \(10^{-14}\)). It additionally evaluates 12 finite-time non-Hermitian propagators at time \(800\) in hopping units for two distinct positive capture rates, and confirms convergence to the predicted norms to within \(3\times10^{-5}\) for the tested examples.

The infinite-family proof above is analytic; these finite tests corroborate it rather than establish it. Run the programs with Python 3, SymPy, NumPy, and SciPy from the directory containing both scripts. Their tested inputs, constraints, and tolerances are embedded in source, not dependent on external data.

## Relationship to prior work

Caruso et al. (2009, arXiv:0901.4454) identify invariant dark subspaces and local-trap trapping dynamics on fully connected quantum networks. Their construction is a general precursor, but the inspected manuscript does not give the divisor-resolved star-arm capture law. Yalouz and Pouthier (2018, DOI below) study a *different* symmetric two-generation dendrimer-like extended-star graph: hubs each lead to peripheral leaves. Their three decaying modes and trap-rate analysis do not contain a classification for arbitrary unequal linear arms. Vieira and Amorim (arXiv:2404.04094, originally public in April 2024) analyze weighted symmetric spiders and suppression of the transient root occupation, not irreversible central absorption or a coprimality-based exact survivor projector.

The underlying path eigenvalues, divisibility of equal rational fractions, invariant-state idea, and linear-algebraic projector construction are standard. The claim of potential originality here is the combined exact classification and full site-level law for the specified unequal-arm absorbing family, not the general theory of spectral controllability or the familiar equal-arm special case. A historical equivalent under controllability, star-tree spectral, or graph-theoretic terminology may still exist.

## Limitations

No claim is made about heterogeneous couplings, arm-dependent onsite potentials, multiple absorbing vertices, decoherence, optimal capture speed, or finite-time robustness. The exceptional equal-arm and common-divisor modes are infinitely stable only under the exact coherent model. The literature comparison is best-knowledge rather than a guarantee of publication priority; specifically, certain graph-controllability literature uses different operators and may contain convertible special cases. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
