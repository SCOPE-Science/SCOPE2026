# Counting-color rigidity of connected finite endofunctions

## Finding
Let \(A=(V,R)\) and \(B=(W,S)\) be nonempty finite relational structures, where \(R(u,v)\) means \(f(u)=v\) for a total endofunction \(f:V\to V\), and analogously \(S\) is the graph of \(g:W\to W\). Assume that each directed functional graph is weakly connected, meaning its underlying undirected graph is connected. Loops and antiparallel edge pairs are allowed. Then
\[
 A\equiv_{\mathrm C^2}B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\cong B,
\]
where \(\mathrm C^2\) is first-order logic with arbitrary counting quantifiers and only two reusable variable names, with no function symbols. Moreover, in every such structure the stable directed color-refinement classes coincide with the orbits of its automorphism group. The connectedness hypothesis matters: the directed \(6\)-cycle and two disjoint directed \(3\)-cycles are nonisomorphic but \(\mathrm C^2\)-equivalent.

## Assumptions and scope
The signature contains exactly one binary relation and equality. The binary relation is constrained to be the graph of one total unary function; the operation itself is **not** a function symbol in the logical language. A finite functional digraph is weakly connected precisely when it has one directed cycle, possibly a loop, with inward rooted trees attached to its cycle vertices. An isomorphism preserves directed edges. The stated equivalence concerns comparisons **within** this class; it does not say that a connected functional digraph is uniquely specified among all arbitrary directed graphs.

For an endofunction \(f\), define a directed refinement of the vertex coloring by starting with a color detecting \(R(v,v)\) and then recording the old color, the color of \(f(v)\), and, for every old color \(c\), the count of preimages of \(v\) having color \(c\). Its simultaneous stabilization on a disjoint union is denoted \(\chi\). This refinement is no stronger than \(\mathrm C^2\): every color at each finite round is definable by a one-free-variable \(\mathrm C^2\) formula, using \(R(x,y)\), \(R(y,x)\), and counting quantifiers. Consequently, if \(A\equiv_{\mathrm C^2}B\), the cardinality of every final color class is the same in \(A\) and \(B\).

## Proof
**1. Colors separate cycle vertices from tree vertices.** Fix a stable refinement, computed simultaneously on the disjoint union of two inputs. In each component iteratively delete every vertex of current indegree zero, where indegrees refer to the induced subgraph on the remaining vertices. At the outset, indegree is constant on a stable color class, because all preimage-color counts are constant. If all previously deleted vertices are unions of stable color classes, the number of surviving predecessors is likewise constant on each surviving color class. Thus every pruning step removes whole stable classes. The vertices that survive all pruning steps are exactly those on directed cycles. Hence cycle membership is a union of stable color classes, consistently across both structures, and no stable class mixes cyclic and noncyclic vertices.

**2. The cyclic color word is fixed.** Let \(K\) be the set of cycle colors in either connected structure. Each class \(c\in K\) has one well-defined successor color \(s(c)\in K\), because the successor color is part of the stable signature. Every vertex on the cycle has precisely one predecessor on the cycle. The stable preimage-color counts therefore yield exactly one cyclic predecessor color \(p(c)\) for each \(c\), and \(p\) and \(s\) are inverse permutations of \(K\). Since there is only one directed cycle in each structure, \(s\) acts transitively on \(K\). The cyclic order of colors is consequently forced by \(s\), up to rotation. The number of repetitions of that color pattern is the cardinality of any cyclic color class, which is known from the stable color histogram. Thus the entire directed cycle, with its colors, is determined up to color-preserving isomorphism.

**3. The attachments are fixed.** Every noncycle vertex has a finite pruning stage. For a class removed at the first stage, its members have no incoming vertices and carry the same singleton rooted-tree type. Inductively, the rooted preimage tree of any member of a class removed at stage \(j\) is determined by the multiset of the stable colors of its predecessors, all of which were removed at earlier stages: each such color already determines the predecessor's rooted subtree by induction. Therefore any two noncycle vertices of the same stable color have isomorphic rooted preimage trees. At a cycle vertex of color \(c\), remove the unique incoming edge from its cyclic predecessor of color \(p(c)\); the remaining predecessor counts determine exactly the multiset of attached rooted-tree types. All cycle vertices of color \(c\) have the same attachment type. The cyclic color word from Step 2 and these root attachments reconstruct the full connected functional graph up to isomorphism.

It follows that equal stable color histograms for two connected structures imply an isomorphism. The forward implication of the theorem follows from definability of each refinement color in \(\mathrm C^2\); the converse follows from isomorphism invariance of first-order logic.

**4. Stable classes are automorphism orbits.** An automorphism preserves every refinement color. Conversely, the transitive cyclic permutation of color classes described in Step 2 makes every two cycle vertices of the same color interchangeable by a rotation of the cycle and compatible isomorphisms of their identical attached rooted trees. For noncycle vertices, induct on their distance from the directed cycle. Vertices of the same stable color have successors of the same stable color, which are interchangeable by the induction hypothesis. After aligning those successors, their respective predecessor rooted trees are isomorphic by Step 3; an automorphism of the parent rooted tree can exchange the two equal-colored predecessor branches. Therefore vertices with the same stable color are in the same automorphism orbit.

**5. Boundary.** In the directed cycle on six vertices and the disjoint union of two directed triangles, every vertex has no loop, no reciprocal-neighbor pair, precisely one outgoing arc, and precisely one incoming arc. All vertices therefore have identical two-variable atomic relation-count profiles, and this single color is stable under the full directed color refinement corresponding to \(\mathrm C^2\). Both structures have six vertices, hence are \(\mathrm C^2\)-equivalent. They are not isomorphic because they have different numbers of weak components. This example shows why the one-cycle hypothesis cannot simply be dropped.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` constructs each rooted unlabeled tree by a complete one-leaf extension of the smaller rooted trees. It then constructs each connected functional graph as a directed cycle (up to cyclic rotation, **not** reflection) of rooted-tree types with a specified total number of vertices. For every order from \(1\) through \(12\), it computes simultaneous directed color-refinement signatures of all constructed isomorphism types. No signatures collide. The number of connected types at each order matches the established enumeration A002861; at order \(12\) there are \(16949\) types and \(16949\) refinement signatures. Additionally, exact permutation tests show that refinement classes equal automorphism orbits through order \(7\). The directed six-vertex boundary example is checked directly. Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing the program; its expected output is in `verification_output.txt`.

The finite computations are corroboration only. Steps 1–5 above prove the unrestricted statement for every finite order, with no computational extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Kiefer, Schweitzer, and Selman (2015) provide a broad classification of finite structures **identified among all structures** by \(\mathrm C^2\), including directed relational structures. Their identification problem differs from the relative-class claim here: a directed cycle, for example, is not identified against arbitrary disconnected functional graphs of the same local profile. Their Theorem 27 does not state the present connected-endofunction rigidity theorem. Kučera and Trnková (1988) give polynomial-time algorithms for isomorphism testing of unary algebras; an algorithm deciding isomorphism does not by itself imply that two-variable counting logic decides it. Harary (1959) and OEIS A002861 enumerate connected functional digraphs without asserting the refinement rigidity. Literature priority remains provisional; the full text of the 1988 article was not accessible in the present review.

## Limitations
The theorem applies to one total unary map represented **relationally**, and to weakly connected finite structures. It does not extend to arbitrary disconnected maps, as Step 5 demonstrates. It makes no claim for several unary operations, arbitrary binary relations, or a language with a genuine function symbol allowing nested terms. Only self-review has been performed; no independent audit or exhaustive historical-priority certification has been undertaken.
