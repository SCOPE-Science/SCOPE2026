## Correctness — PASS
The argument uses a stable equitable coloring that is explicitly definable in two-variable counting logic. Peeling by indegree-zero vertices is a union-of-color-classes process, so the cycle positions are recognized from their stable colors even though connectivity is not part of the logical vocabulary. The successor permutation of cyclic colors, the per-color cycle multiplicity, and recursively determined rooted-tree attachments reconstruct the connected endofunction uniquely. The orbit assertion follows by compatible cycle rotations and rooted-tree automorphisms. The six-vertex disconnected example has equal full two-variable atomic-type counts and stable uniform color. A complete independent canonical enumeration of connected functions through twelve points confirms the no-collision assertion, but is not substituted for the all-order proof.

Kučera–Trnková's 1988 isomorphism algorithms do not establish a counting-logic characterization on the material accessible here; however, the full article was not accessible, creating a real remaining priority risk. Harary's classical enumeration and OEIS A002861 determine the isomorphism-type counts but not the logical equivalence classes. Direct literature and mathematical-record searches found no statement implying the theorem. Historical originality is provisional, not certified.

## Value — PASS
This gives a complete logical recognition result for a standard class of unary relational structures of every finite order, together with an exact description of their automorphism orbits, rather than a small isolated computation. The connection between unique-cycle decomposition and color refinement explains exactly why a simple counting logic succeeds on connected mappings while it fails already for disconnected permutations. It provides a class-level test applicable to arbitrarily large connected functional digraphs.

## Scope and closest literature
The theorem does not claim identification among arbitrary directed graphs, nor does it apply to multiple unary operations, a genuine function-symbol language, or disconnected maps. Kiefer–Schweitzer–Selman (2015) and Kučera–Trnková (1988) are the closest conceptual comparisons; Harary (1959) and A002861 corroborate the connected-function enumeration.
