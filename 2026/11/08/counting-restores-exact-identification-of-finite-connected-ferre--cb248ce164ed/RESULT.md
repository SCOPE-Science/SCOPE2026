# Counting restores exact identification of finite connected Ferrers graphs at two variables

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal F\) be the class of finite, simple, connected, undirected bipartite graphs with at least one edge and no induced two-edge matching (also called connected Ferrers, chain, or difference graphs). Use the pure adjacency language, without named bipartition classes. For any \(G,H\in\mathcal F\), unrestricted two-variable first-order logic **with** counting quantifiers satisfies
\[
G\equiv_{C^2}H\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\cong H.
\]
In contrast, counting-free two-variable first-order logic has exactly **three** equivalence classes on \(\mathcal F\): the single edge \(K_2\); stars \(K_{1,r}\) with \(r\ge2\); and all remaining connected Ferrers graphs. All claims concern unrestricted quantifier depth; no bound on counting thresholds is imposed.

## Assumptions and scope
A chain graph has a bipartition \(A\sqcup B\) such that the neighborhoods of the vertices in each side are totally ordered by inclusion. For a connected chain graph with an edge, all neighborhoods are nonempty. Within a given side, any two vertices having equal degree have identical neighborhoods, while distinct neighborhood classes have different degrees. This follows from nestedness: strict inclusion of finite neighborhoods forces strictly greater cardinality. We allow any positive side sizes, including stars and \(K_2\).

Color refinement (one-dimensional Weisfeiler--Leman) begins with one uniform color and replaces each vertex color by its previous color together with the multiset of its neighbors' previous colors. A stable color class is equitable: the number of neighbors in any other fixed stable class is the same for every vertex in the class. The resulting cell cardinalities and intercell neighbor counts are invariant under \(C^2\)-equivalence, by the established correspondence between two-variable counting logic and color refinement. A color class in a chain graph contains at most one full neighborhood-twin class from each bipartition side, since vertices with different degrees are already separated after the first round, and twins cannot be separated by refinement.

## Proof
**Lemma (the equitable-cell dichotomy).** Let \(\mathcal P\) be the stable color partition of a connected chain graph \(G\). Either every cell lies entirely within one bipartition side, or every cell intersects both sides and has equally many vertices from each side.

Indeed, if a cell \(C\) intersects both sides, choose \(a\in C\cap A\), \(b\in C\cap B\). If some cell \(D\) has a positive neighbor count from \(C\), equitability gives both \(a\) and \(b\) a neighbor in \(D\); thus \(D\) intersects both sides. Since \(a\) represents an entire twin class and every vertex in \(D\cap B\) has the same adjacency to \(a\), the positive count from \(a\) to \(D\) is exactly \(|D\cap B|\). Similarly the positive count from \(b\) is exactly \(|D\cap A|\). Equitability therefore forces \(|D\cap A|=|D\cap B|\). The quotient graph of cells, with an edge whenever the intercell neighbor count is positive (allowing loops), is connected because \(G\) is connected. The mixed-cell property propagates along all quotient edges, hence every cell is mixed and is balanced (every cell has a neighbor, possibly itself). This proves the lemma.

**Reconstruction from the stable quotient.** Suppose all cells are side-pure. By the degree/twin observation each cell is exactly one twin class. Its size is known. Two cells are either completely joined or have no edges, and their adjacency is determined by the neighbor count. The side bipartition is uniquely recovered up to global exchange from connectedness of this quotient; indeed the full graph can be reconstructed simply by replacing every cell with its given number of mutually nonadjacent twins and placing all or no edges between each pair according to the quotient.

Suppose instead all cells are mixed and balanced. Then each cell \(C\) contains exactly \(|C|/2\) vertices of each side, one neighborhood-twin class per side. For any two (possibly equal) cells \(C,D\), edges can occur only between their opposite-side halves, and both directional cross-half adjacency indicators must agree: otherwise vertices of the two sides in \(C\) would have unequal numbers of neighbors in \(D\), contradicting equitability. Whenever these indicators agree and are one, all such opposite-side pairs are adjacent, so the common neighbor count from \(C\) to \(D\) is exactly \(|D|/2\); otherwise it is zero. Consequently the stable quotient counts, together with the cell sizes, uniquely reconstruct a representative with two equal-size halves per cell, joined according to those counts.

The two reconstruction cases cannot be confused between \(C^2\)-equivalent chain graphs. In the pure case, every cell induces an empty graph and all diagonal equitable neighbor counts vanish. In the mixed case, balance implies \(|A|=|B|=m\). The nonempty top-degree twin classes in \(A\) and \(B\) each consist of vertices adjacent to the entire opposite side, so both have degree \(m\). They must lie in the same stable cell: if separated they would each lie in pure cells, contradicting the all-mixed conclusion. Since the two top classes are adjacent, that cell has positive internal neighbor count. Thus the mixed case has a diagonal quotient entry that the pure case lacks.

The stable cell cardinalities and equitable neighbor counts therefore determine \(G\) uniquely up to isomorphism *within* \(\mathcal F\). Since \(C^2\)-equivalence is exactly equality of the stable color-refinement invariants, it follows that \(G\equiv_{C^2}H\) implies \(G\cong H\). The converse follows from isomorphism invariance.

**Counting-free contrast.** In any connected graph in \(\mathcal F\), every vertex has a neighbor. In \(K_2\), every two distinct vertices are adjacent; in a star with at least three vertices, exactly one vertex is adjacent to all other vertices; in any other member of \(\mathcal F\), each vertex has both an adjacent and a distinct nonadjacent vertex. The latter condition holds since nonstars have at least two vertices on each bipartition side. For any two graphs in each of the latter two categories, Duplicator maintains a permanent two-pebble partial isomorphism: a move is answered by a vertex equal to the stationary pebble, adjacent to it, or nonadjacent to it, as required; the relevant response types always exist. The star case additionally matches the center versus leaf unary type. In \(K_2\), completeness identifies its class. A sentence asserting the existence of a universal vertex separates stars from nonstars, and the sentence asserting completeness separates \(K_2\) from the others. Hence precisely three unrestricted \(\mathrm{FO}^2\) theories occur.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` (standard library only). It enumerates every nonisomorphic connected Ferrers graph of orders two through thirteen, using positive canonical twin-class staircases modulo side exchange, and applies simultaneous one-dimensional color refinement with canonical global colors. It confirms that no two distinct staircases share a stable color histogram and also verifies the three counting-free coarse classes. A second check directly tests the mixed-cell dichotomy and the quotient reconstruction conditions for every enumerated graph. These finite checks corroborate, but do not prove, the unrestricted assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Ehrenborg and van Willigenburg describe Ferrers graphs and their combinatorial structure [1]. Arvind, Köbler, Rattan, and Verbitsky give the foundational stable equitable partition formalism and the equivalence between color refinement and two-variable counting logic [2]. Kiefer, Schweitzer, and Selman classify graphs identified by \(C^2\) *among all graphs* [3]. Our statement is instead relative to the connected Ferrers class: for example, \(K_{3,3}\) is not identified by \(C^2\) among all graphs because nonisomorphic regular six-vertex graphs share its uniform color refinement, although it is identified among connected Ferrers graphs. Neither inspected counting-logic source states the relative Ferrers rigidity and the opposite three-theory counting-free collapse. Related classifications of connected Ferrers graphs in larger-width counting-free logic do not imply rigidity in \(C^2\).

## Limitations
The \(C^2\) rigidity is **relative** to connected Ferrers graphs, not an assertion of absolute \(C^2\)-identification among all finite simple graphs. Disconnected graphs, isolated vertices, named-side languages, bounded quantifier depth, and counting-free logics of width three or higher are outside the claimed equivalence. The proof relies on graph connectivity and nesting of neighborhoods. No claim of historical priority beyond the inspected references and recorded searches is made; independent mathematical and historical audit remains unperformed.
