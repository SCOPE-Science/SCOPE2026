# Review: counting and two-variable rigidity of connected Ferrers graphs

**Correctness (PASS).** A connected chain graph has strictly degree-ordered different neighborhood classes on each fixed bipartition side. Therefore every stable color cell contains at most one such class per side. A mixed stable cell forces every neighboring cell to be mixed and balanced by equitability, and the connected quotient forces all cells to be balanced mixed. Both the pure and balanced-mixed cases reconstruct a unique graph from cell counts and neighbor counts. The cases differ by the presence of a positive diagonal quotient count. The independent two-pebble proof shows the three exact counting-free equivalence classes. The executable finite test checks 2,079 nonisomorphic staircases of orders 2-13 with zero collisions; this corroborates, rather than replaces, the mathematical proof.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** Ferrers enumeration [1] has a distinct topic, and general graph color refinement/absolute counting-logic identification [2,3] does not state this relative Ferrers theorem. Accessible prior Ferrers results at counting-free width three and above do not imply the two-variable counting classification. Exact alias/equivalence searches against published records and historical claims found no exact or stronger relevant theorem. Historical priority is provisional, especially for older difference-graph literature, and the exact complete priority status is not claimed.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The classification resolves a natural finite model-theory invariant for an entire structurally canonical graph family, and locates a strong expressive-power discontinuity already at two reusable variable names. The intrinsic equitable-partition reconstruction is more informative than an isolated enumeration.

**Limitations.** This is a *relative* graph-family result, and uses unrestricted counting thresholds and quantifier depth. Disconnected structures, isolated vertices and identification among arbitrary graphs are not covered. Finite experiments are not infinite certificates.
