# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact norm identity follows by taking the modulus of the elementary trigonometric binomial. In the subcritical domain the Gaussian majorant permits global dominated convergence. At the critical point the change of variable \(x=n^{1/4}u\), the inequality \(t-\log(1+t)\ge t^2/[2(1+t)]\), and an integrable quartic-to-Gaussian majorant prove both the sharp gamma constant and density convergence. In the supercritical domain, the globally dominating exponent \(f(y)=-y^2+\log(1+cy^2)\) has exactly two nondegenerate maximizers; a uniform local expansion through order \(n^{-1}\) proves the exponential rate, Gaussian prefactor, finite-size correction, and equal splitting of probability mass. The corresponding tails are controlled globally by \(f_n\le f\) and \(f(y)\le-y^2/2\) far from the origin. This checks normalization and each quantifier for fixed \(c>0\). The standalone checker reproduces symbolic saddle identities and 15 high-precision integral comparisons; these experiments are not treated as an infinite-domain proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty

Alpay–Colombo–Diki–Sabadini–Struppa, arXiv:2304.11551v1, Sections 2 and 3.1, specify the same underlying Gaussian-superoscillatory functions and their coherent-state decomposition, but their convergence theorems use a fixed target parameter and do not state the degree-dependent norm or mass transition. Alpay–De Martino–Diki–Struppa, arXiv:2407.12502v1, Section 4 and Proposition 4.7, give a finite exact Gabor-norm sum valid for any single \((n,a)\). This is an important competing formula: replacing \(a\) by \(\sqrt{1+cn}\) is legitimate, but a finite sum is not the sharp critical quartic and two-saddle large-degree asymptotic. The present theorem claims neither the finite formula nor an improvement of the fixed-parameter transform identity. The accessible historical-claim comparison yielded no matching growing-parameter Gaussian norm law. Semantic research-record searches under the natural aliases also produced no directly covering claim, but neither negative search nor a partition proves uniqueness. A possibly unlocated general uniform steepest-descent result is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The finite-frequency superoscillatory series is a natural canonical model; its Gaussian Hilbert energy is precisely the norm of its coherent-state superposition. Asking how rapidly the nominal target frequency can grow with degree before Hilbert energy destabilizes is a structural question. The exact threshold \(a_n^2-1\asymp n\), the quartic critical \(n^{1/4}\) squared-norm law, the above-threshold exponential rate *and nontrivial prefactor*, and the transition from central mass to two spatial peaks give a complete answer on the naturally scaled fixed-\(c\) family. This is not merely a Taylor expansion for a fixed parameter or a numeric example. The result is limited to this canonical family and does not establish a uniform nearcritical scaling window.
