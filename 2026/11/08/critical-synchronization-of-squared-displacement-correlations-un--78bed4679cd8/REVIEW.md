The model contains independent rate-\(\alpha\) coordinate-specific Poisson clocks and a distinct rate-\(\beta\) environment clock. The conditional quadratic semigroup and these covariances reconstruct both lag formulas. The two-sided Poisson representation supplies a stationary law and future reset coupling supplies uniqueness; finite fourth moments justify taking generator expectations. ## Originality — PASS, with residual publication risk
Santra, Basu and Sabhapandit, arXiv:2205.00549v1 (2022), analyze Brownian motion with a stochastic diffusivity and **simultaneously reset the whole position vector and the diffusivity**; their one-time distributions and relaxation formulas do not imply the displayed cross-coordinate law when coordinate resets are independent. Kumar, Sadekar and Basu, arXiv:2008.03294v1 (2020), study different resetting protocols for a self-propelled active Brownian particle with orientation, not the shared Brownian-square environment here. Stojkoski, Sandev, Kocarev and Pal, arXiv:2107.11686v1 (2021), give resetting-time autocovariances for ordinary Brownian diffusion rather than cross-coordinate squared-displacement covariances under two independent reset mechanisms. Full article sections containing the models, derived quantities, and conclusions were compared by statements and reset maps rather than titles alone. Established generic Markov-generator and semigroup tools are used but do not alone state this identity or its rate-matching boundary. ## Mathematical value — PASS
Multiple independently reset tracers sharing a common fluctuating diffusivity form a natural non-equilibrium extension of the studied stochastic-diffusivity transport model. The exact cross- to same-coordinate *quadratic* correlation ratio provides a scale-free measure of common-environment memory that changes its long-time limit at equality of reset rates. The critical \(1/t\) deficit is distinguishable from exponential convergence on either side, and the exact collective-radius variance shows why infinitely many such coordinates fail to self-average at the second-moment level. These facts classify a physically motivated pairwise dynamical observable and its collective consequence rather than performing an arbitrary finite-parameter computation.

## Limitations
Poisson clock and independent Brownian assumptions are essential; no extension to general renewal clocks, other environments, common coordinate resets, or arbitrary state-dependent resets is proved. The rate-boundary is for a relative correlation ratio; both underlying absolute covariances decay. The asymptotic in dimension is a variance statement, not a limiting distribution. No independent validation has been conducted. Literature scrutiny cannot guarantee that no equivalent result exists outside inspected sources.
