# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A maximal coupling between an independent Bernoulli loss set and a zero-or-one loss set gives an **exact** per-round mismatch probability. An intermediate always-acquire process dominates the clumsy process, and its discrepancies can only originate during clumsy loss rounds; any later successful acquisition clears the corresponding discrepancy. The exact one-label recurrence and a union bound prove a finite-time process coupling, uniform throughout the critical completion window. Unseen coupon variance is bounded by its mean, controlling premature completions. The established clumsy limiting law [2, Theorem 4.10] then transfers to the independent-loss careless model. The standalone exact-rational checker passed 1,813 assertions; it supplements the proof and cannot certify the limiting distribution by computation.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk

The **new** assertion is the set-valued quantitative bridge and its critical careless consequence, not the already published clumsy critical limit. Cruciani–Dudeja [1] analyze the careless model's expected completion scales; Attrill–Garoni [2] derive the Gumbel-plus-immigration–death limit only for the clumsy model with a one-label update; Long [3] proves fixed-loss flux theorems, which do not cover shrinking losses on the critical scale. Comparison of their full accessible text, targeted research-record searches and accessible earlier claim statements found no directly covering pathwise bridge. A sufficiently general earlier rare-perturbation coupling theorem could imply it; independent literature review is warranted.

## Value — PASS

The problem is motivated by the recognized gap between two natural, recently studied nonmonotone coupon mechanisms. Matching aggregate expected losses does not automatically imply hitting-time equivalence, since completion is a rare event and the update rules differ. The result establishes an explicit small path-disagreement probability across a window containing completion, showing why the non-Gumbel critical law is robust to replacing one-label erasure with independent simultaneous erasures. The exact finite bound makes the observation usable without an unproved independence assumption. The finding is restricted to uniform selection and fixed \(c\); it does not provide moment limits or a better rate for the clumsy model itself.
