# Crown-graph dissociation complexes: sphere wedges and an integral symmetry lattice

## Finding
For every integer \(n\geq3\), let \(C_n\) be the crown graph with vertices \(a_1,\ldots,a_n,b_1,\ldots,b_n\) and edges \(a_i b_j\) exactly when \(i\neq j\). Write \(X_n=\operatorname{Ind}_2(C_n)\) for the simplicial complex of subsets inducing connected components of size at most two (equivalently, maximum induced degree at most one, or no three-vertex path as a not-necessarily-induced subgraph). Then

\[
X_n\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{2}}S^2.
\]

Let \(W_n=\ker(\mathbb Z^n\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Z)\), equipped with the coordinate-permuting action of \(S_n\). The natural \(S_n\times C_2\)-action on \(C_n\), simultaneously permuting paired labels and optionally interchanging the two vertex sides, acts on the only nonzero reduced integral homology by

\[
\widetilde H_2(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong \bigwedge^2 W_n\otimes\mathbb Z_{\mathrm{sgn}_{C_2}}.
\]

For \(\sigma\in S_n\), let \(f(\sigma)\) be its number of fixed labels and \(t(\sigma)\) its number of disjoint transpositions in the cycle decomposition. With \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) indicating layer interchange, its rational character is

\[
\chi(\sigma,\varepsilon)=(-1)^\varepsilon\left(\binom{f(\sigma)}2-t(\sigma)-f(\sigma)+1\right).
\]

The integral homology is free abelian, and \(\widetilde H_q(X_n;\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(q\neq2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graphs are finite, undirected, loop-free and simple. All simplicial complexes use their ordinary geometric realizations; homology is reduced integral simplicial homology unless a coefficient ring is displayed. This is a theorem for all integers \(n\geq3\), not an inference from a finite list of computations. The involution in \(C_2\) swaps \(a_i\) with \(b_i\) for every index, and \(S_n\) sends each \(a_i,b_i\) pair to \(a_{\sigma(i)},b_{\sigma(i)}\). The degree-two isomorphism is an integral group-module isomorphism, not a claim about a preferred basis or an equivariant simple-homotopy collapse.

## Proof
**1. Exact face description.** For a vertex subset \(F\), let \(A=\{i:a_i\in F\}\) and \(B=\{j:b_j\in F\}\). A selected left vertex \(a_i\) has \(|B\setminus\{i\}|\) selected neighbors, and a selected right vertex \(b_j\) has \(|A\setminus\{j\}|\) selected neighbors. Consequently, \(F\in X_n\) precisely when both numbers are at most one for every selected index. If both \(A,B\) are nonempty, they each have cardinality at most two. If one has cardinality two, the other is contained in it; if both have cardinality two, \(A=B\). Hence every mixed face lies in a tetrahedron

\[
T_{ij}=2^{\{a_i,a_j,b_i,b_j\}},\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq n,
\]

where \(2^U\) denotes the full simplex on vertex set \(U\). (For a mixed face with coincident single index choose any second index, possible because \(n\ge3\).) All unmixed faces lie in the simplices \(L=2^{\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}}\) or \(R=2^{\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}}\). Conversely, every listed simplex has induced maximum degree at most one. Therefore the exact cover is

\[
X_n=L\cup R\cup\bigcup_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[n]}2}T_{ij}.
\]

**2. First nerve.** Every nonempty intersection of elements of this cover is itself a full simplex and hence contractible. Put \(K_n^{\star}\) for the nerve of the tetrahedra alone. Its vertices are unordered pairs \(\{i,j\}\); a collection of such pairs is a face if and only if they have a common index. Indeed the corresponding tetrahedra intersect in the simplex generated by the left and right vertices of the common indices. For each face of \(K_n^{\star}\), adjoining either \(L\) or \(R\) gives a face in the full cover nerve, whereas adjoining both does not because \(L\cap R=\varnothing\). The full nerve is exactly the simplicial suspension \(K_n^{\star}*S^0\). The finite good-cover nerve theorem gives

\[
|X_n|\simeq\Sigma|K_n^{\star}|.
\]

**3. Second nerve.** For each index \(i\), the pairs containing \(i\) span a full simplex \(S_i\) in \(K_n^{\star}\). The \(S_i\) cover \(K_n^{\star}\). For \(i\neq j\), \(S_i\cap S_j\) is the single vertex \(\{i,j\}\); any triple intersection is empty. Thus their nerve is the one-dimensional complete graph \(K_n\) (not its clique complex). Another application of the good-cover nerve theorem yields

\[
|K_n^{\star}|\simeq|K_n|,
\qquad
|X_n|\simeq\Sigma|K_n|.
\]

The connected graph \(K_n\) has \(\binom n2\) edges and \(n\) vertices, so it has cycle rank \(\binom n2-n+1=\binom{n-1}{2}\) and is homotopy equivalent to that many circles. Its suspension is a wedge of that many two-spheres, proving the homotopy statement and integral homology concentration.

**4. Integral naturality and the layer sign.** All covering simplices and their intersections are preserved as families by \(S_n\times C_2\). The standard simplicial incidence-resolution proof of the nerve theorem is natural for these permutations: it uses the augmented chain complexes of cover intersections, which are simplices with canonical equivariant augmentation. Accordingly the induced integral-homology isomorphisms commute with the group actions. The \(S_n\)-action on the second nerve \(K_n\) permutes its labeled vertices; the layer involution fixes \(K_n^{\star}\) and exchanges the two suspension apices \(L,R\), acting by \(-1\) on suspended reduced homology. Therefore

\[
\widetilde H_2(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(K_n;\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb Z_{\mathrm{sgn}_{C_2}}.
\]

For the integral vertex-permutation module \(V=\mathbb Z^n\) with augmentation \(\epsilon:V\to\mathbb Z\), the oriented one-chains of \(K_n\) are naturally \(\bigwedge^2 V\), and their boundary is \(\partial(u\wedge v)=\epsilon(u)v-\epsilon(v)u\). Its kernel is \(\bigwedge^2W_n\) integrally: choose a vector of augmentation one to split \(V\cong W_n\oplus\mathbb Z\) as abelian groups, after which the boundary restricts to an isomorphism from the cross-term to \(W_n\) and vanishes on \(\bigwedge^2W_n\). The kernel description itself is canonical and \(S_n\)-equivariant.

**5. Character.** A permutation with \(f\) fixed points and \(t\) two-cycles has trace \(f\) on oriented zero-chains and trace \(\binom f2-t\) on oriented one-chains: fixed unordered edges contribute positively if their endpoints are individually fixed and negatively if the endpoints are exchanged. The constant zero-homology character equals one. Since \(K_n\) is connected, the equivariant Euler identity on cellular chains gives \(\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\mid H_1(K_n;\mathbb Q))=\binom f2-t-f+1\). The layer switch multiplies this by \(-1\), establishing the displayed character.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_crown.py` from the extracted package. No third-party packages are required. The program independently (i) enumerates subsets by the degree condition, (ii) enumerates all faces from the stated simplex cover, (iii) checks the two descriptions agree, (iv) computes reduced mod-two simplicial homology with augmentation, (v) checks the intersection criterion for bounded families of tetrahedra, and (vi) computes signed reduced Lefschetz numbers for every element of \(S_n\times C_2\) through \(n=7\). Its recorded output is in `verify_output.txt`. All 11,824 group elements checked across \(2\leq n\leq7\) agree with the formula. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the all-\(n\) good-cover proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Taylan's 2015 paper introduced and treated graph devoid complexes \(D(G;F)\), including \(D(C_m;P_k)\) for cycles and paths, and proved a general matching-tree method. The special case \(n=3\) of the present family is a six-cycle and is already within that cycle theorem; it is not claimed as a new case. Deshpande and Singh subsequently classified higher-independence complexes of complete multipartite graphs, fully whiskered graphs, paths, cycles and perfect rooted trees, rather than the crown graphs \(K_{n,n}\setminus M\). Their Theorem 3.5 is not applicable to these non-complete-bipartite graphs, while their cycle theorem only covers \(n=3\) in this family. No inspected primary theorem gives the all-\(n\) integral symmetry lattice or the complete layer-switch character. These comparisons do not establish absolute publication priority.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns \(\operatorname{Ind}_2\), not the ordinary independence complex, edge matching complex or spaces of particle configurations. The generalization to arbitrary complements of bipartite matchings with unequal sides is not established. The group-module statement is not an equivariant simple-homotopy or stable homotopy classification. The absence of a matching literature result in inspected sources is evidence, not proof, of novelty.
