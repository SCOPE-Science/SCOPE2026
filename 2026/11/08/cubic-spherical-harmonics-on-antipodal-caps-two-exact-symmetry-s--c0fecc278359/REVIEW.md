# Mathematical review: cubic antipodal-cap concentration

## Correctness — PASS
The spherical-coordinate distribution is exactly \(\mathrm{Beta}(1/2,(d-1)/2)\) for \(t=\omega_d^2\). Four mutually orthogonal transverse harmonic degrees exhaust pure harmonic cubics. Their squared radial factors yield the four eigenvalue integrals, and the stated dimension formula checks against the full degree-three harmonic dimension. All five pairwise difference identities used for ordering follow from a beta-weight integration-by-parts identity with rational polynomial certificates. Their signs prove both switches and the absence of other extremal ties. The thin-cap constant follows from endpoint asymptotics with explicit normalizers. The companion checker verifies the polynomial identities symbolically, explores the required boundaries, and cross-checks the ordinary two-sphere spectrum against independently evaluated spherical harmonics. The general result is an analytical theorem, not an extrapolation from numerical cases.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
The closest antecedent is Simons–Dahlen (2006), which addresses exactly the double-cap geometry on the ordinary two-sphere, but in the full low-pass Slepian setting. Its general analytic matrix formulas encompass the computation of individual degree-three entries when \(d=3\); this special-dimensional calculation is credited and is not claimed to be intrinsically new. The present contribution is the **simultaneous crossing of three distinct symmetry sectors at \(u=1/(d+2)\)**, a second crossing at \(u=3/(d+2)\), their exact all-dimensional multiplicities, and an all-dimensional sharp condition asymptotic. Jaming–Speckbacher (2020) gives general large-sieve concentration bounds on two-point homogeneous spaces but no such spectrum. Michel–Plattner–Seibert (2021) develops numerical and commuting-operator constructions for caps, focusing on the ordinary sphere. The pre-existing degree-two antipodal-cap spectral result is not a proof of this degree-three theorem: cubic harmonics have four sectors and a new triple collision rather than the quadratic three-sector single collision. The literature and published-record searches found no equivalent all-dimensional crossing classification; nonetheless an unrecognized general Gegenbauer/representation-theoretic identity could imply it. These source-specific limits constrain priority assessment.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Antipodal polar gaps arise naturally in spherical signal recovery and geodesy. Pure-degree blocks govern what is observable at a fixed multipole. The exact first crossing has a large maximal eigenspace, a phenomenon absent in the simpler quadratic case, while the second crossing identifies a different optimal symmetry. The result gives a complete all-dimensional optimizer and equality classification, a persistent least-observable sector, and the sharp thin-cap instability exponent and coefficient. These are structural and stability facts for a natural region and frequency block, not an arbitrary finite census. It does not solve the full low-pass or finite-noise reconstruction problems.
