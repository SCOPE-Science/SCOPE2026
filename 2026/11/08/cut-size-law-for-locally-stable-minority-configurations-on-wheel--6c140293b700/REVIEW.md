# Scientific review: Gaussian stable-wheel cut fluctuations

## Correctness — PASS for the narrowed final theorem
The known equilibrium histogram is encoded by the primitive three-state transfer matrix with characteristic equation \(\lambda^3=u(\lambda+1)\). Its simple dominant analytic eigenvalue gives a uniform logarithmic moment-generating-function expansion. Differentiation yields the explicit first and second cumulant slopes and positivity of the variance; the characteristic-function proof gives the CLT for all wheel sizes tending to infinity. The known exact optimal-cut counts are at most linear, while the Perrin equilibrium total is exponential, proving exponentially rare optimum cuts. Independent small-state checks corroborate but do not prove the normal limit.

## Originality — PASS only for the distributional refinement
*A sharp equilibrium and efficiency phase transition on weighted wheel networks* (November 8, 2026) establishes the full size-refined stable cut formula, maximum cut, extrema counts and price-of-anarchy for every positive spoke weight below two, including this unit-weight model. These results, and the old Perrin enumeration, are covered. The examined prior theorem does not supply the explicit Gaussian typical-cut fluctuation, variance coefficient or concentration about the population mean. Those results require analytic reweighting and a Perron-root calculation, not a new state count. Generic transfer-matrix/quasi-powers theory remains relevant prior machinery; a specialized older normal-limit statement may exist, so priority is best-of-knowledge.

## Mathematical value — PASS for the narrowed final theorem
The cut quality of a uniformly sampled stable configuration is a natural efficiency observable of a concrete anti-coordination network. Its exact typical scaling and nonzero fluctuation variance provide information distinct from the known extremal price-of-anarchy and equilibrium counts. The theorem quantifies the large gap between typical stable and maximum cuts and its sample-to-sample dispersion, rather than relabeling an existing integer sequence.

## Boundaries
Only uniformly sampled fixed states of synchronous tie-retaining open-neighborhood minority dynamics on unweighted wheels are addressed. No basin-size distribution, dynamical mixing, or arbitrary weight limit is implied. The earlier weighted-wheel theorem is the source of the covered combinatorial facts.
