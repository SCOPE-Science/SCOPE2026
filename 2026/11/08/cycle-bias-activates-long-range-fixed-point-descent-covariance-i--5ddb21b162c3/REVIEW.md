# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim's exact domain is \(n\ge3\), \(\theta>0\). Ewens-weight conditioning on a fixed point deletes one one-cycle and leaves an Ewens permutation on the remaining labels. The ordinary marginal descent formula is reconstructed by classifying the one- and two-cycle completions of two prescribed image arrows. Those identities yield every positionwise covariance by conditioning; the wrap-around comparison is computed separately, with its double-fixed-arrow correction. Exact rational simplification gives the stated cyclic covariance sign for all permitted parameters and the ordinary-count boundary at \(n=3\). The supplied standard-library verifier passes 1,252 independent rational comparisons from exhaustive small permutation distributions; it supports, but is not a replacement for, the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

The nearest published uniform-case result already gives the \(\theta=1\) bidiagonal dependence and cyclic cancellation; those specializations are explicitly attributed. Féray's Ewens partial-image moment lemma is a stronger underlying calculation tool, but its full text does not state the present all-parameter long-range constant, indexed covariance signs or cyclic phase transition. Reutenauer/Gessel–Reutenauer and Gessel–Zhuang give cycle-type and ordinary descent-set generating formulas, which encompass aggregate ordinary moments and therefore preclude novelty claims for that component by itself. Their count-level statements do not distinguish the location of a fixed point and do not include the last-to-first one-line comparison. The full text of the published uniform result was not accessible, and a different equivalent Ewens specialization may exist; independent priority review remains needed. Absence of search results alone is not treated as proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The classical uniform permutation has a sharply local fixed-point–descent covariance matrix and zero fixed-point/cyclic-descent covariance. This theorem identifies the exact mechanism by which cycle weighting destroys local covariance support, including its common remote value, and determines a universal positive/zero/negative transition of the circular aggregate at the natural no-bias parameter. The distinction between ordinary and circular descent dependence is structural and persists at every sample size, not a fitted or arbitrarily sliced numerical fact.

## Limits

Only mixed covariances and the stated pairwise independence are established, not total-count independence or higher moments. The cycle-type/descent-set literature can recover some aggregate consequences, and the extension's priority is provisional pending external validation.
