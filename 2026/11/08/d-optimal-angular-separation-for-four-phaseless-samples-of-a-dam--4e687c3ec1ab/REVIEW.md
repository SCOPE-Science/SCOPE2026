# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The stated coordinates give precisely the four intensity equations. The explicit Vandermonde roots are \(1,r^2,re^{i\theta},re^{-i\theta}\), producing the exact signed determinant; the log derivative of the nonconstant determinant factor is proportional to \(6rx^2-(1+r^2)x-4r\). The endpoint signs prove one interior maximizing cosine, the other root being outside the interval. The resulting optimal angle and the full boundary failure are proved for every specified modulus. A standalone deterministic checker independently tests the determinant and design on finite parameter grids; it supports but cannot replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk.** Beinert–Hasannasab (arXiv:2103.10086, 2021; journal 2023) proves generic phaseless dynamical retrieval from collision-free spectral products (Theorem 5) and studies perturbation sensitivity. That prior theorem **does imply** the injectivity ingredient in the present normalized two-mode model; neither the known Vandermonde identity nor this ingredient is claimed as new. The precise fixed-modulus D-optimal angular problem, unique closed-form maximizing phase, reciprocal-modulus symmetry, and angular range are not present in the inspected relevant statements, Sections 8–10, and appendix. The 2021 polarization paper (arXiv:2102.00536) uses additional interferometric measurements and does not state this design optimum. Closest available records concerning optimal observation horizons and four-frequency determinant designs address different measurement matrices; historical equivalence in older D-optimal experimental design remains a risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** In critically sampled two-mode intensity experiments the four real lifted coordinates are directly estimated by a square system, and the determinant controls the generalized variance under equal-variance intensity noise. Thus the exact design criterion has an immediate purpose beyond reproducing a Vandermonde formula. The result settles the complete global optimizer and all equality cases over the natural phase parameter at each fixed damping ratio; it also explains why equal moduli destroy recoverability. This does not imply E-optimality, physical shot-noise optimality, or optimality in other sampling families.
