# Degree-one chamber generation for plane Chebyshev arrangements

## Finding
Let \(2\le a<b\) be coprime integers, let \(T_j\) be the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, and let \(C_{a,b}\) be the real projective closure of \(T_b(x)=T_a(y)\). Include the line at infinity in the arrangement \(\mathcal A_{a,b}\). Write \(\mathscr C_{a,b}\) for the chambers of its real affine complement and
\[
A_{a,b}=\operatorname{Map}(\mathscr C_{a,b},\mathbb Q).
\]
Give this chamber algebra the two-step filtration defined by the region-cycle map, with \(\mathsf F^0 A=\mathbb Q\mathbf1\), \(\mathsf F^1 A=\ker\gamma_{\mathbb Q}\), and \(\mathsf F^2 A=A\), as in Yoshinaga's recent construction. Let \(B_{a,b}\) be the **unital subalgebra** generated by \(\mathsf F^1 A\).

**Theorem.** The degree-one classes generate the chamber algebra if and only if \((a,b)=(2,3)\):
\[
B_{a,b}=A_{a,b}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(a,b)=(2,3).
\]
For every other coprime \(2\le a<b\), there is the quantitative deficiency
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}(A_{a,b}/B_{a,b})\ \ge\ 
\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}2-a+1\ >0.
\]
Indeed the algebra generated in degree one always has \(\dim_{\mathbb Q} B_{a,b}\le a+1\), whereas the number of chambers is exactly \(\frac12(a-1)(b-1)+2\). The claim is about this specified filtration and affine chart, not arbitrary filtrations on a chamber algebra.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb R\) for the curve and \(\mathbb Q\) for the chamber functions. The orientation of the real normalization is induced by decreasing real parameter \(t\) in
\[
\nu(t)=(T_a(t),T_b(t)).
\]
The integer-valued winding function \(w\) is normalized to zero above the whole curve on each regular vertical fibre; reversing the orientation changes \(w\) to \(-w\), without changing \(\mathbb Q[w]\). The arrangement includes the line at infinity. All intersections mentioned in the proof are real, and each affine self-intersection is an ordinary real node.

## Proof
**1. Geometry and chamber count.** The identity \(T_b(T_a(t))=T_a(T_b(t))\) parametrizes \(C_{a,b}\). Since \(a\) and \(b\) are coprime, the function field of the parametrized image is \(\mathbb R(t)\): the degree \([\mathbb R(t):\mathbb R(T_a(t),T_b(t))]\) divides both \(a\) and \(b\). The image has projective degree \(b\), so it coincides with the irreducible degree-\(b\) equation \(T_b(x)-T_a(y)=0\). Its normalization has exactly one point over the line at infinity.

An affine singularity must satisfy \(T_b'(x)=T_a'(y)=0\). The critical points are \(x_i=\cos(i\pi/b)\) and \(y_j=\cos(j\pi/a)\), with \(1\le i<b\) and \(1\le j<a\), and the defining equation holds exactly when \(i\equiv j\pmod2\). Since \(a,b\) are coprime, at least one of \(a-1,b-1\) is even. Thus exactly
\[
N=\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}2
\]
critical pairs lie on the curve. At each, the two second derivatives are nonzero and have matching signs because \(T_n''(\cos(j\pi/n))=(-1)^{j+1}n^2/\sin^2(j\pi/n)\). Hence the Hessian of \(T_b(x)-T_a(y)\) has negative determinant, and the singularity is an ordinary node with two real branches. There are no other affine singularities. In particular this is a totally real rational-curve arrangement. The normalization maps its real affine line properly onto a connected curve with \(N\) transverse nodes.

Compactify the affine plane to a sphere and the proper immersed normalization line by a single point at infinity. The resulting connected embedded graph has \(N+1\) vertices and \(2N+1\) edges (each node has two normalization preimages). Euler's formula gives
\[
\#\mathscr C_{a,b}=2-(N+1)+(2N+1)=N+2.
\]
The same count follows from the affine region formula in the motivating paper.

**2. Projection-degree bound.** For a point \((x_0,y_0)\) outside the curve with \(x_0\) a regular value of \(T_a\), consider the vertical half-ray towards increasing \(y\). With this normalization orientation, the signed-intersection definition of the winding function gives
\[
w(x_0,y_0)=\sum_{\substack{t\in\mathbb R:\ T_a(t)=x_0\\T_b(t)>y_0}}\operatorname{sgn}T_a'(t).
\]
There are \(k\le a\) distinct real roots of \(T_a(t)=x_0\), with \(k\equiv a\pmod2\). Since \(T_a\) has positive leading coefficient, its derivative has alternating signs at the ordered real roots, starting with a positive sign at the largest root. Consequently at most \(\lceil a/2\rceil\) of their derivatives are positive and at most \(\lfloor a/2\rfloor\) are negative. Any partial signed sum therefore lies in
\[
-\lfloor a/2\rfloor\ \le\ w\ \le\ \lceil a/2\rceil.
\]
Every chamber contains points on regular vertical fibres, so \(w\) takes **at most \(a+1\) different integer values** on all chambers.

By the integral winding-basis theorem of Yoshinaga, \(\mathsf F^1 A=\mathbb Q\mathbf1+\mathbb Qw\) for this one-curve affine arrangement. Hence \(B_{a,b}=\mathbb Q[w]\). On a finite set of chambers, the dimension of \(\mathbb Q[w]\) equals the number of distinct values of \(w\): evaluation gives the upper bound, while Lagrange interpolation gives characteristic functions of each level set. Therefore
\[
\dim B_{a,b}\le a+1,
\qquad\dim(A_{a,b}/B_{a,b})\ge N-a+1.
\]

**3. All nonexceptional parameters.** For \(a\ge3\), one has \(b\ge a+1\) and
\[
N-a+1\ge\frac{a(a-1)}2-a+1=\frac{(a-1)(a-2)}2>0.
\]
For \(a=2\), coprimality forces \(b\) odd. If \(b\ge5\), then \(N-a+1=(b-3)/2>0\). Thus generation fails for every pair other than \((2,3)\).

**4. The exceptional pair.** For \((a,b)=(2,3)\), there is one node and exactly three chambers. The vertical-ray formula shows three distinct winding values. At \((x_0,y_0)=(0,0)\), the two roots of \(T_2(t)=0\) are \(t=\pm1/\sqrt2\), and \(T_3(t)=-t\); the upper intersection has negative \(T_2'\), so \(w(0,0)=-1\). At \((2,0)\), the roots are \(t=\pm\sqrt{3/2}\), and \(T_3(t)=3t\); the upper intersection has positive \(T_2'\), so \(w(2,0)=1\). At \((0,2)\), both intersections lie below the ray origin and \(w(0,2)=0\). These points avoid the curve. Hence \(\mathbb Q[w]\) has dimension three, equal to \(\dim A_{2,3}\), and generation holds. This completes the proof.

## Verification
The self-contained `verify.py` uses integer arithmetic to check the parity/node formula and the strict deficiency inequality for every coprime pair \(2\le a<b\le200\), and symbolically checks the signs and relative heights needed for the three exceptional winding witnesses. Running `python verify.py` prints `CHEBYSHEV_CHAMBER_CHECK_OK`. This finite check is an additional reproducibility test; the parameter-uniform theorem is proved by the preceding arguments, not by bounded enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Yoshinaga, *Regions of totally real arrangements of rational curves*, arXiv:2611.00861v1 (first public October 31, 2026), introduces the filtration, proves the winding basis for its degree-one step (Corollary 5.4), calculates the Chebyshev node and chamber counts (Section 7.2), and notes failure of degree-one generation for the individual \((3,4)\) example (Section 8). These established statements are prerequisites, not claimed as new. The present result establishes the previously unstated **if-and-only-if classification of the entire coprime Chebyshev family** and the quantified deficiency using the vertical projection-degree obstruction.

Koseleff and Pecker, *Chebyshev diagrams for rational knots*, arXiv:0906.4083, give the parametrization and node count and study knot projections. Those curve facts alone do not compare the degree-one-generated winding subalgebra with the full real chamber algebra. The Varchenko–Gelfand results for hyperplane arrangements, including those in Yagi and Yoshinaga (arXiv:2509.19905), concern different objects and do not imply the curved-arrangement dichotomy.

## Limitations
The theorem is about the filtration attached to the chosen affine chart and a single Chebyshev component, with a line at infinity. It gives a lower bound, not generally the exact dimension of the proper generated subalgebra. The criterion for arbitrary polynomial curves, other affine charts, and the broader geometrical meaning of the filtration are not classified here. Priority remains provisional: earlier winding-number work might contain an equivalent implicit result. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
