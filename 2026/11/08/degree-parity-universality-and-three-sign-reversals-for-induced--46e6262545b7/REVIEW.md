# Research assessment

## Correctness
PASS. The full conditional degree-parity argument proves a precise all-order identity. Its only probabilistic step—independence of edges external to the conditioned induced subgraph—is exact. The graph automorphism factor gives the correct unlabelled-subset mean. The strict three-threshold corollary follows from factoring an odd power of \(q\) against \(1-5q^4\). An independent program exhausts finite graphs and checks 420 exact rational equalities; these are checks, not substitutes for the proof.

## Originality
PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty. The inspected primary work on even-degree graphs, joint counts of vertex degrees, and motif-count means/variances does not directly establish the normalized mixed covariance or the three sign changes. The closely relevant Lin thesis *Motif counts, clustering coefficients, and vertex degrees in models of random networks* (2008) could not be located in accessible full text; an implicit generating-function reduction is a residual risk. The known parity-character and induced-motif expectation identities are not asserted novel. The novelty claim is limited to the joint identity and phase consequences.

## Value
PASS. The all-motif shape-free dependence law eliminates detailed motif topology from a mixed finite-sample covariance, in sharp contrast with variances of motifs. The path example has three exact internal sign changes in one natural mixed graph statistic for infinitely many graph sizes, providing a nontrivial dependency phase portrait useful for motif-and-degree diagnostics.

## Closest literature and limits
Grimmett and Janson (arXiv:0709.3039) study conditioned even-degree graphs, not the unrestricted mixed covariance. Lin and Reinert (arXiv:0912.4812) derive multivariate degree-count approximations and covariances, not induced motif cross-moments. Matias et al. (REVSTAT 2006) analyze motif-count means and variances; Picard et al. (November 2006 report) similarly analyze motif-count distribution approximations. The result is only for homogeneous independent-edge graphs. Historical priority and independent validation remain unconfirmed.
