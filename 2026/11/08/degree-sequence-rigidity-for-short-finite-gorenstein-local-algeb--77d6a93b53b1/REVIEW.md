# Mathematical review of degree-sequence rigidity for short finite Gorenstein local algebras

## Correctness — PASS
The algebra is explicitly associative and commutative, since its maximal ideal has cube zero and its quadratic multiplication tensor is symmetric. The nondegenerate tensor implies that the socle is precisely the one-dimensional square of the maximal ideal. Its graph has \(q^{n+1}-1\) vertices. Counting the kernel of \(B(u,-)\) and taking care of the exclusion of the vertex itself gives exactly three potential degrees, with a characteristic-sensitive difference between isotropic and anisotropic vectors. Finite-field quadratic Gauss sums and Frobenius-semilinearity yield the isotropic counts. Standard finite-field symmetric-form classification completes the isomorphism reconstruction. A separate direct finite-field graph verifier passed 109 instances, including extension fields; these checks corroborate, but do not certify, the universal arguments. No unproved infinite step is inferred from enumerations.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources
The closest structural antecedent is Ganesh Kadu's Proposition 3.6 (arXiv:2010.06924), which identifies the products with bilinear orthogonality and the Gorenstein/nondegeneracy condition. The form classification is also classical; neither element is presented as new. Arunkumar et al. (arXiv:2207.11741) investigate induced-subgraph universality and restrictions for zero-divisor graphs. The 2024 full text by Ndago, Oduor, and Ojiema studies adjacency, distance, and Laplacian matrices for a larger quadratic radical layer, with \(\dim(\mathfrak m^2)=s(s+1)/2\), not this one-dimensional layer for \(s\ge2\). Kadu and Tanpure (arXiv:2609.36021) compare three compression variants of graphs of the same ring; that is not graph-degree recovery of the underlying ring. Distinct mathematical research records on chain rings and reduced products were compared without finding stronger coverage. The claimed addition is the exact all-characteristic degree-multiset reconstruction of full algebra-isomorphism type for the stated family. Search cannot establish publication priority; a more general ring-reconstruction theorem might imply the result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Recoverability of an Artinian local algebra from a coarse graph statistic is a natural structural question because zero-divisor graphs ordinarily forget multiplication. The all-dimensional theorem detects the two finite-field Witt/similarity types exactly where they exist, including the alternating/nonalternating dichotomy in characteristic two, and does so from degrees rather than full adjacency. It supplies a natural complete classification for an infinite family, not an arbitrary small instance or a parameter substitution. The boundary is restricted to equicharacteristic Gorenstein Hilbert function \((1,n,1)\), and no statement is made about other ring classes.
