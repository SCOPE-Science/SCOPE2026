# Degree-two Blaschke failure of wandering generation at the sharp positive Bergman-weight threshold

## Finding
Let \(A^2_\alpha(\mathbb D)\), \(\alpha>-1\), be the weighted Bergman Hilbert space with its usual area norm. An invariant subspace \(\mathcal M\) of a multiplier \(M_B\) has the wandering subspace property (WSP) when
\[
\mathcal M=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{B^n f:n\geq0,\ f\in\mathcal M\ominus B\mathcal M\}.
\]
For every \(0<\alpha\leq1\), **the smallest degree among nonconstant finite Blaschke products whose multiplication operators admit a closed invariant subspace failing WSP is exactly two**.

More precisely, set
\[
q=\alpha+2,\qquad \rho_\alpha=\tan^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2q}\right).
\]
For every \(\alpha>0\), \(0<\rho_\alpha<1\). Choose *any* real \(a\) with \(\rho_\alpha<a<1\), and put
\[
B_{\alpha,a}(z)=\frac{z^2-a}{1-az^2},\qquad
\mathcal E=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{z^{2n}:n\geq0\}\subset A^2_\alpha(\mathbb D).
\]
Then \(B_{\alpha,a}\) is a degree-two finite Blaschke product, \(\mathcal E\) is \(M_{B_{\alpha,a}}\)-invariant and cyclic (generated by the constant function), and
\[
\dim(\mathcal E\ominus B_{\alpha,a}\mathcal E)=1,
\quad
\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{B_{\alpha,a}^{n}(\mathcal E\ominus B_{\alpha,a}\mathcal E):n\geq0\}\ne\mathcal E.
\]
Indeed, every element in the generated subspace vanishes at
\[
z_0=i\sqrt{\rho_\alpha/a}\in\mathbb D,
\]
whereas the constant function does not. This construction also works for \(\alpha>1\), but degree one is already sufficient in that range.

The exact transition behind the witness is the even-degree reproducing kernel
\[
F_\alpha(t)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(\alpha+2)_{2n}}{(2n)!}\,t^n,
\qquad |t|<1.
\]
For \(-1<\alpha\leq1\), this analytic function has **no zero** in the open unit disk if \(\alpha\leq0\), and has **exactly one**, a simple zero \(t=-\rho_\alpha\), if \(0<\alpha\leq1\). The root enters from the boundary at \(\alpha=0\), with
\[
1-\rho_\alpha\sim\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha \quad(\alpha\downarrow0).
\]
The zero-free statement for \(\alpha\leq0\) concerns this kernel and construction only; it is not a classification of all degree-two Blaschke multipliers in that parameter range.

## Assumptions and scope
The unit disk is \(\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}\), normalized area measure is \(dA=dx\,dy/\pi\), and
\[
\|f\|_\alpha^2=(\alpha+1)\int_{\mathbb D}|f(z)|^2(1-|z|^2)^\alpha\,dA(z).
\]
Here \((q)_m=q(q+1)\cdots(q+m-1)\), with \((q)_0=1\). Degree refers to the number of zeros of a *nonconstant* finite Blaschke product, counted with multiplicity; unimodular constant multipliers are excluded. All closures and orthogonal complements are taken in the original weighted Bergman Hilbert norm, not an equivalent renorming. The root and WSP obstruction are assertions about the entire infinite-dimensional space, not finite truncations.

## Proof
**1. Exact even-kernel identity and its zeros.** Monomial orthogonality and beta integration yield
\[
\|z^m\|_\alpha^2=\frac{\Gamma(q)m!}{\Gamma(m+q)},\qquad
K_{\mathcal E}(z,w)=\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{(q)_{2n}}{(2n)!}(z^2\overline w^{\,2})^n=F_\alpha(z^2\overline w^{\,2}).
\]
The binomial theorem, with analytic branches fixed at the origin, gives for \(|u|<1\)
\[
F_\alpha(u^2)=\frac{(1-u)^{-q}+(1+u)^{-q}}2.
\]
For \(u\ne0\), this expression vanishes precisely when
\[
\left(\frac{1+u}{1-u}\right)^q=-1,
\]
where the power uses the principal logarithm on the right half-plane. The Cayley ratio has positive real part, so its argument lies strictly between \(-\pi/2\) and \(\pi/2\); equality forces its modulus to be one. Thus all zeros have \(u\) purely imaginary, and the positive possible angles are \(\theta=(2j+1)\pi/q\), with odd positive integers \(2j+1<q/2\). In the range \(1<q\leq3\), a solution therefore exists precisely when \(q>2\), and the only scalar solution is
\[
u=i\tan\!\left(\frac\pi{2q}\right),\qquad t=u^2=-\tan^2\!\left(\frac\pi{2q}\right).
\]
Its simplicity follows because \(u\mapsto(1+u)/(1-u)\) has nonzero derivative, and the zero equation has a simple root away from \(u=0\). The boundary expansion follows by differentiating \(\tan^2(\pi/[2(\alpha+2)])\) at \(\alpha=0\), where its derivative is \(-\pi/2\). In fact, the displayed negative root exists for every \(\alpha>0\), including \(\alpha>1\); no exhaustive zero count above \(\alpha=1\) is needed.

**2. Multiplication on the even subspace.** Identify \(G(w)=\sum_{n\geq0}g_nw^n\) with \(G(z^2)\in\mathcal E\). The induced norm is \(\sum_n|g_n|^2\|z^{2n}\|_\alpha^2\), equivalent to the ordinary \(A_\alpha^2\) norm because
\[
\frac{\|z^{2n}\|_\alpha^2}{\|z^n\|_\alpha^2}\longrightarrow2^{-(\alpha+1)}>0.
\]
For the disk automorphism \(b_a(w)=(w-a)/(1-aw)\), multiplication is bounded and bounded below on this induced Hilbert space, and its closed range is precisely the codimension-one subspace of functions vanishing at \(a\). To justify the division assertion: in the ordinary weighted Bergman norm, division by \(w\) is bounded on functions vanishing at zero, since
\[
\frac{\|w^{n-1}\|_\alpha^2}{\|w^n\|_\alpha^2}=\frac{n+q-1}{n}\leq q\quad(n\geq1).
\]
The weighted unitary automorphism action transports this assertion from zero to \(a\), and norm equivalence transports it to the induced space. Consequently,
\[
B_{\alpha,a}\mathcal E=\{f\in\mathcal E:f(\sqrt a)=0\},\qquad
\mathcal E\ominus B_{\alpha,a}\mathcal E=\mathbb C K_{\mathcal E}(\cdot,\sqrt a).
\]
Also \(\mathcal E=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{B_{\alpha,a}^n:n\geq0\}\): the inverse identity \(w=(b_a(w)+a)/(1+ab_a(w))\) and the norm-convergent power series for \((1+ab_a)^{-1}\) approximate all monomials \(w^n\) by polynomials in \(b_a\); monomials are dense in the induced space.

**3. The obstruction.** Fix \(\alpha>0\), \(a\in(\rho_\alpha,1)\), and let \(z_0=i\sqrt{\rho_\alpha/a}\). Then \(a z_0^2=-\rho_\alpha\), so
\[
K_{\mathcal E}(z_0,\sqrt a)=F_\alpha(-\rho_\alpha)=0.
\]
Every member of the closed invariant subspace generated by the one-dimensional wandering space vanishes at \(z_0\), since evaluation is continuous. But \(1\in\mathcal E\) and \(1(z_0)=1\). Thus the generated subspace is proper. This supplies the asserted degree-two examples.

**4. Minimality for \(0<\alpha\leq1\).** A degree-one finite Blaschke product is a unimodular scalar multiple of a disk automorphism \(\phi\). The weighted composition map \(Uf(z)=(\phi'(z))^{q/2}f(\phi(z))\), using any consistent analytic logarithm of the nonvanishing derivative, is unitary on \(A^2_\alpha\) by the weighted change-of-variables formula and intertwines \(M_z\) with \(M_\phi\). Multiplication by a unimodular scalar does not change WSP. By the weighted Bergman shift theorem of Shimorin (also recorded in Lin--Xu--Yu and Xu--Yu), \(M_z\) has WSP for every closed invariant subspace when \(-1<\alpha\leq1\). Hence no degree-one Blaschke product provides a failure in \(0<\alpha\leq1\). Step 3 gives degree two, proving exact minimality.

## Verification
The standalone script `kernel_checker.py` performs 363 exact-rational assertions for the even kernel series at integer parameters \(q=2,3,4\) and checks the explicit \(\alpha=1\), \(a=2/3\), \(z_0=i/\sqrt2\) witness: \(a z_0^2=-1/3\) and \(F_1(-1/3)=0\). These calculations are corroboration only. The exhaustive kernel-zero and infinite-dimensional WSP statements are proved above through analytic identities, unitary equivalence, and bounded point evaluation.

## Relationship to prior work
Xu and Yu (2026) prove the wandering theorem for powers of weighted Bergman shifts and the existence, for every \(\alpha>-1\), of some finite Blaschke multiplier failing WSP on a cyclic invariant subspace. Their Theorem 12 uses sufficiently large degrees depending on the weight, and their explicit classical Bergman example has degree three. Their kernel formula and division/cyclicity lemma support the setting but do not give the sharp degree-two threshold or the exact least degree for \(0<\alpha\leq1\). Lin, Xu, and Yu (2026) establish the shift's sharp WSP range, not a finite Blaschke degree threshold. Gallardo-Gutiérrez, Partington, and Seco (2020) prove WSP for finite Blaschke products after selecting equivalent Hilbert norms, which cannot be transferred to the original inner product used here. Other recent exact Bergman-kernel zero formulas concern different radial weights and do not imply the multiplier/wandering assertion.

## Limitations
The minimum degree is classified for \(0<\alpha\leq1\). The kernel zero-free statement at \(\alpha\leq0\) does **not** rule out counterexamples from other degree-two finite Blaschke products or invariant subspaces. At \(\alpha>1\), degree-one failure is already known; the degree-two construction is not minimal there. No optimal choice of \(a\), quantitative codimension of the generated wandering space, or classification of arbitrary finite Blaschke products is claimed. Publication priority has not been independently certified.
