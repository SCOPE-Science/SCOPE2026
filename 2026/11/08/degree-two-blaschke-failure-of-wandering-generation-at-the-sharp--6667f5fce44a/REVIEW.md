# Scientific review: degree-two wandering obstruction

## Correctness — PASS
The weighted monomial normalization is explicitly calculated, giving the even subspace's reproducing kernel. The even binomial identity, the Cayley map into the right half-plane, and the principal-branch argument classify the kernel zeros for \(-1<\alpha\le1\) without relying on finite sampling. The even-coefficient Hilbert norm is equivalent to the usual weighted Bergman norm (same \(\alpha\)); division by an automorphism factor is bounded on the vanishing codimension-one subspace. Consequently the wandering subspace is precisely the evaluation kernel at \(\sqrt a\). Its generator vanishes at a second point while the constant function does not, proving the failed spanning claim. The degree-one lower bound follows from conformal unitary equivalence and the known shift wandering theorem for \(\alpha\le1\). The root boundary expansion follows by differentiation. The standalone 363 exact-rational checks corroborate algebraic identities but are not treated as infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)
The closest work, Xu--Yu, *The Beurling-type theorem for powers of weighted Bergman shifts*, arXiv:2610.37035v1, gives an existence theorem for finite Blaschke products of sufficiently large degree (Theorem 12), and its explicitly displayed classical Bergman counterexample has degree three (Example 13). Neither assertion identifies the least degree at positive weights. Lin--Xu--Yu, arXiv:2609.36723v1, gives the shift threshold, not a quadratic finite-Blaschke witness. The 2020 Dirichlet-space paper obtains wandering under an equivalent changed norm, so it does not cover the original Hilbert inner product. Other published exact Bergman kernel-zero results use different weights or invariant-subspace kernels and do not logically imply the quadratic WSP statement. The source manuscript, related primary texts, previous claim summaries, and scientific database matches were examined. No equivalent or stronger exact-minimum theorem was identified; an overlooked older formulation remains a real priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The least Blaschke degree is a canonical invariant of the operator family, not a freely selected truncation parameter. Its determination at two for every \(0<\alpha\le1\) closes the qualitative and quantitative gap between a complete degree-one wandering theorem and a general existence construction of unspecified, potentially large degree. The explicit even-kernel root supplies a geometric mechanism and its transition at zero weight; the result has neither trivial formal inheritance from the shift theorem nor merely a numerical example.

## Scope and residual risks
The result does not establish whether every quadratic finite Blaschke multiplier has WSP for nonpositive weight; it also does not classify all invariant subspaces, arbitrary rational inner multipliers, or sharp gap dimensions. Another earlier source may contain an equivalent quadratic inner-function example or an indirect general theorem. These limitations do not alter the analytical proof or the stated limited minimality claim.
