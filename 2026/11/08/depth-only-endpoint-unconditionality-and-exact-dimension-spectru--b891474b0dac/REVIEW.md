# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Each terminal kernel row collapses to a path-only signed-mass expression because every first-divergence sibling subtree retains its exact dyadic total mass irrespective of pruning. The finite Bellman envelope is proved for every state in the closed interval, solves to the displayed \(F_h\), and yields the full equality cases. The fixed-dimension spectrum is established by the two necessary height inequalities and by a one-leaf-at-a-time refinement construction covering every intermediate integer leaf count. Direct exact-rational kernel enumeration checks 1,275 leaf rows on all 197 ordered binary tree shapes of up to seven leaves; it is not substituted for the infinite-family proof.

## Originality
PASS, with bibliographic qualification. Geiss–Müller, *Haar type and Carleson constants* (2008), treats arbitrary Haar collections but proves Banach-space Haar-type/Carleson equivalences for finite \(p\), not the signed \(L^\infty\) endpoint invariant or a finite-leaf spectrum. Gach–Nickl–Spokoiny (public 2011) treats spatially adaptive Haar resolution in density estimation, not operator sign norms. Osękowski (2013) treats restricted weak-type Haar projection and martingale bounds, not exact finite-height endpoint norms. The known complete-generation dyadic endpoint formula by itself does not state the behavior of irregular prunings or the full fixed-dimension spectrum; those require the row-reduction and combinatorial realization arguments here. Related unpublished and older indexed literature could still contain equivalent statements.

## Mathematical value
PASS. A spatially adaptive multiresolution basis can have only linearly many terminal atoms but exactly the same worst-case signed sup-norm amplification as a balanced basis with exponentially many atoms. The exact complete spectrum and sharp comb-versus-minimal-height classification quantify this non-obvious limitation of pruning: at equal dimension \(N\), the extremal constants differ by a factor asymptotic to \(N/\log_2N\). This is a natural structural invariant and a complete classification, not an isolated numeric example.

## Prior comparison and limitations
Published full-text comparisons: Geiss–Müller arXiv:0902.1955, Sections 2–3 (Carleson condition and Theorems 3.1–3.2); Gach–Nickl–Spokoiny arXiv:1111.2807, introduction and variable-resolution projection formulation; Osękowski DOI:10.1155/2013/646012, introduction and Theorems 1–4. None of the inspected claims dominates the present complete finite-tree result. The main residual risk is an equivalent endpoint identity in less discoverable early martingale or Haar-basis literature. Arbitrary biases, other local wavelet bases, suppression and infinite trees are excluded.
