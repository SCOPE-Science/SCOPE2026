# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The component identification is taken from the published ribbon Richardson construction, not derived by a bare toric analogy. The proof constructs all \(U\), \(V\), and \(W\) Plücker charts directly from normalized \(2\times n\) representatives. These are exactly smooth affine spaces or smooth-affine factors times rank-one determinantal cones; their union covers the component. The determinantal rank calculation identifies the singular locus, the Segre degree computes multiplicity, and the quotient of the Segre Picard group by the hyperplane line class computes the local divisor class and canonical obstruction. The included Python script checks the relevant finite lattice calculations and finite-field counts, but is not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority reservations

The first source identifies the components and their toric Richardson construction, including a singular threefold example. General positroid smoothness methods and fixed-point multiplicity algorithms exist, and complete matroid Gorenstein classifications concern related but nonidentical global invariants. The newly claimed fact is the uniform **local analytic isomorphism classification and exact reduced singular support** for every maximal rank-two component, plus their multiplicity and canonical class consequences, not those general algorithms. Search comparisons included alternative names (rank-two matroid orbit closures, complete-tripartite toric edge varieties, ribbon Richardson varieties) and previously published mathematical results. No matching statement was located. The abstract-only comparison of arXiv:2609.30257 and inaccessible full text of arXiv:2608.28735 leave a residual historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

These are natural newly introduced algebraic varieties with nontrivial singularities and component geometry. Their local analytic types are a structural classification across the complete infinite rank-two family, not isolated numerical experiments or an arbitrary slice. The result identifies precise loci where the dualizing sheaf fails to be \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier, and gives transverse multiplicities useful in local intersection theory and degeneration analysis. These consequences are not obtained merely from knowing the number of components or their moment polytopes.

## Limits

Higher rank, the full reducible union's singular scheme and all component intersections are outside scope. The priority assessment is provisional, and no independent mathematical verification has been performed.
