# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem gives all quantifiers and uses exact affine coordinates for a full-dimensional quadrilateral. The reverse-containment condition is equivalent to four support inequalities. A nonnegative linear dependence of three outward facet normals gives the lower bound. A concrete center, which makes the same three inequalities equalities and has a nonnegative fourth slack, proves sharpness, including the parallelogram endpoint. The chord-length tent geometry of every triangle proves the claimed reduction, and two monotonic branches yield the sharp complete directional range. An independently written standard-library rational checker reconstructs both certificates in 11,724 rational cases; the universal proof is analytic.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk

Kipp–Sagmeister (arXiv:2610.05113v1, Definition 1.1 and Section 5) supplies the symmetrization and process-convergence setting but no formula for one-step Minkowski asymmetry of a triangle. Bakaev–Yehudayoff (arXiv:2607.03815v1, Sections 1 and 3) gives general Minkowski symmetry theory and near-simplex stability, not the two-diagonal exact formula. Classical endpoint asymmetry facts are explicitly prior. Related exact works on centroid-chord asymmetry for quadrilaterals, Minkowski asymmetry of special hexagons, and preservation of central symmetry by generalized Steiner operations concern different functionals, polygon classes, or input hypotheses. The independent status of an equivalent much older quadrilateral parametrization is a residual risk, not ruled out by unsuccessful searches.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The formula resolves an entire natural two-dimensional affine moduli space via intrinsic diagonal fractions, and supplies an exact answer to the one-step quantitative symmetry question naturally posed by the recent interpolation between shaking and Steiner symmetrization. It provides a sharp all-direction inequality and precise optimizer geometry, including discontinuous changes of expression at the natural parameter boundary \(m=\lambda\). These are structural and complete results, not a selected numerical example, although they are restricted to planar triangles and quadrilaterals.

## Limits and literature

Only the formulas explicitly proved in the result are reviewed. A separate historical search cannot establish uniqueness, and neither finite rational testing nor a self-review establishes independent certification. The proof has not been extended to arbitrary convex polygons or repeated processes.
