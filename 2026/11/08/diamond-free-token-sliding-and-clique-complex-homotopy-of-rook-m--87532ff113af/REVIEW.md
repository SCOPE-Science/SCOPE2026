# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For a clique of at least two token-sliding states, a fixed adjacent pair determines a unique common \(k-1\)-vertex core; a competing Johnson-top triangle would contain adjacent input vertices and violate state independence. This establishes the all-graph clique count. An induced diamond in the token-sliding graph would lift to an induced diamond of the input graph. Maximal cliques in a diamond-free graph share no edges, so deleting their nonanchor higher faces yields an explicit sequence of elementary collapses to dimension one. Rook placement enumeration supplies every coefficient, and connectivity plus the Euler characteristic supplies the circle-wedge rank. The permutation arguments verify girth, bipartiteness, and four-cycle census, with the known star-transposition diameter correctly attributed. Boundary cases with \(k=1\) or no vacant rows/columns were checked. The standalone verifier checks 41 rook instances and 5,405 graph-and-token-size cases; finite checks corroborate rather than replace the proofs.

**Originality — PASS, provisionally.** Avis and Hoang's arXiv:2203.16861 contains the common-core triangle lemma in its full accessible text, so that observation is specifically attributed. The authors discuss girth and clique realization but do not state the all-graph clique-count identity, diamond-free preservation, one-dimensional clique-complex collapse, or the exact rook generating function and homotopy profile. The classical star-transposition Cayley-graph diameter is expressly excluded from the new contribution. Other relevant matching/chessboard-complex findings concern the independence complex of the original rook graph, not the clique complex of its fixed-cardinality token-sliding reconfiguration graph. The nine database searches and accessible earlier claims identified no implication covering this combined theorem. Residual risk remains from differently named reconfiguration-topology or clique-count results not indexed or inaccessible in the literature searched.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The theorem gives a structural dimension reduction for every diamond-free input graph and, on a canonical two-parameter matching family, a closed all-clique enumerator, integral homotopy type, exact first Betti number, sharp girth phases and a precise four-cycle census. These quantify topology of *configuration spaces under constrained moves*, rather than routine properties of the input rook graph itself. The unrestricted structural collapse and sharp parameter boundaries make the result useful beyond the finite tests.

**Limitations.** No independent audit was performed. Literature priority remains provisional; a different token-movement convention would not fall within the theorem. The general arbitrary-graph clique identity alone does not imply connectivity or homotopy of arbitrary reconfiguration graphs.
