# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** For each parameter, the bounded prefix discrepancy is zero exactly at substitution-block boundaries and has opposite signs inside a block depending on its parent letter. This yields an equivalence between pure Abelian periods and horizontal discrepancy progressions. The base-\(L\) column maps are either constant or bijective; the shifted progression at length \(L^t\) freezes precisely at a constant column among the lowest \(t-1\) digits. The complement count \((L-1)(2\min(a,b))^{t-1}\) then follows by independent digit choices. An executable finite checker independently corroborates 4,200 instances and 100 censuses; the proof handles all parameters.

**Originality (PASS, with priority risk).** The 2026 uniform-morphism criterion of Filimonova and Puzynina, Theorem 4, guarantees an Abelian period for a rank-one fixed point but does not determine which exact substitution powers work after each shift. The 2013 bounded weak-Abelian-periodicity theorem of Avgustinovich and Puzynina also classifies existence at the fixed point rather than the shifted exact power spectrum. Indexed results inspected for equivalent digit criteria, coincidence-column reductions, and shift censuses did not establish a stronger statement. An unindexed specialization remains possible; these comparisons are not a proof of historical uniqueness.

**Value (PASS).** The full parameter family exposes a genuine shift-versus-scale phenomenon: the no-coincidence case has no nonaligned power periods, while the coincidence case has a sharp first power and a computable sparse exceptional set. The exact census at every natural substitution scale is a useful quantitative invariant in the emerging study of Abelian periodicity of morphic words, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

**Limits.** The theorem does not classify non-power Abelian periods for nonaligned shifts. Independent verification and historical priority confirmation remain outstanding.
