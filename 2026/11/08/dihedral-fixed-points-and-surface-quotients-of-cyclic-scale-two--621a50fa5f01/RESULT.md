# Dihedral fixed points and surface quotients of cyclic scale-two Rips complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge7\), let \(X_n\) be the clique complex of the square \(C_n^2\) of the ordinary cycle on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Equivalently, this is the inclusive graph-metric Vietoris–Rips complex at distance two. The dihedral group \(D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\,srs=r^{-1}\rangle\) acts by \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\).

The complete geometric fixed-point classification is as follows. Every nonidentity rotation has empty fixed-point space. Every reflection \(s_a(i)=a-i\) has a fixed-point space homotopy equivalent to two points (\(S^0\)): for odd \(n\) it is one closed interval and one isolated point; for even \(n\) and even \(a\) it is two closed intervals; for even \(n\) and odd \(a\) it is two isolated points. Thus \(|X_n|^H\) is empty for every subgroup \(H\) containing a nonidentity rotation, and is homotopy equivalent to \(S^0\) for any order-two reflection subgroup.

The quotient of \(|X_n|\) by a rotational subgroup of order \(d\mid n\) is an annulus if \(n/d\) is even and a Möbius band if \(n/d\) is odd. The quotient by **every subgroup containing a reflection** is a closed topological disk. In particular, \(|X_n|/D_n\) is a disk. The trivial-subgroup quotient is the original annulus for even \(n\), or Möbius band for odd \(n\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), and two distinct vertices are adjacent when their cyclic distance is at most two. Its clique complex contains exactly the \(n\) triangles \(T_i=\{i,i+1,i+2\}\) together with their faces, with all indices modulo \(n\). Geometric fixed points are points in the geometric realization fixed by the action, not merely realizations of vertexwise-fixed subcomplexes. A subgroup containing a reflection but no nonidentity rotation is necessarily generated by one reflection.

## Proof
**The cyclic strip.** Every edge \(\{i,i+1\}\) belongs to precisely the triangles \(T_{i-1}\) and \(T_i\), while each edge \(\{i,i+2\}\) belongs only to \(T_i\). No vertex is on a nonmanifold link for \(n\ge7\); therefore these \(n\) triangles form a connected compact triangulated surface with nonempty boundary and with Euler characteristic \(n-2n+n=0\). The boundary edges \(\{i,i+2\}\) form \(\gcd(n,2)\) cycles. The classification of compact surfaces yields a Möbius band for odd \(n\), and an annulus for even \(n\). Alternatively, the triangles form the standard cyclic strip with one boundary cycle for odd \(n\) and two for even \(n\).

**Rotations.** If \(r^j\ne1\) stabilizes a nonempty face, that face must be a union of orbits of this rotation and have at most three vertices. A two-point orbit has antipodal vertices, at cyclic distance at least three; a three-point orbit has vertices spaced \(n/3\ge3\), likewise not a clique. Larger orbits do not fit in a face. Consequently no nonempty simplex is stabilized. Barycentric subdivision therefore has no vertex fixed by the rotation, so the geometric fixed-point set is empty. This also proves that every rotational subgroup acts freely.

**Reflections.** Set \(s_a(i)=a-i\). A reflection-invariant nonempty face is either (i) a fixed vertex satisfying \(2i=a\), (ii) an exchanged pair \(\{u,a-u\}\) that is an edge, or (iii) an invariant triangle \(\{v-1,v,v+1\}\) whose central vertex satisfies \(2v=a\). In barycentric subdivision, fixed vertices represent precisely these invariant faces, with edges whenever one such face is properly contained in another. If \(n\) is odd, the invariant-face poset has one three-element 'V'-shaped poset (a fixed vertex and an exchanged edge both contained in an invariant triangle), and one extra isolated exchanged edge. If \(n\) is even and \(a\) is even, it has two separate copies of the same 'V' shape. If \(n\) is even and \(a\) is odd, it has two isolated exchanged edges and no invariant triangles or vertices. Thus the fixed set consists of two contractible components, with the asserted homeomorphism types. Since any subgroup with a nonidentity rotation has no fixed points, the subgroup-fixed classification follows.

**Cyclic quotients.** A subgroup \(\langle r^{n/d}\rangle\) of order \(d\) acts freely on the triangulated band. The quotient is a connected compact surface of Euler characteristic zero and nonempty boundary. If \(n\) is odd, its sole boundary component descends to one quotient component. If \(n\) is even, the rotation by \(n/d\) steps exchanges the two parity boundary cycles exactly when \(n/d\) is odd. Thus the quotient has one boundary component when \(n/d\) is odd and two when it is even. The classification of compact surfaces identifies the quotient as respectively a Möbius band and an annulus.

**Dihedral quotients.** Let \(H\) be a subgroup containing a reflection. Its rotational subgroup has order \(d\), hence \(|H|=2d\) with exactly \(d\) reflections. All nonidentity rotations act freely, and each reflection has fixed-set Euler characteristic two. The finite-group Euler-characteristic formula therefore gives \(\chi(|X_n|/H)=\frac1{2d}(d\cdot2)=1\). Point stabilizers are either trivial or generated by a reflection, since rotations act freely. Their local actions on the surface are either mirror reflections or half-turns, and the quotient is a compact connected topological surface with nonempty boundary. The classification of compact surfaces now forces a disk.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` rebuilds every face directly from cyclic graph distance, confirms the face vector \((n,2n,n)\) and checks invariance of all nonempty faces under every nonidentity rotation and reflection for all \(7\le n\le30\). For each reflection it constructs the full poset of invariant faces, using **all** strict face inclusions in the fixed barycentric subdivision, and checks the two-component patterns. Execution of the packaged code produces the embedded `verify_output.txt`. These finite checks support, but are not substituted for, the general proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
The recent work of B. A. Wani, *Edge ideals of complements of a class of circular interval graphs*, arXiv:2609.31105 (first public September 25, 2026), identifies annuli and Möbius bands among related circle-power clique complexes. The ambient homeomorphism type is not claimed as new here. The present claim is the **all-subgroup geometric fixed-point and quotient classification under the labeled dihedral action**. Independent searches also considered the equivalent descriptions as scale-two cyclic Rips complexes, circular-interval graph clique complexes, and triangulated cyclic strips.

## Limitations
This does not give a classification for graph powers beyond two or for \(n<7\), where extra simplices occur. The quotient and fixed-point statements are **topological**, not assertions of equivariant simplicial collapse. The complete text of the motivating 2026 preprint was inaccessible during comparison; equivalent results hidden in inaccessible appendices or unindexed sources cannot be ruled out. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
