# Scientific review of the Dirichlet–Hilbert pair-distance law

## Correctness — PASS
Equation (6) is the unhalved cross-ratio Hilbert distance on an open simplex, and normalized independent gamma vectors give the stated independent symmetric Dirichlet points. The normalization cancels from the max–min log ratio, leaving **independent** log beta-prime variables rather than an intractable dependent vector. Conditioning on the unique sample minimum gives the all-parameter CDF. Dominated convergence and a beta integral give the exact short-radius coefficient. At concentration one, the hypergeometric beta integral and logistic extreme order statistics give the exact CDF and mean. The exponential tails, jointly assessed, yield independent Gumbel extremes; tail bounds give convergence of the first two moments. The package's checker passed independent numeric CDF, direct-normalized-sample, asymptotic-moment, small-radius and special-function tests. The checker does not prove any infinite-dimensional assertion.

## Originality — PASS, provisionally
Foertsch–Karlsson (2004) classify Hilbert geometries isometric to normed spaces but do not discuss random pair distances. Nielsen–Sun (2017) analyze Hilbert simplex computation and clustering. Nielsen–Sun (2023, Figure 7 and Section 2) display histograms of uniformly random Hilbert-simplex pair distances and give their metric formula, but not the claimed complete distribution, beta-prime reduction, or sharp two-sided high-dimensional scaling. The earlier fixed-center Euclidean ball-mass identity implicitly yields a double integral for the **uniform** two-point CDF when averaged over its center; it does not cover general symmetric Dirichlet concentration or the concentration-dependent two-Gumbel and variance laws. published-finding corpus searches for projective/Hilbert-distance Dirichlet ratios, harmonic means, and joint Gumbel extremes found no implication-equivalent record. The short-radius exponent alone is a standard dimension effect, but the exact coefficient and induced metric law are not reported in the inspected primary sources. Residual risk: obscure unindexed statistics or geometric-probability literature, including potential transformations of beta-prime sample ranges, could have stated equivalent laws.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Uniform Hilbert-distance histograms were already used to assess geometric embeddings; exact distributions instead give non-simulation calibration. The natural Dirichlet concentration family has a genuine change in distance scale and a new sharp centering constant as dimension grows. The statement treats all finite simplex dimensions and all fixed positive concentrations rather than one parameter slice; the exact small-ball coefficient and fluctuation variance give different local and global geometric diagnostics. These are explicit natural metric invariants of an independently motivated probability-simplex model, not merely an arbitrary parameter substitution. No implication for nonsimplicial domains or arbitrary sampling laws is claimed.

## Principal comparisons and limits
The metric/isometry results of Foertsch and Karlsson are background rather than a quantitative pair-distance law. Nielsen and Sun's experimental histograms for \(10^6\) uniform pairs already cover the *phenomenon*, not the exact finite distribution. For the uniform case, the prior fixed-center identity partially covers a special case by implicit integration; its extension to a general symmetric Dirichlet sampling density and the two-extreme limit requires the independent work documented here. Numerical tests are finite and cannot establish the limit. Historical priority has not been independently verified.
