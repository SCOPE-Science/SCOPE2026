# Distance-driven reversal of search efficiency under a persistent two-state reset clock

## Finding

Consider a Brownian searcher with diffusion generator \(D\partial_{xx}\) on \(( -\infty,L]\), an absorbing target at \(L>0\), and instantaneous returns to \(0\). The reset-clock environment is an independently switching, stationary, symmetric two-state continuous-time Markov chain, with switching rate \(\nu>0\) in either direction. Conditional reset rates are \(r+\delta\) and \(r-\delta\), where \(r>\delta>0\); a particle reset does **not** change the environmental state. Initially the searcher is at \(0\) and the environment has probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\). Let \(M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)\) denote the mean first-passage time to \(L\), and let \(M_{\mathrm{const}}(L)\) be that for an ordinary constant reset rate \(r\), with the same diffusivity.

Define
\[
 s=\sqrt{\nu^2+\delta^2},\qquad b_{\pm}=r+\nu\pm s,\qquad c=\frac{1+\nu/s}{2},\qquad h_L(b)=\frac{b}{\exp(L\sqrt{b/D})-1}.
\]
Then \(0<b_-<r<b_+\), \(1/2<c<1\), and the mean search time is **exactly**
\[
\boxed{M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)=\frac{2\nu+(1-c)h_L(b_-)+c h_L(b_+)}{h_L(b_-)h_L(b_+)+2\nu[c h_L(b_-)+(1-c)h_L(b_+)]}.}
\]
The constant-clock benchmark is \(M_{\mathrm{const}}(L)=1/h_L(r)\). For *every* quadruple \((D,r,\delta,\nu)\) satisfying the strict conditions above, there are distances \(0<L_{\mathrm{near}}<L_{\mathrm{far}}<\infty\) such that
\[
M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)>M_{\mathrm{const}}(L)\quad(0<L<L_{\mathrm{near}}),\qquad
M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)<M_{\mathrm{const}}(L)\quad(L>L_{\mathrm{far}}).
\]
Consequently the two search-time curves have **at least one finite positive crossing**. No uniqueness claim is made about the crossings.

More sharply, as \(L\downarrow0\),
\[
 M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)-M_{\mathrm{const}}(L)
 =\frac{L}{\sqrt D}\left[\frac{c}{\sqrt{b_-}}+\frac{1-c}{\sqrt{b_+}}-\frac1{\sqrt r}\right]+O(L^2),
\]
where the bracket is strictly positive. As \(L\to\infty\),
\[
\frac{M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)}{M_{\mathrm{const}}(L)}
\sim \frac{r}{c b_-}\exp\!\left[-\frac{L}{\sqrt D}(\sqrt r-\sqrt{b_-})\right]\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus variability at a *fixed mean resetting rate* inevitably harms sufficiently nearby-target search but helps sufficiently distant-target search, regardless of the strictly positive switching frequency or contrast.

For illustration, with \(D=r=\nu=1\) and \(\delta=1/2\), the means are approximately \(0.2283244195\) versus \(0.2214027582\) at \(L=0.2\), and \(18.557622395\) versus \(19.085536923\) at \(L=3\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope

The Brownian motion, phase-jump clock, and conditional reset clocks are independent given the phase path. More precisely, the phase process \(J_t\in\{1,2\}\) has row generator \(Q=\begin{psmallmatrix}-\nu&\nu\\\nu&-\nu\end{psmallmatrix}\). Reset events are generated by the Cox hazard \(\lambda_{J_t}\), with \(\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(r+\delta,r-\delta)\). A reset replaces position by \(0\) but leaves \(J_t\) unchanged; the environment switches even during searches and across resets. The search terminates upon first hit of \(L\), without further resetting. We consider the annealed mean over the stationary initial phase, \(M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)=\mathbb E T_L\). All parameters are strictly positive with \(0<\delta<r\). Since the conditional reset rate lies in \([r-\delta,r+\delta]\), every search attempt has a probability of finding the target bounded below by \(\exp(-L\sqrt{(r+\delta)/D})>0\), while its expected duration is at most \(1/(r-\delta)\). Hence \(\mathbb E T_L<\infty\).

## Proof

**1. Exact Markov-renewal equation despite persistent environmental memory.** Let \(H\) be the first-passage time of independent free Brownian motion from \(0\) to \(L\), so \(\mathbb E\exp(-zH)=\exp[-L\sqrt{z/D}]\) for \(z\ge0\). Define \(B=\Lambda-Q\), a symmetric positive-definite matrix, and the matrix functions
\[
 E=\exp[-L\sqrt{B/D}],\qquad F=B^{-1}(I-E).
\]
The killed-phase semigroup before a reset is \(\exp(-Bt)\). For one attempted excursion, the matrix of reset-before-hit probabilities with the phase retained at the reset is
\[
R=\int_0^\infty\Pr(H>t)\exp(-Bt)\Lambda\,dt=F\Lambda.
\]
Indeed \(\int_0^\infty\Pr(H>t)\exp(-bt)dt=(1-\mathbb E e^{-bH})/b\), applied by spectral calculus to \(B\). The vector of expected attempt durations is \(F\mathbf1\). The strong Markov property at resets, with environmental phase retained, therefore gives the mean vector \(m=F\mathbf1+F\Lambda m\). Multiplication by \(F^{-1}\) yields
\[
 m=(h_L(B)-Q)^{-1}\mathbf1,
\qquad h_L(B)=B(\exp[L\sqrt{B/D}]-I)^{-1}.
\]
This derivation does not replace the persistent environment by independent reset trials; the phase state at each reset is retained in \(R\). The substochastic matrix \(R\) has row sums bounded above by \(1-\exp[-L\sqrt{(r+\delta)/D}]\), so \(I-R\) is invertible.

**2. Two-eigenvalue reduction.** In the orthonormal phase basis \(e_{\mathrm{s}}=(1,1)/\sqrt2\), \(e_{\mathrm{a}}=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\),
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}r&\delta\\\delta&r+2\nu\end{pmatrix},\qquad -Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&2\nu\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The eigenvalues of \(B\) are \(b_\pm\). The projector onto \(b_-\) has symmetric-sector diagonal weight \(c=(1+\nu/s)/2\), and antisymmetric-sector diagonal weight \(1-c\). With \(h_\pm=h_L(b_\pm)\), the symmetric and antisymmetric diagonal entries of \(h_L(B)\) are \(H_{00}=ch_-+(1-c)h_+\) and \(H_{11}=(1-c)h_-+ch_+\). Since \(\det h_L(B)=h_-h_+\),
\[
\det(h_L(B)-Q)=h_-h_++2\nu H_{00}.
\]
Taking the symmetric component of \((h_L(B)-Q)^{-1}\mathbf1\), equivalent to the stationary-phase average, gives \((H_{11}+2\nu)/(h_-h_++2\nu H_{00})\), which is the asserted formula.

**3. Strict short-distance disadvantage.** For every fixed \(b>0\), \(h_L(b)=\sqrt{Db}/L+O(1)\). Therefore \(h_L(B)-Q=(\sqrt D/L)B^{1/2}+O(1)\) and its inverse has the expansion \((L/\sqrt D)B^{-1/2}+O(L^2)\). This implies
\[
M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)=\frac{L}{\sqrt D}\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{b_-}}+\frac{1-c}{\sqrt{b_+}}\right)+O(L^2).
\]
The spectral mean satisfies \(cb_-+(1-c)b_+=r\), while \(b_-\ne b_+\) and both weights lie strictly between \(0\) and \(1\). Strict Jensen convexity of \(b\mapsto b^{-1/2}\) gives \(c b_-^{-1/2}+(1-c)b_+^{-1/2}>r^{-1/2}\). Since \(M_{\mathrm{const}}(L)=L/\sqrt{Dr}+O(L^2)\), the small-distance difference is strictly positive.

**4. Strict large-distance advantage.** As \(L\to\infty\), \(h_L(b)\sim b\exp[-L\sqrt{b/D}]\). Since \(b_-<r<b_+\), we have \(h_+/h_-\to0\). The explicit formula, and strictly positive \(\nu,c\), then imply
\[
M_{\mathrm{sw}}(L)\sim\frac1{c h_L(b_-)}\sim\frac{\exp[L\sqrt{b_-/D}]}{c b_-}.
\]
The constant-rate mean is asymptotic to \(\exp[L\sqrt{r/D}]/r\); their ratio has the claimed exponentially vanishing equivalent. Continuity for \(L>0\) and opposite strict signs near \(0\) and \(\infty\) prove at least one positive crossover.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone Python script `verify.py` evaluates the closed formula independently against a matrix renewal-resolvent computation using \(F=B^{-1}(I-\exp[-L\sqrt{B/D}])\), over 432 parameter choices. It also compares with a separately discretized two-phase backward boundary-value equation on a truncated interval; four distances give small, convergent finite-difference errors. Its selected examples test both signs and print the numerical values above. The test is deterministic and does not constitute proof of statements quantified over all parameters or distances; those are proved analytically.

Run: `python verify.py` (Python standard library for the renewal tests; NumPy and SciPy additionally allow finite-difference checks, which are skipped if unavailable).

## Relationship to prior work

Arutkin and Reuveni (2025, arXiv:2509.25365v1) introduced independently fluctuating resetting intensities retained through particle resets, derived their stationary propagator and spatial crossover, and explicitly identified first-passage observables as future work. The present theorem treats the genuinely nonrenewal first-passage problem and finds a fixed-mean, target-distance reversal not implied by their stationary-position laws.

Bressloff (2020, DOI:10.1088/1751-8121/ab97e0) solves a related switching-*diffusivity* search with a *constant* resetting hazard and randomized internal state at resets. Its renewal equations do not cover the present independently switching *reset hazard* whose phase survives resets. Pal, Kundu and Evans (2016, arXiv:1512.08211) consider a hazard depending on time since the last reset: that is an age-renewal protocol, not the externally persistent phase-modulated hazard here. General reset-clock stationary stochastic ordering does not imply the phase-conditioned first-passage formula or its opposite target-distance comparisons. These contrasts concern model assumptions and implications, not just differences in terminology.

## Limitations

The theorem establishes at least one crossing, not uniqueness or the exact crossing location. It concerns mean first-passage time for an absorbing point on the one-dimensional half-line, equal stationary probabilities of the two phase states, strictly positive switching and reset rates, and phase retention across particle resets. It does not assert a general ordering at intermediate target distances, an optimum over switching speeds, or robustness under phase-state resetting. The general matrix-renewal method is standard; the assessed scientific content is its explicit persistent-reset-clock solution and universal opposite-distance comparison. Publication priority is qualified by the possibility of an unindexed general Markov-modulated-resetting first-passage theorem that implies this special case. No independent review has been performed.
