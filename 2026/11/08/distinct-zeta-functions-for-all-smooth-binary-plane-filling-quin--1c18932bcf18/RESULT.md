# Distinct zeta functions for all smooth binary plane-filling quintics

## Finding

For every geometrically smooth plane quintic over \(\mathbf F_2\) containing all seven rational points of \(\mathbf P^2\), its genus-six Hasse–Weil zeta numerator belongs to one of ten explicitly listed polynomials. These ten numerators are pairwise distinct, with precisely one polynomial for each projective-equivalence class under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\). Consequently, the Jacobians of representatives of different classes are pairwise nonisogenous over \(\mathbf F_2\). Exactly 1,512 defining equations belong to the ten classes.

## Assumptions and scope

Order the 21 degree-five monomials as \(X^aY^bZ^{5-a-b}\) with \(a=0,\ldots,5\) and, for each \(a\), \(b=0,\ldots,5-a\). A nonnegative integer \(v\) represents the form comprising exactly those monomials whose zero-indexed bit in \(v\) equals one. The representatives below have coefficients in \(\mathbf F_2\). Projective equivalence means change of homogeneous coordinates by \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\), not arbitrary abstract isomorphism.

The statement concerns the ten projective classes of geometrically smooth plane-filling degree-five curves in fixed projective coordinates over the ground field \(\mathbf F_2\). It concerns neither arbitrary genus-six curves nor singular plane quintics.

## Proof

The space of binary degree-five homogeneous forms has dimension 21. Evaluation on the seven rational points of the Fano plane gives seven independent binary linear conditions; its 14-dimensional kernel contains exactly \(2^{14}-1=16,383\) nonzero plane-filling equations. The verifier constructs this kernel by row-reduction, enumerates every vector and all 168 invertible binary \(3\)-by-\(3\) coordinate matrices, and partitions the 16,383 equations into exactly 139 projective orbits. (Over \(\mathbf F_2\) there is no nontrivial scalar identification.)

Geometric smoothness is decided independently by two complete affine-chart tests. In each of the three standard projective charts, the ideal generated by the dehomogenized equation and its two partial derivatives is computed over \(\mathbf F_2\). The curve has no geometric singularities precisely when this ideal is the unit ideal in every chart, because the ideal's being proper is equivalent to having a common zero over an algebraic closure (weak Nullstellensatz). A second check uses Euler's identity in odd degree in characteristic two and computes the homogeneous-gradient ideal in each chart. The tests agree on all 139 representatives, retaining exactly ten orbits, whose sizes add to \(1,512\).

For \(n=1,\ldots,6\), the verifier represents \(\mathbf F_{2^n}\) using the irreducible binary polynomials \(t+1\), \(t^2+t+1\), \(t^3+t+1\), \(t^4+t+1\), \(t^5+t^2+1\), and \(t^6+t+1\), respectively. It verifies their irreducibility. Every rational projective point is uniquely represented by one of \([a:b:1]\), \([a:1:0]\), \([1:0:0]\). Exact field evaluation determines the numbers \(N_n=\#C(\mathbf F_{2^n})\). An independently structured projective-point enumeration reproduces these counts for \(n=1,2,3,6\). The displayed table is the complete result.

A smooth plane quintic has genus \(g=(5-1)(5-2)/2=6\). Write its zeta function as \(Z_C(T)=P_C(T)/((1-T)(1-2T))\), where \(P_C(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{12}c_kT^k\). The formal identity \(\log P_C(T)=\sum_{n\geq1}(N_n-2^n-1)T^n/n\) determines \(c_0=1\) and recursively \(k c_k=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{k-j}(N_j-2^j-1)\) for \(1\leq k\leq6\). The curve's Weil functional equation supplies \(c_{12-k}=2^{6-k}c_k\) for \(0\leq k\leq5\). Thus the six counts determine the entire numerator, and the ten listed initial coefficient sequences give ten distinct full polynomials. Distinct Weil polynomials imply that the corresponding abelian varieties are not isogenous over the ground field (Tate's isogeny theorem).

## Relationship to prior work

The plane-filling property and the degree-five \(q+3\) setting come from Asgarli and Ghioca. The prior complete finite classification of Jacobian \(2\)-ranks and \(a\)-numbers of these ten classes does not determine their entire Frobenius Weil polynomials or imply pairwise nonisogeny. Lomont earlier enumerated rational point counts of binary plane curves of degree at most five, including counts over extensions, to find extremal examples; this result differs in selecting the natural ten smooth plane-filling classes and giving their complete zeta functions and the isogeny separation. The older enumeration leaves a residual database-priority risk.

## Limitations

This is an exhaustive finite-field classification for exactly \(\mathbf F_2\) and degree five. The conclusion does not say the Jacobians are geometrically nonisogenous or that every possible genus-six Weil polynomial occurs. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed. A previously archived complete output of Lomont's computations, if available, could imply these numerical data without explicitly presenting this classification.
