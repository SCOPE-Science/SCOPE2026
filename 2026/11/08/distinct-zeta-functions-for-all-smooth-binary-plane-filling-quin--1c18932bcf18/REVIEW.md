# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. Complete orbit enumeration of the 14-dimensional space of binary plane-filling quintics, geometric smoothness by unit Groebner ideals on three charts using two derivative formulations, exact extension-field point counts, and the genus-six Weil functional equation. The attached executable verifier also checks irreducibility of field moduli and cross-checks extension counts with an alternate projective enumeration. The finite exhaustive proof is valid for precisely the stated field, degree and all ten classes; it is not a probabilistic sample.

## Originality

PASS subject to residual database and unindexed literature risk. Asgarli–Ghioca's 2023 paper establishes the degree-\(q+3\) plane-filling framework, not these zeta functions. Lomont (2002) performed broader binary-curve point counts to seek record curves, but its inspected published tables list extremal examples rather than the complete ten smooth plane-filling-class Weil polynomials and pairwise nonisogeny conclusion. An earlier finite classification of \(2\)-ranks and \(a\)-numbers is weaker: these invariants do not determine the ten distinct Weil polynomials. An unpublished or archived numerical database could imply the tables; this limitation should be checked independently before any priority claim.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The degree-\(q+3\) plane-filling locus over the smallest field is a natural finite arithmetic moduli slice. Its ten geometrically smooth projective classes were previously distinguished using local tangent and Newton data; the present complete zeta calculation distinguishes all ten by Frobenius itself, proving their Jacobians pairwise nonisogenous over the base field. It is an exact arithmetic classification with an explicit independent invariant, not a routine renaming or a random small example.

## Limitations

The result is specific to binary plane quintics containing all seven rational points; it asserts neither geometric nonisogeny nor any broad-field analogue. Earlier computational datasets remain a priority risk. Verification is same-model only.
