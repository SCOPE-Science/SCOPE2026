# Mathematical review of the singular low-rate crossover

**Correctness.** The killed-interval boundary-value solution, restart renewal identity, and explicit expansion with \(\kappa a\to0\) and \(\kappa m\to u>0\) prove the tail asymptotic and the conditional Bernoulli limit. The upper bound by the unconditional probability of two or more resets gives tightness on \(\{0,1\}\); the fixed-rate comparison follows because the no-reset exceedance is exponentially smaller than the full exceedance. The proof does not infer an infinite-parameter result from finite simulations.

**Originality, best of knowledge.** The marginal stopped maximum, marginal geometric reset count, renewal \(p_mq_m^n\) law, fixed-count Gumbel limit, and high-maximum size bias are prior mathematics or specializations of broader published renewal analysis (2026-10-25). The surviving claim is solely the explicit singular low-Poisson-rate Bernoulli boundary. No inspected earlier theorem covers the coupled limit \(r\downarrow0,\ \sqrt{r/D}\,m\to u\in(0,\infty)\); a missed general renewal scaling theorem is possible.

**Mathematical value.** Reset-count burden and spatial-excursion risk are natural observables in search and restarting diffusions. The positive no-reset mass under rare-maximum conditioning exposes a precise noncommuting order of limits hidden by the usual fixed-rate high-excursion selection law. The parameter \(u\) measures the barrier in diffusion lengths, and the explicit Bernoulli law is a boundary classification, not merely a new name for a standard distribution.

**Scope.** Driftless one-dimensional Brownian motion, independent exponential clocks, fixed positive target distance and diffusion, and finite nonzero \(u\). The original broader conditional extreme claims are credited to earlier sources; no claim of general non-Poisson universality or full priority is made.
