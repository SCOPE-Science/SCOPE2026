# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The stopped process renews on every Poisson reset. One-cycle target absorption, upper-barrier crossing, and reset probabilities solve the same killed Brownian boundary-value equation and form a genuine probability partition. Its geometric iteration proves the finite-threshold joint distribution exactly. The fixed-rate asymptotics retain the correct Brownian normalization \(\kappa=\sqrt{r/D}\); the conditional Gumbel center uses the exact factor \(1+e^{\kappa a}\). High-threshold count conditioning produces a normalized size-biased geometric law, and a separate low-rate asymptotic yields a Bernoulli crossover. The package's deterministic checker confirms representative identities and finite-parameter convergence; finite tests do not prove the limit theorems.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** Guo–Yan–Chen's full first-passage extremal analysis treats the maximum and its occurrence time, not the reset count jointly. Sunil–Blythe–Evans–Majumdar's first-passage cost paper supplies the geometric reset count and joint count/time/cost transforms, not the stopped maximum. Singh–Pal's different joint extremal/count formula concerns a fixed observation horizon, and Hartmann–Majumdar–Schehr consider large-time or independent-ensemble maxima. The two known marginal distributions do not determine their joint coupling. The exact joint law, its sharp conditional count center and high-maximum count selection are not established by the inspected results; an unindexed renewal-excursion theorem or fuller treatment of a related source could still imply them.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Reset count is a natural cost/budget observable in a target-search process, while the maximum measures exposure to dangerous excursions. The theorem gives their exact dependence, detects strict stochastic inflation of maximum risk at higher reset counts, and quantifies two dissimilar conditioning experiments. The low-rate Bernoulli edge resolves the otherwise discontinuous inference that extreme excursions must involve at least one reset. This boundary layer is a precise transport-risk statement, not merely a reparametrization of the published marginal maximum formula.

**Scope and limits.** The result is for driftless Brownian search, a fixed positive target distance and independent instantaneous Poisson resets. It does not address other reset laws, higher dimensions, unknown parameters, or finite-sample inference. Gumbel and geometric size bias are classical mechanisms; novelty is claimed only for their exact first-passage coupling, constants and nonuniform low-rate transition. Literature coverage is bounded by the sources actually inspected.
