# Review of exact dyadic cubic-curve counting law

## Correctness — PASS

The complete statement follows from the identity \(x^3+(s-x)^3=s^3-3sx(s-x)\), two-branch lifting for odd \(s\), and the square-root fiber classification when \(s\) is even. The exact square-fiber second-moment recurrence \(B_m=2^{m+1}+4B_{m-2}=m2^m\) gives the additive energy; the square-residue recurrence \(Q_m=2^{m-3}+Q_{m-2}\) gives the support cardinality. Orthogonality gives the Fourier moment with the stated normalization. A standalone exhaustive checker passes through exponent ten; it is corroboration, not proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison

The nearby papers of Wooley (2014), Hughes–Wooley (2019), and Hickman–Wright (2018) concern cubic Weyl moments, discrete integer-interval restriction, and general ring restriction, respectively. Their inspected theorems do not establish these two exact aggregate dyadic formulas. Dehnavi–Mirzaee Shamsabad–Mahmoodi Rishakani (2019) solve general quadratic congruences modulo powers of two and therefore cover pointwise solution counts; the reported novelty is restricted to the *aggregated* cubic-curve additive energy and sumset laws. Published-result searches likewise found no dominating aggregate formula. Historical-priority risk persists, especially from local-density literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The cubic curve is the first odd-degree nonquadratic graph underlying a prominent Fourier restriction problem. At powers of two, ramification creates unusual concentration: the complete fourth moment grows by a \(k^2/4\) factor relative to the square of support size, while the sumset density converges exactly to \(5/18\). The theorem counts every two-sum fiber and derives two natural exact global invariants for an infinite family, rather than merely an arbitrary finite slice. It does not claim unrestricted weighted extension estimates.
