# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)

The branch formulas for \(H=T^2\) are obtained by direct composition and all have absolute derivative \(4\). The indexed dyadic boundary set is forward-invariant; its non-hole intervals give the explicitly enumerated transition graph. Primitive connectivity through the full-image interval gives a unique Perron root. The stated row eigenvector equations yield the parity polynomials, and graph-directed contraction gives the dimension. Each n-step survivor cylinder has length \(4^{-(n-1)}\) times the corresponding terminal interval length, proving the exact tail formula. Strict Perron deficit from \(4\) implies almost-sure first capture. Direct iteration proves parity-specific post-capture orbits. The second-order expansion follows by solving \(\delta(1-\delta)^k=p\) with \(kp\to0\). The finite checks of 22 parameter values and 24 independent cylinder lengths test, but do not replace, these all-parameter arguments.

## Originality (PASS within inspected evidence)

The 2023 Silva paper supplies the family and broad local/Milnor attractor analysis, so existence of such attractors is not presented as new. The explicit dyadic family of complete survival matrices, their root polynomials, exact initial-condition first-hit distribution, dimension, and second-order escape law were not found in accessible source material. General Markov-hole literature supplies methods and related formulae for different holes, not an explicit coverage of this fixed alternating flat-top family. The closest published-record comparison concerning optimally alternated endpoint holes of an expanding integer map uses fundamentally different time-dependent holes and does not imply this fixed central-hole spectrum. A later closely related 2025 paper had an accessible abstract and first page but not complete text; that and unindexed exact calculations are acknowledged priority risks.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

The dyadic cutoffs are critical-preperiodic parameters generated by the natural finite orbit of the limiter boundary. The classification gives for an infinite family exact escape rates, finite-time capture distributions, Hausdorff dimensions, and a subtle parity transition between superstable trapping and finite capture by a locally repelling periodic state. The second-order law quantifies memory of the critical preperiod rather than merely restating a general Markov-matrix prescription. These are natural quantitative invariants of a published controlled expanding system, not arbitrary finite tables.
