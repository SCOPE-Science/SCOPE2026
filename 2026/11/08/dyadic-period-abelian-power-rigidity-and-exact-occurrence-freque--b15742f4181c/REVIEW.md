# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof derives the one-coordinate difference between neighboring blocks at each dyadic scale using the exact \(2\)-adic valuation formula; this makes Parikh equality equivalent to literal equality. It then classifies all possible constant runs of exceptional bits, proves the repeated root primitive, and computes occurrence frequencies by residue-class densities. `check_factors.py` and `check_arithmetic.py` independently confirmed all of their explicitly bounded tests. The all-length claims rest on the proof, not on finite extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with historical risk

A complete public-text comparison was made with the 2010 general bounded-Abelian-complexity theorem, 2014 cyclic-complexity theorem (Proposition 10 for period doubling), and 2014 work on 2-abelian complexity. The general Abelian-power existence result does not fix dyadic periods. A conjugacy-class count and an equivalence-class count for single factors do not yield the precise dyadic Abelian-power factor sets, equality-to-ordinary powers, or occurrence frequencies. No directly dominating statement was identified in these sources; older unindexed repetition studies may require further historical review.

## Value — PASS

For all dyadic block lengths simultaneously, the result classifies all Abelian square and cube factors, excludes higher powers, and gives exact individual-factor frequencies. The contrasting non-dyadic Abelian fourth power shows this is a substantive arithmetic boundary for a classical automatic word rather than a generic assertion. It offers a complete invariant at an infinite family of structurally distinguished lengths.
