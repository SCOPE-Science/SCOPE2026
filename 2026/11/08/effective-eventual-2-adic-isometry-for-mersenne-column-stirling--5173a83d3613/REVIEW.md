# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The exact inclusion-exclusion proof yields a normalized congruence modulo \(2^{n+2}\) for every permitted \(m,n,a,b\), including arbitrarily large \(\nu_2(a-b)\). The conditions bound odd-base binomial remainders and even-base terms separately. The only external theorem used is the exact \(n+1\) doubling valuation in Zhao--Zhao--Hong (2014); its exceptional \(m=n=2\) case is outside the theorem. The verifier independently checks 25 finite recurrence cases and 48 high-exponent modular differences. Finite checks are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality
PASS, with historical-priority qualification. The 2014 paper explicitly identifies the all-pair statement as still open for \(k=2^m-1\ge15\). Its own Mersenne theorem handles doubling pairs, while the 2012 all-pair theorem excludes Mersenne columns. The new uniform divided-difference congruence supplies the missing implication. A 2019 broad Stirling-valuation paper has been compared by subject and statements and does not state the exact difference theorem. Unlocated equivalent arguments remain possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. An explicitly recorded conjectural gap is resolved in its eventual form for an infinite, natural family of Stirling columns, with a uniform quantitative threshold depending only on the multiplier-difference valuation. The theorem does not attempt to classify all low exponents or claim an optimal cutoff.
