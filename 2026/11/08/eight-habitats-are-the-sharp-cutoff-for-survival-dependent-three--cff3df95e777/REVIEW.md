# Research review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact event-to-component criterion and Bernstein expansion prove that enumerating integer gain vectors suffices; the standard graph atlas covers all nonisomorphic graphs through seven sites. The bundled checker replayed all 1,253 atlas entries, 32,191 nontrivial terminal choices, and checked all 19 absent links of the eight-site witness. Direct BFS independently checked both winning gains on all 32 witness survivor subsets. The finite atlas's *completeness* is a cited mathematical input, not proven by the checker. The positive-parameter root is exact; no numerical fitting.

**Originality — PASS subject to documented priority limits.** Comparison with Hock–Mumby [1], Xie et al. [2,3], Romero's survey [4], the graph atlas [5] and available metadata for Romero [6] shows distinct failure structure and intervention objective. Published database records matching generic terminal reliability do not by themselves cover the exact three-terminal site-percolation augmentation claim, coefficientwise cutoff or witness. A specialized closed-form/stronger result may still exist, especially [6], which was not fully readable; no independent historical-priority certification is claimed.

**Value — PASS.** Three protected core habitats, ephemeral stepping-stones and a single added corridor give a natural site reliability objective. A sharp smallest graph where unknown habitat-survival probability changes the optimal intervention, with a nontrivial exact algebraic switch and exhaustive lower bound, is useful as a small-network benchmark for robust connectivity design. The claim has no empirical calibration and does not imply geographic corridor recommendations.

**Closest work and limitations.** [1] individual dispersal-route reliabilities, [2,3] edge-failure global three-terminal graph design, [4] reliability taxonomy, [6] incompletely accessible augmentation. The exact census assumes [5] supplies every unlabeled graph through seven vertices. Results exclude heterogeneous/correlated site losses, edge failures, nonprotected terminals, costs and multiple added corridors.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
