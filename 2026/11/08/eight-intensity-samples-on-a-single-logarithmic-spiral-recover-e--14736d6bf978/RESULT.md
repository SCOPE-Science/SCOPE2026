# Eight intensity samples on a single logarithmic spiral recover every complex quadratic polynomial

## Finding
Fix \(z(t)=\exp[t(\tfrac12\log 2+i\pi/4)]\) and set \(z_j=z(j)=(1+i)^j\), for \(j=0,\ldots,7\). For any two complex polynomials \(p,q\) of degree at most two, the eight identities \(|p(z_j)|^2=|q(z_j)|^2\) imply \(q=\eta p\) for some \(|\eta|=1\). Seven or fewer complex point evaluations cannot provide this universal phase-retrieval property. Every nonempty open subarc of this spiral also contains eight points with the property; indeed the good ordered eight-tuples are open and dense among tuples of distinct parameter values on that subarc.

## Assumptions and scope
All coefficients are unrestricted complex numbers, including polynomials of lower degree and the zero polynomial. The samples are ordinary pointwise squared moduli, not derivatives, Fourier transforms, or moments. Global phase is the only allowed equivalence. The explicit eight nodes include \(z_0=1\) and \(z_7=(1+i)^7\); no circular or line-based supplementary samples are needed. The generic statement concerns the same fixed analytic spiral, with its real parameter varying inside an arbitrary nonempty open interval.

## Proof
Let \(v(z)=(1,z,z^2)^T\), and let \(\mathcal H_3\) denote the real nine-dimensional space of complex Hermitian \(3\times3\) matrices. The real-linear intensity lift is \(\mathcal L(H)=(v(z_j)^*Hv(z_j))_{j=0}^7\). A convenient real coordinate order for \(H\) is \((h_{00},h_{11},h_{22},\operatorname{Re}h_{01},\operatorname{Im}h_{01},\operatorname{Re}h_{02},\operatorname{Im}h_{02},\operatorname{Re}h_{12},\operatorname{Im}h_{12})\). The corresponding \(8\times9\) real matrix has rows
\[
M(z)=\bigl(1,|z|^2,|z|^4,2\operatorname{Re}z,-2\operatorname{Im}z,2\operatorname{Re}z^2,-2\operatorname{Im}z^2,2|z|^2\operatorname{Re}z,-2|z|^2\operatorname{Im}z\bigr).
\]
Direct arithmetic in Gaussian integers at the eight specified nodes gives the exact nonzero minor obtained by deleting the last column,
\[\det M_{\widehat9}=-19660800000\ne0,\]
and the exact kernel coordinate vector
\[u=(256,-150,2,-48,-96,3,3,-9,3)^T.\]
The assertions \(Mu=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}M=8\) follow by multiplying the displayed rows and computing that minor (see `verify_spiral.py`). Thus \(\ker\mathcal L\) is precisely the real line generated by
\[
H_0=\begin{pmatrix}256&-48-96i&3+3i\\-48+96i&-150&-9+3i\\3-3i&-9-3i&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det H_0=-111540\ne0.
\]
For \(p\) write \(p(z)=a^Tv(z)\), and set \(u_p=\overline a\), so \(|p(z)|^2=v(z)^*u_pu_p^*v(z)\). If two polynomials have the same eight intensities, their Hermitian rank-at-most-two difference \(u_pu_p^*-u_qu_q^*\) belongs to \(\ker\mathcal L\). Because every nonzero element of the latter is an invertible rank-three matrix, the difference is zero. Equality \(u_pu_p^*=u_qu_q^*\) implies \(q=\eta p\), \(|\eta|=1\), also when one polynomial is zero.

For sharp minimality, consider any \(m\le7\) point evaluations. If their evaluation vectors fail to span \(\mathbb C^3\), they cannot retrieve phase (a nonzero vector in the common nullspace has all intensities zero). Otherwise the real measurement kernel in \(\mathcal H_3\) has dimension at least \(9-m\ge2\). Choose independent Hermitian matrices \(A,B\) in it. The continuous function \(\det(A\cos t+B\sin t)\) has opposite values at \(t\) and \(t+\pi\), and therefore vanishes somewhere. The corresponding Hermitian matrix \(S\ne0\) (independence rules out cancellation) is singular. It cannot be positive or negative semidefinite: with spanning evaluation vectors, a nonzero semidefinite Hermitian matrix gives a nonzero measurement at some point. Consequently \(S\) has precisely one positive and one negative eigenvalue, and one zero eigenvalue. The spectral theorem expresses \(S=xx^*-yy^*\) for two non-collinear vectors \(x,y\), producing distinct polynomials with the same \(m\) intensities. Hence no \(m\le7\) design is phase retrieving.

Finally fix the spiral \(z(t)\). For each ordered real eight-tuple \(\mathbf t\), all entries of the lifting matrix \(M(\mathbf t)\) are real analytic in \(\mathbf t\). Let \(D(\mathbf t)\) be the determinant of the minor obtained by deleting its last column, and let \(c(\mathbf t)\) be the vector of signed maximal minors of the full \(8\times9\) matrix. Then \(M(\mathbf t)c(\mathbf t)=0\). Form its associated Hermitian matrix \(H(c(\mathbf t))\). The real analytic scalar \(F(\mathbf t)=D(\mathbf t)\det H(c(\mathbf t))\) does not vanish at \((0,1,\ldots,7)\), by the exact calculations above. A nontrivial real analytic function on connected \(\mathbb R^8\) has no open set of zeros. Thus \(F\ne0\) on an open dense set of eight-tuples in any nonempty parameter cube, and restricting further to distinct entries leaves an open dense set. Wherever \(F\ne0\), the same rank-three nullspace argument gives universal phase retrieval.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify_spiral.py` uses exact symbolic Gaussian-integer arithmetic to construct the eight rows; it tests \(\operatorname{rank}M=8\), \(Mu=0\), the displayed \(8\times8\) minor, \(\det H_0=-111540\), and \(v(z_j)^*H_0v(z_j)=0\) at all eight nodes. No floating-point threshold is used. The universal minimality and arbitrary-subarc results are proved by the real-Hermitian argument and analytic continuation above rather than by finite experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
General finite-dimensional phase-retrieval theory establishes the sharp eight-measurement threshold for arbitrary complex three-dimensional frames and the Hermitian low-rank-kernel criterion. Bodmann--Hammen give explicit \(4d-4\) constructions using two circles; they do not identify a single logarithmic spiral carrying eight evaluations. Jaming--Rathmair study finite-dimensional undersampling and analytic trigonometric-polynomial ambiguities in a distinct measurement model. The new specific assertion is the exact Gaussian-integer spiral certificate, together with its full-rank Hermitian obstruction and the consequent existence of minimal designs on every arbitrarily short open subarc. The familiar nine-point full-linear-lifting criterion along a nonaliasing geometric progression does not imply eight-point nonlinear phase retrieval: the present nullspace is nontrivial but contains no rank-two Hermitian difference.

## Limitations
The result is for polynomial degree at most two, and for the indicated fixed spiral. It does not classify all possible geometric progressions, provide a universal eight-point design for higher-degree polynomials, establish linear recovery of unrestricted Hermitian coefficient matrices from eight samples, or assert any uniform noise conditioning on short subarcs. Earlier general eight-vector phase-retrieval theory is acknowledged; the proposed distinct feature is the explicit one-spiral point-evaluation geometry and its local genericity.
