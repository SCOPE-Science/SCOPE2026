# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is exactly eight rank-three equivalence classes of Kneser graphs and the sharp full two-point extension threshold. A three-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé lemma reduces rank-three theory of vertex-transitive graphs to the collection of actual third-vertex adjacency masks of each ordered-pair orbit. The set-theoretic calculation shows that three masks always exist for \(k\ge2,n\ge2k+1\), and the fourth exists for intersection \(t\) exactly when \(n\ge3k-t\). This gives the complete table including singleton, clique, edgeless, matching, mixed, triangle-free and full-extension boundary cases. The Rado comparison follows from its standard finite-extension property. An independent script confirms 138 bounded instances and the representative signatures, without being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS subject to historical-priority risk
The October 2026 Johnson-graph limit-theory paper does not treat Kneser disjointness. Voronov–Dergachev–Zhukovskii–Neopryatnaya (2021) uses Kneser graphs in three-pebble subgraph-containment lower bounds; some particular indistinguishability comparisons are therefore prior results, not new contributions here. The 2025 existentially closed critical Cayley-graph paper addresses generic 2-e.c. constructions and order thresholds, not all rank-three Kneser parameter classes. Searches over equivalent rank-three, two-extension, pair-common-neighbor and Kneser aliases did not identify a statement implying the full eight-case classification. A general unindexed or inaccessible model-theory result could still cover it; absence from search is not novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete natural classification of a canonical finite graph family under one fixed logical resource, with exact sharp boundaries at the disjointness, nonedge-extension and triangle thresholds. It determines the first finite Kneser model satisfying the Rado graph's full two-parameter extension axioms and a separate first occurrence of genuinely mixed pair profiles. Both invariants are motivated independently by bounded-depth logical indistinguishability and existential closure, rather than chosen arbitrary subfamilies. The result does not claim a new generic graph extension axiom, or the absolute smallest 2-e.c. graph.
