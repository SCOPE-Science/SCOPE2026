# Scientific review: coextinction moment-identifiability threshold

## Correctness — PASS

The service indicators have failure probabilities \(q^{d_i}\), with pairwise joint failure probability \(q^{u_{ij}}\). These supply the exact first two factorial moments, and the polynomial coefficients identify the plant-degree and pair-union multisets. The lower bound is exhaustive: for at most three plants, the zero-service probability and first two moments determine the whole law; for at most two pollinators, only the two single-survivor degrees remain, determined by their first two power sums. The sole remaining smaller case, four plants and three pollinators, has 2,161 labelled valid matrices and no law-separated moment twins. The 4-by-4 witness is connected, has identical moment signatures, and yields the exact all-served gap \(q^3(1-q)\). Two independent code paths reproduce the entire five-pair connected eight-species census.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence, provisional

The standard last-partner-loss rule and community-robustness dependence on topology are established prior context, not claimed as new. The inspected full text of Vieira et al. (2013) addresses simulated trait and phylogenetic-diversity loss, not identifiability of the full law from mean and variance. The accessible summaries of Memmott et al. (2004), Campbell et al. (2012) and Vieira and Almeida-Neto (2014) address extinction tolerance, topology and stochastic cascades rather than the exact eight-species cutoff or complete connected first-exception layer. Search of related mathematical formulations, catalogues and direct aliases revealed no covering result; historical priority remains provisional because not every plausible source was accessible in full.

## Value — PASS

Expected richness and its variance are standard summaries of resilience under pollinator loss, but these two complete response curves fail to control the high-reliability event that all plant species retain service. The first possible failure is located at a sharp, provably smallest eight-species boundary, its complete connected exceptional layer is enumerated, and an explicit connected witness has a maximum missed full-service probability of \(27/256\), over ten percentage points. The result distinguishes moment-based evaluation from conservation-relevant tail risk without requiring an empirical extrapolation.

## Closest literature and limitations

Memmott, Waser and Price (2004), doi:10.1098/rspb.2004.2909, motivates the topological last-partner model. Campbell et al. (2012), doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.86.021924, examines vulnerable topologies under a different dynamic model. Vieira, Cianciaruso and Almeida-Neto (2013), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0081242, was inspected in full HTML including its model and results. Vieira and Almeida-Neto (2014), doi:10.1111/ele.12394, examines more complex stochastic coextinctions, distinct from the fixed Boolean service rule. Results apply to independently extinct pollinators in a fixed bipartite network; no broader ecological model or independent external audit is claimed.
