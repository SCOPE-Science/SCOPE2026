# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The branch-conservation probability identity yields the exact single-taxon gain \(G_x(q)=\sum_{e\in P_T(x)}q^{|C_e|}\) with no neglected factor. Componentwise clade-size profile dominance proves all-risk optimality on the continuum \(0<q<1\). Exhaustive unordered-root-split recursion lists exactly the 24 anonymous binary shapes for 2–7 leaves, and the displayed certificate lists an explicit dominant profile for every shape. A structurally independent labeled enumeration verifies 11,465 rooted labeled trees (counting all 1–7 leaf cases); the 8-leaf gain difference factors as \(q^3(q^2+q-1)\), with a unique root in the unit interval. Independently enumerating all 128 survivor outcomes after protecting each of 8 taxa confirms the rational expected-PD gain formulas. Remaining mathematical limits: no theorem for nonunit edges or multiple protections; no extrapolation beyond this sharp existence bound.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk
Hartmann–Steel (2006) Eq. (1) and Steel–Mimoto–Mooers (2007) Theorem 3.1 already provide the general expected-PD and marginal HED scores. Faller–Pardi–Steel (2008) treat distributions of future PD, and Billionnet (2022) compares conservation choices under different probability assignments, so sensitivity of rankings in general is prior art. The new finite-topology cutoff does not follow as a stated or covering theorem from the inspected texts: none establishes that *all* binary unit-edge shapes through seven tips have a universally optimal taxon, nor an eight-tip minimal counterexample with its exact probability crossover. Searching published research findings for the cutoff, aliases, componentwise clade-size profiles, and risk-dependent taxon switching yielded no direct implication. The 2007 Pardi–Goldman optimization work was not available in full text and remains a documented risk; as always, unnoticed equivalent or broader shape-classification work may exist.

## Value — PASS
A one-taxon equal-cost rescue decision is a canonical minimal Noah's Ark allocation. Unknown common extinction probability is a natural parameter of the independent field-of-bullets model. The sharp order-seven guarantee and eight-tip obstruction identify precisely the smallest evolutionary tree where a single robust conservation priority is impossible. This is a meaningful complete small-tree boundary with exact symbolic threshold, not a numerical improvement or a generic parameter substitution. The unit-edge assumption is an explicit theoretical baseline; real conservation priorities need actual branch lengths, heterogeneity, economic costs, and validation.
