# Eight vertices are sharp for adjacent Katz-centrality reversals on trees

## Finding
For a finite, simple, undirected tree \(T\), define its Katz score at vertex \(v\) by
\[
K_v(t)=\bigl[(I-tA_T)^{-1}\mathbf 1\bigr]_v,\qquad 0<t<\rho(A_T)^{-1}.
\]
Call an edge \(\{u,v\}\) *reversing* if the sign of \(K_u(t)-K_v(t)\) differs at two admissible positive parameters. Then no tree on at most seven vertices has a reversing edge. Among the 23 unlabelled trees of order eight, exactly two tree-and-edge isomorphism classes have reversing edges. The two representatives are:

1. \(E_1=\{01,05,12,14,23,56,57\}\), with reversing edge \(\{0,5\}\);
2. \(E_2=\{01,05,12,13,14,56,57\}\), with reversing edge \(\{0,5\}\).

Here every two-digit word denotes an unordered edge between the indicated integer labels from \(0\) to \(7\). The representatives have different degree multisets. All vertex pairs tested in the cutoff claim are **adjacent**, and the claim concerns changes in the attenuation parameter on a *fixed* tree, not perturbations of the network.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are unweighted finite simple trees; centrality uses uniform additive baseline \(\mathbf1\), not a node-dependent baseline or row-normalized adjacency. Normalization by a common positive scalar and subtracting the constant baseline do not affect the ranking. The open admissible interval \((0,\rho(A_T)^{-1})\) excludes its endpoints. Equality at isolated parameters is not counted as a strict reversal. For trees with one vertex there are no edges and the assertion is vacuous.

## Proof
Let \(n=|V(T)|\) and \(D(t)=\det(I-tA_T)\). The characteristic matching-polynomial identity for a forest follows immediately by expanding the determinant: no permutation cycle of length three or more can contribute, so only disjoint transpositions along edges occur. Thus
\[
D(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(-1)^j m_j(T)t^{2j},
\]
where \(m_j(T)\) counts the \(j\)-edge matchings. Let \(w_{k,v}=\bigl(A_T^k\mathbf1\bigr)_v\), with \(w_{0,v}=1\). These integers are generated by \(w_{k+1,v}=\sum_{z\sim v}w_{k,z}\). Write \(D(t)=\sum_{j=0}^n d_jt^j\). Since
\[
D(t)(I-tA_T)^{-1}\mathbf1=\operatorname{adj}(I-tA_T)\mathbf1
\]
is a polynomial vector of degree at most \(n-1\), its coefficients are **exactly**
\[
N_v(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^k d_jw_{k-j,v}\right)t^k.
\]
Consequently, \(K_u(t)-K_v(t)=[N_u(t)-N_v(t)]/D(t)\). The denominator is strictly positive throughout the admissible interval, since \(I-tA_T\) is positive definite there.

Here is a finite, integer-arithmetic non-reversal certificate for any particular edge. If \(H(t)=N_u(t)-N_v(t)\) vanishes identically, the endpoints tie at every attenuation. Otherwise remove powers of \(t\) and trailing zero coefficients, obtaining \(P(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_kt^k\), where \(c_0\ne0\). Find a positive rational \(R=a/b\) satisfying \(D(R)\le0\). Positivity of the determinant in the valid interval implies \(\rho(A_T)^{-1}\le R\). Substitute \(t=a x/[b(1+x)]\) for \(x>0\) and form the integer polynomial
\[
Q(x)=b^d(1+x)^d P\left(\frac{ax}{b(1+x)}\right)
=\sum_{j=0}^d q_jx^j,
\quad q_j=\sum_{k=0}^j c_k a^k b^{d-k}\binom{d-k}{j-k}.
\]
If all \(q_j\) are nonnegative, or all are nonpositive, then \(Q(x)\) has a fixed strict sign for \(x>0\). Thus \(P\), \(H\), and the Katz score difference are nonzero and fixed-sign on the entire admissible interval. Every comparison in the non-reversal census is certified by this simple coefficient test.

The supplied `verify_katz.py` enumerates one representative of each unlabelled tree on two through eight vertices and constructs \(D,N_v\) via the exact formulas above. The rational bound \(R\) is obtained by a finite grid search in exact rational arithmetic followed, where necessary, by 32 exact rational bisections, and the code explicitly verifies \(D(R)\le0\). The two uncertified edges at order eight are exactly the two representatives above, and both actually reverse. For \(E_1\), direct adjugate evaluation gives
\[
K_0(t)-K_5(t)=\frac{t(2t^5-3t^4-6t^3+4t^2+2t-1)}{1-7t^2+13t^4-5t^6},
\]
with values \(-17/137\) at \(t=1/3\) and \(1191/7169\) at \(t=3/7\). For \(E_2\),
\[
K_0(t)-K_5(t)=\frac{-t(6t^3-3t^2-3t+1)}{1-7t^2+11t^4},
\]
with values \(-2/31\) at \(t=1/4\) and \(3/29\) at \(t=1/3\). These parameter pairs are admissible without numerical spectral approximations: the maximum row sums of \(A_T^4\) are respectively \(25\) and \(32\). Therefore \(\rho(A_T)^4\le25\) or \(32\), and \(25(3/7)^4<1\) and \(32(1/3)^4<1\). Both strict sign changes are inside their admissible intervals.

For completeness of tree enumeration, the separate `verify_trees.py` constructs **every** Prüfer code of length \(n-2\) for every \(2\le n\le8\), giving \(n^{n-2}\) labelled trees, then reduces each to an unrooted canonical code by repeated leaf pruning to its center(s) and sorted rooted codes. It compares the resulting complete canonical-code set with the 1, 1, 2, 3, 6, 11, 23 representatives used by the centrality checker. Prüfer correspondence is bijective, and recursively sorted rooted codes characterize rooted-tree isomorphism, so this is a logically complete, independently constructed enumeration check, not a random sample.

## Verification
Install the declared Python dependency with `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt`, then run `python verify_katz.py` and `python verify_trees.py` from the extracted package directory. The first script performs exact matching, walk, determinant and transformed-coefficient arithmetic for every edge, checks the complete count table, verifies both rational witness sign changes and fourth-walk spectral bounds, and prints `EXACT_CERTIFICATE_OK`. The second independently checks the tree representatives by all \(262144\) Prüfer codes of order eight, and all smaller orders. Optional cross-checks by symbolic matrix inversion reproduce both displayed rational functions. Neither script uses floating-point decisions in its mathematical certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Katz (1953) introduced attenuated walk-based status. Benzi and Klymko's publicly available 2013 preprint established that parameter-dependent walk centralities interpolate between degree and principal-eigenvector regimes. Aprahamian, Higham and Higham studied parameter selection for centrality rankings, not a smallest-tree adjacent-vertex reversal classification. Their broad results motivate this question but do not imply the sharp order-eight cutoff or classify the two edge examples. The distinction matters: generic rank changes can involve nonadjacent vertices or general graphs, whereas this theorem concerns an edge inside one tree. The original definition and the broad parameter-limit phenomena are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The cutoff and classification are only for unweighted **trees** and **adjacent** vertices, with uniform baseline. The computer-assisted finite part explicitly enumerates all trees through eight vertices; it is not a theorem classifying larger trees, general connected graphs, directed networks or weighted graphs. Exact arithmetic and two independently constructed tree enumerations establish the finite scope, not uniqueness in the universe of all centrality formulations. Historical priority is provisional until broader external review; related graph-centrality literature may contain unindexed equivalent formulations. No independent audit has been performed.
