# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Katz rank difference equals an exact integer polynomial divided by the strictly positive determinant \(D(t)\) on the legal interval. The determinant is the matching polynomial of a tree, and numerator coefficients are reconstructed from exact walk counts. For every nonidentical adjacent pair in all trees of orders at most seven, a computed integer coefficient transformation is unisigned on a rational interval covering the entire legal attenuation interval. The separate Prüfer census proves that no tree is omitted. The two eight-vertex exceptions possess independently evaluated rational score differences of opposite signs at certified admissible attenuation parameters. Scope does not exceed the complete exact enumeration and analytic witness steps.

## Originality — PASS (within inspected evidence)
Katz's 1953 construction and Benzi–Klymko's preprint (arXiv:1312.6722, 2013-12-23) give the definition and degree/eigenvector parameter limits, but not the adjacent-edge tree minimum. Aprahamian–Higham–Higham (2015 preprint) focuses on selecting resolvent attenuation to match exponential rankings. Boldi–Luongo–Vigna (2017) studies order changes after adding an arc; van der Grinten et al. (2018) studies computing Katz rankings efficiently at given parameters. These do not imply the complete eight-vertex cutoff, as changes in graph topology, numerical ranking computation, or generic limiting comparisons do not supply the order-by-order exact tree classification. Targeted searches of indexed mathematical literature did not identify this exact result. This is a checked but non-exhaustive priority assessment, not a claim that every publication has been examined.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A fixed-edge relative influence can reverse even on an acyclic connected network, contrary to a naive expectation that the more locally connected endpoint stays ahead. The theorem isolates the **least** network complexity permitting the effect and classifies both minimal configurations. Exact finite certificates make the boundary reusable as a test case for tuning attenuated-walk centralities. It neither asserts a general structural theorem for all trees nor calls routine determinant calculations independently significant.

## Limitations and source risk
The complete enumeration uses exact code and a standard graph-enumeration library, with a separate Prüfer-code completeness cross-check. The finite result is exact for the stated class, but is not an all-tree asymptotic theorem. The inspected centrality papers do not exhaust the broader historical literature; unindexed centrality-order results could predate this observation. In particular, rank-perturbation and localization results with different parameters should not be conflated with this fixed-tree attenuation theorem.
