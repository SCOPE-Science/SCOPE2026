# Scientific review: genus-three cyclic-four elliptic/Prym strata

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The hyperelliptic discriminant condition is derived from the polynomial and its derivative. The cyclic-cover birational transformation, elliptic involution, canonical differential basis, and modified Cartier coefficient extraction are explicit. Both exact Cartier matrices and the factorizations of their determinants are reconstructed by a multinomial certificate and independently by polynomial multiplication in extension fields. Stable semilinear rank is proved using the invariant two-dimensional offdiagonal and one-dimensional middle blocks, rather than inferred from a single matrix rank. The elliptic/Prym invariants follow from a prime-to-characteristic isogeny and additivity of their \(p\)-divisible invariants. The open boundary is handled; no unexamined coefficients are needed.

**Originality (O): PASS, with qualification.** Cavalieri--Pries (arXiv:2308.14891) prove mass formulae for cyclic four-branch families, but their Section 7 explicitly imposes odd covering degree and their Section 6 does not state the characteristic-seven/eleven individual parameter conditions. Moriya--Kudo (arXiv:2209.02926) study general decomposed Richelot methods, rather than the present pair of complete tables. Ohashi et al. (arXiv:2401.10500, Theorem 4.3(3)) already know the superspeciality of \(y^2=x^8-1\) at primes \(p\equiv7\pmod8\), covering the single \(c=0\) special curve in characteristic seven; that special case is acknowledged, not presented as an independently new theorem. Frei (arXiv:1703.03516) supplies general large \(a\)-number constraints, not the present classification. A journal article on levels of polynomial pairs (Boix--Noordman--Top, 2020) discusses the special \(x^8-1\) curve but was accessible only as an abstract and extended excerpt. Searches for exact equations, inertial descriptions, quotient/Prym aliases, and implication-equivalent tables in the literature and mathematical records did not identify a stronger covering theorem. The remaining risk is an unindexed equivalent calculation in an alternate genus-three dihedral-family presentation; absence from a search index is not proof of priority.

**Mathematical value (V): PASS.** Four-branched cyclic covers are naturally moduli-dimensional-one objects whose nonordinary mass has been studied, yet a mass count does not determine which elliptic or Prym factor degenerates at each geometric parameter. This classification gives both loci and their interaction in the first two nontrivial primes \(p\equiv3\pmod4\): their coincidence and whole-Jacobian superspeciality at \(p=7\) contrasts with the strict separation and two superspecial-Prym/ordinary-elliptic points at \(p=11\). The multiplicity-two characteristic-eleven Hasse zeros reveal scheme thickness invisible from a set-theoretic point count. This is a complete motivated arithmetic classification on an explicit one-dimensional family, not an arbitrary isolated numeric parameter experiment. It does not determine full Newton polygons or geometric isomorphism classes.

**Residual scientific limitations.** An alternative parameterization could make some of these factorized expressions appear in older literature; independently verify historical priority. Checks over extension fields support, but do not replace, the characteristic-general polynomial proof at each listed prime. Mathematical independent audit not performed.
