# Mathematical review: elliptic traces for the sextic graph

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is exact for every finite field of characteristic greater than five, including characteristic eleven. Fixing the first-coordinate sum separates the zero fiber from equivalent nonzero fibers; an elementary factorization reduces each nonzero fiber to the union of a diagonal-square locus and an auxiliary quartic curve. The quartic count follows by quotienting under coordinate sign changes, lifting the conic with quadratic characters, and evaluating the remaining two cubic character sums. The double-counted intersection has the explicit quartic-root correction. All factor coefficients and exceptional prime restrictions are stated and checked. In characteristics other than eleven, the cubics are squarefree, so the elliptic Hasse bound applies; characteristic eleven is evaluated directly. The checker independently verifies all components over twenty fields, including three quadratic extensions. Finite checks do not replace the argument for arbitrary fields.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
The nearest mathematical precedents use additive energy in finite-field Fourier extension and derive formulas for different curves. Mockenhaupt–Tao gives the general representation-count mechanism and moment-curve framework. The separately published quartic-graph energy theorem uses a discriminant involution, and the published fifth-power-graph spectrum uses a conic; neither computes the two elliptic sums or the characteristic-eleven degeneration here. Iosevich–Koh treats quadratic surfaces. Koh–Shen treats general extension estimates for plane algebraic curves, but a full-text comparison was not completed because available access routes did not return the paper. The general framework and sharpness criteria are not identified as giving this exact elliptic-trace expression. Indexed semantic, alias, and formula searches found no explicit sextic equivalent. Unindexed character-sum evaluations and the inaccessible relevant Koh–Shen full text are residual priority risks; searches alone are not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Exact constant-input fourth moments are intrinsic Fourier extension invariants and directly determine additive energy. For the natural sparse monomial curve of degree six, the problem changes from conic counting in adjacent classical degrees to two genuinely elliptic traces, yielding arithmetic fluctuations of Hasse size. The characteristic-eleven specialization is a structural and asymptotic phase change in the normalized fourth moment, from \(3\) to \(4\). This is an infinite-family exact classification with a natural exceptional characteristic, not a selected finite-sample statistic. It does not claim a sharp unrestricted Fourier-extension norm or a complete weighted extremizer census.
