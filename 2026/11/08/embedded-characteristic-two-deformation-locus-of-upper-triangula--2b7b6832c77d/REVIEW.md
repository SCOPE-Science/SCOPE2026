# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The inner-twist equation is obtained by direct squaring, using that the center of the triangular algebra over a commutative ring consists of scalar matrices. The reversal orbits on upper-triangular positions give an explicit global normal form after normalizing the first diagonal unit. All components, dimensions, finite-field counts, and the characteristic-two local ring follow from this normal form. The tangent excess is exactly one on the support \(z_1=\cdots=z_m=0\), and \(\epsilon=t\) obstructs lifting to order three. An exhaustive prime-field matrix-unit checker corroborates the point counts and involution identities in seven small configurations; it does not establish the infinite cases.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The foundational classification of algebra involutions is established in Spiegel (2005) and Di Vincenzo–Koshlukov–La Scala (2006). Fornaroli–Pezzott (2022, arXiv:2209.09690) was inspected in full HTML, including its introduction and main classification; it works in characteristic unequal to two and classifies equivalence classes, not the unquotiented characteristic-two nonreduced parameter scheme. The relevant 2023 Kulguskin–Tapkin paper classifies involutions over commutative rings up to equivalence, but its full text could not be inspected; it may subsume or anticipate some coordinate and deformation aspects. The 2006 full paper was also inaccessible. Actual mathematical-index searches for scheme nonreducedness, reversal-inner involutions, equivalent parameter forms, and finite-field counts yielded no decisive covering result; this absence is not proof of novelty. Prior results on nonsmooth automorphism schemes of unrelated algebras do not imply this exact triangular anti-involution locus. Historical priority remains provisional.

## Value — PASS

The natural unquotiented parameter scheme distinguishes field-valued involution classification from infinitesimal deformation geometry. Its reduced space is smooth and geometrically irreducible in characteristic two, yet its embedded nilpotent component produces a sharp codimension-\(m\) nonsmooth locus and an explicit first-order deformation blocked at second order; neither invariant can be read from orbit counts alone. This is a motivated structural boundary in the modular theory of algebra involutions rather than a numerical specialization of a known identity. The uniform finite-field counts are secondary consequences, not the sole novelty/value claim.
