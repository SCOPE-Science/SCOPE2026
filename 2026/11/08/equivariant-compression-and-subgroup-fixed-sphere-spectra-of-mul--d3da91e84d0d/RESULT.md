# Equivariant compression and subgroup-fixed sphere spectra of multipartite clique-free complexes

## Finding
Let \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\), where \(r\ge2\) and all part sizes are positive, and set \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). For every integer \(t\ge2\), write \(X_t=\mathsf{CF}_t(G)\) for the simplicial complex of subsets with no clique of size \(t\), and let \(Y_t=\operatorname{sk}_{t-2}\Delta^{r-1}\), with \(Y_t=\Delta^{r-1}\) if \(t>r\). The natural action of \(\Gamma\) on graph parts makes \(Y_t\) a \(\Gamma\)-space.

**Theorem.** There is a \(\Gamma\)-equivariant strong deformation retraction \(|X_t|\to\sigma(|Y_t|)\cong|Y_t|\), with the same deformation for every \(t\) and compatible with the full clique-free filtration. For any subgroup \(H\le\Gamma\), let its orbits on parts be \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_m\), and put \(w_j=|Q_j|\). If \(w_j\ge t\) for every \(j\), the geometric fixed-point space \(|X_t|^H\) is empty. Otherwise choose a minimum-weight part orbit \(Q_0\), of weight \(w_0<t\), and denote the other orbits by \(Q_j\). Then
\[
|X_t|^H\simeq\bigvee_{\substack{S\subseteq\{Q_j:j\ne0\}\\t-w_0\le\sum_{Q_j\in S}w_j<t}} S^{|S|-1}.
\]
The wedge is a point when there are no indexing subsets. Thus all reduced integral homology groups are free, with \(\widetilde\beta_d\) equal to the number of qualifying subsets of cardinality \(d+1\). The sphere-wedge formula for a scalar quota complex is known; the equivariant compression and the identification of its weights as part-orbit sizes are the content of this theorem.

For example, for \(G=K_8\), \(t=5\), and \(H\) generated by a disjoint transposition and three-cycle, the part-orbit weights are \((2,3,1,1,1)\) and
\[
|\mathsf{CF}_5(K_8)|^H\simeq S^2\vee S^1\vee S^1.
\]
The ordinary complex, by contrast, is a wedge of \(\binom{7}{4}=35\) three-spheres. For \(K_5\), \(t=3\), and a transposition subgroup, the fixed space is \(S^1\vee S^0\), hence has two connected components.

## Assumptions and scope
A complete multipartite graph is the graph whose vertices form disjoint, nonempty independent parts \(V_1,\ldots,V_r\), with precisely the cross-part pairs adjacent. Nonadjacency of distinct vertices determines the unique part partition. Every automorphism permutes these parts and preserves the cardinality of each. The geometric realization \(|X_t|\) uses nonnegative barycentric coordinates \(x_v\) with \(\sum_v x_v=1\); its faces are exactly the vectors supported on fewer than \(t\) distinct parts. The fixed space is the pointwise-fixed geometric realization, not the realization of the subcomplex on individually fixed vertices. Wedges with \(S^0\) use the usual pointed convention and may be disconnected.

## Proof
**1. An explicit equivariant retraction.** Define two affine maps on the barycentric realizations by
\[
\pi(x)_i=\sum_{v\in V_i}x_v,\qquad
\sigma(y)_v=\frac{y_i}{n_i}\quad (v\in V_i).
\]
A vector supported on fewer than \(t\) graph parts maps under \(\pi\) into \(|Y_t|\). Conversely, the support of \(\sigma(y)\) occupies exactly the parts in the support of \(y\), hence \(\sigma(y)\in|X_t|\). Moreover \(\pi\sigma=\mathrm{id}\). Define
\[
F_s(x)=(1-s)x+s\sigma\pi(x),\qquad 0\le s\le1.
\]
For every \(s\), all nonzero coordinates lie in the same set of occupied parts as those of \(x\), so \(F_s(x)\in|X_t|\). We have \(F_0=\mathrm{id}\), \(F_1=\sigma\pi\), and \(F_s(\sigma(y))=\sigma(y)\). Since an automorphism sends each \(V_i\) bijectively onto another part of the same size, \(\pi\), \(\sigma\), and \(F_s\) are \(\Gamma\)-equivariant. None of the formulas depends on \(t\); therefore all retractions commute with the filtration inclusions.

**2. Geometric fixed points become a weighted threshold complex.** Fix \(H\le\Gamma\). A point \(y\in|Y_t|\) is \(H\)-fixed exactly when its barycentric coordinates are constant across each orbit \(Q_j\) of parts. Thus \(y\) is a convex combination of uniform barycenters of some complete part orbits \(Q_j\). Such a combination belongs to \(|Y_t|\) exactly when the union of its occupied part orbits has fewer than \(t\) parts, or equivalently
\[
\sum_{Q_j\text{ occupied}} w_j<t.
\]
Hence \(|Y_t|^H\) is PL-homeomorphic to the realization of the **scalar quota complex** \(K(w,t)\) on the part-orbit symbols with positive integer weights \(w_j\) and quota \(t\). The equivariant strong deformation retraction restricts to an ordinary strong deformation retraction on \(H\)-fixed points. If no orbit has weight below \(t\), the quota complex has no geometric vertices and the fixed space is empty.

**3. Exact integral homotopy type.** Suppose there is an admissible orbit \(Q_0\) of minimum weight \(w_0<t\). Drop other orbit symbols with weight at least \(t\), since they are not vertices. Let \(A\) be the subcomplex on remaining symbols other than \(Q_0\) with total weight \(<t-w_0\), and let \(B\) be the subcomplex on those symbols with total weight \(<t\). Then
\[
K(w,t)=B\cup(Q_0*A),\qquad B\cap(Q_0*A)=A.
\]
Every face \(S\in B\setminus A\) satisfies \(t-w_0\le w(S)<t\). Every vertex of \(S\) has weight at least \(w_0\); consequently every proper face of \(S\) belongs to \(A\). Thus the faces in \(B\setminus A\) are mutually nonnested and are attached to \(A\) along their entire boundaries. Contracting the cone \(Q_0*A\), a contractible subcomplex, produces one sphere of dimension \(|S|-1\) for each such \(S\). If \(A\) has empty realization, the cone is a single vertex and \(B\) is discrete; the same statement holds with zero-spheres. This gives a homotopy equivalence to the stated wedge, with no torsion and no additional homotopy restrictions. The underlying quota-complex wedge theorem was established by Pakianathan and Winfree; the preceding argument includes its required specialization.

## Verification
Run the standalone standard-library Python script `verify.py` in the same directory. It enumerates subgroup vertex orbits, identifies their projections to part orbits, builds all fixed-orbit faces both from their supports and by independent forbidden-clique enumeration, verifies dominated-vertex reductions to the weighted quota model, and compares direct reduced mod-two homology with the stated subset-count prediction. The output `verify_output.txt` records 50 cases, 807 fixed-orbit faces, 483 quota faces, and 28 verified dominated-vertex deletions, ending with `ALL_QUOTA_FIXED_CHECKS_OK`. Examples include trivial, transitive, part-swapping and within-part actions. The all-parameter integral claim is established by the preceding proof, not by the finite computations. The direct affine retraction is proved symbolically and does not require floating-point certification.

## Relationship to prior work
Ghosh and Selvaraja (arXiv:2602.09623v1, first public 2026-02-10) define and study clique-free complexes, proving shellability results for diamond-free chordal graphs and particular graph modifications. Their results do not give a group-equivariant deformation of complete multipartite clique-free complexes or classify geometric fixed points. Pakianathan and Winfree (2013) prove the wedge-of-spheres theorem for scalar quota complexes; that theorem explains the sphere count *after* identifying the fixed spaces as quota complexes and is not claimed as new. In the older polyhedral-complex literature, replacements of vertices by contractible fibers give analogous nonequivariant intuition. The specific full-automorphism, filtration-natural compression and its arbitrary-subgroup orbit-weight formula are the claimed extension; possible undocumented equivariant analogues remain a literature-overlap risk. A separate 2026 study of path-free complexes of complete multipartite graphs concerns a different forbidden subgraph and does not imply the clique-free statement.

## Limitations
The graph must be complete multipartite, the forbidden pattern is a complete \(t\)-clique, and the group acts by graph automorphisms. The theorem is a classification of equivariant homotopy type and fixed-point homotopy groups, not an equivariant **simplicial** isomorphism to a smaller complex. The wedge splittings of different fixed spaces need not be compatible with subgroup-inclusion maps. This does not establish new general results about arbitrary scalar quota complexes or arbitrary polyhedral joins.
