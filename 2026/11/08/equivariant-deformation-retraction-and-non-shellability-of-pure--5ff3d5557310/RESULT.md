# Equivariant deformation retraction and non-shellability of pure strong Morse complexes of unbalanced complete multipartite graphs

## Finding
Let \(t\ge 3\), \(a_1,\ldots,a_{t-1}\ge1\), and \(n\ge2\) satisfy \(n>\max_{i<t}a_i\). Let \(G=K_{a_1,\ldots,a_{t-1},n}\) be the **unfilled, one-dimensional** complete multipartite graph. Write \(C\) for its unique largest part, \(R=V(G)\setminus C\), and \(s=|R|=\sum_{i<t}a_i\). Let \(X=\mathcal{SM}_{\mathrm{pure}}(G)\), the subcomplex generated by the maximum-dimensional facets of the strong discrete-Morse matching complex obtained from injective vertex filtrations.

**Theorem.** The dimension of \(X\) is \(n-1\). Its facets are exactly the \(s(n+1)\) simplices \(F_r\) and \(F_{r,c}\), for \(r\in R\), \(c\in C\), described below. As a space with its natural \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-action, \(|X|\) strongly deformation retracts equivariantly onto the discrete \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-set \(R\). In particular,
\[
\widetilde H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\ker\!\left(\mathbb Z[R]\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb Z\right),\qquad
\widetilde H_j(X;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(j\ne0),
\]
where the kernel is an integral permutation-augmentation module. For every \(1\le k\le n\), the number of faces with exactly \(k\) vertices is
\[
f_{k-1}=s\left[\binom nk+n\binom{n-1}{k-1}\right].
\]
Every connected component is collapsible and individually shellable. Nevertheless \(X\) is **not shellable**, because \(s\ge2\) and \(n-1\ge1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is treated as a one-dimensional simplicial complex: triangles of the graph, where present, are **not** filled as two-simplices. A vertex of \(\mathcal{SM}(G)\) is a vertex–edge pair \((v,\{v,w\})\). For a total order on \(V(G)\), a pair is created exactly when \(v\) has precisely one earlier neighbor \(w\); the full strong matching is the collection of all such pairs. The full strong complex is the downward closure of these matchings. Its *pure* subcomplex is generated by those facets having the greatest dimension, rather than by every inclusion-maximal face of the full complex. No classification is asserted for tied largest parts.

For \(r\in R\) and \(c\in C\), abbreviate \(y_{r,c}=(c,\{r,c\})\) and \(x_{r,c}=(r,\{r,c\})\). Define
\[
F_r=\{y_{r,d}:d\in C\},\qquad
F_{r,c}=\{x_{r,c}\}\cup\{y_{r,d}:d\in C,\ d\ne c\}.
\]

## Proof
**1. Exactly one earlier neighbor.** In an unfilled graph, the descending link of a newly added vertex is the discrete set of its earlier neighbors. A nonempty zero-dimensional link is a simplicial cone exactly when it has one vertex. The defining strong-filtration algorithm therefore pairs \(v\) with \(\{v,w\}\) if and only if \(w\) is its unique earlier neighbor.

**2. First-three-parts restriction.** In any total order, let \(P,Q\) be the parts of the first two vertices belonging to different parts. At the first appearance of any third part, that new vertex has earlier neighbors in both \(P\) and \(Q\), so it is unpaired. Every subsequent vertex also has at least two earlier neighbors from distinct parts, so no later vertex is paired. All pairs therefore come from the initial ordering restricted to \(P\cup Q\), where the induced graph is complete bipartite.

**3. Bipartite maximum and equality.** On an unfilled complete bipartite graph with part sizes \(p,q\), the unique-earlier-neighbor rule produces at most \(\max(p,q)\) pairs. To see this, suppose the first vertex belongs to the \(p\)-part. If its second vertex in that part appears after the second \(q\)-part vertex, only \(q\)-part vertices can contribute, at most \(q\) of them. If it appears before the second \(q\)-part vertex, only the first \(q\)-part vertex can contribute on that side, and at most \(p-1\) later vertices on the \(p\)-side can contribute: total at most \(p\). If two or more \(p\)-vertices precede the first \(q\)-vertex, this only lowers the possibilities. The opposite initial-part case is symmetric. Missing second vertices of singleton parts are understood as occurring after the end of the order.

Since \(C\) is uniquely largest, a gradient of size \(n\) must be supported on \(C\) and a single other part, and must attain the equality case with exactly one vertex \(r\) of that other part present before all but possibly the first \(C\)-vertex. If \(r\) precedes all \(C\)-vertices, all \(n\) pairs are \(y_{r,c}\), giving \(F_r\). Otherwise one vertex \(c\in C\) precedes \(r\), which pairs as \(x_{r,c}\), and the other \(n-1\) vertices of \(C\) pair as \(y_{r,d}\), giving \(F_{r,c}\). In both cases, any remaining vertices are placed last. Thus all listed simplices arise, all have \(n\) vertices, no larger gradient exists, and no additional maximal-dimensional facets exist.

**4. Equivariant homotopy.** For each \(r\in R\), let \(Y_r\) be generated by \(F_r\) and the \(n\) facets \(F_{r,c}\). Its vertex set is disjoint from that of \(Y_{r'}\) for \(r\ne r'\); hence \(X=\bigsqcup_{r\in R}Y_r\). The facet \(F_r\) is an \((n-1)\)-simplex. Each additional facet \(F_{r,c}\) is a cone from the new vertex \(x_{r,c}\) over the codimension-one face \(F_r\setminus\{y_{r,c}\}\). Send \(x_{r,c}\) linearly to the barycenter of this opposite face, fixing the base vertices \(y_{r,d}\). Straight-line interpolation stays inside each flap simplex and agrees on all intersections; it gives a strong deformation retraction \(|Y_r|\to|F_r|\). For a literal simplicial collapse, choose any base vertex \(y_{r,d}\) with \(d\ne c\) for each flap \(F_{r,c}\). Pair each face containing \(x_{r,c}\) but not \(y_{r,d}\) with the face obtained by adjoining \(y_{r,d}\); processing these free pairs in decreasing face dimension removes that flap without altering the base simplex. Repeat for each \(c\), proving \(Y_r\) collapsible. Then contract \(|F_r|\) linearly to its barycenter. Graph automorphisms preserve the uniquely largest part \(C\), permute the labels \(r,c\), and commute with these barycentric maps. The disjoint union of barycenters is naturally the \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-set \(R\). The augmentation-kernel assertion follows from equivariant \(H_0\).

**5. Face count and shellability.** In each component, faces without a flap vertex are precisely the \(\binom nk\) size-\(k\) subsets of \(F_r\). A face containing the unique flap vertex \(x_{r,c}\) uses \(k-1\) of the other \(n-1\) base vertices, giving \(\binom{n-1}{k-1}\) choices per \(c\). There are \(s\) components, proving the face formula. Within each component the central facet followed by the flaps is a pure shelling. Distinct components have disjoint vertex sets, however; a pure complex of positive dimension with more than one connected component cannot have a shelling, since the first facet from a new component has empty intersection with every earlier facet. As \(t\ge3\), \(s\ge2\).

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` independently enumerates every injective vertex order for 16 complete multipartite parameter tuples, reconstructs all full strong gradients using the graph's earlier-neighbor rule, selects maximum-size gradients, and compares the complete facet sets to the formula. It also checks the entire face-count formula, separation by distinguished vertex \(r\), invariance under permutations within and between equal-sized parts, and all reduced mod-two Betti numbers. The largest tested graph has nine vertices (\(9!\) orders). All cases pass as recorded in `verify_output.txt`. The uniform theorem is established by the preceding proof, not by finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens-White, *Complexes of strong discrete Morse matchings* (arXiv:2609.06844, first posted 2026-09-06), introduce the full and pure constructions and classify paths, stars, simplices, their boundaries and the pure cycles; their Section 10 treats ordinary complete graphs. Their star-graph calculation corresponds to the one-vertex complement boundary case, whereas the theorem here concerns genuinely multipartite graphs with at least three nonempty parts and more than one connected component in the pure complex. Fernández, *Strong discrete Morse theory* (arXiv:2504.15729; DOI:10.1017/prm.2025.10105), supplies the descending-dominance framework, not a homotopy classification of these matching complexes. The ordinary Morse complexes and the full strong complex are different objects; known homotopy types of those objects do not determine the pure subcomplex's equivariant homotopy type.

## Limitations
The theorem requires a **unique** largest part and the **unfilled** graph; equality among the largest parts may produce different facets and homotopy. No conclusion is made about the homotopy type of the full strong complex, the ordinary Morse complex, the flag-clique complex, or other graph families. The numerical verification is restricted to the listed small cases. Unindexed equivalent work and independent mathematical validation remain possible.
