# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** For bounded complex coefficient sequences, the local scalar map \(z\mapsto az+b\bar z\) has norm \(|a|+|b|\), with norming numerical values on the corresponding outer circle. Finite-rank truncations and weakly null coordinate vectors give the essential norm. The barycentric-measure argument forces any essential-norming weakly null sequence to concentrate on the scalar equality locus; disjoint tail coordinates realize every convex combination. This reconstructs the exact formula, its complete convex-hull realization theorem, the outer-contact identity for the ordinary essential numerical range, and the conjugation discontinuity. The proof separately handles vanishing coefficients and essential norm zero. The 3,005 finite diagnostics support but do not establish these universal arguments.

**Originality — PASS, best-knowledge qualified.** Majumdar–Müller (arXiv:2610.16823v1, Sections 3 and 5) establish that the real-linear essential-maximal numerical range is compact and convex, but do not compute all diagonal instances, characterize their norm-circle contacts, or classify their possible shapes. Kołaczek–Müller (arXiv:2508.04347v1, Section 6) concern ordinary numerical-range convexity. Carvalho–Diogo–Mendes–Soares (arXiv:2212.11659v1, Theorem 3) supply the ordinary essential numerical range for complex-linear block diagonals but omit the norm-saturation invariant and antilinear component. Fong (1979, Section 2) proves an upper-semicontinuity result for the complex-linear essential-maximal range, compatible with the sharp **lower** Hausdorff discontinuity here. Searches in published research summaries and the accessible historical claim ledger found no mathematically equivalent or dominating claim. The pure-antilinear maximal-range paper of Amara–Liu–Zhu (2025/2026) was accessible by abstract only; a general characterization elsewhere or a realification reduction remains a priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The recently defined essential-maximal numerical range has an exact complete realization/rigidity theorem on a canonical class of mixed real-linear operators: only convex hulls of compact pieces of the essential-norm circle occur, and all of them occur. The norm-one conjugation family shows the largest conceivable Hausdorff instability under perturbations tending to zero, despite continuous behavior of the ordinary essential numerical range. The outer-contact reconstruction also identifies what information in the ordinary essential numerical range determines the norming invariant. These are structural and geometric conclusions not supplied by compact-convexity alone, rather than a rephrased numerical estimate.

Limits: the result is restricted to coordinatewise operators; the realization theorem is a classification at fixed essential norm, not a classification of arbitrary real-linear operators. The proof does not rely on any finite experiment. Independent audit and complete publication-priority determination remain outstanding.
