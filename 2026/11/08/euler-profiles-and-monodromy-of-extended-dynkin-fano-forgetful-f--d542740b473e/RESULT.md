# Euler profiles and monodromy of extended-Dynkin Fano forgetful fibrations

## Finding
Let \(p_k:X_k\to\mathbb P^k\cong\operatorname{Sym}^k(\mathbb P^1)\) be any of the five smooth complex Fano forgetful maps in Belmans--Reineke, *Series of Fano quiver moduli from extended Dynkin quivers* (arXiv:2610.22203v1, Table 1), with \(k\geq3\). Let \(T\subset\mathbb P^1\) be the finite exceptional set of regular parameters, with cardinality \(s=0,0,1,2,3\) respectively for the series \(\widetilde A_1(a),\widetilde A_1(b),\widetilde A_2,\widetilde A_3,\widetilde D_4\). For each effective divisor \(D\) of degree \(k\), write
\[
D=\sum_{x\notin T}m_x[x]+\sum_{\tau\in T}m_\tau[\tau],
\]
with only finitely many nonzero multiplicities. Then the *topological Euler characteristic of every geometric fibre* is
\[
\boxed{\chi(p_k^{-1}(D))=\prod_{x\notin T}(m_x+1)\prod_{\tau\in T}(2m_\tau+1).}
\]
The product includes only positive multiplicities, so empty products equal one. The reduced locus \(B_k\) over which \(p_k\) is not smooth is precisely
\[
\boxed{B_k=\Delta_k\cup\bigcup_{\tau\in T}H_\tau,\qquad
\deg(B_k)=2k-2+s,}
\]
where \(\Delta_k\) is the irreducible discriminant of degree-\(k\) binary forms with repeated roots and \(H_\tau=\{D:m_\tau\geq1\}\) is a hyperplane. This equality describes *reduced support*, not scheme-theoretic discriminant multiplicities.

Over \(U_k=\mathbb P^k\setminus B_k\), the fibre is \((\mathbb P^1)^k\). Its integral cohomology local systems satisfy, for every \(0\leq j\leq k\),
\[
R^{2j}(p_k|_{U_k})_*\mathbb Z\simeq
\mathbb Z\bigl[\{J\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}:|J|=j\}\bigr]_{\!U_k},
\qquad R^{2j+1}(p_k|_{U_k})_*\mathbb Z=0,
\]
where the right side is the local system of the natural \(S_k\)-permutation module associated with the ordered-root cover. Its image on \(R^2\) is the full symmetric group \(S_k\). A small loop around a general point of \(\Delta_k\) acts by a transposition; in degree \(2j\), the rank of its \(-1\)-eigenspace is \(\binom{k-2}{j-1}\). A small loop around a general point of any \(H_\tau\) has trivial action on all fibre cohomology, although the limiting fibre is singular. In particular, the two types of components of \(B_k\) have opposite generic Euler jumps: from \(2^k\) to \(3\cdot2^{k-2}\) along \(\Delta_k\) and to \(3\cdot2^{k-1}\) along \(H_\tau\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\), in the five specific framed extended-Dynkin quiver configurations of Table 1 in arXiv:2610.22203v1, not for arbitrary orientations or framings. The integer \(k\geq3\) is arbitrary. The original construction identifies the unframed coarse moduli with \(\mathbb P^k\), and its regular simple parameters with \(\mathbb P^1\); the exceptional points arise from the non-homogeneous tubes in the regular representation category. In the five cases the framing dot-product with the imaginary root is two; at an exceptional tube, the two stable constituents receive one framing generator apiece. Topological Euler characteristic means the ordinary singular-cohomology Euler characteristic of the compact complex fibre. The cohomology local systems are for the classical complex topology on \(U_k\).

## Proof
**1. Decomposition into local factors.** The representation-theoretic Luna stratification of the forgetful morphism (Belmans--Reineke, Proposition 11 and Corollary 12) identifies its fibre over a divisor \(D\) with a product of two kinds of projective framed nilpotent moduli: a Jordan-loop factor \(J_m\) for a regular nonexceptional root of multiplicity \(m\), and an oriented two-cycle factor \(C_m\) with dimension vector \((m,m)\) and framing \((1,1)\) for an exceptional root of multiplicity \(m\). Each factor has dimension \(m\). These identifications also show that a divisor with simple support avoiding \(T\) has fibre \((\mathbb P^1)^k\).

**2. Jordan-factor Euler characteristic.** Present \(J_m\) as the moduli of length-\(m\) nilpotent quotients of the free \(\mathbb C[t]\)-module \(\mathbb C[t]e_1\oplus\mathbb C[t]e_2\) generated by its two distinguished framing vectors. There is a torus action scaling \(t,e_1,e_2\) independently. A torus-fixed quotient has a monomial kernel, so is determined by truncation lengths \(a,b\geq0\), with \(a+b=m\); conversely every such pair gives one torus-fixed quotient. There are exactly \(m+1\). Since \(J_m\) is projective, torus localization of the Euler characteristic (valid even for singular varieties) gives \(\chi(J_m)=m+1\).

**3. Two-cycle-factor Euler characteristic.** Write the two directed arrows as \(u:0\to1\) and \(v:1\to0\). The free framed path representation has one path of every nonnegative length beginning at vertex zero and one beginning at vertex one. Independently scale the two arrows and the two frame generators. Distinct paths carry distinct weights. A fixed finite-dimensional nilpotent quotient is obtained by retaining the paths of lengths \(<a\) from vertex zero and \(<b\) from vertex one. Its vertex dimensions are
\[
\left(\left\lceil\frac a2\right\rceil+\left\lfloor\frac b2\right\rfloor,
\left\lfloor\frac a2\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac b2\right\rceil\right).
\]
For both dimensions to equal \(m\), the lengths must have the same parity. The even-even solutions are \((a,b)=(2c,2(m-c))\), for \(0\leq c\leq m\), giving \(m+1\) solutions. The odd-odd solutions are \((a,b)=(2c+1,2(m-1-c)+1)\), for \(0\leq c<m\), giving \(m\) more. There are no others. Projectivity and torus localization give \(\chi(C_m)=2m+1\). Multiplicativity of Euler characteristic on products proves the boxed formula for all \(D\).

**4. Exact non-smooth base locus.** If all roots of \(D\) are distinct and avoid \(T\), the fibre is a product of smooth lines; the morphism is flat with smooth geometric fibres there, hence smooth. At a general point of \(\Delta_k\), exactly one ordinary root has multiplicity two; its Jordan factor is singular. Explicitly, at the framed representation with zero nilpotent endomorphism and two framing vectors forming a basis, the local equations, after gauge normalization and trace elimination, are \(a^2+bc=0\) in three variables. At a general point of \(H_\tau\), exactly one exceptional root is simple; its two-cycle factor has an affine chart with both framings normalized and equation \(uv=0\), so is singular. The image of the non-smooth locus is closed because the morphism is proper, and it contains the dense open subsets just exhibited in every component. Thus the reduced non-smooth base set is exactly \(\Delta_k\cup\bigcup H_\tau\). The binary discriminant is irreducible of homogeneous coefficient degree \(2k-2\); each fixed-root condition is a distinct linear equation. The degree follows.

**5. Integral monodromy.** On the ordered configuration cover of \(U_k\), the product decomposition labels each of the \(k\) projective-line factors. Its degree-two hyperplane class is locally constant because any change of trivialization is a complex projective automorphism and acts trivially on \(H^2(\mathbb P^1,\mathbb Z)\). Hence the ordered cover makes the whole fibre cohomology local system constant; its deck transformations only permute the even-degree generators \(h_i\), with \(h_i^2=0\). The ordered configuration cover has full deck monodromy \(S_k\), generated by exchanges of neighboring distinct roots. Consequently \(R^{2j}\) is the permutation module on \(j\)-subsets, and the full image on \(R^2\) is \(S_k\). Around a simple double-root discriminant, the local exchange is a transposition. Of the \(\binom kj\) subsets, precisely \(\binom{k-2}{j-1}\) unordered pairs are exchanged, giving the \(-1\)-eigenrank. A loop around \(H_\tau\) with all roots distinct fixes their labels and therefore acts trivially. The generic Euler jumps follow from the product formula using the multiplicity profiles \((2,1^{k-2})\) and an exceptional simple root with \(k-1\) ordinary simple roots.

## Verification
A standalone standard-library checker, `CHECK.py`, counts all torus-fixed path cutoffs for \(1\leq m\leq15\), verifies the five possible fibre Euler characteristics in the fourfold base case as \(4,3,6,9,5\), tests the discriminant degree formula for \(3\leq k\leq11\) and \(0\leq s\leq3\), and computes the integral transposition permutation matrices on all exterior-subset degrees. It returned `FINITE_CERTIFICATE_OK`. These finite tests do not prove the unbounded statement: the proof is the path-counting classification, the cited fibre decomposition, and the geometric argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Belmans and Reineke (arXiv:2610.22203v1) construct the five Fano families, prove flatness, identify fibres over Luna strata with nilpotent framed moduli, and list the discriminants and fibre types for \(k=2\). This provides the input but does not state the all-multiplicity Euler function, the exact all-\(k\) reduced critical-value divisor or its integral cohomological monodromy. Their earlier fourfold paper arXiv:2607.12895 treats particular fourfolds, not the \(k\geq3\) assertion. Prior literature on Euler characteristics of Nakajima quiver varieties involves related but different moduli; the fixed-path enumeration here concerns the specifically framed nilpotent Jordan and two-cycle fibres of these forgetful maps. The source's full fibre classification is stronger in a different direction; it is not claimed as new.

## Limitations
Only the five prescribed Fano series and \(k\geq3\) are covered. The discriminant equality is set-theoretic with reduced support; neither scheme multiplicities nor the full derived pushforward, perverse sheaf decomposition, integral ring of the total space, or fibres of arbitrary framings are computed. The finite checker supplements but does not replace the proof. Historical priority for the componentwise Euler functions, especially in earlier Quot or cyclic-quiver literature, has not been exhaustively certified; external independent validation is not provided.
