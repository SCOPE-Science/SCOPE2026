# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The affine reduction is invertible. The noncentral box-section lemma handles all positive real coefficients, strict feasibility, signed offsets, and coefficient equalities. The critical inequality comes from comparing the coordinatewise midpoint of extrema with the affine hyperplane: a hypothetical center requires \(\sum_i\min(b_i,\delta)=2\delta\), which is impossible when \(r\ge3\) and no weight dominates. Its sufficient branch explicitly parameterizes a full cube. Applying that lemma to each full-dimensional active-coordinate facet establishes the all-dimensional classification; in the remaining cases there is a direct zonotope construction. The finite checker uses exact rational vertex sets and successfully checked 6,827 instances. No finite census is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

The full text of Anantharaman's open-access manuscript (arXiv:2305.18351v2, sections 1–4) explicitly disclaims a complete characterization, giving only four-dimensional examples. The dimension-four special case was previously obtained and is acknowledged, not reused as a new theorem. This work proves the all-dimensional rigidity lemma, not implied by the four-dimensional result, and obtains an exact criterion for every dimension and all zero/dominant coefficient boundaries. Shephard's classical criterion for zonotopes and Ambrus's volume-criticality results have different scopes. Search coverage does not logically prove uniqueness; older off-center box-section literature remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The work supplies a natural full-dimensional classification for the very family in which central sections can fail to preserve zonotopality, and answers a published explicit characterization gap. The rigidity lemma gives a reusable structural obstruction for high-dimensional noncentral box sections; the criterion is computationally immediate and includes all degenerate normal supports. This is not a table lookup or a parameter-only tweak.

## Closest sources and scope

Anantharaman (2019/2023), sections 1–4, supplies the original question, examples and face obstruction. Shephard (1967) supplies classical central-symmetry characterizations. Ambrus (2021) studies section volume stationary points, not zonoid membership. The earlier dimension-four criterion is included as already known, and no distinct new four-dimensional claim is made.

## Residual uncertainty

The possibility of an equivalent but unindexed all-dimensional statement remains. The result is for central hyperplanes of affine cubes/boxes, not arbitrary zonotopes or lower-codimension sections. Independent audit has not been performed.
