Pick's theorem gives \(h_P^*=1+(I+B-3)t+It^2\), and its discriminant is negative exactly at \((B,I)=(3,1),(3,2),(3,3)\). The lattice count of \(P\times[0,m]\) is exactly \((mn+1)L_P(n)\), yielding the displayed numerator transform. Wagner's established product theorem, explicitly stated in Liu [3, Theorem 2.2(i)], handles the real-rooted base cases. In all three exceptional base cases the new cubic discriminants expand into polynomials in \(q=m-1\) with nonnegative coefficients and are positive whenever \(m>1\). At \(m=1\) the quadratic discriminant is \(4(k-1)^2\). All numerator coefficients are nonnegative with constant one, ruling out nonnegative zeros. The full analytic argument applies to all integer \(m\ge1\), \(I\ge0\), \(B\ge3\), with no enumeration assumption. The standalone program reproduced all three exact polynomial discriminants and 117,600 exact count comparisons.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The theorem isolates a natural, dimension-three geometric class for which real-rootedness is universal without a lattice-width threshold. More importantly it proves **root repair**: even the three possible non-real-rooted planar Ehrhart numerators become entirely real-rooted after taking a unit-segment Cartesian product, although ordinary product-preservation theorems cannot be applied to these inputs. This completes a structural classification for all planar-base lattice prisms and answers a concrete geometric question prompted by recent width-based real-rootedness research, rather than optimizing an arbitrary slice or merely restating Wagner's theorem.
