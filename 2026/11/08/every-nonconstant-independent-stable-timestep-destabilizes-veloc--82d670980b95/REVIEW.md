# Review of the independent-step velocity-Verlet instability theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact displayed velocity-Verlet matrix is symplectic, elliptic for every fixed step in \((0,2)\), and preserves the positive form \(G_h\). The commutator calculation gives trace strictly greater than 2 for every distinct pair, proving noncompactness for the subgroup generated by any two support points of a non-point-mass law. No invariant finite set of projective lines exists: one line conflicts with ellipticity, two lines require both steps to equal \(\sqrt2\), and at least three lines would force the generated projective group to be finite. The full law inherits noncompactness and total irreducibility from this subgroup, and its matrix logarithmic moment is automatically finite on the bounded timestep interval. Therefore the standard Furstenberg theorem yields a strictly positive almost-sure top Lyapunov exponent for every non-point-mass law. The physical-time result follows by the strong law. Symbolic and exact-rational checks corroborate the identities, without replacing the infinite theorem.

**Originality — PASS, with residual precedence risk.** Classical Furstenberg positivity is acknowledged. Piché (arXiv:1209.5173) and Richardson–Finn (arXiv:1108.0322) treat periodic or prescribed/adaptive timestep modulation rather than the every-distribution independent-step almost-sure classification. Sharma and collaborators (arXiv:2108.05420) study energy-preserving adaptive variational methods, not this independently randomized fixed-frequency velocity-Verlet map. Sturman–Thiffeault analyze random shears rather than this particular family. A related published result for randomly stepped symplectic Euler concerns growth of *expected squared energy* for a different first-order matrix family, which neither implies this second-order almost-sure theorem nor supplies its all-pairs hyperbolic commutator. Searches did not reveal a prior identical all-parameter statement, but unindexed specialist results remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** Variable-step resonance is a recognized issue in long-time integration. The theorem gives a sharp zero-versus-positive almost-sure instability classification for every independent stable-step probability law on an important second-order symplectic scheme without a minimum jitter threshold, and shows deterministic stable alternation does not protect against independent random ordering. It distinguishes sample-path exponential sensitivity from mean-square instability, while not claiming a rate constant or applicability to nonlinear, feedback-selected, or general correlated-step methods.

**Scientific limits.** Furstenberg's general theorem is used as an external standard result. There is no explicit numerical bound for the positive exponent. The algebraic verifier checks the finite identities and not the infinite probabilistic theorem. Exact existing specialist coverage cannot be ruled out absolutely.
