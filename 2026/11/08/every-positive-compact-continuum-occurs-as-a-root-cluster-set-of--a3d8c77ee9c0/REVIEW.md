# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reconstructs the diagonal positive-root formula, the uniform fixed-coordinate difference estimate, and the equivalence of WOT limits and vector Cesàro limits. The necessity argument derives connectedness directly from vanishing successive jumps. The sufficiency argument constructs shrinking polygonal tours for an arbitrary compact connected target without assuming path connectedness, samples the resulting Lipschitz curve at logarithmic times, and applies an exactly telescoping inverse-Cesàro weight formula. Uniformly bounded logarithmic weights make all shifts bounded below and above. The scalar asymptotic numerical range follows from a compact connected parameter set times the probability simplex. The finite checker verifies algebraic identities and sample bounds, not the infinite conclusion.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Ji and Yang (2026, arXiv:2608.21553) prove the bounded-below connectedness constraint and the scalar interval cluster set for one weighted shift, and construct a disconnected example when bounded-below fails. Their inspected full-text sections do not provide the all-continua joint realization classification for finite sums. Nayak and Shekhawat (2024, arXiv:2410.16318, Section 5, Proposition 5.2) characterize norm convergence of the positive-root sequence of a single weighted shift through uniform window means; their inspected full text does not supply the prescribed nonconvergent WOT cluster-set realization for finite sums. The other closest published findings concern power-norm profiles or vector maximizing-sequence clusters, different invariants; the comparisons are implication-based. Older summability literature (Barone, 1939; Leviatan and Lorch, 1971) is relevant and its complete relationship to the inverse-Cesàro realization remains a publication-priority risk because the full relevant texts were not obtained. These limits prevent claims of definitive precedence or independent novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result completely classifies a natural family of operator-valued cluster sets: one shift gives only intervals, but two bounded-below shifts already give arbitrary positive planar continua, not merely coordinatewise interval products. The exact numerical-range formula explains which geometric information is invisible to this scalar invariant. It is structurally stronger than isolated examples of failure of convergence or disconnectedness and provides an explicit realizability method.

## Scope and limitations

The classification assumes finitely many positive-weight bounded-below unilateral shifts. No extension to arbitrary bounded-below operators, infinite sums, or prescribed uniform weight bounds is claimed. The construction and proof are mathematical rather than an independent external validation; finite computational checks only support the algebraic transformations.
