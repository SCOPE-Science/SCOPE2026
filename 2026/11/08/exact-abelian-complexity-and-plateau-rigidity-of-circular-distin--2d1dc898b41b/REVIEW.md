# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof reduces arbitrary factor lengths to residues of the period; the crucial combinatorial lemma establishes connected phase fibers for every Parikh vector by analyzing proper-support intervals and their complements. Equality of adjacent vectors is equivalent to equality of letters at distance \(r\), and the explicit two-orientation run count proves the formula. Convexity of the positive-part contributions yields the full plateau and its exact endpoints. Direct enumeration using the packaged checker passes on 3344 parameter profiles; exhaustive finite checks are corroboration, not an infinite proof. The result requires pairwise distinct run letters and at least three runs.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** Fici–Puzynina (2022), Sections 3.1–3.2, gives general boundedness for periodic words and exact results for other word families, not this distinct-run formula. Chen–Wen (2018/2019), Theorems 1–2, treats generalized Thue–Morse and automatic sequences, not these circular single-run letters. Searches for equivalent periodic Parikh-spectrum formulas, cyclic-window rigidity and longest-run plateaux located no stronger coverage in the material examined. The known coordinate-permutation group classification of cyclic run words is a different invariant and does not imply these counts. Unindexed equivalent work remains possible.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This determines the entire infinite two-parameter family of length and run-profile spectra, rather than simply observing periodic boundedness. The key connected-fiber lemma supplies a structural reason for efficient counting, and the surprising invariance under cyclic permutations of runs plus the sharp longest-run phase transition make the formula useful in studying Abelian factor profiles and identifying the information lost by commutative equivalence.

## Scientific limits
The result is restricted to at least three distinct run letters, each appearing in one cyclic run. The numerical checks are finite and not a proof. The prior-art comparison cannot exclude every unindexed or inaccessible equivalent publication.
