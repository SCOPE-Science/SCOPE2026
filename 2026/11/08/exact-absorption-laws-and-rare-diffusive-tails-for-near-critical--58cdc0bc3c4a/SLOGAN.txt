For the simple sticky walk with \(m\) players initially holding only one or two cards, the complete absorption-time distribution is an exact two-branch mixture of gambler’s ruin laws. At sparse excess \(1\le k=o(m)\), the mean grows like \(mk\) while the time divided by its mean tends to zero in probability; a universal theta-series describes the rare \(m^2\)-scale trajectories carrying that mean.
