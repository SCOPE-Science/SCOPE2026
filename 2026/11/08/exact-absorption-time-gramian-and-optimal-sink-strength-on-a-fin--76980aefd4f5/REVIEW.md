# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite-dimensional Lindblad sink is reduced to the stable no-jump generator \(A=i\Omega B/2-\kappa P/2\). The strict spectral stability argument excludes dark modes. An explicit, all-size, parity-structured matrix solves \(A^\dagger Q+QA=-I\) entry by entry, with first-row and terminal-boundary identities handled separately. Uniqueness of the stable Lyapunov solution proves the survival integral and mean absorption formula. Strict convexity gives the unique optimum; exact rational residuals and an independent eigenspace integral provide reproducible consistency tests. Risks: the proof assumes positive \(\kappa,\Omega\), finite path, and the stated Lindblad mechanism.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Riberi, arXiv:2605.08056v1 (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08056v1), gives the physical boundary-sink model on a *semi-infinite* path, not the complete finite-length integrated survival operator. Its conclusions explicitly identify finite geometries as future work. Varbanov–Krovi–Brun, arXiv:0803.3446v1, treat repeated random projective detection rather than autonomous physical loss, and their graph calculations do not present the displayed finite-path sink kernel. Gupta, arXiv:2512.15130v2, considers finite *unitary* periodic chains with real defects, without absorption. Searches for finite-path dissipative lifetime, Lyapunov Gramian, and uniform coupling optimum, together with available indexed mathematical claims, found no equivalent or dominating formula. A prior unindexed special case of the general Lyapunov method is a residual priority risk; an absence of matches alone is not proof of global novelty.

## Value — PASS

The uniform finite path is the natural finite-geometry counterpart of the recent semi-infinite absorbing model. The result determines every initial site's mean loss time and the entire time-integrated operator without diagonalizing an arbitrary-length non-Hermitian matrix. The size-independent uniquely optimal sink coupling and the counterintuitive linear far-end versus quadratic bulk lifetime scaling give physically distinguishable, exact transport-design predictions. These are more substantial than simply applying the general Lyapunov identity or computing a few small cases.

## Scope and prior-work limits

The claim is not a new general Lindblad solution, not a general graph characterization, and not a statement for repeated projective monitoring. The literature comparisons are qualitative where earlier papers employ different detection mechanisms. Numerical tests cannot establish the arbitrary-size statement independently of the proof.
