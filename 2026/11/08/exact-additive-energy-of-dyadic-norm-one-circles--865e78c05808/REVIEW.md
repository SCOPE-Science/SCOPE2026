# Scientific review: exact additive energy of dyadic norm-one circles

## Correctness — PASS
The proof fixes the exact ring, normalization, group law, quadruple ordering, and parameter range. Circle points number \(2^{k+1}\) by the four dyadic square roots of each unit congruent to one modulo eight. The circle reduces four-to-one onto \(i^j(1+2iz)/(1-2iz)\) modulo \(2^{k-1}\), with \(j=0,1,2,3\) and \(z\bmod 2^{k-3}\). The difference-energy group identity reduces the count to a real-part congruence, and an explicit ten-case numerator table leads to \(T'_k=6(k-2)2^{k-3}\) and hence \(E(C_k)=3(k-2)2^{2k+3}\). The supplied checker independently verifies direct convolution energy and angular incidences at levels four through nine. These checks corroborate, rather than replace, the all-depth proof. The square-root and valuation facts are explicitly justified within the result.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
The closest inspected primary sources were Nica, *Unimodular graphs and Eisenstein sums* (arXiv:1505.05034), Sections 1, 5, 6, and Hickman–Wright, *The Fourier restriction and Kakeya problems over rings of integers modulo N* (arXiv:1801.03176), Introduction and Section 2. Nica restricts finite valuation-ring theory to odd residue cardinality (where two is invertible); Hickman–Wright's results concern uniform restriction inequalities and particularly polynomial graph surfaces, not this exact circle fourth moment. The closest accessible historical circle claim is for odd primes congruent to three modulo four, and cannot be specialized to residue characteristic two. Searches also compared the additive-energy statement with its identical unweighted fourth-moment formulation, related dyadic rings, and finite-field norm-one conics; no dominating exact formula was identified. General 2-adic local-density identities could nonetheless contain an implicit earlier derivation and remain a historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem determines a complete, canonical fourth-order additive statistic for a natural p-adic norm-one variety at every dyadic depth. In particular \(E(C_k)/|C_k|^2=6(k-2)\) identifies a depth-linear concentration rather than the depth-independent behavior characteristic of many finite-field circle counts. The exact Fourier identity makes this structural feature relevant to restriction and convolution, without claiming weighted sharp extension constants.

## Limitations
No full two-point convolution histogram or sharp arbitrary-weight extension norm is established. The result applies only for \(k\ge4\), and independent mathematical review and full historical-priority determination remain outstanding.
