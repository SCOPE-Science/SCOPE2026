# Review of exact vertex-atomic simplex projection norms

## Correctness — PASS

Normalized Dirichlet-one barycentric second moments yield a diagonal-plus-rank-one mass matrix, and its exact Sherman--Morrison inverse has positive diagonal and strictly negative off-diagonal entries. A positive kernel region is therefore one geometrically explicit simplex cap. Its volume product, affine average, and atomic contribution prove the sharp formula. Convex interpolation and opposite-sign kernels prove that only vertices are norming. The one-atom formulas, positive derivative, Taylor coefficient and limiting expression follow by direct algebra. A standalone rational checker independently checks inversion, reproduction, cap integrals in dimensions 1 and 2, and one-atom identities. No finite test is used as an infinite proof.

An accessible historical Dirichlet-weighted affine-simplex theorem has an absolutely continuous measure and does not imply mixed volume-plus-vertex-atomic formulas. The earlier equilateral metric-star leaf-atom formula applies to a different graph-spline space, not an affine simplex. The full arXiv HTML of Hangelbroek et al. Full arXiv text and PDF front matter of Melenk--Wurzer (2013), especially the introduction, theorem framework and proof, concern unweighted volume projection and boundary trace estimates, not this point-mass measure. ## Value — PASS

This solves the exact endpoint stability and positivity-preservation problem for the full natural \(m\)-parameter family of mixed volume and vertex-quadrature measures on any simplex. It replaces general projection bounds with a computable sharp constant, determines the extremizing geometry, and exhibits a nonlocal strict amplification with a vanishing first variation under a single vertex mass. These are motivated invariants for weighted least-squares and local simplex mass matrices, not merely a single numerical instance.

## Scope and limitations

Only real affine projectors with a fixed positive continuous uniform-volume component and finite nonnegative vertex masses are treated. The formula does not claim which coordinate wins the finite maximization for arbitrary weights beyond the proved single-atom case. Bibliographic priority is subject to independent verification. Numerical and rational checks are supplementary.
