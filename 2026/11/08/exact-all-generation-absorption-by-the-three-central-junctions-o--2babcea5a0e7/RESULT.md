# Exact all-generation absorption by the three central junctions of a Sierpinski gasket

## Finding

For each integer \(g\ge1\), construct the level-\(g\) finite Sierpinski-gasket graph \(G_g\) by gluing three copies of \(G_{g-1}\) at their pairwise corner junctions, beginning with a triangle \(G_0\). Let \(C_g\) consist of the three junction vertices that separate the top-level copies. On the one-excitation Hilbert space \(\mathbb C^{V(G_g)}\), let \(L_g\) be the combinatorial graph Laplacian and let
\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=J L_g-i\sum_{v\in C_g}\gamma_v|v\rangle\langle v|,
\qquad J\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\},\quad\gamma_v>0.
\]
The dimension of the cyclic subspace generated by the three junction-site vectors is exactly
\[
B_g=\begin{cases}6,&g=1,\\13\,2^{g-2}+1,&g\ge2.\end{cases}
\]
Consequently, with \(N_g=(3^{g+1}+3)/2\) vertices, the nondecaying dark-space dimension and the eventual absorption probability of an initially maximally mixed excitation are exactly
\[
D_g=N_g-B_g,\qquad P_{\mathrm{capture}}(g)=\frac{B_g}{N_g}.
\]
In particular, \(P_{\mathrm{capture}}(g)\sim (13/6)(2/3)^g\). The formula is independent of the individual positive loss rates. Unlike absorption at the three *exterior* corners, the absorbing set here consists of the three internal top-level junctions.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(G_0\) is a three-cycle. At level \(g\), three disjoint scaled copies of \(G_{g-1}\) are joined by identifying each pair of adjacent inner corner vertices; they share no edges. The three newly identified vertices form \(C_g\). A convenient embedding places the outer corners at \((0,0),(2^g,0),(0,2^g)\), and the three junctions at \((2^{g-1},0),(0,2^{g-1}),(2^{g-1},2^{g-1})\). Exterior vertices have degree two; all other vertices degree four. The dynamics uses uniform Laplacian hopping with real nonzero scale \(J\); no dephasing, unequal edge weights, or additional onsite potentials are assumed. Absorption is the loss of norm under the finite non-Hermitian no-jump evolution. No assertion is made about absorption times, its rates, or pure-state capture independent of the initial state.

## Proof

**1. Absorption as a cyclic-rank problem.** Write \(P\) for the orthogonal projection onto the span of the three vectors \(|v\rangle\), \(v\in C_g\), and let \(\mathcal K_g=\operatorname{span}\{L_g^k\operatorname{ran}P:k\ge0\}\). The orthogonal complement of this invariant subspace is the direct sum, over Laplacian eigenvalues, of eigenspace vectors vanishing on all three junctions. On it, \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is Hermitian, hence has no norm loss. On \(\mathcal K_g\), any eigenvector with a real eigenvalue of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) would satisfy \(\sum_v\gamma_v|\langle v|\psi\rangle|^2=0\), hence vanish on all junctions, contradicting membership in \(\mathcal K_g\). All remaining eigenvalues have strictly negative imaginary parts, so their exponential contributions, including possible Jordan factors, decay. Thus the eventual surviving projector is the dark projector, and the maximally mixed capture fraction is \(\dim\mathcal K_g/N_g\).

**2. Boundary Schur map.** For a generic complex spectral parameter \(z\), eliminate all vertices other than the *exterior* three corners in \(L_h-zI\). By the permutation symmetry of the gasket the resulting Schur complement is \(S_h(z)=a_h(z)I+b_h(z)(\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T-I)\). Denote its eigenvalues on the one-dimensional fully symmetric and two-dimensional standard representations by \(s_h=a_h+2b_h\) and \(t_h=a_h-b_h\), respectively. Initially \(s_0=-z\) and \(t_0=3-z\).

For three glued copies, after interior elimination and before removing their three junctions, the exterior-to-junction block is \(b_h(\mathbf1\mathbf1^T-I)\). The exterior block is \(a_hI\); the junction block is \((2a_h+z)I+b_h(\mathbf1\mathbf1^T-I)\). The correction \(+z\) prevents counting the spectral shift twice at a shared vertex. Taking the next exterior Schur complement gives the rational identities
\[
 s_{h+1}=\frac{6s_ht_h+z(s_h+2t_h)}{4s_h+2t_h+3z},\qquad
 t_{h+1}=\frac{3s_ht_h+z(s_h+2t_h)+3t_h^2}{s_h+5t_h+3z}.
\]
All identities are identities in the rational-function field; apparent singularities are handled by reduction.

Define integer polynomials
\[
r_0(z)=z,\quad q_0(z)=3-z,\qquad
r_{j+1}=r_j(5-r_j),\quad q_{j+1}=(3-r_j)q_j-r_j.
\]
Substitution into the preceding Schur map, beginning at \(h=0\) (and checking the \(h=1\) symmetric case), proves by induction that
\[
t_h(z)=\frac{zq_h(z)}{r_h(z)}\quad(h\ge0),\qquad
s_h(z)=-\frac{z(q_{h-1}(z)+1)}{2-r_{h-1}(z)}\quad(h\ge1).
\]
For clarity, the first relation is read as a rational identity even at \(h=0\), when its apparent factor \(z\) cancels.

**3. Junction resolvent.** Eliminate the three exterior corners instead, leaving the three central junctions. Its symmetric and standard Schur eigenvalues are
\[
 m_s=z+\frac{6s_ht_h}{s_h+2t_h},\qquad
 m_t=z+\frac{3t_h(s_h+t_h)}{s_h+2t_h},\qquad h=g-1.
\]
The corresponding scalar junction-resolvent functions are \(R_s=1/m_s\) and \(R_t=1/m_t\). When \(g=1\), substitution of \(s_0=-z\), \(t_0=3-z\) yields
\[
R_s(z)=\frac{2-z}{z(z-4)},\qquad R_t(z)=\frac{2-z}{z^2-7z+9}.
\]
Each has two simple poles. For \(g\ge2\), abbreviate \(r=r_{g-2}(z)\), \(q=q_{g-2}(z)\) and define the polynomials
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A&=2q+r,\\
 P&=q(r-1)(r-4)+r(r-3),\\
 K&=q(r-3)+r-1,\\
 T&=q(r-3)(r^2-5r+3)+r(r-2)(r-4).
\end{aligned}
\]
Direct simplification of the Schur formulas gives
\[
\boxed{R_s(z)=\frac{P}{zAK},\qquad
R_t(z)=\frac{(r/z)(r-5)P}{AT}.}
\]
Here \(r/z\) is a polynomial, since \(r_0=z\) and each recursion preserves divisibility by \(z\).

**4. No cancellations, at every level.** Put \(j=g-2\) and \(d=2^j\). Both \(r_j\) and \(q_j\) are degree-\(d\) integer polynomials with leading coefficients opposite and equal to \(\pm1\); in particular every root of a polynomial \(r_j-c\) with integer \(c\) is an algebraic integer, as are the corresponding values of \(q_j\). We need the following three exclusion facts:

(a) If \(r_j(z)=0\), then \(q_j(z)\ne0\). Induct on \(j\): at the base \(z=0\), \(q_0=3\). A preimage of zero has previous \(r\)-value zero or five. In the latter case, \(q_{j}=-2q_{j-1}-5\) cannot vanish, since that would make the algebraic integer \(q_{j-1}\) equal to \(-5/2\).

(b) If \(r_j(z)=2\), then \(q_j(z)\ne-1\). At \(j=0\), \(q_0(2)=1\). For \(j\ge1\), put \(y=r_{j-1}(z)\). Then \(y^2-5y+2=0\), and \(q_j=-1\) would imply \(q_{j-1}=-y/2\). But \(y/2\) is not an algebraic integer: its quadratic field norm is \(1/2\).

(c) If \(r_j(z)=5\), then \(q_j(z)\ne-5/2\), since \(q_j(z)\) is an algebraic integer.

The exact polynomial identities
\[
\begin{aligned}
2P-(r-1)(r-4)A&=-r(r-2)(r-5),\\
(r-3)P-(r-1)(r-4)K&=4,\\
(r-3)(r^2-5r+3)P-(r-1)(r-4)T&=r(r-5)
\end{aligned}
\]
now prove that \(P\) has no common zero with \(A,K,T\): for \(A\), a common root would require \(r\in\{0,2,5\}\) and \(q\in\{0,-1,-5/2\}\), respectively, excluded by (a)–(c); for \(K\), the second identity is the nonzero constant four; for \(T\), the third identity reduces possible common roots to \(r=0\) or \(r=5\), again excluded by (a),(c). Furthermore, \(P(0)=4\cdot3^{j+1}\ne0\). Thus \(R_s\) is reduced.

For \(R_t\), any additional numerator zero is a zero of \(r/z\) or \(r-5\). The former implies \(r=0\) at a nonzero \(z\), where \(A=2q\ne0\) and \(T=-9q\ne0\). At \(r=5\), \(A=2q+5\ne0\) and \(T=3(2q+5)\ne0\). Hence \(R_t\) is also reduced. No possible cancellation has been inferred from finite spectral data.

**5. Pole count and rank.** From the leading coefficients above,
\[
\deg A=d,\quad\deg K=2d,\quad\deg T=4d,
\]
so the reduced denominators have degrees \(1+3d\) and \(5d\), respectively. Each scalar resolvent is a positive-weight spectral Stieltjes transform of the self-adjoint finite Laplacian, so all its actual poles are simple and real. Consequently the symmetric junction channel has exactly \(1+3d\) active spectral poles, and the standard channel exactly \(5d\). Each standard pole contributes rank two to the three-site spectral restriction, irrespective of whether the same eigenvalue also occurs in the symmetric channel. Therefore
\[
\dim\mathcal K_g=(1+3d)+2(5d)=1+13d=1+13\,2^{g-2}
\]
for \(g\ge2\). At \(g=1\), the two poles in each channel give \(2+2\cdot2=6\). This finishes the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library `verify.py` constructs each finite gasket directly from its edges, forms graph-Laplacian actions independently, and computes exact Krylov ranks over two distinct prime fields for levels \(1\le g\le5\). All ten tested ranks match the closed formulas; the values of \((N_g,B_g,D_g)\) are \((6,6,0)\), \((15,14,1)\), \((42,27,15)\), \((123,53,70)\), and \((366,105,261)\). Execute `python3 verify.py` in a folder containing the extracted file. These are finite-field checks, not proof for arbitrary \(g\); the all-generation proof is the rational-function and algebraic-integer argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Darázs, Anishchenko, Kiss, Blumen and Mülken (2014), Section V and Table IV, study the same Laplacian quantum walk on finite Sierpinski gaskets, and report numerical long-time survival fractions for traps at the *central* versus *outer* three sites. Their central-site dark-space numerators for sizes \(15,42,123,366,1095\) are respectively \(1,15,70,261,886\), in agreement with the present formula. The finite data and the physical trapping setup are prior work, not new claims. The contribution here is the exact all-level central-junction law, its closed asymptotic coefficient, and a proof without numerical non-Hermitian eigenvalue thresholds. Burgarth, D'Alessandro, Hogben, Severini and Young (2011) connect graph zero forcing with controllability; their general sufficiency criterion is not a central-junction rank formula. General spectral-decimation methods for gasket Laplacians are relevant background, but the specific observable-channel degree calculation and noncancellation proof are essential here. Earlier work on exterior-corner capture proves a different absorber support and does not by itself determine the rank for the interior junction triplet.

## Limitations

The dynamics assumes the finite unweighted gasket, the combinatorial Laplacian, uniform real hopping scale and strictly positive loss at all three central junctions. The theorem is an exact infinite-family rank law, not a result on finite-time absorption speed, arbitrary initial-state capture, edge disorder, dephasing, other trap locations, or an infinite-volume spectral limit. Numerical finite-level values were already published; the historical originality of the all-level central formula is supported by inspected sources but is not independently certified. Earlier specialist spectral-projector identities might offer an alternative derivation.
