# Exact alternating-knowledge monoids on finite two-agent epistemic frames

## Finding

Let \(W\) be a nonempty finite set of \(N\) worlds, and let \(E,F\) be any two equivalence relations on \(W\). Consider the Boolean event operators \(K_R(X)=\{u\in W:\forall v\,(uRv\Rightarrow v\in X)\}\) for \(R=E,F\). Their composition monoid \(\mathcal M(E,F)\) includes the identity (the empty word). We determine its cardinality from the geometry of the two partitions, and the sharp maximum for every \(N\).

Form the bipartite incidence multigraph \(G\): its left vertices are the \(E\)-classes, its right vertices the \(F\)-classes, and each world \(u\) is one edge joining its two classes. For \(A,B\in\{L,R\}\), let \(d_{AB}\) be the maximum, over pairs of vertices \(s,t\) of types \(A,B\) lying in one connected component, of the ordinary shortest-path distance \(d_G(s,t)\). In particular, \(d_{LL},d_{RR}\) are even (possibly zero) and \(d_{LR}\) is odd and at least one. Set

\[
 m_E=1+\min(d_{LL},d_{LR}),\qquad m_F=1+\min(d_{RR},d_{LR}).
\]

Then the **exact number of different operations** generated by the two knowledge operators is

\[
 |\mathcal M(E,F)|=m_E+m_F-1+\mathbf 1_{\{E\ne \Delta_W\ \land\ F\ne\Delta_W\}},
\]

where \(\Delta_W\) is equality. Every nonempty word beginning with \(K_E\), respectively \(K_F\), reaches the common-knowledge operator over the join \(E\vee F\) at precisely length \(m_E\), respectively \(m_F\); all earlier such alternating words are different. The two chains share only that final operation.

The **sharp maximum** over all ordered pairs \((E,F)\) on an \(N\)-element carrier is

\[
 \max_{E,F}|\mathcal M(E,F)|=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&N=1,\\
 2,&N=2,\\
 2N,&N\ge3\text{ odd},\\
 2N-1,&N\ge4\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]

For every \(N\ge3\), it is attained when the incidence graph is the simple path with exactly \(N\) edges: the two agents' classes are successive overlapping pairs of worlds, with singleton endpoint classes.

## Assumptions and scope

No independence, regularity, connectedness, or restriction on class sizes is assumed. Repeated intersections of the same two classes are represented by parallel edges, one per world; shortest-path distance is taken between class vertices, not worlds. When computing \(d_{AB}\), only vertex pairs in the same connected component are eligible. Distinct event operations mean differing on at least one subset \(X\subseteq W\), not merely on a fixed valuation. The result concerns **pure compositions** of the two necessity operators: it does not include disjunction, negation, arbitrary modal formulas, or distributed-knowledge primitives.

## Proof

**1. Faithful relational representation.** For any binary relations \(R,S\subseteq W^2\), \(K_R\circ K_S=K_{R\circ S}\), where \(u(R\circ S)v\) means \(uRx\) and \(xSv\) for some \(x\). The representation is injective: if \((u,v)\in R\setminus S\), evaluate both operators on \(W\setminus\{v\}\); then \(u\notin K_R(W\setminus\{v\})\) and \(u\in K_S(W\setminus\{v\})\). Consequently we may count the products of the relations \(E,F\).

**2. Distance normal form.** Idempotence \(E^2=E\), \(F^2=F\) removes consecutive repeated letters. For an alternating word \(T_{E,\ell}\) of length \(\ell\ge1\) beginning with \(E\), let \(L(u)\) and \(R(u)\) denote the endpoint class vertices of world-edge \(u\). If \(\ell=2t+1\), then

\[
 u\,T_{E,2t+1}\,v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 L(u),L(v)\text{ lie in one component and }d_G(L(u),L(v))\le2t.
\]

If \(\ell=2t\ge2\), then

\[
 u\,T_{E,2t}\,v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 L(u),R(v)\text{ lie in one component and }d_G(L(u),R(v))\le2t-1.
\]

The analogous statements for \(T_{F,\ell}\) exchange \(L\) and \(R\). To prove the equivalences, choose the successive intermediate world-edges in a relational product; successive shared classes trace a bipartite walk of the indicated length. Conversely, each bipartite walk lifts by selecting its traversed world-edges to a relational-product witness. A shorter path of the correct endpoint parity can be padded by immediate backtracking along an edge; reflexivity of each class relation permits the corresponding repetitions of an intermediate world. These arguments work equally with parallel edges and disconnected graphs.

**3. Two strict chains.** Write \(C=E\vee F\), the equivalence relation whose classes are the connected components of \(G\) viewed as sets of world-edges. All alternating products are reflexive and contained in \(C\). By the distance forms, the first odd \(E\)-leading word equal to \(C\) has length \(d_{LL}+1\), and the first even one has length \(d_{LR}+1\). Thus its first equality with \(C\) occurs at \(m_E\). The same argument gives \(m_F\).

Every alternating word \(T_{E,\ell}\) is contained in \(T_{E,\ell+1}\), because its newly appended letter is reflexive. If these two consecutive relations are equal, the shorter word is invariant under right multiplication by the newly appended letter, and also by its already-final letter (idempotence). Hence each of its rows is closed under both \(E\) and \(F\). Reflexivity and containment in \(C\) then force every row to be its full \(C\)-class, so the relation is already \(C\). Therefore there are exactly \(m_E\) distinct nonempty \(E\)-leading operations and \(m_F\) nonempty \(F\)-leading ones.

If a word beginning with \(E\) equals one beginning with \(F\), their common relation is invariant under left multiplication by both generators (each begins with an idempotent letter). Its rows are therefore identical for worlds linked by \(E\) or \(F\), and hence for all worlds in the same \(C\)-class. Reflexivity and containment in \(C\) force that relation to equal \(C\). The two chains consequently intersect only at \(C\). Finally, identity is already among these words precisely when \(E\) or \(F\) equals \(\Delta_W\); otherwise it adds one new operation. This gives the exact cardinality formula.

**4. Sharp maximum.** Put \(e=|W|=N\); each connected component of the incidence graph with \(e_j\) world-edges has at most \(e_j+1\) vertices. With \(h=\max_j e_j\), every finite graph distance is at most \(h\). Since \(d_{LL},d_{RR}\) are even and \(d_{LR}\) odd, each minimum of an even and an odd distance is at most \(h-1\). Thus \(m_E,m_F\le h\le N\). If the graph is disconnected, \(h\le N-1\), and the number of operators is at most \(2N-2\).

For connected \(G\) and even \(N\ge4\), suppose both \(m_E=m_F=N\). We would have \(d_{LL},d_{RR}\ge N-1\), hence both at least \(N\) by even parity. They would both equal \(N\). But a connected graph with \(N\) edges admitting a shortest path of length \(N\) must itself be that spanning path: it has \(N+1\) vertices and uses every edge. A path of even length \(N\) has its two endpoints on **one** side of a bipartition, so it cannot have both a left-to-left and a right-to-right distance of \(N\). Thus one of \(m_E,m_F\) is at most \(N-1\), yielding \(2N-1\). For odd \(N\), the general bound is \(2N\).

A simple path of \(N\) edges attains the bounds: when \(N\) is odd, \(d_{LL}=d_{RR}=N-1\) and \(d_{LR}=N\), so \(m_E=m_F=N\); when \(N\) is even, with endpoint side \(L\), \(d_{LL}=N\), \(d_{RR}=N-2\), \(d_{LR}=N-1\), giving \((m_E,m_F)=(N,N-1)\). For \(N\ge3\), both equivalence relations are nonidentity, so the cardinality is their sum. For \(N=1\), both are equality; for \(N=2\), each equivalence relation is either equality or universal, and the monoid contains at most those two operations. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone `checker.py` constructs every set partition of \(N\) labelled worlds for each \(1\le N\le6\), forms every ordered pair of partitions, and computes the relational-product closure by iterated Boolean-matrix composition. Separately, it builds the bipartite incidence graph, obtains its side-specific shortest-path diameters by breadth-first search, and evaluates the formula. The two methods agree for all \(44{,}168\) ordered pairs. The observed sharp maxima for \(N=1,2,3,4,5,6\) are respectively \(1,2,6,7,10,11\). Run `python3 checker.py` in a directory containing that standalone file. Exhaustive verification to six worlds is corroboration, not a substitute for the all-size proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The multi-agent \(\mathsf{S5}\) semantics and the importance of alternating agent modalities are explicit in Bílková, Fussner and Kuznets (2025). Their paper concerns proof calculi, interpolation, and the finite-model property, rather than counting a fixed finite frame's pure operator-composition monoid. Schulman (2011) studies a different bipartite quantifier semigroup: alternating existential and universal *neighbor maps between the vertex sides*; here both generators are equivalence-class universal quantifiers *on the edge-world set*. The two constructions have distinct carriers, generators, and claims. Graph distance, equivalence joins, and the idempotence of knowledge operators are standard tools; the proposed synthesis is the exact side-distance formula and sharp world-count extremum.

## Limitations

The result does not classify nested modal formulas containing Boolean connectives, possible-world restrictions with non-equivalence agent relations, three or more independently generated agent modalities, or infinite frames. An older algebraic presentation of the same two-idempotent relational monoid may exist under non-epistemic terminology; searches cannot establish absolute historical priority. All proofs and checks here concern the precise finite two-equivalence setting only. Independent validation has not been performed.
