# Scientific review of the two-meridian harmonic sampling theorem

## Correctness

PASS. The associated Legendre basis is complete for the pure degree eigenspace, the meridional trace is an exact parity-restricted Fourier space, and the sampled nodes form an invertible discrete Fourier system. The exact evaluation matrix is block triangular, producing the sine-product determinant and kernel. The rational denominator count and the linear singular-value crossing follow from elementary number theory and invertible block reductions. The finite numerical checker is corroboration and is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS under the available primary-literature and mathematical-record comparisons. The closest explicit spherical polynomial interpolation construction in zu Castell–Laín Fernández–Xu (arXiv:math/0407448, Sections 1–3) uses multiple latitudes and all degrees up to a cutoff, not two intersecting meridians and one pure harmonic degree. The full-text 2024 nonuniform spherical harmonic transform paper addresses transform evaluation and meridian doubling, not an exact angular determinant or rational-angle nullity. The 2021 spherical Slepian work concerns spatial-spectral concentration and provides the focal scientific setting. Previously studied low-degree equatorial restrictions agree with, but do not imply, the all-degree product determinant. Residual risk: an unlocated classical nodal-set/dihedral-reflection or finite-group representation formula may already imply the result; available searches cannot prove priority.

## Mathematical value

PASS. An explicit, cardinal-minimal spatial sampling rule for a natural family of spherical Laplace eigenspaces either succeeds exactly or loses a precisely determined number of degrees of freedom at rational angles. The sine-product factorization gives the full arithmetic singularity locus, its multiplicities and local linear degeneration, not merely generic interpolation or isolated examples. The theorem is specific to pure-degree harmonic signals and does not claim uniform stability or mixed-bandwidth recovery.
