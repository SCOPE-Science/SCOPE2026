# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The proof uses the necessary exactly-half-ones condition for an antisquare, the base-divisibility spacing law, a strict length obstruction for all cases except base two at orders at most three and base three at order two, and an independent parity/valuation treatment for those cases. For base two the alternating-window condition is both necessary and sufficient once all odd positions are ones. For base three, the two endpoint valuations are independent of the intervening forced zeros. The exact counts follow by a disjoint valuation-class census; floor-series limits establish the densities with vanishing boundary errors. The standalone finite verifier, extracted and executed from this same package, passed the stated base and cutoff ranges. The finite test does not establish an all-length theorem by itself.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)
The complete manuscript of Baranwal et al. [1] describes universal minimal antisquares and their avoidance thresholds, not these valuation-word occurrence-count formulas. Currie [2] analyzes the exact ordinary period-doubling word and its forbidden patterns; that earlier work substantially covers the base-two support, which is expressly credited here. The new composite assertion is exact all-base support together with complete arithmetic starting-position tests, finite-prefix formulas and densities. The inspected primary works have different targets; the searched published mathematical record had nearby work on antipalindromic attractors and period-doubling string attractors but no implication of these occurrence formulas. Unindexed work or elementary independently known formulas remain risks; the search does not prove universal novelty.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The question is the natural exact antisquare factor-frequency problem for a canonical family of automatic words, not an arbitrary restricted sample. The answer gives a complete classification at every base and all lengths, a sharp base-two/base-three/base-four transition, and exact arithmetic counts at every finite cutoff. Position and density laws provide useful quantitative information beyond the already-known forbidden-factor conditions. The proof remains elementary; no broader automatic-sequence theory is claimed.
