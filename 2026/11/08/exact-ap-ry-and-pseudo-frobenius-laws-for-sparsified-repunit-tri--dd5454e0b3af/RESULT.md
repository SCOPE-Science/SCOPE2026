# Exact Apéry and pseudo-Frobenius laws for sparsified repunit triples

## Finding
For every integer base \(b\geq2\) and every odd integer \(n\geq3\), let
\[
R_j=\frac{b^j-1}{b-1},\qquad
S_{b,n}=\langle R_n,R_{n+2},R_{n+6}\rangle.
\]
Put
\[
a=R_n,\quad u=b^n(b+1),\quad m=b^4+b^2+1,
\quad a=km+r\quad(0<r<m),
\]
and define the explicit coin cost and weight, for \(t\geq0\), by
\[
c(t)=\left\lfloor\frac{t}{m}\right\rfloor+(t\bmod m),
\qquad W(t)=ut+ac(t).
\]
Then the complete Apéry set of \(S_{b,n}\) with respect to \(a\) is
\[
\operatorname{Ap}(S_{b,n};a)=\{W(t):0\leq t<a\}.
\]
The residue \(r\) is determined by \(n\bmod6\):
\[
r=\begin{cases}
1,&n\equiv1\pmod6,\\
b^2+b+1,&n\equiv3\pmod6,\\
b^3+b,&n\equiv5\pmod6.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the pseudo-Frobenius set is exactly
\[
\operatorname{PF}(S_{b,n})=
\begin{cases}
\{W(a-1)-a\},&n=3,\\
\{W(a-1)-a,\ W(km-1)-a\},&n\geq5\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Its Frobenius number and genus are the explicit quantities
\[
F(S_{b,n})=W(t_*)-a,\qquad
t_*=\begin{cases}
a-2,&n\equiv1\pmod6,\\
a-1,&n\equiv3,5\pmod6,
\end{cases}
\]
\[
g(S_{b,n})=\frac{(u-1)(a-1)+km(k+m-2)+2kr+r(r-1)}2.
\]
The semigroup is symmetric if and only if \(n=3\). It has embedding dimension two and type one for \(n=3\), and embedding dimension three and type two for every odd \(n\geq5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The base is an arbitrary integer \(b\geq2\); the index is an arbitrary odd integer \(n\geq3\). The notation \(\langle x,y,z\rangle\) means the additive submonoid of nonnegative integers generated by \(x,y,z\). Its genus counts nonnegative gaps, its Frobenius number is its largest gap, and its pseudo-Frobenius numbers are the gaps \(f\) for which \(f+s\) belongs to the semigroup for every nonzero semigroup element \(s\). Type is the number of pseudo-Frobenius numbers. Symmetry is equivalently the condition \(2g=F+1\).

When \(n\) is even, the three repunits share the nontrivial factor \(b+1\), so the triple is not a numerical semigroup; no statement about its Frobenius number is made. For the admitted odd indices, \(a\equiv1\pmod{b+1}\), whence \(\gcd(a,u)=1\). Therefore \(S_{b,n}\) is a numerical semigroup.

## Proof
**Step 1: A two-denomination parametrization.** Direct summation gives
\[
R_{n+2}=a+u,\qquad R_{n+6}=a+mu.
\]
Every element can be written as \(za+x(a+u)+y(a+mu)\), where \(x,y,z\geq0\) are integers. Its residue modulo \(a\) equals that of \(u(x+my)\). For any fixed \(t=x+my\), the least possible value of \(x+y\) is
\[
c(t)=\lfloor t/m\rfloor+(t\bmod m).
\]
Indeed replacing \(m\) copies of the unit denomination by one \(m\)-denomination strictly reduces the number of coins, and leaves the same \(t\); the greedy quotient/remainder representation therefore attains the minimum. Hence the least element associated with coefficient \(t\) is \(W(t)=ut+ac(t)\).

**Step 2: Reduction to the first \(a\) coefficients.** For integers \(t\geq0\), \(h\geq1\), the identity
\[
c(t+ha)-c(t)=ha-(m-1)\left(\left\lfloor\frac{t+ha}{m}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{t}{m}\right\rfloor\right)
\]
implies
\[
c(t+ha)-c(t)>-(m-1).
\]
The bound follows because the difference of the two floors is at most \(ha/m+1\). Moreover
\[
u=b^n(b+1)\geq b^4+b^3>b^4+b^2+1=m.
\]
Thus
\[
W(t+ha)-W(t)=a\bigl(hu+c(t+ha)-c(t)\bigr)>0,
\]
and, because the expression in parentheses is an integer, the right-hand side is at least \(a\). Since \(u\) is invertible modulo \(a\), each residue class modulo \(a\) is attained by precisely one \(t\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}\); further coefficients in that residue are more costly. This proves the stated complete Apéry set. We will also use the stronger wrap consequence
\[
W(t+ha)-a\in S_{b,n}\quad(h\geq1),
\]
where the \(t\) on the right is any nonnegative coefficient (reduce it first into \([0,a-1]\) if needed).

**Step 3: The two maximal Apéry positions.** For a numerical semigroup, \(W(t)-a\) is pseudo-Frobenius exactly when \(W(t)\) is maximal in the Apéry set under the semigroup order. This follows directly from the definition by adding each generator and checking membership after subtraction of \(a\). Here
\[
c(t+m)=c(t)+1,
\]
and for \(t\not\equiv m-1\pmod m\), also \(c(t+1)=c(t)+1\). Consequently, if \(t<a-1\) is not a block endpoint, then
\[
W(t)+(a+u)=W(t+1),
\]
so \(W(t)\) is not maximal. If \(t\equiv m-1\pmod m\) and \(t+m<a\), then
\[
W(t)+(a+mu)=W(t+m),
\]
so it is not maximal either. Since \(r>0\), only \(t=a-1\) and (when \(k\geq1\)) \(t=km-1\) remain.

Both remaining positions are maximal. Adding \(a+mu\) to either gives \(W(t+m)\), with \(t+m\geq a\); Step 2 makes \(W(t+m)-a\) a semigroup element, so the sum lies outside the Apéry set. Adding \(a+u\) to \(W(a-1)\) gives \(a(c(a-1)+1+u)\), whose subtraction of \(a\) is a nonnegative multiple of \(a\). Finally, if \(k\geq1\), at \(t=km-1\) the coin-cost drop gives
\[
W(km-1)+(a+u)=W(km)+a(m-1),
\]
whose subtraction of \(a\) belongs to the semigroup. Thus these and only these positions are maximal, proving the pseudo-Frobenius formula. The criterion using only the three generators is sufficient: once an added generator leaves the Apéry set, adding further semigroup elements cannot bring it back.

**Step 4: Residues and the Frobenius maximum.** Because
\[
R_6=(b+1)m,\qquad b^6\equiv1\pmod m,
\]
the remainder of \(R_n\) modulo \(m\) equals that of \(R_1\), \(R_3\), or \(R_5\), according to \(n\bmod6\). The displayed three values follow. They are strictly between \(0\) and \(m\). For \(n=3\), \(a=R_3<m\), so \(k=0\); for every odd \(n\geq5\), \(a>m\), so \(k\geq1\).

On each block \([jm,(j+1)m-1]\), the function \(W(t)\) is strictly increasing, and successive full-block endpoint values increase by \(um+a>0\). For \(k\geq1\), the maximum among \(0\leq t<a\) is therefore attained at \(a-1\) or \(km-1\). Their difference is
\[
W(a-1)-W(km-1)=ru-a(m-r-1).
\]
Now
\[
b^2-1<\frac{u}{a}=\frac{(b^2-1)b^n}{b^n-1}<b^2.
\]
For \(r=1\), the difference is negative because \(m-2>b^2\). For \(r=b^2+b+1\) or \(r=b^3+b\), it is positive: in both cases \(rb^2-m+1>0\), explicitly \(b^3\) and \(b^2(b-1)(b^2+1)\), respectively. The formula for \(F\) follows, since the maximum Apéry value minus \(a\) is the Frobenius number. When \(n=3\), the single candidate is \(a-1\), as stated.

**Step 5: Genus, embedding dimension, and symmetry.** The Apéry sum identity gives
\[
g=\frac1a\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}W(t)-\frac{a-1}{2}
=\frac{(u-1)(a-1)}2+\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}c(t).
\]
Summing the \(k\) full blocks and the final block of length \(r\) gives
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}c(t)=\frac{km(k+m-2)}2+kr+\frac{r(r-1)}2,
\]
which proves the claimed genus formula.

When \(n=3\), \(R_{n+6}=R_9\) is a multiple of \(R_3\), so the third generator is redundant. The first two generators are coprime, and the Apéry-maximal set above has one element; thus the semigroup is symmetric with type one. For odd \(n\geq5\), \(m<a\) and the third generator is itself the Apéry representative \(W(m)=a+mu\). It cannot be expressed using \(a\) and \(a+u\): such an expression would require at least \(m\) copies of \(a+u\) (because its coefficient of \(u\) is congruent to \(m\pmod a\)), giving value at least \(m(a+u)>a+mu\). Hence the generating set is minimal of size three. Its two distinct pseudo-Frobenius numbers prove type two and nonsymmetry. Alternatively, direct substitution in \(2g-F-1\) yields a strictly positive quantity: it is \(u+k-m+2\) if \(n\equiv1\pmod6\), and \(k(m-1)(m-r)\) if \(n\equiv3,5\pmod6\) and \(n\geq5\).

## Verification
The standalone `checker.py` computes repunits, the formulas and all predicted pseudo-Frobenius numbers using integers only. It independently builds the Apéry set by Dijkstra shortest paths on residues modulo \(a\) using the actual three generators. It checks the Apéry multiset, Frobenius number, genus, pseudo-Frobenius condition (via generator additions), and symmetry for bases \(2,3,4,5\) and odd indices through \(15,11,9,7\), respectively. The last line of `checker_output.txt` is `ALL CHECKS PASSED`.

For example, \(S_{2,5}=\langle31,127,2047\rangle\) has \(F=3159\), \(g=1690\), and pseudo-Frobenius set \(\{2509,3159\}\); \(S_{2,7}=\langle127,511,8191\rangle\) has \(F=51048\), \(g=25710\), and pseudo-Frobenius set \(\{49019,51048\}\). The proof of the infinite statement is independent of these finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work
Branco, Colaço and Ojeda (first public preprint 2021-12-02) determine Apéry sets and invariants for **fully generated** generalized repunit semigroups. That family contains every intermediate offset generator and is distinct from the sparse triple here: in the current family the intermediate \(R_{n+4}\) is absent, and its absence changes the Apéry coin system.

Liu, Xin, Ye and Yin (first public preprint 2023-06-06; primary MSC 11D07) develop a general Apéry minimization model and solve the Frobenius problem for **complete** generalized Mersenne/repunit-type generating lists. Their Lemma 2.4 supplies a conceptual minimization starting point, but the printed exact results do not give the present sparse triple's closed Apéry, pseudo-Frobenius, Frobenius, genus, or symmetry laws. Specializing their general model to a list with two nontrivial generators leaves a minimization, resolved explicitly here together with the nontrivial residue-class and maximal-element analysis.

Moscariello (first public 2016-08-22) gives generic finite-minimization formulas for three-generated numerical semigroups; these provide an algorithm, not the explicit base/index-dependent classification obtained here. Selmer's Apéry genus formula and the Apéry characterization of pseudo-Frobenius numbers are standard antecedents used in the proof.

## Limitations
The claim is for odd indices \(n\geq3\) and integer bases \(b\geq2\). It does not determine other sparsification patterns or general minimal presentations. Closed formulas are given in terms of an explicitly determined quotient/remainder, not expanded polynomials in \(b\) and \(n\). Search-based originality remains provisional; an unindexed treatment or a differently parameterized stronger formula may exist. No independent expert validation has been performed.
