# Exact arithmetic-progression spectrum of modular hyperbolas over residue rings

## Finding
For every integer \(N\ge2\) and any invertible residue \(a\in R^\times\), where \(R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), consider the modular hyperbola
\[
H_{a,N}=\{(x,ax^{-1}):x\in R^\times\}\subseteq R^2.
\]
An ordered affine arithmetic progression is a sequence \(P_j=P_0+j\Delta\) in \(R^2\). A three-term progression may have repeated vertices; it is *distinct* if all three vertices differ. Two cyclic progressions are called the same unrooted oriented cycle if they differ only by shifting the initial index; reversing the step counts as a different orientation unless the two indexed progressions already coincide.

For every factor \(p^k\parallel N\) define
\[
t(p,k)=\begin{cases}
\lceil k/2\rceil,&p\text{ odd},\\
\max\{1,\lfloor k/2\rfloor\},&p=2,
\end{cases}\qquad
M=\prod_{p^k\parallel N}p^{t(p,k)},\qquad Q=N/M.
\]
The following statements all hold.

**Exact steps and automatic extension.** Three consecutive graph vertices, whose first coordinates are \(x,x+h,x+2h\), form an affine progression if and only if \(h\in MR\). In that case, *all* vertices \((x+jh,a(x+jh)^{-1})\), for every integer \(j\), lie on the same affine progression, with exactly \(d=N/\gcd(N,h)\) distinct vertices.

**Canonical cycle decomposition.** For each unit class \(c\pmod M\), the graph points with \(x\equiv c\pmod M\) constitute one affine cycle of length \(Q\), generated by the first-coordinate step \(M\). These \(\varphi(N)/Q\) cycles partition the whole graph. Every progression with three consecutive graph points remains inside one of these cycles. Thus \(Q\) is the largest possible number of distinct vertices in such a cyclic progression, including the cases \(Q=1,2\).

**Complete length distribution.** For every divisor \(d\mid Q\), the number of rooted oriented cyclic progressions of exact length \(d\) is
\[
R_N(d)=\varphi(N)\varphi(d),
\]
and their number modulo cyclic rotations of the initial vertex is
\[
C_N(d)=\frac{\varphi(N)\varphi(d)}{d}.
\]
No other lengths occur. The number of ordered three-term progressions with three *distinct* vertices is exactly
\[
A_N^{\mathrm{distinct}}=\varphi(N)\bigl(Q-1-\mathbf1_{2\mid Q}\bigr).
\]
In particular, distinct three-term progressions exist if and only if \(Q\ge3\).

For instance \(N=36\) has \(M=6\), \(Q=6\) and \(\varphi(36)=12\). Its inverse graph is the disjoint union of two affine cycles of six vertices; it admits precisely \(48\) ordered three-term progressions with distinct vertices.

## Assumptions and scope
All products are over prime-power factors of \(N\). The unit \(a\) may vary arbitrarily: multiplication of the second coordinate by \(a\) is an invertible additive transformation of \(R^2\), so every result is independent of \(a\). The domain is all residue rings \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) with \(N\ge2\), including even and nonsquarefree moduli. The modulus-one case is omitted. Counts are of labeled points or rooted/oriented cycles as defined above; they are not counts of collinear Euclidean representative points.

## Proof
**1. Three-point identity.** Write \(f(x)=ax^{-1}\) on units. Whenever \(x,x+h,x+2h\) are units, ordinary fraction identities in the commutative ring \(R\) give
\[
f(x)-2f(x+h)+f(x+2h)
=\frac{2ah^2}{x(x+h)(x+2h)}.
\]
The denominator and \(a\) are units, so these three graph points form an affine progression precisely when \(2h^2=0\) in \(R\), equivalently \(N\mid2h^2\). This identity is exact; it does not assume that \(2\) is invertible.

**2. Every allowable step lies in an explicit ideal.** At an odd factor \(p^k\parallel N\), the last divisibility condition is equivalent to \(v_p(h)\ge\lceil k/2\rceil\). At \(2^k\parallel N\) with \(k\ge2\), it gives \(v_2(h)\ge\lceil(k-1)/2\rceil=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\). When \(k=1\), divisibility by \(2\) alone imposes no restriction, but the requirement that both \(x\) and \(x+h\) are units forces \(h\) to be even. For every \(k\ge1\) we therefore have \(v_2(h)\ge\max\{1,\lfloor k/2\rfloor\}\). By the Chinese remainder theorem the complete allowed step set is exactly the additive subgroup \(T=MR\subseteq R\).

Conversely, every \(h\in T\) is divisible by each prime dividing \(N\), so if \(x\in R^\times\), then \(x+jh\) is a unit for every integer \(j\). Furthermore \(N\mid2h^2\). Consequently the fraction identity from step 1 applies to every consecutive triple \(j,j+1,j+2\). The second difference of the sequence \(f(x+jh)\) vanishes for every integer \(j\), whence induction in both directions gives
\[
f(x+jh)=f(x)+j\bigl(f(x+h)-f(x)\bigr).
\]
Thus the entire graph sequence is affine, including for negative indices.

**3. Maximal canonical cycles.** Since \(T=MR\) is cyclic of order \(Q=N/M\), every coset \(x+T\) has exactly \(Q\) elements. Because \(M\) contains every prime dividing \(N\), these cosets are either entirely units or entirely nonunits. The unit cosets are in bijection with \((\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times\); their total number is \(\varphi(N)/Q\) (equivalently, \(\varphi(M)\)), and each graph fiber over a unit coset is an affine cycle by step 2 with step \(M\) and length \(Q\). Every allowed three-term step lies in \(T\), so no such progression can move between different fibers. This proves the asserted partition and maximality within progressions containing at least three consecutive graph vertices.

**4. Exact length and multiplicity.** An additive step \(h\in T\) has cyclic order \(d=N/\gcd(N,h)\) in \(R\), with \(d\mid Q\). A cyclic group of order \(Q\) contains exactly \(\varphi(d)\) elements of order \(d\) for each divisor \(d\mid Q\); there are no others. Every admissible start \(x\in R^\times\) gives one rooted oriented cycle, and the first-coordinate projection makes different pairs \((x,h)\) different as rooted progressions. This yields \(\varphi(N)\varphi(d)\) rooted cycles. Exactly \(d\) shifts of the root represent each oriented cycle, yielding \(C_N(d)=\varphi(N)\varphi(d)/d\). Finally, the vertices at indices \(0,1,2\) are all distinct exactly when the step has order at least three. Subtracting the unique order-one step and, if \(2\mid Q\), the unique order-two step, gives the stated count. Since every divisor of \(Q\) occurs, a distinct triple exists precisely for \(Q\ge3\). This completes the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify_progressions.py` uses only Python's standard library. Run `python verify_progressions.py`. It enumerates every start and candidate step modulo each \(N\in\{2,3,\ldots,160\}\), tests the inverse-coordinate three-term condition directly, checks full affine extension around the entire step orbit, and compares its exact length distribution with the formula. This is a finite regression check rather than the proof of the infinite statement; the proof is the ring identity and prime-adic/CRT argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Shparlinski's survey *Modular Hyperbolas* (first deposited March 15, 2011; arXiv:1103.2879) studies distribution and several geometric and arithmetic properties of the same hyperbolas, but the surveyed material inspected does not give the all-modulus affine-progression step/cycle spectrum. Khan, Magner, Senger and Winterhof, *Two Combinatorial Geometric Problems Involving Modular Hyperbolas* (deposited April 25, 2013; arXiv:1304.6943), prove bounds for ordinary Euclidean lines through integer representatives and exhibit long collinear fibers for prime powers. Their Euclidean collinearity and incidence statements do not by themselves state the modular progression counts or the canonical cyclic partition, especially at dyadic and mixed moduli. The earlier result on primitive affine-line incidence over odd prime powers likewise counts line intersections rather than rooted affine cycles or their exact length spectrum. An equivalent implicit formulation in this wider incidence literature remains possible.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns affine progressions in the additive group of the finite ring squared, not Euclidean arithmetic progressions of chosen integer representatives. It does not classify arbitrary collinear point triples that are not equally spaced, nor does it address nonunit levels of \(xy\equiv a\pmod N\). Historical priority remains conditional on the inspected sources; no claim of independent validation or exhaustive bibliographic coverage is made.
