# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact criterion \(N\mid6h^3\) and \(N\mid6h^2x\) is obtained from the first two successive second differences of a cubic polynomial and is necessary and sufficient over rings even when \(2\) or \(3\) is not invertible. The local valuation threshold gives a sharp upper bound on the additive order of the step; choosing \(x=0\) attains it at all prime powers simultaneously by the Chinese remainder theorem. Counting nonzero steps by truncated valuation, including the zero-step contribution, yields the stated closed local formulas. The unusual factors at primes two and three follow from an exact two-digit lifting identity. Independent exact-integer tests cover moduli through 90 and local formulas at six primes through exponent eight. The proof is unconditional for every positive modulus at least two.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The full inspected text of Cook and Magyar (2010) concerns polynomial restrictions on the common difference over finite fields, not progressions *on* the cubic graph of a general residue ring. The complete nine-page text of Vinh (2017) concerns three-term progressions in product sets, with no statement of a four-term cubic-graph classification. The Covert--Iosevich--Pakianathan work is linked through finite prime-power ring geometry, but its accessible abstract concerns distance and dot-product configurations. Searches for cubic polynomial graph progressions modulo prime powers, exact counts and maximal lengths located no covering statement. Related known cubic-graph additive-energy and spectrum results address different invariants and do not imply these pointwise progression criteria. Lack of an identified published statement is not a uniqueness proof; elementary-method rediscovery remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
There are natural geometric reasons to ask both how long a genuine arithmetic progression can lie on a polynomial graph and how many such configurations exist. The exact maximum \(L_N\) exhibits a nontrivial prime-field versus prime-power transition, including exceptional behavior at primes two and three, and the product formula classifies all composite moduli without ad hoc casework. The local counting formula records singular nilpotent layers rather than merely detecting nonexistence. These sharp, complete invariants can be used in modular additive-combinatorial configuration questions; no unsupported general theorem is claimed.
