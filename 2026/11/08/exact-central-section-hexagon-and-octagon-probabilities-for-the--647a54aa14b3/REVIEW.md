The claim specifies the exact polyhedron, Haar normal law, and exceptional zero-measure great circles. The twelve facet inequalities determine fourteen vertices, twenty-four axial-to-cube edges, and signed-permutation symmetry; exhaustive exact triple-of-facets intersections independently confirm no other vertices exist. Counting crossings for the two possible signs of the largest-coordinate subtraction gives six or eight vertices. The normal sphere decomposes into eight equilateral spherical triangles, each of area \(3\arccos(1/3)-\pi\), plus twenty-four corner triangles; spherical excess proves the exact probability law. The embedded checker independently reconstructs the facets and verifies ninety-six exact rational sign cases and 240,000 sample directions. This is a genuine all-orientation proof, not extrapolation from simulations.

The full article and the figure were read. Publicly indexed central-section laws for the regular icosahedron and rectangular boxes concern different solids and do not imply this law without an additional object-specific combinatorial classification. Search on the exact probability expressions, normals criterion, and rhombic-dodecahedron central section terminology found no directly covering source. Searches alone are not proof of priority. Historical priority therefore remains provisional.

## Value — PASS

The rhombic dodecahedron is the natural Wigner–Seitz cell of a face-centered-cubic lattice. The exact stereological frequency of hexagonal versus octagonal center sections gives a complete, nontrivial geometric observable for this physically familiar shape, with a simple necessary-and-sufficient normal-coordinate criterion. The value is in the complete exact law for a natural object; it is not a claim that the elementary triangle-inequality or spherical-excess techniques themselves are new. The result does not require a broad new theory to be useful, and is not an arbitrary chosen parameter.
