# Scientific review: central sections of a right equilateral triangular prism

## Correctness — PASS
The result concerns a prism with positive equilateral-base circumradius and height, and a uniformly oriented central plane. Generic-plane transversality is established by excluding the finite union of great circles on which vertex signs vanish. Each section vertex is a crossing of one of nine prism edges. The three base-direction dot products sum to zero, which reduces all sign patterns to the four possible counts of crossed vertical edges. Those cases give section vertex counts \(4,5,4,3\). Conditioning on the azimuthal angle and the uniformly distributed absolute vertical normal coordinate gives the threshold CDF. Exact one-variable integration proves the three probabilities. A direct differentiation yields a strictly increasing scalar function whose unique unit crossing is the global pentagon maximizer. The derivative of the triangle probability is positive, giving an exact aspect-ratio inverse. The independent executable checks direct geometric edge intersections rather than assuming the event classification.

The 2014 arXiv text of Padrol and Pfeifle concerns which polygons are realizable as polytope sections and their intersection complexity. Planar polarity identifies the same count with the shadow vertex count of the polar triangular bipyramid. Additional record searches using this dual formulation did not identify a matching or dominating law. Published results on central rectangular-box sections, central Platonic-solid sections, and shadows of quadrangular pyramids concern different solids or sampling operations, without an implication of the exact one-parameter law here. The 2023 paper of Shchur and Konstantinova is the closest application-driven source: its abstract reports algorithms and numerical angle-density examples for a triangular prism, not the present closed-form central-section polygon probabilities or unique height optimum. Its full text was inaccessible; this is a material residual priority risk, not a claim of comprehensive exclusion. Other unindexed stereological sources remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result completely characterizes the orientation-only central-section combinatorial distribution for a natural one-parameter five-faced solid. It identifies a nontrivial *interior* aspect ratio maximizing pentagonal slices, as opposed to assuming a monotone dependence on elongation, and proves exact shape identification from the triangle frequency alone. This is motivated by section sampling in geometric stereology and by geometric combinatorics of pentahedral sections. The result is not asserted for general triangular prisms or arbitrary-offset slicing.
