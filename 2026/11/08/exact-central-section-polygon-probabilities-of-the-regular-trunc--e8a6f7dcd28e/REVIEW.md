# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The 24-vertex model has 36 edges, and a generic central-plane section has one vertex per edge crossed. The 12 antipodal vertex-normal great circles meet pairwise in 132 distinct points without triple concurrency, giving 134 chambers. The bundled exact \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\) code enumerates all 134 sign regions, all face corners, all edge-crossing counts, and exactly six spherical-angle multisets. Girard's formula yields the specified 4,6,8,10 probabilities. An independent 36-edge spherical-lune calculation gives the same first moment, and direct 500,000-sample simulation agrees. This is a finite exact proof, not extrapolation from simulations.

## Originality
PASS at best-knowledge, subject to historical priority risk. Lonke's 1998 cube-section paper treats expected face counts of the ordinary cube and its high-dimensional analogs; neither its object nor its conclusions supply the 134-cell area table or full four-probability law here. Finch's width paper studies a different functional. Socha's 1988 random-section article uses a different plane model and targets sectional angle distributions. Searches for truncated-cube section type and close variants in scholarly and research records found related sections of different solids, not an exact copy or dominating exact type table. The strongest residual risk is unindexed classical polyhedral stereology or an implicit instance in a general arrangement formula; generic computability alone does not present the evaluated law.

## Value
PASS. The regular truncated cube is a canonical Archimedean solid whose random central sections have four genuinely occurring even-sided types, unlike the ordinary cube's two types. An exact full distribution, with a surprisingly small decagonal probability and a complete chamber orbit census, gives an intrinsically motivated finite geometric invariant; it distinguishes all four section types, not merely an expectation or a routine scaling of another solid.

## Scope and limitations
The normal is Haar uniform on the sphere and the plane passes through the center. No conclusion for affine offset planes, nonregular truncations or stability is asserted. A generic hyperplane-arrangement algorithm exists in principle, but the 134-cell exact closed-form evaluations are essential.
