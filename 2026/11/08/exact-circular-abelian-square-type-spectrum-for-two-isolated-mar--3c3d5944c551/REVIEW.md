# Scientific review of the two-mark circular Abelian-square type spectrum

## Correctness (C): PASS

The exact domain is every pair of nonnegative integer gaps \(p,q\). Every Abelian-square factor is short enough to visit each of the two marked positions at most once, so its two equal half-counts can only both be zero or both be one. The longest unmarked run gives the zero-mark indicator. The smaller oriented circular distance between the two marks gives a necessary threshold for the one-mark-per-half indicator, and the explicit starting-offset construction makes the same threshold sufficient. Counting both disjoint type labels gives the formula; integer parity cases and odd-length interval coverage prove the corollaries. An independently written cyclic-factor enumerator reproduced every type at all half-lengths for \(3{,}600\) pairs. The proof is infinite and the census is only a consistency check.

## Originality (O): PASS with documented residual risk

The closest primary result is Deep Bhattacharjee, *The minimum number of distinct abelian squares in a binary circular word*, `hexagon:2610.00473v2`, specifically the complete non-equivalent-type section, which states an odd-length conjecture and proves a formula for circular words with only two runs. The present two-separated-mark family has four cyclic runs when both gaps are positive; the two-run proposition covers only degenerate boundary members. Its general minimum theorem concerns distinct literal factors, not the exact per-half-length type spectrum. Simpson's 2018 survey and the 1997 Fraenkel–Simpson–Paterson universal extremal-length theorem do not supply the stated gap-dependent formula. Fazekas et al. 2026 analyze linear-word distinct-count minima, not these circular types. Targeted indexed mathematical-record searches of the formulas, aliases and broad coverage returned no logically covering finding; a close keyword result concerns binary adjacent-pair multispectra, a different invariant. Historical priority remains provisional because not all possible unpublished or inaccessible sources can be excluded.

## Value (V): PASS

An explicit October 2026 conjecture requests \(\sum_{m\le k}z_m\ge k\) in odd-length circular binary words. This theorem proves a *termwise stronger statement* for the first naturally nontrivial sparse-letter family, provides a complete infinite two-parameter type spectrum, and classifies precisely when these four-run words attain the conjectured global minimum. That gives a motivated structural model and new infinite extremal families, rather than an arbitrary finite census or a routine restatement of the pre-existing two-run case.

## Comparison boundaries and scientific limitations

The general odd-length conjecture remains open outside two marked positions. The statement counts equivalence classes of Abelian squares, not distinct literal factors, and the finite enumeration does not extend the scope of the proof. Potential priority from unindexed work is a residual risk.
