# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact projector is derived from the loss-free real eigenspaces of the dissipative generator. The Fourier calculation exhausts all equal-energy combinations at fixed total momentum: only exchange pairs survive in nonresonant sectors, while precisely one total-momentum-\(\pi\) sector becomes flat on even cycles. Subtracting its single detected diagonal Fourier direction gives the projector, dimension, and symmetric-pair survival law. The argument includes all positive, unequal collision-loss rates and excludes zero hopping. A standalone, independently implemented Krylov-rank check and exact projector identities agree for all ring sizes from three through ten. Finite checks are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on the examined material, subject to historical-priority risk

Krovi–Brun's continuous-time dark-space theory addresses detector-invisible eigenstates generically but does not compute this entire many-site collision projector. Štefaňák et al. analyze time-dependent meeting on an infinite coined walk, not irreversible collision absorption on a finite ring. Qin et al. analyze nearest-neighbor interacting two-particle walks with no such loss, and Kepesidis–Hartmann analyze single-particle loss at a localized lattice site. The exact phase-sensitive arbitrary-input projector, including its parity and mod-four dimension jump, is not implied by the statements examined. The Fourier and Pauli-exclusion ingredients are not novel in themselves; an unindexed exact observability or two-body loss calculation may precede this result.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Permanent survival under repeated collision loss is a natural open-system observable, distinct from a one-time meeting probability. The theorem solves it for every initial pure or mixed state, every finite ring size, and arbitrary positive site-dependent rates, with a sharp odd/even bosonic trapping transition and an additional mod-four antipodal interference law. Its explicit projector can be reused to evaluate coherent wavepackets and mixed inputs without evolving the dissipative system numerically.

## Limits

The result is not a finite-time decay-rate theorem and does not cover interactions, defects, unequal one-particle hoppings, or loss missing from any collision site. Historical priority remains to be checked by specialists against less discoverable dissipative Hubbard and observability literature.
