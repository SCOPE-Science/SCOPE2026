# Exact colour-refinement stabilization rounds for cycle–path lollipop graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge3\) and \(\ell\ge1\), let \(L_{m,\ell}\) be the undirected simple graph made by attaching a path of \(\ell\) edges to exactly one vertex of a cycle of length \(m\), with no other identifications. Start one-dimensional colour refinement (1-WL) from the uniform vertex colouring \(c_0\), and put
\[
c_{r+1}(v)=\bigl(c_r(v),\ \{\!\{c_r(w):w\sim v\}\!\}\bigr),
\]
where the doubled braces denote a multiset (not an additional operation in the graph). Let \(s(m,\ell)\) be the least \(r\ge0\) for which the partitions induced by \(c_r\) and \(c_{r+1}\) coincide. Then
\[
\boxed{s(m,\ell)=\max\left\{\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor,\ \min\{m,\ell\},\ \left\lceil\frac\ell2\right\rceil\right\}.}
\]
Equivalently, \(s(m,\ell)=\max\{\lfloor m/2\rfloor,\ell\}\) for \(\ell\le m\), while \(s(m,\ell)=\max\{m,\lceil\ell/2\rceil\}\) for \(\ell\ge m\). The stable colour classes are exactly the vertex-orbits of the lollipop's reflection automorphism: the attachment vertex alone, one class for each cycle distance \(1,\ldots,\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), and one singleton for each pendant-path vertex. The result is an exact depth threshold for stabilization of the one-variable vertex types induced by two-variable counting logic.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, simple, undirected, uncoloured initially, and connected. The attached path has \(\ell\) new vertices, so the graph has \(m+\ell\) vertices. Vertex distances along the tail are counted from the attachment vertex (the endpoint at distance \(\ell\) is a leaf). A *strict round* is one that refines the vertex partition. The stated \(s\) counts strict rounds, not the extra unsuccessful update used to certify stabilization. The graph signature uses adjacency and equality. No claim is made about refinement run in a shared palette with a second comparison graph or about the minimum quantifier depth of distinguishing two whole-graph sentences.

## Proof
Label the attachment vertex \(a\) (degree three), the terminal tail vertex \(b\) (degree one), the cycle vertices \(z_0=a,z_1,\ldots,z_{m-1}\) cyclically, and the tail vertices \(u_1,\ldots,u_\ell=b\), with \(u_0=a\) for convenience. All other vertices have degree two. A rooted, depth-\(d\) *degree-marked unfolding* of a vertex is the rooted tree of non-backtracking walks of length at most \(d\), with each unfolded vertex marked by its degree in the original graph. By induction on \(r\ge1\), two vertices have the same \(c_r\)-colour if and only if their degree-marked unfoldings of depth \(r-1\) are rooted-isomorphic. At \(r=1\) colours are degrees, and the inductive step sorts the preceding rooted neighbourhood types into multisets. This is an exact finite-depth characterization, not a probabilistic invariant.

For a cycle vertex write \(d=\min\{i,m-i\}\), ranging from zero to \(k=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\). An automorphism reflects the cycle and exchanges the two vertices at each distance \(1\le d\le k\), except where the antipodal vertex is unique. Each tail vertex is fixed. Thus the automorphism-orbit classes have the forms asserted, and no refinement can split them.

The complete unfolded graph has degree-three marks every \(m\) edges along the bi-infinite lift of the cycle, with a branch of length \(\ell\) ending in a degree-one mark at each such vertex. A cycle vertex of distance \(d\) from \(a\) sees degree-three marks at distances \(d\) and \(m-d\) in opposite directions on this lift; its closest degree-one mark is at distance \(d+\ell\). A tail vertex \(u_x\), \(1\le x\le\ell-1\), sees its nearest degree-three and degree-one marks at distances \(x\) and \(\ell-x\) along opposite directions. These observations remain valid for degree-marked finite-depth unfoldings, provided that a mark is within the inspected radius.

Put \(T=\max\{k,\min(m,\ell),\lceil\ell/2\rceil\}\). We show that \(c_T\) distinguishes different automorphism-orbit classes. For different cycle distances \(d_1<d_2\le k\), the degree-three mark at distance \(d_1\) is visible within radius \(T-1\); the second cycle position has no such mark at that distance. For two different tail positions, since \(T\ge\lceil\ell/2\rceil\), all tail positions except possibly the unique exact midpoint for even \(\ell\) have a closest degree-three or degree-one mark visible within radius \(T-1\). The type and distance of the visible closest mark determines the tail position; the possible unmarked midpoint is unique.

For a cycle position at distance \(d\ge1\) and a tail position \(u_x\), first suppose \(x\ne d\). If \(x<d\), the degree-three mark at distance \(x\le T-1\) is visible from \(u_x\) but not from the cycle position. If \(x>d\) and \(d\le T-1\), the cycle position sees a degree-three mark at distance \(d\), while \(u_x\) does not. In the remaining case \(d=T\), necessarily \(d=k\), and \(x>d\); because \(T\ge\lceil\ell/2\rceil\), a degree-one mark at distance \(\ell-x<T\) is visible from the tail position, whereas the cycle position cannot see a degree-one mark that close. If \(x=d\), the first degree-three mark occurs at the same distance. For \(\ell\le m\), the tail position sees a degree-one mark at distance \(\ell-d\le T-1\), strictly earlier than the nearest degree-one mark from the cycle. For \(m<\ell\), the cycle position sees a second degree-three mark in the opposite direction at distance \(m-d\le T-1\), whereas the tail position cannot; these cases separate the views. The special vertices \(a\) and \(b\) have unique degrees three and one. Thus \(c_T\) is exactly the orbit partition, which is equitable and therefore stable, proving \(s(m,\ell)\le T\).

To prove the reverse bound, we exhibit distinct orbits that the indicated earlier round still merges for each of the three terms defining \(T\). For \(k=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), cycle vertices at distances \(k-1\) and \(k\) have identical degree-marked unfoldings through depth \(k-2\) (for \(k=1\), use the uniform initial colouring). Hence \(s\ge k\). For the \(\lceil\ell/2\rceil\) term, the two central consecutive tail vertices \(u_{\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor}\) and \(u_{\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor+1}\) have identical unfoldings through depth \(\lceil\ell/2\rceil-2\) (again use \(c_0\) for the trivial boundary), so \(s\ge\lceil\ell/2\rceil\). Finally, \(z_1\) and \(u_1\) (distinct orbits) have identical degree-marked unfoldings through depth \(\min(m,\ell)-2\): up to that depth their only visible distinguished source is the common degree-three attachment vertex; neither the leaf in the tail direction nor the next degree-three mark beyond the lifted cycle is visible. If \(\min(m,\ell)=1\), the uniform colouring gives the needed lower bound. Therefore \(s\ge\min(m,\ell)\), proving equality.

## Verification
The supplied standard-library script `verify.py` constructs exact adjacency sets and performs 1-WL with integer palettes. It checks all \(2730\) pairs \(3\le m\le44\), \(1\le\ell\le65\) against the closed formula and the orbit partition. An independent rooted non-backtracking degree-marked unfolding implementation checks the colour/equivalence lemma for all rounds in \(160\) smaller parameter pairs. Run with `python3 verify.py`; the expected final line is `VERIFY_OK`. The exhaustive checks over stated finite ranges complement, but do not replace, the preceding proof for all integers.

## Relationship to prior work
Arvind, Köbler, Rattan and Verbitsky (arXiv:1502.01255) and Kiefer, Schweitzer and Selman (arXiv:1503.08792) analyze colour-refinement identification and counting logic, but their inspected relevant sections do not give an iteration formula for lollipops. Kiefer and McKay (arXiv:2005.10182) study sharp global iteration bounds, not this two-parameter family. The detailed unicyclic treatment in *Canonical Labeling of Sparse Random Graphs* (STACS 2025, §2.3) characterizes graph-identifiability and periodic rooted-tree decorations: its relative identification arguments explain why stable colors are informative, but do not state an exact number of rounds. Kiefer and de Mel (arXiv:2510.20802) classify graphs requiring the global maximum number of iterations, not the exact dynamics of this family in all parameter ranges. In particular, no claim of a new general colour-refinement algorithm or a new graph-isomorphism criterion is made here.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to the uncoloured cycle-with-one-tail family and measures within-graph partition stabilization from a uniform start. It is not a bound for all unicyclic graphs, a bound on an inter-graph comparison executed jointly, or a claim about the exact minimum quantifier depth of arbitrary whole-graph distinguishing sentences. Historical originality remains subject to independent literature review, especially unpublished or unindexed calculations. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
