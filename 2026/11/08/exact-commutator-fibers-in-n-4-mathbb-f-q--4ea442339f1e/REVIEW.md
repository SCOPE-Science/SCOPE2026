# Self-review: fixed commutator fibers in \(N_4(\mathbb F_q)\)

## Correctness — PASS
The four-by-four matrix-unit multiplication law gives the three explicit bilinear commutator coordinates and rules out any output outside the derived three-space. The projection to the first two coordinates has \(q^2(q^2-1)\) preimages per nonzero pair and \(2q^4-q^2\) over zero. Exactly \(q^2\) projected sextuples have a vanishing functional for the third coordinate. Counting extensions gives the displayed three fiber sizes and the total mass \(q^{12}\). An independently computed adjoint-rank distribution also recovers the zero fiber. Full direct enumeration over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4\) and separate checks over prime fields of orders \(2,3,5\) passed. Those checks are supplementary to the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with publication-priority risk
The closest same-object work of Bounds (arXiv:2301.03422, full HTML inspected) classifies functional identities for linear centralizing maps, not prescribed associative-commutator fibers. Kaur–Sharma–Singh (arXiv:2006.01493, relevant PDF sections inspected) concerns conjugacy branching and commuting probabilities of the group \(I+N\); its results imply the **zero** algebra-commutator count for odd fields, but do not state or directly imply the complete nonzero additive-commutator fiber stratification. Fulman–Guralnick (arXiv:1611.05499, introduction and matrix-algebra sections inspected) concerns zero fibers in full/classical Lie algebras, not fixed nonzero outputs of \(N_4\). A separate exact-fiber result for Kronecker path algebras concerns a nonisomorphic algebra with a different multiplication law and does not imply these formulas. Additional exact sources may exist; human publication-priority verification remains appropriate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The associative commutator distribution is a natural word-map invariant. The four-by-four strictly triangular algebra is the first member of this series whose derived space is not entirely central. The complete formula reveals a precise two-to-one multiplicity distinction between nonzero central and noncentral commutators, plus the singularity at zero, uniformly across all characteristics and finite extension fields. This is a complete classification of a motivated first non-Heisenberg case, not a selected parameter slice or isolated numeric count.
