# Exact conditioning and optimal stagger of two Gaussian spectral-bin intensity measurements

## Finding

Consider the eight intensity samples of a three-tone analytic signal obtained at four quarter-period times in each of two distinct, symmetrically centered Gaussian spectral bins. Their real-linear Hermitian lifting has an **exact eight-value singular spectrum**, and its best relative sampling offset and sharp linear inverse noise amplification are explicit.

For positive \(\alpha,\Delta,\omega\), write
\[
a=e^{-\alpha(\Delta-\omega)^2},\qquad b=e^{-\alpha\omega^2},\qquad c=e^{-\alpha(\Delta+\omega)^2}.
\]
Thus \(a>c>0\) and \(b>0\). Set
\[
U=2\sqrt{(a^2+c^2)^2+2b^4},\quad
L=2(a-c)\min\{a+c,b\},\quad T=2\sqrt2\,ac.
\]
The eight singular values (with multiplicity) are
\[
\begin{gathered}
U,\quad 2(a^2-c^2),\quad
2b(a+c),\quad2b(a+c),\\
2b(a-c),\quad2b(a-c),\\
2\sqrt2\,ac\sqrt{1+|\cos(2\eta)|},\quad
2\sqrt2\,ac\sqrt{1-|\cos(2\eta)|}.
\end{gathered}
\]
When \(\sin(2\eta)\neq0\), the lifting has rank eight and its sharp operator condition number on the orthogonal complement of its kernel is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\kappa(\eta)=\frac{U}{\min\{L,T\sqrt{1-|\cos(2\eta)|}\}}.}
\]
At \(\sin(2\eta)=0\), the lifting has rank seven (so its full-eight-output inverse condition number is infinite). The sharp optimized condition number is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\min_{\sin(2\eta)\ne0}\kappa(\eta)=\frac{U}{\min\{L,T\}}.}
\]
If \(L\ge T\), the complete optimal-offset set is \(\eta\equiv\pi/4\pmod{\pi/2}\). If \(L<T\), the complete optimal set is the nontrivial plateau
\[
|\cos(2\eta)|\le 1-(L/T)^2.
\]
Near any aligned-grid resonance \(\eta_0\in(\pi/2)\mathbb Z\),
\[
\kappa(\eta)\sim\frac{U}{4ac\,|\eta-\eta_0|}.
\]
The quotient qualification is essential: the full Hermitian domain has dimension nine, so the lifting always has a one-dimensional kernel in the rank-eight regime.

## Assumptions and scope

Use a real Gaussian frequency multiplier \(e^{-\alpha(\xi-s)^2}\) at center \(s=-\omega\) and \(s=+\omega\), acting on a complex three-tone coefficient vector supported at frequencies \(\{-\Delta,0,\Delta\}\). The two frequency responses, in increasing tone order, are \((a,b,c)\) and \((c,b,a)\). Collect intensities at phase angles \(t_{0,k}=k\pi/2\) and \(t_{1,k}=k\pi/2+\eta\), where \(k=0,1,2,3\). Use the angular-frequency Fourier convention \(e^{i\xi t}\): physical observation times are the phase angles divided by \(\Delta\); a common phase and Fourier sign convention do not affect the spectrum.

For \(w_0=(a,b,c)\), \(w_1=(c,b,a)\), put
\[
v_{j,k,m}=(w_j)_m e^{im t_{j,k}},\qquad m=0,1,2.
\]
Define \(\mathcal L_\eta:\operatorname{Herm}(3)\to\mathbb R^8\) by
\[
\mathcal L_\eta(H)_{j,k}=v_{j,k}^*Hv_{j,k}.
\]
Equip Hermitian matrices with the Frobenius norm \(\|H\|_F\), and the sample vector with the ordinary Euclidean norm. The condition number is the ratio of largest to smallest of the eight nonzero singular values, equivalently the optimal ratio on the orthogonal complement of the one-dimensional nullspace, with rank-deficient configurations assigned infinite value. These norm and sampling normalizations are part of the theorem.

## Proof

Choose the Frobenius-orthonormal basis comprising the three diagonal matrices, and for each pair of distinct indices the symmetric real and imaginary off-diagonal matrices divided by \(\sqrt2\). A four-point unitary discrete Fourier transform, applied separately to each bin's outputs, orthogonally decomposes the measurement operator into its zero-frequency (three columns), first-frequency (four columns), and Nyquist (two columns) sectors. Orthogonal column changes within the real and imaginary sectors eliminate unimodular factors without modifying singular values.

The zero-frequency block on the diagonal subspace is
\[
2\begin{pmatrix}a^2&b^2&c^2\\c^2&b^2&a^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its two nonzero singular values are
\[
2\sqrt{(a^2+c^2)^2+2b^4}=U,\qquad 2(a^2-c^2).
\]
Its nullspace is generated by \(\operatorname{diag}(b^2,-(a^2+c^2),b^2)\). This is a matrix with rank three, so its nullspace element is not a rank-two obstruction to pure-state phase retrieval, but that qualitative observation is not required for the present spectral theorem.

For each of the cosine and sine first-harmonic coefficients, the relevant block is, up to orthogonal row/column rotations,
\[
2b\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\c&a\end{pmatrix},
\]
with singular values \(2b(a+c)\) and \(2b(a-c)\). Each occurs twice, accounting for four singular values in total.

The remaining two coefficients correspond to the real and imaginary parts of \(H_{02}\). At Nyquist frequency their two sample rows form, up to orthogonal changes, the block
\[
2\sqrt2\,ac\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\cos(2\eta)&-\sin(2\eta)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its Gram matrix has eigenvalues \(8a^2c^2(1\pm|\cos(2\eta)|)\), yielding the two claimed angular singular values. The sectors are orthogonal, so their union is the complete spectrum, not an estimate.

The largest singular value is always \(U\). Indeed, write
\[
(a^2+c^2)^2+2b^4-b^2(a+c)^2
=(a^2+c^2-b^2)^2+b^2(a-c)^2+b^4.
\]
The right side is strictly positive since \(a>c\). Also \(U>2(a^2+c^2)\ge4ac\), and \(2\sqrt2 ac\sqrt{1+|\cos2\eta|}\le4ac\). These comparisons dominate every other singular value.

The smallest of the six \(\eta\)-independent singular values is exactly \(L=2(a-c)\min\{a+c,b\}\), and the smaller angular singular value is \(T\sqrt{1-|\cos2\eta|}\). This gives the condition number formula and the rank dichotomy. The angular factor is at most one, with equality precisely at quarter-grid offsets. If \(L\ge T\), only quarter-grid offsets achieve the maximal smallest singular value. If \(L<T\), every offset at which the angular singular value is at least \(L\) attains the common optimum, giving the stated plateau inequality. Finally \(1-|\cos(2\eta)|\sim2|\eta-\eta_0|^2\) near a resonance. Therefore the least singular value is asymptotically \(4ac|\eta-\eta_0|\), proving the sharp condition-number blow-up.

## Relationship to prior work

Liu, Ning, Wu and Zhang (2026) prove phase retrieval for Paley--Wiener signals from two Gaussian-convolution/wavelet magnitude sequences. Their hypotheses concern square-integrable bandlimited functions and infinite sampling sequences; a finite sum of pure spectral tones is not a Paley--Wiener \(L^2\) function. Their results do not determine the present finite eight-sample Hermitian-lifting singular spectrum or sharp offset optimization.

Conca, Edidin, Hering and Vinzant establish generic complex phase-retrieval injectivity at the \(4d-4\) measurement threshold, which is eight at complex dimension three; this does not provide the singular spectrum or optimal condition number of this structured Gaussian-bin design. Earlier two-frequency-bin Gabor phase-retrieval results likewise address uniqueness and sampling conditions rather than the exact finite Hermitian-lifting condition number calculated here. An eight-sample injectivity criterion for two rotated four-point circles is also known; the new result concerns quantitative conditioning and the complete optimal-offset set, not rediscovery of injectivity.

## Limitations

The sharp constants are for the indicated **linear lifting** between a real nine-dimensional Hermitian matrix space and eight real intensities, using Frobenius/Euclidean norms, on the identifiable quotient. They are not asserted as optimal stability constants for nonlinear reconstruction of a signal vector modulo its global phase. The result does not compare all other measurement designs, nor treat more than three tones or nonsymmetric bin centers. It does not imply injectivity on all Hermitian matrices (impossible with eight measurements). Numerical checks are finite corroboration, not an infinite proof. Publication-priority uncertainty remains for specialized finite Gaussian-bin tomography literature not accessible in full.
