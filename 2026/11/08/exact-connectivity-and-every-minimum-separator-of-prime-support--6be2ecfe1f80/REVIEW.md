# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The squarefree-cyclic subgroup product is least common multiple of orders, giving a one-to-one disjointness graph of deficiency subsets. Its degree minimum is exactly \(2^t-1\) at the deficiency layer \(|S|=r-t\). The surviving singleton clique argument forces every disconnected component after at most this many deletions to be an isolated vertex; all cuts are exactly the neighborhoods of minimum-degree vertices. Edge-connectivity follows from standard inequalities. Direct exhaustive checks cover the 4-, 5-, and 6-prime cases when \(t=2\); other checks are bounded corroboration.

**Originality: PASS, subject to historical priority risk.** Gutiérrez–Zuleta–García-Claro (arXiv:2610.40860v1, Theorem 4.17) prove 2-connectivity of the \(t=2\) graph, not its exact minimum cut size or full separator classification. Rather (arXiv:2605.01565v1) treats a weighted, unfiltered element graph, not the same vertex set, and explicitly leaves the classification of minimum cuts open in that domain. Ekinci–Gauci study uniform-layer Kneser graphs. Searches of algebra and graph-theory primary material and mathematical findings did not locate the same result; an implicit general nonuniform-disjointness theorem remains possible.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The result gives the complete optimal failure modes of a natural filtration of subgroup-product networks, strengthens qualitative two-connectivity to \(2^t-1\) exact connectivity, counts \(\binom rt\) distinct optimal separators, and proves every optimal cut isolates exactly one prescribed subgroup. These invariants expose structural behavior and algebraic bottlenecks across a growing infinite family.

**Limitations.** Squarefree cyclic groups only; \(t\ge2\), \(r\ge2^t\); sufficiency of the rank threshold only; no minimum-edge-cut classification or statement about weighted ring-element graphs. Finite experiments are not a universal proof. Independent historical-priority and mathematical audit have not been performed.
