# Exact consensus reliability from directional runs in mixed-median opinion rings

## Finding
Let \(n\geq3\) individuals occupy a cyclic social network. Each individual is independently assigned one of three local response types: weighted average (A), clockwise-dominant weighted median (R), or counterclockwise-dominant weighted median (L), with respective probabilities \(a,r,\ell\geq0\), \(a+r+\ell=1\). All local interactions have positive weights on the two distinct cycle neighbors and zero self-weight. Median types R and L place strictly more than half their influence on the indicated neighbor; the strength of that strict preference may vary from vertex to vertex. At each time the individuals synchronously move a fixed positive fraction, strictly less than one, toward their local response, and a median response is selected by projecting the individual's opinion onto the weighted-median interval.

**Deterministic statement.** For strictly direction-biased median vertices, the unique weighted median of their two neighbor opinions is exactly the opinion of their dominant neighbor. Thus the specified model is a lazy finite-state linear stochastic influence system. Delete all A vertices from the circular type word, preserving cyclic order. Every initial real opinion vector converges to consensus if and only if the resulting R/L cyclic word has at most one clockwise R-to-L transition (with zero transitions for an empty or monochromatic word). If two such transitions exist, an open set of initial opinions converges to nonconsensus equilibria. More precisely, if \(J\) denotes the number of clockwise R-to-L transitions, the number \(B\) of closed communication classes of the influence matrix, equivalently the dimension of its fixed-point space, is exactly \(B=\max\{1,J\}\). This property depends only on types and directional preference, not on the strength of bias or the adjustment fractions.

**Complete fragmentation law.** With \(J\) defined above, the exact finite distribution for \(n\geq3\) is
\[
\Pr(J=0)=(a+r)^n+(a+\ell)^n-a^n,
\]
and for each integer \(1\leq j\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor\),
\[
\Pr(J=j)=\sum_{k=2j}^{n}\binom nk a^{n-k}\frac{k}{j}\sum_{h=j}^{k-j}\binom{h-1}{j-1}\binom{k-h-1}{j-1}r^h\ell^{k-h}.
\]
The number of closed recurrent influence classes (equivalently the dimension of the fixed-point space) is \(B=\max\{1,J\}\): in particular, \(\Pr(B=1)=\Pr(J=0)+\Pr(J=1)\), and \(\Pr(B=j)=\Pr(J=j)\) for \(j\geq2\). This is a law of *asymptotic closed influence classes*, not of the number of distinct observed limiting opinions: transient vertices can converge to nontrivial harmonic combinations of multiple recurrent-class opinions, and special initial conditions can make the values coincide.

**Exact random-assignment law.** Write \(P_n(a,r,\ell)\) for the probability that the resulting dynamics reaches consensus from *every* initial opinion vector. For \(r\ne\ell\),
\[
P_n=(a+r)^n+(a+\ell)^n-a^n+\frac{nr\ell}{r-\ell}\big((a+r)^{n-1}-(a+\ell)^{n-1}\big).
\]
The continuous extension when \(r=\ell=t\) is
\[
P_n=2(a+t)^n-a^n+n(n-1)t^2(a+t)^{n-2}.
\]
In the critical sparse-type regime \(r=\alpha/n\), \(\ell=\beta/n\), with fixed \(\alpha,\beta\geq0\), this tends, when \(\alpha\ne\beta\), to
\[
e^{-\beta}+e^{-\alpha}-e^{-(\alpha+\beta)}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha-\beta}(e^{-\beta}-e^{-\alpha}),
\]
and when \(\alpha=\beta=c/2\) to \((2+c^2/4)e^{-c/2}-e^{-c}\). For fixed \(r=\ell=t\in(0,1/2]\), the leading asymptotic is \(P_n\sim n^2t^2(1-t)^{n-2}\).

**Exact-balance boundary.** In a different, balanced model in which every median voter weights the two neighbors at exactly one half each and each vertex is independently median with probability \(p\), consensus is guaranteed from every initial condition if and only if there are at most three median vertices. Its probability is precisely \(\sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom nj p^j(1-p)^{n-j}\) (interpreting binomial coefficients outside their usual range as zero). Consequently a network with all-median vertices and \(n\geq4\) never has the universal-consensus guarantee at exact half-weights; arbitrarily small uniform bias in one cyclic direction restores that guarantee for every type assignment. This is a discontinuity in the *universal-consensus guarantee*, not a discontinuity assertion for the trajectory from a particular initial vector.

## Assumptions and scope
Index cycle vertices by \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with distinct counterclockwise and clockwise neighbors \(i-1\) and \(i+1\). For an A vertex, the local response is \(w_i^-x_{i-1}+w_i^+x_{i+1}\), with \(w_i^\pm>0\) and \(w_i^-+w_i^+=1\). For an R vertex, \(w_i^+>1/2\); for an L vertex, \(w_i^->1/2\). Median responses minimize \(w_i^-|z-x_{i-1}|+w_i^+|z-x_{i+1}|\), and ties select the minimizer closest to \(x_i\). Each vertex has a fixed \(\gamma_i\in(0,1)\) and updates \(x_i(k+1)=(1-\gamma_i)x_i(k)+\gamma_i H_i(x(k))\). Randomness refers only to one-time independent assignment of A/R/L types, never to the initial opinions, temporal updates, or numeric influence weights conditional on types. The exact-balance boundary substitutes T vertices with \(w_i^-=w_i^+=1/2\) and draws A/T types independently.

The assertion uses universal convergence from all \(x(0)\in\mathbb R^n\). It does not assert disagreement for all initial vectors of an unfavorable configuration.

## Proof
**1. Cohesion reduction.** For a nonempty set \(S\), the type-dependent cohesion criterion of Cheng and Mei [1, Definition 3 and Theorem 11] demands that every A vertex in \(S\) have all its positive influence in \(S\), while every median vertex have at least half its influence in \(S\). Under the stated positive two-neighbor weights, an A vertex in a cohesive set must have both neighbors in the set. An R vertex must have its clockwise neighbor in the set, and an L vertex must have its counterclockwise neighbor in the set. The published theorem, whose synchronous update and median-tie hypotheses agree with ours, says that convergence to consensus from every initial state is equivalent to the absence of two disjoint nonempty cohesive sets. If disjoint sets exist, an open set of initial opinions has persistent dissensus.

**2. Oriented boundary lemma.** Any nonempty *proper* cohesive subset of a ring decomposes into disjoint non-wrapping cyclic intervals. In each interval, the starting vertex has its counterclockwise neighbor outside, so it cannot be A or L and must be R. The ending vertex has its clockwise neighbor outside and must be L. Every such interval therefore runs clockwise from R to L, with at least two vertices. Conversely, any clockwise interval from an R vertex to a subsequent L vertex is cohesive: its endpoints retain their dominant neighbors inside, and every strictly interior vertex retains both neighbors. In particular, the interval between two *consecutive median vertices* of types R then L is cohesive, even if any number of averaging vertices lie between them.

If two disjoint cohesive sets exist, choose one component interval of each. Within either interval the median-type subsequence begins with R and ends with L, so it contains an R-to-L transition; the two transitions are distinct. Conversely, two R-to-L transitions between consecutive medians define two disjoint cohesive intervals. Thus the published criterion becomes exactly the stated bound of at most one R-to-L transition. There is no hidden exceptional case when there are zero, one, or two median vertices, nor when an interval wraps around the labeled origin.

**3. Number of closed influence classes.** Since \(w_i^+>1/2\) at R vertices and \(w_i^->1/2\) at L vertices, the strict two-point median minimizes the absolute deviation uniquely at the favored neighbor's opinion, even when the two neighbor opinions coincide. Therefore all updates are linear, with positive self-loop \(1-\gamma_i\), and positive influence arcs to both neighbors at A vertices, just clockwise at R, just counterclockwise at L. A closed strongly connected component cannot have an outgoing influence arc. If the reduced circular word contains both R and L, every R-to-L consecutive median pair and the A sites between them form a closed strongly connected interval. Any other proper closed communicating class would have to start at R and end at L by the boundary argument; but consecutive medians that are both R or both L cannot close an interval, so no other class exists. Thus these intervals are *all* closed classes, one for each R-to-L transition; no other closed component exists. If the word is monochromatic (or empty), the directed neighbor graph is strongly connected: each vertex either follows the common ring direction, or at A vertices follows both directions, and any A-only gap attaches to that directed circuit. Consequently the number of closed classes is exactly \(B=\max\{1,J\}\). Standard finite lazy Markov-chain decomposition gives convergence to a fixed-point vector with one free constant per closed communicating class and transients uniquely harmonically extended from those constants. Each class has positive holding probabilities and is aperiodic. This proves the fixed-space dimension claim and also independently proves the all-initial-state consensus criterion. An open set of initial vectors induces unequal limiting constants when there are at least two closed classes.

**4. Exact fragmentation count.** Conditional on the \(k\) median sites, exactly \(j\geq1\) R-to-L transitions mean exactly \(j\) nonempty circular runs of R and \(j\) nonempty runs of L. If \(h\) of the \(k\) medians are R, compositions of the R and L run lengths give \(\binom{h-1}{j-1}\binom{k-h-1}{j-1}\) choices after choosing a cyclic run boundary. There are \(k\) choices of a labeled start median, and each cyclic word is represented \(j\) times by a distinguished R-run start; the number of binary words is hence \(\frac{k}{j}\binom{h-1}{j-1}\binom{k-h-1}{j-1}\). Multiplying by \(\binom nk a^{n-k}r^h\ell^{k-h}\) and summing yields the displayed distribution for positive \(j\). With no R-to-L transition, the reduced word is monochromatic or empty, giving \((a+r)^n+(a+\ell)^n-a^n\). Every type assignment is counted exactly once, so these probabilities sum to one; the relationship to \(B\) follows from Step 3.

**5. Exact consensus probability.** Conditional on the \(k\) median positions, each circular R/L word having at most one R-to-L transition is either monochromatic or has one contiguous run of each type. For every \(k\geq1\), the total probability weight of such words, omitting the A positions, is
\[
r^k+\ell^k+k\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}r^j\ell^{k-j}.
\]
There are \(\binom nk\) ways to choose the median positions; the \(k=0\) contribution is \(a^n\). For \(r\ne\ell\), the inner sum is \(r\ell(r^{k-1}-\ell^{k-1})/(r-\ell)\). Binomial summation and the differentiated identity \(\sum_k\binom nk k a^{n-k}z^{k-1}=n(a+z)^{n-1}\) give the first displayed formula. Taking \(r\to\ell\) gives the second. Both remain valid at nonnegative zero probabilities by continuity.

**6. Limits.** Substitute \(r=\alpha/n\), \(\ell=\beta/n\) into the exact formula, using \((1-z/n)^n\to e^{-z}\). For equal rates use the symmetric expression directly. For fixed \(r=\ell=t>0\), the quadratic polynomial multiplier of \((1-t)^{n-2}\) dominates the other two exponential terms, yielding the stated equivalent (at \(t=1/2\), the term \(a^n\) vanishes and the equivalent still holds).

**7. Exactly half-weighted medians.** At a T boundary vertex, it is enough to retain either neighbor; an A boundary vertex still cannot occur. Therefore a proper cohesive interval must have T endpoints, and any interval with T endpoints is cohesive. Two disjoint such intervals exist if and only if there are at least four T vertices: necessity follows because each disjoint interval consumes two, and sufficiency follows by pairing four T sites in circular order. The published universal-consensus criterion gives the bound of three. Independence makes the number of T sites binomial, proving the displayed exact probability. If all medians strictly favor the same direction, the reduced word is monochromatic and the deterministic theorem yields universal consensus.

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_ring.py` exhaustively enumerates every assignment of types A/R/L for each ring size \(3\leq n\leq8\), independently enumerates *all* nonempty subsets to detect disjoint cohesive sets, computes closed communicating classes by directed reachability and compares both with the R-to-L transition and class-count criteria. It also compares the complete \(J\) distribution against independent enumeration and checks exact consensus probability formulas with rational integer weights for a symmetric and an asymmetric triple of type probabilities. It also exhausts all A/T assignments for \(3\leq n\leq9\) against the four-median threshold. There are 10,844 enumerated network-type configurations in these checks. Execute `python3 verify_ring.py`; the proof above, not finite enumeration, establishes all sizes and parameter values.

## Relationship to prior work
Cheng and Mei [1] establish the exact general consensus-vs-disjoint-cohesion theorem for mixed weighted averaging and median rules. The distinction between universal consensus and observed consensus at a finite horizon is explicit in their simulations. Their theorem is **used**, not claimed as new. The new contribution is the exact number of closed influence classes and complete distribution of fragmentation under i.i.d type labels, the ring-specific directional-run equivalence, a closed consensus-reliability law, the sparse-type limit, and the exact-balance discontinuity. Because strictly dominant two-point medians copy one neighbor, this ring model reduces to a finite Markov influence matrix; the fixed-instance closed-class theorem itself is standard, and novelty is restricted to the oriented ring-class characterization and exact distribution under heterogeneous social-response types. The general criterion requires searching for cohesive sets on an arbitrary network and does not state this cyclic reduction or its closed reliability law. Earlier weighted-median works [2,3] concern asynchronous pure-median or continuous-time pure-median dynamics, respectively. Their known concepts of cohesion are acknowledged, and neither is used to claim novelty for the underlying median rule.

## Limitations
The quantitative result applies to nearest-neighbor rings with no self-weights and strict directional influence at all median vertices; arbitrary graphs, long-range links, non-independent type labels, and time-varying weights are outside scope. The half-weight model is a separate boundary regime, not the continuous extension of strict-bias labels. The critical sparse limit concerns universal-consensus reliability over random *type assignments*, not the probability of convergence to consensus given a random initial vector on a non-guaranteeing network. Its scientific relevance is an exact structural benchmark, not a calibrated empirical prediction. A proof of originality relative to all potentially unpublished or inaccessible literature is not claimed.
