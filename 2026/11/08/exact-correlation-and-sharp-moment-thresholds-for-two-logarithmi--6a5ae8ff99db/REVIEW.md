# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed generator, killed Green density and its derivative jump were reconstructed. Integrating against each positive power gives the exact mean and its boundary. Strong Markov temporal ordering yields the mixed second moment, including divergence when \(\beta\le p+q\). Algebra gives covariance, variances and the Pearson formula. Strict monotonicity and the sharp ceiling follow from an elementary one-parameter square-root factor. The embedded independent Green-kernel quadrature checker passed 248 comparisons, but correctness of the divergence and limiting claims rests on the proof, not on finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Radice (arXiv:2307.12699, Sections VI–VII) provides only individual inverse-gamma marginals and explicitly identifies the between-functional correlation question as a future direction. Marginals alone do not imply mixed moments. The highly relevant Kearney–Martin (arXiv:2007.13129) correlation result is for an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator, not an inverse-position drift, and neither it nor general Bessel hitting-time marginals provide this exact power-family boundary and correlation geometry. Searches for equivalent formulations and broader coverage found no direct statement; generic potential-theory methods could still implicitly subsume the derivation, so publication priority should be checked by an independent specialist.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The work answers a specifically motivated gap in the source literature without claiming an unavailable full joint law. It identifies a sharp cross-moment boundary distinct from individual mean/variance thresholds, demonstrates universal independence from initial position and diffusion strength, and computes a strictly subunit strong-attraction correlation ceiling even though the two observables share a trajectory. In the natural time–area example these results quantify coupling between duration and cumulative displacement, not just either marginal distribution.

## Scientific limits

No general multivariate distribution is derived. Finite-variance correlation is not defined at or below its exact threshold. Later or uninspected general Bessel additive-functional references may contain implicit or equivalent formulas. No independent audit has been performed.
