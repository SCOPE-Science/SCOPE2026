# Mathematical review: exact cube affine-projection norms

## Correctness — PASS

The exact normalized Gram basis yields \(K(x,y)=1+3x\cdot y\). Separate convexity and a strictly positive conditional density about zero prove that all, and only, cube vertices maximize the kernel \(L^1\) mass. The Irwin--Hall positive-part formula gives the rational finite sum in every dimension. The one-coordinate uniform integral yields a nonnegative quadratic increment identity, proving strict growth. A characteristic-function expansion with controlled tails and Fourier-inversion density gives the independent \(1/20\) and \(1/6\) relative corrections, totaling \(13/60\). Exact-rational integration of the B-spline density independently reproduces the finite formula for dimensions 1 through 18. Finite checks are supporting evidence, not a substitute for the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (with residual literature risk)

The low-dimensional interval and square cases are already included in previously established interval/regular-polygon statements and are not counted as novel. The verified earlier simplex, Euclidean-ball, Dirichlet-simplex, product-alphabet, and spline statements concern different domains or measures and do not imply the continuous cube's all-dimensional rational sum, strict dimension increment, or refined asymptotic. The primary text of Hangelbroek et al. (arXiv:1005.2424) proves bounds for local kernel spaces on smooth manifolds. Cohen et al. (arXiv:1111.4422) studies random-sample least squares, not the same continuous operator. Białas-Cież et al. (arXiv:2311.18656) gives general discretization and interval enclosures for Lebesgue constants on cube, simplex, and ball, including numerical square examples, but does not supply these all-dimensional exact conclusions. A search of accessible published claims and targeted literature found no covering formula; incomplete indexing and older textbooks remain priority risks. The claim requires independent scholarly review.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The degree-one \(L^2\) projection is a canonical stability operator for multivariate approximation on an axis-parallel box. The result gives its sharp uniform error-amplification factor for arbitrary dimension, every norming point and extremizing datum, and the exact loss of positivity. The nonnegative increment identity explains how adding one coordinate increases instability. The rational finite-sum arithmetic and the \(13/60\) second-order correction distinguish the cube's dimension dependence from known simplex (linear) and Euclidean-ball (square-root) geometries. This is an exact natural invariant and a dimension-structure law rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Scientific boundaries

The claims are restricted to real affine projection with uniform continuous volume and do not extend to higher degrees, vertex-counting measures, weighted product laws, or general convex domains. The novelty judgment is limited by accessible bibliographic coverage.
