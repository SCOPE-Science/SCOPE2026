# Exact cyclic one-step preimage multiplicity distribution of elementary cellular automaton Rule 30

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge1\), let \(F_n:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^n\) be the cyclic, indexed version of elementary cellular automaton Rule 30, defined by
\[
(F_n(x))_i=x_{i-1}\oplus(x_i\lor x_{i+1}),\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
Let \(C_{n,j}=\#\{y\in\{0,1\}^n:\#F_n^{-1}(y)=j\}\). Define \(s_0=3\), \(s_1=s_2=1\), and \(s_n=s_{n-1}+2s_{n-3}\) for \(n\ge3\). Write \(\delta_n=1\) if \(3\mid n\), and zero otherwise. The entire multiplicity distribution, including outputs with no predecessor, is
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{n,0}&=(1+s_n-2\delta_n)/2,\\
C_{n,1}&=2^n-1-s_n+3\delta_n,\\
C_{n,2}&=(1+s_n-6\delta_n)/2,\\
C_{n,3}&=\delta_n,\\
C_{n,j}&=0\quad(j\ge4).
\end{aligned}
\]
The unique output with three predecessors, when it exists, is the all-ones output \(1^n\). It exists exactly when \(3\mid n\).

## Assumptions and scope
The input and output are labeled position vectors, not rotation classes. All indices are cyclic, including the cases \(n=1,2\) where the three neighborhood positions need not be distinct. The operation \(\oplus\) is XOR and \(\lor\) is Boolean OR. Only one synchronous update is considered.

## Proof
**Predecessor counting.** Encode a bit pair \((u,v)\) as the integer \(2u+v\). Inverting one Rule 30 equation with prescribed output bit \(b\) maps the successor pair \((u,v)\) to the preceding pair \((b\oplus(u\lor v),u)\). The two maps, represented by their images on \(0,1,2,3\), are
\[
g_0=(0,2,3,3),\qquad g_1=(2,0,1,1).
\]
Every predecessor of a cyclic output \(y_0\cdots y_{n-1}\) corresponds to exactly one fixed state of \(g_{y_0}\circ\cdots\circ g_{y_{n-1}}\). This is bijective even for \(n=1,2\), as a fixed pair enforces the required cyclic closure.

**Maximum multiplicity.** The semigroup generated by \(g_0,g_1\) has \(41\) elements. Its only rank-three transformations, identified by shortest generating words, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{word}&0&1&01&11&011&111\\
\hline
\text{fixed points}&2&0&1&0&0&3
\end{array}
\]
There are no rank-four elements except the empty-word identity. Appending \(g_0\) to any rank-three transformation lowers the rank to at most two, whereas appending \(g_1\) permutes each of the two three-step rank-three cycles. These claims follow from the explicit four-point maps and a finite exhaustive closure, reproducible in the attached independent script. A cyclic output with at least two zero symbols can be rotated to begin with zero and reach a second zero: its composition then has rank at most two, so at most two fixed points. For a cyclic output with exactly one zero, the relevant transformations have the repeating fixed-point pattern \(2,1,0\), hence never three. For an all-ones output, powers of \(g_1\) have exactly three fixed points precisely at positive exponents divisible by three, and otherwise zero. Thus \(C_{n,3}=\delta_n\), the three-predecessor output is unique, and \(C_{n,j}=0\) for \(j\ge4\).

**Second moment.** Index the four de Bruijn states by the ordered pairs \(ab\in\{0,1\}^2\). Define the nonnegative integer \(16\times16\) matrix \(P\), indexed by ordered pairs of de Bruijn states \((ab,de)\), with one transition to \((bc,ef)\) for each \(c,f\in\{0,1\}\) satisfying
\[
a\oplus(b\lor c)=d\oplus(e\lor f).
\]
Then \(\operatorname{tr}(P^n)\) counts ordered pairs of indexed length-\(n\) predecessor rings with identical outputs; consequently
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P^n)=\sum_y|F_n^{-1}(y)|^2.
\]
The exact integer characteristic-polynomial calculation gives
\[
\det(tI-P)=t^{11}(t-2)(t-1)(t^3-t^2-2).
\]
The calculation uses Newton identities on the explicit matrix and exact integer arithmetic; the complete calculation is in `verify_semigroup.py`. If \(s_n\) is the power sum of the three roots of \(t^3-t^2-2\), its initial values and recurrence are exactly those in the statement, so for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\sum_y|F_n^{-1}(y)|^2=2^n+1+s_n.
\]
Finally \(\sum_jC_{n,j}=2^n\), \(\sum_jjC_{n,j}=2^n\), and \(\sum_jj^2C_{n,j}=2^n+1+s_n\). Together with \(C_{n,3}=\delta_n\) and the exclusion of larger fibers, these four equations uniquely yield the stated formulas.

## Verification
`verify_semigroup.py` constructs the two generators, exhausts their \(41\)-element semigroup, checks all rank-three cases, constructs the \(16\)-state pair matrix and computes its characteristic polynomial using exact integer Newton identities. It also compares predicted fiber counts with direct fixed-point enumeration of outputs through length twelve and verifies the second moment through length sixteen. `verify_direct.py` independently computes every Rule 30 image on all \(2^n\) inputs for each \(1\le n\le16\) and compares the entire multiplicity histogram and the triple-output criterion. Run `python3 verify_semigroup.py` and `python3 verify_direct.py` with Python 3's standard library. These checks are finite corroborations; the all-length proof is the preceding structural argument and exact characteristic-polynomial identity.

## Relationship to prior work
Wolfram introduced and analyzed elementary cellular automata and Rule 30 in the 1983 review [1]. Jen [2, especially pp. 434–435] obtains four one-step predecessors for an arbitrary finite Rule 30 word when its infinite-lattice boundary choices are unrestricted. That count does **not** imply the present cyclic closure conditions, where indexed outputs have zero to three predecessors. Fukś [3] studies multiple-step preimage counts of local words, another boundary/time regime. The present exact all-length *finite cyclic output-fiber histogram*, including its three-predecessor congruence obstruction, is not stated in the inspected portions of those works. The general de Bruijn-matrix technique is established prior art; the derived spectral identity and exact histogram are the specific claims assessed here.

## Limitations
The result is for binary Rule 30, synchronous one-step indexed cyclic rings. It does not address infinite lattices with free boundaries, multi-step preimages, other cellular automaton rules, or temporal attractor statistics. The 1983 review was consulted through bibliographic/abstract material rather than full text, and a related 1988 cylindrical-automata article was available only at abstract level; a historical tabulation in inaccessible literature cannot be excluded. Historical priority and independent validation remain unconfirmed. The precise 1983-07-01 date is the earliest day-verified public source used; earlier undated preprint notices were not treated as day-verified.
