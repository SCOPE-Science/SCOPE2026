# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For each prime power, the proof partitions starts by whether an interval contains zero, one, or two multiples of the prime. In the one-multiple case, precisely the square-gcd residues divisible by the prime square are admissible; they are counted by the independently derived alternating-divisor recurrence. Two multiples a prime apart cannot both have valuation at least two. CRT multiplies the local counts, and the second finite difference recovers all maximal-run counts. The independent finite checker corroborated all its enumerated cases. Risk: computational tests cannot replace the infinite proof, which is supplied separately.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Tóth (arXiv:1111.4842) and OEIS A206369 determine the one-point quantity, not the simultaneous all-length counts or sharp run spectrum. The Schemmel totient is the classical all-units consecutive-block count: it exactly covers the squarefree special case but not the prime-power correction (e.g., at modulus 9 and block length 2 its value is 3, while the square-gcd count is 5). The accessible HTML of Pabhapote–Laohakosol (2010), especially Section 2, was inspected for Schemmel, Lucas, Klee, and Nageswara Rao generalizations; its displayed counting conditions do not impose arbitrary even prime-power valuation. The related Jacobsthal paper of Costello and Watts (arXiv:1209.3464) addresses coprimality with primorials; only its abstract could be inspected. Searches using square-gcd, alternating divisors, cyclic runs, adjacent gcd-square pairs, equivalent local valuations, and maximal-run spectra did not locate a covering formula. Historical priority is not guaranteed, particularly because that later paper's full text was inaccessible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The familiar square-gcd interpretation of an arithmetic function motivates a natural multi-point refinement. The theorem gives the complete distribution of cyclic runs for every modulus (not a computed sample), including all nonzero lengths, counts and a sharp extremum. The prime-square exception has an explicit arithmetic explanation; knowing the one-point totient cannot yield these run lengths. This provides a useful exact local model for consecutive residue constraints of Jacobsthal type. Risk: the elementary CRT mechanism may be independently rediscoverable; the value is the complete sharp classification, not proof complexity.

Closest literature is the one-point square-gcd totient in Tóth and OEIS and the classical consecutive-units Schemmel totient, whose squarefree branch is acknowledged as covered; the generalized-totient article was examined in full, and the Jacobsthal paper is a further comparator. The theorem does not assert an asymptotic over moduli, an answer for arbitrary nonconsecutive shifts, or independently established historical priority.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
