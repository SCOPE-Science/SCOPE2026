# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof reduces degree exactly to alternating-interval counts using the established Type-A characterization, then derives the complete weighted suffix recurrence by disjoint append cases. The finite-moment argument has explicit geometric tails, conditional linear regression, covariance propagation and finite-sum variance; the limit proof uses an atom-mixing Markov chain and martingale differences with uniformly bounded fourth moments. Direct deletion-output brute force and rational arithmetic verify the critical finite identities in the provided checker. No finite test is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
The published Type-A characterization is acknowledged as prior and is not the new claim. The public exact graph-edge count gives the mean degree but does not specify higher moments or the full distribution. The new combination of an all-length degree enumerator, an exact finite variance and an explicit Gaussian coefficient is not stated in the inspected relevant literature. Source-specific novelty remains conditional on unindexed or inaccessible earlier work, especially in word-pattern statistics.

## Value — PASS
The complete degree distribution describes how many competing candidate words a particular input has under two-read one-deletion ambiguity. The average-only edge count suppresses this heterogeneity. Knowing the entire distribution supports degree-sensitive graph estimates (for instance the standard Caro–Wei independent-set bound), while the proven nondegenerate \(\sqrt n\)-scale and exact variance coefficient quantify typical deviations. This is a natural all-length stochastic graph invariant, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.
