# Scientific review: Exact diameter law and opposite endpoint exponents for regular-tetrahedron planar shadows

## Correctness — PASS

The vertex coordinates and the six projected edge vectors give the exact squared-diameter formula \(D^2=(8-4Y^2)/3\) with \(Y=\min\{a-b,b-c\}\) for ordered absolute normal coordinates. A sharp elementary inequality gives the exact support and the twelve smallest-diameter unoriented planes. The full CDF follows from a spherical chart and a split one-dimensional integral, with proper orientation normalization. A local spherical strip argument gives the square-root upper tail, while a transverse triangular tangent-cone calculation yields the quadratic lower tail with its coefficient. The independently executable numerical checker confirms the edge identity, exact quadrature at multiple thresholds, and both endpoint coefficients. It is corroboration, not a substitute for the analytic derivation.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority

The closest directly inspected source is Finch's *Planar Projections and Second Intrinsic Volume* (arXiv:1203.2464, 2012), whose relevant pages concern Haar shadow area, perimeter, and polygonal vertex counts. Böröczky and Henk's 1997 preprint on random projections of regular polytopes concerns projected face-count expectations and spherical angles. The natural equivalent description as a centered-radius law of projected tetrahedral difference bodies (scaled cuboctahedra), and alternatives under the names projected diameter and maximum Feret diameter, were separately compared. None of the accessible relevant results establishes the presented CDF, sharp minimum, or both boundary laws. A later broad paper on random line widths was accessible only at abstract level and is not treated as conclusively excluded; an earlier unindexed cuboctahedral radius computation remains a real residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A planar shadow's diameter is its largest caliper length, a basic metric not determined by the marginal distribution of scalar width or by expected shadow area alone. The theorem supplies a complete distribution with elementary one-dimensional quadrature, the precise best and worst orientations, and sharp, inequivalent endpoint power laws. The lower endpoint isolates twelve special projective normal directions, while the upper endpoint is attained along coordinate-equality walls; this explains the exponent contrast structurally rather than as a numeric curiosity.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to a regular tetrahedron in three dimensions and Haar orthogonal plane projection. The formula is exact quadrature, not a claimed elementary antiderivative. The original broad width preprint's unavailable full text leaves a priority risk. Independent expert and historical audits remain unperformed.
