# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The polytope is exactly the zero-sum \(\ell_1\)-ball of radius \(2\), with nonempty proper subset inequalities defining its facets. At each root vertex, strictly inward motion against all active facets is equivalent to (1), independently checked against explicit facet evaluation. Two illuminated roots with distinct tails and heads violate the total-positive-mass bound; the illuminated bipartite edge set must be a star and contains at most \(n-1\) vertices. The \(n\) explicit tail directions partition the vertices and hence illuminate the entire boundary. Summing fractional vertex constraints gives the matching lower bound. Equality forces full-star directions, and the connected off-diagonal bipartite incidence graph gives all fractional and ordinary optimizers. The proof treats every integer \(n\ge3\), arbitrary real directions, and boundary points; the finite integer verifier supplied only additional cross-checks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The symmetric edge polytope of the complete graph and the difference body of a simplex are canonical geometric objects; a complete illumination classification across all dimensions is mathematically natural. The bipartite-star obstruction and the connected-incidence rigidity are structural, not a small numerical instance: they yield both ordinary and fractional exact invariants, the sharp maximal number of roots reached by one direction, explicit optimal directions, and a continuum of fully classified fractional optimizers. The object has quadratically many vertices but a linear exact illumination number. No conclusion is claimed for other graphs or higher illumination multiplicities.

The fractional statement is for finite support and the classification concerns aggregate weights, not unique direction vectors.
