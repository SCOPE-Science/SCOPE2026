# Exact discriminant factors and normalized monodromy of cycle-polynomial projections

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), put \(d=2^{n-2}\) and \(I=\{2,\ldots,n\}\). Write \(\Gamma_n(X,x_2,\ldots,x_n)\) for the cycle polynomial normalized to be monic of degree \(d\) in \(X=x_1\), as in [1, Definition 3.2 and Corollary 3.7]. For \(S\subseteq I\) with \(|S|\ge3\), let \(\Gamma_S\) be the same monic cycle polynomial on the variables indexed by \(S\), and let \(\Gamma_S^-\) result from negating any one argument. The latter does not depend on the chosen argument because the cycle polynomial is invariant under every even sign flip [1, Lemma 3.4]. Set
\[
Q_n=\begin{cases}1,&n=3,\\ \displaystyle\prod_{2\le i<j\le n}(x_i^2-x_j^2)^{2^{n-3}},&n\ge4.\end{cases}
\]
Then the exact monic discriminant in \(X\), including its scalar coefficient, is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Disc}_X(\Gamma_n)=2^{d(d-1)}\prod_{i=2}^n(1-x_i^2)^{2^{n-3}}\,Q_n\!\prod_{\substack{S\subseteq I\\3\le|S|\le n-2}}(\Gamma_S\Gamma_S^-)^{2^{n-|S|-1}}.}
\]
An empty product is one; in particular, the pair factor \(Q_n\) is absent when \(n=3\). The irreducible components are exactly the hyperplanes \(x_i=\pm1\), the hyperplanes \(x_i=\pm x_j\) for \(n\ge4\), and the hypersurfaces \(\Gamma_S=0\), \(\Gamma_S^-=0\) for \(3\le|S|\le n-2\). These factors are distinct. Over \(\mathbb C\), the discriminant is a perfect square for every \(n\ge4\), and is nonsquare for \(n=3\).

The splitting field over \(K=\mathbb C(x_2,\ldots,x_n)\) is a Galois extension with group \((\mathbb Z/2)^{n-2}\) acting regularly on the \(d\) roots. The normalization of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[X,x_2,\ldots,x_n]/(\Gamma_n)\) is described explicitly below. Its projection to \(\mathbb A^{n-1}\) has codimension-one branch divisor precisely \(\prod_{i=2}^n(x_i^2-1)=0\), with local index two along each hyperplane. Thus the remaining discriminant components detect collisions in the nonnormal monogenic model rather than genuine field ramification.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\); all polynomial rings and varieties are affine, and discriminants are univariate discriminants in \(X\) of the **monic** cycle polynomial. For \(s\ge3\), the irreducible polynomial \(\Gamma_s\) is the unique normalized polynomial whose zero locus is the cosine image of the linear hyperplane \(\theta_1=\theta_2+\cdots+\theta_s\), as developed in [1]. The source also establishes the root representation and even-sign symmetry. There is no assertion here about the full algebraic boundary of an arbitrary graph elliptope, or about real branch behavior.

## Proof
**1. Root field and Galois group.** Put \(A=\mathbb C[x_2,\ldots,x_n]\) and adjoin independent symbols \(y_i\) satisfying \(y_i^2=x_i^2-1\). Define \(z_i=x_i+y_i\), whose inverse is \(x_i-y_i\). Set
\[
B=A[y_2,\ldots,y_n]/(y_i^2-x_i^2+1:\ i\in I),\qquad L=\operatorname{Frac}(B).
\]
Since the divisors \(x_i=1\) have pairwise independent parity valuations on the classes \(x_j^2-1\), these are independent in \(K^*/K^{*2}\). Thus \([L:K]=2^{n-1}\) with Galois group \(G=(\mathbb Z/2)^{n-1}\), generated by independent involutions \(y_i\mapsto-y_i\).

For a sign vector \(\delta\in\{\pm1\}^{I}\), write
\[
 w_\delta=\prod_{i\in I}z_i^{\delta_i},\qquad r_\delta=\frac{w_\delta+w_\delta^{-1}}2.
\]
By [1, Corollary 3.7], the roots of \(\Gamma_n\) are exactly the distinct \(r_\delta\), indexed by \(\delta\) modulo \(\delta\sim-\delta\). The group \(G\) changes selected signs \(\delta_i\), and its diagonal element changing all signs fixes every \(r_\delta\). This is the full common stabilizer: if a sign change not equal to the identity or the diagonal fixed one root, irreducibility and the distinct root representation would contradict the full \(2^{n-2}\)-element orbit (alternatively compare the Laurent monomials \(w_\delta\)). Therefore the splitting field is \(F=L^{\langle(-1,\ldots,-1)\rangle}\) of degree \(d\) over \(K\), with the asserted regular quotient Galois group.

**2. One unordered root-pair orbit.** Put \(m=n-1\), so \(d=2^{m-1}\). The unordered pairs of distinct sign classes are partitioned by unordered bipartitions \(\{S,T\}\) of \(I\), with \(S,T\ne\varnothing\), according to which coordinates differ in sign. For a fixed bipartition there are \(d/2=2^{m-2}\) root pairs, in bijection with pairs of sign classes on \(S\) and \(T\). Formally choose angles \(\theta_i\) satisfying \(x_i=\cos\theta_i\); this is only mnemonic for the algebraic Laurent identity, which holds after substituting \(z_i=e^{\mathrm i\theta_i}\). The two roots for local phase sums \(u\) on \(S\) and \(v\) on \(T\) are \(\cos(u+v)\) and \(\cos(u-v)\). Their squared difference is
\[
 (\cos(u+v)-\cos(u-v))^2=4(1-\cos^2u)(1-\cos^2v).
\]
For every nonempty subset \(S\subseteq I\), set
\[
 P_S=\prod_{\eta\in\{\pm1\}^S/\{\pm\mathbf1\}}(1-r_{S,\eta}^2),\qquad
 r_{S,\eta}=\tfrac12\left(\prod_{i\in S}z_i^{\eta_i}+\prod_{i\in S}z_i^{-\eta_i}\right).
\]
The product is a polynomial in the selected \(x_i\) because it is invariant under independent radical sign changes. The product of squared differences for the root-pair orbit of \(\{S,T\}\) is exactly
\[
4^{2^{m-2}}P_S^{2^{|T|-1}}P_T^{2^{|S|-1}}.
\]
There are \(2^{m-1}-1=d-1\) unordered nontrivial bipartitions. Multiplying them therefore gives the **exact** identity
\[
 \operatorname{Disc}_X(\Gamma_n)=2^{d(d-1)}\prod_{\varnothing\ne S\subsetneq I}P_S^{2^{m-|S|-1}}.
\]
Here we used the defining monic discriminant \(\prod_{\alpha<\beta}(r_\alpha-r_\beta)^2\), with no hidden sign or normalization constant.

**3. Identify all factors.** Directly \(P_{\{i\}}=1-x_i^2\). For two variables, the same cosine-difference identity gives
\[
 P_{\{i,j\}}=(x_i^2-x_j^2)^2.
\]
For \(s=|S|\ge3\), choose \(i_0\in S\) and group the sign classes according to a sign vector on \(S\setminus\{i_0\}\) modulo overall negation. For each such local vector with phase sum \(\phi\), the paired product is
\[
 (1-\cos^2(\theta_{i_0}+\phi))(1-\cos^2(\theta_{i_0}-\phi))
 =(\cos^2\phi-x_{i_0}^2)^2.
\]
The monic-root formulas for \(\Gamma_S(x_{i_0},x_{S\setminus\{i_0\}})\) and \(\Gamma_S(-x_{i_0},x_{S\setminus\{i_0\}})\) show that their product squared is precisely \(P_S\); hence
\[
 P_S=(\Gamma_S\Gamma_S^-)^2.
\]
Substitution into the preceding subset identity yields the boxed factorization with exponents \(2^{n-3}\) for each singleton, \(2^{n-3}\) for each pair difference, and \(2^{n-s-1}\) for each lower-cycle pair of cardinality \(s\ge3\). The range restriction to proper subsets explains the exceptional \(n=3\) case. Over \(\mathbb C\), split \(1-x_i^2\) into its two linear factors and \(x_i^2-x_j^2\) into two linear factors. Irreducibility of \(\Gamma_S\) is in [1, Section 3]; its odd-sign twist is also irreducible and distinct, as seen by specializing all variables except one to one: the two specialized polynomials become \( (X-1)^{2^{s-2}} \) and \( (X+1)^{2^{s-2}} \), respectively. Distinct variable-support sets give distinct lower-cycle factors, while no such factor is linear. Every exponent is even for \(n\ge4\). For \(n=3\), the singleton factors each have exponent one, proving nonsquareness.

**4. Normalization and branch.** The algebra \(B\) is a product of smooth affine hyperbolas, since each relation is \((x_i+y_i)(x_i-y_i)=1\); hence \(B\) is normal. Let \(h\in G\) be the diagonal sign change. Then \(B^{\langle h\rangle}\) is normal (invariants of a normal domain under a finite group in characteristic zero) and finite over \(A\). It has fraction field \(F=K(r_\delta)\), so it is exactly the integral closure of the monogenic coordinate ring \(A[X]/(\Gamma_n)\). The quadratic cover \(\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A\) is étale away from the hyperplanes \(x_i=\pm1\), with inertia generated by one sign change along any such generic hyperplane. Its image in \(G/\langle h\rangle\) is nontrivial when \(n\ge3\), so the normalized cover has index-two ramification there, and no other codimension-one ramification. In particular, the additional discriminant factors arising from pairs and proper cycles are collision/non-normality components, not new branch components of the normalized extension. \(\square\)

## Verification
The included standard-library script `verify.py` computes discriminants directly from roots and compares them with the all-subset product using exact arithmetic modulo the prime \(1000000007\). It tests five deterministic samples for each \(m=2,3,4,5,6,7,8\), covering degrees \(2\) through \(128\), and separately tests the key identity \(P_S=(\Gamma_S\Gamma_S^-)^2\) five times for every \(|S|\) from three to eight. All 65 test cases pass. The integral identity and Galois/normality results are proved above for arbitrary \(n\), not inferred from those finite tests.

The initial cases are
\[
 \operatorname{Disc}_X\Gamma_3=4(1-x_2^2)(1-x_3^2),
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{Disc}_X\Gamma_4=4096\prod_{i=2}^4(1-x_i^2)^2\prod_{2\le i<j\le4}(x_i^2-x_j^2)^2.
\]

## Relationship to prior work
Laurent–Mascarin–Telen [1] identify the hypersurface, prove monicity and give its complete algebraic root representation in Corollary 3.7, as well as a smaller-cycle resultant identity in Theorem 3.12. Those are indispensable premises. Their inspected Section 3 does not state the present all-subset discriminant factorization, its precise multiplicities, the fixed-field Galois group, or the distinction between discriminant components and normalized branch components. The new calculation organizes differences of all pairs of source roots into nontrivial bipartitions and identifies the resulting factors with every smaller signed cycle polynomial. Bel-Afia–Meroni–Telen [2] study general Chebyshev varieties and singular loci; the accessible abstract does not resolve the specific discriminant and branch comparison, so priority beyond the inspected material remains provisional.

## Limitations
This is a statement over \(\mathbb C\) for monic cycle polynomials and the projection forgetting one cycle coordinate. It is not a formula for the total multivariate singular scheme, the scheme-theoretic conductor, or real semialgebraic branch geometry. Full historical-priority verification against all specialized Chebyshev/Lissajous sources, including possible supplements, is not claimed. Finite-field tests validate polynomial identities at sampled points but do not certify irreducibility or infinite identities; those claims rely on the algebraic proof and cited primary properties.
