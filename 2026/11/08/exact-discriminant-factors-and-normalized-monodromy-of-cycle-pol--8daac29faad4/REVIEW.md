# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof starts with the monic algebraic root formula in Laurent–Mascarin–Telen, separates the independent quadratic radical extension from its diagonal fixed field, partitions all unordered root pairs by the difference support modulo complement, and obtains the exact scalar and every exponent via squared cosine differences. The factor identities for singleton, pair and larger subsets are proved. Normalization uses the explicit smooth multiquadratic cover and characteristic-zero invariants; its codimension-one inertia calculation is separate from discriminant multiplicities. Sixty-five deterministic finite-field checks (35 full identities and 30 component identities) independently corroborate the formulas, but they are not treated as infinite proofs.

**Originality — PASS, provisional priority.** The closest primary source [1] gives the individual roots and resultant recurrence. Its Section 3, inspected through the proofs of the root formula and resultant theorem, does not provide the complete discriminant decomposition, the Galois normalization description, or the intrinsic-versus-mongenic branch distinction. Semantic searches of available research records for aliases and stronger invariants and a claim-level comparison with accessible historical records produced no equivalent theorem. Root formulas enable the calculation but do not automatically state or subsume the nontrivial all-pair orbit factorization. The related Chebyshev-variety source [2] was only available as an abstract for this comparison, leaving historical priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** Discriminant components and their multiplicities are natural enumerative data for finite projections. The theorem gives a sharp, uniformly indexed decomposition in unbounded dimension and separates field ramification from collisions caused by nonnormality, information relevant to projection geometry and elimination methods. The transition to a square discriminant for every \(n\ge4\) is a structural consequence rather than a low-dimensional numerical increment.

**Scientific limitations.** The proof works over \(\mathbb C\) and establishes only codimension-one branching of the normalized projection, not its conductor or complete singular scheme. Independent validation and exhaustive literature priority remain unperformed.
