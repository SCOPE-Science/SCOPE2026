# Scientific review: nearest-vertex squared-distance fluctuations on regular simplices

## Correctness — PASS
The normalized domain is a nondegenerate regular \((m-1)\)-simplex with \(m\ge2\) vertices, circumradius \(R>0\), and a point uniformly sampled by Euclidean volume. The barycentric identity reduces squared nearest-vertex error exactly to a linear combination of the largest coordinate and the sum of squared coordinates. Dirichlet integration yields the quadratic moments, while a translated-simplex inclusion-exclusion argument yields the mixed cubic moment. The reported covariance, variance, strict positivity, and large-dimensional results follow with the stated normalizations. The Gumbel argument uses normalized independent exponentials and both law-of-large-numbers and maximum-limit estimates. The variance limit is independently obtained from the exact formula, not inferred merely from weak convergence.

Verification evidence: the provided `verify.py` integrates the same mixed cubic moment independently over ordered barycentric coordinates using exact rational Dirichlet monomials for all integer \(2\le m\le16\). It checks the two smallest cases and direct simulations. Finite checks do not certify all dimensions; the analytic proof does.

## Originality — PASS (subject to historical priority)
Onn and Weissman, *Annals of Operations Research* 189 (2011), first online May 19, 2009, Section 2 and Remark 3, derive the first two moments of the *maximum uniform spacing*, but not the joint moment with quadratic barycentric mass or nearest-vertex distortion variance. Conway and Sloane, *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 28 (1982), derive polytopal centroidal second moments and lattice Voronoi quantization results, not this fixed finite vertex code's error variance. Pyke's classical spacing survey is background. Restle (1999) studies dependence of products of uniform spacings; only its official repository abstract was accessible, so possible methodological overlap remains an explicit priority risk. Kabluchko, Litvak and Zaporozhets (arXiv:1511.08479, 2015) study random one-dimensional projection widths and a different Gumbel limit. Relevant published comparison records concern simplex hyperplane sections, polytope extremal geometry and lattice/vertex constructions, not the mixed Dirichlet-statistic formula. A full historical proof of priority is not asserted.

The new contribution under review is the exact positive mixed covariance, finite-dimensional variance and geometric error fluctuation law taken together, not the marginal moments of a maximum, Dirichlet basics, or the general Gumbel theorem.

## Value — PASS
For a finite regular-simplex vertex code, the mean squared error alone does not quantify uncertainty around the average distortion. Its exact variance is a natural second risk statistic; the positive but slowly decaying correlation explains why the largest barycentric weight cannot be treated as independent of radial position. The non-Gaussian limit and exact \(m^{-2}\)-variance scale identify the correct high-dimensional fluctuation regime. These provide a complete all-dimensional answer to this specific, motivated dispersion question rather than a recomputation of a previously known mean.

## Closest sources and limitations
The most relevant source inspected in material detail is the full author-uploaded text of Onn–Weissman. The complete Conway–Sloane article is accessible as a scanned PDF; the geometric quantization and centroidal-moment sections were checked, with no finite-simplex nearest-vertex variance identified. Pyke's Berkeley survey text was inspected. Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets was checked through its arXiv HTML full-text sections on width and Gumbel behavior. Remaining risk: obscure quantization and maximal-spacing work may contain an equivalent mixed moment. The claim does not cover nonregular simplices, alternate quantizers or finite-sample estimation.
