# Scientific comparison and limitations

**Correctness (PASS).** A Legendre polynomial identity reduces the sign-corrected Delannoy value to a single function of \(N=n(n+1)\). Its first two Taylor coefficients vanish exactly by the 2-adic logarithm and dilogarithm identity; a uniform bound makes all normalized summands beyond index 16 even. Two independent exact-rational calculations verify the remaining 14 coefficients, showing that the cubic coefficient is a unit and all higher coefficients are even. A direct modulo-eight check covers the complementary stratum. The proof covers every positive integer, and the included verifier checks all 600 first cases without extrapolation.

**Originality (PASS, historically qualified).** Lengyel 2021 explicitly states the matching special cases as unproved Conjectures 2 and 3 (his Section 2); the stated all-index result proves both, and his Conjecture 1. His Theorem 3 only supplies a weaker lower bound. Sun 2010 and 2016 principally address weighted sums, identities and odd-prime congruences, while Mikić 2023 determines the 3-adic valuation of \(D_n\), not the 2-adic valuation of \(D_n-(-1)^n\). The 2026 Legendre-valuations paper is concerned with polynomial values at primes, not this difference. Semantically nearby published research records concern other divisibility phenomena. The exact matching earlier claims were explicitly labeled conjectures by Lengyel (2021), rather than theorems; no later proof was located by targeted searches. A hidden equivalent historical formulation remains possible; priority has not been independently certified.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The result settles Lengyel 2021 Conjectures 1–3 and is a complete sharp arithmetic classification for a standard lattice-path sequence, explaining an unanticipated cubic ramification at \(n(n+1)=0\). The unusually strong cancellation is structural (the 2-adic dilogarithm vanishes at 2), not an arbitrary numerical slice or a routine divisibility bound. It provides exact orders in every 2-adic index stratum.

**Limits.** No conclusion for odd primes, full residue images or representation frequencies. The finite certificate proves a bounded coefficient identity; the infinite theorem additionally requires the displayed uniform tail estimate and analytic cancellations.
