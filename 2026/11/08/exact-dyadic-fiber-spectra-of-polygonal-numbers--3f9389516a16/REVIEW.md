# Scientific assessment: dyadic polygonal fibers

## Correctness — PASS

The displayed polygonal polynomial is integer-valued for every integral index. The even-order cases reduce respectively to a parity-unit difference factor and to an affine transform of the standard square map on the full ring modulo \(2^k\). For odd order, the exact factorization of twice a value difference distinguishes same-parity impossibility from opposite-parity collisions; reducing the latter modulo \(2^{k+1}\) forces an actual index-sum equality, with the unique reflection center determined by the inverse of \(s-2\). Counting the disjoint reflection pairs establishes every stated fiber count, image size, and extremal criterion. Standard square-root valuation counts complete the even-divisible-by-four case. The separate checker enumerates \(1253\) parameter pairs and verifies \(186092\) inputs; it does not substitute for the proof at arbitrary parameters.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

The closest triangular-residue paper (Ulness 2022) contains the \(s=3\) permutation case; that specialization is expressly not claimed as new. The complete quadratic-congruence solution of Dehnavi et al. (2019) can answer individual target equations and contains the classical square-root counts used here, but does not give the all-target half-period polygonal image spectrum or the inverse-governed odd-order reflection census. For odd order, the associated integral quadratic equation modulo \(2^{k+1}\) counts roots across twice the index domain; selecting the first half creates the new collision classification. The generalized polygonal representation paper of Bandyopadhyay–Baruah (2024) concerns additive sums, not individual modular images. Rivest's classical full-integral-polynomial permutation criterion is a related general tool, but is not this uniform image-fiber classification for integer-valued half-integral polynomials. Targeted database, source and prior-ledger searches found no dominating result. The complete Ulness text was not retrievable in the search session; indexed relevant portions were inspected, and this remains a bibliographic risk. Absence from search is not a proof of publication priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem settles an intrinsically natural saturation and collision question for every polygonal order at every dyadic scale, rather than testing isolated numerical values. Its main new structural feature is the full odd-order spectrum, with an exact modular-inverse formula for the number of missing and double residues. It also identifies all residue-permutation shapes and those that cover exactly half the residues twice. The result is self-contained and supplies an explicit collision pairing; it does not purport to solve additive polygonal-number representations or arbitrary composite moduli.

## Closest literature, limitations and risks

Dehnavi et al. (2019), DOI 10.7546/nntdm.2019.25.1.75-83, is the strongest general root-count source and provides known local ingredients. Ulness (2022), DOI 10.3390/appliedmath2030020, covers the triangular specialization. Bandyopadhyay and Baruah (2024), arXiv:2409.02023, treats generalized polygonal sums. Rivest (2001), DOI 10.1006/ffta.2000.0282, characterizes usual polynomial permutations. The novel classification could conceivably have an unindexed equivalent.
