# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The identities for the four classes modulo four are exact factorizations of the fixed cubic binomial polynomial. Every auxiliary chart has odd linear coefficient and all higher coefficients even in \(\mathbb Z_2\), so its divided difference is a unit and it permutes every power-of-two residue ring. This gives the exact branch-by-branch fiber counts. The half-period counterexample proves minimality and the compatible cylinder law proves the Haar density. The standalone verifier checked the predicted counts independently for levels one through thirteen. The scope is exactly the column \(\binom n3\) at powers of two; there is no reliance on finite extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority qualified)

Sun–Zhang [1], in Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2, proves p-adic surjectivity for fixed lower binomial index three; that known conclusion is not a new contribution. The complete \(5\)-versus-\(1\) fiber law, least period, and normalized Haar density are not specified there, nor logically determined by mere surjectivity. Barat–Grabner [2] treats the distribution of a full two-index Pascal-triangle family in the main results of Sections 3 and 4, not the fixed column with lower index three. Targeted published-literature and mathematical database comparisons did not identify a stronger covering claim. Historical uniqueness cannot be certified; an unlocated equivalent or derivation in the literature is the remaining risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete exact classification of a natural distribution invariant of the fixed binomial coefficient sequence: every residue, its multiplicity, the least period, and the continuous two-adic limiting measure. It is motivated by the already-established fixed-column p-adic surjectivity question and exposes a sharp contrast between that surjectivity and nonuniformity. The first two columns are uniform, so the third is the first nonuniform example; this is not merely a normalization correction or a numerical table. The four-coordinate decomposition is structurally informative for other integer-valued polynomial distributions, without claiming a further theorem about them.
