# Scientific review: exact hypercube edge-clearance law

## Correctness — PASS

Let the cube have side \(s\) and half-side \(h=s/2\). The normalized distances to the nearest faces are independent uniform \([0,1]\) variables. An edge leaves one coordinate free, so the normalized squared distance to the edge union is exactly the sum of the \(d-1\) smallest squared face gaps. The order-statistic joint density \(d!\) gives an integral with factor \(1-u_{d-1}\). For \(t\le1\), the radius truncation is wholly inside the unit coordinate cube, giving the exact two-term polynomial. For \(1<t<\sqrt2\), the only newly clipped part has its largest coordinate above one; its remaining coordinates fit inside the unit range, permitting an exact one-dimensional ball-section integral. A beta integral yields the positive leading term \(K_d(t-1)^{(d+2)/2}\), proving the specified differentiability order. Explicit integration of the ordered pair gives the complete \(d=3\) law. A rescaled simplex integral at the cube center proves the upper-tail coefficient; subtracting the squared maximum of independent uniforms from their squared sum proves the exact second moment. The cubic polynomial and full-dimensional end behavior were independently checked numerically. ## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority risk

The low-radius \(d=3\) polynomial is a classical three-cylinder union calculation and is expressly **not** a novelty claim. The relevant new statement is the complete three-dimensional finite-segment distribution together with an exact all-dimensional first-clipping formula, its sharp singular exponent and a sharp universal upper-tail constant. Tong and Pan (arXiv:1602.03407v1; full HTML inspected) derive two-point distances in planar polygons, not distance to a fixed one-skeleton. Mathai, Moschopoulos and Pederzoli (1999; abstract inspected) treat random pairs inside a cube. Bourke's 2003 perpendicular-cylinder formula is related to the admitted classical low-radius term. The Hildebrand–Kong–Turner–Uppal report, catalogued as 2012, could not be read completely and is a residual risk. Mathematical-record comparison of equivalent terms including *edge clearance*, *tubular neighborhoods*, *hypercube skeleton*, *Steinmetz cylinders*, *distance distribution*, and published cubical invariant claims found no governing formula with the same objects and implications. This is a best-knowledge, not a certified, historical judgment.

## Value — PASS

The distance to a finite one-skeleton measures Euclidean clearance from an edge framework, a natural intrinsic metric statistic of cubical geometry. The scale where nearest-edge tubular regions begin to be clipped by cube faces is geometrically canonical, not an arbitrarily selected cutoff. The exact nonanalytic exponent \((d+2)/2\) and coefficient distinguish dimensions, and the support endpoint law isolates an opposite, center-dominated geometric regime. The complete three-dimensional law is a compact, self-contained fundamental example. These results are more than restatements of the previously known short-radius polynomial or of the elementary second moment.

## Limitations and closest literature

The cited source date is a precisely verified public arXiv submission date (21 January 2016) for an independently relevant predecessor, not a date of discovery of these results. The original dates of the older cylinder texts were established at only month/year or year precision. No claim is made to new cylinder-union volumes in the untruncated regime. The full primary texts for two older plausible sources were unavailable. An earlier paper may contain an equivalent integral or a more general tube formula; novelty remains provisional. The proof covers \(d\ge3\) and a uniform random point in a cube, not arbitrary orthotopes or nonuniform distributions. A full CDF for \(d>3\) past the first clipping interval remains open in this treatment.
