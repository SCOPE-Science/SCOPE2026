# Research review

## Correctness — PASS

The new statement specifies the graph, full-dimensional polar, dual root lattice and Euclidean volume normalization. Every integer potential either has total range at most the dilation or has a nonedge as its unique extreme pair. Because the missing graph is a star, the latter forces the distinguished vertex to be the minimum or maximum; fixed overshoot contributes exactly a binomial-difference factor times a core-coordinate power. Disjointness of the overshoot levels and sign classes is proved. Leading coefficients and the root-coordinate Gram determinant give the Euclidean volume. The matching comparator is independently re-derived, and a strict termwise inequality proves the all-dilation comparison. The finite checker rebuilds the original edge-difference inequalities and (optionally) the halfspace polytope directly.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority limits

Primary full-text comparisons covered the explicit bipartite **primal** Ehrhart result of Higashitani–Jochemko–Michałek (2018), the metric/polar connections and graph constructions of D'Alì–Delucchi–Michałek (2019), and the general polar bounded-cut equivalence of D'Alì–Juhnke-Kubitzke–Koch (2023). None of the inspected applicable statements supplies the evaluated all-dilation star-deletion formula or its strict comparison with the same-size deleted matching. The existing matching formula is not relabeled as new: it is proved again only as the comparator. Generic bounded-cut correspondences do not evaluate the two-parameter sum. Remaining risk: an independent graph-height-function or chordal clique-amalgam counting result may be equivalent, and priority has not been exhaustively established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The studied graph is the smallest nontrivial dense chordal clique-amalgam where missing edges share a vertex, in contrast to independent missing edges. Its exact polar Ehrhart polynomial governs all dilations and an intrinsic geometric volume. The multiplicative overshoot term exhibits a genuinely nonadditive interaction among removed edges; the strict inequality quantifies the effect of concentrating an equal deletion budget at one vertex. For two deleted edges it gives a complete ordering of the only two graph isomorphism classes, for every dimension and every positive dilation. This is a natural, reusable comparison of geometric lattice invariants rather than an arbitrary numerical slice or a reparameterized matching count.

## Verification and limitations

The standalone checker exactly enumerated 32 integer instances, checked 45 Ehrhart numerator and leading-coefficient profiles, proved 175 finite strict comparison profiles by exact computation, and independently checked six coordinate hull volumes. These tests support but do not replace the symbolic all-parameter argument. The theorem does not assert maximality among all missing-edge graphs, gamma-positivity, or independently certified novelty.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
