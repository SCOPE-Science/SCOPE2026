# Review of the entrance-time census for cyclic majority dynamics

## Correctness — PASS
For labelled cyclic rings of length \(n\ge3\) with synchronous radius-one majority, the equal-bond/disagreement-bond encoding is exact. Equal bonds never disappear. A disagreement-bond gap of length \(g\) decreases by two each update until it is of length zero or one; a fully alternating ring is an exceptional initial two-cycle. The resulting formula \(T=\lfloor g/2\rfloor\) gives an exact time, not merely an upper bound. The even-disagreement-parity restriction and two-to-one vertex lift justify the transfer-matrix trace, including the distinct addition of two already-periodic alternating configurations. The extremal and logarithmic random-time deductions follow from that same encoding. The accompanying standalone checker directly simulated 524280 states and agreed with the bond rule and cumulative transfer census. These finite checks do not replace the proof for arbitrary \(n\).

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with limitations)
Fuks (1997) supplies the classical rule and special-preimage timing arguments used in density classification, and older threshold theory supplies the fact that eventually only period one or two occurs. Inokuchi–Sato (2000) consider fixed boundary, not the cyclic ring. Tosic–Agha (2004) and Tosic (2016) concentrate on types and counts of fixed/recurrent configurations; the fixed-state count alone is not novel. Nakar–Ron (2022) give general-radius structural restrictions on recurrent configurations but not, in the material inspected, labelled exact time-indexed entrance distributions. The new object is the all-time trace formula, its exact population of maximizers and typical-time law; no one-size numerical table or known period-two theorem implies these counts. The nearest published-results database matches concerned rule 128, parity automata, quadratic reversible rules and temporal trace complexity, rather than this dynamical invariant. Historical priority remains uncertain for unindexed exact transient enumerations.

## Mathematical value — PASS
An exact full first-entry census for a standard nonlinear cellular automaton is a natural complete classification. It separates a worst-case linear transient of \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\) from the logarithmic settling time of a random starting state, while specifying the exact rarity \(2n/2^n\) of maximum-duration states. It also handles the phase distinction between fixed configurations and the special initial alternating two-cycle. The transfer formula is useful for arbitrary finite ring sizes and all temporal cutoffs, not a small computational example.

## Sources and risks
Primary materials inspected include Fuks, arXiv:comp-gas/9703001 (1997); Inokuchi–Sato, DOI 10.5109/13492 (2000); Tosic–Agha, ACRI (2004); Tosic's open 2016 manuscript on fixed-state counting; and Nakar–Ron, arXiv:2205.08972 (2022). The 2022 paper shares the exact cyclic object but addresses spatial structure of recurrent states, leaving the proposed finer time distribution unresolved by its stated theorems. The 2016 paper is a material overlap at time zero and is not treated as independent novelty. There remains a residual possibility of equivalent findings in unindexed sources.
