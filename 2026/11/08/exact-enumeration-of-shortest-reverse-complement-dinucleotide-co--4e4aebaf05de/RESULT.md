# Exact enumeration of shortest reverse-complement dinucleotide covering cycles

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma=\{A,C,G,T\}\), with Watson–Crick complementation \(\bar A=T\), \(\bar T=A\), \(\bar C=G\), and \(\bar G=C\). Define the reverse complement of a dinucleotide by \(\rho(ab)=\bar b\bar a\). An indexed circular word \(w=w_0\cdots w_{L-1}\) is *reverse-complement complete of order two* if, for every \(ab\in\Sigma^2\), at least one of \(ab\) and \(\rho(ab)\) occurs among its cyclic windows \(w_iw_{(i+1)\bmod L}\).

The known shortest length is \(L=10\). **There are exactly \(544\) shortest cycles modulo cyclic rotation**, or exactly \(272\) after additionally identifying a cycle with its full-word reverse complement. If the eight alphabet permutations commuting with Watson–Crick complementation are also allowed, there are exactly \(37\) equivalence classes: \(31\) of size \(16\) and \(6\) of size \(8\) in the original \(544\)-cycle population.

All \(544\) lexicographically least rotations appear in `catalog.txt`. For example, `AACAGCCGAT` is an optimal cyclic word.

## Assumptions and scope

Words are circular, not linear probes: the final-to-first dinucleotide is included. Alphabet labels are initially fixed. When counting \(544\), two words are equivalent only if they differ by rotation, not by reversal, complement, or relabeling. When counting \(272\), full-word reverse complementation is added. When counting \(37\), full-word reverse complementation and all letter permutations \(\pi\) with \(\pi(\bar a)=\overline{\pi(a)}\) are added; there are eight such permutations.

The claim is a finite classification for exactly the DNA alphabet and dinucleotide windows, not an assertion about longer window lengths, linearized molecules or biochemical behavior.

## Proof

**Ten required orbit classes and a sharp lower bound.** The sixteen dinucleotides split into four fixed points of \(\rho\), namely `AT`, `TA`, `CG`, `GC`, and six two-element pairs:

| Pair | Alternatives |
|---|---|
| 1 | `AA`, `TT` |
| 2 | `AC`, `GT` |
| 3 | `AG`, `CT` |
| 4 | `CA`, `TG` |
| 5 | `CC`, `GG` |
| 6 | `GA`, `TC` |

Every cyclic window represents just one orbit, hence every complete cycle has length at least ten. The exhibited ten-letter word reaches the bound. In any ten-letter optimum, each orbit is therefore visited exactly once, so the ten cyclic dinucleotides are distinct, comprise all four fixed points, and choose exactly one edge from each of the six alternatives. There are exactly \(2^6=64\) candidate edge sets.

**Graph reduction.** Regard the chosen dinucleotides as distinct directed edges on the four nucleotide vertices. A length-ten cyclic word with exactly these windows is precisely a cyclic Eulerian ordering of the ten edges. For each of the \(64\) choices, let \(d^+(v)\), \(d^-(v)\) be the vertex degrees. Balance \(d^+(v)=d^-(v)\) is necessary. Exactly \(24\) choices are balanced, and each is connected in the Eulerian sense: its directed in-arborescence count is positive. The other \(40\) choices contribute no cycles.

For one balanced choice, form \(L=D^+-M\), where \(M_{uv}\) counts selected edges from \(u\) to \(v\), and \(D^+\) is the diagonal outdegree matrix. The directed matrix–tree theorem gives the common rooted in-arborescence count \(t=\det L^{(T)}\), deleting the row and column of `T`. The directed BEST theorem gives exactly \(t\prod_{v\in\Sigma}(d^+(v)-1)!\) cyclic edge-orderings. The values of this product, determined by the six binary choices and a three-by-three integer determinant, are exhaustive:

| Balanced edge sets | In-arborescences \(t\) | Factorial product | Cyclic tours per set |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 3 | 12 | 36 |
| 8 | 3 | 4 | 12 |
| 8 | 5 | 4 | 20 |

Consequently the number of shortest cycles modulo rotation is

\[8\cdot36+8\cdot12+8\cdot20=544.\]

No smaller rotational period is possible: any such period would repeat a dinucleotide orbit, contrary to the fact that all ten orbits occur once. Hence no adjustment for nonprimitive cycles is needed. `verify_graph.cpp` implements all sixty-four choices, the degree test, the determinant, and the BEST factors directly, without using the enumerated word catalog.

**Reverse-complement quotient.** Every optimum chooses exactly one edge of each nonfixed orbit. Whole-word reverse complementation exchanges the chosen edge with the unchosen one in every such orbit. Therefore no optimum can be rotationally equal to its reverse complement. The \(544\) rotation classes form disjoint pairs, yielding \(544/2=272\) physical strand-unoriented classes.

**Nucleotide-symmetry quotient.** The group generated by the eight complement-preserving nucleotide relabelings and whole-word reverse complementation has order \(16\). On the \(544\) rotation classes, its identity fixes all \(544\). Exactly two additional operations have fixed points: the relabelings with images `CATG` and `GTAC` in `ACGT` order, each followed by full-word reverse complementation, fix \(24\) cycles apiece. Every other group element fixes zero. Burnside's formula therefore gives

\[\frac{544+24+24}{16}=37.\]

Explicit orbit partition of the complete catalog gives \(31\) orbits of cardinality \(16\) and \(6\) of cardinality \(8\), confirming both Burnside's count and \(31\cdot16+6\cdot8=544\). The fixed-point and orbit checks are implemented in `verify_direct.py` and require no graph-enumeration results.

## Verification

Use Python 3.10 or later and a C++17 compiler. In the directory containing the four supplied verification artifacts:

```sh
python3 verify_direct.py
g++ -std=c++17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic verify_graph.cpp -o verify_graph
./verify_graph
```

`verify_direct.py` independently examines all \(4^{10}=1,048,576\) rooted ten-letter words, tests every circular dinucleotide orbit, reduces qualifying strings to their lexicographically least rotations, checks the complete `catalog.txt` list, and independently verifies the two symmetry quotients. It reports `DIRECT_VERIFY_OK` with \(5440\) qualifying rooted strings, \(544\) rotational classes, \(272\) strand-unoriented classes, and \(37\) total symmetry classes. `verify_graph.cpp` reports `GRAPH_VERIFY_OK`, with \(24\) balanced, connected edge selections and the total \(544\). The independent graph- and word-based procedures have different enumeration spaces and do not share the catalog as an input.

## Relationship to prior work

Orenstein and Shamir (2013) studied shortest double-stranded DNA sequences covering all k-mers up to reverse complement, established optimal sequence construction, and explicitly asked for the number of different optimal reverse-complement-complete sequences. Their Table 1 reports minimum length ten for dinucleotide windows but not this cycle enumeration. The present classification answers its simplest nontrivial four-nucleotide cyclic instance. It does not claim the length result or the BEST and matrix–tree tools as new.

Schmidt and Alanko (2023) study minimum plain-text representations of general k-mer sets without repetitions, using bidirected de Bruijn graphs. Their optimization over sets of output strings does not evaluate the population of shortest, single circular DNA words here. This calculation is a finite application of classical directed Euler-tour enumeration, with its own full word-based cross-check.

## Limitations

The classification is for the circular length-ten, order-two DNA setting only. It supplies no all-order formula and does not solve Orenstein and Shamir's enumeration question for arbitrary window sizes. A linear length-ten word has only nine non-wrapping dinucleotide windows and is not covered by this claim. Historical priority for an unindexed earlier small-instance census has not been independently established. Biological efficacy of any particular cyclic oligonucleotide is outside the mathematical model.
