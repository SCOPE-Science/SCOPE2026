# Exact enveloping-group coincidences for nonresonant prime-square Alexander quandles

## Finding
Let \(p\) be any odd prime, \(V=\mathbb F_p^2\), and let \(T\) be an invertible linear map satisfying \(\det(I-T)\ne0\). Write \(Q_T\) for the connected Alexander quandle with operation \(x\triangleright y=(I-T)x+Ty\). Its enveloping group is generated by symbols \(e_x\), for \(x\in V\), with relations \(e_xe_ye_x^{-1}=e_{x\triangleright y}\). Its inner automorphism group is generated by the bijections \(y\mapsto x\triangleright y\).

The natural **nonresonant locus** is \(\det T\ne1\). On this locus:

1. \(G(Q_T)\cong V\rtimes_T\mathbb Z\), and \(G(Q_T)\cong G(Q_U)\) precisely when \(T\) is similar to \(U\) or to \(U^{-1}\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).
2. There are exactly \(p^2-2p-1\) distinct isomorphism classes of these connected quandles, but exactly \(\frac{p^2-2p-1}{2}\) distinct isomorphism classes of their enveloping groups. Each enveloping-group type arises from exactly two mutually dual, nonisomorphic quandles.
3. \(\operatorname{Inn}(Q_T)\cong V\rtimes\langle T\rangle\). Two such inner groups are isomorphic exactly when \(\langle T\rangle\) and \(\langle U\rangle\) are conjugate cyclic subgroups of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\). This coarser invariant can fail to determine the enveloping group.

For an explicit instance over \(\mathbb F_5\), take
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\2&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
U=T^7=\begin{pmatrix}0&3\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both have order \(24\), both are connected and nonresonant, and their inner groups are the same subgroup of \(\operatorname{AGL}_2(\mathbb F_5)\). Yet their enveloping groups are not isomorphic.

For \(p=3,5,7,11\), the corresponding numbers of quandle types are \(2,14,34,98\) and of enveloping-group types are \(1,7,17,49\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
The prime is odd, the underlying additive group is the two-dimensional vector space \(V\), and \(I-T\) is invertible; the quandle is consequently connected. The nonresonant condition \(\det T\ne1\) is intrinsic: the central tensor term in the known adjoint-group structure theorem vanishes exactly there. The result does **not** assert equivalence of representation categories whenever their enveloping groups are nonisomorphic; an enveloping-group isomorphism is only a sufficient mechanism for the tensor-category coincidence studied in the motivating paper. Distinct quandle-isomorphism classes are understood without retaining a chosen basis of \(V\).

## Proof
**1. Exact nonresonance boundary.** Clauwens's adjoint-group theorem, applied to \(Q_T\), describes its enveloping group using the tensor cokernel \(S(V,T)=\operatorname{coker}(I-\tau)\), with \(\tau(v\otimes w)=Tw\otimes v\), over \(\mathbb F_p\). The tensor square is four-dimensional. If \(T=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\), the matrix of \(\tau\) in the ordered standard tensor basis is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0&0\\0&0&a&b\\c&d&0&0\\0&0&c&d\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct determinant expansion gives
\[
\det(I-\tau)=\det(I-T)(1-\det T).
\]
Since \(\det(I-T)\ne0\), the cokernel vanishes exactly when \(\det T\ne1\). When \(\det T=1\), the characteristic polynomial of \(\tau\) is \((X^2-\operatorname{tr}(T)X+1)(X^2-1)\); its root \(1\) is simple for odd \(p\), because \(\det(I-T)\ne0\). Therefore \(\dim S(V,T)=1\) on the complementary connected locus. Clauwens's multiplication then reduces, in the nonresonant case, to \(G(Q_T)\cong V\rtimes_T\mathbb Z\). Reversing the chosen generator of the infinite cyclic factor replaces \(T\) by \(T^{-1}\) and does not affect the conclusions.

**2. Abstract isomorphism detection.** Write \(G_T=V\rtimes_T\mathbb Z\). Its derived subgroup is
\[
[G_T,G_T]=(T-I)V=V,
\]
because \(T-I\) is invertible. Hence \(V\) is characteristic, and the abelianization is \(\mathbb Z\). Every isomorphism \(G_T\to G_U\) restricts to some \(A\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\) and induces multiplication by \(+1\) or \(-1\) on the infinite cyclic quotient. Compatibility with conjugation gives \(ATA^{-1}=U\) or \(ATA^{-1}=U^{-1}\). Conversely, either similarity yields an isomorphism of semidirect products, proving the equivalence. The known classification of connected Alexander quandles (Nelson's module criterion) says \(Q_T\cong Q_U\) exactly when \(T\) and \(U\) are similar.

**3. Counting similarity classes.** Every nonscalar \(2\times2\) matrix is cyclic; its similarity class is therefore determined by its characteristic polynomial, equivalently by its trace \(s\) and determinant \(d\). The admissible determinant has \(p-2\) choices \(d\in\mathbb F_p^\times\setminus\{1\}\), while the trace has \(p-1\) choices \(s\ne1+d\); each such pair gives one nonscalar companion-matrix class. Scalar classes \(T=aI\) are admissible precisely for \(a\in\mathbb F_p^\times\setminus\{1,-1\}\), giving \(p-3\) additional classes. Thus there are \((p-2)(p-1)+(p-3)=p^2-2p-1\) quandle types. If \(T\) were conjugate to \(T^{-1}\), writing \(d=\det T\) and \(s=\operatorname{tr}T\) would give \(d=d^{-1}\) and \(s=s/d\). Since \(d\ne1\), odd characteristic forces \(d=-1\) and \(s=0\); then \(\det(I-T)=1-s+d=0\), a contradiction. Thus inversion pairs all classes freely, yielding half as many enveloping-group types.

**4. Inner-group comparison and counterexample.** Connectedness means the translations arising from the maps \(y\mapsto (I-T)x+Ty\) exhaust \(V\). Hence \(\operatorname{Inn}(Q_T)=V\rtimes\langle T\rangle\). Its derived subgroup is \(V\), which is characteristic. As in step 2, an isomorphism of such finite semidirect products induces a matrix \(A\) on \(V\) and a generator-preserving automorphism on the cyclic quotient, so \(\langle T\rangle\) and \(\langle U\rangle\) must be conjugate. Conversely a conjugacy between these cyclic subgroups induces a group isomorphism. For the displayed matrices \(U=T^7\) and \(T\) has order \(24\), so \(\langle U\rangle=\langle T\rangle\). Yet \(\operatorname{tr}T=2\) and \(\det T=3\), whereas \(\operatorname{tr}U=1\) and \(\det U=2\). The inverse \(T^{-1}\) has trace \(4\) and determinant \(2\). Hence \(U\) is similar to neither \(T\) nor \(T^{-1}\), giving the claimed contrast.

## Verification
Run the standalone Python 3 program `quandle_dimension2_checker.py` with no external dependencies. It enumerates all \(2\times2\) matrices over primes \(3,5,7,11\), independently computes tensor-cokernel dimensions for the smaller primes, checks conjugacy against complete \(\operatorname{GL}_2\) conjugation orbits for \(p=3,5\), and verifies the explicit order-\(24\) collision. The recorded output is in `verification_output.txt`. These finite tests support but do not replace the proof for every odd prime.

## Relationship to prior work
Clauwens (2010), Theorem 1, gives a structural presentation of adjoint groups of **all** connected Alexander quandles; the semidirect decomposition here is a specialization, not claimed new. Nelson (2002), Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.4, classifies Alexander quandle isomorphism via module equivalence. Maassarani (October 2026) exhibits dual quandles with isomorphic enveloping groups and establishes the representation-category implication; its separate October 2026 paper proves equal cardinality for connected Alexander quandles with isomorphic enveloping groups, then constructs unequal-size examples outside that family. The contribution here is the **complete nonresonant prime-square isomorphism criterion, exact class count, sharp resonant boundary, and counterexample separating inner-group and enveloping-group information**. No conclusion that different enveloping groups force inequivalent abstract tensor categories is made.

## Limitations
The determinant-one connected stratum has a one-dimensional tensor obstruction and is intentionally not classified here. Characteristic two, non-connected quandles, modules of dimension greater than two, and arbitrary tensor equivalences not induced by enveloping-group isomorphisms are outside the claim. Historical priority could be affected by another treatment of metabelian semidirect groups or Alexander-quandle envelopes under different terminology. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
