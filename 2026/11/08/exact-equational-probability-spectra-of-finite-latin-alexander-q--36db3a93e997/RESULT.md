# Exact equational probability spectra of finite latin Alexander quandles

## Finding

Let \(p\) be prime, let \(V\) be a nonzero finite-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_p\), and choose \(T\in\operatorname{GL}_{\mathbb F_p}(V)\) with \(I-T\) invertible. Endow \(V\) with its single basic binary operation
\[
x*y=T x+(I-T)y.
\]
This is a finite latin Alexander quandle. Write \(R=\mathbb F_p[T]\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_p}(V)\). Its **equational probability spectrum** (for equations between terms using only the displayed binary operation and variables) is exactly
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{PSpec}(V,*)=\{|rV|^{-1}:r\in R\}.}
\]
In particular, every listed probability occurs already in an equation \(\tau_r(x,y)\approx y\) with a binary term \(\tau_r\).

For an explicit finite formula, factor the minimal polynomial of \(T\) as \(m_T(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}f_i(t)^{e_i}\), with the \(f_i\) distinct monic irreducible polynomials over \(\mathbb F_p\). Write the elementary-divisor decomposition of the \(\mathbb F_p[t]\)-module \(V\) as
\[
 V\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{h_i}\mathbb F_p[t]/(f_i^{\lambda_{ij}}),
 \quad 1\le\lambda_{ij}\le e_i,\quad\max_j\lambda_{ij}=e_i.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{PSpec}(V,*)=\left\{p^{-\sum_{i=1}^{s}(\deg f_i)\sum_{j=1}^{h_i}\max(\lambda_{ij}-a_i,0)}:\ 0\le a_i\le e_i\right\}.}
\]
This gives every attainable probability, including all possible intermediate values, directly from rational canonical data. The spectrum has its smallest possible size, \(\{1,|V|^{-1}\}\), if and only if \(m_T\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_p\). This latter minimality criterion is a specialization compatible with the general 2026 classification of minimal spectra; the principal claimed new content is the **complete attained-rank spectrum and binary-term realization** for all, including non-minimal, members of the family.

## Assumptions and scope

The field is exactly \(\mathbb F_p\); the parameter \(p\) can be any prime and \(\dim_{\mathbb F_p}V\ge1\). Both \(T\) and \(I-T\) are required to be invertible. There are no other basic operations, constants, inverse quasigroup operations, or scalar symbols in the term language. Each variable in an equation is assigned an independent uniform element of \(V\). Term variables may repeat. The value \(0\) cannot occur in this spectrum because all equations are homogeneous linear constraints, hence have the all-zero solution. The operation is idempotent, latin, and self-distributive: these follow from \(T+(I-T)=I\), invertibility of the two coefficients, and their commutativity.

## Proof

**1. All equations have probability \(1/|rV|\) for some \(r\).** Every term in variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) has the form \(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_jx_j\), where \(c_j\in R\) and \(\sum_j c_j=I\). This follows by induction from \(x*y=T x+(I-T)y\). For two terms, equality is equivalent to
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_jx_j=0,\quad d_j\in R,\quad\sum_jd_j=0.
\]
The image of this \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear map \(V^k\to V\) equals \(\sum_jd_jV\). Since \(R\cong\mathbb F_p[t]/(m_T(t))\) is a finite principal ideal ring, the ideal generated by all \(d_j\) has a single generator \(r\in R\); hence \(\sum_jd_jV=rV\). Every fiber of a homomorphism of finite abelian groups has equal cardinality, so the probability of zero is \(1/|rV|\). This proves one inclusion.

**2. Binary terms realize every coefficient of \(R\).** For a binary term \(\tau(x,y)\), there is a unique \(c\in R\) with \(\tau(x,y)=cx+(I-c)y\). Let \(C\) be the set of coefficients so obtained. The variable terms give \(0,I\in C\), and substitution into the operation gives closure under
\[
 a\diamond b=Ta+(I-T)b.
\]
Put \(U=I-T\). Because \(T,U\) are units of the *finite* commutative ring \(R\), the map \(F_b:C\to C\), \(F_b(a)=Ta+Ub\), is injective for every fixed \(b\in C\); finiteness makes it a permutation of \(C\). For any \(b,c\in C\), the composition \(F_cF_b^{-1}\) is translation by \(U(c-b)\), so the set \(C\) is invariant under translations by the additive subgroup \(U\langle C-C\rangle\).

Let \(H=\langle C-C\rangle\), an additive subgroup of \(R\). It contains \(I\), because \(0,I\in C\). Also, since \(F_0(a)=Ta\) permutes \(C\), the group \(H\) is preserved by multiplication by \(T\). Thus \(I,T,T^2,\ldots\in H\); taking integer, hence \(\mathbb F_p\), linear combinations shows \(H=\mathbb F_p[T]=R\). Because \(U\) is a unit, \(UH=R\). Therefore \(C\) is invariant under *every* additive translation of \(R\), and since \(0\in C\), we conclude \(C=R\). For every \(r\in R\), choose a binary term \(\tau_r\) with coefficient \(r\). The equation \(\tau_r(x,y)\approx y\) is exactly \(r(x-y)=0\) and has probability \(1/|rV|\), proving the reverse inclusion. This argument uses only finite-set bijectivity, not the existence of extra operation symbols.

**3. The exact primary-block formula.** In \(R\cong\mathbb F_p[t]/(m_T)\), every element has an \(f_i\)-adic valuation \(a_i\) truncated to the range \(0,\ldots,e_i\) in each Chinese-remainder factor. Every possible tuple \((a_1,\ldots,a_s)\) occurs, by the Chinese remainder theorem. On the cyclic summand \(\mathbb F_p[t]/(f_i^{\lambda_{ij}})\), multiplication by an element of valuation \(a_i\) has image of size \(p^{(\deg f_i)\max(\lambda_{ij}-a_i,0)}\). Multiplying these sizes over all summands gives the displayed exact set.

**4. Criterion for minimality.** If \(m_T\) is irreducible, \(R\) is a field, so every nonzero \(r\) is invertible and \(|rV|=|V|\), while \(r=0\) gives \(|rV|=1\). Conversely, if \(m_T\) is not irreducible, then \(R\) is not a field and has a nonzero nonunit \(r\). Since \(R\) acts faithfully on \(V\), \(rV\ne0\). But \(rV\ne V\): otherwise the finite linear operator \(r\) would be bijective and its inverse, a power of \(r\), would lie in \(R\), contradicting noninvertibility. Hence \(1<|rV|<|V|\), giving a strictly intermediate probability. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone \(\texttt{checker.py}\) independently enumerates the finite coefficient ring and closes binary-term coefficients under the defining operation. It then counts solutions of every binary-term equation \(\tau_r(x,y)\approx y\) for six finite-field representations. The program evaluates 27,318 binary assignments and finds no discrepancy. The six cases include a scalar action, a nonsemisimple Jordan block, a split semisimple action, an irreducible quadratic action in characteristic two, a repeated irreducible quadratic primary block, and mixed Jordan sizes. The generated \(\texttt{checker_output.txt}\) ends in \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\). The infinite-parametric classification is established by the four-step proof above, not by finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Cardó (March 2026, arXiv:2603.19487) defined and investigated minimal equational spectra of binary groupoids and classified semigroups, motivating further explicit quasigroup families. Cardó (April 2026, arXiv:2604.08446) studied general spectrum and primality phenomena. More decisively, Cardó, Kearnes, and Szendrei (October 27, 2026, arXiv:2610.37841), Theorems 2.1 and 3.5, classify **minimal** spectra in full generality, including algebras polynomially equivalent to vector spaces. Their Theorem 4.6 further proves that affine algebras have **finite** spectra by obtaining probabilities from images of module homomorphisms. That broad finite-spectrum argument overlaps with Step 1 and is credited. It does not state which image sizes are attainable by terms in this *single-binary-operation* reduct or the primary-block formula, nor does it establish the reverse inclusion via the finite affine-coefficient closure lemma. The claim of novelty rests on those precise constructive and complete-spectrum assertions, **not** on the general minimal-spectrum classification or on generic module structure theory. Related Alexander-quandle literature develops affine term and automorphism methods, without a covering exact probability-spectrum result identified in the inspected sources.

## Limitations

All claims are for latin Alexander quandles on finite prime-field vector spaces and their **single-operation** equational language. The result does not extend unqualified to nonlatin Alexander quandles, groups with non-elementary underlying additive structure, expansions by constants, or additional basic scalar/linear operations. Equal rank values from different valuation tuples are listed only once; the theorem is about the set of probabilities, not counts of equations. A general 2026 paper already classifies minimal spectra, so the minimality criterion is a specialization, not asserted as a new general classification. Possible historical anticipation of the reverse-inclusion result in clone theory remains an originality risk. Scientific review is same-model only; no independent audit has been performed.
