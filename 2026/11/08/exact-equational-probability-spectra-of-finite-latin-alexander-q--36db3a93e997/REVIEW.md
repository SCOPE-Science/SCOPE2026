# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

All polynomial coefficients are in the finite principal ideal ring \(R=\mathbb F_p[T]\). The proof constructs every coefficient of \(R\) as a binary term using finite injectivity of coefficient substitution, a translation subgroup, and the invertibility of both \(T\) and \(I-T\). It then shows that every equation is a homogeneous linear map whose image is one principal-ideal multiple of \(V\). Primary block lengths give the exact image-size formula. The proof also checks both implications of the minimal-spectrum condition, including faithfulness of the ring action. The independent checker closed term coefficients and counted 27,318 assignments across six test cases; finite cases only corroborate the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The March 2026 Cardó paper investigates minimal spectra of groupoids and does not supply the exact spectrum of latin Alexander quandles. The April 2026 Cardó paper provides general spectrum background. Crucially, the October 27, 2026 paper by Cardó–Kearnes–Szendrei already gives a **general classification of minimal spectra** (Theorem 3.5) and the general finite-spectrum fact for affine algebras (Theorem 4.6), including a broad linear-image calculation. These are recognized as covering earlier results; their converse minimality framework cannot itself count all intermediate probabilities. The proposed theorem additionally proves that every image size from a specific single-generator endomorphism ring is attained by binary terms and gives every value explicitly from elementary divisor data. Searches for exact/equivalent Alexander-quandle spectra and prior local claims did not uncover covering work. Remaining risk: preexisting clone descriptions might imply the coefficient-realization lemma without naming probability spectra.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural, infinite and extensively studied family of medial latin quasigroups is given a complete answer to a recently prominent equational-probability question. The result distinguishes irreducible, split and nonsemisimple linear actions, gives every intermediate probability (not just a finiteness statement), and includes a short constructive binary-term realization. The exact divisibility pattern can be used to compare linear actions with the same underlying size. The value is in full spectral classification; a restatement of the general minimal-spectrum criterion would not independently meet this bar.

The original 2026 groupoid paper and the subsequent October 2026 general classification are both part of the essential prior comparison.
