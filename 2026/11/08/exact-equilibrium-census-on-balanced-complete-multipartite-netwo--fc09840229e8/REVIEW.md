# Research review: exact equilibrium census on balanced complete multipartite networks

## Correctness — PASS

All players choose one of two actions and obtain one payoff unit from each opponent of the other action. Fixing part occupancies \(x_i\) and total \(X\), the single-player best-response inequalities are \(2(X-x_i)\leq(r-1)n\) for every action-\(1\) player and \(2(X-x_i)\geq(r-1)n\) for every action-\(0\) player. A mixed part forces equality; this proves the three-way case classification by the number \(t\) of mixed parts. Counting choices of mixed parts, monochromatic classes and labeled subsets yields the finite formula without overcounting. For three parts the only additional Nash family has each part half of each color, and exists only for even \(n\). Independent multilinearity proves the global maximum cut has exactly \(2n^2\) edges. Thus the welfare and parity-price-of-anarchy assertions are logically derived rather than extrapolated from small instances. The shipped standard-library verifier tests 34 part-occupancy instances, 17 full labeled-profile instances, and 11 additional tripartite formula instances and checks their welfare distribution. Boundaries \(n=1\), \(r=2\), and even/odd sizes are included. Risk: none identified within the stated symmetric, complete, equal-part model; numerical testing does not independently certify the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The central comparison is between entire implications, not terminology. The initiating Deligkas–Eiben–Gutin–Neary–Yeo study covers general binary coordination/anti-coordination polymatrix games and optimization complexity, but its inspected statement and theorem material does not provide the exact balanced-complete-multipartite Nash census or the tripartite parity law. Lampis relates Nash equilibria to locally maximal cuts and analyzes algorithmic complexity; general equivalence alone does not evaluate this family-specific counting problem. Cao–Yang discusses a maximum independent-cut problem for an asymmetric anti-coordination regime rather than this full homogeneous action-profile census.

The closest accessible published mathematical result is the separate classification of majority C-colorings of complete multipartite graphs: that demands at least half of the neighbors have the *same* color, while Nash anti-coordination here demands at least half have the *opposite* color. The earlier result counts the all-half-split colorings, a common boundary subfamily, but neither implies nor counts the large optimal Nash family \(6(2^n-1)\) on the tripartite graph. The accessible preceding complete-bipartite Nash census does cover the \(r=2\) specialization; the new result explicitly treats all \(r\ge3\), with a new tripartite parity-and-efficiency conclusion. Exact searches over aliases and candidate formulas found no statement dominating the new result. Some older and unindexed literature may be equivalent, and the original full Bramoullé (2007) article was not inspected, although a full later exposition of its mathematical framework was reviewed. No claim of absolute uniqueness or external priority certification is made.

## Value — PASS

The object is the canonical homogeneous two-action anti-coordination game on a natural dense multipartite network. Its equilibrium set is not determined by the max-cut optimum: local one-vertex stability allows a large extra inefficient family exactly at even part size. The result gives a complete all-size occupancy classification and a polynomial-summand exact enumerator for arbitrary numbers of equal parts. For three parts it gives an exact odd–even transition in worst Nash welfare, a constant inefficiency factor \(4/3\), and an exponential separation between efficient and inefficient equilibrium counts as the part size grows. These are meaningful structural and game-theoretic invariants, not a single arbitrary numerical slice or a recomputation of an existing table. This is an exact deterministic equilibrium statement; it does not predict which equilibrium a particular stochastic process reaches.
