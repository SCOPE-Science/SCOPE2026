# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The deterministic map reduces on \(x+y=c\) to a two-dimensional linear system with invariant \(bx+au\) and contracting eigenvalue \(1-a(1-c)-bc\); this gives the exact limiting equilibrium selection formula. Disintegration of a uniform four-coordinate simplex state gives a \(\operatorname{Beta}(2,2)\) pair mass and two independent uniform conditional proportions. Their conditional convolution has a positive-part formula, whose integral over the pair mass simplifies to the displayed rational density. The checker derives the same formula directly from symbolic polynomial antiderivatives, verifies endpoint and central identities, and numerically checks normalization and conditional convolution for four parameter pairs. Singular boundary-layer limits are direct uniform-on-compacts rational limits followed by integration. Finite checks corroborate but do not replace this proof.

## Originality — PASS within investigated literature

Diyorov–Rozikov, arXiv:2210.00247, §2, proves the deterministic limit on each invariant fiber; Omirov–Rozikov, arXiv:2409.19687, §2, generalizes the deterministic fiber approach. Neither states the pushforward probability law under a uniform random starting simplex. Roozegar, arXiv:1508.01731, studies Dirichlet-distributed random averaging weights and some beta laws. This model's weight \(bc/[a(1-c)+bc]\), where \(c\sim\operatorname{Beta}(2,2)\), is non-Dirichlet for unequal coefficients and yields a non-Beta density; the symmetric Beta special case is not claimed to be new. Additional exact-form and alias searches found no identical published formula. Homei (2012) was located in abstract form only, and general transformed-weight results or unindexed equivalent calculations could affect priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The equilibrium manifold is already known, but knowing that every initial point selects an equilibrium does not quantify how often different equilibria are selected by a geometrically neutral ensemble of starting states. The exact family provides that missing distribution, distinguishes genuinely asymmetric mixing from the symmetric Beta baseline, and identifies the noncommutation between a near-boundary selection event and a strongly asymmetric parameter limit. The \(1/\kappa\) scale and the exact crossover profile are useful for interpreting rare near-fixation states; no broader population-genetic universality is claimed.

## Constraints and remaining risks

This is a theorem for the named finite-dimensional deterministic model with the specified artificial but canonical uniform initial-state prior. It does not include finite-population genetic drift, stochastic time evolution, boundary coefficients, or independent publication-priority certification. The mathematically adjacent Homei paper could not be checked beyond its abstract. All algebraic computations are reproducible from the included standalone source.
