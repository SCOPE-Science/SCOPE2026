# Self-review

**Correctness: PASS.** The full claim is limited to integers \(k\ge2\) and the intrinsic Euclidean metric. The oriented top-Laplacian \(NI-ss^{\mathsf T}\) has balanced sign blocks; its centered columns give an explicit orthogonal free-sum isometry. All facet, ellipsoid, volume, polar and covariance assertions are derived, including the \(k=2\) boundary. The computation replays the primal and polar volumes and the actual second-moment triangulation; those checks are finite corroboration, not the infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS (provisional historical priority).** The closest primary work is Juhnke-Kubitzke and Köhne, arXiv:2301.11602: Theorem 3.2 provides the matrix, Theorem 5.3 counts the facets, Theorem 5.4 classifies combinatorial type, and Corollary 6.7 counts lattice-normalized volume. None of the relevant examined sections establishes the present intrinsic Euclidean free-sum/Santaló metric package or the evaluated Mahler product. Braun–Meyer, arXiv:1706.07085, treats graph Laplacian simplices rather than this top-dimensional simplicial-complex polytope. Known general simplex and free-sum identities are acknowledged; independent historical review might uncover an equivalent derivation.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The result supplies a complete all-dimensional affine/metric profile of a natural cyclic-polytope realization: an explicit Santaló-Mahler product, rigid John/Löwner contacts, isotropic interior-volume covariance in a non-centrally-symmetric family, and an unbounded \(\sqrt{k}\) comparison with the simplex product. These are precise, naturally motivated invariants not reported by the lead source. The work does not claim any global extremal theorem or solve the lead source's Ehrhart questions.

The most material residual risk is an unlocated earlier isometric free-sum identification; priority should not be treated as independently settled.
