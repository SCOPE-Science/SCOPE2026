The result concerns the explicit full-dimensional Minkowski sum of the \(n\) edge diamonds of a simple cycle, \(n\ge3\). The proof checks the norm normalization, the indicator-level representation of the support function, the equality conditions in the Euclidean triangle inequality, the exact cyclic coverage counts, the maximizing edge-assignment argument, and strict convexity for all farthest vertices. The two invariant ellipsoids are forced to be Euclidean balls by independent coordinate reflections and cyclic permutation. The explicit contact orbits are shown to expose facets of codimension one. That formula is acknowledged as prior-implied and not the novelty basis. The new mathematical claim is the simultaneous John–Löwner classification, the orientation-based farthest-point structure, and the exact full inward/outward contact counts for every cyclic edge-diamond sum. The source's full accessible discussion of geometric properties and later enumerative results does not state these. Earlier type-B generalized permutohedron studies by Eur et al. and Thawinrak concern lattice points and volume. Similar-looking parity and ellipsoid records for symmetric edge polytopes or root-direction zonotopes use different convex bodies, and neither their objects nor formulas contain these cyclic edge-diamond conclusions. ## Mathematical value — PASS
Cyclic edge-diamond sums are a canonical sparse, highly symmetric specialization of the new Minkowski-sum family. Their extremal Euclidean ellipsoids are natural affine/metric geometric invariants; the classification supplies both exact radii and *all* touching face orbits in every dimension, not a single numerical sample. The farthest-vertex count has a nontrivial even/odd split driven by maximum independent sets, and the inball facet contacts switch from a full sign orbit in dimension three to a mixed orbit in dimension four and only coordinate facets thereafter. The general assignment-to-order reduction provides a reusable combinatorial mechanism for other hypergraphs. This does not claim a new result for known subset inequalities alone.
