# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The facet equations used in the proof are precisely the full arbitrary-parameter system of Bayer et al., Theorem 2.3(c). The lower and upper halfspace distance conditions yield the sharp minimum over subset sizes. The proposed uniform center meets every inequality. At any active upper size, all coordinates of any optimal center are independently forced to the same value, proving uniqueness and exact tangencies. The purportedly redundant size cannot minimize when the first parameter equals one. For rank realization, a one-spike cumulative-sum family has a strictly unique differentiable minimum at any prescribed rank; small rational perturbations and scaling produce positive integral parameters. The proof covers all dimensions at least two and all allowed parameters, without treating finite numerical tests as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional
The full arbitrary-parameter facet and vertex theory of Bayer et al. and the earlier two-parameter theory of Hanada–Lentfer–Vindas-Meléndez were materially examined in open full text. A published two-parameter inball result has only singleton/full-set upper contact phases, which cannot imply realization of every intermediate rank by positive integer parameters. Liu–Thawinrak's later broader parking-polytope work treats faces, fans, enumerative and polymatroid geometry, not Euclidean inball radii. Targeted published literature and research-record searches found no full arbitrary-parameter all-rank inball classification. The usual finite-halfspace Chebyshev LP is credited as background. The residual risk is an equivalent result under symmetric polymatroids or ranked generalized permutahedra.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The radius is a canonical realization-dependent metric invariant of an actively studied combinatorial-polytope family. More importantly, the result gives a complete contact-stratum classification and establishes that every subset cardinality (including intermediate ranks impossible in the earlier two-parameter family) uniquely controls an insphere on an open parameter region containing positive integral examples. This is an all-dimensional geometric phase theorem, not a small parameter substitution or recomputation of a known table.
