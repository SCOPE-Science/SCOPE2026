# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof independently derives the Gaussian generating function from the characteristic polynomial of every real traceless three-by-three symmetric matrix. Extraction of even coefficients restricts the cubic contribution to even powers, so every coefficient of the discriminant polynomial is nonnegative; the linear term is positive exactly from exponent six onward. The real-root discriminant gives the exact interval and both equality orbits. The included exact-rational code checks direct monomial integration against the generating function and evaluates the critical rational constants. Risks: finite tests do not prove the all-order result; the analytic coefficient and discriminant steps are the required proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
A publicly available exact degree-two fourth-moment result proves the already-known flatness at exponent four, but its theorem contains no exponent six or higher and no two-sided all-even hierarchy. Stanton–Weinstein (1981) and Lu (1987) study quartic norms, while Sogge–Zelditch (2010), Han (2014), and Dai–Feng–Tikhonov (2014) study harmonic-degree asymptotics and do not establish fixed-degree-two exact even-moment constants. The known Pearson skewness–kurtosis relation implies an eigenvalue-parameter bound, not the cubic moment expansion and full harmonic optimizer classification. Remaining originality risk: equivalent all-order formulas may exist in unindexed invariant-theory or quadratic-form literature; full historical coverage cannot be certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result settles a natural sharp reverse Hölder problem simultaneously at every even exponent on the first nontrivial five-dimensional spherical harmonic eigenspace. It explains why the quartic norm fails to distinguish its elements, proves that the sixth norm already distinguishes two opposite geometric extremizers, and determines both sharp constants and all equality configurations for every subsequent even exponent. This is not a single parameter substitution or an isolated numerical computation.

## Scope and limitations
Only real harmonic degree two on the normalized two-sphere and integer even exponents are covered. Real non-even exponents, complex harmonics, and higher degrees are not claimed. The accessible published comparisons are not exhaustive of all historical literature.
