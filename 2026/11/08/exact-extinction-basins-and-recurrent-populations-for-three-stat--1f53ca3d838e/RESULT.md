# Exact extinction basins and recurrent populations for three-state excitation on cocktail-party graphs

## Finding
Let \(r\ge4\) and let \(G_r=K_{2,\ldots,2}\) be the complete \(r\)-partite graph with exactly two labelled vertices in every part (the complement of a perfect matching on \(2r\) vertices). Under deterministic synchronous three-state Greenberg–Hastings dynamics, every state has eventual least period \(1\) or \(3\), the unique fixed state is all zero, and the exact numbers of labelled initial states in the fixed-state and period-three basins are respectively
\[
 Z_r=3\cdot4^r-3+6r,\qquad B_r=9^r-3\cdot4^r+3-6r.
\]
The number of nonzero periodic states and the number of distinct primitive temporal three-cycles are respectively
\[
 P_r=9^r-3\cdot4^r+3-6r(4^{r-1}-1),\qquad C_r=P_r/3.
\]
No other nontrivial primitive periods occur. In particular, exactly \(6r(4^{r-1}-2)\) initial states are transient members of the period-three basin, and a uniformly random initial state goes extinct with probability
\[
 3(4/9)^r+(6r-3)/9^r.
\]
The oscillating probability tends to one exponentially quickly; the fraction of all configurations that are immediately periodic also tends to one. The asserted new domain starts at four parts: the tripartite case is excluded because an earlier complete-tripartite classification already covers it.

## Assumptions and scope
Write the vertex set as pairs \(V=\{a_1,b_1,\dots,a_r,b_r\}\), with every two distinct vertices adjacent except \(a_j,b_j\). Each vertex has a state in \(\{0,1,2\}\). In parallel at every integer time, states \(1\) and \(2\) change to \(2\) and \(0\), while state \(0\) changes to \(1\) exactly when at least one adjacent vertex is in state \(1\); otherwise it remains \(0\). All vertices are individually labelled. A periodic point satisfies \(F^p(x)=x\) for some \(p>0\); its least such \(p\) is its primitive period. Its basin consists of all initial states that eventually enter that temporal orbit class. A three-cycle has three distinct states, and cyclic time translations count as one orbit. Assertions concern every integer \(r\ge4\); no extrapolation from finite enumeration is used in the proof.

## Proof
**1. Triangles determine whether all excitations die out.** For a colouring \(x\), put an antisymmetric integer weight on oriented graph edges: \(\omega_x(u,v)=1\) for \((x(u),x(v))=(0,1)\), \(-1\) for \((1,0)\), and \(0\) otherwise. The finite-graph Greenberg–Hastings circulation theorem of Gravner, Lyu and Sivakoff (2016 preprint, Theorem 3, with its explicit GHM one-form) states that all vertices eventually synchronize if and only if every oriented graph cycle has circulation zero. If some cycle has nonzero circulation, the eventual excitation rate at every vertex is the maximum positive circulation divided by cycle length. This previously proved theorem is used here; the new work is the graph-specific reduction and exact labelled enumerations below.

In a complete multipartite graph, every chordless simple cycle is a triangle or a quadrilateral; a quadrilateral necessarily alternates between two parts. Every such quadrilateral is the oriented sum of the four triangles obtained by coning it to a vertex in any third part. Chord decomposition then shows that all integer edge circulations vanish on all cycles precisely when they vanish on all triangles. The circulation of a triangle under \(\omega_x\) is nonzero (necessarily \(\pm1\)) precisely when its three vertices carry all three distinct states. Call such a triangle *rainbow*. Thus a trajectory reaches the all-zero fixed state precisely when its initial colouring has no rainbow triangle. If there is one, a triangle supplies rate at least \(1/3\). A vertex can be excited at most once in three successive times, so the rate is at most \(1/3\). Hence the common eventual rate is exactly \(1/3\). On a finite periodic orbit, gaps between successive excitations are at least three; averaging to exactly three forces every gap to equal three. All vertices therefore advance by one modulo three at every eventual update. This proves that all nonzero periodic orbits have primitive period three. If the graph synchronizes, equal states necessarily evolve to the all-zero state; hence the all-zero state is the unique fixed point.

**2. Count the extinction basin exactly.** A rainbow triangle consists of one vertex of each colour chosen from three *different* matched pairs. For each matched pair \(j\), let \(S_j\subseteq\{0,1,2\}\) be the set of colours occurring in that pair; it has one or two elements. The existence of a rainbow triangle is precisely the existence of a system of distinct pair representatives for colours \(0,1,2\). By the three-element form of Hall's marriage theorem, such representatives fail to exist if and only if either at least one colour is wholly absent, or all colours are present but two of them occur within just one pair. Indeed the full set of three colours has at least \(r\ge4\) candidate pairs, each present colour has at least one, so the only remaining Hall obstruction is a two-colour subset with exactly one candidate pair. Because each pair contains two vertices, that exceptional pair has its two vertices in those two distinct colours, and every other pair is monochromatic in the third colour.

The number of colourings using at most two of the three colours is \(3\cdot2^{2r}-3=3\cdot4^r-3\), by inclusion–exclusion. The all-three-colour Hall obstructions number exactly \(3\cdot r\cdot2=6r\): choose the lone colour outside the exceptional pair, its location, and the order of its two other colours within the pair. The two classes are disjoint. Consequently \(Z_r=3\cdot4^r-3+6r\). Subtraction from the total \(3^{2r}=9^r\) yields \(B_r\).

**3. Count periodic points exactly.** A nonzero periodic point must rotate every colour by one at every update, so it uses all three colours. Conversely a surjective colouring rotates under the update exactly when each vertex of colour \(i-1\) has a neighbour of colour \(i\), for every \(i\) modulo three. As every vertex has exactly one non-neighbour (its matched mate), rotation can fail for colour \(i\) only when its colour class is a singleton \(\{v\}\) and \(v\)'s mate has colour \(i-1\). Call this a forbidden event. For each \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\) and labelled vertex \(v\), the other \(2r-2\) vertices use only the two remaining colours and must use colour \(i-2\) at least once; there are \(2^{2r-2}-1=4^{r-1}-1\) such assignments. Different forbidden events cannot coexist: if one colour \(i\) is a singleton and its mate carries \(i-1\), a forbidden event for \(i-1\) would force the same mate to be a singleton whose mate carries \(i-2\), contradicting the colour of \(v\); a forbidden event for \(i+1\) would force \(v\)'s mate to carry \(i+1\), also a contradiction. As there are only three colours, these cases cover every pair of distinct events.

Surjective three-colourings number \(3^{2r}-3\cdot2^{2r}+3=9^r-3\cdot4^r+3\). Subtracting the disjoint \(3(2r)(4^{r-1}-1)\) forbidden assignments gives \(P_r\). Every surviving colouring lies on a least-period-three orbit, hence \(C_r=P_r/3\). Subtracting \(P_r\) from \(B_r\) gives the stated exact transient population in the oscillating basin. The displayed extinction probability is \(Z_r/9^r\). Every count is for labelled configurations; no automorphism quotient is applied.

## Verification
Two standalone programs accompany this result. `census.cpp` enumerates the complete deterministic functional graph for \(r=2,3,4,5,6,7\), determines its eventual cycle lengths by indegree peeling, and checks the formulas for \(r=3,4,5,6,7\). `independent_check.py` uses separately written forward-trajectory memoization instead of indegree peeling and independently counts rainbow triangles for \(r=3,4,5\). Both were run and their exact output text is included. They agree on all tested basins and cycle populations. For instance, at \(r=4\) the extinction basin has \(789\) configurations and the oscillating basin \(5772\); there are exactly \(1428\) primitive three-cycles. At \(r=7\), the corresponding counts are \(49191\), \(4733778\), and \(1520610\). The calculations are finite cross-checks, not substitutes for the preceding all-integer proof. Reproduction: compile `census.cpp` with a C++17 compiler and run; separately run `python3 independent_check.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter.

## Relationship to prior work
Greenberg and Hastings (1978), and Durrett and Griffeath (1993), develop excitable dynamics. Gravner, Lyu and Sivakoff (arXiv 2016, Theorem 3) already prove the graph-general circulation criterion and activity formula. Doba (2018) independently develops a comparable finite-graph circulation approach. Those general dynamic implications are not claimed as new. The contribution asserted here is the exact all-size, individually labelled basin and recurrent-population formula for the cocktail-party family, including the Hall-obstruction correction \(6r\) and the disjoint singleton-mate correction \(6r(4^{r-1}-1)\). A previously established result covering all complete tripartite graphs includes \(r=3\), so the new scope explicitly begins at \(r=4\). Known complete-bipartite results (the two-part case) and parity-linear dynamics on multipartite graphs have different domains or update rules. No equivalent population formulas were located in the primary sources inspected; this is not a claim of exhaustive historical priority.

## Limitations
The graph is the complement of a perfect matching, every matched part has exactly two vertices, and only the deterministic three-state threshold-one rule is considered. The result does not give the full distribution of *first periodic-entry times*. The published circulation theorem is used as an input; the exact family-specific counting arguments are supplied in full. The transition at \(r=2\) is genuinely different (two primitive four-cycles on the ordinary four-cycle graph), and \(r=3\) was already encompassed by a prior complete-tripartite classification. Computer checks stop at \(r=7\) and do not prove the unlimited statement. A historically unindexed equivalent enumeration remains possible. No independent external audit has been performed.
