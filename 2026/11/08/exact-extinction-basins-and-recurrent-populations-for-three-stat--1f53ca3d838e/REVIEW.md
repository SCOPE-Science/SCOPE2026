# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed count is derived for every integer \(r\ge4\) by combining the published exact circulation criterion for the three-state Greenberg–Hastings rule on finite graphs with a graph-specific triangle spanning argument. The absence of rainbow triangles is equivalent to eventual extinction. A three-color Hall obstruction counts the extinction basin as \(3\cdot4^r-3+6r\). Rotating surjective colorings are counted after excluding disjoint singleton-mate obstructions, giving \(9^r-3\cdot4^r+3-6r(4^{r-1}-1)\) nonzero periodic points. Division by three counts primitive temporal orbits. Both programs were run: an indegree-peeling enumeration for \(r=2,\ldots,7\) and a separately coded trajectory/rainbow checker for \(r=3,4,5\). Finite checks confirm rather than replace the all-integer proof. The method requires the published theorem's hypotheses, which are satisfied by the undirected connected graph and synchronous three-color update.

## Originality — PASS, with historical uncertainty
The general synchronization/rate characterization of Gravner, Lyu and Sivakoff is **prior work**, not the new claim. The relevant original article and a full alternative finite-graph treatment have been inspected for equivalent all-size labelled counts. Neither states the matched-pair Hall-obstruction and singleton-mate enumeration. Existing two-part and three-part complete multipartite classifications are excluded from the novel domain. Existing binary parity automata have a different update rule. Searches under complete multipartite, cocktail-party, complement-of-matching, Greenberg–Hastings and excitable media formulations did not find a stronger statement implying the explicit counts. This search does not exclude unindexed or differently named older results, and the complete 1978 original journal article was not inspected.

## Value — PASS
The complement-of-matching family is canonical, vertex-transitive and unbounded in size. Its exact extinction and recurrent populations answer two natural statistical questions that the graph-general circulation theorem leaves unenumerated. A surprisingly small \(6r\) Hall correction and a distinct \(6r(4^{r-1}-1)\) recurrent correction produce a sharp, closed extinction probability and a complete primitive orbit census. The result is more than an arbitrary finite count, yet does not claim a broad new theory beyond this family.

## Limits and closest literature
The most relevant primary source is J. Gravner, H. Lyu and D. Sivakoff (arXiv:1610.07320v1), especially the GHM one-form, Lemma 1, Theorem 3 and proof. N. Doba's *The Time Evolution of a Greenberg–Hastings Cellular Automaton on a Finite Graph* (Complex Systems 27, 2018) gives related finite-graph circulation theory, not these labelled counts. Original model developments by Greenberg–Hastings (1978) and Durrett–Griffeath (1993) are acknowledged. The general result is applied, not rediscovered. The first-entry-time distribution is unproved; finite computations do not certify unbounded instances. Earlier unindexed equivalent work remains a risk. The work has no external independent validation.
