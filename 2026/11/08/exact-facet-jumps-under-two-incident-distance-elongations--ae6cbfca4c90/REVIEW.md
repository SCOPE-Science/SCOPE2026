# Scientific review of the two-incident-elongation facet count

## Correctness

**PASS.** The tight-edge connectivity criterion follows from the rank of signed incidence vectors in the normalized potential space. The proof exhaustively distinguishes connected tight graphs with and without an elongated edge. In the former case, the regular-point values are forced into two levels and the distinguished-point values into three explicit positive patterns and their negatives. In the latter case, integrality and the strict distance ceiling reduce the problem to binary potentials, with the complete connectivity table proved. Both endpoints are classified separately; their counts and jumps follow algebraically. Exhaustive, independent integer-half-step enumeration of every candidate extreme potential at three dimensions and three rational parameter values reproduced all nine predictions. These finite checks are not substituted for the universal classification.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** Gordon–Petrov's generic-metric formula and general face graph tools do not evaluate the nongeneric family with precisely the elongated pairs \(ab\) and \(bc\). The closest published exact facet law for interpolated complete multipartite metrics elongates only within equivalence classes; its long-distance graph cannot contain an induced three-vertex path. The published matching-shortening theorem changes disjoint pairs in the opposite direction, and the complete-bipartite snowflake theorem requires an entirely different pattern of long edges. A published inball-contact result for dense graph symmetric-edge polytopes concerns a different invariant at the graph-metric endpoint. These published results do not imply the new *interior* facet formula. Previously established primal–dual polarity and the tight-edge rank test are credited, not claimed as new. An unindexed equivalent specialized enumeration remains possible.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** This is the smallest natural overlapping-long-edge pattern not reducible to partition-based elongation. The closed formula and complete extremizer classification show that neighboring elongations interact nonadditively, producing a strict intermediate facet surplus at every dimension \(N\ge4\). Both endpoint discontinuities and the exact limiting ratio \(7/4\) provide a structural benchmark for sparse metric perturbations and finite-dimensional Lipschitz-free geometry. No general face lattice or arbitrary-edge theorem is implied.

## Closest literature and limits

Gordon and Petrov (arXiv:1608.06848v1, §§1–2) establish the classical Lipschitz/Kantorovich–Rubinstein polytope framework. Godefroy (doi:10.14708/cm.v55i2.1104) supplies the original free-space background. Bredies, Chirinos Rodríguez and Naldi (arXiv:2304.14039, §2) treat the known general extremal-potential criterion. The published multipartite elongation, matching shortening, complete-bipartite snowflake and degree-two-deletion inball results were compared by hypotheses and conclusions, not merely by title. The finding is restricted to real scalars, precisely two equal incident long edges, and facets rather than all faces. Independent audit has not been performed.
