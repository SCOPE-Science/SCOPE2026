# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For every integer \(n\ge3\), every coupling \(J>0\), every real field \(h\), and every spin configuration, the interaction is exactly the squared-degree sum of the plus-edge graph, minus the fixed normalization \(\tfrac12Jn(n-1)\). A degree-switching move removes any gap of at least two; hence every fixed-magnetization ground state and only those states have balanced degrees. A nondecreasing exact finite-difference calculation proves the complete critical-field sets, including the exceptional odd-order rollover. The parity classification at zero field follows from local-spin integrality and the handshaking constraint. Replaying the standalone checker passed exhaustive sector tests for \(n=3,\ldots,6\) (33,864 configurations), all slope and magnetization identities for \(n=3,\ldots,100\), and 2,622 independent constructions for \(3\le n\le25\). Finite checks do not replace the universal argument.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, provisional priority

Caravelli and Markopoulou (arXiv:1008.1340) already study essentially the same complete-root line-graph Ising Hamiltonian; the model and mapping are *not* new. Their examined main calculations concern mean-field low-temperature valence, rather than exact all-order, all-field finite-size ground-state magnetization sectors and arithmetic thresholds. Dyer et al. (arXiv:2005.07944) offer broader approximate partition-function algorithms, not this exact zero-temperature profile. Arbib (1988) and Guruswami (1999) give general line-graph cutting algorithms; the full articles were not available for direct comparison. Searches also considered two-dimensional lattice magnetization degeneracies, graph cuts, degree balancing, line-graph dualities and published mathematical research records. No examined source directly states or implies the complete theorem; priority against unindexed equivalent formulas remains uncertain.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For a physically motivated previously studied finite spin network, the theorem gives the exact magnetization-resolved ground-state energy at every system size and a full arithmetic magnetic phase diagram. It reveals the parity discontinuity among zero-field total-spin sectors and provides a reproducible exact benchmark distinct from finite-temperature mean-field approximations. The jump has order \(n\), not the order \(n^2\) required for nonzero limiting magnetization-density discontinuity.

## Limitations

This is a finite-volume, classical, uniform-coupling, zero-temperature theorem restricted to \(L(K_n)\). It does not classify thermodynamic phase transitions, finite-temperature partition functions or all line graphs.
