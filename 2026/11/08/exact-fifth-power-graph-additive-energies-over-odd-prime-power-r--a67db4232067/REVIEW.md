# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The four-coordinate fifth-power collision system is transformed bijectively to the single congruence \(s a b(a^2+b^2+4s^2)\equiv0\pmod{p^k}\) with a unit scalar for all odd \(p\ne5\). The proof independently counts \(S_p\) simple-zero classes, \(D_p\) transverse-double classes, and the unique origin. The simple-root lifting factor, double-crossing geometric valuation law, and degree-five homogeneity account for every lift and establish the recurrence for every exponent. CRT establishes the composite-modulus extension. Direct integer enumeration corroborates seventeen local and nine composite cases, including a level above the five-step threshold. Boundaries at \(p=2,5\) are explicitly excluded.

## Originality — PASS, with historical qualifications

The published finite-field fifth-power representation spectrum yields only the \(k=1\) specialization. Browning's 2013 integer equal-sums paper concerns asymptotic integer counts, not these local ring energies. Bories' 2010 local-zeta framework contains general tools for evaluating polynomial congruence densities, but neither the exact fifth-power graph formula nor its explicit \(p\)-dependent coefficients appears in the relevant theorem and examples inspected. An earlier distinct-degree quartic-graph local calculation is not a statement about fifth-power collisions; the singular degree and crossing geometry differ. Targeted searches did not identify the same all-\(k\) formula. General Igusa/Newton methods might implicitly yield it after a nontrivial specialization; unindexed earlier evaluations remain a priority risk. No claim of independently established historical priority is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The energy is a canonical two-sum invariant of a polynomial graph and a normalized constant-input fourth moment. An all-exponent evaluation is more informative than a finite census or a basic divisibility bound. The exact transverse-crossing count distinguishes the two quadratic-character classes, the degree-five singular origin produces a genuinely new recurrence scale relative to lower-degree graphs, and CRT yields the invariant over every odd five-free modulus. The distinction between the fields and local rings of equal cardinality provides a concrete arithmetic motivation and prevents the finite-field theorem from settling the local question.

## Closest work and limits

The direct finite-field quintic graph theorem is the closest specialization. The general local-zeta theory is the closest broad framework; there remains a risk that a prior explicit application of that framework has escaped the searches. The result does not address moduli divisible by two or five, complete fiber spectra, or optimal weighted extension norms. Computations are corroborative, not exhaustive for arbitrary exponents.
