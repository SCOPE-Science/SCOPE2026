# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact singular-value recursion follows from the thin SVD of a two-row matrix. With \(c=|1-\alpha|\), the normalized contrast \(r\) maps to \(r/c\) below \(c\), to \(c/r\) above \(c\), and suffers exact rank loss at \(r=c\). Writing \(r=c^x\) reduces the transient to integer subtractions in \(x\); a noninteger exponent reaches the invariant interval \((c,1)\) in finite time, where the contrast map is an involution. The explicit signs of both singular coefficients establish the stronger matrix identity \(E_{t+2}=(1-\alpha)E_t\). The resonant powers \(c^k\) reach rank one exactly at update \(k\), then evolve by the scalar multiplier \(1-\alpha/2\). Equal-singular and zero-rank starts are handled separately. Exact rational tests and 300 deterministic random-frame checks passed. Infinite-time conclusions rest on the proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Shulgin et al. (arXiv:2610.30280v1) already prove the *distinct* \(\alpha=1\) exact-line-search two-row collapse and give a general fixed-step square-root covariance formula; neither those results nor its two-cluster special case states the \(0<\alpha<2,\alpha\ne1\) countable resonant rank-loss classification and finite-transient two-step scalar orbit. Jawanpuria et al. (arXiv:2610.27747v1) prove convergence bounds and invariant rays, not the complete contrast return map. Lau et al. introduce the optimizer. Searches against relevant published mathematical records and other spectral-optimizer analyses found no implication-wise stronger version. It remains possible that an older unindexed two-singular-value map is equivalent.

**Value — PASS.** This is a natural exact phase classification for the first nontrivial matrix rank at all non-line-search stable normalized steps. It distinguishes a generic finite-time transition to a two-step scalar law from exceptional, explicitly characterized finite-time rank loss, revealing an exact-arithmetic discontinuity in the asymptotic rate even on the simplest strongly convex quadratic. It is a complete classification in its domain, not a routine numerical improvement.

**Limitations.** Isotropic quadratic, two rows, \(n\ge2\), exact SVD/polar operations, normalized steps in \(0<\alpha<2\) excluding one. The excluded case is already analyzed by prior work. Extensions to anisotropic curvature and numerical rank decisions are not asserted.
