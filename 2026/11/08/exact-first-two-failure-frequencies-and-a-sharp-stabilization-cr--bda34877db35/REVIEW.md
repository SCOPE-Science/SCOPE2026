# Self-review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The claim is reconstructed for all positive integers \(h,x_0\), with failures at indices \(n\geq2\) defined as non-integer-squares of \(b_n+2a_n/h\). The only external mathematical premise is the exact equivalence in Matijasevick--Rodríguez--Salazar, Proposition 4.1(a): a failure at \(n+1\) occurs precisely when \(\gcd(h,u_n)>1\). Section 2 of that paper establishes coprimality of every \((u_n,v_n)\) and the quadratic recurrence. The new proof independently derives the first two homogeneous numerator polynomials, counts all their roots over each prime field (including \(2\), \(5\), and the quadratic-reciprocity cases), counts the corresponding initial-value classes modulo \(p^{e+1}\), and uses CRT for the joint law. The no-late-failure implication follows from the homogeneous two-step identity at every later index, not from a finite computation. A root of the quartic yields a positive integral counterexample at index \(3\) by starting at \(x_0=hz\). The script checks 168 primes up to 1000, complete residue periods of 22 stabilized and 12 unrestricted multipliers, and 720 independent exact rational recurrence terms. The proof is unrestricted; the experiments are supplementary.

Jones--Manes (2014) concerns Galois groups of iterated rational-function preimage trees rather than this specialized finite-field density; only its accessible abstract was examined, so a stronger hidden implication has not been ruled out by full-text comparison. The 2026 companion preprint on \(z+k/z\) iterates cited in the main source was sought but its full text was not located; it remains a priority risk. published-finding corpus semantic searches for the exact paper, recurrence, quartic, residues, and densities did not return a covering finding. These negative searches are not proofs of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

The source's explicit question asks for the proportion of positive starting values having \(j\) nonsquare terms. The exact first-two joint density for arbitrary \(h\) and the complete all-time Bernoulli law for a sharp infinite family (including multipliers with arbitrarily many primes congruent to \(13,17\pmod{20}\)) are natural quantitative answers to that question. The first absent preimage level is classified by an explicit local splitting condition and is globally decisive for all later levels. The result is neither a table of small instances nor merely a restatement of the source's one-prime bound. It does not purport to classify later failures when a forbidden prime divides \(h\), or to independently validate the source's premise.
