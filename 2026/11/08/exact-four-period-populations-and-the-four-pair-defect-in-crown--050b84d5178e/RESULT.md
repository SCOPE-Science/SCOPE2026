# Exact four-period populations and the four-pair defect in crown-graph excitation

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), the synchronous three-state Greenberg--Hastings automaton on the labelled crown graph \(\operatorname{Cr}_n=K_{n,n}-M_n\) has exactly
\[
P_4(n)=2(2^n-2)(2^n-2-2n)+24\mathbf{1}_{n=4}
\]
initial states of primitive temporal period four (including all four phases of each orbit). In particular, \(P_4(3)=0\), \(P_4(4)=192\), \(P_4(5)=1200\), and \(P_4(6)=6200\). The number of distinct four-cycles is \(P_4(n)/4\). The additive \(24\) at \(n=4\) arises from a unique type of phase pattern using all four phases on both shores; it cannot extend to any larger crown graph.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has distinct labelled vertices \(a_1,\ldots,a_n,b_1,\ldots,b_n\) and edges \(a_ib_j\) precisely when \(i\ne j\). It is connected for \(n\ge3\). A site is in state \(0\) (rest), \(1\) (excited), or \(2\) (refractory). In one synchronous step, \(1\mapsto2\), \(2\mapsto0\), and \(0\mapsto1\) if and only if at least one graph neighbor currently occupies state \(1\); otherwise \(0\mapsto0\). An **initially four-periodic** state is a full state \(x\) with \(F^4(x)=x\); it is primitive if no positive time less than four returns the state. Counts are for labelled full states, not equivalence classes under symmetry or time translation.

## Proof
**Phase lemma.** In a configuration of primitive period four, each vertex is either permanently at rest or has period four, with a unique excited time in each four-step window. Indeed, a vertex ever in state \(1\) must next be \(2\), then \(0\). Its local period divides four and cannot be one or two, so it is four, and two separate excited times cannot occur in a four-step window. If a vertex were always at rest while a neighbor were excited periodically, the former would become excited, a contradiction. Since the crown graph is connected and the full orbit is nonconstant, every vertex is active.

Assign to each vertex a phase \(p(v)\in\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\), the time \(t\) at which it is excited, modulo four. A vertex with phase \(p\) is in states \(1,2,0,0\) at times \(p,p+1,p+2,p+3\). Therefore a phase assignment generates a four-periodic trajectory precisely if every vertex of phase \(p\) has (i) no neighbor of phase \(p+2\) and (ii) at least one neighbor of phase \(p-1\). Condition (i) prevents premature excitation at rest time \(p+2\); condition (ii) produces excitation at the final rest time \(p+3\). The automatic \(1\mapsto2\mapsto0\) transitions supply the other steps. These conditions are both necessary and sufficient; each resulting configuration has primitive period four.

Let \(A_p=\{i:p(a_i)=p\}\) and \(B_p=\{i:p(b_i)=p\}\). The absence of edges between opposite phases in condition (i) implies: whenever \(A_p\) and \(B_{p+2}\) are both nonempty, they are singleton sets equal to the same index. Indeed, all cross pairs except a matched pair are edges. Condition (ii), applied successively to such opposite-phase singleton sets, implies nonempty \(A_{p+1},B_{p+3}\), then \(A_{p+2},B_p\), then \(A_{p+3},B_{p+1}\). Each of these four opposite-phase pairs is again forced to be a matched singleton. Consequently \(n=4\), and every phase occurs exactly once on each shore, with \(p(b_i)=p(a_i)+2\). Conversely every permutation of the four phases among the vertices \(a_i\), together with this matched phase shift, satisfies (i)--(ii): each phase finds its required predecessor across an edge of a *different* index. There are \(4!=24\) such exceptional states, disjoint from the next class.

Suppose no opposite-phase supports coexist. Pick any nonempty \(A_p\). The predecessor requirement (ii) propagates the nonemptiness of \(B_{p-1}\), \(A_{p-2}\), and \(B_{p+1}\). Condition (i), with these nonempty \(B\) supports, rules out phases \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) on shore \(A\), while the nonempty \(A\) supports rule out phases \(p\) and \(p+2\) on shore \(B\). Thus one shore uses exactly the two even phases and the other exactly the two odd phases, with each of its two phases represented. There are two orientations of this parity split.

Fix one orientation. Put \(s=2^n-2\), the number of binary assignments using both available phases, and \(r=s-2n\), those assignments in which each phase appears at least twice: exactly \(2n\) of the \(s\) assignments have a singleton phase. If neither shore has a singleton phase, all predecessor requirements survive deleting the single matched edge, so there are \(r^2\) valid pairs. If exactly one shore has a singleton phase, there are \(2n\) assignments for it and the other shore must be robust and must assign its matched vertex the specific phase that does **not** need that singleton as an external predecessor. Precisely half of the robust assignments satisfy this requirement (exchange the two phase labels), giving \(2(2n)(r/2)=2nr\) pairs. If both shores have singleton phases, the requirements are incompatible. For example, if \(A_0=\{i\}\), then every \(B_1\) vertex must have index different from \(i\). If \(B_1=\{j\}\), then \(j\ne i\), but every \(A_2\) vertex must have an external \(B_1\) predecessor, forcing \(j\notin A_2\). Since \(A_0=\{i\}\) this forces \(j=i\), a contradiction. The analogous case with a singleton \(B_3\) follows by shifting phases. Hence the count per orientation is \(r^2+2nr=r(r+2n)=sr\). Both orientations contribute \(2sr\). Adding the exceptional \(24\) states at \(n=4\) proves the formula. No third class remains.

## Verification
Two standalone scripts independently check the theorem. `verify_dynamics.py` enumerates all \(3^{2n}\) labelled states and directly applies the full graph update four times for \(n=3,4,5,6\). It identifies exactly \(0,192,1200,6200\) primitive four-periodic configurations, respectively. `verify_phases.py` independently enumerates all \(4^{2n}\) phase assignments for \(n=3,4,5\), checks the phase lemma without executing the automaton, and obtains respectively \(0,192,1200\), with exactly \(24\) exceptional phase assignments at \(n=4\). The unrestricted theorem follows from the phase classification and counting proof, not from finite experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
Durrett and Griffeath (1993, arXiv:patt-sol/9303002) study excitable cellular automata and their stable periodic objects primarily on lattices. Gravner, Lyu and Sivakoff (2016, arXiv:1610.07320v1), especially Theorem 3 and Section 4, characterize eventual synchronization or oscillation of three-color GHM on arbitrary finite connected graphs through cycle integrals; that result does not enumerate initially primitive-four states on crown graphs. Existing exact GH classifications on complete bipartite graphs and one-edge deletions address different adjacency structures; they do not imply a formula under deletion of the *entire* perfect matching. The present claim is the exact family-specific four-period population and its exceptional four-pair phase obstruction, not the definition of GHM, the existence of periodic excitation, or the general circulation theorem.

## Limitations
The theorem counts states with primitive period exactly four **at time zero**. It does not count transient basins, other primitive periods, random initial-state probabilities, or other graph families. Public source comparisons cannot exclude every unindexed historical computation; original priority is therefore qualified. No independent audit has been performed.
