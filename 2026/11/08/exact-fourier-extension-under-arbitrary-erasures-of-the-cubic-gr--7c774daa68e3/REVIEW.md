# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

All normalization conventions are explicit. Additive orthogonality reduces the norm quotient to \(Q/S^2\). For nonzero pair sum, equality of cubic sums forces equality of unordered pairs in characteristic greater than three. The exceptional zero-sum fiber has one diagonal contribution precisely when the origin survives. Formula (1) handles both this diagonal and the off-diagonal antipodal collisions without double counting. The inactive masks have no nontrivial fiber collision; Cauchy--Schwarz gives their exact constant and all equality cases. In the active masks the triangle inequality, monotonicity in pair-product moduli, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz, and a positive orphan-mass penalty yield a sharp bound and rigid equality case. The calculation holds for all finite fields of the permitted characteristic, not only primes. A standalone verifier replayed successfully on 2,555 masks and 5,109 random coefficient vectors; it is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The established full cubic-graph theorem gives only the undiluted entire source set; a published conic erasure theorem gives an analogous pair-count classification but has no origin diagonal in its zero-sum fiber, and cannot assert the sharp value and equality structure for origin-containing partially erased cubic masks. The 2024 primary source proves sharp extension for quadratic finite-field geometries, not this masked cubic setting. The complete origin-triggered one-pair transition, with vanishing orphan coefficients and exact \(3-6/(3+4k)\), is therefore not implied by the compared published claims. Mathematical searches did not reveal a dominating general one-exception-fiber result, but undiscovered abstract or folklore coverage remains a risk. No originality conclusion is based solely on absent keyword matches: it is based on the different collision fibers and implications of the compared full statements.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Partial-frequency survival is an intrinsic design and robustness question for sharp Fourier extension, rather than an artificial parameter slice. The theorem completely classifies every erasure mask and all nonzero equality patterns using two natural combinatorial invariants. The sharp distinction between one intact pair with and without the neutral frequency is not captured by full-source results or by centered-conic masks; it explains how one retained frequency causes extremizers to discard otherwise usable unmatched measurements. The theorem makes no minimal-measurement, higher-moment, or noise-stability claim.
