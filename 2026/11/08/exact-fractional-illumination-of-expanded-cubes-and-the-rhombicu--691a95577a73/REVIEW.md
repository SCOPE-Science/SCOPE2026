# Review of the expanded-cube fractional illumination finding

## Correctness — PASS
The 26 supporting halfspaces follow from a support-function proof using nested subset-sum inequalities. For every real \(a>1\) their active normal pattern at each of 24 vertices is the same. The four possible signs at each chosen large-coordinate position give the exact generic illuminated-vertex count, bounded by seven, and strict perturbation covers nongeneric directions. Summing vertex-cover constraints proves the fractional lower bound \(24/7\); a 48-direction orbit with 14 incidences at each vertex attains it. Four specified directions cover all 24 vertices, so the ordinary value is four. The cube endpoint has eight pairwise incompatible vertices. A standalone integer-arithmetic checker confirms all claimed finite incidences.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
The 2012 primary paper by Kiss and Oloff de Wet explicitly covers the regular rhombicuboctahedron with four light sources, but its primary parameter is norm-weighted and it does not compute fractional illumination. The 2026 full-text theorem of Gorodzha is a universal bound and equality classification, not an object-specific evaluation. The published vertex-transitive symmetry formula reduces the new fractional invariant to a maximal incidence number, but computes that number for two different Platonic solids. The published cube–octahedron intersection study concerns an ordinary illumination profile for a different 14-facet family. Source-specific and exact-parameter searches identified no stronger result implying this seven-incidence bound or \(24/7\) profile. General illumination compilations and unindexed historical observations remain an originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This determines an exact geometric invariant for a named 24-vertex Archimedean solid and for every member of a natural noncongruent one-parameter family. The count \(7\) identifies the limiting single-direction incidence mechanism, while the weighted solution \(24/7\) has a nontrivial integrality gap \(7/6\). The sharp jump from the cubic endpoint provides an informative normal-fan boundary phenomenon. This is a natural complete calculation, not an arbitrary sampling or an incremental numerical bound.

## Scope and limitations
The family consists of the signed-permutation hulls of \((a,1,1)\) for all real \(a>1\), plus the separately treated endpoint \(a=1\). Statements are not extended to arbitrary rhombicuboctahedral realizations. Previously known ordinary illumination of the regular member is not rebranded as new. Independent validation and definitive historical priority remain open.
