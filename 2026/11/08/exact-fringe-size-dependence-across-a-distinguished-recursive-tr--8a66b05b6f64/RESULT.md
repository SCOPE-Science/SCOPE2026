# Exact fringe-size dependence across a distinguished recursive-tree branch

## Finding
Let \(T_n\) be the uniform random recursive tree on labels \(1,\ldots,n\), generated by independently attaching vertex \(j\) to a uniformly selected older vertex for each \(j=2,\ldots,n\). For every integer \(n\ge2\) and every \(1\le k<n\), let \(S\) be the number of descendants of vertex \(2\), counting vertex \(2\) itself, and let \(Z_k\) count *all* vertices whose descendant fringe subtree has exactly \(k\) vertices.

Define, for nonnegative integers \(m\),
\[
 \mu_k(m)=\begin{cases}
 0,&m<k,\\
 1,&m=k,\\
 \dfrac{m}{k(k+1)},&m>k.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then \(S\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\), and its exact regression is
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_k\mid S=s]
 =\mu_k(s)+\mu_k(n-s)-\mathbf1_{\{n-s=k\}},
 \qquad 1\le s<n.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(S,Z_k)=\frac{2k-n}{2(n-1)}}.
\]
The covariance is strictly positive when \(n<2k\), zero when \(n=2k\), and strictly negative when \(n>2k\). This classifies every permitted sample size and fringe size, including the boundary \(n=2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The tree is *uniform among increasing rooted labeled trees*, not a uniformly labeled Cayley tree, preferential-attachment tree, or uniformly shaped plane tree. Every parent choice is independent and has probability \(1/(j-1)\) for any older parent. Descendant-subtree size always includes its root. The distinguished vertex is specifically the second arriving vertex; the theorem does not assert the same formula for other labels. The count \(Z_k\) includes the root's fringe tree if its size equals \(k\); because \(k<n\), the root of the full tree is never counted.

## Proof
**1. The distinguished-branch size is uniform.** Partition the vertices at any stage into \(A\), the descendants of vertex \(2\), and \(B\), its complement, containing vertex \(1\). At size \(m\ge2\), if \(|A|=s\), a new vertex is placed in \(A\) with probability \(s/m\), and in \(B\) with probability \((m-s)/m\). Initially both parts have size one. Induction on \(m\) yields
\[
 \Pr(S_m=s)=\frac1{m-1},\quad 1\le s\le m-1.
\]
Indeed, at size \(m+1\) and \(1\le s\le m\), the probability is the sum of incoming transitions from sizes \(s-1\) and \(s\), with missing boundary terms interpreted as zero. The result is \(1/m\). In particular, \(\mathbb E S=n/2\).

**2. The fringe-size expectation.** For an independent uniform recursive tree of size \(m\), write \(F_{m,k}\) for the number of fringe subtrees of size \(k\) and \(f_{m,k}=\mathbb E F_{m,k}\). The transition from \(m\) to \(m+1\) destroys a size-\(k\) subtree when the new vertex attaches to one of its \(k\) vertices, and creates a size-\(k\) subtree when it attaches inside an existing size-\((k-1)\) subtree. Therefore, for \(k\ge2\),
\[
 f_{m+1,k}=\left(1-\frac{k}{m}\right)f_{m,k}
              +\frac{k-1}{m}f_{m,k-1},\qquad m\ge k,
\]
with \(f_{k,k}=1\) and \(f_{m,k}=0\) for \(m<k\). For \(k=1\), the newly inserted vertex is a new leaf and removes the leaf status of its parent if that parent was a leaf, so
\[
 f_{m+1,1}=1+\left(1-\frac1m\right)f_{m,1},\qquad f_{1,1}=1.
\]
Induction first in \(m\), then in \(k\) where necessary, gives \(f_{m,k}=\mu_k(m)\) for every \(m\ge1\), including the special case \(m=k\).

**3. Two conditioned recursive trees.** Record for each arrival \(j\ge3\) whether it joins \(A\) or \(B\). Conditional on this entire membership sequence, a vertex joining a part chooses its parent *uniformly inside that part*: this follows by dividing the probability \(1/(j-1)\) of an individual parent by the probability that the parent lies in the selected part. These internal parent choices are mutually independent across arrivals. Hence the induced trees on \(A\) and \(B\), with labels replaced by their within-part arrival ranks, are independent uniform recursive trees of sizes \(s\) and \(n-s\), conditional on any such membership sequence and thus also conditional on \(S=s\).

For vertices in \(A\), descendant fringe sizes in the full tree coincide with those in the induced \(A\)-tree. The same holds for *nonroot* vertices in \(B\), since a descendant in \(A\) can attach to \(B\) only through its ancestor vertex \(2\), whose parent is the root vertex \(1\). The root of \(B\) is vertex \(1\), which has fringe size \(n\) in the full tree and must not be counted for \(k<n\); within the \(B\)-tree it has size \(n-s\). Thus the exact pathwise identity is
\[
 Z_k=F_{s,k}^{(A)}+F_{n-s,k}^{(B)}-\mathbf1_{\{n-s=k\}}.
\]
Take conditional expectations to obtain the stated regression. No asymptotic approximation enters this argument.

**4. Cancellation and sign.** The function \(s\mapsto\mu_k(s)+\mu_k(n-s)\) is symmetric under \(s\leftrightarrow n-s\), and \(S\) is uniform on a symmetric set about \(n/2\). Its covariance with \(S\) therefore vanishes exactly. The remaining correction gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Cov}(S,Z_k)
 &=-\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(S,\mathbf1_{\{S=n-k\}}\right)\\
 &=-\frac{(n-k)-n/2}{n-1}
 =\frac{2k-n}{2(n-1)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves all three sign cases with no excluded integers.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses only Python's standard library and exact rational arithmetic. Its independent exhaustive construction enumerates every parent sequence (all \((n-1)!\) recursive trees) for \(2\le n\le9\), calculates all fringe sizes, verifies the conditional-regression formula at every permitted size and condition, and checks the covariance and uniform law. A second test checks the exact expectation recurrences and two-colour urn transitions through size \(120\). It reports 14,728 passed exact assertions. Enumeration is a finite consistency check, not the proof of the theorem; the preceding steps prove every integer case.

Run with `python verify.py` in the directory containing this document and the script.

## Relationship to prior work
Feng, Mahmoud, and Panholzer (2008) investigate the global counts of fringe trees of size \(k\) and phase changes as \(k\) scales with tree size. Holmgren and Janson (2014 preprint, subsequently 2015) give the precise mean \(\mathbb E Z_k=n/[k(k+1)]\) for \(k<n\) in their Theorem 1.4, and Theorem 1.15 gives covariances *between two global fringe-size counts*. Seelbach Benkner and Wagner (2022), Section 2.2, Lemma 3, also provide the global mean for general increasing-tree families. Those global counts do not specify a birth-labeled individual subtree. The present result pairs a *distinguished birth-label subtree size* with a global count, with an exact boundary-correction regression and a sign change at \(n=2k\). In particular, covariance formulas for two global counts do not imply the distinguished-vertex covariance: the recursive-tree labels are not exchangeable with respect to birth rank.

## Limitations
The result is specific to uniform attachment and label \(2\). It does not give independence at the zero-covariance boundary, any full joint distribution, or an analogous formula for every later label. Historical priority is not independently certified: some earlier general multivariate or analytic tree methods could imply this identity without stating it explicitly, and the full text of the 2008 article was not fully accessible for a direct complete-section comparison. The 2014 primary text and the 2022 full article were examined for the distinct-versus-global issue. A 2026 study of early-vertex fringe-size splitting and concentration uses related decomposition ideas but was available only in partial primary-text excerpts; its full scope remains a priority risk. No publication or independent verification is claimed.
