# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The identity is derived by an exact positive-part cancellation, independent Gaussian sum-and-difference coordinates, and an explicit two-dimensional polar integral. The resulting closed form is checked at the atom and for a smooth Gaussian margin. Taylor expansion establishes the two distinct rates, and a telescoping expectation plus Cauchy--Schwarz proves the precise single-term finite-cost/finite-second-moment criterion. A standalone numeric checker passed 86 tests. Numerical checks are supplemental, not a replacement for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The central primary preprint by Haji-Ali and Spence (arXiv:2308.07835) develops the same nested maximum estimator and antithetic framework, but the inspected assumptions/results provide general bounds under anti-concentration, not the displayed all-sample Gaussian-mixture formula, explicit atom/smooth constants, or single-term if-and-only-if frontier. Giles and Goda (arXiv:1708.05531) consider multi-decision antithetic MLMC and discuss why positive-measure ties fall outside the smooth-margin assumption; this does not imply the exact formulas. Giles and Haji-Ali (arXiv:1802.05016) treat nested risk probabilities with a discontinuous outer payoff, not this positive-part second moment. The Cauchy--Schwarz feasibility principle and the general smooth-case variance rate are established tools, not claimed as new. The Bourgey–De Marco–Gobet–Zhou initial-margin article is a plausible comparison whose full text was unavailable; the residual possibility of an earlier equivalent specialization remains.

**Value — PASS.** A positive probability of an exact decision tie is a natural modelling distinction in nested risk/decision simulation, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The result supplies the finite-sample second-moment benchmark, exact asymptotic coefficients, and a necessary-and-sufficient limitation for a standard unbiased debiasing architecture. It explains why a seemingly negligible tie atom changes whether finite expected cost and finite variance can coexist. The conclusion does not exclude more sophisticated alternative estimators or imply numerical instability of all MLMC schemes.

**Scope and checks.** The result is restricted to scalar positive-part payoffs, centered Gaussian-margin mixtures, independent Gaussian inner noise, and the specified independent single-term random-level estimator; no broader distributional claims or independent validation are asserted. The exact second-moment integral and the level feasibility proof are both explicit in `RESULT.md` and reproduced diagnostically by `check.py`.
