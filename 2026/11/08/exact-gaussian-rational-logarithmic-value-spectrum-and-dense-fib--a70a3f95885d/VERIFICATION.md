# Verification and mathematical limits

The theorem is fully established by the norm formula, the Bézout construction for every admissible rational logarithmic value, and density of the subgroup generated by two multiplicatively independent positive rational zeros together with Gaussian-rational points on the unit circle. The finite experiment is only a consistency check.

Run `python3 VERIFY.py` using Python's standard library. Expected diagnostic lines:

```
VERIFY_OK gaussian_rational_cases=5280 constructed_rational_levels=452
polar_arithmetic_log_(1+i)=1/4; log_(3/(1+i))=1/12; 1/8_excluded=PASS
independent_kernel_generators_4/27_4/3125=PASS rational_circle_3/5+4i/5=PASS
```

The first test directly calculates the logarithmic derivative of \(5{,}280\) Gaussian rationals with exact rational arithmetic. The second constructs an explicit Gaussian rational for \(452\) admitted rational levels through an integer Bézout certificate, then evaluates the derivative independently from its norm. The checks include the exact values \(L(1+i)=1/4\), \(L(3/(1+i))=1/12\), and the fact that the reduced denominator of \(1/8\) is excluded.

Finite computation cannot establish that no other denominator patterns occur, that all admissible values occur, or density in the plane. Those are proved in RESULT.md. No independent external validation has been conducted.
