# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact scalar projections follow from the cited sketched Arnoldi definitions. Both Student marginal laws are proved using Gaussian regression and an independent normal/chi ratio. The negative-sign formula is the Student tail expressed through regularized beta; the sharp worst case follows from the spectral-angle inequality with an explicit two-dimensional equality case. Endpoint-beta asymptotics give the prefactor, the Student central limit theorem gives the high-condition transition, and a positive density at the reciprocal denominator zero proves both one-sided \(t^{-1}\) tails. The actual bundled checker returns `VERIFY_OK` and is ancillary rather than an infinite-dimensional or asymptotic certificate. Singular cases (eigenvectors, condition one, zero denominators) are handled explicitly. Residual numerical risk is finite-precision behavior not modeled by the exact theorem.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority
The full source text of Güttel–Liu–Nyman (arXiv:2607.10354) provides the scalar projection definitions and only conditional right-half-plane guarantees, not these unconditional Gaussian laws. Güttel–Schweitzer (arXiv:2208.11447, Remark 2.6) previously warns of negative sketched Ritz values, so the qualitative sign failure alone is not new. Grigori–Kressner–Shao–Simunec (arXiv:2601.10248) analyzes a different, similarity-restored projection. The related truncated-Krylov source arXiv:2306.06481 discusses nonstandard Ritz effects. Semantic searches of published mathematical records and accessible prior claims identified no coverage of the exact sign probability, sharp condition-number supremum, simultaneous harmonic reciprocal tail, or high-condition transition. The closest Student-tail, random-Ritz and spectral-filter records concern different operators and do not imply the present statements. General statistical laws of sample correlation and Gaussian quadratic-form ratios are a genuine source of potential prior coverage. Roberts (1995), *On the Existence of Moments of Ratios of Quadratic Forms*, was located and its accessible abstract read; the full text was not accessible for a decisive all-parameter comparison. Accordingly nonexistence of a mean is not claimed as an unprecedented general probabilistic phenomenon. The sharp numerical-method conditioning supremum, sketch-dimension transition, and explicit harmonic tail coefficient together form the assessed contribution. Absence from searches is not a proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The studied scalar projections are explicitly defined in a recent sketch-and-restart matrix-function method, whose convergence assumptions require positive harmonic Ritz locations. The exact sign probability directly measures one possible failure of that assumption and gives an attained dimension-versus-conditioning reliability tradeoff beyond the classical Gaussian sample-correlation calculation. The sharp \(s\asymp\kappa\) transition is a parameter-selection benchmark. The harmonic infinite-mean result distinguishes common uncorrected sketched harmonic projections from ordinary Arnoldi, despite exponentially rare sign failures at fixed conditioning. This is a motivated stability/reliability classification, not merely a normalization or finite-table exercise.

## Limits and related literature
This work concerns a single scalar projection under unconditioned real Gaussian sketches, not the full restarted algorithm, complex-valued sketches, accepted embeddings, or finite-precision computation. The source work arXiv:2607.10354 already provides a sufficient deterministic right-half-plane condition; the present result does not contradict it. Independent historical priority review remains outstanding.
