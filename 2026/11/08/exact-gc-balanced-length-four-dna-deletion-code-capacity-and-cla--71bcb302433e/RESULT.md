# Exact GC-balanced length-four DNA deletion-code capacity and classification

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma=\{A,C,G,T\}\), with Watson--Crick complementation \(A\leftrightarrow T\), \(C\leftrightarrow G\). For a word \(w=w_1w_2w_3w_4\), set \(\rho(w)=\overline{w_4}\overline{w_3}\overline{w_2}\overline{w_1}\) and let \(\Delta(w)\) be the set of its distinct length-three words obtained by deleting one position. A **strand-closed GC-balanced single-deletion code** is a set \(C\subseteq\Sigma^4\) satisfying all four conditions:

1. Every \(w\in C\) contains exactly two letters in \(\{C,G\}\).
2. \(\rho(C)=C\).
3. \(w\ne\rho(w)\) for every \(w\in C\).
4. \(\Delta(u)\cap\Delta(v)=\varnothing\) whenever \(u,v\in C\) are distinct.

The maximum code size is exactly \(14\). There are exactly \(20\) distinct *labeled* maximum codebooks, where no nucleotide or coordinate relabeling is identified. Under the group generated by the eight Watson--Crick-complement-preserving alphabet permutations and reversal of every word, the twenty optima split into four classes of sizes \(4,4,4,8\).

## Assumptions and scope

The positions in every four-letter word are linearly ordered. A deletion removes exactly one of the four symbols and does not report its position. The received three-letter word retains the relative order of surviving symbols. A codebook contains both orientations of each selected strand pair, not one representative per pair. No Hamming, homopolymer, or biochemical constraint beyond the four explicitly stated conditions is imposed.

## Proof

**Reduction to a finite orbit graph.** Exactly \(\binom42 2^4=96\) four-letter words have GC-content two. Reverse complementation partitions them into \(52\) orbits: \(8\) fixed words and \(44\) two-word orbits. The fixed words are forbidden. For every two-word orbit \(P\), its two deletion shadows are disjoint (checked directly using the full deterministic enumeration of all \(96\) words in the standalone checker). Consequently, all \(44\) pairs can be selected individually. Two distinct orbit vertices are called adjacent in the **conflict graph** when the union of the deletion shadows of one meets the union for the other. Each feasible codebook corresponds bijectively to an independent set of orbit vertices and contains exactly twice as many DNA words. The graph has \(44\) vertices and \(344\) conflict edges; these finite counts are reconstructed from the explicit formulas in the checkers.

**Sharp upper certificate.** The supplied `certificate.json` lists \(14\) subsets of orbit indices, each of which is a clique in the conflict graph. Every orbit vertex appears in at least two of these cliques (exactly \(36\) appear twice and \(8\) appear three times). A conflict-free set \(I\) meets each clique in at most one vertex, hence

\[
2|I|\le\sum_{j=1}^{14}|I\cap Q_j|\le14.
\]

Thus \(|I|\le7\), and every admissible codebook contains at most \(14\) words. This is a finite, explicitly checkable rational clique-cover certificate, not an inference from a solver termination status.

**Attainment.** The following seven reverse-complement pairs have mutually disjoint combined single-deletion shadows:

```
AACC / GGTT
AAGG / CCTT
ACGA / TCGT
AGTC / GACT
CCAA / TTGG
CTAC / GTAG
GGAA / TTCC
```

Their union is a code of \(14\) words, proving equality.

**Enumeration of all optima.** The lexicographically indexed list of the \(44\) admissible strand pairs is recorded in `certificate.json`. Every compatible seven-subset is visited exactly once by the increasing-index recursive search in `verify_certificate.py`: at each step the candidate list is restricted to larger indices compatible with every chosen pair. The checker compares the resulting complete list with the \(20\) explicitly stored maximum selections. Independently, `independent_census.cpp` generates the same graph using base-four integers and counts all independent seven-sets as \(20\) and all independent eight-sets as \(0\). Alphabet permutations commuting with complementation form an eight-element group; since GC-content is exactly two out of four, these permutations preserve the feasible set even when they exchange the two complementary nucleotide classes. Including reversal doubles the action to \(16\) transformations. Direct evaluation of this finite action partitions the \(20\) optima into four orbits of sizes \(4,4,4,8\).

## Verification

Run from the directory containing the listed files:

```
python3 verify_certificate.py
c++ -O2 -std=c++17 independent_census.cpp -o census
./census
```

The Python checker uses only the standard library; it reconstructs the entire candidate universe, verifies all \(14\) conflict cliques, proves the covering multiplicities, checks the attaining code, enumerates all maximum compatible selections, and recomputes the symmetry classes. The independent C++ checker has no external dependencies; it recomputes the DNA words and deletion shadows from base-four encodings and independently counts seven- and eight-orbit independent sets. On the packaged files both completed with `CERTIFICATE_OK`, `UPPER_BOUND_OK`, `ATTAINMENT_OK`, `CENSUS_OK`, and `DIRECT_CENSUS_OK` checks.

## Relationship to prior work

King's 2003 treatment of GC-balanced DNA codes proves bounds and constructions in the Hamming-distance model, rather than the exact one-deletion shadow-packing capacity with reverse-complement closure and fixed GC-content studied here. D'yachkov and coauthors' 2003 *Exordium for DNA Codes* formulates reverse-complement strand-pair codes based on deletion similarity, but does not state the present optimum or extremizer census. Their 2005 *On DNA Codes* gives constructions and bounds for block and deletion similarity, including an unrestricted quaternary length-four example; its stated constructions do not imply the result for the GC-content-two subfamily. The present claim concerns the intersection of GC balance, strand closure, self-complement exclusion and one-deletion decoding, and not the discovery of these individual constraints or classical deletion-correcting codes.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns only length-four DNA words, GC-content exactly two, a single deletion, literal ordered three-symbol outputs, and exact strand closure without self-reverse-complementary words. It does not classify longer codes, multiple deletions, substitutions, thermodynamic hybridization or experimental usability. The twenty-code census is a finite exhaustive mathematical result verified by two explicit algorithms; it is not an all-length asymptotic statement. Historical priority has not been independently established.
