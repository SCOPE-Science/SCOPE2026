# Exact generated algebra and coarsest tuned partition of the strict natural-number order

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal B_<=(\mathcal P(\mathbb N),\cap,\cup,\neg,\varnothing,\mathbb N,\Diamond_<)\), with
\[\Diamond_< U=\{n\in\mathbb N:\exists m>n\;(m\in U)\}.\]
For arbitrary finitely many generators \(P_1,\ldots,P_k\subseteq\mathbb N\), including \(k=0\), give each \(n\) its atomic color \(c(n)\in\{0,1\}^k\). Let \(I\) be the nonempty set of colors occurring infinitely often, let \(E=\{n:c(n)\notin I\}\), and put \(N=0\) when \(E=\varnothing\), otherwise \(N=1+\max E\). Then the subalgebra \(\langle P_1,\ldots,P_k\rangle\) has exactly
\[\boxed{2^{N+|I|}}\]
elements. Its atoms are precisely the singleton sets \(\{0\},\ldots,\{N-1\}\) and the infinite tail color classes
\[H_a=\{n\ge N:c(n)=a\}\qquad(a\in I).\]
Equivalently, this partition is the unique coarsest finite partition refining the initial atomic-color partition that is tuned for the strict order. For example, a single generator \(P=\{m\}\) yields exactly \(2^{m+2}\) elements for every \(m\in\mathbb N\).

## Assumptions and scope
The order is the **strict** ordinary order on \(\mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\). Only finite Boolean operations and the ordinary strict-future possibility operator \(\Diamond_<\) are admitted: no next-time operator, converse modality, infinitary unions, counting, or additional constants. A generated subalgebra means the smallest Boolean modal subalgebra containing the stated finitely many arbitrary subsets. A color occurring infinitely often is unbounded in \(\mathbb N\). Because the alphabet has \(2^k\) elements, the complement of \(I\) occurs only finitely often and \(N\) is well-defined. The assertion is a structural exact count, not an algorithm for determining recurrence from an arbitrary infinite presentation of the generators.

A finite partition \(\mathcal Q\) is tuned for \(<\) when, for every two parts \(U,V\in\mathcal Q\), existence of any ordered pair \(u<v\) with \(u\in U,v\in V\) implies that every point in \(U\) has a strict successor in \(V\).

## Proof
**Step 1: upper bound and tuning.** Define
\[\mathcal Q=\{\{n\}:0\le n<N\}\cup\{H_a:a\in I\}.\]
All \(H_a\) are nonempty, infinite, and cofinal, so \(\mathcal Q\) is a partition with \(N+|I|\) parts. Every generator is a union of its parts. If a union \(U\) of parts contains some \(H_a\), then \(U\) is cofinal, so \(\Diamond_<U=\mathbb N\). If \(U\) contains no infinite part, it is a finite subset of \([0,N)\). Its strict-future preimage is empty when \(U\) is empty, and is exactly \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) when \(m=\max U\). Each such preimage is again a union of parts of \(\mathcal Q\). Thus all unions of parts form a Boolean modal subalgebra, and therefore
\[|\langle P_1,\ldots,P_k\rangle|\le 2^{N+|I|}.\]
This argument proves that \(\mathcal Q\) is tuned.

**Step 2: definability of every singleton.** For each color \(a\), let \(\chi_a\) be its conjunction of propositional literals, interpreted as the full set \(C_a=\{n:c(n)=a\}\). If \(N=0\), every color with any occurrence is recurrent, and each part \(H_a=C_a\) is directly a Boolean combination of the generators. Suppose \(N>0\). Set
\[F=\bigcup_{a\notin I} C_a=E,\qquad L=F\setminus\Diamond_<F.\]
The union defining \(F\) is finite, hence is a Boolean combination of the generators. Since \(N-1=\max F\) and the relation is strict, \(L=\{N-1\}\). For every \(t\) with \(1\le t\le N-1\), iterated strict preimages satisfy
\[\Diamond_<^{\,t}\{N-1\}=\{0,\ldots,N-1-t\},\]
and \(\Diamond_<^{\,N}\{N-1\}=\varnothing\). Taking successive differences isolates every \(\{j\}\) with \(0\le j<N-1\). Thus all first \(N\) singleton parts belong to the generated subalgebra.

**Step 3: definability of each infinite part.** Let \(T=\mathbb N\setminus\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\); it is a Boolean combination of the definable singleton sets (and equals \(\mathbb N\) if \(N=0\)). Then \(H_a=T\cap C_a\) for each \(a\in I\), so every part of \(\mathcal Q\) is generated. Every union of parts therefore belongs to the algebra. Combined with Step 1, this proves equality and its exact cardinality.

**Step 4: optimality of tuned refinement.** Let \(\mathcal R\) be any finite tuned refinement of the atomic-color partition. The Boolean algebra of unions of parts of \(\mathcal R\) is closed under \(\Diamond_<\): by tuning, each part is either entirely contained in or entirely disjoint from \(\Diamond_<\) of any union of parts. Hence it contains the subalgebra generated by the \(P_i\), including all \(\mathcal Q\)-parts established above. Consequently every \(\mathcal R\)-part lies within one \(\mathcal Q\)-part; that is, \(\mathcal R\) refines \(\mathcal Q\). Since Step 1 shows \(\mathcal Q\) itself is tuned, it is the unique coarsest such refinement.

For the example \(P=\{m\}\), the recurrent palette consists only of the zero color, \(E=\{m\}\), and \(N=m+1\), giving exactly \(2^{m+2}\) elements.

## Verification
A standalone Python program, `checker.py`, independently closes Boolean subsets under strict-future preimages on finite quotient frames representing arbitrary finite color prefixes followed by cofinal recurrent colors. Each recurrent-color state has all recurrent-color states as successors, including itself, reflecting infinitely many future positions of every recurrent color in the original infinite strict order. The computation checked all finite prefixes of lengths up to six for one variable, up to four for two variables, and the variable-free case, with all nonempty recurrent palettes. On every instance, the directly generated algebra agrees with the claimed partition algebra and has the predicted size. The script also verifies that the proposed parts are disjoint, exhaustive, and individually generated. The infinite proof above, rather than the finite tests, establishes the full theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Shapirovsky (2019), Example 1, explicitly constructs a finite tuned refinement for both \((\omega,\le)\) and \((\omega,<)\) by isolating all positions up to the last element of a finite initial partition cell and preserving the infinite cells on the tail. This supplies the **upper-bound partition** used here, but does not establish that it is generated, minimal, or that its size is always exact. The new lower-bound argument is specific to the strict modality: the last occurrence \(F\setminus\Diamond_<F\) is definable, and its strict predecessors can be distinguished one by one. Agnew (2026) develops general criteria for local finiteness of transitive-frame complex algebras but does not supply this exact generator-by-generator count.

The reflexive order is distinct: its modal preimage includes the current point, so the singleton-isolation argument does not carry over. Prior exact reflexive ordinal-algebra classifications consequently do not imply the present strict count. A general tuned-partition existence theorem, or even an explicit tuned upper-bound partition, alone does not give the lower bound.

## Limitations
The precise count depends on the infinite color occurrence set, which may be noncomputable from a given infinite description. It applies only to the strict natural-number chain and ordinary finitary Boolean modal generation. It does not claim a uniform bound in the number of generators alone: already one generator \(\{m\}\) yields unboundedly many elements as \(m\) varies. No assertion is made for arbitrary countable orders, higher direct powers, or reflexive semantics. Historical priority of the exact minimality statement remains subject to further literature review; independently validated status is not claimed.
