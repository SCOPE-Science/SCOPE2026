# Mathematical review: seven-one prefix-normal generating function

## Correctness (PASS)

The claim is a formal identity for all lengths, not an extrapolation. The proof converts the prefix-normal definition to four nontrivial gap inequalities, isolates unrestricted first-gap and trailing-zero variables, enumerates the pointed five-dimensional cone's 13 primitive rays and 9 facets, and gives a reproducible pulling triangulation into 15 half-open simplicial cones. Ten cone determinants are one, five are two. The numerator contributions are listed explicitly, and exact rational-function simplification yields the target numerator. The generating-function coefficient and asymptotic calculations follow algebraically. The standalone program recomputes the finite exact certificate from the defining inequalities, tests all fixed-content words through length 20 directly, and compares independent gap counts through length 27. There are no claims of proof by finite experiments alone.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)

Fici and Lipták introduced the property. Burcsi et al. (2014, Lemmas 2–3) explicitly list density 0–6 rational functions; their 2016 expanded Section 4.2 proves rationality for every fixed density while stating that explicit functions above density six had not been computed in that treatment. The present specific density-seven numerator is not a change of notation for those six cases, and its coefficients cannot be derived from the general existence theorem without further integer-cone enumeration. Adamson et al. (2025) cover generator chains, without a seven-one coefficient formula in the relevant results inspected. Semantic database searches retrieved a different prefix-normal defect theorem, not a dominating density-seven theorem. The residual possibility of unindexed, independent equivalent enumeration is explicitly acknowledged; no universal novelty certificate is claimed.

## Value (PASS)

This is the first numerical density left unresolved in the concrete list of a well-defined counting problem whose applications include indexed jumbled matching. The explicit rational function provides every exact cardinality and a sharp leading fixed-content probability. Computing it requires resolving a genuinely nontrivial five-dimensional lattice cone, not a routine change of symbol or a special case of an explicit earlier rational function. It is a self-contained precise answer to a motivated enumerative gap, without an assertion of a broader theorem.
