# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite-dimensional identity follows directly from a two-component orthogonal decomposition of every central \(m\)-section, possible because \(m-1\ge1\). Every admissible point has \(|z|\le\min(h,\sqrt{B/(1-B)})\), and a transverse vector of the required length realizes the equality radius. The Gaussian-squared-projection representation supplies the exact beta CDF and isolates the unique endpoint atom. The beta central limit theorem with the explicitly differentiated odds square root yields the proportional-dimensional critical window; chi-square ratios prove the fixed-codimension limit. Endpoint and degenerate orientations are dealt with explicitly in the proof. A standalone program passed 168 independent numerical and optimization checks; these are supplementary, not exhaustive proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge; historically provisional)

The 2026 random-diameter motivating source was checked via its abstract, but its full text was inaccessible and may contain an example not visible in the abstract. The published full proof of the one-spike ellipsoid beta law was inspected: it concerns smooth quadratic ellipsoids and gives no clipped-cylinder atom or cap-contact transition. The published full right-cylinder shadow calculation was inspected: its quantities are orthogonal projection area and perimeter, not diameter of a central section. Dirksen's accessible abstract concerns cylinder-section volume; Kim–Yaskin–Zvavitch's abstract concerns distributions of volumes. Recent stereology on right circular cylinders uses randomly offset probes and endcap intersection counts rather than the Haar-central all-codimension law. Targeted source, alias, implication, parameter and record searches found no dominating theorem. Search absence does not prove novelty; older exact stereology may contain a low-dimensional formula, and full-paper priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Exact random-section diameters are natural benchmarks for geometric probability and random information recovery. The presence of a nonzero atom at a geometric diameter threshold is a qualitative distinction from smooth ellipsoids, and its sharp, dimension-dependent disappearance through a Gaussian critical window gives a structural phase diagram rather than merely a changed constant. The additional fixed-codimension chi-square scaling exposes a second natural asymptotic regime. The object is a canonical convex product and the result holds in every permitted ambient and section dimension.
