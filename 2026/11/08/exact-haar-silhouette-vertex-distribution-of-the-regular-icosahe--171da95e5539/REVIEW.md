# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof reconstructs the convex icosahedron face graph and the ten antipodal face-normal great-circle arrangement in exact \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\). The complete symbolic certificate establishes all 92 cells, their 20/60/12 profile counts, and their spherical interior angles; Girard's formula yields the probability law. Direct random orthogonal projections of 36,000 samples check the count distribution by an independent convex-hull algorithm. Sampled checks do not substitute for the finite exhaustive algebraic classification.

## Originality
**PASS, provisional.** Glisse--Lazard's 2007 silhouette analysis establishes an expected edge count from dihedral angles and classwise asymptotic bounds, not the full fixed-icosahedron law. Glisse et al. address Poisson-generated random polytopes and a maximum over directions. Central-section polygon laws, area-moment laws, and other Platonic shadow statistics concern different quantities and do not imply this exact law. The older Kettner--Welzl report could not be fully inspected for tables; historical priority and an external audit remain open.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a complete, naturally specified discrete Haar statistic for the most faceted Platonic solid. Exact angle-count profiles and the 92-cell spherical normal arrangement show structural content beyond a numerical mean or routine symmetry count. The probabilities quantify relative prevalence of the hexagonal, octagonal, and decagonal orthogonal shadows.

## Limits
The theorem concerns convex regular icosahedra, parallel orthogonal views from uniformly random directions, and measure-zero exceptional directions removed. The exact checker is finite algebraic proof, not an external independent certificate. First public source date is the 2007-02-14 arXiv version; the older 1996 source is bibliographically dated only to the month.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
