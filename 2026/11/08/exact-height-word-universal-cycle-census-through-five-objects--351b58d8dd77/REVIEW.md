**PASS.** The height-word definition agrees with the primary source. Balanced connected overlap graphs have Euler tours; the forced-outdegree-one vertex lemma is proved from the missing-height condition, and every forced corridor ends at a complete vertex by cycling a maximum letter to the end of an allowed word. Contracting the corridors preserves all edge-distinct cyclic tours. The directed BEST and matrix–tree results give the precise normalization. The actual final-package computations provide exact integer cofactors with an independent modular/CRT method, a Hadamard upper bound, direct small Euler enumeration and the complete order-five labeled corridor certificate. No extrapolation from finite experiments is used.

## Originality

**PASS, with historical-priority risk.** Full relevant sections of Horan–Hurlbert (2012), Sawada–Wong (2020), Sawada–Sears–Trautrim–Williams (2023), and Sawada–Gabrić (2025) were inspected. These establish existence, efficient construction, and a general arborescence-based uniform-sampling method for weak-order universal cycles, but do not evaluate the complete multiplicity at orders four and five. The prior universal enumeration method itself is not claimed as new. The classical BEST/matrix–tree formulas are acknowledged. The searchable ordered Bell table gives counts of weak orders, not counts of cycles. Scientific searches and published research-record comparisons did not locate matching evaluated determinants or a stronger tabulated enumeration. Some unindexed historical calculations could still cover the result.

## Value

**PASS.** This is the complete cardinality of a natural family of cyclic universal schedules, measuring multiplicity rather than existence or one efficient construction. The order-five 106-digit answer and the explicit forced-corridor compression isolate a useful structural simplification in a graph with many deterministic vertices, and the exact computations are reproducible. The result is explicitly finite and makes no unsupported all-order closed-form or applications claim.
