# Scientific review: exact hyperbolic-annulus geodesic escape

**Correctness (PASS).** The geometric tangent condition is reduced rigorously to Euclidean chords of a concentric disk using the Klein model; the model maps hyperbolic radius \(r\) to Euclidean radius \(\tanh r\) and preserves geodesic *segments*. Since the smaller angle between iid uniform azimuths is uniform on \([0,\pi]\), endpoint independence reduces the two-point escape probability to a single explicit integral with the correct area density \(\sinh r/(\cosh b-\cosh a)\). Integration by parts and the substitution \(u=\sqrt{\cosh^2r-\cosh^2a}\) evaluate that integral exactly. Strict monotonicity follows from stochastic dominance; boundary limits follow from Taylor expansion and concentration at large radius. Hyperboloid segment-distance computations, independent quadrature, and limiting checks in `check_hyperbolic_annulus.py` support each analytical stage. The proof is complete for \(0<a<b<\infty\); no computation is treated as an infinite certificate.

**Originality (PASS, provisional priority).** Materially inspected sources are Calka–Tykesson (arXiv:1012.5220, §§1–4: random Poisson obstacles and fixed-origin ray visibility), Cammarota–Orsingher (arXiv:1104.1043, §3.1 Theorem 3.1 and §3.2: Brownian annulus exit harmonic functions), and Xu (arXiv:1410.4052, Introduction, §2.2 and §5: Liouville-random geodesics, Crofton and ideal-polygon chord lengths). They do **not** state or imply the probability for the unique geodesic between two independent *area-uniform* points of a finite hyperbolic annulus. Own earlier Euclidean annulus Beer-index and Euclidean spherical shell calculations have distinct sampling measures and metrics; the spherical-zone theorem concerns positive curvature. Targeted published-finding corpus searches for tangent-angle aliases, disk obstacle penetration and the nonzero infinite-radius limit found related but not covering records. In particular the published hyperbolic illumination-disk expansion concerns volume increments, and the hyperbolic annulus distortion paper concerns quasiconformal maps rather than endpoint-pair visibility. General hyperbolic geometry and integration techniques are prior art and are not claimed as novel. The remaining risk is a potentially unindexed special-case computation in older integral-geometry literature; independent historical priority verification has not occurred.

**Value (PASS).** This is a complete elementary closed law for a canonical nonconvex domain in constant negative curvature. In addition to the exact finite-parameter probability, it proves a strict outer-radius ordering and two sharp boundary limits, one with an explicit thin-annulus square-root constant. The crucial persistent positive probability at infinite outer radius is a curvature-specific phenomenon: the corresponding Euclidean annulus loses obstruction entirely. The invariant is directly a two-point visibility/Beer-index statistic, and the exact value permits reproducible model comparisons and simulation checks. The scope is restricted to concentric annuli in \(\mathbb H^2\), curvature \(-1\), ambient minimizing geodesics and independent Riemannian-area-uniform endpoints. No extension to arbitrary obstacles, higher dimensions, random line samples or Brownian paths is claimed.

**Closest checked literature and limits.** Calka–Tykesson, first public arXiv 2010-12-23; Cammarota–Orsingher, first public arXiv 2011-04-06; Xu, first public arXiv 2014-10-15. Specialized historical integral-geometry work outside these inspected primary texts may have overlapping formulas. The finite checker tests selected configurations, not all radii; the proof covers the continuum analytically.
