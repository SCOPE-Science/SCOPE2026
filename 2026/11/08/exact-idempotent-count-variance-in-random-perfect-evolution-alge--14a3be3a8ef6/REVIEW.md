# Scientific review: exact idempotent-count variance in finite-field evolution algebras

## Correctness (PASS)

The quantified claim concerns every \(n\ge2\), all prime powers \(q\), and uniformly selected labeled invertible structure matrices. A fixed nonzero idempotent contributes probability \(1/(q^n-1)\). The paired probability is either \(1/((q^n-1)(q^n-q))\), for simultaneously independent original vectors and squares, or zero. The omitted pairs are characterized and counted exactly by support and signs, giving the stated binomial closed form. Transposition of structure-matrix conventions preserves the uniform ensemble. The variance identity uses the already proved mean of one; it does not assume independence. The one-dimensional exception and odd/even characteristic boundary are explicit. Exhaustive matrix enumeration over ten finite parameter pairs, including extension fields and dimensions three and four, independently corroborates the formula. The finite checks are not substitutes for the universal proof.

## Originality (PASS, with recorded residual risks)

The full 2026 survey was inspected at its definitions, Remark 23, Question 22 and Conjecture 2. Remark 23 asserts only the unit first moment. The conjecture predicts a large-field permutation fixed-point law without proving the exact finite-\(q\) variance. Wei–Zou 2023 counts isomorphism **classes**, not element idempotents of randomly labeled structure matrices; its theorems therefore do not determine this second moment. The previously obtained dimension-two distribution already determines the \(n=2\) specialization, which is not claimed as new. Actual published-index, primary-literature, and historical-record searches for variance, second factorial moments, and coordinatewise-square dependencies found no equivalent all-dimensional formula. Search failure is not proof of uniqueness; implicit coverage in uninspected older literature remains a risk.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

Finite-field statistics of nonzero idempotents are explicitly motivated by Remark 23 and Conjecture 2 of the 2026 survey. An exact second moment for **every** dimension is the first fluctuation invariant beyond its first-moment observation, distinguishes all odd fields from even fields, and displays the precise correction governing convergence to the proposed permutation fixed-point statistics. The correction is not a restatement of the mean, not implied by a dimension-two classification when \(n\ge3\), and is nonzero in every odd field. Its support/sign origin gives a structural explanation, not merely a tabulated numerical extension. It does not establish the full distribution or conjectured Galois monodromy, and makes no such claim.
