# Exact independence polynomial of triangular commuting graphs over radical-square-zero chain rings

## Finding
Let \(R\) be a finite commutative chain ring whose nonzero maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) satisfies \(\mathfrak m^2=0\), and let \(q=|R/\mathfrak m|\). Thus \(q\) is a prime power, \(q\ge2\), and \(|R|=q^2\). Let \(\Gamma_R\) be the simple commuting graph of the upper triangular matrix ring \(T_2(R)\): its vertices are noncentral matrices, and two distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when they commute. Define its ordinary independence polynomial by
\[
I_R(z)=\sum_{S\subseteq V(\Gamma_R)\atop S\text{ independent}}z^{|S|}.
\]
For every such \(R\),
\[
\boxed{I_R(z)=\bigl(1+q^2(q^2-q)z\bigr)^{q(q+1)}
+q^2(q^2-1)z\bigl(1+q^2(q^2-q)z\bigr)^{q^2}.}
\]
Consequently, for each integer \(k\ge1\), the number \(i_k\) of independent sets of size \(k\) is
\[
i_k=\binom{q(q+1)}k\bigl(q^2(q^2-q)\bigr)^k
+q^2(q^2-1)\binom{q^2}{k-1}\bigl(q^2(q^2-q)\bigr)^{k-1},
\]
where an out-of-range binomial coefficient is zero, and \(i_0=1\). In particular,
\[
\boxed{\alpha(\Gamma_R)=q(q+1),\qquad
\#\{\text{maximum independent sets}\}
=\bigl(q^2(q^2-q)\bigr)^{q(q+1)}.}
\]
Every maximum independent set chooses precisely one matrix above a unimodular generator of each point of \(\mathbb P^1(R)\). The entire polynomial therefore depends only on the residue-field order \(q\), not on the isomorphism type of \(R\).

## Assumptions and scope
A finite commutative chain ring is a finite commutative unital ring whose ideals form a chain. Its unique maximal ideal is principal, say \(\mathfrak m=\pi R\). Here \(\mathfrak m\ne0\) and \(\mathfrak m^2=0\). Every nonzero ideal is then \(\mathfrak m\) or \(R\); \(\operatorname{ann}(\pi)=\mathfrak m\), \(|\mathfrak m|=q\), \(|R|=q^2\), and \(|R^\times|=q^2-q\). These assertions follow because multiplication by \(\pi\) induces an additive-group isomorphism \(R/\mathfrak m\to\mathfrak m\), and an element outside \(\mathfrak m\) is a unit.

The center of \(T_2(R)\) consists of scalar matrices. The graph is simple, with adjacency only for two *distinct* commuting noncentral matrices. A vector \(u=(x,y)\in R^2\) is *primitive* (unimodular) when \(Rx+Ry=R\); a projective point is the free rank-one module \(Ru\) generated by such a vector. All primitive generators of the same point differ by a unit scalar. A vector is *nonprimitive* precisely if both entries lie in \(\mathfrak m\). Independent sets are unordered subsets, not tuples.

## Proof
Write \(Q=q^2\), \(U=q^2-q\), and \(P=q(q+1)\). For
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}d&e\\0&f\end{pmatrix},
\]
put \(\rho(A)=(a-c,b)\) and \(\rho(B)=(d-f,e)\). Direct multiplication shows
\[
AB=BA\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\det(\rho(A),\rho(B))=0.
\]
Further, \(A\) is noncentral exactly when \(\rho(A)\ne0\). For each nonzero \(v\in R^2\), the fiber \(\rho^{-1}(v)\) has exactly \(Q\) members, parametrized by \(c\in R\). Distinct elements in one fiber commute. Hence an independent set contains at most one matrix from each vector fiber, with \(Q\) choices when it selects that vector.

There are \(Q^2-q^2\) primitive vectors in \(R^2\). A primitive vector generates a unique projective point, and each point has \(U=Q-q\) primitive generators. Thus the number of projective points is
\[
\frac{Q^2-q^2}{Q-q}=q(q+1)=P.
\]
If \(u\) is primitive, complete it to an invertible basis of \(R^2\). In that basis, \(\det(u,v)=0\) forces \(v\) to be a scalar multiple of \(u\). When \(v\) is also primitive, that scalar must be a unit. Therefore two primitive vectors have zero determinant exactly when they generate the same projective point. Consequently, for every projective point one may independently select no matrix, or one of the \(QU\) matrices over its primitive generators; choices from different points never commute. This gives \((1+QUz)^P\) independent sets without a nonprimitive vector.

There are exactly \(q^2-1=Q-1\) nonzero nonprimitive vectors \(v\in\mathfrak m^2\). Every pair of them has zero determinant because \(\mathfrak m^2=0\), so an independent set may contain at most one of them. Write any one as \(v=\pi w\), where the reduction \(\bar w\in(R/\mathfrak m)^2\) is nonzero. For primitive \(u\),
\[
\det(v,u)=\pi\det(w,u)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\det(\bar w,\bar u)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bar u\in (R/\mathfrak m)\bar w.
\]
Here the first equivalence uses \(\operatorname{ann}(\pi)=\mathfrak m\), and the last is linear algebra over the residue field. Reduction induces a surjection \(\mathbb P^1(R)\to\mathbb P^1(R/\mathfrak m)\), and each residue projective point has exactly \(q\) projective lifts. For example, a residue point with first coordinate nonzero has normalized lifts \((1,t)\) with \(t\in R\) reducing to the prescribed slope; there are \(q\) of these, and likewise for the point at infinity. Thus the chosen nonprimitive vector commutes with exactly the \(q\) primitive projective fibers over its residue direction, and is noncommuting with every primitive vector from the other \(P-q=q^2\) projective points.

Selecting exactly one nonprimitive vector gives \(Q(Q-1)\) choices of the matrix in its fiber, a factor of \(z\), and independently for each of the \(q^2\) compatible primitive projective points, \(1+QUz\) choices. Therefore independent sets containing one nonprimitive vector contribute \(Q(Q-1)z(1+QUz)^{q^2}\). Adding the two disjoint cases proves the polynomial and coefficient formula.

The first summand has degree \(P\) with leading coefficient \((QU)^P\); the second has degree \(q^2+1<P\) because \(q\ge2\). Thus all maximum independent sets use precisely one primitive matrix from each projective point and none from nonprimitive vectors, proving the claimed extremal census. The formula involves only \(q\), proving residue-field universality.

## Verification
A standalone Python program `verify.py` constructs arithmetic in \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\), \(\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^2)\), \(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\), and \(\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^2)\) directly from addition and multiplication functions. It recomputes primitive vectors, projective points, pairwise determinant adjacency, nonprimitive exclusion counts, and the closed polynomial. For both rings with residue size \(q=2\), it independently enumerates every compatible subset of the 15 nonzero vector fibers and lifts its size-\(k\) contribution by \(|R|^k\). Both yield 610173 independent matrix subsets and 262144 maximum independent sets; the graph has 60 vertices and independence number 6. For residue size \(q=3\), it checks all structural local-projective incidences and the closed polynomial, but does not exhaustively enumerate every independent subset of the 720-vertex matrix graph. The field boundaries \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\) are also exhaustively checked against their different, semisimple formula. A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK`.

These finite checks support, but do not replace, the proof for all finite chain rings of radical length two and all prime-power residue orders.

## Relationship to prior work
Cheraghpour, Ghosseiri, Jafari, and Seyfpour (first publicly posted 20 February 2024, arXiv:2402.12619v1) establish the general determinant reduction for triangular commuting graphs and study degrees, cliques, connectivity and certain independence numbers. They do not provide the complete independence-set distribution for radical-square-zero chain rings in their inspected full text. Their finite-field theorem is the boundary case in which the nonprimitive term vanishes, not a direct proof for local rings with nilpotents.

An independently published result, “Exact independence number for triangular commuting graphs over residue rings” (published-finding corpus, 4 October 2026), proves \(\alpha(\Gamma(T_2(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)))=|\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)|\). Its inspected proof bounds extremal sets by choosing distinct projective points; it does not determine the full independence polynomial, the number of maximum independent sets, or residue-field universality across nonisomorphic chain rings. The present polynomial specializes to its independence-number conclusion when \(R=\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z\), and is strictly more informative for the specified radical-square-zero family.

## Limitations
The formula concerns the simple commuting graph of upper triangular \(2\times2\) matrices and **chain rings of radical length exactly two**. It does not claim the same polynomial for arbitrary local rings with nonprincipal square-zero radicals, higher radical length, noncommutative coefficient rings, or larger matrix size. It does not classify the graph up to isomorphism, or independent sets up to graph automorphism. General combinatorial polynomial-enumeration methods could in principle subsume the formula without mentioning this graph; targeted comparison did not identify such a covering specialization. Publication priority remains provisional until independently checked.
