# Review of the circular-arc coordinate VC profile

## Correctness

PASS. The cyclic-order reduction is exact for distinct angular coordinate values, and ties cannot add traces. A single cyclic order generates exactly \(d\) traces in each nonempty proper cardinality layer, giving the unrestricted middle-layer upper bound. The displayed constructions for \(d=3,4,5,6,7\) cover every binary pattern, as independently checked from complete powersets by the accompanying standalone program. These upper and lower bounds prove each VC value for one through eight features. The exhaustive computation only verifies the finite displayed witnesses; it is not used to infer the universal upper bound.

## Originality

PASS within accessible evidence. The ordinary real-valued stump dimensions in Leboeuf–LeBlanc–Marchand (2020) concern a single linear threshold rather than a two-ended circular arc. Earlier universal-cycle results by Dębski–Lonc (2016) concern potentially long cyclic sequences and fixed-size subset coverage, rather than a minimum number of permutations covering all subset cardinalities. Meihua–Meiling Guan–Jirimutu (2019) and predecessors already give tight seven-cycle decompositions of triples at order seven; **that decomposition is not claimed as new**. The compared statements do not supply the whole simultaneous pair/triple coverage and exact one-coordinate circular-arc VC profile at feature counts one through eight. Searches under alternate terms such as cyclic-interval covering and circular coordinate stumps did not establish an equivalent profile. Residual risk: an unlocated classification of coverings by several cyclic orders may imply this exact profile.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Circular covariates naturally represent phases, angles, and directions. One-arc-on-one-feature classifiers are a minimal interpretable model for such data, but their capacity is not governed by the known single-threshold line-stump formula. The exact VC-profile exhibits two genuinely discrete effects: three circular coordinates still shatter at most five points, whereas a fourth shatters six; and five through eight coordinates all shatter exactly seven points. The finite witnesses meet the middle-layer lower bounds, including a tight simultaneous design at seven points. These sharp initial cutoffs supply a natural benchmark for larger-dimensional cyclic-order coverings. No claim of a full asymptotic solution is made.
