# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The theorem's domain is every nonempty proper subset of the labeled classical Černý automaton for each integer \(n\geq3\). Inverse rotation shifts the least cyclic run-start left by exactly one while it is positive; a nonexpanding inverse merge cannot decrease this run-start. Consequently the first growth requires at least \(r\) rotations and one merge. The proposed word achieves this length, and equality forces uniqueness. A separate complete membership-bit count yields each histogram coefficient, including the boundary class \(r=n-1\). The included checker exhaustively verifies reverse shortest-path distances for all subsets through eleven states and optimal-word uniqueness through eight states; the all-state proof is independent of the experiment.

## Originality

**PASS under the inspected evidence, with residual historical-priority risk.** Miccinesi (2026), arXiv:2609.21603, Section 8.1, proves that the classical Černý family necessarily contains subsets not extensible within \(n-1\) letters but does not characterize them or give their exact lengths. Berlinkov–Ferens–Szykuła (2017), arXiv:1704.08233, gives general preimage problems and algorithms, not the family-wide optimal word and full distribution. Related forward avoidance and rank-deficiency theorems do not imply an arbitrary-target minimum inverse growth formula. Priority against unpublished or poorly indexed specialist notes remains unresolved; search absence is not a proof of originality.

## Value

**PASS.** The result is a complete exact classification of the natural extension statistic that underlies a standard synchronization method. It resolves, for a classical extremal automaton family, precisely which subsets breach the \(n-1\) extension threshold and how rare they are, and supplies unique shortest witnesses and a closed all-parameter distribution. The value is the sharp structural boundary and description of the complete subset lattice, not a claim to improve general reset bounds.

## Limitations

Only the classical labeled two-letter Černý family is covered. The finding does not imply new reset-word length bounds. Finite checks cannot by themselves prove the infinite theorem; the proof supplies the needed general argument. Definitive historical priority and independent validation are outstanding.
