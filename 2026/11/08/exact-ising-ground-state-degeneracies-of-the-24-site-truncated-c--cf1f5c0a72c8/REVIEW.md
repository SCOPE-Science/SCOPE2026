# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The 24-vertex graph and its two bond classes are explicitly defined, as is the Hamiltonian's normalization. The claim is the full labeled degeneracy profile, not an assertion that the general three-branch energy formula is new. The complete Gray-code enumeration counts all \(2^{24}\) configurations exactly and agrees with a completely separate combinatorial enumeration of the appropriate constrained cube-port assignments. Their counts give the critical-field magnetization profiles and the five degeneracies, with all bond parameters positive. The proof identifies all equality cases and their exhaustive combinatorial counts; full-state enumeration separately checks the global energy comparisons. Both checkers are included, not just logs.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with limitations

This is a new exact enumeration on the physically natural 24-site truncated cube, not the earlier general phase-energy theorem. The prior general triangle-expansion work supplies the structural ground-manifold description and hence implies the forms of the enumerated problems, but does not itself provide their exact cube-specific nontrivial solutions. The prior truncated-cube independence-complex record may already supply or imply the upper-critical independent-set count; the joint five-region degeneracy profile, especially the 450, 11682 and 828 low/intermediate counts, is the distinctive candidate claim. Full text of the 2004 and 2015 related primary articles was inspected; the 2018 closely motivated quantum material article was accessible only by abstract. No examined source supplied all these exact counts, but unindexed tables remain a substantive priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The specific object is a documented finite magnetic trimer cluster of 24 sites, so its exact degeneracy and zero-temperature entropy at both field crossings are natural physical and combinatorial invariants. Counts at the two crossing fields exceed both adjacent plateau counts by large factors (\(11682\) and \(26441\)), a concrete testable finite-cluster distinction. The result does not purport to introduce new bulk scaling or a new universal energy diagram.
