# Scientific review: repeated tortoise on Sturmian factor languages

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is proved for every irrational slope in the open unit interval and all positive integer lengths and iteration counts. The rotation-word factor partition gives \(n+1\) distinct cells, the weight split gives two inequivalent arcs, and crossing an internal cut exchanges precisely two adjacent letters with the exchanged \(1\) having rank \(\lceil j\alpha\rceil\). The critical converse does not infer injectivity from adjacent noncollisions: for any two cells separated by a late cut it selects the largest crossed cut, whose differing output digit survives because at least \(k\) ones precede it. The resulting exact class count and threshold follow. The script independently reproduced 11,200 complexity comparisons, 320 orbit-language comparisons, and 25,550 sampled rotation cells. Finite checks support rather than replace the general argument.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Campbell and Rampersad (arXiv:2604.25811, first made public April 28, 2026) define the relevant equivalence and solve selected automatic-word cases, and their conclusion expressly raises Sturmian generalizations. Defant and Kravitz (arXiv:1809.09158) define the sorting operator but do not enumerate its images on Sturmian factor sets. Peltomäki (arXiv:1905.06138) gives the Sturmian rotation interval framework for Abelian periods rather than an iterated tortoise quotient. An accessible earlier specific result for a **single** tortoise pass and slopes greater than \(1/3\) is a genuine partial overlap: it is a \(k=1\) consequence of the new formula, not an all-iterate theorem. Meaning-based indexed searches and title/phrase searches found no statement that implies the full formula. Unindexed work and easy-to-overlook reformulations remain the main risks.

## Value — PASS

This is a natural all-parameter solution to a recent factor-complexity question. It identifies the entire sequence of complexity drops between ordinary Sturmian factor complexity and Abelian complexity, not just an isolated count or computation. The exact rank of each discarded rotation cut and the first length at which Abelian equivalence splits offer a structural explanation and a useful benchmark for partial sorting of symbolic-dynamical languages.

## Scope and limits

Binary Sturmian languages with irrational slope only; no claim about arbitrary periodic words, nonbinary alphabets or general stack-sorting operations. Independent validation and exhaustive global historical priority assessment have not been performed.
