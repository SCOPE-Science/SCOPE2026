# Review of exact Reeve-tetrahedron covering and torus statistics

## Correctness — PASS
The pointwise scale is derived from the unique nonnegative barycentric presentation relative to an integer translate. Fixing the third lattice coordinate gives an exact fractional-part minimization; translating that coordinate by a full height-period only increases the objective, and the minimum within one period occurs at the last grid point not exceeding the smaller transverse fractional coordinate. The resulting formula is valid for every real input, without assuming a generic point or neglecting tie cases. Its supremum is approached by explicitly compatible fractional coordinates and equals the covering radius. The torus mean and uncovered unit-volume follow from independent uniform coordinates and exact elementary integrals. The limit law follows from a deterministic uniform error bound. A separate exact-arithmetic program checks 19,773 pointwise rational instances and the integration identities. The program is supplementary to, rather than a substitute for, the proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with recorded priority risk)
The closest accessible full-length recent source, Codenotti–Freyer–Krivokuća (arXiv:2606.14442v1), concerns minimal covering bodies and sharp planar covering-radius Minkowski inequalities, rather than the pointwise entry-scale law or torus coverage fraction of the three-dimensional Reeve family. Codenotti–Santos–Schymura (2021/2022) study *non-hollow* lattice simplices, whereas the Reeve family is hollow. Negative searches do not establish global uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Reeve's hollow tetrahedra are a standard lattice-geometric test family in which normalized volume grows unboundedly. A complete pointwise lattice-covering threshold is a natural geometric invariant beyond lattice-point counts. Exact covering radius, exact fraction of the fundamental torus uncovered at unit scale, mean threshold, and the limiting law jointly describe a qualitative degeneration: despite diverging tetrahedron volume, the covering threshold approaches one while the uncovered fraction decays like twice the reciprocal height. The exact coverage fraction is not implied by the mere radius or generic algorithms for computing covering minima. The result is deliberately limited to the standard lattice and this named family; no global minimization over lattices or classification of all empty tetrahedra is claimed.
