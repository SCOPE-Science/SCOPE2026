# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof derives the unique adjacent marker gaps \(g_j=(B-1)B^j\), uses cyclic ordering to exclude factors with three or more marked symbols, and solves the two-marker case via the maximal-leading-padding rotations and their predecessor-gap constraints. All three residual small classes are exhibited by actual factors. The Abelian result follows from the exact minimal span of \(r\) consecutive powers and the unit-step changes in weights of sliding windows. The complete-factor finite enumerator reproduced the formulas for bases \(2\) through \(11\) and lengths \(1\) through \(160\); finite tests do not establish the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Bell–Shallit (2021) supplies the definition, general upper bound and automaticity but not the exact geometric-power Lie classes; its Cantor sequence uses a different support. De Luca–Fici (2022) treats Sturmian languages, not words with unbounded marker-free gaps. Fici–Postic–Silva (2019) treats Abelian antipowers and complexity of the different Cantor/Sierpiński word. Indexed mathematical records and accessible earlier research claims offered no implication covering the exact rotation classes. The Abelian logarithm is an elementary companion identity and is not asserted to be new by itself. An unindexed equivalent theorem remains a real bibliographic risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The statement is a complete classification of a naturally defined modern complexity invariant for every length in an infinite family of automatic words. The exhaustive exceptional set and the divergence between eventually constant Lie complexity and unbounded Abelian complexity make the exact answer mathematically informative beyond a tiny example or a renaming.

## Limitations
The proof covers one-sided geometric-power support only. It does not determine ordinary factor complexity, and no independent validation has taken place. Literature searches, however broad, cannot establish uniqueness.
