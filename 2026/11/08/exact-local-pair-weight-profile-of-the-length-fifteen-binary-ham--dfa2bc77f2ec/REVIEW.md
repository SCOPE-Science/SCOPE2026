# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For a nonzero proper binary support the cyclic pair weight is its Hamming weight plus the number of runs. In a binary \([15,11,3]\) Hamming code, pair weight four occurs exactly at consecutive collinear triples of parity-check columns. The group \(\mathrm{GL}(4,2)\) has size \(20,160\), acts freely on all ordered column matrices, and is transitive on ordered distinct nonzero starting pairs. The explicit finite suffix recurrence evaluates to the seven claimed integer coefficients and their normalization by \(96\). Both the recursive Python and independent forward C++ implementations replay from the packaged source, and the analytical first two factorial moments agree.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The primary coding sources provide the pair metric, optimality bounds, constructions and existence but do not imply the frequency distribution across all \(64,864,800\) indexed Hamming-code presentations. In equivalent Steiner-system language, Blackburn–Etzion's cyclic results and Erskine–Griggs's small-order tables establish existence of block-avoiding sequencings, not the full count of cyclic consecutive lines. The local length-seven classification in the accessible prior ledger cannot imply the fifteen-coordinate enumeration. Exact-number, alias and broader-coverage searches yielded no covering statement. Unindexed tabulations or unpublished calculations remain a residual priority risk, and one early conference article was accessible only in abstract.

## Value — PASS

Length fifteen is the first binary Hamming length with parity-check dimension four, and its \(35\) projective lines give a natural nontrivial interleaving design problem. The seven-value distribution counts all coordinate arrangements by the exact number of vulnerable pair-weight-four codewords; the zero class gives the precise population of optimally interleaved codes, rather than just an isolated construction. A distinct sharp upper endpoint of six describes the maximum local concentration of vulnerable triples. The finite result is useful as a reproducible benchmark for selecting and evaluating symbol-pair layouts.
