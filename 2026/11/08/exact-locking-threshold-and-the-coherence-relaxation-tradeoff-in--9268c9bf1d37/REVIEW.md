# Review of the exact locking threshold and the coherence–relaxation tradeoff

**Correctness — PASS.** At a stationary lock the two outer force-balance equations imply equality of nearest-neighbor phase sines. The resulting symmetric and complementary branches exhaust equilibria. The complementary branch has a negative weighted-Laplacian trace and is not linearly attracting; symmetric Jacobian eigenvalues are derived explicitly. Strict concavity of the positive-phase torque yields the exact unique stable branch and fold threshold. Envelope differentiation and the implicit function theorem give strict budget/coherence monotonicity and both sharp asymptotics. The accompanying script computes the full Jacobian independently at representative parameters.

**Originality — PASS within available literature.** The prescribed detuning-weighted budget normalization comes from Pando et al. Their finite-exponent results concern large-ensemble coherence and continuum self-consistency, not this exact triad classification or vanishing relaxation exponent. Mikaberidze–Taylor supplies the general budget lower bound, so this lower bound is explicitly excluded from the new contribution. Tanaka–Aoyagi studies budget-constrained network optimization, Xu et al. studies frequency-weighted mean-field effects, and Xin–Kikkawa–Liu studies standard uniformly coupled small Kuramoto systems, while Garijo–Gómez–Arenas develops finite-size convex geometry for equal edge couplings. No inspected source establishes the entire exact finite-\(p\) coherence-versus-relaxation law. The latter source's full text was not available, leaving an acknowledged priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Detuning-based reallocation is proposed to improve coherence at fixed hardware cost. A sharp, fully classified smallest connected example shows that improving the stationary coherence objective can simultaneously destroy dynamical robustness, with exact matching exponential scales. This is a motivated boundary to large-network finite-exponent optimization, not a numerical parameter slice or a restatement of the generic budget lower bound.

**Scientific limitations.** All claims concern local transverse linear stability of a symmetric, three-node deterministic Kuramoto flow, not global synchronization or optimal allocation in larger ensembles. The source comparisons are not independent historical certification; the standalone tests corroborate but do not certify the analytic proof.
