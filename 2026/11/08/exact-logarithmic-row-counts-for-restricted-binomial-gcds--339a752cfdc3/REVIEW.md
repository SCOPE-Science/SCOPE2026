# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Lucas's theorem gives an exact equivalence between prime divisibility of the selected gcd and minimal zero-sum base-prime digit tokens. A length bound of the cyclic group and a swapping proof for maximal-length minimal zero sums determine the unique degree-maximal digit patterns. The finite-profile formula follows by multinomial placements at periodic exponent classes; all remaining profiles contribute only a lower polynomial degree. The proof covers every \(m\ge2\), prime \(p\nmid m\), and all admissible row multipliers. The standalone integer checker replayed 11,866 independent digitwise-Lucas comparisons, 763,424 cyclic zero-sum words, and five exact-profile families. No infinite conclusion rests on finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The main eligible research source (Fairfax-Ball, arXiv:2610.01328v1) studies *maximal valuations* and *least extremal rows*, not the frequency of rows with positive valuation. McTague's theorem already covers the \(p\equiv1\pmod m\) regime, and its \(h=1\) counting consequence is acknowledged as prior coverage. The September 2026 minus-one classification can imply much of the \(h=2\) case. Wu's full arXiv text treats a different conditional valuation characterization; it does not supply a uniform quasi-polynomial or leading coefficient for all coprime parameter pairs. The contribution is the complete arbitrary-order statement, particularly \(h\ge3\), with the explicit \(h^{1-m}\) leading-factor law. The relevant primary full texts and research-record searches identified no stronger covering result. Unindexed work or a general previously published digit-language enumeration result could affect priority; the minus-one full text was inaccessible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The recent sharp-maximum result leaves a distinct natural quantitative question: how frequently does a fixed prime divide the gcd across all rows? The theorem proves exact quasi-polynomiality for geometric cutoffs, a sharp asymptotic with arithmetic leading constant for ordinary cutoffs, and identifies the least eventual quasipolynomial period four in the natural order-four instance. It demonstrates that positive valuations, though constructively attainable, are only polylogarithmically frequent, and it isolates multiplicative order as the controlling invariant for the highest-degree term. This is a complete natural family of precise row-count invariants rather than an arbitrary small numerical instance.

## Limitations and closest literature

The conclusion is restricted to fixed \(m,p\), coprime pairs, and first-power prime divisibility. It does not classify higher prime-adic valuations or a prime-uniform average. Prior work in the congruence classes \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod m\) overlaps special cases; those cases are not individually claimed as novel.
