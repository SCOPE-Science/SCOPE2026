# Scientific review: exact repeated-subsequence length for two-run powers

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem quantifies every positive run length and number of periods, every integer repetition exponent at least two, and the zero-optimum boundary. The argument proves a nonstandard necessary inequality: each maximal single-symbol block in a repeated subsequence consumes enough distinct source runs to cover its ceiling-length capacity, while split cyclic endpoint blocks contribute at least the same ceiling cost as the joined cyclic block. This yields separate source-run inequalities and the exact mixed-root length bound. Constructive mixed and unary witnesses attain every displayed competing bound. The binary root alternatives exhaust all possibilities. The standalone program checks 213 exhaustive configurations, 28,642 cyclic-cost probes, and 78 independently partitioned square optima. The finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations
The strongest direct primary comparison is Bannai–I–Köppl, *Longest (Sub-)Periodic Subsequence* (arXiv:2202.07189, initially 2022-02-15). Its inspected sections develop general algorithms for periodic/subperiodic subsequences and LCS-based factorizations but not a closed all-exponent optimal length on \((0^a1^b)^k\). Inoue–Inenaga–Bannai (arXiv:2006.00216, 2020-05-30) provide a general one-input longest-square algorithm and a partition/LCS identity. Inoue et al. (CPM 2018, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2018.15, 2018-05-18) handle two-input common square subsequences by matching rectangles. These algorithmic theorems compute an answer on general inputs but do not themselves express the stated closed form or distinguish mixed and unary roots.

The nearest structural comparisons involve the shortest embedding span of an arbitrary pattern in freely chosen windows of an infinite periodic word, and square-subsequence counts for powers of *distinct-letter* permutations. Neither is the present fixed-origin binary run word with arbitrary positive run lengths and arbitrary prescribed repetition exponent. A sufficiently worked-out specialization of the general embedding framework could conceivably imply the result; no exhaustive priority claim is made. Targeted research-record and literature searches did not reveal an equivalent formula, but indexing gaps remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Longest periodic or square scattered subsequences are an established algorithmic problem with costly general methods. This result resolves the optimum throughout a natural infinite four-parameter compressed-input family by a closed formula. It also gives a sharp structural distinction between non-unary roots, whose entire attainable maximum is governed by complete periods, and unary roots, which can exploit leftover runs. The explicit imbalance thresholds quantify precisely when this takeover occurs, rather than presenting a small numerical or parameter-specific speedup. The cyclic ceiling-budget lemma is reusable for other capacity-constrained repetitive subsequences. The theorem does not enumerate optimal roots or extend to unrestricted run sequences.
