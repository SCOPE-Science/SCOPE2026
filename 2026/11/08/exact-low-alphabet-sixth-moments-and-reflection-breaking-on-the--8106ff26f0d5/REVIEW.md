# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

A complete coefficient gauge reduces the problem to exactly \(q^3\) cases for each of the five phase alphabets. The sixth-power Fourier moment is exactly the coefficient energy of the cube of the normalized polynomial. Independent formal expansion gives the 21-term Laurent polynomial in the proof. Every one of the 440 normalized possibilities was checked in exact cyclotomic arithmetic, with exact comparison in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), and all ties counted. The standalone program reproduces the classification, and an exact Gaussian-rational computation checks the continuous-phase witness. For discrete torus sizes at least seven, nonaliasing follows from the width-six support of the cubed polynomial. These checks prove the finite alphabet theorem but do not establish the unrestricted continuous minimum.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The closest published mathematical result is *Sharp fourth-moment phase rigidity for the six-point triangular Fourier aperture* (2026): its full mathematical proof studies the same six sites but a **fourth moment**, not the new sixth-power objective. Its sharp fourth-moment inequalities do not imply the exact sixth-moment optimizer counts or pentaphase radical. *Sharp sixth moments and phase localization for three consecutive Fourier cosets* (2026) gives an unrestricted coefficient-norm *maximum* for a different finite-group spectral support. Both were read and compared at the statement and implication level. Pai–Chen (arXiv:2102.04043) and Zhang–Fan–Mesnager (arXiv:2107.11599) treat complementary two-dimensional array sets/pairs, not a single triangular array's sixth power. A full earlier small-polyphase-array table comparison has not been completed, leaving a real bibliographic risk. Search absence is not a proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The triangular total-degree-two aperture is a canonical nonrectangular, six-site spectral shape. Its sixth moment measures the energy of threefold Fourier convolution and is not determined by the known sharp fourth-moment value. The first five nontrivial phase alphabets exhibit an exact nonmonotone hierarchy, including an algebraic fifth-root optimum with four reflection-breaking normalized equality patterns. The full exact count and a rational witness separating all five alphabets from continuous phases make this a usable low-complexity benchmark. It is a narrow self-contained classification, not a claimed global continuous solution or a theorem about all apertures.

## Scientific limitations

Only phase orders two through six and nonaliasing rectangular grids are covered. The exact continuous minimum and higher alphabet orders remain open in this treatment; an equivalent older specialist table cannot be fully excluded. No independent audit has been performed.
