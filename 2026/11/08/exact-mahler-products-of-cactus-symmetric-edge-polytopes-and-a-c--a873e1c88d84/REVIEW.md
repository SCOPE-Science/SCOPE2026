# Scientific assessment: cactus symmetric-edge Mahler products

## Correctness — PASS

The exact claim is for connected simple unweighted cactus graphs, with intrinsic volumes in the zero-sum Euclidean space. The proof makes all necessary changes of coordinates explicitly: articulation gluing yields a linear free sum, and the cycle-edge map has determinant modulus equal to the cycle length. Both parity cases are treated in the facet-by-facet primal volume calculation. The polar is identified with the central cube section, whose density has a finite inclusion–exclusion evaluation. The constant and both parity corrections in the asymptotic expansion come from an explicitly evaluated fourth Gaussian moment and the parity-sensitive central-binomial Stirling expansion. Independent numerical polar–primal hull computations for twelve graphs agree with the predicted exact ratios. Verification is corroborative; the proof does not infer an infinite statement from a finite run.

The most delicate points are the nonorthogonal linear decomposition and the even-cycle nonsimplicial facet triangulation. The former preserves Mahler product by affine invariance; the latter decomposes each balanced facet into exactly half the cycle length in simplices with the stated determinants. Neither step introduces a spurious determinant or facet multiplicity.

## Originality — PASS, subject to independent priority confirmation

Primary comparison: Alexander–Fradelizi–García-Lirola–Zvavitch, arXiv:1911.10642, Introduction and §§3,5, gives Lipschitz-free polytopes, their \(\ell_1\)-sum Mahler-product multiplication, and the four-cycle equality case, but not the evaluated product for arbitrary cycles or the parity correction. Kálmán–Tóthmérész, arXiv:2201.10501, §6, provides cycle Ehrhart and primal-volume data, not the joint primal–polar Mahler expression. Bartha–Fodor–González Merino, arXiv:2005.08292, Introduction, gives the exact diagonal cube-section integral/finite formula and its leading asymptotic, without connecting them to graph Mahler products. D'Alì–Juhnke-Kubitzke–Koch, arXiv:2307.04933, §5, provides polar cut/flow Ehrhart data for generalized symmetric edge polytopes but does not evaluate the claimed cactus volume products in the inspected sections. These established ingredients are cited and **not claimed as original**. The assessed new statement evaluates their combined geometric invariant on an entire naturally decomposable graph family and resolves its parity-specific next asymptotic term. Exact database and prior-research searches found no same statement; this is evidence of a gap, not a certificate of uniqueness.

The closest coverage risk is a previously unnoticed special evaluation or an implicit identical corollary from Lipschitz-free or flow-polytope literature. The structural gluing and individual volume ingredients are already known and constrain the originality margin. The review does not claim a novel general free-sum theorem, a novel cube-section law, or a novel pure primal volume.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The normalized Mahler product is a natural affine invariant governing comparison with the cross-polytope benchmark. A cactus is a nontrivial yet canonical class in which global graph geometry decomposes into independent cyclic building blocks. A single expression gives the exact product for every member, including arbitrarily many cycles and bridges, and resolves the phenomenon that long odd and even cycles approach the same limiting factor from opposite directions with two different sharp first corrections. This is a complete natural family classification, not an arbitrary finite sample or a generic multiplication of known numerical values. Its scope is limited to unweighted cactus graphs; no paper-sized downstream application or universal Mahler extremality is claimed.

## Remaining scientific limits

The independent priority and proof audit remains unperformed. An earlier equivalent formula under transportation-cost-space, Lipschitz polytope, graph-matroid, or flow terminology is possible. The asymptotic does not prove finite-size global monotonicity of either parity subsequence. Finite convex-hull computations independently check examples but cannot certify the all-dimensional formulas.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
