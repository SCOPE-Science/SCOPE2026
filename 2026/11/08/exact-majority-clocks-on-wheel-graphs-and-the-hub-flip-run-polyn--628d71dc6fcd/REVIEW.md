# Review of the exact wheel-majority temporal classification

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof uses a strictly decreasing two-time energy whenever a state differs from its two-step predecessor; the weighted self-diagonal resolves ties without changing the dynamics. It then classifies fixed and constant-hub two-cycle strings by their step-two connected components. The remaining two-cycles are in bijection with hub-zero rim strings satisfying exact density and no-isolated-one conditions. The cycle-run enumeration follows by marking a run boundary and dividing by the number of runs. Direct graph updates and an independent polynomial/bitstring enumeration agree for every rim size from three through nineteen. The infinite claim rests on algebraic identities and the counting proof, not on finite testing.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty.** The general period-one-or-two theorem is classical (Goles–Olivos 1980). The related cyclic majority enumeration of Agur–Fraenkel–Klein (1988) uses self-inclusive radius-one local majority and addresses fixed strings, so it does not imply this open-neighbor wheel hub-flip condition or the hub-dependent two-cycle formula. General social-network majority literature treats convergence rather than these exact wheel counts. Accessible research-record queries and claim comparisons gave no theorem that entails the stated enumeration. An unindexed equivalent theorem, or relevant inaccessible older paper, remains a residual risk; this is not a claim of certified priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The wheel graph is the natural minimal model of local cyclic interactions coupled to one globally connected voter. Its hub produces new cycles of a different symmetry class and a nontrivial joint inequality on occupancy and adjacent occupied bonds. The full infinite-family primitive-cycle enumeration, including the precise parity exceptions, is a structural dynamical invariant and a reproducible benchmark; it is not merely the classical two-period bound or a small finite table.

**Scientific limitations.** The theorem does not enumerate nonperiodic transient basins. Test programs stop at finite graph sizes, whereas the proof applies to all eligible wheel sizes. Publication priority is qualified by the sources inspected and the explicitly recorded residual risks.
