# Scientific review: complete split-graph majority periodic states

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is quantified over all positive clique and independent-set sizes and over every labelled binary initial configuration. Each clique vertex sees the global occupied population except itself; each independent vertex sees exactly the clique. This yields an exact three-case update law, including the tie boundary. It proves that unanimous consensus gives the only two fixed states and exhausts primitive two-cycles by the initial clique's being mixed or unanimous. In the mixed case, two successive complementing steps require total half occupation, forcing either a half-filled stationary independent set or the stated imbalance value. The two-cycle types have disjoint supports and no hidden fixed points; division by two counts orbits. The same local law gives first periodic-entry time at most three and an explicit state attaining exactly three for all \(c>s\ge3\) of common parity. Two distinct finite verifiers corroborate the exact formulas, including a complete labelled census and weighted state-orbit counting; finite checks are supplementary, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty
Goles–Olivos' general eventual period bound subsumes only the assertion that eventual periods are at most two, not the split-graph orbit classification, exact binomial counts or sharp witness. The 2014 Benjamini–Chan–O'Donnell–Tamuz–Tan paper concerns infinite/unimodular and random graphs rather than labelled periodic-point populations on split graphs. The arXiv:1508.03519v1 full text was examined at its definitions, main bound and symmetry theorem: it provides a graph-module contraction upper bound on convergence time, not a split-graph exact periodic census. Hence even if an upper bound applicable to these graphs is inferred from its theorem, it does not give the counted orbit classes. Exact-title, synonym, parameter and implication searches did not reveal stronger coverage; search absence is not itself a proof of novelty. Earlier unindexed classifications or the unavailable original Goles–Olivos full text remain priority risks. The result is distinct from an equal-part complete-multipartite classification, which does not cover a split graph with one large independent part and singleton clique parts in general.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Complete split graphs interpolate between stars and complete graphs and are canonical threshold graphs with two highly symmetric social groups. Fixed-point enumeration alone hides exponential recurrent multiplicity. The full primitive two-cycle count separates three mechanisms—uniform disagreement, imbalance-driven clique flips, and stationary half-filled independent vertices—and shows different arithmetic phase boundaries depending on the two part sizes. Coupling these exact orbit counts with an attained uniform three-step entrance bound is a natural complete dynamical classification, not an arbitrary one-off numerical census. It provides exact benchmarks for module-based convergence bounds and for studying persistent disagreement in dense deterministic networks.
