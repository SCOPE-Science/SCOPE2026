For uniformly distributed locations on a circle, the opposing-gap strategyproof lottery has an exact positive expected social-cost advantage over random dictatorship, equal to \(1/4-2^{-s-1}\binom{s}{(s-1)/2}\) for odd \(s=n\) or \(s=n-1\). Yet random dictatorship, opposing-gap selection, and their half-and-half mixture share the same strictly positive non-Gaussian \(n^{-1/2}\)-scale approximation penalty, described by four times the supremum of an explicit stationary Gaussian process.
