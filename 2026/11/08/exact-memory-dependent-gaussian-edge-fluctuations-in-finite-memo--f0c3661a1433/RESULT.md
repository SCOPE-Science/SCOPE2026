# Exact memory-dependent Gaussian edge fluctuations in finite-memory Pólya threshold graphs

## Finding

Let a threshold graph be constructed by successively adjoining either an isolated vertex or a vertex adjacent to every older vertex. The binary choice is generated by a finite-memory Pólya contagion process with fixed memory length \(M\). Although the associated stationary \(M\)-block law was known, the following explicit global graph fluctuation law evaluates the entire memory penalty.

**Theorem.** Fix an integer \(M\ge1\), \(0<\rho<1\) and \(\delta>0\). Begin with the first \(M\) draws of a two-color Pólya urn whose initial red fraction is \(\rho\) and reinforcement-to-initial-total ratio is \(\delta\). For each \(t>M\), draw a binary variable \(Z_t\), conditional on the past, with
\[
\Pr(Z_t=1\mid Z_1,\dots,Z_{t-1})
=\frac{\rho+\delta\sum_{j=1}^M Z_{t-j}}{1+M\delta}.
\]
At step \(t\), add vertex \(t\); when \(Z_t=1\), join it to all \(t-1\) older vertices, and otherwise join it to none. Write \(E_k\) for the number of *non-loop* edges on the first \(k\) vertices, so that \(E_k=\sum_{t=1}^k(t-1)Z_t\). Set
\[
\mathcal D_M=1+2M\delta+\frac{M(M-1)\delta^2}{1+\delta},
\qquad v=\rho(1-\rho).
\]
Then almost surely \(E_N/\binom N2\to\rho\). Moreover, in the Skorokhod space \(D([0,1])\),
\[
\boxed{\left\{\frac{E_{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}-\rho\binom{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}{2}}{N^{3/2}}:0\le t\le1\right\}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \left\{\sqrt{v\mathcal D_M}\int_0^t s\,dB_s:0\le t\le1\right\},}
\]
where \(B\) is standard Brownian motion. The Gaussian limit has covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(G(s),G(t))=\frac{v\mathcal D_M}{3}\min(s,t)^3.
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{\sqrt N\left(\frac{E_N}{\binom N2}-\rho\right)
\Longrightarrow\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{4v}{3}\mathcal D_M\right).}
\]
The exact memory-dependent variance multiplier is strictly increasing in \(M\) and satisfies \(\mathcal D_M\sim M^2\delta^2/(1+\delta)\) as \(M\to\infty\) with \(\delta\) fixed. The limit theorem itself is asserted for each **fixed** \(M\), not for simultaneous growth of \(M\) and \(N\).

For comparison, if reinforcement is never removed (the classical infinite-memory Pólya choice process), then
\[
\frac{E_N}{\binom N2}\longrightarrow P\quad\text{almost surely},
\qquad P\sim\operatorname{Beta}\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\delta},\frac{1-\rho}{\delta}\right),
\]
a nondegenerate random limit. Thus finite and infinite reinforcement memory have different first-order edge-density behavior.

## Assumptions and scope

The first \(M\) draws have the standard exchangeable Pólya distribution: for every binary word \((z_1,\ldots,z_M)\) containing \(r\) ones,
\[
\Pr(Z_1=z_1,\ldots,Z_M=z_M)
=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(\rho+j\delta)\prod_{j=0}^{M-r-1}(1-\rho+j\delta)}{\prod_{j=0}^{M-1}(1+j\delta)}.
\]
Empty products are one. The subsequent sliding-memory transition is exactly the finite-memory urn scheme: only reinforcements associated with the most recent \(M\) draws remain in the urn. Each parameter \(M,\rho,\delta\) is fixed independently of the graph size \(N\). No connectivity conditioning is imposed; the edges are counted whether or not the final graph is connected. Self-loops, present in some threshold-graph conventions, are excluded from \(E_N\).

## Proof

**1. Stationarity and finite-memory moments.** Put \(X_t=Z_t-\rho\) and \(a=\delta/(1+M\delta)\). The \(M\)-bit history \(H_t=(Z_{t-M+1},\ldots,Z_t)\) evolves by deleting its oldest coordinate and appending a bit whose conditional success probability is \((\rho+\delta\sum H_t)/(1+M\delta)\). Every such probability lies strictly between zero and one. Any history can reach any other in \(M\) steps by prescribing the next \(M\) bits, and the all-zero history has a self-loop. Thus the finite-state chain is irreducible and aperiodic.

The stated exchangeable \(M\)-block distribution is invariant: extend it by the displayed predictive probability to \(M+1\) observations; the resulting \(M+1\)-block law is the standard exchangeable Pólya law. Its first and last \(M\) coordinates have identical distributions. This also proves the initialization is stationary. The known two-coordinate moments of this block law are
\[
\mathbb E Z_t=\rho,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(Z_t)=v,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_t,Z_{t-h})=\frac{v\delta}{1+\delta}
\quad(1\le h<M).
\]
Here and below expectations refer to this stationary process.

**2. Martingale innovation and its exact variance.** Define for \(t>M\)
\[
\varepsilon_t=X_t-a\sum_{j=1}^M X_{t-j}.
\]
The predictive rule gives \(\mathbb E(\varepsilon_t\mid Z_1,\ldots,Z_{t-1})=0\). These bounded innovations form martingale differences. Their common conditional variance is \(p_t(1-p_t)\), where \(p_t=(\rho+\delta\sum_{j=1}^M Z_{t-j})/(1+M\delta)\). Stationarity and the displayed block covariances yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_\varepsilon^2
&:=\mathbb E\varepsilon_t^2
=v-a^2\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{j=1}^M X_{t-j}\right)\\
&=v-a^2\left(Mv+M(M-1)\frac{v\delta}{1+\delta}\right)\\
&=\frac{v}{(1+M\delta)^2}
\left(1+2M\delta+\frac{M(M-1)\delta^2}{1+\delta}\right)
=\frac{v\mathcal D_M}{(1+M\delta)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first equality uses orthogonality of a martingale innovation to the preceding history, equivalently the law of total variance.

**3. Functional martingale limit.** By the finite-state ergodic theorem,
\[
\frac1N\sum_{t=M+1}^{\lfloor Ns\rfloor}\mathbb E(\varepsilon_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
\longrightarrow s\sigma_\varepsilon^2
\]
almost surely for every \(0\le s\le1\), and the convergence is uniform in \(s\) by monotonicity of the quadratic-variation sums and continuity of their limiting function. Since innovations are uniformly bounded, the martingale functional central limit theorem gives
\[
N^{-1/2}\sum_{t=M+1}^{\lfloor Ns\rfloor}\varepsilon_t
\Longrightarrow \sigma_\varepsilon B_s.
\]
Discrete integration by parts, or the continuous-mapping theorem for the weighted martingale transform with the deterministic integrand \(s\), therefore gives
\[
N^{-3/2}\sum_{t=M+1}^{\lfloor Ns\rfloor}(t-1)\varepsilon_t
\Longrightarrow\sigma_\varepsilon\int_0^s u\,dB_u.
\]

**4. Recovering the edge count.** Let \(W_k=\sum_{t=1}^k(t-1)X_t\). Multiplying the autoregressive identity by \(t-1\), summing, and shifting its \(M\) lag indices proves
\[
\sup_{1\le k\le N}
\left|(1-Ma)W_k-
\sum_{t=M+1}^k(t-1)\varepsilon_t\right|
=O_M(N).
\]
Indeed, replacing the shifted coefficient \(t-1\) by \(t-j-1\) costs at most \(j\sum_{t\le N}|X_t|\le jN\), and each of the \(O(M^2)\) boundary terms is at most \(N\). This deterministic bound is uniform in the sampled path. Since \(1-Ma=1/(1+M\delta)\), division by \(N^{3/2}\) establishes the stated functional limit with factor \((1+M\delta)\sigma_\varepsilon=\sqrt{v\mathcal D_M}\).

Ergodicity also gives \(N^{-1}\sum_{t\le N}X_t\to0\) almost surely; summation by parts implies \(W_N/N^2\to0\) almost surely and therefore \(E_N/\binom N2\to\rho\). Evaluating the functional limit at time one and using \(\binom N2/N^2\to1/2\) proves the density CLT. Itô isometry gives the covariance kernel.

Finally, \(\mathcal D_{M+1}-\mathcal D_M=2\delta+2M\delta^2/(1+\delta)>0\) and the stated quadratic asymptotic follows immediately. For infinite memory, the usual Pólya de Finetti representation makes the entire draw sequence conditionally i.i.d. Bernoulli with random Beta success parameter \(P\); the weighted strong law yields the asserted random density limit. This last comparison is classical and is not claimed as a new result.

## Verification

Run `python3 check.py` using Python 3's standard library. The checker enumerates every binary memory state for \(M=1,2,3,4\), tests three independent rational parameter pairs per memory, verifies transition row sums and the stationary Pólya block law, and solves the finite-state Poisson equation by an independent rational Gaussian elimination. It evaluates the full covariance-sum coefficient as \(2\langle f,(I-P+\Pi)^{-1}f\rangle_\pi-\langle f,f\rangle_\pi\) and compares it exactly with \(v\mathcal D_M\). It additionally checks the innovation-variance identity, block covariances, and edge expectation normalization. All 402 exact arithmetic assertions pass.

This finite checker tests the algebra at selected parameters and does not prove the functional central limit theorem. The infinite-dimensional convergence rests on the analytic ergodic and martingale arguments above; the stated \(O_M(N)\) remainder is deterministic.

## Relationship to prior work

Yu, Alajaji, and Gharesifard (2026) introduced Pólya threshold graphs, studied degree laws, Laplacian structure, and consensus, and presented numerical investigations of their finite-memory variant. Their conclusion explicitly proposes further study of the finite-memory model's stochastic properties and its memory-size dependence. The graph process here is exactly that binary threshold construction, **without** their optional condition that the final vertex be universal.

Alajaji and Fuja (1993 technical report; 1994 journal version) had already developed the same finite-memory Pólya contagion sequence for communications. They proved its block distribution and ergodicity. Zhong, Alajaji, and Takahara (2007) reiterated those known results and analyzed a more general queue-based communication channel. The stationary Beta-binomial block law, the finite Markov representation, and the underlying general martingale central limit theorem are **not** new claims here. The additional result is the explicit polynomial \(\mathcal D_M\) for the global edge statistic, together with its process-level limit and Gaussian covariance kernel. A general finite-state invariance principle by itself gives a Gaussian limit with a resolvent covariance, but does not display this memory-dependent polynomial coefficient.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes \(0<\rho<1\), \(\delta>0\) and **fixed** positive integer \(M\). It does not assert a functional limit when memory grows with graph size, nor a theorem for threshold graphs conditioned to be connected. Its variance is for the non-loop edge count, not a consensus observable. Stronger historical results may imply the exact polynomial through an unexamined transformation; novelty has not been independently certified. Although exact finite Markov computations support the algebra, they do not replace the proof of a functional limit.
