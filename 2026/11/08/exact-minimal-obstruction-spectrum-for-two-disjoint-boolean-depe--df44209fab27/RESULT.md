# Exact minimal-obstruction spectrum for two disjoint Boolean dependence atoms

## Finding
Let \(Q=\{0,1\}^4\) with named coordinates \((x,y,u,v)\). For every Boolean team \(T\subseteq Q\), interpret
\[
\Phi=\operatorname{dep}(x,y)\lor\operatorname{dep}(u,v)
\]
using lax splitting disjunction. There are precisely **16 inclusion-maximal satisfying teams**, each containing 12 assignments, and exactly **32,377** satisfying teams among the \(2^{16}=65,536\) labelled subsets of \(Q\). The 132 inclusion-minimal teams falsifying \(\Phi\) have cardinality polynomial
\[
12z^4+16z^5+96z^6+8z^8.
\]
Under the group generated by independently complementing the four Boolean coordinates and exchanging the two ordered pairs \((x,y)\) and \((u,v)\), the 132 obstructions form exactly **nine orbits**. Consequently every nonsatisfying team contains a nonsatisfying subteam of at most eight assignments, and eight is the smallest uniform bound. This is a finite Boolean-universe statement, not a claim of unrestricted coherence for arbitrary universes.

## Assumptions and scope
A team is a **set**, not a multiset, of four-tuples over the fixed two-element universe. The atom \(\operatorname{dep}(x,y)\) holds on a subteam \(S\) iff every two assignments in \(S\) with equal \(x\)-values have equal \(y\)-values; the second atom is defined analogously for \((u,v)\). The disjunction holds iff \(T=T_1\cup T_2\), where \(T_1\) satisfies the first atom and \(T_2\) the second. Subteams may be empty and may overlap. All team counts refer to the sixteen individually labelled tuples, not to isomorphism classes.

For reproducibility, assign tuple \((x,y,u,v)\) the index \(i=8x+4y+2u+v\), and represent a team by the 16-bit integer \(t=\sum_{i\in T}2^i\). An orbit representative such as `0x5a69` denotes a 16-bit team mask under this convention.

## Proof
**Step 1: exact maximal satisfying envelopes.** Let \(f,g:\{0,1\}\to\{0,1\}\) be functions. Define
\[
M_{f,g}=\{(x,y,u,v)\in Q:y=f(x)\ \text{or}\ v=g(u)\}.
\]
For every \(T\),
\[
T\models\Phi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists f,g\;T\subseteq M_{f,g}.\tag{1}
\]
For the forward direction, a witness split supplies a partial function \(x\mapsto y\) on \(T_1\) and a partial function \(u\mapsto v\) on \(T_2\); extend them arbitrarily to total binary functions. For the reverse direction, split each tuple according to which equality in the defining disjunction of \(M_{f,g}\) it satisfies. There are four choices for each of \(f,g\), hence 16 envelopes. For every \((x,u)\), exactly one tuple violates both equalities, so \(|M_{f,g}|=12\); the 16 envelopes are distinct, hence pairwise incomparable, and all are maximal satisfying teams by (1).

**Step 2: transversal duality.** Let \(C_{f,g}=Q\setminus M_{f,g}\), a four-element rectangle selecting for each \((x,u)\) precisely the tuple \((x,1-f(x),u,1-g(u))\). Equation (1) gives
\[
T\not\models\Phi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
T\cap C_{f,g}\ne\varnothing\quad\text{for all sixteen pairs }(f,g).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the inclusion-minimal counterteams are **exactly** the minimal transversals of the explicitly defined family of 16 four-element sets. This derivation is independent of any computation.

**Step 3: finite complete certificate.** The nine orbit representatives and their orbit sizes are:

| Representative mask | Tuple count | Orbit size |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `0x0033` | 4 | 4 |
| `0x003c` | 4 | 8 |
| `0x112c` | 5 | 16 |
| `0x143a` | 6 | 16 |
| `0x14a3` | 6 | 32 |
| `0x14aa` | 6 | 16 |
| `0x1863` | 6 | 16 |
| `0x1866` | 6 | 16 |
| `0x5a69` | 8 | 8 |

A finite exhaustive verification checks the following **complete** statements, not a sample. Apply all 32 transformations (16 coordinate-complement choices times either choice of pair exchange) to the nine masks. Their union has 132 distinct elements; each intersects every \(C_{f,g}\), and deletion of any selected assignment produces a team disjoint from some \(C_{f,g}\). Finally, among all 65,536 masks, every team meeting all sixteen \(C_{f,g}\) contains at least one of the 132 generated masks. The standalone `verify_orbit_certificate.py` implements these checks literally. The last statement establishes that no other inclusion-minimal obstruction exists, including none with seven or more than eight tuples. Summing the displayed orbit sizes grouped by cardinality yields the stated polynomial, and `0x5a69` is an explicit indispensable eight-tuple obstruction.

The same 65,536-mask universe is independently enumerated by `verify_profiles.py` using the 16 maximal satisfying envelopes and by `verify_2sat.py` using a conflict-clause implication graph rather than function envelopes. Both give 32,377 satisfying teams, 33,159 violating teams, and the same minimal-obstruction size polynomial. In the implication graph, for every pair of tuples conflicting with \(\operatorname{dep}(x,y)\), one cannot color both red, while for every pair conflicting with \(\operatorname{dep}(u,v)\), one cannot color both blue. The resulting 2-CNF formula is satisfiable exactly when the split exists. Testing mutual literal reachability is an exact 2-SAT criterion.

**Step 4: sharp local test.** By (2), the counterteam family is upward closed under inclusion. Any counterteam contains an inclusion-minimal counterteam. Step 3 proves each such minimal team has size at most eight, establishing the local test. Since `0x5a69` is minimal of size eight, no smaller universal witness bound works.

## Verification
From a directory containing the three included programs, run `python3 verify_profiles.py`, `python3 verify_2sat.py`, and `python3 verify_orbit_certificate.py` with standard Python 3 (no third-party libraries). All three were executed successfully from the actual included file contents. Their complete-state loops range over exactly \(2^{16}\) masks, not a random sample. The nine representative masks and orbit sizes are additionally supplied in `obstruction_orbits.tsv`. These finite computations certify the exact small-domain counts; the general envelope equivalence (1), the transversal equivalence (2), and the sharpness inference are mathematical arguments.

## Relationship to prior work
Fröhlich, Kolaitis and Meier (arXiv:2508.16146, first public 2025-08-22; CSL 2026, Table 1 and Proposition 8) classify this **unrestricted-domain** disjunction as incoherent and use a 2-SAT characterization for two dependence atoms. Their unrestricted incoherence is not contradicted: a fixed two-element universe has only sixteen possible four-tuples, and the question here is the **exact** finite-universe obstruction profile. Barlag, Fröhlich, Hannula, Kolaitis, Kontinen, Meier and Väänänen (arXiv:2605.31269, first public 2026-05-29) study the general coherence problem and propositional Boolean-team semantics, including general size bounds, but the inspected sections do not supply this exact 132-obstruction, nine-orbit classification. The equivalence with 2-SAT and the general notion of coherence are prior facts, not claimed discoveries.

## Limitations
The exact counts are established only for teams with four named Boolean variables and the stated **two** unary dependence disjuncts. They are not claimed for larger alphabets, repeated rows, additional predicates, alternative disjunction semantics, or unrestricted structures. The term “at most eight” is deliberately a bounded-subteam obstruction statement: it is not used to rewrite the unrestricted-domain incoherence result. The enumeration proves precisely this finite mathematical claim; it does not establish priority over every unindexed source. Independent review has not been performed.
