# Scientific review: exact max-sum ball-covering cardinalities

## Correctness — PASS
The definition is finite and field-specific. Every component finite covering can be inflated and scaled so its radius exceeds one while remaining below the center norm; this guarantees that its one-coordinate lift covers *all* product-sphere points with a unit coordinate in that block. Conversely, any product ball omitting the origin can meet at most one embedded coordinate sphere: a contact on axis \(j\) forces the center's \(j\)-th block norm to exceed the radius, while every other block norm is at most the radius. Counting the disjoint ball families gives the reverse inequality, and equality yields the localization of all optimal coverings. The operator-space isometry follows by testing the canonical basis of \(\ell_1^m\). The Euclidean component constant is proved from elementary positive spanning and a Gaussian-free regular-simplex covering; no computational extrapolation is used. The finite checker makes only subsidiary grid assertions.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest direct source is Mishra–Paul–Sain–Sohel (2026), arXiv:2607.10353. Its Theorem 3.19 gives countable BCP stability and allows one-coordinate centers for \(p=\infty\); Theorem 3.31 gives \(mn+1\) for **strictly convex domains** and smooth ranges, which excludes the present \(\ell_1^m\) domain for \(m>1\). The finite minimum count and necessary *localization of every optimal cover* are not stated there. Luo–Liu–Wang (2017) have the same axis-exclusion observation in the qualitative product proof, but not the sharp finite upper bound or optimal-covering rigidity. Luo–Zheng (2019/2020) state countable stability of sequence sums; only its abstract was accessible. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches yielded no directly matching exact-cardinality theorem. This is a best-knowledge originality assessment, not publication-priority certification; the result may be recoverable from general finite-cover cone characterizations or an older uninspected theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant \(\kappa\) and its operator-space values are explicit objects of current research (2026 source, Section II). The result gives a **complete sharp formula** for arbitrary finite real max-sums and a necessary structure theorem for all optimal coverings, rather than merely preserving qualitative BCP. It makes the fixed-dimension operator-space phenomenon concrete: for a real Euclidean range, an \(\ell_1^m\) domain produces exactly \(m(n+1)\) balls, which exceeds the universal dimension lower bound \(mn+1\) by \(m-1\). This complement to the strict-convexity theorem is a motivated classification, independent of unpublished claims of downstream applications.

## Closest coverage and limits
The 2017 product theorem establishes the qualitative BCP implication; the 2026 theorem establishes countable BCP and a different finite operator-space regime. Neither inspected statement supplies the present sharp finite counts or localization as a stated theorem. The result concerns finite-dimensional **real** max-sums and does not assert extensions to complex fields, other sum exponents, arbitrary operator domains, or countably infinite products.
