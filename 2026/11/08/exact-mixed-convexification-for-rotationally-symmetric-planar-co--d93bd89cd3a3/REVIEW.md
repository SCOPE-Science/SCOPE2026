## Correctness — PASS

The entire claimed equivalence follows from two exact, logically independent necessary conditions: each side-exposed segment of the Minkowski sum must be filled by two endpoint translates, forcing \(\lambda\ell\ge2\sin(\pi/n)\); and the origin must belong to the union, forcing the antipodal gauge bound by rotational symmetry. Conversely, the first condition covers every exposed boundary face, and the second makes every translated body contain the origin. Radial convexity then covers the full Minkowski sum. The argument includes zero-length faces, attained endpoints, all dimensions of exposed faces in the plane, and \(\lambda=0\). The regular-polygon arithmetic and the inequality making the facet bound dominant for \(m>n\) were separately checked. The embedded verifier passed 82,508 supplementary finite tests; finite sampling is not the proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result completely classifies a natural geometric invariant across all planar rotationally symmetric convexifiers of a regular polygon's vertices, not just one handpicked shape. The sharp competition between central coverage and facet saturation distinguishes two genuinely different obstructions; the two-regular-polygon corollary exhibits both arithmetic divisibility and a singular dependence on angular alignment. The exact critical dilation and attained endpoint are potentially useful benchmarks for finite convexification, generalized polygonal coverings, and sensitivity of nonconvex unions. The generality and all-parameter classification exceed a routine isolated calculation.
