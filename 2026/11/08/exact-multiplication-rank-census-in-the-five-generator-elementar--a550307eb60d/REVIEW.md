# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The defining group algebra is isomorphic to the five-variable squarefree algebra in characteristic two. Its automorphisms preserve multiplication ranks. A rank-four quadratic leading term is equivalent to \(ab+cd\) because all higher-degree corrections are eliminated successively using the surjective derivative ideal; its rank is \(12\). A decomposable quadratic leading term is equivalent, up to multiplication by a unit, to \(ab+\gamma cde\); a direct tensor-differential block calculation gives ranks \(8,10\). The cubic, quartic, quintic, linear and unit cases are separately exhausted. There are \((q-1){5\brack2}_q\) nonzero rank-two bivectors, \(q^{10}-1-(q-1){5\brack2}_q\) rank-four bivectors, and \(q^9\) cubic lifts with zero residual obstruction per decomposable quadratic form. All coefficient exponents follow from these disjoint cases. Original multiplication matrices, rather than derived classifications, were checked over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_4\). Finite computations alone would not suffice; the normal-form arguments provide the proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The closest same-ring source is Carlson–Friedlander–Pevtsova (2011, arXiv:1106.4474), whose freeness criterion already covers elements having nonzero linear part and whose focus is rank varieties along linear shifted subgroups, not complete finite-field counts throughout the augmentation ideal. Moreno-Socías–Snellman (first preprint 2000, arXiv:math/0007089) analyze generic homogeneous exterior-algebra principal ideals mainly in characteristic zero, including generic even-degree cases; this establishes context, not the complete characteristic-two inhomogeneous five-variable polynomial. Lundqvist–Nicklasson (2016, arXiv:1604.06820) concern Lefschetz properties for linear-form powers; their 2018 arXiv:1803.03563 work concerns generic homogeneous principal ideals over the complex numbers. No inspected exact statement directly covers the rank-eight/rank-ten cubic obstruction together with all finite-field multiplicities. This remains a priority assessment, not a proof of global uniqueness; a broader unnoticed group-algebra or exterior-ideal classification is the residual risk.

## Value — PASS
The first five-generator obstruction separates two algebra elements with the same initial quadratic form, explaining precisely where initial-form rank predictions fail. The full elementwise count is a natural principal-ideal dimension/Jordan-type invariant of the regular modular group-algebra module and is valid uniformly for all finite fields of characteristic two. The sharp first-rank-jump boundary and finite-field strata give usable structural benchmarks, rather than an arbitrary isolated computation. The result does not enumerate distinct ideals, and generalization to higher group ranks remains open.
