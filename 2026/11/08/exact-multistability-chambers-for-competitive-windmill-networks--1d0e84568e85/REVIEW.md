# Review of an exact windmill-community phase diagram

## Correctness

PASS. The full equilibrium support argument works for all integer \(k\ge2\) and all positive parameters away from the transition curves: an active support must have a positive-definite principal interaction matrix and strictly negative outsider invasion exponents; the symmetric system makes the necessity transparent. The two-leaf principal submatrix distinguishes \(\alpha<1\) from \(\alpha>1\); the hub–leaf principal submatrix distinguishes \(\beta<1\) from \(\beta>1\). The exhaustive support cases lead to exact threshold and abundance formulas. Independent rational enumeration of every support for \(84\) parameter instances and three graph sizes agrees. The finite test does not prove the infinite statement; the all-size proof is given in RESULT.md.

## Originality

PASS under a bounded literature comparison, with residual priority uncertainty. Meng et al. study feasibility/stability across ecological network arrangements and full census only at small graph orders. Hong and Pego establish the general invasion-stability framework, exclusion principles, and strong-competition clique constructions, which already imply exponential multiplicity in related graphs. They do not give the exact windmill-specific \((\alpha,\beta)\) phase diagram. Fried–Kessler–Shnerb explain clique behavior; that behavior is acknowledged as prior art rather than claimed. The candidate is the exact finite-strength chamber partition, including the hub-present branches and the changing complete list of stable supports. A hidden specialization of general network theorems or an unindexed motif treatment could cover the result and remains a publication-priority risk.

## Value

PASS. Friendship networks represent a hub plus mutually competing ecological pairs. The result describes, for every network size, both the number and complete identity of locally stable alternative communities, their precise positive abundances, two biologically interpretable invasion thresholds, and onset of an extra hub-only attractor. Especially for strong paired competition, it distinguishes \(2^k\) hub-containing communities from \(2^k\) hub-absent ones at an exact finite-strength cutoff; prior exponential-state examples alone do not determine this boundary.

## Scientific scope and limitations

The conclusions exclude nonhyperbolic transition curves and make no claim about basin sizes, long-time selection among attractors, global structural stability, or independent verification. The support criterion and independent-pair principle are classical; the bounded-parameter classification is the assessed contribution. Potential unindexed equivalent results are a residual originality risk.
