# Review of the Nash-product law in master-priority random stable marriage

## Correctness — PASS
The unique stable matching is generated by agents in the common women-side priority order. For each number \(k\) of remaining women, the current man's independent random permutation yields the maximum of a uniform \(k\)-subset of positive Borda scores. These scores are mutually independent, and the product of women-side scores is deterministically \(n!\). The exact distributional factorization follows, and the mean is \(n!(n+1)^{n-1}\). A uniform \(O_s(k^{-2})\) comparison between the \(s\)-th moments of normalized finite-population maxima and \(\operatorname{Beta}(k,1)\) controls the product tail, yielding every exponential limiting moment and weak convergence. The exact-reproduction script verifies all profiles through \(n=4\); the proof, not the finite test, establishes the general result.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Irving, Manlove and Scott's 2008 Section 2 Theorem 2.1 supplies the fixed-master-list uniqueness and greedy construction; neither its relevant inspected section nor the introductory material derives a random Nash-product law. Desai et al.'s 2026 *Fair Stable Matching: A Nash Social Welfare Approach* is the most relevant framing and was available as an abstract, but its complete text could not be inspected; an all-n random-profile product distribution is not stated in that accessible abstract. Caragiannis and Homrighausen (2024) analyze expected **additive** welfare under random serial order for fixed preferences, a different averaging mechanism and invariant. Targeted exact-distribution, probability-law, alias and stronger-coverage searches did not identify an equivalent. The uninspected 2026 full text, other unpublished derivations and the elementary nature of the components remain explicit publication-priority risks. No priority certainty or external independent validation is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A unique stable allocation can still have substantial random welfare dispersion as preferences vary. The full all-population law, simple exact mean and nondegenerate normalized \(\operatorname{Exp}(1)\) limit jointly give a substantive quantitative answer for the rank-based Nash objective, showing that the welfare product does not self-average under the natural normalization. They connect matching-market structure with an exact independent order-statistic product and its fluctuation law.

## Limits and closest literature
The result needs complete strict lists, independent uniform male preferences, one common female ranking, balanced sides and positive Borda utility. It concerns the product across both sides rather than the original-scale geometric mean. The nearest primary papers are the 2008 master-list structural result, the 2026 Nash-welfare preprint and the 2024 random-serial-dictatorship expectation analysis, cited in `RESULT.md`.
