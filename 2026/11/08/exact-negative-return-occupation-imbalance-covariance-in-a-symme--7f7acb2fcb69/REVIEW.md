# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The bridge is decomposed into Catalan-weighted signed excursions. Conditional sign averaging changes the squared occupation imbalance into the sum of squared excursion half-lengths. The exact marked generating function is differentiated and coefficients extracted; the resulting \(n(n-1)\) factor proves all finite-size signs. A separate bivariate enumeration proves the uniform sojourn marginal, and two exact variance formulas plus central-binomial asymptotics imply the limiting correlation. The extracted standalone program checks 92 parameter-case checks, including exhaustive rational-arithmetic enumeration up to 16 steps and dynamic weighted decompositions through \(n=80\). The infinite claim rests on algebraic generating-function identities, not finite tests.

## Originality — PASS (with priority risk)

Actual semantic searches against published research records and comparison with accessible earlier findings found no equivalent finite return–quadratic-sojourn covariance. Lindell–Holst (2007), Proposition 3.1, is a close general excursion-length-count law; its scope could be specialized to recover the unsigned length statistic after additional marked-moment extraction and independent excursion-sign averaging. Their inspected paper addresses excursion length order statistics, not the stated signed occupation quadratic covariance, sign classification, or explicit limiting correlation. The present joint calculation is a nontrivial moment evaluation rather than a direct restatement or parameter choice from a printed formula. Residual risk remains that an uninspected classical joint local-time–sojourn law explicitly implies or already records this identity.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Zero-return counts and positive sojourn times are canonical dual observables of a tied-down random walk. Chung–Feller uniformity makes the marginal occupation distribution exceptionally simple but does not determine its dependence on returns. The exact negative covariance at every finite nontrivial size and a nonvanishing limiting correlation expose a substantial structural coupling between excursion fragmentation and sign occupation imbalance; their computation requires a marked two-statistic excursion construction and moment cancellation.

## Limitations and closest literature

The result is for equal-probability \(\pm1\) bridges only. Prior excursion-length counting techniques may imply the same formula with additional calculations, so historical priority is qualified.
