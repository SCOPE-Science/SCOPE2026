# Mathematical review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For each nontrivial character, the unnormalized weight-one and weight-two Fourier coefficients are derived by elementary coordinate counting. The capped distance transform is a quadratic in the first coefficient, and its strictly concave vertex stays between two adjacent arithmetic thresholds because \(2<(3^p-1)/(3^p-2^p)<3\) for \(0<p<1\). This yields a unique critical character level across all nonbinary alphabet sizes. The four-coordinate obstruction at exponent one, the negative-type interval and strictness theorems, and continuity give the unique endpoint. Centering yields the exact Gram rank. Both asymptotics are proved by uniform first-order expansions with one parameter fixed. A separate checker exhaustively compared direct Fourier sums with the formulas and tested multiple regimes. Risk: the proof uses standard finite-metric strict negative-type theory, for which Sánchez (2011) is explicitly cited.

## Originality
**PASS under bounded historical comparison.** The binary cap-three result covers the two-symbol alphabet only. The nonbinary theorem has a different critical Fourier degree \(n-\lceil(n-3)/q\rceil\), establishes the absence of mode switches throughout \((0,1)\), and gives a second asymptotic law as alphabet size tends to infinity. Sánchez (arXiv:1108.0451v1) gives a general finite-metric criterion, Murugan (arXiv:1112.5657v1) treats vertex-transitive metrics and ordinary binary Hamming subsets, and Brouwer et al. (2018) give Krawtchouk eigenvalues and minima for individual distance-relation graphs; none of their inspected statements directly gives the full powered-capped classification. The public-indexed q-ary Hamming radius-three Turán result concerns a different positive-definite mass objective; its underlying full text was not accessible in this review. General Krawtchouk and metric-transform machinery may make the new specialization derivable, and a historical source not located here could contain it. In particular Deza–Maehara (1990) remains a named textual-access risk.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Capping a product metric is a natural bounded-distortion operation, but it destroys negative type. The theorem determines the exact Hilbert-snowflake threshold for every nonbinary alphabet and dimension, the precise Fourier obstruction, and its large multiplicity in the limiting Gram matrix. The explicit arithmetic layer and contrasting asymptotic regimes quantify the geometry of the change, beyond a single small numerical example or generic restatement of a finite-metric criterion. The result does not assert conclusions about joint growth of both parameters or caps greater than three.
