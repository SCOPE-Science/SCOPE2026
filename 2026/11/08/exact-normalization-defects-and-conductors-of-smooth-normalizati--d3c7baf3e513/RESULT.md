# Exact normalization defects and conductors of smooth-normalization toric germs

## Finding

Fix integers \(s\ge 2\), \(2\le n<m\) with \(\gcd(n,m)=1\), and positive integers \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{s-1}\). Consider the affine monomial algebra
\[
R=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_{s-1},y^n,y^m,x_1^{\lambda_1}y,\ldots,x_{s-1}^{\lambda_{s-1}}y]
\subset B=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_{s-1},y].
\]
The ring \(B\) is its normalization. Put \(H=\langle n,m\rangle\), \(c=(n-1)(m-1)\), and, for every integer \(b\ge0\), define
\[
q(b)=b-\max\bigl(H\cap[0,b]\bigr),\qquad Q(b)=\max_{t\ge b}q(t).
\]
Both maxima exist; \(q(b)=Q(b)=0\) for \(b\ge c\). For \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_{s-1})\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{s-1}\), put
\[
u(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\left\lfloor\frac{a_i}{\lambda_i}\right\rfloor.
\]
The conductor of \(R\subset B\), as an ideal of \(B\) (and hence of \(R\)), is the explicit monomial ideal
\[
\boxed{(R:B)=\bigoplus_{\substack{b\ge0,\ a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{s-1}\\u(a)\ge Q(b)}}\mathbb C\,x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_{s-1}^{a_{s-1}}y^b.}
\]
The normalization defect is a finite-length invariant supported at the unique nonnormal point, and is exactly
\[
\boxed{\delta(R):=\dim_{\mathbb C}(B/R)
=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{s-1}\lambda_i\Bigr)
\sum_{\substack{0\le b<c\\ b\notin H}}
\binom{q(b)+s-2}{s-1}.}
\]
For the natural consecutive-generator subfamily \(m=n+1\), the sum evaluates to the closed expression
\[
\boxed{\delta(R)=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{s-1}\lambda_i\Bigr)\binom{n+s-1}{s+1}.}
\]
All assertions hold simultaneously for every allowed parameter tuple, not just generically.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\). The parameters are integers \(s\ge2\), \(2\le n<m\), \(\gcd(n,m)=1\), and \(\lambda_i\ge1\). The coordinate algebra is the semigroup ring of the minimal-embedding-dimension smooth-normalization family described in Dalbelo--Rodrigues Hernandes--Ruas, Theorem 3.6. The invariant \(\delta(R)\) is the colength of the finite normalization module; it is also the delta invariant of the associated finite monomial map-germ at the origin. The conductor here means the ring-theoretic ideal \((R:B)=\{f\in B:fB\subseteq R\}\), not just the numerical-semigroup conductor \(c\).

## Proof

**1. Normalization and finiteness.** Every \(x_i\) lies in \(R\), while \(y\) is integral over \(R\), since \(y^n\in R\). Thus \(B=R[y]\) is finite over \(R\). Since \(s\ge2\), for any index \(i\), the fraction-field identity \(y=(x_i^{\lambda_i}y)/x_i^{\lambda_i}\) shows that \(R\) and \(B\) have the same fraction field. The polynomial algebra \(B\) is normal, so it is the integral closure of \(R\).

**2. Exact monomial-membership criterion.** Fix nonnegative integers \(a_i,b\). A product of the displayed generators has exponent \(a=(a_i)\) in the \(x\)-variables and exponent \(b\) in \(y\) precisely when one can choose nonnegative integers \(t_i\) satisfying
\[
t_i\le\lfloor a_i/\lambda_i\rfloor,
\qquad b-\sum_i t_i\in H.
\]
Here \(t_i\) counts the generator \(x_i^{\lambda_i}y\); the unused \(x\)-exponents are provided by the generators \(x_i\). Conversely, every factorization into generators yields such integers. The attainable sums \(\sum_i t_i\) fill every integer from \(0\) to \(u(a)\), by incrementing the \(t_i\) one at a time. By definition, \(q(b)\) is the smallest nonnegative \(t\) for which \(b-t\in H\). It follows that
\[
\boxed{x^a y^b\in R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u(a)\ge q(b).}\tag{1}
\]
Distinct monomials are linearly independent in \(B\), so the criterion controls all of \(R\), without hidden linear cancellations.

**3. The conductor.** A monomial \(x^a y^b\in B\) belongs to \((R:B)\) if and only if its products by every monomial in \(B\) belong to \(R\). Multiplying by \(x_i\) can only increase \(u(a)\); therefore the critical products are precisely \(x^a y^{b+t}\) for integers \(t\ge0\). By (1), this is equivalent to
\[
u(a)\ge\max_{t\ge0}q(b+t)=Q(b).
\]
The numerical semigroup generated by two coprime positive integers \(n,m\) has conductor \(c=(n-1)(m-1)\). Indeed, for any \(b\ge c\), choose \(j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) with \(b-jm\equiv0\pmod n\); then \(b-jm\ge-(n-1)\), so its divisibility by \(n\) forces \(b-jm\ge0\), and hence \(b\in H\). Meanwhile \(c-1=nm-n-m\notin H\), since its residue forces the \(m\)-coefficient to be at least \(n-1\), already too large. Thus every \(b\ge c\) is in \(H\), and \(Q(b)=0\) there. The conductor ideal is monomial because the semigroup algebra and its normalization have the common multigrading by exponents. Hence the displayed monomial direct sum is exactly the ideal \((R:B)\). The same argument gives \(Q(0)=n-1\): no run of nonsemigroup integers has length at least \(n\), whereas \(q(n-1)=n-1\).

**4. Exact colength.** By (1), a monomial can fail to be in \(R\) only when \(b<c\) and \(b\notin H\). For fixed such \(b\), let \(q=q(b)\ge1\). The missing exponent vectors satisfy \(u(a)<q\). Write uniquely
\[
a_i=\lambda_i h_i+r_i,\quad h_i\ge0,\quad 0\le r_i<\lambda_i.
\]
Then \(u(a)=\sum_i h_i\). For each nonnegative vector \((h_i)\) with sum at most \(q-1\) there are \(\prod_i\lambda_i\) independent residue choices. The stars-and-bars identity yields
\[
\#\{(h_i)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{s-1}:\sum_i h_i\le q-1\}
=\binom{q+s-2}{s-1}.
\]
Summing over the finitely many gaps of \(H\) proves the formula for \(\delta(R)\). Moreover the missing monomials are killed in \(B/R\) by \(y^c\) and by a sufficiently high power of each \(x_i\), so the quotient is supported at the origin. Its global \(\mathbb C\)-dimension equals its local length there.

**5. Consecutive semigroup generators.** If \(m=n+1\), the gaps of \(H\) are exactly
\[
b=kn+r,\qquad 0\le k\le n-2,\quad k<r\le n-1,
\]
with \(q(kn+r)=r-k\). Indeed, the elements of \(H\) in the \(k\)-th block are precisely \(kn,kn+1,\ldots,kn+k\); later blocks have no gaps. Consequently, the sum from step 4 reduces to
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1-k}\binom{j+s-2}{s-1}
=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+s-2-k}{s}
=\binom{n+s-1}{s+1},
\]
by two hockey-stick identities. This proves the closed formula.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify_normalization.py` uses direct, independent exponent decompositions into the original generators. It checks the membership criterion, the complete finite conductor test, the exact monomial colength, and the consecutive-exponent closed form for 138 parameter tuples, spanning \(2\le n\le7\), dimensions \(2\le s\le4\), several \(m>n\) and positive \(\lambda_i\). Run `python3 verify_normalization.py`; the observed terminal line was `NORMALIZATION_DEFECT_AND_CONDUCTOR_FINITE_CHECK_OK 138`. The script tests finitely many instances only. The all-parameter proof is the argument above.

For example, in dimension \(s=2\) with \(n=3\), \(m=4\), and \(\lambda_1=1\), the formula gives \(\delta(R)=4\). In dimension \(s=3\) with \(n=4\), \(m=5\), and \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=(2,3)\), it gives \(\delta(R)=90\).

## Relationship to prior work

Dalbelo--Rodrigues Hernandes--Ruas, arXiv:2412.05083, Theorem 3.6, identify this semigroup family, its smooth normalization, and its isolated singularity; their investigated invariants are structural normal forms and defining ideals. Rodrigues Hernandes--Ruas, arXiv:2105.04759, Proposition 5.1, estimate the delta invariant for the same monomial-map family but give upper and lower bounds, not the exact all-parameter gap sum. Their Proposition 5.3 calculates the distinct stable-double-point invariant, and their later discussion explicitly distinguishes it from delta in general. For example, at \(s=2\), \(n=3\), \(m=4\), and \(\lambda_1=1\), their double-point formula gives \(d(f)=3\), while the present normalization colength is \(\delta(R)=4\); thus that prior double-point formula does not imply this count. The calculation here provides the exact delta invariant and the full conductor ideal for all dimensions. Earlier work on parametrized monomial surfaces in four-space (DOI:10.1093/qmath/hay052) is relevant historical prior art; its full text could not be inspected, and the surface subcase therefore retains a priority risk. No assertion of exhaustive historical novelty or independent certification is made.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to the explicit corank-one minimal-embedding-dimension monomial family over \(\mathbb C\); it does not compute the delta invariant for the more general semigroups with extra generators or higher corank. The formula is a finite gap sum, not necessarily an elementary closed expression when \(m\ne n+1\). No statement about deformation spaces, normalizations in positive characteristic, or unrelated toric singularities is implied. The 138 finite tests do not constitute a proof for all parameters. The 2018 surface paper was not fully accessible, so historical priority there remains unsettled.
