# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)

The statement fixes the single shared binary environment, strict conditional Bernoulli independence, admissible \((p,q,w)\) and all odd positive panel sizes. The exact two-added-voter binomial identity yields an increment whose sign is \(\operatorname{sgn}(R-z^m)\). Monotonicity of \(z^m\) proves all cases, including ties and unattained limits; the near-complementary scaling follows from expansions with fixed interior \(q,w\). A standalone exact rational checker reports 3,095 passing assertions. Its finite tests do not replace this all-size proof. The relative ranking of panel sizes is unaffected by the unknown outcome's actual label under the stated symmetric competence model.

## Originality (PASS within inspected material; residual risk)

The primary Kao–Couzin 2014 paper's fluctuation model is mathematically the present two-state conditional-iid mechanism, and it already identifies finite-optimal-size phenomena and a numerical parameter diagram; those are not new claims. The inspected indexed article material includes its figure 3 and discussion, but no closed-form all-integer optimizer/tie threshold or inverse-distance boundary law. Galesic–Barkoczi–Katsikopoulos analyze mixed easy/difficult tasks, but the accessible abstract and institutional report do not establish this exact all-parameter theorem. The complete 2016 article and portions of earlier correlated-jury literature remain priority risks; one cannot infer their absence of coverage merely from abstracts. General binomial recurrences are classical, and there is no claim to their novelty. Current conclusion is best-knowledge noncoverage of the specific phase-map and sharp boundary package, not proof of absolute historical uniqueness.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

The 2014 shared-environment model explicitly motivates finite optimal majority sizes but mainly offers an environmental diagram. The result replaces finite search and plotted regimes by an exact global integer optimizer, resolves adjacent ties, distinguishes real optima from limits unattained by any finite panel, supplies a sharp threshold in the probability of favorable tasks, and shows unbounded optimal committee size at a quantitative \(\delta^{-1}\) rate. This is a natural exact classification of the model's central decision-design variable, not an arbitrary parameter slice. The theorem is an idealized benchmark and does not justify finite committee selection in elections or social governance.
