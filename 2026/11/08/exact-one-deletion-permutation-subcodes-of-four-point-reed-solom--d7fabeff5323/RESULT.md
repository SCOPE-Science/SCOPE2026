# Exact one-deletion permutation subcodes of four-point Reed–Solomon codes over \(\mathbb F_7\)

## Finding
Fix any four-element evaluation set \(S\subset\mathbb F_7\), in any fixed order, and the degree-less-than-two Reed–Solomon code
\[
C_S=\{(a+bs)_{s\in S}:a,b\in\mathbb F_7\}\subset\mathbb F_7^4.
\]
A channel first applies an arbitrary coordinate permutation and then deletes exactly one coordinate. Let \(M(S)\) be the largest cardinality of a subcode whose sets of possible received words are pairwise disjoint. Write \(T=\mathbb F_7\setminus S\).

**Theorem.** If \(T\) is an arithmetic-progression triple \(\{u-d,u,u+d\}\) with \(d\ne0\), then \(M(S)=13\), and there are exactly \(896\) maximum subcodes. If \(T\) is not such a triple, then \(M(S)=14\), and there are exactly \(4374\) maximum subcodes. There are exactly \(21\) evaluation sets of the first kind and \(14\) of the second kind. Every maximum subcode contains the seven constant words. All other selected codewords have pairwise different supports and are classified by the disjoint-pair triple packings in the proof. In the arithmetic-progression case the \(14\) optimal support packings are in bijection with the \(14\) non-progression triples through their three uncovered coordinate pairs. In the non-progression case the \(14\) possible complement triples split into exactly two optimal seven-triple packings.

## Assumptions and scope
The field is exactly \(\mathbb F_7\), the evaluation points are four distinct field elements, polynomials have degree less than two, coordinate permutations are unrestricted, and **exactly one** symbol is deleted. A subcode is an arbitrary subset of the \(49\) distinct polynomial evaluation words; it need not be linear. The maximum-subcode counts distinguish subsets of the original ordered codewords, not merely their unordered supports. Only the error model defined here is asserted; no secret-sharing privacy guarantee follows from the result.

## Proof
**1. Channel reduction.** For slope \(b=0\), the received word has three copies of \(a\). These seven classes are mutually distinguishable and disjoint from all nonconstant classes, so all seven constant words belong to every maximum subcode. For \(b\ne0\), the four symbols \(a+bs\) are distinct, so an arbitrary permutation followed by one deletion yields precisely all ordered permutations of the four three-element subsets of the support \(U=a+bS\). Hence two such words conflict exactly when their four-element supports intersect in at least three elements. Words with identical supports conflict automatically.

**2. Affine orbits.** The complements of the possible supports are \(a+bT\), for \(b\ne0\). On the \(35\) three-subsets of \(\mathbb F_7\), the affine group has exactly two orbits. The \(21\) three-term arithmetic progressions form one orbit, represented by \(\{0,1,2\}\) with stabilizer order two. The \(14\) other triples form one orbit, represented by \(\{0,1,3\}\) with stabilizer order three. This can be verified directly by the \(42\) maps \(t\mapsto a+bt\), \(a\in\mathbb F_7\), \(b\in\mathbb F_7^*\); the two computed orbits are disjoint and exhaust all \(35\) triples. Each support thus corresponds respectively to exactly two or three different nonconstant codewords.

**3. Packing formulation.** Two distinct four-element supports \(U,V\) have \(|U\cap V|=1+|T_U\cap T_V|\), where \(T_U=\mathbb F_7\setminus U\) and similarly for \(V\). Consequently a correcting subcode corresponds to a family of triples from the appropriate orbit with pairwise intersection at most one, followed by choosing exactly one of the two or three codewords representing each selected support.

**4. Complete finite packing certificate.** Index the \(21\) unordered pairs of field elements. Assign every triple \(A\) its exact 21-bit pair-incidence mask \(P(A)\). Two triples intersect in at most one point exactly when their pair masks are disjoint. The independent certificate `certificate.cpp` iterates every one of the \(2^{21}\) subsets of the progression orbit and every one of the \(2^{14}\) subsets of the other orbit, accepting only families whose pair masks are pairwise disjoint. Its complete counts are: maximum packing size six with \(14\) maximizing families among the \(21\) progression triples, and maximum size seven with exactly \(2\) maximizing families among the \(14\) other triples. For each six-progression packing, the three uncovered pair indices are precisely the three edges of one non-progression triple; these \(14\) omitted triples are all different. The two seven-triple non-progression packings partition the \(14\) available triples. The exhaustive loop examines each possible family once, and uses integer bit operations without numerical tolerances or optimization solvers. The separate `checker.py` rederives all these counts by recursive independent-set search and also checks actual polynomial source/output sets.

**5. Maximum code counts.** From Steps 1–4, the two code-size maxima are \(7+6=13\) and \(7+7=14\). Every selected support allows, independently, a choice of one of two or three distinct codewords. Thus the numbers of maximum subcodes are \(14\cdot2^6=896\) and \(2\cdot3^7=4374\). Since affine maps preserve the codeword-conflict structure, these results hold for every evaluation set in its corresponding orbit.

## Verification
From the directory containing the two included standalone source files, run `python3 checker.py`, then `c++ -std=c++17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -o certificate certificate.cpp` and `./certificate`. The Python checker independently evaluates all \(49\) source words for one representative in each affine orbit, compares all \(1176\) unordered source-word pairs per representative, checks the two distinct packing enumerations and their structural descriptions, and constructs disjoint-output witnesses for every maximum triple packing. The C++ checker exhaustively enumerates the \(2^{21}+2^{14}\) possible triple families. Both were executed successfully for this result; the latter gives a finite exhaustive proof, not an extrapolation from sampled instances.

## Relationship to prior work
Con (2024) introduced robust Reed–Solomon coding under adversarial permutations followed by insertion/deletion, with large-field existence constructions. Zhang, Sun, Zhang and Ge (2026) prove asymptotic alphabet bounds, a histogram sphere bound for growing blocklength, and two-dimensional decoding in that model. Their histogram technique justifies the output-support viewpoint, but their inequalities do not give the exact restricted-packing values here. Camps-Moreno, Lau, López and Santos (first available December 2025) characterize the coordinate-permutation automorphism groups of Reed–Solomon codes; the later work of Martínez-Peñas and Rodríguez-Ballesteros extends such group results to additive Reed–Solomon codes. Those group results clarify the affine symmetry in Step 2, but do not themselves determine the maximum disjoint **one-deletion** balls or classify the restricted triple packings of Step 4. Source availability limitations are described below.

## Limitations
The numerical answers are restricted to one finite field and blocklength four, rather than a general length or alphabet formula. The proof does not establish any claim about ordered one-deletion codes without permutation, multiple deletions, or subcodes required to be linear. The primary permutation-insdel manuscripts were inspected in their accessible arXiv HTML full-text versions, including algebraic conditions, main theorems and two-dimensional constructions; they do not explicitly determine these nonlinear finite-field subcode optima. Implicit coverage or older restricted-packing literature remains a residual priority risk. All calculations are independently reproducible from the included code; no independent mathematical audit has yet been performed.
