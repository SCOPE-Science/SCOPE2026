# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The single-pass binary tortoise map is explicitly normalized, and the theorem quantifies over every irrational slope strictly between one-third and one. The proof starts with the established \(n+1\) Sturmian factor count, derives uniqueness of the left-special suffix, and proves existence of the required predecessor contexts by perturbing the characteristic mechanical intercept. Balance forbids \(00\) above one-half and simultaneously forbids \(11\) and \(000\) between one-third and one-half. The collision equations exhaust every possible pair of earliest-one positions; the would-be third type is ruled out when its suffix is nonempty. Lengths one, two, and three are handled separately. A reproducible high-precision numerical factor census at eight slopes checks 648 slope-length pairs and an independently generated-prefix scan. These checks support, but do not replace, the infinite symbolic proof.

## Originality (O): PASS with residual priority risk

Campbell–Rampersad (arXiv:2604.25811v2, Sections 3–7) establish a bounded difference between ordinary and tortoise complexity for linear-complexity bounded-gap binary words and give exact results for paperfolding and Thue–Morse, but do not determine the slope-dependent Sturmian spectrum; the conclusion raises the Sturmian question. Their left-special suffix lemma does not alone prove the existence, disjointness, and exhaustiveness of these Sturmian fibers. De Luca–Fici (arXiv:2206.00995v2, Sections 1–3) give the *Lie* complexity of Sturmian words; their equivalence by cyclic rotations is not tortoise equivalence. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches showed no result implying the stated spectrum. Searches are not a uniqueness proof; specialized or unindexed work could overlap.

## Value (V): PASS

The theorem answers an explicitly motivated question for an uncountable set of Sturmian shifts and every factor length, not a finite parameter census. It identifies the sharp change from a single nontrivial image fiber above slope one-half to two separated collision families below it, with the exceptional triple at length three. Its classification includes the canonical Fibonacci word and supplies exact structure beyond the earlier bounded-deficit theorem.

## Closest literature and limits

The primary nearest theorem is Campbell–Rampersad's general bounded-deficit result and its special-factor lemma. The De Luca–Fici Sturmian Lie-complexity formula is a distinct quotient and does not subsume this calculation. Slopes at most one-third, rational boundaries, higher iterates and other alphabets remain unaddressed. Numerical experiments are not infinite certificates, and independent validation is outstanding.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
