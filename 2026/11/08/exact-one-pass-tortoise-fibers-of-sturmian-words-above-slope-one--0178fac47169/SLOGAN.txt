For every irrational Sturmian slope \(1/3<\alpha<1\), one application of the binary tortoise map has an exact, intercept-independent complexity: \(n\) images at lengths \(n\ge2\) above slope \(1/2\), and \(n-1\) images at lengths \(n\ge4\) between slopes \(1/3\) and \(1/2\). All nontrivial fibers are explicitly classified by characteristic left-special prefixes; the lower-slope class has exactly two double fibers at every length at least four.
