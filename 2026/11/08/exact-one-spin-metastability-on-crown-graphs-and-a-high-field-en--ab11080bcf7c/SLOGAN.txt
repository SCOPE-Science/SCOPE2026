Deleting one perfect matching from a complete bipartite antiferromagnet creates exponentially many one-spin-stable configurations at generic positive fields, while the undeleted complete bipartite graph has only two at noninteger subcritical fields. On a crown graph with \(2n\) spins the field \(h=(n-1)J\) produces an isolated entropy spike: the number of weakly stable configurations is exactly \(2^{n+1}+n-1\), compared with \(n+2\) a fixed fractional amount below and \(1\) the same amount above.
