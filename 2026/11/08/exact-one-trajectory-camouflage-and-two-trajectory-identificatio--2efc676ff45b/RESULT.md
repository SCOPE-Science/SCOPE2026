# Exact one-trajectory camouflage and two-trajectory identification of a hard ecological interaction

## Finding

A hard three-species interaction can be **exactly**, not just approximately, indistinguishable from pairwise Lotka–Volterra dynamics on every trajectory in a positive conserved-product leaf. Nevertheless, two suitably nonresonant trajectories with different conserved products identify the entire five-coefficient per-capita polynomial of the focal species and rule out every pairwise Lotka–Volterra explanation shared across both experiments.

Specifically, consider the higher-order Lotka–Volterra family
\[
\dot x=x(r-bx+\theta yz),\qquad
\dot y=y(\lambda-y),\qquad
\dot z=z(y-\lambda),
\tag{1}
\]
with \(r,b,\lambda>0\), \(\theta\ne0\), and positive starting abundances. Fix any \(q>0\) such that \(g_q=r+\theta q>0\). The leaf \(\mathcal L_q=\{(x,y,z)>0:yz=q\}\) is invariant; on the **entire** leaf, (1) has exactly the same solutions as the pairwise system
\[
\dot x=x(g_q-bx),\quad \dot y=y(\lambda-y),\quad \dot z=z(y-\lambda).
\tag{2}
\]
This observational equivalence holds for all future times and all positive initial \(x,y\) on that leaf, not only near equilibrium.

For an individual trajectory on \(\mathcal L_q\), suppose \(x(0)\ne g_q/b\), \(y(0)\ne\lambda\), and \(g_q/\lambda\notin\mathbb N\). Then the four observed functions \(1,x(t),y(t),z(t)\) are linearly independent on every nonempty open time interval, whereas \(yz=q\). Consequently the five regressors \(1,x,y,z,yz\) have **exact rank four**, with kernel generated by \(yz-q\). Thus the five coefficients of a general focal per-capita function
\[
F_x(x,y,z)=c_0+c_xx+c_yy+c_zz+c_{yz}yz
\tag{3}
\]
are not individually identifiable from one such exact full-state trajectory.

If two trajectories satisfy the same nonresonance and nonstationarity conditions on \(\mathcal L_{q_0}\) and \(\mathcal L_{q_1}\) with \(q_0\ne q_1\), the pooled five regressors have **rank five**. All five coefficients in (3), including the hard interaction \(c_{yz}=\theta\), are then uniquely identifiable from the two continuous exact observations and their derivatives; no single pairwise focal growth function can match both. In this setting one trajectory is insufficient but two are sufficient.

For a pairwise surrogate fitted exactly to any nonresonant first trajectory on \(\mathcal L_{q_0}\), the focal per-capita prediction error on \(\mathcal L_{q_1}\) is the nonzero constant \(\theta(q_1-q_0)\). When both \(g_{q_0}\) and \(g_{q_1}\) are positive, the true and surrogate limiting focal abundances on the second leaf differ by exactly \(\theta(q_1-q_0)/b\).

## Assumptions and scope

Species abundances \(x,y,z\) are positive and fully observed continuously, along with exact time derivatives or equivalently differentiable noiseless trajectories. Dynamics are deterministic and autonomous. The classification of identifiable coefficients is **within the specified polynomial dictionary (3)** and its pairwise submodel \(c_{yz}=0\), not among arbitrary nonlinear models or hidden-state mechanisms. The triadic term \(\theta yz\) is a hard higher-order term for the focal species: it combines two distinct other species and disappears in every two-species subsystem. No signs for ecological effects are asserted beyond the stated parameter conditions.

The exact rank claims require both focal and second-species trajectories to be nonstationary and require the indicated noninteger ratio. The full-leaf equivalence does **not** require these nonresonance assumptions. Positive equilibrium limits are asserted only where \(g_q>0\). This is an explicitly integrable family with a conservation law; generic ecological systems need not exhibit the same camouflage.

## Proof

**1. Invariant leaves and explicit solutions.** Differentiate the partner product:
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(yz)=yz\big((\lambda-y)+(y-\lambda)\big)=0.
\]
Thus \(yz=q\) and the first equation of (1) reduces to \(\dot x=x(g_q-bx)\). For \(g_q>0\), the positive global solutions are
\[
y(t)=\frac{\lambda}{1+h e^{-\lambda t}},\quad
z(t)=\frac{q}{y(t)},\quad
x(t)=\frac{g_q}{b+k e^{-g_q t}},
\]
where \(h=\lambda/y(0)-1>-1\), \(k=g_q/x(0)-b>-b\). Denominators remain positive for \(t\ge0\). The pairwise system (2), started at the same point of \(\mathcal L_q\), has the same unique solution. If \(g_q>0\), then \(x(t)\to g_q/b\), \(y(t)\to\lambda\), and \(z(t)\to q/\lambda\); all limits are positive.

**2. Independence on one nonresonant orbit.** Under the extra hypotheses, \(h\ne0\) and \(k\ne0\). Put \(s=e^{-\lambda t}\) and \(p=g_q/\lambda>0\). Near \(s=0\), the functions \(1\), \(y=\lambda/(1+hs)\), and \(z=q(1+hs)/\lambda\) are analytic in integer powers of \(s\), while
\[
x=\frac{g_q}{b+k s^p}=\frac{g_q}{b}-\frac{g_qk}{b^2}s^p+O(s^{2p}).
\]
If \(p\notin\mathbb N\), no nonzero multiple of \(x\) can equal an analytic combination of \(1,y,z\): the leading noninteger power has a nonzero coefficient and cannot occur in an ordinary Taylor series. To justify using the asymptotic expansion, a linear relation on any open time interval analytically continues along the positive global solution to all later times. Hence the coefficient of \(x\) in such a relation is zero. A remaining relation \(A+Cy+Dz=0\) becomes \(Cy^2+Ay+Dq=0\), which holds for continuously varying \(y\); therefore \(A=C=D=0\). This proves independence of \(1,x,y,z\). Since \(yz=q\) everywhere, the five regressors have exactly the one-dimensional kernel spanned by \((-q,0,0,0,1)\).

**3. Pooling two leaves.** A coefficient vector \((A,B,C,D,E)\) that vanishes against the five regressors on both trajectories satisfies \((A+E q_i)+Bx+Cy+Dz=0\) on each leaf \(q_i\). Step 2 implies \(B=C=D=0\) and \(A+E q_i=0\), for \(i=0,1\). Subtracting and using \(q_0\ne q_1\) gives \(E=A=0\). Thus the pooled dictionary has trivial kernel and rank five. An exact fit to (3) is unique. Since (1) has coefficient \(\theta\ne0\) in front of \(yz\), no pairwise polynomial (with \(E=0\)) can match both observations.

**4. Exact out-of-leaf prediction error.** By Step 2, the unique pairwise focal function matching the first nonresonant trajectory is \(g_{q_0}-bx\), with zero linear coefficients of \(y,z\). Evaluated on the second leaf, true minus surrogate per-capita growth is
\[
(r-bx+\theta q_1)-(g_{q_0}-bx)=\theta(q_1-q_0).
\]
Their limiting focal abundances, started positively on the second leaf, are \(g_{q_1}/b\) and \(g_{q_0}/b\), respectively. Subtraction yields the stated exact difference. This completes the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Calleja-Solanas et al. (arXiv:2605.06301, first submitted May 7, 2026) consider quadratic-per-capita higher-order Lotka–Volterra systems and present numerical examples where pairwise fits closely follow higher-order trajectories, while emphasizing that their analysis is conceptual rather than an exact identifiability classification. The result here is an **exact** infinite-time camouflage on a two-dimensional invariant leaf, together with a sharp rank-four/rank-five observational distinction and a precisely quantified off-leaf prediction error. The mechanism is intentionally specialized; it does not contest the authors' broad empirical or mechanistic conclusions.

Malizia et al. (Nature Communications 15, 5184, 2024; arXiv:2305.06609) formulate higher-order connectivity reconstruction through a linear feature/design matrix and make full column rank a condition for identification. The result here identifies a concrete ecological dynamical obstruction to that condition on one entire leaf, proves that it is exactly one-dimensional on a nonresonant orbit, and shows how a second trajectory removes it. Kleinhesselink et al. (Ecology Letters 25, 1604–1617, 2022) distinguish hard and soft higher-order interactions and explain controlled experimental designs; they do not give this conserved-product rank transition. These distinctions are based on the cited source content and should not be interpreted as proof of global priority.

## Limitations

The conservation law is a tuned structural condition, not a generic consequence of high-order ecology. The rank statement concerns continuous, noise-free, fully measured trajectories and a fixed five-term dictionary; finite noisy sampling can be ill-conditioned. This does not claim that two arbitrary initial conditions identify all hard higher-order interactions in general, nor that the interaction is mechanistically real rather than a phenomenological term. No stochastic or spatial model is covered. The explicit construction supports a possibility and an intervention principle, not a frequency claim about ecological communities.
