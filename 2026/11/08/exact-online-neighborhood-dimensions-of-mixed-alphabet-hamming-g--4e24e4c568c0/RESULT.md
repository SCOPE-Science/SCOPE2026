# Exact online neighborhood dimensions of mixed-alphabet Hamming graphs

## Finding
For arbitrary integers \(d\ge1\) and \(2\le q_1\le\cdots\le q_d\), consider the Cartesian product of complete graphs
\[
G(\mathbf q)=K_{q_1}\square\cdots\square K_{q_d}.
\]
Its vertices are words \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\), with \(0\le x_i<q_i\); two words are adjacent exactly when they differ in one coordinate. For a center \(c\), the open and closed concepts are respectively
\[
N^\circ(c)=\{x:d_H(x,c)=1\},\qquad N^\bullet(c)=\{x:d_H(x,c)\le1\}.
\]
Write \(v_\circ,\ell_\circ\) and \(v_\bullet,\ell_\bullet\) for the VC and Littlestone dimensions. **The new theorem gives the complete online dimensions \(\ell_\circ\) and \(\ell_ullet\).** The table records the previously determined static VC dimensions \(v_\circ\), \(v_ullet\) only as context; those older static formulas and the previously known equal-alphabet online cases are not claimed as original. Every online entry is proved uniformly here, including the genuinely unequal factor sizes:

| Parameters | \((v_\circ,\ell_\circ)\) | \((v_\bullet,\ell_\bullet)\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(d=1\) | \((1,1)\) | \((0,0)\) |
| \(d=2,\ (q_1,q_2)=(2,2)\) | \((1,1)\) | \((1,1)\) |
| \(d=2,\ (q_1,q_2)=(2,3)\) | \((2,2)\) | \((2,2)\) |
| \(d=2,\ q_1=2,\ q_2\ge4\) | \((3,3)\) | \((2,2)\) |
| \(d=2,\ (q_1,q_2)=(3,3)\) | \((2,3)\) | \((2,3)\) |
| \(d=2,\ 3\le q_1\le q_2,\ q_2\ge4\) | \((3,3)\) | \((3,3)\) |
| \(d=3,\ (q_1,q_2,q_3)=(2,2,2)\) | \((2,2)\) | \((3,3)\) |
| \(d=3\), all other tuples | \((3,3)\) | \((3,3)\) |
| \(d\ge4\) | \((3,3)\) | \((3,3)\) |

Together with the previously known static values, the unique strict static/adaptive gap for either concept convention occurs at \(K_3\square K_3\), where both dimensions are respectively two and three. The online heterogeneous phase boundary is \(K_2\square K_q\) for \(q\ge4\): open ranks are three but closed ranks remain two. These differences persist for arbitrarily large \(q\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite; the center of each concept is a vertex of the **full** product. A support \(S\) is VC-shattered if the concept traces on \(S\) comprise all \(2^{|S|}\) subsets. The Littlestone dimension is the largest integer \(h\) such that a complete binary tree of depth \(h\), with a vertex query at every internal node, has each root-to-leaf answer sequence realized by some concept center. Repeated queries cannot create a nontrivial split. Dimensions of a constant one-concept class are zero. The table is for full products with each \(q_i\ge2\), not arbitrary code-restricted center families.

## Proof
**1. Uniform adaptive bound.** Fix a vertex query \(x\). For a second distinct query \(y\), common positive centers for the open relation must lie in \(N^\circ(x)\cap N^\circ(y)\), and for the closed relation in \(N^\bullet(x)\cap N^\bullet(y)\). If \(d_H(x,y)=1\), the common centers lie on the coordinate line through \(x,y\): this is the line with \(x,y\) removed in the open case, and the entire line in the closed case. If the distance is two, there are exactly two common centers; at distance at least three there are none. This remains true when the coordinate alphabet sizes differ.

On any fixed coordinate line \(L\), every open-neighborhood query cuts the possible centers on \(L\) in a co-singleton, a singleton, or the empty set. Indeed, a query on \(L\) is adjacent to all its other line members; a query off \(L\) can have at most one adjacent line member. For closed queries the corresponding traces on centers in \(L\) are the entire line, a singleton, or the empty set. In each case every query has a branch with at most one surviving hypothesis, so this restricted center class has Littlestone dimension at most one. Two surviving centers also have dimension at most one. Therefore, following a first positive label, no second positive label can lead to a subtree of depth two; the first-positive branch has dimension at most two, and the entire online class has dimension at most three. Identical successive queries have an empty branch and cannot violate the argument.

**2. Uniform static bounds.** Every mixed product is an induced subgraph of an equal-alphabet Hamming graph with \(q=\max_iq_i\); every open concept is the restriction of an ambient open neighborhood. Adler–Benediktsson–Macpherson, Theorem 4.6, establishes an upper VC bound of three even for these induced restrictions. For closed concepts we give an independent proof. Suppose four points \(S\) are shattered. There is a center \(x\) whose closed ball covers all four and another center \(y\ne x\) whose ball covers exactly three. Three points must therefore lie in \(N^\bullet(x)\cap N^\bullet(y)\), forcing \(d_H(x,y)=1\) and hence those three points to lie on one coordinate line \(L\). But the intersection of any closed ball with \(L\) is either all of \(L\), one point, or empty. If the fourth support point is on \(L\), a two-point trace is impossible; if it is off \(L\), a trace consisting of two of the three line points and the fourth is impossible. Both contradict shattering. Thus \(v_\bullet\le3\).

**3. One- and two-factor cases.** If \(d=1\), the open concepts are complements of singletons, giving both ranks one; all closed concepts coincide with the full set, giving ranks zero. For \(K_2\square K_2\), open neighborhoods have only two distinct traces (the two bipartition parts); closed neighborhoods are three-element subsets of a four-element set, whose positive/negative root version spaces cannot both support depth one. Both have VC and Littlestone dimension one.

In \(K_2\square K_q\) with \(q\ge3\), closed balls are row-column crosses. Any set of three points spanning both rows meets **every** cross, preventing the empty trace. If all three points lie in one row, a cross meets that row in all three or at most one, preventing a two-point trace. Thus closed VC dimension is at most two; two distinct points in the same row are shattered when an unused column exists. For every vertex query \((r,c)\), the negative-answer centers have exactly the opposite row and a column different from \(c\). They lie on one coordinate line and hence have Littlestone dimension at most one, proving \(\ell_\bullet\le2\). This establishes both the static comparison and the new closed online rank two for all \(q\ge3\), and the already separated \((1,1)\) at \(q=2\).

In \(K_3\square K_3\), a three-support shattered by closed crosses would require an empty trace, hence must occupy at most two rows and at most two columns. Three points in a single row or column cannot be shattered. Otherwise the support is an L-shaped three-point subset of a two-by-two subgrid. Its corner cannot be isolated by any cross, contradicting shattering. Two same-row points are shattered, yielding closed VC dimension two. For \(K_3\square K_4\), the three-point set \(\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,2)\}\) is shattered by crosses: its eight traces are realized respectively by centers \((2,3),(2,0),(2,1),(0,0),(1,2),(1,0),(1,1),(0,2)\) in subset-bitmask order \(0\) through \(7\). Thus the earlier closed VC dimension is three when both factors have at least three symbols and at least one has four.

For open neighborhoods, Adler–Benediktsson–Macpherson, Theorem 4.7, gives VC dimension at most two on \(K_3\square K_3\), while an explicit two-point shattered support is \(\{(0,0),(0,1)\}\). Every smaller mixed rectangle embeds in this one. In \(K_2\square K_4\) the three points \(\{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2)\}\) are open-neighborhood shattered, witnessed for the eight subset masks by \((1,3),(1,0),(1,1),(0,2),(1,2),(0,1),(0,0),(0,3)\). Hence all remaining two-factor rectangles have open VC dimension three.

For \(K_2\square K_3\), each open ball has three elements. After the first positive answer there are at most three hypotheses, fewer than the four required to shatter a depth-two tree; hence open Littlestone dimension is exactly two. For \(K_3\square K_3\), both the open and closed systems admit a complete three-level binary mistake tree. Explicit trees with all eight validating leaf centers appear in the independently executable `verify_mixed_hamming.py` output. This is the sole case in the table where \(\ell>v\).

**4. Three and more factors.** The closed radius-one balls of the binary three-cube \(K_2\square K_2\square K_2\) shatter \(\{(0,0,0),(0,0,1),(0,1,0)\}\) by all eight traces, so closed VC and Littlestone dimensions are three for every product with \(d\ge3\). For open balls, the binary three-cube has VC dimension two by Theorem 4.7 and Littlestone dimension at most two because each open ball contains exactly three centers. A three-factor product with any alphabet at least three contains \(K_2\square K_2\square K_3\), in which the open support \(\{(0,0,0),(0,1,1),(1,0,1)\}\) is shattered. All products with at least four factors contain the binary four-cube, whose open support \(\{(0,0,0,0),(0,0,1,1),(0,1,0,1)\}\) is shattered. Combining these witnesses, induced embeddings, and uniform bounds proves every table entry.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify_mixed_hamming.py`. The independent checker directly constructs every open/closed neighborhood and all Boolean traces on supports of sizes up to four; an exact minimax recursion calculates the complete finite online rank, rather than assuming the theorem. It checks thirteen parameter vectors (26 distinct concept families) including heterogeneous rectangles, small cubes, and both exceptional ternary configurations. It separately builds and validates two explicit complete depth-three mistake trees for \(K_3\square K_3\), checking all eight root-to-leaf paths against their actual center labels. The observed summary is in `checks.txt`. This finite computation confirms representative cases; it is not used to deduce the unrestricted claims.

## Relationship to prior work
Adler, Benediktsson, and Macpherson's original paper, arXiv:2007.16042v1, Section 4, Lemmas 4.1–4.5 and Theorems 4.6–4.7, proves the universal **open** neighborhood VC upper bound and the equal-alphabet static classification. Those conclusions, as well as later exact equal-alphabet online results and an exact open rectangular-rook-graph shattering polynomial, are treated as existing results and are **not** claimed as new. In particular the entire open two-factor VC layer follows from existing enumeration. The **entire exact closed VC classification for mixed alphabets** was also available earlier and is expressly not asserted as new here. The new contribution is the *exact online* Littlestone classification for arbitrary unequal alphabets, especially the nontrivial closed online rank-two upper bound on every \(K_2\square K_q\) and the corresponding exact open online cutoff. The existing closed VC bound two does **not** imply its online rank is two: \(K_3\square K_3\) has static VC two but online rank three. The proof here establishes the additional online obstruction needed for the two-row family. The theorem also gives a unified all-factor adaptive statement that neither an open-only rectangular support polynomial nor equal-alphabet online results cover. Searches of available published-research records and relevant original literature did not locate a theorem with the exact heterogeneous table; this is not proof of absolute historical priority.

## Limitations
Full products only: exact numbers for arbitrary induced subgraphs, code-restricted centers, different radii, or multi-parameter neighborhood formulas are not asserted. The uniform upper bound three does hold for induced subgraphs by concept restriction. The online theorem is a proof for all finite positive dimensions and factor sizes, not extrapolation from the checks. Historical uniqueness and independent mathematical audit have not been established.
