# Scientific assessment: exact open and closed \(q\)-numerical disks of antiunitaries

## Correctness — PASS
For every complex Hilbert space of dimension at least three, with antiunitary \(A\), the symmetric antilinear part \(T=(A+A^{-1})/2\) has the same quadratic form as \(A\). Polarization gives its quadratic radius exactly \(\|T\|\). The identity \(T^2=(I+A^2)^*(I+A^2)/4\) converts that norm to a unitary spectral supremum. Decomposing the second unit vector into its prescribed \(q\) component and an orthogonal component gives the sharp scalar envelope and its exact equality condition. Connectedness, circular symmetry and an isotropic unit vector imply the full centered disk, not merely its outer radius. Spectral calculus yields the necessary-and-sufficient peripheral eigenspace criterion, including \(\rho=0\) and singleton boundaries. The explicit direct sums distinguish missing spectral-edge eigenvectors from norm-attaining blocks. A standalone script passed 603 algebraic and finite-block diagnostics. Finite tests are illustrative; the analytic steps prove the infinite-dimensional conclusion.

D. Kołaczek and V. Müller (2024) address ordinary antilinear numerical ranges, not the constrained \(q\)-range spectral test. Classical \(q\)-numerical range results for complex-linear normal operators are not assertions about antiunitaries. Targeted semantic searches did not identify a dominating statement. ## Mathematical value — PASS
The new result fills an explicit quantitative and topological gap left by a 2026 structural theorem: it computes a complete radius function and gives a sharp spectral test for the previously unspecified open-versus-closed alternative. Two infinite-dimensional antiunitaries may have identical numerical radius profiles yet opposite boundary inclusion for all \(|q|\ge\rho\), and the transition occurs exactly at \(|q|=\rho\). The result therefore detects genuine continuous-spectrum behavior absent in finite dimension. It does not claim new planar annular radii or an extension to arbitrary bounded antilinear maps.
