# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The cut-incidence identity accounts for open neighborhoods and identifies each Boolean trace exactly. Every bipartite support shape on one, two, or three edges is covered; explicit eight-trace witnesses establish sufficiency and their absence proves all boundary restrictions. The universal four-point exclusion is a direct application of Adler–Benediktsson–Macpherson (arXiv:2007.16042v1, Theorem 4.6) via an induced-subgraph inclusion. The standard-library checker enumerated 177840 small supports, classified triples, and excluded 250441 four-point supports on its tested boards. Finite verification is supporting evidence, not a proof of the universal exclusion.

## Originality — PASS under accessible comparisons
Adler–Benediktsson–Macpherson already prove VC dimension at most three for Hamming-graph open neighborhoods and sharpness for homogeneous Hamming graphs; this is credited. Their relevant primary full text contains no two-parameter coefficientwise rectangular-rook census or exhaustive support-shape classification. Coudert–Csikós–Ducoffe–Viennot focus on closed graph neighborhoods and general computation. Semantic searches of mathematical research records and accessible earlier claims identified no equivalent profile. Residual risk: an equivalent result might appear in lattice-graph, bipartite cut-incidence or line-graph terminology not indexed by these searches.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact bivariate shattering polynomial is a natural refinement of a bounded VC-dimension theorem: it determines the number of shattered samples at every cardinality on all rectangular rook graphs, including unequal-sided instances absent from the homogeneous-Hamming threshold theorem. A complete structural classification explains the coefficients and sharp small-board discontinuities rather than merely substituting parameters into existing dimensional bounds. The precise coefficient counts may inform sample-complexity and typical-support comparisons for finite definable adjacency systems.
