# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact claim is about the side from which a one-dimensional Brownian trajectory reaches a point while a two-state gate is active. Its backward equations contain position resetting but not gate resetting; the active-state boundary values are distinct on the two half-lines while the inactive solution and derivative match. The weighted-sum and difference diagonalization gives a unique bounded solution, and the explicit formula and all derivatives were recalculated. A finite-time uniform absorption opportunity ensures the stopped process is absorbed almost surely. The global monotonicity uses a strict analytic derivative inequality rather than numerical inspection. The independently discretized two-sided backward problem converges to the formula. Important constraints: all rates, diffusivity and initial distance are positive; the gate occupancy is held fixed when switching speed varies.

**Originality — PASS, with priority qualifications.** The primary source arXiv:2512.09739 contains the same physical model and displays simulated directional hitting fractions in its Section III.3 and Fig. 5, but does not state the analytic conditional or stationary side-arrival formulas or their global monotonicities. Prior gated-boundary studies arXiv:2107.02148 and arXiv:2601.01588 use reflecting nonreactive boundaries, so no opposite-side absorption occurs. arXiv:2601.13414 treats a permeable/impermeable *interface* without absorption on the permeable side, and arXiv:2009.02739 addresses a different gate-reset variant. Semantic published-record searches and accessible previous-claim comparisons did not identify equivalent or broader coverage. A qualitative trend in the primary source's simulation discussion is in tension with the mathematical monotonicity; further empirical reconciliation and independent priority assessment are outstanding.

**Value — PASS.** The source singles out the *side of absorption* as a hard-to-calculate measurable statistic, and treats it only numerically. Its exact evaluation distinguishes transparent from reflecting gating, settles how changing reset and gate-switching rates moves directional absorption, and exhibits a singular fast-switching observable that cannot be read off from convergence of ordinary first-passage-time distributions. These are motivated structural results for a recognized physical search model, rather than a parameter substitution or a tiny numerical improvement.

The source first appeared publicly on 10 December 2025.
