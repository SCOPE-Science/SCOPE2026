# Exact ordinary and fractional illumination of full braid-fan polytopes

## Finding
Let \(n\ge2\), and write \(H=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_{i=1}^n x_i=0\}\). Let \(P\) be a full-dimensional polytope in an affine translate of \(H\) whose normal fan, modulo the all-ones direction, is exactly the type-\(A_{n-1}\) braid fan. Equivalently, its \(n!\) vertices \(v_\pi\), indexed by permutations \(\pi\) of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), have outward normal cones
\[
N_P(v_\pi)=\{w\in H:w_{\pi(1)}\le\cdots\le w_{\pi(n)}\}.
\]
Every such polytope has **ordinary and fractional directional illumination numbers**
\[
\boxed{I(P)=I^*(P)=n.}
\]
Furthermore, for every nonzero direction \(u\in H\) for which every nonempty proper subset has nonzero coordinate sum, exactly \((n-1)!\) vertices are illuminated by \(u\); an arbitrary direction illuminates at most \((n-1)!\) vertices. The explicit \(n\) directions \(u^{(i)}=n e_i-\mathbf1\), \(1\le i\le n\), illuminate disjoint classes of \((n-1)!\) vertices and together illuminate the entire polytope. Thus the exact result is stable under every deformation preserving the full braid normal fan, including nonsymmetric deformations.

## Assumptions and scope
The illumination of a boundary point \(x\) by \(u\ne0\) means that \(x+\varepsilon u\in\operatorname{int}_{\operatorname{aff}P} P\) for some \(\varepsilon>0\), with the interior taken in the affine span. \(I(P)\) is the least number of directions illuminating every boundary point. \(I^*(P)\) is the infimum of the total mass of a finite nonnegative Borel measure on the unit sphere of \(H\) that assigns mass at least one to the directions illuminating each boundary point. Scaling an illuminating direction by a positive constant does not matter.

The fan-equality assumption is essential to this proof; *coarsening* the braid fan is not covered. The standard strict permutohedron \(\operatorname{conv}\{(a_{\pi(1)},\ldots,a_{\pi(n)}):\pi\in S_n\}\), for any strictly increasing real numbers \(a_1<\cdots<a_n\), satisfies the assumption, after translation. So do the positive-weight complete graphical zonotopes \(\sum_{1\le i<j\le n}c_{ij}[e_i,e_j]\) with every \(c_{ij}>0\); unequal weights ordinarily destroy permutation symmetry without changing the normal fan.

## Proof
**1. Exact vertex illumination criterion.** The chamber \(N_P(v_\pi)\) is simplicial in \(H\). Its extremal rays are generated by the centered indicator vectors of the final segments \(\{\pi(j+1),\ldots,\pi(n)\}\), for \(1\le j<n\). The strict inward condition at \(v_\pi\) is negativity against every nonzero outward extremal generator. Since \(\sum_i u_i=0\), the generator for the final segment has dot product
\[
\sum_{h=j+1}^n u_{\pi(h)}=-\sum_{h=1}^{j}u_{\pi(h)}.
\]
Thus \(u\) illuminates \(v_\pi\) if and only if
\[
S_j(\pi,u):=\sum_{h=1}^{j}u_{\pi(h)}>0\quad\text{for all }1\le j<n.
\]
The strictness follows from illumination into the relative interior, not merely into the tangent cone's boundary.

**2. Exact number for generic directions.** Call \(u\) generic when \(\sum_{i\in A}u_i\ne0\) for every nonempty proper subset \(A\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Fix a permutation of the labeled indices and write the \(n\) cyclic prefix sums \(S_0=0,S_1,\ldots,S_{n-1}\). No two are equal: their difference is a nonempty proper consecutive subsum. Rotate the permutation to start immediately after the unique minimum of these \(n\) values. Every proper prefix sum of that rotation is strictly positive; for each other rotation at least one proper prefix sum is nonpositive. Cyclic rotation acts freely on the \(n!\) labeled permutations in orbits of size \(n\). Consequently precisely \(n!/n=(n-1)!\) permutations satisfy the vertex criterion.

For an arbitrary nongeneric direction, each vertex illuminated by it remains illuminated throughout some neighborhood of that direction, because the inequalities are strict. A sufficiently small generic perturbation therefore illuminates all those vertices, showing that the original direction illuminates **at most** \((n-1)!\) vertices.

**3. Matching construction.** For \(u^{(i)}=ne_i-\mathbf1\), every proper subset has a nonzero coordinate sum. At a vertex indexed by \(\pi\), if \(\pi(1)=i\) then
\[
S_j(\pi,u^{(i)})=n-j>0\qquad(1\le j<n).
\]
If \(\pi(1)\ne i\), already \(S_1=-1<0\). Thus \(u^{(i)}\) illuminates exactly those \((n-1)!\) vertices whose first label is \(i\). These \(n\) classes partition all \(n!\) vertices. Every boundary point belongs to a proper face and hence to a face containing some vertex. Any direction illuminating that vertex satisfies all facet inequalities strictly at every point of the containing face; therefore a vertex-illuminating family illuminates the whole boundary. It follows that \(I(P)\le n\).

**4. Both sharp lower bounds.** A single direction illuminates at most \((n-1)!\) of the \(n!\) vertices, so at least \(n\) directions are necessary: \(I(P)\ge n\). For any admissible fractional illuminating measure \(\mu\), sum the \(n!\) vertex constraints and interchange the finite sum with integration:
\[
n!\le\int\#\{\pi:u\text{ illuminates }v_\pi\}\,d\mu(u)
\le (n-1)!\,\mu(S(H)).
\]
Therefore \(\mu(S(H))\ge n\). Unit point masses at the normalized \(u^{(i)}\) give the opposite bound \(I^*(P)\le n\). Combining proves both equalities and the exact generic-incidence assertion.

## Verification
The standalone script `braid_illumination_verifier.py` is a supplementary finite, exact-integer test of the partial-sum and outward-facet criteria. Run with `python3 braid_illumination_verifier.py`; its recorded output is `BRAID_FAN_ILLUMINATION_EXACT_OK 1258632`. It checks all labeled permutations through \(n=8\), both generic and nongeneric example directions, the explicit \(n\)-direction partition, and independently the active facet inequalities of nonsymmetrically weighted complete-graph zonotopes through \(n=6\). These tests are not an infinite-dimensional certificate; the proof above establishes the universal claim.

## Relationship to prior work
Kong and Zeng (2025) determine an *affine-hyperplane vertex covering* invariant of standard and nonstandard permutohedra; this is different from the directional illumination/positive-homothety-cover invariant. Their sharp \((n-1)!\) bound applies to vertices **lying in an affine hyperplane**, not vertices illuminated by one direction, and it does not give the present all-deformation assertion. The geometry of braid-fan deformations and graphical zonotopes is described by Padrol, Pilaud and Poullot (2023) and by Postnikov, Reiner and Williams (2008); their statements concern normal fans and face combinatorics, not illumination counts. Godland and Kabluchko (2023) calculate projections and conic angle sums for belt polytopes, but not the exact covering of their vertices by inward cones.

Naszódi (2009) introduces the fractional illumination invariant; Ivanov (2026) proves a broad illumination upper bound for primitive polytopes; Gorodzha (2026) proves a general fractional upper bound. These inequalities do not specialize to the claimed exact value. An earlier exact calculation for the regular truncated octahedron already gives the \(n=4\) symmetric example, and elementary planar illumination gives \(n=3\); the contribution here is the complete all-dimensional, full-normal-fan classification, including arbitrary nonsymmetric deformations and the uniform exact incidence law.

## Limitations
No statement is made about generalized permutohedra with a strictly coarser normal fan (such as hypersimplices), nor about arbitrary affine-hyperplane covers of their vertex sets. The proof determines ordinary and fractional **directional** illumination, not other quantitative illumination functionals. Historical priority among obscure publications on Coxeter fans and illumination is not conclusively settled; the checked literature did not reveal a dominating result. Independent mathematical auditing has not been carried out.
