# Scientific review: orientation and optimal-set spectra of cycle bouquets

## Correctness — PASS

The proof applies to every integer \(r\ge2\) and every tuple \(\ell_i\ge3\). The local indegree condition forces each petal to be one of exactly two directed-cycle orientations or \(\ell_i-1\) convergent-path orientations. The hub has positive indegree precisely when at least one petal is cyclic. Internal domination forces the hub into every feasible set; a directed-cycle petal requires exactly \(\ell_i-2\) internal selections, a noncyclic petal exactly \(\ell_i-3\), and exactly one extra cyclic predecessor is needed to dominate the hub. These sharp local lower bounds attain the global optimum \(b+c\). Counting all valid orientations and all optimal sets yields the two stated polynomials. Direct orientation and vertex-subset enumeration agrees for 14 test profiles (424 valid orientations), but the proof—not those finite checks—establishes the arbitrary-parameter result.

## Originality — PASS (provisional publication priority)

Hansen–Lai–Yue (1999) introduce the equivalent open-orientable-domination invariant and prove interval realization for complete graphs, not the present two bouquet polynomials. Anderson–Dravec–Johnston–Kuenzel (arXiv:2311.16307v1, public 2023-11-27) provide general theory and unicyclic/extremal cases; their inspected sections do not compute these distributions for cycle bouquets. Blázsik–Nagy (arXiv:2411.04560) classify a global upper-extreme case and may subsume the two-petal upper-value corollary, but not the full two-parameter enumeration. Relevant published research-record searches under cycle-bouquet, friendship, open orientable domination, directed total domination, cactus, and equivalent terms produced no matching theorem. Non-coverage in a search is not a uniqueness proof. An unindexed enumeration or generic graph-gluing transfer theorem is the main residual risk; a specialist should assess priority independently.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A bouquet has arbitrary cyclic rank but a single cut vertex, so it is a motivated first multi-cycle extension of the unicyclic class. The result counts every valid orientation at every achievable directed total-domination number and, more strongly, every optimal-set/orientation pair. It exhibits an arbitrary gap \(r-1\) between extremes while realizing every intermediate value, connecting to the long-standing interval-spectrum question for open orientable domination. The explicit local orientation type classification and weighted product/derivative transfer are structurally informative beyond scalar bounds or a single finite instance. The known scalar specializations are acknowledged, not presented as new discoveries.

## Limits

These are same-review mathematical judgments rather than independent certification. No claim is made for arbitrary cactus graphs or graph families with differently arranged blocks. Priority uncertainty remains around older terminology and general block-root factorization results. Finite test cases are corroborative only.
