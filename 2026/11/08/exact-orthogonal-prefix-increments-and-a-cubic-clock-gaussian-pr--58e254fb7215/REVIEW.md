# Research review: nested inversions in asymmetric shelf shuffles

## Correctness — PASS
The physical insertion rule gives the pair kernel. Conditional expectations on the earlier and later card have separate projections; the exact degenerate residual is a martingale with quadratic mean of order \(n^2\). Orthogonality and the zero-sum first projection yield the finite-prefix covariance formula for every integer \(k\le\ell\). A bivariate partial-sum invariance principle yields the Gaussian integral process; direct integration shows the cubic Brownian clock. Exact enumeration from physical shelf stacks independently tests finite cases. Risks: none presently identified in the algebra, but the checker cannot validate functional weak convergence by itself.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk
The accessible full-text Section 3 of Clay (arXiv:2510.00343) treats one-deck inversions and its marginal Gaussian approximation; those results are prior and are not relabeled. Tripathi (arXiv:2606.18047) studies asymmetric shelf-shuffle inversions, but its full text remained unavailable; its abstract and journal preview do not establish whether nested-prefix covariances are considered. Published-search and research-record comparisons found no directly covering statement of the exact nested-prefix covariance identity or the particular coupled trajectory. Prior general sequential U-statistic theory may imply a functional limit but does not itself specify this model-specific finite-sample identity. No historical uniqueness is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A shuffle's inversion count is a natural, widely studied permutation-disorder statistic. The exact lack of cross-prefix covariance beyond the shorter prefix makes incremental disorder assessments especially tractable without assuming independence. The cubic-clock law provides a joint, sequential uncertainty description and simultaneous Gaussian limit, beyond separate marginal deck sizes. This is a structural finite-sample cancellation plus a motivated process consequence, rather than a small improvement to a known one-time error rate.

## Boundaries
The theorem does not imply independent increments before scaling, a mixing theorem, a growing-shelf theorem, or previously unknown one-deck unbiased normal approximations. The unavailable 2026 primary full text remains a priority risk requiring independent review.
