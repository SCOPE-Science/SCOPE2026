# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Every zero set is a nonattacking rook placement, counted by \(\binom mk\binom nk k!\). For a chosen \(t\)-subset of zeros the union of open neighborhoods has exactly \(t(m+n-t-1)\) other vertices, and inclusion–exclusion supplies each weight coefficient without cancellation assumptions. The lower bound on the number of defenders is attained; the even and odd minimum-multiplicity formulas follow by an exhaustive separation into perfect-matchings, single-defender-plus-matching, and one-two-edge-path-plus-matching cases. Every proof step is valid for all \(m,n\ge2\). The total condition is automatic when a clique fiber has at least three vertices, while \(2\times2\) is explicitly treated. Direct exhaustive enumeration and symbolic checks corroborate all displayed quantities, but finite checks are not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The full directly relevant arXiv manuscript and its open-access journal version were inspected, particularly Section 3.3 and Theorem 3.4. The paper establishes the same scalar minimum for the total variant and provides optimal examples; this part is prior art and is excluded from the claimed novelty. It does not present the complete Roman weight polynomial or the number of all optimal assignments. Searches for exact rook-graph label enumerators, Cartesian-product versions, and equivalent Roman terminology did not identify a directly covering primary result. A closest public complete-multipartite polynomial concerns a different graph class and cannot be substituted here. Residual risk: an unindexed rook-graph enumeration, a stronger general Roman-enumerator theorem, or an equivalent result under alternate terminology may cover the claim; a failed search alone is not a priority guarantee.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is a natural counting refinement of a previously studied Roman-domination optimization problem on the canonical two-dimensional Hamming graph. The formula counts *all* feasible weight classes, while the parity-sensitive minimum-coefficient theorem distinguishes the two structurally different optimal covering patterns. The exact boundary between ordinary and total outer-independent Roman functions is also explicit. These distinctions are useful for extremal multiplicities and labelled configuration distributions, beyond routine re-evaluation of a known scalar.

## Limitations and relevant literature
The closest work is Cabrera Martínez–Kuziak–Yero (arXiv:2112.05476v1, Theorem 3.4), which covers the minimum but not the asserted full counts. The earlier foundational OIRDF paper (DOI:10.3390/sym12111846) was available via abstract and bibliographic descriptions, not fully accessible in one attempted route. An equivalent unpublished or incompletely indexed formula cannot be ruled out. The script checks finite cases and does not replace mathematical review.
