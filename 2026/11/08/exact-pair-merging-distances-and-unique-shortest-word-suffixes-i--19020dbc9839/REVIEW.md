# Scientific review of the pair-merging suffix-chain theorem

**Correctness: PASS.** The fully specified labeled transitions and pair-state normalization remove convention ambiguity. The integer-valued potential covers all \(\binom n2\) ranks without repetition. Direct transition casework shows a unique outgoing edge drops the rank by one while the alternative never drops it; this proves both the shortest lengths and uniqueness of the associated word. The rank-residue argument establishes the explicit suffix word. The separate standard-library verifier agrees with reverse BFS for all cases through 80 states and checks 10,666,600 pairs in the extended finite ranges. Computation is corroboration, not proof by enumeration.

**Originality: PASS, with residual risk.** Behague–Johnson (arXiv:2008.12166, §1) explicitly know the extremal \(\binom n2\) rendezvous time and a realizing Černý pair; this maximum is not claimed here. Gusev (arXiv:1404.6731, §3 and Fig. 3) already gives the odd-state pair automaton, so odd-state implications of that graph are significant earlier coverage. The claimed addition is the explicit *whole* pair-distance bijection in both parities, the unique shortest word for each pair, and the shared suffix-chain law. Direct full-text inspection and searches did not find these stated results; implicit or unindexed equivalent coverage remains possible. Rank-of-image and prescribed-image reachability theorems concern different initial sets and quantifiers; they do not imply the claimed pair-merging spectrum.

**Value: PASS.** Pair rendezvous distances are natural state-recovery invariants in synchronizing automata. The result gives a complete distribution, a sharp boundary value, and a rigid canonical shortest-word structure for an infinite benchmark family. The suffix-chain provides optimal synchronization of any specified pair by a table-free rank-to-word rule. The theorem is not merely an isolated sample computation or a rephrasing of the known extremal bound.

**Residual limitations.** The result covers two-state merging for this labeled family and makes no general reset-threshold assertion. The strongest bibliographic risk is derivability from Gusev's odd-state transition representation, although a both-parity explicit formula and shortest-word uniqueness are not stated there.
