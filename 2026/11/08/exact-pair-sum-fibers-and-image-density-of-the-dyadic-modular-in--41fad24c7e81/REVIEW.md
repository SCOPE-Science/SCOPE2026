# Review of the dyadic inverse-graph convolution theorem

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof checks all cases: zero first sum, unequal valuations, valuation one with even completed-square roots, and higher valuations with odd roots. The modular division uses only unit division modulo the correct lower modulus. The twofold image count uses exact square classes and includes the exceptional zero target. The additive energy argument has a bijective parametrization of all equal-sum quadruples and verifies its factor of 128 from lifts. Exhaustive independent convolution checks for exponents 4 through 11 agree with all formulas.

## Originality

**PASS, bounded comparison.** The natural equivalents searched were two-dimensional inverse-graph pair sums, dyadic modular-hyperbola Minkowski sums, pointwise convolution fibers, Kloosterman Fourier fourth moments and square-root densities. Shparlinski's *Modular Hyperbolas* treats point distribution and Kloosterman sums. Bower–Evans–Luo–Miller's **coordinate** sumsets are one-dimensional projections \(u+u^{-1}\), not the image of the addition map on two copies of the two-dimensional hyperbola. Gong–Veys–Wan's Kloosterman power moments overlap the fourth-moment **corollary** and are not used as a novelty claim, but do not imply the explicit support law or individual fibers from their stated moment formulas. Nica's moment and Selberg–Kuznetsov results are relevant broader context without an identified complete pointwise twofold dyadic law. A previous odd-prime-power inverse-graph convolution classification does not establish the characteristic-two valuation-one branch. No directly dominating published claim was identified. Historical priority remains uncertain, since an alternative earlier modular quadratic-image theorem could imply the result.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The two-dimensional Minkowski sum of a modular hyperbola is a canonical additive-geometric object relevant to Fourier sampling and Kloosterman transforms. The complete fiber law gives exact attainable targets, their representation counts, and the unexpectedly low limiting image density \(1/96\), rather than merely reproducing a moment. The parity-dependent first-order term and special valuation-one stratum distinguish the dyadic case from generic odd-prime-power lifting. The energy corollary is presented for completeness without asserting that moments themselves are new.

## Boundaries and risk

The claimed domain is powers of two of exponent at least four, with unit inverse graph and ordered pairs. The conclusion is exact pointwise convolution and image size, not a sharp weighted Fourier restriction inequality. Novelty is a best-knowledge conclusion under the cited comparisons rather than formal proof of historical priority. Numerical experiments alone do not prove the all-exponent theorem.
