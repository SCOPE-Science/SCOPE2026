# Scientific review: five-run palindromic defect

**Correctness — PASS.** For binary palindromes the number of symbol transitions is even. A five-run word has only four transitions, so the exhaustive classes have zero, two, or four transitions. The full interior run is necessary in every two-transition palindrome, and all four transitions force equal lengths of the two \(1\)-runs. Their possible overlaps are accounted for explicitly. The family count yields the formula with no assumptions on relative run lengths; the defect-spectrum upper bound and witnesses are constructive. Direct center-expansion checks compared literal factor sets and defects for all 7,776 tuples with positive run lengths at most six and compared exact spectra for lengths 5 through 30. Risk: the check is bounded, while the proof relies on elementary but exhaustively analyzed transition classes.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Searches of published research records for five-run palindromic defect, exact defect spectra, and run-length richness returned no quantitative formula of this form. The 2015 Guo–Shallit–Shur text includes richness conditions but not the stated quantitative defect spectrum in the relevant inspected sections. The 2016 Guo–Shallit–Shur paper offers necessary-and-sufficient richness conditions in terms of run-lengths, and therefore may cover the zero-defect Boolean corollary. Its full journal text could not be inspected; the article abstract alone cannot establish absence of a stronger result. The 2026 Kim–Kwon paper studies infinite-word complexity, a different invariant and domain. Residual risk: the numerical formula might be derivable immediately from the 2016 characterization or another unindexed source; priority is not certified.

**Value — PASS.** Defect quantifies how far a word falls short of palindromic richness. Four or fewer binary runs are rich by the same transition count, whereas five positive alternating runs are the smallest run-count class that permits positive defect, so an all-parameter formula and *complete sharp lengthwise spectrum* delimit the first nontrivial complexity boundary. This is a natural classification, rather than an arbitrary finite sample or a mere restatement of a rich-word test. Its potential use is as an exact baseline for quantitative defect questions at larger run count and for finite-word structural benchmarks. No downstream theorem is asserted.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Guo–Shallit–Shur (2015, arXiv:1503.09112; 2016, DOI 10.1016/j.ipl.2016.07.001); Kim–Kwon (2026, arXiv:2609.16596). The work concerns positive-length five-run binary words, not arbitrary alphabets, infinite words or more runs. The 2016 article's complete content remains an unresolved historical-priority risk.
