# Self-review

## Correctness (PASS)

The theorem quantifies over all integer bases \(b\ge3\) and all positive lengths. The marker enumeration and gap formula are exact by binary carry analysis. The valuation symmetry around each consecutive marker pair identifies its entire forced core, while the unequal external valuations prove a sharp symmetric zero-padding limit. One-centered palindromes and all-zero factors are treated separately; these exhaust the cases by parity of the number of markers. Distinct central gap sizes certify distinct type-three factors, and explicit interval inequalities yield the sharp uniform bound. Independent string-level enumeration verifies 1120 base-length cases but is used only as a diagnostic; the infinite classification follows from the symbolic proof.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)

The closest paper about the original ternary Cantor word, Chen–Lü–Wu (https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04063), studies \(k\)-Abelian complexity, not an explicit palindromic factor-language formula. The Cantor-like permutation-complexity study (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2015.12.022) uses the same all-base morphism but a different invariant. Schaeffer–Shallit (https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02074) supplies general decidability and examples for other sequences, not the exact classification. Allouche–Baake–Cassaigne–Damanik (https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0106121) treats general palindrome complexity and primitive substitutions; the generalized Cantor substitution is nonprimitive. Searches of other mathematical research records and accessible earlier claims identified no equivalent or stronger covering statement. The risk of unindexed equivalent prior work cannot be eliminated without broader specialist review.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

A digit-restricted Cantor automatic word is a standard self-similar, nonprimitive substitution family and the previously studied factor/permutation/Abelian invariants make its palindromic spectrum a well-motivated question. The result gives an entire infinite factor-language classification, identifies all maximal palindromes, computes exact complexity at every length, and proves an unexpectedly small sharp bound three uniformly over all integer bases, including the exact ternary parity law. The dyadic gaps and symmetry obstruction are structural, not an arbitrary small-case enumeration.
