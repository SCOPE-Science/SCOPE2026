# Scientific review: exact symmetric-orbit spectra in harmonic outer billiards

## Correctness — PASS
The single claim concerns the two explicitly specified tangent-intersection families, not all outer-billiard periodic points. The proof fixes a consistent outer-map orientation, computes the physical tangent length and curvature radius from the original harmonic table, and derives the area-preserving one-step Jacobian from the actual tangent midpoint relation. Consecutive tangent frames rotate by a fixed angle, making the return derivative conjugate to a constant determinant-one matrix raised to the primitive period. Its exact trace separates the two families for the entire strict-convexity interval. Strict phase monotonicity, the boundary phase limit, and a quadratic equation give all and only scalar-monodromy parameters for the elliptic family. The distinct eigenvalues of the one-step co-rotating matrix justify the stronger assertion of scalar return monodromy, not merely repeated multipliers. The two asymptotic laws follow from explicit trace expansions. A fresh standalone checker reconstructed the outer map numerically and passed 104 finite-difference Jacobian comparisons and 2,070 resonance checks. These experiments do not prove the universal statements and do not cover nonlinear bifurcations.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The motivating paper describes the same symmetric points as coexisting elliptic and hyperbolic examples but gives no quantified linear-instability law. The complete parameter classification determines exactly when the return is the strong degeneracy \(\pm I\), not merely whether an orbit is elliptic. Its threshold count varies nontrivially with the harmonic degree, while the hyperbolic exponent diverges logarithmically as the table approaches loss of strict curvature. The explicit finite resonance census is useful for locating degeneracies before investigating KAM islands and perturbative bifurcations, and the exact multipliers make dynamical comparisons reproducible. The scope is restricted to named cycles; no nonlinear stability or global periodic-orbit classification is inferred.
