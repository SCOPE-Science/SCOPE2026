# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The exact continuum persistence identity follows from the reflected Brownian heat kernel for arbitrary endpoints. For positive-definite endpoint covariance the two-dimensional Gaussian integral reduces, by an explicit linear scaling and polar coordinates, to an evaluated one-dimensional rational integral. Its large-negative-parameter asymptotic proves the logarithmic high-scale constant. Rank-one cases follow from one Gaussian half-line integral; zero endpoints cannot sustain a nonnegative Brownian bridge. Eight independent parameter examples, rank-one quadrature cases and large-scale diagnostics support the calculations, with 37 numerical assertions passing. Numerical checks are not used to prove infinite-scale or continuum statements.

## Originality (PASS, subject to historical priority)
The closest process-classification source is Raket, arXiv:2610.36762v1, Theorem 2.1, which identifies Gaussian endpoint covariance processes but does not state the integrated persistence law or its determinant-sensitive logarithmic deficit. The companion arXiv:2610.33747v1 studies boundary operators and Gaussian conditionings, rather than this full-cone survival functional. The known deterministic-endpoint bridge reflection identity and Gaussian orthant identity are inputs, not claimed discoveries. Recorded mathematical literature comparisons and research-record searches found no directly dominating exact rank-dependent persistence statement. A related older paper on maxima of Gaussian bridges is a priority risk because its full text was not inspected.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
Nonnegative-path survival is a natural sign-constrained functional for the complete family of Brownian covariance kernels. The explicit probability is usable for exact sign-constrained Gaussian path calculations, and the distinction between full-rank and perfectly correlated endpoints exposes a sharp change in the rate of approach to the endpoint-orthant limit. The logarithmic coefficient depends on the covariance determinant and cannot be read off from the two endpoint sign probabilities. No new universal Brownian reflection principle is claimed.

## Residual limitations
The covariance must remain fixed as the variance scale diverges, and no uniform near-rank-one transition is proved. Full historical priority remains conditional on access to broader Gaussian-bridge extremal literature. Independent validation has not been performed.
