# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact finite law follows from an explicit three-term product-Laplacian pseudoinverse, a fully evaluated block transfer-current matrix and the determinant generating function. The one-dimensional constant subspace has eigenvalues \(1\) and \(2/(m+n)\), and the two difference subspaces have the asserted eigenvalues. The leaf-extremum calculation has the strictly positive explicit second derivative throughout the admissible real interval, including endpoint cases. The Poisson approximation follows from a standard finite Bernoulli coupling bound. A separate exact-integer graph Laplacian calculation, small-graph full spanning-tree enumeration and exact-rational generating-function comparisons yield 2522 assertions. These are finite corroborations, not replacements for proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Relevant general frameworks were inspected: Hladký–Nachmias–Tran (arXiv:1711.09788, Sections 1 and main theorem), Nachmias–Peres (arXiv:2011.08833, Introduction and Section 2) and Rapoport (arXiv:2312.14992, Introduction, Sections 3–4 and complete-graph example). Nachmias–Peres already imply the qualitative Poisson local limit, so no novelty is claimed for that limit. Rapoport's general degree formulas can in principle be specialized to rook graphs, but the inspected statement does not evaluate the all-parameter three-summand law or establish the strict fixed-degree leaf extrema. The separate rook-graph adjacent-degree covariance result addresses a joint second moment, not the marginal degree law. Targeted scholarly and mathematical-record searches uncovered no direct formulation of the evaluated sharp extremum. General formula specialization and undiscovered exact computations remain priority risks.

## Value — PASS

The original graph degree can be held fixed while its two Cartesian factors and vertex count vary. The exact root-leaf probability provides the expected leaf fraction, and its **strict balancing minimizer** is a natural finite-size design/comparison question in uniform spanning trees. The structural independent-summands representation is substantially more informative than a single probability or a generic limiting law: it supplies all finite degree probabilities, uniform error control across degenerate aspect ratios, and an exact extremal principle. This is a motivated local invariant, not an arbitrary fixed-size enumeration.

## Closest literature and limits

The universal high-degree regular graph Poisson limit is known (Nachmias–Peres 2020), and general transfer-current/degree formulas are known (Rapoport 2023). Those broader methods are acknowledged rather than claimed as new. The full joint degree field, full leaf-count distribution, and independent mathematical/priority validation are outside scope.
