# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The result is confined to the regular truncated tetrahedron and exactly Haar-uniform orientations of planes through its centroid. The supplied executable certificate uses rational integer determinants to reconstruct the actual polyhedron and its edges, exhausts the 12-great-circle arrangement, verifies 134 chambers and the corner-count table, and checks the spherical-excess calculation. The proof derives all probabilities for all generic normals; sampling is not offered as an infinite proof. Degenerate directions form a measure-zero union of great circles.

## Originality
PASS on the accessible scientific comparisons, provisionally. Jiao–Torquato's complete 2011 preprint treats packing, not central section counts. Socha's complete 1988 paper treats individual section-angle distributions for random affine planes, not the present random variable. Ohser–Mücklich's 1995 abstract concerns stochastic section-profile inversion of prisms and random polyhedra. Voss (1982) is the strongest named risk, explicitly deriving frequencies of section polygon counts for families of polyhedra; its complete article was inaccessible. The random affine-plane slicing measure indicated in the abstract is distinct from central orientation measure, but its exact treated families cannot be excluded. This is not independent historical certification, and no conclusion is based solely on a failed search.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The full three-point probability law of a non-centrally-symmetric Archimedean solid is a natural exact invariant of random central sections. Unlike a mean vertex count, it distinguishes the substantial odd-section mass and all possible combinatorial types. Exact spherical chamber counts explain the probabilities and provide a reproducible nonnumerical result relevant to polyhedral section stereology. The work does not claim a general classification of all polyhedra.

## Limits
Historical priority risk for Voss (1982); no random-offset distribution, perturbation stability, or independent validation. Closest related exact results for different polyhedra do not imply these formulas.
