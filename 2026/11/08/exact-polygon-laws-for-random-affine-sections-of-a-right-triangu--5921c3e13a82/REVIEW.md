# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim applies to every positive apex height and defines the invariant law of intersecting affine planes explicitly, including the crucial width weighting. Sorting projected base-vertex heights yields four exact offset-length identities; in particular the pentagon intervals split the apex heights and intersect the base range, while the hexagon intervals lie outside the apex range and below or above the projected median base height. The nine actual bipyramid edges provide a complete crossing count. Integrating the offset lengths over Haar directions yields two single-angle integrals, which integrate by parts to the stated closed arctangent functions. Normalizing by the mean offset width yields the four probabilities. Strict concavity of \(F\), positivity of the width, endpoint signs of \(Q\), and strict decrease of \(Q\) prove that the pentagon optimum exists and is unique; direct monotonicity proves that the hexagon probability decreases. A separate standard-library checker reconstructs the actual edge graph and 140,000 width-weighted random affine sections at each of three heights, evaluates independent quadratures, and confirms the numerical optimizer. Its finite tests support but do not establish the all-height proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
Brandenburg–De Loera–Meroni (arXiv:2304.14239v1, examined full HTML, including chamber theorems, sections, and examples) classify cell chambers of affine hyperplane sections but do not integrate invariant measures for this body. Van der Jagt–Jongbloed–Vittorietti (arXiv:2305.02864v1; inspected the accessible full journal PDF, including IUR sampling and numerical density discussions) concern section-area densities rather than polygon counts. Sukiasyan (1982, pages 809–812; examined source text and page images) reports numerical probabilities for triangular pyramids, triangular prisms, rectangular parallelepipeds, and hexagonal prisms, not symmetric triangular bipyramids, and not an exact analytic height optimization. Black–Criado (arXiv:2406.06936v1) concern planar projections/shadows rather than noncentral sections. Closest comparable studies of triangular-prism section probabilities and of the same bipyramid's extremal shadow areas do not logically imply this different complete four-type IUR section distribution; polarity alone does not preserve Crofton probability. Specific semantic searches found no dominating/equivalent exact formula; this is not an exhaustive historical priority claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The five-vertex triangular bipyramid is a canonical minimal nonsimplex three-polytope admitting four nontrivial affine section polygon types, including hexagons. The theorem supplies a closed law for all four categories across every positive height, not just a single sampled parameter, and a strict unique optimizer of a natural stereological frequency. The delicate distinction between angle-uniform and invariant width-weighted random-plane probabilities has measurable consequences. The results are not obtained solely from a generic chamber-count algorithm or relabeling an existing prism law.

## Limitations and residual risks
The source comparisons cannot exclude every alternative stereological formulation, particularly older non-English or non-digitized literature; this is a qualified originality assessment, not an independent historical-priority certificate. The formulas do not cover skew bipyramids, nonsymmetric heights, or the distribution of area/perimeter. Numerical decimals have not been represented as exact algebraic constants.
