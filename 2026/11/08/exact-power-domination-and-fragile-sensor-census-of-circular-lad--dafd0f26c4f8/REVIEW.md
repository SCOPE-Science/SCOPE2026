# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem uses a complete distance characterization: a close pair seeds two consecutive observed rungs and propagates around the prism; without such a pair, separated induced closed stars each have two unobserved neighbors at their outer vertices and cannot force. The cyclic gap encoding covers every failed placement and every cardinality with a precise \(n/k\) rooting correction; the two-color trace-matrix determinant independently derives the rational polynomial. The Lucas factorization and observation formulas are algebraic consequences. For the sparse limit, the exact \(11n\) candidate sensor pairs have a bounded-degree dependency graph, so factorial moments give the Poisson law. The standalone verifier independently tests the original forcing dynamics for all sensor subsets on prisms through nine columns. This does not convert finite tests into an unrestricted proof; the structural and moment proofs provide unrestricted validity.

Their related fault-tolerance theorem already addresses maximum failing configurations. These ingredients are credited and not claimed. Brimkov et al. Searches for circular ladder/prism/generalized Petersen aliases, third-power independence polynomials, and fragile sensors did not reveal a directly covering result. The official full-text 2024 endpoint could not be inspected in full; this is a substantive publication-priority risk, acknowledged rather than concealed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A labelled power-domination polynomial is a standard structural graph invariant measuring all sensor configurations, not merely the optimum. Here the exact failure polynomial has coefficients determined by color-changing cyclic gaps, and the total count unexpectedly collapses to a Lucas expression with nontrivial parity and ternary corrections. The same structure gives an exact complete-observation probability and coverage expectation for random failures, plus a nontrivial sharp sparse survival window with explicit constant \(11\). These features give concrete reliability consequences, well beyond a routine minimum-size or parameter renaming.
