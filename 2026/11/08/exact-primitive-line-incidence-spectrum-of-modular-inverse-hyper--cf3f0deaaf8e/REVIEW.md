# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof normalizes primitive affine lines without duplicates and reduces each intersection to unit roots of a quadratic congruence. The analysis distinguishes coefficients divisible by the prime, separable unit discriminants, and singular discriminants of every possible valuation, including \(k=1\) and \(k=2\). All counts are nonnegative and sum to the number of lines. The independent exhaustive script checks 292402 complete line incidences across 15 prime-power cases and checks the exact first moment; the infinite result rests on the proof and Hensel root counts, not on those finite tests.

## Originality — PASS (limited historical search)
Khan–Magner–Senger–Winterhof, arXiv:1304.6943, §1 Lemma 6/Proposition 7, already give the crucial quadratic-discriminant root counts and address Euclidean lines through integer representatives; those ingredients are credited. The exact distribution of intersection sizes over all *primitive modular affine lines*, the count of lines attaining each tangency depth, and the stable proportional distribution do not appear in the inspected text and are not numerically specified by its Euclidean-line bound. Ford–Khan–Shparlinski's 2010 paper and Shparlinski's 2011 survey address other geometric statistics. No equivalent census was located through targeted literature and mathematical-record searches. Its absence from these searches is not a proof of unique historical priority; an equivalent in finite-chain-ring geometry is a residual risk. The 2010 paper was not read in full, but its described topic differs.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural incidence distribution of a modular hyperbola is a complete combinatorial-geometric invariant: it distinguishes empty, simple, secant, and higher-order tangency lines, counts every type, and identifies sharp maximal collinearity within the ring-affine geometry. Unlike a single quadratic root count, the result enumerates *all distinct primitive lines*, incorporates unit restrictions, and yields stable level-independent incidence probabilities and a limiting empty-line probability. This is a substantive complete classification, not a recoding of finite numerical data. Its scope is deliberately odd prime powers; the dyadic and general composite cases are not addressed.
