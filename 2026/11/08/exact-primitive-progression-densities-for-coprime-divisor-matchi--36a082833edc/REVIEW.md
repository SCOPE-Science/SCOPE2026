# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

Alekseyev's complete progression criterion is explicitly cited, with the new residue count proved independently for fixed prime powers. Invertible common differences have exactly two possible multiple counts; zero differences modulo a prime create the successful residue pair sector. CRT and Möbius inversion give the required conditional primitive-pair density with a fixed-modulus logarithmic error. The monotonicity and prime-power phases follow algebraically. An independent exact-arithmetic bipartite-matching verifier checks 2703 finite examples. The infinite statement relies on the proof, not on those examples.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The closest complete source, Alekseyev's October 2026 paper, decides individual matches and proves the all-progressions criterion. It does not calculate the relative density of successful primitive starts and differences, its Euler factors, or divisibility monotonicity. McNew–Pomerance's density concerns matchable integers, a different random variable. Related existing studies of matching multiplicities ask a different counting question. The result is an explicit quantitative consequence of the published existence criterion, not a new criterion. An equivalent local-probability reformulation may nevertheless have appeared elsewhere.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Once strong matchability is no longer universal, the natural next invariant is the exact frequency of progressions that remain matchable, rather than the binary question of existence or universality. The theorem gives a complete all-integer answer and exhibits a nontrivial saturation transition: for prime powers the fraction decreases linearly over a finite exponent window, then stays at \(1/(p+1)\); it cannot vanish at any fixed integer, yet has infimum zero. Divisibility monotonicity supplies a structural comparison across all integers. This quantifies how robust divisor matchings are under primitive progression perturbations.

## Sources and limitations

Primary: Alekseyev, arXiv:2610.37512v1, Sections 1–6. Secondary: McNew and Pomerance, arXiv:2604.05304v2, Sections 1, 2, 6. Mathematical claims about matching existence depend on the primary theorem. The asymptotic error is not uniform in \(n\), and counts the existence rather than multiplicity of matchings. Historical priority remains open to independent evaluation.
