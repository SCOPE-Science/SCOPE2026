# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every connected wheel, a period-two state has equal two-step firing counts at all vertices, hence one firing at every vertex; a uniform count zero or two would give a fixed point. The elementary threshold test yields the complete, independent chip intervals for a prescribed phase partition. When the hub fires, the rim partitions are precisely cyclic binary words avoiding three consecutive A's and having at least one B. Each such word contributes a positive consecutive-mass interval \([3m-r,4m+r-1]\), where \(r\) is its number of isolated A-runs. Since \(r\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), this proves the exact support and extremal coefficients. Weighted cyclic transfer walks give the polynomial. The word intervals all share the same mass midpoint, proving reciprocity. The unweighted derivative and rational generating function follow from the four-by-four matrix determinant. The recorded programs were executed from actual standalone paths, with 649296 exhaustively tested finite states across \(m=3,4,5,6\) and full symbolic polynomial comparisons for \(3\le m\le12\). Finite checks supplement, not replace, the all-parameter proof. Risk: independent formal verification has not been performed; no computation substitutes for the algebraic argument.

## Originality — PASS (with historical priority limitations)

The exact domain is **initially primitive two-cycle states of sinkless parallel chip-firing on the labelled wheel, graded by total chip count**, including its sharp mass band and endpoint populations. Jiang (arXiv:1003.0943, Section 2) proves common firing-count and chip-complement identities and classifies period possibilities for complete bipartite/multipartite graphs. Jiang–Scully–Zhang (arXiv:1211.6786, Sections 2, 5–7) classify firing words, not wheel mass populations. Dall'Asta (arXiv:cond-mat/0601267) studies degree-two rings, not hub-and-spoke wheels. Selig (arXiv:2202.06487, especially Section 3) studies dissipative sink-based Abelian/stochastic sandpile recurrent states, not the sinkless parallel map. The established general cut enumerator for the **unweighted number** of primitive two-cycle states can recover \(H_m(1)\) when specialized, but gives neither the chip-mass polynomial nor its boundary-support theorem without an additional wheel-specific derivation. Targeted mathematical-result searches returned no same-object/same-invariant claim. Neither failure of search nor partitioning proves uniqueness. Full Bitar–Goles 1992 text was unavailable; its abstract and historical review provide no affirmative domination, but this is an explicit residual historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The conserved chip count is a natural organizing invariant in fixed-energy sandpile dynamics. The wheel is a canonical sparse graph created by coupling a ring to one universal hub. The complete chip-mass polynomial and the sharp parity-dependent endpoint populations describe how one hub qualitatively changes the primitive-two energy spectrum compared with a simple ring. The proof identifies the structural statistic governing the energy extremes: the number of isolated A-runs, rather than the ordinary number of firing vertices. This is a natural infinite-family classification, not an arbitrary fixed-size enumeration or an ungraded restatement of the general cut count. The result does not claim a downstream theory or attractor-basin classification.

## Nearest literature and limitations

The closest dynamical full texts are Jiang 2010 and Jiang–Scully–Zhang 2012; the closest wheel-graph counting full text is Selig 2022 but treats a different dissipative process. Dall'Asta 2006 provides the degree-two ring comparison. Bitar and Goles 1992 was checked by abstract and bibliographic content only; institutional full-text access was unavailable. Priority risk from unindexed older enumeration remains. Numerical checks cover finite sizes only. Scope excludes transients, fixed-point census, and periods other than two.
