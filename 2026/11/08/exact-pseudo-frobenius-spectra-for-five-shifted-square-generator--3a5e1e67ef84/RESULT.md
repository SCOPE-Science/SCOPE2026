# Exact pseudo-Frobenius spectra for five shifted-square generators

## Finding

For each integer \(a\ge34\), put
\[
S_a=\langle a,a+1,a+4,a+9,a+16\rangle.
\]
Let \(\ell(n)\) be the least number of summands drawn, with repetition, from \(\{1,4,9,16\}\) whose sum is \(n\), with \(\ell(0)=0\). If \(q\) is the residue of \(a\) modulo \(16\), let \(K_q\) be the set in the table below. The **entire pseudo-Frobenius set** is
\[
\operatorname{PF}(S_a)=
\big\{r+a(\ell(r)-1):r\in\{7,15\}\cup\{a-k:k\in K_q\}\big\}.
\]
Here a pseudo-Frobenius number is an integer \(x\notin S_a\) such that \(x+s\in S_a\) for every positive \(s\in S_a\). Thus the **type** is exactly \(t(S_a)=2+|K_q|\).

| \(q\) | \(K_q\) | \(t(S_a)\) |
|---:|:---|---:|
| 0 | \({1,2,4,9}\) | 6 |
| 1 | \({1,2,3,5,10}\) | 7 |
| 2 | \({1,3,4,6,11}\) | 7 |
| 3 | \({1,2,4,5,7,12}\) | 8 |
| 4 | \({1,3,5,6,8,13}\) | 8 |
| 5 | \({1,2,4,6,7,9,14}\) | 9 |
| 6 | \({1,3,7,8,10,15}\) | 8 |
| 7 | \({1,2,4,8,9,11,16}\) | 9 |
| 8 | \({1,3,9,12}\) | 6 |
| 9 | \({1,2,4,10,13}\) | 7 |
| 10 | \({1,2,3,5,11,14}\) | 8 |
| 11 | \({1,3,4,6,12,15}\) | 8 |
| 12 | \({1,2,4,5,7,13,16}\) | 9 |
| 13 | \({1,3,6,8,14}\) | 7 |
| 14 | \({1,2,4,7,9,15}\) | 8 |
| 15 | \({1,3,8,16}\) | 6 |

The type is periodic in \(a\) with **least period** \(16\), and takes precisely the values \(6,7,8,9\). The range begins sharply for this displayed spectrum rule: the same rule is false at \(a=33\), as it incorrectly includes \(65\), whereas \(65+(33+16)=114\notin S_{33}\). In particular, for \(a=34\), the pseudo-Frobenius numbers are \(\{101,109,117,125,130,132,133\}\).

## Assumptions and scope

All integer semigroups are additive submonoids of \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) containing zero. Every \(a\ge34\) gives a numerical semigroup because consecutive generators occur, and all five listed generators are minimal since \(a+16<2a\). No assertion is made for \(a<34\). The analysis concerns the finite square-offset set \(\{0,1,4,9,16\}\), not all square offsets.

## Proof

**1. Exact coin-length lemma.** The coin recursion
\[
\ell(n)=1+\min_{d\in\{1,4,9,16\},\ d\le n}\ell(n-d)\quad(n\ge1)
\]
with \(\ell(0)=0\) gives the two exact finite rows
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\ell(0),\ldots,\ell(15))&=(0,1,2,3,1,2,3,4,2,1,2,3,3,2,3,4),\\
(\ell(16),\ldots,\ell(31))&=(1,2,2,3,2,3,3,4,3,2,3,3,4,3,4,4).
\end{aligned}
\]
The same recursion determines \(\ell(32),\ldots,\ell(47)\); comparing against \(\ell(16),\ldots,\ell(31)\) yields \(\ell(n+16)=\ell(n)+1\) for \(16\le n\le31\). For \(n\ge32\), every \(n-d\ge16\) for \(d\in\{1,4,9,16\}\), so strong induction in the recursion extends that identity to **every** \(n\ge16\). (All finite rows are generated by the exact checker.) Define
\[
(\delta_0,\dots,\delta_{15})=(0,1,1,2,1,2,2,3,2,1,2,2,3,2,3,3).
\]
Thus, if \(n=16h+s\ge16\) and \(0\le s<16\), then \(\ell(n)=h+\delta_s\), and \(\ell(n)\ge\lceil n/16\rceil\). Moreover \(\ell(r)\le4\) for \(0\le r<16\), and \(\ell(16h+s)\le h+3\) for \(h\ge1\).

**2. Complete Apéry set for the stated range.** For \(0\le r<a\), let \(w(r)\) be the smallest member of \(S_a\) congruent to \(r\pmod a\). Any representation using the four shifted generators has total offset \(r+ma\) for some integer \(m\ge0\); after using \(\ell(r+ma)\) such generators, and allowing further copies of \(a\), its weight is at least
\[
r+a\bigl(m+\ell(r+ma)\bigr).
\]
The choice \(m=0\) is attainable with exactly \(\ell(r)\) offsets. To exclude any improvement, observe: if \(r<16\),
\[
m+\ell(r+ma)-\ell(r)\ge m+\lceil34m/16\rceil-4\ge0
\quad(m\ge1).
\]
If \(r=16h+s\ge16\),
\[
m+\ell(r+ma)-\ell(r)\ge m+\lceil34m/16\rceil-3>0.
\]
Consequently \(w(r)=r+a\ell(r)\) for all residues and all \(a\ge34\).

**3. Exact pseudo-Frobenius criterion.** Every pseudo-Frobenius number has the form \(w(r)-a\) for some \(1\le r<a\). Because the five generators generate \(S_a\), it is enough to test that adding each generator lands in \(S_a\). Adding \(a\) always works, and for an offset \(d\in\{1,4,9,16\}\), this is equivalent to
\[
\begin{cases}
\ell(r)\ge\ell(r+d),&r+d<a,\\
\ell(r)+1\ge\ell(r+d-a),&r+d\ge a.
\end{cases}
\tag{*}
\]
For \(1\le r\le15\), there is no wrap when \(a\ge34\). The two finite length rows from Step 1 show that precisely \(r=7,15\) satisfy all four inequalities. For \(16\le r\le a-17\), the \(d=16\) inequality fails by \(\ell(r+16)=\ell(r)+1\). It remains to analyze \(r=a-k\) for \(1\le k\le16\).

**4. An exhaustive symbolic 16-by-16 comparison.** For \(a=16m+q\), \(0\le q<16\), define the explicit integer-valued function
\[
\theta(q,k)=\left\lfloor\frac{q-k}{16}\right\rfloor
+\delta_{(q-k)\bmod16},\qquad 1\le k\le16.
\]
As \(a-k\ge18\), the no-wrap part of criterion (*) is exactly
\[
\theta(q,k)\ge\theta(q,k-d)
\quad\text{for every }d\in\{1,4,9,16\}\text{ with }d<k.
\tag{**}
\]
Substituting the displayed 16 entries of \(\delta\) into the finite inequalities (**) for all \(16\times16\) pairs \((q,k)\) produces **exactly** the sets \(K_q\) in the table. This is a finite, reproducible integer calculation, not a statistical inference; the packaged checker performs every comparison and asserts equality with the whole table.

It remains to check wrap inequalities, because (**) by itself is only necessary. Choose the unique \(a_0\in\{34,35,\ldots,49\}\) with \(a_0\equiv q\pmod{16}\). The same finite checker tests *every* pair \((a_0,k)\) and verifies that whenever (**) holds, all the required wrap inequalities
\[
\ell(a_0-k)+1\ge\ell(d-k)\qquad(d\in\{1,4,9,16\},\ d\ge k)
\]
hold. The proof reduces to exactly these 256 integer checks because for \(a=a_0+16t\), \(t\ge0\), all no-wrap comparisons are unchanged by the coin-length lemma, while each left side of a wrap inequality increases by \(t\) and its right side is fixed. A pair rejected by (**) is rejected for all such \(a\). Therefore the table supplies *all and only* qualifying residues. Combined with Steps 1–3 it proves the complete formula.

**5. Sharp cutoff and exact period.** At \(a=33\), the displayed table would include \(r=32\), hence \(w(32)-33=65\), but \(65+(33+16)=114\) lies outside the semigroup, since the genuine Apéry minimum in that residue is \(147\). This gives a direct obstruction to extending the formula to \(33\). The type table repeats every \(16\); its entries at residue \(0\) and \(4\) differ, ruling out period \(4\), and its entries at residues \(2\) and \(10\) differ, ruling out period \(8\). Hence no proper divisor of \(16\) is a period, proving minimality.

## Relationship to prior work

Liu and Xin (2022, arXiv:2210.02722) study the *same finite shifted-square generator family*, prove coin-length stability and a Frobenius-number formula. Their Section 4 does not classify the complete pseudo-Frobenius set or type. Liu, Xin, Ye and Yin (2023, arXiv:2304.09039) generalize Frobenius-number methods for shifted-offset families, again without the present pseudo-Frobenius table. Bogart, Goodrick and Woods (2019, arXiv:1911.09136) prove general eventual quasi-polynomial behavior of type, which is far broader qualitatively but does not supply the exact sets, 16-type table, or sharp beginning here. Stamate and Strazzanti (2026, arXiv:2601.19629) prove an eventual bijection of pseudo-Frobenius sets for arbitrary shifted numerical semigroups, with a general sufficient bound in their notation. For the present offsets their stated \(n>r_k^2\) hypothesis applies only once \(n>256\); it neither enumerates the 16 exact sets nor establishes the cutoff 34. The published general results are significant prior structure, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The theorem is for exactly five generators with offsets \(0,1,4,9,16\); it does not classify all square-offset lengths or smaller \(a\). The exhibited boundary is the failure of this exact spectrum law at 33, not a classification of every \(a<34\). Some full prior literature could have an equivalent unpublished or unindexed evaluation; checking accessible sources cannot certify absolute global novelty. A separate independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
