# Exact pure temporal modality spectrum on finite linear horizons

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\geq2\) and a complete lattice \(L\) with distinct bottom \(0\) and top \(1\). Write \(T_n=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\), in its natural strict linear order. On functions \(f:T_n\to L\) define strict-past and strict-future necessity by
\[
(Pf)(i)=\bigwedge_{j<i}f(j),\qquad
(Ff)(i)=\bigwedge_{j>i}f(j),
\]
where an empty meet is \(1\). Form the monoid of all composite operations generated by \(P,F\), including the identity. **The exact cardinality is**
\[
\boxed{\lvert\langle P,F\rangle^1\rvert=2(n-1)(2n-1).}
\]
Its first values for \(n=2,3,4,5,6,7\) are \(6,20,42,72,110,156\).

More precisely, the operations are uniquely represented by the following binary relations on \(T_n\): the identity relation; the empty relation; the \(2(n-2)\) nonrectangular strict-order powers; and all \(4(n-1)^2\) rectangles formed from a nonempty proper initial or final segment in each coordinate. Every operation is represented by a modal word of length at most \(2n-1\). The same cardinality holds for the pure nested past/future operators in the finite linear-time instance of the temporal semantics of Martins and Rodrigues (2026), using the common six-valued base lattice.

## Assumptions and scope

This is a classification of **unary operator words only**, such as \(PFP f\). It does not count formulas containing Boolean, many-valued or binary connectives, additional temporal operators, or arbitrary nonlinear or infinite time orders. The set of times is exactly \(T_n\), not an interval with additional exterior times. The lattice must be nontrivial; otherwise every operator is identical.

Use relational composition \(R\circ S=\{(i,j):\exists k\ ((i,k)\in R\land(k,j)\in S)\}\). Let \(p=\{(i,j):j<i\}\) and \(q=\{(i,j):j>i\}\). Associate to a relation \(R\) the meet transformer \((H_R f)(i)=\bigwedge_{j:(i,j)\in R}f(j)\). Then \(H_R\circ H_S=H_{R\circ S}\); repeated occurrences do not matter because lattice meets are idempotent. Thus words in \(P,F\) correspond to relation words in \(p,q\).

A proper initial segment of size \(a\), with \(1\leq a\leq n-1\), is \(I_a=\{0,\ldots,a-1\}\); a proper final segment of size \(a\) is \(J_a=\{n-a,\ldots,n-1\}\). A rectangle means one of \(I_a\times I_b\), \(I_a\times J_b\), \(J_a\times I_b\), \(J_a\times J_b\), where both sizes are between \(1\) and \(n-1\).

## Proof

**Step 1 (meet representation is faithful).** For all relations \(R,S\), the meet identity \(H_R H_S=H_{R\circ S}\) follows by expanding and reassociating finite meets. If \(R\ne S\), choose an edge \((i,j)\) in their symmetric difference, and take \(f(j)=0\) and \(f(k)=1\) for \(k\ne j\). The two outputs at \(i\) differ. Therefore distinct relations produce distinct operations over *every* nontrivial complete lattice, not merely over the two-element Boolean lattice.

**Step 2 (pure words).** For each positive integer \(a\), the relation \(p^a\) consists exactly of pairs \((i,j)\) with \(i-j\geq a\), because a strictly descending path of \(a\) edges exists precisely when those indices are separated by at least \(a\). Dually, \(q^a\) has \(j-i\geq a\). The \(n-1\) positive nonempty powers in each direction are distinct, and both \(p^n,q^n\) are empty. Only the extreme nonempty powers \(p^{n-1}=J_1\times I_1\) and \(q^{n-1}=I_1\times J_1\) are rectangles: all shorter nonempty powers have staircase boundaries rather than Cartesian-product boundaries.

**Step 3 (mixed words always become rectangles).** If \(a,b\geq1\), then
\[
p^a q^b=J_{n-a}\times J_{n-b},\qquad
q^a p^b=I_{n-a}\times I_{n-b}
\]
when the corresponding sizes are positive, and otherwise the relation is empty. Indeed a path from \(i\) first moving down by at least \(a\) and then up by at least \(b\) exists exactly when \(i\geq a\) and the endpoint \(j\geq b\): intermediate vertex \(0\) works; the reverse case uses \(n-1\). A word containing both directions has a first change of direction, so its relation becomes a rectangle. If \(R=A\times B\), then for either \(s\in\{p,q\}\),
\[
R\circ s=A\times s(B),\quad
p(B)=\{j:\exists k\in B\ (j<k)\},\quad
q(B)=\{j:\exists k\in B\ (j>k)\}.
\]
For every nonempty interval segment \(B\), \(p(B)\) is an initial segment (possibly empty), and \(q(B)\) a final segment (possibly empty). No such image is the entire chain. Consequently, once rectangular, the relation stays a proper prefix/suffix rectangle or becomes empty under further letters. This rules out all other relation types.

**Step 4 (all four rectangle orientations occur).** For any \(1\leq a,b\leq n-1\), the following four words realize every listed rectangle:
\[
\begin{aligned}
p^{n-a}q^{n-b}&=J_a\times J_b,\\
q^{n-a}p^{n-b}&=I_a\times I_b,\\
p^{n-a}q p^{n-b}&=J_a\times I_b,\\
q^{n-a}p q^{n-b}&=I_a\times J_b.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the third formula, for example, the first change gives \(J_a\times J_{n-1}\) and the last pure-past block sends that final segment to \(I_b\). These are all distinct because nonempty proper prefixes, nonempty proper suffixes, and their sizes are uniquely determined by the relation's row and column supports.

**Step 5 (count and depth).** There are \(4(n-1)^2\) distinct rectangles, \(2(n-2)\) further distinct nonrectangular nonempty pure powers, the empty relation, and the identity. The count is
\[
4(n-1)^2+2(n-2)+2=2(n-1)(2n-1).
\]
The displayed rectangle expressions have length at most \(2n-1\). The identity has length zero, the empty relation is \(p^n\), and each remaining pure power has length at most \(n-2\). This establishes the finite modal-word depth bound. Every statement holds for all \(n\geq2\), without an empirical extrapolation.

Run `python finite_tense_check.py` with Python 3 (standard library only). For each \(2\leq n\leq12\), the checker independently enumerates the closure of the two strict-order relations under Boolean composition, constructs the complete canonical family of rectangles and powers by direct set masks, and compares the two sets exactly. It prints the cardinalities \(6,20,42,72,110,156,210,272,342,420,506\). For \(2\leq n\leq6\), it additionally evaluates every resulting meet operator on all binary input functions and verifies all are pairwise distinct. The infinite-family proof is the five-step argument above, not the finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Martins and Rodrigues, *Merging and Evolution of Data Bases: A Many-Logic Approach* (arXiv:2610.40812v1, October 29, 2026), Definition 4.4, introduce strict past/future operations interpreted by infima along temporal flows. Their article treats merging, temporal semantics and information-quality measures, not this exact all-length algebra of pure past/future compositions on fixed finite linear horizons. The new theorem holds for all nontrivial complete lattices and therefore for their six-valued case.

Adsul, Gastin and Kulkarni (CONCUR 2025, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.CONCUR.2025.5) study expressive completeness and algebraic recognizability of temporal fragments with strict past/future. Their algebraic classifications concern languages of words or traces and do not directly enumerate the operators obtained from *only modal nesting* on an \(n\)-point chain. Standard relation composition, Boolean matrix multiplication, and the general theory of temporal logic remain prior mathematics; an older equivalent two-generator relation-semigroup classification under different terminology is a residual priority risk.

## Limitations

There is no assertion of novelty for general tense logic, general full relation semigroups, or fragments permitting extra Boolean connectives. The count requires a nontrivial common lattice and exactly \(n\) linearly ordered times; general partial temporal orders can have a different monoid. For a particular temporally evolving model, the restricted set of realizable input valuations may identify further operators, so the count is a universal semantics count (or an exact count when arbitrary common-lattice input streams are allowed). The argument does not provide an independently verified historical-priority certificate; independent audit remains outstanding.
