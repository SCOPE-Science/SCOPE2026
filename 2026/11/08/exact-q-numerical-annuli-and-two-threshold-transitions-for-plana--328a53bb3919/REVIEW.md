# Mathematical review: planar antiisometry \(q\)-ranges

## Correctness (PASS)
The statement quantifies over all nonzero scaled antiunitaries on complex two-dimensional Hilbert space and all overlap parameters in the closed unit disk. Writing the antilinear operator as \(UC\), the symmetric/skew split of the scaled-unitary matrix has scalar squared singular values because its skew part is a single alternating \(2\times2\) block. Takagi factorization proves that the ordinary numerical modulus takes every value between zero and \(a=w(A)\). In dimension two, the complementary component has modulus exactly \(\sqrt{R^2-s^2}\), so varying its phase reduces the two radii to one-dimensional minima and maxima. The analytical endpoint derivation proves both thresholds, including degenerate cases. The finite checker is supportive only; it does not replace these arguments.

## Originality (PASS, best-knowledge)
Majumdar–Müller (arXiv:2610.16823, Section 6) give the shape and an exceptional two-dimensional skew example, not the radii for the full scaled-antiunitary class. Kołaczek–Müller (2024, Section 2) concern ordinary antilinear numerical ranges; their symmetric/skew results are attributed, not claimed. Kołaczek–Müller (arXiv:2508.04347) study ordinary real-linear numerical ranges. The relevant published mathematical-result search entries on \(q\)-ranges concern complex-linear rank-one/Jordan operators and do not imply the present antiunitary formula. The Cambridge article of Jia–Liu (2025 online; 2026 print) was available only through its abstract; it concerns ordinary numerical ranges, leaving a publication-priority risk if its inaccessible full text contains a stronger disguised result.

## Value (PASS)
The initiating paper explicitly singles out dimension two as exceptional. This result gives a complete calculation for the natural dimension-two scaled-antiunitary class, rather than only a counterexample to disk shape. It identifies independently the hole-closing threshold and the norm-plateau threshold, supplies their precise dependence on intrinsic operator invariants, and reveals both possible threshold orderings. It adds quantitative structure to the dimension-two case of the new general framework, without claiming a general theorem for arbitrary antilinear maps.
