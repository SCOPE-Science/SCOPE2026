# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The derivation identity uniquely gives the block parameters \((A,\phi,\lambda)\); \(D^2=0\) forces \(\lambda=0\), \(A^2=0\), and \(\phi A=0\). For symplectic square-zero \(A\), the isotropic image \(W\) and a nonsingular symmetric form on \(V/W^\perp\) determine one another bijectively. This is verified by reconstructing the map and checking its image, kernel, and symplectic relation. Standard exact isotropic-subspace and nonsingular symmetric-matrix formulas count these maps. The central functional has precisely \(q^r\) choices preserving rank and \(q^{2m-r}-q^r\) increasing it by one. Finite enumeration independently checks all defining equations and rank distributions over several fields, including characteristic two and a degree-two extension field. The product bound yields summable domination and therefore full total-variation convergence, rather than a mere fixed-deficit heuristic.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
La Rosa and Mancini's arXiv:2301.11058 studies derivation algebras of two-step nilpotent algebras and their Heisenberg cases, principally over complex and real fields; no finite-field square-zero derivation rank census or corank limit is in its inspected full text. MacWilliams's symmetric-form count and the symplectic-isotropic-subspace formula are known inputs. Lewis et al., arXiv:1011.4539, Section 4 (especially equation (4.5)), establishes the former count rather than the full Heisenberg derivation problem. Related restricted-derivation existence and classical nilpotent-orbit results do not themselves supply the central-functional rank split. Checks of explicit, equivalent, and broader formulations identified no direct coverage, but cannot rule out every unlocated general theorem; publication priority is provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The rank spectrum of square-zero elements in a derivation algebra is a natural finite-field invariant relevant to infinitesimal symmetries and nilpotent-operator geometry. The full exact distribution exhibits a nontrivial interaction between a symplectic constraint and an independent central functional, and the limiting deficit retains positive mass in multiple ranks. The proof supplies a uniform, characteristic-independent structural explanation and an explicit asymptotic probability law, not just a small numerical table or a rename of a known symmetric-matrix formula.

## Limits and risk
The statement treats ordinary derivations, not restricted derivations or arbitrary nilpotent Lie algebras. MacWilliams's original 1969 full text was not available for direct inspection, although its formula was checked in the full accessible Lewis et al. article. Broader classical nilpotent-orbit treatments may subsume the symplectic part but do not obviously settle the displayed derivation rank count. Independent publication-priority assessment remains necessary.
