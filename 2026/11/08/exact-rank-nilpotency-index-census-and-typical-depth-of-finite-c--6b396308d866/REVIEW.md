# Scientific assessment: rank–index census of finite-chain partial-isometry nilpotents

## Correctness — PASS

The exact object is the inverse monoid of all partial bijections preserving the ordinary metric on a finite integer chain; its zero is the empty map. All nonempty nilpotents have no periodic components. The proof first treats maps of rank at least two as uniquely oriented translations or reflections, then counts rank-one maps only as nonzero translations. This controls the primary duplicate and exceptional cases. For translations, powers are governed by selected consecutive arrows on residue-class paths; avoiding a path of \(k\) edges is equivalent to the vanishing of the \(k\)-th power. Reflection restrictions have square zero exactly when no reflected pair is doubly selected and no fixed point is selected. These facts yield the full rank–index polynomial with no uncounted cases.

The rank and total specializations are algebraic consequences of the joint formula, with the full monoid order taken from established literature. The asymptotic proof uses the geometric limit for displacement, the negligible reflection component, and upper/lower longest-run bounds for independent indicators in one sampled map. The standalone verifier independently builds and composes all actual partial maps through length nine and checks all rank/index cells, all closed rank totals, and monoid sizes. It passes. Finite verification is corroboration only; the proof covers every positive chain size and the stated asymptotic limit.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

Kehinde and Umar, arXiv:1101.0049v1, establish finite-chain partial-isometry monoid structure, rank and fixed-point distributions and full cardinalities; the exact nilpotency-index statistics are not among the statements in the inspected preprint. In particular, a fixed-point-free reflection can still contain a two-cycle, so their fixed-point-free table cannot be substituted for the nilpotent census. Fernandes and Quinteiro, arXiv:1502.04829v1, give monoid presentations rather than the claimed bivariate enumerator. Ali, Jada and Zubairu, arXiv:2206.15299v1, study minimal generating-set ranks in larger contraction monoids, a different invariant. An earlier Ali–Jada–Zubairu article studies nilpotent-generated subsemigroups of the order-preserving partial-isometry submonoid; its complete text was not accessible, leaving a concrete residual priority risk. Searches of related research records and exact numerical sequences did not locate equivalent joint formulas, which alone does not establish novelty. The precise contribution is the mixed-orientation rank/index refinement and its probability law, not the known monoid geometry or total monoid count.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Domain rank and nilpotency depth are basic, distinct invariants of elements of a finite inverse monoid. Their exact joint distribution simultaneously refines finite-chain structural enumeration and detects how nilpotency can take more than two compositions. The reflection sector is sharply separated from the translation sector; this produces the exact parity-dependent nilpotent total, asymptotic density \(1/3\), and logarithmic typical nilpotency index. Unlike a small hand-picked parameter slice, the formulas cover every rank and nilpotency index in every chain size. These are meaningful invariants for comparing finite inverse-semigroup families and studying random partial transformations. The logarithmic index scale is related to but is not implied by earlier results for the larger monoid of total order-preserving contractions.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest same-object source is Kehinde–Umar (arXiv:1101.0049v1, 2010), especially its cardinality and fixed-point tables. Fernandes–Quinteiro (arXiv:1502.04829v1) and Ali–Jada–Zubairu (arXiv:2206.15299v1, 2022) establish related monoid structure and generating-rank results, respectively. The 2018 Ali–Jada–Zubairu journal article on nilpotents in the order-preserving isometry submonoid could not be fully examined; the abstract emphasizes generated-subsemigroup ranks, but its contents might overlap with some count specializations. Broader classical renewal/word-enumeration theorems also provide potential prior implications for the longest-run portion. Accordingly publication priority is provisional, especially for isolated univariate specializations. The stated contribution is the complete mixed-orientation joint formula with a self-contained derivation. No independent review is implied.
