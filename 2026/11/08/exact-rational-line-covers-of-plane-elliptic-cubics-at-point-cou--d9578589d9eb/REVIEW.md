# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The degree-three intersection bound forces any optimal \(N/3\)-line cover to partition all rational points into disjoint triples summing to the elliptic identity. A finite abelian group's total sum is its unique involution when such an element exists, so the obstruction is strict. The zero-sum partition theorem supplies exact covers otherwise. In the exceptional case, a quotient of order three and a near transversal of the kernel's Cayley table produce \(N/3-1\) disjoint zero-sum triples, leaving only three points; two additional rational lines cover these. Both constructions and quantifiers have been checked, including small groups and fields of arbitrary characteristic. The six finite geometry calculations and 500 cyclic constructions corroborate the proof; they do not substitute for its general group-theoretic inputs.

## Originality

PASS subject to historical-priority risk. The closest 2026 line-covering article obtains a qualitative \(N/3+O(1)\) estimate for odd-order elliptic groups and does not state the exact all-field involution dichotomy. The 2018 partition theorem determines when an exact triple partition exists, but does not address the sharp one-extra-line geometric cover in the exceptional case. The 2019 near-transversal theorem supplies an independent ingredient, not the plane-cubic covering result. Comparison considered equivalent zero-sum hypergraph formulations and stronger group-theoretic statements. A residual risk is that the combined covering classification might have appeared previously as an unstated or explicitly published consequence elsewhere.

## Value

PASS. This is an exact existence and obstruction classification for a natural finite-field curve invariant across an unbounded family, sharpening the motivating article's asymptotic discussion. The exceptional groups are separated by a concrete one-line gap from the Bézout limit, and the proof supplies an optimal cover in every such group rather than just a nonexistence obstruction.

## Boundaries

The theorem requires an \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational flex and \(3\mid|E(\mathbb F_q)|\); neither the nondivisible point-count case nor all optimal-cover classifications are addressed.
