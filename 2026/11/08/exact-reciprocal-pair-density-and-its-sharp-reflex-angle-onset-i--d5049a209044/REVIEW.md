# Scientific review: reciprocal pairs in reflex-sector nearest-neighbor graphs

## Correctness

PASS. A two-point Palm/Mecke calculation gives the exact union-void integral without overcounting: a vertex has one nearest eligible destination, and reciprocal eligibility requires opposite directions in the same sector. The reflex angular overlap consists of exactly two arcs; symmetry reduces it to the claimed one-interval quadrature. The limit aperture \(\pi\) is verified by the circular-lens area \(2\pi/3-\sqrt3/2\). Local truncation at distance \(T\) has dependence radius \(2T\), finite-range covariance of order area, an exact \(e^{-\alpha T^2/2}\) discarded-event bound, and negligible boundary strip, proving the empirical limit. Forty finite numerical diagnostics replayed successfully; they are not relied on for the infinite claims.

## Originality

PASS under best-knowledge literature comparison, with residual historical-priority risk. The motivating paper's full text expressly leaves reflex-sector apertures outside its analysis, and its observable is total edge length rather than directed two-cycles. Rohrmann–Zurbriggen's inspected full paper recovers the already-classical unrestricted reciprocal-neighbor fraction, without a direction-sector parameter. Cox's accessible abstract concerns ordinary planar nearest-neighbor ranks; Penrose–Wade's accessible abstract concerns general stabilization and normal approximation, not this sharp reciprocal-angle coefficient. Targeted searches for mutual/reverse directional nearest neighbors, sector angles, Palm pair counts and equivalent directed two-cycle densities did not identify the exact reflex-sector formula or its onset coefficient. General Poisson void-probability machinery alone is not a published evaluation of this new parameter-dependent invariant.

## Value

PASS. The motivating work singles out reflex apertures as an unexplored structural regime. Reciprocity is an elementary, natural graph invariant, identically absent in the original directed-forest regime but present with positive bulk density after the angle threshold. An exact thermodynamic limit and nonzero first-order onset coefficient quantify a genuine directed two-cycle phase transition. The exact restricted-angle probability is not supplied by the classical full-plane mutual-pair constant.

## Closest literature and remaining limits

The full-plane endpoint \(p(2\pi)\) is previously known and is explicitly credited; it is not claimed as new. The finding is confined to homogeneous Poisson inputs and fixed apertures, and does not settle the motivating paper's reflex-aperture edge-length fluctuation problem. General local-score laws of large numbers are standard tools; novelty is asserted only for the exact aperture-dependent reciprocity law and coefficient. Historical-priority comparisons of old paid or inaccessible articles may be incomplete; independent review has not been conducted.
