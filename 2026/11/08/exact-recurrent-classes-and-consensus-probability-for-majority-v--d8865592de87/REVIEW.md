# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim has a sample-path proof for every integer \(k\ge2\). A leaf pair that becomes unanimous is immutable under any future three-person majority update. Every initially mixed pair becomes unanimous at its first independent Poisson event, so the process reaches the permanent-pair sector almost surely. In that sector, each mixed-color fixed-pair partition has exactly a two-hub-state closed class, and each uniform partition reaches one consensus singleton. The precise exponential race gives conditional consensus probability \(a/(a+m)\) or \(b/(b+m)\) when the initial hub opposes the only color initially locked in leaf pairs; the other cases follow by permanent-pair invariance. Direct summation with Bernoulli initial colors gives the claimed closed expression. Independent exact-rational finite-state checks pass all 2,835 certificates for one to five petals. These tests are confirmatory, not the infinite-family proof.

Searches for loose hyperstar, sunflower, petal-star majority, polarized recurrent classes, binomial consensus probabilities, and stronger related results did not reveal an exact covering source. ## Mathematical value — PASS
The hypergraph is a canonical connected sparse structure with maximum overlap at one central individual. The theorem completely identifies a non-consensus mechanism for *simultaneous* group-majority voting, determines the exact law under arbitrary starting conditions and iid starts, and proves consensus becomes exponentially unlikely with more petals. It gives a natural rigorous test for hypergraph mean-field approximations and for statements claiming connectivity promotes agreement. This is not simply a finite table or an arbitrary computational slice.

## Limits and independent review
The result requires a fixed three-uniform loose hyperstar, simultaneous adoption by every member of the selected group, and independent equal-rate hyperedge Poisson clocks. It does not assert corresponding claims for rewired hypergraphs, single-node updates, larger groups or generic overlapping networks.
