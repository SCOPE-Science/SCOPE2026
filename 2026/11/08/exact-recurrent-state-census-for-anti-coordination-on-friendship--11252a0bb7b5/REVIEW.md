# Scientific assessment

**Correctness.** The four-state peripheral-edge update is idempotent at a fixed hub color. A fixed configuration excludes an edge monochromatic in the hub color; the hub then sees at least half opposite neighbors. For a primitive two-cycle the hub must switch each step. Exactly three edge patterns return under two alternating hub colors, and at least one currently hub-matching monochromatic edge enforces both strict hub changes. Consequently the labelled initial fixed-state and primitive two-state counts for every integer \(m\ge1\) are \(2\cdot3^m\) and \(2(3^m-2^m)\), respectively. Complete independent bit-vector enumeration through \(m=7\) corroborates the all-parameter proof.

**Comparison with existing results.** Classical symmetric-threshold theorems establish eventual period at most two but do not count immediate recurrent states. Turau's minority and majority classifications apply to trees, not the triangle-containing friendship family. A published asynchronous closed-neighborhood minority theorem for friendship graphs has a recurrent class of size \(2\cdot3^m\), matching one numerical fixed-state cardinality here under a different dynamics. That shared cardinality is not claimed as a separately new invariant. The distinct synchronous primitive-two classification and its hub-switching mechanism were not found in the inspected primary literature or related graph records; historical priority remains qualified.

**Mathematical significance.** The exact infinite-family census of fixed states and immediate primitive two-orbits provides a natural structural benchmark for coupled triangular anti-coordination dynamics. It describes initial recurrence only, not transient basins, stabilization times or arbitrary network dynamics.

**Limits.** The general proof, not finite enumeration, establishes the formulas. External expert attestation and machine-checked formalization are not asserted. Full text of the 1980 threshold-period paper was not accessible, and unindexed equivalent claims remain possible.
