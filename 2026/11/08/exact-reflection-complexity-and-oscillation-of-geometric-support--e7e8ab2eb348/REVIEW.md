# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** All geometric bases \(B\ge2\) and factor lengths \(n\ge0\) are quantified. The proof classifies every multi-marked factor by its unique first gap \((B-1)B^j\), enumerates all admissible starting positions, and counts exactly the two-mark reversal collisions. The exceptional pair at \((B,n)=(2,3)\) and the short-length boundary cases are treated explicitly. Direct exhaustive finite-prefix enumeration against the exact expression passed 1,350 base-length cases; another 99,997 base-two checks and 18 large-scale asymptotic diagnostics passed. The infinite conclusions follow from the written proof, not the test log.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The 2024 primary paper [1] defines reflection complexity, proves computable regularity for automatic sequences and evaluates named classical examples. Examination of its definitions, general implications, automatic-sequence theorem and examples found no corresponding explicit calculation for geometric support. The 2026 general follow-up [2] proves minimum-growth characterizations rather than the sharp formula and nonconvergent base-dependent normalized spectra here. Semantic record searches for the same geometric-support object, reversal-class equivalence and related formulas returned no statement that implies the proposed one. An equivalent unindexed publication remains a residual risk; searches are not proof of novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The invariant is a natural factor-complexity statistic introduced for automatic sequences. This gives its exact value at every length for an infinite family of standard base-recognizable words, including a base-two cancellation to \(2\lceil n/2\rceil+2\), and a sharp distinction between convergence at base two and a nontrivial full interval of accumulation values at each larger base. The count is derived from structural combinatorics of all factors, not a parameter-substitution, finite table or routine isolated bound.

**Closest literature and limitations.** [1] Allouche et al., *The reflection complexity of sequences over finite alphabets*, arXiv:2406.09302 (first public June 13, 2024), and [2] Dvořáková–Pelantová, *Reflection on the Reflection Complexity*, DOI 10.1007/s00224-026-10278-7 (2026). A further 2026 preprint about group-generalized reflection complexity was available in abstract only during comparison. This is not an independent audit and not a classification of all automatic sequences.
