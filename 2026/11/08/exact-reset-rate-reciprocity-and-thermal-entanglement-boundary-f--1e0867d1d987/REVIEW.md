# Assessment of the exact global-reset entanglement phase diagram

## Correctness: PASS
The hypotheses specify a trace-preserving completely positive reset, coherent pair-conversion Hamiltonian, and separate local phase-flip Lindblad terms. For every positive reset rate the renewal resolvent yields a unique stationary state. The two-dimensional even-parity subsystem reduces exactly to a stationary population imbalance and one imaginary coherence; the odd populations remain fixed at the reset values. Applying the Wootters spin-flip formula to this positive X-state gives the exact concurrence. The derivative factors as a strictly positive prefactor times \(4J^2-r^2\), and the positivity condition is a quadratic in the reset rate with root product \(4J^2\). The two exceptional parameter cases (no spectator weight and no preparation imbalance), threshold equality, and the pure-state universal-bound equality are stated explicitly. The symmetric product Gibbs-preparation cutoff follows from a single strictly monotone inequality on \(s\in[0,1/2]\).

A standalone checker corroborates the proof with exact symbolic identities and independent full 16-by-16 master-equation solves followed by spin-flip eigenvalue concurrence, across boundary and nonboundary preparations, with 273 successful assertions. Finite tests do not establish the universal theorem without the accompanying algebraic proof.

## Originality: PASS, with bounded publication-priority risk
Kulkarni and Majumdar (arXiv:2307.07485, sections II, III.3 and IV) already derived the general Poisson-reset density-matrix framework and analyzed concurrence for a *pure-reset transverse-field Ising pair*. Rose et al. (arXiv:1806.01298, sections III.2 and V.1) already derived stationary spectral/resolvent formulas for general quantum reset processes, so the renewal representation and its direct stationary-state specialization are not claimed as new general results. Hartmann, Dür and Briegel (arXiv:quant-ph/0703138, sections 2.3–2.4 and Appendix B) already established entanglement with *individually resetting qubits* in dissipative systems, including the qualitative creation and disappearance of entanglement when imperfect mixed reset states are used. The existence of a finite-rate entangled window in some models is not claimed as a new general phenomenon. Murauer et al. (arXiv:2610.40638, sections II–IV) use discrete-time pure-state reset protocols, which have different stationary phase-resonance geometry.

The distinct assessed statement is the **complete exact phase diagram** for coherent pair conversion with *joint* continuous-time Poisson resetting to arbitrary diagonal separable preparations and simultaneous independent local dephasing. In particular the sharp thermal-preparation cutoff, dephasing-independent exact optimizer \(2J\), and reciprocal pair of entanglement endpoints \(r_-r_+=4J^2\) were not found in the inspected relevant primary texts or the searched scientific records. The general stationary resolvent is stronger as a representation theorem, but does not itself state the concurrence sign geometry or the global optimization result; those require the specific invariant-block reduction and maximization. Publication-priority risk remains because an unindexed equivalent specialization of the resolvent, an XY-model calculation, or an undocumented supplement could imply the result.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The preparation mixture and local phase noise are natural experimental imperfections in a two-qubit entanglement-generation protocol. A qualitative claim that some intermediate reset rate is useful does not specify if entanglement is possible at a given preparation temperature, its exact rate window, or the optimal reset rate when dephasing changes. The theorem answers all these questions sharply for an analytically tractable but physically nontrivial Markovian pair, and proves the structurally informative reciprocal-rate identity and the exact \(1/2\) concurrence ceiling. The finite-temperature threshold is a genuine all-rate separability-versus-entanglement distinction, not an arbitrary parameter enumeration. The mathematical contribution is deliberately model-specific; neither a general quantum-channel phase diagram nor a many-body result is inferred.

## Scientific limitations and priority comparisons
The result concerns **joint** reset of both qubits, not individual local resets; unitary pair conversion without single-qubit detuning, and only phase-flip decoherence. Its preparation Gibbs temperature is not the equilibrium temperature of the coupled Hamiltonian. The explicit stationary matrix follows from the established renewal framework; only the full concurrence classification and sharp optimization are asserted as contributions. Published comparisons were performed at the level of generators, preparation states, observables and parameter quantifiers, rather than title matching. No independent publication-priority certification, experimental validation or independent scientific audit has been performed.
