# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The identity for the first-return distribution is proved for the exact set \(H_\ell\), not a surrogate inducing interval. Symmetry, the monotone full branches and the identity \(\phi_\ell(1-c_\ell)=1\) rule out skipped returns. The inverse recurrence gives \(w_m-w_{m-1}=\ell-\ell(1+\ell)/(2w_m)+O_\ell(w_m^{-2})\). Establishing \(w_m=\ell m+O(\log m)\) before summation justifies the harmonic correction. The telescoped difference for the renormalized \(w_m\) is summable, giving the genuine constant \(K_\ell\), its remainder, and the three-term survival formula. All initial-set, boundary, exponent and normalization conditions are explicit. The standalone code independently tests the threshold geometry and critical coefficients; it is supporting evidence, not the proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

The closest primary statement is Coates–Luzzatto–Muhammad, arXiv:2209.12725, Proposition 2.6 and Section 4.1, giving leading tails with errors at any relative power strictly below one, and Proposition 3.5 giving only leading preimage decay. The published analysis does not identify the explicit symmetric central-Lebesgue return law nor its relative \((\log n)/n\) and \(1/n\) coefficients. Bansard-Tresse–Freitas, arXiv:2303.17320, Section 4, states the corresponding leading induced tail; it does not assert the present three-term expression. Muhammad–Schindler, arXiv:2211.15648, Section 4, treats regularly varying induced tails and measure-theoretic consequences rather than this second-order exact formula. Search results on ordinary parabolic Fatou coordinates and related first-return asymptotics did not reveal a statement subsuming the normalized central-section law. A broadly applicable, unindexed parabolic/renewal expansion remains the principal priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Intermittent maps are distinguished by their long excursions and power-law returns. The coefficients of the first deviation from the leading power determine precision of finite-time survival estimates in the natural crossing section. The logarithmic coefficient is strictly positive and determined by the neutral exponent across a continuous family; the next constant is well-defined by a convergent limit. The theorem also supplies the exact all-integer tail, so it is not an arbitrary numerical slice. It does not purport to prove new mixing rates or a general theorem for asymmetric maps.
