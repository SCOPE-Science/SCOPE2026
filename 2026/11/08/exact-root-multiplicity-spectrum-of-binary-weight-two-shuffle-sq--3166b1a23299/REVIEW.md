# Research review

## Correctness — PASS
The root characterization follows from exhaustive necessary-and-sufficient allocation inequalities for a single-one root, including zero-length gaps. Pairwise comparison of its two lower and three upper bounds yields four precise gap conditions for at least \(r\) roots. Simultaneously reducing all three gaps by \(2(r-1)\) gives a bijection to the one-root existence set of smaller half-length, whose exact count is evaluated by a finite arithmetic sum for arbitrary half-length. The tail law, maximum, mass distribution and weak limit follow without extrapolation from experiments. An independent interleaving DP verifies the finite cases up to half-length 11, and arithmetic enumeration independently checks all tails through half-length 64.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Grytczuk–Pawlik–Pleszczyński (2023), Theorem 3 and Corollary 1, already prove the existence criterion and the \(r=1\) aggregate \(1+3\binom m2\), which are expressly excluded from novelty. Datko–Pawlik (2025), Sections 2–3, investigate numbers of roots in general, count descendants of a fixed one-one root, and give selected unique-root families; their inspected material does not contain the exact complete conditional multiplicity counts for every length in the two-one class. Grytczuk–Pawlik–Ruciński (2025), Propositions 1–3 and 6, address recognition by ordered graphs, not this root-fiber distribution. Indexed-research, equivalence and accessible-history comparisons did not reveal a covering statement. Possible unindexed/implicit historical equivalences remain the main originality risk.

## Value — PASS
The precise sizes of the fibers of the map from root words to shuffle-square words are a natural inverse problem, explicitly investigated by Datko–Pawlik. Unlike the known total count or a bounded table, the result identifies *all* distinct-root multiplicities at every length and gives a nondegenerate limit distribution. It resolves a structural all-length restricted family without claiming unrestricted enumeration or general binary root count formulas.

## Scope and residual risks
The domain consists only of binary words with exactly two ones, including non-shuffle-square words in the probability model. Distinct self-shuffle roots, not colored interleavings, are counted. Priority cannot be guaranteed in unindexed historical literature. Published open-access full text and indexed sections were inspected as available; direct publisher opening of the 2025 root paper returned a rate-limit response, although its indexed sections were readable. Independent validation is pending.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
