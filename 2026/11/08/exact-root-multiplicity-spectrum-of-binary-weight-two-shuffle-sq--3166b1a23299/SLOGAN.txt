For binary words with exactly two ones, the number of distinct self-shuffle roots has an explicit interval formula and a complete all-length multiplicity spectrum: the number of words with at least \(r\) roots is \(1+3\binom{m-3(r-1)}2\) whenever \(m\ge3r-2\), and zero otherwise. After normalization by half-length, the root count converges to a law with mass \(1/4\) at zero and density \(\frac92(1-3t)\) for \(0<t<1/3\); the previously known existence count is its \(r=1\) case.
