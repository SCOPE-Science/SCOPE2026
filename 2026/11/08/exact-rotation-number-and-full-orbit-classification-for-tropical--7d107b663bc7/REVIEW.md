# Scientific review: exact rotation law for tropical rank-two mutations

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is formulated on the full plane for positive \(p,q\) with \(pq<4\), and makes no claims at or above the nonelliptic threshold. The two elementary reflection-shear factors of the mutation are globally invertible and have determinant \(-1\) on every linear branch, so their composition is homogeneous, orientation preserving and area preserving. The four sign cases show directly that the previously known piecewise quadratic integral is exactly invariant, including all switching boundaries by continuity. Its two positive-definite quadratic pieces yield Jordan level ovals. Normalizing the coordinates makes the sector-area calculation a sum of three identical positive-cross-term integrals and one negative-cross-term integral. Both elementary integrals are evaluated explicitly with \(\kappa=2\cos\theta\).

The decisive step is geometric rather than numerical: a positive-homogeneous area-preserving homeomorphism that maps every oval to itself sends a radial sector bijectively to a radial sector of the same area. The sector-area coordinate therefore produces a rigid circle rotation. The image of the positive horizontal ray remains in the lower half-plane, and its image under a specified oriented linear coordinate change is exactly a rotation through \(-2\theta\), fixing the increment \(2\theta/(\pi+2\theta)\). The rational denominator computation is exact, and irrational density and unique ergodicity follow from classical rigid-rotation dynamics. The verification script completed 22,756 counted exact-arithmetic and numerical checks; the proof of the universal statement is independent of those finite tests.

## Originality — PASS within checked literature

Machacek–Ovenhouse [1] explicitly introduces this very mutation, proves the quadratic integral in Theorem 3.5, and gives a periodicity theorem for \(pq=4\cos^2(\pi/m)\) with integer \(m\ge3\) in Theorem 3.12. This has been credited and not recast as new. The present claim gives the **explicit rotation fraction for every real elliptic parameter** and derives a complete irrational/rational orbit classification; e.g. the period-11 case \(\theta=2\pi/7\) is not implied by their integer-reciprocal-angle theorem. The original full-text proof and its periodicity theorem were directly inspected, including its later discussion and stated questions. Related full-text rotation papers [2,3] concern different piecewise planar maps and do not imply an exact rotation number for this map.

Direct searches for the map, its parameters, equivalent sector-area conjugacy, complete circle rotation and higher-numerator rational periods returned no matching prior statement in the accessible published research records. The negative search evidence is not a uniqueness proof. Broader, unindexed and future publications may contain an independent equivalent computation. General facts about sector-area coordinates and irrational circle rotations are classical and are explicitly excluded from the novelty assertion.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The invariant level curves and a sparse integer-denominator periodicity family were already mathematically motivated by the published integrable mutation system. Its complete rotation number is the natural missing invariant: it determines every orbit for the entire elliptic two-parameter regime, distinguishes all rational from all irrational cases, provides exact least periods in parameter families not covered by the original theorem, identifies the unique ergodic distribution on each nonzero oval, and gives an exact enclosed-area formula. Those conclusions provide usable quantitative information about how real-valued mutation interpolates between finite-type behavior and quasiperiodic dynamics. It is not an arbitrary small subcase, a renamed known statement, or merely a numerical census.

This work is limited to the tropical map for \(0<pq<4\). It does not claim integrability of the nontropical map, new results on \(pq\ge4\), or a global planar ergodicity assertion. The original conserved quadratic, generic homeomorphism-to-circle mechanism, special periodic family, and classical circle-rotation theory remain prior work.
