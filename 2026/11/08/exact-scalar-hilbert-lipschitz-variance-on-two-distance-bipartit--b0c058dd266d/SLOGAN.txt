For every snowflaked complete-bipartite metric with cross distance \(1\), within-part distance \(t\in[1,2]\), and uniform measure, the sharp scalar Lipschitz variance reduces to a finite two-integer lattice maximum, while the Hilbert sharp variance has an explicit two-threshold formula. The target gap is strict before \(t=2\), vanishes at \(t=2\) exactly when the larger part is even, and has a unique computable maximum at the Hilbert-isometry threshold for balanced even parts.
