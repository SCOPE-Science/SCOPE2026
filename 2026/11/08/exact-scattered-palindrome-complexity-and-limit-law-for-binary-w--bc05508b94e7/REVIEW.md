# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The four possibilities for the number of ones in a scattered palindrome give a disjoint exhaustive partition; for the two-one class, the only overlap issue is resolved by three explicit integer rectangles and a case split on the outer gap order. Uniform-composition convergence gives the simplex law. Independent exponential normalization and exact rational integration give both limiting moments. The standalone verifier also compares with independently generated subsequence sets for all 3,876 length-at-most-18 weight-three words. The proof, not the finite tests, establishes the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with stated risk)
The closest direct predecessor, Holub–Saari (2009), concerns recognition of a binary word from the *set* of its scattered palindromes and longest subsequences, not cardinality conditioned on exactly three ones. Mahalingam–Pandoh (2021), explicitly inspected through its extremal sections, treats general scattered-palindrome upper bounds, alphabet-size-defined maxima and finite tables, not the exact three-marker gap law or Dirichlet limit. Rubinchik–Shur (2015) treats contiguous palindromic factors under iid sampling. The available published-index and own-claim searches identified related problems, but no statement that entails both the formula and moment law. An equivalent unindexed specialization remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The result solves the exact finite-word cardinality for an infinite natural fixed-content class and identifies the order-\(n^2\) fluctuations of a distinct-scattered-subsequence statistic. Its explicit nonzero limit variance makes the difference from deterministic first-order approximation substantive. The four-gap formula makes collision corrections transparent and is reusable for fixed-composition subsequence calculations.

## Scientific limitations
No claim is made about larger Hamming weight, the exact finite-size extremum, a closed-form limit density, or external independent validation. Unlike contiguous-factor counting, scattered subsequences can exhibit quadratic rather than square-root scale here.
