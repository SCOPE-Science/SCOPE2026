The geometric polynomial identity is exact. For negative coefficients a primitive companion matrix and its unique Perron root give a sharp lower boundary. For positive coefficients, a strict reverse-triangle bound excludes every possible unit-circle crossing before the explicit upper boundary. Sample exact-arithmetic and numerical checks are supporting evidence, not the universal proof.

## Originality: PASS with publication-priority risk
The 2023 preprint and 2024 journal paper formulate the all-odd-age high-survival flip criterion as a conjecture, after proving a lower-survival sufficient range; the 2012 and 2017 papers announce or numerically support the qualitative odd-age pattern. The present claim does not reclaim those prior observations: it proves the sharp complete Schur interval for every real polynomial coefficient and all odd degrees, explicitly excluding earlier complex crossings and distinguishing finite versus absent biological thresholds. Searches of mathematical research records and primary literature did not reveal a previously supplied all-degree proof for this particular family. A general historical Schur criterion may subsume the statement; this remains a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value: PASS
This gives a concise uniform analytical solution to a named open stability-comparison question, replacing numerical checks and sufficient conditions at high survival with exact necessary-and-sufficient conditions and a transversal multiplier computation. The threshold also distinguishes recruitment laws for which a positive equilibrium can never lose linear stability at finite population size, across every odd age-class dimension. These distinctions are intrinsic to the published biological model, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Scope and limitations
No general nonlinear supercritical flip or period-two existence is claimed, and the case of survival probability exactly one is excluded. The result is local and applies to equal fecundity and equal survival parameters, not arbitrary Leslie matrices. Earlier qualitative assertions are credited, not reassigned.
