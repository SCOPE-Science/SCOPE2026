# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every fixed alphabet size \(q\ge2\), the proof explicitly identifies the independent Bernoulli transition pattern, reconstructs the established Fibonacci run-product formula, and applies iid renewal cycles with a geometrically tight final tail. The exact centering \(\mu_q\) and normalization \(\sigma_q^2\) follow by the renewal-reward strong law and random-index central limit theorem, and the latter is strictly positive. For the all-length second moment, ordered pairs of feasible matchings lead to the stated three-state matrix; its characteristic polynomial and the exact sign identity \(P_p(\lambda_q^2)=\lambda_q p(p-1)<0\) establish strict Perron separation. Direct-output enumeration and exact rational moment comparison passed \(59{,}557\) source instances. That finite replay is supporting evidence, not a substitute for the proof.

It therefore covers the mean, but not an expectation of the **square** of the ball size, not the exact paired-matching transfer matrix, nor the strict spectral separation of that new moment. Their later arXiv:2607.19566 addresses coding and covering, not a comparable source-random dispersion theorem in the accessible material; its complete source was not accessible for a comprehensive check. The general disordered monomer–dimer CLT of Lam–Sen is closely related and may subsume the *qualitative* normal-law mechanism in broader probabilistic models; that generic conclusion is not the claimed channel-specific novelty. The exact second-moment identity and strict relative-variance exponent are the decisive claimed additions. Relevant research-record queries produced no known implication covering that package. Historical priority and unindexed or inaccessible source risks remain.

## Value — PASS

The recent coding literature uses center-specific balls and their average sizes for packing and rate arguments. This result proves that sphere volumes have exponentially increasing variance relative to the squared mean, identifies the exact spectral inflation factor, and computes the logarithmic source-to-source fluctuation variance using convergent one-dimensional series. It supplies an exact finite-length benchmark for stochastic channel-volume estimates and isolates a structural mismatch between annealed and typical ambiguity. This goes beyond reindexing an existing mean formula or a finite table; the strict spectral gap is an all-length phenomenon.
