# Exact semantic persistence criterion for finite positive instantial neighbourhood frames

## Finding

A finite positive instantial neighbourhood frame supports **persistent truth for every formula under every upward-closed propositional valuation** precisely when its two neighbourhood assignments satisfy the following independent, order-sensitive witness conditions. These conditions are strictly weaker than requiring literal inclusion of neighbourhood families along the underlying order.

Let \(P=(W,\le)\) be a nonempty finite poset and \(\operatorname{Up}(P)\) its family of upward-closed subsets. For every \(w\in W\), take arbitrary, possibly empty, neighbourhood families \(N_{\Box}(w),N_{\Diamond}(w)\subseteq\operatorname{Up}(P)\). Write \(\operatorname{Max}(W)\) for the maximal worlds and \(\downarrow T=\{x\in W:\exists t\in T\;x\le t\}\).

**Theorem.** Under the standard positive instantial semantics specified below, every formula has an upset truth set under every upset valuation **if and only if**, for every \(w\le v\), both of the following hold:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{(B)}\quad &\forall U\in N_{\Box}(w)\;\exists V\in N_{\Box}(v):
 U\cap\operatorname{Max}(W)\subseteq V\subseteq U,\\
\text{(D)}\quad &\forall T\in N_{\Diamond}(v)\;\exists S\in N_{\Diamond}(w):
 T\subseteq S\subseteq\downarrow T.
\end{aligned}
\]

Empty neighbourhoods are not excluded, and the criteria include them without exceptions. The resulting criterion exactly characterizes closure of the upset lattice under **all** the instantial operators, simultaneously at every finite arity.

The weakening is proper already on the two-point chain \(0<1\). Let \(H=\{1\}\), \(W=\{0,1\}\), and set \(N_{\Box}(0)=N_{\Diamond}(0)=\{W\}\), \(N_{\Box}(1)=N_{\Diamond}(1)=\{H\}\). Conditions (B) and (D) hold, but neither \(N_{\Box}(0)\subseteq N_{\Box}(1)\) nor \(N_{\Diamond}(1)\subseteq N_{\Diamond}(0)\) holds. This is minimal in poset cardinality for a strict comparable pair.

## Assumptions and scope

The proposition alphabet is countably infinite, so every finite family of upsets can be named by distinct propositional atoms. Valuations send atoms to upsets of \(P\). Formulas are generated by atoms, \(\top,\bot,\wedge,\vee\), and two independent primitive modalities \(\Box(\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n;\psi)\) and \(\Diamond(\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n;\psi)\), for **every integer \(n\ge0\)**. For sets \(A_1,\ldots,A_n,B\subseteq W\), define the operations at a world \(w\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
 w\in f_n(A_1,\ldots,A_n;B)&\iff\exists U\in N_{\Box}(w)\;[U\subseteq B\land \bigwedge_{i=1}^n(U\cap A_i\ne\varnothing)],\\
 w\in g_n(A_1,\ldots,A_n;B)&\iff\forall T\in N_{\Diamond}(w)\;[\bigvee_{i=1}^n(T\subseteq A_i)\lor T\cap B\ne\varnothing].
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(n=0\), the displayed conjunction is true and the disjunction false. Interpret modalities on the truth sets of their arguments. No family-inclusion conditions are assumed. The theorem is about semantic persistence for all such valuations and formulas, not about axiomatizability or completeness of a particular deductive calculus. The finiteness assumption is used essentially in the necessity direction to name all relevant tests with finitely many instance arguments.

## Proof

We first characterize closure of the primitive operations on \(\operatorname{Up}(P)\). Since atomic truth sets are upsets and finite unions/intersections preserve upsets, this gives the formula-level result by structural induction. Conversely, arbitrary upset arguments of any one primitive operation can be realized by distinct atoms, so persistence of every formula forces closure of each primitive operation.

**(B), necessity.** Fix \(w\le v\) and \(U\in N_{\Box}(w)\). Use the scope upset \(B=U\). For each maximal world \(m\in U\cap\operatorname{Max}(W)\), use the singleton upset \(A_m=\{m\}\) as an instance argument. Every such singleton meets \(U\), so \(w\in f_n((A_m)_m;U)\). Closure implies the same at \(v\), witnessed by some \(V\in N_{\Box}(v)\). The scope forces \(V\subseteq U\), and meeting each singleton forces \(U\cap\operatorname{Max}(W)\subseteq V\). If \(U=\varnothing\), the instance list is empty and the scope forces an empty witness at \(v\); the same deduction holds.

**(B), sufficiency.** Fix upset scope \(B\) and upset instance arguments \(A_i\). If \(w\in f_n((A_i)_i;B)\), choose witness \(U\in N_{\Box}(w)\) and obtain \(V\in N_{\Box}(v)\) by (B). Then \(V\subseteq U\subseteq B\). For every \(i\), choose \(x\in U\cap A_i\). Finiteness lets us choose a maximal world \(m\ge x\); because \(U\) and \(A_i\) are upsets, \(m\in U\cap A_i\cap\operatorname{Max}(W)\subseteq V\cap A_i\). Thus \(V\) witnesses the same operator at \(v\). The zero-instance case is included.

**(D), necessity.** Fix \(w\le v\) and \(T\in N_{\Diamond}(v)\). Let \(B=W\setminus\downarrow T\), an upset. List **all** upsets \(A\) of \(P\) for which \(T\nsubseteq A\); there are finitely many. Every listed \(A\) fails to contain \(T\), and \(T\cap B=\varnothing\). Therefore \(v\notin g_n((A)_A;B)\). Closure of its truth set implies \(w\notin g_n((A)_A;B)\). Hence some \(S\in N_{\Diamond}(w)\) obeys \(S\cap B=\varnothing\) and \(S\nsubseteq A\) for every listed \(A\). The first relation gives \(S\subseteq\downarrow T\). If \(T\nsubseteq S\), then \(S\) itself is one of the listed upsets, contradicting \(S\nsubseteq S\). Thus \(T\subseteq S\). For \(T=\varnothing\) the list is empty, and the argument forces \(S=\varnothing\), again proving (D).

**(D), sufficiency.** Argue contrapositively. If \(v\notin g_n((A_i)_i;B)\), there is \(T\in N_{\Diamond}(v)\) such that \(T\nsubseteq A_i\) for every \(i\), and \(T\cap B=\varnothing\). By (D) choose \(S\in N_{\Diamond}(w)\) with \(T\subseteq S\subseteq\downarrow T\). Then \(S\nsubseteq A_i\) for every \(i\), since \(T\subseteq S\); also \(S\cap B=\varnothing\), since \(B\) is upset, \(T\cap B=\varnothing\), and \(S\subseteq\downarrow T\). Hence \(w\notin g_n((A_i)_i;B)\). This proves closure by contraposition, including \(n=0\).

**Strictness.** On the two-point chain choose the families in the Finding. The only maximal world is \(1\). For the box condition \(W\cap\operatorname{Max}(W)=H\subseteq H\subseteq W\); for the diamond condition \(H\subseteq W\subseteq\downarrow H=W\). Both literal family inclusions fail. A one-element poset has no pair of distinct comparable worlds at which inclusions could be weakened, so two worlds is the smallest nontrivial counterexample. \(\square\)

## Verification

A self-contained `checker.py` enumerates all upsets of seven finite posets and tests every choice of scope upset and every subset of distinct instance upsets, including zero arguments. It verifies that operator persistence agrees with (B) and (D) across 8,384 neighbourhood-family comparisons: exhaustive comparisons where the number of upsets permits and fixed-seed sampling elsewhere. It additionally checks the strict two-world counterexample. Reproduce with `python3 checker.py`. This is a finite consistency check; the universal theorem follows from the proof, not from the enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Das, Khanra and Sardar (arXiv:2606.08083v1, posted 6 June 2026), Section 3, define positive instantial neighbourhood logic and **sufficient** frame conditions \(N_{\Box}(w)\subseteq N_{\Box}(v)\) and \(N_{\Diamond}(v)\subseteq N_{\Diamond}(w)\) for \(w\le v\), and use those inclusions to prove truth persistence. The new theorem characterizes necessity as well as sufficiency **for finite frames with all arities**. The strict two-world witness demonstrates genuinely additional frames with persistent semantics. The 2020 coalgebraic duality for classical instantial neighbourhood logic treats Boolean algebras and descriptive neighbourhood frames, not this positive finite-poset exact criterion. The 2017 original instantial logic paper also concerns a different Boolean framework.

An exact arity-recovery theorem for finite Boolean instantial neighbourhood frames (published-finding corpus, `SCOPE-sharp-finite-instance-arity-cutoff-for-instantial-neighbourhood--d0e614d1df15`) recovers frames from their operations at bounded arity; it does not characterize which ordered two-sided assignments yield upset-preserving positive operators. These statements are incomparable in assumptions and conclusions. No historical uniqueness is asserted.

## Limitations

The necessity proof assumes arbitrary upset valuations with **enough distinct atoms** and availability of **every finite arity**, particularly zero arity. For a fixed small alphabet or arity-bounded fragment, these criteria may be stronger than necessary. Finiteness cannot be omitted without replacing the test family by potentially infinitary arguments and accounting for maximal elements. The result characterizes semantic persistence alone; soundness, completeness and representation questions for enlarged frame classes need separate study. An equivalent formulation may exist in general ordered-coalgebraic simulation literature; accessible sources do not rule that out. No independent mathematical or historical-priority review has been performed.
