# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. A shattered edge set must lie in a Hamiltonian cycle; the path-complement lemma provides a Hamiltonian cyclic order whenever at least five forced-path blocks remain. The proof checks the small remaining block counts directly, including the exceptional two-path top stratum. Every quantified case is covered for all integer graph orders at least three. The independent finite checker exhausts 209,087 nonempty supports through graph order seven and representatives for eight and nine; these checks supplement rather than replace the proof. Dirac's minimum-degree theorem is applied only in its valid range, at least six blocks.

## Originality
PASS within the inspected material. The closest historical predecessor (Kranakis et al., 1994) treats edge-VC dimension for connected edge sets and vertex-VC dimension for cycles. These are distinct concept families and neither statement implies a coefficientwise Hamiltonian-tour edge trace classification. Schaefer (2000) addresses vertex path-VC complexity; Coudert et al. (2024) addresses closed graph neighborhoods. The semantic literature and mathematical-record searches found no dominating exact all-support theorem, but historical priority remains a residual risk, particularly in tour-polytope and trace terminology.

## Value
PASS. The complete shattering polynomial of a canonical spanning-subgraph incidence family is a natural structural invariant. The precise three-edge lower limit for each of two spanning path components at the maximal support size is not a generic VC-counting consequence and explains a sharp boundary in realizable tour traces. This supports exact finite sampling probabilities and distinguishes support geometry beyond a single scalar dimension.

## Limitations
The result does not address incomplete host graphs, directed tours, all simple cycles, or nonspanning paths. A broad historical-priority search cannot certify uniqueness; independent validation is outstanding.
