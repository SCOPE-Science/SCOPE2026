# Exact shattering and online learning for maximal intersecting edge families

## Finding
For each integer \(n\geq4\), let \(E_n=\binom{[n]}2\), and let \(\mathcal M_n\) be the family of all inclusion-maximal pairwise-intersecting subfamilies of \(E_n\). Regard \(\mathcal M_n\) as a Boolean concept class on the query domain \(E_n\), answering whether an edge belongs to the unknown maximal family. Its shattered-support complex \(\mathsf S_n\) consists **exactly** of those edge sets having at most three edges and with every two edges incident. Equivalently, \(\mathsf S_n\) is the two-dimensional skeleton of the clique complex of the line graph \(L(K_n)\). The maximal shattered supports are precisely the three-edge stars and three-edge triangles.

Writing \(s_j(n)\) for the number of shattered supports of cardinality \(j\), the complete polynomial and learning dimensions are
\[
\Phi_n(z)=\sum_{j\geq0}s_j(n)z^j=1+\binom n2z+3\binom n3z^2+(n-2)\binom n3z^3,\qquad
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal M_n)=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal M_n)=3.
\]
The geometric realization of this shattering complex has homotopy type
\[
|\mathsf S_n|\simeq\bigvee^{\beta_n} S^2,\qquad
\beta_n=\binom n2+(n-5)\binom n3-1.
\]
This last consequence uses the structure of the two-skeleton and is distinguished from the previously studied homotopy type of the *full* line-graph clique complex.

## Assumptions and scope
All edges are two-element subsets of the labeled set \([n]\). Pairwise intersecting means that any two distinct edges share an endpoint; maximal means inclusion-maximal among subfamilies of \(E_n\), not necessarily maximum cardinality. A support \(S\subseteq E_n\) is shattered if \(\{M\cap S:M\in\mathcal M_n\}=2^S\). The Littlestone dimension is the maximum depth of a complete binary adaptive membership-query tree with a realizable concept on every root-to-leaf branch. Empty support is included. The theorem starts at \(n=4\); for \(n\leq3\), the maximal-family description degenerates and is not covered by the stated polynomial.

## Proof
**Classification of concepts.** If a family contains two distinct incident edges \(ab\) and \(ac\), every further edge meeting both either contains \(a\) or is \(bc\). If the family contains an edge \(ad\) with \(d\notin\{b,c\}\), the edge \(bc\) cannot belong, and maximality yields the full star centered at \(a\). Otherwise a maximal family not having a common endpoint is exactly the triangle \(\{ab,ac,bc\}\). A family of just one edge extends and is not maximal. Thus the concepts are exactly the \(n\) full stars \(M_a=\{ab:b\ne a\}\) and the \(\binom n3\) three-edge triangles \(M_{abc}=\{ab,ac,bc\}\). They are distinct for \(n\geq4\), and each is maximal, since a nonincluded edge is disjoint from a chosen member.

**Necessary conditions for shattering.** If \(S\) contains two disjoint edges then no concept labels both positive. Thus every two edges of \(S\) must meet. Any collection of at least four pairwise-intersecting two-element edges is a star: a non-star intersecting family must lie inside one triangle. For a support containing four edges, the all-positive labeling therefore forces four edges of one common star, but no concept contains exactly three of those four: another star contains at most one, and a triangular concept contains at most two. Hence no size-four support is shattered. Shattering is hereditary, so no larger support is shattered.

**Sufficiency for all supports up to three.** Every three-edge star \(S=\{ab,ac,ad\}\), with distinct \(a,b,c,d\), realizes all eight labels. The all-positive trace comes from \(M_a\), a two-positive trace from the triangle on \(a\) and the two corresponding leaves, a one-positive trace from the star at its corresponding leaf, and the empty trace from the triangle on the three leaves. Every three-edge triangle \(S=\{ab,ac,bc\}\) likewise realizes all labels: the full trace comes from \(M_{abc}\); each two-positive trace is a star at the shared endpoint; each singleton is obtained from a triangle consisting of its endpoints and an external point; the empty trace is the star at such an external point. Such an external point exists because \(n\geq4\). Every pair of incident edges extends to a three-edge star, and every singleton extends to one as well. Heredity proves sufficiency at all lower cardinalities.

**Enumeration.** The domain has \(\binom n2\) edges; its line graph has \(3\binom n3\) adjacent pairs. A three-edge pairwise-intersecting family is either a star \((n\binom{n-1}{3})\) or a triangle \((\binom n3)\). Their sum is \((n-2)\binom n3\). There are no faces of size four or more, giving the polynomial and VC dimension three.

**Online dimension.** The VC dimension furnishes a complete adaptive tree of depth three. Conversely, fix any first query edge \(e=ab\). The positive-branch concepts consist of the two stars \(M_a,M_b\) and the triangles \(M_{abc}\) for \(c\notin\{a,b\}\). For any subsequent distinct edge query \(f\), its positive subbranch within this class contains no hypotheses if \(f\cap e=\varnothing\), and exactly two hypotheses if \(f\) meets \(e\): the star at their common endpoint and the triangle on their union. A class of at most two hypotheses has Littlestone dimension at most one. Thus the first positive-branch class has Littlestone dimension at most two, so no root query can support a depth-four complete tree. This proves \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal M_n)=3\).

**Topological consequence.** Let \(X=\Delta L(K_n)\), the full clique complex of the line graph. Its maximal simplices are the full edge stars and the three-edge triangles. Let \(X_0\) be the union of the \(n\) full star simplices. Two distinct star simplices meet in exactly one vertex (the edge joining their centers), while three meet trivially. All intersections are contractible or empty, so the nerve lemma gives \(X_0\simeq K_n\), regarded here as a one-dimensional simplicial complex. Adjoining all triangular edge-family simplices kills the fundamental group: under the nerve equivalence their attaching cycles are the three-cycles in this complete graph, which normally generate its free fundamental group. Hence \(X\), and equally its two-skeleton \(\mathsf S_n=X^{(2)}\), are simply connected. The complex \(\mathsf S_n\) is connected, two-dimensional, and has face numbers \(f_0=\binom n2\), \(f_1=3\binom n3\), and \(f_2=(n-2)\binom n3\). Therefore its integral \(H_2\) is free of rank \(f_0-f_1+f_2-1=\beta_n\). For a simply connected two-dimensional CW complex, the Hurewicz and Whitehead theorems give homotopy equivalence to a wedge of that many two-spheres.

## Relationship to prior work
The stars/triangles classification rests on elementary two-uniform intersection structure, familiar from the Erdős–Ko–Rado setting; it is not asserted as a new extremal-family theorem. Frankl and Pach (2020) investigate a different notion of shattered *matchings* in much larger uniform intersecting families. Goyal, Shukla, and Singh (2020, revised 2021) investigate the topology of the **full** clique complex of a line graph, and cite the earlier wedge-of-two-spheres result for \(\Delta L(K_n)\). Their theorem explains why simple connectivity of the skeleton is compatible with known topology; the full-complex homotopy statement is not claimed as new. Guingona, Kolesnikov, Nierwinski, and Soucy (2021) study VC and Littlestone dimensions for zero sets of linear combinations, a different concept class. Fuentes and Kamat (2024) study extremal *sizes* of intersecting families of subgraphs of a disjoint matching. None of these inspected statements determines the full membership-query shattering complex and online dimension for **all inclusion-maximal** two-uniform intersecting families. Searches did not establish historical novelty conclusively; alternative terminology for edge clique-family learning remains a residual risk.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the two-uniform setting only, with \(n\geq4\); it does not extend the stated polynomial to maximal intersecting families of three-sets or arbitrary uniformity. The homotopy conclusion is a structural corollary, rather than a competing claim to the known homotopy theorem for the full line-graph clique complex. Computational checks cover only the stated finite ranges. A broader priority search or independent review could uncover a theorem expressed through clique-complex skeleta or an equivalent learning-theoretic duality.
