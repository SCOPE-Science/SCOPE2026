# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Componentwise signed-incidence rank is proved from edge constraints on a spanning tree. Every tree has independent columns; a unicyclic component has independent columns if and only if its unique fair-sign cycle is unbalanced; multicyclic components are dependent. This proves the exact finite-size expectation identity. Explicit cycle counts, joint factorial-moment convergence, subcritical pseudoforest estimates, and balanced-short-cycle upper bounds prove the sharp limiting law including \(c=0\), \(c=1\), and \(c>1\). Exhaustive rational rank tests and seeded simulations validate the model and selected special cases without substituting for the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** Searches covered SparseStack, two-block CountSketch, signed incidence rank, balanced/unbalanced cycles, random bipartite multigraphs, 2-XORSAT and full-rank phase transitions, including relevant published mathematical records. The closest numerical papers (Jeendgar et al. 2026; Camaño et al. 2025; Heidary 2026) do not give exact two-block rank probabilities. Huang–Rudelson–Tikhomirov 2026 establish singular-value collapse for constant sparsity but not rank failure. The classic signed-incidence theorem and the 2-XORSAT papers provide parts of the mechanism, but the inspected non-bipartite formula does not imply the precise bipartite signed-multigraph result without new specialization and calculation. Important residual risk: a prior bipartite signed-graph / 2-XORSAT enumeration may already state the same limit under a different name. Historical-record searches are not proof of global uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** The exact coherent-subspace rank survival law gives a decisive failure-probability floor for the simplest multi-block sparse sketch. It quantifies the two-block regime where constant oversampling cannot give success approaching one, yields an explicit 95% rank necessary-row threshold, and separates literal singularity from the already-known vanishing positive singular-value phenomenon. The finite component formula is an exact test oracle for sketch implementations; this is not merely a single collision check.

**Scope and risks.** Model assumes iid block hashes and fair signs in exact arithmetic, two blocks, and a fixed coordinate frame. No statement about full singular-value distribution, other stack counts, floating-point effects, or adaptive subspaces. Mathematical references are cited in RESULT.md; priority against older bipartite 2-XORSAT work remains a research risk.
