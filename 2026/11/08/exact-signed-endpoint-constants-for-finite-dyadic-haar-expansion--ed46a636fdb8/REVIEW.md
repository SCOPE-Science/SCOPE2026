# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** For each terminal dyadic interval, all Haar multipliers off its ancestry vanish from the kernel row. The exact dyadic row-sum formula is reduced without inequality to a one-dimensional finite-horizon control problem. The Bellman function has the globally verified affine-in-absolute-value form \(F_h(b)=a_h+c_h|b|\), with \(c_0=1\), \(a_0=0\), \(a_h=a_{h-1}+(1+c_{h-1})/2\), \(c_h=(1-c_{h-1})/2\). The resulting closed form and optimal sign choices prove the claimed value at every depth including the zero and one-stage cases. Independently constructed rational kernel rows and exhaustive rational finite controls checked the normalizations; tests are not substituted for induction.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** The relevant reviewed primary literature treats infinite-system \(L^p\) unconditionality, weak-type endpoint estimates, and optimal symmetric rearrangement ranges, not the exact fixed-depth signed \(L^\infty\) operator norm or the oscillatory \(2^{-d}\) correction. Osękowski (2013) was read in accessible full HTML; Osękowski (2016) had its full theorem statements, problem definition and later sharp statements inspected in the accessible PDF; Astashkin et al. (2022) had the principal and range theorems inspected in HTML. Choi's 1992 full text was not accessible, but available descriptions explicitly concern all-horizon bounds for fixed finite \(p\), which do not imply this \(p=\infty\) fixed-depth formula. Queries for the exact rational expression, endpoint aliases and finite-horizon martingale transforms found no covering published theorem; this absence does not prove global novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The finite-depth signed unconditionality constant measures the exact rate at which the Haar basis fails to be unconditionally bounded at the \(L^\infty\) endpoint. The natural depth parameter has an all-depth closed answer, a nontrivial asymptotic slope \(2/3\), a precise alternating finite-size correction and a complete optimizer rule. These distinguish it from qualitative non-unconditionality and unrelated arbitrary finite tests.

Closest conceptual literature is Osękowski (2013, 2016), Choi (1992), and Astashkin et al. (2022); none of the inspected statements covers the exact fixed-depth problem. Unreadable older sources and unindexed equivalent presentations remain the main originality risks. Weighted/nonbinary martingales, incomplete trees and coefficient suppression are outside the claimed result.
