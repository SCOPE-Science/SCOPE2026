# Review of the flat-cone distance law

## Correctness — PASS

The statement fixes intrinsic length distance, normalized area measure, cone-angle range, compactness, radius quantifier and exactly which surface categories are covered. The proof computes the difference between a disk's angular measure on a cone and in the plane; Tonelli applies to its nonnegative deficit and the rescaled integration is finite. The nonstandard integral was recomputed by the substitution \(v=tu\), independent numerical integration of single-cone ball areas, and the separate exact pillowcase convolution argument. Positivity follows from the integral. Compactness localizes all small-radius ball-area defects near finitely many isolated singularities, and their areas add without interactions. The orbifold classification is a direct Gauss--Bonnet/Diophantine argument and the signature constants have explicitly positive gaps. Numerical checks are ancillary to the written proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical uncertainty

The closest primary material is Troyanov (arXiv:math/0702666; full HTML read on conical geometry and Gauss--Bonnet), Sağlam (arXiv:1602.04240; full HTML read on length structures and cone models), and Charitos--Papadoperakis--Tsapogas (arXiv:1306.1759; full HTML read on geodesics and conical surfaces). Their statements establish geometry, triangulations or geodesic behavior, not the quartic term for an area-averaged intrinsic random-pair distance distribution. The smooth Bertrand--Diguet--Puiseux curvature expansion is not an exact finite-angle formula; comparison of four cone signatures with identical Euler characteristic shows the claimed statistic is strictly more discriminating. A published polygon **interior** short-distance quartic coefficient concerns boundary corners, and distance laws on smooth tori have no isolated cone singularities. Neither implies this cone-local coefficient or the five-way orbifold classification.

Borzellino's 1992 orbifold dissertation contains Bishop-type volume comparison, a plausibly relevant antecedent. Its catalogue and abstract were accessible but the full dissertation could not be inspected; this is recorded as a remaining priority risk, not evidence of absence. The review does not claim that all historical literature has been exhausted.

## Value — PASS

A two-point intrinsic distance distribution is a natural invariant of compact metric-measure geometry. Unlike the total angle defect/Euler characteristic, the exact nonlinear coefficient \(\sum K(\alpha_j)\) distinguishes the four spherical Euclidean orbifold cone-order signatures; together with the flat torus it gives a complete five-way reconstruction from just two small-radius coefficients. A global classification is obtained by one locally motivated integral, not by an arbitrary sample of numerical configurations. The general cone-surface identity and its rigorous finite-radius exactness make the result reusable for additional metric-measure comparisons.

## Limits and literature

The theorem excludes angles above \(2\pi\), boundary or mirror singularities, and smoothly varying curvature. It does not recover cone locations, the full cone-angle multiset of a general flat surface, or isometry class inside one orbifold signature. Exact numerical checks never substitute for infinite-domain proof. Further historical comparison may be needed, especially for the inaccessible Borzellino dissertation.
