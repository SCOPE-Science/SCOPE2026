# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The all-field Fourier diagonalization is a known rank-distance association-scheme fact. The new endpoint comparison is proved by an exact adjacent-difference inequality; all ranks \(k\ge3\) are dominated, and a complete check of \(k=0,1,2\) isolates the binary square exceptional family and the dimension-two tie. The exceptional endpoint cannot vanish for integer \(u\). Independent direct Fourier computations matched 673 small-field characters; 760 additional integer parameter cases passed. The numerical evidence is supplementary, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
The spectrum and q-Krawtchouk table are classical (Delsarte, 1978, Appendix Theorem A2) and not claimed as new. Brouwer et al. (2018, Section 7) study different spectral extrema, especially most negative eigenvalues and second-largest absolute eigenvalues; neither is the nearest-to-zero rank-two multiplier. The inspected statement of Schmidt (2020) is on symmetric/quadratic forms. Searches for nearest-zero generalized q-Krawtchouk modes, all-parameter rank-two condition numbers, and the binary-square switch identified no covering exact theorem. Historical priority uncertainty remains, particularly because an unknown general q-Krawtchouk separation theorem could imply these conclusions.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exact all-prime-power classification of stability for a canonical rank-two rank-metric Cayley convolution. It determines the inverse operator's worst-case \(\ell^2\) amplification, identifies each least-stable Fourier sector, and proves a sharp exceptional switch at binary square matrices. The generic condition number depends on field order and column count but not row count. These are structural recovery facts rather than an arbitrarily selected finite invariant.

## Limits
No claims concern other rank shells, other operator norms, incomplete sampling, or independent historical validation. The strongest prior result supplies all eigenvalues; the new contribution is the uniform global minimization of their absolute values. The checker uses prime-field Fourier transforms and integer symbolic checks, but extension fields are covered by the published diagonalization and analytic inequalities, not by direct finite experiments.
