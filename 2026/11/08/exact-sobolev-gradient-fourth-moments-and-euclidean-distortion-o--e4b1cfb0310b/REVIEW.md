# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof writes the intrinsic gradient as a tangential orthogonal projection, evaluates the required three spherical moments exactly, and supplies explicit Lagrange stationary and second-variation arguments ruling out three-distinct-eigenvalue extrema for every ambient dimension at least four. It separately handles dimensions two and three via Newton identities. The comparison norm optimality follows by averaging arbitrary ellipsoidal comparisons under an irreducible real orthogonal conjugation action. The standalone checker independently tests the critical moment and Hessian identities. Finite tests are treated as corroboration, not as proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** Relevant published scalar harmonic inequalities and a directly related exact scalar degree-two theorem were compared by mathematical implication rather than title. The scalar theorem provides the upper spectral inequality but does not state the tangential-gradient mixed moments, lower spectral parity classification of the gradient ratio or the exact Hilbert distortion of the induced vector-valued Sobolev norm. The source inspection covered the original scalar theorem's proof and the introductions, main statements and methods of two arXiv papers. A specialized source phrasing the same norm as a matrix invariant could remain unindexed. It is not claimed that the scalar degree-two bound or generic group-averaging argument is new.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The pointwise carré-du-champ of an eigenfunction is a natural nonlinear energy observable. The exact all-dimension gradient norm, its complete extremizer geometry, its Hilbertianity cutoff in dimensions two and three and its optimal parity-dependent Euclidean distortion form a cohesive natural norm classification. They are not a numerical fitting of arbitrary coefficient restrictions. The result does not claim extensions to general eigenfunction degrees or nonround manifolds.
