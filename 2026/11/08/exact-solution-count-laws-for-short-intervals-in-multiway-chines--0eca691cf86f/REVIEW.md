# Scientific review

## Correctness
The reduction from multiway CRT-compatible residue tuples to a common intersection of cyclic arcs is bijective because every interval has length at most the common pairwise gcd. The lower extremum follows from consecutive complements and the upper extremum from nested arcs. Unit translations alter the count by at most one, proving the gap-free integer spectrum. For each marked subset, complementary gap lengths count all containing arcs; the \(g/r\) composition factor follows from distinguishing one cyclic subset member, and binomial inversion yields the full probability mass function. Independent finite exact checks validate endpoints, masses and moments, including direct CRT counts.

## Originality
Gibson (arXiv:1302.0917v1, Theorem 2.2) counts compatible pairs of residue intervals and points to more-than-two congruences in Section 6. Its pairwise lower bound is recovered when \(k=2\), but it does not state the higher-way complete count spectrum or probability law. The conventional CRT gives solvability for each fixed residue tuple, not the exact distribution of counts over translated interval systems. Sun (1991) addresses periods and union coverings rather than the same translated multiway intersection statistics. Related mathematical-index and literature searches produced no equivalent theorem; this is a qualified literature-based assessment, not a claim of guaranteed priority.

## Value
The problem is expressly motivated by the density-CRT literature's request for more-than-two congruences and structural information. The result supplies a complete exact classification of possible solution counts, a sharp all-placements solvability threshold, and all probability masses through explicit cyclic-gap compositions, not just the obvious two-set lower bound. The formula applies uniformly for every number of congruences, any common gcd and every allowed interval length, while making clear why it does not resolve arbitrary-gcd or long-interval cases. Its full distribution controls fluctuations as well as worst-case compatibility.

## Closest literature and limits
The main comparator is Gibson (2013 preprint / 2014 journal). The generalized CRT is foundational and the circular-gap argument is elementary but its combined all-order enumeration is not reported in the inspected main source. The continuous random-arc coverage literature (Siegel 1978, Ailam 1966) may imply related overlap moments; these accessible abstracts are not a full comparison and do not establish the discrete multiway full law. Historical priority is not exhaustively established; a previously published cyclic-arc intersection count could imply the result. No general Lonely Runner result is asserted.
