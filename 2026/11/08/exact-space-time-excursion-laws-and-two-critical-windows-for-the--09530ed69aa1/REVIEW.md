# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The affine branch images yield the exact Markov-level transition kernel under initially uniform Lebesgue sampling. The departure mixture is computed from four branch lengths; skip-free downward first-passage generating functions are proved by their quadratic equation and Lagrange inversion. The maximum law follows from a harmonic function and the memoryless geometric upper overshoot; the return and maximum scaling limits follow respectively from a uniform ballot local limit with an atom at nonreturn and from direct substitution. Independent exact-rational propagation and finite-height systems test the normalizations. Critical risk: the time-window interchange of the sum over initial geometric heights and the asymptotic limit; it is controlled by an exponential initial-height tail and the stated first-passage bounds, not by finite simulation.

**Originality: PASS within inspected evidence.** Ji–Ma's primary article (2023) gives the auxiliary map and qualitative recurrence transition, but the inspected Markov-map discussion does not present this joint all-parameter law or the two crossover profiles. Classical skip-free random-walk formulas are used as prior tools, not claimed original. A previously available critical-only excursion result with a different starting-level formulation covers some specializations; the distinctive present claims are the exact four-level Lebesgue mixture over all parameters and the pair of simultaneous critical windows. Searches of the relevant primary full text, published research summaries, and published research records did not identify a dominating statement. Residual risk: an unindexed model-specific treatment or a sufficiently specialized earlier general first-passage crossover theorem might cover parts of the result. A negative search is not proof of priority.

**Value: PASS.** A conservativity/dissipativity transition for a concrete countably branched interval model becomes quantitatively measurable: both its temporal and spatial excursion observables are exactly soluble, but their critical parameter resolutions differ. This identifies how to distinguish and scale two natural rare-excursion questions in the same dynamical system. It is not a normalization check or arbitrary finite slice.

**Limitations.** The auxiliary map is not the original Fibonacci bimodal map nor its induced map, and the claim does not transfer either excursion law to them. Numerical checks support finite values only. Publication priority remains open to independent literature investigation.
