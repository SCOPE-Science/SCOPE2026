# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof specifies probability normalization, the precise cyclic graph distance, the fixed first Fourier character and the range of the attenuation parameter. Strict convexity of squared arccosine produces an exact affine minorant simultaneously for every grid point; its expectation proves the universal bound. A symmetric positive measure attains the bound, and both nonnegative summands in the exact defect identity characterize all equality cases. Cases with zero-radius or singleton shells and attenuation breakpoints are included. The zero-notch arithmetic gap and its odd-order limit follow from direct trigonometric interpolation. The standalone checker passed 39,300 deterministic checks, which are supplementary and finite.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected literature and mathematical records, not independent priority certification. Tan–Zhang (arXiv:2609.07646, especially Lemma 2.1, Remark 3.3, Proposition 5.1) proves arc-diameter and near-diameter results under vanishing moments; none implies the exact least graph-distance second moment for every allowed first-Fourier modulus, its full frontier, or complete optimizer family. Peherstorfer (arXiv:1001.2451, Sections 1–2) treats trigonometric quadrature existence and weights rather than this fixed-grid metric extremization. Coeurjolly–Le Bihan (arXiv:1012.4361, Sections 2–3) studies intrinsic and extrinsic variance for named circular probability families rather than a universal cyclic measure inequality. Semantic mathematical-record searches produced near results in Fourier positivity, cyclic uncertainty, and Turán extremals but not the claim or a dominating implication. Generic convex-hull duality is an underlying standard method; an independently published identical application remains possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. In positive Fourier filter design, a suppressed first harmonic must incur spatial dispersion. The theorem identifies the complete attainable sharp tradeoff, optimizer geometry, exceptional quarter-period arithmetic and a quantitative exact defect decomposition at all grid sizes, not merely a loose uncertainty constant. The objective is intrinsic squared cycle distance, a natural measure of localization; the full boundary can be used to benchmark finite cyclic filters and to infer distance-shell localization from near-optimal attenuation.

## Closest literature and limitations

The subject is restricted to positive probability weights, the first Fourier character and discrete centers; complex signed measures, multiple simultaneous notches and arbitrary frequencies require different analysis. The optimization is a one-moment convex problem and does not claim broader harmonic-analysis conjectures. No independent audit or publication-priority certification has occurred.
