# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A unitriangular incidence congruence gives the determinant and full inertia. A complete symmetric-group decomposition yields the degree-two eigenvalue, the standard two-by-two block and the radial three-by-three block, with dimensions summing to the whole matrix. The cubic coefficients and asymptotic separation are explicitly derived. Independent finite eigenspectrum and determinant checks hold for dimensions four through thirteen; they are not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS under bounded comparison.** Searches of finite Walsh sampling, first-band Boolean-ball concentration, quadratic-band fixed-weight shell concentration and related Hamming schemes did not reveal a theorem asserting the present full spectrum of the *square* radius-two Walsh minor and its fourth-power condition law. The shell version has a nontrivial kernel and therefore cannot directly imply the present invertible square result. Khalid, Kennedy and McEwen treat a continuous ball; Mixon and Peterson treat Boolean Fourier-spectral learning. A broader representation-theoretic theorem could still subsume this special case; independent priority confirmation remains outstanding.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The matrix models a natural exact quadratic Fourier interpolation problem using the smallest cumulative Hamming ball of the same cardinality as the coefficient space. The complete eigenspectrum, exact determinant and parity counts, and dimension-fourth conditioning quantify a nontrivial distinction between algebraic identifiability and numerical recovery. The result is structural for every dimension rather than a special numerical instance.
