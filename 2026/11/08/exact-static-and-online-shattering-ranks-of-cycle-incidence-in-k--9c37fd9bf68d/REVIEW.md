# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The static five-edge obstruction is geometric: a proposed trace would require connecting two prescribed disjoint path components using nonexistent within-part edges. The online six-level obstruction is distinct: after five positive edges of a six-cycle, its closing edge cannot receive a zero answer. Exact 15-cycle counting closes the small boundary, while the four-support trace table and the full 32-leaf adaptive certificate prove the two lower bounds. The independent checker re-enumerates cycles rather than trusting masks supplied by the certificate. No claim is made for larger left bipartition sizes.

## Originality (PASS within accessible comparisons)
Mofidi's two 2022 cycle-hypergraph papers provide a close starting point. Their general bounds, friendship/wheel computations, and cycle-count analysis do not contain the checked exact \(K_{3,b}\) static/online pair or the exceptional \(b=3\) transition. Related previously recorded work on Hamiltonian cycles of complete graphs studies a different family and cannot imply cycle edge incidence in \(K_{3,b}\). Accessible indexed searches and known historical records did not show equivalent coverage. Exact historical uniqueness cannot be guaranteed outside accessible publications.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The three-vertex side is the smallest complete-bipartite regime permitting both four- and six-cycles. Its cycle-incidence hypotheses demonstrate a stable strict separation of adaptive from static shattering, with a sharp one-column threshold, despite their uniformly bounded cycle length. The classification is uniform in the arbitrarily large other part and exposes a concrete structural obstruction to depth six; it is not merely an enumerated small graph.
