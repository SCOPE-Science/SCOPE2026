# Exact strong uniqueness modulus for a five-coordinate projection

## Finding
Let \(V\) be the real two-dimensional subspace of \(\ell_\infty^5\) generated by
\[
u=(1,4/5,1/4,-1/4,-4/5),\qquad v=(0,3/5,1,1,3/5).
\]
There is a unique real projection \(P_*:\ell_\infty^5\to V\) of minimal operator norm \(845/659\). More precisely, **for every** real linear projection \(P\) onto \(V\),
\[
\boxed{\quad\|P\|-\frac{845}{659}\ \ge\ \frac{1508}{493257}\,\|P-P_*\|.\quad}
\]
The constant \(1508/493257\) is the **largest possible**, and equality holds for nontrivial projections arbitrarily close to \(P_*\). Here all norms are induced from \(\ell_\infty^5\), and the assertion is restricted to real scalars.

The minimal projection and its norm were determined in earlier work; the new content here is its uniqueness and the **exact sharp global strong-uniqueness (linear error-bound) constant**, including an equality direction.

## Assumptions and scope
Use the coordinates \((x_1,x_3)\) on \(V\), so that \(V=\operatorname{ran}U\) with
\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\13/20&3/5\\0&1\\-1/2&1\\-19/20&3/5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every projection onto \(V\) is uniquely \(P=UF\), where \(F\) is a real \(2\times5\) matrix satisfying \(FU=I_2\). Define
\[
F_*=\begin{pmatrix}
520/659&105/1318&110/659&-110/659&-105/1318\\
130/659&665/5272&315/659&260/659&455/5272
\end{pmatrix},\qquad P_*=UF_*.
\]
A direct multiplication gives \(F_*U=I_2\). Each row of \(P_*\) has absolute sum \(r=845/659\); the only zero entries are in positions \((2,5)\) and \((5,2)\). Every competing projection has the form \(P_*+UH\) with \(H\in\mathbb R^{2\times5}\) and \(HU=0\). The allowed perturbations form a six-dimensional real vector space.

## Proof
**Supporting forms.** Write \(Q=UH\). For each nonzero entry of \(P_*\), put \(s_{ij}=\operatorname{sgn}((P_*)_{ij})\). The row sign patterns are
\[
(+,+,+,-,-),\quad (+,+,+,+,0),\quad(+,+,+,+,+),\quad(-,+,+,+,+),\quad(-,0,+,+,+).
\]
Define seven real linear forms \(L_1,\ldots,L_7\) on \(H\) as the signed row sums of \(Q\): one form for each of rows \(1,3,4\), and two forms for row \(2\) (respectively row \(5\)) obtained by assigning \(+1\) and \(-1\) to its single zero-position sign. Order them as row \(1\), row \(2+\), row \(2-\), row \(3\), row \(4\), row \(5+\), row \(5-\). With \(g(H)=\max_{1\le k\le7}L_k(H)\), the elementary inequalities \(|a+b|\ge |a|+\operatorname{sgn}(a)b\) for \(a\ne0\), together with \(|b|\ge\pm b\), yield
\[
\|P_*+UH\|-r\ge g(H).\tag{1}
\]

**Positive dual identity and simplicial geometry.** Put
\[
(c_1,\ldots,c_7)=(104/659,\ 29/1318,\ 143/659,\ 120/659,\ 120/659,\ 29/1318,\ 143/659).
\]
These numbers are strictly positive and sum to one. Exact rational expansion gives the identity
\[
\sum_{k=1}^7c_kL_k(H)=\frac{416}{659}(HU)_{11}-\frac{13}{2636}(HU)_{12}+\frac{429}{659}(HU)_{22}=0.\tag{2}
\]
The four scalar constraints \(HU=0\) are independent. The eleven-by-ten coefficient matrix consisting of those four constraints and all seven forms has rank ten. Consequently the map \(H\mapsto(L_1(H),\ldots,L_7(H))\) is a linear isomorphism between the six-dimensional perturbation space and the hyperplane \(\sum c_kz_k=0\). In particular, \(g(H)>0\) for \(H\ne0\).

For each \(k\), let \(H^{(k)}\) be the unique perturbation satisfying \(HU=0\) and \(L_j(H^{(k)})=1\) for all \(j\ne k\). Equation (2) forces \(L_k(H^{(k)})=1-1/c_k\). For any \(H\ne0\), set \(z_j=L_j(H)/g(H)\) and \(\alpha_k=c_k(1-z_k)\). These coefficients are nonnegative, sum to one, and the isomorphism yields
\[
\frac{H}{g(H)}=\sum_{k=1}^7\alpha_kH^{(k)}.\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\{H:HU=0,\ g(H)\le1\}\) is exactly the simplex with these seven vertices.

**Exact vertex certificate.** Solving the ten rational linear equations for each omitted form and taking the maximum absolute row sum gives the following exact operator norms, in the stated order:
\[
\bigl(\|UH^{(1)}\|,\ldots,\|UH^{(7)}\|\bigr)
=\left(\frac{1013}{104},\frac{493257}{1508},\frac{9739}{572},\frac{2909}{104},\frac{2909}{104},\frac{493257}{1508},\frac{9739}{572}\right).\tag{4}
\]
All vertex systems are nonsingular; their exact determinants (with variables ordered rowwise) are
\[
-4056/15625,\quad 1131/31250,\quad-5577/15625,\quad936/3125,\quad-936/3125,\quad1131/31250,\quad-5577/15625.
\]
The accompanying checker reproduces all entries of (2)--(4) using exact rational linear algebra. The triangle inequality in (3) now proves
\[
\|UH\|\le \frac{493257}{1508}\,g(H).
\]
Combining with (1) proves the asserted global inequality. Strict positivity of \(g\) for nonzero \(H\) proves uniqueness of \(P_*\).

**Sharpness.** The vertex \(H^{(2)}\) has \(g(H^{(2)})=1\) and \(\|UH^{(2)}\|=493257/1508\). Its entries are
\[
H^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}
-996/29&-15465/754&7805/377&11965/377&-50435/754\\
2/13&-97945/3016&160395/1508&-162309/1508&8025/232
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For all sufficiently small \(t>0\), none of the nonzero entries of \(P_*+tUH^{(2)}\) changes sign, and both zero positions are treated by their two supporting forms. Hence
\[
\|P_*+tUH^{(2)}\|=r+t,\qquad\|tUH^{(2)}\|=t\,\frac{493257}{1508}.
\]
In particular, the exact choice \(t=1/100000\) is checked explicitly. Therefore no larger strong-uniqueness constant is possible.

## Relationship to prior work
Deregowska and Lewandowska, *Projection Constants and Banach-Mazur Distance in Codimension Two* (arXiv:2610.06141, first posted 2026-10-05), introduced the five-coordinate plane in their higher-codimension obstruction, proving an explicit projection bound \(13/10\). A subsequent deposited exact-norm result (opaque record identifier `1ca0f1dc-8910-4854-b55a-c6a0f0659375`) determines \(\lambda(V,\ell_\infty^5)=845/659\) and a minimizing projection. Neither the bound nor the exact norm is claimed as new here.

Strong uniqueness is classical: Deregowska and Lewandowska, *A note on generalized projections in c0* (2014), gives a general criterion and formulas in a coordinate-supported generalized-projection family. This five-coordinate plane is not of that family, and the cited formulas do not give the present sharp coefficient. Related work on strong uniqueness of minimal projections supplies general criteria but not this exact six-dimensional projection-affine-space modulus.

## Limitations
The result treats only real projections onto the specified plane in real \(\ell_\infty^5\). It does not assert uniqueness or a stability bound for higher-dimensional spaces built from this plane, nor for complex scalar projections. The norm estimate is a global, sharp Lipschitz-type error bound, not a statement that every near-minimizer is computable by a particular numerical algorithm. Literature comparison is best-knowledge rather than an independent priority certification; older general results on strongly unique minimal projections could require further human comparison.
