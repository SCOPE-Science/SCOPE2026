# Exact subbrace and ideal counts in products of cyclic prime-power braces

## Finding
For a prime \(p\) and integers \(n,m\ge2\), \(1\le k<n\), \(1\le \ell<m\), let \(R=R_{n,k}(p)\times R_{m,\ell}(p)\), where the first ring has additive group \(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) and product \(x*y=p^kxy\) modulo \(p^n\), and the second is analogous. Equip \(R\) with its radical-ring brace operation \(x\circ y=x+y+x*y\).

Put \(M_{a,b}=\min(n-a,m-b)\), \(s_{a,b}=\min(M_{a,b},k+a,\ell+b)\) for \(0\le a\le n\), \(0\le b\le m\). The numbers \(S\) of subbraces (including the zero and whole brace) and \(I\) of ideals are exactly
\[
S=\sum_{a=0}^{n}\sum_{b=0}^{m}\left[p^{s_{a,b}}+\mathbf1_{\{k+a=\ell+b\}}p^{k+a}\max(0,M_{a,b}-k-a)\right],
\]
\[
I=\sum_{a=0}^{n}\sum_{b=0}^{m}p^{\min(M_{a,b},k,\ell)}.
\]
Moreover, the proof identifies every subbrace and every ideal by a unique four-parameter additive subgroup. In particular, the exact number of subbraces which are not ideals is \(S-I\). The two component braces are individually Dedekind, yet their product is Dedekind exactly when
\[
(k>\ell\ \text{and}\ m=\ell+1)\quad\text{or}\quad(\ell>k\ \text{and}\ n=k+1).
\]
For example, at \(p=2\), the parameters \((n,m,k,\ell)=(3,2,2,1)\) give \((S,I)=(18,18)\), whereas \((4,3,2,1)\) gives \((38,32)\).

## Assumptions and scope
All rings here are associative, commutative and need not have a multiplicative identity. The indicated powers are literal integers, not residue-field exponents. Each component is nilpotent because its product raises \(p\)-adic valuation by at least one; consequently \(x\circ y=x+y+x*y\) makes \(R\) a finite two-sided brace. An additive subgroup is a subbrace if and only if it is closed under \(*\), and a brace ideal if and only if it is a ring ideal. The second equivalence uses \(\lambda_x(y)=y+x*y\) and the fact that both brace groups are abelian in the commutative radical-ring setting.

## Proof
**1. All additive subgroups, uniquely.** The projections of an additive subgroup \(H\le (R,+)\) are uniquely \(p^a\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) and \(p^b\mathbb Z/p^m\mathbb Z\). Goursat's lemma for these cyclic groups says that their quotients by the two coordinate kernels are cyclic of the same order \(p^c\), for a unique \(0\le c\le M_{a,b}\), related by an isomorphism given by multiplication by a unique unit \(t\bmod p^c\) when \(c\ge1\). Thus
\[
H_{a,b,c,t}=\{(p^a u,p^b v):u\equiv tv\pmod{p^c}\}.
\]
The numbers \(u,v\) range over the corresponding cyclic quotient coordinates; for \(c=0\) the congruence is vacuous and there is a single parameter value. Equivalently, \(H\) is additively generated by
\[
(p^{a+c},0),\quad(0,p^{b+c}),\quad(p^a t,p^b),
\]
with the evident interpretation for \(c=0\). These data are unique, so counting parameter choices does not overcount subgroups.

**2. Subbrace criterion.** Bilinearity reduces closure under multiplication to the products of these three generators. All are automatically in \(H\), except possibly the square of the last generator. Dividing its coordinates by \(p^a\) and \(p^b\) respectively gives the condition
\[
p^{k+a}t^2\equiv tp^{\ell+b}\pmod{p^c}.
\]
Since \(t\) is a unit when \(c\ge1\), this is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{p^{k+a}t\equiv p^{\ell+b}\pmod{p^c}.}
\]
For \(c=0\), it holds automatically. This is a necessary and sufficient condition, not just a test on examples.

**3. Exact count of subbraces.** Fix \(a,b\) and abbreviate \(M=M_{a,b}\), \(\alpha=k+a\), \(\beta=\ell+b\). For every \(c\le\min(M,\alpha,\beta)\), all \(\varphi(p^c)\) units work, with \(\varphi(1)=1\), and their sum is \(p^{\min(M,\alpha,\beta)}\). If \(c\) exceeds that minimum, a solution exists only if \(\alpha=\beta<c\). In that case the congruence becomes \(t\equiv1\pmod{p^{c-\alpha}}\), allowing exactly \(p^\alpha\) units for each of the \(M-\alpha\) additional values of \(c\). Summing gives exactly \(S\).

**4. Exact count of ideals.** A subbrace \(H\) is a ring ideal exactly when it absorbs multiplication by the two ambient additive generators \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). Their products with \((p^a t,p^b)\) give conditions \(p^kt\equiv0\pmod{p^c}\) and \(p^\ell\equiv0\pmod{p^c}\). Thus \(H\) is an ideal exactly when \(c\le\min(k,\ell)\), and the subbrace congruence is then automatic. Summation of the \(\varphi(p^c)\) admissible parameters for each \(a,b\) gives exactly \(I\).

**5. Sharp Dedekind boundary.** Each cyclic factor is Dedekind since every additive subgroup \(p^a\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) is an ideal. If \(k=\ell\), the additive diagonal generated by \((1,1)\) is a subring but is not an ideal: the element \((p^k,0)\) does not belong to it because \(n,m>k\). If \(k>\ell\) and \(m\ge\ell+2\), take
\[
H=\{(u,pv):u\equiv v\pmod{p^{\ell+1}}\}.
\]
It fits the classification with \((a,b,c,t)=(0,1,\ell+1,1)\); its product congruence holds because \(k\ge\ell+1\), but its ideal criterion fails since \(c>\ell\). The case \(\ell>k\), \(n\ge k+2\) is symmetric.

Conversely, assume \(k>\ell\), \(m=\ell+1\), and let \(H\) be any subbrace. For every \(h=(x,y)\in H\), the additive multiple \(p^kh=(p^kx,0)\) belongs to \(H\) since \(k\ge m\). Its square satisfies \(h*h=(p^kx^2,p^\ell y^2)\in H\). Subtracting \(x(p^kh)\) yields \((0,p^\ell y^2)\in H\). If \(p\mid y\), then \(p^\ell y=0\bmod p^m\); otherwise \(y\) is a unit and the additive cyclic groups generated by \(p^\ell y^2\) and \(p^\ell y\) coincide. In either case \((0,p^\ell y)\in H\). Thus both summands of \((u,v)*h=(up^kx,vp^\ell y)\) belong to \(H\) for every ambient \((u,v)\). Therefore every subbrace is an ideal. The case \(\ell>k\), \(n=k+1\) is symmetric. This proves the equivalence and the entire statement.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` enumerates every additive subgroup through explicit generating sets, tests generator products for closure and ideal absorption without using the formula to decide closure, and independently recomputes both closed-form counts. It tests all 45 parameter configurations with \(p=2\), \(2\le n,m\le4\), and \(p=3\), \(2\le n,m\le3\), as appropriate. For all 18 configurations with \(n,m\le3\), it also exhaustively compares generator-product tests to products of every pair of subgroup elements. The recorded output is `VERIFY_OK cases=45 exhaustive_cases=18 subgroup_patterns=2267 example_good=(18, 18) example_bad=(38, 32)`. These finite tests are corroborative; the universal proof above is algebraic.

## Relationship to prior work
Caranti, Del Corso, Di Matteo, Ferrara and Trombetti, *On Dedekind Skew Braces* (arXiv:2507.23550, first posted July 31, 2025), establish in Theorem 4.1 that finite prime-power skew braces with cyclic additive or multiplicative group are Dedekind. This motivates examining their products. Ballester-Bolinches, Esteban-Romero, Kurdachenko and Pérez-Calabuig, *On left braces in which every subbrace is an ideal* (arXiv:2405.04213), develop structural Dedekind-brace theory and discuss non-Dedekind small examples, not the present four-parameter exact subbrace and ideal census.

The purely ring-theoretic question whether **every** subring is an ideal predates brace theory: R. L. Kruse's *Rings in which all Subrings are Ideals. I* (1968), DOI 10.4153/CJM-1968-083-1, together with its sequel classifies Hamiltonian rings. Thus the Dedekind yes/no boundary alone is not claimed as a historically independent classification problem. The claim assessed here is the *exact total number* of all subbraces and ideals in this natural infinite family, including a unique parametrization and a quantitative defect \(S-I\); this does not follow as a numerical formula merely from the H-ring classification. Earlier ring theory can imply qualitative special cases.

## Limitations
The enumeration concerns direct products of exactly two **specified** cyclic radical rings over the **same** prime. It is not a classification of all rank-two braces or of all Hamiltonian rings. The number \(S-I\) counts actual subbraces, not conjugacy or isomorphism classes. No claim is made for noncommutative skew braces or products over several distinct primes. The historical novelty assessment remains qualified: older complete subgroup-lattice classifications could conceivably contain an equivalent weighted count, and the sequel to Kruse's paper was not fully inspected.
