# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact claim concerns every fixed-point-free involution on every even-sized labeled alphabet. The proof separately establishes the arch-count interpretation of the subsequence universality index, a necessary-and-sufficient midpoint alphabet-cover criterion, the surjection/Stirling counting functions, and the sharp minimal-length counts. The four-letter rational specialization was independently simplified symbolically. The attached verifier enumerates 189,008 half-words across alphabet sizes two, four, six, and eight and checks the complete finite histograms against the coefficient formula. These finite checks are supplementary and do not substitute for the all-length proof. The assumptions exclude fixed letters and odd-length reverse-complement symmetry.

## Originality — PASS, with stated bibliographic risk
Barker et al., *Scattered Factor-Universality of Words* (arXiv:2003.04629, Section 4), give the closest pointwise antecedents: ordinary palindromes and concatenation under alphabet permutations. Those results establish neither the full distribution for fixed-point-free reverse-complement symmetry nor the four-letter rational specialization or exact shortest-word counts. Adamson et al., *k-Universality of Regular Languages* (arXiv:2311.10658, Section 4), develop generic regular-language counting algorithms, not an explicit enumeration of the nonregular symmetry family. The public record *Closed-form law for binary subsequence universality* gives an unrestricted binary count; direct inspection found no involutive-symmetry claim or arbitrary-even-alphabet formula. Relevant published comparisons and searches for aliases, reductions, generating functions, and shortest-word parameters found no dominating statement. The midpoint method is related to known arch arguments and is not portrayed as an independently invented technique; overlooked equivalent work remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Reverse-complement symmetry is a natural constraint on finite words, including four-letter DNA-style alphabets. Its exact all-length subsequence-universality distribution is a complete counting invariant, not a finite sample or a generic automata decision algorithm. The minimal-length enumeration makes a sharp quantitative distinction between even and odd universality indices for every paired alphabet; in four letters it gives the explicit sequence \(8,24,192,576\) at the first four levels. The exact formulas are reproducible and provide a benchmark for symmetric subsequence-rich constructions without claiming empirical DNA performance.
