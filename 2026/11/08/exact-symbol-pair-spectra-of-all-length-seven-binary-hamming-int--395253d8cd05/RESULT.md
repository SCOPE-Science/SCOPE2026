# Exact symbol-pair spectra of all length-seven binary Hamming interleavers

## Finding
Let \(C(H)=\{x\in\mathbb F_2^7:Hx^\mathsf T=0\}\), where the seven columns of \(H\) run once through all nonzero vectors in \(\mathbb F_2^3\), in an arbitrary order. Regard the seven positions as a labeled cycle and put \(w_2(x)=|\{i\in\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z:(x_i,x_{i+1})\ne(0,0)\}|\). There are exactly thirty distinct indexed codes \(C(H)\). Their complete cyclic symbol-pair weight enumerators \(W_H(z)=\sum_{x\in C(H)}z^{w_2(x)}\) are exactly
\[
W_a(z)=1+a z^4+(7-a)z^5+(7-a)z^6+(a+1)z^7,\qquad a\in\{0,1,2\},
\]
with multiplicities respectively \(2,14,14\) for \(a=0,1,2\). Consequently, exactly two of the thirty indexed Hamming codes have minimum cyclic pair distance five; the other twenty-eight have minimum distance four. Since the symbol-pair Singleton bound gives \(|C|\le 2^{7-d_2+2}\), the first two (and only those two) are MDS symbol-pair presentations of this code. Equivalently, among the 360 unoriented Hamilton cycles on seven fixed Fano points, precisely \(24,168,168\) have respectively \(0,1,2\) consecutive Fano-line three-windows. Thus an arbitrary ordering of a fixed \([7,4,3]\) Hamming code has optimal pair distance five in exactly \(24\) out of \(360\) cyclic orders considered modulo rotation and reversal.

For reproducible examples, encode the seven nonzero parity-check columns by integers from \(1\) to \(7\) using their binary digits. Representatives of the three spectrum classes are:

| \(a\) | Ordered parity-check columns | Number of indexed codes | Minimum pair distance |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | `1,2,4,3,6,7,5` | 2 | 5 |
| 1 | `1,2,3,4,6,5,7` | 14 | 4 |
| 2 | `1,2,3,4,5,6,7` | 14 | 4 |

## Assumptions and scope
All statements concern the binary length-seven \([7,4,3]\) Hamming code, cyclic pairs including the wraparound pair, and the thirty *distinct indexed linear codes* (not all \(7!\) redundant parity-check matrices). An unoriented cycle is a permutation of seven fixed Fano points modulo the dihedral group of order \(14\), without Fano relabeling. Set \(P=\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\}\); a Fano line is a three-point set \(\{u,v,u+v\}\) for distinct \(u,v\in P\). Write \(a\) for the number of Fano lines whose points form three consecutive vertices somewhere in the chosen cyclic ordering.

## Proof
**Codewords and pair weights.** Since the parity-check columns are precisely \(P\), a weight-three word belongs to the nullspace precisely when its support is a Fano line. There are seven such lines. The xor of all seven nonzero vectors is zero, so the all-one word belongs to the code. Complementation therefore identifies the seven weight-three words with the seven weight-four words. Along with the zero word and the all-one word these exhaust the \(2^4=16\) codewords (rank of \(H\) is three). For a line support \(L\), let \(t(L)\in\{0,1,2\}\) be the number of cyclic *edges* whose two endpoints both lie in \(L\). Its weight-three indicator word has six endpoint incidences less one for each such internal edge, and hence pair weight \(6-t(L)\). The complementary weight-four word has zeros exactly on \(L\), and so has pair weight \(7-t(L)\).

**Only three spectra.** Every one of the seven edges of the vertex seven-cycle lies on precisely one Fano line, because each pair of points specifies a unique line. Consequently, if \(b_j=|\{L:t(L)=j\}|\), then \(b_0+b_1+b_2=7\) and \(b_1+2b_2=7\), whence \(b_0=b_2=a\), \(b_1=7-2a\). A Fano line with two internal cycle edges is necessarily one of the cyclic triples of consecutive points. Any two Fano lines intersect in exactly one point. Fixing a cyclic triple in positions \(0,1,2\), any other length-three cyclic interval meeting it in exactly one vertex must occupy positions \(2,3,4\) or \(5,6,0\). Those latter two triples are disjoint, so they cannot both be Fano lines. Thus \(a\le2\). The preceding pair-weight identities yield exactly the polynomial displayed above.

**Count the interleavers.** There are \(7!/14=360\) seven-point cycles up to rotation and reversal. Each fixed Fano line occupies a consecutive three-position block in \(3!4!/2=72\) such cycles: order its three vertices within the block and cyclically arrange this block with the four remaining labeled vertices, dividing by reversal. Hence \(\sum a=7\cdot72=504\) over all cycles. For any two distinct lines, they meet in a unique point. If both are consecutive triples, their three-position arcs meet at the same endpoint and form a five-position block; there are \(2\) choices for which line is on the left, \(2\) orders for each line's remaining pair, and \(2\) orders for the two outside vertices, divided by \(2\) for reversal. Therefore exactly \(8\) unoriented cycles contain any specified pair of line-blocks, and \(\sum\binom a2=\binom72\cdot8=168\). Let \(N_j\) count cycles with \(a=j\). Since \(a\le2\), \(N_2=168\), \(N_1+2N_2=504\), and \(N_0+N_1+N_2=360\). Thus \((N_0,N_1,N_2)=(24,168,168)\).

**Pass to indexed codes.** Each unoriented cycle represents exactly \(14\) ordered lists of the seven Fano points, so the \(7!\) parity-check column lists split as \((336,2352,2352)\). Two such lists define the same nullspace exactly when their parity-check matrices differ by an invertible three-row transformation. The group has order \(|\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)|=(7)(6)(4)=168\), and acts freely on these lists. Because a row transformation preserves the nullspace and its pair enumerator, division by \(168\) gives precisely \((2,14,14)\) distinct indexed codes of each enumerator. In \(W_a\), the smallest positive exponent is five when \(a=0\), and four otherwise. Finally, for \(|C|=16\), the symbol-pair Singleton inequality \(|C|\le2^{7-d_2+2}\) rules out distance greater than five; hence the two distance-five presentations are exactly the MDS presentations. This completes all assertions.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` enumerates all \(5040\) column orderings, explicitly computes each of the \(16\) codewords by the parity equation, directly measures all cyclic pair weights, deduplicates actual indexed codeword sets, and checks all thirty spectra and their multiplicities. The independent `verify_fano.py` enumerates all \(360\) cycles and their line triples, verifies the moment counts, and enumerates the complete Fano automorphism action. Both use only Python's standard library. Execute `python verify.py` and `python verify_fano.py`; successful runs print `DIRECT_VERIFY_OK` and `FANO_VERIFY_OK` respectively. These finite checks corroborate the complete, independent proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Cassuto and Blaum introduced the symbol-pair read channel (2010), and Chee et al. (arXiv:1211.1728, first posted 2012-11-07) derive its basic metric and Singleton bound. Jha, Parampalli, and Singh (arXiv:2409.10979, 2024, Section 3) explicitly identify a cyclic \([7,4,3]\) Hamming code with pair distance five, but do not enumerate all thirty indexed presentations and their complete weight spectra. Known general Hamming-code and Fano-plane descriptions supply the incidence structure; the moment computation and exhaustive distribution among all cyclic interleavings are the present result. An indexed length-eight extended Hamming-code spectrum classification is a different problem: puncturing changes cyclic adjacency, and its five aggregate enumerator types do not imply these length-seven spectra or multiplicities.

## Limitations
This is a complete finite classification for seven coordinates, not an all-length Hamming-code formula or a new coding-bound improvement. It does not establish performance in a particular physical channel, characterize decoding complexity, or settle analogous distributions for higher-order window reads. The exact historical priority of the 24/168/168 Fano-cycle incidence count remains subject to independent exhaustive historical review; the consulted papers and indexed results did not show this count. Source access for the 2010 original conference paper was limited to public abstract and metadata; a different, fully accessible 2012 paper supplies the formal metric used here. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
