# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The object and quantifiers are explicit: any Hermitian two-level Hamiltonian, nonzero coupling between orthogonal preparation and detector states, positive Poisson measurement rate and both return/transition preparations. The transition and return Laplace transforms are derived directly from the exact failed-projector renewal. The cubic discriminant stays negative for the entire parameter range, establishing a unique real pole, strict spectral dominance, and positive leading tail coefficients. Solving the pole stationarity conditions gives the unique maximizing rate; endpoint bounds rule out other optima. Independent no-click-generator calculations and 448 finite checker assertions support the algebra, without replacing the proof.

## Originality — PASS (priority qualified)

Kulkarni–Majumdar, arXiv:2305.15123, supplies the renewal transform, the resonant Jaynes–Cummings cubic and qualitative/numerical existence of the late-tail optimal rate; the claim does not appropriate these. The full-text Sections 4, 5.1 and 5.2 do not state the explicit optimization constant or the all-detuning solution. Kessler–Barkai–Ziegler, arXiv:2012.01763, treats random probing, means and second moments; Ziegler–Barkai–Kessler, arXiv:2012.15182, addresses quantized mean returns. No inspected primary comparison states the present unique analytic tail design for all coupled qubits and both protocols. A highly relevant published renewal-optimization record concerns a different fully connected model and mean rather than exponential-tail optimization. The major residual priority risk is implicit derivability from the earlier cubic, not the absence of a matching title.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The primary paper explicitly identifies the slowest tail scale as a separate optimization target but leaves its minimizer graphical/implicit in its resonant example. The present result closes that control problem by proving an exact, global and detuning-uniform optimum, quantifying how it differs from mean-time optimization and establishing the shared tail design for return detection even when the return mean has no finite minimizer. The parameter is physically natural (Hamiltonian transition visibility), and the precise target is useful when controlling asymptotically rare delays. The bound is numerically modest (at most about six percent rate shift); its value is the exact rigorous separation of two operational criteria, not a claim of large practical performance gains. No assertion is made for finite-quantile optimality or general measurement noise.
