# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The reversible paired-row matrix is invertible. Its exact characteristic polynomial is the square of the length-\(n+1\) geometric sum. Every path-adjacency eigenspace has geometric multiplicity one, yielding cyclicity of the paired-row matrix. A cyclic-module gcd calculation gives the exact kernel dimension and all fixed counts; the Jordan multiplicities establish sharp global order. Both included exact computational checks pass (1,116 binary rank comparisons and exhaustive states for seven path sizes). No unproved asymptotic extrapolation is used.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Macfarlane's 2004 full paper, especially the introduction, model equations, RCA3 section, and final remarks, analyzes single-seed weight dynamics in related reversible second-order models; its RCA3 rule includes the center site. It does not supply finite open-path paired-row orbit counts. Tadaki's 1993 preprint (full text inspected, including the rule-90 Dirichlet setup, spectral analysis, and conclusion) concerns first-order finite linear CA; the 2015 finite reversibility study uses periodic spatial boundaries. Mathematical-record searches for open-boundary second-order Rule90, fixed-point formulas, and cyclic-module equivalences produced no stronger covering result; a record on finite-support periods for reversible rule 115 concerns a different rule. A prior direct algebraic equivalence remains possible, as the polynomial and cyclic-module methods are classical.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result classifies *all* primitive temporal orbits for *every* finite open path, not a single small-system count or just the period upper bound. The formula resolves how the odd gcd and binary valuations of the spatial length and temporal exponent jointly determine recurrence. Full periodic-orbit populations are natural dynamical invariants and the sharp order gives a measurable all-state horizon. The value lies in an explicit infinite-family census, not in originality of the standard linear-algebra tools.
