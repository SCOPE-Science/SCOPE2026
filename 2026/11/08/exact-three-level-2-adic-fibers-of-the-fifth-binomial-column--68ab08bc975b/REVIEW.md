# Scientific review: fifth binomial column

**Correctness — PASS.** Vandermonde's identity gives the exact period \(2^{k+2}\); a half-period counterexample gives minimality. An explicit eight-row polynomial expansion yields four odd-unit linear 2-adic isometry branches with depth one, two with depth two, and two with depth three. These branch images imply the exact Haar density and all finite fiber counts, including the exceptional initial levels. The standalone exact-rational verifier independently checks every table coefficient and enumerates all fibers for twelve levels. Infinite claims rely on the algebraic argument, not finite testing.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Sun–Zhang, arXiv:0812.3089, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 assert coverage/density for fixed binomial columns but do not provide the complete residue counts. OEIS A349593 and A385557 already determine the exact period, which is **not claimed to be new**. Barat–Grabner's author-hosted manuscript, Sections 3–4, studies asymptotic distribution with both indices varying in Pascal's triangle and does not imply this vertical-column law. Separate indexed work on the third column finds only a two-level distribution, not the three-level fifth-column theorem. Searches for fixed fifth-column residue counts, equivalent p-adic pushforward densities and stronger exact counting tables found no covering result. An equivalent result in unindexed older sources cannot be ruled out.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The universal-image question for fixed binomial columns is explicit in the archival literature. This theorem refines that qualitative statement to an exact all-level law and proves a new change in the number of output-density strata: uniform for the first, second and fourth columns; two-level for the third; three-level for the fifth. The exact period and branch partition provide a reusable structural model for local distribution questions. The result is not claimed to settle general indices or odd primes.
