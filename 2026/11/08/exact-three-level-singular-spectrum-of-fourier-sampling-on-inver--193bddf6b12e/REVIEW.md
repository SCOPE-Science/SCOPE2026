# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact domain consists of all Hermitian two-by-two matrices over quadratic finite-field extensions, including characteristic two. The trace pairing is nondegenerate and conjugate by the determinant-preserving adjugate to the polar form of the determinant. A norm-preimage character sum proves the three Fourier values of the determinant-zero cone; its \(q^2+1\) mutually nonorthogonal isotropic lines yield three exact Gram eigenvalue levels. The complementary blocks of a normalized finite-field Fourier unitary then give the claimed singular values on its invertible complement with correct multiplicities and no zero. The incidence block calculation and q=2,3,4,5 checker verify boundaries. No extrapolation from finite experiments is used.

## Originality — PASS with residual historical risk

The exact self-restricted square Hermitian Fourier operator and its condition number are different from finite-field quadratic-extension bounds (Iosevich–Koh) and from the complete-space rank-metric Hermitian translation-scheme eigenmatrix (Stanton; Schmidt). These sources give inputs to the proof but not the two-sided principal-block singular spectrum; the new incidence reduction and unitary-complement comparison supply it. Related first-coordinate sampling and split-cone first-band studies use different operators and cannot imply the complete spectrum here. The inaccessible full Stanton article could contain a broader consequence, and complete historical priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The invertible locus of a natural rank stratification is a canonical, critically sampled Fourier domain; injectivity and exact conditioning matter independently of the cone's one-sided Fourier coefficients. The result gives the full infinite-family singular spectrum, uniform invertibility across all prime powers, including characteristic two, and a sharp quantitative stability factor \(q\). It distinguishes a two-sided reconstruction statement from an already-known translation-scheme character sum.
