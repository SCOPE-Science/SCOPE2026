# Mathematical review

## Correctness (C): PASS
The claim is a full singular spectrum of a precisely defined square additive Fourier matrix, not a spectrum of the nonabelian group Fourier transform. The Fourier coefficients of the invertible locus at ranks zero, one and two are derived by exact elementary counting. The identity \(HH^*=q^4I-q^2C_W/(q-1)+qJ\) follows by substitution of those coefficients. An elementary commutant calculation proves the nonzero-vector permutation representation decomposes multiplicity-free into irreducibles of dimensions \(1\), \(q\) and \(q+1\), the latter repeated over distinct scalar-central characters. Schur orthogonality yields all Gram eigenvalues, their complete multiplicities and strict positivity. The proof treats \(q=2\) explicitly as the case with no middle sector. A standalone independent direct-matrix checker matches all levels for \(q=2,3,4,5\), including \(\mathbb F_4\); the proof, not the finite checks, establishes the universal quantifier.

## Originality (O): PASS under bounded historical comparison
The rank-stratum Fourier sums are existing theory, and the representation decomposition is classical. Full sections of Gnedin–Kerov (arXiv:math/0406614) and Prasad (arXiv:0712.4051) were read for those ingredients; the relevant rank-character analysis of Gurevich–Howe (arXiv:2107.02240) was also inspected. These primary works establish ingredients, not an exact self-restricted additive Fourier singular spectrum on the full invertible locus. The closest inspected published matrix Fourier-band spectra consider only first-coordinate bands on the rank-one locus or determinant-one locus, not a square Fourier operator on every invertible matrix. Exact aliases and wider coverage were checked through mathematical-database searches and primary-source comparison. The original 1978 Delsarte article was not available for a full-text read; its abstract and later rank-scheme formulas present a genuine residual priority risk. No absolute uniqueness or priority certification is asserted.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
The full invertible locus is a natural, large critical-sampling set; its restriction to both spatial and frequency variables is a substantially harder and more informative object than a coordinate sub-band. The result gives exact stable reconstruction and sharp conditioning in every finite field, revealing only three attenuation scales and their exact dimensions. It is a complete natural singular-value classification rather than an arbitrary finite cutoff or cosmetic reparameterization. Its limitations are restricted matrix size and lack of eigenphases, not any missing proof of the claimed singular magnitudes.

## Closest literature and residual risks
- P. Delsarte (1978) and Gurevich–Howe (2021): general rank-scheme Fourier coefficients; a stronger uninspected Delsarte result might subsume the present specialization.
- Gnedin–Kerov (2004) and Prasad (2007): classical representation-theoretic ingredients used, rather than new claims about general linear group characters.
- Published finite-field rank-one coordinate-band and determinant-one coordinate-band Fourier spectra: distinct sampling sets and smaller Fourier bands; no stated implication of the full \(N\)-by-\(N\) singular-value list.
