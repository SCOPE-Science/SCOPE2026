The exact algebraic enumeration determines all \(90\) circle crossings, \(92\) chambers, \(30\) edges and the \(20/60/12\) chamber-to-section incidence pattern, including the full normal-angle signatures. Every sign decision is exact in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]\). The spherical excess calculation then gives the three probabilities and checks their total. ## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

The full published text of Glisse–Lazard's 2007 silhouette paper yields a general expectation formula, not this full exact distribution. The public full result on central sections of the regular icosahedron has different input and gives only a ten-/twelve-vertex law; polarity transforms it into a *dodecahedron projection*, not the present *dodecahedron section*. Existing icosahedron projection illustrations enumerate possible shapes but give no Haar probabilities. The previously described 92-face rectified-truncated-icosahedral polyhedron has matching combinatorial face counts; that fact is acknowledged, and no originality is claimed for the raw tessellation multiplicities. The present mathematical distinction is the chamber-to-section incidence plus exact solid angles and distribution. Unindexed stereology or older spherical tessellation literature may already contain the law; that risk remains unresolved by the searchable sources.

This is a complete, natural three-point probability law for central sections of one of the five regular convex solids, equally interpretable as the projected-outline complexity of its polar. It is not a numerical lookup or a restatement of a known expected vertex count. The exact conditional probabilities quantify separate hexagon, octagon and decagon events, with direct geometric interpretation and reproducible proof.
