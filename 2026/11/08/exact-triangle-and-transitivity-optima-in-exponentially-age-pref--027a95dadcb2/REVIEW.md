# Research assessment: exact motif-density and transitivity optima

## Correctness — PASS
The object is the simple projection of the fixed-parameter, two-parent independent exponential-age attachment graph, not the original multigraph when counting wedges or triangles. Each vertex has at most one new triangle because it chooses at most two distinct older neighbors. Independence across arrivals makes the exact triangle intensity \(\sum_{a,b\ge1}2p_a p_{a+b}(2p_b-p_b^2)\). For centered triples, separate independent older and younger simple neighbor counts and evaluate the first two factorial moments of the Poisson-binomial incoming degree. The algebra agrees with the stated rational functions. A stationary two-sided geometric-age coupling differs from the finite graph at finitely many edges almost surely; a finite-range truncation, Birkhoff's theorem and monotone convergence establish both almost-sure limits. The derivative polynomials and sign changes establish distinct strict global maxima. The standalone exact-rational checker passed 58 tests, including direct enumeration of all ordered parent-choice histories through six vertices at three rational parameters. The finite checks are corroboration, not proof of the infinite statements.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical-priority assessment
The earliest dated motivating source is Aguech et al., *Exponentially preferential trees* (2025-04-14), which has one parent per vertex and determines degree behavior, but cannot yield nonzero simple-graph triangle counts. The finding asks for clustering of its natural two-parent extension and gives exact optimized formulas. Pachon–Stephenson (2025-04-03) construct triangles by an affine-degree/edge mechanism rather than independent geometric-age parent choices, so their clustering theorem does not imply the formulas. Tang–Wang–Wu (2026-11-02), independently describe the two-parent multi-edge model but prove recurrence and infinite collisions rather than the two optimized motif laws; their complete text was used only as a comparative inspection. An accessible earlier result on the same extension concerns maximum degree, not motif counts. Targeted published-literature, semantic mathematical-record and accessible-prior-claim comparisons yielded no equivalent or stronger theorem. This is not a guarantee that no unindexed or unpublished prior result exists.

## Mathematical value — PASS
In the smallest non-tree extension of a natural recently studied growth mechanism, both triangle intensity and global transitivity are canonical network statistics. Unlike a routine finite expectation for a selected graph, the theorem supplies almost-sure limits at every fixed age bias, exact rational formulae, and sharp distinct universal optimizers of two competing clustering objectives. It is meaningful that maximizing the raw number of triangles requires a weaker age bias than maximizing the share of closed connected triples, even though the graph is recurrent for every positive age-bias strength. A full fluctuation theorem or higher-parent classification is neither asserted nor needed for this exact structural distinction.
