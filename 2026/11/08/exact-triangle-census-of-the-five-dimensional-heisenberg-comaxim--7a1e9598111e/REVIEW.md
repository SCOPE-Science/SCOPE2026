# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The dimension-pattern decomposition is exhaustive because every two quotient subspaces must span dimension four. Each rank-pattern contribution is counted explicitly. The subtle rank-two triple term is derived by counting two-plane complements via invertible linear maps, and three Lagrangian complements via invertible symmetric 2-by-2 matrices; the latter count remains valid in characteristic two. Direct finite quotient-subspace enumeration over two prime fields checks every triple independently of the final formula. The symbolic proof, not finite samples, covers all finite fields.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The 2026 primary articles cover three-dimensional triangle counts, selected four-dimensional graphs, and general quotient blow-ups; they do not state the five-dimensional triangle polynomial. The prior explicit higher-dimensional Heisenberg weighted graph and edge enumeration is a structural starting point but does not explicitly evaluate its triangles; this result requires a new nontrivial weighted triple count, particularly the symmetric-invertible-matrix correction. Searches for equivalent triangle counts, aliases, and specific numeric table entries did not locate an equivalent formula. Earlier unindexed calculations remain possible, and a full graph description logically determines its triangles without itself evaluating them.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The 2026 motivating paper specifically studies triangle counts as a natural invariant, making the first five-dimensional Heisenberg case a mathematically motivated next boundary. A closed polynomial valid for all prime powers, including characteristic two, distinguishes five rank patterns and reduces its delicate all-Lagrangian contribution to a classical but nontrivial symmetric-matrix count. This is neither an arbitrary parameter slice nor a mere numeric table for one field.
