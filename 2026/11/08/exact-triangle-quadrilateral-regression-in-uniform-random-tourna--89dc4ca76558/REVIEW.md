# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The complete proof partitions the covariance contributions by overlap of two triples, a triple and a quadruple, and two quadruples. The crucial local probabilities are \(\Pr(\text{cyclic triple})=1/4\), \(\Pr(\text{strong quadruple})=3/8\), and conditional strong-quadruple probabilities \(3/4\) versus \(1/4\) for a cyclic versus transitive overlapping triple. The only nonzero mixed covariance comes from nested triple–quadruple pairs. The only off-diagonal strong-quadruple covariances come from three-vertex overlaps. Algebra yields the stated finite moments, regression slope and residual variance. A standalone exact rational complete enumeration through six vertices confirmed each formula; the infinite range follows from the overlap proof, not the enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification

The nearby primary statements concern asymptotic subgraph-count distributions (Andersson 1998), deterministic four-vertex tournament identities and extremal bounds (Linial–Morgenstern 2014), four-vertex tournament densities (Coregliano 2015), and extremal 3-/4-cycle density constraints (Chan et al. 2019). The inspected full arXiv texts of the latter two do not state the exact random finite covariance, Pearson correlation or residual-variance fraction. The accessible Andersson material is limited to the abstract and citations; its general theory might contain an implicit or explicit version and was not conclusively excluded. This limitation is a real historical-priority risk, rather than a claim of exhaustive novelty.

## Value — PASS

Directed triangles and four-cycles are canonical tournament motifs, and their joint behavior is a natural null-model statistic. Exact variance explained \((4n-12)/(4n-11)\) and the residual fraction \(1/(4n-11)\) are a sharp structural dependence law across all graph orders, not an arbitrary finite coefficient. The result supplies a precise analytic benchmark for using three-cycle counts to predict four-cycle counts in random tournaments.
