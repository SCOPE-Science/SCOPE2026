# Exact two-peak covering-radius profile of a planar parallelogram interpolation

## Finding
For the two planar, unit-area, lattice-covering parallelograms
\[
 P=\{a(1,0)+b(1/2,1):0\le a,b\le1\},\qquad
 Q=\{a(1,1)+b(-3/2,-1/2):0\le a,b\le1\},
\]
put \(K_t=(1-t)P+tQ\) for \(0\le t\le1\), and let \(\mu(K_t)\) be the covering radius with respect to \(\mathbb Z^2\). Writing \(s=\min\{t,1-t\}\), the entire inverse-covering-radius profile is
\[
 \boxed{\mu(K_t)^{-1}=\min\left\{1+\frac{s}{14},\,\frac76-\frac{s}{2}\right\}.}
\]
It follows that the profile has exactly two strict local (and global) maxima, both \(49/48\), at \(t=7/24\) and \(t=17/24\), and precisely one strict interior local minimum, \(11/12\), at \(t=1/2\); the endpoint values are \(1\). The corresponding covering radii at the two optimal parameters and at the midpoint are \(48/49\) and \(12/11\), respectively. This exactly quantifies the nonconcavity of the second parallelogram pair highlighted in the motivating paper.

## Assumptions and scope
For a full-dimensional compact convex set \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\),
\[
\mu(K)=\inf\{r>0:rK+\mathbb Z^2=\mathbb R^2\}.
\]
Translation of \(K\) does not affect this quantity. The result is only for the explicitly specified pair \(P,Q\), not arbitrary covering pairs, and makes no assertion that either of the two maximizers is unique among unrelated families. Both endpoints satisfy \(\mu(P)=\mu(Q)=1\).

## Proof
**1. Symmetry and facet data.** Subtract the centers of \(P,Q\); their interpolation is the centrally symmetric zonotope generated by the four vectors
\[
v_1=(1-t,0),\quad v_2=((1-t)/2,1-t),\quad
v_3=(t,t),\quad v_4=(-3t/2,-t/2).
\]
The integer unimodular matrix
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad M^2=-I,
\]
interchanges the two centered parallelograms (up to central reflection), and preserves \(\mathbb Z^2\). Thus \(\mu(K_t)=\mu(K_{1-t})\). It suffices to prove the formula for \(0\le t\le1/2\).

The supports of the zonotope in the four normal directions \((0,1),(1,-3),(1,-1),(2,-1)\) give its exact facet inequalities
\[
 |y|\le A,\quad |x-3y|\le B,\quad |x-y|\le C,\quad |2x-y|\le D,
\]
where
\[
 A=\frac{2+t}{4},\quad B=\frac{7-3t}{4},\quad
 C=\frac{3-t}{4},\quad D=\frac{4+3t}{4}.
\]
Indeed the support in normal \(w\) is \(\frac12\sum_{i=1}^4|\langle w,v_i\rangle|\). These are the four facet-normal pairs of the full-dimensional zonotope when \(0<t<1\), while at endpoints some are redundant and the same inequality system remains exact.

**2. Upper covering certificate.** Define
\[
 r_a=\frac{14}{14+t},\qquad r_b=\frac6{7-3t},\qquad r=\max\{r_a,r_b\}.
\]
We prove \(rK_t+\mathbb Z^2=\mathbb R^2\). For \(|y|\le rA\), the horizontal section of \(rK_t\) is the interval \(I(y)=[L(y),U(y)]\), with
\[
 L(y)=\max\{3y-rB,\ y-rC,\ (y-rD)/2\},\quad
 U(y)=\min\{3y+rB,\ y+rC,\ (y+rD)/2\}.
\]
Let \(W(y)=U(y)-L(y)\). Convexity and central symmetry make \(W\) concave and even on \([-rA,rA]\); consequently it is nonincreasing on \([0,rA]\).

Put \(y_0=1-rA\) and \(y_L=r(B-C)/2\). Direct substitution on \(0\le t\le1/2\) yields
\[
 \frac12\le rA<1,\quad 0\le y_0,y_L\le\frac12,\quad
 rD\ge1,\quad y_0\ge r(D-2C),\quad
 y_L\ge\frac{r(2B-D)}5.
\]
Also \(r\ge r_a\), and the explicit interval endpoints give
\[
 W(y_0)=\min\left\{r(7/2+t/4)-5/2,\,
 r(3/2+t/4)-1/2,\,rD\right\}\ge1,
\]
\[
 W(y_L)=r(1+t/4)\ge1.
\]
The displayed elementary inequalities follow, respectively, by multiplying through by the positive denominators \(14+t\) and \(7-3t\); they include \(t=0\) and \(t=1/2\). Hence \(W(y)\ge1\) for \(0\le y\le\max\{y_0,y_L\}\). At such heights, the intervals attached to lattice row \(0\), translated by integer horizontal distances, already cover the entire horizontal line.

For \(\max\{y_0,y_L\}\le y\le1/2\), both lattice rows at heights \(0\) and \(1\) are active. The bounds above, together with \(1-y\ge1/2\ge y_L\), show that their intervals have endpoints
\[
 U(y)=\frac{y+rD}{2},\quad L(y)=3y-rB,
\]
\[
 U(y-1)=3(y-1)+rB,\quad L(y-1)=-\frac{1-y+rD}{2}.
\]
The two gaps on the horizontal circle satisfy
\[
 U(y)-L(y-1)=\frac12+rD\ge1,
 \qquad U(y-1)-L(y)=2rB-3\ge0.
\]
These inequalities guarantee that the union of the two intervals and their integer horizontal translates covers the line. Symmetry in \(y\) and translation by integer vertical distances then cover every point in the plane. This proves \(\mu(K_t)\le r\).

**3. Matching lower bounds.** A direct comparison gives \(r_a\ge r_b\) exactly for \(0\le t\le7/24\). In this range, at radius \(r_a\), the row \(0\) section at height \(y_0=1-r_aA\) has width exactly one, with the first term in the minimum for \(W(y_0)\) active. For any \(r'<r_a\) sufficiently close, put \(y'_0=1-r'A\). By continuity, at a height just below \(y'_0\), only row \(0\) is active, but its section has width strictly less than one, because
\[
 W(y'_0)\le r'(7/2+t/4)-5/2<1.
\]
Consequently integer horizontal translates leave a gap, so \(r'K_t+\mathbb Z^2\ne\mathbb R^2\). Covering is monotone in radius (after centering), hence \(\mu(K_t)\ge r_a\). At \(t=0\) the same conclusion follows from the lattice-fundamental parallelogram and its area.

For \(7/24\le t\le1/2\), put \(r=r_b\). The slice at height \(1/2\) has
\[
 I(1/2)=[0,u],\qquad I(-1/2)=[-u,0],
 \qquad u=(1/2+rD)/2\le1.
\]
The inequality is equivalent to \(t\le1/2\). At every \(r'<r_b\) sufficiently close, the first interval is strictly inside \((0,1)\) and the second inside \((-1,0)\). These are the only active vertical lattice rows, and neither their integer horizontal translates contains \(x=0\). Thus \((0,1/2)\) is uncovered, proving \(\mu(K_t)\ge r_b\).

Combining both lower bounds with the upper certificate yields \(\mu(K_t)=\max\{r_a,r_b\}\). Taking reciprocals and applying \(t\leftrightarrow1-t\) proves the displayed formula. The two affine branches cross at \(t=7/24\), with common value \(49/48\), and the unique central trough and endpoint values follow by inspection.

## Verification
The supplementary file `verify_profile.py` is a pure-standard-library exact-rational checker, executable as `python verify_profile.py`. It independently checks the facet inequalities, critical scales, branch switch, and row-covering certificates at twelve rational parameters, together with 120,000 rational horizontal-slice point tests and 15,000 independent rational torus points. Its output is `EXACT_RATIONAL_OK`. These finite checks do not establish the continuum theorem; the analytic section argument above does.

## Relationship to prior work
Codenotti, Freyer, and Krivokuća introduce exactly the pair \(P,Q\) used here, as their second, qualitatively nonconcave example, and say that its covering-radius interpolation has at least two local maxima and an intermediate local minimum. Their discussion does not furnish the exact inverse-radius function, transition parameters, sharp values, or a complete extremum count. The same paper's earlier *different* pair yields the sharp constant in its planar Brunn–Minkowski inequality for inverse covering radii. A separate published exact-profile calculation concerns only that earlier pair and does not imply the present formula: its midpoint is a trough, whereas the present example has two interior peaks around a midpoint trough. The present statement concerns the entire profile of the second pair and does not strengthen the paper's general inequality over arbitrary convex bodies.

## Limitations
The formula is specialized to this one ordered pair of lattice-covering parallelograms. No general classification of all planar inverse-covering-radius profiles is asserted. The derivation is an exact mathematical proof with computational corroboration, but no independent mathematical audit or definitive historical-priority certification has been performed.
