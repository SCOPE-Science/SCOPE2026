# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The determinant polarization, exact \(p^{3j}\)-to-one smooth reduction of the special-linear group, normal form for primitive singular determinants, and general factorization count \(bc=z\) yield a pointwise formula for every target. Invertible primitive targets require a discriminant-sensitive square-root count; the singular repeated-root strata are retained explicitly, not reduced naively to the finite-field formula. Counting rank-one matrices and nonsquare discriminant fibers gives the exact sharp minimum multiplicity. The normalizations of additive convolution and the uniform probability ratio are checked algebraically and with exact integer computations. Independent specialist verification remains outstanding.

## Originality — PASS (bounded)

The closest **published finite-field** results concern additive matrix rings [1], Gauss sums over special-linear groups [2], the special-unit Cayley graph [3], and exact \(n\)-by-\(n\) matrix-sum representation formulas [4]. The 2025 Luo–Ji theorem is decisive coverage at depth one and is expressly excluded from the novelty claim. Its field hypotheses do not imply the higher-depth repeated-root discriminant formula without additional local-ring analysis. Source searches for finite-chain-ring analogues returned no directly dominating theorem; this does not establish absolute novelty. A prior local density theorem or general matrix ring additive identity could still cover the claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The object is the canonical determinant-one subset of the additive matrix ring at increasing arithmetic depth. Exact pointwise convolution, not a single arithmetic example, gives a sharp lower bound for every target, its complete equality set, and a nontrivial depth-independent minorization relative to uniform measure. The singular discriminant layer is a substantive distinction from the field theory; it is quantitatively essential for the full formula.
