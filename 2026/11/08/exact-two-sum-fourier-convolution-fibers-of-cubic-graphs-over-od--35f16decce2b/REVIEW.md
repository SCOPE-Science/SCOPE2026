# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The ordered convolution claim follows from the identity \(4(x^3+y^3)=s^3+3su^2\), the invertible centering map for odd moduli, and exhaustive classification of square roots modulo odd prime powers by valuations and simple-root lifting. The two cases \(v_p(3s)\ge k\) and \(v_p(3s)<k\) cover every fiber, including characteristic three. The positive-fiber histogram counts available square classes exactly; the energy formula follows independently from the product congruence \(ab\equiv0\pmod{p^\ell}\) and a complete valuation sum. The normalization of the fourth Fourier moment follows from character orthogonality. The Chinese remainder extension is a direct Cartesian-product decomposition. The companion integer checker tested every target at 21 small prime powers and six composite-modulus CRT cases; the proof, not the finite tests, establishes the unbounded cases.

## Originality — PASS, conditional on accessible published evidence
The central result is a complete two-sum fiber and positive multiplicity classification for cubic graphs over every odd prime-power cyclic ring, together with its sumsets and fourth moments, including ramification at three and composite moduli. The already published finite-field cubic-graph fourth-moment result overlaps the \(k=1\), \(p\ne3\) part and is expressly not claimed as new. The 2026 spectral-synthesis work treats exact arithmetic moments of paraboloids and general synthesis inequalities rather than this cubic graph. The same-curve 2019/2021 discrete restriction paper treats high moments over integers, not complete modular two-sum fibers. A separately published fifth-power-graph result classifies a different monomial curve over finite fields, and the prime-square cubic-graph spectrality work concerns orthogonal bases, not additive convolution multiplicities. Searches of indexed mathematical records and literature did not identify an equivalent prime-power all-fiber classification. Non-indexed local congruence or local-zeta computations could still subsume the formulas, so priority is not certified.

## Value — PASS
The invariant is not an arbitrary small numerical slice: the complete ordered two-sum representation spectrum determines convolution norms, the twofold sumset, and a Fourier fourth moment of a canonical sparse moment curve. Its dependence on both the prime-power depth and the exceptional prime three is invisible in a prime-field-only treatment. A uniform classification of all fibers for every odd modulus is a useful structural description in finite-ring Fourier analysis; it does not assert a new unrestricted extension estimate.
