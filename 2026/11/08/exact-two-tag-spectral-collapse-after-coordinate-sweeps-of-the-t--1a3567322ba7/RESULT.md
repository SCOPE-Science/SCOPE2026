# Exact two-tag spectral collapse after coordinate sweeps of the Thorp shuffle

## Finding

For every integer \(d\ge1\), put \(n=2^d\). For the two-labelled-card kernel \(P\) of a complete forward coordinate sweep, and uniform ordered-distinct-pair kernel \(\Pi\),
\[
P^m=\Pi+n^{-(m-1)}(P-\Pi),\qquad m\in\{1,2,\ldots\}.
\]
The full eigenvalue list (with algebraic multiplicities) is \(1\) once, \(1/n\) exactly \(d-1\) times, and \(0\) exactly \(n(n-1)-d\) times. If \(j\) is the *highest* coordinate where the initial two addresses differ, then
\[
\left\|P^m((x,y),\cdot)-U_2\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=\frac{n-2}{2^j(n-1)n^{m-1}}.
\]
Thus two sweeps make the worst-start two-card distance exactly \((n-2)/(2n(n-1))\), even though the first sweep can leave distance \((n-2)/(2(n-1))\).

## Assumptions and scope

Card positions are binary vectors \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\in\{0,1\}^d\), with coordinate \(1\) updated first. At coordinate \(i\), all \(n/2\) disjoint cube edges of that direction independently swap their endpoint cards with probability \(1/2\); coins are independent across edges, coordinates, and sweeps. The two marked cards are distinct, and their ordered positions form the state space \(\Omega_2=\{(x,y):x\ne y\}\). The kernel \(U_2\) assigns probability \(1/[n(n-1)]\) to every such state. A sweep corresponds to \(d\) ordinary Thorp shuffles in the standard rotating frame; every distance here uses the rotating frame after a complete sweep, where the cumulative deterministic coordinate rotation is the identity.

## Proof

Write \(Z=X\oplus Y\) for the bitwise XOR of the marked-card positions, and let \(J(z)=\max\{i:z_i=1\}\) for every nonzero \(z\). Fix an initial difference \(z\ne0\) and set \(j=J(z)\). Before the update of coordinate \(j\), that coordinate remains unequal, so the two marked cards cannot occupy the same updating edge in any earlier coordinate. Each of their lower-coordinate bits is therefore generated by independent fair flips, and the first \(j-1\) coordinates of their XOR are independent fair bits. At the update of \(j\), the two cards share an edge **exactly if** all those lower bits are zero; in that event their difference at \(j\) remains one. Otherwise two different edges refresh their \(j\)-difference to a fair bit. At every subsequent coordinate at least one previously processed difference bit is nonzero, so the cards again use different edges and all higher difference bits are fresh fair bits.

For any output \(w\ne0\) this yields the exact one-sweep law
\[
q_j(w)=\Pr(Z_{\mathrm{new}}=w\mid J(Z_{\mathrm{old}})=j)
=\begin{cases}
1/n,&\text{if some }w_i=1\text{ with }i<j,\\
2/n,&\text{if }w_i=0\ (i<j)\text{ and }w_j=1,\\
0,&\text{if }w_i=0\ (i\le j).
\end{cases}
\]
At each updated coordinate the first card's new bit is independently fair even when the pair shares an edge: a shared coin gives the first card a fair orientation, while different-edge coins give two independent fair orientations. That first-card bit is independent of the XOR difference at the same coordinate, conditional on the earlier revealed differences. The later difference evolution depends only on earlier differences, not on absolute addresses. Induction over coordinates therefore shows that the first card's entire final address is uniform and independent of the final XOR. Hence the ordered pair endpoint kernel is \(P((x,y),(u,v))=q_j(u\oplus v)/n\).

Counting nonzero strings by their highest one-bit gives the exact reduced transition for \(J\):
\[
K(j,k)=\frac{2^{k-1}+\mathbf1_{\{j=k\}}}{n},\qquad 1\le j,k\le d.
\]
Set \(\pi_k=2^{k-1}/(n-1)\). Then
\[
K=\frac1n I+\frac{n-1}{n}\mathbf1\pi^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The uniform law on ordered distinct pairs is invariant under a sweep, since each realization is a permutation of positions. Under that law the difference \(Z\) is uniform among the \(n-1\) nonzero strings and \(J\sim\pi\). Consequently \(\sum_j\pi_j q_j\) is uniform on nonzero differences. From the exact rank-one formula, the distribution of \(J\) after \(r\) sweeps started at \(j\) is \(\pi+n^{-r}(e_j-\pi)\). Applying the final sweep and its explicit two-card endpoint law proves \(P^m=\Pi+n^{-(m-1)}(P-\Pi)\) for every \(m\ge1\).

The \(d\) row laws \(q_j\) are linearly independent because their \(J\)-pushforwards are the \(d\) independent rows of \(K\), whose eigenvalues are \(1\) and \(1/n\) (multiplicity \(d-1\)). Thus \(P\) has rank \(d\). Its polynomial identity and the full-rank reduction give the announced spectrum, including the \(n(n-1)-d\) zero eigenvalues. Finally, comparing \(q_j\) to uniform on nonzero differences, the only positive deviations occur on the \(n/2^j\) strings with lower \(j-1\) bits zero and bit \(j\) one. Their total positive mass equals \((n-2)/(2^j(n-1))\), which is exactly the one-sweep total variation. The power identity multiplies that signed difference by \(n^{-(m-1)}\). The worst initial state has \(j=1\). At \(d=1\), the formula correctly gives zero distance and the \(1/n\) multiplicity is zero.

## Verification

Run \(\texttt{python3 checker.py}\). The standalone exact-arithmetic verifier constructs the actual two-card swap dynamics with rational probabilities and checks every initial difference and every ordered endpoint for all \(1\le d\le5\). It additionally composes actual full two-sweep kernels for every starting pair for \(1\le d\le3\), comparing them pointwise to \(\Pi+(P-\Pi)/n\), and checks the reduced-index transition and total-variation expressions. A completed run reported 39,668 exact rational equalities. These finite tests support the formulas but do not substitute for the all-\(d\), all-\(m\) proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

The motivating 2026 full-deck mixing paper gives the exact arbitrary-\(k\) one-sweep routing probability \(P_k(x,y)=n^{-k}2^{C(x,y)}\) for feasible marked-card paths (its Lemma A.1) and studies high-dimensional marked-card smoothing. This **already covers the one-step two-card kernel**, so that kernel is not presented as new. The finding here is the rank-one Markov reduction of the highest differing bit and the resulting closed form for every iterate, the exact spectral multiplicities, and the sharp worst-start two-tag total-variation profile. A separate 2026 full-deck theorem establishes mixing after a fixed constant number of complete sweeps, but not this two-label exact law. The exact routing kernel determines all finite-step transition matrices in principle; the present explicit all-dimension spectral collapse is therefore an algebraic refinement, not an independent construction of a new shuffle model.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns **only two specified labels**. It does not establish mixing of three or more tagged cards, the full deck, or a sharp whole-deck cutoff. The existing one-sweep routing formula is strong prior input, and earlier exact two-tag analyses not found in the inspected sources would remove historical originality. The full chain is nonreversible in general; the eigenvalue statement is for its ordinary real transition operator, not a singular-value claim.
