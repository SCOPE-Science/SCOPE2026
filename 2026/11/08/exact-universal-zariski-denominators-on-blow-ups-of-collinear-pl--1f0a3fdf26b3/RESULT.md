# Exact universal Zariski denominators on blow-ups of collinear plane points

## Finding

Fix an algebraically closed field \(k\), an integer \(n\ge3\), and \(n\) distinct collinear points of \(\mathbb P^2_k\). Let \(X_n\) be the blow-up of these points. Define \(u(X_n)\) to be the **least positive integer** such that, for every pseudo-effective integral divisor \(D\) with Zariski decomposition \(D=P+N\), both \(u(X_n)P\) and \(u(X_n)N\) are integral divisors. Then
\[
\boxed{u(X_n)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,n-1).}
\]
Moreover, the set of positive reduced denominators occurring among the coefficients of negative parts of such decompositions is **exactly** \(\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}\). Every denominator in this set already occurs for an explicitly specified effective integral divisor, and for every denominator it can also be realized by a **big** integral divisor. The big cone has exactly
\[
\boxed{2^{n+1}-n-1}
\]
Zariski chambers, counted by their different negative supports, including the nef chamber.

The earlier known sharp bound \(n-1\) for the *largest single denominator* is not asserted as new. The theorem resolves the distinct natural question of the **least simultaneous denominator-clearing multiple**, and provides explicit witnesses for every intermediate denominator.

## Assumptions and scope

Write \(H\) for the pullback of a line, \(E_1,\ldots,E_n\) for the exceptional curves, and \(L=H-\sum_i E_i\) for the strict transform of the common line. Thus \(H^2=1\), \(H E_i=0\), \(E_i^2=-1\), \(E_i E_j=0\) for \(i\ne j\), \(L^2=1-n\), and \(L E_i=1\). The term *integral* refers to integer coefficients in the prime-divisor group, and *denominator* means the denominator in lowest terms of a nonzero coefficient of the negative part \(N\). Because \(D\) is integral, clearing the coefficients of \(N\) simultaneously clears those of \(P=D-N\).

No general-position or characteristic-zero assumption is used. Points are distinct, proper (not infinitely near), and collinear. The statement concerns all integral pseudo-effective divisors, including those that are not big. The chamber count concerns the standard decomposition of the big cone by support of the negative part.

## Proof

**1. Complete curve and cone geometry.** If \(C\subset X_n\) is an irreducible curve other than \(E_i\) or \(L\), its image is a plane curve of degree \(d>0\) with multiplicities \(m_i\ge0\) at the blown-up points. Bézout with the common line gives \(\sum_i m_i\le d\), since that line is not a component. In particular \(m_i\le d\), and
\[
C^2=d^2-\sum_i m_i^2\ge d^2-(\sum_i m_i)^2\ge0.
\]
Thus the only irreducible negative curves are \(L,E_1,\ldots,E_n\). Furthermore
\[
[C]=d[L]+\sum_i(d-m_i)[E_i]
\]
with nonnegative coefficients, and \(H=L+\sum_iE_i\). Consequently the closed cone of effective curves is generated by \(L,E_1,\ldots,E_n\). A rational divisor \(P\) is nef if and only if \(P L\ge0\) and \(P E_i\ge0\) for every \(i\).

**2. All possible negative supports and their denominators.** A negative part can be supported only on a negative-definite subset of \(\{L,E_1,\ldots,E_n\}\). Every subset of exceptional curves alone has intersection matrix \(-I\). For \(L\) together with a subset \(I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\) of \(k\) exceptional curves, the intersection matrix is
\[
M_I=\begin{pmatrix}-(n-1)&\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\\ \mathbf1&-I_k\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its Schur complement is \(-(n-1-k)\); hence it is negative definite precisely when \(0\le k\le n-2\). Its determinant has absolute value \(n-1-k\). If an integral divisor is written as
\[
D=aH-\sum_{i=1}^n b_iE_i,\qquad a,b_i\in\mathbb Z,
\]
and the negative support is \(L\cup\{E_i:i\in I\}\), write \(N=\lambda L+\sum_{i\in I}\mu_iE_i\). Orthogonality of \(P=D-N\) to the components of \(N\) gives the exact solution
\[
\lambda=\frac{\sum_{j\notin I}b_j-a}{n-1-|I|},\qquad \mu_i=\lambda-b_i\quad(i\in I).
\]
Thus each coefficient denominator divides \(n-1-|I|\), one of \(1,\ldots,n-1\). If the support consists only of exceptional curves, orthogonality gives integral coefficients. Every Zariski coefficient denominator therefore divides at least one integer from \(1\) through \(n-1\), so the least universal multiplier is **at most** their least common multiple.

**3. All denominators really occur, including for big divisors.** Fix any \(r\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\). Select \(I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\) with \(|I|=n-1-r\), and let \(J\) be its complement, so \(|J|=r+1\). The divisor
\[
D_r=rH-\sum_{j\in J}E_j=(r-1)H+L+\sum_{i\in I}E_i
\]
is effective and integral. Set
\[
N_r=\frac1r\bigl(L+\sum_{i\in I}E_i\bigr),\qquad P_r=D_r-N_r.
\]
The support matrix of \(N_r\) is negative definite by Step 2. Direct intersection computations give
\[
P_rL=0,\quad P_rE_i=0\ (i\in I),\quad P_rE_j=1-1/r\ (j\in J).
\]
By Step 1, \(P_r\) is nef. Hence \(D_r=P_r+N_r\) is the Zariski decomposition, and the coefficient of \(L\) has *reduced denominator exactly* \(r\). For a big-divisor realization, put
\[
Q_r=(r+1)H-\sum_{j\in J}E_j=\sum_{j\in J}(H-E_j).
\]
This is nef, satisfies \(Q_rL=Q_rE_i=0\) for \(i\in I\), and has \(Q_r^2=r(r+1)>0\). Therefore \(D_r+Q_r=(P_r+Q_r)+N_r\) is a Zariski decomposition of an integral **big** divisor with precisely the same negative part. Each \(r\) must divide any universal clearing multiple. Combining with Step 2 proves the formula for \(u(X_n)\) and the exact denominator spectrum.

**4. Number of negative-support chambers.** The allowed supports are the \(2^n\) subsets of exceptional curves alone (including the empty support), and the sets consisting of \(L\) with \(k\) exceptional curves for \(0\le k\le n-2\). Every set actually occurs on a big divisor: for an exceptional-only support \(I\), take \(D=H+\sum_{i\in I}E_i\), with \(P=H\). For a support \(L\cup\{E_i:i\in I\}\), take \(D=Q_r+L+\sum_{i\in I}E_i\), with \(Q_r\) as above (where \(r=n-1-|I|\)); the negative part is the indicated integral negative-definite divisor, while \(Q_r\) is nef and big. Hence the number of distinct big-cone Zariski chambers is
\[
2^n+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\binom nk=2^{n+1}-n-1.
\]
The established general correspondence between Zariski chambers and negative-definite supports gives the stated count; no additional Zariski chambers occur.

## Verification

The self-contained Python 3 program `verify_zariski.py`, using only standard-library exact rational arithmetic, checks the intersection formulas, each constructed Zariski decomposition, negative-definiteness Schur complements, nef intersections, big witnesses, denominator spectrum, and support count for every integer \(2\le n\le24\). Run `python3 verify_zariski.py`; the recorded output is `verification_output.txt` and ends with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite computations corroborate the calculations. The all-\(n\) statement rests on the general proof in Steps 1–4, not on finite checking.

## Relationship to prior work

Bauer, Pokora, and Schmitz (arXiv:1411.2431, first public version 2014-11-10), Example 3.4, explicitly analyze **the same family** of blow-ups of collinear points. They prove that the largest single reduced denominator is \(n-1\), identify the only negative curves, and derive the determinant \(n-1-k\) for mixed negative supports. Those facts are antecedents, and the upper bound on the universal lcm follows immediately from their matrices. The additional claim here is the **attainment of every intermediate reduced denominator** by a uniform explicit family, yielding the exact least common clearing multiple; this is not logically supplied by determinant bounds or by attainment of only the largest denominator.

Bauer, Caibăr, and Kennedy (arXiv:0911.4500) provide the general linear-algebra theory underlying decomposition. Hanumanthu and Ray (arXiv:1910.05038, Example 3.7) revisit the four-collinear-point surface to analyze Weyl/Zariski chamber intersections, but do not identify the full denominator spectrum or least universal clearing multiple. Their established chamber/support correspondence supports the auxiliary chamber count. Historical priority against additional specialized sources remains provisional.

## Limitations

This result does **not** solve the bounded-negativity conjecture or the question of whether the *largest single denominator* is polynomially bounded in the negativity constant across arbitrary surfaces. The lcm can grow much faster than \(n-1\) without contradicting the known exact maximum denominator on each \(X_n\). The formula is specialized to ordinary blow-ups at distinct points lying on one line; it does not cover general point configurations or infinitely near points. Its originality is asserted only for the simultaneous clearing multiple and the all-denominator witness family, not for the known classification of negative curves or maximal denominator. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
