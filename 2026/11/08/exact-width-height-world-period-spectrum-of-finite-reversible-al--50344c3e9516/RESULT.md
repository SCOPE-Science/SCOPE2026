# Exact width-height-world period spectrum of finite reversible Alexandrov dynamics

## Finding
Let \(n,w,h,t\) be positive integers. Define \(Q(1)=\varnothing\); for \(t>1\), let \(Q(t)\) contain exactly the maximal prime power \(p^{v_p(t)}\) for each distinct prime \(p\mid t\). Write \(s(t)=\sum_{q\in Q(t)}q\). A **capacity-\(w\), at-most-\(h\) packing** means a partition of this multiset into at most \(h\) groups, each having sum at most \(w\).

**Theorem.** A nonempty finite \(T_0\) Alexandrov space \(X\), a homeomorphism \(f:X\to X\) of **exact order \(t\)**, and the simultaneous bounds
\[
|X|\le n,\qquad\operatorname{width}(X)\le w,\qquad\operatorname{height}(X)\le h
\]
exist if and only if \(t=1\), or both \(s(t)\le n\) and a capacity-\(w\), at-most-\(h\) packing of \(Q(t)\) exist. For \(t>1\), a witnessing space can have exactly \(s(t)\) points. The exact least attainable height under the width bound, absent an independent height cap, is the minimum number of these bins.

**Corollary (uniform temporal law).** If only the width bound \(w\) is imposed on finite nonempty \(T_0\) Alexandrov spaces with invertible dynamics, the possible orders of the homeomorphism are exactly the positive divisors of \(L(w)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,w)\). Consequently \(\bigcirc^{L(w)}\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi\) is valid for every formula \(\varphi\) in the full Boolean temporal-topological language, and \(L(w)\) is the *least* universally valid positive temporal exponent (provided at least one freely valued propositional atom is present).

For the prescribed period \(t=27720=2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\), the block multiset is \(\{11,9,8,7,5\}\), whose sum is \(40\). At capacity \(11\), no two blocks fit together, so the minimum height is \(5\). At capacity \(12\), \(7+5=12\), so the exact minimum height falls to \(4\). In particular, the minimal height is not determined merely by the number of points or by monotonic changes in the least common multiple.

## Assumptions and scope
The specialization order of a finite \(T_0\) Alexandrov space is a finite poset; choose the convention that open sets are upsets. A homeomorphism is exactly a poset automorphism. The width is the maximum cardinality of an antichain, and the height the maximum cardinality of a chain, counting vertices. The temporal operator \(\bigcirc\) is interpreted by inverse image under \(f\): \(\llbracket\bigcirc\varphi\rrbracket=f^{-1}[\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket]\). Atomic valuations are arbitrary subsets, not necessarily open. The theorem concerns **existence of an automorphism of a specified order**, not the stronger requirement that the *whole* automorphism group be cyclic of that order.

There is no claim that the same period spectrum holds on an individual prescribed poset; the witness space is allowed to vary within the indicated bounds. The case \(t=1\) uses a one-point space for all positive \(n,w,h\).

## Proof
**1. The intrinsic level decomposition.** For each point \(x\) of a finite poset, let \(r(x)\) be the maximum number of vertices in a chain ending at \(x\). If \(x<y\), appending \(y\) to a maximum chain ending at \(x\) shows \(r(x)<r(y)\). Therefore the level \(P_j=\{x:r(x)=j\}\) is an antichain of size at most \(w\), and there are at most \(h\) nonempty levels. Every automorphism preserves this rank, so every one of its permutation cycles lies inside a single level.

**2. Extracting the prime-power packing.** Let \(f\) have order \(t>1\), the least common multiple of all permutation-cycle lengths. For each prime \(p\mid t\) select one cycle whose length is divisible by \(q_p=p^{v_p(t)}\); assign \(q_p\) to the rank level containing that cycle. A cycle can receive several different prime-power blocks. These blocks are pairwise coprime and all divide its length \(m\) jointly. Their sum is at most their product (for at least two pairwise coprime integers at least \(2\); for one, equality), and their product divides \(m\). Thus **the total size of all assigned blocks in one cycle is at most its length**. Summing over distinct cycles in any rank level gives total assigned size at most the cardinality of that level, hence at most \(w\). Across all levels this is at most \(|P|\le n\), and there are at most \(h\) levels. The assigned blocks therefore give exactly the required packing and world bound.

**3. Building the sharp witnesses.** Conversely suppose \(Q(t)\) admits such a packing into \(b\le h\) nonempty groups, where \(t>1\). Form the ordinal sum \(P=A_1\oplus\cdots\oplus A_b\) of antichains, with \(|A_j|\) equal to the sum of the blocks assigned to group \(j\). Within each antichain install pairwise disjoint permutation cycles whose lengths are precisely its assigned blocks, and let \(f\) be their product. Any such levelwise permutation is an order automorphism of the ordinal sum. There are exactly \(s(t)\le n\) points, the height is \(b\le h\), and the width is \(\max_j|A_j|\le w\). The order of \(f\) is \(\operatorname{lcm}(Q(t))=t\). For \(t=1\) take one fixed point. This proves equivalence and sharpness of the minimum-world and minimum-height bounds.

**4. Uniform temporal consequence.** Any automorphism cycle is an antichain and hence has length at most \(w\). Its length divides \(L(w)\); the order of the automorphism therefore divides \(L(w)\). All formulas are preserved after \(L(w)\) iterations, under any valuation. For the converse, for every \(m\in\{1,\ldots,w\}\), a discrete \(m\)-point space with an \(m\)-cycle admits a valuation of one atom that distinguishes \(p\) from \(\bigcirc^k p\) whenever \(m\nmid k\). Thus any universal exponent \(k>0\) must be divisible by every \(m\le w\), hence by \(L(w)\). Conversely, every divisor \(t\mid L(w)\) has each maximal prime-power block at most \(w\), and the construction in step 3 (one bin per block) realizes it. No universal exponent smaller than \(L(w)\) exists.

## Verification
Two independent standalone standard-library scripts accompany this result. Run `python3 checker.py` and `python3 spectrum_check.py`. The first exhaustively enumerates all \(242\) labelled nonempty partial orders on one to four points and their \(419\) order automorphisms, checking every order against the packing necessity; it additionally constructs \(286\) witnesses for positive periods up to \(120\) and varying width limits. The second independently enumerates orders of products of permutation groups on antichain levels and matches them to prime-power bin packing for all capacities \(1\) through \(8\) and heights \(1\) through \(4\) (\(32\) parameter combinations). Both scripts returned their success markers. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-parameter proof above.

The small exact tradeoff is \(L(11)=L(12)=27720\), with prime-power blocks \(11,9,8,7,5\), whose sum is \(40\). For width \(11\), each block is isolated in its own level; at width \(12\), only the pair \(7+5\) among these blocks fits, giving four levels.

## Relationship to prior work
Bezhanishvili, Bussi, Ciancia, Fernández-Duque, and Gabelaia (2026) introduce reversible topological reachability models and prove general modal completeness for finite, Alexandrov, and polyhedral spaces. Their full original text was consulted, including semantics, finite-model axioms, main completeness argument and conclusions; it does not state a simultaneous sharp cardinality/width/height period spectrum.

Barmak and Barreto (2023 preprint; 2024 journal) establish the minimum number of points required for a finite poset whose **entire automorphism group** is a prescribed cyclic group; their full open preprint develops automorphism cycles, the antichain-orbit lemma, and ordinal sums. Those ingredients, and the familiar minimal permutation-degree prime-power sum, are antecedents, not claimed as novel. Requiring only one specified automorphism is mathematically different from requiring the entire automorphism group to be cyclic. The new claim here is the exact joint **world/width/height** feasibility law and its temporal validity consequences, not the standard orbit lemma or the prime-power arithmetic alone.

An earlier result about **eventual temporal periods on arbitrary fixed rooted trees under continuous, possibly noninvertible maps** gives a leaf-budget spectrum. Its objects, admissible maps and budget differ from finite poset homeomorphisms under independent width/height/world constraints; the earlier claim does not imply the joint bin-packing criterion. The prime-power cost itself is shared background. Searches do not establish priority; a specialized equivalent result not identified here remains possible.

## Limitations
The finite \(T_0\) and invertibility assumptions are essential. A noninvertible map can have a transient phase, and an infinite homeomorphism can have unbounded order. The exact packing criterion only concerns existence of **some** bounded poset, not homeomorphisms of a given prescribed topology. The arithmetic feasibility problem may be computationally demanding at large parameters; no complexity-theoretic classification is asserted. Nor is a new theorem claimed for the classical Landau permutation order or the minimal faithful degree of cyclic permutations. Verification is self-reviewed; an independent mathematical audit and exhaustive historical-priority review have not been performed.
