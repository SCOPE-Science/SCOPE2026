# Scientific review: complete-bipartite symmetric-edge widths

**Correctness — PASS.** The Gaussian support identity is an exact equality for the stated Euclidean coordinates. Conditioning on one sample's minimum and maximum proves the finite CDF integral, and independent radial Gaussian decomposition converts it into every positive directional-width moment. The balanced limit follows from the joint asymptotic independence of four Gaussian extremes and the negligible chi-radius correction, with uniform integrability for two moments. The gamma-quotient transform has a direct beta–gamma integral proof and gives the stated constant variance. The deterministic numerical checker passed from its actual package source. Numerical tests are not used as infinite proofs.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** The closest complete-bipartite reference is Higashitani–Jochemko–Michałek (arXiv:1807.07678), whose relevant full-text sections concern facets, Ehrhart coefficients, real-rootedness and triangulations, not random intrinsic Euclidean widths. D'Alì–Delucchi–Michałek (doi:10.37236/10387), Chen–Davis–Korchevskaia (arXiv:2107.12315), and D'Alì–Juhnke-Kubitzke–Koch (arXiv:2307.04933) treat complementary combinatorial and matroidal invariants. Relevant published research-index results on the same graph concern inballs, ellipsoids and graph spectra. Neither these results nor recoverable prior research titles imply a directional-width law. Broad two-sample extreme-statistic literature is a residual historical-priority risk; search absence is not proof of novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The invariant is the complete Haar-width distribution, a central metric feature of a canonical family of reflexive lattice polytopes. A finite-dimensional full moment formula plus a non-Gaussian high-dimensional limit and explicit all-cumulant transform are not consequences of known facet or Ehrhart counts. The simple limiting variance and its sharp geometric normalization distinguish the result from a routine finite computation. The formulas provide reproducible benchmarks for random projection and high-dimensional polytope geometry, without requiring a further application theorem.

Limits: the balanced asymptotics do not cover unequal part sizes or yield convergence rates, the finite law is integral rather than elementary, and all historical priority remains provisional.
