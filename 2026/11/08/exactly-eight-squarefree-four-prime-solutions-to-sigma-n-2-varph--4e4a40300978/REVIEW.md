# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is limited to positive squarefree integers with exactly four distinct prime factors. Its exact multiplicative identity and strictly decreasing local ratio provide global restrictions: the first two primes are one of four pairs, and the third prime is below an explicit bound in each case. Comparing exact rational inequalities leaves twelve triples. The equation forces the fourth prime uniquely; the table explicitly rejects four composite and one nonintegral values and accepts eight primes, each checked in the original product identity. A standalone exact rational program checks every critical finite inequality and table entry. The proof, rather than the finite program alone, establishes exhaustiveness.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
OEIS A062699 already lists the eight numerical terms; these integers are not newly discovered. The proposed novel mathematical statement is the **unbounded, exhaustive** classification of the squarefree four-distinct-prime stratum. Broughan–Delbourgo (2013) treat at most three prime factors and finiteness; Broughan–Zhou (2014) treat at most three in their main classifications and illustrate the four-prime case with a single example. A Dedekind-psi formulation and targeted searches for a previous four-prime theorem gave no identified equivalent. These checks do not establish absolute historical priority: unindexed or inaccessible prior work could prove the same all-four-prime assertion.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The first squarefree support layer beyond the explicitly classified at-most-three-prime solutions is natural. A complete finite classification in an unbounded Diophantine family supplies a structural theorem stronger than simply adding numeric terms to an existing sequence. Its short monotone-product proof also highlights the mechanism behind global finiteness and the unique-last-prime reduction. It makes no claim about nonsquarefree solutions.

## Sources, uncertainty, and limitations
Primary sources inspected: *On the Ratio of the Sum of Divisors and Euler's Totient Function I* (2013), *II* (2014), OEIS A062699 and the equivalent Dedekind-psi sequence A292390. Original journal papers were inspected beyond their abstracts, including the first paper's low-prime classification and the second paper's theorems and example tables. The sequence and its recorded terms are prior art, not novelty evidence. Novelty is qualified by incomplete coverage of less discoverable literature. This is not an independent validation.
