# Scientific comparison and self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The reflection-principle representation is exact for the specified independent static diffusion coefficient. The auxiliary density-sign function is strictly convex, positive at zero, divergent at infinity, and forced negative in between by the cumulative CDF comparison. That proves exactly two *simple* density roots. Its derivative identity with the CDF difference proves exactly one cumulative crossing. The explicit second derivative of the complementary-error-function kernel yields the time bracket, while a uniform bounded-law Taylor expansion and strict root separation prove the weak-heterogeneity limits. All quantifiers, support assumptions, and chronological sign directions have been checked; a standalone program provides independent symbolic derivatives and representative numerical tests. Finite tests are not used as proof of uniqueness.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The nearest primary representation is Uchida–Miyoshi–Washizu, arXiv:2502.20705, equation 18. Its Section V asserts crossover existence for ergodic dynamically fluctuating diffusivity, not an arbitrary nonconstant *quenched* law with exactly one CDF zero, two simple density zeros, a sharp support bracket, and universal weak-disorder positions. Sposini–Chechkin–Metzler, arXiv:1809.09186, reports crossover for a dynamically fluctuating model; Sposini et al., arXiv:2403.10143, concern earliest successes among multiple searchers and do not imply the global crossing count for a single trajectory. Budini–Cáceres, arXiv:1712.04996, and Lanoiselée–Moutal–Grebenkov, Nature Communications 9, 4398, give related superstatistical/heterogeneous first-passage results, but not the statement or a stronger global covering theorem in inspected material. Actual indexed-record searches and accessible previous claim comparisons found no equivalent. These checks cannot rule out all priority risks; independent scholarly audit has not occurred.

## Value — PASS

The exact crossing count rules out recurring reversal in a natural one-dimensional heterogeneous first-passage observable. The two additional density crossings explain the mechanism behind the single cumulative reversal. The dimensionful bracket supports time-window decisions without knowledge of the full distribution, and the weak-disorder limits identify robust, observable crossover scales even for very weak heterogeneity. This is a structural all-distribution classification, not a special-law numerical fit or a mere repetition of the existing erfc representation.

## Scientific limits and residual risks

The model assumes positive bounded diffusivity sampled once, an absorbing half-line endpoint, and comparison to the arithmetic mean. It excludes time-evolving, unbounded, or degenerate laws and makes no claims about finite mean first-passage times. Specialized literature not indexed by the checked sources might contain equivalent comparison theorems; publication priority therefore remains qualified. The checks and review here do not constitute independent mathematical validation.
