# Scientific comparison of the revised weak-disorder theorem

The revised result is the distribution-independent limit of **three crossing locations** for small, quenched mean-zero perturbations of a constant diffusivity. The reflection-principle formulas and the convexity argument supply the definitions and existence of the crossings, while a compact-uniform second-order expansion of the density-sign function establishes the two limiting density-crossing times. The previously established strict support bracket squeezes the cumulative crossing to its universal limit.

## Prior coverage

The 2026-11-08 theorem *A unique Brownian first-passage crossover for every static diffusivity mixture* already establishes all positive-law one-CDF/two-density crossing counts, chronological signs, nondegenerate extrema, distance scaling, and the strict support bracket. Its full theorem and mathematical proof are stronger than the general crossing-count portion of the earlier statement here. None of those global conclusions is claimed as original by this revision. Uchida–Miyoshi–Washizu (arXiv:2502.20705) and Sposini et al. (arXiv:2403.10138) provide earlier first-passage representations and heterogeneous-search comparisons.

## Surviving contribution and boundaries

The new quantitative theorem resolves the precise leading positions of all three crossings as the disorder amplitude tends to zero for any bounded centered nondegenerate perturbation. The constants involve \(3\pm\sqrt6\) and a universal common absorption probability \(\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{3/2})\). This is a useful controlled small-heterogeneity time-scale benchmark, not a novelty claim about the already known global crossing theorem. It does not determine corrections beyond leading order, claim finite-disorder universality, or extend to pathwise dynamic randomness. An unindexed perturbative calculation could still contain these exact constants.

The revised RESULT supplies all necessary expansions and boundary arguments, and the independent finite numerical diagnostics corroborate representative cases without substituting for the analytic proof. This text is a scientific comparison, not a certification of publication priority or an expert attestation.
