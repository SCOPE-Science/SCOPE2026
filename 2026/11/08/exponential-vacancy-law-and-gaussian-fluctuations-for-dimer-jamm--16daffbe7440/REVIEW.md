# Scientific review: irreversible dimers on complete split graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The exact two-count Markov recurrence follows from the counted number of cross edges and clique-internal edges, with boundary cases explicitly covered. The fluid limit uses equal-rate exponential clocks, bounded jumps, uniformly Lipschitz drift and a deterministic first integral. Its terminal value is controlled by a pathwise clique-vertex sandwich. The Gaussian fluctuation proof uses a high-probability positive vacancy-density barrier, an almost-martingale first integral, a vanishing tail bracket, and the bounded-increment martingale central limit theorem. The variance integral and the diffusion linearization have been independently checked symbolically. A second, labeled-vertex exact recursion agrees with the reduced recursion in all \(49\) cases with \(0\le a,b\le6\); independent seeded finite stochastic calculations agree with the large-size means and variances. These computations are finite checks; the limiting proof is analytic. One must not extend the claim to regimes with vanishing independent-set density or to unequal edge rates without new arguments.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence
Cadilhe, Araújo and Privman (2006, arXiv:cond-mat/0611413) review the random sequential adsorption framework and solvable lattices, but do not evaluate this dense split-graph family. Gamarnik and Goldberg (2008, arXiv:0807.1277) treat regular graphs of fixed degree and large girth, including bounded-degree fluctuations; those hypotheses and conclusions do not subsume the dense split-graph law or its Gaussian coefficient. Krivelevich et al. (2023/2024, DOI:10.1002/rsa.21200) treat random greedy independent sets via local limits and mention matching on line graphs, not the growing-degree split-graph result. The published full texts inspected for these three works contain no direct equivalent. A published result on random greedy independent sets of complete multipartite graphs concerns vertex-wise selection, and neither its claim nor its object implies a random edge-greedy matching law here. Pippenger (1989, DOI:10.1137/0402034) and Dyer–Frieze (1991, DOI:10.1002/rsa.3240020104) are closest foundational texts, but full-text access was incomplete; a matching special case in either is a residual historical priority risk. Search failures are not claims of global uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is natural because random irreversible dimers may jam on graphs admitting perfect matchings, so the optimum matching number does not predict their kinetic coverage. The complete split family is a minimal two-population, high-connectivity example of that gap. The result identifies both a sharp macroscopic coverage law and a nontrivial second-order Gaussian variance for all strictly positive fixed population ratios, together with an exact finite-size distribution recurrence. In the balanced perfectly matchable case, the persistent uncovered fraction \(1/(2e)\) makes the distinction quantitative. This is a physical-statistical benchmark rather than a renaming of static matching counts.
