# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

For a fixed full-dimensional base, the product vertices plus the apex exhaust the pyramid vertices. Conditioning on the apex produces the exact vertex covariance as a positive Kronecker block plus a rank-one correction. Integrating horizontal sections gives the height beta law and independently produces the volume covariance by conditional expectation. The common spectral lemma follows from the Schur complement and an operator-norm estimate; its hypotheses hold at respective rates \(v^{-m}\) and \((mr)^{-2}\). The largest base covariance eigenvalues are strictly positive. The clique-windmill corollary follows from explicit standard simplex moments. Verification replays exact-rational covariance entries for three different bases, including a translated simplex, and does not substitute finite checks for the asymptotic argument.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limits

Full-text material inspected in the 2018 stable-set polytope and Cayley-sum papers concerns combinatorial adjacency, diameter, integer decomposition, and triangulations, not two covariance sampling conventions. The closest accessible historical claim concerning cographs gives **all uniform-volume moments** for that special graph class; it neither treats uniform vertex sampling nor all full-dimensional base polytopes, and hence does not imply the universal ratio theorem as stated. Published mathematical-record searches also surfaced distinct product-body isotropic-constant and graph-cone circumradius statements. Direct searches for product-pyramid vertex/volume covariance contrast and its sharp growth-rate constants found no matching result. This is best-knowledge originality, not certified historical priority; a general earlier second-moment result may exist beyond inspected material.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem separates two canonical samplings of *the same* growing polytope family, exhibiting a universal exponential-versus-quadratic difference of condition numbers with the exact positive coefficient for every fixed base polytope. The coefficient is independent of both base sampling centroids, despite their appearance in every finite covariance. The result extends beyond lattice, centrally symmetric, stable-set, or simplex bases and specializes to explicit complete-graph windmill stable-set polytopes. The comparison is a structurally motivated metric phenomenon rather than a finite table or an arbitrary parameter slice.
