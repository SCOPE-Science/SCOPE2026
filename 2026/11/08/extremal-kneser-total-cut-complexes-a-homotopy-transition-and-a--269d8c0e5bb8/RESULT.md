# Extremal Kneser total-cut complexes: a homotopy transition and a binary orientation law

## Finding

For integers \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge2k\), let \(G_{n,k}=\operatorname{KG}(n,k)\) be the Kneser graph on the \(k\)-element subsets of \([n]\), with adjacency given by disjointness. Set \(m=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\). Let \(X_{n,k}=\Delta^t_m(G_{n,k})\) be the total maximum-independent-set cut complex: its facets are the complements, within \(V(G_{n,k})\), of the independent vertex sets of cardinality \(m\).

The complete nonequivariant homotopy classification is
\[
X_{n,k}\simeq
\begin{cases}
S^{m-1},&n=2k,\\
\displaystyle\bigvee^{m}S^{n-k-1},&n>2k.
\end{cases}
\]
In fact, for \(n=2k\) the complex is simplicially the boundary of the \(m\)-cross-polytope. For \(n>2k\), it has a good cover whose nerve is precisely the \((n-k-1)\)-skeleton of the \((n-1)\)-simplex.

The natural permutation action of \(S_n\) on \(X_{n,k}\) has the following **integral** homology description, with all unspecified reduced groups zero. For \(n>2k\), write \(U_n=\ker(\mathbb Z^{[n]}\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Z)\). Then
\[
\widetilde H_{n-k-1}(X_{n,k};\mathbb Z)
\cong_{\mathbb Z S_n}\bigwedge^{n-k}U_n.
\]
After tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\), this is the irreducible hook Specht module \(S^{(k,1^{n-k})}\), of dimension \(m\).

At the boundary \(n=2k\), the sphere's top integral homology is an orientation line with the following **binary arithmetic dichotomy**:
\[
\widetilde H_{m-1}(X_{2k,k};\mathbb Z)\cong_{\mathbb Z S_{2k}}
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{sgn},&k-1\text{ is a power of }2,\\
\mathbf1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The first cases are an orientation-reversing transposition at \(k=2,3,5\), and an orientation-preserving transposition at \(k=4,6,7,8\). Thus the homotopy dimension, homology multiplicity, and symmetry type change sharply between \(n=2k\) and \(n>2k\).

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(\Delta^t_d(G)\) means exactly the simplicial complex generated by complements of independent sets of size \(d\). The maximal possible size of such an independent set in \(G_{n,k}\) is \(m\) by the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem. The parameters are integers \(k\ge2\), \(n\ge2k\). All homology uses integral coefficients unless explicitly stated. A wedge of spheres is a nonequivariant homotopy statement; the \(S_n\)-action is asserted on integral homology, not as an equivariant wedge splitting.

The maximum-independent-set level is intrinsic: \(\Delta^t_{m+1}(G_{n,k})\) is void. The boundary \(n=2k\) must be treated separately because equality in Erdős–Ko–Rado is not uniquely attained by stars there.

## Proof

**1. Identifying the maximum independent sets.** An independent set of \(\operatorname{KG}(n,k)\) is an intersecting family of \(k\)-subsets. Erdős–Ko–Rado gives maximum size \(m\). If \(n>2k\), equality holds only for the \(n\) full stars \(\mathcal S_i=\{A\in\binom{[n]}k:i\in A\}\), one for each \(i\in[n]\). If \(n=2k\), every \(k\)-subset has exactly one disjoint \(k\)-subset, its complement, and every maximum independent family selects exactly one member of each complementary pair.

**2. The strictly supercritical case \(n>2k\).** Let \(D_i\) be the simplex on \(V(G_{n,k})\setminus\mathcal S_i\). The \(D_i\) cover \(X_{n,k}\). For every nonempty \(T\subseteq[n]\), their intersection is a simplex whose vertex set consists of the \(k\)-subsets disjoint from \(T\). Its geometric realization is nonempty exactly when \(|T|\le n-k\), and is then contractible. The ordinary nerve lemma therefore gives
\[
X_{n,k}\simeq\operatorname{sk}_{n-k-1}\Delta^{n-1}.
\]
For \(q=n-k-1\), match every **nonempty** face not containing vertex \(1\), of cardinality at most \(q\), with the face obtained by adjoining \(1\); in addition to the critical vertex \(\{1\}\), the only unmatched nonzero-dimensional faces are the \(q\)-simplices not containing \(1\). This standard acyclic element matching shows that the \(q\)-skeleton is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of \(\binom{n-1}{q+1}=\binom{n-1}{k-1}=m\) \(q\)-spheres.

The action on integral homology follows from the **equivariant Čech chain double complex** of the simplex cover. Its vertical homology has a copy of \(\mathbb Z\) in degree zero for every nonempty intersection, with the natural signed permutation of its star indices, and is zero in positive degree. The other augmentation computes simplicial chains of the union. Both augmentations respect \(S_n\), so the reduced top homology as an integral \(S_n\)-module is canonically the reduced top homology of the skeleton, not merely a rank match.

For \(q\ge1\), the latter is \(\ker(\partial:\bigwedge^{q+1}\mathbb Z^{[n]}\to\bigwedge^{q}\mathbb Z^{[n]})\), where \(\partial\) contracts with the sum-of-coordinates functional. The splitting \(\mathbb Z^{[n]}=\mathbb Z e_1\oplus U_n\), with the functional taking value one on \(e_1\), identifies this kernel integrally with \(\bigwedge^{q+1}U_n=\bigwedge^{n-k}U_n\). Over \(\mathbb Q\), the exterior power of the standard \(S_n\)-module is the irreducible hook representation indexed by \((k,1^{n-k})\).

**3. The equality boundary \(n=2k\).** Here \(G_{2k,k}\) is a disjoint union of \(m=\tfrac12\binom{2k}{k}\) edges \(\{A,A^c\}\). Every maximum independent set chooses one vertex from each edge. Its complement makes the opposite choice, hence the total maximum-cut complex itself consists of all subsets containing at most one vertex from each pair. It is exactly the join of \(m\) copies of the zero-sphere: the boundary of an \(m\)-dimensional cross-polytope, and thus \(S^{m-1}\).

The \(S_{2k}\)-action permutes the complementary pairs, possibly exchanging their endpoints. Its degree on the cross-polytope boundary is the determinant of this signed permutation action. Over \(\mathbb Q\), use the \(m\)-dimensional anti-complement space \(W\) spanned by \(e_A-e_{A^c}\). For the transposition \(\tau=(1\,2)\), no complementary pair is exchanged internally (as \(k\ge2\)); fixed pairs correspond to \(k\)-subsets that contain both \(1,2\) or neither. Thus
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\tau\mid W)=\binom{2k-2}{k-2},\qquad
\dim W=m=\binom{2k-1}{k-1}.
\]
The number of negative eigenvalues is therefore
\[
\frac12\left(m-\binom{2k-2}{k-2}\right)=\frac12\binom{2k-2}{k-1}.
\]
Consequently \(\deg(\tau)=(-1)^{\binom{2k-2}{k-1}/2}\). Writing \(r=k-1\), Legendre's formula yields \(v_2(\binom{2r}{r})=s_2(r)\), the number of ones in the binary expansion of \(r\). Since \(r\ge1\), the exponent \(\binom{2r}{r}/2\) is odd precisely when \(r\) is a power of two. Because transpositions generate \(S_{2k}\) and all are conjugate, the entire orientation character is the sign character in those and only those cases. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The reproducible program `verify.py` independently enumerates maximal intersecting families for \((n,k)=(4,2),(5,2),(6,2),(6,3),(7,2)\), checks every nonempty facet-intersection pattern in three supercritical examples, and explicitly constructs the signed action on complementary pairs for \(2\le k\le9\). It also compares, at each conjugacy-class representative of \(S_n\) for six supercritical parameter pairs, a signed Lefschetz trace calculation on the nerve skeleton with the claimed exterior-power character. The actual output appears in `verify_output.txt`. All tests passed.

Finite enumeration independently checks these instances but is **not** used to infer the theorem for unbounded parameters: the Erdős–Ko–Rado equality theorem, nerve proof, integral chain double complex, and binary valuation argument establish the unrestricted statements.

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced total cut complexes and treated cycles, chordal and bipartite graphs, grids and prisms; their general graph-complex definitions supply the invariant here. Shen, Song, Yu, Zhou and Zhuang (first public December 10, 2025) connect total cut complexes of cycles with neighborhood complexes of stable Kneser graphs and examine several other graph families. Carnero Bravo (first public February 24, 2026) develops bounded-independence duality and computes total cut types for cycle powers and certain multipartite and Cartesian-product families. Their graph-specific theorems do not state this extremal-level full Kneser classification or its binary orientation law.

Erdős–Ko–Rado gives the **entire equality classification** needed in the proof. The nerve lemma, exterior-power identification, and \(2\)-adic valuation formula are classical. Thus the novelty claimed is specifically the combination identifying the topological regime change and the integral symmetry laws for the extremal cut complexes, not the underlying classical ingredients. Other published implications or unindexed equivalent formulations remain possible.

## Limitations

No claim is made for lower cut parameters, for \(n<2k\), for equivariant homotopy wedge splittings in the supercritical case, or for integral decomposition into Specht lattices beyond the explicit exterior-power module. The full homotopy result is nonequivariant, while the module result is equivariant on homology. Independent validation is not included.
