# Extremal shattered faces of Catalan noncrossing perfect matchings

## Finding
Fix an integer \(m\ge 2\) and a strictly convex polygon with \(2m\) labeled vertices \(0,1,\dots,2m-1\) in cyclic order. Let \(E\) denote all polygon chords, including boundary edges. Let \(\mathcal P_m\subseteq 2^E\) consist of the edge sets of all straight-line noncrossing perfect matchings of these vertices. A support \(S\subseteq E\) is **shattered** when for every \(A\subseteq S\) some \(M\in\mathcal P_m\) satisfies \(M\cap S=A\).

**Theorem.** The VC dimension is
\[
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal P_m)=m-1.
\]
A support \(S\) of maximum size \(m-1\) is shattered **if and only if** it is a matching of size \(m-1\) in the boundary cycle \(C_{2m}\) (that is, it comprises \(m-1\) pairwise vertex-disjoint polygon boundary edges). Hence the number of maximum-size shattered supports is exactly
\[
s_{m-1}(\mathcal P_m)=m^2.
\]
Equivalently, these top-dimensional faces are canonically indexed by the pairs consisting of one even-indexed and one odd-indexed vertex left uncovered by the boundary matching. For the degenerate two-vertex case \(m=1\), the unique perfect matching is the only concept, the VC dimension is zero, and the empty support is its unique shattered support.

## Assumptions and scope
All vertices are distinct and in strictly convex position; only straight-line noncrossing, unordered perfect matchings are concepts. Queries ask edge *membership*, not whether two edges are compatible or adjacent in a flip graph. The theorem concerns VC shattering and the **top-dimensional** coefficient only, not the full shattered-support polynomial or all inclusion-maximal (possibly smaller) shattered supports. The count \(m^2\) is of labeled supports on the fixed vertex set, not of symmetry classes.

## Proof
**1. Upper dimension bound.** If an \(m\)-element support \(S\) were shattered, the all-positive trace would give a perfect matching \(M\) containing all \(m\) edges of \(S\), hence \(M=S\). Choose \(e\in S\). Any perfect matching containing the other \(m-1\) edges must pair the two remaining vertices together, so it also contains \(e\). This excludes the trace \(S\setminus\{e\}\). Since any shattered support of size larger than \(m\) would have no all-positive trace, the dimension is at most \(m-1\).

**2. Boundary-edge avoidance lemma.** Let \(r\ge1\). On \(2r\) cyclically ordered vertices, given at most \(r-1\) pairwise disjoint boundary edges \(B\), there is a noncrossing perfect matching avoiding all of \(B\). Induct on \(r\). The case \(r=1\) has no forbidden edge. If \(|B|\le r-2\), choose any boundary edge outside \(B\) (there is one), match its endpoints, and remove them; the remaining forbidden edges are still boundary edges and number at most \(r-2=(r-1)-1\). If \(|B|=r-1\), choose a forbidden edge and a neighboring boundary edge sharing one endpoint; this neighboring edge is not forbidden because \(B\) is a matching. Match that neighboring pair, remove both vertices, and discard any forbidden edge incident with them. At least one forbidden edge disappears, so at most \(r-2\) remain on the smaller polygon. The inductive matching, together with the newly chosen boundary edge, is noncrossing and avoids the original forbidden set. At every deletion, surviving boundary edges remain boundary edges in the cyclic order on the undeleted vertices.

**3. Every near-perfect boundary matching is shattered.** Let \(S\) be a set of \(m-1\) pairwise disjoint original boundary edges and prescribe an arbitrary positive subset \(A\subseteq S\). Fix all edges in \(A\) and remove their endpoints. There remain \(2r\) vertices, where \(r=m-|A|\ge1\). The negative edges \(B=S\setminus A\), numbering \(r-1\), remain pairwise disjoint boundary edges of the reduced convex polygon. By step 2 there is a noncrossing perfect matching of these remaining vertices avoiding \(B\). Adjoin the prescribed positive boundary edges; they cannot cross the new edges. This gives the exact trace \(A\). Thus every prescribed labeling is realized, and in particular the dimension is at least \(m-1\).

**4. Necessity of boundary edges at the top stratum.** Suppose \(|S|=m-1\) and \(S\) is shattered. The all-positive trace produces a noncrossing perfect matching \(M=S\cup\{e\}\), with exactly one additional edge \(e\). Suppose some \(f\in S\) is a diagonal, not a boundary edge. A chord in a noncrossing perfect matching cuts the other vertices into two nonempty even-sized open arcs; indeed, no matching edge may cross the chord, and the endpoints of the chord are already paired. As \(e\) is disjoint from and noncrossing with \(f\), both endpoints of \(e\) are on the same side of \(f\). On the opposite side, at least two vertices are matched entirely by edges of \(S\). Pick one such edge \(g\in S\) on that opposite side. Now require all edges of \(S\setminus\{g\}\) to be present and \(g\) to be absent. The fixed chord \(f\) separates the two open arcs. Every vertex on the side opposite \(e\), except the two endpoints of \(g\), is already paired by the required edges. No remaining edge may connect across \(f\); therefore the endpoints of \(g\) must be paired to each other, forcing \(g\), a contradiction. Thus every edge of \(S\) is a boundary edge. The all-positive witness also proves that edges of \(S\) are disjoint. This establishes the claimed equivalence.

**5. Counting.** A boundary matching of size \(m-1\) leaves exactly two polygon vertices uncovered. Such a matching exists if and only if the uncovered vertices have opposite index parity: the two open boundary paths between these vertices must each contain an even number of vertices. In that case each of those paths has a **unique** perfect matching by neighboring boundary edges, which determines the support. There are \(m\) even-indexed and \(m\) odd-indexed vertices, so exactly \(m^2\) supports arise.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_noncrossing_matching.py` with Python 3.9 or newer. The standalone checker generates the Catalan family recursively and *independently* by exhaustive pairings filtered for crossings. It compares the two families for \(m=1,\ldots,6\), then enumerates every candidate top support (all \((m-1)\)-subsets of every noncrossing perfect matching), calculates its realized traces, and checks equivalence with being a near-perfect boundary matching. The observed top-stratum counts for \(m=2,3,4,5,6\) are \(4,9,16,25,36\), respectively. It also excludes any shattered full matching support in these cases. The unlimited-parameter statements follow from the proof above, not from the finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Milich, Mütze, and Pergel (first arXiv version February 6, 2020) study the same Catalan family, but their principal results concern flip graphs, centered flips, vertex degrees, components, and diameters. Those quantities do not determine the edge-membership shattered-support complex or identify its top-dimensional faces. Sheyn and Tessler (2011), building on earlier geometric-matching literature, prove that deleting any \(r-1\) edges on \(2r\) points still permits some noncrossing perfect matching. This **already implies** the edge-avoidance step when applied after contracting prescribed positive boundary pairs; no priority is claimed for the avoidance principle. Keller and Perles (2009 preprint) classify minimum *blocking* edge sets, which obstruct every perfect matching, but these are not the same as supports realizing all binary traces. The genuinely additional statement here is that *only* near-perfect boundary matchings can be maximum shattered supports, together with their exact \(m^2\) census. Targeted current literature and semantic-database searches did not reveal an equivalent upper-stratum classification; historical priority is not conclusively established.

## Limitations
The result does not classify lower-dimensional shattered supports, their possible inclusion-maximality, or the Littlestone dimension. The publication-priority comparison is limited by search coverage and by the absence of independent review. The \(m=1\) case is stated separately because a geometric two-gon is convention-dependent. The finite checker is corroboration only; it is not a formal proof checker and does not verify the unrestricted induction.
