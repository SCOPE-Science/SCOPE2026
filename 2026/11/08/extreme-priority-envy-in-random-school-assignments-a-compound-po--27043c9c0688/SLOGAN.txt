For an exogenously fixed perfect matching with independent uniform school priorities and student preferences, the number of very-justified-envy pairs exceeding a priority advantage of \(n-h\), for fixed \(h\ge2\), converges to a compound-Poisson sum of binomial school clusters. The probability of no such violation tends to \(\exp(-h+2-2^{-(h-1)})\), and the exact finite-market expected count is \(h(h-1)/4\).
