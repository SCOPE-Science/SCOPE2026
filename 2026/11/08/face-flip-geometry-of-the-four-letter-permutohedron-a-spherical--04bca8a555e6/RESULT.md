# Face-flip geometry of the four-letter permutohedron: a spherical shortcut and medianity transition

## Finding
Let \(P\) be the graph of the three-dimensional permutohedron on four symbols (the truncated octahedron), embedded as the boundary of the polyhedron. Its 24 vertices, 36 edges and 14 faces consist of six square faces and eight hexagonal faces. Let \(\mathcal M(P)\) be its 169 perfect matchings. For a subset \(\mathcal F\) of its faces, construct the face-flip graph \(R(P,\mathcal F)\) on \(\mathcal M(P)\), joining two distinct matchings precisely when their symmetric difference is the entire boundary edge set of one face in \(\mathcal F\).

For all 14 faces permitted, \(R(P,\mathcal F)\) is connected, bipartite, and has 460 edges, radius seven, diameter nine, and the eccentricity spectrum: 69 states of eccentricity seven, 92 of eccentricity eight, and eight of eccentricity nine. Its number of unordered pairs at each distance \(d=1,\ldots,9\), in order, is
\[
(460,1324,2428,3138,3032,2436,1204,170,4).
\]
The graph is not median. Among all \(\binom{169}{3}=790244\) triples of distinct states, 79,828 triples have no median, 9,642 have two medians, and 700,774 have one median. Three particularly natural states, the three uniform-generator perfect matchings, form a triple with pairwise distances \((4,6,4)\) and no median.

For **each of the 14 possible choices** of one omitted face, the resulting conventional bounded-face (plane) resonance graph remains connected and is median. If the omitted face is a square, it has 410 edges, radius eleven, and diameter 22; if a hexagon, it has 440 edges, radius eleven, and diameter 21. Hence permitting the outer face as an additional spherical flip lowers the diameter to nine and destroys medianity.

## Assumptions and scope
A perfect matching contains exactly one edge at every graph vertex. A face-flip replaces the matching edges along an alternating facial cycle by the other edges of that face. No other cycles, simultaneous moves or nonmatching intermediate configurations are allowed. Distances are shortest-path distances within the respective finite reconfiguration graphs; these are connected by the enumeration below. The result concerns precisely the four-letter permutohedron, not larger permutohedra or arbitrary plane graphs. A median of three states \(A,B,C\) is a state lying simultaneously on shortest paths between each pair; in symbols, it belongs to \(I(A,B)\cap I(A,C)\cap I(B,C)\), with \(I(A,B)=\{X:d(A,X)+d(X,B)=d(A,B)\}\).

## Proof
**Complete combinatorial model.** Identify the vertex set with permutations of \((0,1,2,3)\). Join two words when one is obtained from the other by exchanging neighbouring positions. These are the 36 edges of the four-letter permutohedron. For any vertex word, closing under the two commuting exchanges in positions one and three produces a square face; closing under the first two or the last two position exchanges produces a hexagonal face. Distinct cosets give exactly six squares and eight hexagons. Each edge is in precisely two listed facial cycles. Independently, the 24 signed coordinate permutations of \((0,1,2)\), joined at squared Euclidean distance two, realize the same truncated-octahedral graph; its supporting planes \(x_i=\pm 2\) and \(\pm x_1\pm x_2\pm x_3=3\) identify the six and eight faces, respectively.

**Exhausting perfect matchings.** On a labelled finite graph, recursively choose the least uncovered vertex and pair it in turn with every uncovered neighbour. Remove that pair and recur; output a matching when no vertices remain. Every perfect matching occurs exactly once, because its partner at each least-uncovered vertex is forced. In either explicit 24-vertex model this recursion produces exactly 169 distinct 12-edge sets. This count agrees with the known truncated-octahedral perfect-matching count and is not an originality claim.

**Exact legal flips.** Give the 36 graph edges fixed integer positions and encode each matching as a 36-bit integer. For a facial boundary \(F\), with bitmask \(e(F)\), a legal transition from matching \(m\) is exactly \(m\mapsto m\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}e(F)\) when the result is among the enumerated perfect matchings. This is equivalent to exchanging alternating half-edges around the face: if both endpoints are perfect matchings and their difference is the whole facial cycle, exactly one incident face edge occurs at each face vertex in each matching. It is also easy to test directly by checking alternating occupancy. Enumerating those transitions finds 50 matching pairs per square face and 20 per hexagonal face. Distinct faces cannot produce the same unordered pair of matching states, so the full flip graph has \(6\cdot50+8\cdot20=460\) edges; deleting one square or hexagonal face removes 50 or 20 edges, respectively.

**Global distances and medianity.** Run ordinary breadth-first search from every one of the 169 vertices of each face-flip graph. Each search traverses all 169 states, proving connectivity; its discovered levels are the exact minimum flip numbers. Taking maxima per row gives all eccentricities, and tabulating unordered pairs gives the displayed all-face distance histogram, which sums to \(\binom{169}{2}=14196\). Repeating for each of the 14 individual face omissions gives the asserted radius and diameter by face type, without sampling choices of outer face. For the all-face graph, for every unordered pair \(\{A,B\}\), form the exact finite interval \(I(A,B)\) from the distance matrix. Intersect the three corresponding intervals for every one of the \(\binom{169}{3}\) unordered triples, yielding the median multiplicity census above. A concrete witness of nonmedianity is the triple whose members consist of all adjacent-transposition edges generated respectively in positions \((1,2)\), \((2,3)\), and \((3,4)\). For each omitted-face type, the representative graph's same complete interval test yields one median per triple; the 14 direct metric computations cover every omission, and the symmetries of the permutohedron act transitively on square faces and, separately, on hexagonal faces, giving medianity for all corresponding omissions. A second enumeration, using integer coordinates and alternation rather than coset construction and symmetric-difference lookup, independently reproduces the complete counts.

These exhaustive, finite procedures are explicitly implemented in the two accompanying dependency-free Python programs; no asymptotic or infinite-case inference is made.

## Verification
Run `python3 verification/permutohedral_face_flips.py` and `python3 verification/signed_coordinate_face_flips.py` with Python 3.10 or later. Both independently enumerate all 169 perfect matchings, verify all 14 facial cycles, build each required transition graph, and perform all-source shortest-path calculations. Both reproduce the all-face 460-edge radius-seven diameter-nine graph, all 14 deleted-face diameters, the full distance distribution, and the median-count census. The first also constructs and tests the explicit three-generator nonmedian witness and directly checks the median property for one square-omission and one hexagon-omission graph; the other checks all omissions' distances independently. These scripts are complete executable descriptions of the finite certificate, rather than extrapolation from a few numerical examples.

## Relationship to prior work
Strunje (1986) already counted the 169 perfect matchings of the truncated octahedron; this cardinality is not claimed as new. Lam and Zhang's (2003) plane-bipartite matching lattice theory and Zhang–Yang–Yao's publicly available 2010 presentation treat the conventional resonance graph formed by flips at bounded faces. The broad medianity theory for resonance graphs on surfaces, including a 2026 theorem for proper subsets of faces and separate examples of nonmedianity when all faces are used, gives context for the median/nonmedian contrast but not these exact truncated-octahedral distance distributions. Turaev (2014) treats matching transformations via more general even-cycle moves. None of these cited results supplies the displayed 169-state all-face metric census, the two sharp deleted-face diameters, or the canonical three-matching no-median witness; the contribution is this object-specific exact comparison, not the general medianity theorem or the known matching count.

## Limitations
The conclusion is exact only for this finite graph and the specified facial moves. The source-priority search cannot exclude equivalent unpublished or unindexed historical computations, particularly because one plausible original 2003 article was not available in full; its main theorem was checked through a full-text later preprint. No independent scholarly audit has been performed. The scripts verify mathematical exhaustiveness via their proofs of enumeration coverage and exact integer operations; they do not certify implementations in a formal proof assistant.
