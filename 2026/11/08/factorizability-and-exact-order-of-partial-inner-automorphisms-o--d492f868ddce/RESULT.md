# Factorizability and exact order of partial inner automorphisms of full linear monoids

## Finding
Let \(K\) be any field, \(n\geq 1\), \(V=K^n\), and \(S=\operatorname{End}_K(V)\). For \(g,h\in S\) put
\[
D_{g,h}=\{A\in S:(gh)A=A=A(gh)\},\qquad \phi_{g,h}(A)=hAg.
\]
Let \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) be the inverse monoid generated by these partial isomorphisms. Every \(\phi_{g,h}\), and therefore every element of \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\), is the restriction of an ordinary inner automorphism \(A\mapsto Q^{-1}AQ\) for some \(Q\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\). In particular, this inverse monoid is **factorizable**: every element is the product of an idempotent partial identity and a global unit.

More precisely, the distinct idempotent domains are exactly \(S\), \(\{0\}\), and
\[
\mathcal B(F,U)=\{A\in S:\operatorname{im}A\subseteq F,\ U\subseteq\ker A\},
\qquad 0<F<V,\quad 0<U<V,
\]
for arbitrary (independently chosen) nonzero proper subspaces \(F,U\). Every element of the inverse monoid is exactly one of the restrictions \(\psi_Q|_{\mathcal B(F,U)}\), or a full conjugation \(\psi_Q\), or the unique map on \(\{0\}\), with \(\psi_Q(A)=Q^{-1}AQ\). Two restrictions on one fixed nonzero domain coincide precisely when their conjugators differ by a unit acting pointwise trivially on that domain, characterized below. Equality and composition are thereby explicit in terms of subspaces and matrices.

For \(K=\mathbb F_q\) set \(G_n(q)=|\operatorname{PGL}_n(q)|=|\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|/(q-1)\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{n,k,u,c}(q)&={n\brack k}_q{k\brack c}_q{n-k\brack u-c}_q q^{(k-c)(u-c)},\\
H_{n,k,u,c}(q)&=q^{c(u-c)+u(n-k-u+c)}|\operatorname{GL}_{u-c}(q)|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \({a\brack b}_q\) is the Gaussian binomial coefficient, and \(|\operatorname{GL}_0(q)|=1\). The **complete order formula**, valid for every prime power \(q\) and \(n\geq1\), is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
|\operatorname{Inn}(M_n(\mathbb F_q))|=1+G_n(q)
+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{u=1}^{n-1}
\sum_{c=\max(0,k+u-n)}^{\min(k,u)}
N_{n,k,u,c}(q)\frac{G_n(q)}{H_{n,k,u,c}(q)}.}
\]
Each quotient is an integer. In particular the orders for \((n,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)\) are respectively \(52,201,15506\). For \(n=1\) the order is \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All vector spaces and maps are finite-dimensional over a **field**. The structural factorization holds over finite or infinite fields. The order formula assumes the finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), with \(q\) any prime power. Composition is ordinary composition of partial functions; the displayed global conjugation convention is \(\psi_Q(A)=Q^{-1}AQ\). A partial identity on \(D\) is denoted \(\operatorname{id}_D\). The two degenerate domains are \(\{0\}\) and \(S\), not families with arbitrary zero or full subspaces.

## Proof
**1. Elementary domains.** Set \(T=gh\), \(F=\ker(T-I)\), and \(U=\operatorname{im}(T-I)\). The equations \(TA=A=AT\) are equivalent to \(\operatorname{im}A\subseteq F\) and \(U\subseteq\ker A\), giving \(D_{g,h}=\mathcal B(F,U)\). Necessarily \(\dim F+\dim U=n\). Conversely every pair of subspaces with these complementary dimensions arises: choose a linear map \(N\) with \(\ker N=F\), \(\operatorname{im}N=U\), and take \(g=I\), \(h=I+N\).

**2. Extension of each generator.** Put \(F'=\ker(hg-I)\), \(U'=\operatorname{im}(hg-I)\). The restrictions \(g:F'\to F\) and \(h:F\to F'\) are inverse isomorphisms. The map \(g\) also induces an isomorphism \(\bar g:V/U'\to V/U\). It is well-defined by \(g(hg-I)=(gh-I)g\); it is onto because \(w-ghw\in U\) for all \(w\), and it is injective because \(gv=(gh-I)w\) implies \(v=(hg-I)(hw-v)\).

These two maps are compatible on the intersection: \(g(F'\cap U')=F\cap U\). A linear extension lemma now supplies an invertible \(Q\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\) with \(Q|_{F'}=g|_{F'}\) and induced quotient map \(\bar Q=\bar g\). To prove the lemma, extend the isomorphism on \(F'\cap U'\) to an isomorphism \(U'\to U\), glue it to \(g|_{F'}\) on \(F'+U'\), then lift the prescribed quotient isomorphism on a complement. The glued map is invertible since the two summands intersect exactly in the prescribed intersection; the quotient lifts complete a basis.

For every \(A\in D_{g,h}\), its image lies in \(F\), so \(Q^{-1}A=hA\), while \(A\) vanishes on \(U\), so \(AQ=Ag\). Consequently \(\phi_{g,h}(A)=Q^{-1}AQ\). This proves the essential extension statement for arbitrary \(g,h\), including singular ones.

**3. All idempotent domains and factorization.** Each elementary partial identity \(\operatorname{id}_{D_{g,h}}\) belongs to \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\), as \(\phi_{g,h}^{-1}\phi_{g,h}\). Intersections of domains correspond to
\[
\mathcal B(F,U)\cap\mathcal B(F',U')=\mathcal B(F\cap F',U+U').
\]
For nonzero proper \(F,U\), put \(k=\dim F\), \(u=\dim U\). If \(k+u\leq n\), intersect all elementary \(\mathcal B(F_i,U)\) with \(F_i\) of dimension \(n-u\) containing \(F\); their images intersect to \(F\). If \(k+u\geq n\), intersect all elementary \(\mathcal B(F,U_i)\) with \(U_i\subseteq U\) of dimension \(n-k\); their sum is \(U\). Thus all claimed domains occur; every intersection of elementary domains is again one of them, or \(S\) or \(\{0\}\). Distinct nonzero domains recover \(F\) as the sum of all member images and \(U\) as the intersection of all member kernels, so no overcount occurs.

Every generator restricts a unit conjugation by step 2, and products of such restrictions again restrict a unit conjugation. Every restriction to an available idempotent domain equals a product of its partial identity with the global conjugation. Hence the description is both necessary and sufficient, and \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) is factorizable.

**4. Pointwise stabilizers.** For \(0<F,U<V\), a unit \(Q\) acts as the identity on \(\mathcal B(F,U)\) if and only if it commutes with every rank-one operator \(f\otimes\ell\) for \(f\in F\), \(\ell\in U^\perp\). Rank-one tensor comparison gives a single \(\lambda\in K^\times\) satisfying \(Q|_F=\lambda I_F\) and \((Q-\lambda I)V\subseteq U\). Conversely these conditions plainly imply \(QA=AQ\) for all such \(A\). The scalar is uniquely determined and can be normalized to \(1\) in \(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\).

Write \(k=\dim F\), \(u=\dim U\), \(c=\dim(F\cap U)\), and \(d=n-k-u+c\). In an adapted decomposition
\[
V=(F\cap U)\oplus F_0\oplus U_0\oplus X
\]
with respective dimensions \(c,k-c,u-c,d\), a normalized stabilizer fixes \(F\) pointwise, acts invertibly on \(U_0\) modulo \(F\cap U\), maps \(U_0\) arbitrarily into the intersection, and sends each basis vector of \(X\) to itself plus an arbitrary vector of \(U\). Thus its exact order is \(H_{n,k,u,c}(q)\). The number of distinct conjugation restrictions on that domain is \(G_n(q)/H_{n,k,u,c}(q)\). The global domain has trivial pointwise stabilizer in the projective group, while the zero-only domain has a single map.

**5. Counting domains.** Choose \(F\) of dimension \(k\) in \({n\brack k}_q\) ways, \(F\cap U\) of dimension \(c\) in \({k\brack c}_q\) ways, and the image of \(U\) in \(V/F\) in \({n-k\brack u-c}_q\) ways. Lifting the latter subspace as a graph into \(F/(F\cap U)\) gives \(q^{(k-c)(u-c)}\) possibilities. Multiplying yields \(N_{n,k,u,c}(q)\). Sum the number of distinct restrictions over all nonzero domains, then add the single zero map and \(G_n(q)\) global conjugations, giving the formula.

## Verification
Two standalone Python programs (`verify.py`, `verify_full.py`) use only the standard library. `verify.py` independently enumerates all matrices and elementary domains for \((n,q)=(1,2),(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)\), checks their exact subspace realization, sandwich idempotents, inclusion order and stratum counts. `verify_full.py` exhaustively tests every pair of generating matrices for extension to conjugation at \((2,2)\) and \((2,3)\), respectively \(256\) and \(6561\) pairs. It additionally enumerates every subspace pair and all conjugation restrictions for \((n,q)=(3,2)\), obtaining \(15506\) distinct partial maps and matching every predicted pointwise-stabilizer size. The verification programs check finite cases; the proof above establishes arbitrary dimensions and fields.

## Relationship to prior work
Araújo, Bentz, Kinyon, Konieczny, Malheiro and Mercier define the partial-inner construction and explicitly pose the classification of the endomorphism-monoid case as Problem 7.9 in their 2026 article. Its full accessible text does not present the conjugation-extension lemma or this factorization and order formula. Dolinka and East (2015 preprint, 2018 publication) classify matrix sandwich semigroups and count their idempotents. Those results explain the multiplication structure on individual domains, but not the extension of *partial maps* to global conjugation, the inverse-monoid factorization, nor the resulting global order. Earlier descriptions of elementary idempotent domains and of the rank-one and two-dimensional special cases are compatible with and covered as boundary cases here; they alone do not imply factorization or the formula for arbitrary ranks.

## Limitations
This determines the partial-inner inverse monoid for full finite-dimensional linear endomorphism monoids only. It does not address arbitrary matrix subsemigroups, modules over nonfields, infinite-dimensional vector spaces, or partial automorphisms not generated by the specified inner maps. Historical priority is subject to further examination of equivalent results under variant-semigroup and restriction-monoid terminology. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
