# Review of the bounded-orbit fiber classification

## Correctness

**PASS.** The affine normal form has a directly checked invertible coordinate change and exact scalar recurrence. The moving-band boundary equality proves escape of every out-of-band orbit; a separate super-solution excludes all bounded orbits below the lower transverse threshold. Uniform inverse branches of slope magnitude two give all finite-stage intervals and gaps exactly. The gap sum gives the fat-Cantor length; successive survivor lengths give the first-escape distribution. The general proof covers every real \(0<s<1\), every real transverse coordinate and all integer depths. Exact-rational computations reconstruct selected cylinders but are not substitutes for the proof.

## Originality

**PASS relative to inspected evidence.** The closest primary source, Ghosh–McLachlan–Simpson (2024), establishes the invariant reduction **on** the common-stable-multiplier line, not a global classification of bounded initial states across the entire plane. Its Proposition 10.1 requires an additional strict interior condition and is not a covering result for the full-tent boundary considered here. Fatoyinbo–Simpson (2022) describes general noninvertible bifurcation regimes, and Ghosh–McLachlan–Simpson (2023) proves trapping-region robust chaos; the inspected arguments do not derive exact off-line Cantor fibers or their gap/escape laws. Similar Cantor or geometric-escape calculations for other systems do not entail these parameter-specific formulas. Searches of published mathematical research records and primary literature found no statement implying the theorem. Residual risk: equivalent unindexed analyses or nonautonomous full-tent perturbation calculations may predate it.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A common stable eigenvalue makes a natural two-saddle normal-form slice exactly reducible, while the critical slopes \(2\) and \(-2\) create a sharp full-tent boundary. The theorem supplies a complete all-initial-condition boundedness phase diagram, discovers fat Cantor sections of constant positive measure independent of both displacement and contraction, and quantifies the complete finite-escape distribution. The resulting infinite-area bounded-orbit set is a geometric consequence not furnished by an invariant-line attractor description. These structural facts distinguish bounded initial data from individual invariant attractors.
