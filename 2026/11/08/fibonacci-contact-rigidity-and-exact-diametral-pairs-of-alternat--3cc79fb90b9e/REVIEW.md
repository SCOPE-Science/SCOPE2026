# Review: Fibonacci contact rigidity and exact diametral pairs

## Correctness — PASS

The argument bounds the alternating adjacent-sum body inside a Cartesian product of right triangles, plus a terminal interval in odd dimension. Each factor's circumball has an exact strict-convexity equality condition. Two feasible antipodal vertices saturate the product radius and diameter; hence the enclosing ball is optimal and unique. Every outer contact must be a product vertex, and checking even-index interfaces gives path-independent-set configurations when \(s\ge2\), with the precise \(s=1\) exceptions. Complementation about the unique center classifies all diametral pairs. The proof covers all integer \(d\ge2,s\ge1\), not merely enumerated lattice points. Exact independent enumeration verifies 28 small cases and 74,364 feasible integral points; it does not substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The motivating paper of Jiang–Wen–Zhong, arXiv:2607.14887v1 (2026-07-16), gives the defining family and the triangular-product structure at \(s=1\), and focuses on Ehrhart/transfer-matrix phenomena. The product-of-triangles observation, the planar circumball, and Fibonacci path-independent-set enumeration are known pieces, not claimed as discoveries. The new content is a complete equality-case classification of smallest-enclosing-sphere contacts and all diametral pairs across both parities and the sharp twist transition. Related circuit-diameter literature uses a different metric; published cycle fractional-stable-set contact theorems treat another polytope. Searched wording, aliases, and broader variants yielded no dominating statement. The motivating preprint's complete text could not be obtained; its abstract and public technical synopsis alone do not exclude an unquoted geometric theorem, leaving a genuine residual risk. No claim of exhaustive historical priority is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The investigated family is a natural two-period deformation of adjacent-sum polytopes presently studied for its lattice enumeration. Even though the enclosing radius is inherited from a product relaxation, characterizing all equality cases is a separate structural problem: a sharp parameter threshold (genuinely different contact cardinalities for dimensions at least four) changes the full contact set to weighted stable sets of a path, and turns exponentially many diametral pairs into one. Both parity classes and every positive integer twist are covered. This is a complete natural extremal classification, rather than an arbitrary special case or a recomputation of a known Fibonacci table.

## Risks and limitations

Full text of the motivating manuscript was not accessible despite public-source retrieval attempts, leaving possible source overlap. The known \(s=1\) even-dimensional Cartesian-product containment and Fibonacci recurrence are not themselves novel. The result does not extend to cyclic constraints or arbitrary real twists. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
