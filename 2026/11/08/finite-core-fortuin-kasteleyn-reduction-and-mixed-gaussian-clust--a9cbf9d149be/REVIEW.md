# Self-review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The theorem gives explicit finite graph definitions, fixed-parameter quantifiers and normalization. The identity \(k(\omega)=N_0+\kappa(H_n)\) is exact for every configuration, so the FK law is a bounded graph-component tilt of an independent column law. The multinomial triangular array has singleton means of order \(\sqrt n\), pair bridge intensities of order one and vanishing triple-bridge probability. Its joint Poisson/Gaussian limit is preserved by the bounded finite-graph tilt. The component population differs from singleton counts by a tight number of bridge vertices. The standalone rational finite-state checker replays 66 assertions; finite enumeration is supportive and not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** The independent percolation case \(q=1\) is a known special case and is not asserted as new. When \(q\ne1\), the hub limit is a *correlated* finite FK graph rather than an independent graph with rescaled probabilities: the explicit triangle conditional probabilities distinguish the two laws. Edwards–Sokal (1988) proves the representation and coupling, not this fixed-hub scaling limit; its four-page text was examined. Bollobás–Grimmett–Janson (1996) studies the homogeneous complete graph with a growing number of sites, and its available abstract does not settle this result. Bollobás–Janson–Riordan (2008) treats independent random connected atoms and growing sparse networks, not FK component-weight tilting on a fixed hub core; the relevant full-text introduction, model, and principal results were examined. Grimmett (2002) provides general FK background, but only abstract-level material was accessed. Searches for the precise fixed-hub scaling law found no equal or dominating theorem. Broad FK/Potts expansion methods may make analogous formulas implicit; independent literature review remains valuable.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A nontrivial Gibbs component weight survives elimination of an unbounded population of ordinary vertices and produces a correlated effective finite network. The theorem simultaneously identifies the rare bridge law, the conditional Gaussian cluster-mass fluctuations, an exact finite-volume factorization, and a closed two-hub connectivity crossover. The triangle conditional test shows a qualitative statistical-mechanical effect that cannot be obtained by merely substituting a probability in the independent percolation answer. The result is a finite-core scaling theorem, not a general giant-component phase transition.
