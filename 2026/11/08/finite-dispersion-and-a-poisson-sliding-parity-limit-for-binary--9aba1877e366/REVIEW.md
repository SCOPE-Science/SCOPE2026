# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The deterministic identity follows by comparing two deletion histories at all shifted coordinates; no nonconsecutive repeat is overlooked. Independent Bernoulli edge flips give the stated covariance at each lag. The rare-point-process mapping is continuous almost surely for the Poisson limit, and the symmetry-difference and moment identities are derived by elementary interval geometry and Poisson parity. The checker independently enumerates 8192 finite source/burst cases and rational weighted moments for 132 parameter choices. It is not a proof of the continuum limit; the analytical coupling/mapping argument provides that step.

## Originality
PASS on accessible sources, with residual risk. Ye and Ge (arXiv:2610.05696, initially posted 2026-10-05) address code construction and redundancy under substring edits and burst deletions; an accessible introduction and section map did not reveal a sparse-Markov source scaling result. Lu and Zhang (arXiv:2201.10259) treat burst deletion followed by insertion and its fixed sphere bound. Bogachev and Ratanov (2011) derive occupation laws for the telegraph process, mainly single-time position occupation; a two-time lag-parity decoding-sphere functional is not identified from accessible material. Published overlap-window covariance work differs in its corruption statistic. Searches of published research findings for aliases, variance, Poisson limits, sparse Markov sources and contiguous deletion spheres found related but non-dominating results. The 2026 manuscript could not be revisited in full after an initial partial HTML read, and the complete 2011 telegraph article was not inspected; unrecognized implicit equivalence remains a priority risk.

## Value
PASS. For correlated block sources with rare transitions, a burst length proportional to block length creates a scale of \(n\), not a Gaussian fluctuation about the mean. The result identifies the limiting non-Gaussian ambiguity law, its exact moments, and an explicit conditional component, making the sparse/long-burst regime quantitatively usable. This is a structural, motivated boundary regime rather than a marginal finite-size improvement.

## Limitations
Binary symmetric flip chains only, known burst length, no capacity or code construction, no independent validation and no definitive priority certification. The limit is described by a Poisson functional rather than a complete elementary density.
