# Review of finite-field idempotent descent

**Correctness — PASS.** Character orthogonality is valid because the finite group order is prime to the coefficient characteristic. The direct Fourier multiplication identity reduces idempotence to signed-doubling orbit equations. On an active orbit the equations force inverse-paired coordinates and repeated squaring, hence a well-defined root-of-unity parameter. Frobenius descent merges these into larger orbits and imposes the additional root equation; coprimality shows the number of allowable roots is the stated gcd. Möbius inversion counts the character orbits, and Fourier inversion proves existence of every counted algebra element. The self-contained executable checks many complete small enumerations. Modular characteristic and even-order groups are explicitly excluded.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature and records.** The closest earlier result is a characteristic-zero signed-doubling orbit formula on the same type of quandle algebra, and the 2026 Fourier treatment by Elhamdadi, Ta, and Virgin already establishes the relevant character identity and integral rigidity. Neither statement alone supplies the arithmetic finite-field count: both Frobenius-orbit fusion and the additional root-exponent gcd are required. The characteristic-three modular case for the three-element quandle is separate because character Fourier inversion fails there. Relevant primary sources, published records, and overlapping earlier claims were compared by implication rather than title. Remaining risk: a more general finite-field/monomial-binomial descent theorem may already imply this calculation; publication priority is provisional.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Idempotent sets of quandle algebras are used to construct and compare knot-coloring enhancements; their exact finite-field sizes and spectra are natural invariants, rather than arbitrary parameter slices. The formula handles all finite abelian odd-order groups and all nonmodular finite fields, and gives a sharp arithmetic distinction between fields for the same quandle. It extends character-orbit structure to rational-point enumeration, with nontrivial dependence on coefficient-field Frobenius.

**Limitations.** The count includes zero and concerns finite-field points, not idempotent orbits under automorphisms or knot invariants themselves. It excludes modular characteristic and even-order groups. Direct exhaustive verification is finite; external priority confirmation and independent audit are not performed.
