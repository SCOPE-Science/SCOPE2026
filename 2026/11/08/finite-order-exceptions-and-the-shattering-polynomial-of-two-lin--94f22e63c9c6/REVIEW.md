# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** All quantified claims are conditional on the existence of a projective plane of order \(q\ge2\). The six-support upper bound follows from line uniqueness; five-support sufficiency is proved by explicit traces, subject to private-line and disjoint-line conditions. Double counting of incidences and collinear triples yields the polynomial. The \(q=2\) and \(q=3\) boundaries are handled separately. Direct verification in orders 2, 3, 4, and 5 matches all coefficients through degree five; exhaustive six-support checks cover orders 2, 3, and 4 only. The universal six-support impossibility is proved rather than inferred from sampling.

**Originality — PASS with qualification.** The 2018 union-of-concepts framework and 2019 union theorem do not give this finite-plane coefficientwise enumeration. The much closer Eleftheriou–Papadopoulos–Westhead paper (arXiv:2501.09847, 2025) already proves the five-point types and VC dimension five over the real affine plane; those are explicitly prior knowledge. Its full-text Propositions 5.2 and 7.1 do not state absolute labeled counts for finite projective planes and do not establish the nontransferable small-order \(q=3\) boundary (only the bow-tie type survives). The present incidence proof makes those distinctions explicit. The comparison is not a proof that no earlier equivalent enumeration exists.

**Value — PASS.** For the natural finite two-line range space, VC dimension alone conceals a sharp finite-order collapse: all types are present from order four, but one incidence type disappears at order three and the entire size-four/five strata disappear at order two. The explicit polynomial is uniform over Desarguesian and non-Desarguesian planes of a given order, gives exact small-sample frequencies, and implies a vanishing \(10/q\) density of maximum shattered supports. These are finite-design invariants, not a relabeling of the real-plane classification.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Eleftheriou, Papadopoulos, and Westhead (2025), Propositions 5.2 and 7.1, is the decisive related paper and already covers the real-plane type classification. Csikós, Kupavskii, and Mustafa (2018) concern unions of half-spaces, not finite projective line unions. Cambie, Girão, and Kang (2019) provide general set-system union context. Historical priority under other formulations remains possible; no claim of independent validation is made. Projective planes are assumed to exist for the stated order.
