# Finite \(p\)-closed foliation fibers: étale versus infinitesimal

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(p>0\). Let \(E\) be an ordinary elliptic curve, \(Y\) any elliptic curve, and \(A=E\times Y\). Fix nonzero invariant vector fields \(\epsilon\) on \(E\) and \(\delta\) on \(Y\), normalized by \(\epsilon^{[p]}=\epsilon\) and \(\delta^{[p]}=h\delta\), where \(h=1\) for ordinary \(Y\) and \(h=0\) for supersingular \(Y\). Let \(D=P_1+\cdots+P_d\) be any reduced effective divisor on \(Y\) with \(d\ge2\), and consider the smooth rank-one foliations \(\mathscr F_f\subset T_A\) obtained by saturating the rational line generated by \(\delta-f\epsilon\), as \(f\) ranges over rational functions having a simple pole at each \(P_i\) and no other poles.

Define the **scheme-theoretic \(p\)-closed fiber** on this affine polar-divisor chart by the restricted-Lie equation \((\delta-f\epsilon)^{[p]}\in k(A)(\delta-f\epsilon)\), interpreted as coefficient polynomial equations on \(H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(D))\), and restrict to functions with exact polar divisor \(D\). Set
\[
N_p(d)=\frac{(p-1)^d+(p-1)(-1)^d}{p}.
\]
Then the resulting finite \(k\)-scheme is (noncanonically) a disjoint union of exactly \(N_p(d)\) copies of
\[
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{Spec} k[c]/(c^p-c),&Y\text{ ordinary},\\
\operatorname{Spec} k[c]/(c^p),&Y\text{ supersingular}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular its scheme length is \(pN_p(d)\), independent of the ordinarity of \(Y\), while its number of geometric points is \(pN_p(d)\) if \(Y\) is ordinary and \(N_p(d)\) otherwise. It is empty exactly when \(p=2\) and \(d\) is odd. The components are torsors under the constant group scheme \(\mathbb Z/p\) in the ordinary case and under \(\alpha_p\) in the supersingular case.

## Assumptions and scope
The base is algebraically closed; \(p\) is any prime and \(d\ge2\). Curves are smooth, projective and geometrically integral. Only a *split product* \(A=E\times Y\) is claimed, with \(E\) ordinary and both invariant fields fixed as above. The polar points \(P_i\) are distinct but otherwise arbitrary. The scheme structure is the explicitly specified \(p\)-curvature vanishing subscheme of the affine space of functions, not a claim about every possible alternative scheme structure on a moduli functor. Each \(f\) gives a distinct embedded foliation: comparing the \(\delta\) coefficients shows that two proportional fields \(\delta-f\epsilon\) and \(\delta-f'\epsilon\) require \(f=f'\). Isomorphism classes modulo automorphisms of \(A\) are *not* counted.

## Proof
**1. Smooth extension and the closure equation.** Since \(\epsilon\) and \(\delta\) commute, \(\epsilon(f)=0\), and \(\epsilon^{[p]}=\epsilon\), the Jacobson formula for the restricted \(p\)-th power gives
\[
(\delta-f\epsilon)^{[p]}
=h\delta-\bigl(f^p+\delta^{p-1}(f)\bigr)\epsilon.
\]
The coefficient of \(\delta\) in the original generator is one, so the line is \(p\)-closed precisely when
\[
T(f):=f^p+\delta^{p-1}(f)-hf=0.
\]
This is an actual polynomial equation in the affine coefficients of \(f\), hence defines a closed subscheme, with its polar-exact open sublocus. At a pole \(P_i\), if \(t\) is a local uniformizer, then \(t(\delta-f\epsilon)\) is regular and its \(\epsilon\) coefficient is a unit. Away from the poles the \(\delta\) coefficient is a unit. Consequently the saturation defines a smooth line subbundle of \(T_A\), and generic restricted-Lie closure is equivalent to local closure because the saturated line is locally free.

**2. Logarithmic solutions realizing every allowed residue vector.** Let \(\omega_Y\) be the nowhere-zero invariant differential dual to \(\delta\). Riemann--Roch gives \(\dim_k H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(D))=d\). The residue map
\[
\operatorname{Res}:H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(D))\longrightarrow k^d,
\qquad f\longmapsto (\operatorname{res}_{P_i}(f\omega_Y))_i
\]
has kernel \(k\cdot 1\) and image the hyperplane of vectors whose entries sum to zero. The image statement follows from the global residue theorem and the dimension count.

For every vector \(\mathbf a=(a_i)\in\mathbb F_p^d\) with \(\sum_i a_i=0\), select integer representatives \(\tilde a_i\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\). Their sum is divisible by \(p\). Put \(m=p^{-1}\sum_i\tilde a_i\in\mathbb Z\) and choose any divisor \(B_0\) of degree \(m\). Surjectivity of multiplication by \(p\) on \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(Y)(k)\) then supplies a degree-\(m\) divisor \(B\) with \(pB\sim\sum_i\tilde a_iP_i\): indeed, divide the degree-zero class of \(\sum_i\tilde a_iP_i-pB_0\) by \(p\) and add its representative to \(B_0\). Thus there is a rational function \(g\neq0\) with \(\operatorname{div}(g)=\sum_i\tilde a_i P_i-pB\). The logarithmic derivative \(f_{\mathbf a}=\delta(g)/g\) has at most simple poles at the \(P_i\) and residues \(a_i\); away from these points its logarithmic differential is regular because every divisor order of \(g\) is divisible by \(p\). Conjugating the derivation \(\delta\) by multiplication by \(g\) shows
\[
(\delta+f_{\mathbf a})^p=g^{-1}\delta^p g
=h(\delta+f_{\mathbf a}),
\]
while Jacobson's formula gives \((\delta+f_{\mathbf a})^p=\delta^p+f_{\mathbf a}^p+\delta^{p-1}(f_{\mathbf a})\). Hence \(T(f_{\mathbf a})=0\). The superscript \(p\) here denotes the ordinary power of differential operators.

**3. Exact equations for the full finite scheme.** Choose an \(\mathbb F_p\)-basis \(\mathbf a^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf a^{(d-1)}\) of the residue hyperplane and put \(f_j=f_{\mathbf a^{(j)}}\). Then \(1,f_1,\ldots,f_{d-1}\) is a \(k\)-basis of \(H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(D))\). Write any \(f\) uniquely as \(f=c+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}b_j f_j\). The additive nature of \(T\), together with \(T(f_j)=0\), gives the **identity of polynomial families**
\[
T(f)=(c^p-hc)+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}(b_j^p-b_j) f_j^p.
\]
Because \(1,f_1^p,\ldots,f_{d-1}^p\) are \(k\)-linearly independent (take \(p\)-th roots of coefficients and use the original basis), the coefficient equations defining \(T(f)=0\) scheme-theoretically are exactly
\[
c^p-hc=0,\qquad b_j^p-b_j=0\quad(1\le j<d).
\]
Thus the ambient \(p\)-closed coefficient scheme is \(\operatorname{Spec}k[c]/(c^p-hc)\times(\mathbb Z/p)^{d-1}\).

**4. Exact pole condition and count.** The residues of \(f\omega_Y\) are \(\sum_jb_j\mathbf a^{(j)}\). On the closed scheme, all the \(b_j\) are \(\mathbb F_p\)-valued. Exact simple poles at all \(P_i\) require that each resulting residue be nonzero, leaving one copy of \(\operatorname{Spec}k[c]/(c^p-hc)\) per vector in \((\mathbb F_p^*)^d\) whose coordinates sum to zero. Additive-character orthogonality gives the count
\[
\#\{(a_1,\ldots,a_d)\in(\mathbb F_p^*)^d:\sum a_i=0\}
=\frac{(p-1)^d+(p-1)(-1)^d}{p}=N_p(d).
\]
For \(h=1\), the polynomial \(c^p-c\) is separable with \(p\) roots; for \(h=0\), \(c^p\) defines \(\alpha_p\), of length \(p\) and one geometric point. The empty-case and numerical formulas follow. This proves the theorem for every reduced divisor and every allowed characteristic.

## Verification
The accompanying `CHECK.py` is a standalone Python standard-library verifier. It exhaustively counts all nonzero zero-sum residue vectors for primes \(2,3,5,7\) and small degrees, checks the general closed formula on these instances, and checks the predicted scheme lengths and geometric point counts. It also checks the characteristic-two rational examples \(f=x/(x+1)\) for the ordinary elliptic curve \(y^2+xy=x^3+1\), and \(f=1/(x+1)\) for the supersingular elliptic curve \(y^2+y=x^3\), using their normalized derivations \(x\,d/dx\) and \(d/dx\). Execution produced `CERTIFICATE_OK`. The all-prime, all-divisor result rests on the algebraic proof above, not on finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Andreatta--Goren, arXiv:2610.32975v1 (2026-10-23), classify smooth turbulent foliations on abelian surfaces and give criteria for \(p\)-closedness. Their moduli description identifies the fixed-polar-divisor parameter space, and their theorems describe existence of \(p\)-closed foliations for reduced polar divisors; these results are input and are not claimed anew. The present result determines the **finite scheme structure and the length-versus-point-count jump** on this explicitly defined split-product locus. The argument combines their setup with the restricted-Lie identity, the residue theorem and \(p\)-divisibility of an elliptic Picard variety. General Cartier and generalized-Jacobian theory is related, but by itself does not identify this specified scheme of foliations or its exact-pole components. No prior assertion of this complete scheme-fiber classification was located in the searches performed; historical priority remains subject to broader verification.

## Limitations
The claim is restricted to split abelian surfaces with an ordinary first factor, reduced pole divisors and a specified natural \(p\)-curvature subscheme. It does not determine fibers for nonreduced pole divisors, nonsplit extensions, supersingular first factors, or quotient schemes modulo automorphism. The noncanonical component identifications are not claimed to be compatible with variation of the divisor. Only the described scheme is classified; no assertion is made about all moduli-theoretic presentations of the \(p\)-closed locus. Search coverage was finite, and independent review has not been performed.
