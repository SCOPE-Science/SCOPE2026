# Review: Finite pair-distance spectroscopy of Euclidean ellipsoids

**Correctness — PASS.** The unit-ball difference is isotropic and its radial calibration follows by an exact ball-intersection beta integral. Squared uniform-sphere coordinates are Dirichlet-\(1/2\), hence the normalized pair-distance moment generating function is \(\prod_i(1-\lambda_i t)^{-1/2}\). Truncation and power \(-2\) recover the characteristic polynomial exactly. Vandermonde-rank arguments prove the generic cutoffs; quadratic-power-sum inequalities prove the sharp two-moment interval, with explicit extremizers. Independent symbolic checks are executable in the accompanying program.

**Originality — PASS, with explicit priority limitations.** García-Pelayo (2005) treats spheroid distance densities, Parry–Fischbach (2000) treats ellipsoid distance distributions/eccentricity expansions, and García-Pelayo (2011) establishes polynomial relationships between distance and density moments. These are substantial antecedents, but the accessible statements do not assert the finite \(d\)-moment axis recovery, its minimality, and the sharp all-dimensional feasible ratio. A public result on recovering ellipsoid axes from central-section-volume moments is a genuinely different measurement experiment: it does not furnish the pair-distance calibration or its sharp two-moment range. See the structured comparison records for actual searches and the admitted incomplete full-text inspections.

**Value — PASS.** Distance-only recovery of a full \(d\)-parameter shape from a provably minimal number of scalar population observables is a natural inverse geometric-probability problem. The exact feasible range of two moments, the shape-rigidity equality for balls, and the ellipse area formula are useful restrictions on model-fitting observations; they are not mere output checks. No finite-sample stability or novel forward density theorem is claimed.

Historical priority is provisional: complete copies of the 2000 and 2011 most closely related papers were unavailable in this assessment; the earlier 2000 source has only month-level publication evidence.
