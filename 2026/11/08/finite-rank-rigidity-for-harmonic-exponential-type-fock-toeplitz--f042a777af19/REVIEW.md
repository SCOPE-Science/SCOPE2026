# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The exact assertion concerns a finite product on the dense exponential-type invariant domain and its rank there. The published kernel recursion and cancellation proposition imply a nonzero entire joint-exponential-type amplitude for every nonzero-factor product. The new proof bounds its Taylor derivatives uniformly in the kernel parameter, identifies the formal Borel transform of every coherent-state image, and shows that widely separated parameters have disjoint *possible* singularity disks. Every nonzero image has an actual singular obstruction in its own disk, by Liouville's theorem. Linear dependence would remove such an obstruction, so is impossible. Choosing arbitrarily many parameters away from a discrete zero set supplies infinite rank. No finite experiment substitutes for the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not independently certified)

The closest primary paper, Liu–Qin–Huang (arXiv:2610.30209v1, Section 4), proves only the zero-product dichotomy, not an all-rank dichotomy or a separated-parameter independence theorem. Borichev–Rozenblum covers single Toeplitz operators, while Bauer–Le addresses different bounded or restricted-symbol products. The public finite-frequency exponential-polynomial algebra theorem covers a strictly smaller symbol class and only nonzero products. Comparison of those *implications*, published search results, and available historical claim descriptions yielded no covering statement. Residual risk remains from earlier results in Fourier–Borel or analytic-functional terminology, and publication priority has not been independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The question whether a nonzero Toeplitz product can collapse to positive finite rank is a natural strengthening of both the finite-rank and zero-product problems. The theorem gives a complete rank-zero/otherwise-infinite classification for the full recent harmonic exponential-type class, with arbitrarily large independent coherent-state witnesses and a mechanism distinct from a single-factor finite-rank theorem. It does not purport to classify compactness or higher-dimensional symbols.
