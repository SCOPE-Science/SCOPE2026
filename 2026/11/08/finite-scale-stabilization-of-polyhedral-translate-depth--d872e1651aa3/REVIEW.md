# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The irredundant facet description supplies a positive margin between each relative-interior facet point and all other facets. A missing translate violates a facet, giving one side of the depth comparison. Conversely, a facet-aligned translate covers the entire compactly supported halfspace slice when the dilation is at least twice the support radius divided by the minimum facet slack; continuity from below handles atoms at the query hyperplane exactly. For continuous everywhere-positive densities, a maximizing bounded translate is strictly smaller in measure than the open halfspace whose boundary it approaches. These arguments prove all quantifiers and endpoints, independently of the supplementary rational tests. The cutoff is sufficient, not asserted sharp.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge)
The closest primary source is Molchanov–Nagy, arXiv:2610.26108. Section 5, Theorem 5.2, gives only an asymptotic tangent-cone limit under a boundary-null hypothesis. Section 7 describes a finite-sample arrangement algorithm. The new all-query, explicitly finite cutoff, identification with facet-normal halfspace masses, and validity for arbitrary compactly supported measures with atoms do not follow as *statements* of those results. The analogous halfspace-depth literature considers unrestricted directions, not large translated polytopes. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches did not show equivalent coverage; older descriptions of polyhedral statistical depth remain a residual historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Exact finite stabilization is a structural contrast to asymptotic convergence of translating smooth bodies, and replaces high-complexity translate arrangements by finitely many scalar marginal ranks for large-enough polytopes. It holds for every polytope, every compactly supported law and every query point. The Gaussian strictness argument shows the support assumption marks a genuine difference rather than merely a proof convenience. The focus is mathematical geometry of a natural new depth operator, not an arbitrary numerical special case.

## Scope and limitations
No claim of an optimal dilation threshold, an equality with all-direction Tukey depth, or a finite stabilization theorem for nonpolyhedral bodies. Earlier terminology could conceal prior coverage; that risk is documented in the structured audit. Computational tests verify finite instances only.
