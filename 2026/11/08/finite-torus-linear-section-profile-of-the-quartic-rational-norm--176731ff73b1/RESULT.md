# Finite-torus linear-section profile of the quartic rational normal scroll

## Finding
Let \(q\ge5\) be any prime power, let \(t=q-1\), and let \(X=S(1,3)\subset\mathbb P^5\) be the quartic rational normal scroll with dense-torus embedding
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto[1:x:x^2:x^3:y:xy],\qquad (x,y)\in(\mathbb F_q^\times)^2.
\]
For each \(1\le r\le6\), the maximal number of rational torus points contained in a codimension-\(r\) projective linear section of \(X\) is, in order,
\[
(3t,\;2t,\;t+1,\;t,\;1,\;0).
\]
Equivalently, the length-\(t^2\), dimension-six toric evaluation code of the polygon \(\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3,0),(1,1),(0,1)\}\), generated by \(1,x,x^2,x^3,y,xy\), has complete generalized Hamming-weight hierarchy
\[
\boxed{(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4,d_5,d_6)=(t^2-3t,\;t^2-2t,\;t^2-t-1,\;t^2-t,\;t^2-1,\;t^2).}
\]
The third extremum is attained by a complete ruling fiber together with exactly one torus point off that fiber.

## Assumptions and scope
All geometric intersections in the theorem count **distinct rational torus points**, not intersection multiplicities or points on the projective boundary. The field may have any positive characteristic and need not be prime. The bound \(q\ge5\) guarantees \(t>3\) and the injectivity of the evaluation of univariate polynomials of degree at most three. The codimension is ambient linear codimension in \(\mathbb P^5\); a codimension-six projective linear section is empty by convention. For each \(r\), an \(r\)-dimensional vector space \(U\) of linear forms on \(\mathbb P^5\) has common torus zero set \(Z_{\mathcal T}(U)\); its projective linear intersection is \(\mathbb P(U^\perp)\cap\mathcal T\). The \(r\)-th generalized Hamming weight is \(t^2-\max_{\dim U=r}|Z_{\mathcal T}(U)|\).

## Proof
Write every section uniquely as
\[
f(x,y)=A(x)+yB(x),\qquad\deg A\le3,\quad\deg B\le1.
\]
If it vanishes on the full torus, then for every nonzero \(x\), the polynomial of degree one in \(y\) is identically zero, since \(t\ge4\). Therefore \(A\) and \(B\) have at least \(t\) roots, exceeding their degrees, and both vanish identically. Thus the code has dimension six.

**One linear form.** If \(B=0\), the zero set has at most three full vertical fibers, hence at most \(3t\) points. If \(B\ne0\), each nonroot of \(B\) contributes at most one \(y\); at the possible unique root of \(B\), either zero or all \(t\) values of \(y\) occur. Thus there are at most \(2t-1\) points. The polynomial \((x-r_1)(x-r_2)(x-r_3)\), with three distinct nonzero roots, attains \(3t\).

**Two forms.** Work temporarily over an algebraic closure. If two linearly independent forms \(A_i+yB_i\) are coprime, their torus common zeros have distinct \(x\)-values except potentially where all four coefficients vanish (which would give a common vertical factor, excluded). When at least one \(B_i\ne0\), eliminating \(y\) gives the nonzero polynomial \(A_1B_2-A_2B_1\) of degree at most four; hence there are at most four common torus zeros. If both \(B_i=0\), coprimality implies none. If the forms have a common factor involving \(y\), that factor has degree one in \(y\) and the residual multipliers are independent univariate polynomials of degree at most one: they have no common root. Because the factor has no vertical factor (otherwise its multiples would have dimension at most one), it vanishes at at most one \(y\) for each nonzero \(x\), hence contributes at most \(t\) points. Finally, if a common factor is a vertical polynomial \(h(x)\), its degree is at most two. For degree two, the residual sections are independent linear polynomials in \(x\), so the common zeros lie in the two vertical fibers: at most \(2t\). For degree one, dividing by \(x-r\) leaves two independent sections \(A_i(x)+c_iy\) with \(\deg A_i\le2\). If both \(c_i=0\), their common roots give at most one further full fiber. Otherwise elimination gives at most two additional points. In both cases the bound is \(2t\) because \(t\ge4\). Two sections \((x-r)(x-s)\) and \(x(x-r)(x-s)\), with \(r\ne s\) nonzero, attain two full fibers. If the common vertical roots are not rational, the same bounds remain valid after restricting to rational points.

**Three forms.** If a three-dimensional section space has no common irreducible factor over an algebraic closure, choose one nonzero member; because none of its finitely many factors divides every member, a second generic member avoids all those factors (a finite union of proper linear subspaces over the infinite algebraic closure). The two are coprime, and the preceding elimination bounds the entire three-form common zero set by four, which is at most \(t+1\). If the three-dimensional space has a nonconstant common factor, it cannot involve \(y\), because the space of sections divisible by a \(y\)-linear polynomial has dimension at most two. Nor can it contain a vertical factor of degree at least two, because all sections divisible by such a factor form a space of dimension at most two. Hence its common factor is \(x-r\), and the residual three-dimensional subspace lies in the four-dimensional space \(V'=\langle1,x,x^2,y\rangle\). A nonzero evaluation functional on \(V'\) determines the torus pair \((x,y)\) uniquely projectively, since its coordinates are \([1:x:x^2:y]\). The annihilator of any three-dimensional subspace of \(V'\) is one-dimensional, so its torus base locus has at most one point. It follows that the original space has at most one point outside the possible full fiber \(x=r\), for a total of \(t+1\). Equality is attained by
\[
U=(x-r)\langle x-s,(x-s)^2,y-b\rangle,
\]
where \(r,s,b\) are nonzero and \(r\ne s\). The common zero set is the full fiber \(x=r\) and the point \((s,b)\).

**Four forms.** The common zeros of a four-dimensional section subspace are those torus evaluation points lying on its annihilator line in \(\mathbb P^5\). Three such collinear points must all have the same \(x\)-coordinate: if their \(x\)-coordinates are distinct, the projections to the first three coordinates \([1:x:x^2]\) are independent by Vandermonde; if two \(x\)-coordinates agree, the line joining those two points has constant first-four-coordinate projection and cannot contain a point with another \(x\)-coordinate. Therefore any line contains at most two points or lies on a vertical ruling fiber as far as its rational torus intersection is concerned. Since \(t\ge4\), there are at most \(t\) common zeros. The four-dimensional space \((x-r)\langle1,x,x^2,y\rangle\) attains exactly the full fiber \(x=r\).

**Five and six forms.** The projective evaluation map is injective on torus points, since its first, second and fifth coordinates recover \(x\) and \(y\). The annihilator of a five-dimensional subspace is one projective point, so it contains at most one torus evaluation point; the hyperplane of sections vanishing at a fixed torus point attains one. Six independent sections have no common zero because the constant section \(1\) belongs to the full space. Subtracting the six common-zero maxima from the torus cardinality \(t^2\) proves both formulations.

## Verification
The accompanying self-contained Python program `check.py` verifies the six explicit witnesses for prime fields of orders five, seven, and eleven. For the field of order five, it checks *every* torus-point subset whose cardinality would violate the proposed upper bound for each of the first five generalized weights, using exact modular Gaussian elimination. The observed common-zero counts are respectively \((12,8,5,4,1,0)\), \((18,12,7,6,1,0)\), and \((30,20,11,10,1,0)\). The exact program output is in `check_output.txt`. The proof above, not the finite checks, establishes the theorem for arbitrary prime powers.

## Relationship to prior work
Hansen's August 2000 preprint gives toric/Hirzebruch evaluation-code constructions, dimensions and minimum-distance bounds, but not the full hierarchy of linear-section torus intersections. Joyner's 2002 arXiv paper develops toric codes and examples. Little and Schenck's 2005 paper treats toric minimum-distance bounds via Minkowski polygons. In fact this very six-monomial vector space is also a weighted Reed–Muller space of weighted degree at most three for weights \(1,2\); Geil and Thomsen (2011) give its general construction and single-codeword minimum-distance bounds, not its complete six generalized weights. The 2015 work *Projective Reed–Muller type codes on rational normal scrolls* evaluates on all rational projective-scroll points rather than only the dense torus and studies dimension and minimum distance, not the six higher-weight maxima here. González Sarabia and collaborators' 2016 preprint (published 2018) considers complete intersections and certain projective-torus evaluation codes; its general results do not give the quartic-scroll torus linear-section profile. Beelen and Datta's 2017 work covers complete spaces of bounded-total-degree polynomials, not this six-monomial trapezoid. Patanker and Singh's 2020 paper concerns squarefree/hypersimplex toric supports, whereas the present polygon includes cubic powers and is not a hypersimplex. No priority claim based merely on terminology or unsuccessful database search is implied.

## Limitations
The maximal counts concern rational points of the dense torus, not every rational point on the projective scroll or intersection schemes with multiplicities. The bound \(q\ge5\) is essential for the stated six-dimensional code and prevents small-field monomial collisions. Other subspaces may attain the maxima; the argument only classifies enough geometry to give exact maxima and explicit witnesses, not every extremizer. Historical priority is provisional pending specialist comparison with all relevant older projective-geometry and coding literature.
