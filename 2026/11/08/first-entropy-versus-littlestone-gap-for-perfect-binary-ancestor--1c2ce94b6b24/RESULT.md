# First entropy-versus-Littlestone gap for perfect binary ancestor ideals

## Finding

For the perfect rooted binary tree \(T_h\) of height \(h\), let \(\mathcal I_h\) consist of every ancestor-closed subset of vertices, including the empty subset. Regard every such subset as its binary membership function on \(V(T_h)\). For \(h=0,1,2,3\), respectively, the class sizes and Littlestone dimensions are
\[
\bigl(|\mathcal I_h|\bigr)=(2,5,26,677),\qquad
\bigl(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal I_h)\bigr)=(1,2,4,8).
\]
Consequently the first strict gap, within this height-indexed family, between the counting upper bound and actual online complexity occurs at height three:
\[
\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal I_3)=8
<\lfloor\log_2|\mathcal I_3|\rfloor=9.
\]
Thus a realizable online learner of the 15-vertex hierarchy has optimal worst-case mistake bound eight, even though cardinality alone permits nine. The claim concerns this particular natural height sequence; it does not assert a global extremal characterization of all posets.

## Assumptions and scope

The tree \(T_h\) has levels \(0,\ldots,h\), with every non-leaf vertex having exactly two children. Vertices are identified with their positions in the tree, not unordered isomorphism classes. An ideal is \(I\subseteq V(T_h)\) such that if \(v\in I\) then all ancestors of \(v\) also belong to \(I\). The empty ideal is included. A Littlestone mistake tree of depth \(d\) is a complete binary decision tree of depth \(d\) whose internal nodes query vertices and whose every root-to-leaf sequence of zero/one answers is realized by at least one ideal. The Littlestone dimension is the greatest possible depth, with empty branches excluded. The exact result covers precisely \(0\le h\le3\). Source dates are those of the earliest public preprint versions, not their later journal versions.

## Proof

**Cardinality.** Write \(N_h=|\mathcal I_h|\). At height zero the sole vertex can be included or omitted, so \(N_0=2\). At positive height, an ideal either omits the root, in which case it is empty, or includes the root, in which case its restrictions to the two child trees are arbitrary independent ideals. Hence
\[
N_h=1+N_{h-1}^2,
\]
which gives \(2,5,26,677\) for heights zero through three.

**Three standard dimension facts.** (i) Any concept class with \(N\) elements has \(\operatorname{Ldim}\le\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor\), since a depth-\(d\) mistake tree requires at least \(2^d\) distinct witnesses. (ii) If a class shatters a fixed set of \(r\) points in the ordinary VC sense, then it shatters an adaptive depth-\(r\) tree: query those \(r\) points in a fixed order. (iii) If \(\mathcal C\) and \(\mathcal D\) are classes on disjoint instance domains, then
\[
\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal C\times\mathcal D)
=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal C)+\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal D).
\]
For the lower bound in (iii), concatenate optimal mistake trees for the factors. For the upper bound, induct on the total number of hypotheses: a valid first query belongs to one factor, and at least one of its two nonempty branches decreases that factor's Littlestone dimension by at least one, by the defining recurrence. Apply the induction hypothesis to the branch products; the resulting minimum branch dimension is at most the sum of factor dimensions minus one. The base case of singleton classes is immediate.

**Lower bounds.** The \(2^h\) leaves form an antichain. Any labeling of the leaves occurs as the leaf trace of the ideal generated by exactly the leaves labeled one, so \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal I_h)\ge2^h\). For heights zero, one and two, the cardinality values from above imply the reverse inequality \(\operatorname{Ldim}\le1,2,4\) respectively. These dimensions are therefore exact.

**Upper bound at height three.** There are fifteen vertices, at depths zero through three. For every possible first vertex query, it suffices to exhibit one answer branch with Littlestone dimension at most seven. If the query is at depth zero, the negative branch has one ideal (the empty ideal). If it is at depth one, the negative branch has \(1+N_2=27\) ideals: either the root is absent, or the root is present and the opposite height-two child subtree is arbitrary. Hence the negative branch has dimension at most \(\lfloor\log_2 27\rfloor=4\). If the query is at depth two, the negative branch has
\[
1+(1+N_1)N_2=1+6\cdot26=157
\]
ideals: the root-absent option; otherwise six choices on the side containing the forbidden grandchild (its parent absent, or parent present and the sibling height-one subtree arbitrary), multiplied by 26 opposite-side choices. Its Littlestone dimension is at most \(\lfloor\log_2 157\rfloor=7\).

If the first query is instead at depth three, it queries a leaf. In the positive branch that leaf and all three of its ancestors are fixed present. The remaining variable vertices form three disjoint sibling subtrees, of heights zero, one and two. Their ideal classes vary independently, so by (iii) and the already proved lower-height dimensions the positive branch has Littlestone dimension exactly \(1+2+4=7\). All query positions have been exhausted. A full depth-nine mistake tree would require both children of its first query to support depth eight, which is impossible; thus \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal I_3)\le8\). The eight leaves give equality.

**First strict gap.** For heights zero through two, \(\lfloor\log_2 N_h\rfloor=1,2,4\), matching their exact dimensions; at height three, \(\lfloor\log_2 677\rfloor=9\) exceeds eight. This proves the specified first-gap statement.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library Python program `hierarchy_check.py` implements two distinct exact algorithms on all four trees. One computes online dimension by deletion of forced ancestors or descendants from induced posets, using the recursive game rank. The other enumerates actual binary membership assignments, retains precisely the ancestor-closed ones, and recursively splits hypothesis subsets by each vertex query. Both yield class sizes \((2,5,26,677)\) and ranks \((1,2,4,8)\), including the height-three computation from 677 explicit hypotheses. `checker_output.txt` contains the successful output. The proof above is independent of the computation; in particular, finite testing is not offered as an unproved general-height theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Littlestone dimension is the classical worst-case online-mistake parameter. Chase, Freitag and Reyzin (arXiv:2310.04812v1, 7 October 2023) develop general query-learning and compression consequences of this dimension; those general bounds do not themselves evaluate the dimension of this ideal class. Avdiukhin and collaborators (arXiv:2302.04492v1, 9 February 2023) analyze online learning of hierarchical trees using labeled tuples, which is a different instance and concept domain from vertex-membership queries into ideals of a fixed tree. The current statement concerns the exact first cardinality-versus-Littlestone gap for this explicitly defined sequence, not a new general online-learning inequality. A description by rooted subtrees or by models of the Horn implications “included child implies included parent” is equivalent. No identical statement was identified in targeted comparisons; lack of a match does not prove historical novelty.

## Limitations

Only heights zero through three are determined. There is no assertion of a formula for larger heights, all rooted trees, arbitrary posets, or more general Horn classes. The algebraic proof uses inclusion of the empty ideal; changing the class changes its cardinality and potentially its dimension. Independent scientific validation has not been performed. The exact first-gap calculation may be implicit in older online-learning or poset folklore under terminology not captured by the available searches.
