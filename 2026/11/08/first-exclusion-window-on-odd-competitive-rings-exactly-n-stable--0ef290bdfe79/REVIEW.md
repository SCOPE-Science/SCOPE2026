## Originality — PASS (priority uncertainty remains)

The 2021 Marcus–Turner–Bunin paper analyzes the identical competition matrix on sparse networks and proves the even-path spectral cutoff. Its whole-network emphasis is local tree components in typical large random graphs, not an odd-cycle complete census of stable support patterns and their invasion constraints. Hong–Pego (2021) gives a general exclusion theorem and examples of multiplicity, but its antichain bound cannot determine these exact \(n\) patterns or the threshold interval. Lischke–Löffler (2017) provides a general computational method for fixed finite instances, not an observed all-size ring formula in the accessible material; the full paper remained inaccessible and is a residual priority risk. Semantic mathematical-record searches for odd-ring competition, exact endpoints, stability supports and biomass did not return a covering claim. Searches cannot establish uniqueness; an unindexed exact equivalent remains possible.

## Value — PASS

This is a natural finite-dimensional ecological competition question motivated by exact subgraph stability thresholds: what becomes stable immediately after complete coexistence loses stability on a *frustrated odd interaction ring*? The theorem supplies a sharp full interval, an all-size attractor census, exact species-loss geometry, and a biomass-versus-richness reversal. These are structural ecological properties, not arbitrary finite parameters. Their boundaries are analytic, and they distinguish ring-wide invasion stability from isolated subgraph feasibility. Potential extensions include basin geometry and perturbations of interaction weights; neither is claimed to be solved.
