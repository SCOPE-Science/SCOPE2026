# Scientific review of the generalized Frobenius formulas

## Correctness

PASS. The claim concerns exactly the largest integer with at most one nonnegative coefficient-vector representation for each of two non-arithmetic-progression generator families. A direct transformation of every representation to \(n=au+2t\) reduces the proof to the minimum total number of coins for each weighted offset \(t\). The two cases are separately resolved: in the first, the extremal residue is \(t_0=a-1\), and in the second it is \(t_0=7\). The proof explicitly constructs a two-representation witness in every residue below each bound and shows the proposed last exceptional integer has exactly one representation, including the exclusion of larger possible offsets. A standalone dynamic program independently checked 66 parameter values in each family through \(4a^2\). These experiments corroborate, but are not the infinite argument. The excluded \(k=0\) values were independently checked.

## Originality

PASS within inspected sources. The directly matching Robles-Pérez–Rosales paper, arXiv:2112.05501, Section 3, obtains classical Frobenius numbers and Apéry sets for exactly both families, and Remark 2.4 explicitly lists generalized \(p\)-Frobenius numbers as further work. Its results do not specify the largest integer with at most one representation. Komatsu–Ying's arXiv:2206.13052, Theorem 1, solves the distinct arithmetic-progression case \((a,a+d,a+2d)\), not either of the offset patterns here. The full text of Hwang–Song (2023), Sections 2–3, concerns different equally spaced sexy-prime triples (offsets \(0,6,12\)) and classical Frobenius, genus and type invariants, not \(p=1\) for the two target families. Published records, exact-formula searches, semantic research-record searches, and recoverable historical mathematical claims disclosed no implication covering the two formulas. Search non-discovery is not a guarantee of novelty.

## Mathematical value

PASS. A paper devoted specifically to these two prime-triplet-shaped numerical-semigroup families posed generalized \(p\)-Frobenius numbers as a future problem. The present result closes the first nonclassical case \(p=1\) uniformly across both infinite families and shows that their sharp quadratic thresholds are different, with a distinct extremal residue mechanism in each. The formulas apply whether or not the generators happen to be prime, making them applicable to every actual prime triplet of these shapes. This is an exact motivated structural classification, not an arbitrary finite numerical sample or rewording of the ordinary Frobenius result.

## Closest literature and limitations

The first source supplies the classical \(p=0\) case but not the denumerant threshold; the second supplies \(p\)-general theory for the different equal-spacing geometry. No result for \(p\ge2\), prime quadruplets, or other admissible constellations is asserted. The mathematical verification is internal and does not constitute external certification.
