# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The top cohomology of \(\operatorname{Gr}(3,\mathbb C^{\ell+3})\) is integrally generated by the rectangular Schubert point class, with integral Pieri multiplication. Every top-degree element of the subalgebra \(\mathbb Z[e_1,e_2]\) is an integral combination of the displayed monomials; hence their exact gcd is *precisely* the top index. `CHECK.py` exhaustively enumerates the admissible rectangle partitions for \(1\leq\ell\leq16\), evaluates every top monomial, and agrees with a second, separately implemented Chern-root alternant calculation. Exact stored integers, including the full width-thirteen array, are compared on replay. This proves the stated finite classification; it does not support an all-width extension.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The nearest source is Xiong (arXiv:2610.28780v1): Corollary 7.4 concerns rational high-degree generation, Appendix C.3 states the classical intersection-number/Kostka identity and includes finite top-pairing checks, and Remark 7.9 declines to assert integral analogues. Neither rational generation nor the classical formula implies the observed gcd classification without additional integer evaluation. Reiner--Tudose (arXiv:math/0309281) and the Polymath Jr. filtration paper (arXiv:2011.03179) likewise concern rational filtrations, rather than these integer indices. Searches for rectangular Kostka gcds, two-Chern integral index, saturation, and the exact widths found no identified matching claim. The complete 174-file computational supplement to the 2026 source was not reconstructed and some specialized historical Kostka-divisibility references may exist: priority remains provisional.

## Value — PASS

The computation resolves a natural structural question omitted from the rational filtration theorem: does top-degree rational generation lift to an integral generator? It establishes the sharp first obstruction, its complete initial width window, and a nonmonotone *return* to integral generation. The integer index separates cohomology sublattices that have identical rational top-degree dimensions. This is a motivated boundary classification rather than an arbitrary sample of individual intersection numbers.
