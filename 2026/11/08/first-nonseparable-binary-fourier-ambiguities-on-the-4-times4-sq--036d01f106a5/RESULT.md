# First nonseparable binary Fourier ambiguities on the \(4\times4\) square

## Finding

For each square side length \(n\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), let \(\mathcal B_n=\{-1,+1\}^{n\times n}\). For \(a\in\mathcal B_n\), write its bivariate Fourier polynomial as \(P_a(z,w)=\sum_{y=0}^{n-1}\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}a_{y,x}z^x w^y\). The Fourier-intensity fiber of \(a\) consists of all binary arrays with the same complete function \(|P_a(e^{i\theta},e^{i\phi})|^2\) on the continuous two-torus. Arrays differing by a global sign or by a simultaneous reversal of both coordinate axes are identified as trivially equivalent; **no individual-axis reversal is identified**.

The complete fiber counts, measured in trivial-equivalence classes, are as follows. At side lengths \(1,2,3,4\), the numbers of singleton fibers are respectively \(1,6,134,16488\), and the numbers of double fibers are respectively \(0,0,1,12\). There are no fibers of size greater than two. The unique double fiber at side \(3\) comprises two separable arrays, both outer products of binary vectors. At side \(4\), exactly four double fibers comprise separable arrays, and exactly eight comprise two nonseparable arrays. Thus side \(4\) is the first square size permitting a Fourier-magnitude ambiguity between nonseparable binary arrays under the stipulated equivalences.

An explicit nonseparable ambiguous pair at side \(4\) is

```text
A =            B =
+-+-           +--+
-++-           +-+-
++++           ++--
--++           ++++
```

where the arrays use row-major order, the plus symbol denotes \(+1\), and the minus symbol denotes \(-1\). The displayed pair has equal full aperiodic autocorrelation, but the two arrays are not related by global sign or simultaneous central reversal, and neither is separable.

## Assumptions and scope

All array entries are *real* and equal to \(+1\) or \(-1\). The intensity is sampled on the **continuous** two-dimensional torus (not just an aliased discrete-frequency lattice). Every location of the \(n\times n\) array is occupied; no zero entries or translated windows are allowed. Trivial equivalence means precisely the group generated by global sign and simultaneous central reversal \(a_{y,x}\mapsto a_{n-1-y,n-1-x}\). A binary square is separable iff \(a_{y,x}a_{0,0}=a_{y,0}a_{0,x}\) for every coordinate pair. The result makes no claim about larger sides, unrestricted complex amplitudes, or arbitrary rectangular dimensions.

Define the aperiodic autocorrelation at shift \((d_y,d_x)\in\mathbb Z^2\) by
\[
 C_a(d_y,d_x)=\sum_{\substack{0\le y,y+d_y<n\\0\le x,x+d_x<n}}a_{y,x}a_{y+d_y,x+d_x}.
\]
The zero-shift term is always \(n^2\), and \(C_a(-d_y,-d_x)=C_a(d_y,d_x)\). Therefore it suffices to record the \(n-1\) shifts \((0,d_x)\) with \(1\le d_x<n\) and the \((n-1)(2n-1)\) shifts \((d_y,d_x)\) with \(1\le d_y<n\) and \(1-n\le d_x\le n-1\).

## Proof

**Step 1: equivalence of intensity and integer invariants.** Expanding \(P_a(z,w)\overline{P_a(z,w)}\) for unit-modulus \(z,w\) gives the trigonometric polynomial with Fourier coefficients \(C_a(d_y,d_x)\) (up to the harmless choice of sign for the shift index). Uniqueness of finite trigonometric-polynomial coefficients implies that two arrays have equal intensity at every torus point if and only if the listed nonzero-lag autocorrelation vectors are equal. Sign and central reversal preserve these vectors.

**Step 2: no omitted binary inputs or equivalences.** Because multiplying all signs by \(-1\) changes no intensity, each sign orbit has exactly one array with upper-left entry \(+1\). This yields exactly \(2^{n^2-1}\) normalized arrays. Number the entries by \(j=ny+x\); encode normalized arrays by an even bit integer \(c\in[0,2^{n^2})\), where bit \(j\) equals one iff that entry is \(-1\). Enumerating \(c=2w\) for \(0\le w<2^{n^2-1}\) therefore covers all original arrays modulo global sign exactly once. Central reversal reverses the \(n^2\) bits and then, if necessary, complements all bits to restore upper-left sign \(+1\). The minimum of these two even integer codes is a unique canonical representative for the combined sign/reversal orbit.

**Step 3: exact complete finite classification.** The accompanying standalone integer-only checker computes the full autocorrelation vector of each of these codes, assigns its canonical orbit representative, and constructs a map from autocorrelation vectors to sets of canonical representatives. Consequently the number of representatives in each set is *exactly* the size of the corresponding Fourier-intensity fiber, without randomized sampling or a numerical threshold. Enumerating the four finite domains gives the asserted fiber histograms:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
n &1&2&3&4\\\hline
\#\{\text{singleton fibers}\}&1&6&134&16488\\
\#\{\text{double fibers}\}&0&0&1&12\\
\#\{\text{fibers of size}>2\}&0&0&0&0
\end{array}.
\]
The same checker directly determines separability from the rank-one identity stated above. Its exact tally is: for \(n=3\), one double fiber with two separable representatives; for \(n=4\), four double fibers with both representatives separable and eight double fibers with neither representative separable. The checker additionally verifies that the sum of the fiber cardinalities is exactly the number of enumerated sign/reversal orbits, ruling out discarded representatives.

**Step 4: explicit independently checked witness.** The printed masks correspond to row-major bit codes \(12442\) and \(3238\). Their canonical sign/reversal representatives differ, and both violate the rank-one identity. Their nonzero-lag integer autocorrelation vectors agree term by term. The checker verifies the witness equality a second way by independently multiplying the two-variable coefficient polynomials \(P_a(z,w)P_a(z^{-1},w^{-1})\), summing all \(n^4\) coefficient pairs and comparing every required lag. This provides an additional check on the direct shift-sum implementation, while the complete domain enumeration in Step 3 proves the remaining claims.

## Relationship to prior work

Novikov and Xu (2026) construct broad families of multidimensional Fourier phase-retrieval ambiguities and Pauli partners. Their general results motivate looking for exact small-alphabet, fixed-support thresholds, but the inspected material does not state the present exact binary square fiber census. Katz, Rahman, and Ward investigate the **one-dimensional** binary aperiodic autocorrelation problem, including a finite enumeration of ambiguous sequence lengths. Their results do not enumerate genuine bivariate square masks; notably an individual-axis reversal of a *separable* bivariate array may create a double fiber under the stricter simultaneous-reversal equivalence used here.

The closest retrieved four-by-four Fourier-aperture census is entitled “Complete binary optimizer orbits of the \(4\times4\) square Fourier aperture” (published-finding corpus, October 2026); it classifies 32 minimizers of a *single scalar* fourth moment, not all fibers of the *full autocorrelation vector*. The explicit witness here has fourth moment \(400\), whereas that cited work concerns the minimum \(304\). The title and accessible statement of that record do not imply the present census, although its complete manuscript was not available for a decisive exclusion of unpublished ancillary tables.
