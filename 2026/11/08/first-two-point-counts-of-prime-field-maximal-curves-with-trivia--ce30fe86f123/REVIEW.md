# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For each odd prime \(p\) and integer \(n\ge2\), the proof classifies every fiber over the first two base fields, including both rational places excluded from the affine coordinates. The additional place over \(u=0\) is recovered by the exact local equation \(u+t^{p-1}=B(u)t^p\); the unique point over infinity follows from the valuation with denominator \(p\). The norm-one kernel/image calculation supplies all \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\)-fibers. The result agrees with exhaustive exact computations for nine small parameter pairs, including every phase of the dichotomy. Finite verification does not replace the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority qualified)
The motivating paper [1] establishes a large-field maximality property and a ramification description, but its inspected relevant sections do not state the two smallest-field point counts. Large-field maximality does not determine the displayed low Frobenius traces. The earlier Weierstrass-semigroup result on the same family computes pole orders, not arithmetic point counts. Searches for the exact curve equation, the first two point counts, zeta coefficients, aliases and relevant families found no equivalent claim. The other recent maximal-curve paper [2] treats Chebyshev hyperelliptic curves, not the same family. It remains possible that a general quadratic-form or character-sum formula in earlier work could be specialized to imply the result; this is residual historical-priority risk, not affirmative novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The family is defined over the prime field, has unbounded genus, trivial geometric automorphism group, and is maximal only over a very large specified extension. Its first two rational point counts are therefore natural, previously unstated arithmetic invariants and give the initial coefficients of the canonical Hasse–Weil zeta numerator. Their dependence on the congruences \(p\mid(2n+1)\) and \(p\pmod4\) is a genuine two-stage arithmetic phase transition, useful as exact input to further zeta-factor and Frobenius-trace investigations. No general zeta formula is asserted.
