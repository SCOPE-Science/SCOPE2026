# Five concentration phases and a fivefold resonance for quartic spherical harmonics in antipodal caps

## Finding
Let \(C_t=\{\omega\in\mathbb S^2:|\omega_3|\ge t\}\), \(0<t<1\), and let \(\mathcal H_4\) be the nine-dimensional real space of spherical harmonics of exact degree four. For \(f\ne0\) write
\[
Q_t(f)=\frac{\int_{C_t}|f|^2\,d\sigma}{\int_{\mathbb S^2}|f|^2\,d\sigma},
\]
where \(\sigma\) is normalized surface area. Let \(E_m\) be the real azimuthal-order-\(m\) subspace, of dimension one for \(m=0\) and two for \(m=1,2,3,4\). The concentration quadratic form is diagonal on \(\mathcal H_4=\bigoplus_{m=0}^4 E_m\), with eigenvalue \(\lambda_m(t)\) on \(E_m\).

Define four strictly ordered transition parameters \(\alpha<\beta<\gamma<\delta\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=\sqrt{s_*},\quad 455s_*^3-625s_*^2+293s_*-27=0,\quad 0<s_*<\tfrac17,\\
\beta&=\sqrt{\frac{5+2\sqrt{15}}{35}},\qquad
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac37},\qquad
\delta=\sqrt{\frac{15+2\sqrt{30}}{35}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(s_*\) is the unique real root of its cubic. Numerically the cutoffs are \(0.346887335740\ldots\), \(0.603465389999\ldots\), \(0.654653670708\ldots\), and \(0.861136311594\ldots\), respectively.

**Sharp upper envelope.** For all nonzero \(f\in\mathcal H_4\),
\[
\lambda_4(t)\le Q_t(f)\le \max_{0\le m\le4}\lambda_m(t).
\]
The upper maximizing order, in the five successive open intervals separated by \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\), is **\(3\to0\to2\to1\to0\)**. At the four boundary cutoffs, the maximizing order sets are respectively \(\{0,3\}\), \(\{0,2\}\), \(\{0,1,2\}\), and \(\{0,1\}\), with maximal eigenspace dimensions **3, 3, 5, 3**. For every interior cutoff, \(E_4\) is the *unique* bottom eigenspace. In particular, at the five-dimensional resonance \(t=\gamma\),
\[
\lambda_0(\gamma)=\lambda_1(\gamma)=\lambda_2(\gamma)=1-\frac{243\sqrt{21}}{2401},
\quad \lambda_3(\gamma)=1-\frac{423\sqrt{21}}{2401},
\quad \lambda_4(\gamma)=1-\frac{513\sqrt{21}}{2401}.
\]
The upper norming geometry is therefore *reentrant*: zonal order zero is optimal on two disconnected cutoff intervals, separated by orders two and one. All upper and lower inequalities and equality characterizations are exact and attained by nonzero functions in the stated eigenspaces.

## Assumptions and scope
The degree is exactly four, not a low-pass collection of degrees zero through four; coefficients and harmonics are real; caps have the same angular radius and opposite centers. Cutoffs satisfy \(0<t<1\), so the caps have positive area and no overlap of positive area. Any normalization of the global surface measure cancels in \(Q_t\). At \(t=0\) all concentration eigenvalues equal one; at \(t=1\) they all equal zero. These degenerate endpoints are excluded from the strict classifications. Rotating the common polar axis does not change the theorem.

For \(m\ge1\), \(E_m\) is the span of the real and imaginary parts of \(P_4^m(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi}\); \(E_0\) is generated by \(P_4(\cos\theta)\). Here \(P_4(x)=(35x^4-30x^2+3)/8\), and an associated Legendre function is \(P_4^m(x)=(-1)^m(1-x^2)^{m/2}P_4^{(m)}(x)\). Any nonzero vector in a maximal (respectively minimal) eigenspace and only such vectors attains the corresponding sharp bound.

## Proof
**Step 1 (diagonalization and exact eigenvalues).** Azimuthal orthogonality eliminates cross terms of distinct orders; cosine/sine cross terms at the same order also integrate to zero on both the whole sphere and the symmetric caps. Therefore each order sector is an eigenspace. By reflecting the lower cap across the equator, the ratio on \(E_m\) is exactly
\[
\lambda_m(t)=\frac{\int_t^1 (1-u^2)^m[P_4^{(m)}(u)]^2\,du}{\int_0^1 (1-u^2)^m[P_4^{(m)}(u)]^2\,du}.
\]
Expanding and integrating yields all five exact polynomials:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_0(t)&=1-\tfrac{81}{64}t+\tfrac{135}{16}t^3-\tfrac{999}{32}t^5+\tfrac{675}{16}t^7-\tfrac{1225}{64}t^9,\\
\lambda_1(t)&=1-\tfrac{135}{16}t^3+\tfrac{459}{16}t^5-\tfrac{585}{16}t^7+\tfrac{245}{16}t^9,\\
\lambda_2(t)&=1-\tfrac{45}{32}t+\tfrac{15}{2}t^3-\tfrac{351}{16}t^5+\tfrac{45}{2}t^7-\tfrac{245}{32}t^9,\\
\lambda_3(t)&=1-\tfrac{105}{16}t^3+\tfrac{189}{16}t^5-\tfrac{135}{16}t^7+\tfrac{35}{16}t^9,\\
\lambda_4(t)&=1-\tfrac{315}{128}t+\tfrac{105}{32}t^3-\tfrac{189}{64}t^5+\tfrac{45}{32}t^7-\tfrac{35}{128}t^9.
\end{aligned}
\]

**Step 2 (decisive exact difference identities).** Write \(s=t^2\). Subtracting the polynomials gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_0-\lambda_3&=\tfrac{3t(1-t^2)}{64}(455s^3-625s^2+293s-27),\\
\lambda_0-\lambda_2&=\tfrac{3t(1-t^2)}{64}(7s-3)(35s^2-10s-1),\\
\lambda_0-\lambda_1&=\tfrac{9t(1-t^2)}{64}(7s-3)(35s^2-30s+3),\\
\lambda_2-\lambda_3&=\tfrac{45t(1-t^2)^3}{32}(7s-1),\\
\lambda_1-\lambda_3&=\tfrac{15t^3(1-t^2)^2}{8}(7s-1),\\
\lambda_1-\lambda_2&=\tfrac{15t(1-t^2)^2}{32}(7s-3)(7s-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The cubic's derivative \(1365s^2-1250s+293\) has negative discriminant \(-37280\) and positive leading coefficient, so the cubic strictly increases on the entire real line. Its values at \(0\) and \(1/7\) have opposite signs. Hence \(0<\alpha<1/\sqrt7\), with a unique crossing of orders zero and three.

For \(0<t<1/\sqrt7\), order three exceeds orders one and two, so comparison with order zero alone establishes order three on \((0,\alpha)\) and order zero on \((\alpha,1/\sqrt7)\). For \(1/\sqrt7<t<\gamma\), order two exceeds orders one and three; \(\lambda_0-\lambda_2\) changes sign exactly once at \(\beta\). For \(\gamma<t<1\), order one exceeds orders two and three; \(\lambda_0-\lambda_1\) changes sign only at \(\delta\) in this interval. These comparisons prove the five upper phases. The factorized equalities prove all transitions and prohibit additional maximizers at their boundaries.

**Step 3 (strict lower bound).** The four remaining differences are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_0-\lambda_4&=\tfrac{3t(1-t^2)}{128}(805s^3-935s^2+271s+51),\\
\lambda_1-\lambda_4&=\tfrac{15t(1-t^2)^2}{128}(133s^2-58s+21),\\
\lambda_2-\lambda_4&=\tfrac{135t(1-t^2)^3}{128}(7s+1),\\
\lambda_3-\lambda_4&=\tfrac{315t(1-t^2)^4}{128}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first bracket equals \(51+s(805s^2-935s+271)\), which is bounded below for \(0\le s\le1\) by \(51-1605/3220>0\), since the quadratic has minimum \(-1605/3220\). The second bracket has negative discriminant and positive leading coefficient. The other two brackets are manifestly positive. Thus \(\lambda_4<\lambda_m\) for all \(m<4\), throughout \(0<t<1\).

**Step 4 (multiplicities and sharpness).** The dimensions of \(E_m\) are \(1,2,2,2,2\). The three and five-dimensional transition multiplicities follow from the strict comparisons in Steps 2–3. Substitution of \(t=\sqrt{3/7}\) in the five polynomials gives the stated exact resonance values. The Rayleigh quotient of the diagonal concentration form is a weighted average of its sector eigenvalues, with equality precisely on the direct sum of maximizing or minimizing sectors. This proves all claims.

## Relationship to prior work
Simons, Dahlen and Wieczorek (2004 preprint, 2006 article) establish spherical spatiospectral concentration and the azimuthal decomposition of its matrix. Simons and Dahlen (2006) explicitly formulate the *antipodal double-cap* concentration matrix and study general-bandwidth spectra and commuting operators. Those formulas motivate the present fixed-degree restriction but do not state the five sharp order phases, the reentrant zonal maximizing region, or the exact fivefold resonance for \(\mathcal H_4\). Gilbert and Slepian (1977) consider concentration of finite-degree polynomial spaces on intervals, an important general precursor but a different optimization across polynomial degrees rather than across the five associated-Legendre orders of one irreducible spherical harmonic space. A related degree-three fixed-harmonic phase classification does not imply the degree-four result: here a new order-two upper phase and a second zonal phase occur, with four crossings instead of two.

## Limitations
This result is restricted to real degree-four harmonics on \(\mathbb S^2\) and two equal antipodal caps. It does not solve the larger low-pass spherical Slepian problem, classify general regions, or prove a uniform all-degree law. The original contributions asserted here are the exact global mode-order classification, strict bottom sector, and transition multiplicities—not the already known general concentration-matrix construction. An older relevant polynomial-concentration paper was accessible only through bibliographic and abstract material; an equivalent unindexed historical classification remains possible. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
