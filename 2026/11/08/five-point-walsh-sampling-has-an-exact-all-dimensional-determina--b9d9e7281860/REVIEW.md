# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The two affine row-set types exhaust all five-point subsets for every binary dimension at least four. The exact local determinant distributions were established over all \(56+4368\) projected column sets using two independent integer determinant algorithms; the quotient-lifting formula proves the dimension-uniform histogram. The independent Cauchy--Binet identity agrees with the result. No finite extrapolation is used to claim the theorem for all dimensions.

## Originality
PASS with residual historical-priority risk. The order-16 absolute minor histogram is **already included in the finite tables**, with all four numbers independently matching the order-16(a) table of Brent and Osborn, *On minors of maximal determinant matrices*, and the maximum order-five determinant \(48\) is classical. Neither is claimed as original. A preceding all-dimensional four-by-four Walsh rank census uses related projection methods, but it has neither five-point affine rank-four masks nor maximum-volume \(48\) configurations. The present statement is a uniform formula in \(Q=2^d\) for all dimensions, including an affine-orbit explanation and every maximizing five-point sampling configuration. Brent--Osborn's finite tables, general mean-square theorem, and the prior finite Fourier uncertainty rank census do not themselves assert or logically imply this all-dimensional classification without new orbit-and-lift arguments. The accessible primary works have been compared on the actual determinant-counting objects, not just their titles. Historical priority may remain hidden in design or code-weight enumerators.

## Value
PASS. The complete five-by-five determinant magnitude distribution measures the frequency of singular sampling masks, the different nonzero discrete volumes, and all maximum-volume configurations for the infinite Sylvester family. It supplies a natural small-order benchmark between general minor mean-square theory and finite Fourier restricted-sampling uncertainty. It goes beyond the previously tabulated single-order counts and has a nontrivial limiting law and geometric optimizer characterization.

## Closest literature and limitations
Brent--Osborn (arXiv:1208.3819) reports a finite census through matrix order \(21\), covering the special order-16 numerical distribution; Brent--Osborn (arXiv:1208.3330) gives the Cauchy--Binet moment but not the full higher-dimensional histogram. Kravvaritis--Mitrouli (ETNA 2008) provides general minor algorithms and possible values for binary Hadamard cores. Krahmer--Pfander--Rashkov (arXiv:math/0611493) treats Fourier support/rank rather than subdeterminant magnitude. The result is restricted to five-point masks of binary Sylvester Fourier transforms. Independent validation and exhaustive historical priority are not claimed.
