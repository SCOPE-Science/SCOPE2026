# Review: five rational point orbits on the characteristic-three Clebsch cubic

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is exact over the field of nine elements, with the standard characteristic-three Clebsch model \(\sigma_1=\sigma_3=0\). The proof includes the necessary smoothness check and two exhaustive line orbits of respective sizes \(15\) and \(12\), giving all \(27\) geometric lines. The five rational orbit representatives satisfy the equation. Their computed stabilizer orders \(12,8,4,4,2\) give orbit sizes \(10,15,30,30,60\). The orbits are pairwise disjoint, and their sizes sum to the independently established \(\(145=q^2+7q+1\)\) rational points of a split cubic surface at \(q=9\). Both actual standalone programs checked all crucial arithmetic claims: one from group orbits, the other by exhaustive point and line census. The general cubic-surface point-count theorem and \(27\)-line theorem are standard; the two group/line certificates are checked from source.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority caveat
Dolgachev–Duncan, *Automorphisms of cubic surfaces in positive characteristic* (arXiv:1712.01167, initial 2017-12-04), provides the characteristic-three model and identifies the automorphism group \(S_5\). Betten–Hirschfeld–Karaoglu (2017), Table 1 and Corollary 3.2, already gives the split \(q=9\) diagonal surface, its 10 Eckardt points, projective automorphism-group order 120, and its total rational points. Betten–Karaoglu (2018 preprint / 2019 journal) enumerates split cubic-surface classes and group orders, including semilinear symmetry; its orbit algorithms classify surfaces and line configurations, rather than the 145 individual rational points on this exceptional surface. The new result is the complete **five-orbit rational-point stratification**, the two nonisomorphic order-four stabilizers on different orbits, the orbit-specific incidence counts, and the absence of a free point orbit. These are neither a restatement of the equation nor a direct consequence of just the number 145, the order 120, or the ten Eckardt points. No exact-dominating statement was identified in relevant accessible sources or mathematical-record searches. Historical finite-geometric tables from older work remain a residual priority risk; search absence is not a proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The smallest extension supporting a split characteristic-three Clebsch cubic is a naturally distinguished arithmetic setting. Describing **all** its rational points by projective symmetry, not just their cardinality, makes the geometry of the exceptional line-covering case explicit and separates two distinct stabilizer types at equal orbit size. The uniform involution-fixed-point property is a structural consequence that does not follow from surface-wide point counts or Eckardt counts alone. This is a natural complete finite classification with no arbitrary choice of a numerical slice. It does not generalize to larger finite fields and makes no assertion about their orbit spectra.

## Closest literature and limitations
The direct primary comparisons were the full accessible Dolgachev–Duncan preprint (normal forms and characteristic-three group theory), the open-access Betten–Hirschfeld–Karaoglu article (Table 1, Sections 2–5), and the full-text Betten–Karaoglu preprint (Tables 1–3 and orbit-classification sections).
