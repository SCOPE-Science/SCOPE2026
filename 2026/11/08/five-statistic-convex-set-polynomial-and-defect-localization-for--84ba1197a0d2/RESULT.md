# Five-statistic convex-set polynomial and defect localization for standard-example posets

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(S_n\) be the standard-example poset on \(a_1,\ldots,a_n,b_1,\ldots,b_n\), with \(a_i<b_j\) exactly when \(i\ne j\), and no other strict comparabilities. Let \(H_n\) denote the complete height-two poset obtained by restoring all \(a_i<b_i\). Every subset of either poset is convex.

For a finite poset \(P\), write \(\Phi(P;s,t,x,y,z)\) for the polynomial
\[
\sum_{X\subseteq P\text{ convex}}
 s^{|\min X|}t^{|\max X|}x^{|\downarrow\max X|}y^{|\uparrow\min X|}z^{|X|},
\]
where the upward and downward closures are computed in the **ambient** poset and the contribution of the empty set is \(1\). Use \(\binom{n}{j}=0\) when \(j\notin\{0,\dots,n\}\), and set \(\delta_j=1\) for \(j=1\), \(0\) otherwise. Then the entire five-variable polynomial of \(S_n\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(S_n;s,t,x,y,z)={}&1
+\sum_{a=1}^{n}\binom na s^at^a x^a y^{n+a-\delta_a}z^a
+\sum_{b=1}^{n}\binom nb s^bt^b x^{n+b-\delta_b} y^b z^b\\
&+\sum_{a=2}^{n}\sum_{b=2}^{n}\binom na\binom nb
s^at^b x^{n+b}y^{n+a}z^{a+b}\\
&+\sum_{b=2}^{n}\binom nb t^b x^{n+b}z^{b+1}
\big((n-b)s y^n+b s^2 y^{n+1}\big)\\
&+\sum_{a=2}^{n}\binom na s^a y^{n+a}z^{a+1}
\big((n-a)t x^n+a t^2 x^{n+1}\big)\\
&+n(n-1)st x^ny^nz^2
+n s^2t^2 x^{n+1}y^{n+1}z^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Under the identity matching of the elements of \(S_n\) and \(H_n\), the five-tuple of statistics \((|\min X|,|\max X|,|\downarrow\max X|,|\uparrow\min X|,|X|)\) is **unchanged** for exactly
\[
\boxed{(2^n-n)^2}
\]
of the \(4^n\) labelled subsets \(X\). It changes for precisely \(2n2^n-n^2\) subsets. Consequently, under a uniformly chosen subset, the probability that all five statistics survive deletion of the perfect matching is exactly \((1-n/2^n)^2\), which tends to one exponentially quickly. Yet the polynomial detects deletion: the coefficient of \(s^2t^2x^{n+1}y^{n+1}z^2\) equals \(n\) for \(S_n\) and \(0\) for \(H_n\).

## Assumptions and scope

All posets are finite, with the strict height-two comparabilities specified above. The two levels have \(n\) individually distinguished elements each. Polynomial coefficients count subsets, not isomorphism classes; the lower-level and upper-level selections are allowed to be empty. The statistic \(|\downarrow\max X|\) counts the full order ideal generated by the maximal elements of \(X\), and \(|\uparrow\min X|\) counts the full upset generated by its minimal elements; neither is computed within \(X\) alone. The parameters \(s,t,x,y,z\) are commuting indeterminates. The formula is asserted for every \(n\ge3\), including the boundary at \(n=3\), without asymptotic assumptions.

## Proof

Write any subset uniquely as \(X=A\cup B\), where \(A\subseteq\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\), \(B\subseteq\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}\), and \(a=|A|\), \(b=|B|\). Since both posets have height two, there is no element strictly between two comparable distinct elements, so every subset is order-convex.

If \(a,b\ge2\), every selected upper element has a selected lower predecessor, and every selected lower element has a selected upper successor: each upper element misses only one possible lower predecessor, and each lower element misses only one possible upper successor. Thus \(\min X=A\), \(\max X=B\), and their upward and downward closures respectively contain all \(n\) upper and all \(n\) lower elements. The statistical tuple is \((a,b,n+b,n+a,a+b)\), with \(\binom na\binom nb\) choices. This gives the double sum.

If \(b=0\), the tuple is \((a,a,a,n+a-\delta_a,a)\): a singleton lower element has only \(n-1\) strict upper neighbours in \(S_n\), while two distinct lower elements together dominate all upper elements. This yields the first single sum. By order duality, if \(a=0\), the tuple is \((b,b,n+b-\delta_b,b,b)\), giving the second single sum.

If \(a=1\), say \(A=\{a_i\}\), and \(b\ge2\), the set \(\max X\) consists of the \(b\) selected upper elements. All lower elements occur in its downward closure, so \(|\downarrow\max X|=n+b\). If \(b_i\notin B\), the singleton \(a_i\) is the unique minimum and its upward closure has size \(n\); the tuple is \((1,b,n+b,n,b+1)\). For each choice of \(B\), there are \(n-b\) such \(i\). If \(b_i\in B\), then \(b_i\) is an additional minimum, while the upward closure has size \(n+1\), so the tuple is \((2,b,n+b,n+1,b+1)\); there are \(b\) such \(i\). This gives the fourth displayed sum. The dual argument for \(b=1,a\ge2\) gives the fifth sum.

For \(a=b=1\), if the indices differ, the selected elements are comparable: there are \(n(n-1)\) choices with tuple \((1,1,n,n,2)\). If the indices agree, they are incomparable: there are \(n\) choices with tuple \((2,2,n+1,n+1,2)\). These are the last two terms. Together these seven disjoint cases exhaust all \(4^n\) subsets, completing the polynomial proof.

For comparison with \(H_n\), all five statistics agree when \(a,b\ge2\), when \(a\ge2,b=0\), when \(a=0,b\ge2\), or when \(a=b=0\). In every other case at least one statistic changes: for singleton-only selections an ambient closure changes, for one singleton level and a nonempty other level either the closure or a set of extremal elements changes, and for \(a=b=1\) a closure changes if the indices differ, while both extremal-set sizes change if they agree. The number of unchanged subsets is therefore
\[
1+2\big(2^n-n-1\big)+\big(2^n-n-1\big)^2=(2^n-n)^2.
\]
Subtracting from \(4^n\) and dividing by \(4^n\) proves the probability assertions. The only way to obtain two minima, two maxima, two selected elements and **both** closure sizes \(n+1\) in \(S_n\) is to select an incomparable diagonal pair \(\{a_i,b_i\}\). Two selected elements in one level instead have closure sizes \((2,n+2)\) or \((n+2,2)\). The poset \(H_n\) has no incomparable cross-level pairs, so the stated distinguishing coefficient is zero there. No independence hypothesis is used.

## Verification

The standalone script `artifacts/verify.py` constructs each ordered relation directly, enumerates **all** \(2^{2n}\) labelled subsets for \(2\le n\le7\), compares each complete five-statistic coefficient with the formula, and independently compares the corresponding signatures under \(S_n\) and \(H_n\) through \(n=8\). It also checks the summed formula and two boundary coefficients for \(2\le n\le100\). It prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. These finite experiments are consistency checks only; the proof above covers every integer \(n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work

George and Yeats introduced the five-variable generalized-interval polynomial in the public November 18, 2025 preprint cited below; the October 16, 2026 follow-up uses the equivalent unique-convex-set presentation. Its treatment of disjoint unions and ordinal sums includes an explicit complete height-two polynomial, while its additional examples cover chains and two-row product posets. The present calculation addresses the standard examples \(S_n\) of arbitrarily large order dimension, which are not obtained from the complete height-two poset by the permitted disjoint-union or ordinal-sum formulas once \(n\ge3\). The explicit all-coefficient formula and sharp defect-localization law are not stated in the inspected source material. The general definition of the polynomial and the elementary convexity of height-two posets are prior background, not claimed as new. The comparison is one of statements and their implications, not a claim of exhaustive historical priority.

## Limitations

This result applies to the standard example with precisely one missing cross-relation per lower element. It does not give a general deletion theorem for arbitrary height-two posets, nor an enumeration of all possible order types of the convex subsets. Every subset is convex in a height-two poset, so the novelty lies in the full five-statistic polynomial and the exact perturbation law rather than the unrefined \(4^n\) count. Although the main motivating sources were read in relevant full-text sections and targeted searches were performed, unindexed earlier literature may contain equivalent specializations. Historical priority is provisional, and the result is not extended to general matching deletions.
