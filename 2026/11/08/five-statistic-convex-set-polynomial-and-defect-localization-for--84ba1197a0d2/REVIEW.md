# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** A height-two poset makes every subset convex. The proof partitions selections uniquely by their two shore cardinalities and whether singleton indices coincide; all seven cases are exhaustive and their closure sizes follow from the omitted diagonal comparabilities. The formula has the correct \(4^n\) total at all variables equal to one. The exact affected-subset count follows from explicit necessary and sufficient statistical equality classes, not an unproved probabilistic approximation. A separately coded direct brute-force enumerator agrees with every coefficient through \(n=7\) and verifies the perturbation count through \(n=8\). These experiments are not an infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS (provisional historical priority).** The closely related November 2025 and October 2026 preprints of George and Yeats define the invariant, develop poset-operation formulas, and explicitly treat complete height-two, chain and two-row products; no complete standard-example formula or deleted-matching statistical agreement count was located in their inspected sections. The standard example's definition and order dimension are classical. Searches for equivalent five-variable generalized-interval/convex-set polynomials, missing-perfect-matching comparability, and standard examples located no covering result. The general polynomial definition alone is not the explicit theorem; the complete height-two expression does not formally imply the new defect formula without analysing the deleted comparabilities. The two preprints belong to the same research lineage, not independent negative evidence. Further unindexed or unpublished enumerations remain a risk.

## Value

**PASS.** The standard examples are canonical height-two posets of arbitrarily large order dimension, outside the series-parallel family emphasized in the motivating work. The full five-variable polynomial gives the joint extremal-antichain/closure/size distribution, not just its trivial total; the exact agreement law quantifies how a large structured deletion can become exponentially invisible to randomly selected convex subsets. The coefficient distinguishing the two posets remains nonzero at every order, showing simultaneous high-probability stability and exact algebraic detectability. This provides a concrete benchmark for the discriminatory and stability behaviour of the recently introduced invariant.

## Scope and remaining uncertainty

No general matching-deletion theorem is asserted beyond this family. Published source coverage is not exhaustive, and full independent validation has not been performed.
