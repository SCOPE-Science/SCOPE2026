# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every algebraically closed characteristic-seven ground field, smoothness is equivalent to \(b\Delta
e0\), with \(\Delta\) the explicit discriminant of \(z^3+z^2+az+b\). The degree-ten separable hyperelliptic model has genus four. The order-three quotient is the elliptic curve \(Y^2=X(X^3+X^2+aX+b)\); its Hasse coefficient is \(u\). A direct independent symbolic multinomial computation reconstructs every entry of the genus-four Cartier matrix. Its distinguished rank-two block has determinant \(3b^2v\), yielding \(3b^2uv^2\) for the full Hasse determinant. On \(v=0\), \(u=-a^2(a+2)^2\) and smoothness excludes its zeroes; the block has rank one. The Cartier *stable* rank of this block drops from one to zero exactly when \(2a^3+(a+1)^5=0\), as follows from \(M^{(7)}M\), not an ordinary square over general coefficient fields. The quintic is separable and disjoint from the excluded parameters. Rank calculations, prime-to-seven Prym additivity and scheme-theoretic minor identities reconstruct all four strata. Python checks were rerun on the packaged programs. No finite check is presented as an infinite proof. Residual mathematical risk: conventions for semilinear Cartier iteration or Prym decomposition would be material; the proof states and independently derives both.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Kudo–Nakagawa–Takagi, arXiv:2210.14822, introduce exactly this curve family and give the general Cartier coefficient-position formula and a superspecial-search algorithm. Their relevant Sections 2.2 and 3.1 and reported Theorems A/B do not provide a characteristic-seven four-stratum geometric \(7\)-rank classification, a five-point rank-zero Prym locus, or the reduced-versus-double Hasse divisor structure. The general formula is acknowledged as prior, and the present work requires explicit specialization, elimination and semilinear iteration. Li–Mantovan–Pries–Tang, arXiv:1805.06914, address Newton strata of Moonen's twenty special cyclic-cover families; their listed sextic monodromy data do not match the \((6;1,2,3,3,3)\) inertia here. Targeted published-finding corpus searches found no implication covering this theorem, and existing related coefficient classifications in characteristic five or on different genus-four families do not cover this complementary congruence class. A priority risk remains from unindexed calculations or unpublished explicit consequences of broader algorithms.

## Value — PASS
The two-parameter genus-four \(C_6\) family is a focal object of a published superspecial-curve algorithm. Its *first* characteristic \(7\) with \(7\equiv1\pmod3\) has a qualitatively different behavior from the characteristic-five case: generic ordinary Jacobians, a whole divisor with Prym rank one, and exactly five reduced points where the threefold Prym has rank zero, while Prym rank two never occurs. The distinction between a reduced Cartier-rank-drop scheme and a doubled Hasse divisor is a substantive geometry-of-strata observation, not merely an extra example or a routine numerical re-enumeration. The five-point count is attached to a natural invariant defined before calculation. It is not claimed that any new general theorem on all genus-four curves follows.

## Relationship to literature and limitations
The explicit coefficient family, its elliptic quotient, Cartier formula and classification of possible automorphism groups are prior work (arXiv:2210.14822). The comparison with special cyclic-cover Newton tables (arXiv:1805.06914) concerns possible stronger coverage, not authorship. This result makes no claim about geometric isomorphism classes, the full Newton polygon, other characteristics or complete moduli of genus-four curves. Exact reproducibility code is included.
