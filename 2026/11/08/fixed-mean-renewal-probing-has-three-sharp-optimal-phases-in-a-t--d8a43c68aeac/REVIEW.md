# Scientific review: fixed-mean renewal probing phase diagram

## Correctness — PASS
The claim fixes the exact Hamiltonian, initial and target rays, renewal law, mean constraint, and strict-positive interval convention. A two-by-two unitary calculation yields \(p(\tau)=\alpha\sin^2(\Omega\tau)\), and the rank-one failed-projector yields a genuine regeneration after every unsuccessful probe. Tonelli's theorem then gives \(\mathbb ET_{\mathrm{det}}=\mu/[\alpha\mathbb E\sin^2(\Omega\tau)]\), without requiring the attempt duration to be independent of its outcome. The explicit concave majorant of \(\sin^2x\) proves a **global** mean-constrained optimum for arbitrary integrable positive waiting times; all equality and nonattainment cases follow from its contact sets. The verification program produced 3061 passing checks, including detuned Hamiltonians, but no finite enumeration is presented as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (with publication-priority risk)
Kulkarni–Majumdar's 2023 Sections 4 and 6, especially equation (135), give the renewal-mean identity and Poissonian detection physics, not a prescribed-mean optimization over all waiting laws. Kessler–Barkai–Ziegler's 2020 paper proves the generic mean-time identity and explores fixed/gamma/exponential probing at matched means, without a three-piece fixed-mean global optimization. The later public fully connected quantum result obtains the *unconstrained* all-renewal optimum with the same tangent-root constant; that portion and the low-mean limiting value are acknowledged as prior coverage. The central new statement instead determines the exact **function of the prescribed mean**, the deterministic-to-resonant optimizer transition, and all support/equality/nonattainment cases. Neither an indexed equivalent nor broader result explicitly implying this full phase law was located. Standard concave-envelope optimization and an unindexed general restart theorem remain genuine novelty risks; independent literature review is needed before publication.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A prescribed average probe spacing is a natural measurement-budget constraint, distinct from choosing the best free sampling rate. The result proves that high-frequency measurements need not exhibit divergent Zeno detection times if intervals can be randomized; establishes when deterministic sampling is exactly optimal; and shows that slower budgets are optimally spent at resonant intervals instead. Its exact transitions and optimizer classification are structural and operationally interpretable, not an arbitrary parameter substitution. Real implementations with minimum inter-measurement gaps, nonzero probe costs or feedback require separate analysis.

Primary source: M. Kulkarni and S. N. Majumdar, arXiv:2305.15123, 2023-05-24, Sections 4 and 6. Earlier measurement framework: D. A. Kessler, E. Barkai and K. Ziegler, arXiv:2012.01763, 2020-12-03. Scientific limitations are exact two-state projective dynamics, i.i.d. intervals, and no minimum-duration or measurement-cost constraint. The short-mean optimum is an infimum, not a positive-wait minimizer.
