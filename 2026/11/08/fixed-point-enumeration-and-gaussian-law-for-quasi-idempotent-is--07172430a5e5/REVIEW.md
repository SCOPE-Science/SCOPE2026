# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The basin decomposition is bijective: second-iterate fibers of a monotone map are intervals; the map preserves each fiber, and each fiber has a unique fixed root. The threshold \(t\) on either side gives exactly \(2^{L-1}\) possibilities for positive side length \(L\), independently on both sides. These counts lead to the stated rational bivariate series after subtracting idempotents. For the central limit theorem, the proper-map subtraction is exponentially smaller near the positive dominant simple pole of the all-depth-at-most-two series, and the basin-length distribution has positive variance. Exhaustive enumerations through degree ten and transfer-function checks through degree sixteen corroborate the algebra. The analytic proof, not the finite checks, establishes the result for every degree.

The OEIS A060557 univariate height-at-most-two sequence and its recurrence are acknowledged as pre-existing data, not independent novelty. No inspected primary source or searched research result gives the combined fixed-point-refined result. ## Value — PASS
Quasi-idempotents are natural generating elements of full order-preserving transformation monoids. The exact counts by fixed points quantify their internal structure rather than only their generator rank; the Gaussian law and exponential rarity show a nontrivial typical behavior and distinguish structural importance from numerical abundance. The all-degree refinement resolves a concrete enumerative and asymptotic question motivated by the existing classification literature rather than a random finite numerical slice.
