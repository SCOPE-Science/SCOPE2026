# Fixed-residue rank congruence for modular skew group algebras

## Finding

**Theorem (fixed-residue rank obstruction).** Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic \(p>0\), let \(A\ne0\) be a commutative unital \(k\)-algebra with connected \(\operatorname{Spec}A\), and let a finite \(p\)-group \(G\) act on \(A\) by \(k\)-algebra automorphisms. Assume there is a \(G\)-stable maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) on whose residue field \(K=A/\mathfrak m\) the induced \(G\)-action is trivial. Form the untwisted skew group algebra \(R=A\rtimes G\).

For every integer \(n\ge1\) and every idempotent \(E\in M_n(R)\), the image of right multiplication by \(E\) on the free left module \(R^n\), considered as an \(A\)-module, has constant rank belonging to
\[
\{0,|G|,2|G|,\ldots,n|G|\}.
\]
Every listed rank occurs. Consequently every finitely generated projective left \(R\)-module has \(A\)-rank divisible by \(|G|\); the image of the constant-rank homomorphism on \(K_0(R)\) is precisely \(|G|\mathbb Z\); and **the only idempotents of \(R\) are \(0\) and \(1\)**.

**Infinite-dihedral specialization.** For every field \(k\) of characteristic \(2\), \(k[D_\infty]\) has exactly two idempotents. Every finitely generated projective module has even rank when restricted to \(k[t,t^{-1}]\).

**Sharp counterexample without a fixed residue.** For \(A=k[x]\) and \(G=C_p\) acting by translations \(s(x)=x+1\), the skew group algebra has a nontrivial idempotent
\[
e=-x^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}s^i
\]
whose image in the regular left module has underlying \(A\)-rank \(1\). In particular the fixed-residue assumption cannot simply be deleted.

## Assumptions and scope

The action is untwisted, and \(G\) is a finite \(p\)-group. The base algebra \(A\) need not be a domain, Noetherian, finitely generated, or reduced. The connectedness assumption means there are no nontrivial idempotents in \(A\), equivalently the rank of any finite projective \(A\)-module is constant. The residue-field condition means that \(\mathfrak m\) is stable under \(G\) and each element acts as identity on \(A/\mathfrak m\); a \(G\)-fixed \(k\)-rational point is sufficient, but not necessary.

The rank of an \(A\)-projective module is its common finite local rank on connected \(\operatorname{Spec}A\). No inference is made about whether projective \(R\)-modules are free, or about the kernel of the rank homomorphism on \(K_0(R)\).

## Proof

**1. Restriction and constant rank.** As a left \(A\)-module, \(R=\bigoplus_{g\in G}Ag\) is free of rank \(|G|\). Given \(E^2=E\) in \(M_n(R)\), right multiplication on row vectors in \(R^n\) is a left \(R\)-linear, hence left \(A\)-linear, idempotent. Its image \(P\) is a direct summand of \(A^{n|G|}\), hence is a finitely generated projective \(A\)-module. Its local rank is a locally constant function on \(\operatorname{Spec}A\), and therefore is a constant integer \(d\) because this spectrum is connected.

**2. The fixed fiber.** Since \(\mathfrak m\) is stable, \(\mathfrak m R\) is a two-sided ideal, and the trivial induced action on \(K\) yields a ring isomorphism
\[
R/\mathfrak m R\cong K[G].
\]
Direct-summand decomposition commutes with reduction, so \(P/\mathfrak m P\) is the image of the idempotent \(\overline E\in M_n(K[G])\). It is a finitely generated projective left \(K[G]\)-module.

**3. The modular group algebra is local.** For any field \(K\) of characteristic \(p\) and any finite \(p\)-group \(G\), \(K[G]\) is local. One elementary induction starts with a central subgroup \(\langle z\rangle\) of order \(p\) when \(G\ne1\). The central ideal generated by \(z-1\) is nilpotent, since \((z-1)^p=z^p-1=0\). The quotient is \(K[G/\langle z\rangle]\), which is local by induction; lifting across a nilpotent ideal preserves locality. The trivial group gives the field \(K\). A finitely generated projective left module over any local ring is free. Thus for some integer \(j\) with \(0\le j\le n\),
\[
P/\mathfrak m P\cong (K[G])^j.
\]
Taking dimensions over \(K\) and using constancy of \(A\)-rank gives \(d=j|G|\). This proves both divisibility and the advertised finite list.

**4. Attainment, idempotents, and Grothendieck rank.** For each \(j=0,\ldots,n\), the diagonal matrix with \(j\) identity diagonal entries and \(n-j\) zero entries attains rank \(j|G|\). Every finitely generated projective \(R\)-module is a summand of some \(R^n\), so all its \(A\)-ranks are divisible by \(|G|\). The image of the induced rank map \(K_0(R)\to\mathbb Z\) is therefore contained in \(|G|\mathbb Z\), while \([R]\) maps to \(|G|\), proving equality. When \(n=1\), an idempotent has image \(A\)-rank either \(0\) or \(|G|\). A finite projective \(A\)-module of rank \(0\) is zero, and its complement in \(A^{|G|}\) is zero when the rank is full. Thus the idempotent is respectively \(0\) or \(1\).

**5. Infinite dihedral groups.** Let \(D_\infty=\langle t,s\mid s^2=1,sts=t^{-1}\rangle\). Over a field of characteristic \(2\), its group algebra is \(k[t,t^{-1}]\rtimes C_2\). The Laurent algebra is a domain, hence connected. Its maximal ideal \((t-1)\) is stable under inversion, and its residue is \(k\) with trivial induced action. The theorem applies with \(|G|=2\). An independent direct check works for arbitrary Laurent support: writing an idempotent as \(f+gs\), with \(f,g\in k[t,t^{-1}]\) and \(\sigma(f)(t)=f(t^{-1})\), its \(s\)-coefficient equation in characteristic \(2\) reads \(g(f+\sigma(f))=g\). If \(g\ne0\), the Laurent domain forces \(f+\sigma(f)=1\); evaluation at the fixed point \(t=1\) gives \(0=1\), impossible. Therefore \(g=0\), and domain idempotence yields \(f=0\) or \(1\).

**6. Translation boundary.** Let \(s\) act on \(k[x]\) by \(s(x)=x+1\), so \(s^p=1\) in characteristic \(p\). Put \(N=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}s^i\) and \(a=-x^{p-1}\). The elementary finite-field polynomial identity
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(x+i)^{p-1}=-1
\]
holds in \(\mathbb F_p[x]\) (all positive-degree coefficients cancel, and the constant is \(-1\)). Therefore the orbit trace \(\sum_i s^i(a)=1\), whence \(NaN=N\) and \((aN)^2=aN\). Since the coefficients of both \(1\) and \(s\) in \(aN\) are nonzero, this idempotent is neither zero nor one. Moreover \(R(aN)=AN\): every \(b s^i(aN)=b s^i(a)N\), and \(\sum_i s^i(a)N=N\) proves \(N\) lies in the image. Thus the image has \(A\)-rank \(1\). Translation has no stable maximal ideal with trivial residue action: the relation \(x=x+1\) would hold in its residue field, which is impossible.

## Verification

The companion program `verify.py`, using only Python's standard library, enumerates all \(2^{2(2d+1)}\) characteristic-two Laurent-skew elements supported in exponents \(-d\) through \(d\) for \(d=0,1,2\), and finds only idempotents \(0,1\). It independently multiplies the translation idempotent and checks the polynomial trace for \(p=2,3,5,7\). Reproduction: run `python3 verify.py` and check `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks are not a proof for arbitrary coefficients or supports; the general theorem follows from the projective-module argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Dimitrov, Paquette, Wehlau and Xu (arXiv:2310.09591v1, posted 2023-10-14) classify idempotents for the infinite dihedral group algebra **over algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from two**. Their theorem does not address the characteristic-two consequence here; in that characteristic the usual equivalence between idempotents and involutions by \(2e-1\) is unavailable.

Liping Li, *Representations of modular skew group algebras* (2015), proves primitivity statements for idempotents in modular skew groups of finite-dimensional basic algebras. Its Lemma 3.1 uses locality of group algebras of stabilizer \(p\)-groups, a related mechanism; it does not state the present general fixed-residue rank congruence for arbitrary connected commutative base algebras, nor the translation boundary.

Bovdi–Mikhovski (1970), *Idempotents of crossed products*, includes theorems on **central** idempotents and supports, as well as idempotents for group algebras of locally nilpotent groups. Those hypotheses and conclusions do not give the stated noncentral idempotent and projective-rank classification for arbitrary untwisted modular skew products.

Lorenz (1985), *K0 of skew group rings and simple Noetherian rings without idempotents*, is a potentially broader related source. Only its abstract was accessible: it describes constructions of simple rings and computations of \(K_0\) for certain skew groups. Full-text implication comparison remains a publication-priority risk. Standard facts about projective ranks, local group rings, and \(K_0\) are used as prior tools, not claimed new.

## Limitations

The theorem does not address twisted crossed products or general groups whose orders have factors prime to the characteristic. It requires connectedness of the base spectrum and a stable maximal ideal with trivial action on its residue field. The rank restriction is a necessary condition on projective modules, not a classification up to isomorphism. The characteristic-two infinite-dihedral corollary concerns idempotents, not all involutions. Priority remains provisional pending any unavailable broader literature, especially Lorenz (1985).
