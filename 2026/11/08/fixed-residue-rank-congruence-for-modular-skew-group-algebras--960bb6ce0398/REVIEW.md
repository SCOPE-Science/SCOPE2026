# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces an arbitrary matrix idempotent to a direct summand of a finite free module over the connected commutative base. Its constant rank is read at a stable residue fiber, where the skew ring becomes a modular group algebra. A short induction proves this algebra local, so the reduced projective is free and its base-field dimension is divisible by the group order. Rank-zero/full cases establish the two-idempotent corollary; diagonal matrices prove exact attainment of every permitted rank. The polynomial trace identity and crossed-product multiplication prove that the translation counterexample is an idempotent of rank one. The attached code checks sample Laurent coefficients and translation examples. No finite test is substituted for the general argument.

## Originality — PASS, provisional publication priority

The 2023 dihedral classification explicitly assumes characteristic different from \(2\). Li's 2015 modular skew-group primitivity lemma treats finite-dimensional basic algebras and gives a related group-ring-locality mechanism but does not supply the connected-base fixed-residue projective rank theorem. Bovdi–Mikhovski's 1970 crossed-product theorems emphasize central supports and locally nilpotent group algebras rather than this arbitrary-base rank restriction. Mathematical-record and literature searches found no stated equivalent theorem. Lorenz's 1985 paper is highly relevant by title and \(K_0\), but its full text could not be inspected; stronger implication is a residual risk rather than a claimed exhaustive exclusion.

## Value — PASS

The full \(K_0\)-rank image \(|G|\mathbb Z\) is a structural invariant for an unrestricted natural class of modular skew products, and detects the absence of noncentral idempotents even when the base has zero divisors or infinitely many variables. It closes a concrete characteristic-two boundary left outside a recent dihedral classification. The sharp translation-action contrast demonstrates that the fixed-residue hypothesis controls a real jump in projective ranks rather than merely simplifying a proof. This is an exact structural constraint, not a full classification of projective modules.

## Limitations and risks

The theorem requires finite \(p\)-group, untwisted action, connected base spectrum and a fixed residue with trivial induced action. It does not classify nonfree projective classes or all positive-characteristic group rings. A potentially stronger 1985 \(K_0\) result has been identified only from its abstract; independent and publication-priority validation remain outstanding.
