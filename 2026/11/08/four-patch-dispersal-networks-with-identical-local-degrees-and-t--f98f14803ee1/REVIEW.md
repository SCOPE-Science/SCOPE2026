# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact finite graph enumeration is small and independently checkable by two characteristic-polynomial algorithms. The five realizations, their degrees, strong connectivity, reciprocity, polynomials, and positive Perron-root ordering were checked. A direct Perron–Frobenius argument establishes the local linear stability threshold. The proof does not use finite experiments to infer a theorem for larger network sizes.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence; historical priority remains provisional.** Shtilerman and Stone (2015) provide the metapopulation spectral threshold and approximate in/out-degree-correlation and symmetry comparisons at large scale, not the exact minimal four-patch and five-route realization classification in the scientific passages inspected. Kwapisz (1996) gives general digraph spectral-radius bounds; Bıyıkoğlu and Leydold (2008) concerns undirected degree sequences. Targeted published-finding corpus and literature searches retrieved no claim implying this exact finite classification. Access to complete original text for some material was restricted; no claim of proven historical uniqueness is made.

**Value — PASS.** The exact smallest network and smallest dispersal budget identify where commonly recorded local connectivity and reciprocity data first fail to determine a mathematically relevant patch-invasion threshold. The three fully classified realizations provide a compact test case for interpreting degree-correlation proxies. Its value is a boundary/classification within a natural ecological model, not a global-network theorem or a field prediction.

**Scientific limitations.** All edges are unit weight, and stability is local to extinction in a deterministic occupancy model. The selected five-route degree profile is not asserted to represent every larger graph, and the equality-threshold case is excluded. Historical priority may be affected by unindexed small-digraph spectral tables or unavailable text.
