# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The general statement is reduced by translation and rotation to the Joukowski ellipse. For every input of degree at most \(d\), multiplying its angular intensity by \(w^{2d}\) produces a polynomial of degree at most \(4d\) with exactly equal extreme coefficients \((ab)^d|c_d|^2\). The coefficient constraint and the nonunit product of the distinct nodes give the stated interpolation formula without a rank assumption. Equal complete intensities force the rational quotient to be invariant under two distinct involutions; their composition is dilation by \((b/a)^2\), which has infinite order and hence admits only constant rational invariants. The exceptional equispaced-offset example is an exact polynomial identity and proves genuine nonuniqueness rather than mere failure of a linear method. The real-profile dimension calculation uses polarization and a triangular harmonic basis; its conclusion is restricted to complete-profile linear interpolation. The supplementary checker validates 64 finite identities/comparisons, but every quantified assertion rests on the written proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

The closest full primary text inspected is Chalendar–Partington's 2024 analysis of pairs of circles and lines, which has no theorem for a single ellipse with finite nodes or an exact angular-offset criterion. Pohl–Li–Boche's 2017 two-circle construction attains the same generic four-per-degree count in a different measurement geometry and cannot be read as a one-ellipse result. The studied continuous line results of Wellershoff and Liehr do not directly cover the claimed ellipse grid. The nearest relevant prior polynomial measurement constructions on pairs of circles and rays do not imply this statement via a degree-preserving transformation. Meaningful novelty is the complete good/bad angular-shift classification on one noncircular ellipse and its explicit resonance witness, not the already known general measurement count. Search and literature access cannot guarantee historical uniqueness; an unlocated general algebraic-curve result remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result gives a checkable deterministic sampling rule, a complete and sharp offset classification for a natural maximally symmetric family, and explicit counterexamples at every forbidden shift. The exact profile interpolation formula separates the finite-sampling problem from rational phase rigidity. This is a natural analytical inverse problem, not a numerical slice or a repackaging of the general frame-count inequality. It does not establish noise-uniform stability, optimal nonlinear measurement count, or a stable factorization algorithm.
