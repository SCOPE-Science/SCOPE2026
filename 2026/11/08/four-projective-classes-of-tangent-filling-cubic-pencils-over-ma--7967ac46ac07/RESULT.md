# Four projective classes of tangent-filling cubic pencils over \(\mathbb F_3\)

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_3\). A *tangent-filling cubic pencil* is a two-dimensional vector subspace \(U\subset k[X,Y,Z]_3\) such that all four members of \(\mathbb P(U)(k)\) define geometrically smooth cubics, and at every point of \(\mathbb P^2(k)\) there passes the tangent line at a smooth \(k\)-rational point of each member. The tangent-filling condition is imposed separately on **each** member; non-rational members of the pencil are not asserted smooth.

There are exactly **22,464** such pencils. They form **four free orbits** of size **5,616** under \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(k)\), hence four projective-equivalence classes. No vector subspace of dimension three or more has all its nonzero \(k\)-projective members smooth and tangent-filling. Consequently, projective dimension one is the **sharp maximum** for a linear system with this simultaneous property.

In two of the four classes, the four members have \(k\)-rational point counts \((6,6,6,7)\) and the base complete intersection has four \(k\)-points. In the other two classes, member counts are \((4,4,7,7)\) and \((4,6,6,6)\), respectively, and the base has three \(k\)-points. The base scheme of every qualifying pencil is **geometrically reduced** and has degree nine; thus it consists of nine geometric points, with precisely three or four \(k\)-rational points. The two classes having the same member-count profile are inequivalent (as shown by complete group-orbit enumeration).

An explicit generator table is given by the following exact coding. In monomial order
\[
M=(X^3,Y^3,Z^3,X^2Y,X^2Z,XY^2,Y^2Z,XZ^2,YZ^2,XYZ),
\]
write \(F_c=\sum_{i=0}^{9}a_iM_i\), where \(a_i\in\{0,1,2\}\) are the least-significant-first base-three digits of the integer \(c\). Each row specifies \(U=\langle F_{c_1},F_{c_2}\rangle_k\); the full four-member codes are listed to facilitate replay.

## Assumptions and scope
All forms and coordinate transformations are defined over \(k=\mathbb F_3\). A projective cubic is counted once for each scalar class, normalized by requiring its first nonzero coefficient in the above fixed monomial order to equal one. A pencil is an *unparameterized* two-dimensional linear subspace, not an ordered pair of equations. Projective equivalence is via \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\), not via isomorphism of elliptic curves or abstract pencils. Here \(\#\mathbb P^2(k)=13\), \(\#\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)=5616\), and there are \((3^{10}-1)/2=29524\) scalar-normalized nonzero cubic equations. Geometric smoothness means nonsingularity after base change to an algebraic closure.

## Proof
**Exact tangent predicate.** List the thirteen projective points and thirteen dual lines by their unique representatives with first nonzero coordinate one. For each normalized coefficient vector \(a\), evaluate \(F_a(P)\) at all thirteen points; if it vanishes and the triple of partial derivatives \(\nabla F_a(P)\) is nonzero, its normalized gradient determines a tangent line. Take the union of the four-point sets of all such tangent lines. The form is tangent-filling precisely if this union has all thirteen points. This uses only arithmetic modulo three. Exhaustively applying it to all 29,524 normalized cubic forms yields exactly 3,744 tangent-filling forms, with respective rational-point frequencies \(936\), \(1872\), and \(936\) for counts \(4\), \(6\), and \(7\).

**Geometric smoothness.** Coordinate permutations, \(X\mapsto-X\), and \(X\mapsto X+Y\) generate \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(k)\). Their induced substitutions on ten coefficient coordinates partition the 3,744 tangent-filling forms into exactly three orbits, represented by codes \(345\), \(346\), and \(3105\) of sizes \(936\), \(936\), and \(1872\). For representatives of the four candidate pencil classes, all thirteen distinct member codes were independently tested: on each of the three charts, the ideal generated by the dehomogenized equation and its three partial derivatives has Gröbner basis containing one over \(k\). This proves absence of singular points even over the algebraic closure. Because every tangent-filling form occurs in one of the four pencil orbits below, these thirteen chart tests combined with equivariance and the three form orbits prove geometric smoothness for every counted tangent-filling form. The separate check of the three form-orbit representatives is also available in the verifier.

**Pencil enumeration.** For two distinct normalized forms \(F\), \(G\), the other two projective members of their span have normalized equations \(F+G\) and \(F-G\). Keep the pair exactly when both of those normalized forms belong to the 3,744-element tangent-filling set. For each accepted pair, form the sorted four-element tuple of member codes. Every projective line over \(k\) has four points and is produced by its six unordered pairs. Exhaustive iteration over all unordered pairs of tangent-filling forms and deduplication by four-tuples produces exactly 22,464 lines, with the member-count distributions and base-point cardinalities in the finding table. By the preceding smoothness proof, these are exactly the desired pencils; no unexamined singular member is included.

**Projective classification.** Apply the aforementioned four explicit linear coordinate substitutions to each cubic in each pencil, normalize every transformed equation, and sort the resulting four codes. An orbit search on the complete 22,464-element set produces exactly four orbits, each of size 5,616, represented by the four rows above. Since \(\#\mathrm{PGL}_3(k)=5616\), every stabilizer is trivial. The four orbits exhaust the set, so the representatives are pairwise inequivalent and complete.

**Maximal dimension.** Any projective net with all thirteen \(k\)-members smooth and tangent-filling contains a qualifying pencil. Hence it suffices to consider the four pencil orbit representatives. For each representative span \(U=\langle F,G\rangle\) and each of the 3,740 tangent-filling forms \(H\notin U\) (modulo scalar), test its nine distinct coset elements \(H+sF+tG\), for \(s,t\in k\), after normalization. In all four cases no candidate has all nine forms tangent-filling. Thus no tangent-filling net exists. A higher-dimensional subspace contains a net, so no such larger space exists. The existing four pencils establish sharpness of dimension one.

**Base scheme.** For each of the four representative pairs \((F,G)\), the two cubics are distinct smooth irreducible curves and share no component. Bézout therefore makes their base a zero-dimensional complete intersection of length nine. To test geometric reducedness, for each of the three affine charts compute the ideal generated by \(F,G\) and the three \(2\times2\) minors of their gradient matrix. The Gröbner basis is the unit ideal in each chart, so the two gradients are linearly independent at every geometric base point. All nine points are simple. Direct projective evaluation gives exactly four, four, three, three rational base points in the respective representatives. Equivariance transports this statement to every qualifying pencil.

## Verification
Run from this directory using Python 3 and SymPy:

```
python3 enumerate_tf_pencils.py
python3 verify_tf_smooth.py
python3 check_nets.py
python3 base_reduced.py
```

The first program exhausts every scalar-normalized cubic and every eligible pair, writes `tf_pencil_orbits.csv`, computes all four projective orbits, member-count and rational base-point histograms. The second performs the exact symbolic smoothness checks, including full orbit coverage. The third exhausts all third-generator candidates over each of the four orbit representatives. The fourth checks transversality of the nine geometric base points using symbolic Gröbner bases. All arithmetic is exact in characteristic three; no random search, numerical tolerance, or extension-field cutoff is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli–Ghioca introduced the tangent-filling condition and established general bounds and examples [1]. Their results do not classify projective pencils of four tangent-filling plane cubics over \(\mathbb F_3\). Asgarli–Ghioca investigated smooth rational members of pencils and gave existence results [2], without the tangent-filling incidence restriction or this small-field projective-orbit census. Asgarli–Ghioca–Yip studied distributions of blocking members in pencils [3], principally under a *rationally empty base-locus* hypothesis; every pencil here has three or four rational base points, and the blocking property of the rational curve point set is not the same as the tangent-filling condition on its rational tangent lines. Separate earlier calculations of individual tangent-filling cubics and binary cubic pencils do not imply the linear compatibility count or the four free orbits. The count and maximality are asserted on the strength of the complete exact computations, not on a failed literature search.

## Limitations
No claim is made that all geometrically parametrized members of a pencil are smooth or tangent-filling; some non-\(k\)-rational members may be singular. Projective equivalence of equations is distinct from abstract isomorphism of genus-one curves. The classification applies only to ternary cubics over \(\mathbb F_3\), not other characteristics or degrees. Historical priority is not independently certified, and the completeness proof depends on executable exhaustive finite arithmetic and exact Gröbner computations, rather than a shorter closed-form orbit formula.
