# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The proof specifies aperiodic binary Sturmian words by irrational rotation, reduces slopes above one-half using complementation, and derives the exact alternation identity for slopes below one-half (where adjacent ones cannot occur). Three distinct discontinuities give four genuine factor classes. Explicit case analysis of the four possible threshold orders proves exactly one reduced Abelian merge, including its parity condition. Rank gaps count ordinary factors and interval lengths give occurrence frequencies by equidistribution. The standalone checker completed 3,000 rotation-cell tests and direct-prefix enumerations for four slopes and 100 lengths each. The infinite statement is proven symbolically; no numerical finite-range extrapolation is used.

## Originality (O): PASS with residual risk

Campbell–Currie–Rampersad, *Reduced complexities for sequences over finite alphabets* (arXiv:2509.16034, sections 1–2), introduces the reduction and computes Thue–Morse and paperfolding cases, but not this Sturmian classification. Carey–Clampitt, *Partitioned Factors in Christoffel and Sturmian Words* (arXiv:1804.08724, sections 2–2.2), gives the rotation correspondence and fixed-partition height profiles. Its equivalence classes are not classes by the number of maximal equal-letter runs, and its theorems do not state the three-threshold fiber counts or Abelian merger. Launer–Puzynina–Voloshinova (arXiv:2607.07620) treats permutation-group equivalences on fixed-length original factors, not collapsing runs. Targeted mathematical-record and literature queries returned no equivalent or stronger result. This cannot exclude an unindexed specialization or a derivation from existing Sturmian theory, so historical priority requires independent review.

## Value (V): PASS

The result gives a natural, full-length and all-slope solution for newly introduced reduced complexity invariants on the canonical minimal-complexity aperiodic shift, not merely a one-off instance. The fiber-size and frequency classification determines how each of the \(n+1\) classical factors coalesces and how the reduced Abelian equivalence collapses exactly one pair. This clarifies the structural distinction among ordinary, reduced, and reduced Abelian complexity on an important infinite family. It does not claim a general result for other words.
