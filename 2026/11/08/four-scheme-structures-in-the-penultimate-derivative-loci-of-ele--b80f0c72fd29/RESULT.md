# Four scheme structures in the penultimate derivative loci of elementary symmetric forms

## Finding

Fix an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic \(p>0\), and integers \(n\ge d\ge4\). Put \(L=n-d+1\), \(r=L+1\), and let \(c\in k\) be the image of \(\binom{r}{2}\). For the elementary symmetric polynomial \(e_d\), let \(J_{n,d}\) be the homogeneous ideal generated by all its squarefree partial derivatives of order \(d-2\). There are exactly four exhaustive cases, as ideals rather than merely as radicals:

1. If \(L\ne0\) in \(k\) and \(c\ne0\), then
   \[J_{n,d}=(x_ix_j:1\le i<j\le n).\]
   Its affine scheme is a reduced union of \(n\) coordinate axes. Its projectivization consists of \(n\) reduced points of degree \(n\).

2. If \(L\ne0\) in \(k\) and \(c=0\), then
   \[J_{n,d}=(x_ix_j-x_ax_b: i<j,\ a<b).\]
   Its affine scheme is the reduced union of the \(n\) coordinate axes and the diagonal line. Its projectivization has \(n+1\) reduced points and degree \(n+1\).

3. If \(L=0\) in \(k\) and \(c\ne0\), necessarily \(p=2\) and \(L\equiv2\pmod4\). With \(t=x_n\), \(y_i=x_i-t\) for \(i<n\), \(m=n-1\), and \(P=y_1y_2\), then
   \[J_{n,d}=(y_i y_j-P:1\le i<j\le m,\ t^2+P).\]
   The projective scheme consists of \(n\) disjoint curvilinear points of length \(2\), supported at the coordinate points; its degree is \(2n\). The affine cone is Cohen–Macaulay, with no embedded associated components.

4. If \(L=0\) in \(k\) and \(c=0\), the same coordinate change gives
   \[J_{n,d}=(y_i y_j-P:1\le i<j\le m).\]
   This is the radical ideal of the union of the \(n\) two-planes on which all but one of the original coordinates are equal. Their projectivizations are \(n\) reduced lines meeting at the diagonal point, and have total degree \(n\).

In particular all cases have explicitly determined defining ideals and have no embedded components. The four cases in characteristic \(2\) cycle with \(L\pmod4\) through \(1,3,2,0\), respectively. In odd characteristic, the second case occurs precisely at \(L\equiv-1\pmod p\), the fourth at \(L\equiv0\pmod p\), and all other values give the first case.

## Assumptions and scope

The field is algebraically closed of positive characteristic. The quantifiers are every integer \(n\ge d\ge4\); thus \(2\le r\le n-2\), a bound used to select disjoint indices in the edge-space argument. The ideal is the order-\(d-2\) squarefree derivative ideal in \(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\), not the Jacobian ideal of the hypersurface \(e_d=0\), nor the ideal of the order-of-vanishing locus. The affine schemes are homogeneous cones; the degrees refer to their projectivizations in \(\mathbb P_k^{n-1}\). The notation \(L=0\) and \(c=0\) in conditions means equality after reduction to \(k\), not equality as integers.

## Proof

**1. Reduce to quadratic incidence vectors.** Since \(e_d\) is multilinear, differentiation once in each variable of a \((d-2)\)-subset \(I\) leaves precisely \(e_2\) in the complement of \(I\). Such complements have cardinality \(r=n-d+2\). Hence the ideal is generated by
\[f_U=\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset U}x_ix_j,\qquad |U|=r.\]
The problem is to compute the linear span of these quadratic forms over \(k\); equality of the spans gives equality of the generated ideals.

**2. Compute that span without characteristic restrictions.** Let \(E\) be the formal \(k\)-vector space on unordered edges \(X_{ij}\), of dimension \(N=\binom n2\). Let \(W\) be the span of \(R_U=\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset U}X_{ij}\) over all \(r\)-subsets \(U\). For four distinct indices \(i,j,a,b\), choose an \((r-2)\)-subset \(C\) disjoint from them; the condition \(r\le n-2\) guarantees its existence. The identity
\[R_{C\cup\{i,a\}}-R_{C\cup\{i,b\}}-R_{C\cup\{j,a\}}+R_{C\cup\{j,b\}}=X_{ia}-X_{ib}-X_{ja}+X_{jb}\]
shows that the four-cycle space \(H\) lies in \(W\).

The quotient \(E/H\) has basis \(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1},b\) under the explicit identifications
\[[X_{in}]=a_i,\qquad [X_{ij}]=a_i+a_j+b\quad(i,j<n).\]
Indeed, all \(X_{ij}-X_{in}-X_{jn}\), for \(i,j<n\), become equal modulo four-cycles, so these \(n\) classes span; the displayed formula respects every four-cycle and has \(n\)-dimensional image, proving independence. Thus \(\dim H=N-n\).

Write \(c=\binom r2\) in \(k\). If \(n\notin U\), the image of \(R_U\) is
\[L\sum_{i\in U}a_i+cb,\]
and if \(n\in U\), it is
\[L\sum_{i\in U\setminus\{n\}}a_i+(c-L)b.\]
When \(L\ne0\), differences obtained by swapping one index span the \((n-2)\)-space generated by \(a_i-a_j\). Modulo those differences, all \(a_i\) become a single class \(a\). The two row types map to \((Lr,c)\) and \((L(r-1),c-L)\) in the coordinates \((a,b)\). Their determinant is
\[L\{r(c-L)-(r-1)c\}=L(c-rL)=-Lc,\]
since the integer identity \(rL=2\binom r2\) holds in every characteristic. Thus the image \(W/H\) has dimension \(n\) when \(Lc\ne0\), and \(n-1\) when \(L\ne0,c=0\). When \(L=0\), all images are multiples of \(cb\); the image dimension is \(1\) if \(c\ne0\) and \(0\) otherwise. Therefore
\[\dim W=\begin{cases}N,&L\ne0,c\ne0;\\N-1,&L\ne0,c=0;\\N-n+1,&L=0,c\ne0;\\N-n,&L=0,c=0.\end{cases}\]

**3. Identify the generators in the first two cases.** The quadratic forms \(f_U\) are linear combinations of the \(N\) squarefree monomials \(x_ix_j\). If \(c\ne0\) and \(L\ne0\), their span has dimension \(N\), so they span all such monomials. If \(c=0\), each \(f_U\) has total edge-coefficient sum \(c=0\), so the span is contained in the codimension-one space generated by all differences \(x_ix_j-x_ax_b\); its dimension \(N-1\) gives equality.

**4. Identify the two remaining cases.** Put \(m=n-1\), \(t=x_n\), and \(y_i=x_i-t\) for \(i<n\), with \(y_n=0\). The quadratic incidence form expands exactly as
\[f_U=e_2(y_i:i\in U)+Lt\sum_{i\in U}y_i+ct^2.\]
When \(L=0\), the middle term vanishes. Let \(P=y_1y_2\), and let \(K_m\) be the linear space of forms spanned by \(y_i y_j-P\) for \(i<j\le m\). It has dimension \(\binom m2-1=N-n\). If \(c=0\), every \(f_U\) belongs to \(K_m\), since its coefficient sum on the \(y_i y_j\) equals \(c=0\); the computed dimension \(N-n\) proves equality. If \(c\ne0\) while \(L=0\), then \(p=2\) and \(L\equiv2\pmod4\), and \(c=1\). The number of \(y_i y_j\) pairs appearing in \(f_U\) is either \(\binom r2\) or \(\binom{r-1}2\); modulo \(2\) both equal \(1\) because their difference is \(L=0\). Thus every \(f_U\) belongs to \(K_m+k\,(t^2+P)\). This space has dimension \(N-n+1\), equal to \(\dim W\), proving equality. (The coordinate change is invertible.)

**5. Prove the radical and component assertions.** For \(m\ge3\), let \(I_m=(y_i y_j-P:i<j\le m)\). In the quotient every mixed quadratic monomial equals \(P\), and every mixed homogeneous monomial of degree \(h\ge2\) equals the same class \(Py_1^{h-2}\): the relations imply \(P y_i=P y_j\) for all indices by multiplying two equal-pair relations using a third distinct index, and induction then gives the claim. The homogeneous degree-\(h\) quotient is therefore spanned by \(y_1^h,\ldots,y_m^h,Py_1^{h-2}\) for \(h\ge2\), by the \(m\) variables in degree one, and by the unit in degree zero. These classes are independent on the union of the \(m\) coordinate axes and the diagonal line: evaluations at the axes force the pure-power coefficients to vanish, then evaluation on the diagonal forces the mixed coefficient to vanish. Hence \(I_m\) equals the radical ideal of that union scheme-theoretically. It is reduced and one-dimensional, so a linear form avoiding its components is a nonzerodivisor; hence its coordinate ring is Cohen–Macaulay.

The first and second cases have the analogous squarefree monomial and equal-pair ideals in the original coordinates, and are reduced unions of the asserted lines. In the fourth case \(J_{n,d}=I_m\) does not involve \(t\): the product with \(\mathbb A_t^1\) is a reduced union of \(m+1=n\) affine planes, described in the original \(x\)-coordinates as all-but-one-equal partition planes. In the third case, \(J_{n,d}=(I_m,t^2+P)\). Its coordinate ring is free of rank two over \(k[y_1,\ldots,y_m]/I_m\), with basis \(1,t\), since the last relation is monic in \(t\). Consequently it is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension one and has no embedded primes. On each of the \(m\) coordinate-line components of the reduced base, \(P=0\) and the local fiber is \(k[t]/(t^2)\); on the diagonal component, \(P=s^2\) and in characteristic two the local relation is \((t+s)^2=0\). All \(m+1=n\) projective components are therefore disjoint curvilinear length-two points. This also shows directly that their affine support is exactly the \(n\) coordinate axes in \(x\)-coordinates. The degrees in the statement follow by summing the projective lengths or degrees of components. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` checks, by modular Gaussian elimination, equality of the complete quadratic spans of the original derivative generators and the four candidate ideals for \(112\) parameter triples, with \(4\le n\le10\) and characteristics \(2,3,5,7\). It separately enumerates \(20\) small finite-field point sets and checks support equality. Its expected completion marker is `CERTIFICATE_OK`. The finite checks are corroborating; the preceding rank argument and radical/flatness calculations establish the result for all parameters, including algebraically closed infinite fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Ying Xie, *Derivative and multiplicity loci of elementary symmetric polynomials in positive characteristic* (arXiv:2610.00259v1, 24 September 2026), Theorem 1.1, computes the dimensions of all derivative loci, Theorem 1.3 identifies the larger-dimensional components, and Theorem 1.4 computes generic local rings at coordinate components. Section 7 explicitly leaves smaller-dimensional and embedded components, primary decompositions, and global reducedness unclassified. The present result computes the entire homogeneous defining ideal in the natural quadratic-derivative order \(d-2\), distinguishing an additional diagonal component and a global doubled-point case, and proves the absence of embedded components in every case. The dimensional and generic-local conclusions are compatible with the cited work, but they do not imply the displayed global quadratic ideals. Ian Orzel, *Computing the Elementary Symmetric Polynomials in Positive Characteristics* (arXiv:2509.05009v3), studies the order-two zero space and algebraic complexity rather than these higher-derivative schemes. No general claim is made about novelty of the known dimension or generic-local results.

## Limitations

This is a complete classification only at derivative order \(d-2\), with \(n\ge d\ge4\), in positive characteristic. The theorem neither settles every derivative order nor determines general higher multiplicity schemes, their equivariant Hilbert series, or all partition-space intersection multiplicities outside this specific family. The absence of a matching formula in searched records is not evidence of historical uniqueness; earlier intersection-theoretic or combinatorial descriptions might be equivalent. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
