# Scientific review: Four-season ordering law for two-habitat population persistence

## Correctness

**PASS.** For positive symmetric phasewise exchange, each exponential has an explicit symmetric two-state hyperbolic form with strictly positive traceless multiplier. The trace-interchange computation produces the exact sign factor \(K(q_b-q_c)(q_a-q_d)\) with \(K>0\). All 24 four-phase orders reduce to the three dihedral classes and both strict inequalities follow when the ratios are distinct. Common determinant and two-state Perron monotonicity transfer the trace comparison to growth exponents. The growth/decay example is proved with exact rational matrix-exponential truncations and an analytic remainder bound, not an unchecked decimal calculation. Remaining scientific boundary: no claims for asymmetric exchange or five phases.

## Originality

**PASS under accessible-evidence comparison, with priority risk.** The initiating 2023/2024 two-habitat dispersal-growth treatment introduces the model, general Floquet criterion and piecewise-constant examples, but its inspected model/results/examples/appendices do not state the all-order four-phase optimality theorem or a fixed-environment order-only sign reversal. The 2020 matrix-rearrangement theorem concerns words in two matrices and operator norm, not four different phase matrices or ecology-based ordering parameters. The 2024 joint-spectral-radius paper concerns binary words, repetitions and forbidden maxima. Cyclic/reversal trace symmetries are known and expressly credited. Literature searches found no covering four-season ratio-based ranking. The principal risk is a general four-factor product comparison unknown or poorly indexed, not a conclusion that an unsuccessful search proves novelty.

## Value

**PASS.** Seasonal scheduling is a natural control question when habitats receive fixed total local conditions and dispersal opportunities. The theorem solves all 24 possible four-season schedules exactly without optimising nonlinear equations or evaluating monodromy numerically, and identifies a sharp three-to-four-phase structural boundary. The certified pair of schedules has identical exposure, duration, local sink averages and exchange rates but opposite signs of the population's long-run growth exponent. This is a substantive ecological consequence of phase ordering, not merely a relabelling or a small numerical improvement.

## Closest literature and residual limitations

The closest primary source is Benaïm–Lobry–Sari–Strickler, arXiv:2311.04706v2, especially Sections 2, 3 and 4 and appendices. Relevant matrix comparators are Alaifari et al., arXiv:1904.05239v2, and Vladimirov, arXiv:2406.17524v1. An undiscovered equivalent general factor-ordering theorem remains possible. The finding is for the linear cooperative growth exponent and does not prove nonlinear demographic persistence.
