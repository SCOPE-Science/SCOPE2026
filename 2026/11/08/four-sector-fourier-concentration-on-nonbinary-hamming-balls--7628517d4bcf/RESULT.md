# Four-sector Fourier concentration on nonbinary Hamming balls

## Finding
Fix integers \(q\ge3\) and \(n\ge3\). Let \(G=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^n\), with uniform probability measure, and let \(\mathcal H_1\) be the first Fourier band: the constant function and all nontrivial characters supported on one coordinate. For \(0\le k\le n\), write \(B_k=\{x\in G:\#\{i:x_i\ne0\}\le k\}\) and \(C_{n,k}=P_1M_{\mathbf1_{B_k}}P_1\big|_{\mathcal H_1}\).

For \(1\le k\le n-1\), put \(p=(q-1)/q\), and define, with \(W_j\sim\operatorname{Bin}(j,p)\),
\[
\beta=\Pr(W_{n-1}\le k-1),\qquad
\alpha=\Pr(W_{n-1}=k),\qquad
r=\Pr(W_{n-2}=k),\quad s=\Pr(W_{n-2}=k-1),
\]
\[
m=\beta+\alpha/q,\quad d=\beta+(q-1)\alpha/q,\quad
e=(q-1)(r-s)/q^2,\quad D=d+(n-1)e,\quad c=\sqrt{q-1}\,\alpha/q.
\]
The **entire spectrum**, with multiplicities (merging equal levels), consists of
\[
\boxed{\lambda_\pm=\tfrac12\left(m+D\pm\sqrt{(m-D)^2+4nc^2}\right)\quad(1\text{ each}),}
\]
\[
\boxed{\beta\quad(n(q-2)\text{ times}),\qquad \mu=\beta+\frac{q-1}{q}s\quad(n-1\text{ times}).}
\]
For every \(1\le k\le n-2\) these levels obey the **strict, universal ordering**
\[
\boxed{0<\lambda_-<\beta<\mu<\lambda_+<1.}
\]
Thus Fourier restriction to every nontrivial ball is injective, and its exact optimal squared lower and upper sampling bounds for \(1\le k\le n-2\) are \(\lambda_-\) and \(\lambda_+\). Its sharp singular-value condition number is \(\sqrt{\lambda_+/\lambda_-}\).

At \(k=n-1\), the multiplicity-merged spectrum is \(\lambda_-<\beta<1\), where \(\mu=\lambda_+=1\); the lower bound remains \(\lambda_->0\). At \(k=0\) the sole nonzero eigenvalue is \([1+n(q-1)]/q^n\) (multiplicity one); at \(k=n\) every eigenvalue is one.

The spectrum also has a **symbol-independent central-limit law**. Fix \(q\ge3\), let \(n\to\infty\), and take integers \(k_n\) for which
\[
\frac{k_n-np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}\longrightarrow z\in\mathbb R.
\]
Writing \(\phi(z)=e^{-z^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\) and \(\Phi(z)=\int_{-\infty}^z\phi(t)\,dt\), we have \(\beta,\mu\to\Phi(z)\) and
\[
\boxed{\lambda_\pm\longrightarrow\Phi(z)-\frac{z\phi(z)}2\pm\phi(z)\sqrt{1+\frac{z^2}{4}}.}
\]
In particular, despite the extra \(n(q-2)\)-dimensional nonbinary transverse sector, the two persistent outliers and the limiting sharp condition number are independent of the fixed alphabet size. At \(z=0\) that limit is
\[
\boxed{\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{2/\pi}}{1-\sqrt{2/\pi}}}.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet size \(q\) is any integer, not necessarily prime. A character on \(G\) is \(\chi_h(x)=\exp(2\pi i\sum_jh_jx_j/q)\). The ambient inner product is \(q^{-n}\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\). The band \(\mathcal H_1\) has complex dimension \(1+n(q-1)\), and the concentration operator acts on that band with the stated normalization. Binomial probabilities outside the range \(0\le j\le m\) are zero. The spectral statements are exact for every finite \(n,q,k\) in the specified ranges; the limit fixes \(q\) and finite \(z\).

## Proof
**Orthogonal decomposition.** Write \(Y_i=\mathbf1_{\{x_i\ne0\}}\) and \(p=(q-1)/q\). For each coordinate, the nonconstant one-coordinate character span splits orthogonally into the radial line generated by \(u_i=(p-Y_i)/\sqrt{p(1-p)}\), and the \((q-2)\)-dimensional space \(V_i\) of functions that vanish at zero and have zero sum on the \(q-1\) nonzero symbols. Consequently
\[
\mathcal H_1=\operatorname{span}\{1,u_1,\ldots,u_n\}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^nV_i.
\]
Conditioned on active coordinates, nonzero symbols remain independent and uniform. Hence \(C_{n,k}\) annihilates all cross terms between transverse and radial spaces and between distinct transverse coordinates. It acts on every \(V_i\) by the scalar \(\beta=\Pr(W_{n-1}\le k-1)\). This accounts for \(n(q-2)\) eigenvalues.

**Radial moments.** Conditioning on coordinate \(i\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\mathbf1_{B_k}=m=\beta+\alpha/q,\quad
\mathbb E(u_i\mathbf1_{B_k})=c=\sqrt{q-1}\,\alpha/q,
\]
\[
\mathbb E(u_i^2\mathbf1_{B_k})=d=\beta+(q-1)\alpha/q.
\]
Condition on two coordinates and use \(W_{n-2}\) for the remainder to obtain, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
\mathbb E(u_iu_j\mathbf1_{B_k})=e=\frac{q-1}{q^2}(r-s).
\]
The radial Gram matrix in basis \(1,u_1,\ldots,u_n\) is therefore
\[
\begin{pmatrix}m&c\mathbf1^T\\c\mathbf1&(d-e)I_n+e\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\end{pmatrix}.
\]
On the \(n-1\)-dimensional radial contrast space it has eigenvalue \(\mu=d-e\). Binomial conditioning yields \(\alpha=(r+(q-1)s)/q\), so \(\mu=\beta+(q-1)s/q\). On \(\operatorname{span}\{1,n^{-1/2}\sum_i u_i\}\), the matrix is exactly \(\begin{pmatrix}m&\sqrt n c\\\sqrt n c&D\end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(\lambda_\pm\).

**Strict ordering.** For the characteristic polynomial \(F(t)=(t-m)(t-D)-nc^2\) of this two-dimensional block, the identities above give
\[
F(\beta)=-\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}{q^2}\alpha s<0,
\qquad F(\mu)=-\frac{n(q-1)}{q^2}rs.
\]
When \(1\le k\le n-2\), all \(\alpha,r,s\) are positive; thus \(\lambda_-<\beta<\mu<\lambda_+\). For \(k=n-1\), \(r=0\) and \(\mu=\lambda_+=1\), whereas \(F(\beta)<0\). To show \(\lambda_->0\), any first-band function vanishing on \(B_1\) must vanish identically: evaluate it first at zero, then at all points supported on exactly one coordinate, varying that symbol over all nonzero choices; each one-coordinate part must be constant on its entire alphabet and have zero mean, so every part and the overall constant vanish. Since \(B_1\subset B_k\), the restriction Gram is positive definite for every \(k\ge1\). To show \(\lambda_+<1\) for \(k\le n-2\), use the complementary set, containing all points of weights \(n\) and \(n-1\). A first-band function vanishing there must, by varying every nonzero coordinate independently, have one-coordinate terms constant on the nonzero symbols; it is therefore affine in the \(Y_i\). Evaluating successively at weight \(n\) and each weight-\(n-1\) point forces all coefficients to zero. Thus the complementary Gram is positive definite and \(C_{n,k}<I\). The endpoint formulas follow directly from restrictions to one point or the complement of the weight-\(n\) shell.

**Gaussian limit.** With fixed \(q\) and \((k_n-np)/\sqrt{np(1-p)}\to z\), binomial central and local limit theorems give \(m,\beta,d\to\Phi(z)\), \(\sqrt n c\to\phi(z)\), and \(ne\to-z\phi(z)\). The last assertion follows also from the exact ratio of adjacent binomial probabilities \(\Pr(W_{n-2}=k)/\Pr(W_{n-2}=k-1)=(n-1-k)p/[k(1-p)]\) and the local limit theorem. Consequently the radial block converges entrywise to
\[
\begin{pmatrix}\Phi(z)&\phi(z)\\\phi(z)&\Phi(z)-z\phi(z)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its two eigenvalues give the boxed limits. Meanwhile \(\beta,\mu\to\Phi(z)\). At \(z=0\), the limiting extreme levels are \(1/2\pm1/\sqrt{2\pi}\), giving the stated condition number.

## Relationship to prior work
Marceca, Romero, and Speckbacher study Fourier concentration spectral bounds for general domains (arXiv:2301.11685, first posted 2023-01-27). Their results give deviation estimates, not the exact finite-alphabet four-sector spectrum or the universal ordered eigenvalues here. Hogan and Lakey (arXiv:1812.08905, 2018) treat binary hypercube concentration and associated eigenspaces; that paper is confined to alphabet size two and does not give the four-sector nonbinary ball law.

The previously published *Exact q-ary Hamming-shell Fourier concentration and the arithmetic tightness criterion* (2026-10-11) gives individual single-shell spectra and the radial/transverse decomposition. The current ball is a sum of shell operators, but their radial rank-one blocks have different eigenvectors; summing their **eigenvalues** does not yield the present exact ball spectrum or strict interlacing. The existing *Exact first-Walsh-band concentration spectra on Boolean Hamming balls* (2026-10-11) establishes the binary (three-sector) specialization and its Gaussian limit, but has no \((q-2)\)-dimensional transverse character spaces. The present claim determines a nonbinary four-sector spectrum, its global strict ordering, and its alphabet-independent central-limit outliers. Conditional binomial identities and central/local limit theorems are standard tools, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns only the first Fourier band, full Hamming balls, and uniform ambient counting measure. It does not classify higher Fourier bands, arbitrary subsets, weighted samples, or moving-alphabet limits. The extreme spectral outliers each have multiplicity one and do not carry positive limiting empirical spectral mass. Numerical tests are not infinite proofs. A more general association-scheme decomposition could imply the result without identical wording, and historical priority remains provisional; the comparison with prior shell decomposition is an especially relevant residual risk.
