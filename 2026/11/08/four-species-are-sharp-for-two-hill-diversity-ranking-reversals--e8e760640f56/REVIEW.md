# Scientific review: sharp four-species diversity-profile crossing threshold

## Correctness — PASS
For three or fewer positive species abundances, the majorization case eliminates any reversals, while the remaining ternary ordering gives an exponential power-sum difference with no more than three zeros counting multiplicity. Equal richness and normalization force zeros at orders zero and one; after removal of the order-one singularity in Hill diversity, at most one possible reversal remains. The proof includes an elementary inductive justification of the generalized Descartes lemma, including multiplicities. For the four-species witness, the exact power-sum differences at orders two, three, four are \(4,-54,496\) before division by powers of 40. A sign-variation bound of four and the forced two zeros give exactly two genuine crossings. Appending common positive species preserves the whole root pattern.

The packaged checker independently confirms the integer certificates, the seven combined exponential terms, four sign changes, and exact extension identities. Its 8,778 ternary integer-profile comparisons are consistency checks, not an infinite proof. Quantifiers and boundaries are stated: every positive finite probability vector of the same richness, every real diversity order above zero, and the special Shannon limit.

## Originality — PASS within inspected literature, with residual priority risk
Hill (1973) established the effective-species family. Tóthmérész (1995) established and illustrated ecological diversity-profile noncomparability, including one intersection in a five-versus-six-species example, but the inspected full text does not state the minimal richness for two crossings, nor a four-species pair crossing between orders two and four. Chao and Jost (2015) study diversity profiles and estimation rather than this sharp population-level crossing threshold. The already-established fact that profiles can intersect does not imply the sharp four-species two-crossing theorem, and general majorization monotonicity cannot rule out this incomparability case. Prior mathematical literature on power-sum inequalities and Rényi-entropy spectra remains a historical-priority risk despite searches for aliases and equivalent formulations.

A separate comparison with available prior scientific claims involving species accumulation curves, rarefaction, and sample richness variance found distinct objects and variables: sample-size-dependent quantities do not prove diversity-order-dependent profile intersection counts. The claimed contribution is not the exponential-polynomial zero-count lemma itself, nor the existence of some diversity-profile crossing.

## Value — PASS
Order-dependent diversity comparisons determine whether two ecological communities can be consistently ranked when rare and common species receive different emphasis. The existence of a single intersection was already a recognized reason to report complete profiles. This result establishes a sharper and practically interpretable boundary: three species cannot produce two reversals, but four can produce a rank pattern that returns to its original direction between the standard Simpson order two and orders three and four. The exact finite witness works without estimation assumptions, and the construction extends to any larger richness without introducing new crossings. This is a structural threshold, not a routine evaluation of an arbitrary diversity vector.
