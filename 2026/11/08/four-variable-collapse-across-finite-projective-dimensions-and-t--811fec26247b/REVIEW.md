# Review of the projective-dimension classification

**Correctness.** The extension-profile argument lists all realizable responses to zero, one, two, or three stationary points, including all equality cases. The only two geometric availability conditions are the existence of an off-line point and the positive \((q-1)^2\) complement of the three sides of a noncollinear triangle. Both hold for finite planes, including non-Desarguesian planes, and for higher projective spaces. The four-pebble invariant is perpetual. The five-variable witness uses two genuine disjoint lines in dimension at least three and is false in every projective plane. A prime-field checker independently corroborates the local profiles.

**Originality.** Earlier work develops modal logics of finite projective geometries and general finite-variable pebble games. The available full papers inspected address distinct languages or general tools rather than the cross-dimensional classification in ternary point collinearity. Existing projective-plane-only four-variable classifications do not logically imply the new comparison with higher-dimensional spaces. Stebletsova's 1998 finite-dimensional modal report is a specific source of unresolved historical-priority risk because its full paper was not accessible; its cited main subject is the equational/modal theory of Lyndon algebras.

**Value.** The theorem completely classifies a canonical family at a precise logical width, including all projective dimensions and all finite fields. It establishes a sharp expressive-power threshold: four variables cannot see the difference between a projective plane and any higher-dimensional projective space with the same line-size category, whereas five can. This is a dimension-uniform structural boundary, not a finite-table recomputation.

**Limitations.** Counting-free logic and pure ternary point collinearity only; no complete five-variable classification or counting-logic conclusion. Historical priority and mathematical validity have not been independently audited.
