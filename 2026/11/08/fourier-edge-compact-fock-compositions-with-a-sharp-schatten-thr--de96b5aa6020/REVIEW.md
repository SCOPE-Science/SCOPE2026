# Scientific review: Fourier-edge Fock weighted composition operators

## Correctness (PASS)
The entire symbol is an absolutely convergent integral of entire functions. Its normalized kernel profile is \(|g_a(x+iy)|e^{-\beta y^2}\), which is in \(C_0(\mathbb C)\) by Gaussian decay in \(y\) and uniform Riemann--Lebesgue decay in \(x\). A change of variables and compact restriction then prove operator compactness independently of operator-ideal assumptions. The precise Fourier endpoint \(\Gamma(1-a)e^{\pm i\pi(1-a)/2}|x|^{a-1}\), together with a Gaussian-dominated uniform bound, gives the Schatten phase boundary using the published Gaussian Fock Schatten criterion [2]. The Hilbert--Schmidt formula is independently established by the orthonormal basis, Plancherel and Tonelli. The same Fourier asymptotic supplies the exact horizontal-window constants. Critical risks assessed: the \(\lambda\ne0\) change of variables, normalization \(1/\pi\), the power \(2\beta\) in the Gaussian, and the non-equivalence of horizontal truncation with singular-value truncation.

## Originality (PASS, best-knowledge)
The motivating 2026 paper [1] explicitly asks in Question 7.1 whether every compact weighted composition operator on its weighted Fock spaces must be Hilbert--Schmidt. The accessible main theorem, examples and question were inspected. The new entire weight supplies a negative answer already at the Gaussian member of the family and adds a full Schatten phase boundary and sharp critical horizontal-window asymptotics. Du's 2011 paper [2] already characterizes general Schatten membership via an integral, but supplies neither this construction nor its phase constants; that prior theorem is used openly. Related Fock-type operator literature [3] gives general integral criteria, not this exact explicit family. Targeted searches of published records and accessible earlier local claims returned no equivalent or dominating Fourier-edge construction; absence of a hit is not a proof of publication priority.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
This is a motivated counterexample to an explicitly posed compact-versus-Hilbert--Schmidt question, not a parameter substitution into a known example. The extra structure identifies exactly which Schatten ideals contain each compact operator, and distinguishes logarithmic from power divergence at the boundary. The Fourier concentration mechanism explains why pointwise compactness can coexist with a non-integrable kernel profile; it provides reusable evidence for other radial spaces but makes no theorem about them.

## Closest literature and limitations
[1] Barik--Sen--Paul, arXiv:2607.17170, has the compactness/HS criteria and Question 7.1; the explicit construction and quantified boundary are not stated in the inspected material. [2] Du (2011), Theorem 2.3, is the acknowledged stronger *general criterion* that reduces the Schatten step to an integral; its possession of that criterion does not alone supply the needed weight, compactness or sharp constants. [3] Mengestie--Takele (2023) studies generalized Fock-type weights. Residual priority risk: an unlocated older Fourier-weight example or an equivalent formulation of the same negative answer. Independent publication-priority verification was not performed. No assertion is made about all radial exponents, singular-value asymptotics, or independent mathematical certification.
