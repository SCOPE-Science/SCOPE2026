# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof supplies a complete character-sum calculation, an exact cyclotomic zero criterion for prime moduli, Gaussian-binomial mixed factorial moments, finite q-binomial inversion, and domination for weak convergence. The packaged standalone program verifies Fourier residue sums against eigenspace dimensions, and finite moment/inversion identities, with exact arithmetic at five small-field points. Crucial boundaries (prime field, arbitrary dimension at least three, fixed-prime limit, no quantitative rate) are stated. The chosen plus-shift convention is consistent throughout; switching to minus-shifts complex-conjugates the multipliers and leaves the complete multiset and singular values unchanged. Finite tests support but do not replace the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to bounded historical comparison.** Finite-field matrix cycle indices in Fulman's arXiv:math/0003195 and the familiar kernel-nullity laws are credited; no novelty is attached to these components in isolation. The earlier two-dimensional rank-one-idempotent case does not imply the asserted exact rank formula and empirical singular law for all dimensions. The closest indexed Fourier work examines low-coordinate restrictions to rank-one matrix varieties rather than the whole additive-convolution operator on the matrix algebra. No covering full-dimensional rank-one-idempotent Fourier-convolution formula was found. Nevertheless a stronger unlocated matrix character-sum theorem, or an implicit assembly of known cycle-index results, is a material remaining historical risk, so independent literature validation is warranted.

## Value
**PASS.** The operator is a natural conjugacy-invariant convolution on a large finite abelian group. Its macroscopic kernel is invisible to the cardinality of its support and is exactly controlled by a joint rational-eigenspace statistic. The result gives the full normalized singular-value distribution, a computable finite-dimensional nullity expression, and a nonzero limiting zero-spectral mass; these answer the natural spectral-rank and invertibility questions rather than a cosmetic parameter exercise.

## Limits and closest antecedents
The finite-matrix cycle-index survey of Fulman provides powerful related distributional tools; the two-by-two idempotent-convolution result is a genuine lower-dimensional case. The new proof does not establish results for extension fields or uniform asymptotics as field size varies. The zero-atom limit is not alone a novelty claim about random-matrix eigenvalue-free events.
