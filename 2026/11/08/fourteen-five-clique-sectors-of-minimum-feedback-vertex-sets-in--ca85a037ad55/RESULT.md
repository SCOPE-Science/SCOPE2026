# Fourteen five-clique sectors of minimum feedback vertex sets in the Heawood graph

## Finding
Let \(H\) be the 14-vertex Heawood graph, the point–line incidence graph of the seven-point Fano plane. The minimum feedback vertex sets of \(H\) have size \(4\), and there are exactly \(70\) of them. Each of their complements is an induced tree on ten vertices. For each of the seven Fano lines \(L\), define a five-vertex block \(B_L\) consisting of the four points outside \(L\) and the line-vertex \(L\); dually, for each point \(p\), define \(B_p\) consisting of \(p\) and the four line-vertices not incident with \(p\). The optimal feedback vertex sets are **exactly** the four-subsets of these fourteen blocks, with no repetitions. Their one-token-jump exchange graph is \(14K_5\) (140 edges). Their adjacent-token-slide graph is \(70K_1\) (no edges).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs here are finite, undirected and simple. A feedback vertex set \(S\subseteq V(H)\) is a vertex subset for which the induced graph \(H-S\) is a forest. It is *minimum* if it has smallest possible cardinality. The token-jump graph has the minimum sets as its vertices and an edge exactly when two such sets differ by removing one vertex and adding another. The token-slide graph has only those jump edges where the removed and added vertices are adjacent in \(H\). The Fano plane has seven points and seven lines; each line contains three points, each point lies on three lines, and any two distinct points lie on a unique line (dually for lines). A fully explicit incidence model is \(P=\{0,\ldots,6\}\) with \(L_i=\{i,i+1,i+3\}\pmod 7\). These are incidence indices, not graph edges within one color class.

## Proof
**1. Optimal size and independence.** The Heawood graph is cubic with 14 vertices and 21 edges. Deleting \(t\) vertices leaves at least \(21-3t\) edges on \(14-t\) vertices, whereas a forest on that many vertices has at most \(13-t\) edges. Therefore \(21-3t\le13-t\), so \(t\ge4\). At \(t=4\), a surviving forest has ten vertices and exactly nine edges: the number of surviving edges is \(21-12+e(H[S])=9+e(H[S])\). Thus \(S\) must be independent, and if it is feedback its complement must be a connected tree.

**2. All four-set types.** Classify an independent four-set according to how many point-vertices it contains. If it consists of four points, its complementary three Fano points must be collinear: otherwise their three distinct joining lines form a surviving six-cycle. Conversely, if the complementary points lie on a line \(L\), only three point-vertices remain, all on \(L\), and no surviving incidence cycle exists (each such cycle would require at least three noncollinear points). Hence these sets are exactly the seven \(P\setminus L\).

If the selected set has three points and one line \(L\), independence forces those three points to lie in the four-point complement \(P\setminus L\). Conversely, deleting \(L\) and any three of those four points leaves the three points on \(L\), one additional point \(q\), and the six other lines. An incidence cycle must use at least three point-vertices. A six-cycle would use three points, two of which lie on \(L\), so the necessary joining line \(L\) is missing. An eight-cycle would use all four remaining points, including three on \(L\); two of those three must occur consecutively around the four-point cyclic order, again requiring the absent line \(L\). A longer incidence cycle would need at least five distinct points, impossible. This proves all \(7\binom43=28\) such sets are feedback sets.

A candidate with two points and two lines, if independent, must choose two lines \(L,M\) and their two common nonincident points. The lines \(L,M\) meet at one point; let \(a_1,a_2\) be their other points on \(L\) and \(b_1,b_2\) their other points on \(M\). The four cross-lines joining \(a_i\) to \(b_j\), for \(i,j\in\{1,2\}\), are distinct from \(L,M\) and from one another. The remaining incidence graph therefore contains the eight-cycle \(a_1-a_1b_1-b_1-b_1a_2-a_2-a_2b_2-b_2-b_2a_1-a_1\), where \(xy\) denotes the Fano line through \(x,y\). This type is impossible. The cases with zero or one point follow by point–line duality. Therefore every minimum feedback vertex set is one of the four-subsets of the fourteen blocks specified in the finding, and all those subsets are valid. Each block contributes five distinct sets: distinct blocks intersect in at most two vertices (two distinct lines meet in one point and cover five points; the cross-type intersection is at most one point plus one line). The total is \(14\cdot5=70\).

**3. Exchange and sliding.** Any two different four-subsets of one five-element block share exactly three vertices; they are joined by a token jump, yielding a complete graph \(K_5\). Since different blocks intersect in at most two vertices, no jump edge can cross between blocks. This gives exactly \(14K_5\), with \(14\binom52=140\) edges. Each block is independent in the underlying incidence graph: its four point-vertices are pairwise nonadjacent, and its sole line-vertex is disjoint from them, with the dual argument for the other seven blocks. Consequently, every permitted jump exchanges nonadjacent vertices of \(H\); not a single jump is a token slide, giving \(70K_1\).

## Verification
Two standalone, dependency-free Python 3 scripts independently enumerate all four-sets in nonidentical models. `verify_fano.py` uses point-line incidence masks and union–find forest testing, reconstructs the fourteen prescribed five-blocks, checks their independence and pairwise intersections, and reconstructs both move graphs. `verify_lcf.py` uses the independent \([5,-5]^7\) LCF cubic-graph realization with DFS cycle detection and pairwise set-intersection exchanges, also checking the absence of feedback sets of size below four. Extract both programs and run `python3 verify_fano.py` and `python3 verify_lcf.py`. Their recorded outputs are supplied as `verify_fano.out` and `verify_lcf.out`. Both report 70 minimum sets, fourteen five-state jump components, 140 jump edges and zero slide edges. The infinite mathematical inference is not based on extrapolation: the proof above is a complete argument for this fixed graph.

## Relationship to prior work
The Fano-plane representation and symmetry of the Heawood graph are standard and documented by J. Baez (2015). Bousquet, Hommelsheim, Kobayashi, Mühlenthaler and Suzuki (2023) study feedback vertex set reconfiguration on graph classes. Their cited theorem guarantees connected fixed-cardinality exchanges for subcubic graphs without a \(K_{3,3}\) minor and their nonminimum-set result ensures connectedness when an extra vertex is available. This theorem instead gives a complete optimal-set decomposition for a specific cubic, nonplanar, vertex-transitive graph, along with a sharp separation between jumping and sliding. The published results inspected do not state this full Fano-block census or the exact \(14K_5\) graph; the claim does not include the known definitions, the Heawood graph's construction, or any general complexity theorem.

## Limitations
The statement is for the Heawood graph only, not arbitrary cubic graphs or incidence graphs of larger projective planes. No claim is made that these exact figures were never computed privately or in unindexed references. The complete 2023 publisher article was not available for page-by-page comparison, although its accessible introductory results, theorems and section descriptions were inspected; this leaves a historical-priority risk. Independent scholarly assessment has not been performed.
