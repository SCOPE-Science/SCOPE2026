# Fourth-order Gauss ramification on smooth cubic surfaces in characteristic two

## Finding

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic \(2\) and let \(X=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^3_k\) be a smooth cubic surface whose cubic equation has a nonzero coefficient tensor on squarefree monomials. Write \(a_{ijk}\) for the coefficient of \(x_ix_jx_k\) with pairwise distinct indices, and set
\[
p=[a_{123}:a_{023}:a_{013}:a_{012}]\in\mathbb P^3.
\]
Assume \(p\notin X\). There is a unique hyperplane \(H\) with equation \(L=0\) such that the directional polar \(\partial_pF=L^2\), up to nonzero scaling. Then \(R=X\cap H\) is a smooth **ordinary elliptic plane cubic**. For the Gauss morphism \(\gamma:X\to(\mathbb P^3)^\vee\), the differential has rank exactly \(0\) at every point of \(R\) and rank exactly \(2\) at every point outside \(R\). Moreover, if \(I_2(d\gamma)\) denotes the local ideal generated by the \(2\times2\) minors of any local matrix representing \(d\gamma:T_X\to\gamma^*T_{(\mathbb P^3)^\vee}\), then
\[
\boxed{I_2(d\gamma)=\mathcal I_R^4}\qquad\text{as ideal sheaves on }X.
\]
In particular, the scheme of differential rank at most one is **exactly the fourth Cartier thickening** \(4R\), not merely the reduced critical plane cubic. Its divisor class is \(4\mathcal O_X(1)\) and its intersection degree against a hyperplane is \(12\).

## Assumptions and scope

All statements are over an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\). The particular cubic embedding in \(\mathbb P^3\) is fixed. We require a nonzero squarefree-cubic coefficient tensor and require its canonical point \(p\) not to lie on the smooth surface. The theorem concerns the actual sheaf of maximal minors of the differential to the ambient *dual projective space*. It does not identify a discriminant or singular scheme of the dual surface, nor does it assert that the Gauss morphism is finite. The condition \(p\notin X\) is essential to the smoothness of \(R\) and to the fourth-power scheme identity as stated.

## Proof

**1. Canonical point and normal form.** In characteristic \(2\), every cubic is the sum of monomials divisible by a square and the four squarefree monomials. Its Hessian \(B_x=\left(\partial_i\partial_jF(x)\right)\) is alternating. The vector \(p\) displayed above is in the kernel of \(B_x\) for every \(x\), and Euler's identity for the gradient gives \(B_xx=0\). The contraction of the nonzero squarefree three-form with \(x\) has rank \(2\) whenever \(x\ne p\), so \(\ker B_x=\langle x,p\rangle\). The polar \(\partial_pF\) has zero Hessian, hence is a square \(L^2\) of a linear form, since the field is perfect. Since \(p\notin X\), Euler's identity gives \(L(p)^2=\partial_pF(p)=F(p)\ne0\); thus \(L(p)\ne0\). Choose coordinates with \(p=[0:0:0:1]\) and \(L=x_3\) (rescaling the equation if required). There are no terms \(x_3x_i^2\), and the cubic has the **exact** form
\[
F=C(x_0,x_1,x_2)+x_3^2M(x_0,x_1,x_2)+x_3^3,
\]
where \(C\) is ternary cubic and \(M\) is linear. The coefficient of \(x_0x_1x_2\) in \(C\) is nonzero. This decomposition follows coefficientwise from \(\partial_3F=x_3^2\).

**2. Smooth ordinary critical curve.** On \(H=V(x_3)\), the restricted equation is \(C=0\). If this plane curve had a singular point \(q\), the first three derivatives of \(F\) would vanish at \(q\); so would \(\partial_3F(q)=0\), contradicting smoothness of \(X\). Therefore \(R\) is smooth. Its Hasse invariant is the squarefree coefficient of \(C^{2-1}=C\), which is nonzero by the preceding paragraph (equivalently, the smooth plane cubic meets the standard characteristic-two Frobenius-splitting criterion). Hence \(R\) is ordinary and has genus one.

**3. Exact pointwise differential ranks.** At a smooth point \(x\in X\), let \(g=\nabla F(x)\). The affine tangent space is \(T=\ker(g: k^4\to k)\), containing the radial vector \(x\); the differential of \(\gamma=[g]\) is induced by \(B_x:T\to(k^4)^*/kg\). Since \(p\notin X\), \(x\ne p\), so \(B_x\) has rank \(2\) and kernel \(\langle x,p\rangle\). If \(x\notin R\), then \(g(p)=\partial_pF(x)=x_3^2\ne0\), so \(p\notin T\) and \(\ker(B_x|_T)=kx\). Also \(\operatorname{im}B_x\) annihilates \(p\), whereas \(g(p)\ne0\), so passage to the quotient by \(kg\) preserves rank \(2\). If \(x\in R\), then \(g\) annihilates \(x\) (Euler) and \(p\), and hence lies in \(\operatorname{im}B_x=(\langle x,p\rangle)^\perp\). Choose \(w\) with \(B_xw=g\). Alternation gives \(g(w)=B_x(w,w)=0\), so \(w\in T\). The restriction \(B_x(T)\) is then exactly \(kg\), and \(d_x\gamma\) has rank \(0\). No rank-one points occur.

**4. Scheme-theoretic order four.** Work near any point of \(R\). Choose an affine chart on \(\mathbb P^2=H\), with coordinates \((y,t)\), such that \(C_y\) is invertible on the chosen neighborhood; also use \(u=x_3\) as the transverse coordinate. The local equation is \(f=c(y,t)+u^2m(y,t)+u^3=0\). The implicit-function rule on the smooth surface gives
\[
\partial_uy=u^2/f_y,\qquad
\partial_ty=f_t/f_y.
\]
Let \(G=(G_0,G_1,G_2,G_3)\) denote a local homogeneous lift of the gradient. Then \(G_3=u^2\), and for the other coordinates \(G_i=C_i+u^2M_i\). The identities above, together with the vanishing differential of the plane-cubic Gauss map in characteristic \(2\), show that **every local coordinate entry** of \(d\gamma\) is divisible by \(u^2\): indeed the \(u\)-derivative is \(u^2/f_y\) times a Hessian column, while the \(t\)-derivative is projectively zero at \(u=0\) and differs from its value there only by terms divisible by \(u^2\). Therefore \(I_2(d\gamma)\subseteq(u^4)\).

To prove equality, it suffices to show that \(u^{-2}d\gamma\) has rank \(2\) on \(R\). Let \(v\) be the nonradial tangent vector to \(R\) corresponding to \(\partial_t\); let \(B^C\) denote the ternary Hessian of \(C\). Since \(B^C\) has rank \(2\), has radial kernel, and is alternating, we have \(B^Cv=\lambda g_C\) with \(\lambda\ne0\), where \(g_C\) is the ternary gradient of \(C\). In a target chart with \(G_j\) invertible, the \(t\)-derivative of the ratio \(G_3/G_j=u^2/G_j\), divided by \(u^2\) and restricted to \(R\), is \(\lambda/G_j\ne0\), whereas the corresponding \(u\)-derivative divided by \(u^2\) is zero because \(\partial_uG_j\) is already divisible by \(u^2\). On the other hand, the \(u\)-column of \(u^{-2}d\gamma\), along \(R\), equals the class of \(B^Ce_y/c_y\) modulo the line \(kg_C\), and is nonzero: otherwise \(B^C\) would have rank at most one because \(B^C\) sends the entire tangent plane to \(kg_C\). These two divided differential columns are independent. Thus a \(2\times2\) minor is a unit times \(u^4\), proving \(I_2(d\gamma)=(u^4)\) on every such chart, and hence globally \(I_2(d\gamma)=\mathcal I_R^4\).

## Verification

A standalone script `verify.py` computes all projective points of the explicit smooth example
\[
F=x_0x_1x_2+x_1^2x_2+x_0^3+x_2^3+x_3^3
\]
over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_4\), and \(\mathbb F_8\). It computes gradients, Hessians, tangent bases and Gauss differential ranks by exact finite-field operations, and verifies that the rank is \(0\) precisely on \(x_3=0\) and otherwise \(2\). A symbolic local-chart check verifies that a differential \(2\times2\) minor is exactly \(u^4/t^3\) on the chart \(x_0=1\), \(t=x_2\ne0\), confirming the order four in this example. Run `python verify.py` from the directory containing the embedded script. Expected output is in `CHECKS.txt`. The universal fourth-power theorem is proved algebraically above, not inferred from finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Fukasawa, Furukawa and Kaji (2010) study Gauss-rank-zero embeddings; Furukawa (2012) treats cubic hypersurfaces and the dependence of the generic rank on embeddings. Dolgachev and Duncan (first posted December 2017) explicitly identify the canonical point and the square polar defining the canonical plane for cubic surfaces in characteristic two, and classify their normal forms. Those canonical-point and plane constructions are established context here. The distinct assertion is that, **when the canonical point is off the surface**, the scheme defined by all maximal minors of the ambient Gauss differential is the *fourth power* of the canonical-plane ideal, and that the exact pointwise ranks jump directly from two to zero. Targeted source and mathematical-record comparisons did not locate this scheme-theoretic order-four result. A relevant 2010 full text was not available for direct inspection; specialist priority checking remains appropriate.

## Limitations

This is a statement about a smooth cubic surface and its fixed cubic embedding in characteristic two. It assumes the canonical point exists and is off the surface. It makes no theorem about canonical points lying on the surface, all arbitrary projective embeddings of the same abstract surface, the scheme of the dual variety, or the exact degree of the Gauss morphism. Full historical novelty is provisional pending further archival inspection and independent review.
