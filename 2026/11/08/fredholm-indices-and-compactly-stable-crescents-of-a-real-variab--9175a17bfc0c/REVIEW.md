# Scientific review: Fredholm indices of the real-variable Cesàro operator

## Correctness — PASS
The norm-preserving logistic change of variables sends the bounded integral operator to the inverse of \(d/du+b_p\) on \(W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)\), with exact tail constants \(1/q\) and \(1/p\). The factorization of \(C_p-\lambda I\) has its domain and operator order explicitly checked. Compactness of the decaying coefficient difference reduces Fredholmness to a piecewise-constant first-order equation, where the number of decaying modes and the matching condition at the origin give exact kernel/cokernel dimensions. Escaping Weyl packets establish the non-Fredholm boundary lines, and nonclosed \(W^{1,p}\) range handles zero. The calculations identify two tangent circles, all indices, and exact residual/eigenvector descriptions. The checker passed 118 sample diagnostics, none invoked as an infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Belli, Gül, Ross and Siskakis (arXiv:2606.08600, Theorem 1.3 and Sections 2–8) give the norm, ordinary spectrum, point spectrum, and semigroup representation; they do not state a Fredholm spectrum, any index computation, the full closed-range/cokernel dichotomy, or compactly persistent crescents. Their prior \(p=2\) work describes the Hilbert multiplier model and already yields its circle case; this case is not new. General first-order Fredholm/dichotomy theory (Latushkin and Tomilov, arXiv:math-ph/0308002) is a significant broader-method priority risk. It requires a typed identification of the concrete integral operator with a differential inverse and computation of both limiting rates and actual defect spaces; the explicit full-parameter claim was not found in the inspected literature or indexed public records. Accessible prior local findings about the same integral operator concern eigenvector sampling or commutator singular values, not this Fredholm classification. The possibility of an unindexed direct corollary remains open.

## Value — PASS
The 2026 source's crescent spectrum does not indicate which interior points remain spectral under compact perturbations; the Fredholm index answers exactly that question for every \(1<p<\infty\). In particular, the entire crescent is topologically protected against arbitrary compact perturbations, with opposite index signs across \(p=2\), and the \(p>2\) interior is a closed codimension-one residual spectrum. These are natural full-spectrum structural invariants rather than arbitrary finite parameters or routine accuracy gains. The limiting \(p=2\) case is credited to the earlier normal-operator result; the classification is not extended to general Cesàro kernels.

## Closest sources and limitations
The primary open-access 2026 paper was inspected through its theorem, spectrum, semigroup, invariant-subspace and concluding sections. The original \(L^2\) paper provides the prior normal operator case. Classical dichotomy theory offers a general proof strategy and potential overlap; its full monograph-level scope has not been exhaustively compared. The analogous Cesàro–Hardy operator has a different kernel, as the primary authors note. Quantitative essential resolvent bounds and noncompact perturbations are outside the theorem.
