# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The object is the reduced union of all fifteen geometric matching planes in the Segre cubic, descended under an arbitrary permutation of the six coordinates over every finite field of characteristic at least five. The geometric multiplicity classification follows from pairing zero coordinates and equal-size opposite nonzero value classes; its only positive values are \(1,2,3,6\). For an intersection of any subset of matching planes, the linear equations are those of a six-vertex graph, with one parameter per bipartite connected component, giving the exact projective dimension. Twisted Frobenius invariance forces saturation by the permutation action, and finite-field descent counts the resulting projective linear space. Binomial inversion on all \(2^{15}-1\) nonempty subsets proves the eleven exact polynomial rows, checked by the embedded full symbolic enumerator; its applicability is independent of the finite-field size. The second checker independently enumerates five nontrivial classes over \(\mathbb F_{25}\) and \(\mathbb F_{125}\). The degree-five obstruction also has a separate orbit-length proof and yields the specified zeta function by extension counts.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Avilov, *On forms of the Segre cubic* (arXiv:1812.11363, inspected Section 2), gives the geometrical plane and node configuration and Galois forms but not the exact arithmetic point-incidence spectra of the plane union. Cheltsov–Tschinkel–Zhang, *Equivariant geometry of the Segre cubic and the Burkhardt quartic* (arXiv:2308.15271v1, inspected Sections 2.4 and 6), analyzes automorphisms and equivariant geometry over characteristic zero rather than the eleven positive-dimensional boundary spectra over finite fields. Accessible earlier research claims give complete whole-threefold point counts for the eleven twist types, and separately the rational Frobenius orbits of the ten nodes, fifteen planes, and sixty node–plane flags. The former count does not separate boundary incidences; the latter is a finite étale configuration and cannot determine positive-dimensional strata. The present theorem explicitly claims neither the classical configuration nor either earlier result as new. Targeted full-statement mathematical searches found no exact eleven-row table or the fifth-degree boundary emptiness. The non-detection is not proof of uniqueness: a classical or unpublished equivalent point-incidence calculation may exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The plane union is a canonical boundary of a well-studied singular cubic threefold, and its stratification by the number of geometric components passing through a rational point is invariant under coordinate relabeling and finite-field descent. Classifying all eleven twists, rather than an unmotivated finite sample, gives complete extension counts and the finite-étale orbit obstruction for the type with a five-cycle. It demonstrates an arithmetic distinction between the rationality of the ambient threefold and rational points on its geometrically large boundary. This is more than a numerical recomputation of the previously known node–plane flag counts.

## Limitations and closest comparisons
The field characteristic must be at least five for the stated geometric model; the proof uses odd characteristic. Not every possible moduli presentation of a cubic form is treated. The displayed tables are counts of points with exactly a specified number of *geometric* planes, not counts of rational planes, and no claim is made about the complementary open threefold. Earlier whole-variety point-count and zero-dimensional incidence results are acknowledged and not claimed as novel. An equivalent older formula for the full boundary remains a possible priority risk; this same-model analysis is not independent verification.
