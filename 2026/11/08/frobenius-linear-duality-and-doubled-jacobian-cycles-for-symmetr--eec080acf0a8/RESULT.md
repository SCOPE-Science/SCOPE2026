# Frobenius-linear duality and doubled Jacobian cycles for symmetric determinant hypersurfaces

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), \(n\ge4\), and \(V\) an \(n\)-dimensional \(k\)-vector space. Let \(\mathbb P=\mathbb P(\operatorname{Bil}_{\mathrm{sym}}(V))\), of dimension \(N=n(n+1)/2-1\), parameterize symmetric bilinear forms, and let \(X_n=V(\det M)\subset\mathbb P\) be the integral determinant hypersurface of the universal symmetric matrix \(M=(m_{ij})\). Write \(R_n=\{[M]:\operatorname{rank}M\le n-2\}\) with its reduced structure, \(U_n=\{[M]:\operatorname{rank}M=n-2\}\), and \(Z_n\subset X_n\) for the projective hypersurface Jacobian singular scheme, defined by the determinant and all its partial derivatives.

**Claim.** (1) The projective dual \(X_n^*\subset\mathbb P^*\) is the **linear** coordinate subspace \(\mathbb P^{n-1}\) on the diagonal-dual coordinates. The Gauss map on \(X_n^{\mathrm{sm}}=\{\operatorname{rank}M=n-1\}\) is the composition of the smooth kernel map \(\kappa:[M]\mapsto[\ker M]\) with the relative Frobenius map of degree \(2^{n-1}\): \([v_1:\cdots:v_n]\mapsto[v_1^2:\cdots:v_n^2]\). In contrast, in characteristic different from \(2\) the dual is the quadratic Veronese \(v_2(\mathbb P^{n-1})\).

(2) The support of \(Z_n\) is \(R_n\). On \(U_n\), an étale/local algebraic coordinate system makes its ideal exactly \((u,w,v^2)\), whereas \(R_n\) has ideal \((u,v,w)\). Thus \(Z_n|_{U_n}\) is a square-zero double structure on \(U_n\), with invertible nilradical \(\mathcal N\cong\det(\mathcal K)\otimes\mathcal O_{U_n}(-1)\), where \(\mathcal K\) is the rank-two kernel bundle of the universal symmetric bilinear map. The fundamental cycle is \([Z_n]=2[R_n]\) in the codimension-three Chow group; this asserts **no** global purity or description of possible lower-dimensional associated structure on \(R_n\setminus U_n\).

(3) For \(n=4\), \(\deg R_4=10\) and hence \(\deg Z_4=20\). The dual jumps from a degree-eight quadratic Veronese threefold in characteristic different from \(2\) to a degree-one linear three-space in characteristic \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All projective duals and scheme lengths are taken over an algebraically closed base field of the indicated characteristic. A symmetric **bilinear** form in characteristic \(2\) is represented by a symmetric matrix; it is not identified with a quadratic-form coefficient vector. The full Jacobian ideal is \(J=(\det M,\partial\det M/\partial m_{ij}:i\le j)\). The assertion about \(\mathcal N\) concerns only the smooth rank-exactly-\(n-2\) stratum; no formula for the entire nonreduced scheme along smaller ranks is claimed.

## Proof
**The Gauss image.** Jacobi's cofactor identity gives \(\partial(\det M)/\partial m_{ii}=\operatorname{cof}_{ii}(M)\) and \(\partial(\det M)/\partial m_{ij}=2\operatorname{cof}_{ij}(M)=0\) for \(i<j\). On the rank-\(n-1\) locus, the adjugate has the form \(\lambda vv^\mathsf T\) for a nonzero kernel vector \(v\) and \(\lambda\ne0\). Hence the Gauss point is \([v_1^2:\ldots:v_n^2]\), with every off-diagonal dual coordinate zero. Every line \(kv\subset V\) is the kernel of some symmetric bilinear form of rank \(n-1\): choose a nondegenerate form on \(V/kv\). Thus the Gauss image is the entire linear \(\mathbb P^{n-1}\). The kernel morphism is smooth: above \([v]\) it is the open set of nondegenerate forms in the projective vector bundle of symmetric bilinear forms on \(V/kv\). The remaining factor is relative Frobenius, purely inseparable of degree \(2^{n-1}\). If the characteristic is different from \(2\), the off-diagonal derivatives are instead \(2\lambda v_iv_j\), giving the full quadratic Veronese.

**Set-theoretic singularity.** The adjugate vanishes at rank at most \(n-2\). At rank \(n-1\), some kernel coordinate is nonzero, hence some diagonal cofactor \(\lambda v_i^2\) is nonzero. The hypersurface is smooth there and the support of its Jacobian scheme is exactly \(R_n\). The irreducibility of the determinant locus and the rank loci follows from the incidence families of bilinear forms with a specified kernel; the rank-exact strata are nonempty dense opens in their irreducible closures.

**Local scheme and its gluing.** At any rank-\(n-2\) point choose coordinates so that an \((n-2)\)-square principal block \(A\) is invertible. The Schur complement \(S=C-B^\mathsf TA^{-1}B\) is a symmetric \(2\times2\) block \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&v\\v&w\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), and changing from \((A,B,C)\) to \((A,B,S)\) is an algebraic coordinate isomorphism on \(\det A\ne0\). There \(\det M=(\det A)(uw+v^2)\). Since \(\det A\) is a unit, its full Tjurina ideal is precisely \((u,w,v^2)\). The rank-\(n-2\) ideal is \((u,v,w)\), so its nilpotent conormal ideal inside \(Z_n|_{U_n}\) is locally generated by \(v\), with \(v^2=0\). When a kernel frame changes by \(g\in\mathrm{GL}_2\), the alternating matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&v\\v&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) is carried modulo the diagonal directions to \(\det(g)v\) times the same matrix. Accounting for the projective tautological scaling, the nilpotent ideal transforms as \(\det(\mathcal K)\otimes\mathcal O(-1)\). This identifies the line sheaf globally on \(U_n\).

As \(R_n\) is irreducible and its rank-\(n-2\) stratum is dense, the localization of the Jacobian scheme at its unique minimal prime has length \(2\). Therefore \([Z_n]=2[R_n]\) as a fundamental cycle, regardless of any lower-dimensional embedded behavior.

**The degree-ten rank locus for \(n=4\).** Resolve \(R_4\) by the incidence projective bundle over \(G=\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)\): a point consists of a two-dimensional kernel \(K\subset V\) and a nonzero symmetric bilinear form on the quotient \(Q=V/K\). The projection is birational because a generic rank-two form has a unique kernel. The bundle of symmetric bilinear forms on \(Q\) has rank \(3\) and formal Chern roots \(-2x,-x-y,-2y\), where \(x,y\) are roots of \(Q\). If \(h_4\) is the fourth complete symmetric polynomial, the projective-bundle and Grassmannian integration formulas give
\[
\deg R_4=\int_G h_4(2x,x+y,2y)=[x^3y^2](x-y)h_4(2x,x+y,2y)=10.
\]
The displayed coefficient is verified independently in the standalone program. Multiplying by generic Jacobian length \(2\) gives \(\deg Z_4=20\).

## Relationship to prior work
Ilic–Landsberg (1996) treat constant-rank symmetric degeneracy loci and duals in characteristic zero, not the Jacobian thickening in characteristic \(2\). Grenet–Monteil–Thomassé (2012) give the characteristic-two involution expansion for symmetric determinants, which explains the vanishing off-diagonal derivatives but does not state the full higher-size dual/Gauss/Jacobian-cycle package. Cunha–Ramos–Simis (2018) analyze gradient ideals and duals of symmetric determinantal degenerations under an explicit characteristic-zero hypothesis. An earlier computation for the \(3\times3\) characteristic-two determinant determines its entire doubled Veronese singular scheme; that smaller case is not claimed as new and does not by itself supply the \(n\ge4\) statements. The rank-two symmetric-matrix degree is classical; the novelty claim concerns its linkage to the characteristic-two Jacobian cycle and the simultaneous Gauss collapse, not the degree \(10\) on its own.

## Limitations
Historical priority remains provisional. Other early literature on symmetric degeneracy loci or modular duality could contain an equivalent general result. The theorem does not assert the absence of lower-dimensional embedded components of \(Z_n\), a global ribbon across deeper rank strata, or a scheme-theoretic description of its dual image beyond the reduced linear dual. The Jacobian line bundle is asserted only on the smooth rank-\(n-2\) stratum. The finite-field enumerations and symbolic checks do not replace the general cofactor, Schur-complement and intersection-theoretic arguments.
