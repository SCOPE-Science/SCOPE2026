# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The original polynomial becomes \(XU^3-ZV^3\) by the characteristic-three binomial identity. Its Jacobian ideal is exactly \((U^3,V^3)\), yielding the complete-intersection invariants. The explicit \(\mathbb P(\mathcal O(-3)\oplus\mathcal O(-1))\) map is finite, birational and normal, so it is the normalization. On both singular-line charts, the local inclusion \(k[t^3,u,tu]\subset k[t,u]\) has conductor \((u,tu)^2\), establishing the global square conductor. The line-classification argument treats both possibilities for restricted \(U,V\), and the finite-field zeta follows from the bijection on rational points. All relevant quantifiers and model choices are stated in RESULT.md. The exact verifier corroborates low-extension counts and line classification.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

Rams–Schütt arXiv:1212.3511 identifies the classical equation and a *different* good-reduction model in characteristic three. Polo-Blanco–van der Put–Top arXiv:0904.2374, §3.1.1, contains a triple-line ruled-quartic normal form whose special monomial case gives the **same formal equation**; neither the equation nor its basic ruled appearance is claimed as novel. Their classification assumes characteristic zero or greater than three and does not establish its wild characteristic-three ninefold Jacobian thickening, purely inseparable normalization, square conductor, or finite-field zeta. The line classification here is supporting structural context rather than an independent novelty assertion. Targeted literature and mathematical-record searches did not return an equivalent statement. This is not proof of absolute priority; specialist literature on inseparable ruled quartics could overlap.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The classical smooth Schur quartic has 64 lines, whereas a natural primitive integral equation undergoes a distinctive bad reduction with infinitely many lines and a purely inseparable singular directrix. Determining the normalization, conductor and full line set gives a structural explanation of that jump and distinguishes this model from the known smooth good-reduction one. The exact zeta function connects this nonnormal degeneration to the arithmetic of a Hirzebruch surface, beyond a routine point-count experiment.
