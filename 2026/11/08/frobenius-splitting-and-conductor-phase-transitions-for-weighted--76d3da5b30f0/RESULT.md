# Frobenius-splitting and conductor phase transitions for weighted rational cones

## Finding

Fix integers \(d\ge2\) and \(e\ge1\), and an algebraically closed field \(k\). Let
\[
R_{d,e}=k[s^d,s^{d-1}t,\ldots,st^{d-1},\ s^{e-1}t^{de-e+1},s^{e-2}t^{de-e+2},\ldots,t^{de}]
\]
be the weighted homogeneous coordinate ring of the rational curve of type \((d,e)\). Write \(B_d=k[s^d,s^{d-1}t,\ldots,t^d]\) for its normalization, with an element \(s^jt^{da-j}\) assigned degree \(a\).

The following hold for all such \(d,e\):

1. A lattice monomial \(s^jt^{da-j}\) of \(B_d\) belongs to \(R_{d,e}\) if and only if \(j\ge a\bmod e\), where the remainder is taken in \(\{0,\ldots,e-1\}\).
2. The normalization conductor \((R_{d,e}:B_d)\subset B_d\) has exactly the monomial basis \(\{s^jt^{da-j}:a\ge0,\ 0\le j\le da,\ j\ge e-1\}\). For \(e\ge2\), its radical is the height-one prime generated by the positive-\(s\)-exponent monomials; its contraction cuts out the entire reduced nonnormal locus, an affine line. Restriction of the normalization to this line is the degree-\(e\) finite map \(\mathbb A^1\to\mathbb A^1\), \(u\mapsto u^e\).
3. The seminormalization is the monomial subring of \(B_d\) containing all lattice monomials with \(j\ge1\) and all boundary monomials with \(j=0\) and \(e\mid a\). Thus \(R_{d,e}\) is seminormal exactly for \(e=1\) or \(e=2\).
4. If \(\operatorname{char}k=p>0\), then \(R_{d,e}\) is Frobenius split (equivalently \(F\)-pure) **if and only if** \(e=1\), or \(e=2\) and \(p\ne2\). In particular the nonnormal members with \(e=2\) are Frobenius split in every odd characteristic but not in characteristic two; none of the members with \(e\ge3\) are Frobenius split in any positive characteristic.

## Assumptions and scope

The field is algebraically closed, with arbitrary characteristic for (1)--(3) and positive characteristic for (4). The parameter \(e\) is the *weight*, not a field extension degree. The affine cone \(\operatorname{Spec}R_{d,e}\), not the coarse projective curve, is the singularity object. The coordinate ring has dimension two, and the normalization is the \(d\)-th Veronese ring in two variables. The ring in the theorem is the specific weighted log-complete-series coordinate ring, not an arbitrary weighted determinantal scroll.

## Proof

**The semigroup and normalization.** Index a monomial of total \((s,t)\)-degree \(da\) by its pair \((a,j)\), with \(j\) the exponent of \(s\). The degree-one generators have \(j=1,\ldots,d\), while the degree-\(e\) generators have \(j=0,\ldots,e-1\). At degree \(a\), using exactly \(h\) weight-\(e\) generators, \(0\le h\le\lfloor a/e\rfloor\), the possible \(j\) form the full integer interval
\[
[a-eh,\ d(a-eh)+(e-1)h].
\]
The intervals for successive \(h\) overlap or are adjacent: for \(h\ge1\), the upper endpoint for \(h\) minus the lower endpoint for \(h-1\), plus one, equals \((d-1)(a-eh)+(e-1)(h-1)\ge0\). Their union is therefore precisely \([a\bmod e,da]\cap\mathbb Z\). This proves (1). Every degree-\(a\) lattice monomial lies in \(B_d\), and \(t^d\) is both integral over \(R_{d,e}\) (its \(e\)-th power is \(t^{de}\)) and belongs to the fraction field: for \(e\ge2\),
\[
t^d=\frac{s^{e-1}t^{de-(e-1)}}{(st^{d-1})^{e-1}}.
\]
For \(e=1\) it is already a generator. As \(B_d=R_{d,e}[t^d]\) and the Veronese ring is normal, it is exactly the normalization.

**The conductor and geometry.** A monomial \(m=s^jt^{da-j}\in B_d\) belongs to \((R_{d,e}:B_d)\) precisely when every product with a monomial of \(B_d\) lies in \(R_{d,e}\). For arbitrary added degree \(b\ge0\), the smallest possible added \(s\)-exponent is zero, so the condition is \(j\ge(a+b)\bmod e\) for all \(b\). The maximum of these residues is \(e-1\), proving the conductor formula. For \(e\ge2\), the radical in \(B_d\) comprises exactly monomials of positive \(s\)-exponent: taking a sufficiently high power reaches the conductor, whereas a boundary monomial never does. Its quotient in \(B_d\) is \(k[t^d]\). The contracted radical in \(R_{d,e}\) has quotient \(k[t^{de}]\): only boundary exponents divisible by \(e\) occur. Off the conductor, finite normalization is an isomorphism; at its generic point the local ring is not normal because the extension of residue function fields \(k(t^d)/k(t^{de})\) has degree \(e>1\). Hence the nonnormal support is exactly this affine line, with the claimed map \(u\mapsto u^e\).

**Seminormalization.** The cone lattice group is \(\mathbb Z^2\) (the two weight-one monomials with exponents \(j=1,2\) generate the needed differences). For a lattice exponent \((a,j)\) in the cone, all sufficiently large positive multiples are in the original semigroup exactly when \(j\ge1\) or \(j=0\) with \(e\mid a\). This is the standard eventual-multiple characterization of the seminormalization of an affine monoid. Directly, the resulting monoid is seminormal: if its doubles and triples contain a boundary exponent \((a,0)\), then \(e\mid2a\) and \(e\mid3a\), hence \(e\mid a\); all positive-interior exponents are already present. The original semigroup contains every such exponent precisely when \(e\le2\). When \(e\ge3\), \((e-1,e-2)\) is a missing interior exponent whose double and triple lie in the semigroup; this also directly proves failure of seminormality.

**Frobenius.** Let \(p\) be the characteristic. For a monomial semigroup ring over perfect \(k\), if the monoid is \(p\)-root closed in its group (that is, \(pu\in S\implies u\in S\)), projection of \(R_{d,e}^{1/p}\) onto monomials with integral lattice exponents is a well-defined \(R_{d,e}\)-linear Frobenius splitting. Conversely, Frobenius purity forces principal ideals to be Frobenius closed: if \(u\) is a lattice exponent with \(pu\in S\) but \(u\notin S\), then \(z\in\operatorname{Frac}R_{d,e}\setminus R_{d,e}\) with \(z^p\in R_{d,e}\), contradicting purity after clearing a denominator. For \(e=1\), the monoid is normal. For \(e=2\), its only missing lattice points have \(j=0\) and odd \(a\); they remain missing after multiplication by odd \(p\), but \((1,0)\) is a missing \(2\)-root of \((2,0)\). For \(e\ge3\), the missing exponent \(u=(e-1,e-2)\) satisfies \(pu\in S\) for every prime \(p\ge2\), since \(p(e-2)\ge e-1\). These observations prove the exact classification.

## Verification

The accompanying `check_weighted_curve.py` is a standalone Python 3 checker using only the standard library. Running `python3 check_weighted_curve.py` independently generates the semigroup from the original weighted generators and compares membership with the proposed interval formula for all \(1\le d\le7\), \(1\le e\le8\), and degrees at most \(22\). It also checks conductor membership over all residue classes and Frobenius-root witnesses or root closure for primes \(2,3,5,7,11\). The output is `WEIGHTED_CURVE_SEMIGROUP_MEMBERSHIP_OK 56` and `CONDUCTOR_AND_FROBENIUS_ROOT_TESTS_OK 280`. These checks are finite diagnostics; the unrestricted assertions are proved above.

## Relationship to prior work

Banks--Ramkumar (2026) study varieties of minimal degree in divisible weighted projective spaces, including weighted determinantal rational curves, and distinguish such geometry from standard rational normal curves. Davis--Sobieska (first public preprint 2024) give the explicit weighted rational curves of type \((d,e)\), their determinantal ideals, Hilbert series, Cohen--Macaulayness, and generalized Koszul behavior, and note their failure of normality for \(e\ge2\). The present theorem concerns the new explicit seminormalization/conductor and characteristic-dependent splitting classification of their affine coordinate rings; it does not claim either motivating paper's results as new. Bruns--Li--Römer, Nguyen, and De Stefani--Montaño--Núñez-Betancourt provide general semigroup criteria, which are compatible with the proof, but the inspected sources do not state this weighted-family parameter classification or the conductor-cover geometry.

## Limitations

The result is specific to the monomial coordinate rings displayed, over algebraically closed fields. The method does not classify all weighted determinantal scrolls, nor does Frobenius splitting imply normality. Earlier general monoid criteria can simplify the argument and leave a possible historical-priority risk for an unlocated explicit example. The finite checker is not a proof of the infinite parameter claim. The characteristic-zero statement concerns seminormality/conductor, not Frobenius purity.
