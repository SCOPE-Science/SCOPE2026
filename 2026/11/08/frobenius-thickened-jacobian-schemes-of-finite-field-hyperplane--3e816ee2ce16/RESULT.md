# Frobenius-thickened Jacobian schemes of finite-field hyperplane arrangements

## Finding
For every prime power \(q=p^a\), every integer \(N\ge4\), and \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_q\), let \(D\subset\mathbb P^{N-1}_k\) be the reduced divisor consisting of *every* hyperplane defined over \(\mathbb F_q\). Its full Jacobian singular scheme is the \(q\)-th Frobenius thickening of its reduced codimension-two incidence locus. More precisely, with the definitions below,
\[
J(F)=I^{[q]}=\bigcap_{L}\mathfrak p_L^{[q]},\qquad \sqrt{J(F)}=I=\bigcap_L\mathfrak p_L.
\]
Here \(L\) ranges over the \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational codimension-two linear subspaces of projective space. Both \(S/I\) and \(S/J(F)\) are Cohen--Macaulay; there are no embedded components. Every component has a generic transverse infinitesimal rectangle \(k(L)[[u,v]]/(u^q,v^q)\), of length \(q^2\) and socle degree \(2q-2\).

Put \(d=1+q+\cdots+q^{N-2}\). The **minimal graded free resolution** of the Jacobian quotient is
\[
0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=0}^{N-2}S(-q(d+q^j))\longrightarrow S(-qd)^N\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow S/J(F)\longrightarrow0.
\]
In particular,
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}_{S/J(F)}(t)=\frac{1-Nt^{qd}+\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}t^{q(d+q^j)}}{(1-t)^N},\quad
\deg\operatorname{Proj}(S/J(F))=q^2{N\brack2}_q,
\]
where \({N\brack2}_q=\frac{(q^N-1)(q^{N-1}-1)}{(q^2-1)(q-1)}\). The resolution and complete primary decomposition apply simultaneously to all such dimensions and field sizes, including non-prime \(q\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work over the algebraic closure \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_q\) in characteristic \(p\), with \(q=p^a\) and \(N\ge4\). Set \(S=k[x_1,\ldots,x_N]\). Let
\[
F=\det\big(x_j^{q^i}\big)_{0\le i<N,\,1\le j\le N}
\]
be the Moore determinant, which differs by a unit from the product of all nonproportional \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear forms in the variables. Let \(B=(x_j^{q^i})_{0\le i<N-1,\,1\le j\le N}\) and let \(D_j=(-1)^{j+1}\det B_{\widehat j}\) be its signed maximal minors, with \(I=(D_1,\ldots,D_N)\). The notation \(I^{[q]}\) means the *Frobenius power* generated by the \(q\)-th powers of elements of \(I\), not the ordinary ideal power. The Jacobian ideal is generated by all first partial derivatives of \(F\); Euler's relation places \(F\) itself inside that ideal since \(\deg F\equiv1\pmod p\).

## Proof
**1. Exact polar identity.** Differentiating the determinant along its row of first powers, while every higher Frobenius power has zero ordinary derivative, gives
\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_j}=(-1)^{j+1}\det\big(x_h^{q^i}\big)_{1\le i<N,\,h\ne j}=D_j^q.
\]
The last equality uses the Frobenius homomorphism (and the compatible sign convention). Consequently \(J(F)=I^{[q]}\) as *homogeneous ideals*, without saturation or a set-theoretic qualification.

**2. Reduced support of the minor ideal.** The Moore rank criterion says that the matrix of \(N-1\) successive Frobenius powers of the coordinates of a geometric point has rank \(\min(N-1,r)\), where \(r\) is the \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear dimension spanned by those coordinates. Therefore the maximal minors vanish exactly when \(r\le N-2\). This is the union of all rational codimension-two subspaces. It has pure codimension two. The Hilbert--Burch theorem applied to the \((N-1)\times N\) matrix \(B\) gives the exact sequence
\[
0\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N-2}S(-(d+q^i))\xrightarrow{B^\mathsf T}S(-d)^N\to I\to0,
\]
with signed minors as generators. Its quotient is Cohen--Macaulay. At a generic point of a rational codimension-two flat, change coordinates over \(\mathbb F_q\) to make that flat \(x_{N-1}=x_N=0\). The other \(N-2\) coordinates are \(\mathbb F_q\)-linearly independent in its function field, so the relevant Moore minor of these coordinates is a unit. Expanding the two minors obtained by deleting the last two columns shows their linear terms are independent units times \(x_{N-1}\) and \(x_N\). Thus \(I\) is generically reduced. The Cohen--Macaulay property gives \(S_1\), while generic reducedness gives \(R_0\); by Serre's criterion \(I\) is radical and equals \(\bigcap_L\mathfrak p_L\).

**3. Frobenius thickening and local multiplicities.** The polynomial ring is regular, so its Frobenius map is flat. Applying the exact Frobenius functor to the primary intersection in step 2 yields
\[
I^{[q]}=\bigcap_L\mathfrak p_L^{[q]}.
\]
For any \(L\), its prime \(\mathfrak p_L\) is generated by two independent \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear forms \(\ell_1,\ell_2\), hence \(\mathfrak p_L^{[q]}=(\ell_1^q,\ell_2^q)\) is primary to \(\mathfrak p_L\). These are all associated components. At the generic point of \(L\), the completed transverse quotient is exactly \(k(L)[[u,v]]/(u^q,v^q)\). Its standard monomial basis \(u^iv^j\), \(0\le i,j<q\), proves length \(q^2\).

**4. Minimal graded resolution and degree.** Frobenius flatness transports the Hilbert--Burch complex in step 2 to the displayed free resolution, multiplying every shift by \(q\). All matrix entries lie in the homogeneous maximal ideal, so it is minimal. Taking its alternating graded ranks gives the Hilbert series. There are \({N\brack2}_q\) distinct rational codimension-two flats, each of projective degree one and generic multiplicity \(q^2\); additivity of the fundamental cycle gives the stated degree.

## Verification
The included standalone \(`verify_moore.py`\) uses exact dictionary-based polynomial arithmetic without external packages. It checks the product factorization and every derivative/minor identity for \((q,N)=(2,3),(3,3),(2,4)\), independently checks the closed-form degree against Gaussian-binomial counting, and exhaustively checks all \(585\) points of \(\mathbb P^3(\mathbb F_8)\): coordinate ranks \(1,2,3\) occur \(15,210,360\) times, and the gradient vanishes exactly for ranks \(1,2\). It also verifies independent transverse minor differentials at a rank-two point. The output is embedded as \(`verification_output.txt`\). These finite checks are corroboration, not a proof for arbitrary \(N,q\).

## Relationship to prior work
The Moore determinant factorization and Hilbert--Burch theorem are classical. Wakefield--Yoshinaga (2007, arXiv:0707.2672) analyze Jacobian schemes of complex hyperplane arrangements and their reconstruction. Pink--Schieder (2010, arXiv:1007.4796) study the complement of all finite-field hyperplanes and its compactifications. Aluffi (2011, arXiv:1103.2777) relates arrangement singular schemes to Segre classes in characteristic zero. Migliore--Nagel--Schenck (2019, arXiv:1908.03939) treat Cohen--Macaulay properties of related singular-locus schemes in characteristic zero. The structural part asserted here is the characteristic-\(p\) equality of the complete Jacobian ideal with the Frobenius power of its reduced incidence ideal, together with its explicit global primary decomposition, graded resolution, and local lengths for \(N\ge4\). General freeness criteria alone do not assert this particular ideal equality. No claim of a new general determinant theorem or a new general Hilbert--Burch theorem is made.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the complete finite-field hyperplane arrangement over its algebraic closure; it does not classify partial/deleted arrangements or higher Jacobian ideals. The statement is scheme-theoretic but does not compute all Segre classes or the full embedded deformation theory at intersections of several codimension-two components. Historical priority is provisional: implicit derivability from classical freeness and determinant theory is a meaningful risk, and specialist finite-field arrangement sources may contain an equivalent formulation. No independent scientific audit has been carried out.
