# Full \(C_2\times S_4\) homology character of the Franklin matching complex

## Finding
Let \(F\) be the Franklin graph with the explicit LCF presentation below, and let \(\mathcal M(F)\) be its ordinary matching complex. The previously known nonequivariant homotopy type is \(\mathcal M(F)\simeq\bigvee^{12}S^3\). The new result is the **full rational representation on third homology** under all \(48\) graph automorphisms.

Let \(z\) be the central half-turn \(i\mapsto i+6\pmod{12}\). Let \(H\) be the subgroup generated by the conjugacy class of the involution whose action on vertices \(0,\ldots,11\) is the tuple
`(1,0,5,4,3,2,7,6,11,10,9,8)`.
Then \(H\cong S_4\), \(\operatorname{Aut}(F)=H\times\langle z\rangle\), and with \(U\) the three-dimensional standard rational representation of \(S_4\), \(W\) its two-dimensional irreducible, \(\operatorname{sgn}\) its sign, and \(\varepsilon(z)=-1\),
\[
\widetilde H_3(\mathcal M(F);\mathbb Q)
\cong
\bigl(U\oplus(U\otimes\operatorname{sgn})\oplus W\bigr)\boxtimes\mathbf1
\;\oplus\;
\bigl(\operatorname{sgn}\oplus(U\otimes\operatorname{sgn})\bigr)\boxtimes\varepsilon.
\]
The central involution has trace \(4\) with \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspace dimensions \(8\) and \(4\), respectively. No nonzero third-homology class is invariant under the full automorphism group.

In the deterministic class order of the included verifier, conjugacy-class sizes are
\[
(1,3,3,6,6,8,8,6,6,1),
\]
and the corresponding third-homology character values are
\[
(12,0,0,-2,0,0,-2,2,0,4).
\]
The vertex-permutation representative of each class is printed by `verify_franklin_character.py` and retained in `character_output.txt`; the order is fixed by lexicographically sorting all graph automorphisms and repeatedly extracting the class of the smallest unclassified element.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices of \(F\) are \(\{0,\ldots,11\}\), with indices modulo \(12\). Include all cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and, for even \(i\), the chord \(\{i,i+5\}\), or for odd \(i\), the chord \(\{i,i-5\}\). Identify duplicated undirected edges. There are \(18\) distinct edges, each vertex has degree three. A matching is any set of edges with pairwise disjoint endpoints, including the empty matching. A nonempty matching of size \(k+1\) is a simplicial \(k\)-face.

All homology is reduced homology. The representation is over \(\mathbb Q\); the result does not classify integral \(\mathbb Z[\operatorname{Aut}(F)]\)-modules or equivariant homotopy splittings. The prior nonequivariant wedge type is not claimed as new.

## Proof
**Integral topological premise.** Exhaustively enumerate the \(1091\) nonempty matchings. Their face vector, indexed by dimensions \(0\) through \(5\), is \((18,117,336,417,186,17)\). With the \(18\) graph edges sorted lexicographically, apply the deterministic matching rule: for pivot edge \(e\) from first to last, pair each still-unmatched face not containing \(e\) with its still-unmatched admissible coface obtained by adding \(e\). The algorithm produces \(539\) pairs. It leaves exactly one critical vertex and twelve critical three-cells. Direct every paired Hasse incidence upward and each other incidence downward. Kahn's algorithm traverses all \(1091\) vertices of this directed Hasse diagram, using all \(3942\) nonempty-face cover incidences; hence the matching is acyclic. Forman's discrete-Morse theorem gives a CW complex with one zero-cell and twelve three-cells, so \(\mathcal M(F)\simeq\bigvee^{12}S^3\) over the integers. This independently verifies the nonequivariant premise; it is not the asserted novelty.

**Automorphism enumeration.** An exhaustive injection-preserving backtracking algorithm assigns images to all twelve graph vertices, checking both adjacency and nonadjacency against every previously assigned vertex. Exactly \(48\) automorphisms are found and closure under composition is checked. Conjugation partitions the group into ten classes of the sizes stated above. The half-turn \(z\) commutes with every automorphism. The six conjugates of the displayed involution generate a subgroup \(H\) of size \(24\) disjoint from \(zH\). Conjugating the four distinct subgroups of order three in \(H\) yields a faithful action on four objects, identifying \(H\) with \(S_4\). Thus \(\operatorname{Aut}(F)\cong S_4\times C_2\) with an **explicit** splitting.

**Character computation.** For each automorphism \(g\), let \(g_E\) be its permutation of the eighteen graph edges. The reduced simplicial Lefschetz number is
\[
\widetilde L(g)= -1 + \sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in\mathcal M(F)\\g_E\sigma=\sigma}} (-1)^{|\sigma|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g_E|_\sigma).
\]
Homology is concentrated in dimension three, so \(\widetilde L(g)=-\chi(g)\), where \(\chi(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g|\widetilde H_3(\mathcal M(F);\mathbb Q))\). Consequently
\[
\chi(g)=1+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in\mathcal M(F)\\g_E\sigma=\sigma}}(-1)^{|\sigma|}\operatorname{sgn}(g_E|_\sigma).
\]
There are two independent finite evaluations. The first directly examines all \(1091\) faces, retaining stable ones and calculating their permutation signs by inversion parity. The second partitions the eighteen edges into cycles of \(g_E\), discards any edge orbit containing incident edges, and sums \((-1)^{\#\text{selected orbits}}\) over mutually endpoint-disjoint unions of the remaining orbits. They agree exactly for each of the \(48\) automorphisms and yield the ten values in the finding.

**Irreducible decomposition.** Restriction to \(H\cong S_4\) and its coset \(zH\) gives five \(S_4\) conjugacy types: identity, transpositions, double transpositions, three-cycles and four-cycles. The pairs \((\chi(h),\chi(zh))\), in that order, are
\[
(12,4),\;(-2,2),\;(0,0),\;(0,-2),\;(0,0).
\]
The characters on the central \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspaces are half the sum and difference, respectively. Comparison with the ordinary \(S_4\) character table gives \(U\oplus(U\otimes\operatorname{sgn})\oplus W\) and \(\operatorname{sgn}\oplus(U\otimes\operatorname{sgn})\). This proves the displayed decomposition; it has no trivial representation of the full product. Orthogonality checks give \(|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)^2=5\), exactly five distinct irreducibles, in agreement with the result.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_franklin_character.py` and `python3 verify_franklin_boundary.py` from a directory containing the two included scripts, with ordinary Python 3 and no additional packages. Recorded stdout appears in `character_output.txt` and `boundary_output.txt`. The first verifies the integral acyclic Morse matching, full graph automorphism group and splitting, both independent character computations, ten conjugacy classes, and irreducible identities. It ends with `FRANKLIN_FULL_AUT_MATCHING_CHARACTER_PASS`.

The independently implemented second script enumerates all subsets of the eighteen graph edges, reconstructs the simplicial chain complex and computes reduced boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\). The ranks by chain degree are \((1,17,100,236,169,17)\), giving reduced Betti dimensions \((0,0,0,12,0,0)\), consistent with the integral Morse proof. The second script ends with `FRANKLIN_MATCHING_MOD2_BOUNDARY_PASS`. Finite-field agreement is a cross-check, not the proof of integral topology.

## Relationship to prior work
Shareshian and Wachs (arXiv:math/0409054) treat general matching and chessboard complexes, focusing on complete and complete bipartite graphs and their integral homology. Engström (arXiv:math/0512420) develops independence-complex reductions for claw-free graphs, a framework applicable via line graphs. Neither inspected argument computes this graph-specific full representation.

The closest public exact Franklin result, *Edge-orbit matching-complex splitting for the Franklin graph* (published-finding corpus record `2026/10/11/SCOPE-edge-orbit-matching-complex-splitting-for-the-franklin-graph--be4888fadcca`), already establishes the twelve-sphere wedge and the two edge-orbit deletion/link homotopy types. Inspection of its full `RESULT.md` finds no automorphism trace table, central eigenspace decomposition or \(S_4\times C_2\)-module formula. The present result **does not reassert** that nonequivariant finding as original; its new claim is exactly the full rational symmetry representation. Standard equivariant matching enumeration can express traces as alternating permutation-character sums, but that general identity does not itself supply the explicit ten-class computation or the decomposition for this graph. Searches under the line-graph independence-complex, crossed-prism, and Franklin matching-complex names found no published stronger instance-specific coverage. An unindexed or inaccessible equivalent calculation remains possible.

## Limitations
This is an exact representation theorem for one natural finite graph, not a uniform family classification. It does not construct canonical homology generators, determine integral representation lattices, or prove an equivariant wedge-of-spheres splitting. The finite proof code is executable but is not an independently certified formal proof. A targeted literature comparison does not establish global publication priority.
