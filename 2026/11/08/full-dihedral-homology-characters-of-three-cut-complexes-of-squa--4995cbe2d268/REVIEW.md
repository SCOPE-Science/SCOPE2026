# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite relative-chain model has exactly the complement families of sizes zero through four, with connected triples counted as \(4n\) and four-sets uniquely the \(n\) consecutive blocks. An explicit degree argument excludes larger sets. The long-exact sequence of the full simplex relative to the ordinary cut complex identifies the only relative homology and its dihedral action. The oriented Hopf trace produces the complete reflection formulas, and the averaging idempotent produces the invariant dimensions. The previously known sphere-wedge theorem supplies the required homological concentration. A standalone Python verifier corroborates the signed group traces at every \(9\le n\le22\) and independently constructs the ordinary cut complexes and boundary ranks at \(9\le n\le12\). These finite runs are not substituted for the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence

The nearest full primary-source match is the published *Rational cyclic homology representations of three-cut complexes of cycle powers*: it covers the same parameter family but only the cyclic subgroup; its explicit limitations exclude reflection characters. The original squared-cycle shellability paper and later graph-power extension give nonequivariant sphere counts and homotopy types, not the full dihedral action. Related dihedral results for unpowered cycles and other graph complexes do not imply the trace at reflections of a squared cycle. The new contribution is exactly the full reflection-character formula and the resulting dihedrally invariant homology; the already known rotations and ranks are credited. Potential equivalent unindexed literature remains a residual risk, not a proved absence.

## Value — PASS

The squared cycle is a canonical highly symmetric graph, and the ordinary three-cut complex is an established topological construction. Rational dihedral representations refine known homology dimensions and cyclic modules: distinct axis types have different traces, while explicit fixed dimensions distinguish genuine invariant homology from mere cycle-rotation invariance. The theorem covers all \(n\ge9\) with a uniform, explainable combinatorial mechanism, rather than a few isolated calculations.

## Scope and limitations

Only rational homology representations are classified. No integral-lattice module, equivariant homotopy splitting, or parameter below nine is asserted. The standalone computation checks finite instances; the paper's proof establishes the uniform claim. No independent external audit has been conducted.
