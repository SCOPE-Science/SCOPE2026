# Scientific review: full-support singular opinions at a critical random-action learning rate

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact update is a valid stochastic row of a three-agent random-actions system with two absorbing zealots. A common-uniform monotone coupling gives contraction factor \(1-\alpha(1-s)\); conditional first and second moments give the stationary mean and variance. Affine rescaling reduces the support problem to two contractions with elementary interval-overlap and strong-separation cases. At the contact parameter, a two-sided stationary construction gives an ergodic binary action sequence whose entropy rate is \(\mathbb E H(X)\); the positive stationary variance makes this strictly smaller than \(\log 2\). Ergodic typical-word covers establish a full-mass null set and the stated quantitative upper bound on the measure's Hausdorff dimension. Strictly positive conditional action probabilities exclude atoms. The standalone exact-rational checker completed 550 identities; the infinite conclusions rely on the proof, not the checker.

## Originality
**PASS on a best-knowledge basis.** Leshem--Scaglione (2017) state the baseline random-actions update and treat consensus under an irreducibility hypothesis; their bounded-confidence fluctuation illustration is not a two-opposite-zealot stationary theorem. Abrahamsson--Danev--Larsson (2019/2020/2024) develop the one-stubborn-agent convergence phenomenon, which does not cover two opposing fixed actions. Cao--Cortez (2024/2025) prove a Cantor support in a different mean-field pairwise-persuasion model; their symmetric branching probabilities are externally averaged, whereas this theorem conditions a self-action on the present opinion, giving a singular law even at interval support. Barnsley--Demko--Elton--Geronimo (1988) and classical ergodic coding techniques supply general mathematical machinery; none of the inspected material specifies the present exact opposing-agent feedback, stationary variance, critical singularity, and entropy deficit as a combined theorem. Searches did not identify an equivalent record, but this is not proof of priority.

## Value
**PASS.** With deterministic balanced opposing influences, one might expect a smooth equilibrium once the geometric support fills the opinion interval. The theorem exhibits a rigorously different outcome: full support and a singular distribution coexist, with a quantitative loss of dimension depending on the self-trust parameter. This distinguishes geometric accessibility, statistical likelihood, and social-feedback noise in the smallest network with opposing immutable opinions. It also provides exact confidence-parameter and variance formulas for model evaluation.

## Closest literature and limits
The strongest conceptual overlap is with general place-dependent iterated-function-system theory, the two-stubborn-agent binary-voter literature, and the fractal-equilibrium work of Cao--Cortez; those ingredients and differences are credited in the result. Only the accessible abstract, not the full 2024 Abrahamsson--Danev--Larsson revision, was available. The accessible Cao--Cortez 2024 preprint and the source's 2017 and 2020 preprints were examined materially. An undiscovered stronger theorem or a related special case in an inaccessible revision remains an originality risk. Absolute continuity for rates below the critical boundary is not decided. The statement concerns this exact three-agent parameterized update, not arbitrary random-actions networks.
