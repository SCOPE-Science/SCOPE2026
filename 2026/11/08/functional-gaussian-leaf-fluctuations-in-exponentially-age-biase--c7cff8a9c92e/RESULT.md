# Functional Gaussian leaf fluctuations in exponentially age-biased recursive trees

## Finding

For each fixed \(r\in(0,1)\), let \(G_n\) be the rooted random recursive tree with vertices \(0,1,\ldots,n\) in which vertex \(t\), independently of all other parent choices, attaches to \(j<t\) with probability
\[
\Pr(\operatorname{parent}(t)=j)=\frac{(1-r)r^{t-j-1}}{1-r^t}.
\]
Equivalently, the attraction weight of vertex \(j\) is \(a^j\), where \(a=1/r>1\). A *leaf* is a vertex with no children, and \(L_n\) denotes the number of leaves of \(G_n\) (including a possible root leaf). Define
\[
p_h=(1-r)r^{h-1},\qquad
\mu_r=\prod_{h=1}^{\infty}(1-p_h)
\]
and, for every integer \(d\geq1\),
\[
R_d=\prod_{h=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{p_hp_{h+d}}{(1-p_h)(1-p_{h+d})}\right),
\qquad
v_r=\mu_r(1-\mu_r)+2\mu_r^2\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}(R_d-1).
\]
Every product and the variance series converges. The following hold simultaneously:

1. \(L_n/n\to\mu_r\) almost surely, and \(\mathbb E L_n=n\mu_r+O_r(1)\).
2. \(v_r>0\), with the explicit lower bound
   \[
   v_r\geq\frac{\mu_r(1-r)^2}{3(1+r)}.
   \]
   For every \(d\geq1\), \(R_d<1\), so the limiting leaf indicators separated by \(d\) arrivals have *strictly negative covariance*.
3. In the Skorohod \(J_1\) topology on \(D([0,1])\),
   \[
   \left(\frac{L_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}-\mu_r\lfloor nt\rfloor}{\sqrt n}:0\leq t\leq1\right)
   \Longrightarrow (\sqrt{v_r}\,B(t):0\leq t\leq1),
   \]
   where \(B\) is standard Brownian motion.
4. In the *ordered* weak-preference limit (first let \(n\to\infty\) at fixed \(r<1\), then \(r\uparrow1\)),
   \[
   \mu_r\longrightarrow e^{-1},\qquad
   v_r\longrightarrow e^{-1}-2e^{-2}>0.
   \]
   Both limits differ from the corresponding fixed-uniform-attachment values at exactly \(r=1\): the leaf density \(1/2\) and leaf-count variance coefficient \(1/12\). Thus the parameter-to-time limits do not commute. As \(r\downarrow0\), both \(\mu_r\) and \(v_r\) tend to zero.

## Assumptions and scope

The parameter \(r\) is fixed independently of \(n\), and satisfies \(0<r<1\). The tree is grown with one new vertex and one new edge per step, with attachment probabilities determined solely by birth labels, not by current degrees. The claim concerns the full number of zero-outdegree vertices, not the outdegree of a fixed vertex or the degree/leafdegree joint law of a degree-preferential tree. The functional limit is in distribution; the leaf proportion is almost surely convergent. No uniform joint \(r,n\) rate is asserted in part 4.

## Proof

**1. A stationary parent-lag representation.** On a two-sided time axis take independent \(H_t\), \(t\in\mathbb Z\), with \(\Pr(H_t=h)=p_h\) for \(h\geq1\). The directed edge from \(t\) points to \(t-H_t\). Define the stationary leaf indicator
\[
I_j=\mathbf 1\{\text{there is no }t>j\text{ with }H_t=t-j\}.
\]
Independence over the future times gives \(\Pr(I_0=1)=\mu_r\); the infinite product is positive because \(\sum_{h\geq1}p_h=1\), with each \(p_h<1\). For \(d\geq1\), times \(1,\ldots,d\) must avoid vertex 0, while times \(d+h\), \(h\geq1\), must avoid both 0 and \(d\). Consequently
\[
\mathbb E(I_0I_d)=\prod_{h=1}^{d}(1-p_h)
\prod_{h=1}^{\infty}(1-p_h-p_{h+d})=\mu_r^2R_d.
\]
All joint factors are nonnegative because \(p_h+p_{h+d}\leq p_h+p_{h+1}<1\) for \(r\in(0,1)\), with strict positivity except at the limiting endpoint. In particular, \(0<R_d<1\). Writing \(x_h=p_hp_{h+d}/[(1-p_h)(1-p_{h+d})]\), \(1-R_d\leq\sum_hx_h\), and since \(1-p_h\geq r\),
\[
0<1-R_d\leq r^{-2}\sum_{h\geq1}p_hp_{h+d}
=\frac{(1-r)r^{d-2}}{1+r}.
\]
The covariance series is therefore absolutely convergent.

**2. Exponential mixing and a functional CLT.** The sigma-field generated by \(I_j\), \(j\geq h\), is a function of \(\{H_t:t\geq h+1\}\). If all possible edges from times \(t\geq h\) into \(( -\infty,0]\) are deleted, the sigma-field generated by \(\{I_j:j\leq0\}\) becomes independent of the former. The probability of any deleted edge is bounded by
\[
\sum_{t=h}^{\infty}\Pr(H_t\geq t)=\frac{r^{h-1}}{1-r}.
\]
Thus the stationary bounded sequence \((I_j)\) is strongly mixing at an exponential rate. In particular, the covariance sum defining \(v_r\) is the asymptotic variance per summand, and the standard bounded, exponentially strongly mixing functional central limit theorem [4] applies if \(v_r>0\). The sequence is ergodic, so the strong law also holds.

**3. Strictly positive asymptotic variance.** Condition on all \(H_t\) except at times \(t\) divisible by three; at those times condition only on \(B_t=\mathbf1_{\{H_t\in\{1,2\}\}}\). For every selected time with \(B_t=1\), the independent residual choice is \(H_t=1\) with probability \(1/(1+r)\), or \(H_t=2\) with probability \(r/(1+r)\). Distinct selected times affect disjoint pairs of vertices. Let \(E_t\) be the condition that \(B_t=1\), \(H_{t-1}=1\), and no \(s>t\) chooses the parent vertex \(t-1\). This event is measurable under the conditioning: future unexposed choices have only lags 1 or 2 and cannot reach \(t-1\). On \(E_t\), vertex \(t-2\) already has a child, and switching the lag at \(t\) toggles exactly the leaf status of \(t-1\). Therefore the conditional variance of \(\sum_{j=1}^n I_j\) contains, for each selected \(t\) with \(3\leq t\leq n\), the term \(r/(1+r)^2\) whenever \(E_t\) holds. Because
\[
\Pr(E_t)=(1-r^2)(1-r)\prod_{h=2}^{\infty}(1-p_h)
=(1-r^2)(1-r)\frac{\mu_r}{r},
\]
the law of total variance implies
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{j=1}^nI_j\right)
\geq\frac13\frac{r}{(1+r)^2}\Pr(E_t)
=\frac{\mu_r(1-r)^2}{3(1+r)}>0.
\]
This establishes the lower bound and justifies the nondegenerate functional CLT.

**4. Coupling to finite growing trees.** For each \(t\geq1\), replace \(H_t\) by an independently re-sampled lag conditional on \(H_t\leq t\) only when \(H_t>t\). The resulting \(H'_t\) are independent and \(\Pr(H'_t=h)=p_h/(1-r^t)\) for \(1\leq h\leq t\), the exact finite-tree parent law. Since \(\sum_{t\geq1}\Pr(H_t>t)=\sum_t r^t<\infty\), only finitely many parents are changed almost surely; their effect on any leaf count is at most twice their number. For finite-time leaf counting, the additional discrepancy from unknown future children of vertices \(0,\ldots,k\) is bounded by
\[
C_k=\sum_{t>k}\mathbf1_{\{H_t\geq t-k\}}.
\]
Each \(C_k\) is a sum of independent Bernoulli random variables whose success probabilities are \(r^{t-k-1}\), so its exponential moments are bounded uniformly in \(k\). A union bound and the Borel–Cantelli lemma therefore yield \(\max_{0\leq k\leq n}C_k=O_r(\log(n+2))\) almost surely. Consequently
\[
\max_{0\leq k\leq n}\left|L_k-\sum_{j=1}^k I_j\right|
=O_r(\log(n+2))\quad\text{almost surely}
\]
(up to a finite random additive constant). This transfers the functional CLT and the strong law; the expected discrepancy is uniformly bounded because \(\sup_k\mathbb E C_k=1/(1-r)\) and the expected number of replacements is finite, proving \(\mathbb EL_n=n\mu_r+O_r(1)\).

**5. Weak-preference and rigid-preference boundaries.** Write \(\varepsilon=1-r\). Since \(\sum_hp_h=1\), \(\max_hp_h=\varepsilon\), and \(\sum_hp_h^2=\varepsilon/(1+r)\), expansion of \(\sum_h\log(1-p_h)\) gives \(\log\mu_r=-1+O(\varepsilon)\). For the covariance products, uniformly summing first in the time separation yields
\[
\sum_{d\geq1}\sum_{h\geq1}p_hp_{h+d}
=\tfrac12\left[\left(\sum_hp_h\right)^2-\sum_hp_h^2\right]
=\frac{r}{1+r}.
\]
Taylor expansion of \(\log R_d\), with \(\max_hp_h\leq\varepsilon\), bounds the sum over all \(d\) of the absolute errors in replacing \(R_d-1\) by \(-\sum_h p_hp_{h+d}\) by \(O(\varepsilon)\). Thus \(\sum_d(R_d-1)\to-1/2\), implying \(v_r\to e^{-1}-2e^{-2}\). As \(r\downarrow0\), \(\mu_r\leq1-p_1=r\), and the strictly negative covariance terms imply \(0<v_r\leq\mu_r(1-\mu_r)\to0\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library. It exhaustively enumerates every parent-choice sequence for sizes \(1\leq n\leq6\) and three rational parameter values, checking the exact one-point/two-point leaf probabilities and resulting second moments against independently derived product formulas. It also checks truncated stationary products, covariance signs where resolved above floating-point precision, strict variance positivity diagnostics, and the weak-preference trend. A complete replay produced **838 passed assertions**. These finite checks are diagnostics; the functional theorem and its endpoint limits are proved analytically above, not by finite enumeration. Products and series can be evaluated to any prescribed precision using geometric tail bounds.

## Relationship to prior work

The source of the recent leaf-statistics motivation is Hartle and Krapivsky [1], whose 2026 paper treats degree-driven attachment (also comparing uniform recursive trees and a redirection model), and which analyzes concentration and leafdegree. The exponential age-by-label attachment law itself was defined and studied earlier by Aguech et al. [2], publicly presented as early as March 2024 [3]; it is not new here. Their full accessible article and presentation concern outdegree laws of individual vertices and their birth-time phases, not a functional limit for the total leaf count, its negative spatial covariances, or the weak-preference noncommutation. The present result is a cross-model leaf-fluctuation calculation, not an assertion that the 2026 source introduced the exponential model. The functional invariance principle invoked as a standard ingredient is due to earlier mixing theory [4]. The proof supplies its own new product covariance evaluation and coupling.

## Limitations

This theorem treats fixed \(a>1\) only. It does not give a critical joint limit when \(a=a_n\downarrow1\), treat \(a<1\), or determine the whole empirical outdegree profile. The numerical checker proves nothing for infinite samples. Historical priority for the particular explicit covariance series and crossover remains subject to independent literature verification; published models or general factor-of-iid theorems may imply pieces of the statement. The asserted novelty is limited to the combined explicit total-leaf variance, functional limit, and boundary behavior for exponential birth-label attachment.
