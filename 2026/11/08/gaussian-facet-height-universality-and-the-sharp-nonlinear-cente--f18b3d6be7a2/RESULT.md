# Gaussian facet-height universality and the sharp nonlinear-centering transition for spherical random polytopes

## Finding

Let \(d\ge3\) and \(n>d\), and let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be independent uniform points on the unit sphere \(S^{d-1}\). The **typical facet** means the candidate simplex \(\operatorname{conv}(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\), *conditioned* on being a facet of the hull of all \(n\) points. Denote the signed outward supporting-plane height by \(H_{d,n}\): its facet plane has equation \(\langle u,x\rangle=H_{d,n}\) with the whole hull in \(\langle u,x\rangle\le H_{d,n}\). This signed height can be negative. The result is **not** about choosing a uniformly random facet conditional on each realized polytope.

Write \(m=n-d\), \(\rho_d=m/d\), \(\phi\) and \(\Phi\) for the standard normal density and distribution function, \(\lambda(x)=\phi(x)/\Phi(x)\). For every \(\rho\ge0\) there is a unique \(r_\rho\ge0\) satisfying
\[
r_\rho=\rho\lambda(r_\rho),\qquad r_0=0.
\]
Put
\[
\sigma_\rho^2=\left[1+\rho\lambda(r_\rho)(r_\rho+\lambda(r_\rho))\right]^{-1}.
\]
The following statements hold.

**Uniform finite-dimensional concentration.** For every \(d\ge3\) and \(m\ge1\), putting \(Y_{d,n}=dH_{d,n}\) and \(\kappa_d=1-2/d-1/d^2>0\), for every real \(t\),
\[
\mathbb E\exp\bigl(t(Y_{d,n}-\mathbb E Y_{d,n})\bigr)\le \exp\bigl(t^2/(2\kappa_d)\bigr).
\]
Consequently \(\operatorname{Var}(Y_{d,n})\le1/\kappa_d\), and for every \(s>0\),
\[
\Pr(|Y_{d,n}-\mathbb E Y_{d,n}|\ge s)\le2\exp(-\kappa_d s^2/2).
\]

**Moving-center total-variation Gaussian law.** If \(d\to\infty\) and \(\rho_d\to\rho\in[0,\infty)\), then
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(\mathcal L(dH_{d,n}-\sqrt d\,r_{\rho_d}),\,N(0,\sigma_\rho^2)\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]
All fixed absolute moments converge, and in particular
\[
\lim_{d\to\infty}d^2\operatorname{Var}(H_{d,n})=\sigma_\rho^2.
\]
The function \(\rho\mapsto\sigma_\rho^2\) is strictly decreasing for positive \(\rho\), with \(\sigma_0^2=1\) and \(\sigma_\rho^2\downarrow0\) as \(\rho\to\infty\) *after* the finite-ratio limit.

**Gaussian–spherical universality.** Let \(G_{d,n}\) be the signed supporting-plane height of the labeled candidate facet generated by the first \(d\) independent standard Gaussian vectors in \(\mathbb R^d\), conditioned on being a facet of their \(n\)-point Gaussian convex hull. Under the same finite-ratio hypothesis,
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(\mathcal L(\sqrt d\,H_{d,n}),\mathcal L(G_{d,n})\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This compares distributions of metric offsets of two *different* random-polytope models, under their respective facet conditioning.

**Sharp centering crossover.** Let \(\mu=\sqrt{2/\pi}\). If \(m=o(d)\) and \(m/d^{3/4}\to\tau\in[0,\infty)\), then
\[
dH_{d,n}-\frac{\mu m}{\sqrt d}\ \Longrightarrow\ N(-\mu^3\tau^2,1).
\]
If instead \(d^{3/4}\ll m\ll d\), the same linearly centered quantity tends to \(-\infty\) in probability. Therefore \(d^{3/4}\) is the exact threshold at which the previously natural linear centering ceases to describe the fluctuations; the nonlinear saddlepoint centering remains valid for all finite \(\rho\).

## Assumptions and scope

All points are independent; both models use Euclidean orthogonal projections and outward *signed* hyperplane offsets; candidates are the first \(d\) labeled observations and are conditioned to form facets. Fixing \(d\) while increasing \(m\), or taking \(m/d\to\infty\), is outside the Gaussian limit statements. The concentration inequality applies to every finite \(d\ge3\) and \(m\ge1\). The event defining each typical facet has positive probability. In the Gaussian model, the normal direction is selected by the orientation for which the other sample points lie on the inner side.

## Proof

**1. Exact spherical facet density.** The affine Blaschke–Petkantschin calculation, as explicitly stated in Bonnet–O'Reilly [3, equation (6.3)], gives the *normalized* conditional density
\[
p_{d,m}(h)=\frac{(1-h^2)^{\alpha_d}F_d(h)^m}{\displaystyle\int_{-1}^1(1-s^2)^{\alpha_d}F_d(s)^m\,ds}\,\mathbf 1_{(-1,1)}(h),
\quad \alpha_d=\frac{d^2-2d-1}{2},
\]
where
\[
F_d(h)=\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((d-1)/2)}\int_{-1}^h(1-s^2)^{(d-3)/2}\,ds
\]
is the CDF of one coordinate of a uniform spherical direction. All constants independent of \(h\) cancel under conditioning. The two possible outward facet orientations together give precisely this formula, rather than a density restricted to positive offsets.

**2. A global curvature inequality.** For \(d\ge3\), the coordinate density \(F_d'\), proportional to \((1-h^2)^{(d-3)/2}\), is log-concave on \((-1,1)\); its CDF \(F_d\) is also log-concave (by integration of a log-concave density over a half-line). Set \(y=dh\). Apart from an additive constant, its negative log-density is
\[
V_{d,m}(y)=-\alpha_d\log(1-y^2/d^2)-m\log F_d(y/d),\qquad |y|<d.
\]
The second term is convex, while direct differentiation gives
\[
\frac{d^2}{dy^2}\left[-\alpha_d\log(1-y^2/d^2)\right]
=\kappa_d\frac{1+y^2/d^2}{(1-y^2/d^2)^2}\ge\kappa_d.
\]
Thus \(V_{d,m}''\ge\kappa_d\) everywhere. The one-dimensional strong-log-concavity log-Sobolev inequality, followed by the Herbst argument for the 1-Lipschitz observable \(y\), proves the claimed centered moment-generating bound; the variance and Chernoff tail bounds follow. The potential diverges at both endpoints, and ordinary smooth approximation justifies all boundary integrations.

**3. Uniform local normal approximation.** Put \(x=\sqrt d\,h\). The density expansion of a scaled spherical coordinate, integrated once, gives uniformly in \(C^2\) on any fixed compact \(x\)-interval
\[
F_d(x/\sqrt d)=\Phi(x)+\frac{\phi(x)(x^3-3x)}{4d}+O(d^{-2}).
\]
Indeed, Stirling's formula gives its density as \(\phi(x)[1+(-x^4+6x^2-3)/(4d)+O(d^{-2})]\), and the displayed primitive vanishes at negative infinity. Hence for every bounded \(\rho_d\), the logarithm of the unnormalized density of \(x\) is, uniformly together with its first two derivatives on compact sets,
\[
d f_{\rho_d}(x)+g_{\rho_d}(x)+O(d^{-1}),
\qquad f_\rho(x)=-x^2/2+\rho\log\Phi(x),
\]
\[
g_\rho(x)=x^2-x^4/4+\frac{\rho\,\phi(x)(x^3-3x)}{4\Phi(x)}.
\]
The negative inverse-Mills derivative identity
\[
\lambda'(x)=-\lambda(x)(x+\lambda(x))
\]
shows \(f_\rho''(x)=-1-\rho\lambda(x)(x+\lambda(x))<0\). The strict inequality follows from the positive variance of a standard normal conditioned to lie below \(x\), which also gives \(-1<\lambda'(x)<0\). The score \(f_\rho'(x)=-x+\rho\lambda(x)\) has exactly one zero \(r_\rho\); that zero is nonnegative. As \(\rho_d\to\rho\), the saddle stays in a compact interval. The actual finite-dimensional mode of the \(x\)-density is \(r_{\rho_d}+O(d^{-1})\), by uniform \(C^2\) expansion and strict curvature.

For \(y=dH_{d,n}-\sqrt d\,r_{\rho_d}=\sqrt d(x-r_{\rho_d})\), Taylor expansion at the moving saddle gives, locally uniformly in \(y\),
\[
\log\frac{p_Y(y)}{p_Y(0)}\longrightarrow-\frac{y^2}{2\sigma_\rho^2}.
\]
The curvature inequality from Step 2 bounds the density above by its value at the finite-dimensional mode times \(\exp[-\kappa_d(y-y_{\mathrm{mode}})^2/2]\). The mode remains bounded in \(y\)-coordinates, and integration on a fixed neighborhood of the mode bounds the normalization below by a fixed multiple of its maximum. These facts give a uniform integrable Gaussian envelope. Dominated convergence yields the normalized local Gaussian density; Scheffé's theorem yields total-variation convergence. The envelope gives convergence of all fixed absolute moments and the variance formula.

For strict monotonicity of \(\sigma_\rho^2\), rewrite \(\rho=r/\lambda(r)\), an increasing function of \(r\ge0\), and the inverse variance as \(1+r^2+r\lambda(r)\). Its derivative in \(r\) is \(2r+\lambda(r)+r\lambda'(r)>r+\lambda(r)>0\). The limiting values follow immediately from \(r_0=0\) and \(r_\rho\to\infty\) as \(\rho\to\infty\).

**4. Gaussian comparison.** The first \(d\) Gaussian points have sample mean independent of their centered affine residuals. The unit normal to the \((d-1)\)-dimensional residual span is therefore independent of that mean, and its signed plane offset is a normal variable of variance \(1/d\). Conditioning on the remaining \(m\) Gaussian points lying on the inner side weights an offset \(g\) by exactly \(\Phi(g)^m\). Consequently
\[
q_{d,m}(g)=\frac{\exp(-dg^2/2)\Phi(g)^m}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R}\exp(-ds^2/2)\Phi(s)^m\,ds},\qquad g\in\mathbb R.
\]
The log-density is exactly \(d f_{\rho_d}(g)\). The same moving-saddle Laplace argument gives total variation convergence of \(\sqrt d(G_{d,n}-r_{\rho_d})\) to \(N(0,\sigma_\rho^2)\). The corresponding spherical variable is \(\sqrt d(\sqrt d H_{d,n}-r_{\rho_d})\). Total variation is unchanged by applying a common invertible affine transformation, so the triangle inequality proves the claimed unscaled sphere-versus-Gaussian total variation comparison.

**5. Sharp critical centering.** The analytic implicit equation \(r=\rho\lambda(r)\) at \((r,\rho)=(0,0)\), with \(\lambda(0)=\mu\) and \(\lambda'(0)=-\mu^2\), gives
\[
r_\rho=\mu\rho-\mu^3\rho^2+O(\rho^3).
\]
Therefore
\[
\sqrt d\,r_{m/d}-\mu m/\sqrt d=-\mu^3m^2/d^{3/2}+O(m^3/d^{5/2}).
\]
If \(m/d^{3/4}\to\tau\), the shift tends to \(-\mu^3\tau^2\) and the moving-center normal law has limiting variance one, proving the first crossover assertion by Slutsky. If \(d^{3/4}\ll m\ll d\), the displayed shift tends to minus infinity because the error is smaller by \(m/d\to0\), while moving-centered fluctuations are tight. This proves the sharpness statement.

## Verification

The standalone numerical script `verify.py` is included. It evaluates the exact conditional density using a regularized incomplete-beta spherical-coordinate CDF, solves the saddle equation by scalar bracketing, checks the finite-dimensional curvature numerically, and compares the integral variance/CDF to the limiting normal law. It independently draws sphere points, finds candidate affine hyperplane normals by singular-value decomposition, conditions geometrically on the facet event, and compares simulated offsets to direct integration. It also numerically compares the spherical and Gaussian tilted densities in total variation and evaluates the first nonlinear centering shift. Representative results: at \((d,m)=(600,1800)\), computed variance of the centered law was \(0.46175\) against limiting \(0.46130\); at \((500,1000)\), the sphere/Gaussian total-variation distance was approximately \(0.0163\). The script prints `ALL_HEIGHT_UNIVERSALITY_CHECKS_PASSED`. These finite calculations do not replace the asymptotic proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Bonnet and O'Reilly [3] provide the exact facet-height integral used in Step 1, the near-simplex Gaussian limit when \((n-d)/\sqrt d\) is bounded, the first-order typical-height laws in intermediate/proportional regimes, and a different gamma/normal result in a super-exponential sample-size regime. The contribution here is the **finite-ratio moving-centered total-variation CLT with its exact variance**, **universal finite-sample curvature bound**, **sphere/Gaussian metric-offset TV equivalence**, and the **sharp \(d^{3/4}\) centering crossover**. These do not assert a new derivation of the underlying density or the earlier first-order laws. Kabluchko, Temesvari, and Thäle [1] evaluate expected facet numbers/intrinsic volumes for beta and limiting spherical/Gaussian models; Böröczky, Lugosi, and Reitzner [2] study expected Gaussian-polytope facet counts. Their observables differ from the conditional typical facet offset here.

## Limitations

This result is specific to the *annealed, labeled-candidate conditioned* notion of typical facet. It makes no statement about choosing a facet uniformly within each realized random polytope, about extreme facet heights, or about joint face counts. The total-variation limits require a finite limiting ratio \((n-d)/d\); the concentration inequality is valid for each finite \(d\ge3\). Numerical checks are finite and are not a proof of infinite-dimensional limits. Historical-priority assessment is based on inspected accessible primary work and semantic searches; an equivalent result under different stochastic-geometric terminology could exist. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
