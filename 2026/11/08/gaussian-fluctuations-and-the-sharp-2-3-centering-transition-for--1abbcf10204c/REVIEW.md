The infinitely differentiable harmonic kernel admits an arbitrarily accurate Euler--Maclaurin expansion, and the missing finite tail contributes precisely the first \(a/n\) correction. Monotone first-passage inversion proves the asserted random-time limit. The critical offset is the algebraic comparison of \(B_n^2/n\) with \(\sqrt{B_n}\). Finite enumerations and numerical tests were independently replayed from the deliverable, but the infinite result rests on these analytic steps.

Related published mathematical records on reset coupons, Zipf expanding maps, and generic rare-event statistics do not state a covering quorum result. Classical Lyapunov and Euler--Maclaurin techniques are acknowledged as known. The possibility of stronger general empirical-process or inverse-process results in unexamined sources remains a residual priority risk.

These are natural exact stochastic invariants of the original model and require a nontrivial second-order uniform harmonic expansion.
