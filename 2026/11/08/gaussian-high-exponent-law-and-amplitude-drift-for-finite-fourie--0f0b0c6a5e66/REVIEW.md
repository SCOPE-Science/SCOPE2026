## Originality — PASS, provisional priority
The closest full-text comparison is the published sharp \(L^2\to L^6\) coefficient-norm result for the three frequencies \(\{0,1,3\}\); it proves one isolated, finite-exponent amplitude optimum, but does not imply the present full-arbitrary-aperture large-even-exponent Gaussian law, universal amplitude correction or eventual phase classification. Jaming–Kellay–Saba (2023, arXiv:2303.10919) study lower \(L^1\) inequalities, not the high-\(p\) optimized upper norm; Ganzburg–Saucedo–Tikhonov (2024, arXiv:2403.13149) concern derivative-order dependence, and Ganzburg (2019, arXiv:1901.04400) spectral-bandwidth limiting constants. Searches of published mathematical results for high exponent, amplitude drift, coefficient profile, sparse Fourier and Nikolskii asymptotics did not reveal an implication covering all three components. The elementary even-integer majorant is prior art. A related general statement could still appear under asymptotic Laplace optimization terminology; priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For a natural sharp coefficient-to-function norm on *every fixed finite integer frequency aperture*, the result determines the first frequency-sensitive correction to the extremal norm and a complete explicit first-order correction for each optimal coefficient, with a uniqueness-and-phase-rigidity theorem rather than mere existence. The correction distinguishes spectral outliers from central modes and is invariant under the genuine modulation/reparameterization symmetries; it can quantify the high-exponent behavior of finite-band Fourier synthesis and compare different aperture geometries. The result goes beyond the trivial \(p=\infty\) Cauchy–Schwarz extremum and beyond applying a scalar Laplace estimate to a fixed, already-chosen coefficient vector. Scope is only fixed finite integer apertures and sufficiently large even exponents, not growing bandwidth or all real exponents.
