Every nontrivial mixture of two Gaussian measures on \(\mathbb C^d\) gives a weighted Fock reproducing kernel with infinitely many zeros, although each Gaussian endpoint is zero-free. As the faster-decaying component has weight \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the first zero escapes at the sharp rate \(\log(1/\varepsilon)/[a(1-a/b)]\), even while the weight potential converges in every uniform smooth norm to a strictly plurisubharmonic quadratic.
