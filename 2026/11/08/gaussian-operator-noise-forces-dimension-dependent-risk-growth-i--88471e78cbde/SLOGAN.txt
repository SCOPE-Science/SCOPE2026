For a once-sampled doubly noisy scalar Fourier mode, a positive spectral-damping floor makes each fixed Kaczmarz iterate square-integrable but cannot prevent ensemble risk from growing as \(\sqrt t\) in real modes or \(\log t\) in circular complex modes, with exact leading constants. Without that floor the mean-square error is infinite at the very first iteration; in either case the fixed-data iterates converge almost surely to the noisy reciprocal \(B/A\).
