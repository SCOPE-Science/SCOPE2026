# Generating idempotent tuples in height-two incidence algebras: a star law and a field-size crossover

## Finding
Let \(P\) be a labeled finite poset of height at most two, \(E(P)\) its strict comparable (hence covering) pairs, and \(A=I(P,\mathbb F_q)\). For every integer \(k\ge1\), the number \(G_{P,k}(q)\) of ordered tuples of \(k\) idempotents generating \(A\) as a unital \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra is
\[
G_{P,k}(q)=\sum_{\phi:P\hookrightarrow\{0,1\}^k}\ \prod_{(u,v)\in E(P)}\left(q^{d_H(\phi(u),\phi(v))}-q\right).
\]
The sum ranges over injective maps of the labeled vertex set into the binary cube, and \(d_H\) is Hamming distance. An empty sum is zero, and an empty product is one.

For the star poset \(P_r\) with one minimal vertex and \(r\ge1\) labeled maximal vertices, the formula specializes to the exact polynomial coefficient
\[
G_{r,k}(q)=2^k r!\,[z^r]\prod_{h=2}^k\left(1+(q^h-q)z\right)^{\binom{k}{h}}.
\]
There are \(2(q+1)^r\) idempotents in \(I(P_r,\mathbb F_q)\). Thus the probability \(p_{r,k}(q)\) that \(k\) independent uniform idempotents generate it is
\[
p_{r,k}(q)=\frac{r!\,[z^r]\prod_{h=2}^k(1+(q^h-q)z)^{\binom{k}{h}}}{(q+1)^{kr}}.
\]
For every fixed integer \(r\ge2\), the asymptotic failure probability as \(k\to\infty\) undergoes a field-size crossover:
\[
1-p_{r,k}(2)\sim 2r(2/3)^k,\qquad
1-p_{r,k}(q)\sim\binom r2\left(\frac{q^2+1}{(q+1)^2}\right)^k\quad(q\ge3\text{ fixed}).
\]
The dominant obstruction is a single star edge at \(q=2\), but a collision of two leaf diagonal codewords at every fixed \(q\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope
All posets are finite and labeled, all fields are \(\mathbb F_q\) with \(q\) a prime power, and generation includes the identity and scalar matrices. The general formula applies to posets with no chains of three distinct elements. A star has a single minimal element below each of \(r\) pairwise incomparable maximal elements. The asymptotic statement fixes \(r\ge2\) and fixes \(q\) before letting the number \(k\) of generators grow. Exponents \(k\) and \(r\) are integers, and \([z^r]\) denotes ordinary coefficient extraction.

## Proof
**Step 1: Local generation criterion.** Write each candidate generator as \(a_s=(a_s(u,u),a_s(u,v))\), indexed by \(s=1,\ldots,k\). The diagonal projection from the incidence algebra to \(\mathbb F_q^P\) is an algebra homomorphism. Its image generated by these elements is the entire diagonal algebra exactly when the \(k\)-coordinate diagonal words \(\phi(u)=(a_s(u,u))_s\) are pairwise different: distinct point evaluations can be separated by polynomials over a field. For each covering relation \(u\prec v\), project to its two-by-two triangular corner. Its unital generated algebra is the whole corner if and only if the off-diagonal vector \(b_{uv}=(a_s(u,v))_s\) is not in the scalar span of the nonzero diagonal-difference vector \(\delta_{uv}=(a_s(u,u)-a_s(v,v))_s\). Indeed the span of the identity and the \(k\) projected generators fills the three-dimensional triangular corner exactly when these two vectors are not proportional. Necessity and sufficiency jointly for all covers are the matrix incidence algebra generator criterion of Longstaff and Rosenthal (2000), Theorem, and are independently rederived by restricting the incidence algebra to every covering corner and separating all diagonal coordinates.

**Step 2: Idempotency.** If the poset has height at most two, the strict upper-diagonal ideal \(J\) has \(J^2=0\). For an idempotent \(a_s\), the diagonal entries obey \(a_s(u,u)^2=a_s(u,u)\), hence lie in \(\{0,1\}\). On any strict comparable pair \(u<v\), the off-diagonal equation is \((a_s(u,u)+a_s(v,v)-1)a_s(u,v)=0\). Thus \(a_s(u,v)=0\) whenever the endpoint bits agree, and it can be any of \(q\) field values otherwise. The equations for different edges are independent because \(J^2=0\).

**Step 3: Count.** Fix an injective binary-word assignment \(\phi\). For an edge whose endpoint words have Hamming distance \(h\ge1\), exactly \(h\) coordinates of \(b_{uv}\) are free, giving \(q^h\) idempotent-compatible vectors. Exactly \(q\) of them are multiples of \(\delta_{uv}\) and hence fail generation. Therefore exactly \(q^h-q\) pass. Multiply over edges and sum over all injective diagonal assignments. This proves the general identity (and includes the zero cases \(h=1\)).

**Step 4: Star coefficient.** Fix the \(k\)-bit root word (\(2^k\) choices). Relative to it, the \(r\) labeled leaves must receive distinct difference words. A difference word of weight \(h\) gives factor \(q^h-q\), and there are \(\binom{k}{h}\) choices. The weighted elementary-symmetric polynomial gives the displayed coefficient times \(r!\). For one idempotent, independently choosing each leaf bit and its edge coordinate gives \(1+q\) choices per leaf after fixing the root bit; multiplying by two root bits gives \(2(q+1)^r\).

**Step 5: Sharp crossover.** Condition on the root bit vector. For each of the \(k\) generators, the relative leaf bit agrees with the root with probability \(1/(q+1)\), and differs with probability \(q/(q+1)\). The leaves are independent. A specified edge fails the local generation test with exact probability
\[
b_k=\frac{1+q(2^k-1)}{(q+1)^k}\sim q\left(\frac2{q+1}\right)^k.
\]
The count \(1+q(2^k-1)\) is the sum over the all-equal diagonal word (one choice), and over all nonzero relative words (\(q\) forbidden off-diagonal vectors each). Two specified leaf diagonal words collide with exact probability
\[
c_k=\left(\frac{q^2+1}{(q+1)^2}\right)^k.
\]
For fixed \(r\), generation fails exactly when one of the \(r\) edges fails or two of the \(r\) leaf words collide. The intersection of two distinct edge-failure events is \(b_k^2\) (conditional independence given the root). An intersection between an edge-failure event and any collision is bounded by \(b_k\). Two distinct leaf-collision events on a shared leaf have probability \(\bigl((q^3+1)/(q+1)^3\bigr)^k=o(c_k)\); disjoint pairs have probability \(c_k^2\). If \(q=2\), then \(2/(q+1)> (q^2+1)/(q+1)^2\), hence the \(r\) edge events dominate and yield \(r b_k\sim 2r(2/3)^k\). If \(q\ge3\), then \((q^2+1)/(q+1)^2>2/(q+1)\), so the \(\binom r2\) collision events dominate, and the stated asymptotic follows. Every remainder is exponentially smaller with fixed \(r\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` independently enumerates all idempotents in small star incidence algebras and tests actual unital algebra generation by repeatedly closing the linear span of words under multiplication. It checks \((r,k,q)=(1,2,2),(1,3,2),(2,2,2),(2,3,2),(1,2,3),(1,3,3)\). The resulting generating-tuple counts are respectively \(8,96,0,768,24,336\). A separate weighted enumeration of labeled leaf codewords checks additional cases, including \((r,k,q)=(3,4,3)\). Exact rational comparison checks the asymptotic leading terms at large finite \(k\). All tests end in `VERIFY_OK`. Computations are finite corroboration, not the proof for all posets or fields.

## Relationship to prior work
Longstaff and Rosenthal (2000) establish the unrestricted incidence-algebra generator criterion; it is used here, not claimed as new. Kolegov (2024, first public date 2024-03-05) determines the minimum number of idempotent generators by hypercube embeddings; the minimum threshold itself is known. Kolegov (2025) gives a product formula for unrestricted random tuples over finite incidence rings, including the \(q^k-q\) local edge factor. That unrestricted product does not count idempotent tuples, where the admissible edge-coordinate dimension is Hamming distance and the global diagonal assignment is constrained to the binary cube. The present result computes the complete weighted idempotent-tuples enumerator in height two, the star coefficient expression, and a field-size-dependent sharp asymptotic obstruction law. No independent priority guarantee is asserted.

## Limitations
The general weighted formula is proved only when the incidence radical has square zero (height at most two); in longer posets, idempotency couples coordinates through intermediate vertices. The coefficient formula concerns labeled star leaves, not isomorphism orbits. The crossover fixes the field and the number of leaves before sending the tuple length to infinity. The complete 2024 journal article was not accessible; its abstract reports minimal numbers rather than the present counts, but uninspected derivations could contain related enumerative arguments. The 1998 upper-triangular minimum-generator paper was also not read in full. Independent mathematical review and publication-priority verification remain open.
