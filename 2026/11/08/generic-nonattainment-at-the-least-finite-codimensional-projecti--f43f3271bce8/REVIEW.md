# Review of generic nonattainment at the minimal finite-codimensional projection constant

## Correctness — PASS

The claim's base domain is the class of positive, pairwise singular Borel probability tuples on a perfect compact metric space. The exact projection norm is obtained from the mutually singular measure basis and the finite-rank Daugavet equation. A measurable disjoint-band projection proves the sharp infimum of two; regularity of measures and finite-dimensional matrix inversion also provide continuous near-minimizers. Norm-two attainment is forced by continuous coordinate functions being one on their respective measure supports and zero on the others. Conversely, finite disjoint closed supports admit continuous separating functions. The Baire assertions follow from total-variation lower semicontinuity and dense finite disjoint-support approximants, together with standard generic nonatomic/full-support measure properties. The Lipschitz lower bound is a scalar integral estimate, and the upper construction has exact rational coefficients. The included checker passed 11,000 exact rational assertions; no finite computation is used to claim the infinite-dimensional results.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Kania and Lewicki (arXiv:2604.10771v1) provide the previously known Daugavet projection-duality theorem, a hyperplane norm-attainment criterion, and nonattaining codimension-two families at projection constants strictly above two. This finding does not re-claim their duality or the value two; it classifies **every** pairwise-singular positive finite annihilator at the minimal constant by topological support separation, establishes a dense/meagre dichotomy, and gives a quantitative inverse-tolerance regularity cost. The Lenz–Stollmann generic-measure results supply prior measure-theoretic input. A separate codimension-two projection-interval result \(2+t/(1+t+t^2)\), \(0<t\le1\), likewise lies strictly above two. Searches for the exact support criterion, weak-star-continuous contractive measure-span projections, generic minimal-projection nonattainment, and the Lipschitz cost found no theorem directly covering the combined statement. General contractive-projection theory could still subsume its support criterion; independent priority review remains desirable.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The minimal possible projection constant is not enough to predict the existence of an optimal projection. The theorem isolates the decisive topological invariant (pairwise disjoint closed supports) within a natural measure-theoretic class, across every finite codimension, and shows that even though optimizers are dense, their existence is generically exceptional. The explicit interval example quantifies the ill-conditioning of near-minimizers by the optimal inverse-error **exponent**. This addresses the minimal endpoint omitted by the recent strictly-superminimal codimension-two constructions and distinguishes measurable separation from topological separation. The general theorem does not treat signed measures, and the optimal Lipschitz constant is open.
