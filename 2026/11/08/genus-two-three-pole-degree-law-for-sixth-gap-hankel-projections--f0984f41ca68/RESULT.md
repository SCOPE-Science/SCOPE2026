# Genus-two three-pole degree law for sixth-gap Hankel projections

## Finding
Let \(n\ge7\), and let \(H_{2,n}\subset\mathbb A^n_{\mathbb C}\) be the irreducible rank-at-most-two Hankel determinantal variety, whose ideal is generated by the three-by-three minors of the three-row Hankel matrix \((x_{i+j-1})\). The coordinate projection recording the four positions \(\{1,2,6,n\}\) has exact generic degree
\[
\deg\pi_{\{1,2,6,n\}}=2n-7+\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor-\mathbf1_{\{n\equiv2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)\}}.
\]
The first three observations cut out a generically smooth genus-two curve. On its smooth projective completion, the last coordinate has precisely three poles, with respective orders \(n-6\), \(n-1-\mathbf1_{\{n\equiv2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)\}}\), and \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\). Their sum is the asserted degree.

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over \(\mathbb C\), and \(n\) ranges over every integer at least seven. The coordinate projection degree is the degree of the induced finite extension of rational function fields, equivalently the number of completions of a general four-coordinate observation, without multiplicity. The rank-at-most-two locus is the reduced irreducible variety, not an unexamined scheme structure of exceptional fibers. It is enough to work with generic first, second, and sixth observations \(a,b,c\); the nonvanishing conditions used below hold on a dense open subset.

## Proof
**Recurrence chart and genus.** A general point of \(H_{2,n}\) has a unique order-two recurrence
\[
x_{j+2}=s x_{j+1}+t x_j,\qquad x_1=a,\quad x_2=b.
\]
Indeed the recurrence is unique whenever \(x_1x_3-x_2^2\ne0\); this condition holds generically. Fixing \(x_6=c\) imposes
\[
F(s,t)=(2as+b)t^2+(as^3+3bs^2)t+bs^4-c=0.
\]
This curve is irreducible and generically smooth: its discriminant in \(t\),
\[
\Delta(s)=(as^3+3bs^2)^2-4(2as+b)(bs^4-c),
\]
is generically a squarefree polynomial of degree six. To verify generic squarefreeness it suffices to exhibit one specialization; \((a,b,c)=(1,2,3)\) gives \(\Delta(s)=s^6-4s^5+20s^4+24s+24\), coprime to its derivative. Hence the smooth projective model of the generic three-observation fiber is a hyperelliptic curve of genus two. The recurrence chart is birational to its image in the Hankel variety: the first two recurrence coefficients recover uniquely from the first four entries wherever the initial two-by-two Hankel determinant is nonzero.

**All points at infinity.** Regard \(F=0\) as a two-sheeted cover of the \(s\)-line. There are two points over \(s=\infty\): at the first, denoted \(P\), \(t/s\longrightarrow-b/a\), and at the second, denoted \(Q\), \(t/s^2\longrightarrow-1/2\). The two distinct Newton slopes imply that \(1/s\) is a uniformizer at both points. There is exactly one more point at infinity, denoted \(R\), above the finite coordinate \(s_0=-b/(2a)\): the leading coefficient of \(F\) as a quadratic in \(t\) vanishes simply there, while its coefficient of \(t\) does not vanish generically. Thus \(t\) has a simple pole at \(R\), with \(s\longrightarrow s_0\). There are no further points where \(s\) or \(t\) has a pole. Since every \(x_j\) is a polynomial in \(s,t\), these three points contain the full polar support of \(x_n\).

**Pole at \(P\).** The equation \(F=0\) gives an expansion \(t=-(b/a)s+b^2/a^2+O(s^{-1})\). The recurrence implies that \(x_3,x_4,x_5\) are bounded there. For example \(x_3=bs+at\) is bounded, then \(x_4=sx_3+bt\) is bounded, and \(x_5\) must be bounded because \(x_6=sx_5+tx_4=c\). Dividing these equations successively by \(s\) shows
\[
x_3\longrightarrow b^2/a,\quad x_4\longrightarrow b^3/a^2,\quad x_5\longrightarrow b^4/a^3.
\]
Consequently \(x_7=s c+t x_5\) has leading term \((c-b^5/a^4)s\), nonzero for generic \(a,b,c\). For every \(j\ge8\), in \(x_j=sx_{j-1}+tx_{j-2}\) the first summand has strictly larger pole order than the second. Induction therefore gives \(\operatorname{ord}_P(x_n)=-(n-6)\).

**Pole at \(Q\) and the period-four cancellation.** For the formal recurrence polynomial \(x_n(s,t)\), put \(u=t/s^2\) and define \(f_j,g_j\) by
\[
x_j=a s^{j-1}f_j(u)+b s^{j-2}g_j(u),\quad
f_1=1,\ f_2=0,\ g_1=0,\ g_2=1,
\]
with \(f_j=f_{j-1}+u f_{j-2}\) and the same recurrence for \(g_j\). The equation \(F=0\) at \(Q\) forces
\[
u=-\frac12-\frac{b}{4as}+O(s^{-2}).
\]
At \(u=-1/2\), the roots of the characteristic quadratic are \((1\pm i)/2\); their fourth powers equal \(-1/4\). Thus \(f_j(-1/2)=0\) exactly when \(j\equiv2\pmod4\). For all other \(j\ge7\), the coefficient of \(s^{j-1}\) in \(x_j\) is nonzero, proving pole order \(j-1\). When \(j=4k+2\), elementary differentiation of the two recurrences yields
\[
f_j(-1/2)=0,\quad g_j(-1/2)=(-1/4)^k,\quad f'_j(-1/2)=4k(-1/4)^k.
\]
The coefficient of \(s^{j-2}\) is therefore \(b(1-k)(-1/4)^k\), which is nonzero for every \(k\ge2\), that is, for \(j\ge10\). Hence
\[
-\operatorname{ord}_Q(x_n)=n-1-\mathbf1_{\{n\equiv2\pmod4\}}.
\]
The exceptional \(j=6\) is deliberately excluded because the fixed function \(x_6=c\) has no pole.

**Pole at \(R\).** Here \(t\) has a simple pole and \(s\to-b/(2a)\). The highest \(t\)-coefficient in \(x_{2k+1}\) is \(a\) at degree \(k\), while the highest \(t\)-coefficient in \(x_{2k}\) is \(b+(k-1)as\) at degree \(k-1\). For odd indices at least seven the coefficient is nonzero. For even indices at least eight its value at \(s_0\) is \((3-k)b/2\ne0\). Thus
\[
-\operatorname{ord}_R(x_n)=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

**From poles to generic completions.** The degree of a nonconstant rational function on a smooth projective irreducible curve in characteristic zero equals the degree of its pole divisor. Applying this to \(x_n\) on the function field of \(F=0\) gives the claimed sum. A generic value of \(x_n\) has that many distinct preimages; they avoid the exceptional rank-one locus and the recurrence-chart boundary. Each preimage therefore corresponds to one and only one rank-two Hankel completion. This proves the stated degree for every \(n\ge7\).

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_hankel_sixth.py` checks the squarefree genus-two discriminant at an exact rational specialization, the characteristic-polynomial coefficient identities for every \(7\le n\le81\), and the resulting degree formula. It also matches all six degrees already tabulated in the motivating article: for \(10\le n\le15\) they are \(16,20,22,25,26,30\). These finite tests support, but do not replace, the preceding all-index pole-order proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Reed and Tsakiris (arXiv:2610.27554v1), Theorem 1, show that four distinct observed anti-diagonals suffice for generic finiteness in rank two. Their Theorem 5 gives the bound \(4\max\{4,n-6\}\) for the present observation pattern, and their Section 5.2, Remark 7, records the six individual degrees at \(n=10,11,12,13,14,15\). Those six values are prior work and are **not** claimed as new. The article's exact all-index Theorem 6 concerns three consecutive indices, a different observation pattern. The present claim is the unrestricted all-\(n\) formula for the sixth-gap pattern, with genus-two geometry and three polar branches. Earlier general determinantal-matroid and classical Prony constructions supply the background recurrence method, not an identified stronger statement about this particular field-extension degree. An unknown equivalent formulation in older sparse interpolation or algebraic elimination literature remains a historical-priority risk.

## Limitations
Only the complex, rank-at-most-two, four-observation family \(\{1,2,6,n\}\), with \(n\ge7\), is classified. Exceptional data, nonreduced completion fibers, monodromy, fields of positive characteristic, and other observation patterns are not addressed. The genus-two fiber is generic, not a claim about all fixed observations. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
