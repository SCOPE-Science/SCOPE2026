# Review of the sixth-gap Hankel projection degree

**Correctness — PASS.** The recurrence chart is birational on the generic Hankel rank-two locus. The generic three-observation curve is a smooth genus-two double cover; three nonaffine places are individually located. The pole orders are proved separately and sum to the claimed field-extension degree. The leading-coefficient cancellation is checked symbolically and algebraically, not inferred from finite samples.

**Originality — PASS, qualified historical priority.** Reed–Tsakiris (arXiv:2610.27554v1) explicitly tabulate six of these degrees (Section 5.2, Remark 7) and give a nonsharp general upper bound (Theorem 5); those numerical cases are not new. Their all-index exact formula (Theorem 6) has three consecutive observed indices, which is not this pattern. A search of mathematical records and recoverable older research claims found related patterns with third observations \(4\) and \(5\), and a distinct sparse pattern \(\{1,3,5,n\}\), but not the present general degree law for third observation \(6\). Possible unpublished or differently named exact Prony elimination formulas remain an originality risk.

**Value — PASS.** Four-coordinate projection degree is the natural finite-completion invariant for the first unresolved low-rank Hankel observation patterns. This computes an infinite genus-two family rather than an arbitrary finite sample; it explains why the observed degree sequence changes periodically and improves the primary general upper bound for large \(n\).

**Limitations.** Characteristic zero, generic data, one observation pattern, and reduced underlying variety only. The computation does not classify exceptional fibers, monodromy or positive-characteristic behavior.
