# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For any symmetric finite initial relationship matrix in the stated cube with fixed unit diagonal and opinion diameter below the confidence radius, the network equation reduces exactly to the polynomial FOAF system. The initial support components are invariant, the weight cube is forward invariant, and the system is analytic. The proof establishes vanishing of lower Taylor coefficients and calculates the first nonzero coefficient by the weighted-geodesic splitting identity: every shortest path has exactly one split at each of its \(d-1\) internal vertices. Monotonicity and logistic comparison show immediate positive-time componentwise closure and eventual saturation; inversion gives the unique small-threshold crossing asymptotic. The packaged `verify.py` reproduces 4,832 exact rational coefficient and zero tests over independent finite examples. Finite checks are corroboration, not a replacement for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge)

The closest direct source, Nugent--Gomes--Wolfram (2023, arXiv:2305.09483), specifies the FOAF edge equation and recognizes repeated triadic closure, but its analytical statements and network simulations do not supply the first nonzero Taylor order, the weighted-geodesic constant, or the corresponding threshold law for arbitrary initial weighted graphs. Di Giovacchino--Higham--Zygalakis (2021, arXiv:2111.05715) treats stochastic binary edges and edge-density bistability rather than the same continuous weighted network equation. Classical matrix-power shortest-walk counting is acknowledged, not claimed as new. Keyword and semantic searches for FOAF, weighted geodesic coefficients, and small-time triadic closure found no dominating result; absence from searches is not a proof of priority. The full journal revision of the initiating paper could not be inspected, and an unindexed equivalent remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The result resolves a structurally motivated question left open by the model's focus on qualitative densification: which initially missing ties arise at what order in time, and how does initial topology set the scale of weak links? The complete componentwise closure classification, sharp distance-dependent vanishing order, multiplicity-sensitive coefficient, and threshold asymptotic together quantify a natural network hierarchy in arbitrary finite weighted graphs. The formula distinguishes a chain of three ties from two parallel short routes without arbitrarily restricting to one small instance. It gives a precise interpretation of rapid formal densification versus slow effective trust formation.

## Boundaries and residual risks

The all-interacting bounded-confidence hypothesis must not be omitted: distance-triggered deactivation changes the governing equation. Neither eventual opinion consensus rates nor moderate-threshold times are claimed. Numerical and rational finite tests are not exhaustive for unbounded graph order. Priority cannot be guaranteed against inaccessible journal revisions or other unindexed continuous-triadic-closure results.
