# Review of geodetic bases of triangular graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The shortest-path/edge-cover equivalence is two-sided. The odd-order component-count argument reduces every optimum to a triangle or a five-vertex path together with independent edges; it includes the boundary \(n=3\). The labelled enumerator counts disjoint structures with unique decompositions. Explicit three-slide transfers and the buffer argument establish connectivity for every odd order. Two independent finite programs check the definitions, counts and exchange graphs on the stated ranges; their outputs are recorded, but the all-order conclusions rely on the symbolic proof.

## Originality — PASS with priority risk
The strongest directly plausible earlier line-graph sources were examined in their actual mathematical sections. Goudar et al. (2012), Theorem 3.12, gives only \(g(L(K_n))\le(n+1)(n-2)/2\), which is weaker than the parity-sharp equality and implies neither the complete basis classification nor the mobility dichotomy. Ashalatha et al. (2013), Theorem 3.11, gives an upper bound for a distinct non-split parameter and does not cover this ordinary geodetic result. Chartrand et al. (2002) provides the general definition and background without this family classification. Searches for triangular graphs, their line-graph equivalence, matching/leaf formulations, complete geodetic polynomials, and geodetic-set reconfiguration found no explicit matching classification. Related published-finding corpus records concern complete multipartite geodetic polynomials and strong-edge variants, neither of which implies a theorem for the line graph of a complete graph. Unindexed or unavailable older work remains a residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The triangular graphs form a canonical distance-regular infinite family. Their full minimum geodetic-set configurations have a natural parity transition: even-order sets are frozen perfect matchings, whereas odd-order sets have two explicit structural types, computable multiplicities, exact transition degrees and connected slide motion. The local no-shared-leaf criterion and three-slide transposition give explanations beyond a scalar invariant, making the classification relevant to geodetic placement and reconfiguration.

## Limits
No assertion of independent verification, publication priority, or relocation diameter is made. The finite programs establish only their enumerated ranges. The graph-theoretic symbolic arguments extend the results to all \(n\ge3\).
