# Geometric fixed-point homotopy of every natural involution of circular-ladder independence complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(P_n=C_n\square K_2\) be the circular ladder with vertices \((i,a)\), where \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(X_n=\operatorname{Ind}(P_n)\). Its natural symmetry group \(D_n\times C_2\) is generated by \(r(i,a)=(i+1,a)\), \(s(i,a)=(-i,a)\), and layer exchange \(t(i,a)=(i,1-a)\).

**Theorem.** Every nonidentity involution in this group has a geometric fixed-point space that is empty or has the following **integral homotopy type**. Write \(s_b=r^b s\), and put \(k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Define \(L_m=P_m\square K_2\) for \(m\ge1\); its independence complex is \(S^{\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor}\).

When \(n=2k+1\) is odd, both \(|X_n|^{\langle s_b\rangle}\) and \(|X_n|^{\langle s_bt\rangle}\), for every \(b\), are homotopy equivalent to \(S^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\). In both cases their fixed-point simplicial models are exactly \(\operatorname{Ind}(L_k)\).

When \(n=2k\) is even, \(k\ge3\), the **four** reflection types have exact fixed-point simplicial models:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{involution}&\text{fixed-point model}&\text{integral homotopy type}\\\hline
s_{2b}&\operatorname{Ind}(L_{k+1})&S^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\\
s_{2b+1}&\operatorname{Ind}(L_{k-2})&S^{\lfloor(k-3)/2\rfloor}\\
s_{2b}t&\operatorname{Ind}(L_{k-1})&S^{\lfloor(k-2)/2\rfloor}\\
s_{2b+1}t&\operatorname{Ind}(L_k)&S^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}.
\end{array}
\]

For either parity, the fixed-point space of \(t\) is empty. When \(n=2k\), the remaining two involutions satisfy
\[
|X_{2k}|^{\langle r^k\rangle}\cong|\operatorname{Ind}(C_k\square K_2)|,
\qquad
|X_{2k}|^{\langle r^kt\rangle}\cong|\operatorname{Ind}(M_{2k})|,
\]
where \(M_{2k}\) is the Möbius ladder with \(k\) rungs. The *integral homotopy types* on the right are completely specified by the functions
\[
\mathsf C(4q)=\bigvee^3 S^{2q-1},\quad
\mathsf C(4q+1)=S^{2q-1},\quad
\mathsf C(4q+2)=S^{2q},\quad
\mathsf C(4q+3)=S^{2q+1},
\]
\[
\mathsf M(4q)=S^{2q-1},\quad
\mathsf M(4q+1)=S^{2q},\quad
\mathsf M(4q+2)=\bigvee^3S^{2q},\quad
\mathsf M(4q+3)=S^{2q}.
\]
Here \(q\ge0\) and only inputs \(k\ge3\) are used, so expressions with negative sphere dimensions never occur. The functions \(\mathsf C\) and \(\mathsf M\) are the previously published ordinary homotopy classifications of circular and Möbius ladder independence complexes; their use here is credited, not claimed as new.

The new geometric fixed-point conclusions distinguish different involution-fixed *sphere dimensions* even when the previously known complete rational homology character gives the same trace. For example, when \(n=12\), vertex-axis and edge-axis reflections have fixed spaces \(S^3\) and \(S^1\), respectively, although the two reflections have the same rational homology trace on \(X_{12}\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple, all geometric fixed points mean fixed points of the induced simplicial action on geometric realization (not the realization of the vertexwise fixed subcomplex), and the homotopy equivalences are integral. For \(n=5\), the two reflection types both give \(S^0\). For even \(n=6\), the length \(k-2=1\) is allowed: \(L_1=K_2\). The acting group is the indicated natural \(D_n\times C_2\), not necessarily the entire graph automorphism group in exceptional small cases. No assertion is made about arbitrary higher-order subgroup-fixed spaces or a compatible system of equivariant equivalences.

## Proof
**1. Exact model for geometric fixed points.** For any finite graph \(G\) and involutive graph automorphism \(g\), partition \(V(G)\) into its \(g\)-orbits. Call an orbit *admissible* if no edge of \(G\) lies entirely inside it. Form a graph \(Q_g\) with one vertex for each admissible orbit and an edge between two distinct orbit-vertices if any original edge joins their orbits. Then
\[
|\operatorname{Ind}(G)|^{\langle g\rangle}\cong|\operatorname{Ind}(Q_g)|.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, barycentric coordinates of a fixed point are constant on each \(g\)-orbit. An inadmissible orbit must have zero coordinate, and the union of any positive orbits must be an independent set. Assigning to each orbit the total barycentric mass carried by it yields a simplexwise linear homeomorphism with inverse obtained by distributing that mass uniformly over orbit members. This proves (1), including the case with no admissible orbit, whose realization is empty.

**2. All reflection quotients.** It suffices to take \(s_b\) with \(b=0\) (vertex-axis type) or, if \(n\) is even, \(b=1\) (edge-axis type). For \(s_b\), a column orbit \(\{i,b-i\}\) yields one vertex orbit in each layer except that the paired column orbit crossing a reflection edge contains an original horizontal edge and is forbidden. Vertex-axis singleton columns remain admissible. The retained column orbits line up as a path. Thus, for odd \(n=2k+1\), the quotient is \(L_k\); for even \(n=2k\), a vertex-axis quotient is \(L_{k+1}\) and an edge-axis quotient is \(L_{k-2}\).

For \(s_bt\), a paired column \(\{i,b-i\}\) yields two vertex orbits \(\{(i,0),(b-i,1)\}\) and \(\{(i,1),(b-i,0)\}\). These are admissible even when the two columns are adjacent, because their members lie in opposite layers. Conversely, a singleton column has its two layer vertices in one orbit containing a rung edge and is discarded. Along a consecutively chosen representative half-cycle, two orbits in the same layer-coordinate are adjacent precisely across consecutive positions, while the two orbits over each position meet across the rung; reflection-fold edges add no other edges. Therefore the quotient graphs are \(L_k\) for odd \(n\), \(L_{k-1}\) for even vertex-axis reflections, and \(L_k\) for even edge-axis reflections. These are exact graph identifications, not merely matching face counts.

**3. The open-ladder suspension lemma.** For \(m\ge3\), label the two vertices of rung \(j\) by \(a_j,b_j\). Delete \(a_2\). In \(L_m\setminus N[a_2]\), the vertex \(b_1\) is isolated, so the link of \(a_2\) in \(\operatorname{Ind}(L_m)\) is contractible. By the deletion-link homotopy cofibre, \(\operatorname{Ind}(L_m)\simeq\operatorname{Ind}(L_m-a_2)\). In the latter graph \(N(a_1)=\{b_1\}\subseteq N(b_2)=\{b_1,b_3\}\); the standard open-neighborhood fold removes \(b_2\) without changing the independence-complex homotopy type. The remaining graph is the disjoint union \(K_2\sqcup L_{m-2}\). Since independence complexes of disjoint graph unions are joins, and \(\operatorname{Ind}(K_2)=S^0\),
\[
\operatorname{Ind}(L_m)\simeq S^0*\operatorname{Ind}(L_{m-2})\simeq\Sigma\operatorname{Ind}(L_{m-2}).
\tag{2}
\]
The initial cases \(\operatorname{Ind}(L_1)\cong S^0\) and \(\operatorname{Ind}(L_2)\simeq S^0\) prove, inductively for all \(m\ge1\), that \(\operatorname{Ind}(L_m)\simeq S^{\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor}\). This yields every reflection row.

**4. Remaining involutions.** The elements of order two in \(D_n\times C_2\) are exactly \(t\), all \(s_bt^e\) with \(e\in\{0,1\}\), and, for even \(n=2k\), \(r^k\) and \(r^kt\). Every orbit of \(t\) is a rung edge, so its fixed space is empty. For the half-turn \(r^k\), its paired columns are nonadjacent since \(k\ge3\); representative columns \(0,\dots,k-1\) give the ordinary circular ladder \(C_k\square K_2\). For \(r^kt\), representative half-turn orbits have opposite layers across the identification between columns \(k-1\) and \(0\); this is precisely the Möbius-twisted ladder \(M_{2k}\). Apply (1) and the established *ordinary* integral homotopy classifications of these two graph families (Agarwal--Basak, Theorems 12 and 15) to obtain \(\mathsf C(k)\) and \(\mathsf M(k)\). This exhausts all involutions. \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py`, requiring only the Python 3 standard library, independently reconstructs the original circular-ladder graph and the complete vertex orbits for every specified involution. It removes inadmissible orbits, computes all inter-orbit edges from the original graph, and compares the result edge by edge against independently constructed open, circular or Möbius ladder models. It also enumerates every independent face of each finite quotient and computes complete reduced mod-two Betti numbers by boundary matrix elimination. The checked ranges are \(5\le n\le18\) for exact quotient-graph equality and \(5\le n\le14\) for independent homology comparisons. Successful replay prints `ALL_CIRCULAR_INVOLUTION_CHECKS_OK: orbit_graphs=70, graph_edges=840, homology_cases=50, faces=6320`. These finite checks corroborate the complete formulas; (1), (2), the explicit quotient identifications and the cited ordinary theorems prove the unrestricted integral statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v2 (2026-09-14), Theorems 12 and 15, establish the ordinary homotopy classifications for Möbius and circular ladders that are used in Step 4. They do not give a geometric fixed-point homotopy classification for all involutions. Their first version (2026-08-31) treats Möbius ladders only; the circular-ladder theorem appears in the September 14 revision. The public mathematical record *Complete dihedral and layer-swap homology representations of circular ladders* (2026-10-11) determines rational homology characters of all natural symmetries and derives an open-chain signed face-count for plain reflections; it does not classify the geometric fixed-point homotopy spaces. In particular, a rational character determines only a reduced fixed-point Euler characteristic by Lefschetz, not the fixed space's sphere dimension or integral homotopy type. At \(n=12\), the two plain reflection types demonstrate this strict loss of information: their characters agree, but the fixed spaces are \(S^3\) and \(S^1\). Formula (1) is a general orbit-model observation; its generality is not presented as the new contribution. The contribution is the complete set of graph-specific fixed-space identifications and the integral involution table.

## Limitations
The natural group is the specified dihedral-by-layer-swap subgroup, and only its order-two elements are classified. The theorem does not supply the entire equivariant homotopy type, inclusion maps among arbitrary subgroup-fixed spaces, or homotopy types of fixed spaces of higher-order elements. It uses the previously published ordinary Möbius and circular ladder homotopy classifications without re-proving them. The novelty assessment is subject to possible stronger unindexed equivariant graph-complex literature.
