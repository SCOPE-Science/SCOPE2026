In symmetric two-species Ricker competition, cross-competition \(\alpha>1\) forces the initially larger species to survive and the other to become extinct, regardless of whether the survivor fluctuates periodically or irregularly. The losing population has the exact universal logarithmic decay exponent \(\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log(\text{loser}_n)=-(\alpha-1)R\), and the coupling threshold \(\alpha=1\) is sharp.
