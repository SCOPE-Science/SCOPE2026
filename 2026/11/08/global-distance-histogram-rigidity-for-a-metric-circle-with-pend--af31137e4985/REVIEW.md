# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Equation (2) is obtained from ordered arclength-pair counting and four independently defined sampling regimes (circle–circle, arm–same-arm, circle–arm, and distinct-arm). Its sole density jump identifies the loop half-circumference; its one-sided zero slope recovers the unknown arm count. Distributional differentiation yields equation (1) with exact multiplicity \(-k\). The induction in equation (3) is unconditional because every positive residual contribution involving an unrecovered arm lies strictly above its smallest remaining length. Repetitions are handled by integer atom multiplicity, not assumed away. Rational checks and simulated CDF comparisons supplement the analytic proof.

**Originality — PASS (provisional).** Searches of the scientific research-record index for global intrinsic metric-graph distance histograms, pendant-edge lollipop constructions, and cycle-plus-star reconstruction did not return a covering theorem. Relevant full-text comparisons separate richer point-cloud topology recovery (Lecci–Rinaldo–Wasserman), local profile and circle-rigidity results (Mémoli–Needham), and mean-distance computations (Garijo–Márquez–Silveira, Baptista–Kennedy–Mugnolo) from this global *one-dimensional* histogram rigidity. An earlier metric-star inverse argument does not handle the circle-induced jump or the shifted arm atoms. Residual risk: older unindexed geometric-network tomography, indirect implications of general reconstruction methods, or terminology differences; absence of a search hit is not proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** Distance histograms lose the labels and locations of individual point pairs and are not injective for general metric spaces. Here, a natural topologically nontrivial network family admits an exact and collision-robust inverse theorem. The loop discontinuity and shift-corrected atom peeling reveal an interpretable separation of cyclic and branching geometry; this is more informative than another formula for mean distance or a restricted generic case. The result does not claim noise-robustness, arbitrary graph reconstruction, or a general downstream consequence.

**Closest sources and material limitations.** Mémoli–Needham, arXiv:1810.09646, abstract, introduction, Section 4 definitions and Theorems 9–12 (especially global circle characterization, Theorem 11), deals with global versus local invariants but contains no identified reconstruction of the stated finite pendant-arm family from one global histogram. Garijo et al.'s open HTML full text was inspected for claims about continuous mean distance and cactus graphs. Lecci et al.'s accessible PDF introduction and metric graph reconstruction setup concern point clouds rather than a single global histogram. No independent historical-priority audit has been performed.
