# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The shortest-path formulas account for the four pole-routing possibilities for points on different branches and the complementary shortest pole path for points on the same branch. The exact tail formula separates six ordered-pair types, normalizes by total arclength squared, and proves the complete jump list by analyzing which polygon-area derivatives can be discontinuous. The only nontrivial boundary case is coincident half-cycle sums; all coefficients are strictly negative, so no cancellation occurs. Reconstruction from the number and locations of jumps plus the exact small-radius density includes all positive real parameters and equality configurations. Exact-rational verification and independent simulations supplement, but do not replace, the analytic argument.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The closest published qualitative prior is Mémoli–Needham, `arXiv:1810.09646v5`, Remark 4.6, which mentions piecewise-linear global shape densities and jumps associated with metric-graph loops. Its inverse results concern topological information and local metric-graph signatures, not the exact three-length theta reconstruction with all coefficients and tie handling. Bose et al., `arXiv:1304.1909v1`, analyze farthest-point network geometry and do not obtain a global two-point distance histogram; the related cactus query paper `arXiv:1411.1879v1` likewise has a different objective. Baptista et al., `arXiv:2312.04952v2`, treat mean distances rather than full distributions. Searches for theta/banana metric graphs, geodesic-distance histograms, jump singularities and inverse problems did not yield the quantified theorem. This negative search is not a uniqueness guarantee; independent historical-priority assessment is still needed.

## Value — PASS
The result is a complete inverse theorem for a natural, minimal non-cactus network: two cycles sharing a path. A full unlabeled global pair-distance histogram recovers the metric parameters even at nongeneric symmetry points. In contrast to cactus graphs, the longest-cycle discontinuity has a short-path-screening coefficient. This phenomenon gives a useful test case and obstacle for stronger metric-graph reconstruction questions, beyond merely computing a single mean or a restatement of a known cycle-count fact.

## Limitations
Exact distributions and strictly positive lengths are essential; there is no proof of finite-sample robustness or a general theorem for larger networks. Potential historical-priority risks remain from specialized metric-network distance-distribution studies not retrieved in full. No independent mathematical or historical audit has been performed.
