# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact projective ratio law follows directly by subtracting the shared per-capita growth term. A bipartite-edge bound supplies a positive logistic lower comparison for total density; monotonicity of the first species' share supplies a uniform positive lower bound for that species. Alternating induction then gives exponential even-species extinction and positive odd-species ratios. The total density converges by a forced logistic comparison; the positive extinction prefactor follows from absolute integrability of deviations of each surviving predecessor. The supplied standalone code passed 540 finite-time identity comparisons and 20 long-time assertions. Finite calculations are independent checks, not a replacement for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest primary source is Samanta et al., arXiv:2609.09027v1, whose mean-field equations and symmetric independent-set equilibrium restriction were inspected, along with its stability discussion and simulations. Those statements permit many non-adjacent supports and do not entail convergence from every strictly positive initial point to the odd-index support, nor sharp extinction rates. Searches for projective-ratio and global-convergence formulations in the relevant research index and web literature did not reveal an equivalent theorem. Earlier cyclic May–Leonard dynamics and the three-species balance-simplex framework have different interaction structures or weaker inspected conclusions. Unindexed equivalences or general competitive-system results not examined in full remain risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The motivating problem is selection of a realized biodiversity pattern, not merely enumeration of permissible equilibrium supports. The result replaces a broad family of admissible independent-set equilibria by a deterministic all-interior selection law, proves convergence rather than relying on local stability or plots, and supplies exact extinction exponents and prefactors useful for late-time scale comparisons in multi-level ecological chains. The result covers arbitrary fixed chain length and heterogeneous positive predation strengths without imposing symmetry on the latter.
