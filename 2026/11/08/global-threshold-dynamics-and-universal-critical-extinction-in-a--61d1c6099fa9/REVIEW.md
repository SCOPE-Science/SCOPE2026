# Mathematical review: Global threshold dynamics and universal critical extinction in a fear–refuge predator–prey flow

## Correctness — PASS
The exact scalar comparison bounds prey independently of predator abundance and then bounds predator growth by a logistic inequality. The prey comparison also gives the sharp limsup bound \(\limsup u\le U\). Monotone nullcline geometry proves existence and uniqueness of positive coexistence precisely when \(QU>m\), and the coexistence Jacobian has strictly negative trace and positive determinant. The exact Dulac divergence is strictly negative throughout the positive quadrant. The only two boundary saddles have stable manifolds confined to invariant axes; there is no connecting graph that attracts a positive orbit. Thus the planar omega-limit analysis gives global coexistence attraction, with hyperbolicity providing eventual exponential convergence. At and below the invasion threshold, comparison shows predator extinction and prey recovery to \(U\). At equality, the local forced stable equation gives \(x/y\to-B/A\) and the reciprocal predator derivative tends to \(C\), proving both universal critical amplitudes. Actual symbolic identities and finite integrations are recorded in VERIFICATION.md and verify.py; numerical or symbolic examples are not substituted for the analytic proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The primary literature explicitly left rigorous global attraction open. The result resolves all three invasion regimes for every positive state, without a technical cross-interaction bound that was imposed for boundedness in the source. More importantly, exact neutral invasion is nonhyperbolic, so ordinary local stable-eigenvalue calculations do not give either extinction rate. The explicit coupled constants \(C=\eta+QB/A\) and \(B/(AC)\) quantify feedback from fear, refuge, crowding and predator competition at this natural bifurcation boundary; the contrast with exponential decline on the subcritical side is mathematically meaningful.
