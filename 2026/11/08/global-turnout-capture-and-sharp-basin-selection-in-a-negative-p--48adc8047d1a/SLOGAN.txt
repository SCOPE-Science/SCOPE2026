In a two-candidate negative-partisanship model's subcritical bistable wedge, every nonzero electorate reaches full turnout after at most \(\lceil\log_2(1/s_0)\rceil\) rounds, where \(s_0\) is its initial turnout. A unique unstable two-cycle then separates convergence to balanced polling from **finite-time** capture by alternating unanimous support; the capture clock diverges logarithmically near this sharp threshold. At \(\lambda=2/3,\gamma=41/20\), the same 80:20 committed split converges to parity from 50% turnout but ultimately alternates unanimously from full turnout.
