# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness: PASS

The argument identifies the farthest vertex by minimizing the dot product with one signed basis vector; it is valid at interior points and, with the explicit convention, at ties. The exact \(\ell_1\)-norm update shows one-step entry into the interior. There the coordinate-gap inequality is strict at \(0<\lambda\le1/2\); the scalar two-cycle then follows from a linear recurrence, and no additional periodic orbits can coexist with this global convergence. Signed coordinate permutations imply exactly equal Lebesgue basin measures. Sharpness is supported by a strict winner-switch witness for every \(\lambda>1/2\). Finite rational tests support, but do not substitute for, the proof.

## Originality: PASS within checked evidence

The nearest primary source, Abdulaziz, arXiv:2407.02506v1 §5, contains the planar even-polygon two-cycle result; the two-dimensional orthoplex is therefore excluded. The all-dimensional \(d\ge3\) cross-polytope result has different vertex geometry from the regular-simplex result, and the farthest-orthogonal-projection problem uses a different update. Searches for cross-polytope, orthoplex, octahedron, farthest-vertex contraction and periodic attractors revealed no dominating published theorem in the checked sources. Search absence cannot establish absolute novelty; papers indexed under alternative terminology remain a risk.

## Mathematical value: PASS

The orthoplex is a canonical higher-dimensional regular polytope. Exact primitive-orbit count, global attraction, explicit coordinates, equal basin volumes and a sharp permanent-selection cutoff give a natural full classification rather than an arbitrary special point or routine numerical parameter gain.

## Limitations

Global classification is proved only for \(0<\lambda\le1/2\). The sharpness witness for larger \(\lambda\) says only that one-step trapping fails, not that global attraction fails. Basin volumes are normalized Lebesgue volumes; tie points can have convention-dependent labels. Publication priority beyond inspected primary literature remains open.
