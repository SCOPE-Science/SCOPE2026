# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(n\ge3\), the exact degree \(n-1\) makes the vertex-level protection threshold \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\). A selected vertex on one shore loses at most its paired index on the other shore, giving an exhaustive two-case alliance criterion. Exhaustive single-deletion analysis then classifies locally minimal alliances. For \((t+1,t+1)\) pairs, reducing to an alliance with disjoint shore indices requires exactly one deletion from each shore, which is possible at intersection size two and impossible for intersection size at least three. This proves global minimality and yields the cardinality polynomials. Original-neighborhood brute-force checks through eight matched-pair orders corroborate the full proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The distinction between deletion-local and inclusion-global minimality is mathematically substantive for nonhereditary defensive alliances. The theorem gives every labelled irredundant configuration in an infinite family of regular bipartite graphs, exact two-point cardinality spectra, both upper parameters, and an explicit intersection-two obstruction explaining how a locally minimal set can contain precisely two smaller alliances. This structural discrepancy is not implied by an extremal alliance number alone.
