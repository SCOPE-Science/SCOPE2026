# Graphwise coprimality densities along quadratic Beatty vectors

## Finding

Let \(s\ge 2\), let \(r_1,\ldots,r_{s-1}\) be **distinct rational primes**, and define positive integers
\[
a_0(n)=n,\qquad a_i(n)=\lfloor n\sqrt{r_i}\rfloor\quad(1\le i<s,\ n\ge1).
\]
For any finite simple graph \(G\) on vertex set \(\{0,\ldots,s-1\}\), let \(A_G(X)\) count \(1\le n\le X\) for which \(\gcd(a_i(n),a_j(n))=1\) for every edge \(\{i,j\}\) of \(G\). Then
\[
A_G(X)=C_G X+o_G(X),\qquad C_G=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}Q_G(1/p)>0,
\]
where
\[
Q_G(t)=\sum_{I\subseteq V(G)\,\mathrm{independent}}t^{|I|}(1-t)^{s-|I|}.
\]
Here an independent vertex set contains no edge of \(G\). The product converges absolutely. In particular, if \(G\) is complete on three vertices, with \(r_1=2\) and \(r_2=3\), then
\[
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac1X\#\{1\le n\le X:\gcd(n,\lfloor n\sqrt2\rfloor)=\gcd(n,\lfloor n\sqrt3\rfloor)=\gcd(\lfloor n\sqrt2\rfloor,\lfloor n\sqrt3\rfloor)=1\}
=\prod_p\left(1-\frac3{p^2}+\frac2{p^3}\right).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All graph parameters and radical primes are fixed as \(X\to\infty\). No independence of the values \(a_i(n)\) as random integers is presumed. A graph may have isolated vertices or no edges; the latter case gives \(C_G=1\). The notation \(o_G(X)\) allows dependence on the fixed radical primes as well as on the graph. This is a natural-density result, not a uniform theorem in a growing graph or varying radicals.

## Proof

**1. Simultaneous residue equidistribution.** The numbers \(1,\sqrt{r_1},\ldots,\sqrt{r_{s-1}}\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\). Indeed, the multiquadratic extension generated by the square roots of distinct primes admits independent sign changes; applying these automorphisms to a putative rational linear relation kills every coefficient. By the multidimensional Weyl equidistribution criterion, for every integer \(m\ge1\), every residue \(a\pmod m\), and every residue vector \((b_1,\ldots,b_{s-1})\pmod m\),
\[
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac1X\#\{n\le X:n\equiv a\pmod m,\ \lfloor n\sqrt{r_i}\rfloor\equiv b_i\pmod m\ (1\le i<s)\}=m^{-s}.
\]
To verify this directly, write \(n=a+mt\) and observe that the congruence \(\lfloor n\sqrt{r_i}\rfloor\equiv b_i\pmod m\) is the condition that the fractional part of \(\sqrt{r_i}t+a\sqrt{r_i}/m\) belongs to \([b_i/m,(b_i+1)/m)\). These boxes have volume \(m^{-(s-1)}\), and the progression \(n\equiv a\pmod m\) has density \(1/m\).

**2. Large-prime pair lemma.** Suppose \(\alpha>1\) is a quadratic irrational. Every positive integer \(h\) satisfies \(\|h\alpha\|\ge c_\alpha/h\) for some \(c_\alpha>0\), where \(\|\cdot\|\) is distance to the nearest integer. An elementary proof writes \(\alpha=\sqrt{u/v}\) in lowest terms with nonsquare rational \(u/v\): if \(k\) is the nearest integer to \(h\alpha\), the nonzero integer \(uh^2-vk^2\) gives \(|h\alpha-k|\ge1/(v(h\alpha+k))\gg_\alpha1/h\). For \(n=pk\), the conditions \(p\mid n\) and \(p\mid\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor\) imply \(\{\alpha k\}<1/p\). The preceding lower bound forces \(k\gg_\alpha p\), whereas \(k\le X/p\); hence \(p\ll_\alpha\sqrt X\). For fixed \(p\), the points \(\{\alpha k\}\), \(1\le k\le\lfloor X/p\rfloor\), have circular separation \(\gg_\alpha p/X\), since their differences obey \(\|(k-\ell)\alpha\|\gg_\alpha1/|k-\ell|\). Thus at most \(O_\alpha(X/p^2+1)\) such \(k\) lie in an interval of length \(1/p\).

Likewise let \(\alpha,\beta>1\) and suppose \(\rho=\beta/\alpha\) is quadratic irrational. If \(p\mid\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor\) and \(p\mid\lfloor\beta n\rfloor\), write \(\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor=pk\), \(\lfloor\beta n\rfloor=p\ell\). Both \(k,\ell\) are positive. Writing \(\alpha n=pk+\theta\) and \(\beta n=p\ell+\eta\), where \(0\le\theta,\eta<1\), yields
\[
\|\rho k\|\le(1+\rho)/p,\qquad 1\le k\le\alpha X/p.
\]
Bad approximability gives \(k\gg_\rho p\) whenever this can force a restriction (for small \(p\), enlarge constants), and therefore \(p\ll_{\alpha,\beta}\sqrt X\). For fixed \(p\), the circular spacing of \(\{\rho k\}\) gives at most \(O_{\alpha,\beta}(X/p^2+1)\) eligible values of \(k\). Each \(k\) comes from at most one integer \(n\): the interval \(pk\le\alpha n<pk+1\) has length \(1/\alpha<1\). Hence, summing over all primes \(p>B\) and noting that only \(p\ll\sqrt X\) matter,
\[
\#\{n\le X:\exists p>B\text{ prime dividing both prescribed coordinates}\}
\ll_{\alpha,\beta}X/B+\sqrt X.
\]
The same bound holds for a pair \((n,\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor)\). Every required slope is quadratic irrational: \(\alpha=\sqrt{r_i}\) for edges adjacent to vertex zero, and \(\rho=\sqrt{r_j/r_i}\) for edges between nonzero vertices.

**3. Fixed-prime sieve.** Fix \(B\ge2\), put \(M_B=\prod_{p\le B}p\), and require only that no edge has both endpoints divisible by any prime \(p\le B\). Step 1 implies that the full coordinate residue vector is uniformly distributed modulo \(M_B\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives the limiting probability
\[
C_G(B)=\prod_{p\le B}\sum_{I\subseteq V(G)\,\mathrm{independent}}p^{-|I|}(1-1/p)^{s-|I|}.
\]
For each \(p\), the subset \(I\) is exactly the set of coordinates divisible by \(p\). Thus the finite product is exact, not a heuristic.

**4. Remove the cutoff.** An index counted by the truncated sieve but not by \(A_G(X)\) has a prime divisor \(p>B\) shared by the endpoints of some edge. A union bound over the finitely many edges and Step 2 give
\[
0\le\#\{n\le X:\text{passes all primes }p\le B\}-A_G(X)\ll_G X/B+\sqrt X.
\]
Taking the upper and lower limits after division by \(X\), then letting \(B\to\infty\), proves the claimed natural density. For each edge the probability of shared divisibility at \(p\) is \(p^{-2}\), so \(1-Q_G(1/p)\le|E(G)|/p^2\). Also \(Q_G(1/p)\ge(1-1/p)^s>0\). Consequently the infinite product converges to a strictly positive number. In the complete three-vertex case, the admissible sets have size zero or one, giving \(Q_G(1/p)=1-3/p^2+2/p^3\).

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library-only `verify.py` enumerates the graph constraints using exact integers. In particular, \(\lfloor n\sqrt r\rfloor\) is evaluated as `math.isqrt(r*n*n)`, with no floating-point rounding. For \(X=100000\), the path on the three entries with radicals \(2,3\) has \(42810\) admissible indices; the complete graph has \(28608\); the complete four-vertex graph with radicals \(2,3,5\) has \(11494\). Euler products truncated to primes below \(10000\) are approximately \(0.428257912679\), \(0.286755872147\), and \(0.114890809814\), respectively. These finite tests confirm sample values only; the argument above establishes the unrestricted limit.

Run the exact checker with `python3 verify.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Holdridge (arXiv:2611.02223v1, November 1, 2026, primary MSC2020 11K06) establishes a \(1/\zeta(s)\) density for **one joint greatest common divisor** of integer parts; her theorem and the earlier work of Champagne do not impose pairwise or graphwise gcd conditions. Her introduction distinguishes the joint-gcd problem from other questions about gcds of integer parts. Bergelson and Richter (arXiv:1611.08044) study joint gcds for Hardy-field tuples, again not the prescribed edgewise conditions.

Arias de Reyna and Heyman (arXiv:1403.2769, 2014/2015) establish precisely the corresponding **graphwise Euler factor** for independently ranging integer tuples in boxes, and Fernández and Fernández (arXiv:1310.3802) establish the complete-graph factor for random integer tuples. Those local factors are therefore not claimed to be new. Their independent-coordinate box asymptotics do not count the values of the single-parameter deterministic curve \((n,\lfloor n\sqrt{r_1}\rfloor,\ldots)\). What is established here is the missing infinite-prime sieve along that thin curve, using badly approximable ratios. No stronger published deterministic graphwise theorem was located in the inspected sources; unlocated equivalent work remains a priority risk.

## Limitations

The square-root radicands must be distinct rational primes and fixed, and the graph must be fixed. This ensures both full torus equidistribution and quadratic irrationality of every pairwise slope ratio. The method does not treat arbitrary irrational slopes, rationally dependent slopes, nonlinear polynomials, or a growing number of vertices. The theorem supplies \(o(X)\), not an explicit power-saving error. The reported numeric decimals are finite-prime truncations, not rigorous enclosures of the full products. Historical priority remains subject to unindexed work and independent mathematical review.
