# A power-saving graphwise coprimality estimate for quadratic Beatty vectors

## Finding

Fix \(s\ge2\), distinct rational primes \(r_1,\ldots,r_{s-1}\), and a simple graph \(G\) on \(\{0,\ldots,s-1\}\). Put \(a_0(n)=n\) and \(a_i(n)=\lfloor n\sqrt{r_i}\rfloor\) for \(i>0\). Let \(A_G(X)\) count positive integers \(n\le X\) with \(\gcd(a_i(n),a_j(n))=1\) for every edge \(\{i,j\}\). Write \(e=|E(G)|\) and \(D=2^{s-1}\). If \(e\ge1\), then for \(X\ge2\),
\[
A_G(X)=C_GX+O_{G,r_1,\ldots,r_{s-1}}\big(X^{1-\delta}\big),\qquad
\delta=\frac{1}{8D e(s+1)},
\]
where
\[
C_G=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}Q_G(1/p),\qquad
Q_G(t)=\sum_{I\text{ independent in }G}t^{|I|}(1-t)^{s-|I|}.
\]
The product converges absolutely and is positive. For the edgeless graph \(A_G(X)=\lfloor X\rfloor\) and \(C_G=1\). For example, for \((n,\lfloor n\sqrt2\rfloor,\lfloor n\sqrt3\rfloor)\) the count with all three pairs coprime is
\[
X\prod_p(1-3/p^2+2/p^3)+O(X^{1-1/384}).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The graph and prime radicals are fixed. No independence assumption on the integer values \(a_i(n)\) is needed. The exponent is explicit but not optimized, and the error constant may depend on the fixed radicals and graph. The proof below takes \(X\) integral; passing to \(\lfloor X\rfloor\) changes the main term by \(O(1)\).

## Proof

**1. An all-divisor tail.** For any positive irrational \(\rho=\sqrt{u/v}\) with \(u,v\) positive integers, there is \(c_\rho>0\) with \(\|k\rho\|\ge c_\rho/k\) for positive integers \(k\), where \(\|\cdot\|\) is distance to the nearest integer. In fact, if \(l\) is the nearest integer, then \(|u k^2-vl^2|\ge1\) and \(k\rho+l\ll_\rho k\), giving the bound by rationalizing.

For a fixed edge and integer \(d\ge1\), simultaneous divisibility by \(d\) at its two endpoints implies a condition
\[
1\le k\ll X/d,\qquad \|k\rho\|\ll 1/d,
\tag{1}
\]
with at most one \(n\) per eligible \(k\). For an edge from vertex zero, set \(n=dk\); if \(\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor=d l\) then \(0\le \alpha k-l<1/d\), with \(\rho=\alpha=\sqrt{r_i}\). For an edge between positive vertices with slopes \(\alpha,\beta\), write \(\alpha n=dk+\theta\), \(\beta n=dl+\eta\), where \(0\le\theta,\eta<1\). Then \(\rho=\beta/\alpha\) and \(|\rho k-l|\le(1+\rho)/d\). For fixed \(k\), \(\alpha n\in[dk,dk+1)\) permits at most one integer \(n\) because \(\alpha>1\). Each ratio used in (1) is an irrational square root of a positive rational, by distinctness of the primes. The constants are allowed to depend on the slopes.

Combining the lower and upper bounds in (1) gives \(k\gg d\) and hence \(d\ll\sqrt X\) whenever a solution exists. Among \(k\) up to a constant times \(X/d\), the circular distances between distinct \(\{k\rho\}\) are \(\gg d/X\), by applying the same lower bound to their differences. Those satisfying (1) lie in the arc about zero of length \(O(1/d)\) (or the entire circle for bounded small \(d\)), so there are \(O(X/d^2+1)\) of them. Summing over **all integers** \(Y<d\ll\sqrt X\) and the finitely many edges gives
\[
\#\{n\le X:\text{some edge has endpoint gcd}>Y\}\ll X/Y+\sqrt X.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, if the gcd exceeds \(Y\), that gcd itself is an eligible divisor. This argument uses no primality of \(d\).

**2. Quantitative residue counts.** Let \(\alpha=(\sqrt{r_1},\ldots,\sqrt{r_{s-1}})\). The multiquadratic field generated by its coordinates has degree \(D\): the distinct primes have independent square classes, so all independent changes of signs are its automorphisms. Applying the sign changes also shows that \(1,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{s-1}\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\). For a nonzero integral vector \(h\) with \(\|h\|_\infty\le H\), let \(l\) be the nearest integer to \(h\cdot\alpha\). The nonzero algebraic integer \(h\cdot\alpha-l\) has nonzero integer norm. All its other \(D-1\) conjugates have size \(O(H)\) as \(|l|=O(H)\). Thus
\[
\|h\cdot\alpha\|\gg H^{-(D-1)}.
\tag{3}
\]

Set \(\gamma=1/(2D)\). A block of \(T\) points of the rotation by \(\alpha\) on \((\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^{s-1}\), with any initial translate, has normalized discrepancy \(O(T^{-\gamma})\) for axis-parallel boxes. To see this, the geometric sum bound and (3) bound each normalized exponential sum with \(0<\|h\|_\infty\le H\) by \(O(H^{D-1}/T)\). The Erdős–Turán–Koksma inequality for star discrepancy weights these terms by \(\prod_i\max(1,|h_i|)^{-1}\) and adds \(O(1/H)\). The sum of the weights is \(O((1+\log H)^{s-1})\); choosing \(H=\max(1,\lfloor T^{1/(2D)}\rfloor)\) proves the discrepancy bound, since the second term is \(O(T^{-(D+1)/(2D)}(1+\log T)^{s-1})\). An arbitrary box is a signed sum of at most \(2^{s-1}\) anchored boxes, with the same bound up to a constant.

Fix any modulus \(1\le M\le X\) and a residue vector \((b_0,\ldots,b_{s-1})\) modulo \(M\), represented by \(0\le b_i<M\). Along the progression \(n=b_0+Mt\), each condition \(a_i(n)\equiv b_i\pmod M\) for \(i>0\) is equivalent to \(\{t\alpha_i+b_0\alpha_i/M\}\in[b_i/M,(b_i+1)/M)\). There are \(T=X/M+O(1)\) positive \(n\le X\) in the progression, the box volume is \(M^{-(s-1)}\), and the discrepancy bound is independent of the translate and box. Hence uniformly in \(M\) and the residues,
\[
\#\{n\le X:a_i(n)\equiv b_i\pmod M\ (0\le i<s)\}
=X/M^s+O\big((X/M)^{1-\gamma}+1\big).
\tag{4}
\]

**3. Truncated Möbius sieve.** Write \(g_f(n)\) for the endpoint gcd of edge \(f\). For \(Y\ge1\), replace each indicator \(\mathbf1_{g_f(n)=1}=\sum_{d\mid g_f(n)}\mu(d)\) by \(\sum_{d\mid g_f(n),\ d\le Y}\mu(d)\) and multiply the truncated sums over the edges. The two products agree unless some \(g_f(n)>Y\). In all cases their difference is at most \(1+\prod_f\tau(g_f(n))\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\) for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), since \(g_f(n)\ll X\) and \(\tau(k)\ll_\lambda k^\lambda\) for every \(\lambda>0\). This last bound follows from the prime power factors \(a+1\) in \(\tau(k)\): they are bounded by \(p^{a\lambda}\) for all sufficiently large \(p\), and the finitely many smaller primes contribute bounded constants. By (2), replacing the indicator product in \(A_G(X)\) by the truncated product incurs error
\[
O_\varepsilon\big(X^\varepsilon(X/Y+\sqrt X)\big).
\tag{5}
\]

Expand the truncated product over tuples \((d_f)_{f\in E(G)}\) with each \(d_f\le Y\). Set \(M=\operatorname{lcm}_f d_f\le Y^e\) and \(L_i=\operatorname{lcm}_{f\ni i}d_f\) (empty lcm is one). Simultaneous divisibility is exactly \(L_i\mid a_i(n)\) for all \(i\). Among the \(M^s\) residue vectors there are \(\prod_i(M/L_i)\) satisfying these conditions. Applying (4) to each, for \(Y^e\le X\), gives the count
\[
X/\prod_i L_i+O(M^s X^{1-\gamma}).
\]
Summing over at most \(Y^e\) tuples, the truncated Möbius count is \(X S(Y)+O(X^{1-\gamma}Y^{e(s+1)})\), where
\[
S(Y)=\sum_{d_f\le Y\ \forall f}\frac{\prod_f\mu(d_f)}{\prod_i L_i}.
\tag{6}
\]

**4. Main sum and error.** In the absolute multiple series associated to (6), only squarefree \(d_f\) contribute. At each prime \(p\) a subset \(F\subseteq E(G)\) is chosen, namely the edges whose divisor is divisible by \(p\). The denominator contributes \(p^{|V(F)|}\), where \(V(F)\) is the set of incident vertices. Every nonempty \(F\) has at least two vertices, so the absolute series converges by bounding its local factors by \(1+(2^e-1)p^{-2}\). To bound its tail outside \(d_f\le Y\) for all edges, mark one edge with \(d_f>Y\) and bound that indicator by \((d_f/Y)^{1/2}\). The weighted absolute series with factor \(d_f^{1/2}\) also converges: its nonempty terms at \(p\) are at most \(p^{-3/2}\). Summing over the possible marked edges bounds the tail by \(O(Y^{-1/2})\).

The signed local sum is \(\sum_{F\subseteq E(G)}(-1)^{|F|}p^{-|V(F)|}\), which equals \(Q_G(1/p)\) by finite inclusion-exclusion: in an independent Bernoulli selection of vertices with inclusion probability \(1/p\), subtract the events that both endpoints of a prescribed edge are selected. Thus the absolute convergence just proved identifies \(\lim_{Y\to\infty}S(Y)=C_G\). Each factor is at least \((1-1/p)^s>0\) and differs from one by at most \(e/p^2\), giving positivity. For \(K_3\), the independent sets have sizes zero or one, so the factor is \(1-3/p^2+2/p^3\).

To finish, let \(\theta=\gamma/(2e(s+1))\), take \(Y=\lfloor X^\theta\rfloor\) and set \(\varepsilon=\theta/4\). Then \(Y^e\le X\), the residue-counting error is \(O(X^{1-\gamma/2})\), and replacing \(S(Y)\) by \(C_G\) costs \(O(X^{1-\theta/2})\). The two terms in (5) are \(O(X^{1-3\theta/4}+X^{1/2+\theta/4})\), both \(O(X^{1-\theta/2})\) because \(\theta\le 2/3\). Since \(\gamma/2\ge\theta/2\), this gives the stated power saving with \(\delta=\theta/2\).

## Verification

The uniform bound is proved analytically. The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact integer square roots and gcds to check some sample counts against finite-prime products. The finite computation neither supplies nor tests the claimed error exponent. To replay the finite checks, use `python3 verify.py` with the standard library.

## Relationship to prior work and limitations

Champagne's Lemma 4.3 [1] controls the upper density of common large prime divisors of two Beatty sequences with linearly independent slopes, including either type of edge here. For a fixed graph, taking a union over the edges and adjoining ordinary fixed-modulus equidistribution and the Chinese remainder theorem already gives the qualitative graphwise density theorem. The proof of that lemma also yields an effective large-prime tail on these slopes via bad approximability; that does not estimate the full graph count with a power error. The local product coincides with the one obtained for independently ranging integer tuples in boxes by Arias de Reyna and Heyman [2]. For a single edge from vertex zero, Banks and Shparlinski [4, Theorem 1.1] already give a stronger error, \(O(X^{4/5+o(1)})\) for a quadratic irrational (type one). Their result with multiple polynomials concerns one joint gcd and strictly increasing degrees, not a graph of prescribed pairwise constraints among several linear floor coordinates.

The contribution here is an explicit power-saving error for arbitrary fixed edge constraints along the thin quadratic sequence: the proof uses spacing for all common divisors and a truncated edgewise Möbius sum with polynomially bounded moduli instead of removing a prime cutoff after the limit. The discrepancy ingredient is the classical Erdős–Turán–Koksma inequality, used here with the multiquadratic norm bound (3). This argument is limited to fixed graphs and fixed radicals and does not claim optimality or uniformity in either.
