# Grassmann distance degree of balanced rational-normal-scroll ruling curves

## Finding

For every pair of integers \(n\geq 2\) and \(d\geq 2\), let \(\mathcal R_{n,d}\subset\operatorname{Gr}(d,d(n+1))\) be the curve of ruling \((d-1)\)-planes of the balanced rational normal scroll \(S(n,\ldots,n)\), with \(d\) equal blocks. In the projection-matrix (chordal-distance) model of the Grassmannian, its Grassmann distance degree is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{GDdeg}(\mathcal R_{n,d})=4n-2.}
\]

Consequently, this Grassmann distance degree equals the Euclidean distance degree established for the same curve in Friedman--Hoşten--Rosana, Theorem 2.2. The equality is uniform in the number \(d\) of ruling blocks; it does **not** assert equality of degrees for arbitrary Grassmannian subvarieties or for intrinsic geodesic distance.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\), with its standard **bilinear**, rather than Hermitian, quadratic form on the algebraic parameter spaces. Put \(V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb C^{n+1}\), and write
\[
 v(t)=(1,t,\ldots,t^n),\qquad s(t)=v(t)v(t)^{\mathsf T}=\sum_{j=0}^n t^{2j}.
\]
For finite \(t\), the corresponding \(d\)-plane is spanned by copies of \(v(t)\), each in its own coordinate block. The same construction extends at \(t=\infty\), and the map \(\mathbb P^1\to\operatorname{Gr}(d,d(n+1))\) is an embedding because projection to any single block recovers the degree-\(n\) Veronese parameter.

For \(s(t)\ne0\), the orthogonal projector onto this \(d\)-plane is
\[
 P(t)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{v(t)^{\mathsf T}v(t)}{s(t)},\ldots,\frac{v(t)^{\mathsf T}v(t)}{s(t)}\right).
\]
The algebraic Grassmann distance degree counts nondegenerate complex critical points of \(\operatorname{tr}(P(t)Q)\) for generic rank-\(d\) orthogonal-projector data \(Q\), excluding the isotropic projection-chart boundary and the algebraic cut locus. This is the definition used by the cited papers. No assertion is made about the number of **real** critical points.

## Proof

**1. A genuine Grassmannian-data family.** For each \(i\), choose a vector \(u_i\in\mathbb C^{n+1}\) with \(u_i^{\mathsf T}u_i\ne0\), and let \(Q\) be the orthogonal projection onto the direct sum of the \(d\) lines generated by the \(u_i\) in their respective blocks. Such a \(Q\) is a point of \(\mathrm{pGr}(d,d(n+1))\), not an arbitrary ambient symmetric matrix. If \(p_i(t)=u_i^{\mathsf T}v(t)\) and \(q_i=u_i^{\mathsf T}u_i\), then
\[
 F_Q(t):=\operatorname{tr}(P(t)Q)=\frac{f(t)}{s(t)},\qquad f(t)=\sum_{i=1}^d\frac{p_i(t)^2}{q_i}.
\]
All coefficients in this formula are algebraic functions of the chosen nonisotropic projective directions.

**2. Dominance after disregarding additive constants.** Let \(W=\mathbb C[t]_{\leq 2n}\) and \(\overline W=W/\mathbb C s\). Addition of a multiple of \(s\) to \(f\) changes \(F_Q\) only by a constant and leaves its critical scheme unchanged. We show that the map from the above block-diagonal data family to \(\overline W\) is dominant.

Freeze the final \(d-2\) blocks, and consider the first two vectors at \(p_1(t)=1\) and \(p_2(t)=2+t^n\). Their squared norms are \(1\) and \(5\). A basis of tangent variations for the first normalized line consists of \(\delta p_1=t^j\) for \(1\leq j\leq n\). For the second it consists of \(\delta p_2=t^j\) for \(1\leq j<n\), together with \(\delta p_2=1-2t^n\): all satisfy the norm-preserving tangent condition \(2\delta c_0+\delta c_n=0\). The corresponding numerator variations are
\[
 \delta f=2\delta p_1+\frac{2}{5}(2+t^n)\delta p_2.
\]
Reduce these \(2n\) polynomials modulo \(s\) to the ordered basis \(1,t,\ldots,t^{2n-1}\) of \(\overline W\). Their determinant, with the tangent vectors ordered as just stated, is
\[
 -\frac{4^{n+1}}{5^n}\ne0.
\]
Indeed the \(n\) first columns give the coefficients of \(t,\ldots,t^n\), the next \(n-1\) give successively those of \(t^{n+1},\ldots,t^{2n-1}\), and the last column has nonzero constant coefficient \(8/5\); the displayed product follows by triangular elimination (including the ordering sign). Equivalently, the tangent image in \(W\) is exactly the hyperplane where the constant and leading coefficients sum to zero, whereas \(s\) does not lie in that hyperplane. Thus the differential to \(\overline W\) has full rank \(2n\), proving dominance in characteristic zero.

**3. Generic simple critical points.** The polynomial \(s(t)=(t^{2n+2}-1)/(t^2-1)\) has degree \(2n\) and simple complex roots. For a generic class \([f]\in\overline W\), the rational map \(f/s:\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1\) has degree \(2n\), has only simple poles, and has simple ramification away from its poles and from infinity. More explicitly, its critical polynomial is
\[
 H_f(t)=f'(t)s(t)-f(t)s'(t).
\]
After homogenization this is a section of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(4n-2)\), depending linearly on \([f]\). The resulting linear system is base-point-free: away from zeros of \(s\) one can vary the first derivative of \(f/s\); at a zero of \(s\), the value is \(-f s'\), which can be made nonzero; and at infinity the coefficient of the local linear term of \(f/s\) can be varied. Bertini's theorem on the smooth curve \(\mathbb P^1\) therefore makes the generic zero divisor reduced. Genericity also keeps it away from the simple poles and infinity. Its degree is \(4n-2\). By the dominance in step 2, the same statements hold for a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the genuine Grassmannian-data family.

**4. Exclusion of the algebraic cut locus.** For block-diagonal data as in step 1, the determinant of the matrix of pairings of the ruling rows with a basis of the data plane is, up to a nonzero factor, \(\prod_i p_i(t)\). Thus the cut-locus boundary on the ruling curve is supported at zeros of the \(p_i\). For general data each \(p_i\) has simple zeros, avoids zeros of \(s\), and the sets of zeros for different \(i\) are disjoint. At a zero of \(p_i\), the derivative of \(p_i^2/(q_i s)\) vanishes, but the sum of derivatives of the other terms is generically nonzero: for \(d\geq2\), one can vary a different \(p_j\) to make that derivative nonzero at any fixed such zero. The incidence of a zero of \(p_i\) and a zero of \(H_f\) therefore imposes two independent conditions before allowing the one-dimensional parameter \(t\) to vary. Its image is a proper closed subset of data. Hence the open sets of step 3 can also be chosen to avoid critical points on the cut locus.

**5. Passage to generic data.** Nondegeneracy of all \(4n-2\) critical points and their exclusion from the chart boundary and cut locus are Zariski-open conditions on \(Q\). Step 4 produces an actual rank-\(d\) projector satisfying them. Since \(\mathrm{pGr}(d,d(n+1))\) is irreducible, they hold for generic projector data \(Q\). Thus \(\operatorname{GDdeg}(\mathcal R_{n,d})=4n-2\). Independently, the already published ED formula of Friedman--Hoşten--Rosana gives the same value, because the least common multiple of the identical denominators is \(s\), of degree \(2n\). This completes the proof.

Run `python3 CHECK.py` with Python 3. The standalone checker verifies exact squarefreeness of the critical polynomial, pole avoidance, cut-locus avoidance, and its prescribed degree for \(n=2,\ldots,8\) and \(d=2,3,4\), using a fixed prime and deterministic rational witness data. It also verifies the nonzero Jacobian determinant formula for these seven values of \(n\). The modular calculation is a **finite corroboration**, not the proof for arbitrary \(n,d\); the proof is steps 1--5 above.

## Relationship to prior work

Friedman--Hoşten--Rosana, *Euclidean Distance Optimization Within the Grassmannian*, arXiv:2610.02018 (first public version 2026-10-01), Theorem 2.2, computes **ED** degrees of ruling curves of general rational normal scrolls. Its Theorem 4.4 discusses when **GD** and ED degrees are expected to agree for low-dimensional models, but does not give the balanced ruling-curve GD calculation or the dominance argument above. Its formulas for products of Grassmannians, matroid varieties and Schubert varieties do not specialize to the present nonconstant one-parameter family. The earlier paper of Friedman--Rosana--Sturmfels, arXiv:2601.22843, develops GD degrees for line Grassmannians and general complete intersections, not the displayed balanced-scroll calculation. The present claim is specifically the constrained-data GD equality; the ED value is acknowledged prior work.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to **balanced** scrolls \(S(n,\ldots,n)\) with \(n,d\ge2\), the algebraic chordal metric, and generic complex data. It does not determine all GD degrees for unbalanced scrolls, exceptional data, or the intrinsic geodesic metric. A literature search is not a proof of historical uniqueness, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
