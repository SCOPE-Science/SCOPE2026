# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact endpoint-vacancy recursion \(h_k'(t)=-e^{-t}h_{k-1}(t)\) gives \(u_k(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-v)^j/j!\), hence the exact one-edge availability probabilities and critical mean \(e^{-2-x}\). A clock's acceptance is influenced only along strictly time-decreasing nearest-neighbor paths: the finite-radius approximation error is at most \(2/R!\). Taking \(R=\lceil3\log n\rceil\), disjoint radius-\(R\) windows are independent, while the contribution of dependent \(r\)-tuples is at most \(O_r(R/n)\). All factorial moments converge to the Poisson moments. The absence of available pairs is equivalent to saturation. Doob's compensated-Poisson maximal inequality transfers the limit from continuous time to number of trials. Direct rational enumeration independently checked the finite formulas through eight sites; it does not establish the infinite limit.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk
Gerin's full-text arXiv:1411.8002 studies this identical dimer parking process and proves the first-order number-of-attempts law, not the order-\(n\) Gumbel correction. The full text of Shneer and van de Ven's *Per-site occupancy in the discrete parking problem* (doi:10.1016/j.spl.2016.09.019) computes fixed-site final occupancy and jamming density, not the distribution of the last successful attempt. Earlier Page vacancy work and related papers on means and thermodynamic jamming do not, on accessible evidence, state this extreme-value limit. Exact historic priority cannot be proved by search; Page's historical complete text and some older mean-attempt literature were inaccessible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The established \(n\log n\) number-of-attempts law leaves the complete order-\(n\) terminal fluctuations unresolved. This proof identifies a natural Poisson rare-gap mechanism, the explicit non-arbitrary shift of two, and the standard Gumbel distribution for both time and trial count. The result is informative for the jamming latency of a canonical random sequential adsorption process and requires a nontrivial dependency-localization argument, not merely the earlier first-moment formula.
