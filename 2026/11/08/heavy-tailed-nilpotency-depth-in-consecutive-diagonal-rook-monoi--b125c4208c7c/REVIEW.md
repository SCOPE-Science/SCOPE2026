# Mathematical review

## Correctness: PASS
The domain is the nonzero nilpotents of the consecutive-diagonal block inverse monoid, not the full rook monoid. For each signed nonzero offset, counting consecutive interval placements gives the exact cumulative distribution; difference of tails gives every integer depth. Uniform integral comparison proves the limiting mass function. An exact divisor-pair rearrangement and the elementary Dirichlet divisor summatory estimate prove the sharp mean constant. A Riemann-sum calculation plus a uniform summable tail bound proves the second moment, and a finite-offset calculation gives the macroscopic tail. The direct-map verifier checks every depth through dimension twelve and the divisor-weighted mean. Finite checks are not proofs of the limiting statements.

## Originality: PASS relative to inspected evidence
Fikioris and Fikioris, *A subsemigroup of the rook monoid* (arXiv:2111.01863), already established the monoid, individual indices, total order, and nilpotent count. The follow-up *An extension to “A subsemigroup of the rook monoid”* (arXiv:2302.09496) establishes the analogous index formula for a real-parameter enlargement. These are strong starting results, and their elementwise equations permit finite counts to be computed by summation; the elementary restatement of those equations is **not** credited as new. The separately established asymptotic harmonic law, sharp divisor-driven mean constant, linear second-moment coefficient, and linear-scale rare tail were not located in the inspected texts. Elliott, Levine, and Mitchell (arXiv:2303.12387) count monogenic (inverse) subsemigroups up to isomorphism, a different statistic. No global priority guarantee follows from searches.

## Mathematical value: PASS
Nilpotency index is a natural power invariant in an explicitly studied inverse monoid. The limit law assigns mass \(1/[j(j-1)]\) at index \(j\), so its mean is infinite even though each finite monoid has finite depth. The sharp \(\log n+2\gamma-5/6\) expectation and linear variance identify genuinely different scales; the macroscopic tail quantifies rare elements that control second moments. These are structural distributional distinctions, not an arbitrary small parameter table. The exact cumulative count allows all finite spectra to be reconstructed.
