# Hexagon two-cell model for scale-two Rips complexes of line graphs of high-girth graphs

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite connected simple graph with at least one edge and girth at least six (trees have infinite girth). Write \(L(G)\) for its line graph, equipped with unit-edge shortest-path distance, and write \(h(G)\) for the number of unoriented simple six-cycles of \(G\), with no restriction on how those cycles overlap. For every six-cycle \(C\), attach a two-disk to the one-dimensional CW graph \(G\) along the cycle's edge loop; denote the resulting finite two-dimensional CW complex by \(X(G)\). The main conclusion is
\[
\operatorname{VR}_{\le 2}(L(G))\simeq X(G)\vee\bigvee^{h(G)}S^2.
\]
This is an *ordinary integral homotopy equivalence* for every such \(G\); overlapping six-cycles, including shared edges and paths, are allowed. Here \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}\) uses the full set of edge-subsets of \(G\) whose corresponding line-graph vertices are pairwise at distance at most two. No metric on interior points of the geometric graph is used.

Let \(b=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1\), and let \(r\) be the rational (equivalently integral free) rank of the subgroup of \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\) generated by oriented six-cycle classes. Then
\[
H_1(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(L(G));\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(G;\mathbb Z)/\langle[C]:C\text{ a six-cycle}\rangle,
\]
\[
H_2(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(L(G));\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{2h(G)-r},\qquad
\widetilde H_i=0\quad(i\ge3).
\]
The scale-one inclusion induces a surjection in integral first homology with exactly the displayed six-cycle subgroup as kernel. Torsion in the degree-one quotient is not excluded by the assertion.

For example, the Heawood graph has \(b=8\), \(h=28\), and rank \(r=8\) for its hexagon lattice, so the scale-two line-graph Rips complex has integral second homology \(\mathbb Z^{48}\) and vanishing rational first homology. The theorem makes the stronger integral statement through the indicated quotient, without assuming that its quotient is torsion free.

## Assumptions and scope
A six-cycle is a sequence of six distinct source-graph vertices and six consecutive edges, counted up to rotation and reversal. An edge of \(G\) is a vertex of \(L(G)\); adjacency in \(L(G)\) means the source edges share a graph vertex. For \(v\in V(G)\), write \(S_v\) for the set of source edges incident to \(v\), and \(\Delta(S)\) for the full simplex on vertex set \(S\). All statements are homotopy statements about finite abstract simplicial complexes or their geometric realizations. The girth bound forbids cycles of lengths three, four, and five; it does not prohibit six-cycles sharing edges.

## Proof
**Step 1 (the neighborhood core).** Put
\[
Y=\bigcup_{v\in V(G)}\Delta(S_v)=\operatorname{Cl}(L(G)),\qquad
B=\bigcup_{uv\in E(G)}\Delta(S_u\cup S_v).
\]
The equality for \(Y\) follows because three pairwise incident edges lacking a common endpoint form a triangle of \(G\), which is forbidden. Every face of \(B\) is a scale-two Rips face: for \(uv\) as above, every edge incident to \(u\) is at line-graph distance at most two from every edge incident to \(v\), through the edge \(uv\). Thus \(Y\subseteq B\subseteq K\), where \(K=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(L(G))\).

If \(\sigma\in B\setminus Y\), it contains two disjoint edges \(a,b\) of \(G\). There is exactly one source edge \(c(\sigma)=uv\) such that \(\sigma\subset S_u\cup S_v\): two different connectors between the disjoint edges \(a,b\) would give a triangle or four-cycle. The unique connector is not one of \(a,b\), and adding or removing it preserves membership in \(B\setminus Y\). Pair each lower face \(\sigma\in B\setminus Y\) omitting \(c(\sigma)\) with \(\sigma\cup\{c(\sigma)\}\). Remove pairs in decreasing order of lower-face cardinality. Any other remaining coface of a lower face has the same connector (it contains \(a,b\)); its own matching partner has greater lower-face cardinality and is removed earlier. Consequently every pair is a genuine elementary free-face collapse, and
\[
B\searrow Y.
\]
The cover of \(Y\) by the star simplices \(\Delta(S_v)\) has single-vertex intersections for adjacent source vertices and otherwise empty intersections; triple intersections are empty. Its nerve is exactly \(G\), so \(Y\simeq G\), with each cyclic sequence of source edges mapped to its corresponding source-graph loop.

**Step 2 (classify every face outside the core).** Two disjoint source edges at line-graph distance two have a unique connecting source edge, again by absence of triangles and four-cycles. Consider a Rips triangle of source edges \(\{a,b,c\}\) that is not a face of \(B\). It has a disjoint pair, say \(a,b\), with connector \(d\). If \(c\) meets one of \(a,b\), either all three edges lie in \(S_u\cup S_v\) for the endpoints of \(d=uv\), or its required distance-two connection to the other edge creates a simple cycle of at most five source edges. For clarity, label \(a=A B\), \(d=B C\), \(b=C D\). If \(c=A X\) and \(c\) lies at line distance at most two from \(b\), the required extra connecting edge from \(A\) or \(X\) to \(C\) or \(D\) closes a triangle, quadrilateral, or pentagon with the path \(A-B-C-D\); if \(c=B X\), the triple is centered at \(d\). The cases at \(b\) are symmetric. Hence \(c\) is disjoint from both \(a,b\).

Now the three pairwise disjoint edges \(a,b,c\) have three unique pairwise connectors. Contract the three selected edges: the connectors form a triangle on the three contracted vertices. If two connectors meet the same endpoint of a selected edge, their union with the other connectors and the necessary portions of the remaining selected edges produces a cycle of length at most five. Hence the two connectors at each selected edge meet *opposite* endpoints. Their union is a simple source six-cycle, and \(a,b,c\) are its alternate edges. Conversely each six-cycle provides exactly two such noncentered Rips triangles. Uniqueness of pairwise connectors makes their six-cycle representation unique.

**Step 3 (maximality without any edge-disjointness hypothesis).** Let \(\{a,b,c\}\) be an alternating triple of source edges from a six-cycle. Suppose a fourth source edge \(f\) lies at line distance at most two from all three. If \(f\) meets one alternate edge, the absence of triangles, quadrilaterals, and pentagons prevents it from meeting another alternate edge, and its distance-two connection to the opposite alternate edge would close a cycle of length at most five along the six-cycle. If \(f\) meets none, choose for each of \(a,b,c\) a connector joining \(f\) to that edge (which exists by the distance-two condition). The two endpoints of \(f\) must carry three connectors, so two connectors use the same endpoint. Their other endpoints lie on distinct alternate edges of the six-cycle; these two endpoints are joined by a path of length at most three within that six-cycle. Together with the two connectors this produces a cycle of length at most five, contrary to girth. Thus no fourth vertex is compatible with an exceptional triangle. Every face in \(K\setminus B\) is exactly one of the \(2h(G)\) alternating two-simplices, and no higher-dimensional face outside \(B\) occurs. This argument does not require different six-cycles to be edge-disjoint.

**Step 4 (attaching maps and sphere splitting).** The three edges on the boundary of any exceptional two-simplex are distance-two pairs of source edges. Their unique connecting source edges define paths of two edges in \(L(G)\), lying in \(B\). Under \(B\searrow Y\), each boundary edge is homotopic relative its endpoints to this length-two path, because it bounds a centered triangle with the connector. The attaching loop is therefore the six-edge loop around the corresponding hexagon in \(L(G)\), which under \(Y\simeq G\) is the original six-cycle in \(G\). The two alternate triangles from a given hexagon have the same attaching homotopy class, possibly with reversed orientation. Attach the first disk for each hexagon to get \(X(G)\), even when the loops overlap. Once a first disk is attached, the second disk's attaching loop is nullhomotopic, so it contributes one wedged \(S^2\). Homotopy invariance of CW attachments gives the claimed homotopy type.

**Step 5 (integral homology and persistence).** The cellular chain group of \(X(G)\) in dimension two is free on \(h(G)\) hexagon disks, and its boundary map to the original graph's integral cycle lattice has image the hexagon-generated subgroup of rank \(r\). Its kernel is a free abelian group of rank \(h(G)-r\). Adding the \(h(G)\) sphere summands yields \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{2h(G)-r}\); the degree-one group is the graph cycle lattice modulo the hexagon subgroup, and there are no higher reduced homology groups. Finally \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le1}(L(G))=Y\) and \(Y\hookrightarrow B\hookrightarrow K\) induces the stated surjective first-homology map. \(\square\)

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_line_hex.py` from this directory. The standard-library verifier constructs both \(K\) and \(B\) independently, enumerates every source six-cycle, checks the exact identity of the exceptional simplices with the two alternate-edge triangles, executes all elementary collapses \(B\searrow Y\) while independently checking each free face, and compares mod-two Betti numbers with the cell-model predictions. It tests cycles of lengths six through eight, overlapping-theta families, subdivisions of complete graphs on four, five and six vertices (with extensive shared hexagon edges), and the Heawood graph. The embedded output ends in `LINE_GRAPH_RIPS2_HEXAGON_CW_CHECKS_OK`. The tests are finite diagnostics, not a proof for arbitrary graph order. The unrestricted proof consists of Steps 1–5.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita's 2025 work studies closed-neighborhood complexes and their relation to other constructions; it supplies the natural neighborhood-core perspective, but not the above classification of the **larger** Rips complex. Goyal–Shukla–Singh determine \(\operatorname{Cl}(L(G))\simeq G\) for triangle-free source graphs, which is only the scale-one/core term here. A published line-graph neighborhood-complex collapse identifies \(B\searrow Y\) under absence of triangles and quadrilaterals; that result alone does not identify the exceptional hexagonal Rips triangles or their attaching maps. A previously documented **line-graph** hexagon theorem treats exactly the edge-disjoint-hexagon subclass and yields wedges of circles and two-spheres there; it cannot imply this overlapping-hexagon theorem, where the hexagon relations may be dependent and do not allow private-edge cancellations. Separately, a published hexagon-packing theorem for **vertex** scale-two Rips complexes likewise assumes pairwise edge-disjoint source hexagons; its graph family is different and cannot imply the present line-graph result. A 2024 article on line-graph Vietoris–Rips persistence for graph representation learning focuses on learned edge filtrations and expressivity rather than a uniform exact graph-metric homotopy formula. Adamaszek's graph-power paper proves a girth-based square collapse for the input graph itself and analyzes the total graph of a subdivision; its Theorems 5.1–5.2 concern the subdivision square and the ordinary line-graph clique complex, not the square of the line graph. The 2025 torus-grid Vietoris–Rips classification concerns a different family of base graphs. No equivalent all-overlaps line-graph theorem was found in the checked literature; this does not establish publication novelty absolutely.

## Limitations
Girth at least six is essential to the connector uniqueness and three-edge classification; smaller girth cases are not covered. The result treats the single inclusive graph-metric scale two and first-homology inclusion from scale one; it does not determine higher scales or the full persistence barcode. The homotopy equivalence is not claimed equivariant under graph automorphisms. The formula permits possible torsion in \(H_1\) arising from the six-cycle lattice; no general saturation assertion is made. Finite computational checks cannot certify arbitrary graph instances by themselves. The literature comparison cannot rule out unindexed or differently formulated equivalent theorems.
