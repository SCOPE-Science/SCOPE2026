# Higher Frobenius osculating loci of rational normal curves are smooth balanced scrolls

## Finding
For any algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic \(p>0\), any integer \(e\ge2\), and \(m=p^e\), the order-\((m-1)\) osculating locus of the degree-\(d\) rational normal curve, for every \(d\ge2m-1\), is the smooth rational normal scroll
\[
\mathcal O_{m-1}(C_d)=S(q_0,\ldots,q_{m-1}),\qquad q_r=\left\lfloor\frac{d-r}{m}\right\rfloor\quad(0\le r<m).
\]
It has dimension \(m\), codimension \(d-m\), degree \(d-m+1\), and a prime homogeneous determinantal ideal generated by the minors described below. The natural osculating incidence morphism has generic purely inseparable degree \(m\), and the curve meets a general ruling plane at one geometric point with scheme-theoretic length \(m\). At \(d=2m-1\), the locus is \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1}\) embedded by \(\mathcal O(1,1)\). In particular, in characteristic two the third-osculating locus of \(C_7\subset\mathbb P^7\) is \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^3\), with generic incidence degree four.

## Assumptions and scope
Take the standard degree-\(d\) Veronese curve
\[
C_d:[s:t]\mapsto[s^d:s^{d-1}t:\cdots:t^d]\subset\mathbb P^d_k.
\]
Osculating spaces are the span of Hasse derivatives through order \(m-1\), equivalently the linear spans of length-\(m\) infinitesimal neighborhoods on the smooth curve. Write the ambient coordinates \(X_0,\ldots,X_d\). The range \(d\ge2m-1\) ensures every \(q_r\ge1\), so the scroll is smooth rather than a scroll cone. The theorem concerns the reduced image and generic, not special, incidence fibers; no characteristic-zero comparison is asserted.

## Proof
**1. The prime-power Lucas identity.** For \(0\le r,j<m=p^e\) and any integer \(a\ge0\), Lucas's theorem in base \(p\) gives
\[
\binom{ma+r}{j}\equiv\binom rj\pmod p.
\]
Indeed, the digits of \(j\) occupy the bottom \(e\) positions while those of \(ma\) are all zero there. On the affine curve chart \(s=1\) with parameter \(t\), let \(D^{[j]}\) denote the Hasse derivative \(D^{[j]}t^i=\binom ij t^{i-j}\). On the dense incidence chart where the coefficient of \(D^{[0]}\) is one, a general osculating-space point has coordinates
\[
x_i=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_j\binom ij t^{i-j},\qquad \lambda_0=1,
\]
with terms having \(j>i\) interpreted as zero. For \(i=ma+r\), Lucas gives
\[
x_{ma+r}=z^a w_r,\quad z=t^m,\quad
w_r=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\lambda_j\binom rj t^{r-j},\quad w_0=1.
\]

**2. Exact image and defining ideal.** Put \(q_r=\lfloor(d-r)/m\rfloor\). Form the \(2\times(d-m+1)\) matrix by concatenating, for each \(0\le r<m\), the \(q_r\) columns
\[
\begin{pmatrix}X_{ma+r}\\X_{m(a+1)+r}\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0\le a<q_r.
\]
Every point of the osculating incidence has rank at most one in this matrix, by the preceding factorization on a dense chart. The maximal-minor rank-one equations define the standard projective rational normal scroll \(S(q_0,\ldots,q_{m-1})\); since each \(q_r>0\), its determinantal homogeneous ideal is prime and its projective variety is smooth. Conversely, the dense scroll chart \(X_0=1\) has unique coordinates \(z=X_m\), \(w_r=X_r\) for \(1\le r<m\), with the remaining coordinates \(X_{ma+r}=z^a w_r\). Since \(k\) is algebraically closed, there is \(t\) with \(t^m=z\). The triangular equations for the \(w_r\) have diagonal coefficient one in \(\lambda_r\), so they uniquely recover \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{m-1}\) from \(t,w_1,\ldots,w_{m-1}\). Thus the dense scroll chart lies in the image, proving equality of reduced closed images and the stated prime ideal. The standard scroll dimension and degree formulas give dimension \(m\), degree \(\sum_r q_r=d-m+1\), and ambient dimension \(d\). Since \(q_r\) differs by at most one across \(r\), the scroll is balanced.

**3. Inseparability and curve contact.** The rational function field of the scroll on this chart is \(k(z,w_1,\ldots,w_{m-1})\), while that of the osculating incidence is \(k(t,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{m-1})\). The triangular change of coordinates identifies the latter with \(k(t,w_1,\ldots,w_{m-1})\), with \(t^m=z\). The element \(z\) is not a \(p\)-th power in the scroll function field (its valuation along \(z=0\) is one). Therefore \(T^m-z\) has minimal purely inseparable degree \(m\); the generic incidence degree is exactly \(m\). On the original curve, \(\lambda_j=0\) for \(j>0\), hence \(w_r=t^r\) and the scroll ruling parameter is \(z=t^m\). Globally this extends as \([s:t]\mapsto[s^m:t^m]\), the \(e\)-fold relative Frobenius, after identifying the Frobenius twist by the chosen coordinates. Its generic fibers are length-\(m\) fat points supported at a unique geometric point. The pullback of a general scroll ruling plane to the embedded curve is precisely such a fiber, proving the contact statement.

## Verification
The bundled standalone `verify.py` reconstructs the prime-power binomial identities for \((p,m)=(3,3),(2,4),(5,5),(2,8),(3,9)\) throughout a finite range of \(d\), tests the predicted directrix sums, and evaluates every matrix minor on explicitly generated finite-field incidence points. It independently checks \((p,m,d)=(3,3,5),(3,3,8),(2,4,7),(2,4,10),(5,5,9),(2,8,15)\), with exact counts of distinct images. Its output ends in `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED`. These tests are finite corroborations; the Lucas identity, the scroll's prime determinantal ideal, and the function-field argument constitute the proof for all stated parameters. The \(m=3\) and \(m=5\) checks are low-order controls outside the theorem's \(e\ge2\) novelty range.

## Relationship to prior work
Gmainer and Havlicek, *Nuclei of Normal Rational Curves* (arXiv:1304.0088v1, 2013), §3, give the Hasse-osculating matrix and characterize intersections of osculating subspaces (nuclei), not their united image or generic incidence degree. Aprodu–Farkas–Papadima–Raicu–Weyman, *Koszul Modules and Green's Conjecture*, §§5.4, 5.9, establish the characteristic-two **first tangential** scroll and discuss a containing scroll for higher-characteristic tangential surfaces (Remark 5.17). These are distinct from the Frobenius-power \((m-1)\)-osculating locus with \(e\ge2\) considered here: the cited first-tangent results concern order one, not order \(p^e-1\). A historical article by T. G. Room (1932) on configurations of osculating spaces was located bibliographically, but full text could not be inspected, so priority remains provisional.

## Limitations
The result is over an algebraically closed field and restricted to \(e\ge2\), \(d\ge2p^e-1\). The boundary with some \(q_r=0\), special fibers of the incidence map, and possible historical anticipation in older projective-geometric literature are not classified. No outside expert or independent scientific audit has been performed.
