On rooted phylogenetic trees with at most three taxa, greedy selection of equally costly but imperfect conservation interventions always maximizes expected rooted phylogenetic diversity. Four taxa are sharp for failure even when every untreated species has the same survival probability \(1/2\): a fully rational example has a unique greedy two-species gain \(129/25\), below the optimal \(269/50\) by \(11/50\).
