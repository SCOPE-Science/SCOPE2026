# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Both 26-block systems are checked against all 78 unordered point pairs, and the four-for-four Pasch exchange preserves pair incidences. Independent face generators agree on every subset. Complete directed-Hasse acyclicity checks verify the two matching certificates: respectively 566 and 563 ordinary pairs, with critical-cell profiles \((c_0,c_3,c_4)=(1,6,18)\) and \((1,4,21)\). Sparse gradient elimination and independent recursive gradient reduction produce identical signed integer boundaries. The \(6\times18\) and \(4\times21\) boundaries have explicit maximal minors of determinant \(+1\), so the three-cell groups cancel integrally. Forman's theorem and the third Hurewicz isomorphism justify upgrading those integer calculations to wedge-of-four-spheres homotopy equivalences. Direct mod-two ordinary-chain ranks give independent checks of the stated Betti numbers. The exact script and its output are included; no finite sample is used to infer an infinite claim.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
The nearest pre-existing statements are the 2003 complete isomorphism-class enumeration and the 2004 general formulas for independent-set counts. Those results establish the two types and enumerative information, but neither computes the integer Morse attaching degrees or states the complete integral homotopy types. A 2023 treatment of geometric representations and general discrete-Morse literature were compared for plausible implication; the search found no explicit equivalent or strictly stronger case result. The theta-complex Alexander duality viewpoint does not by itself yield these homotopy equivalences. The preceding related nine-point affine-plane calculation concerns a different hypergraph and does not imply the order-thirteen classification. Risks remain from unindexed work, incomplete access to a later geometric paper, and possible unnoticed implications of broader theorems.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exhaustive classification for the **smallest Steiner triple system order with two nonisomorphic designs**, rather than an arbitrary vertex slice. It answers a natural topological question arising from established enumeration of independent point subsets: what are the full homotopy spaces whose face counts were previously studied? Both are nonpure complexes (with 117 and 132 maximal five-point faces) yet are torsion-free four-sphere wedges, with ranks 12 and 17; proving this requires 2-connectivity, integral acyclic Morse models, and primitive attaching matrices, none of which follows from the known independent-set polynomials alone. The different wedge multiplicities give a concrete topological distinction between the two design types. No sweeping theorem for all Steiner triple systems is claimed.
