# Scientific review of matching-deleted bipartite second independence complexes

## Correctness — PASS

The argument classifies every allowed face by the induced maximum-degree-one condition. Every unmatched-to-matched cross-edge is in exactly one triangle and no higher face; the associated collapses are free. Every remaining unmatched-to-unmatched cross-edge is maximal, and its endpoints lie in the connected core. Two explicitly contiguous simplicial retractions reduce that core to a union of two contractible complexes meeting in the complete-graph one-skeleton. The homotopy pushout and the graph cycle rank give the claimed integral wedge. The boundary cases with a singleton bipartition side or zero/one/two deleted edges are covered separately. Finite direct enumeration, collapse checks, retraction-contiguity checks, and exact mod-two homology agree in all 127 parameter triples through side sizes six. The finite tests are corroboration, not the proof of the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

Taylan's 2015 full text establishes the equivalent \(P_3\)-devoid definition and handles forests/cycles, including the small six-cycle case. The 2017 Nilakantan–Shukla and 2019 Goyal–Shukla–Singh full texts compute ordinary independence for graph families that include crowns, not second independence. Deshpande–Singh's 2020 full text gives complete multipartite higher-independence (covering exactly the undeleted baseline), cycles and other families, but does not encompass the noncomplete bipartite matching-deleted family. Mathematical-record searches for crown graph, bipartite matching deletion, dissociation complex, \(P_3\)-free complex and the exact proposed multiplicities found no dominating theorem. Specific known special cases are acknowledged rather than claimed new. A stronger unindexed result remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The classification covers every partial matching deletion and all rectangular bipartition sizes, not an arbitrarily chosen finite graph. It identifies the exact transition from circle homology to two-sphere homology as a canonical 1-factor is removed, explains it via explicit collapses and the matching-pair tetrahedral core, and provides both integral homotopy groups in the relevant degrees without a computer-dependent argument. This answers a natural structural question adjacent to published complete-multipartite formulas and the older \(P_3\)-devoid theory. No claim of publication priority or independent validation is made.

## Closest literature and risks

The closest full-text results are Taylan (2015, arXiv:1501.06754, Sections 3 and 5), Nilakantan–Shukla (2017, arXiv:1709.04789, Sections 1 and 3), Goyal–Shukla–Singh (2019, arXiv:1905.06926, Section 3), and Deshpande–Singh (2020, arXiv:2001.05448, Section 3). The major residual risk is an equivalent formula stated under dissociation or devoid-complex nomenclature in an unindexed source. The result does not address filtration inclusion maps, higher parameters, or equivariant refinements.
