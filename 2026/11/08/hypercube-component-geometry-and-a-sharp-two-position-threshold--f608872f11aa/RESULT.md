# Hypercube component geometry and a sharp two-position threshold for derangement swaps

## Finding
For every integer \(n\geq3\), let \(D_n\) be the set of permutations \(\pi=(\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(n))\) of \([n]\) satisfying \(\pi(p)\ne p\) at every position. Define \(G_n^{(r)}\) on \(D_n\): two permutations are adjacent if one is obtained from the other by swapping the entries in positions \(p<q\) with \(q-p\leq r\), with both endpoints in \(D_n\).

**Theorem.** The adjacent-position graph \(G_n^{(1)}\) has precisely \(2^{n-2}\) connected components. They are exactly the nonempty fibers of the sign vector
\[
\sigma(\pi)=\bigl(\mathbf1\{\pi^{-1}(i)<i\}\bigr)_{i=2}^{n-1}\in\{0,1\}^{n-2}.
\]
Exactly two vertices of \(G_n^{(1)}\) are isolated, namely \((n,1,2,\ldots,n-1)\) and \((2,3,\ldots,n,1)\). For \(n\geq4\), \(G_n^{(2)}\) is connected. If the connected components of \(G_n^{(1)}\) are contracted inside \(G_n^{(2)}\) and loops and parallel edges are discarded, the resulting simple quotient is exactly the hypercube \(Q_{n-2}\), with component labels \(\sigma\) and edges between Hamming-distance-one labels. At \(n=3\), even \(G_3^{(2)}\) has two isolated vertices.

This is a sharp locality threshold: for every \(n\geq4\), allowing swaps whose position distance is at most two restores connectivity, while allowing only neighboring-position swaps does not.

## Assumptions and scope
The entries are distinct labels \(1,\ldots,n\), their home positions carry the same labels, and no intermediate state may have a fixed point. Position distance is ordinary linear distance, not cyclic wrap-around distance. The quotient contracts the components under distance-one swaps; an original distance-two edge inside one component produces no quotient edge. A sign coordinate concerns the **position of symbol** \(i\), not the value at position \(i\). No assertion about the diameter of \(G_n^{(2)}\) is made.

## Proof
**1. The signs are invariant under neighboring swaps.** A symbol \(i\) may pass from a position smaller than \(i\) to one larger than \(i\) using swaps of consecutive positions only by occupying its own home position at an intermediate vertex. This is forbidden. Therefore \(\sigma\) is constant along every edge of \(G_n^{(1)}\).

**2. Every sign fiber is nonempty and connected.** Fix any sign vector, and let \(L\) contain the symbols directed left (those whose positions are required smaller than their values), together with \(n\). Let \(R\) contain the remaining symbols, including \(1\). Define \(c(L,R)\) as the increasing list of \(L\) followed by the increasing list of \(R\). This is a derangement of the required signs: the \(j\)-th symbol of \(L\) is at least \(j+1\), so lies strictly to the right of its position \(j\); the \(j\)-th symbol of \(R\) is at most \(|L|+j-1\), so lies strictly to the left of its position \(|L|+j\).

Take any permutation in the same fiber. Whenever an \(R\)-symbol immediately precedes an \(L\)-symbol, swap them. The \(R\)-symbol moves one step farther right and the \(L\)-symbol one step farther left, hence both remain off their home positions. Repeating puts all \(L\)-symbols before all \(R\)-symbols. Now sort each block increasingly using adjacent descent swaps. In an \(L\)-block descent, the right-hand, smaller symbol was already strictly to the left of home; moving it farther left is legal, and the larger symbol moving right stays to the left of home because its new position was valid for the smaller symbol. For an \(R\)-block descent, the left-hand, larger symbol was already strictly right of home; moving it farther right is legal, and the smaller symbol moving left stays right of home. Thus every vertex reaches \(c(L,R)\) by legal swaps. Every one of the \(2^{n-2}\) possible vectors therefore yields exactly one connected component.

**3. Exactly two isolated vertices.** If every internal symbol is directed right, the forced positions, proceeding from largest label down, give the sole permutation \((n,1,2,\ldots,n-1)\). If every internal symbol is directed left, proceeding from smallest label up gives the sole permutation \((2,3,\ldots,n,1)\). Each is isolated by step 2. Any other signature has at least one internal symbol on each side, hence \(2\leq |L|\leq n-2\). In the canonical representative the adjacent entries at positions \(|L|,|L|+1\) are \(n,1\). Exchanging them is legal: \(n\) moves to a position at most \(n-1\), and \(1\) moves to a position at least \(2\). Thus the other components are nontrivial and contain no isolated vertices.

**4. Distance-two swaps change at most one sign coordinate.** A swap of entries in positions \(p,p+2\) moves each entry across the single intermediate position \(p+1\). A sign can change only for the symbol \(i=p+1\): moving the symbol \(p\) across position \(p\) would give a fixed point at an endpoint, and similarly for symbol \(p+2\). Symbol \(p+1\), if present at one of the two endpoints, can switch sides, but no other sign changes. Distance-one swaps change none. Therefore each nonloop edge of the quotient joins Hamming-distance-one vectors.

**5. Every hypercube edge is realized.** Fix an arbitrary vector, let \(L,R\) be as in step 2, and select any \(i\in L\cap\{2,\ldots,n-1\}\). Begin with \(c=c(L,R)\) and move symbol \(i\) right by adjacent swaps until its position is \(i-1\). It never enters its forbidden position. Each \(L\)-symbol it passes moves one place left, which is legal. Each crossed \(R\)-symbol \(r\) has \(r<i\); its original position in \(c\) is \(r+|\{\ell\in L:\ell>r\}|\geq r+2\), since both \(i\) and \(n\) lie in \(L\). Moving such an \(R\)-symbol one place left is therefore also legal. This operation leaves the entries in positions \(i\) and \(i+1\) unchanged from \(c\).

Let \(j\) be the entry now in position \(i+1\). If \(j\ne i-1\), directly exchange positions \(i-1\) and \(i+1\). The symbol \(i\) lands at \(i+1\); symbol \(j\) lands at \(i-1\ne j\). Thus exactly the sign coordinate of \(i\) changes. If \(j=i-1\), first exchange entries in positions \(i\) and \(i+1\), then exchange entries in positions \(i-1\) and \(i+1\). The preliminary exchange is legal: its first endpoint is \(j=i-1\), which can occupy position \(i\), and its second endpoint is the entry \(c(i)\ne i+1\). To see this last inequality, notice that \(i-1\) must belong to \(R\), as an \(L\)-entry of value \(i-1\) could only occur before position \(i-1\). If the preceding entry \(c(i)\) also lies in the increasing \(R\)-block, it is smaller than \(i-1\). Otherwise it is the last entry of the \(L\)-block, namely \(n\), and \(i-1\) must be the first \(R\)-entry \(1\). In the latter case \(i=2\) and \(n\geq4\), so \(n\ne i+1\). The final distance-two swap is therefore legal. All symbols other than \(i\) remain on their previous side of their home position, while \(i\) changes from left to right.

This gives a quotient edge for every vector whose \(i\)-coordinate is one to the vector with that coordinate zero. Every edge of \(Q_{n-2}\) appears, and step 4 excludes extra edges. Hence the quotient is exactly \(Q_{n-2}\) for \(n\geq4\). It is connected; since each fiber is connected, the full graph \(G_n^{(2)}\) is connected. At \(n=3\) the only derangements, \((2,3,1)\) and \((3,1,2)\), differ in all three positions, so cannot be joined by one transposition of any positions, and the claimed boundary follows.

## Verification
Two standalone implementations, `verify_structure.py` and `verify_components.cpp`, are included. The Python program independently reconstructs all components for \(3\leq n\leq8\), all quotient edges, and the unique isolated vertices. It also checks the constructive crossing operation for **every** sign vector and every available coordinate through \(n=14\). The C++17 implementation independently enumerates derangements through \(n=9\), uses disjoint-set unions to reconstruct the distance-one components and the distance-two connectivity, and verifies every quotient edge and the complete hypercube adjacency matrix. The largest test uses 133,496 derangements and obtains 128 components and 448 quotient edges.

The proof is uniform in \(n\); these finite computations are corroboration rather than an extrapolation.

As a distinct metric warning, same-sign components need not be isometric subgraphs of the ordinary permutohedron. At \(n=6\), the two derangements \((3,1,2,5,6,4)\) and \((3,6,2,5,1,4)\) have ordinary adjacent-transposition distance five but distance seven inside \(G_6^{(1)}\). At the first permutation the only legal neighboring swap is between positions three and four, containing symbols two and five; those two symbols have the same relative order at the destination. Thus that first exchange must later be undone, forcing at least two extra swaps. The seven-step sequence of swaps at positions \(3,2,3,4,3,2,3\) achieves equality. This calculation is also checked directly by exhaustive breadth-first search in the Python verifier.

## Relationship to prior work
Baril and Vajnovszki, *Gray code for derangements* (Discrete Applied Mathematics 140, 2004), provide a generation procedure with a successor obtained by one or two unrestricted transpositions, or a three-element rotation. That method is not a path in the graph restricted to single swaps of **neighboring positions**. Its combinatorial definition and transformation cases were examined in the openly available paper.

Jain, *Transpositions in Derangements* (2021), studies a graph where one may transpose **any pair** of positions. The paper proves that unrestricted-transposition graph connected for orders greater than three and explicitly observes that some derangements admit no legal adjacent-element swap. This earlier observation of frozen vertices is acknowledged. The present theorem instead counts every connected component, identifies its invariant, proves a sharp position-range connectivity threshold, and determines the quotient component graph. The two adjacency models are mathematically different; the unrestricted graph's connectivity does not imply the range-two assertion.

The classical permutohedron has unrestricted adjacent swaps on **all** permutations. Neither that connectivity statement nor general Gray-code existence implies connectivity after deleting every permutation with a fixed point.

## Limitations
Historical priority is qualified: relevant exact component or quotient classifications might appear in older sources or unpublished computations not located during the literature comparison. The dated 2004 paper is an early primary source for this direction, not a claim that the subject began then; an earlier exact public release date of that paper was not verified. The scope covers ordinary linear position distance and the forbidden main diagonal only. The proof does not determine the distribution of sizes or diameters of the individual components, nor the diameter or mixing properties of the connected range-two graph. No independent scholarly audit has been performed.
