# Assessment of ideal-dimension classification

## Correctness — PASS
The decomposition into uniserial branch modules is exact. For any ideal, the intersection with each branch determines one exponent; multiplying a general ideal element by that branch variable confines its coordinate to a one-dimensional socle layer. The prohibition on coordinate axes follows from minimality and conversely makes the chosen intersections exact. Inclusion-exclusion counts subspaces without coordinate axes without a transversality assumption, and the split perfect pairing on the radical gives a dimension-reversing involution in every characteristic. Eight direct prime-field RREF enumerations confirm the complete dimension distributions in the listed finite configurations; they do not establish the general statement by themselves.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
Aitor Iribar Lopez's 2023 mathematical answer gives the two-branch nodal-curve ideal normal form over the complex field; that special-case classification is prior work, not a claimed new contribution. Moore (2007) studies homological resolutions of local fibre products, Conca–Römer (2007) studies generic initial ideals, and Kassel–Reutenauer (2015) counts Laurent-ring ideals. These statements do not furnish the all-branch finite-field ideal-dimension census together with radical duality and the multibranch Grassmannian stratification. A general uniserial-module submodule enumeration may imply the formula, and priority is not independently certified. Previous relevant mathematical records were checked by claim and implication rather than title matching.

## Value — PASS
Counting ideals by dimension, not merely cardinality, is an intrinsic ring-theoretic invariant, and the arbitrary-length, arbitrary-branch result gives it uniformly for finite fields. The coordinate-axis-avoiding Grassmannian strata identify nonmonomial families and explain their arithmetic counts. The unexpected dimension-palindromicity follows from a module-level self-duality despite non-Gorensteinness of the ambient ring. These are structural results, not a single arbitrary finite-instance computation.

## Limitations
The result concerns truncated monomial fibre products and the reduced geometry of fixed-intersection strata; it does not assert a reduced full ideal scheme or a classification of ideal-isomorphism classes. Priority against every possible general submodule-counting theorem remains an open comparison risk.
