# Idempotent derivations of the free radical-cube-zero algebra: a characteristic-two orbit and rank census

## Finding
Let \(k\) be a field, \(m\ge 2\), \(V=k^m\), and let
\[
A_m=T_k(V)/(V^{\otimes 3})=k1\oplus V\oplus(V\otimes V)
\]
be the associative unital tensor algebra truncated after degree two (the ideal in the quotient is generated by **all** words of degree three). Multiplication is concatenation when total positive degree is at most two and is zero otherwise. A derivation means a \(k\)-linear map \(D:A_m\to A_m\) satisfying the ordinary Leibniz identity, and **idempotent** means \(D^2=D\) as a linear endomorphism.

**Theorem.** If \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), the zero derivation is the only idempotent derivation of \(A_m\). If \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), every idempotent derivation has a unique expression
\[
D(1)=0,\qquad D(v)=Pv+Qv,\qquad
D(u\otimes v)=Pu\otimes v+u\otimes Pv,
\]
where \(P\in\operatorname{End}(V)\) is a projection, \(P^2=P\), and \(Q\in\operatorname{Hom}(V,V\otimes V)\) satisfies
\[
(L_PQ+QP)=Q,\qquad L_P=P\otimes1+1\otimes P.
\]
Conversely, every such \((P,Q)\) gives an idempotent derivation. If \(r=\operatorname{rank}P\), \(s=m-r\), then its rank on the entire \(1+m+m^2\)-dimensional algebra is exactly
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}D=r+2rs.}
\]
There are precisely \(m+1\) conjugacy classes under the full algebra-automorphism group \(\operatorname{Aut}_k(A_m)\), indexed by \(r=0,\ldots,m\). Each is represented by the degree-preserving derivation \(D_P\) with \(P=\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0_s)\) and \(Q=0\). Their rank values are pairwise distinct.

For a finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) of even order, the **exact number** of idempotent derivations with degree-one projection rank \(r\) (equivalently, full rank \(r+2r(m-r)\)) is
\[
\boxed{N_{m,r}(q)=\begin{bmatrix}m\\r\end{bmatrix}_q
q^{\,r(m-r)+r^3+3r(m-r)^2},\qquad 0\le r\le m.}
\]
The total number is \(\sum_{r=0}^{m}N_{m,r}(q)\). For finite fields of odd order the total is \(1\).

As a geometric statement, in characteristic two the **reduced** algebraic variety of idempotent derivations has exactly \(m+1\) smooth geometrically irreducible connected components. The component indexed by \(r\) is a vector bundle of rank \(r^3+3r(m-r)^2\) over the variety of rank-\(r\) projections on \(V\), and hence has dimension
\[
\boxed{2r(m-r)+r^3+3r(m-r)^2.}
\]
In other characteristics its reduced geometric variety is a single point.

## Assumptions and scope
The field \(k\) is arbitrary, including imperfect fields; \(m\ge2\) is essential for ruling out scalar parts in generator images. The algebra is the **noncommutative** truncated free associative algebra; its degree-two part has dimension \(m^2\), not \(m(m+1)/2\). Algebra automorphisms and derivations fix the field pointwise. Component assertions concern the underlying reduced variety over an algebraic closure and make no assertion about possible nonreduced structures. The rank formula counts ranks of derivations as \(k\)-linear endomorphisms of the full algebra, not just their maps on generators.

## Proof
**Step 1: every derivation preserves the augmentation ideal.** Any derivation kills \(1\). Write the scalar part of its value at each generator \(x_i\) as \(c_i\). For distinct generators \(x_i,x_j\), apply the Leibniz identity to the defining relation \(x_ix_jx_i=0\). Its degree-two term is
\[
c_i(x_jx_i+x_ix_j)+c_j x_i^2=0.
\]
The three displayed degree-two monomials are distinct basis elements, in every characteristic. Hence \(c_i=c_j=0\). Varying \(i,j\) proves that all scalar parts vanish. Thus \(D(V)\subset V\oplus(V\otimes V)\); uniquely write \(D(v)=Pv+Qv\). The Leibniz identity forces \(D|_{V\otimes V}=L_P\). Conversely, every \(P,Q\) defines a derivation: words of length three map to zero because the images of generators have positive degree, and all higher defining relations remain zero.

**Step 2: idempotence.** On degree-one vectors, \(D^2=D\) is equivalent to
\[
P^2=P,\qquad L_PQ+QP=Q.
\]
On the degree-two space it is equivalent to \(L_P^2=L_P\). When \(P^2=P\), the operator identity is
\[
L_P^2-L_P=2(P\otimes P).
\]
If \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), a nonzero projection cannot have \(P\otimes P=0\), so \(P=0\). The equation involving \(Q\) then forces \(Q=0\). In characteristic two \(L_P^2=L_P\) automatically follows from \(P^2=P\), proving the promised parametrization.

**Step 3: solve the linear constraint.** Decompose \(V=U\oplus Z\) with \(U=\operatorname{im}P\) of dimension \(r\) and \(Z=\ker P\) of dimension \(s\). In characteristic two, \(L_P\) is zero on \((U\otimes U)\oplus(Z\otimes Z)\) and the identity on \((U\otimes Z)\oplus(Z\otimes U)\). Applying \(L_PQ+QP=Q\) separately on the domain \(U\) and \(Z\) gives
\[
Q(U)\subseteq(U\otimes U)\oplus(Z\otimes Z),\quad
Q(Z)\subseteq(U\otimes Z)\oplus(Z\otimes U).
\]
These are independent linear choices, so the solution space has dimension
\[
r(r^2+s^2)+s(2rs)=r^3+3rs^2.
\]
For the full derivation, its block matrix on \(V\oplus(V\otimes V)\) is \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}P&0\\Q&L_P\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). Its image has dimension \(r+2rs\): indeed the image of \(Q(Z)\) already belongs to \(\operatorname{im}L_P\), while each \(u\in U\) contributes one new direction modulo the degree-two space. The unit lies in the kernel. Distinct \(r\) give distinct ranks, since equality for \(r\ne t\) would force \(2(r+t)=2m+1\), impossible for integers.

**Step 4: automorphism orbits.** Every automorphism of \(A_m\) is uniquely determined by
\[
\Phi(v)=Mv+Sv,\quad M\in\operatorname{GL}(V),\quad S\in\operatorname{Hom}(V,V\otimes V),
\]
with \(\Phi(u\otimes v)=Mu\otimes Mv\). It is invertible exactly when \(M\) is. Projection rank \(r\) is invariant under conjugation, and \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\) is transitive on rank-\(r\) projections. For \(M=I\), conjugation of the standard degree-preserving \(D_P\) by \(\Phi_S\) produces the quadratic term \(L_PS+SP\) in characteristic two. Since the condition on \(Q\) is precisely \(Q=L_PQ+QP\), taking \(S=Q\) obtains any permitted quadratic term. Thus each rank stratum is one automorphism orbit.

**Step 5: finite-field counts and geometry.** The number of rank-\(r\) projections on \(\mathbb F_q^m\) is \(\begin{bmatrix}m\\r\end{bmatrix}_q q^{rs}\): choose the image and then choose a complementary kernel. For each projection there are \(q^{r^3+3rs^2}\) admissible maps \(Q\). Multiplying yields \(N_{m,r}(q)\). Over any characteristic-two field, the rank-\(r\) projection variety is the smooth irreducible homogeneous space \(\operatorname{GL}_m/(\operatorname{GL}_r\times\operatorname{GL}_s)\), of dimension \(2rs\). The displayed constraints on \(Q\) form a constant-rank vector subbundle, proving smoothness and irreducibility. Projection rank is locally constant in algebraic families of projections, so the strata are also closed and open in the reduced idempotent-derivation locus; they are its components.

## Verification
The standalone `checks/verify.py` uses independent coordinate arithmetic for fields of orders \(2,3,4\). For \(m=2\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), it enumerates **all** \(2^{14}\) candidate generator-image assignments, including possible constant terms, tests the Leibniz identity on all basis products and idempotence on all basis elements, and obtains rank-stratified counts \((1,96,256)\). An independent finite-field linear-system calculation enumerates every possible projection and computes the dimension of the constrained \(Q\)-space, checking \((m,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2)\). In particular, over \(\mathbb F_4\), the rank counts are \((1,5120,65536)\); over \(\mathbb F_2\) with \(m=3\) they are \((1,229376,458752,134217728)\). The second script `checks/verify_orbits.py` directly enumerates all \(1536\) algebra automorphisms when \(m=2,q=2\), and obtains the three conjugacy orbit sizes \((1,96,256)\). Run either script with a Python 3 standard-library interpreter; the expected output is recorded in `checks/expected_output.txt`. Finite tests are not the proof for arbitrary \(m\) or fields.

## Relationship to prior work
The monomial-algebra derivation framework and Hochschild-cohomology setting are classical. Sánchez-Flores, *On the semisimplicity of the outer derivations of monomial algebras*, arXiv:1004.2820 (first public version April 16, 2010), §2.2, treats **radical-square-zero** monomial algebras and identifies the first Hochschild cohomology of the multiple-loop case with a matrix Lie algebra in characteristic zero. The present algebra has **nonzero degree-two products**, and the theorem concerns the equation \(D^2=D\), its actual derivation orbit decomposition, and characteristic-dependent finite-field counts; none of those statements follows from the cited radical-square-zero cohomology result.

Hoffmann and Kowalski, *Integrating Hasse–Schmidt derivations*, arXiv:1212.5788 (first public version December 23, 2012), study iterative higher derivations, including extensions of results about idempotent derivations **on fields**. Their integration results do not supply the finite-dimensional, noncommutative conjugacy and rank census above. Redondo and Román, *Gerstenhaber algebra structure on the Hochschild cohomology of quadratic string algebras*, arXiv:1504.02495 (first public version April 9, 2015), study another quiver-and-relations class, restricted by the special-biserial/string conditions and quadratic relations; they do not establish these idempotent-derivation strata.

A general equivalence of idempotent derivations with parity gradings in characteristic two is not claimed as new. The claimed refinement is the explicit classification, orbit/fiber geometry, all-field obstruction, rank spectrum, and exact finite-field mass formula for this specific natural family. The possibility that a broad pre-existing graded-algebra theorem already implies the specialization remains a priority risk.

## Limitations
The theorem requires at least two free generators, and counts ordinary algebra derivations rather than higher or twisted derivations. It does not address longer tensor-algebra truncations, or the nonreduced scheme structures potentially present in characteristic two or other characteristics. The results have not been independently validated, and exhaustive historical priority is not certified. The finite-field tests cover selected small ranks and prime powers only.
