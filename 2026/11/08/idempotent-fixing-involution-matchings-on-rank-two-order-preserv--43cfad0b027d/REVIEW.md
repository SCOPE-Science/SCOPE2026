# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
Every rank-two isotone map is represented uniquely by a cut and an ordered two-point image. The mutual-inverse criterion is obtained by computing both compositions on those two image values; all three branches of the formula are covered, and the two nonfixed branches are mutual inverses of one another under the matching. The bijection with increasing triples proves the cycle count, and chain reversal transforms the two cases into one another. Direct finite enumeration independently confirmed the identities and counts for all \(2\le n\le16\), totaling 7,820 elements. Limits: the finite computation alone does not certify arbitrary \(n\), and only rank two is claimed.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority qualified)
Theorem B of Higgins (arXiv:2610.01912v1, 2026-10-01) gives a permutation matching, a weaker property than an involution; the paper's balanced-weight construction does not imply the explicit involution formula. Higgins (2024), Proposition 2.3, treats a divisible row–column parameter; it applies to the rank-two class when \(n\) is even but not odd. Other congruence cases mentioned there do not uniformly cover odd sizes. The 2019 orientation-preserving involution does not restrict to all isotone transformations. Searches of the accessible research records did not identify the explicit triple-based classification. Residual risk: an elementary special-case construction could be implicit in older literature, and a general matching theorem might admit a non-obvious specialization. The claim of priority is accordingly limited to checked evidence.

## Value — PASS
The explicit formula provides a concrete all-size involution on the first nontrivial isotone-transformation Green class, strengthening the known permutation existence result while preserving every idempotent. It works uniformly for the odd chain sizes not reached directly by the divisible-incidence theorem, gives a reversal-equivariant construction, and pairs non-idempotents with a canonical three-point index. This is a structural step in a natural involution-matching problem, not a claim about the full monoid.

## Scientific limitations
No all-ranks involution is constructed. No uniqueness, strong inverse property or general higher-rank extension is established. Historical-priority risk remains without independent external audit.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
