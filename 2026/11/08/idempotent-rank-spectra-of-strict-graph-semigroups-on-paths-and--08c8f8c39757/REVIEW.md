# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The idempotent/fixed-point constraint forces each chosen image vertex to be an endpoint of its own matched edge. Linear and cyclic runs of marked dimers then give the displayed binomial coefficients, including the all-dimer even-cycle boundary and the empty map. The conflict-graph neighborhoods are unions of two cliques, with the three-cycle handled separately. The cited Chudnovsky–Seymour result yields all-negative real roots, and componentwise multiplicativity follows from fixed images. Separate direct partial-map enumerations, oriented-matching enumerations, and claw tests agree with the exact formulas. The finite experiments corroborate but do not establish the infinite claims.

**Originality — PASS (with residual priority risk).** Margolis–Rhodes, arXiv:2609.39370v2, Sections 3 and 7–8, supply the definition, regularity, and counts of all strict maps, not composition-idempotents by rank. Their Section 11 constructs selected idempotents but does not enumerate the full family. The 2020 foundational continuous-map paper and published searches of related diagram monoids are not statements of this exact constrained enumeration. Chudnovsky–Seymour provides the broader root theorem, but not the identification or coefficients of the present semigroup rank polynomial. Searches for alternate terminology, binomial coefficients, oriented matchings, and real-rooted rank polynomials found no equivalent. The possibility of an earlier identical oriented-matching enumeration under different names is a residual risk; absence from search results does not certify priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Idempotents and their rank stratification are canonical structural data of finite semigroups, governing retractions and regular classes. The complete closed formulas characterize their multiplicities for two fundamental infinite families of newly studied strict graph semigroups and extend multiplicatively to every maximum-degree-two graph. The claw-free representation adds a global shape theorem (negative real roots and ultra-log-concavity) not readable from a count of all semigroup elements. The formulas distinguish even-path and even-cycle top-rank counts and provide direct exact checks for semigroup calculations.

The theorem is limited to strict semigroups, and the root conclusion to maximum-degree-two source graphs. There has been no independent audit.
