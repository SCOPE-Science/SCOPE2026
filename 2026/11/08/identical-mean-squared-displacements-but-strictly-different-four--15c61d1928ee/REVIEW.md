# Scientific review: matched-MSD active dimer distinguishability

## Correctness — PASS

The exact object is the stationary, independent product of a harmonic separation Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process and either a covariance-matched Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck or symmetric telegraph stiffness process, integrated in time with an independent translational Brownian term. Every parameter is positive except the nonnegative bare translational diffusion; the physical dimer subfamily has strictly positive diffusion. The original continuum dynamics and normalization are checked against Cocconi–Alston–Bertrand (arXiv:2304.07738v1), main equations (2), (11), and (17), and its S1–S15 derivations. Stationary Gaussian and telegraph four-point functions are computed independently and their three disconnected covariance pairings are explicitly subtracted. Ordered-time integration generates the exact cumulants and strict ordering. Dominated convergence gives the long-time constants and their dimensionless ratio. A conditional-Gaussian quadratic-form argument gives the finite exponential-moment interval, while a bounded-sign Schur estimate gives the telegraph sub-Gaussian bound. The standalone code passed 193 finite numerical and algebraic diagnostics including independent Gaussian covariance-operator trace checks. Numerical tests are not used as infinite-time proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with stated residual risks

The founding 2023 active-bound-state source supplies the two SDE mechanisms, their product drift distributions at a time, and their second-moment/MSD and entropy formulas. Its main text and inspected supplemental material do not state finite-time fourth displacement cumulants, the strict all-lag comparison, or the spectral moment-domain dichotomy after matching the complete MSD. Shee–Chaudhuri (arXiv:2108.12228), sections 2 and 7, compute displacement fourth moments for OU speed multiplied by a rotationally diffusing **unit** orientation; the model does not reduce to OU separation amplitude multiplied by an independent telegraph or Gaussian stiffness as an identity, and no paired covariance-matched ordering is stated. Patel–Shee–Chaudhuri (arXiv:2410.22004), model and section V, treat a trapped particle with rotational orientation and OU speed and do not cover the unconfined dimer center-of-mass increment. Sarkar–Santra–Basu (arXiv:2210.09035) provide a related general physical motivation involving distinct active noise with common pair covariances, but study boundary-driven harmonic chains, with different observables. Accessible research-record comparisons for OU products, telegraph forcing, fourth cumulants, and finite-time moment domains returned no covering statement. General Gaussian quadratic-form results could supply part of the proof but not the telegraph comparison and its universal ratio. Priority against unindexed equivalent fourth-order formulas or stronger stochastic-process identities still needs independent review.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The source explicitly uses enhanced MSD as its transport observable, but covariance matching makes the two microscopically different force models indistinguishable by **all** two-time displacement measurements. The new all-time positive fourth-cumulant gap supplies a mathematically exact discriminator that requires no heuristic time-window choice, while the long-time ratio measures the relaxation-rate ratio without knowing force amplitudes or translational diffusivity. The sharp contrast between sub-Gaussian and finite-radius exponential moments provides an additional structural distinction. These are observable, mechanistically motivated extensions of the source problem rather than routine recomputations of its MSD. They do not claim an optimal estimator, repulsive extension, or a full large-deviation principle.
