# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. Every isolation and gap indicator requires exactly six missing edges. Distinct locally overlapping indicators require at least nine missing edges, giving mixed factorial-moment convergence to independent Poisson variables. Federico's deterministic three-class decomposition, combined with independently sharpened size-two/three boundary counts, bounds exceptional disconnections by \(O(q_n+nq_n^7+nq_n^{10})\). The six-neighbor and cut boundary counts were checked with the included finite verifier (53,256 assertions). The infinite claim uses analytic estimates, not simulation.

## Originality

PASS, with historical-priority qualifications. Federico (2024) determines off-critical connectivity and the competing isolations/gaps but does not give the exact simultaneous critical-window law at the equal-exponent case. Chakraborty, Luh and Singh (2026, Section 7) expressly ask for critical-window laws at small bandwidth, while their established results assume bandwidth growing faster than logarithmically. The present result is not implied by either displayed theorem: simultaneous limiting dependence and the critical error estimate require additional work. Related continuous-space random geometric graph work remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The third power is the unique uniform fixed-bandwidth cycle power where the two natural obstruction exponents coincide. Its critical connectivity law differs substantively both from the gap-dominated square-cycle limit and from the isolation-only growing-bandwidth law. This is a motivated critical boundary calculation, not an arbitrary finite parameter specialization.

## Limits and closest literature

The result does not determine all component sizes, nor extend to spatially varying retention. The local witness bound relies on the geometrical decomposition published by Federico (2024) and its critical-scale proof is explicitly specialized in the result. Historical uniqueness beyond the inspected sources cannot be guaranteed.
