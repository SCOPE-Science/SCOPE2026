# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The projection argument is a bijection: an induced matching cannot occupy one clique fiber twice, and its occupied base graph has only isolated vertices and paired adjacent vertices. Conversely every such component admits independently \(\binom m2\) internal or \(m^2\) cross-edge choices, respectively. The domination criterion for inclusion-maximality uses \(m\ge2\) in its essential reverse implication. On cycles, the forbidden patterns \(111\) and \(000\), run lengths one or two, and the exactly \(n/k\) label multiplicity establish every coefficient of the finite formula. The support spectrum and purity cutoff then follow. The independent finite verifier reconstructs the original graph and edge-compatibility relation.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Brešar–Dravec–Hedžet–Samadi, arXiv:2406.04020, Theorem 3.1 and its proof, supply the known *maximum induced-matching cardinality* of lexicographic products and related projection reasoning; this is not claimed as new. Yuan–Yang–Zheng–Gong, arXiv:2410.11288, give global sharp counts for connected graphs, not these exact cardinality coefficients. Orlovich–Zverovich study minimum/maximum sizes in broad graph classes rather than the exact cycle-blowup enumeration. The new assessed content is the domination-restricted weighted family, its exact cyclic maximal-matching polynomial and spectrum, and the exceptional purity classification. Equivalent formulations (strong products with complete graphs, independence sets of squared line graphs, dominating dissociation supports) were searched. No direct prior coverage was identified, but an implicit/uncatalogued formula or derivation from the pre-existing projection proof is a genuine residual publication-priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The explicit formula gives all possible sizes and numbers of irredundant induced-matchings of an infinite, naturally parametrized product-graph family. The sharp nonmonotone purity classification \(n\in\{3,5\}\) distinguishes a rare well-indumatched phenomenon not settled by the known maximum induced-matching value; the general support bijection also provides an exact enumerative route for arbitrary clique blowups. This is a structural classification, not a routine scalar substitution into a previous formula.

## Evidence and limitations

Finite direct edge enumeration covers 150 small arbitrary-base profiles and 11 cyclic profiles; a separate symbolic transfer check covers 434 cyclic parameter pairs. General proofs do not depend on these finite checks. Publication priority remains provisional in view of related lexicographic-product structure and possible unindexed equivalents.
