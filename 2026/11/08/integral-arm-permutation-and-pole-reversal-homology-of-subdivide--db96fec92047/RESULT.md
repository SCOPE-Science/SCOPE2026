# Integral arm-permutation and pole-reversal homology of subdivided multitheta graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(p\geq3\), let \(T_p\) have vertices \(a,b,x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_p,y_p\) and precisely the edges \(a x_i,x_i y_i,y_i b\), for \(1\leq i\leq p\). Thus \(T_p\) consists of \(p\) internally disjoint paths of length three between two poles. Write \(X_p=\operatorname{Ind}(T_p)\) for the simplicial complex of independent vertex sets. The full graph automorphism group is \(S_p\times C_2\), where \(\sigma\in S_p\) permutes the paths and \(\tau\in C_2\) exchanges \(a,b\) and simultaneously exchanges every \(x_i,y_i\). There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
X_p\simeq S^1\vee S^{p-1}.
\]
As integral \(S_p\times C_2\)-modules, its only nonzero reduced homology groups are the rank-one modules
\[
\widetilde H_1(X_p;\mathbb Z)\cong \mathbf1_{S_p}\boxtimes\varepsilon_{C_2},\qquad
\widetilde H_{p-1}(X_p;\mathbb Z)\cong\operatorname{sgn}_{S_p}\boxtimes\varepsilon_{C_2}^{\,p}.
\]
Here \(\varepsilon_{C_2}(\tau)=-1\), and the second exponent is interpreted modulo two. Equivalently, for \(\sigma\in S_p\) and \(e\in\{0,1\}\), the respective integral homology actions are multiplication by \((-1)^e\) and \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)(-1)^{pe}\). All other reduced integral homology is zero. When \(p=2m\), \(T_p\) is the Kronecker double cover of the friendship graph of \(m\) triangles sharing one vertex; the theorem then determines its full, potentially larger-than-lifted automorphism action.

## Assumptions and scope
The graphs are finite, simple and undirected. Independence means no adjacent pair of vertices. All homology has integral coefficients unless a coefficient field is explicitly given. \(p\ge3\) is essential for the two nonzero homology degrees to be distinct and for the two poles to be exactly the vertices of degree \(p\). The group \(S_p\times C_2\) is the entire automorphism group because each of the \(p\) two-internal-vertex paths must map as a block, while the poles may be exchanged. All equivariance claims concern homology representations, not an equivariant homotopy wedge decomposition.

## Proof
**1. Deletion at a pole.** Put \(B=\operatorname{Ind}(T_p-a)\). Deleting the remaining pole \(b\) leaves the disjoint union of edges \(x_i y_i\), so its independence complex is the join \(D=\bigast_{i=1}^p\{x_i,y_i\}\cong S^{p-1}\): it is the boundary of the \(p\)-dimensional cross-polytope. The independent sets containing \(b\) consist of \(b\) together with a subset of the \(x_i\)'s, so \(B=D\cup(b*\Delta_X)\), where \(\Delta_X\) is the full simplex on \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_p\}\), contained in \(D\). Both \(\Delta_X\) and its cone are contractible. Collapsing the attached cone along the contractible subcomplex, or using the homotopy pushout of \(D\leftarrow\Delta_X\rightarrow b*\Delta_X\), yields \(B\simeq D\simeq S^{p-1}\), with inclusion \(D\hookrightarrow B\) inducing an integral isomorphism in degree \(p-1\).

**2. Reattaching the first pole.** The link of \(a\) in \(X_p\) is \(L=\operatorname{Ind}(T_p-N[a])\), where \(N[a]=\{a,x_1,\ldots,x_p\}\). The remaining graph is the star centered at \(b\) with leaves \(y_1,\ldots,y_p\). Hence \(L=\Delta_Y\sqcup\{b\}\simeq S^0\). Since \(B\simeq S^{p-1}\) is path connected and both components of \(L\) are contractible, the inclusion \(L\hookrightarrow B\) is nullhomotopic (choose paths joining the two component images). The deletion-link pushout \(X_p=B\cup_L(a*L)\) is its homotopy cofiber; therefore \(X_p\simeq B\vee\Sigma L\simeq S^{p-1}\vee S^1\). This also proves integral homology is torsion-free and concentrated exactly in degrees \(1,p-1\).

**3. Path permutations.** The top homology inclusion \(\widetilde H_{p-1}(D;\mathbb Z)\rightarrow\widetilde H_{p-1}(X_p;\mathbb Z)\) is an isomorphism: it is so into \(B\) by step 1, and the long exact deletion-link sequence is an isomorphism from \(B\) because \(\widetilde H_{p-1}(L)=\widetilde H_{p-2}(L)=0\) when \(p\ge3\). The join \(D=\bigast_i S_i^0\) has its top orientation reversed by exchanging two join factors (the Koszul interchange sign is \((-1)^{(0+1)(0+1)}=-1\)). Thus \(S_p\) acts by the sign character. In the same long exact sequence \(\widetilde H_1(X_p;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H_0(L;\mathbb Z)\) because \(B\) has zero first homology for \(p\ge3\). The two connected components of \(L\), the full simplex on all \(y_i\) and the isolated vertex \(b\), are both preserved by every path permutation. Consequently \(S_p\) acts trivially on \(\widetilde H_1\).

**4. Pole reversal.** The involution \(\tau\) preserves the cross-polytope boundary \(D\) and reverses the orientation of each factor \(S_i^0=\{x_i,y_i\}\). Therefore its eigenvalue on \(\widetilde H_{p-1}(X_p;\mathbb Z)\) is \((-1)^p\). It remains to compute the eigenvalue \(\lambda\in\{\pm1\}\) on \(\widetilde H_1\). The simplicial Lefschetz number of \(\tau\) equals one: a nonempty independent face fixed as a set by \(\tau\) cannot contain either member of any adjacent pair \(\{x_i,y_i\}\), and the only remaining nonempty fixed face is the edge \(\{a,b\}\). Its contribution is \((-1)^1(-1)=+1\) because \(\tau\) reverses that oriented edge. On the other hand, the integral Lefschetz formula and step 2 give
\[
1=\operatorname{tr}(\tau|H_0)-\operatorname{tr}(\tau|H_1)+(-1)^{p-1}\operatorname{tr}(\tau|H_{p-1})=1-\lambda-1=-\lambda.
\]
Hence \(\lambda=-1\). Because arm permutations commute with \(\tau\), the two characters determine every \(S_p\times C_2\) element. This completes the proof.

**5. Double-cover motivation.** If \(F_m\) has hub \(h\) and petals \(h u_i v_i h\), its categorical product with \(K_2\) has lifted hubs \((h,0),(h,1)\). For each petal there are exactly two internally disjoint three-edge paths between these hubs, namely \((h,0)-(u_i,1)-(v_i,0)-(h,1)\) and \((h,0)-(v_i,1)-(u_i,0)-(h,1)\). All these \(2m\) paths have mutually disjoint interiors and there are no other edges, establishing \(F_m\times K_2\cong T_{2m}\). For \(m\ge2\), the preceding formula applies. The top homology then has the arm-permutation sign action and is fixed by pole reversal; the circle class is fixed by arm permutations and reversed by pole reversal.

## Verification
The standalone Python program `check.py` builds the complete independence complex from the graph definition, computes its simplicial boundaries modulo two for \(p=3,4,5\), and checks signed simplicial Lefschetz traces for all \(2p!\) elements when \(p=3,4,5,6\), plus eight specified elements when \(p=7\). The program reports `ALL_VERIFY_OK`. The exact integral and infinite-parameter results are proved above, independently of those finite computations. Ordinary (unreduced) mod-two Betti profiles include a degree-zero rank one; the theorem is stated in reduced homology. Reproduce using `python3 check.py` from the extracted package directory.

## Relationship to prior work
Carnero Bravo's 2026 theorem on generalized Mycielskians expresses their *nonequivariant* independence-complex homotopy types in terms of \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) and \(\operatorname{Ind}(G\times K_2)\); it does not compute the integral \(S_p\times C_2\)-module of \(\operatorname{Ind}(T_p)\). The double-cover identification above provides a natural input to that earlier formula, but no new generalized-Mycielskian theorem is claimed here. Przytycki and Silvero's 2024 Example 3.8 treats the ordinary three-arm theta graphs and their homotopy types, not the arbitrary-arm integral automorphism characters here. A separate published finding on graph-metric Vietoris–Rips persistence of length-three multi-theta graphs concerns Rips complexes, whose degree-one homology has rank depending on \(p\), rather than these independence complexes. These distinctions are by mathematical object and implication, not title matching.

## Limitations
No equivariant wedge equivalence is constructed; the theorem determines homology modules and ordinary homotopy type only. The hypothesis \(p\ge3\) cannot simply be dropped: at \(p=2\), \(T_p=C_6\) and both sphere summands are degree one, so there is no claimed pair of separate natural rank-one representations in different degrees. The result does not classify unequal arm lengths, nor arbitrary Kronecker double covers, nor the symmetry action on generalized Mycielskian complexes. Overlap with unindexed or inaccessible literature remains possible; no independent external audit has occurred.
