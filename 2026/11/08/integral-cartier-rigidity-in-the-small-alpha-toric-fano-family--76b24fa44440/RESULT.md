# Integral Cartier rigidity in the small-alpha toric Fano family

## Finding
Let \(n\geq3\) and let \(X_n\) be the complete complex toric variety defined by the face fan of the lattice polytope
\[
Q_n=\left\{(d_0,\ldots,d_n)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_i=0,\quad -(2n-1)\leq d_i\leq2\right\},
\]
inside \(N=\{d\in\mathbb Z^{n+1}:\sum d_i=0\}\). Write \(K_{X_n}\) for its canonical Weil divisor. The complete integral global divisor classification is
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)\cong\mathbb Z^{n^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)=\mathbb Z\,[-(4n-2)K_{X_n}],
\]
where \([-K_{X_n}]\) is primitive in the free class group. In particular,
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)/\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)
\cong\mathbb Z^{n^2-1}\oplus\mathbb Z/(4n-2)\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus the Picard rank is one although the Weil-class rank grows quadratically. The precise global Gorenstein index is \(4n-2\). The previously known descriptions of this family, its Gorenstein index, and the abstract Weil class group are inputs/comparisons; the new assertion is the **saturation of the Cartier lattice**, including its Picard generator and the resulting integral Weil–Cartier quotient.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties are over \(\mathbb C\). The fan consists of cones over all faces of \(Q_n\) (including proper faces and the origin). The lattice \(N\) is the cyclic-difference realization of the dual lattice used in Liu--Zhu [1]. Its dual is \(M=\mathbb Z^{n+1}/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\). Every ray is generated by
\[
v_{p,q}=2\mathbf1-(2n+1)e_p-e_q\in N,\qquad 0\leq p\ne q\leq n,
\]
where \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\), so there are exactly \(n(n+1)\) rays [1, Proposition 2.1]. The case \(n=2\) is excluded: its toric surface has a different Picard rank. The result concerns integral divisor classes, not all singularity types or Cox-ring relations.

## Proof
**1. Primitive rays generate the entire cocharacter lattice.** Put \(m=n+1\), \(\ell=2n-1\) and \(k=2n+1\). For any two distinct indices \(q,r\), choose an index \(p\notin\{q,r\}\). Then
\[
v_{p,q}-v_{p,r}=e_r-e_q.
\]
The differences \(e_r-e_q\) generate \(N\) integrally. By the standard toric divisor sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{n(n+1)}
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)\longrightarrow0,
\]
its cokernel is torsion-free and has rank \(n(n+1)-n=n^2\). This recovers the relevant specialization of Wu's class-group formula [2, Proposition 5.2(iv)] and is included to fix the integral normalization.

**2. Cartier support functions have only one rational parameter modulo characters.** For each \(i\), the polytope facet \(d_i=2\) contains exactly the vertices \(v_{p,q}\) with \(p,q\ne i\). If a torus-invariant \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier divisor has coefficients \(h_{p,q}\), linearity on its cone means that for some scalars \(c_i,u^{(i)}_j\),
\[
h_{p,q}=c_i-k u^{(i)}_p-u^{(i)}_q\qquad(p,q\ne i).
\]
Define, for \(p\ne q\),
\[
S_{p,q}=\frac{h_{p,q}+h_{q,p}}2,\qquad
A_{p,q}=\frac{h_{p,q}-h_{q,p}}2.
\]
Because \(m\geq4\), every triple of distinct indices \(p,q,r\) lies inside some upper facet (choose \(i\) outside the triple). The preceding representation on that facet gives the identity
\[
(k+1)A_{p,q}=(k-1)(S_{p,r}-S_{q,r})
\quad\text{for all distinct }p,q,r.\tag{1}
\]
Fixing \(p,q\), the right-hand difference is independent of the third index. Consequently the symmetric complete-graph edge weights \(S_{p,q}\) satisfy the four-point identities
\[
S_{p,r}+S_{q,s}=S_{p,s}+S_{q,r}
\quad(p,q,r,s\text{ pairwise distinct}).\tag{2}
\]
For completeness, (2) implies \(S_{p,q}=z_p+z_q\): choose three indices \(0,1,2\), define \(z_0=(S_{0,1}+S_{0,2}-S_{1,2})/2\), \(z_1=S_{0,1}-z_0\), \(z_2=S_{0,2}-z_0\), and \(z_j=S_{0,j}-z_0\) for all remaining indices. Applying (2) first to two base indices and then to arbitrary remaining pairs verifies all edges. Equation (1) now fixes
\[
A_{p,q}=\frac{k-1}{k+1}(z_p-z_q).
\]
Set \(u_p=-2z_p/(k+1)\). Combining symmetric and antisymmetric parts yields
\[
h_{p,q}=-k u_p-u_q.
\]
Equivalently, allowing a constant character normalization, all rational Cartier data have the form
\[
h_{p,q}=\langle u,v_{p,q}\rangle+t,
\qquad u\in M_{\mathbb Q},\ t\in\mathbb Q.\tag{3}
\]
Conversely, every such array is rational Cartier: every maximal cone lies over a facet \(d_i=-\ell\) or \(d_i=2\), and the constant function \(1\) is rationally linear on each of those cones. Hence (3) gives the **entire** rational Cartier space. Modulo principal characters, its dimension is one.

**3. Integral Cartier data force a divisible constant.** Suppose the coefficients \(h_{p,q}\) in (3) are integers. For distinct \(q,r\), choose \(p\) outside them. Then
\[
h_{p,q}-h_{p,r}=-u_q+u_r\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus \(u\) represents an element of \(M=\mathbb Z^m/\mathbb Z\mathbf1\); subtracting its associated principal divisor, we obtain an integral constant \(t=h_{p,q}-\langle u,v_{p,q}\rangle\in\mathbb Z\).

The rational supporting characters giving the value \(-1\) on the two types of facets are respectively \(-e_i/2\) on \(d_i=2\) and \(e_i/\ell\) on \(d_i=-\ell\), considered in \(M_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q^m/\mathbb Q\mathbf1\). The local linear character for (3) is \(u-t(-e_i/2)\) or \(u-t(e_i/\ell)\), respectively. It is integral on every facet **if and only if** \(2\mid t\) and \(\ell\mid t\). These conditions are necessary because \(e_i/r\notin M\) for every integer \(r>1\), and sufficient by the displayed local characters. Since \(\ell=2n-1\) is odd, this is equivalent to
\[
2(2n-1)\mid t.\tag{4}
\]
For \(h_{p,q}=1\), the invariant divisor is \(-K_{X_n}=\sum_{p\ne q}D_{p,q}\). Modulo principal divisors, (3)--(4) give precisely
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)=\mathbb Z\,[2(2n-1)(-K_{X_n})].
\]
The ampleness and \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier property of \(-K_{X_n}\) are known from [1], so this is a positive primitive Cartier polarization. The index \(2(2n-1)\) agrees with Wu [2, Proposition 5.2(i)]; it is not claimed as independently new.

**4. The quotient contains exactly one finite cyclic obstruction.** Let \(w=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb Z^{n(n+1)}\), the coefficient vector of \(-K_{X_n}\). The subgroup \(M+\mathbb Zw\) is saturated in \(\mathbb Z^{n(n+1)}\): an integral vector in its rational span has form (3), and the integrality argument of step 3 forces \(u\in M\) and \(t\in\mathbb Z\). Therefore \([-K_{X_n}]\) is primitive in \(\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)\), and
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)/\mathbb Z[-K_{X_n}]
\cong\mathbb Z^{n^2-1}.
\]
Taking the quotient instead by the Cartier generator in step 3 produces exactly the cyclic summand \(\mathbb Z/(4n-2)\mathbb Z\), together with the displayed free part. This proves the theorem.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library. It constructs every ray from the definition, forms the exact rational three-index equations (1), and checks that their solution space has dimension \(n+1\) for \(n=3,4,5,6\), matching the global character space plus one constant. It also checks the span of the vertices of each facet, the ray count, and the least positive common denominator of the facet characters. Expected output ends with `EXACT_CHECKS_PASS`. These are finite checks only; the unrestricted theorem is established by the four-index argument and integral lattice saturation above.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu--Zhu [1] construct the varieties, give the vertices and the anticanonical polytope, and prove K-polystability and the alpha invariant. Wu [2, §5] studies a broader family and **already states** the Gorenstein index, \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality criterion, and abstract class-group formula, all of which imply the corresponding assertions here (and are explicitly credited). An earlier public computation [3] determines local class groups at two types of torus fixed point in the same Liu--Zhu family. Those local calculations do not determine the integral global gluing of Cartier characters. The new content here is the uniform rank-one Cartier support-function lemma, identification of the **primitive global Picard generator**, and the resulting complete integral quotient by Picard. The latter is not merely the known local class group or the abstract class group.

## Limitations
The result excludes \(n=2\), where all cones are simplicial and the Picard rank is not one. It does not determine the full singular locus, the Cox relations, or the Picard groups of Wu's entire two-parameter family. Literature priority for the Picard subgroup remains subject to independent verification, particularly under the equivalent terminology of toric divisorial support functions of box-slice polytopes. The computations do not amount to a machine proof for arbitrary dimension. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
