# Review of the integral global Cartier lattice

## Correctness — PASS
The assertion is about the complex toric varieties with rays \(v_{p,q}=2\mathbf1-(2n+1)e_p-e_q\) for all \(n\ge3\). The ray differences give an integral generating set for the cocharacter lattice and hence a torsion-free Weil class group. The key new argument proves that the linearity equations on the upper facets force every rational Cartier coefficient array to be a principal character plus a scalar constant. The proof uses the three-index identity, the four-point additive-separation lemma, and the fact that every three indices lie in an upper facet. It checks the special boundary \(n=3\) explicitly within the uniform four-point proof. Integrality of a coefficient array forces the character and scalar to be integral; the upper and lower local facet characters respectively impose divisibility by \(2\) and \(2n-1\). The saturation argument then establishes that \([-K]\) is primitive in the Weil class group and yields the claimed quotient. Exact independent-in-code finite linear algebra for dimensions 3--6 corroborates the equations, but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
Liu--Zhu give the polar-box construction but not the global Picard lattice. Wu's more general construction *does* state the index, \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality, and the abstract Weil class group, so these pieces are expressly treated as prior art rather than independent discoveries. The indexed local-divisor-group result at the torus fixed points of the same family is also distinct: local class groups do not identify the global integral Cartier support-function sublattice. The substantive new statement is \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)=\mathbb Z[-(4n-2)K_{X_n}]\) with its primitive embedding and exact global quotient. Relevant full primary article sections and an expanded recent sequel were inspected; their claims do not imply this integral Picard identification without the new upper-facet gluing argument. A previously unlocated equivalent result under box-slice or hypersimplex terminology remains possible. The indexed local-result summary could be read, but its full deposit could not be retrieved, which limits the historical comparison.

## Value — PASS
For a natural K-polystable \(\mathbb Q\)-Fano family with known small alpha invariant, the result exhibits an extreme mismatch between Weil-class rank \(n^2\) and Picard rank one. It gives the primitive ample Cartier polarization and a full integral decomposition of the Weil--Cartier obstruction group, distinguishing finite index effects from free non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality. This global integrality is not supplied by the known abstract Weil group and local cyclic quotient descriptions alone, and it is relevant to line bundles and globally defined toric polarizations. The work is a natural complete classification of a structural invariant, not an arbitrary single-parameter computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary sources are Liu--Zhu (arXiv:2607.04005v1, full HTML §§2--4) and Wu (arXiv:2608.14115v2, full HTML §5, especially Proposition 5.2). A previously published local class-group result on this same family was compared by claim summary and its identifier `06b390f22d0b`; its full text could not be checked. The case \(n=2\) is excluded, as are the broader Wu family and all complete singularity classifications. Priority and mathematical validity have not received independent review.
