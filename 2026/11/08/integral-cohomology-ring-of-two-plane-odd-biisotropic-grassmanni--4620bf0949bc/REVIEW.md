# Scientific review: integral cohomology ring of two-plane odd biisotropic Grassmannians

## Correctness
**PASS.** The object is the smooth transverse intersection of two Plücker hyperplanes in \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n+1)\). The additive cohomology and torsion-freeness are supplied by Fonarev--Kuznetsov's Białynicki-Birula calculation. Integral weak Lefschetz supplies lower-degree Schubert bases. Pieri's rule gives a middle intersection matrix with determinant one, and in each upper degree supplies a square pairing matrix that is lower triangular with diagonal one. These integral pairing tests establish restriction surjectivity, not just rational surjectivity. Projection formula and perfect integral duality then identify the kernel with the annihilator of the squared Plücker class. The standalone checker reproduces the pairing identities in all degrees for \(2\le n\le12\); the universal proof is symbolic.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual historical-priority uncertainty.** The 2026 Fonarev--Kuznetsov paper explicitly says in Remark 1.2 that it only determines cohomology as graded abelian groups and that the ring is a separate question. This work gives an integral ring presentation for every two-plane case, not merely the already-known Betti numbers. Ordinary Grassmannian Schubert calculus, weak Lefschetz and the two-hyperplane description are acknowledged classical tools; the nontrivial new combination is the unimodular lattice argument in every degree. Searches for general linear-section ring presentations and isotropic Grassmannian Pieri rules produced no directly encompassing theorem, but broader earlier literature on special Grassmannian sections remains a priority risk. An unpublished 2026 manuscript cited by the motivating paper is not available for full comparison.

## Value
**PASS.** The source explicitly poses the multiplicative cohomology problem. The result answers it uniformly for the entire geometrically natural two-plane subfamily, giving integral products and not just rational cohomology or one isolated characteristic number. The determinant-one calculation prevents hidden finite-index obstructions in high-degree restriction. The different primitive Plücker degrees also prove that the additive comparison with an ordinary Grassmannian does not extend to a ring isomorphism.

## Scope and limitations
The finding does not determine cohomology rings for higher-dimensional biisotropic planes, quantum/equivariant structures, or non-general pencils. The annihilator formula is an effective presentation rather than a minimal generating set for its ideal. The prior-work search cannot establish historical uniqueness or independent mathematical validation.
