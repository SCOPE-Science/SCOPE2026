# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The defining graph, independent-four-set condition, and all 511 subset decisions are reconstructed by independent forbidden-set and maximum-matching methods. The explicit ascending-element matching has 192 face pairs, one critical vertex and four critical five-cells; complete 389-node, 1544-arrow acyclicity is verified. The resulting integral wedge is justified by the standard discrete-Morse theorem. Signed simplicial Lefschetz traces are computed for all eight geometric symmetries, but the integral claim does not rely on character equality. Four explicit top-dimensional integer cycles have zero boundaries, the 36-by-36 matched incidence matrix has determinant one, and a four-vector orbit under quarter-turn has unimodular determinant minus one and the correct diagonal-reflection sign. These exhibit an integral determinant-twisted permutation basis. Integral equivariant Alexander duality yields the dual integral permutation lattice, and direct dual-complex traces independently verify its character. The accompanying checker replays these finite claims without external dependencies.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The original 2022/2024 total-cut paper tabulates the rank four for the three-by-three total-four-cut complex. That rank, and the nonequivariant four-sphere conclusion obtainable using Alexander duality and standard topology, are NOT presented as novel. The new claim is the exact integral dihedral representation lattice, in both dual formulations. The 2024/2026 grid results cover total three-cut, the 2026 robust-clique results cover thresholds two and three and edge-glued higher thresholds, and the 2026 dual-complex paper covers different graph families or total-two-cut grids. Searches under robust-clique, bounded-independence, total-cut, and dihedral-representation aliases found no primary result deriving this character. Relevant related results on ordinary cut complexes or three-cut complexes concern different simplicial complexes. An unindexed direct character calculation remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The natural eight-element geometric symmetry group acts on a topological invariant whose rank was already known. Identifying an integral permutation-lattice structure, rather than supplying another rank or only a rational character, distinguishes the two types of reflection (diagonal versus axis) and explains their traces by a geometric four-square action. The dual complex has a one-dimensional invariant subspace while the robust complex has none, a distinction relevant to quotient and equivariant computations. The example lies at the first square-grid corner-gluing case not covered by the published higher-threshold edge-gluing homotopy theorem. It is a motivated exact finite equivariant classification, not a claim of a large-parameter advance.
