# Integral dihedral homology of the squared-cycle neighborhood complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge9\), set \(G_n=C_n^2\), the graph with vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and an edge between vertices of cyclic distance one or two. Its **open** neighborhood complex \(K_n=\mathcal N(G_n)\) has a natural dihedral action generated by \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\). Denote by \(\mathbb Z_+\) the trivial integral representation and by \(\mathbb Z_{\varepsilon}\) the one-dimensional representation on which rotations act as \(+1\) and reflections act as \(-1\). Then
\[
\widetilde H_j(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z_{\varepsilon}^{\oplus2},&j=1,\\
\mathbb Z_+,&j=2,\\
0,&j\notin\{1,2\}.
\end{cases}
\]
Moreover, the full finite symmetry quotient has \(H^*(|K_n|/D_n;\mathbb Q)\cong H^*(S^2;\mathbb Q)\) as graded rational vector spaces (indeed as graded rings, since only degrees zero and two survive). The integral homology of the quotient is not asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
All indices are reduced modulo \(n\). The graph is simple, and \(K_n\) contains precisely those nonempty vertex subsets having a common graph neighbor. We take simplicial chain groups with their ordinary signed orientation conventions. The result is uniform for every \(n\ge9\), including both parities. It concerns the natural dihedral group of order \(2n\); no claim about a larger full automorphism group of any exceptional graph is needed.

## Proof
**1. Explicit collapse.** The maximal simplices of \(K_n\) are the \(n\) distinct tetrahedra
\[
Q_i=\{i-2,i-1,i+1,i+2\}.
\]
For each \(i\), the triangle \(B_i=\{i-2,i-1,i+2\}\) has unique common graph neighbor \(i\), so it is a free facet of \(Q_i\). To verify the uniqueness, intersect the four-neighbor sets: \(N(i-2)\cap N(i-1)=\{i-3,i\}\), and only \(i\) also belongs to \(N(i+2)\). These equalities hold for \(n\ge9\). Remove the elementary free pairs \((B_i,Q_i)\) for all \(i\), in any order.

Next take \(E_i=\{i-2,i+2\}\). The two vertices of \(E_i\) have exactly one common graph neighbor, namely \(i\), because \(N(i-2)\cap N(i+2)=\{i\}\). Their only original cofaces of dimension two are \(B_i\) and \(C_i=\{i-2,i+1,i+2\}\). After removing \(B_i\), the edge \(E_i\) is free in \(C_i\); remove the pairs \((E_i,C_i)\). These are valid \(2n\) elementary collapses, and every remaining maximal simplex belongs to
\[
T_n=\big\langle \{i,i+1,i+3\},\{i,i+2,i+3\}:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\big\rangle.
\]
Direct inspection of the four triangles of each \(Q_i\), and the edge sets, shows that the remaining complex is exactly \(T_n\). The subcomplex \(T_n\) is invariant under the full dihedral action, so its inclusion is equivariant. We do **not** assert that each intermediate collapse is equivariant.

**2. It is a torus.** The face vector of \(T_n\) is \((n,3n,2n)\), where the edges have differences one, two, or three. Each vertex link is the six-cycle on its six neighboring vertices; this follows by listing the six triangles containing that vertex. The one-skeleton contains every edge \(\{i,i+1\}\), so \(T_n\) is a connected closed triangulated surface. It has Euler characteristic \(n-3n+2n=0\). Its oriented fundamental two-cycle is
\[
F_n=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z}
\big([i,i+1,i+3]-[i,i+2,i+3]\big).
\]
The boundary cancels exactly: in each summand, edges of difference three cancel directly, and the remaining edges of differences one and two cancel after cyclic shifts of the index. Every facet occurs with coefficient \(\pm1\), so \(F_n\) is a primitive orienting cycle. Thus the connected surface is orientable of Euler characteristic zero, hence a torus. Consequently the only nonzero reduced homology groups are \(\mathbb Z^2\) in degree one and \(\mathbb Z\) in degree two, with no torsion.

**3. Action in degree two.** Rotations preserve \(F_n\) by reindexing. Each reflection \(r^as\) sends a positively ordered facet \([i,i+1,i+3]\) to the **negative** of a facet of the second type and sends \([i,i+2,i+3]\) to the **negative** of a facet of the first type. Accordingly, reflections also preserve \(F_n\). The full dihedral action on \(H_2\) is trivial.

**4. Action in degree one.** The signed simplicial Lefschetz number of a symmetry \(g\), computed on the invariant torus triangulation, is \(L(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g|H_0)-\operatorname{tr}(g|H_1)+\operatorname{tr}(g|H_2)=2-\operatorname{tr}(g|H_1)\). Each nontrivial rotation fixes no simplex setwise: no edge of cyclic length at most three is preserved by a nontrivial rotation for \(n\ge9\), and each triangular facet has pairwise distinct cyclic edge lengths one, two and three, so it is not rotationally stabilized either. Thus \(L(r^a)=0\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(r^a|H_1)=2\), including the identity by the Euler characteristic. Since each such integral homology matrix has finite order, it is semisimple over \(\mathbb C\); trace two in rank two forces the identity matrix. Hence **all rotations act identically on integral first homology**.

For \(g=r^as\), no triangular facet is setwise fixed: a reflection stabilizing a triangle would exchange two vertices and thus force two equal incident cyclic edge lengths, but the three lengths are distinct. Every setwise fixed edge is swapped end-for-end and contributes \(-1\) to the trace on oriented one-chains. If \(n\) is odd, there is one fixed vertex and three reversed edges (one of each cyclic length one, two and three). If \(n\) is even and \(a\) is even, there are two fixed vertices and two reversed edges (of cyclic length two). If \(n\) is even and \(a\) is odd, there are no fixed vertices and four reversed edges (two of length one and two of length three). Thus in every case \(L(g)=4\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(g|H_1)=-2\). A finite-order rank-two integral matrix with trace \(-2\) is \(-I\), proving the claimed integral representation \(\mathbb Z_{\varepsilon}^{\oplus2}\).

**5. Rational quotient.** For any finite group acting on a finite complex, averaging cochains over characteristic zero identifies the rational cohomology of its orbit space with the invariant rational cohomology of the original complex. In degree one, reflection acts as \(-I\) and leaves no invariants; in degree two all symmetries fix the orientation class. The asserted rational quotient Betti numbers follow.

## Verification
The included standalone `verify.py` uses the Python standard library. It reconstructs the original neighborhood complex and both complete elementary-collapse sequences, verifies that the remainder equals the stated triangulated torus, checks all six-vertex links, and directly computes signed simplicial Lefschetz numbers for every dihedral element, for all \(9\le n\le80\). The script checks 72 graph sizes and 3,204 rotations plus 3,204 reflections. Run with `python3 verify.py`; its expected output is in `verification_output.txt`. These checks confirm the finite cases but the preceding uniform combinatorial proof, not extrapolation, establishes the infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work
Datta and Upadhyay (first public 2004-03-25, arXiv:math/0403433), Example 1 and Lemma 2.2, already constructed the exact same triangulated torus as their \(T_{n,1,2}\), proved it is a torus, and identified its natural dihedral automorphism group. **Neither the torus construction nor the existence of the dihedral group is claimed as new.** Kozlov (2005, arXiv:math/0503613), a foundational source for neighborhood complexes and explicit simple-homotopy collapse algorithms, motivates studying concrete collapses in these complexes. Shukla (first public 2018-02-13, arXiv:1802.04526), Theorem 2.5(C), Lemma 4.2, and Theorem 4.4, treats more general four-regular circulant neighborhood complexes and constructs surface models. Specializing these descriptions to the present graphs can recover the underlying surface information. **That nonequivariant surface fact is not claimed as new.** The present claim is the complete **integral dihedral action** on both homology degrees, with a uniform signed Lefschetz proof and full-symmetry rational quotient calculation. The inspected published statements do not determine those integral representation matrices or quotient invariants.

## Limitations
No integral cohomology ring or integral homology of the orbit space is calculated. The collapse sequence itself is not asserted to be equivariant, only the inclusion of the dihedrally invariant terminal subcomplex. The publication-priority comparison is limited to the inspected sources and searchable records; an equivalent unindexed dihedral-equivariant calculation could exist.
