# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof is valid uniformly for every integer \(n\ge9\). Explicit common-neighbor intersections make exactly \(2n\) named collapse pairs free; the terminal dihedrally invariant subcomplex has face vector \((n,3n,2n)\), connected one-skeleton, links that are six-cycles and a primitive integral orientation cycle. Those properties prove it is a torus and its integral homology is torsion-free. The signed Lefschetz counts cover both parity classes and every conjugacy type of reflection; finite-order rank-two matrices with traces \(2\) or \(-2\) are respectively \(I\) or \(-I\). The quotient calculation uses finite-group rational invariants, not any unwarranted integral transfer assertion. A standalone finite checker validates the mechanism for 72 sizes; the uniform proof does not depend on extrapolating those checks.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
Datta and Upadhyay (2004) already constructed exactly the terminal torus \(T_{n,1,2}\), proved its topology and natural dihedral automorphism group; none of these known statements is claimed as new. The earlier formal deformation methods of Kozlov (2005) are foundational, not a calculation of this dihedral action. Shukla (2018), especially Theorem 2.5(C), Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.4, already provides surface calculations for general four-regular circulant neighborhood complexes; the underlying nonequivariant torus computation is not reclaimed. The complete integral two-generator dihedral module and the rational homology of the full symmetry quotient require symmetry trace information absent from the inspected primary text. Search comparisons included the published graph-neighborhood-complex record index, direct equivalent-formulation searches, neighborhood complexes of cycle powers, and broader toroidal quotient results. Remaining priority risk: an equivalent equivariant specialization may exist in other literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A natural infinite family of four-regular circulant graphs has symmetry-sensitive homology not determined by ordinary genus or Betti numbers. The two nonzero first-homology classes are separately changed in sign by every reflection, while the top orienting class is preserved. This makes a complete integral dihedral representation classification and a sharp, uniform rational homology classification of the orbit spaces; it is not an arbitrary numerical slice or a routine renaming of an existing nonequivariant surface theorem.

## Scientific limitations
The result does not calculate integral quotient homology, equivariant homotopy type, or full integral quotient cup products. Searches and reviewed accessible sources cannot prove publication priority absolutely. The finite checker verifies examples but does not replace the proof of the all-parameter theorem.
