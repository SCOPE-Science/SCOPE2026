# Integral dihedral symmetry on the first critical Vietoris–Rips cycle-power wedge

## Finding
For each integer \(k\ge2\), put \(n=3k\). Let \(C_n\) have vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\) with shortest-path metric, and let \(X_k=\operatorname{VR}_{\le k}(C_n)\), the flag complex on pairs at cyclic distance at most \(k\). With \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\), the canonical dihedral group \(D_n\) acts on \(X_k\).

Write \(P_k=\mathbb Z[e_0,\ldots,e_{k-1}]\), indexed cyclically, with the action \(r(e_j)=e_{j+1}\) and \(s(e_j)=e_{-j}\). Write \(A_k=\ker(\sum:P_k\to\mathbb Z)\) for its augmentation lattice, and \(\varepsilon\) for the orientation character with \(\varepsilon(r)=1\) and \(\varepsilon(s)=-1\). Then there is a natural integral isomorphism of \(D_n\)-modules
\[
\widetilde H_2(X_k;\mathbb Z)\cong\varepsilon\otimes A_k.
\]
All other reduced integral homology groups vanish. This is a refinement of the previously known nonequivariant homotopy equivalence \(X_k\simeq\bigvee^{k-1}S^2\), which is **not** claimed as new.

In particular, for all integers \(a\), the complete rational homology character is
\[
\operatorname{tr}(r^a)=\begin{cases}k-1&k\mid a,\\-1&k\nmid a,\end{cases}
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(r^as)=1-\#\{j\in\mathbb Z/k:2j\equiv a\pmod{k}\}.
\]
For odd \(k\) every reflection has trace zero. For even \(k\), reflections with even \(a\) have trace \(-1\), and those with odd \(a\) have trace \(1\). The order-three rotation \(r^k\) acts trivially on this integral homology lattice; hence this action factors through the dihedral group on \(k\) letters. The fixed subspace of \(\widetilde H_2(X_k;\mathbb Q)\) under all rotations, and therefore under all dihedral symmetries, is zero.

## Assumptions and scope
The cycle metric is the shortest-path metric with unit edges, and the Vietoris–Rips radius is **inclusive**. The variable \(k\) is an arbitrary integer \(\ge2\), with no upper cutoff. The module isomorphism is over \(\mathbb Z\), not only after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\). Statements concern the natural dihedral action; there is no assertion about the complete abstract simplicial automorphism group of \(X_k\).

## Proof
**1. Maximal-clique dichotomy.** A simplex of \(X_k\) is either contained in one of the consecutive arcs
\[
S_i=\{i,i+1,\ldots,i+k\}\quad(i\in\mathbb Z/3k),
\]
or is one of the exceptional triangles
\[
T_j=\{j,j+k,j+2k\}\quad(j\in\mathbb Z/k).
\]
For completeness, rotate any nonempty clique to contain vertex \(0\). Its other vertices lie in the positive block \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\) or the negative block \(\{2k,\ldots,3k-1\}\). If only one block is occupied, the clique lies in an arc of length \(k\). Otherwise let \(a\) be the largest positive vertex and \(b\) the smallest negative one. Adjacency of these vertices requires \(b-a\le k\) or \(b-a\ge2k\). In the first case, the inequalities \(a\le k\), \(b\ge2k\) force \(a=k\), \(b=2k\), and no additional positive or negative vertex is adjacent to both, giving the exceptional triangle. In the second case the whole clique is contained in the wraparound arc from \(b\) through \(0\) to \(a\), of length \(3k-b+a\le k\). This proves the dichotomy. For \(k\ge2\), the \(3k\) consecutive arcs and the \(k\) exceptional triangles are precisely the maximal cliques, and an exceptional triangle is not a face of any higher-dimensional simplex.

**2. A circle subcomplex.** Let \(Y_k\subset X_k\) be generated by the consecutive arc simplices \(S_i\). It is the nerve of the \(3k\) equally spaced closed circular arcs of length \(k/(3k)=1/3\). Every nonempty finite intersection of those arcs is an interval or a point: two arcs shorter than half the circumference cannot produce a disconnected intersection, and intersecting further arcs preserves this property within the first arc. Those arcs cover the full circle, so the nerve theorem gives \(Y_k\simeq S^1\), with \(H_1(Y_k;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), no higher homology, and the induced rotation and reflection actions on \(H_1\) equal to \(+1\) and \(-1\), respectively.

To fix orientation and avoid any ambiguity in the attaching degrees, send the vertex \(i\) to \(\zeta_i=\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\,i/(3k))\), extend affinely over each simplex of \(Y_k\) in \(\mathbb C\), and radially normalize to \(S^1\). Each simplex lies in a closed arc of angular width \(2\pi/3\), so its convex hull avoids the origin. The restriction to the cycle's edges winds once positively; because \(H_1(Y_k;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), this supplies its positive integral generator. The map is dihedral-equivariant under plane rotations and conjugation.

**3. Exact relative sequence.** By Step 1, \(X_k\) is obtained from \(Y_k\) by attaching exactly the \(k\) exceptional two-simplices, with their full boundaries already lying in \(Y_k\). Orient each as \(t_j=[j,j+k,j+2k]\). Thus the relative chain complex has one free group \(C_2(X_k,Y_k;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[t_0,\ldots,t_{k-1}]\), with zero groups in all other relative degrees. Under the circle map from Step 2, each boundary \(\partial t_j\) traverses three successive 120-degree chords of the unit circle and winds once positively. Hence the connecting map in the integral homology exact sequence is precisely
\[
\partial:\mathbb Z[t_0,\ldots,t_{k-1}]\longrightarrow H_1(Y_k;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,
\qquad \partial(t_j)=1.
\]
It is surjective. Exactness therefore gives \(\widetilde H_2(X_k;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial\), \(\widetilde H_1(X_k;\mathbb Z)=0\), and vanishing in all remaining reduced degrees. In particular there is no torsion.

**4. Equivariant signs.** The actions on oriented relative two-simplices are
\[
r(t_j)=t_{j+1},\qquad s(t_j)=-t_{-j}.
\]
The first identity includes the wraparound case, because a cyclic three-vertex permutation is even. The second identity is the sign of reversed vertex order. The target \(H_1(Y_k;\mathbb Z)\) carries the orientation character, so the augmentation connecting map is equivariant. Its kernel is exactly \(\varepsilon\otimes A_k\) over \(\mathbb Z\), without choosing noncanonical rational splittings.

**5. Character and invariants.** The ordinary permutation character of \(P_k\) at a group element equals the number of fixed letters in \(\mathbb Z/k\). Subtracting the invariant constant line gives the character of \(A_k\); multiplying by the orientation sign gives the character of \(\varepsilon\otimes A_k\). Rotation by \(a\) fixes every letter when \(k\mid a\), and fixes no letter otherwise. A reflection \(r^as\) fixes exactly the letters solving \(2j\equiv a\pmod k\). These facts give all displayed trace formulas. The only rotation-invariant vectors in \(P_k\otimes\mathbb Q\) are constant, which have zero intersection with \(A_k\otimes\mathbb Q\). This proves the invariant-space claim.

## Verification
The included standard-library-only program `critical_cycle_verify.py` independently constructs \(C_{3k}^k\) from distances, enumerates maximal cliques by a pivoted Bron–Kerbosch search, expands all nonempty faces, confirms the exact maximal-clique decomposition and reduced Euler characteristics, and evaluates **signed reduced Lefschetz traces** for every rotation and reflection when \(2\le k\le8\). It checks \(12+18+24+30+36+42+48=210\) dihedral actions. The recorded exact output is `critical_cycle_verify.out`. The code's finite checks are cross-checks, not the proof for arbitrary \(k\); the uniform argument above is the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek's *Clique complexes and graph powers* (first public arXiv version 2011-04-03, Corollary 6.7) already determines the nonequivariant homotopy type of clique complexes of cycle powers, including the case \(n=3k\) and radius \(k\). Adamaszek–Adams–Frick–Peterson–Previte-Johnson's *Nerve complexes of circular arcs* (first public arXiv version 2014-10-16, Theorem 8.2) explicitly recalls this classification; its Example 8.3 describes the nine-vertex special case as a circle subcomplex with three attached triangles. **Neither the known wedge dimension nor the nine-vertex attaching picture is asserted as new.**

The 2014 paper, Section 7, also determines dihedral homology actions for its **nerve** complexes \(\mathcal N(n,k)\). Section 8 compares their nonequivariant homotopy types with the **clique/Rips** complexes \(\overline{\mathcal N}(n+k,k)\) via a map reducing vertex indices modulo \(n\) (Theorem 8.5). In the present critical case that map goes from \(3k\) to \(2k\) vertices and is not equivariant for the full rotation of \(D_{3k}\). Consequently the nerve-representation formulas do not themselves assert the integral \(D_{3k}\)-lattice here. The new content is its explicit uniform full-dihedral identification, including the integral orientation twist, kernel of the order-three rotation, and parity-sensitive reflection character.

Related searchable records concern three-cut complexes of cycle powers, cycle matching complexes, bounded-independence complexes and other Rips graph families; their homology characters cannot directly identify this Rips complex's full dihedral module.

## Limitations
Only the natural dihedral action is classified. No equivariant homotopy equivalence or general formula away from this first one-third-radius critical regime is claimed. The homotopy type and the existence of the exceptional triangles are prior results; the integral equivariant **kernel identification** and character formula are the scientific claim assessed here. Equivalent unindexed literature cannot be ruled out without independent publication-priority review.
