# An explicit two-dimensional spine for three unordered points on the Petersen graph

Let \(P=\mathrm{KG}(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph: its vertices are the two-element subsets of a five-element set, with disjointness as adjacency. Let \(B_3(P)\) be the configuration space of unordered triples of distinct points in the geometric graph. Bisect each edge of \(P\) and take the unordered discrete cubical configuration complex \(X\) on this subdivision.

**Theorem.** The complex \(X\) collapses by 2,880 elementary free-face pairs, all in dimensions \((2,3)\), onto a two-dimensional cubical subcomplex with cell counts \((2300,7590,5310)\). This subcomplex is a deformation retract of \(B_3(P)\) under the inclusion determined by the bisection. No CW complex of dimension less than two has this homotopy type.

## Proof

There are 25 vertices and 30 edges after bisection. The original ten vertices have degree three and the added vertices have degree two. A path between distinct essential vertices has at least two subdivided edges. A noncontractible edge loop has at least ten subdivided edges, since the Petersen graph has girth five. The two bounds required at particle number three are two and four, respectively. Prue–Scrimshaw's stable-equivalence theorem, including its unordered version, therefore gives a deformation retraction \(B_3(P)\to X\).

A \(k\)-cube uses \(k\) open edges with pairwise disjoint closures and \(3-k\) distinct vertices outside their endpoints. These cells are regular: the closed edges and fixed vertices of a cell have disjoint supports, so forgetting the particle order does not identify any two points of its closed product cube. Replacing one open edge by either endpoint gives its two corresponding codimension-one faces. This gives precisely \((2300,7590,8190,2880)\) cells in dimensions zero through three. In the checker, label original vertices lexicographically by their two-element subsets; order original edges as lexicographically ordered pairs of vertex indices, put new vertex \(10+i\) in original edge \(i\), and list its two half edges in ascending order of original endpoints. Enumerate cells by increasing edge-index tuple and then by increasing fixed-vertex tuple, in lexicographic order.

The initial queue consists of all squares with exactly one cubic coface, in enumeration order. Repeatedly take the first queued square, skipping it if it has been removed or no longer has exactly one remaining cubic coface. Otherwise delete the square and that cube; visit that cube's six faces in order of moving-edge index and then of endpoints, updating the coface lists and appending squares that now have exactly one coface. Every actual deletion is an elementary collapse: the square has just one surviving coface, the latter is maximal, and the cubical incidence is regular. No other surviving closed cell contains the deleted square. In particular, the cells that remain always form a subcomplex.

The accompanying standalone checker constructs the full incidence relation and verifies that the queue eliminates every one of the 2,880 cubes with a distinct square. No cells of dimension at most one are removed. The surviving counts are thus \((2300,7590,8190-2880)\). Because the discrete model is a deformation retract of the continuous space and each elementary collapse is a deformation retraction, so is the surviving spine. The elementary collapse statement refers to the cubical model, not to a proposed cubulation of the whole continuous space.

The spine is connected (its 1-skeleton is checked directly); its Euler characteristic is \(2300-7590+5310=20\). The Petersen graph is 3-vertex-connected and nonplanar: deleting any two vertices leaves it connected, as checked by enumeration, and its 15 edges violate the planar girth-five bound \(e\le 5(v-2)/3\) for \(v=10\). Ko–Park's integral first-homology formula for a simple triconnected nonplanar graph gives the graph cycle rank \(15-10+1=6\) and one exchange summand of order two. Consequently \(H_1(B_3(P);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^6\oplus\mathbb Z/2\). In a two-dimensional CW complex, \(H_2\) is a subgroup of the free cellular 2-chain group and is free. Euler characteristic therefore gives \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{25}\). Higher groups vanish, and the nonzero second homology proves minimality of the dimension.

## Prior work and scope

The degree-two rational dimension 25 and rational degree-three vanishing were already established by the earlier Petersen three-particle symmetry computation, which determines the full \(S_5\)-character. The first integral group follows from Ko–Park. The contribution here is the explicit free-face collapse to a cubical spine; integral degree-two freeness follows from that geometric conclusion. Rational vanishing alone would not imply the existence of such a spine or the absence of integral torsion. The proof concerns only three unordered points on this graph and does not classify the configuration space up to homotopy, compute its cohomology ring, or describe an equivariant collapse.
