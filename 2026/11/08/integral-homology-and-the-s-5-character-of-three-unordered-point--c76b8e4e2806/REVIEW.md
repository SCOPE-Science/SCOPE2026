# Scientific scope and prior-work comparison

## Correctness of the narrowed claim

The once-subdivided K5 satisfies the three-particle cubical discretization criterion. An exact 15-vertex, 20-edge cell enumeration yields chain ranks (455,1560,1650,520), and the signed integral differentials square to zero. All 520 columns of the degree-three differential are eliminated by integral unit pivots, so its image is a primitive direct summand of C2. The second homology embeds in the free quotient C2/im(d3), and is therefore torsion-free. The degree-two differential has rank 1,100 by a separate 1,098-unit-pivot plus (1,2) residual Smith calculation. It follows that H2 is free of rank 30. The finite certificate supports the algebraic calculation; the topological model identification uses the published subdivision theorem.

## Exact prior mathematical coverage

The October 18, 2026 complete K5 unordered-three-point result, cited in RESULT.md, already provides the full degree-two rational S5 character (30,0,-2,0,0,0,0), all five Specht constituents and rational Betti ranks. Those results are not new here. Ko and Park's general graph braid first-homology theorem covers the rank-six free term and order-two first-homology torsion. Ramos and Yun's 2026 Table 8 addresses ordered three-point K5 rational representations, which do not determine integral torsion of the unordered quotient. Earlier wheel-graph homology calculations, including K4, do not specialize to this exact graph. The remaining non-implied result is the torsion-free integral H2 calculation.

## Mathematical motivation and limitations

A canonical nonplanar complete graph has torsion in H1, while integral torsion in higher graph-configuration homology is a genuine phenomenon. Showing that this particular H2 has no torsion is a meaningful exact integer invariant, not a new rational representation theorem. This small-case result does not classify all complete graphs, establish an integral S5 lattice, or provide a generic torsion-free theorem. Historical priority for the integral computation remains best-of-knowledge because an unindexed earlier Smith-form computation might exist.
