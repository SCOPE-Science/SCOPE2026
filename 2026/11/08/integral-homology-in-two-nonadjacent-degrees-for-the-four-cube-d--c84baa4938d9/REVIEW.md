# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The defining face predicate was reconstructed from the closed neighborhoods of the four-cube and checked against the complementary dominating-set predicate on all \(65{,}536\) subsets. Both yield exactly \(45{,}706\) nonempty faces. A deterministic staged matching yields \(22{,}847\) disjoint pairs and critical dimensions \(\{0:1,7:10,9:1\}\). The *entire* \(329{,}552\)-incidence directed Hasse graph topologically sorts; an alternative pivot ordering gives the same critical vector and independently passes its own complete acyclicity check. Forman's theorem therefore supplies a Morse CW model whose cellular chains have no nonzero differential in degrees seven or nine. This proves the precise torsion-free integral homology and six-connectivity; it does not resolve the nine-cell's attaching homotopy class. The standalone checker and exact recorded output are included.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

Marietti–Testa's forest case [1] and Taylan's chordal case [2] cannot cover the cyclic, nonchordal four-cube. Matsushita–Wakatsuki [3] relate the dominance complex to the neighborhood complex of the **complement** but do not evaluate that object for the four-cube. Closely related published records were inspected at the level of complete scientific statements and proofs: the independence complex of the four-cube has a forbidden-edge face predicate [4], its open-neighborhood complex uses the **original** rather than complement graph [5], the total three-cut complex has independent-triple complement facets [6], and the bounded-domination graph complex uses graph edges as simplicial vertices [7]. None of those statements implies the current two-degree homology classification. Distinct naming alone is not relied on; definitions and implications are compared. Equivalent uncatalogued literature remains a bibliographic risk, not an established novelty proof.

## Value — PASS

The four-dimensional cube is the smallest hypercube in dimensions one through four whose dominance complex has reduced integral homology in **two distinct dimensions**: exhaustive Morse checks of the smaller cubes give their respective single-degree classes. A complete integral classification for a canonical symmetric graph is a natural benchmark for the topology of dominating-set systems; the separation between degrees seven and nine raises a genuine attaching-map question not settled by Betti numbers. Torsion-freeness, precise connectivity and the full homology groups are stronger than face numbers or Euler characteristic alone. The calculation is a natural complete finite invariant rather than a parameter-fitted slice or a routine relabeling of a known table.
