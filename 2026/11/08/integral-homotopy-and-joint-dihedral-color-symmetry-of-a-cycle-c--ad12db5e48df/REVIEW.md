# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The 24-vertex categorical graph is given by an exact adjacency predicate, not a named-graph guess. The standalone verifier enumerates 18,721 nonempty independence faces; checks every covering arrow of the face poset for acyclicity after 9,353 Morse pairings; and leaves one zero-, six four-, and eight five-dimensional critical cells. Exact signed reduction yields a six-by-eight integer boundary matrix of rank five with a five-by-five unit determinant minor, eliminating integral torsion and ensuring that the last three five-cell attaching degrees vanish. This proves an integral wedge, not just finite-field Betti numbers. An independent mod-two boundary rank computation confirms the homology dimensions. The 96 natural graph permutations and their equivariant Lefschetz numbers are exhaustively checked, and possible rational characters are separated by rank-one classification and character orthogonality. Risk: reliance on the publicly supplied finite verifier and the standard Forman chain-reduction theorem; no independent external audit.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected material
Engström (arXiv:math/0508148, 2005) develops general independence-complex methods, without evaluating this categorical product. Goyal–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:1905.06926, 2019) handle products of complete graphs and related constructions, not this cycle–complete example. The most relevant full text, Antolín Camarena–Carnero Bravo (arXiv:2307.12401; journal 2024), explicitly lists this *same graph* among examples with nonzero free homology in two degrees and asks if cycle–complete product homology is torsion-free (Question 3.19). Their results allow undetermined Moore-space summands and do not give the exact integral homotopy type or the joint natural dihedral–color rational character. A nearby mathematical record on the different product \(C_7\times K_4\) does not dominate this case. Exact wording, parameter, categorical/Cartesian/line-graph dualities, and character searches did not retrieve an implication of the new result. Risk: an unindexed same-parameter computation or more powerful unknown theorem could preempt publication priority; this self-review does not certify priority.

## Value — PASS
The 2024 article itself explicitly identifies this product among unresolved adjacent-degree free-homology examples and asks a torsion-freeness question. Determining its *integral* wedge-of-spheres homotopy type settles the meaningful concrete ambiguity and distinguishes torsion-free from potential Moore-space behavior. The joint symmetry characters go beyond known free homology support and are informative for representation comparisons of graph-product independence complexes. It is a deliberately chosen natural example, not a random slice; no unjustified general-family implication is claimed.

## Scope and limitations
Only the finite product and its natural 96-element subgroup action are classified. No family-wide theorem, integral representation-lattice decomposition, equivariant homotopy splitting, or independent priority audit is asserted.
