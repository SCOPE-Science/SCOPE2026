# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The complete 25-vertex complex is enumerated in two independent ways. Its 4,215 nonempty simplices admit an explicit 2,074-pair Morse matching with one critical vertex, 51 critical three-cells and 15 critical four-cells. Every one of 17,225 Hasse incidences is oriented, and an exhaustive 4,215-vertex topological sort certifies acyclicity. The 51-by-15 signed **integral** Morse attaching matrix is zero entry by entry, not merely mod two. The only four-cell attaching maps target a wedge of three-spheres, where the Hurewicz isomorphism identifies their classes with these degree columns. Thus all attaching maps vanish and the wedge decomposition follows. Direct mod-two boundary ranks are an independent check. Finite enumeration is exhaustive for the stated one graph and is not a theorem for other sizes.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** The exact same 5-by-5 toroidal triangular complex occurs in Huijse–Mehta–Moran–Schoutens–Vala (arXiv:1112.3314, §II and Appendix B.4), whose already published 51 and 15 rational dimensions are acknowledged and not new. The central new assertion is the **integral homotopy equivalence**; in particular, neither the 66-state table nor cohomology-rank calculations imply the attaching maps vanish. Jonsson's triangular-grid cross-cycle theorem and Adamaszek's special-cycle constructions do not state this finite integral homotopy type. Targeted published-literature and mathematical-index searches revealed no same-claim result. Residual risk: equivalent unindexed or unpublished homotopy calculation; full historical priority is not certified.

**Value — PASS.** The five-by-five periodic triangular grid is a natural small, fully two-dimensional instance of the hard-hexagon lattice with known supersymmetric-fermion ground-state dimensions. Its previously known adjacent rational homology groups leave substantial possible higher attaching-map ambiguity. An explicit full integral wedge decomposition resolves that gap, giving information strictly beyond those known ground-state counts while leaving the larger grid-classification problem open. No application beyond this topological classification is claimed.

**Boundaries.** This is a nonequivariant, one-graph theorem. It makes no statement about larger tori, arbitrary twisted boundary conditions, integral symmetry modules or an infinite-lattice classification. Independent validation and definitive literature-priority verification have not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
