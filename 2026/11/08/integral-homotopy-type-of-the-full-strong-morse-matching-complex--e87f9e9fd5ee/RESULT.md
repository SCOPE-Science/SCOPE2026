# Integral homotopy type of the full strong Morse matching complex of the nine-cycle

## Finding
Let \(C_9\) be the *unfilled* cycle with nine vertices, regarded as a one-dimensional simplicial complex. Its **full strong discrete-Morse matching complex** satisfies
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal{SM}(C_9)|\simeq\bigvee^{5}S^4.}
\]
Moreover, it admits an **optimal acyclic face-poset matching** with exactly one critical vertex, five critical four-simplices, and no other critical faces. Hence \(\widetilde H_4(\mathcal{SM}(C_9);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^5\), with all other reduced integral homology zero.

There are exactly 2,634 nonempty faces. The full complex has 36 simplices not present in the pure strong-matching subcomplex: 18 three-simplices and 18 four-simplices. In contrast, the previously established pure subcomplex has homotopy type \(S^2\vee\bigvee^4 S^4\). Thus the change from pure to full strong matchings materially changes the integral homotopy type.

## Assumptions and scope
Label the graph vertices by \(v\in\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\), with graph edges \(\{v,v+1\}\). **Do not fill** the graph cycle with a two-cell. A strong gradient comes from an injective ordering of the nine graph vertices, as in Knudson--Owens-White's definition. The *full* strong matching complex is the downward closure of all strong gradients, rather than the subcomplex generated only by maximum-cardinality strong gradients, or the ordinary Chari--Joswig Morse complex.

Encode a vertex of \(\mathcal{SM}(C_9)\) by a directed graph incidence: integer \(2v\) denotes the pair from vertex \(v\) to the preceding graph edge \(\{v-1,v\}\), while \(2v+1\) denotes the pair from vertex \(v\) to the following graph edge \(\{v,v+1\}\). There are 18 possible incidence labels. Encode a simplex by the integer bit mask of its incidence labels; its simplicial dimension is its bit population minus one.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive and exact face construction.** In a one-dimensional unfilled cycle, the descending link of a vertex \(v\) is the discrete set of its earlier neighbors. This link is a cone exactly when it consists of one point. Thus a strong matching pairs \(v\) with its unique earlier neighbor if and only if precisely one of \(v-1,v+1\) precedes \(v\); in all other cases \(v\) is left unpaired. All strong gradients arise from one of the \(9!=362{,}880\) injective vertex orders. The attached reproducible program enumerates **every** one of these orders and takes the full downward closure, producing 510 distinct generating strong gradients and the exact nonempty face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6)=(18,126,438,810,792,378,72).
\]
The 90 inclusion-maximal faces comprise 72 six-simplices and 18 four-simplices. The pure complex generated by the six-simplices has 2,598 nonempty faces; the 36 faces outside it consist of 18 three-simplices and 18 four-simplices.

**2. Fully specified acyclic matching.** Order the 18 incidence labels by
```
(6, 2, 12, 17, 15, 14, 4, 8, 10, 11, 5, 1, 9, 3, 7, 0, 13, 16)
```
Starting from the full set of nonempty faces, process these labels in the displayed order. At label \(b\), pair every currently unmatched face \(\sigma\) omitting \(b\) with \(\sigma\cup\{b\}\) when both faces remain unmatched, and remove both from consideration. The resulting 1,314 pairs are face--coface incidences differing by one vertex. Exactly six faces remain unmatched, whose decimal bit masks are
```
(64, 10402, 20741, 35368, 70916, 75809)
```
They consist of one zero-simplex and five four-simplices.

To verify acyclicity without assuming it from the toggling recipe, construct **every** covering edge of the Hasse diagram of the nonempty face poset. Orient an unmatched covering edge from the coface down to the face, and reverse a matched covering edge. This makes a directed graph with exactly 2,634 vertices and 11,538 directed edges. The supplied program performs a complete topological sort and confirms that all 2,634 vertices are removed; therefore the directed graph is acyclic. These are exhaustive checks, not statistical tests.

**3. Homotopy conclusion.** Forman's discrete-Morse theorem therefore produces a CW complex homotopy equivalent to \(\mathcal{SM}(C_9)\) with precisely one zero-cell and five four-cells. Its three-skeleton is a point, so every four-cell is attached by the unique map from \(S^3\) to a point. This CW complex is exactly a wedge of five four-spheres. The integral homotopy assertion follows without an uncomputed boundary matrix or a homology-only inference.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens-White (arXiv:2609.06844v1, first public 2026-09-06) define full and pure strong matching complexes, prove the homotopy type of the **pure** complexes for all cycles (Theorem 8.6), and provide a **homology** table for the full complexes through cycle length 21 (Table 1). Their table already gives the rank-five degree-four homology at length nine, but an abstract homology group does not imply a wedge-of-spheres homotopy classification. The explicit optimal acyclic matching and integral homotopy classification here supply that missing step. The same paper gives \(\mathcal{SM}_{\mathrm{pure}}(C_9)\simeq S^2\vee\bigvee^4S^4\), which does not classify the full complex.

A published rational rotation-character computation concerns the **pure** strong matching complexes and does not imply the claimed integral homotopy type of the full complex. The ordinary discrete-Morse matching complex \(\mathfrak M(C_9)\) is a different simplicial complex. No general statement about other full-cycle lengths is claimed here.

## Limitations
The classification and perfect matching are for the unfilled nine-cycle only. They do not establish an all-length pattern, an equivariant homotopy equivalence, an explicit homotopy-equivalence map to a wedge, or a collapse directly onto a simplicial wedge. The verification uses exhaustive finite enumeration and exact graph algorithms; it is not independently certified by a separate mathematical review. Unindexed equivalent work may exist.
