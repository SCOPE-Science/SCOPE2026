# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The specified Kneser model fixes the graph and the 15-edge ordering. Exhaustive independent face predicates agree on all 32768 masks; the 331 nonempty faces have dimension counts 15,75,145,90,6. The 159 matched pairs are disjoint; a topological-sort examination of all 990 covering incidences verifies the matching is acyclic. Exact integer unit-pivot cancellation gives the displayed critical 7-by-7 boundary with two primitive pivots and rank two. The attaching maps of the remaining three-cells are null after integral basis transformations because the two-skeleton is a wedge of two-spheres. Independent unreduced simplicial ranks over four prime fields corroborate reduced Betti ranks five in degrees two and three. The standalone verifier and expected output are provided. No infinite extrapolation is made.

## Originality
**PASS relative to inspected sources.** Four primary full manuscripts were inspected for definitions, hypotheses, theorem statements, and relevant sections. The substantial older matching-complex results of Björner et al., Shareshian–Wachs and Jonsson concern complete underlying graphs, whereas this claim concerns the Petersen graph as underlying graph. The 2024 Mondal–Mukherjee–Saha paper's observation that \(\mathcal M(K_5)\) *is* the Petersen graph is categorically different from taking matchings *in* the Petersen graph. Nikseresht gives algebraic classification criteria but not this homotopy decomposition. Available public result statements on Petersen two-matching and Petersen discrete-Morse complexes have different simplicial faces. Exact, alias, line-graph, degree, and parameter searches found no directly covering result. An inaccessible full text of a nearby public record or unindexed earlier computation remains a genuine priority risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The Petersen graph is a canonical cubic Kneser graph and smallest snark, so the ordinary matching complex is intrinsically motivated, not a tuned parameter slice. The complex has facets in dimensions two, three, and four, yet its homotopy type is a mixed wedge in dimensions two and three with equal multiplicities. This supplies exact integral homotopy rather than only an Euler characteristic, field Betti numbers, or an algebraic non-Cohen–Macaulay criterion. It provides a small reproducible test case for topology of matching complexes beyond complete graphs and for comparisons with higher-matching constructions; this value does not require an infinite-family theorem.
