# Integral mixed-sphere homotopy of the triangular graph neighborhood complex \(\mathcal N(T_7)\)

## Finding
Let \(T_7=L(K_7)\) be the triangular graph, whose 21 vertices are the two-element subsets of \([7]=\{1,\ldots,7\}\), with two vertices adjacent exactly when their subsets intersect in one point. Its ordinary open Lovász neighborhood complex is homotopy equivalent, over the integers, to
\[
\boxed{\mathcal N(T_7)\simeq \bigvee^{246}S^3\ \vee\ \bigvee^{7}S^4.}
\]
In particular, its reduced integral homology consists of \(\mathbb Z^{246}\) in degree three and \(\mathbb Z^7\) in degree four, and vanishes otherwise; its fundamental group and second homotopy group vanish. The claimed homotopy equivalence is stronger than the previously published mod-two Betti-number calculation and the integral homology obtained by combining an earlier theorem about unbalanced bipartite graph complexes with a relative long exact sequence.

## Assumptions and scope
For a finite simple graph \(G\), define \(\mathcal N(G)\) to be the simplicial complex whose nonempty faces are exactly the vertex sets \(\sigma\subseteq V(G)\) admitting at least one common open neighbor in \(G\). Here graph vertices are **edges of** \(K_7\); no graph loops or closed neighborhoods are allowed. Orientation of a simplex is the increasing order of the lexicographically ordered 21 edge-vertices. The theorem concerns this particular graph and the integral homotopy type of its complex, not an assertion for all triangular graphs.

A face is therefore exactly a nonempty collection \(F\subseteq E(K_7)\) for which some distinct \(a,b\in[7]\) satisfy
\[
\text{every } \{u,v\}\in F\text{ has precisely one endpoint in }\{a,b\}.
\]
Indeed, the open neighbors of the vertex \(\{a,b\}\) in \(L(K_7)\) are the ten edges in the complete bipartite graph \(K_{\{a,b\},[7]\setminus\{a,b\}}\).

## Proof
**1. Exact face set and comparison complex.** Enumerate the nonempty subsets of these 21 ten-element open neighborhoods; deduplicate the resulting 21-bit masks. This enumerates **all** faces, not merely facets. The number is \(18{,}494\), and the face vector, in dimensions zero through nine, is
\[
(21,210,1190,3780,5187,4410,2520,945,210,21).
\]
For a structural comparison, let \(B_{7,2}\) be the simplicial complex of graphs on seven labeled vertices embeddable in a complete bipartite graph \(K_{r,7-r}\) with \(r\in\{1,2\}\). Direct face enumeration, or the defining characterization above, proves
\[
B_{7,2}=\mathcal N(T_7)\cup\{\operatorname{Star}_{K_7}(v):v\in[7]\},
\]
where these seven additional faces are distinct six-edge full stars, hence five-simplices. Their **proper** faces already belong to \(\mathcal N(T_7)\). The standalone verifier independently constructs both complexes and establishes this face-set identity, including all seven exceptions.

**2. Complete acyclic discrete-Morse matching.** Encode each nonempty face by its 21-bit characteristic integer. Consider the 21 vertices of \(T_7\) in lexicographic order. Starting with all faces unmatched, at stage \(i\) pair each still-unmatched face \(\sigma\) not containing vertex \(i\) with \(\sigma\cup\{i\}\), provided that partner is still unmatched. Pairs are considered in increasing integer-mask order. The paired faces differ in precisely one vertex, and none is used twice. This explicit algorithm pairs \(9{,}120\) distinct covering pairs. Exactly 254 faces remain unmatched: **one vertex, 246 three-simplices, and seven four-simplices**.

Orient every unmatched Hasse-diagram covering relation downward, from coface to face, and every matched covering relation upward. The verifier constructs all \(98{,}805\) covering relations among the nonempty faces and performs an exact topological sort of this directed graph. Every one of the \(18{,}494\) faces is visited, proving absence of directed cycles. The matching is therefore acyclic and yields a CW model with precisely the displayed critical cells (Forman's discrete Morse theory).

**3. The integral attaching map.** All simplicial incidence coefficients are \(\pm1\), with sign determined by the deleted vertex's position in the increasing order. Eliminate a matched pair consisting of a three-simplex \(s\) and a four-simplex \(t\) by the integral unit-pivot relation obtained from \(\partial t\). More explicitly, if \([t:s]\in\{\pm1\}\) is the signed coefficient, project an eliminated three-simplex onto the free group generated by critical three-simplices via
\[
P(s)=-[t:s]\sum_{u\in\partial t,\ u\ne s}[t:u]P(u).
\]
A critical three-simplex projects to its own basis generator; a three-simplex that is the *upper* member of a pair with a two-simplex projects to zero. Acyclicity ensures that these recursive integral eliminations terminate. Applying the projection to the signed boundary of each of the seven critical four-simplices gives the **identically zero** \(246\times7\) integral Morse boundary matrix. The bundled checker recomputes every nonzero coefficient and asserts that all seven columns vanish over \(\mathbb Z\); no finite-field inference is used for this step.

The three-skeleton of the Morse CW model is \(\bigvee^{246}S^3\), since it has one zero-cell, no one- or two-cells, and 246 three-cells. The Hurewicz isomorphism \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{246}S^3)\cong H_3(\bigvee^{246}S^3;\mathbb Z)\) identifies every four-cell attaching map with its integer degree vector. All such vectors are zero, so all seven attaching maps are nullhomotopic. The resulting CW model, and thus the original simplicial complex, is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{246}S^3\vee\bigvee^7 S^4\), proving the theorem.

**4. Distinct integral homology cross-check.** Jonsson's unbalanced-bipartite-complex theorem gives \(B_{7,2}\simeq\bigvee^{246} S^3\); his published Euler-characteristic table gives reduced Euler characteristic \(-246\) at \((n,p)=(7,2)\). Since \(B_{7,2}\) differs from \(\mathcal N(T_7)\) by exactly seven five-simplices whose boundaries lie in the latter, the relative simplicial chain complex has \(C_5(B_{7,2},\mathcal N(T_7))\cong\mathbb Z^7\) and no other chain groups. The relative long exact sequence independently yields reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z^{246}\) in degree three and \(\mathbb Z^7\) in degree four. This cross-check alone does **not** establish the full homotopy classification: the zero integral Morse attaching map in step 3 is the additional essential computation.

## Verification
The complete, standalone Python 3 verifier is `verify.py`; run it by `python3 verify.py` without third-party packages. `verification_output.txt` records the actual successful execution. It independently enumerates the 21 open neighborhoods and all faces, checks the seven-star comparison with the unbalanced-bipartite complex, replays every matching step, visits all \(98{,}805\) Hasse cover relations in an acyclicity topological sort, performs exact signed integer Morse elimination, and independently computes all mod-two simplicial boundary ranks. The latter are
\[
(20,190,1000,2534,2646,1764,756,189,21),
\]
in boundary degrees one through nine. They reproduce the previously known mod-two Betti ranks, but it is the exact **integral zero Morse boundary and topological acyclicity** that prove the homotopy statement. The script exits with `ALL_CHECKS_PASS` only when every assertion succeeds.

## Relationship to prior work
Jonsson's \(B_{n,p}\) theorem (Chapter 14.3, Theorem 14.12) applies to the *larger* complex of unbalanced bipartite graphs, not the open-neighborhood complex itself. The distinction is exact: the seven complete stars belong to \(B_{7,2}\) but do not belong to \(\mathcal N(T_7)\). The theorem and relative homology determine the **integral homology groups** here, so those homology ranks are expressly **not** presented as a new result. They do not, by themselves, exhibit a homotopy equivalence with a mixed-dimensional sphere wedge.

The published triangular-graph neighborhood-complex calculation *Mixed-dimensional mod-two neighborhood homology first appears at \(T_7\)* determines reduced mod-two Betti ranks \(246\) and \(7\) and explicitly leaves integral homotopy unresolved. It does not supply the present integral CW attaching calculation. Fröberg's open-access treatment of neighborhood complexes discusses several other families of graphs rather than this triangular graph. These comparisons include line-graph, Johnson-graph, unbalanced-bipartite and common-neighbor descriptions, not merely matching titles. A potentially unindexed equivalent homotopy classification cannot be excluded.

## Limitations
The classification is for exactly \(T_7=L(K_7)\) and the ordinary **open** neighborhood complex; the result does not imply analogous homotopy types for \(T_n\) with \(n\ge8\), for closed-neighborhood complexes, or an equivariant homotopy equivalence. The discrete-Morse certificate is a finite exhaustive computation supported by a standalone exact checker; independent mathematical review and definitive publication-priority verification remain outstanding. No claim is made that the known integral homology ranks alone establish the homotopy type.
