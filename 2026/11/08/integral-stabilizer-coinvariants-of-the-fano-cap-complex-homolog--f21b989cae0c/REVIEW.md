# Scientific review: integral Fano cap-complex coinvariants

## Correctness — PASS
The object is fixed completely by seven nonzero binary three-vectors and seven line triples. The integral chain calculation is not inferred from finite-field ranks: a signed triangle-boundary minor has determinant one, seven tetrahedron boundaries have private unit coefficients, and consequently \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^6\) with no hidden torsion. The symmetry action is computed on this actual quotient lattice. Each order-two coinvariant claim has both a determinant-two spanning minor and rank five modulo two; the order-seven case has the analogous exact prime-seven pair. Determinant-one minors prove vanishing for the two larger subgroups. The verifier recomputes these facts and all \(168^2\) group-action multiplications. This proves each claimed integral quotient, not merely an experimental approximation.

## Originality — PASS against the inspected evidence
The closest published equivariant calculation identifies a six-dimensional **rational** Heawood independence-homology representation with character \((6,-2,0,2,-1,-1)\). The new Fano-cap rational character matches the order-wise values, so **neither its dimension nor its rational representation is claimed as original**. Integral subgroup coinvariants do not follow from a rational module isomorphism: rationalization kills their finite torsion, including the order-two phenomena now computed. Tsukuda relates hypergraph incidence complexes and Alexander duals at the homotopy level, but does not calculate any of the five stabilizer coinvariant groups. Kim's hypergraph star-cluster theorem gives a different general reduction and does not address Fano point/line symmetry. Superdock's finite-plane complexes use different allowed-face families. Searches of the indexed results and accessible local prior claims revealed no implication yielding these integral quotients. The group and lattice are canonical; equivalent representation-lattice computations in older or unindexed literature remain the principal residual risk.

## Value — PASS
The Fano cap complex is a natural seven-vertex hypergraph independence complex, and its full collineation group has distinguished point and line stabilizers of order twenty-four, alongside Singer-cycle normalizers. Computing the **integral** fixed-symmetry obstructions for these canonical subgroups is a motivated refinement of a known rational module: both local stabilizer quotients carry exactly one order-two class, although the order-twenty-one normalizer and the entire collineation group have no coinvariants. These are exact invariants that distinguish local from global integral symmetry and cannot be read from Betti numbers or rational characters. A broader classification of projective planes is neither required nor asserted.
