# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The full statement is for each integer \(n\ge4\), not for an extrapolated finite range. The pairwise-intersecting edge classification proves that the only cells outside the union of full star simplices are relative two-cells indexed by triples. The radial graph comparison identifies the star-union first homology integrally with the cycle lattice of the complete graph. With the explicit exceptional-triangle orientation, the connecting homomorphism is the usual simplicial boundary \(\partial_2\). Triangles through a fixed ground label give an **integral** basis of the entire first-cycle lattice; hence the relative boundary is onto without any torsion issue. Its kernel is precisely \(\bigwedge^3L_n\), as can be proved by a split exact sequence on the permutation lattice. This also eliminates all other reduced homology degrees. The complete character formula follows from the exterior-power generating function. The executable check confirms the independently computed Lefschetz traces for every one of 5,904 permutations across four small sizes and independently computes the predicted mod-two and mod-three simplicial Betti numbers. The finite checks are corroboration only.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources
The exact same simplicial object is already studied by Linusson–Shareshian–Welker (2004) under the equivalent name \(\mathsf{NM}_2(n)\), the complex of graphs without a matching of size two. Their Theorem 1.1 determines its nonequivariant wedge-of-spheres homotopy type, and **their Question 1.13 explicitly asks for the full \(S_n\)-homology module of \(\mathsf{NM}_k(n)\)**. The result here answers that question for \(k=2\) over the integers, establishing the actual exterior-power lattice rather than merely a rank. Their Section 4.1 gives a matching-theoretic Morse proof and no complete \(S_n\)-module formula. Barmak's later Theorem 4.11 independently confirms the known wedge count without proving the integral group module. Braun treats stable Kneser subgraphs, while Feng–Wang and Feng treat other subset sizes. Indexed open-neighborhood and Čech findings are different complexes. No inspected literature or mathematical-result record gives or implies the same integral \(S_n\)-module for this complex. An unindexed solution to the 2004 equivariant question remains a publication-priority risk; a negative search alone cannot prove novelty.

## Value — PASS
The ordinary two-subset Kneser graphs form a canonical symmetric family, including the Petersen graph. The 2004 authors explicitly posed their complete symmetric-group homology problem (Question 1.13), and this resolves its first nontrivial matching-size case uniformly and integrally. The nonequivariant wedge-of-spheres classification leaves the automorphism action completely undetermined; different integral lattices can have identical rank and rational characters. The construction gives a natural exact integral homology representation for the *entire family*, a closed cycle-count character, irreducibility, and vanishing of all invariant homology. These are structural invariants useful for comparing equivariant topology and for distinguishing integral from rational symmetry information, not a collection of arbitrary small-parameter calculations. Standard simplex homology enters only after the new relative chain identification.
