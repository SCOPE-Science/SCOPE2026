# Integral symmetry modules for higher-independence complexes of two-ended subdivided double stars

## Finding
Let \(a,b\ge1\). Let \(T_{a,b}\) be the tree consisting of the path \(u-w-v\), together with a set \(A\) of \(a\) leaves attached to \(u\) and a set \(B\) of \(b\) leaves attached to \(v\). Write \(L=A\sqcup B\), \(m=a+b\), \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(T_{a,b})\), and \(U_C=\ker(\mathbb Z[C]\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Z)\) for each nonempty leaf set \(C\). The action of \(G\) permutes the two end hubs \(\{u,v\}\) and the two leaf blocks, fixing \(w\).

For each integer \(r\ge1\), the only potentially nonzero reduced integral homology of the \(r\)-independence complex \(X_r=\operatorname{Ind}_r(T_{a,b})\) occurs in degrees \(r-1\) and \(2r-1\). Its complete **natural integral \(G\)-module** description is
\[
\widetilde H_{r-1}(X_r;\mathbb Z)\cong_G
\begin{cases}
\det\mathbb Z[\{u,v\}]\otimes\bigwedge^{r-3}U_L,&3\le r\le m+2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
\widetilde H_{2r-1}(X_r;\mathbb Z)\cong_G
\begin{cases}
\bigwedge^{r-1}U_A\otimes\bigwedge^{r-1}U_B,&1\le r\le\min(a,b),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The second formula uses the **graded-factor interchange action**: if \(a=b\), exchanging the two end hubs and the leaf blocks acts on the displayed tensor factors by the natural interchange multiplied by \((-1)^r\). On the first module, the hub exchange acts on \(\det\mathbb Z[\{u,v\}]\) by \(-1\). All module isomorphisms are over \(\mathbb Z\), with no localization or rationalization.

In particular, for \(a=b=3\), \(r=3\), the reduced groups in degrees two and five are each \(\mathbb Z\). Hub interchange negates both generators; a transposition within one leaf block fixes the degree-two generator and negates the degree-five generator. These two actions are invisible from sphere multiplicities alone.

The nonequivariant ranks and wedge homotopy types are **not asserted to be new**. The finding is the simultaneous natural integral representation of every graph automorphism in both possible homological degrees.

## Assumptions and scope
A face of \(\operatorname{Ind}_r(T)\) is a vertex subset whose induced connected components each contain at most \(r\) vertices. The graph has exactly the edges \(uw,wv\), all \(u\)-to-\(A\) edges, and all \(v\)-to-\(B\) edges. The full automorphism group is \(S_a\times S_b\) if \(a\ne b\), and \((S_a\times S_a)\rtimes C_2\) if \(a=b\). Exterior powers of degree beyond the rank are zero. Reduced groups use augmented simplicial chains and integral coefficients. In the displayed second isomorphism, the action upon a block exchange includes the orientation sign of the join, not merely an unsigned swap of tensor factors.

## Proof
**1. An equivariant double-star calculation.** Let \(D_{a,b}\) be the double star obtained by removing \(w\) and joining \(u\) directly to \(v\). For \(q\ge1\), let \(K_q=\operatorname{Ind}_q(D_{a,b})\) and \(Y_q\) be its subcomplex of faces missing at least one hub. It is the union of the full leaf simplex \(\Delta(L)\), the cone \(u*(\operatorname{sk}_{q-2}\Delta(A)*\Delta(B))\), and the analogous cone \(v*(\Delta(A)*\operatorname{sk}_{q-2}\Delta(B))\). Each cone attaches to \(\Delta(L)\) along a contractible subcomplex (because each opposite leaf block is nonempty). Hence \(Y_q\) is contractible. Here \(\operatorname{sk}_{-1}\) consists of the empty face alone.

For \(q\ge2\), the relative simplicial chains \(C_*(K_q,Y_q;\mathbb Z)\) are generated by the faces containing both hubs. Such a face is exactly \(\{u,v\}\sqcup F\) with \(F\subseteq L\), \(|F|\le q-2\). Orient the hubs in that order. The relative boundary removes leaf vertices, while the hub-removal terms vanish. Thus the relative chain complex is the augmented truncated simplex chain complex on \(L\), shifted by two and tensored with \(\det\mathbb Z[\{u,v\}]\). Explicitly, in exterior-algebra coordinates its differential is contraction with the summation map on \(\mathbb Z[L]\). For \(2\le q\le m+1\), its top kernel is canonically \(\bigwedge^{q-2}U_L\); every lower homology group vanishes. This follows, for example, by using any chosen leaf to give a chain contraction of the full augmented simplex and then restricting below the truncation degree. Since \(Y_q\) is acyclic, the relative long exact sequence yields
\[
\widetilde H_{q-1}(K_q;\mathbb Z)\cong_G\det\mathbb Z[\{u,v\}]\otimes\bigwedge^{q-2}U_L.
\]
For \(q=1\) and \(q\ge m+2\), the double-star complex is contractible. The displayed isomorphism is natural for every graph automorphism; the hub-orientation sign cannot be discarded.

**2. Delete the middle vertex.** In \(X_r\), let \(Y=\operatorname{del}_{X_r}(w)\) and \(K=\operatorname{lk}_{X_r}(w)\). Direct inspection of induced connected components gives
\[
Y=\operatorname{Ind}_r(K_{1,a})*\operatorname{Ind}_r(K_{1,b}),
\qquad K=\operatorname{Ind}_{r-1}(D_{a,b})\quad(r\ge2).
\]
For \(r=1\), the link \(K=\Delta(L)\) is contractible. There is an equivariant simplicial decomposition \(X_r=Y\cup(w*K)\) with intersection \(K\), and hence a natural long exact sequence in reduced homology.

**3. Homology and symmetry of each star.** A star with center \(c\) and leaf set \(C\) has the decomposition
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_r(K_{1,|C|})=\Delta(C)\cup(c*\operatorname{sk}_{r-2}\Delta(C)).
\]
The two terms are contractible, and their intersection is a simplex skeleton. Its augmented boundary calculation gives, naturally under leaf permutations,
\[
\widetilde H_{r-1}(\operatorname{Ind}_r(K_{1,|C|});\mathbb Z)
\cong\bigwedge^{r-1}U_C\quad(1\le r\le|C|),
\]
with other reduced groups zero; the complex is contractible for \(r\ge|C|+1\). The integral join Künneth theorem therefore gives the second claimed module for \(Y\) in degree \(2r-1\). Swapping two join factors whose homological degrees are both \(r-1\) has sign \((-1)^{((r-1)+1)((r-1)+1)}=(-1)^r\). This proves the graded factor-interchange rule, including the \(r=1\) boundary case.

**4. Exact equivariant sequence.** For \(r\ge2\), Step 1 applied with \(q=r-1\) gives a possible reduced homology group of the link \(K\) only in degree \(r-2\), with the first displayed module when \(3\le r\le m+2\). Step 3 gives possible reduced homology of \(Y\) only in degree \(2r-1\). These degrees and their adjacent long-exact-sequence terms do not meet for any \(r\ge2\). Therefore the natural maps
\[
\widetilde H_{2r-1}(Y;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\widetilde H_{2r-1}(X_r;\mathbb Z),\qquad
\widetilde H_{r-1}(X_r;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\widetilde H_{r-2}(K;\mathbb Z)
\]
are equivariant isomorphisms. All remaining reduced groups vanish. At \(r=1\), the link is contractible, so the upper-degree isomorphism remains valid; no lower group appears. At \(r\ge m+3\), the entire complex is a simplex. This proves all integer ranges, parity signs, and integral module descriptions.

**5. Optional complete rational character formula.** For a permutation \(h\) on a nonempty finite set \(C\), let
\[
P_C(h,z)=\frac{\prod_{c\in\operatorname{Cycles}(h)}(1-(-z)^{|c|})}{1+z}.
\]
The coefficient \([z^j]P_C(h,z)\) is the character of \(\bigwedge^jU_C\). If \(g\) fixes the two hubs, its lower and upper characters are respectively \([z^{r-3}]P_L(g,z)\) and \([z^{r-1}]P_A(g,z)\,[z^{r-1}]P_B(g,z)\), restricted to the degree ranges above. If \(g\) exchanges them, necessarily \(a=b\); the lower character is \(-[z^{r-3}]P_L(g,z)\) and the upper character is \((-1)^r[z^{r-1}]P_A(g^2,z)\). This follows by tracing the signed tensor-factor exchange. An absent homology degree has character zero.

## Verification
The independent standard-library Python program `verify.py` enumerates all graph-automorphism permutations for each of the nine parameter pairs \(1\le a,b\le3\), every \(1\le r\le a+b+4\), and every admissible simplex. It calculates each reduced simplicial Lefschetz trace from oriented fixed faces and independently compares it against the two module-character predictions. It checks 1,136 equalities, including 1,064 cases with nonidentity automorphisms, and independently computes all 72 reduced homology-rank profiles over the two-element field from actual simplicial boundary matrices. Its exact output is in `verification_output.txt` and ends with `ALL_VERIFY_OK`. These exhaustive finite cases corroborate—but do not replace—the all-parameter chain proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Deshpande and Singh, *Higher Independence Complexes of Graphs and Their Homotopy Types* (2020/2021), compute nonequivariant homotopy for several graph families, including trees with a leaf attached to every non-leaf vertex (their Theorem 3.10). Abdelmalek and collaborators (2021) establish vertex decomposability for caterpillar-graph higher independence complexes; Sawant (first posted May 24, 2026) proves this property for all trees. These topological/shellability statements do not determine the complete integral action of graph automorphisms. Separately, the nonequivariant sphere ranks of the present two-ended family have been computed previously and are not claimed here. The new assertion concerns the precise two integral homology lattices as natural modules, including the parity sign under hub exchange.

## Limitations
The theorem is for the particular three-core-vertex caterpillar with nonempty leaf blocks. It neither classifies arbitrary trees nor asserts an equivariant homotopy wedge decomposition. The verifier covers bounded finite instances; its checks are not a certificate for the universal quantifiers. Results derived from homology representations do not distinguish all possible higher attaching maps. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
