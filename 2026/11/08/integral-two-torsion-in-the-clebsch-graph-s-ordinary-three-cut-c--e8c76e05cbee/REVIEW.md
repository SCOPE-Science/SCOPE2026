# Scientific review: Clebsch ordinary three-cut complex

**Correctness — PASS.** The graph is explicitly the 5-regular folded five-cube on even-weight length-five binary words, with edges at Hamming distance four. The standalone verifier checks the complete 400-facet definition against an independent complementary-triple membership test, enumerates all 65,198 nonempty simplices, and exhaustively proves the 32,590-pair Morse matching acyclic on all 519,776 nonempty Hasse incidences. Precisely one vertex, sixteen eleven-cells and one twelve-cell are critical. Exact signed gradient elimination yields a single Morse boundary column with greatest common divisor two, giving the homotopy equivalence to fifteen eleven-spheres and one degree-two Moore space. A separate small Alexander-dual chain calculation corroborates the homology in five field characteristics; the integral proof is the signed attaching map, not finite-field extrapolation.

**Originality — PASS against inspected evidence.** The two principal cut-complex papers (arXiv:2304.13675 and arXiv:2407.08158) were inspected in their relevant definitions, theorem statements, family classifications and applicability conditions. Neither gives the Clebsch three-cut homotopy type. The first paper's skeleton-based torsion-free result does not apply because this complex has forty missing eleven-faces; its connected-triangle-free homotopy classification addresses two-cuts. Stronger published results for triangle-free graphs with edge-disjoint squares likewise have inapplicable hypotheses: the Clebsch graph has forty squares and each graph edge belongs to four of them. Searches for Clebsch, folded five-cube, connected-triple Alexander duality and Moore torsion did not locate an exact or stronger covering result in the inspected indexed sources. Related previous calculations of other graphs are acknowledged, not represented as the present finding.

**Value — PASS.** The 5-regular Clebsch graph is the unique strongly regular graph with parameters \((16,5,0,2)\), a canonical edge-transitive triangle-free graph. Its forty densely overlapping four-cycles put the three-cut complex outside important torsion-free criteria. The calculation supplies a natural, exact and reproducible integral classification, revealing an eleven-dimensional two-torsion Moore summand alongside fifteen free sphere summands. It is not a routine substitution into a known family theorem or a small numerical improvement: the torsion and attaching degree are essential features beyond the graph parameters or rational Betti numbers.

**Closest sources and remaining risks.** The foundations are arXiv:2304.13675, especially its introduction, Theorem 6.1 and the discussion of the face-lattice criterion, and arXiv:2407.08158, especially its graph families and definitions. A. E. Brouwer's Clebsch graph account fixes the ambiguity between 5-regular and 10-regular versions. Other relevant indexed claims address different graph families or exclude graphs with overlapping squares. Unindexed equivalent results, inaccessible full text of some secondary claims and unrecognized theorem-level implications are residual originality risks. No general theorem for other graphs or independent audit is claimed.
