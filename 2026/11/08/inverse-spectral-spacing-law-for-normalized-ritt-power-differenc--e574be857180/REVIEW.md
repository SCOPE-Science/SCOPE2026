# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The result has explicit hypotheses on the fixed spectral ray, strict decreasing eigenvalue deficits, and the asymptotic gap ratio. The Ritt resolvent condition follows from a uniform positive lower bound for \((1-|1-re^{i\phi}|)/r\). A Taylor estimate gives \(O(n^{-1})\) uniform comparison with the scalar envelope \(xe^{-bx}\). Monotonicity reduces every large-time maximum to two neighboring levels, whose unique crossing yields the lower constant. Peaks recover the universal upper constant; vanishing successive differences close the full cluster interval. The inverse and Schatten realization are explicit. The diagnostic checker passes 297 finite assertions, but is not relied on for infinite quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest checked articles establish general Ritt criteria and quantitative norm bounds, and analyze sparse *time-index* sampling. The statement here instead reconstructs the *spatial spectral-spacing ratio* from the limiting interval of normalized power differences for normal ray-spectrum contractions. The available full-text sections relevant to power differences and diagonal operators were compared by mathematical implication rather than matching titles; none was identified as stating the exact cluster-interval/inverse result. Search failure does not prove global novelty, and broader Tauberian or semigroup results under different terms could cover it. This remains a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Ritt property supplies an upper bound on the normalized power differences but does not determine their long-time distribution. The finding supplies a complete sharp three-regime classification (dense, geometric, superlacunary gaps), an exact inverse invariant independent of the ray angle, and realizations arbitrarily close to the identity in each prescribed Schatten quasi-norm. In particular, analyticity alone does not imply convergence of the normalized norms. The exact interval and its invertible dependence on spectral geometry are meaningful beyond a routine Ritt membership check.

## Prior-work and scope limitations
Borichev–Gomilko–Tomilov (arXiv:2607.10507) develop discrete Ritt criteria; Badea (arXiv:2609.27458, especially Section 5.e) determines sampling conditions for Ritt membership along time subsequences; Mahillo–Rueda (arXiv:2404.12946, Section 3) give growth bounds for Ritt–Kreiss operators. None of the inspected claims determines the present fixed-operator accumulation interval or its spectral-spacing inverse. The result is limited to a common spectral ray and sequences with a ratio limit; it has not been independently audited.
