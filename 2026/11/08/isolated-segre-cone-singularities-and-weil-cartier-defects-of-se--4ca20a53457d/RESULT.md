# Isolated Segre-cone singularities and Weil–Cartier defects of second-hypersimplex toric varieties

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(X_n\subset\mathbb P^{\binom n2-1}_{\mathbb C}\) be the projective toric variety parametrized by \([x_1:\cdots:x_n]\mapsto[x_ix_j]_{i<j}\) on the dense torus. More generally, replace every coordinate by \(c_{ij}x_ix_j\), where \(c_{ij}\in\mathbb C^*\). The resulting varieties have equivalent intrinsic geometry under diagonal projective transformations. The following is an exact classification:

1. The reduced singular locus consists of precisely the \(\binom n2\) coordinate torus-fixed points. At each one, the entire affine chart is the cone of rank-at-most-one \(2\times(n-2)\) matrices, equivalently the affine cone over the Segre embedding \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{n-3}\). Its Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity is \(n-2\) and its local divisor class group is \(\mathbb Z\).
2. The global integral divisor groups are \(\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)\simeq\mathbb Z^{n+1}\), \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)=\mathbb Z[\mathcal O_{X_n}(1)]\), and \(\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)/\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)\simeq\mathbb Z^n\).
3. The anticanonical class is Cartier, indeed \(-K_{X_4}=2[\mathcal O_{X_4}(1)]\), exactly when \(n=4\). In that case all six singularities are ordinary threefold nodes. For every \(n\ge5\), \(X_n\) is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein at any of its \(\binom n2\) singular points and hence not globally \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\), \(n\ge4\), and all scalings \(c_{ij}\) are nonzero. We consider the **normal projective toric variety itself**, not the intersections defining a statistical likelihood problem, and the reduced singular locus rather than a nonreduced Jacobian scheme. Coordinates \(z_{ij}\) are indexed by unordered pairs. The polytope is the second hypersimplex \(\Delta(2,n)=\{(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\in[0,1]^n:\sum t_i=2\}\).

## Proof
**Charts and singularities.** Fix a pair \(i<j\). In the affine chart \(z_{ij}\ne0\), rescale to \(z_{ij}=1\), and for every \(k\notin\{i,j\}\) put \(A_k=z_{jk}/z_{ij}\) and \(B_k=z_{ik}/z_{ij}\). On the dense torus, \(A_k=x_k/x_i\) and \(B_k=x_k/x_j\). Therefore all \(2\times2\) minors \(A_kB_\ell-A_\ell B_k\) vanish. Also \(z_{k\ell}/z_{ij}=A_kB_\ell=A_\ell B_k\). These identities show that the chart algebra is generated by the \(A_k,B_k\), subject to the rank-one relations. Conversely, every generic rank-one matrix \((A_k;B_k)\) has a common nonzero row factor and lies in the parameterized torus image; the generic rank-one locus is dense in the irreducible determinantal cone. Thus the chart is *exactly* the normal determinantal variety \(\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}A_k\\ B_k\end{pmatrix}\le1\), including its boundary.

The rank-one \(2\times m\) cone (\(m=n-2\ge2\)) is smooth at each rank-one nonzero matrix: if an entry is nonzero, its minors solve the remaining matrix entries as regular coordinates. At the zero matrix all first derivatives of its quadratic defining minors vanish, while the cone has dimension \(m+1<2m\). Its only singular point is zero. The coordinate charts \(z_{ij}\ne0\) cover \(X_n\); the respective origins are the distinct coordinate points \(e_{ij}\). This proves the singular-point count and exhaustiveness. Normality follows from the determinantal charts (alternatively from the normal rank-one Segre semigroup).

The projectivized tangent cone at each origin is the smooth Segre variety \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1}\) in \(\mathbb P^{2m-1}\). Its degree, hence the Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity of the cone vertex, is \(\binom m1=m\). The local class group is \(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1})/\mathbb Z[\mathcal O(1,1)]\cong\mathbb Z\). The canonical class corresponds to \(\mathcal O(-2,-m)\) modulo \(\mathcal O(1,1)\). It is rationally trivial in this quotient exactly when \(m=2\), proving the \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein threshold. For \(m=2\) the local equation is the nonsingular-rank quadratic \(A_1B_2-A_2B_1=0\), an ordinary threefold node.

**Global Weil divisors.** The character lattice of the dense torus is \(M=\{(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum u_i=0\}\), and the dual one-parameter lattice is \(N=\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\). The \(2n\) facets of \(\Delta(2,n)\) are \(t_i=0\) and \(t_i=1\). Their primitive normal rays, under a harmless common sign change, are \(\overline e_i\) and \(-\overline e_i\). These rays generate \(N\) integrally, so the toric divisor sequence \(0\to M\to\mathbb Z^{2n}\to\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)\to0\) has torsion-free cokernel. Its rank is \(2n-(n-1)=n+1\).

**Cartier divisors.** Write the coefficients of a torus-invariant Weil divisor on the two sets of rays as integers \(a_i,b_i\). A maximal normal cone corresponds to the vertex \(e_i+e_j\) and has rays \(\overline e_k\) for \(k\notin\{i,j\}\), and \(-\overline e_i,-\overline e_j\). Its unique linear ray relation is \(\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}\overline e_k-(-\overline e_i)-(-\overline e_j)=0\). The Cartier compatibility condition is therefore \(\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}a_k=b_i+b_j\) for all pairs \(i<j\). Put \(A=\sum a_i\) and \(c_i=a_i+b_i\). These constraints become \(c_i+c_j=A\) for every pair. Because \(n\ge4\), they force \(c_1=\cdots=c_n=t\) and \(A=2t\). Conversely, these integral conditions imply all maximal-cone Cartier conditions.

Principal character divisors change \(a_i\) and \(b_i\) by opposite amounts with total character sum zero, leaving \(t\) fixed; the quotient of the Cartier divisor lattice by principal divisors is therefore \(\mathbb Z\), detected by \(t\). The divisor with \(a_1=a_2=1\), all other \(a_i=0\), and \(b_i=1-a_i\) has \(t=1\), and its support polytope is a translation of \(\Delta(2,n)\). It represents \(\mathcal O_{X_n}(1)\). Since the map \([a,b]\mapsto a_1+b_1\) on the *Weil class group* sends this hyperplane class to \(1\), that class is primitive in the free abelian class group. Consequently the integral Weil–Cartier quotient is \(\mathbb Z^n\).

Finally, \(-K_{X_n}\) is the sum of the \(2n\) invariant prime divisors, so its coefficients satisfy \(a_i=b_i=1\). Its Cartier compatibility equation becomes \(n-2=2\); even over \(\mathbb Q\), this holds exactly for \(n=4\). At \(n=4\), \(t=2\) gives \(-K_{X_4}=2\mathcal O_{X_4}(1)\). This independently agrees with the local canonical obstruction.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses rational Gaussian elimination on the Cartier constraint matrices for \(4\le n\le8\), confirms their ranks equal \(n\), checks the hyperplane support vector and the anticanonical threshold, and directly enumerates rank-one matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\) for selected sizes. The counted numbers are \(10,22,46\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(33,105,321\) over \(\mathbb F_3\), matching \(1+(q^2-1)(q^m-1)/(q-1)\) for \(m=2,3,4\). Exact symbolic lattice arguments above, not finite cases, prove the unrestricted claims. Python standard library is the only requirement. Run `python verify.py` in the directory containing this document and the script.

## Relationship to prior work
The squarefree quadratic toric parametrization and its second-hypersimplex moment polytope are prior constructions. Clarke, Hoşten, Kushnerchuk and Oldekop study maximum-likelihood degrees, establish the second-hypersimplex degree and its principal \(A\)-determinant, and describe the facet geometry; see their Section 3. Oldekop's subsequent 2026 study treats Euler stratifications and delta-matroids of scaled varieties. These results motivate the intrinsic singularity and integral-divisor problem but the inspected material does not state the present simultaneous isolated-singularity, local determinantal-cone, and global Weil–Cartier classification. The Segre-cone class-group description and toric divisor sequence are standard tools, not newly claimed lemmas.

## Limitations
The classification applies to the projective toric variety, and every nonzero diagonal rescaling, not to the likelihood critical-point schemes. It does not determine nonreduced Jacobian singular schemes or mixed-characteristic deformation theory. The 2026 preprint's publicly indexed abstract was examined but its full text could not be retrieved in this study; this leaves a concrete residual historical-priority risk despite targeted comparisons with the available 2024 primary full text. No claim of independent mathematical validation or exhaustive historical priority is made.
