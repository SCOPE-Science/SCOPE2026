# Review of the joint star-configuration theorem

## Correctness (C): PASS
The radial-order stratification of a star configuration space gives an arm-and-label-equivariant graph model with vertices consisting of arm words, optionally with one particle at the center. Every transfer edge joins different vertex types, so the signed cellular action has no edge reversals. The count of fixed word distributions for each common cycle length is \(d^{c_d}c_d!\binom{c_d+a_d-1}{a_d-1}\). Counting a fixed central particle gives the \(B\) term; the Euler–Lefschetz trace identity gives the formula. Simultaneous arm/label orbits of graph vertices and edges are integer partitions and Young covers. The graph is connected for at least three arms, proving the quotient Betti formula. A concrete six-cycle for every \(k\ge5\), checked against the cases \(k\le4\), proves the sharp onset. Full group enumeration validates the finite sample \((m,k)=(4,4),(3,5)\) and two smaller control cases; the uniform proof covers all parameters.

## Originality (O): PASS relative to sources inspected
The graph model and ordinary Betti numbers, particle-only representation of Lütgehetmann (2014), 2017 branch-permutation stability, and 2024 particle-configuration generator/relations results are prior mathematics, not claimed discoveries. The present claim is the complete **joint** arm-and-label character for all \(k\ge4\), together with the simultaneous symmetry-quotient's explicit Young-lattice graph and its first cycle at five particles. Actual searches for these exact equivalences, all-cycle-length formulas, and quotient homotopy recovered no covering result. Prior two- and three-particle special cases are expressly excluded. Published or unindexed equivalent reformulations remain a priority risk; a failed search alone does not establish world-wide novelty.

## Value (V): PASS
The problem is motivated by simultaneous relabeling of robotic agents and arm symmetries of a canonical graph; the full group representation measures loops that are invisible after only one type of relabeling. The two-rank Young-lattice quotient turns a high-rank graph into a transparent partition-theoretic invariant. The sharp and stable five-particle appearance of a quotient loop is a qualitative new boundary, not a routine increase in a finite numerical count. The complete rational character and orbit classification form one structural theorem rather than an arbitrary slice.

## Closest literature and risks
Ghrist (1999), Farley–Sabalka (2004), Lütgehetmann (2014, 2017), Wawrykow (2024), and indexed specialized configuration-space results were inspected at the relevant sections. The theorem does not recover an integral group-module lattice or Borel equivariant cohomology. The literature search is substantial but not exhaustive; self-review does not constitute independent validation.
