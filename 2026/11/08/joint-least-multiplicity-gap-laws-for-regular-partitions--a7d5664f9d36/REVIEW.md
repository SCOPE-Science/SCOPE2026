# Review of the joint least-multiplicity-gap theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The exact count is a subtraction-of-required-copies bijection for every allowed size, with a layer-cake identity for nonmonotone threshold vectors. The generating-function Euler-product limit and standard Ingham Tauberian theorem yield a coefficient asymptotic for \(\ell\)-regular partitions; addition of a part of size one verifies the monotonicity hypothesis. For shifts of order \(\sqrt n\), the ratio has the claimed exponential limit. The scale and leading constant follow from an exact arithmetic-series formula. Independent finite partition enumeration checks 9,600 cases. Risk: the limit depends on classical analytic inputs rather than an entirely elementary derivation. No finite experiment is used to certify the infinite argument.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Wagner's 2011 full-text Theorems 1 and 3 prove individual fixed-\(r\) Rayleigh/Weibull marginals, but do not state simultaneous cross-threshold distributions. Bhowmick–Meher arXiv:2609.31104 studies individual fixed-threshold moments in unrestricted partitions; its indexed abstract was accessible, but the full preprint could not be read. Published-literature searches for joint least-\(r\)-gap laws and exponential-minimum couplings found no matching claim in the returned records; accessible published claim statements did not overlap. The multivariate survivor for arbitrary, even nonmonotone, thresholds and exponential-running-minimum dependence are the precise contribution; univariate marginal laws and classical Tauberian estimates are excluded from priority claims. Fristedt's general theory and inaccessible contemporary full text remain priority risks, and database searches do not prove uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Distinct fixed-threshold marginals do not determine their joint law or coalescence structure. The result determines their complete simultaneous dependence at the natural critical \(n^{1/4}\) scale, gives an exact finite-\(n\) count for any threshold vector, and identifies an explicit record-minimum process. The object is a natural extension of least-gap statistics to \(\ell\)-regular partitions, rather than an arbitrarily chosen finite slice. It does not address growing \(R\), quantitative error, or finite tie-event probabilities.
