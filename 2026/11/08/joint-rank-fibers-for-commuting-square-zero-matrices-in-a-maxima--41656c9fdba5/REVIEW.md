# Mathematical review: joint rank fibers for commuting square-zero matrices

## Correctness — PASS
The commutant of the maximal square-zero block has the exact \(2\times2\) block form given in the proof. The condition \(B^2=0\) reduces to \(X^2=0\) and \(XY+YX=0\). The rank-\(s\) square-zero \(X\) count is a choice of image, kernel, and quotient-to-image isomorphism. For fixed \(X\), solving the anticommutator equation produces the stated block affine space with \(q^{2s(r-s)}\) free choices besides \(E\); the independent kernel argument gives \(\operatorname{rank}B=2s+\operatorname{rank}E\). This is a proof for every \(q,r\), including characteristic two, not an experimental inference. Separate literal matrix checks reproduce the claimed integer fibers for several fields and a whole-space binary case.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
Kokol Bukovšek–Košir–Novak–Oblak determine attainable products and their rank restrictions, but not finite-field joint fiber multiplicities. Britnell–Wildon treat commuting similarity classes, not the number of labeled square-zero partners with a prescribed product rank. Fulman–Guralnick count a broader, rank-unrefined family of commuting nilpotents, which does not directly provide this rank-of-product stratification. An exact comparison of statements and parameters, not title differences, supports the distinction. A general unpublished or unlocated Hall-polynomial calculation might imply the result. A related paper on sums of commuting square-zero matrices was not available in full and creates a lesser residual risk.

## Value — PASS
A maximal square-zero matrix is the canonical free-module Jordan stratum for the dual-number algebra. The count is a complete two-parameter natural invariant, for all finite fields and all even dimensions, refining a known nontrivial product-rank upper bound. In particular the positive-product-rank strata, absent from annihilating-pair enumerations, control the sum's nilpotency index in odd characteristic. The result gives concrete finite-field enumerations and structural boundaries, rather than a renamed existence claim.

## Evidence and limitations
Verification was performed by three standalone reproducible programs. Their finite instances corroborate but are not substitutes for the general block-rank proof. Historical priority has not been independently certified and broad general module-counting results could potentially subsume the formula.
