# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every characteristic-two field and every \(N=2m\) or \(N=2m+1\), \(m\ge1\), derivations admit the displayed two-coefficient identification with \(B=k[s]/(s^m)\). Directly computing the square and commutator on the algebra generator gives the two relations \(ab=b^2=0\) and the pairing relation \(ab'+a'b=0\). The minimum-valuation argument proves every simultaneously commuting square-zero tuple lies in a **common** component \(L_j^r\), and direct substitution proves the converse. Independent dimension/intersection computations give all reduced geometric components. Rank is the rank of \(v\mapsto(av,bv)\), hence the valuation expression and rank polynomials. Inclusion–exclusion along nested neighboring intersections gives the complete multivariate rank generating function. These steps hold in all dimensions without finite-field assumptions.

The standalone checker, run on the original multiplication matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_4\), and \(\mathbb F_8\), separately compares the original equations and full rank histograms against the formula. Its finite cases corroborate rather than prove the infinite theorem. Risks: nonreduced scheme structures are outside the claim; no formal proof assistant or independent validation was used.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The nearest existing one-operator square-zero derivation result already determines the individual components and its one-variable total. Those facts and the one-operator specialization are background, not claimed as separate discoveries. An earlier commuting-derivation result without square-zero conditions addresses different objects and does not imply the restricted simultaneous joint-rank distribution. A restricted Witt-algebra all-tuple result applies to truncation degree equal to the characteristic rather than arbitrary characteristic-two truncation degrees. Related finite-field rank-profiles for another square-zero algebra concern a different derivation Lie algebra, so no direct implication follows.

The inspected original arXiv work of Evans–Fuchs treats the standard \(p\)-dimensional restricted Witt algebra, Yao–Chang 2013 explicitly assumes \(p>3\), and Ngo treats classical Lie algebras; these do not state the present characteristic-two infinite truncation family or its complete multivariate labeled rank count. Carlson–Friedlander–Pevtsova motivates the rank-invariant viewpoint without supplying this census. The 2015 Yao article on higher Witt tuples could be inspected only at abstract/bibliographic level after unsuccessful full-text access; this is a named historical-priority risk. Mathematical index searches returned no directly covering claim; that search failure is not proof of uniqueness. Possible unpublished or broader restricted-Lie enumeration remains unexcluded.

## Value — PASS

Commuting tuples of restricted-square-zero derivations are a natural model for elementary abelian restricted Lie subalgebras and their representations. Individual ranks of derivations are intrinsic linear invariants, while their **joint** rank profile measures correlations not determined by one-operator counts. The theorem reduces every tuple over an arbitrarily truncated one-variable algebra to a common member of a finite linear-component arrangement, gives every geometric component and intersection in all dimensions, and provides an explicit **complete multivariate rank-generating polynomial** for all tuple arities and finite fields of characteristic two. The exact adjacent-truncation parity stability and all-arity rational generating function give useful structural/computational consequences. This is a natural complete infinite-family classification rather than a numerical parameter slice. It does not claim novelty for the already-known single-operator geometric decomposition or imply unrelated structural theorems.
