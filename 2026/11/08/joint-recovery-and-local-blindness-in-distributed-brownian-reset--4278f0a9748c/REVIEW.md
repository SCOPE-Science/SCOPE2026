# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

**Claim scope and checks.** The claim concerns one-dimensional Brownian motion with generator \(D\partial_{xx}\), Poisson reset rate \(r>0\), and identically distributed independent initial/reset sites drawn from a compactly supported probability measure, not necessarily a density. The observable is the physical-time first-passage mean as a function of the point target. The one-attempt success transform is \(H(b)=\int e^{-\sqrt{r/D}|b-y|}\mu(dy)\). Renewal gives \(M=(1-H)/(rH)\), without assuming that a reset crossing over the target counts as a hit. Compact support yields the exact tail exponential for extracting \(\kappa\); the integral calibration is strictly monotone with range \((0,\infty)\), uniquely extracting \(r\), then \(D\), and the distributional Green inverse extracts atoms as well as densities. The local equivalence uses an exact two-exponential decomposition with distributional differentiation. The instability proof gives a uniform Fourier-multiplier remainder \(O(n^{-3})\), positive separation in total variation, and matching upper and lower \(n^{-2}\) bounds on any suitable fixed observation interval. Standalone finite diagnostics replay successfully but are not substituted for proof.

**Risks and boundaries.** Compact support is crucial for the positive-tail logarithmic slope; without it, the stated calibration procedure is not proved. Exact spatial scans and exact physical time scaling are required for identification. The local ambiguity result fixes the physical parameters. Statistical finite-sample performance and global minimax bounds are not derived.

## Originality — PASS (within checked coverage)

**Closest primary literature.** Méndez, Flaquer-Galmés and Campos, arXiv:2401.01125v1, Section II and Eqs. (14)–(15), supply the *forward* exponential-kernel mean law and later analyze forward efficiency and fixed-target equivalence. This prior forward formula is explicitly credited. Toledo-Marin and Boyer, arXiv:2206.14387v1, Sections II–V, treat forward first-passage, reset counts and entropy for prescribed random reset distributions, not joint inverse target scans. Abundo, arXiv:2410.16889v1, Sections 3–4, treats inverse first-passage problems from a given time distribution or mean at a fixed target, with known reset parameters and specified random-position conventions; Section 4.3 expressly permits many reset-location distributions for a *single* mean. It does not give identification from all target locations, the precise partial-window fiber, or the quantified high-frequency instability. Comparisons are based on inspected primary text, not title similarity.

**Equivalent formulations and stronger coverage.** The known forward identity and Helmholtz Green identity imply a simple recovery of the measure once \(r,D\) are given; the added inverse problem identifies both physical parameters from the same response, characterizes exactly what a finite spatial window loses, and proves a specific quadratic resolution obstruction. A different stationary-law inversion for a renewal-reset Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process identifies a reset-*time* distribution from a stationary *position* marginal with known physical parameters; neither measurement nor unknown object matches this result. A prior fixed-mean Brownian-resetting bound concerns full hitting-time transforms at one target under known physical parameters; it does not imply moving-target tomography. Searches in published-finding corpus and public literature did not reveal a matching or dominating theorem. Absence of a search hit is not proof of global priority.

**Residual priority risk.** General inverse Helmholtz-potential literature or unindexed tomography papers may contain an equivalent combined theorem. Publication priority needs human expert checking; this review does not purport to be an independent audit.

## Mathematical value — PASS

**Motivation.** Random resetting positions are measurable indirectly through search times, and fixing a single target leaves major ambiguities. It is natural to ask how scanning target location changes identifiability when both the reset frequency and the diffusivity are also unknown. The result supplies a complete yes/no answer for ideal full spatial scans, an exact classification of all local-data aliases (two exterior exponential moments), and a sharp quadratic small-scale loss for smooth perturbations. These constraints make an experimentally motivated inverse problem mathematically precise: a unique ideal inverse can nevertheless be severely ill-conditioned. It is more than a restatement of the forward mean formula, and the local indistinguishability is a full if-and-only-if classification, not a single contrived counterexample.

**Limitations.** The derived instability is qualitative for inference and quantitative for one oscillatory family; it does not establish an optimal noisy-estimation rate. No external experiment, computational certificate for infinite limits, or independent validation is claimed.
