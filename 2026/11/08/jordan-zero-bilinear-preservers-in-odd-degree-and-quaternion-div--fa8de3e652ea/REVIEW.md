# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For odd central division degree, any two invertible anticommuting elements would contradict reduced-norm invariance and odd parity in characteristic not two. For quaternion division algebras, reduced-trace invariance reduces all nonzero Jordan-zero pairs to orthogonal pairs in the three-dimensional pure-quaternion space. Six off-diagonal and two diagonal-difference tensors span the eight-dimensional kernel of the nondegenerate trace form. This gives a unique full target-valued bilinear normal form; its four factorable and four obstruction parameters are accounted for explicitly. The degree-two split case uses the established idempotent-generated determination theorem. The exact rational and finite-field checker output `VERIFY_OK` corroborates, rather than substitutes for, this proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification

Alaminos–Brešar–Extremera–Villena (2019) Example 4.6 gives one nonfactorable scalar bilinear functional on the real quaternions. Its existence is not claimed as new. Jia–Xiao (2026) Theorem 1.2 decides a qualitative determination property but does not compute the positive defect or the entire compatible-map space. The present all-field, all-target quaternion normal form and exact quotient, together with the odd-degree reduced-norm case, are not consequences of those stated results. Original primary text of both papers was materially read and the nearest semantic and equivalence searches compared. No equivalent was found, but a generic orthogonality-preserver theorem may exist and absence in a search cannot prove priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The class of bilinear maps annihilating zero Jordan products is a natural object in algebraic preserver theory. Rather than only refuting factorization, the result classifies the complete compatible space for any quaternion division form, computes its obstruction quotient and detects the degree-two split/non-split dichotomy. For odd division degree it identifies a structural norm-parity boundary explaining the vacuity of the zero-product test. This is a motivated complete classification, not a parameter renaming or a finite lookup. The remaining higher-even-degree cases are outside its scope.
