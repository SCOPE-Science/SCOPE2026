# Kirillov index and mod-three stabilizers of the three-step path Lie algebras

## Finding

For every field \(k\) (including characteristic \(2\)) and every integer \(n\ge2\), the Gallo–Videla three-step graph Lie algebra \(L_n=\mathfrak n_{P_n}(k)\) on the path with \(n\) vertices has
\[
\operatorname{ind}_k L_n=n-1,\qquad
\max_{\varphi\in L_n^*}\operatorname{rank} B_\varphi=2n-2,
\]
where \(B_\varphi(x,y)=\varphi([x,y])\). In particular, for \(n\ge3\), the index exceeds the dimension of the center by exactly one.

A single explicit maximizing functional has a **mod-three stabilizer transition**. Let \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) be the vertex basis, \(e_i\) the edge \(v_iv_{i+1}\) for \(1\le i<n\), and \(w_i\) the path \(v_i-v_{i+1}-v_{i+2}\) for \(1\le i\le n-2\). Define \(\varphi_n\in L_n^*\) by
\[
\varphi_n(v_i)=0,\quad \varphi_n(w_i)=1,\quad
\varphi_n(e_i)=
\begin{cases}1,& n\equiv2\pmod3\text{ and }i=1,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
Then its stabilizer \(\operatorname{rad} B_{\varphi_n}\) is precisely \(W\oplus kz_n\), with \(W=\operatorname{span}\{w_1,\ldots,w_{n-2}\}\) and
\[
z_n=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{1\le i\le n,\ i\equiv2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)}v_i,&n\equiv0\pmod3,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{1\le i\le n,\ i\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)}v_i,&n\equiv1\pmod3,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{1\le j\le n-1,\ j\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)}e_j,&n\equiv2\pmod3.
\end{cases}
\]
For the isolated-vertex path \(P_1\), the associated algebra is one-dimensional abelian and its index is \(1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The path \(P_n\) has consecutively labeled vertices \(1,\ldots,n\); edges are \(\{i,i+1\}\). The construction is over an **arbitrary field**, with no restrictions on characteristic or cardinality. For \(n\ge2\), the algebra has basis \(V\oplus E\oplus W\) as above, of dimension \(n+(n-1)+(n-2)=3n-3\), and its nonzero defining brackets, up to alternation, are
\[
[v_i,v_{i+1}]=e_i,\qquad
[v_i,e_{i+1}]=w_i,\qquad
[e_i,v_{i+2}]=w_i.
\]
All other basis brackets vanish. The center for \(n\ge3\) is \(W\), of dimension \(n-2\), whereas for \(n=2\), \(e_1\) is central. The **Kirillov index** is the minimum, over all linear functionals, of the dimension of the radical of their alternating Kirillov bilinear form.

## Proof

**1. Uniform upper bound on Kirillov rank.** Every \(w_i\) is central, so the Kirillov form vanishes on \(W\). Its remaining matrix, using the vertex basis followed by the edge basis, is the alternating \((2n-1)\times(2n-1)\) block matrix
\[
M_\varphi=\begin{pmatrix}C&D\\-D^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Over any field, including characteristic two, an alternating bilinear form has even rank. Since \(2n-1\) is odd, \(\operatorname{rank}M_\varphi\le2n-2\). Hence \(\operatorname{ind}L_n\ge(3n-3)-(2n-2)=n-1\).

**2. The displayed functional and the mixed block.** For \(n\ge3\), evaluate all path coordinates at \(1\). The mixed \(n\times(n-1)\) block then has, for \(1\le i\le n-2\), exactly the two contributions
\[
D_{i,i+1}=1,\qquad D_{i+2,i}=-1,
\]
with all other entries zero (overlaps are summed). In particular, writing \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in k^n\), the equations \(D^{\mathsf T}x=0\) are
\[
x_3=0,\qquad x_{n-2}=0,\qquad x_i=x_{i+3}\quad(1\le i\le n-3).
\]
They split the vertex coordinates into three residue classes modulo three. The residue class of \(3\) is killed by the first boundary condition, and the residue class of \(n-2\) by the second. If \(n\equiv0\) or \(1\pmod3\), these are distinct classes, so \(\dim\ker D^{\mathsf T}=1\) and \(\operatorname{rank}D=n-1\). If \(n\equiv2\pmod3\), they coincide, so \(\dim\ker D^{\mathsf T}=2\) and \(\operatorname{rank}D=n-2\). This argument uses only \(\pm1\) and so works in any characteristic.

**3. The full-rank cases.** If \(n\not\equiv2\pmod3\), the chosen functional has \(C=0\). As \(D\) has full column rank, the alternating matrix has rank \(2(n-1)\). Its radical outside \(W\) is the one-dimensional space \(\ker D^{\mathsf T}\), generated by precisely the displayed vertex vector \(z_n\).

**4. The deficient mixed-block cases.** Suppose \(n\ge5\) and \(n\equiv2\pmod3\). The left kernel of \(D\) is spanned by the vectors \(u\) and \(v\) whose coordinates are \(1\) on vertex indices congruent to \(1\), respectively \(2\), modulo \(3\), and \(0\) elsewhere. The \(C\) block for the chosen functional is supported only on \((1,2)\), with \(C_{12}=1\), \(C_{21}=-1\). Hence \(u^{\mathsf T}Cv=1\) and the restriction of \(C\) to \(\ker D^{\mathsf T}\) is nondegenerate. Standard simultaneous row-and-column elimination of a block alternating form (or a direct decomposition into the \(\operatorname{rank}D\) hyperbolic pairs and the restriction to its left kernel) gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_{\varphi_n}=2\operatorname{rank}D+\operatorname{rank}(C|_{\ker D^{\mathsf T}})
=2(n-2)+2=2n-2.
\]
Its only noncentral radical direction lies in the edge space \(\ker D\). This kernel is generated by \(e_1+e_4+e_7+\cdots+e_{n-1}\): successive nonzero entries of the relevant columns cancel in rows \(3,6,9,\ldots,n-2\). This is exactly the claimed \(z_n\). In characteristic two, minus equals plus but the same nondegeneracy and cancellation remain valid.

**5. The small boundary.** When \(n=2\), the algebra is the three-dimensional Heisenberg algebra with \([v_1,v_2]=e_1\) central. Evaluating \(e_1\) at \(1\) produces a Kirillov form of rank \(2\) and radical \(ke_1\), as claimed. For \(n=1\), the algebra is one-dimensional abelian. In all cases, the upper bound is attained; the index and radical statements follow.

## Verification

The attached standalone `verify.py` independently constructs all basis brackets from commutators of the matrix units \(E_{i,i+d}\) with \(1\le d\le3\), rather than inserting the displayed Kirillov block as an assumption. Its exact finite-field Gaussian elimination confirms the explicit maximal-rank functional, mixed-block rank, and radical generator for every \(2\le n\le18\) over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7\) (\(68\) witness checks). It also exhaustively checks the upper bound on all noncentral coefficients of functionals over \(\mathbb F_2\) for \(2\le n\le8\) and over \(\mathbb F_3\) for \(2\le n\le5\), a total of \(13{,}382\) functionals. Run `python3 verify.py`. Exact replay output is in `verification_output.txt`.

The unrestricted theorem is proved above; finite tests are not offered as a proof for arbitrary \(n\) or fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Gallo and Videla, arXiv:2610.33963v1 (first public version October 25, 2026), define the three-step graph Lie algebra, identify its center and graded dimensions, and exhibit the path realization as the quotient of the strictly upper-triangular matrix Lie algebra by its fourth and higher superdiagonals (Sections 2–4). They do **not** compute the path family’s Kirillov index or the mod-three location of a maximal functional's radical. Their path realization is used here to motivate the independent checker.

Burde and Dekimpe, arXiv:2505.08331v1 (May 13, 2025), study the index of free nilpotent, filiform, and **two-step** graph Lie algebras. In Section 4, their matching-number formula concerns the two-step algebra with central edge space, so it does not imply the present three-step formula with a nonzero vertex-edge block. The present construction has different brackets and generally a different index from the associated two-step graph algebra. No priority is claimed for isolated small path examples; the uniform all-length result and explicit mod-three stabilizers are the claim under review.

## Limitations

The classification concerns the Gallo–Videla algebra of a **path**, not arbitrary trees, cycles, or unrelated constructions called graph Lie algebras. It determines the maximal Kirillov rank, index, and the stabilizer of one explicit maximal functional; it does not enumerate all coadjoint orbits or the rank distribution of every functional. The priority comparison cannot rule out a pre-existing result formulated solely in terms of band-truncated strictly upper-triangular Lie algebras rather than graphs. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
