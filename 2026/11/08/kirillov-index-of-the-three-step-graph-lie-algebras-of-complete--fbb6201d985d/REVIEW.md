# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim is about the Kirillov index of the Gallo–Videla three-step graph Lie algebra of \(K_{a,b}\), for all fields and \(a,b\ge2\). The proof verifies the edge and length-two-path bracket signs, the central path-space dimension, the alternating block-matrix upper bound, a unimodular \(5\)-by-\(5\) mixed-block minor for \(K_{2,3}\), and an induction preserving such a minor when vertices are added. For the boundary \(K_{2,2}\), the mixed determinant is identically zero, and a rank-three witness gives exact noncentral rank six. The calculation is field-independent, including characteristic two. Standalone exact-arithmetic checks reproduce the rank maxima in three exhaustive finite cases and verify 36 explicit witnesses. Finite checks support, but do not replace, the general proof.

## Originality
**PASS, qualified.** In Gallo and Videla's October 2026 source the center and graded dimensions are calculated but not the coadjoint-stabilizer/Kirillov index. Burde and Dekimpe's earlier index results cover free and two-step graphical Lie algebras, not the new three-step construction with noncentral edges; their general rank criterion is not a covering evaluation. The closest accessible published results on other three-step graph families do not imply the present two-parameter formula or the four-cycle exception. The systematic equivalent-formulation, broader-coverage, exact-table, and implication comparisons are documented in `AUDIT.json`. A possible unindexed earlier equivalent calculation is a residual historical-priority risk, not evidence of novelty by itself.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The Kirillov index is a standard structural invariant governing minimal coadjoint-stabilizer dimension. The family comprises all genuine complete bipartite graphs with both parts nontrivial, including cyclic rather than tree examples. The result both attains the natural abelian-ideal bound outside one sharp exception and isolates the smallest cyclic graph's degeneracy. It supplies a uniform arbitrary-field answer absent from known center and lower-central-series formulas, and the excess index over the center can grow with both part sizes.
