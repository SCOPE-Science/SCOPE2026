# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact norm identity follows by (i) proving all maximizers integer-valued using strict convexity and tight-edge connectivity, (ii) using diameter at most two to reduce integral functions to two or three consecutive levels, (iii) using exclusion of a \(K_{2,2}\) in the complement to force one extreme level to consist of one vertex, and (iv) optimizing the resulting exact quadratic \(F_n(k)\). Conversely every proposed potential is feasible, so no unproved achievability step remains. The boundary case of a complete graph has \(\Delta=0\) and is handled separately. The proof, rather than numerical enumeration, covers every integer \(n\ge5\). Exact checks corroborate the result, including all four-/five-vertex qualifying graphs.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Targeted mathematical-record searches did not reveal the **per-graph** order/maximum-missing-degree norm identity for diameter-two graphs with four-cycle-free complements. The accessible abstract of Addario-Berry–Janson–McDiarmid concerns random-graph spread; its full text was inaccessible in this review; Yancey treats optimal functions and other graph families; Lancien–Pernecká provide the Banach-space context. Prior extremal deletion-budget bounds concern the worst graph in an entire budget class and do not imply the asserted equality for every graph in the forbidden-four-cycle family. The connected tight-edge argument and the elementary distance-two decomposition themselves are not claimed as novel. A possible equivalent older classification in the graph-metric or biclique literature is the main residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The formula classifies a natural sharp Banach-to-Hilbert embedding constant across a broad infinite family that includes complementary cycles, missing stars and numerous irregular deleted-edge patterns. It eliminates almost all graph structure from the parameter dependence, gives every optimizer and the sharp global gain \(2/n\), and identifies an exact eleven-to-twelve transition in a canonical subfamily. This is a structural robustness phenomenon, not a tiny numerical constant or an arbitrarily fixed graph.

## Limits
No conclusion is asserted for complements containing non-induced four-cycles, graphs of diameter at least three, nonuniform vertex measures, or other moments. No external independent audit has occurred; the source-inspection and search record is documented in the structured scientific review.
