# Scientific review: late spatial switching for diffusion exit odds

## Correctness — PASS

The stated hypotheses give strictly positive smooth joint exit densities and a simple Dirichlet Sturm–Liouville spectrum. A two-path exchange at the first meeting yields the two-time inequalities. Differentiating log ratios gives spatial nonincrease; the boundary-flux eigenfunction expansion gives an eventually unique zero, and evaluating the third mode at the zero of the second yields the stated exponential displacement coefficient. The nonzero sign of the second coefficient follows from the two boundary derivative signs. The standalone finite-chain and diffusion-grid tests are corroboration, not a substitute for the continuum proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

Newton and Vega Coso, *Stochastic Orders of the Exit Pair and the Monotone Response to Resetting* (arXiv:2611.01756, Sections 7, 9 and 10.3), establish a finite birth–death analogue, treat constant-coefficient Brownian drift, and explicitly leave general-diffusion switching open. The present result covers arbitrary smooth uniformly elliptic variable drift and volatility and gives a new third-mode asymptotic for the moving switch. Evans and Williams, *Transition Operators of Diffusions Reduce Zero-Crossing* (1998), prove variation-diminishing transition-operator results, not the claimed boundary-exit odds and sharp late-node shift. Searches for equivalent formulations and stronger statements did not uncover direct coverage. Classical Karlin–McGregor literature may implicitly contain the two-time positivity ingredient; its original full text was not accessible for a definitive comparison. The originality assessment does not equate unsuccessful searching with proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS

The motivating paper explicitly poses the continuum switching problem. Its resolution identifies the eventual spatial sign of the exact-time exit preference for an entire variable-coefficient diffusion class, and the third-mode coefficient quantifies the speed and direction of the boundary between preferences. The precise eigenvalue-gap rate and its possible cancellation supply information beyond qualitative monotonicity. The object is a natural first-passage statistic relevant to stochastic resetting and competing absorbing boundaries.

## Limits and closest literature

The hypotheses require smooth uniformly elliptic, time-homogeneous diffusions on compact intervals. Nothing is claimed about jumps, higher dimensions, degenerate endpoints, universal monotonicity in time at each point, or a nonzero third-mode coefficient in every example. The main historically relevant texts are arXiv:2611.01756, Karlin–McGregor (1960), and Evans–Williams (1998); a broader classical-positivity priority search remains desirable.
