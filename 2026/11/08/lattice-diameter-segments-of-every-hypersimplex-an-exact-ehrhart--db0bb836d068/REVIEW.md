# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A coordinate bound forces every maximum lattice segment in the \(t\)-dilated hypersimplex to have exactly \(t\) integral steps and a balanced ternary primitive direction. Each signed support fixes two endpoint-coordinate sets, and the remaining bounded coordinates obey exactly one hypersimplex sum constraint. Reversal is free, so the direction multiplicity is half the balanced multinomial coefficient. This yields an exact, disjoint count with no hidden general-position assumption. Ehrhart polynomiality applies to each residual integral hypersimplex; the unique \(p=1\) highest-degree contribution yields the strictly positive Eulerian coefficient. Boundary cases \(n=2\), extreme ranks, and zero residual coordinates were checked explicitly. The standalone verifier reproduced 333 exact assertions, including direct enumeration of pairs rather than merely recounting the same signed-support formula.

**Originality — PASS, provisionally.** The closest source, Averkov et al. (arXiv:2508.20009), provides eventual quasipolynomiality of maximal lattice-segment counts for general lattice polytopes and a planar periodic example; neither implies the hypersimplex's closed all-dilation polynomial, balanced-direction classification, degree, or Eulerian leading coefficient without a further argument. Nan Li's hypersimplex paper concerns ordinary Ehrhart counting and its \(h^*\)-statistics; combining it with the published general result still requires the new maximal-segment bijection. Targeted searches for hypersimplex maximal lattice segments, uniform-matroid line sections, equivalent capped-composition formulations, and more general formulas found no covering statement. An unindexed equivalent combinatorial enumeration is a residual priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The lattice-diameter-segment count was introduced as a natural discrete-geometric invariant whose general behavior is only eventually quasipolynomial. Hypersimplices are canonical uniform-matroid base polytopes. The result classifies *all* maximizing directions, provides a closed sum of Ehrhart polynomials in any rank and size, and gives a sharp dimension-dependent growth-degree drop and Eulerian coefficient; it is not merely the standard lattice-point count of one hypersimplex or an isolated small numerical example.

**Limitations.** No priority guarantee outside the searched material, no assertion for nonuniform matroids, no independent audit. The finite verifier corroborates rather than proves the unrestricted parameter claims.
