# Self-review of the Dirichlet affine-projection norm theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The degree-one kernel is reconstructed independently from the full Dirichlet covariance matrix. Convexity reduces its \(L^1\) evaluation norm to vertices and an explicit local sign disagreement makes all nonvertex evaluations strictly smaller. Truncated beta moments give the claimed exact constant. A three-coordinate Dirichlet aggregation coupling proves strict dominance by the smallest concentration parameter; the conditional law genuinely straddles the kink. Fractional beta moments and the beta central limit theorem justify the two asymptotic regimes. Exact-rational checks reproduce the projection identity, beta integrals, uniform benchmark, and integer-parameter ordering. No enumeration is used as a proof of real-parameter uniqueness.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Griffiths and Spanò provide the general Dirichlet degree-one kernel but do not give the sharp endpoint norm, least-coordinate comparison, balancing optimality, or singular concentration boundary. The 2026-10-04 published simplex result completely covers the uniform-volume case, which is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. The 2023 polynomial-mesh result treats numerical Lebesgue-constant evaluation, not this exact weighted law. A 2013 Jacobi-kernel paper was available at abstract level only; unindexed or inaccessible equivalent weighted formulas remain possible. The novelty conclusion is based on statement/implication comparisons, not on missing search hits.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Dirichlet weights are a natural family of highly nonuniform simplex sampling measures. This theorem determines their exact affine least-squares amplification, positivity defect, every norming evaluation location, and the unique best parameter allocation at fixed concentration; in particular, no knowledge of the distribution among nonminimal parameters is needed. The rare-coordinate boundary and high-concentration Gaussian regimes give sharp, nontrivial stability consequences. These are natural complete structural answers, not an arbitrary finite-parameter computation.

## Limitations
Only real degree-one orthogonal projection under positive Dirichlet weights is covered. The Gaussian limit requires strictly interior limiting proportions. Literature overlap cannot be ruled out beyond inspected accessible material, and independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
