# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For every allowed length profile and \(r\geq\max_i\ell_i\), the hub deletion is a simplex and the link is exactly the bounded-prefix complex. The internal-deficient subcomplex admits a contractible, acyclic leaf-toggle matching. The quotient relative chain groups are the explicitly oriented truncated augmented exterior complex; its integral homology is the determinant-twisted exterior power of the augmentation kernel, and the same chain identification is natural under arm-length-preserving permutations. The quotient has no 1-cells other than the basepoint, so simply connected homology concentrated in one degree upgrades to the claimed sphere wedge. The proof includes both boundary ranges. The standalone verifier checks 59 direct face/homology cases, 42 actual Hasse matchings, and 85 signed Lefschetz characters. These finite calculations corroborate rather than prove the all-parameter statement.

## Originality — PASS, subject to ordinary literature uncertainty

The closest directly relevant primary text is Sawant's 2026 general vertex-decomposability theorem for trees (arXiv:2605.25150); that theorem has no formula for arbitrary unequal spider lengths and does not identify the integral automorphism module. Deshpande--Singh (arXiv:2001.05448, Theorems 3.8, 3.10 and 5.6) give explicit formulas for fully whiskered and perfect rooted families, whereas the present central hub is not whiskered and the arms may have unequal lengths. Abdelmalek et al. (arXiv:2106.10863) supply broad shellability results, not these ranks or characters. Indexed mathematical-record searches and the accessible earlier two-edge-arm special case did not establish implication for the unequal-arm generality, although the matching mechanism is related. No search proves uniqueness, and an equivalent unindexed generalization remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact high-parameter regime is natural: once \(r\) exceeds every arm length, deleting the hub gives a simplex, making the hub-link topology the remaining invariant. The theorem determines the entire homotopy type and integral symmetry module for infinitely many unequal spiders. It shows unexpectedly that individual lengths disappear from ordinary topology while their parities remain visible equivariantly. The threshold \(B+1\) and binomial ranks are sharp, and the comparison of \((2,2,4)\) with \((2,3,3)\) demonstrates the distinction without appealing to speculative applications. The smaller \(r\)-range remains outside scope.
