# Review of the local cycle–record covariance theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** A uniform partial permutation has exactly one path component per unexposed domain, and the remaining completion is a uniform permutation of these components. This conditional decomposition is used to reduce the target covariance to a finite enumeration of completed cycles. For a prescribed completed cycle avoiding the record position, residual image exchangeability gives the record probability \(1/(i-k)\); the only completed cycle containing that position and permitting the record is its fixed point. Subtracting the product of means proves the exact all-size law, with careful treatment of \(i=1\), \(i=n\), and the zero-path case. Independent exact enumeration verifies the claims for small sizes; the infinite argument is mathematical rather than experimental.

**Originality — PASS (historical qualification).** The 2000 text of Mahmoud, §1.10, proves marginal cycle/record equidistribution by a bijection, not an equality of these statistics on the original permutation. Gnedin (arXiv:math/0611538v1, §§1–2) treats record counts, joint lower/upper records, and coherent permutation measures; Gnedin–Gorin (arXiv:1202.3680, introduction) treat the classification of record-dependent measures. Neither inspected primary source proves the same-permutation local cycle–upper-record covariance. Related results on cycle–inversion and fixed-point–descent covariances address different joint observables. Residual uncertainty concerns possible implicit coverage by older general multivariate permutation enumerations. Search absence is not proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem quantifies a nontrivial coupling between two canonical, identically distributed permutation statistics. The exact positive position profile, the domination of its final-position term, and the \(1/(n\log n)\) correlation decay explain how substantial dependence in the last record event can coexist with asymptotic decorrelation in global cycle and record counts. These distinctions are meaningful for record-based algorithms and permutation-statistic dependence and do not follow from Foata equidistribution alone.

The finite checks are not a substitute for the universal proof, and no extension to nonuniform permutation models is asserted.
