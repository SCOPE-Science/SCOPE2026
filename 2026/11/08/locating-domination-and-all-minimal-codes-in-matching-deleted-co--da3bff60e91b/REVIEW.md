# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The explicit signature identity \(N(a_i)\cap S=\{b_j:j\in J\setminus\{i\}\}\) and its symmetric partner give necessary and sufficient conditions for location and domination. The polynomial enumeration partitions index states into zero or one omitted matched index, excludes both low-occupancy shores (disjoint cases for \(n\ge4\)), and adds all exceptional one-shore patterns. For inclusion-minimality, the proof constructs a size-\(n-1\) two-shore subcode via a two-color Hall matching when \(n\ge5\), and handles \(n=4\) separately. No reliance on numerical extrapolation is made. Independent direct signature enumeration verifies all subsets for orders 4 through 9; the mathematical proof applies to every integer \(n\ge4\).

## Originality — PASS (provisional publication priority)
The closest explicit result is Murtaza–Fazil–Javaid–Benish (arXiv:1709.05152v1, Lemma 3.3): the identity functigraph of \(K_n\) is \(K_n\square K_2\), the **complement** of the present crown graph. Its location-domination number \(n-1\) is acknowledged and does not imply a crown all-set polynomial or a full minimal-code census. Fazil–Hussain–Akbar–Ali (2022) defines the location-domination polynomial and works out complete multipartite graphs, the Petersen graph and lexicographic products; these are different objects. A 2019 paper using the name "crown graph" concerns the sunlet, not the matching-deleted biclique. Searches for matching-deleted bipartite identification codes, all-set polynomials, and minimal codes did not find a statement implying the present two-polynomial classification. Search cannot establish absolute novelty; unindexed or inaccessible work remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Identifying faulty vertices via distinct nonempty sensor signatures is the defining motivation of location-domination. The crown graph is a natural regular bipartite network and the double cover of a complete graph. A full all-set polynomial records redundant sensor placements of every cardinality, while a separate exact minimal-code polynomial records irredundant sensor configurations; the two are mathematically different invariants. The classification exhibits a sharp two-level minimal-code spectrum and exponentially many minimum codes for all \(n\ge5\), including the exceptional eight-vertex crown \(n=4\).

Source comparisons were conducted at the level of objects and logical implication, including complementation and alternate uses of "crown". Finite enumeration is only corroborative. The result does not determine locating-total, identifying, or complement-graph code polynomials; publication priority needs external expert checking.
