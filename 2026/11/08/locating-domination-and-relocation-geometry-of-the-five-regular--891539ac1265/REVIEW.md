# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The open-neighborhood trace definition is precisely implemented. The lower bound of five follows from the impossibility of twelve nonempty distinct four-bit signatures at only twenty available external incidences. Every one of 4,368 candidate five-sets is classified by induced graph type and tested by direct trace uniqueness. Two distinct Cayley presentations and two different enumeration/adjacency algorithms independently establish 416 minima, all transitions, connectivity and shortest paths from every state. The exact scripts and outputs are supplied and were executed from the final package paths. Counts are finite exhaustive proofs only.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest older literature supplies definitions and generic bounds, not the exact Clebsch optimum-state and reconfiguration classification. In the same Clebsch graph, the known 96 minimum *identifying codes* test closed-neighborhood signatures of **all** vertices; they are the 16 independent and 80 star locating-dominating states but do not imply the 320 additional single-edge states or mobility of the 416-state space. Likewise, the known 1,056 dominating five-sets satisfy only a weaker condition, so their relocation graph does not determine the smaller feasible induced subgraph. Other Clebsch studies concern zero forcing, coloring, homotopy, feedback vertex sets or metric identification, none of which supplies this condition or these distances. Searches for naming and numerical aliases found no equivalent result. Unindexed prior work remains a risk; source limitations are not proof of global novelty.

## Value — PASS
The canonical sixteen-vertex triangle-free strongly regular graph has natural optimal detector placements. A full exact three-orbit-shape census and connected optimal-placement configuration networks are meaningful combinatorial invariants. The sharp graph-edge movement restriction changes diameter and turns 80 configurations into leaves without disconnecting the solution space; this is not obtainable from either the minimum cardinality or the weaker domination census.
