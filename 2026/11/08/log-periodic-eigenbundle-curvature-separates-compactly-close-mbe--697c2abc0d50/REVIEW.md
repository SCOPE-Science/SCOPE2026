# Review of the logarithmic curvature theorem

**Correctness (PASS).** The exact triangular decomposition reduces the Fredholm and point spectra to a backward shift, a compact quasinilpotent tail and a rank-one coupling. The displayed eigenvector solves every coefficient recurrence and is holomorphic and total on the punctured disk. The common quasinilpotent-part formula follows from an explicit deep-negative-tail split; the curvature is exactly the variance of its Laurent exponents. Completing squares gives the theta approximation with two derivative controls; Fourier inversion proves genuine nonconstant least-period-one oscillation and the exact mean. The angular harmonic-mean argument excludes bounded invertible similarity for distinct damping parameters. The Schatten difference identity follows from diagonal singular values. Finite calculations check only finite truncations.

**Originality (PASS, with access risk).** Aznay–Chabbabi, *On Mbekhta's conjecture and pseudo-Fredholm operators on Banach and Hilbert spaces* (arXiv:2610.23229, Section 2), provide the specific logarithm-base-two shift and analytic-core analysis, but do not derive a holomorphic eigenbundle, theta curvature, least logarithmic period, exact curvature mean or parameter recovery. Jiang–Ji–Keshari (arXiv:1901.03993) establish the general Cowen–Douglas curvature framework, not this boundary calculation. Searches for equivalent theta and weighted-bilateral formulations identified no explicit covering theorem. Clark–Misra (1985) and Ji–Li–Yang (2001) are highly relevant older publications, but their complete texts could not be accessed; these leave a genuine historical-equivalence risk, not a claim of certified global priority. A general weighted-shift curvature framework alone does not supply the theta asymptotic, phase oscillation or precise mean without further argument.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** Spectra and the quasinilpotent part cannot tell the operators apart; even every Schatten-ideal size of their pairwise perturbations is finite. Yet a concrete geometric eigenbundle invariant recovers the otherwise hidden positive damping parameter by an explicit logarithmic average, with a sharp nonconstant phase profile. This provides a transparent spectral-versus-geometric rigidity example directly motivated by a recent pseudo-Fredholm counterexample rather than a generic reparametrization.

**Limitations and residual risks.** The formula is confined to the explicit exponential-tail weighted shifts; no uniform small-parameter asymptotics or general bilateral-shift classification is established. Related 1985 and 2001 primary texts remain inaccessible, so an earlier equivalent statement cannot be definitively excluded. Numerical truncations are not proofs of global spectral and geometric assertions.
