## Value — PASS
At the marginal \(p=1\) high-temperature flat direction, knowing only \(Z=\Theta(T^k\log T)\) or equipartition does not identify how typical microstates populate the two sublattices. The new weak limit resolves that natural uncertainty quantitatively: it determines the temperature exponent of a randomly chosen sublattice, the independent interaction-energy fluctuations and the within-sublattice composition law, with one exact leading coefficient for the whole balanced complete-bipartite family. These are usable diagnostics for finite-volume Gibbs samplers and a concrete normalization benchmark for marginal entropic-order expansions. The result is limited to a physically motivated solvable graph family and does not assert thermodynamic phase stability.
