## Correctness — PASS
The four-point failure event has exactly one contained point. The exact area disintegration gives the cone-measure boundary law \(f=h/(2A)\), and the local determinant calculation reduces the leading event to a product of adjacent boundary gaps. The integrated collision kernel yields \(\mathbb E[(U-V)_+]=1/6\), giving precisely \(A^{-3}\int h^4/\kappa\,ds\). The proof isolates macroscopic-gap, balanced-gap and fourth-point-close sectors as lower-order contributions. The coefficient is invariant under invertible linear maps because the probability experiment itself is. Exact rational polynomial arithmetic checks the threefold support example, and direct uniform-shell simulations check the sign and slow approach of the log-normalized defect. Computation alone is not an asymptotic proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
Marckert (arXiv:1402.3512, 2014) treats convex-position probabilities for interior-uniform disk samples. Marckert (arXiv:1511.03658, 2015) proves extremal comparisons for uniform samples in a convex domain; neither theorem covers a nonconvex shell. The published result *Thin-shell convexity moments recover facet cone volumes* studies the probability that the **entire hull remains in the shell**, which is distinct from all four sampled points being extreme; its smooth-body limit gives no rate for this statistic. The 1991 Carnal–Hüsler paper was available only as an abstract and is an acknowledged residual priority risk. Search results do not establish novelty by themselves; implication comparisons found no stronger coverage.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A boundary-sampled strictly convex curve makes all sampled points extreme, while the infinitesimally thick area shell is nonconvex and four-point failures appear. The new leading law identifies the non-analytic scale, its exact curvature–support coefficient, and affine invariance in a full smooth-body class. Ellipses have the universal coefficient \(2/\pi^2\), whereas threefold nonelliptic perturbations do not; the statistic therefore detects geometric shape beyond affine ellipses. This is a motivated structural boundary law rather than an isolated finite calculation.
