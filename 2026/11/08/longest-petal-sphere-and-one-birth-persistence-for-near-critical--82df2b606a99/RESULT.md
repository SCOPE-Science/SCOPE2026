# Longest-petal sphere and one-birth persistence for near-critical avalanches on directed roses

## Finding
For integers \(k\geq2\) and \(L_1,\ldots,L_k\geq3\), let \(R(L_1,\ldots,L_k)\) be the directed rose obtained by identifying a single vertex \(h\) of \(k\) consistently oriented cycles of lengths \(L_1,\ldots,L_k\). Start parallel chip firing with \(k-1\) chips at \(h\) and one chip at every other vertex. Put \(m=\max_iL_i\). The avalanche complex of this configuration satisfies
\[
\mathcal A(R(L_1,\ldots,L_k),c_0)\simeq S^{m-2}.
\]
More precisely, the parallel firing orbit has minimal period \(m\). Its time-filtration \(\mathcal A_t\), generated by all firing sets at times \(0,\ldots,t\), is contractible for \(0\leq t<m-1\), and is homotopy equivalent to \(S^{m-2}\) for all \(t\geq m-1\). Therefore, over any coefficient field, its reduced persistent homology has exactly one infinite interval \([m-1,\infty)\) in degree \(m-2\), and no other intervals. The integral reduced homology is \(\mathbb Z\) in that degree and zero otherwise. Adding one chip at the hub at time zero makes the avalanche complex a full simplex, hence contractible.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(h\) be the common hub. In petal \(i\), write its other vertices as \(v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,L_i-1}\), and its directed edges as
\[
h\to v_{i,1}\to v_{i,2}\to\cdots\to v_{i,L_i-1}\to h.
\]
The petal interiors are pairwise disjoint, so the graph has \(1+\sum_i(L_i-1)\) vertices and \(\sum_iL_i\) directed edges, and no sink. The hub has outdegree \(k\); every other vertex has outdegree one. In parallel firing, every vertex whose chip count is at least its outdegree fires simultaneously, sending one chip along every outgoing arc. The firing set \(F_t\) consists of the vertices firing at time \(t\). The avalanche complex is the downward-closed simplicial complex generated by all \(F_t\), with the empty face understood in the usual simplicial convention. The time-filtration \(\mathcal A_t\) uses \(F_0,\ldots,F_t\). All times are nonnegative integers. The starting number of chips is exactly \(\sum_iL_i-1\), one below the number of directed edges.

## Proof
**1. Explicit orbit.** At time zero, the hub has \(k-1\) chips and every peripheral vertex has one chip. Hence all peripheral vertices fire and the hub does not. At time one the hub has \(2k-1\) chips and the unique zero on each petal is at its first peripheral vertex.

For every integer \(1\leq t\leq m\), write \(N(t)=|\{i:L_i\leq t\}|\). We claim that at time \(t\) the hub contains \(2k-1-N(t)\) chips and the peripheral chips are all one, except that the vertex \(v_{i,t}\) contains zero whenever \(t<L_i\). In particular no other peripheral vertex has zero. The assertion for \(t=1\) follows from the first firing step because all petal lengths are at least three. For \(1\leq t<m\), at least one petal has \(L_i>t\), so \(N(t)\leq k-1\). The hub therefore has at least \(k\) chips and fires. Every peripheral vertex other than the listed zeros fires. The zero advances one vertex along every still-active petal, and disappears after reaching its last peripheral vertex; this follows directly from the single incoming arc at every peripheral vertex, with the hub's firing providing the input to the first vertex. At time \(t\), the peripheral vertices pointing into the hub all fire except when \(t=L_i-1\), so the hub's net chip loss on passage to \(t+1\) is exactly \(|\{i:L_i=t+1\}|\). This proves the claimed formula by induction.

At time \(m\) every petal has completed its moving zero and the hub has \(2k-1-k=k-1\) chips, so the entire configuration equals its time-zero value. For \(0\leq t<m\) the firing sets are therefore exactly
\[
F_0=V\setminus\{h\},\qquad
F_t=V\setminus\{v_{i,t}:L_i>t\}\quad(1\leq t<m).
\]
Fix a petal \(j\) of maximum length \(m\). The hub is excluded from \(F_0\) and included in every later firing set, and for each \(1\leq t<m\), the vertex \(v_{j,t}\) is excluded from \(F_t\) but included in all other firing sets. Consequently all \(m\) firing sets are distinct; no shorter positive period is possible.

**2. The nerve.** Regard each \(F_t\) as the full simplex it spans. Every nonempty finite intersection of these simplices is again a full simplex and hence contractible. Their nerve is therefore homotopy equivalent to the avalanche complex by the simplicial nerve lemma. The intersection of all \(m\) sets is empty: the hub is missing from \(F_0\), while each peripheral vertex \(v_{i,r}\), where \(1\leq r<L_i\leq m\), is missing from \(F_r\). On the other hand, every proper subfamily has nonempty intersection: omit one time \(s\); if \(s=0\) the hub lies in every retained set, and if \(1\leq s<m\) the vertex \(v_{j,s}\) of the chosen longest petal lies in every retained set. Thus the nerve consists of every proper subset of an \(m\)-element vertex set, i.e. the boundary of an \((m-1)\)-simplex, which is \(S^{m-2}\). This proves the integral homotopy and homology assertions.

**3. Filtration and one-grain transition.** If \(0\leq t<m-1\), the vertex \(v_{j,m-1}\) belongs to every one of \(F_0,\ldots,F_t\). Accordingly \(\mathcal A_t\) is a cone, hence contractible. At time \(m-1\), the full nerve becomes the boundary sphere established above; repetition of the configuration at time \(m\) means no new simplices appear later. The claimed unique infinite persistence interval follows, including the reduced degree-zero case. If one additional chip is put at the hub initially, the hub has \(k\) chips while all peripheral vertices still have one, so the first firing set is all vertices and the resulting avalanche complex is a simplex.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with the Python standard library. The script independently constructs the graph, simulates parallel firing until the first repeated configuration, compares all firing sets against the theorem, checks every proper intersection in the nerve and all early common intersections, and tests the one-chip transition. It exhaustively checks all \(3900\) ordered length tuples of two through five petals with each length from three through seven, accounting for \(25516\) firing steps. It also independently builds the simplicial boundary matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\) for six representative cases, recovering one reduced homology generator precisely in degree \(m-2\). Expected terminal output is `ALL_VERIFY_OK`, and `verification_output.txt` records the actual run. These finite tests corroborate rather than replace the proof for arbitrary \(k\) and lengths.

## Relationship to prior work
Riihimäki and Smith [1], first posted on June 25, 2026, define avalanche complexes and their time filtrations and establish sphere results for single directed cycles and several path configurations. Their Corollary 3.33 describes the near-full-chip directed cycle; their Lemma 3.27 uses the nerve method for congruence-class zeros on a **single cycle**. Here the central vertex has arbitrary outdegree \(k\geq2\), cycles share that vertex, the initial configuration is nonbinary at the hub for \(k>2\), and the resulting sphere dimension and first persistent birth depend on the **longest** petal despite the number and lengths of the others. The method of using the nerve is credited to [1]; neither its general form nor the known single-cycle sphere is claimed as new. A standard chip-firing reference [2] supplies dynamical background but not this avalanche-complex homotopy or persistence theorem.

## Limitations
The theorem applies only to consistently oriented bouquets with disjoint petal interiors, every length at least three, and the stated near-critical initial configuration (and the specified one-chip modification). It does not classify arbitrary chip distributions, more general directed cacti, different orientations, or the complete persistence of other configurations. An equivalent unindexed theorem cannot be excluded. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
