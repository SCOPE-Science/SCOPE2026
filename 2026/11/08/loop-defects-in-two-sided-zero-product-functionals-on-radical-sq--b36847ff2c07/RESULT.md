# Loop defects in two-sided zero-product functionals on radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Finding

Let \(k\) be **any field**, let \(Q\) be a finite quiver with at least one vertex (parallel arrows, loops, and directed cycles are permitted), and let \(A=kQ/J^2\), where \(J\) is the ideal generated by the arrows. Write \(n=|Q_0|\), \(m=|Q_1|\), and let \(\ell\) be the number of loops, counted with multiplicity. Set \(N=n+m=\dim_k A\).

A bilinear functional \(\varphi:A\times A\to k\) has the **two-sided zero-product property** if \(\varphi(x,y)=0\) whenever \(xy=yx=0\). Let \(\mathcal F\) be the vector space of these functionals, and let \(\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal F\) consist of those of product form \(\varphi(x,y)=\tau_1(xy)+\tau_2(yx)\), with \(\tau_1,\tau_2\in A^*\). Then

\[
\boxed{\dim_k\mathcal F=n+2m,\qquad \dim_k\mathcal P=n+2m-\ell,\qquad\dim_k(\mathcal F/\mathcal P)=\ell.}
\]

In particular, \(A\) is two-sided zero-product determined (2-zpd) **if and only if \(Q\) has no loops**, with no acyclicity assumption. For every loop \(a:i\to i\), let \(\alpha_i(x)\) denote the coefficient of the vertex idempotent \(e_i\) in \(x\), and \(\lambda_a(x)\) the coefficient of the arrow \(a\). Then the classes of

\[
\boxed{\varphi_a(x,y)=\alpha_i(x)\lambda_a(y)}
\]

form a basis of \(\mathcal F/\mathcal P\). Every two-sided-zero-product functional consequently has a decomposition

\[
\varphi(x,y)=\tau_1(xy)+\tau_2(yx)+\sum_{\substack{a\in Q_1\\s(a)=t(a)}}c_a\alpha_{s(a)}(x)\lambda_a(y).
\]

The loop coefficients \(c_a\) are uniquely determined, although \(\tau_1,\tau_2\) need not be unique. If \(k=\mathbb F_q\), the numbers of all two-sided-zero-product and product-form bilinear functionals are respectively \(q^{n+2m}\) and \(q^{n+2m-\ell}\); the product-form fraction is \(q^{-\ell}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The arrow ideal \(J\) is used only to impose all products of two arrows equal to zero. For a vertex \(i\) and an arrow \(a:s(a)\to t(a)\), the multiplication rules in \(A\) are

\[
e_i e_j=\delta_{ij}e_i,\qquad e_i a=\delta_{i,s(a)}a,\qquad a e_i=\delta_{i,t(a)}a,\qquad ab=0
\]

for all arrows \(a,b\). The vertex-and-arrow basis exists over every field. No restrictions on its characteristic or cardinality are used in the proof. All dimensions are over \(k\). The 2-zpd terminology concerns bilinear **functionals**, not merely linear maps on the algebra.

## Proof

**1. The universal tensor quotient.** Let

\[
S=\operatorname{span}_k\{x\otimes y\in A\otimes_k A:xy=yx=0\},\qquad W=(A\otimes_k A)/S.
\]

The functionals on \(W\) are exactly the bilinear functionals in \(\mathcal F\), so \(\dim\mathcal F=\dim W\). Every tensor \(a\otimes b\) of two arrows belongs to \(S\), because \(J^2=0\). Similarly \(e_i\otimes e_j\in S\) for \(i\ne j\), and \(e_i\otimes a,a\otimes e_i\in S\) whenever \(i\notin\{s(a),t(a)\}\).

For a nonloop arrow \(a:i\to j\) (so \(i\ne j\)), both products of \(e_i+a\) and \(e_j-a\), in either order, are zero. Their tensor therefore gives

\[
e_i\otimes a\equiv a\otimes e_j\pmod S.
\]

Likewise, the mutually annihilating pair \((e_j+a,e_i-a)\) gives

\[
e_j\otimes a\equiv a\otimes e_i\pmod S.
\]

These identities are valid even in characteristic two. Thus \(W\) is spanned by the \(n\) cosets of \(e_i\otimes e_i\), together with at most two cosets per arrow: \(e_{s(a)}\otimes a\) and \(a\otimes e_{s(a)}\). In the loop case these are the two remaining mixed tensors without an additional relation. Consequently

\[
\dim W\le n+2m.
\]

**2. Rank of the product-pair map.** Consider the linear map

\[
P:A\otimes_k A\longrightarrow A\oplus A,\qquad P(x\otimes y)=(xy,yx).
\]

It annihilates \(S\). Its image has the explicit independent generating family

\[
(e_i,e_i)\quad(i\in Q_0),\qquad (a,0),(0,a)\quad(a\text{ a nonloop}),\qquad (a,a)\quad(a\text{ a loop}).
\]

These are all possible components of a product pair, and each is obtained from a displayed basis tensor. Hence

\[
\operatorname{rank}P=n+2(m-\ell)+\ell=n+2m-\ell.
\]

The annihilator of \(\ker P\) is exactly the space \(\mathcal P\) of product-form bilinear functionals; therefore \(\dim\mathcal P=n+2m-\ell\).

**3. Independent loop obstructions.** Fix a loop \(a:i\to i\). The coefficient of \(e_i\) in \(xy\) is \(\alpha_i(x)\alpha_i(y)\). If \(xy=0\) and \(\alpha_i(x)\ne0\), this forces \(\alpha_i(y)=0\). The coefficient of \(a\) in \(xy\) is

\[
\alpha_i(x)\lambda_a(y)+\lambda_a(x)\alpha_i(y).
\]

It must also vanish, forcing \(\lambda_a(y)=0\). Thus \(\varphi_a(x,y)=\alpha_i(x)\lambda_a(y)\) vanishes whenever \(xy=0\), and a fortiori whenever \(xy=yx=0\). In particular \(\varphi_a\in\mathcal F\).

For each loop \(a:i\to i\), the tensors \(e_i\otimes a\) and \(a\otimes e_i\) have identical images \((a,a)\) under \(P\). Every product-form functional consequently has equal values on these two tensors. But \(\varphi_b(e_i,a)-\varphi_b(a,e_i)\) is \(1\) if \(b=a\) and \(0\) for all other loops \(b\). The \(\ell\) classes \([\varphi_a]\) are therefore linearly independent modulo \(\mathcal P\).

It follows that

\[
\dim W=\dim\mathcal F\ge\dim\mathcal P+\ell=n+2m.
\]

Combining this with the upper bound from Step 1 proves all three dimension identities. Independence and dimension also show that the loop forms give a basis of the quotient, yielding the displayed complete decomposition. The loop-free criterion and finite-field enumerators follow immediately. \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` performs independent finite-field enumeration of all pairs \((x,y)\) with \(xy=yx=0\) in ten small quiver examples over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). It builds the multiplication table from the vertex/arrow rules, computes by exact modular elimination (i) the span of pure tensors \(x\otimes y\) and (ii) the rank of the product-pair map, and tests all loop-obstruction functionals on the entire zero-product locus. In all twenty tests, the zero-product tensor span has dimension \(N^2-(n+2m)\), the product-pair map has rank \(n+2m-\ell\), and the defect is exactly \(\ell\). Run `python3 verify.py` from the extracted package; successful completion prints `VERIFY_OK`.

For instance, the oriented two-cycle \(1\to2\to1\) has \((n,m,\ell)=(2,2,0)\). Its 16-dimensional tensor square has a ten-dimensional mutually annihilating pure-tensor span, equal to \(\ker P\), even though the algebra has a directed cycle. The one-loop algebra \(k[t]/(t^2)\) has a one-dimensional obstruction; the two-loop one-vertex algebra has a two-dimensional obstruction. These are complete finite checks only for their stated small cases, not substitutes for the arbitrary-field proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Bajuk and Brešar introduced the 2-zpd condition and gave tensor-space equivalents, sufficient homological criteria, and a classification for finite-dimensional simple nondivision algebras in *Two-sided zero product determined algebras* (arXiv:2209.13194v1, first publicly posted 27 September 2022). The present proof uses the same definition but neither assumes semisimplicity nor finite global dimension, and computes the **entire** space of zero-product bilinear functionals on radical-square-zero quiver algebras. In particular, oriented cycles are included; these are outside the finite-global-dimension route used in their Theorem 3.1.

Hu and Xiao characterized finite-dimensional algebras generated by idempotents, a related property because zero-product-determined algebras are generated by idempotents. That one-sided criterion alone does not yield the loop-by-loop two-sided defect calculation. Later work by Jia and Xiao on two-sided zero products of **incidence algebras** concerns finite posets, a different algebra class: a radical-square-zero oriented cycle, for example, is not a poset incidence algebra. The exact defect formula for arbitrary loops and multiple arrows is not asserted in the inspected material. Literature priority is provisional; a broader unlocated 2-zpd theorem might imply this calculation.

## Limitations

The theorem is for finite quivers and the specific quotient by all paths of length two. It does not claim the same loop criterion for higher radical powers, nonsplit species, nonfield base rings, arbitrary finite-dimensional algebras, or representation categories. The exact all-field theorem is proved by tensor-space arguments; computational enumeration is limited to the displayed finite fields and examples. Full comparison with some potentially relevant later functional-identity literature was not possible from accessible primary full text, so independent publication-priority assessment remains open.
