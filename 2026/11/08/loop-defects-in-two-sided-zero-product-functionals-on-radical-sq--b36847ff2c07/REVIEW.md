# Scientific assessment

## Correctness

PASS. The proof computes the universal quotient of the tensor square by mutually annihilating pure tensors. Arrow-square-zero and two explicit mutually annihilating combinations reduce its spanning set to one tensor per vertex and two per arrow. The product-pair map has rank equal to the number of vertices plus twice the nonloop arrows plus once the loop arrows. Independent loop-coordinate bilinear forms achieve the spanning upper bound. The same argument covers arbitrary field characteristics and all finite quivers, including parallel arrows and directed cycles. The exact finite-field verifier independently tests twenty cases and checks the functional obstruction condition directly. Risk: enumeration by itself proves only finite cases; the general result rests on the written tensor proof.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected sources. Bajuk–Brešar (arXiv:2209.13194v1), inspected through its full nine-page preprint, supplies the 2-zpd definition, tensor equivalents, and finite-global-dimension and simple-algebra cases, not the loop-defect formula. Hu–Xiao's one-sided idempotent-generation criterion does not determine the two-sided functional quotient. Jia–Xiao's later incidence-algebra result covers a different class and cannot directly cover an oriented two-cycle with radical square zero, which is not an incidence algebra. The possible relevance of Koşan–Lee–Lin's symmetric-algebra results remains a literature risk because accessible text was an abstract, not its full proof; a broader unpublished or unlocated theorem might imply the result. A failed search is not a novelty proof. The claim is a provisional mathematical originality assessment, not publication certification.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a complete necessary-and-sufficient graph-theoretic criterion for the two-sided zero-product property on **all** radical-square-zero quiver algebras, and an exact dimension count of its failure. It resolves the finite-global-dimension limitation for this class by including oriented cycles, and determines a canonical quotient of bilinear functionals together with its explicit basis and finite-field enumerator. The loop count is the natural first-extension invariant of a split basic algebra, not an arbitrarily chosen numerical slice. The simplicity of the proof does not reduce the structural scope or classification content.
