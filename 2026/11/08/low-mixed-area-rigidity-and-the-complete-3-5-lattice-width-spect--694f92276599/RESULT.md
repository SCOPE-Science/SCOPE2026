# Low mixed-area rigidity and the complete \(3:5\) lattice-width spectrum

## Finding

Let \(u\in\mathbb Z^2\) be primitive, and let \(P,Q\) be nonempty lattice polytopes (segments and points are permitted) with positive directional widths
\[
\operatorname{w}_u(P)=ad,\qquad \operatorname{w}_u(Q)=bd,
\]
where \(d\ge1\) and \(1\le a<b\) are integers with \(\gcd(a,b)=1\). Define the normalized mixed area by
\[
\operatorname{V}(P,Q)=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}_2(P+Q)-\operatorname{Vol}_2(P)-\operatorname{Vol}_2(Q)}2,
\quad \operatorname{Vol}_2(R)=2\operatorname{area}(R).
\]
Then **the exact low mixed-area spectrum** is
\[
\{\operatorname{V}(P,Q):0\le\operatorname{V}(P,Q)<\min(d,b)\}
=\{ka:k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\ ka<\min(d,b)\}.
\]
Moreover, whenever \(0<\operatorname{V}(P,Q)<\min(d,b)\), the smaller-width polytope \(P\) must be a lattice segment, and \(Q\) must be two-dimensional. The segment \(P\) is parallel to every segment in \(Q\) joining a point of minimal \(u\)-height to a point of maximal \(u\)-height. This rigidity statement is part of the finding, not merely a consequence of counting.

A complete, all-parameter section of the discrete mixed-area diagram follows. Let \(\Phi_{3d,5d}\) be the set of normalized mixed areas of lattice-polytope pairs with directional widths \(3d\) and \(5d\) in a fixed primitive direction. Then, for every integer \(d\ge1\),
\[
\Phi_{3d,5d}=\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z_{\ge0},&d=1,\\
\{0\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge2},&d=2,\\
\{0\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge3},&d=3,4,\\
\{0,3\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge5},&d\ge5.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the missing value \(4\) persists for every dilation index \(d\ge5\), even though \(7\), which does not belong to the semigroup generated by \(3\) and \(5\), is always attainable. The parameter \(d=5\) is the sharp onset of this persistent hole.

## Assumptions and scope

All polytopes are convex hulls of finitely many points in \(\mathbb Z^2\), with no full-dimensionality assumption. The directional width is \(\operatorname{w}_u(P)=\max_{x\in P}\langle u,x\rangle-\min_{x\in P}\langle u,x\rangle\). A vector is primitive when its two integer coordinates are relatively prime. Each occurrence of mixed area uses the normalized lattice convention above; for lattice segments \([0,A]\) and \([0,B]\), this gives \(\operatorname{V}([0,A],[0,B])=|\det(A,B)|\). The direction \(u\) is fixed for each pair, but the answer is independent of which primitive direction is chosen by simultaneous unimodular coordinate change. The theorem applies to all positive integers \(a,b,d\) satisfying the stated coprimality and strict-order conditions, and to all such lattice polytopes.

## Proof

**1. Rigidity below the two natural barriers.** A simultaneous unimodular transformation allows \(u=(0,1)\). Pick vertices \(p_-,p_+\in P\) attaining the minimum and maximum vertical coordinate, and similarly \(q_-,q_+\in Q\). Let \(A=p_+-p_-\), \(B=q_+-q_-\). Their vertical components are \(ad\) and \(bd\). By monotonicity of mixed area and the segment determinant formula,
\[
\operatorname{V}(P,Q)\ge |\det(A,B)|
=d\,|bA_1-aB_1|.
\]
When \(0<\operatorname{V}(P,Q)<d\), the determinant must vanish. Consequently \(A\) and \(B\) are parallel. Write them as \(A=a t v\), \(B=b t v\), where \(v=(v_1,v_2)\) is a primitive integer vector with \(v_2>0\) and \(t\) is a positive integer. To justify this normalization, \(v_2\) divides both vertical components \(ad\) and \(bd\), so coprimality forces \(v_2\mid d\) and \(t=d/v_2\).

Set \(J=[q_-,q_+]\), whose lattice length is \(bt\). If \(P\) contained any lattice point outside the line parallel to \(v\) through \(p_-\), its width in the primitive integer direction \((-v_2,v_1)\) would be at least one. The segment–polygon mixed-area formula would therefore give
\[
\operatorname{V}(P,Q)\ge\operatorname{V}(P,J)
=bt\operatorname{w}_{(-v_2,v_1)}(P)\ge b,
\]
contrary to the other hypothesis \(\operatorname{V}(P,Q)<b\). Hence \(P\) lies on the line containing \([p_-,p_+]\), and, because the endpoints already attain its full vertical range, \(P=[p_-,p_+]\). If \(Q\) were a segment it would equal \(J\), which is parallel to \(P\), giving zero mixed area. Thus \(Q\) is two-dimensional. Finally,
\[
\operatorname{V}(P,Q)=at\operatorname{w}_{(-v_2,v_1)}(Q),
\]
where the final width is an integer. In particular, \(a\mid\operatorname{V}(P,Q)\).

**2. Realization of all allowed small values.** For every integer \(k\ge1\) with \(ak<\min(d,b)\), set
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(a,ad)\},\qquad
Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(b,bd),(0,k)\}.
\]
Because \(k< b/a\le b\le bd\), the two vertical widths are exactly \(ad\) and \(bd\). The primitive normal \((-d,1)\) to the direction \((1,d)\) has width \(k\) on \(Q\), so the segment–polygon formula gives \(\operatorname{V}(P,Q)=ak\). Zero is realized by parallel segments of the prescribed widths. This proves the first asserted equality and its rigidity statement.

**3. Uniform semigroup realizations for the \(3:5\) section.** Every nonnegative integer combination \(3m_1+5m_2\) occurs at widths \(3d,5d\), for all \(d\ge1\). Here is an explicit construction, equivalent to the semigroup construction in [1, Proposition 4.3]. Write \(m_i=h_i d+r_i\) with \(0\le r_i<d\). Put \(I=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0)\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3,3d),(3,3d-r_2)\},\\
Q_0&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(0,r_1),(5,5d)\},\\
P&=P_0+h_2I,\qquad Q=Q_0+h_1I.
\end{aligned}
\]
The directional widths remain \(3d,5d\). Direct normalized mixed-area computation gives \(\operatorname{V}(P_0,Q_0)=3r_1+5r_2\); Minkowski bilinearity and \(\operatorname{V}(P_0,I)=3d\), \(\operatorname{V}(Q_0,I)=5d\) then yield \(\operatorname{V}(P,Q)=3m_1+5m_2\). Thus \(0,3,5,6\) and every integer at least \(8\) occur.

**4. A non-semigroup witness at \(7\) in every section.** Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_d&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3,3d),(1,d+1),(0,1)\},\\
Q_d&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(5,5d),(3,3d+1),(0,2)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These have widths \(3d,5d\). They are the simultaneous images of \(P_1,Q_1\) under the unimodular shear \((x,y)\mapsto(x,y+(d-1)x)\), hence their normalized mixed area is constant. Direct convex-hull and shoelace calculations at \(d=1\) give
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_2(P_1)=4,\quad
\operatorname{Vol}_2(Q_1)=11,\quad
\operatorname{Vol}_2(P_1+Q_1)=29,
\]
so \(\operatorname{V}(P_d,Q_d)=(29-4-11)/2=7\) for every \(d\ge1\). The shear also provides an exact explanation of the parameter-independent construction.

**5. The sole remaining small value and the phase transition.** The segment pair
\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,3d)\},\qquad
\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3,5d)\}
\]
has normalized mixed area \(d\). Thus \(1\) occurs when \(d=1\) and \(2\) occurs when \(d=2\). At \(d=1\), the segments from the origin to \((1,3)\) and \((1,5)\) have mixed area \(2\); the segment to \((1,3)\) and the triangle with vertices \((0,0),(2,5),(0,3)\) have mixed area \(4\), since the primitive normal \((-3,1)\) has range \(4\) on that triangle. For \(d=2,3,4\), put
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,3d)\},\quad
Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,5d),(1,2d+4)\}.
\]
The vertical widths are \(3d,5d\), and the primitive normal \((-3d,1)\) takes values \(0,-d,4-d\) on \(Q\). For \(d=2,3,4\), their range is \(4\), hence \(\operatorname{V}(P,Q)=4\). By the first part, however, \(4\) is impossible once \(d\ge5\): it lies below \(\min(d,5)=5\) but is not divisible by \(3\). Likewise \(1,2\) are excluded for every \(d\ge3\), and \(1\) is excluded for \(d=2\). All positive integers not excluded occur by the constructions in Steps 3–5. This proves every branch of the complete spectrum.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify.py` uses integer convex hulls, shoelace areas, and direct Minkowski-sum vertex addition. It checks all semigroup constructions for \(1\le d\le40\) and \(0\le m_1,m_2<12\), all \(7\)-witnesses in this range, small-value witnesses including \(d=1,2\), arbitrary-coprime-width realization families, and a fixed-seed randomized sample of lattice-polytope pairs. It produced **22,200 successful integer-arithmetic checks**. The checks are finite corroborations; the infinite-dimensional-in-parameters assertions follow from Steps 1–5. Run with `python3 verify.py` from the extracted directory using Python 3 without external packages.

## Relationship to prior work

Gerrity and Soprunov [1] introduced the fixed-width discrete diagram and proved the general gap, semigroup, and saturation inclusions in their Theorem 4.5. Their Remark 4.6 establishes the exact spectrum for the single width pair \((30,50)\), using a classification of mixed-area-four pairs to exclude \(4\). That specific instance is **prior work**, not a newly claimed special case. The present result instead obtains an all-coprime-width rigidity principle and its exact low-value spectrum for every \((ad,bd)\), then uses it to classify the entire \(3:5\) ratio for every integer \(d\). The new exclusion argument does not require the small-mixed-volume classification database. Soprunov and Soprunova [2] classify the possible unconstrained pairs of area and mixed-area data, but do not fix a primitive directional width. Averkov and Soprunov [3] describe relations among mixed areas of four arbitrary convex planar bodies; those inequalities do not encode this lattice-width divisibility obstruction.

## Limitations

The general theorem classifies only normalized mixed areas **strictly below** \(\min(d,b)\); it is not a complete mixed-area diagram for arbitrary \((a,b)\). The complete all-value statement is confined to the ratio \(3:5\). The polytopes may be lower dimensional; forbidding segments changes the realization problem. The proof establishes mathematics, not independent historical priority. The exact single case \((30,50)\) belongs to prior literature. An independent mathematical review has not been performed.
