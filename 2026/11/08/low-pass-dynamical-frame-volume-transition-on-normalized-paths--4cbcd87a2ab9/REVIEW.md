# Scientific review: low-pass dynamical frame-volume transition

## Correctness — PASS
The result defines a normalized path, the first \(r\) spectral modes, the number of consecutive observation times, and D-optimality as a Gramian determinant. The normalized-path eigenvectors and eigenvalues are derived from the cosine recurrence, and the Vandermonde determinant separates all temporal evolution dependence from the sensor coordinate. The resulting exact degree-weighted cosine product proves the Gramian-volume ratio. The three-mode proof bounds the secondary interior trigonometric lobe and establishes the exact seven-vertex transition. The all-band result uses a product comparison and explicit elementary inequalities for \(n\ge4(r-1)^3\). Small boundary cases are treated explicitly. A standard-library program supplies separately implemented finite numerical cross-checks of the matrices and identities, but does not substitute for the general proof.

The inspected introduction and abstract do not contain the sharp low-pass D-optimal site classification; the full finite-path section was not accessible on later fetches, so this comparison carries a specified priority risk. Parlangeli–Notarstefano characterize graph observability, not frame-volume optimization. Cabrelli et al. provide general finite-index dynamical sampling theory; Yin–Schaposnik analyze movement stability of sensor designs under projector perturbations, not this exact bandlimited path configuration. ## Value — PASS
The exact first nontrivial three-mode transition solves a natural placement question in low-pass graph dynamical sampling, not an arbitrary graph parameter. It shows that the full-band endpoint-optimal rule can reverse under a bandwidth constraint. The all-fixed-band theorem gives an explicit guaranteed regime for optimal sensor placement, and the invariance under any injective spectral evolution makes the rule useful for several dynamical measurement models. The invariant is the D-optimal frame volume, which measures joint recovery geometry; no claim is made about smallest singular values.
