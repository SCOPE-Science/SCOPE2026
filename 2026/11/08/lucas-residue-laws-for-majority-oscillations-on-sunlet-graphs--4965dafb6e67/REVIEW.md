# Scientific review: Lucas-residue laws for majority oscillations on sunlet graphs

## Correctness — PASS

The binary majority update on the \(2n\)-vertex sunlet has explicit equations \(M_n(a,b)=(\operatorname{maj}(a_{i-1},a_{i+1},b_i),a)\). The period-two condition is **equivalent** to two local allowed-neighbor constraints at each indexed site. Their length-\(n\) cyclic solutions are counted without rotational quotient by the trace of an explicitly defined \(16\times16\) zero-one matrix. Integer Newton identities certify its characteristic polynomial; its spectral power sum gives all three residue cases exactly. Separate size-four matrices establish the fixed-state and pure-cycle Lucas corrections. The odd-degree symmetric-threshold energy argument establishes eventual periods no larger than two. Independent state-space enumeration examines every state for \(3\le n\le11\) and agrees with all closed forms. The independent size-16 transfer checker verifies the determinant and trace arithmetic for \(3\le n\le30\). The universal theorem follows from the exact local equivalences, not from extrapolating finite experiments.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Goles–Olivos (1980/81) proves the general fixed/two-cycle restriction, and Kuhlman et al. (arXiv:1108.2974v1) gives a related bi-threshold framework with an English reconstruction of the classical argument. These cover **possible periods** but not their sunlet populations. Tošić–Agha (2004) studies one-dimensional ordinary majority cellular automata on lines/rings and proves pattern restrictions there; the sunlet update contains a distinct pendant bit per site, generating a second-time-level constraint and a nonidentical transfer spectrum. Nakar–Ron (2022) treats ordinary one-dimensional radius-based majority, while Turau (2022) treats tree graphs; their stated scopes exclude the unicyclic sunlet. Closely related published results on binary **parity** automata concern a different update rule and therefore cannot imply this nonlinear majority census. Direct searches for sunlet/pendant cycle/corona majority attractor counts and closed Lucas formulas did not identify the claimed result. Search failure cannot establish novelty; unindexed work and the unavailable original Goles–Olivos full text remain risks, but no inspected stronger theorem or exact table covers these quantities. This review does not claim novelty for Lucas numbers, the general energy argument, or transfer matrices as a method.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sunlet is a canonical sparse unicyclic graph family, and its pendant leaves act as one-step local memory for the cycle. The resulting exact three-way classification of fixed states, all period-two states, and globally flipping period-two states exposes a concrete structural effect: on the undecorated majority ring there are at most one two-cycle, whereas on the decorated family there are asymptotically \(\varphi^{2n}/2\) of them. It also separates mostly partially frozen oscillations from fully toggling ones at a sharp exponentially small ratio \(\varphi^{-n}\), and gives the exact periodic-state proportion. These are naturally motivated invariant populations for infinitely many graph sizes, not an arbitrary finite cutoff.

The result does not characterize attraction basins, transient lengths, or higher-alphabet and asynchronous systems. Hidden earlier equivalences remain possible; external validation is not asserted.
