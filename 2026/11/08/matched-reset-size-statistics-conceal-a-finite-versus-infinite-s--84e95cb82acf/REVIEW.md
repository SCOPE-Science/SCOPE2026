# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The invariant Gaussian scale-mixture representation follows by exponential contraction of the reset-modulated homogeneous propagator. The first-event decomposition yields a positive affine perpetuity, and its finite positive fractional moments are characterized using subadditivity or Minkowski sufficiency, together with strict necessity from its nonzero additive term. This validates the iff growth-exponent test even at equality. The signed third-divided-difference identity gives strict separation for every real order above two. At \(s=1/4\), exact fractions \(17/32\) and \(481/1024\) give respectively \(+\lambda/8\) and \(-31\lambda/256\) fourth-moment growth exponents, and the stationary second-moment identity yields \(4D/\lambda\). The B fourth moment follows from the legitimate fourth-moment balance. Uniform convergence of scaled root equations establishes the two distinct limiting constants. The independent checker confirms finite specializations; it does not establish infinite-order statements.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Galla (arXiv:2602.21635v1, main text, Appendix A.4, Supplement S1.1–S1.2) gives the random-amplitude Gaussian approximation and stationary surrogate density, including numerical tests, but not this matched two-law stationary fourth-moment split or the universal real-order cutoff comparison. Biroli et al. (arXiv:2406.08387) treat deterministic rescaling without the positive growth chosen here. Nakao (arXiv:cond-mat/9802030) studies multiplicative Gaussian-noise intermittency. Behme–Lindner (arXiv:1301.3272) and classical perpetuity work supply broad existence and moment methods: the elementary fractional-moment criterion is not claimed as an invention. The mathematical comparison is the specific low-statistics indistinguishability versus fourth-moment nonidentifiability, strict interpolation order and singular gap. No indexed result inspected states this conjunction or supplies it as a displayed corollary. Broader unindexed comparison and Lévy-functional results could cover parts, so independent priority review is still needed.

## Value — PASS

The motivating theory specifically replaces fast random reset amplitudes by a Gaussian diffusion determined by the first two jump moments. Two physically admissible protocols with the same those moments, even the same **exact** spatial variance, exhibit opposite qualitative fourth-moment behavior. This is a natural substantive boundary for the approximation when fluctuations beyond variance matter; the strict all-order and \(1/s\)-scaled cutoff results show it is not an isolated arithmetic example. The result is self-contained and reproducible; it makes no universal claim for other parameter choices.
