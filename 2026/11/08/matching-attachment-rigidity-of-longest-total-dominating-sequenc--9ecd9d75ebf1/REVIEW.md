# Scientific review: longest total dominating sequences in triangular graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The graph is \(T_n=L(K_n)\), \(n\ge3\), with standard **open** neighborhoods. After any prefix the undominated vertices are exactly the edges among unused ground vertices together with selected edges constituting isolated two-vertex components. This gives all five legal types, the two conservation identities, and the exact length formula \(L=n-u-b_1-c_2\). Endpoint arguments force \(L\le n-1\); equality requires \(n\) odd, one last-unused-vertex attachment and no isolated-isolated merges. The even construction attains \(n-2\). A matching-first/attachment-last bijection gives the odd formula \(n!(n-1)!/2^{(n-1)/2}\), and contracting a near-perfect matching proves the unordered spanning-tree characterization. The two independent standalone computational checks agree for all \(3\le n\le11\); they do not substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified historical priority)
The closest foundational research is Brešar–Henning–Rall (arXiv:1601.07525v1), which defines Grundy *total* domination and gives general structural principles. Brešar–Kos–Nasini–Torres (arXiv:1608.06804v1) considers trees as input graphs, split graphs and modular decomposition, not longest total dominating sequences in triangular graphs. The Kneser paper of Brešar–Kos–Torres concerns the *complement* graph with disjointness adjacency; its abstract/excerpt did not provide a reduction implying this result. OEIS A290716 counts minimal *ordinary* dominating sets of triangular graphs, a different invariant and cardinality from longest ordered *total* dominating sequences. The relevant sections and text-searchable full texts of the first two papers were inspected, including searches for triangular graphs and line graphs; no covering statement was identified. Search across mathematical records, equivalent terms, and relevant previously established claims likewise found no implication of the odd factorial count or near-perfect-matching classification. This supports a qualified originality judgment; an unindexed or inaccessible publication may still have priority.

## Value — PASS
The number of ordered *maximum* total dominating sequences, unlike simply the maximum length, records how many genuinely different optimal open-neighborhood discovery processes exist. On the natural dense line graphs of cliques, a sharp parity effect forces longest odd-order sequences to reveal an initially disjoint near-perfect matching followed by a tree-building phase. The factorial formula and the characterization of exact unordered supports as trees with a near-perfect matching provide a useful nontrivial extremal structure and a checkable benchmark for sequence enumeration. They are not just a substitution into a general graph bound.

For the Kneser complement comparison, only public abstract/excerpt material could be confirmed; no whole-document negative conclusion is drawn from that incomplete inspection. The theorem does not count even-order optima, and its odd ordered count is not the number of distinct trees. All source dates are first verified public preprint dates, not later journal dates.
