# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof fixes the complete state space and local rule, derives the local module ranks from \(x+x^{-1}=x^{-1}(x+1)^2\), and carries out an invertible multivariate change of variables using the Frobenius identity at a power-of-two exponent. It reconstructs the full spatial Jordan form, not just the nilpotency index. The reversible matrix identity, its kernel dimension, and the phase-space dimension then force every unipotent block to have maximal possible size. The fixed-point formula follows from ranks of Jordan powers, and positive differences certify realization of every claimed period. The two independent exact-check scripts replayed successfully, including three- and four-dimensional examples. The finite tests are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The exact new object is a reversible **two-time-level** nearest-neighbor wave on a \(d\ge3\) torus with all circumferences powers of two. Martin–Odlyzko–Wolfram (1984) explicitly provide general algebraic approaches and discuss multidimensional one-time-level and higher-order cases, but their relevant multidimensional bound excludes side lengths divisible by the characteristic and their higher-order section does not give this complete formula. Macfarlane (2004) treats one-dimensional reversible second-order systems. Chang et al. (2017; preprint 2016) treat Kronecker-sum generalized-Jordan methods with predominantly null boundaries for **one-time-level** automata. A previously recorded two-dimensional parity-torus theorem and a one-dimensional wave classification are treated as covering those separate special cases; no novelty is asserted for either. Precise searches of published findings and relevant source formulations found no formula that implies the full stated \(d\ge3\) wave Jordan and orbit classification. Residual risk: an equivalent unindexed derivation via general module methods may exist; no claim of absolute first discovery is made.

## Value — PASS

This is a naturally specified infinite multidimensional discrete-wave family rather than an arbitrary finite slice. Its entire high-dimensional phase operator unexpectedly has identical unipotent blocks; the maximum side length alone determines the global period, while transverse volume controls all orbit multiplicities. The result gives complete fixed-point and primitive-cycle populations for every dimension and power-of-two size, serving as an exact benchmark for the transition from one-time-level nilpotent rules to reversible waves.
