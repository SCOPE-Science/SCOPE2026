# Scientific review: maximal edge-general-position sets of trees

## Correctness — PASS
The final claim is for every finite, simple, undirected tree with at least three vertices. The first bijection proves that any edge-general-position set of size at least two is precisely the leaf-edge set of the minimal connected subtree containing it, using both the path-through-an-internal-edge obstruction and the fact that one path contains at most two leaf edges. The second bijection proves that an addition is possible exactly when the hull has an internal vertex with an unused incident edge; it accounts for additions at arbitrary distance through the tree's unique attachment point. The leaf-degree identity proves the lower and upper cardinalities. Full-or-terminal rooted child choices yield an exact maximal-set recurrence. The arbitrary-root polynomial recurrence counts every connected subtree once; its explicit one-edge correction is necessary. The two-edge maximal coefficient, top coefficient, and path-or-star equivalence follow by degree-suppression and the leaf-degree identity. All one-edge and small-tree boundary cases are treated in the stated domain.

The standalone verifier independently enumerates all edge subsets and shortest paths for every labelled tree of orders three through seven and 450 further trees of orders eight through ten, comparing both polynomial distributions and all stated extremal multiplicities. Finite checks corroborate rather than prove the infinite claim.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty
The earliest public primary source is Manuel, Prabha, and Klavžar's 2021-05-10 manuscript (arXiv:2105.04292), which proves the **maximum** tree edge-general-position number equals its leaf count. The general one-sided-edge obstruction in its proof is explicitly credited. Their article does not state the maximal-set characterization, the smallest maximal cardinality, all-set or maximal-set recurrences, or the well-edge-general-position classification. Tian, Klavžar, and Tan's manuscript (arXiv:2302.01587) studies extremal maximum cardinalities and block graphs; its paper-wide statements and tree material do not yield the new conclusion. The 2022 generalized edge-geodesic-cover manuscript (arXiv:2207.07357) addresses different maximum and covering problems. The published published-finding corpus spider-tree theorem is **strong coverage of the one-branch-vertex special case**; its complete text was inspected and its contribution is excluded from novelty. The full arbitrary-tree saturated-hull bijection and lower formula do not follow from a spider-only reduction. An existing result concerning the general-position polynomial of *tree coronas* uses related connected-subtree enumeration but applies to vertices of a different graph and does not characterize inclusion-maximal edge sets. published-finding corpus and indexed literature were checked under aliases including lower edge general position, maximal edge-general-position sets, full tree enumerators, Steiner hull leaves, and subtree generating functions. No equivalent all-tree result was located. Search cannot certify priority against unindexed sources.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The research question asks for the full feasible and inclusion-maximal configurations of a natural distance-based edge property on **all trees**, rather than just its long-known maximum size. A second hull-saturation condition solves maximality, which is not captured by ordinary leaf-subtree enumeration. The result determines the minimum cardinality generated by a greedy maximal construction, classifies the trees for which all such constructions have equal size, gives exact counts at both extremities, and evaluates both full polynomials in polynomial time. The path-or-star boundary is genuinely graph-structural and the result covers arbitrarily many branching vertices, unlike the prior spider case. None of the main new claims is simply the old maximum theorem restated, and no arbitrary small parameter slice is chosen.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are the three Manuel–Prabha–Klavžar and Tian–Klavžar–Tan manuscripts on edge general position, plus the published spider special case. The tree maximum and entire spider subclass are already known; publication priority for arbitrary-tree distributions remains provisional. The claim does not cover general graphs or higher-order edge-position variants. The program is not a proof for unbounded tree orders, and independent mathematical validation has not occurred.
