# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim covers all two-hub trees with \(n\ge4\), all fixed survivor counts and independent survival probabilities. The three-case hub decomposition yields exact expectation and probability-generating formulas. An exchangeable-indicator finite-difference identity establishes convexity of the one-hub hypergeometric expectation, yielding the exact all-count convex order and strictness. Exact factorial moments yield the variance gap, and the full-participation deletion ceilings give \(2n-2\). The standalone enumerator checked all statements on its stated finite domain using a direct graph algorithm, rather than reusing the proof's combinatorial formulas for the graph outputs.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Angulo's full text (arXiv:2609.10565, sections 2–4 and Appendices A–D) establishes financing from unilateral withdrawal and compares single-withdrawal means; it neither classifies the two-hub all-count mean degeneracy nor orders the distributions by convex risk. Shang's branch-weight work records a related vertex-deletion statistic, not these percolation distributions. A study of site percolation on random recursive trees has a different random-graph object and does not imply the fixed two-hub assertions. Searches for equivalent full-output laws, diameter-three tree aliases and convex-majorization formulations found no decisive covering result, but an unindexed equivalent reliability theorem remains possible.

## Value — PASS

A financing institution can observe identical supportable bills **and** identical expected service after every possible count of random outages across networks of distinctly different hub centralization. Despite that complete mean indistinguishability, risk-sensitive convex objectives admit a sharp total ordering and non-vanishing, indeed quadratic, variance gaps. This is a motivated, complete structural counterexample to mean-only reliability ranking, not a bare variance computation or an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Scope and residual risk

The risk ordering is limited to two-hub trees and equal-probability vertex survival or uniformly sampled surviving subsets. It does not cover heterogeneous failure probabilities, correlated outages, arbitrary host graphs, or stochastic strategic equilibrium. Standard convex-order theory is used as a proof tool. Independent literature indexing is incomplete, so exact equivalence in an inaccessible source cannot be ruled out.
