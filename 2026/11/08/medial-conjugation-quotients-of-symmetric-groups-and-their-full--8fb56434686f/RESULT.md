# Medial conjugation quotients of symmetric groups and their full automorphism groups

## Finding

For every integer \(n\geq3\), let \(Q_n=\operatorname{Conj}(S_n)^{\mathrm{med}}\) be the universal medial quotient of the conjugation quandle \(x*y=y^{-1}xy\). Write \(p(n)\) for the number of partitions of \(n\), \(s(n)\) for the number of partitions of \(n\) with **pairwise distinct odd parts**, and \(e(n)\) and \(o(n)\) for the numbers of even and odd conjugacy types in \(S_n\), respectively. Explicitly, a partition \(\lambda\) of \(n\) is an even type if \(n-\ell(\lambda)\) is even; \(e(n)+o(n)=p(n)\).

**Theorem.** The elements of \(Q_n\) consist of one point for each ordinary nonsplit cycle type and two points for each distinct-odd cycle type. Therefore
\[
|Q_n|=p(n)+s(n).
\]
Let \(\tau\) interchange the two points belonging to each distinct-odd cycle type and fix all other points. Let \(\epsilon(v)=0\) or \(1\) according as the cycle type represented by \(v\) is even or odd. The entire operation is
\[
u*v=\tau^{\epsilon(v)}(u).
\]
The intrinsic symmetry group and **full** automorphism group are
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(Q_n)=\langle\tau\rangle\cong C_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(Q_n)\cong
(C_2\wr S_{s(n)})\times S_{e(n)-s(n)}\times S_{o(n)}.
\]
Here \(C_2\wr S_{s(n)}=C_2^{s(n)}\rtimes S_{s(n)}\) uses the natural permutation action on the \(s(n)\) pairs; \(S_0\) denotes the trivial group. In particular,
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_n)|=2^{s(n)}s(n)!\,(e(n)-s(n))!\,o(n)!.
\]
The outer automorphism group is this automorphism group modulo its central order-two subgroup generated by the simultaneous pair-swap \(\tau\).

## Assumptions and scope

The symmetric group \(S_n\) has its usual multiplication of permutations and \(A_n\) is its alternating subgroup. All results hold for every integer \(n\ge3\), with no field assumption. An isomorphism of quandles is a bijection respecting their two-variable operation. A universal medial quotient is a quotient by the smallest quandle congruence forcing the identity \((a*b)*(c*d)=(a*c)*(b*d)\). For a group \(G\), the derived subgroup \(G'=[G,G]\) acts by conjugation on the underlying set of \(G\). The elementary group-orbit reduction below is a proof tool; the symmetric-group classification and full automorphism group are the asserted new conclusions.

## Proof

**1. The group-orbit description.** For every group \(G\), the quotient \(\operatorname{Conj}(G)^{\mathrm{med}}\) is precisely the set of conjugation orbits of \(G'\) in \(G\). To prove necessity, observe that \(x*1=x\). In any medial image, substituting \(d=1\) gives \((a*b)*c=(a*c)*b\); hence the right translations of all images commute. In the original conjugation quandle the right translations are conjugations by group elements, and their commutators generate the conjugation action of \(G'\). All points in any \(G'\)-orbit must therefore have identical images. Conversely, the orbit relation is a quandle congruence: if \(y\) is replaced by a \(G'\)-conjugate then \(y\) changes by multiplication with an element of \(G'\), which induces the identity on orbit classes; changing \(x\) inside its orbit is also respected because \(G'\) is normal. The quotient right translations factor through the abelian group \(G/G'\) and consequently commute. For any quandle with commuting right translations, self-distributivity gives \(R_{c*d}=R_dR_cR_d^{-1}=R_c\), and similarly \(R_{b*d}=R_b\); hence the medial law follows. The orbit quotient is medial and is forced by every medial quotient, proving universality.

**2. Exactly which symmetric-group classes split.** For \(n\ge3\), \(S_n'=A_n\). The ordinary conjugacy class of a permutation \(g\) breaks into one or two \(A_n\)-conjugacy orbits. It breaks into two precisely when the centralizer \(C_{S_n}(g)\) has no odd element, since the double cosets of \(A_n\) and \(C_{S_n}(g)\) have index two in that case. An even-length cycle is itself an odd element of this centralizer. Two cycles of the same odd length can be exchanged by an odd element commuting with \(g\). Conversely, when all cycle lengths are odd and distinct, the centralizer is generated by powers of these odd cycles, all of which are even. Therefore the split classes are **exactly** the distinct-odd cycle types, giving \(p(n)+s(n)\) orbits. Every such split class consists of even permutations.

**3. Exact binary operation.** Conjugating by an even permutation preserves each \(A_n\)-orbit. Conjugating by an odd permutation exchanges the two \(A_n\)-orbits inside every split \(S_n\)-class, and fixes every nonsplit \(A_n\)-orbit. Thus the common induced action of the right operand is the identity for even parity and \(\tau\) for odd parity. This proves the displayed operation formula. There is at least one split class (the identity-type partition with distinct odd parts is not needed: a distinct-odd partition exists, e.g. one odd cycle when \(n\) is odd and the sum \((n-1)+1\) when \(n\) is even), so \(\tau\) is nontrivial. There also exist odd permutations (transpositions). Hence the inner group is exactly \(\langle\tau\rangle\cong C_2\).

**4. Full automorphism group.** An automorphism \(f\) of \(Q_n\) conjugates the characteristic inner automorphism group to itself, so it necessarily commutes with its unique nonidentity element \(\tau\). Moreover it preserves whether a right translation is the identity or \(\tau\); therefore \(\epsilon(f(v))=\epsilon(v)\). Conversely, any permutation \(f\) of \(Q_n\) that commutes with \(\tau\) and preserves \(\epsilon\) satisfies \(f(u*v)=\tau^{\epsilon(v)}f(u)=f(u)*f(v)\). Such permutations can freely permute the \(s(n)\) two-point \(\tau\)-orbits and independently swap each one, yielding \(C_2\wr S_{s(n)}\); freely permute the \(e(n)-s(n)\) even fixed points; and freely permute the \(o(n)\) odd fixed points. These three factors act independently, proving the automorphism group formula and its order. The simultaneous swap \(\tau\) is the diagonal central element of the first factor, and the quotient by it is the outer automorphism group.

## Relationship to prior work

Bardakov and Sangare (2026, Theorem 5.4) study the medialization of conjugation quandles, prove that \(\operatorname{Conj}(G)\) itself is medial precisely when \(G'\) is central, and derive bounds for the order of a medialization. Their Section 5 does not compute the precise quotient multiplication, partition counts, or full automorphism group for the symmetric groups. Bonatto and Stanovský (2019) develop a general correspondence involving orbit congruences and displacement subgroups; their framework supplies an alternate route to the derived-subgroup-orbit reduction in Step 1. That **general reduction alone is not asserted as original**. The new mathematical content is the uniform permutation-cycle model, complete binary operation, and explicit full wreath-product automorphism group for all \(n\ge3\). Higashitani and Kurihara (2020) study other symmetric-group-derived homogeneous quandles, in particular generalized Alexander structures, and do not give this medial quotient's automorphism group. Earlier formulas for conjugacy-class splitting in the alternating group are classical and are used as inputs, not claimed as discoveries.

## Limitations

The result does not classify all finite-group medializations or all homomorphisms out of them, and does not assert any novelty for the classical criterion for splitting symmetric-group conjugacy classes. The proof assumes \(n\ge3\); the small abelian cases \(n=1,2\) are excluded. An equivalent automorphism formula might exist in earlier work under the vocabulary of two-translation quandles, affine meshes, or abelian congruences; the searches described in `AUDIT.json` did not resolve absolute historical priority. The scientific review is not an independent audit.
