# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The directed zero-range stationary law has exact product weights by incoming/outgoing cancellation. Conditioning on slow-site mass gives a strictly log-concave weak-composition weight; an explicit quadratic gives its modal location. A uniform concavity bound and a local two-term curvature expansion yield Gaussian total fluctuations. Conditional weak-composition survival ratios yield independent exponentials and Poisson exceedances under the stated stronger growth condition, hence the Gumbel maximum. The standalone checker passed 130 finite balance, exact-weight, modal, and asymptotic-diagnostic tests; only the analytic argument establishes the infinite claims.

## Originality (PASS, bounded evidence)
The inspected Angel–Evans–Mukamel (2004) full text addresses one defect; Chleboun–Grosskinsky (2010) studies a different homogeneous condensation mechanism; Angel–Evans–Levine–Mukamel (2007) studies nonconserving mesocondensates. Two-defect and fixed-finite-defect stationary limits are not stronger statements implying a growing-defect Gaussian/exponential/Gumbel limit. However, the 2016 Rafferty thesis and 2014 Zeng article are particularly relevant and were not available for complete comparison. Thus priority is provisional; the evidence does not establish exhaustive uniqueness.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
Growing subextensive defect populations interpolate between a finite number of condensate receivers and a macroscopic density of defects. The three distinct natural fluctuation levels—Gaussian aggregate mass, exponential selected-site occupation, and Gumbel maximum—quantify this crossover. The exact finite canonical distribution and all-limit scaling hypotheses make the result reproducible and potentially useful for mesoscopic zero-range benchmarks, rather than an arbitrary finite computation.

## Source and verification limitations
Full-text priority comparison remains incomplete for Rafferty (2016) and Zeng (2014). The mathematical proof is based on an exact stationary formula and asymptotic expansions; finite tests cannot certify the limit. Dynamical mixing, supercritical densities and extensive defect fractions are not established.
