# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For binary vertices, distance differences are the dot product of a landmark sign row with the vertex difference, and every ternary displacement occurs. If the rows have rank at most three, complete them, if needed, to a span generated by three independent sign rows. A nonzero cofactor null vector of these three rows has entries in {0,±4}, since any 3-by-3 sign determinant is divisible by 4 and has absolute value at most 3√3. Dividing by 4 gives a nonzero ternary null vector for the original rows. Thus no smaller landmark set resolves, and for four landmarks nonsingularity is necessary and sufficient. A fresh exhaustive enumeration using pair separation (not the sign test) considered every subset of size 0–4, generated legal slides to unoccupied Hamming neighbors and computed breadth-first distances from every basis. It reproduced 928 vertices, 3584 edges, one component, degree counts 7:512, 8:384, 16:32, and eccentricity counts 8:64, 9:592, 10:272. The radius and diameter are 8 and 10. Coordinate permutations and translations act by slide-graph isometries, and canonicalization under these actions reproduced all eight representatives and their orbit sizes, degrees and eccentricities exactly, including the two degree-16 populations of size 16 with eccentricities 9 and 10.

PASS

The supported distinct claim is the exact ordinary-metric-basis, adjacency-restricted slide graph of Q4, in particular its distance invariants and orbit-resolved eccentricities. ## value

PASS

Requiring a single hypercube-edge move is a natural geometric restriction on matroid exchanges. It makes reachability and shortest legal sequences separate questions from static metric dimension. The exact radius and diameter give sharp best-center and worst-case move counts; the eight-orbit census explains why even maximum-degree bases need not have the same farthest distance. These are useful exact low-dimensional benchmarks, not only a count of bases or an arbitrary parameter substitution.
