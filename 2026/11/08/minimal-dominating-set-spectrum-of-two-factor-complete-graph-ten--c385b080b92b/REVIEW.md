# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The closed-neighborhood domination criterion is exactly the absence of an unselected row-column cross covering all selected cells. A matching of size three directly dominates; otherwise the bipartite matching-cover theorem reduces every case to two lines, which are classified with explicit deletion witnesses. The five families are disjoint and the counting factors follow from labeled rows and columns. Boundary orders \(m=2\) and \(n=2\) are explicitly handled; eleven exhaustive graph instances independently matched complete families and coefficients, with five additional constructed-case tests. Finite checks are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Defant–Iyer (arXiv:1708.01305, §§2 and 4) give the two-factor minimum and upper domination parameters and bound minimal-set sizes, but do not provide the five-family cardinality polynomial. Anderson–Kuenzel–Rall (arXiv:1909.09955, Theorem 3) determine well-dominated product cases, not a cardinality census. Burcroff–O'Brien (arXiv:2110.00709, §5) establish unimodality for polynomials counting **all** dominating sets; their results do not imply the cardinality distribution of the inclusion-minimal generators. A closely related classification of *total* dominating sets concerns a distinct open-neighborhood property, not ordinary domination. The full theorem and exact count were not located in available research records or targeted OEIS searches. Alternative naming and unindexed sources remain genuine priority risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Minimal dominating sets are the irredundant generators of ordinary domination and are studied via their upper and well-dominated parameters. The exact polynomial provides the entire labeled minimal-solution distribution for every pair of factor sizes, sharper than either extremal parameter. The gap \(5,\ldots,n-1\) for \(K_3\times K_n\) is a structural obstruction of arbitrarily large length and is not determined by the known minimum or upper domination number alone.
