# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The invariant second-moment identity fixes the coefficient and normalization of the orthogonal projection kernel. The exact uniform norm equals its absolute kernel integral because the orthogonal group acts transitively. Averaging arbitrary projections and determining the one-dimensional stabilizer-fixed matrix space proves minimality among **all** continuous-function projections, not merely among orthogonal ones. For fixed rank, Jiang's total-variation Gaussian-block approximation and the exact variance justify convergence of absolute first moments. The gamma-density calculation supplies the constants. A positive digamma-integral identity proves strict rank monotonicity; Stirling gives its limit. In the sparse growing-rank range the same published Gaussian-block theorem and a centered chi-squared CLT give the universal constant, with exact variance proving uniform integrability. Boundary rank one and dimension two satisfy the formulas. The checker independently tested the beta identity and four random Haar cases; it is diagnostic rather than a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Jiang (2006) proves the orthogonal-block total-variation approximation, not projection-constant identities. Defant, Galicer, Mansilla, Mastyło and Muro (2024 preprint) study spherical-polynomial projection constants, including the related rank-one setting; the present contribution excludes the rank-one identity from its novelty assertion. Neither inspected source covers the full family of nontrivial real Grassmannians with arbitrary rank, the strictly increasing all-fixed-rank constants, and the growing sparse-rank limit. Related harmonic-analysis literature on Grassmannians supplies a conceptual setting but is not a verified duplicate of the sharp rank-profile claim. Abstract searches do not prove novelty; unindexed stronger theory, a hidden corollary, or uninspected appendices remain genuine risks. All claims of noncoverage are limited to the material inspected.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result gives a minimum-norm projection constant in a canonical finite-dimensional invariant approximation space on an entire series of compact homogeneous manifolds. The strict hierarchy and its Gaussian saturation distinguish finite-rank geometry from sparse growing-rank geometry, rather than merely rephrasing a preexisting block limit; the exact kernel links approximation stability, matrix-block statistics, and representation-theoretic minimality. The unstudied critical rank window remains an explicitly motivated future problem.

## Closest literature and scientific limits

Jiang, arXiv:math/0601457 (2006), provides the crucial block normal approximation. Defant et al., arXiv:2405.12123 (2024), provide the closest projection-constant framework on real spheres. The first rank is a known spherical setting, and the present proof claims only the higher-rank extension and sharp all-rank sparse profile. Critical ranks comparable to the square root of dimension, dense ranks, and more general harmonic degrees remain outside the proof. No independent review or publication priority is asserted.
