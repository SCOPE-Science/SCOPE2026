# Minimal quadratic phase retrieval from two rotated square orbits

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal P_2=\{p(z)=a_0+a_1z+a_2z^2:a_j\in\mathbb C\}\). For radii \(r_1,r_2\geq0\) and relative angle \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\), define the ordered eight-point observation family
\[
\mathcal Z(r_1,r_2,\alpha)=\bigl(r_1 i^k\bigr)_{k=0}^3\ \sqcup\ \bigl(r_2e^{i\alpha}i^k\bigr)_{k=0}^3,
\qquad \mathcal I(p)=\bigl(|p(z)|^2\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal Z}.
\]
Repeated points are retained as repeated measurements. **The exact global phase-retrieval criterion** is
\[
\mathcal I(p)=\mathcal I(q)\Longrightarrow q=\omega p\text{ for some }|\omega|=1
\quad\text{for every }p,q\in\mathcal P_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r_1r_2>0,\quad r_1\ne r_2,\quad\sin(2\alpha)\ne0.
\]
Thus two concentric square orbits with distinct positive radii recover every complex quadratic polynomial up to global phase precisely when their relative rotation is not an integer multiple of \(\pi/2\). The eight magnitude measurements are **globally cardinality-minimal** among *all* complex phase-retrieval frames in dimension three.

There is also an exact lifted spectral law. On the nine-real-dimensional Hilbert space \(\operatorname{Herm}_3\) of Hermitian matrices with Frobenius norm, put \(v(z)=(1,z,z^2)^\top\) and
\[
\mathcal L(X)=\bigl(v(z)^*Xv(z)\bigr)_{z\in\mathcal Z}\in\mathbb R^8,
\qquad u=r_1^2,\quad v=r_2^2.
\]
For every nondegenerate configuration in the criterion, \(\mathcal L\) has rank eight and precisely the one-dimensional kernel
\[
\ker\mathcal L=\mathbb R K,\qquad
K=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(uv,-(u+v),1\bigr).
\]
The kernel generator is invertible and indefinite; it cannot be a difference of two rank-one positive matrices. In orthonormal real coordinates, the eight nonzero singular values of \(\mathcal L\) are exactly the two singular values of \(D\), the two of \(C\) each repeated twice, and the two of \(O\), where
\[
D=2\begin{pmatrix}1&u&u^2\\1&v&v^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C=2\begin{pmatrix}r_1&r_1^3\\r_2&r_2^3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
O=2\sqrt2\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\v\cos(2\alpha)&-v\sin(2\alpha)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently the squared-product singular determinant is
\[
\boxed{\det(\mathcal L\mathcal L^*)=
2^{18}(uv)^4(u-v)^6\bigl[(uv)^2+(u+v)^2+1\bigr]\sin^2(2\alpha).}
\]
For fixed distinct positive radii this determinant is maximized **exactly** when \(\alpha\equiv\pi/4\pmod{\pi/2}\). This is an exact optimality statement for the squared-product singular determinant, **not** a claim that the full spectral condition number has the same unique optimum.

A quantitative global lifting-stability consequence is available. Let \(s_+(r_1,r_2,\alpha)\) be the smallest of the eight positive singular values specified by \(D,C,O\). Then, for every \(x,y\in\mathbb C^3\),
\[
\|\mathcal L(xx^*-yy^*)\|_2\ \geq\
\frac{s_+(r_1,r_2,\alpha)\min\{uv,1\}}
{\sqrt{(uv)^2+(u+v)^2+1}}\ \|xx^*-yy^*\|_{\mathrm F}.
\]
This is an explicit positive uniform estimate on rank-two lifted differences, not asserted to be the sharp inverse stability constant.

## Assumptions and scope

All radii are finite, nonnegative real numbers. A common rotation of all sample points does not change the conclusions, so the first square has angle zero. The measurements are raw, equally weighted squared moduli, one per point; the singular spectrum depends on this normalization. Global phase means multiplication of all three polynomial coefficients by the same unit complex scalar. There are no restrictions on zero coefficients, root locations, or normalization of \(p\). The conclusions concern complex, not merely real, coefficients.

## Proof

**1. Fourier decomposition of four samples per circle.** For an arbitrary \(X=(X_{jk})_{j,k=0}^2\in\operatorname{Herm}_3\), write \(z_{\ell k}=r_\ell e^{i\theta_\ell}i^k\), with \(\theta_1=0\), \(\theta_2=\alpha\), and \(u_\ell=r_\ell^2\). Direct expansion gives
\[
v(z_{\ell k})^*Xv(z_{\ell k})=
X_{00}+u_\ell X_{11}+u_\ell^2X_{22}
+2\operatorname{Re}\!\left[z_{\ell k}(X_{01}+u_\ell X_{12})
+z_{\ell k}^2X_{02}\right].
\]
The four-point discrete Fourier transform isolates a constant, a complex first harmonic, and one real Nyquist harmonic. All four samples vanish precisely when
\[
X_{00}+u_\ell X_{11}+u_\ell^2X_{22}=0,\quad
X_{01}+u_\ell X_{12}=0,\quad
\operatorname{Re}(e^{2i\theta_\ell}X_{02})=0
\]
for each \(\ell=1,2\) with positive \(r_\ell\). (The positive radial factors are removable.) When \(u\ne v\), the two first-harmonic equations force \(X_{01}=X_{12}=0\); when also \(\sin(2\alpha)\ne0\), the two Nyquist equations force \(X_{02}=0\). The diagonal equations then have a one-dimensional kernel generated by \(K\) above. Since \(uv>0\), \(\det K=-uv(u+v)\ne0\).

**2. Phase-retrieval criterion, sufficiency.** For \(p(z)=\sum a_jz^j\), put \(x=(\overline{a_0},\overline{a_1},\overline{a_2})^\top\). Then \(|p(z)|^2=v(z)^*xx^*v(z)\). Equal intensities for two polynomials yield \(\mathcal L(xx^*-yy^*)=0\). But the Hermitian difference has rank at most two, whereas every nonzero matrix in \(\mathbb RK\) has rank three. Hence \(xx^*=yy^*\), which is equivalent to equality of the polynomials up to unimodular scalar (including the zero polynomial).

**3. Necessity and explicit ambiguities.** If \(r_1=r_2=r\), the distinct polynomials \(p(z)=r\) and \(q(z)=z\) have equal moduli at every sample. If one radius is zero and the other is \(r>0\), the polynomials \(p(z)=rz\) and \(q(z)=z^2\) agree in modulus at the origin and every point of modulus \(r\). If both radii vanish, nonuniqueness is immediate. If both radii are positive and \(\sin(2\alpha)=0\), the distinct polynomials \(p(z)=1+iz^2\), \(q(z)=1-iz^2\) have equal sampled intensities because the square of each sample point is real. None of these pairs differs by a global unit phase.

**4. Complete lifted singular spectrum.** Apply the orthonormal four-point real Fourier basis separately on each circle. On the input, use diagonal matrix units followed by the real and imaginary parts of the three off-diagonal entries, each scaled by \(\sqrt2\), so the coordinates are Frobenius-orthonormal. The constant-frequency block is \(D\). The first-frequency block is two identical copies of \(C\), up to orthogonal row rotations that remove \(\theta_\ell\). The Nyquist-frequency block is \(O\). This gives the asserted multiset of singular values without omitted or hidden sectors. The Gram determinant of the rectangular block \(D\) is
\[
\det(DD^\top)=16(u-v)^2\bigl[(uv)^2+(u+v)^2+1\bigr].
\]
Also \(\det C=4r_1r_2(v-u)\) and \(\det O=-8uv\sin(2\alpha)\). Multiplying the two-row Gram determinants and the contributions from the two copies of \(C\) yields exactly the boxed formula. For fixed radii every factor except \(\sin^2(2\alpha)\) is strictly positive and constant, so the angular determinant optimum and all equality angles follow.

**5. Minimality of eight arbitrary complex intensity samples.** Suppose any system of at most seven squared-modulus linear measurements recovered every vector in \(\mathbb C^3\). Its Hermitian lifting has a real kernel of dimension at least two because \(\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{Herm}_3=9\). Choose independent kernel matrices \(H_1,H_2\). The continuous function \(t\mapsto\det(\cos t\,H_1+\sin t\,H_2)\) changes sign under \(t\mapsto t+\pi\), since the determinant is cubic. Thus the kernel contains a nonzero singular Hermitian matrix \(H\). If \(H\) is indefinite, it has one positive and one negative eigenvalue, so it is a difference of two rank-one positive matrices and yields two different signals with equal measurements. If \(H\) is semidefinite, every measurement vector is annihilated by \(H\); consequently the measurement vectors do not span \(\mathbb C^3\), and a nonzero signal is indistinguishable from zero. Both contradict universal phase retrieval. Therefore eight is the absolute minimum, attained by the nondegenerate two-square family.

**6. Quantitative stability.** Let \(\|K\|_{\mathrm F}=\sqrt{(uv)^2+(u+v)^2+1}\). The smallest singular value of the invertible diagonal \(K\) is \(\min\{uv,1\}\), since \(u+v\) cannot be smaller than both \(uv\) and \(1\). By the best rank-two approximation theorem, any Hermitian rank-at-most-two \(H\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm F}(H,\mathbb RK)
\geq \frac{\min\{uv,1\}}{\|K\|_{\mathrm F}}\|H\|_{\mathrm F}.
\]
On the orthogonal complement of its kernel, \(\mathcal L\) is bounded below by \(s_+\), proving the stated stability inequality for \(H=xx^*-yy^*\).

The accompanying standalone Python checker constructs all eight raw measurement rows independently in a Frobenius-orthonormal Hermitian basis and compares their direct singular values with the three analytic block spectra. It also compares the direct squared-product Gram determinant with its closed form, checks the kernel, explicit ambiguity pairs, degeneracy ranks, and angular determinant optimality on finite test sets. ## Relationship to prior work

Chalendar and Partington, *Phase retrieval on circles and lines* (first publicly posted 2024-03-24, arXiv:2403.16255), establish rigidity from the **entire** modulus trace on two disjoint circles, including concentric circles (Theorem 2.16); this does not imply recovery from four specified points per circle or identify the sharp relative-angle exclusions. Theorem 2.11 concerns different inner-function data assumptions.

Bodmann and Hammen, *Stable phase retrieval with low-redundancy frames* (arXiv:1302.5487), construct \(4d-4\) polynomial intensity measurements using two **intersecting** circular configurations. Philipp, *Phase Retrieval from 4N-4 Measurements* (arXiv:1403.4769), uses polynomial and derivative magnitudes on the unit circle. Conca, Edidin, Hering, and Vinzant, *An algebraic characterization of injectivity in phase retrieval* (arXiv:1312.0158), prove generic \(4d-4\) sufficiency; their generic result does not decide the exact exceptional set in the constrained two-concentric-square family. The known Hermitian rank-two criterion is a prerequisite, not claimed as a new contribution. The new claim is the complete **if-and-only-if two-square geometry**, its full eight-mode lifted singular spectrum, and its exact angular determinant law.

## Limitations

The theorem covers only the three-dimensional complex polynomial space and the two equally weighted four-point square orbits. It does not classify arbitrary eight-point configurations or prove sharp minimax noise robustness over radii. The determinant angular optimum concerns the product of nonzero squared singular values; the supplied global difference bound is positive but not asserted optimal. Source comparisons leave a residual possibility of equivalent constructions in earlier finite-frame or phase-shifting literature. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
