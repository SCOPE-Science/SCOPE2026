# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The cross criterion is an iff by the tensor adjacency definition. Koenig's theorem partitions minimal sets into matching-number at least three (where a three-edge matching is already total dominating) and matching-number at most two (where a two-vertex bipartite cover must use two vertices from one side). The proof establishes exactly two selected cells on each of two lines, proves their inclusion-minimality, counts intersection rectangles once and treats the smallest and disconnected boundaries. The actual standalone checker exhaustively compares generated families with the original graph definition over nine instances. Its finite result supports rather than replaces the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with publication-priority risk

The closest prior result is Kazemi's Proposition 7 on \(K_n\times K_2\), proving the upper \(k\)-tuple total-domination number for that one-factor-of-order-two case and posing Question 1 about arbitrary factors. The new theorem establishes the \(k=1\) case for all factor orders and fully classifies/counts every inclusion-minimal set, not just an extremum. Burcroff–O'Brien study ordinary domination polynomial unimodality for the same graphs; the open-neighborhood, inclusion-minimal total-domination statement is not an implication of their results. Bahadır–Ekim–Gözüpek study well-totally-dominated graphs generically, without this product enumeration. A prior exact equivalent hidden under another nomenclature or in an unindexed table remains possible; no claim of absolute novelty is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem gives a complete natural two-parameter classification, an exact two-coefficient enumerator, and the full \(\gamma_t\)/\(\Gamma_t\) phase transition. It resolves the \(k=1\) specialization of a published direct-product question beyond the previously known factor-two boundary. It also classifies which products are well-totally-dominated, a structural rather than merely computational corollary.

## Limits

No result is claimed for \(k>1\), more than two factors, or arbitrary factor graphs. Exhaustive checks are finite. Priority remains provisional pending broader scholarly review.
