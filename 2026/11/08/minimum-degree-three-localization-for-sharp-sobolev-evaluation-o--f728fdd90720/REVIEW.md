# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof explicitly identifies the centered Kirchhoff Green representer and its evaluation norm through effective resistance, establishes both the single-edge integral identity and the double integral, and computes the multipartite vertex resistances from the graph Laplacian decomposition. Its strict endpoint-class comparison is pointwise on entire edge profiles, while the degree-threshold sign proof separately treats bipartite graphs, multipartite graphs with a single vertex per part, and the remaining graphs. The low-degree degeneracies are handled separately, including the entire circles \(K_{1,1,1}\) and \(K_{2,2}\). The exact-rational program independently calculates grounded-Laplacian resistances and subdivided-network arclength integrals in finite instances. There is no extrapolation from these finite checks to the infinite family: the analytic identities supply the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Solomyak (arXiv:math/0206235, 2002) treats general Sobolev approximation estimates, not exact mean-zero point-evaluation maxima on complete multipartite graphs. Baker--Faber (arXiv:math/0407428, 2004, relevant resistance and Foster sections) establishes general electrical-network techniques, not the size-ordering and degree-three localization theorem. The fully inspected Das--Yang article (doi:10.1186/s13660-017-1570-1, 2017, especially Theorem 2.3 and Sections 3--5) determines discrete vertex resistance values and resistance-matrix spectra, not continuum embedding norms. Gervacio's 2012 article is expressly credited for prior complete-multipartite vertex resistances; its linked full text was not accessible through the current journal endpoint, creating a bibliographic uncertainty rather than evidence of complete coverage. The known sharp two-part subfamily with both class sizes at least three is treated as already covered; the finding concerns arbitrary multipartite size profiles, strict dominance of the largest two classes, the exact minimum-degree-three dichotomy, sharp constants, and equality functions. Published-record and semantic mathematical-search comparisons found no explicit equivalent or stronger claim, but unindexed or implicit coverage is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural invariant is the exact norm of point evaluation on the mean-zero metric-graph energy Hilbert space, whose supremum is the sharp Sobolev embedding constant. Unlike an arbitrary fixed graph or finite table recomputation, the theorem classifies *all* complete multipartite edge geometries for an arbitrary number of parts, and the surprising threshold depends only on the minimum combinatorial degree rather than on all part sizes. It also gives a constructive maximizing-point formula, a strict edge-class screening principle, a quadratic location deficit, and every equality function. These precise data are useful in graph Green-kernel localization and sharp edgewise approximation estimates.

## Closest literature and limits

The constituent multipartite vertex resistance formula is known, and general Green/resistance identities are classical. The novelty resides in optimizing the integrated continuum resistance over all edges and proving the sharp universal geometry of the maximizers. The theorem is restricted to unit edges, uniform arclength, real first-order Sobolev energy, and complete multipartite graphs. The full-text access limit for the 2012 Gervacio article and the possibility of an unindexed equivalent theorem require independent bibliographic scrutiny. Exact finite arithmetic is corroboration, not a substitute for the proof.
