# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The row-capacity lower bound accounts for every row because its diagonal vertex has no external neighbour. Sharpness uses an explicit symmetric circulant 0–1 matrix of row sum \(k\). Equality forces every row to have exactly \(k\) omissions, and the saturation condition forces every cross-row matching edge to have either both endpoints omitted or neither; this is precisely matrix symmetry. The converse, coefficient extraction, complement symmetry, involutions and looped path/cycle component formula follow with all parameters \(m\ge2\), \(1\le k<m\). Direct adjacency-based tests checked all minimum-row-profile candidates for \(2\le m\le5\) and all subsets for the lower bound with \(m=2,3\); unrestricted correctness rests on the proof, not on enumeration.

**Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority.** Božović's 2026 paper *On \(k\)-limited domination: complexity and Sierpiński graphs* (arXiv:2610.01584v1) establishes scalar results and provides a construction; it does not classify/count every optimal set. The earlier paper *On \(k\)-limited domination in graphs* (arXiv:2606.22422) establishes definitions/general bounds. Bounded searches of published mathematical records and the literature found no stronger all-minimum-set theorem for \(S(2,m)\). The involution numbers are standard and already present in OEIS A000085. A third-party indexed scalar-conjecture resolution may exist; this does not by itself cover the structural enumeration, but its full content was not inspected. This is not a claim of exhaustive publication priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The natural new question is how many optimal capacity-limited defender placements exist, not only their size. At the first recursive Sierpiński level, all optimal configurations unexpectedly encode regular undirected graphs with loops counting once; this gives an exact all-capacity enumerator, a duality of capacities, involution counts at \(k=1\), and a labelled path/cycle formula at \(k=2\). These are all-optimum structural and counting results for an infinite two-parameter family rather than arbitrary numerical samples.

**Limits and closest literature.** The theorem is confined to \(S(2,m)\), not higher recursive levels, and its all-capacity formula uses coefficient extraction rather than a uniform elementary closed form. The prior scalar results and pre-existing involution sequence are not claimed as new; independent mathematical validation remains outstanding.
