# Minimum zero forcing sets of cycle powers: a complete census

## Finding
For integers \(k\ge2\), \(n\ge2k+1\), let \(G=C_n^k\) be the \(k\)-th power of the labelled cycle with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Its zero forcing number is the previously known value \(Z(G)=2k\). Write \(z(G;2k)\) for the number of distinct labelled minimum zero forcing sets, equivalently the coefficient of degree \(2k\) in the zero forcing polynomial. Then
\[
z(C_n^k;2k)=\begin{cases}
n,&n=2k+1,\\
kn,&n=2k+2,\\
(2k-1)n,&n\ge2k+3.
\end{cases}
\]
Every minimum set has the precise form \(B=I_v\setminus\{w\}\), where \(I_v=\{v-k,v-k+1,\ldots,v+k\}\) (cyclic residues), \(v\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), and \(w\in I_v\setminus\{v\}\). For \(n\ge2k+3\), exactly \(n\) such sets are a run of \(2k\) consecutive vertices and have two representations \((v,w)\). Every other set has exactly one representation. These latter sets number \(2n(k-1)\). Under the dihedral action, the minimum sets form exactly \(k\) orbits: one orbit of size \(n\) (consecutive runs), and \(k-1\) orbits each of size \(2n\), indexed by the cyclic distance \(j=1,\ldots,k-1\) from \(v\) to the deleted interior vertex. The first two boundary counts were already implied by general zero forcing polynomial results; the all-parameter count and classification beyond \(n\ge2k+3\) are the new statement.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, undirected and labelled. The power \(C_n^k\) joins distinct residues at cyclic distance at most \(k\). Every vertex has exactly \(2k\) neighbors when \(n\ge2k+1\). The ordinary zero forcing rule allows a blue vertex with exactly one white neighbor to color that neighbor blue. A set is zero forcing if a finite sequence of such steps colors every vertex; its minimum size is \(Z(G)\). The coefficient \(z(G;r)\) counts labelled zero forcing sets of cardinality \(r\). The cases \(k=1\), other graphs, skew forcing, and the other coefficients of the polynomial are not claimed as new results.

## Proof
**1. Minimum cardinality and a necessary first move.** In a \(2k\)-regular graph, a blue set smaller than \(2k\) cannot make any first force: the forcing vertex would need at least \(2k-1\) other blue neighbors, hence at least \(2k\) initially blue vertices including itself. Conversely, any \(2k\) consecutive vertices are zero forcing: if the current blue vertices include a cyclic interval of at least \(2k\) consecutive residues, the vertex lying \(k-1\) positions to the left of its right end has precisely the next residue to the right as its only white neighbor (until the cycle is fully colored). Repeating extends the interval one step at a time. This recovers \(Z(G)=2k\), already proved in the cited literature.

For an arbitrary zero forcing set \(B\) with \(|B|=2k\), take a vertex \(v\in B\) making its first force to a neighbor \(w\notin B\). All other \(2k-1\) neighbors of \(v\) must lie in \(B\); as there are exactly \(2k\) initially blue vertices, \(B=\{v\}\cup(N(v)\setminus\{w\})=I_v\setminus\{w\}\). Conversely, for every such \((v,w)\), \(v\) forces \(w\); then the entire interval \(I_v\) is blue. The interval-extension argument proves that this set forces all vertices. Thus the representation is necessary and sufficient.

**2. Counting representations for \(n\ge2k+3\).** There are \(2kn\) ordered pairs \((v,w)\), with \(w\) a neighbor of \(v\). Put \(t=n-(2k+1)\ge2\). The complement \(J_v=V(G)\setminus I_v\) is a cyclic consecutive interval of length \(t\). If the same set \(B\) also arises from \(I_u\setminus\{w'\}\), then both \(J_v\) and \(J_u\) are subsets of the white set \(V\setminus B\), which has size \(t+1\). Hence \(|J_u\cap J_v|\ge t-1\). For two distinct length-\(t\) cyclic intervals with \(t\ge2\) and complementary length \(2k+1\ge5\), overlap at least \(t-1\) forces a one-step shift, \(u=v+1\) or \(u=v-1\) modulo \(n\). (To verify this elementary interval fact, each interval must contain all but at most one vertex of the other, so their two endpoints can differ only by one.) In that case \(I_v\cap I_u\) is exactly a run of \(2k\) consecutive residues, and their common \(B\) must be this entire intersection. Therefore only consecutive runs have multiple representations. Every consecutive run has exactly two: its possible containing intervals are formed by adjoining its predecessor or its successor, and the removed vertex is that adjoining endpoint. There are exactly \(n\) consecutive runs, all distinct because \(2k<n\). Hence the number of distinct sets is \(2kn-n=(2k-1)n\), with the stated multiplicities of representation.

The white complement of a nonconsecutive set consists of an isolated deleted interior vertex and one run of \(t\ge2\) white vertices. Its unique isolated white vertex has distance \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}\) from the center of the containing \(I_v\). The two blue runs have lengths \(k+j\) and \(k-j\). Cyclic translation and reversal act transitively on each fixed \(j\), and these unequal run-length multisets distinguish distinct \(j\). For a fixed \(j\), either sign of the deleted position and any of \(n\) centers are allowed, giving an orbit of size \(2n\). The consecutive runs are one orbit of size \(n\).

**3. Two short-cycle boundaries.** If \(n=2k+1\), the graph is complete. Any initial set of size \(2k\) misses just one vertex and forces it, so there are exactly \(n\) sets. If \(n=2k+2\), the graph is the complete graph minus the matching of antipodal pairs. A \(2k=n-2\) subset is zero forcing exactly when the two omitted vertices are not antipodal: antipodal whites are seen together by every remaining blue vertex and cannot be forced, while, for any nonantipodal whites \(a,b\), the blue vertex antipodal to \(a\) sees \(b\) but not \(a\), forces \(b\), and the final white vertex is then forced. There are \(\binom n2-n/2=kn\) nonantipodal white pairs. This count is an instance of the general coefficient formula of Boyer and collaborators and is not claimed as new.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` constructs the cyclic graph, applies the ordinary white-neighbor forcing rule, enumerates all candidate \(2k\)-element subsets for each tested graph, and independently constructs the first-move representation family. Exhaustive comparisons pass for all \(36\) parameter pairs \(3\le n\le13\), \(1\le k\le\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\). Additional exact tests confirm the counting and ability to force for all represented sets through \(n=40\); independent dihedral permutation enumeration verifies the \(k\)-orbit breakdown in 100 parameter pairs through \(n=25\). These finite experiments do not prove the unrestricted theorem; the preceding combinatorial argument does.

## Relationship to prior work
Abiad, Flagg, Ghasemi Nezhad and Zeijlemaker, arXiv:2610.03892 (first submitted 2026-10-02), Proposition 8, determine \(Z(C_n^k)=2k\) and exhibit a consecutive minimum set, without counting all minimum sets. Deaett and Meyer, arXiv:1511.07920, Theorem 5.4, already obtained the scalar zero forcing number for consecutive circulants; that earlier priority is acknowledged. Boyer and collaborators, arXiv:1801.08910, define the zero forcing polynomial, provide the ordinary-cycle formula (Proposition 6), the complete-graph polynomial (Proposition 3), and a general size-\(n-2\) coefficient test (Theorem 1). Thus the boundary cases and the order-two cycle case are not new. The contribution here is the classification, exact coefficient and dihedral orbit structure for all \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge2k+3\). Searches of those primary sources, related graph-forcing work, and mathematical result records did not identify stronger prior coverage. This is a best-knowledge priority assessment, not an independent uniqueness certification.

## Limitations
Only minimum zero forcing sets are counted, not all coefficients of the zero forcing polynomial or arbitrary graph powers. The vertices are labelled, and the dihedral orbit classification is for the standard cyclic labelling. The statement is an ordinary zero forcing result, not skew, PSD, or power domination. For \(n=2k+1\) and \(n=2k+2\), the counts are known consequences of general earlier results. No independent mathematical audit has yet been performed, and unknown literature may affect the priority claim.
