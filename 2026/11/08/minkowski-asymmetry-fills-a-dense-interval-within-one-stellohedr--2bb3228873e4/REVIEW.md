# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact source facet representation and vertex extremizers are verified in Bayer et al., Theorem 2.3. For a proposed reflection factor, its necessary-and-sufficient lower-coordinate and upper-subset constraints give a sharp universal lower bound from the sum of the lower-coordinate inequalities and the full-set upper inequality. At the claimed center the remaining constraints are equivalent to the elementary fact that the largest \(k\) members of a strictly increasing sequence have average no smaller than the overall average. Equality of the \(n+1\) aggregate bounds forces uniqueness. The dense interval follows from a parameter limit proved using exact sums, and the Hausdorff limits use the complete vertex description. The separate checker passes 360 enumerated parameter LPs, seven classical dimensions, and 30 endpoint-family tests; these corroborate but do not establish the all-dimensional proof.

**Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional).** Hanada–Lentfer–Vindas-Meléndez give volume/face information in the classical/two-parameter family. Bayer and coauthors supply the full parameter-dependent facet/vertex theorem and **already prove the stellohedral face lattice**; those elements are explicitly cited as prior. Their materially inspected full text does not assert the Minkowski-asymmetry coefficient, its unique center, or a dense interval of affine values. Beck–Schymura's zonotope setting uses the asymmetry coefficient but does not cover these parking-function polytopes. Public research summaries include an exact coefficient for *one other simplex-sum realization of a stellohedron*; that is a different geometric realization, and face-lattice equivalence cannot imply the present formula or dense-range theorem. Searches under parking-function, coefficient-of-asymmetry, star-graph associahedron, polymatroid, stellohedron and dense affine-spectrum descriptions found no statement implying the complete final claim. Residual possibility: an equivalent formula could be implicit in general polymatroid center theory or an uninspected text.

**Value — PASS.** Minkowski asymmetry is a canonical affine-invariant measure of distance from central symmetry, with extremal cube and simplex values. The result gives its exact coefficient and uniquely determining center for every member of a natural arbitrary-parameter family, while showing that a *single fixed stellohedral combinatorial type* has a dense asymmetry spectrum reaching arbitrarily close to both universal extremes, despite its unchanging face lattice. This is a structural non-rigidity theorem, not a routine change of parameters or a numerical catalog.
