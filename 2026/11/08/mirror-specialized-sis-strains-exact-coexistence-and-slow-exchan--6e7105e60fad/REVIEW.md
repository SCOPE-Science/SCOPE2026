Adding the three epidemic classes on each patch reduces patch-mass imbalance to a scalar eigenvalue \(-2d\). At any strictly positive equilibrium, the infected nullvectors are Perron vectors of two irreducible Metzler matrices. Subtracting their determinant equations proves the indispensable identity \(S_1=S_2\), and the strictly increasing Perron eigenvalue in \(s\) yields the sharp existence criterion and the *smaller* quadratic root. The infected nullvectors and per-patch mass then enforce equal amplitude, proving positive-equilibrium uniqueness. Reflection decomposes the transverse four-dimensional Jacobian into two two-by-two matrices with strictly negative traces and strictly positive determinants; with \(-2d\), these establish local exponential stability on the five-dimensional mass-conserving manifold. The exact finite-migration cutoff follows by carefully squaring a positive inequality, including exclusion of equality. The large-\(d\) determinant and trace expansion identifies the \(d^{-1}\) composition eigenvalue with the stated coefficient and rules out other slower modes. No global or stochastic assertion is present.

## Originality (O) — PASS on a best-knowledge basis

The initiating Doumatè–Issa–Salako article [1] derives reproduction/invasion criteria and coexistence existence for certain regimes, with numerical large-mobility coexistence, but explicitly leaves positive-coexistence uniqueness/global stability and spatial dependence open (Section 2.3.6). It does not provide the mirrored two-patch forced-susceptible identity, exact uniqueness theorem, all-mobility cutoff, or relaxation coefficient. Tuncer–Martcheva [2] and Wu–Tuncer–Martcheva [3] concern related reaction–diffusion models and general invasion/permanence conclusions rather than this finite-dimensional closed form; [3] uses standard incidence. Chen–Shi–Shuai–Wu [4] studies a different constant-coefficient competition patch framework and does not directly imply the parameter-specific SIS identities. Adetola–Castellano–Salako [5] covers equal dispersal in an infinite-dimensional diffusion model under a spatially *constant transmission-rate ratio*, whereas the present rates have the two different ratios \(b/a\) and \(a/b\) (because \(a<b\)). Later work [6] treats variable total population on a continuous spatial domain, unlike the conserved-total-host finite-patch system. The earlier Lou–Salako reaction–diffusion study [7] is thematically near but is not verified to cover these equations. The assessed novelty is the conjunction of the exact mirrored positive-equilibrium classification and the strong-migration composition spectral gap, not a claim that spatially maintained coexistence itself is new.

Published abstracts and available mathematical source text were examined at the stated level; full text of [5–7] was unavailable. These access gaps and unindexed equivalent work remain material publication-priority risks. Similarity or absence of a database match is not proof of originality. No independent audit was performed.

## Value (V) — PASS

In a recognized model of opposing viral strains competing for susceptibles, symmetry of patch specialization is a natural testbed for when mixing destroys spatial niche coexistence. The exact boundary distinguishes finite migration that preserves both strains from finite migration that eliminates strictly positive coexistence, including the critical threshold. Even with enough average infectivity that coexistence survives every finite mixing rate, the local strain-composition relaxation gap closes as \(d^{-1}\), a dynamical effect that cannot be inferred from a reproduction-number threshold or visual steady-state segregation alone. The explicit coefficient connects the two transmission advantages, average fitness and mixing, furnishing quantitative timescale information relevant to numerical simulation and mechanistic interpretation. The result is not claimed to generalize to asymmetric patches or to resolve the whole paper's global-attraction question.
