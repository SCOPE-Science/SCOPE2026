# Review of the two-exit spatial-resetting theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The domain, generator normalization, admissible predictable intensities, first-hit stopping rule, and boundary conditions are explicit. The proposed value function is globally twice continuously differentiable, satisfies the exact nonlinear Bellman boundary-value identity, and has a strict sign pattern that selects the unique pointwise optimal rate away from switching points. The no-reset and interior formula agree at the midpoint. The stopped Dynkin comparison, together with a uniform geometric tail bound on exit time, proves optimality against nonstationary and history-dependent controls, rather than only stationary candidate rates. The monotonicity and limiting claims follow from strict decrease of \(\tanh z/z\) for \(z>0\). A separate discrete policy iteration implementation passed 60 parameter cases and 11,260 numerical value comparisons. The proof, not discretization, establishes the continuum result.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The exact two-exit mirror-window selection rule and the optimal value for arbitrary finite ceiling are absent from the checked closely related primary texts: Evans–Majumdar (2011) solves a one-target diffusion case with a nonresetting window and identifies its known-target one-sided instantaneous optimum, but does not solve the two-absorbing-endpoints finite-rate control; De Bruyne–Mori (2021/2023) provides the generic resetting Bellman paradigm, but its interval appendix has boundary reinjection with discounted rewards rather than first-exit absorption; Aspman–Mastropietro–Mareček (first public 2025) studies preset spatial rates and a single first-passage target in potential landscapes; and Pal et al. (2025) treats competing exit targets under constant-rate resetting. The general optimal-control generator equation is expressly prior knowledge; novelty is the sharp solved two-target feedback, intensity-independent switch locations, and closed-form all-starting-point value. Targeted published-index queries, primary-source comparisons, and comparison with accessible prior scientific claims produced no covering statement. An unseen same-model solution in the broader literature remains a real publication-priority risk; lack of a search hit alone is not taken as proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
With two competing absorbing endpoints, resetting too close to either target can destroy valuable near-escape trajectories. This makes the selection of a spatially variable Poisson reset rate a natural search-design problem, not an arbitrary parameter slice. The theorem replaces a continuum of possible control functions by a universal geometrically prescribed central interval, unexpectedly independent of rate ceiling and diffusivity. It quantifies the exact achievable benefit, identifies the midpoint no-benefit boundary, and provides the sharp strong-reset limit. This offers a benchmark for finite-rate feedback in bounded searches and a substantial structural boundary-value result beyond plugging a rate into a preexisting formula.

## Closest literature and unresolved risks
- Evans and Majumdar, *Diffusion with Optimal Resetting* (arXiv:1107.4225, especially Sections 3.1–3.2). Their exact instantaneous-control rule assumes a single target, and their window has a different reset/no-reset orientation.
- De Bruyne and Mori, *Resetting in Stochastic Optimal Control* (arXiv:2112.11416; Appendix A and Appendix B). The general Bellman selector is prior work; the appendix does not impose two absorbing Dirichlet endpoints or solve this stationary first-exit minimization.
- Aspman, Mastropietro and Mareček, *First passage time in space-dependent stochastic resetting* (arXiv:2509.11809, Sections II–V and Appendices A–C). Their prescribed piecewise rates are for a single target with drift potentials, without the global two-exit rate-optimization classification.
- Pal et al., *Universal criterion for selective outcomes under stochastic resetting* (arXiv:2502.09127; Section III). Their two-absorbing-boundary first-passage formulas assume constant Poisson reset rate.

The analysis excludes reset energy/time cost, partial observations, non-Brownian increments, and objective functions conditioned on which endpoint is hit. Independent replication, experimental validation and an external publication-priority audit have not been performed.
