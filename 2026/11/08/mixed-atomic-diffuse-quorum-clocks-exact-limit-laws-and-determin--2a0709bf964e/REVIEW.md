## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, subject to historical-priority uncertainty)

Müller and Šebek, *Weighted-threshold Coupon Collection*, arXiv:2607.08551v1, derive a homogeneous-rate limit assuming all maximal quorum weights vanish (Theorem 4.1), while their heavy Zipf regime uses rates aligned with nonuniform weights. Neither case implies the simultaneous finite-heavy plus positive-diffuse limiting process for homogeneous clocks. This new theorem explicitly classifies its mixed law, sharp number of fixed-time atoms and failure of total-variation convergence. Published mathematical search and comparisons with accessible related records found no directly covering statement. Chang and Ross, *The Multiple Subset Coupon Collecting Problem* (2007), concerns general finite stopping rules; only its abstract was available, leaving a genuine residual prior-art risk. The conclusion is not independent originality certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A macroscopic coupon weight can cause sudden quorum attainment, while the remaining individually negligible weights accumulate deterministically. Their interaction is a natural missing boundary regime for weighted partial collection. The exact survival function, complete sharp classification of up to \(2^r\) deterministic crossing atoms and explicit total-variation obstruction give structurally meaningful answers unavailable from the purely diffuse or pure-jump cases. The theorem also covers all positive moments and discrete sampling without additional restrictions on diffuse weight profiles.

## Restrictions and closest sources

The model has equal discovery rates and a strictly positive diffuse total weight, with finitely many heavy coupons and a fixed strict quorum. The pure-atomic boundary, infinite heavy collections and nonuniform discovery rates are not included. Close sources: Müller–Šebek (2026), sections 4 and 7; Chang–Ross (2007, abstract only); Anceaume–Busnel–Sericola (2014, count threshold rather than weight threshold).
