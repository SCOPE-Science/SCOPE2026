# Mixed-dimensional closed-neighborhood homotopy of crown graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 5\), let \(H_n\) be the crown graph obtained from the complete bipartite graph \(K_{n,n}\) by removing a perfect matching. Its closed-neighborhood complex \(\mathcal N[H_n]\) has the **integral homotopy type**
\[
\mathcal N[H_n]\simeq\bigvee^{n^2-3n+1}S^2\ \vee\ S^{n-2}\ \vee\ S^{n-2}.
\]
In particular its only nonzero reduced integral homology groups are \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{n^2-3n+1}\) and \(\widetilde H_{n-2}\cong\mathbb Z^2\), and its fundamental group is trivial. Since \(n\ge5\), the two dimensions are distinct. This is an unrestricted theorem for an infinite family, not an inference from finite homology calculations.

## Assumptions and scope
Take disjoint labeled shores \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\) and \(B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}\). Put an edge \(a_ib_j\) precisely when \(i\ne j\). A face \(\sigma\) of the closed-neighborhood complex of a graph \(G\) is any subset of the vertex set for which there exists \(v\in V(G)\) with \(\sigma\subseteq N_G[v]=\{v\}\cup N_G(v)\). The empty face is included; homotopy assertions concern geometric realizations. All wedges have a common basepoint. The complex is also the *vertex-centered, radius-one, discrete-ball nerve*; it is not the open-neighborhood complex.

## Proof
**1. Two-sided cover.** Let \(U\) be the subcomplex generated by the \(n\) closed neighborhoods of vertices in \(A\) and let \(V\) be the corresponding subcomplex generated by neighborhoods of vertices in \(B\). The maximal generators are
\[
F_i=\{a_i\}\cup(B\setminus\{b_i\}),\qquad E_i=\{b_i\}\cup(A\setminus\{a_i\}).
\]
Consequently, \(\mathcal N[H_n]=U\cup V\), exactly (not merely up to homotopy).

**2. The two spherical halves.** The faces of \(U\) entirely contained in \(B\) form \(\partial\Delta(B)\), the boundary of the \((n-1)\)-simplex, since a proper subset of \(B\) misses at least one index. For each \(i\), every face of \(U\) involving \(a_i\) lies in \(a_i*\Delta(B\setminus\{b_i\})\); no other \(a_j\) occurs in that cone. Because its base is a simplex, this cone simplicially collapses onto its base, **relative to the base**. One explicit collapse chooses any pivot \(p_i\in B\setminus\{b_i\}\), and, in decreasing size, removes the pairs
\[
\bigl(\{a_i\}\cup T,\ \{a_i,p_i\}\cup T\bigr),\qquad T\subseteq B\setminus\{b_i,p_i\}.
\]
At each step the first member is a free codimension-one face in the remaining complex; its only remaining strict coface is the indicated second member. Different \(i\) do not interfere, because they use different apex vertices. Thus \(U\searrow\partial\Delta(B)\simeq S^{n-2}\). The symmetric argument gives \(V\searrow\partial\Delta(A)\simeq S^{n-2}\).

**3. Exact intersection.** A face of \(U\) contains at most one vertex of \(A\), while a face of \(V\) contains at most one vertex of \(B\). Therefore their intersection contains no simplex of dimension two or higher, and its edges are exactly \(a_ib_j\) for \(i\ne j\). In other words \(U\cap V\) is the original crown graph \(H_n\) as a one-dimensional simplicial complex. For \(n\ge3\), this graph is connected, with \(2n\) vertices and \(n(n-1)\) edges, hence
\[
\beta_1(H_n)=n(n-1)-2n+1=n^2-3n+1.
\]
Therefore \(H_n\simeq\bigvee^{n^2-3n+1}S^1\).

**4. Homotopy pushout and attaching maps.** The inclusions \(H_n\hookrightarrow U,V\) are simplicial cofibrations. Since \(n\ge5\), both targets are homotopy equivalent to \(S^{n-2}\), which is simply connected. Every map from the connected graph \(H_n\) to either of these targets is nullhomotopic: choose a spanning tree, contract its image using simple connectivity, then nullhomotope each remaining fundamental loop. An ordinary union of subcomplexes is a homotopy pushout. Replacing its two maps by constant maps thus gives the standard decomposition
\[
U\cup V\simeq U\vee V\vee\Sigma H_n.
\]
Here \(\Sigma H_n\simeq\bigvee^{n^2-3n+1}S^2\). Combining the three factors proves the claimed integral homotopy equivalence. Every integral-homology and fundamental-group statement follows immediately.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify_crown_neighborhood.py` with Python 3 and no third-party packages. The program constructs all closed neighborhood generators from graph adjacency, enumerates every face, checks \(U\cup V\) and the exact crown-graph intersection, performs the stated elementary collapses with a free-face test at each individual pair, and independently computes full reduced simplicial homology over \(\mathbb F_2\). The checked values for \(2\le n\le8\) are shown in `verification_output.txt`; for \(n=5,6,7,8\), the nonzero reduced dimensions and ranks are respectively \((2:11,3:2)\), \((2:19,4:2)\), \((2:29,5:2)\), and \((2:41,6:2)\). A control calculation for \(K_{n,n}\), \(2\le n\le6\), finds only \((n-1)^2\) two-dimensional classes. Finite computations corroborate, but do not prove, the general statement; the proof above does.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita [1], first public 2025-11-16, introduces a systematic treatment of closed-neighborhood complexes and provides general comparisons to independence complexes of canonical double covers, including implications for fundamental groups. His Theorems 1.2 and 1.5 do not state this crown-graph wedge decomposition or give the required two-sided null-attachment calculation. Adams, Shukla and Singh [2] prove a closed-neighborhood result for hypercube graphs: the cubical graph \(Q_3\) is isomorphic to \(H_4\), so their scale-two formula *already covers that boundary case*, giving seven two-spheres. That previously known special case is not the contribution asserted here; the new claim starts at \(n=5\). Larger crown graphs are not hypercubes (the \((n-1)\)-regular crown has \(2n\) vertices, while a hypercube of degree \(n-1\) has \(2^{n-1}\) vertices, equal only when \(n=4\) for \(n\ge3\)). Published complete-multipartite closed-neighborhood/Čech results [3] do not cover crown graphs: their omitted cross edges violate the complete multipartite adjacency condition for \(n\ge3\). Known Lovász **open**-neighborhood and matching/independence-complex results on \(K_2\times K_n\) address different simplicial face sets. Searches found no stronger statement implying the entire \(n\ge5\) formula; this is not a guarantee against unindexed work.

The comparison with the complete bipartite graph is mathematically sharp. Its closed-neighborhood complex is \(\bigvee^{(n-1)^2}S^2\), because the two shore-generated subcomplexes are contractible and intersect in \(K_{n,n}\). Deleting a perfect matching creates two homology classes in a **new high dimension** \(n-2\) for every \(n\ge5\), while changing the rank in dimension two. This gives a natural graph-perturbation distinction, not a numerical fit to a finite table.

## Limitations
Only crown graphs formed by deleting a full perfect matching from \(K_{n,n}\), with \(n\ge5\), are classified. The method does not calculate equivariant homology representations, partial-matching deletions, or general bipartite graphs. The finite verification is only over \(\mathbb F_2\); integral assertions rest on the homotopy-pushout proof. The checked bibliography may omit unindexed equivalent formulations. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed.
