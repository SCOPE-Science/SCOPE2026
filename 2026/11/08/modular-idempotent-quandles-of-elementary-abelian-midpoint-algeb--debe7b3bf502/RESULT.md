# Modular idempotent quandles of elementary-abelian midpoint algebras

## Finding
Let \(p\) be an odd prime, \(r\geq1\), and \(K\) any field of characteristic \(p\). Write \(G=(\mathbb F_p)^r\) additively, and let \(Q=M(G)\) be its midpoint quandle with \(x*y=(x+y)/2\). On the free \(K\)-module \(K[Q]\), extend \(e_xe_y=e_{(x+y)/2}\) bilinearly. Put \(\ell=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\) and \(N=(p^r-1)/\ell\).

**Theorem.** The nonzero idempotents of \(K[Q]\) form, under the quandle-algebra multiplication, a midpoint quandle on an elementary abelian \(p\)-group of \(\mathbb F_p\)-dimension \(N\dim_{\mathbb F_p}K\). In particular, when \(K=\mathbb F_q\) with \(q=p^f\), the exact number of idempotents, including zero, is
\[
\boxed{1+q^{(p^r-1)/\operatorname{ord}_p(2)}},
\]
and the nonzero idempotent quandle is isomorphic to \(M((\mathbb F_p)^{fN})\). The original basis quandle embeds in this nonzero-idempotent quandle. The isomorphism is not asserted to be canonical.

The nonzero idempotents are additionally a subgroup under the **ordinary commutative group-ring multiplication** after transporting \(K[Q]\) to \(K[G]\); this auxiliary multiplication is different from the quandle-algebra multiplication. The subgroup is an elementary abelian \(p\)-group. Its natural midpoint operation agrees with the original quandle-algebra product restricted to the idempotents.

For instance, for \(p=3,r=2,K=\mathbb F_3\) there are exactly \(82\) idempotents, of which \(81\) are nonzero; the original quandle has only nine elements. For \(p=7,r=1,K=\mathbb F_7\) there are exactly \(50\), of which \(49\) are nonzero; an explicit nonbasis idempotent is
\[
e_0+e_1+e_2+4e_3+2e_4+2e_5+4e_6.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient field has **the same odd characteristic** as the underlying elementary abelian group. The group rank is positive, and no assumptions of algebraic closure or finiteness of the coefficient field are made. All idempotents mean elements \(u\) satisfying \(u^2=u\) using the nonassociative quandle-algebra product. Zero is an idempotent but is excluded from the midpoint-quandle structure. For an infinite coefficient field the dimension statement is a cardinal-dimension assertion; the finite-field cardinality formula is asserted only for finite \(K\).

## Proof
**Step 1: transport the nonassociative product.** In the ordinary commutative group algebra \(R=K[G]\), write \(X^gX^h=X^{g+h}\) and let \(\alpha(X^g)=X^{g/2}\), a ring automorphism. The coefficientwise linear identification \(e_g\mapsto X^g\) takes the quandle-algebra product to
\[
u\star v=\alpha(uv)=\alpha(u)\alpha(v),
\]
where the unstarred product on the right is the ordinary associative group-ring product. The order of \(\alpha\) is \(\ell\). Write \(I=\ker(\varepsilon:R\to K)\) for the ordinary augmentation ideal.

**Step 2: the zero-augmentation idempotent vanishes.** Augmentation is multiplicative for \(\star\), hence every \(\star\)-idempotent \(u\) has augmentation \(0\) or \(1\). Because \(G\) is a finite \(p\)-group in characteristic \(p\), \(I\) is nilpotent: for a basis \(g_1,\ldots,g_r\), the ring is \(K[z_1,\ldots,z_r]/(z_1^p,\ldots,z_r^p)\) with \(z_i=X^{g_i}-1\). If \(\varepsilon(u)=0\), then \(u=\alpha(u)^2\) lies in \(I^2\), and inductively in \(I^{2^j}\) for every \(j\); nilpotence forces \(u=0\).

**Step 3: the augmentation-one solutions are a group eigenspace.** Let \(U=1+I\) with ordinary group-ring multiplication, so \(U\) is an abelian group. For every \(v\in I\), the Frobenius identity yields \(v^p=0\): express \(v\) as a linear combination of \(X^g-1\) and use \((X^g-1)^p=X^{pg}-1=0\). Thus \((1+v)^p=1\), making \(U\) an \(\mathbb F_p\)-vector space under its multiplicative group law. Choose \(h\in\mathbb F_p\) with \(2h=1\). For \(u\in U\), the idempotency equation
\[
u=\alpha(u)^2
\]
is equivalent to \(\alpha(u)=u^h\), where powers are ordinary group-ring powers and \(h\) is interpreted modulo \(p\). Accordingly the set \(E\) of augmentation-one \(\star\)-idempotents is the \(h\)-eigenspace of the \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear automorphism \(\alpha:U\to U\). Therefore \(E\) is an elementary abelian subgroup of \(U\).

**Step 4: equivariant linearization without a logarithm.** For \(j\ge1\), put \(U_j=1+I^j\), terminating in \(\{1\}\). The map
\[
(1+w)U_{j+1}\longmapsto w+I^{j+1}
\]
is an \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear, \(\alpha\)-equivariant isomorphism \(U_j/U_{j+1}\cong I^j/I^{j+1}\): the cross term \(ww'\) lies in \(I^{2j}\subseteq I^{j+1}\). The group generated by \(\alpha\) has order \(\ell\mid p-1\), so its order is invertible in \(\mathbb F_p\). Maschke's averaging argument splits every successive extension of these finite filtrations as \(\mathbb F_p[\langle\alpha\rangle]\)-modules. The analogous additive filtration of \(I\) splits too. Hence \(U\) and the additive \(\mathbb F_p\)-module \(I\) are (noncanonically) isomorphic **as representations of** \(\langle\alpha\rangle\), including when \(K\) is infinite. Thus they have the same \(h\)-eigenspace dimension.

**Step 5: count the eigenvectors.** The elements \(X^g-X^0\), for \(g\neq0\), constitute a \(K\)-basis of \(I\); \(\alpha\) permutes this basis by \(g\mapsto g/2\). Every nonzero element of \(G\) belongs to an orbit of length exactly \(\ell\), so there are \(N=(p^r-1)/\ell\) disjoint cycles. On the span of any one cycle, the permutation operator has exactly one eigenvector for eigenvalue \(h=2^{-1}\), because \(h^\ell=1\). Consequently the \(h\)-eigenspace of \(I\) has \(K\)-dimension \(N\) and \(\mathbb F_p\)-dimension \(N\dim_{\mathbb F_p}K\). By Step 4, \(E\) has that dimension as an elementary abelian \(p\)-group. Over \(K=\mathbb F_q\), \(|E|=q^N\), and Step 2 adds only the zero idempotent.

**Step 6: recover the quandle.** For \(u,v\in E\), \(\alpha(u)=u^h\) and \(\alpha(v)=v^h\), so
\[
u\star v=\alpha(uv)=(uv)^h.
\]
This is the midpoint operation on the elementary abelian group \(E\) written multiplicatively. It is closed and gives precisely \(M((\mathbb F_p)^{N\dim_{\mathbb F_p}K})\) up to group isomorphism. Each basis element \(X^g\) belongs to \(E\), since \(\alpha(X^g)=X^{hg}=(X^g)^h\), giving the stated embedded copy of \(Q\). This completes the proof.

## Verification
The standalone file `check_idempotents.py` exhaustively enumerates coefficient vectors and evaluates the bilinear multiplication directly, without using the representation-theoretic formula as an acceptance test. It checks \((p,r,q)=(3,1,3),(5,1,5),(3,2,3),(7,1,7),(3,1,9)\), the last using \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^2+1)\). The respective total numbers of idempotents are \(4,6,82,50,10\); it additionally checks augmentation counts and full quandle closure in the prime-field cases indicated in the program. Recorded output is in `verification_output.txt`. The general conclusion is proved symbolically, not extrapolated from these enumerations.

## Relationship to prior work
Bardakov–Elhamdadi, *Idempotents and Powers of Ideals in Quandle Rings* (arXiv:2601.07057), studies commutative quandle rings and gives explicit idempotents when the coefficient characteristic is **different from** the odd prime quandle order (Proposition 5.5). It does not state the equal-characteristic full idempotent-quandle classification here. Zhang, *Group-ring methods for Alexander quandle rings* (arXiv:2610.08204), gives the relevant transported product, integral augmentation results, and characteristic-zero Fourier idempotent arguments, but not a modular principal-unit eigenspace census. Elhamdadi–Ta–Virgin (arXiv:2609.03799) studies Fourier idempotent methods for integral quandle algebras, not this characteristic-\(p\) distribution. Churchill et al. (arXiv:2608.09149), especially Sections 3–4 and 8, examines core quandles and several finite/integral idempotent questions without the stated general elementary-abelian modular classification.

A previously recorded three-element special case gives \(q+1\) idempotents for the dihedral quandle \(R_3\) in characteristic three; the present theorem specializes to exactly that case when \(p=3,r=1\). That special case is not claimed as new. The contributions here are the uniform formula for all odd primes and all positive ranks, the filtration-based modular proof, and the complete midpoint-quandle structure of the nonzero idempotents. A published complete classification for larger elementary abelian groups was not located; this absence is not a proof of absolute historical priority.

## Limitations
The result does not cover groups with elements of order \(p^2\), characteristic two, coefficient characteristics different from \(p\), or noncommutative/nonmidpoint Alexander quandles. It is a classification of the idempotent set, not a classification of the full quandle-algebra automorphism group. The representation equivalence in Step 4 is noncanonical; no preferred coordinate basis of the idempotent quandle is asserted. Prior-work searches and the inspected full-text material do not establish uniqueness or independently certified novelty.
