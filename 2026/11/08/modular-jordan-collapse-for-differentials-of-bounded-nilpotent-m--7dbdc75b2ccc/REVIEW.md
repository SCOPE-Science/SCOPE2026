# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof gives the polynomial identity \(\mathcal D_{N,d}^2(X)=dN^{d-1}XN^{d-1}\), vanishing of the cube, and a rank calculation using monomial bases in \(k[s,t]/(s^a,t^b)\). The latter is valid in every characteristic because surviving monomials have coefficient one. The kernel dimension gives finite-field first-order lift counts. Exact finite-field checks of the full derivative matrices in characteristics two, three, and five corroborate the ranks and predicted Jordan multiplicities. No finite enumeration is used to infer an infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Kressner (2014) gives the divided-difference representation of the derivative but not this modular Jordan-type formula. Moreau–Yu (2014) concerns complex nilpotent orbit jet geometry. Noferini (2025) solves the general Fréchet-Jordan question over characteristic-zero algebraically closed fields; the present work makes no novelty claim in characteristic zero and explicitly addresses the positive-characteristic divisibility jump. The 1997 Norman paper could not be inspected in full; a broader modular result remains possible. The scientific originality assessment is therefore best-knowledge provisional rather than an independently certified priority assertion.

## Value — PASS

A complete Jordan signature of a natural differential controls its nilpotency index, tangent fiber dimensions, and finite-field lift multiplicities. The sharp collapse when \(p\mid d\) provides a structural boundary that characteristic-zero theory cannot directly establish. The statement holds uniformly for arbitrary Jordan partitions, matrix dimensions, and positive characteristics, not a selected finite example.

The theorem excludes arbitrary polynomial derivatives and higher-order jets. The direct finite-field checks cover only enumerated or sampled small cases; the uniform assertion relies on the proof.
