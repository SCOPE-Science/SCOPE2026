# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact solvability criterion follows from the trace obstruction on each nonzero-eigenvalue Jordan block, and sufficiency is constructed over the original finite field by the derivation \(t^2d/dt\) on each permitted cyclic primary module. The nonstandard junction is the centralizer-unit factor: the proof uses \(|C_{\mathrm{GL}}(Y)|\), not the vector-space centralizer size, and cancels the affine fiber \(q^{c(Y)}\) exactly. The product then follows by the Euler series and unique factorization of monic polynomials. The exact first nonnilpotent contribution checks the normalization. Direct original-matrix tests and independent rational-series coefficient extraction agree in the listed configurations. This is a proof for all dimensions and all finite fields, with finite checks as corroboration only.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The full-text characteristic-zero representation-space analysis of Iyudu (arXiv:1209.0746v1, §§2 and 13) uses nilpotent \(Y\) and already notes that a nonempty fiber in \(X\) is a translated centralizer; neither is claimed new. Shirikov (2007) addresses positive-characteristic prime ideals and automorphisms; its inspected abstract, contents and selected statements do not state the finite-field rational-canonical admissibility test or enumerator. Bourgeois (2014, §2, Remark 6) provides an explicit *known* characteristic-three nonnilpotent example. The classical Feit–Fine commuting-pair generating function, also treated by Fulman–Guralnick, supplies a related counting method but not the present restricted sum. Feinberg (1979), in a published two-page theorem established \(I=[A,B]\) solvability exactly when the Jordan-block sizes of \(A\) are multiples of \(p\). On any invertible primary block, \([X,Y]=Y^2\) reduces directly to this result via \([Y^{-2}X,Y]=I\). Consequently the general block admissibility test itself is treated as known in substance; the assessed contribution is the **specific closed all-dimensional generating product for the full finite-field Jordan-plane representation census**, with the criterion used as an input, not the previously known existence or linear solvability phenomenon. Searches of the linked published mathematical research archive for the relation, its modular block criterion, and equivalent matrix-pair counts returned no implication-level match. The point-count product is a fairly short derivation from established structural and enumeration tools; it was not found stated in inspected sources. A broader unlocated Ore-extension or centralizer-weighted formula remains an explicit residual historical-priority risk; absence in search is not proof of priority. No independent historical audit is represented.

## Value — PASS
The Jordan plane is a standard infinite-dimensional algebra with nontrivial finite-dimensional representation spaces. In positive characteristic, the existence and cardinality of its matrix representation fibers change discontinuously at block length \(p\). The all-dimensional enumerator is a natural arithmetic invariant of the entire representation variety, not an isolated small-field example. The precise comparison with the characteristic-zero nilpotence picture and the first positive nonnilpotent contribution make the transition meaningful; an exact finite-field census has plausible reuse for modular representation-space arithmetic. It does not purport to classify isomorphism classes or geometric components.
