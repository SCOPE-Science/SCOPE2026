# Scientific review: maximum induced-matching mobility in triangular graphs

**Correctness (PASS).** Every edge of \(T_n\) is a marked triple, and two such edges can coexist in an induced matching if and only if their supports are disjoint. This proves the maximum \(\lfloor n/3\rfloor\), the classification and exact number of states. Replacements are confined to the old triple plus the unused labels; the formulas for jump and slide degrees enumerate all possibilities without double-counting. When no labels are free, supports are fixed and independent three-state centre changes give products of \(K_3\); otherwise, legal slides interchange any occupied label with a free label, generating the entire state space. The two standalone implementations independently and exhaustively verify small instances and complete move graphs.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** Published primary work on maximal induced matching counts in triangle-free graphs is not applicable to triangular graphs, which contain triangles, and does not give the exact reconfiguration graph. Claw-free independent-set reconfiguration results establish general algorithmic theory but not the present matching-edge formulas. Regular-induced-subgraph reconfiguration moves one endpoint vertex per step rather than replacing a selected whole edge; its complexity results do not imply this theorem. A paper on graph products studies induced matching numbers rather than this exact configuration space. Historical and index searches did not identify the same implication, but absence from a search index cannot prove absolute novelty. Unindexed matching-packing reconfiguration literature is a residual priority risk.

**Value (PASS).** The natural maximum-induced-matching problem is solved at the level of the entire optimum configuration space on a classical symmetric graph family. A strict modulo-three connectivity threshold, exact regular degrees for two genuinely different whole-edge moves, and Cartesian-product identification of every disconnected component give structural information not contained in an induced-matching number alone. These components describe when optimum matchings are reachable without reducing cardinality.

The full claim is restricted to the explicitly defined edge-token operations. The finite experiments are not substituted for the infinite proof. Independent scholarly validation has not been performed.
