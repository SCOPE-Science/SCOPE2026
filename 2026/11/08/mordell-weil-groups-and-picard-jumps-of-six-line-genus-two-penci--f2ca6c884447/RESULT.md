# Mordell--Weil groups and Picard jumps of six-line genus-two pencils

## Finding
Let \(L_0,\ldots,L_5\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\) be distinct lines with no triple intersection. Choose \(p\in L_0\) away from all nodes so that the ten lines \(\overline{p(L_i\cap L_j)}\), \(1\le i<j\le5\), are distinct. Let \(X\) be the minimal resolution of the double cover of \(\mathbb P^2\) branched over \(\sum_iL_i\). The pencil through \(p\) produces a genus-two curve \(C\) over \(K=\mathbb C(t)\) and its Jacobian \(J\). **Then**
\[
J(K)\cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^4\oplus\mathbb Z^{\rho(X)-16}.
\]
The torsion subgroup is thus fixed for every admissible arrangement and choice of \(p\); its free rank records exactly the Picard-number jump above sixteen. In particular, rank is zero for very general six lines and one for a very general six-line arrangement tangent to a smooth conic. The rank-zero assertion for very general coefficients is prior information, not the new part of this theorem.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties are over \(\mathbb C\). The lines are labeled; their nodes are distinct. The ten directions condition excludes the finitely many points \(p\) causing two of the nodes among \(L_1,\ldots,L_5\) to lie on one pencil member. The branch double plane has fifteen ordinary nodes, and \(X\) is its smooth K3 resolution. Blowing up the pencil's two base points (the second infinitely near the first) gives \(f:Y\to\mathbb P^1\) with a section \(O\), so \(\rho(Y)=\rho(X)+2\). A smooth fiber is a hyperelliptic genus-two curve with six \(K\)-rational Weierstrass points: the fixed branch point at \(p\) and five others associated to \(L_1,\ldots,L_5\).

## Proof
**1. Fibers and a divisor lattice.** By local resolution of the double plane, or the fiber description of Bannai--Tokunaga [1, Section 2.2], there are exactly ten singular fibers from the ten nodes \(L_i\cap L_j\) with \(i,j>0\), and one distinguished singular fiber from \(L_0\). A pair-collision fiber has two reduced components: a smooth genus-one curve and an exceptional rational curve \(E_{ij}\), meeting transversely at two points. Thus \(E_{ij}^2=-2\). Different \(E_{ij}\)'s lie in different fibers and do not intersect. The distinguished fiber has seven components: a central rational curve \(B\) with multiplicity two, and six multiplicity-one rational arms; the section meets one of the arms. The other five arms are exceptional curves \(A_1,\ldots,A_5\) over \(L_0\cap L_i\). Their intersection numbers are \(A_i^2=-2\), \(A_iB=1\), and \(A_iA_j=0\) for distinct indices. The central curve has \(B^2=-3\): before the two pencil blow-ups it is the proper transform of a branch line, a \((-2)\)-curve on the K3 surface, and the first blow-up at \(p\) lowers its square by one. Equivalently, intersecting the central fiber with \(B\) gives \(2B^2+6=0\).

The subgroup \(T\subset\operatorname{NS}(Y)\) generated by the section \(O\), a fiber \(F\), the ten \(E_{ij}\), and \(B,A_1,\ldots,A_5\) has rank \(18\). The section and fiber have a unimodular intersection block (its determinant is \(-1\)), and are orthogonal to the chosen nonidentity components. The latter comprise ten isolated \((-2)\)-vectors and the six-by-six star matrix
\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}
-3&1&1&1&1&1\\1&-2&0&0&0&0\\1&0&-2&0&0&0\\1&0&0&-2&0&0\\1&0&0&0&-2&0\\1&0&0&0&0&-2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det(G)=16.
\]
Thus \(T\) is nondegenerate of rank \(18\) and absolute discriminant \(2^{10}\cdot16=2^{14}\).

**2. Exact relationship with rational divisor classes.** Because \(Y\) is regular, any Cartier divisor on the generic fiber extends to a divisor on \(Y\) by Zariski closure. Subtracting its degree times \(O\) yields a surjection \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\to J(K)\). A divisor restricts to the identity in the degree-zero Picard group exactly when its difference with a principal divisor is vertical, so the kernel is generated by \(O\) and the irreducible fiber components. The relation within each fiber expresses the component meeting \(O\) through \(F\) and the remaining components; the kernel is therefore exactly \(T\). Since \(h^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=0\), \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)=\operatorname{NS}(Y)\). We conclude
\[
J(K)\cong\operatorname{NS}(Y)/T,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}J(K)=\rho(X)+2-18=\rho(X)-16.
\]
This conclusion holds at every admissible Picard-number jump, without a genericity assumption on \(X\) beyond the configuration in the statement.

**3. Uniform torsion rigidity.** Choose an affine coordinate on each general pencil line with the fixed Weierstrass point at infinity. The other five branch points have equations \(x_i(t)=a_it+b_i\), with \(a_i,b_i\in\mathbb C\). The ten simple zeros of \(x_i-x_j\) are pairwise distinct, so the parameter line is a generic transverse affine section of the type-\(A_4\) braid hyperplane arrangement \(\{x_i=x_j\}\) in \(\mathbb C^5\). By the affine Lefschetz theorem for arrangement complements [3], the inclusion of this line minus its ten discriminant points induces a surjection on fundamental groups onto the pure five-strand braid group \(P_5\). The Jacobian torsion local system over the punctured base is the restriction of the universal hyperelliptic Jacobian local system with these five labeled moving branch points and a sixth at infinity.

For every positive integer \(n\), a point of \(J(K)[n]\) is a monodromy-invariant point of the finite local system \(H_1(C_t,\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)\). The pure braids \(\sigma_i^2\), \(i=1,\ldots,4\), act by the squares of Picard--Lefschetz transvections
\[
T_{v_i}^2(w)=w+2\langle w,v_i\rangle v_i
\quad\pmod n,
\]
where the four vanishing cycles \(v_i\) of a chain between the five moving branch points form a unimodular basis of \(H_1(C_t,\mathbb Z)\). (The square arises because the ordered roots make a full, not half, twist.) If \(w\) is fixed by all four squares, primitiveness of each \(v_i\) gives \(2\langle w,v_i\rangle=0\pmod n\). Unimodularity then gives \(2w=0\pmod n\). Hence **every rational torsion point has order at most two**. Conversely, all six branch points are \(K\)-rational; their differences generate the full sixteen-element group \(J[2](K)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^4\). Thus \(J(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}=J[2](K)\), for every arrangement in scope. A finitely generated Mordell--Weil group with this torsion and the free rank computed above has the claimed form.

**4. Generic and conic-tangent specializations.** The Picard-number computations for double planes branched along six lines in Lombardo--Peters--Schuett [2, Sections 3.2--3.5] give \(\rho(X)=16\) for very general six lines and \(\rho(X)=17\) for very general six lines tangent to a common smooth conic. Inserting these values proves the stated rank-zero and rank-one cases. The former was already known; the uniform statement and rank-one specialization are the claimed extension.

## Verification
The standalone `verification/verify_lattice_braid.py` uses only the Python standard library. It checks the ten different pair-collision directions in an explicit general-position six-line witness; the star matrix determinant \(16\); the generic-lattice index \(16\) from discriminants \(2^{14}\) and \(2^6\); that four standard vanishing cycles form an integral unimodular basis; and the fixed-point count \(\gcd(n,2)^4\) for their squared transvections for every \(2\le n\le12\). Its exact output is `GENUS_TWO_LATTICE_AND_BRAID_CHECK_OK 10 16 11`. These finite tests corroborate the computations but do not replace the geometric arguments for arbitrary arrangements or all torsion orders.

## Relationship to prior work
Bannai--Tokunaga [1] construct the genus-two pencil and describe its singular fiber types. Lombardo--Peters--Schuett [2] compute generic and special Neron--Severi lattices for double six-line planes. The affine Lefschetz principle [3] and pure-braid Picard--Lefschetz action [4] supply the monodromy input. These are ingredients, not original assertions. The very-general rank-zero group had already been determined previously and is not claimed as new. The additional result is the complete group at **every** admissible Picard jump, especially the assertion that special arrangements never acquire extra torsion. Literature inspection did not identify an equivalent uniform statement; historical priority remains provisional.

## Limitations
The proof is over \(\mathbb C\), assumes no triple line intersections and ten distinct pair-collision directions, and determines the abstract Jacobian group, not canonical generators for its free part, nor its Mordell--Weil height pairing. Picard numbers of individual specialized configurations still need to be computed independently. It does not classify degenerations violating these assumptions. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
