# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem fixes its rooted, unit-edge, full-binary model and the uniform fixed-deletion law. The edge-loss representation is complete for two or three deleted taxa: pendant losses, at most one contained cherry and at most one three-leaf clade, with the latter implying the former. Taking the two exact moments proves both variance identities. The bounds on disjoint cherry and pitchfork clades establish strict recovery of each shape index. The infinite construction has exactly the counted clades and the resulting variance differences have explicit positive numerators. Exact direct-subset enumeration of all rooted nonplane binary shapes on four through eleven leaves and four instances of the infinite family independently matches the formulas. The infinity claim rests on the algebra, not extrapolation from enumeration.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Nipperess and Matsen's first-posted 2012 general rooted-phylogenetic-diversity rarefaction covariance formula covers computation of these variances and is acknowledged without asserting a new generic method. Disanto and Wiehe's first-posted 2011 work and Choi–Thompson–Wu's 2020 work establish distributions of cherry/pitchfork shape indices, including small shape counts. The inspected primary discussions do not state that two near-complete variance values recover exactly the two indices, or exhibit the strict two-to-three-loss ranking reversal for arbitrarily many taxa. Combining the generic moment method with known six-leaf shape tables yields finite examples in principle, but does not itself prove the all-size construction. Broader combinatorial-phylogenetic literature could contain an equivalent index-recovery observation; historical priority is not independently established.

## Value — PASS
The first two potentially fluctuating stages of *near-complete* fixed-budget phylogenetic rarefaction are mathematically natural. Their variance pair identifies two interpretable tree-shape counts, while an infinite family proves a limitation of using one deletion budget to rank taxa-loss uncertainty across ecosystems with equal total evolutionary branch length. The exact result is self-contained and offers a reproducible benchmark for comparisons of phylogenetic diversity under species loss. The result is not claimed as a measured conservation effect or as a classification of entire tree shapes.

## Closest work and limitations
Primary background: Nipperess and Matsen (2012/2013), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3699894/ ; Disanto and Wiehe (2011/2013), https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1295 ; Choi, Thompson and Wu (2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12643 . Their general formulae and subtree statistics are important predecessors. The theorem does not extend automatically to unequal branch lengths, independent extinctions or unrooted PD. Broader historical coverage remains a residual risk.
