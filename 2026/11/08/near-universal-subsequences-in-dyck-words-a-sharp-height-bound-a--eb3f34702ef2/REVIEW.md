# Review of the Dyck near-universality theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The maximum-pair-matching formulation follows from greedy binary arch factorization; a prefix cut crosses at most one matched edge, giving the sharp inequality. The first two layers follow from the explicit height-two encoding. The third layer is counted by exact 3-state formal matrix inversions at Dyck height three. An independent finite enumerator checks 82,499 paths through semilength 11, and exact symbolic inversion checks the generating-function algebra. No infinite conclusion is drawn solely from finite testing.

## Originality
PASS, with bibliographic qualification. Barker et al. establish arch factorization; Adamson et al. count regular-language accepted words; the published unrestricted binary rational generating function does not impose Dyck conditions. Barcucci et al. and other sources cover classical height-two Dyck enumeration, which is expressly credited. Mol, Rampersad and Shallit study Dyck factors of automatic words instead of all Dyck words by subsequence universality. None of the inspected statements implies the exact deficit-two Dyck formula. Unindexed equivalents remain a risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The exact third near-universal layer is a complete natural enumerative invariant for an infinite family, and the global height obstruction reduces a context-free problem to a height-three automaton. It separates true subsequence universality from height alone and provides a reusable technique for deeper deficit layers.

## Limitations and review status
The theorem does not classify arbitrary lower universality indices. The height-two count and arch characterization are not claimed as new independently.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
