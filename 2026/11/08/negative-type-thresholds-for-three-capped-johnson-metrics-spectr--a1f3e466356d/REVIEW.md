# Mathematical review

## Correctness
PASS. The Johnson adjacency identity and the classical harmonic spectrum yield exact necessary and sufficient conditional-negative-definiteness inequalities for all real zero-sum coefficients. Schoenberg's fractional-power principle supplies nesting, the embedded eight-element four-subset metric supplies an explicit finite upper barrier, and the concave spectral polynomial proves unique critical degree in the stated regime. Asymptotic limits follow from uniform expansion for bounded scaled frequency and quadratic suppression of unbounded frequencies. The packaged checker verifies exact finite cases and supplies non-proof numerical diagnostics.

## Originality
PASS under bounded comparison, not a global priority guarantee. The classical 1938 negative-type theory and Johnson association-scheme eigenvalues furnish methods rather than the new threshold/mode-transition theorem. The known four-subset special case is subsumed, but does not imply general subset ranks or the rank-density asymptotic and changing critical index. Primary accessible texts were inspected for parameters, implications, and the type of quantities controlled. An unindexed older general result could imply some part of the theorem; no claim of independently established priority is made.

## Value
PASS. The negative-type exponent is the exact threshold for the powered metric to admit an isometric embedding into real Hilbert space. The complete spectral test covers all subset ranks and ambient sizes, and its sharp fixed-rank and rank-density scaling regimes identify genuinely different geometric behavior, including degree migration not visible in the four-subset case. This motivated classification is not merely a small numerical refinement.
