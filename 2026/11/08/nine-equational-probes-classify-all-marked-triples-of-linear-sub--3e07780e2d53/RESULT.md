# Nine equational probes classify all marked triples of linear subspaces

## Finding
Fix any field \(F\), dimension \(n\ge 0\), and an ordered triple \((A_1,A_2,A_3)\) of subspaces of \(F^n\). Let \(L(A_1,A_2,A_3)\) be the **bounded** sublattice generated by these subspaces, using intersection, vector-space sum, the zero subspace, and the ambient space. Isomorphisms of marked bounded lattices must preserve the ordered triple and both bounds.

For each subset \(S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\), put \(P_S=\bigwedge_{i\in S}A_i\), with empty meet equal to the ambient space, and \(Q_S=\bigvee_{j\notin S}A_j\), with empty join equal to zero. Define the eight tests
\[
E_S:\qquad P_S=P_S\wedge Q_S.
\]
Define the ninth test
\[
E_\triangle:\quad A_1\wedge(A_2\vee A_3)=(A_1\wedge A_2)\vee(A_1\wedge A_3).
\]
**Theorem.** Two ordered triples of finite-dimensional subspaces, possibly over different fields and in different ambient dimensions, generate isomorphic marked bounded lattices if and only if these nine equalities have precisely the same truth values. Every one of the \(2^9=512\) possible truth patterns is attained over **every** field. Consequently, there are exactly \(512\) marked bounded lattice types of subspace triples, independent of the field. Among universal families of yes/no equational tests, nine tests is the minimum possible for a complete classification.

More precisely, let \(T=\{S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}: E_S\text{ fails}\}\) be the collection of indices of failed tests among the eight tests \(E_S\), and let \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) indicate failure of \(E_\triangle\). The least possible ambient dimension realizing this fingerprint is
\[
\delta(T,\varepsilon)=|T|+2\varepsilon.
\]
If \(T\ne\varnothing\), the fingerprint occurs in every dimension \(n\ge\delta(T,\varepsilon)\). If \(T=\varnothing\) and \(\varepsilon=1\), it occurs in exactly the positive even dimensions. The all-true fingerprint \((T,\varepsilon)=(\varnothing,0)\) occurs only in dimension zero.

Writing \(H(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(8,s)}\binom8k\) for \(s\ge1\), and \(H(s)=0\) for \(s\le0\), the number \(N(n)\) of marked types realizable in one fixed ambient dimension is
\[
N(0)=1,\qquad N(n)=H(n)+H(n-2)+\mathbf1_{\{n\ge2\ \mathrm{and}\ n\ \mathrm{even}\}}\quad(n\ge1).
\]
In particular \((N(0),N(1),N(2),N(3),N(4))=(1,8,37,100,199)\); for even \(n\ge10\), \(N(n)=511\), and for odd \(n\ge11\), \(N(n)=510\).

## Assumptions and scope
All fields are permitted, including finite fields of arbitrary characteristic. All ambient spaces have finite nonnegative dimension. Meets and joins are the actual intersection and vector-space sum. The zero and unit are **named** in the generated bounded lattice, and the three generators are ordered and named. Equational types are kernels of evaluation of every bounded lattice term in three generator names; they are not claims about the full first-order theories of the ambient subspace lattices. Identical term kernels are equivalent to marked bounded-lattice isomorphism by the canonical quotient construction.

## Proof
**1. The nine indecomposable blocks.** The classical three-subspace decomposition, equivalently the injective \(D_4\)-quiver normal form, splits any triple into a direct sum of nine kinds of blocks, with nonnegative integer multiplicities. Eight are one-dimensional blocks \(B_S\), one for each subset \(S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\): the \(i\)th subspace equals the full line on \(B_S\) exactly when \(i\in S\). The ninth is a two-dimensional block \(B_\triangle\): its three subspaces are distinct lines, represented by \(F e_1\), \(F e_2\), and \(F(e_1+e_2)\).

For a direct reduction, first split off directions outside the sum of the three subspaces by taking a complement to that sum. Split off their common intersection by any projection onto it: every subspace contains the image and so splits compatibly. Then each pair intersection is disjoint from the third subspace. A projection onto that pair intersection with kernel containing the third subspace splits it compatibly from all three. Repeating for each pair leaves pairwise disjoint subspaces. For each remaining subspace in turn, take an internal complement to its intersection with the sum of the other two, and split off this complement by a projection that kills those other two. Any inclusion of an unaltered subspace in the sum of the other two persists after a projection of this kind: apply the complementary projection to the inclusion. The residual triple thus has pairwise disjoint subspaces and every pair spans the residual ambient space. In the direct sum of the first two, projection of the third onto either summand is then an isomorphism. The third is the graph of an isomorphism between the first two; choosing a basis in one summand and its image in the other splits this graph into copies of \(B_\triangle\). Each split-off space of the other kinds decomposes into lines. This proof requires no algebraic closure or field-size assumption.

The multiplicities are uniquely recoverable from ordinary dimensions. Write \(s=\dim(A_1+A_2+A_3)\) and \(t=\dim(A_1\cap A_2\cap A_3)\). Then \(m_{\varnothing}=n-s\), \(m_{\{i\}}=s-\dim(A_j+A_k)\) for \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), \(m_{\{i,j\}}=\dim(A_i\cap A_j)-t\), and \(m_{\{1,2,3\}}=t\). The diagonal multiplicity is \(m_\triangle=\dim A_1-\sum_{S\ni1}m_S\). The ambient dimension identity is \(n=\sum_S m_S+2m_\triangle\).

**2. The bounded term kernel depends only on which blocks occur.** In a direct-sum decomposition, any sum or intersection of decomposed subspaces is computed componentwise. The named bounds are also componentwise. This remains true for every recursively nested bounded lattice term. Repeated copies of a single block give identical term evaluations on every copy. Hence a term equation holds on the whole triple if and only if it holds on each block type of **positive** multiplicity. The lattices generated inside the nine individual blocks are the same marked lattices over every field: a bounded two-element chain for a line block and the three-atom diamond for the plane block. Thus the whole equational theory is determined by the nine-bit support vector of the multiplicities, also when comparing different fields.

**3. The nine tests recover that support exactly.** On the one-dimensional block \(B_R\), the left side of \(E_S\) is the full line exactly when \(S\subseteq R\), while the right side is the full line exactly when additionally \(R\cap(\{1,2,3\}\setminus S)\ne\varnothing\). Therefore \(E_S\) fails on \(B_R\) if and only if \(R=S\). The same equality holds on \(B_\triangle\) for every \(S\): for \(|S|=0\), the join of the three lines is the plane; for \(|S|=1\), the other two lines span the plane; for \(|S|=2\) or \(3\), the meet is zero. Conversely, distributivity \(E_\triangle\) holds on every one-dimensional block and fails on \(B_\triangle\), where its two sides are respectively the first line and zero. A test fails on the direct sum exactly when its uniquely detected block type occurs. Therefore the nine tests identify the complete block support. By step 2, equality of fingerprints implies equality of all bounded term identities, and conversely unequal fingerprints yield an explicit separating equation.

**4. All supports and dimension thresholds.** Given any subset \(T\) of the eight one-dimensional block indices and any \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\), take one copy of each selected one-dimensional block and, when \(\varepsilon=1\), one copy of \(B_\triangle\). This realizes precisely the desired failed-test set in dimension \(|T|+2\varepsilon\). Any realizing triple must contain at least those blocks, so no smaller dimension works. When \(T\ne\varnothing\), extra dimensions can be absorbed by increasing the multiplicity of any selected one-dimensional block by one each time. When \(T=\varnothing\) and \(\varepsilon=1\), only copies of the two-dimensional block are possible, so precisely positive even dimensions work. Empty support forces zero dimension. Count the nonempty \(T\)'s of sizes at most \(n\) for \(\varepsilon=0\), at most \(n-2\) for \(\varepsilon=1\), and add the even-dimensional diagonal-only case; this gives \(N(n)\) as stated.

**5. Information-theoretic sharpness.** Since all \(512\) support vectors occur and yield distinct marked term-equation types, any fixed complete family of \(b\) binary equational questions must give at least \(512\) different answer strings. Therefore \(2^b\ge512\) and \(b\ge9\). The displayed nine equations achieve equality.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` evaluates all nine tests on their exact one-dimensional and two-dimensional normal-form blocks. It enumerates the closure of all bounded lattice terms in the nine-block product (30 distinct term functions), enumerates all 512 supports, and checks pairwise distinct marked term kernels and the fixed-dimensional counting formula. The separate `finite_models.py` enumerates actual subspaces, joins, and meets over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\) in dimensions one through three, and checks all 26,405 ordered triples against the predicted nine-test support. Outputs are `verify_output.txt` and `finite_models_output.txt`. These finite checks are corroborative; the universal classification and dimension statements follow from the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Etingof and collaborators' treatment of the \(D_4\) quiver supplies the classical nine-block decomposition. The earlier Resultary record [*Three-variable field blindness for finite-dimensional subspace lattices*](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/11/2/SCOPE-three-variable-field-blindness-for-finite-dimensional-subspace-l--0c8924687c3f) uses the same normal form for first-order theories with three variables in the language of inclusion, not for term identities of a fixed marked generating triple. Grätzer's text gives Dedekind's classical 28-element free modular lattice on three (unbounded) generators; with external ambient bounds the block evaluator here has 30 term functions. These classical facts already explain generic three-generator finiteness. The claimed contribution is the explicit minimal equational fingerprint, its independent realizability over every field, and the fixed-dimensional census.

## Limitations
All types are **marked and bounded**: unmarked isomorphism classes could merge, and omitting bounds changes the term language. The nine tests classify algebraic term identities, not arbitrary first-order types, and multiplicities larger than one are deliberately forgotten. The finite model enumerations only cover the stated small prime fields and dimensions. Published literature could contain an equivalent result under the terminology of congruences of the free bounded modular lattice; the literature comparison does not establish exhaustive priority.
