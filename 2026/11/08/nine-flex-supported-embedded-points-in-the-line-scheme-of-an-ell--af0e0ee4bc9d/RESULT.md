# Nine flex-supported embedded points in the line scheme of an elliptic cubic cone

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb C\), let \(E=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^2\) be any smooth plane cubic, and let \(S=V(F(X,Y,Z))\subset\mathbb P^3_{[W:X:Y:Z]}\) be its projective cone. Write \(\mathscr F=F_1(S)\) for the **scheme-theoretic** Fano scheme of lines, as the zero scheme of the cubic restriction section of \(\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathcal U^\vee\) on \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)\). Its reduced scheme is \(E\) via the ruling lines from the vertex, and its nilradical is \(\mathcal N\).

**Theorem.** At a nonflex point of \(E\), the completed local ring of \(\mathscr F\) is \(k[[t,e]]/(e^2)\). At each flex it is \(k[[t,e]]/(te^2,e^3)\). All nine flexes are distinct. There are no other embedded contributions. Moreover,
\[
\mathcal N/\mathcal N^2\simeq\mathcal O_E(1),\qquad
\mathcal N^2\simeq\bigoplus_{p\in\operatorname{Flex}(E)}k(p),\qquad\mathcal N^3=0.
\]
In particular \(\mathscr F\) has generic scheme length two along \(E\), plus a total length-nine embedded nilpotent layer supported precisely on its nine flexes. In the Plücker embedding,
\[
P_{\mathscr F}(n)=\chi(\mathscr F,\mathcal O_{\mathscr F}(n))=6n+12.
\]
Thus its Plücker degree is \(6\) and its arithmetic genus is \(-11\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). The cubic \(F\) is arbitrary subject only to smoothness of its projective zero curve \(E\). A flex means a point where its tangent line has contact order three. The line scheme refers to the full Hilbert/Fano scheme, **not** the reduced variety of ruling lines. The parameter \(n\) is any sufficiently large integer for the Hilbert polynomial statement; the displayed Euler characteristic equality in fact holds for all integers \(n\).

## Proof
**1. Support.** A line in the cone not passing through its vertex \([1:0:0:0]\) would project isomorphically to a line in \(E\). A smooth plane cubic contains no line, so every line on \(S\) is a ruling, giving \(\mathscr F_{\rm red}\simeq E\).

**2. Equations for all infinitesimal lines.** Near a ruling over \(p=[1:b:d]\in E\), represent lines by the two spanning vectors
\[
(W,X,Y,Z)=(1,0,a,c),\qquad (0,1,b,d).
\]
For arbitrary homogeneous cubic \(F\), requiring \(F(v,a u+bv,c u+dv)\equiv0\) gives exactly the four equations
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(1,b,d)&=0,\\
aF_Y(1,b,d)+cF_Z(1,b,d)&=0,\\
\tfrac12 a^2F_{YY}(1,b,d)+acF_{YZ}(1,b,d)+\tfrac12c^2F_{ZZ}(1,b,d)&=0,\\
F(0,a,c)&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Other projective charts are obtained by permuting the base coordinates. Since \(E\) is smooth, the first equation has a local parameter \(t\). Since its gradient is nonzero, the second can be solved for one of \(a,c\); write the remaining normal parameter as \(e\). The other two equations have the exact shape \(\kappa(t)e^2=0\) and \(\tau(t)e^3=0\). Here \(\kappa(p)=0\) precisely if the tangent has contact order at least three, and \(\tau(p)\ne0\) whenever \(p\) is a flex, because the tangent is not a component of a smooth cubic.

**3. Local scheme and all exceptional points.** At a nonflex, \(\kappa\) is a unit, giving \(k[[t,e]]/(e^2)\). At a flex, \(\tau\) is a unit. For a smooth plane cubic in characteristic zero the flexes are the nine reduced points of its \(3\)-torsion group after choosing a flex as origin: \(p\) is a flex exactly when \(3p\) is linearly equivalent to a hyperplane section, and multiplication by three is étale of degree nine. Accordingly the tangent-contact coefficient \(\kappa\), equivalently the Hessian section restricted to \(E\) up to a unit, has a **simple zero** at each flex. Its local equation is therefore \(te^2=e^3=0\). The local ideal \((e)\) has \((e)^2=(e^2)\), a one-dimensional residue at the flex and zero elsewhere. All lines are already covered by the ruling charts, proving there are no other components or embedded points.

**4. Global first nilpotent layer.** Along \(E\), the tautological rank-two bundle of ruling lines splits as \(\mathcal U=\mathcal O_E\oplus\mathcal O_E(-1)\): the first summand is the fixed vertex direction. Set \(Q=\mathcal O_E^3/\mathcal O_E(-1)\). The gradient of \(F\) defines a surjection \(Q\to\mathcal O_E(2)\). Its kernel \(K\) is the tangent-direction line of the embedded curve before tensoring by \(\mathcal O_E(1)\): indeed \(T_E=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal O_E(-1),K)\), so \(K\simeq T_E\otimes\mathcal O_E(-1)\simeq\mathcal O_E(-1)\). First-order motions of the fixed vertex of a ruling line, subject to the linear \(F\)-constraint, are sections of \(K\). Consequently the normal (first-order infinitesimal) direction of \(E\) in \(\mathscr F\) is \(K\), and its dual conormal is \(\mathcal N/\mathcal N^2\simeq K^\vee\simeq\mathcal O_E(1)\). This is valid also at flexes: there \(\mathcal N/\mathcal N^2\) remains locally freely generated by \(e\), while only its square gains a residue. The local calculations give the claimed description of \(\mathcal N^2\) and \(\mathcal N^3=0\).

**5. Hilbert polynomial.** The Plücker line bundle restricts to \(\det(\mathcal U^\vee)=\mathcal O_E(1)\). The successive quotients of the nilpotent filtration of \(\mathcal O_{\mathscr F}\) are \(\mathcal O_E\), \(\mathcal O_E(1)\), and nine skyscraper sheaves of length one. The elliptic curve has \(\chi(E,\mathcal O_E(n))=3n\). Additivity gives \(3n+3(n+1)+9=6n+12\), as asserted.

## Verification
The accompanying executable `verify.py` uses exact rational polynomial arithmetic (SymPy) to expand the general cubic equation on a Grassmann chart, verify the four Fermat-cubic chart equations, eliminate a normal coordinate, compute the local flex and nonflex ideals, and confirm that the Hessian of \(X^3+Y^3+Z^3\) cuts the curve at nine simple flexes. It then checks the nilpotent residue and Hilbert polynomial identities. Run with `python verify.py`; the accompanying `verification_output.txt` records the output. These are finite symbolic checks of proof identities, not a computer enumeration of every smooth cubic.

## Relationship to prior work
Eisenbud and Harris, *The Geometry of Schemes*, §IV.3, define scheme-theoretic Fano schemes and explain that nonreduced structures occur; Exercise IV-80 asks about a **flat limit under smoothing** of a cubic cone, rather than the intrinsic local nilpotent structure calculated here. Galkin–Shinder (arXiv:1405.5154, §4.1) explicitly replace the Fano scheme by its reduction, and their Example 5.5 identifies ruling lines of a cone with the base. Ilten (arXiv:1411.3025, Theorem 2.2) classifies Fano schemes of **toric** surfaces, not cones over elliptic cubics. The present statement retains the full nonreduced scheme and detects the extra flex-supported order-three layer and its global Hilbert polynomial. It does not assert novelty of the cone's rulings or of the classical nine flexes.

## Limitations
The result is proved in characteristic zero, not across characteristics two or three, where the Hessian/flex geometry may differ. It describes the Fano scheme of lines, not the flat limit of the 27 lines of smoothing cubic surfaces. Primary literature comparison found no exact covering theorem in the sources inspected, but this does not establish exhaustive historical priority; further specialist comparison remains appropriate. The verification code corroborates explicit identities, while the global conormal and flex arguments are mathematical proofs rather than computational experiments.
