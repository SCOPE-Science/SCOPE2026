# Nine-torsion in the seven-by-seven toroidal queens independence complex

## Finding
Let \(Q\) be the attack graph of queens on the square torus \((\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z)^2\), with 49 vertices. The full simplicial complex \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(Q)\) of all nonattacking placements satisfies
\[
\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z,&i=2,\\
\mathbb Z^{120},&i=3,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The nonempty face vector in dimensions zero through six is \((49,588,2156,2450,882,196,28)\). The torsion subgroup has **order nine and exponent nine**, not two independent order-three summands.

## Assumptions and scope
Rows and columns are residues \(r,c\in\{0,1,\ldots,6\}\), with residues computed modulo seven. Distinct squares \((r,c)\) and \((r',c')\) attack if and only if at least one of \(r=r'\), \(c=c'\), \(r-c=r'-c'\), or \(r+c=r'+c'\) holds modulo seven. A simplex is any subset with no attacking pair, including subsets smaller than a full seven-queen placement. All boundary maps use sorted square indices \(7r+c\) and standard alternating signs. The empty simplex is included in the **augmented** chain complex so that all groups are reduced. The result concerns integral simplicial homology, not a claim about the entire homotopy type.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive simplex enumeration.** For each cardinality \(k=0,1,\ldots,7\), choose an increasing ordered set of \(k\) rows, then an injective \(k\)-tuple of columns, retaining a placement precisely when the two diagonal-residue sets also have cardinality \(k\). This covers every nonattacking placement exactly once. In a second independent method form the graph on 49 squares with an edge between compatible squares and enumerate all cliques recursively in increasing order. The two resulting face sets agree exactly, with cardinalities \((1,49,588,2156,2450,882,196,28)\), including the empty simplex.

**2. Exact integral chain reduction.** In the augmented simplicial chain complex let \(C_{k-1}\) be freely generated by the \(k\)-element faces. In these bases \(\partial_{k-1}(v_1<\cdots<v_k)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}(v_1<\cdots<\widehat v_i<\cdots<v_k)\). For each descending boundary, repeatedly select a currently nonzero coefficient \(u=\pm1\) at row \(r\) and column \(c\), and replace every other column \(j\) by \(j-(a_{rj}/u)c\); then delete pivot row and column. This is a unimodular elementary splitting of a contractible two-term direct summand and preserves all integral homology groups. The verifier chooses the pivot minimizing the exact tuple comprising fill-in cost, total support, row, column, making the calculation reproducible.

The unit cancellation counts at original face cardinalities \(7,6,5,4,3,2,1\) are respectively \((28,168,714,1615,540,48,1)\). At cardinality four, after the 1,615 unit cancellations, the remaining matrix has 541 rows and 121 columns. Its 84 nonzero columns are all rational multiples of one reference column, verified by exact cross-multiplication on all 541 rows. Every multiplier is an integer, and their greatest common divisor is one. The reference column's entries have greatest common divisor nine; every entry of the whole remaining matrix is divisible by nine, and their greatest common divisor is nine. Thus its rank is exactly one and its *unique* nonzero Smith invariant is nine.

No other nonunit boundary survives. Descending cancellation of the 540 unit pairs at cardinality three leaves exactly one row-space generator for the degree-two homology target; the residual primitive boundary vector has coefficient \(\pm1\) on that survivor, and its lower boundary is checked to be zero. The final reduced chain complex has only \(C_3\cong\mathbb Z^{121}\), \(C_2\cong\mathbb Z\), with \(\partial_3\) of rank one and Smith invariant nine. Consequently \(\ker\partial_3\cong\mathbb Z^{120}\) and \(\operatorname{coker}\partial_3\cong\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\), proving the claim.

**3. Independent checks.** The second face enumerator constructs original boundary matrices independently of the integral reduction. Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5\) yields ranks, in ascending cardinality order, respectively \((1,48,540,1616,714,168,28)\), \((1,48,540,1615,714,168,28)\), and \((1,48,540,1616,714,168,28)\). The single rank drop in characteristic three independently detects 3-primary torsion, while the **integral** greatest-common-divisor certificate distinguishes order nine from order three. The reduced Euler characteristic computed from the face vector is \(-120\), matching the free degree-three rank.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3.10 or later, with no third-party packages. `verification_output.txt` contains the complete expected standard output. The program builds all 6,350 simplices, checks closure, compares two independent enumerations, performs unit-pivot integer chain cancellations, certifies the rank-one Smith invariant by exact divisibility and gcd calculations, checks the residual chain identity, and independently computes boundary ranks modulo 2, 3, and 5. It terminates in `ALL_CHECKS_PASS` only after every assertion succeeds. The verification is a finite complete calculation on the specified 49-vertex complex; it does not extrapolate to larger boards.

## Relationship to prior work
Cairns (2001) formalizes toroidal queen placement, including non-square boards; its main theorem counts maximum placements for non-square tori and does not determine homology of the square-board placement complex. Shareshian and Wachs (2004) determine torsion for rook-placement chessboard complexes; these permit diagonal attacks and therefore are strictly larger complexes, so their homology theorems do not imply this result. Morrison and Speeter (2023) analyze toroidal queen attack graphs through gonality and independence numbers, not the homology of the *entire* independence complex. Sabour (2025) studies a toroidal queen simplicial model and associated sheaf/cohomological obstructions, but does not give this ordinary integral homology calculation. Limonchenko and Yu (2026) treat generalized chessboard complexes based on row/column capacity constraints, not toroidal diagonal exclusions.

## Limitations
This classification is for exactly the seven-by-seven periodic queen attack graph; it makes no claim for other board sizes, for ordinary nonperiodic queens, or for equivariant or full homotopy type. Exact computer-assisted enumeration provides a mathematical finite certificate but remains subject to implementation review. The available literature comparison does not establish definitive publication priority; equivalent unindexed work is possible. Independent verification by another investigator has not been performed.
