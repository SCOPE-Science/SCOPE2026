# No nonsymmetric numerical semigroup of genus at most 27 has a symmetric ideal square

## Finding
Let \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be an additive numerical semigroup containing zero. Its *ideal square* \(S^{\langle2\rangle}\) is the additive semigroup generated by products \(uv\) with \(u,v\in S\setminus\{0\}\). Let \(g(S)=|\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\setminus S|\), and let \(F(S)\) denote the largest gap, with \(F(\mathbb Z_{\ge0})=-1\). Symmetric means \(2g(S)=F(S)+1\).

**Theorem (verified exhaustive cutoff).** For every numerical semigroup \(S\) with \(g(S)\le 27\), the implication
\[
 S^{\langle2\rangle}\text{ symmetric}\ \Longrightarrow\ S\text{ symmetric}
\]
holds. Exactly \(3,220,696\) numerical semigroups were exhausted over the genera \(0,\ldots,27\). Hence the least genus \(\mu\) of a nonsymmetric numerical semigroup with symmetric ideal square satisfies \(\mu\ge 28\). The example \(\langle12,18,20,39,67\rangle\) from Chen–Hyde–Laurens–Piermarini–Simons has \(g=41\) and a symmetric ideal square, so \(28\le\mu\le41\).

## Assumptions and scope
The domain is all cofinite additive submonoids of nonnegative integers; no restriction on multiplicity, embedding dimension, or conductor. The square is multiplication of ideals **followed by additive closure**, not the Minkowski sum \(S+S\) (which equals \(S\)). When semigroups are represented as usual with zero adjoined, this is the semigroup generated by all pairwise products of their positive minimal additive generators. Symmetry is checked by the classical genus–Frobenius criterion.

## Proof
The certificate is the self-contained exhaustive C++ enumeration in `verification/census_all.cpp`, together with two partitioned replay runs (`verification/census_upper.cpp` and `verification/census_at28.cpp` when applicable); their exact output is included as text. The relevant reductions justify **exhaustive**, rather than sampled, coverage:

1. Every numerical semigroup other than \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) has a unique parent obtained by adjoining its Frobenius gap. Adjoining that gap preserves additive closure: if it were a sum of two smaller positive elements of the child, closure would already place it in the child. The parent has genus one smaller, and the adjoined element is a minimal additive generator of the parent greater than the parent's Frobenius number. Conversely, deleting any minimal positive generator \(x>F(S)\) yields a numerical semigroup of genus one larger; closure is preserved by minimality. Thus, inductively from \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), the rooted generator-deletion tree generates **every** numerical semigroup of each genus exactly once.
2. For a gap set encoded by an integer bit mask, the positive multiplicity \(m\) is its least positive nongap; \(F\) is its largest gap; and every minimal generator satisfies \(x<F+m+1\). Indeed if \(x\ge F+m+1\), then \(x=m+(x-m)\) with \(x-m>F\), so \(x\) is decomposable. All candidates are independently tested for decomposability. At genera at most \(27\), the bound \(F\le 2g-1\) ensures no gap index reaches the 128-bit limit; the program treats an attempted overflow as a fatal error.
3. If \(u_1,\ldots,u_e\) are the minimal additive generators of \(S\), distributivity shows \(S^{\langle2\rangle}=\langle u_i u_j:1\le i,j\le e\rangle\). Its multiplicity is \(m^2\), where \(m=u_1\). Dijkstra's algorithm on residues modulo \(m^2\), with edges of weights \(u_i u_j\), computes exactly the minimal representative \(w_r\) of each residue in this square (its Apéry set). Its genus and Frobenius number are therefore
\[
 g(S^{\langle2\rangle})=\frac1{m^2}\sum_{r=0}^{m^2-1}w_r-\frac{m^2-1}2,\qquad F(S^{\langle2\rangle})=\max_r w_r-m^2.
\]
The algorithm tests the resulting exact integer equality \(2g=F+1\) for every **nonsymmetric** \(S\) in the enumerated tree. No qualifying instance occurs.
4. The recorded number of nodes at each genus matches the independently published numerical-semigroup enumeration, including \(1,270,267\) at genus \(27\). These counts corroborate the tree enumeration but are not used as a substitute for its generation proof. The full checks were divided into lower-genus and upper-genus runs only to bound individual replay times; the union of ranges covers all nodes and every eligible nonsymmetric node.

Thus every possible nonsymmetric source of genus at most \(27\) has been examined, proving the finite but universal stated implication. The published genus-41 example independently bounds \(\mu\) above.

## Verification
Compiling the C++ program under a standards-conforming C++17 compiler with optimization and replaying the documented commands reconstructs the semigroup tree and the residue shortest paths using integer arithmetic. Separate Python enumeration in `verification/independent_low.py` uses finite sets rather than bit masks and checks genera through 17. The exact logs of the actual split complete runs are included in `verification/low_run.txt`, `verification/high_run.txt`. The genus-41 example was separately checked via the same Apéry formulas, yielding genus 41 and Frobenius 73 for its base, and genus 1950 and Frobenius 3899 for its square. Finite computational exhaustion supports the explicit cutoff; it says nothing about genera larger than \(27\).

## Relationship to prior work
Chen, Hyde, Laurens, Piermarini and Simons, *The Arithmetic of Semirings Part I: Ideals* (first posted 12 July 2026), Section 4.1, explicitly exhibit a nonsymmetric semigroup with a symmetric multiplicative ideal square and discuss the behavior of symmetry under ideal multiplication. Their statement gives an example at genus 41 but no exclusion of smaller genera. Their Corollary 4.3 treats two-generator semigroups, all intrinsically symmetric, and cannot exclude higher-embedding-dimensional sources. NumericalMonoid's published genus counts enumerate objects but make no claim about multiplicative-square symmetry. The present cutoff is a new exhaustive *absence* result, not the previously known existence example and not an exact determination of \(\mu\).

## Limitations
This is a computer-assisted exact finite theorem, conditional only on correct implementation/execution of the supplied transparent exhaustive algorithm and ordinary integer-machine behavior. It does **not** establish an infinite classification or the exact minimum in the remaining range \([28,41]\). Duplicate or unindexed earlier results could affect priority. Two code paths use the same mathematical Apéry principle; the small-genus Python crosscheck is independent but does not independently exhaust the largest genera. This is not independent mathematical auditing.
