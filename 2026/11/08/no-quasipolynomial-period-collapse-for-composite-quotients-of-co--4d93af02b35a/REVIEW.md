# Scientific review: least period of consecutive-generator quotient Frobenius branches

**Correctness (PASS).** Liu's source provides the precise branch formula for every integer \(a>p\); the new proof forces \(I_p(q)<I_p(0)\) for each proper positive divisor \(q\) of every composite \(p\). The cycle of residues at \(r=q\) and a short-index witness enforce this inequality. The infinite-intersection/Chinese-remainder argument then excludes every alternate quasipolynomial period not divisible by \(p\). An independent direct membership test corroborates the premise on finite ranges, but does not replace proof.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** Liu (arXiv:2607.23076) proves a period dividing \(p\), explicitly warns that the least period might be smaller, and establishes distinctness information only for prime moduli. The present claim deliberately concerns **composite** moduli. Comparable quotient studies focus on genus or other formulas, and semantic searches of relevant published records uncovered no stronger implication. General unindexed or incompletely inspected literature remains a priority risk; searches do not prove absolute uniqueness.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** Exact primitive quasipolynomial period is a natural invariant left open by a recent residue-class formula. Showing uniformly that *all* composite moduli resist period collapse closes a genuine structural gap. The proof also explains the obstruction with a divisor-residue cycle.

**Limitations.** Only consecutive generators, fixed composite modulus and integer \(a>p\) are treated. Earlier broad quotient literature was not exhaustively inspected; independent review is outstanding.
