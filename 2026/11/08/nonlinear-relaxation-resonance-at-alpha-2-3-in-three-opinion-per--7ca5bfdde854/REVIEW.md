# Scientific review: three-opinion persuasion–polarization relaxation

## Correctness — PASS
The original mean-field equation reduces exactly to a three-coordinate mass-conserving system. The relative-entropy dissipation identity and exclusion of all three vertex limits establish convergence to the unique interior coexistence state from every nonvertex initial state. The exact normal coordinates separate symmetric and antisymmetric modes. Variation-of-constants, including separate remainder estimates on each side of the rate crossing, proves the three sharp long-time formulas and the unique critical parameter \(\alpha=2/3\). The coefficient of the faster-mode limit above resonance is permitted to vanish, as stated. Exact-rational and numerical computations supplement this analytical proof rather than being taken as exhaustive proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty
Cao and Reed's original arXiv:2408.00148v1 is the source of the IPP equation and explicitly analyzes \(\alpha\in\{0,1/2,1\}\). Its accessible full version does not establish the joint arbitrary-interior-parameter coexistence selection and \(\alpha=2/3\) nonlinear rate resonance. Classical detailed-balance Lyapunov theory (Horn–Jackson, 1972) already covers the general entropy mechanism and is credited. Mobilia (2011) uses different constrained-voter reactions and a continuum of polarized equilibria. An independent result search under equation aliases, symmetry reductions, and parameter-crossing descriptions did not locate a dominating claim; 106 accessible prior claims were compared for overlap. The complete later 2025 SIAM publication was not accessible, so stronger later coverage remains a material priority risk. No uniqueness of the mathematical result is certified by a search failure.

## Value — PASS
This resolves a well-motivated rate-selection question for the minimal nontrivial three-opinion persuasion–polarization model. The key structural result is that the middle share can be slaved to the square of the extreme-opinion imbalance rather than to its own linear mode. The sharp \(2\lambda=\beta\) crossing produces a critical \(t e^{-\beta t}\) relaxation law and universal late undershoot for initially asymmetric data below and at the crossing. Those consequences are not supplied by general convergence alone.
