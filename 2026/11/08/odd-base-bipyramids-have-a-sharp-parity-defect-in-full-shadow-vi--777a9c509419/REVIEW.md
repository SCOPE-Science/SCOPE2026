# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact result follows from a fully specified affine reduction of orthogonal projection to the planar convex hull of a regular polygon and two antipodal Cauchy-distributed points. The derived point-density integrates to one. The all-vertex event is proved equivalent to exactly one violated base support inequality for each antipodal point; the relevant polygon edges are nonadjacent for odd base order at least five. The nearest and second-nearest normal angles yield an exhaustive piecewise radial interval. The angular integral has a checked elementary arcsine primitive, and the expansion through cubic order produces the \(n^{-2}\) limit. The derivative sign of the radial Cauchy kernel proves the global-maximizer bracket and hence convergence of maximizers without an unjustified finite-order uniqueness assertion. The standalone checker reproduces the formula by independently integrated radial probabilities and explicit convex hulls. Critical limits are analytic, not inferred from simulations.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
The nearest high-relevance prior geometric result is the known full-vertex law for a **single** regular-polygonal pyramid. Its even-bipyramid corollary is explicitly excluded. The odd two-apex joint event is not determined by the single-apex marginal: opposite facets are offset by half an angular cell, yielding a different exact formula and a sharp halving of the asymptotic coefficient. Black and Criado provide general expected-shadow bounds, not the exact odd-bipyramid all-vertex law. Search comparisons included aliases for dipyramids, central symmetric added apex points, stereological projection and silhouette probabilities, and the closest indexed research-record titles. No exact dominating comparison was located; this absence is not a proof of global priority. Historical equivalent angular calculations remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Full-silhouette probability is a natural combinatorial observable of projected convex polytopes. The parity change from redundant to nonredundant visibility of the second apex is structural, not a mere change of numerical constants. An exact odd-order law, a sharp probability/height asymptotic and the factor-two obstruction are needed to distinguish one-apex and two-apex random shadows. The previously established even-order law is not counted as new.

## Closest literature and limits
Black–Criado (arXiv:2406.06936, 2024) analyzes bounds for mean random-shadow complexity; a related exact one-apex regular-pyramid computation already covers even base counts. The odd-bipyramid claim may have unrecognized prior equivalents in classical polyhedral integral geometry. Finite-order uniqueness of the optimal height is not established.
