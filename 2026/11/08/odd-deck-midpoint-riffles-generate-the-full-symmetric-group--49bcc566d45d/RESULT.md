# Odd-deck midpoint riffles generate the full symmetric group

## Finding

For every odd integer \(n\ge3\), let \(d_k\) be the deterministic midpoint-interleaving permutation associated with the cut \(k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}\), as defined below. Then

\[
\left\langle d_1,d_{n-1}\right\rangle=
\begin{cases}
\mathfrak S_n,&n\equiv3\pmod4,\\
\mathfrak A_n,&n\equiv1\pmod4.
\end{cases}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{\langle d_1,d_2,d_{n-1}\rangle=\mathfrak S_n\quad\text{for every odd }n\ge3.}
\]

Consequently, the unglued midpoint-interpolation shuffle with independent cuts \(K\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,1/2)\) is irreducible and aperiodic on \(\mathfrak S_n\), and its law from every initial ordering converges in total variation to uniform measure. This is qualitative convergence; no polynomial mixing-time bound is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope

The cards have distinct labels and deck positions are \(1,\ldots,n\). Given a cut \(k\) with \(0<k<n\), the left packet comprises input positions \(1,\ldots,k\), the right packet \(k+1,\ldots,n\). Give the \(i\)-th left card key \((2i-1)/(2k)\) and the \(j\)-th right card key \((2j-1)/(2(n-k))\). Merge the packets in increasing key order, preserving order within each packet. Write \(d_k\) as the output list of input-position labels (that is, output position maps to input position), and set \(d_0=d_n\) to the identity. Equal midpoint keys cannot occur when \(n\) is odd.

Every cut has strictly positive binomial probability. Fresh cuts at successive steps produce independent, label-blind permutation increments. Multiplication conventions are immaterial to the generated subgroup, provided they are fixed consistently.

## Proof

**No ties.** If two midpoint keys coincide, then \((2i-1)(n-k)=(2j-1)k\). With \(g=\gcd(k,n-k)\), both \(k/g\) and \((n-k)/g\) must be odd, whence their sum \(n/g\) is even. This contradicts odd \(n\).

**The two peripheral cuts.** Put \(n=2a+1\) and \(p=a+1\). Direct midpoint comparison gives the one-line output permutations

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_1&=[2,3,\ldots,a+1,1,a+2,\ldots,2a+1],\\
d_{n-1}&=[1,2,\ldots,a,2a+1,a+1,a+2,\ldots,2a].
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(A=d_1\) is an \((a+1)\)-cycle on \(S=\{1,\ldots,a+1\}\), and \(B=d_{n-1}\) is an \((a+1)\)-cycle on \(T=\{a+1,\ldots,2a+1\}\); both fix the complement of their supports. The intersection of their supports is exactly \(\{p\}\).

**Elementary generation lemma.** If cycles \(A\) and \(B\) have supports \(S\) and \(T\), respectively, with \(S\cap T=\{p\}\) and both support sizes at least two, their subgroup contains the alternating group on \(S\cup T\). Indeed, for any \(s\in S\setminus\{p\}\) and \(t\in T\setminus\{p\}\), choose nonidentity powers \(A^i\) and \(B^j\) whose **inverses** take \(p\) to the indicated points. Their commutator is a three-cycle supported on \(\{p,s,t\}\). As the powers vary, all such mixed three-cycles and their inverses occur. Fixing \(t\) and conjugating one mixed three-cycle by another produces all \((p,s_1,s_2)\) with distinct \(s_1,s_2\in S\setminus\{p\}\); fixing \(s\) similarly produces all \((p,t_1,t_2)\) with distinct \(t_1,t_2\in T\setminus\{p\}\). Together with mixed three-cycles these include every three-cycle containing \(p\). Those three-cycles generate the full alternating group: a three-cycle disjoint from \(p\) is a product of two three-cycles containing \(p\). This proves the lemma, including the three-point boundary case.

Applying the lemma yields \(\mathfrak A_n\le\langle A,B\rangle\). Both generating cycles have sign \((-1)^a\). If \(n\equiv3\pmod4\), then \(a\) is odd and \(A\) is odd, so the group is \(\mathfrak S_n\). If \(n\equiv1\pmod4\), then \(a\) is even, both generators are even, and the group equals \(\mathfrak A_n\).

**An odd third cut.** In the remaining case write \(n=4t+1\), with \(t\ge1\). At cut \(k=2\), the left midpoint keys are \(1/4\) and \(3/4\), while the right packet has \(4t-1\) cards. Exactly \(t\) right-packet cards precede the first left card, and exactly \(3t-1\) precede the second. These are precisely the cross-packet inversions, giving \(4t-1\) inversions, an odd integer. Hence \(d_2\) is odd and \(\langle d_1,d_2,d_{n-1}\rangle=\mathfrak S_n\). For \(n\equiv3\pmod4\) the first and last cuts already suffice.

**Convergence of the random walk.** All selected generators occur with positive probability because \(\Pr(K=k)=2^{-n}\binom nk>0\). In a finite permutation group, the semigroup generated by positive-probability increments equals the group generated by them (an inverse is a positive power). The chain is therefore irreducible. Since \(K=0\) and \(K=n\) act as the identity with positive probability, it is also aperiodic. Every permutation increment is a bijection, so the transition matrix is doubly stochastic and uniform measure is stationary. The finite irreducible aperiodic Markov-chain convergence theorem gives total-variation convergence.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` enumerates all relevant midpoint keys using exact rational arithmetic for odd deck sizes from \(3\) through \(63\), checks both peripheral-cut formulas and the second-cut parity, tests the mixed three-cycle commutator support, and exhaustively enumerates the generated subgroups at \(n=3,5,7\). The observed test count is 31,378. Reproduce with `python verify.py` using the Python standard library. These finite checks test the definitions and algebra but are not a substitute for the all-order proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Jiang's unglued midpoint-interpolation model is exactly the present sampling rule. Its main mixing bounds for the unglued chain treat even decks; Section 13.3 explicitly says that no upper theorem is claimed for odd decks. The result here gives the missing qualitative reachability and convergence, not the quantitative polynomial bound.

The older theory of groups generated by cycles, including work of Bagiński and Gromadzki, provides closely related general algebraic background. The generation lemma is proved directly here and is not presented as a new group-theory principle. The claimed contribution is identifying three **actual positive-probability midpoint cuts**, determining the exact two-cut subgroup for every odd size, and translating that to ergodicity of this cut-dependent shuffle.

Xia, Zhang, Zhang and Zhu classify groups of equal-pile perfect shuffles. Their hypotheses specify equal pile sizes and therefore do not directly cover the variable and usually unequal binomial cuts above. Sellke, Shi and Wang establish cutoff under arbitrary cut-size laws but **uniformly random interleavings conditional on each cut**, rather than the deterministic midpoint interleaver. Those results likewise do not provide the asserted odd-midpoint reachability statement.

## Limitations

This is a qualitative all-odd-deck classification. No polynomial or sharp large-deck mixing time, cutoff, spectral-gap bound, or extension to other midpoint conventions is established. The algebraic lemma has classical antecedents; the model-specific theorem has not received independent historical-priority or mathematical verification. The 2017 two-cycle paper was available by abstract in the literature comparison; its detailed text was not relied upon.
