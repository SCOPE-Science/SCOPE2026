# Scientific review: odd-power margins of cyclic monomial inverse semigroups

## Correctness — PASS

The proof is a complete argument for all odd \(n\ge3\) and \(m\ge1\). A rank-one matrix-unit multiplier detects every nonzero support position. A two-arrow transposition multiplier compares the two \(n=2k+1\) power coefficients and forces all relative phase labels to agree. Common scalar phases disappear from \(n\)-th powers and define a congruence. The inverse-semigroup marginal condition is then resolved by the published idempotent-centralization lemma; conjugation and orbit counting are direct. The 49-element even-boundary check is separately proved by cases. Exact finite tests checked all multipliers in four odd small instances and the even counterexample. The proof, not finite enumeration, establishes the unlimited parameters.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk

The 2025 motivating paper gives definitions and structural lemmas but explicitly asks whether the power-word marginal equivalence is a congruence; it does not classify the specific cyclic monomial inverse family. Brookes (2020) classifies congruences on a broader partial wreath product, so the existence of the particular scalar congruence is within prior machinery; it does not identify the power-word marginal equivalence with this congruence, prove the odd-exponent rigidity, or give the even counterexample. Searches under power-word marginal congruence, cyclic partial monomial matrices, scalar/projective rook quotient and partial wreath-product terminology identified no equivalent statement. Residual risk: the power-word identification may have appeared in older sources under a different vocabulary, and the full literature cannot be certified exhaustively.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem resolves a natural, explicit class of the published marginal-congruence problem, rather than simply counting a chosen arbitrary subset. It identifies exactly what information power words erase, supplies a complete quotient structure and closed count in every dimension, determines the marginal inverse subsemigroup, and demonstrates that oddness is a genuine mathematical boundary. These are structural invariants of a standard family of inverse monoids, with the distinction between odd and even exponents furnishing a motivating limitation.

## Closest sources and limits

Araújo–Araújo–Kinyon (2025), §§1–2,5, supplies the definition, the centralization lemma and Problem 11, but not the asserted explicit family. Brookes (2020), §§2–3, provides a general partial-wreath-product congruence classification; the additional exact power-word identification is the new assertion. The result does not handle all inverse semigroups, all finite decorating groups, or every even exponent. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
