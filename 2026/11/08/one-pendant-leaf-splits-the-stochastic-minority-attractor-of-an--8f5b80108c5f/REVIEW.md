# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The asserted states are exactly the two closed sets obtained by fixing the hub and pendant leaf to opposite bits and requiring the clique's number of ones to be one of the two middle values. A constructive path from every initial configuration first sets the leaf opposite the hub and then adjusts ordinary clique vertices; closure and irreducibility follow from the adjacent-level Boolean-lattice inclusion graph. The proof identifies the exact per-edge transition probability and derives the full Laplacian spectrum through a verified up/down incidence commutator. The published odd-clique baseline is used only for comparison. Exhaustive terminal-component tests for the first four sizes and exact symbolic spectrum tests for the first three sizes agree; the proof establishes all sizes. Remaining risk: as with any theoretical proof, unrecognized algebraic errors are possible pending independent review.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The question is motivated by the published single-class attractor of odd cliques and asks whether a one-edge pendant perturbation preserves recurrence properties. It produces a sharp and counterintuitive separation: total recurrent population stays fixed, but the one class fractures into two and a designated spin freezes permanently; meanwhile the exact within-class relaxation gap remains of order inverse graph size. This is an infinite-family structural result, not a recomputation of a known table or an arbitrary finite sample. Open aspects include absorption-time laws and extensions to several leaves.
