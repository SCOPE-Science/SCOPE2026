# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For the divisor set \(\{1,p,q,pq\}\), the four inequalities on forbidden prime supports are precisely the complete Hall constraints. Each primitive four-term progression over a prime \(r\ge5\) has at most one root among the four indices, with each location occurring with local probability \(1/(r+1)\). Inclusion-exclusion computes the exact occupancy probabilities. CRT independence and Möbius inversion give the stated coprime-parameter mean, while the two largest local bases determine the exact exponential constant \(24\). The executable test checks all 256 abstract Hall configurations, all 1,327,104 allowed residue quadruples for the first two-progression case, and additional independent exact finite recurrences. The finite tests are not an infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS with residual priority uncertainty.** Alekseyev's single-progression theorem and McNew–Pomerance's original matchability results do not assert one **identical** bijection working for independently specified progressions; quantifying over distinct progressions changes the mathematical requirement. The present result is not the separate-matching multi-divisor density for a single shared progression. The explicit prime-pair examples show that the interchangeable-quantifier conclusion is false. Searches for common perfect matchings of divisor sets and simultaneously primitive progressions, equivalent CRT conditions, and the explicit all-order density found no dominating statement. The reduction to Hall's theorem is standard and is not claimed as original. Priority in inaccessible or unindexed sources cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The four-divisor semiprime case is the first nontrivial two-prime divisor matching structure; simultaneous reuse of one matching across several progressions is natural, yet separate existence gives no solution. The new full Hall classification, positive but exponentially decaying all-order success density, explicit covariance-like obstruction at two progressions, and asymptotic constant \(24\) settle a mathematically natural parameter family rather than a hand-picked numerical instance. It does not resolve the analogous many-prime divisor problem.
