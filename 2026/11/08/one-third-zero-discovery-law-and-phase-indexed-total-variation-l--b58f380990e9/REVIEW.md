# Scientific review: dyadic doubling-window discovery

**Correctness (PASS).** The independent Poisson-clock decomposition, exact cyclotomic factorization and finite telescoping product yield the one-third zero-event limit. A coupling between the fixed count window and a deterministic-time Poisson window has an explicit \(O(n^{-1/3})\) error by clock concentration and a \(5/u\) first-hit intensity bound; the missing Bernoulli-series tail has mean \(e^{-n}\). A standard dyadic uniform bound gives the expectation rate. The bundled checker passed 120 grouped exact algebra/rational and numerical comparisons, used as supporting checks rather than a substitute for the infinite proof.

**Originality (PASS, historically qualified).** Bogachev–Gnedin–Yakubovich (2008), Example 2.2, already prove the Poissonized expectation \(1-e^{-t}\), which is not claimed as new. Derbazi–Gnedin–Marynych (2023) explicitly exclude geometric frequencies from their Poisson record theorem. Barbour–Gnedin (2009) analyze single-time small occupancy counts, not the first-hit void product. Targeted searches found no equivalent product or total-variation phase law. Older unindexed dyadic-urn references remain a plausible historical-priority risk; no independent novelty certification is claimed.

**Value (PASS).** The natural window from \(n\) to \(2n\) has expected one new category but an exact limiting probability \(1/3\) of none, contradicting the Poisson(1) benchmark. The theorem gives a quantitative, full phase-indexed distributional approximation in the bounded-small-count regime omitted by the main 2023 record theorem.

**Scope and limitations.** The constant and proof use \(2^{-j}\) and window ratio two; the TV error rate is not proved sharp. Nothing is asserted for arbitrary sampling probabilities or independently verified priority.
