# Mathematical review: one-token mobility of paired domination

## Correctness — PASS
The lower bound counts how many ground vertices can be met by one matched pair of selected line-graph vertices; equality at \(n=3m+1\) forces disjoint centered three-vertex paths and one omitted vertex. The subsequent move classification checks all insertions after deletion of either path edge and keeps distinct the incident (slide) from disjoint (jump) option. The bouquet-of-squares position graph is connected, and swaps on its edges generate all permutations of ground labels, proving connectivity without relying on finite testing. Each state has \(4m\) neighbors; direct local triangle classification produces two families of \(m\) triangles through each state. Both verification programs passed. No infinite-family conclusion is inferred from the finite counts alone.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Haynes–Slater (1998) defines paired domination; Dutton–Brigham (1999) supplies general domination/line-graph relationships. Mynhardt–Teshima (arXiv 2017) defines paired-domination slide reconfiguration and proves general realizability, but its inspected relevant section does not classify \(T_{3m+1}\). Studer–Haynes–Lawson (2000) concerns induced-paired domination and was only partially inspected owing to scanned text. No direct coverage of this one-token-slide census was found in the inspected scientific records or targeted bibliographic searches.

Crucially, an earlier statement on maximum induced matchings in triangular graphs already supplies the disjoint-triple enumeration and analyzes **whole-edge** replacements of an induced matching, each changing two selected vertices. This is accepted as prior coverage of the state count. Connectivity for those larger moves does not establish connectivity for one-selected-vertex slides. The present proof independently establishes the stricter move graph's \(4m\)-regular connectedness and exact triangle distribution. The very general prior graph-reconfiguration results, count sequences, and different domination variants are not implication-equivalent to the claim. Unindexed or unread historical material is a residual priority risk, not evidence of coverage.

## Value — PASS
Minimum paired-dominating configurations model guard positions with adjacent backups. A single moved guard must not break feasibility, a stricter operational requirement than replacing an entire matched backup pair. The theorem completely describes the optimal configurations' feasible local mobility in a natural infinite congruence family, identifies its transitive connected exchange mechanism, and enumerates both the edges and the two kinds of local three-step alternatives. This is a structural reconfiguration result rather than merely a repeat of a known optimum or count.
