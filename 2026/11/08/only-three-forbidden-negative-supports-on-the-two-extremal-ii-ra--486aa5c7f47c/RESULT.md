# Only three forbidden negative supports on the two extremal \(II^*\) rational elliptic surfaces

## Finding
Let \(X_{22}\) and \(X_{211}\) denote the smooth extremal rational elliptic surfaces over \(\mathbb C\) with respective singular fibers \(II^*+II\) and \(II^*+I_1+I_1\). For either surface, precisely three of the \(2^{10}=1024\) subsets of negative irreducible curves fail to have negative-definite intersection matrix. Consequently the Zariski-chamber polynomial by support cardinality, including the nef chamber, is
\[
Z_X(t)=(1+t)^{10}-2t^9-t^{10}
=1+10t+45t^2+120t^3+210t^4+252t^5+210t^6+120t^7+45t^8+8t^9.
\]
In particular, \(Z_X(1)=1021\); the maximal negative support has nine curves and occurs in eight chambers. Although the two surfaces have different additional fiber configurations and different modular behavior, they have the same negative-support stratification.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). The surfaces are the unique relatively minimal rational Jacobian elliptic surfaces with the stated fiber configurations in Miranda--Persson's classification (Theorems 4.1 and 5.4). A negative curve means a reduced irreducible curve of negative self-intersection. A chamber support is the set of negative irreducible components appearing in the negative part of the Zariski decomposition of a big real divisor. Its size is the number of components; the nef chamber has empty support. No arbitrary additional blow-ups or other extremal fiber types are included.

Label the nine components of the \(II^*\) fiber by \(F_0,\dots,F_8\). Their intersection graph has edges \((0,1),(0,2),(2,3),(0,4),(4,5),(5,6),(6,7),(7,8)\), all fiber component squares \(-2\), and the unique positive fiber multiplicity vector
\[
(6,3,4,2,5,4,3,2,1).
\]
Label the zero section \(S=F_9\); its square is \(-1\) and it meets exactly \(F_8\), transversely in one point. Thus there is one further edge \((8,9)\). The short arm means \(F_0-F_2-F_3\); its multiplicity-two terminal component is \(F_3\).

## Proof
**Classification of negative curves.** Let \(F\) be the nef elliptic fiber class. For each irreducible curve \(C\) with \(C^2<0\), the canonical identity \(K_X=-F\) and adjunction give
\[
C^2=2p_a(C)-2+F\cdot C.
\]
If \(C\) is not vertical then \(F\cdot C\ge1\) and \(p_a(C)\ge0\). Negativity forces \(p_a(C)=0\), \(F\cdot C=1\), and \(C^2=-1\); hence \(C\) is a section. The two classified surfaces have Mordell--Weil group of order one (Miranda--Persson, Theorem 4.1), so this is the unique zero section \(S\). A vertical negative irreducible curve must be a component of a reducible singular fiber. The only such fiber is \(II^*\), whose nine components are \(-2\)-curves. Thus the listed ten curves are exhaustive.

**Supports without the section.** The intersection form of a Kodaira fiber on its components is negative semidefinite, with one-dimensional radical generated by the positive multiplicity vector displayed above (Zariski's lemma). Therefore the full nine-component support is not negative definite. Every proper principal submatrix is negative definite: a nonzero radical vector supported on a proper subset would contradict positivity of all coordinates of the unique radical generator. All of the other \(2^9-1\) subsets qualify.

**Supports containing the section.** Put \(B=-((C_i\cdot C_j))\), so the fiber diagonal entries are \(2\), the section diagonal entry is \(1\), and each graph edge contributes off-diagonal entries \(-1\). If all nine fiber components are included, \(B\) has a singular fiber principal submatrix, hence is not positive definite. If precisely \(F_i\) is excluded, denote by \(A_i\) the positive-definite negative fiber intersection matrix on the remaining eight fiber components. For \(i\ne8\), the Schur complement of \(A_i\) in \(B\) is \(1-(A_i^{-1})_{88}\); for \(i=8\) the section is isolated and the complement is \(1\). Direct exact elimination along the three arms gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
i&0&1&2&3&4&5&6&7&8\\\hline
(A_i^{-1})_{88}&5/6&8/9&7/8&1&4/5&3/4&2/3&1/2&0
\end{array}.
\]
The case \(i=3\) is therefore singular, and all eight other nine-curve supports containing the section are negative definite. Any support containing the section but omitting at least two fiber components lies in a nine-curve support obtained by omitting one fiber component other than \(F_3\). It is consequently negative definite because a principal submatrix of a positive-definite matrix is positive definite. This proves there are exactly three forbidden subsets:
\[
\{F_0,\ldots,F_8\},\qquad
\{S,F_0,F_1,F_2,F_4,F_5,F_6,F_7,F_8\},\qquad
\{S,F_0,\ldots,F_8\}.
\]

**Chambers.** The negative-support correspondence for Zariski chambers on smooth projective surfaces (Bauer--Funke--Neumann, Proposition 1.1) gives one chamber for each negative-definite support, including the empty support. All \(1024\) subsets are allowed except the two of cardinality nine and the one of cardinality ten. Subtracting those monomials from the binomial generating function establishes the stated polynomial and count.

## Verification
The standalone program `artifacts/verify.py` builds the actual ten-by-ten symmetric integer intersection matrix from the stated graph. It checks the affine fiber multiplicity kernel, then independently tests all \(1024\) principal submatrices for positive definiteness by (i) exact Bareiss principal minors and (ii) exact rational \(LDL^T\) elimination. It asserts agreement of these methods, identifies the three excluded supports, and verifies every coefficient and the total. Execute with `python3 artifacts/verify.py`; the recorded output is `artifacts/verification.log`. Computation exhausts the finite ten-curve support problem; the proof of geometric exhaustiveness is separate and theoretical.

## Relationship to prior work
Miranda and Persson (1986), Theorems 4.1 and 5.4, classify the two surfaces and their unique sections, but give no Zariski-chamber polynomial. Bauer, Funke and Neumann (2009), Proposition 1.1, give the general negative-definite-support correspondence, and Bauer and Schmitz (2012) analyze chamber counting for other surfaces. The new assertion is the complete explicit three-exception support classification for these particular \(II^*\) elliptic surfaces; neither the general correspondence nor the fiber table contains that computation. The two surfaces share the \(\widetilde E_8\) configuration while their other singular fibers differ.

## Limitations
The computation applies to the named surfaces over \(\mathbb C\), not to every rational elliptic surface, positive-characteristic reduction, or additional blow-ups. It counts Zariski chambers, not Mori chambers. No independent mathematical audit has yet been performed. Historical priority is provisional: a specialized older chamber census or equivalent affine-root-subset enumeration may not have been indexed by the searches performed.
