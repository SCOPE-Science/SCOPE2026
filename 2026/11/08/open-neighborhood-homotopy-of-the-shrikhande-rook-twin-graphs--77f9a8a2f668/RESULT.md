# Open-neighborhood homotopy of the Shrikhande–rook twin graphs

## Finding
Let \(S\) be the Shrikhande graph and \(R=K_4\square K_4\) the four-by-four rook graph. These are the two nonisomorphic strongly regular graphs with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\). For the **ordinary open** Lovász neighborhood complex \(\mathcal N(G)\), both have precisely the same integral homotopy type:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal N(S)\simeq\mathcal N(R)\simeq\mathbb{RP}^{2}\vee\bigvee^{55}S^{2}.}
\]
In particular, both have fundamental group \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), reduced integral first homology \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), reduced integral second homology \(\mathbb Z^{55}\), and no other nonzero reduced homology groups. This equality contrasts with known distinctions between the two graphs' ordinary independence-complex and **closed-neighborhood** Čech homotopy types.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are simple, finite and without loops. Set \(V=(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\), ordered as \((i,j)\mapsto4i+j\). In \(S\), vertices are adjacent if their difference is in \(\{\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1),\pm(1,1)\}\). In \(R\), two distinct vertices are adjacent if they agree in at least one coordinate. Both graphs have degree six, and every pair of distinct vertices has precisely two common neighbors. Their induced vertex neighborhoods are a six-cycle and two disjoint triangles, respectively, confirming nonisomorphism.

Here \(\mathcal N(G)\) consists exactly of vertex sets \(F\subseteq V\) having some **external common adjacent vertex**: \(F\in\mathcal N(G)\) if and only if there is \(v\notin F\) such that every vertex of \(F\) is adjacent to \(v\). The empty face is included conventionally. Equivalently it is generated by the sixteen simplices on the **open** neighborhoods \(N_G(v)\). Closed neighborhoods \(N_G[v]\), which include their center, generate a different complex.

## Proof
**1. Exhaust the simplicial face posets.** For each graph compute its sixteen open-neighborhood bit masks and generate all their nonempty submasks. Independently test all \(2^{16}-1\) nonempty vertex subsets for containment in some neighborhood. The two procedures produce the same face sets. For each graph the face vector in dimensions zero through five is
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(16,120,320,240,96,16),
\]
so the nonempty face count is \(808\) and the ordinary Euler characteristic is \(56\).

**2. Exhibit an acyclic matching.** Initialize every nonempty face as unmatched. Visit pivot vertices \(v=0,1,\dots,15\). For each pivot, traverse the then-unmatched faces in increasing integer bit-mask order. If \(v\notin F\) and both \(F\) and \(F\cup\{v\}\) remain unmatched, match this covering pair and remove them from the unmatched set. The procedure gives exactly \(375\) disjoint covering pairs in each graph. The remaining critical cells are exactly one zero-cell, one one-cell, and \(56\) two-cells. The certificate constructs the entire directed Hasse diagram (\(2,736\) covering incidences), orienting each matched cover upward and every other cover downward. A complete topological sort removes all \(808\) face nodes in both instances, proving that both matchings are acyclic. Finite acyclic discrete Morse theory gives a CW complex with these critical cells and no others.

**3. Compute the integral attaching degrees.** The \(15\) matched vertex–edge pairs form a spanning tree in the one-skeleton, hence contract to the unique critical vertex. One critical edge remains. The other \(104\) graph edges are paired with two-simplices. Orient each edge from its smaller endpoint to its larger endpoint and each triangle by increasing vertex order. For each matched edge \(e\) paired with triangle \(t\), the oriented boundary \(\partial t=\sum_{j=0}^{2}(-1)^j[t\setminus\{v_j\}]\) has coefficient \(\pm1\) on \(e\); solve this equation recursively to express its surviving critical-edge coefficient in terms of the other two edges. Assign coefficient zero to each tree edge and coefficient one to the critical edge. The acyclic matching guarantees termination, and the explicit checker performs exact integer substitutions on all \(120\) edges.

The \(56\) critical triangles' attaching degrees, after cancellation of the matching pairs, have distributions
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &-2&0&+2\\\hline
S&4&39&13\\
R&9&38&9
\end{array}
\]
(the sign convention depends on cell orientations). Hence in both cases their greatest common divisor is exactly \(2\). The integral cellular boundary \(C_2\longrightarrow C_1\) is therefore a rank-one row reducible by unimodular column operations to \((2,0,\ldots,0)\).

**4. Deduce the homotopy type, not only homology.** A connected CW complex with one vertex, one edge and \(56\) two-cells is obtained from \(S^1\) by maps \(S^1\to S^1\) classified by their integral degrees. Changing the bases of the two-cells by elementary attaching-map slides implements the Euclidean algorithm on those degrees without changing homotopy type. The resulting CW complex has one two-cell attached to \(S^1\) by degree \(2\), and \(55\) two-cells attached nullhomotopically. The degree-two two-cell gives the standard CW model for \(\mathbb{RP}^2\); the others contribute a wedge of \(55\) two-spheres. This proves the displayed equivalences and all indicated integral invariants.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify_neighborhood_twins.py` reconstructs the graphs, checks all strongly regular parameters, distinguishes their local graphs, compares two independent full face enumerations, checks the entire matching and its complete directed-Hasse acyclicity, and evaluates the exact signed Morse boundary coefficient for every remaining critical two-cell. It independently computes the rank \(104\) of the original second boundary over \(\mathbb F_2\) and checks both resulting degree distributions. Run `python3 verify_neighborhood_twins.py`; the accompanying `verification_output.txt` records the actual results, concluding `ALL_CHECKS_PASS`. Finite enumeration proves this **finite graph** statement because the entire finite domain, matching, covering graph and cellular boundary are examined, not merely sampled.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov [1] shows that open neighborhood complexes and \(\operatorname{Hom}(K_2,G)\) have the same simple homotopy type in general, but gives no homotopy calculation for these two strongly regular graphs. Matsushita [2] relates their fundamental groups to graph-theoretic fundamental groups, but does not determine the present integral homotopy types.

The published calculation [3] of \(\mathcal N(K_m\square K_n)\) gives a higher-homology formula in degrees at least three and a quotient by a canonical core, but **explicitly leaves the first and second homology and the total homotopy type unresolved**. Its specialization to \(m=n=4\) does not imply the degree-two attaching map or the \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) fundamental group found here. The earlier comparison [4] uses **closed-neighborhood Čech nerves**, with \(23\) Shrikhande and \(63\) rook two-spheres; it does not cover the open-neighborhood complex. The graph-twin comparison [5] proves distinct **independence-complex** sphere wedges, again with a different condition on simplices. Taken together, these closest results motivate the exact open-neighborhood contrast without entailing it.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to the explicitly defined two sixteen-vertex graphs and to **open** neighborhoods. It does not classify open-neighborhood complexes of general strongly regular graphs or determine equivariant homotopy types under automorphisms. The homotopy equivalences are nonequivariant. The priority search and inspected literature cannot rule out a mathematically equivalent unindexed or unpublished result. There has been no independent external mathematical validation.
