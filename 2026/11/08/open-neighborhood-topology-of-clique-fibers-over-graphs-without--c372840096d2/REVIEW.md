# Scientific review: high-girth clique-fiber open-neighborhood classification

## Correctness — PASS
The statement is for every finite connected simple graph without triangles or four-cycles and every integer \(m\ge4\), including singleton base. The open neighborhood sets are written explicitly as \(F_{v,a}=(R_v\setminus\{(v,a)\})\cup O_{v,a}\). The graph-girth hypotheses guarantee uniqueness for faces using two off-row neighbors, making the first elementary free-face matching legal. After those pairs are removed, any face containing one off-row vertex and at least two row vertices is unique to its center, making the second elementary collapse legal. The residual faces are exactly enumerated in RESULT.md. Per base edge the \(m(m-1)\) cross one-cells are linked by \(2m(m-2)\) two-cell relations whose incidence graph is connected; a unimodular spanning-tree minor cancels \(m(m-1)-1\) excess one-cells, leaving \(m^2-3m+1\) null-attached two-cells. The row boundaries are simply connected for \(m\ge4\), so there are no unaccounted row-loop attaching classes. Local cancellations preserve the row boundaries and do not couple different base edges. This yields the wedge over \(H\) integrally. The exact elementary-collapse count is derived by counting all disjoint pairs, not extrapolated from tests. The included independent finite checker reconstructed 19 original complexes directly from graph adjacency, verified every one of 6,844 free-face collapses, checked all surviving simplices and interface relation connectivity, and calculated reduced mod-two homology. Finite checks alone are not asserted as the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS with bounded-search uncertainty
Searches used aliases including open Lovász neighborhood, graph-complex common-neighbor nerve, Cartesian clique-fiber, girth-five base, and Petersen/branching tree specializations. The closest published cycle-product theorem [3] supplies the case \(H=C_n\), \(n\ge5\), with the same local crown cancellation; it is explicitly not claimed as new. Its theorem is restricted to one cycle, and its full proof does not state a classification for arbitrary branching connected high-girth graphs. The published full high-girth clique-fiber Čech theorem [4] does cover arbitrary base graphs but uses **closed** neighborhoods and produces \(H\vee\bigvee^{e(m-1)^2}S^2\), which differs even in the homology degrees for \(m\ge5\). The graph-join suspension result [5] uses a different graph operation. Matsushita [1] and Shukla [2] provide historical motivation and comparison without a found covering theorem. Residual uncertainty: a folklore or unindexed arbitrary-base extension of the cycle argument might exist, and the older articles [1] and [2] were available in abstract form rather than inspected in complete full text. A negative search is not a priority certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exact integral homotopy classification of a mathematically natural two-factor graph family with arbitrary tree branching and any number of independent base cycles. The result depends only on the vertex and edge counts of the base, yet allows graph bases of arbitrarily high degree; it yields a free fundamental group and explicit higher-dimensional spheres, including mixed-dimensional topology for \(m\ge5\). For instance, the Petersen base and width four give six circles and eighty-five two-spheres, a genuine multiple-cycle case not contained in the prior cycle-only classification. The exact simplicial free-collapse count provides constructive structural control. It is neither an isolated small-graph computation nor a renamed application of the closed-neighborhood theorem.

## Closest literature, scope and residual risks
Primary archive-era entry is Matsushita's open-neighborhood fundamental-group article, first public 2012-10-10, arXiv:1210.2803. Strongest published conceptual overlap is the full cycle-product theorem [3]; strongest similar domain with a different complex is the closed Čech high-girth theorem [4]. The proof requires no triangles or four-cycles in the base and clique width at least four. No equivariant homotopy classification, persistence theorem, or result beyond these bounds is asserted. Full text of the archive-era fundamental-group article and Shukla's degree-restricted article was not material inspected; their available abstracts state no stronger theorem, but this is a documented accessibility limit. The package contains no independent validation.
