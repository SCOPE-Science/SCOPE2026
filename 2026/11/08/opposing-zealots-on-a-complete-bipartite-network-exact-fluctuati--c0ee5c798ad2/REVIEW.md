# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim states an exact domain (both part sizes at least two, two opposed fixed zealots, uniform-neighbor independent continuous-time copying) and an asymptotic regime (both part proportions positive). Lumpability follows from permutation symmetry. Every count state reaches the all-free-zero state; all except one transient corner communicate with it, giving a unique stationary law. First and second stationary generator equations are solved exactly, and ordered-pair multiplicities give the asserted finite variance. The limiting generator is a neutral two-allele Wright–Fisher operator with immigration drift, whose uniquely determined moment sequence is Beta with shapes summing to two. The part disagreement has vanishing stationary second moment, allowing passage from the averaged part fractions to the population fraction. A standalone exact-arithmetic program independently solves 16 finite generators and checks the symbolic moment algebra and twelve beta moments. Risk: the finite program is not an infinite proof, so the self-contained analytic limit argument is essential.

## Value — PASS

The input is a natural and motivated open-system voter model: precisely two persistent opposing sources produce a unique stationary state even where the zealot-free system would absorb at consensus. The theorem gives a complete finite second-moment formula and a nondegenerate limiting *law*, not just expected consensus or a numerical approximation. A concrete qualitative consequence is that increasing population size does not eliminate opinion fluctuations; changing the relative group sizes skews the entire limiting distribution, with an exact variance profile and uniform law at balance. This supplies structural benchmarks for heterogeneous-network opinion models. The symmetric well-mixed analogy is credited, and no downstream universality beyond the specified model is asserted.
