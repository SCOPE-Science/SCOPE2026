# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem fixes all conventions for one-line and cyclic indexing. The weighted partial-injection lemma is proved by contracting forced paths, accounting for all prescribed cycles; in the two-arrow events only the aligned image placement makes two forced loops. Direct differentiation of the normalized Ewens partition function yields the two covariances. Each derivative numerator is a quadratic with positive leading coefficient and negative constant, proving one positive root; evaluations at one prove the ordered thresholds. The positive-root formula proves the claimed asymptotics. Exact independent finite enumeration checks the formulas but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest uniform result is Diaconis–Evans–Graham's complete unweighted linear and circular succession analysis; it does not weight by the cycle count of the original permutation. Reilly–Tanny give ordinary succession counts, not their original-map Ewens cycle covariance. Mansour–Shattuck give an explicit joint enumeration of cycles and *within-cycle* successions and explicitly note that usual line successions have different joint distributions when coupled to other permutation statistics. Ma–Qi–Yeh–Yeh's broader succession/Eulerian generating functions do not explicitly evaluate the claimed \(\theta\)-dependent pair of covariances or the two unique thresholds in the inspected text. The uniform special cases and the standard Ewens normalizer are not claimed as novel. Residual risks include a possible implicit specialization of a broad permutation enumerator and lack of full access to the earliest 1979 article; these were assessed as nondispositive for the explicit original-map weighted phase classification.

## Value — PASS
The count of retained oriented adjacencies is a classical observable for permutation randomization. The number of cycles is the canonical Ewens sufficient statistic. The theorem supplies a qualitative counterexample to the intuition that raising cycle bias necessarily raises retained local structure, and reveals an exact boundary-sensitive sign reversal: a single wraparound site makes the two natural adjacency statistics respond to cycle count in opposite directions at the uniform law. It gives a complete sharp threshold classification for every sample size, not just an isolated numerical gain.

## Limits
The covariances at the uniform point are very small as sample size grows, and the opposite-sign parameter interval shrinks. No full joint law or statistical power guarantee is claimed. Direct independent bibliographic and scientific audit may further refine historical priority.
