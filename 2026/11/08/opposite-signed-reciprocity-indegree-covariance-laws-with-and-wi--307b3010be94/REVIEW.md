# Review

## Correctness — PASS

Conditioning on one reciprocal pair converts two rowwise selection probabilities to \((k-1)/(n-2)\) in the loopless model and \((k-1)/(n-1)\) in the loop-permitted model. The resulting exact endpoint and nonendpoint indegree PGFs simplify to opposite signed binomial second differences. The derivation holds for each integer \(n\ge3\) and every allowable \(k\), including deterministic boundaries, and the fixed-outdegree Poisson-profile limit follows coefficientwise. Exact independent enumerations verify 86 coefficients in 17 finite configurations; these are checks, not the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority qualification

The closest directly matching row-specified model is McKay and Skerman (arXiv:1302.2446), whose results concern degree sequences; reciprocity between distinct vertices is not determined by such a sequence. Zlatić and Štefančić (arXiv:0902.3542) analyze reciprocity under other sampling mechanisms, Hansen and Jaworski's 2008 random mappings address one-out local laws, and Durak et al. (arXiv:1210.5288) impose reciprocal-degree constraints. Searches also considered equivalent descriptions as paired directed 2-cycles, random fixed-row binary matrices and mixed moment/cumulant formulas. None of the inspected relevant claims covers the two all-size signed covariance PGFs. The Arney–Bender 1982 mapping paper was only accessible through bibliographic/abstract material and remains a concrete risk for the one-out subcase. Absence of a detected result is not a proof of absolute priority.

## Value — PASS

The object and covariance are naturally motivated by cross-dependence between mutually reciprocated edges and the in-degree profile. The contribution gives an all-bin exact mixed statistic, an invariant double-zero from fixed total degree, opposite signs at indegree zero under a single structural modeling choice, and an explicit fixed-outdegree Poisson second-difference limit. These features are not implied by knowing each statistic's marginal distribution.

## Scientific limits

No full joint law, functional central limit theorem, or extension to dependent or with-replacement selections is claimed. Historical prior-art access remained incomplete for some older mapping papers. Independent audit has not been performed.
