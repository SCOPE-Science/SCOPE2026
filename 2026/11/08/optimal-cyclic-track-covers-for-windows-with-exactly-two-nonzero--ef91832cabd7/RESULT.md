# Optimal cyclic track covers for windows with exactly two nonzero symbols

## Finding
Fix integers \(q\ge 2\) and \(n\ge 3\). In the alphabet \(\Sigma=\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}\), let \(\mathcal F_{q,n}\) be the length-\(n\) words having exactly two nonzero positions. A *cyclic track* is a nonempty circular word whose every circular length-\(n\) window lies in \(\mathcal F_{q,n}\). A *track cover* is a finite collection of tracks in which each word in \(\mathcal F_{q,n}\) occurs as a window exactly once across all tracks. Tracks are identified up to circular rotation, but not reversal, symbol permutation, or permutation of the positions within windows; collections are unordered.

Set \(s=q-1\), and define
\[
A_s=s^{2s-2}((s-1)!)^{2s},\qquad
B_s=\begin{cases}s^{s-2}((s-1)!)^s,&s\ge2,\\1,&s=1.\end{cases}
\]
Then the minimum number of tracks in a cover is exactly \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Every minimum cover has one track for each shorter circular distance \(d\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}\) between its nonzero positions, and the number of minimum covers is exactly
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_{q,n}=A_s^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}\,B_s^{\mathbf 1_{\{2\mid n\}}}}
\]
For \(d<n/2\), the corresponding track contains exactly \(n s^2\) symbols and has exactly \(A_s\) possible circular realizations. If \(n\) is even, the antipodal class \(d=n/2\) has track length \((n/2)s^2\) and exactly \(B_s\) circular realizations. In particular, a single exact-two-nonzero-window universal cycle exists precisely when \(n=3\); at all longer lengths the optimal number of tracks is greater than one.

Examples: \(\mathcal C_{3,3}=4\), \(\mathcal C_{3,4}=4\), \(\mathcal C_{3,5}=16\), and \(\mathcal C_{4,4}=124416\). In the binary case \(\mathcal C_{2,n}=1\) for every \(n\ge3\), although its unique optimal *collection* uses \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) tracks.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet has one distinguished zero and \(s=q-1\) distinct, named nonzero symbols. All windows overlap in the usual shift-register order. A track is not a concatenation of separate circular sequences: its last \(n-1\) symbols overlap the beginning. Each permitted word is regarded as a distinct directed edge, even when it shares endpoints with another word. Only exact weight two is allowed; neither shorthand representations of weight-two words nor weight ranges are substituted.

## Proof
**1. Invariant classes.** Build the restricted directed de Bruijn multigraph \(G\). Vertices are the length-\(n-1\) prefixes and suffixes of \(\mathcal F_{q,n}\). Each word \(w=(w_1,\ldots,w_n)\) is one edge from its prefix to its suffix. The two nonzero positions in \(w\), viewed on the circle of \(n\) positions, have a shorter circular separation \(d\), with \(1\le d\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). In shifting an admissible window one position, if the departing symbol is zero, the appended symbol must be zero; if it is nonzero, the appended symbol must be nonzero. Thus the two *support positions modulo \(n\)* rotate, and their separation \(d\) is unchanged. Consequently there are no edges of \(G\) between different separation classes.

**2. Forced paths and junction graph.** Fix \(d\). In this class, a length-\(n-1\) vertex with two nonzero symbols has one admissible outgoing edge (append zero). A vertex with one nonzero symbol has \(s\) outgoing edges (append one of the \(s\) nonzero symbols). Call the latter vertices *junctions*, also when \(s=1\) and they have degree one. At each junction, after choosing the new symbol, all subsequent edges up to the next junction are forced. The length of the forced segment is the clockwise separation of consecutive nonzero support positions. If \(d<n/2\), the two gaps \(d\) and \(n-d\) are distinct. Thus the junctions have two types, \(X_a\) and \(Y_a\) for each nonzero color \(a\). Each \(X_a\) has exactly one forced directed segment to \(Y_b\) for every nonzero \(b\), and each \(Y_a\) similarly has one to \(X_b\) for every \(b\). The segments have lengths \(d\) and \(n-d\), respectively (the assignment of these lengths to the two directions depends only on naming the types). Hence suppressing all degree-one internal vertices gives the directed complete bipartite graph with vertex classes \(\{X_a\}\) and \(\{Y_a\}\), every arc present in both directions. It has \(2s\) junctions and \(2s^2\) directed arcs.

If \(n=2d\), the two gaps coincide, so there is only one junction type \(X_a\). The forced segments all have length \(d\), and there is one segment from \(X_a\) to \(X_b\) for each ordered pair \((a,b)\), including \(a=b\). Suppression yields the complete directed graph with loops on \(s\) vertices, with \(s^2\) arcs. Both contracted graphs are strongly connected, including \(s=1\). Suppression or subdivision of the forced segments is a bijection on Euler tours, because the internal vertices have in- and outdegree one. This proves that each separation class has an Euler tour. It also proves that the \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) classes are exactly the strongly connected components of \(G\) on its edge-bearing vertices.

**3. Track lengths and minimum track count.** For \(d<n/2\), the lengths of the \(s^2\) directed segments in each direction are \(d\) and \(n-d\), giving \(n s^2\) distinct window edges. In the antipodal case there are \(s^2\) directed segments of length \(d=n/2\), giving \((n/2)s^2\) edges. Their sum is \(\binom n2 s^2=|\mathcal F_{q,n}|\). A cyclic track corresponds to a closed edge walk, which cannot change separation class. Every class requires at least one track. Each class is Eulerian and therefore can be covered by one track. Thus exactly \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) tracks are necessary and sufficient, with precisely one Euler tour in each class for a minimum cover.

**4. Exact number of tours.** Use the directed BEST theorem with one specified first edge: for a strongly connected Eulerian labeled directed graph, the number of cyclic Euler tours is \(t_r\prod_v(\deg^+(v)-1)!\), where \(t_r\) is the number of directed spanning in-arborescences toward the tail \(r\) of that edge. Because each edge occurs just once per tour, choosing a fixed edge as first removes the rotation ambiguity without dividing by the track length.

For the bidirected \(K_{s,s}\), the out-Laplacian has eigenvalues \(0,2s\), and \(s\) with multiplicity \(2s-2\). Its cofactors are all \(s^{2s-2}\) (also one when \(s=1\)). There are \(2s\) vertices of outdegree \(s\), so BEST gives \(A_s\). For the complete directed graph with all loops, the out-Laplacian is \(sI-J\). Its in-arborescence cofactor is \(s^{s-2}\) when \(s\ge2\), and one when \(s=1\). There are \(s\) vertices of outdegree \(s\), giving \(B_s\). Forced-path subdivision does not change these Euler-tour counts.

There are \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\) unequal-gap classes and, for even \(n\), one antipodal class. Their choices are independent and their gap indices distinguish them even though the collection is unordered. Multiplication gives the claimed formula. All cases, including \(q=2\) and the single-class boundary \(n=3\), are included.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_two_active_cycles.py` reconstructs the original unsuppressed graphs from all legal words, groups edges by circular separation, checks balance, computes integer directed Matrix-Tree cofactors with the exact Bareiss algorithm, and compares BEST counts against the stated formula. It tests 31 components in instances with \(2\le q\le5\), \(3\le n\le8\) subject to its displayed test ranges. For selected small instances it *also* counts fixed-first-edge Euler tours by exhaustive edge-identity depth-first search, independent of the matrix-tree calculation. Run `python3 verify_two_active_cycles.py` using Python 3 without extra dependencies. The finite tests corroborate rather than replace the general proof in the preceding section.

## Relationship to prior work
Ruskey–Sawada–Williams (2012) explicitly observe the disconnectedness of the ordinary exact-fixed-weight overlap graph and instead construct a universal cycle using a *shorthand* representation, equivalent to a range of two adjacent Hamming weights. Campbell–Janik-Jones–Sawada (first public 12 March 2026) develop efficient universal-cycle constructions for bounded-weight languages and discuss this same distinction. Gabrić–Imam–Janik-Jones–Sawada (12 March 2026) study efficient decoding of bounded-weight cycles. Mitchell–Wild (first public 24 July 2026) consider nonbinary weight-constrained universal sequences built from nontrivial ranges. None of the materially inspected passages states the exact optimal number of full-length exact-weight-two tracks together with the complete orbit count of all minimum covers; a complete priority claim is not asserted. Standard BEST and matrix-tree enumerations are used, not claimed as new. The new contribution is the forced-path reduction to two canonical small junction graphs and the resulting complete all-\(q\), all-\(n\) cover count.

## Limitations
The result applies to full-length windows with exactly two nonzero positions, with one distinguished zero; it does not enumerate minimum covers for exact weights three or more, for non-Hamming additive weights, or under alphabet relabeling and reversal. The direct numerical checks are finite and are not an independent verification of the all-length proof. The full 2026 Mitchell–Wild preprint was not accessible in this inspection, and unindexed equivalent results remain a priority risk.
