# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Independent construction of the 2n-state Markov generator and Fourier conjugate blocks gives lambda_{k,+}=p(cos(phi)-1)-u+sqrt(u^2-p^2 sin^2(phi) +/-2i delta u p sin(phi)). For 0<delta<1 the positive real part B_u(v) decreases with v^2; combining increasing spatial dissipation on positive-cosine arcs with explicit negative-cosine bounds for n=4..7 proves gamma=min{2u,p(1-cos theta)+u-B_u(p sin theta)} for n>=4, theta=2pi/n. The bijective t=z/a parametrization and derivative of (t-1)(1-delta^2 t^2)/(t+1) yield unique cubic t^3+t^2-t=delta^-2, optimizer and full-gap comparison. The delta^(2/3) expansion follows by singular balance. Independently built numerical full generators for n=4,5,7,10 and delta=.01,.2,.8 (12 cases) match the predicted global candidate and nearby comparisons. These finite matrices supplement, not replace, analytic proof. Spectral gap does not imply total-variation mixing optimality.

## originality

PASS

The exact asymmetric one-particle Fourier generator is already in Mallmin/Blythe/Evans (arXiv:1905.10260); the symmetric finite-ring exceptional-point optimization is in their arXiv:1810.00813. The submitted novel comparison is the unique global full-generator optimum with two distinct tumble rates and its nonanalytic 2/3 cusp as bias vanishes. Inspected related primary spectral and large-deviation discussions and Resultary neighbours do not supply that optimization as a theorem. No claim that the Fourier block or symmetric optimum is new; priority remains best-of-knowledge.

## value

PASS

A global and unique tuning law for a natural nonreversible finite Markov process, together with singular bias rounding of a known exceptional-point optimum, is a motivated spectral boundary not a routine re-labeling. It quantifies how weak directional bias changes the optimized relaxation scaling, potentially useful in active-particle model design.
