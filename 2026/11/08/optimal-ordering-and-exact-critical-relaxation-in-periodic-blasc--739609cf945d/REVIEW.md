# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For arbitrary finite critical words and all real starting points in \([0,1)\), the Cayley transform is an exact rational identity. The rescaled inverse-square variable satisfies \(Z_{n+1}=Z_n+2c_j+c_j^2/Z_n\) with \(c_j>0\). This proves global radial convergence, gives an \(O(\log k)\) remainder, then a summable \(O(\log k/k^2)\) error in each block drift, yielding the stated logarithmic coefficient and finite constant. The adjacent-transposition proof is exact and addresses all word permutations; endpoint and empty-case conditions have been checked. The standalone program corroborates identities and finite cases, without being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, provisional)
The closest motivating source is González-Tokman–Oldfield, arXiv:2607.15488v1, Theorem 3.2 and Remark 3.8: these study stationary random measures and qualitative critical behavior, not the sharp rearrangement of a fixed multiset of periodic switching maps. Reppekus (2023), Theorem 14, gives the classical local parabolic power law; Klimeš–Mardešić–Radunović–Resman (2021), Section 1.1, gives the general formal power-log framework. Those results encompass the generic exponent or expansion mechanism but do not, by themselves, give the explicit word-order extremizers or exact phase coefficients across critical schedules. Comparisons with the motivating paper, established parabolic theory, and related published mathematical statements using exact map and periodic-switching terminology identified no matching ordering principle; the closest logarithmic critical-relaxation statement found concerns a different rational flip map. Absence of matches does not establish global novelty, so historical priority remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The motivating paper identifies critical intermittent behavior under iid switching and contrasts it with deterministic parabolic attraction. The new structural question asks how rearranging identical frequencies of contracting and expanding maps changes *how quickly* critical deterministic orbits reach the boundary. An exact all-word rearrangement theorem and phase-resolved two-term rate provide a parameter-free answer, a sixfold change in the principal inverse-square rate for a three-map example, and a reproducible benchmark for comparing periodic and random forcing. This is a natural optimal-order question, not an arbitrary finite slice or a known-table recomputation.

## Scope and risks
The result is for real radial inputs and finite periodic words at exact zero product Lyapunov exponent. It does not transfer to iid critical dynamics or establish analytic conjugacy classes, stochastic exit-time laws, or circle-typical convergence. The logarithmic term is consistent with known parabolic asymptotic theory; novelty is specifically the exact all-word coefficients and strict ordering extremizers. The general formal theory may simplify parts of the proof, and additional unlocated specialist literature could contain the same rearrangement principle. Independent review is outstanding.
