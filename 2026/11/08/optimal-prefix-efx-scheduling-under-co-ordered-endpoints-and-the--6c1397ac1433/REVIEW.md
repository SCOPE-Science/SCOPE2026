An independent verifier `CHECK.py` passed 14,965 small exact tests, a supplement not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest motivating article by Borst, Shahkarami and Vaish studies general fair interval scheduling, supplies the long-versus-disjoint-short lower-bound motif, and establishes weaker worst-case ratio bounds for unrestricted streams (arXiv:2608.15159, Sections 3–4), whereas the new theorem assumes a specific structural order and obtains exact prefix fair optimum. Curbelo–Malko (arXiv:2401.05648) study a different non-start-ordered online coloring objective. The older graph-coloring literature already implies static equitable coloring of proper/unit interval graphs; no novelty is asserted for that constituent. Actual targeted semantic-database and literature searches did not identify the composite online theorem coupled with the minimal arbitrarily-small nesting obstruction, but such a compound claim could be implicit in unindexed scheduling or claw-free coloring work.

**Value — PASS.** The distinction between adversarial interval nesting and proper presentations is mathematically motivated by the general \(2-1/m\) lower bound and by scheduling systems with nearly equal task durations. Achieving optimum welfare and EFX after *every* arrival is stronger than merely producing some equitable static coloring; the exact \(m+2\) boundary demonstrates that the geometric assumption cannot be discarded, even in an almost-unit-length family. These are structural and sharp claims rather than a routine isolated calculation.

**Proved limits.** The theorem is unweighted, homogeneous, finite, half-open, and start-ordered; no result is claimed for arbitrary arrival orders or weighted utilities. There is no independent verification, and possible prior implication in historical online interval scheduling remains the principal originality risk.
