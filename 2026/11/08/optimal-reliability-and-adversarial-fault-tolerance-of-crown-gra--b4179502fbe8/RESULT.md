# Optimal reliability and adversarial fault tolerance of crown-graph actuation

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(\operatorname{Cr}_n=K_{n,n}\setminus M\) have deleted matched pairs \(P_i=\{u_i,v_i\}\), and let \(L\) be its unweighted combinatorial Laplacian. In the fixed continuous-time system \(\dot z=-Lz+B_Su\), where \(B_S\) contains independent coordinate inputs at the selected vertices \(S\), controllability holds if and only if \(S\) meets at least \(n-1\) of the \(n\) pairs. If \(c_i=|S\cap P_i|\), sorted so that \(c_{(1)}\le c_{(2)}\le\cdots\), the same installed set remains controllable after every deletion of at most \(f\) actuator channels if and only if \(c_{(1)}+c_{(2)}>f\). The corresponding minimum installed counts for \(f=0,1,2,3\) are respectively \(n-1,n,2n-1,2n\); for \(f\ge4\) no set can be robust. Moreover, if each installed actuator independently fails with the same probability \(p\in(0,1)\), then for a fixed initial budget \(m\in\{0,\ldots,2n\}\) the maximum probability of post-failure controllability is \(0\) for \(m<n-1\), \((1-p)^{n-1}\) for \(m=n-1\), and, for \(n\le m\le2n\), \[r^a s^b+a p^2 r^{a-1}s^b+b p r^a s^{b-1},\qquad a=m-n,\ b=2n-m,\ s=1-p,\ r=1-p^2,\] with any term having zero coefficient omitted. At \(m=n-1\), the optimizers choose one vertex from each of exactly \(n-1\) pairs; at \(m\ge n\), every optimizer chooses at least one vertex from every pair and both vertices from exactly \(m-n\) pairs. These optimizer descriptions are complete for \(0<p<1\).

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(n\) be an integer at least three. The undirected crown graph \(\operatorname{Cr}_n\) has vertices \(u_1,\ldots,u_n,v_1,\ldots,v_n\) and edges \(u_i v_j\) exactly when \(i\ne j\). Its Laplacian \(L\) is the unweighted, symmetric combinatorial Laplacian, not a normalized or adjustable-weight Laplacian. Installed actuators are individual, independent coordinate input channels: \(B_S=[e_w:w\in S]\). Failure deletes an input channel while retaining the vertex and all graph edges. For the random model, installed input channels fail independently with identical probability \(p\in(0,1)\); no distribution is imposed on the dynamical initial state. A controller may use arbitrary time-dependent real inputs at the surviving channels. The optimization is over all subsets \(S\) with \(|S|=m\), not over arbitrary input-vector designs.

## Proof
**1. Exact eigenspaces and controllability.** Index each half in corresponding deleted-pair order and write \(J=\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\mathsf T\). Then
\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}(n-1)I&I-J\\I-J&(n-1)I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its mutually orthogonal eigenspaces are \(\operatorname{span}\{(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)\}\) with eigenvalue \(0\), \(\operatorname{span}\{(\mathbf1,-\mathbf1)\}\) with eigenvalue \(2n-2\), \(\{(x,x):\mathbf1^\mathsf T x=0\}\) with eigenvalue \(n\), and \(\{(x,-x):\mathbf1^\mathsf T x=0\}\) with eigenvalue \(n-2\). The four eigenvalues are distinct for every \(n\ge3\). The two high-multiplicity eigenspaces each have dimension \(n-1\).

By the symmetric PBH test, \((-L,B_S)\) is controllable exactly when no nonzero Laplacian eigenvector vanishes on all of \(S\). In either \((x,x)\) or \((x,-x)\) eigenspace, choosing either vertex of pair \(P_i\) observes the same coordinate \(x_i\) up to sign. Hence each is observed injectively precisely when at least \(n-1\) distinct pair indices are selected: if two indices \(i,j\) are absent, the nonzero vector \(x=e_i-e_j\) is invisible; if at most one is absent, \(x\perp\mathbf1\) forces that remaining coordinate to vanish. At least \(n-1\) selected pair indices also observe the two one-dimensional constant eigenspaces. This proves the first equivalence, without assuming simple spectrum or using structural controllability.

**2. Arbitrary actuator-channel deletion.** Let \(c_i\in\{0,1,2\}\) count installed channels in pair \(P_i\), in increasing order \(c_{(1)}\le c_{(2)}\le\cdots\). By Step 1, loss of controllability occurs exactly when two distinct pairs have no surviving channel. The minimum number of installed channels that must be deleted to cause this is \(c_{(1)}+c_{(2)}\), allowing zero deletions from an originally empty pair. Thus robustness against every choice of at most \(f\) failed channels is equivalent to \(c_{(1)}+c_{(2)}>f\). The resulting exact minimum counts are \(n-1\) for \(f=0\), \(n\) for \(f=1\), \(2n-1\) for \(f=2\), and \(2n\) for \(f=3\). Indeed, the cheapest sorted count profiles are respectively \((0,1,\ldots,1)\), \((1,\ldots,1)\), \((1,2,\ldots,2)\), and \((2,\ldots,2)\). For \(f\ge4\), even the full set fails when the channels of any two pairs (four channels) are deleted.

**3. Random failures and the exact probability.** Put \(s=1-p\) and \(r=1-p^2\). A pair with zero, one, or two installed channels has post-failure nonempty probability respectively \(0,s,r\). Failures of distinct pairs are independent. The control system remains controllable precisely when at most one pair is empty after failures. If \(a\) pairs are doubly installed, \(b\) are singly installed, and \(c=n-a-b\) are empty from the outset, the reliability is \(0\) for \(c\ge2\), is \(r^a s^b\) for \(c=1\), and for \(c=0\) equals
\[
\mathcal R(a,b,0)=r^a s^b+a p^2 r^{a-1}s^b+b p r^a s^{b-1},
\]
where terms with zero coefficients are absent. This follows by adding the disjoint events of no empty pair, one empty doubly installed pair, and one empty singly installed pair. The \(c=1\) case requires all initially nonempty pairs to survive.

**4. Budget-optimal placement, including strictness.** When \(m<n-1\), at least two pairs are initially empty. When \(m=n-1\), positive reliability requires \(c=1\), with \(n-1\) singly installed pairs. Suppose \(n\le m\le2n\), and set \(a=m-n\), \(b=2n-m\). A placement having no empty pairs has exactly \(a\) double and \(b\) single pairs, so its reliability is \(\mathcal R(a,b,0)\). Any placement with two empty pairs has zero reliability. A placement with exactly one empty pair must instead have \(a+1\) double and \(b-2\) single pairs (and exists only when \(b\ge2\)), with reliability \(r^{a+1}s^{b-2}\). Direct subtraction gives
\[
\mathcal R(a,b,0)-r^{a+1}s^{b-2}=r^a s^{b-2}\left[p s(b-2)+\frac{a p^2 s^2}{r}\right]>0.
\]
The strict sign holds for every \(n\ge3\), \(0<p<1\) and feasible \(b\ge2\): if \(a=0\) then \(b=n\ge3\), while if \(b=2\) then \(a=n-2\ge1\). Therefore every optimum at budget \(m\ge n\) covers all pairs, and its allocation is exactly as stated. These are all the possible profile types, completing the proof for every order, budget and permitted failure probability.

## Verification
Execute `python3 artifacts/verify.py` from this package. Its exact-integer modular algebra tests the Laplacian's four eigenspaces, the PBH rank condition for every selected input set through \(n=6\), and direct Kalman controllability ranks through \(n=4\). Exact rational arithmetic enumerates probability models and exhaustive budget optima for \(3\le n\le6\) at three rational probabilities. It also tests adversarial failure criteria and the separate zero-forcing comparison. The verifier returns `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks corroborate but do not replace the general eigenspace and inequality proofs above.

## Relationship to prior work
Burgarth, D'Alessandro, Hogben, Severini and Young (2011) establish zero forcing as a sufficient, not necessary, test for controllability of graph-defined dynamics; they do not give this crown-family robustness or reliability profile. Klee and Stamps (2021/2022) explicitly give the already-known characteristic polynomial of the crown Laplacian. This paper uses the same spectrum but derives the necessary coordinate-observation criterion and optimizes the resulting probabilistic failure event; neither the spectrum itself nor the general PBH test is claimed new. Ballotta and Joseph (2026) provide a general robust actuator-selection formulation with full-spark constraints, but do not evaluate these particular graphs or derive this closed-form reliability optimization. A published 2026 calculation of edge metric bases on crown graphs gives the same pair-coverage pattern for a different graph invariant; that known edge-resolving result does not itself establish fixed-Laplacian actuator reliability. The current claim is the precise combined control/fault/reliability law, not the familiar crown spectrum or the edge-metric basis count.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to unweighted, fixed-Laplacian coordinate actuation and deletion of installed input channels. It does not assert robust controllability under edge-weight changes, uncertain system matrices, correlated or unequal failure probabilities, joint actuation, nor continuous-time input-amplitude constraints. The endpoints \(p=0\) and \(p=1\) are excluded from the strict optimizer classification because ties arise there. Specialist papers under different network-control or reliability terminology may affect publication priority despite the comparisons performed.
