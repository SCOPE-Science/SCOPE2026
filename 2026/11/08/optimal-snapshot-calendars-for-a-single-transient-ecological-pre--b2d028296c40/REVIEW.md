# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

A disjoint first-detection decomposition is integrated exactly, reducing calendar optimization to a scalar Bellman recursion. The one-variable objective has a unique maximizer for every positive rate ratio and every finite observation count. The exact recurrence is checked at the equal-rate boundary, the special ratio of two is verified rationally, and rigorous expansions plus matching lower and upper bounds prove the two large-count asymptotics. The standalone package checker passes 9,032 tests involving independent quadrature, rational identities, optimized and randomized schedules, and limiting diagnostics. Finite checks are not treated as proofs of infinite statements.

## Originality — PASS within accessible evidence, with priority risk

The motivating open-occupancy research of Kendall et al. (2013) models entry and exit, but its full text could not be inspected. Full-text ecological survey-optimization studies by Guillera-Arroita et al. (2014), Moore and McCarthy (2016), and the arXiv report by Yu et al. (2013) were compared by assumptions and implication, not title matching; each has a different temporal population model, observation action, or objective and none supplies the presented all-count deterministic snapshot schedule and equal-spacing regret theorem. MacKenzie and Royle (2005) address occupancy-inference study design. Broad inspection scheduling remains a possible historical-equivalence risk. These limitations prevent claiming exhaustive novelty certification, but the accessible material does not establish coverage of this precise theorem.

## Value — PASS

The single transient visit is a motivated boundary of the occupancy-closure assumption. An exact unique all-count optimal schedule, a solvable rate-ratio family, and a universal logarithmic loss from periodic monitoring answer substantive design questions: whether observation times should cluster or spread and how much equally spaced monitoring can lose. This is an exact idealized benchmark, not a field recommendation.
