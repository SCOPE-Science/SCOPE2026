# Ordered projective triangles realize the rational magnitude-homology representation of PG(2,3)

## Finding
Let \(\Pi=\operatorname{PG}(2,3)\) and let \(G\) be its \(26\)-vertex point-line incidence graph with the shortest-path metric. The finite group \(K=\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_3)\), of order \(5616\), acts on \(G\). Let \(\jmath\) be the point-line polarity given by the standard symmetric dot product, and put \(\Gamma=\langle K,\jmath\rangle\), of order \(11232\). Define \(\mathcal T\) to be the disjoint union of ordered triples of noncollinear points and ordered triples of nonconcurrent lines. It has \(2\cdot13\cdot12\cdot9=2808\) elements, and \(\Gamma\) acts transitively on it.

**Theorem.** There is an isomorphism of rational \(\Gamma\)-modules
\[
\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}(G;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q[\mathcal T]\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{V_4}^{\Gamma}\mathbf1,
\]
where \(V_4\) is the four-element subgroup fixing an ordered noncollinear point triangle. In particular the character of \(K\) is \(2808\) at the identity, \(72\) at every projective involution, and \(0\) at every other group element; there are exactly \(117\) involutions. Thus \(\dim\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}(G;\mathbb Q)^K=2\) and \(\dim\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}(G;\mathbb Q)^\Gamma=1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The projective plane has thirteen points and thirteen lines, with four incidences at each vertex; its incidence graph has fifty-two edges, girth six, and diameter three. The magnitude chain group \(C_{k,\ell}\) is freely generated by \((k+1)\)-tuples of consecutively distinct graph vertices with successive distances summing to \(\ell\). The boundary deletes an internal vertex \(v_i\) only when \(d(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})=d(v_{i-1},v_i)+d(v_i,v_{i+1})\), with sign \((-1)^i\). The statement concerns bidegree \((k,\ell)=(3,4)\) and rational representation theory; no equivariant integral basis is claimed.

Represent each projective point by a nonzero vector in \(\mathbb F_3^3\) normalized so its first nonzero coordinate is one. Represent each projective line by a similarly normalized nonzero covector. Point \(x\) is adjacent to line \(a\) exactly when \(a\cdot x=0\). A projective matrix acts on points by \(M\) and lines by \(M^{-T}\), while \(\jmath\) interchanges a point and the line having the same coordinates. The latter preserves incidence and normalizes \(K\).

## Proof
**Integral chain premises.** The length-four chain complex splits by its ordered pair of endpoints. Exhaustive enumeration of all pairs, with exact signed integer boundary construction and unit-pivot integer row/column elimination, produces precisely the following local dimensions and ranks:

| Endpoint distance | Number of blocks | \(\dim C_{2,4}\) | \(\dim C_{3,4}\) | \(\dim C_{4,4}\) | \(\operatorname{rk}\partial_3\) | \(\operatorname{rk}\partial_4\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| zero | 26 | 12 | 36 | 28 | 12 | 24 |
| two | 312 | 17 | 45 | 19 | 17 | 19 |
| one or three | remaining 338 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The complete enumeration is reproducible in `verify_ternary_pg_mh.py`. Its integral elimination checks all compositions \(\partial_3\partial_4=0\), finds no residual nonunit factor, and shows \(\partial_3\) is onto. The global ranks are therefore \(\dim C_{2,4}=5616\), \(\dim C_{3,4}=14976\), \(\dim C_{4,4}=6656\), and \(\operatorname{rk}\partial_4=6552\), giving \(\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}(G;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{2808}\). The rank agrees with the earlier published projective-plane endpoint-rank formula; **that rank is not the new claim**.

**Equivariant trace identity.** The preceding surjectivity gives \(H_{2,4}=0\). Girth six and Corollary 1.2 of Asao–Hiraoka–Kanazawa give \(H_{4,4}\cong\mathbb Z^{104}\). An explicit equivariant integral basis for this diagonal homology consists of the \(104\) chains \((u,v,u,v,u)\) indexed by oriented graph edges: each is a cycle, these are distinct basis vectors, and the published rank forces them to span (there are no \(C_{5,4}\) boundaries). Therefore for any graph automorphism \(g\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_{3,4})=
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_{3,4})-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_{2,4})
-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_{4,4})+\#\{(u,v):u\sim v,\ g(u)=u,\ g(v)=v\}.
\]
Here each chain-module trace is exactly the number of its ordered tuple generators whose vertices are **all** individually fixed by \(g\); no orientation sign occurs because the group acts coordinatewise on ordered tuples.

**Exhaustive finite character calculation.** Every element of \(K\) has a unique nonsingular \(3\times3\) matrix representative over \(\mathbb F_3\) normalized to have first nonzero entry one. Filtering all \(3^9\) possible matrices leaves exactly \((27-1)(27-3)(27-9)/2=5616\) projectivities. The standalone checker `pg_mh_symmetry.py` reconstructs each incidence-preserving vertex permutation, counts fixed chain tuples in all three degrees, and applies the trace identity. It finds the exact trace distribution
\[
(2808:1),\qquad (72:117),\qquad (0:5498).
\]
It independently checks that the \(117\) nonidentity order-two elements are precisely those with trace \(72\), and recomputes the trace directly by endpoint-indexed chain blocks for every fixed-point profile.

**Permutation-character comparison.** An ordered noncollinear point triple consists of two distinct points and any of the nine points outside their unique joining line; there are \(1404\). The same count holds dually for lines. The checker independently enumerates all \(2808\) triangles and, for all \(5616\) projectivities, counts the triangles fixed pointwise. **Every resulting permutation trace agrees exactly with the magnitude-homology trace.** For each element of \(\jmath K\), the group action interchanges the point-point and line-line endpoint summands, and also the two kinds of triangles; both traces are zero. The two rational \(\Gamma\)-characters consequently agree at all \(11232\) elements. Character theory in characteristic zero gives the claimed abstract rational module isomorphism.

The action on \(\mathcal T\) is transitive: \(K\) acts transitively on ordered noncollinear point triples, with four diagonal projectivities fixing a triple, and \(\jmath\) interchanges point and line triangles. Hence the permutation representation is \(\operatorname{Ind}_{V_4}^{\Gamma}\mathbf1\). The two \(K\)-orbits produce a two-dimensional fixed space; the single \(\Gamma\)-orbit produces one fixed line.

## Verification
Run `python verify_ternary_pg_mh.py` and then `python pg_mh_symmetry.py` from the directory containing both files, using standard Python with no external dependencies. The first program constructs all \(338\) nonzero endpoint blocks, verifies exact signed boundaries, and uses integer unit pivots. The second independently constructs projective transformations, verifies their incidence action, tests every group-character value against direct fixed-tuple counts and the triangle permutation character, and checks all \(117\) nonidentity involutions. `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt` records actual successful execution. These finite exhaustive computations, paired with the displayed equivariant Euler–Poincaré argument and the published diagonal rank theorem, constitute the proof for the **finite** graph under discussion; they make no assertion about arbitrary projective-plane orders.

## Relationship to prior work
Asao, Hiraoka and Kanazawa establish the girth-based diagonal rank used in the equivariant trace identity and the vanishing context for first off-diagonal magnitude homology. Gu constructs algebraic Morse methods for graph magnitude homology. The earlier result *Endpoint-localized first off-diagonal magnitude homology of finite projective planes* already proves the \(q^2\) endpoint rank over every field for any plane of order \(q\); its full result and proof were checked. Thus the \(2808\)-dimensional nonequivariant rank is **previously covered** and not claimed as original. The new assertion identifies the **rational symmetry module**, the complete character of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_3)\), and the polarity-fixed subspace; numerical ranks alone do not determine these. Siemons and Smith study a distinct homology built from incidence maps on the lattice of projective subspaces, not graph-metric magnitude homology.

## Limitations
The representation equivalence is abstract and rational; equality of rational characters does not construct a canonical triangle-labelled cycle basis, prove an integral equivariant isomorphism, or classify magnitude homology of projective planes of orders other than three. The computational proof is exhaustive in exactly \(5616\) group elements but does not substitute for a formula over all orders. No independent external audit has been performed. A mathematically equivalent group-action computation in unindexed literature remains a bibliographic possibility.
