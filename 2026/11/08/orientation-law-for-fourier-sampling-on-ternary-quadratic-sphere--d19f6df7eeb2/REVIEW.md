# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The displayed measurement normalization yields the exact identity \(\mathcal A_L^*\mathcal A_L=|S_j|^{-1}H^*\operatorname{diag}(m)H\) with \(HH^*=q^2I\). The isometry type of an annihilator line gives all fiber sizes: a hyperbolic-plane decomposition for isotropic lines, and a binary nondegenerate residual form for anisotropic lines. Explicit quadratic Gauss sums derive the necessary solution counts. Algebraic rank comparison covers \(q=3\), both signs, and all odd prime powers. The included exact finite-field checker tested every direction for \(q=3,5,7,9,11\) and the predicted direct Gram identity on small fields. The proof, rather than the finite tests, supports universal quantifiers.

## Originality
PASS relative to reviewed accessible evidence. The closest contemporary motivation is Pham–Xue (arXiv:2610.29664), whose full-text main theorem and recovery section concern extension bounds and recovery from missing frequencies *on* a sphere, not full frequency-plane frames restricted *to* a sphere. Fraser–Rakhmonov (arXiv:2503.15072) prove general finite-field projection estimates and subspace Plancherel, without this complete ternary quadratic-sphere fiber spectrum, rank-maximizing conic, or conditioning dichotomy. The standard quadratic counts and Fourier diagonalization are acknowledged inputs. Specific equivalent and dominating formulations were searched in the mathematical research index and web literature; no directly covering record was found. A prior unindexed equivalent specialized calculation remains possible.

## Value
PASS. Selecting a full two-dimensional Fourier subspace gives a natural \(q^2\)-coefficient sensing model on a nonzero ternary sphere. The exact spectral law tells which coefficient combinations are invisible, classifies *all* choices of sensing plane, and optimizes recoverable dimension among these planes. It also identifies a meaningful stability-versus-identifiability tradeoff: isotropic planes recover nearly all coefficients, and the negative-radius-sign case is exactly tight on that recoverable subspace. The isolated ternary-field tie is mathematically necessary, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limits and risks
The result does not assert independent publication novelty, handle incomplete planes, or solve general spherical restriction conjectures. Its numerical checks cover only selected finite fields; the universal result depends on the independent algebraic argument. The comparison is strongest for the inspected primary texts and indexed research records, and less certain against unindexed classical finite-geometry computations.
