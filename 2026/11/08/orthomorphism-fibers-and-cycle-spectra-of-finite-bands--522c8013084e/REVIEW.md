# Scientific review: orthomorphism fibers of finite bands

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is all finite nonempty bands, with no normality or unit assumption. The proof first derives preservation of every ideal by the orthomorphism, uses equality of ideal-membership profiles to obtain \(\mathcal J\)-class preservation, then uses the finite orbit of \(\alpha\) to establish its own class preservation. The classical rectangular-band decomposition reduces the problem to one \(a\times b\) rectangular class, where the two independently chosen permutation families \((\tau_i)\) and \((p_\mu)\) give *both directions* of the classification, including uniform fiber cardinalities. Cycle-index multiplication follows from disjoint invariant \(\mathcal R\)-classes. No step extrapolates from finite experiments. A direct Cayley-table checker exhaustively verifies all 41,952 permutations across seven finite band examples, including nontrivial gluing between rectangular classes. The general proof is symbolic.

The August 25, 2026 Araújo–Bentz–Cameron–Hendrey–Kinyon paper studies existence, ideal/Green restrictions, principal-factor reduction, and special band cases, but its inspected Sections 1, 12 and 15 do not state the complete orthomorphism image, constant-fiber theorem, full factorial count, or cycle-index factorization for **all** finite bands. Problem 15.6 explicitly requests cycle-structure information; the present result settles its orthomorphism-only aspect for bands, not for general semigroups. The classical decomposition into rectangular bands is acknowledged, as are previously known uniqueness of complete maps on semilattices and all-permutations cases for one-sided-zero bands. Searches for equivalent enumeration under “complete mapping,” “orthomorphism,” “band,” “rectangular band,” and Cayley-table transversal terms produced no dominating published theorem. Group/finite-field cycle-type papers do not cover non-group bands. This is a qualified priority assessment, not uniqueness certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete structural classification, exact enumeration, and exact cycle-type distribution across the whole natural class of finite bands, not a choice of isolated parameters. The uniform fiber and Green-\(\mathcal R\)-preserving characterization explain why cross-class multiplication is invisible to these statistics, including for distinct semilattice gluings. It supplies a concrete structural answer for a substantial natural subclass of a stated 2026 cycle-structure research problem. This has standalone use for counting Cayley-table transversals and comparing structurally different bands. The theorem does not determine the cycle spectrum of the complete mapping itself, joint cycles, arbitrary semigroups, or distributions of other graph/semigroup invariants.
