# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For the exceptional u-free color class, the complete-bipartite induced subgraph is threshold iff ordinary-left count <=1 or all-right count <=1, giving formal EGF F. For the unique u-containing class, the usual H accounts for a=0 or total-right<=1; exactly the new configurations a=1,c=0,b>=2 yield a star plus isolated u and contribute x(e^y−1−y). Every other large pattern contains induced C4 or P4. Exponential product for q named colors with u assigned one of q choices produces the exact coefficient and deletion increment. The positivity conditions follow by placing remaining vertices in q−1 admissible classes; q=1,2,3 boundary cases are explicit. An independently implemented forbidden-induced-subgraph brute force matched integer coefficient recursion in 103 bounded (m,n,d,q) cases.

## originality

PASS

Best-of-knowledge original sharp perturbation invariant: the complete-multipartite enumerator gives only the undeleted baseline F^q. Deleting a star creates a precise exceptional color-class monomial x(e^y−1−y), leading to a new exact all-palette coefficient correction and sharply different two- versus three-color stability. The graph is not complete multipartite when both deleted and retained right neighbors exist. The Bang-Jensen–Bessy–Reinald work proves general partition hardness and alternating-cycle equivalence, not this explicit fixed-star deletion enumeration in accessible abstract; the full primary paper could not be obtained and remains a named risk.

## value

PASS

A deletion star is the simplest structured perturbation of a biclique, and threshold-induced partitions are a natural forbidden-subgraph coloring statistic. The exact all-palette correction and sharp robust-two-color versus sensitive-three-color phase boundary solve a motivated combinatorial stability question, not just another arbitrary small instance.
