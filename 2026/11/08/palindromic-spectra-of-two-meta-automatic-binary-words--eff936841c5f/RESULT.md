# Palindromic spectra of two meta-automatic binary words

## Finding
Let \(M_1\) and \(M_2\) be the binary sequences defined in the next section, and let \(P_i(n)\) denote the number of **distinct** palindromic contiguous factors of length \(n\) in \(M_i\). For every integer \(n\geq 1\),

\[
P_1(n)=\begin{cases}
2,&1\leq n\leq 9\ \text{or}\ n\in\{14,16\},\\
3,&n\in\{10,12\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently, \(M_1\) has exactly \(28\) distinct nonempty palindromic factors and its longest palindromic factors have length \(16\). In contrast, for every integer \(r\geq0\), the prefix of \(M_2\) of length \(8^r\) is a palindrome. Thus \(M_2\) has palindromic factors of unbounded length.

## Assumptions and scope
Work with right-infinite words indexed from position \(0\). On the alphabet \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) define two prolongable \(4\)-uniform morphisms

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_1:&\quad 0\mapsto0112,\quad1\mapsto1230,\quad2\mapsto2301,\quad3\mapsto3023;\\
\sigma_2:&\quad 0\mapsto0132,\quad1\mapsto2310,\quad2\mapsto3201,\quad3\mapsto1023.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(\tau\) be the letter-to-letter coding \(\tau(0)=\tau(2)=0\) and \(\tau(1)=\tau(3)=1\). Define \(M_i=\tau(\sigma_i^\omega(0))\), where \(\sigma_i^\omega(0)\) is the infinite fixed point beginning with \(0\). These are the sequences introduced by Campbell and Cloitre, also indexed as OEIS A392736 and A391614. A factor is a finite contiguous subword; a palindrome equals its reversal. Empty palindromes are excluded from the count of \(28\).

The subject of this result is palindromic-factor complexity, not the published ordinary-factor complexity or the original nested-recursion construction.

## Proof
### 1. Exhaustive adjacent-letter language of the first morphism
Let \(S_j\) be the set of length-two factors of \(\sigma_1^j(0)\). Direct expansion of the four stated images gives

\[
S_4=S_5=\{01,02,03,11,12,13,21,22,23,30\}=:S.
\]

Every letter occurs in \(\sigma_1^2(0)\). For later levels, the length-two factor set of a substituted word depends only on its letter set and its previous length-two factor set: adjacent pairs either lie inside one image or cross the boundary between images of an adjacent pair. Because the letter set has stabilized and \(S_4=S_5\), induction gives \(S_j=S\) for all \(j\ge4\). The iterates are nested prefixes, so \(S\) is exactly the adjacent-letter language of \(\sigma_1^\omega(0)\).

### 2. Finite and exhaustive palindrome certificate
Partition the infinite four-letter fixed point into aligned blocks \(\sigma_1^3(c)\), each of length \(64\), for successive letters \(c\) of the fixed point. Every factor of length \(n\le18\) lies in one or two consecutive such blocks. Hence its binary image is a factor of \(\tau(\sigma_1^3(xy))\) for some \(xy\in S\). Conversely, every \(xy\in S\) occurs in the fixed point, so all factors found in these ten binary words actually occur in \(M_1\). This is an equality of the complete factor sets for all \(1\le n\le18\), not an extrapolation from a long sample.

Enumerating the palindromic factors in these ten words of length \(128\) gives, in order of \(n=1,\ldots,18\),

\[
(P_1(1),\ldots,P_1(18))=
(2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,3,0,3,0,2,0,2,0,0).
\]

The complete list of words and the precise enumeration procedure can be regenerated by the accompanying standalone \(\texttt{verify_pal.py}\), which also checks the displayed adjacent-letter set. At length \(16\), the two palindromes are \(1010010110100101\) and \(1010101001010101\), proving the maximum length is attained.

There is no length-\(11\) palindrome and no length-\(18\) palindrome. Every odd palindrome of length at least \(11\) contains a centered length-\(11\) palindrome, and every even palindrome of length at least \(18\) contains a centered length-\(18\) palindrome. Therefore none exists outside the finite range displayed. The sum of the counts is \(28\), completing the \(M_1\) classification.

### 3. Unbounded palindromic prefixes of the second word
Campbell and Cloitre's digit formula (Theorem 26) states that \(M_2(n)\) is the parity of the binary digits of \(n\) in positions **not** congruent to \(2\pmod3\), counting the least significant position as \(0\). It follows directly, for all \(n\ge0\) and \(0\le j<8\), that

\[
M_2(8n+j)=M_2(n)\oplus c_j,
\qquad(c_0,\ldots,c_7)=(0,1,1,0,0,1,1,0),
\]

where \(\oplus\) is addition modulo \(2\). Therefore \(M_2\) is also the fixed point beginning with \(0\) of the binary \(8\)-uniform morphism

\[
\psi(0)=01100110,\qquad \psi(1)=10011001.
\]

Both images are palindromes. A morphism that sends every letter to a palindrome sends a palindromic word to a palindromic word, since reversal commutes with applying the morphism to an already reversed word. Thus \(\psi^r(0)\) is a palindrome for every \(r\ge0\); it is the prefix of \(M_2\) of length \(8^r\). Such lengths are unbounded.

## Verification
Run \(\texttt{python3 verify_pal.py}\) using Python's standard library. The script reconstructs the ten adjacent pairs from morphic iterates of depths four and five, enumerates every palindrome of length at most \(18\) from the ten exhaustive two-block covers, and independently checks those sets against the direct prefix of length \(4^7\). It also checks \(4096\) digits of each word against separately implemented nested recurrences (\(8192\) checks total), verifies the \(8\)-block identity for \(4096\) indices, and verifies the first four claimed nontrivial palindromic prefixes. The infinite claims follow from the proofs, not from those finite comparisons.

The replay output was \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\), with the complexity vector in the proof and total \(28\).

## Relationship to prior work
Campbell and Cloitre, *Meta-Automatic Sequences* (arXiv:2602.23395, earliest arXiv version February 25, 2026), derive the defining automata and \(4\)-uniform morphisms for both sequences, as well as ordinary factor-complexity estimates and an explicit digit formula for \(M_2\). Their inspected Sections 3–5 do not determine palindromic-factor complexity; the present result uses their morphisms and digit formula as established starting points. OEIS A392736 and A391614 list the underlying sequences but not the palindrome spectrum or their contrasting behavior.

Schaeffer and Shallit, *Closed, Palindromic, Rich, Privileged, Trapezoidal, and Balanced Words in Automatic Sequences* (arXiv:1508.02074), show decidability and automatic/regular descriptions for palindrome properties of automatic words. Their general result supplies a method in principle but does not calculate this pair's exact language or establish this contrasting invariant. The finite covering proof here gives explicit independent certificates for this pair.

## Limitations
The exact numerical spectrum applies to \(M_1\) only; for \(M_2\), the result proves unbounded palindromic factor lengths and an infinite sequence of palindromic prefixes, not the exact number of distinct palindromes at every length. The finite enumeration is exhaustive because of the proved two-block cover and pair-set stabilization, not simply because a long prefix was sampled. The calculation has undergone self-review but no independent mathematical audit. As with any newly proposed exact property, equivalent unindexed earlier results remain a historical-priority risk.
