# Which cut bounds are needed for biclique incenter polytopes?

## Finding

Let \(4\le p<q\) be integers, \(N=p+q\), and let \(A,B\) be the two vertex classes of \(K_{p,q}\). Work with the centered graphical zonotope generated by the segments \([-(e_i-e_j)/2,(e_i-e_j)/2]\) for \(i\in A,j\in B\), in the Euclidean space \(\sum_i x_i=0\). The inradius, the exact all-cut system for the optimal centers, and the dimension of their locus were established previously [1,2]. Here we determine which cardinalities in that system can give inequalities beyond the singleton bounds.

Put \(\delta=(q-p)/2\) and let \(E=\{x:x_B=0,\ \sum_A x_i=0,\ |x_i|\le\delta\ (i\in A)\}\). Apart from singletons and their complements, every cut with \(|S\cap A|\ne p/2\) gives a strict bound throughout \(E\). Thus for odd \(p\) the entire locus is \(E\). For even \(p\) the only further bounds that may be needed are
\[
 \sum_{i\in T}x_i\le L,\qquad |T|=p/2,\qquad
 L=\frac{pq}{4}-\frac p2\sqrt{\frac{\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor}{N-1}}.
\]
These are redundant through \(N=p^2-1\), including equality at the last value, and genuinely truncate \(E\) starting at \(N=p^2\). In the latter regime they define exactly \(\binom p{p/2}\) distinct new facets. These are facets of the **center locus**, not facets of the original zonotope touched by an inscribed ball.

## Proof

As in [1,2], the intrinsic inradius is \(\rho=(p/2)\sqrt{N/(N-1)}\). The exact criterion for a ball of this radius centered at \(x\) is
\[
 2|x(S)|\le d(S)-p\sqrt{\frac{|S|(N-|S|)}{N-1}}
 \quad (\varnothing\ne S\subsetneq A\sqcup B),\tag{1}
\]
where \(x(S)=\sum_{i\in S}x_i\), and \(d(S)\) is the number of cut edges. Indeed the zonotope cut supports are \(d(S)/2\) and the intrinsic norm of the cut normal is \(\sqrt{|S|(N-|S|)/N}\). The cut description follows by telescoping any linear objective along its nested superlevel sets. The gap \(p\) of the biclique Laplacian gives \(d(S)\ge p|S|(N-|S|)/N\), and for \(2\le |S|\le N-2\) one has \(|S|(N-|S|)\ge 2(N-2)>N^2/(N-1)\). Hence the right side of (1) is positive for such cuts. The singleton bounds give exactly \(x_B=0\) and \(|x_i|\le\delta\) on \(A\), and the \(B\)-singletons also certify \(\rho\).

Write \(a=|S\cap A|, b=|S\cap B|, s=a+b\). Complementation does not change (1), so it suffices to take \(a\le p/2\). When \(a=0\), \(x(S)=0\) on \(E\), and the bound is strict unless \(S\) is a singleton or the complement of a singleton. For \(1\le a<p/2\), \(2|x(S)|\le a(q-p)\). Moreover
\[
d(S)-a(q-p)=ap+(p-2a)b\ \ge\ p\sqrt{a+b}.
\tag{2}
\]
To check (2), set \(c=p-2a\). The difference of the squares of its left and right sides is \(c^2b^2+(2apc-p^2)b+p^2a(a-1)\). It has nonnegative coefficients if \(2ac\ge p\). Otherwise \(c(2a-1)<2a\) forces \(c=1,a\ge2\), and then the discriminant \(p^2((2a+1)^2-8a^2)\) is negative. This proves (2) in every case. Since \(\sqrt{s(N-s)/(N-1)}<\sqrt s\) for \(s>1\), this range imposes no nonsingleton restrictions on \(E\).

For even \(p\) and \(a=p/2\), \(d(S)=pq/2\). Minimizing the bound in (1) over \(b\) gives \(L\), by taking \(s\) nearest to \(N/2\). Complements account for the opposite oriented inequalities. The maximum of a middle sum on \(E\) is \(a\delta\), obtained by taking \(+\delta\) on \(T\), \(-\delta\) on \(A\setminus T\). Subtracting,
\[
L-a\delta=\frac p4\left(p-2\sqrt{\frac{\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor}{N-1}}\right).
\]
The square of the doubled square-root term is \(N+1\) at odd \(N\) and \(N+1+1/(N-1)\) at even \(N\). Across consecutive integers \(N\ge8\) this strictly increases. It equals \(p^2\) at \(N=p^2-1\) and exceeds \(p^2\) at \(N=p^2\), proving the boundary.

In the truncated regime \(0<L<a\delta\); positivity follows from the nonsingleton cut slack at the origin. Thus the new locus still has relative interior in \(x_B=0,\ \sum_A x_i=0\). For each oriented middle subset \(T\), assign \(L/a\) on \(T\) and \(-L/a\) on its complement in \(A\). This point makes precisely its own oriented middle sum equal to \(L\), every other oriented middle sum strictly smaller, and all box bounds strict. There is a neighborhood within the active hyperplane in the center locus, so the inequality defines a facet. The oriented subsets are distinct; complementary subsets define opposite facets.

## Consequences and checks

In the untruncated phase the vertices of \(E\) require at least \(p-1\) active box coordinates. For even \(p\), this forces a split of \(p/2\) coordinates at each endpoint, giving \(\binom p{p/2}\) vertices. For odd \(p\), there is one zero and a balanced split of the remaining coordinates, giving \(p\binom{p-1}{(p-1)/2}\) vertices. At the even boundary, \(p=4\) gives \(L=7=2\delta\) at \(q=11\) and \(L=12-16/\sqrt{15}<2\delta=8\) at \(q=12\). The corresponding checks for \(p=6\) are \(L=69/2=3\delta\) at \(q=29\) and \(L=45-54/\sqrt{35}<3\delta=36\) at \(q=30\). These checks illustrate rather than prove the threshold. The accompanying `verify_bipartite_centers.py` tests the same mathematical system against cut inequalities on finite instances; it is unchanged.

## Relationship to prior work and scope

[1] already gives a finite cardinality-indexed description of every optimal center, the radius, the dimension, and the origin-only assertion for equal parts of order at least three. Its constants minimize over the number of vertices of the cut in the larger part. This note proves the uniform redundancy estimate (2) and uses it to resolve the parity and facet transition that the earlier general system does not state. It claims neither the original all-center cut representation nor a count of vertices in the truncated regime. The threshold is for unweighted bicliques in the incidence-coordinate Euclidean metric; no analogous claim is made for weights or other graphs. The general graph cut-radius formula [2] and the combinatorial studies [3,4] are background. Priority of the precise redundancy result remains subject to earlier equivalent formulations.
