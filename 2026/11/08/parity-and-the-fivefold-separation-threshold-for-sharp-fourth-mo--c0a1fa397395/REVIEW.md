# Review of the fivefold monomial separation theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is for integers \(0\le\ell<h\), real coefficients, and normalized uniform measure on \([-1,1]\). Every moment used is \(0\) for an odd exponent and \(1/(j+1)\) for an even exponent. With opposite parity, both the exact fourth-power quotient and its sharp maximization reduce to a one-parameter quadratic. The sign of \(C-B\) is controlled by a quadratic with its unique positive root strictly between \(5\ell+2\) and \(5\ell+3\); the parity restriction removes the first integer. All equality cases follow from strict concavity or strict endpoint comparison. Same-parity nonoptimality follows from an explicitly nonzero first variation at the higher monomial, with the lower monomial treated separately. The large-degree limit follows by direct rational limits. The independent exact-rational checker reproduces thousands of cases; it is supplementary, not a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The relevant original sources are Borwein–Erdélyi, *Müntz spaces and Remez inequalities* (arXiv:math/9501225, full five-page text inspected), and Borwein–Erdélyi–Zhang, *Müntz systems and orthogonal Müntz–Legendre polynomials* (Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 342 (1994), 523–542; accessible textual rendering inspected in the sections on inequalities, orthogonality and Nikolskii-type estimates). Those texts concern density/Remez estimates and \(L^2\) orthogonalization/derivatives/pointwise inequalities; the specific two-power sharp fourth-moment embedding, universal integer fivefold boundary, and its equality classification were not found. Searches additionally checked the exact monomial claim, parity aliases, two-term Nikolskii terminology, stronger \(L^p\)-norm results, and published related sharp fourth-moment spectral-pair claims. No equivalent or strictly dominating statement was identified. The general quadratic optimization identity is elementary and is not presented as novel; the new statement is the arithmetic exponent-gap classification and sharp norm evaluation. A complete priority guarantee cannot be made because a specialised, unindexed treatment or an implicit general theorem could subsume the statement.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A two-degree Müntz space is the elementary building block for lacunary polynomial approximation. Whether adding a lower-degree mode actually enlarges the sharp \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) norm is a natural nonlinear stability question. The answer is not determined by the larger single-monomial moment alone: the exact threshold is a universal factor of five in the exponents, and below it both monomials are compulsory while beyond it the higher monomial is rigidly optimal. The exact nonnegative gain \((C-B)^2/(2C-A-B)\), complete norming ratios, the opposite same-parity behavior, and the macroscopic transition at \(h/\ell=5\) make the cutoff structurally informative rather than a selected numerical example. No claim is made that the two-term result solves general multi-degree Müntz norm problems.

## Scientific limitations and literature

The exact formula is restricted to real opposite-parity monomial pairs on the symmetric uniform interval, while same-parity pairs have only an extremizer obstruction. It does not address arbitrary weight, complex coefficients, or three-or-more-term Müntz systems. Textual access to the 1994 manuscript was possible, but a graphics-page retrieval failed; priority remains subject to checking other potentially relevant literature. The 1995 published-source date is established from the explicitly dated public arXiv record rather than inferred from a revision or journal year.
