# Research review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is the single complete joint first-entry and period classification on the connected crown graph for all parameters at least three. The proof starts with the exact neighbor count at each matched vertex. When the part size is even, the response map takes every word into the set of unanimous or balanced words, on which it is involutive. When the part size is odd, the four weight bands give an exhaustive transition table. The identified intersections explain exactly which states enter period two, which reach a fixed state, and which require three steps. Each multinomial/central-binomial count is independent of runtime data. A direct full-state C++ census (3 through 10) and a separate four-pair-pattern weighted census (3 through 26) agree with each nonzero and zero joint population; both ran from the packaged sources. The unbounded result rests on the algebraic case analysis, not on extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification

The closest general result, Goles and Olivos (1980), gives a period-at-most-two theorem for symmetric threshold networks, not these crown-graph counts or the parity gap. Kaaser–Mallmann-Trenn–Natale (2015) give a general voting-time framework, an asymmetric-graph contraction bound, and a complexity result; their exact bound does not determine the family-specific sharp values and no crown-graph theorem appears in their searched accessible full text. Agur–Fraenkel–Klein (1988) count fixed points in a one-dimensional cyclic neighborhood rather than in a crown graph. Retrieved crown-graph coloring, spectrum, and domination findings have different invariants and do not imply these dynamics. Search failures are not a novelty proof, and the inaccessible 1980 full text creates a limited priority risk. No accessible claim in the current research ledger covers the crown majority map; the closest prior majority records concern different graphs.

## Value — PASS

This graph family is a canonical matching-defect perturbation of a complete bipartite network. Deleting a perfect matching yields a sharp even-versus-odd split: a one-update bound versus a three-update bound, polynomial versus exponentially small immediate recurrence probability, and many versus a unique primitive two-cycle. The theorem gives the entire labelled temporal census, eventual basin populations, and explicit sharpness witnesses for every size; these are natural dynamical quantities rather than an arbitrarily selected statistic. It complements general voting-time estimates with a nontrivial exactly solvable family.
