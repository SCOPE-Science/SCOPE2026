# Scientific review: parity boundary layers for periodic linear-spline projection

## Correctness — PASS
The exact cyclic Gram matrix, its periodized inverse, the piecewise-affine evaluation kernel and the absolute-value segment integral are derived with their normalizations. The supremum over evaluation points is attained precisely at knots by convexity and strict opposite-sign cancellation for adjacent kernels. The cardinal kernel has absolute mass \(2\), and periodizing it gives the finite \(n\) formula. The exponentially small defect is reduced to the overlap of two cardinal tails with an \(O(q^{2m})\) remainder, and the two parity-dependent overlap integrals are evaluated piecewise exactly. The formulas hold for all integers \(n\ge3\), not only tested sizes. The included standalone checker independently inverts 19 rational Gram matrices and computes their exact operator norms, and checks the two coefficients against direct overlap integration. Those finite checks do not prove the asymptotics; the geometric tail and explicit integral argument do.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Passenbrunner (arXiv:1103.1949, Sections 1–2) gives the interval-spline norm monotonicity and limiting value \(2\), but interval boundary conditions are not the periodic parity-sensitive topology. Passenbrunner (arXiv:1608.06720, Introduction, Theorems 1.3–1.4, Section 3) proves uniformly bounded periodic spline projections and almost-everywhere convergence, not the exact finite-period exponential defect. Antonopoulos–Dougalis (arXiv:2106.09060, Sections 3–5, available full-text rendering) gives cyclic inverse-gram estimates and discusses computability at spline order two but does not furnish the two explicit overlap coefficients. A broad inverse-gram expression for graph-based finite-element projections can supply exact even-cycle finite-knot values; the main new information here is the sharp even/odd asymptotics and coefficient separation. This comparison is not a proof of global uniqueness; an earlier specialized periodic cardinal-spline identity remains a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
The projector norm controls worst-case amplification of uniform periodic least-squares finite-element fitting. At a fixed mesh spacing, it is not enough to know the universal limiting constant \(2\): this theorem determines the finite-size loss to leading order and reveals an unequal even/odd boundary layer with two explicit convergent constants. The parity effect arises from signs of the cyclic Green kernel, not from an arbitrary parameter slice. The theorem gives all finite-knot norms and identifies every norming point, allowing a complete exact baseline for uniform periodic linear elements. No claim is made for arbitrary meshes or higher polynomial degree.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest exact interval result is the 2011 Passenbrunner paper; the closest general periodic operator result is the 2016 Passenbrunner paper; and the nearest later uniform-grid approach is the 2021 Antonopoulos–Dougalis paper. The new theorem is deliberately limited to real-valued, periodic, continuous, degree-one splines with uniform mesh, and does not claim monotonicity across mesh counts. Equivalent unindexed formulae and unreviewed alternative derivations remain possible.
