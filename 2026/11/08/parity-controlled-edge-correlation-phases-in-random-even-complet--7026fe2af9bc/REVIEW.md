A one-dimensional Curie--Weiss Gaussian mixture makes its source-sector ratios positive moments of a nonconstant random variable in \([0,1)\). Complementation turns the high-density even-order comparison into strict monotonicity and strict log-convexity of these moments. The central point follows from the minimum cut size. Independent exact-enumeration and Fourier checks passed for orders four through seven at seven rational densities. No finite calculation substitutes for the general proof.

Neither inspected source states or dominates the finite-order incidence/disjointness covariance phase law, especially the even-order high-density sign split. Searches for exact pair-edge covariance, odd-source ratios and parity-dependent signs did not produce a closer claim. The standard building blocks are known; implicit coverage in broader Ising or graph correlation results remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** This resolves a natural structural question about conditional edge dependence in a classical random-graph model at every finite order and density. It identifies an incidence-sensitive parity transition, exact independence at the balanced point, and persistent positivity for disjoint edges. The distinction between high-density even and odd vertex counts cannot be inferred from unconditioned independent-edge behavior or low-density positive association alone.

**Limitations.** Restricted to distinct edges of complete graphs, with no claim for arbitrary hosts or higher-order dependence. Historical uniqueness and the full priority claim require external review.
