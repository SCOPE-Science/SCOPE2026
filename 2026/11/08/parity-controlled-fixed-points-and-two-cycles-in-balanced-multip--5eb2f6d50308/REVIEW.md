# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof classifies parts as homogeneous or mixed and uses the exact common external-one count in each part. Mixed parts cannot enter a homogeneous state along an initially periodic trajectory and therefore must tie at both phases; then all homogeneous parts flip. Comparing two consecutive mixed-part ties forces half the homogeneous parts to be one. When two or more parts are mixed, their occupancies are forced to be exactly half of the part size, giving the binomial product factor; the single mixed-part case yields the different odd-number-of-parts term. The fully homogeneous and fixed cases are enumerated separately and all boundaries \(r\ge4\), \(n\ge1\) are covered. Two standalone exact checkers independently confirm 19 profile parameter cases and 11 bitwise parameter cases (124400 labelled states). Neither finite experiment is treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest previously recorded exact strict-majority result treats three parts and its balanced specialization, where one mixed shore can freeze; its theorem does not cover the new simultaneous multiple-mixed-shore families for arbitrarily many parts. A multipartite parity automaton result studies XOR, and a multipartite majority-coloring result studies static coloring, neither implying the present synchronous majority census. Goles–Olivos (1980) and Benjamini et al. (arXiv:1405.2486v1) establish general period-two behavior without the labelled exact census; Tosic–Agha (2004) focus on one-dimensional threshold automata. Full text of the original Goles–Olivos article was not accessible and a historically unindexed equivalent remains possible. The review establishes only best-knowledge noncoverage, not an absolute historical priority certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result completely classifies the initially recurrent configurations for a natural high-symmetry infinite family, including a previously absent mechanism of any number of independent frozen balanced parts synchronized with flipping homogeneous parts. It yields a sharp parity-dependent existence and asymptotic growth transition; in particular, the highest-order oscillation population scales as a binomial power with exponent growing with the number of parts, and disappears at odd part sizes. These are structural statements beyond the prior tripartite case and beyond direct numerical enumeration.

## Limitations and closest sources
The formulas apply to the balanced graph and the strict tie-retaining synchronous rule, not unequal part sizes or transient basin counts. Relevant primary comparisons are Goles–Olivos (1980), Benjamini et al. (2014), Tosic–Agha (2004), a related three-part exact census, and the differing multipartite XOR and static-coloring results.
