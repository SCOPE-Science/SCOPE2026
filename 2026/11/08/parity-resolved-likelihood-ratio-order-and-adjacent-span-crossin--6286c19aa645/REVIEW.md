# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof is a complete finite matching count, an explicit likelihood-ratio comparison with support extension, and opposite upper-tail witnesses for the two adjacent-parity cases. No asymptotic extrapolation or computational inference is needed. Independently enumerating all matchings through seven chords and testing further exact rational cases through forty-one chords confirmed the formulas, including the support and boundary cases. The proof's domain is \(n\ge3\), the shorter-arc span \(0\le a\le n-1\), and the two-step restriction \(a\le n-3\).

## Originality

**PASS, with historical-priority qualification.** Acan (2013, dissertation Chapter 3.1; 2015, arXiv:1501.01489v1 Section 2) treats the identical conditioning variable and proves exact first and second moments, together with an asymptotic marginal degree distribution, but does not state the two-step monotone likelihood-ratio result or the universal adjacent-span incomparability. The new law is consistent with and implies the known first moment; it is not presented as a new mean formula. Acan–Pittel (2014, arXiv:1406.2867) concerns global crossing counts and components, a different conditioning. Searches for conditional chord-degree stochastic order and likelihood-ratio order did not locate another explicit theorem; a possibility of historical implicit coverage remains, especially through exact matching enumeration literature.

## Value

**PASS.** The degree of a marked chord is a basic local statistic of the chord intersection graph. Conditional means increase with span, but that fact alone offers no guarantee about tail risk. The theorem identifies exactly two strong same-parity likelihood-ratio chains and a sharp obstruction at *every* consecutive positive pair. This is a motivated structural distinction about the whole degree distribution, not an arbitrary isolated parameter or mere recomputation of the established mean.

## Limitations and closest literature

The result does not classify nonadjacent opposite-parity pairs or arbitrary nonuniform chord laws. The most directly overlapping dissertation predates the fully dated arXiv source; its available bibliographic metadata specifies 2013 but not a verified exact first public calendar day. The novelty assessment is based on substantive reads of its degree chapter, the 2015 paper's complete corresponding section, and the relevant introduction of the 2014 global-crossing paper, not on a proof that no earlier literature exists. Independent validation is not represented.
