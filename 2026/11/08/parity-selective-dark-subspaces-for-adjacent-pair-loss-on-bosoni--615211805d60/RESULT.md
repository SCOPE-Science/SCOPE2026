# Parity-selective dark subspaces for adjacent-pair loss on bosonic rings

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 3\), let two identical, noninteracting bosons hop with real uniform coupling \(J\ne0\) around the cycle \(C_n\). At each edge \(\{j,j+1\}\), the jump operator \(L_j=\sqrt{\gamma_j}\,b_jb_{j+1}\) annihilates an adjacent pair, where every \(\gamma_j>0\) may differ. Within the two-boson sector, the maximal *permanently loss-free* subspace \(\mathcal D\) has dimension

\[
\dim\mathcal D=\begin{cases}
0,&n\text{ odd},\\
n-1,&n\equiv0\pmod4,\\
n-2,&n\equiv2\pmod4.
\end{cases}
\]

Every protected vector has *zero hopping energy*. For even \(n\), the complete orthogonal projector is explicitly given below. The eventual pair-capture probability of any normalized initial two-boson density matrix \(\rho\) equals \(1-\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\mathcal D}\rho)\), independently of all positive edge-loss rates.

Writing \(r=\min\{(b-a)\bmod n,(a-b)\bmod n\}\) for the initial distance of an occupation-basis pair \(\lvert a,b\rangle_B\), the eventual capture probability \(\eta_n(r)\) is exactly \(1\) for odd \(n\). For even \(n\), with \(m=n/2\), it is

\[
\eta_n(r)=\begin{cases}
1-2(n-1)/n^2,&r=0,\\
1,&r=1,\\
1-(3n-4)/n^2,&2\le r<m,\ r\text{ even},\\
1-1/n,&3\le r<m,\ r\text{ odd},\\
1-4(n-1)/n^2,&r=m,\ m\text{ even},\\
1,&r=m,\ m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, an initially adjacent pair is always eventually captured, while an initially antipodal pair is captured with probability one precisely in the \(n\equiv2\pmod4\) even-ring class. The latter can occur despite a nonzero protected subspace.

## Assumptions and scope

The Hilbert space is \(\mathcal H_B=\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb C^n)\) with normalized basis \(\lvert a,a\rangle_B=\lvert a\rangle\otimes\lvert a\rangle\) and, for \(a\ne b\), \(\lvert a,b\rangle_B=(\lvert a\rangle\otimes\lvert b\rangle+\lvert b\rangle\otimes\lvert a\rangle)/\sqrt2\). All site indices are modulo \(n\). The one-body Hamiltonian is \(h=J A(C_n)\) and the two-body Hamiltonian is its symmetric restriction \(H=(h\otimes I+I\otimes h)|_{\mathcal H_B}\). There are no coherent two-body interactions, magnetic fluxes, site potentials, dephasing, or additional jump operators.

The jump \(L_j=\sqrt{\gamma_j}\,b_jb_{j+1}\) maps the adjacent two-boson basis state to vacuum and annihilates every other two-boson basis state. Thus its no-jump contraction is generated by \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}=H-iW/2\), with

\[
W=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\gamma_j\lvert j,j+1\rangle_B\langle j,j+1\rvert_B.
\]

The rates are finite and strictly positive; they need not be translation-invariant. The asserted eventual capture probability is the probability of at least one pair-loss jump at arbitrarily large time, not a claim about the capture time or the strong-loss Zeno approximation.

For even \(n=2m\), define the simultaneous translation \(T\lvert a,b\rangle_B=\lvert a+1,b+1\rangle_B\), its alternating-momentum projector and the alternating edge vector by

\[
Q_{\pi}=\frac1n\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}(-1)^sT^s,\qquad
\lvert e_{\pi}\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^j\lvert j,j+1\rangle_B.
\]

With the normalized one-body Fourier vector \(\lvert\phi_k\rangle=n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}e^{2\pi i k j/n}\lvert j\rangle\), define \(\lvert\phi_k\vee\phi_\ell\rangle=(\lvert\phi_k\rangle\otimes\lvert\phi_\ell\rangle+\lvert\phi_\ell\rangle\otimes\lvert\phi_k\rangle)/\sqrt2\) for \(k\ne\ell\). The complete loss-free orthogonal projector is

\[
\boxed{P_{\mathcal D}=Q_{\pi}-\lvert e_{\pi}\rangle\langle e_{\pi}\rvert
+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\lvert\phi_k\vee\phi_{k+m}\rangle\langle\phi_k\vee\phi_{k+m}\rvert.}
\]

For odd \(n\), \(P_{\mathcal D}=0\).

## Proof

**1. Momentum reduction.** The hopping Hamiltonian commutes with simultaneous translation. Its orthonormal bosonic eigenvectors are \(\lvert\phi_k\vee\phi_\ell\rangle\) for distinct momenta and \(\lvert\phi_k\rangle^{\otimes2}\) for coincident momenta. Their energies are

\[
E_{k\ell}=2J\left(\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n}+\cos\frac{2\pi\ell}{n}\right).
\]

The simultaneous-translation momentum is \(p\equiv k+\ell\pmod n\). For fixed \(p\), choosing \(\ell\equiv p-k\) makes the energy

\[
E_{k,p-k}=4J\cos\frac{\pi p}{n}\cos\frac{\pi(2k-p)}n.
\]

If \(\cos(\pi p/n)\ne0\), equality of two energies within this momentum sector forces \(k'=k\) or \(k'=p-k\) modulo \(n\), so each distinct bosonic momentum pair has a simple energy within the sector. If \(n\) is even, the single exceptional momentum \(p=m=n/2\) instead has energy identically zero. If \(n\) is odd, no exceptional momentum occurs.

**2. Edge-loss Fourier amplitudes.** For a distinct-momentum bosonic plane-wave pair, the coefficient on \(\lvert j,j+1\rangle_B\) is proportional to

\[
e^{2\pi i p j/n}\bigl(e^{2\pi i k/n}+e^{2\pi i\ell/n}\bigr).
\]

For a coincident-momentum pair, the corresponding coefficient is a nonzero constant multiple of \(e^{2\pi i p j/n}\); the same vanishing condition therefore applies. The coefficient vanishes for all edges if and only if \(n\) is even and the two momenta are antipodal, \(\ell\equiv k+m\pmod n\). Every such pair has energy zero. Since all \(\gamma_j>0\), a vector is in \(\ker W\) precisely when its coefficients on all edges vanish; Fourier orthogonality on the full edge orbit prevents cancellation between different total-momentum sectors.

**3. Exhaustive dark-space classification.** In each nonexceptional momentum sector, the hopping energies are simple. Consequently its only possible dark eigenvectors are the antipodal plane-wave pairs from step 2. The \(m\) distinct antipodal pairs are indexed by \(k=0,\ldots,m-1\), and exactly one of them (\(k=0\)) belongs to the exceptional momentum \(p=m\). In that exceptional sector the entire Hamiltonian is zero, while the edge-loss support has rank one: Fourier transforming the edge states leaves exactly the vector \(\lvert e_{\pi}\rangle\) in that sector. The orthogonal complement of this vector in the \(p=m\) sector is dark. Combining the mutually orthogonal sector contributions gives the displayed projector, and proves that every dark vector has energy zero.

The exceptional sector has dimension \(m+1\) if \(m\) is even (two coincident-momentum pairs are present) and dimension \(m\) if \(m\) is odd. Removing its single bright edge vector and adding the other \(m-1\) antipodal plane-wave pairs yields \(2m-1=n-1\) or \(2m-2=n-2\), respectively. For odd \(n\), all simple sector eigenvectors have nonzero edge-loss amplitudes and hence no dark eigenvector exists.

**4. Localized capture laws.** Let \(\lvert0,r\rangle_B\) denote the normalized occupation-basis state at distance \(0\le r\le m\) (with \(m=n/2\) for even \(n\)). Its weight in \(Q_{\pi}\) is \(1/n\) except at antipodal separation \(r=m\), where that weight is \([1+(-1)^m]/n\). Its squared overlap with \(\lvert e_{\pi}\rangle\) is \(1/n\) for \(r=1\), and zero otherwise. Each of the \(m-1\) antipodal plane-wave states outside the exceptional sector has squared overlap \(2/n^2\) at \(r=0\), \(4/n^2\) for even positive \(r\), and zero for odd \(r\). Summing these orthogonal contributions gives the stated piecewise capture probabilities with no omitted separation or endpoint cases.

**5. Long-time probability and rate-independence.** For \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}=H-iW/2\), every no-jump norm obeys \(\frac{d}{dt}\|\psi(t)\|^2=-\langle\psi(t),W\psi(t)\rangle\le0\). An eigenvector with an undamped (real) eigenvalue must lie in \(\ker W\) and be an eigenvector of \(H\). The contraction property excludes nontrivial Jordan blocks on the real spectral axis. All remaining eigenmodes decay, while the orthogonal dark subspace is invariant and undamped. Thus the surviving norm of a pure input tends to \(\|P_{\mathcal D}\psi\|^2\); linearity gives eventual capture \(1-\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\mathcal D}\rho)\) for any normalized density matrix. Positivity of the rates, not their equality, is the only loss assumption used.

## Verification

The companion `verify_rank.py` independently constructs the *unnormalized integer symmetric-tensor* hopping matrix and computes the adjoint Krylov observability rank over each of two distinct primes. For every \(3\le n\le16\), both ranks agree with the analytic dimension formula. This modular check is a finite corroboration, not a proof for all dimensions.

The companion `verify_projector.py` instead uses a normalized symmetric-tensor Hamiltonian, spectral eigenspace kernels of a deliberately nonuniform strictly positive edge-loss matrix, and a separately constructed Fourier projector. For every \(3\le n\le14\), it checks projector equality, idempotence, stationarity, annihilation by the loss matrix, rank, and every possible localized separation. Both checkers are standalone and print `PASS` when their tests succeed; see `rank.log` and `projector.log` for recorded output. The proof above establishes the infinite family independently of these finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Ates, Olmos, Li and Lesanovsky [1] introduce distance-selective two-body lattice loss, the jump operators \(\sigma_j^-\sigma_{j+R}^-\), and strong-dissipation binding; their explicit two-particle example studies distance three and a strong-loss approximation, not this exact finite-rate edge-loss dark projector. Everest, Hush and Lesanovsky [2] study nonlocal losses in many-body hard-core bosons, particularly the strong-loss Zeno dynamics and its effective Hamiltonian; the finite-rate unprotected versus indefinitely dark distinction is different. Qin and collaborators [3] solve Hermitian nearest-neighbor two-particle interactions and analyze bound/scattering states (principally odd ring lengths), not irreversible pair annihilation. Krovi and Brun [4] provide a general spectral explanation of dark states in measured quantum walks, while Gamble and collaborators [5] give the noninteracting two-boson graph Hamiltonian; neither evaluates the stated ring/edge-loss rank and initial-distance laws.

The *on-site collision-loss* problem is inequivalent: its detector tests double occupancy, not neighboring occupancy. In that problem, antipodal momentum pairs have nonzero diagonal amplitude, but they have zero edge amplitude here; for example the present \(n=8\) symmetric-sector dark dimension is seven, rather than four for on-site collision loss. The present formula is therefore not a reformulation of an on-site collision projector. Absence of an equivalent in the examined literature is not a proof of global historical novelty.

## Limitations

The model is a two-particle, translation-invariant, flux-free, noninteracting hopping Hamiltonian with loss supported at *every* nearest-neighbor pair configuration and independent positive jump rates. The theorem does not apply automatically to hard-core constraints, coherent nearest-neighbor interactions, extra on-site collision loss, missing edge-loss channels, nonuniform hopping, or magnetic flux. It classifies eventual capture, not finite-time decay scales or robustness under perturbations. No claim of independent audit or exhaustive priority certification is made.
