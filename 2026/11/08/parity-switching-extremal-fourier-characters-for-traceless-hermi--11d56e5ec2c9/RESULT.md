# Parity-switching extremal Fourier characters for traceless Hermitian nilpotent walks

## Finding

Let \(q\ge2\) be any prime power and \(n\ge3\). Write \(K=\mathbb F_{q^2}\), \(F=\mathbb F_q\), \(H_n=\{A\in M_n(K):A^*=A\}\), and \(V=\{A\in H_n:\operatorname{tr} A=0\}\), where \(^*\) is conjugate transpose. Let \(S=\{X\in V:\operatorname{rank} X=1,\ X^2=0\}\). The undirected additive Cayley graph of \(V\) generated by \(S\) has valency
\[
D_{q,n}=\frac{q^{2n-1}+(-1)^n(q-1)q^{n-1}-1}{q+1}.
\]
Every Fourier character of \(V\) is indexed uniquely by a Hermitian matrix \(A\) modulo \(F I\). For \(t\in F\) put \(d_t(A)=\dim_K\ker(A+tI)\). Its adjacency eigenvalue is
\[
\lambda(A)=\frac{(-q)^n}{q(q+1)}\sum_{t\in F}(-q)^{d_t(A)}-\frac1{q+1}.
\]
The sharp nonprincipal **absolute** adjacency eigenvalue is
\[
L_{q,n}=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}+q-1)+1}{q+1},&n\text{ odd},\\[5pt]
\displaystyle\frac{q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}+2)+1}{q+1},&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Every character realizing \(|\lambda|=L_{q,n}\) has eigenvalue **\(-L_{q,n}\)**. If \(n\) is odd, these characters are exactly the classes of rank-one square-zero Hermitian matrices. If \(n\) is even, they are exactly the classes of rank-one Hermitian matrices with nonzero trace. Their respective Fourier multiplicities are
\[
\begin{cases}
D_{q,n},&n\text{ odd},\\
\displaystyle\frac{(q-1)q^{n-1}(q^n-1)}{q+1},&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Moreover \(L_{q,n}<D_{q,n}\). Thus these Cayley graphs are connected and nonbipartite, and the exact \(\ell^2\)-operator norm of the normalized adjacency walk on mean-zero functions equals \(L_{q,n}/D_{q,n}\). In particular, the character family at the slowest absolute decay switches from nilpotent rank one in odd matrix size to nonnilpotent rank one in even matrix size.

## Assumptions and scope

All fields are finite, including characteristic two. No condition on \(q\bmod 2\) or on the parity of the underlying prime is imposed. The trace of a Hermitian matrix belongs to \(F\). The group \(V\) has order \(q^{n^2-1}\). Since \(X^2=0\) implies \(\operatorname{tr}X=0\), writing \(S\subset V\) imposes no extra restriction. The adjacency operator is \(Tf(Y)=\sum_{X\in S}f(Y+X)\); the normalized walk is \(D_{q,n}^{-1}T\). The results concern the largest nontrivial eigenvalue *in absolute value*, not the entire multiplicity distribution of all eigenvalues and not total-variation mixing.

## Proof

**1. Parametrization and counting.** A nonzero rank-one Hermitian matrix can be written \(vv^*\) with \(v\in K^n\setminus\{0\}\), because every nonzero scalar in \(F\) is a norm from \(K^\times\). The map \(v\mapsto vv^*\) has exactly \(q+1\) preimages at every nonzero image. Moreover \((vv^*)^2=(v^*v)vv^*\). Consequently \(S\) is precisely the set of nonzero images of isotropic \(v\), with \(v^*v=0\). Fix a nontrivial additive character \(\psi:F\to\mathbb C^\times\). The one-variable Hermitian Gauss sum satisfies, for \(a\in F^\times\),
\[
\sum_{x\in K}\psi(a x^{q+1})=1+(q+1)\sum_{u\in F^\times}\psi(au)=-q.
\]
Diagonalization of a Hermitian form by congruence shows that a Hermitian matrix \(B\) of rank \(r\) has quadratic exponential sum
\[
\sum_{v\in K^n}\psi(v^*Bv)=(-q)^r q^{2(n-r)}.
\]
Orthogonality in the scalar variable gives
\[
N_0:=\#\{v:v^*v=0\}=q^{-1}\big(q^{2n}+(q-1)(-q)^n\big)
=q^{2n-1}+(-1)^n(q-1)q^{n-1}.
\]
As each \(X\in S\) corresponds to exactly \(q+1\) nonzero isotropic vectors, \(|S|=(N_0-1)/(q+1)=D_{q,n}\).

**2. Fourier diagonalization.** The bilinear pairing \((A,X)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(AX)\) on \(H_n\) is nondegenerate over \(F\): each off-diagonal \(K\)-coordinate pairs by the nondegenerate field trace \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}\), and diagonal coordinates pair normally. Thus the annihilator of the traceless hyperplane \(V\) consists exactly of the scalar matrices \(F I\), proving the claimed character parametrization. Using scalar orthogonality and the Gauss sum,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda(A)&=\frac1{q+1}\left(\sum_{v:v^*v=0}\psi(v^*Av)-1\right)\\
&=\frac1{q(q+1)}\sum_{t\in F}(-q)^{n-d_t(A)}q^{2d_t(A)}-\frac1{q+1}\\
&=\frac{(-q)^n}{q(q+1)}\sum_{t\in F}(-q)^{d_t(A)}-\frac1{q+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For scalar \(A\), precisely one \(d_t=n\), and this formula yields \(D_{q,n}\).

**3. The integer optimization.** Suppose \(A\) is nonscalar. Distinct eigenvalues have disjoint eigenspaces, so \(0\le d_t\le n-1\) and \(\sum_{t\in F}d_t\le n\). Write \(B=\sum_{t\in F}(-q)^{d_t}\). If some \(d_t=n-1\), all remaining \(d_u\) vanish except possibly one, which can equal one. Hence the two possible values are
\[
B_1=q-1+(-q)^{n-1},\qquad B_2=(-q)^{n-1}-2.
\]
If instead every \(d_t\le n-2\), then for \(n\ge4\)
\[
|B|\le q+\sum_{d_t>0}q^{d_t}\le q+q^{n-2}+q^2.
\]
For the second inequality, fix the number \(s\) of positive \(d_t\). Convexity of \(q^x\) makes the maximum under \(\sum d_t\le n\), \(1\le d_t\le n-2\), occur at an extreme partition. For \(s=2\) it is bounded by \(q^{n-2}+q^2\); for \(s\ge3\) it is at most \(q^{n-s+1}+(s-1)q\le q^{n-2}+2q\le q^{n-2}+q^2\); the \(s=0,1\) cases are immediate. When \(n\ge5\), this is strictly smaller than both \(q^{n-1}+q-1\) and \(q^{n-1}+2\). For \(n=4,q\ge3\), it is smaller than \(q^3+2\); for \(n=4,q=2\), the possible positive-dimension patterns have \(|B|\le8<10\). When \(n=3\) and no dimension equals two, every positive dimension is one and \(B=q-s(q+1)\), with \(s\le\min(q,3)\); this is strictly smaller in absolute value than \(q^2+q-1\).

For odd \(n\), \(B_1=q^{n-1}+q-1\) is the unique largest possible absolute value of \(B\), with \(B_2=q^{n-1}-2\). For even \(n\), \(B_2=-q^{n-1}-2\) is the unique largest possible absolute value, and \(B_1=-q^{n-1}+q-1\). The extra \(-1\) in \(\lambda(A)=(((-1)^nq^{n-1})B-1)/(q+1)\) puts the sharp extreme at \(-L_{q,n}\) in both cases, and is strictly smaller in absolute value on all other nonscalar classes.

**4. Equality, multiplicity, and connectivity.** A Hermitian \(A\) with an \((n-1)\)-dimensional eigenspace is \(A=-tI+C\) for a rank-one Hermitian \(C\). If no other eigenvalue occurs, then \(C\) has trace zero and satisfies \(C^2=0\); this is the odd-size extremal family. If the remaining dimension is a distinct eigenspace, then \(C\) is diagonalizable rank one with nonzero trace; this is the even-size extremal family. Conversely both types realize the specified patterns. As \(n\ge3\), a difference of two rank-one matrices cannot be a nonzero scalar matrix; hence passage to the character quotient by scalars causes no collisions.

There are \((q^{2n}-1)/(q+1)\) nonzero rank-one Hermitian matrices in total. Of these, the isotropic ones number \(D_{q,n}\); when \(n\) is even, subtracting the isotropic count leaves \((q-1)q^{n-1}(q^n-1)/(q+1)\). Finally, direct subtraction of the explicit expressions shows \(0<L_{q,n}<D_{q,n}\) for all \(q\ge2,n\ge3\) (the smallest boundary cases are \((q,n)=(2,3)\) and \((2,4)\), with pairs \((D,L)=(9,7)\) and \((45,27)\)). On an undirected Cayley graph, every eigenvalue of absolute value \(D\) corresponds either to a disconnected component (positive) or a bipartite component (negative). Their absence proves connectivity and nonbipartiteness. Fourier orthogonality yields the sharp mean-zero \(\ell^2\)-operator norm.

## Verification

`CHECKER.py` is a standalone exhaustive finite-field checker. It implements \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_9\) arithmetic, enumerates all Hermitian matrices for \((q,n)=(2,3),(3,3),(2,4)\), computes each nullity tuple and eigenvalue, verifies the sharp bound and the predicted number of maximizing matrices modulo scalar frequencies, and independently computes direct character sums for designated frequencies (all Hermitian matrices at \((2,3)\)). The expected output ends with three lines beginning `PASS p`, reporting respectively \((D,L)=(9,7),(56,25),(45,27)\). Run `python CHECKER.py`. The checker is corroborative finite evidence, not the infinite-family proof; the proof is the Gauss-sum identity and the integer optimization above.

## Relationship to prior work

Hermitian-form association schemes already give rank-based Fourier eigenvalues for the *full* Hermitian matrix additive group (Schmidt, 2017, §2, including equation (4); Cioabă and Gupta, 2021, §1.3). The present trace-zero square-zero generator set is the rank-one Hermitian relation intersected with a trace hyperplane. One can derive its Fourier multipliers by averaging known rank-one coefficients over scalar shifts; therefore the Fourier identity alone is not claimed novel. The sharp *absolute* extremum and equality classification must additionally optimize the entire field-rational eigenspace partition and distinguish two parity regimes. The inspected full-rank association-scheme papers do not state that optimized trace-zero classification. Orel's rank-one Hermitian adjacency graphs concern a different full/invertible matrix vertex set and graph-core problems. Ihringer studies Hermitian polar-space generators and incidence/Erdős–Ko–Rado extremizers, not the present additive walk. A related traceless ordinary-matrix rank-one nilpotent walk has different (non-Hermitian) rational eigenline counts and does not imply this parity switch.

## Limitations

The work supplies the spectral radius and its maximizers, not all adjacency eigenvalue multiplicities or a sharp total-variation cutoff. Small-field verification does not establish the arbitrary-prime-power statement independently. No complete historical priority or independent expert validation is asserted. General association-scheme treatments or an unindexed additive Hermitian nilpotent Cayley-graph source might imply the new extremal classification and would require further checking.
