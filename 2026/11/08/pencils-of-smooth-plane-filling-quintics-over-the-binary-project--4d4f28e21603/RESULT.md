# Pencils of smooth plane-filling quintics over the binary projective plane

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_2\), let \(B=\mathbb P^2(k)\) be its seven-point rational projective plane, and put
\[
W=H^0(\mathbb P^2_k,\mathcal I_B(5))
 =\{F\in k[X,Y,Z]_5:F(P)=0\text{ for all }P\in B\}.
\]
A *smooth filling pencil* means a two-dimensional \(k\)-linear subspace \(U\subset W\) for which **all three nonzero \(k\)-members** cut out geometrically smooth plane curves. There is no requirement that every member indexed by \(\mathbb P^1(\overline{k})\) be smooth.

**Theorem.** There are exactly \(16{,}800\) smooth filling pencils. Under \(G=\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\), they form exactly \(108\) orbits: \(96\) orbits of size \(168\) with trivial stabilizer, and \(12\) orbits of size \(56\) with cyclic stabilizer \(C_3\). Among the \(16{,}800\) pencils, precisely \(672\) have three members in the same individual \(G\)-orbit, \(3{,}024\) have exactly two members in the same individual \(G\)-orbit, and \(13{,}104\) have three pairwise distinct individual \(G\)-orbit types. The first possibility occurs **if and only if** the pencil has stabilizer \(C_3\).

The accompanying `pencil_orbits.csv` gives a polynomial-mask representative of every one of the \(108\) pencil orbits, together with its orbit size, stabilizer order, and three individual-curve type indices. Every such pencil has a base complete intersection of degree \(25\), with the seven points of \(B\) appearing *reduced and transversely* and with residual scheme length \(18\) supported away from \(B\).

## Assumptions and scope

All equations are homogeneous ternary quintics over \(k\), considered up to their nonzero scalar factor (which is unique over \(k\)). Smoothness always means smoothness after extension to \(\overline{k}\). Projective equivalence means an action of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\), not equivalence after extension of constants or merely abstract curve isomorphism.

For exact encoding, order the \(21\) monomials \(X^aY^bZ^c\), with \(a+b+c=5\), first by decreasing \(a\), then decreasing \(b\). The integer mask \(M(F)=\sum_j f_j2^j\) records the \(j\)-th binary coefficient. In the CSV, columns `F_mask`, `G_mask`, `F_plus_G_mask` are decimal masks of a basis \(F,G\) and their sum; `member_type_*` indices start at zero and refer to the individual-curve orbit table below. For example, the first row has masks \((213034,593124,675022)\). Thus it represents the smooth pencil spanned by the first two recorded equations.

There are \(14\) independent coefficients in \(W\) after imposing the seven rational-point vanishing conditions, so \(|W|=2^{14}=16{,}384\). The underlying ten individual smooth-curve orbits are represented by:

These ten individual orbits contain \(1{,}512\) geometrically smooth quintic forms altogether. A basis consisting entirely of smooth members can have dimension at most two: at any fixed \(P\in B\), differentiation gives a linear map from that basis space into the two-dimensional cotangent space \(T_P^*\mathbb P^2\), and smoothness of every nonzero member at \(P\) makes the map injective. This elementary dimension restriction is context, not the new enumerative classification.

## Proof

**1. Finite coefficient space and group.** Evaluate all \(21\) degree-five monomials at the seven nonzero vectors of \(k^3\). Binary row reduction has rank seven, leaving a \(14\)-dimensional kernel \(W\). The three substitutions \((X,Y,Z)\mapsto(X+Y,Y,Z)\), \((Y,X,Z)\), and \((Y,Z,X)\) generate \(\operatorname{GL}_3(k)=\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\), of order \(168\). Their exactly computed binary matrices on \(W\) partition the \(16{,}383\) nonzero vectors into \(139\) orbits.

**2. Exhaustive *geometric* smoothness.** For each of those \(139\) polynomial-orbit representatives, form \(F\) and its three partial derivatives and compute the ideal generated by them after dehomogenization on the charts \(X=1\), \(Y=1\), and \(Z=1\). The Jacobian criterion together with the Nullstellensatz says that \(F=0\) is geometrically smooth exactly when these three ideals are each the unit ideal. Exact Gröbner bases in \(\mathbb F_2\) find \(10\) smooth polynomial orbits, of the sizes in the table, totalling \(1{,}512\) distinct nonzero smooth forms. Since all orbit transformations are invertible, testing one representative per orbit is sufficient.

An independent finite-field check gives the same classification without Gröbner bases. A degree-five reduced plane curve has arithmetic genus \(6\). If it has \(s\le5\) geometric irreducible components and component-normalization genera \(g_i\), then the normalization genus formula gives
\[
\sum_{Q\in\operatorname{Sing}(C)(\overline{k})}\delta_Q
=6-\sum_i g_i+s-1\le10.
\]
Thus a singular reduced quintic has a singular point of Frobenius orbit degree at most \(10\). A nonreduced quintic over the perfect field \(k\) has a repeated factor of degree at most two, which has a nontrivial \(k\)-rational zero by the elementary degree-\(<3\) Chevalley–Warning count; the full quintic is singular there. Consequently, checking the homogeneous gradient at every point of \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_{2^r})\) for \(1\le r\le10\) is an **exhaustive geometric smoothness test**, not merely a bounded heuristic. For degree five in characteristic two, Euler's identity also implies \(F=XF_X+YF_Y+ZF_Z\), so a common zero of the partial derivatives lies on \(C\). The second verifier performs this test independently using explicit finite-field arithmetic, independently recovering the ten smooth orbits and the count \(1{,}512\).

**3. Pencils.** Encode the smooth subset of \(W\) as a set \(S\) of \(1{,}512\) distinct nonzero fourteen-bit vectors. Each two-dimensional vector subspace has exactly the three nonzero vectors \(u,v,u\oplus v\), where \(\oplus\) denotes binary vector addition. Enumerating exactly those triples with \(u,v,u\oplus v\in S\), and imposing \(u<v<u\oplus v\) so that each subspace is counted once, gives precisely \(16{,}800\) pencils. This is an exhaustive finite classification: the entire \(14\)-dimensional coefficient space is represented, and no untested pencils remain.

**4. Projective orbit and member-type counts.** Apply the three projective generators to each sorted three-element pencil and form their orbit closures. This partitions all \(16{,}800\) pencils into \(96\) orbits of size \(168\) and \(12\) orbits of size \(56\), giving \(108\) orbits. The stabilizer orders follow from orbit–stabilizer, and any order-three subgroup of \(G\) is cyclic. Label each smooth member by its one of ten individual-projective orbits. The exhaustive distribution is \(672\) triples with one type, \(3{,}024\) with exactly two types, and \(13{,}104\) with three types. Every size-\(56\) orbit has a constant member-type triple, and none of the size-\(168\) orbits does. This proves the stated equivalence. The CSV lists all \(108\) orbit representatives, allowing individual replay of these assertions.

**5. The base scheme.** For an accepted pencil \(U=\langle F,G\rangle\), the three gradients \(dF(P)\), \(dG(P)\), and \(d(F+G)(P)\) are all nonzero in the two-dimensional vector space \(T_P^*\mathbb P^2\) for each \(P\in B\). Hence the first two are linearly independent and \(C_F,C_G\) intersect transversely at \(P\). Each smooth plane quintic is geometrically irreducible (distinct positive-degree plane components intersect and would make it singular), so two different members have no common curve component. Bézout gives total intersection length \(25\); the seven rational base points contribute length one each, leaving precisely length \(18\) away from those points.

## Verification

The two independent implementations reproduce the exact same census:

F2_FILLING_QUINTIC_PENCILS_FIELD_PASS 16384 139 1512 16800 108 96 12 672 3024 13104

Run from the directory containing the attached files:

```sh
python3 verify_pencils_groebner.py --verify-csv pencil_orbits.csv
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 verify_pencils_field.cpp -o pencils_field
./pencils_field
```

The Python program uses SymPy \(1.14.0\) and performs all three affine-chart Gröbner tests in characteristic two. The C++ program is standard C++17 with no external dependencies and checks all projective points over finite fields of extension degrees up to ten. The machine-readable CSV consists of a header and \(108\) complete projective-class representative rows; each integer mask is decoded by the explicit monomial order in this document.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli and Ghioca, *Smoothness in pencils of hypersurfaces over finite fields* (arXiv:2203.07169, March 14, 2022), study existence and large-field bounds for pencils whose rational members are smooth. They do not classify pencils constrained to contain every point of the binary projective plane. Asgarli, Ghioca, and Reichstein, *Linear families of smooth hypersurfaces over finitely generated fields* (arXiv:2207.00918, July 2, 2022), construct smooth linear families without this common-base-point constraint; their general existence result does not imply the present counts or orbit types. Asgarli and Ghioca, *Plane-filling curves of small degree over finite fields* (arXiv:2307.03072, July 6, 2023), study degree \(q+3\) filling curves and a particular one-parameter pencil (Section 4, Proposition 4.5), but not the complete census of all pencils all of whose ground-field members are smooth for \(q=2\).

Classification of **individual** smooth binary filling quintics and classification of smooth binary filling **quartic** pencils are logically different from the present incidence and orbit classification of *quintic pencils*. Their previously known individual-curve counts are independently reproduced here and not claimed as new. The enumerative relation among the three members of a pencil, the \(108\) pencil orbits, and the stabilizer/member-type correspondence are the claims of this result.

## Limitations

Only pencils with all three \(k\)-rational members smooth are classified; some members defined over extensions may be singular. The count is over \(\mathbb F_2\) and concerns *plane-filling* quintics, not arbitrary quintic pencils or fields of other sizes. The member-type comparison uses projective \(\mathbb F_2\)-equivalence; no abstract isomorphism, geometric isomorphism, or Jacobian isogeny equivalence is claimed. Historical priority may be affected by unindexed exact finite-field enumerations. Independent mathematical audit has not been conducted.
