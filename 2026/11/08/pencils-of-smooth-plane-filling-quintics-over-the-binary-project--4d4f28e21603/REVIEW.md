# Scientific review: smooth binary plane-filling quintic pencils

**Correctness (C) — PASS.** The exact coefficient domain is the \(14\)-dimensional binary space of ternary quintics vanishing at the seven rational points of the projective plane. The classification first exhausts its \(139\) projective equation orbits, then proves geometric smoothness on all three affine charts by exact Jacobian Gröbner ideals. A second independent implementation enumerates gradients over all projective points of \(\mathbb F_{2^r}\) for \(1\le r\le10\); the normalization genus formula and the low-degree repeated-factor argument make this extension bound exhaustive for quintic singularities. Both independently recover \(1{,}512\) smooth quintics, \(16{,}800\) all-smooth pencils, \(108\) projective pencil classes, and the \(96\)/\(12\) stabilizer-orbit split. The CSV gives \(108\) individually replayable projective orbit representatives. The claim that all base schemes contain seven *transverse* rational base points follows from the three nonzero differential vectors at each of the seven points and Bézout. No reliance on empirical inference or on unproved irreducibility assumptions remains.

**Originality (O) — PASS, with residual historical-priority uncertainty.** The closest published sources are Asgarli–Ghioca's 2022 work on the existence of smooth pencils, Asgarli–Ghioca–Reichstein's 2022 general smooth linear systems, and Asgarli–Ghioca's 2023 study of degree-\(q+3\) filling curves. The 2023 paper discusses a particular one-parameter family and the existence of a single smooth member (Proposition 4.5), not a census of *every* pencil with all three rational members smooth over \(\mathbb F_2\). The 2022 general papers do not impose seven common rational base points. Existing complete counts of individual smooth binary filling quintics (and binary filling-quartic pencils) do not state the second-order quintic pencil incidence classification or the \(108\) orbit/stabilizer stratification. Searches for synonymous descriptions, orbit tables, and equivalent stronger claims gave no covering record. A previously unindexed exact table or an implicit implementation of the individual-curve census could still affect historical priority; search failure alone does not prove uniqueness.

**Mathematical value (V) — PASS.** These are the complete smooth pencils in a geometrically canonical basepoint-constrained linear system at the first degree beyond the minimum for a smooth plane-filling curve. Their exact count and exhaustive projective classification answer a natural question distinct from counting individual curves. The cyclic-stabilizer characterization by member-projective-type coincidence and the transverse seven-point base locus supply structural, reusable information. The individual smooth-curve count is independently rechecked, not presented as the contribution.

Scientific limits: only the three ground-field members are required to be smooth; there is no claim that extension-field members remain smooth, or that abstract isomorphism classes or any larger field are classified. The CSV classification is under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\). No independent expert audit or independent validation has been performed.
