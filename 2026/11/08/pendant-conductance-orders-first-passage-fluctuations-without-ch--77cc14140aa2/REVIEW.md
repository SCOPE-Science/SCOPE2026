# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** With symmetric continuous-time edge jump rates, the core skeleton is unchanged by inserting independent rate-\(w\) excursions at the attachment vertex's core occupation clock. The conditional excursion count is Poisson with mean \(wL_v\) and each excursion lasts an independent rate-\(w\) exponential. Its exact Laplace transform proves the conditional mean and variance. The Poisson-exponential kernels have the semigroup identity \(s/(1+ts)\mapsto s/(1+s(t+u))\), preserve conditional means, and yield convex ordering by Jensen. Strict variance when \(\mathbb EL_v>0\) plus fixed means excludes either direction of first-order stochastic dominance. The zero-rate and infinite-rate limits use, respectively, a Poisson occurrence bound and an \(L^2\) conditional variance identity. The extracted code checks 167 exact rational and symbolic assertions against full finite Markov generators. Risks: using rate-one normalized random walks instead would invalidate the statement; the strict condition is not universal when the attachment is inaccessible before absorption.

**Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk.** Grebenkov–Tupikina, *Heterogeneous continuous time random walks* (arXiv:1712.01020), Sec. II and Sec. III, derive general heterogeneous propagators and discuss first-passage density changes; this is an important broad analytical precursor. Their examples concern nonuniform interval travel times and traps, not a pendant-edge weight family with mean invariance and convex order. Oliveira–Peres (arXiv:1807.06858), Introduction and hitting-time results, analyze discrete-time lazy walks and bounds rather than this symmetric-rate comparison. Zatloukal (arXiv:2012.15399), Sec. II, gives discrete-time local-time resolvents, but not this continuous-time pendant excursion law. Relevant mathematical-record searches found no reported equivalent explicit theorem. Generic Markov resolvent and excursion tools are broad enough to *derive* the statement after additional analysis, so priority remains a genuine residual uncertainty, not a certified absence.

**Value — PASS.** The exact result resolves a natural parameter sensitivity question for first-passage time distributions on arbitrary finite networks. It separates invariant means from strict convex-order reduction, gives an exact universal \(1/w\) variance law, and identifies a singular rare-trap regime where distributional convergence fails to preserve means. The all-core generality and an explicit mean-preserving kernel coupling make the comparison structural rather than a cheap numerical counterexample. It does not claim a new general theory of Markov excursions or imply the same behavior for other clock normalizations.
