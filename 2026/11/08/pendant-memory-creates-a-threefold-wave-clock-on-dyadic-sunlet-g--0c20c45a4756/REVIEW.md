# Research review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim specifies a sunlet with simple dyadic rim length \(n=2^r\ge4\), an arbitrary field \(\mathbb F_{2^f}\), and all two-time-level states. Direct block multiplication gives \((A+I)^2=cA\), with \(c=z^{-1}(z+1)^2\) in \(\mathbb F_q[z,z^{-1}]/((z+1)^n)\). The temporal identity \(T^3+I=(T+I)(\mathcal A+I)T\), with both exterior factors invertible, makes the fixed-space ranks exact: \(4\cdot2^j\) at time \(3\cdot2^j\), for \(0\le j\le r\). The only state whose period divides a power of two is zero. Consequently successive fixed-space subtraction proves the stated primitive-period counts, and positivity proves sharp order \(3n\). The two provided independent implementations passed: exhaustive orbits of all 65,536 states for \(n=4,q=2\) and exact rank checks for \(n=4,8,16,32,64\). The tests alone are not offered as infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Searches covered sunlet/corona/necklace terminology, characteristic-two second-order reversible waves, periodic-point counts, the \(3n\) clock, and published result summaries. The closest underlying dynamical sources were Macfarlane (2004) and Steeb–Hardy (2007). The inspected Steeb–Hardy reversible-rule section addresses ring discretizations and general reversibility, not an all-state exact orbit formula on sunlet graphs. The available Macfarlane abstract addresses weights of configurations for several one-dimensional rules, not the asserted sunlet graph census; because its full text was not obtained, this comparison carries a nontrivial residual priority risk. An existing result about majority updates on sunlets uses a different nonlinear rule, while the known reversible-wave census on bare cycles has no pendant leaves; neither statement implies the present characteristic-two threefold clock. No exact sunlet-wave table or covering theorem was identified. This is evidence of noncoverage among checked material, not a claim that failed searches certify priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sunlet is the natural one-leaf-per-site corona of a cycle, and powers-of-two circumferences are the canonical modular degeneration regime in characteristic two. The exact new period factor of three and complete primitive-period population, valid at all dyadic sizes and over every finite characteristic-two field, give a motivated dynamical comparison to the undecorated cycle. The theorem goes beyond a generic matrix-invertibility argument by obtaining precise ranks at all temporal scales and an exact orbit census. No generality beyond this natural family is implied.
