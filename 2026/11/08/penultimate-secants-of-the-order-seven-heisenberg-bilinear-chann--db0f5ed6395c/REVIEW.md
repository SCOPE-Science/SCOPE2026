# Self-review of the order-seven secant-dimension result

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact Jacobian specialization \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_7]\to\mathbb F_{29}\) sends a primitive seventh root to an element of exact order seven. All four matrices with \(2401\) rows and \(2320\) selected derivative columns have full column rank in a fresh replay of the included C++17 verifier, so each corresponding complex matrix has a nonzero maximal minor. The analytic two-scaling upper bound gives equality, and the source's triality symmetries and a direct-sum tangent argument cover all \(49\) ordered type pairs and all orders \(1\) through \(16\). An independent coefficient-sum implementation tested \(240\) derivative identities over \(\mathbb F_{29}\). Numerical experiments or isolated rows alone would not establish the theorem; the exact finite-field minors and lifting argument do.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** Nguyen--Nguyen's primary manuscript (sections 6--11) supplies exact widths only for \(q=2,3,4,5\), uniform second secants, and selected extension towers of characteristics \(2,3,5\). Its Conjecture 11.1 explicitly leaves \(q=7\) unresolved. The relevant 2017 group-based tripod literature states the general nondefectivity problem as a conjecture and proves a leaf-count bound inapplicable to the three-leaf sixteenth secant. The abstract invariant-secant bound also does not imply this result. Searches of the available published research index did not produce an equivalent exact \(q=7\), order-\(16\), all-type classification. Search nonappearance is not a proof of absolute historical novelty.

**Value — PASS.** This resolves all non-filling orders through the first expected filling threshold for the smallest prime characteristic omitted by the motivating paper's exact certificates, uniformly for all twisted types. It gives the natural terminal subgeneric dimension and codimension, substantially beyond the source's second-secant theorem, and a reusable finite-field certificate near the predicted generic-width transition. The result stops short of resolving the predicted generic width.

**Scientific limitations:** The proof uses exact finite-field computations and explicit source reductions. It does not determine the dimension at order \(17\), equations, singularities, decomposition uniqueness or the generic fusion width. Historical completeness and independent proof validation remain outstanding.
