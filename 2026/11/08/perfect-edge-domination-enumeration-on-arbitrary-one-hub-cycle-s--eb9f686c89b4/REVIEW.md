# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The external-edge condition is reconstructed as an exact bijection between perfect edge dominating edge sets and independent white-vertex subsets for which selected-degree conditions hold. Internal paths force distance-three separation of white positions, terminal leaves force nonwhite penultimate vertices, and the high-degree root is constrained to either zero or all but one white neighbours. The four resulting structural cases give the stated formula without overcounting. The three tail sequences are justified from explicit word counts and their base cases. Independent full edge-subset tests cover 468 finite instances; the unrestricted quantifiers follow from the proof, not the tests.

## Originality

PASS (provisional historical priority). The 2026 primary work presents general algorithms and a connected-cycle maximum conjecture, but its inspected definitions, main algorithms and concluding section do not give the stated multivariate arm-length product formula. Earlier one-tail formulas are actual special cases but do not establish the coupled degree-\(r+2\) root rule for arbitrarily many arms. Exact-result semantic searches covered arbitrary-armed unicyclic spider graphs, lollipop/tadpole variants, and edge-domination aliases. A prior one-tail claim was identified and excluded from novelty. No logically covering formula was found. Published but unindexed work remains a risk.

## Value

PASS. Perfect-edge-domination counting is algorithmically hard on general graphs, and these one-hub unicyclic spiders provide a nontrivial infinite family with arbitrarily many independent length parameters. The formula classifies every solution by its root configuration, not merely the minimum, and it identifies a sharp linear-to-exponential growth transition after subdividing every pendant edge once. It extends beyond the previously enumerated single-tail family and provides a reproducible benchmark for broader counting algorithms.

## Closest literature and limitations

Grippo--Lin--Moyano--Vera (arXiv:2607.03894v1) gives the counting setting, general series-parallel algorithms and open extremal question; one-tail lollipop work is subsumed, not claimed as new. Lu--Ko--Tang (2002) supplies earlier general algorithms. The formula does not solve the connected-cycle extremal conjecture for arbitrary spiders and does not cover graphs with multiple attachment roots. Historical priority is provisional.
