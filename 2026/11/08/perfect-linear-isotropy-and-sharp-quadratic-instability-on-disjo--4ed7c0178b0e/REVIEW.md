# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For the explicitly specified pure harmonic spaces and pairwise disjoint signed-coordinate caps, the harmonic eigenspaces exhaust the traceless quadratic dimension. Sign symmetry kills cross-sector terms and permutation symmetry makes each sector scalar. The proof checks the cap-moment integrals and their boundary derivative identity directly; these yield the exact eigenvalues, the strict positive gap, and the asymptotic coefficient. The standalone checker returned `AXIS_CAPS_SPECTRUM_CHECKS_PASS 490`, but the theorem rests on the analytic derivation and its fixed-dimension Taylor expansion. Limits: no analysis of overlapping caps, higher harmonics, finite noisy samples or uniform dimension growth.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk
The closest main-text comparison is Michel–Plattner–Seibert, arXiv:2103.14650v1, whose spherical Slepian kernel construction and commuting cap operators are acknowledged rather than re-claimed. Zandieh–Han–Avron, arXiv:2202.12995v1, studies almost-optimal randomized whole-sphere reconstruction, not restricted cross-polytope observation domains. Snowball–Olver, arXiv:2012.11493v1, develops single-cap PDE polynomial bases. Simons–Dahlen–Wieczorek, arXiv:math/0408424, provides the earlier general Slepian setting; its original PDF was inspected in relevant sections and searched for axis-cap special cases, although not read line-by-line. Comparisons with published hemisphere spectral laws, global quadratic harmonic fourth norms and polyhedral cap data did not identify an equivalent statement. The general Slepian formulation makes this a computable matrix problem but does not itself supply the two exact spectral levels, positive fourth-moment gap, and narrow-cap coefficient. Risk: these specialized formulas could occur implicitly in earlier signed-permutation harmonic or multicap literature; search absence alone does not certify novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is naturally motivated by structured missing observations and by the temptation to interpret coordinate isotropy as stable polynomial recovery. The result provides a complete all-dimensional two-band classification and proves the surprising sharp divergence in degree-two conditioning even though degree-one conditioning is exactly one at every allowed cap radius. The exact coefficient \((d+1)/4\) quantifies a real obstruction to extending second-moment isotropy guarantees to quadratic harmonics. Scope restrictions are explicit; the result does not imply finite-sample noise bounds or an optimal design theorem over arbitrary regions.
