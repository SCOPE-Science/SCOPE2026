# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns the explicitly defined perfect-matching complex of the sixteen-vertex, thirty-two-edge four-cube. The proof enumerates all 272 perfect matchings, all 30,561 faces, and independently checks all 41,025 graph matchings by a recursive perfect-completion test. Two sequential-toggle discrete-Morse matchings are verified acyclic by exhaustive directed Hasse-graph topological sorts. The forward matching has only 124 critical three-cells and 91 critical four-cells (plus a basepoint); exact signed integer Morse-boundary reduction gives seventy unit pivots and zero residual matrix. The Hurewicz classification of the four-cell attaching maps therefore yields precisely \(54\) three-spheres and \(21\) four-spheres. Reverse-order integral Morse calculations and direct unreduced mod-two boundary ranks agree. Executing `verify.py` reproduces all finite checks.

## Originality — PASS against inspected sources
The same simplicial construction is studied for honeycomb families in Bayer–Jelić Milutinović–Vega (arXiv:2209.02803) and for rectangular two-row strips and polygonal line tilings in Chandrakar–Singh (arXiv:2407.05809). Their inspected theorems, proofs, and concluding scope statements do not cover the four-cube \(Q_4\cong C_4\square C_4\), a four-regular toroidal square grid. Agarwal–Basak (arXiv:2608.30601) studies cubic Möbius ladders instead. Kiwi–Loebl (arXiv:1406.3317) studies toroidal-grid perfect-matching parity types and bijections, not the homotopy of the simplicial complex generated by them. Published records on the rectangular four-by-four grid's *ordinary* matching complex and the cube's independence complex have different face conditions; they imply neither this integral homotopy type nor the attaching-map calculation. Targeted equivalent-formulation and broader-coverage searches uncovered no equivalent theorem. Non-indexed equivalent computations remain possible, and negative searches do not prove global novelty.

## Value — PASS
The four-cube is the smallest four-regular hypercube and is simultaneously the canonical four-by-four square torus. The result computes a natural but nontrivial perfect-matching-complex homotopy type beyond the honeycomb, ladder, and line-tiling examples for which formulas were available. Its 272 facets form a pure seven-dimensional complex, yet there are nonzero homotopy-sphere summands in *two* strictly lower degrees, showing non-shellability. The precise integral homotopy decomposition is not implied by counts of perfect matchings, an Euler characteristic, or parity identities. It supplies a tractable reference example for future work on perfect matching complexes of periodic grids and on the distinction between matching and perfect-matching complexes; no general family formula is claimed.

## Remaining scientific limits
The proof does not supply an equivariant action, a homotopy classification for \(Q_d\) with other dimensions, or a theorem about all toroidal square grids. The literature search cannot exclude unpublished or unindexed exact equivalents. Independent validation has not been performed.
