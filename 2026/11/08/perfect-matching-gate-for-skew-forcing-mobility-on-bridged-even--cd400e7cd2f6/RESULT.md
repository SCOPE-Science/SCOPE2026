# Perfect-matching gate for skew-forcing mobility on bridged even-cycle cacti

## Finding
For every finite tree \(T\) on \(k\ge2\) vertices \(1,\ldots,k\), and integers \(r_i\ge2\), construct \(G(T;\mathbf r)\) from vertex-disjoint even cycles of lengths \(2r_i\), using vertices \(v_{i,0},\ldots,v_{i,2r_i-1}\) in cyclic order, by adding precisely the bridge \(v_{i,0}v_{j,0}\) whenever \(ij\in E(T)\). The resulting graph is a connected simple cactus. Let \(O_i=\{v_{i,1},v_{i,3},\ldots,v_{i,2r_i-1}\}\).

**Theorem.** The skew zero forcing number is at least \(k\), and equality holds **if and only if** the bridge tree \(T\) has a perfect matching. If \(T\) has a perfect matching, the complete family of minimum skew zero forcing sets is
\[
\mathcal B(G)=\bigl\{\{u_1,\ldots,u_k\}:u_i\in O_i\ \text{for every }i\bigr\}.
\]
In particular, the number of minimum sets is \(N=\prod_{i=1}^k r_i\). Their one-token-exchange graph is exactly \(\square_{i=1}^k K_{r_i}\), with \(N\sum_i(r_i-1)/2\) edges, regular degree \(\sum_i(r_i-1)\), and radius and diameter \(k\). Their one-token-sliding graph is the edgeless graph \(NK_1\).

As a realization consequence, **every Cartesian product of a positive even number of nontrivial complete graphs is the token-exchange graph of minimum skew zero forcing sets of some connected cactus whose token-sliding graph has no edges**. This takes the bridge tree to be a path on that even number of cycle blocks.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, undirected and simple. A skew color change is allowed whenever **any** vertex, whether currently black or white, has exactly one white neighbor; that neighbor becomes black. A skew zero forcing set is an initial set of black vertices from which a sequence of these changes makes all vertices black. The minimum possible initial cardinality is \(Z^-(G)\). A token exchange replaces one black vertex with a different vertex while remaining among minimum skew forcing sets; a token slide additionally requires the replaced vertices to be adjacent in \(G\). The cycle blocks have even lengths at least four; all interblock bridges meet the designated index-zero port of their cycle. No exact value of \(Z^-(G)\) is asserted when \(T\) lacks a perfect matching, beyond the rigorous strict lower bound \(Z^-(G)>k\).

## Proof
**1. A forest forcing criterion.** A finite forest \(F\) can be skew-forced from the empty black set exactly when it has a perfect matching. For the forward implication, every forcing vertex has no white neighbor immediately after it forces, hence can never force again. Starting empty and coloring every vertex requires exactly \(|V(F)|\) forces, one performed by each vertex. Assign to each vertex its unique forced neighbor. This is a permutation: each vertex forces once and is forced exactly once. Every cycle in this directed permutation would give a cycle in the underlying forest unless it has length two, so all cycles have length two. Their vertex pairs are edges of a perfect matching. For the reverse implication, take a matched leaf edge \(uv\) with leaf \(u\). Initially \(u\) can force \(v\); the remaining forest after deleting \(u,v\) has a perfect matching, so inductively it can be forced without needing \(u,v\) to act. Once the remaining vertices are black, \(v\) can force the leaf \(u\). The empty forest is the base case.

**2. Disjoint parity forts give the lower bound.** Call \(F\ne\varnothing\) a skew fort when every vertex has either zero or at least two neighbors in \(F\). No skew forcing set disjoint from \(F\) can succeed: before the first vertex of \(F\) becomes black, no vertex has a unique white neighbor in \(F\). For each cycle block \(i\), \(O_i\) is a skew fort. Every even-indexed vertex of that block sees exactly two vertices of \(O_i\), every odd-indexed vertex sees none, and bridge endpoints are even ports, so other blocks see none. Because these \(k\) forts are disjoint, every skew forcing set contains at least one vertex in each \(O_i\), hence has size at least \(k\). Every candidate of cardinality \(k\) is necessarily \(S=\{u_i:u_i\in O_i\}\), choosing one odd-indexed vertex per block.

**3. Reduction to perfect matching of the bridge tree.** For any such candidate \(S\), let \(H=G-S\). Deleting one vertex from each even cycle breaks that cycle into a path; joining these paths by the original bridges gives a tree \(H\). Relative to its port \(v_{i,0}\), each such path consists of two arms with an **even** number of vertices on each arm, since the removed vertex has odd index. In every perfect matching of \(H\), each arm must therefore be matched internally: matching its port to a first arm vertex would strand an odd number of vertices in that arm. Thus the port must be matched along a bridge. The bridges chosen at ports consequently form a perfect matching of \(T\). Conversely, given any perfect matching of \(T\), match all ports along its corresponding bridges, and independently match consecutive vertices in each even-sized arm; this yields a perfect matching of \(H\). Hence \(H\) has a perfect matching if and only if \(T\) has one, regardless of which \(u_i\in O_i\) were deleted.

The white-neighbor counts of a vertex in \(H\) are exactly its white-neighbor counts in \(G\) with \(S\) initially black; the already-black neighbors in \(S\) never affect a legal move. Consequently, \(S\) skew-forces \(G\) if and only if the empty set skew-forces \(H\). By step 1 this holds if and only if \(H\), equivalently \(T\), has a perfect matching. Together with step 2 this proves the precise equality criterion and the complete classification when equality holds.

**4. Exchange and sliding graphs.** When \(T\) has a perfect matching, minimum sets are tuples of independently chosen elements of the \(O_i\), so a legal one-vertex exchange changes exactly one coordinate, and every such change is feasible. The resulting graph is the Cartesian product of cliques \(\square_i K_{r_i}\), giving the stated vertex count, edge count and degree. Graph distance is Hamming distance, so because every \(r_i\ge2\), the eccentricity of every configuration is \(k\). No two distinct vertices within \(O_i\) are adjacent in \(G\), and the only bridges join even-indexed ports. Thus no exchange is a token slide; the sliding graph consists of \(N\) isolated vertices. Every even positive number of factors is realized by a path bridge tree, which has a perfect matching.

## Verification
The independent programs `check_bitmask.py` (integer adjacency masks, simultaneous forcing waves, exhaustive candidate sets) and `check_sets.py` (tuple-labeled vertices, set-based propagation, explicit relocation graph with all-pairs shortest paths) require only Python's standard library. Reproduce with `python3 check_bitmask.py > check_bitmask.out.json` and `python3 check_sets.py > check_sets.out.json`, then compare the outputs. Both test six explicit constructions: three two-cycle cacti (including unequal cycle lengths), a four-cycle-block matched path, a three-block unmatched path, and an unmatched four-block star. Every initial set of size less than \(k\) and every \(k\)-set is tested; minimum-set families, exchange/slide counts, and, in the second program, all pairwise exchange distances are compared with the theorem. These finite checks corroborate but do not replace the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work
DeAlba (2014) develops skew zero forcing, matching relations for trees and certain bipartite and unicyclic graphs, and discusses cactus examples and the scalar skew-forcing number of a necklace of diamonds. Its bipartite-cactus rank corollary has an additional rank-equality hypothesis and does not classify the full family of minimum configurations or their exchanges. The forest matching criterion in step 1 is an elementary known structural mechanism and is **not** claimed as an original discovery. The present sources are connected cacti with at least two vertex-disjoint even cycles joined at their ports; the exact bridge-tree perfect-matching gate and complete mobility classification do not follow from those published statements. Bong et al. (2025) study token-exchange and token-slide reconfiguration of minimum skew forcing sets. Their Cartesian-product constructions from **disjoint unions** do not imply an isomorphic reconfiguration graph from the **connected** bridged-cycle sources here; the cycle and tree examples in their relevant sections do not state this family-wide characterization. The novelty claim is confined to the bridged-cycle classification and its connected-source realization consequence. Searches cannot certify global historical priority.

## Limitations
The bridge graph must be a tree and every bridge in a cycle block must meet its designated even-indexed port. Without a perfect matching of the bridge tree, the theorem asserts only a strict lower bound and no exact optimum or reconfiguration structure. The all-order proof is symbolic; the computations are exhaustive only on the six stated finite examples. Searches of accessible research records and relevant literature did not establish a prior equivalent statement, but unindexed results and uninspected portions of related papers remain possible. No independent scholarly audit has been performed.
