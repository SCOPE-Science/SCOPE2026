# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact character-pair proof establishes frequency projection injectivity, the root-of-unity orthogonality dichotomy, and an identification of the generated Fourier support with the annihilator of the common level-set period subgroup. The claim counts frequency sets, not equivalence classes, and handles the endpoints including constant and aperiodic functions. A finite integer-only cyclotomic checker tests all graph sets and all possible frequency subsets through the stated small orders; exhaustive cases are distinguished from representative and histogram-only checks. The proof, not the checker, establishes the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources

Tan and Zhang (arXiv:2610.02218v1, Theorem 1.1) classify which square-free cyclic sets *admit* a spectrum, and their Lemma 2.1 supplies the necessary root-of-unity comparison. They do not state the complete period-dependent **partner graph classification**, spectrum count, or Möbius distribution. The claimed theorem also includes nonsquare-free orders. Inspected passages of Malikiosis (arXiv:2005.05800, Sections 3 and 8–10) analyze general spectral-pair and cyclotomic constraints; the exact graph-period formula was not identified in those passages. The rest of that longer manuscript was not exhaustively assessed. Earlier-equivalent-formulation risk remains, particularly in combinatorial Hadamard pairs and harmonic pairs.

## Value — PASS

The number of orthogonal character bases is a natural, nontrivial invariant of a spectral set: a yes/no spectrality theorem cannot distinguish \(q\) bases from \(q^m\) bases. The classification identifies the controlling structural statistic (the full translation-period subgroup) and gives the exact distribution of this invariant for every coprime prime-fiber family, including repeated-prime cyclic orders. This provides a complete natural family rather than a single small computation. It does not claim a general theorem for non-graph supports.

## Sources and limitations

The strongest source implication checked is Tan–Zhang's transversal characterization, not merely a title comparison. The root-of-unity coefficient lemma is explicitly credited. The present classification uses an additional zero-support/annihilator argument to characterize *all* compatible partner graphs and invert their multiplicities. A previously unnoticed equivalent treatment remains an originality risk; independent audit and general composite-fiber extensions are not claimed.
