# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The elementary identity \(F^2=(f\circ g,g\circ f)\) gives invariant periodic cores. The restriction of \(g\) to these cores has a concrete inverse, and the conjugacy with \(T(x,z)=(h(z),x)\) proves **all** iteration-count identities, rather than extrapolating from finite examples. The formulas include both unequal alphabet sizes and one-element domains. Two independently organized finite-census programs corroborate the theorem, with the graph program also verifying a boundary counterexample.

## Originality
PASS, subject to explicitly stated residual risks. Searches for bipartite two-cycle square identities, cross-only maps, off-diagonal iteration, and periodic-point zeta formulae, along with targeted published-finding corpus comparisons and historically accessible claims, did not identify a statement covering the arbitrary finite-map periodic-core normal form. The most relevant primary full texts were checked for their actual hypotheses: Kuhlman et al. establish period restrictions for threshold systems, Turau's tree papers classify and count fixed and pure two-cycle configurations, and Aledo et al. treat specified MAX/MIN Boolean rules. None inspected states the universal formula that yields mixed period-two populations directly from a fixed-point count. However, a classic equivalent under alternating dynamics, conjugate coupled maps, or symbolic-dynamics terminology could exist outside the inspected material. Nonappearance in search is not proof of novelty.

## Value
PASS. The formula gives the *entire* periodic-point spectrum and a canonical recurrent normal form for arbitrary cross-only dynamics, not only a fixed-point identity. In an important natural subclass, odd-degree bipartite synchronous majority systems, it converts the fixed-point census into the number of every immediate primitive two-cycle, including mixed cycles that the cited tree counting papers do not enumerate en bloc. It also exposes a sharp boundary: retaining states at ties can invalidate the square law even on the four-vertex path. The result does not need a stronger graph theorem to make this gap useful.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kuhlman et al. (arXiv:1108.2974), Turau (arXiv:2202.01580; arXiv:2312.13769), Aledo et al. (DOI 10.1155/2020/9708347). The generalized identity may have an unpublished or differently named antecedent; no independent originality adjudication was undertaken. The theorem applies only to finite cross-only systems. The finite programs are corroboration, not the proof.
