# Periodic Rhaly modulation: a trace-class Gram sum rule and a Toeplitz modulus

## Finding
For a nonzero complex sequence \(c=(c_1,\dots,c_q)\) of period \(q\ge2\), extended periodically, let the discrete Cesàro operator \(C\) and the modulated Rhaly operator \(R_c\) on \(\ell^2(\mathbb N)\) be
\[
 (Cx)_n=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n x_j,\qquad
 (R_cx)_n=\frac{c_n}{n}\sum_{j=1}^n x_j.
\]
Write \(\mu=q^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^q|c_r|^2>0\), \(b_r=|c_r|^2-\mu\) (extended periodically), and \(G_c=R_c^*R_c-\mu C^*C\). Then:

1. \(G_c\) is self-adjoint and trace class, with the **exact conditional sum rule**
\[
\operatorname{Tr}G_c=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_n}{n}
=-\frac1q\sum_{r=1}^q b_r\,\psi(r/q),
\]
where \(\psi\) is the digamma function. The scalar series converges by periodic mean-zero cancellation; absolute convergence is not asserted.

2. The full Fredholm essential spectra and essential norms are
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(R_c^*R_c)=[0,4\mu],\quad
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(|R_c|)=[0,2\sqrt\mu],\quad
\|R_c\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=2\sqrt\mu.
\]

3. Let \(\mathfrak T\) be the \(C^*\)-algebra generated by all bounded Hardy-space Toeplitz operators and \(\mathfrak Q\) its commutator ideal. Then \(|R_c|\in\mathfrak Q\) **for every** periodic \(c\), while
\[
R_c\in\mathfrak T\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_1=\cdots=c_q.
\]
The last equivalence is an application, explicitly credited below, of Sang's published Rhaly membership criterion, not a new criterion for arbitrary coefficients.

Two instructive sharp examples are \(c_n=(-1)^n\), for which \(|R_c|=|C|\) exactly but \(R_c\notin\mathfrak T\), and \((c_1,c_2)=(1,0)\), for which \(\mu=1/2\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(|R_c|)=[0,\sqrt2]\), and \(\operatorname{Tr}G_c=(\log2)/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All Hilbert spaces and operators are complex. The index set is \(\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}\). The \(q\) coefficients need not be nonzero individually, distinct, real, or of equal modulus; only the vector \(c\) is nonzero. Period \(q\) need not be minimal. For a bounded operator on a separable Hilbert space, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}\) means the Fredholm essential spectrum (spectrum of its image in the Calkin algebra), and \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{ess}}\) is distance in operator norm to the compact operators. The canonical coefficient identification \(H^2(\mathbb D)\simeq\ell^2(\mathbb N)\) is used when discussing the Toeplitz algebra.

## Proof
**1. Nuclear Gram cancellation and trace.** The discrete Hardy inequality gives \(\|C\|\le2\), so \(R_c=D_cC\) is bounded, where \(D_c\) is diagonal with entries \(c_n\). Thus
\[
(G_c)_{ij}=\sum_{n\ge\max(i,j)}\frac{b_n}{n^2}=:F_{\max(i,j)}.
\]
Since \(b_n\) is periodic with mean zero, all its finite consecutive partial sums are uniformly bounded. Discrete Abel summation against the decreasing sequence \(n^{-2}\) gives \(|F_m|\le K m^{-2}\), for a constant \(K\) depending only on \(c\). Decompose this matrix into the lower triangle (including the diagonal) and the strict upper triangle:
\[
 G_c=\sum_{i\ge1}F_i\,e_i\otimes(e_1+\cdots+e_i)^*
 +\sum_{j\ge2}F_j\,(e_1+\cdots+e_{j-1})\otimes e_j^*.
\]
Each summand is rank one. The sum of its trace norms is at most \(\sum_i |F_i|(\sqrt i+\sqrt{i-1})<\infty\). Consequently this is a nuclear decomposition and \(G_c\in\mathcal S_1\). The trace equals the absolutely convergent diagonal series \(\sum_iF_i\).

For each positive integer \(N\), reversing a **finite** summation and the absolutely convergent tail yields
\[
\sum_{i=1}^NF_i=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{b_n}{n}+NF_{N+1}.
\]
The last term is \(O(N^{-1})\). The first term converges by the Dirichlet test, proving the first trace equality. Grouping the periodic harmonic series by residue class and using \(\psi(z+1)-\psi(z)=1/z\) yields the digamma identity, because the common divergent term cancels when \(\sum_rb_r=0\).

**2. The essential spectral interval of \(C^*C\).** We give the continuum reduction to make the spectral endpoint explicit. The matrix of \(C^*C\) is \(A_{\max(i,j)}\), where \(A_m=\sum_{n\ge m}n^{-2}=m^{-1}+O(m^{-2})\). Let \(H\) be the continuous Hardy operator on \(L^2(1,\infty)\),
\[
 (Hf)(x)=x^{-1}\int_1^x f(y)\,dy.
\]
Then \(H^*H\) has kernel \(1/\max(x,y)\). Let \(J:\ell^2(\mathbb N)\to L^2(1,\infty)\) map \(e_n\) to the indicator of \([n,n+1)\). The matrix entries of \(J^*H^*HJ\) differ from \(1/\max(i,j)\) by at most \(1/\max(i,j)^2\). Hence
\[
 C^*C-J^*H^*HJ\in\mathcal S_2,
\]
because \(\sum_{i,j\ge1}\max(i,j)^{-4}=\sum_{m\ge1}(2m-1)m^{-4}<\infty\).

The unitary logarithmic coordinate change \((Uf)(u)=e^{u/2}f(e^u)\) transforms \(H^*H\) into the integral operator on \(L^2(0,\infty)\) with kernel \(e^{-|u-v|/2}\). This is the half-line compression of convolution on \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) with Fourier multiplier
\[
 m(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-|s|/2}e^{-its}\,ds=\frac1{1/4+t^2}.
\]
For each real \(t\), smoothly cut off and normalize \(e^{itu}\) on a logarithmic interval of length \(L\) positioned beyond \(L^2\). Fourier concentration shows that these weakly null unit vectors are Weyl sequences for the half-line operator at \(m(t)\). Their inverse-logarithmic images have derivatives tending to zero in \(L^2(1,\infty)\), so projecting them onto the unit intervals \([n,n+1)\) changes their norm by \(o(1)\). The compact Hilbert–Schmidt matrix difference above transfers these Weyl sequences to \(C^*C\). Thus every \(m(t)\in(0,4]\) belongs to \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(C^*C)\), and closedness adds zero. The discrete Hardy inequality yields \(0\le C^*C\le4I\). Therefore
\[
 \sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(C^*C)=[0,4].
\]
Since \(G_c\) is trace class, Weyl invariance gives \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(R_c^*R_c)=[0,4\mu]\). Applying continuous functional calculus in the Calkin algebra yields the essential spectrum of \(|R_c|\) and the exact essential norm stated above.

**3. Toeplitz-modulus separation.** Sang proved that \(C\in\mathfrak Q\), and that a bounded Rhaly operator with coefficients \(a_n\) belongs to \(\mathfrak T\) exactly when
\[
\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\sup_{\substack{N\ge1\\0\le k\le\delta N}}
N\sum_{n\ge N}|a_{n+k}-a_n|^2=0.
\]
The commutator ideal contains the compact operators (the shift's rank-one defect generates them). Thus \(R_c^*R_c=\mu C^*C+G_c\in\mathfrak Q\), and positivity plus continuous functional calculus gives \(|R_c|\in\mathfrak Q\).

If \(c\) is constant, \(R_c\) is a scalar multiple of \(C\). If \(c\) is not constant, write \(a_n=c_n/n\). Then
\[
 a_{n+1}-a_n=\frac{c_{n+1}-c_n}{n}+O(n^{-2}),
\]
uniformly in \(n\). Periodic averaging implies
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}N\sum_{n\ge N}|a_{n+1}-a_n|^2
 =\frac1q\sum_{r=1}^q|c_{r+1}-c_r|^2>0,
\]
where \(c_{q+1}=c_1\). The permitted choice \(k=1\) in Sang's criterion therefore rules out \(R_c\in\mathfrak T\). This completes the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Sang (2026, arXiv:2608.25740), Theorems 1.1–1.2, proves \(C\in\mathfrak Q\) and characterizes bounded Rhaly membership in the Toeplitz algebra. The membership dichotomy here is a specialization of that published result. Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Partington (2024, arXiv:2408.03182) study Rhaly spectra and invariant subspaces, including bounded coefficient diagonal factorizations. Brown, Halmos, and Shields (1965) establish basic Cesàro spectral theory. The present quantitative contribution is the trace-class **Gram averaging**, its exact residue-class trace and essential singular-spectrum interval, together with the modulus/Toeplitz contrast; it does not claim a new general membership criterion.

## Limitations
The trace sum rule assumes a bounded periodic coefficient profile, not general almost-periodic profiles. In particular, it does **not** assert that \(|R_c|-\sqrt\mu|C|\) is trace class, nor that the actual operator norm always equals the essential norm. The published Toeplitz criterion and the standard Hardy inequality are used as inputs. A broader pre-existing Gram averaging theorem under alternative terminology may cover part of this calculation; no independent publication-priority or mathematical audit has been performed.
