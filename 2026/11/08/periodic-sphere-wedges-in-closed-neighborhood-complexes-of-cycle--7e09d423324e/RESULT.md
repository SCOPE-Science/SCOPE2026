# Periodic sphere wedges in closed-neighborhood complexes of cycle-deleted complete bipartite graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 7\), let \(G_n\) have two vertex shores \(U=\{u_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\) and \(V=\{v_j:j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\), with an edge \(u_iv_j\) exactly when \(j\notin\{i,i+1\}\). Thus \(G_n\) is obtained from the complete bipartite graph on two shores of size \(n\) by deleting one spanning Hamiltonian cycle. Define its closed-neighborhood complex \(X_n\) to consist of all vertex sets contained in the closed graph neighborhood of some vertex.

Put \(b_n=n^2-4n+1\). The complete integral homotopy type of \(X_n\) is
\[
X_n\simeq
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\bigvee^{b_n}S^2\vee\bigvee^{4}S^{2k-2},&n=3k,\quad k\ge3,\\
\displaystyle\bigvee^{b_n}S^2\vee\bigvee^{2}S^{2k-1},&n=3k+1,\quad k\ge2,\\
\displaystyle\bigvee^{b_n}S^2\vee\bigvee^{2}S^{2k-1},&n=3k+2,\quad k\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, \(X_n\) is simply connected and has torsion-free integral homology. The extra spheres have dimension and multiplicity depending periodically on \(n\) modulo three. For instance, \(X_7\simeq\bigvee^{22}S^2\vee\bigvee^2S^3\), whereas \(X_9\simeq\bigvee^{46}S^2\vee\bigvee^4S^4\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple; indices are read modulo \(n\). A closed graph neighborhood contains the center vertex and every adjacent vertex. The simplicial complex is generated by the full simplices on the closed neighborhoods. The theorem concerns all and only integers \(n\ge7\). All homotopy equivalences are non-equivariant, with integral, not only field-coefficient, conclusions.

## Proof
**1. The two-shore cover.** Let \(A\) be the subcomplex of \(X_n\) generated by the neighborhoods centered in \(U\), and \(B\) that generated by those centered in \(V\). Thus \(X_n=A\cup B\). A face of \(A\) contains at most one \(U\)-vertex, and a face of \(B\) contains at most one \(V\)-vertex. It follows that \(A\cap B\) is exactly the original bipartite graph \(G_n\), viewed as a one-dimensional simplicial complex: a mixed pair \(\{u_i,v_j\}\) lies in both precisely when it is an edge of \(G_n\).

**2. Retract each shore.** The star of \(u_i\) in \(A\) is the simplex on \(\{u_i\}\cup (V\setminus\{v_i,v_{i+1}\})\), and no other maximal simplex of \(A\) contains \(u_i\). Removing the cone apex \(u_i\) by elementary collapses relative to its base, for every \(i\), gives a simplicial collapse from \(A\) to the complex \(L_n\) on \(V\), generated by the sets \(V\setminus\{v_i,v_{i+1}\}\). The identical argument collapses \(B\) onto an isomorphic copy of \(L_n\) on \(U\).

**3. Identify the shore homotopy type.** Recall that the combinatorial Alexander dual of a complex \(K\) on a fixed \(n\)-element ground set is \(K^*=\{\sigma:V\setminus\sigma\notin K\}\). The minimal nonfaces of the independence complex \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\) are exactly the adjacent pairs of the \(n\)-cycle. Consequently
\[
L_n=\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)^*.
\]
The classical cycle-independence calculation (Kozlov; see Adamaszek, cited below) gives
\[
\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigvee^2 S^{k-1},&n=3k,\\
S^{k-1},&n=3k+1,\\
S^k,&n=3k+2.
\end{cases}
\]
Combinatorial Alexander duality over \(\mathbb Z\) says \(\widetilde H_i(L_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{n-i-3}(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n);\mathbb Z)\). Thus the reduced integral homology of \(L_n\) is concentrated in degree \(2k-2\) with rank two when \(n=3k\), and degree \(2k-1\) with rank one in the other two congruence classes.

The minimum vertex-cover size of \(C_n\) is \(\lceil n/2\rceil\ge4\) for \(n\ge7\). Therefore every subset of \(V\) containing at most three vertices is a face of \(L_n\): its complement still contains an edge of \(C_n\). In particular \(L_n\) contains the full two-skeleton of a simplex, and is simply connected. Its homology is free and concentrated in a single degree at least three. Successive applications of the Hurewicz theorem, followed by the homological Whitehead theorem applied to maps from a wedge of spheres representing a homology basis, show that \(L_n\) is homotopy equivalent to the corresponding wedge of spheres. This conclusion is integral; it is not an inference from finite-field ranks alone.

**4. Split the intersection.** The graph \(G_n\) is connected: any two \(U\)-vertices have a common \(V\)-neighbor when \(n\ge7\), and all vertices have positive degree. It has \(2n\) vertices and \(n(n-2)\) edges, so its first Betti number is \(b_n=n(n-2)-2n+1=n^2-4n+1\). Hence \(G_n\simeq\bigvee^{b_n}S^1\). Both \(A\) and \(B\) are simply connected by Step 3, so the two inclusion maps \(G_n\to A,B\) are nullhomotopic (their domain is a graph). The diagram of simplicial inclusions \(A\leftarrow G_n\rightarrow B\) consists of cofibrations, so the ordinary union computes its homotopy pushout. Replacing both maps by nullhomotopic maps yields
\[
X_n\simeq A\vee B\vee\Sigma G_n
\simeq L_n\vee L_n\vee\bigvee^{b_n}S^2.
\]
Substituting the three cases of Step 3 gives exactly the stated classification. This also proves simple connectivity and torsion-freeness.

## Verification
A self-contained Python 3 program in `checks/verify.py` constructs every neighborhood simplex for \(n=7,8,9,10,11\). It checks the exact equality \(A\cap B=G_n\), the two Alexander-dual shore descriptions, the complete two-skeleton, and the full reduced simplicial Betti numbers over \(\mathbb F_2\). The observed nonzero ranks are \((\beta_2,\beta_3)=(22,2)\) for \(n=7\), \((33,2)\) for \(n=8\), \((\beta_2,\beta_4)=(46,4)\) for \(n=9\), \((\beta_2,\beta_5)=(61,2)\) for \(n=10\), and \((78,2)\) in degrees \(2,5\) for \(n=11\). The recorded output is `checks/verification_output.txt`. Run `python3 checks/verify.py` from the package root. The program uses only the Python standard library. The unrestricted homotopy theorem rests on the proof above; modular tests are corroboration only.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita's 2025 preprint introduces systematic structural relationships for closed-neighborhood complexes, including an Alexander-dual description and a suspension relation to canonical graph double covers. Its relevant results do not identify the closed-neighborhood homotopy type of these cycle-deleted bipartite graphs. Adamaszek's paper records the previously known three-step recurrence for independence complexes of cycles; that classical result and combinatorial Alexander duality supply the shore homology here, but not the new two-shore homotopy-pushout calculation. A recent treatment of linear resolutions of **open** neighborhood complexes and results about scale-two Rips complexes of bipartite-factor complements concern different functors and do not determine this closed-neighborhood wedge decomposition. Matching-deleted complete bipartite graphs provide a distinct nearby deletion model; they have one forbidden edge per vertex rather than the two linked cyclically here.

## Limitations
No statement is made for \(n<7\), for other deleted spanning subgraphs, or for equivariant decompositions under graph automorphisms. The proof establishes nonequivariant homotopy types, not explicit simplicial elementary collapses of the whole union onto the displayed wedge. The source comparison cannot exclude unpublished or unindexed stronger results, and independent external mathematical review has not been performed.
