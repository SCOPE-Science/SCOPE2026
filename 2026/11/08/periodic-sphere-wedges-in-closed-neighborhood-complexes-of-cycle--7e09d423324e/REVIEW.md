# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** Each closed-neighborhood facet is specified by the graph formula, and splitting by the shore of its center gives two subcomplexes whose intersection is the graph itself. Collapsing the unique apex of each same-shore facet produces the Alexander dual of the cycle independence complex. For \(n\ge7\), its complete two-skeleton guarantees simple connectivity, and integral Alexander duality plus Kozlov's classical cycle formula supplies homology concentrated in a single positive degree. The Hurewicz and homological Whitehead theorems turn this into a sphere wedge. Both gluing maps from the connected one-dimensional intersection are nullhomotopic; the homotopy pushout therefore splits. Direct face enumeration and mod-two boundary calculations agree for \(7\le n\le11\). No finite experiment is used as proof of an infinite claim.

**Originality (PASS, with residual risk).** The closest examined literature is Matsushita's general closed-neighborhood theory, Adamaszek's cycle-independence recurrence, and Ehrenborg–Hetyei's Alexander duality. The ingredients are credited; their stated results do not assert the cyclically forbidden bipartite graph classification or the unsuspended wedge decomposition. Published-result searches found nearby open-neighborhood, graph-Rips, and matching-deleted-graph claims, but none implies this closed-neighborhood theorem. Other unindexed or inaccessible stronger work may exist.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** Removing a spanning Hamiltonian cycle is a canonical next deletion model after removing a perfect matching from a dense bipartite graph. The resulting integral homotopy type exhibits a growing quadratic family of two-spheres together with higher-dimensional sphere multiplicities controlled by a three-periodic cycle invariant. A homology-only computation would not by itself establish this unsuspended wedge; the nullhomotopic-gluing argument supplies the structural step. The theorem does not assert results for different forbidden patterns.
