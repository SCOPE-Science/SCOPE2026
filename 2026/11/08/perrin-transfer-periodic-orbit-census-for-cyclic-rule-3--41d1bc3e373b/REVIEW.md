# Scientific review of the cyclic Rule 3 census

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is deduced from explicit Boolean identities, not from an observed finite pattern. The relation \(F^3=SF\) and the characterization of the recurrent image by cyclic avoidance of \(101\) establish the one-update maximum entrance time. On the recurrent image, \(F^2=S\) converts even temporal fixed-point conditions to spatial repetitions. Odd temporal fixed-point conditions reduce to independent dominating sets on a step-cycle of odd length; that step is coprime to the length, so the graph is an ordinary cycle. The two transfer matrices encode the required local constraints, and Möbius inversion counts primitive orbits. The independent programs agree with the formulas on the stated finite ranges. They do not prove the unbounded theorem, which is supplied by the algebraic and combinatorial argument.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority limits
The closest **same-rule** antecedent is the pointwise Rule 3 iteration formula on H. Fukś's author-maintained page, supported by his 2003 work on preimage sequences. It implies the elementary shift identity and is not presented as a new part of the finding. Binder's 1991 theorem concerns a finite interval with fixed boundaries and predicts a single global two-cycle, not the many long periodic-ring cycles here. Bisdorff and Marichal count maximal independent sets on cycles, giving the known Perrin sequence without a Rule 3 temporal interpretation. The 2025 Špale–Stehlík Figure 3 shows the known alternating-profile spatial drift but does not count recurrent configurations or primitive orbits. The new assessed statement is the **joint even/odd gcd-dependent formula for every temporal fixed-point count**, its Perrin bijection for odd times, and the resulting all-period orbit census. Direct searches for Rule 3 with Perrin numbers, cyclic forbidden \(101\), and primitive periods returned no matching published classification. This is evidence of a gap, not proof of unique priority; equivalent unpublished or unindexed work remains possible. The known pointwise formula makes this a mathematical enumeration consequence of established dynamics, not an independent discovery of the Rule 3 evolution law.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For a classical one-dimensional nonlinear cellular automaton, the result supplies an exact global temporal zeta/orbit census at every ring length rather than a finite experiment or a vague stabilization claim. The odd/even split has structural content: odd-time fixed points unexpectedly correspond to maximal independent sets (Perrin numbers), while even-time fixed points are forbidden-word transfer traces. The complete primitive-orbit formula and the exponential asymptotic scarcity of recurrent initial states give reusable dynamical invariants. A pointwise rule formula alone does not list these invariants; the combinatorial change of viewpoint is substantive even though no more general theorem about other cellular automata is asserted.
