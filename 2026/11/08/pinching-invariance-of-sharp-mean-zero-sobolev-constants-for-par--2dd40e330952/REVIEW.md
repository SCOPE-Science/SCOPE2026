# Review of the sharp banana-to-rose pinching invariance claim

**Correctness — PASS.** Both point-evaluation diagonals are derived from direct orthogonal decompositions of all continuous edgewise-\(H^1\) functions, including the separate endpoint-difference mode of the parallel-edge graph. The interval reproducing kernel, integration terms, edge midpoint extrema, and strict longest-edge comparison are written explicitly in the proof. Independent rational Poisson-stiffness computations confirm \(230\) finite instances. The exceptional two-edge circle and all graph vertices are covered analytically; the finite checker does not replace the all-length proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The arclength Sobolev embedding bounds of Solomyak, Baker–Faber's metrized-graph Laplacian framework, and Cinkir's rose/banana *tau* formulas are distinct in claims and normalization. Cinkir's tau constant strictly changes under endpoint identification when there are at least three parallel edges, whereas the proved sharp arclength Sobolev evaluation maximum remains unchanged. Relevant previously established mathematical claims concern discrete/metric graph families (including equal-length parallel edges with a pendant arm), not this all-positive-length graph-pair invariance and matching Green extremizers. Published mathematical-record searches for the graph pair, pinching, Green diagonals and equivalent terms did not identify a covering statement; this is not proof of priority. Possible unstated consequences of general Green formulas remain the principal residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** A natural topological pinching changes cycle rank and a classical tau invariant, yet preserves the optimal mean-zero Sobolev constant over an entire continuous family of arbitrary edge lengths. The theorem explains this by a strictly positive extra antisymmetric evaluation mode that vanishes precisely at norming midpoints, and gives complete optimizer rigidity and a sharp two-edge exception. This is a structural comparison of function spaces under a graph quotient, not merely an isolated table entry.

**Limits and bibliography.** All claims use real first-order energy and unweighted, unnormalized arclength on finite compact graphs. The relevant sources are Solomyak, arXiv:math/0206235; Baker–Faber, arXiv:math/0407428; and Cinkir, EJC 18 (2011), P81. Their inspected material supports the methods and the comparison, but additional unindexed literature could affect priority. No independent mathematical audit has occurred.
