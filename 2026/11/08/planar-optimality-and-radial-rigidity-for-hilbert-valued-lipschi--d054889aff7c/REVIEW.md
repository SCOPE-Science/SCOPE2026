# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact variance identity expresses every edge increment through nested-tail probabilities. On any fixed arm the quadratic inner-product coefficients are all strictly positive, and the cross-arm contribution is linear in that arm's increments. Pairwise Cauchy--Schwarz and the triangle inequality give an achievable armwise upper bound, whose equality forces full-length parallel increments. Consequently all global maximizers are radial, and the problem reduces exactly to minimizing a sum of positive-length unit vectors. The polygon inequality, including the dominant-arm equality case, proves the sharp value, complete equality classification and planar sufficiency. The signed residual gives the exact one-dimensional threshold. An exact-rational checker tests the algebra and multiple boundaries independently; no finite computation is used to assert an infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Fiedler (1990) already gives a tree-variance relation in the uniform unweighted case, and Göring--Helmberg--Wappler (2005, Corollary 30) already prove radial optimal-realization geometry and existence of planar optimizers for unit-edge uniform trees. These are acknowledged as prior, not claimed as new. The 2011 Göring--Helmberg--Reiss preprint gives weighted-node and weighted-edge SDP formulations and general separator/rank structure but does not give the present weighted-spider exact formula or complete optimizer and minimum-rank classification. The foundational optimization paper Sun--Boyd--Xiao--Diaconis (2006), sets up the general maximum-variance-unfolding semidefinite program with unweighted vertices and edge upper-distance constraints. Its Sections 4.1--4.7 and numerical examples do not state the explicit weighted-spider polygon formula or classify its target dimension. Addario-Berry--Janson--McDiarmid (2009) concern scalar spread, while Lee--Naor--Peres (2007) concern metric distortion/Markov convexity. The nearest accessible scalar weighted-spider calculation has a different target and an exact sign-partition residual, without the planar optimizer theorem. Targeted searches for Hilbert Lipschitz spider variance, maximum variance unfolding, equivalent polygon/centroid formulations and related database claims found no directly covering theorem. A stronger unindexed theorem may still exist; publication-level novelty is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS
Beyond the existing uniform-tree and general-SDP results, the theorem gives an exact, finite-input answer to a classical maximum-variance-unfolding objective on all positive-length spiders with arbitrary positive vertex probabilities. It resolves every extremal map and proves a strict target-dimension threshold: two dimensions always attain the optimum, whereas the scalar problem has a signed partition obstruction. The three-leaf uniform star exhibits a genuine scalar/Hilbert gap. Arbitrarily long, nonuniform arms are covered without solving an SDP. This is a natural family-wide structural classification rather than an isolated finite numerical instance.

## Scientific limitations and residual risks
The complete proof applies to the stated spider class and Hilbert targets only. Equivalent prior results might be hidden in semidefinite-embedding or graph-optimization literature under different terminology. The general MVU SDP is prior art and is not claimed as new. No independent audit has been conducted.
