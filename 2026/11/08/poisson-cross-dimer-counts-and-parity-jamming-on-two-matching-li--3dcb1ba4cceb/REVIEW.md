# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The reduced Markov chain has exact transition rates: within either clique a uniformly sampled vacant pair erases a hypergeometric number of active matching edges, and each active bridge reacts at rate \(\lambda\). On the \(s=nt\) scale, bounded jumps and quadratic variations give the explicit fluid law \((u_A,u_B,w)=((1+s)^{-1},(1+s)^{-1},(1+s)^{-2})\), hence finite-horizon Poisson arrivals with intensity \(\lambda(1+s)^{-2}\). A new bounded Lyapunov function \(h/a+h/b\) has generator drift at most \(-\min\{1,2\lambda\}h\); this controls the infinite-time crossing tail. The final parity formula follows exactly from maximality within each clique. `verify.py` checked the generator and independent finite-state/simulation diagnostics; computation is not used as an infinite-size proof.

**Originality — PASS on examined evidence, provisional historical priority.** Penrose–Sudbury's open full text covers graph RSA, regular trees and degree-bounded central limits, not this growing-degree two-clique cross-dimer count. Dyer–Frieze (1991) is closest in edge-greedy algorithm and forest-focused results, but its full text was not accessible; only the abstract was read. Aronson et al. use a different vertex-first rule. Searches for dual formulations, edge-greedy matching and equivalent Poisson connector laws uncovered no dominating statement. The separate-bridge barbell model and complete multipartite adsorption problems do not imply a Poisson count of \(n\) simultaneously available matching connectors in this graph. Unindexed matching asymptotics remain a residual risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result explains a counterintuitive limit in a natural two-community random packing network: a linear number of physical bridge edges creates only a finite Poisson number of accepted cross-dimers, yet the parity of that count determines whether irreversible dynamics achieves a perfect matching. The proof contributes a uniform-in-time Lyapunov argument bridging fluid scaling and the terminal jammed observable, which finite-horizon laws alone cannot determine.
