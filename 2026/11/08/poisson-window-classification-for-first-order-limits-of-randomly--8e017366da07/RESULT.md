# Poisson-window classification for first-order limits of randomly colored equivalence classes

## Finding

Let \(b_t,m_t\to\infty\) be arbitrary integer sequences. The base structure has \(m_t\) equivalence classes, each with \(b_t\) vertices. Independently color every vertex red with probability \(1/2\), and let \(P\) denote red vertices. For each fixed integer \(k\ge0\), define
\[
\lambda_{k,t}=m_t 2^{-b_t}\binom{b_t}{k},
\]
where the binomial coefficient is zero when \(k>b_t\).

**Exact convergence criterion.** In the language \(\{E,P,=\}\), with \(E\) the equivalence relation and without a linear order, every first-order sentence has a limiting satisfaction probability **if and only if** every sequence \(\lambda_{k,t}\) has a limit in \([0,\infty]\), allowing \(+\infty\). In this case the limiting probabilities are all zero or one **if and only if** no \(\lambda_{k,t}\) tends to a finite positive constant.

**Critical law and complete spectrum.** At most one \(k\) can have \(\lambda_{k,t}\to c\in(0,\infty)\). In that case, the numbers of classes with exactly \(k\) red and exactly \(k\) blue vertices converge jointly to **independent** \(\operatorname{Poisson}(c)\) variables \(X,Y\). The complete set of limiting first-order sentence probabilities equals
\[
\left\{\Pr((X,Y)\in B): B\text{ belongs to the finite Boolean algebra generated by }\{X\ge j,Y\ge j:j\in\mathbb N\}\right\}.
\]
Every member of this set is realized by an explicit first-order sentence that counts distinct equivalence classes of the indicated two kinds. The critical window for type \(k\) is
\[
m_t\,2^{-b_t}b_t^k/k!\longrightarrow c.
\]
For example, taking \(k=0\) and \(m_t=\lfloor c2^{b_t}\rfloor\) makes the limiting probability that some class is entirely red equal to \(1-e^{-c}\). At critical \(k\), the same limit holds for the existence of a class with exactly \(k\) red vertices.

## Assumptions and scope

The integer index \(t\to\infty\) is arbitrary, the numbers \(m_t\) of classes and \(b_t\) of vertices per class both tend to infinity, and the base structure is deterministic up to relabeling. The only randomness is a mutually independent fair binary color at each vertex. Every first-order sentence is fixed independently of \(t\). Equality and the reflexive, symmetric, transitive relation \(E\) are interpreted in their usual way. There is no built-in ordering or ability to name class indices. Limits of \(\lambda_{k,t}\) are in the extended nonnegative reals. The zero-one assertion concerns limiting probabilities of *all* first-order sentences, not a single sentence or fixed quantifier rank.

## Proof

**Step 1: Exact finite-rank profile.** Fix a positive integer \(q\). For all sufficiently large \(t\), \(b_t>2q\). Each equivalence class has one of the following \(q\)-truncated color types: \((k,q)\) for \(0\le k<q\), meaning exactly \(k\) red vertices; \((q,k)\), meaning exactly \(k\) blue vertices; or \((q,q)\), meaning at least \(q\) of each color. Define the global profile by truncating the number of classes of each of these \(2q+1\) types at \(q\).

Two finite colored equivalence relations with the same truncated global profile satisfy exactly the same sentences of quantifier rank at most \(q\). Indeed, in the \(q\)-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game, the duplicator maintains a color-preserving partial bijection between previously visited classes and between their already selected vertices. A move inside a visited class can be matched, because the corresponding class has the same truncated number of both colors and fewer than \(q\) vertices have previously been played. A move into a fresh class can be matched in a fresh class of the same truncated type because at most \(q-1\) classes have already been visited and the two class-counts agree up to \(q\). This preserves equality, \(E\), and \(P\) through \(q\) rounds.

**Step 2: Sufficiency.** A single class has exactly \(k\) red vertices with probability \(p_{k,t}=2^{-b_t}\binom{b_t}{k}\). For each fixed \(k\), \(p_{k,t}\to0\). Hence its total count, distributed as \(\operatorname{Bin}(m_t,p_{k,t})\), converges after truncation at \(q\) to zero, \(\min(q,\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_k))\), or the constant \(q\), according as \(\lambda_{k,t}\to0\), \(\lambda_k\in(0,\infty)\), or \(\lambda_{k,t}\to\infty\). For a finite mean, the binomial probability-generating function converges to that of the stated Poisson law. For a diverging mean, the variance is at most the mean, so Chebyshev’s inequality makes the count exceed every fixed threshold with probability tending to one. The same holds symmetrically for exactly \(k\) blue vertices.

For fixed \(k\),
\[
\frac{\lambda_{k+1,t}}{\lambda_{k,t}}=\frac{b_t-k}{k+1}\longrightarrow\infty
\]
whenever \(b_t>k\); thus at most one \(k\) has a finite positive limit. If one exists, all smaller fixed types have vanishing means and all larger fixed types have diverging means. The two critical categories (exactly \(k\) red and exactly \(k\) blue) are disjoint for \(b_t>2k\), each with probability \(p_{k,t}\), so their joint probability-generating function is
\[
\bigl(1+p_{k,t}(s-1)+p_{k,t}(u-1)\bigr)^{m_t}\longrightarrow\exp(c(s-1)+c(u-1)).
\]
They converge to independent Poisson counts. The generic type has one-class probability tending to one; since \(m_t\to\infty\), the number of generic classes exceeds \(q\) with probability tending to one. These facts yield joint convergence of the finite truncated \(q\)-profile in all cases. Each fixed rank-\(q\) sentence is constant on each profile value, so its probability converges.

**Step 3: Necessity.** For fixed \(k\), the sentence \(\theta_k\) asserting that some \(E\)-class contains exactly \(k\) red vertices is first-order: require a representative \(x\), identify \(k\) distinct red \(E\)-neighbors of \(x\), and universally exclude any additional red \(E\)-neighbor. For \(k=0\) use only the universal exclusion. Independence across classes gives
\[
\Pr(\neg\theta_k)=(1-p_{k,t})^{m_t}.
\]
As \(p_{k,t}\to0\), the elementary identity \(-\log(1-p_{k,t})/p_{k,t}\to1\) implies this probability converges **if and only if** \(m_tp_{k,t}=\lambda_{k,t}\) converges in \([0,\infty]\). Indeed \(x\mapsto e^{-x}\) is a homeomorphism of the extended nonnegative half-line onto \([0,1]\). Thus convergence of every sentence forces convergence of every \(\lambda_{k,t}\).

**Step 4: Zero-one dichotomy and full critical spectrum.** If no \(\lambda_k\) is finite positive, every fixed-rank profile converges in probability to a single deterministic value, so every sentence has limit zero or one. Conversely, if \(\lambda_k=c\in(0,\infty)\), the sentence \(\theta_k\) has limit \(1-e^{-c}\in(0,1)\). The preceding profile argument also shows that in the critical case every sentence's limit is the probability of a finite Boolean combination of threshold events in the two independent Poisson variables \(X,Y\). Conversely, the condition that there are at least \(j\) distinct \(E\)-classes with exactly \(k\) red vertices is first-order for every fixed \(j\), and likewise for exactly \(k\) blue vertices. Finite Boolean combinations of these formulas realize every member of the displayed spectrum. Finally \(\binom{b_t}{k}\sim b_t^k/k!\) for fixed \(k\), establishing the claimed scale and the explicit examples. \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work

Koponen's 2026 paper concerns structural prerequisites for first-order convergence laws of random expansions and provides a useful motivation for examining explicit base structures. Its Corollary 2.14 and Remark 2.15 address different structural hypotheses rather than this necessary-and-sufficient critical-window law. Koponen and Karlsson (2025) prove a zero-one law for expansions of linear preorders when all equivalence classes grow faster than every logarithm (and satisfy an upper-growth restriction); that regime excludes the present fixed-\(k\) critical window, where \(b_t\) is logarithmic in the number of classes. Their full paper was not obtained, so statements beyond its public abstract are not asserted. Koponen's bounded-degree results (arXiv:2401.04802) assume a uniform degree bound, whereas the relevant equivalence classes here have unbounded size and Gaifman degree. Dawar, Grädel and Hoelzel (2020) study random expansions of powers of fixed structures, not these independently colored growing equivalence classes. The Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé method and Poisson convergence of rare multinomial categories are classical; the claimed new contribution is their exact if-and-only-if synthesis and complete critical logical spectrum for this specified model.

## Limitations

The conclusion concerns exactly two independently fair colors, equal-size classes, an unordered equivalence relation and ordinary first-order sentences. It does not address ordered quotients, nonidentical class sizes, biased color probabilities, second-order logics or variable formulas whose quantifier rank grows with \(t\). No uniform convergence rate across arbitrary class-size and class-count sequences is asserted. An inaccessible closely related 2025 conference chapter and potentially older general logical convergence results create residual historical-priority risk. No claim is made that the elementary EF and Poisson components are individually new.
