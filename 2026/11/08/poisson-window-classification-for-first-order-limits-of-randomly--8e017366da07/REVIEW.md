# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For every fixed rank \(q\), an Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game gives a complete sufficient finite profile: the numbers of equivalence classes with exactly \(k<q\) red vertices, with exactly \(k<q\) blue vertices, or with at least \(q\) vertices of each color, all truncated at \(q\). Independent binomial class coloring makes every rare fixed-\(k\) count obey the zero/Poisson/infinity trichotomy. Ratios of consecutive class intensities diverge, so two different fixed sizes cannot both have finite positive means. When a size is critical the red-rare and blue-rare categories converge jointly to independent Poisson variables. The existence of a class with exactly \(k\) red vertices is first-order definable and has complement probability \((1-p_{k,t})^{m_t}\), which proves the necessary direction. Every displayed threshold probability is first-order realizable. Finite tests independently compare exact EF games and profile equality, categorical multinomial probabilities, and critical binomial-to-Poisson values. Risks: finite tests cover only small models; the infinite conclusion depends on the stated EF invariant and limit arguments, which were reconstructed directly.

It identifies precisely the critical logarithmic regime that destroys the zero-one law, proves a genuinely two-coordinate independent-Poisson limit even though the underlying coloring is binary, and completely classifies attainable logical probability limits. These are motivated finite-model-theoretic invariants, not an arbitrary finite table.
