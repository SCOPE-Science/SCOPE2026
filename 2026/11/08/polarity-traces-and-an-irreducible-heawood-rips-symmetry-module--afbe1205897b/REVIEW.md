# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The integral theorem follows from the canonical contractible cover and the signed injection \((i_U,-i_V)\) of Mayer–Vietoris chains; swapping the cover pieces reverses its sign and thus twists the graph cycle lattice. All hypotheses (finite projective plane of order \(q\ge2\), inclusive graph metric at scale two, and point–line graph automorphisms) are explicit. The edge and vertex trace formulas follow from signed graph chains, and the polarity case counts exactly flags fixed setwise. Exact finite computations build all 336 Fano graph automorphisms and all 331 Fano scale-two simplices; direct alternating simplicial traces agree for all group elements. The 9 conjugacy classes, squared character norm 336, and invariant sum 0 are checked. Separate field-three and field-four computations check additional cases and distinguish polarities. This is a mathematical proof for all finite projective planes, not extrapolation from finite experiments. The accompanying code and deterministic output are part of the reproducibility package.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The closest *published result with the identical projective-plane Rips object* already establishes \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(H)\simeq\bigvee^{q^3}S^2\) and the covering \(X=U\cup V\). That prior source is treated as covering the entire nonequivariant homotopy and barcode calculation. Its proof is already the natural source of the signed-Mayer–Vietoris mechanism; the latter is only a formal equivariant refinement, not a separately claimed breakthrough. However its full published result does not specify the automorphism module, polarity trace, or the absolutely irreducible 8-dimensional Fano character. This exact nine-class classification, the zero-invariant conclusion and the distinct two polarity traces at \(\operatorname{PG}(2,4)\) are not supplied by that nonequivariant statement. Adamaszek's graph powers and Larrión–Pizaña–Villarroel-Flores's bipartite-square fundamental-group theorem also do not imply this character, and published Heawood independence or matching-complex characters concern different spaces. Searches include graph-cycle aliases, equivalences, polarity descriptions, broader theorems and named 336-element graph automorphisms. Residual risk remains that an independent group-character table or unindexed note already contains an equivalent derivation; absence from search is not proof of world priority.

## Value — PASS
The canonical finite projective plane family and its first nontrivial Rips thickening give a motivated topological invariant. The known rank \(q^3\) alone does not identify the integral action of point–line dualities. The general sign-twisted cycle-lattice structure converts geometry into character values, while the full Fano irreducibility and two different polarity traces for the same \(\operatorname{PG}(2,4)\) complex supply nontrivial, testable structural information. Nothing is asserted about a wider theorem of equivalently valuable strength, and no general group-representation result for all planes is claimed beyond the exact module and trace formula. This value does not depend on novelty of the previously established wedge count.
