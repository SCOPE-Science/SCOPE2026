# Polynomial-graph phase retrieval: a complete affine-versus-curved dichotomy and exact profile dimension

## Finding
Let \(P\in\mathbb R[t]\), and let \(\Gamma_P=\{t+iP(t):t\in\mathbb R\}\). For every integer \(d\ge1\), complex polynomials \(p,q\) of degree at most \(d\) have equal moduli on any nonempty open arc of \(\Gamma_P\) only when \(q=\eta p\), \(|\eta|=1\), **if and only if** \(\deg P\ge2\). When \(P\) is affine or constant, distinct degree-one polynomials have identical moduli on the full graph.

More precisely, for every non-affine \(P\) of degree \(m\ge2\), put
\[
D_{m,d}=(d+1)^2-\bigl(\max\{d-m+1,0\}\bigr)^2.
\]
The real vector space of whole-graph intensity profiles
\[
\mathscr I_{P,d}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\left\{t\mapsto |p(t+iP(t))|^2:\deg p\le d\right\}
\]
has **exact** dimension \(D_{m,d}\), independent of the coefficients of \(P\) apart from its degree. For Lebesgue-almost every ordered \(D_{m,d}\)-tuple of distinct points in any fixed nonempty real interval, their squared moduli linearly reconstruct every profile in \(\mathscr I_{P,d}\). Consequently these nodes recover every degree-at-most-\(d\) polynomial up to global phase. No smaller number of scalar node evaluations can linearly reconstruct **all profiles**. This is not a lower bound for nonlinear phase retrieval.

There is a lifting threshold: \(D_{m,d}=(d+1)^2\) exactly when \(m>d\). In that regime the entire Hermitian coefficient-product matrix is linearly identifiable. For \(m\le d\), it has an explicit nonzero Hermitian kernel of real dimension \((d-m+1)^2\), yet phase retrieval remains valid. For each fixed generic node configuration, the measurement map on rank-one coefficient-product matrices also has a positive (not explicitly quantified) inverse Lipschitz constant relative to Frobenius distance.

## Assumptions and scope
\(P\) has real coefficients and the graph is traversed using real \(t\). \(p\) and \(q\) have arbitrary complex coefficients; the zero polynomial is included. The degree \(d\ge1\) is fixed and the curve degree \(m\ge2\) is arbitrary. The word 'generic' means outside the zero locus of a nonzero real polynomial in the sample parameters, and therefore on a nonempty Zariski-open dense set of full measure in each interval product. Smoothness of the graph follows from its first coordinate \(t\); no boundedness assumption on \(P\) is needed. The stability constant may depend on \(P,d\) and the chosen nodes.

## Proof
**1. Polynomial-graph rigidity.** Put \(u(t)=t+iP(t)\), \(v(t)=t-iP(t)\). For any complex polynomial \(a\), let \(a^*(z)=\overline{a(\overline z)}\); then \(|a(u(t))|^2=a(u(t))a^*(v(t))\) for real \(t\). If intensities for \(p,q\) agree on an interval, their difference is a polynomial in \(t\), so it vanishes identically. If either polynomial is identically zero, so is the other. Otherwise the rational function \(r=q/p\) satisfies the rational identity
\[
r(u(t))r^*(v(t))=1. \tag{1}
\]
The modulus condition as \(t\to+\infty\) forces \(p,q\) to have the same degree, and the limiting quotient \(c=r(\infty)\) satisfies \(|c|=1\).

Suppose \(r\) is not constant. Its first nonconstant Laurent term at infinity is uniquely of the form \(r(z)=c(1+a z^{-k}+O(z^{-k-1}))\), with an integer \(k\ge1\) and \(a\ne0\). Put \(A=a i^{-k}\). Since \(P(t)\) is real and \(|P(t)|\asymp t^m\), we may write, as \(t\to+\infty\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
a u^{-k}+\overline a v^{-k}
&=(A+\overline A)P(t)^{-k}\\
&\quad+ik(A-\overline A)tP(t)^{-k-1}
+O\bigl(t^2|P(t)|^{-k-2}\bigr).
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
\]
The remainder from higher Laurent terms and their products in (1) is
\(O(|P|^{-k-1}+|P|^{-2k})\). As \(m\ge2\), \(t/P(t)\to0\). If \(A+\overline A\ne0\), the first term in (2) dominates every other term, contradicting (1). Thus \(A\) is purely imaginary. Because \(A\ne0\), the next term \(ik(A-\overline A)tP^{-k-1}\) is nonzero and dominates: its ratio to \(O(t^2|P|^{-k-2})\) grows like \(|P|/t\to\infty\), to \(O(|P|^{-k-1})\) like \(t\to\infty\), and to \(O(|P|^{-2k})\) at least like \(t|P|^{k-1}\to\infty\). This again contradicts (1). Hence \(r\equiv c\), proving \(q=cp\) and \(|c|=1\).

If \(\deg P\le1\), \(\Gamma_P\) is a straight line. Choose a point \(z_0\) on that line and a nonzero vector \(n\) perpendicular to it. The monic linear polynomials \(z-(z_0+n)\) and \(z-(z_0-n)\) have equal moduli at every point of the line, but cannot be unimodular multiples. Thus the dichotomy is sharp.

**2. Exact intensity-space dimension.** Introduce algebraically independent variables \(U,V\), and consider
\[
\Phi:\mathbb C[U,V]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[t],\quad
U\mapsto u(t),\;V\mapsto v(t).
\]
Since \(t=(U+V)/2\) on the image, its kernel is the principal ideal generated by
\[
F_P(U,V)=U-V-2iP\bigl((U+V)/2\bigr).
\]
Indeed the invertible substitution \(s=(U+V)/2\), \(w=(U-V)/2\) identifies the quotient by \((F_P)\) with \(\mathbb C[s,w]/(w-iP(s))\cong\mathbb C[s]\). Since \(\deg P=m\ge2\), both \(\deg_U F_P\) and \(\deg_V F_P\) equal \(m\), with nonzero leading coefficients.

Let \(W_d\) consist of polynomials of degree at most \(d\) **separately** in \(U,V\); its complex dimension is \((d+1)^2\). If \(F_PH\in W_d\), the additive degree formula in each variable for nonzero polynomial products forces \(\deg_U H\le d-m\), \(\deg_V H\le d-m\). Conversely each such \(H\) gives a kernel element. The kernel on \(W_d\) therefore has complex dimension \(\max\{d-m+1,0\}^2\).

The conjugate-linear involution \(J\sum c_{jk}U^jV^k=\sum\overline{c_{jk}}V^jU^k\) preserves this kernel and has fixed-point space of real dimension equal to the complex dimension. Its fixed points correspond to Hermitian matrices of coefficient products. Rank-one positive semidefinite matrices span all Hermitian matrices over \(\mathbb R\) by polarization. Hence the real span of squared intensities has the claimed dimension \(D_{m,d}\).

**3. Generic optimal interpolation.** Choose a real basis \(b_1(t),\ldots,b_D(t)\) of the profile space. These are real polynomials, linearly independent on every nonempty interval. There therefore exist \(D\) distinct parameters in any interval for which the determinant \(\det(b_k(t_j))_{1\le j,k\le D}\) is nonzero (otherwise all evaluation vectors on the interval lie in a proper subspace). The determinant is a nonzero real polynomial in the \(D\) parameters, so its zero locus has Lebesgue measure zero. For every other tuple, solving this real linear system reconstructs the entire profile and step 1 identifies \(p\) up to phase. Fewer than \(D\) scalar evaluations cannot inject a \(D\)-dimensional real space into a lower-dimensional real space by a linear map.

**4. Lifting threshold and stability.** The Hermitian lifting kernel dimension from step 2 vanishes exactly when \(d<m\). Although a nonzero higher-rank kernel exists when \(d\ge m\), step 1 implies that no nonzero Hermitian matrix of rank at most two can vanish identically on the graph: an indefinite rank-two matrix would be the difference of two rank-one positive matrices with identical intensities, while a nonzero semidefinite matrix cannot vanish on all polynomial-evaluation vectors. The generic finite evaluation map has the same kernel as the entire profile map. The compact set of Hermitian matrices of rank at most two and unit Frobenius norm consequently has a strictly positive minimum measurement norm. This proves the stated inverse Lipschitz inequality on rank-one positive matrices, including arbitrarily close pairs; no uniform-in-parameters constant is asserted.

## Verification
The self-contained `check_rank.py` performs exact modular arithmetic over the prime field of order \(1009\), embedding \(i\) as a square root of \(-1\). For five graph degrees \(2\le m\le6\) and nine coefficient degrees \(0\le d\le8\), it constructs every coefficient-product polynomial and compares its exact coefficient-matrix rank with \(D_{m,d}\) (45 cases). Four additional configurations verify full-rank interpolation at consecutive sample parameters for selected cubic, quartic, and quintic graphs. This is finite corroboration, not a proof for arbitrary degrees or real coefficients. The preceding four proof steps are the complete analytical argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Chalendar and Partington (arXiv:2403.16255, initially public March 24, 2024) prove holomorphic uniqueness results for pairs of intersecting lines and configurations of circles; their Corollary 2.3 concerns **two** curves meeting at irrational angle. No such pair is needed here, and their statements do not calculate the rectangular quotient dimension of a single polynomial graph. Liehr (arXiv:2308.05722, public August 10, 2023) characterizes unions of parallel lines via an arithmetic progression criterion; a single non-affine graph is not such a union. Przybyłek and Siedlecki (arXiv:1907.09371, public July 22, 2019) provide nonadaptive polynomial phaseless interpolation at unrestricted complex nodes, not this exact algebraic-curve threshold. Pakovich and Shparlinski (arXiv:1805.02913, public May 8, 2018) study intersections of rational-function unit level curves; their exceptional composition classification does not directly give the present one-curve phase-rigidity and rank theorem.

The quadratic special graph \(P(t)=t^2\) had already been studied separately in prior work: it admits a four-per-degree explicit sampling condition. That special case is **not** claimed again as original. Here the claim is the full affine-versus-nonlinear classification for **every** real polynomial graph, together with the exact degree-dependent rectangular lifting-kernel formula and its transition at \(m=d+1\). General tools from polynomial ideal theory underlie the dimension argument and are not themselves claimed new.

## Limitations
Generic nodes are shown to exist, but a uniformly well-conditioned deterministic node prescription is not provided. The minimal nonlinear phase-retrieval sample count is not determined, and the generic-node theorem concerns linear reconstruction of the whole intensity profile. The theorem does not apply to arbitrary transcendental graphs, general rational parametrizations, or all holomorphic non-polynomial functions. Related broader results for rational algebraic curves may affect historical priority; independent external mathematical validation has not been carried out.
