# Positive-characteristic local derivations of null-filiform algebras: exact ranks and periodic limits

## Finding

Fix a prime power \(q=p^f\), an integer \(n\ge 1\), and the nonunital nilpotent associative algebra
\[
B_n=t\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^{n+1}).
\]
Write \(e_j=t^j\) for \(1\le j\le n\). Thus \(e_i e_j=e_{i+j}\) for \(i+j\le n\), and is zero otherwise. A *local derivation* means a \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear map \(\Delta:B_n\to B_n\) for which every \(x\in B_n\) admits some algebra derivation \(D_x\) satisfying \(\Delta(x)=D_x(x)\). The witness derivation may depend on \(x\).

**Classification.** A linear map is a local derivation precisely when
\[
\Delta(e_j)=\begin{cases}
0,&p\mid j,\\
\sum_{i=j}^{n}c_{ij}e_i,&p\nmid j,
\end{cases}
\]
with arbitrary coefficients \(c_{ij}\in\mathbb F_q\). In particular, the characteristic imposes *entire zero columns* at exponent multiples of \(p\), not merely diagonal restrictions.

Let \(\ell=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\), \(m=n-\ell\), and \(r=n-p\ell\). The \(\mathbb F_q\)-dimension of the local-derivation space is
\[
D=\sum_{\substack{1\le j\le n\\p\nmid j}}(n-j+1)
=\frac{n(n+1)}2-\ell(n+1)+\frac{p\ell(\ell+1)}2.
\]
There are exactly \(q^D\) labeled local derivations, and no such map has rank greater than \(m\). A uniformly sampled local derivation has rank exactly \(m\) with probability
\[
\boxed{P_{n,p,q}=(1-q^{-1})^r\prod_{h=1}^{\ell}(1-q^{-(h+1)})^{p-1}.}
\]
Consequently, for each fixed characteristic and field \(q\), along the residue class \(n\equiv r\pmod p\),
\[
\lim_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)}}P_{n,p,q}
=(1-q^{-1})^r\prod_{h=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{-(h+1)})^{p-1}>0.
\]
For \(q=2\), the two limiting constants (even and odd \(n\), respectively) are approximately \(0.577576190173\) and \(0.288788095087\). This is a genuine residue-class effect, not a global limit over all \(n\).

**Full rank distribution.** List the indices not divisible by \(p\) in descending order \(j_1>\cdots>j_m\), and set \(d_i=n-j_i+1\). Define integer numbers \(F^{(0)}_0=1\), with other \(F^{(0)}_s=0\). For \(i=1,\ldots,m\) and all \(s\ge0\), set
\[
F^{(i)}_s=q^sF^{(i-1)}_s+\mathbf1_{s\ge1}(q^{d_i}-q^{s-1})F^{(i-1)}_{s-1}.
\]
Terms at impossible indices are zero. Then \(F^{(m)}_s\) is **exactly** the number of local derivations of rank \(s\), for all \(0\le s\le m\). This is a finite exact recurrence, not a probabilistic approximation.

## Assumptions and scope

The result uses \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear derivations and local derivations, not additive maps lacking field linearity. The algebra is the *nonunital* ideal \(t\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^{n+1})\), not the unital ring \(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^{n+1})\). The base field may be any finite extension of its prime field; no algebraic-closedness or restriction on \(n\) is imposed. The rank is rank of the underlying \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear map. The probability is uniform on the \(q^D\) local derivations.

## Proof

**Step 1: all derivations.** Because \(B_n\) is generated by \(t\) as a nonunital algebra, a derivation is determined by \(b=D(t)\in B_n\), and
\[
D_b\left(\sum_{j=1}^n a_jt^j\right)=\left(\sum_{j=1}^n ja_jt^{j-1}\right)b.
\]
Conversely every \(b\in B_n\) defines a derivation by this formula. The relation \(t^{n+1}=0\) causes no obstruction, since \((n+1)t^n b=0\) for every \(b\in t\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^{n+1})\). Hence \(\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\operatorname{Der}(B_n)=n\).

**Step 2: pointwise range.** For \(x=\sum a_jt^j\), if all coefficients \(a_j\) with \(p\nmid j\) vanish then \(x'=0\), so \(D_b(x)=0\) for every derivation. Otherwise let \(s\) be the least \(j\) with \(p\nmid j\) and \(a_j\ne0\). In the quotient, \(x'=t^{s-1}u(t)\) with \(u(0)=sa_s\ne0\). Multiplication by \(u\) is invertible, while \(b\) ranges over \(t\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^{n+1})\). Therefore
\[
\{D(x):D\in\operatorname{Der}(B_n)\}
=t^s\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^{n+1}).
\]
This equality includes \(s=n\) and every finite characteristic.

**Step 3: classification.** Applying the local condition to each basis element gives zero on \(e_j\) for \(p\mid j\) and membership in \(\langle e_j,\ldots,e_n\rangle\) otherwise. Conversely, suppose those conditions hold. For an arbitrary \(x\) with \(x'\ne0\), choose \(s\) from Step 2. Since all columns at \(p\)-divisible exponents vanish, \(\Delta(x)\) is a sum of vectors in \(\langle e_j,\ldots,e_n\rangle\) with \(j\ge s\). Thus \(\Delta(x)\in t^s\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^{n+1})\), which equals the space of possible values \(D(x)\). If \(x'=0\), then \(\Delta(x)=0\). This proves sufficiency for every \(x\), not just basis vectors. The allowed coefficients are independent, giving the dimension and labeled count.

**Step 4: exact rank recursion.** The \(j_i\)-th nonzero-eligible column is an arbitrary vector in \(U_{j_i}=\langle e_{j_i},\ldots,e_n\rangle\), of dimension \(d_i\). As the indices decrease, the already chosen column span is contained in the next space \(U_{j_i}\). If that span has dimension \(s\), exactly \(q^s\) choices preserve rank and exactly \(q^{d_i}-q^s\) raise it by one. Multiplying by the counts for preceding choices proves the stated recurrence for every \(s\), with base case \(F^{(0)}_0=1\). This also proves that the largest attainable rank is \(m\), because the choice of independent columns has positive count at every step.

**Step 5: maximal-rank product and limits.** For every new column to increase rank, the number of choices is \(q^{d_i}-q^{i-1}\). Divide their product by \(q^D=\prod_iq^{d_i}\) to get
\[
P_{n,p,q}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-q^{i-1-d_i}).
\]
For an eligible index \(j_i\), the number of larger eligible indices is
\[
i-1=(n-j_i)-(\lfloor n/p\rfloor-\lfloor j_i/p\rfloor).
\]
Consequently \(d_i-(i-1)=1+\lfloor n/p\rfloor-\lfloor j_i/p\rfloor\). There are \(r\) eligible indices in the final incomplete block, each contributing \((1-q^{-1})\), and \(p-1\) eligible indices in each preceding full block, with contributions \((1-q^{-(h+1)})\) for \(h=1,\ldots,\ell\). This yields the exact displayed formula. The infinite product converges to a strictly positive number because \(\sum_{h\ge1}q^{-(h+1)}<\infty\) and all factors are positive. It proves the asserted residue-class limits.

## Verification

A standalone `verify.py` program enumerates every structurally allowed matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\) through \(n=5\) and over \(\mathbb F_3\) through \(n=4\), computes its rank by independent Gaussian elimination, and checks the rank recurrence and maximal-rank product. For all cases with at most one million total linear maps, the program independently enumerates *all* linear maps and directly tests pointwise membership in derivation-value sets obtained from the original Leibniz rule; its accepted maps agree exactly with the structural classification. The cases include \((n,q)=(3,2),(4,2),(3,3)\). All checks end in `VERIFY_OK`.

Representative complete rank histograms are: for \((n,q)=(4,2)\), \((F_0,F_1,F_2)=(1,21,42)\); for \((n,q)=(4,3)\), \((F_0,F_1,F_2,F_3)=(1,176,2928,3456)\); for \((n,q)=(5,2)\), \((F_0,F_1,F_2,F_3)=(1,49,294,168)\). Finite experiments verify sample parameters only; the all-parameter proof is Steps 1–5.

## Relationship to prior work

Abdurasulov, Ayupov and Yusupov [1], Theorem 5.1, classify local derivations of complex null-filiform associative algebras as triangular maps, and Theorem 5.5 handles their 2-local derivations. Here the object is defined over *positive-characteristic finite fields*, where every \(p\)-th-power basis element must be annihilated by each local derivation. Theorem 5.1's characteristic-zero triangular formula does not impose these zero columns; the pointwise-range proof above handles arbitrary finite characteristic. No novelty is asserted for the complex case or the 2-local rigidity result.

Zieliński [2] fully describes local derivations on polynomial rings in characteristic two and formal power-series rings over arbitrary fields. Those are different, untruncated and, in the power-series setting, unital algebras. Their general theory motivates consideration of \(p\)-power obstructions but does not state the present finite nilpotent algebra's exact rank spectrum or residue-class full-rank probability. General finite-field random-matrix estimates for prescribed zero patterns [3] are also acknowledged: the descending nested-subspace counting argument is standard finite-field linear algebra, not a newly claimed universal theorem. The particular algebraic identification and its arithmetic rank-density consequences are the contribution asserted here.

The explicit full-text comparisons include [1, §5.1], [2, §§3–4], and [3, §§1–2]. No covering result was identified in accessible mathematical-research records; absence from a search is not a proof of priority.

## Limitations

Only \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear *local* derivations of the nonunital, one-generated null-filiform algebra are classified. Neither nonlinear 2-local maps nor more general filiform structures, multivariate truncations or the unital truncated ring are treated. Full rank means the maximum rank \(n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor\), not rank \(n\). The limiting probability exists only along a fixed residue class modulo \(p\). Literature priority is provisional; a general classification of local derivations on finite truncated rings or Ferrers matrices, if available elsewhere, could imply some counting consequences. No independent audit is claimed.
