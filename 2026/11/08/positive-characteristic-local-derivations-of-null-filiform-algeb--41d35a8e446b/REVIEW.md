# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(n\ge1\) and prime-power \(q=p^f\), an algebra derivation is uniquely specified by its value on the generator \(t\). The Leibniz rule gives \(D_b(x)=x'b\), where \(b\in t\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^{n+1})\). For nonzero derivative \(x'\), its first nonvanishing term has exponent \(s-1\), with \(p\nmid s\); the possible derivation values form exactly the ideal beginning in degree \(s\). This proves both necessity and sufficiency of the zero-column/nested-support classification, not merely a basiswise necessary condition. Descending-column span inclusion then proves the rank recurrence and full-rank probability by exact finite-field counts. The infinite product is positive because its deficits are summable. Exhaustive small-field and small-dimension tests independently check the original local-derivation condition, matrix ranks, recurrence, and product; the universal proof is algebraic and does not rest on enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Theorem 5.1 of Abdurasulov–Ayupov–Yusupov gives the triangular classification for complex null-filiform algebras. Its positive-characteristic specialization cannot simply retain every triangular coefficient, as \(D(t^{kp})=0\) for every derivation. Zieliński's full-text treatment concerns untruncated polynomial and power-series rings; the formal power-series characterization does not state this finite nonunital algebra's exact rank distribution. Salmond–Grant–Grivell–Chan study general zero-pattern random matrices and bounds, but not the derivation-specific forced zero-column pattern, exact all-rank recurrence, or periodic limiting probability. General Ferrers-board matrix rank counting underlies the elementary counting step; novelty is not claimed for that general technique. Searches for aliases and stronger implications in mathematical research records and primary literature found no direct coverage. Residual risk: a general finite-truncation or q-rook theorem might imply some or all of the enumerative consequence; priority needs independent literature verification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The char-zero triangular local-derivation picture has a sharp arithmetic boundary: every exponent divisible by \(p\) becomes an entirely forced-zero column. The result identifies the full local-derivation space, every rank multiplicity, and a nonvanishing maximal-rank limit that oscillates periodically with the truncation length. This gives structural and probabilistic information about a canonical class of nilpotent associative algebras, beyond a sample computation or an isolated dimension count. In the binary case the even/odd limiting probabilities differ by a factor of two, an exact nontrivial asymptotic phenomenon.
