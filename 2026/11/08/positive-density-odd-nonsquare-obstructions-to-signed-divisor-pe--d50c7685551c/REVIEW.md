# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement distinguishes the signed-divisor condition from ordinary semiperfectness. For an odd abundant square \(a\), the odd integer \(\sigma(a)-2a=2q+1\) gives a target subset sum strictly between \(qm+E\) and \((q+1)m\) whenever \(E=\sigma(a)(\sigma(m)-m)<m\). Every subset of proper nontrivial divisors has the form \(Bm+R\), with integral \(B\ge0\) and \(0\le R\le E\). The gap excludes equality. The fixed-primorial estimate and an absolutely convergent reciprocal-square tail prove a positive lower density of suitable nonsquare cofactors. The fact \(a=11025\) has \(\sigma(a)=22971\) is verified by exact factorization. No computational extrapolation is used in the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Ross (2024), Conjecture 17, predicted the absence of odd nonsquare abundant non-\(\{-1,1\}\)-perfect numbers. Campbell (2026), Theorem 3.1, supplies an infinite *prime* family of such counterexamples, which as a fixed-core prime family has density zero. Campbell's Theorem 2.4 supplies positive density of abundant non-\(\{-1,1\}\)-perfect numbers, but its constructed members are all even. The current theorem establishes positive lower density of *odd nonsquare* counterexamples. The methods adapt Campbell's Lemmas 2.1–2.3 and Theorem 3.1, and this close technical relationship is acknowledged rather than presented as independent machinery. No covering statement was found in the examined primary texts or targeted searches; historical priority remains uncertain outside accessible indexed literature.

## Value — PASS
The 2024 odd-nonsquare conjecture was refuted in 2026 by infinitely many examples; knowing only infinitude is compatible with a density-zero exceptional set. Demonstrating a positive lower density establishes a qualitatively stronger distribution statement in precisely that natural counterexample class, rather than a narrowly chosen computation. The explicit \(a=11025\) construction and quantitative positive constant make the assertion concrete, though the bound is not optimized.
