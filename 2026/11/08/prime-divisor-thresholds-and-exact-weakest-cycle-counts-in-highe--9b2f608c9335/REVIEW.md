# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim concerns primitive periodic points of the precise piecewise-affine map, excluding its fixed face. The primary source establishes a one-to-one correspondence between admissible non-all-left primitive words and physical periodic points; the new proof reconstructs the monomial derivative, its coordinate cycles, the per-cycle lower bound from right visits, and the sharp equality criterion from zero-length gaps. The prime-divisor cutoff is attained by an explicitly admissible primitive word for each period above the cutoff. Enumeration counts *indexed words* first, establishes their primitiveness, then divides by the least period to count geometric cycles. The exact-rational program checks all primitive words in 110 finite dimension–period cases and 1,440 physical periodic orbits. These finite tests are not a substitute for the unrestricted proof.

## Originality
**PASS on inspected evidence, with priority risk.** Saiki–Takahasi–Yorke (arXiv:2202.04304, Sections 2–3 and Appendix A) establish existence, both spectral types, coding, and nonuniform expansion but not the least-period optimum or the extremizer count. The public *Exact real-spectrum residue law for the twisted baker map* computes the signed permutation characteristic polynomial and spectral-type proportions, but expressly leaves fine multiplier distributions and least-period orbit counts outside its scope. A pre-existing dimension-three calculation identifies the case of threshold three and its exact \(n-3\) cycle count; the present claim is restricted to dimensions \(M\ge4\) and yields the smallest-prime-divisor threshold and full counting formula across all of them, including composite dimensions. Takahasi's arXiv:2409.01261 concerns a different heterochaos family. Equivalent deductions might exist in unindexed monomial-cocycle literature.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The motivating published question is the extreme weakness of expansion despite every isolated periodic orbit being repelling. Qualitative nonuniform expansion does not answer how weak a multiplier can be at a *prescribed primitive period*. The sharp finite-period optimum, its change at the least prime divisor of dimension, its real-versus-nonreal contrast between prime and composite dimensions, and its complete orbit multiplicities give a natural quantitative refinement. For even dimensions the minimizer is uniquely a cycle for every larger period; other prime factors produce polynomially growing numbers. The finding is a whole-family structural result, not an arbitrary numerical slice or a minor constant change.

## Prior coverage and limitations
The dimension-three optimum and cycle census are prior work; the two-dimensional spectral-gap distribution is a different invariant. The general real-spectrum residue law is a separate published precursor. Published findings were compared by statements and implications rather than titles. The theorem does not extend to arbitrary baker maps, nonperiodic points, or singular-value growth. Finite verification does not prove infinity statements, and external publication priority cannot be guaranteed.
