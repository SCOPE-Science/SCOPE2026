# Scientific review: prime-index generalized-totient Lambert numerators

## Correctness — PASS
The coefficient definition specializes at a prime to \(P_p=S_p^2+pX^{p-1}\). In both \(P_p\) and \(H_p\), the shifted polynomial is monic, is a pure leading monomial modulo \(p\), and has constant term \(p(p+1)\), so the stated Eisenstein conclusions apply for all primes in their respective domains. The companion identity is derived explicitly, not assumed from experimental examples. For \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), the sign change of \(H_p\) between \(-1\) and \(0\) gives a negative real root. The elementary field-degree argument then proves that the doubled-degree polynomial \(H_p(X^2)\) is irreducible. Definitions, degree counts, cases \(p=2\) and \(p=3\), and the \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) exclusion were checked against the proof. Exact finite polynomial tests pass but are not substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Van der Kamp's Sections 4.10–4.11 derive the two polynomial families and explicitly leave their irreducibility as an observation/conjectural expectation. The prime-index shifted-Eisenstein and negative-root arguments provide the asserted conclusions for infinite natural subfamilies. Holleboom's 2014 paper concerns prime decomposition of the Fourier transform, without Lambert numerator irreducibility. Dilcher–Robins (2015) concerns a different self-inversive gcd-power coefficient family, not the triangular divisor-weighted numerators. Research-index, wording, explicit-polynomial, and related-source searches did not identify covering results, but this is not historical proof of novelty. The Dilcher–Robins full text was not available for inspection, so historical-priority risk remains.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The two irreducibility remarks in a published number-theory paper concern naturally defined numerators of generalized-totient Lambert series. Their prime-index subfamilies have unbounded degrees. The result proves all prime indices for the first numerator and infinitely many prime indices for the second after its known factor, using a shared structural mechanism and a distinct real-embedding obstruction. It is not an arbitrary isolated coefficient computation and does not claim the unresolved composite-index or \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) cases.
