# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The separation equivalence reduces the claim exactly to noncommuting powers of two symmetric-group elements. For degrees at least five, power-generated subgroups are normal and either trivial or contain the nonabelian alternating subgroup. Degrees three and four are classified by their respective power-generated normal-subgroup tables; degree two is abelian. The least nondivisor is necessarily a prime power, and every eligible prime power has an explicit witness. The independently implemented brute-force checker passed 2,889 group cases and 19,999 arithmetic cases. The latter test ranges are finite and not asserted to prove the general theorem.

**Originality — PASS with residual priority risk.** The central exact domain is the two-block words \(0^{a}1^{b}\) and \(1^{b}0^{a}\), with arbitrary positive exponents and minimum permutation-DFA size. Demaine et al. (2011) set up the general identity approach but do not state this classification. Bulatov et al. (2016/2017) explicitly know the degree-three identity and study the structurally different alternating-block \((xy)^a(yx)^b\) identity, including a global worst-case missing-six phenomenon. Xu and Regan (2026) prove asymptotic worst-case lower bounds for a general recursive positive-word construction and cite the alternating-block literature; their inspected full text gives no exact prime-power spectrum for pure swapped blocks. The Karpova–Shur (2021) abstract and later citations concern numerical identity classes, but its full article was not available for direct comparison. That and other unindexed implicit reductions remain priority risks. Similarity searches in a published mathematics-results index found no matching statement; lack of a match is not a proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem determines *every* parameter of a natural two-run word-pair separation problem, identifies the precise three low-degree defects in a generic group-theoretic threshold, and gives a complete sparse state-count spectrum. The absence of every non-prime-power size, even as both block lengths vary, is a structural restriction rather than a computation of one chosen numeric instance. Exact minima can be used as regression cases and constraints for restricted reversible automaton separation. No broad bound for arbitrary words is claimed.

The principal unresolved risk is an earlier equivalent or stronger statement implicit in unavailable portions of the 2021 positive-identity literature. The result should not be described as independently validated or as a historically definitive first discovery.
