# Primitive Igusa quartic degeneration at two: a doubled parabolic quadric and its complete rational line boundary

## Finding
Let \(R=\mathbb Z_{(2)}\), write \(\sigma_j\) for the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(j\) in six homogeneous coordinates \(x_0,\ldots,x_5\), and set
\[
 H_R=V(\sigma_1)\subset\mathbb P_R^5,\qquad
 \mathcal X=V(\sigma_2^2-4\sigma_4)\subset H_R.
\]
The family \(\mathcal X\) is a flat primitive integral model of the classical Igusa quartic. Its entire special fibre, including its scheme structure, is the **nonreduced double**
\[
 \mathcal X_{\mathbb F_2}=2Q,\qquad
 Q=V(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\subset\mathbb P^5_{\mathbb F_2},
\]
where \(Q\) is a smooth parabolic quadric threefold. The fifteen flat closures \(L_\pi\) of the classical distinguished singular lines, indexed by perfect matchings \(\pi\) of the six coordinates, specialize to **all fifteen** \(\mathbb F_2\)-rational projective lines geometrically contained in \(Q\). Every \(\mathbb F_2\)-point of \(Q\) lies on three such lines, and their reduced union \(D\subset Q\) contains all of \(Q(\mathbb F_2)\).

For every positive integer \(m\), with \(q=2^m\), the complement \(U=Q\setminus D\) satisfies
\[
 \#Q(\mathbb F_q)=q^3+q^2+q+1,\qquad
 \#D(\mathbb F_q)=15q-15,\qquad
 \boxed{\#U(\mathbb F_q)=q^3+q^2-14q+16}.
\]
In particular \(U(\mathbb F_2)=\varnothing\), whereas \(\#U(\mathbb F_4)=40\), and
\[
 Z(U/\mathbb F_2,T)=
 \frac{(1-2T)^{14}}{(1-T)^{16}(1-4T)(1-8T)}.
\]
This is one result about a specified integral degeneration and the arithmetic of the natural distinguished-line boundary on its reduced support.

## Assumptions and scope
The model is **the primitive polynomial** \(\sigma_2^2-4\sigma_4\) on \(H_R\). Over characteristic zero, Newton identities on \(H_R\) give
\[
 (\textstyle\sum_i x_i^2)^2-4\textstyle\sum_i x_i^4
 =-4(\sigma_2^2-4\sigma_4).
\]
Therefore simply reducing the familiar, un-divided left-hand equation modulo two would give the zero equation, not the flat closure specified here. No claim is made that this primitive special fibre is the characteristic-two modular compactification: the latter has a different, generically reduced/inseparable-cover construction.

For a perfect matching \(\pi=\{\{i,j\},\{k,l\},\{r,s\}\}\), define its line \(L_\pi\cong\mathbb P_R^1\) by
\[
 x_i=x_j=a,\quad x_k=x_l=b,\quad x_r=x_s=c,\quad a+b+c=0.
\]
These are the flat schematic closures of the familiar classical fifteen singular lines (their equations are saturated over \(R\), rather than obtained by retaining only the equation \(2(a+b+c)=0\)). The complement \(U\) is taken in the **reduced** \(Q\), not in the nonreduced special fibre.

## Proof
**1. Primitive flat special fibre.** The polynomial \(F=\sigma_2^2-4\sigma_4\) has nonzero reduction \(\bar F=\bar\sigma_2^2\) in the polynomial ring of the rank-five projective hyperplane \(H_R\). The quotient by this primitive nonzerodivisor is torsion-free over the discrete valuation ring \(R\) and hence flat. Consequently the scheme-theoretic special fibre has ideal \((\bar\sigma_2^2)\) in \(H_{\mathbb F_2}\), exactly twice the effective Cartier divisor \(Q\).

**2. The quadric is geometrically smooth.** Write \(V=\{x\in\mathbb F_2^6:\sum_i x_i=0\}\) and \(q(x)=\sigma_2(x)\). The polar form of \(q\) on \(V\) is \(B(x,y)=\sum_i x_i y_i\). Its radical is the line generated by \(\mathbf1=(1,1,1,1,1,1)\). But \(q(\mathbf1)=\binom62=15=1\) in \(\mathbb F_2\), so the polar radical has no point on \(Q\), even after extension of scalars. Thus \(Q\) is a smooth parabolic quadric in \(\mathbb P^4\). Over each perfect field \(\mathbb F_q\) with \(q\) a power of two, its quadratic form is equivalent to \(uv+st+r^2\). For every quadruple \((u,v,s,t)\) there is exactly one \(r\) with \(r^2=uv+st\); hence there are \(q^4\) affine zeros, including the origin. Projectivizing gives \(\#Q(\mathbb F_q)=(q^4-1)/(q-1)\).

**3. The fifteen line families really lie in the primitive closure.** On a paired-coordinate locus as above, put \(h=a+b+c\) and \(s=ab+ac+bc\). One has \(\sigma_2=h^2+2s\), \(\sigma_4=a^2b^2+a^2c^2+b^2c^2+4abch\), and \(s^2-(a^2b^2+a^2c^2+b^2c^2)=2abch\). Therefore
\[
 F=h^4+4h^2s-8abch,
\]
which vanishes when \(h=0\), over the entire base \(R\). All fifteen projective line families are defined over \(R\) and are pairwise distinct over its residue field.

**4. The reduced lines exhaust the rational lines.** A nonzero vector in \(V(\mathbb F_2)\) has even Hamming weight. The equation \(q=0\) holds exactly for vectors of weight four, so the fifteen points of \(Q(\mathbb F_2)\) correspond to the fifteen **duads** \(A\subset\{0,\ldots,5\}\) of zero-coordinate positions. A perfect matching \(\pi\) gives exactly the three duad-points consisting of its three constituent pairs, hence is a projective line in \(Q\). Conversely any projective \(\mathbb F_2\)-line in \(Q\) joins two different duads \(A,B\) with \(q(p_A+p_B)=0\). Since \(B(p_A,p_B)=|A\cap B|\pmod2\), this holds precisely if \(A,B\) are disjoint. The third duad is their complementary pair, hence the line is the \(L_\pi\) associated to that unique perfect matching. Every duad occurs in exactly three perfect matchings, since the remaining four labels have three pairings.

**5. Counts and zeta.** Two distinct \(\mathbb F_2\)-lines that meet over an algebraic closure meet at an \(\mathbb F_2\)-point, by linear algebra. The only intersections of the fifteen lines are therefore the fifteen rational duad-points, with exactly three lines through each. Over every \(\mathbb F_q\), each of the fifteen lines contains \(q+1\) points; inclusion-exclusion corrects by two for each of the fifteen triple points. Hence \(\#D(\mathbb F_q)=15(q+1)-30\). Subtraction from \(\#Q\) proves the displayed formula for every \(m\ge1\). Exponentiating \(\sum_{m\ge1}\#U(\mathbb F_{2^m})T^m/m\) gives the zeta function.

## Verification
The bundled standard-library program \(scripts/verify.py\) enumerates all fifteen rational points and all rational contained lines, independently constructs the fifteen lines from perfect matchings, compares the two exact sets, and checks the complete triple-incidence profile. It also enumerates the 85 points of \(Q(\mathbb F_4)\) by direct arithmetic in \(\mathbb F_4\), and verifies the \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_4\) complement counts. Its recorded output is \(verification/output.txt\). These finite experiments are checks, not substitutes for the all-extension proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical fifteen distinguished singular lines and their Cremona–Richmond incidence are established mathematics. Ren–Sam–Sturmfels (2013, §2) explain the \(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_2)\) and \(S_6\) combinatorics in the usual characteristic-zero Igusa setting. Dolgachev (2024, §3) gives an important **distinct characteristic-two modular construction**, in which the fixed locus of an involution is an **inseparable double cover** of a quadric and carries a fifteen-line Cremona–Richmond configuration. That result is a close structural comparison, not a proof that the particular primitive integral Igusa hypersurface above has special fibre the **nonreduced double divisor** \(2Q\), or that its specific flat line limits exhaust the lines defined over \(\mathbb F_2\) with the stated complement count. The abstract \((15_3)\) configuration is not claimed to be new. The finding is the specified integral-to-characteristic-two degeneration together with the arithmetic completeness of its distinguished boundary.

## Limitations
This does not describe a good or modular reduction of the Igusa compactification, nor a canonical model for every integral coordinate presentation. It does not identify the nonreduced singular scheme of a separately defined modular special fibre. The closed characteristic-two quadric and its line arrangement are special to the primitive symmetric integral presentation. The priority assessment is provisional, especially against specialist treatments of integral Igusa models and Dolgachev's characteristic-two construction.
