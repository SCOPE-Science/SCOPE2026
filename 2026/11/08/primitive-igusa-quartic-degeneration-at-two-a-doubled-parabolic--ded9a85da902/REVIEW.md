# Review of the primitive Igusa degeneration and its rational line boundary

## Correctness — PASS
Newton identities make the familiar characteristic-zero Igusa equation equal to \(-4\) times the specified primitive polynomial. The latter has reduction \(\sigma_2^2\), so the hypersurface is flat with nonreduced double-quadric special fibre. The quadratic polar radical is anisotropic, proving smoothness of its reduced support. The fifteen matching-indexed integral line families satisfy the quartic equation exactly. A projective binary line on the quadric joins two disjoint duads, and hence every such line is one of the fifteen flat limits. The incidence count gives the all-extension complement formula and its zeta function. The standalone enumerator checks binary and quartic-field base cases. No extrapolation is used.

## Originality — PASS, with material qualifications
The abstract Cremona–Richmond \((15_3)\) configuration is classical and excluded from the novelty claim. Ren–Sam–Sturmfels explain its symplectic form over characteristic zero. Dolgachev's 2024 characteristic-two modular construction is the closest published result: it identifies a quadric and a fifteen-line configuration, but its fixed locus is an **inseparable double cover**, unlike the **nonreduced double Cartier divisor** in this precise primitive integral flat family. The inspected source does not formulate the flat-limit identification for this primitive model or the exact finite-field complement zeta function. Earlier work on the same abstract line incidence does not by itself determine the primitive family and reduction. Exact published prior results may still exist; priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Bad reduction of the classical Igusa hypersurface is a natural issue because an unnormalized symmetric defining equation vanishes identically when reduced modulo two. Specifying the unique primitive polynomial resolves that genuine integral-model ambiguity and shows a sharp geometric failure of reducedness. The fifteen distinguished boundary curves land exactly on every rational generator line of the smooth reduced quadric, exhausting all of its base-field points. This gives a complete and geometrically motivated arithmetic boundary statement, not an arbitrary sample or a claim that the familiar incidence combinatorics itself is new. The point-free complement and uniform zeta function distinguish this flat closure from the modular characteristic-two inseparable-cover model.

## Primary sources and remaining limitations
Ren–Sam–Sturmfels, arXiv:1303.1132, §2 (2013); Dolgachev, DOI:10.1112/blms.13033, §3 (2024). The latter gives strong related geometric coverage, and specialist work on integral models could contain the exact degeneration. Nothing here establishes a canonical good model or independent validation.
