# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is explicitly on symbolic positive-height least-period points. The positive-drift Dyck-to-full-shift bijection counts \(\binom n{(n-d)/2}M^{(n+d)/2}\) fixed-period points. Repetitions carry both period and height multiplicatively, so two-variable Möbius inversion proves the exact primitive formula; checking \(d=1\) and \(d=2\) gives the parity correction. Binomial large-deviation and Stirling bounds yield the sharp near-neutral prefactors; an exponentially small repeat correction permits the standard binomial Gaussian limit. A full exact small-period enumeration corroborates the formulas but does not substitute for their proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Takahasi (2024) concerns unrefined multiplier classes and convergence of periodic distributions, while Takahasi (2025) provides level-1/level-2 large deviation principles and the rate function for the opening/closing frequency. Those imply neither the exact finite-\(n\) least-period fixed-height divisor spectrum nor the odd/even correction and sharp rare-shell prefactors. The 2014 unrefined prime-orbit paper does not state a height-refined period formula. The full 2005 Hamachi–Inoue paper was not obtainable, leaving a specific residual risk; its known total-count results are credited, and no claim is made to invent the underlying positive-drift bijection.

## Value — PASS
The small-height classes represent the least nonneutral symbolic multipliers and hence the weakest strictly nonzero finite-period drift. A global LDP only fixes their exponential rarity and cannot distinguish the parity-specific primitive period-half subtraction or its sharp polynomial prefactor. The exact census supplies the natural unweighted primitive-orbit invariant, while the Gaussian law contrasts weak exceptional drift with the typical periodic multiplier. This is a quantitative refinement of a specifically motivated hyperbolicity statistic rather than an arbitrary fixed-digit enumeration.

## Boundaries and residual risk
The result is restricted to symbolic Dyck shift periodic points, not exact periodic-point counts for any particular geometric realization. An earlier specialized multiplier-height enumeration, especially in Hamachi–Inoue (2005), could diminish originality. No independent audit has occurred.
