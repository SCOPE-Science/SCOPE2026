# Review of the quadratic hypersimplex sampling theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is proved for all integers \(n\ge5\), \(2\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). The proof constructs a complete orthogonal decomposition of the traceless symmetric matrix space, evaluates exact singleton and pair-subset incidence moments on each summand, and proves that the cross block vanishes. This yields both complete singular spectra, including the \(n=2k\) kernel. The ratio of the two scalars reduces to a single integer Pell equation, and the positive solutions give every allowed tight pair. The projective fourth-moment identity follows from tightness and the orbit second moment. The checker uses rational arithmetic to confirm exact Gram polynomials and the Pell recurrence, but the infinite claims rely on the proof rather than finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority
Source-level comparisons include the full mathematical setup and spherical framelet section of Xiao–Zhuang (arXiv:2303.05365), the projective \((t,t)\)-design definition and examples in Hughes–Waldron (2018), and the Petersen-code hypersimplex case in Bachoc–Vallentin (2007). The known \(28\)-line example for \((n,k)=(8,2)\) is explicitly excluded from novelty. None of the inspected statements supplies both explicit singular values at every \((n,k)\) and the Pell-only classification of tight orbits. Searches of related indexed records uncovered adjacent Johnson-face concentration and tight-frame results but not a statement that implies this one. A general unlocated association-scheme or projective-design theorem remains a material historical-priority risk; these searches do not prove global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The degree-two harmonic band is the first nontrivial setting where evaluating all centered constant-weight subset vectors gives more than one symmetry sector. Exact spectra give immediate sharp conditioning and an exact rank-loss boundary, and the Pell equation unexpectedly classifies infinitely many perfectly conditioned real projective designs. The result connects constant-weight Johnson geometry, quadratic harmonic sampling, and exact fourth-moment cubature; the construction at \((27,6)\) is not a trivial repetition of the classical \((8,2)\) example.

## Limits
The result pertains only to the full equal-weight orbit, real quadratic harmonics, and Euclidean measurement norms; it does not provide optimality among arbitrary samples, robust noisy estimation, or a regular spherical \(4\)-design without antipodal symmetrization. Historical priority remains subject to independent checking.
