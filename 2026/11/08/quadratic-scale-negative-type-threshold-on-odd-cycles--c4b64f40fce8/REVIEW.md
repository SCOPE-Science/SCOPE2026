# Scientific review: quadratic-scale negative type of odd cycles

## Correctness — PASS
The exact Fourier spectrum at exponent one follows by differentiating the finite geometric sum. It is strictly negative on all nonconstant frequencies of every odd cycle. The perturbed spectrum, after division by the positive factor \(n^{t/n^2}\), has a uniform \(O_T(n^{-2})\) Taylor error because \(x\log^2x\) is bounded at the origin. The normalized derivative coefficients converge by Riemann sums at fixed frequency; the bounded-variation Abel estimate provides the required \(O(1/j)\) uniform high-frequency tail. The integral identity for even modes gives a strictly maximal scaled derivative at frequency two. These jointly imply the two-sided asymptotic threshold, not just an upper bound. Fourier diagonalization and Schoenberg's equivalence provide the metric conclusions.

The packaged numerical checker corroborates the exact exponent-one eigenvalues and sign-crossing order in eight odd cycle sizes. The proof does not depend on the checker or extrapolate from it.

## Originality — PASS under bounded historical comparison
The directly compared antecedents are: Graovac--Jashari--Strunje (1985), exact *unpowered* cycle distance spectrum; Horak--LaRose--Moore--Rooney--Rosenthal (2010), the *four-point* roundness of cycles; Sánchez (2011), general negative-type determinant method, the five-cycle and a nonsharp odd-cycle bound; and Murugan (2011), general spectral machinery for vertex-transitive metric spaces. None of their inspected statements implies the explicit \(n^{-2}\) coefficient in the generalized-roundness threshold. The four-point bound is of order \(n^{-1}\) above one and therefore cannot determine the finer negative-type scale. Published mathematical record searches and checked historical claims revealed no matching constant, equivalence, or dominating theorem.

Residual risk is an unlocated powered-distance cycle spectrum or previous sharp asymptotic result, including possible consequences of general metric-transform theory. A failed literature search cannot establish unconditional priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The shortest-path metric on odd cycles is a canonical finite Cayley metric and a model for convergence to the continuous circle. Its supremal negative-type exponent is an intrinsic, nontrivial Hilbert-embeddability threshold. Determining its first asymptotic term, including the constant and the genuinely controlling Fourier frequency, is substantially more informative than the previously known convergence to one. The contrasting \(n^{-1}\) behavior of ordinary four-point roundness reveals a quantitative separation between related metric invariants in a natural infinite family.

## Scientific boundaries
The theorem is asymptotic, not a complete closed formula for every finite odd cycle. A numerical first-frequency observation for the tested orders is explicitly not promoted to a universal statement. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
