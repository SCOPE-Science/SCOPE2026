# Review: quadratic singular-number decay of Cesàro/shift-power commutators

## Correctness — PASS
The matrix identity \(CS^m-S^mC=-S^mB_m\) is established by a direct coefficient calculation and is checked exactly on finite truncations. The factorable row weights have two-sided quadratic bounds. The upper singular-value estimate follows from a dyadic diagonal-block singular-number count and an exponentially approximable rank-one-per-block remainder. The lower estimate uses mutually orthogonal zero-sum block subspaces whose images remain in their blocks, together with the norm bound for the inverse discrete summation matrix. The min–max argument proves the lower estimate at every index, not merely on a subsequence. The partial-fraction calculation gives the exact Hilbert–Schmidt norm. The finite checker reports 20,678 exact assertions; it is corroboration, not a substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge; priority not certified)
Sang's full accessible 2026 paper resolves the membership of the Cesàro operator in the full Toeplitz \(C^*\)-algebra and records Barría–Halmos's Hilbert–Schmidt asymptotic-Toeplitz result. Its sections 1–5 do not state two-sided \(n^{-2}\) approximation-number decay for the ordinary commutators \([C,S^m]\). A general Rhaly operator reduction is already known and is credited: the 2025 Bellavita–Dellepiane–Stylogiannis abstract explicitly treats \(\mathcal S_p\) with \(p>1\), not the sharp weak-\(\mathcal S_{1/2}\) threshold. The accessible summary of their 2026 sequel concerns dyadic Schatten-membership criteria; without its full text, an exact general approximation-number theorem cannot be excluded. Published mathematical-result searches located a trace-class characterization of monotone Rhaly matrices, results on Cesàro *self*-commutators and compact generalized Cesàro operators, but no covering quadratic singular-number statement for these shift-power commutators. Distinctions are implications rather than title-only comparisons. The 2026 follow-up and classical discrete Volterra literature remain residual priority risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The basic Cesàro operator is noncompact and has a delicate place in the Toeplitz operator algebra. A qualitative Hilbert–Schmidt commutator estimate does not determine its optimal compact-ideal regularity. This theorem computes the exact *rate class* of every shift-power commutator, including a strict weak/strong endpoint separation below trace class and a closed formula for the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. It resolves a coherent spectral approximation question for a canonical operator family, not a disconnected numerical exercise. It does not determine the sharp leading coefficient or address arbitrary symbols.
