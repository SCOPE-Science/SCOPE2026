# Scientific review of the Ciani bitangent splitting theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The asserted domain is every odd prime power and precisely the smooth parameters outside \(\{-2,-1,2\}\). The three-family construction supplies four permanent, twelve all-nonzero, and twelve coordinate-sparse bitangents over an algebraic closure. The displayed restriction-to-line square identities are exact and independently reproduced by `verify_symbolic.py`. Smoothness and disjointness are checked under the same parameter restrictions, and the established 28-bitangent theorem for smooth genus-three plane quartics in characteristic not two proves exhaustion. The two nonzero-square conditions follow in both directions from reciprocal-root identities, giving the rationality formula. Since every nonzero ground-field element becomes a square over the quadratic extension, the Frobenius-cycle conclusion is rigorous. Direct independent square-restriction enumerations agree in 201 prime-field cases and 29 quadratic-extension-field cases. The finite tests are checks, not a substitute for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to the identified priority risk
The starting Ciani pencil, four permanent bitangents, automorphism symmetries, and geometric orbit sizes are prior knowledge. Edge (1945) states the four lines and studies special hyperflexes, without the two-character finite-field test. Janasz–Pokora–Zieliński (arXiv:2308.16514, §3) study complex arrangements and quadruple points, not finite-field bitangent descent. Bethea–Brazelton (arXiv:2410.09242, Theorem 1.1 and §4.4) prove the complex \(S_4\)-orbits have sizes \(4,12,12\), but orbit sizes alone do not specify which two twelve-line families are fixed by Frobenius at a given finite-field parameter. Their supplementary tables list selected numerical complex examples, not parameter-wise finite-field square criteria. The Betten–Kaplan–Karaoglu finite-field database is field-specific and does not give a uniform formula over all odd finite fields. No inspected prior article or mathematical catalogue was found that states or directly implies the exact two-character formula across the complete pencil. A classical full explicit bitangent computation in an uninspected older source might contain an equivalent specialization, so historical priority remains a residual risk; the absence of search hits is not a proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Frobenius action on all 28 geometric bitangents is a natural arithmetic refinement of a classical genus-three automorphism stratum. The two-character formula classifies this action for every odd prime power and every smooth pencil parameter, yields a sharp three-valued rational-bitangent spectrum, and proves universal quadratic splitting of the entire bitangent configuration. This goes beyond checking a few finite curves and gives an exact field-of-definition bound for an infinite family. There is no claimed statement about tangency points or all genus-three quartics.

## Sources and limitations
The comparison uses the full relevant complex-geometric and orbit-classification sections of arXiv:2308.16514 and arXiv:2410.09242, the selected-curve supplementary bitangent tables, the short original Edge article, and the finite-field quartic catalogue. The classical 28-bitangent theorem supplies the only external enumerative completeness step. Potential undiscovered older explicit formulas and historical rediscovery remain the main originality uncertainty. The computations use exact arithmetic; no experimental extrapolation or independent review is claimed.
