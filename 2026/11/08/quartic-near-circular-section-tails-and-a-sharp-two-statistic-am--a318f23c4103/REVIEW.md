# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is the family of three-dimensional centered ellipsoids with three pairwise distinct positive inverse-square semiaxes, under Haar-uniform central plane normals. The section eccentricity is the exact eigenvalue ratio of the restricted quadratic form. A two-coordinate Taylor expansion at each of the four and only four optical normals gives a nonsingular linearized gap with coefficient \(2\sqrt{(\beta-\alpha)(\gamma-\beta)}\). Dividing the four spherical patch areas by \(4\pi\) proves the quartic tail and its constant. Interlacing and an explicit principal plane prove the exact maximum; a perfect-square factorization proves the sharp bound; a scalar rational involution proves both branches of the inverse classification. The biaxial formulas follow by direct diagonalization and uniform cosine measure. The verifier checks the algebra and eigenvalues independently, but no finite simulation is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority caveat

The exact optical-plane directions are classical (Hayes, 1998); they are not the claim. The full random-cut filamentarity approach of Makarenko, Fletcher and Shukurov (2014), and Landrø's cross-sectional aspect-ratio histograms (2015), concern related distributions but the inspected texts do not give this explicit quartic small-eccentricity coefficient, the optimal coefficient–maximum inequality, or the complete two-preimage theorem for these two scalar statistics. An earlier one-spike random-radius result covers related spheroidal information, explicitly treated here as background. The closest available public mathematical-record entries on random section volumes and one-spike ellipsoids have different observables and neither implies the triaxial coefficient and two-branch classification directly. Searches cannot rule out independently published equivalent statements not located during this review.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Near-circular random sections are a natural measure of how strongly a three-dimensional shape can masquerade as a sphere under planar observation. The quartic-to-quadratic transition identifies a genuine symmetry-class change. The sharp coefficient bound has an intrinsic equality characterization (the squared intermediate semiaxis is the arithmetic mean of the two extreme squared semiaxes), while the exact twofold ambiguity identifies the information discarded when a complete section histogram is reduced to only its support endpoint and small-eccentricity tail. These are precise geometric and inverse-problem conclusions, not merely numerical tabulations.

## Limits

The result holds for central sections sampled by Haar orientation, not arbitrary stereological selection rules. Its asymptotics are nonuniform as eigenvalue gaps collapse. No finite-sample recovery guarantee, full-law ambiguity or independent validation is asserted.
