For a strictly triaxial ellipsoid, the probability that a Haar-random central planar section has eccentricity at most \(\varepsilon\) is asymptotic to an explicit constant times \(\varepsilon^4\), whereas spheroids exhibit quadratic decay. The quartic coefficient and the maximal possible squared eccentricity satisfy a sharp inequality, and generically identify exactly two—not one—non-similar triaxial shapes.
