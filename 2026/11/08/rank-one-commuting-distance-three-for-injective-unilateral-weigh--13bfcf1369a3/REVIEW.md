# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For \(1\le p<\infty\), the orbit of the first coordinate vector spans a dense set because every unilateral shift weight is nonzero. The commutation equation uniquely determines all basis columns of a bounded operator from its first column, with the exact coefficient \(c_k d_{n+k}/(d_kd_n)\). A non-scalar commuting operator has a first nonzero strictly lower diagonal; every putative eigenvector's first nonzero coordinate forces its eigenvalue to be the common diagonal, while the next forced coordinate is nonzero. Thus it has empty point spectrum. A nonzero finite-rank commuting partner would supply an invariant finite-dimensional range and therefore an eigenvector over \(\mathbb C\), a contradiction. The known parity chain proves the sharp upper bound. For the explicit decaying shift, power norms and Schatten sums are exact. The finite checker passed 542 grouped cases but is not treated as proof of infinite point-spectrum absence.

## Originality — PASS, with residual source risk

Szczepanski (arXiv:2507.14297v2, Proposition 3.2 and Remark 3.4) already gives the three-edge upper bound; this contribution does not relabel that upper bound as new. His Section 4, Problem 4.5 explicitly asks whether two edges always suffice for nonzero compact quasinilpotents, with Volterra as a positive example. The new lower bound, proved through the structure of the entire commuting algebra, gives a negative example satisfying still stronger Schatten regularity. Searches for commuting-shift chains, non-scalar commutant eigenvalues, equivalent formulations, and published related results found no covering statement. Ambrozie–Bračič–Kuzma–Müller (2013) remains a specific older full-text priority risk: only its abstract was accessible, which discusses existence of finite chains, not this exact distance. A failed search is not a novelty certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This answers a concrete published open yes/no question, and identifies the exact minimal rank-one commuting distance for an infinite natural family rather than simply repeating a known upper bound. It separates compactness, quasinilpotence, and even membership in all Schatten ideals from the existence of a short route to finite-rank operators; the mechanism is a structural empty-point-spectrum theorem for non-scalar commutant members.

## Scope of conclusion

Complex one-sided shifts with all weights nonzero; not every compact quasinilpotent, not real Banach spaces or shifts with zero weights. The proof is self-contained within this scope. Independence of this review is not claimed.
