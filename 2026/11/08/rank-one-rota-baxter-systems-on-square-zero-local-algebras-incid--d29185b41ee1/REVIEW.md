# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The image-line lemma follows by choosing inputs on which the rank-one functional is nonzero, then comparing the scalar and radical coordinates of a square proportional to its generator. All four possibilities for two nonzero image-line types, together with each one-zero boundary, are exhausted. In the radical/radical case the two vector equations force both defining functionals to vanish on the span of both images; the proof explicitly treats all four zero/nonzero combinations of their restrictions to the radical. Each converse is verified by substitution. Projective-line and annihilator-space counts produce the seven disjoint formulas without overcounting operator representatives. The incidence-stratum dimensions use two nonzero rank-one bundle fibers over irreducible bases. Direct exhaustive testing of the original two identities on seven finite configurations passed; these computations do not establish the arbitrary-field theorem by themselves.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)

Brzeziński (arXiv:1503.05073v1, 2015), Definition 2.1, provides the two-operator system; its Lemma 2.3 and Example 2.4 give structured examples with module-linearity or orthogonal multipliers, not the present all-rank-at-most-one census. The present theorem admits operators nonzero on the radical despite vanishing on the identity, which cannot be multiplier operators. Prior single-operator Rota–Baxter classifications, including those of Guo–Rosenkranz–Zheng, overlap the diagonal \(R=S\) specialization and are credited rather than asserted novel. Relevant search and full-text checks did not identify an implication yielding the entire coupled classification, incidence dimensions and finite-field counts. A broader unlocated two-operator classification could still dominate the result; the search cannot certify publication priority.

## Value — PASS

The coupled two-operator identities define the natural first nontrivial finite-dimensional test case for Rota–Baxter systems. Their rank-one locus is not just the diagonal Rota–Baxter locus: it has mixed scalar/radical solutions and separate equal/distinct radical-image incidence strata. The theorem gives a complete characteristic-free classification and an exact joint finite-field census for arbitrary radical dimension. The geometric dimension tie at \(m=3\) and reversal at \(m=4\) are structural information beyond simply checking low-dimensional examples. The finding remains deliberately limited to rank at most one and does not assert a full irreducible-component decomposition.

## Closest literature and residual risk

- T. Brzeziński, *Rota-Baxter systems, dendriform algebras and covariant bialgebras*, arXiv:1503.05073v1 (2015): exact system definition and standard examples; no identified classification of all rank-one pairs on this family.
- S. Zheng, L. Guo and M. Rosenkranz, *Classification of Rota-Baxter operators on semigroup algebras of order two and three*, arXiv:1402.3702v1 (2014), published 2019: a single-operator classification, not the two-operator pair census.
- Related rank-one and square-zero single-operator classifications may determine special diagonal sections; those sections are not independently claimed novel.

The most significant scientific risk is an unidentified general rank-one coupled-system theorem or a stronger classification in the operated-algebra literature. The finite checks do not certify infinity, and publication priority is not independently established.
