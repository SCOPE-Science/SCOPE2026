# Scientific review of the rank-spectrum theorem

## Correctness (PASS)
The statement counts all idempotent Nijenhuis operators by rank for each nonzero defining endomorphism over every finite field. For an idempotent, its torsion vanishes precisely when both image and kernel are Lie subalgebras; this follows by evaluation on the image/image, kernel/kernel and mixed summands, including characteristic two. Every such complementary pair lies in exactly one of three cases relative to the codimension-one abelian ideal. The two one-sided cases contribute the invariant-subspace terms, while the two-sided case contributes an exactly counted complement incidence of \((q-1)q^m\) for every disjoint invariant pair. The proof correctly includes the zero and identity maps and the squarefree and Jordan specializations. All eleven direct exhaustive finite-field computations reproduce the exact rank spectra. The code is corroboration, not the infinite-family proof.

## Originality (PASS, provisional priority)
Avetisyan's Proposition 3 already classifies the underlying Lie subalgebras and is explicitly credited; the projection-to-subalgebra criterion is also established background. The new mathematical assertion is the uniform *ordered complementary-subalgebra rank count* with a nontrivial two-sided incidence correction, together with complete rank spectra in Jordan/irreducible and squarefree classes. The inspected Nijenhuis deformation source contains the original torsion concept but not this finite-field rank enumerator. Similar operator enumerations on semisimple Lie algebras, free class-two Lie algebras, and associative matrix algebras involve different defining identities or structures and do not imply the formula for arbitrary almost-Abelian actions. Searches for equivalent and stronger counts did not identify a decisive covering theorem. Priority is provisional: an unlocated general invariant-subspace incidence formula or projector count could imply this specialization.

## Value (PASS)
The rank distribution of idempotent Nijenhuis maps is a natural invariant of the Lie algebra and controls the availability of direct decompositions into complementary Lie subalgebras. The theorem gives every rank over every finite field and dimension for the entire almost-Abelian family. It visibly distinguishes irreducible, cyclic Jordan, and scalar actions, including the disappearance of all intermediate ranks under irreducible actions of sufficiently large dimension. This is a complete invariant calculation, not an arbitrary small case or a restatement of subalgebra normal forms. The disjoint-invariant-pair term isolates the extra incidence arising when neither side lies in the abelian ideal.

## Closest literature and scope limits

Nunes da Costa, *Nijenhuis forms on \(L_\infty\)-algebras and Poisson geometry*, arXiv:1308.6119: the Nijenhuis condition and general deformation theory, not the finite-field rank spectra here. This is an enumeration of individual idempotent maps, not conjugacy orbits, all Nijenhuis operators, or nonreduced schemes. Independent validation and exhaustive historical-priority verification have not been performed.
