# Rank-two Segre configuration loci: components, intersections, and motivic class

## Finding

For the Segre embedding of \(X=\prod_{j=1}^r\mathbb P^{m_j}\) with \(r\ge2\) and every \(m_j\ge1\), the reduced locus \(Z\subset X^n\) of \(n\ge3\) ordered points spanning a projective line or point has exactly \(r+2^{n-1}-1\) irreducible components. The components, all pairwise intersections, and the class of \(Z\) in the Grothendieck ring of varieties are given explicitly below. This is a closed geometric and motivic description for the rank-at-most-two case of Segre-constrained determinantal matrices.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of arbitrary characteristic. Fix \(r\ge2\), integers \(m_1,\ldots,m_r\ge1\), and \(n\ge3\). Put \(D=\sum_{j=1}^r m_j\), \(X=\prod_{j=1}^r\mathbb P^{m_j}_k\) and \(M=\prod_{j=1}^r(m_j+1)-1\). Let \(\sigma:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^M\) be the standard Segre embedding. Define \(Z\) as the **reduced** closed subvariety of \(X^n\) cut out set-theoretically by the \(3\times3\) minors of the \(n\times(M+1)\) matrix whose rows represent the \(\sigma(P_i)\). Thus \(Z\) is the set of ordered tuples of Segre points of vector-span dimension at most two. No claim about the unreduced determinantal scheme or embedded associated primes is made.

For each index \(j\), set \(B_j=\prod_{h\ne j}\mathbb P^{m_h}\). Define \(L_j\) to consist of tuples with every factor except the \(j\)-th constant across the \(n\) entries and with the \(j\)-th factors lying on some projective line in \(\mathbb P^{m_j}\). For every nontrivial bipartition \(\{S,S^c\}\) of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), define \(E_S\) by \(P_i=A\) when \(i\in S\) and \(P_i=B\) when \(i\notin S\), for some \(A,B\in X\); use one representative per unordered bipartition. The small diagonal is \(\Delta X=\{P_1=\cdots=P_n\}\).

## Proof

**1. Quadratic trichotomy.** The homogeneous ideal of a Segre variety is generated by quadratic flattening minors. If a projective line meets the Segre image in three distinct points, then every defining quadratic vanishes at three points on that line and hence vanishes identically. Thus the entire line lies in the Segre image. A line contained in a Segre variety varies exactly one projective factor: for any pair of distinct decomposable tensor points defining such a line, if two factors changed then a two-factor flattening of a general linear combination would have rank two, contrary to decomposability. Conversely, varying one factor along a line plainly gives a line in the Segre image. Therefore any tuple in \(Z\) either has at most two distinct entries (and belongs to some \(E_S\)), or has at least three distinct entries on a line in the Segre image (and belongs to a unique \(L_j\)).

**2. Components.** Each \(E_S\) is a closed embedded copy of \(X\times X\); its dimension is \(2D\). Each \(L_j\) is closed: the fixed-factor equations and the rank-at-most-two condition on the \(j\)-th coordinate matrix are closed. The subset with at least two distinct \(j\)-th coordinates has a unique spanning projective line, hence is isomorphic to a nonempty open subset of the incidence family \(B_j\times\bigl((\mathbb P\mathcal U)^{\times_{\operatorname{Gr}} n}\bigr)\), where \(\mathcal U\) is the tautological rank-two bundle. This family is irreducible and has dimension \((D-m_j)+2(m_j-1)+n=D+m_j+n-2\), and the diagonal closure fills \(L_j\). Thus \(L_j\) is irreducible. Each \(E_S\) has a dense open subset where its two values differ in every factor, and so lies on no \(L_j\); distinct two-value components have different equality patterns on dense opens. Each \(L_j\) has a dense open subset with at least three distinct entries and so is contained in no \(E_S\) or \(L_h\) for \(h\ne j\). Consequently these are precisely all the irreducible components. There are \(2^{n-1}-1\) unordered nontrivial bipartitions.

**3. Intersection arrangement.** Two distinct ruling components intersect precisely on the small diagonal, because their common tuples must be constant in every factor. For two distinct bipartitions, being constant on the classes of both implies being constant on all indices; hence \(E_S\cap E_T=\Delta X\). Finally, a two-value tuple belongs to \(L_j\) precisely when the two values agree in all factors except possibly the \(j\)-th. The two \(j\)-th values may be arbitrary, because any pair lies on a line. Hence, as reduced varieties, \(E_S\cap L_j\cong B_j\times\mathbb P^{m_j}\times\mathbb P^{m_j}\). These three statements include all boundary coincidences.

**4. Grothendieck-class formula.** Write \(X_0=[X]\), \(P_j=[\mathbb P^{m_j}]\), \(G_j=[\operatorname{Gr}(2,m_j+1)]\), and \(N=2^{n-1}-1\), in \(K_0(\operatorname{Var}_k)\). In \(L_j\), the all-equal locus has class \([B_j]P_j\). Every other tuple determines a unique projective line in its varying factor. The incidence with its diagonal removed is a Zariski-locally trivial \((\mathbb P^1)^n\setminus\Delta\mathbb P^1\) bundle over the Grassmannian. Therefore
\[
[L_j]=[B_j]\Bigl(P_j+G_j\bigl([\mathbb P^1]^n-[\mathbb P^1]\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
Since distinct \(L_j\) meet only in \(\Delta X\),
\[
[\bigcup_jL_j]=\sum_j[L_j]-(r-1)X_0.
\]
For a fixed bipartition \(S\), its intersection with \(\bigcup_jL_j\) is the union of the \(r\) copies of \(B_j\times\mathbb P^{m_j}\times\mathbb P^{m_j}\), which meet pairwise only along \(\Delta X\). Different \(E_S\) meet only inside \(\Delta X\), already counted. Thus the scissor relation yields
\[
\boxed{[Z]=\sum_{j=1}^r[B_j]\Bigl(P_j+G_j\bigl([\mathbb P^1]^n-[\mathbb P^1]\bigr)\Bigr)-(r-1)X_0+N\Bigl(X_0^2-\sum_{j=1}^r[B_j]P_j^2+(r-1)X_0\Bigr).}
\]
This is an exact identity in the Grothendieck ring, not just an Euler-characteristic identity.

**5. Equidimensionality.** Component dimensions are \(2D\) for every \(E_S\) and \(D+m_j+n-2\) for \(L_j\). They are all equal exactly when \(m_1=\cdots=m_r=m\) and \(n=D-m+2=(r-1)m+2\). This gives the claimed sharp criterion.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` enumerates finite-field points of the Segre configuration locus, computes the rank of each row matrix by modular Gaussian elimination, and compares the result with the motivic expression evaluated under the point-counting ring homomorphism \(\mathbb L\mapsto q\). It tests five cases: \((q;(m_j);n)=(2;(1,1);3),(2;(1,1);4),(3;(1,1);3),(2;(1,2);3),(2;(1,1,1);3)\). Expected point counts are respectively \(261,729,928,1449,2295\). These tests are only independent finite corroboration. The argument above proves the identities for every \(r,m_j,n\) in the stated ranges and every algebraically closed characteristic.

## Relationship to prior work

Alstad, Duff and Katzman, *Segre-Determinantal Loci and the Image Variety for Three Flatland Cameras* (arXiv:2606.31965v1, first public 2026-06-30), study the hyperplane-containing case, proving primality, expected codimension and a universal Gröbner basis for maximal minors. Their Remark 2.2 identifies higher-codimension common-subspace loci as a harder extension. The present result addresses the extreme first non-hyperplane condition: the span of the points has projective dimension at most one, or equivalently all \(3\times3\) minors vanish. For \(X=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) this is their codimension-two common-subspace case; for general \(X\) the codimension of the containing line is \(M-1\). The rank-two condition produces a reducible variety with exponentially many collision-pattern components, rather than the prime hyperplane locus.

Pratt, *The Segre Determinant* (arXiv:2505.09204v1, 2025-05-14), treats the common-bilinear-hypersurface determinant, not a stated complete motivic description of tuples spanning a line. The classification of lines inside Segre varieties and the generation of their ideals by quadrics are classical; neither is asserted to be new. The additional statement here is the complete intersection arrangement and its Grothendieck-class formula for arbitrary products and tuple lengths. The motivating paper’s Section 2 and Example 2.7 were also inspected. Example 2.7 proves nonprimality for the ideal of 4×4 minors in the three-factor \(\mathbb P^1\) case, but neither classifies the components of the 3×3-minor locus nor computes its motivic class. The corresponding lower-rank family theorem is not a consequence of the nonprimality example. A differently named classical configuration-space treatment remains a historical-priority risk.

## Limitations

The claim applies to the **reduced** rank-two locus and makes no assertion about radicality, primary decomposition, Cohen–Macaulayness, or embedded primes of the unreduced minors ideal. It does not classify the higher-rank common-linear-subspace loci. The basic incidence trichotomy is elementary, and the originality is confined to the combined arbitrary-format component/intersection and motivic formula. Full historical priority and independent validation remain outstanding.
