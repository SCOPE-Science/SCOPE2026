# Rank-two translation fixed spaces in the scale-three Hamming Rips filtration

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge2\) and every two-dimensional binary linear subspace \(W\le\mathbb F_2^n\), let \(X_{n,r}=\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(\mathbb F_2^n)\) for the Hamming metric, and let \(W\) act on the vertices by translations. Write \(s(W)\) for the number of coordinates on which some element of \(W\) is nonzero, and \(d(W)=\max_{w\in W}|w|\), where \(|w|\) is Hamming weight. All fixed sets below are **geometric**, not vertexwise-fixed subcomplexes or homology invariants.

**Complete fixed-space phase diagram through scale three.** The space \(|X_{n,r}|^W\) is empty for \(0\le r<2\). At scale \(2\), it consists of exactly \(2^{n-2}\) isolated points if \(d(W)\le2\), and is empty otherwise. At scale \(3\) there are exactly three possibilities:

\[
|X_{n,3}|^W\cong_{\mathrm{PL}}
\begin{cases}
\varnothing,&d(W)>3,\\
|Q_{n-2}|,&d(W)\le3,\ s(W)\le3,\\
\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{2^{n-4}}|C_4|,&s(W)=4,\ d(W)\le3.
\end{cases}
\]

Here \(|Q_k|\) means the **one-dimensional cubical graph** of the \(k\)-cube, not its filled cubical body; \(Q_0\) is one point. In the last case all nonzero codewords have weights \(2,3,3\). No two-dimensional \(W\) with larger support can have a nonempty fixed space at scale three. For \(n\ge3\), the cube-graph case has integral homotopy type \(\bigvee^{(n-4)2^{n-3}+1}S^1\), where zero circle summands means a point; for \(n=2\) it is a point. The remaining nonempty case is a disjoint union of \(2^{n-4}\) circles.

Among all two-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_2^n\), there are precisely \(\binom n2+\binom n3\) whose fixed set is nonempty at scale two, and at scale three there are precisely \(\binom n2+4\binom n3\) cube-graph cases and \(6\binom n4\) disjoint-circle cases. All other subspaces give the empty fixed space at scale three. The inclusion from scale two to three is the inclusion of the full vertex set of the connected cube graph whenever the scale-two fixed set is nonempty.

## Assumptions and scope

The ambient binary cube is a finite Hamming metric space. The inclusive Rips convention admits a simplex exactly when all mutual Hamming distances are at most the scale. The subgroup has order four and consists solely of translations. Isomorphism means a piecewise-linear homeomorphism between geometric realizations. All binomial coefficients with upper index smaller than lower index equal zero. The scale-three graph \(Q_{n-2}\) has \(2^{n-2}\) vertices and, for \(n\ge3\), \((n-2)2^{n-3}\) edges. The statements cover every \(n\ge2\), including the exceptional zeroth cube dimension.

## Proof

**1. Orbit-fixed model.** Let \(G\) be a finite group permuting the vertices of any finite simplicial complex \(K\). In a point fixed by \(G\), barycentric coordinates are constant on each complete vertex orbit. A nonzero orbit weight is permitted exactly when that entire orbit is a face. Normalized weights over admissible orbits identify \(|K|^G\) PL-homeomorphically with a simplicial complex whose vertices are admissible vertex orbits and whose simplices are families of orbits with union a simplex of \(K\). Since a Vietoris–Rips complex is flag, this orbit complex is the clique complex of the graph joining orbit vertices exactly when every cross-orbit pair has distance at most the scale.

**2. Translate and classify the small code.** Every translation orbit is a coset \(x+W\) of size four and has diameter \(d(W)\). Thus no admissible orbit exists for \(r<d(W)\), proving the empty cases. To classify \(d(W)\le3\), choose a basis for \(W\) and group its coordinate columns into three nonzero types \((1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\) with multiplicities \(a,b,c\); zero columns correspond to unused coordinates. The weights of the three nonzero words are \(a+c,b+c,a+b\), all at most three. Because the matrix has rank two, at least two of \(a,b,c\) are positive. The pair-sum inequalities force \(a+b+c\le4\). Up to permuting coordinates and changing the chosen basis, the only cases are:

* Support two: the full \(\mathbb F_2^2\), weights \(1,1,2\).
* Support three: either the even-parity plane with weights \(2,2,2\), or the plane generated by \(110\) and \(001\), with weights \(1,2,3\).
* Support four: the plane generated by \(1110\) and \(0111\), with weights \(2,3,3\).

For support two, each coset is labeled by a tail \(y\in\mathbb F_2^{n-2}\); its maximum cross-orbit distance to the coset at \(z\) equals \(2+d_H(y,z)\). Hence the scale-three orbit graph is exactly \(Q_{n-2}\). At scale two it is discrete.

For support three, let \(m=n-3\). There are two cosets of the active three-coordinate plane, labeled by \(p\in\mathbb F_2\), and independently a tail \(y\in\mathbb F_2^m\). In the even-parity case, active-coordinate maximum differences equal two within a parity type and three between types. Thus at scale three the graph has edges \((p,y)(p,z)\) for \(d_H(y,z)=1\) and \((0,y)(1,y)\), namely \(Q_m\mathbin\square K_2\cong Q_{n-2}\). In the mixed-weight case, the active-coordinate maximum is three for equal types and two for different types. Thus edges run between \((0,y)\) and \((1,z)\) exactly when \(d_H(y,z)\le1\). Replacing the type label \(p\) by \(p+\sum_i y_i\pmod2\) identifies this graph with \(Q_{n-2}\). In both cases it has no triangles. At scale two the even-parity case has only isolated orbit vertices, while the mixed-weight case has none.

For support four, put \(m=n-4\). The four cosets of the active plane \(W\le\mathbb F_2^4\) form the quotient \(\mathbb F_2^4/W\). Every orbit has active diameter three. For any two distinct cosets, their active difference set is a full coset of \(W\); exactly one nonzero difference coset contains \(1111\) and thus has maximum weight four. The other two nonzero difference cosets have maximum weight three. Consequently the active orbit graph is \(K_4\) minus one perfect matching, which is \(C_4\). Since every active difference set, including \(W\) itself, has maximum weight at least three, any two orbits whose tails differ in even one coordinate have union diameter at least four. The full orbit graph is therefore a disjoint copy of \(C_4\) for each of the \(2^m\) tail choices. It has no triangles. The absence of admissible orbits at scale two follows from \(d(W)=3\).

**3. Count subgroups.** A two-dimensional binary subspace has exactly three nonzero words. For each two-element support there is one admissible plane. For each three-element support there are four full-support planes, three of mixed type and one even-parity type; the other three planes on three coordinates have smaller support. For each four-element support there are six admissible planes, indexed by the choice of the support of their unique weight-two word. No larger support occurs. Therefore the scale-three cube cases number \(\binom n2+4\binom n3\) and the circle-union cases number \(6\binom n4\). At scale two only the support-two and even-parity support-three planes remain, numbering \(\binom n2+\binom n3\). The total number of rank-two subspaces is \((2^n-1)(2^n-2)/6\), so subtraction yields the number of empty cases.

**4. Integral homotopy.** The graph \(Q_k\) is connected, with \(2^k\) vertices and \(k2^{k-1}\) edges for \(k\ge1\). Collapsing a spanning tree gives precisely \(k2^{k-1}-2^k+1\) circles (zero at \(k=1\)); \(Q_0\) is a point. The other graph is literally a disjoint union of circles. All the displayed homotopy statements are integral, and the claimed filtration inclusion follows because increasing the scale retains every admissible orbit and adds exactly the enumerated compatible edges.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` uses the standard Python library only. It directly enumerates every two-dimensional translation subgroup of \(\mathbb F_2^n\) for \(2\le n\le8\), builds each orbit from the original Hamming metric, checks scales one, two and three, constructs an explicit coordinate labeling of every predicted cube graph and checks every adjacency, and tests every circle component and parameter count. The resulting log is in `verification_output.txt`. All 14,311 subgroups checked satisfy the classification. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the proof for arbitrary \(n\).

Run with `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing the two files. No additional packages are required.

## Relationship to prior work

Galetto, Montaño and Wellner, *Homology of Vietoris-Rips complexes of hypercube graphs via group actions* (arXiv:2606.20784, publicly posted June 18, 2026), compute characteristic-zero homology representations of the entire hyperoctahedral symmetry group through scale three. Their Theorem 6.2 describes the action on ambient homology; it does not classify geometric fixed spaces of rank-two translation subgroups. Bendersky, Elia and Grbić (arXiv:2605.00705) study ambient connectivity, coconnectivity, and cohomology classes, rather than the fixed-space phase diagram. A rational character decomposition or an individual-involution fixed-space homotopy type does not, by itself, determine the common geometric fixed space of two involutions. The present scale-three result concerns this different equivariant invariant. The scale-two discrete-point case is included as a previously accessible baseline for the transition and is not separately claimed as an original theorem.

## Limitations

The result covers rank-two *translation* subgroups, not general affine or coordinate-permutation subgroups, nor higher-rank translation subgroups. It covers Rips scales at most three and makes no assertion about larger scales or the full equivariant homotopy type. The finite verifier is not an infinite proof. The literature comparison does not exclude as-yet-unindexed equivalent results; independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
