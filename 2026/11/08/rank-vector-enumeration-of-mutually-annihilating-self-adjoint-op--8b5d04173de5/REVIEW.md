# Scientific review: rank-vector enumeration of mutually annihilating self-adjoint operators

## Correctness — PASS

For a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear space, self-adjoint operators annihilating one another (including squares) have a totally isotropic joint image. The passage from operators with image in a fixed isotropic space to symmetric maps on its dual is bijective, preserves individual ranks, and translates full joint image into a zero common radical. Subspace-lattice Möbius inversion gives the exact rank-vector formula. Isotropic-frame counting and nonsingular symmetric-form counting yield the two closed factors. These proofs hold in all odd finite fields, dimensions and tuple lengths specified. Independent direct matrix enumeration found 5697 versus 81 pairs for split versus nonsplit four-spaces over the ternary field, including the full rank-vector distribution; the supplementary integer formulas agree with each coefficient. Limit: finite checks corroborate, not replace, the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

Schröer's 2002 manuscript studies geometric irreducible components of unrestricted annihilating nilpotent matrix pairs over algebraically closed fields, not counts on the fixed self-adjoint locus. Oblak (2006) classifies possible Jordan-type pairs; Bondarenko–Gerasimova–Sergeichuk (2008) gives simultaneous-similarity canonical pairs. Brouwer–Gow–Sheekey (2014) studies individual self-adjoint nilpotent operators; Huang (2021) counts unrestricted annihilating pairs by generating functions. None of the examined full primary texts contains this all-arity Witt-sensitive rank-vector finite-field formula, nor a stronger statement from which it is an immediate corollary. Multiple semantic-record queries and accessible previous claim summaries yielded no dominating record. This does not establish comprehensive historical priority: unexamined generalized orthogonal incidence enumerators could subsume the result.

## Value — PASS

The full rank-vector distribution measures the intersection of a natural annihilating representation variety with a fixed self-adjoint subspace, a setting where orthogonal Witt type is genuinely visible. The exact all-dimensional formula identifies the common isotropic image as a structural organizing invariant. The explicit split/nonsplit discrepancy of 5697 versus 81 ternary four-dimensional pairs shows that classical unrestricted annihilation counts cannot by themselves capture this invariant. The arbitrary tuple length, individual-rank control, and full image-span stratification offer substantive information beyond an isolated small experiment.
