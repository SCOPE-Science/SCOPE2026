For a reflected Brownian searcher with a newly drawn frozen Gaussian propulsion velocity on each attempt, the mean and second moment of the first-passage time grow at exponentially different rates as propulsion width increases; the corresponding moment-biased velocities concentrate near \(\sigma^2L/D\) and \(2\sigma^2L/D\). This rare outward-velocity effect makes weak Poisson resetting beneficial at large noise even for starting points where resetting is detrimental without propulsion.
