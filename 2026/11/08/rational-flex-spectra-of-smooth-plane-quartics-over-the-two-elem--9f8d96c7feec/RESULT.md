# Rational flex spectra of smooth plane quartics over the two-element field

## Finding
Let \(C\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb F_2}\) be a geometrically smooth plane quartic. A rational point \(P\in C(\mathbb F_2)\) is an **ordinary rational flex** when its tangent line has intersection multiplicity exactly \(3\) with \(C\) at \(P\), and a **rational hyperflex** when that multiplicity is \(4\). Write \(o(C)\) and \(h(C)\) for their counts. Every such curve belongs to exactly one of the following eleven strata, with the indicated numbers of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\)-classes and distinct nonzero defining quartic polynomials:

| \((o,h)\) | Projective classes | Defining equations |
|---|---:|---:|
| \((0,0)\) | 17 | 1764 |
| \((0,1)\) | 14 | 1932 |
| \((1,0)\) | 13 | 2184 |
| \((0,2)\) | 3 | 420 |
| \((1,1)\) | 7 | 1176 |
| \((2,0)\) | 10 | 1512 |
| \((0,3)\) | 1 | 28 |
| \((1,2)\) | 2 | 336 |
| \((2,1)\) | 5 | 672 |
| \((3,0)\) | 5 | 728 |
| \((4,0)\) | 1 | 168 |

In particular, \(o(C)+h(C)\leq4\), and the unique projective equivalence class attaining four rational flexes has **four ordinary flexes and no rational hyperflex**. One representative is
\[
Y Z^3+X Y Z^2+X Y^3+X^3 Z=0.
\]
The full spectrum contains \(78\) projective classes represented by \(10920\) defining quartic equations. Projective equivalence here is equivalence under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), equivalent to \(\mathbb F_2\)-isomorphism for smooth nonhyperelliptic genus-three curves by their canonical embeddings.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is exactly \(\mathbb F_2\); geometric smoothness means no singular point over its algebraic closure. Every homogeneous degree-four form in three variables is eligible, and each nonzero coefficient vector occurs exactly once. The flex count concerns only rational curve points and rational tangent contact. It makes no assertion that all geometric flexes are rational, or about higher extensions of the field.

## Proof
Write the \(15\) degree-four monomials in the precise order
\[
\{X^aY^bZ^{4-a-b}:0\leq a\leq4,\ 0\leq b\leq4-a\},
\]
ordered increasingly first by \(a\), then by \(b\). Represent each homogeneous form by its coefficient bit mask from \(1\) through \(2^{15}-1\). Since \(\mathbb F_2^\times\) is trivial, these represent every nonzero form without scalar repetition.

Generate \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)=\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) with the two adjacent coordinate transpositions and the elementary shear \(X\mapsto X+Y\). Polynomial substitutions and breadth-first orbit closure partition all \(32767\) nonzero forms into projective orbits, with each orbit size dividing \(168\). The code checks the full partition, so no representative is omitted.

Geometric smoothness is checked *exactly*, not merely on \(\mathbb F_2\)-points: on each of the three affine charts \(X_i=1\), form the ideal generated by the dehomogenized quartic and all three dehomogenized homogeneous partial derivatives. The curve is smooth over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\) if and only if all three ideals are unit ideals. Buchberger reduction in \(\mathbb F_2[u,v]\), using leading monomial reductions and all S-polynomial pairs until closure, decides this condition, by the weak Nullstellensatz. It leaves precisely \(78\) smooth projective orbits comprising \(10920\) equations.

For a rational point \(P\) on a smooth representative, its tangent is the line \(\nabla F(P)\cdot (X,Y,Z)=0\). Choose a distinct rational point \(Q\) on this line. The polynomial \(F(P+tQ)\) starts at degree at least two; its first nonzero coefficient occurs at degree \(2\), \(3\), or \(4\), and that degree is the tangent contact multiplicity at \(P\). The coefficients are calculated without differentiation approximations: for each monomial, expand the products \((P_i+tQ_i)^{e_i}\) in characteristic two using Lucas's binomial-parity criterion. A degree-three first term is an ordinary flex and a degree-four first term a hyperflex. The calculation is independent of the chosen rational \(Q\) because any other tangent-line local parameter differs by a local unit.

The first verifier computes these contact orders for each of the \(78\) geometrically smooth orbit representatives and weights by the verified orbit sizes. The second verifier reconstructs each of the \(10920\) smooth equations and independently computes all local contact coefficients via explicit multi-index binomial sums, giving the same eleven-row table. Thus the finite computation is an **exhaustive exact proof** over all objects in the stated domain, not an experiment on sample curves.

The representative with coefficient mask \(4418\) is precisely the displayed polynomial. Its four rational points are \([1:0:0]\), \([0:1:0]\), \([0:0:1]\), and \([1:1:1]\). At each the tangent restriction has initial term \(t^3\) (the last has \(t^3+t^4\)); thus all four are ordinary flexes. The orbit is of size \(168\), so its projective stabilizer is trivial. The table shows that it is the sole orbit attaining four rational flexes.

## Verification
Run the two included Python 3 programs using only the standard library, from the directory containing both files:

```sh
python3 verify_orbits.py
python3 verify_direct.py
```

The first must print `ALL_ORBITS 279 SMOOTH_PROJECTIVE_CLASSES 78 SMOOTH_EQUATIONS 10920`, its eleven stratum rows and `VERIFY_ORBITS_OK`. The second must print `DIRECT_EQUATIONS 10920`, `DIRECT_EXTREMAL_EQUATIONS 168`, and `VERIFY_DIRECT_OK`. Both programs use exact integer and bitset arithmetic and raise an assertion on any discrepancy. The two independent contact implementations agree on the complete defining-equation distribution; the algebraic smoothness test was also independently cross-compared with a computer-algebra Gröbner calculation for all \(279\) orbit representatives.

## Relationship to prior work
Nart and Ritzenthaler classified smooth nonhyperelliptic genus-three curves up to finite-field isomorphism in characteristic two and gave rational normal forms, counts of isomorphism classes, and Newton-polygon strata. That classification supplies foundational coverage of the objects, but it does not state this eleven-stratum arithmetic tangent-contact distribution or its unique extremizer. Pacini and Testa study reconstruction from geometric inflection lines; their main reconstruction result concerns other characteristics and does not supply a rational flex census over \(\mathbb F_2\). Existing work on geometric Weierstrass/flex loci and bitangents is likewise distinguished from the specific field-rational ordinary-versus-hyperflex partition. The defining objects, their isomorphism count, and the general contact criterion are not claimed as new; only the complete stated contact distribution and sharp unique extremum are claimed.

## Limitations
The result is a finite-field-specific, exhaustively certified classification; it does not imply corresponding bounds over \(\mathbb F_{2^n}\) for \(n>1\). It does not determine nonrational geometric flexes, automorphism types for every class, or new general existence theorems. Exhaustive code and arithmetic certainty do not alone establish historical priority. Older equivalent computations, especially as consequences of normal-form classifications, remain a possible residual literature risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
