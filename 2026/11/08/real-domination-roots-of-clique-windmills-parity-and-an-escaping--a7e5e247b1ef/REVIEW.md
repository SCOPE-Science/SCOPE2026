# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The domination polynomial follows from an exhaustive partition of dominating sets by presence of the universal vertex. The real axis is partitioned into positive values, the endpoints, \((-1,0)\), and \(( -\infty,-1)\); the latter is reduced to two exact parity cases. Each permitted nonzero root solves a scalar equation with one strictly decreasing and one strictly increasing side (or an explicitly strictly decreasing ratio), giving existence, uniqueness, and simplicity. Bounded limits follow by fixed-parameter bracketing, while the diverging-root asymptotic follows from an exact logarithmic equation. Direct graph enumeration and exact small-degree real-root counts are consistent; neither replaces the unrestricted proof.

## Originality

**PASS, provisional historical priority.** The 2025 diagonal generalized-friendship polynomial and its global zero annulus are previously known; the 2026 friendship-root theorem completely covers the triangular \(k=2\) case. Neither inspected paper gives the exact \(k\ge3\) windmill real-zero classification or identifies the parity-driven divergent negative root. The older cycle-flower paper concerns a different graph construction; the general real-root-density theorem cannot determine the zeros of a fixed family. Research-database and primary-literature comparisons found no implication covering the stated result. The possibility of unindexed or inaccessible literature remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Roots of domination polynomials are a motivated and active topic. This is not a restatement of the known generating polynomial: it resolves the real-root count and locations across an infinite two-parameter family. The odd versus even blade-size transition changes the number of real roots and whether a negative root remains bounded or escapes to infinity, an asymptotic phenomenon not visible in the triangular friendship case.

## Scope and residual risk

The result is restricted to identical complete blades with a universal common vertex and fixed blade size in asymptotics. It does not locate complex roots, and it has not received an independent mathematical audit. Historical priority is provisional.
