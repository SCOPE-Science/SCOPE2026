# Reciprocal-bias bipartite social pressure: the full equilibrium curve and an exact complete-bipartite flow

## Finding

Fix a bias parameter \(\gamma>1\) and define the social-pressure response
\[
 f_\eta(u)=\frac{\eta u}{1+(\eta-1)u},\qquad 0\le u\le1,\quad \eta>0.
\]
The inverse identity is \(f_{1/\gamma}=f_\gamma^{-1}\). On the two parts \(A,B\) of a finite connected bipartite social network, assign bias \(\gamma\) to all members of \(A\), and bias \(1/\gamma\) to all members of \(B\). Consider the *continuous-time mean-field flow*
\[
 \dot x_i=f_{\gamma_i}\!\left(\sum_j W_{ij}x_j\right)-x_i.
 \tag{1}
\]

**Theorem (two complementary statements).**

**(a) Complete equilibrium census on every connected weighted bipartite graph.** If every graph edge has strictly positive weight, there are no self-loops, and \(W\) is the row-normalized weighted adjacency matrix, then **all** equilibria of (1) in the cube are exactly
\[
 \mathcal E=\left\{x:\ x_i=p\ (i\in A),\quad
 x_j=\frac{p}{\gamma-(\gamma-1)p}\ (j\in B),\quad 0\le p\le1\right\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular the equilibria form a continuous arc joining the all-zero and all-one configurations; there are no other boundary or nonuniform equilibria.

**(b) Exact global solution and selection law on the unweighted complete bipartite graph.** Suppose the graph is \(K_{m,n}\), with arbitrary integers \(m,n\ge1\). Given any initial state \(x(0)\in[0,1]^{m+n}\), set
\[
 a_0=\frac1m\sum_{i\in A}x_i(0),\quad
 b_0=\frac1n\sum_{j\in B}x_j(0),\quad
 k=\gamma-1,\quad L_0=\gamma-ka_0,\quad
 R=\frac{1+kb_0}{L_0}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\gamma^{-1}\le R\le\gamma\). For every real \(t\ge0\), put
\[
 L(t)=\sqrt{\frac\gamma R+\left(L_0^2-\frac\gamma R\right)e^{-2t}},\qquad
 a(t)=\frac{\gamma-L(t)}k,\qquad
 b(t)=\frac{RL(t)-1}k.
 \tag{4}
\]
The **unique solution** of (1) is
\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 x_i(t)&=a(t)+[x_i(0)-a_0]e^{-t}&& (i\in A),\\
 x_j(t)&=b(t)+[x_j(0)-b_0]e^{-t}&& (j\in B).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{5}
\]
The nonlinear ratio \(R=[1+kb(t)]/[\gamma-ka(t)]\) is conserved exactly, and \(L(t)^2\) approaches \(\gamma/R\) exactly at exponential rate \(2\), whereas within-part deviations decay exactly at rate \(1\). Every trajectory converges to the **specified** member of (2) with
\[
 \boxed{p_* =\frac{\gamma-\sqrt{\gamma/R}}{k},\qquad
 q_*=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma R}-1}{k}.}
 \tag{6}
\]
The limiting point is an interior equilibrium unless the initial state is identically zero or identically one. Formulas (3)–(6) also hold at those two endpoints.

## Assumptions and scope

All time variables in (1)–(6) are continuous and nonnegative. The graph is finite, connected, undirected, and bipartite with parts \(A\) and \(B\), no self-loops and strictly positive weights on its edges. Row normalization is by positive weighted degree. The population can contain a single member in either part. Bias is constant within each part and *reciprocal* across parts. The state is any vector of real probabilities in the closed cube. The vector field in (1) is globally Lipschitz on the cube, and points inward (or tangentially) on each boundary face, so existence, uniqueness and cube invariance follow.

This theorem addresses the associated **continuous-time ODE** generated by the drift \(F(x)-x\). The initiating paper also studies a **discrete-time stochastic urn system** and a decreasing-gain discrete expected recursion. Equation (5) is *not* an exact solution for either discrete system; no claim is made about the distribution, selection probabilities, or sample paths of the original stochastic process.

## Proof

**Step 1: inverse and order.** For any \(u\in[0,1]\), \(f_\gamma\) is strictly increasing with image \([0,1]\), and straightforward substitution gives
\[
 g(u):=f_{1/\gamma}(u)=\frac{u}{\gamma-(\gamma-1)u},\qquad
 f_\gamma(g(u))=u.
 \tag{7}
\]

**Step 2: no other equilibria on any connected bipartite graph.** Let \(x\) be a fixed point of (1) and let \(p=\max_{i\in A}x_i\). Every vertex \(j\in B\) obeys
\[
 x_j=g\left(\sum_{i\in A}W_{ji}x_i\right)\le g(p).
\]
Choose a vertex \(i_0\in A\) where \(x_{i_0}=p\). Its equilibrium equation and (7) imply \(\sum_{j\in B}W_{i_0j}x_j=g(p)\). Every summand is at most \(g(p)\), with all present weights strictly positive, so **every neighbor** of \(i_0\) must have state exactly \(g(p)\). At any such neighbor \(j\), injectivity of \(g\) implies \(\sum_i W_{ji}x_i=p\); all its neighbors in \(A\) must therefore have state \(p\). Connectivity lets this implication propagate alternately through all edges. Hence \(x_i=p\) throughout \(A\) and \(x_j=g(p)\) throughout \(B\). Conversely, substituting these values into (1) proves that each \(p\in[0,1]\) is a fixed point. This proves (a), including \(p=0,1\).

**Step 3: complete-bipartite reduction.** On \(K_{m,n}\), every member of a given part has the same neighbor mean, equal to the ordinary mean of the other part. Writing \(a(t)\) and \(b(t)\) for the two part means gives the autonomous planar equations
\[
 a'=\frac{\gamma b}{1+kb}-a,\qquad
 b'=\frac{a}{\gamma-ka}-b.
 \tag{8}
\]
Within each part, subtracting the mean equation from any individual equation proves
\[
 (x_i-a)'=-(x_i-a),\quad (x_j-b)'=-(x_j-b).
 \tag{9}
\]

**Step 4: conserved ratio and exact integration.** Write \(L=\gamma-ka\) and \(H=1+kb\). Multiplying the first and second equations in (8) by \(H\) and \(L\) respectively gives
\[
 H a'+L b'=\gamma b-aH+a-bL=0.
 \tag{10}
\]
Consequently \((H/L)'=0\), so \(H=RL\) with \(R\) as in (3). Substitution into (8) yields
\[
 a'=\frac1k\left(L-\frac{\gamma}{RL}\right),\quad
 L'=-ka'=\frac\gamma{RL}-L,
 \quad (L^2)'=2\left(\frac\gamma R-L^2\right).
 \tag{11}
\]
Integrating the last scalar linear equation and selecting the positive root (because \(1\le L\le\gamma\)) gives (4). Solving the definitions of \(L,H\) gives \(a(t),b(t)\) and (9) gives (5). These formulas have the correct initial values and solve the ODE, so uniqueness finishes the proof of exact integration.

**Step 5: global selection and exceptional initial data.** Since \(R>0\), (4) implies \(L(t)\to\sqrt{\gamma/R}\). Formula (6) follows, and \(q_*=g(p_*)\) by \(R L_\infty^2=\gamma\). From \(R=H_0/L_0\), the lower extremum \(R=1/\gamma\) requires \(a_0=b_0=0\), and the upper extremum \(R=\gamma\) requires \(a_0=b_0=1\). Because all coordinates lie in \([0,1]\), these conditions mean precisely the all-zero or all-one initial states. Every other \(R\) is strictly between the bounds and yields \(0<p_*,q_*<1\). This proves (b).

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 script `verify.py` checks critical scalar conservation and linearization identities using `fractions.Fraction`, compares the closed-form full-dimensional solution to an independent fourth-order Runge–Kutta integrator, and substitutes the equilibrium arc into independently normalized positive rational bipartite weights. Execute with `python verify.py` from the directory containing the file. The recorded run returned:

```text
VERIFY_OK exact_identities=10128 independent_trajectories=250 weighted_graph_equilibria=1260
```

The exact rational checks are finite tests of the equations; the proof of the general statements is the algebra and connectivity argument above, not the finite enumeration. No random seed, numerical integrator, or experiment is needed to establish (2)–(6).

## Relationship to prior work

Tang, Adler, Ajorlou and Jadbabaie define the Möbius-like response, the social-pressure update map and the equilibrium equations. Their analysis assumes a nonbipartite graph in key places and explicitly observes that reciprocal biases on a loopless bipartite path make both boundary linearizations marginal; their two-community worked example uses self-loop parameters and does not treat the simultaneous zero-self-loop degeneration with an exact flow or a continuum of all equilibria. The present theorem classifies precisely this reciprocal-bias bipartite regime and integrates the associated continuous-time flow on every \(K_{m,n}\). Their general Lyapunov theorem does not by itself give the first integral, rate decomposition, or initial-condition-to-equilibrium selection law.

Tang et al.'s later estimation article and Jadbabaie et al.'s originating article study stochastic inference and consensus. Chen and Lucas study a **different** graph-reinforced urn with one ball allocated per graph edge and a simplex-valued state, although their regular-bipartite equilibrium interval supplies a genuine structural analogy rather than a logical implication. Chen et al.'s nonlinear biased-assimilation system uses a different update rule. The comparisons do not imply uniqueness in the whole literature.

## Limitations

The exact trajectory solution requires a loopless *unweighted complete bipartite* interaction graph; on an arbitrary weighted bipartite graph the theorem gives the entire equilibrium set but **not** a global trajectory formula or convergence rate. Biases must be partwise constant and exactly reciprocal. The theorem is about the continuous-time ODE, not the decreasing-gain expected recursion and not the underlying interacting urn's random equilibrium selection. The publicly accessible literature compared here does not rule out unpublished, obscure, or differently formulated equivalent results; independent verification has not been carried out.
