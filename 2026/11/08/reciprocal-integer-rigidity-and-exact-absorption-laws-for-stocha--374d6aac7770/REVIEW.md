# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The stated theorem uses exact independent nearest-integer stochastic rounding on nonnegative integers. The induction verifies support closure under every reciprocal-integer factor and identifies the output law from its preserved conditional mean; the converse constructs three strictly positive masses at time two for every other contraction. Absorption follows from the same exact distribution and the tail-sum identity, including all integer boundaries; elementary differentiation proves the sharp phase extrema. The standalone rational checker ran 13,234 assertions with 144 constructive nonreciprocal witnesses. The finite checks do not establish infinite quantifiers; the written proof does.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Primary sources examined include the full survey of Croci, Fasi, Higham, Mary and Mikaitis (2021 public deposit; revised full text) and the full papers *The Positive Effects of Stochastic Rounding in Numerical Algorithms* (2022) and *Stochastic Rounding Variance and Probabilistic Bounds: A New Approach* (2022). The texts define stochastic rounding and analyze bias, error and variance, but the inspected discussions do not furnish the characterization of distributional composition under reciprocal-integer contraction, its sharp two-step converse or the absorption-time lattice-phase formula. Semigroup and double-rounding searches were also compared against published mathematical records. Unlike the elementary equality of expected values, the claim has distributional content: a nonreciprocal factor can produce three possible second-step values where one rounding gives at most two. A priori, an equivalent result could appear under linear-interpolation-kernel terminology; this remains the main specialist-precedence risk. Search silence alone is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS

The complete reciprocal-integer versus all-other-factor classification answers when repeated unbiased numerical quantization has exactly the same law as a single quantization. It shows there need be no accumulated output variance, a mathematically structural effect that differs from standard unbiasedness, and gives an exact finite-time absorption distribution and genuinely oscillating expected iteration overhead relevant to choosing integer-grid decay budgets. The sharp counterexample demonstrates why no universal composition guarantee extends beyond the reciprocal-integer case. The scope is a deliberately clean scalar grid-decay model, not general machine floating-point arithmetic.
