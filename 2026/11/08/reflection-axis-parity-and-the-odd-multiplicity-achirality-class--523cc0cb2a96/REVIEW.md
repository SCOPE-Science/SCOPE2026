# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof fixes an arbitrary reflection and derives the exact window-start involution. For each palindromic length-\(k\) factor, perfection supplies a unique occurrence in each residue modulo \(n\), forcing a fixed residue to lift to a fixed start modulo the whole cycle. The inequality \(q^{\lceil k/2\rceil}f_n(C)\le f_L(C)\le2\) settles both parity cases. The two exceptional odd-multiplicity families are verified symbolically using coprime step sizes, and the bracelet assertion follows from a free involution. The finite checker independently confirms 14 parameter configurations and does not serve as the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The foundational 2016 paper's Proposition 7 establishes preservation under reversal but not a classification of fixed necklaces; its Theorem 20 enumerates rotation classes rather than dihedral orbits. The classical no-pure-reversal result for binary de Bruijn sequences covers only \(n=1\) in the present notation. The 2024 Lyndon-pair construction selects lexicographically greatest representatives, and the 2024 reverse-complement analysis fixes a different involution. The generic 2013 symmetric-bracelet enumeration has no perfect-necklace residue constraints. Comparisons with relevant published results found no equivalent or stronger result for all odd multiplicities. Residual risk: unindexed perfect-necklace or universal-cycle symmetry literature, and a potential unrecognized consequence of existing enumeration machinery.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete existence/nonexistence classification for achiral perfect necklaces for every odd multiplicity and alphabet size, including explicit witnesses for all exceptions; it converts a known rotation-class enumeration to a dihedral bracelet enumeration without a symmetry correction in the nonexceptional regime. The independent even-multiplicity axis condition is structurally sharp in the sense that a concrete even-multiplicity example has the allowed reflection parity. The question is motivated by passing from universal-cycle/necklace counts to unoriented cycles; the result is not claimed to classify all even cases.
