The closest motivating source [1] proves ordinary tree vertex decomposability but not fixed-point types. Ordinary path formulas in [2] concern the ambient complex and do not determine geometric reflection fixed points. Importantly, Ehrenborg–Hetyei's Theorem 4.4 [3] **already proves** the qualitative assertion that every interval-hypergraph independence complex is contractible or sphere-like, and therefore qualitatively covers these models. This contribution does not reassert that part as new. Rather, it adds exact dimensions, both parity-dependent residue criteria, and an explicit equivariant-to-orbit reduction for all \(n,r\). These precise formulas are not statements of [1], [2] or [3], and available mathematical-record comparisons identified no stronger claim implying them. A special case may remain in unindexed literature, particularly a later application of [3].

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural reflection involution is a canonical symmetry of every path. Existing nonequivariant path homotopy types and the qualitative interval-family theorem do not determine where reflection-fixed spheres occur or their dimensions. The complete periodic classification gives those values simultaneously for infinitely many path sizes and all higher independence thresholds, including parity, disconnected \(S^0\), and empty behavior. It is an exact two-parameter invariant with a uniform structural proof rather than an arbitrary finite instance or a renamed pre-existing sphere-or-point theorem.
