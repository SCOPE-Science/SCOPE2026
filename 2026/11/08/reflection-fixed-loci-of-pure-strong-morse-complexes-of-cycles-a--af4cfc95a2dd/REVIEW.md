# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The starting pure-cycle facet list consists of two consecutive absent cycle edges, one contiguous positive block and one contiguous negative block, as published for this family. Reflection exchanges incidence signs. The proof first characterizes the admissible two-point incidence orbits by a necessary and sufficient two-edge forward-gap criterion, then lists both exact interval families and proves both completeness and mutual incompatibility, giving the promised PL model without limiting the parameter. The complete orbit-facet checker independently confirms the formulas in 68 axis cases up to \(n=50\); a separate complete original-simplex enumeration tests nine axis cases. The finite tests are corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS under available evidence. Exact and semantic searches for geometric reflection-fixed loci, pure strong matching complexes, dihedral characters and the Knudson--Owens--White cycle construction identified the October 2026 pure-cycle **rotation** theorem, not fixed-point homotopy under reflections. A full public text comparison with the separate full-strong-cycle dihedral virtual-character theorem shows that its objects are different. The primary preprint's abstract is available, but its PDF/full-text could not be accessed in this round, so incidental prior coverage remains a concrete risk, not a claimed proof of novelty. Additional published claims were also compared.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Exact geometric fixed-point spaces encode genuinely equivariant topology not recoverable from ambient ordinary homotopy type, rational rotation characters, or even an alternating reflection trace. The two-simplex PL model covers every reflection in an infinite natural family, and distinguishes the two axis geometries by simplex dimensions when \(n\) is even. This fills a natural symmetry gap left by the existing rotation-only calculation, with an explicit reusable orbit-face method.

## Residual limitations
The result does not describe dihedral orbit spaces or fixed loci of more general subgroups. Source access and unindexed literature remain limitations; no independent mathematical validation has been carried out.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
