# Review of the eleven-cycle Fourier sampling classification

## Correctness

**PASS.** The Gram moment reduction is exact. The six-frequency obstruction uses a self-contained generalized Vandermonde/e_3 identity and the fact that an integer combination of eleven prime roots of unity with 20 nonnegative unit terms cannot vanish. The seven-node system has unique real solutions, and a standalone checker certifies all 20 dihedral-orbit Cramer signs using rational analytic enclosures and integer determinants. This is an exhaustive finite proof, not interpolation from partial tests. The calculation is limited to the specified eleven-point group and four coordinates.

## Originality

**PASS with residual historical risk.** The closest general sources are finite Gabor block analysis (Asipchuk–De Carli–Rodriguez, arXiv:2605.16139), unweighted spectral pairs (Fu–Song, arXiv:2607.19858), and positive trigonometric quadrature theory (Peherstorfer, arXiv:1001.2451; Luo–Yu–Meng, DOI 10.13447/j.1674-5647.2014.04.07). The exact 33-subset/three-dihedral-orbit statement and unique-weight classification are not present in inspected accessible material. An existing three-consecutive-site theorem covers a smaller aperture and does not imply the four-site exact support census. Full specialist positive-quadrature texts were not accessible; earlier equivalent enumeration remains possible.

## Value

**PASS.** The result distinguishes precisely which optimally sparse positive harmonic sampling designs are feasible on a natural finite Fourier grid. Only 33 of 330 combinatorially possible optimal support patterns work, and they exhibit a surprisingly rigid reflection geometry. The explicit support criterion, uniqueness, and reproducible exact certificate make the invariant useful for testing general frame-design and quadrature methods. No stability or higher-modulus claim is made.
