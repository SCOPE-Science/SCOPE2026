# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The complete conjugacy orbit of the regular nilpotent block has size \(L_{n,q}\). For a target of rank \(n-1\), the factor rank has exactly two cases: invertible with one right factor, or rank \(n-1\) with prescribed hyperplane image and \(q^n\) right factors. Counting each and multiplying by the orbit gives the exact integer formula. Division by the established nilpotent-product probability yields the stated conditional distribution. The limits use an absolutely summable tail \(\sum_{j\ge2}q^{-j}\). Exact independent finite enumeration of full-matrix pairs and fixed-target fibers corroborates the proof, and the symbolic formula tests cover \(203\) field/dimension pairs. The mathematical proof, not finite extrapolation, establishes arbitrary dimension.

## Originality: PASS, with explicit historical risk
The 2026 motivating work and Kovács's 1987 abstract state an **unstratified** nilpotence probability. They do not, from the material actually inspected, present the all-dimensional regular-nilpotent product count or its fixed-field positive limit. Exact regular nilpotent counts for a **single** uniform matrix do not determine factorization-weighted counts, and the prior upper-triangular product model is genuinely different. Targeted searches of claim aliases, Jordan-type descriptions, published mathematical research records, and recoverable earlier claims found no identical or stronger assertion. Full contents of Kovács's 1987 and 1988 articles remain unavailable to compare. This warrants historical priority caution rather than a claim of universal novelty certification.

## Value: PASS
The maximal nilpotency index is the natural endpoint of the nilpotence-index filtration, not an arbitrary parameter selection. The theorem evaluates a complete natural probability for all finite fields and all matrix sizes, adds an exact positive large-rank limiting density, and separates full-matrix sampling from the previously studied triangular sampling where the top index can be asymptotically rare. This refinement is useful when an aggregate nilpotence probability conceals the internal Jordan structure. No broader Jordan-partition distribution is claimed.

## Limitations and closest literature
The exact formula covers only two independent unrestricted factors; it is not a Jordan-partition census. Closest prior work: Sarma–Subedi (2026), Kovács (1987, 1988), classical Jordan centralizer enumeration, and results on upper-triangular two-factor products. Full primary text for the older Kovács papers was not accessible, so implicit coverage remains the strongest priority risk. Finite checks cannot certify the infinite theorem or publication novelty.
