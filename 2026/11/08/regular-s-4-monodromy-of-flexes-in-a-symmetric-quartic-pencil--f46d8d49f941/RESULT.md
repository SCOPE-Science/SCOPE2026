# Regular \(S_4\) monodromy of flexes in a symmetric quartic pencil

## Finding
For the smooth complex genus-three plane quartic pencil
\[
C_a:\quad X^4+Y^4+Z^4+a(X^2Y^2+Y^2Z^2+Z^2X^2)=0,
\qquad a\in B:=\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb C}\setminus\{-2,-1,2\},
\]
let \(\mathcal F\to B\) be the relative flex-point scheme, namely the intersection of each curve with its cubic-coordinate Hessian determinant. The cover has \(24\) points in a general geometric fiber. On \(B^\circ=B\setminus\{0,3\}\), the flex-point cover is connected and is a **regular Galois cover of degree \(24\) with deck and monodromy group \(S_4\)**. Within the smooth parameter space, its only branch parameters are \(0\) and \(3\), each with twelve simple ramification points. Local inertia at \(0\) is a double transposition in \(S_4\); local inertia at \(3\) is a transposition. These two inertia classes are not conjugate.

This statement concerns the variation of all flex points with the pencil parameter, not merely the familiar twelve hyperflexes of the Fermat and Komiya–Kuribayashi special members.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\). All statements about ramification concern the indicated open parameter line \(B\), not the excluded singular fibers or the fiber at parameter infinity. The group \(G\) is generated by permutations of \(X,Y,Z\) and independent coordinate sign changes modulo the global sign; thus \(G=(\mathbb Z/2)^2\rtimes S_3\cong S_4\), of order \(24\).

For a smooth complex plane quartic, the scheme cut out by the curve and its Hessian records the degree-\(24\) canonical inflection divisor, counting a hyperflex twice. We use this standard Hessian characterization. The smoothness restriction \(a\notin\{-2,-1,2\}\) is established in the cited literature and can also be checked from the three partial derivatives.

## Proof
**1. Symmetric reduction.** Put \(U=X^2\), \(V=Y^2\), \(W=Z^2\), and \(e_1=U+V+W\), \(e_2=UV+UW+VW\), \(e_3=UVW\). Write \(F=e_1^2+(a-2)e_2\), and let \(H\) be the determinant of its \(3\times3\) matrix of second partial derivatives in \(X,Y,Z\). Direct differentiation yields the exact polynomial identity
\[
H+24a(a+6)e_1F
=72(a+2)\bigl(ae_1^3+3(a-2)^2e_3\bigr).
\]
For a flex of a smooth member, \(F=H=0\), so \(e_2=-e_1^2/(a-2)\) and \(e_3=-ae_1^3/[3(a-2)^2]\). Necessarily \(e_1\ne0\): otherwise all elementary symmetric functions of \(U,V,W\) vanish, forcing \(U=V=W=0\) in characteristic zero. Projective rescaling gives the unique normalization \(e_1=1\). Hence the squared coordinates \(U,V,W\) are the three roots (in some order) of
\[
P_a(T)=T^3-T^2-\frac{T}{a-2}+\frac{a}{3(a-2)^2}.
\]
Its discriminant is
\[
\operatorname{disc}_T(P_a)=\frac{4(a-3)(a+1)^2}{3(a-2)^4}.
\]
For \(a\in B^\circ\), the three roots are distinct and nonzero; their six orderings and four projective square-root sign choices give exactly \(24\) distinct flexes. The projective signed-permutation group \(G\) acts simply transitively on them.

**2. The cubic root extension has group \(S_3\).** Clear the denominators in \(P_a(T)\) to obtain
\[
N(a,T)=3(T^3-T^2)(a-2)^2-3T(a-2)+a.
\]
Viewed as a primitive quadratic in \(a\) over \(\mathbb C(T)\), its discriminant is
\[
\operatorname{disc}_a N=-24T^3+33T^2-6T+1.
\]
The latter has odd degree and is not a square in \(\mathbb C(T)\); therefore \(N\) is irreducible in \(\mathbb C(T)[a]\), hence also in \(\mathbb C(a)[T]\) by Gauss's lemma. The nonsquare cubic discriminant above proves that the splitting field \(L\) of \(P_a\) over \(\mathbb C(a)\) has group \(S_3\).

**3. The projective sign extension has degree four.** Label the three roots \(u_1,u_2,u_3\) in \(L\). The function field of the cover obtained by marking one flex is
\[
K=L\left(\sqrt{u_1/u_3},\sqrt{u_2/u_3}\right).
\]
The cubic is separable at \(a=0\) and specializes to \(P_0(T)=T(2T^2-2T+1)/2\). Thus exactly one root vanishes simply at each prime of \(L\) over \(a=0\), and the other two roots are units. The three Galois conjugate primes correspond to each of \(u_1,u_2,u_3\) being the vanishing root. At a prime where \(u_1\) vanishes, the valuation-parity vector of \((u_1/u_3,u_2/u_3)\) is \((1,0)\); where \(u_2\) vanishes it is \((0,1)\). Hence the two square classes are independent in \(L^*/(L^*)^2\). Consequently \([K:L]=4\), \([K:\mathbb C(a)]=24\). The field \(K\) is stable under permuting the roots and independent square-root sign changes, so it is already a Galois extension with group \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\rtimes S_3\cong S_4\). Its degree agrees with the number of geometric flexes; this proves irreducibility and regular monodromy of the relative flex cover.

**4. Branch points and conjugacy classes.** Above a smooth parameter other than \(a=0\) and \(a=3\), the cubic is separable and its roots are all units. Local analytic choices of the roots and their square roots therefore trivialize the flex cover; it is unramified. At \(a=0\) a single root crosses zero linearly. A local continuation around that parameter reverses the sign of exactly the corresponding coordinate, acting on the four projective sign choices as a nontrivial element of the normal Klein four subgroup of \(S_4\). In \(S_4\) this is a double transposition. At \(a=3\), the cubic has factorization \(P_3(T)=(T-1)^2(T+1)\), and its discriminant has a simple zero. The two roots near \(1\) exchange under a small loop, while the nonzero square roots can be chosen analytically: inertia is the coordinate transposition, belonging to the transposition class of \(S_4\). Each nonidentity order-two element acts without fixed points on the regular \(24\)-element fiber. Therefore both local branch fibers have twelve simple ramification points. In either special fiber the twelve limiting flex points each have Hessian-intersection weight two, hence are hyperflexes; their existence is classical and not claimed new.

## Relationship to prior work
Fuertes and Streit (2006, Section 3, Theorem 10) already identify the same symmetric quartic family, its automorphism group \(S_4\), and a closed Wronskian formula for its Weierstrass points. Kuribayashi and Komiya (1977) and subsequent work classify exceptional Weierstrass-point behavior. Pacini and Testa (2013) explicitly study the Fermat and Komiya–Kuribayashi twelve-hyperflex fibers; Badr and Saleem (2013) classify point orbits in a larger three-parameter family. These results cover the equation, fiberwise symmetry, Wronskian and isolated exceptional fiber counts, and none of those facts is presented as new. The claim here is the **degree-\(24\) function-field extension, proof of connected regular \(S_4\) monodromy across the pencil, and the two nonconjugate local inertia classes at the smooth special parameters**. Kulikov's subsequent result on generic degree-four plane curves concerns the full parameter space, not this restricted symmetric pencil.

## Limitations
This is a theorem over \(\mathbb C\), not over positive characteristic. No statement is made about ramification over singular members \(a=-2,-1,2\), or at parameter infinity, or about an arithmetic Galois group over \(\mathbb Q(a)\). Fiberwise Weierstrass-point classifications and the Wronskian were already known. Historical priority of the specialized monodromy statement remains subject to further literature review, particularly older sources on Kuribayashi curves; a full copy of the 1977 source was not inspected. The review is not independent validation.
