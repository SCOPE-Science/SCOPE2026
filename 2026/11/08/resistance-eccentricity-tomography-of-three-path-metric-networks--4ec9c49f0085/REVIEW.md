# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. Series–parallel reduction yields an exact pointwise quadratic resistance law; its maximum localizes on longest arcs because \(m\ge k\rho>2\rho\). The larger root of the resulting quadratic recovers the longest length; remaining two arc lengths come from their sum and reciprocal sum. The four-arm multiset counterexample preserves both symmetric data and the maximum. A majorization comparison and an exact factorization prove sharp two-sided bounds, including all equality cases. The independent nodal solver uses rational arithmetic rather than the closed formula and agrees on every case tested. The full claim has an analytic proof; finite checks are corroboration only.

## Originality

PASS, with historical-priority risk. Baker–Faber Theorem 10 already proves the *general edge-resistance quadratic* and is fully credited; no novelty is claimed for it or for the series–parallel calculation alone. The proposed new claim is the minimal three-invariant inverse characterization for all three-path networks, sharp nonidentifiability on four or more paths, and exact eccentricity extremum. Comparison to the inspected full Baker–Faber text and related published mathematical summaries identifies no stated reconstruction, same-observable table, or global extremum covering those claims. Their general formula underpins the proof, but does not itself assert the inverse information threshold. Wider priority may remain unlocated.

## Value

PASS. This is a natural complete inverse question in metric graph resistance geometry: do three global electrical observations recover the geometric path-length multiset? It has an explicit affirmative reconstruction precisely at three paths and constructive failure in higher parallel networks. The sharp fixed-total-length inequality identifies which shapes minimize or asymptotically maximize resistance eccentricity, with exact geometric equality. These features are more than a pointwise resistor calculation, though the special network family is limited.
