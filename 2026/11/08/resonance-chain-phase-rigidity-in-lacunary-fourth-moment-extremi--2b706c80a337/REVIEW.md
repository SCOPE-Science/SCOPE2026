# Review of resonance-chain phase rigidity

## Correctness — PASS

The exact pair-sum convolution identity yields the complete fourth moment in (1). Binary representation gives precisely the adjacent dyadic doubling collisions, and no other collisions; higher integer bases have none. Compression of active powers preserves all existing nonnegative resonance terms, while the appended-frequency perturbation has a positive *first-order* numerator term and only a second-order normalization change. This proves strict growth and excludes vanishing coefficients by induction, including the base transition. The exact maximization \(1+4s-(7/2)s^2\) gives \(15/7\) at \(s=4/7\). Finally, saturation of every positive collision term forces a linear recurrence for all coefficient phases. The standalone script checks pair sums and fourth moments by direct convolution using integer complex data. Finite experiments are checks, not a proof of the all-length result.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

Ciccone–Gonçalves arXiv:2208.09441 concerns a different geometric-circle \(L^6(\mathbb R^2)\) extension problem with arithmetic conditions; its explicit discussion of ratio-two gaps motivates rather than implies the present coefficient \(L^4(\mathbb T)\) theorem. Dyakonov arXiv:2005.08885 concerns geometric extreme/exposed points for \(L^1\) lacunary spaces, not \(L^4\) maximizers. The closest full-text published record treats only the four-character truncation \(\{0,1,2,4\}\) and already establishes rigidity in that isolated case. This earlier four-character conclusion is excluded from novelty attribution. The all-length strict-support and phase conclusion for every longer dyadic truncation, along with the base-three phase transition, was not found in the checked sources. Residual risk: a stronger, perhaps generically formulated theorem on positive additive-resonance hypergraphs could imply this result without mentioning dyadic frequencies. Priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem is a complete structural classification of *which coefficient coordinates and phases* can occur at maximizers across an infinite, naturally defined family of lacunary Fourier spaces. Full support is not forced by the existence of an extremizer or by the Fourier moment identity alone: it uses a strict variational perturbation plus a compression comparison for arbitrary missing frequencies. Strict growth rules out stabilization at any finite truncation. Base three is the exact arithmetic boundary between all-phase Sidon extremizers and dyadic character-phase rigidity. These facts reduce the remaining sharp-constant problem to positive real magnitude optimization and supply a nontrivial all-length boundary for lacunary extremal theory. This does not assert an exact constant for every dyadic length, uniqueness of positive magnitude profile, or stability under perturbation.
