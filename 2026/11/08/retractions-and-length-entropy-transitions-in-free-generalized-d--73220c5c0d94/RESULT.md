# Retractions and length-entropy transitions in free generalized dimonoids

## Finding

Let \(G_n\) be the free generalized dimonoid on a labeled alphabet of \(n\geq1\) generators. Every idempotent endomorphism of \(G_n\) is a retraction onto the free sub-g-dimonoid generated by a **nonempty subset of the original generators**; there are no other retraction images.

For an integer \(L\geq1\), let \(E_n(L)\) count idempotent endomorphisms for which each generator image has word length at most \(L\). Define
\[
H_s(L)=\sum_{j=1}^{L}2^{j-1}s^j=\frac{s((2s)^L-1)}{2s-1}.
\]
Then the complete image-rank profile and total count are
\[
E_{n,s}(L)=\binom ns H_s(L)^{n-s}\quad(1\leq s\leq n),
\qquad E_n(L)=\sum_{s=1}^n E_{n,s}(L).
\]
Here image rank means the least number of free generators of the image; \(H_n(L)^0\) is interpreted as \(1\). The total number of endomorphisms under the same bound is \(H_n(L)^n\).

For fixed \(n\geq2\), put
\[
\beta_n=\max_{1\leq s<n}(2s)^{n-s},\quad
M_n=\{s:(2s)^{n-s}=\beta_n\},\quad
C_n=\sum_{s\in M_n}\binom ns\left(\frac{s}{2s-1}\right)^{n-s}.
\]
As \(L\to\infty\),
\[
E_n(L)\sim C_n\beta_n^L,
\qquad
\frac{E_n(L)}{H_n(L)^n}
\sim C_n\left(\frac{2n-1}{n}\right)^n
\left(\frac{\beta_n}{(2n)^n}\right)^L.
\]
In particular, idempotents are exponentially sparse among length-bounded endomorphisms. A uniformly sampled bounded idempotent has image rank in \(M_n\) with probability tending to one; conditional limiting weights are proportional to the summands defining \(C_n\).

Writing \(W\) for the positive Lambert function, every maximizing rank \(s_n\in M_n\) satisfies
\[
s_n=\frac{n}{W(2en)}+O(1)\sim\frac n{\log n}
\qquad(n\to\infty).
\]
At rank three, \(M_3=\{1,2\}\) and \(E_3(L)=5\cdot4^L-6\cdot2^L+2\). Consequently the limiting proportions of idempotents with image ranks one and two are respectively \(3/5\) and \(2/5\), a genuine two-rank coexistence.

## Assumptions and scope

A generalized dimonoid has two associative binary operations \(\dashv\) and \(\vdash\) satisfying \((x\dashv y)\dashv z=x\dashv(y\vdash z)\) and \((x\dashv y)\vdash z=x\vdash(y\vdash z)\); no inside-associativity identity is assumed. We use the standard free model \(G_n\), cited below, in which elements are pairs \((w,u)\) consisting of a nonempty word \(w\) in the \(n\)-letter alphabet and a binary control word \(u\in\{a,b\}^{|w|-1}\). The free generators are \((x,\varepsilon)\), with empty control \(\varepsilon\), and
\[
(w,u)\dashv(w',u')=(ww',ua^{|w'|}),\qquad
(w,u)\vdash(w',u')=(ww',u'b^{|w|}).
\]
Length always refers to \(|w|\), not the control length. Endomorphisms are endomorphisms of both operations and cannot send a generator to an empty word. These statements concern finite labeled generator sets, arbitrary integers \(L\geq1\), and the iterated limits stated, not an enumeration of arbitrary nonfree generalized dimonoids. The case \(n=1\) has exactly one idempotent, the identity, at every length bound.

## Proof

**Step 1: substitution and the fixed-letter lemma.** Both operations concatenate the first-coordinate words, so the projection \(\pi:G_n\to X^+\), \(\pi(w,u)=w\), takes any endomorphism \(f\) to a unique nonerasing free-semigroup endomorphism \(\phi\) satisfying \(\pi f=\phi\pi\). If \(f^2=f\), then \(\phi^2=\phi\). Fix a generator \(x\), and write \(\phi(x)=y_1\cdots y_m\). Since all \(\phi(y_i)\) are nonempty, length comparison in \(\phi^2(x)=\phi(x)\) gives
\[
m=\sum_{i=1}^m |\phi(y_i)|\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\phi(y_i)|=1\quad\text{for all }i.
\]
The equality of the two words now forces \(\phi(y_i)=y_i\) at each position. Thus every letter occurring anywhere in an image is individually fixed. Let \(S\) be the nonempty set of such letters. For \(y\in S\), the image \(f(y)\) has first-coordinate word \(y\) and hence must be exactly \((y,\varepsilon)\). For each \(x\notin S\), the first-coordinate word of \(f(x)\) uses only letters in \(S\). The same is therefore true for the entire image \(f(G_n)\).

**Step 2: converse and exact image.** Conversely choose any nonempty \(S\subseteq X\), set \(f(y)=(y,\varepsilon)\) for \(y\in S\), and choose \(f(x)\) arbitrarily from the free sub-g-dimonoid \(G(S)\) for each \(x\notin S\). The free universal property extends these choices to an endomorphism. It restricts to the identity on \(G(S)\), and its image equals \(G(S)\); hence \(f^2=f\). No choice with \(x\notin S\) can accidentally fix \(x\), because its word uses only \(S\). Therefore \(S\) is intrinsic and the classification is disjoint.

**Step 3: exact enumeration.** A length-\(j\) element over an \(s\)-letter subalphabet admits \(s^j\) first-coordinate words and independently \(2^{j-1}\) control words. There are \(H_s(L)\) possible images of each of the \(n-s\) nonfixed generators. Choosing \(S\) gives \(\binom ns H_s(L)^{n-s}\) maps, proving the entire profile. Unrestricted bounded substitutions give \(H_n(L)^n\) endomorphisms.

**Step 4: fixed-rank asymptotics.** For each fixed \(s\geq1\),
\[
H_s(L)=\frac{s}{2s-1}(2s)^L(1-(2s)^{-L}).
\]
For fixed \(n\), take the largest exponential base among the finitely many terms. This proves \(E_n(L)\sim C_n\beta_n^L\) and the limiting weights. Since \(\beta_n<(2n)^n\) for \(n\geq2\), division by the total bounded-substitution count proves exponential sparsity. For \(n=3\), the two maximal bases are \(2^2=4\) and \(4^1=4\), with respective coefficients \(3\) and \(2\). Direct substitution gives the claimed exact expression and limit weights.

**Step 5: maximizing support size.** Maximize the real-variable function \(h_n(t)=(n-t)\log(2t)\) on \(1\leq t\leq n-1\). Its second derivative is \(-n/t^2-1/t<0\), and its unique unconstrained critical point solves \(n/t=1+\log(2t)\), namely \(t=n/W(2en)\). For \(n\geq3\), this point lies in the admissible interval; a discrete maximizer must be one of its two neighboring integers. For \(n=2\), the only admissible rank is one. Therefore \(s_n=n/W(2en)+O(1)\), and the standard positive-branch asymptotic \(W(z)\sim\log z\) proves the final relation.

## Verification

The standalone file `CHECKER.py` enumerates every bounded substitution of generator images in the indicated cases and tests \(f(f(x))=f(x)\) **using the actual two binary operations** on control-word pairs. Its substitution evaluator recursively reconstructs each pair from a binary term; it also verifies that identity substitution returns every enumerated pair unchanged. The program separately computes the predicted support-wise counts from the closed formula and asserts equality. Run `python3 CHECKER.py` with Python 3, standard library only. Recorded exact checks include \((n,L)=(2,4)\), with \(31\) idempotents among \(28{,}900\) maps; \((3,2)\), with rank-profile \((27,30,1)\) among \(9{,}261\) maps; and \((4,1)\), with \(41\) idempotents. The output ends in `VERIFY_OK`. Finite enumeration does not prove the infinite-rank or large-length statements; the preceding proof does.

## Relationship to prior work

Zhuchok and Zhuchok (2026) establish determinability of free g-dimonoids from endomorphism semigroups, describe the free pair model and their automorphism groups, and classify **constant idempotent endomorphisms** in their proof. The present classification concerns **all** idempotent endomorphisms and gives exact bounded-length profiles, asymptotic growth constants, and the two-rank coexistence phenomenon. The result relies on their free-model construction; it does not claim their determinability theorem or constant-idempotent subcase as new.

Earlier work on semiretractions of free monoids, of dimonoids, and on free idempotent-generated semigroups is related but has different objects or operations. The fixed-letter argument is familiar in ordinary nonerasing word substitutions; the full two-operation normal form and its binary-control entropy determine the quantitative statements here. In particular, the exponential base \((2s)^{n-s}\) is distinct from the ordinary free-semigroup base \(s^{n-s}\).

## Limitations

The counts depend on the standard length grading and labeled generators. The asymptotic support statement first sends \(L\to\infty\) for each fixed rank and only then studies the maximizing rank as \(n\to\infty\); no joint-limit concentration estimate is asserted. The result does not classify all sub-g-dimonoids, arbitrary congruences, or idempotents in unrelated finitely generated g-dimonoids. Earlier inaccessible literature on semiretractions may merit further historical checking; no independent novelty validation is claimed.
