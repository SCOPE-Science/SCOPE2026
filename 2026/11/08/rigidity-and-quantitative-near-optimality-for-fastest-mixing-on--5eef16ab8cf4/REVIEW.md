# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every integer \(n\ge3\), the argument gives an explicit complete parametrization of feasible symmetric stochastic kernels on the graph with its missing perfect matching. The part-sign Rayleigh bound and opposite-sign mean-zero trace bound prove the value and force equality. Positive-semidefinite zero trace implies eigenvector rigidity; the row/column incidence connectivity then forces all allowed weights identical. The new stability bound follows from the spectral positive-semidefinite estimate, the connected three-step incidence graph, and explicit control of the mean edge weight. Replayed rational formulas and direct full-matrix tests support the derivations; the infinite proof does not depend on them.

## Originality — PASS, conditional on indexed and inspected literature

Boyd–Diaconis–Parrilo–Xiao's edge-transitive theorem already determines the optimal **invariant** edge probability and SLEM on the crown graph; these values are credited as known. Its existence-by-symmetrization argument does not classify all non-invariant optimal kernels or establish the stated explicit entrywise stability bound. Boyd–Diaconis–Xiao's earlier SDP formulation notes possible nonuniqueness in general. The related spectral-width work of Göring–Helmberg–Reiss optimizes a different edge-budget objective. Indexed semantic comparisons and accessible historical claims did not contain a result implying the stated rigidity/stability theorem. Risk: equivalent unindexed prior literature or a previously published unstated consequence of general theory may reduce priority; no such derivation was found in the inspected material.

## Value — PASS

An invariant optimizer need not exhaust the solution set of a convex spectral design problem. Here a natural edge-transitive network has a uniquely forced globally optimal full transition matrix, and near-optimal values quantitatively constrain **all** allowable transition entries without assuming symmetric edge allocations. This is useful for identifiability of optimized mixing kernels and for interpreting approximate semidefinite solutions; the result is structural rather than an unmotivated small-graph computation. The displayed stability constant is not asserted sharp.
