# Rigidity of equality in the sharp three-generator Frobenius bound

## Finding
For each triple of integers \(1\le a<b<c\) with \(\gcd(a,b,c)=1\), let \(F(a,b,c)\) be the greatest integer outside the additive semigroup generated by \(a,b,c\), using the convention \(F=-1\) if the semigroup contains every nonnegative integer. Set \(k=\lceil c/2\rceil\). The sharp bound of Chen, Hu, Mao and Zhang is
\[
F(a,b,c)\le b\left\lceil\frac c2\right\rceil-c-1.
\]
**Equality classification:** equality holds precisely for
\[
(c,a,b)=(2k,1,2)\quad\text{or}\quad(c,a,b)=(2k,k,k+1),\qquad k\ge2.
\]
There are consequently no equality triples with odd largest generator, and exactly two with any fixed even largest generator at least four.

## Assumptions and scope
All generators are positive, pairwise distinct, strictly increasing, and have joint gcd one. They need not be pairwise coprime. The Frobenius number is defined relative to *nonnegative* integer coefficients. The result classifies equality in the particular bound of arXiv:2610.32219v1 for three generators, **not** equality in the older bound maximizing over all triples with a fixed largest generator.

## Proof
Write \(k=\lceil c/2\rceil\), let \(G=\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z\), and let \(A=\{0,a,b\}\subseteq G\). We first show \(kA=G\). Suppose otherwise, and let \(H\) be the stabilizer of \(kA\). If \(H\) is trivial, the iterated form of Kneser's theorem gives
\[
|kA|\ge k(|A|-1)+1=2k+1>c,
\]
a contradiction. If \(H\) is nontrivial, the quotient \(G/H\) has order \(d=c/|H|\le c/2\le k\). The image \(\overline A\) generates that cyclic quotient because \(\gcd(a,b,c)=1\). Every vertex of the directed Cayley graph of the quotient has a simple shortest walk from zero using the nonzero elements of \(\overline A\), and therefore a walk of at most \(d-1\) edges. Thus \(k\overline A=G/H\). Since \(kA\) is \(H\)-periodic, it must then equal \(G\), another contradiction. Hence \(kA=G\).

For each residue \(\rho\in G\), let \(R(\rho)\) be the least nonnegative value of a combination of \(a,b\) in that class. Since \(kA=G\), we have \(R(\rho)\le kb\). The Apéry-residue identity gives \(F(a,b,c)=\max_{\rho}R(\rho)-c\). The previously proved sharp upper bound guarantees \(R(\rho)\le kb-1\) for every residue. If equality in that bound holds, one class has \(R(\rho)=kb-1\). Covering this class by \(kA\), choose a representative \(u=xa+yb\), \(x,y\ge0\), \(x+y\le k\), with \(u\equiv kb-1\pmod c\) and \(u\le kb\). As \(c\ge3\), \(u\ne kb\), and minimality forces \(u=kb-1\). The identity
\[
kb-u=(k-x-y)b+x(b-a)=1
\]
forces \(x=1\), \(x+y=k\), and \(b-a=1\). Therefore **every equality triple has consecutive smaller generators** \(b=a+1\).

It remains to decide when \(B=k(a+1)-c-1\) is a gap. For the two-coin semigroup \(\langle a,a+1\rangle\), the interval of values representable using exactly \(j\) coins is \([ja,j(a+1)]\), because changing a coin from \(a\) to \(a+1\) increases the sum by one. Equivalently, a nonnegative integer \(N\), written \(N=qa+r\) with \(0\le r<a\), is representable precisely when \(q\ge r\).

If \(c=2k-1\) is odd, then \(B=k(a-1)\) and \(a\le2k-3\). For \(a=1\), \(B=0\) is representable. For \(a\ge k\), write
\[
B=(k-1)a+(a-k),\qquad0\le a-k\le k-3,
\]
so \(B\) is representable. If \(2\le a<k\), write \(k=ha+t\) with \(h\ge1\), \(0\le t<a\). When \(t=0\), \(B=(k-h)a\). When \(t>0\), write \(B=(k-h-1)a+(a-t)\). The latter is in the exactly-\(k-h-1\)-coin interval because
\[
(k-h-1)-(a-t)=(h-1)a+2t-h-1\ge0.
\]
(The inequality follows directly for \(h=1\) from \(t\ge1\), and for \(h\ge2\) from \(a\ge2\).) Thus every odd-\(c\) candidate has \(B\) representable and equality is impossible.

If \(c=2k\) is even, then \(B=k(a-1)-1\), with \(a\le2k-2\). For \(a\ge k+1\),
\[
B=(k-1)a+(a-k-1),\qquad0\le a-k-1\le k-3,
\]
and \(B\) is representable. If \(2\le a<k\), write \(k=ha+t\), \(h\ge1\), \(0\le t<a\). For \(t=a-1\), we have \(B=(k-h-1)a\). Otherwise \(B=(k-h-1)a+(a-t-1)\); this belongs to the exactly-\(k-h-1\)-coin interval since
\[
(k-h-1)-(a-t-1)=(h-1)a+2t-h\ge0.
\]
Here, when \(h=1\), the assumption \(a<k\) forces \(t\ge1\); when \(h\ge2\), use \(a\ge2\). Hence every even-\(c\) candidate except \(a=1\) or \(a=k\) has \(B\) representable.

For \(a=1\), \(b=2\), \(c=2k\), the semigroup contains one, so \(F=-1=B\). For \(a=k\), \(b=k+1\), \(c=2k\), we have \(B=k^2-k-1\). Modulo \(k\), any representation using \(k,k+1,2k\) would require at least \(k-1\) copies of \(k+1\), whose sum is \(k^2-1>B\). Thus \(B\) is not representable. The sharp upper bound then forces \(F=B\). This proves both necessity and sufficiency.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` provides two complementary exact checks: Dijkstra's shortest-path algorithm for Apéry residues of all joint-coprime triples with \(c\le120\) (not assuming consecutive smaller generators), and direct interval-criterion checks for all \(2\le k\le2000\) with admissible consecutive smaller generators. The finite computations independently stress-test the statement and boundary cases; the unrestricted proof above uses Kneser's theorem and integer inequalities.

Run `python verify.py` in the same directory. No nonstandard Python packages are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Chen, Hu, Mao and Zhang proved the upper bound and supplied infinite equality examples but did **not** classify all equality triples. Their general construction for three generators supplies the \(\{k,k+1,2k\}\) family; the additional family \(\{1,2,2k\}\) follows from the \(F=-1\) convention. The contribution here is the if-and-only-if rigidity statement and the exclusion of all other triples, including every odd largest generator.

Aguiló and Miralles (2005) classified extremizers for the *different* objective obtained by maximizing \(F(a,b,c)\) over all pairs \(a<b<c\) at fixed \(c\), building on results of Dixmier and Hamidoune. Their published extremal objective is \(2(\lfloor c/2\rfloor-1)^2-1\) for even \(c\), generally larger than the equality value in the new two-largest-generator inequality. Their classification therefore does not imply the present theorem. The finite-group ingredient is the classical Kneser sumset inequality.

## Limitations
This classification is restricted to three strictly increasing generators; no uniqueness theorem is claimed for four or more generators. Classical background theorems are used and not claimed as new. Exact historical priority cannot be established solely by indexed searches; an unindexed earlier equality characterization remains possible. Computational checks are finite corroboration and are not an independent mathematical audit.
