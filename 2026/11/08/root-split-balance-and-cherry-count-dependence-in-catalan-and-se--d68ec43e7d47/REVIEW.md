# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The definitions distinguish uniform ordered Catalan trees from permutation-generated binary-search trees. The Catalan proof supplies the bivariate root-split marked generating functions and exact coefficient extraction; the independent BST proof uses the uniform split and exact cherry-count regression. The zero case, strict signs, constants, and nonmonotone conditional expectations follow by exact algebra. The executable script checks the critical coefficients and finite distributions independently, with 1820 successful assertions. Enumeration alone is not presented as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
The inspected primary article by Blum, François and Janson (https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0702415, pp. 1–6 and theorem sections) treats Sackin/Colless statistics, not cherries paired with root-only squared imbalance. The inspected primary Wu–Choi article (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.03139, introduction, Theorems 1–6 and related main sections) treats the cherry/pitchfork joint distribution and marginal cherries, not the root-split mixed covariance. A publicly indexed result on squared BST root imbalance versus *total path length* has different functional, result and scale. General split or generating-function methods could yield these identities upon specialization; the assessment does not claim no earlier mathematical implication is possible.

## Value — PASS
The cherry count and root split are natural shape statistics, and both null models are canonical. This exact model contrast exposes a difference between order-\(n^{3/2}\) dependence and bounded dependence while retaining a negative sign, together with a nonmonotone conditional regression not visible from aggregate balance statistics. It is a natural exact dependency diagnostic, rather than a parameter-sliced or solely computational identity.
