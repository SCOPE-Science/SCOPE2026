# Scale-three Rips spheres of affine nets after deleting one parallel class

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal A\) be an affine plane of finite order \(q\ge2\), and fix one of its \(q+1\) parallel classes of lines. Delete the \(q\) **line vertices** in this class from the point–line incidence graph, retaining every point vertex, all other line vertices, and their incidence edges. Call the resulting connected graph \(G\), with the unit-edge shortest-path metric. At inclusive radius three its Vietoris–Rips complex is *simplicially isomorphic* to a join of \(2q\) discrete spaces of cardinality \(q\):
\[
\operatorname{VR}_{\le3}(G)\cong\underbrace{D_q*\cdots*D_q}_{2q\text{ factors}}
\simeq\bigvee^{(q-1)^{2q}} S^{2q-1},
\]
where \(D_q\) denotes a discrete set of \(q\) points. Its top-dimensional reduced integral homology is free of rank \((q-1)^{2q}\) and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. In the integral Vietoris–Rips persistence module, all of these \((2q-1)\)-dimensional classes are born at radius \(3\) and die at radius \(4\): there are exactly \((q-1)^{2q}\) corresponding \([3,4)\) intervals over every field. No claim is made here about the lower-dimensional persistence bars.

In particular, for the order-three affine plane, the graph is the \(18\)-vertex Pappus configuration graph and the radius-three Rips complex is a wedge of \(64\) five-spheres.

## Assumptions and scope
An affine plane of order \(q\) has \(q^2\) points and \(q(q+1)\) lines, \(q\) points per line, and \(q+1\) lines through each point. Its lines partition into \(q+1\) parallel classes of \(q\) mutually disjoint lines; lines in different classes meet in exactly one point, and any two distinct points lie on exactly one line. These axioms, not a finite-field coordinatization, are the hypotheses. In particular, the statement is valid for non-Desarguesian finite affine planes when they exist; it does not assert existence of a plane of every numerical order.

Delete one specified parallel class of line vertices, not the corresponding point vertices or incidence edges among remaining vertices. **Distances are recomputed intrinsically in this deleted graph**, not inherited from the larger affine-plane incidence graph. The deletion partitions the point set into \(q\) blocks of \(q\) points, namely the point sets of the deleted lines. The remaining line vertices likewise fall into \(q\) parallel classes, each with \(q\) vertices. These are the claimed \(2q\) blocks. For each finite graph \(X\), \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(X)\) is the abstract simplicial complex of vertex subsets whose pairwise unit-edge graph distances are at most \(r\), including equality. The wedge notation specifies ordinary, nonequivariant homotopy type.

## Proof
**1. Intrinsic graph distances.** Write \(P\) for points and \(L\) for the remaining lines. Any two different point vertices from *different* deleted-line blocks are joined by their unique affine line, which has not been deleted; hence their distance is \(2\). Two distinct points from the *same* deleted-line block have no common retained line, so have no length-two path. Choose a point \(x\) outside that block. Their unique retained connecting lines to \(x\) give a four-edge path, so their distance is \(4\). (Bipartite parity rules out odd distances between points.)

Any two retained line vertices from *different* parallel classes meet at a point, giving distance \(2\). Two distinct lines from the *same* class do not meet and therefore have distance at least \(4\). Choose points \(p\) and \(p'\) on the two lines lying in different deleted-line blocks. This is possible for \(q\ge2\) because every retained line meets every deleted line exactly once. Their joining line is a retained line, giving a four-edge path between the two line vertices; thus the distance is \(4\).

A point and a retained line have distance \(1\) exactly when incident. If nonincident, take a point \(x\) on that retained line outside the deleted-line block of the first point. The unique line joining these two points is retained, yielding a three-edge path; bipartiteness excludes distance \(2\). Thus all nonincident point–line distances equal \(3\). Every vertex pair is covered by these cases, so the diameter is \(4\).

**2. Exact simplicial identification.** Among any two distinct vertices, the distance exceeds \(3\) precisely when both lie in the *same* one of the \(2q\) blocks of size \(q\) specified above. Thus the distance-three graph is *exactly* the complete \(2q\)-partite graph with equal part size \(q\). The clique complex of a complete multipartite graph is exactly the simplicial join of the discrete vertex sets of its parts. This proves the isomorphism, not merely a homology comparison.

**3. Integral homotopy and persistence.** The reduced integral homology of \(D_q\) is \(\mathbb Z^{q-1}\) in degree zero, with no other reduced homology. The join formula, or the expression \(D_q*Y\simeq\Sigma(D_q\wedge Y)\) with basepoints, applied repeatedly, gives a wedge of \((q-1)^{2q}\) spheres in dimension \(2q-1\). Equivalently, after choosing a point in each block as reference, products (simplicial joins) of the \(q-1\) zero-cycles formed by differences of vertices give a free integral basis for top homology; these classes have no torsion. All other reduced homology groups vanish.

For completeness, no radius-two simplex has more than \(q+1\) vertices. A simplex containing only point vertices, or only line vertices, contains at most one vertex from each of the \(q\) corresponding blocks. A mixed simplex containing at least two distinct points and two distinct lines is impossible: two points have at most one common retained line, whereas every mixed point–line pair within a radius-two simplex must be incident, since nonincidence distance is \(3\). If a mixed simplex has only one line, all its points lie on it and there are at most \(q\) such points; dually, if it has only one point, all its lines pass through it and there are at most \(q\) such lines. The radius-two complex therefore has dimension at most \(q\), strictly below \(2q-1\) for \(q\ge2\). Hence every top-degree class at radius three is born there. At radius four the graph diameter is reached and the Rips complex is the full simplex; every such class dies. As graph distances are integral, the asserted bars are precisely \([3,4)\).

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify_net_rips.py` builds the incidence graphs of the Desarguesian affine planes over prime fields of orders \(2,3,5,7\), deletes the vertical parallel class, and recomputes *every* shortest-path distance by independent breadth-first search. It checks all block-wise pair distances and the full bipartite incidence count. For orders \(2\) and \(3\), it exhaustively enumerates every nonempty simplex of the proposed join and verifies its face vector and the complete mod-two boundary ranks independently. The order-three complex has nonempty face vector \((18,135,540,1215,1458,729)\) and mod-two reduced homology only in degree five, of rank \(64\). The script reports `AFFINE_NET_SCALE3_CHECKS_OK` and its exact output is embedded as `verification_output.txt`.

This finite verification is a reproducibility check, not the proof for arbitrary affine planes. The preceding incidence-axiom argument supplies the unrestricted integral result.

## Relationship to prior work
The closest published affine-plane incidence calculation treats the **full**, undeleted point–line incidence graph. Its scale-two Rips complex has sphere summands in degrees two and \(q\), while at scale three its Rips complex is contractible because any point is then adjacent in the distance-three graph to all other vertices. Removing a parallel class changes intrinsic point–point distances from two to four within each deleted direction; therefore the old full-graph result neither implies nor contradicts this new high-dimensional fixed-radius homotopy type. The projective-plane incidence classification is likewise a different incidence geometry, and a Pappus-graph Čech calculation uses a different nerve construction. The broad graph-metric Rips analysis of Adams and collaborators studies Cartesian-product torus grids rather than affine nets.

The complete multipartite join form is elementary once the exact intrinsic distance partition is known; the substantive result is that a canonical incidence deletion realizes that join uniformly in every finite affine plane and creates a sharp homotopical transition. Available mathematical research records and relevant indexed primary sources were compared at the level of objects, metrics, parameters, and implied results. A formulation in unindexed literature on transversal designs, affine nets, Latin-square incidence graphs or graph powers could nonetheless cover this theorem.

## Limitations
No claim is made for arbitrary partial linear spaces, other line-deletion patterns, weighted graph distances, nonintegral scales, or lower-degree persistence bars. The result is a classification of the exact radius-three complex and its top-dimensional persistence interval, not an equivariant classification or a new theorem about existence of affine planes. The prime-order tests do not independently verify non-Desarguesian planes; those cases follow from the axiomatic proof. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
