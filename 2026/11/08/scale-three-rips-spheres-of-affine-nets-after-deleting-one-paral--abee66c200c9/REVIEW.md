# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The central claim is an exact simplicial isomorphism, not an inference from mod-two ranks. All six possible distance situations are determined using affine-plane incidence and graph bipartiteness: point–point across/within deleted blocks, line–line across/within retained parallel classes, and incident/nonincident point–line pairs. The only forbidden pairs at radius three lie within the same one of \(2q\) disjoint \(q\)-point blocks. The clique complex therefore is the join of those discrete blocks, whose integral homotopy type is a free wedge of \(2q-1\)-spheres with multiplicity \((q-1)^{2q}\). A separate maximum-simplex-size bound \(q+1\) at radius two proves these top classes are born at three; graph diameter four proves their death at four. Independent exact shortest-path checks at orders 2, 3, 5, 7, and complete mod-two chain calculations for 2 and 3, reproduce the stated specialized values. Finite checks do not replace the all-order proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Affine planes and their resolvable incidence structures are canonical finite geometries, with both Desarguesian and potentially non-Desarguesian instances. The result identifies a sharp and considerable topological phase change: the full incidence graph has a contractible radius-three Rips complex, whereas deleting one parallel line class yields \((q-1)^{2q}\) independent high-dimensional integral sphere classes, uniformly for every affine plane. It gives an exact combinatorial model and a complete top-degree persistence barcode with no need for numerics or existence assumptions beyond the plane axioms. This is a motivated infinite-family boundary under a natural symmetry-preserving deletion, not an arbitrary finite table entry or simple restatement of the undeletion theorem.
