# Scale-two Rips homotopy of arbitrary once-subdivided graphs

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite, connected, simple graph with at least one edge. Its once-subdivided graph \(S(G)\) is obtained by replacing each edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) with the two-edge path \(u-m_e-v\). Give the vertices of \(S(G)\) unit-edge shortest-path distance, and use the inclusive Rips convention: a finite vertex set is a simplex if all of its pairs have distance at most the scale. Let \(\operatorname{Cl}(G)\) be the clique (flag) complex of \(G\), and let \(t(G)\) be the number of unordered vertex triples spanning a triangle in \(G\). Then, without any girth, degree, planarity, or clique-number restriction,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(S(G))\simeq\operatorname{Cl}(G)\vee\bigvee^{t(G)}S^2.}
\]

The wedge splitting is an ordinary, generally noncanonical homotopy equivalence. It holds integrally and does not assume that \(\operatorname{Cl}(G)\) is itself a sphere wedge. Moreover, the scale-one inclusion induces, after the natural identification of \(S(G)\) with the underlying graph of \(G\), the canonical quotient
\[
H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_1(\operatorname{Cl}(G);\mathbb Z),
\]
whose kernel is the subgroup generated by oriented triangular cycles in \(G\). In every positive degree, the reduced integral homology of the scale-two complex is the reduced integral homology of \(\operatorname{Cl}(G)\), with an additional free rank-\(t(G)\) summand in degree two.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected, unweighted, and simple; \(G\) is connected and has at least one edge. The metric is on the *discrete vertex set* of the subdivision, not on every point of a geometric metric graph. A triangle is an ordinary three-edge cycle, not necessarily an induced component. At scale one the Rips complex is precisely the bipartite subdivision graph. At scale two it is the flag complex of the graph connecting vertex pairs of subdivision-distance one or two. No statement about scale three or higher is included.

## Proof
**1. Exact distance taxonomy.** Call the original vertices \(p_v\) and the subdivision vertices \(m_e\). Distinct originals \(p_u,p_v\) have distance at most two exactly when \(\{u,v\}\in E\). An original \(p_v\) and a subdivision vertex \(m_e\) have distance at most two exactly when \(v\in e\), by bipartite parity. Two distinct subdivision vertices \(m_e,m_f\) have distance at most two exactly when the distinct edges \(e,f\) share an endpoint. These assertions remain valid even when \(G\) contains triangles or four-cycles.

**2. Exhaustive simplex classification.** Write \(X=\operatorname{Cl}(G)\), embedded on the original vertices. In the scale-two complex, all simplices are contained in one of the following:

- \(X\) (cliques using only original vertices);
- \(C_v=\{p_v\}\cup\{m_e:v\in e\}\), for \(v\in V\);
- \(D_e=\{p_u,p_v,m_e\}\), for \(e=\{u,v\}\in E\);
- \(T_{uvw}=\{m_{uv},m_{vw},m_{uw}\}\), for every triangle \(\{u,v,w\}\) of \(G\).

Indeed, a clique containing at least three originals contains no subdivision vertex; one containing two originals and a subdivision vertex is one of the \(D_e\); one containing exactly one original lies in a \(C_v\). A pairwise-intersecting collection of at least two edges of a simple graph is either a star at one vertex or (for exactly three edges) the three edges of a triangle. This proves the all-subdivision-vertex cases and rules out larger exceptional simplices.

**3. A core that collapses to \(X\).** Let \(A\) be generated by \(X\), all \(C_v\), and all \(D_e\), *excluding* the interiors of the exceptional triangles \(T_{uvw}\). For each \(v\), successively, in decreasing cardinality, pair every face \(\sigma\) consisting of at least two subdivision vertices incident to \(v\) with \(\sigma\cup\{p_v\}\). At the moment of pairing, \(\sigma\) is a free codimension-one face: different edges of a simple graph have at most one common endpoint; all strict larger faces within \(C_v\) have already been removed; and \(X\) and the \(D_e\) have at most one subdivision vertex. The pairs are distinct for different \(v\). All \(C_v\) thereby reduce to their incidence edges \(\{p_v,m_e\}\). For each \(e=\{u,v\}\), collapse \(D_e\) along \(\{p_u,m_e\}\), followed by the remaining edge \(\{p_v,m_e\}\) along its now free vertex \(m_e\). The residual complex is exactly \(X\). This gives an explicit finite sequence of elementary simplicial collapses \(A\searrow X\).

**4. All additional triangles attach trivially.** By step 2 the full Rips complex is obtained from \(A\) by attaching precisely the \(t(G)\) closed two-simplices \(T_{uvw}\) along their full boundary loops; distinct interiors are disjoint. A side \(m_{uv}m_{vw}\) of such a boundary can be replaced, using the triangle in \(C_v\), by the two-edge path \(m_{uv}-p_v-m_{vw}\). Perform this on the three sides, then use the three triangles \(D_{uv},D_{vw},D_{uw}\) to replace the resulting six-edge loop by the original loop \(p_u-p_v-p_w-p_u\). The latter is nullhomotopic in \(X\subseteq A\) because \(\{p_u,p_v,p_w\}\) is a simplex of \(X\). Thus every attaching map \(S^1\to A\) is nullhomotopic, before any of the exceptional two-cells is attached. Attaching finitely many two-cells along nullhomotopic loops to a connected CW complex yields its wedge with that many two-spheres. Combining this with \(A\searrow X\) proves the boxed formula.

**5. The first persistence map.** At scale one, \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le1}(S(G))=S(G)\), since it is bipartite and has no triangles. The triangles \(D_e\) homotope every subdivided edge-path \(p_u-m_e-p_v\) to the original edge \(p_u-p_v\) inside \(A\), so the scale-one-to-two map on first homology agrees with \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\). The clique complex \(X\) is obtained from the graph by attaching all triangular two-faces and then cells of dimension at least three. Hence the kernel on integral first homology is exactly the subgroup generated by the triangle cycles. The extra triangles \(T_{uvw}\) have nullhomotopic boundaries and do not change first homology.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` independently constructs subdivision graphs, checks their radius-two distance graphs, enumerates all simplicial cliques and the four generating types, and verifies that the only faces missing from \(A\) are the exceptional triangle interiors. It replays the elementary-collapse sequence, checking each pair is free in the entire remaining core and that the residual complex equals \(\operatorname{Cl}(G)\). It separately computes exact mod-two simplicial boundary ranks for the full complex and verifies the predicted homology. Every labeled simple graph with one through five vertices was checked (\(1+2+8+64+1024=1099\) graphs, including disconnected stress cases), along with the house graph, octahedral graph, \(K_6\), \(K_{3,3}\), a triangulated hexagon, and the Petersen graph. The result was `SUBDIVISION_RIPS2_EXHAUSTIVE_SMALL_AND_NAMED_OK 1099 graphs`.

The proof is not extrapolated from these finite checks: steps 1–5 apply to arbitrary finite connected simple graphs. For the finite disconnected stress cases the checker verifies the componentwise face/collapse and homology identities, not an unqualified global wedge formula.

## Relationship to prior work
A public classification of the graph-metric Rips filtration of the once-subdivided *complete* graph proves the special case \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(S(K_n))\simeq\bigvee^{\binom n3}S^2\). Its proof introduces the same four kinds of maximal faces when the original-vertex clique is the entire simplex; its stated limitation explicitly excludes arbitrary base graphs. The present theorem replaces that contractible core with an arbitrary \(\operatorname{Cl}(G)\), proves an elementary collapse onto it, and identifies the resulting nontrivial fundamental-group and higher-homotopy contributions. The complete-graph result cannot imply the arbitrary-graph splitting because \(\operatorname{Cl}(G)\) need not be contractible, simply connected, or a sphere wedge.

Adams and collaborators' 2025 work investigates finite torus grids with the \(\ell^1\) metric; its statements concern periodic Cartesian grids, not subdivided arbitrary graph incidence metrics. Earlier metric-gluing theorems address specified wedges and short-path gluings of metric graphs, not the uniform radius-two vertex-filtration of all edge subdivisions. General clique-complex decomposition techniques provide useful context but do not by themselves state this exact triangle-count splitting.

## Limitations
The equivalence is a generally noncanonical *homotopy equivalence*, not an explicit PL homeomorphism and not a claim of equivariant or natural wedge-splitting. It concerns once-subdivided, unweighted simple graphs and graph-vertex distances only. No persistence beyond scale two is computed. Finite tests prove only the tested finite instances and include no independent human audit. Exhaustive and targeted bibliographic searches cannot exclude every unindexed theorem expressed in different terminology.
