# Scientific review: subdivision-graph scale-two Rips splitting

## Correctness
**PASS.** The vertex-distance taxonomy is exhaustive and valid under arbitrary graph triangles and high degrees. The pairwise-intersecting-edge lemma classifies all subdivision-vertex cliques. The core is reduced by an explicit sequence of genuine elementary simplicial collapses to the original clique complex; the only missing cells are one two-simplex per graph triangle. Their attaching loops are homotopic, via recorded core triangles, to filled original triangles and hence nullhomotopic. The CW attaching argument gives an unrestricted integral homotopy splitting, not merely a Betti-number prediction. The first-homology persistence map follows from the same deformation. The complete small-graph census and named tests check the model, every collapse freeness condition, and independent mod-two boundaries; these are supporting finite checks rather than the infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with remaining bibliographic risk.** The closest publicly accessible result for once-subdivided complete graphs treats \(S(K_n)\), where the original-vertex core is contractible; its complete `RESULT.md` was inspected and expressly excludes arbitrary base graphs. It neither asserts nor implies the preservation of potentially nontrivial \(\operatorname{Cl}(G)\) and the universal triangle-count splitting. The 2025 torus-grid paper (accessible preprint text), 2018 metric-gluing paper (PDF text with visual inspection), and 2021 general homotopical-decomposition paper (HTML full text, relevant sections) cover different graph classes or general decomposition principles, not the stated all-graphs classification. Semantic research-record searches and targeted literature searches under graph subdivision, graph-square clique complexes, line graphs, triangle attachments and equivalent formulas found the complete-graph special case but no broader covering theorem. A differently indexed equivalent result remains possible; absence from search is not a proof of novelty.

## Value
**PASS.** This classifies a genuinely unrestricted family of Rips complexes rather than extending a numerical table. An arbitrary finite flag complex may occur as the clique complex of a graph, so the theorem retains general homotopy phenomena, including nonfree fundamental groups and higher-dimensional or torsion-bearing topology, while separating a systematic free two-dimensional summand. It also identifies the precise integral first-persistence map from graph cycles to clique-complex cycles. Such a general reduction is useful for understanding how local triangles alter graph-metric thickening without erasing unrelated topological structure. The result is not claimed to classify higher scales or provide a canonical splitting.

## Closest literature and limitations
The complete-graph subdivision result is covered exactly when \(G\) is complete, and the new statement is not original on that subfamily. The torus-grid paper provides the recent motivation for graph-metric Rips homotopy classification but has a different metric family. Metric-gluing and general decomposition papers may yield alternate methods; no theorem with the present unrestricted object and triangle invariant was found in the material inspected. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed; finite checks cannot establish the all-graph statement on their own.
