# Scale-two Vietoris–Rips complexes of periodic honeycomb graphs

## Finding

For any integer \(L\ge5\), let \(H_L\) be the periodic hexagonal (honeycomb) graph whose vertices are black and white symbols \(B_{i,j},W_{i,j}\), indexed by \((i,j)\in(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)^2\). Join \(B_{i,j}\) to exactly \(W_{i,j},W_{i-1,j},W_{i,j-1}\). The graph has \(2L^2\) vertices, \(3L^2\) edges and a natural cellular embedding in the torus with \(L^2\) hexagonal faces.

Give its vertices unit-edge shortest-path distance and let \(K_L=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(H_L)\): each finite set is a simplex exactly when every pair of its vertices has graph distance at most two. Then
\[
\boxed{K_L\simeq T^2\vee\bigvee^{L^2}S^2,\qquad L\ge5.}
\]
Consequently its integral homology is \(H_0(K_L)=\mathbb Z\), \(H_1(K_L)=\mathbb Z^2\), \(H_2(K_L)=\mathbb Z^{L^2+1}\), and \(H_q(K_L)=0\) for \(q\ge3\). Moreover, the scale-one inclusion \(H_L\hookrightarrow K_L\) induces a surjective integral homomorphism on first homology, whose kernel is the primitive rank-\(L^2-1\) lattice generated by the oriented boundaries of the hexagonal faces, with the unique relation that their coherently oriented sum vanishes.

## Assumptions and scope

All indices are modulo \(L\). The bound \(L\ge5\) ensures that shortest noncontractible graph cycles have length at least \(2L>6\), so the only simple six-cycles are the hexagonal faces of the usual torus honeycomb cellulation. The graph is simple, connected, bipartite and 3-regular. Scale two is inclusive, and distances are graph distances among vertices, not Euclidean distances or the length metric on interiors of graph edges. The torus \(T^2=S^1\times S^1\) has its ordinary CW homotopy type. This concerns the ordinary integral homotopy type, not an equivariant splitting.

## Proof

**1. Geometry of the periodic honeycomb.** Regard the black vertices as points on the triangular lattice. A black-to-black walk of length two has coordinate difference among \((\pm1,0),(0,\pm1),(1,-1),(-1,1)\); the same holds for the white vertices after a coordinate shift. For \(L\ge5\), the local triangular-lattice clique structure is unchanged by passing to the quotient: each color-class distance-two graph has triangular 3-cliques but no 4-cliques. Its triangles consist precisely of (a) three neighbors of one opposite-color vertex and (b) the alternate three vertices of a hexagonal face. The two kinds are distinct. Between opposite colors, graph distance at most two means graph adjacency (by bipartiteness); four vertices with two of each color would give a four-cycle, which does not exist. Thus every four-vertex simplex in \(K_L\) is the closed star \(\{v\}\cup N(v)\) of a graph vertex, and there are no larger simplices. Every triangle not contained in such a four-vertex simplex consists of one alternating triple in one hexagonal face, with precisely two such triangles per face.

For clarity about the finite quotient, the two-step displacement in a color class has coordinate increments of absolute value at most one. A noncontractible graph closed walk must make a nonzero total displacement divisible by \(L\) in one coordinate, requiring at least \(L\) two-step moves, so length at least \(2L\). In the infinite honeycomb, every six-cycle is a single face boundary; hence the same holds for \(H_L\) for \(L\ge5\).

**2. Collapse the centered part.** Let \(Y\subseteq K_L\) be the union of the full tetrahedra \(\Delta[\{v\}\cup N(v)]\), one centered at each graph vertex. Since the graph has no four-cycle, each distance-two vertex pair has a unique common neighbor. Hence every face of \(Y\) containing a distance-two edge belongs to exactly one centered tetrahedron. For each center \(v\), first delete the tetrahedron and its free opposite triangle \(N(v)\). Next delete each remaining triangle \(\{v,a,b\}\), where \(a,b\) are distinct neighbors of \(v\), together with its free opposite distance-two edge \(\{a,b\}\). These are genuine elementary free-face collapses in \(Y\), and distinct centers do not interfere. The surviving complex is exactly the original graph \(H_L\), and the collapse fixes that graph pointwise. Under this deformation retraction every distance-two edge \(ab\) is homotopic relative endpoints to the two-edge path \(a-v-b\) via its unique midpoint.

**3. Attach the noncentered faces.** By Step 1 the only faces of \(K_L\) outside \(Y\) are exactly \(2L^2\) maximal triangles: for each hexagonal face, the black alternating triple and white alternating triple. Their boundary edges already belong to \(Y\). Under the retraction of Step 2, the attaching loop of either alternating triangle becomes the oriented six-edge boundary of that hexagonal face, up to reversal. Homotopy invariance of CW-cell attachment therefore gives a CW model for \(K_L\) consisting of graph \(H_L\) and **two 2-cells attached along each facial hexagon loop**.

**4. Identify the homotopy type.** First attach one of these two 2-cells along each of the \(L^2\) hexagonal face loops. This is exactly the standard cellular torus obtained by gluing the regular periodic honeycomb hexagons, hence homeomorphic to \(T^2\). Each of the remaining \(L^2\) cells now attaches along a loop already bounded by an existing disk, so its attaching map is nullhomotopic. Attaching a 2-cell along a nullhomotopic loop to a connected CW complex adds a wedge summand \(S^2\). This proves the displayed homotopy equivalence integrally, with no inference from finite samples.

**5. The scale-one map.** The graph has cycle rank \(3L^2-2L^2+1=L^2+1\). Its first homology surjects onto that of the attached complex because only 2-cells are added. The kernel is the image of the first \(L^2\) cellular attaching boundaries; the remaining duplicated 2-cells add no new boundary classes. In the oriented cellular chain complex of the torus, these face boundaries have precisely one integral relation, their coherently oriented sum (equivalently \(H_2(T^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\)). The resulting quotient is free \(\mathbb Z^2\), so this image is primitive and has rank \(L^2-1\). This establishes the last assertion. \(\square\)

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` from the embedded artifact directory. The dependency-free program builds the graph from its defining modular incidences, independently computes all shortest distances through radius two, enumerates all Rips edges/triangles/tetrahedra, checks that tetrahedra are precisely the closed graph neighborhoods, identifies the two noncentered alternating triangles for every hexagon, and checks that each graph edge belongs to exactly two face hexagons (so the known edge-disjoint-hexagon theorem does not apply). It then calculates exact mod-two boundary-matrix ranks in each dimension for \(L=5,6,7,8\). In every case the output is \(\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_1=2\), \(\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_2=L^2+1\), and \(H_3=0\), together with the stated face vector. The exact standard output is included as `verification_output.txt`. The all-\(L\) integral theorem relies on Steps 1–5, not on these finite examples or on a mod-two-to-integral inference.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams, Adetowubo, Barriga-Acosta, Feng and Sterling study \(\ell^1\) Rips complexes of **square** torus grids \(C_n\square C_n\); their Theorem 5.8 gives a torus homotopy type in a stable small-scale regime. The present graphs are degree-three hexagonal lattices, not Cartesian products of cycles; their hexagonal faces contribute an additional \(L^2\) independent 2-sphere summands. The square-grid theorem therefore does not imply this result.

Adamaszek's 2013 *Clique complexes and graph powers*, Proposition 2.2, establishes a collapse to the original graph when the graph girth is at least seven at scale two. The present graphs have girth exactly six, so the hypothesis is violated; the same work treats an isolated six-cycle but not overlapping facial hexagons on a torus.

A published hexagon-packing result in the published-finding corpus mathematical record treats graphs whose different six-cycles are **edge-disjoint**; the honeycomb family lies outside that hypothesis, since every edge borders two facial hexagons. A separate exact result for the 24-vertex Nauru graph obtains a torus with twelve 2-spheres, but it concerns one specific generalized Petersen graph and does not imply the present arbitrary-\(L\) honeycomb torus classification. The 2026 affine Coxeter-group Rips paper proves eventual contractibility for sufficiently large scales with standard Coxeter generators, rather than a scale-two homotopy calculation. Unindexed prior equivalent calculations remain a risk; the comparison is evidence-based and is not a proof of uniqueness.

## Limitations

The restriction \(L\ge5\) is essential to the stated proof because smaller quotients can contain additional short nonfacial cycles and simplices. This does not classify any higher Rips scales, other periodic quotient lattices, or the integral equivariant action of the translation group. The result gives ordinary homotopy equivalence, not a canonical PL-homeomorphism. The finite checks do not independently validate infinite-family or integral assertions, which are proved by the combinatorial and CW arguments.
