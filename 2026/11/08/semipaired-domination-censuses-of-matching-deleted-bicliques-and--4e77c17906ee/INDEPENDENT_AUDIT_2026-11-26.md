# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Same-shore pairs have a common neighbor since only two opposite vertices can be forbidden; a deleted cross pair has a three-edge path and is the only forbidden type. In any pairing of an even set of size >=4, switching the partners of a forbidden pair and another pair removes that forbidden pair and creates none. For domination, two selected vertices on each shore suffice; a singleton in a mixed set must be unmatched or accompanied by its deleted partner. Two singletons must be both unmatched or be a deleted pair, and a pure shore must be selected in full. Counting these disjoint cases yields D and its even part minus tz^2 yields S. A minimal four-set contains no unmatched cross pair: for 2+2 this requires one selected shore to be entirely matched; for 1+3 the singleton is either unmatched and faced by three matched vertices, or matched with its partner in the triple. Full size-four shores add the indicators. Every larger even mixed dominating set contains a dominating 2+2 or 1+3 subset; larger even full shores are minimal and there are no other cases. Size four exists by choosing 2+2, so the absence of unmatched pairs gives minimum four. In the crown-to-one-restored comparison the shore indicators cancel (the intermediate expressions omit the same two terms at n=4); the difference is -(n-1)^2 - 2 binom(n-1,2) + 2 binom(n-1,3), giving the claimed identity and signs, including -8 at n=9 and 15 at n=10. No unmatched cross pair exists in the crown, so its stated even-order spectrum follows. Independent enumeration matched all three polynomials for 78 instances and 83,378 nonempty subsets, with both shores 3–6 and every t. The unrestricted claim rests on the proof, not this bounded replay.
