# Seven vertices are sharp for two outbreak-variance ranking reversals on trees

## Finding

Consider a finite undirected tree \(T\), independently retain each edge with the same probability \(p\in(0,1)\), and let \(X_s(p)\) denote the number of vertices in the open-edge component of a prescribed seed \(s\). For two seeds \(s,t\), a **variance ranking reversal** means a change of the sign of \(\operatorname{Var}(X_s(p))-\operatorname{Var}(X_t(p))\) as \(p\) increases through an interior parameter value.

**Sharp cutoff and first-layer classification.** No tree with at most six vertices admits two distinct variance ranking reversals for any two seeds. At seven vertices two reversals occur; among all seven-vertex tree-and-unordered-seed-pair configurations, exactly two isomorphism types have two distinct reversals. Every other such configuration has at most one interior crossing. Both exceptional types have exactly two simple crossings.

One explicit seven-vertex tree has vertices \(0,1,\ldots,6\) and edges

\[
\{\{0,1\},\{0,4\},\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{4,5\},\{4,6\}\}.
\]

The seed \(s=0\) is the degree-two central connector, and \(t=1\) is a degree-three branch vertex. Their exact difference is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(X_0(p))-\operatorname{Var}(X_1(p))
=p(1-p)\bigl(-1+9p-3p^2-8p^3-4p^4\bigr).}
\]

The bracket has exactly two simple zeros in \((0,1)\), approximately \(0.1172051549\) and \(0.7098428707\). The difference is negative, positive, negative on the three consecutive intervals. The decimals only illustrate the already exact two-root proof.

A second exceptional tree consists of one degree-three branch vertex with two one-edge leaf arms and one four-edge leaf arm. Its exceptional seed pair consists of the branch vertex and the next vertex down the long arm. In one orientation the variance difference is

\[
 p(1-p)\bigl(1-6p-4p^2+7p^3+5p^4+3p^5+p^6\bigr),
\]

and again has exactly two simple interior crossings. All source pairs referred to in this classification are *unordered*; exchanging their order changes the sign but not the crossings.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is a connected, finite, simple, unweighted **tree**; there is one deterministic initial seed, each edge is independently open with probability \(p\), and infection is exactly the connected component of the seed in the open-edge subgraph. This is an independent-transmission or bond-percolation epidemic, not the continuous-time Markovian SIR model with correlated transmissions from a shared infectious period. There are no loops, repeated edges, heterogeneous parameters, or additional seeds. The minimality and uniqueness assertions quantify over *all* trees and vertex pairs with the stated order, not a particular family such as paths. The endpoints \(p=0\) and \(p=1\) are deterministic and excluded from reversal counting.

## Proof

For any tree \(T\) and vertex \(s\), let \(d(u,v)\) denote graph distance, and write \(I_v\) for the indicator that the unique \(s\)-to-\(v\) path is entirely open. Then \(X_s=\sum_v I_v\), and independence of edges gives

\[
\mathbb E X_s(p)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}p^{d(s,v)}.
\]

The union of the unique \(s\)-to-\(u\) and \(s\)-to-\(v\) paths has

\[
\ell_s(u,v)=\frac{d(s,u)+d(s,v)+d(u,v)}2
\]

edges. Hence the *exact* variance polynomial is

\[
 V_{T,s}(p)=\sum_{u,v\in V(T)}p^{\ell_s(u,v)}-
 \left(\sum_{v\in V(T)}p^{d(s,v)}\right)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

This identity follows from unique tree paths, including diagonal pairs \(u=v\); no asymptotics or independence between vertex-infection indicators is asserted.

For any two seeds, the difference \(H(p)=V_{T,s}(p)-V_{T,t}(p)\) has integer coefficients and vanishes at both \(0\) and \(1\). Thus \(H(p)=p(1-p)Q(p)\) for an integer polynomial \(Q\), or \(H\equiv0\). A finite exact classification now uses Cayley's Prüfer encoding: every labeled tree on \(n\) vertices is generated by an \((n-2)\)-tuple from \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\). Unrooted tree isomorphism types are deduplicated by canonical parenthesis encoding from the center or pair of centers. For each representative tree and every unordered pair of vertices, equation (1) yields the exact coefficients of \(Q\).

To count interior roots without numerical approximation, for \(d=\deg Q\) form

\[
 R(z)=(1+z)^d Q\left(\frac{z}{1+z}\right).
\tag{2}
\]

The increasing map \(p=z/(1+z)\) bijects \((0,\infty)\) with \((0,1)\), preserving positive-root multiplicity. Descartes' rule of signs bounds the number of positive roots by the number of sign changes in the *exact integer coefficients* of \(R\). Writing \(Q(p)=\sum_{k=0}^d q_kp^k\), the coefficient of \(z^j\) in (2) is exactly \(\sum_{k=0}^j q_k\binom{d-k}{j-k}\); the finite test therefore uses integers only.

The exhaustive check gives the following counts. "Nonidentical pairs" counts unordered vertex pairs with \(H\not\equiv0\), on one representative of each unrooted isomorphism class. The last column counts those with a two-sign-change upper bound under (2).

| Vertices | Unrooted tree classes | Nonidentical pairs | Pairs with two-sign-change bound |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| 4 | 2 | 7 | 0 |
| 5 | 3 | 21 | 0 |
| 6 | 6 | 64 | 0 |
| 7 | 11 | 182 | 3 |

In every nonidentical pair through six vertices, equation (2) has exactly one coefficient sign change; hence there is at most one interior zero and at most one reversal. On seven vertices, the three listed source pairs with two sign changes fall into exactly two vertex-pair-marked tree isomorphism classes (one exceptional tree has an automorphism interchanging its equivalent choices). All the remaining 179 nonidentical pairs have exactly one sign-change upper bound. Identical variance polynomials produce no strict reversals.

For the displayed seven-vertex witness, direct substitution into (1) gives the bracket

\[
 Q_1(p)=-1+9p-3p^2-8p^3-4p^4.
\]

Its coefficient sequence in descending degree has exactly two sign changes, so Descartes permits at most two positive roots; moreover \(Q_1(0)=-1\), \(Q_1(1/2)=3/2\), and \(Q_1(1)=-7\). The intermediate value theorem forces one root in each of \((0,1/2)\) and \((1/2,1)\). They must be distinct, simple, and the only positive roots. Since \(p(1-p)>0\) inside \((0,1)\), the claimed two strict reversals follow.

For the second exceptional class, choose the orientation

\[
 Q_2(p)=1-6p-4p^2+7p^3+5p^4+3p^5+p^6.
\]

Descartes again gives at most two positive roots, while \(Q_2(0)=1\), \(Q_2(1/2)=-109/64\), and \(Q_2(1)=7\); hence exactly two simple interior roots. Canonical marked-tree encoding identifies the three exceptional representative pairs with these two shapes. This completes the sharp cutoff and the seven-vertex classification.

The checker constructs every labeled tree through seven vertices from Prüfer sequences, canonically deduplicates unrooted trees, constructs (1) by integer distance counting, factors out \(p(1-p)\), and applies (2) with exact integer coefficients. It checks the tree counts, both source-pair isomorphism classes, and all sign variations. Independently of (1), it enumerates all \(2^6\) edge configurations of the displayed witness, expands their exact Bernoulli probabilities and reproduces both full variance polynomials. ## Relationship to prior work

Moore and Newman (2000) derive outbreak-size distributions and moments for small-world site and bond percolation, providing the cluster-based disease interpretation and the distinction between randomly chosen and prescribed sources. Their formulas concern their specific large-system ensemble, not rank changes between fixed roots of arbitrary seven-vertex trees. Miller (2008) analyzes epidemic size and probability bounds under heterogeneous transmission on networks, not the fixed-tree second-moment ordering here. Bucur and Holme (2020) exhaustively compare **expected** outbreak sizes across seed vertices in a Markovian SIR setting, rather than the two-interior-crossing phenomenon of bond-percolation **variances**. A separately established path result gives exactly one variance crossover for different seed positions on every path; it neither covers branching trees nor implies the present sharp two-crossover boundary. These are statement-and-scope comparisons, not a claim that exhaustive historical priority has been independently established.

## Limitations

The cutoff is for connected trees, independent homogeneous undirected bond retention, and two variance reversals **within one fixed graph**. It does not classify all general graphs, non-tree epidemics, heterogeneous per-edge probabilities, Markovian SIR transmission, or the maximal number of reversals for trees with eight or more vertices. The finite classification is computer-assisted but exact and reproducible; the full open-ended historical priority of the finite-tree crossover question remains uncertain.
