# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a sharp, finite two-reversal boundary for homogeneous independent bond retention on trees. The unique-path indicator expansion gives the full variance polynomial for every seed. A separately coded Bernoulli edge-mask expansion reproduces the two witness polynomials. The exhaustive Prüfer enumeration and canonical center-based tree signatures cover *all* trees through seven vertices, while integer-coefficient Möbius transformation plus Descartes' rule proves the small-tree upper bound. Two exact sign brackets and a two-root Descartes bound prove both minimal witnesses' roots are simple. The result makes no claim beyond the finite cutoff or the specified process.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

Moore–Newman (2000) treat outbreak moments for large small-world network models rather than within-tree seed comparisons. Miller (2008) treats heterogeneity extremal bounds rather than source-wise variance crossover polynomials. Bucur–Holme (2020) study exact mean outbreak size in Markovian SIR graphs, not the fixed-tree independent-bond variance ranking. A related path-only theorem proves one crossing on paths but does not include branching trees. No source inspected or relevant indexed record establishes the seven-vertex two-crossing boundary or its two first-layer marked-tree classes. The conclusion is a best-evidence originality assessment, not a claim of independently established priority; a historically overlooked finite-tree result remains possible.

## Value — PASS

Variance of outbreak size quantifies seed-specific uncertainty at fixed transmissibility, distinct from expected size. The exact seven-vertex threshold identifies the smallest branching contact structure in which comparing the uncertainty from two seeds can change direction twice under a single increasing transmission parameter. The complete minimal-layer classification shows this is a reproducible topology effect, not an arbitrary curve-fitting sample. It gives a boundary for attempts to generalize single-crossing conclusions from paths to trees and a benchmark for exact epidemic-influence comparisons.

## Relationship to relevant literature and limits

Sources: Moore and Newman (2000), arXiv:cond-mat/0001393v1; Miller (2008), arXiv:0803.0999; Bucur and Holme (2020), DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008052. Their objects/moments were compared by reading available HTML text, not merely titles. The result is limited to independent, homogeneous edge outcomes on finite trees. It does not transfer automatically to Markovian SIR with shared random infectious periods, to cyclic graphs, or to all larger tree orders.
