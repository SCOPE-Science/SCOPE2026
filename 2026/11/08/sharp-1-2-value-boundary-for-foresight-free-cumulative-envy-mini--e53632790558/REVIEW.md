# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The central claim is proved for every finite horizon and every integer starting imbalance by backward induction on a suffix value function. The two invariants—monotonicity on parity classes and the special difference \(0\le F_W(2)-F_W(0)\le2\)—prove the greedy action always minimizes the full suffix objective for the \(\{1,2\}\) alphabet. Exact four-good assignments and a two-state lower bound verify sharpness with value \(3\) and with value \(0\). A reverse-triangle argument rules out earlier failures for arbitrary nonnegative values. The standalone finite checker independently corroborates but does not substitute for these infinite proofs.

**Originality — PASS, with publication-priority limits.** Goldberg, Robinson, and Teh (arXiv:2610.09843v1, Sections 4 and Appendix 0.B.1) establish greedy exactness for identical binary \(\{0,1\}\) valuations and a general dynamic program; these do not imply greedy exactness for all arbitrary-order positive \(\{1,2\}\) sequences. Elkind et al. (arXiv:2410.14593) establish temporal EF1 feasibility; Amanatidis et al. (arXiv:2505.22174) analyze MMS/EF1 guarantees for two-value preferences. Direct statement/implication comparisons and targeted title/phrase searches found no complete covering result. Published indexing is incomplete, and older equivalent signed-discrepancy lemmas cannot be ruled out absolutely.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The initiating work proves strong hardness for cumulative envy minimization, but its uncomplicated greedy optimality is guaranteed only when the identical values are binary. The present theorem gives a nontrivial sharp next tractability island: in the strictly positive two-size class, an online rule is *exactly optimal against complete foresight* for every horizon, despite failing to minimize discrepancy at every prefix. Its sharp \(3\)-value and zero-value counterexamples explain which modelling assumptions make this simplification possible. It is an exact algorithmic boundary, not merely a finite isolated count or a routine restatement of prefix balancing.

**Limitations and risks.** Two agents; identical additive values; positive alphabet \(\{1,2\}\) up to a uniform scale; fixed arrival order; unweighted sum of maximum envies only. No claim of independent verification, priority over all unindexed papers, broader-valued universality, or complexity hardness outside the domain. The finite checker does not certify the infinite theorem.
