# Scientific review: sharp Fourier-grid sampling on noncircular ellipses

## Correctness — PASS
The claim uses the real *linear span* of squared-modulus profiles, not only the nonlinear rank-one image. Conjugate-polynomial polarization justifies replacing intensities by arbitrary Hermitian coefficient forms. The Joukowski linear change of coordinates gives an explicit principal quadratic kernel ideal; bidegree controls its Hermitian dimension. Highest-harmonic exclusion and the dimension count identify the entire trigonometric subspace, so the discrete Fourier sampling criterion follows with no genericity loophole. The rational quotient on two concentric circles is forced constant by incompatible reflection symmetries, resolving zeros and the global phase. Exact Fraction-rank tests and independent finite Fourier reconstruction checks replay from the packaged checker. The circle limit, degree-one boundary, singular shifted grids and distinctions between linear profile interpolation and nonlinear phase retrieval are explicitly covered.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The ellipse polynomial uniqueness consequence is **not** asserted to be new: Joukowski pullback reduces it to the previously published two-circle uniqueness theorem of Chalendar–Partington (2024). The actual proposed increment is the complete ellipse Hermitian restriction-kernel structure, its exact intensity Fourier space, and the sharp-size equispaced design with exact exceptional offsets. The 2019 phaseless polynomial interpolation result allows unrestricted selected nodes and does not provide these ellipse-constrained linear-profile statements; the 2023 parallel-line classification is also different. Similar dimensional behavior on polynomial graphs recorded previously does not imply the ellipse result, and published-record searches found no direct covering ellipse sampling theorem. The older Garg–Pal (2018) ellipse polynomial zero-location paper was identifiable by abstract, but its full text was not available; unexpected additional related work remains possible. The absence of a hit is not a proof of publication priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The sharp \(4d\) real sampling threshold gives a deterministic exact design on a natural noncircular analytic curve, including an if-and-only-if criterion that identifies an entire family of singular rotations of an otherwise optimal grid. It separates complete phase uniqueness from failure of Hermitian-matrix recovery at degrees at least two. The abrupt circle limit has both a distinct rank law \(2d+1\) and a different uniqueness behavior. These are structural sampling facts, not a mere interpolation algorithm renaming; the result does not claim optimal nonlinear sample complexity, quantitative conditioning bounds, or numerical robustness.
