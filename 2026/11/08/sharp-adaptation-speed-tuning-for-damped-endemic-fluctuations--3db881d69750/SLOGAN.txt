In a prevalence-responsive SIS network, keeping the ratio of link-breaking to link-creation rates fixed while adjusting their common speed gives a unique fastest local endemic relaxation at an explicit critically damped speed; making the network react arbitrarily fast is strictly slower. A sharp nonempty speed interval produces damped, infinitely repeated endemic-prevalence crossings, and the optimal spectral decay gain over the fast-network limit is exactly \(1+\sqrt{\omega/(\tau+\omega)}\).
