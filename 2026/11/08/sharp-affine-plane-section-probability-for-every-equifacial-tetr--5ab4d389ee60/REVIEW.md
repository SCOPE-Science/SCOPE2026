# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The invariant plane measure has a width-weighted direction marginal. Counting intersected edges gives the expectation of the 3/4-valued section complexity; the polyhedral mean-width identity yields the exact formula. Direct normalization of facet normals proves the exterior-angle formula. The Schläfli differential identity is explicitly derived, not merely invoked; its stationarity consequence and the strictly smaller boundary maximum prove unique global optimality at the regular tetrahedron. The upper endpoint is sharp but is never attained by a positive-volume tetrahedron. Five direct plane-sampling tests and 100,000 additional shape tests support these steps, without replacing the proof.

## Originality
PASS, subject to historical-priority risk. Finch's 2011 mean-width analysis treats the regular tetrahedron and does not imply a sharp probability extremum across all equifacial tetrahedra. The 1992 random-projection literature concerns shadows, not translation-invariant affine sections. A closely related published central-plane section diagram has different plane sampling and therefore does not cover the present range theorem. Schneider's older random-flat inequalities have relevant general scope, but only abstract-level material was accessible, leaving a priority uncertainty; none of the inspected text states this shape classification. No claim of proven global uniqueness from database searches is made.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The result identifies a unique extremal shape over the full two-parameter similarity family of equifacial tetrahedra, the exact attainable probability interval, and a nontrivial aspect-dependent flattening law. It distinguishes two natural models of random section sampling and supplies a geometric explanation through the normalized edge-angle functional, rather than merely recomputing the regular tetrahedron's width.

## Scope and remaining risks
All statements require nondegenerate Euclidean equifacial tetrahedra and motion-invariant affine planes conditioned on intersection. The extremum does not extend by the present argument to arbitrary tetrahedra or central planes. Published source access was incomplete for Schneider's random-flat work; independent verification and historical-priority assessment remain open.
