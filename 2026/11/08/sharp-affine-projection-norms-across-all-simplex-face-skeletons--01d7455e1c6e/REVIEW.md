# Scientific review — simplex face-skeleton projector

## Correctness: PASS

Uniform choice of an \(r\)-vertex face and conditional Dirichlet barycentric law give an exact two-eigenvalue Gram matrix. Its explicit inverse yields a normalized reproducing kernel. Convexity locates the norm at vertices; strict sign conflict proves no nonvertex can tie when \(r\ge2\). A one-dimensional beta-tail integral computes the sharp norm and positivity defect. Differentiating the kernel positive mass after the face-rank change of variables proves the strict ambient-dimension monotonicity and sharp ceiling. The two asymptotic regimes follow from exact limits. Exact rational checker results test, but do not replace, the analytic proof.

## Originality: PASS, with residual risks

The previously published volume-simplex formula is covered at \(r=m\) and is expressly not claimed as new. An accessible full text of that result addresses only uniform full-dimensional volume. The closest general projector articles (Hangelbroek et al. 2010; Crouzeix–Thomée 1987) concern kernel-network or globally coupled finite-element stability, not face-balanced lower-dimensional measures or this exact two-parameter norm. Białas-Cież et al. 2023 address numerical Lebesgue-constant evaluation and do not give a covering closed skeleton formula in inspected material. Targeted published-result searches and the comparison literature did not identify an equivalent face-rank law. Unindexed or differently termed skeletal quadrature literature remains a priority risk, and no independent literature audit has been performed.

## Mathematical value: PASS

For the canonical hierarchy from vertex observations through all face dimensions to volume, the result identifies every exact endpoint norm with a single formula and proves a sharp stability ceiling determined by *face rank* rather than ambient dimension. The monotone approach to the optimal ceiling and the distinct proportional-rank scaling law characterize two structurally different high-dimensional regimes. The exact positivity defect and norming vertices provide sharp operator-theoretic witnesses rather than merely moment or matrix calculations.

## Scope and limitations

Equal face probabilities are essential for affine invariance; arbitrary Hausdorff-weighted irregular skeletons are not covered. The operator is local, real, and degree one. The exact volume-only endpoint is older. A priority risk remains if prior specialized skeletal cubature results contain an unlocated equivalent formula.
