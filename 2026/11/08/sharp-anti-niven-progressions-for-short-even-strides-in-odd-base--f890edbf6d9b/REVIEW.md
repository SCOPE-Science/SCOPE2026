# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof constructs a CRT residue avoiding each prime divisor of \(b-1\) across exactly \(p-1\) terms; the existence follows because each unshared prime is at least \(p\). The bound on \(d(p-2)\) confines all low offsets to two base-\(b\) digits. Their digit sums differ from the offsets by either zero or \(b-1\), which makes the corresponding number-minus-digit-sum discrepancy equal to \((b-1)\) or \(2(b-1)\) after a simultaneous prime-residue choice of exponent. The gcd argument is exact. The matching upper bound comes from the inevitable multiple of \(p\) in any \(p\) consecutive progression terms. Exact independent finite tests cover 1343 eligible parameter pairs, though these tests are not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, within inspected evidence

The original full arXiv manuscript (arXiv:2308.05228) was read through its statements, proofs, and concluding Conjecture 4.3. Its maximality result is unconditional for difference two, while the even-difference assertion is presented as a conjecture. Targeted searches of later literature and published mathematical records did not identify the short-stride parameter theorem. Its explicit \(d=4,p=5\) corollary is beyond the original statement for difference two. A differently indexed subsequent proof is possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result establishes sharp maximum lengths for a natural two-parameter region of an expressly posed number-theoretic progression problem. It gives infinitely many constructions without restrictions on extra prime factors of \(b-1\), not just isolated numerical witnesses. It also settles the natural first nontrivial even step, \(d=4\), across the entire family with least admissible prime \(p=5\). The two-digit carry restriction is a meaningful boundary: larger strides may require distinct arithmetic ideas.

## Limits and closest literature

The published \(p-1\) upper bound is reused, not claimed as new. The main theorem does not settle all even differences, and the external comparison is not an exhaustive uniqueness certificate. Verification includes symbolic proof and finite independent computations; no independent scientific audit was performed.
