For any odd base \(b\) and even stride \(d\), if \(p\) is the least prime dividing \(b-1\) but not \(d\) and \((p-2)d\le b+1\), the longest arithmetic progression of integers coprime to their base-\(b\) digit sums has exactly \(p-1\) terms, and this length occurs infinitely often. In particular, stride four attains its sharp four-term limit in every odd base with \(5\mid b-1\) and \(3\nmid b-1\).
