# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

Both directions of the sharp polynomial threshold follow from an exact Vandermonde determinant and explicit indistinguishable polynomials. The all-holomorphic irrational case uses equality of zero orders at the accumulation point, a holomorphic quotient, and a leading-Taylor-term/dense-rotation contradiction. The global-phase and zero cases are included. The exact-arithmetic checker confirms finite instances and nine-by-nine inversion, without pretending to establish an infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The closest primary article, Chalendar and Partington (arXiv:2403.16255), proves phaseless uniqueness on two fixed intersecting rays and a sequential version requiring a sequence on each fixed ray. One-point-per-radius irrational-spiral sampling has no repeated ray and does not satisfy those assumptions. Perez (arXiv:2009.02061) covers two segments, Liehr (arXiv:2308.05722) covers unions of parallel lines, and Grohs–Liehr–Rathmair (arXiv:2308.00385) cover Fock-space perturbations of Liouville sets. Neither those inspected statements nor the closest mathematical database and accessible prior-claim searches establish the sharp denominator classification, first-prefix full-intensity Vandermonde interpolation, or unrestricted holomorphic irrational-spiral uniqueness. A general real-analytic uniqueness-set result not explicitly phrased as phase retrieval remains a possible stronger prior theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural complete arithmetic classification of one-complex-ratio phaseless sampling geometries, with a sharp finite-degree information threshold and constructive full-intensity recovery. It shows that a set meeting each ray through the accumulation point at most once can nevertheless determine every holomorphic function up to phase, in contrast to previously studied two-ray configurations. The result is mathematical uniqueness, not an efficient or stable noisy measurement design.

## Closest literature and limitations

Chalendar–Partington, arXiv:2403.16255 (Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.5); Perez, arXiv:2009.02061 (Theorem 2.1); Liehr, arXiv:2308.05722 (Theorem 1.1); Grohs–Liehr–Rathmair, arXiv:2308.00385 (Theorem 2.4). The finite sample count is optimal only for linear recovery of an entire ambient intensity profile. No uniform stability, no minimum nonlinear sample count, and no independent scientific audit are established.
