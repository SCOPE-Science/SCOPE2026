# Sharp binary symmetry asymptotics for random perfect evolution algebras

## Finding

For each integer \(n\ge2\), choose \(M=(m_{ij})\) uniformly from \(\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)\) and form the labeled evolution algebra \(E_M\) with natural basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\), products \(e_ie_j=0\) for \(i\ne j\), and \(e_i^2=\sum_jm_{ij}e_j\). Put \(p_n=\Pr(\operatorname{Aut}(E_M)\ne1)\) and
\[
 t_n=\binom n2\frac{2^{-2n+1}}{(1-2^{-(n-1)})(1-2^{-n})}.
\]
Then, for every \(n\ge32\),
\[
 \boxed{|p_n-t_n|\le250n^3 2^{-3n}.}
\]
In particular \(p_n\sim n(n-1)2^{-2n}\). With conditional probability \(1-O(n2^{-n})\), a nontrivial automorphism group is exactly \(C_2\), generated by a unique transposition of natural-basis vectors. If \(u_n\) denotes the number of isomorphism classes of perfect \(n\)-dimensional evolution algebras over \(\mathbb F_2\), then
\[
 \boxed{u_n=\frac{|\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)|}{n!}\bigl(1+t_n+O(n^3 2^{-3n})\bigr).}
\]
The implied constants are absolute. These are one asymptotic symmetry law and its orbit-counting consequence for the same family.

## Assumptions and scope

The underlying field is exactly \(\mathbb F_2\). Matrices are independently sampled uniformly from the finite set of **invertible** \(n\times n\) binary matrices; the model is uniform on *labeled structure matrices*, not on algebra isomorphism classes. The algebra is commutative but need not be associative; its natural basis defines the coordinates. Permutations of basis positions are regarded as ordinary unlabeled algebra isomorphisms when counting \(u_n\). Probability statements concern the limit \(n\to\infty\), whereas the displayed error bound holds for every integer \(n\ge32\). Write \(G_n=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)\), \(\kappa=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-2^{-j})^{-1}<4\).

## Proof

**1. Automorphism reduction.** An automorphism has an invertible coordinate matrix \(T\). For \(i\ne j\), the product \(T(e_i)T(e_j)=0\) gives a row-vector of coordinatewise products multiplied by \(M\). Since \(M\) is invertible, the coordinatewise products vanish. Thus the supports of the \(n\) nonzero images \(T(e_i)\) are pairwise disjoint. In an \(n\)-element coordinate set, every support is a singleton; consequently \(T\) is monomial. Because \(\mathbb F_2^\times=\{1\}\), it is a permutation. Comparing the squares shows that a permutation \(\sigma\in S_n\) is an automorphism exactly when
\[
 m_{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)}=m_{ij}\quad(1\le i,j\le n),
\]
which is equivalent to \(MP_\sigma=P_\sigma M\). This identifies \(\operatorname{Aut}(E_M)\) with the simultaneous row-column permutation stabilizer of \(M\).

**2. Exact transposition event.** For \(\tau=(1\ 2)\), put \(N=P_\tau-I\). In characteristic two, \(N^2=0\), \(\operatorname{rank}N=1\). Choose a basis \(u,v,w_1,\ldots,w_{n-2}\) with \(Nv=u\) and \(Nu=Nw_i=0\). An endomorphism \(T\) commuting with \(N\) is uniquely described by
\[
 Tu=a u,\quad Tv=a v+z+c u,\quad Tw=Bw+f(w)u,
\]
where \(a,c\in\mathbb F_2\), \(z\in W=\langle w_i\rangle\), \(f\in W^*\), and \(B\in\operatorname{End}(W)\). It is invertible if and only if \(a=1\) and \(B\in\operatorname{GL}(W)\). Hence
\[
 |C_{G_n}(P_\tau)|=2^{2n-3}|G_{n-2}|.
\]
Using \(|G_n|=2^{n^2}\prod_{j=1}^n(1-2^{-j})\) gives the exact probability
\[
 \Pr(\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(E_M))
 =\frac{2^{-2n+1}}{(1-2^{-(n-1)})(1-2^{-n})}.
\]
Thus \(t_n\) is the sum of these probabilities over the \(\binom n2\) transpositions. The full-matrix (not invertibility-conditioned) probability of the same symmetry is \(2^{-2n+2}\); conditioning on perfection halves its first-order constant.

**3. Bound for permutations with larger support.** If a nonidentity permutation moves exactly \(k\) indices, it fixes \((n-k)^2\) ordered pairs \((i,j)\). The remaining \(2kn-k^2\) pairs belong to orbits of size at least two. Therefore the vector space of binary matrices fixed under simultaneous permutation has codimension at least
\[
 d(k)=kn-\frac{k^2}{2}.
\]
Since \(|G_n|\ge2^{n^2}/\kappa\), the probability that any given such permutation acts is at most \(\kappa2^{-d(k)}\). There are at most \(n^k\) permutations of support \(k\). Put \(T_k=n^k2^{-kn+k^2/2}\). For \(n\ge32\), the ratio \(T_{k+1}/T_k=n2^{-n+k+1/2}<1/2\) when \(3\le k\le n/2\). Hence \(\sum_{3\le k\le n/2}T_k\le2T_3=2^{11/2}n^32^{-3n}\). For \(k>n/2\), use \(T_k\le(n2^{-n/2})^k\), \(n2^{-n/2}\le2^{-n/4}\), and a geometric series to obtain \(\sum_{k>n/2}T_k\le n^32^{-3n}\). Since \(\kappa<4\),
\[
 \Pr(\text{some automorphism moves at least three indices})<188 n^3 2^{-3n}.
\]
To see \(\kappa<4\), note \(\prod_{j\ge1}(1-2^{-j})\ge(1/2)(3/4)(1-\sum_{j\ge3}2^{-j})=9/32>1/4\).

**4. Overlapping transposition events.** If two different transpositions share a point, their product is a 3-cycle; if disjoint, their product moves four points. A matrix fixed by both must be fixed by the product. The bounds from step 3 show that each overlap has probability at most \(4\,2^{-3n+9/2}\) or \(4\,2^{-4n+8}\), respectively. There are at most \(n^3/2\) pairs of the first type and \(n^4/8\) of the second. For \(n\ge32\), their total is below \(47 n^3 2^{-3n}\). The union bound and the first two Bonferroni inequalities thus yield
\[
 |p_n-t_n|\le(188+47)n^3 2^{-3n}<250n^3 2^{-3n}.
\]
Every group not generated by a unique transposition either contains a permutation moving at least three indices or contains two distinct transpositions. The same bounds give a failure probability \(O(n^32^{-3n})\); dividing by \(p_n\sim n(n-1)2^{-2n}\) yields the conditional claim.

**5. Isomorphism-class enumeration.** Over \(\mathbb F_2\), two perfect labeled evolution algebras are isomorphic exactly when their structure matrices are conjugate under the embedded permutation group \(S_n\). Burnside's lemma therefore gives
\[
 u_n=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}|C_{G_n}(P_\sigma)|.
\]
The identity contributes \(|G_n|\), the transpositions contribute \(|G_n|t_n\), and the bound from step 3 makes the remaining contribution \(O(|G_n|n^32^{-3n})\). This proves the final formula.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone Python checker builds all binary matrices in dimensions \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\), filters those of full rank, and directly tests all simultaneous permutation symmetries. It checks the exact transposition centralizer cardinality and its summed incidence count, as well as Burnside integrality of the isomorphism-class counts. It finds \(6\), \(168\), and \(20{,}160\) invertible matrices, respectively; the respective nontrivial-symmetry counts are \(2\), \(24\), and \(1{,}344\). The exact orbit counts are \(4\), \(33\), and \(908\). A separate 17-variable enumeration of the order-five transposition-fixed matrix space confirms exactly \(21{,}504\) invertible matrices. Run `python3 check.py` to reproduce. The finite checks validate specializations; the uniform infinite theorem is established by the proof above, not by experiments.

## Relationship to prior work

The October 2026 evolution-algebra survey of Kaygorodov, Martín González, and Viruel explicitly asks for the asymptotic chance that a random evolution algebra has trivial automorphism group (Question 22). The Costoya–Mayorga–Viruel work on permutation representations establishes monomiality of automorphisms of perfect algebras; that structural fact is also proved directly above. Classical graph-asymmetry results show that most independently sampled directed graphs have no automorphisms, and finite-matrix centralizer theory supplies the background for single-permutation stabilizers. The contribution here is the sharp **invertibility-conditioned** binary symmetry probability, its quantitative concentration on a unique transposition, and the corresponding second-order count of perfect-algebra isomorphism classes. In particular, the factor-of-two difference from the unconditioned transposition probability cannot be read directly from graph asymmetry without treating matrix invertibility.

## Limitations

The field is \(\mathbb F_2\), not a general \(\mathbb F_{2^s}\) or odd-characteristic field; diagonal scalings then need separate treatment. The asymptotic holds for uniform invertible labeled matrices, not for isomorphism-class uniform sampling or sparse random structures. No claim is made for exact finite-dimensional probabilities beyond the stated transposition formula and explicit error bound. The argument combines classical ingredients, so historical priority for an equivalent unstated asymptotic in matrix-orbit enumeration remains a residual risk. Independent mathematical assessment has not occurred.
