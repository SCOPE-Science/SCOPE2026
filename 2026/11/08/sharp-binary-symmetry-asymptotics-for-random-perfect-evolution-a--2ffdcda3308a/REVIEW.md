# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The exact transposition centralizer is obtained from the rank-one square-zero operator \(P_\tau-I\). Every algebra automorphism is a coordinate permutation because the natural-basis support vectors of an invertible evolution algebra are disjoint and the binary field has no nontrivial units. The support-based orbit codimension bound is valid for all permutations and the geometric tail and transposition overlaps yield the advertised numerical error bound for \(n\ge32\). Exhaustive small-dimensional calculations check the matrix model and Burnside counts. The asymptotic does not rely on extrapolation.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual priority risk.** The motivating 2026 survey explicitly asks for the asymptotic probability of trivial automorphisms. Classical graph asymmetry proves a broader qualitative phenomenon without an invertibility condition, while matrix centralizer theory covers ingredients of the fixed-permutation count. The checked primary sources do not give the sharp conditioned coefficient, concentration on one transposition, or refined class count together. An equivalent consequence buried in older matrix-orbit enumerations has not been completely excluded.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A natural random-algebra symmetry question receives a sharp estimate with a quantitative remainder and a description of almost all exceptional automorphism groups. The invertibility condition changes the leading transposition probability by a factor of two, and the orbit formula translates the result into second-order isomorphism-class enumeration. No assertion about other finite fields or the singular case is made.

## Closest literature and limitations

The 2026 survey (arXiv:2610.22248v1, Question 22) motivates the exact statistic. The 2025 Costoya–Mayorga–Viruel paper establishes general monomiality. Erdős–Rényi's random-graph asymmetry and Britnell–Wildon's centralizer theory cover adjacent qualitative and algebraic ingredients, not the verified conditioned statement in their inspected material. The Britnell–Wildon arXiv full text was inspected in its introduction, centralizer-dimension result (Proposition 3.3), and permutation-centralizer section, with a whole-document search for probability and asymptotic statements; none was found, though an unstated implication via the underlying theory remains possible. The model is uniform on labeled invertible binary matrices; the results do not generalize automatically to higher finite fields or sparse matrices.
