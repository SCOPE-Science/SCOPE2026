# Scientific review: centroid capture under boundary sampling

## Correctness: PASS

Affine invariance reduces edge-conditioned containment to two exactly computable integrals. The two-on-one-side integral yields \(1/9+4\log2/27\), and the one-on-each-side integral yields \(2\log2/3\); the exhaustive multinomial partition gives the claimed symmetric polynomial. Triangle inequalities on normalized side lengths and positive coefficients prove the sharp range. Pairwise averaging proves strict Schur concavity. Symbolic integration and independent Euclidean Monte Carlo reproduce the critical computations. Boundary degeneracies occur with probability zero, and the lower endpoint is correctly labeled a nonattained infimum.

## Originality: PASS (provisional historical priority)

Prékopa (1972), Section 5, derives the equilateral or interior-uniform centroid-capture value \(2/27+20\log2/81\); this number is explicitly not claimed as new. The earlier single-point-per-side centroid probability \(2\log2/3\) is likewise credited. Ionascu (2016/2019) studies interior-uniform points and a radial projection law, not the arclength-weighted three-edge law of arbitrary triangles. Wendel (1962) requires centrally symmetric sampling; a triangle perimeter distribution lacks that hypothesis. Published regular-polygon vertex conditional-area calculations concern discrete vertex samples rather than continuous boundary capture. Primary readable texts and targeted mathematical-record searches showed no full implication covering the arbitrary perimeter weights, its strict shape majorization, and sharp lower endpoint. An inaccessible full text (Halász--Kleitman 1974), and possible older weighted-boundary absorption formulas, remain priority risks; independent literature certification has not occurred.

## Value: PASS

The question isolates a natural geometric choice hidden by affine-invariant interior sampling: physical arclength weighting of the boundary biases the edges differently. Its exact formula gives a complete two-parameter shape law and a strict majorization principle, identifying the maximizing triangle and the previously unreported sharp nonattained lower limit. The weighted-edge extension clarifies precisely which part of the classical shape independence survives under a different boundary measure. These distinctions are structural and go beyond a single isolated numerical probability.

## Limitations

Only nondegenerate triangular boundaries and centroid containment are treated; other points, polygons, and nonuniform within-edge densities are outside scope. The already known equilateral and one-per-side constants are not independent novelty claims. No independent verification or exhaustive historical-priority certification has been performed.
