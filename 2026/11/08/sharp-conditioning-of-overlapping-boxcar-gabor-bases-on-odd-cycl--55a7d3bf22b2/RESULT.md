# Sharp conditioning of overlapping boxcar Gabor bases on odd cyclic fibers

## Finding

For integers \(a\ge 1\) and odd \(b\ge 3\), write \(N=ab\). On \(\mathbb C^N\), consider the critical (square) finite Gabor system with translation step \(a\), modulation step \(b\), and the consecutive-boxcar window of length \(r\). Among **all overlapping lengths** \(a<r\le N\) for which this Gabor system is a basis, the sharp ratio of optimal upper and lower frame bounds is
\[
\inf_{\substack{a<r\le ab\\\mathcal G_r\text{ a basis}}}\frac{B_r}{A_r}
=\csc^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2b}\right).
\]
Equality occurs **exactly** at the \(a\) lengths \(a+1\le r\le 2a\); there are no other extremizers. At each optimal length the exact (unnormalized-window) bounds are
\[
A_r=4a\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2b}\right),\qquad B_r=4a.
\]
The minimal spectral condition number is \(4b^2/\pi^2+1/3+O(b^{-2})\) as odd \(b\to\infty\). The nonoverlapping length \(r=a\) is a tight basis, but is excluded from the overlapping design problem.

More generally, for **every** window length \(1\le r\le N\), this theorem gives the entire exact frame-operator spectrum, and hence the exact basis criterion, as in the proof below.

## Assumptions and scope

Identify indices with \(\mathbb Z_N\), give \(\mathbb C^N\) its standard Hermitian norm, and set
\[
(T_tf)(x)=f(x-t),\qquad (M_sf)(x)=e^{2\pi i sx/N}f(x).
\]
The window \(g_r\) is \(1\) at residues \(0,1,\dots,r-1\) and \(0\) elsewhere. The measured Gabor family is
\[
\mathcal G_r=\{M_{b\ell}T_{ak}g_r:\ 0\le\ell<a,\ 0\le k<b\}.
\]
Thus the number of vectors equals the ambient dimension \(N=ab\). The frame bounds are the smallest and largest eigenvalues of its frame operator; the lower bound is positive exactly when the family is a basis. The optimization includes only integer lengths with \(r>a\), so every translated boxcar strictly overlaps another translated boxcar in at least one coordinate. The parity restriction that \(b\) be odd is essential to this particular exact formula.

## Proof

**1. Complete spectral reduction.** Write \(r=qa+s\) with \(0\le s<a\), and, for \(0\le u<a\), put \(q_u=q+1\) when \(u<s\), and \(q_u=q\) otherwise. For any index \(x=u+at\), the \(a\) modulation characters satisfy
\[
e^{2\pi i b\ell(u+at)/N}=e^{2\pi i\ell u/a}.
\]
An \(a\)-point unitary Fourier transform in the modulation label therefore isolates the residue class \(u\). On the resulting \(b\)-coordinate fiber, the \(b\) translated columns form a circulant matrix generated by \(q_u\) consecutive ones (possibly after a cyclic shift), multiplied by \(\sqrt a\). A \(b\)-point unitary Fourier transform diagonalizes that circulant matrix. Consequently the full multiset of eigenvalues of the Gabor frame operator is
\[
\boxed{\ \lambda_{u,j}
=a\left|D_{q_u}(\omega_b^j)\right|^2,\qquad
D_h(z)=\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}z^t,\quad
\omega_b=e^{2\pi i/b},\quad
0\le u<a,\ 0\le j<b.\ }
\]
The empty sum \(D_0\) is zero. Since \(0\le q_u\le b\), the geometric sum is nonzero on every \(b\)-th root of unity exactly when \(\gcd(q_u,b)=1\). Hence
\[
\mathcal G_r\text{ is a basis}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\gcd(q_u,b)=1\text{ for every }0\le u<a.
\]
The \(j=0\) eigenvalue of each fiber is \(a q_u^2\), while the remaining eigenvalues are \(a\sin^2(\pi q_uj/b)/\sin^2(\pi j/b)\).

**2. Sampled Dirichlet-kernel inequality.** For an odd integer \(b\ge3\), let \(\alpha=\pi/(2b)\). Whenever \(3\le h<b\) and \(\gcd(h,b)=1\), we claim
\[
\boxed{\ \min_{1\le j<b}|D_h(\omega_b^j)|<h\sin\alpha.\ }
\]
For even \(h\ge4\), take \(j=(b-1)/2\). The geometric-sum identity gives
\[
|D_h(\omega_b^j)|=\frac{\sin(h\alpha)}{\cos\alpha}.
\]
Since \(h\alpha<\pi/2\), strict decrease of \(x\mapsto\sin x/x\) on \((0,\pi)\) implies
\[
\frac{\sin(h\alpha)}h<\frac{\sin(2\alpha)}2=\sin\alpha\cos\alpha,
\]
which proves the inequality in this case.

For odd \(h\ge3\), let \(k=(h-1)/2\), and choose an integer \(j\) closest to \(bk/h\). Coprimality and oddness imply
\[
\left|j-\frac{bk}{h}\right|\le\frac{h-1}{2h}.
\]
As \(b\ge h+2\), this integer lies between \(1\) and \(b-1\). Set \(x=\pi j/b\), and put
\[
\delta=\frac{(h-1)\alpha}{h},\qquad
E=\frac\pi{2h}+\delta.
\]
The closeness estimate yields \(|\sin(hx)|\le\sin((h-1)\alpha)\) and \(\sin x\ge\cos E\). We claim \(\cos E\ge1-1/h\). For \(h\ge5\), \(E<\pi/h\) and
\[
\cos E\ge1-\frac{E^2}2>1-\frac{\pi^2}{2h^2}\ge1-\frac1h,
\]
where the last step uses \(h\ge5>\pi^2/2\). For \(h=3\), \(b\ge5\) gives \(E\le7\pi/30\), and the bound \(\cos E\ge1-E^2/2>2/3\) is immediate. Thus strict subadditivity \(\sin((h-1)\alpha)<(h-1)\sin\alpha\) on the relevant positive interval shows
\[
|D_h(\omega_b^j)|
\le\frac{\sin((h-1)\alpha)}{\cos E}
<\frac{(h-1)\sin\alpha}{1-1/h}
=h\sin\alpha.
\]
This proves the claim for both parities.

**3. Sharp minimax and equality cases.** The case \(h=2\) has precisely
\[
\max_{0\le j<b}|D_2(\omega_b^j)|=2,
\qquad\min_{0\le j<b}|D_2(\omega_b^j)|=2\sin\alpha,
\]
using oddness of \(b\). Every basis at an overlapping length has some fiber with \(q_u\ge2\). If any \(q_u\ge3\), the strict inequality in Step 2, together with the \(j=0\) fiber eigenvalue \(a q_u^2\), forces
\[
\frac{B_r}{A_r}>
\frac{q_u^2}{q_u^2\sin^2\alpha}=
\csc^2\alpha.
\]
If instead all fiber lengths satisfy \(q_u\le2\), the overlap condition implies \(a+1\le r\le2a\). At each such length, at least one fiber has length \(2\) and every other fiber has length \(1\) or \(2\). As \(2\sin\alpha\le1\) for odd \(b\ge3\), the exact spectral extremes of all fibers are \(4a\sin^2\alpha\) and \(4a\). Hence all and only the specified lengths minimize the condition number.

Finally, Taylor expansion of \(\csc^2(\pi/(2b))\) gives the stated large-\(b\) asymptotic. Every implication above holds for all stated integer parameters, with no asymptotic hypotheses needed for the classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Asipchuk, De Carli and Rodriguez establish finite-Gabor block equivalence for subgroup time-frequency lattices; their general block-reduction theorem provides context for Step 1 but does not supply the global all-length optimization, the sharp odd-fiber constant, or the complete equality classification here. Earlier work on the discrete Gabor and Zak transforms treats spectral diagonalization, which is an ingredient rather than the optimized statement. Faulhuber and Shafkulovska study continuous Gabor frame-bound optimization with different windows and lattice conditions. This finding is a quantitative optimization **within** the critical finite cyclic subgroup setting, rather than a claim of novelty for Zak diagonalization itself.

## Limitations

The sharp formula is proved for odd fiber size \(b\), the unweighted boxcar window, and the specified square cyclic lattice. It does not optimize over arbitrary weights, other window shapes, noncritical lattices, or even fiber sizes. The nonoverlapping support case is intentionally excluded. No assertion is made about uniqueness among general, non-boxcar windows. The possible existence of an equivalent older optimized statement under alternative terminology remains a bibliographic risk; independent literature-priority validation has not been performed.
