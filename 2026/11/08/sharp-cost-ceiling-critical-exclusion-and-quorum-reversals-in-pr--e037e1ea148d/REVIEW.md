# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact multinomial sum isolates the single pivotal composition and reduces the optimization to a risk-weighted binomial point mass, with one unique maximum. A compact ratio-invariant slice excludes that boundary maximum for every trajectory containing ordinary cooperation, yielding exponential protection elimination even at equality; the asymptotically autonomous scalar edge argument proves convergence. The adjacent-quorum ratio and its strict crossing follow from a monotone elementary-power sequence and a decreasing risk factor. The stated numerical witness is exact rational arithmetic. The attached checker independently verified 13,680 finite compositions plus optimizer and crossing checks; the analytical infinite-domain arguments do not rely on these finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Li et al. (arXiv:2609.28132, sections 2–4 and appendices A–C) give the underlying risk-weighted pivotal payoff and numerically distinguish stability mechanisms, but not the exact global relative-payoff cost ceiling, critical-exclusion conclusion, or unique adjacent-quorum risk crossover. Nöldeke–Peña (2020, sections 2–3) already establish the binomial pivotal maximum in a different two-action threshold participation game, so that component is explicitly prior knowledge. Standard dominance elimination is also prior knowledge. The new claim lies in the integrated three-strategy risk-dependent ceiling, its weak-boundary extinction, and sharp nonmonotone quorum comparison. Searches of research records and primary literature did not identify covering formulas, but an unindexed equivalent is possible.

## Value — PASS

Quorum-severity effects are a central parameter question in the motivating risk-protection model, whose reported numerical stability-threshold comparisons are not a general monotonicity theorem. The proven critical cost gives an absolute, easily computable impossibility test for protection's relative success without solving a nonlinear equilibrium system. The strict adjacent-quorum reversal proves that although quorum two is globally best, raising an already-high quorum can restore a positive incentive under high collective risk. Equality-case exclusion, finite-group exact thresholds and distinct extensive-versus-fixed-quorum large-group scales make the test useful as an analytic benchmark rather than a numerical restatement of published examples.

## Boundaries and sources

The threshold does not characterize protective coexistence or stability below the ceiling. The new finding is deterministic and model-specific; the 2020 binomial mode and classical dominance principle are cited as prior facts.
