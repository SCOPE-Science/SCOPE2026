# Scientific review: sharp cubic-gap stability for periodic atomic mean constraints

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is reconstructed from the centered Green diagonal for the periodic derivative-energy Hilbert space, with \(N\ge3\), positive mass \(S\), unit arclength, and equal weights \(S/N\). The distributional identity \(r''=2(\delta_0-dt)\) implies \(V''=-2\) between atoms. Gap integration gives both the exact midpoint-potential identity and the exact \(L^2\) derivative identity; substituting these into the Green diagonal proves the displayed three-term deficit with no limiting argument. The cyclic gap-cubic identity and strict convexity prove uniqueness up to rotation. Equality functions follow from Hilbert-space Riesz representation. All 4,410 independent exact-rational pointwise variational checks passed in the bundled checker; these checks support but do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The basic resistance Green identities are established by Baker–Faber (arXiv:math/0407428), and Solomyak's general metric-graph work (arXiv:math/0206235) already treats Sobolev approximation. Hardin–Kendall–Saff (arXiv:1208.5261), Theorem 1, **already implies the isolated point-potential minimax equal-spacing principle** for a constant minus \(r\); that principle is not claimed as new. The classical pair-potential energy optimization is also prior groundwork. The distinct supported finding is the exact all-configurations combined Sobolev embedding identity, with three separately nonnegative quantitative terms, the explicit \(N,S\)-dependent sharp constant, and the equality-function classification. Santin (arXiv:2210.15959) concerns Brownian-bridge interpolation power functions on an interval, not periodic mean constraints. Searches of publication records and accessible earlier claims found no equivalent quantitative combined identity. Unindexed periodic quadrature and reproducing-kernel work may nevertheless contain it; no independence or priority guarantee is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Site placement changes the mean-zero constraint itself, so this is a natural sharp embedding design problem, not merely a change of variables or a coefficient in a known table. The exact optimal constant and uniform gap-cubic stability quantitatively control loss from unequal spacing for all atomic masses and all finite site counts. The additional midpoint-defect and slope-square terms explain separately why irregular placements fail to saturate the optimum. The finite site count is arbitrary and the result identifies every norming point in the optimal placement. Applications outside equal masses and first-order periodic energy are not claimed.
