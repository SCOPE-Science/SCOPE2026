# Scientific review: matching-deleted biclique anti-coordination

## Correctness — PASS
The model is explicitly the finite unit-weight two-action anti-coordination game on \(K_{n,n}\) with \(t\) disjoint cross-edges removed, with \(n\ge2\), \(1\le t\le n\), and indifferent individual deviations permitted. The exact Nash inequalities are \(\sigma_i(Y-\mathbf1_{i\le t}\tau_i)\le0\) and \(\tau_i(X-\mathbf1_{i\le t}\sigma_i)\le0\). If a magnetization has magnitude at least two, these inequalities force one of the two opposite-uniform profiles. Otherwise even \(n\) forces both magnetizations to zero, with equality across all deleted pairs. Odd \(n\) forces each magnetization to \(\pm1\); the four sign combinations split into precisely the stated equal-sign and cross-sign classes. The crossed-edge count is \((n^2-XY)/2\) minus the number of unequal deleted pairs. These identities prove the stated equilibrium-count and welfare formulas for every admissible parameter, including tie and parity boundaries. Independently implemented exhaustive enumeration of 757,296 profiles across 42 graph cases \(2\le n\le8\), \(0\le t\le n\), confirmed both individual Nash conditions and aggregate cut histograms. Finite enumerations support but do not replace the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (with residual priority risk)
The existing complete-bipartite baseline \(t=0\), the max-cut game/stable-cut equivalence, and the generic half-welfare bound are known and are not represented as novel. Primary full-text inspections included Qiu et al., *Networked Anti-Coordination Games Meet Graphical Dynamical Systems* (arXiv:2301.02889), Deligkas et al., *Some coordination problems are harder than others* (arXiv:2311.03195), Lampis, *Minimum Stable Cut and Treewidth* (arXiv:2104.13097), and Vanelli et al., *On games with coordinating and anti-coordinating agents* (arXiv:1912.02000). The papers develop general equilibria/dynamics, welfare complexity, stable-cut complexity, and complete-interaction counting respectively, but the inspected material does not supply an exact \(K_{n,n}-M_t\) labeled Nash census or the odd-size matching-deletion threshold. Targeted research-record searches and searches for crown-graph stable cuts, local max-cuts, missing-matching equilibria, and majority aliases found related crown-graph coloring/domination facts, not a theorem that dominates this result. Accessible prior claims were compared as implications: the previously recorded balanced complete multipartite equilibrium census handles \(t=0\) but not deleted cross-edges. Other research-record hits count non-CIS pairs or odd colorings, which are inequivalent invariants. Absence of a located covering theorem does not prove global novelty, and an unindexed equivalent remains possible.

## Value — PASS
Removing a matching is the smallest controlled, degree-heterogeneous perturbation of complete-bipartite interaction, directly relevant to fragility of efficient stable cuts under deleted social contacts. The full \((n,t)\) equilibrium count, welfare spectrum, and abrupt odd-population threshold are neither routine enumerations from the complete-bipartite case nor small numerical refinements of the generic one-half bound. The sharp transition from every equilibrium optimal to inefficient equilibria after a single additional matching-edge deletion is a meaningful structural limit for decentralized anti-coordination. The result is explicitly limited to homogeneous, static, unweighted binary games, and no claims are made about arbitrary deletions or stochastic processes.

## Closest literature and limitations
The complete-bipartite case and general max-cut/stable-cut equivalence are established background. Qiu et al. analyze anti-coordination updates, Deligkas et al. general welfare computation, Lampis stable-cut complexity, and Vanelli et al. complete-interaction networks. The exact formula is new only relative to the directly inspected and searchable evidence; publication priority remains subject to unindexed prior work. The exhaustive verifier covers graph sizes through eight vertices per side; the all-sizes claim rests on the written parity/magnetization proof. Independent external audit has not occurred.
