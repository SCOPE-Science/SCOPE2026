Deleting disjoint cross-ties from a complete bipartite anti-coordination network has an exact, parity-dependent effect on every pure Nash equilibrium. For odd \(n\), removing fewer than \((n+1)/2\) matching edges preserves universal efficiency, but removing exactly \((n+1)/2\) already creates inefficient equilibria whose welfare is nearly half the optimum; closed formulas classify and count all equilibria for every matching-deletion size.
