# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The full four-row observation matrix has an exact parity reduction to a positive \(2\times2\) Gram block and an independent odd scalar. The smaller even eigenvalue is strictly increasing in squared half-angle cosine, while the odd scalar is strictly decreasing, proving a unique global maximin crossing. The quadratic and endpoint asymptotics follow algebraically. Direct construction tests the eigenvalues independently; the companion checker passed 1695 assertions. No numerical grid or plot was used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
The earliest focal source, Aldroubi et al. (2020), develops heat dynamical sampling and stability bounds but does not present this exact four-row, three-mode angular E-optimal design in its inspected full preprint. Huang–Needell–Tang (2021) treats randomized graph sampling, a different sampling optimization. The published two-sensor cycle result on spectrally demixed graph frames does not imply the analysis of the raw two-time measurements because their retained temporal cross terms are essential to the new formula. The 2009 Lu–Vetterli diffusion trade-off abstract is highly relevant, but its full text was inaccessible: priority remains a residual risk rather than a certified absence. This work does not claim to originate the space–time sampling paradigm or standard block diagonalization.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is the smallest complex periodic Fourier heat band with a nontrivial conjugate-frequency eigenvalue degeneracy, requiring two spatial sites. It gives a complete, sharp design at the minimum two-snapshot setting. The explicit dependence of the uniquely optimal sensor separation on diffusion time, with opposite-angle limits and a quadratic coincident-time stability collapse, is structural information useful for sensor design rather than an isolated numerical parameter. The result does not extend automatically to higher bands, other noise models, or different time grids.
