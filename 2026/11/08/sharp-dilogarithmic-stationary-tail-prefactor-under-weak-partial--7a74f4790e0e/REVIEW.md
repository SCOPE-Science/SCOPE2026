# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact stationary independent-Laplace representation is a stated and verified premise from Tal-Friedman–Roichman–Reuveni, arXiv:2205.03363. For its geometric product, Euler–Maclaurin gives the dilogarithm and the endpoint normalization. Strict positive saddle curvature follows from the power series. The independent-factor characteristic-function inequality provides the integrable Fourier majorant needed for a uniform tilted *local* central limit theorem, not merely weak convergence. Exact exponential tilting and dominated convergence yield the complete \(\sqrt h\) tail prefactor. The four proof steps specify every domain and the fixed-positive-displacement scope. A standalone checker passed 48 deterministic tests; tests do not substitute for analytic steps.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The model, stationary infinite sum, moment generating product, variance and Gaussian crossover are already present in Tal-Friedman, Roichman and Reuveni (2022). Vysotsky (2021) develops generic rate-level results for weighted sums and Saulis–Deltuvienė (2008) develop normal/moderate deviation laws for geometrically discounted sums, but the inspected portions do not cover the stated fixed-positive-distance sharp prefactor and dilogarithmic rate together. Aurzada (2019) has nonnegative stretched-exponential assumptions that exclude this centered Laplace example. Targeted literature and database searches found no equivalent complete theorem, but an unlocated general sharp triangular-array result could implicitly cover it. Priority is not established by absence of matches.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The model's known Gaussian limiting shape is insufficient to predict probabilities of displacements of order \(1/h\) as reset contraction vanishes. The exact nonlinear rate and the model-specific endpoint prefactor quantify experimentally relevant extremes beyond second moments and distinguish Gaussian-core and exponential-tail behavior. This is a motivated asymptotic question on the same physical observable, not an arbitrary moment or a routine normalization identity.

## Limitations and risks

The asymptotic is uniform only on fixed compact subsets of positive scaled displacement. The prefactor has not been independently audited. Existing broad strong-large-deviation theory may yield an implicit version; a full priority search is inherently incomplete.
