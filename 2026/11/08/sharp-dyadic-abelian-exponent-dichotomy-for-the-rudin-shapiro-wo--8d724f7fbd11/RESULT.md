# Sharp dyadic Abelian exponent dichotomy for the Rudin–Shapiro word

## Finding
Let \(r(n)\in\{-1,+1\}\) be the classical Rudin–Shapiro sequence, \(r(n)=(-1)^{u(n)}\), where \(u(n)\) is the number of overlapping adjacent occurrences of `11` in the base-two expansion of \(n\). An Abelian \(e\)-power of block length \(L\) is a concatenation of \(e\) consecutive length-\(L\) blocks having the same number of occurrences of each sign. Define
\[
E(k)=\max\{e\ge1:\text{an Abelian }e\text{-power of block length }2^k\text{ occurs in }r\}.
\]
For **every integer \(k\ge0\)**, this maximum exists and equals
\[
\boxed{E(k)=\begin{cases}4,&k\text{ even},\\3,&k\text{ odd}.\end{cases}}
\]
More precisely, for even \(k\), an Abelian fourth power begins at \(7\cdot2^k\), and no Abelian fifth power of block length \(2^k\) occurs anywhere. For odd \(k\), an Abelian cube begins at \(9\cdot2^k+2^{k-1}\), and no Abelian fourth power of that block length occurs anywhere. These statements concern only **power-of-two block lengths**, not all possible Abelian periods.

## Assumptions and scope
Indices begin at zero and factors are contiguous. Signed sums determine Abelian equivalence of equal-length sign words: the count of \(+1\) is one-half the sum of length and signed sum. All absence statements quantify over every starting position \(i\ge0\) in the one-sided infinite word. The infinite precursor word is the fixed point beginning in `a` of
\[
\sigma(a)=ab,\quad \sigma(b)=ac,\quad\sigma(c)=db,\quad\sigma(d)=dc,
\]
with coding \(a,b\mapsto+1\), \(c,d\mapsto-1\). This is the same \(r\) as the adjacent-`11` definition.

## Proof
**1. Aligned block sums.** Fix \(k\ge1\), let \(L=2^k\), and put \(x_q=r(q)\). For \(0\le j<L\), the binary digits of \(qL+j\) consist of those of \(q\) followed by \(k\) digits of \(j\). Exactly one new adjacency can cross the junction. Therefore
\[
r(qL+j)=x_q r(j)(-1)^{(q\bmod2)\lfloor j/2^{k-1}\rfloor}.
\]
Let \(P_k=\sum_{j<L}r(j)\) and \(Q_k=\sum_{j<L}(-1)^{\lfloor j/2^{k-1}\rfloor}r(j)\). Set \((P_0,Q_0)=(1,1)\). Splitting the next dyadic block in halves yields
\[
(P_{k+1},Q_{k+1})=(P_k+Q_k,P_k-Q_k).
\]
Thus \((P_{2s},Q_{2s})=(2^s,2^s)\) and \((P_{2s+1},Q_{2s+1})=(2^{s+1},0)\). Put \(H=P_k>0\). The signed sum \(B_q\) of the aligned length-\(L\) block is \(Hx_q\) when \(k\) is even, and \(Hx_q\) for even \(q\), zero for odd \(q\), when \(k\) is odd.

**2. Arbitrarily positioned sums.** Write any starting position as \(i=qL+t\), where \(q\ge0\) and \(0\le t<L\). Define \(P(t)=\sum_{j<t}r(j)\), \(Q(t)=\sum_{j<t}(-1)^{\lfloor j/2^{k-1}\rfloor}r(j)\) for \(k\ge1\). The sum of the \(j\)-th of consecutive length-\(L\) blocks starting at \(i\) is exactly
\[
S_j=B_{q+j}-x_{q+j}C_{(q+j)\bmod2}(t)+x_{q+j+1}C_{(q+j+1)\bmod2}(t),
\]
where \(C_0=P\) and \(C_1=Q\). For \(t<L/2\), \(P(t)=Q(t)\). For \(t\ge L/2\), the first half cancels in the difference, giving \(P(t)+Q(t)=2P_{k-1}\). Dividing by \(H\), the only possible normalized coefficient pairs \((p,q)=(P(t)/H,Q(t)/H)\) belong to
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
& t<L/2&t\ge L/2\\\hline
k\text{ odd}&p=q&p+q=1\\
k\text{ even}&p=q&p+q=2.
\end{array}
\]
For \(k=0\), only \(t=0\) exists, and the relevant coefficients are \(p=q=0\); the even-case calculation below includes this case.

**3. Complete six-letter factor certificate.** Iterate the displayed four-letter substitution seven times on `a`, obtaining a word of length \(128\). Its set \(F\) of length-six factors has exactly \(40\) elements and is closed under the operation \(F\mapsto\bigcup_{u\in F}\operatorname{Fact}_6(\sigma(u))\): the resulting set is precisely \(F\). Every length-six factor of \(\sigma(v)\), for any finite \(v\) of length at least six, is contained in \(\sigma(u)\) for some length-six factor \(u\) of \(v\). Induction and the prefix property of the fixed point therefore show that \(F\) is the complete length-six factor language of the infinite precursor. Coding gives exactly \(36\) possible sign patterns of length six. The complete words are in `CERTIFICATE.json` and are regenerated by `verify.py`.

**4. A finite exact obstruction, not a finite-prefix assumption.** For a sign pattern \((x_0,\ldots,x_5)\), starting aligned-block parity \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\), and normalized coefficients \(p,q\), divide the formula for \(S_j\) by \(H\). In the odd-\(k\) case the aligned contribution is \(x_j\) for even \(\epsilon+j\) and zero otherwise; in the even-\(k\) case it is always \(x_j\). The coefficient removed from \(x_j\) is \(p\) at even \(\epsilon+j\) and \(q\) otherwise, with the opposite coefficient added to \(x_{j+1}\).

For the odd case impose \(S_0=S_1=S_2=S_3\). For the even case impose \(S_0=S_1=\cdots=S_4\). On each of the two allowed coefficient lines from Step 2, these are elementary linear equations in one real unknown. Exhaustive **exact rational** elimination over all \(36\) sign patterns, both parities, and both coefficient lines has **no solution**. The independently inspectable elimination program `verify.py` evaluates the linear functions at \(0\) and \(1\), computes every candidate common root as an exact rational fraction, and checks all equations. The four mode counts (each \(72\) cases) and their contradiction categories are recorded in `CERTIFICATE.json`. The full obstruction consists of \(288\) finite one-variable systems; the saturation argument in Step 3 makes it exhaustive for the infinite word. Consequently, odd \(k\) permits no Abelian fourth power and even \(k\) permits no Abelian fifth power.

**5. Sharp examples.** The signs \((r(7),r(8),r(9),r(10))\) are \((+1,+1,+1,+1)\). For every even \(k\), the aligned consecutive blocks starting at \(7L\) therefore all have signed sum \(H\), giving an Abelian fourth power. For odd \(k\), take \(q=9\), \(t=L/2\). The signs \((r(9),r(10),r(11),r(12))\) are \((+1,+1,-1,-1)\), and \(P(t)=Q(t)=H/2\). Substitution in the formula of Step 2 gives three consecutive signed sums equal to zero, hence an Abelian cube. This proves the displayed dichotomy.

## Relationship to prior work
Lü, Chen, Wen and Wu (2016) determine the Rudin–Shapiro Abelian-complexity function, using extrema of **single-block signed sums**; Lü and Han (2022) further analyze its growth and extremal scales. Those statements concern counts of possible Parikh vectors at a fixed factor length, whereas this theorem classifies the **maximum length of consecutive runs of equal Parikh vectors**, a correlation requiring joint information about neighboring factors. The 2022 survey of Fici and Puzynina describes Abelian powers in several other sequences and the broader theory; no formula covering the present dyadic Rudin–Shapiro exponent dichotomy was located in the inspected sections. This relationship does not amount to a proof of bibliographic uniqueness.

## Limitations
Only integer exponents and exact powers-of-two Abelian block lengths are addressed. Neither the maximal exponents at non-dyadic periods nor exact densities of starting positions are determined here. The mathematical proof is complete conditional on the auditable finite language and rational-elimination certificate; an independently authored scientific review has not been performed. Unindexed equivalent prior literature remains a bibliographic risk.
