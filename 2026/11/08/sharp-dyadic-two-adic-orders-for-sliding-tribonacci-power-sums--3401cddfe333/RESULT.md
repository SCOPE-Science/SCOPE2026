# Sharp dyadic two-adic orders for sliding Tribonacci power sums

## Finding
Let \(T_0=T_1=0\), \(T_2=1\), and \(T_{n+3}=T_{n+2}+T_{n+1}+T_n\) for every \(n\ge0\). For each integer exponent \(e\ge3\) and dyadic length \(2^r\), \(r\ge1\), define
\[
F_{e,r}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^r-1}T_{n+j}^{e},\qquad
G_e(r)=\gcd\{F_{e,r}(n):n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\}.
\]
The gcd denotes the positive common divisor of the entire set (equivalently, the nonnegative generator of the ideal in \(\mathbb Z\) generated by all the sums). The exact two-adic order is
\[
\boxed{\nu_2(G_e(r))=\begin{cases}
0,&r=1,\\
1,&r=2,\\
r-2,&r\ge3\text{ and }e\text{ odd},\\
r-1,&r\ge3\text{ and }e\text{ even}.
\end{cases}}
\]
In fact, for every even \(e\ge4\), every \(r\ge3\), and every \(n\ge0\), the stronger uniform statement holds:
\[
F_{e,r}(n)\equiv2^{r-1}\pmod{2^r}.
\]
For every odd \(e\ge3\), every \(r\ge3\), and every \(n\ge0\), one has \(2^{r-2}\mid F_{e,r}(n)\), with sharpness witnessed by the initial window:
\[
F_{e,r}(0)\equiv2^{r-2}\pmod{2^{r-1}}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Indices are nonnegative; exponent \(e\) is an arbitrary integer at least three (not restricted by parity or size); \(r\) is an arbitrary positive integer. We study only the exact power of \(2\) dividing the gcd, not its odd part. The sequence uses the normalization \(T_0=T_1=0,T_2=1\); sources using \(0,1,1\) are shifted by one, which does not alter the all-start sliding-window gcd. For integer \(x\ne0\), \(\nu_2(x)\) is the largest integer \(v\ge0\) such that \(2^v\mid x\). The expressions are sums of positive powers and hence the gcd is positive.

## Proof
**1. Four-step periodicity and high-power congruences.** Write
\[
X_n=(T_n,T_{n+1},T_{n+2})^{\mathsf T},\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\1&1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad X_{n+1}=AX_n.
\]
Direct integer matrix multiplication gives
\[
A^4=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2\\2&3&4\\4&6&7\end{pmatrix}
=I+2\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&1&2\\2&3&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently \(A^4\equiv I\pmod2\). Squaring repeatedly shows that for each \(r\ge2\) there is an integer matrix \(B_r\) with
\[
A^{2^r}=I+2^{r-1}B_r.
\]
The assertion starts at \(r=2\); if it holds for \(r\), its square is
\(I+2^r(B_r+2^{r-2}B_r^2)\), which is of the required form at \(r+1\). It follows that
\[
T_{j+2^r}=T_j+2^{r-1}d_{r,j},
\]
where \(d_{r,j}\in\mathbb Z\) is a fixed integer-linear form in the components of \(X_j\). Therefore, for each fixed \(r\), both \(T_j\bmod2\) and \(d_{r,j}\bmod2\) are periodic in \(j\) with period dividing four. The first sequence begins \(0,0,1,1\).

**2. Dyadic doubling of power-window sums.** For \(r\ge3\), split the window into halves:
\[
F_{e,r+1}(n)=2F_{e,r}(n)+R_{e,r}(n),
\]
where
\[
R_{e,r}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^r-1}\bigl(T_{n+j+2^r}^{e}-T_{n+j}^{e}\bigr).
\]
Substitute \(T_{h+2^r}=T_h+2^{r-1}d_{r,h}\) and expand by the binomial theorem. Every term of degree at least two in \(2^{r-1}d_{r,h}\) is divisible by \(2^{2r-2}\), hence by \(2^{r+1}\) for \(r\ge3\). Thus
\[
R_{e,r}(n)\equiv e2^{r-1}
\sum_{j=0}^{2^r-1}T_{n+j}^{e-1}d_{r,n+j}\pmod{2^{r+1}}.
\]
The factor \(T_{n+j}^{e-1}d_{r,n+j}\bmod2\) has period dividing four. Since \(2^r\) consists of the even number \(2^{r-2}\) of four-term periods for \(r\ge3\), this displayed sum is even. For odd \(e\), consequently
\[
R_{e,r}(n)\equiv0\pmod{2^r}.
\]
For even \(e\), the additional factor \(2\mid e\) yields the stronger
\[
R_{e,r}(n)\equiv0\pmod{2^{r+1}}.
\]
Both assertions hold for every starting index \(n\), all indicated exponents, and all \(r\ge3\).

**3. Base length eight.** Every block of eight consecutive Tribonacci terms contains exactly four odd entries, by the binary period four. For even \(e\ge4\), every odd \(x\) satisfies \(x^e\equiv1\pmod8\), and every even \(x\) satisfies \(x^e\equiv0\pmod8\). Hence, for every \(n\),
\[
F_{e,3}(n)\equiv4\pmod8.
\]
For odd \(e\ge3\), the same parity count makes every \(F_{e,3}(n)\) even. At the initial index the eight terms are \(0,0,1,1,2,4,7,13\); even terms raised to \(e\ge3\) vanish modulo four, while \(x^e\equiv x\pmod4\) for odd \(e\). Therefore
\[
F_{e,3}(0)\equiv1+1+7+13\equiv2\pmod4.
\]

**4. Complete induction and gcd sharpness.** For even \(e\ge4\), if \(F_{e,r}(n)\equiv2^{r-1}\pmod{2^r}\) holds for every \(n\), the doubling identity with \(2^{r+1}\mid R_{e,r}(n)\) yields \(F_{e,r+1}(n)\equiv2^r\pmod{2^{r+1}}\). The base case in step 3 gives the result for all \(r\ge3\), uniformly in \(n\). Hence the gcd has two-adic valuation exactly \(r-1\).

For odd \(e\ge3\), the base case shows every length-eight sum is divisible by \(2=2^{3-2}\) and the initial sum is not divisible by four. The doubling identity with \(2^r\mid R_{e,r}(n)\) sends divisibility by \(2^{r-2}\) to divisibility by \(2^{r-1}\) for every \(n\), and sends the initial congruence \(F_{e,r}(0)\equiv2^{r-2}\pmod{2^{r-1}}\) to \(F_{e,r+1}(0)\equiv2^{r-1}\pmod{2^r}\). This proves exact gcd order \(r-2\).

Finally, for \(r=2\), any four consecutive terms contain exactly two odd terms, so all four-term power sums are even; \(F_{e,2}(0)=T_0^e+\cdots+T_3^e=2\), giving order one. For \(r=1\), \(F_{e,1}(1)=T_1^e+T_2^e=1\), so the gcd equals one. All cases are proved.

## Verification
The companion `checker.py` independently computes the Tribonacci recurrence modulo \(2^{16}\), then checks every exponent \(3\le e\le33\), every \(1\le r\le10\), and all starts \(0\le n<80\) against the sharp valuation and the stronger uniform even-exponent congruence. It also evaluates exact arbitrary-precision integer windows for \(3\le e\le9\), \(1\le r\le6\), and \(0\le n<25\), computing their gcd without using the matrix proof. The checker emits `VERIFY_OK` only on complete success. These bounded checks do not prove the infinite result; the displayed matrix and binomial arguments do.

## Relationship to prior work
Gryszka, *A note on the Fibonacci m-step sequences modulo q* (2022), addresses linear congruences for Tribonacci terms along arithmetic progressions and transfer among initial conditions. Although it employs the same underlying recurrence, its congruences are in the first powers of the terms; the exact dyadic orders for nonlinear sliding-window power sums here do not follow by linearity. The author's openly available September 2022 preprint uses the title *modulo p*; the journal page dates publication to October 2022.

Bilu–Luca–Nieuwveld–Ouaknine–Worrell, *On the p-adic zeros of the Tribonacci sequence* (2022 preprint), considers valuations of individual Tribonacci terms and p-adic interpolation, not gcds of nonlinear windows. Mbirika–Spilker, *GCD of sums of k consecutive squares of generalized Fibonacci numbers* (2022 preprint), proves results for order-two recurrences and squared terms, not arbitrary powers of the order-three Tribonacci sequence. These objects and quantifiers differ, and none of the inspected statements imply the sharp odd/even exponent dichotomy above. The literature comparison is bounded by the material inspected and is not an exhaustive novelty guarantee.

## Limitations
Only the two-adic valuation of the gcd is determined. Odd prime factors and the complete gcd are not classified; no assertion is made for exponent one or two. The proof is specific to this Tribonacci normalization up to shifts and does not automatically extend to arbitrary three-term recurrences. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed. Further equivalent results could exist in unindexed or uninspected literature.
