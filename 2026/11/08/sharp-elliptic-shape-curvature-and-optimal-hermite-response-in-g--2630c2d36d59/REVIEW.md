# Scientific review: elliptical Gaussian localization

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim concerns a simple isolated eigenvalue of a real-analytic positive trace-class family, rather than finite-dimensional truncation. At the disk, the exact eigenvalues are incomplete-gamma/Poisson tails and the largest has gap \(se^{-s}\). The first shape derivative couples the leading eigenvector only to the normalized second Hermite mode, with exact value \(s^2e^{-s}/\sqrt2\); the second derivative of its diagonal entry is \(-2s^2e^{-s}\). The full simple-eigenvalue perturbation formula, *including optimization of the input signal*, produces the asserted curvature; the quarter-turn symmetry ensures an even analytic eigenvalue. An independently assembled quadrature checker returned 148 successful numerical assertions across four areas. Bounds and dimension restrictions are stated. Risk: no uniformity in area; this is explicitly excluded.

**Originality — PASS, provisional.** Exact disk extremality is Nicola–Tilli's theorem, not new. Gómez–Guerra–Ramos–Tilli (arXiv:2307.09304v1, Introduction p.4, stability theorem and Sections 5–6) prove sharp stability exponents and work with superlevel-set perturbations; they explicitly identify small-eccentricity ellipse eigenfunctions as unknown. They do not calculate the optimized second-order ellipse coefficient. Ramos (arXiv:2607.21590, Theorems 1,3,4 and Section 5) proves nearby *constructed-domain* inverse eigenfunctions, quadratic-order deficits and local maximizer rigidity, but not this fixed-ellipse coefficient or eigenvector derivative. Abreu–Dörfler concerns inverse rigidity rather than this curvature. published-finding corpus search compared concrete synonymous queries and closest records; no implication covering this formula was found. Risks: shape-calculus literature might contain an equivalent coefficient expressed in different coordinates, and priority requires external scrutiny.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact first eigenfunction of even a low-eccentricity ellipse was explicitly singled out as a missing example in a quantitative-stability paper. The finding computes the sharp leading stability deficit for the *optimized* signal (not simply testing the Gaussian), identifies its first mode of adaptation and singles out the area where this shape sensitivity is greatest. The distinction between a Gaussian test coefficient and the optimized coefficient is quantitatively substantial: the latter is reduced by the factor \(2/(s+2)\). It is a natural exact model and benchmark for shape stability, though it is local and restricted to a one-parameter ellipse family.
