# Review: Sharp endpoint unconditionality on biased binary filtrations

## Correctness — PASS

For an arbitrary positive product law on a depth-\(d\) binary filtration, the conditional-expectation kernel has the signed-node decomposition stated in the result. At each stage the active signed mass stays in \([-1,1]\), and the total variation of the completed row is **exactly** the sum of sibling absolute masses plus the continuation. The Bellman recursion therefore has no omitted branches or assumed cancellations. The affine-induction comparison is checked against both signs for the small child and against both signs for the large child; the latter uses convexity in the state and nonnegative endpoint gaps. It proves the full recurrence and all dimensions, including depth zero. Strict inequalities for unequal biases establish the unique rare-child evaluation path and alternating signs. The homogeneous closed form follows from a verified linear recurrence. Exact-rational kernel-row enumeration (16 configurations; 10,586 sign/row settings) separately reproduces the recursion, and 120 rational closed-form instances pass. These finite checks corroborate rather than replace the proof. No independent audit has occurred.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The known fair-binary finite-depth Haar sign-transform constant is a strict specialization, recovered when every minority probability is \(1/2\); that specialization is not claimed as new. The new theorem is about **all** strictly biased product probabilities, including nonstationary level biases and unique rare-child norming atoms. Its depth-one value already differs from the fair formula, so fair-splitting identities cannot imply it by substitution. Osękowski's 2013 full text studies restricted weak-type Haar/martingale-transform inequalities rather than the finite-depth signed \(L^\infty\) row norm. Thiele–Treil–Volberg's 2014 full preprint treats nonhomogeneous martingale bases and weighted \(L^2\) estimates, not sharp unweighted \(L^\infty\) finite-depth values. Related later work on optimal target ranges and endpoint estimates was considered at abstract level; no equivalent formula was identified, but these abstract-only comparisons are not full-text exclusions. Targeted claim-, parameter-, duality-, and recurrence-oriented mathematical-record searches found no exact coverage. This supports best-knowledge originality, not a proof of historical uniqueness. A hidden equivalent result in older martingale-transform theory is the main residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The model is a natural nonhomogeneous analogue of the canonical Haar unconditionality problem. Unlike merely proving endpoint divergence, the theorem gives sharp constants for arbitrary depthwise biases through a two-scalar recurrence, determines exactly where every maximizer occurs and how ancestral signs alternate, and computes the full homogeneous bias-dependent slope \(2(1-r)/(1+r)\) with an exponentially small signed correction. This distinguishes all strictly biased laws from the fair law, provides explicit extremizers useful as sharpness witnesses, and produces an exact finite-depth benchmark for analyses of nonhomogeneous filtrations. It does not claim more general graph/tree or \(L^p\) implications.

The preexisting fair-depth formula is acknowledged rather than counted as original. Unindexed priority, node-dependent probabilities, nonbinary branching, suppression signs, and non-endpoint norms remain outside the proven claim.
