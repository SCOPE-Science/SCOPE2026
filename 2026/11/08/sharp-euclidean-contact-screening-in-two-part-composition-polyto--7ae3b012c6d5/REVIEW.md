# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses the full H-inequality definition of the two-part composition polytope, reconstructs its four vertex classes, evaluates all four squared-distance expressions and proves strict inequalities for mixed vertices and (except one boundary) the origin. An explicit positive weighted barycentric certificate on the contact vertices proves the displayed enclosing radius is globally minimal and its center unique. The arithmetic root of the phase polynomial is irrational for all \(q\ge3\), excluding missing integral equality cases. The exact rational checker exhaustively samples 6,400 parameter pairs, with independent small-dimensional H-to-V reconstruction; this is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

The relevant source by Athanasiadis (arXiv:2510.23903, Sections 1–3) gives the defining inequalities, combinatorial equivalence to products of simplices, and theorems about lattice-point/Ehrhart enumeration and gamma-positivity. It does not derive minimum Euclidean enclosing spheres, unique centers, contact-vertex screening, or a metric phase boundary. Chapoton's arbor work supplies the broader objects and enumerative questions, not this metric classification. Related parking-function polytopes, partial permutohedra and other two-simplex constructions have different canonical embeddings and vertex sets, so their known sphere results do not logically imply these formulas. Multiple semantic and literal literature searches, plus comparison to accessible prior mathematical claims, identified no covering statement. An unindexed equivalent general metric theorem remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The result addresses a canonical metric invariant for a freshly studied infinite family of full-dimensional lattice polytopes, not an arbitrary finite parameter computation. It determines the entire minimum enclosing ball and **all** equality cases and exhibits a strict, irrational-threshold-controlled loss of a whole block of contact vertices. This metric distinction is invisible to the known product-of-simplices combinatorial type and supplies a natural baseline for higher-part composition polytopes. No broader downstream theorem is asserted or required for this exact structural classification.

## Scope and limitations

The result is restricted to \(p,q\ge1\), to precisely two composition parts, and to the displayed standard coordinate embedding and Euclidean norm. It does not imply corresponding formulas for affine images, enclosing ellipsoids, or longer compositions. Finite checks are not an exhaustive infinite certificate.
