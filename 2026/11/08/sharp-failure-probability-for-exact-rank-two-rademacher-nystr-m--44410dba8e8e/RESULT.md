# Sharp failure probability for exact rank-two Rademacher Nyström recovery

## Finding
Let \(A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) be any real positive-semidefinite matrix of rank exactly two, and let \(\Omega\in\{-1,1\}^{n\times m}\) have independent fair signs, with \(m\ge2\). For the exact-arithmetic, untruncated Nyström approximation
\[
\widehat A=A\Omega(\Omega^{\mathsf T}A\Omega)^\dagger\Omega^{\mathsf T}A,
\]
the sharp worst-case probability of failing to recover \(A\) is
\[
\sup_{\substack{A\succeq0\\ \operatorname{rank}(A)=2}}
\Pr(\widehat A\ne A)=
\begin{cases}
2^{1-m},&n=2,\\
3\cdot2^{-m}-2\cdot4^{-m},&n\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
Every supremum is attained. For \(n\ge3\), the \(3\times3\) example given below (with zero padding when needed) is extremal for every \(m\ge2\). In particular, failure probabilities for \(m=2,3,4\) are respectively \(5/8\), \(11/32\), and \(23/128\), rather than the full-rank two-dimensional values \(1/2\), \(1/4\), and \(1/8\).

This answers a concrete finite-sketch rank-recovery question: a rank-two positive-semidefinite matrix may require a larger discrete-sign sampling budget in ambient dimension three than in dimension two, even though its exact rank is unchanged.

## Assumptions and scope
The signs are fully independent over all rows and columns, each taking either value with probability \(1/2\). The estimator is the **untruncated** Nyström formula with the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse in exact arithmetic. No spectral conditioning or finite-precision guarantee is asserted. The bound is for every integer \(n\ge2\) and \(m\ge2\), and every rank-two real positive-semidefinite \(A\), without incoherence or spectral-gap assumptions. A projective line is a one-dimensional linear subspace of \(\mathbb R^2\); distinct nonzero vectors define the same line when they are scalar multiples of one another.

## Proof
**1. Exact recovery is a planar spanning event.** Factor \(A=B^{\mathsf T}B\), where \(B\in\mathbb R^{2\times n}\) has full row rank. Set \(W=B\Omega\). Algebra for the pseudoinverse gives
\[
\widehat A=B^{\mathsf T}W(W^{\mathsf T}W)^\dagger W^{\mathsf T}B
=B^{\mathsf T}P_WB,
\]
where \(P_W\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{range}(W)\). Since \(B\) maps \(\mathbb R^n\) onto \(\mathbb R^2\), \(\widehat A=A\) if and only if \(P_W=I_2\), equivalently \(\operatorname{rank}(W)=2\).

**2. Two elementary small-ball restrictions.** For one random sign vector \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n\), put \(Z=B\varepsilon\), \(z=\Pr(Z=0)\), and \(p_L=\Pr(Z\in L\setminus\{0\})\) for each projective line \(L\) carrying positive mass. There are finitely many such lines, and \(\sum_Lp_L=1-z\). Choose two linearly independent columns of \(B\). Conditioning on all other signs, at most one assignment of these two signs can solve \(B\varepsilon=0\). Therefore \(0\le z\le1/4\).

For any line \(L\), choose a nonzero linear functional \(\ell\) annihilating \(L\). The linear form \(\ell B\varepsilon\) is nontrivial, since \(B\) has rank two. Conditioning on all but one sign with nonzero coefficient shows that its probability of zero is at most \(1/2\). Thus
\[
0\le p_L\le \frac12-z=:c,
\qquad c\ge z,
\qquad \sum_Lp_L=1-z=2c+z.
\]

**3. Sharp projective occupancy bound.** The \(m\) independent planar columns span a space of dimension at most one precisely when all nonzero columns lie on one projective line (or all are zero). Disjointly classifying this event by the common nonzero line gives
\[
F_m=z^m+\sum_L\big[(z+p_L)^m-z^m\big].
\]
For fixed \(z\), the function \(u\mapsto(z+u)^m-z^m\) is convex for \(u\ge0\), and all the projective masses lie in \([0,c]\) with total \(2c+z\). Their descending sequence is majorized by the finite sequence \((c,c,z,0,\ldots)\): its first mass is at most \(c\), its first two sum to at most \(2c\), and all later partial sums cannot exceed the total mass. Convexity therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_m&\le z^m+2\big[(z+c)^m-z^m\big]
+\big[(2z)^m-z^m\big]\\
&=2^{1-m}+(2^m-2)z^m\\
&\le 2^{1-m}+(2^m-2)4^{-m}
=3\cdot2^{-m}-2\cdot4^{-m}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The argument works for every integer \(m\ge2\), including degenerate or unequal atom masses.

**4. Equality and the ambient-dimension distinction.** For \(n=3\), choose
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A=B^{\mathsf T}B=
\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Under uniform independent signs, \(B\varepsilon\) equals the zero vector with probability \(1/4\), while its nonzero values occupy exactly the three projective lines generated by \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\), and \((1,1)\), each with probability \(1/4\). Substituting into the exact occupancy expression yields \(F_m=3\cdot2^{-m}-2\cdot4^{-m}\). Zero columns may be appended to \(B\) for any \(n>3\) without changing its rank or the projected-sign law.

For \(n=2\), every full-row-rank \(B\) is invertible. Its four input sign vectors form exactly two antipodal projective pairs, each of probability \(1/2\), with no zero projection. Consequently \(F_m=2(1/2)^m=2^{1-m}\), for every admissible \(A\). These statements prove both sharp cases.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` performs integer-exact projective-occupancy calculations using rational fractions, and independently enumerates sign tuples for the sharp three-coordinate witness at \(m=2,3,4\). It exhaustively checks every rank-two \(2\times n\) matrix with entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) for \(n=2,3,4\), tests several independent sample counts, and exercises random larger integer matrices. Its recorded output is `checks.txt`. This finite testing corroborates the formulas but is not used to infer the universal result; the proof above supplies that conclusion.

## Relationship to prior work
Saibaba and Międlar (2023), especially their Section 3.2 and Appendix D, give broad sub-Gaussian rank and Nyström approximation guarantees, but their bounds use unspecified constants and do not compute the exact worst-case rank-two Rademacher failure probability. Dereziński et al. (2020/2022) study approximate expected random projections in a stable-rank regime, not this rank-two finite atomic event. Balabanov et al. (2023) analyze a distinct structured Hadamard sketch and associated Nyström approximations. The short argument here uses elementary hypercube concentration and an exact convex occupancy bound to solve a separate, discrete exact-recovery question; no broader quantitative-accuracy guarantee is claimed.

## Limitations
The result is special to **exact** rank two and independent dense Rademacher sketches. Failure means nonzero exact matrix residual, not a lower bound on its norm. Exact-arithmetic recovery can be numerically unstable for ill-conditioned inputs. The proof gives the worst-case failure probability, not a full characterization of all extremizing matrices or any rank-three analogue. The underlying one-bit anti-concentration bounds are elementary and not claimed as discoveries; novelty is claimed only for their sharp combination into the Nyström rank-recovery law.
