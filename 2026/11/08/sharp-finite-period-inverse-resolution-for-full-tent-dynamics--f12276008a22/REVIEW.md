Their exact gaps and the smaller-period exclusion prove that the largest available interpolation interval has length \(2/(2^N+1)\). The almost-everywhere slope bound then gives the optimal peak bound, attained by an admissible triangular perturbation. Uniform expansion and uniqueness of fixed points in inverse-branch words prevent additional low-period orbits. Smooth compactly supported perturbations provide the full-jet statement without relaxing the constant. The proof handles all integer cutoffs \(N\ge2\) and \(0<\delta<1\), including boundary/turning-point conditions.

The algebraic periodic-point grid is classical background and is not independently claimed as new. The distinct proposed contribution is the exact finite-data radius and the sharp jet-preserving nonidentifiability. No dominating claim was found by semantic searches of the published mathematical-results index and the accessible earlier claims. **Value — PASS.** Finite observations are unavoidable in the inverse problem prompted by the 2026 infinite-spectrum rigidity theorem. This result identifies the **exact** model-dependent spatial resolution, not merely an exponential order or a generic perturbation. The constant is forced by the unique largest periodic-point gap and is attained inside a uniformly expanding full-branch class; moreover, even matching all finite periodic multipliers and derivative jets cannot improve the minimax rate. These facts describe a precise information limit for finite spectral reconstruction, independently of any downstream theorem.

The result applies only to the described class and does not supply an inverse upper bound from unlabelled finite spectra. The alternative smooth maps approach a supremum but need not attain it. Computational checks use finite cutoffs and do not constitute an independent proof of universal claims.
