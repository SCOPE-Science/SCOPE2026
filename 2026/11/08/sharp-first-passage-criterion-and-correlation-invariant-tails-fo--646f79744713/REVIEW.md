# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The pathwise Poisson subordination formula yields an exact matrix transform \(\pi[D(s)P]^n\mathbf1\) for accumulated rates. The one-site killed-walk spectral kernel is nonnegative, allowing Tonelli summation to a resolvent. A simple Perron eigenvalue and positive stationary weights give an exact leading resolvent singularity \(h(s)^{-1}\); integration yields an iff mean criterion. Regular variation and Potter domination give a sharp survival equivalent. Operational hitting-time decomposition extends the conclusions to fixed starting sites. A standard-library script independently enumerates finite Markov/emission paths and checks spectral and perturbation identities, replaying 116 assertions. The asymptotic argument is analytic, not extrapolated from those finite tests. Risks: switching between discrete and continuous time, aperiodicity requirement, and normalization have been addressed in the proof; no bound uniform in growing starting position is claimed.

**Originality (PASS, provisional).** Levit--Reuveni's 2026 source [1] already provides the general spectral subordination equation and an iid Mittag-Leffler-rate phase transition. Grebenkov's 2018 work [2] already develops first-passage transforms and matrix methods for switching fixed diffusivities. The claim here is different: a sharp first-moment criterion for *arbitrary hidden-Markov rate emission distributions*, a stationary-mixture invariant sharp survival coefficient despite Markov temporal dependence, and a logarithmically corrected critical classification for heterogeneous tails. The relevant full texts, titles, hypotheses, and methods were compared, together with semantic mathematical-result searches. This is a best-knowledge assessment, not a claim of proof of literature uniqueness. Residual risk: another Markov-additive renewal or diffusing-diffusivity theorem may imply the same formulas under a more general notation.

**Value (PASS).** A recent mean-first-passage phase transition is known for independently refreshed rates. Natural physical environments have persistent finite-state regimes with different rate tails; it matters whether persistence changes the transition or leading survival. The theorem answers both questions and reveals a sharp second parameter at the critical power exponent, giving an exact boundary not resolved by the prior iid one-parameter exponent calculation. It supplies both a distribution-free integrability test and an explicit asymptotic constant, rather than a generic upper bound.

**Closest literature and limits.** [1] B. Levit and S. Reuveni, arXiv:2609.26925v1 (full online manuscript, equations (2)--(4)); [2] D. S. Grebenkov, arXiv:1812.07249v1 (switching-diffusion section and integrated-diffusivity matrix formula); [3] Angstmann et al., arXiv:2511.18256v1 (independently compounded steps). The result assumes primitive fixed finite-state mixing, conditionally independent emissions, and positive finite rates. Regular variation is necessary for the stated sharp asymptotic but not for the mean criterion. No independent audit has been performed.
