# Sharp first three-round refinement thresholds in trivially perfect graphs

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph. Starting from one vertex color, perform simultaneous **color refinement**: each vertex receives the pair consisting of its previous color and the multiset of its neighbors' previous colors. Let \(r(G)\) count the **strict partition refinements**, namely the number of successive steps that increase the number of vertex color classes, stopping when a step does not split any class. This convention does not count an unsuccessful final stabilization check.

Among **trivially perfect graphs**, equivalently finite \((P_4,C_4)\)-free graphs, the least order permitting \(r(G)\ge3\) is exactly **eight**. Up to isomorphism, exactly one eight-vertex graph realizes this threshold: the disjoint union \(K_{1,3}\sqcup P\), where \(P\) is the four-vertex *paw* (a triangle with one pendant vertex attached to a triangle vertex).

Among **connected** trivially perfect graphs, the least order permitting \(r(G)\ge3\) is exactly **nine**. Up to isomorphism, its unique nine-vertex witness is the cone \(K_1\vee(K_{1,3}\sqcup P)\).

These are exact class-relative minimum-order and uniqueness statements, not claims about unrestricted graphs. The strict splitting maxima for all trivially perfect graphs of orders \(1,\ldots,9\) are respectively \(0,0,1,1,2,2,2,3,3\); the corresponding connected maxima are \(0,0,1,1,1,2,2,2,3\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and nonempty. Trivially perfect means that no four vertices induce a chordless four-vertex path or cycle. Vertex colors initially coincide. The threshold concerns the color partition of vertices in a single graph, not a comparison of separate graphs. In descriptive-complexity terms, each successive vertex-color partition corresponds to the pointed two-variable counting-logic refinement hierarchy; the exact algorithmic convention above fixes any possible offset between rank and round counts.

## Proof
**Canonical exhaustive domain.** Every connected nonempty trivially perfect graph has at least one universal vertex, and deleting a universal vertex leaves a trivially perfect graph. Consequently, the graphs in this class are exactly the comparability graphs of rooted forests: two vertices are adjacent precisely when one is an ancestor of the other. This construction is complete by induction on order. It is injective on unlabelled rooted forests: the universal vertices of a connected graph form a uniquely determined initial chain (possibly its entirety); deleting that chain identifies the multiset of lower components recursively. Disjoint connected components determine the outer forest. Thus, unordered rooted forests are canonical, and exhaustive enumeration of them is an exhaustive enumeration of isomorphism classes.

Write \(\mathcal T_n\) for unlabelled rooted trees of order \(n\) and \(\mathcal F_n\) for unordered forests of total order \(n\), including \(\mathcal F_0=\{\varnothing\}\). Each member of \(\mathcal T_n\) is uniquely a new root over a forest in \(\mathcal F_{n-1}\). Each member of \(\mathcal F_n\) is uniquely a sorted tuple of rooted trees with total order \(n\). The included `check_tp_cutoff.py` implements both recursive constructions as exact finite sets of immutable tuples, with nondecreasing tuple order preventing repetitions. The exhaustive type counts for \(n=1,\ldots,9\) are \(1,2,4,9,20,48,115,286,719\), and the corresponding connected counts are \(1,1,2,4,9,20,48,115,286\). No heuristic sampling or upper limit on generated types is used.

**Exact refinement verification.** The checker constructs the comparability adjacency lists for every canonical forest up to order nine and iterates color classes according to the defining multiset recurrence until no class splits. Its independently implemented second refinement procedure splits existing classes directly using their full neighbor-count vectors to current classes, and agrees exactly on every generated graph. The full exact round-count histograms are in `check_tp_cutoff_output.txt`; thus the maxima and uniqueness statements are exhaustive consequences of the recurrence and the canonical generation proof above.

**Explicit first witnesses.** In \(K_{1,3}\sqcup P\), there are two vertices of degree three (the two component centers), two vertices of degree two (the paw's other triangle vertices), and four of degree one (three star leaves and the paw leaf). First refinement produces these three degree classes. The next refinement separates the degree-three centers because they have, respectively, three degree-one neighbors and one degree-one plus two degree-two neighbors. The degree-one vertices remain indistinguishable in that step because their degree-three neighbors had the same preceding color. In the third step the three star leaves separate from the paw leaf, witnessing a third strict refinement. Adding a universal vertex changes each old degree by one, keeps all their mutual neighbor-color patterns after accounting for the unique new vertex, and adds a singleton vertex; hence the nine-vertex cone has the same three strict splitting steps. The exhaustive scan confirms the unique witnesses at these first two orders.

## Verification
Run `python3 check_tp_cutoff.py` in the directory containing this file. It uses only the Python standard library. It enumerates every unordered rooted forest of order at most nine, independently computes strict refinement rounds by two different algorithms, checks the maximum and uniqueness claims, and prints its complete count histograms. The accompanying `check_tp_cutoff_output.txt` is the actual successful replay. Full enumeration is a proof of the finite cutoffs because the canonical forest recurrence is exhaustive and nonredundant; the enumerator has no random, approximate, timeout, or sampling stages.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical rooted-forest characterization of trivially perfect graphs underlies the exhaustive domain. Rubio-Montiel (2015) studies related graph-coloring characterizations of the same class, not color-refinement iteration thresholds. Arvind, Köbler, Rattan and Verbitsky (2015) give the color-refinement and counting-logic framework, but not these sharp class-relative minimum orders. Kiefer and McKay (2020) determine unrestricted-graph extremal iteration numbers and exhibit general infinite families, without establishing the first three-splitting thresholds or uniqueness restricted to trivially perfect graphs. The finding is not a new proof of their general extremal estimates. The direct fixed-order calculation uses its own exhaustive enumeration, rather than inferring the answer from an unrelated table.

## Limitations
The assertion is only about the first occurrence of **three** strict splitting steps, and only for trivially perfect graphs. It does not determine the maximum number of steps in this class for arbitrary order or claim unique maximizers beyond the two first occurrences. Unbounded first-order quantifier rank, counting-logic graph-isomorphism identification, and global amenability are different questions. Historical priority beyond the inspected sources is not independently certified.
