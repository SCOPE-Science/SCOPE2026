# Scientific review: sharp fixed-mean stationary purity range for a renewal-reset qubit

## Correctness

**PASS.** The result concerns the phase-stationary renewal clock, which is available for all strictly positive integrable interarrival laws, including deterministic lattice renewals. The renewal-reward identity reduces the stationary off-diagonal amplitude to \(\chi=(\mathbb E e^{iX}-1)/(im)\) for \(X=\Omega T>0\) with \(\mathbb EX=m\); the stationary purity is an exact quadratic in \(|\chi|\). The global upper bound uses a concave majorant \(g\) of the complex chord length; both steps of the inequality and all equality cases are proved. The lower equality condition comes from the nonnegative function \(1-\cos X\), and a positive two-point approximation proves the otherwise unattained floor. Convex interpolation proves the absence of missing interior values. The diagnostic checker separately verifies renewal integrals, boundary extremizers and random finite-support instances; no finite sample is treated as a proof of the theorem.

## Originality

**PASS, with publication-priority reservations.** Sevilla and Valdés-Hernández, arXiv:2209.05643, Secs. 2.1, 3.1, 3.3 and 4, already derive the general quantum renewal stationary state and purity functional (notably Eqs. 27 and 42). They do not establish the exact prescribed-mean purity interval, its two sharp mean thresholds, the uniqueness of the deterministic maximizer, the family of all resonant maximizers, or the attainability threshold for the dephased minimum. Those, rather than the renewal identity or Jensen's inequality, constitute the final claim. Wald et al., arXiv:2410.11497, Secs. 2 and 4, study discrete-time gate resetting, resonances and the absence of pointwise convergence for certain clocks; this is a different dynamics and time-sampling convention. Rose et al., Phys. Rev. E 98, 022129 (2018), consider state-independent Poissonian quantum resetting, not optimization over all mean-constrained renewal laws. Available results on mean-constrained qubit projective first detection and stationary renewal age/residual correlations use related renewal and concavity methods but optimize different observables. First-detection mean time is a stopping-time quantity, not stationary quantum purity, so its phase diagram does not imply the present two threshold laws. The known Erlang-renewal purity-versus-reset-state-fidelity ordering theorem, including its fourteen-stage reversal, concerns a comparison between two observables **inside a prescribed integer-shape law family**; it gives no sharp range of purity over **all** interarrival distributions with a fixed mean, and cannot imply the optimizer support or strict nonattainment here. A previously unindexed specialized purity optimization, or a broad concave-envelope theorem whose hypothesis specializes directly, cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Prescribing the mean interarrival fixes a physically meaningful reset-budget scale while allowing choice of regular or randomized renewal protocols. The full sharp stationary-purity interval answers whether a target amount of long-time coherence is achievable, determines exactly which renewal schedules retain most coherence, and distinguishes two independent thresholds: half-turn switching of maximum-purity protocols and full-turn feasibility of complete dephasing. The theorem includes mixed reset states, arbitrary integrable positive interarrivals, strict equality and nonattainment characterizations. It is a natural complete optimization classification, not a numerical slice or a reformulation of the known renewal equation.

## Closest literature and limits

The strongest direct prior formula is Sevilla and Valdés-Hernández's stationary renewal purity functional, arXiv:2209.05643, Eq. 42. Wald et al. (arXiv:2410.11497) emphasize that periodic resetting from a synchronized initial phase need not converge pointwise; the result here deliberately concerns the phase-stationary occupation ensemble and does not claim otherwise. The physical model excludes dissipation between resets, repeated measurements that do not reprepare a fixed state, reset costs, and many-level Hamiltonians with several incommensurate gaps. Priority cannot be fully certified by literature/database searches alone.
