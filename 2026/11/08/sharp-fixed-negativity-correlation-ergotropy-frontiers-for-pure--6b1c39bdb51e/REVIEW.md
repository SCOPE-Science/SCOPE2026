# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The one-parameter parametrization of every ordered qutrit Schmidt spectrum at fixed trace-norm negativity follows by solving two quadratic constraints. Differentiating the outer-weight spread gives a single feasible critical point; exact endpoint comparisons prove the claimed lower and upper ergotropy extrema. The rank-two/full-rank boundary follows from the feasible-interval endpoint and is exactly \(\mathcal N=\log2\). Both limit spectra are treated separately; no finite sampling is used as an all-spectrum argument. The standalone independent-matrix numerical check passed 72,109 deterministic assertions, including direct partial transpose and passive-energy evaluation after arbitrary local basis rotations. Boundary floating-point evaluation is handled by explicit equal-weight endpoint limits.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Hajizade–Ameri–Askari, arXiv:2610.05592, Sec. III.2, Eq. (12) already proves correlation ergotropy for **each** pure Schmidt spectrum and Eq. (8) gives logarithmic negativity. The present theorem does not reclaim these identities. Its new work is the full **constrained** fixed-negativity interval, global sharpness, equality-state uniqueness, and the \(\log2\) optimizer-rank transition. The motivating text's simplex plot and dynamical trajectory plot do not provide those mathematical extremal conclusions. Alimuddin–Guha–Parashar, arXiv:1912.09780, independently provides the general passive-energy/ergotropic-gap equivalence; Yang et al., arXiv:2406.13319, develops multipartite qubit gap polytopes; and Shi et al., arXiv:2205.11080, examines battery work versus quantum resources. Their inspected relevant sections contain no covering fixed-negativity two-qutrit sharp classification. Scientific record searching found no exact duplicate or stronger fixed-negativity result; this is not a proof of absolute publication priority.

## Value — PASS
The same logarithmic negativity permits a whole nontrivial interval of locked work. This is a motivated operational inverse problem for a qutrit quantum battery: how much work remains inaccessible to local operations when only an entanglement measure is known? The complete sharp frontier, unique extremizing Schmidt spectra, and rank-change threshold answer it for the natural smallest system with an independent third spectral degree of freedom. The optimizer rank changes at an exact physically meaningful entanglement value instead of at an arbitrary numerical slice. No claim is made about whether a specific experimental charging path reaches the extremes.
