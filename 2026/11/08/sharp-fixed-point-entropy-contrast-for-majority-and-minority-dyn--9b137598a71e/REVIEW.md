# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The central claim is a simultaneous exact fixed-state classification and sharp leading asymptotic for both tie-retaining rules on the labelled helm graph. At degree-one leaves, fixed minority forces opposite color and fixed majority forces identical color. Rim and hub inequalities give necessary **and sufficient** forbidden-word conditions: minority forbids `111` among rim/hub agreements, majority forbids `010` among rim/hub disagreements, both with at most half of positions equal to one. The run decompositions and factor-of-two hub symmetry are exact for all \(n\ge3\). The explicit four-state weighted matrices, their cubic characteristic factors, the majority saddle at \(y=3/4\), mean \(1/2\), variance \(1/3\), and the loop-based Fourier aperiodicity provide the stated asymptotics. Independent exact word enumeration and direct vertex-by-vertex simulation support the proof without replacing it. All coefficients and constants were rechecked from the actual scripts and recorded outputs.

## Originality — PASS, with historical qualification

The most relevant complete primary document, Agur–Fraenkel–Klein (1988, doi:10.1016/0012-365X(88)90005-2), determines *other* cyclic language counts: avoidance of both isolated-bit patterns and avoidance of both constant triple patterns on ordinary rings. Neither statement determines fixed-point counts on helms, which impose only one forbidden word at a time **and** a hub-induced half-density cutoff; the parity-sensitive saddle coefficient is not given there. Wacław–Burda (arXiv:0801.1224) analyze random Gaussian couplings and metastability complexities, not this deterministic family. The previously recorded sunlet majority census has the pendant leaves but no hub and therefore no global half-density constraint; the wheel and fan results have no rim leaves. The earlier squared-cycle minority/majority entropy contrast concerns a different degree-four, hub-free graph and does not imply either helm formula. Searches of mathematics research records and relevant graph sources did not reveal equivalent explicit formulas. Banks–Djirar (1992) is a plausible methodological predecessor whose full text was unavailable; the positive priority decision is qualified and not a claim that a search proves absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The helm is a standard connected core–rim–leaf family, not an arbitrary parameter slice. The same topology and tie convention produce two qualitatively distinct fixed-state statistical-mechanical regimes: a noncritical tribonacci exponential for minority and a half-density boundary saddle with a \(n^{-1/2}\) penalty, exact base \(\sqrt3\), and an even–odd prefactor for majority. The result explains these distinctions through local constraints and the global hub threshold, gives complete exact populations for every size, and quantifies an exponential entropy separation. It is mathematically more informative than finite enumeration or an unmotivated parameter value.
