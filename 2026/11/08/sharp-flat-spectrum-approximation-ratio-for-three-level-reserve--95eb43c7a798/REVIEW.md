# Scientific review: sharp flat-spectrum reserve-greedy allocation ratio

**Correctness — PASS.** The result fixes the exact coefficient \(c(w)\), the three-width source algorithm, the certificate budget and integer payload objective. The first and second reserve stages reduce to explicit floor counts; their errors are uniformly bounded because the second group has bounded size. Strict convexity of \(c(w)\) gives the piecewise-linear lower convex envelope of the integer optimum; rounding adjacent-width mixtures gives the uniform upper bound. The limiting ratio is fractional linear on either side of the middle-coefficient budget, so its unique maximum is proved analytically. Jensen's inequality certifies exact finite-rank uniqueness of the uniform middle-width assignment at that budget. The packaged exact-rational verifier independently executes the reserve rule and checks 17,723 cases and assertions; the analytical proof supplies the unrestricted quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** Carson, Chen and Khan (2026), arXiv:2610.40134v1, Section 2.2, Algorithm 1 and Remark 28, establish the certificate and feasibility and acknowledge that the rule need not minimize stored bits. Their Section 4.2--4.3 and Supplement B compare finite empirical ratios against dynamic programming, including flat-spectrum cases, but do not state the exact all-budget limiting ratio, its unique maximizing budget, or its linear maximum-precision deterioration. The 2021 fixed-point allocation and greedy mixed-precision works concern different allocation rules and objectives; their publicly accessible abstracts were reviewed, but their full proofs were not. A more general historical treatment of this particular reserve heuristic could exist.

**Value — PASS.** The three-tier calculation replaces uncalibrated finite heuristic comparisons with a complete, sharp asymptotic worst-budget guarantee and a concrete stringent-certificate witness. For bitwidths \((8,12,32)\), rank \(64\) and a certificate at approximately \(3.13\%\) of a partial identity block's maximum norm, the feasible greedy allocator stores \(1676\) component bits where \(768\) suffice, a ratio \(419/192\). The theorem pinpoints the reason: an aggressive low-precision suffix consumes budget and forces almost all remaining components to the largest width. It is a performance statement about certificate-constrained factor payload, not actual reconstructed error, execution time, or a full hierarchical representation including metadata.
