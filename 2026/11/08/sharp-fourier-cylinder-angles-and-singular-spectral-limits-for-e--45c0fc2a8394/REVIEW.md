# Scientific review: Fourier–cylinder angles for even-base Cantor measures

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem has explicit quantifiers: each even base at least four, every positive cylinder depth, and the standard fair-sign Bernoulli convolution. The proof checks both orthonormal systems independently and computes the cross-Gram matrix by conditioning on the first digits. A least-differing-digit cosine is exactly zero even for half-integer frequencies, which makes the finite prefix matrix unitary. The cross-Gram factorization then proves every principal cosine, not just a determinant or numerical rank. Monotonicity of the positive Fourier product proves the sharp extremal mode; coupling of finite and infinite digit words proves the quantitative singular limiting distribution; direct product expansion gives the high-base constant. The packaged checker passed 3,446 tests of unitarity, singular values, direct cylinder quadrature, coupling bounds, parity identities, and the asymptotic coefficient. Numerical tests are corroboration, not the proof of the infinite assertions.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The most relevant starting points are Jorgensen–Pedersen's spectral-pair/finite-Hadamard construction and Tang–Wu's 2026 study of mock-Fourier partial sums and cylinder martingales. Jorgensen–Pedersen establish the separate Fourier product and Hadamard ingredients; Tang–Wu prove a weak-type maximal inequality and compare Dirichlet kernels across cylinders. Neither inspected work states the exact cross-filtration principal-angle multiset, the sharp all-level projection constant for every even base, or the singular-continuous limiting angle distribution. Known Hadamard-filter methods may make the factorization accessible, and equivalent treatments are not ruled out solely by the absence of terminology. Strichartz's original full text was inaccessible in available routes; its abstract does not decide the comparison. No claim of independent publication priority is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result evaluates a natural obstruction to exchanging two approximations of the same fractal \(L^2\) space: finite spectral truncation and finite spatial-digit resolution. It gives exact conversion conditioning and every principal angle rather than a generic frame estimate, and discovers a persistent singular-continuous limiting distribution even as both dimensions grow. It provides a sharp all-level lower bound and shows quantitatively that the two filtrations become nearly coincident in large even bases. The precise invariant is relevant to multiresolution approximation and mock-Fourier reconstruction, independent of whether it later yields an endpoint maximal theorem.

## Closest literature and limitations
See Tang–Wu, arXiv:2607.27796, Sections 1–5; Jorgensen–Pedersen, arXiv:funct-an/9709007, Sections 2–4; and Strichartz's 2000 article (abstract only). The theorem does not extend to arbitrary spectral subsets, biased digits or odd bases, and does not prove \(L^1\) bounds or pointwise convergence. The most significant originality uncertainty is an equivalent cross-filtration treatment implicit in older Hadamard-pair or mock-Fourier literature. Self-review is not an independent audit.
