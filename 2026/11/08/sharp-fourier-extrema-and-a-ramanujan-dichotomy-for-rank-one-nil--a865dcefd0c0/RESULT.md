# Sharp Fourier extrema and a Ramanujan dichotomy for rank-one nilpotent matrix Cayley graphs

## Finding
Let \(q\) be any prime power and \(n\ge2\). On the additive group \(H=\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb F_q)=\{A\in M_n(\mathbb F_q):\operatorname{tr}A=0\}\), form the simple undirected Cayley graph \(\Gamma_{n,q}\) generated by all nonzero rank-one square-zero matrices. The graph is connected, has \(q^{n^2-1}\) vertices, and has valency
\[
k_{n,q}=\frac{(q^{n-1}-1)(q^n-1)}{q-1}.
\]
Its nonprincipal adjacency spectrum has exact largest and smallest eigenvalues
\[
\lambda_2=\frac{(q^{n-1}-1)^2}{q-1},\qquad
\lambda_{\min}=-\frac{q^n-1}{q-1}.
\]
For \(n\ge3\), the multiplicity of \(\lambda_2\) is \((q-1)q^{n-1}(q^n-1)/(q-1)=q^{n-1}(q^n-1)\); its characters are exactly those represented by matrices diagonalizable with two distinct rational eigenvalues of geometric multiplicities \((n-1,1)\). For \(n=2\), the multiplicity is \(q(q^2-1)/2\).

The graph is Ramanujan, in the usual connected regular graph sense (allowing the trivial eigenvalue \(-k\) when bipartite), **if and only if \(n=2\)**. The graph \(\Gamma_{2,2}\) is the three-dimensional cube. For \(n=2\), its entire adjacency spectrum, with multiplicities, is
\[
\left\{(q^2-1)^{[1]},\quad(q-1)^{[q(q^2-1)/2]},\quad
(-1)^{[q^2-1]},\quad(-(q+1))^{[q(q-1)^2/2]}\right\},
\]
where superscript square brackets denote multiplicities and not exponentiation.

## Assumptions and scope
All statements hold for every prime power \(q\), including even characteristic and nonprime fields, and every integer \(n\ge2\). The Fourier transform is unnormalized. A fixed nontrivial additive character \(\psi:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb C^\times\) is used, and every additive character of \(H\) has the form \(\chi_B(X)=\psi(\operatorname{tr}(BX))\) for some \(B\in M_n(\mathbb F_q)\); labels \(B\) and \(B+cI\) are equivalent. The Fourier multiplier \(\lambda(B)\) is the sum of \(\chi_B\) over the connection set. The trace in the character is the matrix trace over \(\mathbb F_q\), not the field trace to its prime field. The adjacency operator is unweighted and contains no loops.

For a frequency matrix \(B\), let \(d_t(B)=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\ker(B-tI)\) and let
\[
e(B)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_q}\frac{q^{d_t(B)}-1}{q-1}.
\]
This is the number of rational projective eigenlines of \(B\), including all eigenspaces but counting each line once. The complete character-indexed spectrum is
\[
\boxed{\lambda(B)=q^{n-1}e(B)-\frac{q^n-1}{q-1}.}
\]
No assertion is made that every integer between the spectral extrema occurs as an eigenvalue, or that the complete multiplicity distribution for \(n\ge3\) has a simple closed form.

## Proof
**1. Connection set and connectedness.** Every nonzero rank-one square-zero matrix has a representation \(X=uv^T\) with \(u,v\in\mathbb F_q^n\setminus\{0\}\) and \(v^Tu=0\). Every such matrix has exactly \(q-1\) representations under \((u,v)\mapsto(cu,c^{-1}v)\). For each nonzero \(u\), exactly \(q^{n-1}-1\) nonzero \(v\)'s are perpendicular to \(u\); this proves the valency formula. The connection set is stable under \(X\mapsto-X\). It spans \(H\): it contains all off-diagonal elementary matrices \(E_{ij}\) with \(i\ne j\), while
\[
(e_i+e_j)(e_i^T-e_j^T)=E_{ii}-E_{ij}+E_{ji}-E_{jj}
\]
is rank-one square-zero, so subtracting off-diagonal terms yields every traceless diagonal difference \(E_{ii}-E_{jj}\). This argument also holds in characteristic two. Hence the Cayley graph is connected.

**2. Complete character formula.** The nondegenerate trace pairing on the full matrix space restricts to a pairing on \(H\) with annihilator exactly \(\mathbb F_q I\). For each nonzero \(u\), character orthogonality on the hyperplane \(u^\perp\) gives
\[
\sum_{v\in u^\perp}\psi(v^TBu)=
\begin{cases}q^{n-1},&Bu\in\langle u\rangle,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
Delete \(v=0\), sum over \(u\ne0\), and divide by \(q-1\). There are exactly \((q-1)e(B)\) nonzero vectors spanning eigenlines, giving
\[
\lambda(B)=\frac{q^{n-1}(q-1)e(B)-(q^n-1)}{q-1}.
\]
This is the stated formula, and explains why its values are real integers even when \(\psi\) is not real.

**3. Exact extrema and equality spaces.** If \(B\) is scalar, \(e(B)=(q^n-1)/(q-1)\), giving the valency. Conversely a non-scalar matrix has at most \(n-1\) independent eigenvectors for any single rational eigenvalue. If its distinct rational eigenspaces have dimensions \(d_1,\dots,d_r\), then \(\sum_i d_i\le n\), and
\[
\sum_i\frac{q^{d_i}-1}{q-1}\le\frac{q^{n-1}-1}{q-1}+1.
\]
Indeed, concentrating the dimension among at most two eigenspaces maximizes the strictly convex discrete function \((q^d-1)/(q-1)\); for a non-scalar matrix equality requires exactly two rational eigenspaces, of dimensions \(n-1\) and \(1\), with total dimension \(n\). Thus equality holds precisely for diagonalizable matrices with two distinct eigenvalues and multiplicities \((n-1,1)\). The stated \(\lambda_2\) follows by substitution. The lower bound \(e(B)\ge0\) is attained exactly when \(B\) has no eigenvalue in \(\mathbb F_q\); such matrices exist because there is an irreducible polynomial of degree \(n\) over every finite field. This proves \(\lambda_{\min}\). The eigenvalue \(k\) is simple because the generators span \(H\).

**4. Multiplicities.** For \(n\ge3\), the eigenspaces \(U\) and \(W\) corresponding to the eigenvalue of multiplicity \(n-1\) and the simple eigenvalue are distinguished. There are \(q(q-1)\) ordered distinct eigenvalue choices, \((q^n-1)/(q-1)\) hyperplanes \(U\), and \(q^{n-1}\) complementary lines \(W\). Each character label is represented by exactly \(q\) matrices \(B+cI\); division gives \(q^{n-1}(q^n-1)\). For \(n=2\) the two eigenspaces are not distinguished, so the count is divided by two, giving \(q(q^2-1)/2\).

For \(n=2\), every nonscalar matrix belongs to exactly one of the following rational canonical types: split semisimple with two eigenlines; one Jordan block over a rational eigenvalue with one eigenline; or irreducible characteristic polynomial with no rational eigenline. The number of character classes \(B+\mathbb F_qI\) of these types is, respectively,
\[
\frac{q(q^2-1)}2,\qquad q^2-1,\qquad\frac{q(q-1)^2}2.
\]
The middle number follows by counting \(q\) possible eigenvalues times \(q^2-1\) nonzero Jordan nilpotent matrices and dividing by \(q\). The last follows from the \((q^2-q)/2\) irreducible monic quadratics and their \(q(q-1)\)-element similarity classes, again dividing by \(q\). Substituting \(e=2,1,0\) yields the four-level spectrum shown above.

**5. Ramanujan dichotomy.** For \(n=2\), the valency is \(k=q^2-1\). At \(q=2\), the spectrum is \(3,1,-1,-3\) with multiplicities \(1,3,3,1\); the three generators form a basis of \(\mathbb F_2^3\), so the graph is the cube, and \(-k\) is the permitted bipartite trivial eigenvalue. For \(q\ge3\), the largest nontrivial absolute eigenvalue is \(q+1\), and \(q+1\le2\sqrt{q^2-2}\), since \(3q^2-2q-9\ge0\). Thus all graphs with \(n=2\) are Ramanujan.

For \(n=3,q=2\), the valency is \(21\) and \(\lambda_2=9>2\sqrt{20}\). For \(n=3,q\ge3\), one has
\[
\lambda_2=(q-1)(q+1)^2,\qquad k=(q+1)(q^3-1),
\]
and \(\lambda_2^2>4k\), because \((q-1)(q+1)^3>4(q^2+q+1)\). For fixed \(q\), put \(a=q^{n-1}\). The ratio
\[
\frac{\lambda_2^2}{k}=\frac{(a-1)^3}{(q-1)(qa-1)}
\]
is strictly increasing with \(a>1\), since its logarithmic derivative is \(3/(a-1)-q/(qa-1)>0\). Thus the failure persists for all higher \(n\). In the remaining case \(q=2,n\ge4\), this ratio already exceeds \(4\) at \(n=4\), so the same conclusion holds. Therefore no graph with \(n\ge3\) is Ramanujan.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python checker enumerates every rank-one square-zero generator and every character class \(B+\mathbb F_qI\), independently evaluates the complex character sum, calculates rational eigenline counts, and verifies the claimed spectra in the cases \((n,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,5),(3,2)\). It reports respectively the spectra \(\{3^1,1^3,(-1)^3,(-3)^1\}\), \(\{8^1,2^{12},(-1)^8,(-4)^6\}\), \(\{24^1,4^{60},(-1)^{24},(-6)^{40}\}\), and \(\{21^1,9^{28},5^{21},1^{84},(-3)^{98},(-7)^{24}\}\). Field arithmetic in this checker is over prime fields; the proof applies to all prime powers and does not require extrapolating finite tests. The checker exits with `NILPOTENT_CAYLEY_FOURIER_CHECKS_OK` when all tests pass.

## Relationship to prior work
Finite-group Fourier diagonalization of Cayley graphs is standard; DeCorte, de Laat, and Vallentin (2013) discuss this framework and a different family of graphs on invertible matrices. Gavrilyuk and Koolen (2015) study the bilinear-forms graph generated by **all** rank-one matrix differences, without the trace-zero/nilpotence condition, on the full matrix additive group. The present graph instead lives on the trace-zero additive subspace and permits only square-zero rank-one differences. Its eigenvalues count fixed projective eigenlines of frequency matrices, rather than depending only on frequency rank as in the unconditioned bilinear-forms graph. The resulting sharp nontrivial extremum, full spectrum in dimension two, and exact Ramanujan threshold are the claimed distinction. Related earlier matrix-convolution computations for rank-one idempotents use a disjoint trace-one connection set, and do not imply this Ramanujan classification without a separate calculation.

## Limitations
Historical priority is assessed under bounded literature review, not certified exhaustively. A broader treatment of conjugation-invariant translation graphs or affine quadratic-cone graphs may already imply the dimension-two case. A stronger general theorem on rank-one nilpotent difference graphs, if found, would affect originality. This statement does not classify all intermediate eigenvalue multiplicities for \(n\ge3\), nor claim expansion optimality beyond the Ramanujan test. The proof is for graph adjacency over finite fields, not matrix multiplication Cayley graphs. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
