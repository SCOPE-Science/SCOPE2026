# Sharp fourth-moment interval and an exact deficit for binary fourth Wiener chaos

## Finding

Let \(X,Y\) be independent standard real Gaussian variables, and let \(\mathcal C_4^{(2)}\) be the five-dimensional real fourth homogeneous Wiener chaos generated by them. Every nonzero \(F\in\mathcal C_4^{(2)}\) satisfies the sharp bounds
\[
105\le \frac{\mathbb E[F^4]}{(\mathbb E[F^2])^2}\le639.
\]
The minimum is attained precisely by the nonzero real harmonic quartics
\[
F=\operatorname{Re}\{z(X+iY)^4\},\qquad z\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}.
\]
The maximum is attained precisely by nonzero scalar multiples of \(H_4(v_1X+v_2Y)\), where \(v_1^2+v_2^2=1\) and \(H_4(t)=t^4-6t^2+3\). The upper endpoint also follows from standard Wiener-contraction estimates; the dimension-specific lower endpoint, its equality classification and the polynomial deficit identity below are the main contribution.

For the unique real coordinates \(a,b_1,b_2,c_1,c_2\) in the basis defined below, set \(B=b_1+ib_2\), \(C=c_1+ic_2\), \(U=|B|^2\), \(V=|C|^2\) and \(Q=\mathbb E[F^2]\). The following is an exact identity, not an estimate:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[F^4]-105Q^2={}&786432a^4+497664U^2+7077888a^2V\\
&+221184U(a^2+3V)+3760128|aB+\overline B C|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
All five summands are nonnegative. In particular the deficit vanishes exactly on the harmonic-quartic subspace, giving a quantitative algebraic obstruction to equality.

## Assumptions and scope

All coefficients and random variables are real except the auxiliary complex numbers \(B,C\); \(\mathbb E\) is expectation with respect to the product of two standard Gaussian probability measures. With \(R=X^2+Y^2\), use the five polynomials
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_0&=R^2-8R+8,\\
\Phi_{2,c}&=(X^2-Y^2)(R-6),&\Phi_{2,s}&=2XY(R-6),\\
\Phi_{4,c}&=X^4-6X^2Y^2+Y^4,&\Phi_{4,s}&=4XY(X^2-Y^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
They span \(\mathcal C_4^{(2)}\), with unique decomposition
\[
F=a\Phi_0+b_1\Phi_{2,c}+b_2\Phi_{2,s}+c_1\Phi_{4,c}+c_2\Phi_{4,s}.
\]
Their squared \(L^2\) norms are respectively \(64,48,48,192,192\), and all distinct vectors are orthogonal. The fourth Wiener chaos consists of linear combinations of the five Hermite products \(H_j(X)H_{4-j}(Y)\) for \(0\le j\le4\); the displayed basis is an invertible linear recombination of these products. This fixes the normalizations and rules out confusion with ordinary homogeneous degree-four polynomials, which need not be centered or Wick ordered.

## Proof

**First, the fourth-moment identity.** In polar form \(X=\sqrt R\cos\theta\), \(Y=\sqrt R\sin\theta\), the angle is uniform and independent of \(R\), with \(\mathbb E[R^k]=2^kk!\) for all nonnegative integers \(k\). The five basis vectors become \(R^2-8R+8\), \(R(R-6)\cos2\theta\), \(R(R-6)\sin2\theta\), \(R^2\cos4\theta\), and \(R^2\sin4\theta\). Angular orthogonality and the radial moment formula give
\[
Q=64a^2+48U+192V
\]
and, by expanding fourth powers and retaining only zero angular frequencies,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[F^4]={}&1216512a^4+739584U^2+3870720V^2\\
&+4626432a^2U+9658368a^2V+6359040UV\\
&+7520256a\operatorname{Re}(B^2\overline C).
\end{aligned}
\]
For example, the only phase-sensitive term arises from the angular identity \(\mathbb E_\theta[(b_1\cos2\theta+b_2\sin2\theta)^2(c_1\cos4\theta+c_2\sin4\theta)]=\operatorname{Re}(B^2\overline C)/4\). Subtracting \(105Q^2\) and using
\[
|aB+\overline B C|^2=a^2U+UV+2a\operatorname{Re}(B^2\overline C)
\]
proves the exact nonnegative identity in the Finding. Since \(a^4\) and \(U^2\) have strictly positive coefficients, equality at the lower bound forces \(a=0\) and \(B=0\). Conversely, these conditions make every term zero. With \(C\ne0\) they describe exactly the real and imaginary parts of the fourth power of \(X+iY\), up to real linear combination and nonzero scaling.

**Second, the upper endpoint.** Write \(F=I_4(f)\) as a fourth multiple Wiener integral of a real symmetric tensor \(f\in(\mathbb R^2)^{\odot4}\). Its variance is \(24\|f\|^2\). The multiple-integral product formula and the orthogonality of Wiener chaoses give
\[
\mathbb E[F^4]=\sum_{r=0}^{4}\bigl(r!\tbinom4r^2\bigr)^2(8-2r)!\,\|\widetilde{f\otimes_r f}\|^2.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz for contractions and contractivity of symmetrization give \(\|\widetilde{f\otimes_r f}\|\le\|f\|^2\). Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E[F^4]\le(40320+184320+124416+18432+576)\|f\|^4
=368064\|f\|^4=639Q^2.
\]
The individual coefficients after division by \(24^2\) are \(70,320,216,32,1\). Rank-one tensors \(f=\lambda v^{\otimes4}\) realize equality in every contraction and correspond to \(F=\lambda H_4(v_1X+v_2Y)\) for unit \(v\). Conversely, upper equality forces \(\|f\otimes_3f\|=\|f\|^2\). Viewing \(f\) as a matrix from the symmetric third tensor power to \(\mathbb R^2\), the contraction \(f\otimes_3f\) is a positive-semidefinite Gram matrix. Equality in its Hilbert–Schmidt norm-versus-trace inequality forces matrix rank one. Full symmetry of \(f\) then forces \(f\) itself to be a fourth tensor power. This exhausts all upper extremizers.

## Relationship to prior work

Nualart and Peccati (2005) established the fourth-moment criterion for Gaussian convergence in fixed Wiener chaos via contractions; they did not supply the sharp dimension-two, order-four lower endpoint or the displayed five-term deficit. Malicet, Nourdin, Peccati and Poly (2015) proved a general inequality for products of squares of chaotic random variables; specializing all factors to one variable yields a substantially weaker fourth-moment lower bound than \(105\), with no binary fourth-chaos equality classification. Latała's moment estimates (2005 preprint, 2006 journal) give order-dependent two-sided comparison constants for Gaussian chaoses rather than this exact fixed-dimensional extremal interval. The standard contraction method already yields the upper endpoint \(639\), which is included for a complete norm-range statement, not claimed as a new general inequality. The exact lower identity is a separate finite-dimensional conclusion and is not a named assertion in the primary sections inspected.

## Limitations

The sharp lower bound and deficit identity are proved only for the real fourth Wiener chaos generated by **two** independent standard Gaussian variables. They do not extend here to arbitrary Gaussian dimension, other chaos orders, or complex Gaussian coefficients. The norm bound at the upper endpoint follows from classical contraction theory. The literature comparison cannot rule out every unindexed historical computation of a binary quartic Gaussian moment. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
