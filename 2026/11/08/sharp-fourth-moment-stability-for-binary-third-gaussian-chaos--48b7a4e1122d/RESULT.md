# Sharp fourth-moment stability for binary third Gaussian chaos

## Finding
Let \(X,Y\) be independent standard real Gaussian random variables, with product Gaussian law \(\gamma_2\). Let \(\mathcal C_3\) be the four-dimensional, real third Wiener chaos in \(L^2(\gamma_2)\), i.e. the eigenspace of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator \(\Delta-x\cdot\nabla\) with eigenvalue \(-3\). For every nonzero \(F\in\mathcal C_3\),
\[
\boxed{30\le \frac{\mathbb E F^4}{(\mathbb E F^2)^2}\le93.}
\]
The lower bound is attained exactly by nonzero homogeneous harmonic cubics, equivalently by nonzero real linear combinations of \(X^3-3XY^2\) and \(3X^2Y-Y^3\). The upper bound is attained exactly by nonzero multiples of \(H_3(v_1X+v_2Y)\), where \(v_1^2+v_2^2=1\) and \(H_3(t)=t^3-3t\). In particular, the best \(L^4/L^2\) constants are the fourth roots of \(30\) and \(93\).

More precisely, let \(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\) be the \(L^2(\gamma_2)\)-orthogonal projection from \(\mathcal C_3\) onto the two-dimensional subspace generated by \(X(X^2+Y^2-4)\) and \(Y(X^2+Y^2-4)\). The **sharp transverse stability constant** is
\[
\boxed{\lambda_*=72-8\sqrt{39}>0},
\]
and every \(F\in\mathcal C_3\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E F^4-30(\mathbb E F^2)^2
\;\ge\;\lambda_*(\mathbb E F^2)\,\mathbb E[(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}F)^2].}
\]
Besides the pure harmonic cubics, equality in this stability inequality occurs precisely for mixed components of positive \(L^2\)-norms \(a,b\), with relative angular phase \(\pi\) and
\[
\frac ab=\frac{2\sqrt{39}-9}{5\sqrt3}.
\]
These are exactly the nontrivial equality cases showing optimality of \(\lambda_*\).

## Assumptions and scope
All coefficients and functions are real; moments are with respect to standard two-dimensional Gaussian probability. Set \(r^2=x^2+y^2\) and define the orthonormal basis
\[
\begin{aligned}
U(x,y)&=\frac{x^3-3xy^2}{\sqrt{24}},&
V(x,y)&=\frac{3x^2y-y^3}{\sqrt{24}},\\
W(x,y)&=\frac{x(r^2-4)}{\sqrt8},&
Z(x,y)&=\frac{y(r^2-4)}{\sqrt8}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(\operatorname{span}\{W,Z\}\); its complementary space \(\operatorname{span}\{U,V\}\) is the harmonic-cubic subspace. If \(F\ne0\), write
\[
F=a(\cos\alpha\,U+\sin\alpha\,V)+b(\cos\beta\,W+\sin\beta\,Z)
\]
with \(a,b\ge0\), and arbitrary phases when a component vanishes. Then \(\mathbb E F^2=a^2+b^2\), \(\mathbb E[(\Pi_{\mathrm{rad}}F)^2]=b^2\), and the invariant relative phase is \(\delta=\alpha-3\beta\).

## Proof
**1. Complete orthogonal decomposition.** Each displayed basis element solves \((\Delta-x\cdot\nabla)F=-3F\); direct Gaussian integration gives squared norm one and pairwise orthogonality. The third-chaos dimension in two Gaussian variables is four, so these form a basis. The first two are harmonic homogeneous cubics, whereas the latter two are trace-type Wick cubics.

**2. Exact quartic identity.** Using Gaussian polar coordinates \((R\cos\theta,R\sin\theta)\), the angle is uniform and independent of \(R\), and \(\mathbb E R^{2j}=2^j j!\). The unnormalized angular factors are \(R^3\cos3\theta\) and \(R(R^2-4)\cos\theta\). Angular integration, with \(\cos^3\theta=(3\cos\theta+\cos3\theta)/4\), yields the exact normalized contractions
\[
\mathbb E U^4=30,\quad\mathbb E W^4=66,\quad
\mathbb E U^2W^2=28,\quad\mathbb E UW^3=10\sqrt3,\quad\mathbb E U^3W=0.
\]
Rotational symmetry removes the remaining mixed terms. Consequently, for **every** real \(F\in\mathcal C_3\),
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E F^4=30a^4+168a^2b^2+66b^4+40\sqrt3\,ab^3\cos\delta.}\tag{1}
\]
The equality is an identity on the whole four-dimensional space, not a finite-sample approximation.

**3. Sharp lower bound and stability.** Subtracting \(30(a^2+b^2)^2\) from (1) gives
\[
\mathbb E F^4-30(a^2+b^2)^2
=b^2\left(108a^2+36b^2+40\sqrt3\,ab\cos\delta\right).
\]
For \(a,b\ge0\), the parenthesis is at least the Rayleigh quotient numerator of
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}108&-20\sqrt3\\-20\sqrt3&36\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are \(72\pm8\sqrt{39}\), hence the parenthesis is at least \(\lambda_*(a^2+b^2)\). Because \(\lambda_*>0\), the lower fourth-moment endpoint is reached precisely at \(b=0\). For \(b>0\), equality in the stability estimate requires \(\cos\delta=-1\) and the positive least-eigenvalue eigenvector of \(M\), namely \(a/b=(2\sqrt{39}-9)/(5\sqrt3)\). Thus the stability coefficient is attained and cannot be increased.

**4. Sharp upper bound.** Equation (1) and \(\cos\delta\le1\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
93(a^2+b^2)^2-\mathbb E F^4
&\ge 63a^4+18a^2b^2+27b^4-40\sqrt3\,ab^3\\
&=63\left(a-\frac b{\sqrt3}\right)^2
\left(a^2+\frac{2ab}{\sqrt3}+\frac{9b^2}{7}\right)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last quadratic factor is strictly positive unless \(a=b=0\). For nonzero \(F\), equality requires \(b>0\), \(a=b/\sqrt3\), and \(\cos\delta=1\). These phase and amplitude conditions are equivalent to \(F\) being a nonzero scalar multiple of \(H_3(v\cdot(X,Y))\) for a unit vector \(v\) (choose \(v=(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)\)). This proves the upper endpoint and completes the equality classification.

## Verification
A standalone Python 3 script, `check_binary_cubic.py`, verifies the four Gaussian second moments and all cross moments exactly. It independently expands the fourth-moment polynomial in the four **unnormalized** basis coefficients and checks all 35 possible quartic coefficient positions against the formula derived from (1). All arithmetic is integral or rational; there is no numerical quadrature, simulation, or external package. The script also checks the upper-endpoint factorization after an exact rational substitution and the characteristic polynomial of the stability matrix. Run `python3 check_binary_cubic.py` beside this document. The analytical proof supplies the universal quantifiers and equality cases; finite tests alone would not.

## Relationship to prior work
The fourth-moment theorem of Nualart and Peccati (2005) characterizes Gaussian convergence in fixed Wiener chaos by fourth moments and contractions, but does not state a sharp fixed-two-coordinate cubic moment range or transverse stability constant. Schulte and Thäle (2014) refine fourth-moment convergence with cumulant deviation bounds, not the finite-dimensional exact extremizers. Malicet, Nourdin, Peccati and Poly (2015) establish broad product-moment inequalities for chaotic random variables; their stated inequalities do not imply the exact binary cubic fourth-moment identity or the sharp eigenvalue stability remainder. The upper example \(H_3(G)\) and its fourth moment are classical; the contribution here is the complete two-dimensional **lower** endpoint, its global extension to all trace-containing third-chaos elements, and the best transverse stability constant.

A previously established sharp fourth-moment theorem for *homogeneous cubic spherical harmonics on the two-sphere*, restricted to the two-coordinate harmonic subspace, can account for the value \(30\) **within that harmonic subspace**. It does not cover the nonhomogeneous Wick-cubic radial component, the global four-dimensional minimum, or the exact \(\lambda_*\) stability remainder. For example, its value \(350/143\) for \(x(x^2-3y^2)\) on \(\mathbb S^2\) converts to the Gaussian value \(30\) by multiplication by the third-dimensional radial factor \(135135/11025\). This distinction is essential: a special-case moment value is not presented as an independent contribution.

## Limitations
This theorem is restricted to real two-coordinate **third Wiener chaos** with the standard Gaussian law and fourth moments. It does not classify the analogous sharp minima for arbitrary Gaussian dimension or chaos order, nor does it assert a new general upper fourth-moment principle. The reported originality concerns the precise combined lower-extremal/stability statement, subject to the possibility of an unindexed equivalent classical invariant-theoretic computation. Independent mathematical validation is not asserted.
