# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Independent-sign expansion gives the exact second and fourth moments. Strict convexity under collisions reduces maxima to matchings; a one-variable mass convexity calculation settles all ranks. A total-variance identity proves the lower bound and classifies equality. Double signed-permutation symmetry proves the exact Euclidean distortion. Exact rational group enumeration and defect checks corroborate the proof for finite test cases, without being used as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Published work on generalized Ewens permutation matrices and circle-wreath-product spectral traces does not address arbitrary real coefficient matrices or sharp optimization over their signs and supports. The Coxeter-statistic paper provides the group realization but a different invariant. Centered ordinary permutation Weingarten calculus can yield moment tensors, not a stated sharp signed-group norm classification. The closest related coefficient calculation for the symmetric group imposes rank-one, zero-sum coefficient factors and lacks independent signs; its claims neither dominate nor imply the present result. Searches of public mathematical records and prior claim statements found no equivalent sharp result. A specialized historical theorem using alternate group-representation terminology could still overlap.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact norm and optimal Euclidean distortion hold simultaneously for all ranks. The rank-four equality transition is structural: small ranks favor diffuse complete matchings, rank four has a continuum of matching amplitudes, and larger ranks localize to one matrix entry. The explicit signed and permutation variance decompositions quantify why the lower endpoint has star support. These are natural invariants of the full defining matrix-coefficient space, not an arbitrary restricted parameter slice.

## Limitations
Real coefficients and full uniform signed permutations only; no claim for even-signed subgroups, complex coefficients, biased laws, or higher exponents. The finite checker is a consistency test and is not a substitute for the analytical proof. The bibliographic comparison cannot certify uniqueness against unindexed literature.
