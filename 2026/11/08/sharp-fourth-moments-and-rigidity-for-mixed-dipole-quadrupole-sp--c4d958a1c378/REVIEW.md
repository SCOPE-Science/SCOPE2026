# Scientific review — mixed dipole–quadrupole fourth moments

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is an exact statement over all real coefficients \(v\in\mathbb R^3\), trace-free symmetric \(A\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\), with \(f\ne0\). Spherical Gaussian radial factoring gives the second and fourth moment expressions, including the mixed invariant \(|Av|^2\). The trace-free identity \(\operatorname{tr}(A^4)=\operatorname{tr}(A^2)^2/2\) holds in exactly three dimensions. Both quartic deficits are verified algebraically and are nonnegative, and eigenvalue equality identifies all minimizers and maximizers. Exact rational monomial integrations and independent quadrature reproduce the formula and witnesses. The finite checks supplement, rather than replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk

The closest published exact result located concerns pure degree-two harmonics only (published-finding corpus record `d4106097159f`); its constant \(15/7\) corresponds to setting the dipole coefficient to zero, while the present sharp endpoints \(45/28\) and \(375/112\) lie strictly on opposite sides and require both degrees. The material inspected from Marzo–Ortega-Cerdà (arXiv:math/0703588), Sogge–Zelditch (arXiv:1011.0215), and Dai–Feng–Tikhonov (arXiv:1408.1877) concerns general sampling/norm comparison, eigenfunction moments, or sharp asymptotic orders for fixed-degree harmonics—not the exact coupled two-sided moment range. Search failure alone is not proof of novelty. An old equivalent result under multipole terminology remains possible; Stanton–Weinstein (1981) was examined at abstract level only.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first nonconstant bandlimited spherical polynomial space \(\mathcal H_1\oplus\mathcal H_2\), of real dimension eight, is a natural canonical object. Its complete sharp \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) range does not follow by combining separate degree-one and degree-two constants: the mixed covariance invariant dictates opposite geometry at the two endpoints. The identities quantify both energy-split and angular/spectral departure from extremality and can be reused as exact stability and conditioning benchmarks. The result is a complete natural classification, not an arbitrary finite evaluation.

## Limitations and risks

The result is restricted to normalized uniform surface measure on \(\mathbb S^2\), real mean-zero degree-at-most-two functions, and exponent four. It makes no claim for the complex space, for constants, or for higher degree/dimension. The originality comparison includes inspected primary preprints and the closest public fixed-degree theorem but cannot exclude every older or unindexed publication. Independent validation has not been performed.
