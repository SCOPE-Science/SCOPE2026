# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof specifies the uniform probability measure on the finite symmetric group, real nonzero centered coefficient vectors, and a homogeneous fourth-to-second moment ratio. The partition-of-four expansion supplies the exact fourth moment, the second moment is independently checked, and a separate three-coordinate smoothing argument proves that extrema of the zero-sum fourth-power quotient have two levels. A bilinear rectangle comparison yields the unique boundary maximizers for every dimension. Direct exact-rational permutations for the small cases and an independent standalone checker support, but do not replace, the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The nearest classical source (Barbour–Janson, arXiv:0907.0347v1, Introduction and Theorem 2.1) concerns asymptotic combinatorial permutation statistics and a second-moment formula, not exact rank-one fourth-moment maximizers. Filmus–Kindler–Lifshitz–Minzer (arXiv:2009.05503, Sections 1 and 6) treat global hypercontractivity for permutations, not this sharp localized rank-one moment and its complete small-dimensional classification. A published finite abelian co-singleton Fourier result proves the same numerical fourth-power constant for the mean-zero hyperplane, and its proof covers the separate fourth-power lemma used here; that claim is explicitly excluded from the novelty assertion. Its domain consists of \(n\) points rather than permutation coefficients on \(n!\) points and does not imply the permutation moment identity or dimension-four exception. Current searches have not identified a direct rank-one permutation counterpart; historical unindexed coverage remains a risk.

## Value — PASS
Rank-one coefficients are the canonical elementary matrix coefficients of the standard representation of the symmetric group. Their sharp \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) amplification is a natural finite-dimensional invariant pertinent to permutation hypercontractivity and to the tails of Hoeffding statistics. The complete real all-dimension classification, including the nontrivial balanced-to-single-outlier switch between dimensions four and five, goes beyond a stand-alone power-sum expansion. It neither claims a theorem about all degree-one functions nor a general tail inequality.

## Limits and residual risks
Only real rank-one coefficients and fourth moments are addressed. Two ingredients are pre-existing mathematics; the new claim is the combined sharp permutation statement. A 1979 sample-kurtosis paper was readable only as an abstract. An equivalent earlier statement under statistical, Fourier, or representation-theoretic terminology cannot be excluded; no independent audit was performed.
