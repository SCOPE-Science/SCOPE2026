# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For every integer \(n\ge5\) and positive height \(h\), the proof identifies the full-vertex event with a singleton block of negative side-facet normals. The normal-fan silhouette criterion and the Gaussian triple-sign formula produce the exact arcsine law; a derivative identity establishes the unique optimizer; a uniform local Taylor expansion establishes both asymptotics. Numerical checks independently reconstruct convex hull shadows for multiple side counts and height ratios, and high-precision calculations check the asymptotic coefficient. Numerical sampling is not an infinite proof. No degenerate viewing directions are included in the probability event.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional
Black and Criado's *Random Shadows of Fixed Polytopes* (arXiv:2406.06936; initial public version June 11, 2024) gives expectation bounds for random projected polytopes, rather than a maximal-shadow probability or optimizing-height formula for regular polygonal pyramids. The published full *Exact five-vertex shadow law and unique height optimum for right rectangular pyramids* treats the adjacent four-base-vertex case, which is excluded from the new \(n\ge5\) theorem. Unlike that case, \(n\ge5\) admits three or more silhouette types and requires a singleton normal-cone calculation. The closest full texts were inspected, and semantic searches of alternative formulations, broader results, exact tables and logical implications found no dominating coverage. Residual risk: an unindexed earlier equivalent spherical-geometric calculation remains possible; a failed search does not prove novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A maximally complex planar shadow is a standard, motivated geometric-probability event. The all-side-count formula and strict unique height optimum show a structural relation between pyramid shape and retention of vertices. The sharp limit \(n^2p_n(h_n^*)\to2\pi^2/(3\sqrt3)\), together with the finite-n optimizer and fixed-height profile, identifies an unexpected shrinking \(n^{-2}\) likelihood that is not extractable from general expected-shadow-size bounds. The restriction to regular bases is a meaningful coherent family, not an arbitrary finite cutoff.

## Limitations and source comparison
The proof does not classify all shadow vertex counts, optimize over nonregular pyramids, or treat perspective views. The 2024 preprint's 2026 journal date was not used as its first public date. A 2012 paper on cube and tetrahedron projections was consulted at abstract level only and was not treated as full-text evidence of absence. The publication record for right rectangular pyramids was read in full; it covers the excluded four-base-vertex case. Independent mathematical auditing has not occurred.
