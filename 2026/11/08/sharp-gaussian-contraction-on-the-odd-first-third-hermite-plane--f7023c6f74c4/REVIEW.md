# Scientific review: sharp Gaussian contraction on the odd first–third Hermite plane

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses the exact Gaussian mixed fourth moments \((3,6,42,324,3348)\), derived from the double-factorial rule, and the full rational stationary polynomial for the normalized semigroup quotient. Its quartic discriminant reduces to a degree-six polynomial with no positive zeros by a rational Sturm chain, proving that the positive critical ratio is unique for every positive semigroup parameter. Independent polynomial resultant and Sturm computations establish uniqueness of the contractivity threshold in the required interval. The strict all-input Nelson-radius comparison is certified by a sum of two positive quadratics. The packaged checker reproduces all critical identities with exact arithmetic; sample isolations are diagnostics only.

## Originality — PASS, with specified risks

Bakry–Bolley–Gentil (arXiv:1003.5072, first public March 26, 2010) gives general Gaussian semigroup hypercontractivity and dimension-dependent bounds, whereas van Neerven–Portal (arXiv:1702.03602) treats general Gaussian \(L^p\)-to-\(L^q\) mapping and complex time. Inspection of their relevant primary sections found neither the sharp first–third Hermite coefficient ratio nor the algebraic contraction threshold. An earlier exact sixth-moment problem on the same Hermite plane concerns a different output exponent, and a previously established first–second-chaos semigroup bound covers a different input space; neither implies the present theorem. Targeted searches for spectral-masked variants, mixed fourth moments, algebraic coefficients, and exact tables did not locate covering literature, but failed searches alone are not proof of novelty. The older Weissler paper (DOI:10.1016/0022-1236(79)90080-6) could not be read in full; its abstract treats full semigroup mapping conditions and remains a bibliographic risk, along with unindexed equivalent calculations.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first and third Hermite modes are the smallest natural odd Gaussian spectral pair containing a nonlinear level. The result determines the complete semigroup norm profile and rigid extremizers, and identifies the exact threshold for \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) contractivity. Its contraction radius exceeds the unrestricted Nelson radius, while both eigenmodes participate in every optimizer at every positive parameter. The distinction between full-space and spectral-restricted smoothing is meaningful for studying invariant Gaussian chaos subspaces.

## Scope and limits

The proof concerns real coefficients, one standard Gaussian variable, odd Hermite degrees one and three, and output exponent four. No higher odd levels, general spectral masks, other exponents, or complex-time theorem is claimed. A primary 1979 full-text comparison was inaccessible, so literature priority is qualified rather than certified. Numerical approximations do not replace the exact algebraic definitions.
