# Review of the cyclic dinucleotide ambiguity peak

## Correctness
PASS. Each nonconstant binary cyclic pair-count profile corresponds to a pair of weak compositions modulo cyclic permutation of run pairs. Burnside gives an exact count. The nonidentity terms are exponentially negligible compared with the balanced peak. Entropy strictly localizes the global maximum; uniform binomial Stirling estimates give the Gaussian and its exact constant. A sharp global upper bound uses the maximum fair-binomial point probability and a matching balanced integer construction. A separate exhaustive literal-word checker corroborates all profiles for lengths 1–15; this is not substituted for the proof.

## Originality
PASS, with qualified historical priority. Whittle's exact Markov transition-total enumeration is an antecedent to the count formula and is not claimed as new. Jacquet, Knessl and Szpankowski (2010) count the number of distinct cyclic types, not the largest number of necklaces belonging to one type or the uniform centered Gaussian law. The closest published-finding corpus source treats linear, not cyclic, length-two multispectra. Material read included the full open 2010 paper and full published linear result. Whittle (1955) full text was inaccessible: the extent of its explicit asymptotics remains a residual originality risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. A cyclic two-mer profile models a very short-read circular-string reconstruction channel. The theorem gives a sharp asymptotic worst-case ambiguity with the leading constant and describes how that ambiguity falls away from balanced pair counts, which neither a table nor a global number-of-profile formula supplies.

## Scope and limitations
Binary cyclic words are compared only up to rotation; they are not identified under reversal or complementation. Exact finite-length maximizers and higher-gram alphabets are not classified. No independent specialist review has occurred.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
