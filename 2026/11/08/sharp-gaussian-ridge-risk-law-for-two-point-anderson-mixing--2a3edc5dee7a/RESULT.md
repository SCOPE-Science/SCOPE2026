# Sharp Gaussian ridge-risk law for two-point Anderson mixing

## Finding

A symmetric ridge makes the two-point scalar Anderson error have finite moments of every positive order under independent Gaussian evaluation noise. The mean-square error has a sharp inverse-square-root blow-up as the ridge vanishes. On the noise-variance scale, the fourth-order correction gives an explicit and unique calibration among fixed positive scale factors when the trial midpoint is offset from the fixed point.

Write \(R_\sigma(\lambda)\) for the expected squared error at ridge \(\lambda>0\). With contraction \(r\), trial gap \(d\), and midpoint offset \(\theta\) as specified below, define
\[
p_\sigma(0)=\frac{\exp(-(1-r)^2d^2/(4\sigma^2))}{2\sigma\sqrt\pi},\qquad m_2=(1-r)^2\theta^2+\sigma^2/2.
\]
At each fixed \(\sigma>0\),
\[
\lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}\sqrt\lambda\,R_\sigma(\lambda)=\frac{\pi d^2}{2}p_\sigma(0)m_2>0.
\]
For \(\theta\ne0\) and fixed \(c>0\), as \(\sigma\downarrow0\),
\[
R_\sigma(c\sigma^2)=\frac{d^2+4\theta^2}{2d^2(1-r)^2}\sigma^2+Q(c)\sigma^4+o(\sigma^4),
\qquad
Q(c)=\frac{r^2\theta^2c^2-[rd^2+(12r+4)\theta^2]c+3(d^2+12\theta^2)}{d^4(1-r)^4}.
\]
The unique minimizer of this fourth-order coefficient over \(c>0\) is
\[
c_*=\frac{rd^2+(12r+4)\theta^2}{2r^2\theta^2}.
\]
This is not a claim of finite-noise optimality over arbitrary \(\lambda\).

## Assumptions and scope

Fix \(x_*\in\mathbb R\), \(0<r<1\), \(d>0\), \(\theta\in\mathbb R\), and \(\sigma>0\). The deterministic sites are \(x_0=x_*+\theta-d/2\) and \(x_1=x_*+\theta+d/2\). Observe \(Y_j=x_*+r(x_j-x_*)+\varepsilon_j\) with independent \(N(0,\sigma^2)\) errors. Put \(F_j=Y_j-x_j\). The output \(Z_\lambda=(1/2+\gamma)Y_0+(1/2-\gamma)Y_1\) uses the unique minimizer of
\[
[(1/2+\gamma)F_0+(1/2-\gamma)F_1]^2+\lambda\gamma^2.
\]
A penalty \(\eta(w_0^2+w_1^2)\) on the two mixing weights gives the same construction with \(\lambda=2\eta\). The loss is \(R_\sigma(\lambda)=\mathbb E[(Z_\lambda-x_*)^2]\). The sites are fixed, not generated by a noisy iteration.

## Proof

Set \(b=(1-r)\theta\), \(t_0=-(1-r)d\), \(W=b-(\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_1)/2\), and \(T=t_0+\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_0\). The Gaussian sum and difference are independent; \(W\) has variance \(\sigma^2/2\) and \(T\) variance \(2\sigma^2\). Their role in the objective is \((-W-\gamma T)^2+\lambda\gamma^2\), which gives \(\gamma=-WT/(T^2+\lambda)\). Since \(Y_0-Y_1=-(T+d)\), the exact error and risk are
\[
E_\lambda=\theta+Wh_\lambda(T),\qquad h_\lambda(t)=\frac{dt-\lambda}{t^2+\lambda},\qquad
R_\sigma(\lambda)=\mathbb E[(\theta+bh_\lambda(T))^2]+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\mathbb E[h_\lambda(T)^2].
\]
The bound \(|h_\lambda(t)|\le 1+d/(2\sqrt\lambda)\) proves finiteness of each positive moment.

For fixed \(\sigma>0\), write \(h_\lambda=u_\lambda+v_\lambda\) with \(u_\lambda(t)=dt/(t^2+\lambda)\) and \(|v_\lambda(t)|\le1\). If \(p_\sigma\) is the density of \(T\), substitution \(t=\sqrt\lambda s\) and dominated convergence give
\[
\sqrt\lambda\,\mathbb E[u_\lambda(T)^2]
\longrightarrow d^2p_\sigma(0)\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{s^2}{(1+s^2)^2}\,ds=\frac{\pi d^2}{2}p_\sigma(0).
\]
The bounded term and the cross term in \(h_\lambda^2\), as well as the terms of degree at most one in \(h_\lambda\) in the risk formula, vanish after multiplication by \(\sqrt\lambda\): Cauchy–Schwarz bounds each unbounded lower-order term by \(O(\lambda^{-1/4})\). The coefficient of \(\mathbb E[h_\lambda^2]\) is \(m_2\).

For the small-noise expansion put \(D_\lambda(t)=\theta+bh_\lambda(t)\), \(H(t)=d/t\), and \(J(t)=\partial_\lambda h_\lambda(t)|_{\lambda=0}=-t^{-2}-dt^{-3}\). On a fixed symmetric neighborhood of \(t_0\) away from zero, all needed derivatives are uniformly bounded for small \(\lambda=c\sigma^2\); the Gaussian probability of its complement is exponentially small, and the bound on \(h_\lambda\) controls that complement by a polynomial in \(1/\sigma\) times the Gaussian tail. Thus the Gaussian Taylor rule there, \(\mathbb E f(T)=f(t_0)+\sigma^2 f''(t_0)+\frac12\sigma^4 f^{(4)}(t_0)+o(\sigma^4)\), applies to the risk terms, uniformly for \(c\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\). The coefficients depending on \(\lambda\) can therefore be Taylor-expanded at zero locally. In particular \(D_0(t_0)=0\) and \(D_\lambda(t_0)=r\theta\lambda/(t_0^2+\lambda)\).

The coefficient of \(\sigma^2\) is \(2b^2(H')^2+H^2/2\) evaluated at \(t_0\). The coefficient of \(\sigma^4\), with the same evaluation convention, is
\[
\frac{r^2\theta^2}{t_0^4}c^2+c\,[4b^2H'J'+2b^2JH''+HJ]+3b^2(H'')^2+4b^2H'H'''+\tfrac12(H^2)''.
\]
Inserting \(H,J,b,t_0\) yields the displayed \(Q(c)\). Its positive quadratic coefficient for \(\theta\ne0\) gives the unique positive minimizer \(c_*\).

## Verification

The supplied `verify.py` uses Python 3, NumPy, SciPy, and SymPy (`python verify.py`), comparing the update algebra and Taylor coefficients and testing Gaussian integration and the ridge scaling. The stored output reports 155 assertions. The script also checks unregularized Gaussian ratio behavior as a baseline. The limiting claims for positive ridge follow from the proof, not finite quadrature or random trials. For \(r=1/2\) and \(\theta=d=1\), the formulas give \(c_*=21\) and \(R_\sigma(21\sigma^2)=10\sigma^2-1140\sigma^4+o(\sigma^4)\).

## Relationship to prior work and limitations

The unregularized ratio law, exact reciprocal-tail constant, and moment cutoff in this independent Gaussian model already follow by taking correlation zero in [Gaussian coupling phase diagram for depth-one Anderson extrapolation](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/11/2/SCOPE-gaussian-coupling-phase-diagram-for-depth-one-anderson-extrapola--30f28a658eb2), items 1–2. Its variables map to the present ones by taking its contraction deficit to be \(1-r\), its midpoint offset to be \(\theta\), and its ratio variables to be \(W,T\). The cutoff also follows from the scalar depth-one case of the [Gaussian moment threshold for noisy Anderson extrapolation](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/10/26/SCOPE-sharp-gaussian-moment-threshold-for-noisy-anderson-extrapolation--6e96c99497d7). These unregularized facts motivate the use of a ridge; they are not part of the finding here.

Scieur, d'Aspremont and Bach, [Nonlinear Acceleration of Stochastic Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.07270), use coefficient-norm regularization and bound the effect of noise for stochastic accelerated iterates. The results here evaluate exact leading constants in a fixed-site one-step model. Related analyses include Toth et al., [Local Improvement Results for Anderson Acceleration with Inaccurate Function Evaluations](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1080677), which studies inaccurate iterative evaluations; Wei, Bao and Liu, [Stochastic Anderson Mixing for Nonconvex Stochastic Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01543); and Pollock and Rebholz, [Filtering for Anderson Acceleration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12953). The sharp limits do not cover adaptive iterations, correlated or non-Gaussian noise, multidimensional fixed-point maps, or a global small-noise minimization over arbitrary penalty scales.
