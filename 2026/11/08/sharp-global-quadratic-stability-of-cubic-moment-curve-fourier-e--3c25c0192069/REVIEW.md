# Scientific review: sharp cubic moment-curve stability

## Correctness
PASS. The claimed global coefficient \(5-4/q\), with its quantifiers over all finite fields of characteristic greater than three and all nonzero Fourier inputs, follows from the exact sixth-moment multiset identity. The three-draw expectation is \(F_3(\theta)=6-9s_2+4s_3\). The deficit splits into the asserted squared-distance term plus \(4[(q-2)e_2-3qe_3]/q\), which is nonnegative by normalized Newton and Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities. Point masses attain the global ratio, proving optimality. Equality is uniform amplitude or one-point support. Exact rational and direct Fourier tests corroborate but do not replace this proof.

## Originality
PASS relative to the inspected accessible statements. The 2026 moment-curve extension preprint establishes uniform maximization and the probability reduction, while the contemporaneous permutation-match preprint strengthens qualitative majorization. The closest published quantitative result proves a nonoptimal general coefficient \(d(d-1)/2\) (equal to \(3\) in this instance), and the separate Hessian result gives local curvature but does not establish the global optimal coefficient. Neither statement dominates the present exact coefficient \(5-4/q\), its nonnegative decomposition, or its two equality strata. Full texts of the two 2026 primary preprints were unavailable, so an unlocated equivalent calculation remains a priority risk.

## Value
PASS. Quantitative near-extremizer stability is a natural next question after a sharp extension theorem. The cubic case is the first genuinely nonlinear rearrangement functional; its exact best global coefficient and boundary equality differ substantially from local curvature and strictly improve the known general quadratic bound. This is an exact stability invariant for an infinite field family, not an arbitrary finite enumeration. The classical Newton inequality is used as a tool rather than claimed as a discovery.

Closest records are the Biswas et al. endpoint result (arXiv:2609.29882v1, 2026-09-24), Gonçalves' component-wise inequality (arXiv:2609.31979v1, 2026-09-25), and the published mathematical findings *Quadratic stability for endpoint finite-field moment-curve extension* and *Isotropic quadratic stability at the uniform finite-field moment-curve extremizer* (both October 2026). The claim does not address higher degree, characteristic two or three, Euclidean restriction, or phase stability. Both primary 2026 manuscripts were accessible only through abstracts and secondary exposition, not full text.
