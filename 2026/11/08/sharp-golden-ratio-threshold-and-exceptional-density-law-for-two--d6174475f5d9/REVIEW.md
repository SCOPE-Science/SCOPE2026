A second inverse exists precisely when the fractional part of its normalized first inverse exceeds \(1-1/\alpha\). Splitting \(\{v+(1-u)/\alpha\}\) before and after its only possible carry gives two disjoint branches, one identical to the published range test and the other an explicit triangle. The triangle is empty exactly at and above \(\varphi\). Under rational independence Kronecker–Weyl equidistribution converts its area to the stated density. The direct finite verification independently compares the exact range to both branches; no finite test is used as an infinite proof. All domain restrictions, strict inequalities, and quadratic exceptions have been addressed.
