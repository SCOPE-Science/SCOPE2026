# Mathematical review of the adjacent-degree hemispherical concentration theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The Hermitian concentration operator splits into explicit two-dimensional signed-order blocks plus two singletons. Exact associated-Legendre Sturm--Liouville boundary calculation yields all off-diagonal entries. The highest-order block is globally largest by log-convexity of \(F_n\) and the strict Wallis comparison for \(G_n\). The full equality spaces, multiplicities, monotone sharp constant, and asymptotic follow algebraically. Independent quadrature reproduces 496 block coefficients through degree 30. The test is corroborative, not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority).** Primary Slepian literature gives the general operator and its matrix integrals, but not the explicit two-adjacent-degree eigenvalues or their strict highest-order maximality. Jaming--Speckbacher's 2020 estimates concern different, generally low-pass and nonsharp, concentration parameters. The closest publicly available half-energy multiplicity result does not specify noncentral singular values or an optimizer. Specific keyword, formulation, and implication comparisons did not find a covering result. The general Slepian formalism makes the special case accessible, so an unlocated specialized calculation remains a risk.

**Value — PASS.** A two-eigenvalue spectral shell is the minimal nontrivial frequency band permitting unequal hemisphere energy. This theorem determines the exact best possible concentration and every achieving mode at all degrees (highest-order Gaussian-beam pairs except for the constant-plus-dipole lowest-degree case), the complete concentration spectrum, and the sharp high-frequency barrier \(\frac12+1/\sqrt{2\pi}<1\). This is a natural quantitative bandpass uncertainty result, not a restatement of the known half-energy eigenvalue count or a finite benchmark.

The result concerns only the two-adjacent-degree scalar band on \(S^2\); no other cap, general mask, or independent validation is asserted.
