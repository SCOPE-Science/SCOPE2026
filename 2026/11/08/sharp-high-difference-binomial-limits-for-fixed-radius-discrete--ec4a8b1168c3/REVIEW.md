# Review of the high-difference smoothing law

## Correctness
PASS. Fourier-Plancherel, the exact change of variables and fixed-degree coefficient compactness prove both limits. The positivity constraint survives coefficientwise limits. Uniqueness and alternation of the monic half-line weighted extremizer establish the strict gap, while uniformly exponentially suppressed polynomial tails localize the Nyquist extremum. The three-contact quadratic and the square-domination argument prove the two radius-two constants. The stored checker reports finite numerical corroboration; no finite run is treated as proof of an infinite limit.

## Originality
PASS relative to inspected evidence, not certified publication priority. Gaitán–Garzón–Madrid (2026) prove several exact fixed derivative orders and an exact unrestricted/Fourier-positive squaring relation; Richardson (2023/2026) studies the second derivative; Kravitz–Steinerberger (2021) treat low orders. The closest published three-point result treats only radius one. General weighted Chebyshev uniqueness/alternation is a known tool. The positive radius-two constant already follows from earlier radius-one and squaring results and is credited accordingly. The new substance is the fixed-radius high-order law, universal binomial shape, localization and evaluated **unrestricted** five-tap constant. A full-text equivalent under another weight or normalization remains a residual literature risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This gives a sharp asymptotic solution, including a natural exact leading invariant, to a missing high-order regime of a motivated Fourier smoothing problem. It distinguishes optimal signed from Fourier-positive kernels and produces a concrete quantitative five-tap positivity penalty. The limiting binomial window and frequency boundary layer explain the structure of high-order optimization for every fixed support radius, rather than computing an arbitrary isolated test case.

## Scope and limits
The radius is fixed before the derivative order tends to infinity. General leading constants are exact variational quantities but not all have closed forms; a numerical evaluation is supplied for the five-tap case. No exact finite-order formulas, joint-radius uniform estimates, or broad publication-priority certification are claimed.
