The histories and local delay equations are specified unambiguously. Exact prehistory substitution gives a sign change in the second-nested feedback precisely at \(\eta M=1\), with explicit trajectories. The global reference solution is a bounded triangle wave; all published invisibility-theorem hypotheses are verified for the strict side, including the amplitude conditions. Exact rational checks of 140 parameter triples and up to 25 nesting orders corroborate these algebraic steps. Finite testing is explicitly separate from the proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

Bolduc-St-Aubin, Subramanian and Krauskopf (arXiv:2610.39281v1, 2026), Theorem 1.1 and Section 6, already establish the strict sufficient invisibility criterion and forecast that the nesting map may cycle outside contraction. The new result does not reassert these as original: it constructs the exact clipped-history family, establishes the local failure for the equality and supercritical sides, proves an all-orders critical two-cycle, and shows that a unique implicit branch nevertheless remains invisible locally. Keane et al. (2019) and Humphries et al. (2021) study distinct state-dependent feedback dynamics, not this implication. Semantic literature searches found no equivalent claim in inspected sources. Residual risk: general dynamical-systems treatments of nonexpansive fixed-point iterations could imply the elementary two-cycle after reduction; no matching model-specific sharpness classification was identified. Worldwide priority is not independently established.

## Value — PASS

The motivating source explicitly leaves the noncontracting history regime open. The result gives an exact boundary for that open mechanism and separates three phenomena that are often conflated: invisibility of a DDE, convergence of nested delay substitution, and uniqueness of an implicit delay. It also shows robust immediate feedback reversal above the boundary with arbitrarily weak state-response coefficient, obtained by adjusting the history slope. This is a motivated boundary, not a numerical or unmotivated parameter slice.
