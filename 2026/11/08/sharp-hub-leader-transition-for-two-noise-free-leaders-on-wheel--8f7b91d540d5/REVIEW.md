# Review of the wheel two-leader classification

**Correctness — PASS.** The graph automorphisms partition leader pairs into one hub–rim orbit and rim–rim separation orbits. Block inversion gives the exact inverse-trace formula with positive Schur pivot. The tridiagonal inverse recurrence yields the Green diagonal and row-sum identities; summing their geometric tails and applying Cauchy–Schwarz gives a uniform strict bound for every rim size at least nine. Exact-rational inversion resolves all smaller sizes, and a standalone verifier independently checks 323 rational identities and leader-pair comparisons. The finite enumeration is not used to justify the infinite portion of the theorem.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual priority risk.** Inspected foundational papers arXiv:1302.0450v1, arXiv:1407.1569v1 and arXiv:1412.6595v1 give general objective formulations and other families, not this explicit three-regime wheel classification. The broader resistance formulas can evaluate any fixed candidate but do not imply the sharp all-size orbit comparison without additional analysis. Accessible records and targeted full-text searches revealed no dominating exact wheel statement, but unindexed equivalences cannot be ruled out. One earlier wheel-related research statement concerns controllability, not grounded inverse-trace optimal placement.

**Value — PASS.** With two leaders, vertex degree alone would favor the hub, but the exact optimal geometry instead chooses well-separated rim vertices on small wheels, switching to the hub only after a sharp size threshold. The finding gives all optimizers, their counts, a constructive comparison formula and a uniform infinite-family proof in a standard coherent-network control model. It is more than a direct substitution into a general selection algorithm.

Independent publication-priority assessment is not complete. Scientific scope is restricted to unweighted wheels, two noise-free leaders and the inverse trace without an extra scaling factor. The lower-bound proof is analytic; finite checks are reproducibility support.
