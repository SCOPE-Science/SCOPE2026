# Sharp inball transition for Catalan pellytopes

## Finding

Consider the Catalan pellytope of Bossinger and Gürdoğan in its specified Newton-polytope coordinates. For every pair of integers \(n\ge d\ge3\), set
\[
Q_{k,j}=\operatorname{conv}\left\{0,e_j,e_j+e_{j+1},\ldots,e_j+\cdots+e_{j+k-1}\right\},\qquad
P_{d,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k+1}Q_{k,j}\subset\mathbb R^n.
\]
With the standard Euclidean metric, its largest inscribed ball has radius
\[
\boxed{r_{d,n}=\min\left\{\frac d2,\frac{d(2n-d+1)}{2\bigl(2+(n-1)\sqrt2\bigr)}\right\}.}
\]
There is a sharp change in the geometry of the incenter set. If
\[(2-\sqrt2)(n-1)<d-1,\]
its incenter is unique and exactly \(n+1\) facets touch the ball: the upper first-coordinate facet, the lower last-coordinate facet, and every adjacent-difference facet. If
\[(2-\sqrt2)(n-1)>d-1,\]
the set of incenters has affine dimension exactly \(n-2\); there exist incenters for which only the four endpoint coordinate facets touch the ball. The equality case cannot occur for integers \(n\ge d\ge3\).

This concerns the *particular Euclidean embedding* given above, not arbitrary affine or unimodular realizations of these combinatorial polytopes.

## Assumptions and scope

An incenter is a center of a largest closed Euclidean ball contained in the full-dimensional convex body. All dimensions and norms refer to the ambient \(\mathbb R^n\). The summands are zero-inclusive staircase simplices, whose vertex coordinates are consecutive prefix indicator vectors. The parameter restriction \(d\ge3\) isolates the genuinely intermediate/new family; the earlier pellytope \(d=2\) and hypercube \(d=1\) are not claimed as new instances.

Write
\[
a_i=\min(d,i),\qquad b_i=\min(d,n-i)\quad(1\le i<n),
\]
and let \(w_i\) be the maximum possible value of coordinate \(i\) on \(P_{d,n}\). The exact coordinate widths satisfy
\[
w_1=w_n=d,\quad w_{i+1}-w_i=b_i-a_i,\quad w_i>d\;(1<i<n).
\]

## Proof

**1. Supporting hyperplanes.** The normal-ray theorem for this exact family (Bossinger--Gürdoğan, arXiv:2609.38456, Proposition 3.19, as stated in its introduction) says that the inward facet-normal rays are generated by \(\pm e_i\) and \(e_i-e_j\) for \(1\le i<j\le n\) with \(j-i\le d-1\). Thus the outward normals can be taken as \(\pm e_i\) and \(e_j-e_i\) with the same index range; there are no further facet directions.

The support function is additive over Minkowski summands. Directly from the prefix vertices,
\[
h_{P_{d,n}}(-e_i)=0,\quad h_{P_{d,n}}(e_i)=w_i,
\]
and, with \(\ell=j-i\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\),
\[
\boxed{h_{P_{d,n}}(e_j-e_i)=\sum_{t=i}^{j-1}b_t-\binom\ell2.}\tag{1}
\]
For the last identity, a summand starting at \(s\) can contribute a positive unit precisely if \(i<s\le j\) and its prefix reaches \(j\). Among the \(\min(d,n-s+1)\) permitted lengths starting at \(s\), exactly \(j-s\) are too short. Summing over \(s=i+1,\ldots,j\) gives (1). A Euclidean ball of radius \(r\) centered at \(c=(c_1,\ldots,c_n)\) is contained in the polytope precisely when
\[
r\le c_i\le w_i-r\quad(1\le i\le n),\tag{2}
\]
\[
c_j-c_i+\sqrt2 r\le\sum_{t=i}^{j-1}b_t-\binom{j-i}{2}
\quad(1\le i<j\le n,\ j-i<d).\tag{3}
\]

**2. Two universal upper bounds.** The first coordinate ranges from zero to \(d\), so (2) forces \(r\le d/2\). The adjacent instances of (3), summed from the first coordinate to the last, give
\[
c_n-c_1+(n-1)\sqrt2 r\le\sum_{t=1}^{n-1}b_t.
\]
Since \(c_1\le d-r\) and \(c_n\ge r\),
\[
\bigl(2+(n-1)\sqrt2\bigr)r\le d+\sum_{t=1}^{n-1}b_t
=\frac{d(2n-d+1)}2.\tag{4}
\]
This proves the claimed formula is an upper bound.

**3. A path-feasibility lemma.** Put \(r_*\) equal to the minimum of the two bounds in (4), and \(\theta=\sqrt2r_*\). We first check
\[
\theta>\frac{d-1}{2}.\tag{5}
\]
The bound \(d/2\) has this property. For the bound in (4), after multiplying positive denominators the claim reduces to
\[
\sqrt2\left[(d+1)n-d^2+2d-1\right]>2(d-1).
\]
Its left bracket is at least \(3d-1\), because \(n\ge d\), so the inequality holds.

By (5), every long-difference facet inequality (3) follows from the adjacent ones: summing the \(\ell\) adjacent inequalities gives
\[
c_j-c_i+\sqrt2r_*\le\sum_{t=i}^{j-1}b_t-(\ell-1)\theta
\le\sum_{t=i}^{j-1}b_t-\binom\ell2.
\]
The last inequality is strict whenever \(\ell\ge2\).

It remains to solve (2) and
\[
c_{i+1}\le c_i+b_i-\theta.\tag{6}
\]
These forward difference constraints are feasible if and only if, for every \(1\le i\le j\le n\),
\[
w_i+\sum_{t=i}^{j-1}b_t\ge\left(2+(j-i)\sqrt2\right)r_*.\tag{7}
\]
Necessity follows by chaining (6) between the box endpoints. For sufficiency, take \(c_1=w_1-r_*\), and recursively set
\[
c_{j+1}=\min\{w_{j+1}-r_*,\ c_j+b_j-\theta\}.
\]
Then each center coordinate is the minimum of all upper bounds obtained from previous indices; (7) ensures it is at least \(r_*\).

To prove (7), fix \(L=j-i\). As a function of the starting index \(i\), the integer numerator
\[
N_i(L)=w_i+\sum_{t=i}^{i+L-1}b_t
\]
is concave, because consecutive first differences are \(b_{i+L}-a_i\), which decrease with \(i\). Its two endpoint values (at \(i=1\) and \(i=n-L\)) both equal \(d+S_L\), with \(S_L=\sum_{t=1}^{L}b_t\). Thus \(N_i(L)\ge d+S_L\). The sequence \(S_L\) is concave in \(L\), since the increments \(b_L\) decrease. The ratio
\[
\frac{d+S_L}{2+L\sqrt2}
\]
is therefore at least the smaller of its endpoint ratios, which are \(d/2\) at \(L=0\) and the right side of (4) at \(L=n-1\). Both are at least \(r_*\); hence (7) follows. The recursive center exists and satisfies all facets, proving the exact radius formula.

**4. Incenter transition and contacts.** The second bound is strictly smaller than \(d/2\) exactly when \((2-\sqrt2)(n-1)<d-1\). In that regime, equality in (4) forces equality in both endpoint coordinate constraints and every adjacent constraint. Consequently every incenter has the uniquely determined coordinates
\[
c_j=d-r_*+\sum_{t=1}^{j-1}(b_t-\sqrt2r_*).
\]
For \(1<j<n\), the coordinate boxes are strict: the concavity argument proving (7) yields strict inequalities on proper subintervals because the full-chain bound is strictly smaller than the coordinate-width bound. All long-difference facets are strict by (5). Exactly the claimed \(n+1\) facets are tangent.

In the other regime \(r_*=d/2\), every incenter has \(c_1=c_n=d/2\). Because the full-chain ratio is now strictly *larger* than \(d/2\), the inequalities (7) are strict on every positive-length interval, while every interior coordinate has \(w_i>d\). A standard strict version of the same forward difference-constraint construction (apply a sufficiently small positive slack to each nonforced inequality) gives a center satisfying all interior coordinate and adjacent-difference inequalities strictly; (5) then makes all longer-difference facets strict too. Thus the remaining \(n-2\) coordinates vary in an open set, giving affine dimension \(n-2\). At such a generic center, exactly \(x_1=0\), \(x_1=d\), \(x_n=0\), \(x_n=d\) are tangent. Equality of the two radius branches would imply \((2-\sqrt2)(n-1)=d-1\), impossible for integer \(n\ge d\ge3\). This completes the proof.

## Verification

A standalone Python script `verify_pellytope.py` independently calculates support values from the prefix vertices of every Newton simplex, checks all stated facet inequalities at the recursively constructed center for \(3\le d\le n\le16\), and compares with separate linear-program optimizations for \(3\le d\le n\le12\). It also tests the dimension-change mechanism by finding strictly positive slack at a center with fixed endpoint coordinates in twenty-three long-regime cases. The script reports 12,551 direct support assertions, 55 independent linear-program optima, and 23 positive-slack examples. These are finite checks; the proof above covers all integers in scope.

## Relationship to prior work

Bossinger and Gürdoğan introduce \(P_{d,n}\), identify its normal fan, and enumerate its vertices via chord diagrams (arXiv:2609.38456). Their normal-ray classification supplies the geometric premise used in Step 1; the inradius, its sharp threshold, and the full-dimensional incenter change are different claims. The earlier pellytope construction \(d=2\) is due to Bossinger, Telek and Tillmann-Morris (arXiv:2410.08002). Literature on Loday's associahedron generally concerns a different Euclidean realization, and unimodular equivalence does not preserve an inscribed Euclidean radius. General statements about stair-step Minkowski sums or alcoved normal fans do not by themselves determine the exact ball center or tangent-facet transition in these coordinates.

## Limitations

The claim is specific to the stated Newton-polytope embedding and \(n\ge d\ge3\). It is not an affine-invariant statement and does not classify all tangent facets at every boundary point of the long-regime incenter locus. The full detailed body and appendices of the 2026 originating manuscript were not available for complete text inspection after its introductory mathematical material was accessed; its abstract and introduction do not establish a prior exact inball formula, but an unseen equivalent result is a residual priority risk. No independent audit has been performed.
