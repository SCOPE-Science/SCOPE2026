# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The Fourier-diagonalization claim is reconstructed for all prime powers and even symplectic dimension at least six. Every nonzero decomposable wedge has \(q(q^2-1)\) ordered independent presentations; the character sum over each rank-\(r\) alternating form is \(q^{2n-r}\), and the excluded dependent pairs number \(q^{n+1}+q^n-q\). These statements yield the displayed exact multiplier, with no assumption that characteristic is odd. The corank-even identity forces a nonnegative integer \(J(B)\); consequently every eigenvalue lies on one arithmetic lattice. The strict inequality \(2(q^{n-2}-1)/(q^2-1)<q^{n-2}\) gives the sharp lower endpoint. A nilpotent Jordan pencil supplies an actual attaining character; counting isotropic pairs gives the sharp upper endpoint. The proof is infinite-family algebra, not extrapolation from numerics.

Finite verification (executed from the packaged scripts before acceptance): full FFT spectra \((q,n)=(2,4),(3,4),(2,6)\); \(27\) direct-sum checks in the dimension-six ternary case, including the minimizing witness; and \(50\) characteristic-two extension-field checks over \(\mathbb F_4\). The smaller dimension-four cases check Fourier normalization, not novelty. Limit: finite tests alone do not certify the all-parameter theorem.

Its count formulas are sufficiently broad to be useful in deriving Fourier multipliers; this connection is acknowledged without claiming a new general counting method. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The support is the full natural cone of nonzero Plücker vectors from the symplectic isotropic line Grassmannian. Exact invertibility of convolution by this nontrivial structured cone is not a normalization check: it gives a quantitative reconstruction guarantee for every input and an exact optimal noise-amplification factor \(q^n-1\). The proof characterizes **all** least-stable Fourier frequencies by a singular-member criterion for an alternating-form pencil and provides an explicit all-characteristic witness. The lattice formula identifies a forbidden interval of Fourier eigenvalues and explains why zero cannot occur. The quantity is motivated independently of the calculation by invertible deconvolution on a symplectically invariant support, not by an arbitrary low-dimensional numerical slice.
