Euclidean rounding of a cube lowers its squared isotropic constant to second order in every dimension, with the exact coefficient \(-(d-1)(4-\pi)/48\); signed inner offsets leave it constant, creating a sharp \(C^1\)-but-not-\(C^2\) transition. For the three-dimensional cube the entire positive-radius profile is explicit and strictly decreasing from \(1/12\) to the Euclidean-ball limit.
