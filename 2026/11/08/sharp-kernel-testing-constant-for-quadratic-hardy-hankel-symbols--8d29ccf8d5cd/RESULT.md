# Sharp kernel-testing constant for quadratic Hardy Hankel symbols

## Finding
Let \(H_{\overline\psi}:H^2(\mathbb D)\to H^2_-\) be the Hardy Hankel operator generated by a nonzero quadratic analytic symbol
\[
\psi(z)=az+bz^2,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb C,\quad (a,b)\ne(0,0).
\]
For the normalized Szegő kernels \(k_w\), its *kernel-testing norm* obeys the **sharp uniform inequality**
\[
\boxed{\quad\|H_{\overline\psi}\|\le
\frac{3\sqrt3}{5}\sup_{w\in\mathbb D}\|H_{\overline\psi}k_w\|_2.\quad}
\]
Equality holds **if and only if** \(a\ne0\) and \(|b|=\sqrt6\,|a|\). Consequently the worst possible relative loss of reproducing-kernel testing, across this entire minimal-degree nontrivial polynomial family, is exactly
\[
\boxed{\ \sup_{(a,b)\ne(0,0)}\frac{\|H_{\overline{az+bz^2}}\|}{\sup_{w\in\mathbb D}\|H_{\overline{az+bz^2}}k_w\|_2}=\frac{3\sqrt3}{5}.\ }
\]
By the Nehari and Bonsall identities this is equivalently the optimal degree-two norm-comparison constant \(\|\psi\|_*\le (3\sqrt3/5)\|\psi\|_G\). The already-known qualitative statement that nonmonomial polynomial symbols do not satisfy the strong reproducing kernel thesis is not asserted as new.

## Assumptions and scope
All scalars are complex. The Hardy space \(H^2(\mathbb D)\) carries orthonormal basis \(e_j(z)=z^j\), \(j\ge0\); \(H^2_-=L^2(\partial\mathbb D)\ominus H^2\) has basis \(z^{-j}\), \(j\ge1\). With \(P_-\) the orthogonal projection to \(H^2_-\), set \(H_{\overline\psi}f=P_-(\overline\psi f)\). Define
\[
k_w(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-|w|^2}}{1-\overline w z},\qquad w\in\mathbb D.
\]
The theorem includes pure linear and pure quadratic symbols, as well as arbitrary complex relative coefficient phase. The conclusion compares the operator norm with the **supremum of output Hilbert norms**, not pointwise values of the symbol.

## Proof
**Step 1: exact operator norm.** Direct projection shows that \(H_{\overline\psi}e_0=\overline a z^{-1}+\overline b z^{-2}\), \(H_{\overline\psi}e_1=\overline b z^{-1}\), and \(H_{\overline\psi}e_j=0\) for \(j\ge2\). Its nonzero matrix block is
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}\overline a&\overline b\\\overline b&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Diagonal input/output unitaries remove the coefficient phases. Put \(A=|a|\), \(B=|b|\), and \(L=\|H_{\overline\psi}\|\). The top singular value of \(M\) is
\[
L=\frac{A+\sqrt{A^2+4B^2}}2>0,
\qquad L^2-AL-B^2=0.
\]
Hence \(u:=B/L\) satisfies \(0\le u\le1\) and \(A/L=1-u^2\).

**Step 2: an explicit witnessing reproducing kernel.** From the first two Taylor coefficients of \(k_w\),
\[
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_w\|_2^2=
(1-|w|^2)\left(\left|\overline a+\overline b\,\overline w\right|^2+B^2\right).
\]
For each \(r\in[0,1)\), an appropriate phase of \(w\) with \(|w|=r\) aligns the first two summands (when a coefficient vanishes the phase is immaterial). Therefore
\[
K^2:=\left(\sup_{w\in\mathbb D}\|H_{\overline\psi}k_w\|_2\right)^2
=\max_{0\le r<1}(1-r^2)\left[(A+Br)^2+B^2\right].
\]
The supremum is attained for \(0\le r<1\), because the displayed function is continuous on \([0,1]\), zero at \(r=1\), and positive somewhere. Use the specific admissible radius \(r=u/2\), which is always at most \(1/2\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{K^2}{L^2}
&\ge\left(1-\frac{u^2}{4}\right)
\left[\left(1-u^2+\frac{u^2}{2}\right)^2+u^2\right]\\
&=\left(1-\frac{u^2}{4}\right)\left(1+\frac{u^4}{4}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The exact polynomial identity
\[
\left(1-\frac{u^2}{4}\right)\left(1+\frac{u^4}{4}\right)-\frac{25}{27}
=\frac{(8-3u^2)(3u^2-2)^2}{432}\ge0
\quad(0\le u\le1)
\]
proves \(K^2\ge(25/27)L^2\), or \(L/K\le3\sqrt3/5\). If equality holds, the identity forces \(u^2=2/3\), equivalently \(B=\sqrt6\,A\) and \(A>0\).

**Step 3: sharpness and equality.** Set \(a=1\), \(b=\sqrt6\). Then \(L=3\). After phase alignment the squared kernel output is
\[
F(r)=(1-r^2)(7+2\sqrt6\,r+6r^2),\qquad 0\le r\le1.
\]
Its derivatives are
\[
F'(r)=2\sqrt6-2r-6\sqrt6\,r^2-24r^3,\qquad
F''(r)=-2-12\sqrt6\,r-72r^2<0.
\]
Thus its unique global maximizer is \(r=1/\sqrt6\), where \(F(r)=25/3\). In particular
\[
K=5/\sqrt3,\qquad L/K=3\sqrt3/5.
\]
A global scalar multiple and the phase-normalization unitaries show the same equality for every \(a\ne0\) with \(|b|=\sqrt6|a|\). Conversely, Step 2 excludes every other coefficient-modulus ratio. This proves the theorem for all complex coefficients, including the boundary cases.

## Verification
The proof uses only explicit matrix singular values, the exact normalized Szegő-kernel coefficients, and the displayed polynomial factorization. The factors are checked by exact rational arithmetic in the standalone `check_quadratic_hankel.py`. The same script compares explicit complex matrix actions with the kernel formula, tests representative coefficient phases, and verifies the extremal example. Run:

```bash
python3 check_quadratic_hankel.py
```

A successful local run returned `QUADRATIC_HANKEL_CHECK_OK 2621`. These finite checks do not prove the all-coefficient theorem; Steps 1–3 do.

## Relationship to prior work
Dyakonov, *The Strong Reproducing Kernel Thesis for Hankel Operators and the Garsia Norm* (arXiv:2609.33242, first submitted 2026-09-27), formulates the SRKT and recalls the Nehari and Bonsall identities. In its Section 2 it specifically notes a quadratic polynomial of the form \(cz+z^2\) with a strict SRKT deficit. Its later addendum gives a characterization of norm-attaining equality cases and explicitly recovers monomial rigidity for polynomials. It does **not** provide a sharp degree-two uniform loss constant, an optimizer of the coefficient ratio, or the universal inequality proved here.

The previously published published-finding corpus monomial-rigidity result and shifted-Blaschke finite-rank classification were read at their complete `RESULT.md` statements and proofs. They classify *equality* of the two norms; neither their propositions nor their proofs state or bound the optimal non-equality ratio over all quadratic polynomials. A separate logarithmic-Hilbert Hankel result gives a \(\sqrt2\) gap for an unbounded infinite-rank symbol, not the sharp finite-degree constant.

Dyakonov's earlier study of \(G\)-extremality (arXiv:2404.05565), Brevig–Seip's results on minimal and maximal Hankel norms (arXiv:2301.07937), and the weighted reproducing-kernel testing literature concern different equality or boundedness criteria and supply no identified dominating quantitative degree-two formula. This work does not claim novelty for the Hankel matrix computation, Garsia identity, or qualitative failure of SRKT.

## Limitations
The exact constant \(3\sqrt3/5\) is established only for analytic symbols of degree at most two with zero constant term. It is not asserted as the optimal constant for all finite-rank, compact, or bounded Hankel operators. The theorem quantifies a canonical lowest-degree failure regime; it does not solve the general strong reproducing kernel thesis. Independent publication-priority review has not been performed, and unindexed earlier specialized inequalities remain a possible overlap risk.
