# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The definitions refer to all independent lattice triples, not just bases. A subset-sum diameter lemma bounds all seven nonempty subset sums of any frame whose packet radius meets the proposed threshold. For FCC the resulting finite short-vector argument removes axis vectors, restricts the Gram entries to \(\{-1,-1,0\}\), and counts the 24 admissible root triples. Every surviving packet is checked geometrically: four tetrahedra need radius \(1\), two have radius \(\sqrt3/2\). For doubled BCC the same necessity argument removes even axis vectors, leaving precisely eight triples from two complementary cube tetrahedra; each packet tetrahedron has exact circumradius \(\sqrt5/2\). A deep-hole witness and the established packet-covering implication prove equality with classical covering radii. These arguments are exhaustive for arbitrarily large lattice vectors, because the necessary subset bound excludes them. The packaged verifier independently replays all finite candidate cases with exact arithmetic.

## Originality — PASS (historical-priority risk noted)
The motivating 28 October 2026 manuscript [1] develops the general invariant, its inequalities and coordinate-lattice examples, not the exact original packet-radius values or complete optimizing-frame enumeration for FCC/BCC. Its equality for an *extended* projected-triangulation invariant is not a theorem about the original restricted common-frame invariant. The 2018 Delaunay/Voronoi study [2] addresses lattice cells, rather than minimizing over all common frames. Searches of mathematical records and literature under FCC, BCC, \(D_3\), \(D_3^*\), original packet radii, and equivalent tetrahedron/circumsphere descriptions found no decisive prior coverage. Older crystallographic literature could contain an implicit equivalent combinatorial enumeration; [2]'s full text was unavailable, and this is retained as a risk rather than interpreted as proof of absence.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The new common-frame invariant is generally larger than the ordinary covering radius, so proving equality for the canonical dual FCC and BCC lattices is a meaningful positive boundary example, contrasting with known higher-dimensional separation. The exact 24-versus-8 classification gives a rigid, complete answer about optimal *lattice frames*, not merely a routine calculation of a known sphere radius. The result identifies every minimizer and proves that no proper-sublattice frame can be optimal despite the definition permitting such frames. It provides reproducible small-dimensional benchmarks for the common-frame lattice covering problem, with no claim of a general lattice theorem.
