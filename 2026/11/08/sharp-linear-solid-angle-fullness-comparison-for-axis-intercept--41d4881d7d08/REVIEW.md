# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Gaussian cone argument proves that minimum-angle vertices are exactly those with the largest coordinate-axis intercept. The gnomonic chart has the precise spherical Jacobian \((1+\|z\|^2)^{-d/2}\), giving an exact simplex integral for the ratio of the least unnormalized solid angle to fullness. Pointwise strict bounds, monotonicity in the second-largest intercept, and dominated convergence prove the sharp range and equality classification for every \(d\ge2\). The beta-moment calculation proves the claimed high-dimensional asymptotic. The accompanying verifier independently checks the three-dimensional trihedral formula and numerical constants; finite checks are supplementary and are not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The lower bound in this note is expressly acknowledged as a known implication. The full exact constant, rank-two degenerating maximizing regime (in dimensions at least three), and the location of the minimum-angle vertex are separately compared with accessible primary results, related tetrahedral-quality literature including Dompierre and coauthors (2003), historical records, and mathematical-record search results. The closest earlier tetrahedral study (Liu–Joe 1994) concerns broad shape-measure comparisons and formulas; its full text was not available, and any unreported equivalent specialization remains a residual priority risk. A prior angle-sum result for trirectangular tetrahedra concerns a different objective, as do sharp results for chain orthoschemes.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The axis-intercept simplex is a natural canonical mesh element in orthogonal-coordinate discretizations. The exact two-sided linear comparison of minimum angle and fullness is markedly stronger than the general quadratic converse for arbitrary simplices. Optimizing a meaningful dimension-dependent constant and locating its extremal degenerate shapes both clarify what geometric restrictions prevent sliver-type pathology. The result covers every dimension and every positive aspect ratio, rather than an arbitrary finite list or a single test configuration. The high-dimensional constant asymptotic quantifies the cost of this improved equivalence.

## Limitations and remaining risks
The result is restricted to orthogonal-corner simplices and uses unnormalized solid angles. Prior broad angle formulas are not claimed new. Full-text inspection of Liu and Joe (1994) was unavailable; a precise old specialization cannot be excluded. No external mathematical audit has been performed.
