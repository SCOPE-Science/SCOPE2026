For a uniformly weighted five-point cyclic metric with unit sides and diagonals of length \(t\in[1,2]\), the sharp scalar Lipschitz variance is \((6-4t+4t^2)/25\), whereas the Hilbert variance is \(\min\{(1+t^2)/5,(5+\sqrt5)/10\}\). At the golden ratio, every Hilbert extremizer changes from a four-dimensional isometric configuration to a planar regular pentagon, and both absolute and relative scalar–Hilbert variance gaps attain their unique maxima.
