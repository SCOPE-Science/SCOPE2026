# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses a rank criterion for vertices of the finite Lipschitz polytope (connected tight-edge graph), strict convexity of centered variance, and an exhaustive analytic classification: either the two parts are separately constant, or one part spans its full distance and the other is forced into a smaller interval. The possible values are exactly \(0,s\) and \(s-1,1\); the identity \(Q=sS-b(s-1)\) then completes the variance square. The midpoint calculation follows from coverage of an integer interval by \(3k+[0,b]\) for \(b\ge2\). This establishes all real \(1<s<2\), not only sampled parameters. A rational spanning-tree enumeration and exact endpoint checks independently corroborate the argument, but finite tests are not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual risks

The 1998 foundational metric-isoperimetry result and the fully read 2009 arXiv paper develop spread and general properties, not the fixed two-cluster sharp variance formula. The 2009 paper's complete-bipartite lemma concerns random edge lengths, not homogeneous pair distances. A related publicly indexed 2026 result counts facets of the Lipschitz-free ball for the *same* metric family; full text was unavailable, and it may contain additional remarks. Its stated facet-count formula alone does not evaluate the variance quadratic, the bounded two-integer discrepancy, or the \(s=3/2\) parity law. The complementary regime \(0<s\le1\) was previously resolved separately, and no claim is made for it. A differently formulated or unindexed covering result cannot be excluded; no independent priority audit has occurred.

## Value — PASS

The exact sharp variance is the optimal Lipschitz–Poincaré constant of a natural two-homogeneous-class metric that interpolates between discrete equilateral and graph-metric endpoints. It determines *every* optimizing level pattern and reduces a continuous \(N\)-dimensional optimization to a bounded arithmetic two-integer discrepancy, evaluable with \(O(N)\) comparisons. The universal closed formula at the nontrivial metric midpoint distinguishes genuine parity frustration from the geometric baseline, while the endpoints connect two classical exact constants. This gives a natural sharp invariant and a rigorous complete optimizer classification, rather than isolated examples.

## Limits and closest work

The two classes must each contain at least two points, within-class distances must match, and weights must be uniform. Beyond the midpoint the arithmetic minimum is not replaced by a global single polynomial. Closest sources are Alon–Boppana–Spencer (1998), Addario-Berry–Janson–McDiarmid (2009 arXiv), and the publicly indexed snowflaked-complete-bipartite facet-count study (index summary only). A published formula outside the searched sources remains possible.
