**Correctness: PASS.** Every finitely supported symbol lies in a finite subgroup algebra because the ambient abelian group is torsion. The norm-one Fourier idempotents are an exhaustive orthogonal resolution of the identity. In each corner, finite Fourier approximants can be shifted by an arbitrarily small scalar to obtain an invertible element; this is sufficient for local openness when only the first factor is finitely supported. For two finite factors, the displayed three-case construction gives exact correction and the stated linear/quadratic lower radii. The simultaneous-zero corner obstructs any radius above the square of the perturbation radius, while submultiplicativity bounds the no-common-zero regime from above. The exact-rational executable corroborates the identities but is not used as an infinite proof.

**Value: PASS.** The still-open torsion case motivates identifying the location of possible non-open pairs. This theorem rules out every pair with one finitely supported factor, including zero-product pairs, and gives a complete sharp linear-versus-quadratic local modulus classification when both factors are finitely supported. The two exponents distinguish a natural structural boundary (common Fourier zeros) and identify which local counterexample strategies cannot work; the result does not claim to resolve global openness.
