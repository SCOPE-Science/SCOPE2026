# Scientific clarification of the final result

The final claim is **local convolution openness in \(\ell^1(G)\) at a pair with only one finitely supported factor**. An earlier published complete finite-tuple theorem already implies the two-finitely-supported linear/quadratic aperture dichotomy, which is no longer offered as new.

**Correctness.** A finite subgroup contains the finite support. Its Fourier idempotents are norm-one orthogonal central projections. Each corner has dense units by finite-support approximation followed by finite-group Fourier decomposition, so even an arbitrary infinitely supported second corner component can be approximated by an invertible element. The explicit two-case factors solve an arbitrary small perturbation exactly with controlled \(\ell^1\)-norm changes. No finite experiment is used to prove the arbitrary-group result.

**Originality.** The November 8 published theorem covers all-finitely-supported tuples and their sharp exponents; it does not cover the arbitrary-second-factor theorem. Flores identifies the global torsion case as open; Draga–Kania stable rank alone does not imply pointwise local openness. A broad finite-spectrum Banach-algebra factorization principle may predate this construction and remains the main priority risk.

**Value.** The remaining result excludes the entire one-finite-factor stratum from possible non-openness in the hard torsion-abelian class, even when the second factor is infinitely supported. This is a motivated structural boundary and not a repetition of the known finite-tuple exponents.

This corrected evaluation supersedes the earlier novelty attribution of the finite-pair aperture dichotomy; original records remain part of historical evidence.
