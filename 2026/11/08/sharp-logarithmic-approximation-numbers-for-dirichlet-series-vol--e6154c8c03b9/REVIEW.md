# Mathematical assessment: logarithmic singular-value law for Dirichlet-series Volterra operators

## Correctness — PASS
The operator's coefficient formula is obtained by integrating a finite Dirichlet product, and the factorization \(T_g=DB\) is exact. The multiplier \(B\) splits into orthogonal copies of polynomial multiplication on the finite Hardy polydisc; the norm is the boundary maximum \(C_g\). A rank-\(N\) cutoff proves the upper singular-value bound. To prove sharpness, one fixed finite near-maximizer is transferred into linearly many mutually orthogonal prime-free blocks at integer scales comparable to \(N\). The slowly varying denominator is uniformly equivalent to \(\log N\) on these finite blocks, and the min-max principle gives the lower bound. The counting law is a valid inversion of the positive logarithmic singular-value equivalent. The finite checker performed 323 consistency assertions and is not used to infer the infinite limit.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with residual risk)
The close source *Volterra operators on Hardy spaces of Dirichlet series* (Brevig–Perfekt–Seip, 2016, Section 7) already proves polynomial-symbol compactness and nonmembership in every finite Schatten class. That stronger qualitative nonmembership conclusion is not claimed as new. Its coefficient lower-bound calculation establishes no exact rate coefficient for the ordered singular values, and its rank cutoff contains no matching lower min-max subspaces. Bommier-Hato (2020, Section 6) likewise treats nonmembership rather than sharp singular-number asymptotics on weighted spaces. Tong–Wang–Yuan (2026, Introduction, Theorems 1.1–1.4) give BMOA and Bergman-space compactness criteria, not Hilbert \(\mathscr H^2\) singular asymptotics. Direct searches for multiplicative-convolution, Bohr-lift and Volterra approximation-number formulations and comparison with accessible earlier mathematical claims found no equivalent statement. Residual priority risk remains from a potentially more general older result about compact diagonal multipliers composed with multiplicative shifts, which could imply the theorem even without its current terminology. Negative searches are not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural unknown is the rate at which compact Dirichlet-series Volterra operators admit finite-rank approximation, a question not answered by their known qualitative compactness and Schatten obstructions. The theorem gives a positive, computable leading constant for *every* nonconstant Dirichlet polynomial, including interfering multi-prime symbols. Its positive-density prime-block mechanism explains why the asymptotic coefficient is the *maximal* Bohr multiplier modulus rather than an average over phases. The equivalent counting law precisely quantifies the unusually slow compactness. The one-term formula is only a check and illustration, not the principal contribution.

## Scientific limitations
The theorem is restricted to finite Dirichlet polynomials on the coefficient \(\mathscr H^2\) space. No extension to general symbols or to weighted or Banach spaces is asserted. The method includes a genuine infinite-dimensional min-max argument; finite computations only check identities. Possible broader spectral asymptotic literature remains a priority risk.
