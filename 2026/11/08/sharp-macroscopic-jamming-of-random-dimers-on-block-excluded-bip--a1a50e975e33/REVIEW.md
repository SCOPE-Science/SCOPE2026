# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The four-type generator is exact: admissible edge counts are \(xv\), \(uy\), and \(uv\), and a rate \(1/n\) per edge produces a globally Lipschitz density-dependent drift. Bounded martingale quadratic variation proves convergence on every compact time interval. The scalar integral transformation solves the limiting system, including its diagonal limit. Terminal-jamming convergence is proved separately by the finite-time consumption bound: any future removal of a bad vertex needs an opposite good vertex remaining at the cutoff. The Hall construction and Jensen argument establish the sharp universal feasible maximum, including the equality case. The included verifier tests exact rational finite-state laws and seeded large-size simulations; these tests do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with documented uncertainty

Penrose--Sudbury (2005) defines graph dimer adsorption, proves general forward equations and derives Bethe-lattice results; its examined theorems do not imply the two-type dense bipartite limit. Arnosti (2021) studies random-edge greedy matching and gives distributional comparisons over random bipartite graph ensembles; Theorems 1--2 and the examined discussion do not yield the fixed two-block asymptotic formula or its sharp Hall-feasible extremum. Dyer--Frieze (1991) studies fixed-graph random greedy matching and therefore presents the most important historical risk, but its full text was inaccessible: only its abstract and later published descriptions could be compared. The result is not a new definition of random-edge matching; the claim is the two-parameter jamming function and the unique sharp feasible maximum. Searches of research records and 103 previously available mathematical claims did not identify an implication covering it. A failed search is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The missing-edge block creates a graph with an exact Hall-perfect-matching boundary. Nevertheless an irreversible local deposition rule leaves a linear number of vertices unmatched. The derived two-parameter law quantifies that inefficiency and identifies the unique largest asymptotic waste, \(1/(2e)\) per side, among all fixed-proportion block deletions preserving perfect-matchability. This distinguishes combinatorial packability from kinetic packing yield in a natural physical model rather than tabulating an arbitrary instance.
