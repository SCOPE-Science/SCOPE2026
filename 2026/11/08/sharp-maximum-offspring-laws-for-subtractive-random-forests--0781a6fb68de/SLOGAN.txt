The maximum number of children in a subtractive random forest has a sharp almost-sure scale determined by the decay of its attachment-lag probabilities: stretched-exponential masses with \(-\log q_j\sim c j^\beta\) give \(((\beta+1)\log n/c)^{1/(\beta+1)}\), while polynomial masses with \(-\log q_j\sim s\log j\), \(s>1\), give \(\log n/(s\log\log n)\). Matching upper-tail bounds and independent blocks of forced attachments yield the constants despite dependence between vertex degrees.
