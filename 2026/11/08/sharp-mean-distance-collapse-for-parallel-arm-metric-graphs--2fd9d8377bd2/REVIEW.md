# Scientific review: sharp mean-distance collapse for parallel-arm metric graphs

**Correctness — PASS.** The independent within-edge and between-edge integrations yield an exact cubic functional for arbitrary positive lengths. Pairwise balancing is strictly improving except at equality, and the derivative factorization leaves only endpoint minima. The displayed endpoint comparison and compactness establish strictness and the unique degeneration. Numerical checks on independent quadrature and random vectors agree, but do not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk.** Baptista, Kennedy and Mugnolo (2024) give a universal edge-count bound and the equilateral flower value; Garijo, Márquez and Silveira (2023) give weighted-graph computations. Neither stated result implies the sharp positive-length parallel-arm infimum or unique degenerating optimizer. The three-arm law and equal-arm specializations are known and not claimed new. Full text of Doyle and Graver (1982) was unavailable, so historic priority is provisional.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The complete global solution of the first genuine many-parallel-path length-allocation problem reveals a strict boundary-collapse optimizer for every four-or-more-arm graph, contrasting with the three-arm equilateral minimum. This is a motivated natural constrained geometric optimization across all positive real arm lengths.

**Limits.** The result covers only the stated intrinsic metric graphs and uniform arclength sampling, and has not undergone independent mathematical validation.
