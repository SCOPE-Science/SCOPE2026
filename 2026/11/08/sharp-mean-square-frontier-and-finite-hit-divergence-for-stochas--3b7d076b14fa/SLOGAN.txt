For the one-oracle stochastic reflected-gradient update on a scalar quadratic, the exact all-initialization mean-square stability region depends only on the first two random-curvature moments and is \(vh(1+3mh)<2m\). A binary nonnegative-curvature oracle gives a striking separation: every trajectory reaches the exact optimum in finite time almost surely, yet the expected squared error grows exponentially, while ordinary stochastic gradient descent with the same calls converges in mean square.
