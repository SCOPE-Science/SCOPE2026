# Sharp minimal-absent-word capacity with three one-markers

## Finding

Let \(w\) be any finite binary word with exactly three symbols equal to \(1\), and let \(\mathcal M(w)\) be the set of its *minimal absent contiguous factors* over the fixed alphabet \(\{0,1\}\). For every length \(n\ge7\),
\[
\max_{|w|=n,\,|w|_1=3}|\mathcal M(w)|=n.
\]
Exactly two labeled words attain the maximum:
\[
110^{n-3}1\qquad\text{and}\qquad10^{n-3}11.
\]
Moreover, the full set \(\mathcal M(w)\), not just its cardinality, has the explicit zero-gap classification given below for **every** length \(n\ge3\). In particular no MAW contains more than four ones, and all its elements are generated by three small families of southwest-closed lattice rectangles and finitely many endpoint tests.

## Assumptions and scope

A *factor* is a contiguous substring, including the empty word when needed for the membership criterion. A nonempty word \(v\) is *minimal absent* for \(w\) when it is absent from \(w\) but all its proper factors occur. For \(|v|\ge2\), it is enough that its length-\(|v|-1\) prefix and suffix occur. A missing singleton is a MAW by convention. Counts concern distinct factor strings, not occurrences. The alphabet is always \(\{0,1\}\), even for \(w=111\).

Write uniquely
\[
w=0^a1\,0^b1\,0^c1\,0^d,\qquad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad n=a+b+c+d+3.
\]
Set \(L=\max(a,b,c)\), \(R=\max(b,c,d)\), \(K=\min(L,R)\), and \(G=\max(a,b,c,d)\). For any finite set \(E\) of pairs of nonnegative integers, define
\[
F_E(p,q)\iff (\exists(r,s)\in E)\,[p\le r\land q\le s]
\]
for nonnegative integers \(p,q\), with \(F_\varnothing\) false. Define the one-marker rectangle corners
\[
E_1=\{(a,b),(b,c),(c,d)\},\qquad F_1=F_{E_1},
\]
and, for each \(h\ge0\), the two-marker corners (retaining duplicates only once)
\[
E_2(h)=\bigl(\{(a,c):h=b\}\bigr)\cup\bigl(\{(b,d):h=c\}\bigr),\qquad F_{2,h}=F_{E_2(h)}.
\]
For a nonempty \(E\), put \(\ell_E=\max_{(r,s)\in E}r\) and \(r_E=\max_{(r,s)\in E}s\). For \(1\le p\le\ell_E\), put \(H_E(p)=\max\{s:(r,s)\in E,\ r\ge p\}\), with the maximum of the empty set equal to \(-1\). The following explicit recipe generates a **set** (so repeated candidates are counted once).

**Zero-one count \(0\).** Include \(0^{G+1}\).

**Zero-one count \(1\).** Include \(0^{L+1}1\) if \(d>L\), and include \(10^{R+1}\) if \(a>R\). For each distinct \(p\in\{a+1,b+1,c+1\}\) with \(1\le p\le L\), set \(q=H_{E_1}(p)+1\). If \(q\ge1\), \(F_1(p-1,q)\) and \(F_1(p,q-1)\), include \(0^p10^q\).

**Zero-one count \(2\).** Include \(10^h1\) for every integer \(0\le h\le K\) with \(h\notin\{b,c\}\). For each distinct \(h\in\{b,c\}\), set \(E=E_2(h)\). Include \(0^{\ell_E+1}10^h1\) if \(F_1(\ell_E+1,h)\), and include \(10^h10^{r_E+1}\) if \(F_1(h,r_E+1)\). For each distinct \(p\in\{r+1:(r,s)\in E\}\) with \(1\le p\le\ell_E\), put \(q=H_E(p)+1\); if \(q\ge1\), \(F_E(p-1,q)\) and \(F_E(p,q-1)\), include \(0^p10^h10^q\).

**Zero-one count \(3\).** For each \((h,k)\in\{b,c\}^2\setminus\{(b,c)\}\), include \(10^h10^k1\) if \(F_{2,k}(h,0)\) and \(F_{2,h}(0,k)\). Also include \(0^{a+1}10^b10^c1\) if \(F_{2,b}(a+1,c)\), and include \(10^b10^c10^{d+1}\) if \(F_{2,c}(b,d+1)\).

**Zero-one count \(4\).** Include \(\bigl(10^b\bigr)^3 1\) precisely if \(b=c\), \(a\ge b\), and \(d\ge b\).

No other MAWs exist. These five groups are partitioned by their number of one-symbols, not by run-length-encoding type.

## Proof

**Rectangle lemma.** A factor with one one has the form \(0^p10^q\) and occurs exactly when \(F_1(p,q)\). Indeed, the unique one is respectively the first, second, or third marked position, with adjacent available zero-run lengths \((a,b)\), \((b,c)\), or \((c,d)\). Similarly a factor with exactly two ones has the form \(0^p10^h10^q\), occurs only for \(h=b\) or \(h=c\), and occurs exactly when \(F_{2,h}(p,q)\). A factor containing exactly three ones has inner zero gaps \((b,c)\) and occurs precisely when its exterior zero lengths are at most \((a,d)\). All these equivalences hold even when some gaps are zero.

**Frontier lemma.** Let \(E\) be a nonempty collection of at most three nonnegative rectangle corners, and let \(F_E\) be their southwest union. An absent point \((p,q)\) with \(p,q\ge1\) has both immediately preceding coordinate points in \(F_E\) if and only if \(p=r+1\) for some first coordinate \(r\) of a corner, \(q=H_E(p)+1\ge1\), and \(F_E(p-1,q)\), \(F_E(p,q-1)\) hold. The reason is that for each supported \(p\), the admissible \(q\)-coordinates are precisely \(0,\ldots,H_E(p)\); a minimal missing point requires a strict drop from \(H_E(p-1)\) to \(H_E(p)\), which can occur only when a rectangle ceases to be active at \(p=r+1\). If \(H_E(p)=-1\), no positive \(q\) has its predecessor in \(F_E\). There are at most \(|E|-1\) supported strict drops, hence at most two such points for \(E_1\) and at most one for \(E_2(h)\) when \(b=c\).

**Classification.** With no ones, the sole minimal absent factor is \(0^{G+1}\). With one one, interior zero exteriors are controlled by the frontier lemma for \(E_1\); a zero exterior forces the corresponding unique axis endpoint, and deleting the remaining one imposes respectively \(d>L\) or \(a>R\). With two ones, if either exterior zero length is positive then the inner gap must belong to \(\{b,c\}\), by the rectangle lemma and deletion of that outer zero. For such a gap, interior exteriors are its \(E_2(h)\) frontier; one zero exterior gives its unique endpoint and the indicated \(F_1\) test after deleting the terminal one. When both zero exteriors vanish, deleting either one gives the two bounds \(h\le L\) and \(h\le R\), producing exactly the missing internal gaps \(0\le h\le K\) outside \(\{b,c\}\).

With three ones, any nonzero outer zero exponent forces the observed interior pair \((b,c)\), and then at most one outer zero can be added minimally; deletion of the opposite terminal one gives the two \(F_2\) tests. Without outer zeros, deletion of the first or third one gives the two tests for \((h,k)\). Four ones require no outer zeros, because deletion of an outer zero would leave four absent ones. Deleting the first and last ones shows the three internal gaps must all equal \(b=c\), and the two resulting three-one factors occur precisely when \(a,d\ge b\). Five or more ones are impossible because either maximal proper factor still has at least four ones. This proves both necessity and sufficiency of every candidate in the recipe, since each stated test supplies the required maximal proper prefix and suffix while the rectangle/border condition excludes the full candidate.

**Sharp bound and equality.** Put \(t=|\{b,c\}|\in\{1,2\}\). The number of missing-gap two-one factors \(10^h1\) is exactly \(K+1-t\), since \(b,c\le K\). The remaining candidates admit the following elementary upper bounds by class: one zero-only word; at most four one-one words (two axis endpoints and two frontier drops); at most \(2t+\mathbf1_{t=1}\) additional two-one words; at most \(2+3\mathbf1_{t=2}\) three-one words; and at most \(\mathbf1_{t=1}\) four-one words. Consequently
\[
|\mathcal M(w)|\le\begin{cases}K+11,&t=1,\\K+13,&t=2.\end{cases}
\]
In particular \(|\mathcal M(w)|\le K+13\). Let \(m=\max(a,b,c,d)\) and \(s=a+b+c+d-m\). If \(s\ge11\) then \(|\mathcal M(w)|\le m+13<m+s+3=n\).

It remains to settle \(s\le10\). Choose any maximum-gap coordinate; fix the other three gaps as an ordered triple \((u,v,z)\) of nonnegative integers satisfying \(u+v+z\le10\), let \(M=\max(u,v,z)\), and insert \(m\) at the chosen coordinate. Check \(m=M,M+1,M+2\). There are exactly \(4\cdot3\cdot\binom{13}{3}=3432\) such cases, including redundant tie choices. The exact integer test in `VERIFY.py` uses only the classification recipe and establishes (i) \(|\mathcal M(w)|\le n\) in every case, (ii) no non-listed equality for \(n\ge7\), and (iii) equality at the anchor \(m=M+2\) only for \((a,b,c,d)=(0,2,0,0)\) and \((0,0,2,0)\).

To cover **all** larger \(m\), observe analytically that for \(m\ge M+2\) every comparison between a gap (or a gap plus one) and another gap is constant in truth value: the unique largest gap stays strictly ahead. In the finite endpoint/frontier groups, therefore, the number of candidates is constant. The only size depending on \(m\) is \(K+1-t\), which is affine with slope \(1\) if the largest gap is internal (\(b\) or \(c\)) and slope \(0\) if it is external (\(a\) or \(d\)). Thus \(|\mathcal M(w)|=\varepsilon m+\beta\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) on this entire infinite ray. Meanwhile \(n=m+u+v+z+3\). If equality is absent at \(m=M+2\), it cannot appear later. The only equality anchors are the two indicated internal-gap words; both have slope \(1\), so they remain equalities for every larger \(m\). For \(n\ge7\) these are precisely \((0,0,n-3,0)\) and \((0,n-3,0,0)\), as claimed. The two words each attain exactly \(n\) MAWs by direct application of the recipe.

## Verification

Run `python3 VERIFY.py` in a directory containing only the standalone included verifier; it uses Python's standard library. It compares the rectangle recipe against an independently constructed complete factor set for all \(10626\) binary three-one words through length \(23\), tests the \(3432\)-case finite residual bound and equality roots, and checks the two infinite-family formulas at representative lengths through \(1000\). The successful output is:

```
CLASSIFICATION_OK 10626
FINITE_REDUCTION_OK 3432 AFFINE_EQUAL_BASES 2
VERIFY_OK
```

The finite check is a certificate only for the small-complement base cases. Infinite validity comes from the proved rectangle classification, the coarse bound, and the affine-stability reduction; finite testing alone would not establish the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Pinho–Ferreira–Garcia–Rodrigues (2009) develop the central minimal-absent-factor notion and general algorithms. Crochemore–Fici–Mercaş–Pissis (2015) give the general language formulation and a linear sequence-comparison algorithm, with the broad \(\sigma n\) bound. Charalampopoulos–Crochemore–Fici–Mercaş–Pissis (2018) show that the asymptotic \(\Theta(\sigma n)\) bound is attainable, including a two-marker construction, but not this exact three-marker extremal characterization. Akagi–Okabe–Mieno–Nakashima–Inenaga (2022) derive general RLE-type bounds and an output-sensitive representation for all MAWs; those statements do not yield the claimed exact length bound or labeled extremizers at fixed one-count three. The present claim concerns exact finite-length extremizers and a specialized full gap classification, not a new general algorithm or a new \(\Theta(n)\) phenomenon.

## Limitations

The sharp two-extremizer assertion is stated only for \(n\ge7\); shorter lengths have additional maximizing words. The classification holds for all \(n\ge3\), only for three ones and the fixed binary alphabet. No result is asserted for circular factors, higher alphabets, other fixed weights, or minimal absent **subsequences**. The finite base of the extremal proof is reproduced by a standard-library program rather than a handwritten table. Equivalent unindexed published special cases may remain a historical-priority risk. No independent mathematical validation is claimed.
