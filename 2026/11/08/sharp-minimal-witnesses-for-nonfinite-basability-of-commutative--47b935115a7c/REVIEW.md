# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is restricted to every integer \(d\ge5\). The proof shows that under the published full-power finite-basis criterion, every preterminal nonzero nilpotency layer has at least two elements and the last one at least one; this yields \(2d-2\). It explicitly constructs the hook and parity-label semigroups, proves associativity and cardinalities, and verifies exact nilpotency index and that all critical powers vanish. The parity-label family exists for odd \(d\ge5\); an invariant of the unique minimal generating set separates it from the hook family. A fresh checker exhaustively checked all triples for 16 constructed models through \(d=13\), but those finite checks do not replace universal proof. Reliance on the published criterion [1] is explicit.

## Originality — qualified PASS

Closest literature: Wang–Yin–Wang [1] already proves the criterion and exhibits the four-element \(d=3\) base case. Distler [2], Lemmas 2.1(iii) and 2.4, imply the filtration *lower bound*; that lower bound is therefore credited to prior work, not claimed new. Distler's small-coclass classification treats coclass at most two, whereas the examples for arbitrarily large \(d\) have coclass \(d-2\). Distler–Mitchell [3] counts the index-three case. Searches found no statement covering sharp extremal attainability for **every** \(d\ge5\) and the parity-dependent family of nonisomorphic minimizers. A possibly earlier equivalent monomial-semigroup construction remains a priority risk; database nonmatches alone do not establish novelty.

## Value — PASS

The minimum host size for a full power semiring with no finite identity basis, at prescribed nilpotency index, is a natural arithmetic invariant in the newly solved finite-basis classification. An exact sharp formula for unbounded indices distinguishes a necessary filtration bound from realizability, and the parity family shows that optimal objects need not be unique. The full power semiring has exponentially many elements in this host size, making the minimum especially relevant when searching for small witnesses. This is not a classification of all minimizers, and no result about noncommutative semigroups is asserted.
