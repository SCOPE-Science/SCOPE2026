For Euclidean path tetrahedra, the largest achievable smallest dihedral angle is \(\arccos((\sqrt5-1)/2)\), attained only by consecutive perpendicular edge ratios \(\sqrt\varphi:1:\sqrt\varphi\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). The entire shape superlevel-set geometry has a second sharp boundary: it is unbounded through a minimum angle of \(\pi/4\), compact above \(\pi/4\), and collapses to the unique optimum at the sharp maximum.
