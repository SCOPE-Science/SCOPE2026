# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses the exact mixed-measure nodal Gram matrix, its positive Schur complement, direct inversion, and integrations of explicitly affine signed kernels. The largest evaluation norm occurs only at minimum-mass leaf endpoints; strict kernel inequalities and convexity exclude all other points. The rational sign identity and the shrinking-arm calculation prove the threshold and discontinuity with all parameters quantified. The included exact-rational checker independently tested 1,440 mass configurations, 25 phase configurations, and three jump identities. Finite enumeration supports, but does not replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The known formula for purely arclength-weighted metric stars is a covered limiting case, expressly excluded from the new contribution. De Boor (1976) and Kayumov (arXiv:math/0408418, 2004) analyze interval spline projectors, while Grigorieff–Sloan (1998) treats discrete interval-spline projections. Their inspected statements do not imply the exact mixed-measure metric-star kernel norm, a two-threshold atomic amplification regime, or the discontinuity from a vanishing new edge. Boman (2006) addresses weighted finite-element projection estimates and is a plausible more general antecedent, but only its abstract could be checked. Different formulations may exist elsewhere. The originality conclusion reflects these documented limitations, not exhaustive priority certification.

## Value — PASS

A point mass at a junction represents a distinct natural least-squares observation regime from arclength-only data. Sharp amplifier size matters for sup-norm stability in hybrid continuous-discrete regression. The boundary fractions \(1/4\) and \(1/3\) give a complete qualitative phase diagram; the positive collapsing-arm jump detects instability invisible to convergence of total edge mass. The result is a complete theorem over arbitrary finite star degree, not an isolated instance or routine restatement of the atom-free calculation.

## Scope and risk

The theorem applies only to constant positive edge densities and one positive hub atom; it does not extend to nonconstant densities or general tree geometries. The comparison with Boman (2006) was limited to the abstract; an exact equivalent result in unindexed work remains possible. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
