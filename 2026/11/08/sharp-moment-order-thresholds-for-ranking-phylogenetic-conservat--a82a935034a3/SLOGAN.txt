In rooted phylogenetic conservation, joint extinction probabilities through order \(k\) always determine which single species to protect when there are at most \(k+1\) species, and this threshold is sharp: for every \(k\geq1\), two exchangeable extinction laws on \(k+2\) taxa agree on every lower-order joint extinction and on total extinction but select different unique optimal taxa. For four taxa, even pairwise-independent extinctions with the same all-extinct probability can reverse the optimal intervention because of a hidden three-way extinction interaction.
