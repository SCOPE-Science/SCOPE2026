# Sharp negative-type threshold and critical Euclidean dimension for rank-three Grassmann graphs

## Finding

Let \(q\ge2\) be a prime power, \(n\ge6\), and \(\mathcal G_q(n,3)\) the finite Grassmannian of three-dimensional linear subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^n\). Its canonical graph metric is
\[
d(U,W)=3-\dim(U\cap W)\in\{0,1,2,3\}.
\]
Put \([r]_q=(q^r-1)/(q-1)\), \(u=[n-5]_q\), \(A=q^3u-q-1\), and \(C=q^4u\). There is a unique \(\beta_{q,n}\in(0,1)\) satisfying
\[
A-(A+C+1)2^{\beta_{q,n}}+C3^{\beta_{q,n}}=0.
\]
**Theorem.** The metric has \(t\)-negative type exactly when \(0\le t\le\beta_{q,n}\); it has strict \(t\)-negative type exactly when \(0\le t<\beta_{q,n}\). At the critical exponent, exactly the degree-two spherical-harmonic sector loses strictness. Its dimension is
\[
\left[{n\atop2}\right]_q-\left[{n\atop1}\right]_q.
\]
For every \(0<t<\beta_{q,n}\), the least real Euclidean dimension for a configuration realizing \(\|x_U-x_W\|^2=d(U,W)^t\) is \(\left[{n\atop3}\right]_q-1\); at \(t=\beta_{q,n}\) it drops sharply to
\[
\left[{n\atop3}\right]_q-1-\left[{n\atop2}\right]_q+\left[{n\atop1}\right]_q.
\]
For \(q=2,n=6\), the threshold is approximately \(0.27053081961038\) and the embedding dimension drops from \(1394\) to \(806\).

There are two explicit asymptotic laws, with natural logarithms:
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}q^{n-2}\beta_{q,n}
=\frac{q-1}{q\log(3/2)-\log2}\quad\text{for fixed }q,\qquad
\lim_{q\to\infty}q^{n-2}\beta_{q,n}
=\frac1{\log(3/2)}\quad\text{for fixed }n\ge6,
\]
where the second limit is along prime powers.

## Assumptions and scope

The number of vertices is \(N=\left[{n\atop3}\right]_q\), and Gaussian binomials are defined by
\[
\left[{n\atop j}\right]_q
=\prod_{r=0}^{j-1}\frac{q^{n-r}-1}{q^{j-r}-1},\qquad
\left[{n\atop0}\right]_q=1.
\]
The Grassmann graph joins subspaces meeting in dimension two. Its shortest-path metric agrees with \(3-\dim(U\cap W)\) and has diameter three. For \(t>0\), its powered distance matrix is \(D_t=(d(U,W)^t)_{U,W}\), with zero diagonal. At \(t=0\), set all off-diagonal entries equal to one, leaving diagonal entries zero. The condition of \(t\)-negative type means \(a^TD_ta\le0\) for every real vector \(a\) with \(\sum_Ua_U=0\); strictness means strict inequality for each nonzero such vector.

A Euclidean realization of the squared distances \(d^t\) has Gram matrix \(-\tfrac12HD_tH\), where \(H=I-N^{-1}J\). Its minimum possible ambient Euclidean dimension equals the rank of this Gram matrix. This is a statement about Hilbert embeddings of the snowflaked metric \(d^{t/2}\), not about spectral graph-theoretic terminology sometimes also called 'negative type'.

## Proof

**1. Four common harmonic sectors.** Let \(A_i\) be the adjacency matrix of pairs of Grassmann distance \(i\), for \(0\le i\le3\), with \(A_0=I\). Their real symmetric Bose–Mesner algebra has four orthogonal common eigenspaces \(E_j\), \(0\le j\le3\). The standard incidence-filtration proof of the Grassmann association-scheme spectrum gives
\[
\dim E_j=\left[{n\atop j}\right]_q-\left[{n\atop j-1}\right]_q,
\qquad
\theta_j=q^{j+1}[3-j]_q[n-3-j]_q-[j]_q
\]
for the \(A_1\)-eigenvalue on \(E_j\); the Gaussian coefficient at \(j=-1\) is zero. These formulas also follow by counting the action of \(A_1\) on the space of indicators of three-planes containing a fixed \(j\)-plane, modulo the space generated by lower-dimensional incidence indicators. The Grassmann intersection numbers are
\[
b_i=q^{2i+1}[3-i]_q[n-3-i]_q\quad(0\le i<3),
\qquad c_i=[i]_q^2\quad(1\le i\le3).
\]
Writing \(v=b_0\) and \(a_i=v-b_i-c_i\), the distance-matrix recurrence
\[
A_1A_i=b_{i-1}A_{i-1}+a_iA_i+c_{i+1}A_{i+1}
\]
for \(i=1,2\) yields each \(P_i(j)\), the eigenvalue of \(A_i\) on \(E_j\), from \(P_0(j)=1\), \(P_1(j)=\theta_j\). (For \(i=1\), the coefficient \(b_0\) of \(A_0\) is the valency.)

**2. The explicit nontrivial multiplier table.** Put \(h=q^2+q+1\) and \(u=[n-5]_q\ge1\). Substitution and elementary cancellation give
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &P_1&P_2&P_3\\\hline
E_1& q^2(q+1)(1+qu)-1 &
\dfrac{q^2(qu+1)(q^4u-(q+1)^2)}{q+1} &
-\dfrac{q^6u(qu+1)}{q+1}\\[4pt]
E_2& q^3u-q-1 &-q(q^3u+q^2u-1)&q^4u\\[4pt]
E_3&-h&qh&-q^3
\end{array}
\]
All denominators cancel for these integer-valued association-scheme eigenvalues. Every nonprincipal row satisfies \(1+P_1(j)+P_2(j)+P_3(j)=0\), since \(A_0+A_1+A_2+A_3=J\). On \(E_j\), the powered-distance matrix therefore has eigenvalue
\[
\Lambda_j(t)=P_1(j)+2^tP_2(j)+3^tP_3(j).
\]
The constant sector \(E_0\) is irrelevant to conditional negative type.

**3. Eliminate the first and third sectors.** For \(j=1\), the first two coefficients in the table are positive and the third is \(-1-P_1(1)-P_2(1)\). Since \(3^t\ge2^t\ge1\) for \(t\ge0\), one has \(\Lambda_1(t)\le-3^t<0\).

For \(j=3\), \(\Lambda_3(t)=-h+qh2^t-q^33^t\), and \(\Lambda_3(0)=-1\). If \(q\ge3\), then
\[
\frac{q^2}{h}\ge\frac9{13}>\frac23>\frac{\log2}{\log3},
\]
so \(\Lambda_3'(t)<0\) for all \(t\ge0\). For the remaining field size \(q=2\), the strict bound \(3^t\ge2^{3t/2}\), with \(y=2^{t/2}\ge1\), gives
\[
\Lambda_3(t)=-7+14\,2^t-8\,3^t
\le-7+14y^2-8y^3\le-\frac{35}{54}<0.
\]
The last inequality is the exact maximum of the cubic over \(y\ge1\), attained at \(y=7/6\). Hence neither sector can obstruct conditional negative type at any nonnegative power.

**4. Exactly one crossing in the second sector.** The second row gives
\[
\Lambda_2(t)=A-(A+C+1)2^t+C3^t,
\quad A=q^3u-q-1>0,\quad C=q^4u>0.
\]
At zero it equals \(-1\); at one it equals \((q-1)(q^3u+1)>0\). Its derivative is positive at every \(t\ge0\), because
\[
\frac{A+C+1}{C}=1+\frac1q-\frac1{q^3u}\le\frac32
<\frac{\log3}{\log2},
\]
and \((3/2)^t\ge1\). Thus exactly one zero occurs in \((0,1)\), all smaller multipliers are negative, and all larger ones positive. Together with step 3 this proves the complete negative-type interval and identifies \(E_2\) as the only critical nullspace.

**5. Embedding dimension.** The Schoenberg double-centering identity gives \(G_t=-\frac12HD_tH\), positive semidefinite exactly on the above interval. For \(t<\beta_{q,n}\), all three nonconstant sector eigenvalues are strictly negative and \(\operatorname{rank}G_t=N-1\). At the endpoint only \(E_2\) vanishes, with multiplicity \(\left[{n\atop2}\right]_q-\left[{n\atop1}\right]_q\); hence the claimed dimension drop. A Euclidean realization in that rank exists by spectral factorization, and no smaller affine dimension can reproduce the complete squared-distance matrix.

**6. Asymptotic laws.** The derivative at zero is
\[
\Lambda_2'(0)=q^3u\bigl(q\log(3/2)-\log2\bigr)+q\log2.
\]
This is positive and diverges in both asymptotic regimes. For fixed \(q\) and \(n\to\infty\), the factor \(q^3u\) is asymptotic to \(q^{n-2}/(q-1)\). For fixed \(n\ge6\) and \(q\to\infty\), the factor \(q^3u\) is asymptotic to \(q^{n-3}\). Taylor's formula at zero, with the second derivative uniformly of the same asymptotic order as the first in each regime and \(\beta_{q,n}\to0\), yields \(\beta_{q,n}\Lambda_2'(0)\to1\). Substitution proves both stated limits.

## Verification

Run the standard-library checker via `python3 check.py`. It independently generates all \(1395\) three-spaces of \(\mathbb F_2^6\) by span enumeration, verifies the distance-sphere sizes \((1,98,784,512)\) and every intersection-array layer directly, then independently computes the Grassmann spectral recurrence and checks the closed eigenvalue table, signs, multiplicities and trace identities for eight field sizes and six ambient dimensions. It also bisects the critical root and checks the endpoint ranks. The script prints `GRASSMANN_THREE_NEGATIVE_TYPE_PASS` only if every assertion succeeds. These finite tests support but do not replace the general mathematical proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Li and Weston [1] establish the strict-negative-type interval framework and its non-strict endpoint. Sánchez [2] supplies a general finite-metric distance-matrix criterion; it is not an explicit all-prime-power rank-three Grassmann threshold or a critical-embedding-dimension theorem. Aalipour and collaborators [3] study *ordinary* distance spectra, in particular Johnson graphs and certain other distance-regular families, rather than the powered Grassmann metrics. Gavrilyuk and Koolen [4] develop the Grassmann intersection-parameter and spectral framework while studying graph characterizations, without treating these powers or their unique critical degree-two sector.

These classical scheme parameters determine the raw commuting distance matrices, and the new assertion is their uniform powered sign comparison, exact root, endpoint nullity and two asymptotic laws. A broader distance-regular metric theorem or a previously unlocated calculation may subsume the result; the priority comparison is bounded rather than exhaustive. In particular the algebraic-graph-theory phrase 'distance-regular graph of negative type' sometimes refers to classical parameters with negative base \(b\), which is not equivalent to conditional negative definiteness of powered metric distances.

## Limitations

This theorem covers Grassmann graphs with three-dimensional vertices and \(n\ge6\), not the larger-diameter Grassmann families. It does not assert a universal optimization over arbitrary graph metrics, distortion bounds outside the Euclidean-isometry question, or independent mathematical certification. The quantitative embedding conclusions concern powered **squared** distances. Independent review of the derivation and exhaustive historical-priority verification remain outstanding.
