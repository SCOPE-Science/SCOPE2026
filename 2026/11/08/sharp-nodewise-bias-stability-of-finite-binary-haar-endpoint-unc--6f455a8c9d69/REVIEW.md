**Correctness — PASS.** The stated finite-tree Hilbert basis has the prescribed normalization. The complete signed kernel-row identity follows from multiplying the two Haar values and summing the sibling-block masses. The suffix has exact Lipschitz budget one, yielding coordinatewise monotonicity without a differentiability assumption. A backward Bellman induction gives the sharp generationwise envelope. The fair value and first-defect coefficient follow by an explicitly linear recurrence; selecting a node at the earliest biased generation avoids the false comparison of a biased node lying behind a majority ancestor. Exact-rational matrix and recurrence diagnostics corroborate, but do not replace, this all-depth proof.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic qualification.** The closest already-established finite Haar constants apply to uniform or level-dependent splitting. The contribution reviewed here is a global theorem for arbitrary *node-dependent* binary trees, with an exact single-node stability coefficient, a unique fair minimizer, and a demonstrated majority-first norming leaf. The inspected 2013 Osękowski paper treats restricted weak-type bounds, Thiele–Treil–Volberg (2014) treats nonhomogeneous weighted \(L^2\), and Osękowski (2016) treats weak-type \(L^1\)-endpoint inequalities, not the present finite-depth \(L^\infty\) extremal profile. The older Choi martingale-transform paper was identified but not inspected in full; an equivalent isolated result in unindexed literature remains a real priority risk. Search absence by itself is not a novelty certificate.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Endpoint unconditionality on arbitrary finite filtrations is a natural robustness problem because real conditional probabilities vary from node to node. A sharp universal upper envelope alone would not determine stability of fair splitting: the nonzero explicit coefficient at the first biased generation supplies such stability and is exactly attainable for a single biased node. The majority-first counterexample shows that the natural greedy interpretation of a product-filtration optimizer fails in the general setting. These are structural facts for all finite depths, not a dimension-specific table or an unmotivated finite sample.

No matching theorem was found in inspected primary sources, but unidentified translations of Bellman inequalities could cover it; independent bibliographic review remains appropriate. The universal upper envelope need not be the norm of each specified tree, and the first-defect lower bound refers to the *earliest* biased generation.
