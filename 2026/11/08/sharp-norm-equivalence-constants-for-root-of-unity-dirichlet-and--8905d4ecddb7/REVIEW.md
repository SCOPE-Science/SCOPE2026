# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The result follows from two independently identified norms on the polynomial core. The non-extreme pair \(a=(1-z^m)/2\), \(b=(1+z^m)/2\) yields the exact de Branges–Rovnyak tail identity \(2|t_0|^2+4\sum_{j\ge1}|t_j|^2\). Averaging local Dirichlet Gram coefficients over the complete root-of-unity orbit gives \(r|t_0|^2+m\sum_{j\ge1}|t_j|^2+\sum_{j\ge0}|t_j-t_{j+1}|^2\). The resulting half-line Jacobi matrix has the stated essential band and at most one out-of-band eigenvalue per residue. Its recurrence yields the sharp spectral extrema; the eigenvectors or band-edge approximants establish sharpness and attainment. A reproducible checker verifies 36,616 finite exact/algebraic and numerical assertions, without treating finite truncations as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The first-posted 2026 characterization by Bellavita–Dellepiane–Luo (arXiv:2610.36943; Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 5.3) provides equivalence of sets for the inner-symbol family \(b=(1+u)/2\), and this is explicitly acknowledged. Dellepiane–Peloso–Tabacco (arXiv:2505.16572; Theorem 2.1 discussion and §4) describes finite-atomic equality of spaces, without these canonical optimal norm ratios. The 2024/2025 Bellavita–Dellepiane embedding paper and the 2013 Costara–Ransford characterization are closer in topic but the accessible evidence concerns qualitative embeddings or equality, not the present sharp root-of-unity Jacobi constants or their attainment. Searches for aliases involving Clark measures, the multiplier \(1\pm z^m\), best embedding constants, and Jacobi norm comparisons yielded no decisive covering assertion. The accessible prior norm-Gram computation for \(b(z)=c(1+z)/2\) concerns one residue and has no Dirichlet norm comparison. These comparisons do not establish absolute novelty; older special-case calculations could remain undiscovered.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The family is canonical: the equally weighted atoms are exactly the Clark measure at \(1\) of \(u(z)=z^m\). The exact constants are optimal in infinite dimension, distinguish attained from unattained norm embeddings, and show the equivalence-condition number deteriorates at the sharp \(\sqrt m\) rate. This quantifies the cost of identifying two naturally equivalent norms in the finite-atomic examples, a property not given by existence theorems alone.
