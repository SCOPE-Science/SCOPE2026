# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses a finite product for each signed binomial coefficient, exact separation into \(p\)-blocks, a cancellation identity at exponent one, and an elementary binomial valuation bound. The base case computes a nonzero leading unit using \(\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}H_b\equiv1\pmod p\); the inductive step proves a block difference divisible by the precise required power. Therefore the displayed congruence determines the valuation without extrapolating finite experiments. Scope: every odd prime, \(q\ge1\), \(t\ge2\). Risk: none identified in the four independently reconstructed steps; independent audit remains outstanding.

## Originality — PASS, with historical limitations

Calkin's complete 1998 paper, including Lemma 12, establishes binary-weight divisibility; it does not give the odd-prime exact exponent-dependent formula. Sun's full Franel preprint was checked around its main theorems and generalized sums: it addresses different congruences. The closely related 2017 paper by Lengyel could not be fully accessed after open-source attempts; only the abstract, original first page and downstream contextual citations were available. Its stated rate-of-convergence problems use different index patterns in accessible discussions, but absence of stronger coverage in inaccessible sections has not been established. Wiesner's June 2025 question states the exact odd-prime subcase as a conjecture and displays no proof. published mathematical-results index semantic searches for the row, exponent and valuation found no implication of the theorem; a failed search by itself would not be proof of priority. The residual priority risk is explicitly retained.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This solves an expressly posed, numerical-evidence-supported odd-prime branch of a natural generalized Franel valuation question, and upgrades that branch to a congruence for all signed integer exponents. Its mathematical mechanism is a cancellation-based lifting principle specific to the prime-power-minus-one row. The two-adic branch is not claimed.

## Limitations

The signed result for odd exponents is not an unsigned generalized Franel statement; the unsigned corollary requires even exponent. The result does not address the two-adic problem or other rows. Finite tests corroborate only tested ranges. The Lengyel full-text access limitation prevents a complete historical-priority comparison.
