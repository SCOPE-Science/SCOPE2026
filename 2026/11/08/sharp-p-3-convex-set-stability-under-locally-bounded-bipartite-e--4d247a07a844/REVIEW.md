# Review of sharp \(P_3\)-convex-set stability under local bipartite deletion

**Correctness — PASS.** For each proper convex set, the cross-edge deletion degree bound yields two exact incidence inequalities \((n-b)(a-1)\le da\) and \((m-a)(b-1)\le db\). Cases with two selected vertices on only one shore contradict the shore-size threshold; when both shores have at least two selected, monotonicity of \(t/(t-1)\) and \(a\le d+1\) yield \(m\le2d+2\), again impossible. Every remaining set is convex. All coefficients follow, and explicit max-deleted-degree-\(d\) witnesses show the threshold is sharp for every positive integer \(d\). The provided adjacency-based checker successfully tests finite instances; the proof holds for all admissible parameters and graphs.

**Originality — PASS, provisional priority.** The closest motivating source, Dourado–Grippo–Lin–Protti (arXiv:2603.04706v1), covers definitions, exact counting on trees and threshold graphs, split-graph hardness and generic exact algorithms, rather than this local-deletion stability theorem. Centeno–Penso–Rautenbach–Pereira de Sá (2013) study minimum hull/geodetic generators; Cappelle et al. (2022) study hull and interval numbers under diameter-two assumptions. These statements do not determine the all-coefficient polynomial or the optimal bound for the distinct near-complete bipartite class. Targeted searches for crown graphs, near-complete bicliques, bootstrap closed sets, and degree-bounded deletion did not locate an equivalent or stronger covering result. Unindexed prior literature and the unavailable full texts of the older hull-focused papers leave a priority risk; search absence is not proof of novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The class is defined by a natural adversarial local defect budget, with exponentially many possible deletion patterns. All such graphs nevertheless have identical \(P_3\)-convex sets and a four-term cardinality polynomial when both shores pass the optimal threshold. The result precisely identifies which two-vertex seeds spread to every vertex and exhibits an exact phase boundary by constructing a stable four-vertex obstruction one shore size below. It is a structural and enumerative consequence beyond generic computability of the invariant or knowledge of a scalar hull number.

**Limits and evidence.** Only graphs satisfying the specified two-shore lower bounds and per-vertex deletion degree constraint are covered. The sharpness example applies when both shore sizes are reduced together; it is not a complete classification of unbalanced boundary pairs. The verifier exhausts all partial-match deletions of \(K_{5,5}\), samples additional dimensions and checks the infinite witness formula over finitely many \(d\). It does not replace the all-parameter proof. Historical priority is not independently certified.
