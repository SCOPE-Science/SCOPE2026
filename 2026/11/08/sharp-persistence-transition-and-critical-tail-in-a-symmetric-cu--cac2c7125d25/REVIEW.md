# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The centered cubic map, full-branch invariance and multiplier are checked directly. Factoring the fixed-point polynomial gives the exact three-regime sign-drift classification. The survival event reduces without ergodic assumptions to a one-dimensional inverse-branch endpoint interval. For the critical parameter the exact inverse equation gives reciprocal increments \(3\sqrt3-33/y+O(y^{-2})\), proving both asymptotic coefficients, including the logarithmic correction. Finite numerical checks from the included standalone script corroborate, but do not replace, this analytic proof.

The 2024 model article analyzes expanding Lorenz regimes and periodic spiking, but does not give the symmetric fixed-endpoint persistence trichotomy or critical tail coefficients in its inspected sections, appendices and conclusions. The original article is also accessible as an institutional dissertation reprint. The 2023 neutral Lorenz-flow article concerns a different continuous-time system and polynomial mixing, not this particular voltage map or its first-sign-change distribution. Broad parabolic intermittency theory already implies the *qualitative* inverse-time mechanism under suitable hypotheses; this work does not claim that general mechanism. Searches of published mathematical research records and prior local claim history found no equivalent exact combined statement. Residual risk: equivalent unindexed model-specific calculations.

## Value — PASS
The paper itself motivates investigation beyond its expanding regime, particularly slow transients near ghost fixed points. The equality of the two branch images and the fixed endpoints selects a mathematically natural curve, not an arbitrary numerical parameter sample. Along it a complete dynamical threshold occurs at the exact cubic-extremum contact: finite-mean exponential residence, almost-sure escape with infinite mean and an exact logarithmically corrected power tail, then stable permanent residence of a positive-measure set. The precise first-sign-change law matters to finite observation windows in map-based spiking models. It makes no claim about generic neuron models or stationary spike statistics.
