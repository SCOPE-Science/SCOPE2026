# Sharp phase-visibility degrees for two-site finite Heisenberg signals

## Finding

For every integer \(N\ge2\), every nonzero cyclic separation \(d\in\mathbb Z_N\), and every two strictly positive amplitudes \(a,b\), put \(m=N/\gcd(N,d)\), the additive order of \(d\). On the one-parameter family
\[
 x^{(\theta)}=a e_0+b e^{i\theta}e_d,\qquad \theta\in\mathbb R,
\]
consider polynomials in signal coordinates and their complex conjugates that are invariant under the finite cyclic time-frequency (Heisenberg) group \(H_N\). The smallest **total polynomial degree** at which an invariant can vary with the relative phase \(\theta\) is
\[
 \boxed{\delta_H(N,d)=\min\{N,2m\}.}
\]
For polynomials invariant additionally under the entire global scalar circle \(S^1\), the corresponding sharp degree is
\[
 \boxed{\delta_{\rm proj}(N,d)=2m.}
\]
Both thresholds are realized by explicit orbit invariants. At prime cyclic length \(p\), all projective invariants of degree below \(2p\) have constant value on the entire two-site relative-phase family. This is a **nongeneric lower-degree obstruction** compatible with generic degree-six projective separation in the literature.

## Assumptions and scope

Indices are interpreted modulo \(N\); \(e_j\) are standard coordinate vectors in \(\mathbb C^N\), and \(\omega=\exp(2\pi i/N)\). The finite Heisenberg action is generated by \((T_kx)_j=x_{j+k}\), \((M_rx)_j=\omega^{rj}x_j\), and \((Z_hx)_j=\omega^h x_j\), for \(k,r,h\in\mathbb Z_N\). An **unitary polynomial invariant** is a complex-valued polynomial in the independent formal variables \(x_j,\overline{x_j}\), evaluated on actual complex vectors and invariant under these actions. Its degree counts both holomorphic and antiholomorphic factors. A **projective invariant** is invariant under \(H_N\) and also under \(x\mapsto\lambda x\) for every \(|\lambda|=1\). The threshold is the first total degree at which some such invariant is nonconstant as \(\theta\) varies with fixed positive \(a,b\). It does not measure the degree needed for full global orbit separation.

## Proof

**1. Weight selection.** Any polynomial invariant is, in particular, invariant under all modulations \(M_r\) and central phases \(Z_h\). These two commuting diagonal actions make each monomial an eigenvector. Since distinct formal monomials are linearly independent, invariance forces each monomial with nonzero coefficient to have both weights zero modulo \(N\). Any monomial that does not vanish identically on the displayed family takes the form
\[
 x_0^u x_d^v\overline{x_0}^{s}\overline{x_d}^{t},
 \qquad u,v,s,t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]
Its total degree is \(L=u+v+s+t\), and its restriction equals
\[
 a^{u+s}b^{v+t}e^{i(v-t)\theta}.
\]
The central-weight and modulation-weight constraints are, respectively,
\[
 u+v-s-t\equiv0\pmod N,\qquad d(v-t)\equiv0\pmod N.
\]
Because \(m\) is the order of \(d\), the latter congruence is equivalent to \(m\mid(v-t)\).

**2. Finite-group lower bound.** Suppose \(L<\min\{N,2m\}\). The first congruence has an integer left-hand side of absolute value at most \(L<N\), so it forces \(u+v=s+t=k\), with \(L=2k\). As \(2k<2m\), both \(v,t\le k<m\); hence \(|v-t|<m\). Together with \(m\mid(v-t)\), this gives \(v=t\). Thus every monomial surviving on the two-site family is phase-independent. Monomials containing any other coordinate vanish there. Therefore every invariant of degree less than \(\min\{N,2m\}\) is constant on that family.

**3. Circle-invariant lower bound.** Under an arbitrary scalar \(\lambda\in S^1\), each monomial has weight \(\lambda^{u+v-s-t}\). Circle invariance therefore forces \(u+v=s+t\) with no restriction on total degree. If \(L<2m\), the argument in Step 2 yields \(v=t\). Thus every projective invariant of degree less than \(2m\) is phase-blind.

**4. Matching witnesses.** Define
\[
 Q_d(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_N} x_{j+d}^{m}\overline{x_j}^{m},
 \qquad I_N(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_N} x_j^{N}.
\]
Shifts reindex both sums. Modulation multiplies each term of \(Q_d\) by \(\omega^{rmd}=1\), because \(N\mid md\); arbitrary global phases cancel between its two factors. Hence \(Q_d\) is projectively invariant and has degree \(2m\). The polynomial \(I_N\), already employed by Katz [1], is invariant under shifts, modulations and central \(N\)-th-root phases, and has degree \(N\). On the family, \(I_N(x^{(\theta)})=a^N+b^Ne^{iN\theta}\), which is nonconstant since \(b>0\).

If \(d\not\equiv-d\pmod N\), exactly one summand of \(Q_d\) survives, giving \(Q_d(x^{(\theta)})=a^m b^m e^{im\theta}\). If \(d\equiv-d\pmod N\), then \(m=2\) and exactly two summands survive, giving \(Q_d(x^{(\theta)})=2a^2 b^2\cos(2\theta)\). In either case the response is nonconstant for all positive \(a,b\), proving the claimed upper bounds and all equalities.

**5. Prime-group consequence.** If \(N=p\) is prime, every nonzero \(d\) has order \(p\). Hence \(\delta_{\rm proj}=2p\) and \(\delta_H=p\). For primes larger than three, this is an arbitrarily large nongeneric obstruction despite the generic degree-six projective-separation result [1]. In fact a continuum of phases remains distinct modulo the finite modulation subgroup, except at discrete congruences; constant low-degree invariants therefore fail even to separate those relative-phase orbits.

## Verification

The included standalone `CHECK.py` enumerates exponent vectors for degrees up to thirteen and cyclic lengths from two through sixteen, verifying both lower-degree selection rules and the claimed first admissible degree whenever it is in range. It also evaluates both witnesses directly on two-site signals and checks their invariance under shifts, modulations and appropriate scalar phases through cyclic length twenty-eight. The checker passed 3,016 assertion groups. The infinite and all-degree theorem follows from the weight-congruence proof, not from the finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Katz [1], §§3–6 and Theorem 6.1, proves that degree-six unitary invariants distinguish **generic** Heisenberg orbits modulo a global phase for \(N>3\), and exhibits the degree-\(N\) polynomial invariant \(I_N\). The new claim asks a different question: **at what exact total degree can any invariant first respond to the relative phase on a specified two-point support?** Generic separation alone does not imply a support-specific response bound. The lower bound above is derived independently from diagonal character weights, not as a restatement of generic orbit separation. Bandeira et al. [2] provide general invariant-based orbit-recovery theory but do not state this exact finite-Heisenberg two-site phase threshold in the inspected material. The expression \(I_N\) and the underlying character-weight selection principle are prior tools, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

This is a phase-sensitivity threshold, **not** a claim that the first nonconstant invariant alone separates all distinct phase orbits. In particular, at the antipodal separation the degree-four witness is real-valued and does not distinguish all phases. The proof concerns exact polynomial invariants, two-site support, and the specified finite Heisenberg action; it does not give noise stability, sample complexity or a comparable result for three or more occupied coordinates. Equivalent formulations in older invariant-theory treatments are a residual publication-priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
