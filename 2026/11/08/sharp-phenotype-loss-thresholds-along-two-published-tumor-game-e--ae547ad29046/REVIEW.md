The matrices were reconstructed from the January 22, 2026 primary preprint and the tangent-space symmetric forms have exact leading principal minors of signs \((- , + , -)\) at all three endpoints. Convex interpolation therefore preserves strict conditional negativity. The linear equal-payoff systems yield two cubic coordinate crossings, whose monotone derivatives prove unique thresholds. Supported face coordinates remain positive, and their missing-strategy invasion gaps are negative precisely beyond the respective thresholds. The relative-entropy identity proves convergence of every interior trajectory, with the log-abundance identity yielding the exact extinction exponents. The standalone rational checker verifies these algebraic identities. Scope excludes zero-support starts and stochastic dynamics.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

Rodrigues (https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.1761), sections 3–7 and Appendix A, publishes the three matrices, generic face-solving equations, and qualitative simulation comparisons. The examined text does not give the interpolated cubic extinction thresholds, exact endpoint compositions, or phenotype-specific asymptotic invasion losses. Hofbauer and Sandholm's *Stable Games* (https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/14466/) already proves classical global convergence in strictly stable games; the abstract Lyapunov method is not new and is not claimed as new. The novel portion consists of the sharp quantitative ecological classification on the two natural matrix segments, requiring matrix-specific algebra not provided by the generic theorem. Semantic research-record queries and accessible local claim scans did not identify covering results. Search absence is not proof of universal novelty; equivalent unpublished work remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The paper uses precisely these three matrices to compare neutral, acidic and buffered tumor environments. The two exact thresholds establish where their four-phenotype coexistence description ceases to hold along the simplest interpolations: loss of P at about \(25.69\%\) of the acidic path and loss of R only at about \(99.47\%\) of the buffering path. The latter has a very small terminal exponential extinction rate, \(1/3340\), making the strict extinction conclusion difficult to infer from finite-time simulation. The mathematical result is useful for distinguishing permanent coexistence from slow exclusion without pretending that a linear matrix parameter is measured pH.
