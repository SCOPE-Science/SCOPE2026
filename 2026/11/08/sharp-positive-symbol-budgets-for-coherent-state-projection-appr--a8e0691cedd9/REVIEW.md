# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The unit-normalized Gaussian coherent projector and the positive Toeplitz quantization are explicitly defined. The proof uses two necessary matrix-element constraints (vacuum and the average of the degree-one states) and the monotone-likelihood-ratio identity \(q_{d+1}(s)=s q_d(s)/d\). The sharp bathtub inequality, including its equality case for arbitrary nonradial measurable symbols, is derived directly. The one-variable minimax has one crossing for budgets above \(1/2\), and the candidate radial-ball operator's complete homogeneous-degree eigenvalue ordering verifies exact attainment. The high-budget expansion follows from integer Gamma small-argument asymptotics. A finite independent calculation of Gamma cumulative probabilities and spectral ordering corroborated the stated formulas, but is not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified best knowledge)

The motivating 2026 Fourier-recognition theorem explains when a bounded-symbol Toeplitz representation exists, not the symbol-budget cost for approximating a coherent rank-one projection. Borichev–Rozenblum excludes exact nonzero finite-rank representation, but gives no quantitative positive bounded-symbol approximation law. Galbis's Fock radial estimates involve the operator norm and symbol integral mass, not a constrained distance to a coherent projection; Dewage–Ólafsson provide quasi-radial spectral formulas, but no minimax optimization or uniqueness. Searches across numerical, phase-space, anti-Wick, Toeplitz, and gamma-tail descriptions found no statement with the exact minimax formula and \(d\)-dimensional sharp asymptotic. This is a best-knowledge priority assessment, not an assertion of proven uniqueness across all literature. A general rearrangement/minimax theorem may conceivably cover the result without referring to Toeplitz operators explicitly.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A rank-one coherent projection is an atomic quantization target that is excluded from exactly represented nonzero bounded-symbol Toeplitz operators. The theorem quantifies the cost of approximating it with a positive symbol capped in \(L^\infty\): a unique bang-bang optimizer, a sharp budget phase change at \(1/2\), an exact gamma-root error formula for every dimension, and a dimension-dependent inverse-budget exponent. These are natural numerical characteristics of the quantization scheme rather than arbitrary cuts of a table; the exact rate can inform positive phase-space mask design.
