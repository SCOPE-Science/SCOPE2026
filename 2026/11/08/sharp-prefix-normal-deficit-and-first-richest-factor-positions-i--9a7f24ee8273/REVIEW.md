# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The substitution's exact four-block structure and parity-dependent factor-weight maps give correct all-length recurrences for minimum and maximum ones. Subtracting the actual prefix weight gives the displayed base-four recursion, and induction proves its sharp envelope. A second induction on the four block starting residues establishes that the *first* globally maximal factor at lengths \(N_r=(4^r-1)/3\) starts at index \(N_r\), not merely that a richest factor exists. Two finite verification programs reproduced the direct factor counts, exact recurrence instances and witness uniqueness. Infinite assertions follow from the two mathematical inductions; no finite experiment was extrapolated to infinity.

## Originality — PASS, with significant priority risk

The nearest primary literature is Parreau–Rigo–Rowland–Vandomme (arXiv:1405.3532), which supplies powerful recurrence information on period-doubling abelian extrema, and Burcsi–Fici–Lipták–Ruskey–Sawada (arXiv:1401.6346), which defines the prefix-normal factor-weight comparison. The abelian work comes close to the **numerical** deficit recursion and is explicitly acknowledged as such, but extremal values do not establish *where the first maximizing factor occurs*. The all-scale position-rigidity theorem and its exact earliest index are the distinguishing contributions. A known unbounded-deficit result concerns the ordinary paperfolding word, not the period-doubling fixed point. published-finding corpus and accessible prior-claim searches found no dominating earliest-position statement. An unindexed equivalent first-occurrence theorem or an overlooked implication in the full factor-occurrence literature remains a real risk.

## Value — PASS

Prefix-normal deficits quantify the failure of a fixed reference prefix to represent the heaviest jumbled factor. The result gives an all-length exact digit recursion and optimal logarithmic envelope, as well as a constructive infinitely repeated obstruction to prefix repair. More distinctively, it identifies the *earliest* maximally one-rich factor at a nontrivial infinite family of lengths: the maximum is first reached by the immediately adjacent length-matched block. This is positional information absent from ordinary abelian-complexity counts, with a concrete role in first-occurrence and jumbled-index problems. It is not a small finite-table increment.
