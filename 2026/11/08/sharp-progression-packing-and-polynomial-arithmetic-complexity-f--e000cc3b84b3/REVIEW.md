# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The divisor-of-all-gaps lemma is an exact consequence of the Euclidean identity for \(b^u-1\). The inequalities preserve all integer quantifiers and cover composite bases, arbitrary positive progression steps, and origins at zero. Equality analysis distinguishes targets of one or two ones from rigid targets of at least three. In the complexity argument, three ones imply a proper divisor relation on exponent differences; the first two one positions together with their exponent gap determine the full support, yielding the stated count. Finite enumerations cross-check but do not replace these proofs.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The main primary paper arXiv:2309.03180 establishes general asymptotic automatic-sequence results, not this exact power-support packing law or polynomial upper bound. The logarithmic support envelope for Abelian pattern Sturmian words has a visually similar binary sparse example but a different quantifier domain and invariant; neither its published summary nor the primary pattern-complexity abstract implies this arithmetic-progression bound. Accessible prior work on the same power-support sequences concerns contiguous factors, palindromes and absent words, not unrestricted arithmetic sampling. An unindexed equivalent or indirect specialist corollary remains a residual bibliographic risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result settles a natural all-base extremal statistic for the arithmetic-factor language of a canonical sparse automatic word, rigidly classifies every shortest high-weight witness, and supplies an explicit quadratic-logarithmic factor-complexity upper bound beyond the general subexponential statement. The polynomial counting argument relies on bounded exponent configurations rather than simply reformulating the logarithmic weight ceiling. Exact total complexity and matching lower bounds are not established.
