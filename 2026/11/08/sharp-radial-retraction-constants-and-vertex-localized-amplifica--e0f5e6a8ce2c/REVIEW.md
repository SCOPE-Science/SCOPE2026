# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The outer derivative on an active facet is exactly \(r^{-1}(I-u\otimes\varphi)\). The norm of the rank-one map is reconstructed from the determinant identity (5), not guessed from the known Radon classification. Polygon vertex maximization and facet convexity reduce the global operator constant to endpoints. A rotation parity test determines both the chord gauge and the exact determinant maximum. The nonattainment assertion follows because the local amplification is strictly less than the sharp expansive constant off boundary vertices, while a generic segment meets the exceptional set in arclength zero. The standalone independent finite geometry checks pass; they corroborate rather than establish universal statements.

## Originality
PASS, qualified. The nonexpansive congruence class is a classical Radon result, and the quantitative regular-octagon instance is already covered by a stronger weighted-octagon profile. Both are credited and excluded from the claim of novelty. Inspected published results on Zbăganu and Euler--Lagrange constants have the same numerical trigonometric factor, but their statements do not imply this radial map's sharp Lipschitz constant without an additional theorem. The all-even regular-polygon quantitative law and its vertex-localized nonattainment were not located in the queried records or relevant accessible complete papers. Older inaccessible primary papers leave an explicit bibliographic risk. Failure to locate an equivalent theorem is not proof of absolute priority.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The radial map is a classical canonical nonlinear retraction with an intrinsic sharp quantitative Banach-space constant. The formula resolves the full regular even-polygon family, quantifies the transition from nonexpansive Radon polygons to expansive ones, and gives all possible sharp local boundary configurations plus strict two-point nonattainment. It goes beyond a small numerical improvement or a single polygon calculation; it makes precise how infinitesimal retraction amplification varies with polygon side count and parity.

## Closest literature and limits
Martini--Swanepoel (2004) covers Radon nonexpansivity; an earlier weighted-octagon calculation covers the regular octagon; related polygonal Zbăganu and Euler--Lagrange results share the numerical secant-square constant but differ in the defined invariant. The 1967 and 1974 original papers could not be fully inspected. The theorem does not address minimum Lipschitz constants over arbitrary retractions, nonregular polygons, or higher-dimensional balls.
