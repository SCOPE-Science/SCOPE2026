# Scientific review: random intrinsic distances in truncated hyperbolic cusps

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses the exact upper-half-plane distance formula and geodesic convexity of a horoball, so the intrinsic quotient distance is attained by a nearest horizontal translate. Normalized hyperbolic area gives independent exponential height coordinates. The minimum and difference of the two heights are independent with rates two and one, and the vertically rescaled circular separation has the explicitly integrated triangular density. For radii at or beyond the stated geometric threshold, an exact quadratic root comparison makes the one-dimensional tail integral valid; no extrapolation to smaller radii is used. A uniform fixed-width binomial expansion, with the exact second and fourth triangular moments, supplies the first three coefficients and a controlled remainder. A common-variable coupling proves strict stochastic order and the tail determines the exponential-moment cutoff. The bundled independent-coordinate simulator and quadrature checker ran successfully on 1,100,000 sample pairs. Numerical checks do not replace these analytic arguments.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority

Literature and research-record queries used the aliases *truncated parabolic cylinder*, *horoball quotient*, *finite-area hyperbolic cusp*, *uniform random point distance*, *intrinsic geodesic pair distance* and *far-tail inverse width*. The first analyzes spectral resonances using standard cusp coordinates; the second studies recurrent excursions of a single typical geodesic. Their mathematical objects, sampling measures and conclusions do not imply this independent-pair distance law. Existing indexed random-distance treatments of bounded hyperbolic disks also differ in geometry and in the lack of a parabolic quotient. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact far-tail law is a metric-probability invariant for a natural finite-area noncompact negatively curved surface with boundary. The leading term is independent of the horocycle length, but the second term recovers that geometric parameter, yielding a concrete inverse-rigidity theorem rather than only a decay rate. Strict stochastic ordering provides a global comparison, and the moment threshold identifies the precise exponential integrability boundary. These are motivated by statistical geometry and the recovery of noncompact cusp geometry from random pairwise distances, not by an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Boundaries and residual risk

The result concerns intrinsic distances inside a truncated cusp, not ambient distances in an unspecified complete hyperbolic surface. The exact integral is only asserted beyond its proved radius threshold, the asymptotics fix the width, and the historical search may be incomplete. All historical-priority conclusions are provisional; correctness and value here are mathematical evaluations of the supplied claim.
