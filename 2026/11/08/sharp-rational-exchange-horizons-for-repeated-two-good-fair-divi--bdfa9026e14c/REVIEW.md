# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is an exact characterization, not a computational conjecture. The single-round EF1 condition forces opposite recipients of the two strictly positive goods. Aggregate envy then forces precisely half of each item type to each agent, excluding odd horizons and yielding a central binomial count of round schedules. For aggregate Pareto dominance, every competitor is uniquely described by two integers giving the numbers of the two good types assigned to agent 1. A weak improvement of both utilities can only be an exchange of goods in opposite directions. Its slope must fall between the two value ratios; when these ratios differ, at least one improvement is strict. The integer exchange numerator must not exceed half the horizon. Endpoint fractions prove the sharp integer-valuation bound and the consecutive-ratio family proves equality. Independent finite direct enumerations corroborate the whole proof. Limits: no extension to mixed/zero values or opposite rankings is asserted.

## Value — PASS

The initiating article asks when overall efficiency and fairness coexist with local fairness. Its impossibility example leaves open what happens for arbitrary values and horizons even with just two recurring goods. The result answers that natural classification completely on the common-ranking positive domain, finds a sharp integer-input worst-case horizon, and shows that inefficient balanced schedules can persist for arbitrarily many periods before a discrete trading opportunity appears. The central-binomial count makes the multiplicity of successful histories explicit. The result is an exact structural boundary, not a routine recomputation of a known table.
