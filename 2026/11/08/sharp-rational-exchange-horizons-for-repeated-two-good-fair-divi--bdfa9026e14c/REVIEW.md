# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Two positive goods cannot go to one agent in an EF1 round. If agent 1 gets A in x of the split rounds, the two overall envy inequalities give x >= k/2 and x <= k/2 under the strict shared ranking. At k=2m each gets m of each type, with binomial(2m,m) choices of orientation order. Any nonzero Pareto improvement over the balanced counts must transfer opposite types in opposite directions; the two weak utility inequalities give exactly the closed interval of exchange ratios t/s between the agents' distinct ratios, with the direction forced by their order. An endpoint leaves one agent indifferent and strictly improves the other. The arbitrary competing total counts are realizable without imposing split rounds; since t>s the binding feasibility bound is t<=m. Distinct endpoints enclose a rational; equal ratios imply that any weak improvement is neutral for both. Reduced integer endpoints give the numerator bound. For the sharp family the permitted right endpoint is (h+1)/h and, for h>=2, all t<=h and s<t give t/s>=h/(h-1)>(h+1)/h; h=1 is immediate. The witness gives utility changes (0,1).
