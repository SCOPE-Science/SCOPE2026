# Scientific review: joint odd/even nonoverlined gaps

## Correctness — PASS
The removal/reinsertion operation is a bijection because a mandatory nonoverlined part can always be removed even if its size also occurs once overlined. The exact joint survival identity holds at every integer boundary, including a negative residual weight. Overpartition log-concavity (Engel 2014/2017, with the initial coefficients checked) makes the joint survival no greater than the product of marginals, while the weighted-layer representation of squares turns it into a covariance statement. The strict inequality at index \(n\ge4\) follows from an explicit nonzero \(r=s=1\) term. The asymptotic constant is reconstructed from Sills's Rademacher main term: the mixed logarithmic difference is \(-\pi AB/(4n^{3/2})\) to leading order, and the two weighted Gaussian sums each asymptotically equal \(4n/\pi^2\). Exponentially decaying marginal bounds control every excluded tail. The checker reproduces \(208\) combinatorial bijection instances and \(108036\) finite inequalities, plus five finite covariance benchmarks. Finite checks alone do not prove the limit.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Aricheta and Donato (arXiv:2309.04398) derive the single, all-size **nonoverlined** mex marginal. Donato (arXiv:2507.04402) studies the mex of overlined-only and all-part versions. The 2026 paper of Veena and Fathima (arXiv:2609.32207; first posted 2026-09-26) weights the **odd values of the full mex**; it does not study the least missing odd size as a separate variable. Sarkar, Thejitha and Fathima (arXiv:2609.21649; first posted 2026-09-18) study a full-part mex with even-value restriction. He et al. (arXiv:2501.12864) study consecutive absent chains and separable classes. Bhoria, Eyyunni and Santra (arXiv:2604.12557, first posted 2026-04-14; primary MSC 11P81/11P83/11P84) count *all* missing integers below the largest part in ordinary partitions and overpartitions, rather than the joint first missing nonoverlined odd and even sizes. None of these results determines the joint **odd and even nonoverlined** gap survival law or the asymptotic squared-gap covariance. Engel and Mukherjee establish earlier one-dimensional log-concavity/finite-difference inputs, and Sills supplies the known overpartition Rademacher expansion. The contribution claimed is the joint two-color statistic and the sharp mixed covariance coefficient, not those ingredients. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches did not identify an equivalent claim. Undiscovered equivalence through general conditioning theorems for random partitions remains possible, so historical priority is qualified.

## Value — PASS
A pair of gap statistics can have independent weak limits and still exhibit a substantial, quantifiable negative finite-size dependence. The explicit \(-4/\pi^3\) coefficient identifies the first nonvanishing mixed second-moment correction, which cannot be inferred from marginal mex asymptotics alone. The result covers every overpartition size, proves the exact finite-size dependence sign, and characterizes the natural \(n^{1/4}\) gap scale and its second-moment dependence. It is motivated by the active 2025–2026 literature on parity-refined overpartition mex quantities, not by an arbitrary residue window.

## Closest literature and limitations
The strongest coverage risk is an unlocated general theorem on joint multiplicative partition statistics that might already imply the mixed coefficient. Sources explicitly treating even/odd statistics generally condition on the parity of the **single** mex, an inequivalent variable. The present result excludes interpretations that count overlined-only appearances as present; it supplies no sharp uniform error term beyond asymptotic equivalence.
