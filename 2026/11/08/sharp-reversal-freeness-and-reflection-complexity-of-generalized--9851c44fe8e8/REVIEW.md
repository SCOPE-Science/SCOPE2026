If a length-three factor and its reversal both occur, the two increments are exactly \(\pm1\) in opposite order, so the factor is palindromic. If a length-four factor and its reversal occurred, a \((-1,+1,-1)\) increment pattern would occur; divisibility by the base at distance two excludes this for bases at least three, while the neighboring even-integer valuation-one obstruction excludes base two. Longer reversed pairs are thereby excluded. The resulting gcd cases for length-two pairs and the length-three palindrome construction verify all boundary cases.
