# Sharp RK4 positivity horizons on triangular graph diffusion

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge4\), let \(T_n=L(K_n)=J(n,2)\) be the triangular graph with vertex set the two-element subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), adjacent exactly when the subsets intersect. Let \(L_n\) be its unweighted combinatorial Laplacian, and apply the classical four-stage, fourth-order Runge–Kutta method to \(u'=-L_nu\). Its one-step matrix is
\[
P_n(h)=I-hL_n+\frac{h^2L_n^2}{2}-\frac{h^3L_n^3}{6}+\frac{h^4L_n^4}{24},\qquad h\ge0.
\]
Define
\[
F_n(h)=24-(36n-72)h+(28n^2-88n+48)h^2-(15n^3-62n^2+68n-24)h^3.
\]
There is exactly one positive root \(\tau_n\) of \(F_n\), and the **exact endpoint-positivity horizon** is
\[
P_n(h)\text{ entrywise nonnegative}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\le h\le\tau_n.
\]
Let \(\alpha\in(2,3)\) be the unique positive root of \(\alpha^3-4\alpha^2+12\alpha-24=0\), numerically \(\alpha=2.78529356340528\ldots\). The exact spectral-stability horizon is \(h_{\mathrm{stab}}=\alpha/[2(n-1)]\). The two horizons coincide when \(n=4\), but \(\tau_n<h_{\mathrm{stab}}\) for every \(n\ge5\). Thus every \(n\ge5\) has a nonempty interval of *spectrally stable but non-positive* RK4 steps.

Moreover, if \(\gamma=1.03893381686387\ldots\) is the unique positive root of \(24-36\gamma+28\gamma^2-15\gamma^3=0\), then
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\tau_n=\gamma,\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tau_n}{h_{\mathrm{stab}}}=\frac{2\gamma}{\alpha}=0.7460138712227\ldots .
\]
The complete positivity horizon applies, without alteration, to *any* strongly regular graph with intersection parameters \(\bigl(\binom n2,2(n-2),n-2,4\bigr)\), since the proof uses only these parameters. In particular, this includes the nonisomorphic Chang graphs with the same parameters as \(T_8\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and unweighted; their Laplacian is \(L=D-A\). The graph \(T_n\) has \(\binom n2\) vertices and degree \(2(n-2)\). Positivity means that for **every** initial vector \(u_0\ge0\), the **end-of-step** vector \(P_n(h)u_0\ge0\). It does not assert positivity of intermediate RK stages. Spectral stability means that every eigenvalue of the one-step matrix has modulus at most one. The timestep \(h\) is an arbitrary nonnegative real number, without a priori stability restriction. All thresholds include their endpoints.

## Proof
Write \(A\) for the adjacency matrix of \(T_n\), \(J\) for the all-ones matrix, and \(d=2(n-2)\). For adjacent two-subsets there are exactly \(n-2\) common neighbors; for disjoint two-subsets there are exactly four. Consequently
\[
A^2=(d-4)I+(n-6)A+4J,\qquad AJ=dJ,\qquad L_n=dI-A.
\]
These identities reduce every polynomial in \(L_n\) to the three-dimensional algebra generated by \(I,A,J\). Direct multiplication of the RK4 polynomial gives, for distinct vertices \(u,v\),
\[
(P_n(h))_{uv}=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{hF_n(h)}{24},&u\sim v,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{h^2}{6}\bigl[12-(12n-8)h+(7n^2-10n+4)h^2\bigr],&u\not\sim v.
\end{cases}
\]
The nonedge bracket is strictly positive for all real \(h\): its leading coefficient is positive and its discriminant is
\[
(12n-8)^2-48(7n^2-10n+4)=-192n^2+288n-128<0
\quad(n\ge4).
\]
The diagonal entries are also strictly positive for every \(h\ge0\). Indeed the scalar RK4 stability polynomial \(r(z)=\sum_{j=0}^4(-z)^j/j!\) satisfies \(r(z)>e^{-z}>0\) for every \(z>0\) by the signed fifth-order Taylor remainder. Since \(L_n\) is real symmetric and positive semidefinite, \(r(hL_n)\) is positive definite and has strictly positive diagonal entries.

It remains to determine the sign of \(F_n\). Put \(a_n=15n^3-62n^2+68n-24\), \(b_n=28n^2-88n+48\), and \(c_n=36n-72\), all positive for \(n\ge4\). We have
\[
F_n'(h)=-c_n+2b_n h-3a_nh^2,
\]
whose quadratic discriminant is
\[
4b_n^2-12a_nc_n=-16Q(n),\qquad
Q(n)=209n^4-1252n^3+2576n^2-2208n+720.
\]
For \(n=4+t\) with \(t\ge0\), \(Q(4+t)=209t^4+2092t^3+7616t^2+11808t+6480>0\). Hence \(F_n'<0\) on the real line. Because \(F_n(0)=24\) and \(F_n(h)\to-\infty\) as \(h\to\infty\), it has exactly one positive root and the positivity equivalence follows.

The adjacency eigenvalues are \(d,n-4,-2\), so the Laplacian eigenvalues are \(0,n,2(n-1)\). The identity
\[
r(z)-1=\frac{z}{24}(z^3-4z^2+12z-24)
\]
and the strict positivity of \(r(z)\) show that \(|r(z)|\le1\) exactly for \(0\le z\le\alpha\); the cubic has strictly positive derivative \(3z^2-8z+12\), so \(\alpha\) is its unique real root. Therefore \(h_{\mathrm{stab}}=\alpha/[2(n-1)]\).

Substitution and reduction by \(\alpha^3=4\alpha^2-12\alpha+24\) give the exact identity
\[
8(n-1)^3F_n\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{2(n-1)}\right)
=-4(n-4)\left[n^2(\alpha^2-9\alpha+42)+n(6\alpha-60)+24\right].
\]
Since \(2<\alpha<3\), the square bracket exceeds or equals \(24(n-1)^2>0\). Hence it vanishes at \(n=4\) and is strictly negative for \(n\ge5\), proving the strict positivity–stability gap.

Finally, \(F_n(x/n)\to24-36x+28x^2-15x^3\) uniformly on bounded \(x\)-intervals. The limiting cubic is strictly decreasing (its derivative has negative discriminant) and has one positive root \(\gamma\). Taking two points on either side of \(\gamma\), uniform convergence brackets the unique root \(n\tau_n\), so \(n\tau_n\to\gamma\). The ratio limit follows. Every step relied only on \((d,\lambda,\mu)=(2(n-2),n-2,4)\), proving the final parameter-class statement.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 standard-library script `verify.py` reconstructs \(T_n\) for \(4\le n\le8\), checks the common-neighbor identities, constructs the RK4 matrix by exact rational matrix multiplication at four rational timesteps, and compares every offdiagonal entry against both closed formulas. It checked 6,024 offdiagonal entries with exact equality, verified positive diagonals and row sums, checked coefficient discriminants for \(4\le n\le100\), and bracketed roots on several sizes. Run `python3 verify.py`. These finite checks supplement, but do not replace, the all-\(n\) proof above.

Representative sharp horizons are \(\tau_4=0.464215593900880\ldots\), \(\tau_5=0.319767107786475\ldots\), and \(\tau_8=0.166471538671765\ldots\). A step just beyond \(\tau_n\) makes **every adjacent offdiagonal entry negative**, even while the update remains spectrally stable for \(n\ge5\) within the stated interval.

## Relationship to prior work
Gottlieb, Shu and Tadmor (2001) establish general strong-stability-preserving constructions and the distinction between convex-stability guarantees and other stability properties; their analysis does not give the present graph-specific sharp endpoint-positivity horizon. Horváth (2005) establishes positivity thresholds for large classes of Runge–Kutta initial-value problems and discusses invariant positive cones; its inspected problem classes and examples do not provide this all-\(n\) triangular-graph polynomial threshold. Fekete, Ketcheson and Lóczi (2016 preprint) study positivity of convective semi-discretizations and centered heat-equation stencils, not the Johnson graph \(J(n,2)\). General spectral facts about triangular graphs are classical. The contribution here is the complete sharp graph-family endpoint classification and its strict, asymptotically quantified separation from scalar spectral stability. No claim of a new Runge–Kutta scheme, a general RK positivity theorem, or universal method-of-lines positivity is made.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns a fixed symmetric unweighted graph generator, arbitrary nonnegative input vectors, and the **end-of-step** RK4 matrix. It does not ensure nonnegative intermediate stages, nonlinear positivity, or the same threshold after changing edge weights, graph family, or integrator. The numerical root values are approximations; the exact roots are defined algebraically. Publication-priority comparisons cannot rule out uncatalogued specialist literature on RK4 matrix functions of Johnson association schemes. Independent validation has not been performed.
