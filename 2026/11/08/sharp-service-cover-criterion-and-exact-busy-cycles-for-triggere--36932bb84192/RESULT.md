# Sharp service-cover criterion and exact busy cycles for triggered-cluster queues

## Finding

Consider a work-conserving single-server queue with clusters of jobs. A cluster starts at a primary epoch \(t_i\) and carries finitely many positive service requirements \(x_{i,0},\ldots,x_{i,m_i}\), arriving at relative epochs \(0=d_{i,0}\le d_{i,1}\le\cdots\le d_{i,m_i}\). No later arrivals are generated by jobs outside their own cluster. Couple this *staggered queue* to the *batch queue* that moves every cluster's entire work \(B_i=\sum_{j=0}^{m_i}x_{i,j}\) to its primary epoch without changing anything else.

The **service-cover criterion** is
\[
  d_{i,j}\le\sum_{h=0}^{j-1}x_{i,h}
  \quad(1\le j\le m_i).
\]

**Deterministic theorem.** For every locally finite primary schedule, every family of finite, positive service marks satisfying service cover, and an initially empty pair of queues with no pending triggered jobs, the two queues have identical maximal busy intervals (defined as intervals separated by positive-duration idle gaps). Conversely, if a single cluster violates service cover, there exists an arrival schedule, namely this cluster alone, for which the busy intervals differ. Thus service cover is necessary and sufficient for busy-interval equality *uniformly over all primary schedules*. The condition allows a trigger to occur after the primary job's own service requirement, provided the earlier released secondary work bridges the intervening time.

**Poisson corollary and sharpness.** Let the primary epochs be a homogeneous Poisson process of rate \(\lambda>0\). Suppose cluster marks are independent and identically distributed across primaries, independent of the primary process, with finite cluster size, finite strictly positive service requirements, finite nonnegative offsets, and \(\mathbb E B<\infty\). Assume \(\rho=\lambda\mathbb E B<1\). Then the coupled queues, started empty and without pending triggers, have identical busy and idle cycles almost surely *if and only if* the mark distribution satisfies service cover with probability one. If service cover has positive failure probability, the first cycle differs with positive probability.

Under service cover, write \(L\) for the length of a busy period and \(K\) for the number of primary clusters entering during that period, including the initiating cluster. Their joint transform \(F(z,s)=\mathbb E[z^K e^{-sL}]\) is the solution in \([0,1]\) obtained by the branching-process construction of
\[
 F(z,s)=z\,\mathbb E\!\left[\exp\bigl(-[s+\lambda(1-F(z,s))]B\bigr)\right],
 \quad 0\le z\le1,\ s\ge0.
\]
In particular, the busy-period Laplace transform \(\beta(s)=F(1,s)\) obeys the classical batch-queue fixed-point equation,
\[
 \mathbb E L=\frac{\mathbb E B}{1-\rho},
\]
and each successive idle duration is independent exponential of rate \(\lambda\). The busy/idle cycle law is independent of the distribution of offsets, including dependencies within each cluster, as long as service cover holds.

When \(B=b>0\) is deterministic and \(\rho=\lambda b<1\), the number of primary clusters in a busy period follows the exact Borel law
\[
 \Pr\{K=k\}=\frac{e^{-\rho k}(\rho k)^{k-1}}{k!},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,
\]
with \(L=bK\). The Borel formula is a known batch-queue result, not a new probability distribution.

## Assumptions and scope

All queues have unit service speed, no abandonment, no breakdowns, and unlimited buffer capacity. Work conservation, not a particular service order, governs the busy indicator. Positive service requirements are attached to the arrival epochs, which may coincide. An arrival exactly when the residual workload reaches zero begins service without a positive idle gap; busy intervals are maximal intervals separated by nonzero idle lengths. At any finite horizon only finitely many primary clusters and released jobs occur. The deterministic theorem is an initially empty sample-path statement; it does not assert equality of workloads, waiting times, service-order histories, or individual sojourn times. For the stochastic corollary, the queues are coupled using the same marked primary process and the service-cover condition is imposed markwise.

## Proof

**1. Isolated-cluster characterization.** A cluster beginning at time zero, with no other arrivals, remains continuously busy until it has exhausted its full workload \(B\) if and only if every secondary epoch obeys service cover. Indeed, before the first trigger the available work is \(x_0\), so no gap occurs precisely when \(d_1\le x_0\). Assuming no previous gap, the workload just before the \(j\)-th trigger is \(\sum_{h<j}x_h-d_j\). Nonnegativity is exactly the \(j\)-th service-cover inequality. After the final trigger the remaining work drains continuously and the clearing time is precisely \(B\). If any inequality first fails, a positive idle gap occurs before that trigger. The assertion remains true for coincident release epochs, with the empty-sum and equality conventions above.

**2. Comparison until the first clearing.** Begin both queues empty and consider the first busy interval initiated at epoch \(a\). Let \(\tau\) be the first clearing time of the staggered queue after \(a\), if finite. Any primary arriving at \(t_i\in[a,\tau)\) introduces an isolated cluster whose workload is positive throughout \([t_i,t_i+B_i)\) by Step 1. Adding all other arrivals cannot reduce workload: from any common empty starting state the reflected unit-drift workload is monotone under adding nonnegative arrival jumps. Hence \(\tau\ge t_i+B_i\). Every trigger from every primary occurring in \([a,\tau)\) has therefore arrived by \(\tau\). The staggered system has no pending triggered job at this clearing. As it has not idled during \([a,\tau)\), its cumulative work equals \(\tau-a\). This is also the full work of the corresponding batches of primaries before \(\tau\).

The batch queue cannot clear strictly before \(\tau\). Otherwise, at its first clearing \(v<\tau\), its total incoming work from primaries in \([a,v]\) equals \(v-a\); staggered work released up to \(v\) is no greater than this amount. Since the staggered queue has remained busy on \([a,v]\), its workload would be nonpositive at \(v\), contradicting its first clearing being \(\tau\). Thus the batch queue remains busy until \(\tau\), where its accumulated input is \(\tau-a\), and clears exactly there. If the staggered queue never clears, the same contradiction rules out any earlier batch clearing. At the shared clearing, both queues have no pending triggers, so the argument restarts from an empty common state at the next primary arrival. Induction gives identical busy and idle intervals for every finite observation horizon.

**3. Necessity and marked-Poisson version.** For a deterministic cluster violating a service-cover inequality, Step 1 yields a positive idle gap in the staggered queue, while the batch queue stays busy throughout \([0,B)\). The one-cluster arrival schedule therefore distinguishes them. In the Poisson setting, if the mark event of failure has positive probability, then conditional on a failing initiating mark there is positive probability of no further primary arrivals until after all jobs in that cluster have arrived and completed in either comparison. On this event the first busy intervals differ. Finiteness of the marks makes this conditional Poisson no-arrival probability strictly positive, proving necessity. The sufficiency argument is pathwise and therefore holds almost surely when the service-cover event has probability one.

**4. Exact Poisson busy-cycle law.** Since Step 2 leaves no pending triggered jobs at clearing epochs, each new busy cycle begins with one primary and a fresh independent future marked Poisson process. Busy and idle intervals are identical, path by path, to those of the \(M/G/1\) system whose service times are the whole cluster workloads \(B\). For this standard queue, conditional on an initiating workload \(B=b\), the number of subsequent primaries arriving during the associated service is Poisson with mean \(\lambda b\), and each initiates an independent descendant busy subperiod. The branching decomposition gives
\[
 F(z,s)=z\,\mathbb E\bigl[e^{-sB}e^{-\lambda B(1-F(z,s))}\bigr].
\]
The subcritical condition \(\lambda\mathbb EB<1\) ensures a finite branching family almost surely and selects the transform via branching or monotone iteration from zero; at \((z,s)=(1,0)\) the appropriate value is one. Differentiating at \((1,0)\) yields \(\mathbb EL=\mathbb EB/(1-\rho)\). Strong Markov independence of fresh Poisson arrivals gives exponential idle durations. For deterministic \(B=b\), the number of offspring is Poisson with mean \(\rho\), so the known total-progeny (Borel) distribution gives the displayed probabilities and \(L=bK\). No limit approximation is used.

## Verification

The bundled `verify.py` checks the deterministic comparison in 700 seeded, exactly rational, multiply triggered samples (allowing secondary offsets to exceed primary service), exact boundary behavior, an explicit failure past the boundary, and Borel normalization and mean identities. It reports 711 passed checks. The computational samples do not prove the universal or infinite-time results; Steps 1–4 above provide those proofs. Run `python verify.py` with Python 3; only the standard library is needed.

## Relationship to prior work

Rahnamania, Mandjes and Ashtiani (2026) analyze the same motivating primary/secondary triggered-arrival architecture with general delays, deriving stochastic bounds for stationary workload and class-specific system times, rather than exact busy-cycle identities. Their related September 2026 paper treats exponential triggering delays by matrix-analytic methods. Inoue and Takine (2003), Section 2.2, Lemma 1, already prove equality of the busy indicators for a special clustered periodic cell-arrival model with equal deterministic service times and back-to-back cells. That earlier result is a relevant precursor, not omitted; its fixed cell-train setting is a strict special case of the service-cover family and does not itself give the necessary-and-sufficient criterion under random heterogeneous marks or the marked-Poisson busy-period transform. The busy-period transform, exponential idle law and Borel example use classical \(M/G/1\) theory. The new contribution claimed here is the sharp service-cover reduction and its exact triggered-cluster consequence, not the known queueing transforms considered separately.

## Limitations

This is a one-server, work-conserving result. The busy/idle equivalence is not a workload or sojourn-time equivalence. It can fail when service cover fails, even if the failure is rare. The deterministic equivalence is uniform over schedules; a particular schedule with other arrivals may happen to conceal a failing cluster's internal idle gap. The Poisson characterization uses independent, identically distributed marks across clusters; it does not claim the same stochastic necessity for arbitrary correlated arrival processes. While the 2026 motivating paper and a particularly close 2003 primary result were examined, historical priority against all possible batching or workload-reflection formulations is not independently certified.
