# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The normalized spherical measure splits into independent uniform height and azimuth, yielding an exact one-dimensional sign-area integral for every trace-zero quadratic after spectral normalization. Explicit strict concavity, exact endpoint integration, and a paired-derivative argument establish the sharp interval and complete equality cases. A global curvature bound gives the stated quantitative rigidity; the standalone checker independently corroborates representative areas. Boundary eigenvalue multiplicities and zero-eigenvalue cases are covered.

## Originality
PASS, qualified. The sharp area of a positive domain over the *entire family* of traceless three-dimensional quadratics is not stated by the inspected nodal-geometry and spherical quadratic-form density sources, and the retrieved sharp harmonic-norm record does not imply a sign distribution. Hillier provides fixed-spectrum density information, which is stronger for that *different* question but does not resolve the spectral optimization or rigidity shown here. The Saldanha--Tomei paper is especially relevant by object but was only accessible through its abstract and an excerpt; its full content is an originality risk. Searches do not prove absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Global sign-area imbalance is an intrinsic, geometrically interpretable nodal statistic distinct from moments and the number of nodal domains. The sharp interval, every equality orbit, the unique balanced rank-two orbit, the monotone spectral interpolation and a quantified rigidity inequality together classify an entire five-dimensional harmonic space at fixed degree without an arbitrary slice.
