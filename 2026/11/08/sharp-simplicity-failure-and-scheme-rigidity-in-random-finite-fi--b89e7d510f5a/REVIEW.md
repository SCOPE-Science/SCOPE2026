# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The sample space is \(\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)\), with \(q\) fixed. The proof reconstructs the strongly connected graph criterion from ideals and invertible squares; the criterion holds after arbitrary scalar extension. Every singleton directed cut has conditional probability exactly \((q-1)/(q^n-1)\), and all pairwise intersections of the \(2n\) singleton events are \(O_q(q^{-2n})\). All larger cuts contribute \(O_q(n^2q^{-2n})\), giving the sharp factor \(2n\). For the scheme assertion, the Elduque–Labra exact sequence (Theorem 3.2) plus a graph loop and strong connectivity force every diagonal multiplier to be one even over test rings with nilpotents. Permutation orbits of ordered pairs and the \(\ell^2\)-norm estimate prove the explicit automorphism union bound. There is no inference from a finite test to an asymptotic theorem.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The motivating October 15, 2026 survey explicitly records random simplicity and automorphism probabilities as Question 22. It establishes neither the sharp \(\operatorname{GL}_n\)-conditioned failure coefficient nor a joint finite-field automorphism-*scheme* bound. The 2019 paper provides the *underlying* group-scheme exact sequence (not claimed as new). Earlier ideal classifications and low-dimensional isomorphism counts do not calculate this probability. Published random-matrix singularity results concern full rank of a random matrix rather than simplicity of its associated evolution algebra given full rank. published-finding corpus queries about random perfect evolution algebras, conditional invertible support digraphs, and graph scheme rigidity returned no dominating prior claim. Residual risk: conditioned random digraph probabilities or a differently worded random-evolution-algebra note may already imply part or all of the result; this is a best-knowledge originality assessment rather than proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Question 22 of a 2026 research survey asks for asymptotic probabilities for random evolution algebras. The conditioned family is the natural open set of perfect algebras. The result gives a first-order **sharp** rarity law for failure of absolute simplicity, an unconditional simplicity asymptotic, and a quantified vanishing bound for nontrivial *group schemes* rather than ordinary automorphism points. The coefficient \(2n(q-1)/(q^n-1)\) is not merely the standard nonsingularity constant: it distinguishes two kinds of codimension-one directed cuts after conditioning. The finding is substantive despite relying on prior graph and scheme reductions.
