In a uniformly sampled perfect evolution algebra over \(\mathbb F_q\), nonsimplicity is typically caused by a single one-way isolated vertex, whereas decomposability requires a two-way isolated vertex: their probabilities have sharp, field-uniform main terms \(2n(q-1)/(q^n-1)\) and \(n(q-1)q^{-2n+1}/(1-q^{-n})\). Consequently, a nonsimple algebra is decomposable with asymptotic conditional probability \(1/(2q^{n-1})\), while an exact exponential generating function counts every possible number of indecomposable summands.
