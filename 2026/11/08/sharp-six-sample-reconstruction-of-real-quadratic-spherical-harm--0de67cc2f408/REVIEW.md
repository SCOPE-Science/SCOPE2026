# Scientific review of the two-great-circle quadratic-harmonic sampling theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The five-dimensional space and probability normalizations are explicit. Three equispaced samples integrate the squared restriction of every quadratic exactly, because its nonzero Fourier frequencies are \(2\) and \(4\). The two-circle Gram matrix follows independently from the planar fourth-moment tensor, and its spectral decomposition is exact. Of its five eigenvalues only a strictly increasing block eigenvalue and a strictly decreasing scalar eigenvalue can control the minimum. Their unique crossing is at \(\cos\theta=5/9\). The exact eigenspaces also yield the equality classification and the sharp \(5/9\) remainder. The bundled checker reproduced sample-frame and tensor-formula identities with exact arithmetic. Its finite tests support, but do not establish, the continuum proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical residual risk.** Simons–Dahlen–Wieczorek (2006), Section 4, treats restriction to positive-area regions through a concentration Gram matrix, not singular measure on two great circles or six sample locations. McEwen–Price (2015), Section III, treats great-circle **averages** (Funk–Radon data), not the complete traces or phase-independent three-point energy samples. Ivanov–Petrushev (2014) treats irregular sampling at high-degree and dense grids, not this sharp six-observation design. Searches for equivalent two-triangle frames, optimal great-circle separation, and the cosine \(5/9\) threshold found no directly covering statement. A general frame or optimal-design source not located by these searches could in principle contain an equivalent specialized result; no proof of global historical uniqueness is asserted.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** A real quadratic harmonic has five degrees of freedom, while three observations already capture its full quadratic energy along each great circle. Two such minimal component designs must be tilted to span the global harmonic space. The result identifies the *unique* optimal tilt, explains the perhaps counterintuitive failure at a right angle, determines exact equality modes, and supplies a sharp stability gap. It solves a natural structured reconstruction problem rather than an arbitrary small parameter table or a routine moment computation. No broader six-node optimality or degree extension is asserted.

Full accessible material was inspected for the first two; the third was compared through the available article sections. The conclusion is limited to real degree-two harmonics and equally weighted triples on two circles. Possible unindexed equivalent formulas and absence of independent audit remain the principal risks.
