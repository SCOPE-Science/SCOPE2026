# Sharp six-slide mobility of minimum identifying codes in the four-by-four rook graph

## Finding

Let \(R=K_4\square K_4\) be the four-by-four rook graph. A minimum **closed-neighborhood identifying code** is a smallest vertex set \(C\) whose closed-neighborhood traces \(N[v]\cap C\) are nonempty and pairwise distinct. The existence and the cardinality of its minimum codes, and their **unrestricted token-jump** reconfiguration, were determined previously: \(\gamma_{\mathrm{ID}}(R)=6\), there are \(2208\) minimum codes, and the unrestricted one-for-one replacement graph has \(28800\) edges and diameter \(6\). **None of these earlier facts is proposed as new.**

The finding concerns the **strictly adjacency-restricted token-slide** reconfiguration graph \(\mathcal T(R)\): exchange one selected vertex with one **adjacent** unselected vertex, retaining an identifying code of minimum size. The complete exact result is

\[
|V(\mathcal T(R))|=2208,\qquad |E(\mathcal T(R))|=14976,
\qquad \operatorname{rad}(\mathcal T(R))=\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal T(R))=6.
\]

The graph is connected; exactly \(1344\) configurations have slide-degree \(12\), and exactly \(864\) have slide-degree \(16\). Thus eliminating \(13824\) unrestricted jumps—nearly half the transitions—**neither disconnects this optimal-code space nor increases its worst-case shortest reconfiguration distance**. This exact connectivity, local-degree spectrum, transition count and no-diameter-penalty conclusion is the new claim.

For context, the nonisomorphic Shrikhande graph \(S\) has the same strongly regular parameters \((16,6,2,2)\) as \(R\). Earlier work already classifies its \(96\) five-vertex optimum codes and their unrestricted jump graph as \(16C_6\). With the *slide* restriction, the checkers also show \(\mathcal T(S)\cong96K_1\); this supplementary contrast is a direct consequence of the previously known code classification and is **not** claimed as an independently original census. The contribution assessed here is the full slide-graph classification on \(R\).

## Assumptions and scope

Every vertex is a pair \((a,b)\in(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\), encoded by integer \(4a+b\in\{0,\ldots,15\}\). In \(S\), distinct vertices are adjacent if their coordinate difference lies in

\[
\{(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(1,1),(-1,-1)\}\pmod4.
\]

In \(R\), distinct vertices are adjacent when their first or second coordinates agree. Write \(N_G[v]\) for the **closed** neighbourhood of a vertex. An identifying code is a subset \(C\subseteq V(G)\) such that the sixteen sets \(N_G[v]\cap C\), as \(v\) varies, are all **nonempty and pairwise distinct**. A minimum code has smallest possible cardinality. The graph \(\mathcal T(G)\) has all minimum identifying codes as vertices; two are adjacent precisely when one removes \(u\in C\) and inserts \(w\notin C\) with \(uw\in E(G)\), and the resulting set is a minimum code. For \(\mathcal J(G)\), remove the requirement \(uw\in E(G)\). The central slide assertion concerns exactly the named 16-vertex rook graph; the supplementary Shrikhande checks and jump checks are contextual, not generalized claims.

## Proof

**Lemma 1 (finite identification criterion).** Represent subsets of \(V(G)\) by 16-bit nonnegative integers, with bit \(v\) indicating membership. If \(B_v\) is the mask of \(N_G[v]\), then \(C\) is an identifying code if and only if

\[
C\mathbin{\&}B_v\ne0\quad\text{for every }v,
\quad\text{and}\quad
C\mathbin{\&}(B_v\oplus B_w)\ne0\quad\text{whenever }v<w.
\]

Here \(\&\) is bitwise AND and \(\oplus\) is XOR. The first conditions are exactly domination; the second assert that the intersections with the two neighbourhoods differ in at least one coordinate. This equivalence is an elementary exhaustive test, with no heuristic or approximate filtering.

**Lemma 2 (minimum layer).** Identification is monotone under adding code vertices: distinct nonempty intersection signatures remain distinct and nonempty when additional coordinates are supplied. Thus ruling out all identifying codes of size \(k-1\) also rules out every smaller code. For \(S\), the standard information bound \(2^{|C|}-1\ge16\) forces \(|C|\ge5\); exhaustive evaluation of all \(\binom{16}{5}\) five-subsets gives exactly \(96\) solutions. For \(R\), Foucaud et al.'s Proposition 10 yields \(\gamma_{\mathrm{ID}}(R)=\lceil(3\cdot4-1)/2\rceil=6\), because \(R=L(K_{4,4})\). Independently of that published formula, the finite checker finds zero identifying five-subsets and exactly \(2208\) identifying six-subsets.

**Lemma 3 (edge criterion).** If two size-\(k\) codes \(C,D\) differ by precisely one removed vertex \(u\) and one inserted vertex \(w\), their bitmasks have XOR popcount two. They are adjacent in \(\mathcal J(G)\) exactly in that case and in \(\mathcal T(G)\) precisely if additionally \(u\) and \(w\) are adjacent in \(G\). Hence every transition is generated by finite loops over the codes and every generated transition is legal.

**Complete finite proof certificate (new slide claim).** The included `verify.py` enumerates **every** subset in the required size layers, forms identification signatures by literal neighbourhood intersections, constructs all legal moves, computes every component, and performs complete breadth-first search from **every** rook-state vertex. The included independent `verify.cpp` enumerates every one of the \(2^{16}\) bitmasks with the logically equivalent pair-separation criterion of Lemma 1, constructs move edges by **unordered pairs of valid states**, and independently does every breadth-first search. The programs use independently written graph constructions and independent approaches to identifying-code testing and edge generation. Each rejects any mismatch in the theorem's counts and structure.

The complete enumerations give the following figures. The jump rows are **previously known** and serve only as comparison and independent checks of the vertex layers:

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrl}
\text{graph/model}&\text{states}&\text{edges}&\text{components}\hline
S/\mathrm{slide}&96&0&96\text{ singleton components}\\
S/\mathrm{jump}&96&96&16\text{ six-cycles}\\
R/\mathrm{slide}&2208&14976&1\text{ component}\\
R/\mathrm{jump}&2208&28800&1\text{ component}
\end{array}
\]

For \(S\), the closed-form prior code classification also determines that no legal one-step jump exchanges adjacent vertices. For the newly assessed \(R\) slide graph, a breadth-first search from **every** one of the \(2208\) nodes reaches every other node and returns eccentricity exactly six, establishing both claimed radii and diameters; direct degree enumeration gives the announced two-class degree spectrum. The already-known jump diameter six supplies an independent conceptual lower bound for the slide diameter, but it does **not** imply that the slide diameter is at most six. These tests are exhaustive for the theorem's two fixed finite graphs. Therefore the stated classification follows from Lemmas 1–3 and the complete finite computation.

Run `python3 verify.py --certificate` to re-enumerate the states, moves, component structures and all rook-state eccentricities and regenerate `states.txt` and `edges.txt`. Run `g++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify.cpp -o verify` followed by `./verify` for a logically independent check. The included `verify_python_output.txt` and `verify_cpp_output.txt` are actual execution results. `states.txt` records all valid minimum codes in canonical lowercase hexadecimal mask order, prefixed `S` or `R`. `edges.txt` lists every undirected reconfiguration edge, with prefixes `ST`, `SJ`, `RT` or `RJ` for graph and move model. Both endpoints are 16-bit lowercase hexadecimal masks with the smaller first. An empty `ST` edge section is intentional.

All vertex masks are on the same 0-based encoding \(4a+b\). No random generation, timeout, extrapolation, external database, precomputed library, or unprovided dependency is used. For completeness, `verify.cpp` runs its state-membership test on all \(65536\) subsets, not merely on a preselected table. The text certificate files can be regenerated and compared byte for byte to the embedded copies.

## Relationship to prior work

Foucaud, Gravier, Naserasr, Parreau and Valicov, *Identifying codes in line graphs* (first public preprint 1 July 2011), give the same closed-neighborhood identifying-code model. Their Proposition 10 already establishes the minimum code cardinality \(\gamma_{\mathrm{ID}}(R)=6\), since \(R=L(K_{4,4})\). This cardinality is an input and not original here.

A previous complete finite classification of the **same two** strongly regular graphs already establishes that \(R\) has \(2208\) minimum identifying codes and its unrestricted one-vertex **jump** graph is connected with \(28800\) edges and diameter six; it also establishes the analogous \(S\) optimum-code classification and jump cycles. Those results are recognized as covered. The present novelty claim is narrowly restricted to what their *jump adjacency data* do **not** imply: the specific adjacency-restricted slide edges, exact slide degree spectrum, connectedness, and proof by complete all-pairs distances that the full rook slide graph retains diameter six. More generally, an abstract jump graph without the underlying original-graph incidences does not determine which one-for-one moves remain legal slides.

Mynhardt and Teshima, *A note on some variations of the \(\gamma\)-graph* (first public preprint 7 July 2017), define exactly this adjacency-restricted minimum-identifying-code reconfiguration model and prove realizability: prescribed graphs occur as identifying-code reconfiguration graphs of constructed host graphs. That theorem gives no result for the specific fixed host \(K_4\square K_4\) and does not imply the 14976 slide transitions or the all-state six-step metric.

The Shrikhande and four-by-four rook graphs are a classical nonisomorphic strongly regular pair with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\); their equal degree and spectrum are insufficient to force identical optimal-code mobility. Existing independence- or neighborhood-complex comparisons concern different functorial constructions, not identifying-code slides.

## Limitations

The central new claim is a **complete finite** classification of token slides for the specific order-sixteen rook graph, not an assertion about all strongly regular graphs or larger rook families. The Shrikhande computation is supporting contrast, not a distinct originality claim. Its proof relies on transparent exhaustive integer arithmetic, not an independent formal theorem prover. The correctness of the code and exhaustive coverage can be audited directly from both standalone implementations and the finite certificates. Historical priority remains qualified: an equivalent unindexed table or study of the exact two state graphs may exist.
