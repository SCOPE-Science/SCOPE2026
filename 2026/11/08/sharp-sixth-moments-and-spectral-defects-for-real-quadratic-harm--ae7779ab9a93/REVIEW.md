# Scientific review: sharp sixth moment and spectral defects on the three-sphere

## Correctness

**PASS.** The domain is the full nine-dimensional real degree-two harmonic space on the probability three-sphere. Gaussian cumulants, radial Gaussian moments and Newton identities yield the stated sixth-moment formula. The three sharp spectral inequalities follow from AM–GM, Cauchy–Schwarz, and a complete Lagrange-multiplier classification on the compact zero-sum unit sphere. Their common equality configurations prove both endpoint constants and all equality cases. Exact rational coefficient comparison from an independent Dirichlet-moment expansion corroborates the central identity. The quaternion matrix-coefficient identification is checked on all nine basis elements.

## Originality

**PASS with a residual historical risk.** The closest accessible primary published theorem, *Sharp L4/L2 norm and extremizers for degree-two spherical harmonics* (2026-09-20), was read in full: it treats the same degree-two harmonic object and all ambient dimensions, but its exact theorem, proof, and equality statements concern fourth rather than sixth moments. It does not state the lower sixth-moment constant or the determinant-and-cubic-trace defect identities. Hillier (2001) studies densities of spherical quadratic forms, while Dai–Feng–Tikhonov (2014) study sharp asymptotic orders as harmonic degree grows. The published literature search and semantic database searches did not locate an equivalent two-sided sixth-moment theorem. The full texts of some older plausible studies were inaccessible, so equivalent matrix-invariant formulations remain a risk rather than being asserted absent.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The sharp reverse-Hölder question for a whole irreducible nine-dimensional harmonic space is natural, and the theorem solves both extremal directions at exponent six, not just a special profile. It gives inequivalent spectral equality orbits and two separate exact nonnegative remainder decompositions. The rotation-group reformulation identifies the same result as a sharp norm comparison for every real matrix coefficient of the fundamental rotation representation, explaining the utility of the specific dimension and exponent. The result is not a general all-exponent statement or a quantitative metric-distance theorem.
