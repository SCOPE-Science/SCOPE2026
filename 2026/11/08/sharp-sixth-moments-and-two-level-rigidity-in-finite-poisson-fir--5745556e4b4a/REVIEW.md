# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is an exact coefficient-space moment inequality, with the lower odd-dimensional number defined through a finite algebraic comparison rather than an unproved choice of multiplicity. Its starting central-moment polynomial follows from independent Poisson cumulants. Compactness ensures a minimizer. The negative second-order perturbation excludes zero coordinates; the strictly convex stationary equation forces exactly one positive and one negative level. Exact differentiation of the resulting rational function yields a cubic with exactly one minimizing root for each multiplicity. Power means handle even dimensions and a spike attains the upper bound. The claim uses no finite search as a proof for all real parameters.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk

The sixth moment itself is already computable from standard Poisson cumulants, as in the general partition framework of Privault (arXiv:1204.4854). Last and Penrose (arXiv:0909.3205) develop the first-chaos framework, including finite independent Poisson counts. Dirksen (arXiv:1208.3885) proves norm-comparison isomorphisms with constants depending on exponents, not these exact finite-dimensional endpoints and all extremizers. A targeted search did not locate a theorem specifying the parity-sensitive two-level optimum and all its cubic candidates. Undiscovered equivalent specializations remain possible; no search exhaustiveness or independent novelty certification is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sharp lower and upper norm embeddings of an entire natural family of finite-dimensional Poisson first-chaos spaces are now expressed algebraically. The genuine geometric content is the all-coordinate, two-level rigidity of minima and an even/odd change: equal-magnitude balanced directions minimize in even dimension, while odd dimension forces nonflat amplitudes. The finite root prescription avoids a remaining continuous optimization even when the winning multiplicity has not been reduced to a closed general index. This is more than a direct cumulant computation.

## Scope and closest literature

All statements require real coefficients, equal strictly positive intensities, independent counts and the sixth moment. The exact minimum in odd dimension is a finite comparison, not a proof that its most balanced candidate always wins. The moment identity is classical, the optimization is the proposed contribution, and a hidden equivalent theorem remains a live risk. The verifier corroborates symbolic and finite calculations and is not independent validation.
