For fully actuated finite-difference heat control, the theta scheme's terminal energy is uniformly comparable with exact semidiscrete control energy precisely when \(k=O(h)\) and \((2\theta-1)k=O(h^2)\); Crank–Nicolson thus tolerates a linear time–space scale, but every fixed \(\theta>1/2\) requires a quadratic scale. A two-parameter formula gives the exact highest-frequency energy bias at the critical boundary.
