# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The proof partitions descendants by their unique first and last changed positions, counts anagram endpoints by inclusion–exclusion, and proves strict loss under repeated letters by labeled-realization counting. A path-difference optimization yields the binary triple-window extrema and all extremizer counts. The two-state transfer matrix derives the entire binary distribution, with first two moments following from pairwise independence of distinct binary parity forms. The independently enumerated instances and standalone checker corroborate all parts.

**Originality (PASS, with residual historical-priority risk).** The nearest 2026 source introduces permutation bursts and gives asymptotic redundancy bounds and coding constructions, whereas this theorem determines individual exact spheres, all larger-alphabet extremizers and the full binary length-three spectrum. Searches for alternative names and stronger formulations found no equivalent published record. The primary preprint's full text was inaccessible; therefore an implicit equivalent theorem in it cannot be excluded. The limited-permutation channel permits many disjoint adjacent transpositions and is not the same channel.

**Value (PASS).** Exact one-error sphere volumes, maximal sizes, the smallest alphabet needed to attain them, and the number of attaining centers are natural finite-length channel invariants. The result settles these at every burst length for the large-alphabet regime and completely settles the first nontrivial smaller-alphabet case, including its full distribution. This supplies a quantitative counterpart to asymptotic code-redundancy bounds without claiming a code-size theorem.
