# Scientific review: square-flip metrics for the four-cube

## Correctness — PASS
The claim specifies the labelled binary four-cube, every perfect matching, and exactly the elementary alternating squares as moves. All 272 states are covered by a unique first-unmatched-vertex recursion, separately by a constrained bijection enumeration. The 24 possible squares are exhaustive by choosing two coordinate axes and the other two fixed bits. Both independently implemented transition generators give 768 square-flip edges and component sizes 264 and eight singletons. Breadth-first search over the entire mobile graph from every mobile state gives the claimed radius 7, diameter 8, eccentricity counts 252 and 12, and exact pair-distance tally. Both verifiers check one explicit diametral pair; the Python verifier additionally enumerates the complete 384-element cube symmetry action, confirms the peripheral orbit and the three four-cycles of farthest pairs. These finite exhaustions prove only the stated finite assertions. A separate independent scholarly audit has not occurred.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The 272 tilings and 264-plus-eight connected-component decomposition are **not** new: Östergård–Pettersson and the more directly relevant Klivans–Saldanha work cover them. The present result is restricted to sharp shortest-path metrics and the organization of the twelve extremal tilings in this already known component. Klivans–Saldanha's Example 2.2 and Remark 8.2 discuss exact components, the twist, and frozen states, but do not provide the claimed diameter, eccentricity distribution, or diametral-pair geometry in those materially inspected sections; a full-PDF term search found no occurrence of “diameter.” Bonamy et al.'s matching-reconfiguration work considers the same four-cycle operation but gives complexity and algorithmic theorems for graph classes, not this finite cube's metric. Search comparisons of names, aliases (four-cube, tesseract, 2-by-2-by-2-by-2 box, dimers, domino tilings, switch graph), and metric parameters did not find a dominating result. A previously recorded broader move set allowing both four- and six-cycle trades does not determine distances in the square-only subgraph: deleting those extra edges can increase shortest paths or disconnect states. Historical priority remains qualified because the complete 2012 enumeration article was not available for material inspection and unindexed computations may exist.

## Value — PASS
The already known nine-component separation shows that elementary-square flips are restrictive in the smallest nontrivial four-dimensional box. Its **sharp intra-sector flip cost**, however, is not supplied by component counts. Determining whether 7 or 8 flips suffice, identifying exactly which configurations need the extra move, and showing that all worst-case pairs form three four-cycles in one symmetry orbit produces a natural complete benchmark for dimer-reconfiguration geometry. Bonamy et al. explicitly motivate diameters as informative for the reachability and sampling geometry of flip chains, without claiming that a small finite diameter proves rapid mixing. The theorem does not require a larger-dimensional generalization for this specific sharp finite conclusion to have mathematical content.
