For every nondegenerate Euclidean \(d\)-simplex, the mean squared distance between two surface-uniform points exceeds \(\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{2d^2}\) times that between two volume-uniform points, and this dimension-dependent factor is sharp but unattained. Thus the squared-distance boundary/interior comparison holds for every triangle and tetrahedron, yet already reverses for some four-dimensional simplices and for all higher dimensions.
