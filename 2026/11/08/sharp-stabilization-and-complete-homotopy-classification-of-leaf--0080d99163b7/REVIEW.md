# Scientific review: leaf-symmetry multipath quotients

## Correctness — PASS

The arc color set has four elements because the indegree/outdegree constraints at each of two hubs allow at most one arc of each hub-side orientation. Repeated use of a leaf forces one of exactly two directed through-paths; selecting both gives a forbidden directed four-cycle. These necessary and sufficient conditions give the complete partition-signature cell classification for every integer \(n\ge1\). Every simplex stabilizer is pointwise because colors never repeat, and all restrictions are distinct, yielding a regular CW quotient rather than an invalid vertex-orbit shortcut. The eleven specified free-face deletions are valid over the entire stabilized family, verified by complete incidence reconstruction. The smaller three cases are proved using two disjoint intervals, a tetrahedral sphere with two added edges, and an explicit collapse to a four-triangle sphere. Limits: no equivariant homotopy result prior to taking the geometric quotient is asserted.

## Originality — PASS, with stated residual risk

The primary 2026 multipath papers discuss bidirected polygons and vertex decomposability of directed-forest complexes; the earlier complete-bipartite construction has only one directed orientation. A prior unquotiented \(K_{2,6}\) homotopy/character result, and indexed quotient results for Morse/Boolean/flex complexes, have different spaces or actions and do not imply the present geometric orbit classification. The exact partition-poset stabilization and the free-face contractibility proof are absent from the materially inspected sources. Indexed searches alone cannot certify universal novelty; an unindexed or differently named equivalent quotient theorem remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result identifies an entire natural infinite equivariant family, not a selectively chosen finite instance: quotient spaces are spheres/wedges at the three smallest leaf counts, then undergo a sharp transition to a uniformly collapsible space at the fourth. The stabilized regular-CW face poset, explicit elementary-collapse certificate, and necessity to distinguish genuine geometric quotients from naïve simplicial orbit constructions provide structural and reusable information. The nontrivial pre-stable cases demonstrate that contractibility is a real threshold rather than a formal consequence of finite group quotients.
