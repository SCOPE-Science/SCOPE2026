# Sharp stabilization and exact deficits of four-point near-paired sumsets

## Finding
For every integer \(a\ge4\) and \(d\in\{2,3\}\), put \(b=a+d\), \(A_{a,d}=\{0,1,a,b\}\) and \(S_{a,d}=\langle d,b-1,b\rangle\), the additive submonoid of nonnegative integers generated by these three integers. Let \(G_{a,d}=\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\setminus S_{a,d}\), a finite set. For every integer \(h\ge a-1\), the exact sumset is
\[
hA_{a,d}=[0,bh]\setminus\{bh-s:s\in G_{a,d}\}.
\]
This threshold is optimal as the first \(h\) at which the *entire* reflected deficit set equals \(G_{a,d}\) for every larger \(h\), except for \((a,d)=(4,3)\), whose sharp threshold is \(2\).

The stabilized missing-point count is the genus \(g_{a,d}=|G_{a,d}|\):
\[
g_{a,2}=\left\lfloor\frac{a+1}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 g_{a,3}=\begin{cases}
a+1,&a\equiv0\pmod3,\\
a+2,&a\equiv1\pmod3,\\
(2a+2)/3,&a\equiv2\pmod3.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently \(|hA_{a,d}|=(a+d)h+1-g_{a,d}\) at and after the sharp threshold.

For \(d=2\), there is also an exact formula **at every** \(h\ge1\). Set \(T(z)=m(m+1)/2\), where \(m=\max(0,z)\). Then
\[
|hA_{a,2}|=\frac{3h^2+h}{2}-T(2h-a-1)+T(h-a)+h+1
 -\max\left(0,\left\lfloor\frac{2h-a-1}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All sets are subsets of the ordinary integers. Repeated summands are allowed in \(hA\), and \(h\) is a positive integer. The reflection \(bh-hA\) means \(\{bh-n:n\in hA\}\). The "sharp threshold" is the least positive \(H\) such that the *untruncated* deficit sets \(\{bh-n:0\le n\le bh,\ n\notin hA\}\) equal the fixed finite set \(G_{a,d}\) for every \(h\ge H\); this does not simply assert equality after intersecting with \([0,bh]\). Empty sums are permitted only in the additive semigroup definition, not in \(hA\). The semigroups are cofinite because \(\gcd(d,b-1,b)=1\).

## Proof
**1. A covered initial interval.** Choose \(t\) summands from \(\{a,b\}\) and \(h-t\) summands from \(\{0,1\}\). For each \(0\le t\le h\), all such sums are
\[
U_t=\bigcup_{j=0}^{t}[at+dj,\ at+dj+h-t].
\]
When \(t\le h-d+1\), successive component intervals touch or overlap, so \(U_t=[at,(b-1)t+h]\). If \(h\ge a-1\), adjacent intervals of this latter form touch or overlap, since
\[
a(t+1)-((b-1)t+h)=a-(d-1)t-h\le1.
\]
They therefore fill the entire integer interval
\[
[0,(b-1)(h-d+1)+h]=[0,bh-(b-1)(d-1)].
\]
Write \(C=(b-1)(d-1)\). All possible absent sums are consequently among the top \(C-1\) positions, with reflected deficits \(s\) satisfying \(0\le s<C\).

**2. Exact reflected deficits.** Reflecting each generator across \(b\) gives
\[
b-A_{a,d}=\{b,b-1,d,0\},\qquad bh-hA_{a,d}=h\{0,d,b-1,b\}\subseteq S_{a,d}.
\]
It remains to show that each \(s\in S_{a,d}\) with \(s<C\) has a representation using at most \(a-1\) nonzero generators. For \(d=2\), \(C=b-1=a+1\), so \(0\le s\le a\). A semigroup expression for such \(s\) cannot contain \(b-1\) or \(b\); it has \(s/2\le\lfloor a/2\rfloor\le a-1\) copies of \(2\). For \(d=3\), \(C=2(b-1)=2a+4\), so \(s\le2a+3\). An expression can contain at most one of \(b-1=a+2\) and \(b=a+3\). Without such a generator, it contains at most \(\lfloor(2a+3)/3\rfloor\le a-1\) copies of \(3\). With one, its length is at most \(1+\lfloor(a+1)/3\rfloor\le a-1\) for \(a\ge4\). Zero-padding gives a representation by exactly \(h\) elements of \(\{0,d,b-1,b\}\) for every \(h\ge a-1\). Combined with the initial interval, this proves \(bh-hA_{a,d}=S_{a,d}\cap[0,bh]\), equivalently the asserted full sumset identity once the next genus and threshold facts are established.

**3. Genus and the gap maximum.** When \(d=2\), \(S_{a,2}\) is generated by \(2\) and the smaller odd element of \(\{a+1,a+2\}\); its gaps are the positive odd numbers below that odd generator, with genus \(\lfloor(a+1)/2\rfloor\) and maximal gap at most \(a\). When \(d=3\), reduction of \(b-1,b\) modulo \(3\) gives the following. If \(a\equiv0\pmod3\), then \(S_{a,3}=\langle3,a+2\rangle\), of genus \(a+1\) and maximum gap \(2a+1\). If \(a\equiv1\pmod3\), then \(S_{a,3}=\langle3,a+3\rangle\), of genus \(a+2\) and maximum gap \(2a+3\). If \(a\equiv2\pmod3\), the Apéry set modulo \(3\) is \(\{0,a+2,a+3\}\), hence the genus equals \((2a+2)/3\) and the maximum gap equals \(a\). These statements follow either by enumerating residues modulo \(3\) or, for two coprime generators, the elementary gap-count formula. Each maximum gap is at most \(2a+3\). At \(h\ge a-1\), the value \(bh\) exceeds all these gaps (including the minimal \((a,d)=(4,3)\)); thus \(G_{a,d}\subseteq[0,bh]\), and Step 2 gives the untruncated identity.

**4. Sharpness.** For \(h=a-2\), the integer \(n=a-1\) is absent from \(hA_{a,d}\): it is too small to contain \(a\) or \(b\), and requires more than \(h\) copies of \(1\). Its reflected deficit is
\[
s=bh-n=(a+d)(a-2)-(a-1).
\]
For \(d=2\), this is \(a^2-a-3\), strictly larger than the maximal gap (at most \(a\)). For \(d=3\), this is \(a^2-5\), strictly larger than the maximal gap (at most \(2a+3\)) for every \(a\ge5\). In all these cases \(s\in S_{a,d}\), proving the full deficit identity is false at \(h=a-2\), so no smaller stabilization threshold is possible. In the single exceptional case \((a,d)=(4,3)\), direct addition gives \(2A_{4,3}=\{0,1,2,4,5,7,8,11,14\}\), with reflected missing deficits \(\{1,2,4,5,8,11\}=G_{4,3}\). Since the general argument covers every \(h\ge3\), the sharp threshold is \(2\); at \(h=1\), the maximum possible deficit is only \(7\), but \(11\in G_{4,3}\), so threshold \(1\) is impossible.

**5. Exact formula for \(d=2\), including transients.** For \(0\le t<h\), all consecutive two-step intervals in \(U_t\) overlap or touch and collapse to \(I_t=[at,(a+1)t+h]\), of size \(t+h+1\). These intervals have increasing starts and ends; adding each next interval overlaps its predecessor by \(\max(0,t+h-a+1)\) elements (where \(t=0,\dots,h-2\)). Their total union therefore has cardinality
\[
\frac{3h^2+h}{2}-T(2h-a-1)+T(h-a).
\]
The remaining layer \(t=h\) consists of the \(h+1\) points \(\{ah+2j:0\le j\le h\}\). Exactly \(\max(0,\lfloor(2h-a-1)/2\rfloor+1)\) of these already lie in the last interval \(I_{h-1}\); none of the other points lies in any earlier \(I_t\), since the interval endpoints are increasing. Adding the newly contributed points gives the claimed all-\(h\) formula.

## Verification
A standalone exact-integer script, `verify.py`, separately constructs \(hA\) by bitset shifts, constructs semigroup membership by dynamic programming, and recomputes many small parameter cases by independent set-based sum enumeration. Checks reproduced: 12,632 transient-formula comparisons, 882 stabilized reflected-hole comparisons, 294 sharpness predecessor comparisons, 294 semigroup genus comparisons, and 1,568 independent set-sum comparisons. The implementation covers \(4\le a\le150\) for both \(d=2,3\). Its tests are finite corroboration only; Steps 1–5 above provide the infinite-domain proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Shi-Qiang Chen and Quan-Hui Yang, *On the size of h-fold sumsets* (arXiv:2607.29535, first posted July 31, 2026), give an exact general counting procedure, and an explicit truncated-binomial formula for \(\{0,1,\ldots,s,a,b\}\) when \(b=qa+r\) and \(r=0\) or \(qs+r\ge a\). Here \(s=1\), \(b=a+d\), so the simple-formula hypothesis fails for \(d=2,a\ge4\) and \(d=3,a\ge5\); the special \((a,d)=(4,3)\) meets it. The present result supplies a sharp full-gap stabilization threshold, exact genus and reflected missing sets for these two families, and a separate all-\(h\) closed formula when \(d=2\), rather than claiming priority for arbitrary-set computability.

Jun-Yu Zhou and Quan-Hui Yang, *On the structure of the h-fold sumsets* (2021, DOI: 10.5802/crmath.191), prove an eventual interval-and-fringes theorem for general finite sets. That theorem gives neither the sharp \(a-1\) threshold here nor the specified exceptional case and genus formulas. Granville and Shakan (2020), Theorem 1, give this same eventual *two-sided gap-set shape* for every set with \(1\) as a generator, once \(h\ge2\lfloor b/2floor\). Their reflected-gap statement is therefore not novel here; what is proved afresh is the strictly smaller **exact least threshold** \(a-1=b-d-1\) (with the exceptional boundary), the explicit genus in both families, and the \(d=2\) all-\(h\) formula. Wu, Chen and Chen (2011) provide another earlier general bound.

## Limitations
The claim is restricted to the two natural differences \(d=2,3\) and \(a\ge4\). The \(d=3\) transient cardinalities below stabilization are not given by a closed formula here. The all-\(h\) \(d=2\) count and the stabilized formula are mathematical deductions from the proof, not assertions of independent publication or external audit. A substantially broader unindexed exact result might subsume the theorem; relevant older publisher full texts were not all accessible. Finite exact tests do not substitute for the symbolic argument.
