# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The metric quotient formula and surjective Haar endomorphism yield two independent uniform cover displacements. The disk–rectangle overlap integral gives the exact four-branch CDF. Its shape derivative is strictly negative for the radius range between shorter half-side and circumscribed radius when the aspect ratio is nonsquare. The four-corner area expansion yields the sharp quartic survival exponent and coefficient. Computation from the standalone file supports these analytic steps but is not a substitute for them.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
Wu (arXiv:1402.6667, 2014) introduces the flat pillowcase and branched covers in a cohomological context. The 2014 torus-distance discussion gives the ordinary torus disk-square CDF, a known input. Silverman (1978) studies torus distance statistics, but only its abstract was accessible and it may contain unexamined relevant details. General local cone-surface asymptotics already imply the small-radius quartic coefficient for four angle-π points; that local coefficient is not claimed as new. The all-radius quotient law, strict all-threshold fixed-area shape optimum and quartic endpoint-tail constant are the proposed contributions. An unindexed older quotient law or a stronger disk–rectangle rearrangement theorem remains a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete probability law and a sharp shape optimization on the moduli of rectangular four-cone-point flat pillowcases. First-order stochastic domination simultaneously settles all increasing distance losses, rather than optimizing a handpicked moment; the rare farthest-distance regime has a precise quartic exponent.

## Scope and limitations
All-radii factorization holds for any flat torus inversion quotient; the elementary CDF and strict optimizer are stated only for rectangular planar metrics. No skew-lattice optimizer or independent historical certification is claimed.
