# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim quantifies over every integer \(m\ge1\), every \(1\le k\le m\), and positive oriented transmission rates. Leaf exchangeability reduces all feasible monitoring sets to two classes. Conditioning on the uniformly chosen seed gives a zero atom, one exponential, or a sum of two independent exponentials. Substitution yields a signed survival difference equal to a constant times a strictly positive convolution integral at every positive time, including coincident rates. This proves the exact stochastic-order dichotomy and full-distribution tie. A separate full-state rational CTMC transform implementation checks 6,048 finite assertions, without being treated as a proof for infinite parameter ranges.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence, with residual priority risk

The 2007 general outbreak-detection paper establishes submodular sensor rewards and approximation methods, not this exact directed-star phase diagram. The 2014 directed interhospital SI surveillance study uses heuristic simulation on empirical patient-flow graphs and does not present a universal star first-detection law. The 2019 sentinel-placement study simulates an SIR intervention objective and does not imply the equality-in-law threshold. Multiple terminology, graph, formula and probability-order queries returned no controlling equivalent in the accessible research record. Nonetheless absence of a retrieved match does not establish historical uniqueness, and older specialized sensor/hitting-time literature remains a risk.

## Value — PASS

The full-distribution conclusion is stronger than optimizing one mean and specifies an exact budget-dependent reversal of the usual high-degree-hub monitoring heuristic. A six-vertex example shows optimal inclusion for two monitors, exact distributional indifference for three, and strict exclusion for four, all without changing rates or graph structure. This supplies a reproducible idealized benchmark for selecting monitoring sets under many time-dependent penalties and clarifies a sharp limitation of degree-only monitoring decisions. It is not a clinical or empirical surveillance prescription.

## Closest literature and limitations

Closest sources: Leskovec et al., *Cost-effective outbreak detection in networks* (2007), DOI 10.1145/1281192.1281239; Ciccolini et al., *Efficient surveillance for healthcare-associated infections spreading between hospitals* (2014), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1308062111; Colman et al., *Efficient sentinel surveillance strategies for preventing epidemics on networks* (2019), DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007517. The main theorem requires a uniform single seed, independent exponential edge transmissions, perfect continuous observation, permanent infection and a star topology.
