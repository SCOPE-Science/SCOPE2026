# Scientific review: strand-closed four-mer synchronization codes

**Correctness — PASS.** The definitions impose reverse-complement closure, all three possible proper overlaps, and the inclusion of self-comparisons. The first/last letter reduction leaves just one or two complementary-pair representatives; the one-letter case has a proved upper bound nine. In the two-letter case, the orbit catalog is generated from all eligible four-mers. Six isolated orbit nodes contribute at most eight words, while a verified five-clique partition limits the remaining contribution to nine. The 17-word witness realizes equality. Exactly eight feasible selections among 162 equality candidates, transferred over four endpoint alphabets, give 32 different maxima; direct complement-preserving permutation actions give four orbits of size eight. Two independently implemented enumerations corroborate these steps. The proof is finite and exhaustive, with no extrapolation.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Blackburn's 2013 non-overlapping-code theory establishes the unrestricted framework, and Stanovnik--Moškon--Mraz (2024, Section 5) determine ordinary four-letter maxima, including 27 for a four-symbol alphabet. The latter result does not assert the same maximum under reverse-complement closure, and its eight 27-word unrestricted optima do not imply the number or structure of closed maximum codebooks. Searches for cross-bifix-free, mutually uncorrelated, strongly comma-free, reverse-complement invariant/closed, Watson--Crick and DNA four-mer formulations did not identify a prior statement equivalent to 17 with the 32-codebook classification. Closest indexed research records concerned distinct lengths, different coding errors or general unrestricted non-overlap. Inaccessible or unindexed historical sources and alternative terminology remain genuine priority risks; absence from search results is not a uniqueness proof.

**Value — PASS.** A two-strand, four-symbol, four-position synchronization design is a natural first small instance in which the involution interacts nontrivially with multi-letter prefix--suffix restrictions. Imposing strand closure reduces the exact optimal codebook from 27 to 17 codewords and produces four genuinely distinct optimal structural types, not a generic restriction of a previous witness. The complete classification informs selection among strand-closed synchronization codebooks. These are mathematical design properties, not asserted biochemical performance.
