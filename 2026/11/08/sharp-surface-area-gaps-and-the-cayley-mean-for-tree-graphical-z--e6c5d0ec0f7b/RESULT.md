# Sharp surface-area gaps and the Cayley mean for tree graphical zonotopes

## Finding

**Theorem.** Fix an integer \(n\ge5\). For any tree \(T\) on \(n\) vertices, set
\[
Z_T=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(T)}[-(e_i-e_j)/2,(e_i-e_j)/2]\subset H_n,
\quad H_n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_i x_i=0\}.
\]
Let \(A(T)=\mathcal H^{n-2}(\partial_{H_n}Z_T)\) denote the total intrinsic Euclidean surface area, and let \(s_e\) be the smaller cardinality of the two vertex components of \(T-e\). Then
\[
A(T)=2\sum_{e\in E(T)}\sqrt{s_e(n-s_e)}.\tag{1}
\]
Set \(A_{\mathrm{star}}=2(n-1)\sqrt{n-1}\), \(A_{\mathrm{path}}=2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sqrt{j(n-j)}\), and
\[
\Delta_n=2\bigl(\sqrt{2(n-2)}-\sqrt{n-1}\bigr)>0.\tag{2}
\]
Up to graph isomorphism, the star is the unique global surface-area minimizer, and the path is the unique maximizer. Among nonstar trees, the unique minimizer is a star with one edge subdivided once and has area \(A_{\mathrm{star}}+\Delta_n\). Among nonpath trees, the unique maximizer is the three-arm tree with arm lengths \(1,1,n-3\) and has area \(A_{\mathrm{path}}-\Delta_n\). Thus the first-to-second area gaps coincide exactly at both extremes.

If \(\mathcal T_n\) is a uniformly random labeled tree, then its exact expected area is
\[
\mathbb EA(\mathcal T_n)=\frac{1}{n^{n-2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}
\binom nk k^{k-1}(n-k)^{n-k-1}\sqrt{k(n-k)},\tag{3}
\]
with the convention \(1^0=1\); moreover
\[
\mathbb EA(\mathcal T_n)=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,n^{3/2}\log n+O(n^{3/2})\qquad(n\to\infty).\tag{4}
\]
Here all extremal uniqueness claims are among *unlabeled* tree isomorphism types; each labeled realization gives an equivalent equality case.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are simple trees with unit edge generators in the canonical incidence-vector realization. The ambient metric is the Euclidean metric induced on the zero-sum hyperplane. The surface area is \((n-2)\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the boundary of the \((n-1)\)-dimensional parallelotope. The smallest component count \(s_e\) is an integer in \(1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Formula (1) also holds for all \(n\ge2\); the extremal statements are made for \(n\ge5\) to avoid coincidences of tree types. The random tree is uniformly distributed over all \(n^{n-2}\) labeled trees on \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), not over unlabeled isomorphism classes.

## Proof

**Facet volumes.** Choose any orientation of the edges and let \(B\) be the \(n\times(n-1)\) matrix whose columns are \(b_e=e_i-e_j\). The columns form a basis of \(H_n\). Deleting any one row yields a reduced incidence matrix with determinant \(\pm1\). By Cauchy--Binet, \(\det(B^\mathsf TB)=n\), so the intrinsic \((n-1)\)-volume of \(Z_T\) is \(\sqrt n\). The two facets corresponding to edge \(e\) are translates of the parallelotope generated by the remaining columns. Let \(w_e\in H_n\) be the unique vector with \(\langle w_e,b_f\rangle=\mathbf1_{e=f}\). If deletion of \(e\) divides the vertices into a set \(S\) of size \(k\) and its complement, then, up to sign,
\[
w_e=\mathbf1_S-\frac{k}{n}\mathbf1,
\qquad \|w_e\|=\sqrt{\frac{k(n-k)}n}.
\]
The distance between the two facet planes is \(1/\|w_e\|\), and the parallelepiped volume is the facet area times this height. Each facet therefore has area \(\sqrt n\|w_e\|=\sqrt{k(n-k)}\). Summing both facets for all \(n-1\) edges proves (1).

**Extremal bound from edge-cut tails.** Put \(d=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), \(f(k)=\sqrt{k(n-k)}\), and
\[
F_k(T)=\#\{e\in E(T):s_e\ge k\}\quad(1\le k\le d).
\]
For \(k\ge2\), the edges counted by \(F_k\) induce a connected subtree if nonempty. Indeed, the path between two such edges also separates at least \(k\) vertices on each side. If the resulting core has \(h\ge1\) edges, its two terminal edges each cut off a vertex set of size at least \(k\) outside the rest of the core. Counting the \(h+1\) core vertices and at least \(k-1\) additional vertices at each end yields \(n\ge h+2k-1\). Consequently
\[
F_k(T)\le\max\{0,n-2k+1\}=F_k(P_n),\tag{5}
\]
where \(P_n\) denotes the path graph. Strict monotonicity of \(f\) on \(1,\ldots,d\), together with the exact summation-by-parts identity
\[
\frac12A(T)=(n-1)f(1)+\sum_{k=2}^{d}\bigl(f(k)-f(k-1)\bigr)F_k(T),\tag{6}
\]
proves that the path has largest area. Equality in (6) implies \(F_2(T)=n-3\); since \(F_2(T)=n-1-\ell(T)\), where \(\ell(T)\) is the number of leaves, this forces exactly two leaves and hence a path. Conversely every nonstar has at least one nonpendant edge, so \(F_2(T)\ge1\); (6) yields \(A(T)\ge A_{\mathrm{star}}+2(f(2)-f(1))\). Equality requires \(F_2=1\), hence exactly two internal vertices adjacent by the unique nonpendant edge, with one side of size two. This is precisely the once-subdivided star, proving the unique second minimum.

For any nonpath \(\ell(T)\ge3\), so \(F_2(T)\le n-4=F_2(P_n)-1\). Together with (5)--(6) this gives
\[
A(T)\le A_{\mathrm{path}}-2(f(2)-f(1)).\tag{7}
\]
The three-arm tree with lengths \(1,1,n-3\) has exactly three leaves and exactly one size-2 cut (when \(n\ge5\)); its remaining cuts, along the longest arm, realize all the path tail bounds for \(k\ge3\), so (7) is sharp. For \(n\ge6\), equality in (7) requires exactly three leaves and \(F_3=n-5\). Every tree with three leaves has a unique branch vertex of degree three and three arm lengths \(a\le b\le c\), with \(a+b+c=n-1\). If \(b\ge2\), then the edges one step inward from the leaves on two different arms give at least two distinct size-2 cuts, in addition to three size-1 cuts, contradicting \(F_3=n-5\). Thus \(a=b=1\), giving the unique three-arm equality tree. When \(n=5\), the only unlabeled trees other than star and path are the three-arm tree \((1,1,2)\). Both extremal gaps equal \(2(f(2)-f(1))=\Delta_n\).

**Cayley expectation.** Fix a nonempty proper labeled set \(S\) of size \(k\). The number of labeled trees in which exactly one edge crosses \((S,S^c)\), separating two connected trees on either side, is \(k^{k-2}(n-k)^{n-k-2}\,k(n-k)\). For singleton sides use the unique one-vertex tree. Multiplying by \(\sqrt{k(n-k)}\), summing over \(\binom nk\) possible sets \(S\), and dividing by two because each unordered cut is counted from both sides, then multiplying by two facets per edge, produces (3). The formula is exact for every \(n\ge2\).

For the asymptotic, use uniform Stirling's representation \(r_m=\log(m!)-(m+\tfrac12)\log m+m-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)\), for integers \(m\ge1\), with \(r_m=O(m^{-1})\). The \(k\)-th summand of (3) is *exactly*
\[
\frac{n^{5/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,k(n-k)}\exp(r_n-r_k-r_{n-k}).\tag{8}
\]
Uniformly in \(1\le k\le n-1\), the exponential is \(1+O(1/k+1/(n-k))\). The main harmonic sum is
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac1{k(n-k)}=\frac{2H_{n-1}}n,\qquad H_{n-1}=\log n+O(1),
\]
while the error summed in (8) is \(O(n^{3/2})\), because \(\sum k^{-2}(n-k)^{-1}+k^{-1}(n-k)^{-2}=O(1/n)\). Hence (4). No numerical enumeration is used to infer the limit.

## Verification

The standalone file `check_surface.py` checks the Gram-determinant facet-volume identity and enumerates every nonisomorphic tree on \(5\) through \(11\) vertices, confirming the unique first and second extremizers. It separately enumerates labeled Prüfer trees on \(2\) through \(6\) vertices and verifies (3) numerically to floating-point tolerance. Four larger dimensions provide non-proving checks on the asymptotic constant. Successful execution returned `TREE_ZONOTOPE_SURFACE_EXTREMAL_CAYLEY_CHECKS_PASSED`. This finite testing is supplementary to the mathematical proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The general intrinsic-volume formula for arbitrary zonotopes of Joós and Lángi, also obtained by Brazitikos and McIntyre, already implies a general algorithm for the facet areas; equation (1) is a special-case evaluation, **not** claimed as a new universal zonotope formula. Padrol, Pilaud, and Poullot describe the graphical zonotope setting and its deformations, without the stated tree surface-area extremal ladder or random-label expectation. General cut-size extremal orderings can supply the star/path extreme comparison; the second-extremizer uniqueness, exact equal gap on both sides, and logarithmic Cayley expectation (3)--(4) are the distinct contributions asserted here. Wang studies extremal functions of **leaf-set** split sizes in phylogenetic trees, a different invariant from the **vertex-set** split sizes in (1). None of the checked comparison statements entails (3)--(4) or the paired sharp second-extremizer assertion.

## Limitations

The surface area is not invariant under arbitrary affine transformations; all conclusions use the specified unweighted Euclidean incidence realization. The extremal uniqueness is up to graph isomorphism, not labeled identity. The Cayley expectation is an expectation and leading-order asymptotic only; no concentration theorem or limit distribution is asserted. Generic intrinsic-volume identities and some majorization comparisons are established prior mathematics. No exhaustive search of all possible terminological aliases or historical manuscripts is claimed, and the priority of the combined second-extremal and random expectation assertions remains provisional pending independent review.
