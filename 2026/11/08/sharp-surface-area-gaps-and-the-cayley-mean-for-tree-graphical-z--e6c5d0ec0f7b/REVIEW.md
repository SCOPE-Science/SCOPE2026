# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Facet Euclidean normalizations are derived from reduced incidence determinants and centered cut indicators, yielding the exact formula with a factor of two for opposite facets. The tail-count argument proves uniform path domination; equality conditions isolate each second-best tree. Cayley partition counting and uniform Stirling bounds establish the stated expectation and leading coefficient. Verification separately enumerates finite cases and provides numerical checks, not infinite certification.

## Originality

**PASS; historical priority provisional.** The general intrinsic-volume formula in Joós--Lángi (and the earlier Brazitikos--McIntyre identity) already covers the facet-volume step. General tree split majorization may cover the extreme path/star cases. The assessed novelty concerns the *simultaneous* sharp second-extremizer classification and identical gaps at both ends, together with the exact Cayley expected area and logarithmic asymptotic. Padrol--Pilaud--Poullot treat deformation cones rather than this metric/statistical pair. Wang's phylogenetic splits count **leaves**, whereas the present Euclidean facet normals count **all vertices**. Relevant published mathematical records concern width variance or other invariants, and do not furnish these conclusions. Broader unindexed tree additive-functional literature remains a risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Canonical tree zonotopes have a common volume but surface area discriminates their shape and supplies an exact second-extreme stability threshold. The exact Cayley mean and its logarithmic correction quantify the typical geometric scale, beyond either extremal shape; each is a natural all-order invariant rather than a single computed example.

## Closest literature and limitations

Joós and Lángi, arXiv:2206.03204, Corollary 1, provide a general zonotope intrinsic-volume formula. Padrol et al., arXiv:2111.12422, provide the graph-zonotope framework. Wang (2019), DOI:10.1016/j.aam.2018.12.004, proves related leaf-split extremality. The statement is confined to the standard, unit-weighted Euclidean edge-incidence realization and uniform *labeled* Cayley trees; it does not assert a limit distribution, concentration or priority over uninspected synonyms.
