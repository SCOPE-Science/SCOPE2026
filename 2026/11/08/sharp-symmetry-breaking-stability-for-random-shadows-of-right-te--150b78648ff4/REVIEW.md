# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The final claim is a globally sharp quadratic remainder for the probability that a random planar orthogonal shadow of a nondegenerate trirectangular tetrahedron is triangular. Every symbol is defined in the theorem. The probability reduction is independently established by the spherical-cone argument; the resulting three-variable optimization is reduced to two equal normalized squared face areas by a derivative-level-set argument. A boundary comparison excludes the noncompact/degenerate limits. Uniqueness of the univariate extremum follows from an exact degree-nine Sturm count and elementary derivative signs. Equality occurs both at equal legs (where the quadratic dispersion vanishes) and at a unique non-equiaxial two-equal-legs class. The standalone verifier reproduced exact sign variations, the rational boundary comparison and numeric geometry checks. Finite random trials do not prove the universal inequality.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations

Feldman–Klain (2008) establishes the probabilistic solid-angle interpretation for simplex shadows; Katsuura (2020), Theorem 2, already obtains the unrefined sharp minimum of the sum of solid angles for right tetrahedra. Those statements are antecedents, not novel content. Katsuura's full six-page article does not give a globally optimal quadratic coefficient for imbalance among right-face area squares or its off-center equality case. Korotov (2026), Theorem 5.5, includes a dispersion defect for isogonic tetrahedra expressed in different reciprocal tangent variables; the generic right tetrahedron is not isogonic, and the estimate does not imply the sharp right-tetrahedron remainder. Brandts–Cihangir–Křížek (2015) treats aggregate dihedral-angle ranges for nonobtuse simplices; its accessible abstract does not establish the refined coefficient. Relevant published record summaries involve tetrahedral shadow-area moments, not the sharp count-probability stability inequality. A full-text comparison for the 2015 item and the closest indexed result was unavailable, so absolute historical priority is not established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The dispersion is geometric: de Gua's theorem identifies each simplex coordinate with the squared area of a perpendicular face divided by the squared area of the oblique face. The result quantifies how strongly a simple observable—the chance of a triangular random shadow—detects imbalance in physical face areas. The exact sharp coefficient is attained away from the symmetric reference shape, a meaningful nonlocal symmetry-breaking phenomenon absent from the prior qualitative extremal theorem. This is an exact inverse-stability benchmark for a classical random-shadow model, not a routine rewording of its nonquantitative lower bound.

## Closest sources and boundaries

The proof relies on the 2008 shadow probability identity and the well-known trirectangular geometry used in the 2020 extremal result. It does not claim a new uniform-direction projection formula. The result excludes arbitrary tetrahedra, nonorthogonal legs, and finite-sample inference guarantees. An independently performed review or audit is not claimed.
