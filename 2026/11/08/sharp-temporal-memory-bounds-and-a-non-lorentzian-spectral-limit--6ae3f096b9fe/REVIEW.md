# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The physical claim is precisely the stationary hydrodynamic right support edge of the Thomas–Fermi free expansion, not a microscopic extremal particle. The proof reconstructs the exact scale ODE and energy integral, establishes the stationary age last-reset covariance, obtains strict lower and upper bounds from covariance association and law of total variance, and derives both scaling limits by dominated \(L^2\) convergence. The exact exponential factorial moments give the coefficient \(2/5\), and the uniform upper envelope controls integrals and Fourier transforms. The included verifier separately tests the ODE, covariance envelopes and asymptotics. No finite sample is used as a proof of a universal claim.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The 2026 motivating manuscript derives the reset Thomas–Fermi scale and one-time density/edge distribution, but the inspected stationary-density, edge-statistics and concluding sections did not supply these two-time inequalities or frequency spectra. The 2021 resetting-autocorrelation paper establishes the general method and the constant-drift stationary exponential autocorrelation, so that baseline and its special case are expressly excluded from the originality claim. The 2020 review supplies the already-known last-renewal framework. Searches for exact edge covariance, aliases under quadratic age, and the \(7/5\) factor did not identify a covering result; a negative search cannot prove publication priority.

**Value — PASS.** Two different physical regimes of the same recent condensate model have sharply distinct universal edge-correlation spectra despite stationary support-edge distributions being obtainable by a one-time renewal mixture. The all-rate strict envelope and bounded relaxation time offer experimentally meaningful temporal diagnostics, and the rapid-reset non-Lorentzian profile distinguishes quadratic early expansion from a constant-drift approximation. The statement is model-specific and does not claim a general theorem for all resetting processes.
