# Sharp three-element associativity obstruction for \(\mathbf{BM}\)-algebras

## Finding

Consider a **marked \(\mathbf{BM}\)-algebra**: a nonempty magma \((A,\cdot)\) with distinguished elements \(B,M\in A\) satisfying, for every \(x,y,z\in A\),
\[
((B\cdot x)\cdot y)\cdot z=x\cdot(y\cdot z),\qquad M\cdot x=x\cdot x.
\]
An isomorphism must preserve the operation and the two distinguished elements. The distinguished elements may coincide. The algebra is *generated* if the smallest submagma containing \(B\) and \(M\) is \(A\).

**Theorem.** The least cardinality of a nonassociative finite marked \(\mathbf{BM}\)-algebra is **three**. At that cardinality there are precisely **six marked isomorphism classes** of nonassociative examples, supported on precisely **two unmarked magma types**. Exactly **one of the six** is generated by its distinguished elements. Its canonical multiplication table, with row \(x\) and column \(y\) denoting \(x\cdot y\), is

| \(\cdot\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(2\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(0\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(1\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(1\) |
| \(2\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(1\) |

with \(B=0\) and \(M=2\). In particular \(M\cdot M=1\), so \(B,M\) generate the entire carrier. Nevertheless,
\[
(1\cdot2)\cdot2=1\ne0=1\cdot(2\cdot2).
\]
Thus a finite algebra generated by the bluebird and mockingbird can be nonassociative even at the first possible order.

The full census at cardinalities \(1,2,3\) is:

| \(\lvert A\rvert\) | Marked labelled structures | Marked isomorphism classes | Generated marked isomorphism classes | Nonassociative marked isomorphism classes | Nonassociative generated classes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(1\) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| \(2\) | 14 | 7 | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| \(3\) | 204 | 36 | 7 | 6 | 1 |

Here *labelled structures* counts each pair of choices of \(B,M\) separately when one operation has multiple witnesses.

## Assumptions and scope

All finite carriers are nonempty and labelled \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) for enumeration. The operation need not be associative, commutative, or cancellative, and the two distinguished elements need not generate the carrier unless expressly stated. This is the equational notion of \(\mathbf{BM}\)-algebra used in the cited 2026 work. It should not be confused with other unrelated notions also called BM-algebras. Results about generated algebras are explicitly restricted to the submagma generated by \(B,M\).

## Proof

**Step 1: finite exhaustive reduction.** There are exactly \(n^{n^2}\) binary operation tables on a fixed labelled \(n\)-element carrier. For each table \(t\), the possible mockingbirds are precisely the rows satisfying
\[
t(M,x)=t(x,x)\quad\text{for all }x,
\]
and the possible bluebirds are precisely those \(B\) satisfying
\[
t(t(t(B,x),y),z)=t(x,t(y,z))\quad\text{for all }x,y,z.
\]
These are finite universal tests, not heuristics. For each possible pair \((B,M)\) of witnesses, check associativity on all triples, and check generation by repeatedly closing \(\{B,M\}\) under the table. No arbitrary cutoff on the generation depth is used: closure terminates once no element is added.

**Step 2: complete exact enumeration.** Applying the tests for \(n=1,2,3\) examines, respectively, \(1,16,19683\) operation tables. The numbers of satisfying labelled marked algebras are \(1,14,204\), of which \(0,0,36\) are nonassociative. Of the marked satisfying algebras, respectively \(1,8,42\) are generated, and \(0,0,6\) of those are nonassociative. All counts are obtained by exhaustive integer-only loops reproduced in `checker.py`, with the exact defining equations written in its executable tests.

**Step 3: isomorphism and independence of labels.** The symmetric group on the carrier acts simultaneously on the operation table and on \(B,M\). Applying every permutation and choosing the lexicographically least transported triple produces a canonical representative. For order three, Burnside's lemma provides a separate check on the full marked classification: the identity fixes \(204\) marked algebras, each of the three transpositions fixes \(4\), and neither three-cycle fixes any. Hence the number of marked isomorphism classes is
\[
\frac{204+3\cdot4}{6}=36.
\]
In the nonassociative subfamily, only the identity fixes any example, fixing \(36\), so there are exactly \(36/6=6\) classes. For *generated* marked algebras, an automorphism fixing \(B,M\) fixes every term in \(B,M\), hence every element; thus the symmetric-group action is free. The \(42\) labelled generated three-element algebras give exactly \(42/6=7\) classes, of which \(6/6=1\) is nonassociative. The same free-action argument gives \(8/2=4\) generated two-element classes.

**Step 4: explicit classification of the first obstruction.** Each nonassociative three-element marked algebra is isomorphic to exactly one marking of one of these two displayed multiplication tables (rows indexed by \(0,1,2\), with entries in column order \(0,1,2\)):

- **Type I:** rows \((0,0,0)\), \((0,0,0)\), \((0,2,0)\); the four allowed markings are \((B,M)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\).
- **Type II:** rows \((0,0,0)\), \((0,0,1)\), \((0,0,1)\); the two allowed markings are \((B,M)=(0,1),(0,2)\).

The six marked structures have trivial automorphism groups, and no two displayed markings are isomorphic. The Type I structures have submagma generated by the marks of size at most two, as does Type II with marks \((0,1)\); only Type II with marks \((0,2)\) is generated. Direct substitution into the identities verifies every displayed example. For Type I, \((2\cdot1)\cdot1=2\ne0=2\cdot(1\cdot1)\); for Type II, \((1\cdot2)\cdot2=1\ne0=1\cdot(2\cdot2)\). This completes the smallest-order and uniqueness claims.

## Verification

Run `python3 checker.py` in a directory containing that file. It uses the Python standard library only, enumerates every binary table for sizes one, two and three, checks the marked \(\mathbf B\) and \(\mathbf M\) universal equations, computes canonical representatives and fixed-point counts under relabelling, checks generated submagmas, and asserts the six exact marked canonical tables and unique generated nonassociative type. Its success marker is `BM_MINIMAL_NONASSOCIATIVE_CENSUS_OK`. All assertions must pass. The enumeration is exhaustive at these cardinalities and makes no assertion about the number of larger finite algebras.

## Relationship to prior work

Takuma Imamura, *Finite BM-algebras and fixed-point combinators*, arXiv:2610.37108 (first public version, 27 October 2026), proves that all finite \(\mathbf{BI}\)-algebras are associative and that every finite \(\mathbf{BM}\)-algebra has a \(\mathbf Y\)-element. The proof also constructs a quotient with better algebraic properties. The cited results do not state the six minimal nonassociative marked types or the unique generated obstruction. They show why requiring a distinguished identity element is a genuinely stronger structural restriction. The paper's public `magma-search` program provides a general tool capable of enumerating small magmas; this result instead specifies and verifies the exact first nonassociative classification. Its explicit finite census may be reproducible with that existing utility and is not a new general enumeration algorithm.

Imamura, *Bluebirds and mockingbirds cannot produce a fixed-point combinator*, arXiv:2609.21765, concerns failure of uniform fixed-point terms in the free combinatory fragment; that statement does not imply this small-algebra associativity classification. The lack of a closed \(\mathbf{BM}\)-term defining a fixed-point combinator is compatible with every finite \(\mathbf{BM}\)-algebra having some such element.

## Limitations

The classification concerns only orders at most three and exact equational \(\mathbf{BM}\)-algebras. The unique generated nonassociative representative is not a classification of all generated finite \(\mathbf{BM}\)-algebras of larger sizes. The result neither addresses existence of fixed-point combinators in the free term algebra nor characterizes the full finite variety. Historical priority is not independently certified; private or unindexed outputs of general-purpose magma enumerators could contain equivalent small tables. There has been no independent mathematical audit.
