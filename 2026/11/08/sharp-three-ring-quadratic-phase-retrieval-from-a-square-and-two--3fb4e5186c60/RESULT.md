# Sharp three-ring quadratic phase retrieval from a square and two diameters

## Finding
Let \(r,s,t>0\). For a complex quadratic polynomial \(p(z)=a+bz+cz^2\), take its squared moduli at the eight points
\[
\mathcal Z(r,s,t)=\{r,ir,-r,-ir,se^{i\pi/4},-se^{i\pi/4},t,-t\}.
\]
The measurements determine *every* \(p\) up to multiplication by a common unimodular constant if and only if
\[
\boxed{r\ne s,\qquad r\ne t,\qquad ((s^2-r^2)(s^2-t^2))^2\ne4r^2s^4t^2.}
\]
For \(r\ne s\) and \(r\ne t\), the real kernel of the associated Hermitian measurement map is exactly the line generated by
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}r^2t^2&0&i\Delta\\0&-(r^2+t^2)&0\\-i\Delta&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Delta=\frac{(s^2-r^2)(s^2-t^2)}{2s^2}.
\]
In particular, the eight-sample phase retrieval failure is an exact algebraic resonance, not merely a genericity condition.

If \(r\ne t\), put \(x=s^2\). The two *outer* resonance values are the positive roots of
\[
x^2-(r+t)^2x+r^2t^2=0.
\]
They lie respectively below \(\min(r^2,t^2)\) and above \(\max(r^2,t^2)\). The two *inner* resonance values solve
\[
x^2-(r-t)^2x+r^2t^2=0.
\]
They exist as distinct positive roots strictly between \(r^2\) and \(t^2\) exactly when \(\max(r/t,t/r)>2+\sqrt3\), and merge into one double root \(x=rt\) at equality. If this ratio is smaller, there are no inner resonances. Hence the number of positive exceptional middle radii is respectively two, three, or four; additionally \(s=r\) always fails and \(t=r\) always fails. No seven arbitrary complex point evaluations universally retrieve all complex quadratics.

## Assumptions and scope
The observations are exact complex-point intensities \(|p(z)|^2\), with labelled sampling points and unrestricted complex coefficients; equality is modulo multiplication of the *whole* polynomial by a scalar of modulus one. All three radii are strictly positive. Repeated nodes are permitted in the statement and necessarily lead to failure. The conclusion is universal, not almost-everywhere identifiability or a noise-stability estimate.

## Proof
Let \(v(z)=(1,z,z^2)^T\) and \(Q_H(z)=v(z)^*Hv(z)\) for Hermitian \(3\times3\) matrices \(H\). Write
\[
Q_H(z)=A+B|z|^2+C|z|^4+2\Re\{(D+E|z|^2)z+Fz^2\},
\]
where \(A,B,C\) are real, and \(D,E,F\) are complex. This is a real-linear parametrization of all nine Hermitian degrees of freedom. For \(Q_H\) to vanish at the square \(\pm r,\pm ir\), subtract opposite-vertex equations, then subtract horizontal from vertical equations. The resulting necessary and sufficient conditions are
\[
D=-r^2E,\qquad A=-r^2B-r^4C,\qquad \Re F=0.
\]
Set \(F=if\) with \(f\in\mathbb R\). At the antipodes \(\pm t\), the difference gives \((t^2-r^2)\Re E=0\), and the sum gives
\[
(t^2-r^2)\bigl(B+(t^2+r^2)C\bigr)=0.
\]
When \(t\ne r\), this forces \(\Re E=0\) and \(B=-(r^2+t^2)C\), with \(A=r^2t^2C\). At the diagonal antipodes \(\pm se^{i\pi/4}\), their difference gives \((s^2-r^2)\Re(Ee^{i\pi/4})=0\). If \(s\ne r\), since \(E\) is already purely imaginary, this forces \(E=D=0\). The common diagonal equation is
\[
(s^2-r^2)(s^2-t^2)C-2s^2f=0.
\]
Thus \(f=\Delta C\), proving precisely the displayed one-dimensional kernel for the noncolliding radii.

Two polynomials have identical measurements exactly when their coefficient vectors \(u,v\in\mathbb C^3\) give a Hermitian kernel matrix \(uu^*-vv^*\). Every such matrix has rank at most two. Conversely, each nonzero indefinite Hermitian kernel matrix of rank at most two admits a spectral decomposition \(uu^*-vv^*\) with nonproportional vectors, giving an ambiguity; a semidefinite kernel matrix also gives an ambiguity if one evaluation vector does not span its range. For this sample set, the evaluation vectors span \(\mathbb C^3\) because it contains at least three distinct points, so no nonzero semidefinite matrix lies in the kernel. Therefore a nonzero kernel matrix can obstruct universal phase retrieval exactly when it has rank at most two.

The determinant of the displayed generator equals
\[
-(r^2+t^2)(r^2t^2-\Delta^2).
\]
It is nonzero precisely off the resonance. On resonance the generator is indefinite of rank two, and the ambiguity can be seen explicitly:
\[
p(z)=z^2-i\Delta,\qquad q(z)=\sqrt{r^2+t^2}\,z,
\]
which satisfy \(|p(z)|^2=|q(z)|^2\) at all eight sample points when \(\Delta^2=r^2t^2\). They are not unimodular multiples.

If \(s=r\), the six samples at the first two orbits belong to one circle. A quadratic intensity restricted to a fixed circle is a real trigonometric polynomial of degree at most two (five real Fourier coefficients); the remaining two point evaluations therefore give measurement rank at most seven. If \(t=r\), two samples repeat existing square vertices and rank is at most six. In either case the Hermitian kernel has real dimension at least two. Every two-dimensional real subspace of Hermitian \(3\times3\) matrices contains a nonzero singular matrix: for linearly independent \(H_0,H_1\), continuity and the oddness of determinant give a zero on the circle \(H_0\cos\theta+H_1\sin\theta\). Since no nonzero semidefinite matrix belongs to the kernel, this singular matrix is indefinite, hence has rank two and gives an ambiguity. The same argument proves failure for at most seven arbitrary point evaluations of degree-two polynomials, provided there are at least three distinct nodes; with fewer distinct nodes failure is immediate from noninjective polynomial evaluation.

Finally resonance means \(|(s^2-r^2)(s^2-t^2)|=2rts^2\). Writing \(x=s^2\), it splits into the two asserted quadratics. The outer quadratic is positive at zero, negative at \(r^2\) and \(t^2\), and positive for sufficiently large \(x\); hence it has one root on either side of the radial interval. The inner quadratic has discriminant \((r-t)^4-4r^2t^2=(r^2+t^2)(r^2+t^2-4rt)\). It has real roots exactly when \(\max(r/t,t/r)\ge2+\sqrt3\); at equality its double root is \(rt\), and above equality its two positive roots lie strictly between \(r^2\) and \(t^2\). This proves the exact classification.

## Verification
The standalone `check.py` uses exact SymPy arithmetic to independently construct the eight-by-nine Hermitian lifting matrix, verify the claimed kernel generator, compute ranks in generic and repeated-radius cases, and verify explicit ambiguous polynomial pairs at all four simple resonance branches and the double-root boundary. Execute `python check.py` with SymPy installed. The infinite-dimensional quantifiers over positive radii are proved by the symbolic reductions above; finitely many evaluations in the checker are corroboration, not a substitute for those reductions.

## Relationship to prior work
Pohl, Li and Boche (2017, Theorem 7 and surrounding discussion) give a minimal eight-sample quadratic reconstruction construction with a five-plus-three allocation on two concentric circles. That construction supplies relevant context but does not imply the exact three-ring four-plus-two-plus-two failure locus, its explicit Hermitian kernel, or the radial resonance transition. Bodmann and Hammen (2013) establish low-redundancy complex phase retrieval using their own structured frame geometry, not the above prescribed constellation. Chalendar and Partington (2024) study phase retrieval from circles and lines, including continuous-circle uniqueness; their continuous measurement hypotheses do not imply a universal classification at these eight prescribed points. A general eight-sample lower bound is therefore acknowledged rather than claimed as novel.

## Limitations
The result concerns exact, labelled scalar intensity samples of quadratic polynomials. It does not establish a conditioning bound near the forbidden radii, nonquadratic degrees, or arbitrary three-circle angular allocations. The literature comparison cannot exclude an unidentified equivalent specialized geometric-frame computation; independent review has not been performed.
