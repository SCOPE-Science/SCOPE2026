# Scientific assessment

**Correctness: PASS.** For \(m\geq2\), total Roman domination has weight four exactly while a surviving adjacent pair dominates. The proof gives deletion patterns for the upper bounds, exhaustive lower-bound enumeration for \(m=2,3,4\), and a reduction sending any four-deletion counterexample on \(m>4\) to one on \(m=4\). The independent direct label-enumerator confirms the boundary cases. The scope is all integers \(m\geq1\); the finite computation is an exact certificate for its stated three instances, not a substitute for the reduction.

**Originality: PASS, with historical-priority risk.** The 2026 introduction of total Roman bondage treats complete bipartite graphs and shows its relationship to ordinary total bondage. The 2024 paper provides multipartite upper bounds, and the 2025 paper studies \(k\)-total bondage on standard families. None of the inspected material supplies the exact tripartite sequence \(1,4,5\), a four-vertex cutoff, or a dominating-pair obstruction classification for this family. The weight-four equivalence itself is credited as prior. Unidentified equivalent formulations of ordinary total bondage remain a possible priority risk.

**Value: PASS.** The first complete tripartite family obtained by adding an unrestricted third part to two doubleton parts already exhibits a nontrivial jump from \(4\) to \(5\) and stabilization independent of graph order. This is a sharp infinite-family edge-deletion threshold for a recently introduced vulnerability parameter, not a single small-instance calculation. The four-representative argument illustrates why a bounded finite obstruction suffices to control all larger members.

**Limits.** The result does not classify arbitrary tripartite graphs or count extremal deletion sets. The proof of the finite base lower bounds is computer-assisted and reproducible by the supplied standalone script; no independent mathematical audit has taken place.
