# Sharp two-arc Fourier observability: a Legendre constant and asymptotic antipodal design

## Finding
For every fixed integer \(m\ge1\), two shrinking equal arcs with distinct fixed centers \(\pm\theta\) have an explicit smallest Fourier concentration eigenvalue to leading order. The coefficient depends on separation and is uniquely maximized at antipodal centers. This optimization holds also when the centers may move with the arc width: every choice of maximizing centers converges to antipodal placement, and
\[
\lim_{\alpha\downarrow0}\alpha^{-(2m+1)}\max_{\alpha\le\theta\le\pi-\alpha}\lambda_m(\alpha,\theta)
=\frac{2^{4m+1}}{\pi(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}^{3}}.
\]
The contribution is the coefficient for arbitrary fixed interior angles and its all-degree optimization over moving placements; the separately known exact three-mode optimum is used only as a benchmark.

## Assumptions and scope
On \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\) use normalized Haar measure \(dt/(2\pi)\). The space \(V_m\) consists of \(f(t)=\sum_{k=-m}^m a_ke^{ikt}\) with complex coefficients and norm squared \(\sum_k|a_k|^2\). Take \(0<\alpha<\pi/2\) and \(\alpha\le\theta\le\pi-\alpha\). Let \(E_{\alpha,\theta}\) be the union of the arcs centered at \(\theta\) and \(-\theta\), each of half-width \(\alpha\). Boundary contact is harmless. Define \(\lambda_m(\alpha,\theta)=\min_{\|f\|_2=1,\ f\in V_m}\int_{E_{\alpha,\theta}}|f(t)|^2dt/(2\pi)\). Write \(B_m=\binom{2m}{m}\) and \(S_m(c)=\sum_{k=0}^{2m}|[z^k](z^2-2cz+1)^m|^2\). For fixed \(0<\theta<\pi\) the precise result is
\[
\lim_{\alpha\downarrow0}\frac{\lambda_m(\alpha,\theta)}{\alpha^{2m+1}}
=C_m(\theta):=\frac{2^{4m+1}(\sin\theta)^{2m}}{\pi(2m+1)B_m^2 S_m(\cos\theta)}.
\]

## Proof
**Limiting direction and lower bound.** Write \(f(t)=e^{-imt}P(e^{it})\) with \(\deg P\le2m\). The polynomial \(Q_\theta(z)=(z-e^{i\theta})^m(z-e^{-i\theta})^m\), divided by its coefficient norm, yields \(\lambda_m=O(\alpha^{2m+1})\). For unit minimizers \(f_\alpha\), expand \(\alpha^{-m} f_\alpha(\pm\theta+\alpha u)\) to order \(m\) on \([-1,1]\). The remainder is uniformly \(O_m(\alpha)\) by the coefficient norm bound. The rescaled observation cost bounds the two local \(L^2\) norms, and equivalence of norms in the degree-\(m\) polynomial space bounds every rescaled Taylor coefficient. In particular the unscaled derivatives of orders below \(m\) tend to zero at both centers. Any subsequential limit of the unit coefficient vectors must therefore be the normalized \(Q_\theta\) times a unit phase. The local leading Taylor coefficient at either center has modulus \((2\sin\theta)^m/\sqrt{S_m(\cos\theta)}\).

The least squared \(L^2([-1,1],du)\) norm for a monic degree-\(m\) polynomial is \(h_m=2\,4^m/[(2m+1)B_m^2]\), attained by the monic Legendre polynomial: the usual Legendre polynomial has leading coefficient \(B_m/2^m\), squared norm \(2/(2m+1)\), and is orthogonal to every lower degree. The same minimum multiplied by the squared modulus of the leading coefficient holds for complex coefficients. Taking a lower-limit subsequence of the two rescaled Taylor polynomials gives the lower bound \((2\sin\theta)^{2m}h_m/[\pi S_m(\cos\theta)]\); the factor \(1/\pi\) accounts for two arcs and Haar normalization.

**Matching upper bound.** The linear map from \(V_m\) to the pair of jets of orders \(0,\ldots,m-1\) has one-dimensional kernel spanned by \(e^{-imt}Q_\theta(e^{it})\), by divisibility at the two distinct points. It therefore has a bounded right inverse for fixed \(\theta\). Starting with the unit kernel vector, prescribe jet corrections of order \(\alpha^{m-j}\) for the degree-\(j\) Taylor coefficients, to put the corresponding lower coefficients of the two rescaled monic Legendre polynomials in each jet (multiplying by the local leading coefficient). The correction vector has size \(O(\alpha)\). After renormalization it leaves the limiting leading coefficient unchanged and realizes the two Legendre costs. This proves the formula for \(C_m\). The proof is compact-uniform for \(\theta\) in the open interval: the interpolation map has a continuous bounded right inverse on each compact subinterval, the Taylor remainder bounds are uniform, and any test sequence of angles then has a subsequence tending to an interior angle to which the lower-bound argument applies.

**Moving centers.** Parseval gives \(S_m(c)=\int_0^{2\pi}(2\cos t-2c)^{2m}dt/(2\pi)\). In its expansion in even powers of \(c\) all coefficients are positive, and \(S_m(0)=\sum_j\binom mj^2=B_m\). Thus the denominator is strictly minimized at \(c=0\), where also \(\sin^{2m}\theta=1\); \(C_m\) is uniquely maximized there. On either arc one of the distances \(|e^{it}-e^{\pm i\theta}|\) is at most \(\alpha\) and the other at most \(2\sin\theta+\alpha\), by the chord-distance triangle inequality. Consequently the normalized trial polynomial gives \(\lambda_m(\alpha,\theta)\le K_m\alpha^{2m+1}(\sin\theta+\alpha)^{2m}\), uniformly in admissible \(\theta\). A subsequence of maximizing angles cannot tend to either endpoint, since the antipodal normalized eigenvalue tends to the positive \(C_m(\pi/2)\). Existence of maximizers follows from continuity on the compact admissible interval. Compact-uniform convergence and the unique interior maximizer prove both the stated optimized value and the convergence of all maximizing sequences.

**Antipodal conditioning.** The Gram matrix at \(\theta=\pi/2\) is \(\alpha/\pi\) times the sum of the two evaluation Gram matrices plus \(O_m(\alpha^3)\) in norm, by symmetric Taylor integration. The evaluation vectors have squared norm \(2m+1\) and their mutual inner product has modulus \(1\), the absolute value of the sum of \((-1)^k\) from \(-m\) to \(m\). The top eigenvalue is therefore \((2m+2)\alpha/\pi+O_m(\alpha^3)\). Dividing by the least eigenvalue gives the condition number asymptotic \((2m+2)(2m+1)B_m^3\alpha^{-2m}/2^{4m+1}\).

## Verification
The standalone `check.py` with `mpmath` constructs the Fourier Gram matrix by integrating each moment and compares high-precision eigenvalues with the coefficient for \(1\le m\le5\), alongside combinatorial and finite-width checks. The recorded output is `NARROW_TWO_ARC_CHECKS_PASS 112`. These finite tests supplement the proof; they do not establish exact finite-width optimality for \(m\ge2\). In the three-mode case the known exact benchmark is \(\lambda_1(\alpha,\pi/2)=(2\alpha-\sin 2\alpha)/\pi\), with leading term \(4\alpha^3/(3\pi)\), in agreement with the formula above.

## Relationship to prior work
*Antipodal maximin sampling and a quarter-circle tightness bifurcation for three Fourier modes* (2026-11-01) proves the unique exact antipodal maximin design for three modes, with that very value. Multiplication by \(e^{-it}\) identifies its modes \(0,1,2\) with \(-1,0,1\) without changing energies; common rotation and interchange of the two arcs identify the center ranges. Its all-width optimum is not claimed here as a new result.

*Exact spectral staircases for periodic narrow-arc Fourier observability* (2026-11-08) gives the full shrinking-arc spectrum for finite frequency sets and equally spaced centers. Two equally spaced centers and the consecutive frequency block recover the leading coefficient at the antipodal arrangement, not the arbitrary-angle formula or the optimization over all admissible moving centers. The shared date is not evidence of priority. The low-bandwidth moment-norm theory in *Fixed-index low-bandwidth spectra for arbitrary two-window Fourier concentration* (2026-10-25) treats another limiting regime. Marceca–Romero–Speckbacher give spectral counting bounds, Andén–Romero study multitaper estimation, and Batenkov–Diab bound clustered confluent Vandermonde singular-value orders; those statements do not supply the angle-dependent coefficient or its placement optimization here.

## Limitations
Bandwidth is fixed before width tends to zero. The theorem optimizes the smallest concentration eigenvalue for two equal continuously observed arcs, not the full condition number over placements, and makes no exact finite-width claim for \(m\ge2\). Comparison with older Slepian, approximation and confluent sampling work is provisional.
