# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the fair middle-third Cantor distribution, digit independence yields \(\lvert\widehat\mu(n)\rvert=\prod_{j\ge1}\lvert\cos(2\pi n/3^j)\rvert\) and integer-frequency scaling by three. A rigorous rational estimate gives \(C_3>1/3\). The proof identifies four residue classes modulo \(3^k\) that can retain prefix products larger than \(C_3\), and checks all three descendants of each such class. Classes outside the four have prefix product at most \(C_3\), and subsequently strict decrease gives strict inequality. The only integers surviving every level after removing factors of three are \(\pm1\). The two-character Gram eigenvalues \(1\pm\lvert\widehat\mu(u-v)\rvert\) prove both sharp bounds and equality. The checker verifies finite cases but does not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to accessible evidence

De Dios Pont–Liehr–Taylor, arXiv:2607.08656, proves *nonexistence of a frame for the entire space*, and supplies Fourier self-similarity; neither Theorem 1.1 nor the relevant Fourier-product and main-proof sections establishes the maximum over all nonzero integer coefficients or its complete equality classification. Zuberman, arXiv:2605.18471, classifies *orthogonal* exponentials and related maximal systems for Cantor measures, rather than exact conditioning for all nonorthogonal integer pairs. Related records on finite cyclic Fourier erasures, finite Cantor approximants and Cantor-set capacity do not contain or imply this sharp coefficient maximum. Existing infinite-product and scaling identities are acknowledged and not claimed as novel. Residual risk: an older Riesz-product/Fourier–Stieltjes extremum may be equivalent under different terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Two-character systems form the natural first nontrivial finite-dimensional stability question for a singular measure whose whole-space Fourier-frame problem has a negative answer. The exact optimal lower bound, spectral condition number, and all equality gaps quantify that contrast. The sharp constant and strict classification are not automatic from a Fourier-product identity or from absence of an infinite-dimensional frame.

## Closest literature and boundaries

The motivating source is J. de Dios Pont, L. Liehr and M. A. Taylor, *Cantor measures with odd base do not admit Fourier frames* (2026, arXiv:2607.08656). See also L. Zuberman, *On the spectra of Cantor measures* (2026, arXiv:2605.18471). The claim is restricted to distinct integer frequencies for the fair base-three law; it does not imply a complete Fourier frame, and it does not cover arbitrary real frequencies or larger frequency sets. Independent publication-priority verification remains outstanding.
