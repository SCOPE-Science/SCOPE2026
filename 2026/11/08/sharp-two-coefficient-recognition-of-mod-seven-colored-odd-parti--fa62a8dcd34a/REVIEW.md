# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The classification follows from a formal characteristic-seven power-series identity, invertible residue extraction, an explicit seven-by-seven exact coefficient certificate, and the previously proved five positive families. Both independent exact-integer algorithms agree for all 98 canonical coefficients. The proof treats all \(k\ge1\), all \(r\in\{0,\ldots,6\}\), and all \(n\ge0\). The first-sample witness \(a_3(6)\equiv0\), \(a_3(13)\equiv1\) verifies optimality of the two-coefficient rule.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Hirschhorn–Sellers, arXiv:2507.09752, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 4.1 **already give all five positive families and the entire color-shift principle**. They do not claim those five families are the only ones or that two coefficients recognize every congruence. Maity–Saikia–Talukdar, arXiv:2610.35292, concerns seven-power congruences for selected colors; Paksok–Saikia, DOI:10.5269/bspm.79605, gives other-modulus results. The claim's novelty is confined to the exhaustive converse and sharp universal decision test. Searches of published mathematical records found no statement giving that precise converse, although an equivalent earlier observation may exist. No absence-of-search claim is treated as proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The object is a natural infinite family containing ordinary partitions, overpartitions, and three-color reciprocal-crank partitions, with strong new higher-seven-power interest. Determining *which* residue classes work uniformly for *all* numbers of colors is the natural complement to a known one-sided classification. The theorem closes that exact converse for every pair, provides a uniform sharp finite test, and excludes two entire color classes. The shortness of the finite certificate does not depend on an arbitrarily selected parameter cutoff: seven is the intrinsic modulus and the two coefficients are proved sufficient and necessary.

## Limitations and priority

The original five congruences, their shifts, and the seven-color formal congruence are cited rather than claimed as newly found. The conclusion is only at modulus seven and step seven. Historical priority is not independently certified; the recently related two-parameter extension was available only at the abstract level, which leaves a residual literature risk.
