# Sharp two-point tangent defect for smooth plane cubics over \(\mathbb F_5\)

## Finding

Let \(C\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb F_5}\) be a geometrically smooth plane cubic, and let \(\mathcal T(C)\) denote the union of the \(\mathbb F_5\)-rational tangent lines to \(C\) at its \(\mathbb F_5\)-rational points. Then
\[
 |\mathcal T(C)|\leq29<31=|\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_5)|.
\]
Equality is attained, and up to \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_5)\) there are exactly two attaining curves:
\[
 C_8:\ X^3+XZ^2+2Y^2Z=0, \qquad
 C_{10}:\ X^3+XZ^2+Y^3+4YZ^2=0.
\]
They contain respectively \(8\) and \(10\) rational points. Each orbit comprises exactly \(93{,}000\) scalar-normalized cubic equations, so there are \(186{,}000\) extremal equations in total. Their projective stabilizers each have order \(4\). The two genus-one Jacobians are nonisogenous over \(\mathbb F_5\), as their zeta numerators are respectively \(1+2T+5T^2\) and \(1+4T+5T^2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The field throughout is exactly \(\mathbb F_5\). Smooth means geometrically smooth (not merely nonsingular at rational points), and equivalence is under projective linear coordinate changes defined over \(\mathbb F_5\). A tangent at a rational smooth point \(P=(p_0:p_1:p_2)\) of a homogeneous cubic \(F\) is the line with equation \(\sum_{i=0}^2 \partial_iF(P)X_i=0\). The union \(\mathcal T(C)\) is a subset of the \(31\) rational points of the ambient plane, not a union of geometric points over the algebraic closure. Cubic forms are counted up to multiplication by \(\mathbb F_5^\times\); distinct such forms have distinct projective plane zero schemes.

## Proof

The argument is an exhaustive finite classification with two independently implemented integer-arithmetic checkers. Every cubic in three variables has ten coefficients in the ordered monomial basis
\[
 X^3,\ X^2Y,\ X^2Z,\ XY^2,\ XYZ,\ XZ^2,\ Y^3,\ Y^2Z,\ YZ^2,\ Z^3.
\]
Normalize the first nonzero coefficient to \(1\), and enumerate the subsequent coefficients freely in \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). The total is
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{9}5^j=(5^{10}-1)/4=2{,}441{,}406.
\]
Enumerate the rational projective points uniquely as \((a:b:1)\), with \(a,b\in\mathbb F_5\); \((a:1:0)\), with \(a\in\mathbb F_5\); and \((1:0:0)\). For every form and each of these \(31\) points, evaluate \(F\) and its three partial derivatives modulo \(5\). Whenever \(F(P)=0\) and all partial derivatives vanish, exclude the form. This leaves \(1{,}895{,}000\) forms with no rational singular points, a *superset* of all geometrically smooth cubics.

For every remaining form, compute the set of rational points on each rational tangent using the exact congruence \(\nabla F(P)\cdot Q=0\), and take their set union as a \(31\)-bit integer mask. Complete enumeration yields the following precise coverage histogram, where the first coordinate is the number of covered rational points and the second is the number of scalar-normalized cubic equations:
\[
 (0,4000),\ (11,93000),\ (15,186000),\ (19,186000),
\]
\[
 (21,341000),\ (22,62000),\ (24,341000),\ (25,124000),
\]
\[
 (27,248000),\ (28,124000),\ (29,186000).
\]
In particular, no rationally nonsingular equation covers more than \(29\) rational points, proving the upper bound for all geometrically smooth cubics. Among the \(186{,}000\) equations at coverage \(29\), precisely \(93{,}000\) have \(8\) rational curve points and precisely \(93{,}000\) have \(10\) rational curve points.

For each displayed representative, apply the Jacobian smoothness criterion *over the algebraic closure*: in each of the three charts \(X=1\), \(Y=1\), \(Z=1\), the ideal generated by the dehomogenizations of \(F,F_X,F_Y,F_Z\) has reduced Gröbner basis \(\{1\}\) over \(\mathbb F_5\). Thus both representatives are geometrically smooth. Direct point-and-tangent evaluation gives \(|C_8(\mathbb F_5)|=8\), with precisely \((1:0:1)\) and \((4:0:1)\) uncovered, and \(|C_{10}(\mathbb F_5)|=10\), with precisely \((1:0:0)\) and \((0:1:0)\) uncovered.

Apply the projective change-of-variables generators \(X\leftrightarrow Y\), \(Y\leftrightarrow Z\), \(X\mapsto2X\), and \(X\mapsto X+Y\), reducing coefficients to their scalar-normalized form after each move. These generate \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_5)\), whose order is \((5^3-1)(5^3-5)(5^3-5^2)/(5-1)=372{,}000\). Breadth-first exact orbit enumeration yields \(93{,}000\) forms for each representative, and their orbits are disjoint because the rational point counts differ. Together the orbits exhaust the previously counted \(186{,}000\) extremal equations, completing the classification and yielding stabilizer order \(372{,}000/93{,}000=4\).

A smooth plane cubic is a genus-one curve with a rational point, hence its Jacobian has Frobenius trace \(6-\#C(\mathbb F_5)\). The point counts \(8\) and \(10\) give traces \(-2\) and \(-4\), hence the two stated Weil polynomials. Different Frobenius characteristic polynomials imply that the elliptic Jacobians are not isogenous over the base field.

## Verification

The self-contained C++17 program `verify_cubic_tangents_f5.cpp` enumerates all normalized forms, evaluates the rational tangency union, computes the exact coverage histogram, and enumerates the projective orbits. Reproduce using `c++ -O3 -std=c++17 verify_cubic_tangents_f5.cpp -o check_cubics && ./check_cubics`. Its success line is `CUBIC_F5_TANGENT_EXTREMAL_PASS 2441406 1895000 29 186000 93000 93000`.

Independently run `python3 verify_independent_f5.py` with NumPy and SymPy. That code separately constructs an array of all normalized equations, precomputes incidence bitmasks, independently counts their rational tangents, and verifies the representatives' geometric smoothness with Gröbner bases in all three affine charts. Its success lines begin `VECTOR_CUBIC_F5_CENSUS_PASS 2441406 1895000 29 186000 93000 93000` and `REP_SMOOTH_AND_COVER_PASS`. The C++ proof covers projective orbits and the Python proof covers smoothness; both independently recover the critical complete census.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli and Ghioca's *Tangent-filling plane curves over finite fields* (initial arXiv version dated February 26, 2023) introduced this tangent-filling question and obtained a large-characteristic nonexistence result. Their Theorem 1.1 requires \(p\ge4(d-1)^2(d-2)^2\), which becomes \(p\ge16\) for cubics; their Theorem 1.2 requires \(q\ge d^2(d-1)^6\), which becomes \(q\ge576\). Neither bound applies to the present field of five elements. Their explicit example theorem concerns \(q\ge11\). Their statements do not specify the sharp deficiency-two bound or the two exceptional projective classes over \(\mathbb F_5\).

The finite-geometry literature on elliptic curves and rich secants concerns a related but different incidence condition; a classification or older table equivalent to this exact tangent-union distribution could nonetheless exist outside the sources inspected.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to smooth cubics over \(\mathbb F_5\), with projective equivalence over that base field. The rationally nonsingular census includes geometrically singular equations; these are used only to prove an upper bound, while geometric smoothness of the two extremal orbits is proved separately. No assertion is made about higher degrees, other finite fields, incidence over extension fields, or elliptic Jacobians over algebraic closures. The exhaustive enumeration is a finite proof only because every normalized equation is enumerated and the exact finite-field operations and smoothness checks are fully provided. Historical priority is not independently certified.
