# Sharp two-round invisibility of commutation in finite equivalence frames

## Finding
Let \(E\) and \(F\) be two named equivalence relations on a nonempty finite universe. Their relational composites commute when \(E\circ F=F\circ E\). The **least first-order quantifier rank** of a sentence defining this property among all such structures is **three**. Moreover, any commuting frame and noncommuting frame satisfying exactly the same rank-at-most-two sentences have a combined universe of size at least \(10\); this is sharp. At combined size \(10\), exactly three distinct rank-two theories occur as common theories of such opposite-status pairs.

The ordered sizes and the corresponding distinct signatures are \((4,6):(120)\) and \((6,4):(56,120),(88,120)\). In these pairs the first size always refers to the commuting frame. The numerical signatures are canonical finite encodings defined below, not logical symbols.

## Assumptions and scope
All universes are **nonempty**; each of \(E,F\) is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. The first-order signature contains equality and these two binary predicates, no parameters, named elements, functions, counting quantifiers, or built-in orders. Quantifier rank is the largest nesting depth of ordinary first-order quantifiers, so a sentence of rank two may still use multiple two-quantifier subformulas. The relations are **named**; interchanging them is not part of isomorphism.

For a point \(x\), define \(m(x)\) as the bitmask of atomic two-point configurations realized by \(y\). For each \(y\), set \(t(x,y)=4[x\ne y]+2[E(x,y)]+[F(x,y)]\), where square brackets denote the indicator of a true condition. Then \(m(x)=\sum_{t\in T_x}2^t\) where \(T_x=\{t(x,y):y\in M\}\). Finally the rank-two signature is the increasing tuple of distinct values of \(m(x)\) as \(x\) varies. For equivalence relations, the self-pair contributes bit \(3\), and other points contribute bits \(4,5,6,7\).

## Proof
**1. Rank-two normal form.** In the two-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game on two relational structures, after a first matched point \(x\leftrightarrow x'\), Duplicator can respond to every possible second move in either structure exactly when \(T_x=T_{x'}\). Indeed, with two pebbled points a partial isomorphism is precisely the equality and the two relation truth values of the ordered pair; converse/symmetry add no further tests. At the first move, Duplicator therefore wins exactly when both structures realize the same set of masks \(m(x)\). By the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem, equality of the displayed tuples is equivalent to agreement on **all** sentences of quantifier rank at most two. This equivalence holds in arbitrary nonempty finite structures in the stated signature, not only those with commuting relations.

**2. Explicit four/six witness.** Take the four-point structure with \(E\)-class labels \((0,0,1,1)\) and \(F\)-class labels \((0,1,0,1)\). Its row–column incidence graph is the complete bipartite \(2\times2\) grid. Take the six-point structure with \(E\)-class labels \((0,0,1,1,2,2)\) and \(F\)-class labels \((0,1,0,2,1,2)\). Its incidence graph is a six-cycle; for instance \(E(0,1)\) and \(F(1,4)\) hold, while there is no \(z\) with \(F(0,z)\) and \(E(z,4)\). The first pair commutes and the second does not. In **both** frames, each point has one self-pair, a distinct \(E\)-only neighbor, a distinct \(F\)-only neighbor, and a point related by neither; there is no distinct common \(E,F\)-neighbor. Thus every pointed mask is \(2^3+2^4+2^5+2^6=120\). Step 1 makes the two frames rank-two equivalent.

**3. Exact rank three.** The sentence
\[\forall x\,\forall y\,\big((\exists z\,[E(x,z)\wedge F(z,y)])\leftrightarrow(\exists z\,[F(x,z)\wedge E(z,y)])\big)\]
asserts equality of the composites and has quantifier rank \(3\). Step 2 excludes any rank-at-most-two definition.

**4. Finite sharpness.** Every pair of equivalence relations on one or two points commutes. Moreover, if a noncommuting structure had the rank-two signature of a one- or two-point frame, then all pairs of distinct points would have a single uniform \((E,F)\) truth pattern; each relation would therefore be either equality or universal, and the relations would commute. For a noncommuting structure on exactly three points, the two nontrivial equivalences are distinct partitions into a pair and a singleton. Their shared central point has an \(E\)-only neighbor and an \(F\)-only neighbor but no point related by neither. No commuting pair of equivalences can realize that mask: where both types of neighbors exist, the complete rectangular-intersection property of commuting equivalences forces a point in the opposite cell, which is unrelated to the central point by either relation. Thus no three-point noncommuting frame shares a rank-two signature with *any* commuting frame.

It remains for a total of at most \(10\) to compare only sizes at most \(6\) on both sides, with noncommuting side at least \(4\). The complete finite enumeration in `checker.py` generates each set partition exactly once by restricted-growth words and, for each ordered pair, computes the two products independently by exhaustive existential evaluation. For all pairs with total size at most \(9\), **no** common rank-two signature occurs between commuting and noncommuting frames. For total exactly \(10\), the intersections of signature sets are exactly \((120)\) for commuting/noncommuting sizes \((4,6)\), empty for \((5,5)\), and \((56,120),(88,120)\) for \((6,4)\). Cases involving a side with at least seven points and total at most ten are analytically excluded by the one-, two-, and three-point arguments above. Because the program loops over all partitions of every carrier size through six and over every ordered pair of such partitions, these are **exhaustive finite certificates**, not sampled cases. Steps 1–3 are unrestricted proofs; Step 4 is a finite exhaustive search with a complete search-space justification.

## Verification
Run `python3 checker.py` using Python's standard library. The final output is `RANK_TWO_COMMUTATION_SHARP_OK partition_pairs 44168 optimal_signatures 3`. The program checks all \(1^2+2^2+5^2+15^2+52^2+203^2=44168\) ordered pairs of partitions on labeled carriers of sizes one through six. It explicitly checks the example by direct two-round game responses as well as mask equality. The proof of the finite cutoff requires the program's executable enumeration together with the analytic elimination of nondescribed larger-size pairs in Step 4; a log line in isolation would not certify a result. The exhaustive code is included directly and has no external data inputs.

## Relationship to prior work
The source by Ding and Yang (first posted March 27, 2026) studies expressivity and axiomatization of multi-agent bundled modalities, including an S5 case, not first-order quantifier-rank thresholds for commutation. Rumenova and Tinchev describe **exactly** the class of two commuting equivalence relations and its rectangular-matrix representation; their presentation also uses Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games and quantifier rank. These structural and logical tools are prior and are not claimed as new. Their inspected statements address representability, decidability, and finite model properties, rather than this exact *minimum opposing-status pair size* or the three optimal rank-two signatures. Pratt-Hartmann analyzes decidability/complexity of two-variable logic with equivalence predicates, not the stated quantitative depth-two separation. Ordinary EF correspondence and enumeration of set partitions are standard tools. The asserted contribution is the **sharp combined-size threshold and its complete three-signature boundary**, together with a tight rank comparison for this specific property.

## Limitations
The result concerns two *named* equivalence relations and **ordinary first-order sentence quantifier rank**; it is not a result about modal depth, propositional frame-validity rank, counting quantifiers, or arbitrary binary relations. The optimality includes only the total cardinality of two opposing-status frames, not separately minimal sizes subject to other constraints. Complete historical novelty is not established. The 2022 full journal article on the identical frame class was not accessible in complete form; its author slide presentation was inspected substantially. Specialist older work may contain an equivalent numerical cutoff. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
