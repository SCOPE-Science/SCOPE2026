# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
The unilateral shift's multiplier norm gives an exact scalar maximum. Positivity including the constant term classifies the complete peripheral set, excluding distant maximizers. The cumulant and raw-moment Taylor expansions control the logarithm uniformly through the order required for the \(O(n^{-2})\) remainder after optimization. Strict curvature of the limiting profile yields the correction \(k^2B\). Exact rational comparisons and an independent two-atom closed formula corroborate the constants; finite tests are not infinite proofs.

## Originality (O): PASS, qualified best-knowledge assessment
Arnold (arXiv:2606.14323) studies operator-norm decay rates under resolvent conditions, Dungey (doi:10.1112/plms/pdm058) gives a qualitative characterization of the order \(n^{-1/2}\), and Katznelson–Tzafriri (doi:10.1016/0022-1236(86)90101-1) gives a qualitative peripheral-spectrum criterion. Arnold Section 6.1 already computes the exact scalar optimum for the equal-weight Bernoulli multiplier \(P(z)=(1+z)/2\) while proving an operator estimate in another function-space norm; this particular binary formula is prior, not a standalone novelty. The inspected literature does not cover the arbitrary finite-support four-moment coefficient or its sign reversal. Searches of equivalent Fourier multiplier, convolution-power, cumulant and unilateral-polynomial-shift formulations produced no direct match. Residual risk: a more general published saddle-point or local-limit theorem may give the formula implicitly; selected sections of Arnold’s accessible HTML were inspected, but Dungey’s full text was inaccessible and a broader theorem may remain unrecognized. This is not independent priority confirmation.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
The operator-power defect \(\|T^n(I-T)^k\|\) is a central quantitative regularity observable. An exact second coefficient strengthens known order bounds, separates distributions sharing the Gaussian leading scale, and exposes a sign reversal even within three-point positive laws. The known equal-weight Bernoulli benchmark extends to a mass-independent two-atom coefficient, whereas a three-point-supported law has a positive correction; this is a motivated sharp boundary rather than an arbitrary finite sample.

## Limitations
The law is fixed, finitely supported, nonnegative, includes positive mass at zero, and is nondegenerate. The result does not determine uniform constants across laws, nor assert any Ritt-class equivalence. It has not been independently audited.
