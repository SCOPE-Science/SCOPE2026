## Correctness
**PASS.** The unit-density reduction to the semisimple quotient is exact. The Artin–Wedderburn matrix-block elimination leaves only products of finite fields; separable Wedderburn–Malcev splitting makes the radical a module over the field-product complement. The five possible semisimple field-degree profiles without degree-one factors are exhaustive, and the module-divisibility calculation leaves only degrees \((2,3)\) and a two-dimensional radical. Peirce-bimodule divisibility kills the mixed components, and the remaining one-dimensional twisted bimodule has only the identity and Frobenius actions. The former makes the ring commutative; the latter gives the unique extremizer. The supplied checker verified the concrete construction and numerical counts for three prime fields. A symbolic proof, not the checker, covers every prime.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The proportion of units (equivalently the number of nonunits) is an intrinsic finite-ring invariant. Dimension seven is the first odd prime-field dimension where a noncommutative algebra can have more than the local-ring unit proportion \(1-p^{-1}\), making it a motivated structural cutoff rather than an arbitrary table entry. The result supplies both a sharp extremal value and a unique algebraic model, revealing that two unequal residue-field degrees are forced by small radical-module dimensions. It quantifies a real boundary in the classification of unit-rich finite rings and can guide further dimension-dependent extremal questions without claiming them resolved.

The local square-zero Frobenius-twisted component is classical (Raghavendran 1969). The new claim is its forced appearance in a globally sharp seven-dimensional optimum.
