# Sharp universal gcds for the four depth-three branches of m-ary partition congruences

## Finding

Let \(m\ge2\) be an integer. Write \(b_m(N)\) for the number of unordered partitions of a nonnegative integer \(N\) into parts \(1,m,m^2,\ldots\). For \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
G_m(a,b)=\gcd_{n\ge1} b_m(m^4n-m-am^2-bm^3),\qquad
D_m=\begin{cases}4,&2\mid m,\\1,&2\nmid m.\end{cases}
\]
The gcd of an infinite sequence means its positive generator as an ideal of \(\mathbb Z\). **Theorem.** For all such \(m,a,b\),
\[
G_m(a,b)=\frac{m^3}{D_m}\begin{cases}
3,&m\equiv6\pmod9\ \text{and}\ a=b,\\
3,&m\equiv3\pmod9\ \text{and}\ a\ne b,\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Moreover, the first three arguments are sharp witnesses:
\[
G_m(a,b)=\gcd_{1\le n\le3} b_m(m^4n-m-am^2-bm^3).
\]
Thus the known depth-three congruence modulus is always exactly the full universal gcd, apart from a completely classified additional factor of three.

## Assumptions and scope

No primality, parity, or size condition on \(m\) beyond \(m\ge2\) is imposed. Each of the four branches \((a,b)\in\{0,1\}^2\) is included. The input integers in the count are nonnegative for every \(n\ge1\). This theorem concerns divisibility of **all** values on each branch; it does not give an explicit formula for each individual partition number nor higher-depth gcds.

## Proof

**1. Reduce an infinite sequence to a cubic rational generating numerator.** Work in integer formal power series. Let \(U_m(\sum_{j\ge0} c_jq^j)=\sum_{j\ge0} c_{mj}q^j\) and set
\[
K_1(q)=\frac{mq}{(1-q)^2},\quad
K_2(q)=U_m\!\left(\frac{q^a K_1(q)}{1-q}\right),\quad
K_3(q)=U_m\!\left(\frac{q^b K_2(q)}{1-q}\right).
\]
The standard generating-function decomposition of m-ary partitions yields
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}b_m(m^4n-m-am^2-bm^3)q^n
=K_3(q)T(q),\quad
T(q)=\prod_{j\ge0}(1-q^{m^j})^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, write the full partition series as \(F(q)=\prod_{j\ge0}(1-q^{m^j})^{-1}=(1-q)^{-1}F(q^m)\). Repeatedly use the identity \(U_m(f(q)g(q^m))=U_m(f(q))g(q)\) to select the coefficient indices \(m^4n-m-am^2-bm^3\); the three successive selections give exactly \(K_1,K_2,K_3\), leaving the tail \(T\). This is also the depth-three instance of the factorization in Rødseth--Sellers (2001), Lemma 6. Since \(T(0)=1\) and both \(T\) and \(T^{-1}\) have integer coefficients, multiplication by \(T\) preserves the ideal generated by all coefficients and also the ideal generated by the first three nonconstant coefficients. Consequently, the required infinite gcd and first-three gcd equal those of \(K_3\).

**2. Compute the cubic coefficient polynomial exactly.** Set \(k_i(n)=[q^n]K_i(q)\). Extracting coefficients from the above operators gives, for \(n\ge1\),
\[
k_2(n)=\frac m2(mn-a)(mn+1-a),\qquad
k_3(n)=\frac m2\sum_{j=1}^{mn-b}(mj-a)(mj+1-a).
\tag{2}
\]
The first formula follows because the coefficient of \(q^{mn}\) in \(mq^{1+a}(1-q)^{-3}\) is \(m\binom{mn+1-a}{2}\); the second is its partial sum after the next operator. Applying \(\sum_{j=1}^L j=L(L+1)/2\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^L j^2=L(L+1)(2L+1)/6\), and rewriting in the integer-valued binomial basis, produces
\[
k_3(n)=A\binom{n+2}{3}+B\binom{n+1}{2}+Cn,
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
A=m^6,\qquad
B=-\frac{m^4}{2}\big((m-1)(2m+1)+2a+2mb\big),
\]
\[
C=\frac{m^3}{12}\big(2m^3-3m^2-2m+3+6ma-6a+6m^2b-6b+12ab\big).
\tag{4}
\]
These are integers: \(A\) and \(B\) are visibly integral, while \(C=k_3(1)-A-B\) is integral by (2). The change of basis
\[
\binom{n+2}{3}=\binom n3+2\binom n2+\binom n1,
\qquad \binom{n+1}{2}=\binom n2+\binom n1
\]
shows the gcd of all \(k_3(n)\) is \(\gcd(A,B,C)\), because the binomial-basis coefficients are \(A,2A+B,A+B+C\) and integer combinations preserve their gcd. This gcd is already determined by \(k_3(1),k_3(2),k_3(3)\): the matrix from binomial-basis coefficients to the first three values is unit triangular. By (1), the first-three claim follows as well.

**3. Evaluate the gcd by exact valuations.** For branch \((a,b)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\), respectively, the parenthetical factor \(F=12C/m^3\) has these factorizations:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
(a,b)&F\\\hline
(0,0)&(m^2-1)(2m-3)\\
(0,1)&(m^2-1)(2m+3)\\
(1,0)&(m-1)(2m^2-m+3)\\
(1,1)&(m+1)(2m^2+m+3)
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(A=m^6\), no prime outside those dividing \(m\) can divide the gcd. Suppose \(p^e\Vert m\). For \(p\ge5\), the bracket in \(B\) is congruent to \(-1+2a\equiv\pm1\pmod p\), and the four values of \(F\) are \(\pm3\pmod p\). Thus \(v_p(C)=3e\) and the gcd has exponent \(3e\). For \(p=2\), the bracket in \(B\) and \(F\) are odd, giving
\[
v_2(A)=6e,\quad v_2(B)=4e-1,\quad v_2(C)=3e-2,
\]
so the gcd exponent is \(3e-2\).

Finally let \(p=3\) divide \(m\), with \(v_3(m)=e\). The bracket in \(B\) is \(\pm1\pmod3\), so \(v_3(B)=4e\). Each factor \(F\) is divisible by \(3\). If \(e\ge2\), (5) gives \(v_3(F)=1\), hence \(v_3(C)=3e\). If \(e=1\), put \(m=3u\), with \(3\nmid u\). Dividing the four expressions (5) by three and reducing modulo three shows
\[
3\mid F/3\iff
\begin{cases}
u\equiv2\pmod3,&a=b,\\
u\equiv1\pmod3,&a\ne b.
\end{cases}
\]
In this case the gcd exponent is \(4\) if the displayed condition holds and \(3\) otherwise, since \(v_3(A)=6\) and \(v_3(B)=4\). Equivalently, the extra factor of three occurs exactly in the two residue/branch cases stated in the theorem. Combining prime exponents completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` checks the symbolic \(A,B,C\) coefficient identities against a direct independent coin-change partition dynamic program. The recorded output in `verification_output.txt` includes 7,996 exact parameter-branch gcd checks for \(2\le m\le2000\), 68 base-branch trials and 204 exact coefficient comparisons for \(2\le m\le18\). Run `python3 verify.py` with the Python standard library. These finite calculations test formulas and boundary cases, but the infinite claim is established by the algebra and valuations above.

## Relationship to prior work

Rødseth and Sellers, *On m-ary Partition Function Congruences: A Fresh Look at a Past Problem* (2001), Theorem 1 proves the shared **lower** modulus \(m^3/D_m\) for all four branches and supplies the generating-function operator recurrence used here. It neither asserts universal sharpness nor identifies the branch-dependent factor of three. Sun and Zhang, *On the Enumeration and Congruences for m-ary Partitions* (2017), give digit-summation formulas but not this four-way all-\(n\) gcd. Żmija, *High order congruences for M-ary partitions* (first public preprint 7 March 2024), establishes high-order divisibility bounds for more general bases, but its bounds do not imply this sharp residue-dependent equality for the four shifted branches. The present result uses established identities to supply a new exact classification, not a new general divisibility theorem.

## Limitations

No conclusion is made about \(m\)-ary partition congruences beyond the displayed four branches at depth three, about exact values of individual partition numbers, or about primitive higher-depth gcds. The four-branch formula and the first-three-term reduction are the complete claim. Prior work outside the inspected literature could conceivably contain equivalent results; an independent external mathematical audit has not been performed.
