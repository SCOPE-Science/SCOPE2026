# Sharp weighted tetrahedral Cauchy surface-area quadrature

## Finding
For four fixed regular-tetrahedron view directions, the complete positive-weight profile of the worst surface-area error is explicit. In particular, equal weights are the **unique** minimax choice within this natural four-view family, and the sharp worst error is \(1-\sqrt{2/3}\).

Write \(v_1=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3\), \(v_2=(1,-1,-1)/\sqrt3\), \(v_3=(-1,1,-1)/\sqrt3\), and \(v_4=(-1,-1,1)/\sqrt3\). For a full-dimensional compact convex \(K\subset\mathbb R^3\), put \(S(K)=\mathcal H^2(\partial K)\) and \(b_K(v)=\operatorname{Area}(K\mid v^\perp)\). For positive \(w_i\) with \(\sum_{i=1}^4w_i=1\), define
\[
\widehat S_w(K)=4\sum_{i=1}^4w_i b_K(v_i).
\]
Let \(w_{(1)}\le w_{(2)}\le w_{(3)}\le w_{(4)}\) be the sorted weights and \(d(w)=\min_{\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}}\bigl|\sum_i\epsilon_iw_i\bigr|\). Then
\[
\boxed{m(w) S(K)\le\widehat S_w(K)\le M(w) S(K)}
\]
with exact constants
\[
\boxed{m(w)=2\sqrt{\frac23}(w_{(1)}+w_{(2)}),\qquad
M(w)=\frac4{\sqrt3}\sqrt{\sum_iw_i^2-\frac14d(w)^2}.}
\]
Both constants are globally sharp for the class of full-dimensional compact convex bodies. Consequently, the exact worst relative error equals \(E(w)=\max\{1-m(w),M(w)-1\}\); it is uniquely minimized at \(w_1=w_2=w_3=w_4=1/4\), where
\[
\boxed{E_*=1-\sqrt{2/3}\approx0.1835034191.}
\]
For equal weights, the lower ratio \(\sqrt{2/3}\) is attained by \(D=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:|x_i|+|x_j|\le1\text{ for }i<j\}\), and the upper ratio \(2/\sqrt3\) is attained by the cube \([-1,1]^3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The four view directions are fixed, up to a common orthogonal transformation and independent sign changes; the weights are arbitrary **strictly positive** real numbers summing to one. The body is an arbitrary full-dimensional compact Euclidean convex body, with no boundary differentiability required. The estimator uses ordinary Euclidean orthogonal-projection areas, not squared-projection or higher-moment kernels. The minimax assertion optimizes **weights for these tetrahedral directions**; it does not optimize the locations of four arbitrary directions.

## Proof
**1. Reduction to a directional support function.** The classical Cauchy projection identity for every full-dimensional convex body states
\[
b_K(v)=\frac12\int_{S^2}|\langle v,u\rangle|\,dS_K(u),
\]
where \(S_K\) is the surface-area measure of \(K\). Hence
\[
\frac{\widehat S_w(K)}{S(K)}=\int_{S^2}F_w(u)\,\frac{dS_K(u)}{S(K)},\qquad F_w(u)=2\sum_{i=1}^4w_i|\langle v_i,u\rangle|.
\]
It follows immediately that the minimum and maximum of \(F_w\) on the unit sphere give bounds valid for every \(K\). Conversely, given any unit \(u\), a rectangular slab of fixed square base and thickness \(t\downarrow0\), with its large faces normal to \(u\), has normalized surface-area measure converging to \((\delta_u+\delta_{-u})/2\). Thus its estimator ratio tends to \(F_w(u)\). The bounds obtained from the spherical extrema are therefore sharp over full-dimensional bodies, even when an extremum is not attained by one such body.

**2. Exact minimum from all zonotope facets.** The function \(F_w\) is the support function of the centrally symmetric full-dimensional zonotope
\[
Z_w=\sum_{i=1}^4[-2w_iv_i,2w_iv_i].
\]
The minimum of its support function over unit directions is the radius of its largest origin-centered inscribed ball. For a polytope, this radius equals the minimum distance to its facet supporting hyperplanes. Every triple of tetrahedral generators is linearly independent. Thus every facet is generated by exactly two of the segment directions, and every unordered generator pair supplies a facet, whose normal is perpendicular to both chosen directions. If \(u_{ij}\) is a unit normal perpendicular to \(v_i\) and \(v_j\), then each other tetrahedral direction has absolute scalar product \(\sqrt{2/3}\) with \(u_{ij}\). Therefore
\[
F_w(u_{ij})=2\sqrt{2/3}\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}w_k.
\]
Minimizing over the six pairs leaves the two smallest weights in this sum, establishing \(m(w)=2\sqrt{2/3}(w_{(1)}+w_{(2)})\). The facet argument proves global minimality; sampling six directions alone would not.

**3. Exact maximum by signed vector balancing.** For each unit \(u\), the sum of absolute scalar products equals the maximum of signed scalar products. Interchanging two finite maxima yields
\[
\max_{u\in S^2}F_w(u)=2\max_{\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}}\left\|\sum_i\epsilon_iw_iv_i\right\|_2.
\]
The tetrahedral Gram matrix satisfies \(\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=1\) for \(i=j\) and \(-1/3\) for \(i\ne j\). Consequently,
\[
\left\|\sum_i\epsilon_iw_iv_i\right\|_2^2=\frac43\sum_iw_i^2-\frac13\left(\sum_i\epsilon_iw_i\right)^2.
\]
Choosing the signs that minimize the absolute signed-weight discrepancy proves the stated exact formula for \(M(w)\). A maximizing direction is parallel to a maximizing signed vector; in particular the maximum is attained at the directional level.

**4. Sharp minimax weights.** For any positive weights sorted increasingly, \(w_{(1)}+w_{(2)}\le1/2\), with equality only when all four weights are \(1/4\). Thus
\[
E(w)\ge1-m(w)\ge1-\sqrt{2/3},
\]
and the second inequality is strict for every nonuniform weight vector. For equal weights, the signed discrepancy is zero and the two endpoints are \(m=\sqrt{2/3}\), \(M=2/\sqrt3\). Since \(2/\sqrt3-1<1-\sqrt{2/3}\), equality is obtained in the minimax bound precisely at the uniform weights. In fact the quantitative penalty satisfies
\[
E(w)\ge1-\sqrt{2/3}+2\sqrt{2/3}\left(\frac12-w_{(1)}-w_{(2)}\right).
\]

**5. Nondegenerate equality bodies in the uniform case.** For equal weights, the minimizer normals consist of the twelve signed coordinate permutations of \((1,1,0)/\sqrt2\), and the maximizer normals are \(\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\). One direct check is to order the absolute coordinates of \(u\) as \(a\ge b\ge c\ge0\) and calculate
\[
F_{(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)}(u)=\frac1{\sqrt3}\max\{a+b+c,2a\}.
\]
The lower equality occurs only when \((a,b,c)=(1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2,0)\), and the upper equality only when \((a,b,c)=(1,0,0)\). The rhombic dodecahedron \(D\) in the theorem has precisely these twelve minimizing facet normals; the cube has precisely the six maximizing facet normals. Their complete surface-area measures are supported on the corresponding equality directions, proving exact body-level attainment.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical Cauchy projection formula and the spherical surface-area-measure representation are established tools. Congpei An, *Exact Finite Integral Geometry: Directional Kernels and Spherical Designs* (arXiv:2609.05869v1, September 5, 2026), proves a universal potential-discrepancy norm identity (Theorem 3.2) and generic approximate Cauchy quadrature for spherical designs (Section 6). That norm identity gives an abstract characterization of the error for any fixed directional measure; it does **not** evaluate the weighted regular-tetrahedron four-node zonotope, its six-facet inradius, its signed-weight circumradius, or its unique optimal weight vector. The present exact finite formulas require the additional facet classification and signed-vector algebra. The source's exact quadratic-kernel rule is not the ordinary brightness rule used here.

The published record *Unique minimax three-view Cauchy cubature with quantitative frame rigidity* concerns **three**, rather than four, projection directions. A published *Sharp positive N-direction Cauchy quadrature and angular rigidity* concerns planar perimeter estimation and not spatial surface-area estimation. The published geometric radii calculation for vertex-transitive zonotopes is potentially relevant to the uniform-weight case, but does not itself determine the nonuniform tetrahedral weight profile. The corresponding record abstracts were inspected; their full proofs were not obtained, so historical priority remains provisional. No novelty is claimed for the generic Cauchy identity, Minkowski existence, or the standard zonotope facet construction.

## Limitations
Only the four fixed tetrahedral view axes are optimized in weight; the theorem does not prove global optimality over arbitrary four-direction placements. For unequal weights, endpoint ratios may be limiting slab values rather than values attained by full-dimensional bodies. It gives worst-case geometric errors, not sampling-noise or reconstruction estimates. Historical priority has not been independently certified, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
