# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The general star-support construction realizes every binary assignment by extending a mixed-rank antichain, with one distinct proper-subset blocker for each omitted star element; the all-positive label invokes Sperner width for the upper bound. Neither argument extrapolates finite checks. For the exact five-atom profile, the standalone enumerator lists every maximal clique of the Boolean incomparability graph, directly checks maximality, and enumerates every shattered support by the necessary-and-sufficient trace-splitting test. It finds 310 shattered six-point supports and none on seven points, with 11 explicit permutation orbits. The executable and representative table replay successfully.

## Originality
PASS within accessible evidence, subject to historical-priority risk. Griggs et al. (arXiv:2302.07062) study realizable *sizes of individual maximal antichains*, not VC shattering by the family of all maximal antichains. Ignatov (DOI 10.1134/S1995080223010158) and OEIS A326358/A348260 give *counts and symmetry classes of hypotheses*, whereas this claim counts shattered supports under all binary traces. Duffus--Sands--Winkler (1990) establish maximal-chain and maximal-antichain existence/avoidance, not supportwise all-trace realization. Balogh--Mészáros--Wagner (arXiv:1710.08509) studies different VC-bounded families and related rank matchings. Searches for equivalent formulations and exact six-support data found no dominating statement. Ignatov full text remains inaccessible; global priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Maximal Sperner families are natural extremal objects; measuring their binary membership concept complexity is distinct from counting them and from their Sperner width. The structural star/blocker construction yields a uniform exponential-scale lower bound, while the exact five-atom polynomial and complete eleven-orbit classification identify a first dimension larger than the underlying number of atoms. These nontrivial, reproducible support profiles form a benchmark for higher-dimensional shattering questions, without requiring a claim of a general exact formula.

## Limitations
The two general bounds differ by about a factor two. Exact support profiles are only proved for up to five atoms. The full paper by Ignatov was inaccessible, and alternate naming could hide prior coverage; available source comparisons therefore do not establish absolute priority.
