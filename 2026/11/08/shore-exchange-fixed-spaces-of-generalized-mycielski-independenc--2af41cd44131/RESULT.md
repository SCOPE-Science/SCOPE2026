# Shore-exchange fixed spaces of generalized Mycielski independence complexes

## Finding
For integers \(a\ge 2\) and \(\ell\ge0\), let \(G=K_{a,a}\) have labelled shores \(A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_a\}\) and \(B=\{B_1,\ldots,B_a\}\). Let \(\mu_\ell(G)\) be the generalized Mycielskian defined below, and let \(\tau\) exchange \(A_i\) with \(B_i\) at every level while fixing the apex. Put \(X_{a,\ell}=\operatorname{Ind}(\mu_\ell(G))\).

**Geometric fixed-space classification.** The fixed-point space \(|X_{a,\ell}|^{\langle\tau\rangle}\) is PL-homeomorphic to the independence complex of a level-blowup of the path \(P_{\ell+1}\). More precisely, the level-zero vertex is a single apex; each level \(1,\ldots,\ell\) contains \(a\) mutually nonadjacent vertices, all edges between consecutive levels are present, and no other edges are present. Its independence complex simplicially strongly collapses onto \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_{\ell+1})\). Thus, writing \(\ell=3k+r\) with \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\),
\[
|X_{a,\ell}|^{\langle\tau\rangle}\simeq
\begin{cases}
*, & r=0,\\
S^k, & r=1\text{ or }r=2.
\end{cases}
\]
The first line includes \(\ell=0\), when the fixed space is a point; the spheres for \(k=0\) are \(S^0\).

**Homology and quotient refinement.** With rational coefficients, the unique nonzero reduced homology of \(X_{a,\ell}\) is in degree \(2k\) with rank two when \(\ell=3k\), and in degree \(2k+1\) with rank one when \(\ell=3k+1\) or \(3k+2\). The involution has trace
\[
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\tau_*\mid\widetilde H_*(X_{a,\ell};\mathbb Q)\right)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\ell=3k,\\
(-1)^{k+1},&\ell=3k+1\text{ or }3k+2.
\end{cases}
\]
The trace is understood in the unique nonzero degree, not as an alternating sum. Consequently, \(\widetilde H_*(|X_{a,\ell}|/\langle\tau\rangle;\mathbb Q)\) has rank one in degree \(2k\) for \(\ell=3k\); for \(\ell=3k+1\) or \(3k+2\), it has rank one in degree \(2k+1\) exactly when \(k\) is odd, and otherwise vanishes. There is no other reduced rational quotient homology. The conclusions are independent of \(a\), despite the number of vertices growing linearly in \(a\).

## Assumptions and scope
For any finite graph \(H\), \(\operatorname{Ind}(H)\) is the abstract simplicial complex of independent vertex sets, including the empty face. The \(\ell\)-Mycielskian \(\mu_\ell(G)\) has vertices \(w\) and \((v,t)\), where \(v\in V(G)\) and \(1\le t\le \ell+1\). Its edges are \(\{w,(v,1)\}\) for all \(v\); \(\{(u,t),(v,t+1)\}\) for \(1\le t\le\ell\) whenever \(uv\in E(G)\); and \(\{(u,\ell+1),(v,\ell+1)\}\) whenever \(uv\in E(G)\). The involution is \(\tau(w)=w\), \(\tau(A_i,t)=(B_i,t)\), \(\tau(B_i,t)=(A_i,t)\). The fixed space means fixed points of the induced PL involution on geometric realization, not merely the realization of the fixed-vertex subcomplex. \(P_n\) is the graph path on \(n\) vertices. All assertions hold simultaneously for every integer \(a\ge2\) and \(\ell\ge0\); the base graph is complete bipartite and the level index convention is essential.

## Proof
**1. Geometric fixed points and admissible vertex orbits.** Let \(K\) be any finite simplicial complex with a simplicial involution \(\tau\). A fixed point has a unique barycentric coordinate expression on an underlying simplex, and its coefficients must be constant on each \(\tau\)-orbit of vertices. Thus its supporting simplex is a union of complete vertex orbits. The fixed space is PL-homeomorphic to the simplicial complex whose vertices are the vertex orbits that themselves form faces, with a set of such orbits forming a face exactly when their union is a face of \(K\). The homeomorphism sends an orbit vertex to the uniform barycenter of that orbit; its inverse reads the common orbit coefficients. This is an affine homeomorphism simplex-by-simplex, including intersections.

For \(K=X_{a,\ell}\), the apex orbit \(\{w\}\) is admissible. All other orbits are \(O_{i,t}=\{(A_i,t),(B_i,t)\}\) for \(1\le i\le a\) and \(1\le t\le\ell+1\). Every \(O_{i,\ell+1}\) contains a graph edge, so none is admissible. Every \(O_{i,t}\) with \(1\le t\le\ell\) is independent: at these levels the Mycielskian has no within-level edges. Two distinct admissible orbits at the same level are compatible; two orbits at consecutive levels are incompatible because the complete-bipartite base graph supplies cross-shore edges; all nonconsecutive positive-level orbits are compatible. The apex is incompatible precisely with the orbits at level one. This proves the asserted exact PL model as the independence complex of the level-blown-up path.

**2. Strong collapse and period three.** Within each positive level, the orbit-graph vertices have equal open neighborhoods and are pairwise nonadjacent. In the independence complex, if two such vertices are \(u\) and \(v\), any face containing \(v\) may be augmented by \(u\). Hence every maximal face containing \(v\) also contains \(u\), so \(v\) is a dominated vertex and can be removed by a simplicial strong collapse. Removing all but one orbit-graph vertex from each positive level leaves the ordinary path \(P_{\ell+1}\) with the apex as its first vertex.

For \(n\ge4\), deleting the second vertex of \(P_n\) leaves an isolated first vertex, so the deletion independence complex is contractible, and its link is \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_{n-3})\). The deletion-link mapping cone yields \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_n)\simeq\Sigma\operatorname{Ind}(P_{n-3})\). The initial cases \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) have independence complexes respectively contractible, \(S^0\), and \(S^0\). Induction gives the displayed fixed-space trichotomy with all boundary cases.

**3. Ambient homology.** The two shore simplices of \(\operatorname{Ind}(K_{a,a})\) are disjoint and individually contractible; hence this complex is homotopy equivalent to \(S^0\). Proposition 10 of Carnero Bravo (arXiv:2601.13432v2) gives the nonequivariant formula \(\operatorname{Ind}(\mu_{3k}(G))\simeq \operatorname{Ind}(G)^{*(2k+1)}\vee\Sigma\operatorname{Ind}(G)^{*2k}\), with the corresponding single join/suspension descriptions for residues one and two. Substituting \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\simeq S^0\) yields \(S^{2k}\vee S^{2k}\) at residue zero and \(S^{2k+1}\) at residues one and two. At \(k=0,\ell=0\), this means a homotopy type of three isolated points (two reduced degree-zero generators), verified directly from the apex and shore components. Therefore integral reduced homology is free in the stated unique degree, and the dimensions used below are established by prior nonequivariant work rather than claimed new.

**4. Lefschetz traces.** Barycentric subdivision makes a simplicial involution act without inversions: any invariant chain of nonempty faces has each face individually invariant because its dimensions are distinct. Consequently the fixed-point set is a subcomplex, and the simplicial Lefschetz trace formula gives \(\sum_j(-1)^j\operatorname{tr}(\tau_*\mid \widetilde H_j(X;\mathbb Q))=\widetilde\chi(|X|^{\langle\tau\rangle})\). The fixed-space classification gives reduced Euler characteristic zero at residue zero and \((-1)^k\) at residues one or two. Since the ambient homology occupies only degree \(2k\) or \(2k+1\), respectively, its trace is zero in the first case and \((-1)^{k+1}\) in the others. In the rank-two case, the two rational eigenspaces therefore each have dimension one.

**5. Quotient homology.** The barycentrically subdivided action has no inversions, so the rational cellular chains of the quotient are the coinvariants of its rational simplicial chains. Coinvariants by a finite group are exact over \(\mathbb Q\), and averaging identifies coinvariants with invariants. Thus reduced rational homology of the quotient is the \(+1\)-eigenspace of \(\tau_*\) on reduced rational homology of \(X_{a,\ell}\). The rank-two, trace-zero case contributes one invariant generator in degree \(2k\). In rank one, the generator is invariant precisely when \((-1)^{k+1}=+1\), equivalently \(k\) odd. This proves the complete quotient calculation, including \(\ell=0\).

## Verification
Run the standalone, standard-library-only `verify.py` with Python 3. It independently constructs each full generalized-Mycielskian graph from its edge definition, applies the shore-exchange map, reconstructs admissible fixed vertex orbits and all incompatibility edges, and tests 21 orbit-graph cases for \(a=1,2,3\) and \(0\le\ell\le6\). In 4,434 enumerated invariant faces it checks both independence and involution invariance. It computes mod-two reduced homology from full boundary matrices for 12 fixed complexes and checks 29 signed Lefschetz calculations, including direct full-graph invariant-simplex enumeration for eight smaller cases. The recorded replay ends with `ALL_VERIFY_OK graph_orbit_cases= 21 invariant_faces= 4434 GF2_cases= 12 signed_Lefschetz_cases= 29`. Tests at \(a=1\) check the harmless excluded base-graph boundary and do not broaden the stated theorem. These finite checks corroborate, but do not prove, the unrestricted PL and rational-homology statements; their proof is above.

## Relationship to prior work
Carnero Bravo's 2026 Theorem 5 and Proposition 10 describe the ordinary homotopy type of generalized-Mycielskian independence complexes and provide the ambient homology inputs. They do not supply a geometric fixed-point classification for the balanced-bipartite shore exchange, its three-periodic collapse, the involution trace, or the rational quotient ranks. The earlier work of Goyal, Shukla and Singh treats ordinary homotopy types for generalized Mycielskians of complete graphs, rather than this fixed-point problem. A publicly indexed result on symmetric-group homology of generalized Mycielskians also treats complete graphs with within-level label permutations, not balanced complete bipartite graphs with shore exchange. The present claim is precisely the equivariant fixed-space/trace/quotient refinement, **not** the existing ordinary sphere-wedge calculation. Semantic searches for these specific fixed-space and equivalent orbit-complex formulations found no implication that settles the claim. They do not prove uniqueness; equivalent unindexed work remains a risk.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the single shore-exchange involution of balanced complete bipartite base graphs, with a fixed paired labeling and the stated generalized-Mycielskian convention. It does not determine fixed spaces for arbitrary automorphism subgroups, a full equivariant homotopy decomposition of the ambient complex, or the integral homology of the orbit quotient (which may have torsion not seen rationally). The exact PL model and its strong collapse do not assert that the fixed space itself is PL-homeomorphic to a sphere. Publication priority has not been independently adjudicated.
