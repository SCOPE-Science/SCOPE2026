# Short-deletion necklaces of permutation powers: exact orbit counts and the first phase-wrap collision

## Finding

Let \(q,k\ge 2\). For a permutation \(P=c_0c_1\cdots c_{q-1}\) of \(q\) distinct symbols, put \(W=P^k\), viewed as a *circular word* of length \(qk\). For each integer \(t\) with \(0\le t\le q\), write \(B_{q,k}(t)\) for the number of **distinct circular output words up to rotation** obtained by deleting exactly \(t\) source positions and preserving the cyclic order of the remaining positions. Then

\[
B_{q,k}(t)=\frac1k\sum_{d\mid\gcd(k,t)}\varphi(d)\binom{qk/d}{t/d}-(q-1)\mathbf1_{\{t=q\}},
\]

where \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient function, \(\gcd(k,0)=k\), and \(\mathbf1_{\{t=q\}}\) is one if \(t=q\) and zero otherwise.

The exact structural statement is stronger than the count. Up to rotation through a whole source period, every deletion mask containing fewer than \(q\) positions produces a distinct output necklace. At exactly \(q\) deletions, precisely the \(q\) mask orbits consisting of a deleted block of \(q\) consecutive source positions all produce the same output necklace \([P^{k-1}]\). Every other output necklace still comes from exactly one mask orbit. Thus \(q\) is the first threshold for a failure of orbitwise injectivity, and the first correction to the Burnside mask-orbit count is exactly \(q-1\).

For instance, for \(q=3\), \(k=2\), and \(P=012\), the counts at deletion numbers \(t=0,1,2,3\) are respectively \(1,3,9,8\).

## Assumptions and scope

The symbols \(c_0,\ldots,c_{q-1}\) are **pairwise distinct**, and are kept as labeled symbols: letter relabelings and reversal are not allowed when identifying two output necklaces. The circular source consists of \(k\ge2\) copies of the same ordered permutation. A deletion mask is a subset of the \(qk\) source positions. The surviving letters are read around the original circle and treated as a rotation class; the origin of the reading is immaterial. The theorem covers exactly \(0\le t\le q\). The output is nonempty because \(qk-t\ge q\) under these hypotheses. The statement is not an assertion about arbitrary circular words or deletion numbers larger than \(q\).

## Proof

Identify positions with \(\mathbb Z/(qk)\mathbb Z\), with the letter at position \(j\) equal to \(c_{j\bmod q}\). Shifting a deletion mask by \(q\) positions preserves the source labels and rotates the cyclic output. Hence the cyclic group of order \(k\) generated by this shift acts on masks; its orbits each give a single output necklace.

**Gap reconstruction.** Fix any surviving output necklace \(V\) of length \(m=qk-t\), and choose a cyclic enumeration of its letters \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\). For a chosen embedding into the source, let \(g_i\ge0\) be the number of deleted source positions between the selected occurrences of \(v_i\) and \(v_{i+1}\), with cyclic subscripts. Necessarily

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_i=t,\qquad
 g_i\equiv \operatorname{index}(v_{i+1})-\operatorname{index}(v_i)-1\pmod q,
\]

where \(\operatorname{index}(c_j)=j\) and all indices on the right are modulo \(q\). The congruence follows because moving forward by \(g_i+1\) source positions advances the letter index by exactly that amount modulo \(q\).

If \(t<q\), every \(g_i\) lies in \(\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}\), so the congruence determines every gap uniquely from the labeled output necklace. A starting output occurrence of label \(c_j\) must embed at a source position congruent to \(j\pmod q\); any two such choices differ by shifting the entire deletion mask through a multiple of \(q\). Therefore the map from mask orbits to output necklaces is injective for \(t<q\).

If \(t=q\), each gap lies between zero and \(q\). The congruence still determines its value uniquely except when it equals \(0\) or \(q\), which have the same residue and are possible only between source-consecutive letter labels. If a gap of size \(q\) occurs, it consumes the entire deletion budget, and all other gaps are zero. The surviving circular word is then exactly \([P^{k-1}]\). Conversely, if the output is \([P^{k-1}]\), every successive pair of surviving letters follows the original cyclic permutation order. The congruences force each gap to be a nonnegative multiple of \(q\); since their sum is \(q\), precisely one gap equals \(q\), and the others vanish. Thus all embeddings of \([P^{k-1}]\) correspond to a single deleted circular block of \(q\) consecutive positions.

There are \(qk\) such deletion masks (one for each starting position), and rotation through \(q\) source positions partitions them into exactly \(q\) orbits, distinguished by the starting position modulo \(q\). All other output necklaces have all gaps uniquely reconstructed and hence have exactly one mask orbit. So, at \(t=q\), the output-necklace count equals the mask-orbit count minus \(q-1\).

**Burnside count.** A shift through \(h\) whole source periods has order \(d=k/\gcd(k,h)\) in the acting cyclic group. It partitions the \(qk\) source positions into \(qk/d\) cycles, each of length \(d\). A deletion mask of size \(t\) is fixed by this shift if and only if \(d\mid t\), in which case it is a union of exactly \(t/d\) of these position cycles. There are therefore \(\binom{qk/d}{t/d}\) fixed masks. Exactly \(\varphi(d)\) rotations have order \(d\), for each divisor \(d\) of \(k\). Burnside's lemma gives the number of mask orbits as

\[
\frac1k\sum_{d\mid\gcd(k,t)}\varphi(d)\binom{qk/d}{t/d}.
\]

Combining this with the reconstruction argument proves both the formula and the exact collision classification. Since \(q\ge2\), the correction at \(t=q\) is positive; since it is zero for every smaller \(t\), the threshold is sharp. \(\square\)

## Verification

The included standalone standard-library program `verify.py` independently enumerates all size-\(t\) deletion masks, constructs and canonically rotates every output, computes mask orbits under the period-\(q\) action, checks the divisor formula, and identifies every output fiber. The completed check covers every \(q\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}\), \(k\in\{2,3,4\}\), and \(0\le t\le q\): \(75\) parameter configurations and \(255038\) enumerated deletion masks. All comparisons passed. Execute `python verify.py` in a standard Python environment. The enumeration is corroboration rather than a substitute for the arbitrary-parameter proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Adamson, Deligkas, Gusev and Potapov [1] develop Burnside-type enumeration and algorithms for necklaces, including fixed-content necklaces. Their unrestricted or fixed-content necklace counts do not identify the rotation classes arising specifically as deletion descendants of one prescribed periodic source. The present gap-reconstruction lemma and its exact first-collision correction are additional restrictions not implied by that count alone.

Atashpendar and collaborators [2] investigate deletion embeddings and related counting questions for linear words, notably binary run-length-encoded inputs and supersequences. Their linear embedding counts do not count distinct circular deletion outputs up to rotation.

Luchinin, Puzynina and Rao [3] study reconstructing **binary cyclic sources** from their scattered cyclic subwords; their threshold theorem is a source-identification statement, not a cardinality formula for the descendant necklaces of permutation powers with arbitrary alphabet size.

Bianconi and Brugnoli [4] enumerate all necklaces with a specified number of transitions. That statistic alone does not impose the necessary labeled successor congruences for deletion descendants of a fixed \(P^k\). Only their abstract was accessible during the comparison; an implicit equivalent specialization elsewhere cannot be ruled out.

## Limitations

The theorem counts rotation-equivalence classes of distinct outputs, not deletion embeddings or linear strings. It requires a source that is an exact power of one permutation of pairwise distinct symbols. The range beyond \(t=q\) can have additional gap-wrap ambiguities and is not classified here. The proof establishes the formula for all declared parameters, while finite verification only tests the stated configurations. The novelty assessment compares available primary literature and published-result records; it is not a claim of exhaustive global novelty or independent validation.
