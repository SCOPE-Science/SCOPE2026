# Shortest cyclic binary reversal covers through order five

## Finding

A binary circular word is *reversal-complete of order* \(n\) when, for every \(u\in\{0,1\}^n\), at least one of \(u\) and its reversal \(u^{\mathrm{rev}}\) appears among its cyclic length-\(n\) windows. The following are exact for every \(1\le n\le5\), with circular words identified only under rotation.

| Order \(n\) | Reversal classes | Minimum circular length \(M_n\) | Shortest rotation classes \(N_n\) | Rotation/reversal/complement classes \(B_n\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 3 | 4 | 1 | 1 |
| 3 | 6 | 8 | 2 | 1 |
| 4 | 10 | 14 | 8 | 3 |
| 5 | 20 | 26 | 128 | 36 |

In particular, the first two orders beyond the order-three unobstructed regime have minimum cyclic lengths \(M_4=14\) and \(M_5=26\), with exactly \(8\) and \(128\) optimal necklaces. Every optimal word in the table has exactly one cyclic occurrence of \(0^n\). The attached `cycles_order4.txt` and `cycles_order5.txt` list every optimal necklace as its lexicographically least rotation.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is exactly \(\{0,1\}\); positions are indexed around one oriented cycle and consecutive windows wrap around the boundary. Reversal equivalence applies to each observed window, not automatically to the entire circular word. Thus reverse words, complements and reverse complements are distinct in \(N_n\) unless they are rotations. The final column makes the further quotient under reversal and global bit complementation. The minimum is over all nonempty circular binary words with no restriction on weight or primitivity.

## Proof

Define \(\mathcal R_n=\{\min_{\mathrm{lex}}(u,u^{\mathrm{rev}}):u\in\{0,1\}^n\}\). Reversal is an involution and fixes precisely the binary palindromes, so the number of required reversal classes is
\[
 m_n=\frac{2^n+2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}}2.
\]
A circular word of length \(L\) exposes exactly \(L\) indexed windows (possibly coincident), hence \(L\ge m_n\). Further, the word must contain the palindrome \(0^n\). Rotating to that window shows that all candidates of length \(L\ge n\) are covered by the \(2^{L-n}\) linear strings \(0^ns\), \(s\in\{0,1\}^{L-n}\). This root construction loses no cycles.

Here is an exact finite recurrence to evaluate the number \(T_n(L)\) of rooted strings that are reversal-complete. Index each member of \(\mathcal R_n\) by a distinct bit. A state is a class bitmask \(M\) together with a suffix \(q\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}\). Initially the bit for \([0^n]\) is set and \(q=0^{n-1}\), with count one. For each appended bit \(b\), form the next window \(e=qb\), set the bit for \([e]\), and replace \(q\) by the final \(n-1\) bits of \(e\). Add counts for identical states. After appending exactly \(L-n\) bits, append \(n-1\) *temporary* zero bits to each terminal state to account for precisely the missing wraparound windows. Summing state counts with all class bits set yields \(T_n(L)\). For \(n=1\), the suffix is empty and there are no temporary bits. Induction on the appended suffix length proves that this recurrence counts every rooted word once and no other objects.

The exact recurrence gives the following lower-bound certificate and attainment values:

| \(n\) | Tested lengths \(L\) with \(m_n\le L<M_n\) | \(T_n(L)\) there | \(T_n(M_n)\) |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | none | — | 1 |
| 2 | 3 | 0 | 1 |
| 3 | 6–7 | 0 | 2 |
| 4 | 10–13 | 0 | 8 |
| 5 | 20–25 | 0 | 128 |

Consequently the listed \(M_n\) are the actual minima. An entirely separate enumeration of each rooted binary string at the relevant lengths constructs its complete set of cyclic windows, checks reversal coverage and reduces surviving strings to their lexicographically least rotations. This gives exactly \(1,1,2,8,128\) distinct rotation classes. In each case the canonical and rooted counts coincide. Since every optimal cycle admits at least one root at an occurrence of \(0^n\), the equality also proves that every optimal cycle contains this factor exactly once. Finally, applying the four symmetries generated by reversal and global complement before canonical rotation gives \(1,1,1,3,36\) classes, as listed. This completes the finite exhaustive proof for all claimed orders.

## Relationship to prior work

Burris, Motta and Shipman introduced unoriented de Bruijn sequences and gave an Eulerian-trail framework for shortest *linear* reversal-covering words, including concrete order-four and order-five constructions and a parity obstruction to a one-visit traversal. This supplies the relevant objects and examples, but those results do not determine the minimum **cyclic** lengths, the number of shortest cycles modulo rotation or their full symmetry quotient. Fici, Kociumaka, Radoszewski, Rytter and Waleń studied the general shortest common superstring problem with reversals for linear outputs and established approximation and complexity results, not these cyclic exact counts. The contribution here is the complete shortest-cycle census for the first five binary orders, including both first obstructed orders. No claim is made to invent the underlying de Bruijn graph or reversal-covering problem.

## Limitations

These exact values are proved only for \(1\le n\le5\); no formula for higher orders, arbitrary alphabets or reverse-complement equivalence is asserted. The proof uses exhaustive finite computation with fully specified algorithms rather than a non-computational closed-form evaluation of the five counts. Although relevant published sources and record searches were checked, unindexed prior enumeration or tables remain a historical-priority risk. Independent mathematical and historical-priority assessment has not been performed.
