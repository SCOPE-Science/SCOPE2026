# Signed rank-one cone angles: sharp collapse rates and balanced sign extremizers

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 2\), every unit vector \(u=(u_1,\dots,u_n)\in\mathbb R^n\) with **all coordinates nonzero**, and every \(0<\varepsilon<1\), let \(C_{\varepsilon,u}\) be any full-dimensional simplicial cone whose generator Gram matrix is
\[
G_{\varepsilon,u}=I_n-(1-\varepsilon)uu^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Its normalized solid angle is \(\alpha_{\varepsilon,u}=\mathbb P(N\in C_{\varepsilon,u})\), with \(N\) standard Gaussian in \(\mathbb R^n\). Such a cone exists and is unique up to Euclidean isometry.

**Sharp singular dichotomy.** Let \(g_1,\ldots,g_n\) be independent standard normal random variables. If \(u\) has both positive and negative entries, put
\[
L=\max_{i:u_i>0}(-g_i/u_i),\qquad
U=\min_{j:u_j<0}(-g_j/u_j).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\alpha_{\varepsilon,u}
=\frac{\sqrt\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\mathbb E[(U-L)_+]
+O_u(\varepsilon^{3/2}).}
\]
The leading constant is finite and strictly positive. If all coordinates of \(u\) have the same sign, instead put \(H=\max_i(-g_i/|u_i|)\); then
\[
\boxed{\alpha_{\varepsilon,u}
=\frac12-\frac{\sqrt\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\mathbb E[H]
+O_u(\varepsilon^{3/2}).}
\]
Here \(\mathbb E[H]>0\) for \(n\ge2\). Thus the singular limit is zero, with exactly square-root decay, for mixed signs, and is one half for unanimous signs. The error bounds are for fixed \(n,u\); no uniformity near vanishing coordinates is claimed.

**Sharp sign-partition ordering at fixed spectrum.** When \(|u_i|=n^{-1/2}\), denote the angle by \(A_{n,p}(\varepsilon)\) if exactly \(p\) entries are positive. For every \(0<\varepsilon<1\),
\[
A_{n,p}(\varepsilon)=A_{n,n-p}(\varepsilon),\qquad
A_{n,0}(\varepsilon)>A_{n,1}(\varepsilon)>\cdots>A_{n,\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(\varepsilon).
\]
In particular, among these cones with the *same* generator norms and Gram eigenvalues \(\varepsilon,1,\ldots,1\), all-unanimous sign patterns uniquely maximize the angle, while the most balanced sign patterns minimize it (both up to swapping all signs).

For \(1\le p\le n-1\), if \(z_{(1)}<\cdots<z_{(n)}\) denote the order statistics of \(n\) independent standard normals, then the leading constant has the exact intrinsic spacing form
\[
\boxed{
A_{n,p}(\varepsilon)
=\frac{\sqrt{n\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\binom np}\,
\mathbb E\big[z_{(p+1)}-z_{(p)}\big]
+O_n(\varepsilon^{3/2}).}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The cone is always full-dimensional for \(\varepsilon>0\), because \(G_{\varepsilon,u}\) is positive definite. The statement concerns the **angle of the cone generated by Gram vectors**, not the angle of the polar cone. All coordinates of \(u\) are fixed and nonzero. All random variables in the proofs are independent standard normals unless otherwise stated. The notation \((x)_+=\max\{x,0\}\) is used throughout. Every angle is normalized by the full sphere; the angle of an orthant is \(2^{-n}\).

The classification covers the approaching singular endpoint \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). At \(\varepsilon=1\) all cones become orthants and the strict ordering becomes equality, so that endpoint is deliberately excluded from the strict claim. The Gram eigenvalues and the coordinate diagonal entries are identical for all sign patterns with equal magnitudes, allowing a genuine equal-spectral extremal comparison.

## Proof

**1. Gaussian representation and the exact interval identity.** For a nonsingular generator matrix \(V\) with \(V^{\mathsf T}V=G_{\varepsilon,u}\), the event \(N\in V\mathbb R_+^n\) is equivalent to \(V^{-1}N\in\mathbb R_+^n\). Its Gaussian covariance equals \(G_{\varepsilon,u}^{-1}\). The rank-one inverse identity is
\[
G_{\varepsilon,u}^{-1}=I_n+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\,uu^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Let \(T\) be one further independent standard normal. The coefficient vector therefore has exactly the distribution
\[
(g_1+s u_1T,\ldots,g_n+s u_nT),\qquad
s=\sqrt{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}.
\]
Writing \(R=sT\) and \(\delta=1/s=\sqrt{\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)}\), we have \(\mathbb P(R\le x)=\Phi(\delta x)\), where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function.

If the entries of \(u\) have both signs, the inequalities \(g_i+u_iR>0\) are exactly \(L<R<U\). Conditioning on the \(g_i\) yields the exact identity
\[
\alpha_{\varepsilon,u}
=\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf1_{\{L<U\}}\big(\Phi(\delta U)-\Phi(\delta L)\big)\right].\tag{1}
\]
For unanimous signs, replacing \(u\) by \(-u\) if necessary does not alter the Gram matrix, and the inequalities become \(R>H\). Thus
\[
\alpha_{\varepsilon,u}=\mathbb E[1-\Phi(\delta H)].\tag{2}
\]

**2. Uniform cubic Taylor bound and sharp rates.** For every real \(x\),
\[
\left|\Phi(x)-\frac12-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right|
\le\frac{|x|^3}{6\sqrt{2\pi}},\tag{3}
\]
since \(|e^{-t^2/2}-1|\le t^2/2\) inside the integral defining \(\Phi(x)-\Phi(0)\). As maxima and minima of finitely many nondegenerate Gaussian variables, \(L,U,H\) all have finite third moments. Apply (3) in (1) or (2) and use
\[
\delta=\sqrt\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{3/2}),
\qquad\delta^3=O(\varepsilon^{3/2}).
\]
This proves both displayed asymptotic formulas with the claimed errors. In the mixed case \(\mathbb P(L<U)>0\) by positive Gaussian density on a suitable open rectangle, and consequently \(\mathbb E[(U-L)_+]>0\). In the unanimous case, \(\mathbb E[H]>0\) because the maximum of at least two independent centered nondegenerate Gaussians strictly exceeds either summand in expectation.

**3. Ordering of equal-magnitude sign patterns.** Take \(|u_i|=1/\sqrt n\), with \(p\) plus signs and \(n-p\) minus signs. Conditioning on \(T\) in step 1 gives the exact positive-real integral
\[
A_{n,p}(\varepsilon)=
\mathbb E\!\left[\Phi(aT)^p\Phi(-aT)^{n-p}\right],
\qquad a=\sqrt{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{n\varepsilon}}>0.\tag{4}
\]
For real \(p\in[0,n]\), define \(F(p)\) as the logarithm of the right-hand side of (4). By the **strict** Hölder inequality, \(F\) is strictly convex: equality would force \(\Phi(aT)/\Phi(-aT)\) to be almost surely constant, whereas this ratio is strictly increasing with the nondegenerate \(T\). Replacing \(T\) by \(-T\) shows \(F(p)=F(n-p)\). A strictly convex function symmetric about \(n/2\) strictly decreases on \([0,n/2]\) and increases on \([n/2,n]\). The complete strict ordering and the symmetry follow. All these Gram matrices have eigenvalues \(\varepsilon,1,\ldots,1\) and diagonal entries \(1-(1-\varepsilon)/n\), proving the stated equal-spectral extremality.

**4. Gaussian spacing identity.** In the equal-magnitude mixed case substitute \(t=\sqrt n\,x\) in the integral for the constant from step 2 to obtain
\[
\mathbb E[(U-L)_+]=\sqrt n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\Phi(x)^p\Phi(-x)^{n-p}\,dx.\tag{5}
\]
Now label \(p\) among \(n\) independent normals as group \(X\), and the other \(n-p\) as group \(Y\). For each sample, the set of \(x\) satisfying \(\max X<x<\min Y\) is an interval of length \((\min Y-\max X)_+\). By Tonelli's theorem its expected length is the integral in (5) without \(\sqrt n\). Conditional on the ordered sample values, the labels are uniformly distributed among \(\binom np\) allocations. Exactly one allocation puts the \(p\) labels on the smallest \(p\) observations. In that case the interval length equals \(z_{(p+1)}-z_{(p)}\), while every other allocation contributes zero. Hence
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(x)^p\Phi(-x)^{n-p}\,dx
=\frac{\mathbb E[z_{(p+1)}-z_{(p)}]}{\binom np}.
\]
Substitution into the mixed asymptotic finishes the proof.

## Verification

A self-contained numerical checker in `verify_angles.py` evaluates the exact one-dimensional Gaussian integral of equation (4), independently checks the spectral normalization, tests all finite sign-pattern orders in dimensions \(2\) through \(9\), and compares small-\(\varepsilon\) values against the predicted \(\sqrt\varepsilon\)-rate for unequal and equal coordinate magnitudes. It also checks the two exact planar angle formulas for several \(\varepsilon\) values. This executable test produces 724 successful checks with the cited dependencies `numpy` and `scipy`.

The all-dimensional theorem is proved analytically by steps 1–4. The finite checks do **not** constitute an exhaustive or independent proof. As a normalization sanity check, the mixed-sign planar leading coefficient is exactly \(1/\pi\), matching the expansion of the explicit planar cone angle \((2\pi)^{-1}\arccos((1-\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon))\).

## Relationship to prior work

Philipp Schange, *Solid-angle formulas for simplicial cones and random shifted sections of polytopes* (arXiv:2610.28084v1, publicly submitted 2026-10-20), establishes a general low-rank Gram-matrix integral for cone angles. His rank-one specialization supplies the starting orthant-probability representation, but the inspected treatment does not classify the signed Gram orbits by angle, establish the singular \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) laws for arbitrary sign-mixed vectors, or identify their constants with adjacent normal order-statistic spacings.

Kabluchko and Zaporozhets, *Absorption probabilities for Gaussian polytopes and regular spherical simplices* (2020), study the equal-sign, equicorrelated generalized orthant and its spherical angle; the signed partition family with a sharp singular constant is different. Their earlier *Expected volumes of Gaussian polytopes, external angles, and multiple order statistics* (arXiv:1706.08092v1) connects order statistics to intrinsic volumes; its results do not state the mixed-sign rank-one cone-angle limit or the strict equal-spectrum sign ordering. The spacing formula here is proved directly, not inferred from an unrelated limit theorem.

## Limitations

The claim is confined to negative rank-one Gram perturbations and a fixed nonzero vector \(u\). No rate is asserted uniformly as any \(u_i\) approaches zero, and the sign-ordering comparison requires equal absolute coordinate magnitudes. The singular matrix at \(\varepsilon=0\) does **not** define a full-dimensional simplicial cone; all limits are taken through positive-definite Gram matrices. The proof does not classify arbitrary higher-rank degenerations or positive rank-one perturbations. Literature priority remains provisional pending independent expert review; no independent mathematical audit is represented.
