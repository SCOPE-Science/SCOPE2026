# Single-commutator theorem for split odd-order core-quandle algebras

## Finding

Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group of odd order \(N\), and let \(K\) be a field containing a primitive root of unity of order \(\operatorname{exp}(A)\). Form the generally nonassociative core-quandle algebra \(B=K[\operatorname{Core}(A)]\), with basis \(e_x\) indexed by \(x\in A\) and multiplication \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\). Write \([u,v]=uv-vu\), and let \(B'\) denote the subalgebra generated by all commutators.

**Theorem.** There is an orthogonal direct sum of two-sided ideals
\[
B=B'\oplus C,\qquad B'C=CB'=0,\qquad \dim_K C=|A[3]|,\qquad \dim_K B'=|A|-|A[3]|,
\]
where \(C\) is commutative and \(A[3]=\{x\in A:3x=0\}\). Every element of \(B'\) is **one** commutator \([u,v]\). Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{cw}(B)=
\begin{cases}0,&3A=0,\\1,&3A\ne0.\end{cases}
\]
Here commutator width is zero for the zero commutator subalgebra and otherwise the largest minimum number of commutators needed to express an element of \(B'\).

In particular, for the dihedral quandle \(R_n=\operatorname{Core}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)\) with odd \(n\ge5\), and any field containing a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity, one has
\[
\operatorname{cw}(K[R_n])=1,\qquad
\dim_K K[R_n]'=n-\gcd(n,3).
\]
This is a uniform, unbounded-order answer to the split-coefficient case of the dihedral-quandle commutator-width problem.

## Assumptions and scope

The additive group \(A\) is finite, abelian, and of odd order, including the trivial group. The coefficient ring \(K\) is a field containing a primitive root of unity of order equal to the exponent of \(A\). Such a field necessarily has characteristic coprime to \(N\), so \(N\) is invertible and the characters of \(A\) form a complete \(K\)-basis. No algebraic closure or assumption that the characteristic is odd is needed. This result does **not** assert the width-one conclusion for arbitrary nonsplitting fields or coefficient characteristics dividing \(N\).

The notation \(B'\) means the **subalgebra** generated by commutators, not merely their linear span; both are proved equal below. Multiplication is the original, nonassociative quandle-algebra product, with no associativity assumed.

## Proof

**1. Fourier multiplication.** Let \(\widehat A=\operatorname{Hom}(A,K^\times)\). For \(\chi\in\widehat A\) define
\[
F_\chi=\sum_{x\in A}\chi(x)e_x.
\]
The \(N\) vectors \(F_\chi\) form a basis by character orthogonality, valid since \(N\) is invertible in \(K\). Direct substitution into the multiplication law, with \(z=2y-x\), gives
\[
F_\chi F_\psi
=\sum_{y,z\in A}\chi(2y-z)\psi(y)e_z
=N\,\mathbf 1_{\{\psi=\chi^{-2}\}}F_{\chi^{-1}}.
\tag{1}
\]
This holds in every permitted characteristic, including characteristic two.

**2. Orthogonal ideal decomposition.** Write \(T\chi=\chi^{-2}\). Because \(|A|\) is odd, \(T\) permutes \(\widehat A\). It fixes exactly the characters satisfying \(\chi^3=1\). Moreover, \(T^2\chi=\chi\) implies \(\chi^3=1\), which also implies \(T\chi=\chi\); hence every nontrivial \(T\)-orbit has length **at least three**.

Define \(C=\operatorname{span}_K\{F_\chi:\chi^3=1\}\) and \(D=\operatorname{span}_K\{F_\chi:\chi^3\ne1\}\). Equation (1) shows that both are subalgebras: whenever a product is nonzero, its output is indexed by \(\chi^{-1}\), which has the same order-three status as \(\chi\). If a character \(\chi\) is fixed by \(T\) and \(\psi\) is not, then \(F_\chi F_\psi=0\) since \(T\chi=\chi\ne\psi\), and \(F_\psi F_\chi=0\) since \(T\psi=\chi\) would imply \(\psi=\chi\). Thus \(CD=DC=0\), and both are two-sided ideals. Products within \(C\) are commutative: different Fourier basis vectors multiply to zero, while \(F_\chi^2=N F_{\chi^{-1}}\).

All commutators lie in \(D\). Conversely, for \(\chi^3\ne1\), the two products in
\[
[F_\chi,F_{T\chi}]
=N F_{\chi^{-1}}-N\mathbf 1_{\{T^2\chi=\chi\}}F_{(T\chi)^{-1}}
=N F_{\chi^{-1}}
\tag{2}
\]
show that every basis vector of \(D\) belongs to the **linear span** of commutators. Since \(D\) is a subalgebra, the commutator subalgebra is exactly \(B'=D\). Finite abelian duality gives \(|\{\chi:\chi^3=1\}|=|A[3]|\), yielding the dimensions.

**3. Adjacent-minor surjectivity on cycles.** We prove the elementary lemma: for any field \(K\), integer \(r\ge3\), and arbitrary \((w_0,\ldots,w_{r-1})\in K^r\), there exist vectors \(z_i=(a_i,b_i)\in K^2\), with cyclic indices, satisfying
\[
\det(z_i,z_{i+1})=a_i b_{i+1}-b_i a_{i+1}=w_i
\quad(0\le i<r).
\tag{3}
\]
For \(r=3\), the all-zero target is realized by zero vectors. Otherwise rotate the indices so that \(w_0\ne0\) and take
\[
z_0=(1,0),\qquad z_1=(0,w_0),\qquad
z_2=(-w_1/w_0,-w_2).
\]
For \(r>3\), inductively construct a cycle of length \(r-1\) whose edge weights, in order, are \((1,w_2,\ldots,w_{r-1})\), with vertices \((z_0,z_2,\ldots,z_{r-1})\). Then \(\det(z_0,z_2)=1\). Insert
\[
z_1=w_1z_0+w_0z_2.
\]
The two new edge determinants are \(w_0,w_1\); the remaining ones are unchanged. This completes induction in every characteristic, without root extraction or a genericity assumption.

**4. Every derived element is one commutator.** Let \(d=\sum_{\eta^3\ne1}c_\eta F_\eta\in D\). Partition the nonfixed characters into orbits of \(T\). For an orbit \((\chi_0,\ldots,\chi_{r-1})\), where \(T\chi_i=\chi_{i+1}\) cyclically and \(r\ge3\), prescribe
\[
w_i=N^{-1}c_{\chi_i^{-1}}.
\]
Apply the lemma to find pairs \((a_i,b_i)\) with adjacent determinants \(w_i\). Doing this independently on all orbits, set
\[
u=\sum a_iF_{\chi_i},\qquad v=\sum b_iF_{\chi_i}
\]
(the sums run over every nonfixed orbit). Equation (1) gives exactly
\[
[u,v]=N\sum_{\chi_i^3\ne1}(a_i b_{i+1}-b_i a_{i+1})F_{\chi_i^{-1}}=d.
\]
There are no contributions from different orbits because the only nonzero Fourier products are between \(\chi\) and \(T\chi\). This constructs one commutator for every \(d\in B'\).

Finally \(B'=0\) precisely when every character has order dividing three, equivalently \(3A=0\). Otherwise \(B'\ne0\), so its width cannot be zero and must equal one. For \(A=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), \(|A[3]|=\gcd(n,3)\), proving the stated specialization. \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone script `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. Run `python3 verify.py` in the same directory. It verifies (a) the adjacent-minor lemma by exhaustive finite-field enumeration for multiple cycle lengths, (b) the Fourier product rule by direct multiplication of quandle-basis vectors, (c) the predicted dimension by modular Gaussian elimination on all elementary commutators, and (d) constructive one-commutator witnesses against direct multiplication for 80 specified sample targets. Test groups include trivial, cyclic, and noncyclic groups of orders up to 27 over prime fields of suitable splitting type. Its recorded output is in `verification_output.txt` and ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

These tests corroborate the identities in finite cases; the universal assertion for all odd-order finite abelian groups and all splitting fields follows from the symbolic proof, not enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Bardakov, Passi and Singh (2022), Section 7, introduced commutator width for quandle rings and explicitly posed the dihedral-quandle classification problem (Problem 7.5). Their Theorem 7.2 supplies a general dimension-dependent upper bound for strongly noncommutative and two-transitive quandles, not an exact, all-order width-one theorem. Sangare and Ta (2026), Section 5, give finite-field computations of widths for small dihedral quandles and the characteristic-five exception for \(R_5\); they do not establish the present theorem in all odd orders. Elhamdadi, Ta and Virgin (2026) develop Fourier analysis of Alexander and Takasaki quandle algebras for **idempotent** questions; Fourier methods and the relevant character-product identity are antecedents, not a novelty claim here. The new contribution is the exact universal commutator-surjectivity theorem and orthogonal ideal decomposition for all odd finite abelian cores over splitting fields. No claim of novelty is made for the quandle-algebra definition, finite Fourier transform, or the published computations.

## Limitations

The width-one conclusion assumes the coefficient field splits the characters of \(A\). It is not asserted for arbitrary fields, fields whose characteristics divide \(|A|\), or finite abelian groups of even order. In particular, Sangare and Ta report that \(\mathbb F_5[R_5]\) has elements of commutator length two; this does not contradict the theorem, because characteristic five divides \(|R_5|\), preventing the Fourier basis used here. Historical priority of an equivalent commutator-surjectivity argument under different terminology remains a residual risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
