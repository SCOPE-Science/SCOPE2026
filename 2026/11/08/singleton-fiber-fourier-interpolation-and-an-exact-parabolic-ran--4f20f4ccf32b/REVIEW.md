# Scientific review: Singleton-fiber Fourier interpolation and an exact parabolic rank transition

## Correctness — PASS
The statement uses the unnormalized prime-field Fourier convention and distinguishes rank in characteristic zero from rank modulo auxiliary primes. The orthogonal decomposition into the point mass, the unit-constant vector, and all nontrivial multiplicative characters is complete; each nontrivial character's singular magnitude is exactly its twisted phase sum. Cyclotomic irreducibility of the prime-th root of unity over the \(p-1\)-th cyclotomic field forces each coefficient fiber to vanish if such a twisted sum vanishes. A singleton nonzero-domain fiber contradicts this, and the constant two-by-two block is also nonsingular. For the purely quadratic coupling, the \((p+1)/2\)-by-\((p+1)/2\) square submatrix is a minor of the prime Fourier matrix, so Chebotarev nonvanishing proves the sharp lower rank bound; the sign symmetry proves the matching upper bound. Together these prove every branch and the full squared-singular-value list. The packaged `check_parabola.py` confirms 239 rank cases using exact modular lower-bound certificates and checks 24 multiplicative-character spectral cases numerically; none of these finite tests substitutes for the unrestricted proof.

It does not imply nonvanishing of all twisted multiplicative phase sums for nonzero linear coupling. ## Mathematical value — PASS
The restriction to the same finite-field parabola on both sides of a Fourier pairing produces a natural structured square sampling matrix. Its rank drops from full \(p\) to \((p+1)/2\) when precisely the linear-coordinate coupling is removed; the exact kernel and full singular-value multiset identify the mechanism, rather than giving an isolated small numerical example. The singleton-fiber nonvanishing lemma gives a reusable sufficient criterion for a wider class of multiplicative-Hankel phase matrices, and the explicit rank transition holds uniformly for every odd prime. This informs exact recoverability and spectral conditioning questions for nonlinear Fourier sampling.
