# Singular strata and the Gorenstein threshold for binary Nashtope toric varieties

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), consider the monomial projective variety \(X_n\) over \(\mathbb C\) defined below. It is normal and Cohen--Macaulay of dimension \(2n-1\). Its reduced singular locus is exactly the disjoint union of \(n\) copies of \((\mathbb P^1)^{n-1}\). The transverse singularity on every component is the affine rank-one determinantal cone for \(2\times(n-1)\) matrices. Its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is \(n-1\), and the local divisor class group is \(\mathbb Z\). The variety is Gorenstein if and only if \(n=3\); for \(n\ge4\), it is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein at a singular point.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix an integer \(n\ge3\), and work over \(\mathbb C\). Index players by \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). In projective space with homogeneous coordinates
\[
 Z_{i,\epsilon},\qquad i\in[n],\quad\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{[n]\setminus\{i\}},
\]
there are \(n2^{n-1}\) coordinates. Define \(X_n\) as the Zariski closure of the monomial image
\[
 Z_{i,\epsilon}=u_i\prod_{k\ne i}x_{k,\epsilon_k},
\]
where \(u_i\) and \(x_{k,s}\) are arbitrary torus variables. This is exactly the projective toric variety of the Cayley Nashmatrix for the binary game format of Neuhaus--Portakal--Sodomaco--Tung [1]; it is **not** the Nash discriminant variety in the payoff coefficient space. Define \(S_i\subset X_n\) as the locus where all coordinate blocks except the \(i\)-th vanish. This locus is the Segre image of the \(n-1\) two-dimensional factor spaces indexed by players other than \(i\).

## Proof
**Step 1 (the whole affine chart, scheme-theoretically).** Fix \(i\in[n]\) and a binary tuple \(\delta\) for the other players. Work on \(Z_{i,\delta}\ne0\); complementing binary indices lets us set \(\delta=0\). Define \(y_k=Z_{i,e_k}/Z_{i,0}\) for each \(k\ne i\), where \(e_k\) denotes the tuple with the unique entry \(1\) at \(k\). For every \(j\ne i\) and \(s\in\{0,1\}\), set
\[
 b_{s,j}=Z_{j,\epsilon(j,s)}/Z_{i,0},
\]
where \(\epsilon(j,s)\) has entry \(s\) at \(i\) and zero at every other index different from \(j\). The \(i\)-th coordinate block, divided by \(Z_{i,0}\), consists of all squarefree monomials in the \(y_k\). The \(j\)-th coordinate block consists exactly of
\[
 b_{s,j}\prod_{k\notin\{i,j\}:\,\epsilon_k=1} y_k.
\]
Thus the entire chart coordinate algebra is generated by the \(n-1\) independent \(y_k\) and the \(2(n-1)\) generators \(b_{s,j}\). On the dense torus, these have independent parameters
\[
 y_k=x_{k,1}/x_{k,0},\quad b_{s,j}=a_st_j,\quad
 a_s=x_{i,s},\quad t_j=u_j/(u_ix_{j,0})
\]
(up to a common harmless normalization of the \(a_s\)). Therefore all \(2\times2\) minors of the matrix \(B=(b_{s,j})\) vanish. Conversely, the kernel of the Segre monomial map \(b_{s,j}\mapsto a_st_j\) is precisely the prime ideal of those minors. To see that adjoining the \(y_k\) imposes no new relation, note that the characters \(y_k\) are independent of the \(a_st_j\) characters: the exponent of \(x_{k,1}\) records \(y_k\), whereas the latter involve only \(x_{i,s}\) and \(x_{j,0}\). Hence the chart ring is exactly
\[
 \mathbb C[y_k:k\ne i]\otimes_{\mathbb C}
 \frac{\mathbb C[b_{s,j}:s=0,1,\ j\ne i]}{I_2(B)}.
\tag{1}
\]
This proves an isomorphism of affine schemes, not merely an isomorphism of tangent cones or a set-theoretic description. Every projective point belongs to one of the displayed charts.

**Step 2 (dimension, singular points, and normality).** Put \(m=n-1\ge2\). The rank-one determinantal ring in (1) is the homogeneous coordinate ring of \(\operatorname{Segre}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1})\). It is a normal Cohen--Macaulay domain of dimension \(m+1\): equivalently it is the normal Segre product of polynomial rings, and the maximal-minor presentation is determinantal. Thus every chart is normal and Cohen--Macaulay and has dimension \(m+(m+1)=2n-1\). A rank-one matrix is a smooth point of the rank-at-most-one variety, whereas the zero matrix is singular for \(m\ge2\): its embedding dimension \(2m\) exceeds its dimension \(m+1\). Formula (1) therefore makes the singular locus in this chart precisely \(B=0\). Its coordinate functions are just the monomials in \(y_k\) of the \(i\)-th block, and its projective closure is \(S_i\cong(\mathbb P^1)^m\). If a point has coordinates in two distinct blocks, choose one of its nonzero coordinates to see \(B\ne0\), hence smoothness. The \(S_i\) are projectively disjoint since they lie in distinct coordinate linear subspaces. They are exactly the \(n\) irreducible components of the reduced singular locus, each of dimension \(n-1\) and codimension \(n\).

**Step 3 (multiplicity).** The determinantal cone transverse to \(S_i\) is the cone over \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1}\) embedded by \(\mathcal O(1,1)\). Its degree is
\[
 \int_{\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1}}(H_1+H_2)^m=m.
\]
The Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity at the cone vertex, and hence at a point of \(S_i\) after adjoining the smooth factor, is \(m=n-1\). In the case \(n=3\), the transverse cone is the hypersurface \(b_{0,1}b_{1,2}-b_{0,2}b_{1,1}=0\), a three-dimensional ordinary double point.

**Step 4 (local divisor class and canonical class).** For the normal affine cone \(C\) over \(T=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1}\) with hyperplane class \(H=\mathcal O_T(1,1)\), the vertex local divisor class group is
\[
 \mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_{C,0})
 \cong \mathrm{Pic}(T)/\mathbb Z[H]
 \cong (\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z)/\mathbb Z(1,1)
 \cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The punctured cone is a \(\mathbb G_m\)-bundle over \(T\), giving this class-group quotient. Adding a smooth affine factor does not change the class group of the local product singularity. The canonical class corresponds to \(K_T=\mathcal O_T(-2,-m)\), which maps to \(m-2\) times a primitive generator of this torsion-free group. Hence its local canonical class is torsion exactly when \(m=2\). Since normal varieties are \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein precisely where a positive multiple of the canonical class is Cartier, \(X_n\) is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein at singular points when \(m\ge3\); when \(m=2\), its local cone is a Gorenstein hypersurface, and smooth points are Gorenstein, so \(X_3\) is Gorenstein globally. All singular points have nonzero local divisor class group and therefore are not \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs exponent vectors for the Nashmatrix monomials with exact integers, checks every coordinate's expression in the chart generators and every claimed quadratic minor for every chart and each \(3\le n\le8\), and checks the exponent-lattice dimension and transverse degree. Run `python3 verify.py` in a directory containing that script; the expected terminal line is `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks are consistency tests only; the proof for arbitrary \(n\) is the symbolic chart presentation and standard Segre-determinantal structure in Steps 1--4. No external computational package is required.

## Relationship to prior work
Neuhaus--Portakal--Sodomaco--Tung [1], Definitions 2.3--2.4 and Sections 3.2--3.4, give the Nashmatrix, Nashface combinatorics, lattice bases and Euler obstructions (equal to two on binary prismatic faces), in service of discriminant-degree calculations. Those face obstructions do not identify the exact local rings, every singular stratum, the class group, or the \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein threshold. Abo--Portakal--Sodomaco [2], including their open supplementary discussion of the three-concurrent-lines locus, already give the \(n=3\) monomial parametrization \([MN,LN,LM]\), its dimension, equations and Chern--Mather data. This prior existence and degree information is not claimed as new. The additional result here is the exact all-player chart, complete local singularity classification, multiplicity, divisor class and canonical-class behavior. These are not implied merely by the already reported Euler obstruction or degree computations.

## Limitations
The result concerns the projective toric variety of binary Nashmatrix columns, not the Nash discriminant hypersurface or the Nash equilibrium scheme, and does not treat nonbinary formats. Historical priority is not independently certified. Some potentially relevant earlier toric singularity treatments may encode the conclusions implicitly without these precise formulations. A separate independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
