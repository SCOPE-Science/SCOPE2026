# Singular strata and transverse determinantal cones of balanced truncated Grassmannians

## Finding

Let \(d\ge 3\) be an integer, let \(V=E\oplus F\) with \(\dim_{\mathbb C}E=\dim_{\mathbb C}F=d\), and let \(P=[\bigwedge^dF]\) be the Plücker point of \(\operatorname{Gr}(d,V)\) corresponding to \(F\). Define \(Y_d\) as the closure of the linear projection of this Plücker-embedded Grassmannian away from \(P\), onto \(\mathbb P((\bigwedge^dV)/\bigwedge^dF)\). Equivalently, in the degree-truncation notation of Feigin--Makhlin, \(Y_d=\operatorname{Gr}^{d-1}_d(\mathbb C^{2d})\): exactly the single top-degree Plücker coordinate is discarded.

**Theorem.** The reduced singular locus of the \(d^2\)-dimensional variety \(Y_d\) is precisely one Segre variety,
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(Y_d)_{\mathrm{red}}=Z_d\cong\mathbb P(E)\times\mathbb P(F^*)\cong\mathbb P^{d-1}\times\mathbb P^{d-1},
\]
embedded in the projective tangent-direction space \(\mathbb P(\operatorname{Hom}(F,E))\). Put \(k=(d-1)^2\). For every closed point \(z\in Z_d\), the completed local ring has the explicit isomorphism
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{Y_d,z}\cong
\frac{\mathbb C[[t_1,\ldots,t_{2d-2},u_1,\ldots,u_k,v_1,\ldots,v_k]]}{(u_iv_j-u_jv_i:1\le i<j\le k)}.
\]
Thus the transverse slice is the affine cone over the Segre embedding \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{k-1}\), and **at every singular point**
\[
\operatorname{mult}_z(Y_d)=k=(d-1)^2,\qquad
\operatorname{codim}_{Y_d}Z_d=k+1=(d-1)^2+1.
\]
The variety \(Y_d\) is normal. For the boundary case \(d=2\), the analogous projected variety is smooth, so the lower bound \(d\ge3\) is essential to the singularity statement.

## Assumptions and scope

All schemes and completions are over \(\mathbb C\). The Plücker point \(P\) is part of the original Grassmannian, so the projection from \(P\) is rational and \(Y_d\) means its **scheme-theoretic image closure**. The word singular refers to the ordinary scheme-theoretic nonsmooth locus; the displayed equality specifies its reduced support only. Points of \(Z_d\) are closed complex points, and all completed rings are completed at their respective maximal ideals.

## Proof

**1. The singular candidate.** A line contained in \(\operatorname{Gr}(d,V)\) and passing through \(P=[F]\) consists of the intermediate \(d\)-planes between a hyperplane \(H\subset F\) and a \((d+1)\)-plane \(F+\langle e\rangle\), where \([e]\in\mathbb P(E)\). Its image under projection from \(P\) is a single point. The direction is the rank-one map \(F\to E\) with kernel \(H\) and image \(\langle e\rangle\). These directions constitute the Segre subvariety \(Z_d=\mathbb P(E)\times\mathbb P(F^*)\) inside \(\mathbb P(\operatorname{Hom}(F,E))\). The latter is the projective embedded tangent-direction space at \(P\).

**2. Exact affine chart at a singular candidate.** The group \(\operatorname{GL}(E)\times\operatorname{GL}(F)\) acts transitively on \(Z_d\); it therefore suffices to calculate near the image of the line whose general plane is \(W=\langle e_1,f_2,\ldots,f_d\rangle\). Take the standard Plücker chart where the coordinate of this plane is nonzero. Its preimage in the Grassmannian has a unique row-echelon matrix, with rows
\[
\begin{aligned}
 e_1+\sum_{j=2}^d a_j e_j+c f_1,&\\
 f_i+\sum_{j=2}^d z_{ij}e_j+b_i f_1,&\qquad 2\le i\le d.
\end{aligned}
\]
There are \(2d-2\) free symbols \(a_j,b_i\), exactly \(k=(d-1)^2\) symbols \(z_{ij}\), and one symbol \(c\). The Plücker coordinate indexed by all of \(F\) is \(c\) (up to the fixed orientation sign) and is the **only discarded coordinate**. Retained Plücker coordinates individually recover the symbols \(a_j,b_i,z_{ij}\) (up to signs). The retained coordinate indexed by \(\{e_j,f_1\}\cup(\{f_2,\ldots,f_d\}\setminus\{f_i\})\) is, up to sign, \(a_jb_i-cz_{ij}\). Hence the retained functions also generate \(c z_{ij}\).

Conversely, every retained maximal minor is a polynomial in \(a,b,z,c\) which is at most linear in \(c\). Its coefficient of \(c\) belongs to the ideal \((z_{ij})\): setting every \(z_{ij}=0\), the other \(d-1\) rows involve only columns from \(F\), so the only minor to which a \(c\)-term can contribute is the discarded all-\(F\) minor. Thus the entire coordinate algebra of this affine chart of the image closure is **exactly**
\[
\mathbb C[a_j,b_i,z_{ij},cz_{ij}].
\]
Introduce \(w_{ij}=cz_{ij}\). The standard parametrization of rank-one matrices identifies this subalgebra with
\[
\frac{\mathbb C[a_j,b_i,z_{ij},w_{ij}]}{(z_{ij}w_{i'j'}-z_{i'j'}w_{ij}:\ (i,j)<(i',j'))}.
\]
In other words, the whole affine chart is \(\mathbb A^{2d-2}\times D_k\), where \(D_k\) is the variety of \(2\times k\) matrices of rank at most one. This identification is **scheme-theoretic**, not merely a parametrization of its points: the ideal of the rank-one matrix variety is generated by its \(2\times2\) minors, and its ring is the subalgebra generated by \(z_{ij},cz_{ij}\). Its singular locus is exactly the vertex \(z=w=0\) for \(k\ge2\). On that vertex, the free coordinates \(a,b\) form an affine chart of \(Z_d\). Completing at any such point and translating the free coordinates gives the displayed completed local ring. Since \(d\ge3\), \(k\ge4\), so every point of \(Z_d\) is singular.

**3. No further singularities.** The tangent space of the Grassmannian at \(P\) consists of the Plücker coordinate directions with at most one basis vector from \(E\). Its image after deleting the coordinate of \(P\) is the linear subspace \(T\cong\mathbb P^{d^2-1}\) spanned by the one-swap Plücker coordinates. Every point of \(Y_d\cap T\) lies in some affine chart where one of these one-swap coordinates is nonzero. By the chart computation just completed (after a basis permutation), its neighborhood is smooth unless it lies on \(Z_d\).

It remains to consider points outside \(T\). The quadratic Plücker ideal has no square of the coordinate of \(P\), because \(P\) belongs to the Grassmannian. Every Plücker quadric can therefore be written \(p_P L(q)+Q(q)\), with \(q\) denoting retained coordinates. As the Grassmannian is smooth and its ideal is generated by these quadrics, the linear forms \(L(q)\) together cut out the projective tangent space \(T\). Outside \(T\), one such \(L\) is nonzero, and the missing coordinate is recovered regularly as \(p_P=-Q/L\). This gives an inverse to the projection on that open subset, identifying it with an open subset of the smooth Grassmannian. Consequently the singular locus is exactly \(Z_d\), with its reduced scheme structure as stated.

**4. Multiplicity, codimension, and normality.** The cone \(D_k\) has standard graded pieces of dimension \((r+1)\binom{k+r-1}{r}\), so its Hilbert series is
\[
\frac{1+(k-1)t}{(1-t)^{k+1}}.
\]
The numerator evaluates to \(k\) at \(t=1\); hence the multiplicity at the vertex is \(k\). Smooth factors do not alter this multiplicity. The dimension of \(D_k\) is \(k+1\), whereas the singular locus has dimension \(2d-2\); the codimension is \(k+1\). Rank-one determinantal rings are normal (they are the affine Segre coordinate rings). The chart description near \(Z_d\) and smoothness elsewhere prove normality of \(Y_d\). For \(d=2\), \(k=1\), and \(D_1\cong\mathbb A^2\) is smooth, explaining the boundary.

## Relationship to prior work

The smallest nontrivial case, the nine-dimensional projection of \(\operatorname{Gr}(3,6)\), has previously been evaluated explicitly: its reduced singular locus is \(\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^2\), the transverse cone is a rank-one \(2\times4\) matrix cone, and the multiplicity is four. **That finite case is not a new contribution here.** The present result proves the complete classification for every integer \(d\ge3\), including unbounded singular multiplicities \((d-1)^2\), and establishes the uniform absence of additional singular strata.

Feigin--Makhlin, *Truncated Grassmannians: polytopes, representations, and Cauchy formulas* (arXiv:2610.40891, submitted October 29, 2026), defines the truncations and proves character, coordinate-ring, and polytope formulas. Its sections on varieties and representations do not state this balanced-family singular locus, the completed transverse ring, or its multiplicities. Feigin, *Truncated Grassmannians, blow-ups along Schubert varieties and collineations* (arXiv:2604.00751), identifies graph closures and relevant blow-ups of Grassmannians, but does not compute the contracted target's local determinantal singularities. Feigin, *Birational maps to Grassmannians, representations and poset polytopes* (arXiv:2403.10074), treats the graph closure of the first truncation and its fibers, rather than the local rings of the next-to-full truncation. Huang--Petroulakis--Sottile--Zelenko, *Nontrivial linear projections of the Grassmannian Gr_3(C^6)* (arXiv:2004.10070), studies low-dimensional projection centers **disjoint from the Grassmannian**, whereas this center is an actual Plücker point. Standard Segre-determinantal facts supply the local model **after** the chart has been established, but do not on their own locate its occurrence in the projected Grassmannian.

## Limitations

The proof concerns the balanced case \(\operatorname{Gr}(d,2d)\) and the **single-coordinate** next-to-full truncation. With a larger complementary space or a smaller truncation, the center has higher dimension and the chart calculation does not apply. The nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme, minimal resolutions, and other local invariants are not claimed. Exact finite checks support the chart calculations but do not replace the universal proof. Independent mathematical validation and exhaustive historical-priority certification have not been performed.
