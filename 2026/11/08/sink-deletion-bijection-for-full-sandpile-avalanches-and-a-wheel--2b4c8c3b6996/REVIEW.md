# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The construction is an explicit, invertible map from recurrent full-avalanche states on \(G\) with sink \(s\) and fixed source \(v\) to recurrent states on \(G-s\) with sink \(v\). It uses the one-topple restriction for sink-adjacent additions and the forward and reverse Dhar burning tests. It proves all finite undirected connected graphs (parallel edges allowed), including disconnected deletion cases. The matrix-tree theorem, the fan recurrence, and a cyclic Laplacian determinant establish the wheel formula. Exact checks on 207 connected graphs, 469 eligible sources, and wheels support the proof without replacing it.

**Originality — PASS on the examined evidence, with residual historical risk.** Dartois and Rossin's full wheel study fixes the hub sink. Austin and collaborators provide the known one-topple lemma and the hub-sink wheel avalanche polynomial; these are credited and excluded from the novelty claim. Searches of mathematical records and primary full texts revealed no theorem counting all-systems avalanches by sink-vertex deletion or the wheel hub-versus-rim sink probability transition. Wu–Zhang–Chen 2017 is potentially relevant but its full text was inaccessible; only its abstract about total toppling numbers was inspected. Equivalence via stronger unindexed burning-bijection results remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The all-graph bijection transforms a global dynamical event into a spanning-tree count and unexpectedly removes dependence on the source chosen near the sink. The wheel instance demonstrates a dramatic quantitative boundary-condition dependence, an exponentially rare full avalanche versus a positive limiting chance. The event is a naturally motivated extreme of the avalanche size distribution, not an arbitrary graph parameter.

**Limitations.** Only single-grain additions at sink neighbors and the uniform recurrent state law are covered. Source invariance does not extend to arbitrary nonsink vertices. The paper of Wu et al. is an unresolved source-access risk; independent audit is not yet performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
