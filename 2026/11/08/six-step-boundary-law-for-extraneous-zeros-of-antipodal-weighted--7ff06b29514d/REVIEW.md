**Correctness — PASS.** The projection formula follows directly from the two-by-two evaluation Gram matrix; the exact degree-\(2\beta\) polynomial has no spurious pole roots. Rouché's theorem gives two clusters, analytic implicit-function branches resolve all simple roots, and a positive second-order term decides the critical angles when \(6\mid\beta\). The resulting number \(4\lceil\beta/6\rceil-2\) of disk roots and the quartets of extras are derived for fixed integer exponent and sufficiently near-boundary sites. `CHECK.py` passed 2448 numerical assertions; it is corroboration, not proof.

**Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority).** Hedenmalm–Zhu (1992, Proposition 3) already derive the root-of-unity half-plane criterion for **one prescribed zero**, so the first integer threshold is not independently novel. The distinct result here is the fully resolved two-antipodal-point projection and its exact two-cluster nonreal zero count and critical branches. Felder–Le's projection and extraneous-zero theory (arXiv:2105.01766, §§3, 5), Aleman et al.'s one-point canonical divisors (arXiv:math/0406369, §3), and Bénéteau et al.'s optimal approximants (arXiv:1509.04807) supply context but did not give the exact symmetric two-zero six-step boundary count in the materials inspected. Trieu Le (arXiv:1912.05715, Example 3.3) and Cheng–Mashreghi–Ross (arXiv:1807.06341, §§5–6) establish further one-point examples and general bounds rather than the symmetric two-point exact count. A public mathematical record for arbitrarily many real extra zeros from some finite zero sets has a different quantifier, no exact minimal symmetric two-point asymptotic, and only its summary was accessible. Equivalent older canonical-divisor formulas remain a material risk; failure to find them is not proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** Canonical divisors can have unexpected additional zeros that alter zero-set identification. This classification identifies, in the smallest natural antipodally symmetric zero set, the first kernel-power threshold, exact number and nonreal symmetry of every extra zero, including a nontrivial tangency exclusion at powers divisible by six. It does not require a downstream result to be useful for zero geometry and invariant-subspace interpretation.
