# Review of the six-theory classification

## Correctness — PASS

The atomic reduction was checked against all \(27\) ordered triples drawn from two variable names and the identity constant, as well as equality. The proof isolates precisely the possible square, square-root, inverse, identity, and generic profiles around a nonidentity anchor for each prime exponent. The availability of each profile follows within the order-\(p\) subgroup and from Lagrange's theorem. This supplies a permanent ordinary two-pebble strategy at every quantifier depth. Four explicit two-variable sentences give six distinct truth vectors; hence the upper and lower bounds agree. Two executable checks corroborate all atomic profiles and one-pebble successor sets on eleven finite examples, including nonabelian groups of orders \(27\), \(125\), and \(343\). Finite computations are evidence for the mechanism, not the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest primary text inspected was Gomaa's 2007 dissertation, Chapter 3, including the pebble-game sections and general finite-group expressibility discussion. It gives bounds, not the exact six uncounted two-variable theories of prime-exponent groups. The relevant Brachter–Schweitzer study works with counting logic and bijective games, so its cardinality-sensitive results do not imply this cardinality-insensitive classification. A previously established four-class result for prime-order cyclic groups is subsumed but does not cover noncyclic or nonabelian groups or the exponent-five merger. Searches in mathematical records and the accessible literature did not reveal an equivalent or stronger six-class theorem. Residual risk: an earlier specialized two-variable group result could imply it; the 2010 journal adaptation of the dissertation was checked only at abstract level.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result gives a complete finite-variable theory classification on a natural and mathematically broad family that includes nonabelian finite groups of unbounded order. The cross-characteristic merger \(C_5^2\equiv_{\mathrm{FO}^{2}}C_7\), and the sharp two-to-three-variable barrier for detecting noncommutativity in explicit same-order pairs, are structural consequences not visible from the prime-cyclic special case alone. This identifies exactly how much multiplication is invisible when only two variable names are available.

## Scope and limitations

The result depends critically on using a ternary relation for multiplication and excluding counting quantifiers. It makes no general claim about other exponents or signatures. Originality is a best-knowledge assessment, not a proof of priority, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
