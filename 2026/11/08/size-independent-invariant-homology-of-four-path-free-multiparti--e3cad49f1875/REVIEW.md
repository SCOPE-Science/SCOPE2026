# Mathematical review

## Correctness
The forbidden-path characterization gives an explicit cover by a two-skeleton and contractible join complexes. Their pairwise overlaps lie in the two-skeleton, yielding a direct-sum relative chain complex and the integral equivariant short exact sequence. The cycle modules have no invariants under within-part symmetric groups; normalized vertex sums identify the remaining invariant chains with a two-skeleton of a simplex. The signed Hopf trace produces the claimed full rational character. A standalone independent face-enumeration and signed-trace test verifies 1,266 part-preserving automorphisms across 76 cases. The finite tests do not imply the infinite-family result; the symbolic argument does.

## Originality
The nearest publicly identified preprint is Deshpande–Goyal–Sawant, arXiv:2607.05358, whose accessible abstract concerns structural decomposition, shellability and bipartite homotopy. A known nonequivariant wedge classification gives the rank at the identity, but does not determine the action, the canonical integral extension, or the part-size-independent invariant classes. Searches for corresponding equivariant characters and invariant homology returned no covering record. The inaccessible preprint full text and unindexed equivalent work are residual originality risks.

## Mathematical value
The full symmetry character, and especially the invariant rank \(\binom{m-1}{3}\) independent of every individual part size, distinguish representation-theoretic structure from scalar Betti numbers. The canonical integral extension is directly useful for studying reduction modulo primes, integral symmetry lattices and actions of subgroups, without incorrectly asserting an equivariant splitting. For three parts the total invariant subspace vanishes even when the homology rank is large; for four parts exactly one rational invariant class survives, regardless of sizes.
