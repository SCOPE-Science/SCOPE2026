# Scientific review: smooth but non-Gevrey proportional-reset density

## Correctness

PASS. The stationary characteristic function is a positive product of Laplace characteristic functions. Retaining arbitrarily many factors proves decay faster than any inverse power, justifying smooth Fourier inversion and repeated differentiation of the stationary pantograph equation. The exact even-derivative recurrence gives a product of the odd geometric factors; its exponent is the sum of odd integers, \(n^2\). Its positive limiting product and growth exceeding every \((2n)!^s\) establish the Gevrey and Taylor conclusions. A known absolutely convergent exponential-mode representation establishes analytic continuation on each open half-line. Numerical diagnostics of Fourier integrals, series normalization and recurrences passed 56 assertions. The finite diagnostics are supportive, not the infinite-order proof.

## Originality

PASS with residual literature risk. Pierce, Tal-Friedman et al. and Di Bello et al. already derive the governing equation, characteristic product, stationary distribution and partial-reset Gaussian limit; those are not claimed. Biroli et al. explicitly report an unresolved proof of the cancellation of the first cusp term in their stationary series (their equation (20)), although their figures suggest a Gaussian-like center. Their available full text and the other primary papers do not establish an exact entire origin derivative jet, the sharp superfactorial derivative asymptotic, or the analytic-off-zero/non-Gevrey-at-zero classification. Literature queries for equivalent pantograph regularity assertions and related research records did not identify a covering result. General unindexed pantograph regularity literature could still contain an equivalent theorem, and novelty is not certified independently.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The exact high-order regularity is a structural property of the natural stationary state, resolving a genuine apparent paradox between a Gaussian-like core and a rescaling-driven hierarchy of exponentially separated length scales. It proves that no finite-order Gevrey/Taylor-based local reconstruction works at the reset point, while all finite derivatives nevertheless exist, and gives their exact values and asymptotic normalization. The result also handles negative rescaling without changing the stationary law. It is more than a numerical derivative computation or a small coefficient adjustment.

## Closest sources and limitations

Primary comparisons: arXiv:2204.07215 (Section III); arXiv:2205.03363 (stationary independent-Laplace law); arXiv:2305.13722 (Sections 3.1 and 4); arXiv:2406.08387 (Section II including equations (17)–(20)). The proof assumes drift-free one-dimensional Brownian motion, Poisson resetting, and strictly contractive nonzero fixed reset multipliers. No assertion is made for complete resetting, no resetting, higher dimensions or nonstationary laws. Possible unindexed more general pantograph smoothness results remain a publication-priority risk.
