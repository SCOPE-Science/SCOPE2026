# Smooth set-theoretic complete intersections on a characteristic-two ruled cubic

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\) and let
\[X=V(x^2w-y^2z)\subset\mathbb P^3_k.\]
Write \(L=V(x,y)\subset X\). Let \(\pi:S\to X\) be the normalization, where \(S\) is the rational normal cubic scroll \(S(1,2)\subset\mathbb P^4\), and let \(H=\pi^*\mathcal O_X(1)\). Let \(\Gamma=\pi^{-1}(L)\), a smooth conic.

**Classification.** A smooth geometrically integral curve \(C\subset X\) is a set-theoretic complete intersection of \(X\) with a second hypersurface if and only if
\[C=\pi(D)\quad\text{for some }d\ge1\text{ and some smooth }D\in|dH|\text{ transverse to }\Gamma.\]
For every \(d\ge1\) the indicated class has smooth members transverse to \(\Gamma\); the resulting smooth curves satisfy
\[\deg C=3d,\qquad g(C)=1+\frac{3d^2-5d}{2}.\]
For each such \(C\), a hypersurface of degree \(2d\) cuts out a Cartier divisor supported on \(C\) with generic multiplicity \(2\). This is the **minimal** possible generic intersection multiplicity of a hypersurface with \(X\) supported on \(C\): any such multiplicity is even. Thus smooth set-theoretic complete intersections on this fixed cubic have unbounded degree and genus.

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic exactly \(2\). A smooth curve means a smooth, geometrically integral projective curve. A set-theoretic complete intersection **on** \(X\) means \(C=(X\cap V(G))_{\mathrm{red}}\) for one homogeneous polynomial \(G\), with no other one-dimensional support. The term *generic intersection multiplicity* refers to the coefficient of \(C\) in the effective Cartier divisor \(V(G)|_X\); it need not mean that the intersection is the ordinary doubled ideal of \(C\).

The homogeneous coordinates of \(S\) are \([x:y:z:w:e]\), with determinantal equations
\[xe-yz=xw-ye=zw-e^2=0.\]
Its standard parametrization is
\[[u:v],[a:b]\longmapsto[au:av:bu^2:bv^2:buv],\]
under the usual scroll-bundle identifications. Projection forgetting \(e\) is the finite birational normalization map onto \(X\). The exceptional conductor curve is \(\Gamma=V(x,y)\cap S\); its map to \(L\) is \([u:v]\mapsto[u^2:v^2]\), the degree-two relative Frobenius on \(\mathbb P^1\) under the chosen coordinates.

## Proof
**1. Normalization and local conductor.** On the chart \(w=1\), the coordinate ring is \(A=k[x,y,z]/(x^2-y^2z)\). In its fraction field, \(t=x/y\) is integral with \(t^2=z\), and its integral closure is \(B=k[y,t]\), with \(x=yt\). Its conductor in \(B\) is \(yB=(x,y)\subset A\). Both conductor curves are reduced, with \(L=\operatorname{Spec}k[z]\) and \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]\), mapped by \(z=t^2\). The other charts give the global cubic-scroll normalization. In particular, the inverse image of every closed point of \(L\) is one point of \(\Gamma\) with ramification index \(2\), and \(\pi\) is an isomorphism outside \(\Gamma\).

**2. The smoothness criterion.** At a point of \(\Gamma\), choose local parameters \(t,y\) on \(S\) with \(\Gamma=(y)\). The image local ring is generated by \(t^2,y,ty\). If an integral curve \(D\not\supset\Gamma\) is smooth and meets \(\Gamma\) with multiplicity one, then the restriction of \(y\) to \(D\) is a uniformizer. Its image curve's completed local ring contains \(k[[y]]\), which is the entire completed normalization ring of \(D\), and hence is regular; thus \(\pi|_D:D\to\pi(D)\) is a local isomorphism. Conversely, if \(D\) is smooth and tangent to \(\Gamma\), each element of the image maximal ideal vanishes to order at least \(2\) in the normalization parameter \(t\), so the image local ring is not regular. Since \(\pi\) is finite and is an isomorphism away from \(\Gamma\), the image curve is smooth precisely when \(D\) is smooth and transverse to \(\Gamma\).

**3. Necessity and the singular line.** Suppose a smooth integral \(C\subset X\) is set-theoretically cut out by a homogeneous \(G\) of degree \(m\). If \(C\ne L\), the hypersurface \(V(G)\) cannot contain \(L\). Thus on the normalization the pulled-back Cartier divisor is \(rD\), where \(r\ge1\) is its generic multiplicity and \(D\) is the strict inverse image of \(C\), and
\[r[D]=mH\quad\text{in }\operatorname{Pic}(S).\]
The scroll has \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\cong\mathbb ZH\oplus\mathbb ZF\), where \(F\) is a ruling fiber; \(H\) is primitive. Therefore \([D]=dH\) for an integer \(d=m/r\ge1\). The map \(D\to C\) is finite birational and \(C\) is normal, so it is an isomorphism. By step 2, \(D\) is smooth and meets \(\Gamma\) transversely. The exceptional alternative \(C=L\) is impossible: if a hypersurface section were supported on \(L\), its pullback would have class \(mH=r\Gamma\), but \(H\) and \(\Gamma\) are linearly independent in \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\). This proves necessity.

**4. Explicit Frobenius-square descent and sufficiency.** The cubic scroll is projectively normal, so each \(s\in H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(dH))\) is represented by a homogeneous degree-\(d\) polynomial \(P(x,y,z,w,e)\). In characteristic two, its square has no cross terms. The relation \(e^2=zw\) on \(S\) therefore rewrites \(s^2\) as \(\pi^*G\) for a homogeneous degree-\(2d\) polynomial \(G(x,y,z,w)\). If \(D=(s=0)\) is smooth and transverse to \(\Gamma\), then \(V(G)|_X\) pulls back to \(2D\). Because \(\pi\) is finite surjective and birational, its support is exactly \(C=\pi(D)\) and its generic multiplicity is \(2\). Thus \(C\) is a set-theoretic complete intersection.

**5. Existence for all degrees.** For every \(d\ge1\), \(dH\) is very ample on the smooth cubic scroll. Its complete linear system restricts *surjectively* to \(H^0(\Gamma,\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(2d))\): the degree-\(2\) Veronese coordinates \(z,e,w\) generate all even-degree binary forms under multiplication. Hence a general section has \(2d\) distinct zeros on \(\Gamma\). The smooth/irreducible Bertini open for this very ample system meets this transverse-contact open, yielding a smooth integral \(D\in|dH|\) transverse to \(\Gamma\). As \(H^2=3\) and \(K_S\cdot H=-5\), adjunction gives the degree and genus formulas in the Finding.

**6. Minimality of multiplicity two.** The parity obstruction has a direct proof independent of a computation of any local divisor-class group: if a degree-\(m\) hypersurface has pullback divisor \(rD\), then its restriction to \(\Gamma\) is a degree-\(2m\) binary form in \(u,v\) obtained from a degree-\(m\) polynomial in \(u^2,v^2\); over the perfect field \(k\) this is a square, so every zero has even order. A transverse \(D\) has reduced zeros on \(\Gamma\), and \(D\cdot\Gamma=2d>0\), so the order at each such zero equals \(r\). Hence \(r\) is even. Step 4 realizes multiplicity \(2\), which is minimal.

## Verification
The standalone script `verify.py`, using exact polynomial arithmetic, checks the scroll's cubic equation and \(e^2=zw\), the universal Frobenius-square identity for a linear section, a concrete smooth degree-three example, and the adjunction numerics through \(d=50\). It prints `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED` and is accompanied by `verify_output.txt`. This is a check of algebraic identities and examples, **not** a replacement for Bertini, projective normality, the local conductor theorem, or the proof for arbitrary \(d\).

For a concrete degree-three example, take the scroll section \(s=x+y+e\), which restricts on the normalization to \(a(u+v)+buv=0\). Its image is the twisted cubic parametrized by \([u:v]\mapsto[u^2v:uv^2:u^3+u^2v:v^3+uv^2]\). On \(X\), the quadric \(G=x^2+y^2+zw\) restricts to \(s^2\); therefore its intersection with \(X\) has exactly this twisted cubic as reduced support and generic multiplicity two.

## Relationship to prior work
Hartshorne–Polini, *Divisors class groups of singular surfaces* (arXiv:1301.3222, first posted 2013-01-15), study the same ruled cubic in their Examples 6.3 and 7.12 in characteristic zero. They find that its only smooth set-theoretic complete intersection curves are twisted cubics. Their abstract conductor comparison (Theorem 3.1) also supplies the local-divisor machinery used here; this theorem is **not** claimed as new. Here the characteristic-two Frobenius gluing changes the local and global geometry: the conductor induces degree-two purely inseparable gluing, and the explicit square-descent mechanism realizes smooth self-linked curves in *every* multiple of the scroll hyperplane class. These unbounded families and the characteristic-two completeness statement are the asserted addition.

Apostolos Thoma (1989) treats monomial space curves that are binomial set-theoretic complete intersections, and T. T. Moh (1985) proves general positive-characteristic results for monomial curves and certain cuspidal-plane-projection curves. Those results do not impose this fixed cubic as a defining hypersurface or give the complete smooth-curve classification and minimal generic multiplicity proved here.

Miles Reid, *Nonnormal del Pezzo Surfaces* (1994), discusses normalization/conductor and pathological positive-characteristic gluing on nonnormal del Pezzo surfaces. Those general phenomena are acknowledged; the present result concerns a precise curve classification and surface-equation construction. Earlier specialist literature on wild pinch surfaces remains a priority risk.

## Limitations
The claim is for one fixed cubic surface in characteristic two. It does not classify curves on all nonnormal cubics or on a different characteristic-two cubic. The asserted multiplicity two is generic Cartier intersection multiplicity on the given cubic; the intersection scheme need not equal the naive ideal square of the curve. The smooth divisors in \(|dH|\) are asserted to exist over algebraically closed fields, not necessarily to be defined over a specified small finite field. Historical priority is provisional; independent validation has not been performed.
