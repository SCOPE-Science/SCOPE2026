# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem quantifies over every real \(r>1\) (with a separate ambient Banach space for each \(r\)), and separately over the empty spectrum. With \(Z=d(w,1)\) and \(V=Z\widehat\otimes_\pi Z\), the mapping \(v\mapsto(J_V(e_n\otimes v))_n\) is an isometry into \(\ell_\infty(V^{**})\), so its range is closed, linear and infinite-dimensional. The projective crossnorm identity for simple tensors proves every coordinate's common norm and the symmetry of the normalized basic sequence. All-point allowed-exponent summability follows by restricting each bounded bilinear form to its second vector, using the Lorentz coordinate estimate of [1, Theorem 3.1, equation (3.8)]. For the negative assertion the copied subspace \(X_v=Z\otimes v\subset V\) is hereditarily \(\ell_1\); its canonical image meets the no-strong-normality and weak-star/weak coincidence hypotheses independently, as follows from [1, Lemma 2.4] plus Hahn–Banach extension. Hence [1, Theorem 3.1] excludes all forbidden exponents for every normalized sequence in that span, including selected blocks. Finally the two weights are checked by infinite-series comparisons, including the closed boundary exponent. No finite experiment stands in for an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (subject to independent priority review)
[1, Corollary 3.5 and Theorem 3.6] already realize these *individual* spectra, while [1, Theorem 3.10] obtains a closed linear space of sequences for *open* thresholds through a special coordinate-disjoint estimate. The same paper explicitly asks in §8 for closed-threshold and empty-spectrum spaceability. Unlike the prior argument, the current construction enlarges the predual to the projective tensor dual and applies the hereditary criterion separately along each second-factor fiber, achieving one closed linear family for either forbidden case. [2, Theorem 26] concerns a closed subspace of functionals with no strongly normal sequence, not a closed space of sequences with exact simultaneous all-exponent spectra. The general survey [3] does not provide the statement. An unlocated general-purpose tensor or lineability theorem could still subsume the contribution; no independent priority certificate is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This fills the two specific endpoint/empty-spectrum spaceability cases left as open in [1, §8]. The distinction between one admissible sequence and every nonzero member of a norm-closed infinite-dimensional *linear* family is substantial. The tensor construction removes dependence on a special pure-power Lorentz weight, permitting a logarithmically corrected critical endpoint and an extremely slow weight with no admissible finite exponent. The result also preserves weak nullity, one-symmetry and the entire original/subsequence/block classification, not merely a single obstruction.

## Limits
The ambient dual is allowed to depend on the weight. The theorem uses [1, Theorem 3.1] as a cited nonstandard input and does not reprove its Lorentz block-domination theorem. Independent audit and exhaustive publication-priority verification have not been performed.
