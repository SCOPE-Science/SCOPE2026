# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The four Laplacian eigenspaces of \(K_m\square K_n\) are the tensor products of constant and zero-sum subspaces. Under the PBH test, the highest-eigenvalue space equals the alternating cycle space of \(K_{m,n}\), so uncontrollable modes vanish on selected cells precisely when the complement contains a cycle. For unequal side lengths, the row-constant and column-constant eigenspaces cannot have a nonzero vector supported on a forest, because two full rows or columns create a 4-cycle. At equal side lengths, the eigenvalue collision requires treating row-constant and column-constant modes together, which yields the two-row component-rank test. The minimal bound is the dimension of the highest space. The matrix-tree formula counts rectangular minimum sets, and a complementary-edge cut argument classifies the forbidden square spanning trees as exactly \(n^2\) double stars and \(n^2(n-1)^2\) isolated-selected-edge complements. The \(2\times2\) exception is addressed separately. The embedded stdlib verifier exhaustively checks all actuator sets on five small boards and all minimum-size candidates on the \(4\times4\) board using exact finite-field ranks; an integer-minor bound guarantees these ranks agree with rational ranks. Computations independently corroborate, but do not replace, the infinite-family proof.

## Originality — PASS on the inspected record, with residual priority risk

The focal Burgarth et al. source proves a sufficient zero-forcing condition; it does not characterize all coordinate-actuator sets of rook graphs. Notarstefano–Parlangeli's path-product grid work supplies a PBH and reduction framework, but its graph factors are paths, not complete graphs. Olshevsky studies general minimum diagonal-coordinate controllability and hardness, not this explicit family. Brunetti–Anđelić–Stanić examine Cartesian graph products for a single binary input vector; independent controls at distinct cells are a different question. The repository abstract was read, but its listed open manuscript link returned HTTP 403, leaving residual text-inspection uncertainty. Prior results on subdivisions of \(K_{m,n}\) and on line graphs of multipartite graphs with at least three parts do not imply this theorem for the bipartite line graph: the former adds original vertices and changes the square condition, while the latter excludes the two-part case and gives odd-unicycle rather than forest complements. Semantic searches for rook graph, line graph, PBH, square resonance, spanning forests, and minimum actuator counts returned no exact or stronger instance, though database coverage cannot prove priority. Details and actual query identifiers are preserved in the structured review.

## Value — PASS

This is a complete structural and enumerative solution to minimum independent-sensor/actuator placement on a classical Cartesian graph family. The unweighted square is not merely an easy specialization of the rectangular case: merging two Laplacian eigenspaces creates a second obstruction, with an exact subtraction from the spanning-tree count and a sharp exceptional \(2\times2\) case. The result produces a practical graph-forest test plus a tiny component-rank test instead of repeated PBH rank calculations; its exact labeled count distinguishes topology-induced resonance from straightforward dimension lower bounds.

## Limitations and closest literature
