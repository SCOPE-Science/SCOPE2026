# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The result is reconstructed from the trust game itself, not from a possibly mismatched published graph formula. Each internal directed arc contributes half to each endpoint; each external contribution is a difference of two unanimity games. That yields an exact finite representation as an independent incoming-degree term plus a compound binomial sum over outgoing destinations. Ordered-arc independence is essential and checked explicitly. Poisson approximation, two-root local exposure and factorial moments prove all asymptotic claims. The standalone verifier checked every directed graph of orders 2–4, independently computing all Shapley marginals and exact rational distribution probabilities. This verifies the finite algebra, not the infinite limit, which has a separate proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Bandhana–Kroupa–García (2024) already introduce the binary-edge trust model. Ryzák–Kroupa (2026) already derive a more general deterministic trust-game Shapley formula, so neither the model nor its deterministic specialization is proposed as new. The checked full manuscripts do not formulate the random directed Erdős–Rényi compound-Poisson credit distribution, its empirical nonzero atom, or the zero-credit Poisson critical window. The directed-cycle Shapley game of Khatri (2017) is different because it restricts coalition-order permutations. General Poisson isolate theory applies only after proving the new asymptotic equivalence between zero-credit players and isolates. Relevant published research records and external literature were searched by model, parameters and probabilistic equivalences. An indexed search cannot rule out unpublished or implicit derivations; some general centrality full text was unavailable for inspection.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The equal-positive-trust edge model is explicitly motivated by the originating peer-to-peer reputation framework. The exact distribution gives a population benchmark for uncertainty and inequity in Shapley reputations, not just an average. A positive fraction of agents have zero credit at every finite positive sparse mean degree, and the theorem pinpoints the density at which the last zero-credit agents disappear. Such thresholds matter for the distinction between sparse reputation coverage and global positive allocation. No assertion about arbitrary weighted or undirected networks is made.

## Limitations and references

The proof assumes independent ordered arcs and unit trust weights. It establishes a law of large numbers for empirical credits, but not Gaussian fluctuations of their empirical counts outside the specified Poisson critical window. Publication-priority risk remains for equivalent unindexed work.
