# Spectral zeros and a sharp positive-coupling bifurcation for conjugate shift blocks

## Finding
For a bounded diagonal coupling \(D_c\) with \(c_0\ne0\), the block operator \(T_c=\begin{psmallmatrix}S&D_c\\0&S^*\end{psmallmatrix}\) has essential spectrum \(\mathbb T\), and its entire spectrum off \(\mathbb T\) is the zero locus \(\{\lambda\in\mathbb D:F_c(\lambda^2)=0\}\), where \(F_c(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n\). Each interior eigenvalue has a one-dimensional eigenspace. Even when every diagonal coupling is positive, one exceptional weight causes a sharp two-eigenvalue bifurcation at \(h=t/2\). Two positions of an identical exceptional positive weight give exactly the same singular-value spectrum but different complex point spectra.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over complex \(H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)\) with orthonormal basis \((e_n)_{n\ge0}\) and unilateral shift \(Se_n=e_{n+1}\). Let \(c=(c_n)_{n\ge0}\in\ell^\infty\) with \(c_0\ne0\), and define \(D_ce_n=c_ne_n\). Define \(T_c:H\oplus H\to H\oplus H\) by \(T_c(x,y)=(Sx+D_cy,S^*y)\). The analytic generating function \(F_c(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty c_nz^n\) converges absolutely for \(|z|<1\). The Fredholm essential spectrum means failure of Fredholmness. Algebraic multiplicities and boundary residual spectral types are not asserted.

For the explicit positive family choose arbitrary real \(t>0\), \(h\ge0\), and \(c_n=t+h\mathbf1_{\{n=1\}}\). The isosingular pair uses \(c_n=1+\mathbf1_{\{n=1\}}\) versus \(d_n=1+\mathbf1_{\{n=2\}}\).

## Proof
**Defect pairing in the open disk.** Fix \(|\lambda|<1\). The operator \(S-\lambda I\) is bounded below, since \(\|(S-\lambda I)x\|\ge(1-|\lambda|)\|x\|\), and its range is exactly the closed codimension-one subspace killed by the continuous functional \(L_\lambda(u)=\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda^nu_n\). This follows from \(\ker(S^*-\overline\lambda I)=\mathbb C(1,\overline\lambda,\overline\lambda^2,\ldots)\). The operator \(S^*-\lambda I\) is surjective: \(R_\lambda=S(I-\lambda S)^{-1}\) is a bounded right inverse, and its kernel is \(\mathbb C k_\lambda\), where \(k_\lambda=(1,\lambda,\lambda^2,\ldots)\).

For prescribed \((u,v)\), solve \((S^*-\lambda I)y=v\) as \(y=R_\lambda v+ak_\lambda\). The remaining equation \((S-\lambda I)x=u-D_cR_\lambda v-aD_ck_\lambda\) is solvable if and only if \(L_\lambda(u-D_cR_\lambda v)=aF_c(\lambda^2)\). Hence \(T_c-\lambda I\) is invertible exactly when \(F_c(\lambda^2)\ne0\). When the scalar vanishes, the nullspace is one-dimensional (the choice of \(a\)); because the block is Fredholm of index zero, it is also non-surjective. Its eigenvector may be exhibited explicitly for \(\lambda\ne0\): choose \(y_n=\lambda^n\), \(x_n=\lambda^{-n-1}\sum_{j=0}^nc_j\lambda^{2j}\). Since \(F_c(\lambda^2)=0\), the partial sum equals the negative tail, and the bound \(|x_n|\le\|c\|_\infty|\lambda|^{n+1}/(1-|\lambda|^2)\) proves \(x\in H\). As \(c_0\ne0\), \(\lambda=0\) is not a zero.

**Circle and exterior.** For \(|\lambda|>1\), both triangular diagonal blocks are invertible, so \(T_c-\lambda I\) is invertible. Every \(|\lambda|=1\) belongs to the Fredholm essential spectrum: choose unit vectors supported on successively remote, long coordinate intervals that are approximate eigenvectors of \(S\) for \(\lambda\), with weak limit zero. Their lifts \((x,0)\) are a Weyl singular sequence for \(T_c-\lambda I\), ruling out Fredholmness. Conversely, the two shift diagonal blocks are Fredholm for \(|\lambda|<1\) with indices \(-1\) and \(+1\), and for \(|\lambda|>1\) they are invertible; thus the triangular operator is Fredholm off the circle. This completes the spectrum and essential-spectrum formulas.

**Sharp positive threshold.** For \(c_n=t+h\mathbf1_{\{n=1\}}\), \(F_c(z)=t/(1-z)+hz\). If \(h=0\), it has no zero in \(\mathbb D\). If \(h>0\), the numerator \(t+hz-hz^2\) has roots \(z_\pm=(1\pm\sqrt{1+4t/h})/2\). The positive root exceeds one. The negative root is strictly greater than \(-1\) if and only if \(h>t/2\). When it lies in \(\mathbb D\), its two square roots give exactly the displayed purely imaginary eigenvalues. At equality the negative root reaches the unit circle and produces no interior eigenvalue.

**Same singular data, different complex spectra.** Let the first positive coupling have a single coefficient \(2\) at index \(1\), and the second at index \(2\); all other coefficients are \(1\). In the domain, separate the vector \((0,e_0)\) and the mutually orthogonal two-dimensional blocks spanned by \((e_{n-1},0)\) and \((0,e_n)\), \(n\ge1\). Their images are respectively the one-dimensional output line \((e_0,0)\) and the blocks spanned by \((e_n,0)\), \((0,e_{n-1})\). On those blocks the matrices are \(J_{c_n}=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&c_n\\0&1\end{psmallmatrix}\). This is a two-sided unitary equivalence, *not* a unitary similarity; it proves that the complete singular-value multisets for the two couplings coincide because the multisets \(\{c_n:n\ge1\}\) coincide. The generating-function numerators for the first and second couplings are respectively \(1+z-z^2\) and \(1+z^2-z^3\). The first has one zero in \(\mathbb D\). The second has one positive real root \(r>1\), while its remaining roots are a nonreal conjugate pair with product \(1/r\), hence both lie in \(\mathbb D\). Therefore their interior eigenvalue counts are two and four. (For the second polynomial, \(z^3-z^2-1\) has negative values at both critical points and exactly one real root above one.)

## Relationship to prior work
Ji and Wang's 2026 treatment of conjugate bi-geometric block operators establishes an invariant-subspace theorem for inverses under positive coupling and gives an inverse formula at zero; it does not give the spectral zero criterion across the disk or the sharp spike-induced pair of eigenvalues. The 2026 Fang--Hou--Jiang--Zhang reduction concerns invariant subspaces, not this location of nonnormal eigenvalues. Older general work on spectra of upper triangular operator matrices supplies a broader abstract framework, but the exact generating function for positive diagonal couplings, its threshold, and the isosingular/nonisospectral comparison are not identified in the accessible material. Unlike singular-spectrum information, the present complex spectrum depends on the *position* of a positive diagonal spike, not just its magnitude.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns the unilateral shift and its adjoint with diagonal bounded coupling. It does not classify nondiagonal couplings, algebraic multiplicities, boundary residual types, or invariant subspaces of general inverses. It does not settle Halmos's third problem. The primary comparison is based on the accessible 2026 paper; an older equivalent disguised as a spectral Schur complement cannot be ruled out without further independent bibliographic assessment.
