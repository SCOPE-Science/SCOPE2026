# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof uses the exact defect pairing between the one-dimensional kernel of \(S^*-\lambda I\) and the codimension-one range of \(S-\lambda I\). The scalar condition \(F_c(\lambda^2)=0\) is necessary and sufficient inside the disk, with explicit square-summable eigenvectors. Fredholm indices cancel off the circle; a remote-supported Weyl sequence proves the circle is essential spectrum. The positive spike root condition is algebraically exact. A two-sided (not similar) block decomposition independently verifies the identical singular-value multisets. Numerical truncations are supporting checks only.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge
Compared with Ji--Wang's full accessible 2026 manuscript and its explicit inverse formula, the spectral zero criterion is not asserted or derived there. Their positivity hypothesis guarantees zero is in the resolvent, not that the entire open disk is free of eigenvalues. The new example shows positive coupling can exhibit an interior eigenvalue bifurcation and that identical singular numbers do not determine the complex spectrum. The relevant prior abstract on upper triangular spectral theory is broader but the exact analytic zero characterization and sharp threshold were not located. This is not a proof of publication priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is naturally motivated by the newly studied conjugate bi-geometric operator blocks. The result completely describes their spectra within the natural diagonal subclass, identifies an exact instability threshold under a one-site positive perturbation, and shows why a complete modulus-singular-value classification is insufficient for nonnormal spectral analysis. The zero criterion supports analysis of many other positive and complex sequences, beyond a single numerical toy example.

## Closest literature and limits
Ji--Wang (arXiv:2610.39605v1) proves inverse intransitivity under a strictly positive coupling and discusses the defect scalar at zero, but not the disk-wide characteristic function. Fang et al. (arXiv:2610.11202v1) concerns invariant-subspace reductions. Benhida--Zerouali--Zguitti (2005) treats abstract triangular spectral behavior; a possible hidden specialization remains a priority risk. Nothing here classifies arbitrary couplings, generalized eigenspaces or Halmos's problem.
