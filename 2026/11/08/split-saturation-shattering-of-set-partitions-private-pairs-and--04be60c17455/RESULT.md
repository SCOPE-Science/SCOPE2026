# Split-saturation shattering of set partitions: private pairs and Cayley trees

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\ge 1\) and the labeled set \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). A *split* is an unordered bipartition \(S\mid S^c\) into two nonempty shores. The concept class consists of every set partition \(\pi\) of \([n]\), with response \(h_\pi(S\mid S^c)=1\) exactly when every block of \(\pi\) is contained in one of the two shores (equivalently, \(S\) is a union of blocks). We identify complementary shores as one query.

**Shattering criterion.** A family \(F\) of distinct split queries is shattered if and only if, for each \(Q\in F\), there exists a distinct pair \(x_Q,y_Q\in[n]\) separated by \(Q\) and by no other split of \(F\). Call such a pair a *private crossing pair*. Equivalently, choosing one shore of each split, the resulting family is irredundant in the finite Boolean algebra \(\mathcal P([n])\): no chosen shore belongs to the subalgebra generated by the other shores. Complementing individual chosen shores does not change this condition.

**Exact extremal structure.** For \(n\ge2\), every shattered split family has at most \(n-1\) members, and its size is \(n-1\) exactly when it is the set of fundamental edge splits of a uniquely determined tree with labeled vertex set \([n]\). Hence there are exactly \(n^{n-2}\) shattered supports of maximum size. Furthermore,
\[
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal C_n)=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal C_n)=n-1\quad(n\ge1).
\]

With \(q_n=2^{n-1}-1\) distinct split queries, the shattering polynomial \(\Phi_n(z)=\sum_{F\text{ shattered}}z^{|F|}\) satisfies, for \(n\ge3\),
\[
[z^0]\Phi_n=1,\quad [z]\Phi_n=q_n,\quad [z^2]\Phi_n=\binom{q_n}{2},\quad [z^{n-1}]\Phi_n=n^{n-2}.
\]
For \(n=2\), \(\Phi_2(z)=1+z\); for \(n=1\), \(\Phi_1(z)=1\). Explicit exhaustive computations give
\[
\Phi_4(z)=1+7z+21z^2+16z^3,\qquad
\Phi_5(z)=1+15z+105z^2+300z^3+125z^4.
\]
The complex need not be pure: among inclusion-maximal shattered supports, there are respectively \(3\) of size \(2\) and \(16\) of size \(3\) when \(n=4\), and \(140\) of size \(3\) and \(125\) of size \(4\) when \(n=5\).

## Assumptions and scope

Concepts range over **all** partitions into any positive number of nonempty blocks; no restrictions on block sizes or number of blocks are imposed. A positive answer means a split is respected by the partition, not that a shore is itself exactly one block. Queries are nontrivial unordered splits, not pairwise same-block tests and not exact-block tests. Standard VC shattering and the standard complete binary mistake-tree definition of Littlestone dimension are used. Results for \(n=1\) adopt the unique empty split-query family and dimensions zero. The structural and dimension theorems hold for all integers \(n\ge1\); the displayed small face polynomials and facet counts are finite computations, not asserted as formulas for arbitrary \(n\).

## Proof

**1. Necessity of private crossing pairs.** Suppose \(F\) is shattered. Fix \(Q\in F\) and select a partition realizing label zero on \(Q\) and label one on every other split of \(F\). Because \(Q\) is not respected, some partition block contains elements \(x,y\) on opposite shores of \(Q\). Each other split is respected, so that same block, and hence \(x,y\), lie together on its shore. This is a private crossing pair for \(Q\).

**2. Sufficiency.** Conversely, choose a private crossing pair \((x_Q,y_Q)\) for each \(Q\in F\). Fix any desired binary responses and create a graph on \([n]\) whose edges are precisely the chosen witness pairs of the queries assigned response zero. Take its connected components as the blocks of a set partition. Every split assigned response one leaves both endpoints of each graph edge on the same shore, because the edge is private to a different query. Thus every graph component stays within a shore and the split is respected. Each split assigned response zero has its own crossing edge inside a block and is not respected. Therefore all binary response patterns occur.

**3. Boolean-algebra equivalence.** The subalgebra generated by shore sets \(\{S_R:R\in F\setminus\{Q\}\}\) consists exactly of unions of atoms of the partition of \([n]\) by their simultaneous membership signatures. The shore \(S_Q\) belongs to this subalgebra precisely when it is constant on each such atom, i.e., precisely when no pair has equal signatures on all other splits and different membership at \(Q\). This is exactly failure of a private crossing pair. Hence the criterion is equivalent to classical Boolean-algebra irredundance, a pre-existing notion, not a newly defined invariant.

**4. Forest lemma.** Choose one private pair for each \(Q\in F\), drawing it as an undirected edge labeled \(Q\). No two labels can use the same edge, since each private edge is separated by exactly its own label. Suppose these edges contain a cycle. Along any edge bearing label \(Q\), membership in a chosen shore of \(Q\) flips; along all other edges on that cycle, membership in the same shore stays constant. Traversing the cycle yields one flip and hence cannot return to the starting membership value. Contradiction. Thus the private-pair edges form a forest and \(|F|\le n-1\).

**5. Equality and tree reconstruction.** If \(|F|=n-1\), its private-pair forest is a spanning tree \(T\). For each edge labeled \(Q\), the split \(Q\) separates that edge's endpoints but separates no other tree-edge endpoints. Consequently each connected component of \(T-Q\) lies on one shore, and because the edge crosses \(Q\), these components are exactly the two shores of \(Q\). Thus \(F\) is the family of all fundamental edge splits of \(T\). Conversely, given any labeled tree, its fundamental edge cuts have private crossing pairs at their corresponding edge endpoints and hence are shattered. Their split family determines the tree uniquely: for all vertices \(x,y\), the number of splits in \(F\) that separate \(x\) and \(y\) equals the graph distance \(d_T(x,y)\), so the pairs at distance one recover all edges. Cayley's classical labeled-tree formula now yields \(n^{n-2}\) maximum-size shattered split families.

**6. Exact online dimension.** Static shattering of \(n-1\) edge splits gives a complete adaptive mistake tree of depth \(n-1\) by asking the same fixed queries in order, so \(\operatorname{Ldim}\ge n-1\). For the reverse inequality, follow the all-positive branch of any complete adaptive mistake tree of depth \(d\). After \(j\) positive responses, the common refinement of the \(j\) queried splits has \(a_j\) nonempty atoms, initially \(a_0=1\). Before querying a new split, every partition consistent with previous positive responses refines that common atom partition. If the new split were a union of existing atoms, all such partitions would necessarily respect it and its negative child would be empty, contradicting completeness. Hence the new split strictly subdivides at least one atom and \(a_j\ge a_{j-1}+1\). Since \(a_j\le n\), \(d\le n-1\). The usual VC-to-Littlestone inequality supplies the matching lower bound as above.

**7. Enumerative consequences.** There are \(q_n=2^{n-1}-1\) unordered nontrivial splits. Every single split is shattered: its own crossing pair is private. Two distinct splits cannot generate one another as Boolean subalgebras with a single generator, because their shore sets are neither equal nor complementary; equivalently, each has a private crossing pair relative to the other. Thus every two-query support is shattered. At maximal size, the tree correspondence gives the top coefficient. For the nonpurity witness at \(n=4\), the two splits with designated shores \(\{1,3\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\) are shattered, and the four ground elements have four different two-bit membership signatures; no third split can have a private crossing pair that agrees on both existing splits, so this two-face is maximal. The exhaustive finite data for \(n\le5\) are verified by the accompanying program.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_partition_splits.py` with Python 3 and no external dependencies; success ends with `PARTITION_SPLITS_PASS`. The program independently enumerates every set partition and every split-query support for each \(n\le5\), tests **all** support-label traces directly against the private-pair criterion, generates all Prüfer-code trees and compares their edge-cut families to every maximum-size shattered support, and enumerates all inclusion-maximal shattered supports. It separately evaluates the exact Littlestone recurrence for \(n\le4\). It checks 1, 2, 8, 128, and 32,768 possible query supports for \(n=1,2,3,4,5\), respectively. The finite calculations are checks of the independent infinite-parameter proofs, not replacements for them.

## Relationship to prior work

DePavia, Medrano Martín del Campo, and Tani's arXiv:2305.13402 (first public version May 22, 2023) and Liu and Mukherjee's arXiv:2112.07897 consider learning partitions through **pairwise** same-cluster and related graph oracles. Those queries are not equivalent to asking whether an arbitrary split is respected, so their bounds do not provide this shattering complex, its top Cayley enumeration, or the exact online dimension for these queries. The 2023 article *Injective Split Systems* considers Buneman-graph and median properties of split systems, not hidden-partition saturation traces.

The Boolean-algebra concept of **irredundant families** is classical: see Koppelberg, *Handbook of Boolean Algebras*, vol. 1 (1989), Section 4.4, especially Definition 4.21 and Proposition 4.23. Thus the private-pair characterization also gives an elementary finite realization of an existing algebraic notion; we do **not** claim irredundance itself is new, and existing classical general properties (including maximal-irredundant generation) remain part of prior coverage. The proposed additional contribution is the partition-query VC/online interpretation, the exact labeled-tree classification and count of highest-dimensional faces, and the nonpure facet data. We found no explicit statement of this package in the inspected material, but complete historical priority has not been established.

## Limitations

The theorem uses unrestricted set partitions and all nontrivial unordered split queries; it need not hold for prescribed block counts, restricted shores, or noisy oracles. The face polynomials through five elements and their facet profiles are established by complete small finite enumeration only; no all-order formula for their middle coefficients or the number of all facets is asserted. Established Boolean-algebra results may imply parts of this result through further deductions, and older or differently named treatments of irredundant generators may establish stronger overlap. Originality is a literature-supported assessment, not proof of historical uniqueness. Mathematical verification here is a self-review and finite computational replay, not independent certification.
