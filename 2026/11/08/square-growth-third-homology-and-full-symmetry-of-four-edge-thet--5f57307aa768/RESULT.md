# Square-growth third homology and full symmetry of four-edge theta Rips filtrations

## Finding

For each integer \(m\geq3\), let \(T_m\) be the finite graph consisting of \(m\) internally disjoint paths of four edges between two common poles, with unit edge lengths. Give its \(3m+2\) vertices the intrinsic shortest-path metric. Its **inclusive** Vietoris–Rips filtration has complete integral homotopy classification

\[
\operatorname{VR}_{\leq r}(T_m)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{3m+2}\{*\},&0\leq r<1,\\
\bigvee^{m-1}S^1,&1\leq r<3,\\
\bigvee^{(m-1)^2}S^3,&3\leq r<4,\\
\{*\},&r\geq4.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus the reduced integral barcode has exactly \(3m+1\) degree-zero bars on \([0,1)\), \(m-1\) degree-one bars on \([1,3)\), and \((m-1)^2\) degree-three bars on \([3,4)\), with no other bars or torsion.

The **full graph automorphism group** is \(\Gamma_m\cong\mathfrak S_m\times C_2\). Set \(A_m=\{(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\in\mathbb Z^m:\sum_i z_i=0\}\), the integral augmentation lattice under permutations of the arms, and let \(\varepsilon\) denote the sign character of the pole-switching involution. Its integral homology representations at their nonzero scales are

\[
\widetilde H_1(\operatorname{VR}_{\leq1}(T_m);\mathbb Z)
\cong \widetilde H_1(\operatorname{VR}_{\leq2}(T_m);\mathbb Z)
\cong A_m\boxtimes\varepsilon,
\]
\[
\widetilde H_3(\operatorname{VR}_{\leq3}(T_m);\mathbb Z)
\cong(A_m\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A_m)\boxtimes\mathbf1.
\]

In particular, for an arm permutation \(\sigma\), with \(f(\sigma)\) fixed arms, and pole-swap exponent \(e\in\{0,1\}\), the two nonzero rational homology characters are \((-1)^e(f(\sigma)-1)\) in degree one and \((f(\sigma)-1)^2\) in degree three. The invariant integral third homology has rank one, generated by the **primitive** matrix \(mI_m-J_m\) when the tensor product is embedded as matrices with zero row and column sums. The symmetry quotient of the scale-three complex has \(\widetilde H_3(-;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q\) and all its other reduced rational homology zero.

## Assumptions and scope

Precisely, the vertex set of \(T_m\) is \(\{p,q\}\cup\{a_i,b_i,c_i:1\leq i\leq m\}\); its only edges are \(p a_i\), \(a_i b_i\), \(b_i c_i\), and \(c_i q\) for each \(i\). All vertices are distinct, except that the poles are common to the arms. Distances are among **graph vertices**, not among edge-interior points of the continuous metric realization. The inclusive complex at scale \(r\) has a simplex for each vertex set of pairwise distance at most \(r\); no radius-doubling convention is used. The full symmetry assertion assumes \(m\ge3\), when the two poles are exactly the vertices of degree greater than two.

## Proof

**1. Small scales and girth.** The graph is connected, triangle-free, has \(3m+2\) vertices and \(4m\) edges, and its smallest cycles have length eight (two distinct four-edge arms). Therefore its scale-one Rips complex is the graph itself, a wedge of \(4m-(3m+2)+1=m-1\) circles. Its scale-zero complex consists of isolated vertices. Adamaszek's girth-collapse theorem [1, Proposition 2.2] states that the scale-\(r\) clique complex of a graph with girth at least \(3r+1\) collapses to its scale-one complex when \(r\) is a positive integer. For \(r=2\), this needs girth at least seven; here the girth is eight. Hence the natural inclusion of the scale-one complex into the scale-two complex is a homotopy equivalence, inducing an isomorphism on integral first homology. All scales between consecutive integers give the same simplicial complex.

**2. Exact distance-three nonedges.** Every two graph vertices have distance at most four. The distinct unordered pairs at distance exactly four are **precisely**

\[
\{p,q\},\qquad \{b_i,b_j\}\ (i\ne j),\qquad \{a_i,c_j\}\ (i\ne j).
\]

Indeed, one has distance two within each of the \(a\)- and \(c\)-layers; the distance from either pole to any internal vertex is at most three; \(d(a_i,b_j)\leq3\) through \(p\), and \(d(c_i,b_j)\leq3\) through \(q\). The same-arm distances are at most two, while any path from \(b_i\) to \(b_j\), or from \(a_i\) to \(c_j\) for distinct arms, has at least four edges and has an explicit length-four route. The poles are joined by each four-edge arm, and there is no shorter route. These cases exhaust all pairs.

**3. A canonical simplicial join.** Let \(P\) denote the two-point complex on \(p,q\), let \(B\) denote the \(m\)-point complex on the middle vertices \(b_1,\ldots,b_m\), and let \(L\) be the induced clique complex on the vertices \(a_i,c_i\). By Step 2, every pair of vertices from different blocks \(P,B,L\) has graph distance at most three; within \(P\) and \(B\), no distinct vertices are adjacent in the threshold graph. Within the third block, the \(a\)-layer and \(c\)-layer each span a simplex, and the only cross-layer edges are the \(m\) matching edges \(\{a_i,c_i\}\). There are no cross-layer triangles. Therefore, not merely up to homotopy but as abstract simplicial complexes,

\[
\operatorname{VR}_{\leq3}(T_m)=P*B*L,
\qquad
L=\Delta(\{a_i\})\cup\Delta(\{c_i\})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}[a_i,c_i].
\]

Collapsing each of the two disjoint contractible full simplices of \(L\) to a vertex is a homotopy equivalence, since each inclusion is a CW cofibration. The quotient is a graph with two vertices joined by \(m\) parallel edges, so \(L\simeq\bigvee^{m-1}S^1\). Also \(P\cong S^0\) and \(B\) is a wedge of \(m-1\) zero-spheres, with a chosen basepoint. The join description, together with the standard join/suspension identity, yields

\[
P*B*L\simeq \bigvee^{(m-1)^2}S^3.
\]

All these equivalences are integral homotopy equivalences, and no assumption based on a finite computation enters the argument. At scale four the graph diameter bound makes every vertex set a simplex, so the complex is contractible. The inclusions and absence of intermediate scales give the stated integral bar endpoints.

**4. Integral equivariant structure.** For \(m\ge3\), graph automorphisms must preserve or interchange the two degree-\(m\) poles. They then permute the \(m\) paths independently of that interchange, so \(\Gamma_m\cong\mathfrak S_m\times C_2\). Orient all four-edge arms from \(p\) to \(q\). The graph cycle lattice consists exactly of integer linear combinations of the oriented arms with coefficient sum zero: this identifies its first homology equivariantly with \(A_m\boxtimes\varepsilon\). The scale-one inclusion induces an equivariant isomorphism onto scale-two first homology by Step 1.

For the complex \(L\), orient each inter-simplex bridge \([a_i,c_i]\) from the \(a\)-layer to the \(c\)-layer. The bridge-coefficient map gives an **integral and equivariant** identification \(H_1(L;\mathbb Z)\cong A_m\). Indeed, a one-cycle has bridge coefficients summing to zero, and any such vector is realizable using paths inside the contractible two simplex pieces; a cycle with all bridge coefficients zero is nullhomologous because both pieces are contractible. Arm permutations permute the coefficients. The pole interchange exchanges the two simplex layers and reverses each oriented bridge, so it acts as minus the identity on \(H_1(L;\mathbb Z)\). That involution likewise interchanges the two vertices of \(P\), acting by minus the identity on \(\widetilde H_0(P;\mathbb Z)\), while it fixes the middle vertices of \(B\). The natural reduced-join Künneth isomorphism, with no Tor terms, gives

\[
\widetilde H_3(P*B*L;\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H_0(P;\mathbb Z)\otimes
\widetilde H_0(B;\mathbb Z)\otimes H_1(L;\mathbb Z)
\cong A_m\otimes A_m.
\]

The two minus signs under pole interchange cancel. A permutation of arms acts by the tensor square of its augmentation-lattice action. Its trace is the square of \(f(\sigma)-1\), and the earlier first-homology trace follows from the unsquared augmentation action and the sign of the pole interchange.

**5. Invariant class and rational quotient.** Identify \(A_m\otimes A_m\) with integral \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrices having every row and column sum zero. A matrix fixed by simultaneous permutations of rows and columns has a common diagonal coefficient and a common off-diagonal coefficient. The row-sum constraint forces the invariant lattice to be generated by \(mI_m-J_m\). This matrix is primitive, since it has entries \(-1\) off the diagonal. Finally, finite-group rational transfer after barycentric subdivision (which removes simplex inversions) identifies quotient rational homology with the rational invariants. Since the only nonzero positive reduced homology of the scale-three complex occurs in degree three, the quotient has the claimed single rational degree-three class and no other reduced rational homology. This completes the proof.

## Verification

A standalone Python-standard-library checker, `verify_theta_rips.py`, constructs the graph and independently computes breadth-first-search distances and every Rips simplex at scales one, two and three for \(2\le m\le7\). It performs exact mod-two boundary-rank elimination, checks the complete list of distance-four pairs, the scale-three face-count formula

\[
\#\{\text{nonempty faces}\}=3(m+1)(2^{m+1}+m-1)-1,
\]

and representative signed simplicial Lefschetz traces under arm and pole automorphisms. The archived `verification_output.txt` is the literal stdout of running the standalone checker, ending in `ALL_CHECKS_PASS`. These finite checks corroborate Steps 1–5 but do **not** prove the all-\(m\) theorem or its integral module structure; those follow from the explicit distance classification, simplicial join and integral chain arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Adamaszek [1] studies graph-power clique complexes, proving the girth-based collapse used at scale two and classifying powers of single cycles. These results do not determine the radius-three theta graph with overlapping length-eight cycles. Adamaszek and Adams [2] establish sphere transitions for subsets of a circle, not branching parallel-path graphs. The metric-gluing results of Adamaszek and collaborators [3] apply to certain sufficiently short shared paths. Here the common four-edge arms are long relative to the eight-edge cycles, and the general gluing theorem does not supply the displayed join or the quadratic homology character.

A previously recorded **three-edge theta** calculation [4] has \(m-1\) two-dimensional spheres at scale two and a linear-rank augmentation representation. The present four-edge family instead has \((m-1)^2\) three-dimensional spheres at scale three, a tensor-square action and a primitive invariant, and cannot be obtained by equating the graph or scale parameters. A recorded girth-six classification under edge-disjoint hexagons [5] does not apply to these girth-eight graphs at scale three. Related published-finding corpus searches covered the graph-power, multi-theta, crown-graph independence, integral representation and exact barcode formulations. Absence of an exact hit is **not** treated as a proof of novelty; the comparisons identify differences in objects, dimensions, and hypotheses.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes all arms have exactly four graph edges, uses only graph vertices and unit shortest-path distances, and treats inclusive thresholds. It does not classify nonuniform paths, continuous metric-graph Rips complexes, arbitrary graph powers, or the integral homology of the quotient. The exceptional two-arm case is an eight-cycle whose homotopy calculation fits the formula, but it has a larger automorphism group and is excluded from the stated full-group representation. An unindexed or implicit equivalent classification remains possible; independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
