# Square-law classification for unweighted two-shell Walsh sampling

## Finding
Let \(n\ge2\), \(\Omega_n=\{-1,1\}^n\), and let \(S_k\subset\Omega_n\) be the complete Hamming shell of sign vectors with exactly \(k\) negative coordinates. For \(0\le k<\ell\le n\), put \(Y=S_k\cup S_\ell\). Consider the first Walsh band \(\mathcal H_1=\operatorname{span}\{1,x_1,\dots,x_n\}\), sampled with **equal weight at every distinct point of** \(Y\). Its normalized Gram matrix is
\[
G_{k,\ell}=\frac1{|Y|}\sum_{x\in Y}(1,x_1,\ldots,x_n)^\mathsf T(1,x_1,\ldots,x_n).
\]
The following is a complete classification: \(G_{k,\ell}=I_{n+1}\) if and only if one of the two disjoint cases holds.

**Square dimension.** \(n=s^2\) with integer \(s\ge2\), and the **unique** admissible pair is
\[
\boxed{(k,\ell)=\left(\frac{n-s}{2},\frac{n+s}{2}\right).}
\]

**One below a square.** \(n=d(d+2)=(d+1)^2-1\) with integer \(d\ge1\), and the **only** admissible pairs are the two complements
\[
\boxed{(k,\ell)=\left(\frac{n-d}{2},\frac{n+d+2}{2}\right),\quad
\left(\frac{n-d-2}{2},\frac{n+d}{2}\right).}
\]
No two complete Hamming shells yield an equal-weight tight first-band frame at any other dimension. For instance, dimensions \(3,4,8,9,15,16,24,25,\ldots\) work, whereas \(2,5,6,7,10,\ldots\) do not.

There is a strict third-order distinction. For \(n\ge3\), the tight square-dimensional pairs are also **three-wise independent** under the uniform distribution on \(Y\) (all nonconstant Walsh coefficients of degree at most three vanish). For either near-square pair, every distinct-coordinate cubic Walsh moment equals
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_{Y}(x_i x_j x_h)=\pm\frac{2}{(n-1)(n-2)}}\qquad (i,j,h\text{ distinct}),
\]
with opposite signs for the two complementary pairs; hence they are two-wise but not three-wise independent. The square-dimensional pair is the only two-shell uniform three-wise-independent design for \(n\ge3\).

More generally, the complete real Gram spectrum is explicit for **every** pair of distinct shells. Write
\[
A=\binom nk,\quad B=\binom n\ell,\quad
\alpha=\frac{A(n-2k)+B(n-2\ell)}{A+B},\quad
\beta=\frac{A(n-2k)^2+B(n-2\ell)^2}{A+B},\quad
\rho=\frac{\beta-n}{n(n-1)}.
\]
Then the \(n+1\) eigenvalues, counting multiplicities, are
\[
\boxed{1-\rho\ \text{(multiplicity }n-1\text{)},\qquad
\lambda_\pm=\frac{1+\beta/n\pm\sqrt{(1-\beta/n)^2+4\alpha^2/n}}2\ \text{(once each)}.}
\]
The restriction map is injective precisely when \((k,\ell)\ne(0,n)\). The positive spectrum determines the exact optimal sampling and condition-number constants in the first Walsh band.

## Assumptions and scope
All sets are complete shells, without repetition; every point in their union receives identical counting weight. The Fourier model is precisely the \((n+1)\)-dimensional subspace generated by constant and degree-one Boolean characters, with the ambient uniform inner product on \(\Omega_n\). Tightness refers to \(G_{k,\ell}\) being a positive scalar multiple of identity. Since every diagonal entry of this normalized Gram matrix is one, that scalar necessarily equals one. Equivalently, the indicator of \(Y\), normalized to a probability measure, has zero Fourier coefficients at every weight-one or weight-two nonzero character.

The exact spectrum applies for \(n\ge2\), including endpoint shells and non-tight pairs. The third-order statement assumes \(n\ge3\). The classification does not allow separate adjustable per-shell weights or arbitrary smaller subsets of the shells.

## Proof
**1. The exact Gram matrix and eigenvalues.** Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\) be uniform on \(Y\) and put \(T=\sum_iX_i\). Conditional on shell \(S_j\), \(T=n-2j\), so \(\mathbb ET=\alpha\) and \(\mathbb ET^2=\beta\). By exchangeability, \(\mathbb EX_i=\alpha/n\) and \(\mathbb EX_iX_h=\rho\) for distinct coordinates; the latter follows by expanding \(T^2=n+\sum_{i\ne h}X_iX_h\). Thus
\[
G_{k,\ell}=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&(\alpha/n)\mathbf1^\mathsf T\\
(\alpha/n)\mathbf1&(1-\rho)I_n+\rho\mathbf1\mathbf1^\mathsf T
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The coordinate-sum-zero space has eigenvalue \(1-\rho\) with multiplicity \(n-1\). On the orthonormal span of the constant and normalized coordinate sum, the matrix becomes
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha/\sqrt n\\\alpha/\sqrt n&\beta/n\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose two eigenvalues are precisely the displayed \(\lambda_\pm\).

Its determinant is \((\beta-\alpha^2)/n\), which is strictly positive because \(T\) assumes two distinct values with positive probability. Also \(1-\rho=\frac{n^2-\beta}{n(n-1)}\) is positive unless \(T^2=n^2\) almost surely, meaning the two shells are exactly \(S_0,S_n\). This proves the injectivity claim and the complete spectrum.

**2. Reduction of tightness to a binomial equality.** By the block matrix, \(G=I\) exactly when \(\alpha=0\) and \(\beta=n\). The two shell sums must therefore have opposite signs. Write their values as \(a>0\) and \(-b<0\); these are positive integers of the same parity as \(n\), and \(0<a,b\le n\). The mean-zero condition fixes the two probabilities to \(b/(a+b)\) and \(a/(a+b)\). Under those weights, the second moment equals \(ab\). Thus tightness is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{ab=n,\qquad a\binom n{(n-a)/2}=b\binom n{(n-b)/2}.}\tag{1}
\]
These equations are necessary and sufficient, not merely necessary: the binomial factors are the actual shell sizes, so the second equality realizes the required mixture probabilities.

**3. The binomial rigidity lemma.** Define \(F(t)=t\binom n{(n-t)/2}\) for positive allowable \(t\). For \(0<t<n\), the consecutive ratio is
\[
R(t)=\frac{F(t+2)}{F(t)}=\frac{(t+2)(n-t)}{t(n+t+2)}.
\]
Suppose \(ab=n\) and, without loss of generality, \(a\le b\). Put \(r=(b-a)/2\), an integer. When \(r=0\), equality of the two terms is automatic and \(n=a^2\). When \(r=1\), direct substitution of \(n=a(a+2)\) gives \(R(a)=1\), hence \(F(b)=F(a)\).

For \(r\ge2\), pair each factor \(R(t)\) in \(F(b)/F(a)=\prod_{t=a,a+2,\ldots,b-2}R(t)\) with \(R(v)\), where \(v=a+b-2-t\). Set \(z=t-a\), so \(0\le z\le 2r-2\). Direct multiplication and \(n=ab\) yield the exact identity
\[
1-R(t)R(v)=\frac{2n(a+b)\bigl(2(r-1)(z+1)-z^2\bigr)}{t(n+t+2)v(n+v+2)}.\tag{2}
\]
All denominators are positive. The concave quadratic in parentheses has value \(2(r-1)>0\) at **both** endpoints \(z=0\) and \(z=2r-2\), and hence is strictly positive throughout the intervening interval. Every paired product is therefore strictly less than one; if the number of factors is odd, the unpaired central factor also satisfies \(R(t)^2<1\), hence \(R(t)<1\). It follows that \(F(b)<F(a)\) whenever \(b-a\ge4\). Consequently the second equality in (1) holds **if and only if** \(a=b\) or \(|a-b|=2\).

**4. Enumerating all cases.** With \(ab=n\), the equal case gives \(a=b=\sqrt n\) and the single symmetric pair of shell indices in the first box. The unequal case gives \(\{a,b\}=\{d,d+2\}\) and \(n=d(d+2)\). Its two orders give exactly the two shell pairs in the second box. All indices are integers: \(n\), \(a\), and \(b\) have compatible parity in both families. They lie in \([0,n]\). The lemma excludes every other pair.

**5. Third-order rigidity.** If \(n\ge3\) and \(G=I\), then \(\mathbb ET=0\), \(\mathbb ET^2=n\). On the two-point distribution supported on \(a\) and \(-b\), direct calculation gives \(\mathbb ET^3=ab(a-b)=n(a-b)\). Expanding \(T^3\) and using \(\mathbb ET=0\) shows
\[
\mathbb ET^3=n(n-1)(n-2)\,\mathbb E(X_1X_2X_3),
\]
so the distinct-coordinate cubic moment equals \((a-b)/[(n-1)(n-2)]\). It vanishes in the square case and equals \(\pm2/[(n-1)(n-2)]\) in the near-square case. Every lower nonconstant Walsh moment already vanishes by tightness. Exchangeability proves that these are **all** degree-three coefficients, completing the classification.

`CHECK.py` is a standalone Python 3 standard-library program. Run `python CHECK.py` from the directory containing this file. The expected marker is `CHECK_OK assertions=70325`. The program tests the full exact classification for every \(2\le n\le100\), checks actual Gram entries computed by enumeration of all Boolean vectors for every pair of shells with \(2\le n\le10\), checks the third-order moments at all tight cases in that range, and verifies the rational factorization (2) across applicable divisors through \(n=600\). All tests use integer arithmetic and rational fractions; no numerical tolerance enters. These finite checks are supplementary: steps 1–5 above establish the universal claim for every integer \(n\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work
The standard equivalence between strength-two binary orthogonal arrays and vanishing low-degree Walsh coefficients supplies the terminology. Bannai, Bannai and Zhu study relative designs on Hamming shells and characterize structural consequences of relative-design conditions; their notion of a *tight relative design* concerns attaining Fisher-type cardinality bounds, **not** a scalar Gram matrix on the constant-plus-coordinate Walsh band under equal weights on a union of complete shells. Weinberger studies asymptotic noisy random sampling thresholds for low-degree Boolean subspaces; that result does not classify deterministic, equal-weight sampling on two entire shells. Xie, Wang and Wu study codes from unions of weight layers, including self-orthogonality over the two-element field (parity of integer inner products), which is distinct from **exact real** first-band Gram tightness.

Two other published harmonic-analysis calculations closest in object are *Exact q-ary Hamming-shell Fourier concentration and the arithmetic tightness criterion*, which studies one complete shell and explicitly excludes shell unions, and *Exact first-Walsh-band concentration spectra on Boolean Hamming balls*, which concerns cumulative sets rather than two isolated shells. Their methods motivate the Gram decomposition, but neither states the all-dimension square/near-square classification, the complete list of unweighted two-shell optimizers, or the third-order dichotomy. The exchangeability identities alone are classical; the arithmetic rigidity of equation (1) and its exact cubic consequences constitute the claimed contribution.

## Limitations
This is an exact theorem about complete Boolean shells and equal **per-point** weights. It does not assert that these sets minimize the number of sample points among arbitrary Fourier designs, nor does it classify optional shell-dependent reweighting, higher Walsh bands, nonbinary alphabets, or arbitrary irregular sample subsets. The polynomial identity (2) is proved directly rather than inferred from an experiment. Prior literature might contain an unindexed reformulation through orthogonal arrays, Krawtchouk moment quadrature, or exchangeable pairwise independence; the source inspection is evidence-based rather than a guarantee of global priority.
