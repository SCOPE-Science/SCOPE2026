# Square-localized spheres in closed-neighborhood complexes of triangle-free graphs

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite, connected, simple, triangle-free graph with \(v\) vertices, \(e\) edges, and \(q\geq1\) distinct unoriented four-cycles. Assume that **every graph edge belongs to at most one four-cycle** (all four-cycles, not just a selected packing). Let \(\mathcal C(G)\) be the simplicial complex generated by the vertex sets of the closed neighborhoods \(N_G[u]=\{u\}\cup N_G(u)\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C(G)\simeq\bigvee^{e-v+1-q}S^1\ \vee\ \bigvee^q S^2.}
\]
The exponents are nonnegative. More strongly, \(\mathcal C(G)\) simplicially collapses onto the union \(K(G)\) of the graph \(G\), viewed as a one-dimensional complex, with the complete tetrahedral boundary \(\partial\Delta(Q)\) on the four vertices of **each** four-cycle \(Q\). Thus every isolated square creates precisely one integral two-sphere while killing its independent first-homology class; longer graph cycles survive. The collapse is explicit and natural under graph automorphisms.

The resulting canonical integral homology descriptions are
\[
H_2(\mathcal C(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\bigoplus_{Q\in\mathscr Q(G)}\mathbb Z[\partial\Delta(Q)],\qquad
H_1(\mathcal C(G);\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(G;\mathbb Z)/\langle[Q]:Q\in\mathscr Q(G)\rangle.
\]
The second-homology basis is permuted by every graph automorphism with the sign of its induced permutation on the four vertices of the corresponding square. The second displayed isomorphism is natural as an integral automorphism module (and need not split equivariantly). Also \(\pi_1(\mathcal C(G))\) is free of rank \(e-v+1-q\).

## Assumptions and scope
A four-cycle is a simple unoriented cycle of four distinct graph vertices; cycles are identified under cyclic rotation and reversal. The condition forbids sharing *edges*, not just coincident four-vertex supports. Since \(G\) is triangle-free, no four-cycle has a chord. Four-cycles are not required to be far apart: they may share a vertex and may participate in other, longer cycles. A zero-fold wedge denotes a point. The case \(q=0\) follows from the same collapse, but is not claimed as new: the low-scale girth-reconstruction theorem already covers its homotopy consequence.

The neighborhood complex here is **closed**: a face \(\sigma\subseteq V(G)\) belongs to \(\mathcal C(G)\) exactly when \(\sigma\subseteq N_G[u]\) for some vertex \(u\). It equals the vertex-centered graph Čech nerve at integer scale two in the convention of Adams, Shukla and Singh. It is distinct from the Lovász **open**-neighborhood complex generated by \(N_G(u)\) alone.

## Proof
**1. Uniqueness of common centers.** Since \(G\) is triangle-free, every open neighborhood \(N_G(u)\) is independent. If distinct vertices \(x,y\) have two distinct common neighbors \(u,w\), the four vertices form a four-cycle \(x-u-y-w-x\). If they had a third common neighbor \(z\), the two four-cycles through \(u,w\) and through \(u,z\) would share the edges \(xu\) and \(uy\), contrary to assumption. Thus every pair has at most two common neighbors, and a pair with exactly two determines a unique four-cycle, in which it is an opposite pair. Likewise every set of at least three vertices has at most one common neighbor. If two distinct four-cycles shared two vertices, the vertices would either be adjacent on both cycles (a shared edge), opposite on both (at least three common neighbors), or adjacent on one and opposite on the other (a triangle). Therefore two distinct squares meet in at most one vertex.

**2. Explicit elementary collapses.** For every vertex \(u\), write \(B_u=N_G(u)\) and consider all subsets \(\sigma\subseteq B_u\) with \(|\sigma|\geq2\), *excluding* the pairs of opposite vertices of a four-cycle. Pair
\[
\sigma\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\sigma\cup\{u\}.
\]
The lower simplex \(\sigma\) is contained in no closed-neighborhood simplex centered at a vertex other than \(u\): this is immediate for pairs from the common-center bound and for larger sets from the uniqueness of the common center. The upper simplex is private to \(u\) as well: another closed center for \(\{u,x,y\}\), with distinct \(x,y\in B_u\), would create a triangle (including the possibility that the putative center is \(x\) or \(y\)). No simplex occurs in two pairs.

Perform these pairs in descending order of \(|\sigma|\), independently for each center. When the pair with lower face \(\sigma\) is removed, all its larger cofaces were removed in earlier pairs; its only remaining strict coface is \(\sigma\cup\{u\}\). Thus these are genuine elementary free-face collapses, not merely formal discrete-Morse arrows. Their count is
\[
\sum_{u\in V(G)}\left(2^{\deg(u)}-\deg(u)-1-s_u\right),
\]
where \(s_u\) is the number of four-cycles having \(u\) as a vertex (each such square contributes one opposite-neighbor pair at \(u\)). Every term is nonnegative. The remaining simplices are exactly the original vertices and edges of \(G\), together with the pairs of opposite vertices and the four triangles on each square's four vertices. Hence the remainder is
\[
K(G)=G\ \cup\ \bigcup_{Q\in\mathscr Q(G)}\partial\Delta(Q).
\]
Both the pairing rule and its remaining complex are preserved by every graph automorphism. The inclusion \(K(G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal C(G)\) is therefore an automorphism-equivariant map inducing isomorphisms on homology.

**3. Two disks per square.** On a square \(Q=(a,b,c,d)\), the tetrahedral boundary decomposes into two closed simplicial disks, each with boundary the square rim: one consists of the triangles \(abc\) and \(acd\), meeting along diagonal \(ac\); the other consists of \(abd\) and \(bcd\), meeting along diagonal \(bd\). The diagonals are not edges of \(G\), by triangle-freeness. Distinct square boundaries intersect in at most one vertex, so all the disk interiors and all their added diagonals are pairwise disjoint.

Attach one chosen disk for each \(Q\) to \(G\), obtaining a two-dimensional CW complex \(Y\). Choose one original rim edge \(e_Q\) per square. This edge occurs in exactly one disk boundary, because all four-cycles are edge-disjoint. Each newly attached disk can be collapsed with \(e_Q\) (as a regular CW free edge), leaving the connected graph \(G-\{e_Q:Q\in\mathscr Q(G)\}\). The removed edge is never a bridge after earlier edge removals: its other three square edges remain and connect its endpoints. This graph has \(v\) vertices and \(e-q\) edges, so it has first Betti number \(e-v+1-q\). In particular this integer is nonnegative.

The second disk on each square now attaches along its rim, a loop already filled by the first disk in \(Y\); hence its attaching map is nullhomotopic in \(Y\). Attaching a two-cell by a nullhomotopic map adds a wedge summand \(S^2\). This proves the full integral homotopy equivalence and the free-fundamental-group statement, with no assumption that \(G\) is a cactus.

**4. Natural integral homology and signs.** The relative cellular chain group \(H_2(K(G),G;\mathbb Z)\) has two disk generators for every square. Its boundary homomorphism into \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\) sends both disk generators to the signed rim class \([Q]\). The long exact sequence of the pair gives the stated natural first-homology quotient and a rank-one second-homology kernel per square, represented by the oriented tetrahedral boundary. These kernels are independent because their triangles have disjoint supports. Evaluation on one individually chosen edge from each square produces a homomorphism \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z^q\) that pairs to the identity with the oriented square cycles; thus their span is a primitive direct summand and the quotient is torsion-free. An automorphism maps each tetrahedral boundary to another, with its sign given by the parity of the corresponding four-vertex permutation. This proves the claimed integral automorphism-module descriptions; no equivariant wedge splitting is asserted.

## Verification
The included `verify.py` enumerates the **complete** closed-neighborhood simplicial complex of every connected labeled graph on three through six vertices satisfying the stated hypotheses (3,195 graphs, of which 1,323 contain a four-cycle), then verifies that each prescribed elementary-collapse pair is free at the point of removal and that the exact remaining complex is \(K(G)\). It independently checks the reduced binary homology ranks of that remainder. It further checks 83 random qualifying connected graphs with seven through nine vertices and two explicit eight- and nine-vertex noncactus examples with two edge-disjoint squares. Execute `python3 verify.py`; the included `verify_output.txt` records its output. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, Steps 1–4 for all finite graphs.

For an illustrative noncactus example, take two vertex-disjoint four-cycles and connect one pair of their vertices by an edge and a second pair by another edge, with the connecting edges separated along each square. This graph has \(v=8\), \(e=10\), and \(q=2\); thus its closed-neighborhood complex is \(S^1\vee S^2\vee S^2\), retaining the longer cycle while each square yields one sphere.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita (2025, arXiv:2511.12608) defines the closed-neighborhood complex and proves its relationships with independence complexes and graph fundamental groups, but does not state the square-localized homotopy classification or integral square-orientation module. Adams, Shukla and Singh (arXiv:2212.05871, Lemmas 2.2–2.3) identify the same complex with the integer scale-two graph Čech nerve and recover the graph up to homotopy for girth at least five; their hypercube-specific theorem supplies the one-square \(C_4\) boundary as a special case but not the asserted arbitrary edge-disjoint multi-square result. The published result *Graph-like neighborhood complexes with edge-disjoint four-cycles* (2026) gives a different statement for **open** Lovász neighborhoods and a bipartite double cover, which reduces first homology without producing the present two-sphere summands or automorphism orientation formula. None of these statements directly implies the mixed-dimensional closed-neighborhood classification for graphs possessing squares outside the cactus setting.

## Limitations
The hypotheses require a finite, connected, triangle-free graph with all distinct four-cycles pairwise edge-disjoint, and at least one four-cycle in the claimed new regime. Overlapping squares, triangles, higher-scale Čech complexes, and more general reflexive graph constructions are not classified. The final wedge is a nonequivariant homotopy decomposition; only the separate integral homology-module formulas are natural for graph automorphisms. The listed finite computations are not an infinite proof. Literature searching cannot exclude all unindexed stronger results.
