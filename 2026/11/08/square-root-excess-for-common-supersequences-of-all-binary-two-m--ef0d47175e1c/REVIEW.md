# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For any selected two ones, its embedded fixed-weight-two targets inject into the \(\binom{q+2}{2}\) weak compositions of the excess-zero count \(q\), yielding an unconditional lower bound. The upper construction gives explicit legal indices for both markers and checks the leading, middle, and trailing zero requirements. Arithmetic--geometric mean and a concrete spacing choice give the two asymptotic constants. Short cases were independently checked by a standalone direct-subsequence implementation. The theorem does not use experiments to justify infinitely many cases.

## Originality
PASS, qualified. The checked older SCS literature includes Clifford et al. (2010) on resource-constrained ordering and Lagoutte--Tavenas (2013) on hardness for arbitrary subsets of binary two-marker inputs. The latter's single-marker spacing lemma offers a comparable \(\sqrt{k}\) mechanism but not the two-marker pair-packing lower constant or complete-family construction. Adamson (2023) treats full-alphabet subsequence universality, whereas Ma (2025) treats balanced target composition. Related statements about arbitrary input subsets, complete restricted families, or equivalent lattice-covering models remain possible outside indexed material. The result claims new explicit two-marker bounds, not a new general SCS algorithm.

## Value
PASS. This complete fixed-content input family is a natural intermediate between a single-marker sparse target set and unrestricted binary subsequence universality. The proof gives a nontrivial order-of-growth distinction, explicit constants, and a reproducible all-input construction, with a stronger lower constant than the direct one-marker packing bound. It also isolates the open sharp-constant problem. The contribution is structural rather than a routine finite table.
