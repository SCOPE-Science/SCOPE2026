# Square-zero Jacobian degeneration of the integral Perazzo cubic

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal X\subset\mathbb P^4_{\mathbb Z}\) be the cubic hypersurface defined by
\[
f=x_0u^2+x_1uv+x_2v^2=0.
\]
Let \(\mathcal S\) be its *relative projective Jacobian singular scheme*, defined by the homogeneous ideal generated by \(f\) and all five coordinate partial derivatives. Write \(P=\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb Z}=\operatorname{Proj}\mathbb Z[x_0,x_1,x_2]\).

There is a finite map \(\pi:\mathcal S\to P\), with a canonical split square-zero structure
\[
\pi_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal S}=\mathcal O_P\oplus\mathcal M,\qquad \mathcal M^2=0,
\]
where \(\mathcal M\) admits the exact resolution
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_P(-2)^{\oplus2}
\xrightarrow{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2x_0&x_1\\x_1&2x_2\end{smallmatrix}\right)}
\mathcal O_P(-1)^{\oplus2}\longrightarrow\mathcal M\longrightarrow0.
\]
The whole singular scheme is flat over \(\mathbb Z\), with reduced support \(P_k\) in every geometric fibre over a field \(k\). Its nilradical has zeroth Fitting ideal
\[
\operatorname{Fitt}_0(\mathcal M)=(4x_0x_2-x_1^2).
\]
More precisely, for every field \(k\):

- If \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), let \(i:C\hookrightarrow P_k\) be the smooth conic \(4x_0x_2-x_1^2=0\), parametrized by \([s:t]\mapsto[s^2:2st:t^2]\). Then
  \[\mathcal M_k\cong i_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1_k}(-1).\]
- If \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), let \(j:L\hookrightarrow P_k\) be the reduced line \(x_1=0\). Then
  \[\mathcal M_k\cong j_*\mathcal O_L(-1)^{\oplus2},\qquad \operatorname{Fitt}_0(\mathcal M_k)=(x_1^2).\]

Thus a square-zero direction supported on a smooth conic specializes to **two independent square-zero directions supported on a line**. The scheme, not merely its reduced singular plane, changes in characteristic two, while the family remains flat. For every field \(k\) and integer \(m\ge0\),
\[
h^0(\mathcal S_k,\mathcal O_{\mathcal S_k}(m))
=\binom{m+2}{2}+2m.
\]
In particular, the fibre Hilbert polynomial is
\[
P_{\mathcal S_k}(m)=\frac{m^2+7m+2}{2}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The base is \(\mathbb Z\), not an unspecified model of the complex cubic. The coordinates and the coefficient of \(x_1uv\) are fixed **exactly as displayed**. The singular scheme is the *relative* Jacobian subscheme over the base, including its nilpotent scheme structure; it is not the reduced singular locus. All field statements apply to arbitrary fields, without a perfection or algebraic-closure assumption. The conic is smoothly isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^1_k\) only when \(2\) is invertible.

## Proof

**1. Global Jacobian ideal.** Let \(A=\mathbb Z[x_0,x_1,x_2]\) and \(R=A[u,v]\), with all variables of degree one. Differentiation gives, in the displayed coordinate order,
\[
\partial f=(u^2,uv,v^2,2x_0u+x_1v,x_1u+2x_2v).
\]
The equation \(f\) already belongs to \((u^2,uv,v^2)\), so the Jacobian quotient is precisely the graded square-zero extension \(B=R/J=A\oplus M\), where
\[
M=\frac{A(-1)u\oplus A(-1)v}
{\bigl(2x_0u+x_1v,\;x_1u+2x_2v\bigr)}.
\]
The map \(A\to B\) is finite; taking Proj gives \(\pi:\operatorname{Proj}B\to\operatorname{Proj}A\) with the asserted split square-zero algebra. The quotient by the nilradical is \(A\), proving that the reduced geometric support is always the plane \(P_k\).

**2. Free resolution and flatness.** The map \(A(-2)^2\to A(-1)^2\) has determinant \(\Delta=4x_0x_2-x_1^2\). It is nonzero over \(\mathbb Z\) and remains a nonzero polynomial modulo *every* prime, including \(2\). Since the polynomial rings over fields are domains, the matrix remains injective on every residue-field fibre. The cokernel therefore has no \(p\)-torsion for any prime \(p\) (reduce the two-term free resolution modulo \(p\)), so each graded component of \(B\) is \(\mathbb Z\)-flat. Hence \(\mathcal S\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\) is flat. The displayed resolution sheafifies. The determinant, which is the unique maximal minor, gives \(\operatorname{Fitt}_0(\mathcal M)=(\Delta)\), and the adjugate identity proves \(\Delta\mathcal M=0\).

**3. Every odd-characteristic fibre.** With \(2\) invertible, \(C=V(\Delta)\) is a smooth plane conic. On its parametrization \([x_0:x_1:x_2]=[s^2:2st:t^2]\), the presentation matrix factors as
\[
2\begin{pmatrix}s\\t\end{pmatrix}(s\;t).
\]
Pulling back to \(\mathbb P^1\), the image is the subbundle \(\mathcal O(-3)\subset\mathcal O(-2)^2\) because the row \((s,t):\mathcal O(-4)^2\to\mathcal O(-3)\) is surjective. Its cokernel is \(\mathcal O(-1)\), by the Euler sequence twisted by \(-2\). Since \(\Delta\) annihilates \(\mathcal M\), this identifies the full sheaf with \(i_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\), not merely with its restriction as an \(\mathcal O_P\)-module. The presentation matrix has rank one everywhere on \(C\) and rank two elsewhere.

**4. The characteristic-two fibre.** Modulo \(2\), the presentation matrix becomes \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&x_1\\x_1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Its cokernel is exactly two copies of \(k[x_0,x_2](-1)\), giving \(j_*\mathcal O_L(-1)^2\). Its zeroth Fitting ideal is \((x_1^2)\), even though the module is supported on the *reduced* line \(L\). Explicitly the Jacobian ideal is \((u,v)^2+x_1(u,v)\), so the two local nilpotent generators have square-zero products and are independent on the line.

**5. Hilbert polynomial and sections.** For each field, the free resolution gives
\[
H_{B_k}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^3}+\frac{2t}{(1-t)^2}.
\]
Thus \(\dim_k(B_k)_m=\binom{m+2}{2}+2m\) for all \(m\ge0\). Independently, the sheaf decomposition gives \(H^0(P_k,\mathcal O(m))\) plus, in odd characteristic, \(H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(2m-1))\), and in characteristic two, two copies of \(H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(m-1))\). Both have dimension \(2m\) for \(m\ge0\), including zero at \(m=0\). This proves the stated sheaf-section formula and Hilbert polynomial.

## Verification

The accompanying executable `verify_perazzo.py` differentiates the integral polynomial, checks the determinant, constructs characteristic-\(p\) Gröbner bases, and exhaustively compares the dimensions of homogeneous degrees \(0\) through \(8\) with the predicted Hilbert function for \(p=2,3,5,7,11\). Its recorded output is in `verification-output.txt`. These finite checks corroborate exact algebraic identities; the flatness, fibre descriptions, and all-degree Hilbert formula are established by the proof above, not by extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work

Gondim and Russo (2013, arXiv:1312.1618) give the displayed cubic as a classical nonconical Hessian-zero example and prove the projective classification with reduced singular locus a plane (Theorem 4.2). They explicitly distinguish the Jacobian base-locus scheme from reduced singular support, but the inspected geometric classification does not identify its integral square-zero algebra or the characteristic-two degeneration. Fiorindo, Mezzetti and Miró-Roig (2022, arXiv:2206.02723), Section 6, study the same family over characteristic zero, including the reduced singular plane and polar geometry, but not the nilradical sheaf in mixed characteristic. The new statement concerns that omitted scheme structure and its exact degeneration, not the classical cubic equation, the reduced plane, or vanishing Hessian itself.

## Limitations

Only the *fixed integral equation* above is analyzed. Replacing the coefficient of \(x_1uv\) by two yields a distinct integral model, even though the resulting generic fibres can be projectively equivalent after inverting two. No claim is made about all possible integral Perazzo models, resolution of singularities, the flatness of the blowup of this Jacobian ideal, or independent verification. Although the explicit algebra determines the singular scheme, the originality comparison is limited by the available primary sources and cannot establish absolute historical priority.
