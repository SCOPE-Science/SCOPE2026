# Stable-range integral cohomology and Brauer vanishing for Calabi–Yau Enriques complete intersections

## Finding
Let \(m\ge2\). Let \(Y\subset\mathbb P^{4m+1}_{\mathbb C}\) be a smooth complete intersection of \(2m+1\) quadrics, preserved by the projective involution
\[
\iota:[x_0:\cdots:x_{2m}:y_0:\cdots:y_{2m}]\longmapsto
[-x_0:\cdots:-x_{2m}:y_0:\cdots:y_{2m}],
\]
and assume that \(\iota\) has no fixed point on \(Y\). Set \(X=Y/\langle\iota\rangle\). Thus \(X\) is a smooth projective complex variety of dimension \(d=2m\). The involution acts linearly on \(\mathcal O_Y(1)\), so this bundle descends to an invertible sheaf \(L\) on \(X\); write \(h=c_1(L)\). Set \(u=c_1(K_X)\).

**Theorem.** The natural graded ring homomorphism
\[
\mathbb Z[h,u]/(2u)\longrightarrow H^*(X,\mathbb Z)
\]
is an isomorphism in every graded component strictly below cohomological degree \(d\). Both generators have cohomological degree two. In particular, for \(0\le k<m\),
\[
H^{2k}(X,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z h^k\oplus
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}(\mathbb Z/2)u^jh^{k-j},
\qquad H^{2k+1}(X,\mathbb Z)=0\quad\text{when }2k+1<d.
\]
Furthermore, \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\) with torsion generated by \(K_X\), and \(\operatorname{Br}(X)=0\). For \(m\ge3\) this gives \(H^4(X,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^2\) with nonzero independent torsion classes \(u^2\) and \(uh\). In every dimension \(d\ge4\), the Brauer group is zero even though the canonical bundle has order two.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties and cohomology groups are over \(\mathbb C\), with singular (Betti) cohomology and integral coefficients. The action is *free* and comes from the indicated linear involution, so the quotient map \(\pi:Y\to X\) is finite étale of degree two. The assumptions hold for the generalized Fermat Calabi–Yau complete intersections with free involution of Billi–Palomino–Sarti (2026, Example 4.10 and Theorem 5.10), themselves special members of an earlier family of Enriques varieties of Calabi–Yau type.

The truncation \(H^{<d}\) means all groups of **cohomological degree** strictly less than the complex dimension \(d\), not less than twice that dimension. No claim is made at or beyond the middle degree. The representative of the free generator \(h\) depends on a choice of linearization; replacing \(L\) by \(L\otimes K_X\) replaces \(h\) by \(h+u\) without changing the ring description.

## Proof
**1. Cohomology of the cover.** Iterated integral Lefschetz hyperplane theory for the smooth complete intersection of complex dimension \(d\) identifies
\[
H^q(Y,\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z\,\bar h^{q/2},&q\text{ even},\ 0,&q\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\qquad (0\le q<d),
\]
where \(\bar h=c_1(\mathcal O_Y(1))\). This statement concerns integral cohomology and includes the claim that \(\bar h^j\) is a primitive generator for \(2j<d\). The involution fixes \(\bar h\), hence acts trivially on all of these groups.

**2. Cartan–Leray calculation.** For the free \(C_2\)-action, the multiplicative Cartan–Leray spectral sequence is
\[
E_2^{a,b}=H^a(C_2,H^b(Y,\mathbb Z))\Longrightarrow H^{a+b}(X,\mathbb Z).
\]
For any trivial integral \(C_2\)-module \(\mathbb Z\), periodic group cohomology gives \(H^0(C_2,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), \(H^{2j}(C_2,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2\) for \(j\ge1\), and \(H^{2j+1}(C_2,\mathbb Z)=0\). Consequently, in total degree less than \(d\) the only nonzero \(E_2\)-terms are \(E_2^{0,2b}=\mathbb Z\) and \(E_2^{2a,2b}=\mathbb Z/2\) for \(a\ge1\). No differential can enter or leave one of these terms: a differential raises total degree by one, and every odd-total-degree term below \(d\) is zero already on the second page. Thus the displayed stable-range terms survive to \(E_\infty\).

**3. Multiplicative extensions.** The line bundle \(L\) has \(\pi^*L\cong\mathcal O_Y(1)\), so \(h\) lifts the generator of the filtration-zero term \(E_\infty^{0,2}\). Let \(\mathcal T\) be the order-two line bundle associated with the sign character of the étale double cover; its first Chern class \(u_{\mathcal T}\) is the pullback of the generator of \(H^2(BC_2,\mathbb Z)\), and maps to the nonzero generator of \(E_\infty^{2,0}\). Multiplicativity shows that the order-two classes \(u_{\mathcal T}^jh^{k-j}\) for \(1\le j\le k\) have nonzero images in **different** associated-graded pieces \(E_\infty^{2j,2(k-j)}\). These classes are therefore independent. They account for all \(k\) order-two graded pieces, so no additive extension can produce an order-four element. Meanwhile \(h^k\) restricts to the primitive generator of \(H^{2k}(Y,\mathbb Z)\), splitting off a free \(\mathbb Z\)-summand. This establishes the group isomorphism and its product structure in every degree below \(d\).

**4. Identify the canonical class.** The cover \(Y\) is Calabi–Yau by adjunction, and \(d\) is even. Its nowhere-vanishing holomorphic \(d\)-form transforms by \(-1\) under the fixed-point-free involution, as follows either by the holomorphic Lefschetz fixed-point formula or by the residue construction. Therefore \(K_X\cong\mathcal T\). In particular \(u=u_{\mathcal T}\) is nonzero of order two, and all stated powers of the canonical Chern class are nonzero in the asserted degrees.

**5. Picard and Brauer groups.** By the Calabi–Yau complete-intersection Hodge vanishing and finite étale descent, \(H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=H^2(X,\mathcal O_X)=0\). The exponential exact sequence then identifies \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\) with \(H^2(X,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\). Since \(d\ge4\), step 2 also gives \(H^3(X,\mathbb Z)=0\). The same exponential sequence now yields \(H^2(X,\mathcal O_X^*)=0\); its torsion Brauer subgroup vanishes. Comparison between algebraic and analytic torsion Brauer groups for smooth projective complex varieties proves \(\operatorname{Br}(X)=0\), both for cohomological classes and for Azumaya algebras.

## Verification
The proof uses integral Lefschetz and the multiplicative Cartan–Leray spectral sequence, not an extrapolation from small dimensions. The included standalone Python program `checks/stable_range_table.py` performs a finite, independent consistency check of the positions and multiplicities of the second-page groups, vanishing of possible differentials strictly inside the stable range, and the distinct filtration positions of the products. Its output ends in `E2_ADD_MULT_CHECK_OK` for dimensions four through eighteen. The program does not certify the topological theorems, smoothness, or free action; those hypotheses and arguments are checked mathematically above.

## Relationship to prior work
Billi–Palomino–Sarti, *Quotients of Calabi–Yau Generalized Fermat manifolds and Log Enriques varieties* (2026), identify the free-involution generalized-Fermat family and calculate rational Betti and Hodge data, including the second Betti number one. Boissière–Camere–Sarti, *Logarithmic Enriques varieties* (preprint first posted in 2024), §6.1.5, had introduced the broader complete-intersection construction. Neither inspected source determines the integral stable-range torsion ring or its powers of the canonical Chern class. Frassineti–Rizzo–Tufo–Verni, *On Brauer groups of known Enriques manifolds* (2026), explain Cartan–Leray methods and determine the Brauer groups of certain **hyper-Kähler-covered** Enriques manifolds; their treatment makes clear that Brauer invariants can distinguish higher-dimensional Enriques constructions. The present result concerns **Calabi–Yau-covered** Enriques complete intersections; the general spectral sequence used here is standard and the calculation should not be represented as a new cohomology machine.

The exact integral torsion is invisible to rational Betti numbers. The classes \(u^j\ne0\) in the stable range and the vanishing of the Brauer group despite nontrivial canonical two-torsion provide concrete low-degree topological information for this natural family. No priority claim is made for the Cartan–Leray spectral sequence itself.

## Limitations
The proof applies to smooth complex complete intersections carrying the specified free linear involution, and its ring is described only **strictly below** the middle cohomological degree. It does not determine the middle primitive cohomology, its involution action, the integral groups above the middle, or cases with singular quotients or positive characteristic. The computation follows from classical tools, so historical equivalence through a more general published equivariant-cohomology theorem remains a residual priority risk. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
