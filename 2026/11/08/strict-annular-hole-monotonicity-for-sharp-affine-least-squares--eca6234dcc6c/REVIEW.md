# Scientific review: strict annular-hole monotonicity

## Correctness — PASS

Uniform-area covariance yields the kernel \(1+4x\cdot y/(1+a^2)\). The squared radial coordinate is uniform on \([a^2,1]\); the angular absolute-value integral and its primitive produce the exact norm. Convexity in the radial amplitude, a strict chord-average estimate and differentiation of the moving interval give strict monotonicity on the entire parameter interval. Boundary and small-hole expansions and positivity witnesses follow analytically. The accompanying checker independently verifies the key algebra, direct radial integration at ten ratios and endpoint asymptotics, but no finite check substitutes for the analytical proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The known disk formula is not new and is only the \(a=0\) anchor. The closest examined primary full text, Hangelbroek–Narcowich–Sun–Ward, arXiv:1005.2424 (2010), supplies general \(L^p\) bounds for local kernel-based least squares, not the exact area-annulus norm or a strict hole-radius comparison. Brutman's complex-annulus minimax projection concerns a different operator; only its abstract was accessible and its full text was not certified noncovering. Literature/index queries and accessible related previous claims did not reveal an equivalent or stronger annular statement; unindexed sources remain a real bibliographic risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural disk-to-thin-ring perforation family separates a geometric change in sampling support from a change in the polynomial model. An exact norm law, strict decrease under perforation, the localization of all norming evaluations, and different small-hole versus thin-ring scaling provide an analytic stability boundary, not merely a single numerical constant.

## Limitations

Real, normalized area measure; three-dimensional affine polynomial space; concentric circular annuli only. Nothing is asserted about higher degree, boundary sampling, arbitrary holes, or complex-linear projection. Independent validation remains outstanding.
