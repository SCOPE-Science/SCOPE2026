# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The full-dimensional standard-simplex orthant decomposition yields the exact two-parameter volume polynomial with coefficients \(\binom nk^2\). The normalized reflected polynomial is reduced to the Legendre-associated polynomial \(H_n(p)\). Its simple negative zeros give a positive product factorization whose parameters each decrease strictly with \(p=t(1-t)\). Extracting each interior coefficient gives a positive term from the first elementary symmetric polynomial, so *all* interior mixed-volume ratios decrease strictly in \(p\). At the endpoints the formulas recover the exact sharp simplex and centrally symmetric coefficients. The near-symmetry coefficient follows from a hypergeometric second-moment identity, with its normalization reconstructed in the proof. The bundled exact-rational checker reproduces 1605 finite comparisons; these do not replace the proof for all dimensions.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

A material reading of Fryš–Kotrbatý–Mouamine–Wang (arXiv:2610.06806) shows a universal Godbersen bound and equality rigidity, but not a strict deformation ordering. Artstein-Avidan–Putterman (arXiv:2412.05308) treat the primal volume of unbalanced difference bodies and aggregate weighted inequalities rather than the reflected mixed volumes of the interpolated body divided by its own volume. Fryš–Kotrbatý (arXiv:2609.08612) treat higher-order extensions and weighted inequalities, again not this coefficientwise simplex-path ordering. The closest publicly indexed Santaló-product result concerns the same bodies but a different invariant; an exact Godbersen profile for vertex-truncated simplices is not the reflected-simplex Minkowski interpolation. Targeted mathematical-record queries and 93 accessible historical claims found no equivalent or stronger result. The search is not a proof of historical priority, and older alternate formulations remain a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The newly settled Godbersen endpoint inequality naturally asks how its entire collection of mixed coefficients changes during symmetrization of its extremizers. The result supplies a strict global order for *every* coefficient, exact endpoint/equality behavior, a shape-independent closed polynomial reduction and an explicit quadratic stability coefficient at the symmetric limit. These are structural comparisons of the full mixed-volume profile, not a routine restatement of the endpoint inequality or a numerical table.

## Limitations

The theorem is confined to one simplex and its reflection; no extension to general convex bodies is asserted. Independent review and historical-priority certification have not been performed. Finite computations are diagnostic only.
