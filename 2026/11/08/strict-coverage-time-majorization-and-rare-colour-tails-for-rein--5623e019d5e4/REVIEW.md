# Scientific review of the reinforced-urn coverage theorem

## Correctness (C): PASS

The claim fixes the exact unit-reinforcement predictive law, covers arbitrary strictly positive real initial masses and excludes the degenerate one-colour boundary. Conditional Dirichlet aggregation separates a changed pair's total probability from its Beta-distributed split. The convexity of the rising factorial for at least two pair observations proves strict improvement after every nontrivial balancing transfer; a positive-probability full-outside-coverage event establishes strictness at each attainable time. Gamma-ratio expansion plus a pairwise-absence Bonferroni bound yields the exact rare-event constant and last-unseen-colour law. The moment boundary follows from the tail-sum identity. Exact enumeration checks 2,981 assertions; finite tests do not certify infinite quantifiers.

## Originality (O): PASS, with historical-priority uncertainty

Henze and Holmes (arXiv:1911.01052, 2019) prove the two-colour first-arrival tail; its single-colour moment law is explicitly excluded from originality. Zoroa et al. (arXiv:1608.00155, 2016) prove strict majorization of the full-collection CDF for independent coupon trials with **deterministic** probabilities, and are credited for the fixed-probability predecessor. This does not imply a comparison under two *different Dirichlet mixing laws*; their sampled probability vectors are not connected by pointwise majorization. The present theorem supplies a conditional Beta aggregation and convexity argument that changes the initial reinforcement parameters. Dean (arXiv:2206.05138, 2022) studies a different limit regime and urn-composition observables. The inspected relevant full-paper sections and published research search results did not disclose a stronger theorem covering the stated reinforced initial-mass majorization; an earlier majorization result for Dirichlet–multinomial cover counts remains a priority risk.

## Value (V): PASS

Complete coverage is a natural stopping time in reinforced species discovery. The theorem classifies a global stochastic-order extremizer *for the entire distribution* over all time horizons and jointly identifies exact polynomial survival, moment finiteness and the identity of the last unseen colour. Balancing can change the tail exponent itself, so merely comparing expected times is insufficient or sometimes impossible when the means diverge. The strict all-horizon order and rare-colour classification expose a stable mathematical feature of the reinforcement model beyond routine finite-state enumeration.

## Scientific limitations

Only positive initial masses, finitely many colours and unit identity-reinforcement are covered. The result makes no claim about full-strength likelihood-ratio order or about arbitrary replacement matrices. Relevant prior fixed-probability and single-colour theorems are credited. Published history outside checked results may contain related formulations; independent audit is not performed.
