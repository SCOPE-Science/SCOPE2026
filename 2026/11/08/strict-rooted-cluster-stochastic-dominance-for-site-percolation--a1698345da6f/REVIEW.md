# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The definitions cover every retained vertex subset of each graph and all integer root-component sizes. The proof uses the exact rooted-connected-set boundary identity and full histograms to produce a signed matrix with only five nonzero occupancy rows. Its tail sums are nonnegative for every finite population, with strict positive Bernstein terms precisely at thresholds four to eight for interior site probabilities. Separate bitset-mask and connected-root-boundary algorithms reconstructed every one of the 578 histogram entries, the complete tail matrix and the mean difference. Graph adjacency, strongly regular parameters, and endpoint conventions were independently checked. No finite sampling was used as a substitute for a proof.

## Originality: PASS on examined material, with residual priority risk

Newman–Ziff (2001) supply a generic site-percolation binomial decomposition, not the evaluated rook/Shrikhande comparison. Diskin–Krivelevich (2021) analyze asymptotic percolation on expanding regular graphs, not these exact finite root laws. Huang et al. (2022, Appendix I) distinguish the two graphs by four-clique counts, which do not entail all-occupancy rooted-cluster stochastic dominance without new counting work. The accessible earlier mathematical records and prior exact finite spin/adsorption results on the same graphs do not assert this percolation comparison. A historical or unindexed full connectivity table may predate the calculation, so originality is provisional rather than independently certified.

## Mathematical value: PASS

Site-percolation connected-cluster size is a natural network-reliability and statistical-physics observable. On the canonical nonisomorphic strongly regular cospectral pair, the result goes beyond graph distinguishability: it proves one-sided stochastic comparison simultaneously for every cluster threshold, every fixed occupancy population, and all Bernoulli parameters, with a sharply delimited strictness window. It gives a reproducible exact finite-volume test of how local substructure matters when graph spectra and regularity cannot distinguish candidate media. The claim is a finite-system benchmark, not an infinite-system universality statement.
