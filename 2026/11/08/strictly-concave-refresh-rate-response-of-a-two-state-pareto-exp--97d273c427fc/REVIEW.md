# Scientific review: inverse-calibration geometry of a two-state Pareto exponent

## Correctness — PASS
The source's exact two-channel determinant is algebraically inverted on the correct physical root interval. Strict positivity of the log derivative proves global one-to-one calibration. Polynomial division provides an explicit positive expression for the second derivative of the inverse, hence a strictly negative second derivative for the exponent itself. At both singular endpoints, one-sided expansions give the displayed exact constants; these are analytically established and independently checked with exact rational arithmetic. The source's stochastic tail existence and interpretation are assumptions, not duplicated proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with residual publication-priority risk
Arutkin–Vallée (arXiv:2605.19464, equations 27–39 and Figures 2–3) derive the implicit determinant, physical interval, and limiting Pareto exponents, and draw a qualitative interpolation curve. The exact inverse, universal strict concavity for all nondegenerate two-state parameter values, pole-curvature identity, and two explicit response coefficients are not given there. Beare–Toda (Econometrica 2022, Proposition 5(iv), Appendix B) already treats the direction of a persistence comparative static in a broader Markov multiplicative class, so monotonicity is not asserted as independently new. Searches of published mathematical findings and relevant tail-index literature found no stated strict concavity and endpoint-law combination for this exact model. A broader unindexed perturbation result remains possible; this review does not prove global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
Determining whether a measured stationary tail slope identifies a latent refresh rate is a natural inverse problem highlighted by the model's physical interpretation. The theorem settles identifiability exactly, shows diminishing gains from faster redraws at every rate (not just in numerical plots), and supplies sharp slow- and fast-refresh sensitivities, including the variance-weighted fast coefficient. This provides a precise, model-specific calibration and response benchmark beyond reprinting the known determinant. It does not claim a general theory for other volatility processes or finite-sample inference.

## Boundaries
Assumes two fixed positive diffusivities, independent Poisson redraws, mean-field wealth redistribution, and a stationary tail selected by the cited determinant. The independent audit is pending; publication-priority and empirical-model risks remain distinct from mathematical proof validity.
