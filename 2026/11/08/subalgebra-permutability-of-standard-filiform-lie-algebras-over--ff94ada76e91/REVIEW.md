# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The vector space splits as an abelian ideal and a one-dimensional regular-nilpotent complement. Every subalgebra is either a subspace of the abelian ideal or a complement line augmented by one of the unique invariant Jordan-chain subspaces. For a mixed pair, closure is equivalent to invariance of a subspace sum; intersection-graph counting gives the stated Gaussian sum. For two exterior subalgebras, closure is equivalent to equality of the two residues modulo the next member of the Jordan flag, yielding the exact triangular coefficient count. The three cases partition all ordered pairs, and exact degrees of the count polynomials prove the three-part large-field limit. The direct checker constructs every vector subspace independently and verifies both the original Lie bracket and pair condition in nine finite cases, including dimensions 4 through 6.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)

Adimi–Makhlouf (arXiv:1212.1650v1) treats the same naturally graded filiform family, but computes the coadjoint index. Avetisyan (arXiv:1610.05365v1, Proposition 4) already gives the complete structural classification of subalgebras of almost-Abelian Lie algebras; no originality is claimed for that input. Muhie–Otera–Russo (arXiv:2609.19086v1) studies subalgebra permutability but explicitly calculates dimension three and unrelated simple examples, not the higher standard filiform family. A published adjacent theorem determines adjoint ranks and **element** commutativity, not **subalgebra** pair-incidence. Its full statement and proof were compared. Broader historical results on modular pairs and subalgebra lattices could imply the count; a comprehensive priority audit is not asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The subalgebra commutativity degree is a natural invariant of the finite Lie-subalgebra lattice. This result determines it exactly in every dimension and finite field for a canonical infinite family, and produces a nontrivial dimension-dependent transition: zero, one-quarter, and one in successive large-field regimes. The calculation is more than a subalgebra census: it requires a new mixed incidence count and two-exterior-subalgebra closure criterion. The coincidence of a limiting one-quarter with a different Lie algebra is not claimed novel by itself.

## Limits and closest literature

The proof gives no classification for other maximal-class families, nor a dimension-refined adjacency spectrum. Finite enumeration is corroboration only. Historical priority remains subject to independent verification; a general theorem in the lattice-theory literature could cover these formulas.
