## correctness

PASS

At equilibrium the renewal-reward formula gives age density P(T>a)/μ even for atomic T. Two independent age draws yield the Hilbert–Schmidt kernel cos^(2N)(Ω(A−A′)); expanding it gives nonnegative binomial weights on |m_k|² with the asserted normalization. The exponential integral up to T is Fubini-integrable because E T is finite; |m_k|≤1/(|k|Ωμ). Ratios of central binomial coefficients are bounded by one and tend to one at each fixed k, giving the limit by dominated convergence. Parseval on the period τ with normalized Fourier coefficients τ⁻¹m_k gives τ∫g². For any N≥1 the k=1 term is present; E exp(2iΩT)=1 forces phase one almost surely, so equality in either bound requires positive multiples of τ. Bracketing a mean μ≥τ by neighboring positive multiples realizes equality. For μ<τ, putting mass p=(μ−ε)/(τ−ε) at τ and the remaining mass at ε gives the mean, with A−1=ε(τ−μ)²/[μ²(τ−ε)] by the two-level density, tending to zero. The finite-purity excess divided by the central binomial bound is at most A−1. The wrapped exponential density r exp(−ra)/(1−exp(−rτ)) gives rτ coth(rτ/2)/2 upon integration of its square, confirming the benchmark. None of the argument requires convergence from a synchronized start.

## originality

PASS

The finite-N purity formula is an explicit specialization of known stationary spectral-purity machinery, not a new general identity. A distinct same-day Resultary finding has exactly the N=1 fixed-mean threshold, and the first-harmonic equality argument makes the finite-N extension natural. Credit the distinct many-qubit limit and clock-dependent coefficient across all finite-mean interarrival laws in conjunction with the simultaneous sharp optimization, not the single-qubit threshold alone. Inspected primary passages and the strongest indexed overlap do not establish an earlier result with that content.

The invariant is the loss of global purity under one common random age, not entanglement or a sum of individual-spin entropies. The exact leading constant separates arbitrary clock laws at the same mean, and the one-period cutoff explains when a physically natural strict-positive-interval constraint allows the most mixed stationary phase distribution. The many-qubit constant and the attained-versus-unattained boundary, rather than the binomial specialization alone, are the substantive contributions.
