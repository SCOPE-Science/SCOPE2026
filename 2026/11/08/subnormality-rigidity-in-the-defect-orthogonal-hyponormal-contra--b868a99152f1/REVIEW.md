# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof uses the full separable-space functional model of Maji–Paramanik, not an extrapolation from its finite-rank examples. Its coupled block is explicitly unitarily identified with a two-level bilateral shift. For any positive injective nonidentity coupling, positive operator functional calculus gives \(0<b<a\le1\) for a suitable vector; the exact order-three moment determinant \(-b(a-b)^2<0\) contradicts subnormality. Separating identity-coupled sectors as normal yields the three-way equivalence, including infinite multiplicity and spectral accumulation at zero or one. The commutator calculation is at the distinguished coordinate and the shift wandering subspace.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge)
Maji–Paramanik (arXiv:2610.35880v1, Theorem 3.1) establishes the representation but not an exact subnormality criterion. Sneha B.–Bala–Sarkar (arXiv:2602.22636v1, Theorem 5.2 and concluding finite-rank discussion) establishes finite-rank models and the projection-commutator implication, but does not state the infinite-rank subnormal classification or its universal negative moment witness. Targeted record, literature, and accessible own-history comparisons found no equivalent stronger claim. Standard weighted-shift moment criteria could imply the result by a different formulation; older undiscovered priority remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The new functional model isolates a broad and natural class of hyponormal contractions; determining which of these admit normal extensions is an intrinsic structural problem. The theorem collapses its subnormal members completely to shift-plus-normal operators without any finite-rank or discrete-spectrum assumption. A fixed order-three moment witness rules out *every* genuinely coupled component, giving an economical, precise boundary rather than a numerical illustration. This does not settle subnormality outside the class.
