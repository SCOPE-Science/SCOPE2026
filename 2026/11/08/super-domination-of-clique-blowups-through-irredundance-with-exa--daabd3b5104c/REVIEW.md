## Correctness — PASS

The configuration-level bijection has been reconstructed directly from open- and closed-neighborhood definitions. Two omitted vertices in one clique fiber cannot be privately witnessed, and an omitted singleton in fiber \(u\) has a selected witness in fiber \(v\) if and only if \(N_G[v]\cap U=\{u\}\). Every fiber has a selected vertex because \(m\ge2\). This proves the full weighted enumerator and minimality correspondence without requiring connectivity or a special base graph. The cyclic forbidden-factor lemma, eight-state transfer trace, and upper-cardinality run-gap argument cover the boundary case \(n=3\) and all positive lengths above it. The odd and even maximum-count analyses handle all possible numbers of two-runs, including the all-double-run cases. The standalone verifier checks the original definitions independently and matches the theorem in every completed finite instance. The proof, not the tests, supplies the infinite quantification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The general result is a structural reduction between two distinct and actively studied graph parameters on arbitrary finite bases, stronger than a minimum-number formula: it simultaneously classifies every super dominating set, all inclusion-minimal instances, and the entire cardinality distribution in an infinite class of lexicographic products. The cycle specialization computes every coefficient from a constant-size transfer matrix and gives the complete exact minimum-cardinality multiplicity with a nontrivial parity pattern. This is naturally useful for enumeration and for transferring irredundance knowledge to product graphs. The known base-cycle OEIS tables are not counted as separate discoveries; they are important context and a precise limit on novelty. No assertion of computational tractability for arbitrary \(G\) or of independent verification is made.
