# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Prime-cyclotomic minimal-polynomial divisibility turns Fourier cancellation of the balanced signs into equality of two multisets of polynomial evaluations. Full-rank Vandermonde evaluation identifies the coefficient space with the zero-constant-term interpolation hyperplane. The balanced-value locus is invariant under diagonal field translation, and every translation orbit has exactly one representative in the hyperplane because the Lagrange interpolation weights sum to one. Enumerating balanced fiber partitions proves the stated exact zero-count formula. The independent executable tests directly enumerate six frequency sets and four hyperplane instances, including the first four-pair configuration. The finite enumerations are not being used as infinite proofs.

## Originality
**PASS relative to inspected sources, with publication-priority risk.** Biswas et al. (arXiv:2508.08377) analyze moment-curve endpoint extension through equal power sums and multiset permutations; the inspected manuscript establishes closely related counting ingredients but does not state the support-conditional zero-set law or its diagonal-interpolation argument. Their 2026 sequel (arXiv:2609.29882) concerns the uniform distribution extremal problem, based on its accessible abstract. Li (arXiv:2609.16030) gives finite-parabola uncertainty bounds and zero-set rigidity for arbitrary complex coefficients; its accessible abstract does not decide the all-\(r\) signed critical-degree formula. The new claim is not the established multiset count itself: it is the equality of the Fourier zero number across *every* location choice and the demonstrated loss of that rigidity one degree lower. Full-text comparisons for the 2026 sequel and finite-parabola manuscript remain outstanding. The relevant indexed exact finite-parabola support results address different degrees/coefficient classes, not an implication covering this theorem.

## Value
**PASS.** The signed Fourier zero set is a natural exact uncertainty invariant on the moment curve. At the critical interpolation dimension, a nontrivial high-dimensional family collapses to one fixed degree-\((r-1)\) counting polynomial regardless of support geometry, while explicit degree-four six-atom examples over \(\mathbb F_{11}\) show that this uniformity is not automatic in lower dimensions. The statement is a complete classification across every admissible support and prime, rather than a recomputed global moment or a single numerical sample. The assumption that coefficients are balanced signs is essential; no arbitrary-amplitude conclusion is claimed.

## Limitations and nearest literature
The comparison is relative to the inspected portions of arXiv:2508.08377 and accessible summaries for arXiv:2609.29882 and arXiv:2609.16030. Older unindexed formulations in cyclic coding, interpolation, or finite Fourier uncertainty may give an equivalent formula. The theorem is confined to prime fields, disjoint balanced signed supports, and degree \(2r-1\). The degree-four obstruction is exhibited for \(r=3\), not as a universal lower-degree classification.
