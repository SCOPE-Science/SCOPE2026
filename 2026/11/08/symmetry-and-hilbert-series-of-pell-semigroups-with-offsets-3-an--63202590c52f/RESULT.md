# Symmetry and Hilbert series of Pell semigroups with offsets \(3\) and \(5\)

## Finding
Let \(P_0=0\), \(P_1=1\), and \(P_{k+2}=2P_{k+1}+P_k\). For each integer \(n\ge3\), put \(a=P_n\), \(b=P_{n+3}\), \(c=P_{n+5}\), and \(S_n=\langle a,b,c\rangle\). Then \(S_n\) is a symmetric numerical semigroup if and only if
\[
3\mid n\quad\text{or}\quad n\in\{4,5\}.
\]
For every \(3\mid n\), its Hilbert series \(H_n(t)=\sum_{s\in S_n}t^s\), Frobenius number, and genus are
\[
H_n(t)=\frac{(1-t^{ab/5})(1-t^{5c})}{(1-t^a)(1-t^b)(1-t^c)},\qquad
F_n=\frac{ab}{5}-a-b+4c,\qquad g_n=\frac{F_n+1}{2}.
\]
For \(n=4\) or \(5\), \(S_n=\langle a,b\rangle\) and
\[
H_n(t)=\frac{1-t^{ab}}{(1-t^a)(1-t^b)},\qquad F_n=ab-a-b,\qquad g_n=\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}2.
\]
In particular, \((F_4,g_4)=(1847,924)\) and \((F_5,g_5)=(11395,5698)\). The \(3\mid n\) formulas include \(n=3\), when \(S_3=\langle5,408\rangle\). If \(3\nmid n\) and \(n\notin\{4,5\}\), then \(S_n\) is nonsymmetric and has exactly three minimal generators.

## Assumptions and scope
All indices are integers \(n\ge3\). The semigroup consists of nonnegative integral combinations of its stated generators. Its Frobenius number \(F_n\) is the largest nonnegative integer outside \(S_n\); its genus \(g_n\) is the number of such gaps. A numerical semigroup is symmetric precisely when \(2g_n=F_n+1\). The Hilbert series is a formal power series over any coefficient field. The theorem classifies symmetry and gives formulas for invariants in the symmetric cases, not the general nonsymmetric Frobenius numbers or genera.

## Proof
**1. Pell identities and pairwise gcds.** The recurrence gives \(P_{m+k}=P_{m+1}P_k+P_mP_{k-1}\), and \(\gcd(P_m,P_{m+1})=1\). Therefore \(\gcd(P_m,P_{m+k})=\gcd(P_m,P_k)\), and the Euclidean algorithm on indices gives the strong divisibility identity \(\gcd(P_i,P_j)=P_{\gcd(i,j)}\). In particular,
\[
\gcd(a,b)=\begin{cases}5,&3\mid n,\\1,&3\nmid n,\end{cases}\quad
\gcd(a,c)=\begin{cases}29,&5\mid n,\\1,&5\nmid n,\end{cases}\quad
\gcd(b,c)=\begin{cases}2,&n\text{ odd},\\1,&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]
Consequently \(\gcd(a,b,c)=1\), and each \(S_n\) is numerical. The addition formula also yields
\[
b=5P_{n+1}+2a,\qquad c=29P_{n+1}+12a,\qquad 5c=2a+29b.
\]
For the odd steps three and five, the characteristic roots \(1\pm\sqrt2\) have product \(-1\), and the sums of their respective third and fifth powers are \(14\) and \(82\). Their sampled characteristic equations therefore give, for the stated nonnegative indices,
\[
b=14a+P_{n-3}\quad(n\ge3),\qquad c=82a+P_{n-5}\quad(n\ge5).
\]

**2. Minimal generators and exceptions.** At \(n=3\), \(b=14a\); at \(n=4\), direct recurrence gives \(c=68a+b\); at \(n=5\), it gives \(c=82a\). Thus each is generated by two coprime integers: respectively \(\langle5,408\rangle\), \(\langle12,169\rangle\), and \(\langle29,408\rangle\).

Now let \(n\ge6\). Since \(0<P_{n-3}<a\), \(b\) is not a multiple of \(a\), and because \(c>b>a\), neither \(a\) nor \(b\) is generated by the other two. If \(c=xa+yb\) for nonnegative integers \(x,y\), then \(c<6b\) follows from \(5c=2a+29b\) and \(2a<b\); hence \(0\le y\le5\). The sampled identities give
\[
0=(x+14y-82)a+yP_{n-3}-P_{n-5}.
\]
But \(0<P_{n-5}<P_{n-3}\) and \(5P_{n-3}<a\); the latter follows, for instance, from \(a=5P_{n-2}+2P_{n-3}\). Thus the nonzero integer \(P_{n-5}-yP_{n-3}\) has absolute value strictly less than \(a\), contradicting divisibility by \(a\). All three generators are therefore minimal for \(n\ge6\).

**3. The symmetric progression.** Suppose \(3\mid n\). Set \(A=a/5\), \(B=b/5\) and \(T=\langle A,B\rangle\). Then \(\gcd(A,B)=1\), \(\gcd(c,5)=1\), and \(c=2A+29B\). Reduction of any coefficient of \(c\) modulo five using \(5c=2a+29b\) gives the disjoint residue-class decomposition
\[
S_n=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{4}\bigl(jc+5T\bigr).
\]
For two coprime generators \(A,B\), the classical formulas give \(F(T)=AB-A-B\), \(g(T)=(A-1)(B-1)/2\), and \(\sum_{u\in T}t^{5u}=(1-t^{5AB})/((1-t^{5A})(1-t^{5B}))\). These formulas remain valid at \(A=1\), where \(T=\mathbb N_0\) and \(F(T)=-1\) by convention. Summing the five disjoint translates proves the displayed Hilbert series. In the residue class of \(jc\pmod5\), the largest missing integer is \(5F(T)+jc\) (possibly negative), so \(F_n=5F(T)+4c=ab/5-a-b+4c\). The number of gaps in that residue class is \(g(T)+\lfloor jc/5\rfloor\). Since \(\gcd(c,5)=1\), the residues of \(jc\) are \(0,1,2,3,4\), and
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{4}\left\lfloor\frac{jc}{5}\right\rfloor=2c-2.
\]
Therefore \(g_n=5g(T)+2c-2=(F_n+1)/2\), proving symmetry, including \(n=3\).

**4. The other symmetric exceptions.** At \(n=4\) and \(n=5\), the two-generator reductions in Step 2 and the coprime two-generator formulas give the claimed Hilbert series, Frobenius numbers, genera, and symmetry.

**5. Exclusion of all remaining indices.** For a numerical semigroup minimally generated by three integers, the Herzog–Watanabe three-generator symmetry criterion states that symmetry requires a pair with common divisor \(d>1\), such that the third generator belongs to the semigroup generated by the pair divided by \(d\) (see Fel, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1). Suppose \(n\ge6\) and \(3\nmid n\). The pair \((a,b)\) is coprime. If \(n\) is odd, \(\gcd(b,c)=2\), but \(b/2>a\), so \(a\notin\langle b/2,c/2\rangle\). The only remaining possible pair is \((a,c)\), with gcd \(29\), requiring \(5\mid n\). Then \(n\ge10\). Put \(A=a/29\), \(C=c/29\), so \(\gcd(A,C)=1\), \(A\ge P_{10}/29=82>5\), and the Pell identity says \(b=5C-2A\). If \(b=xA+yC\) for nonnegative integers \(x,y\), then \((x+2)A=(5-y)C\). Positivity forces \(0\le y\le4\), while coprimality forces \(A\mid(5-y)\), impossible because \(A>5\). No pair meets the necessary criterion; hence the semigroup is nonsymmetric. This finishes the classification.

## Verification
The included standalone `check_pell35.py` implements exact integer Pell arithmetic and a shortest-path computation of the full Apéry set modulo \(a=P_n\), independently obtaining the Frobenius number and genus for every \(3\le n\le12\). It checks the stated symmetric/nonsymmetric decisions, gcd identities, and the closed Frobenius formulas in symmetric cases. The executable output is reproduced in `verification_output.txt` and ends with `PELL35_EXACT_APERY_CHECK_OK tested_n=3..12`. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the all-index argument in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Komatsu and Mu (2023, arXiv:2307.08998) derive \(p\)-Frobenius and genus formulas for generalized Pell triplets having an offset-\(2\) generator, \(\langle P_i(u),P_{i+2}(u),P_{i+k}(u)\rangle\). That family does not contain \(\langle P_n,P_{n+3},P_{n+5}\rangle\). Fel (2008, arXiv:0803.1606) classifies symmetry for triples from Fibonacci and Lucas sequences and records the general three-generator criterion used here; its specialized index results do not treat classical Pell triples. The new part here is the exact Pell-offset arithmetic classification, its two isolated exceptions, and the resulting closed Hilbert/Frobenius/genus formulas. The general criterion is acknowledged as prior mathematics rather than claimed as new.

## Limitations
The theorem makes no claim of closed formulas for the nonsymmetric indices, nor for higher-representation \(p\)-Frobenius invariants. It relies on the established Herzog–Watanabe characterization in the nonsymmetric direction. Literature searches cannot rule out all unindexed equivalent work, and no independent scientific audit is claimed.
