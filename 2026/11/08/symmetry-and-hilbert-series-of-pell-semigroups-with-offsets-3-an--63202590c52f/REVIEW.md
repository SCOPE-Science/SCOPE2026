# Scientific assessment: symmetry of Pell semigroups at offsets \(3,5\)

## Correctness: PASS
For the exact domain \(n\ge3\), the proof establishes Pell addition identities and the strong divisibility gcd law; classifies all redundant generators at \(n=3,4,5\); establishes the disjoint five-class decomposition for indices divisible by three; and derives the Hilbert series, genus, and Frobenius number from the two-generator Sylvester formulas. For all other indices it verifies the prerequisites of the classical three-generator criterion and rules out every possible gcd pair by explicit inequalities and divisibility. No bounded computation is used as an infinite proof. A separate full-Apéry-set Dijkstra computation confirmed the closed results at every integer index from \(3\) through \(12\). Correctness risk is concentrated in applying the published Herzog–Watanabe criterion only after proving minimality, which the proof handles.

## Originality: PASS within inspected evidence
The nearest Pell-triplet paper, Komatsu–Mu (arXiv:2307.08998, first public 2023-07-18), has one generator at offset two; its stated results and worked examples are for \(\langle P_i(u),P_{i+2}(u),P_{i+k}(u)\rangle\), rather than the simultaneous offsets three and five. Fel (arXiv:0803.1606, first public 2008-03-11) provides the underlying three-generator symmetry criterion and solves Fibonacci/Lucas (not Pell) triple arithmetic. This theorem does not re-claim that general criterion: it solves the Pell-specific index condition, including the isolated two-generator exceptions, and supplies closed Hilbert/Frobenius/genus formulas. Searches of the relevant mathematical index and public records did not identify a covering exact Pell \(3,5\) classification. Remaining risk: older or unindexed semigroup literature could include an equivalent specialization; absence from search results is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The result is a complete infinite-family classification of a natural semigroup property for fixed consecutive odd index gaps, with sharp exceptional indices and closed invariant formulas in every symmetric case. Symmetry distinguishes complete-intersection/Gorenstein semigroup rings, so the explicit arithmetic criterion is a useful structural invariant. It is not merely a small numerical improvement or arbitrary finite sample. The proof's five-residue decomposition connects the Pell recurrence to the semigroup Hilbert series. The theorem does not determine Frobenius/genus formulas outside the symmetric subfamily.
