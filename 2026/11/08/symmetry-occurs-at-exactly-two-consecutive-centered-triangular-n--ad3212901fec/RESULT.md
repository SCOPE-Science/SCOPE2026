# Symmetry occurs at exactly two consecutive centered-triangular numerical semigroups

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge 2\), define the centered triangular number \(C_n=1+3n(n-1)/2\) and the numerical semigroup \(S_n=\langle C_n,C_{n+1},C_{n+2}\rangle\). Then
\[
S_n\text{ is symmetric}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\in\{2,8\}.
\]
Both exceptional semigroups are three-generated complete intersections. More precisely,
\[
S_2=\langle4,10,19\rangle,\quad F(S_2)=25,\quad g(S_2)=13,
\]
\[
S_8=\langle85,109,136\rangle,\quad F(S_8)=2203,\quad g(S_8)=1102.
\]
Here \(F\) is the largest nonmember and \(g\) the number of nonnegative-integer gaps. The graded semigroup-ring Hilbert series are, respectively,
\[
\frac{(1-t^{20})(1-t^{38})}{(1-t^4)(1-t^{10})(1-t^{19})}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{(1-t^{680})(1-t^{1853})}{(1-t^{85})(1-t^{109})(1-t^{136})}.
\]
These correspond to two-binomial ideals \(\langle X^5-Y^2,Z^2-X^2Y^3\rangle\) and \(\langle X^8-Z^5,Y^{17}-X^9Z^8\rangle\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
All indices \(n\) are integers at least \(2\). Numerical semigroups contain \(0\), and \(\langle a,b,c\rangle\) is the set of all nonnegative-integer combinations of its generators. A numerical semigroup is *symmetric* if every integer \(z\) between \(0\) and its Frobenius number \(F\) satisfies exactly one of \(z\in S\) and \(F-z\in S\). For any numerical semigroup this is equivalent to \(2g=F+1\). The three generators above are minimal and have greatest common divisor \(1\) for every stated index, as verified below. The classification uses the standard three-generator symmetry/gluing criterion, whose hypotheses are explicitly checked.

## Proof
**1. Primitive and minimal triple.** Put \(a=C_n\), \(b=C_{n+1}=a+3n\), \(c=C_{n+2}=a+6n+3\). Every generator is \(1\pmod3\). Hence
\[
\gcd(a,b,c)\mid\gcd(3n,3(n+1))=3,
\]
and the common gcd is \(1\). The strict inequalities \(a<b<c<2a\) hold for all \(n\ge6\), since \(2a-c=(3n^2-15n-4)/2>0\). Thus none of \(a,b,c\) is generated by the other two in this range. Direct inspection of \(\{4,10,19\}\), \(\{10,19,31\}\), \(\{19,31,46\}\), and \(\{31,46,64\}\) shows minimality for \(n=2,3,4,5\).

**2. Pairwise gcds.** Consecutive centered triangular numbers differ by \(3n\). With \(n\) odd, \(a\equiv1\pmod n\); with \(n=2k\), \(a=1+3k(2k-1)\equiv1\pmod k\). Considering parity therefore gives
\[
\gcd(a,b)=\begin{cases}2,&n\equiv2\pmod4,\\1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Applying this to the next index gives \(\gcd(b,c)=2\) precisely when \(n\equiv1\pmod4\). For the nonconsecutive pair, write \(v=2n+1\). Because \(c-a=3v\), \(a\equiv1\pmod3\), \(v\) is odd, and
\[
8a=3v^2-12v+17,
\]
one obtains
\[
\gcd(a,c)=\gcd(a,v)=\gcd(17,v)
=\begin{cases}17,&n\equiv8\pmod{17},\\1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The triple gcd is \(1\), so for every nontrivial pair gcd \(d\) the remaining generator is coprime to \(d\).

**3. The symmetry criterion.** For a numerical semigroup minimally generated by three positive integers with common gcd \(1\), the classical Herzog–Watanabe characterization says it is symmetric if and only if some generator pair has common divisor \(d>1\), and the third generator belongs to the two-generator semigroup obtained by dividing that pair by \(d\). In the positive case the semigroup is a complete intersection. This is the embedding-dimension-three complete-intersection criterion, not an unproved assertion of the present result. We now exhaust its three possible pairs, using Step 2.

**4. The adjacent first pair.** If \(n\equiv2\pmod4\), symmetry from the pair \((a,b)\) requires \(c\in\langle a/2,b/2\rangle\), equivalently \(2c=xa+yb\) for nonnegative integers \(x,y\). For all \(n\ge10\), \(2c<3a\) because \(a>12n+6\), whereas \(2c>2b\) because \(c>b\). Therefore \(x+y\le2\) would force \(xa+yb\le2b<2c\), a contradiction. The two smaller permissible indices are \(2\) and \(6\). At \(n=2\), \(c=19=2\cdot2+3\cdot5\), so the condition holds. At \(n=6\), \((a/2,b/2,c)=(23,32,85)\); reducing \(85=23x+32y\) modulo \(23\) yields \(y\equiv12\pmod{23}\), contradicting \(0\le y\le2\). Hence this pair contributes only \(n=2\).

**5. The adjacent second pair.** If \(n\equiv1\pmod4\), symmetry from \((b,c)\) would require \(a\in\langle b/2,c/2\rangle\), equivalently \(2a=xb+yc\). Because \(a<b<c\), necessarily \(x+y\le1\). But modulo \(3\), every one of \(a,b,c\) is \(1\), while \(2a\equiv2\pmod3\). No choice of \(x+y\in\{0,1\}\) is possible. Hence this pair never contributes.

**6. The outer pair.** If \(n\equiv8\pmod{17}\), symmetry from \((a,c)\) requires \(b\in\langle a/17,c/17\rangle\), equivalently \(17b=xa+yc\). At \(n=8\), \((a/17,c/17,b)=(5,8,109)\) and \(109=9\cdot5+8\cdot8\), so symmetry holds. At \(n=25\), \((a/17,c/17,b)=(53,62,976)\). Any solution \(976=53x+62y\) has \(0\le y\le15\), but reduction modulo \(53\) gives \(9y\equiv22\pmod{53}\), or \(y\equiv26\pmod{53}\), a contradiction. For the remaining indices \(n\ge42\) in this congruence class, \(a>45n+48\) and \(a>51n\), so \(16c<17b<18a\). Since \(a\le c\), this forces \(x+y=17\). Substituting \(x=17-y\), \(b=a+3n\) and \(c-a=3(2n+1)\) yields
\[
y=\frac{17n}{2n+1}=8+\frac{n-8}{2n+1}.
\]
For \(n>8\) this is strictly between \(8\) and \(9\), never an integer. Thus the outer pair contributes only \(n=8\).

Steps 3–6 give the stated equivalence for every integer \(n\ge2\).

**7. Exceptional semigroup invariants.** At \(n=2\), the gluing \(\langle4,10,19\rangle=\langle2\cdot2,2\cdot5,19\rangle\) has relation degrees \(20\) and \(38\), with \(5\cdot4=2\cdot10=20\) and \(2\cdot19=2\cdot4+3\cdot10=38\). The complete-intersection Hilbert numerator is \((1-t^{20})(1-t^{38})\), and its Frobenius number is \(20+38-4-10-19=25\). At \(n=8\), the gluing \(\langle85,109,136\rangle=\langle17\cdot5,109,17\cdot8\rangle\) has relation degrees \(680\) and \(1853\), since \(8\cdot85=5\cdot136=680\) and \(17\cdot109=9\cdot85+8\cdot136=1853\). Its Frobenius number is \(680+1853-85-109-136=2203\). The displayed two-binomial ideals follow from these free gluings. Symmetry gives \(g=(F+1)/2\), which yields \(13\) and \(1102\), respectively.

## Verification
The included dependency-free `check.py` performs exact pairwise-gcd, integer gluing-membership and independent Apéry-shortest-path computations for every \(2\le n\le120\). It verifies precisely the two symmetric indices and their Frobenius numbers and genera, with output in `check_output.txt`. Run `python3 check.py`. These tests are finite corroboration; the universal classification is proved by Steps 1–7. The derivation invokes the published three-generator symmetry/gluing criterion and two-generator Frobenius formula.

## Relationship to prior work
The 2024 preprint of Yin, Mu and Komatsu studies three consecutive generalized-star generators \(1+an(n-1)\) with integer \(a\ge4\), and their representation-count and Frobenius invariants. Centered triangular numbers correspond to \(a=3/2\), outside their hypotheses; their published conclusions cannot be substituted at this parameter. Robles-Pérez and Rosales (2017) study ordinary triangular and tetrahedral-number generators, not consecutive centered triangular generators. Fel's work on consecutive squares/cubes supplies the relevant general symmetry criterion but treats different generating sequences. OEIS A005448 records the underlying centered triangular sequence, not this three-generator symmetry classification. This result adds an all-index yes/no theorem and explicit exceptional complete intersections.

## Limitations
No claim is made for other centered polygonal families, larger generating windows, or generalized-star coefficients. The proof uses the established three-generator gluing criterion; it does not reprove that general criterion. The particular two isolated symmetric semigroups are completely described. Possible equivalent prior results under alternative figurate-number or semigroup notation remain a literature-search risk. No external independent scientific validation has been performed.
