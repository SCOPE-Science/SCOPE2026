# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For every integer \(n\ge2\) the three generators are primitive and minimal. The pair gcds are exactly \(2\), \(2\) or \(17\) in specified residue classes, with no other possibilities. The standard three-generator Herzog–Watanabe gluing criterion reduces symmetry to three exhaustive Diophantine membership tests. All three are proved uniformly, including the exceptional small indices \(2,6,8,25\). The two exceptional complete-intersection Betti degrees give the claimed Frobenius numbers and genera. The finite Apéry computation, for indices \(2\) through \(120\), is a separate check, not the infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS within inspected sources. The 2024 Yin–Mu–Komatsu full preprint addresses integer coefficients \(a\ge4\) in \(1+an(n-1)\), whereas centered triangular numbers have \(a=3/2\). The 2017 Robles-Pérez–Rosales full manuscript studies ordinary triangular and tetrahedral numbers; the Fel manuscript supplies general criteria for squares and cubes, not this classification. OEIS A005448 records the integers, not a semigroup symmetry theorem. Targeted semantic mathematical-index and formula/alias web searches identified no equivalent or dominating centered-triangular classification. A general gluing criterion is used, but its application involves a nontrivial exceptional congruence at \(17\). Residual risk: unindexed equivalent treatment under alternative terminology.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is a complete yes/no symmetry classification for every index of a classical centered-figurate three-generator family, with only two sporadic complete-intersection cases and exact algebraic invariants. The appearance of the outer-pair gcd \(17\) and the elimination of all but one admissible outer index explain a structural feature not supplied by routine numerical tables. It is a natural sharp classification, not a parameter substitution in the known generalized-star results.

## Limitations and closest literature
The statement concerns only three consecutive centered triangular numbers; it does not resolve other centered polygonal families. Prior methods of symmetry and gluing are credited and reused. Primary sources: Yin–Mu–Komatsu (2024), Robles-Pérez–Rosales (2017), Fel (2016 preprint). The original 1970 Herzog paper is cited for the criterion through the mathematical literature, not claimed independently re-proved. External independent validation remains outstanding.
