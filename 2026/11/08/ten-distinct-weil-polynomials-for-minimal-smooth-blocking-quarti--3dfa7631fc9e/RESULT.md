# Ten distinct Weil polynomials for minimal smooth blocking quartics over \(\mathbf F_3\)

## Finding
Let \(C\) range over geometrically smooth plane quartics over \(\mathbf F_3\) whose six rational points form a **nontrivial blocking set**: every rational projective line meets \(C(\mathbf F_3)\), but none is contained in it. Six is the minimum cardinality of such a point set. Exactly \(51{,}948\) homogeneous quartic equations up to nonzero scalar have this property. They form ten \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\)-classes: nine of size \(5{,}616\) and one of size \(1{,}404\).

**New arithmetic classification.** The ten classes have **pairwise distinct** Hasse–Weil zeta numerators. Consequently the ten Jacobians are pairwise nonisogenous over \(\mathbf F_3\), even though all ten curves have precisely six rational points. With \(L_C(T)=1+2T+c_2T^2+c_3T^3+3c_2T^4+18T^5+27T^6\), the exact data are:

## Assumptions and scope
All curves are smooth over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbf F_3\), projective, geometrically connected, and embedded as degree-four plane curves. Their genus is \((4-1)(4-2)/2=3\). A line or point is rational if defined over \(\mathbf F_3\). A six-point set is nontrivially blocking if it meets each of the thirteen rational lines without containing all four points of any line. Equivalence is under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\); Weil polynomials and isogenies are over \(\mathbf F_3\), not its algebraic closure.

Normalize each projective coefficient vector by making its first nonzero coefficient \(1\), in the ordered monomial basis \((X^iY^jZ^{4-i-j})\), where \(i=4,3,2,1,0\) and for each \(i\), \(j=4-i,4-i-1,\ldots,0\). The three point-count fields are represented by the monic polynomials \(u\), \(u^2+1\), and \(u^3+2u+1\) over \(\mathbf F_3\), respectively; a separate check uses \(u^2+u+2\) and \(u^3+2u+2\).

## Proof
First enumerate all \(13\) points and \(13\) lines in \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_3)\), with one nonzero coordinate normalized to \(1\). Of the \(\binom{13}{6}=1716\) six-element point sets, exactly \(234\) are nontrivially blocking; this is an exhaustive finite integer calculation, not a sample. For the least bitmask-encoded such set \(B\), enumerate normalized invertible \(3\times3\) matrices over \(\mathbf F_3\): the group has order \(5616\), and the stabilizer of \(B\) has order \(24\). Therefore the \(234\) sets comprise one projective orbit.

For this fixed \(B\), requiring a homogeneous quartic to vanish at all six points gives a rank-six evaluation matrix in fifteen coefficients, hence a nine-dimensional coefficient kernel. Its \((3^9-1)/2=9841\) nonzero projective elements are enumerated. Exactly \(567\) have **no additional rational points**. For each such candidate, compute the Jacobian ideal generated by its three first partial derivatives on each of the three standard affine charts over \(\mathbf F_3\). Since \(4\equiv1\pmod3\), Euler's identity gives \(F=XF_X+YF_Y+ZF_Z\). Consequently the candidate is geometrically smooth if and only if all three chart Jacobian ideals are the unit ideal; exact finite-field Gröbner bases give precisely \(222\) smooth forms.

The stabilizer of \(B\) acts on these \(222\) forms by coordinate substitution and scalar normalization. Exhaustive multiplication of monomials and orbit enumeration yields nine orbits of size \(24\) and one orbit of size \(6\). The global class sizes are therefore \(234\cdot24=5616\) nine times and \(234\cdot6=1404\) once, giving \(51{,}948\) equations total.

For each of the ten representatives, all projective points over \(\mathbf F_3\), \(\mathbf F_9\), and \(\mathbf F_{27}\) are tested by exact finite-field polynomial evaluation. The counts \(N_1,N_2,N_3\) are exactly the table entries (with \(N_1=6\) for every row). Write \(s_n=3^n+1-N_n\). The logarithmic identity \(-T L_C'(T)/L_C(T)=\sum_{n\ge1}s_nT^n\) implies \(c_1=-s_1=2\), \(c_2=(s_1^2-s_2)/2\), and \(c_3=(-s_1^3+3s_1s_2-2s_3)/6\). The genus-three functional equation then forces coefficients \(c_4=3c_2\), \(c_5=9c_1=18\), and \(c_6=27\). The ten ordered pairs \((c_2,c_3)\) in the table are all distinct.

By the finite-field isogeny theorem for abelian varieties, Jacobians over the same finite field are isogenous if and only if their Frobenius characteristic polynomials coincide; equivalently their curves' zeta numerators coincide. Hence the ten Jacobians are pairwise nonisogenous over \(\mathbf F_3\).

## Verification
From this package, run `python verify_blocking_quartic_zeta.py` (requires Python 3 and SymPy). It derives all point sets, the coefficient kernel, the \(567\) candidates, exact chart ideal tests, the \(222\) smooth forms, all stabilizer orbits, all representative point counts, and all Weil numerators. A successful run ends in `BLOCKING_QUARTIC_ARITHMETIC_REPLAY_PASS`.

Run `python verify_independent_field.py` in the directory containing `representatives.json`; it independently evaluates the ten curves using different irreducible polynomials defining the extensions \(\mathbf F_9\) and \(\mathbf F_{27}\), reconstructs the genus-three Weil numerators, and checks that all ten differ. It ends in `BLOCKING_QUARTIC_INDEPENDENT_FIELD_PASS`. It requires only Python's standard library.

The exhaustive computations are finite mathematical certificates: the degree-four equations and all affine Jacobian ideal tests are complete; the field point counts are complete because each extension has respectively \(13\), \(91\), and \(757\) projective points. Neither numerical root approximations nor probabilistic sampling are used in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli, Ghioca, and Yip, *Plane curves giving rise to blocking sets over finite fields* (arXiv:2208.13299, first posted August 28, 2022), study existence, lower degrees, and projective-triangle constructions; their Theorem 5.1 explicitly assumes characteristic greater than three, and does not compute these ten quartics' zeta functions. Their *Most plane curves over finite fields are not blocking* (arXiv:2211.08523, first posted November 15, 2022) studies asymptotic statistics; Section 7, Example 7.2, gives rational-line union counts in \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_3)\) for degree at least five, but neither a smooth quartic arithmetic census nor the present isogeny separation. LMFDB catalogues abstract isogeny classes of abelian varieties over finite fields; such a list by itself does not identify those realized by the minimum nontrivial blocking plane-quartic locus. These comparisons do not amount to an exhaustive historical priority certification.

## Limitations
The classification applies only to smooth quartics over \(\mathbf F_3\) with the *minimum six-point nontrivial blocking set*. It makes no claim about larger blocking point sets, different ground fields, canonical models outside the plane-quartic locus, or geometric (base-changed) isogenies. The exact zeta values do not imply simplicity or absolute simplicity of the Jacobians. Earlier curve databases or differently indexed incidence tables may contain equivalent combined classifications; independent priority and mathematical audits have not been performed.
