# Ten forbidden negative supports and 3,326 Zariski chambers on the extremal rational elliptic surface with a nine-gon fiber

## Finding
Let \(X/\mathbb C\) be the minimal smooth rational elliptic surface with Kodaira fibers \(I_9+I_1+I_1+I_1\); an explicit Weierstrass model is \(y^2+txy-y=x^3\). Define the negative-support-size polynomial \(Z_X(u)=\sum_\Sigma u^{|\operatorname{Supp}(N_\Sigma)|}\), summing over the Zariski chambers of the big cone and taking the nef chamber to have empty negative support. Then
\[
Z_X(u)=1+12u+66u^2+220u^3+492u^4+768u^5+837u^6+612u^7+270u^8+48u^9.
\]
In particular \(z(X)=3326\); exactly \(48\) chambers have the maximum possible support size \(9\).

There are exactly \(12\) negative irreducible curves: the nine components \(F_0,\ldots,F_8\) of the \(I_9\) fiber, cyclically numbered, and the three torsion sections \(S_0,S_1,S_2\). Here \(S_j\) intersects \(F_{3j}\), indices of the \(F_i\) are modulo \(9\), and each pair of distinct sections is disjoint. The \(F_i\) have self-intersection \(-2\), the \(S_j\) self-intersection \(-1\), and all other intersections are given by the cycle edges and the three section attachments.

The minimal inclusions of curves which *fail* to have negative-definite intersection matrix are exactly **ten**: the whole nine-component fiber; the three four-curve stars \(\{S_j,F_{3j-1},F_{3j},F_{3j+1}\}\) for \(j=0,1,2\); and the six paths connecting two distinct section vertices through either one of the two arcs of the nine-cycle. A subset is a negative part support if and only if it contains none of these ten configurations.

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over \(\mathbb C\), on the smooth relatively minimal elliptic surface (not its singular Weierstrass model). The primitive fiber is a nef divisor and \(K_X\sim-F\). The support size counts irreducible curves, not coefficients or chamber facets. The Zariski-chamber correspondence counts the nef chamber once. Results are for this particular extremal elliptic surface, whose fiber configuration uniquely determines it in characteristic zero.

## Proof
**The surface and sections.** The Weierstrass equation has discriminant \(\Delta=-(t^3+27)\); it has three \(I_1\) fibers at the simple roots of the discriminant and an \(I_9\) fiber at infinity (Jarvis–Lang–Ricks, Section 1.F and Appendix A.1). The point \(P=(0,0)\) is a nonzero flex, as its tangent is \(y=0\) with intersection multiplicity three, hence is a section of exact order three. Its inverse is \(Q=(0,1)\). The two affine points remain distinct from each other and from the zero section \(O\) over every finite base point. Their intersections at infinity are determined by the height pairing as follows.

Shioda–Tate gives Mordell–Weil rank \(10-2-8=0\). Since \(\operatorname{NS}(X)\) is unimodular and the trivial lattice has discriminant \(9\), the torsion subgroup has order \(3\): these are all sections. For the nonzero torsion section \(P\), the height formula gives \(0=2+2(P\cdot O)-c(9-c)/9\), where \(0\le c<9\) indexes the fiber component meeting \(P\). Since \(c(9-c)/9\le 20/9<4\) and \(P\cdot O\ge0\), this forces \(P\cdot O=0\) and \(c=3\) or \(6\). Inversion interchanges components \(3\) and \(6\), so \(O,P,Q\) meet \(F_0,F_3,F_6\), respectively, after cyclic numbering. They are therefore disjoint at infinity as well.

**Exhaustion of negative curves.** For an irreducible curve \(C\) with \(C^2<0\), nefness of \(F\) and adjunction yield \(C^2=2p_a(C)-2+F\cdot C<0\). Therefore \(p_a(C)=0\), with \(F\cdot C=0\) or \(1\). In the first case \(C\) is a component of a reducible fiber, hence one of the nine \(F_i\); in the second it is a section, hence one of \(O,P,Q\). These exhaust all negative curves.

**Ten forbidden configurations.** Replace the negative intersection matrix by its negative, denoted \(A\), with diagonal entries \(2\) for the cycle and \(1\) for the sections, and entries \(-1\) for graph edges. A selected section whose attachment component is omitted forms an isolated positive-definite \(1\times1\) block. When its attachment is present, eliminate the section by its Schur complement; this reduces that cycle vertex's diagonal from \(2\) to \(1\). If the complete nine-cycle is selected, the cycle matrix already has the constant vector in its kernel. Otherwise, the selected cycle components form disjoint paths. Each path has diagonal \(2\), except those vertices with a selected attached section, whose diagonal is \(1\). An unmarked path has the positive-definite Cartan matrix of type \(A_\ell\). A path with one marked endpoint, and no other marks, is positive definite: in an ordering starting at that endpoint, every leading principal minor equals \(1\). If a marked vertex is internal, the three-vertex principal submatrix with diagonal \((2,1,2)\) is singular; this is one of the three stars together with its section. If two marked vertices occur in the same path and neither is internal, they must be its two endpoints; the path matrix has diagonal \((1,2,\ldots,2,1)\) and annihilates the all-ones vector. This is precisely one of the six possible section-to-section arcs. Thus avoiding the nine-cycle, the three stars and the six arcs is necessary and sufficient. Each of the ten is inclusion-minimal and singular, so the list is exact.

**Counting.** By Bauer–Funke–Neumann Proposition 1.1, negative-definite reduced supports correspond bijectively to non-nef Zariski chambers. The included verifier enumerates all \(2^{12}=4096\) masks and independently implements (i) avoidance of the ten forbidden masks and (ii) exact rational Schur-complement positive-definiteness for the full matrix. They agree on every subset and give, by support size \(r=0,\ldots,12\), the counts \((1,12,66,220,492,768,837,612,270,48,0,0,0)\). The count for \(r=0\) is the nef chamber. Summing gives \(3326\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_chambers.py` with Python 3.8 or later, in a directory containing the included code. It uses only the standard library. The stored `verification_output.txt` is reproduced exactly. Every integer principal-submatrix decision is checked using `fractions.Fraction`; the alternate forbidden-support test is purely combinatorial. The assertion that the ten configurations are exactly the minimal non-positive-definite supports is independently tested against the exhaustive matrix enumeration. The computation is finite and exhaustive for the proven twelve-curve configuration; it does not itself prove that the geometry has only these curves.

## Relationship to prior work
Miranda–Persson classify the extremal rational elliptic surfaces. Jarvis–Lang–Ricks (arXiv:0908.1831, first posted August 13, 2009) provide the explicit model and fiber classification. Bauer–Funke–Neumann (arXiv:0912.0958) establish the general chamber/negative-definite-support correspondence and an algorithm, while Bauer–Schmitz (arXiv:1204.4373) improve the computation algorithm and study the Segre–Schur quartic. The previously established general algorithms and the elliptic surface's classification are not claimed as novel. The new assertion is the *explicit* ten-obstruction classification and complete, size-refined chamber count for the cyclic nine-fiber with three torsion sections, which does not follow as a stated numerical theorem in these inspected works. The general algorithm makes the calculation accessible but does not supply its evaluated answer or minimal-obstruction characterization.

## Limitations
The theorem is for this characteristic-zero fiber configuration, not arbitrary elliptic surfaces or positive-characteristic reductions. It concerns Zariski chamber supports, not Mori chambers, their volumes, or wall-incidence numbers. The computational step has an independent exact arithmetic implementation and a theoretical forbidden-configuration proof, but no external independent audit has occurred. Historical priority remains conditional on unindexed specialist literature; an automated literature search cannot prove novelty.
