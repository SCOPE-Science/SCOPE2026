# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Root the original tree at any designated hub. Each eligible branch is the descendant side of an edge. Such sets are laminar; if one is strictly inside another, the parent of its root would be inside the larger branch, contrary to that parent being a hub. Thus distinct selected branches are disjoint. Counting ordered branch label sets, rooted internal trees and the arbitrary complement gives exactly the displayed mixed factorial moment, including the complement of size one and zero contribution when R>n−D. For fixed multiplicities the size factor tends to e^(−R); Poisson moments determine the joint law (and higher bounded moments give uniform integrability). The Borel marks sum to probability one by critical Poisson branching extinction. Independently, for a given component containing 1 of size k=n−d−q, contract it to a vertex with weighted edges k to each of the d hidden vertices and no edge to the q remaining visible vertices. The reduced weighted Laplacian determinant is kd n^(d−1)(d+q)^(q−1), by splitting into within-group sum-zero spaces and the group-constant subspace, also valid at q=0. Multiplying by the internal tree count and choosing the component recovers the claimed deficit law. Its limit has pgf exp(d(T(z/e)−1)), giving tight deficits. In the identification step, the reason two distinct deleted hubs cannot meet a component of at most K vertices is that the connecting path has length at most K+1, contradicting hub separation; two edges to the same hub would form a cycle. A later hub inside such a component would likewise be too close to a deleted hub. A small anchored branch cannot be the root component when n>2K+D and the deficit is at most K. These observations give the pathwise partial-union identity on the stated good event. The explicit forest-extension distance bound and summable marks then justify removing the common truncation in the discrete multiset space. Independent tree enumeration by edge subsets gave 121 exact comparisons on n=2,…,6, including D=3 and impossible branch totals; these corroborate, not prove, the asymptotic statement.

PASS

The supported distinct result is the joint fixed-window partial-union law for offroot component-size multisets, rather than its one-time marginals, its singleton covariance projection, or the general Cayley enumeration principle. The anchor-specific identity is useful as its exact input, but the one-anchor case is covered and the multi-anchor enumeration can be obtained by iterating the cut-and-leaf decomposition in Pitman's fringe-subtree proof. No separate novelty is credited to that decomposition. The inspected sources do not state or give the finite-sequence component-multiset coupling.

At a natural boundary window of the Cayley uncover process, single-time laws leave open how finite component sizes persist or change from one deletion to the next.
