# Scientific review: terminal cyclic quotient of the Fermat quartic threefold

## Correctness — PASS

The source threefold is the smooth Fermat quartic. The cyclic shift has five projective eigendirections, exactly four on the quartic. At those four points, direct differentiation in the Fourier eigenbasis leaves the three tangent characters equivalent to \((1,2,3)\) modulo five. Their four nonidentity Reid–Tai residue sums are \(6,7,8,9\), proving terminality and canonical index five. The action is free away from these points, so the singularity basket is complete. The quotient is quasi-étale in codimension one, and the anticanonical volume follows by degree five. The residue-ring and Chern-class computations establish all cohomological and Euler invariants. The exact standard-library verifier reproduces the finite arithmetic; the proof does not equate finite tests with a general certification of other families.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk

Zhu (arXiv:2610.39627v1, Theorem 1.1 and Example 3.1) records the full Fermat automorphism group and the relevant five-cycle, but the inspected geometric statements classify automorphisms rather than the induced quotient's singularity basket and cohomology. Yang–Yu–Zhu (arXiv:2405.09505) establishes the maximal-size automorphism characterization, not the quotient invariants. General Reid–Tai criteria and Griffiths residue techniques do not themselves specify this quotient's four local weights and six invariant Hodge dimensions. Search comparisons for the exact basket, quotient, eigenweights and Euler characteristics did not locate a published stronger result. The closest related quotient computations involve different varieties and cyclic groups. Classical quotient-Fano catalogues remain a possible source of implicit prior coverage; the search is not a proof of historical novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The distinguished order-five subgroup is canonical up to conjugacy inside the coordinate-permutation part of the maximal Fermat automorphism group. Determining its complete quotient singularity basket together with the canonical index, anticanonical volume, full rational Betti numbers, invariant Hodge dimension and orbifold Euler number supplies a coherent local-to-global geometric description. The jump from \(h^{2,1}(X)=30\) to \(h^{2,1}(Y)=6\) and the four isolated terminal non-Gorenstein points are natural invariants useful in studying threefold quotients and their intermediate Hodge structures, without asserting consequences not proved here.

## Closest literature and limitations

The automorphism group and coordinate shift are known; only the quotient invariants are assessed as new. The result covers neither arbitrary cyclic actions nor integral cohomology, and it does not assert a crepant resolution or a complete birational classification. Specialist historical review of quotient-Fano databases and independent checking remain outstanding.
