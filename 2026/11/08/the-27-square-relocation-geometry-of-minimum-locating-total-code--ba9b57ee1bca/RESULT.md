# The 27-square relocation geometry of minimum locating-total codes on the Pappus graph

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(G\) be the eighteen-vertex Pappus graph, viewed as the point-line incidence graph of the affine plane \(\operatorname{AG}(2,3)\) after deleting one of its four parallel classes of lines. A *locating-total dominating set* \(S\subseteq V(G)\) has an open neighbor in \(S\) at every vertex, and distinct vertices outside \(S\) have distinct sets of neighbors in \(S\). Then
\[
\gamma_t^L(G)=8.
\]
There are exactly \(108\) minimum locating-total dominating sets. Their token-jump (one selected vertex replaced by one arbitrary unselected vertex, retaining minimum feasibility) reconfiguration graph is \(27C_4\), the disjoint union of \(27\) four-cycles. Under token sliding (the replacement vertices must be adjacent in \(G\)), all \(108\) configurations are isolated.

The complete geometric classification is as follows. Let \(\mathcal W\) denote the deleted parallel class, and let \(\mathcal D\) be any of the three retained parallel classes. Choose \(W\in\mathcal W\), choose a retained direction \(\mathcal D\), and choose \(E\in\mathcal D\). Put \(q=W\cap E\). Choose one of the two points \(p\in E\setminus W\), and one of the two retained lines \(\ell\notin\mathcal D\) through \(q\). The eight selected vertices are the four **point vertices** in \(W\cup\{p\}\), and the four **line vertices** in \(\mathcal D\cup\{\ell\}\). Every minimum set arises uniquely in this way. The triple \((W,\mathcal D,E)\) labels one of the \(3\cdot3\cdot3=27\) four-cycle components; the two choices for each of \(p\) and \(\ell\) are the two independent binary coordinates of that cycle.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. The field \(\mathbb F_3\) is used for the nine affine points \((x,y)\). The retained lines are
\[
 x=c,\qquad y=c,\qquad y-x=c\quad(c\in\mathbb F_3),
\]
and the three **deleted** lines are \(x+y=c\). Only the nine retained lines become line vertices. Two graph vertices are adjacent if and only if one is a point, one is a retained line, and the point lies on the line. Thus there are nine point vertices and nine line vertices, with degree three on both sides. The graph is isomorphic to the usual Pappus cubic graph; an explicit eighteen-vertex relabelling certificate appears in `verify_model.py`.

For \(S\), let \(A\) be its selected point vertices, \(B\) its selected line vertices, and \(a=|A|\), \(b=|B|\). The neighbor signatures of unselected point vertices are subsets of \(B\); those of unselected line vertices are subsets of \(A\). Their nonempty signatures cannot coincide across the two bipartition classes. Consequently locating-total domination is equivalent to (i) every affine point lying on a line of \(B\), (ii) every retained line meeting a point of \(A\), and (iii) uniqueness of the signatures on unselected vertices separately in each bipartition class.

## Proof

**Step 1 (minimum size).** Every chosen point is on exactly three retained lines, and every chosen line has exactly three affine points. The two total-domination conditions imply \(a,b\ge3\). If \(a=3\), meeting all nine retained lines forces the three selected points to lie on one deleted-direction line \(W\): otherwise two chosen points share a retained line, so their total retained-line coverage is below nine. Each \(q\in W\) lies on exactly three retained lines, each of which then has the same selected-point signature \(\{q\}\). At most one of these three lines may remain unselected, so \(b\ge6\). Dually, if \(b=3\), coverage of all nine affine points forces the three selected lines to be a complete retained parallel class. Every point on one of those lines has the same selected-line signature; at most one point per such line may remain unselected, so \(a\ge6\). Therefore an LTD-set of size at most seven cannot exist: either one part has size three and the total is at least nine, or both parts have at least four vertices and the total is at least eight.

**Step 2 (four selected points).** Suppose \(a=b=4\). The four selected points must meet all nine retained lines. No three selected points lie on any retained line: if three did, then even the fourth point could not meet both remaining lines of that retained parallel class. Consequently every retained line contains at most two chosen points. The four points yield \(12\) point-line incidences; since there is no triple concurrence on a retained line, the number of *distinct* retained lines met is \(12-t\), where \(t\) counts pairs of chosen points whose joining line has one of the three retained directions. The six pairs of chosen points are partitioned by their unique affine directions. If \(s\) is the number of those pairs on a deleted-direction line, then \(t=6-s\), and covering all nine retained lines demands \(12-(6-s)=6+s\ge9\), hence \(s\ge3\). Partition the four chosen points among the three mutually disjoint deleted-direction lines, with cell sizes at most three. Unless some cell has size three, the sum of the pair counts within cells is at most two. Thus a cell has size three: \(A=W\cup\{p\}\) uniquely, with \(W\) a deleted line and \(p\notin W\).

**Step 3 (four selected lines).** Four retained lines covering all nine points must contain a full retained parallel class \(\mathcal D\). Indeed, if no direction contributes three lines, the occupation profile across the three directions is \((2,2,0)\) or \((2,1,1)\), up to permutation. Two parallel lines cover six points, and each of the remaining two lines, being in a different direction, meets both of these parallel lines; it adds at most one new point. The four lines would cover at most eight points. Thus \(B=\mathcal D\cup\{\ell\}\), uniquely, for a line \(\ell\) of a second retained direction.

**Step 4 (compatibility and sufficiency).** Write \(E\) for the unique line of \(\mathcal D\) containing \(p\), and \(q=E\cap W\). The two retained lines through \(q\) outside \(\mathcal D\) both have signature exactly \(\{q\}\) from the selected point set \(A\). Consequently at least one must belong to \(B\), forcing \(\ell\) to be one of those two lines. Conversely, if \(\ell\) is one of them, all vertices are totally dominated. The five unselected point vertices are distinguished by their selected line from \(\mathcal D\), with the two unselected points on either of the two lines of \(\mathcal D\setminus\{E\}\) distinguished by whether they lie on \(\ell\); on \(E\) there is just one unselected point. For unselected line vertices, the intersection with \(W\) gives three signature groups. At \(q\), only one of the two lines outside \(\mathcal D\) remains unselected, carrying signature \(\{q\}\). At either other point \(q'\in W\), the two outside-\(\mathcal D\) lines are distinguished by whether they pass through \(p\), giving \(\{q'\}\) versus \(\{q',p\}\). All signatures are nonempty and pairwise different. This proves necessity and sufficiency of the stated construction.

There are \(3\) choices of \(W\), \(3\) of \(\mathcal D\), \(3\) of \(E\), \(2\) of \(p\), and \(2\) of \(\ell\), giving exactly \(108\) configurations. The construction also attains the lower bound eight.

**Step 5 (relocation).** Each minimum code has exactly four point vertices and four line vertices. A single legal replacement therefore exchanges two point vertices or two line vertices, never one of each. If the chosen deleted line \(W\) changed, the old and new selected point sets (three vertices on two disjoint parallel lines, plus one extra vertex each) would share at most two points, so could not differ by one replacement. Analogously changing \(\mathcal D\) would change at least two selected line vertices. Thus every legal jump fixes \(W\) and \(\mathcal D\). A point replacement also fixes \(E\), because \(\ell\) meets \(W\) at exactly the point \(q\), which determines \(E\). Likewise, a line replacement fixes \(E\) through the unchanged \(p\). Hence exactly two jumps are possible: exchange the two alternatives for \(p\), or exchange the two alternatives for \(\ell\). These independent involutions produce one \(C_4\) for every \((W,\mathcal D,E)\). Since distinct point vertices are never adjacent to each other in the incidence graph, nor are distinct line vertices, no legal jump can be a token slide.

## Verification

The argument above proves the classification without relying on computation. As independent finite corroboration, `verify_direct.py` examines every candidate selected vertex set by cardinality using a static eighteen-vertex, twenty-seven-edge realization, computes all optimal sets, and constructs both reconfiguration graphs. `verify_geometry.py` builds the affine incidence relation directly from the field \(\mathbb F_3\), checks all \(\binom94^2=15876\) four-point/four-line selections, verifies all three geometric cover forms and the twenty-seven Cartesian squares, and independently checks feasibility. `verify_model.py` checks an explicit vertex bijection between the conventional cubic realization and the affine incidence model. Execution outputs `verify_direct.out`, `verify_geometry.out`, and `verify_model.out` are included. All three checks passed from the finalized package paths. Finite verification is corroboration; the proof itself uses only exact affine-plane incidence.

## Relationship to prior work

The 2014 preprint by Balbuena, Foucaud and Hansberg studies locating-dominating sets and identifying codes in graphs of girth at least five, and specifically identifies locating-total domination as an interesting distinct extension (conclusion, pages 18–19 of the twenty-page version). Its results do not provide this Pappus locating-total classification. Henning and Jafari Rad's 2012 work establishes locating-total domination bounds and special graph families; the available abstract and indexed passages do not supply the present exact incidence classification, although its entire publisher full text was not accessible for a decisive page-by-page comparison. The code predicate here requires every vertex, including selected ones, to have a selected open neighbor. It is neither ordinary closed-neighborhood location domination nor closed-neighborhood identifying coding. The computed geometry is not implied by an optimum size or by prior results about those different predicates.

## Limitations

This is an exact theorem about the Pappus graph, not a uniform classification for all cubic bipartite graphs or all Levi graphs of affine configurations. The result determines the minimum layer and its one-token mobility; it does not classify higher-cardinality LTD sets or multi-token replacements. The novelty comparison is best-knowledge, not a proof of historical priority. An unavailable complete text of the 2012 locating-total-domination paper and potentially unindexed earlier enumerations remain specific residual risks. No independent scholarly audit was performed.
