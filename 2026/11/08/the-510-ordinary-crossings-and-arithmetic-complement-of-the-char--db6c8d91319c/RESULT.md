# The 510 ordinary crossings and arithmetic complement of the characteristic-two sixty-line quartic

## Finding

Let \(a\) satisfy \(a^4+a^3+a^2+a+1=0\) in \(\mathbb F_{16}\), and put \(\mu=1+a^2+a^3\in\mathbb F_4\). For the elementary symmetric polynomials \(e_i\) in five coordinates, consider the smooth quartic K3 surface
\[
S=V(e_1,e_4+\mu e_2^2)\subset \mathbb P^4_{\mathbb F_4}.
\]
Let \(D\subset S\) be the reduced union of its sixty geometric projective lines, known explicitly from Rams--Schuett. The geometric line configuration and the zeta function of the ambient surface have been calculated previously. The finding here concerns the *reduced union* of the lines and its complement.

**Theorem (complete boundary and open-surface arithmetic).** Each of the sixty lines is defined over \(\mathbb F_{16}\). The 60 lines have exactly 510 distinct geometric intersection points, each on exactly two lines, all rational over \(\mathbb F_{16}\); there is no point on three lines. These are ordinary transverse nodes of the reduced divisor \(D\). Every \(\mathbb F_{16}\)-point of any of the sixty lines is one of the 510 nodes. The 30 Frobenius-conjugate line pairs are skew, so
\[
\#D(\mathbb F_{4^m})=
\begin{cases}0,&m\text{ odd},\\60\cdot4^m-450,&m\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]
Consequently, for the complementary open surface \(U=S\setminus D\),
\[
\boxed{Z(U/\mathbb F_4,T)=\frac{(1-16T^2)^{20}}{(1-T)(1-16T)(1-T^2)^{225}(1-3T+16T^2)}.}
\]
In particular \(\#U(\mathbb F_4)=20\) and \(\#U(\mathbb F_{16})=44\). The all-extension formula for \(U\) follows from this zeta identity. The ambient-surface zeta function
\[
Z(S/\mathbb F_4,T)=\frac{1}{(1-T)(1-16T)(1-16T^2)^{10}(1-3T+16T^2)}
\]
is explicitly **prior arithmetic context, not part of the newly assessed contribution**.

## Assumptions and scope

All fields have characteristic two. The fixed model is the explicit \(\mathbb F_4\)-surface above, rather than an unspecified geometric isomorphism class or a change of its descent. The element \(a\) is the class of the polynomial variable in \(\mathbb F_2[a]/(a^4+a^3+a^2+a+1)\). The other choice of primitive cube root \(\mu\) gives the Galois-conjugate model. The surface is smooth by Rams--Schuett, arXiv:1512.01358, Section 8. That source also supplies the sixty-line orbit and the rank-twenty Neron--Severi group generated by those lines. The geometric line orbit and the projective surface zeta function are both prior results. The new assertion is the exact reduced boundary and its open-surface zeta function, not the already determined Frobenius polynomial of the projective K3.

The zeta functions are Hasse--Weil zeta functions. All assertions about points and crossings use geometric points unless a field of definition is expressly given. The divisor \(D\) is the reduced divisor, not a multiplicity-weighted sum.

## Proof

**Line orbit and intersections.** Put \(A=a^3+a+1\) and \(B=a^3+a^2+a+1\). By the explicit formula in Rams--Schuett, a representative line is given in the coordinate hyperplane \(e_1=0\) by
\[
 x_3+x_2+Ax_1=0,\qquad x_4+Bx_2+ax_1=0.
\]
It has the basis
\[
 (a^3,1,0,A,a),\qquad (B,0,1,1,B).
\]
Permuting the five coordinates gives exactly sixty distinct lines: the stabilizer of the displayed line has order two. The quartic equation vanishes identically on each one: the exact verifier evaluates its restriction at all seventeen projective parameter points of \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{16})\), more than its degree four. The cited paper proves that these are all geometric lines.

The same exact computation puts each two-dimensional vector span in reduced row-echelon form over \(\mathbb F_{16}\), records the image under \(x\mapsto x^4\), and enumerates every rational projective point of each line. There are thirty transposed pairs and zero fixed lines. Each conjugate pair is skew because the union of its four basis rows has rank four. Every line has precisely seventeen meeting lines. Among the \(1020\) line-point incidences over \(\mathbb F_{16}\), there are exactly \(510\) distinct points, each of multiplicity two. All geometric intersections of two \(\mathbb F_{16}\)-defined projective lines are \(\mathbb F_{16}\)-rational, so this finite enumeration is exhaustive, even after algebraic closure. Since \(S\) is smooth and any two distinct lines through a point have distinct tangent directions, these points are ordinary transverse crossings.

**Antecedent Picard Frobenius (included to make the open-surface formula self-contained).** Distinct lines on a smooth quartic K3 have intersection one if they meet and zero if they are skew; each line has self-intersection minus two. Let \(G\) be the resulting \(60\)-by-\(60\) Gram matrix. Its reduction modulo \(101\) has rank \(20\). Frobenius permutes its sixty indices in thirty disjoint pairs. Applying \(G\) to the plus and minus eigenspaces of that permutation has ranks \(10\) and \(10\) modulo \(101\), respectively (the verifier supplies an exact finite-field elimination certificate). Ranks modulo \(101\) are lower bounds for the corresponding rational ranks. Since the cited paper proves that the line classes span \(\operatorname{NS}\) of rank \(20\), both lower bounds are equalities, and the Frobenius eigenvalue multiplicities on the rank-twenty Picard group are exactly \(10,10\).

**Antecedent point counts and transcendental factor (reproduced as an input).** Use \(e_1=0\) to represent each projective point uniquely as
\[
 [y_0:y_1:y_2:y_3:y_0+y_1+y_2+y_3],
\]
where the first nonzero \(y_i\) is normalized to one. Exhaustively evaluating \(e_4+\mu e_2^2\) on these \((4^4-1)/3\) and \((16^4-1)/15\) projective vectors gives respectively \(20\) and \(554\) points on \(S\). This previously determined finite count is reproduced by `verify.py` to validate the input to the new open-surface zeta calculation.

For a smooth quartic K3, \(H^1=H^3=0\) and \(\dim H^2=22\). Twenty \(H^2\)-eigenvalues are \(+4\) and \(-4\), ten of each, by the line-class computation. If the remaining two eigenvalues are \(\alpha,\beta\), the two exact point counts give
\[
 \alpha+\beta=20-(1+16)=3,\qquad
 \alpha^2+\beta^2=554-(1+256+20\cdot16)=-23.
\]
Therefore \(\alpha\beta=(3^2+23)/2=16\), recovering the previously determined surface zeta function by the Lefschetz trace formula. Notice that the discriminant of the residual quadratic is \(-55\), matching the discriminant appearing in the published Picard-lattice calculation; this is an arithmetic consistency observation, not an independent input to the proof.

**The boundary and complement.** If \(m\) is odd, a point of \(D(\mathbb F_{4^m})\) would lie on some line \(L\) and, by field Frobenius, on its conjugate; these two lines are skew. Hence the boundary has no such point. If \(m\) is even, all sixty lines and all \(510\) pairwise crossings are rational over \(\mathbb F_{4^m}\); inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
 \#D(\mathbb F_{4^m})=60(4^m+1)-510=60\cdot4^m-450.
\]
Using the exponential definition of zeta functions, these counts yield
\[
 Z(D/\mathbb F_4,T)=\frac{(1-T^2)^{225}}{(1-16T^2)^{30}}.
\]
Since \(S\) is the disjoint union of \(U\) and \(D\), dividing its zeta function by the boundary zeta gives the asserted formula for \(U\). The two all-extension point-count formulas follow immediately from these zeta functions.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` implements \(\mathbb F_{16}\) using the irreducible polynomial \(a^4+a^3+a^2+a+1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). It checks the line orbit of size sixty, all line equations, all pairwise ranks, all \(1020\) line-point incidences, all line Frobenius images, modular Gram ranks, and exhaustive surface counts over \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_{16}\). It confirms \(510\) double points, no geometric higher-order intersections, eigen ranks \(10,10\), and the finite-field counts \(20,554\). Run `python3 verify.py` from a directory containing that file; `verify_output.txt` records the expected output. No external package or network access is required.

The finite computations prove the expressly finite enumerations; the statements for all extension degrees use the field-of-definition argument, K3 cohomology, and the trace formula. No finite sampling is presented as an infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Rams and Schuett, arXiv:1512.01358 (first posted December 4, 2015), Section 8, provide the quartic, the sixty-line \(S_5\)-orbit over \(\mathbb F_{16}\), valency seventeen and the rank-twenty Neron--Severi lattice with discriminant \(-55\). Those statements are prior work. Degtyarev, arXiv:1604.05836, establishes upper bounds and configurations for characteristic-two quartics; Degtyarev--Itenberg--Sertoz, arXiv:1601.04238, classifies large line configurations over the complex numbers; Veniani, arXiv:1606.02135, treats characteristic-two quartic K3 line bounds and the separate 68-line singular family. The original geometric sources do not give the 510 **distinct** intersection points, the all-extension boundary count or the complement zeta function. A previous exact calculation already supplies the Frobenius eigenspace split and the projective surface zeta; neither is claimed as new. An earlier line-graph study already supplies the sixty-line intersection graph, its 510 edges, and the 30 skew Frobenius-conjugate pairs, but graph edges alone do not determine how many distinct geometric intersection points exist (three intersecting lines could be concurrent), nor whether all 17 rational points of each line are intersection points. The node scheme and open-surface arithmetic require the additional line-point census.

The boundary calculation refines the existing geometric graph and projective zeta results. Uninspected specialist records could contain the same line-union point count or complement zeta; priority is provisional.

## Limitations

This calculation concerns this particular \(\mathbb F_4\)-descent and its reduced sixty-line boundary, not arbitrary twists of the same geometric K3, other parameters in the \(S_5\)-invariant quartic pencil, or all K3 surfaces with Picard discriminant \(-55\). The open-surface zeta calculation uses the previously computed projective-surface zeta function as an antecedent; its arithmetic inputs are also independently replayed here. The Picard rank twenty is a published input. There has been no independent external verification or exhaustive historical priority certification. Source and computation checks are reproducible from the included references and script.
