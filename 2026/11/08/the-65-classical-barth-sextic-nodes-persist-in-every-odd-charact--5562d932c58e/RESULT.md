# The 65 classical Barth-sextic nodes persist in every odd characteristic

## Finding

Let \(R=\mathbb Z[\tau,1/2]/(\tau^2-\tau-1)\). In \(\mathbb P^3_R\), with coordinates \([x:y:z:w]\), consider the homogeneous sextic
\[
F=4(\tau^2x^2-y^2)(\tau^2y^2-z^2)(\tau^2z^2-x^2)
 -(1+2\tau)(x^2+y^2+z^2-w^2)^2w^2.
\]
There are **65 pairwise disjoint \(R\)-valued sections**, specified below, of the relative singular locus of \(F=0\). Each section is an ordinary double point in every geometric fiber over \(\operatorname{Spec}R\). In particular, for every algebraically closed field \(k\) of odd characteristic and every \(\tau\in k\) satisfying \(\tau^2-\tau-1=0\), the resulting sextic has **at least 65 distinct ordinary double points**, defined already over the field generated by \(\tau\) over the prime field. This includes characteristics \(3\) and \(5\), without any separability assumption on the quadratic parameter equation.

Equivalently, the designated classical 65-node configuration admits a split finite étale model over \(R\): no odd prime causes two designated nodes to coalesce or a designated quadratic tangent cone to degenerate. This is a statement about the **designated nodes**, not a claim of good reduction for the entire singular scheme.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(\tau^2=\tau+1\), so both \(\tau\) and \(\tau-1\) are units, and \(\tau^2\) is a unit. The base ring \(R\) is the ring of golden-ratio integers with \(2\) inverted. Define the 15 triples
\[
\mathcal D=\{(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\}\ \cup\
\bigcup_{j=0}^{2}\operatorname{cyc}^{j}
\{(1,\epsilon\tau,\epsilon\delta\tau^2):\epsilon,\delta\in\{\pm1\}\},
\]
where \(\operatorname{cyc}(a,b,c)=(b,c,a)\). A triple in the first group is called axial, and a triple in the second group cross-type. The following 65 projective sections are defined over \(R\):

- For each \(v\in\mathcal D\), the 15 infinity points \([v:0]\).
- For each axial \(v\), the two points \([v:1]\), \([v:-1]\); for each cross-type \(v\), the two points \([v:2\tau]\), \([v:-2\tau]\), giving 30 sphere points.
- The eight points \([\epsilon:\delta:\eta:1]\) with independent signs, and the twelve points \([\operatorname{cyc}^j(0,\epsilon(\tau-1),\delta\tau):1]\), with \(j=0,1,2\) and independent signs.

Points are counted as projective sections, not up to surface automorphisms or isomorphism classes. No assertion is made about the scheme-theoretic *entire* singular locus in positive characteristic.

## Proof

First, the coordinates belong to \(R\). Both the polynomial and its four first derivatives vanish identically in \(\mathbb Z[\tau]/(\tau^2-\tau-1)\) at every displayed point. For the first 45 points there is also an immediate geometric explanation. The polynomial is \(4ABC-(1+2\tau)Q^2w^2\), where \(A,B,C\) are the three displayed quadratic factors and \(Q=x^2+y^2+z^2-w^2\). Each \(v\in\mathcal D\) lies in at least two distinct linear factors of \(ABC\). At \([v:0]\), the second summand and its first derivatives vanish because of the factor \(w^2\); at the sphere points they vanish because \(Q=0\). For axial triples \(v\cdot v=1\), and for cross-type triples \(v\cdot v=4\tau^2\). The remaining 20 point/gradient identities follow by substitution using \(\tau^2=\tau+1\). The exact standalone certificate checks all 65 identities without floating-point arithmetic.

For the local quadratic tangent cones, choose any homogeneous coordinate that is a unit in \(\mathbb Z[\tau]\), and work in its affine projective chart. Each of the 65 points has such a coordinate. Direct determinants of the resulting \(3\times3\) Hessians have the following **absolute algebraic norms**, grouped into six symmetry types (with their sizes):

| Type | Number | Absolute Hessian-determinant norm |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Infinity axial | 3 | \(2^{14}\) |
| Infinity cross-type | 12 | \(2^{34}\) |
| Sphere axial | 6 | \(2^{18}\) |
| Sphere cross-type | 24 | \(2^{38}\) |
| Outer diagonal | 8 | \(2^{30}\) |
| Outer cyclic | 12 | \(2^{30}\) |

These are exact identities in \(\mathbb Z\), not numerical estimates. Inverting \(2\) makes every determinant a unit in \(R\). In every fiber of characteristic other than two the quadratic tangent cone therefore has rank three, so each of these points is an ordinary double point and is an isolated singularity of the surface germ.

It remains to prove the 65 sections do not collide in any odd characteristic. Given two distinct coordinate quadruples \(P,Q\), take their six \(2\times2\) projective minors \(m_{ij}=P_iQ_j-P_jQ_i\). The algebraic norm \(N(a+b\tau)=a^2+ab-b^2\) is integral. The gcd of the absolute norms of these six minors is, for **each** of the \(\binom{65}{2}=2080\) pairs, a positive power of two (possibly \(1\)). Exactly 679 pairs have a gcd greater than one. If two sections coincided over a residue field of odd characteristic \(p\), all six minors would vanish at the corresponding prime ideal, and \(p\) would divide all six integer norms. This contradicts their power-of-two gcd. Hence every pair remains distinct in all odd-characteristic fibers. Together with nondegeneracy, this proves the theorem.

## Verification

Run \(`python3 verify.py`\). This standalone standard-library script constructs \(F\) and all its first and second partial derivatives in the **exact ring** \(\mathbb Z[\tau]/(\tau^2-\tau-1)\). It generates all 65 sections, verifies vanishing of the polynomial and gradient, verifies that the Hessian determinant norm for every section is a nonzero power of two, and tests all 2080 pairwise projective-minor norm gcds. Expected output begins \(`BARTH_ODD_NODE_CERTIFICATE_OK`\), with \(`nodes=65`\) and \(`possible_bad_primes={2}`\).

An independent six-representative cross-check is \(`python3 verify_representatives.py`\) (requires SymPy). It differentiates a separate symbolic expression for \(F\), dehomogenizes in chosen charts, and verifies the six signed Hessian norms \(-2^{14},-2^{34},-2^{18},-2^{38},-2^{30},-2^{30}\).

## Relationship to prior work

Barth's original complex sextic and the fact that it has 65 nodes are classical. Its equation and the 15-infinity/50-affine distribution are widely documented. The substantive arithmetic assertion here is **uniform persistence and pairwise separation of all 65 nodes at every odd prime**, including the delicate fibers in characteristics three and five. The usual statement in characteristic zero or at sufficiently large primes does not establish an exceptional-prime-free conclusion; the specific Hessian-unit and minor-norm certificates do.

Bruin, Thomas, and Várilly-Alvarado (arXiv:1912.08908, first posted December 18, 2019), Section 8.1, use Barth's sextic for symmetric-differential constraints over characteristic zero, with finite-field computations for reconstruction. Their studied claim is not uniform preservation of the node configuration in all odd residue characteristics. The 2017 account of Barth's research and MathWorld's description likewise discuss the classical complex configuration. The original 1996 article was not available in complete readable form for comparison, so older priority remains a risk.

## Limitations

This theorem proves **at least** 65 ordinary nodes in every odd characteristic. It does **not** establish that these are all the singularities in the special fibers, or that the full family has no additional bad reduction, or that the hypersurface remains geometrically irreducible in every characteristic. The maximality theorem for 65 complex sextic nodes cannot be transferred to positive characteristic without further justification. The claim is not a new construction of Barth's 65 complex nodes; it is an explicit arithmetic persistence theorem for their integral model. A computer-assisted finite exact certificate proves the critical integer-ring identities; the source code is included and replayable.
