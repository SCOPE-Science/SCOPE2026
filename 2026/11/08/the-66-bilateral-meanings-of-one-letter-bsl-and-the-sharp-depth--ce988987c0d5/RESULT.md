# The 66 bilateral meanings of one-letter BSL and the sharp depth-two threshold

## Finding

For the one-letter language of bilateral state-based logic (BSL) with constants \(\bot\), \(\mathrm{NE}\), literal \(p\), bilateral negation, conjunction, splitting disjunction, and the **internal** possibility operator \(\Diamond\), there are exactly **66 distinct pairs of support and antisupport denotations** on all teams of Boolean valuations. Here two formulas are identified only if both their support and antisupport agree at every team, a stronger equivalence than ordinary support equivalence.

The numbers of such paired denotations definable with maximum \(\Diamond\)-nesting depth at most \(d\) are, respectively,
\[
\lvert\mathcal{B}_0\rvert=39,\qquad
\lvert\mathcal{B}_1\rvert=64,\qquad
\lvert\mathcal{B}_d\rvert=66\quad(d\ge2).
\]
Consequently every formula has a strongly equivalent formula of \(\Diamond\)-depth at most two, and the bound two is sharp. The two profiles requiring depth two are \((10,12)\) and \((12,10)\) in the encoding below, witnessed by \(\Diamond\neg\Diamond p\) and its negation. By contrast, already the depth-zero language realizes all **11 support-only denotations**.

## Assumptions and scope

Use exactly the propositional BSL grammar and bilateral semantics described in Aloni et al., *Teams, Truthmakers, Translations*, Section 3.1. The only classical valuations are \(v_0(p)=0\) and \(v_1(p)=1\). A team is a subset of \(\{v_0,v_1\}\); the four teams, in order, are
\[
\tau_0=\varnothing,\quad \tau_1=\{v_0\},\quad
\tau_2=\{v_1\},\quad \tau_3=\{v_0,v_1\}.
\]
There are no accessibility relations or external epistemic modalities. The support family of \(\varphi\) is \(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^+\), its antisupport family is \(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^-\), and a semantic profile is the ordered pair of these families. For any family \(\mathcal F\) of teams, encode it as the integer
\[
m(\mathcal F)=\sum_{j=0}^3 2^j\,\mathbf 1_{\tau_j\in\mathcal F}.
\]
An ordered pair of such integers denotes a semantic profile. The \(\Diamond\)-depth of atomic formulas and constants is zero, negation preserves depth, conjunction and splitting disjunction take the maximum of their argument depths, and \(\Diamond\) increases depth by one.

Under the standard BSL clauses, \(\Diamond\varphi\) is supported at a team precisely when a **nonempty subteam** supports \(\varphi\), while it is antisupported precisely when that team antisupports \(\varphi\). Splitting disjunction uses union, not necessarily a disjoint partition; its antisupport clause is intersection of antisupport families. Negation exchanges support and antisupport. The empty team is included.

## Proof

Define the following two operations on families of teams:
\[
\mathcal F\star\mathcal G
=\{S\cup T:S\in\mathcal F,\ T\in\mathcal G\},\qquad
E(\mathcal F)
=\{T:\exists S\in\mathcal F,\ \varnothing\ne S\subseteq T\}.
\]
If \(\operatorname{pair}(\varphi)=(A,B)\), these semantics give exactly the following profile operations:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{pair}(\bot)&=(1,15),&
\operatorname{pair}(\mathrm{NE})&=(14,1),&
\operatorname{pair}(p)&=(5,3),\\
\neg(A,B)&=(B,A),\\
(A,B)\wedge(C,D)&=(A\cap C,\ B\star D),\\
(A,B)\vee(C,D)&=(A\star C,\ B\cap D),\\
\Diamond(A,B)&=(E(A),B).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here set operations on numeric masks mean the corresponding operations on the encoded families, not ordinary arithmetic on the numbers. This is a direct rewriting of every semantic clause, and establishes by induction on formulas that the least algebra generated by the three atomic pairs is exactly the set of definable paired denotations.

For a finite upper-bound certificate, write \(\mathcal T\) for the following set of pairs: its left column lists the first component, and its right column lists **every allowed second component** for that first component.

| First mask | Allowed second masks |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 0,1,2,3,4,5,8,10,12,14,15 |
| 1 | 0,8,10,12,14,15 |
| 2 | 0,4,5,8,12 |
| 3 | 0,4,5,8,12 |
| 4 | 0,2,3,8,10 |
| 5 | 0,2,3,8,10 |
| 8 | 0,1,2,3,4,5,8,10,12,14,15 |
| 10 | 0,1,4,5,8,12 |
| 12 | 0,1,2,3,8,10 |
| 14 | 0,1,8 |
| 15 | 0,1,8 |

The row lengths sum to \(66\). The self-contained `checker.py` verifies, using finite four-team loops, that \(\mathcal T\) contains the three generators and is closed under all four profile operations. It also constructs every element of \(\mathcal T\) from those generators by an exhaustive monotone closure iteration. Thus no other formula profiles are possible, and all 66 are indeed definable.

To obtain sharp depth bounds, let \(\mathcal B_0\) be the least set containing the three generators and closed under negation, conjunction and splitting disjunction. For \(d\ge0\), let
\[
\mathcal B_{d+1}=
\operatorname{Cl}_{\neg,\wedge,\vee}
\Bigl(\mathcal B_d\cup\{\Diamond X:X\in\mathcal B_d\}\Bigr).
\]
Induction on syntax proves that \(\mathcal B_d\) is **exactly** the set of paired denotations definable at \(\Diamond\)-depth at most \(d\). Enumerating the finite closures gives cardinalities \(39,64,66,66\), and \(\mathcal B_2=\mathcal T=\mathcal B_3\); thus every subsequent layer is also equal to \(\mathcal T\), by induction. The checker verifies the layer closures and the atomic upper-bound certificate separately.

Finally, the pair of \(\Diamond\neg\Diamond p\) is \((10,12)\), and its negation has pair \((12,10)\). Both are in \(\mathcal B_2\setminus\mathcal B_1\), which the closure calculation explicitly checks; this proves depth two necessary. The 11 distinct first components of \(\mathcal T\) all occur already in \(\mathcal B_0\), so this separation concerns **two-sided denotations**, not support-only equivalence.

## Verification

Run `python3 checker.py` and `python3 checker_direct.py` with an ordinary Python 3 interpreter. The first code implements the semantic operations with bit masks; the second independently represents teams as frozensets and recomputes the layer closures. Both verify the ordered list \(39,64,66,66\). The first additionally checks all \(66\) table pairs for closure under negation, possibility, and each binary connective against all \(66\) partners; computes the two missing depth-two profiles; verifies that the masks describe weak ideals; and checks the full 66-pair table.

The programs perform finite exhaustive verification, not sampling. Completeness for formulas of **arbitrary syntax depth** follows from the preceding structural induction and verified fixed-point closure; it does not follow merely from enumerating sample formulas. The result does not require external theorem provers.

## Relationship to prior work

Aloni, Baltag, van Benthem, Bezhanishvili and Knudstorp (2026), Section 3.1, specify exactly the bilateral BSL language used here and give a faithful semilattice-modal translation in Section 4. They do **not** state the one-letter paired-denotation census or the sharp internal-\(\Diamond\)-depth profile in the inspected text. The result is motivated by their paired translation semantics.

Anttila and Knudstorp (2025) establish general support-expressivity theorems for convex, union-closed weak ideals; the 11 support-only denotations in the present calculation are consistent with this earlier framework, and no novelty is claimed for support expressivity alone. Their inspected text does not identify the **simultaneously realizable support/antisupport pairs** or this exact two-sided depth filtration.

Aloni, Anttila and Yang (2023) study state-based **modal** logics with external accessibility-based operators. Their general bilateral framework does not determine the small algebra for BSL's **internal** nonempty-subteam operator by implication. These distinctions are mathematical, not just terminology; a covering theorem for both polarities together would supersede the present census.

## Limitations

The domain consists of the two valuations of one propositional letter and all four teams. The count does not apply unchanged to two or more propositional letters, first-order teams, arbitrary Kripke frames, or languages with additional external modalities. The depth cutoff is for full **paired** semantics, not for support alone. The exact count is a finite natural invariant, not a claim about asymptotic growth or proof complexity.

The numerical table is fully reproducible from source code, but independent expert review and exhaustive historical-priority verification have not been performed. A previously unpublished or unindexed calculation of the same paired subalgebra remains a priority risk.
