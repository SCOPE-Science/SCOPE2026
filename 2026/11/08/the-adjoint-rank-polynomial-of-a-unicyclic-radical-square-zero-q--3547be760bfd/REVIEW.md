# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For an element with vertex coefficients \(s_v\) and arrow coefficients \(t_e\), direct multiplication makes its commutator row for \(e:i\to j\) equal to \((s_i-s_j)w_e+t_e(u_j-u_i)\). Rows with nonzero vertex difference pivot on unique arrow coordinates; the remaining rows give a restricted incidence matrix. A subgraph of a unicyclic graph has exactly one incidence dependency if and only if it contains the full cycle. This proves the rank formula (1) in RESULT.md without characteristic assumptions. The cycle-sum constraint and finite-field additive-character orthogonality yield equation (2), including the correction for the unique exceptional full-cycle support. All coefficients and reconstruction claims follow. Direct multiplication-table enumeration over three finite fields agrees in 13 cases; finite checks are not substituted for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked material
Jonsson (arXiv:1804.07082, 2018) studies the same cyclic Nakayama radical-square-zero family, but classifies bimodule cells rather than elementwise inner-derivation ranks. Sánchez-Flores (arXiv:1004.2820) treats outer derivation Lie algebras and the incidence differential, not the rank-generating polynomial for inner derivations. A previously obtained commuting probability is a weighted specialization of the rank distribution, not the full distribution; the present formula supplies every rank stratum as well as the reconstruction of the graph's unique cycle length. Related published research on Kronecker commutator fibers and Lie-algebra adjoint ranks treats different algebra families. No directly covering rank polynomial was found in the inspected sources and research-record searches. This is not a claim of worldwide priority: unindexed broader formulas remain a plausible risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The distribution of centralizer dimensions is a natural finite-algebra invariant, considerably finer than the average commuting probability. The closed formula applies across all connected simple unicyclic quiver shapes, all orientations, and all finite fields, and proves an invariant reconstruction theorem: the number of vertices and unique cycle length are readable from the rank polynomial, although tree placements are invisible. This is a natural family-wide classification, not an arbitrary isolated coefficient computation.

## Limitations and closest sources
The formula does not count distinct embedded centralizer subalgebras or handle loops, repeated edges, or multiple independent cycles. Closest primary works are arXiv:1804.07082 (same cyclic radical-square-zero algebras; different bimodule invariant) and arXiv:1004.2820 (outer derivations; different rank question). Unidentified general weighted-matroid or finite-algebra centralizer enumeration theorems could imply the formula. The current review is nonindependent.
