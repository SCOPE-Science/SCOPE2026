# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(n\ge7\), the two closed-neighborhood parity equations eliminate to a reversible order-eight recurrence. Its polynomial is \((t+1)^4(t^4+t^3+t^2+t+1)\), which divides \(t^{20}+1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\); hence every solution is simultaneously \(20\)- and \(n\)-periodic and therefore \(\gcd(n,20)\)-periodic. The six finite period classes are exhaustively certified by all \(256\) initial words, with an independent direct-matrix implementation corroborating complete solution weight distributions through order \(100\). The proof does not extrapolate from those finite checks.

## Originality — PASS, with qualification

Fu–Yang–Jiang's ordinary domination bound and exact value (2007) do not impose odd closed-neighborhood constraints. Torrence–Torrence's generalized-Petersen Lights Out study (2016) proves general nullspace properties and tabulates finite nullities, not the inhomogeneous all-on weight distribution. Karami (2022) classifies feasible equitable two-coloring matrices, not every parity-dominating word. Wang–Zhang (2026), Theorem 1.1, already covers all four minimum perfect-code patterns when \(4\mid n\); this known portion is not proposed as new. The earlier step-two odd-domination enumeration actually agrees off multiples of four; a separate polynomial-divisibility proof establishes equality of solution sets there, so that portion is expressly not claimed as new. The new index-four extension at multiples of four, particularly the seven-weight spectrum for multiples of twenty, is not implied by the earlier step-two enumerator or the perfect-code classifications. A published or unindexed exact enumerator remains a residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS

For a natural infinite family of cubic graphs, the exact all-on switch-count distribution answers not only solvability but optimal strategy cost, multiplicity and all possible nonoptimal costs. The step-three parity system strictly enlarges the known step-two system by **index four** exactly at multiples of four. Two periodic structures intersect at modulus \(20\), yielding a new seven-level spectrum with \(256\) solutions, of which \(192\) do not occur as step-two strategies. The explicit new weights and exact containment distinguish this from a mere parameter increment or isolated small-graph count. The previously known perfect-code minimum is not the sole motivation: the principal new information is the complete inhomogeneous strategy-weight classification, including nonperfect minima at multiples of five.
