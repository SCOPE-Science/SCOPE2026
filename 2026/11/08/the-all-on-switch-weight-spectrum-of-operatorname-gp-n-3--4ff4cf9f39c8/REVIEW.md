## Correctness — PASS

The closed-neighbourhood equations yield an exactly reversible degree-eight polynomial \((t+1)^4\Phi_5(t)\) in characteristic two; its divisibility into \(t^{20}+1\) proves period reduction for **every** allowed \(n\ge7\). Each of 256 initial words has one periodic continuation. Counting them for six divisors of twenty yields the stated full labelled weight tables. The preserved periodic and direct-matrix checkers genuinely implement the two indicated constructions; direct finite checks cannot replace, and are not used to replace, the period-reduction proof.

## Originality — PASS for the narrowed contribution only

The earlier general **two-colour closed-neighbourhood conflict-free census** is exactly the same odd-neighbourhood condition, not a merely related colouring problem. Its \(h_k\) kernel criterion completely subsumes the rank/total-count and basic periodic-polynomial reduction for step three. The previously published step-two weight spectrum covers all orders with \(4\nmid n\). Wang and Zhang's perfect-code classification covers the four least-weight patterns when \(4\mid n\). Therefore none of these portions is credited as new. The surviving content is the exact nonminimum Hamming-weight profile of the affine all-on solution set at multiples of four, including the seven levels and non-step-two multiplicities at multiples of twenty. The earlier general result does not state or logically determine those coefficients without a further nontrivial exact weighted census. Priority is best-of-knowledge: a prior unindexed all-on coset weight enumerator remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS, modest but substantive

All-on switching cost is a natural invariant for a family of cubic graphs. The earlier total count alone does not answer how many strategies use a given number of presses or which nonminimum costs are attainable. The repaired result computes this finite but structurally nontrivial infinite-family exact refinement; the twenty-period resonance is of particular interest. No general theorem across arbitrary inner jumps is claimed.

## Primary comparisons and limits

Torrence–Torrence (2016) provide the original switch-matrix model and finite nullities; their Tables 1–2 are not Hamming-weight tables. Wang–Zhang (2026), Theorem 1.1, give the four perfect-code minima; Karami (2022) imposes exact equal-neighbour counts, a different restriction; Fu–Yang–Jiang (2007) concern ordinary domination. Earlier public result statements and full proofs for the general closed-neighbourhood kernel and step-two spectra were read for direct mathematical implication. The surviving novelty is limited to the weight refinement at \(4\mid n\) and is subject to historical-priority risk.
