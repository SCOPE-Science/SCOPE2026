# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For every characteristic-two field and dimension \(n\ge5\), the linear wedge-annihilator dichotomy follows by splitting off a line and using the rank normal form of alternating bivectors; there can be no annihilator dimension two. The Künneth calculation of the exact graded ranks of wedge multiplication uses the square-zero differential from the alternating bivector and gives a polynomial identity, rather than inferring an infinite theorem from examples. The finite-field enumeration counts the unique annihilator line for rank at least four, and separately counts decomposable three-vectors. A projective incidence vector bundle proves the closed irreducible locus and its dimension, and constant-rank vector-bundle strata prove smoothness and dimensions. A stand-alone direct matrix check exhausts all \(2^{20}\) cubics in six generators over \(\mathbb F_2\) and independently checks the graded profiles of selected canonical cubics in dimensions five through eight. No arithmetic, external oracle, or test log is assumed as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (same-model provisional)
The closest primary texts are Maliakas's 2006 characteristic-two symplectic divided-power differential, the earlier 1997 exterior-contraction result of Gow discussed there, and De Bruyn/Kwiatkowski's and De Bruyn's 2011 trivector/polyvector classification papers. The last two establish broader six-dimensional orbit classifications, and Shaw explicitly has the five binary six-dimensional trivector orbits. Thus the small binary census is **previously implied** and is not a new claim. The all-dimension formula for the *graded ranks of cubic wedge multiplication conditional on a linear annihilator*, the exact finite-field populations of each such stratum, and its irreducible divisibility locus are not stated in the examined material and are not automatic corollaries of the six-dimensional orbit list. Searches also considered aliases (divisible trivectors, decomposable trivectors, exterior multiplication, alternating-rank, symplectic complexes, and cubic annihilator-line strata). The most material unresolved risk is a more general published result on Koszul-type symplectic complexes or divisible multivector loci that could subsume the combined theorem; priority is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS
The property of possessing a degree-one wedge annihilator is a natural algebraic rank-jump locus for cubic elements of exterior algebras. The result gives its closed irreducible geometry, a complete all-dimensional orbit and graded multiplication-rank classification within this locus, and exact finite-field populations. It thereby measures annihilation and the size of each rank stratum, rather than simply adding a small-case enumeration. This provides a tractable model for multiplication ranks of odd forms in characteristic two, while expressly leaving the much harder annihilator-free classification beyond dimension six open.
