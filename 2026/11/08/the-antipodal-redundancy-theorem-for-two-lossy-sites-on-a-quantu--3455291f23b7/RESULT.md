# The antipodal redundancy theorem for two lossy sites on a quantum hypercube

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge2\), let \(Q_n\) be the binary hypercube with vertex set \(\{0,1\}^n\) and adjacency matrix \(A\). Choose two distinct absorbing vertices \(u,v\), with Hamming separation \(d=\operatorname{dist}(u,v)\). On \(\mathbb C^{2^n}\) use the trace-decreasing quantum evolution generated by
\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=JA+\epsilon I-\frac{i}{2}\bigl(\gamma_u|u\rangle\langle u|+\gamma_v|v\rangle\langle v|\bigr),
\qquad J\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\},\quad \epsilon\in\mathbb R,\quad \gamma_u,\gamma_v>0.
\]
Let \(P_B\) project onto the subspace eventually captured by these losses (the orthogonal complement of the maximal nondecaying subspace). Then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}P_B=\begin{cases}n+1,&d=n,\\2n,&1\le d<n.\end{cases}}
\]
For the maximally mixed initial state the eventual capture probability is therefore \((n+1)/2^n\) for antipodal traps and \(2n/2^n\) for every nonantipodal pair. **The complete capture projector is unchanged when a trap is added at the antipode of an existing trap.** Thus antipodal placement is strictly less effective on a maximally mixed input than any nonantipodal pair; the difference is \((n-1)/2^n\). Complete capture from every initial state by two traps occurs exactly in the case \(n=2\) and \(d=1\).

The finding also determines the exact eventual capture probability from *every* localized initial vertex. Define the binary Krawtchouk polynomial
\[
K_k(r;n)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\binom rj\binom{n-r}{k-j},\qquad 0\le k,r\le n,
\]
with out-of-range binomial coefficients interpreted as zero. For \(x\in Q_n\), put \(r=\operatorname{dist}(x,u)\), \(s=\operatorname{dist}(x,v)\), and
\[
a_k=2^{-n}\binom nk,\qquad b_k=2^{-n}K_k(d;n),\qquad c_k=2^{-n}K_k(r;n),\qquad e_k=2^{-n}K_k(s;n).
\]
When \(1\le d<n\), the localized capture probability is exactly
\[
\boxed{\eta(x;u,v)=2^{1-n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{a_k(c_k^2+e_k^2)-2b_kc_ke_k}{a_k^2-b_k^2}.}
\]
When \(d=n\), it is exactly
\[
\boxed{\eta(x;u,v)=2^{-n}\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{K_k(r;n)^2}{\binom nk},}
\]
which is the probability for the single trap \(u\). For any initial density matrix \(\rho\), the total capture probability is \(\operatorname{tr}(P_B\rho)\); an exact formula for the full projector, not only its diagonal, appears below.

## Assumptions and scope
The model is a *single* excitation with uniform coherent nearest-neighbor hopping on \(Q_n\). Each selected site has an independent strictly positive, time-independent absorbing loss. The generator above contains no onsite disorder except the scalar shift \(\epsilon I\), and no dephasing or particle interactions. The graph is labeled, the traps are distinct, and the time interval is \([0,\infty)\). The assertion concerns eventual capture, not an average finite absorption time. The results are independent of the nonzero value or sign of \(J\), of \(\epsilon\), and of both positive loss rates.

## Proof
**1. The protected subspace is the joint spectral kernel of the traps.** Write \(Q=\gamma_u|u\rangle\langle u|+\gamma_v|v\rangle\langle v|\). An undamped eigenvector of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) with real eigenvalue must obey \(\langle\psi,Q\psi\rangle=0\), hence is zero at both absorbing sites and is an eigenvector of the Hermitian \(A\). Conversely every eigenvector of \(A\) zero at both sites stays undamped. Their span \(\mathcal D\) is invariant under \(A\); by Hermiticity its orthogonal complement \(\mathcal B\) is also invariant. Since \(Q\mathcal D=0\) and \(Q\) is Hermitian, both spaces reduce the full non-Hermitian generator. On \(\mathcal B\), all eigenvalues have strictly negative imaginary part (otherwise another undamped eigenvector would lie in \(\mathcal B\)); hence its finite-dimensional semigroup decays to zero, including possible Jordan blocks. Consequently \(P_B=P_{\mathcal B}\), and \(\eta=\operatorname{tr}(P_B\rho)\).

**2. Resolve the hypercube spectrum.** For each binary subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), the normalized Walsh character \(\chi_S(x)=2^{-n/2}(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}x_i}\) is an eigenvector of \(A\) with eigenvalue \(n-2|S|\). Let \(E_k\) be the orthogonal projection onto all \(\chi_S\) with \(|S|=k\). Orthogonality gives
\[
E_k(x,y)=2^{-n}K_k(\operatorname{dist}(x,y);n).
\]
Distinct levels \(k=0,\ldots,n\) have distinct eigenvalues because \(J\ne0\). For each level, the bright sector is precisely \(\operatorname{span}\{E_k|u\rangle,E_k|v\rangle\}\). Different levels are orthogonal, and their sum is \(\mathcal B\).

**3. Compute its rank by a two-site Gram matrix.** The Gram matrix of the projected trap vectors at level \(k\) is
\[
G_k=\begin{pmatrix}a_k&b_k\\b_k&a_k\end{pmatrix}.
\]
At \(k=0\) and \(k=n\) this has rank one. If \(d=n\), the translation from \(u\) to \(v\) flips all bits, so \(K_k(n;n)=(-1)^k\binom nk\); every \(G_k\) then has rank one. The total rank is \(n+1\), and the spans for each level coincide with those obtained using only \(u\).

Suppose \(1\le d<n\) and \(1\le k<n\). Across all subsets \(S\) of size \(k\), the signs \((-1)^{|S\cap (u\oplus v)|}\) occur with both parities: swap in such a subset an element from the nonempty difference set with an element from its nonempty complement, retaining its size. The swap can be arranged for any intermediate \(k\). Thus the average of these signs has absolute value strictly less than one, i.e. \(|K_k(d;n)|<\binom nk\). Hence \(a_k^2-b_k^2>0\) and \(G_k\) has rank two. Adding the ranks across levels yields \(1+2(n-1)+1=2n\). These ranks are the only possible ranks for two distinct trap vertices.

**4. Recover the entire bright projector.** Put the two columns \(E_k|u\rangle,E_k|v\rangle\) into \(W_k\). The orthogonal projector onto their span is \(W_kG_k^+W_k^*\), with \(G_k^+\) the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse, and therefore
\[
\boxed{P_B=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_kG_k^+W_k^*.}
\]
This exact operator formula handles *every* initial pure or mixed state, including coherences. For nonantipodal \(d\) the Gram matrix is invertible at each intermediate level. Using \(G_k^{-1}=(a_k^2-b_k^2)^{-1}\begin{psmallmatrix}a_k&-b_k\\-b_k&a_k\end{psmallmatrix}\) gives the displayed diagonal formula. At each of the endpoint levels the rank-one constant/parity character contributes precisely \(2^{-n}\). For antipodal placement there is one bright direction per level and the projector is the single-trap projector; the diagonal contribution of level \(k\) is \(c_k^2/a_k\), proving the second formula.

**5. Optimality for uniformly mixed inputs.** For \(\rho=2^{-n}I\), \(\eta=\operatorname{rank}P_B/2^n\). There are only two separation classes, so nonantipodal pairs uniquely maximize this criterion as a *class* of trap placements; within that class all choices tie. Since \(n+1<2n\) for \(n\ge2\), antipodal pairs are strictly suboptimal. The rank \(2n=2^n\) occurs only for \(n=2\) among \(n\ge2\), and the corresponding nonantipodal choice then captures every state.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_two_traps.py` independently constructs the full Walsh-shell projectors and the adjacency-generated Krylov subspace, comparing both with the explicit site formula for all \(n=2,3,4,5,6\), every representative trap distance and every initial site (640 localized probabilities). It checks orthogonal-projector idempotence, rank and uniformly mixed capture. The separate `verify_dissipation.py` diagonalizes full non-Hermitian generators with deliberately *unequal* trap rates and compares long-time norm survival against the spectral prediction for all separations at \(n=2,3,4\) (nine Hamiltonians). Both programs use only standard NumPy/SciPy and the Python standard library and can be executed with `python verify_two_traps.py` and `python verify_dissipation.py` from the directory containing these files. These finite numerical tests are corroborations; the proof above establishes the infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work
Moore and Russell's 2001 hypercube quantum-walk study supplies the coherent Walsh/product spectrum and instantaneous-mixing context, but does not introduce two irreversible traps or optimize their placement. Krovi and Brun's 2006 study explains the protected-eigenspace mechanism for quantum hitting-time obstructions, including continuous-time quantum walks, without classifying two local absorbers on \(Q_n\). Novo, Chakraborty, Mohseni, Neven and Omar's 2014/2015 paper proves the hypercube **single-trap** capture probability \(1/\binom{n}{r}\) and mean \((n+1)/2^n\) using a Krylov reduction. These exact one-trap formulas are explicitly acknowledged, and the antipodal-pair formula agrees with them, but the paper does not treat adding a second absorbing vertex or classify its placement. Makmal, Tiersch, Ganahl and Briegel's 2015/2016 work discusses dark states and symmetry reduction for *coined discrete-time* walks on embedded or modified hypercubes, not the present continuous-time homogeneous two-trap loss model. Razzoli, Paris and Bordone's 2020/2021 transport study gives the relevant single-trap framework and explicitly distinguishes multi-trap placements as an open direction of investigation, but does not give the two-trap formulas. Those general mechanisms are prior knowledge; the claimed contribution here is the complete two-trap separation/rank classification, exact site-resolved capture kernel, and antipodal redundancy criterion.

## Limitations
This statement does **not** assert a new general spectral-projector method, the known hypercube eigenvalues, or any finite-time speed-up. A two-site monitoring pattern is much smaller than the arbitrary-detector-set problem and does not imply a classification of larger trap sets. Equivalence with work not indexed or discovered in the checked literature remains a historical-priority risk. Any nonzero onsite disorder, changes to edge couplings, dephasing or multi-excitation interactions can destroy the degeneracies used here. Infinite-dimensional and time-dependent models are outside scope. The numerical tests do not substitute for the analytical infinite-family argument.
