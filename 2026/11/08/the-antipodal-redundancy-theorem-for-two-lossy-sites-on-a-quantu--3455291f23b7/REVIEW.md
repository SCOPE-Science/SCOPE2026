# Scientific review: antipodal redundancy of two quantum hypercube absorbers

## Correctness — PASS
For all integers \(n\ge2\), all distinct traps and all strictly positive trap-loss rates, the non-Hermitian evolution decomposes into a reducing dark space and its decaying orthogonal complement. The Walsh eigenspaces of the hypercube adjacency operator have eigenvalues \(n-2k\) and explicitly normalized Krawtchouk kernels. In each eigenspace the span of the two projected absorber vectors has Gram matrix \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a_k&b_k\\b_k&a_k\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). Its rank equals one at spectral endpoints; equals one at every level for antipodes; and equals two at interior levels for any nonantipodal pair. This proves the bright dimensions \(n+1\) and \(2n\), respectively. The Gram-pseudoinverse projector gives both the full state-dependent capture operator and the stated site-resolved formula. The finite-dimensional decay argument is valid for unequal positive loss rates. Independent Krylov/spectral tests checked 640 site probabilities at dimensions two through six; independent non-Hermitian propagation checked nine unequal-rate cases at dimensions two through four. These experiments are confirmations, not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS on checked evidence, subject to historical priority
The most direct predecessor, Novo et al., arXiv:1412.7209, Sec. “Hypercube,” equations (44)–(47), derives the **single-trap** dimension \(n+1\), site probability \(\binom nr^{-1}\), and uniform-mixture probability \((n+1)/2^n\). These cases are not claimed new. Moore–Russell, arXiv:quant-ph/0104137, gives hypercube coherent spectral structure. Krovi–Brun, arXiv:quant-ph/0606094, gives the general quantum hitting/dark-subspace framework. Makmal et al., arXiv:1510.08388, treat a discrete-time coined setting with different graph modifications and target configurations. Razzoli et al., arXiv:2011.13794, study a continuous-time single-trap model. The checked source sections and available full texts do not give the exact separation-independent \(2n\) bright dimension for two distinct nonantipodal hypercube traps, the antipodal-redundancy dichotomy, or the two-site Krawtchouk-Gram site-resolved formula. The two-trap statement is a special case of general finite-dimensional Krylov-space principles; **those principles are not new**. The classification requires the concrete rank argument for this graph and is not given as a separate theorem in the inspected material. Unindexed or differently worded equivalent work remains possible; no search establishes mathematical priority absolutely.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A natural detector-placement decision for coherent transport has an exact infinite-family answer: a second detector at the unique antipode adds no asymptotic capture at all, whereas every other second detector increases the uniformly averaged capture fraction by \((n-1)/2^n\). Exact site-resolved probabilities and the full bright projector distinguish localized initial states from the maximally mixed criterion. The result also identifies the only dimension in which two local traps capture every state. The strictness of the hypotheses explains the mechanism as spectral degeneracy and phase alignment, not finite-time speed or arbitrary multiloss behavior.
