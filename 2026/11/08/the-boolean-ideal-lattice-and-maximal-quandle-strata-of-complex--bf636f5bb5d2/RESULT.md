# The Boolean ideal lattice and maximal quandle strata of complex core-quandle idempotents

## Finding

Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group of odd order (possibly the trivial group), written additively, and let \(A=\mathbb C[\operatorname{Core}(G)]\) denote the complex quandle algebra with basis \(e_g\) and product \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\). Write \(E=\{u\in A:u^2=u\}\), including zero, with its multiplication inherited from \(A\).

Let \(X=\widehat G\) and let \(\mathcal O_1,\ldots,\mathcal O_r\) be the orbits of \(X\setminus\{1\}\) under the commuting permutations \(\chi\mapsto\chi^{-1}\) and \(\chi\mapsto\chi^2\). An orbit contains characters all of one order \(m>1\). Put \(d=\operatorname{ord}_m(2)\), and associate to that orbit the **odd** integer
\[
N_{\mathcal O}=\begin{cases}
2^{d/2}+1,&d\text{ even and }2^{d/2}\equiv-1\pmod m,\\
2^d-1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
This integer does not depend on the representative character. Denote by \(C_N\) a cyclic group of order \(N\). Adjoin an absorbing symbol \(\bot\) to its core quandle, meaning \(\bot*x=x*\bot=\bot\), and call the resulting idempotent magma \(C_N^{\bot}\).

**Structural classification.** As a magma under quandle-algebra multiplication,
\[
\boxed{E\ \cong\ (\{0,1\},\wedge)\times\prod_{i=1}^r C_{N_{\mathcal O_i}}^{\bot}.}
\]
Every factor is medial and right self-distributive; so is \(E\). The support map \(\operatorname{supp}:E\to2^{\{0,1,\ldots,r\}}\) recording whether augmentation is one (index zero) and which Fourier orbits are nonzero satisfies
\[
\operatorname{supp}(u*v)=\operatorname{supp}(u)\cap\operatorname{supp}(v).
\]
Each support fiber is a connected Latin core quandle on the odd-order abelian group \(\prod_{i\in S\setminus\{0\}}C_{N_{\mathcal O_i}}\). These \(2^{r+1}\) fibers are precisely the **maximal subquandles** of \(E\). In particular, the full idempotent set is generally not a quandle; its individual support strata are.

**Complete ideal-lattice classification.** Call \(I\subseteq E\) a nonempty two-sided ideal if \(I*E\subseteq I\) and \(E*I\subseteq I\). Then
\[
\boxed{I\text{ is an ideal}\iff I=\bigcup_{S\in\mathscr D}\operatorname{supp}^{-1}(S)}
\]
for a unique nonempty **down-set** \(\mathscr D\) in the Boolean lattice \(2^{\{0,1,\ldots,r\}}\). Consequently the ideal lattice is the lattice of nonempty down-sets, and the number of two-sided ideals equals \(D(r+1)-1\), where \(D(s)\) is the Dedekind number (number of all down-sets of a Boolean lattice on \(s\) coordinates). The principal ideal generated by an element of support \(S\) consists of all idempotents with support contained in \(S\).

The full-support, augmentation-one fiber is canonically the core quandle of \(B_G=\prod_i C_{N_{\mathcal O_i}}\). It contains the original \(\operatorname{Core}(G)\) as a subquandle under the canonical injection \(g\mapsto e_g\). For the trivial group, \(r=0\), and \(E=\{0,e_0\}\) with meet multiplication, two maximal singleton subquandles, and two nonempty ideals.

## Assumptions and scope

Coefficients are in \(\mathbb C\); \(G\) is any finite abelian **odd-order** group. Multiplication in \(A\) is bilinear and generally nonassociative. \(E\) is treated only as a magma with the inherited multiplication: ideal means a nonempty subset stable under left and right multiplication by *elements of \(E\)*, not a linear ideal of the ambient algebra. A subquandle is a nonempty subset closed under multiplication whose right translations are bijective on that subset. We count only nonempty ideals; allowing the empty ideal adds one. The original order-\(G\) group algebra is used solely for Fourier bookkeeping.

## Proof

**(1) Fourier coordinates and orbits.** The character Fourier transform \(c_\chi=\sum_{g\in G}a_g\chi(g)\) of \(u=\sum a_g e_g\) is a complex-linear isomorphism \(A\to\mathbb C^X\). Direct expansion of \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\) gives
\[
\widehat{u*v}(\chi)=c_{\chi^{-1}}(u)c_{\chi^2}(v).
\]
Hence \(u^2=u\) exactly when \(c_\chi=c_{\chi^{-1}}c_{\chi^2}\) for all \(\chi\). At the trivial character, \(c_1\in\{0,1\}\). The nonzero support is a union of \(\langle-1,2\rangle\)-orbits, because inversion and doubling permute the finite odd-order dual group. On any active orbit multiply the equations for \(\chi\) and \(\chi^{-1}\): with \(P_\chi=c_\chi c_{\chi^{-1}}\) one obtains \(P_\chi=P_\chi P_{\chi^2}\). Nonzero coordinates and doubling cycles force \(P_\chi=1\) everywhere on that orbit. Therefore
\[
 c_{\chi^{-1}}=c_\chi^{-1},\qquad c_{\chi^2}=c_\chi^2.
\]
An orbit of exact character order \(m\) has either \(d\) points if inversion lies in the doubling cycle, or \(2d\) otherwise, where \(d=\operatorname{ord}_m(2)\). In the first case the consistency equations for an initial value \(z=c_\chi\) reduce to \(z^{2^{d/2}+1}=1\); in the second to \(z^{2^d-1}=1\). Conversely, any such root, propagated by squaring and inversion, defines an idempotent on that orbit, and setting all its coordinates to zero is also allowed. Since each \(N_{\mathcal O}\) is odd, its roots of unity identify multiplicatively with \(C_{N_{\mathcal O}}\). This recovers a known Fourier idempotent parametrization; no novelty is claimed for the pointwise count or its orbit decomposition.

**(2) The complete multiplication law.** On an active orbit write \(z=c_\chi(u)\), \(w=c_\chi(v)\). Then the Fourier product formula becomes
\[
 c_\chi(u*v)=z^{-1}w^2.
\]
If either orbit is inactive, the product orbit is zero. The same law holds after propagation to the other coordinates of the orbit, and at the trivial character the operation is Boolean multiplication. Thus products of idempotents are idempotent and the displayed factorization is a magma isomorphism. Its support law is intersection. Each nonzero local factor uses the core operation \(z*w=w^2/z\), so idempotency, mediality and right self-distributivity follow directly from the factor laws (including their absorbing-zero cases).

**(3) Maximal subquandles.** Fix a support \(S\). Its fiber is stable under multiplication and the nonzero coordinates are a product of \(C_N\)-cores. Each \(N\) is odd, hence every multiplication-by-two map is bijective; the fiber is a Latin quandle. It is connected because right translations generate all translations: \(R_bR_0(z)=z+2b\), and multiplication by two is surjective. Now suppose \(U\) is any subquandle of \(E\). Right multiplication by \(v\in U\) is surjective on \(U\), while every product \(u*v\) has support contained in \(\operatorname{supp}(v)\). It follows that the supports of all elements of \(U\) lie within the support of every \(v\in U\). Interchanging \(v\) shows all these supports are equal. Thus every subquandle lies within a single fiber, and the full fibers are exactly the maximal subquandles.

**(4) All ideals.** If \(I\) is a two-sided ideal and \(u\in I\) has support \(S\), choose any \(T\subseteq S\) and any target \(w\) with support \(T\). On each nontrivial active coordinate of \(T\), solve \(z*v=w\) by choosing \(v\) with \(v_\chi^2=z_\chi w_\chi\); this is possible because \(N_{\mathcal O}\) is odd. Set the other orbit parameters of \(v\) to zero. On the trivial-character coordinate choose its Boolean parameter to match the target whenever it is active. Hence \(u*v=w\), and \(w\in I\). Therefore every support class below \(S\) is fully contained in \(I\). The supports of \(I\) form a nonempty down-set. Conversely if \(\mathscr D\) is a nonempty down-set, the union of its support fibers is a two-sided ideal because supports multiply by intersection. The two constructions are mutually inverse and preserve inclusion. A principal ideal is the down-set union below the support of its generator. This proves the lattice and Dedekind-number claims.

**(5) Canonical embedding.** Each \(e_g\) has Fourier coordinate \(\chi(g)\ne0\) at every character and has augmentation one. The map \(g\mapsto e_g\) is injective and respects \(e_ge_h=e_{2h-g}\), exhibiting \(\operatorname{Core}(G)\) as a subquandle of the full-support fiber.

## Verification

Run `python verify_magma.py` under Python 3 (standard library only). The checker constructs finite-field analogues in seven independently specified group cases with all relevant roots of unity present. It uses Fourier inversion only to construct candidate basis coefficient vectors, then performs **literal bilinear quandle-algebra multiplication** for 29,974 direct element-level comparisons (including the idempotency checks). It exhaustively tests the complete multiplication tables for five small cases and samples 500 products in the larger elementary-abelian case. It separately enumerates **every nonempty subset** of each idempotent magma for group orders one, three and five, checking the two-sided ideal condition and matching the predicted ideal counts of two, five and five. The checker verifies odd local group orders and all support-stratum counts; output concludes `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests corroborate the proof but do not replace the argument for arbitrary finite abelian odd-order groups.

## Relationship to prior work

The Fourier multiplication identity and the idempotency equations are prior results: Elhamdadi–Ta–Virgin (2026) and Zhang (2026, Proposition 5.1 and equation (5.1)). The pointwise orbit counting, root choices and right-Peirce multiplicity distribution already appear in earlier research on the same algebras, and are **not** claimed as new here. Elhamdadi–Nunez–Singh–Swain (2022, Corollary 4.12) prove that idempotents of certain **integral covering-quandle** rings form a quandle; their hypotheses and structures do not classify the complex finite-core case. The contribution here is the actual multiplication magma of the **entire** idempotent set, its maximal connected quandle strata, and its **complete two-sided ideal lattice**. The latest root-count parametrization supplies ingredients, but an enumeration alone does not describe the multiplication or its ideal lattice.

## Limitations

The result concerns odd-order abelian cores over complex coefficients, not characteristic-dividing-order coefficient fields, even-order cores, or arbitrary nonabelian quandles. Ideal classification concerns subsets closed under multiplication within the idempotent magma, **not** ambient linear ideals. The earlier orbit-count result makes the present structural consequences accessible; a historical-priority risk remains if the same multiplication and ideal classification was previously proved under another term. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
