# The Cartier rank-jump divisor in the genus-eight two-biquartic-pole Artin–Schreier family

## Finding
Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic \(3\). For \(a,b,c,d,\lambda\in k\) with \(\lambda\ne0\), let \(C\) be the smooth projective normalization of
\[
y^3-y=x^4+a x^2+c x+\lambda x^{-4}+b x^{-2}+d x^{-1}.
\]
Then \(C\) has genus \(8\) and \(3\)-rank \(2\). For its Cartier operator \(\mathcal C:H^0(C,\Omega_C^1)\to H^0(C,\Omega_C^1)\), the complete rank profile is
\[
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C)=
\begin{cases}4,&ab\ne\lambda,\\3,&ab=\lambda,\end{cases}
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C^r)=2\quad(r\ge2).
\]
Equivalently the Jacobian \(a\)-number is \(4\) off the nonempty smooth divisor \(ab=\lambda\) in the parameter space \(\{\lambda\ne0\}\subset\mathbb A^5\), and is \(5\) exactly on that divisor. The nilpotent Cartier summand has two blocks of length \(2\) and two of length \(1\) generically, versus one block of length \(2\) and four of length \(1\) on the divisor; the bijective summand has dimension \(2\). These block counts describe the semilinear Cartier module, not the full Ekedahl–Oort type.

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficients are arbitrary elements of an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\); no finite-field or genericity condition is imposed except \(\lambda\ne0\). The rational function has exactly two poles, of order \(4\), at \(x=0\) and \(x=\infty\). Since these orders are prime to \(3\), the Artin–Schreier extension is separable of degree \(3\) and its smooth normalization is connected. After placing any two order-\(4\) poles at \(0,\infty\), eliminating divisible-by-\(3\) exponents and constants by an Artin–Schreier change in \(y\), and scaling \(x\), this is a normalized form for the full two-pole \((4,4)\) stratum. The displayed equation is not asserted to give a unique representative of every isomorphism class.

## Proof
**1. Ramification and a regular-differential basis.** Let \(P_0,P_\infty\) be the unique points above the two poles. Each is totally ramified with different exponent \((3-1)(4+1)=10\). The wild Riemann–Hurwitz formula gives
\[
2g(C)-2=-6+10+10=14,\qquad g(C)=8.
\]
In the valuation convention that a local uniformizer has order \(1\),
\[
\operatorname{div}(x)=3P_0-3P_\infty,\quad
\operatorname{div}(dx)=10P_0+4P_\infty,\quad
\operatorname{ord}_{P_0}(y)=\operatorname{ord}_{P_\infty}(y)=-4.
\]
For any integer \(i\) and \(j=0,1,2\), the orders of \(x^iy^jdx\) at the two ramified points are \(10+3i-4j\) and \(4-3i-4j\). At other points these forms are regular because the cover is étale over \(\mathbb G_m\) and \(x,y,dx\) have no poles. Both displayed orders are nonnegative exactly for \(j=0\) and \(-3\le i\le1\), or for \(j=1\) and \(-2\le i\le0\); there are no \(j=2\) cases. The resulting eight forms are independent over \(k\), since \(1,y,y^2\) are linearly independent over \(k(x)\), and constitute a basis by the genus count:
\[
\mathcal B=(x^{-3}dx,x^{-2}dx,x^{-1}dx,dx,x\,dx,
  x^{-2}y\,dx,x^{-1}y\,dx,y\,dx).
\]

**2. Cartier on the basis.** The Cartier operator is \(3^{-1}\)-semilinear and satisfies
\[
\mathcal C(x^i dx)=
\begin{cases}x^{(i-2)/3}dx,&i\equiv2\pmod 3,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\]
for every integer \(i\). Using \(y=y^3-f(x)\) and \(\mathcal C(y^3\omega)=y\mathcal C(\omega)\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C(x^{-1}dx)&=x^{-1}dx,\\
\mathcal C(x^{-1}y\,dx)&=x^{-1}y\,dx,\\
\mathcal C(x^{-2}y\,dx)&=-dx-b^{1/3}x^{-2}dx-c^{1/3}x^{-1}dx,\\
\mathcal C(y\,dx)&=-a^{1/3}dx-\lambda^{1/3}x^{-2}dx-d^{1/3}x^{-1}dx.
\end{aligned}
\]
The operator vanishes on the other four basis elements. These are exact identities; cube roots exist uniquely in the perfect field \(k\).

**3. Rank and stability.** The first two displayed images span a Cartier-bijective two-plane. Modulo that plane, the last two images lie in the space generated by \(dx,x^{-2}dx\) and have coefficient matrix
\[
-\begin{pmatrix}1&a^{1/3}\\b^{1/3}&\lambda^{1/3}\end{pmatrix}
\]
with columns ordered as \(x^{-2}y\,dx,y\,dx\), and rows \(dx,x^{-2}dx\). Its determinant is \((\lambda-ab)^{1/3}\). Since its first column has nonzero \(dx\) coefficient, its rank is \(2\) when \(ab\ne\lambda\) and \(1\) when \(ab=\lambda\). Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C)\) is \(4\) or \(3\) respectively. Applying Cartier again kills \(dx,x^{-2}dx\) and retains the two displayed fixed forms. Therefore for every \(r\ge2\), the image is precisely the fixed two-plane and has dimension \(2\).

**4. Geometric jump and consequences.** Since \(g=8\), \(a(C)=\dim\ker\mathcal C=8-\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C\) gives the asserted values \(4,5\). The parameter divisor \(ab=\lambda\) is smooth because its differential has a nonzero \(d\lambda\) coefficient, and nonempty (e.g. \(a=b=\lambda=1\)). Its complement is nonempty (e.g. \(a=b=0,\lambda=1\)). The stabilized Cartier rank \(2\) agrees with the Deuring–Shafarevich \(3\)-rank formula for two poles. As the nilpotent part has dimension \(6\), with square zero and rank \(2\) or \(1\), the stated block counts follow.

An additional geometric manifestation occurs in the symmetric subfamily \(a=b=t,\ c=d=0,\ \lambda=1\). With \(u=x^2+x^{-2}\), one has \(f=u^2+tu+1\) in characteristic \(3\). The map \(x\mapsto u\) is branched above \(u=1,-1\). The quadratic in \(u\) has its critical point at \(u=t\). Exactly at \(t=1,-1\), that critical point coincides with a branch value; these are precisely the two parameters where the Cartier rank drops. This observation is explanatory and is not used to prove the global formula.

## Verification
The standalone `verification.py` implements \(\mathbb F_9\) as \(\mathbb F_3[z]/(z^2+1)\), constructs the full Cartier matrix independently by scanning the Laurent exponents in the defining rational function, and computes matrix ranks by exact elimination. It tests all \(648\) triples \((a,b,\lambda)\in\mathbb F_9^2\times\mathbb F_9^\times\), at six explicit choices of \((c,d)\), for \(3888\) parameter tuples. It verifies ranks \(3/4\), the square-Cartier rank \(2\), and the predicted symmetric jump parameters. The observed counts are \(3504\) generic and \(384\) jumping parameter tuples. Exact program output is provided in `verification_output.txt`. This finite test is corroboration; the all-field result follows from the preceding proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Farnell–Pries (arXiv:1202.4183, 2012) give general holomorphic-differential and Cartier constructions and prove coefficient-independent \(a\)-number formulas under \(p\equiv1\pmod L\), where \(L\) is the least common multiple of the pole orders. Here \(p=3\), \(L=4\), so their main theorem does not cover the present coefficient-dependent stratification. Booher–Cais (arXiv:1807.10313, 2018) give general upper and lower bounds rather than this exact rank-jump divisor. Groen (arXiv:2208.06504, 2022) gives general bounds for powers of Cartier, not the explicit full rank profile of this two-pole stratum. Duque-Rosero–Lorenzo García–Malmskog–Scheidler (arXiv:2602.22435, 2026) explicitly enumerate the genus-eight \((4,4)\) stratum and study its invariant ring under isomorphisms; that paper is about moduli invariant theory and does not state this \(a\)-number rank-jump formula. These papers' general methods and the standard ramification/genus facts are not claimed as new. The assessed additional result is the exact nonconstant \(a\)-number stratification and stabilized Cartier ranks on the full normalized \((4,4)\) parameter family.

## Limitations
The computation determines Cartier ranks, the \(a\)-number and the structure of the semilinear Cartier operator, but **not** the complete polarized \(3\)-torsion Dieudonné module, full Ekedahl–Oort type, or Newton polygon. The locus equation is stated in the normalized two-pole coordinates; while the jumping property is intrinsically invariant, a quotient-level moduli equation is not claimed. The finite-field verifier is not an infinite-field proof. Historical priority relative to further specialist literature remains subject to independent assessment.
