# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The curve has exactly two wild branch points with different exponent \(10\), yielding genus \(8\). Explicit valuations prove the listed eight monomial differentials form a complete basis; the exact Cartier identities then give a two-by-two minor with determinant \((\lambda-ab)^{1/3}\), all other first-image directions being a fixed two-plane. The second and higher Cartier images are exactly this two-plane. These are formal all-characteristic-three calculations; exact \(\mathbb F_9\) tests are corroborative rather than the proof.

## Originality
PASS, with historical-priority risk. Farnell–Pries's constant-rank theorem requires \(p\equiv1\pmod L\), inapplicable for \(p=3,L=4\); their differential-basis and Cartier formulas are acknowledged prior methods. Booher–Cais and Groen give general ramification-based bounds on the kernel of Cartier and its powers, not this exact coefficient-dependent divisor. Duque-Rosero et al. cover precisely the same genus-eight two-pole order-four **moduli stratum** but determine isomorphism invariant rings, rather than the Cartier rank-jump and its complete power-rank profile. Searches for the defining family, pole-order aliases, rank formula and determinant condition found no statement implying this theorem. These searches do not constitute a proof of global publication uniqueness; obscure or future specialist sources remain possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The \(a\)-number of a Jacobian is a natural moduli-theoretic invariant of its \(p\)-torsion, not an arbitrary selected statistic. A smooth codimension-one jumping divisor gives the exact global \(a\)-number stratification of a natural five-parameter family of genus-eight curves with fixed branching and fixed \(3\)-rank. The result also determines every iterated Cartier rank and distinguishes two nilpotent semilinear block configurations. No claim about full Ekedahl–Oort type or Newton polygons is made.

## Residual limitations
The Cartier-rank information may not separate all \(3\)-torsion group schemes. The result uses a normalized equation rather than a quotient-moduli defining equation. Independent specialist review remains necessary for publication priority and additional group-scheme implications.
