# The characteristic-three binary-quartic discriminant has a cubic triple-root Jacobian component

## Finding
Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic \(3\), and write
\[
 f=aX^4+bX^3Y+cX^2Y^2+dXY^3+eY^4.
\]
Let \(D\subset\mathbb P^4_k\) be the degree-six binary-quartic discriminant hypersurface, defined by the integral discriminant reduced modulo \(3\). Let \(Z\) be its *scheme-theoretic* singular locus, with ideal sheaf generated by the discriminant **and** its five first partial derivatives. Define two reduced closed surfaces:
\[
T=V(c,ae-bd)\subset\mathbb P^4_k,
\quad
E=\{[q^2]:0\ne q\in k[X,Y]_2\}^{\mathrm{sch.cl.}}.
\]
Here \(T\) is the locus of geometric triple-root quartics, while \(E\) is the locus of two-double-root quartics, including quadruple-root quartics. Write \(\mathcal I_T\) and \(\mathcal I_E\) for their ideal sheaves. Then the following equality holds on all of \(\mathbb P^4_k\):
\[
\boxed{\mathcal I_Z=\mathcal I_T^{\,3}\cap\mathcal I_E.}
\]
The first factor is the *ordinary* third power of the ideal sheaf of the smooth quadric surface \(T\). The second factor is the reduced ideal of a smooth projected Veronese surface \(E\cong\mathbb P^2_k\). There are no further associated components. At the generic point of \(T\), the transverse Artin algebra has basis \(1,c,v,c^2,cv,v^2\) and length \(6\); at the generic point of \(E\), the singular scheme is reduced. Hence
\[
\boxed{\deg Z=6\deg T+\deg E=6\cdot 2+4=16.}
\]
In contrast, the characteristic-zero Jacobian scheme has triple-root multiplicity \(2\) on a degree-six triple-root surface, rather than multiplicity \(6\) on the characteristic-three quadric. Both total degrees equal \(16\), but their geometric cycles and infinitesimal structures differ.

## Assumptions and scope
All rings and schemes are over a field of characteristic exactly \(3\). The result is valid without assuming \(k\) algebraically closed; formation of the ideals, their equality, and smoothness may be checked after faithfully flat scalar extension to an algebraic closure. The projective coefficient coordinates \([a:b:c:d:e]\) denote the **unscaled** coefficients of a binary quartic; using binomially normalized coefficients is unsafe in characteristic \(3\). The discriminant is the integral discriminant of the unscaled binary form, not a discriminant divided by \(3\). Its projective singular scheme is \(V(\Delta,\partial_a\Delta,\partial_b\Delta,\partial_c\Delta,\partial_d\Delta,\partial_e\Delta)\); the equation \(\Delta\) cannot be omitted merely from Euler's identity, because \(6=0\) in \(k\). The equalities concern ideal **sheaves** (or saturated projective ideals), not an assertion about an unsaturated homogeneous Jacobian ideal.

## Proof
**1. An exact normal form.** On the projective chart \(a=1\), replace \(x=X/Y\) by \(z-b\). In characteristic \(3\) the quartic becomes
\[
 z^4+c z^2+u z+v,\qquad
 u=d-b^3+bc,\quad v=e+b^2c-bd.
\]
This is an invertible polynomial change of affine coefficient coordinates \((b,c,d,e)\leftrightarrow(b,c,u,v)\). Direct evaluation of the integral discriminant gives
\[
\boxed{\Delta=v(v-c^2)^2-c^3u^2.}\tag{1}
\]
Because \(6=0\) in characteristic \(3\), Euler's identity gives \(a\partial_a\Delta=-b\partial_b\Delta-c\partial_c\Delta-d\partial_d\Delta-e\partial_e\Delta\) on the chart \(a=1\). Thus the original projective Tjurina ideal is generated by \(\Delta\) and its derivatives in the affine coordinates; the changed equation is independent of \(b\). Put \(w=v-c^2\). In \(k[c,u,v]\), the derivatives of the expression (1) are
\[
\partial_c\Delta=-cvw,\qquad
\partial_u\Delta=c^3u,\qquad
\partial_v\Delta=-c^2w.
\]
Since \(c^3u\) is already a generator, the term \(-c^3u^2\) in \(\Delta\) may be removed using it. Thus the full local Tjurina ideal is exactly
\[
J=(c^2w,\,cvw,\,c^3u,\,vw^2).\tag{2}
\]

**2. Exact primary decomposition.** Let \(\mathfrak m=(c,v)\subset k[c,u,v]\). The element \(w=v-c^2\) has initial form \(v\) in \(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}(k[c,v])\cong k[c,v]\), a non-zero-divisor. Consequently
\[
(\mathfrak m^3:w)=\mathfrak m^2,
\quad
\mathfrak m^3\cap(u,w)=u\mathfrak m^3+w\mathfrak m^2.\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, reduce an element in the intersection modulo the independent variable \(u\); its multiple of \(w\) must have coefficient in \(\mathfrak m^2\), after which the remaining multiple of \(u\) belongs to \(u\mathfrak m^3\). From (2), \(w\mathfrak m^2\subset J\): the generators \(c^2w,cvw\) appear directly, while \(v^2w=vw^2+c^2vw\). Also \(u\mathfrak m^3\subset J\), since \(uc^3\in J\) and multiplying the first three generators by suitable monomials gives \(uc^2v,ucv^2,uv^3\). Conversely, every generator in (2) lies in \(w\mathfrak m^2+u\mathfrak m^3\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{J=(c,v)^3\cap(u,v-c^2).}\tag{4}
\]
Both components are primary: the first is the third power of the ideal of a smooth codimension-two plane in this affine chart, and the second is prime. Thus there is no embedded component even at their intersection.

**3. Identification and globalization.** Over \(a=1\), the equations of \(T=V(c,ae-bd)\) become \((c,v)\); the equations of the geometric double-double locus \(E\) become \((u,v-c^2)\). The latter follows by squaring \(q=AX^2+BXY+CY^2\), whose coefficient vector is
\[
[A^2:2AB:B^2+2AC:2BC:C^2].\tag{5}
\]
On the locus \(a=1\), its equations are precisely \(u=0\) and \(v=c^2\); conversely those relations reconstruct a square quadratic after algebraic closure. The triple-root locus is \(T\) because
\[
(\alpha X+\beta Y)^3(\gamma X+\delta Y)
=\alpha^3\gamma X^4+\alpha^3\delta X^3Y
 +\beta^3\gamma XY^3+\beta^3\delta Y^4;
\]
its coefficient matrix has rank one and \(c=0\). The discriminant, its singular scheme, and both coincident-root loci are invariant under invertible linear coordinate changes in \((X,Y)\). Over an algebraically closed field every nonzero quartic has a nonzero value at some point of \(\mathbb P^1\), so images of the chart \(a\ne0\) under \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) cover all projective coefficient space. Equation (4) therefore glues to the claimed equality of ideal sheaves. Faithfully flat descent establishes the assertion over arbitrary \(k\).

**4. Geometry and degree.** The surface \(T=V(c,ae-bd)\) is a smooth quadric in a hyperplane, so \(\deg T=2\). The map (5) is the quadratic Veronese map followed by linear projection from a point outside the secant cubic of \(v_2(\mathbb P^2)\): in the monomial basis \((A^2,AB,AC,B^2,BC,C^2)\), the center is represented by the nondegenerate quadratic form having \(AC\) and \(B^2\) coefficients both nonzero. In characteristic \(3\) its associated symmetric \(3\times3\) matrix has nonzero determinant. Hence the projection is a closed immersion; \(E\) is smooth of degree \((2H)^2=4\). Along the generic point of \(T\) the \(E\)-ideal is the unit ideal and (4) is \(\mathfrak m^3\), of local length \(1+2+3=6\). Along the generic point of \(E\), the \(T\)-ideal is the unit ideal, giving length \(1\). The fundamental cycle of \(Z\) is \(6[T]+[E]\), so its degree is \(16\).

## Relationship to prior work
Chipalkatti, *On equations defining coincident root loci* (arXiv:math/0110224v1, October 19, 2001), studies root-partition loci and their singular supports over \(\mathbb C\); in particular its Theorem 5.4 addresses reduced support, not the characteristic-three projective Tjurina scheme. D'Andrea and Chipalkatti, *On the Jacobian ideal of the binary discriminant* (arXiv:math/0601705v1, January 29, 2006), Proposition 4.1, gives the complete quartic Jacobian primary decomposition over characteristic zero. Its Remark 6.8 explicitly restricts its extension to fields of characteristic zero. In that setting the triple-root locus has degree \(6\) and its Jacobian component length \(2\); here the triple-root support collapses to a smooth quadric of degree \(2\) and its Jacobian component is its entire cubic infinitesimal thickening of length \(6\). Thus the result is neither an unchanged reduction of the older primary decomposition nor a conclusion of the cited characteristic-zero results. This is a best-knowledge literature comparison, not a priority certification.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns the *projective hypersurface singular scheme*, including the equation \(\Delta\), not the derivative ideal alone. It does not describe the normalization or conductor of the quartic discriminant, and it makes no general claim for binary forms of degrees other than four. Full historical priority, including older modular invariant-theory sources, has not been independently established. Symbolic checks complement but do not replace the chart-gluing argument; no independent audit was performed.
