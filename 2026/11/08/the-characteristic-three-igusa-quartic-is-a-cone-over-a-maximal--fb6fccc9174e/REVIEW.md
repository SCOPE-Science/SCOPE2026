Solving the full restricted gradient equation gives exactly fifteen pair partitions; the restricted Hessian has the prescribed two-dimensional radical, establishing fifteen ordinary nodes and no other singularities on the base. Rational double point resolution produces a K3 surface with fifteen split exceptional lines. The exact exhaustive counts over two finite fields, combined with the sharp K3 Weil bound at the second Frobenius power, force the entire Frobenius spectrum and the extension-field formulas. Computational tests are not used as a substitute for infinite-field arguments.

In particular the classical fifteen-line configuration is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim.

The complete arithmetic spectrum, not just one finite census, follows from sharpness of the bound and motivates the classification.
