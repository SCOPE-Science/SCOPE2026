# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite-permutation recurrence and inclusion-exclusion formula give the exact \(2\)-adic falling-factorial interpolation. Factorial divisibility proves convergence both for the series and its divided differences, uniformly on the full \(2\)-adic domain. The derivative congruences for the odd branch and the branch \(4\mathbb Z_2\) establish the two exact distance laws, and equal-cardinality finite residue sets give surjectivity at every level. The proof explicitly excludes the remaining odd congruence classes by the even-index \(1\pmod8\) obstruction. The finite-level verifier independently confirms complete images through \(2^{12}\) and both local distance laws in a bounded range.

Risk: an algebraic slip in one of the divisibility estimates would matter, but the estimates are established directly from consecutive-factor products; no probabilistic or finite-to-infinite inference is used.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk

Miska (arXiv:1508.01987v1, 2015) explicitly provides the falling-factorial interpolation, and proves periodicity and valuation results. Those are not claimed as novel. Inspection of its relevant primary sections did not find the complete dyadic *value image*, its Haar measure, or the two stated branchwise exact distance laws. The database and literature queries found no equivalent claim. The general Hensel technique in that paper could potentially imply this result after additional residue-target calculations, so priority remains qualified rather than certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact image for each modulus and a Haar-measure value resolve a motivated natural invariant of an established combinatorial sequence. The isometric decomposition is a useful structure theorem distinguishing the complete even branch from the restricted odd branch. This is not a table lookup, arbitrary slice, or simple restatement of valuations: valuation data alone does not determine the odd residue image or the full multilevel lifting pattern.

## Scope

All \(2\)-power moduli are proved; odd-prime analogues, representation frequencies, and independent validation remain outside scope.
