# Scientific review: single-deletion binary pair-profile balls

## Correctness
PASS. The deletion-locality identity was derived for both endpoints and interior positions. Eight binary triples collapse to four distinct profile-change vectors, with two further endpoint-only changes, yielding the six-feature model exactly. Inclusion-exclusion on feature sets gives an exact finite four-state rational generating function. A degree-272 cross-multiplication bound makes the coefficient comparison to degree 279 a universal polynomial identity certificate. The independent raw-word enumerator reproduces every profile-ball size distribution through length fourteen. The asymptotic saturation fraction follows from the two endpoint-transition orientations and elementary exponential pattern appearance. The proof excludes no possible input length greater than one.

## Originality
PASS under an explicit historical-priority qualification. The closest published framework is Kiah--Puleo--Milenkovic's 2015 profile-vector channel; its sections 1--2 analyze substitutions and partial read coverage, and its remaining enumeration is of valid profile-vector classes, not exact distinct profiles of deletion descendants. The published published-finding corpus two-multispectrum theorem counts realizable pair-frequency vectors and singleton words, while the related two-read deletion graph theorem counts source-pair intersections of full word-deletion balls. Neither yields the six-feature distribution, and direct literature searches for equivalent terms did not locate it. The full text of Jacquet--Knessl--Szpankowski's Markov-type paper was unavailable; its accessible abstract concerns total Markov-type counts and does not decisively address the new statistic. This is residual priority risk, not evidence of uniqueness.

## Value
PASS. Source-symbol deletion changes a length-dependent number of possible descendant words but, under a two-gram count observation, yields a sharply bounded six-outcome profile ball. The complete rational distribution determines exactly how often each ambiguity size occurs, and the length-nine threshold and limiting one-eighth saturation probability give interpretable channel-design benchmarks. The all-length distribution is more informative than isolated finite enumerations or a bare constant-size upper bound, while making no claim of higher-alphabet or longer-read generalization.
