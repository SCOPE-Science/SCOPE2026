# Scientific review — complete rational point–line incidence for the Fermat cubic surface

## Correctness: PASS
For every prime power \(q\) of characteristic different from three, the theorem counts rational points by incidences with the **geometric** 27 lines. The 27 lines follow from the three coordinate-pairings and the nine cube-root pairs; smoothness excludes characteristic three. In the split case, same-partition intersections yield 18 triple points, and cross-partition intersections yield either 81 ordinary doubles (odd characteristic) or 27 additional triples (characteristic two). A displayed six-line skew configuration and the rational blow-down to a projective plane give \(q^2+7q+1\) points; line-incidence double counting then gives the first two rows. In the nonsplit case, cubic bijectivity yields \(q^2+q+1\), Frobenius fixes exactly three lines, and all other rational geometric-line points lie on their union. The separate characteristic-two concurrency gives the last two rows. Consequently exactly the five stated field orders have zero uncovered points. The two fully independent local programs pass for ten small fields and two full geometric-line incidence configurations; their finite computations are corroboration, not the infinite proof. The standard 27-line theorem and rational blow-down are established input.

## Originality: PASS (best-knowledge, with risk)
The displayed Fermat surface, its 27 lines, 18 classical odd-characteristic Eckardt points, and its 45 characteristic-two star points are prior knowledge. Betten–Hirschfeld–Karaoglu classify the 27-line case over \(\mathbb F_{13}\), hence the isolated q=13 case is not presented as new. Brosowsky–Page–Ryan–Smith discuss all 45 characteristic-two stars and identify Hermitian finite-geometry rational points, strongly covering the q=4 special case. McKean counts arithmetic lines but not exact numbers of rational *points* with zero/one/two/three containing *geometric* lines. Movasati gives the classical line equations but no uniform field-by-field spectrum. Targeted mathematical-record searches returned no identical claim; a nearby paper on Fermat cubic sixer orbits concerns configurations of disjoint lines rather than rational-point strata. The new contribution is the uniform four-layer field-order formulas with all Frobenius-nonsplit cases and the exact total-coverage classification, not any individual known special case. Residual risk: an older finite-geometric incarnation could derive the whole spectrum without naming Fermat cubics.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The intrinsic distribution of rational points among the 27 geometric lines is a natural incidence invariant in arithmetic geometry and finite geometry, not an arbitrarily selected small parameter. The result determines all four strata uniformly for infinitely many finite fields, including the characteristically distinct and nonsplit phenomena, and completely classifies the rare finite fields in which the geometric-line union covers every rational point. The closed formulas have utility in rational-point geometry and incidence constructions. The proof is elementary but the cross-characteristic exact classification is substantive and motivated; neither this value nor originality rests on the verifier programs.
