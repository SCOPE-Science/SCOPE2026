# The complete rational-point and Jacobian groups of a very general six-line genus-two pencil

## Finding
Let \(K=\mathbb C(t)\) and consider the genus-two hyperelliptic curve
\[
 C:\qquad y^2=\prod_{i=1}^{5}(x+a_it+b_i),\qquad a_i,b_i\in\mathbb C.
\]
For **very general** parameters as specified below, its Jacobian and its set of rational points satisfy the exact equalities
\[
 J(C)(K)\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^4,
 \qquad
 C(K)=\{\infty,(-a_1t-b_1,0),\ldots,(-a_5t-b_5,0)\}.
\]
In particular the Mordell–Weil rank is zero, there is no rational Jacobian point of order exceeding two, and there are no non-Weierstrass rational points on this curve.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties are over \(\mathbb C\), in characteristic zero. In homogeneous coordinates \([T:X:Z]\), take the six branch lines
\[
 L_\infty=\{Z=0\},\qquad L_i=\{X+a_iT+b_iZ=0\}\quad(1\le i\le5).
\]
Require the \(a_i\) to be pairwise distinct and the ten values
\[
 t_{ij}=(b_j-b_i)/(a_i-a_j),\quad1\le i<j\le5,
\]
to be pairwise distinct. These conditions give six lines with fifteen distinct ordinary double intersections, no three concurrent; in particular the ten nodes among the five affine branch lines occur in different fibers of the pencil through \(z_o=[0:1:0]\). The conditions define a nonempty Zariski-open set (a rational witness is checked by `VERIFY.py`). Additionally impose the **very general** condition that the minimal resolution of the double plane branched over these lines has generic Picard number \(16\); that latter condition is the generic-lattice input of Clingher–Malmendier–Shaska [2] and must not be dropped. A very general point means a point outside a countable union of proper closed subvarieties of this parameter space. The equations of the generic fiber agree with Bannai–Tokunaga [1, §2.2].

## Proof
**The surface and its lattice.** Let \(Y\) be the minimal resolution of the double cover of \(\mathbb P^2\) ramified along the six lines. It is a K3 surface. The branch pencil through \(z_o\in L_\infty\) becomes a relatively minimal genus-two fibration \(f:S\to\mathbb P^1\) with a section \(O\) after two point blowups of \(Y\), as in [1, Introduction and §2.2]. Consequently \(\operatorname{NS}(S)\) is torsion-free, its rank is \(18\), and its discriminant has the same absolute value as \(\operatorname{NS}(Y)\). The generic transcendental lattice identified in [2, Proposition 3.2] is
\[
 T(Y)\cong H(2)\oplus H(2)\oplus\langle-2\rangle\oplus\langle-2\rangle,
\]
where \(H\) is the hyperbolic plane. It has determinant of absolute value \(2^6\). Unimodularity of the K3 cohomology lattice therefore gives
\[
 \rho(S)=18,\qquad |\operatorname{disc}\operatorname{NS}(S)|=2^6.
\]

**The vertical component lattice.** The generic configuration has exactly ten distinct finite parameters where two branch roots meet and one distinguished fiber at infinity. By the singular-fiber classification in [1, §2.2], the ten finite fibers have type \(\mathrm I_{2-0-0}\), contributing one nonidentity component with square \(-2\) apiece. The fiber at infinity has type \(\mathrm I^*_{0-0-0}\), contributing six nonidentity components. Its negative intersection matrix is encoded by the inverse given in [1, Table 1]: with five leaves labeled first and the sixth central component, the intersection matrix is
\[
 A_\infty=\begin{pmatrix}-2I_5&\mathbf 1\\\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&-3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 |\det A_\infty|=16.
\]
The cited inverse matrix is exactly \(-A_\infty^{-1}\), not merely a matching determinant. The subgroup \(T\subset\operatorname{NS}(S)\) generated by the zero section and all vertical components is generated equivalently by the zero section \(O\), a general fiber \(F\), and these sixteen nonidentity components. All relations among components of a fixed fiber are eliminated by substituting its total class \(F\); there are no relations among components supported on distinct fibers. The block formed by \(O,F\) has matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}O^2&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and determinant \(-1\). The remaining vertical component blocks are mutually orthogonal, so
\[
 \operatorname{rank}T=2+10+6=18,\qquad
 |\operatorname{disc}T|=1\cdot2^{10}\cdot16=2^{14}.
\]
Because both lattices have full rank, \(T\) has finite index in \(\operatorname{NS}(S)\). For such a sublattice the discriminants obey
\[
 [\operatorname{NS}(S):T]^2
 =\frac{|\operatorname{disc}T|}{|\operatorname{disc}\operatorname{NS}(S)|}
 =\frac{2^{14}}{2^6}=2^8,
\]
and hence the index is exactly \(16\).

**The Mordell–Weil group.** As a blowup of a K3, \(S\) has \(q(S)=0\). Thus its Jacobian fibration has no nonzero constant abelian trace. Shioda's higher-genus Mordell–Weil identification [3, Theorem 2.1] applies to this fibration with zero section and gives
\[
 J(C)(K)\cong\operatorname{NS}(S)/T.
\]
The group therefore has exactly \(16\) elements. On the other hand, all six branch points are \(K\)-rational. The five divisor classes \([(-a_it-b_i,0)-O]\) have order two: the divisor of \(x+a_it+b_i\) is twice the indicated finite branch point minus \(2O\). For a genus-two odd-degree hyperelliptic curve, the full geometric two-torsion is generated by these five classes with their single total-sum relation, so the rational two-torsion has \(2^4=16\) elements. This already exhausts \(J(C)(K)\), proving its asserted isomorphism.

**The rational points.** Let \(P\in C(K)\). Its Abel–Jacobi class \([P-O]\) lies in the two-torsion, hence \(2P\sim2O\). Since \(C\) has genus two and \(O\) is Weierstrass, the complete linear system \(|2O|\) is its hyperelliptic pencil: a divisor of degree two is equivalent to \(2O\) exactly when it is \(Q+\iota(Q)\), with \(\iota\) the hyperelliptic involution. Thus \(2P\sim2O\) forces \(P=\iota(P)\). The fixed points of \(\iota\) are precisely the six Weierstrass points already listed. All six are rational over \(K\), proving the exact census.

## Verification
Run `python3 VERIFY.py` using Python's standard library. It checks, in exact rational/integer arithmetic, the fifteen nodes, independence rank of ramification half-classes, a nonempty collision-parameter open set, the determinant of the distinguished six-component fiber, ten square-\(-2\) vertical blocks, the total \(2^{14}\) lattice determinant, and the discriminant index of \(16\). It outputs `VERIFY_OK`. The code is a reproducible consistency check of finite identities; it neither proves the generic Picard-rank theorem nor replaces the published geometric singular-fiber classification or higher-genus Mordell–Weil identification.

## Relationship to prior work
Bannai–Tokunaga [1] introduce the genus-two double-plane fibration and explicitly compute its possible singular fiber types and inverse component matrices, but their focus is Abel–Jacobi arithmetic for quartic bitangent arrangements. Clingher–Malmendier–Shaska [2] identify the generic six-line K3 lattice and study a different, elliptic fibration. Shioda [3] supplies the general higher-genus correspondence between Jacobian sections and the Néron–Severi quotient. The contribution here is the exact combination of these ingredients to classify **both** the rational Jacobian group and every rational point of the genus-two generic fiber. Neither the finite group of order sixteen nor the six-point census is asserted as an independent theorem in the inspected relevant portions of [1–3]. This is a specialization of established machinery and its historical priority remains subject to possible implicit coverage; no claim of first use of the individual component results is made.

## Limitations
The conclusion is for **very general** six-line configurations. Picard-rank jumps on special arrangements can enlarge the Mordell–Weil group, and an arbitrary configuration with merely no triple intersections is not covered. The theorem concerns \(\mathbb C(t)\)-rational points, not rational points over algebraic extensions or over smaller coefficient fields. It does not classify the reduction types after varying coefficients, polarization data, or all possible special loci. The numerical checker validates the finite lattice arithmetic, not the full geometric hypotheses. Independent specialist assessment has not been performed.
