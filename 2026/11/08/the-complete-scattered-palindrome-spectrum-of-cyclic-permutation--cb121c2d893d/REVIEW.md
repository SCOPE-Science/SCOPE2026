# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim counts distinct nonempty palindromic subsequence **strings** of a linear prefix of the cyclic permutation word, not their embeddings. The greedy earliest-embedding endpoint is proved by induction; reversed adjacent modular steps pair to \(q\) if different and \(2q\) if equal, with one central \(q\) in the even case. Each palindrome corresponds to a unique half-string. Repeat/change binomial counts and the prefix-threshold summation reconstruct the bivariate rational series. Coefficient extraction and full-period recurrence follow algebraically. Direct independent enumerations passed 56 host prefixes / 430 cells, and 1915 greedy minimum checks. Computations are tests, not the all-length proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Pandoh's 21 June 2021 seminar slides and Mahalingam–Pandoh's 5 August 2021 full preprint establish the motivation and general or extremal bounds, not the cyclic-permutation exact bivariate spectrum or its growth constant. The known binary alternating case is deliberately excluded from the novelty claim. Holub–Saari (2009) and Müllner–Ryzhikov (2019) address binary minimal palindromicity and longest palindromic subsequences, respectively, not full distinct-string enumeration. Actual research-index searches using cyclic-periodic, repeated-permutation, scattered-palindrome and generating-function aliases found no equivalent record; two nearby records concern subsequence universality or palindromic duplication codes rather than the same invariant. Published-equivalent and unindexed claims remain possible; uniqueness is not certified by search failure.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A complete two-parameter spectrum for every \(q\ge3\) in a natural balanced periodic family addresses the word-specific enumeration gap left by general extremal bounds, separates occurrence counts from distinct subsequence strings, and yields an exact growth exponent. The all-length full-spectrum result is not an arbitrary finite census or a consequence of the cited global maxima. It is restricted to periodic permutations; no unrestricted optimizer classification is asserted.
