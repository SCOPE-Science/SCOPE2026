# Scientific review: polygonal perimeter-loss limit and angle tomography

## Correctness — PASS

For every fixed convex polygon with no consecutive collinear sides, the hull boundary admits an exact vertex-by-vertex chord replacement when each original side contains a sample, an event with exponentially increasing probability. The chord length is \(\sqrt{u^2+v^2+2uv\cos\theta}\). Small disjoint boundary neighborhoods give independent exponential limits for the rescaled nearest distances; an exponential tail bound and the exponentially rare missing-side event establish convergence of all positive moments. The gamma-ratio change of variables produces the Laplace transform and the closed mean. The single-corner small-tail coefficient follows from its two endpoint slopes, and independent convolution gives the full small-tail exponent. Finally, the exact radius of finiteness of the moment-generating function determines the maximum turning angle; dividing the known Laplace factor recursively reconstructs all angles. The stated dependence on known perimeter, and the nonidentifiability of side lengths for rectangles, follow directly. Independent computations verified the finite-hull decomposition 4,800 times, evaluated critical corner integrals, and tested mean predictions. Numerical checks are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

The 2011 full-text paper by Böröczky–Fodor–Hug discusses boundary-sampled intrinsic volumes, but its exact leading formula requires the rolling-ball hypothesis and does not provide this polygonal perimeter-loss distribution. Its general theorem bounds the order in polytopes, without the coefficient or angle tomography. The 2019 full-text paper by Reitzner–Schütt–Werner treats boundary-sampled polytopes but focuses on facet counts and missing volume, not perimeter. The 2024 full-text paper by Prochno–Schütt–Sonnleitner–Werner addresses Hausdorff error in the same planar polygon sampling model; its limit concerns a different geometric functional. Older boundary-hull and missed-area results therefore give relevant motivation and tools, but the inspected statements neither imply this nonlinear independent-corner perimeter law nor its moment-generating-function reconstruction of exterior angles. Searches for aliases, Laplace transforms, perimeter deficits, and angle recovery produced no directly covering theorem. That is positive comparative evidence, not proof of historical uniqueness; unindexed older work and indirect consequences remain residual risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Perimeter is a fundamental intrinsic-volume observable for geometric approximation. The result identifies the full random fluctuation at its natural \(1/N\) scale for **all fixed convex polygons**, gives an exact mean and small-deficit probability, and determines precisely which geometric information survives in the full limiting law. The inverse statement is substantive: at a known perimeter the distribution determines every exterior angle, yet loses edge-length information even among rectangles. This contrasts meaningfully with prior area-loss and Hausdorff-error descriptions and explains why perimeter is a shape-sensitive but incomplete statistic. The theorem is not a mere parameter change or an isolated calculation. No finite-sample stability or unknown-perimeter inverse is asserted.
