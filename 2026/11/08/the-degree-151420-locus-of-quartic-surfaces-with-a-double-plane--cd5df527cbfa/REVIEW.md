# Review of the degree-151420 double-conic quartic locus

## Correctness
**PASS.** The complete-intersection ideal of a possibly degenerate plane conic has quartic squared-ideal dimension 14. The parameter base has dimension 8, so its incidence bundle has dimension 21. Generic smoothness away from the conic and irreducibility follow from Bertini, and the singular support determines the conic uniquely; hence the map to the parameter space is generically birational. The universal squared-ideal bundle has an explicit three-step filtration and the resulting Segre-class integral evaluates to 151420. A second exact torus-localization calculation reproduces the same integer. The argument applies over the complex numbers, without claiming positive-characteristic genericity.

## Originality
**PASS, with priority risk.** Earlier studies classify double-conic quartic surfaces or provide general dimension bounds, but the inspected sections of Slavov's hypersurface singular-locus paper and Ducat's nonnormal-quartic classification do not state the projective degree of this particular parameter locus. Carmeli–Radzivilovsky's explicit enumerative formula concerns singular lines, not conics. The result is not an implication of any numerical formula found in these papers without the new conic-bundle intersection calculation. Searches using conic-degree aliases and the number 151420 produced no equivalent statement. Historically unindexed 19th/20th-century enumerations and possibly implicit general formulas remain a residual risk; this is not a claim of definitive world priority.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The integer 151420 counts a natural enumerative problem: quartic surfaces with a double plane conic through 21 general points. The conic-support component is the classical next support type beyond the line component, for which a separate enumerative theory is already developed. The result bridges the classical geometric classification of quartic double curves with an exact intersection-theoretic projective degree, not an arbitrarily chosen parameter slice.
