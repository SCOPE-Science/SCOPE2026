# Assessment of the Desargues edge-Kempe reconfiguration theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The finite proof covers every possible matching and alternating cycle for the explicitly defined 20-vertex, 30-edge graph. Matching recursion has a branch-by-first-uncovered-vertex completeness argument; complement cycle types \((20)\) and \((10,10)\) exhaust all 60 matchings and imply 32 unlabelled factorisations. An explicit thirty-six-edge certificate, independently checked by direct colouring recursion and matching-factorisation recursion, verifies a twenty-state component isomorphic to the original graph and six further two-state components. Python programs verified from their actual package paths. Limit: this is a bounded finite classification, not an infinite-family claim.

**Originality — PASS, with limited historical assurance.** Belcastro–Haas (2012 initial arXiv release; sections 1, 3–5) discuss 3-edge-Kempe equivalence and class-count theorems. Goedgebeur–Östergård (2021 initial release; sections 2–3 and Tables 1–4) computationally tabulate numbers of equivalence classes by graph order; their 20-vertex records might already imply the weaker seven-class count if graph identities were looked up. They do not give the full 32-state switching graph of the Desargues graph or identify its 20-vertex component with the original graph. No directly equivalent result was found by targeted title, graph-name, alias, state-count, graph-isomorphism and general edge-Kempe searches. A search failure does not guarantee priority: raw unpublished transition data and unindexed computations remain residual risks. The substantive new-looking comparison is adjacency and component geometry, not merely seven components.

**Value — PASS.** The Desargues graph is the canonical cubic symmetric incidence graph of a highly structured configuration and one of the standard generalized Petersen graphs. For this natural object, the remarkable reappearance of the source graph as a whole switching component is not a routine count, parameter substitution or arbitrary slice. It gives a complete shortest-move geometry, explains the coexistence of an accessible 20-state family with six isolated pairs, and provides a compact exact benchmark for edge-colouring reconfiguration studies. It is a motivated self-contained finite structural classification, not a claim of downstream theory.
