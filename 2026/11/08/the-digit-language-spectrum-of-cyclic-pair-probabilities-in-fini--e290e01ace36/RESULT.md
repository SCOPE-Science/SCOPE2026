# The digit-language spectrum of cyclic-pair probabilities in finite abelian \(p\)-groups

## Finding

For a finite group \(G\), let
\[
\theta(G)=\Pr_{x,y\in G}\bigl(\langle x,y\rangle\text{ is cyclic}\bigr),
\]
with independent uniform \(x,y\). The following is an *exact membership test for the rational values of this statistic on noncyclic finite abelian prime-power groups*, with a constructive inverse.

**Spectrum theorem.** A rational number \(q\in(0,1)\) equals \(\theta(G)\) for some noncyclic finite abelian \(p\)-group (where the prime \(p\), order and group are all initially unknown) if and only if the following tests succeed.

1. In lowest terms, the denominator of \(q\) is \(p^{2n-1}\), for a prime \(p\) and integer \(n\ge2\).
2. The number
   \[
   T=\frac{p^{2n}q-1}{p+1}
   \]
   is a positive integer.
3. Reading the ordinary base-\(p\) digits of \(T\) from least significant to most significant, one sees nonempty runs of the digit \(p-1\), with *exactly one zero digit between successive runs*, no other digits, and no trailing zero. Their lengths \(r_1,\ldots,r_m\) satisfy
   \[
   r_1\ge r_2\ge\cdots\ge r_m\ge1,\qquad r_1\ge2,
   \qquad r_1+\cdots+r_m=n.
   \]

If these tests succeed, the group is uniquely determined within the stated family and is explicitly
\[
G\cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r_1}C_{p^{\lambda_i}},\qquad
\lambda_i=\#\{j:r_j\ge i\}.
\]
Consequently, the number of attained rational values having reduced denominator \(p^{2n-1}\) is exactly \(P(n)-1\), where \(P(n)\) denotes the number of integer partitions of \(n\). These values are pairwise distinct across *all* primes and orders. The value \(1\) occurs precisely for cyclic groups and does not determine their orders.

The contribution claimed here is the complete arithmetic image criterion, the run-length decoding rule and the denominator-free inverse; the previously known injectivity of the sum-of-element-orders statistic at a fixed order is **not** claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope

All groups are finite. A finite abelian \(p\)-group has the standard invariant-factor decomposition
\[
G=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}C_{p^{\lambda_i}},\quad
\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_r\ge1,
\quad n=\sum_i\lambda_i.
\]
For \(j=1,\ldots,m=\lambda_1\), set \(r_j=\#\{i:\lambda_i\ge j\}\), \(s_0=0\), and \(s_j=\sum_{h=1}^j r_h\). Thus \(r_j\) are the column lengths of the Young diagram of the partition \((\lambda_i)\). Noncyclic means \(r=r_1\ge2\). The result does not assert injectivity for arbitrary nonabelian groups or arbitrary finite abelian groups with several prime divisors.

## Proof

**Step 1: Exact cyclic-subgroup count.** The subgroup of elements annihilated by \(p^j\) has size
\[
|G[p^j]|=p^{s_j}.
\]
Hence \(p^{s_j}-p^{s_{j-1}}\) elements have order exactly \(p^j\), and the number of cyclic subgroups of that order is
\[
c_j=\frac{p^{s_j}-p^{s_{j-1}}}{p^j-p^{j-1}}.
\]
Every cyclic subgroup of order \(p^j\) is generated by exactly \(p^{2j}-p^{2j-2}\) ordered pairs, by reducing the generating condition modulo its unique subgroup of index \(p\). Every cyclic ordered pair generates one unique cyclic subgroup, so the sets of generating pairs from different cyclic subgroups are disjoint. Including the unique pair generating the identity subgroup gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
N:=p^{2n}\theta(G)
&=1+\sum_{j=1}^m c_j(p^{2j}-p^{2j-2})\\
&=1+(p+1)\sum_{j=1}^m p^{j-1+s_{j-1}}(p^{r_j}-1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
No probabilistic approximation is used.

**Step 2: A positional encoding without carries.** Define
\[
a_j=j-1+s_{j-1}.
\]
Then
\[
T=\frac{N-1}{p+1}=\sum_{j=1}^m p^{a_j}(p^{r_j}-1).
\tag{2}
\]
For each \(j\), \(p^{r_j}-1\) has precisely \(r_j\) base-\(p\) digits, all equal to \(p-1\). Its shifted support occupies positions \(a_j,\ldots,a_j+r_j-1\). The next support starts at
\[
a_{j+1}=a_j+r_j+1.
\]
Thus exactly one zero-digit position separates consecutive blocks; their supports are disjoint, so no carries occur. Conversely, every digit word with the specified nonincreasing run lengths determines the partition \(\lambda_i=\#\{j:r_j\ge i\}\); this gives the finite abelian \(p\)-group in the theorem. The construction has order \(p^n\) because \(\sum_jr_j=n\). Substitution into (1) proves that its probability is precisely the proposed \(q\). This proves both necessity and sufficiency once \(p,n\) are recovered.

**Step 3: Recovering the prime and order from the reduced denominator.** If \(G\) is noncyclic, then \(r_1\ge2\). The first summand of (2) is \(p^{r_1}-1\equiv-1\pmod{p^2}\), while all later summands are divisible by \(p^{r_1+1}\), hence by \(p^3\). Therefore
\[
T\equiv-1\pmod{p^2},\qquad
N=1+(p+1)T\equiv-p\pmod{p^2}.
\]
In particular, the \(p\)-adic valuation of \(N\) is exactly one, so the reduced denominator of \(\theta(G)=N/p^{2n}\) is \(p^{2n-1}\). This determines the prime and \(n\) uniquely from the rational number. Equation (2) then determines the digit word and hence all invariant factors. No group order is supplied to the inverse algorithm.

**Step 4: Exact count of values.** The partitions of \(n\) other than \((n)\) are precisely the groups with \(r_1\ge2\). By the preceding steps their rational probabilities are pairwise distinct, including when primes and orders vary. Hence there are exactly \(P(n)-1\) such values per denominator.

**Boundary cases.** Every cyclic group satisfies \(\theta(G)=1\), so cyclic groups cannot be recognized individually from this statistic. For \(p=2\) and \(G=C_4\oplus C_2\), equation (1) gives \(\theta(G)=17/32\). The associated integer is \(T=11\), whose binary digits read \(1,1,0,1\) from low to high, recovering \((r_1,r_2)=(2,1)\). For \(p=3\) and \(G=C_9\oplus C_3\), one gets \(\theta(G)=83/243\) and \(T=62\), whose ternary digits read \(2,2,0,2\), again recovering \((2,1)\).

## Verification

The standalone file `CHECK.py` uses Python standard-library exact integers and rational arithmetic. It independently computes \(N\) from counts of cyclic subgroups, compares it with the carry-free block formula, checks the denominator and reconstructs every partition by the digit algorithm. It exhausts noncyclic partitions through order exponents \(20\), \(14\) and \(12\), respectively, for primes \(2\), \(3\) and \(5\), finding \(3445\) distinct, correctly recovered cases. For \(40\) smaller prime-power groups it additionally enumerates all group elements and all ordered pairs directly. The recorded output is `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt` and ends in `VERIFY_OK`.

The enumeration verifies only those finite instances. The infinite statements follow from Steps 1–4, not from the checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Patrick, Sherman, Sugar and Wepsic (1993) introduced the cyclic-generation probability and established universal bounds and tuple extensions. Chatterjee and Das (arXiv:2607.13720, first posted July 15, 2026) studied sharp bounds, particular rational levels, and the general formula \(\sum_{g\in G}\Psi(o(g))/|G|^2\), where \(\Psi\) is the Dedekind psi function. The exact counting identity in Step 1 agrees with this published general formula; it is not new.

There is an essential additional overlap: for any finite \(p\)-group, \(\Psi(p^j)=(1+1/p)p^j\) when \(j\ge1\). Thus
\[
\theta(G)=\frac{(p+1)\sum_{g\in G}o(g)-1}{p\,|G|^2}.
\]
Tărnăuceanu's fixed-order rigidity theorem for sums of element orders of finite abelian \(p\)-groups, also discussed and extended by Amiri (2026), already implies fixed-order injectivity of \(\theta\). **Fixed-order injectivity alone is not offered as a new theorem.** The result here is the exact necessary-and-sufficient *description of all attainable reduced rational numbers* by their base-\(p\) digits, with recovery of the otherwise unknown prime and order, and an explicit constructive inverse. The inspected published works discuss formulas, rigidity for the different element-order sum, and bounds, rather than this digit-language image characterization.

Tărnăuceanu and Tóth's *cyclicity degree* (2015) is a different statistic: the fraction of subgroups that are cyclic, not the probability that a random ordered pair generates a cyclic subgroup. Its formulas do not imply this rational spectrum.

## Limitations

The criterion characterizes the spectrum for noncyclic *abelian prime-power* groups, not the complete spectrum over all finite groups. It cannot distinguish cyclic groups from one another. For example, the nonabelian quaternion group \(Q_8\) and the abelian group \(C_2^2\) both have cyclic-pair probability \(5/8\), despite different orders and element-order distributions; hence the inverse claim cannot be extended to arbitrary groups. For general finite abelian groups with multiple prime divisors, cyclic Sylow factors are invisible and numerators can cancel denominators across primes. Prime-power factorization of the rational denominator is an arithmetic operation in the recognition procedure, not a claim of polynomial-time integer factorization. Historical priority beyond the inspected literature is not independently certified.
