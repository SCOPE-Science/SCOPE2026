# The dihedral quotient of the \(3\times3\) grid matching complex is a two-sphere

## Finding
Let \(G=P_3\square P_3\) be the Cartesian graph on the nine positions \(\{0,1,2\}^2\), with nearest-neighbor edges. Write \(X=\mathcal M(G)\) for the simplicial matching complex: its vertices are the twelve edges of \(G\), and a set spans a simplex when its edges have pairwise disjoint endpoints. The order-eight square-symmetry group \(D_4\) acts simplicially on \(X\). Then the **geometric orbit space** satisfies
\[
\boxed{\ |X|/D_4\simeq S^2.\ }
\]
The conclusion concerns the ordinary topological quotient (including points with nontrivial stabilizers), not the Borel homotopy quotient. In particular it gives an integral homotopy classification rather than just an invariant rational Betti number.

## Assumptions and scope
Coordinates are ordered lexicographically, with edges ordered lexicographically by their ordered endpoints. All homology groups are unreduced unless a tilde is shown. The automorphisms are the four rotations and four reflections of the square. The quotient is taken on geometric realizations, not by identifying the vertices of the original abstract complex before realization. The first barycentric subdivision removes inversions: the quotient is a finite regular \(\Delta\)-complex with basis elements indexed by orbits of strict chains of nonempty matching faces.

## Proof
**1. Simply connected source.** Exactly \(130\) nonempty matching simplices occur, with face counts \((12,44,56,18)\) in dimensions zero through three. Label the twelve grid edges lexicographically by \(0,\ldots,11\). Successively toggle the vertices in the order `0,9,3,5,2,7,4,10,8,1,6,11`: at each stage match every currently unmatched pair \((\sigma,\sigma\cup\{e\})\) where both faces remain. The exhaustive reversed Hasse-incidence digraph is acyclic, as verified by the included standalone checker over all \(328\) incidences. The matching has \(62\) pairs and leaves one critical zero-cell and five critical two-cells. Forman's discrete Morse theorem therefore gives \(X\simeq\bigvee^5 S^2\), so \(X\) is simply connected with free integral reduced homology of rank five in degree two only.

**2. Fundamental group of the quotient.** A reflection exchanging top and bottom rows fixes every horizontal edge in the middle row, hence fixes a vertex of \(|X|\). Diagonal reflection interchanges the disjoint grid edges \(\{(0,1),(0,2)\}\) and \(\{(1,0),(2,0)\}\), hence fixes the midpoint of their matching simplex. These two reflections generate \(D_4\). The standard orbit-space fundamental-group theorem for a finite simplicial group action on a simply connected polyhedron (Armstrong) identifies \(\pi_1(|X|/D_4)\) with the quotient of \(D_4\) by the normal subgroup generated by elements having fixed points. Thus \(\pi_1(|X|/D_4)=1\).

**3. Rational second homology.** On the five-dimensional \(H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\), the alternating oriented-face Lefschetz formula yields traces \((5,1,1,1,-1,-1,1,1)\) for identity, the three nonidentity rotations, two axis reflections and two diagonal reflections in that order. Each trace is obtained by a complete enumeration of the matching faces fixed setwise, including the orientation sign. Averaging shows \(\dim H_2(X;\mathbb Q)^{D_4}=1\). Over any coefficient field of characteristic different from two, the cellular orbit-chain complex is the group-coinvariant chain complex of the barycentric subdivision; averaging is exact. As the integral source homology is free and its invariant direct summand over \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\) has rank one, the quotient has exactly one second-homology generator and no positive-degree homology in other dimensions over every such field.

**4. The two-primary issue.** Build the orbit-chain complex of the barycentric subdivision using each strict inclusion chain of nonempty matching simplices as a simplex. Its quotient cellular group ranks are \((20,87,123,54)\), in degrees zero through three. Boundary maps are the alternating deletion of a face from an inclusion chain, followed by orbit canonicalization. The checker tests the full integer identities \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) and \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\). Their ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) are exactly \((19,68,54)\). Consequently \(H_i(|X|/D_4;\mathbb F_2)\) has dimensions \((1,0,1,0)\) for \(i=0,1,2,3\). Since the quotient is simply connected, integral \(H_1=0\). Since its rational \(H_2\) has rank one and its mod-two \(H_2\) has dimension one, universal coefficients exclude two-primary torsion in integral \(H_2\). The characteristic-odd calculation above excludes all odd-primary torsion. Integral \(H_3\) is a subgroup of a free cellular chain group, hence free, and has rational rank zero, hence vanishes. It follows that \(\widetilde H_i(|X|/D_4;\mathbb Z)\) is \(\mathbb Z\) for \(i=2\) and zero otherwise.

**5. Homotopy recognition.** By the degree-two Hurewicz theorem, the simply connected quotient admits a map \(S^2\to |X|/D_4\) representing a generator of \(H_2\). The map is an integral homology equivalence, and hence a homotopy equivalence by the simply connected homological Whitehead theorem. This proves the claim.

The computational steps are exhaustive finite checks; the characteristic-odd transfer, orbit fundamental-group calculation and homotopy recognition are mathematical arguments, not inferred from the finite test.

## Relationship to prior work
Chauhan, Sharma and Shukla's 2026 study proves connectivity and integral homology bounds for the matching complexes of \(3\times n\) grids; it does not state an integral homotopy classification of the **full square-symmetry quotient**. An older 2021 article by Goyal, Shukla and Singh claimed nonequivariant sphere-wedge classifications, but was retracted in its 2025 corrigendum after the authors identified invalid inclusion-map arguments. The present source matching is proved afresh, and the orbit-space computation requires new fixed-point and integral quotient-chain information. Existing records treating axis reflections and half-turns on \(3\times n\) matching homology concern rational character information for a smaller symmetry subgroup; they do not imply the integral \(D_4\)-orbit homotopy type. Similar three-cut or robust-clique complexes on square grids are different simplicial objects.

## Limitations
Only the exact \(3\times3\) grid and its **full** dihedral action are classified. No general square-width quotient theorem, integral equivariant splitting, or homotopy classification of individual reflection quotients is claimed. The 2026 motivating paper's complete text could not be accessed during comparison, so an implicit prior statement in its unavailable sections remains a residual literature risk; no such statement is indicated by its accessible abstract. The theorem has undergone self-review, not independent mathematical validation.
