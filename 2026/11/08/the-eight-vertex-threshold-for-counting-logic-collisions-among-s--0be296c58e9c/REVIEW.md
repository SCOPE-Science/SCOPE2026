# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The small-order enumeration is exhaustive because every split graph has some clique–independent partition and the checker traverses every binary cross-edge matrix for every partition size. The certificate tests actual adjacency-preserving bijections, not just equality of refinement counts. For each order through seven the computed refinement-group counts also equal the independently published split-graph isomorphism counts of Royle. The two eight-vertex graphs have a provably common equitable quotient, a degree-invariant obstruction to isomorphism, and universal-join extensions preserving both properties for every greater order. No finite extrapolation is used for the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Inspected Royle's original enumeration paper, the relevant definitions and general amenability/biregular-replacement results of Arvind–Köbler–Rattan–Verbitsky, and Barrus on fractional degree-sequence realizations. These do not state the exact minimum order eight for nonisomorphic split graphs agreeing in two-variable counting logic or a connected counterpair at every higher order. The broad amenability criterion and biregular replacement lemma give conceptual tools but do not by themselves assert the class-relative minimum. The closest distinct result on split graphs concerns first failure of within-graph orbit refinement at order ten, which does not imply or contradict this between-graph equivalence result. Specialized historical graph-enumeration tables not located remain a genuine priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Split graphs are a standard hereditary class with a canonical clique/independent-set interface. The exact first order at which low-variable counting logic ceases to identify them is a natural logical invariant of that class; its determination removes the possibility of smaller hidden failure and provides a sharp benchmark for refinement-based isomorphism testing. The explicit minimal pair shows that even an equitable two-cell degree partition can conceal the cycle structure of a bipartite incidence matrix. The construction of connected counterpairs at every larger order makes the boundary complete, rather than an isolated observation.

## Limits and closest literature
The comparison is relative to split graphs, not against every simple graph. The finite cutoff was verified by exhaustive computation and requires that certificate; its correctness is not inferred from a mere sample. Royle (2000) enumerates split graphs, Arvind et al. (2015) characterize global amenability, and Barrus (2013) concerns degree-sequence realizations; no priority guarantee is made for unlocated specialized literature.
