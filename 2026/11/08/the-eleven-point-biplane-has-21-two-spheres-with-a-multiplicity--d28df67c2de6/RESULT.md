# The eleven-point biplane has 21 two-spheres with a multiplicity-free simple-group homology action

## Finding

Let \(Q=\{1,3,4,5,9\}\subseteq\mathbb Z/11\mathbb Z\). For every \(i\in\mathbb Z/11\mathbb Z\), define the block \(B_i=i+Q\). Let \(K\) be the abstract simplicial complex whose faces are the subsets of at least one block \(B_i\), together with the empty face. These eleven blocks form the unique symmetric \(2\)-\((11,5,2)\) biplane, also called the Paley or Hadamard biplane. Then

\[K\simeq\bigvee^{21}S^2.\]

The full simplicial automorphism group of \(K\) is \(G=\mathrm{PSL}(2,11)\) of order \(660\). The natural action on its only nonzero reduced rational homology representation satisfies

\[\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\cong V_{10}^{-}\oplus V_{11},\]

where \(V_{10}^{-}\) and \(V_{11}\) are distinct absolutely irreducible rational representations. Their characters, indexed by element orders \(1,2,3,6,5,11\) (merging pairs of conjugacy classes only when their values coincide), are respectively

\[\chi_{10}^{-}=(10,-2,1,1,0,-1),\qquad\chi_{11}=(11,-1,-1,-1,1,0).\]

Thus the full homology character is \((21,-3,0,0,1,-1)\). There are no nonzero \(G\)-invariant reduced rational homology classes; equivalently, \(\widetilde H_*(K/G;\mathbb Q)=0\). In contrast, the affine subgroup \(\mathbb F_{11}\rtimes C_5\), of order \(55\), fixes exactly one dimension of \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\).

## Assumptions and scope

All simplices are unordered subsets of the eleven labeled points. The block complex is the simplicial union of eleven full four-simplices, not its dual incidence graph, flag complex, or geometric surface embedding. Homotopy equivalence is nonequivariant and is over the integers; representation statements are over \(\mathbb Q\). The quotient assertion concerns *reduced rational homology*, not contractibility, simple connectivity, or an equivariant wedge decomposition.

The same \(K\) is the directed **out-neighborhood complex** of the quadratic-residue Paley tournament on eleven vertices: the arc \(i\to j\) exists exactly when \(j-i\in Q\). This identification does not replace the out-neighborhood complex with the directed flag complex.

## Proof

**The design and simplices.** The five nonzero squares modulo eleven are precisely \(Q\). Translation gives eleven distinct blocks, each of size five. Exhaustive verification of the eleven sets shows that each point pair occurs in exactly two blocks and that any two distinct blocks intersect in exactly two points. Every singleton and pair is a face, and every triple **that is a face** occurs in exactly one block (since two distinct blocks have just two common points). The remaining unique faces likewise arise inside individual blocks. This gives the nonempty face vector

\[(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(11,55,110,55,11),\]

with \(242\) nonempty faces. The count of triples also follows from \(11\binom{5}{3}=110\).

**Integral homotopy.** The accompanying checker constructs all \(242\) faces independently from the blocks and the tournament out-neighborhoods and verifies equality. On the nonempty face poset, perform the following completely specified matching: for each pivot \(v\in\{0,\ldots,10\}\) in ascending order, consider all currently unmatched faces not containing \(v\), in ascending order first by cardinality then lexicographically, and match \(\sigma\) to \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) whenever both remain unmatched. The resulting \(110\) disjoint face/coface pairs leave precisely one critical vertex and \(21\) critical triangles. The complete Hasse diagram has \(715\) covering relations. Orient each matched relation upward and every other covering relation downward. Exhaustive topological sorting of the complete resulting digraph visits all \(242\) nodes, certifying no directed cycle. Forman's discrete Morse theorem gives a CW complex with one zero-cell and \(21\) two-cells and no other cells. Their attaching maps are maps of circles to a point, hence nullhomotopic. Consequently the integral homotopy type is the asserted wedge of two-spheres. Independent mod-two boundary reduction gives rank \(45\) for the degree-two differential and no first homology, agreeing with the integral result; this computation is a corroboration, not a substitute for the acyclic matching.

**The full group and character.** The block-preserving permutations generated by

\[t(i)=i+1,\quad m(i)=3i\pmod{11},\quad s=(0,1,10,7,4,6,5,3,9,8,2)\]

(where the displayed tuple for \(s\) lists its images of \(0,\ldots,10\)) generate exactly \(660\) distinct permutations, all preserving the eleven blocks. The published identification of the complete automorphism group of the unique \(2\)-\((11,5,2)\) biplane as \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,11)\), of order \(660\), shows that this is all of \(\operatorname{Aut}(K)\): the blocks are precisely the maximal simplices, hence any simplicial automorphism permutes them.

For each of the \(660\) permutations \(g\), the signed simplicial Lefschetz number is computed as

\[L(g)=\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in K\\g\sigma=\sigma}}(-1)^{\dim\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_\sigma),\]

with the sign measured against the increasing orientation of \(\sigma\). Since \(K\) is connected and has only reduced second homology, \(\operatorname{tr}(g|H_2(K;\mathbb Q))=L(g)-1\). Exact enumeration gives the following complete order/trace/count profile:

| Order of \(g\) | \(1\) | \(2\) | \(3\) | \(6\) | \(5\) | \(11\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Trace on \(H_2\) | \(21\) | \(-3\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(-1\) |
| Elements | \(1\) | \(55\) | \(110\) | \(110\) | \(264\) | \(120\) |

The source character table for \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,11)\) gives the distinct irreducible characters \(\chi_{10}^{-}\) and \(\chi_{11}\) displayed above. Comparing every conjugacy class establishes \(\chi_{H_2}=\chi_{10}^{-}+\chi_{11}\), and semisimplicity in characteristic zero proves the unique direct-sum decomposition. Both constituents have integer-valued characters and each occurs exactly once in the rational module; hence both are defined over \(\mathbb Q\), and each remains irreducible over \(\mathbb C\).

Finally, character averaging gives

\[\dim H_2(K;\mathbb Q)^G=\frac{21-3(55)+264-120}{660}=0.\]

For \(A=\langle t,m\rangle\), which has order \(55\), the same exhaustive character calculation gives \(\sum_{g\in A}\chi_{H_2}(g)=55\), so \(\dim H_2(K;\mathbb Q)^A=1\). A finite group action on a finite simplicial complex admits a barycentric subdivision without inversions; over \(\mathbb Q\) the transfer/averaging argument identifies quotient rational homology with invariant rational homology, proving \(\widetilde H_*(K/G;\mathbb Q)=0\).

## Relationship to prior work

The design and its full simple automorphism group are classical; Devillers and Praeger identify this unique biplane and \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,11)\) in their Example 3.2. Isaacs' published character table for \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,11)\) supplies the irreducible class functions, but does not identify which occur in the homology of this complex. The available general topological work on boolean-representable simplicial complexes uses different hypotheses and does not classify this four-dimensional biplane block complex. Results on biplane surface embeddings concern a different topological construction. The present result is the complete homotopy type and the specific full-group homology character, not the preexisting design parameters or abstract character table.

## Limitations

No equivariant homotopy splitting or integral representation-lattice decomposition is asserted. The character is rational; the quotient conclusion does not imply integral acyclicity. A mathematical claim of publication priority remains provisional because an unindexed calculation may exist, and no independent mathematical audit has been conducted.
