The canonical \((132,213)\)-avoiding permutahedron has a unique inball of radius \(\sqrt2\sqrt{d(d-1)}/(\sqrt{d(d-1)}+\sqrt2)\), with exactly \(d+1\) contacting facets for \(d\ge3\), while retaining the ordinary permutahedron's full circumsphere. Its radius ratio grows as \(d^{3/2}\), even though its face lattice is always a cube.
