# The exact Haar polygon-type law of central regular-dodecahedron sections

## Finding

Let \(D\subset\mathbb R^3\) be a centered regular dodecahedron, and choose a plane \(u^\perp\) through its center with uniformly distributed unoriented normal \(\{u,-u\}\). Let \(N\) count the vertices of the resulting section, counting only genuine corners. Put \(\alpha=\arccos(1/3)\) and \(\beta=\arccos(\sqrt5/3)\). Then almost surely \(N\) is one of \(6,8,10\), with

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(N=6)&=10-\frac{15}{\pi}(\alpha+\beta),\\
\Pr(N=8)&=\frac{15}{\pi}(2\alpha+\beta-\pi),\\
\Pr(N=10)&=6-\frac{15}{\pi}\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]

These probabilities are approximately \(0.638408811559\), \(0.238989468900\), and \(0.122601719541\), respectively. The probability-weighting cells of the oriented normal sphere are a three-orbit arrangement: \(20\) spherical hexagons, \(60\) spherical triangles, and \(12\) spherical pentagons, with exact areas \(2\pi-3\alpha-3\beta\), \(2\alpha+\beta-\pi\), and \(2\pi-5\alpha\), respectively. The cell shape counts and corresponding section-corner counts are deliberately distinguished: a spherical triangle cell corresponds to an octagonal *section*.

## Assumptions and scope

The dodecahedron is convex, regular, three-dimensional, and centered at the origin. The random plane passes through the origin. Its normal is Haar-uniform on the sphere; antipodal normals determine the same plane, without changing the resulting probability. The exceptional normals perpendicular to a polyhedron vertex form finitely many great circles and have probability zero. The law is unchanged by uniformly scaling or rigidly rotating the solid.

Write \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). For explicit verification, scale the solid so its vertex set is the \(8\) triples \((\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)\), the \(4\) triples \((0,\pm\varphi^{-1},\pm\varphi)\), the \(4\) triples \((\pm\varphi^{-1},\pm\varphi,0)\), and the \(4\) triples \((\pm\varphi,0,\pm\varphi^{-1})\), with independent signs in each displayed pair. Every vertex has squared norm \(3\), and adjacent vertices have squared separation \(8-4\varphi\).

## Proof

**1. Sections and normal sign chambers.** For a normal outside the exceptional great circles, the plane passes through a polyhedron edge exactly when the scalar products of its two endpoints with the normal have opposite signs. Each intersected edge contributes exactly one genuine section corner. Thus \(N(u)\) is the number of sign-changing edges among the \(30\) edges. Because vertices occur in antipodal pairs, there are ten distinct hyperplanes \(u\cdot v=0\); their great circles partition the normal sphere into open cells on which \(N\) is constant.

**2. Exact exhaustion of the cells.** Enumerate the \(20\) vertices in \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]^3\), retain one representative from each antipodal pair, and enumerate all \(2^{10}\) candidate sign patterns. Every pair of the ten hyperplanes contributes two oriented intersection rays \(\pm(v_i\times v_j)\). No third hyperplane contains any such ray, as exact nonzero triple products show. For each sign pattern, retain rays for which all ten signed scalar products are nonnegative; a pattern gives a genuine full-dimensional chamber precisely when every signed inequality is strictly positive at at least one retained ray. Indeed a pointed polyhedral cone is generated by its extreme rays, and the sum of its retained rays is then strictly feasible. This is a necessary-and-sufficient test with finite exact arithmetic, not a numerical mesh approximation.

The complete reproducible enumeration, in `verify_dodeca.py`, checks all ten-plane triple determinants; all \(90\) oriented pairwise rays; all sign patterns; all \(30\) edges; and the exact types of each spherical corner. Its output is the following exhaustive table. The entries in the final column are the multiset of *signed normal-vector inner products* at the spherical corners (each normal has norm \(\sqrt3\)):

| Section corners | Spherical-cell sides | Cells | Signed corner products |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 6 | 6 | 20 | three \(1\) and three \(\sqrt5\) |
| 8 | 3 | 60 | two \(-1\) and one \(-\sqrt5\) |
| 10 | 5 | 12 | five \(1\) |

All \(92\) chambers are accounted for; independently, the arrangement of \(10\) general-position great circles has \(10(10-1)+2=92\) cells. No degree-of-freedom assumptions or partial random searches are used in this classification.

**3. Spherical excess and the Haar probabilities.** At a spherical chamber corner cut out by two inward hemisphere normals \(s_i v_i\) and \(s_jv_j\), the interior angle equals \(\pi-\arccos((s_i v_i\cdot s_jv_j)/3)\). Consequently, for a spherical cell with signed corner products \(d_1,\dots,d_h\), its area is exactly \(2\pi-\sum_{j=1}^{h}\arccos(d_j/3)\), by Girard's spherical polygon formula. Applying this formula to the exhaustive table gives the three cell areas in the Finding. Multiplying each area by its number of cells and dividing by \(4\pi\) gives the probability law. These three probabilities sum to one and are strictly positive.

**4. Independent expectation identity.** Adjacent dodecahedron vertices have scalar product \(3-(8-4\varphi)/2=\sqrt5\), so their angle at the origin equals \(\beta\). For a Haar-uniform normal, the probability of separating their signed scalar products is \(\beta/\pi\). Summing over all \(30\) edges proves \(\mathbb E N=30\beta/\pi\). Direct substitution of the three probabilities gives the same identity.

## Verification

Run `python verify_dodeca.py` using standard Python 3 (no external packages). Its scalars are integer pairs representing elements of \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\); multiplication uses \(\varphi^2=\varphi+1\). Signs are decided exactly from the irrationality of \(\sqrt5\), by integer-square comparison. Therefore the combinatorial classification is exhaustive, not based on floating-point tolerance. The script asserts the vertex and edge counts, no triple coincident great-circle intersections, all feasible chamber signatures, and spherical-area normalization. The script prints `EXACT_CERTIFICATE_OK` and the three probabilities. Numerical evaluations of inverse trigonometric functions appear only after the exact enumeration, for presentation.

## Relationship to prior work

The standard geometry of the regular dodecahedron and its symmetric central hexagonal and decagonal sections is described in the MathWorld reference, citing Holden. French and Jordan discuss fixed deterministic dodecahedral slices and volume dissections, not Haar-uniform orientation-dependent section type. Glisse and Lazard investigate expected silhouette complexity for classes of approximating polyhedra, not the fixed regular-dodecahedron central-section polygon law. Frederick's 2023 great-circle analysis explicitly describes the related arrangement as ten icosahedral face-axis circles with sixty triangles, twelve pentagons, and twenty hexagons; that paper supplies preexisting arrangement geometry, which is credited. The distinct result here is the exact map from normal cells to section types and the closed Haar measure of each type, supported by a complete algebraic chamber certificate. Similar recent exact results for central icosahedron sections concern a different solid and a different six-circle arrangement, not this ten-circle dodecahedral law.

## Limitations

This result does not describe noncentral cuts, random projections, or nonregular dodecahedra. The spherical partition itself is substantially described by previous geometric literature, and this note does not claim the combinatorial cell counts alone as novel. Independent assessment of complete historical priority has not been performed; the full text of French and Jordan's closely related deterministic-section article could not be accessed in this comparison. No computer-generated diagram is needed for the proof; the finite exact enumeration is provided as a standalone, auditable artifact.
