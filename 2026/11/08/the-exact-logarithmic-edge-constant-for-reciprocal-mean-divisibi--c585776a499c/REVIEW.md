# Scientific review

## Correctness
The gcd parameterization yields an exact identity, then the floor-error count is \(O_m(X)\). The congruence-restricted primitive harmonic sum is evaluated by Möbius inversion and a uniform arithmetic-progression Riemann-sum bound. The local Euler factors give the stated coefficient without numerical interpolation. The two exact computational implementations agree on the reported ranges. The theorem concerns fixed parameters; it makes no claim of parameter-uniformity.

## Originality
Korsky establishes the relevant harmonic-triple setup and an \(O(X\log X)\) counting estimate used in a different construction. Sawin proves positive-density independent sets for the corresponding edge relation. Neither inspected text states the exact unrestricted edge-count constant or the full fixed-\(m\) Euler-product formula. Fan and Tóth study fixed values of a normalized gcd, which do not by themselves determine the edge condition. OEIS A362931 explicitly tabulates the integral harmonic-mean pair counts and its exact finite sum \(X+2E_2(X)\); OEIS A055081 counts integral-harmonic-mean partners. These earlier tables and the shared coprime parameterization are acknowledged as known, not presented as novel. Search-based priority remains provisional because historical enumerations may not be indexed or readily accessible.

## Mathematical value
The edge count is the natural first-moment statistic of two arithmetic graphs in the Erdős–Graham problem. The exact coefficient describes their logarithmically diverging mean degree and quantifies, by a factor of \(4/3\), the change when reciprocal sums are replaced by reciprocal averages. The uniform fixed-parameter formula supplies a comparison benchmark for variations of the graph, without pretending to solve its extremal independent-set problem.

## Closest sources and limitations
See Korsky, arXiv:2607.05823, sections 2–3; Sawin, arXiv:2607.15419, Theorem 1 and Lemma 2; OEIS A362931 and A055081; and the older harmonic-triple bibliography in MathWorld. The result is not uniform for growing \(m\), does not compute independence numbers, and may have earlier unindexed equivalents.
