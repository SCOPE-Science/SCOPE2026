# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The scope is precisely the rim-only wheel-distance isometries, which equal induced-subgraph partial automorphisms of the rim cycle. For every proper domain, its induced connected components are nonempty paths. Counting cyclic missing-vertex gap compositions gives the exact number of domain subsets of each path multiset; counting component permutations and flips gives its isomorphism multiplicity. A multinomial coefficient extraction establishes the stated full rank spectrum. Separate empty/full cases cover the boundaries. The factorial growth inequalities establish both logarithmic limits with no finite extrapolation. The included verifier checks exact rational coefficients, independently enumerated graph maps, and ambient dihedral restrictions.

## Originality — PASS relative to the inspected sources
Fernandes (arXiv:2307.16257) already studies the same hub-avoiding monoid and its path-run/Green-class structure, monoid generators, and generating-set rank. That prior structure is not presented as new. It does not provide the exact count by element rank or the asymptotic half-factorial cardinality/extension-rate statement. Jajcay et al. (arXiv:1809.04422) treat general partial graph automorphism monoids without the checked cycle rank formula. Dimitrova et al. (arXiv:2101.12468) enumerate partial automorphisms of paths, not cyclic rim subsets. Fernandes–Paulista (arXiv:2205.02196) count the different, dihedral-restriction monoid for the *intrinsic cycle distance*. No identified statement in these checked works implies the present count, because preserving wheel distance on the rim is strictly weaker than preserving intrinsic cycle geodesic distance in general.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The element-cardinality distribution is a basic algebraic invariant distinct from minimal generator rank. For this already studied inverse monoid, the cyclic component method computes every rank coefficient and exposes a substantial abundance of nonextendible partial symmetries. The logarithmic extension law distinguishes two natural metrics on the same rim and gives a quantitative rigidity comparison across all large wheel sizes.

## Limitations and risks
Publication priority may overlap with unlocated specialized enumerations of partial automorphisms of cycles. The logarithmic growth statements do not provide a multiplicative constant or a local limit theorem. The computation only checks finite examples; all-dimensional correctness depends on the path-run argument. The claim concerns the hub-avoiding monoid with its own rim identity, not the full wheel monoid.
