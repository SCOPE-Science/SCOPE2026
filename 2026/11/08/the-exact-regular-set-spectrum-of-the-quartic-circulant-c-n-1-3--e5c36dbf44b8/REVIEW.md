# Review of the exact regular-set spectrum theorem

## Correctness

**PASS.** The graph is connected and four-regular for all allowed orders. Double-counting forces the claimed density. The exact cyclotomic-divisibility calculation restricts the nonconstant Fourier frequencies to orders \(2\), \(5,6\), \(4,8\), or \(3,10\). The binary additive-separability lemma eliminates mixed periods, and integrality of the density eliminates the unwanted period in the coprime-frequency cases. Direct residue checks prove sufficiency and yield the binomial counts. The exceptional \(4\)-periodic boundary is handled without conflating it with the \(8\)-periodic count. Exhaustive direct-neighbour tests for orders \(7\) through \(18\) agree with the formula.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical priority.** The September 2026 work of Abdollahi and collaborators already gives existence and nonexistence for all parameter pairs on general quartic circulants, and the efficient-code special cases precede it; the same 2026 article also proves the uniqueness of the bipartition in the \((0,4)\) case. The theorem here claims neither existence priority nor a new spectral method. It supplies the complete family of *all* labelled solutions in the fixed two-step graph for every parameter, their exact multiplicities and a sharp uniform total of \(106\). Comparisons with the relevant 2026 full texts did not disclose those counts. The 2020 efficient-total-domination paper was accessible only via abstract/secondary quotation and remains a special-case overlap risk. Broader historical priority cannot be proven by database searching alone.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The problem is a natural enumeration refinement of a recently completed existence classification, not an arbitrarily fixed small-order computation. The period-rigidity theorem compresses an exponential family of binary feasibility problems to the periods \(2,3,5,8\), and yields a uniform bound independent of order. The exact multiplicities separate \(4\)- and \(8\)-periodic cases, and reveal when the maximum \(106\) is attained.

## Scope and limitations

The result concerns only \(C_n(1,3)\), \(n\ge7\), and labelled subsets. It does not count automorphism orbits or treat other quartic generator sets. The proof is unrestricted; the finite verifier corroborates it, but is not a substitute. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
