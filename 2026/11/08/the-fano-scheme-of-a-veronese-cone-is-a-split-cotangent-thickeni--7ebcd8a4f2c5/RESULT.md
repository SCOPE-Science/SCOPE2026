# The Fano scheme of a Veronese cone is a split cotangent thickening

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. Let \(V=\nu_2(\mathbb P^2_k)\subset\mathbb P^5_k\) be the quadratic Veronese surface, and let \(X\subset\mathbb P^6_k\) be its projective cone with vertex \(v\). Let \(F=F_1(X)\) be the **complete**, rather than reduced, Grassmannian Fano scheme of lines. There is an isomorphism of schemes
\[
F\ \cong\ \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_{\mathbb P^2_k}
\left(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}\oplus\Omega^1_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\right),
\]
where multiplication on the second summand is zero. In particular, \(F_{\mathrm{red}}\cong\mathbb P^2\); its nilradical \(\mathcal N\) satisfies \(\mathcal N^2=0\) and \(\mathcal N\cong\Omega^1_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\). Every geometric local transverse Artin algebra is \(k[A,B]/(A^2,AB,B^2)\), of length three. The Plücker Hilbert polynomial is
\[
P_F(n)=6n^2+11n+4,
\]
and \(\dim_k H^0(F,\mathcal O_F)=4\). The nontrivial twisting of \(\Omega^1(2)\) shows that this is **not** the product of \(\mathbb P^2\) with the length-three square-zero fat point, despite having that local form.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is algebraically closed, without characteristic restrictions. The ambient linear embedding is specified: \(X\) is the projective cone in \(\mathbb P^6\) over the standard quadratic Veronese image in \(\mathbb P^5\). Its ideal consists of the nine \(2\times2\) minors of the symmetric \(3\times3\) matrix \((X_{ij})_{0\le i,j\le2}\), in the six symmetric coordinates, and contains no equation involving the vertex coordinate \(W\). The line scheme is the closed Grassmannian subscheme defined by coefficientwise restriction of these quadrics to the universal line. A Plücker twist means the restriction of \(\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Gr}(2,7)}(1)\).

## Proof
**1. Underlying lines and the global retraction.** The Veronese surface contains no projective lines: a line of rank-one symmetric matrices would yield a pencil of proportional rank-one tensors, hence be constant projectively. If a line in \(X\) misses \(v\), linear projection away from \(v\) gives a line contained in \(V\), impossible. Thus every geometric line in \(X\) is the ruling \(\overline{vp}\) for a unique \(p\in V\). Scheme-theoretically, the universal line intersects the fixed hyperplane \(W=0\) in a relative Cartier section on the open charts around each such ruling; because the ruling charts cover the support of the Fano scheme, this constructs a morphism \(\rho:F\to V\cong\mathbb P^2\). The ruling construction gives a section \(\sigma:V\to F\) with \(\rho\circ\sigma=\operatorname{id}\).

**2. All defining equations in one Grassmannian chart.** On the chart where the intersection point has \(X_{00}=1\), use affine Veronese coordinates \((t,s)\). Fix the spanning vectors of the universal line so that its point at infinity is \((W;X_{ij})=(0;p)\), with
\[
p=\begin{pmatrix}1&t&s\\t&t^2&ts\\s&ts&s^2\end{pmatrix},
\]
and its other vector is \((1;b)\), with \(b_{00}=0\). Initially write \(b_{01}=A\), \(b_{02}=B\), \(b_{11}=D\), \(b_{12}=E\), \(b_{22}=G\). The coefficient of the affine line parameter in the three minors
\(X_{00}X_{11}-X_{01}^2\), \(X_{00}X_{12}-X_{01}X_{02}\), \(X_{00}X_{22}-X_{02}^2\) gives, with no division by small integers,
\[
D=2tA,\qquad E=sA+tB,\qquad G=2sB.
\]
Every other linearized minor vanishes after these substitutions. The coefficients of the **square** of the affine line parameter in these same three minors are exactly \(-A^2\), \(-AB\), and \(-B^2\). Every remaining quadratic coefficient lies in their ideal (substitute the displayed expressions and expand the other six minors). Therefore the **complete scheme-theoretic** chart ring is
\[
\mathcal O_F(\rho^{-1}(X_{00}\ne0))
\cong k[t,s,A,B]/(A^2,AB,B^2).
\]
No infinitesimal directions or embedded equations have been discarded. This calculation works in characteristics two and three as written.

**3. Gluing the nilradical.** On the overlap \(t\ne0\), use the chart with \(X_{11}=1\). Its reduced coordinates are \(u=1/t\), \(v=s/t\). To normalize the vertex-side vector so that its \(X_{11}\)-entry is zero, subtract \((b_{11}/p_{11})p\) from \(b\). The corresponding two nilpotent coordinate functions become
\[
A'=-A,\qquad B'=tB-sA.
\]
The same formula holds for the change between any two of the three symmetric affine Veronese charts by permutation of coordinates. For \(\Omega^1_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\), in the corresponding local frames of \(\mathcal O(2)\), the differential transformation is
\[
t^2\,d(1/t)=-dt,\qquad t^2\,d(s/t)=-s\,dt+t\,ds.
\]
Thus the transition matrices for the ideal generated by \(A,B\) are **exactly** the transition matrices of \(\Omega^1_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\). Since all products of these local generators vanish, \(\mathcal N\cong\Omega^1(2)\) and \(\mathcal N^2=0\). The global section \(\sigma\) from Step 1 splits the structure morphism at the level of rings, giving the asserted split square-zero algebra.

**4. Plücker invariants.** On the reduced ruling plane, the Plücker coordinates are \(v\wedge\nu_2([x:y:z])\), so \(\mathcal O_F(1)|_{F_{\mathrm{red}}}\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\). From the exact square-zero filtration, for each integer \(n\),
\[
\chi(F,\mathcal O_F(n))
=\chi(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(2n))
+\chi(\mathbb P^2,\Omega^1(2n+2)).
\]
Euler's exact sequence \(0\to\Omega^1(m)\to\mathcal O(m-1)^{\oplus3}\to\mathcal O(m)\to0\), with \(\chi(\mathcal O(m))=(m+1)(m+2)/2\), yields the polynomial \(6n^2+11n+4\). At \(n=0\), the splitting of the square-zero algebra and the Euler sequence give \(h^0(\mathcal O_F)=1+h^0(\Omega^1(2))=1+3=4\). Finally \(c_1(\Omega^1(2))=H\) and \(c_2(\Omega^1(2))=H^2\), so the nilradical is not the trivial rank-two bundle. This proves non-productness globally.

## Verification
Run `python verify_veronese_fano.py` with SymPy installed. The script expands all nine minors on the universal line, verifies that every coefficient vanishes modulo the three stated square-zero relations, verifies that these relations occur among the coefficients, checks the overlap transition, and checks the Hilbert-polynomial identity. It also repeats the coefficient checks modulo characteristics \(2,3,5,7\) and over characteristic zero. The recorded successful output appears in `verification_output.txt`. These are finite symbolic consistency checks; the global scheme isomorphism and all-characteristic result follow from the preceding universal proof, not extrapolation from finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Ilten, *Fano Schemes of Lines on Toric Surfaces* (arXiv:1411.3025v1, 11 November 2014), computes scheme structures on **toric surfaces**, notably nonreduced line families on cone surfaces; it does not address the present three-dimensional cone or its rank-two cotangent thickening. Ilten and Zotine, *On Fano Schemes of Toric Varieties* (arXiv:1605.05745v1, 18 May 2016), classify the reduced components of toric Fano schemes generally, and calculate complete scheme structures in Section 9 specifically when the plane dimension equals the variety dimension minus one. Here the variety has dimension three and the lines have dimension one, outside that nonreduced-structure theorem; their Remark 9.9 explicitly describes the remaining range as challenging. The present computation supplements, rather than replaces, that work with a complete globally glued scheme in this first non-divisorial case. An older paper by Ballico and Cossidente titled *Cones over Veronese varieties and Grassmannians* (Results in Mathematics 38 (2000), 209–212) directly mentions the objects; its full text was not available in this examination, and equivalent coverage cannot be excluded solely from its title.

## Limitations
This result concerns only the quadratic Veronese plane and its specified projective cone, not arbitrary higher Veronese embeddings, Grassmannian cones or arbitrary toric threefolds. The scheme is split as a square-zero extension by the explicit ruling section, but it is not a globally trivial product with a fat point. The symbolic tests do not constitute an independent mathematical audit. The 2000 Ballico–Cossidente paper and any unindexed older sources leave a historical-priority risk; the result is claimed only under the documented overlap comparisons.
