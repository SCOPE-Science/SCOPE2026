For a finite Gaussian Monte Carlo average of linear-plus-quadratic normal responses, the unique variance-optimal coupling is equicorrelated, and full antithetics lose to partial negative correlation below a sharp signal-to-quadratic-noise threshold. Applied to Gaussian randomized cross-validation with scalar linear shrinkage, this gives a unique finite-perturbation phase boundary; at two folds and perturbation \(\alpha=4\), the optimal correlation is \(-9/64\), not the zero-sum value \(-1\).
