# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The fixed-point orbit-barycenter model is an exact simplicial homeomorphism: eligible transposition orbits are cliques, and orbit unions span simplices exactly when the original vertices form a clique. The proof reconstructs the complete finite range by a unique recursive partition enumeration and all unit transfers. An independent enumeration of invariant original cliques matches the orbit-model faces in all eighteen cases. Four verified elementary collapses at \(n=18\) leave a graph with two components and one cycle. All smaller cases collapse to forests; the finite universal quantifier is exhaustive, not an extrapolation. The executable and recorded output are included.

The closest original nonequivariant paper, arXiv:2603.14370 (sections 1–3, 7–8), classifies \(K_n\) up to ordinary homotopy and tabulates Euler characteristics, but it does not determine geometric conjugation-fixed components. The closely related axial study arXiv:2603.22546 (sections 1–3, 5–6, 8) examines the self-conjugate graph vertices, their neighborhoods and a thin spine, not the fixed-point homotopy of \(|K_n|\). Its statements do not imply the first-circle threshold. The local study arXiv:2603.18696 analyzes individual neighborhoods. Actual searches for the geometric fixed-space, first-loop cutoff, conjugation, and equivalences in mathematical research records and primary literature found no covering result. An unindexed or differently phrased fixed-complex calculation remains possible; a negative search is not a proof of global novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Geometric fixed points of a simplicial involution include barycenters of exchanged adjacent vertices, so they differ markedly from the self-conjugate vertex axis that motivated the 2026 axial study. The first appearance of an essential circle is a natural sharp structural cutoff in a canonical family and determines the earliest nontrivial fixed-component fundamental group. The calculation is an exact integral homotopy fact, not a further instance of the already published nonequivariant sphere-wedge formula or an arbitrary coefficient-field table. The entire cutoff through eighteen is reproducibly certified.
