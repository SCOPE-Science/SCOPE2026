# The first cut-sensitive optimal subsequence-universality spectrum of binary de Bruijn cycles

## Finding
A cyclic binary de Bruijn sequence of order \(m\) is a cyclic word \(s\) of length \(2^m\) in which each binary word of length \(m\) occurs exactly once cyclically. For every cut \(j\), let \(w_j\) be the linear word obtained by reading \(2^m+m-1\) consecutive symbols from the periodic extension of \(s\), beginning at \(j\). Let \(u(w)\) be the largest \(k\) for which **every** binary word of length \(k\) occurs as a scattered subsequence of \(w\), and put \(U_-(s)=\min_j u(w_j)\) and \(U_+(s)=\max_j u(w_j)\).

The complete distributions of \((U_-,U_+)\) over de Bruijn cycles modulo rotation, for orders \(m=2,3,4,5\), are:

| Order | \((U_-,U_+)\) | Number of cycles |
|---|---|---:|
| \(2\) | \((2,2)\) | 1 |
| \(3\) | \((3,4)\) | 2 |
| \(4\) | \((5,7)\) | 8 |
| \(4\) | \((6,7)\) | 8 |
| \(5\) | \((10,12)\) | 512 |
| \(5\) | \((11,13)\) | 1536 |

Thus **order five is the first binary de Bruijn order for which the best possible cut changes the subsequence-universality index between different cycles**. The shortest absent scattered subsequence of a best-cut length-\(36\) linearization has length \(13\) for 512 cycles and \(14\) for 1536 cycles.

The order-five counts further split under the group generated by reversal and bit-complementation, after identifying rotations: in the \((10,12)\) class there are 16 orbits of size two and 120 of size four; in the \((11,13)\) class there are 16 orbits of size two and 376 of size four. Thus there are 528 symmetry classes in total. Canonical representatives of the two order-five classes are respectively `00000100011001010011101011011111` and `00000100011001010110100111011111`.

## Assumptions and scope
All words are over \(\{0,1\}\). Different cyclic de Bruijn words are identified only by rotation for the main count. Reversal and bit-complementation are additional symmetries only for the orbit refinement. A linearization at a cut reads the entire cycle and appends the first \(m-1\) symbols of the same cut, so each cut has length \(2^m+m-1\). Scattered subsequences preserve order but need not be contiguous; the empty word is a subsequence of every word. The asserted census is finite, covering all cycles at orders two through five, and makes no claim at larger orders.

## Proof
**1. Greedy arches compute \(u(w)\).** Scan a binary word from left to right and remove, repeatedly, the shortest nonempty prefix containing both symbols. Call each removed prefix a *complete arch*, and let \(a(w)\) be the number removed. Each arch contains both letters, hence any prescribed binary word of length \(a(w)\) is a subsequence: select its successive letters from successive arches. Conversely, let \(c_i\) be the last letter of arch \(i\). Since each binary arch ends at the first occurrence of the letter opposite its first letter, the earliest greedy occurrence of \(c_i\) after the previous arch ends is exactly the end of arch \(i\). After all arches, the remaining suffix omits at least one letter \(b\). Consequently \(c_1\cdots c_{a(w)}b\) is absent as a subsequence, proving \(u(w)=a(w)\). This also holds when there are no complete arches.

**2. A complete, nonredundant search space.** The binary order-\(m\) de Bruijn digraph has one vertex for each length-\(m-1\) binary word and one labeled edge for each length-\(m\) word. Eulerian circuits correspond bijectively to cyclic de Bruijn words. Since the edge labeled \(0^m\) occurs once, starting an Eulerian circuit with this edge at the all-zero vertex fixes a unique representative of each cycle. A depth-first traversal that uses each labeled edge once and returns to its starting vertex therefore enumerates *all and only* de Bruijn cycles without duplicates. The program `verify.py` carries out this complete finite search.

**3. Independent enumeration through shift-register feedback.** Every binary de Bruijn cycle of order \(m\) is also a single cycle of an invertible feedback shift register on \(m\)-bit states. Writing a state as its first bit \(a\) followed by a tail \(r\) of \(m-1\) bits, any such register has next appended bit \(a\oplus g(r)\) for a Boolean map \(g\) on tails. Indeed the two predecessors with the same tail must append different bits for the state transition to be bijective, which forces precisely that form. Conversely, if the resulting permutation of the \(2^m\) states has one orbit, its orbit spells a de Bruijn cycle. Enumerating all \(2^{2^{m-1}}\) Boolean maps \(g\) and retaining those producing a single orbit is therefore a second exhaustive enumeration, structurally different from the Eulerian search. At order five, this examines exactly 65,536 maps and retains exactly 2,048 single-cycle maps. Both enumerators produce **identical sets of canonical cycles** at every order \(2\le m\le5\).

**4. Exhaustive statistic and orbit certificate.** For every canonical cycle generated in Step 2, inspect all \(2^m\) cuts; build the specified linearization, count complete arches, and take the minimum and maximum. The resulting exact tally is the table in the Finding. Independently, a suffix dynamic program computes the shortest *absent* subsequence length directly: at a suffix start \(i\), let \(p_b\) be the earliest position at or after \(i\) containing \(b\). Then the shortest absent length is the minimum, over both bits, of \(1\) if \(p_b\) does not exist and \(1+L(p_b+1)\) otherwise. The base value at an empty suffix is one. This computation is checked against the greedy-arch index plus one at **every cut of every cycle**. For order five, normalize reversal and complement back to the unique rotation beginning \(00000\); their orbits have exactly the sizes and distributions in the Finding. The explicit 2,048-row `CENSUS.csv` is regenerated independently from the shift-register enumeration and checked byte-for-byte as parsed numerical rows. These finite exhaustive certificates prove all tabulated quantities; no extrapolation from sampled cycles is used.

## Verification
With Python 3 standard library, place `verify.py` beside `CENSUS.csv` and run `python verify.py`. The program checks the two complete enumerations, all sliding factors, every cut's independent shortest-absent-subsequence dynamic program, the order-five symmetry-orbit distribution, and the entire saved CSV. It concludes with `DEBRUIJN_SUBSEQUENCE_CENSUS_VERIFY_OK`. The relevant evidence is exactly the pair of embedded files `verify.py` and `CENSUS.csv`; no external solver, network connection, hidden data, or proprietary library is needed.

## Relationship to prior work
Gabrić and Sawada (2026) develop constant-amortized generation of necklaces and a particular de Bruijn cycle, not a distribution over every cycle of the scattered-subsequence universality index. Kosche, Koß, Manea and Siemer (2023) develop general algorithms and characterizations of absent subsequences and arch factorization, but the accessible literature did not identify the present order-five conditioned and symmetry-refined census. Known formulas counting all de Bruijn cycles and all binary words with a specified subsequence-universality index are insufficient alone to infer how that index is distributed *among de Bruijn cycles*. The classifying invariant is a whole-cycle statistic optimized over all cuts, not a prefix statistic or a property of all binary words of the same length.

## Limitations
This is a finite complete classification only through order five. It does not yield a closed formula for all orders, classify cycles by a simpler structural rule, or establish any optimality among non-de-Bruijn strings. The first-occurrence and uniqueness of the result in the entire mathematical literature have not been independently certified. The self-review is not an independent external mathematical audit.
