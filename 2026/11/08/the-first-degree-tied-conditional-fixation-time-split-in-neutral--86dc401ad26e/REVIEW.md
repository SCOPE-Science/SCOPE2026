## Correctness — PASS

The neutral inverse-degree fixation formula follows from edgewise cancellation of weighted mutant mass. The conditional first moment \(u(S)=\mathbb E_S[T\mathbf1_{\mathrm{fix}}]\) satisfies the exact absorbing-chain equation \((I-Q)u=h\), with no interchange of conditional and unconditional time. By listing the nine small connected graph classes, every equal-degree pair through four vertices is automorphic, so the threshold lower bound is structural. ## Originality — PASS (within inspected evidence)

The inverse-degree fixation law is prior art (Sood–Antal–Redner, 2008), as are general exact Markov recursions for fixation time (Hindersin–Traulsen, 2014; Hindersin and colleagues, 2015). The 2014 primary paper analyzes the six four-vertex graph classes and degree-dependent initial sites. McAvoy and Hauert (2015) already show that regular twelve-vertex Frucht networks can have equal fixation probabilities but unequal conditional fixation times; the **qualitative distinction is therefore not new**. The new claim is the sharp five-vertex minimum over all connected unweighted undirected graphs and the entire seven-of-21 order-five classification. The 2019 graph atlas compares graph-averaged fixation probabilities and mean times, not equal-degree vertices inside each graph. The 2024 initial-degree paper uses larger random networks and mean-field approximations. The 2026 mixed-updating paper addresses variable update rules and general bounds. None of these inspected materials states the sharp five-vertex, seven-class exact census. Indexed semantic searches did not surface a statement that covers the new theorem, though this is not an exhaustive historical-priority guarantee.

## Value — PASS

Reproductive value completely fixes neutral single-mutant success probability, but its sufficiency for predicting successful takeover speed is a distinct structural question. The result gives the smallest possible counterexample and the entire first graph-order layer, rather than an arbitrary numerical specimen. Seven of the 21 isomorphism classes exhibit the separation, making the phenomenon a reproducible benchmark for when local degree alone fails to describe the temporal performance of an initially successful lineage.
