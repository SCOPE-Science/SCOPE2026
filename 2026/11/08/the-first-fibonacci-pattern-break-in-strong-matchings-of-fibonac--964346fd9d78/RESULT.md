# The first Fibonacci-pattern break in strong matchings of Fibonacci cubes

## Finding
For a Fibonacci cube \(\Gamma_n\), an *induced matching* is a set of edges whose endpoints induce precisely a disjoint union of edges. Its maximum cardinality is also called the strong matching number \(\nu_s(\Gamma_n)\).

**Exact finite classification.** For dimensions \(1\le n\le8\), the maximum sizes, in increasing order, are
\[
(1,1,1,2,3,5,8,12).
\]
For dimensions \(3\le n\le7\) these equal \(F_{n-1}\) where \(F_0=0\) and \(F_1=1\). Dimension eight is the *first failure in that range* of this observed Fibonacci pattern:
\[
\nu_s(\Gamma_8)=12\neq F_7=13.
\]
Furthermore, \(\Gamma_8\) has **exactly twelve labeled** maximum induced matchings, forming **six orbits of size two** under its full graph automorphism group. The entire list of twelve matchings is in `optima.json`. This is a finite exact result; no all-dimensional recurrence or general asymptotic assertion is made.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(\Gamma_n\) is the induced subgraph of the \(n\)-dimensional Boolean hypercube with vertex set
\[
V_n=\{a_1\cdots a_n\in\{0,1\}^n: a_i a_{i+1}=0\text{ for }1\le i<n\},
\]
where two vertices are adjacent precisely when their Hamming distance is one. Its edges are undirected, and matchings are unlabeled collections of undirected edges. The dimension ranges over the eight explicitly listed positive integers; the stronger optimum-count and symmetry statements concern \(n=8\) only. Two edges are compatible precisely when each of their four inter-edge endpoint distances exceeds one, which simultaneously prohibits shared endpoints and crossedges.

## Proof
**1. Finite reduction.** For each \(n\), form a compatibility graph \(C_n\) whose vertices are the edges of \(\Gamma_n\). Distinct edges \(e=\{a,b\}\) and \(f=\{c,d\}\) are adjacent in \(C_n\) exactly if
\[
\min\{d_H(a,c),d_H(a,d),d_H(b,c),d_H(b,d)\}>1.
\]
Because the original graph is induced on binary words, Hamming distance one means adjacency and zero means a shared endpoint. Thus the cliques of \(C_n\) correspond *bijectively* to induced matchings in \(\Gamma_n\), preserving size. The algorithm `exact_search.py` constructs the whole graph from these explicit definitions.

**2. Exhaustiveness of the exact search.** The recursive procedure considers a selected clique and all candidates adjacent to every member. At each step, it gives the candidate subgraph a proper greedy vertex coloring. Every clique of remaining candidates contains at most one vertex of each color, hence the number of colors is a valid upper bound. It branches on each possible next selected candidate in reverse coloring order, deleting that candidate from the remaining pool after its branch. Its only pruning test is that the selected size plus the coloring upper bound is *strictly less than* the best size already found. It therefore neither discards a possible larger clique nor a tied optimum. At leaves it collects *every* maximum clique, sorting the edge indices canonically. This proof is finite and executable; termination follows because each branch strictly shrinks the candidate set. There are no probabilistic, timeout-based, or assumed exhaustive steps.

**3. Full automorphism group.** The all-zero word is the unique degree-eight vertex of \(\Gamma_8\): it has all eight one-bit neighbors, whereas each nonzero permitted word has at least one zero-to-one move forbidden by an already present one or at least its own one-to-zero move replaces a possible zero-to-one move; more simply, the number of neighbors of a nonzero word is strictly less than eight because at least one neighboring coordinate is blocked (and \(n=8\ge2\)). Every automorphism fixes zero, and so permutes its eight singleton-word neighbors. Two distinct singletons at positions \(i\) and \(j\) have a second common neighbor exactly when \(|i-j|>1\). Therefore their permutation preserves the complement of the eight-vertex path, whose automorphism group has order two, generated by reversal. In addition, \(\Gamma_8\) is an isometric subgraph of the eight-cube: a path of Hamming length from \(x\) to \(y\) is obtained by first deleting the 1s absent from \(y\), then inserting the remaining 1s present in \(y\). Distances to zero and to each singleton recover every bit by
\[
  x_i=\frac{d(x,0)+1-d(x,e_i)}{2}.
\]
An automorphism fixing zero and all singletons must therefore fix every vertex. Reversal is itself an automorphism, so these are exactly the two. Reversal acts freely on the twelve computed optimal edge sets, giving six two-element orbits. The orbit computation is repeated by `exact_search.py` and the full certificate records representatives.

## Verification
Run `python exact_search.py` with Python 3.10 or later. It computes every maximum clique for \(1\le n\le8\), verifies pairwise compatibility of each optimum, and prints the maximum size, maximizer count, orbit count, and visited nodes. Recorded output appears in `exact_output.txt`; in dimension eight it visits 10,283 recursive nodes. `optima.json` lists all twelve maximum matchings and six reversal-orbit representatives in bit-string form.

Run `python ilp_check.py` with NumPy and SciPy. It independently constructs the conflict relation via Hamming distances, formulates pairwise inequalities \(z_i+z_j\le1\), and requires an exact optimal termination status from the mixed-integer optimizer. Its \(1\le n\le8\) optimum sizes agree with the exhaustive clique search; output appears in `ilp_output.txt`. The MILP check corroborates the size claims but is not used to prove the count or orbit statements, which rest on the complete clique search. All computations concern finite graphs explicitly represented in the source files.

## Relationship to prior work
Gravier, Mollard, Špacapan and Zemljič (first publicly posted 2015-04-03) optimize the number \(q_k(n)\) of **vertex-disjoint** subcubes isomorphic to \(Q_k\) in \(\Gamma_n\); for \(k=1\) this is ordinary matching, without the induced requirement that there be no edges between distinct matched endpoints. Their formulas, including Lemma 2.1, Theorem 2.2, and Corollaries 2.4–2.5, do not imply this stronger packing number or its maximizer census. Saygı and Eğecioğlu (2016) give explicit formulas for the same vertex-disjoint hypercube invariant. Klavžar's 2013 survey describes the Fibonacci-cube structure and applications but not the strong-matching census. Drambyan and Petrosyan (2020) proved bounds on the *strong chromatic index* of Fibonacci cubes, and a 2025 dissertation abstract gives its exact value. The strong chromatic index is the minimum number of induced-matchings needed to partition **all** edges; it does not determine the maximum size or number of individual induced matchings. General induced matching bounds likewise do not cover this exact eight-coordinate census. 

## Limitations
The finding is a **finite exact census and first-pattern-break statement**, not a theorem determining \(\nu_s(\Gamma_n)\) for all \(n\). It does not assert that the Fibonacci rule had been conjectured in prior literature. It does not classify maximum matchings in the ordinary (non-induced) sense, hypercubes of higher dimension, Lucas cubes, or strong edge colorings. The originality search did not yield a directly overlapping result, but unavailable/unindexed literature and computational transcription risk remain possible. Independent scholarly audit has not been performed.
