# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The Fourier transform of a three-run unit-cell indicator is a common unit-cell factor times its binary polynomial. The entire magnitude is equivalent to the complete integer aperiodic autocorrelation signature. All normalized three-run supports have exactly five positive run lengths, so every possible span from 5 through 12 is covered by four-cut enumeration; the complete classes count is checked by two independent exact-integer correlation implementations and a separate bitmask enumeration. Counts are 1, 3, 9, 19, 38, 66, 110, 169 signatures at spans 5–12, while reversal-class counts are identical until span 11 and 170 at 12. Exactly one span-12 fiber has two inequivalent classes. The displayed factor reversal proves the witness without numerical approximation. Full mathematical scope is restricted to this grid model, not arbitrary real endpoints.

## Originality

**PASS relative to inspected sources, with explicit residual risk.** Xia–Xu (arXiv:2606.19855, Theorems 1.2/1.7 and Section 5) already prove existence of ambiguous three-interval indicators, but the inspected theorem statements and section text do not assert the **least integer span** or **uniqueness of the first grid ambiguity**. Their exact example formulas were not fully readable in the consulted representation, so the concrete pair might overlap; this does not determine the optimality/classification without a separate census. Katz–Rahman–Ward (arXiv:2308.07467) treat general factor reversal and unimodular sequence classes, without the claimed three-run indicator census. A 2017 sparse binary phase-retrieval study provides examples and generic algorithms, not this exact finite restriction. Relevant public mathematical research records address different cyclic, signed-atom, or higher-order spectral questions. Unindexed earlier exact enumerations remain possible. A complete independent literature audit is not claimed.

## Value

**PASS.** Three intervals are the smallest component class for which general Fourier-magnitude nonuniqueness occurs, and integer-cell apertures form a standard discrete geometric imaging model. The precise first span is a natural finite invariant; the complete first ambiguity fiber separates recoverable small apertures from the first provably indistinguishable binary designs. The result is neither a re-labeled generic factor-flip example nor an arbitrary single large-size census: it determines a sharp boundary and every extremizer under a motivated small-complexity restriction.

## Limitations

Only exact full-frequency magnitudes and three-block binary indicator apertures with integer endpoints are covered. The exhaustive lower bound is finite and checker-assisted; no assertion is made about all real-endpoint geometries, larger grid spans, other support alphabets, or noise stability. Section-5 formulas of a directly relevant preprint were not fully accessible, leaving an example-level originality risk.
