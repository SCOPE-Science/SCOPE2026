# Scientific review

## Correctness
The 24-edge graph model is explicit. The verifier exhaustively generates the face poset and 55,280 incidences, checks an acyclic 5,673-pair discrete-Morse matching, and independently reduces the original mod-two simplicial boundaries. The signed integral Morse matrices have sizes 2-by-23 and 23-by-23, square to zero, and have only unit nonzero Smith factors (two and nineteen). The first has a unimodular 2-by-2 minor. Cellular cancellation in the resulting highly connected CW model gives the exact integral wedge of two four-spheres and four five-spheres. A separate exact verifier checks all earlier even graph sizes from four through fourteen vertices.

## Originality
The closest primary source, Agarwal–Basak (arXiv:2608.30601, initially public 2026-08-31), determines the **perfect** matching and vertex-independence complexes. Its perfect-matching theorem for this graph concerns a strict subcomplex homotopy equivalent to two three-spheres, not the ordinary all-matchings complex. Published Wagner-graph computations have only eight vertices; the sixteen-vertex Möbius–Kantor graph has different edges and graph invariant. Targeted mathematical-literature and published-record searches did not identify a stronger covering statement. The 2026-09-14 revision was accessible in abstract, while the initial paper's full HTML was inspected. Unindexed and inaccessible stronger work remains a genuine risk, not a proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
The first occurrence of two adjacent nonzero reduced homology degrees is a natural topology change within the Möbius-ladder family, not an arbitrary numerical table entry. The computation supplies an exact integral homotopy type for the sixteen-vertex test case with a nonpure Morse CW reduction. It distinguishes ordinary matching topology from previously classified perfect-matching topology and provides a reproducible boundary case for any proposed periodic general classification.

## Limitations
The classification covers this finite graph and the finite first-occurrence check only. No claim about all larger Möbius ladders, symmetry modules, or independent validation is made.
