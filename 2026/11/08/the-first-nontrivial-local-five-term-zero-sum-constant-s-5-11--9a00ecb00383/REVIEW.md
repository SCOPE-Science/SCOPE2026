The claim is equivalent to a finite, exhaustive rank classification of residue-count profiles. The field-lifting equivalence is derived explicitly from residues \(r_i+5y_i\) modulo \(25\). Both independent pure-integer implementations enumerate all \(286\) ten-element profiles and all relevant five-subset constraints; the twelve surviving profile list has been rederived and checked. The proof then excludes an eleven-term obstruction by deletion and elementary count cases. The ten-entry explicit obstruction is checked both analytically and by enumeration. The field eliminations can be replayed from the embedded files; untested higher primes are not claimed.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The origin paper gives a conjecture and general bound of thirteen for \(p=5\), whereas the exact upper bound eleven is the new claim. Sun's one-dimensional result gives the lower bound and conjectures equality. The inspected 2026 follow-up proves higher-rank and variable-length assertions, not this fixed-five-term upper bound. Another related local-zero-sum theorem allows at most five terms, not exactly five, so it cannot imply the fixed-five-term result. Relevant mathematical-record searches found the known exact composite case \(s_1(4)=9\), which cannot cover prime modulus five. The originating journal full text could not be accessed, and nonindexed independent derivations remain possible: priority is therefore qualified, not certified globally.

This is a natural exact invariant explicitly conjectured in recent number-theory literature; \(p=5\) is the first prime not determined by the already known \(3p-2\) bound. A complete sharp instance and a structural twelve-profile obstruction census give an actual answer to the motivating question, not a small routine numerical perturbation. No general all-prime resolution is claimed.
