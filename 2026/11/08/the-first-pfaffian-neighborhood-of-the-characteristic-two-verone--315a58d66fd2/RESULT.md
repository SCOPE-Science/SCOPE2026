# The first Pfaffian neighborhood of the characteristic-two Veronese dual

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), and let \(m\ge 2\). In the projective space of quadratic forms on \(k^{2m+1}\), put
\[
Q(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{2m} a_i x_i^2+\sum_{0\le i<j\le 2m}b_{ij}x_ix_j.
\]
Let \(B\) be the \((2m+1)\)-square alternating matrix with off-diagonal entries \(b_{ij}\) and diagonal entries zero. Write \(w_i=\operatorname{Pf}(B_{\widehat i})\), where the hat means that row and column \(i\) are omitted (all Pfaffian signs agree in characteristic two), and put \(I=(w_0,\ldots,w_{2m})\). Then the reduced dual of the quadratic Veronese variety \(v_2(\mathbb P^{2m})\) is the irreducible hypersurface
\[
D=V(F),\qquad F=Q(w),\qquad \deg F=2m+1.
\]
Its **entire projective Jacobian singular scheme**, not merely its support, is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathrm{Jac}}(D)=V(I^2).}
\]
Equivalently, in the universal polynomial ring,
\[
\boxed{\left(F,\frac{\partial F}{\partial a_i},\frac{\partial F}{\partial b_{ij}}\right)=(w_iw_j:0\le i\le j\le 2m).}
\]
The reduced support is the locus of alternating polar matrices of corank at least three. At every point of its dense corank-three stratum, the scheme is locally the first infinitesimal neighborhood of a smooth codimension-three center. Its transverse Artin ring has Hilbert function \((1,3)\) and length \(4\).

## Assumptions and scope

The field \(k\) is algebraically closed of characteristic exactly \(2\), and \(m\) is any integer at least \(2\). The ambient space \(\mathbb P^{N}\) has \(N+1=(2m+1)(m+1)\) homogeneous coefficient coordinates \(a_i,b_{ij}\). The singular scheme means the projective scheme defined by the hypersurface equation together with **all** its homogeneous coefficient partial derivatives. The Pfaffian ideal \(I\) is computed from the universal alternating matrix, not from a specialized numerical matrix. The result concerns the quadratic Veronese dual and does not assert the same scheme structure for higher Veronese embeddings or for other characteristics.

## Proof

**(1) Pfaffian kernel identity.** For any odd-order alternating matrix, its signed maximal principal Pfaffians form a vector in the kernel. Expanding each component of the matrix-vector product by Pfaffian matchings proves that each term occurs twice with opposite signs over integers and cancels; the identity specializes over \(k\). Hence \(Bw=0\). This is a polynomial identity over the universal coefficient ring.

**(2) The reduced dual equation.** A hyperplane of the quadratic Veronese corresponds to \(Q\); it is tangent at a nonzero vector \(x\) exactly when \(Q(x)=0\) and \(Bx=0\). On the dense open locus where \(B\) has rank \(2m\), its kernel is generated by \(w\). Thus tangency there is equivalent to \(F=Q(w)=0\). Here \(w\) is nonzero, and \(F\), viewed as a polynomial in the diagonal variables \(a_i\) over the function field of the \(b_{ij}\), is a nonzero linear form. The \(w_i\) have no nonconstant common factor: their common vanishing set is the alternating rank-at-most-\(2m-2\) locus of codimension \(3\), by the alternating normal form. Thus \(F\) is primitive in \(k[b][a]\), hence irreducible and reduced by Gauss's lemma. The rank-deficient complement in coefficient space has codimension \(3\), so it cannot contribute another hypersurface component. The dual is exactly \(V(F)\). Every \(w_i\) has degree \(m\) in \(b\), so \(F\) has degree \(2m+1\).

**(3) The exact Jacobian ideal.** In the polynomial ring of coefficients, apply the chain rule to \(F=Q(w)\):
\[
dF=\sum_i w_i^2\,da_i+\sum_{i<j}w_iw_j\,db_{ij}+\sum_i(Bw)_i\,dw_i.
\]
The last term vanishes identically by \(Bw=0\). Therefore
\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial a_i}=w_i^2,\qquad
\frac{\partial F}{\partial b_{ij}}=w_iw_j.
\]
These are **all** generators of \(I^2\). Also \(F=Q(w)\in I^2\), so adjoining the equation \(F\) does not change the ideal. No saturation, radicalization, genericity condition, or finite-field extrapolation is used in this equality.

**(4) The reduced support and transverse length.** Since the partial derivatives include all squares \(w_i^2\), their common geometric zeros are exactly \(w_i=0\) for every \(i\), equivalently \(\operatorname{rank}B\le2m-2\). The rank-\(2m-2\) stratum is dense in this irreducible rank-degeneracy locus. Localize where an alternating minor of size \(2m-2\) is invertible. Splitting off that nonsingular alternating block and taking its Schur complement makes the remaining \(3\)-square alternating block
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&u&v\\u&0&s\\v&s&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The maximal Pfaffian ideal is locally \((u,v,s)\), up to an invertible change of its generators. Hence the Jacobian scheme has local ideal \((u,v,s)^2\). Transversely, the quotient has basis \(1,u,v,s\) and therefore length \(4\). The rank-locus codimension \(\binom 32=3\) is independent of \(m\).

## Verification

The accompanying standalone Python verifier implements exact sparse polynomial arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_2\), constructs all principal Pfaffians, checks the universal matrix-kernel relations, constructs \(F\), and differentiates it with respect to **every** coefficient. It checks the two derivative identities for odd matrix orders \(3,5,7\). Its actual output is included separately. These finite-dimensional checks are corroboration only: the proof above uses the universal Pfaffian identity and works for every integer \(m\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work

The characteristic-two distinction for projective duals and alternating Hessians is classical. Ballico (1990) studies exceptional conormal behavior in characteristic two. Shimada (2006), especially Sections 5–6 and Proposition 6.12, studies critical and degeneracy schemes of the conormal projection for even-dimensional varieties under ampleness/jet hypotheses. The quadratic Veronese embedding does **not** satisfy the higher jet-surjectivity conditions driving Shimada's detailed local normal forms. Kaji (2015), Theorem 0.1, establishes generic finiteness of higher Gauss maps in arbitrary characteristic but gives degree formulas there under characteristic-zero assumptions. These results motivate the setting but do not state the coefficient-space Jacobian equality \(I_{\mathrm{Jac}}=I^2\) or the uniform transverse length \(4\). The half-discriminant polynomial and Pfaffian kernel are classical inputs, not independently claimed as new.

## Limitations

The theorem is only for quadratic Veronese varieties of even projective dimension at least four in characteristic two. The length-four assertion is a **generic transverse** statement on the polar corank-three stratum, not a length claim for every deeper-rank point, nor the multiplicity of the dual hypersurface. No assertion is made about absence of embedded associated primes of \(I^2\) in deeper rank strata. The exact polynomial identity is proved universally, while numerical verification covers only odd matrix orders three, five and seven. Specialized older quadratic-form or Pfaffian literature could contain an equivalent presentation; historical priority requires independent examination. The 1990 Ballico paper is earlier year-only bibliographic evidence, not an invented precise first-public day.
