# Review: Isotropic constants of demihypercubes

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is quantified over every integer dimension \(d\ge3\), in the full-dimensional uniform-interior setting. The known forbidden-set description (Ermel–Walter, equation (2)) makes each excluded region exactly a unit right simplex around an odd-weight cube corner. Disjointness follows from odd–odd Hamming distance at least two. Exact integration and even-coordinate-complement symmetries establish scalar covariance and the isotropic formula with the standard volume normalization. The unique dimension-five minimum is proved for every dimension, not extrapolated from experiments: exact rational inequalities separate dimensions 3–6, and a monotone covariance-deficit inequality combined with the dimension-seven barrier treats all dimensions at least seven. The comparison with the cube and factorial asymptotic follow algebraically. The package checker independently replays finite exact arithmetic and low-dimensional convex-hull volumes. Remaining scientific limits: no assertion for dimension two or non-uniform distributions.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

The closest full-text paper on this geometric family is Borodachov (arXiv:2301.06623v1, Section 3), which studies demihypercube vertices as a spherical code and universal potential minima. It is not a calculation of the uniform *interior* covariance, normalized isotropic constant, or sharp dimension-five minimizer. Ermel–Walter (arXiv:1803.10561v2) provides the underlying forbidden-set facet system, not the metric identity. Kipp (arXiv:2407.01353, introductory definitions and sections on local maximizers and zonotopes) supplies the isotropic-constant normalization and benchmarks, but no demihypercube family formula. A published mathematical record on type-\(D\) spin-weight polytopes concerns John/Löwner ellipsoid contacts of the same parity-orbit body, a different invariant; its full text was inaccessible, so an unnoticed metric extension in that text remains a risk. Published Hanner-polytope isotropic bounds include the dimension-four cross-polytope instance but not the full demihypercube family. Multiple searches for demicube/halfcube, parity polytope, type-\(D\) orbit, covariance, and isotropic constant did not identify the all-dimensional result or its sharp minimum. This is best-knowledge originality, not a guarantee of historical priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The affine-invariant isotropic constant is a canonical high-dimensional convex-geometric quantity, and the demihypercubes are a standard infinite family appearing both as parity-code polytopes and type-\(D\) weight polytopes. The exact formula reveals a nonmonotone dimension profile with a *unique* minimum at five; it rigorously separates the exceptional tetrahedron above the cube benchmark from all higher dimensions below the benchmark. The factorial gap quantifies a significant structural effect: deleting half the cube vertices removes only factorially small interior volume and perturbs normalized isotropy by a precisely determined amount. The dimension-five extremizer and limit are not generic textbook consequences of volume alone; a correct dissection, interior covariance integration, and a global dimensional comparison are needed. This is a natural complete classification for a named family, not a selected numerical instance.

## Closest literature and limitations

The known four-dimensional member is a cross-polytope and its isotropic constant can already be recovered from standard formulas; the claim explicitly treats that as covered prior knowledge. The high-dimensional result concerns only the uniform interior distribution and dimension-dependent demihypercube family. Full historical priority remains provisional, particularly for less searchable coding-theoretic, Coxeter-theoretic, or convex-body sources. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
