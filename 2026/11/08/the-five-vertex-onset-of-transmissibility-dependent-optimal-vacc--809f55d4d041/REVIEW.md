# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The statement quantifies over every simple connected graph of orders 2–5 and every independent bond probability in the open unit interval. The component-square expectation identity follows by conditioning on the open-edge components and the uniform seed. The exact integer coefficient construction enumerates every edge subset for every deletion, with at most six post-deletion edges. A rational projective change of variables turns every required cost difference on each quarter-interval into an integer polynomial with nonnegative coefficients, proving continuous-parameter inequalities rather than relying on sampled probabilities. All 771 connected labeled graphs are included, and the executable replay verifies the 90 labeled exceptional copies, their two canonical classes and exact factors. Endpoint comparisons are explicitly separated.

## Originality

PASS within inspected evidence; historical priority remains provisional. Newman (2002) gives the basic bond-percolation epidemic correspondence and large-network averages, not this optimal-deletion finite classification. Masuda (2009) studies immunization for modular networks using spectral and fragmentation objectives. Holme (2017) is especially close in finite graph size and vaccination motivation, but its continuous-time Markov SIR uses a common exponential infectious period and has correlated transmissions from one infective; its exact expressions do not imply this independent-edge threshold classification. Searches for alternative descriptions (bond reliability, node deletion, expected component size, optimal immunization and parameter switching) revealed no equivalent table or statement, but searched sources do not exhaust the historical literature. The alternative epidemic mechanism and unknown unindexed tables remain the principal priority risks.

## Value

PASS. The motivating practical mathematical question is whether the identity of the most protective node changes when an epidemic's edge transmissibility changes. The natural smallest-order classification is sharp, not an arbitrary fixed graph parameter: smaller connected networks never switch, precisely two five-vertex types do, and both have the same exact crossover. These finite structural distinctions provide clean counterexamples to fixed centrality rankings and a benchmark for model-sensitive vaccination methods. No general-network policy or empirical significance is asserted.

## Closest work and limitations

Newman (2002): percolation connection, not the finite graph optimizer classification. Masuda (2009): modular immunization heuristics and large-scale fragmentation, not the stated finite objective. Holme (2017): exact finite graphs and vaccination, but a mathematically different continuous-time epidemic law. Morone and Makse (2015): optimal percolation for influence over large networks, not this exact uniform-seed outbreak-size objective. Unexamined pre-2016 small-graph reliability tables and algorithmic percolation papers could contain equivalent computations. The theorem only covers one vaccination, independent bond transmissibility and up to five vertices.
