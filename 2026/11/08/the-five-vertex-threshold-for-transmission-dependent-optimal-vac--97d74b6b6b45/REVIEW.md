# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The open-edge component-size identity and independent ordered-pair-connectivity representation agree exactly. All connected unlabeled graphs of orders two to five are obtained by complete mask enumeration and permutation canonicalization (1, 2, 6, 21 classes). For 28 classes, 127 integer Bernstein-type coefficient certificates prove that one vertex minimizes mean final outbreak size throughout the open transmissibility range. The remaining two classes have fully factored differences, giving the unique crossing \(p=1/4\), exact optimizer sets, and strict domination over other vertices. The provided standalone checker uses integer arithmetic and validates all cases rather than sampling transmission parameters.

## Originality (PASS; priority risk remains)
The 2023 Chatterjee–Zehmakan source covers vaccination heuristics and computational hardness for a different synchronous epidemic process, not the exact one-dose bond-percolation classification. Morone and colleagues (2016) study large-network optimal-percolation algorithms and threshold behavior, not this finite uniform-seed risk spectrum. published-finding corpus queries for the source, model aliases, five-vertex graph optimizers, crossover and graph-percolation equivalents returned no directly covering theorem. A 2022 empirical vaccination comparison was available only in abstract. An unindexed exact small-graph result could still establish priority.

## Value (PASS)
The first possible change of optimal vaccination location occurs already at five vertices, but on exactly two connected network topologies; strikingly, both change at the same exact probability and exchange equal-degree vertices. This supplies a precise structural counterexample to static node rankings and a complete, reproducible minimal-scale test for model-sensitive vaccination optimization. The domain is small but natural and the classification exact; no practical generalization to large or heterogeneous epidemics is asserted.

## Scientific limitations and literature comparison
The result assumes independent identical edge transmissibility, one perfect node removal, one uniformly seeded epidemic, simple connected graphs of at most five vertices, and \(0<p<1\). The motivating paper's synchronous infection/recovery/death mechanism is distinct. No larger-network classification, source-independent priority guarantee or external validation is established. Closest accessible primary sources: Chatterjee–Zehmakan, arXiv:2305.16458v1 (2023-05-25), and Morone et al., DOI:10.1038/srep30062 (2016). The abstract-only empirical comparison DOI:10.1038/s41598-022-24652-1 is an outstanding priority-inspection limitation.
