# Research review: Ewens fixed points and inversions

## Correctness — PASS
The Ewens weight of two forced arrows follows by contracting directed paths and retaining a factor of \(\theta\) for every closed cycle. Summing the weights over inverted image pairs gives the known inversion pair probability, and then \(Q_n\). Deleting a specified fixed point yields an Ewens permutation of size \(n-1\); its inversions plus the expected incident inversions give an exact conditional mean. The resulting rational identity has the stated quadratic position profile. Its \(H_n(\theta)\) factorization proves the sharp sign and equality classification; summing positions gives a strictly negative aggregate covariance for every \(n\ge3,\theta>0\). The two limits follow by rational expansion. The full mixed tensor has a separate exact conditional-probability proof. The standalone checker passed 1,672 exact assertions using exhaustive permutations for bounded sizes and exact rational biases. These experiments corroborate rather than prove the all-size theorem. An exact equality at the leading near-uniform threshold is not inferred from a zero limit.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The nearest direct result is Pinsky and Schickentanz, arXiv:2510.20654 (2025), which gives the unconditional inversion probability and expectation, and conditional inversion moments given the **number** of fixed points. Those marginal formulas are credited and are premises of the new proof. Kammoun, arXiv:1805.05253 (2018), treats Ewens/conjugation-invariant permutations and the descent process. Hamaker and Rhoades, arXiv:2206.06567, offer broad local-statistic/cycle-type moment machinery; Campion Loth et al., arXiv:2301.00898, handle weighted inversions within conjugacy classes. None of the inspected relevant statements gives the full selected-position fixed-point/inversion covariance and its sign/scale phase classification. The closest published uniform fixed-point/descent and cycle-count/inversion covariance laws involve different observables; their statements do not imply this tensor. This is a structural explicit consequence of classical deletion plus a published mean, so earlier implicit algebraic coverage remains a real historical-priority risk. Absence from bounded searches is not a uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS
An exact position- and gap-dependent dependence law answers a natural question about the interaction of a local cycle feature (being a fixed point) and a global permutation order statistic (inversion count). The theorem determines every finite-size covariance sign and equality case and exhibits a positive central window coexisting with a strictly negative total covariance. It resolves the width of this window and its near-uniform critical scaling. It is stronger than a marginal inversion expectation or an unquantified covariance statement, without requiring a general limit theorem beyond its scope.

## Limitations and closest prior results
The theorem concerns first mixed moments only; it does not assert joint independence or a full distribution. The near-uniform leading limit does not determine signs at exact threshold equality. The most direct input from Pinsky–Schickentanz is not new. General representation-theoretic and generating-function frameworks may contain implicit consequences not exposed by inspected statements. A separate external mathematical validation has not occurred.
