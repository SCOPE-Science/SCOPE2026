# The fixed-point law of exponent-four core-quandle automorphisms

## Finding

Let \(r\ge1\) be an arbitrary integer, let \(V=\mathbb F_2^r\), and set \(c=|V|=2^r\). The core (Takasaki) quandle \(Q_r\) of the abelian group \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^r\) has operation \(x*y=2y-x\). Write \(X_r(f)=|\{x\in Q_r:f(x)=x\}|\) for the number of fixed points of a quandle automorphism \(f\). When \(f\) is uniform in \(\operatorname{Aut}(Q_r)\), its entire fixed-point distribution is given by the following exact probability generating polynomial:

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[z^{X_r}]=\sum_{d=0}^{r}p_{r,d}H_{2^d}(z),\qquad H_K(z)=1-\frac1K+\frac1K\left(1-\frac1K+\frac{z^K}{K}\right)^K.}
\]

Define \(g_j=|\operatorname{GL}_j(\mathbb F_2)|\) with \(g_0=1\), and Gaussian binomial coefficients \({j\brack d}_2\). Then

\[
\boxed{p_{r,d}=\sum_{j=d}^r\frac{(-1)^{j-d}2^{\binom{j-d}{2}}{j\brack d}_2}{g_j},\quad 0\le d\le r.}
\]

Every automorphism has the unique form \((v,w)\mapsto(Tv+a,Tw+u_v)\), with \(T\in\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb F_2)\), \(a\in V\), and arbitrary \(u:V\to V\). In particular, \(|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_r)|=c^{c+1}g_r\). The count of fixed-point-free automorphisms is therefore the exact integer \(c^{c+1}g_r\sum_{d=0}^rp_{r,d}H_{2^d}(0)\).

For \(r=2\), the complete histogram is

| Fixed points | 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Automorphisms | 2769 | 2048 | 768 | 492 | 54 | 12 | 1 |

Thus exactly \(2769\) of the \(6144\) automorphisms of \(\operatorname{Core}((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2)\) have no fixed quandle element.

Furthermore, for \(P_d=\prod_{i=1}^d(1-2^{-i})\) (with \(P_0=1\)) and \(P_\infty=\prod_{i\ge1}(1-2^{-i})\), define

\[
p_{\infty,d}=\frac{P_\infty}{2^{d^2}P_d^2}.
\]

The entire discrete fixed-point law converges as \(r\to\infty\) to \(\sum_{d\ge0}p_{\infty,d}H_{2^d}(z)\). Its limiting fixed-point-free proportion is

\[
\boxed{\sum_{d\ge0}p_{\infty,d}H_{2^d}(0)=0.47225426132963767697889\ldots.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The rank \(r\) is any positive integer. All automorphisms are ordinary quandle automorphisms, not just automorphisms of the abelian group underlying the quandle. Probabilities sample automorphisms uniformly from the finite full quandle automorphism group. The parameter \(z\) is a formal variable (or a real variable in \([0,1]\) for convergence statements). The integer \(X_r\) counts fixed points of the quandle permutation action. This is not a statement about fixed points of linear maps on \(V\) alone.

## Proof

**1. Quandle coordinates and all automorphisms.** Every element of \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^r\) can be written uniquely as \(x=v+2w\), where \(v,w\in\mathbb F_2^r\) are represented coordinatewise by zero or one. Because \(-v=v+2v\) modulo four and \(2(2w)=0\), the quandle operation becomes

\[
(v,w)*(s,t)=(v,w+v+s)
\]

with additions over \(\mathbb F_2\). The right translations corresponding to \((s,t)\) are \(R_s(v,w)=(v,w+v+s)\). Their products \(R_sR_{s'}\) are exactly the uniform within-fiber translations \(\tau_h(v,w)=(v,w+h)\), for arbitrary \(h\in V\). These generate the displacement group \(D\cong(V,+)\), a characteristic subgroup of the quandle permutation group generated by the right translations. Consequently any quandle automorphism \(f\) normalizes \(D\), inducing a linear \(T\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\), and permutes its orbits \(\{v\}\times V\). Hence

\[
f(v,w)=(\sigma(v),Tw+u_v)
\]

for some permutation \(\sigma\) of \(V\) and some \(u_v\in V\). Substituting into the quandle law gives \(T(v+s)=\sigma(v)+\sigma(s)\) for every pair \(v,s\). Taking \(v=0\) shows \(\sigma(s)=Ts+a\) with \(a=\sigma(0)\). Conversely, every triple \((T,a,u)\) with \(T\) invertible defines a bijective quandle homomorphism by direct substitution. The representation is unique and yields the claimed group order. The displacement argument also works in rank one.

**2. Conditional fixed-point law.** Fix \(T\) and put \(K=|\ker(I-T)|=2^d\). The affine permutation \(v\mapsto Tv+a\) of the quotient \(V\) has no fixed points unless \(a\in\operatorname{im}(I-T)\). There are exactly \(c/K\) such choices of \(a\) out of \(c\), and in those cases there are exactly \(K\) fixed quotient fibers. Thus a uniform \(a\) produces \(K\) fixed fibers with probability \(1/K\) and none otherwise. On a fixed fiber, the equation for a fixed point is \((I-T)w=u_v\). Independent uniform choices of \(u_v\) make this soluble with probability \(1/K\); when soluble, it has exactly \(K\) solutions. The number of successful fixed fibers is therefore binomial with \(K\) trials and success probability \(1/K\). The conditional generating polynomial is precisely \(H_K(z)\). This proves the mixture law once the distribution of \(d\) is known.

**3. Distribution of the linear fixed-space dimension.** Let \(T\) be uniform in \(\operatorname{GL}_r(2)\) and \(d=\dim\ker(I-T)\). For each \(0\le j\le r\), the group \(\operatorname{GL}_r(2)\) acts transitively on ordered independent \(j\)-tuples. Burnside averaging therefore gives expectation exactly one for the number of such tuples fixed pointwise by \(T\). Each \(j\)-dimensional fixed subspace has exactly \(g_j\) ordered bases. Hence

\[
\mathbb E\left[{d\brack j}_2\right]=\frac1{g_j}.
\]

Writing \(p_{r,d}=\Pr(\dim\ker(I-T)=d)\) produces a triangular system \(g_j^{-1}=\sum_{d=j}^r{d\brack j}_2p_{r,d}\). Invert with the standard finite Gaussian binomial identity

\[
\sum_{j=d}^t(-1)^{j-d}2^{\binom{j-d}{2}}{j\brack d}_2{t\brack j}_2=\mathbf 1_{\{t=d\}}.
\]

For completeness, this follows from \({t\brack j}_2{j\brack d}_2={t\brack d}_2{t-d\brack j-d}_2\) and the specialization of \(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-2^i z)=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k2^{\binom k2}{n\brack k}_2z^k\) at \(z=1\): the product vanishes unless \(n=0\). This gives the displayed exact formula for \(p_{r,d}\).

**4. The infinite-rank limit.** Put \(q=\tfrac12\). The identity \({d+s\brack d}_2/g_{d+s}=1/(g_d\,2^{ds}g_s)\) transforms the exact sum into

\[
p_{r,d}=\frac1{g_d}\sum_{s=0}^{r-d}\frac{(-1)^s2^{\binom{s}{2}}}{2^{ds}g_s}.
\]

The summands are absolutely dominated by a rapidly decaying series, uniformly in \(r\). Writing \(g_s=2^{s^2}\prod_{i=1}^s(1-2^{-i})\), the limit sum becomes the Euler product \(\frac1{g_d}\prod_{i=d+1}^\infty(1-2^{-i})\); this is the elementary \(q\)-binomial identity \(\sum_{s\ge0}(-1)^s q^{s(s-1)/2}t^s/\prod_{i=1}^s(1-q^i)=\prod_{i\ge0}(1-tq^i)\), at \(t=q^{d+1}\). Its proof follows by equating coefficients in \(F(t)=(1-t)F(qt)\), with \(F(0)=1\). Therefore \(p_{r,d}\to P_\infty/(2^{d^2}P_d^2)\). The limiting weights sum to one: for every \(D\), Markov's inequality applied to \(\mathbb E[{d\brack1}_2]=1\) shows \(\Pr(d>D)\le(2^{D+1}-1)^{-1}\), uniformly in \(r\). Hence the mixture of bounded generating polynomials converges and its constant term gives the claimed derangement limit.

## Verification

A standalone exact-integer checker is provided as `verify.py`. It independently enumerates every invertible binary matrix in ranks one through four and compares the fixed-space counts to the Gaussian-binomial inversion formula. In ranks one and two, it also constructs every automorphism from the coordinate normal form, checks bijectivity and the quandle homomorphism identity against the full multiplication table, counts all fixed points directly, and compares the complete histogram to the symbolic binomial expression. The results include the rank-two histogram above and `DERANGEMENT_VERIFY_OK`. No finite computation is used as proof of the results for unbounded rank or the asymptotic limit.

## Relationship to prior work

The 2026 paper *Constructions and automorphisms of quandles* identifies the even-modulus direct-product case as an explicit question (Question 3.5); its adjacent theorem treats odd-order Latin Alexander quandles. A 2011 paper by Hou determines the automorphism group of general Alexander quandles; automorphism classification alone is not claimed as new here. The new mathematical assertion is the full fixed-point distribution for the quandle action on exponent-four cores in every rank, including the Gaussian-binomial mixture and limiting derangement density. Arumugam, Dietrich, and Glasby determine fixed-point formulas for ordinary permutational wreath products, but the action here couples each fiber's linear part to the induced affine action on the quotient and is not the standard independent-fiber permutational wreath action. No equivalent fixed-point law was identified in the inspected literature. Hou's full text was not accessible during this review; whether an older theorem implicitly includes this fixed-point distribution remains a historical-priority risk.

## Limitations

This theorem is about \(\operatorname{Core}((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^r)\) and does not classify all even-modulus products or other finite Alexander quandles. The distribution concerns the full ordinary quandle automorphism group, not group automorphisms of the underlying additive group. The exact formula for the fixed-space mixture uses the standard Gaussian-binomial identities, proved or specialized above. The infinite-rank conclusion is a distributional limit and does not imply that the proportion is exactly its limit at finite rank. Independent verification has not been performed; historical priority cannot be certified from incomplete access to the 2011 general automorphism paper.
