# Scientific review of the four-fifths leaf-corona theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The universal bound has an exact Rayleigh witness supported on two nonadjacent core vertices and their two private leaves. Its squared norm is ten; symmetric stochasticity and the missing core edge bound its Dirichlet energy by two, yielding \(\mu\ge4/5\). For the balanced multipartite construction, the three adjacency eigenspaces of the regular core reduce the entire candidate matrix to explicit two-by-two blocks; their eigenvalues lie in the required interval, with a genuine \(4/5\) eigenvalue. For the bipartite strictness statement, equality in the Rayleigh bound forces every same-part witness to be a top eigenvector; the resulting private-leaf probabilities, zero core holdings and constant cross-edge weights imply equal part sizes. For star cores, the forced center probability sum exceeds one. Compactness converts unattainability into strict inequality. The exact-rational verifier tests all structural identities and multiple finite instances, but no finite test substitutes for these proofs.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence

Boyd, Diaconis and Xiao (2004) formulate the general fastest-mixing SDP and solve the unadorned complete-bipartite graph, without the private-leaf corona condition or its equality classification. Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo and Xiao (2007) develop symmetry reduction and discuss complete multipartite and wheel cores, not one private leaf per core vertex. The later clique-lift reduction uses complete interclique edge expansion, which does not produce a private-leaf corona. Standard corona spectral-polynomial sources treat fixed graph matrices rather than minimization over all transition weights. A pre-existing sunlet calculation covers exactly the \(C_4=K_{2,2}\) instance and is explicitly excluded from the newly asserted family-level contribution. Targeted mathematical-index searches did not surface a direct equivalent; this does not establish exhaustive publication priority. The full text of one plausible generalized-corona spectral paper was inaccessible, and specialist indexing could miss earlier exact treatments.

## Value — PASS

The same local four-coordinate obstruction applies to every connected noncomplete core, regardless of its symmetry or edge density; this is stronger than optimizing one preselected weight pattern. The sharp infinite balanced multipartite family shows the bound is genuinely attainable with a size-independent spectral gap, while the exact bipartite balance criterion identifies a structural boundary where attainability is lost. These are natural network mixing and edge-weight design questions, not arbitrary parameter slices. The result gives both a reusable obstruction and explicit globally optimal chains; no paper-sized downstream theorem is required for its significance.

## Limits and closest comparisons

Only uniform-stationary symmetric discrete-time chains are covered. The optimal SLEM for unbalanced bipartite coronas is not calculated; only strict inequality against the common bound is proved. No uniqueness of arbitrary optimal chains with three or more multipartite core parts is claimed. The closest existing small case is the four-core sunlet. The closest literature consists of the two Boyd papers, a clique-lift FMRMC classification, and generalized-corona spectral algebra. Residual risks concern unindexed or inaccessible exact analyses of private-leaf graph coronas.
