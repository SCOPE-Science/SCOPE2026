# Review of the five-variable Steiner quadruple classification

## Correctness
**PASS (same-model).** The proof separates three cases according to the number of distinct fixed pebbles, then the block/nonblock status for four fixed points. In the nonblock case, the four unique completions are necessarily distinct and external, leaving exactly \(v-8\) generic fifth points. This yields a permanent four-pebble game for all orders at least eight, a permanent five-pebble game for systems on the same side of the order-eight threshold, and an explicit five-variable separator. Independent finite-program checks on three constructed systems support every boundary count; the all-orders conclusion is not inferred from those samples.

## Originality
**PASS (best-knowledge qualified).** Grohe and Otto supply general noncounting pebble-game machinery, not this finite-design classification. Horan and Hurlbert study constructions and overlap cycles for Steiner quadruple systems, not finite-variable indistinguishability. Accessible research-record searches produced no matching SQS four/five-variable theorem or stronger exact coverage. Closest indexed design results concern combinatorial invariants, SQS automorphism types, or Steiner-design topology, which do not imply the logical classification. Priority is still provisional: broad design-equivalence literature and the full Huber article were not comprehensively reviewed.

## Value
**PASS.** The result gives a complete, order-independent classification for a natural infinite family of finite hypergraphs at two successive logical widths. It identifies the exact small-order exception and a sharp five-variable witness using four distinct Steiner completions, despite arbitrarily many vertices and many nonisomorphic systems. These facts are relevant as explicit finite-variable inexpressibility benchmarks and do not follow solely from the existence theorem for SQS designs.
