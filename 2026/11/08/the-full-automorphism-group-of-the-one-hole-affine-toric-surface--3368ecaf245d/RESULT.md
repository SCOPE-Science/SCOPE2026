# The full automorphism group of the one-hole affine toric surface

## Finding

Let \(R=\mathbb C[u,uv,v^2,v^3]\subset B=\mathbb C[u,v]\), and \(X=\operatorname{Spec}R\). The full group of algebraic automorphisms of \(X\) is canonically isomorphic, by lifting to the normalization, to
\[
G=\left\{(f,g)\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb A^2):
 f(0,0)=g(0,0)=0,\quad
 \frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(0,0)=0\right\}.
\]
Here \((f,g)\) denotes the geometric plane map \((u,v)\mapsto(f(u,v),g(u,v))\); its pullback on \(B\) sends the coordinate functions \(u\mapsto f\) and \(v\mapsto g\). The group law and derivative map use composition of geometric maps (rather than the opposite-order composition of their pullbacks). Thus the normalization-plane automorphism must fix the origin and preserve the one-dimensional cotangent subspace \(\mathbb C\bar u\subset(u,v)/(u,v)^2\); these conditions are also sufficient. Its first derivative is an arbitrary invertible lower-triangular matrix, and taking the linear part gives a split exact sequence
\[
1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(X)
\xrightarrow{d_0} B_{\rm low}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\simeq K\rtimes B_{\rm low},
\]
where \(B_{\rm low}=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&0\\b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right):ac\ne0\}\subset GL_2(\mathbb C)\), and \(K\) consists of polynomial plane automorphisms of the form \(f=u+O((u,v)^2)\), \(g=v+O((u,v)^2)\).

In the topology of algebraic families (equivalently, in the ordinary ind-group sense), this entire group is connected, not merely its root-generated subgroup. Its action on \(X\) has exactly two orbits: the singular origin and the smooth complement. The orbit statement also follows from more general published orbit criteria; the complete group and its split first-jet description are the main result here.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\) and with algebraic/polynomial automorphisms; no assertion is made about arbitrary analytic, formal, or merely birational transformations. The surface is the reduced affine surface embedded in \(\mathbb A^4\) by
\[
(X,V,Z,W)=(u,uv,v^2,v^3),
\]
so its defining ideal is
\[
(Z^3-W^2,\;ZX^2-V^2,\;XW-VZ,\;VW-XZ^2).
\]
This is exactly the explicit non-normal surface considered in Selin's 2026 counterexample. The choice of normalization coordinates is part of the group description; changes of coordinates conjugate the subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb A^2)\).

## Proof

**1. Ring, normalization, and conductor.** The exponent semigroup generated by \((1,0),(1,1),(0,2),(0,3)\) contains all pairs of nonnegative integers except \((0,1)\): the pure \(v\)-powers of degree at least two are generated by exponents two and three; monomials divisible by \(u\) are products of \(u\), \(uv\), and pure \(v\)-powers. Consequently, for \(\mathfrak m=(u,v)\subset B\),
\[
R=\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C u\oplus\mathfrak m^2
\quad\text{(as vector spaces)}.
\]
Also \(v\) is integral over \(R\), because \(v^2\in R\), and \(v=(uv)/u\) belongs to its fraction field. Since \(B\) is normal, \(B\) is the normalization of \(R\). The conductor \(\{b\in B:bB\subset R\}\) is \((u,v^2)B\): a polynomial with a nonzero constant or linear \(v\) coefficient is excluded by multiplication by \(v\) or by \(1\), respectively; conversely every multiple of \(u\) or \(v^2\) multiplies \(B\) into \(R\). Its radical is \((u,v)\), showing the origin is intrinsically distinguished by the normalization defect.

**2. Necessity of the tangent condition.** Any \(\mathbb C\)-algebra automorphism \(\varphi\) of \(R\) extends uniquely to an automorphism \(\widetilde\varphi\) of the integral closure \(B\). The conductor is intrinsic, so \(\widetilde\varphi\) preserves \((u,v^2)\), hence preserves its radical \((u,v)\) and fixes the origin. Write \(\widetilde\varphi(u)=f\), \(\widetilde\varphi(v)=g\). Because \(f\in R\), its coefficient of the linear monomial \(v\) vanishes. Thus \(\partial f/\partial v(0,0)=0\). The invertible Jacobian of an affine-plane polynomial automorphism implies the diagonal entries of the resulting lower-triangular matrix are nonzero.

**3. Sufficiency and exactness.** Conversely, suppose \((f,g)\) is a plane polynomial automorphism fixing the origin with \(f\equiv a u\pmod{\mathfrak m^2}\), \(a\ne0\). Since it fixes the origin, its induced map preserves \(\mathfrak m^2\); it sends \(\mathbb C+\mathbb C u+\mathfrak m^2\) into itself. The inverse fixes the origin and its derivative is the inverse of an invertible lower-triangular matrix, so the same argument applies to the inverse. Thus \(\widetilde\varphi(R)=R\), giving an automorphism of \(X\). Lifting is unique, so this proves equality with \(G\), not merely inclusion. The derivative homomorphism from \(G\) onto \(B_{\rm low}\) is surjective because every such linear matrix gives a valid automorphism. Its kernel is exactly \(K\); the linear subgroup supplies a splitting.

**4. Connectedness.** For any \(\varphi=(f,g)\in G\), set, for \(t\ne0\),
\[
\varphi_t(u,v)=\left(t^{-1}f(tu,tv),\ t^{-1}g(tu,tv)\right).
\]
Because \(f,g\) have no constant terms, these formulas extend polynomially to \(t=0\), with \(\varphi_0=d_0\varphi\). The inverses are obtained by the same formula applied to \(\varphi^{-1}\) and also extend polynomially at zero. The condition defining \(G\) is preserved for all \(t\). Thus \(\varphi\) and its derivative belong to one algebraic family. The lower-triangular group \(B_{\rm low}\cong\mathbb G_m^2\ltimes\mathbb G_a\) is connected, and its algebraic families join the derivative to the identity. Hence every \(\varphi\) lies in the identity component of \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\).

**5. Orbits.** The conductor is supported at the origin, so normalization restricts to an isomorphism \(\mathbb A^2\setminus\{0\}\cong X\setminus\{0\}\); equivalently, localizing at \(u\) or \(v^2\) gives the corresponding normal coordinate rings. Every point \((a,b)\) with \(a\ne0\) can be sent to \((1,0)\) by a lower-triangular linear automorphism. For a point \((0,b)\), \(b\ne0\), the allowed polynomial shear \((u,v)\mapsto(u+v^2,v)\) gives first coordinate \(b^2\ne0\), after which a lower-triangular linear map finishes. No automorphism moves the origin. Therefore these are exactly the two orbits.

## Relationship to prior work

Selin (2026), Section 6, introduces this exact one-hole surface and proves that its torus-and-root-generated subgroup can be strictly smaller than the identity component. His Theorem 3 does not describe the complete group and explicitly leaves the third-case structure open. The present theorem determines that complete group through an intrinsic first-jet stabilizer and gives its split derivative quotient. Normalization and conductor descriptions themselves are standard and overlap broader computations in the literature; they are used, not presented as independent discoveries. Díaz--Liendo's group comparison (2024) addresses different normalization regimes; Boldyrev--Gaifullin (2021) treat rigid and almost-rigid group classifications. Borovik--Gaifullin--Shafarevich (2021) provide general automorphism orbit criteria, so the two-orbit corollary is not claimed to be historically new. No independent priority certification is asserted.

## Limitations

The formula is for one explicit non-normal toric surface, not a classification for all isolated-singularity non-normal toric surfaces. It describes the full complex algebraic automorphism group as a subgroup of the polynomial plane automorphism group; it is not a finite-dimensional algebraic group presentation, and it does not classify conjugacy classes of its elements or all invariant subgroups. The group is infinite-dimensional. Historical overlap under alternate terminology remains a residual risk, and no independent review has been performed.
