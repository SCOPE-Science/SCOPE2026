# Review of the cube perfect-matching homology character

## Correctness — PASS
The object is the simplicial complex generated by the nine perfect matchings of the three-cube, on twelve graph-edge vertices. There are exactly 48 graph automorphisms. The nine-facet cover has contractible nonempty intersections, hence its nine-vertex nerve computes the same homotopy type. The nerve has twelve triangles and an explicit lexicographically specified sequence of twelve elementary free-edge/triangle collapses. The surviving connected graph has nine vertices and twelve edges, so the complex is a wedge of four circles. This establishes precisely the homology concentration required for the signed Hopf/Lefschetz trace argument. On every one of the 48 automorphisms, two independently constructed signed simplicial traces (the original complex and the nerve) agree with the proposed sign-plus-signed-coordinate character. The finite domain is exhaustively checked, not sampled. The two rational modules are irreducible, by the diagonal-sign weight decomposition and coordinate transitivity; character equality gives the stated rational representation. The accompanying script independently verifies the original boundary ranks in characteristics 2, 3, 5 and 7. The assertion does not infer an integral lattice splitting from rational character equality.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
The strongest identified prior theorem is Agarwal and Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v2, Theorem 25. It *already proves* the nonequivariant wedge of four circles, via the circular ladder with four rungs, and this fact is not claimed new. The theorem contains no full cube-automorphism character. Bayer, Jelić Milutinović and Vega, arXiv:2209.02803v1, concern honeycomb perfect-matching complexes, and Chandrakar and Singh, arXiv:2407.05809, concern polygonal line tilings and open grids. Indexed cube graph results about the ordinary matching complex or its three-cut complex are not statements about the perfect-matching subcomplex, and they have different homological degrees. No inspected primary result or published index entry implies the precise 48-element formula. Unindexed equivalent work and uninspected full texts remain residual risks; failure to find them is not a proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The cubical graph is an unusually symmetric member of the circular-ladder family: its automorphism group has order 48 rather than merely the distinguished ladder subgroup. The homology carries a nontrivial four-dimensional rational action, and the complete decomposition into an irreducible signed three-space and a permutation-sign line distinguishes the positive and negative eigenspaces of the central antipodal involution. The action is not determined by the known four-circle wedge or Betti number. This is a complete, naturally motivated symmetry refinement, not a routine restatement or a generalization of unknown validity.

## Scientific boundaries and closest literature
The classification of the nonequivariant homotopy type is prior work (arXiv:2608.30601v2, Theorem 25). Only the rational character under all 48 cube graph automorphisms is proposed as new. No integral-module or equivariant-homotopy classification is established. The primary literature was read in its accessible full-text form where available; indexed snippets about other graph complexes were not treated as full-text negative comparisons.
