# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The multiplication identity \(XY=r(X)\times c(Y)\) is proved directly. Its square identity determines all idempotents and forces any homomorphism to preserve rectangle supports. The rectangular-band homomorphism identities force exactly two arbitrary independent support maps. Squaring any image forces its target fiber, and the converse product calculation proves that there are no other compatibility requirements. Inclusion-exclusion counts each fiber, after which independent choices give the exact finite sum. The boundary cases \(m=1\) or \(n=1\), the composition rule, and the \(2\)-by-\(2\) count are established algebraically. `CHECK.py` performs independent small-case exact checks; no finite sample is treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations

The closest previously known result for the same object concerns its **automorphism group**. That invertible classification restricts support maps to appropriate permutations and fiber maps to bijections, and neither its statement nor the general automorphism-of-inflations theorem gives a description of arbitrary noninjective self-maps. The current result supplies the complete **endomorphism monoid**, its composition/retraction law, and a finite all-dimensional enumeration that includes highly singular maps. The inspected works arXiv:2604.26901 and arXiv:2610.29525 instead address numerical-semigroup automorphisms and inverse-semigroup isomorphisms. Direct searches for an equivalent endomorphism classification found no matching statement. Similar general results about inflation homomorphisms could conceivably imply a weaker abstract normal form; this is a residual historical-priority risk, not a claim of certified uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The full endomorphism monoid is a natural structural invariant substantially larger than its automorphism group, and its singular elements govern retractions, quotients, and structure-preserving collapses. The theorem characterizes every such map for every finite rectangular band and produces an exact arithmetic count for a nontrivial infinite two-parameter family. In the smallest genuinely two-sided case the number is \(9\,530\,217\), far beyond the invertible subgroup. Unlike another automorphism count, this resolves the distinct natural problem of classifying all noninvertible transformations; the normal form also classifies idempotent endomorphisms and their images.

The closest prior classification for the same object is invertible only. The finite checker does not certify the universal theorem and independent human audit has not been performed. Absolute historical priority is not certified.
