# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The object is the 24-vertex ordinary discrete Morse complex of the bidirected octahedral graph, not its matching complex. Every subset of at most five arrows satisfying the one-outgoing-arc, one-use-per-edge, and directed-acyclicity conditions is a simplex. An independent 5-choice-per-vertex enumeration exactly agrees with the 6125 enumerated faces. The complete greedy matching has 2724 pairs and critical cells of dimensions zero (one) and four (675); a directed-graph topological sort of all 25176 Hasse incidences establishes acyclicity, so Forman's theorem gives homology concentration. Signed fixed-face traces, computed for all 48 graph automorphisms, then equal the rational fourth-homology character. Explicit induction formulas for all ten bipartition irreducibles produce nonnegative integer multiplicities totaling dimension 675. Central eigenspaces and invariant cohomology follow by rational averaging and finite-group transfer. The exact checker is included and was executed against its own program file; finite enumeration here is exhaustive for the defined finite object, not an extrapolation to a graph family.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
Jojić's Theorem 3.2 *does* cover the nonequivariant 675-sphere wedge in the equivalent directed-tree formulation, as does the public same-graph Morse-complex computation; neither is a novelty claim. The inspected full texts of Chari–Joswig and Donovan and collaborators also do not state the exact signed-cycle representation for the octahedral graph. Related published records on the edge-matching complex of the octahedral graph and the discrete Morse complex of \(K_{3,3}\) are different objects. The complete ten-class rational representation is not implied by a rank or shelling statement. Unindexed prior equivariant calculations remain possible; the bibliographic comparison cannot prove global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The octahedral graph is the canonical complete tripartite graph on six vertices and has the full rank-three signed-permutation symmetry. Its previously known 675-sphere wedge does not specify any induced action. The complete rational representation, its substantial central eigenspace asymmetry, and eleven fixed degree-four classes answer a natural geometric symmetry question rather than re-enumerating an already published Betti number. This is a motivated finite structural refinement, not a uniform result for all multipartite graphs.

## Known limits
No integral representation lattice, equivariant homotopy model, integral quotient cohomology, or family theorem is claimed. The only accessible evidence for some older related references is bibliographic, and an equivalent unindexed symmetry calculation cannot be excluded.
