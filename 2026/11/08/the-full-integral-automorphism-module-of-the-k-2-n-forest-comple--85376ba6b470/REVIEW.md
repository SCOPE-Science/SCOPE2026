# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The four-cycle characterization gives an exact union of \(n\) simplicial balls, each bounded by the same join sphere. Relative homology supplies an equivariant integral sum map; its kernel is exactly the sign-twisted augmentation lattice. The full-group sign is checked by the orientation of the common boundary sphere, and the rational quotient follows by characteristic-zero averaging. The standalone finite verifier independently reproduces face counts, Euler characteristics and signed traces for every automorphism at \(n=3,4,5,6\), but is not used as proof for all \(n\).

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature

Kook (2006) works with coned graphs. Kook–Lee (2018), Theorems 5.3 and 5.5, and Moci–Pezzoli (2020), Section 6, provide the already-known top rank, bases and a smaller vertex-stabilizer action, not the complete integral \(S_2\times S_n\) module. Restriction to \(S_{n-1}\) does not distinguish the augmentation and quotient integral lattices: both become permutation modules. The explicit full-group integral module is therefore not the same assertion as the older subgroup formulas. Indexed research comparisons and available historical claims did not identify a covering result. An equivalent source outside the inspected corpus remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The family \(K_{2,n}\) is the smallest natural infinite complete-bipartite family with genuine four-cycles and nonzero graphic-matroid top homology. Its full integral lattice is a stronger invariant than the known Betti number or restricted representation; it gives all automorphism characters, distinguishes the integral \(S_n\) root lattice by \(H^1(S_n,A_{n-1})\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), and determines the rational orbit-space cohomology. This is a structural classification across all \(n\ge3\), not an isolated finite enumeration.

## Sources and limits

Kook (2006), Kook–Lee (2018), Moci–Pezzoli (arXiv:2001.03760), and classical matroid shellability were compared by statements and implications. The general wedge count is prior knowledge, not claimed as a new topological theorem. Integral quotient cohomology, arbitrary \(K_{m,n}\) and independent publication-priority certification are outside scope.
