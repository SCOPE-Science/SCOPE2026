# The full odd-prime p-adic index–value closure of central binomial coefficients

## Finding
Let \(p\) be any odd prime and \(B_n=\binom{2n}{n}\) for \(n\ge0\). In the base-\(p\) addition \(a+a\), count the carries *produced in the lowest \(s\) digits* (including a carry leaving digit \(s-1\)); call the count \(c_{p,s}(a)\), where \(s\ge1\) and \(0\le a<p^s\). For every \(k\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{\{B_n\bmod p^k:n\ge0,\ n\equiv a\pmod{p^s}\}=
\{z\bmod p^k:z\equiv0\pmod{p^{\min(k,c_{p,s}(a))}}\}.}
\]
**Every residue on the right occurs for infinitely many such indices.** In particular the number of residue classes is \(p^{\max(k-c_{p,s}(a),0)}\). This is an exact classification under *every prescribed index suffix*, not merely a scalar density statement.

For \(x\in\mathbb Z_p\), let \(c_p(x)\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}\) be the total number of carries in the infinite base-\(p\) addition \(x+x\), equivalently \(\sup_s c_{p,s}(x\bmod p^s)\). Taking \(p^\infty\mathbb Z_p=\{0\}\), the full two-dimensional closure is
\[
\boxed{\overline{\{(n,B_n):n\ge0\}}^{\ \mathbb Z_p^2}
=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z_p^2:y\in p^{c_p(x)}\mathbb Z_p\}.}
\]
The formula includes the zero-only fibers above \(p\)-adic indices with infinitely many doubling carries.

## Assumptions and scope
The prime \(p\) is odd; all indices are nonnegative integers. The carry leaving each of the fixed \(s\) digits is counted once if produced. The \(p\)-adic valuation of zero is \(\infty\). The conclusion concerns residue **sets**, not the natural density or least position of a residue. No result for the prime \(2\) is claimed.

## Proof
**Step 1: Separation of large blocks.** Write \(M=d p^t\) with fixed positive integer \(d\), and let \(a\ge0\) be fixed. The exact rational factorial identity gives
\[
\frac{B_{a+M}}{B_a B_M}
=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{2a}(1+2M/i)}{\prod_{i=1}^{a}(1+M/i)^2}.
\]
For any precision \(h\ge1\), choose \(t\) sufficiently large relative to \(a,h\) (specifically \(t>h+\max_{1\le i\le2a}\nu_p(i)\) suffices; the empty products for \(a=0\) equal one). Every factor on the right is a \(p\)-adic unit congruent to \(1\pmod{p^h}\). Consequently \(B_{a+d p^t}\equiv B_a B_{d p^t}\pmod{p^h}\). This lemma can be iterated with arbitrarily widely separated positive blocks.

**Step 2: Unit block limits.** For \(1\le d\le (p-1)/2\), Kummer's carry theorem gives \(\nu_p(B_{d p^t})=0\). The exact identity
\[
\frac{B_{pN}}{B_N}=\prod_{\substack{1\le i\le pN\\p\nmid i}}\left(1+\frac{pN}{i}\right)
\]
shows the ratios tend to \(1\) \(p\)-adically when \(N=d p^{t-1}\) and \(t\to\infty\). At \(t=1\), the sum of the inverse residues \(1,\ldots,p-1\) is \(0\pmod p\), so the ratio is \(1\pmod{p^2}\); for \(t\ge2\) this congruence is immediate factor by factor. Define
\[
\beta_d=\lim_{t\to\infty}B_{d p^t}\in\mathbb Z_p^\times,
\qquad \beta_d\equiv B_d\pmod{p^2}.
\]
(The limit exists because the successive ratios are \(1\pmod{p^t}\).)

**Step 3: A uniform, non-Wieferich-free unit-generation lemma.** We prove that the \(\beta_d\) generate \((\mathbb Z/p^h\mathbb Z)^\times\) for **every** \(h\ge1\). If \(p=3\), then \(\beta_1\equiv 2\pmod9\); by the lifting-the-exponent formula, it generates every group \((\mathbb Z/3^h\mathbb Z)^\times\). Suppose \(p\ge5\). Working modulo \(p\), the subgroup generated by \(B_d\), \(1\le d\le (p-1)/2\), contains \(2=B_1\). For every odd prime \(q\le p-2\), the number \(B_{(q+1)/2}\) contains \(q\) to exponent exactly one and its other prime factors are smaller than \(q\); induction therefore puts the residue of each such prime \(q\) in the subgroup. Every integer \(1\le m\le p-1\) factors into primes at most \(p-2\), so the subgroup is the full \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).

If all \(B_d^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^2}\), the same induction through their **integer prime factorizations** implies \(q^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^2}\) for every prime \(q\le p-2\). It would then imply \((p-1)^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^2}\), contradicting the explicit binomial expansion
\[
(p-1)^{p-1}\equiv1+p\pmod{p^2}.
\]
Hence some \(\beta_d^{p-1}\) differs from one modulo \(p^2\). Its powers generate the full principal-unit subgroup \(1+p\mathbb Z/p^h\mathbb Z\) for each \(h\) (odd-prime lifting-the-exponent), while the reductions of the \(\beta_d\) generate the residue-field unit group. Thus the full unit-generation assertion follows.

**Step 4: Prescribing the carries while fixing low digits.** Fix \(s,a\) and put \(c=c_{p,s}(a)\). For every integer \(j\ge c\) there is a nonnegative \(n_0\equiv a\pmod{p^s}\) with **exactly \(j\) doubling carries**: retain the first \(s\) digits, then create \(j-c\) additional carries using digits \((p+1)/2\) when the incoming carry is zero and \((p-1)/2\) when it is one; terminate with the next digit zero. When \(j=c\), the index \(n_0=a\) already works. Kummer then gives \(\nu_p(B_{n_0})=j\). Every index with the specified suffix has at least the \(c\) prescribed carries, proving the required inclusion into the ideal.

Conversely, choose \(k\ge1\) and a desired residue \(z\) with \(j=\nu_p(z)<k\) and \(j\ge c\). Choose \(n_0\) as above. Since \(B_{n_0}/p^j\) is a unit, Step 3 supplies a finite product \(\prod\beta_{d_i}\) equal modulo \(p^{k-j}\) to \((z/p^j)(B_{n_0}/p^j)^{-1}\). Append positive blocks \(d_i p^{t_i}\) one by one, choosing each exponent so large that Step 1 applies at precision \(k\), and \(B_{d_i p^{t_i}}\equiv\beta_{d_i}\pmod{p^{k-j}}\). The resulting index is still congruent to \(a\pmod{p^s}\) and satisfies \(B_n\equiv z\pmod{p^k}\). To obtain infinitely many indices, append arbitrarily many further blocks whose unit products are one modulo \(p^{k-j}\) (repeat \(\beta_1\) its finite order modulo \(p^{k-j}\) times). For \(z=0\), construct an index with at least \(k\) carries and append arbitrary widely separated unit blocks. This establishes both the exact ideal and infinitude.

**Step 5: Full index–value closure.** If \(n_i\to x\) \(p\)-adically, its lowest \(s\) digits eventually agree with those of \(x\); Kummer and Step 4 force \(\nu_p(B_{n_i})\ge c_{p,s}(x\bmod p^s)\) for every \(s\). Therefore any limit \(y\) belongs to \(p^{c_p(x)}\mathbb Z_p\). Conversely, for a given \((x,y)\) with this divisibility condition and each positive integer \(t\), Step 4 produces \(n_t\equiv x\pmod{p^t}\) and \(B_{n_t}\equiv y\pmod{p^t}\). Hence \((n_t,B_{n_t})\to(x,y)\), completing the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Berend and Harmse, *Acta Arithmetica* **84** (1998), Theorem 1.1, already prove that the **unrestricted** central binomial values are dense in \(\mathbb Z_p\) for odd primes. That older result is not claimed here. Their proof studies special index forms; it does not state the exact image for **every** prescribed index suffix or the complete two-dimensional index–value closure, whose fibers can be nontrivial proper ideals and even zero-only. Their Proposition 2.1 also gives strong prime-power scaling used in the literature; the present proof uses a short independent block-limit argument. Barat–Grabner (2001) studies joint distribution of binomial coefficients in Pascal rows and their valuations, not the index–middle-coefficient closure. Burns (2017) obtains modulo-prime Catalan surjectivity and the finite-field central-binomial generator lemma. The \(p=3\) unrestricted slice and some prime-three progression slices are already contained in earlier known results; the accepted assertion is the *all-odd-prime, all-suffix joint classification*, not those slices.

## Limitations
No uniform lower density of each represented residue in its arithmetic progression, effective small witness bound, or statement at \(p=2\) is obtained. A separate, unlocated formulation of the two-dimensional closure may exist; literature priority remains historically qualified. The proof is complete but has not received independent mathematical audit.
