# Review of the odd-prime index–value closure theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The factorial ratio gives the large-block multiplication lemma with exact precision control. Its unit-block limits reduce modulo prime squares to finite central-binomial digits. The key finite group lemma is proved uniformly, without assuming a particular base to be a primitive root: the field-unit generation uses the prime-factor induction; the principal-unit generation uses a contradiction with the explicit non-Teichmüller residue \((p-1)^{p-1}\equiv1+p\pmod{p^2}\). The carry construction correctly covers every possible valuation above the forced low-digit count. The two inclusions into the joint topological closure follow from finite-prefix Kummer bounds and residue-set surjectivity. The verifier checks six finite suffix spectra and 14 prime-square unit groups. Finite checks are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, historically qualified
Berend–Harmse (1998), Theorem 1.1, already yields unrestricted \(p\)-adic density, including infinitely many occurrences. It does not imply *by itself* the present all-suffix fiber formula: density of scalar values says nothing about which values occur when the index is fixed modulo \(p^s\). Their proofs and scaling congruences are closely related; possible unindexed refinements remain a risk. Barat–Grabner (2001) studies asymptotic binomial distribution and valuations in Pascal rows; no exact index–central-binomial joint closure was located. The previously recorded prime-three progression and Catalan residues are covered special cases, not the accepted contribution. The key mathematical novelty claim is the complete two-dimensional carry-ideal closure for every odd prime. Relevant published full text was inspected where accessible; the Cai–Granville 2002 paper was available only as an abstract/review and is identified as an access risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A classical \(p\)-adic density theorem leaves the natural index–value coupling unspecified. Here the answer is a complete closed subset of \(\mathbb Z_p^2\) with fibers \(p^{c_p(x)}\mathbb Z_p\), and the exact answer on every finite index cylinder is explicitly computable by digit carries. It sharply distinguishes ordinary fibers from proper-ideal and zero-only fibers and supplies infinitely many representatives for every allowed finite residue. This is a structural classification of a natural index–sequence graph, not a new numerical slice or a routine rephrasing of scalar density.

## Risks and limitations
Historical priority outside indexed/full-text accessible literature is not proved. The strongest adjacent result is Berend–Harmse Theorem 1.1; its method has substantial overlap, although neither its statement nor its inspected proof supplies the arbitrary-index-cylinder classification directly. Full texts for Barat–Grabner 2001 and Cai–Granville 2002 were not accessible in this check; abstracts and secondary discussion are not grounds for claims that their entire papers are noncovering. The theorem does not address \(p=2\), relative frequencies, or minimal witnesses.
