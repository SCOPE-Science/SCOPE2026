# The ghost-involution character of tetrahedral pure Morse complexes

## Finding

Let \(K=\Delta^3\) be the tetrahedron on four labeled vertices and let \(\partial K\) be its boundary. Write \(\mathcal M_P(K)\) for the simplicial complex generated by maximum-cardinality **acyclic** matchings on the Hasse diagram of nonempty faces. Its simplicial vertices are cover incidences \((\alpha,\beta)\) with \(\alpha\subset\beta\) and \(|\beta|=|\alpha|+1\). The natural vertex-permutation group is \(S_4\).

On \(\mathcal M_P(\partial K)\) there is also the commuting *ghost involution* \(\pi\), acting on a Hasse incidence by
\[
\pi(\alpha,\beta)=(V\setminus\beta,V\setminus\alpha),\qquad |V|=4.
\]
It extends the natural action to \(S_4\times C_2\). Let \(U=\widetilde H_3(\mathcal M_P(\partial K);\mathbb Q)\). In the conjugacy-class order \(1^4,2\,1^2,2^2,3\,1,4\), its character is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
\text{element}&1^4&2\,1^2&2^2&3\,1&4\\\hline
\chi_U(\sigma,1)&81&-1&-3&0&1\\
\chi_U(\sigma,\pi)&9&3&-3&0&1
\end{array}.
\]

The \(\pi\)-positive and \(\pi\)-negative subspaces have dimensions \(45\) and \(36\). More precisely, writing \(S^{\lambda}\) for the rational Specht module indexed by a partition of four and \(E_\pm\) for the one-dimensional \(C_2\)-character of sign \(\pm1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
U\cong{}&\bigl(2S^{(4)}\oplus6S^{(3,1)}\oplus3S^{(2,2)}\oplus6S^{(2,1,1)}\oplus S^{(1,1,1,1)}\bigr)\otimes E_+\\
&\oplus\bigl(S^{(4)}\oplus4S^{(3,1)}\oplus3S^{(2,2)}\oplus5S^{(2,1,1)}\oplus2S^{(1,1,1,1)}\bigr)\otimes E_-.
\end{aligned}
\]

In addition, the natural \(S_4\)-representation on \(\widetilde H_3(\mathcal M_P(\Delta^3);\mathbb Q)\) has the *same* character as the restriction of \(U\): \((81,-1,-3,0,1)\). Thus the two pure Morse complexes have isomorphic rational \(S_4\)-top homology modules, even though the complexes have different face vectors. **This is a character/module assertion, not an equivariant homotopy equivalence or an isomorphism of integral lattices.**

## Assumptions and scope

All simplicial complexes are finite, built from nonempty faces and ordinary Forman/Chari--Joswig discrete Morse matchings, not strong Morse matchings. An admissible matching is a set of Hasse cover edges with no shared endpoints such that reversing those matched edges in the otherwise downward-directed Hasse diagram produces a directed acyclic graph. The *pure* Morse complex is generated by the matchings of largest cardinality, not by all inclusion-maximal matchings. For \(\Delta^3\), there are 28 cover edges and largest matchings have 7 edges; for \(\partial\Delta^3\), there are 24 cover edges and largest matchings have 6 edges. In both cases there are 256 largest matchings.

The ghost map reverses face inclusion and therefore sends acyclic matchings to acyclic matchings. It is a simplicial automorphism of the pure complex, commutes with \(S_4\), and squares to the identity. The result concerns rational degree-three reduced homology only. The fact that this homology is 81-dimensional with all other reduced homology zero is Theorem 22 of Scoville [1]; the classification of the ghost map for the full Morse complex is due to Lin--Scoville [2].

## Proof

**1. Exact simplicial-chain Lefschetz formula.** Orient each simplex of the Morse complex by its Hasse-incidence vertices in increasing lexicographic order. For any simplicial automorphism \(g\), the trace on its chain group in degree \(r\) is the sum, over all setwise \(g\)-fixed simplices with \(r+1\) incidence vertices, of the sign of the permutation of those vertices. Therefore
\[
L(g)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\in\mathcal M_P(K),\;gA=A}
(-1)^{|A|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_A).
\]
The Lefschetz trace identity and the homology concentration cited above imply \(L(g)=1-\chi_{\widetilde H_3}(g)\). This includes orientation signs: simply counting invariant simplices would be incorrect.

**2. Finite exhaustive incidence enumeration.** Represent each of the four original vertices by a bit. A face is an integer in \(\{1,\ldots,15\}\), omitting 15 for the boundary; cover incidences are exactly the ordered pairs \((a,b)\) with \(a\) a proper one-element-deletion face of \(b\). For each possible subset of the 28 (respectively 24) cover incidences, the supplied standalone verifier branches over every inclusion/exclusion choice, rejects reused face endpoints, and decides acyclicity of the modified Hasse orientation by a complete topological-sort test. This is an exhaustive finite proof, not sampling. It finds, by increasing number of matched incidences, the full Morse-complex face vectors
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^3:&\quad(28,300,1544,3932,4632,2128,256),\\
\partial\Delta^3:&\quad(24,216,896,1692,1248,256).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the downward closure of all maximum-cardinality matchings produces the pure face vectors
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_P(\Delta^3):&\quad(28,300,1544,3680,3672,1600,256),\\
\mathcal M_P(\partial\Delta^3):&\quad(24,216,896,1680,1152,256).
\end{aligned}
\]
These total-count checks agree with [1], but the signed symmetry counts below are separate computations.

**3. Exact character enumeration and independent orbit check.** For each of the five \(S_4\) cycle types, enumerate the fixed pure simplices, including their induced ordering sign. On both pure complexes the resulting chain Lefschetz values are
\[
(-80,2,4,1,0).
\]
For the boundary complex, composing with the ghost involution gives
\[
(-8,-2,4,1,0).
\]
For example, under the ghost involution alone, the only invariant pure simplices have 2, 4, or 6 incidence vertices; they contribute \(+12\), \(-36\), and \(+16\) to the chain Lefschetz number. Thus \(L(\pi)=-8\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(\pi|U)=9\). The verifier independently recomputes each nonidentity signed trace by enumerating unions of vertex-orbits of the corresponding permutation and checking that they are acyclic pure matchings; it also checks every one of the 24 coordinate permutations and each of their 24 ghost products. All calculations use exact integers and the complete face sets.

Subtracting each Lefschetz value from 1 proves the two rows of the character table and the equality of the \(S_4\)-restrictions. Since \(\pi\) is an involution, the projection onto its \(\pm1\) eigenspaces is \((1\pm\pi)/2\), so their characters are respectively
\[
(45,1,-3,0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad(36,-2,0,0,0).
\]
Taking inner products with the five rational irreducible characters of \(S_4\), using class sizes \((1,6,3,8,6)\), yields the stated Specht-multiplicity decomposition. Its dimensions sum to \(45+36=81\), completing the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Chari and Joswig [3] introduced these complexes and the acyclic Hasse-matching formulation. Lin and Scoville [2] identified the extra ghost automorphism and the \(S_4\times C_2\) automorphism group of the **full** boundary Morse complex, but did not calculate the ghost action on pure top homology. Scoville [1] determined the nonequivariant sphere-wedge types and homology ranks and gave numerical face vectors, but did not state these symmetry characters or the resulting \(45/36\) ghost-eigenspace decomposition. Their results are used as premises, not claimed as new. Other accessible finite Morse-complex symmetry studies concern different underlying complexes and do not imply these numerical character values.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns a specific, naturally distinguished finite example (the first tetrahedral case with the cited pure three-sphere-wedge result), not a uniform formula for higher-dimensional simplices. It proves an equality of rational representations upon restriction, not an equivariant homotopy equivalence and not an integral-lattice classification. The full boundary Morse complex has homology in two degrees; this work does not split their characters. A logically equivalent unindexed calculation or an unpublished source computation could predate these formulas; no claim of absolute uniqueness is made. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
