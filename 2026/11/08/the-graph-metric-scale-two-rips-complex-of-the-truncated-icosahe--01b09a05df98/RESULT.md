# The graph-metric scale-two Rips complex of the truncated icosahedron is a wedge of twenty-one two-spheres

## Finding

Let \(B\) denote the truncated-icosahedral graph (the 60-vertex graph of Buckminsterfullerene). Equip its vertex set with unweighted shortest-path graph distance. For the **inclusive** Vietoris–Rips complex, in which a finite set is a simplex exactly when every pair has graph distance at most two,

\[\operatorname{VR}_{\le 2}(V(B))\simeq\bigvee^{21} S^2.\]

This is a homotopy equivalence over the integers, not merely a field-homology calculation. In particular the complex is simply connected and its only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{21}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The combinatorial icosahedron has vertices \(0,1,2,\dots,11\). For residues \(i\) modulo five, set \(u_i=2+i\) and \(v_i=7+i\). Its edges are \(0u_i,1v_i,u_i u_{i+1},v_i v_{i+1},u_i v_i,u_i v_{i-1}\). The truncated graph has one vertex \((a,b)\) for every oriented icosahedral edge; there are 60. Two distinct vertices are joined if they are opposite orientations of the same edge, or if they have the same first coordinate \(a\) and their second coordinates are adjacent in the five-cycle induced by neighbors of \(a\). This defines a 3-regular 60-vertex graph with 90 edges and 32 polygonal faces: twelve pentagons and twenty hexagons. It is the truncated icosahedron, independent of a Euclidean embedding.

## Proof

**1. Finite simplicial reduction.** Form the square graph by connecting two distinct vertices whenever they are at distance one or two in \(B\). The required Rips complex \(X\) is precisely the flag complex of this graph. The included deterministic verifier independently regenerates every clique, obtaining 802 nonempty simplices and face vector \((60,270,340,120,12)\) in dimensions zero through four. File `certificate.json` specifies 120 pairs of faces \((\sigma,\tau)\) with \(\sigma\) a codimension-one face of \(\tau\). The verifier checks *against the full current face set at each step* that \(\tau\) is the unique strict coface of \(\sigma\), before deleting the pair. This establishes 120 genuine elementary collapses onto a 2-dimensional complex with face vector \((60,270,232)\). In particular \(X\) has the homotopy type of a connected 2-dimensional CW complex and Euler characteristic \(60-270+340-120+12=22\).

**2. Fundamental group.** Every edge of the square graph at original graph-distance two lies in a triangle with the two edges of an original length-two path. Consequently the inclusion of the original truncated graph into \(X\) surjects onto \(\pi_1(X)\): any added edge can be homotoped relative endpoints to a two-edge path in the original graph. The 1-skeleton of a cellular sphere with the twelve pentagonal and twenty hexagonal faces is this original graph. The verifier reconstructs these 32 faces, checks that every original edge occurs in two face boundaries, that every original vertex has a cyclic three-face link, and that the resulting connected closed surface has Euler characteristic two. Hence all original graph loops are normally generated by its 32 facial boundaries.

Each pentagonal face boundary bounds a simplex in \(X\): every two of its five vertices are at original graph distance at most two. Every hexagonal face \((a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_5)\) bounds the four-triangle disk with triangles \((a_0,a_1,a_2)\), \((a_2,a_3,a_4)\), \((a_4,a_5,a_0)\), and \((a_0,a_2,a_4)\), each verified as a simplex of \(X\). Thus all cellular facial relations vanish in \(\pi_1(X)\). Therefore \(\pi_1(X)=0\).

**3. Integral homotopy conclusion.** As \(X\) is connected, simply connected, and equivalent to a finite 2-dimensional complex, its integral first homology vanishes and its integral second homology is a subgroup of a free abelian simplicial 2-chain group; therefore it is free. The Euler characteristic gives \(\operatorname{rank}H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=22-1=21\). The Hurewicz theorem realizes a basis of \(H_2\) by maps of 2-spheres, giving a map \(\bigvee^{21}S^2\to X\) inducing an isomorphism on every integral homology group. The simply connected homological Whitehead theorem makes this map a homotopy equivalence.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams and collaborators study Vietoris–Rips homotopy of finite torus grids, not the truncated icosahedral graph. Xia and collaborators study persistent homology of fullerene molecular coordinates using **Euclidean** distances, not the unweighted shortest-path graph metric here. The available graph-metric Rips classification of the **truncated octahedron** concerns a different, 24-vertex Archimedean graph; the published girth-at-least-six hexagon-packing theorem cannot apply to this graph, which has pentagonal cycles and girth five. These distinctions were checked at the statement and hypothesis level; full-text overlap with unindexed graph-power literature remains possible.

## Limitations

The claim concerns precisely the 60-vertex graph and inclusive graph-distance threshold two. The 120 collapses need not be symmetry-equivariant. It does not classify higher Rips scales, arbitrary fullerene isomers, or metric filtrations defined by Euclidean molecular coordinates. The wedge decomposition is not asserted to choose a canonical homology generator for each hexagonal face. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
