# Scientific review: graph-period classification for diagonal weight-one Rota–Baxter operators

## Correctness — PASS
For an arbitrary field, substituting a diagonal operator into the weight-one Rota–Baxter identity yields the exact condition \(r_i^2=(2r_i+1)r_j\) on every nonzero structure coefficient. Connectedness makes the support either empty or full. In the latter case the bijection \(u_i=1+r_i^{-1}\) conjugates every edge condition to \(u_j=u_i^2\), including characteristic two. The gcd of closed-walk lengths equals the graph period, and the standard gcd identity for \(2^L-1\) determines the allowed roots. Path independence and the avoidance of \(u_i=1\) are established for all characteristic and field boundaries. The count includes zero and negative identity exactly once each. The finite-field formula follows from cyclicity of \(\mathbb F_q^\times\). A standalone exact checker verifies all 894 strongly connected graph/field cases with at most three vertices and a weighted four-dimensional perfect non-monomial example. The computation is corroborative; the theorem has a symbolic proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
A pre-existing exact scheme-theoretic classification already determines diagonal nonzero-weight Rota–Baxter operators on **single oriented cycles** in every characteristic and gives \(1+\gcd(2^n-1,q-1)\) for cycle length \(n\). This is direct prior coverage for monomial cyclic cases. The 2019 Ladra–Murodov arXiv paper, especially Section 3 and its low-dimensional classification table, treats weight-one operators over \(\mathbb C\), including a two-dimensional period-two special case, and supplies coefficient-equation methodology. Qaralleh–Mukhamedov–Khakimov (2026, Section 5, Theorem 5.2) derive the same rational recurrence for a distinct nilpotent maximal-nilindex family in characteristic zero, with associated periodicity constraints. Neither inspected literature nor the prior cyclic scheme classification states the exact characteristic-uniform count for arbitrary **strongly connected, branching, possibly non-monomial** evolution graphs or its period-only invariance when cycle lengths interact. Searches of mathematical research records and targeted literature searches did not identify a theorem implying this entire result. A finite-field or graph-theoretic recasting might nevertheless have appeared in uninspected literature, and the ingredients of the proof are elementary; historical priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is natural: the directed period of an irreducible multiplication graph controls all diagonal weight-one operators, eliminating dependence on every nonzero multiplication coefficient. The theorem is an exact all-dimension, all-field classification, not a single computed slice. It distinguishes graph periods and finite-field arithmetic, including a perfect non-monomial algebra over \(\mathbb F_4\) with four diagonal operators and the collapse from eight to two operators over \(\mathbb F_8\) when one edge introduces a cycle of relatively prime length. It extends a recent rational-recurrence phenomenon beyond the acyclic nilpotent setting to strongly connected structure graphs, where the recurrence's global closing conditions are genuinely different. It does not claim the classification of off-diagonal operators.

## Closest literature, scope and remaining risks
The same rational map \(f(r)=r^2/(2r+1)\) is a known ingredient from the January 2026 classification; the contribution is the graph-period/previously known reciprocal-variable squaring normalization and the arbitrary-field exact count under strong connectivity. The 2019 classification covers two-dimensional complex cases and hence some numerical specializations. The previous exact weight-one scheme result for a single directed cycle is a substantial, directly relevant special case, and its finite-field count is prior coverage; it does not imply the arbitrary branching-graph cycle-gcd theorem, since the gcd need not itself be a realized cycle length. A previous weight-zero result on weighted permutation evolution algebras concerns a different Rota–Baxter weight and graph class. A 2026 paper on local weight-zero operators is outside the exact weight and graph hypotheses. Priority remains qualified because a complete historical review cannot prove uniqueness from search failures.
