# Scientific review: Heawood dominance complex

## Correctness — PASS
Two independently specified exhaustive face tests agree on every one of the \(2^{14}\) vertex subsets. The reconstructed complex has \(8,851\) faces including the empty face. A concrete finite matching on its ordinary face poset has \(4,424\) paired cover relations, one critical vertex and one critical six-cell. Directly checking all \(52,304\) Hasse incidences verifies that the orientation reversed on matched pairs is acyclic. An independently ordered matching produces the same critical dimensions and passes the same test. The two-cell CW description yields an actual integral homotopy equivalence \(\mathcal D(H)\simeq S^6\). The separate oriented cross-polytopal cycle has zero boundary and coefficient one on a sixth-dimensional face having no seventh-dimensional coface, proving that it is an integral primitive generator. All \(7!\) point permutations were checked for Fano incidence preservation; precisely \(168\) pass and all are even. The explicit part-swapping duality has homological sign \(+1\), proving the full \(336\)-element action is trivial.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
The motivating primary literature is T. Matsushita, arXiv:2009.04145 (first public 2020-09-09), and T. Matsushita–S. Wakatsuki, arXiv:2312.02639 (first public 2023-12-05). Their main theorems concern connectivity bounds, combinatorial Alexander duality, chordal graph spheres, cycles and products of paths. The Heawood graph is neither chordal nor a member of the treated cycle/ladder families. The general Alexander-dual identity does not give this homotopy classification without an independent computation of the complement's neighborhood homology and its integral attaching information. Neither inspected paper provides the primitive cross-polytopal generator or the full integral automorphism action. Searches in published research records for Heawood dominance complexes, Fano-plane incidence dominance, and part-swapping characters returned nearby results on **different** Heawood complexes (matching, independence, two-matching, cut), which neither imply nor determine this one. The 2026 abstract on Cohen–Macaulay dominance complexes is a residual bibliographic risk: complete text could not be inspected, and this nonpure complex does not meet the pure Cohen–Macaulay hypothesis. These negative comparisons are evidence-limited, not proof of globally exhaustive novelty.

## Value — PASS
The Heawood graph is the smallest incidence graph of a projective plane and a canonical highly symmetric cubic bipartite graph. Its dominance complex is nonpure with maximal faces in four dimensions, yet has the exact integral type of a single six-sphere. The explicit embedded cross-polytope realizes the generator and shows the vertex-cover connectivity inequality is sharp at \(\tau(H)=7\), outside chordal-graph classifications. The complete \(336\)-element symmetry action is an additional structural invariant that is not determined by the Betti numbers. This exact natural graph calculation provides a useful boundary example for extending dominance-complex topology to incidence geometries; no unproved higher-order projective-plane generalization is asserted.

## Residual risks and scope
An unindexed or inaccessible statement could in principle subsume the result, and independent expert checking has not occurred. The result does not identify an equivariant homotopy equivalence or classify other finite projective planes. The finite checks are exhaustive for the explicitly defined fourteen-vertex object and do not purport to prove general-family statements.
