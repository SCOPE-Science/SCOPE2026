# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The forbidden-triple characterization is equivalent to induced components having at most two vertices. Two independent face-enumeration methods agree at every length from three through seven. The explicit repeated-toggle acyclic matching at length seven exhaustively covers \(2044\) nonempty faces with \(1021\) disjoint pairs; the directed-Hasse acyclicity check covers \(8932\) incidences. One critical zero-cell and one critical five-cell imply \(S^5\) by discrete Morse theory. The other four lengths have reduced Euler characteristics \(-4,3,-11,-10\) and cannot be spheres. The separate boundary rank check corroborates, but is not used in place of, the homotopy argument.

## Originality
**PASS relative to the inspected sources.** An accessible earlier result already gives the second-independence homotopy types at prism lengths three and five. This is acknowledged rather than treated as novel; its specified finite collection does not include length seven. Taylan's forbidden-subgraph complexes cover path, cycle and forest cases; the exact two-layer cyclic prism is none of these. The Deshpande–Singh higher-independence classifications list complete multipartite graphs, whiskered graphs, paths, cycles and trees, not the prism. The chordal-graph results are not applicable to a circular ladder with induced four-cycles. Independent work on ordinary-independence graph products or on prism Rips, Čech and cut complexes concerns different faces and does not imply this second-independence classification. Actual semantic-record searches under circular ladder, prism, dissociation, forbidden-three-vertex-path and second-independence terminology found no covering statement. Residual risk: equivalent unpublished/unindexed statements remain possible.

## Value
**PASS.** This is a motivated first-occurrence question in the natural periodic-width-two graph family. The seven-prism is the **first** member whose nonflag second-independence complex is spherical: the preceding four lengths have large reduced Euler characteristic magnitude, while at seven a complete acyclic matching reduces the complex to one five-sphere. The exact natural cutoff and homotopy type provide a useful boundary case for prospective prism-family recurrences, without claiming an infinite theorem.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest published framework is Taylan (arXiv:1501.06754); Deshpande–Singh (arXiv:2001.05448) is the closest general higher-independence homotopy classification. The extension beyond this cutoff, symmetry action, and definitive publication priority are unresolved.
