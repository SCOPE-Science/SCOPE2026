## Originality

**PASS relative to inspected evidence; external priority remains uncertain.** Adamaszek's original full-text graph-power classification (arXiv:1104.0433v1, §§2–3, 5–7) does not include the Herschel graph, and its girth-seven square-stability hypothesis is invalid here: the graph has four-cycles. The indexed published Herschel calculation concerns its ordinary independence complex and deletions, not its shortest-path graph-power clique complexes. Separate graph-metric Rips classifications of other named polyhedral and cubic graphs do not imply a universal classification for the Herschel graph. Targeted title-, object-, equivalence-, group-character-, and parameter-based searches found no covering result, but absence from indexed sources is not proof of novelty. The exact graph and its aliases, all-field persistence, possible stronger graph-family statements, and equivalent flag-complex formulations were considered.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The Herschel graph is the smallest non-Hamiltonian polyhedral graph, so it is a canonical, non-arbitrary example rather than a chosen vertex slice. Its distance-two clique complex is five-dimensional as a simplicial complex but has the integral homotopy type of a three-sphere; the next scale collapses, a genuine topology transition not visible from the graph's ordinary cycle space. The complete persistence barcode and the nontrivial full-symmetry orientation character offer structural invariants beyond a numerical Euler-characteristic calculation. The result is finite and graph-specific; its importance is a natural exact benchmark, not an unsupported universal theorem.
