# Scientific review: icosahedral second independence complex

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is for the explicitly labelled twelve-vertex icosahedral graph, not for a family. Every selected face is characterized by induced maximum degree at most one. The executable verifier exhaustively checks the face set (12,66,140,105), 147 matching pairs, all 972 oriented face-poset covering relations for acyclicity, 1/4/24 critical-cell dimensions, and the complete signed integral Morse boundary. A displayed 4-by-4 minor has determinant 1, which makes the degree-three Morse boundary surjective over the integers; the remaining free rank is 20. The CW model has one vertex and no 1-cells, so Hurewicz followed by Whitehead establishes precisely a wedge of twenty 3-spheres. Direct mod-two ranks 11,55,85 provide a separate consistency check. Risk: an implementation error could affect a finite certificate; all relevant code and integer data are embedded and replayable.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
Equivalent descriptions checked include higher/second independence, dissociation, forest-filtration first stage and forbidden-three-vertex-path complexes. The closest full-text sources are Taylan (2015, arXiv:1501.06754), Deshpande–Singh (2020, arXiv:2001.05448) and Carnero Bravo (2023, arXiv:2307.13271). Their inspected graph classifications cover paths, cycles, forests, complete multipartite or whiskered graphs, trees, joins, and selected products—not the icosahedron. Indexed findings for the same underlying icosahedral graph concern its distinct Lovász neighborhood, matching, three-cut or metric complexes; one indexed second-independence result concerns the nonisomorphic Petersen graph. The exact numerical result and its equivalences were queried. Neither those entries nor the inspected full text supplies a stronger implication covering this claim. Risk: indexed searches cannot exclude unseen equivalent tables or unpublished computations.

## Value — PASS
The icosahedron is a canonical vertex-transitive five-regular triangulation of the sphere. Its graph is nonchordal, highly connected and outside the standard higher-independence classifications. The precise degree-three integral homotopy group rank 20 and the absence of lower homotopy are a motivated, natural finite test case for the second-independence/forest-filtration constructions. The primitive attaching calculation proves a genuinely homotopical assertion not obtained from counting independent sets or from finite-field Betti numbers. The result does not assert broad generality or an equivariant classification.
