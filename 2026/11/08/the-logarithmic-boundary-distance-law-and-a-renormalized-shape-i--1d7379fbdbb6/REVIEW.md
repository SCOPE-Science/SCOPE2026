# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The main formula follows from an exact cusp strip integral \(2\sinh(s)[1+\log(h_i/(2\sinh s))]\) plus the compact Fermi strip contribution \(L_0\sinh s+O_P(s^2)\). The artificial horocycle cut creates only finitely many local area errors of size \(O_P(s^2)\). The renormalized sum of side lengths and cusp horocycle logarithms is explicitly converted to the isometry-invariant chord-product formula. Ptolemy and AM–GM yield the quadrilateral sharp minimum and equality class. The triangle proof integrates an exact two-variable hyperbolic-area region and differentiates it. Numeric quadrature and independently sampled quadrilateral test points agree with these formulas. The experiments cannot certify the proof or an infinite claim.

**Originality — PASS, provisionally.** Exact-domain checks distinguish area-uniform point-to-boundary distance from Liouville-uniform geodesic chord length (Xu 2014; Bridgeman–Dumas 2007), from compact-body area/perimeter ratios (Gallego–Solanes 2001), and from decorated cross-ratio dynamics (Arnold–Fuchs–Izmestiev–Tabachnikov 2018). No inspected theorem establishes the \(2n s\log(1/s)\) and shape-dependent next coefficient together. The horocyclic and Ptolemy inputs are known and not presented as new. A broadly applicable prior boundary tube theorem might imply the leading term or more; this is a residual literature risk, not proof of novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Ideal hyperbolic polygons combine finite area with infinite boundary length, so the classical finite-perimeter linear tube expansion fails. The new finite renormalization recovers a genuine isometry-invariant shape parameter from a natural geometric statistic. The regular quadrilateral is the sharp optimizer of that parameter, while the ideal triangle provides a full distribution law and benchmark. This is an interpretable all-polygons result, not an arbitrary numerical parameter or a mere reformulation of a known single case.

**Closest prior work and remaining limits.** Gallego–Solanes treats compact convex domains; Xu treats crossing-geodesic chord distributions; Arnold et al. treat alternating decoration-independent perimeter; Lutz treats decorated tessellation geometry. Historical coverage remains incomplete. Remainders are nonuniform under degeneration, and no whole-distribution formula for higher polygons is claimed.
