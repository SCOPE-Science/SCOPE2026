# Scientific review: logarithmic inner-tube coefficient of ideal hyperbolic three-polyhedra

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claim fixes curvature \(-1\), a finite-sided convex ideal polyhedron, ambient distance to its true boundary, and volume-uniform sampling. A deep ideal-vertex horoball gives the exact product \(Q\times[z_0,\infty)\). Distance \(s\) to a vertical hyperbolic face is equivalent to horizontal Euclidean distance \(z\sinh s\). Every bounded polygon has initial inner-area erosion \(Lt-Ct^2\), where \(C=\sum\cot(\theta/2)\). The integral of the \(-Ct^2\) term against \(z^{-3}dz\) supplies \(-Cs^2\log(1/s)\); its post-cutoff tail is \(O(s^2)\). A compact remainder contributes its finite face area times \(s\), with \(O(s^2)\) edge-and-cut error. The vertex-figure angles equal interior dihedral angles, and each edge has two ideal endpoints. Face Gauss–Bonnet plus Euler gives total area \(2\pi(v-2)\). Tetrahedral Jensen and the ideal-simplex volume Hessian establish the sharp inequality. The executable checker compares genuine clipped eroded polygons with independent cusp integrals. It is corroboration, not proof.

## Originality

**PASS provisionally under the inspected comparison set.** Euclidean polynomial inner-tube theory supplies the local planar ingredient but not the noncompact hyperbolic integral producing a logarithm. Horospherical vertex figures and Schläfli variation do not determine the boundary-layer distribution. The exact ideal-simplex volume formula yields the known volume extremum, but not this logarithmic inner-tube coefficient. A related two-dimensional ideal-polygon setting has a different leading \(s\log(1/s)\) mechanism; it does not imply the three-dimensional second-order law. The closest published geometrical comparison deals with inner-parallel constant-curvature lenses, a different compact/horospherical lens class and conserved edge parameter, not finite-sided ideal cusp polyhedra. An earlier general hyperbolic polyhedral tube theorem might state an equivalent result under different terminology; priority remains provisional. The complete available Koçak–Ratiu article was subsequently inspected in extracted arXiv full text; its definitions and theorems explicitly concern Euclidean polytopes.

## Value

**PASS.** Every convex ideal polyhedron has finite area but infinite total edge length, so the usual finite-edge quadratic correction to a three-dimensional inner tube formula is undefined. The theorem gives a natural replacement with a rigorously determined logarithmic coefficient in terms of intrinsic dihedral data. It is a full arbitrary-ideal-polyhedron law rather than a single benchmark evaluation, and the tetrahedral extremal identifies the regular ideal tetrahedron as uniquely minimizing the unnormalized and volume-normalized coefficients. This supplies shape-sensitive data beyond face area and total volume and a quantitative model for cusp-induced failure of ordinary finite-polytope tube expansions.
