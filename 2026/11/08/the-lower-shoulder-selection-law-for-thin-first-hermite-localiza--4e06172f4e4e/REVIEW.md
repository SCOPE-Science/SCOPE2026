# Scientific review: thin first-Hermite localization annuli

## Correctness — PASS
The radial Hermite–Laguerre density is \(p_n(u)=e^{-u}u^n(u-n)^2/(n!u)\), so the disk-annulus concentration eigenvalues are its integrals over the specified area interval. Its total radial density is one by Poisson variance. A uniform two-term Stirling expansion at the annulus midpoint gives the symmetric principal profile \(x^2e^{-x^2/2}\) and the antisymmetric Hermite correction \(x^2e^{-x^2/2}(x^3-3x)/6\). Poisson tail bounds eliminate distant eigenvalues; nondegenerate maxima and the integer grid yield the stated one-sided expansions with remainder \(O_L(R^{-3/2})\) in eigenvalue scale. The difference is positive and has exact leading coefficient \(2L/(3e\sqrt\pi R)\). The secondary shoulder and Gaussian ratio follow independently. The standalone numerical checker reproduces normalization and the predicted sign/coefficient for four example annuli. No finite test is treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk
The polyanalytic quantization treatment of Keller and Luef (arXiv:1905.07741; https://doi.org/10.1007/s00041-021-09843-0) supplies the first-Hermite setting, not the lower-versus-upper distant-annulus shoulder selection or its signed inverse-radius gap. Knutsen's Gaussian-window unit-area ring calculation (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00041-020-09751-9, Section 3.1) involves a Poisson rather than squared-centered-Poisson density, so its maximizing index and asymptotic corrections do not imply this result. Abreu's Gaussian spectrogram maxima study (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00041-022-09979-7) is stochastic and does not classify this deterministic localization spectrum. A previously studied annulus with width proportional to the square root of its radius has different assumptions and its symmetric leading shoulders do not determine the signed fixed-width gap. Searches for aliases, annular Hermite spectra and first-Laguerre asymptotics found no equivalent implication. A specialized earlier thin-annulus expansion could still establish priority; an independent literature audit has not been performed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For an annulus of fixed physical time-frequency area moving far from the origin, the first excited Hermite window produces two competing spectral bands of equal leading height. The theorem selects the globally concentrating Hermite mode, determines an exact first nonzero difference between competing bands, and contrasts their strength with Gaussian-window localization. This identifies genuine spectral asymmetry obscured by the common leading approximation and is useful in non-Gaussian time-frequency concentration and polyanalytic Toeplitz theory. It does not claim an exact finite-radius transition or classify all higher Hermite windows.
