# Scientific review

### Correctness — PASS
The claim has an explicit and finite domain: the common-in-neighbor relation underlying the outward blocks of the quadratic-residue tournament on nineteen vertices. All 8246 faces were enumerated using two independent constructions. The matching has 4080 disjoint pairs, with 40014 directed face-cover incidences checked for cycles; exactly one critical vertex, four critical two-cells and eighty-one critical three-cells remain. The integer Morse boundary is computed from signed gradient substitutions and independently checked to have a primitive left null relation and a rank-three unimodular minor. Elementary integral cellular algebra and Hurewicz then give one two-sphere and 78 three-spheres. Full mod-two chain ranks agree. Risk: the proof is computer-assisted and merits independent implementation; the finite certificate is complete, not a sampling claim.

### Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
The primary Dowker literature develops general constructions and asymmetric cycle networks, without the exact Paley-nineteen block complex. The closest same-graph published calculation concerns **transitive subtournaments** in the directed flag complex, a different simplex condition, with different homotopy types; the corresponding published research record likewise concerns the flag construction. The historical design classification enumerates incidence designs, not the topological type of the simplicial complex generated by blocks. Equivalent terms and stronger general theorems were searched; no covering statement was found. Risk: unindexed work or a specialized design-complex calculation may exist, and the entire 1974 design-classification article was not reviewed line by line.

### Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural named symmetric design attached to a classical doubly regular Paley tournament, not an arbitrary vertex slice. Determining the integral homotopy type answers a concrete gap left by general Dowker theory and by the distinct directed-flag census. The coexistence of a unique two-sphere with 78 three-spheres is structurally informative; it is not forced by the design's block count or by mod-two Betti numbers alone. The result is deliberately a complete exact invariant of this canonical object, not an unsupported claim about all designs.
