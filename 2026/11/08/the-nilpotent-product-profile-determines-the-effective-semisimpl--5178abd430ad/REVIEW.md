# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is every finite nonzero associative ring with identity, all integers \(k\ge1\), and independent uniform samples. The radical is nilpotent and nilpotence of an element is equivalent to nilpotence of its residue. Uniform samples descend to independent uniform quotient samples. Artin–Wedderburn decomposes the quotient into full matrix rings. The conditional-rank argument reduces nilpotence of \(AB\) to that of its uniform restriction to \(\operatorname{im}A\); the classical nilpotent-matrix count gives conditional probability \(q^{-\operatorname{rank}A}\). Expected kernel size of a uniform random matrix product gives \(1-(1-q^{-n})^k\). Direct-product independence proves the formula. The largest-exponential-base expansion of \(1-p_k\) recovers the effective block sizes and multiplicities recursively. The bound at \(k=2\) has a unique maximizer \(R/J(R)\cong\mathbb F_2\). The proof treats \(k=1\), rank zero, all allowable field orders and the possibility of noncommutativity. Exact direct enumeration of 35 test instances is independent corroboration, not the universal proof.

## Originality — qualified PASS

The matrix-algebra identity for all product lengths is explicitly an earlier result of Kovács (1987); the nilpotent endomorphism count is classical, with a complete accessible proof in Leinster. Sarma and Subedi (2026) already give the two-factor statistic, general bounds, numerical-value information (Corollary 2.18) and triangular cases (Theorem 2.24). Chen and collaborators give adjacent two-factor matrix counts. These are *not* claimed as new. The present claim is the exact probability profile for **each arbitrary finite unital ring** together with a two-way classification of equality of entire profiles by the effective Wedderburn-block multiset, rather than the existing value-set result or one matrix-factor calculation. Searches by nilpotent-product probability, Jacobson radical, Wedderburn quotient, block parameters, ring-by-ring formulas and equivalence of full profiles did not reveal the combined claim. Failure of search is not priority proof. The inaccessible full Kovács (1987) article and possible earlier results phrased as moment-product identities remain explicit priority risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The full probability profile is a natural sequence of ring invariants introduced by a recent nilpotent-product statistic. The exact classification separates what the statistics detect—the unordered multiset of effective simple-block sizes—from what they cannot detect—the radical and individual field size versus matrix size. The theorem applies to every finite unital ring, including noncommutative rings, and includes a sharp finite-ring extremal criterion. It is a coherent structural answer, not a numerical experiment, an arbitrary restricted example, or a rebranding of the classical matrix formula.
