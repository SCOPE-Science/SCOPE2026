# The octahedral quotient and complete rational-point orbit spectrum of the exceptional genus-seventeen maximal curve

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_{121}=\mathbb F_{11}(\alpha)\), where \(\alpha^2=-1\). Let \(C/k\) be the smooth projective model of the curve introduced by Beelen, Montanucci and Niemann (2026):
\[
(y^2-1)^2=1-x^{12},\qquad
z^2=(1+\alpha)f(x)f(\alpha x),\qquad
f(t)=(t+1)(t^2+t+1).
\]
The full automorphism group \(A=\operatorname{Aut}_k(C)\), known to have order \(192\), fixes precisely the rational subfield \(k(\beta)\), where
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle \beta=\frac{(x^8-3x^4+1)^3}{(x^{12}-1)^2}.}
\]
The map \(\beta:C\to\mathbb P^1_k\) is a degree-\(192\) *tame Galois* cover. It has exactly three geometric branch values:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\beta&\text{ramification index}&\text{geometric points}&\text{residue degree over }k\\\hline
0&3&64&1\\
1&4&48&2\\
\infty&4&48&1
\end{array}
\]
Its complete image on rational points, including the exact fiber sizes, is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
b&\infty&0&4+4\alpha&4-4\alpha\\\hline
\#\{P\in C(k):\beta(P)=b\}&48&64&192&192.
\end{array}}
\]
There are no other rational points in any other quotient fiber. Thus \(A\) has exactly four orbits on \(C(k)\), of sizes \(48,64,192,192\); their sum is \(496\), the maximal-point count of this genus-\(17\) curve over \(\mathbb F_{121}\).

## Assumptions and scope
All fields, automorphisms, and rationality assertions are over \(k=\mathbb F_{121}\) unless explicitly described as geometric. The element \(\alpha\) is an arbitrary chosen root of \(t^2+1\), so replacing it by \(-\alpha\) interchanges the two unramified rational quotient values. The curve is the smooth projective normalization of the displayed affine equations. The source establishes its genus, maximality, the full order-\(192\) automorphism group, and a normal order-\(8\) subgroup fixing \(x\). A root appearing with multiplicity in a singular affine fiber must be counted as a place on the normalization, not as a repeated affine root.

## Proof
**1. The octahedral quotient of the \(x\)-line.** The source's two generators act on the \(x\)-line by
\[
\tau(x)=x^{-1},\qquad
\sigma(x)=\frac{x+7+7\alpha}{(7+4\alpha)x+1}.
\]
Their projective matrix group \(B\) has order \(24\), with element-order histogram \((1,9,8,6)\) for orders \((1,2,3,4)\). It is the octahedral group \(S_4\). Exact matrix closure over \(\mathbb F_{121}\) gives three nontrivial short orbits on \(\mathbb P^1(k)\), of sizes \(8,6,12\), characterized respectively by
\[
Q_8=x^8-3x^4+1=0,\qquad
x(x^4+1)=0\ \text{or }x=\infty,\qquad
Q_{12}=x^{12}-1=0.
\]
Each polynomial splits completely into the asserted number of distinct \(k\)-roots. The remaining four \(k\)-orbits each have size \(24\). The exact verifier enumerates the normalized projective matrices and all \(122\) points of \(\mathbb P^1(k)\). This enumeration is exhaustive, not statistical.

In characteristic \(11\), direct coefficient calculation proves
\[
Q_8^3+9x^4(x^4+1)^4=Q_{12}^2.
\]
The degree-\(24\) function \(\beta=Q_8^3/Q_{12}^2\) is invariant under both generators. One exact way to verify invariance is to evaluate the rational functions at all \(110\) nonpole elements of \(\mathbb P^1(k)\): the cross-multiplied difference has degree at most \(48\), so vanishing at \(110\) distinct points proves a rational-function identity. As its numerator and denominator are coprime and have degree \(24\), \([k(x):k(\beta)]=24=|B|\). Its ramification indices over \(0,1,\infty\) are \(3,4,2\), at \(8,6,12\) \(x\)-points respectively. The accumulated ramification is \(8(3-1)+6(4-1)+12(2-1)=46\), the complete genus-zero Riemann--Hurwitz total; hence no other geometric branch points occur.

**2. Pullback to the genus-seventeen curve.** The source proves that \(k(C)/k(x)\) is Galois of degree \(8\), with ramification index \(2\) exactly above the \(12\) zeros of \(x^{12}-1\). The full automorphism group has order \(192\), generated by that order-\(8\) subgroup together with lifts of \(\tau,\sigma\). Therefore
\[
[k(C):k(\beta)]=8\cdot24=192=|A|,
\]
so \(k(\beta)=k(C)^A\). Multiplication of tame ramification indices gives \(3\) over \(0\), \(4\) over \(1\), and \(2\cdot2=4\) over \(\infty\). The corresponding geometric fiber sizes are \(192/3=64\), \(192/4=48\), and \(48\), with no further ramification. The total different degree is \(64(3-1)+48(4-1)+48(4-1)=416\), yielding \(2g-2=-384+416=32\), in agreement with \(g=17\).

**3. Arithmetic of the short fibers.** The \(k\)-rational \(x\)-value \(9=-2\) lies over \(\beta=0\). The first equation has the four distinct roots \(4\pm2\alpha,-4\pm2\alpha\), and the second has the two roots \(\pm(1-\alpha)\). Thus the eight points above \(x=9\) are rational. The full automorphism group is transitive on the \(64\) points above \(\beta=0\), so all \(64\) are rational. At \(x=1\), above \(\beta=\infty\), the local ramified \(y\)-places correspond to \(y=\pm1\), and the two independent \(z\)-values are \(\pm\alpha\). The same transitivity gives \(48\) rational points above \(\infty\).

For the six-point \(x\)-orbit lying above \(\beta=1\), take \(x=0\). On the normalization of the intermediate biquadratic cover there are four \(k\)-rational places: two have leading terms \(y=\pm\sqrt2\), and two have \(y=\pm x^6/\sqrt2+O(x^{18})\), where \(\sqrt2=3\alpha\in k\). But the final quadratic equation specializes to \(z^2=1+\alpha\), which is nonsquare in \(k\), since \((1+\alpha)^{60}=-1\). Therefore all eight geometric points over \(x=0\) have residue degree \(2\). Transitivity makes the entire \(48\)-point branch fiber quadratic, with no \(k\)-points.

**4. The unramified rational fibers.** On the remaining \(96\) rational \(x\)-values, none of the two defining polynomials is ramified, so the product of the numbers of distinct \(k\)-roots in the quartic \(y\)-equation and quadratic \(z\)-equation counts the actual \(k\)-places. Exact enumeration finds \(48\) \(x\)-values with \(8\) rational lifts and \(48\) with none. Those \(48\) values divide into two full \(B\)-orbits of size \(24\): their quotient values are precisely \(4+4\alpha\) and \(4-4\alpha\). Thus each contributes \(24\cdot8=192\) rational points; there are no other unramified rational fibers. Since unramified points of a Galois \(A\)-cover have trivial stabilizer and \(A\) is defined over \(k\), these fibers are precisely two free \(A\)-orbits. Summing with the branch points gives \(48+64+192+192=496\), and completes the orbit census.

## Relationship to prior work
Beelen--Montanucci--Niemann construct \(C\), prove its genus and maximality, determine \(A\), establish the order-eight deck subgroup, and identify the first \(48\)-point orbit. Their automorphism calculations provide the two Möbius actions used here. The classical octahedral quotient map and its \((2,3,4)\) signature are well known, for instance in Beukers' exposition; no novelty is claimed for those abstract facts or for the classical invariant in isolation. The finding specializes this structure to the new genus-seventeen cover, identifies a closed quotient coordinate and its *complete arithmetic* rational-fiber and automorphism-orbit spectra. The cited sources do not state that complete spectrum.

## Limitations
This is an arithmetic classification for the specified genus-seventeen curve over \(\mathbb F_{121}\), not a general classification of maximal curves or octahedral covers. The proof relies on the published construction and automorphism theorem. The computation is exhaustive only because the finite base field and all necessary algebraic ramification arguments are explicitly handled; it makes no claim about analogous curves over other fields. The octahedral rational function is classical up to change of coordinates. Historical-priority comparison is not a formal proof of novelty, and independent specialist audit has not been performed.
