For arbitrary nonempty blocks \(A,B\) over disjoint alphabets, the maximal common subsequences of \((AB)^r\) and \((BA)^s\) consist of the shorter entire input when \(r\ne s\), and exactly the two overlap words \(B(AB)^{r-1}\) and \(A(BA)^{r-1}\) when \(r=s\). In the equal-power case every common subsequence embeds in one of those two words, whose length gap can be arbitrarily large even over a binary alphabet.
