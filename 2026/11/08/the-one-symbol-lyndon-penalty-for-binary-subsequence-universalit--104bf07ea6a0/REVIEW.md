# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact domain is the fixed ordered binary alphabet and all positive integers \(k\), with a separate \(k=1\) boundary. The greedy-arch lemma proves the scattered-universality index exactly; the proof then gives a strict length-\(2k\) obstruction for \(k\ge2\). At length \(2k+1\), the unique-double-one case and the initial-double-zero case exhaust all possible Lyndon words, and explicit comparisons exclude every other candidate. The two surviving words satisfy the defining properties for all \(k\). The finite verifier checks all words at both relevant lengths through \(k=8\) and separately tests literal subsequences through \(k=5\), but the quantifiers in the theorem rest on the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Primary materials examined include Barker et al. (arXiv:2003.04629, especially arch/circular universality), Adamson (arXiv:2304.04583, counting/ranking), Fleischmann et al. (arXiv:2202.07981, near-universality), Bannai et al. (arXiv:2201.06773, longest Lyndon subsequence), and Carpi et al. (arXiv:1406.5895, a different use of “universal Lyndon”). General enumeration or universality criteria do not directly supply the sharp fixed-order Lyndon threshold and all extremizers. Exact-claim, alias and stronger-coverage searches in research records and primary literature found no decisive covering statement. Bibliographic completeness and unindexed equivalents remain unresolved risks; a failed search is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A complete extremal classification at every universality level for a natural intersection of two well-studied word classes is a meaningful exact invariant. The one-symbol penalty and the rigidity of exactly two witnesses provide more than a numerical sample; the contrast with the ternary word `012021` shows that the obstruction is genuinely alphabet-sensitive. No broader theorem or independent certification is asserted.

## Limitations

The proof covers no alphabet larger than two and no enumeration at lengths above the optimum. Formal theorem-prover certification and independent scientific audit are not claimed. The standalone verifier checks finitely many words and serves only as computational corroboration.
