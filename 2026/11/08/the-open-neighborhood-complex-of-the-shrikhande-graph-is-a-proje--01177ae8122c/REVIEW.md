# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Both an exhaustive generating-simplex enumeration and a direct common-neighbor test produce exactly 809 faces. Complete free-face reductions using two opposite tie-breaking orders each perform 256 valid elementary collapses. The resulting finite two-dimensional CW models are independently reduced by 104 unimodular generator/two-cell cancellations; their remaining attaching words are respectively 35 trivial and 21 degree-two words, or 39 trivial and 17 degree-two words. A cellular change of two-cell basis shows that one degree-two disk gives \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) and the other 55 disks contribute two-spheres. Boundary ranks at three primes and Euler characteristic are separate checks. All calculations are regenerated by the embedded standalone verifier.

## Originality
PASS against the inspected evidence, with residual indexing risk. Shukla's full primary article [1] treats lower-degree or four-regular graph classes and not this six-regular graph. Closest public calculations concern *closed* Shrikhande neighborhoods [2], its ordinary independence complex [3], and high homology of the *different rook graph* [4]; none has the same face condition together with a covering theorem. A graph-like neighborhood theorem [5] requires edge-disjoint four-cycles, violated here. Searches checked the Shrikhande, biplane, open-neighborhood and graph-Hom aliases and explicit two-torsion. An equivalent but unindexed treatment is possible.

## Value
PASS. The result specifies a full integral homotopy type and detects a projective-plane two-torsion summand in the natural neighborhood complex of a canonical strongly regular graph. This contrasts sharply with sphere-wedge results for its closed-neighborhood and independence constructions. The homotopy classification is substantial beyond an Euler characteristic or mod-two rank and gives a concrete test case for torsion in neighborhood complexes.

## Scope and risks
The proof is finite and exact, but reproducibility depends on the bundled deterministic executable. The theorem is nonequivariant and not a general classification; graph-Hom descriptions were examined only for implication, not used as verification certificates. Bibliographic coverage cannot establish absolute uniqueness, and independent external audit has not yet occurred.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
