# The ordinary cut filtration of the Grötzsch graph: a Moore-space stage and mixed sphere wedges

## Finding
Let \(G=\operatorname{Myc}(C_5)\) be the eleven-vertex Grötzsch graph, and let \(\Delta_k(G)\) be its **ordinary** cut complex, generated by complements of disconnected induced \(k\)-vertex sets. For all integers \(k\geq1\), its complete integral homotopy spectrum is
\[
\Delta_k(G)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigvee^{10}S^7,&k=2,\\
M(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,6),&k=3,\\
\bigvee^4 S^5\vee\bigvee^5 S^6,&k=4,\\
\bigvee^6 S^4\vee\bigvee^5 S^5,&k=5,\\
\bigvee^{14}S^3,&k=6,\\
\{\mathrm{point}\},&k\in\{7,8\},\\
\varnothing,&k=1\text{ or }k\geq9.
\end{cases}
\]
Here \(M(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,6)\) is the mapping cone of a degree-two map \(S^6\to S^6\), equivalently a fivefold suspension of the real projective plane. In particular,
\[
\widetilde H_6(\Delta_3(G);\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\qquad \widetilde H_j(\Delta_3(G);\mathbb Z)=0\quad(j\ne6).
\]
The \(k=2\) case follows already from the published connected-triangle-free-graph theorem and is included to give the complete filtration, not claimed as new. The nontrivial new stages are \(k=3,4,5,6\), especially the integral Moore-space stage.

## Assumptions and scope
Label the vertices \(v_i=i\), \(u_i=5+i\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\), and \(w=10\). Put edges \(v_iv_{i+1}\), \(v_iu_{i-1}\), \(v_iu_{i+1}\), and \(u_iw\), with indices modulo five. These are exactly twenty edges. There are no triangles; the graph is the Mycielskian of \(C_5\), the canonical smallest triangle-free four-chromatic graph. Every induced subgraph and every cut complex below uses this fixed finite undirected graph.

A \(k\)-subset \(A\subseteq V(G)\) is *disconnected* if the nonempty induced graph \(G[A]\) is disconnected. For \(k\geq2\), \(\Delta_k(G)\) is the downward closure of all complements \(V(G)\setminus A\) with \(|A|=k\) and \(G[A]\) disconnected. The void convention applies when no such set exists, and also for \(k=1\).

## Proof
**Exact face construction.** Enumerate all \(\binom{11}{k}\) subsets of size \(k\) using the graph above. For each disconnected subset, include all nonempty subsets of its complement. An independent membership routine, for each of the \(2^{11}-1\) nonempty vertex subsets, examines disconnected \(k\)-subsets of its complement directly. The two routines agree at every level. The nonempty face counts for \(k=2,3,4,5,6,7,8\) are respectively \(2015,1915,1634,1124,567,171,21\); the numbers of disconnected generating sets are \(35,110,180,176,106,35,5\). For \(k\geq9\) no generating disconnected set exists.

**Acyclic matchings.** On the nonempty face poset, process the vertices in numerical order. At stage \(i\), pair every still-unmatched face \(F\) not containing \(i\) with \(F\cup\{i\}\) if the latter is still unmatched. Within each stage, process faces in increasing bit-mask order. The standalone verifier constructs the entire matching and reverses matched edges in the Hasse diagram. A full topological-sort check proves acyclicity, rather than assuming it from the staged construction. The numbers of pairs and critical cells are:

| \(k\) | Matched pairs | Critical cells by dimension |
|---|---:|---|
| \(2\) | 1002 | one \(0\)-cell, ten \(7\)-cells |
| \(3\) | 954 | one \(0\)-cell, three \(6\)-cells, three \(7\)-cells |
| \(4\) | 809 | one \(0\)-cell, seven \(5\)-cells, eight \(6\)-cells |
| \(5\) | 554 | one \(0\)-cell, eight \(4\)-cells, seven \(5\)-cells |
| \(6\) | 275 | one \(0\)-cell, fifteen \(3\)-cells, one \(4\)-cell |
| \(7\) | 85 | one \(0\)-cell |
| \(8\) | 10 | one \(0\)-cell |

**Integral attaching degrees.** Simplicial orientations use increasing vertex order. If a face \(F\) is paired upward with \(U\), replace \(F\) in a signed chain using \(\partial U=0\) and the incidence \([U:F]=\pm1\). If \(F\) is paired downward, its projection to the reduced Morse chain is zero. Recursive elimination of these paired cells terminates by acyclicity and yields the exact integral Morse differential. For \(k=3\), the differential from the three critical \(7\)-cells to the three critical \(6\)-cells is
\[
D_3=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&-1\\-1&-1&0\\0&-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its absolute determinant is \(2\) and it has a unit two-by-two minor, so its Smith invariants are \((1,1,2)\). For \(k=4\), the seven-by-eight differential has rational rank \(3\) and a rank-three minor of determinant \(-1\). For \(k=5\), the eight-by-seven differential has rank \(2\) and a rank-two minor of determinant \(1\). For \(k=6\), the fifteen-by-one differential has precisely one nonzero entry, equal to \(1\). Every nonzero Smith invariant in these last three cases is therefore a unit; the exact matrices, their ranks, and explicit unit minors are regenerated in the verifier. At \(k=2,7,8\), there are no adjacent positive-dimensional critical cells.

**Homotopy reconstruction.** Forman's discrete-Morse theorem produces a CW complex with precisely these critical cells. In each nontrivial case its positive-dimensional cells occur in one or two consecutive dimensions \(d,d+1\), with \(d\geq3\) and a single zero-cell. Since \(\pi_d(\bigvee^rS^d)\cong\mathbb Z^r\), the integer attaching-degree matrix classifies the attaching maps at this stage. Integral elementary basis changes are realized by homotopy self-equivalences and handle slides. The Smith form \((1,1,2)\) gives the degree-two Moore mapping cone; the other unit invariants cancel corresponding cell pairs, leaving exactly the asserted sphere wedges. The critical-zero-cell-only cases are contractible, while absent facets give the void cases. This establishes **integral homotopy types**, not just ranks over a field.

## Verification
The included standalone script `verify_grotzsch_cuts.py` uses only the Python standard library and recomputes all data from the eleven graph vertices and twenty edges. It checks the independent face-membership algorithms, complete acyclicity over respectively \(10952,10127,8081,4916,2060,465,35\) Hasse incidences, exact signed Morse matrices and their integral determinant/minor witnesses, and direct unreduced mod-two simplicial boundary ranks converted to reduced homology. The exact recorded output is in `verification_output.txt`. Both files belong to this result; no external cached computations are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced the ordinary \(k\)-cut complexes and determined many classes of examples. Their connected-triangle-free theorem implies the \(k=2\) stage here, since \(G\) has eleven vertices and twenty edges, giving \(20-11+1=10\) seven-spheres. Their listed other complete classifications concern different graph families; they do not imply the Grötzsch \(k=3,4,5,6\) stages. Results for triangle-free graphs with no four-cycles, or with edge-disjoint four-cycles, do not apply: \(G\) has exactly ten four-cycles and each of its twenty edges belongs to two of them.

The 2025 paper on realizations and uniqueness of cut complexes treats reconstructibility and recognition rather than integral homotopy classification; its general reconstruction theorem does not supply these attaching degrees. Papers on *total* cut complexes use independent-set complements, a different face condition for \(k\geq3\). The Grötzsch graph has also been studied through other graph-associated topological constructions, including matching and Čech complexes; their homology does not determine the ordinary cut filtration.

## Limitations
No corresponding general theorem for all Mycielskians, all four-chromatic graphs, or arbitrary graphs with overlapping four-cycles is proved. The homotopy equivalences are nonequivariant; no full integral automorphism-module classification is asserted. Literature novelty has been checked against indexed and accessible sources but cannot exclude unindexed or presently inaccessible equivalent work. The computations are exhaustive only for the fixed eleven-vertex graph and finite range stated, not a proof by extrapolation.
