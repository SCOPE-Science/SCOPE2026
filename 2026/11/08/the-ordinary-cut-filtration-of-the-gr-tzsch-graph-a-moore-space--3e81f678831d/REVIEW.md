# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem concerns the specifically labeled eleven-vertex Mycielskian of the five-cycle, using **ordinary** disconnected-set cut complexes rather than independent-set total cuts. The standalone program reconstructs graph edges, all facet complements, all faces by two independent predicates, and every unmatched/matched face incidence. Full directed-acyclicity checks cover all seven nonvoid nontrivial levels. Signed integral gradient-path elimination yields the explicit degree-two determinant at the three-cut stage and unimodular remaining nonzero Smith factors at levels four, five and six. The absence of lower positive-dimensional critical cells makes the homotopy classification follow from the degree-classification of maps into a wedge of equal-dimensional spheres. Separate direct mod-two boundary ranks agree.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
Bayer et al. (arXiv:2304.13675) already imply the two-cut case; that case is disclosed as prior. Their family table and specific triangle-free theorem do not imply the higher stages. Shen et al. (arXiv:2512.12933) address realization, reconstruction and recognition, not the integral Moore or mixed-sphere homotopy types. Related published studies of other complexes on the Grötzsch graph concern different face conditions, and the no-square/edge-disjoint-square results are inapplicable because every graph edge lies on two distinct four-cycles. Searches under Grötzsch, Groetzsch, Mycielski, Mycielskian, Alexander dual, Moore space, three-cut, four-cut and integral two-torsion found no claim implying the result. The closest nonidentical scientific records and residual indexing risks are documented in the structured review.

## Value — PASS
This complete classification is motivated by the canonical smallest triangle-free four-chromatic graph, the first substantive Mycielski chromatic obstruction. Its three-cut complex is not simply a wedge of spheres but a high-dimensional degree-two Moore space; moreover, the four- and five-cut stages have two nonzero free homology degrees. The overlap of four-cycles at every graph edge gives an explicit contrast with sphere-wedge theorems requiring edge-disjoint squares. The result provides a reproducible integral boundary example, without claiming a classification for other Mycielskians or arbitrary overlapping-square graphs.
