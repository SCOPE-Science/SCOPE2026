For a periodically varying juvenile–adult population with three fixed seasonal environments, the sign of the oriented volume \(\det[(a_i,b_i,s_i)_{i=1}^3]\) exactly determines which of the two temporal orientations has greater asymptotic growth, independently of the overall fertility multiplier. Three seasons is the first period where temporal reordering can change the Floquet growth rate, and an explicit positive-parameter example grows under one order while declining under the reverse order.
