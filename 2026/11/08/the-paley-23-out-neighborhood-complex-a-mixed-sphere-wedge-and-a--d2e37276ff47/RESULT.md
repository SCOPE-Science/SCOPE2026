# The Paley-23 out-neighborhood complex: a mixed sphere wedge and affine homology character

## Finding
Let \(Q=\{1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12,13,16,18\}\subset\mathbb F_{23}\) be the nonzero quadratic residues, and orient the complete graph on \(\mathbb F_{23}\) by \(u\to v\) exactly when \(v-u\in Q\). Define \(X\) to be the simplicial complex whose nonempty faces are sets with a common incoming source: \(\sigma\in X\) if and only if there exists \(b\in\mathbb F_{23}\) such that \(b\to v\) for all \(v\in\sigma\). Equivalently, \(X=\langle b+Q:b\in\mathbb F_{23}\rangle\), the complex generated by the \(23\) translated quadratic-residue blocks. This is the out-neighborhood complex, not the directed flag complex.

**Theorem.** There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
X\simeq\bigvee^{209}S^3\vee S^4.
\]
Hence \(\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{209}\), \(\widetilde H_4(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. The complex is pure of dimension ten but not shellable.

The affine group \(G=\mathbb F_{23}\rtimes Q\), acting by \(v\mapsto av+b\), preserves \(X\) and has order \(253\). Its rational third-homology character is precisely
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl((a,b)\mid H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\bigr)=
\begin{cases}
209,&(a,b)=(1,0),\\
2,&a=1,\ b\neq0,\\
0,&a\neq1.
\end{cases}
\]
The \(G\)-action on \(H_4(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) is trivial. Consequently each of \(H_3(X;\mathbb Q)^G\) and \(H_4(X;\mathbb Q)^G\) is one-dimensional.

## Assumptions and scope
The simplicial complex is an ordinary finite **abstract** complex on the vertex set \(\{0,\ldots,22\}\), with all nonempty subsets of each translated block included. The empty face is included for face bookkeeping but not as a topological cell. Integers, not floating point or a single field, govern the attaching-map calculation. The proof gives an ordinary integral homotopy equivalence and a rational symmetry character; it does not assert an equivariant sphere-wedge splitting.

The blocks constitute the canonical symmetric \(2\)-\((23,11,5)\) quadratic-residue difference-set design: each is of size \(11\), there are \(23\), and every unordered pair of points lies in precisely five blocks. The attached checker also verifies these properties from the defining residues.

## Proof
**1. Finite complex.** Represent \(\sigma\) by its \(23\)-bit mask. Generate every submask of each of the \(23\) translated blocks; independently generate every combination of block vertices. Both methods give \(42{,}298\) faces including the empty face, with ordinary-dimensional face vector
\[
(23,253,1771,6325,10373,10626,7590,3795,1265,253,23).
\]
All facets have eleven vertices; hence the dimension is ten.

**2. Complete acyclic matchings.** The certificate successively toggles each vertex in increasing order and matches each surviving face \(\sigma\) not containing that vertex to \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) whenever both survive, discarding both simultaneously from the next stage. It starts with all nonempty faces and leaves exactly \(21{,}039\) pairs and critical dimensions \(\{0:1,3:213,4:5\}\). Every one of the \(244{,}398\) Hasse incidences is directed upward if matched and downward otherwise, and exhaustive topological sorting visits all \(42{,}297\) nonempty faces, proving the matching acyclic. Repeating the entire construction in reverse vertex order independently leaves \(21{,}041\) pairs and \(\{0:1,3:211,4:3\}\) critical cells, again with no directed cycle.

**3. Exact integral attaching degrees.** Use increasing-vertex signs for every oriented simplex boundary. In each acyclic matching, recursively substitute the paired dimension-three faces in the boundaries of the critical four-faces; discard dimension-three faces already paired with dimension-two faces. This recursion follows all signed gradient paths and terminates by the verified acyclicity. The first Morse boundary \(\partial_4\colon\mathbb Z^5\to\mathbb Z^{213}\) has exact rank four: a \(4\times4\) minor on critical-row indices \(14,15,19,31\) has determinant \(\pm1\); an exact rational row elimination has rank four, hence provides the complementary upper bound. The second boundary \(\mathbb Z^3\to\mathbb Z^{211}\) has rank two with a \(2\times2\) unimodular minor on critical-row indices \(4,26\), and exact rank two. All nonzero integral Smith factors in both boundaries are units.

There are no critical cells in dimensions one or two. The Morse three-skeleton is a wedge of three-spheres. Its four-cell attaching maps are classified by \(\pi_3(\bigvee S^3)\cong\bigoplus\mathbb Z\), namely the same signed degree vectors as the Morse boundary columns. A unimodular change of three-sphere basis and four-cell basis reduces the first attaching matrix to four identity columns and one zero column. The four identity pairs cancel by homotopy equivalence, leaving \(209\) three-spheres and one four-sphere. The second matching independently yields the identical conclusion. This determines the integral homotopy type, not merely Betti numbers or finite-field homology.

**4. Symmetry character.** The affine transformations with \(a\in Q\) permute the translated blocks. Every nontrivial translation is a \(23\)-cycle on vertices and fixes no nonempty face (faces have at most eleven vertices), so its Lefschetz number is zero. For each nonidentity multiplier \(a\in Q\), the only setwise invariant nonempty faces are the vertex \(\{0\}\) and the ten-simplex \(Q\): the multiplier has orbits \(\{0\},Q,-Q\), and the action on \(Q\) is an eleven-cycle, with orientation sign \(+1\). The resulting Lefschetz number is two. Every affine element with \(a\neq1\) is conjugate inside \(G\) to its multiplier, so it also has Lefschetz number two. The verified face vector gives Euler characteristic \(\chi(X)=-207\), the identity Lefschetz number. Since \(G\) has odd order and \(H_4(X;\mathbb Z)\) has rank one, its action on the latter is trivial (no nontrivial homomorphism from an odd-order group to \(\{\pm1\}\)). Thus the Lefschetz identity \(L(g)=1-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_3)+1\) produces traces \(209,2,0\) in the three stated cases. Character averaging gives \((209+22\cdot2+230\cdot0)/253=1\) invariant third-homology dimension. Trivial fourth-homology action gives invariant dimension one there.

**5. Non-shellability.** A pure shellable ten-dimensional simplicial complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of ten-spheres (possibly contractible). The computed nonzero third-dimensional reduced homology therefore rules out shellability.

## Relationship to prior work
Dochtermann and Singh introduced the relevant digraph out-neighborhood complex and related it to a directed-edge Hom complex; their article develops general theorems and low-order tournament examples but does not give this Paley-23 classification. Govc, Levi and Smith study flag tournaplexes, whose simplices are oriented complete subgraphs, and directionality filtrations, not the present common-out-neighbor complex. Known Paley-tournament **directed flag** calculations, including order 19 and order 31, concern different simplicial objects. The order-23 construction here can also be described as the simplicial complex generated by the blocks of the quadratic-residue Hadamard symmetric \(2\)-design; both formulations were considered in the overlap check. Existing lower-order out-neighborhood calculations do not imply this mixed-dimensional integral homotopy and its affine character.

## Limitations
The statement is specific to the canonical \(23\)-vertex quadratic-residue tournament, not a classification for all Paley orders or all symmetric designs. The homotopy splitting is nonequivariant; only the rational affine homology character is established. The result has undergone exact reproducibility checks and self-review but no independent audit. An equivalent theorem in literature not returned or not accessible during the bibliographic comparison remains possible; a negative search is not proof of novelty.
