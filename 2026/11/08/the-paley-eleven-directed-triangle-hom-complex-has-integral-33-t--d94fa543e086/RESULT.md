# The Paley-eleven directed triangle Hom complex has integral \(33\)-torsion

## Finding
Let \(T\) be the Paley tournament on \(\mathbb Z/11\mathbb Z\), with an arc \(a\to b\) precisely when \(b-a\) is a nonzero square modulo \(11\); the square set is \(Q=\{1,3,4,5,9\}\). Let \(X=\operatorname{Hom}(\overrightarrow C_3,T)\) be the directed Hom complex in the sense of multihomomorphisms. Then \(X\) is a connected, two-dimensional regular CW complex, with \(165\) vertices, \(495\) edges and \(330\) two-cells (\(165\) triangles and \(165\) squares), and
\[
\widetilde H_i(X;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/33\mathbb Z,&i=1,\\
0,&i\ne 1.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, \(X\) has independent three- and eleven-primary first-homology torsion, even though its rational Betti numbers agree with those of a circle. Every one-cell lies in exactly two or three two-cells; there are \(330\) and \(165\) one-cells of these respective kinds. The natural group generated by the affine target symmetries, cyclic permutation of the source coordinates, and simultaneous negation/source reflection acts trivially on the **free rational first-homology line**; no claim about its torsion action is made.

## Assumptions and scope
The source \(\overrightarrow C_3\) has directed edges \(0\to1\to2\to0\). A cell of \(X\) is a triple \((A,B,C)\) of nonempty subsets of \(\mathbb Z/11\mathbb Z\) satisfying \(A\times B\subset E(T)\), \(B\times C\subset E(T)\), and \(C\times A\subset E(T)\). It is the product \(\Delta^A\times\Delta^B\times\Delta^C\) and has dimension \(|A|+|B|+|C|-3\). The tournament is loopless, so \(A,B,C\) are automatically pairwise disjoint. Each simplex factor uses increasing residue order, with products ordered \(A,B,C\), to fix cellular orientations. All homology is unreduced except where a tilde is displayed. No assumption about a triangulation or an unspecified geometric metric is involved.

## Proof
**Complete cells and connectivity.** For each nonempty bit-mask \(A\), intersect the out-neighborhoods of its vertices to obtain every possible nonempty \(B\). For each such \(B\), intersect the out-neighborhoods of \(B\) with the in-neighborhoods of \(A\) to obtain every possible nonempty \(C\). This exhausts exactly the defining multihomomorphisms, and produces \((165,495,330)\) cells in degrees \((0,1,2)\), none in higher degrees. There are \(55\) cells of each ordered size pattern \((3,1,1)\), \((1,3,1)\), \((1,1,3)\), \((2,2,1)\), \((2,1,2)\), and \((1,2,2)\). The first three patterns are triangular; the latter three are squares. Separately, begin with the \(165\) directed triangle homomorphisms and repeatedly adjoin one legal target vertex to one coordinate. This second enumeration reaches the same \(990\) cells. A spanning-tree search over one-cells reaches all \(165\) zero-cells.

**Integral chain argument.** The cellular complex is \(C_2\xrightarrow{\partial_2}C_1\xrightarrow{\partial_1}C_0\), with ranks \((330,495,165)\). For increasing vertices \(v_0<\cdots<v_m\) in each factor, deleting \(v_j\) has sign \((-1)^j\), multiplied by the parity of the total factor dimensions preceding that factor. The verifier explicitly checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) on all \(330\) top cells.

The matrix of \(\partial_2\) has \(330\) rows (indexed by one-cells) meeting exactly two columns with coefficients \(\pm1\); the other \(165\) rows meet three. Regard the double-incidence rows as signed edges on the \(330\) oriented two-cells. Their graph has exactly five connected components of size \(66\). Within each, select a spanning tree. Its \(325\) incidence rows give integral unit elementary reductions, leaving five component generators \(x_0,\ldots,x_4\); the five unused double-incidence rows are redundant. Every triple-incidence row reduces, up to sign, to one of the following five rows, and each occurs:
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0&2\\
0&2&1&0&0\\
0&0&2&0&1\\
2&0&0&-1&0\\
0&-1&0&2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The relations \(2x_0-x_3=0\), \(-x_1+2x_3=0\), \(2x_1+x_2=0\), and \(2x_2+x_4=0\) give \(x_3=2x_0\), \(x_1=4x_0\), \(x_2=-8x_0\), and \(x_4=16x_0\). The remaining relation \(x_0+2x_4=0\) becomes \(33x_0=0\). Equivalently \(\det M=33\), and a four-by-four minor has determinant \(\pm1\). Thus the Smith invariants of \(\partial_2\) are \(329\) ones and one \(33\); in particular \(\partial_2\) is injective. Note that this gives **torsion**, not a primitive embedding of \(C_2\) into \(C_1\).

The one-skeleton is connected, so \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1=164\). Consequently \(H_2=0\) and the free rank of \(H_1\) is \(495-164-330=1\). Because \(\operatorname{im}\partial_2\subset\ker\partial_1\) and \(C_0\) is torsion-free, all torsion of \(\operatorname{coker}\partial_2\) lies in \(H_1\). The Smith invariant \(33\) therefore gives \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/33\mathbb Z\), with no higher cells or homology.

**Symmetry on the free part.** All \(55\) affine maps \(z\mapsto az+b\), where \(a\in Q\), preserve the target tournament; source coordinate rotation has order \(3\). Each acts on the rank-one free homology by \(+1\), since its order is odd. The map \((A,B,C)\mapsto(-A,-C,-B)\) also preserves every cell. Its signed cellular traces in dimensions zero, one and two are \((5,-5,-10)\), giving Lefschetz number \(0\). Since \(H_0\cong\mathbb Q\), \(H_2=0\), its action on \(H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\) has trace \(1\). These maps generate the stated natural symmetry group, which consequently fixes the free rational line.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing this document. The standard-library-only program reconstructs all \(990\) cells in two different ways; checks all oriented cellular boundaries and the connected one-skeleton; enumerates the signed \(330\)-vertex two-coface graph and a \(325\)-row spanning forest; verifies the entire set of residual relations, the determinant \(33\), and a unit four-by-four minor; and independently checks \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2\) over the fields of \(2,3,5,11,13\) elements. These ranks are respectively \((330,329,330,329,330)\). It also checks the affine, rotation and reflection-converse cellular maps and their signed traces. The expected terminal marker is `PAL11_HOM_INTEGRAL_CERTIFICATE_OK`. `verify-output.txt` records a successful execution; it is not a replacement for running the proof checks.

## Relationship to prior work
Dochtermann and Singh (arXiv:2108.10948, first public 24 August 2021) define directed Hom complexes and examine nontransitive tournaments, notably exhibiting a Möbius-strip complex for a seven-vertex tournament. Their general target-tournament result concerns transitive tournaments; its hypotheses do not hold for the Paley tournament here. Their work does not give the order-eleven cellular calculation or the integral torsion invariant.

The published calculation *The directed Hom complex \(\operatorname{Hom}(\overrightarrow C_3,R_9)\) has the homotopy type of a circle* treats the regular cyclic nine-vertex tournament, which is not the Paley-eleven tournament. It gives a circle without torsion and neither statement implies the other. Directed flag complexes, directed neighborhood complexes, and tournament coloring bounds use distinct functors or ask different questions; results about those objects are not automatically results about this directed Hom complex. Targeted searches using the quadratic-residue, eleven-vertex, multihomomorphism, cellular chain and \(33\)-torsion descriptions did not locate an equivalent full statement. This is evidence against known overlap, not a proof of global novelty.

## Limitations
The result identifies integral homology and its natural rational free-line symmetry, **not** the fundamental group, complete homotopy type, higher cover topology, integral torsion-module representation, or a family-wide formula for Paley tournaments. The finite enumeration is exhaustive for this eleven-vertex object but is not an infinite-family proof. Although the most directly pertinent accessible publications and records were compared, an unindexed or inaccessible equivalent result may exist. All exact finite checks can be rerun from the embedded program; no independent external validation is claimed.
