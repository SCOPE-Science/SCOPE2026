# Scientific review — directed triangle Hom complex of the Paley-eleven tournament

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed invariant concerns the precisely defined regular CW complex of directed multihomomorphisms from the oriented three-cycle into the eleven-vertex quadratic-residue tournament. Complete bit-mask enumeration yields exactly \((165,495,330)\) cells, none of dimension greater than two; a separate constructive expansion reaches the same cells. Signed product-cell boundaries satisfy \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), and all vertices are connected through one-cells. The precise integral reduction has \(325\) unit spanning-forest relations and a residual five-generator relation matrix of determinant \(33\) with a unit four-by-four minor. Its Smith invariants are \(329\) units and \(33\); the resulting homology is \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/33\mathbb Z\) with \(H_2=0\). Independent modular ranks at primes \(2,3,5,11,13\) provide targeted checks. The attached standard-library verifier constructs the cell complex anew and checks the full relation reduction, rather than trusting a stored rank table. The argument does not claim a homotopy equivalence to a circle.

## Value — PASS
Paley-eleven is the canonical highly symmetric prime-order quadratic-residue tournament after Paley-seven, and a direct target for the foundational theory's nontransitive cases. The occurrence of both three- and eleven-primary torsion in first homology of a two-dimensional directed Hom complex is a substantive structural distinction from the nearby one-dimensional and circle examples, not a textbook Euler-characteristic deduction. The signed incidence decomposition into five connected blocks of \(66\) two-cells explains the torsion through a compact, independently reproducible relation matrix. The exact natural invariant is useful without conjecturing a general family theorem. Its limits are strict: no fundamental group, full homotopy type or integral symmetry-module classification has been computed.
