# Scientific review: two-row Monge inballs

**Correctness — PASS.** In the sum-one affine metric the projected coordinate normal has length \(\sqrt{1-(2m)^{-1}}\), and each adjacent Monge normal has length two. The exact ball-containment system reduces the problem to minimizing the \(\ell_1\)-norm of a descending real sequence with adjacent gaps at least two. Pairing opposite ranks proves the sharp lower bound \(\lfloor m^2/2\rfloor\). Equality forces all gaps to be minimal and yields the unique odd case or the even one-parameter segment. The coordinate-facet and Monge-facet contact counts follow directly. A standalone full-LP numerical comparison and finite exact calculations corroborate the proof. Risk: none detected for the stated metric, dimension range, and normalization; finite tests alone would not suffice.

**Originality — PASS under accessible evidence, with priority limitations.** The 2023 facet/volume source does not assert an inball theorem, the 2026 motivating source is explicitly concerned with lattice paths, Ehrhart series and volume, and the available square-Monge incenter theorem is restricted to equal row and column dimensions. Its \(2\times2\) instance is excluded. A separate prior two-row centroid theorem is not an inradius theorem. Targeted mathematical-record and literature queries for rectangles, equivalent Chebyshev formulations, and scalar \(\ell_1\)-variation reductions did not expose an implication covering all \(2\times m\) inball loci. Risks: an unindexed classical reduction might yield the same result, and the 2026 motivating manuscript was not accessible in full during this review.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A natural infinite family of Monge cost-matrix polytopes receives an exact intrinsic metric invariant and complete equality classification. The parity-controlled change from a unique center to a positive-length line segment, plus a precise facet-contact jump at the endpoints, is structural information not supplied by facet enumeration or volume formulas. This is a meaningful non-square case complementary to the square theorem, not a change of notation or a single numerical example. The claim does not purport to solve arbitrary rectangular dimensions.
