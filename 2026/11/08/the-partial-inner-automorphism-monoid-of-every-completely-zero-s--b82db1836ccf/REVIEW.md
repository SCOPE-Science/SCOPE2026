# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Each generator is either the bottom map fixing zero, a full identity, or a bijection between zero-adjoined maximal subgroups; the explicit computations in RESULT.md classify all domains and image maps, and the converse choice of Rees coordinates realizes every inner conjugation. Distinct local subgroups intersect only in zero, forcing the Brandt product. Both the singleton case and infinite cases are addressed. A separate finite checker enumerated 11 models and verified generator-set equality, every composition and expected order. Residual limitation: finite checks alone do not certify the general theorem, which rests on the symbolic argument.

## Originality
PASS, qualified. In arXiv:2605.00041v1, §3 contains the definition and §4 treats completely simple (no-zero) Rees semigroups; Problem 7.5 asks specifically for the zero-Rees classification. The proof above identifies a zero-preserving Brandt ideal and its externally adjoined identity, and computes exactly what group and sandwich data survive. The inverse Brandt specialization is covered by the prior central-congruence theorem for inverse semigroups (Remark 3.11 of arXiv:2605.00041v1); that case is not separately new. The older arXiv:2301.04252 contains prior natural-conjugacy results, including Rees-type cases, but the inspected sections do not state the Brandt-monoid classification. The closest accessible research-record hits concern unrelated partial isometries and elementary matrix gradings. These searches do not prove global historical priority, and an unrecognized stronger prior theorem remains a risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. A full classification answers a specifically posed recent problem for all completely zero-simple Rees semigroups, including infinite groups and index sets. The result has a consequential structural feature: two nonisomorphic semigroups with different sandwich support geometry can have isomorphic partial-inner monoids. This is a natural classification and information-loss theorem, not an arbitrary finite sample or a renaming of the zero-free result.

## Scope and independent scrutiny
Only regular Rees zero-matrix semigroups are covered; nonregular matrices and finite-presentation questions are not. Independent mathematical validation and independent historical-priority checking have not been performed. The 2026 question has an older theoretical antecedent from 2023, which is acknowledged rather than being assigned a fictitious new origin.
