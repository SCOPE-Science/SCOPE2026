# The path uniquely minimizes random greedy independent-set expectation on finite trees

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(n\ge 4\) be an integer, let \(T\) be a finite simple tree on \(n\) vertices, and let \(P_n\) be the \(n\)-vertex path. Inspect the vertices of a graph in a uniformly random order, accepting a vertex exactly when none of its previously accepted neighbors is present. Write \(I(G)\) for the resulting maximal independent-set size and \(m(G)=\mathbb E I(G)\). Then

\[
m(T)\ge m(P_n),\qquad
m(T)=m(P_n)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ T\cong P_n.
\]

The strictness follows from an accompanying structural statement: the *path-shattering functional* \(\nu\), defined below, increases **strictly under every proper Kelmans–Csikvári (KC) transformation of a tree**. The expectation \(m\) itself is not asserted to be KC-monotone.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. Their random vertex order is uniform over all permutations, equivalently generated by independent identically distributed continuous arrival times. Define \(\alpha_0=0\) and \(\alpha_k=m(P_k)\) for \(k\ge1\). The known exact formula is

\[
\alpha_k=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-2)^r(k-r)}{(r+1)!}
\quad(k\ge1),\qquad
\Delta^2\alpha_k:=\alpha_{k+2}-2\alpha_{k+1}+\alpha_k
=\frac{(-2)^{k+1}}{(k+2)!}.
\]

For a tree \(T\), let \(N_T[v]\) denote the closed neighborhood of \(v\), and let \(\mathcal C(T-N_T[v])\) be the collection of connected components of the resulting induced forest. Set

\[
\nu_v(T)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal C(T-N_T[v])}\alpha_{|C|},
\qquad \nu(T)=\sum_{v\in V(T)}\nu_v(T).
\]

A *bare path* \(x=u_0,u_1,\ldots,u_\ell=y\) has internal vertices of degree two. Its KC transformation transfers every edge from \(y\) to a neighbor outside the path, replacing that edge by an edge from the neighbor to \(x\). It is *proper* if neither endpoint is a leaf (equivalently, the move creates one additional leaf). Write the resulting tree as \(T'\).

## Proof

**Step 1: Published first-vertex reduction.** Conditioning on the first accepted vertex gives the exact identity

\[
m(T)=1+\frac1n\sum_{v\in V(T)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal C(T-N_T[v])}m(C).
\tag{1}
\]

Krivelevich, Mészáros, Michaeli, and Shikhelman (KMM&S), Section 8 of [1], prove that \(\alpha_k\) is nondecreasing and subadditive, derive its displayed formula, and establish that under a KC move every off-path contribution satisfies \(\nu_v(T')\ge\nu_v(T)\). If \(a,b\ge1\) are the respective numbers of vertices attached to \(x,y\) away from the bare path, their Theorem 8.4 calculates the sum of on-path increments exactly as

\[
\sum_{v\in\{u_0,\ldots,u_\ell\}}
[\nu_v(T')-\nu_v(T)]
=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}F_j(a,b),
\quad
F_j(a,b)=\alpha_{a+b+j}+\alpha_j-\alpha_{a+j}-\alpha_{b+j}.
\tag{2}
\]

The earlier article proves nonnegativity of (2), but does not claim strictness.

**Step 2: A strictly decreasing positive alternating kernel.** For integers \(a,b,j\ge1\), summing the exact second difference of \(\alpha\) twice yields

\[
F_j(a,b)=(-1)^{j+1}\eta_j(a,b),\qquad
\eta_j(a,b)=\sum_{u=0}^{a-1}\sum_{v=0}^{b-1}(-1)^{u+v}
\frac{2^{j+u+v+1}}{(j+u+v+2)!}.
\tag{3}
\]

Fix \(j\ge1\) and set \(w_k=2^{j+k+1}/(j+k+2)!\) for \(k\ge0\). Writing \(t=j+k+3\ge4\), direct division gives

\[
\frac{w_{k+1}}{w_k}=\frac2t<1,\qquad
\frac{\Delta^2w_k}{w_k}=\frac{t-3}{t+1}>0,
\]
\[
\frac{-\Delta^3w_k}{w_k}
=\frac{t(t+1)(t-4)+4}{t(t+1)(t+2)}>0.
\tag{4}
\]

Thus both \(w_k\) and \(g_k=w_k-w_{k+1}\) are strictly positive, decreasing and strictly convex sequences.

**Checkerboard lemma.** For any positive, strictly decreasing and strictly convex sequence \(h_0,h_1,\ldots\), and positive integers \(a,b\),

\[
\sum_{u=0}^{a-1}\sum_{v=0}^{b-1}(-1)^{u+v}h_{u+v}>0.
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, pair even and odd consecutive indices in both dimensions: each complete two-by-two square contributes \(h_k-2h_{k+1}+h_{k+2}>0\). A leftover last row or column contributes adjacent differences \(h_k-h_{k+1}>0\); if both dimensions are odd, their final unpaired corner is positive. These blocks partition the rectangle, so (5) is strict.

Apply (5) first to \(w\), then to \(g\). The relation \(w_k^{(j+1)}=w_{k+1}^{(j)}\) gives, respectively,

\[
\eta_j(a,b)>0,
\qquad
\eta_j(a,b)-\eta_{j+1}(a,b)>0.
\tag{6}
\]

Hence \(\eta_j\) decreases strictly and is positive. Pairing consecutive terms of the alternating sum gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}F_j(a,b)
=\eta_1-\eta_2+\eta_3-\cdots+(-1)^\ell\eta_{\ell-1}>0
\quad\text{for every }\ell\ge2.
\tag{7}
\]

In view of the nonnegative off-path contributions from [1], this proves \(\nu(T')>\nu(T)\) whenever \(\ell\ge2\).

**Step 3: The adjacent-endpoint case.** If \(\ell=1\), the right side of (2) is empty and equals zero. Because the KC move is proper, both endpoints have off-path neighbors. Fix \(v\) adjacent to \(x\) outside the path, and let \(b=|B|\ge1\) be the total number of vertices in branches attached to \(y\) outside the path. In \(T-N_T[v]\), the remaining component containing \(y\) has \(b+1\) vertices. In \(T'-N_{T'}[v]\), those same vertices split into singleton \(\{y\}\) and branches of positive sizes \(b_1,\ldots,b_s\) summing to \(b\). Thus, by the subadditivity of \(\alpha\) proved in [1],

\[
\nu_v(T')-\nu_v(T)
\ge\alpha_1+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{b_i}-\alpha_{b+1}
\ge 1+\alpha_b-\alpha_{b+1}>0.
\tag{8}
\]

The final inequality holds because for every integer \(b\ge1\),

\[
\alpha_{b+1}-\alpha_b
=\sum_{r=0}^{b}\frac{(-2)^r}{(r+1)!}<1.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed the first two summands cancel, and the remaining terms form an alternating series with decreasing magnitudes, whose partial sums are at most \(2/3\). All other off-path contributions are nonnegative by [1, Theorem 8.4], and the on-path total is zero, so \(\nu(T')>\nu(T)\) also when \(\ell=1\).

**Step 4: Strict global extremality.** The KC transformations induce a finite graded partial order on unlabelled trees of fixed order, with the path as unique minimum, and every nonpath tree reachable from it by a sequence of proper moves [1, Section 8.1; 2]. Steps 2 and 3 therefore give

\[
\nu(T)>\nu(P_n)\quad\text{for }T\not\cong P_n.
\tag{10}
\]

For a tree of order \(n\), every component in (1) is smaller than \(n\). By induction on tree order, using the non-strict path lower bound from [1] for smaller trees (or its already-proved theorem directly), (1) yields

\[
m(T)\ge 1+\frac{\nu(T)}n
>1+\frac{\nu(P_n)}n=m(P_n)
\quad\text{if }T\not\cong P_n.
\tag{11}
\]

The last identity is (1) on the path, whose components are all paths. For \(T\cong P_n\), equality is immediate. This completes the proof.

## Verification

A standalone exact-arithmetic checker (`verify.py`, Python 3.9 or newer with NetworkX 2.8 or newer) verifies formula (3), strict positivity and decrease of its kernel for \(1\le a,b\le17\) and \(1\le j\le22\), and the positivity of alternating sums. It independently computes random-greedy expectations by exact rational first-vertex recursion and shattering functionals for all unlabelled trees of orders four through eleven and all their oriented proper KC moves. The packaged code passed 30,741 exact assertions in its recorded replay.

These finite computations are diagnostic; the statements for all tree orders follow from Steps 1–4, not from enumeration. The proof depends explicitly on the published non-strict KC decompositions and the KC-poset reachability theorem stated in [1, 2].

## Relationship to prior work

KMM&S [1] prove that the path is a **minimizer** of expected random-greedy independent-set size, but explicitly note in Section 8 and their concluding discussion that their proof does not imply **uniqueness**. Their key functional \(\nu\) was only proved nondecreasing under KC transformations. The result here supplies strictness of that functional, including the adjacent-endpoint case omitted by the positive central-path argument, and hence proves uniqueness. The 2020 conference article [3] states the earlier non-strict theorem.

A proper KC move need not increase the random-greedy expectation itself; the theorem intentionally makes no such assertion. The only monotonicity strengthened here is that of the auxiliary \(\nu\).

## Limitations

The result concerns trees under a uniformly random vertex inspection order, not arbitrary connected graphs, nonuniform priorities, weighted independent sets, or adaptive greedy rules. It proves unique minimization, not a universal positive numerical stability gap in terms of distance from the path. The argument uses the published KC decomposition and tree-transformation order rather than providing an alternative proof of those established results.
