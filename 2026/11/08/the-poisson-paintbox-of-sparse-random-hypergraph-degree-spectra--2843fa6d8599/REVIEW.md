# Mathematical review

**Correctness: PASS.** The reduction to independent tagged-intersection classes is exact; the hypergeometric-to-polynomial ratio converges uniformly for fixed tag count, and a Le Cam bound makes the rare-event approximation uniform under the stated maximal-edge-probability hypothesis. Thinning of the Poisson point process reproduces every limiting finite-dimensional joint degree law. Exchangeability and coordinate-moment convergence, together with a uniform mean-degree tail bound, establish the empirical \(\ell^1\) limit. The Laplace-functional and triangular Hausdorff-moment calculations prove the isolate variance and identification claims. The constructive clipping argument realizes every finite spectral measure. The 3,652 passing finite tests independently check the central combinatorial and algebraic identities, but are not used as infinite proofs.

**Originality: PASS, with residual historical risk.** Source [1] concerns monochromatic-edge counts after random coloring rather than vertex incident-edge degrees; [2] treats configuration-model hypergraph simplicity; [3] gives isolation estimates for connectivity in a shotgun model; [4] presents general self-averaging criteria and a different non-self-averaging graph example; [5] studies distinct neighbors after projection. The directly related paper [6] bounds approximations of tagged degrees by independent Poisson laws and analyzes correlated Gaussian approximations, but does not provide the macroscopic-edge Poisson paintbox, random full histogram, or the inverse isolate-fraction law. Goldschmidt--Norris [7] studies a Poisson random hypergraph with fixed-size intensities and essential-edge collapse, not this macroscopic-size random degree spectrum. General exchangeability or point-process theorems may indirectly imply some statements; no specific encompassing theorem was identified after explicit implication searches. This is a best-knowledge originality assessment, not a claim of exhaustive priority.

**Value: PASS.** Hyperedge size is a fundamental heterogeneity parameter in networks with higher-order interactions. This result locates the exact macroscopic-size threshold at which a Poisson-looking typical degree ceases to represent the observed empirical histogram, and identifies the entire size spectrum from the observable limiting isolate-fraction distribution. The finite-size examples and algebraic inversions serve the structural classification; arbitrary parameter slices and numerical gains are not the focus.

**Limitations.** Finite mean-degree intensity, independent edges and within-size exchangeability are indispensable for the stated proof. No rates or dependent-edge universality are claimed. Historical priority may be superseded by a sufficiently general earlier paintbox theorem.
