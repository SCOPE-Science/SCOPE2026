# Scientific assessment: Klein-quartic rational tangent geometry

## Correctness — PASS
The claim uses the complete set of rational tangents, not all geometric tangents. The derivative system gives exactly seven reduced off-curve polar points. The Moore-image description proves those points form an embedded Fano subplane in the ambient plane. The two explicit group orbits exhaust the 24 rational curve points by the Serre genus-three bound; their tangent contact orders and Bézout show that all 24 tangents are distinct. Seven explicit tangent evaluations prove avoidance of the polar subplane, whose line census has exactly 24 avoiding lines. The seven independent join lines at the second orbit representative and the vertex calculation establish that the quartic is precisely the complement of the seven extended Fano lines. Counting the nine lines through each point then gives the exact 0/2/4 spectrum. Pure-Python independent field-model checks reproduce the assertions. A finite computer enumeration is not relied upon as proof of an infinite assertion: the theorem is a finite-plane classification with an explicit symbolic/group argument.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority uncertainty
The 24 rational points and the abstract Fano design on 21 nonzero-coordinate curve points are already in Pellikaan (1998); Elkies (1998) discusses the 24 flex points in the classical setting. Asgarli–Ghioca (2023) poses the general tangent-filling problem. The inspected discussions do not identify the seven off-curve polar zeros as an embedded ambient Fano subplane, prove that the 24 rational tangents are its 24 avoiding lines, or state the exact 0/2/4 tangent incidence partition. These are distinct point/line implication statements, not merely restatements of the known 24 rational points. Published finite-geometric accounts could imply a formulation not indexed by the terms searched; this is residual priority risk, not a proven uniqueness claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A point-maximal genus-three plane curve over a small field nevertheless misses exactly a canonical geometric seven-point subplane by its rational tangents. The identification of the uncovered set with the reduced polar base locus and its dual line-avoidance characterization give a complete natural tangent-filling obstruction and a full incidence stratification, motivating further searches for polar-subplane obstructions in finite-field curves. This goes beyond an arbitrary numeric tabulation, while not purporting to generalize to all curves or fields.
