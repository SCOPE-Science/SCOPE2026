# The polynomial Toeplitz locus of a compact Gaussian phase operator and its arithmetic density

## Finding
Fix a Gaussian Fock scale \(\alpha>0\) and any real \(t>0\). The compact operator \(A_t\) obtained by compressing the bounded radial phase \(\exp(-i\alpha t|z|^2)\) has a polynomial functional calculus whose intersection with **single Toeplitz operators admitting bounded measurable symbols** is exactly describable, although every polynomial in \(A_t\) belongs to the Toeplitz operator algebra.

Let \(u=1+it\), \(r=|u|\), and \(\phi=\arg u=\arctan t\). For every complex polynomial \(P(X)=\sum_{k=0}^Nc_kX^k\),

\[
P(A_t)=T_h\text{ for some }h\in L^\infty(\mathbb C)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
c_k=0\text{ whenever }\operatorname{Re}(u^k)<1.
\]

Whenever the condition holds, the unique bounded symbol is

\[
h(z)=\sum_{k=0}^N c_k\exp\!\left[-\alpha(u^k-1)|z|^2\right].
\]

Define the set of admissible powers \(S_t=\{k\ge0:\operatorname{Re}(u^k)\ge1\}\). Its natural density exists and is

\[
d(S_t)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac12,&\phi/\pi\notin\mathbb Q,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{1+2\lfloor(L-1)/4\rfloor}{L},
 &\phi/\pi=p/q\in\mathbb Q\text{ in lowest terms,}\quad L=2q/\gcd(p,2q).
\end{cases}
\]

For example, \(d(S_1)=3/8\), while \(d(S_{\tan(2\pi/5)})=3/5\). The admissible polynomials form the linear span of the powers indexed by \(S_t\), but not an algebra: \(1\) and \(A_t\) are admissible, while \(A_t^2\) is not, for every \(t>0\).

## Assumptions and scope
The scalar complex Fock space \(\mathcal F^2_\alpha\) consists of entire functions with norm

\[
\|f\|^2=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb C}|f(z)|^2e^{-\alpha|z|^2}\,dA(z).
\]

Write \(T_h=\Pi_\alpha M_h|_{\mathcal F^2_\alpha}\) for the bounded Toeplitz operator with essentially bounded symbol \(h\), and \(e_n(z)=\alpha^{n/2}z^n/\sqrt{n!}\), for integers \(n\ge0\). The parameter \(t\) is strictly positive, and all polynomial coefficients are complex; the zero polynomial is included. Existence requires a **single** essentially bounded measurable symbol, not merely inclusion in the norm-closed algebra generated by bounded-symbol Toeplitz operators or representation with an unbounded symbol.

## Proof
**1. Diagonalization.** Radiality and angular orthogonality show

\[
A_te_n=\frac{\alpha^{n+1}}{n!}\int_0^\infty s^ne^{-\alpha(1+it)s}\,ds\,e_n
=u^{-(n+1)}e_n.
\]

Writing \(q_0=u^{-1}\), the \(n\)-th eigenvalue of \(P(A_t)\) is
\(\lambda_n=\sum_{k=0}^Nc_kq_0^{k(n+1)}\). Since \(|q_0|<1\), the operator \(A_t\) is compact and in every positive Schatten class. The powers are linearly independent: if a polynomial vanishes on \(q_0,q_0^2,\ldots\), it vanishes identically.

**2. Rotation averaging.** Suppose \(P(A_t)=T_h\) with \(h\in L^\infty\). Averaging the symbol under rotations produces a radial essentially bounded symbol \(H(s)\), with \(s=|z|^2\), such that \(T_{H(|z|^2)}=P(A_t)\). Indeed, averaging conjugates \(T_h\) by the unitary rotation representation preserves the diagonal operator \(P(A_t)\). Therefore

\[
\lambda_n=\frac{\alpha^{n+1}}{n!}\int_0^\infty H(s)s^ne^{-\alpha s}\,ds
\qquad(n\ge0).
\]

**3. Pole obstruction, including every possible cancellation.** The Laplace transform \(\mathcal LH(z)=\int_0^\infty H(s)e^{-zs}\,ds\) is holomorphic throughout \(\operatorname{Re}z>0\), with \(|\mathcal LH(z)|\le\|H\|_\infty/\operatorname{Re}z\). Summing the preceding moments for \(|w|<1\), with justified dominated convergence, gives

\[
\alpha\mathcal LH(\alpha(1-w))
=\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda_nw^n
=\sum_{k=0}^N\frac{c_k u^{-k}}{1-u^{-k}w}.
\]

Consequently, on an open neighborhood of \(z=\alpha\),

\[
\mathcal LH(z)=\sum_{k=0}^N\frac{c_k}{z+\alpha(u^k-1)}.
\]

The displayed rational function is meromorphic on the entire complex plane. Its poles are pairwise distinct for different \(k\), because \(|u|>1\) and thus the powers \(u^k\) are distinct. By analytic continuation, a term with nonzero \(c_k\) and \(\operatorname{Re}(u^k)<1\) would cause a nonremovable pole at \(z=-\alpha(u^k-1)\) strictly inside the right half-plane. This contradicts holomorphy of \(\mathcal LH\). Therefore all such coefficients vanish; cancellation among different forbidden powers is impossible.

**4. Explicit sufficiency and uniqueness.** For every allowed \(k\), the function \(h_k(z)=\exp[-\alpha(u^k-1)|z|^2]\) is bounded by one. Its Toeplitz eigenvalues are

\[
\frac{\alpha^{n+1}}{n!}\int_0^\infty s^n e^{-\alpha u^k s}\,ds
=u^{-k(n+1)};
\]

here \(\operatorname{Re}(u^k)\ge1\) makes the integral absolutely convergent. Thus the finite sum \(h=\sum c_kh_k\) produces exactly \(P(A_t)\). If two bounded symbols produce the same Toeplitz operator, their difference is orthogonal (in Gaussian \(L^2\)) to every polynomial in the real and imaginary coordinates, because all mixed monomial matrix coefficients vanish. Completeness of the two-dimensional Gaussian Hermite polynomials forces the difference to vanish almost everywhere, proving uniqueness.

**5. Density of admissible degrees.** The admissibility inequality is \(r^k\cos(k\phi)\ge1\), with \(r>1\). If \(\phi/\pi\) is irrational, the angles \(k\phi\) are equidistributed modulo \(2\pi\). Since \(r^{-k}\to0\), the indicator of admissibility differs in asymptotic density zero from the indicator of \(\cos(k\phi)>0\): for each fixed \(\eta>0\), the only eventual possible discrepancy lies within \(|\cos(k\phi)|\le\eta\), whose limiting frequency tends to zero with \(\eta\). This yields density \(1/2\).

If \(\phi/\pi=p/q\) is reduced, the rotation has exact order \(L=2q/\gcd(p,2q)\). Each residue of the \(L\)-point orbit is visited equally often. Every positive-cosine residue is eventually admissible, while every zero- or negative-cosine residue is forever inadmissible. Among \(L\) roots of unity, exactly \(1+2\lfloor(L-1)/4\rfloor\) have strictly positive real part. The displayed rational density follows. At \(t=1\), the orbit length is \(8\), with \(3\) positive points; at \(t=\tan(2\pi/5)\), the orbit length is \(5\), with \(3\) positive points.

Finally, \(\operatorname{Re}(u^0)=\operatorname{Re}(u)=1\), while \(\operatorname{Re}(u^2)=1-t^2<1\). Thus \(I=A_t^0\) and \(A_t\) are single bounded-symbol Toeplitz operators, whereas \(A_t^2\) is not. This concludes the theorem.

## Verification
The decisive verifications are the explicit Gamma integral, the analytic right-half-plane pole obstruction and the elementary rational-rotation/equidistribution count; none depends on finite computation. A standalone Python diagnostic checks the eigenvalue identities across several positive phase parameters and powers, verifies the pole separation, and checks finite rational rotation counts. It is supplemental rather than a proof of the infinite assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Lin, Lu and Zu (2026), Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, establish a universal Fourier test for bounded-symbol Fock Toeplitz representation and recognize **individual** weighted composition operators. In particular, their composition result already supplies the admissibility condition for a single \(A_t^k\). This theorem does **not** claim a new single-power criterion. Its separate content is the exact **finite-polynomial** classification (notably the impossibility of cancellation), explicit symbol for all allowed polynomial combinations, and the arithmetic density law. The general Fourier test provides a potential alternative proof route but does not explicitly evaluate this polynomial family or derive the resonance densities in the inspected paper.

Esmeral García and Maximenko (2025) study the eigenvalue sequences and uniform approximation by bounded radial Toeplitz operators; approximation in operator norm is distinct from exact equality with one bounded symbol. Bauer, Fulsche and Rodríguez Rodríguez (2024) examine membership in the generated Fock–Toeplitz algebra and weighted composition operators. Every polynomial here is already in that algebra, while a single-symbol representation can fail. Their results do not state the finite-polynomial rigidity or density formula.

## Limitations
This is a one-complex-variable Gaussian Fock theorem for the explicitly defined pure-phase radial Toeplitz generator. It does not classify the norm closure of the representable polynomials, arbitrary entire functional calculus, or arbitrary radial symbols. Single-power bounded-symbol representability is prior work; the claim is limited to the complete finite-combination obstruction and the arithmetic counting of admissible degrees. Publication-priority certainty would require further comparison with all earlier specialized radial moment and weighted composition formulas.
