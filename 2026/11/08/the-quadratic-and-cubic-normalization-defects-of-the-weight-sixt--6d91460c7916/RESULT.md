# The quadratic and cubic normalization defects of the weight-sixteen multiple-zeta toric model

## Finding
Let \(L_{\le16}\) be the Lyndon words on the ordered alphabet \(2<3\), where the weight of a word is its digit sum. Let \(A_{16}\) be the set of nonnegative integer exponent vectors \(a=(a_w)_{w\in L_{\le16}}\) satisfying \(\sum_w |w|a_w=16\), and let \(R=\mathbb C[t x^a:a\in A_{16}]\) with the grading \(\deg(tx^a)=1\). Write \(\overline R\) for its integral closure in its fraction field. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
|A_{16}|&=37,\\
\bigl(\dim R_2,\dim\overline R_2\bigr)&=(630,631),\\
\bigl(\dim R_3,\dim\overline R_3\bigr)&=(6885,6907).
\end{aligned}
\]
All 22 missing cubic monomials are products of a unique missing quadratic monomial with an original degree-one generator, after duplicates already present in \(R_3\) are excluded. Thus the first positive degrees of the normalization Hilbert-function defect are \(\dim(\overline R/R)_1=0\), \(\dim(\overline R/R)_2=1\), and \(\dim(\overline R/R)_3=22\).

The missing degree-two exponent is
\[
h=2e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{223}+e_{233}+e_{2233}.
\]
The single missing quadratic monomial \(t^2x^h\) and the 22 missing degree-three monomials exhaust the corresponding graded differences. No assertion is made about the normalization in degree four or higher.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). A word \(w\) is Lyndon if it is strictly lexicographically smaller than every nontrivial cyclic rotation. Its weight is the sum of its digits, not its length. The ring \(R\) is the homogeneous semigroup algebra for the fixed-weight-16 monomial embedding described by Burmester–Charlton–Kidambi–Lotter–Preiß, not the full multigraded algebra of all multiple-zeta values. Its elements are indexed by \((a,d)\), with \(a\) a nonnegative integer vector and \(\sum|w|a_w=16d\). The affine semigroup \(S\) is generated by the 37 vectors \((a,1)\). The integral closure has monomial basis indexed by \(\operatorname{cone}(S)\cap\operatorname{gp}(S)\); in degree \(d\) these are lattice points in \(dP\) belonging to \(\operatorname{gp}(S)\), where \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A_{16})\). Thus normalized graded dimensions are exact lattice-point counts in the appropriate group, not merely the dimensions of an ambient polynomial ring.

## Proof
**1. The quadratic lattice hole really belongs to the normalization.** Enumeration of all Lyndon words of digit sum at most 16 yields 26 words and 37 nonnegative solutions of total weight 16. The 37 original columns give 630 distinct pair sums, so \(\dim R_2=630\). Set \(h=2e_2+e_3+e_{223}+e_{233}+e_{2233}\). Four weight-16 columns satisfy
\[
2h=(2e_{233})+(2e_3+e_{2233})+(e_2+2e_{223})+(3e_2+e_{2233}).
\]
Also, four (not necessarily distinct) weight-16 columns satisfy
\[
h=(2e_{233})+(2e_3+e_{2233})+(2e_2+e_{23}+e_{223})-(e_3+e_{23}+e_{233}).
\]
The first equality proves \(h\in2P\), and the second proves \((h,2)\in\operatorname{gp}(S)\). Hence \(t^2x^h\) is integral over \(R\) and lies in its fraction field. Direct pair-sum enumeration shows \(h\notin S_2\).

**2. Exact upper bounds by finite integer separating hyperplanes.** Every lattice point in \(dP\) has a nonnegative integer exponent vector of total weight \(16d\). Its four coordinates corresponding to weight-15 Lyndon words vanish, since every generator has those coordinates zero (no word of weight 1). There are 1051 nonnegative weight-32 vectors and 17457 nonnegative weight-48 vectors before this restriction.

The accompanying `CERTIFICATE.json` contains 37 integer linear functionals for degree two and 170 additional functionals for degree three, each on the 22 remaining coordinates. To verify a candidate \(b\) lies strictly outside \(dP\), it suffices to find one such functional \(\ell\) satisfying
\[
\ell(b)>d\max_{a\in A_{16}}\ell(a).
\]
This is an exact integer certificate, since every convex combination of the generators obeys the opposite weak inequality. The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs every Lyndon word and every candidate vector and checks that **each** weight-32 vector other than the 630 pair sums and \(h\), and **each** weight-48 vector other than the 6885 triple sums and the candidates below, either has a forbidden weight-15 coordinate or admits one of these separating functionals. This proves completeness of the upper bound without a floating-point feasibility assumption or extrapolation from sampled vectors.

**3. All remaining cubic holes are integral lattice points.** The set
\[
H_3=\{h+a:a\in A_{16}\}\setminus S_3
\]
has cardinality 22, by exact vector addition. Each \((h+a,3)\) lies in \(\operatorname{cone}(S)\cap\operatorname{gp}(S)\), by Step 1 and closure of the cone and group under addition. Step 2 excludes every further lattice point from \(3P\). Thus \(\dim\overline R_2=630+1=631\) and \(\dim\overline R_3=6885+22=6907\). The 22 classes are precisely the new cubic products of the unique degree-two normalization generator, so no additional degree-three generator is needed to generate the normalization through degree three.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py CERTIFICATE.json` using Python 3.9 or later; no third-party packages are needed. The script checks the 26-word list, all 37 weight-16 exponent vectors, all 630 degree-two and 6885 degree-three semigroup elements, the two explicit normalization identities, the 22 cubic candidates, the 1051 and 17457 exhaustive integer-vector domains, and all exact integer separating inequalities. The observed output ends in `VERIFY_OK`. The actual certificate and script are distributed alongside this text, not inferred from the search log.

## Relationship to prior work
Burmester, Charlton, Kidambi, Lotter, and Preiß construct this fixed-weight motivic MZV toric variety. Their nonnormality theorem already gives the displayed quadratic witness; **the existence or discovery of that hole is not claimed here as new**. Their table of degree-two and degree-three equations is consistent with the unnormalized dimensions \(630\) and \(6885\). The contribution here is an **exhaustive** proof that this is the sole degree-two normalization hole, and a complete description and exact count of the 22 degree-three holes, with the initial normalized Hilbert function. Standard normalization-of-semigroup-ring theorems supply the bridge from exact lattice-point computations to the graded algebra. These data are about this specific MZV-derived toric semigroup, not generic numerical semigroups or unrelated order-16 toric polytopes.

## Limitations
The result does not identify the conductor, the full Hilbert series, a minimal normalization generating set in arbitrarily high degree, or normality in other MZV weights. The exhaustive proof is a computer-assisted finite integer certificate, not a hand enumeration; its correctness rests on the transparent full-domain enumerator and checked integer inequalities in the accompanying files. The motivating paper's explicit quadratic witness is prior art. Historical-priority review remains provisional, and no independent audit is claimed.
