# The rational symmetry character of the Fano-matroid independence complex

## Finding
Let \(P=\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\}\) and let \(K\) be the rank-three Fano-matroid independence complex: its vertices are the seven points of \(P\), its edges are all distinct pairs, and its triangles are precisely the triples \(\{a,b,c\}\) satisfying \(a+b+c\ne0\). The natural automorphism group is \(G=GL_3(\mathbb F_2)\), of order \(168\). Then

\[
\widetilde H_j(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z^{13},&j=2,\\0,&j\ne2.\end{cases}
\]

The complete rational second-homology character \(\chi\), on conjugacy classes of orders \(1,2,3,4,7,7\), is

\[
(13,-3,1,1,-1,-1).
\]

Under the identification \(G\cong PSL_2(7)\), its complexification decomposes as the irreducible representation of degree seven together with the two conjugate irreducible representations of degree three:

\[
\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb C
\cong V_7\oplus V_3\oplus\overline{V_3}.
\]

In particular the quotient \(|K|/G\) is rationally acyclic: \(H^*(|K|/G;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q\) concentrated in degree zero. For cyclic subgroups generated by elements of orders \(2,3,4,7\), respectively, the rank of the invariant second rational homology is \(5,5,3,1\). These are invariants of the specified action, not just the ordinary Betti number.

## Assumptions and scope
The simplicial complex is the matroid independence complex, **not** the independence complex of the Heawood incidence graph and **not** the Fano incidence graph's matching complex. Each edge \(\{a,b\}\) corresponds to a line \(\{a,b,a+b\}\) only when a third vertex is adjoined. Orient each simplex by increasing numerical labels \(1,\ldots,7\), which represent nonzero three-bit vectors. Homology is simplicial homology of the geometric realization; the reduced chain complex has its usual augmentation in degree minus one. The action is induced by linear transformations over \(\mathbb F_2\). The claims about quotient cohomology are over \(\mathbb Q\), not over \(\mathbb Z\).

## Proof
**Integral homology.** The seven lines are the triples \(\{a,b,a+b\}\). Of \(\binom73=35\) triples, exactly seven are lines, so \(K\) has seven vertices, twenty-one edges and twenty-eight triangles. Its one-skeleton is the complete graph, hence the kernel of the edge-boundary map has integral rank \(21-6=15\). In the full two-skeleton of the six-simplex, the boundaries of all triangles generate this entire integral cycle lattice: a cycle based at vertex \(v\) is an integer sum of triangles formed by coning its edges to \(v\).

For a missing collinear triangle \(\{a,b,c\}\) and a point \(x\notin\{a,b,c\}\), each of \(\{a,b,x\}\), \(\{b,c,x\}\), and \(\{c,a,x\}\) is noncollinear, since each Fano pair belongs to precisely one line. With coherent orientations their three boundaries sum to the boundary of \(\{a,b,c\}\). Thus the boundaries of *present* triangles already generate the complete integral cycle lattice; \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)=0\). Since there are no three-simplices, \(H_2=\ker\partial_2\) is a free subgroup of a free abelian group of rank \(28\), and its rank is \(28-15=13\). Connectivity and the absence of higher simplices complete the homology calculation.

**Equivariant character.** For a simplicial permutation \(g\), its trace on the oriented chain group in degree \(k\) is the sum of the signs of its induced permutations on each preserved \(k\)-simplex. Because reduced homology occurs only in degree two, the Hopf trace formula gives
\[
\chi(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g|C_2)-\operatorname{tr}(g|C_1)
+\operatorname{tr}(g|C_0)-1.
\]
The following table records these signed traces. The columns are the element order, the number of elements of that order, the three nonaugmented chain traces, and the resulting homology character:

| Order | Count | \(\operatorname{tr}C_0\) | \(\operatorname{tr}C_1\) | \(\operatorname{tr}C_2\) | \(\chi\) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1 | 7 | 21 | 28 | 13 |
| 2 | 21 | 3 | 1 | -4 | -3 |
| 3 | 56 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 4 | 42 | 1 | -1 | 0 | 1 |
| 7 | 48 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -1 |

For clarity, this table is derived directly from the action on seven points. An involution has point-cycle type \(1^3 2^2\); its three fixed points form one Fano line, and among six invariant triangles formed by a transposition and a fixed point exactly two are collinear. Thus four invariant independent triangles contribute orientation sign minus one. An element of order three has type \(1^1 3^2\); exactly one of its two three-cycles is a Fano line, and the other supplies one positively oriented independent triangle. An order-four element has type \(1^1 2^1 4^1\); its only fixed two-element subset contributes minus one in degree one, and the invariant triple obtained with the fixed point is a Fano line. Order-seven elements transitively permute the seven vertices, hence fix no nonempty simplex of size at most three. This proves the entries without extrapolating finite experiments.

The standard irreducible character table of \(PSL_2(7)\) has degree-seven character \((7,-1,1,-1,0,0)\) and the sum of its conjugate degree-three characters \((6,-2,0,2,-1,-1)\), in class order \(1,2,3,4,7,7\). Their sum is the table above; character orthogonality gives the asserted decomposition uniquely.

**Quotients.** After barycentric subdivision, characteristic-zero simplicial transfer identifies rational cohomology of the finite orbit space with the invariant cohomology of \(K\). The average \(168^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)\) is zero, so the full quotient has no positive rational cohomology. The analogous averages over cyclic groups of orders \(2,3,4,7\) are respectively \(5,5,3,1\). No integral quotient claim is made.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_fano.py` using a standard Python 3 installation. The checker enumerates all \(168\) invertible binary linear transformations and the \(28\) independent triangles, checks all \(28\) integral line-coning identities, checks \(8232\) signed simplicial boundary-equivariance identities, and recomputes all chain and homology character values and cyclic averages. The output shipped as `verification_output.txt` is the recorded execution result. The general integral-homology statement follows from the elementary coning proof above, not from a finite-field rank test or a computation alone.

## Relationship to prior work
The Fano matroid and its independent triples are classical; Cameron's 11 March 2015 discussion provides a publicly dated description. The general theory relating equivariant matroid homology to automorphism representations is developed by Moci and Pezzoli (first publicly posted 11 January 2020). That theory supplies a broad setting and should not be presented as a novelty of this calculation. Their inspected paper focuses its explicit representation computations on complete-graph matroids, rather than an explicit character decomposition for the rank-three Fano matroid. The present calculation is the concrete seven-point binary-matroid character and its cyclic-orbit consequences. General theory may offer alternate derivations; no claim is made that the method of using signed traces is novel.

## Limitations
Only the rational group-module decomposition and ordinary integral homology groups are determined. No integral \(\mathbb Z[G]\)-lattice decomposition, integral quotient homology, equivariant homotopy type or canonical splitting is claimed. The character comparison establishes the complex decomposition; it does not establish an integral splitting. Potential equivalent calculations in less accessible or unindexed literature remain a priority risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
