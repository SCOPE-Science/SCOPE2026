# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every prime \(p=6m+1\), the homogeneous quartic is smooth precisely off the two excluded coefficients \(a=\pm2\). The degree-four plane Hasse--Witt coefficient recipe directly gives a diagonal matrix indexed by \((2,1,1)\), \((1,2,1)\), and \((1,1,2)\). Explicitly expanding each diagonal coefficient produces the polynomials \(A_m\), \(B_m\), and \(D_m\) defined in RESULT.md. The coefficient recurrence proves the relevant second-order differential equation in characteristic zero, and substitution proves the two asserted factorizations modulo \(p\). Their leading coefficients are nonzero because all factorial indices are below \(p\). Since \(B_m\) satisfies a differential equation with invertible highest-derivative coefficient away from \(a=\pm2\) and has nonzero values at both boundary coefficients, its \(m\) geometric zeros are simple and smooth. Stable Frobenius rank equals diagonal rank, giving \((p\text{-rank},a)=(3,0)\) or \((1,2)\). The elliptic quotient is an ordinary \(j=0\) elliptic curve for \(p\equiv1\pmod3\); the degree-two covering isogeny is prime to \(p\), so the Prym has \((2,0)\) or \((0,2)\), respectively. All quantifiers and exclusions are explicit.

The standalone verifier reconstructed the direct nine-entry matrix and independent coefficient factorizations for fifteen primes through \(139\), and checked polynomial squarefreeness and endpoint values; it printed `VERIFY_BIELLIPTIC_PICARD_OK 15 primes`. Such checks are supporting, not a proof of the uniform result. No computational claim about all primes is made.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk

Laga--Shnidman, *The geometry and arithmetic of bielliptic Picard curves* (arXiv:2308.15297, first posted 2023-08-29), supplies the precise motivating family and its quaternionic Pryms. Its inspected text treats moduli, endomorphisms, torsion and isogenies, not the explicit prime-dependent reduced nonordinary divisor or its exact root count. Their *Ceresa cycles* (arXiv:2312.12965) treats a different arithmetic invariant in the same family. Nourozi--Rahmati (arXiv:2306.07823, §3) gives a general Picard Hasse--Witt coefficient recipe. The coefficient recipe alone does not supply the nontrivial two factorizations and the uniform squarefree-root theorem; its stated uniform ordinary conclusion for \(p\equiv1\pmod3\) is inconsistent with the directly computed smooth nonordinary examples (for instance \(p=7,a=0\)). Kazemifard--Tafazolian's 2015 full text addresses two fixed Fermat-type Picard curves and not the moving deformation locus. Public mathematical-record searches and accessible prior-claim comparisons revealed no equivalent reduced-divisor theorem. The abstracts of Estrada--Sarlabous (1991) and Petkova--Shiga (2011) remain priority risks: a general Hasse matrix and a complex hypergeometric Shimura uniformization might admit an equivalent specialization not inspected in full. The result does not claim the underlying family, general coefficient recipe, or Shimura uniformization as new.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The nonordinary locus on this established discriminant-six Prym family is a geometrically natural invariant. The theorem does not merely locate one exceptional curve: it uniformly determines the reduced Hasse divisor over all primes \(p\equiv1\pmod6\), proves each of its \((p-1)/6\) roots is simple and lies on the smooth locus, determines all possible Jacobian Cartier and stable Frobenius ranks, and upgrades the exceptional Prym stratum to superspecial rather than merely nonordinary. The determinant's exact double-vanishing reflects the two-dimensional Prym contribution. These properties provide useful arithmetic structure of the family beyond a generic coefficient algorithm or isolated numerical example. Priority of an equivalent older hypergeometric invariant remains uncertain.

## Limitations and closest literature

Counts refer to marked coefficients; parameters \(a\) and \(-a\) can give isomorphic curves. The claim excludes \(p\equiv5\pmod6\) and does not give roots' minimal fields of definition or full Frobenius polynomials. Estrada--Sarlabous (1991), Petkova--Shiga (2011), Laga--Shnidman (2023), Nourozi--Rahmati (2023), and Kazemifard--Tafazolian (2015) are the closest identified sources. Only the first four sources listed in AUDIT.json as full-text were inspected in their available complete textual form; the other two were assessed on abstracts only. Historical priority is not independently certified.
