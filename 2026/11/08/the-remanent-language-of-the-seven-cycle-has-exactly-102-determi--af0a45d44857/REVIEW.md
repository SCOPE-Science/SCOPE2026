# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The tuple of reachable state subsets accepts exactly the intersection over all 128 orientations, by direct induction on input length. Any empty subset can be merged into one absorbing sink. The complete bitset breadth-first search and embedded certificate enumerate 230 distinct reachable states; Moore's finite refinement yields exactly 102 stable right-language classes. The proof of the refinement algorithm's equivalence to indistinguishability supplies the necessary lower bound, while the quotient supplies the upper bound. The standalone checker reproduces the complete finite certificate, checks direct graph-walk semantics up to length ten, and checks smaller-cycle baselines.

**Originality (PASS against inspected literature and indexed claims).** Pujol (2026), Section 5.2 and Figure 11, introduces the invariant and illustrates the five-cycle but does not enumerate the minimal complete DFA for the seven-cycle. Guzmán-Pro and Hernández-Cruz (2020) provide oriented-path/cycle duality; a path-membership criterion does not supply the numerical Myhill–Nerode quotient of the intersection language. Searches for seven-cycle remanent DFA state complexity, equivalent terms and the exact counts did not locate a prior explicit answer. Priority remains open to an unindexed derivation or stronger unpublished classification.

**Value (PASS).** The deterministic state complexity is a canonical, presentation-independent invariant of a newly studied family of factorial regular languages. The seven-cycle is the first odd cycle beyond the five-cycle example depicted in the initiating paper. An exact 102-state minimization from a 230-state reachable subset construction provides a nontrivial, complete benchmark for future remanent-language construction/minimization methods and prevents erroneous extrapolation from the two smaller-cycle cases. The result does not claim a general growth theorem.
