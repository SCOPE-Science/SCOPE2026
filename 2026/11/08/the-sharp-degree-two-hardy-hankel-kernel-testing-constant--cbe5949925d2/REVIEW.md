# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The complete Hardy Hankel operator is a two-by-two anti-diagonal coefficient matrix, whose top singular value is \((|a|+\sqrt{|a|^2+4|b|^2})/2\). The normalized kernel image has exactly two nonzero coefficients and, after phase optimization, squared norm \((1-r^2)((|a|+|b|r)^2+|b|^2)\). Homogeneity puts the matrix norm at one, giving \(|a|=1-|b|^2\) and \(0\le|b|\le1\). Two exact polynomial identities establish the global uniform lower bound and an attainable sharpness certificate, with the equality parameter uniquely forced by the first identity. The upper bound is norm restriction; the monomial equality cases and nonmonomial strictness are independently justified by the finite-rank norming-vector argument. The attached exact arithmetic checker confirms the algebraic certificates, not the infinite theorem by sampling.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)
The first-public 2026 primary preprint states a qualitative strict quadratic example and its later Addendum classifies polynomial symbols having zero testing deficit. That stronger classification was considered explicitly: it implies strictness for every nonmonomial but gives no positive optimal family-wide constant, no \(25/27\) minimax value, and no worst-parameter classification. The closest published polynomial rigidity, finite-rank shifted-Blaschke and logarithmic Hilbert-Hankel results were compared by their precise mathematical statements, not titles. The first two concern equality rather than a positive sharp gap; the last treats one infinite-rank logarithmic symbol and has a different ratio. Searches for degree-two, rank-two, Garsia, Nehari, Szegő-kernel testing, and numerical constants returned no exact equivalent or stronger general theorem. An unindexed older rank-two norm-comparison result remains the primary residual risk; an independent priority review is required.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The recent strong reproducing kernel thesis problem naturally asks how much norm kernel testing can miss. This finding determines the **best possible comparison constant over the entire first genuinely nonmonomial polynomial Hankel family**, with the unique scale-free worst configuration. It upgrades the known yes/no failure example to a complete sharp quantitative guarantee usable for kernel-based operator norm estimates, without claiming wider-rank or higher-degree validity. A sharp family-wide comparison is mathematically more informative than a chosen numerical instance.

## Closest literature and limitations
Dyakonov (2026, arXiv:2609.33242v3) supplies the precise primary setting and an explicit qualitative quadratic counterexample; its Addendum covers the polynomial equality classification. Related public mathematical notes are *Monomial rigidity for polynomial-symbol Hankel operators obeying the strong reproducing kernel thesis*; *Shifted-Blaschke classification of finite-rank SRKT Hankel operators*; and *An exact square-root-two SRKT gap for the logarithmic Hilbert Hankel symbol*. Full text of all four sources was compared. The present proof applies to analytic polynomials of degree at most two on classical \(H^2\) only, and makes no statement about a globally optimal Hankel/Garsia constant.
