In rooted unit-edge phylogenetic trees with independent homogeneous extinctions, five taxa are necessary and sufficient for a tree to have greater expected surviving phylogenetic diversity but lower probability of retaining at least half its initial diversity. A single four-leaf tree rotation, embedded in every larger caterpillar, has the exact half-retention crossover \(q=(n-4)/(n-2)\) for every \(n\ge5\), despite an expectation advantage \(q^2(1-q)\) at every interior extinction risk.
