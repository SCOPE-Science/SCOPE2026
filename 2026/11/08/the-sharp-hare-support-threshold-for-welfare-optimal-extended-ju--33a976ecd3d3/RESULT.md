# The sharp Hare-support threshold for welfare-optimal extended justified representation

## Finding

Let \(1\le k\le n\) and \(m\ge k\). In an approval-based committee election with \(n\) voters, \(m\) candidates, and committee size \(k\), write \(N(c)\) for the supporters of candidate \(c\) and \(q=\lceil n/k\rceil\). **If every candidate has at most \(q\) supporters, a size-\(k\) committee maximizing total approval welfare can be selected to satisfy extended justified representation (EJR).** More strongly, *every* welfare-maximizing committee with maximum voter coverage among the welfare maximizers satisfies EJR. A deterministic polynomial-time construction uses at most \(n\) welfare-preserving, strictly coverage-increasing single-candidate exchanges starting from any welfare maximizer. Consequently the output is also Pareto optimal, and satisfies PJR and JR.

The support bound is uniformly sharp: for **each** integer \(q\ge1\), an election with \(n=3q\), \(k=3\), and maximum candidate support \(q+1\) has a unique welfare-maximizing committee that violates JR, whereas the best JR/EJR committee loses exactly one unit of welfare. The corresponding welfare price is \((3q+3)/(3q+2)\). These counterexamples require only four candidates if empty ballots are allowed, or at most five candidates if every voter must approve something.

## Assumptions and scope

There is a finite voter set \(V\), \(|V|=n\ge1\), a finite candidate set \(C\), \(|C|=m\ge k\), and integer \(1\le k\le n\). A voter \(v\) may approve any subset \(A_v\subseteq C\), including the empty set. For \(c\in C\), let \(N(c)=\{v\in V:c\in A_v\}\), and \(d(c)=|N(c)|\). A committee is a subset \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|=k\). Its utilitarian welfare is \(\mathrm{sw}(W)=\sum_{v\in V}|A_v\cap W|=\sum_{c\in W}d(c)\), and its coverage is \(\mathrm{cov}(W)=|\{v:A_v\cap W\ne\varnothing\}|\).

For \(1\le\ell\le k\), an \(\ell\)-cohesive group \(S\subseteq V\) satisfies \(|S|\ge\ell n/k\) and \(|\bigcap_{v\in S}A_v|\ge\ell\). EJR requires that every such group contain some voter \(v\) with \(|A_v\cap W|\ge\ell\). JR is the \(\ell=1\) condition: there is no unelected candidate approved by at least \(q\) *unrepresented* voters. No structure is assumed of individual approval ballots beyond the candidate-support bound \(d(c)\le q\).

## Proof

**1. In this domain, JR and EJR are equivalent.** For every integer \(\ell\ge2\), the threshold \(\lceil\ell n/k\rceil\) strictly exceeds \(q=\lceil n/k\rceil\), since \(n/k\ge1\). Any common candidate of an \(\ell\)-cohesive group would therefore have more than \(q\) supporters, contradicting the hypothesis. Hence no \(\ell\)-cohesive group with \(\ell\ge2\) exists. EJR thus imposes only JR.

**2. A coverage-maximal welfare optimizer is JR.** Choose a size-\(k\) committee \(W\) maximizing \(\mathrm{sw}(W)\), then \(\mathrm{cov}(W)\) among all welfare optimizers. Suppose it violates JR. There exists \(c\notin W\) with at least \(q\) supporters who approve no member of \(W\). Since \(d(c)\le q\), exactly \(q\) voters support \(c\), and they are all unrepresented. Since \(W\) consists of \(k\) largest candidate degrees (allowing ties), every selected candidate \(e\in W\) must have \(d(e)\ge d(c)=q\); the support bound implies \(d(e)=q\). Therefore the selected candidates account for exactly \(kq\) supporter incidences. These incidences lie within at most \(n-q\) currently represented voters, since all \(q\) supporters of \(c\) are unrepresented. Because \(kq\ge n>n-q\), some represented voter supports at least two selected candidates. Choose any \(e\in W\) that such a voter supports.

The exchange \(W'=(W\setminus\{e\})\cup\{c\}\) replaces a candidate of degree \(q\) by one of degree \(q\), so \(\mathrm{sw}(W')=\mathrm{sw}(W)\). The old private supporters of \(e\), who approve no other member of \(W\), number at most \(q-1\) because \(e\) overlaps another selected candidate. All \(q\) supporters of \(c\) were unrepresented, and thus disjoint from every old selected support set. Therefore \(\mathrm{cov}(W')\ge\mathrm{cov}(W)+q-(q-1)>\mathrm{cov}(W)\), a contradiction. Thus \(W\) is JR and hence EJR.

**3. Algorithm and efficiency.** Sort candidates by \(d(c)\), choose the top \(k\), and repeatedly search for an unelected \(c\) with at least \(q\) uncovered supporters. If none exists, JR holds. Otherwise the incidence argument above produces a selected \(e\) with an overlapping selected supporter; perform the exchange. Every exchange preserves the maximum welfare and increases coverage by at least one, so there are at most \(n\) exchanges. Scanning candidates and voters suffices for each step, giving polynomial time. For any two committees \(W,Z\), a Pareto improvement over \(W\) would strictly increase \(\sum_v|A_v\cap W|\), contradicting welfare maximality; hence \(W\) is Pareto optimal.

**4. Uniform sharpness at \(q+1\).** Fix any integer \(q\ge1\), let \(n=3q\), \(k=3\), and partition voters into disjoint sets \(H,D,R\) of respective sizes \(q+1,q,q-1\). Give each voter in \(H\) exactly the approvals \(\{a,b,c\}\), each voter in \(D\) approval \(\{d\}\), and each voter in \(R\) an empty ballot. The candidate degrees are \(q+1,q+1,q+1,q\). Hence \(\{a,b,c\}\) is the unique three-candidate welfare maximizer, with welfare \(3q+3\), and leaves the size-\(q\) common-interest group \(D\) unrepresented, violating JR (and EJR). Each JR committee must contain \(d\); the best such committee has welfare \(3q+2\), e.g. \(\{a,b,d\}\), which indeed satisfies EJR: the group \(H\) is covered and has two representatives, the group \(D\) has one, and the remaining voters approve nothing. Consequently the ratio is \((3q+3)/(3q+2)\). If nonempty ballots are required and \(q\ge2\), add a fifth candidate \(f\) approved exactly by the \(q-1\) voters in \(R\). Then all ballots are nonempty, the maximum degree remains \(q+1\), the welfare optimizer is still uniquely \(\{a,b,c\}\), and the JR welfare optimum remains \(3q+2\). For \(q=1\), \(R\) is empty already.

## Verification

A standalone C++17 checker (`verify_quota.cpp`) implements the welfare-preserving exchange algorithm, tests the full EJR definition by enumerating every voter group, and checks all support-bounded approval incidence profiles for \(1\le n\le5\), \(1\le m\le4\), and every \(1\le k\le\min(n,m)\). It tested \(1{,}803{,}670\) eligible election/size instances and performed \(508\) exchanges without failure. It additionally checked the sharpness family's arithmetic at \(q=1,\ldots,100\). Compile with `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_quota.cpp -o verify_quota` and run `./verify_quota`. The infinite theorem and the full sharpness family follow from the proof, not from finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Dong (2026) establishes NP-hardness of finding committees jointly satisfying JR and Pareto optimality in the unrestricted approval domain, with a hard construction using candidate support degrees \(3\) and \(4\) at quota \(3\). The present theorem isolates a structurally different degree-bounded domain, supplies a stronger utilitarian optimum together with EJR, and proves the degree cutoff cannot be increased uniformly. Elkind et al. (2024) study the price of JR and EJR and prove general welfare-tradeoff and algorithmic results. Their illustrated welfare-loss profile uses a more extreme degree imbalance than the uniform \(q+1\) witness here. Schünke (2026) studies tractability on interval domains and Pareto-optimal committee reconfiguration rather than this maximum-support domain. These results motivate the exact missing bounded-incidence condition; none of the inspected statements directly supplies the theorem above.

## Limitations

The cutoff is a **uniform sufficient condition with a sharp family of counterexamples just above it**. It does not assert that a particular election of higher maximum degree lacks an EJR welfare optimum, nor that every welfare-optimal committee is EJR under the cutoff: maximizing coverage among welfare optimizers can be necessary. The theorem is about ordinary dichotomous additive welfare and standard EJR, not EJR+, weighted approvals, or fractional committees. The example is not an NP-hardness threshold at degree \(q+1\). Priority relative to unindexed equivalent exchange lemmas is not certified, and no independent review has been performed.
