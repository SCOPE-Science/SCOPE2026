# The sharp local-state cost of violating simplicial belief independence

## Finding
For each integer \(k\ge2\), the least size of an **upward** directed simplicial model at which the higher-arity independence formula
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_k=\Box_a\bigl(p_{b_1}\vee\cdots\vee p_{b_k}\bigr)\to\bigl(\Box_a p_{b_1}\vee\cdots\vee\Box_a p_{b_k}\bigr)
\]
is **false (not merely undefined)** at some face equals \(k+2\) vertices. Here \(a,b_1,\ldots,b_k\) are distinct agents, and each local atom \(p_{b_i}\) belongs to agent \(b_i\). Only one of the \(k\) non-root agents needs two different local states. For \(k=2\), exactly four vertices suffice and are necessary.

## Assumptions and scope
A chromatic simplicial complex is a nonempty family of nonempty faces, closed under nonempty subsets, with at most one vertex of any color per face. Each vertex \(v\) has a direction \(\rho(v)\), a nonempty downward-closed family of faces containing \(v\), including \(\{v\}\). A direction is *upward* if for any \(X,Y\in\rho(v)\) whose union belongs to the complex, the union also belongs to \(\rho(v)\). Each atom \(p_b\) has a Boolean truth assignment at every \(b\)-colored vertex.

Use the weak Kleene three-valued semantics of [1]: an atom is undefined at a face lacking its agent; propositional negation and Boolean connectives are undefined when any argument is undefined. The modal formula \(\Box_a\varphi\) is undefined at \(X\) if \(a\notin\chi(X)\) or if no face in \(\rho(X_a)\) defines \(\varphi\). Otherwise it is true exactly when \(\varphi\) is true at **every** accessible face where it is defined. A false implication is one whose antecedent is true and consequent false, with both defined. No completeness or purity assumption is imposed: counterexamples are required to be upward, but need not be cups.

The higher-arity formula is the iterated disjunction instance of the two-disjunct independence axiom (Ind) of [1]. All mentioned agents are distinct. Other agents may exist but do not reduce the minimum.

## Proof
**Lower bound.** The occurrence of \(\Box_a\) at a face where the formula is false requires at least one \(a\)-vertex. Since every subformula \(\Box_a p_{b_i}\) must be defined and false, there must also be at least one \(b_i\)-vertex for each \(i\). Thus there are at least \(k+1\) vertices. Suppose exactly \(k+1\). Then each relevant color has one vertex and hence every local atom \(p_{b_i}\) has a fixed truth value wherever defined. Since \(\Box_a p_{b_i}\) is defined and false, its unique local atom must be false at its unique vertex. The true antecedent \(\Box_a(\bigvee_i p_{b_i})\) must be defined, so an accessible face contains all \(k\) colors \(b_i\). At that face every disjunct is false, contradicting truth of the antecedent. Consequently at least \(k+2\) vertices are required. The argument does not assume upwardness.

**Construction.** Take a root vertex \(r\) colored \(a\); vertices \(f_i\) colored \(b_i\), all with \(p_{b_i}\) false, for \(1\le i\le k\); and one extra vertex \(t_k\) colored \(b_k\), with \(p_{b_k}\) true. Form the simplicial complex generated by the two maximal faces
\[
F_1=\{r,f_1,\ldots,f_{k-1},t_k\},\qquad F_2=\{r,f_k\}.
\]
Let \(\rho(r)\) contain **all** faces of this complex containing \(r\), and let \(\rho(v)=\{\{v\}\}\) for every other vertex. Every direction is downward closed, and upwardness is immediate: the root's family is the whole star, while each other family is a singleton. The complex is impure, since \(F_2\) omits the agents \(b_i\) for \(i<k\).

Evaluate at \(X=\{r\}\). The disjunction \(\bigvee_i p_{b_i}\) is defined only on faces containing all \(k\) colors. Every such face is \(F_1\), where the atom \(p_{b_k}\) is true. Thus \(\Box_a(\bigvee_i p_{b_i})\) is defined and true. For every \(i<k\), the accessible face \(F_1\) witnesses failure of \(\Box_a p_{b_i}\), since \(p_{b_i}\) is false there. For \(i=k\), the accessible face \(F_2\) witnesses failure of \(\Box_a p_{b_k}\). All boxes are defined. Hence the consequent is false and the independence implication is false. The vertex count is \(1+k+1=k+2\), completing the proof.

## Verification
Run `python3 independence_checker.py`. It implements the three-valued face semantics directly, not a propositional two-valued shortcut. It checks the explicit upward construction for every \(2\le k\le8\) and independently exhausts all three-color, three-vertex chromatic complexes, downward directions, Boolean atomic assignments and root-containing faces for the binary formula: 6,600 pointed valuation/direction cases and no false instances. The general lower bound and construction above, not finite enumeration, establish the result for all \(k\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Van Ditmarsch, Gomes, Randrianomentsoa and Studer [1] introduce directed simplicial models and the upward/cup distinction, and exhibit a **five-vertex** failure of the two-variable Ind axiom in their Figure 3(b) and surrounding explanation. They do not state an optimal size or general \(k\)-arity vertex threshold in the inspected definitions, independence discussion and searched full-text headings. The present result supplies a four-vertex witness, proves its optimality, and gives an exact threshold and construction for every arity. Their soundness/completeness theory and the fact that cups validate Ind are prior inputs, not claimed here. The related earlier work on simplicial belief [2] is broader semantic background and is not asserted to contain or not contain this numerical optimum without a complete full-text priority check.

## Limitations
The theorem covers the concrete independent-local-atom schema, not every formula of the full language. The models are upward but **impure**; the result does not exhibit a counterexample within cups, where Ind is valid. Three-valued *undefined* outcomes are not counterexamples. The first arity is \(k=2\). No minimal number of faces, formula length, or independent audit is claimed. Earlier equivalent presentations under other simplicial-logic terminology remain a historical-priority risk.
