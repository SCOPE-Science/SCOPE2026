# The sharp nine-ray threshold for multiplicity-one Cox rings of smooth toric point blow-ups

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. If \(X\) is any smooth complete toric surface with at most eight rays (equivalently, Picard number at most six), then the Cox ring of the blow-up of \(X\) at the identity \(e\) of its open torus is generated in Rees multiplicity one. The bound is sharp: there is a smooth complete nine-ray toric surface for which this generation property fails. More precisely, failure on any smooth complete toric surface forces a positively spanning triple of rays with **at least two other rays in each of its three cyclic intervals**. The last condition supplies a geometric obstruction and forces at least nine rays.

Generation in Rees multiplicity one means that the saturated extended Rees presentation of the blow-up Cox ring is generated by the polynomial Cox ring of \(X\), the exceptional variable \(t\), and the elements \(I_Xt^{-1}\) of exceptional multiplicity one; no generator of larger exceptional multiplicity is required. Here \(I_X\) is the toric-point lattice ideal defined below.

## Assumptions and scope

Throughout, \(N\simeq\mathbb Z^2\), \(M=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb Z)\), \(\Sigma\) is a smooth complete two-dimensional fan, and \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\) are its primitive rays in counterclockwise order. Let \(X=X_\Sigma\). Every complete toric surface is projective, so the projective surface criterion cited below applies. In dimension two, smoothness means \(\det(v_i,v_{i+1})=1\), with cyclic indices, and the Picard number is \(r-2\).

Write \(R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]\). For \(u\in M\) let \(\ell(u)=(\langle u,v_1\rangle,\ldots,\langle u,v_r\rangle)\), and set
\[
I_X=\big\langle x^{\ell(u)_+}-x^{\ell(u)_-}:u\in M\big\rangle\subseteq R.
\]
For a three-element index set \(A\), put \(P_A=(x_i:i\in A)\). A triple is *positively spanning* when its three rays constitute a complete three-ray fan, or equivalently are the vertices of a triangle containing the origin in its interior.

## Proof

**1. The source criterion.** Laface–Ugaglia (2026), Theorem 3, states that the Cox ring of \(\operatorname{Bl}_eX\) is generated in Rees multiplicity one precisely when \(I_X\not\subseteq P_A^2\) for every positively spanning three-ray subset \(A\). We apply this proved equivalence; the subsequent fan-counting and sharp numerical threshold are established here.

**2. Sparse-interval lemma.** Let \(A\) consist of positively spanning rays \(v,w,z\), taken in counterclockwise order. Suppose that the interval between \(v\) and \(w\) contains at most one other ray of the smooth fan. We show \(I_X\not\subseteq P_A^2\) by producing a character whose lattice binomial has one monomial of \(A\)-degree exactly one.

If that interval contains no other ray, smoothness permits unimodular coordinates \(v=(1,0)\) and \(w=(0,1)\). Positive spanning forces \(z=(a,b)\) with \(a<0\) and \(b<0\). The dual vector \(u=(-1,0)\) has pairings \((-1,0,-a)\) with \((v,w,z)\). Hence the negative monomial of its binomial has exactly one factor among the variables indexed by \(A\). In particular the binomial does not lie in \(P_A^2\).

If the interval contains exactly one ray \(q\), we choose unimodular coordinates \(v=(1,0)\) and \(q=(0,1)\). Since both consecutive cones are smooth, \(w=(-1,m)\) for an integer \(m\). Write \(z=(a,b)\). If \(a\geq0\) choose \(u=(1,0)\), giving pairings \((1,-1,a)\) with \((v,w,z)\); if \(a<0\), choose \(u=(-1,0)\), giving \((-1,1,-a)\). In either case one monomial has \(A\)-degree one. The character binomial belongs to \(I_X\) by definition, so \(I_X\not\subseteq P_A^2\). Cyclic permutation proves the same conclusion for either other interval.

**3. The universal lower bound.** Suppose the generation property fails. By the source criterion, there is a positively spanning triple \(A\) with \(I_X\subseteq P_A^2\). The sparse-interval lemma implies that each of its three cyclic intervals contains at least two other rays. The intervals are pairwise disjoint apart from their endpoints. Consequently
\[
r\geq3+2+2+2=9.
\]
Thus \(r\leq8\) excludes every obstruction, and the Cox ring is generated in Rees multiplicity one. Since \(\rho(X)=r-2\), the equivalent Picard-number bound is \(\rho(X)\leq6\).

**4. A nine-ray obstruction.** Consider the following nine primitive rays, in counterclockwise order:
\[
(1,0),\ (0,1),\ (-1,1),\ (-2,1),\ (-3,1),\ (-1,0),\ (1,-1),\ (3,-2),\ (2,-1).
\]
All nine cyclic adjacent determinants are exactly one; the ordered rays wind once about the origin, so they define a smooth complete fan, and hence a smooth projective toric surface of Picard number seven. Select the three rays in positions \(2,5,8\) using one-based indices:
\[
v=(0,1),\qquad w=(-3,1),\qquad z=(3,-2).
\]
Their sum is zero and each of their cyclic pairwise determinants equals three, so they positively span and each of their cyclic intervals contains exactly two other fan rays.

For any \(u=(a,b)\in M\), its three pairings are
\[
q_1=b,\qquad q_2=b-3a,\qquad q_3=3a-2b.
\]
They satisfy \(q_1+q_2+q_3=0\) and \(q_1\equiv q_2\equiv q_3\pmod3\). If \(u\ne0\), the pairings are not all zero because the selected rays span \(N_\mathbb R\). Since they sum to zero, both their positive and negative parts are nonzero. If either part had total degree one, the triple of pairings would have to be a permutation of \((1,-1,0)\), contradicting congruence modulo three. Therefore both monomials of every nonzero character binomial have total \(A\)-degree at least two. Thus every binomial generator of \(I_X\) is in \(P_A^2\), and hence
\[
I_X\subseteq P_A^2.
\]
The source criterion now gives failure of generation in Rees multiplicity one. This realizes the minimal possible nine rays and completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone exact-integer script `CHECK.py` verifies all nine cyclic determinants, the three selected determinants, the interval lengths, the zero-sum relation, and 2,600 nonzero bounded dual vectors. It prints `NINE_RAY_FAN_OK 9 TRIPLE_GAPS_OK INTEGER_TESTS_OK 2600` on success. These finite tests are independent arithmetic corroboration, **not** the proof of the universal statement or of containment for all characters: the written sparse-interval argument and the modulo-three argument prove the respective unbounded claims.

## Relationship to prior work

Laface–Ugaglia, *Multiplicity-One Cox Rings of Toric Point Blow-Ups* (arXiv:2608.25554, 2026), Theorem 3, supplies the necessary-and-sufficient criterion in terms of positively spanning three-ray subsets. Its Corollary 4 treats minimal rank-two toric surfaces; Proposition 4.4 and its proof exhibit an exceptional three-ray Gorenstein fan and its role in a special class of partial resolutions. The present finding does **not** claim that the obstructing triangle or the three-ray criterion is new. Its additional statement is the uniform, sharp ray-count/Picard-number threshold across **all smooth complete toric surfaces**, based on the sparse-interval lemma, together with a nine-ray smooth witness.

Laface–Ugaglia, *On blowing up minimal toric surfaces* (arXiv:2403.05942, 2024; journal 2025), concerns minimal Picard-rank-two surfaces and establishes precursor criteria. Hausen–Keicher–Laface, *Computing Cox rings* (arXiv:1305.4343; Math. Comp. 2016), proves finite generation for smooth rational surfaces of Picard number at most six. Finite generation is weaker than generation in **Rees multiplicity one**, and the Picard number of the point blow-up is one greater than that of the original surface. These results therefore do not give the precise generation threshold proved here.

## Limitations

The theorem applies over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero, to smooth complete toric **surfaces**, and to the blow-up of a point in the open torus. It does not assert that every nine-ray surface fails the property, classify all nine-ray obstructions, or determine the degrees and numbers of every higher-multiplicity Cox generator of the witness. The main proof relies on the published three-ray criterion; the independent verification of that published theorem lies outside this work. Historical-priority checks cannot establish uniqueness and should be complemented by expert literature review, particularly concerning consequences of the exceptional Gorenstein three-ray fan.
