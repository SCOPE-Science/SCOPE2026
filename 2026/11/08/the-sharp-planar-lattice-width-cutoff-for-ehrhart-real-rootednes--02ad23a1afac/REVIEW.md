# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Pick's theorem gives \(h^*(z)=1+(B+I-3)z+Iz^2\). The complete discriminant split is elementary and exact. The published planar one-point flatness theorem removes low-interior-point width cases, including its rigid equality class. For the only possible nonpositive discriminants with \(I\ge2\), all primitive normal-form parameters \(D=5,7,9\) are enumerated without omission using coprimality, ten small-width witnesses, and a congruence obstruction to width at most two for the remaining \(D=7\) class. An explicit integer determinant-one matrix proves the last pair equivalent. With positive coefficients and strict discriminant, the two roots are distinct and strictly negative. An extracted standalone verifier passes 151 exact finite checks; the analytic proof supplies universal quantifiers. Risk: correct use of the source's rigidity statement is essential, and has been compared with the actual public Theorem 1.1.

## Originality — PASS (historically provisional)

Nill's large-width result is existential in dimension two, and expressly leaves effective constants unoptimized. Averkov–Codenotti–Freyer–Huang classify width of polygons with at most one interior point, but make no assertion about all Ehrhart roots. Hamm classifies lattice triangles by their first two widths and explores Ehrhart pairs; the 2016 Ehrhart-triangle work provides geometric and pair constraints. These imply portions of the exceptional-case enumeration, but none alone asserts that every polygon of width four has two distinct negative roots with exactly one obstruction at width three. Higuchi's fixed-width multiplicity work plausibly subsumes the finite width-three triangle multiplicity step, but does not, in available sections, state the global roots threshold. Semantic mathematical-record searches and accessible historical claims showed no equivalent formulation. Historical equivalents remain possible, particularly with different terminology. This is a novel-looking sharp synthesis, not a claim that its component lemmas are novel.

## Value — PASS

The motivating 2026 width/real-rootedness theorem gives no sharp dimension-two threshold. The exact threshold \(4\), equivalently sharp strict-cutoff constant \(3\), together with a rigid unique width-three failure, is a natural complete geometric boundary classification for the direct quantitative question. It separates a qualitative asymptotic statement from an effective sharp fact for all lattice polygons, not an arbitrary selected example or a mere coefficient rewrite. A short low-dimensional proof is appropriate to the scope; no higher-dimensional claim is suggested.
