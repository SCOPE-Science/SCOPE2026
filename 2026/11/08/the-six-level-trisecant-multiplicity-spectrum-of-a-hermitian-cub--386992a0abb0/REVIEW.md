## Originality (O): PASS with residual historical-priority risk
Bartoli–Marcugini–Pambianco, arXiv:1510.08375, already prove the underlying \(81\)-point cubic is a complete arc (Table 99). Korchmáros–Nagy–Szőnyi, arXiv:2302.10162, give the Hermitian curve's line characters, an exponent-at-least-four completeness theorem and the exponent-three question. Bartoli–Timpanella (2025) establish completeness for sufficiently large Hermitian parameters with asymptotic lower estimates; those statements do not give the six-value distribution for the binary cubic. The claim explicitly excludes the pre-existing completeness result and instead establishes the exact external secant-multiplicity spectrum, its sharp elevenfold covering threshold and the maximum locus. Searches using the curve, field size, six multiplicities, three-secants, complete arcs and equivalent coding terminology found no explicit covering result in the examined primary literature or mathematical finding records. A differently indexed older finite-geometry incidence table remains possible; these searches do not establish absolute uniqueness.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
The number of full secants through an external point measures the redundancy of an arc's completeness witness; it is a natural, precisely defined invariant relevant to the algebraic-curve arc and its associated near-MDS code. Binary completeness (at least one line) does not imply an elevenfold lower bound or a six-level multiplicity spectrum. The maximal multiplicity is further characterized geometrically by the complement of the embedded \(\mathbb F_4\) subplane. Although the statement concerns one small field and is proved by finite enumeration, its complete incidence distribution and nontrivial extremal stratum are motivated by the published Hermitian-arc completeness problem rather than by an arbitrary coefficient slice.
