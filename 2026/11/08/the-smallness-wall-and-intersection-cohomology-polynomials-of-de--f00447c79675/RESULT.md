# The smallness wall and intersection-cohomology polynomials of degree-two quadric-cycle loci

## Finding
Let \(1\le p<n\) be integers, set \(k=p+1\), \(N=n+1\) and \(b=\binom{k+2}{2}-1\). Write \(C_{p,2}(\mathbf P^n)\) for the reduced complex degree-two Chow variety, and let \(Q_{p,n}\) be the reduced closed irreducible image of the span-incidence parameterization of quadrics (including split and doubled quadrics). The map
\[
g:Y=\mathbf P_{T}(\operatorname{Sym}^2 S^\vee)\longrightarrow Q_{p,n},\qquad T=\operatorname{Gr}(k+1,\mathbf C^N),
\]
where \(S\) is the tautological rank-\(k+1\) subbundle, is small if and only if
\[
n<2k+\binom{k}{2},
\]
and semismall if and only if
\[
n\le 2k+\binom{k}{2}.
\]
For every such small case,
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}\dim_{\mathbf Q} IH^{2j}(Q_{p,n};\mathbf Q)\,q^j
=\begin{bmatrix}n+1\\k+1\end{bmatrix}_{q}\sum_{a=0}^{b}q^a.
\]
At the unique equality boundary \(n=2k+\binom{k}{2}\), the polynomial becomes
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}\dim_{\mathbf Q} IH^{2j}(Q_{p,n};\mathbf Q)\,q^j
=\begin{bmatrix}n+1\\k+1\end{bmatrix}_{q}\sum_{a=0}^{b}q^a
-q^{n-k}\begin{bmatrix}n+1\\k\end{bmatrix}_{q}.
\]
Here the bracket is the Gaussian binomial polynomial. All rational odd-degree intersection cohomology vanishes in the small and equality ranges, and the even groups are pure of Hodge type \((j,j)\). In particular, for plane-conic cycles in \(\mathbf P^5\), the boundary intersection Betti sequence is \((1,2,4,6,9,11,13,13,13,11,9,6,4,2,1)\), with Euler characteristic \(105\).

## Assumptions and scope
All spaces are reduced complex projective varieties and all intersection cohomology uses middle perversity, rational coefficients, and the conventional unshifted grading (equal to ordinary cohomological grading on smooth spaces). The map to the Chow variety is the algebraic morphism described in Chen--Hu--Ma, Section 3. The image \(Q_{p,n}\) is the quadric-cycle locus, not the entire Chow variety when a second component is present. Its definition also covers \(n=p+1\), when the two loci need not be distinct irreducible components. The symbol \(q\) records half the actual cohomological degree.

## Proof
**The morphism and its fibers.** The smooth variety \(Y\) is a projective bundle with fiber \(\mathbf P^b\) over \(T\). Its points are a \((k+1)\)-dimensional subspace \(W\subset\mathbf C^N\) and a quadratic equation on \(\mathbf P(W)\). The associated effective cycle is the quadric hypersurface in \(\mathbf P(W)\). Every irreducible quadric has unique linear span \(\mathbf P(W)\), and a union of two distinct linear \(p\)-planes contained in a common \((p+1)\)-plane also determines its ambient span uniquely. By Chen--Hu--Ma, Proposition 3.1 and its proof, the only positive-dimensional fibers occur over doubled linear cycles \(2[\mathbf P(E)]\), with \(E\in G=\operatorname{Gr}(k,\mathbf C^N)\); the full fiber is
\[
g^{-1}(2[\mathbf P(E)])=\mathbf P(\mathbf C^N/E)\cong\mathbf P^{n-k}.
\]
Thus \(g\) is proper and birational onto its reduced irreducible image; since the source is smooth, it is a resolution of that image. There are no other positive-dimensional fibers. The doubled-cycle locus \(Z\) is isomorphic to \(G\) and is smooth; over \(Z\), the exceptional family is the projectivized universal quotient bundle.

**Exact codimension.** Grassmannian and projective-bundle dimensions give
\[
\dim Y=(k+1)(n-k)+\binom{k+2}{2}-1,\qquad
\dim Z=k(n+1-k).
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{codim}_{Q_{p,n}} Z
=\dim Y-\dim Z=n+\binom{k}{2}.
\]
The only fiber-dimension jump is \(r=n-k\) along \(Z\). The standard semismall inequality is \(2r\le\operatorname{codim}Z\), while strict inequality characterizes smallness; these are respectively \(n\le2k+\binom{k}{2}\) and \(n<2k+\binom{k}{2}\). When the inequality is reversed, the positive-dimensional fiber stratum violates semismallness. If \(r=0\), all fibers are zero-dimensional, and the same strict criterion holds.

**Intersection cohomology in the small range.** A proper small resolution satisfies \(Rg_*\mathbf Q_Y[\dim Y]\simeq IC_{Q_{p,n}}\). Consequently \(IH^j(Q_{p,n})\cong H^j(Y)\), compatibly with rational pure Hodge structures. By the projective-bundle formula and the Schubert-cell decomposition of \(T\),
\[
P_Y(q)=\begin{bmatrix}N\\k+1\end{bmatrix}_q(1+q+\cdots+q^b).
\]
This proves the first formula.

**The equality boundary.** When \(n=2k+\binom{k}{2}\), the codimension of \(Z\) equals \(2r\). The open dense stratum and the smooth closed stratum \(Z\) are the only relevant strata for the projective semismall decomposition. The top homology of each exceptional fiber \(\mathbf P^r\) has rank one, generated by its complex fundamental class. Thus the relevant local system on \(Z\) is constant of rank one. The projective semismall decomposition theorem of de Cataldo--Migliorini (Theorem 3.4.1) yields, after the correct cohomological shift,
\[
H^j(Y,\mathbf Q)\cong IH^j(Q_{p,n},\mathbf Q)\oplus H^{j-2r}(G,\mathbf Q)
\]
as rational vector spaces. In pure Hodge structures the final term has the Tate twist \((-r)\). Since \(P_G(q)=\begin{bmatrix}N\\k\end{bmatrix}_q\), the extra summand contributes \(q^r P_G(q)\) and subtraction gives the displayed boundary polynomial. All summands are pure Tate and even because \(Y\) and \(G\) possess even-dimensional Schubert/projective-bundle cell structures. This proves the claims.

## Verification
The accompanying self-contained \(verify.py\) evaluates Gaussian binomial polynomials by an independent integer-coefficient recurrence, checks the threshold's exact dimension identity and Poincare duality/nonnegativity in a range of parameters, and verifies three explicit conic examples. It checks the boundary \((p,n)=(1,5)\) sequence and Euler characteristic \(105\). It cannot computationally substitute for the geometric fiber classification and the semismall decomposition theorem. The arguments above supply those non-finite steps.

## Relationship to prior work
Chen, Hu and Ma establish the incidence diagram and exceptional fibers and calculate **ordinary** cohomology of the entire degree-two Chow variety. Neither the smallness threshold nor the intersection-cohomology polynomials of the closed quadric-cycle locus are stated in the inspected manuscript. Hu's earlier work gives the degree-two Chow decomposition and virtual invariants, without this specific middle-perversity calculation. The semismall decomposition and its relevant-stratum multiplicity are general theorems of de Cataldo and Migliorini, not newly claimed. The contribution here is the identification of the exact geometric wall and its uniform explicit intersection-cohomology consequences for this natural singular cycle locus. Priority cannot be guaranteed by a finite literature search.

## Limitations
The polynomial formulas cover only the small and equality semismall ranges; no assertion about the non-semismall intersection cohomology is made. The theorem concerns the reduced irreducible image \(Q_{p,n}\), not the intersection cohomology of the generally reducible full Chow variety. The decomposition theorem is invoked as a published theorem rather than independently reproved. No integral intersection-cohomology torsion calculation is attempted. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
