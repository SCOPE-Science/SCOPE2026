# Review of the sparse Stiefel graph theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The support-rigidity lemma applies to every real exponent in the stated range. With exactly one extra row there cannot be two columns with two occupied coordinates, so the full space is a finite graph rather than merely a stratum-by-stratum union. Its signed coordinate vertices, all open-interval edges, connectivity under hole moves, type-D group action, and resulting fundamental-group rank are proved for arbitrary dimension. The Euclidean normalization uses a concrete full-turn loop. The finite exact checker was executed for dimensions 2 through 6; it is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)
The initiating paper of Dalfollo-Daley, Delfín, Fernández, Mars and Xia (arxiv:2610.32615v1) gives a Lamperti classification and the homeomorphism type of each *fixed-support* stratum, but no global gluing graph, type-D Cayley identification, or fundamental group. The ordinary permutation star graph is based on the smaller symmetric group, and Brown's Hilbert/C*-partial-isometry framework concerns a larger space. The injective-word moment-angle complex of Conceição is a distinct polyhedral product. Searches for formulations via signed star transpositions, graph connectivity, restricted embeddings, and free loop rank identified no stronger covering statement. Historical priority is not independently certified, and older group-theoretic terminology remains a possible overlap risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem supplies a natural exact global topological invariant requested by the geometry of restricted isometries: the first spare output coordinate converts isolated monomial embeddings into a connected graph with factorially growing first Betti number. The same operator space maps onto the ordinary real Stiefel manifold, whose fundamental group is much smaller. This is a structural boundary result, not simply a computation for a selected dimension.

## Scope and limitations
The field is real, the spaces are unweighted, the exponent is finite and unequal to two, and the output dimension exceeds the input dimension by exactly one. Complex phases, additional spare rows, and the Hilbert exponent lead to different topology. No publication-priority certificate or independent audit is asserted. The provided checker verifies finite incidences; the analytical proof handles all dimensions.
