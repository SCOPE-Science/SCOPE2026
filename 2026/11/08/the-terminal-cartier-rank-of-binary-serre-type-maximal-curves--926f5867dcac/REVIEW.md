# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The geometric hypotheses and the monomial differential basis are established for the nonsingular normalization in characteristic two. A direct \(2^{n-1}\)-power identity yields an exhaustive congruence test for every basis differential. Exactly \(s\) differentials survive, their images are distinct and even-powered, and the next Cartier iterate vanishes. The standalone checker independently computes and compares the terminal action for seven values of \(n\); these finite checks supplement, rather than replace, the universal argument.

**Originality: PASS (best-knowledge, qualified).** Shafighi's 2026 paper gives the \(a\)-number and a one-step Cartier matrix for the same curves; Garcia--Tafazolian (2008) proves the known bound \(\mathcal C^n=0\). Neither inspected source states the exact penultimate rank or its image basis. Groen's generic rank bounds do not give this degree-four equality, and a 2026 higher-rank paper treats different curves. The one-step formula entails computability of all iterates in principle, and the original 2009 construction could not be read in full; these are explicit residual priority risks. No claim of exhaustive novelty certification is made.

**Value: PASS.** This answers the sharpness question for the natural maximality-based Cartier vanishing bound across an infinite family, not a parameter-free textbook deduction from nilpotency. The last nonzero rank \((n-1)/2\), its explicit differential basis, and the count of longest nilpotent chains refine the known \(a\)-number and \(2\)-rank. They identify natural data relevant to the Cartier rank filtration and Ekedahl--Oort analysis without purporting to compute the entire type.

**Scope and limits.** Only the \(q=2\) family and its penultimate iterate are treated; intermediate iterates, other prime powers, and complete torsion group schemes are not classified. Historical-priority comparison is necessarily qualified by access limits to the 2009 article.
