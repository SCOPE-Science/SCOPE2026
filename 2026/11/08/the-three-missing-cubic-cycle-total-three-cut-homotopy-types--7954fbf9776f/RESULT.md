# The three missing cubic-cycle total three-cut homotopy types

## Finding

Let \(C_n^3\) be the third power of the simple cycle on the vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\); two distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when their cyclic distance is at most three. Write \(BI_3(G)\) for the simplicial complex whose faces are vertex sets containing no independent three-element subset of \(G\). Write \(\Delta^t_3(G)\) for the simplicial complex generated by complements of independent triples, equivalently the combinatorial Alexander dual of \(BI_3(G)\).

The complete homotopy classification at the three lengths \(n\in\{13,14,15\}\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
BI_3(C_{13}^3)&\simeq S^5,&\qquad \Delta^t_3(C_{13}^3)&\simeq S^5,\\
BI_3(C_{14}^3)&\simeq S^5,& \Delta^t_3(C_{14}^3)&\simeq S^6,\\
BI_3(C_{15}^3)&\simeq S^4\vee S^4,& \Delta^t_3(C_{15}^3)&\simeq S^8\vee S^8.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every equivalence is integral. In particular, each of the three pure total three-cut complexes is non-shellable, because its nontrivial reduced homology occurs strictly below its top simplicial dimension.

These three lengths constitute the *entire* unresolved parameter window in Question 49 of Carnero Bravo, *Total cut complexes and their duals*, when both the bound on independence and the cycle-power exponent equal three.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set is labelled \(\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\), with all adjacency and distances computed modulo \(n\). An independent triple has three pairwise cyclic distances strictly greater than three. The empty face belongs to \(BI_3\), but the discrete-Morse matching below acts only on nonempty faces and therefore retains an explicit critical vertex. The theorem makes three exhaustive finite claims, one for each value \(n\in\{13,14,15\}\); it makes no assertion for arbitrary lengths.

For a complex \(K\) on \(n\) vertices, its Alexander dual is \(K^* =\{\sigma\subseteq[n]: [n]\setminus\sigma\notin K\}\). Because the minimal nonfaces of \(BI_3(C_n^3)\) are exactly the independent triples, the facets of its dual are their complements; hence \(BI_3(C_n^3)^*=\Delta_3^t(C_n^3)\) without additional assumptions.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive models without sampled inputs.** Represent a subset \(\sigma\) by the integer bitmask \(m(\sigma)=\sum_{i\in\sigma}2^i\). Enumerate all triples of vertices \(\{a,b,c\}\), and retain exactly those for which every cyclic pairwise distance exceeds three. Denote this complete list by \(F_n\). Then enumerate every mask \(0\le m<2^n\): it represents a face of \(BI_3(C_n^3)\) precisely when \((m\mathbin{\&}t)\ne t\) for every \(t\in F_n\). The exhaustive lists contain \(13,28,50\) independent triples and \(2757,3277,3964\) faces (including the empty face) at the three lengths, respectively. These operations use exact finite integers and Boolean logic.

**2. Explicit complete discrete-Morse matching.** On the nonempty face poset, initialize \(R\) to all nonempty faces. For each \(i=0,1,\ldots,n-1\), visit the members \(\sigma\) of the then-current \(R\) in ascending bitmask order, and whenever \(i\notin\sigma\) and \(\sigma\cup\{i\}\in R\), match the cover pair \((\sigma,\sigma\cup\{i\})\) and remove both faces from \(R\). Once removed, faces never reappear. This is a completely specified partial matching on every nonempty face.

For an independently checkable acyclicity certificate, construct the **complete** Hasse graph of all nonempty faces: for every face \(\tau\) and each \(v\in\tau\) with \(\tau\setminus\{v\}\ne\varnothing\), include the cover relation \(\tau\succ\tau\setminus\{v\}\). Orient all unmatched covers downwards and all matched covers upwards. Execute Kahn's topological sort over *all* vertices and directed edges. The provided verifier asserts that all vertices are visited, ruling out every directed cycle; no selected subset of the Hasse graph is omitted. The Hasse graph contains respectively \(13663,16100,19500\) cover edges, and the matching has \(1377,1637,1980\) pairs.

The remaining critical faces, displayed by their actual vertex sets, are:

- At \(n=13\): \(\{0\}\) and \(\{4,5,7,8,10,11\}\).
- At \(n=14\): \(\{0\}\) and \(\{5,7,8,10,11,13\}\).
- At \(n=15\): \(\{0\}\), \(\{4,7,10,12,13\}\), and \(\{5,8,11,12,14\}\).

Every covering relation, pair and unmatched face is generated and checked by the standalone `bounded_cycle_verifier.py`. Because the matching is acyclic on the complete face poset, Forman's discrete Morse theorem provides a CW model containing precisely one zero-cell and respectively one five-cell, one five-cell, and two four-cells. No positive-dimensional lower cells remain, so all their attaching maps are constant, and their homotopy types are exactly the asserted sphere wedges over \(\mathbb Z\). This is a finite exhaustive proof rather than an inference from homology ranks.

**3. Passage to the total-cut complexes.** By combinatorial Alexander duality on an \(n\)-vertex set,
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_3^t(C_n^3);\mathbb Z)
\cong \widetilde H^{n-j-3}(BI_3(C_n^3);\mathbb Z).
\]
Thus the dual complexes have only the following nonzero reduced integral homology: rank one in dimension five at \(n=13\), rank one in dimension six at \(n=14\), and rank two in dimension eight at \(n=15\). The verifier independently checks, for **every** subset of size at most three, that it lies in the dual complex: an independent triple disjoint from it exists. Therefore all three duals contain the complete two-skeleton of the simplex, and in particular are simply connected (the complete two-skeleton fills every edge loop). By the Hurewicz theorem and the homology form of Whitehead's theorem, a simply connected finite CW complex with reduced integral homology concentrated in degree \(q\ge2\) and free of rank \(b\) is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^b S^q\). This proves the claimed homotopy types of the duals, not just their homology.

**4. Shellability consequence.** Independent triples exist at all three lengths, so \(\Delta_3^t(C_n^3)\) is pure of dimension \(n-4\). The calculated nonzero homology occurs in degrees five, six and eight, respectively; each is strictly less than \(n-4\). A nonempty pure shellable complex has reduced homology only in top dimension. Consequently none of these three dual complexes is shellable.

## Verification

Run `python3 bounded_cycle_verifier.py` with standard Python 3. The script requires no external library. It recomputes every independent triple and face from the graph definition, reconstructs and verifies all matching pairs and complete gradient digraphs, performs independent mod-two boundary rank elimination, enumerates the entire dual two-skeleton, and checks exact critical face identities.

The recorded deterministic output is stored in `checks.txt`. The cases yield respectively \(13,28,50\) independent triples; \(2757,3277,3964\) faces (with the empty face); \(1377,1637,1980\) Morse pairs; and reduced mod-two Betti vectors concentrated as predicted. The program prints `ALL_BOUNDED_CYCLE_CHECKS_OK` only if every listed test passes. Crucially, the **integral** homotopy statements follow from the complete acyclic matchings and the Alexander-duality/simple-connectivity argument, not from mod-two ranks alone.

## Relationship to prior work

The precise motivation is Question 49 of Carnero Bravo (arXiv:2602.21427), which asks for the bounded-independence and total-cut homotopy types throughout \((r+1)d+1\le n\le2rd-r\) when \(d,r\ge3\). Setting \(d=r=3\) gives exactly \(13\le n\le15\), which the present exhaustive classification resolves. The same paper proves results at larger lengths, including \(n\ge16\) for the adjacent parameter regime; those results do not determine these cases.

Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced the total-cut construction and studied cycles and squared cycles. Shen, Song, Yu, Zhou and Zhuang treated total-cut complexes of squared cycles. Chauhan, Shukla and Vinayak treated total **two**-cut complexes of cycle powers. Chauhan and Shukla studied ordinary **three**-cut complexes, which are defined by *disconnected* triples and are not the total three-cut complexes generated by *independent* triples. None of those statements entails the three homotopy types above. The standard Alexander-duality and Morse results are used with attribution, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The finding covers only the three integers \(n=13,14,15\) and the specific parameters \(d=r=3\). It does not settle Question 49 for other parameters, prove a global recurrence, establish canonical homotopy equivalences or derive equivariant homotopy information. The matching certificate is finite and explicitly executable; its completeness relies on enumerating the entire small face poset, and the calculation may be memory-intensive for substantially larger lengths. No independent external mathematical audit is asserted.
