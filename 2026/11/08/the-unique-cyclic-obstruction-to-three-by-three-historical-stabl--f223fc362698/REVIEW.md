# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The entry/exit reachability claim is reduced to stable five-agent predecessors. Each such predecessor is achievable by an entry-only history because an imbalanced submarket with only two agents on its smaller side has at most two stable matchings, both obtained through incremental deferred acceptance from opposite proposing sides. The last-entry argument proves necessity even after arbitrary repeated departures and re-entries. Two independent implementations enumerated every \(6^6=46{,}656\) complete strict profile, found the same four-cell table, and checked the 12 deficient profiles against the explicit 12-element orbit. The finite classification does not rely on extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Yang (2026) exhibits and proves nonreachability for one cyclic market, hence supplies the 12-profile lower bound under relabeling, but does not exclude other deficient profiles. The new statement is the matching upper bound and complete orbit characterization. Cheng (2016) investigates random-order-reachable matchings but does not supply this table. Existing exact three-by-three stability counts alone do not determine historical reachability: of 1,092 three-stable profiles, 1,080 have complete reachability and 12 do not. Searches of bibliographic literature, equivalent descriptions and research records found no exact covering claim; unindexed prior work remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A recent impossibility example is shown to be the sole obstruction class at the first nontrivial complete size. The precise probability \(1/3888\) measures how rarely outcome stability can be sharpened by missing history under impartial culture, while the orbit classification identifies exactly the exceptional preference structure. This is a motivated finite invariant, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limitations and nearby results

Yang's counterexample is prior; the new work neither discovers the example nor proves historical completeness for larger populations. The last-entry criterion is specialized to three-by-three because all five-agent stable predecessors can be reached. Earlier stability-profile sequences count stable outcomes but not their historical accessibility. The implementation is a reproducible exhaustive checker, not independent validation of novelty.
