**Correctness — PASS.** The stochastic event convention, discounted killed-walk boundary fluxes and renewal mean are explicitly fixed. Exponential coefficient comparison proves necessity and sufficiency for *all* neutral distributions, not only guessed symmetric distributions. Symmetric flux averaging produces a closed hyperbolic first-cycle success formula; its log derivative is strictly positive because \(k>\theta\), forcing the unique endpoint pair. Renewal then transfers the strict ordering to every starting state. The infinite-interval asymptotics are derived directly from the closed formula. The included exact finite-chain checker tests 3666 rational/numerical assertions, but finite testing is not represented as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** The 2026 Vega Coso work provides the neutral pair-weight relations and first-cycle splitting flux and is not treated as new; its inspected model, theorem, two-site corollary and conclusion do not optimize mean absorption time across pair supports. The 2011, 2023, 2024 and May 2026 distributed-reset sources address different geometries, an absorbing target at only one site, or optimization over reset *rate* rather than all-rate splitting-neutral *distributions*. Compared statements do not contain the unique discrete edge-pair ordering or the exponential latency separation. A more general unindexed renewal or reset optimization theorem is a live publication-priority risk; searching alone does not prove global novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The neutral constraint fixes left/right fate but does not fix the time needed to reach either endpoint. The theorem completely classifies the unique fastest spatial reset law for arbitrary interval length, bias and positive reset rate, and quantifies a striking exponentially large performance gap from a different neutral policy, supplying an interpretable design rule rather than a routine isolated computation.

Scientific limitations: fixed iid rate-independent reset distribution, instantaneous reset, no relocation cost, expected time until either endpoint, finite interval; different criteria or an arbitrary reset distribution are outside the theorem. The comparison of large-interval means uses different reset-distributed initializations and says so explicitly. No claim of independent validation is made.
