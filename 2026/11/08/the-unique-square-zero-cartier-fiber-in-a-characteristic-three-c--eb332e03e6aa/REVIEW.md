# Scientific review: unique square-zero Cartier fiber of a cyclic quintic family

## Correctness (C) — PASS

For every algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\) and every smooth parameter \(\lambda\ne0,1\), tame Riemann--Hurwitz gives genus \(4\). Valuation checks at the finite branch points and infinity establish the stated four-dimensional basis of regular one-forms. The standard characteristic-three Cartier monomial rule gives the full inverse-Frobenius-semilinear matrix in that basis. Its image has dimension \(2\); its square is supported only on \(e_1\mapsto e_2\), with the ninth power of the sole coefficient equal to \(-\lambda^2(1+\lambda)(\lambda-1)^2\), and its cube vanishes. The indicated factorization establishes the zero-locus over every algebraically closed characteristic-three field, not merely over a finite-field sample. The standalone exact checker independently expands the coefficients of the branch polynomial and tests twisted matrix composition over three fields. The conclusions about \(a\)-number and \(3\)-rank follow from standard Cartier criteria.

**Risk/limit:** The theorem does not infer a complete Ekedahl--Oort or Newton-polygon stratification solely from the Cartier ranks. Those classifications are not claimed.

## Originality (O) — PASS (self-review)

The exact parameter criterion \(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C^2=0\iff\lambda=-1\) is not identified in the inspected closest primary literature. Cavalieri--Pries (arXiv:2308.14891, Example 6.2 and Section 6.1) studies precisely the family \(M[11]\), but its displayed special-family mass and \(a\)-number formulas are conditioned on \(p\equiv1\pmod5\). Li--Mantovan--Pries--Tang (arXiv:1805.06914, Section 6.2) covers the same \(M[11]\) moduli locus, gives the generic \(\mu\)-ordinary module \(N_{4,2}\) for \(p\equiv2,3\pmod5\), and allows the supersingular basic polygon, but supplies no parameter-by-parameter Cartier-square vanishing criterion. Lin--Mantovan--Singhal (arXiv:2406.09632) uses a \(p>15\) condition in the most pertinent degree-five existence result, excluding \(p=3\). The later explicit examples of Streng (arXiv:2501.14902) treat \(M[6],M[8],M[16]\), not this one-parameter classification.

Semantic searches for aliases of this condition, the harmonic special parameter, and the relevant Cartier matrix identified no equivalent published statement. Existing published possibilities for a Newton polygon and generic Ekedahl--Oort data do not logically identify the *unique* point \(\lambda=-1\) where the Cartier square vanishes.

**Residual risk:** A difficult-to-index Hasse--Witt matrix calculation or earlier parameterization of these cyclic covers could already imply the factorization. The searches are evidence for originality, not a proof of global historical uniqueness. An independent historical-priority check is not performed.

## Mathematical value (V) — PASS

The problem is a natural exact stratification of Moonen's one-dimensional special genus-four cyclic quintic family in the first prime \(p=3\) whose \(5\)-power-cover structure is nontrivially permuted by Frobenius. The finding distinguishes a unique geometric fiber by the rank of an iterated Cartier operator even though the basic invariants \(3\)-rank and \(a\)-number are constant. The jumping point is geometrically singled out by an extra involution of the branch configuration, so it is not an arbitrary parameter slice. It gives a reproducible sharp boundary between nilpotence indices two and three over the entire smooth family, refining generic invariants in the literature.

**Limit:** This is a deliberately bounded genus-four/characteristic-three result, not a general theorem for all cyclic covers, not a new Newton-polygon classification, and not independent validation.
