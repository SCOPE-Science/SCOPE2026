# The Wagner graph recolouring threshold and its uniform twelve-move eccentricity

## Finding
Let \(W\) be the eight-vertex Wagner graph, the Möbius ladder on eight vertices. For each \(k\in\{3,4\}\), let \(R_k(W)\) have as vertices all **named-colour** proper mappings \(f:V(W)\to\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\); two mappings are joined exactly when they differ at one vertex. Colours need not all be used, and colour permutations produce distinct configurations.

The exact isomorphism type at the chromatic palette is
\[ R_3(W)\cong C_{24}\sqcup C_{24}. \]
In particular there are \(48\) configurations and \(48\) undirected edges, and both components have radius and diameter \(12\).

Adding one named colour connects the space without increasing its *within-component* maximum distance:
\[ |V(R_4(W))|=2160,\quad |E(R_4(W))|=7872,\quad \operatorname{rad}(R_4(W))=\operatorname{diam}(R_4(W))=12. \]
Every one of the \(2160\) four-colour configurations has eccentricity \(12\), although the graph is not regular: the degree multiplicities at degrees \(4,6,7,8,10\) are \(96,576,384,912,192\), respectively.

For \(R_4(W)\), the complete counts of unordered pairs at each distance \(d=1,2,\ldots,12\) are respectively
\[ (7872,29760,77088,156096,261600,373728,455520,445248,315216,155856,48576,5160). \]
They sum to \(\binom{2160}{2}=2331720\); exactly \(5160\) unordered pairs realize the diameter. The known numbers of colourings \(48\) and \(2160\) were already recorded by chromatic-polynomial enumeration, including OEIS A277444, and are not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
Write \(V(W)=\{0,1,\ldots,7\}\) and put
\[ E(W)=\big\{\{i,(i+1)\bmod 8\}:0\le i<8\big\}\cup\big\{\{i,i+4\}:0\le i<4\big\}. \]
This is the nonplanar, cubic, three-chromatic eight-vertex Wagner graph. It contains the odd five-cycle \(0,1,2,3,4,0\) and admits the proper three-colouring \((0,1,0,1,2,0,1,2)\), verifying its chromatic number directly. All vertices and palette colours are labelled. Recolouring means changing the colour at exactly one vertex and retaining properness on all twelve edges; simultaneous swaps of colour names or Kempe changes are **not** edges unless they coincide with one allowed single-vertex recolouring. Distances between components in \(R_3(W)\) are undefined/infinite; the displayed twelve is the diameter of each connected component only.

## Proof
The claims are exact finite statements. The accompanying independent programs implement an exhaustive proof by finite enumeration and breadth-first search; no random sample or presumed symmetry classification is used for the decisive independent computation. Their correctness follows from the following transparent enumeration lemma.

**Enumeration lemma.** Every proper named \(k\)-colouring of \(W\) is represented by exactly one base-\(k\) integer \(z\) with \(0\le z<k^8\), whose eight digits are the vertex colours. Scanning all such integers and retaining those for which the twelve explicitly listed edge inequalities hold gives precisely the vertex set. For each retained integer, replacing one digit by each different palette digit and retaining it if and only if it is also proper lists exactly the neighbours under the stipulated legal move. Running breadth-first search from *every* retained state gives each finite graph distance exactly, because breadth-first search first discovers a vertex along a shortest edge path. A separate connected-component search determines which distances are infinite.

`wagner_direct.cpp` implements this lemma without orbit reduction. Its all-source searches give at \(k=3\) exactly \(48\) states, two components of \(24\) vertices, and degree two everywhere; every connected finite degree-two graph is a cycle, proving \(C_{24}\sqcup C_{24}\). At \(k=4\) it gives one connected component of \(2160\) vertices, \(7872\) edges, and an eccentricity multiset with exactly \(2160\) entries all equal to \(12\). It directly accumulates the pair-distance profile in the Finding and verifies its sum. Hence the radius, diameter, and diametral-pair count follow.

`wagner.py` is a structurally different implementation. It constructs proper colourings recursively, constructs legal transitions by checking neighbours rather than changing base-\(k\) integers, and computes distances from representatives of orbits generated by the full dihedral vertex symmetries and adjacent colour-label transpositions. These transformations preserve exactly the graph and move rule; every state receives one orbit label and each orbit representative is searched. It independently obtains the same components, degrees, distance distributions, and eccentricities. The full-source C++ implementation removes any dependence of the theorem on correctness of orbit reduction.

As a context check, \(W\) has Grundy number \(4\). Its maximum degree is three, so a greedy colouring cannot introduce more than four colours. Greedy processing in order \((0,1,2,4,3,5,6,7)\) gives colours by vertex index \((0,1,0,2,1,0,1,3)\), demonstrating four. Consequently the general sufficient bound \(k\ge \chi_g(W)+1\) yields connectivity from five colours, but does not entail the exact four-colour result.

## Verification
Run `python3 wagner.py`, followed by `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 wagner_direct.cpp -o wagner_direct && ./wagner_direct`. Both programs are self-contained and take no inputs. The independent executable prints `INDEPENDENT_EXHAUSTIVE_PASS`. At three colours its all-source census yields two \(24\)-cycles and \(576\) unreachable unordered pairs; at four it yields no unreachable pair, all \(2160\) eccentricities equal to \(12\), and exactly \(5160\) diameter pairs. The independent Python result includes all ten dihedral/colour-permutation orbit representatives and their distance-shell profiles. Both checkers were run against the exact accompanying source texts.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical chromatic-polynomial results of Biggs, Damerell, and Sands (1972), reproduced in OEIS A277444, already give the counts \(48\) and \(2160\) for this Möbius ladder. No novelty is assigned to those numbers. Cereceda, van den Heuvel, and Johnson's 2005 report introduced the same single-vertex recolouring graph and established qualitative nonconnectivity of three-colour graphs for chromatic-number-three graphs; it does not give these Wagner-specific components or four-colour distances. Bonamy and Bousquet's dated 2013 preprint proves general sufficient mixing/diameter bounds at larger palettes, including the Grundy threshold. That threshold is five rather than four for this graph. Their 2014 follow-up addresses treewidth-based general bounds, not the present exact metric data. Searches for the precise small-graph profile, alternative graph name, colouring count, and equivalent shortest paths found no covering record. Similarity-search retrieval does not establish global historical uniqueness.

## Limitations
This is a complete computationally assisted proof for this **one fixed eight-vertex graph and only palettes three and four**. It does not establish formulas for larger Möbius ladders or general recolouring mixing times. A general purely symbolic derivation of the twelve-move metric is not claimed. The 2005 report gives month but not first-publication day; the earliest selected exact-day arXiv first posting is 2013-02-14. Historical priority beyond inspected literature and indexed records cannot be guaranteed; in particular, the original 1972 enumeration article was not inspected in full. This result has no independent external scholarly audit.
