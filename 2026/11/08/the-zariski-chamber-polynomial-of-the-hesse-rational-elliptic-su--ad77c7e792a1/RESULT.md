# The Zariski-chamber polynomial of the Hesse rational elliptic surface

## Finding
Let \(X\) be the smooth complex projective surface obtained by blowing up the nine distinct base points of the Hesse pencil
\[
\lambda(X_0^3+X_1^3+X_2^3)+\mu X_0X_1X_2=0
\]
in \(\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\). A Zariski chamber is indexed by the support of the negative part of the Zariski decomposition of a big divisor, with the nef chamber having empty negative support. Let \(z_r\) count chambers whose negative support consists of exactly \(r\) irreducible curves. Then the complete support polynomial is
\[
Z_X(t)=\sum_{r\ge0}z_rt^r
=1+21t+210t^2+1236t^3+4497t^4+10308t^5
+14772t^6+12612t^7+5634t^8+874t^9.
\]
In particular, there are exactly \(50\,165\) Zariski chambers. The largest negative support has nine curves, with exactly \(874\) chambers of that size.

More structurally, the complete set of irreducible negative curves on \(X\) consists of the nine exceptional sections \(E_p\), indexed by points \(p\in\mathbb F_3^2\), and the twelve strict transforms \(L_\ell\) of the Hesse inflection lines, indexed by affine lines \(\ell\subset\mathbb F_3^2\). The twelve lines form four parallel classes of three. A subset of these twenty-one curves is negative definite **if and only if** all four conditions hold:

1. At most two fiber components \(L_\ell\) are chosen from each parallel class.
2. Each chosen exceptional section meets at most one chosen component.
3. Each chosen component meets at most one chosen exceptional section.
4. Whenever two components from the same parallel class are chosen, at most one chosen exceptional section meets either component.

These conditions give an intrinsic incidence-theoretic description of every chamber support, not just their number.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The pencil and its blowup are fixed as above, with its nine distinct reduced base points. The four singular fibers of the induced relatively minimal elliptic fibration have Kodaira type \(I_3\). The counting invariant concerns full Zariski chambers in the big cone of \(X\), not chambers supported by a selected proper subset of negative curves. The nef chamber is counted once. Distinct supports are counted separately even when related by a projective automorphism.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustion of negative curves.** Write \(\pi:X\to\mathbb P^2\), and let \(F=-K_X=3H-\sum_{p}E_p\) be the class of a fiber of the resolved Hesse pencil. Since \(F\) is nef, for any irreducible curve \(C\) one has \(F\cdot C\ge0\). Adjunction gives
\[
C^2=2p_a(C)-2+F\cdot C.
\]
Thus \(C^2<0\) forces \(p_a(C)=0\) and \(F\cdot C\in\{0,1\}\). If \(F\cdot C=0\), the curve is a vertical component in a reducible fiber and has \(C^2=-2\). There are exactly twelve such components, namely the strict transforms of the four triangles of the Hesse pencil. If \(F\cdot C=1\), the curve is a section and has \(C^2=-1\).

To count sections, \(\rho(X)=10\). Shioda–Tate gives Mordell–Weil rank \(10-2-4(3-1)=0\). Let \(T\) be the trivial lattice generated by the zero section, the fiber and the two nonidentity components of each of the four \(I_3\) fibers. Its Gram determinant has absolute value \(3^4=81\), whereas \(\operatorname{NS}(X)\) is unimodular. The Shioda–Tate exact sequence then identifies the finite Mordell–Weil group with \(\operatorname{NS}(X)/T\), of order \(\sqrt{81}=9\). The nine exceptional curves are already nine distinct sections, so they exhaust all sections. Hence the listed twenty-one curves are all the negative curves.

**2. Intersection pattern.** The exceptional sections are pairwise disjoint, with \(E_p^2=-1\). A line through three of the nine base points has strict transform of self-intersection \(1-3=-2\). Two different line components from one triangle meet transversely once at a triangle vertex, while line components from different triangles are disjoint after blowing up their common base point. Furthermore \(E_p\cdot L_\ell=1\) precisely when \(p\in\ell\), and this intersection is zero otherwise. The incidence structure is the affine plane \(\mathbb F_3^2\): twelve lines in four parallel classes and nine points.

**3. Characterization of negative definite supports.** A positive definite matrix cannot contain a singular principal submatrix. The intersection form for three selected components of one \(I_3\) fiber is the negative affine \(A_2\) triangle matrix, with row sums zero; this excludes condition 1's violations. A selected section incident to two selected components gives a three-curve principal submatrix with diagonal \((-1,-2,-2)\), the two components being disjoint; it is singular. Likewise, a selected component incident to two selected sections yields the singular three-curve chain with diagonal \((-1,-2,-1)\). Finally, two adjacent chosen components carrying a chosen section at each end form the singular four-curve chain with diagonal \((-1,-2,-2,-1)\). These are exactly the four forbidden configurations.

Conversely, if none occurs, the incidence graph of the selected curves is a disjoint union of isolated sections, isolated components, section–component edges, two-component edges, and three-vertex paths section–component–component. The negatives of their intersection matrices are positive definite: in the last case, for example, the successive leading principal minors are \(1,1,1\). Hence every admissible support is negative definite. This proves the four-condition equivalence without numerical approximation.

**4. Exact enumeration.** Index points by \((a,b)\in\mathbb F_3^2\). Index lines by the level sets of the four linear forms \(a\), \(b\), \(b-a\), \(a+b\), each at the three values of \(\mathbb F_3\). Every subset of the twenty-one negative curves is uniquely encoded by a nine-bit section mask and a twelve-bit line mask. Enumerating the \(2^{21}=2\,097\,152\) pairs of masks and applying exactly the four incidence conditions yields the coefficient vector
\[
(1,21,210,1236,4497,10308,14772,12612,5634,874).
\]
The standalone exact script in `verification/verify_hesse.py` reproduces this enumeration and, in a separate implementation, enumerates negative definite principal submatrices by exact rational Schur pivots and hereditary backtracking. The two independent counting procedures agree coefficient by coefficient. By Bauer–Funke–Neumann's bijection, each such nonempty negative definite support gives exactly one non-nef Zariski chamber; the empty support gives the nef chamber. The asserted polynomial and total follow.

## Verification
Run `python3 verification/verify_hesse.py` with Python 3 and no third-party dependencies. Its recorded output is `verification/execution.txt`. It constructs the full integral intersection matrix directly from affine-plane incidence, counts all masks by the four-condition criterion, and independently enumerates all positive definite principal submatrices of its negative using exact `fractions.Fraction` arithmetic. There are \(106\,218\) candidate principal matrices tested by the backtracking verifier, and both methods return \(50\,165\) supports including the empty one. The program also checks all nine point and twelve line degrees of incidence and prints `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED`. The proof of exhaustion of negative curves is geometric and is not inferred from finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Artebani–Dolgachev describe the Hesse pencil, its nine base points and twelve inflection lines, its four \(I_3\) fibers and the affine-plane incidence pattern. Bauer–Funke–Neumann establish the general bijection between Zariski chambers and negative definite reduced divisors and compute chamber counts for general-position del Pezzo surfaces. Bauer–Schmitz subsequently give an improved general matrix-counting algorithm and apply it to the Segre–Schur quartic. The geometric Hesse pencil data and the general algorithm are prior work; the particular twenty-one-curve support classification and the complete coefficient polynomial displayed here are not statements found in the inspected parts of those sources. A more specialized historical computation could still contain an equivalent formula, so priority remains provisional.

## Limitations
This is an exact statement for the blowup of the complex Hesse pencil's nine base points. It is not a chamber count for a general blowup at nine points (whose Mordell–Weil group is typically infinite), nor for the altered characteristic-three pencil. The enumeration counts supports, not chamber volumes or automorphism orbits. The exact count and graph criterion are mathematically proved here, but absence from the entire historical literature has not been established and independent validation has not been performed.
