# Supported scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is established analytically from the existing exact minimum-circumscribing-polygon formula. The disk comparator is strictly logarithmically convex by its positive-coefficient reciprocal-power expansion. For all \(k\ge9\), the six-side area decreases within each of three residue classes, reducing the universal bound to three certified inequalities. All exceptional smaller orders are exhausted; the two positive small-order sets have 18 and 30 exact-rational inequality certificates, and the negative sets have explicit affine midpoint witnesses. The same rational checker independently encloses the transcendental constants and verifies all finite signs. The tiling corollary uses the published exponent-zero transfer theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

The closest Lángi–Wang preprint arXiv:2406.10622 (2024) and its 2026 journal version prove the weak square-root classification, not the strict logarithmic endpoint or the complete all-exponent threshold. Their ordinary Dowker convexity and full-alpha obstruction do not imply weak logarithmic convexity at six sides. The decisive comparison is between their square-root weighted arithmetic mean and the strictly more demanding weighted geometric mean here. published-finding corpus searches for Dowker, honeycomb, logarithmic convexity, regular polygon, and exceptional orders returned no statement covering this endpoint claim. Their prior formulas and tiling-transfer theorem remain explicitly credited. Unindexed alternative formulations remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The logarithmic endpoint is the strongest member of the positive-exponent sufficient Dowker family. Its proof upgrades a published honeycomb conclusion from average perimeter to geometric-mean perimeter, and the three exceptional polygon orders have sharp threshold exactly one rather than an unspecified subunit threshold. The exact distinction identifies the reach and limitations of the Dowker certificate without claiming an exceptional honeycomb counterexample.

## Limitations

The proof concerns regular even-sided polygonal norm balls, normal convex unit-area tilings, and the weak Dowker property centered at six sides. It does not settle exceptional honeycomb cases, general polygons, or independent historical priority.
