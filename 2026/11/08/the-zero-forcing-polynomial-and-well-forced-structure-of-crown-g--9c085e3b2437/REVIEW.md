# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The vertex-neighborhood obstruction is invariant under forcing: three whites on one shore cannot be reduced, because every blue vertex opposite sees at least two of them. For two whites per shore, equality of the two index pairs prevents every first move; when the pairs differ, the proof gives explicit legal sequences for disjoint and one-overlap profiles. Monotonicity handles every smaller profile, establishing both directions for all \(n\ge3\). Complement enumeration, exact inclusion-minimality, forcing-number and span consequences follow without finite extrapolation. The quadratic discriminant calculation proves the root threshold. The independent adjacency-based verifier exhaustively checked every labelled starting set for \(n=3,\ldots,9\) and computed 349504 subsets with matching results.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The principal precedent is Boyer et al. (arXiv:1801.08910v1), whose complete-multipartite and cycle polynomials do not cover the entire crown family; the known cycle formula covers the special member \(\operatorname{Cr}_3\cong C_6\), which is not separately claimed as new. Jacob (arXiv:2309.06234v1) provides the motivating span invariant and its general extremal identity, not the family-wide crown polynomial. Brimkov–Carlson (arXiv:2204.01810v1) studies minimal zero forcing sets abstractly; Grood et al. (arXiv:2312.14298v1) treats well-forced trees; Pelayo–Abara (arXiv:2202.04997v1) studies different graph products and maximal failed sets. Indexed mathematical-result searches and alternative-name searches, including categorical products and bipartite doubles of complete graphs, returned no theorem implying the entire exact white-profile classification. That absence is evidence of noncoverage in inspected sources, not proof of absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The infinite regular bipartite family has a complete feasible-configuration characterization, not merely a minimum zero forcing number. It yields the entire size distribution, all inclusion-minimal forcing sets, a polynomial count of minimal configurations, a two-cardinality forcing span and a sharp nonreal-root threshold. These structural properties provide an exact benchmark for forcing-polynomial and well-forced investigations. They do not follow from generic extremal coefficients or from knowing the minimum size alone.

## Limits and closest literature

The work assumes ordinary zero forcing on crowns \(\operatorname{Cr}_n\) for \(n\ge3\) and does not classify arbitrary matching deletions from bicliques. The \(n=3\) cycle specialization is previously known; the family-wide statement is assessed principally for \(n\ge4\). Residual priority risk remains from unindexed literature, equivalent descriptions as direct products and bipartite covers, or indirect implications not discovered during the search. Mathematical checks were carried out without independent validation.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
