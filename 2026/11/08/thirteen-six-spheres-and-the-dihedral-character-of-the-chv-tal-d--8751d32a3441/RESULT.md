# Thirteen six-spheres and the dihedral character of the Chvátal dominance complex

## Finding

Let \(G\) be the specific twelve-vertex Chvátal graph with the twenty-four edges listed below. Its **dominance complex** is the finite simplicial complex
\[
\operatorname{Dom}(G)=\{S\subseteq V(G): V(G)\setminus S\text{ is a dominating set of }G\}.
\]
Here a vertex dominates itself and its adjacent vertices. The complete integral homotopy type is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Dom}(G)\simeq \bigvee^{13} S^6.}
\]
The natural full graph automorphism group is the dihedral group \(D_4=\langle r,s:r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\). Its rational representation on the only nontrivial reduced homology \(\widetilde H_6(\operatorname{Dom}(G);\mathbb Q)\) has character
\[
\chi(r^i s^j)=\begin{cases}
13,&j=0,\ i\equiv0\pmod4,\\
1,&j=0,\ i\equiv2\pmod4,\\
-1,&j=0,\ i\equiv1,3\pmod4,\\
-3,&j=1,\ i\equiv0,2\pmod4,\\
1,&j=1,\ i\equiv1,3\pmod4.
\end{cases}
\]
In terms of the four rational one-dimensional representations \(L_{a,b}\) defined by \(r\mapsto a\), \(s\mapsto b\) for \(a,b\in\{\pm1\}\), and the ordinary two-dimensional rational representation \(E\), the rational module decomposes as
\[
\widetilde H_6(\operatorname{Dom}(G);\mathbb Q)\cong L_{1,1}\oplus 2L_{1,-1}\oplus L_{-1,1}\oplus3L_{-1,-1}\oplus3E.
\]
The invariant subspace is one-dimensional. The wedge conclusion concerns **integral homotopy**; the representation splitting is claimed over \(\mathbb Q\), not as a decomposition of integral \(\mathbb Z[D_4]\)-lattices.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(G\) is finite, loopless and undirected with vertices labeled `0` through `11`. Its complete edge list is

```text
(0,1) (0,4) (0,6) (0,9) (1,2) (1,5) (1,7) (2,3)
(2,6) (2,8) (3,4) (3,7) (3,9) (4,5) (4,8) (5,10)
(5,11) (6,10) (6,11) (7,8) (7,11) (8,10) (9,10) (9,11)
```

Writing \(N[v]\) for the closed neighborhood of \(v\), a nonempty set \(S\) is a face precisely when \(N[v]\not\subseteq S\) for every vertex \(v\). This forbidden-closed-neighborhood condition is equivalent to domination by the complement; the empty set is also a face by convention. The graph is four-regular and triangle-free, with independence number four and vertex-cover number eight.

The full automorphism group is generated by these image tuples, in vertex order `0` through `11`:

```text
r=(4,0,9,10,5,1,3,6,11,8,7,2)
s=(0,4,3,2,1,5,9,8,7,6,11,10)
```

Permutations act on simplices by vertex relabeling. All homology groups have integral coefficients unless \(\mathbb Q\) is displayed.

## Proof

**Exact face construction.** For each of the \(2^{12}=4096\) subsets, compute the closed neighborhood masks and test that none is contained in the subset; independently check that the complementary set meets every closed neighborhood. The two tests agree on all subsets. There are 3098 nonempty faces, with dimensions zero through seven and exact face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6,f_7)=(12,66,220,495,780,840,544,141).
\]
Thus the ordinary Euler characteristic is \(14\) and the reduced Euler characteristic is \(13\).

**A complete acyclic discrete-Morse matching.** Encode each nonempty face by its positive twelve-bit integer mask. Start with the set of all nonempty faces. Traverse the pivot order

```text
(10,4,3,6,11,7,0,2,8,9,1,5)
```

For each pivot, visit the remaining faces in increasing order of (cardinality, integer mask). Whenever a visited face omits the pivot and both it and its pivot extension remain unmatched, match that cover relation and remove both faces from the remaining set. The algorithm produces **1542 disjoint pairs** and exactly **14 unmatched cells**: a single zero-cell and thirteen six-cells. The face-poset cover graph has exactly **16,648** incidences between nonempty faces. Orient each matched cover upward and every unmatched cover downward. Exhaustive directed topological sorting visits every one of its 3098 vertices. Hence there is **no directed cycle** and the matching satisfies Forman's acyclicity condition.

Forman's finite discrete-Morse theorem gives a CW model with exactly one zero-cell and thirteen six-cells and no other cells. Every attaching map of a six-cell to the zero-dimensional skeleton is constant, so the CW complex is the wedge of thirteen six-spheres. This proves the asserted *integral homotopy equivalence*, not just a calculation of homology over a field. The empty face is excluded from the nonempty-face poset when establishing the matching.

**Full symmetry character.** An independent edge-and-nonedge-preserving backtracking enumeration tests all possible images of the twelve vertices and finds exactly eight automorphisms. The displayed permutations satisfy \(r^4=s^2=1\) and \(srs=r^{-1}\), and their eight products exhaust the automorphism set. Thus it is the full group \(D_4\). For any automorphism \(g\), the alternating signed fixed-face formula on the **reduced** simplicial chain complex is
\[
\widetilde L(g)=-1+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\in\operatorname{Dom}(G)\\gS=S}}
(-1)^{|S|-1}\,\operatorname{sgn}(g|_S),
\]
where the sign is the parity of the induced permutation on the vertices of the fixed face. The term \(-1\) is contributed by the empty face. By the simplicial Hopf trace theorem and the already proved homotopy type, \(\widetilde L(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g_*\mid\widetilde H_6)\), with no degree sign change because the only nonzero reduced homology is in even degree six. Exact enumeration on every face yields the eight character values stated above. The standard rational \(D_4\) character table then gives the asserted multiplicities by the inner-product formula \(\langle\chi,\rho\rangle=\frac18\sum_{g\in D_4}\chi(g)\rho(g)\). Their weighted dimensions sum to thirteen. This character determines the rational representation completely.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone script `verify_chvatal_dominance.py` requires only Python's standard library. Run `python verify_chvatal_dominance.py` and compare with `verification_output.txt`. It reconstructs both equivalent face tests on all 4096 subsets; regenerates every matching pair, critical cell and directed covering incidence; performs the exhaustive topological sort; independently computes mod-two homology by exact binary boundary elimination; backtracks all graph automorphisms; and calculates all eight signed Lefschetz traces and rational irreducible multiplicities. The output ends with `CHVATAL_DOMINANCE_WEDGE_AND_CHARACTER_PASS`.

The independent mod-two calculation gives reduced Betti number thirteen in dimension six and zero in all other dimensions. It corroborates the integral wedge but is **not the proof** of integral homotopy: that proof is the exhaustive acyclic matching plus the finite discrete-Morse theorem. Finite computation suffices here because the mathematical object has only twelve explicitly specified vertices; no infinite-family inference is made. The calculation determines the canonical character but not canonical cycle representatives or an integral irreducible-lattice splitting.

## Relationship to prior work

Chvátal's original graph is a classical smallest four-regular triangle-free four-chromatic example. Marietti and Testa developed dominance-complex topology for **forests**, and Taylan established strong homotopy simplifications for **chordal graphs**; the present graph contains induced cycles and is not in either graph class. Matsushita's connectivity inequality for general dominance complexes only bounds the connectivity; it does not compute the exact homotopy type or symmetry action. Here \(\operatorname{conn}(\operatorname{Dom}(G))+2=7<8=\tau(G)\), so that bound is strict.

Matsushita and Wakatsuki relate the Alexander dual of \(\operatorname{Dom}(G)\) to the ordinary open-neighborhood complex of the **complement graph**. This duality is compatible with, but does not itself evaluate, the present twelve-vertex complex. In particular, an exact previously reported calculation for this same graph establishes \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\simeq\mathcal N(G)\simeq S^2\) and determines a dihedral character on second homology. Those are different complexes, and the neighborhood result concerns \(\mathcal N(G)\), not \(\mathcal N(\overline G)\). It does not imply the present six-dimensional dominance-complex classification or character. No new claim is made about the already known independence/neighborhood-complex collapse, the graph's chromatic number or the general Alexander duality theorem.

## Limitations

This is an exact classification for this labeled graph, and hence for every isomorphic copy. It is not a family-wide statement for all four-chromatic or triangle-free graphs. The rational character decomposition does not assert an integral decomposition or equivariant homotopy splitting. Independent verification and a definitive publication-priority judgment have not been performed; exact equivalents in unindexed literature remain a risk. The original 1970 graph article was not examined in complete text, although the graph itself is fully specified and independently checked here.
