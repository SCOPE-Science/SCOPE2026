# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The event is explicitly defined and its exact numerator is the coefficient of the product of nonempty-group subset polynomials. An inclusion–exclusion formula gives a second independent integer representation. The finite algorithm enumerates every unordered nondecreasing integer partition of each species total through 13, groups by function count, tests all unordered equal-total/equal-function pairs and all survivor budgets, and cross-checks every coefficient against inclusion–exclusion. Three boundary pairs are independently checked by direct enumeration of all 8,192 survivor subsets of each 13-species profile. The infinite fixed-four-function family follows from explicit analytic endpoint counts, not an extrapolation. Its strict inequalities hold for every integer \(m\ge5\). No ecological empirical claim is made.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence; priority provisional

Fonseca–Ganade (2001; https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2745.2001.00528.x) provides the motivating ecological functional-group rarefaction model and studies expected retained functions, variance and redundancy indices. It does not state the exact minimum total species count or the complete first layer of *strict all-functions-retained ranking reversals* between matched group counts. Petchey–Gaston (2002; https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2002.2073) uses real and simulated extinction trajectories and trait-based diversity; the inspected main text does not tabulate the sharp full-cover profile comparisons. May (2008; https://doi.org/10.37236/906) solves a related coupon completion problem with independent sampling *with replacement*, not the fixed-size uniform sample without replacement, and does not supply the claimed sharp finite layer. Mallows (1973; https://doi.org/10.1137/0124017) is relevant through general sampling stopping times, but only its abstract was accessible. The claim is about a motivated sharp structural cutoff and three first-layer classes, not novelty of hypergeometric counting itself. Searches were non-exhaustive; absence from search results alone is not a priority proof. Relevant inaccessible full text and broad stochastic-order literature are residual risks.

## Value — PASS

The probability that every function survives is a natural binary robustness criterion, distinct from expected surviving richness. Its ranking need not agree uniformly across survivor budgets even when total species number and functional-group number are held equal. Thirteen is the exact first possible community size, with a complete list of the first possibilities and an explicit infinite family at fixed four-function count. This makes a concrete boundary for when one scalar or one budget cannot reliably order communities by full functional retention; it does not require a downstream application or empirical forecast to be mathematically informative.

## Limits

The result assumes disjoint functional groups, perfect substitutability within each group, uniform fixed-budget species survival, and no interactions. It proves complete minimality only up to the first boundary; later totals are not fully classified. Similar first-coverage stopping-time arguments may exist in older literature, including an inaccessible full text. The review is not an independent scientific audit.
