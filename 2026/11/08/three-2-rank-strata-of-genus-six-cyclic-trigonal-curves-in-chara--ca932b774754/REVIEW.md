# Scientific assessment of the genus-six trigonal Cartier stratification

**Correctness (PASS).** For every algebraically closed field of characteristic two, the seven-degree Kummer cover has genus six and the displayed six differential forms constitute a complete basis. The general Cartier identity gives the explicit semilinear block matrix. Squarefreeness prevents the two columns mapping the \(A\)-eigenspace into the \(B\)-eigenspace from becoming dependent; thus the first Cartier rank is always four. The determinant \(a_2+a_3a_6\) and the twisted rank-one composition, whose extra factor is \(a_3+a_6^4\), rigorously distinguish all later powers. An exhaustive check over \(\mathbb F_4\) and exact tests over \(\mathbb F_8\) independently corroborate the power-rank table. The geometric graph description proves the locus smooth and irreducible inside the squarefree coefficient open set.

**Originality (PASS, with priority risk).** Elkin's 2007 theorem supplies the general Cartier formula and known constancy of the first Cartier rank; neither is claimed as new. England's 2010 paper describes the same genus-six \((3,7)\) family over \(\mathbb C\) but concerns abelian functions, not characteristic-two \(2\)-rank strata. Özman–Pries–Weir address the relevant trielliptic signature but their §6 dimension theorem expressly assumes odd characteristic and does not give the two explicit coefficient equations or higher Cartier-rank sequences. The contribution is the *complete coefficient-level geometric classification* of all three stable ranks and the full iterated rank profiles. No inspected source supplies an equivalent or more general statement that directly gives these conclusions as a named result. A broader unpublished coefficient calculation or alternate model might exist; priority is not independently certified.

**Value (PASS).** The \(2\)-rank is an intrinsic Jacobian invariant, and the locus of its vanishing in an entire natural genus-six cyclic trigonal family is a meaningful moduli question. The explicit smooth codimension-two \(2\)-rank-zero graph, the nonempty rank-two intermediary divisor, and the rank sequences \((4,4,\ldots)\), \((4,3,2,2,\ldots)\), and \((4,3,2,1,0,\ldots)\) give a complete three-stratum structural description, not an isolated numerical example. These distinguish two curves with the same genus, branch type and \(a\)-number; general first-rank bounds alone do not resolve them.

**Risks and limits.** The derivation uses classical Cartier formalism, and an unlocated specialist treatment might have tabulated the same coefficient conditions. No classification of isomorphism classes, Newton polygons or full Ekedahl–Oort types is asserted. Finite tests are corroboration, not infinite-family proofs.
