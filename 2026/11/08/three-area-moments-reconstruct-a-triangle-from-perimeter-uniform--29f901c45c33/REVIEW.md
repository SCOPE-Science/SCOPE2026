# Scientific review: complete perimeter-sampled triangle-area inverse theorem

## Correctness — PASS

Independent edge assignments are exactly a three-type partition. The same-edge component has area zero; the two-on-one-edge component is a product of an independent uniform and an absolute difference of uniforms; the all-distinct component is the explicit affine determinant integral already examined in the literature. The partition weights are the displayed symmetric polynomials, sum to one, and are nonnegative on nondegenerate side ratios. Exact integration yields the first three normalized moments, with cancellation of the third elementary symmetric polynomial for orders one and two. The three moment identities invert by division, Vieta's formulas, and Heron's formula; strict triangle inequalities ensure a unique positive perimeter. Two different area-one isosceles shapes have identical first two moments but different third moments. Cauchy–Schwarz and the strict triangle inequalities give sharp normalized moment endpoints, and a one-parameter isosceles family fills every intermediate value. The included verifier computes conditional moments by exact rational polynomial integration through order six, checks both witness configurations and the moment cancellation, then compares direct Euclidean triangle simulations for four nondegenerate shapes. These finite checks corroborate but do not prove the universal formulas.

## Originality — PASS, provisionally

The prior full-text comparison of Maesumi's 2018 arXiv/2019 journal article identifies a genuine shared conditional ingredient: the single point on each of three *different* sides is the universal third component, with first three moments \(1/4\), \(1/12\), and \(5/144\). That component is explicitly credited, not presented as a new distribution. The present statement is for three independently sampled points from the entire *unlabeled* perimeter, leading to additional same-side and double-side events, a shape-dependent mixture, a cancellation of shape information in the first two raw moments, and an exact three-moment congruence reconstruction with a minimality example. Koroleva and Nikitin's January 2013 circle-boundary extreme-value paper supplies the archive-era geometric-probability motivation, but its circle model does not cover the fixed-triangle inverse theorem. Daley–Ebert–Swift concerns side-direction-generated triangles; the available abstract supplies no direct implication. Published research-record searches for perimeter-uniform triangle area moments, inverse side reconstruction, and aliases did not identify a covering stronger theorem. An unlocated older perimeter-sampling calculation remains a residual historical-priority risk; failed retrieval is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural observation model records areas of random triangles inscribed in a parent triangular boundary, without labeling which edges were sampled. The complete inverse question is whether a few scalar area statistics identify a shape. The result establishes a precise and globally valid positive answer with only three raw moments, supplies a constructive reconstruction for all nondegenerate triangular shapes and scales, and proves that two moments are insufficient even for noncongruent isosceles triangles of equal area. The exact full-law mixture and sharp normalized moment interval explain both the recoverable and the invisible shape information. The work makes no general finite-sample stability or higher-polygon reconstruction claim.
