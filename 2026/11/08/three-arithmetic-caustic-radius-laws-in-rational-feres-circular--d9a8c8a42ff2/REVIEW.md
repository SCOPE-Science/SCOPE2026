# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For \(\alpha=\pi/n\), the four Feres angular maps preserve the signed-even-translation class. Direct positive-probability paths traverse both explicit angle ladders, hence the class contains exactly \(n\) states and is irreducible; almost every infinite class-path therefore visits them all. Nearest-point geometry relative to \(\pi/2\) produces the three radius maps of the fundamental representative. The Liouville angular density pushes to the stated sine-weight sum; exact finite sine identities and change of variables prove the CDF and moments. The unit disk chord has unsigned offset \(\lvert\cos\theta\rvert\). The separate checker passed all finite tests for \(7\le n\le50\). Finite tests do not prove the theorem.

## Originality — PASS within checked literature

The primary full-text material in Vales–Pinto-de-Carvalho (arXiv:2005.01892, Sections 3.1, 4.1, 5, 6.3) defines the same random billiard, the invariant measure, and random caustics but gives no stationary law for the caustic radius. Vales (arXiv:2212.00872, Sections 3, 4, 6) describes the same classes and provides the \(n=7,8\) orbits but no statistical pushforward or modulo-four radius classification. Existing finite-class stationary weights allow the calculation in principle, but do not themselves state or directly evaluate the three exact pushforward laws. Targeted searches for the Feres stationary caustic radius and its congruence-dependent law revealed related spatial-mixing work but no equivalent published radius distribution. Such search absence alone cannot establish priority. General random-billiard methods remain inherited background. Unindexed formulations are a residual publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The random caustic is the precise radius of the dynamically inaccessible central region, directly motivating the distribution, its support and the expected excluded area. The finding classifies this natural invariant at every allowed reciprocal-integer roughness angle, and reveals a non-obvious mod-four distinction: uniformity in two classes, a nonuniform law in one, and sharply different essential suprema for odd and even denominators. This is more informative than merely knowing that nondegenerate caustics exist and that Liouville measure is invariant. It is an exact specialized classification, not a claim about arbitrary billiards or a new random-map framework.

## Limitations

Only the indicated Euclidean unit-circle stationary model is included; no finite-time first-hit distribution, irrational-angle extension, or global mixing conclusion is asserted. Prior formulae can be used to derive parts of the theorem, so priority for the particular closed-form pushforward requires external scholarly confirmation.
