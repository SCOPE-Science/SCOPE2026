# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof exhaustively classifies atomic one-point extensions over stationary tuples of at most three points. With three noncollinear stationary points, the union of the three pair-lines has \(3\ell-3\) points, so the outside counts \(q^2-3q+3\) (affine) and \((q-1)^2\) (projective) are strictly positive for every permitted order. The only availability thresholds are \(\ell=3\) and \(\ell=4\), giving a permanent four-pebble strategy. Distinct triple and quadruple collinearity sentences separate the classes. A five-variable sentence quantifying the intersection of lines gives the sharp cross-type boundary. Six exact prime-field computational checks passed; they are corroboration rather than the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Relevant comparisons include Grohe–Otto's finite-variable pebble-game theorem (general method, not the geometric classification), Venema's two-sorted modal projective-plane analysis (different logic and objects), and the accessible abstract/secondary discussion of Stebletsova's projective collinearity modal logic. Searches for finite-plane point-only four-pebble equivalence and five-variable parallelism found no direct same claim. An earlier projective-plane result in a *two-sorted incidence language* does not automatically imply a four-variable point-only comparison with affine planes: converting the ternary collinearity predicate to sorted incidence uses another quantified line variable. A stronger theorem about finite-variable linear-space logic remains a possible historical risk. The complete Stebletsova report was not inspected.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural question is how much finite-variable point-only collinearity logic can see of the axiomatic distinction between affine and projective geometry. The exact answer at width four is a three-class classification independent of order, coordinatization, and Desargues's theorem; the minimum fifth variable is precisely what permits naming an intersection witness for two lines. This is a motivated expressibility boundary, not a small arbitrary numerical example.
