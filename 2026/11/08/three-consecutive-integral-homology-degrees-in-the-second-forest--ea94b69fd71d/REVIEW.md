# Scientific review: integral homology of a second-stage triple-product forest complex

## Correctness
PASS. The stated graph, induced-linear-forest predicate, integral homology and connectivity are unambiguous. The included standalone verifier independently evaluates every one of the 262144 vertex subsets by two predicates, producing 49743 faces, and independently constructs two opposite-order acyclic matchings, checking each of 322092 Hasse incidences. Signed algebraic Morse reduction gives critical groups of ranks 16, 33, 36, 6 in dimensions 6–9; the boundary from dimension 7 is integrally surjective by an explicit 16-column unit minor, while the two higher boundaries vanish. An independent calculation from the original simplicial boundary matrices over the field of two elements corroborates all Betti ranks. This establishes integer coefficients and torsion-freeness, rather than inferring them from finite field data. The ordinary Morse model has one zero-cell and no cells in dimensions 1–5; Hurewicz and vanishing sixth homology yield six-connectivity. No homotopy wedge decomposition is claimed.

## Originality
PASS on inspected evidence. The founding article arXiv:2307.13271, Question 44, poses positive-stage categorical triple products as a homotopy problem. Its adjacent Theorem 42 and Corollary 43 require different factor patterns. The closest independence calculation, arXiv:2307.12401, is at stage zero; the relevant polyhedral-join work, arXiv:2307.14321, uses lexicographic products. Earlier exact stage-one computations on this graph likewise do not determine stage-two homology; the new answer is qualitatively different, with three adjacent groups. Searches over alternative complex names, categorical-product notation (including the equivalent graph \(C_6\times K_3\)), and mathematical research records found no result whose statement implies this exact integral answer. As always, an unindexed or unavailable equivalent computation remains possible.

## Value
PASS. This computes the entire integral homology, including torsion exclusion and connectivity, of the first nonbinary balanced triple-product case at the genuinely higher forest-filtration level two. It is a directly motivated finite case of Question 44 and reveals an unexpected shift from the previously known single-dimensional stage-one sphere wedge to nonzero classes in three consecutive degrees. This is an exact, natural invariant with independent topological information, not an arbitrary subset census. The result intentionally stops short of a full homotopy classification or a formula across parameters.
