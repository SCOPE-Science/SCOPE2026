# Scientific review of the cyclic seven-tournament multipath theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The asserted theorem is an **integral reduced homology** and rational cyclic-representation computation, not a homotopy-wedge theorem. The vertex and arc conventions were checked against the original path-poset definition. The two independent finite face enumerators agree. Two distinct directed acyclic matchings were checked across every one of the 11,844 Hasse incidences, and exact integer Morse boundaries yield compatible free integral homology. The first reduction has 24, 54 and 17 critical cells in dimensions 3–5 and an 11-rank unit-Smith boundary; the second independently gives ranks 1 and 18 for its nonzero differentials. An explicit determinant-one minor and mod-two boundary ranks corroborate the key computations. Rotation-orbit boundary matrices were constructed with orientation signs, not just orbit counts.

## Originality — PASS against the literature and records actually inspected

The primary full-text transitive-tournament theorem is inapplicable to the cyclic regular tournament, which contains directed cycles. Its Euler-characteristic formulas for related tournaments do not imply three integral homology groups or their cyclic representations. The earlier path-poset article gives construction and general methods but does not give this tournament's homology. The acyclic-quiver comparison theorem does not apply to directed cycles; bidirected polygon calculations concern different edge sets. Directed flag and tournaplex results use different simplicial constructions. Searches were also made for the consecutive-difference tournament under cyclic, regular, round-robin, circulant, arc-forest and multipath terminology. No identified result covers the claim. Remaining risk: an unindexed equivalent calculation or specialized computational table could exist.

## Value — PASS

This is a canonical vertex-transitive digraph, not an arbitrary arc subset. It lies immediately outside the transitive-tournament family handled by existing theory. Its exactly known integral homology spans three consecutive degrees and its rational cyclic symmetry exhibits both fixed and cyclotomic summands. The new result supplies a reproducible, nontrivial test case for multipath topology and equivariant refinements. The scope does not claim a broad theorem or a homotopy-wedge decomposition.
