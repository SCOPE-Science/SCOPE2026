# Scientific review: third projection-width moments of graphical zonotopes

## Correctness — PASS
The support-function normalization, Gaussian radial decomposition in the intrinsic \((N-1)\)-dimensional hyperplane, single/repeated-edge cumulant expansion and exhaustive three-edge shape classification yield the precise finite-motif formula. The less standard Gaussian absolute-moment kernels for star, path and triangle are reduced to displayed one-dimensional integrals or an explicit two-dimensional polar calculation. Independent deterministic quadrature and an exhaustive edge-subset enumeration on small graphs corroborate the formulas. The proof—not finite numerical testing—establishes the all-graph statement; the numerical prism-minus-biclique value is only illustrative.

## Originality — PASS, priority provisional
Padrol–Pilaud–Poullot (arXiv:2111.12422, full available article, Sections 1–2) study deformation cones, not random projection moments. The published *Sharp width-variance extremizers among tree graphical zonotopes* supplies exact first and second moments only; it does not give third cumulants, three-edge motif coefficients, or the prism-versus-\(K_{3,3}\) separation. Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets (arXiv:1511.08479, full text, Section 3) give higher moments for regular cubes, simplices and crosspolytopes; their general radial reduction is acknowledged and does not imply the specific graph-motif evaluation. Search comparisons also tested the total-variation/Gaussian absolute-differences and triangle-motif reformulations. No stronger covering statement was found. Residual risk: an unindexed graph Gaussian-variation or absolute-moment identity could already imply the result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Equal-degree graphs can share the first two spherical width moments while having different third moments. The prism and \(K_{3,3}\) supply a natural and exact six-vertex witness, while the complete formula applies to every connected simple graph and identifies which three-edge motifs drive the separation. This is a geometric distributional distinction invisible to established mean/variance invariants, not a routine rounding of a computed constant.

## Limitations
All results use the canonical unweighted root-coordinate Euclidean embedding. Higher moments, weighted edges, other embeddings and independent historical-priority certification are not claimed. The result remains subject to independent mathematical audit.
