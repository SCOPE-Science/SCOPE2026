# Mathematical review: three even-distance moments for parallelograms

## Correctness — PASS
For two independent area-uniform points, the affine coordinate differences are independent symmetric triangular variables. Their second, fourth, and sixth moments follow by direct integration; the three stated distance moments are exact polynomial identities. The two linear equations for the shape product and its angle-weighted counterpart have nonzero determinant \(41\), allowing unambiguous inversion. The feasibility inequalities are necessary by positive side lengths and positive area and sufficient because the reconstructed quadratic has positive roots and the reconstructed edge-line angle is genuine. The two-moment counterexample uses exact rational arithmetic; its squared side lengths differ, so its shapes are not congruent. The standalone verifier passed exact rational reconstructions, direct polynomial integration and numerical simulations. There are no unproved probabilistic or asymptotic limits in the theorem.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
The closest general predecessor, Sheng (1985), gives **forward** distance-moment formulas for parallelograms and more general planar domains. The full article was not accessible, making a hidden inverse observation an unresolved priority risk. Zhuang and Pan (2011), inspected in full text, derive distance laws for a prescribed \(60^\circ\) rhombus and do not reconstruct unknown side lengths and angles from the first three even moments. Aharonyan (2012), inspected in open PDF, gives mean-distance calculations. Tong and Pan (2016) outline forward distance distributions for arbitrary polygons. Two fully inspected published mathematical findings concern direction-unlabeled projection widths and a boundary/interior fourth-moment ratio, respectively; neither derives the present triple's unique inverse or its exact two-moment collision. A distinct antipodal **boundary**-sampling reconstruction, while similar in inverse ambition, is neither a theorem about independent area-uniform point pairs nor a source of the displayed moment inversion. Thus no checked statement logically covers this claim. Absence of a match is not proof of historical novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Random point-pair distances are canonical measurements in geometric probability and spatial-network models. An exact **minimum number of moments**, constructive shape recovery, and a necessary-and-sufficient moment-feasibility region answer a natural identifiability problem for the complete three-parameter family of positive-area parallelograms. The collision shows that the cutoff at three moments is substantive rather than an artifact of parameter counting. This result does not rely on arbitrary parameter slices or a previously tabulated numerical constant. The moment formulas alone are not claimed new; the mathematically motivated contribution is the exact inverse classification and sharp minimality.
