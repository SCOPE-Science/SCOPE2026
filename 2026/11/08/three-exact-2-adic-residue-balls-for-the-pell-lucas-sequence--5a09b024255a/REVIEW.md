# Mathematical review

## Correctness: PASS
The norm identities and doubling formula imply \(\nu_2(P_m)=\nu_2(m)\), which independently yields the exact odd-index difference valuation \(3+\nu_2(h)\). The finite-injection argument rigorously establishes both odd-index full balls. The remaining even-index values lie in a third ball; the polynomial \(z+4z^2\) is a 2-adic isometry, proving that this ball is also completely attained. The three index parities exhaust all indices; scaling by two gives the stated disjoint support, count, measure, and periodic infinite occurrence. Finite enumeration corroborates, not establishes, these unbounded quantifiers.

## Originality: PASS, with residual priority risk
Lang and Lang (arXiv:1307.7463, 2013), Proposition 5.2, establish only the set of moduli at which Pell–Lucas is *residue-complete*. Their statement is implied by neither the precise three-ball theorem alone nor vice versa without further modulus analysis; it cannot yield the complete explicit occupied classes or their Haar measure at \(2\). The older Carroll–Jacobson–Somer distribution theorem (1994, first-page assumptions) requires odd primes greater than 7 and therefore cannot imply the claimed \(2\)-adic result. A 2025 article concerns the *Fibonacci* sequence, a genuinely different recurrence, and is not a directly applicable classification of Pell–Lucas residues. Searches for the congruence-ball aliases and the published-result index did not identify an equivalent claim; failed searches do not certify novelty. Older or inaccessible classifications remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The precise residue image of a canonical second-kind Lucas sequence is a natural arithmetic invariant. Unlike mere non-residue-completeness, the result gives all occupied classes at every binary depth, including an exact limiting support proportion; the three distinct local branches explain the structural obstruction at the ramified prime. It offers useful complete input for later study of value distributions and modular orbit structure without assuming any unproved downstream theorems.

## Limitations
The limit \(9/64\) is Haar measure of the set of possible values, not the empirical distribution of terms. No conclusion about odd moduli or arbitrary Lucas parameters. Historical priority has not been independently established.
