On a three-agent neighborhood-confidence path with fixed \(0<\varepsilon\le1/4\), increasing the direct-influence weight \(\sigma\) causes three sharply distinct outcomes: perpetual center-opinion oscillations, random polarization, and complete freezing, separated at \(\sigma=\varepsilon\) and \(\sigma=2\varepsilon\). With half-compromise updates, the center's limiting variance jumps from \(1/12\) to \(1/4\) and then to \(0\), although its mean always remains \(1/2\).
