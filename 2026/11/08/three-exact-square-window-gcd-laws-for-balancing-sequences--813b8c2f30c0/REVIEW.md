# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim is for every \(k\ge1\), with the precise three recurrence initializations and every starting position \(n\ge0\). The proof explicitly telescopes the initial sums, calculates the full difference-GCDs using strong divisibility, and verifies every prime divisor through ordinary congruences and exact \(2\)- and \(3\)-adic valuations. The unusually sensitive cases are even \(k\) at the prime \(2\), and the extra \(3\) in the Lucas-cobalancing odd-index formula; both are handled with exact modular arguments. The executable exact-integer checker reproduced 37,440 sliding-window instances, plus the critical auxiliary identities. Finite testing is not substituted for proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources, with residual risk.** The primary 2023 paper asks for all four squared-window GCD formulas in Question 30 and presents the two relations among the three families only as experimental observations, rather than proving the stated formulas. The 2022 generalized Fibonacci square-window theorem has a different recurrence (\(1,1\), not \(6,-1\)); the 2024 Lucas-statistics paper treats periods and entry points rather than square-window GCDs. Targeted exact-phrase searches, mathematical-record searches and accessible same-topic historical claims found no covering theorem. This is a best-knowledge comparison, not a mathematical proof of novelty; any unindexed equivalent result remains a risk.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem supplies a simultaneous exact answer for three of the four families in a specific published open question, proves the two reported computational relations for all lengths, and replaces an infinite shifted GCD with one finite gcd involving the natural balancing number \(B_k\). The distinction of a factor \(2\) for even windows and \(3\) in the odd Lucas-cobalancing case exposes the arithmetic mechanism behind the empirical observations. The remaining gcd \(\gcd(k,B_k)\) and the fourth family are not classified here.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Mbirika--Schrader--Spilker, Journal of Integer Sequences 26 (2023), Article 23.6.4, Section 6.1 and Definitions 6--7, is the directly motivating and closest source. Mbirika--Spilker (2022), arXiv:2204.12626, proves a different-recurrence generalized Fibonacci analogue. Fiebig--Mbirika--Spilker (2024), arXiv:2408.14632, is the related Lucas-sequence modular-statistics study. The complete prime factorization of \(\gcd(k,B_k)\) remains outside the theorem and no independent review has been conducted.
