# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact theorem is a complete border classification for a three-marker cyclic word. A proper border of a cut-after-marker word beginning in zeros must contain one, two, or three ones; the strict comparison of its first and last zero gaps excludes every possibility. The dual cut-before-marker analysis excludes proper borders beginning at either of the last two ones. Other cuts have equal first and last letters. Counting nonnegative gap triples with one, two or three distinct values and dividing tagged-marker representations by three establishes the exact finite distribution, necklace counts and moments. The packaged standalone verifier matched the predicted cuts for every one of 27,405 three-one words through length 29, with a second border algorithm for lengths through 17. The infinite proof does not depend on finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Harju–Nowotka, *Border Correlation of Binary Words* (2003 report/2004 journal), introduced the invariant and established global restrictions and extremal statements. Holub–Müller, *Fully bordered words* (arXiv:1504.02222), characterized the general two-unbordered-conjugate case; this prior theorem overlaps the two-equal-gap branch, so that branch is not presented as a new global characterization. Clokie–Gabrić–Shallit (arXiv:1904.08187) established unrestricted maximum, realized spectrum and unconditional expectation statements. OEIS A321162 and A091838 tabulate different unrestricted statistics. Relevant full-text/primary material and searches did not reveal the new exact three-gap position-level classifier or its fixed-weight distribution. Historical-priority risk remains; the 2003 report was available as extracted text but not visual-page images, and an unindexed equivalent may exist.

## Value — PASS

The full cut pattern is the natural border-correlation observable, not an arbitrary derived parameter. The result gives the first sparse-marker stratum in which three unbordered rotations are possible, an exact gap criterion distinguishing the known fully bordered regime from the three-rotation regime, and all-length enumerative and moment laws. It does not claim a general alphabet-wide theorem, and its narrow scope is substantive because the complete natural family is classified.

## Limits

Exactly three ones, binary alphabet, ordinary borders and uniform sampling conditional on that content. No result is asserted for four or more markers. Finite checks are not exhaustive for infinite length. No independent expert mathematical review was performed.
