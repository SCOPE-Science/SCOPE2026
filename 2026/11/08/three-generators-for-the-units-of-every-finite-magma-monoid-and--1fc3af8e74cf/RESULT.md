# Three generators suffice, and are necessary, for the units of every finite magma monoid

## Finding
Let \(X\) be any nonempty finite set of cardinality \(n\). The **magma monoid** \(M(X)\) consists of all binary operations \(f:X\times X\to X\), with multiplication
\[
(f\diamond g)(a,b)=g(f(a,b),f(b,a)).
\]
Write \(U_n\) for its group of invertible elements and \(d(G)\) for the minimum cardinality of a generating subset of a finite group \(G\) (with \(d(1)=0\)). Then
\[
d(U_n)=\begin{cases}0& n=1,\\2& n=2,\\3& n\ge3.\end{cases}
\]
For every \(n\ge3\), an explicit three-element generating set is described below. In particular, **every generating set of the entire monoid \(M(X)\) contains at least five elements** for \(n\ge3\). This is a lower bound, not a claim that five generators suffice for the full monoid.

## Assumptions and scope
The underlying set is finite and nonempty; no associativity, commutativity or idempotency is imposed on the individual binary operations. The multiplication \(\diamond\) is associative with identity \(f(a,b)=a\). Denote \(N=\binom n2\). Coordinates of the \(N\) unordered pairs of distinct elements are enumerated arbitrarily as \(1,\ldots,N\). In our explicit formulas, permutations act on the left and are composed right-to-left. Reversing composition, as in the convention of \(\diamond\), does not change the generated subgroup.

For \(N\ge2\), let \(B_N=C_2^N\rtimes S_N\) be the signed-permutation group, with base vectors in \(\mathbb F_2^N\) and multiplication
\[
(b,\sigma)(b',\tau)=(b+\sigma b',\sigma\tau),
\qquad (\sigma b')_i=b'_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}.
\]
Write \(e_i\) for the coordinate vector with a single \(1\), \(c_m=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ m)\), and \(t_m=(1\ 2)\).

## Proof
**1. Exact unit structure.** Let \(J(a,b)=(b,a)\), and associate to \(f\in M(X)\) the map
\[
T_f(a,b)=(f(a,b),f(b,a)).
\]
Then \(T_fJ=JT_f\), and every map \(T:X^2\to X^2\) commuting with \(J\) uniquely has this form, by taking the first coordinate. Moreover \(T_{f\diamond g}=T_g\circ T_f\). Thus the magma monoid is anti-isomorphic to the monoid of \(J\)-equivariant maps. Its units correspond exactly to bijective equivariant maps.

The involution \(J\) has \(n\) fixed points \((a,a)\) and \(N\) two-element orbits \(\{(a,b),(b,a)\}\) with \(a\ne b\). An equivariant bijection independently permutes the fixed points, permutes the two-element orbits, and chooses a flip on each such orbit. Therefore, as a group,
\[
U_n\cong S_n\times B_N,
\qquad |U_n|=n!\,2^N N!.
\]
This structural decomposition is background, not the new generator-rank assertion.

**2. Lower bound.** When \(n=1\), \(U_1\) is trivial. When \(n=2\), \(N=1\) and \(U_2\cong C_2\times C_2\), so \(d(U_2)=2\). For \(n\ge3\), \(N\ge3\), and the abelianizations are
\[
(S_n)_{\rm ab}\cong C_2,
\qquad (B_N)_{\rm ab}\cong C_2\times C_2.
\]
For the second identity, the base subgroup \(C_2^N\) has one-dimensional coinvariants under the transitive \(S_N\) action (coordinate-parity), and the quotient \(S_N\) supplies a second, independent sign character. Hence \((U_n)_{\rm ab}\cong C_2^3\). Any generating set maps to a spanning set of this three-dimensional vector space, and \(d(U_n)\ge3\).

**3. Two generators of the signed-permutation factor.** For every \(N\ge2\), define
\[
u=(e_1,c_N),\qquad v=(0,t_N)\in B_N.
\]
The permutation parts \(c_N,t_N\) generate \(S_N\). Direct multiplication yields
\[
u^N=(e_1+\cdots+e_N,1).
\]
The permutation \(c_Nt_N\) fixes coordinate \(2\) and cycles all other \(N-1\) coordinates, including coordinate \(1\). Consequently
\[
(uv)^{N-1}=(e_1+e_3+\cdots+e_N,1).
\]
Multiplying these two pure flips gives \((e_2,1)\). Since the subgroup generated by \(u,v\) projects onto all of \(S_N\), conjugation yields \((e_j,1)\) for every \(j\). These flips generate the whole base subgroup, so \(\langle u,v\rangle=B_N\).

**4. Three generators for all \(n\ge3\).** In \(S_n\times B_N\), take
\[
g_1=(c_n,u),\qquad g_2=(t_n,1),\qquad g_3=(1,v).
\]
Conjugating \(g_2\) by \(g_1^k\), for \(0\le k\le n-2\), gives
\[
g_1^kg_2g_1^{-k}=(c_n^kt_nc_n^{-k},1)=((k+1\ \ k+2),1).
\]
These are all adjacent transpositions; hence \(S_n\times1\subseteq\langle g_1,g_2,g_3\rangle\). Cancelling the first coordinate in \(g_1\) then yields \((1,u)\). Together with \(g_3=(1,v)\), this generates \(1\times B_N\). Thus \(d(U_n)\le3\), and the lower bound proves equality. To realize the generators as actual binary operations, form the corresponding equivariant bijections of \(X^2\) and assign their first coordinates as operation tables. The anti-isomorphism from step 1 preserves the subgroup generated by a set, so these three operations generate \(U_n\) under \(\diamond\).

**5. Five-generator obstruction for the whole monoid.** In any finite monoid, a product is invertible only when both factors are invertible. Thus the units of a generating set must generate the whole unit group, requiring at least three unit generators for \(n\ge3\). There are **two disjoint, indispensable types of nonunits**, distinguished by their action on the diagonal.

For \(f\in M(X)\), let its diagonal restriction be \(\delta_f:X\to X\), \(\delta_f(a)=f(a,a)\). The identity \(\delta_{f\diamond g}=\delta_g\circ\delta_f\) shows that the operations with **nonbijective** diagonal restriction form a two-sided ideal \(I\), because composing a nonbijective self-map of a finite set with any other self-map on either side remains nonbijective. Such operations exist: starting from first projection \(f(a,b)=a\), change \(f(a,a)\) to \(b\ne a\), keeping all other entries. Any generating set must contain a member of \(I\), because the operations with bijective diagonal restriction form a submonoid.

There are also nonunits **outside** \(I\): begin with the first projection and, for a fixed pair \(a\ne b\), set both \(f(a,b)=a\) and \(f(b,a)=a\), leaving every other value unchanged. The diagonal restriction remains the identity, but \(T_f\) is not injective, so \(f\) is a nonunit. A product of units and elements of \(I\) is either a unit or belongs to \(I\), because the complement of the units is an ideal and \(I\) is an ideal. Therefore, to generate this type of nonunit, at least one generator must be **a nonunit with bijective diagonal restriction**. This generator cannot be the necessary generator in \(I\). Consequently, every generating set for \(M(X)\) contains three distinct units and at least one nonunit of each of two disjoint types, for a total of at least five. (The same argument gives the known lower bound four for \(n=2\).)

## Verification
The accompanying `check_unit_rank.py` is a standalone Python 3 standard-library checker; run it with `python3 check_unit_rank.py`. The exact output is in `check_unit_rank_output.txt`. It enumerates all equivariant permutation tables for \(n=1,2,3\), obtaining group orders \(1,4,288\); exhaustively generates \(B_N\) from the two explicit generators for \(2\le N\le6\); exhaustively generates all \(288\) units of \(M(3)\) directly from the **three binary-operation tables**, using \(\diamond\); and exhaustively checks that symmetric operations form a two-sided ideal in \(M(2)\). It also checks predicted group orders and adjacent-transposition identities for \(n=3,4,5,6\). This finite computation is a check, not the proof for all \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
The 2022 work of López-Permouth, Owusu-Mensah and Rafieipour established the basic structure of the magma monoid and its units. Baiduk and Kozerenko's 2026 transformation-semigroup study develops a systematic framework for the magma monoid's Green relations, ideals, idempotents and regular elements. The present result does **not** claim novelty for the known unit-group structure \(S_n\times(C_2\wr S_N)\), or for textbook facts about symmetric-group abelianizations.

Owusu-Mensah's 2026 *Generator Sets for the Magma Monoid* analyzes generators of the full monoid and proves a lower bound of four generators. Its explicitly two-generated \(S_n\times C_2\) is a **subgroup** of the units, not all units once \(n\ge3\). The new sharp result gives three necessary unit generators for all \(n\ge3\), and hence the five-generator obstruction for the full magma monoid. This is a lower-bound improvement, not a new complete generating set for the full monoid.

## Limitations
The minimal number of generators of \(M(X)\) itself is not determined for \(n\ge3\). The nonidentity units' explicit multiplication in operation-table coordinates depends on an arbitrary enumeration of unordered pairs. The article introducing the monoid in 2022 was accessible through bibliographic information and abstracts, but its full text was not available for complete priority checking. The known direct-product decomposition could make these numerical results a consequence of general results about generators of products, even if no explicit equivalent classification was found in the inspected material. Independent mathematical and historical review has not been performed.
