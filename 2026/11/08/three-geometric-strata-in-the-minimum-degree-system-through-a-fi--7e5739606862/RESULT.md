# Three geometric strata in the minimum-degree system through a finite projective plane

## Finding
Let \(q=p^e\ge2\) be a prime power, let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_q\), and let \(\mathcal P=\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_q)\subset\mathbb P^2_k\). Its full linear system of plane curves of minimum positive degree passing through every point of \(\mathcal P\) is the projective plane of equations
\[
C_a:\quad G_a(X)=\det\begin{pmatrix}a_0&a_1&a_2\\X_0&X_1&X_2\\X_0^q&X_1^q&X_2^q\end{pmatrix}=0,
\qquad a=[a_0:a_1:a_2]\in\mathbb P^2(k).
\]
Write \(r(a)=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\langle a_0,a_1,a_2\rangle\in\{1,2,3\}\); this does not depend on scaling the coordinate vector. The complete reduced-factorization, genus, and singularity stratification is:

1. When \(r(a)=1\), \(a\) is \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational and \(C_a\) is the reduced star of the \(q+1\) distinct \(\mathbb F_q\)-lines through \(a\).
2. When \(r(a)=2\), \(a\) lies on a unique \(\mathbb F_q\)-line \(L_a\). The curve is \(C_a=L_a\cup H_a\), where \(H_a\) is a geometrically integral, rational curve of degree \(q\). It has no singularities except possibly \(p_a=a^{1/q}\), has multiplicity \(q-1\) there, and \(L_a\) meets it at that point with intersection multiplicity \(q\). For \(q=2\), \(H_a\) is smooth; for \(q>2\), it has exactly one unibranch cusp.
3. When \(r(a)=3\), \(C_a\) is geometrically integral and rational, of degree \(q+1\), with precisely one singularity: a unibranch cusp at \(p_a=a^{1/q}\) of multiplicity \(q\) and tangent contact \(q+1\). Its delta invariant is \(q(q-1)/2\).

Thus the irreducible locus in the parameter plane is exactly the complement of the union of all \(\mathbb F_q\)-lines (a finite-field Drinfeld arrangement complement). If \(K=\mathbb F_{q^m}\subset k\) contains \(a\), all normalization maps and the singular points are \(K\)-defined, and
\[
\#C_a(K)=\begin{cases}(q+1)q^m+1,&r(a)=1,\\2q^m+1,&r(a)=2,\\q^m+1,&r(a)=3.\end{cases}
\]
Finally, if \(M(X)=\det(X,X^{(q)},X^{(q^2)})\), the defining product, up to a unit, of all \(\mathbb F_q\)-lines, then every nonzero projective polar of \(M\) is the \(q\)-fold (possibly reducible) curve \(C_a\) for a uniquely determined \(a\), after taking coordinatewise \(q\)-th roots of the polar coefficients.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(k\) is algebraically closed of characteristic \(p\), \(q=p^e\), and the forms are allowed to have coefficients in \(k\), **not only in \(\mathbb F_q\)**. The phrase “passing through all finite-plane points” refers to the geometric inclusion \(\mathcal P\subset C_a(k)\); it does not assert that an extension-defined curve is a plane-filling curve *defined over* \(\mathbb F_q\). The field \(K\) used for point counts is an arbitrary finite extension containing the coefficients of \(a\). The \(q\)-root point is \(p_a=[a_0^{1/q}:a_1^{1/q}:a_2^{1/q}]\).

## Proof
**Full minimal linear system.** The homogeneous vanishing ideal of the \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational projective points is generated by the three degree-\(q+1\) forms
\[
U=X_1^qX_2-X_1X_2^q,\qquad V=X_2^qX_0-X_2X_0^q,\qquad W=X_0^qX_1-X_0X_1^q.
\]
This is Tallini's ideal calculation (as reproduced and proved in Homma--Kim, Proposition 2.1). Extension of scalars to \(k\) preserves it. There is no nonzero form of degree at most \(q\) in the ideal, and \(U,V,W\) are independent, so the degree-\(q+1\) linear system is exactly \(\mathbb P\langle U,V,W\rangle\). The determinant \(G_a\) is, up to harmless signs, \(a_0U+a_1V+a_2W\).

**Unique critical point and multiplicity.** Differentiating the determinant in characteristic \(p\) gives the vector identity
\[
\nabla_XG_a=a\mathbin{\times}X^{(q)}.
\]
Thus all three partial derivatives vanish only at \(p_a\), where the determinant vanishes too. For a nonrational parameter \(a\), write local projective coordinates \(X=p_a+u\) near \(p_a\); the determinant becomes
\[
G_a(p_a+u)=\det(a,p_a,u^{(q)})+\det(a,u,u^{(q)}).
\]
The degree-\(q\) term is nonzero precisely because \(a\) and \(p_a\) are linearly independent, equivalently \(r(a)>1\). It is a \(q\)-th power of one nonzero linear form. Hence \(\operatorname{mult}_{p_a}C_a=q\) and the tangent cone is a single line repeated \(q\) times. The gradient's zero set is zero-dimensional, so \(G_a\) is squarefree.

**All linear components.** Let \(L\) be a line component with underlying two-dimensional vector subspace spanned by independent \(v,w\). The coefficients of \(s^{q+1},t^{q+1},st^q,s^qt\) in \(G_a(sv+tw)\) must all vanish. If \(a\notin L\), these four equations force \(v^{(q)}\) and \(w^{(q)}\) both to lie in \(ka\), contradicting their independence. Consequently \(a\in L\). The same vanishing now forces \(L^{(q)}\subseteq L\), hence \(L\) is defined over \(\mathbb F_q\). Conversely, every \(\mathbb F_q\)-line containing \(a\) is visibly a component. Thus the number of line components is \(q+1,1,0\) in ranks \(1,2,3\), respectively.

**No hidden nonlinear factors.** If a reduced degree-\(d\) curve has a unique singular point \(p\) of multiplicity \(d-1\) and is reducible, all components pass through \(p\) by Bézout (all component intersections must be singular). For their respective degrees \(d_i\) and multiplicities \(m_i\) at \(p\), one has \(\sum_i(d_i-m_i)=1\). At least one component therefore has \(d_i=m_i\); an irreducible plane curve of that kind must be a line through \(p\). In rank three there are no lines, so \(C_a\) is integral. In rank two, after removing the unique line, the residual curve \(H_a\) has degree \(q\) and multiplicity \(q-1\) at \(p_a\); if it were reducible, it would have a second line component, which is impossible. For rank one, the \(q+1\) known distinct linear factors already exhaust the degree.

**Cusps and rationality.** For rank three the unique tangent line cannot be a component; its local intersection multiplicity is therefore exactly \(q+1\), by Bézout and the single tangent cone. If there were two local branches, their tangent contacts would sum to at least \(q+2\), since each branch has tangent contact strictly greater than its multiplicity. Thus there is only one branch. Projection from a point of multiplicity degree minus one is birational to \(\mathbb P^1\), and the plane genus formula gives the stated delta invariant. In rank two the unique linear component is the tangent line at \(p_a\); it meets \(H_a\) only there (otherwise \(G_a\) would have another critical point), with total contact \(q\). The same branch argument for multiplicity \(q-1\) shows \(H_a\) is unibranch there, and projection proves rationality. For \(q=2\) its multiplicity is one, so it is smooth; otherwise its only singularity is a cusp of delta \((q-1)(q-2)/2\).

**Parameter and point strata.** Projective rank one means \(a\in\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_q)\); rank two means membership in exactly one \(\mathbb F_q\)-line but not in its rational point set. The complement has rank three. If \(a\in\mathbb P^2(K)\), the unique singularity and every component are \(K\)-defined; the normalization of each integral rational component is \(\mathbb P^1_K\). Each cusp has one rational preimage in the normalization, giving the displayed point counts. Equivalently, among \(Q^2+Q+1\) parameters over \(K=\mathbb F_Q\), \(Q=q^m\), there are \(q^2+q+1\) of rank one, \((q^2+q+1)(Q-q)\) of rank two, and all remaining parameters have rank three.

**Polar identity.** The degree-\(q^2+q+1\) Moore determinant \(M(X)\) is, up to a nonzero scalar, the product of all \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear forms defining projective lines. Its partials are exactly \((U^q,V^q,W^q)\), up to the same orientation. Since \(k\) is perfect, an arbitrary nonzero polar \(b_0M_{X_0}+b_1M_{X_1}+b_2M_{X_2}\) equals \((b_0^{1/q}U+b_1^{1/q}V+b_2^{1/q}W)^q\), proving the polar statement.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` implements exact arithmetic in \(\mathbb F_8\) and \(\mathbb F_{27}\) and enumerates all projective parameter points in both fields. For each of the three ranks it checks one representative's curve-point count, finds the unique gradient-critical point, and enumerates *all* linear factors over that field, comparing with the \(\mathbb F_q\)-lines through the parameter. It also counts every parameter rank. The complete results are in `verify_output.txt`. Both runs returned `PASS`; the script ends with `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED`. The general prime-power theorem is proved symbolically above, and the finite tests are corroborative rather than proofs for all \(q\).

## Relationship to prior work
Tallini's generators and the rank-one \(\mathbb F_q\)-coefficient star appear in Homma--Kim, *Nonsingular plane filling curves of minimum degree over a finite field and their automorphism groups*, arXiv:0903.1918 (2009), Proposition 2.1 and Proposition 2.3. That source treats plane-filling curves **defined over** \(\mathbb F_q\), and its next-degree smooth classification concerns degree \(q+2\), not the geometrically parametrized degree-\(q+1\) system after algebraically closed coefficient extension. Homma's *Fragments of plane filling curves* (2020), Sections 2--5, likewise discusses coefficient matrices over \(\mathbb F_q\), degree \(q+2\) filling curves and degree \(q+1\) *affine*-plane filling curves. The present result instead determines the entire base-changed minimum-degree parameter plane, its reducibility arrangement, cusp geometry, point-count profiles, and its interpretation as the polar linear system of the Moore product. Literature searches did not locate this complete coefficient-extension stratification; historical priority remains provisional.

## Limitations
This is an algebraic-closure coefficient classification, not a counterexample to lower-degree bounds for curves **defined over** \(\mathbb F_q\). The formal analytic isomorphism class of the cusp is not asserted; only the rigorously established multiplicity, unique branch, tangent contact, geometric genus, and delta invariant are claimed. The result does not compute global moduli of plane models modulo unrestricted projective transformations. The novelty assessment requires independent historical verification, especially against older treatments of Frobenius incidence curves and Tallini's 1961 articles.
