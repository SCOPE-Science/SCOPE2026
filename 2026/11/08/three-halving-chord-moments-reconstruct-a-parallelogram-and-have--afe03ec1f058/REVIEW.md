# Scientific review: three halving-chord moments of parallelograms

## Correctness

**PASS.** The sampling law has two side orientations with probabilities \(a/(a+b)\) and \(b/(a+b)\); central symmetry identifies a perimeter-halving chord with twice the center-to-boundary-point distance. Exact integration of the resulting quadratic polynomials gives all three moment identities, including the sixth moment's \(abA^2\) term. The inverse divides by \(A^2>0\) only for a nondegenerate parallelogram; side lengths are the roots of an explicit quadratic and the acute edge-line angle comes from \(\sin\phi=A/(ab)\). The constraints \(A\le ab\le(a+b)^2/4\) are both necessary and sufficient, proving the sharp feasible interval and its rectangle/rhombus equality cases. The rectangle/rhombus pair with equal perimeter and area proves minimality. Exact symbolic and numerical checks reproduced the stated identities. The proof does not require computational exhaustiveness.

## Originality

**PASS, with provisional historical priority.** The 2004–2005 halving-chord literature studies geometric dilation, distance extrema, and constant-distance curves; the 2007 article studies area-halving chords and associated inequalities. The closest published parallelogram fourth-moment comparison explicitly contains the formulas for the second and fourth *centered boundary-radius* moments, which imply the first two present identities. These are credited and **not presented as novel**. That source treats independent pairs and only moments through fourth order, whereas the final claim is the first-three-even-halving-chord inverse, its minimality, and a sharp complete sixth-moment region. A separate projection-width inverse uses direction-uniform projected widths and their maximum, not perimeter-halving distance moments. The searches identified no equivalent or stronger statement that implies the complete new claim. Remaining risks include differently phrased early higher-moment identities and imperfect indexing; no independent priority audit has taken place.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A classical continuum of perimeter-halving chords becomes a direction-unlabeled geometric probe. The sixth moment is the first initial even moment that resolves the side-product ambiguity remaining after perimeter and area have been determined. The theorem supplies an explicit congruence reconstruction, a proof of the minimal number of moments, a complete feasible region, and geometrically meaningful sharp equality families (rectangles versus rhombi). The primary contribution is this structural inverse/attainability classification, not the routine reproduction of older low-order formulas. It does not provide noise stability or apply to general quadrilaterals.
