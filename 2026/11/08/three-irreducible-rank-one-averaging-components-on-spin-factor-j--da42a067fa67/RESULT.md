# Three irreducible rank-one averaging components on spin-factor Jordan algebras

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field with \(\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2\). Let \(n\ge3\), and let \(B\) be a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on an \(n\)-dimensional space \(V\). Write \(J=k1\oplus V\), with spin-factor Jordan multiplication
\[
(s,u)\circ(t,v)=(st+B(u,v),sv+tu).
\]
The *rank-at-most-one averaging locus* consists of linear maps \(P:J\to J\) of vector-space rank at most one satisfying, for all \(x,y\in J\),
\[
P(P(x)\circ y)=P(x)\circ P(y)=P(x\circ P(y)).
\]
Its **reduced geometric solution variety** has exactly three geometrically irreducible components, each defined over \(k\):

* The central-image affine linear space \(C\), whose maps have the form \(P(x)=f(x)1\), of dimension \(n+1\).
* The quadric component \(Q\), the affine cone generated by \(P(x)=\langle a,x\rangle a\) for \(a=(s,u)\ne0\) satisfying \(B(u,u)=s^2\), where \(\langle(s,u),(t,v)\rangle=st+B(u,v)\). Its dimension is \(n\).
* The isotropic-incidence component \(N\), the affine cone generated by \(P(t,w)=B(z,w)(0,u)\), with \(0\ne u\in V\), \(B(u,u)=0\), and \(0\ne z\in u^\perp\). Its dimension is \(2n-3\).

Their set-theoretic pairwise intersections satisfy \(C\cap Q=C\cap N=\{0\}\); \(Q\cap N\) is the cone over the isotropic quadric embedded by \([u]\mapsto[(0,u)\otimes B(u,-)]\), of dimension \(n-1\). In particular, at \(n=4\) the central and incidence components tie in dimension, while from \(n=5\) onward the incidence component is strictly the largest.

For every finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\) of odd cardinality, put
\[
M=|\{u\in V:B(u,u)=1\}|,\qquad Z=|\{0\ne u\in V:B(u,u)=0\}|.
\]
The exact number of **nonzero** rank-one averaging maps is
\[
\boxed{A_{n,B}(q)=(q^{n+1}-1)+(q-1)M+\frac{Z}{q-1}(q^{n-1}-1).}
\]
These summands enumerate maps with central image, noncentral idempotent-containing image, and square-zero image, respectively. Their \(P^2=0\) subcount equals \(q^n-1+Z(q^{n-1}-1)/(q-1)\). Including the zero map increases each total by one.

For \(\chi\) the quadratic character and \(\epsilon=\chi((-1)^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\det B)\), the two quadratic-form counts are explicit. If \(n=2m\),
\[
M=q^{n-1}-\epsilon q^{m-1},\qquad Z=q^{n-1}+\epsilon(q-1)q^{m-1}-1.
\]
If \(n=2m+1\),
\[
M=q^{n-1}+\epsilon q^m,\qquad Z=q^{n-1}-1.
\]
In every allowed dimension the total operator count distinguishes the two determinant square classes of nondegenerate symmetric forms over \(\mathbb F_q\): the coefficient of \(\epsilon\) in the total is \(q^{m-1}(q^{n-1}-q)\) when \(n=2m\), and \((q-1)q^m\) when \(n=2m+1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The defining bilinear form is nondegenerate and symmetric, \(n\ge3\), and the ground field has characteristic different from two. The maps are *linear averaging operators* satisfying the displayed two-sided identity, **not** Rota–Baxter operators. All varieties are considered with their reduced induced structures; no assertion is made about the possibly nonreduced original equation scheme. Geometric irreducibility means irreducibility after base change to an algebraic closure. All exact counts concern labeled linear maps, not automorphism orbits.

## Proof

**Step 1: rank-one reduction.** Every nonzero rank-one map is \(P(x)=f(x)a\) with \(0\ne f\in J^*\), \(0\ne a\in J\), unique up to \((a,f)\sim(ta,t^{-1}f)\). For any bilinear product, substitution into the first averaging identity gives
\[
f(x)f(y)a^2=f(x)f(a\circ y)a.\]
Choosing \(x\) and \(y\) with nonzero functional values forces a scalar \(\lambda\) with \(a^2=\lambda a\) and \(f\circ L_a=\lambda f\), where \(L_a(y)=a\circ y\). Conversely, these two equalities imply both averaging identities because the spin-factor product is commutative. This is an equivalence, not just a necessary condition.

**Step 2: solve the scalar and eigenfunctional equations.** Write \(a=(s,u)\). Its square is \(a^2=(s^2+B(u,u),2su)\). If \(u=0\), then \(s\ne0\), the equation holds with \(\lambda=s\), and \(L_a=s\operatorname{id}\); hence every nonzero \(f\) is allowed. Projectively this is precisely \(C\).

If \(u\ne0\), comparison of vector parts yields \(\lambda=2s\) and \(B(u,u)=s^2\). Identify \(J^*\cong k\oplus V\) through \(f(t,v)=\alpha t+B(z,v)\). Expanding \(f(L_a(t,v))=2s f(t,v)\) for every \(t,v\) gives the two equivalent equations
\[
\alpha u=s z,\qquad B(z,u)=s\alpha.
\]
For \(s\ne0\), \(z=\alpha u/s\), so \(f\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(\langle a,-\rangle\); this produces \(Q\) away from its boundary. For \(s=0\), \(\alpha=0\), \(B(u,u)=0\), and \(z\in u^\perp\setminus\{0\}\). This is exactly the incidence family \(N\). When \(s=0\) and \(z\) is proportional to \(u\), the operator is also on \(Q\). Therefore the complete solution is \(C\cup Q\cup N\), including its zero operator.

**Step 3: algebraic geometry and component minimality.** The rank-one projectivization is the Segre embedding \(\mathbb P(J)\times\mathbb P(J^*)\hookrightarrow\mathbb P(\operatorname{End}J)\). The central family is a projective \(\mathbb P^n\); its cone has dimension \(n+1\). The projective quadric \(B(u,u)=s^2\) in \(\mathbb P^n\) is smooth and geometrically irreducible because its quadratic form is nondegenerate and \(n\ge3\). Its embedding \([a]\mapsto[a\otimes\langle a,-\rangle]\) is closed, giving the cone \(Q\) of dimension \(n\). The boundary \(s=0\) of this quadric is the smooth projective quadric \(B(u,u)=0\) in \(\mathbb P(V)\), of dimension \(n-2\).

The incidence base \(\{([u],[z]):B(u,u)=B(u,z)=0\}\), with both projective vectors nonzero, is a projective bundle with fiber \(\mathbb P(u^\perp)\cong\mathbb P^{n-2}\) over that smooth isotropic quadric. It is therefore geometrically irreducible of dimension \(2n-4\). Its Segre image is closed and has affine-cone dimension \(2n-3\). Every projective point of \(C\) has image line \(k1\), whereas none in \(Q\cup N\) does. The latter meet exactly when \(s=0\) and \([z]=[u]\), producing an \((n-1)\)-dimensional cone. There are points of \(Q\) with \(s\ne0\), and points of \(N\) with \([z]\ne[u]\) because \(n-1\ge2\). Thus none of the three closed irreducible sets is contained in the union of the others, proving they are exactly the components of the reduced solution variety, with the stated intersections.

**Step 4: finite-field counts.** For a central image line, an operator is specified by a nonzero functional, yielding \(q^{n+1}-1\) maps. For \(s\ne0\), the noncentral image line is uniquely normalized to \((1,u)\) with \(B(u,u)=1\); it permits exactly \(q-1\) nonzero multiples of \(\langle (1,u),-\rangle\). For \(s=0\), there are \(Z/(q-1)\) isotropic image lines and exactly \(q^{n-1}-1\) nonzero linear functionals vanishing on \(k1\) and the image vector. The summands are disjoint (notwithstanding the geometric closure intersection) because the case \(s=0\) belongs to the third counting stratum. The square-zero map condition \(P^2(x)=f(a)P(x)\) yields the displayed nilpotent subcount: central images require \(f(1)=0\), noncentral \(s\ne0\) images never yield zero, and every \(s=0\) incidence operator has square zero.

For completeness, the formulas for \(M\) and \(Z\) follow by elementary quadratic Gauss sums. Let \(\psi\) be any nontrivial additive character of \(\mathbb F_q\) and \(G=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_q}\psi(t^2)\), so \(G^2=\chi(-1)q\). Diagonalizing \(B\), additive orthogonality gives for every \(c\in\mathbb F_q\)
\[
\#\{v:B(v,v)=c\}=q^{-1}\sum_{h\in\mathbb F_q}\psi(-hc)\prod_{i=1}^n\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}\psi(hb_i x^2),
\]
where \(b_i\) are the nonzero diagonal coefficients of \(B\). For \(h\ne0\), the product is \(\chi(h)^n\chi(\det B)G^n\). Sum over \(h\) at \(c=0\) and \(c=1\), using \(\sum_{h\ne0}\psi(-h)=-1\), \(\sum_{h\ne0}\chi(h)=0\), and \(\sum_{h\ne0}\chi(h)\psi(-h)=\chi(-1)G\), to obtain both parity cases. Every term and denominator in the enumeration is integral because \(q-1\) divides \(Z\).

## Verification

The attached standalone `check_averaging.py` independently enumerates all normalized nonzero rank-one linear maps on the indicated small spin factors, then evaluates the **original averaging identity on every pair of basis vectors** using the original Jordan multiplication rather than the classification equations. The second averaging identity follows by commutativity. The independent count equals each claimed stratum, and \(P^2=0\) is checked directly. The script's plain-text output is embedded as `CHECKER_OUTPUT.txt`. Cases tested are \(n=3\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) with both discriminant classes; \(n=3\) over \(\mathbb F_5\) with both classes; and \(n=4\) over \(\mathbb F_3\). These finite checks are corroboration, not a substitute for the general proof. Reproduce with `python3 check_averaging.py` using Python 3 and no third-party dependencies.

## Relationship to prior work

Guo–Pei (arXiv:1401.7386v1, 2014) and Cao (arXiv:1401.7389v1, 2014) establish general averaging-operator theory, principally in associative and free-algebra contexts. The result here treats the nonassociative Jordan product and gives its full rank-one reduced geometry and field-sensitive enumeration. Shirokov (arXiv:1412.0246v1, 2014) studies group-action Reynolds averages on Clifford algebras; those particular projections do not classify all averaging maps on Jordan spin factors. Jordan Peirce theory supplies classical background for the eigenfunctional reduction. The square-zero-incidence branch overlaps with previously analyzed rank-one weight-zero Rota–Baxter operators on spin factors; that branch's stand-alone existence and related enumeration are **not asserted as new**. The contribution assessed here is the complete three-component averaging variety, the precise way its strata attach, the dimension transition, and the combined discriminant-sensitive averaging-operator census.

## Limitations

The proof assumes characteristic different from two, a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form, and \(n\ge3\). It does not classify rank-two or higher averaging maps, nonreduced scheme structures, or automorphism-conjugacy classes. Published-priority verification is provisional: an unidentified general classification may cover aspects of the result. Verification of the unrestricted-field geometric statements rests on the proof, not finite exhaustive tests. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
