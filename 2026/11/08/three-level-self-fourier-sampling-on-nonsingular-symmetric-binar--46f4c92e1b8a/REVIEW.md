# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every odd prime power and every nontrivial additive character, the trace pairing on symmetric \(2\times2\) matrices is nondegenerate. Projective parametrization of the determinant-zero cone and exact projective-root counting give the cone Fourier transform, including the origin and the vanishing values. A character-weighted basis of the \(q+1\) isotropic lines diagonalizes the cone Gram operator with the asserted eigenvalues. Complementary blocks of the complete unitary Fourier transform transfer this spectrum to the nonsingular locus and account for the additional unit singular values. The resulting multiplicities sum to \(q^2(q-1)\), and every level is positive. The packaged calculation of finite examples also passed, including an extension field; it is supplementary to the proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison

Iosevich–Koh (arXiv:0804.4505, particularly the introduction and main estimates) study Fourier restriction/extension on nondegenerate quadratic level surfaces. Koh–Shen (arXiv:0908.3266, Sections 2 and 4) give zero-cone Fourier formulas and \(L^p\)-improving bounds; their Fourier formula is a known ingredient, not the present square compressed matrix's diagonalization. Schmidt (arXiv:1410.7184, Sections 1–2) determines eigenmatrices of the ambient symmetric-bilinear-form translation association scheme; it does not state singular values of its Fourier matrix compressed to the nonsingular locus. Historical local comparisons include finite quadratic cones in **even** dimension, self-Fourier sampling on unrestricted nonzero rank-one matrices, and rank-one projection convolution. Those objects/operators differ from this three-dimensional nonsingular-symmetric compression, and their formulas do not imply the displayed multiset without additional analysis.

Residual priority risk: a broad result on compressed Fourier transforms of quadratic cones or symmetric-form association schemes could encompass this special case, even though it was not found in the bounded searches. The review does not assert an exhaustive priority proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Invertible symmetric bilinear forms are a natural quadratic rank stratum. For self-sampling on this stratum, nonsingularity alone does not quantify stability or describe the full energy distribution. The result determines all eigenvalue multiplicities of the positive Gram matrix for infinitely many odd finite fields, gives a universal reconstruction guarantee and an exact condition number, and identifies a transparent projective-conic mechanism. This is a complete natural spectral classification rather than a single numerical example or a renamed general estimate.

## Scientific limits

Characteristic two, higher matrix sizes, and phaseless or erased-data reconstruction are outside scope. Numerical tests cover only the finite cases listed in `VERIFICATION.md`. There is no independent mathematical validation.
