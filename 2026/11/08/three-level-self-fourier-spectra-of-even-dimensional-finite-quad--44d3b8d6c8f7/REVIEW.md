# Scientific review: three-level self-Fourier spectra of finite quadratic cones

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact cone character sum (including the zero-frequency term and quadratic-type sign) is derived by Gauss summation and completion of the square. The Gram operator becomes a scalar plus the projective orthogonality relation and the all-ones operator. Counting perpendicular isotropic points yields the two possible projective eigenvalues; the split four-dimensional and nonsplit ovoid boundary cases are treated separately. The origin/uniform block has explicitly computed trace and determinant. The remaining multiplicities follow from dimension and the unit-modulus trace identity. Each eigenvalue is strictly positive, and normalizing by the ambient Fourier dimension yields the claimed sharp condition number. Directly constructed examples over four prime fields/dimensions and an extension field give independent finite checks of the formulas. Risk: finite tests do not replace the general algebra; the proof provides the required universal coverage.

## Originality
**PASS with residual priority risk.** Iosevich–Koh develops finite-field quadratic-surface restriction and exponential-sum estimates. Koh–Yeom, especially its cone transform Lemma 4.1, supplies a key Fourier-analytic input, but investigates restricted averaging mapping estimates rather than the singular spectrum of the square operator with both domain and frequency coordinates on the full zero cone. Classical orthogonal polar-graph eigenvalues are also prior knowledge. The combined statement of the three singular levels, their exact multiplicities in both types and every characteristic, and the dimension-independent sharp condition number was not located in the inspected primary statements or the bounded indexed comparisons. The nearest indexed result concerns the *degree-one Fourier band* on a split cone, which does not determine the full self-restriction matrix. A general association-scheme theorem might imply this calculation and remains a historical-priority risk; no exhaustive priority claim is made.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The self-dual quadratic cone is a canonical nonlinear support/frequency aperture in finite-field restriction theory. Its full input–output compression has exponentially many degrees of freedom, yet the theorem gives exact singular values, sharp stability of inversion, and an explicit dimension of functions simultaneously supported in space and frequency on the cone. The uniform condition number and the coincidence of the spectra for split and nonsplit types are meaningful structural invariants, rather than one isolated finite computation. The distinction between the quadratic types survives in the multiplicities, so the classification carries geometric content. Limitations: the origin is included; no weighted, nonzero-level, singular-form, or odd-dimensional generalization is established.
