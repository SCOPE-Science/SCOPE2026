# Scientific review: three-marker substring-composition rigidity

## Correctness — PASS

The proof explicitly enumerates every factor with zero, two, or three ones for a four-gap representation, including zero-length gaps. Finite differences of zero-only factor counts recover all four unordered gap lengths. The extremal three-one factor polynomial yields the unordered endpoint pair through sum and product. The remaining two orientational possibilities differ by an explicitly factored nonzero polynomial unless they are already reversal-equivalent. Burnside counting then provides the closed formula. The known four-one example has a directly checked common full spectrum. `verify.py` independently validates the counting identities over 1,820 gap tuples, all reversal fibers for 17,550 weight-three words, 4,096 orientation cases, and the witness. These finite checks do not substitute for the universal proof. Risk: only intended word class, exact multiplicity-weighted observations, and linear rather than cyclic substrings are covered.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Acharya et al. (arXiv:1403.2439, especially Sections 2, 4–9 and the full result summary) give a general factorization characterization and the four-one length-eight counterexample. Their all-word uniqueness theorems apply only to special blocklengths and therefore do not imply weight-three uniqueness at every length. The all-word factorization criterion could in principle prove this theorem but is not itself a fixed-weight classification. Gupta–Mahdavifar (arXiv:2201.09955, Sections II–IV) use gap vectors for reconstruction codes and backtracking; the inspected formulation does not supply all-length three-marker rigidity or the selective-channel statement. Ye–Elishco (arXiv:2208.14963v2, Introduction and Sections II, III, VII–VIII) analyze prefix/suffix information, missing compositions and length-limited observations, not this separation by fragment weight. Indexed literature searches and published research-record comparisons found no exact or stronger result. Nearest accessible historical findings concern fixed blocklength composition spectra, two-letter local multispectra, and k-binomial deletion data—not universal weight-three full-substring-composition rigidity. Residual risk: unindexed or differently termed polynomial-factorization consequences may be equivalent. This is not a claim of exhaustive priority verification.

## Value — PASS

Full-spectrum reconstruction is a substantive inverse problem with published nontrivial ambiguity at length eight. Showing the first Hamming-weight transition from universal uniqueness to ambiguity, identifying exactly which three fragment-weight channels already suffice, and giving an explicit constructive proof for *all* word lengths constitute a motivated complete structural classification. The orbit count quantifies the exact number of unique observations in a natural sparse coding class. The four-one collision was already known and is used only to locate the sharp boundary. This result does not establish robustness to measurement loss within a retained channel.
