# Scientific review: three moments suffice for dyadic two-point Fourier geometry

## Correctness
PASS. The claimed domain is every \(N=2^k\) with \(k\ge2\), and the exact two-sum fiber is proved by separating odd and even first coordinates. Odd sums are controlled by the invertible coefficient \(3s\) and two simple Hensel roots; even sums reduce to square roots modulo \(N/2\). Explicit unit-square multiplicities, zero-square multiplicities, and a zero-product valuation census prove the entire histogram, exact image size, and energy \(kN^2\). The all-degree stabilization follows by Newton's recurrence and evenness, with an explicit degree-two counterexample. The Fourier moment uses normalization \(N^{-3}\), not a sharp arbitrary-weight extension norm. Exact checker replay passes through exponent ten; it is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS under bounded comparison. The principal direct source, Hickman's 2015 thesis, Chapter 4 §4.6, studies the moment curve but assumes prime \(p>n\); it emphasizes invertible Newton denominators and thus excludes the dyadic cubic case. Hickman–Wright (2018), §7, similarly treats restriction inequalities and general congruence counts for \(p>n\), rather than an exact two-sum dyadic fiber law. Mockenhaupt–Tao (2002) develops finite-field restriction for characteristic greater than two. The closest previously recorded local results concern a dyadic two-coordinate cubic graph and Fourier-zero sets for prime-square three-coordinate moment curves, neither of which yields the current all-exponent, complete two-sum classification. Searches considered the equivalent formulations as Vinogradov two-variable congruence systems, Newton power-sum fibers, Fourier fourth moments, and modular square-root distributions. Historical priority is not exhaustively settled: a general earlier local representation-density result may subsume the exact counts.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The object is the canonical three-dimensional moment curve, and the question asks exactly when finitely many moments determine all power sums of two inputs in the smallest problematic residue characteristic. The sharp cubic stabilization threshold and nonvanishing logarithmic collision growth give a structural boundary, while the complete fiber distribution and \(7/24\) limiting image density are natural global invariants of the associated Fourier surface. This is neither a single arbitrary modulus nor a relabeling of a known quadratic-congruence table. It does not claim unrestricted Fourier extension norm sharpness, higher-fold energy, or novel square-root lemmas.

## Closest literature and limitations
Hickman (2015), §4.6, is the closest same-object source but excludes \(p=2\). Hickman–Wright (2018), §7, gives the general moment-curve framework and analytic estimates under larger-prime hypotheses; its main statements do not imply the exact result. Mockenhaupt–Tao (2002) provides foundational field context. Existing quadratic-residue counts are standard ingredients; novelty is restricted to their global dyadic two-point assembly and sharp stabilization.
