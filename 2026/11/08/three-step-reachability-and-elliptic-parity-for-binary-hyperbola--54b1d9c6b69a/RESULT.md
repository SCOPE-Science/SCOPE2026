# Three-step reachability and elliptic parity for binary hyperbola walks

## Finding
Let \(q=2^m\), \(m\ge1\), and \(F=\mathbb F_q\). Write \(S=\{(x,x^{-1}):x\in F^\times\}\) and let \(R_3(a,b)\) count **ordered** triples from \(S\) summing to \((a,b)\). The complete three-step reachability classification follows from the following representation counts.

* At the origin, \(R_3(0,0)=2(q-1)\) when \(m\) is even and \(R_3(0,0)=0\) when \(m\) is odd.
* For each \(a\ne0\), \(R_3(a,0)=R_3(0,a)=q-4\) for even \(m\) and \(q-2\) for odd \(m\).
* For nonzero \(a,b\), put \(c=ab\). If \(c=1\), then \(R_3(a,b)=3q-5\). If \(c\ne1\), then
\[
R_3(a,b)=\#E_c(F)-6,\qquad
E_c:\quad w^2+w=\frac{u+1}{u(cu+c+1)},
\]
where \(E_c\) denotes the smooth projective model. For \(c\in F\setminus\{0,1\}\), it is a genus-one curve and satisfies
\[
R_3(a,b)\ge q-5-2\sqrt q>0 \quad(q\ge16).
\]

Consequently, for \(q=2\) the support is the single point \((1,1)\); for \(q=4\) it is the four-element additive graph of Frobenius; for \(q=8\) the support has size \(42\); for even \(m\ge4\) the support is all \(F^2\); and for odd \(m\ge5\) it is exactly \(F^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\). At \(q=8\), the nonzero-coordinate points occur exactly when \(ab=1\) or \(\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}(ab)=0\). The additive Cayley graph generated by \(S\) is disconnected for \(q=2,4\), but has **diameter exactly three for every \(q\ge8\)**.

## Assumptions and scope
All vector-space additions are componentwise in characteristic two. The step set is punctured: zero is not included. Representation counts include repeated steps, distinguish their order, and are defined for every field order \(q=2^m\). A three-step support assertion means *exactly* three steps, whereas graph diameter allows at most three. The elliptic-curve formula is asserted only for \(c\ne0,1\); it is not extrapolated to singular values.

## Proof
**Symmetry reduction.** Multiplication of the first coordinate by \(s\in F^\times\) and the second by \(s^{-1}\) preserves \(S\); swapping coordinates also preserves \(S\). Thus for nonzero \(a,b\), the count depends only on \(c=ab\). Write an ordered triple as
\[
(x,x^{-1})+(y,y^{-1})+(z,z^{-1})=(1,c),
\]
where \(x,y,z\ne0\), so \(z=1+x+y\). Put \(u=x+y\) and \(v=xy\). The second-coordinate equation, after multiplying by \(xyz\), is
\[
u(u+1)+v=c\,v(u+1), \qquad
v(cu+c+1)=u(u+1).
\]

**The exceptional product \(c=1\).** This last relation becomes \(u(u+1+v)=0\). If \(u=0\), then \(x=y\ne0\) and \(z=1\), giving \(q-1\) ordered triples. If \(u\ne0\), then \(v=u+1\), and \(u=1\) is prohibited by \(z=0\). For every other \(u\), the quadratic \(T^2+uT+u+1\) splits into two distinct roots since
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}\!\left(\frac{u+1}{u^2}\right)
=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}(u^{-1}+u^{-2})=0.
\]
It has nonzero roots, producing \(2(q-2)\) triples. Hence \(R_3(a,b)=3q-5\) for \(ab=1\).

**The generic product and the genus-one curve.** Assume \(c\ne0,1\), and put \(u_0=(c+1)/c\). The three values \(0,u_0,1\) are distinct. At \(u=0\) the algebraic equation forces \(v=0\); at \(u=1\), \(z=0\); and at \(u=u_0\), the equation is inconsistent. All three values must therefore be discarded. At every other \(u\), the two nonzero roots of \(T^2+uT+v\), with \(v=u(u+1)/(cu+c+1)\), lie in \(F\) precisely when
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}
\left(\frac{u+1}{u(cu+c+1)}\right)=0.
\]
There are exactly two ordered roots in this case. The Artin–Schreier curve \(w^2+w=(u+1)/(u(cu+c+1))\) has two distinct rational simple poles at \(u=0\) and \(u=u_0\); each contributes one rational point to its smooth projective model. At \(u=\infty\), the function vanishes, giving two rational points; and at the excluded \(u=1\) it vanishes, giving two more. Therefore \(\#E_c(F)=R_3(1,c)+6\). The two simple poles make the Artin–Schreier extension nontrivial of genus one by the ramification/genus formula. In particular its smooth model has rational points, and the Hasse bound gives
\[
\bigl|\#E_c(F)-(q+1)\bigr|\le2\sqrt q.
\]
For every \(q\ge16\), \(q-5-2\sqrt q>0\), establishing positivity at every generic nonzero-coordinate target.

**Axes and origin.** If the sum is \((1,0)\), the equation becomes \(v=u(u+1)\), with \(u\notin\{0,1\}\). The quadratic splits exactly when \(\operatorname{Tr}(1+u^{-1})=0\). As \(u^{-1}\) runs over \(F^\times\setminus\{1\}\), this produces \(q-4\) triples when \(m\) is even and \(q-2\) triples when \(m\) is odd. Inverting all entries exchanges the two axes. At \((0,0)\), \(z=x+y\ne0\) and the reciprocal condition reduces to \(x^2+xy+y^2=0\). After dividing by \(y^2\), its two roots are the primitive cube roots of unity. They exist in \(F\) exactly when \(3\mid(q-1)\), equivalently when \(m\) is even, and give \(2(q-1)\) ordered triples.

**Small orders and diameter.** For \(q=2,4\), inversion on \(F^\times\) agrees with the additive Frobenius map, so \(S\) lies in a proper additive graph and the Cayley graph is disconnected; the exact three-step supports have sizes \(1\) and \(4\). In the field \(F_8=\mathbb F_2[\alpha]/(\alpha^3+\alpha+1)\), exact enumeration of the six generic nonzero products gives \(R_3(1,c)=6\) at the three products of trace zero, and zero at the three products of trace one distinct from \(1\). With the axes and product-one orbit, the resulting support size is \(14+7+21=42\). All generic products are reachable in three steps for \(q\ge16\) by the genus-one argument. The axes are reachable in three steps for \(q\ge8\), and the only remaining possible obstruction is the origin when \(m\) is odd; it is already reachable in two steps by repeating any single increment. In \(F_8\), the 22 targets absent from the three-step support occur in the one- or two-step support (an exhaustive field calculation verifies this). Thus every target is reachable within three steps for every \(q\ge8\). Two steps never suffice for all targets: the two-step support is
\[
\{(0,0)\}\ \cup\
\{(a,b): a,b\ne0,\ \operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}((ab)^{-1})=0\},
\]
whose size is \(1+(q-1)(q/2-1)<q^2\). Hence the graph diameter is exactly three.

## Verification
The companion script `check_binary_hyperbola_3step.py` constructs the fields of orders \(2,4,8,16,32\) with explicit irreducible binary polynomials, exhaustively enumerates every ordered threefold sum, verifies the axis/product-one/central formulas, compares generic counts with Artin–Schreier trace counts, and checks the Hasse inequality and all support cases. It ran successfully with the terminal marker `BINARY_HYPERBOLA_THREE_STEP_EXACT_CHECKS_PASS`. The infinite-family assertions follow from the preceding proof, not from finite samples.

## Relationship to prior work
Cilleruelo's finite-field Sidon-set work motivates the algebraic hyperbola as a structured additive subset. Forey, Fresán, Kowalski and Wigderson treat **the same hyperbola-derived Cayley sum graph**, proving pseudorandom spectral behavior and the absence of a \(K_{2,3}\) subgraph; their Section 3 gives Kloosterman eigenvalues, including characteristic two. Those spectral and local properties are acknowledged as prior results, not presented here as new. The present theorem instead establishes exact three-step target-by-target reachability and the sharp diameter classification, including the exceptional orders \(2,4,8\), using genus-one point counts. The separate odd-characteristic Euclidean association-scheme treatment of Brittenham and Pakianathan also gives Kloosterman spectral formulas for different walks and does not imply the binary three-step support theorem. General hyperbola spectral estimates do not alone establish the required three-step pointwise positivity in the full stated range.

## Limitations
The result concerns exactly three ordered increments and the diameter of this specific additive Cayley graph; no sharp mixing-time or optimal Fourier-extension constant is claimed. The elliptic-curve proof relies on the classical Hasse bound. The \(q=8\) residue classification is a finite exhaustive computation in a specified field basis. The literature comparison does not rule out an earlier unindexed exact diameter or three-step formula; independent validation has not been performed.
