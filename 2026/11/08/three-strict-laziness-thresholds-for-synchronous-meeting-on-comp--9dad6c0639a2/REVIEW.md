# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The independent-walker product transition law induces exactly the three nonmeeting pair classes and the displayed transient matrix. Positivity of every class's bounded-time absorption probability makes the inverse well-defined with positive determinant. Exact symbolic differences yield the stated quadratic sign tests; opposite endpoint signs prove each root is unique. The signed Sylvester resultant factors into strictly positive terms apart from its leading minus sign, with an independently checked 36-coefficient all-positive shifted expansion. Continuity and the exact two-by-two base witness separate the roots. The full unordered-pair chain was separately solved at six graph sizes and three rational holding rates (267 exact equality checks); computation corroborates rather than replaces the universal algebraic argument.

## Originality (PASS, with historical-priority risk)
The 2016 Kanade–Mallmann-Trenn–Sauerwald and 2018 Oliveira–Peres results yield general meeting/coalescence bounds, not complete split graph holding-parameter phase diagrams. The 2021 Alpern–Zeng fundamental-matrix method covers arbitrary finite graphs conceptually but its explicit solved networks are lines, cycles and grids. The 2023 Eguchi–Ooshita–Inoue–Tixeuil model moves one walker per round under an adversarial non-atomic scheduler, and is not this synchronous process. Relevant prior mathematical research claims, including prior complete bipartite synchronous-walker phase diagrams, were checked for statement implication; the latter graph family does not contain the clique-edge transitions and does not imply the current three-crossing classification. Searches found no direct all-parameter exact split-graph classification. This is qualified evidence, not proof of absence from all literature. A specialized older study could remain undiscovered.

## Value (PASS)
An absorbing matrix algorithm alone leaves unclear whether the three pair-class meeting expectations swap order repeatedly, simultaneously, or at all. The exact theorem proves a genuinely ordered three-crossing/four-regime phase portrait uniformly for every size pair, with strict and uniquely defined boundaries. It identifies the switch in which initial pair type has shortest and longest expected meeting time as synchrony/laziness varies, in a natural nonregular, nonbipartite graph family. This is a structural classification, not a finite numerical example or mere renaming of the matrix inversion.

## Limits
The result concerns meeting means only, assumes two independent synchronized agents with a common positive holding probability and distinct start vertices, and does not establish a rate optimality problem or higher meeting-time moments. Historical priority and external independent mathematical verification remain open.
