# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The independent-walker product transition law reduces the three distinct-pair classes to a transient matrix. Direct Cramer differences give the displayed quadratics with endpoint sign changes. Their exact resultant has 36 strictly positive coefficients after shifting a,b by 2, so its nonvanishing and the base sign witness order the three roots. Exact small-graph full-pair comparisons corroborate the universal proof.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)
The generic absorbing-chain computation is prior work, particularly Alpern and Zeng (2022). Their explicit meeting examples are cycles. General meeting/coalescence bounds of Kanade, Mallmann-Trenn and Sauerwald and of Oliveira and Peres do not imply exact three-orbit ordering. Eguchi and collaborators study a non-atomic adversarial model. The earlier complete-bipartite two-threshold theorem lacks clique edges and does not imply the complete split-graph three-crossing phase diagram. Specialized unindexed prior coverage remains a genuine risk, including possible overlapping portions of a 2023 thesis not fully inspected.

## Value (PASS)
For every clique size a>=2 and independent-set size b>=2, the theorem gives the precise order changes among three geometrically natural pair-start meeting means, with strictly ordered exact transition points. The all-parameter phase portrait is not equivalent to simply implementing a fundamental matrix at one graph size.

## Limits
The result concerns the expectation of first endpoint coincidence for only two synchronously and independently moving agents with homogeneous holding p in (0,1), not full distributions or different movement protocols. A subsequent independent scientific assessment is recorded separately; no external certification is claimed.
