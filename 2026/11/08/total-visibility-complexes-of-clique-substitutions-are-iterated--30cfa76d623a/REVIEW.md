# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact all-set equivalence is proved by projecting geodesics between distinct fibers and lifting an eligible base geodesic using unselected vertices in every intermediate fiber. Pairs inside or across adjacent cliques have no internal path vertices. This yields a literal equality with the standard composition complex, not merely a numerical resemblance. The cited 2013 composition homotopy theorem then gives the integral join equivalence and shifted homology, including torsion; all-set/facet polynomials and minimal nonfaces follow by counting full and nonfull fibers. On cyclic bases, the distance-two obstructions are exhaustive and produce explicit joins of simplex boundaries. Exhaustive small cases and independent shortest-path BFS agree with the statements. This verifies the assumptions \(h\ge2\), connected base, \(m_i\ge2\), full-fiber convention, and the augmented empty-face case. The finite tests are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification

The closest published work is Kuziak–Rodríguez-Velázquez (2023), whose Theorem 4.4 determines the maximum size in lexicographic products but does not state the all-configuration composition or topology. Bujtás–Klavžar–Tian (2025) establish the general distance-two criterion and introduce total-visibility polynomials. Ayzenberg (2013) proves the topology of simplicial compositions. The abstract homotopy theorem and general graph criterion are acknowledged as established results; the assessed new contribution is their exact identification for graph clique substitutions, including the all-face and facet enumerators and the cyclic PL-sphere formulas. Afkhami–Klavžar–Shahmiri–Tavakoli (2026), Section 3, prove a scalar generalized-lexicographic formula **for edgeless replacement graphs**, not the all-set topology of clique replacements. Brešar–Yero (2023) studies smallest inclusion-maximal sizes, principally for other families, and does not state this substitution theorem. Searches under clique substitution, lexicographic product, true-twin expansion, full-fiber obstruction, and simplicial wedge revealed no directly covering statement. An unindexed formulation or an interpretation as a short consequence of the combined existing theorems is a residual publication-priority risk; a failed search is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result transfers an entire graph-geodesic set system through arbitrary unequal clique substitutions, rather than only transferring the extremal total-visibility number. It determines every coefficient, all inclusion-maximal sets and minimal forbidden sets, and gives an integral topological shift of the complete complex, including any existing torsion. This identifies a natural bridge between graph metric visibility and simplicial-composition topology. The cycle corollary supplies explicit sphere triangulations and their exact facet multiplicities for all allowed fiber-size vectors, with a distinct four-cycle case. The significance is the structural transfer and topology, not the routine extraction of a scalar maximum from the formula.

## Closest sources and limitations

- Kuziak and Rodríguez-Velázquez, arXiv:2306.15818, DOI:10.1007/s40840-023-01590-3, Section 4 and Theorem 4.4: scalar lexicographic-product result, no complete face/complex equivalence stated.
- Bujtás, Klavžar and Tian, DOI:10.1007/s00010-025-01197-y, Definition 3.1 and Theorem 5.2: general visibility polynomial and distance-two criterion; necessary ingredient, not an explicit substitution classification.
- Afkhami, Klavžar, Shahmiri and Tavakoli, DOI:10.7151/dmgt.2646, Section 3, Theorem 3.1: generalised lexicographic replacement by edgeless graphs, scalar total clique-visibility; not clique substitution or its complex.
- Ayzenberg, arXiv:1301.4459, Proposition 6.1 and Corollary 6.2: abstract polyhedral-join homotopy theorem, which is used rather than re-proved.
- Brešar and Yero, arXiv:2307.02951, Introduction and concluding remarks: lower visibility invariants, no clique-substitution complex classification found.

The result is limited to clique fibers and is not a claim about arbitrary lexicographic replacement graphs. The claimed novelty may be affected by a prior equivalent statement phrased using squarefree monomial substitutions or polyhedral joins. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
