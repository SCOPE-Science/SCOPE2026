# Translation-fixed topology of radius-one Hamming Čech nerves

## Finding
Let \(d\ge2\) and let \(q_1,\ldots,q_d\ge2\) be arbitrary integers. Write
\[
T=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb Z/q_i\mathbb Z,
\qquad
G=K_{q_1}\mathbin\square\cdots\mathbin\square K_{q_d}.
\]
Identify the vertices of \(G\) with \(T\). Let \(X\) be the **intrinsic graph Čech nerve at scale two**: a nonempty vertex set is a simplex if and only if it is contained in a closed radius-one graph neighborhood
\[
B(c)=\{c\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{d}\{c+a e_i:0\ne a\in\mathbb Z/q_i\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Translation by \(T\) acts simplicially on \(X\). For every **nontrivial** subgroup \(H\le T\), its ordinary geometric fixed-point space \(|X|^H\) has exactly one of the following forms.

**Coordinate case.** If \(H\) is contained in the \(i\)-th coordinate factor \(T_i\cong\mathbb Z/q_i\mathbb Z\), then \(|X|^H\) is *homeomorphic* to a disjoint union of \(|T|/q_i\) simplices of dimension \(q_i/|H|-1\). Moreover, as a \(T/H\)-space, it deformation-retracts equivariantly to the discrete \(T\)-set \(T/T_i\) (on which \(H\) acts trivially).

**Exceptional two-coordinate case.** If \(H=\{0,h\}\) and \(h\) has precisely two nonzero coordinates (necessarily both of additive order two), then \(|X|^H\) consists of exactly \(|T|/2\) isolated points, forming the regular \(T/H\)-set.

**All other cases.** The fixed-point space is empty.

These cases are mutually exclusive. In particular, in the coordinate case
\[
\widetilde H_0(|X|^H;\mathbb Z)
\cong\ker\bigl(\mathbb Z[T/T_i]\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb Z\bigr)
\]
as a \(T/H\)-module, and in the exceptional case the same formula holds with \(T/H\) in place of \(T/T_i\). All positive-degree homology vanishes in every nonempty nontrivial-subgroup fixed-point space. Here \(\varepsilon\) denotes augmentation (sum of coefficients).

## Assumptions and scope
All groups are finite, written additively, and the factors \(T_i\) are embedded as their corresponding coordinates. The product graph is the Cartesian product of complete graphs, so its graph metric is Hamming distance, and its closed radius-one vertex neighborhood is exactly \(B(c)\). The Čech convention is intrinsic, with closed balls of radius one in the unit-edge geometric realization of \(G\), corresponding to scale two. The theorem is for ordinary fixed points of the **geometric realization** under a subgroup of translations, not merely the induced subcomplex on individually fixed vertices. The action of a nontrivial translation on graph vertices is free, even though its action on \(|X|\) can have fixed points. The trivial subgroup is excluded: its fixed space is simply \(|X|\), whose nonequivariant topology has been studied separately. No equality of alphabet sizes, primality, or uniform coordinate subgroup orders is assumed.

## Proof
**1. The nerve is generated by graph balls.** If the closed radius-one balls centered at three or more distinct graph vertices meet at an interior point of an edge, that interior point must be at distance at most one from three centers. In a unit-edge graph, only the two endpoints of that edge can have distance at most one from an interior point; thus this is impossible. If exactly two balls meet in an edge interior, their endpoints are the two centers and their balls already meet at a graph vertex. Consequently every nonempty intersection of vertex-centered closed balls contains a graph vertex, and the Čech nerve equals the downward closure of the finite sets \(B(c)\).

**2. Fixed-point orbit complex.** Let \(H\ne0\). Every vertex orbit is a free coset \(x+H\). On the relative interior of a simplex, barycentric coordinates are unique. A point fixed by all elements of \(H\) has coordinates constant on every orbit, and all orbits with positive coefficients are fully contained in the supporting simplex. It follows that \(|X|^H\) is canonically homeomorphic to the realization of the orbit complex \(Y_H\): its vertices are cosets \(x+H\) that lie inside some \(B(c)\), and collections of such vertices are faces exactly when their **union** lies in one \(B(c)\). The vertex of \(Y_H\) corresponding to a coset maps to the equal-weight barycenter of that coset. This identification is equivariant for the residual translation action of \(T/H\).

**3. Affine-coset lemma.** Put
\[
S=\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^d\bigl(T_i\setminus\{0\}\bigr),
\]
so \(B(c)=c+S\). Suppose an affine coset \(W=x+H\) is contained in \(S\). If \(W\) meets nonzero elements of two different coordinate axes, choose \(u=a e_i\) and \(v=b e_j\) in \(W\), with \(i\ne j\) and \(a,b\ne0\). Then \(h=v-u\in H\) and both \(u+2h=-u+2v\) and \(v-2h=2u-v\) belong to \(W\subseteq S\). The first has nonzero \(i\)-coordinate and hence forces \(2b=0\); the second has nonzero \(j\)-coordinate and hence forces \(2a=0\). Therefore \(h\) has order two. The coset cannot contain a third point \(w\): if \(w=0\), closure under adding differences forces \(u+v\in W\), which has two nonzero coordinates; if \(w\) lies on axis \(i\) (respectively \(j\)), then \(w+h\) has two nonzero coordinates unless \(w=u\) (respectively \(w=v\)); and if \(w\) lies on another axis, \(w+h\) has three nonzero coordinates. Thus \(W=\{u,v\}\) and \(H=\{0,h\}\) with \(h\) supported on exactly two coordinates.

If \(W\) meets no two nonzero coordinate axes, it lies in one axis (possibly including zero), so \(H=W-W\) lies in that coordinate factor. Translating back from \(S\), we obtain the *necessary* dichotomy: an orbit contained in any ball forces \(H\le T_i\) for some \(i\), or \(H\) is the specified two-coordinate order-two group. Conversely, both types do have orbits in graph balls, as shown next.

**4. Coordinate case and equivariance.** If \(H\le T_i\), every coset lies in the unique coordinate line obtained by fixing all coordinates except \(i\). Each such line has \(q_i/|H|\) cosets, and their union is exactly that line, which is contained in \(B(c)\) for any \(c\) on it. No ball contains a complete nontrivial \(H\)-coset from two distinct \(i\)-lines: a ball centered outside a particular \(i\)-line intersects that line in at most one point, whereas each orbit has \(|H|\ge2\) points. Hence \(Y_H\) is the disjoint union, over \(|T|/q_i\) coordinate lines, of full simplices on their \(q_i/|H|\) orbit vertices. The residual group \(T/H\) permutes these simplices transitively, with stabilizer \(T_i/H\); their barycenters supply an equivariant strong deformation retraction to the orbit set \(T/T_i\).

**5. Order-two, two-coordinate case.** For \(H=\{0,h\}\) with \(h=a e_i+b e_j\) and \(2a=2b=0\ne a,b\), each coset \(\{x,x+h\}\) fits in \(B(x+a e_i)\): its endpoints are two distinct leaves on axes \(i\) and \(j\) of that ball. For a fixed center \(c\), there is *exactly one* such complete orbit in \(B(c)\), namely \(\{c+a e_i,c+b e_j\}\). Therefore \(Y_H\) has every \(H\)-coset as a vertex and no edge; it has \(|T|/2\) vertices. Residual translations act freely and transitively on them.

**6. Homology.** The remaining groups give no orbit-complex vertices by step 3. A disjoint union of contractible simplices has the homotopy type of its component set, and the equivariant retraction in step 4 identifies the integral reduced zeroth homology with the augmentation submodule of the indicated permutation module. Step 5 gives the regular-set case. No infinite extrapolation from the computations is used.

## Verification
The self-contained Python 3 program `verify_general.py` enumerates *all* additive subgroups, *all* translation cosets, and *all* graph balls for fourteen mixed and equal-size Cartesian products, including composite alphabet sizes. It builds the orbit nerve independently and checks every component, its expected size, and whether the component is a complete simplex, covering **200 nontrivial subgroups**. The recorded output is `verify_general_output.txt`, ending `ALL_VERIFY_OK nontrivial_subgroups=200`. A separate verifier `verify_prime.py` checks 500 nontrivial vector-subspace actions for binary, ternary, and five-symbol Hamming products. Run `python3 verify_general.py` and `python3 verify_prime.py` from the extracted package directory. The computations check finite examples only; the affine-coset lemma proves the general result.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh determine the nonequivariant sphere-wedge homotopy type for binary Hamming graph Čech complexes at scale two. A more recent mathematical result determines the complete rational homology character for binary hypercube graph automorphisms; its explicit formula entails Euler characteristics of fixed sets for individual elements, but does **not** determine the fixed-point spaces themselves or their residual integral component-permutation modules. Another recent result computes nonequivariant sphere-wedge homotopy for equal-alphabet multinary Hamming products. The present classification treats **every subgroup of the translation group**, **unequal and composite alphabets**, actual fixed-point *homeomorphism types*, and equivariant integral reduced \(H_0\). It is not a restatement of a nonequivariant sphere rank, rational character, or fixed-vertex calculation. Biswas's 2026 work on finite-set Čech complexes under the symmetric-difference metric is a recent motivating context; its full preprint was not accessible for a complete direct comparison. The supporting results and their exact domains are given below. An unindexed equivalent equivariant statement remains possible.

## Limitations
No classification is claimed for subgroups involving permutations of coordinates or symbols, for Čech scales other than two, or for the full equivariant homotopy type under the entire graph automorphism group. The theorem concerns only ordinary fixed-point spaces of the translation subgroup and their residual translation action. The primary recent motivating preprint was available only through its abstract; a full-text comparison was not possible. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
