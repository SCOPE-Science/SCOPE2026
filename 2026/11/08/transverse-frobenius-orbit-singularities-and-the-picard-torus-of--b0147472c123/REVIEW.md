# Review: transverse Frobenius-orbit singularities of higher Ballico–Hefez curves

## Correctness — PASS

The source normalization theorem states that geometric fibers of \(\Phi_n\) at the singularities consist exactly of Frobenius orbits of length \(d\mid n\). Its immersion assertion alone does not prove ordinary crossings. The new argument differentiates the monic coefficient polynomial: the \(d\) branches over one image have tangent polynomials \(-G^{n/d-1}G/(Z-a_j)\). Cancelling their shared factor and evaluating at each root of the separable degree-\(d\) polynomial \(G\) proves their independence, in every characteristic. Formally straightening independent smooth branches yields coordinate axes and \(\delta=d-1\); standard normalization exact sequences then give the stated Picard torus. The closed-point formula is a direct consequence of the source's old fiber theorem. Both exact finite-field tangent-rank enumeration and an independently programmed homogeneous Hilbert-function check support this reconstruction. The finite checks are corroborative only.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest antecedent is Fukasawa–Homma–Kim, arXiv:1108.4227v1, Theorems 6.4 and 6.6: it already gives the defining maps, immersion, inverse-image Frobenius orbits, and rational-point formula. Its Section 2 also computes the plane \(n=2\) zeta function. Hoang–Shimada, arXiv:1402.3372v1, studies the plane \(n=2\) curve, including its nodes and automorphism action. The generalized Jacobian of the plane \(n=2\) nodal curve is already accessible from its classical nodal structure; this is prior mathematics. None of those statements asserts or proves independence of all \(d\ge3\) tangents for the higher-dimensional \(B_n\), which is the necessary local-geometric premise of the precise Picard-group decomposition. In particular the extension-point and zeta formulas follow from the original normalization classification and are **not** counted as independent novelty. Targeted literature and mathematical-record queries revealed no directly covering all-\(n\) local/Picard theorem, subject to undetected specialist publications.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Fukasawa–Homma–Kim higher-dimensional family is a naturally defined infinite family of Frobenius nonclassical, rational, singular projective curves. Determining whether multi-branch images are tangent, cuspidal or genuinely transverse is structurally substantive: the answer controls their conductor, arithmetic genus, and whether the connected Picard scheme is toric or admits unipotent factors. The result gives a uniform, exact, characteristic-independent classification and an explicit arithmetic group structure for every \(n\ge3\), rather than an isolated numerical invariant or routine subspecialization.

## Risks and limits

The 2011 article supplies most global normalization data, so the truly additional proof step is the tangent-independence lemma and its local/Picard consequences. Standard generalized-Jacobian gluing machinery then applies; these general tools are not new. Complete specialist thesis coverage has not been established; independent review could find equivalent local descriptions. The programs inspect only a finite selection of finite fields and degrees.
