# Transverse Segre cones and the full singular locus of three-class rank-two toric positroid varieties

## Finding
Let \(A,B,C\) be three nonempty cyclic intervals partitioning a finite cyclically ordered label set, of respective cardinalities \(a,b,c\ge1\). Consider the rank-two positroid \(M\) whose bases are exactly the pairs with elements in different classes, and the corresponding complex positroid variety \(X=\Pi_M\subset\operatorname{Gr}(2,a+b+c)\). These are rank-two regular positroids, so their positroid varieties are toric. Define the closed support stratum \(S_{AB}\subset X\) by requiring that every Plücker coordinate vanish except those on \(A\)-\(B\) pairs; define \(S_{BC}\) and \(S_{CA}\) similarly.

Each support stratum is the Segre variety \(S_{AB}\cong\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}\) (and cyclic analogues). The three are pairwise disjoint. The **entire reduced singular locus** is
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X)_{\mathrm{red}}=
\bigl(S_{AB}\text{ if }c\ge2\bigr)\ \sqcup\
\bigl(S_{BC}\text{ if }a\ge2\bigr)\ \sqcup\
\bigl(S_{CA}\text{ if }b\ge2\bigr).
\]
Here a parenthesized stratum is omitted if its displayed condition fails.

At *every* point of \(S_{AB}\), the completed local ring of \(X\) is isomorphic to
\[
\mathbb C[[t_1,\ldots,t_{a+b-2},u_1,\ldots,u_c,v_1,\ldots,v_c]]
\big/\bigl(u_iv_j-u_jv_i:1\le i<j\le c\bigr).
\]
The same holds cyclically with the complementary class size. In particular, for \(c\ge2\), the transverse germ is the affine cone over \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{c-1}\) in its Segre embedding, the Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is **exactly \(c\)**, and \(S_{AB}\) has codimension \(c+1\) in \(X\). For a rank-two positroid with only two nonempty parallel classes, its variety is \(\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}\), hence smooth. As a consequence the three-class variety is smooth precisely when all three class sizes equal one; this latter smoothness criterion alone is not claimed as a new discovery.

## Assumptions and scope
All algebraic varieties and local rings are over \(\mathbb C\). The three classes are nonempty and are **cyclic intervals**, ensuring that their matroid is a positroid; regularity follows from the fact that the rank-two matroid has at most three parallel classes. The Plücker coordinate for a pair \(i,j\) is denoted \(p_{ij}\). The defining ideal of a positroid variety in the Grassmannian is generated by the Plücker coordinates indexed by nonbases (the Knutson--Lam--Speyer theorem). The local calculation below also independently verifies reducedness of the affine charts. In the three-class case the complex dimension is \(a+b+c-1\). A rank-two regular positroid with loops is not covered by the statement; deleting loops is a separate trivial coordinate reduction, not included here.

## Proof
**1. Scheme-theoretic affine charts.** Let \(i\in A\), \(j\in B\) and take the standard Grassmannian open chart \(p_{ij}\ne0\). Unique row operations put the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th columns of a representing \(2\times(a+b+c)\) matrix into the form \((1,0)^T\), \((0,1)^T\). Plücker nonbases are precisely same-class pairs. Their vanishing forces every other \(A\)-column to have the form \((x_s,0)^T\), and every other \(B\)-column to have the form \((0,y_t)^T\). Write the \(C\)-columns as \((u_k,v_k)^T\). The only remaining same-class nonbasis equations are
\[
u_iv_j-u_jv_i=0\qquad(1\le i<j\le c).
\]
No cross-class Plücker coordinate is required to be nonzero on the closed variety. Thus the **whole** open scheme \(X\cap\{p_{ij}\ne0\}\), not just its dense matroid stratum, is canonically an affine product
\[
\mathbb A^{a+b-2}\times D_c,
\qquad
D_c=\{U\in\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times c}(\mathbb C):\operatorname{rank}U\le1\}.
\]
The determinantal ideal of all \(2\times2\) minors is prime: \(D_c\) is the affine cone over the Segre image of \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{c-1}\). Hence these equations define the intended reduced closure, and the chart identity holds scheme-theoretically. All points of \(X\) possess some nonzero cross-class Plücker coordinate, so the three pair types cover \(X\).

**2. Singular support.** When \(c=1\), \(D_c=\mathbb A^2\) is smooth. For \(c\ge2\), every nonzero rank-one matrix is a smooth point: after inverting a nonzero matrix entry, the rank-one relations solve for \(c-1\) other entries and give an affine smooth chart of dimension \(c+1\). At the zero matrix all defining quadrics have zero first derivatives; the tangent-space dimension is \(2c\), strictly greater than \(\dim D_c=c+1\). Hence \(\operatorname{Sing}(D_c)_{\mathrm{red}}=\{0\}\) for \(c\ge2\). In the chart based on the \(A\)-\(B\) cross pair, the condition \(U=0\) is exactly that all \(A\)-\(C\) and \(B\)-\(C\) Plücker coordinates vanish, i.e. membership in \(S_{AB}\). The same reasoning in the other two types of cross-pair charts yields the asserted global singular locus. The support strata cannot meet because a Grassmannian point must have at least one nonzero Plücker coordinate, while the permitted sets of nonzero coordinates for distinct strata are disjoint.

**3. Transverse analytic normal form and multiplicity.** The chart identity remains true at every point of \(S_{AB}\). Translating its \(a+b-2\) free coordinates gives the stated completed local ring. The homogeneous degree-\(d\) piece of the standard graded cone coordinate ring of \(D_c\) has dimension
\[
(d+1)\binom{d+c-1}{c-1},
\]
because the Segre embedding has sections of \(\mathcal O(d,d)\). Its Hilbert series is therefore
\[
H_{D_c}(t)=\frac{1+(c-1)t}{(1-t)^{c+1}}.
\]
For the local ring at its vertex, the Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is the numerator evaluated at \(t=1\), namely \(c\). Adding a smooth affine factor does not change that multiplicity. Since \(\dim S_{AB}=a+b-2\) and \(\dim X=a+b+c-1\), its codimension is \(c+1\). Cyclic permutation proves all statements. In the two-class case, the defining cross-Plücker matrix has rank one and identifies the entire variety with its Segre product, which is smooth.

## Verification
A standalone exact-arithmetic checker, `check_charts.py`, recreates normalized column charts for all \(a\in\{1,2,3\}\), \(b\in\{1,2\}\), \(c\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}\). It symbolically verifies same-class minor relations, checks the Jacobian ranks of the determinantal equations at a rank-one point and at the vertex, and confirms the coefficient formula of the Hilbert series for ten degrees. The expected output ends with `CHECK_OK`. These finite checks are consistency tests, **not** a proof of the quantified theorem; the chart and Segre-Hilbert arguments above establish the all-parameter assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
McNeill, *There are no unexpected toric positroid varieties* (first publicly submitted 2026-10-07, arXiv:2610.35914), proves toricity exactly for regular positroids and cites the scheme-theoretic Plücker equation theorem; **that toricity classification is not new here**. A prior rank-two toric positroid parametrization/dimension enumeration also already identifies the two- and three-parallel-class classes, and is not claimed here. Billey--Weaver (arXiv:2204.09013) provides a general smoothness characterization of positroid varieties, and Fluegemann (arXiv:2407.21116) gives methods for smooth points; the isolated smoothness corollary is accordingly not the originality claim. Higashitani--Matsushita (arXiv:2011.07714) treats toric edge rings of complete multipartite graphs and records previously known Gorenstein criteria; **no Gorenstein classification is claimed here**. The precise new assertion is the complete support-stratified local determinantal normal form and its multiplicity on this entire family. It may be implied by an earlier general toric edge-ring localization theorem not identified in the checked literature; priority remains qualified.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the reduced singular locus and completed local rings over characteristic zero, not the potentially nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme, resolutions, canonical divisors, or arbitrary rank and nonregular positroids. It uses the nonbases presentation of a positroid variety. Historical priority is provisional and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
