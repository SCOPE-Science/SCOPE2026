# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For every three nonempty cyclic-interval parallel classes, the standard nonzero-cross-Plücker charts cover the variety. The nonbase equations yield exactly a smooth affine factor times the rank-one \(2\times c\) determinantal cone. The ideal of minors is prime, and its local Jacobian computation proves singularity only at its vertex for \(c\ge2\). The three support Segre varieties are disjoint by Plücker support, so the charts exhaust all singular points. The Hilbert function of the Segre cone gives multiplicity \(c\) at every point of the corresponding stratum; the dimension computation gives codimension \(c+1\). The two-class case is a smooth Segre product. A 30-parameter finite checker corroborates but does not substitute for the all-parameter proof. The nonreduced singular scheme is intentionally not classified.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
The new content is an explicit **whole-family** singular-support stratification and local analytic-type/multiplicity computation, not toricity, the rank-two parallel-class classification, or the global smoothness criterion. McNeill (arXiv:2610.35914) proves regularity characterizes toric positroid varieties; the earlier rank-two class-enumeration work covers their three-class structure; Billey--Weaver (arXiv:2204.09013) characterizes smooth positroid varieties; Fluegemann (arXiv:2407.21116) studies smooth points. None of the inspected passages gives the full local rank-one-matrix product normal forms with complement-size multiplicities. Higashitani--Matsushita (arXiv:2011.07714) concerns edge rings of complete multipartite graphs and previously known Gorenstein criteria, not this support-local type. The local forms are elementary once appropriate Grassmann charts are written, so an earlier general localization result may imply them; exhaustive priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The 2026 toricity characterization leaves a natural local-geometric question: where precisely do these toric positroid varieties fail to be smooth, and what is the transverse singularity along each natural boundary orbit? The theorem answers that question uniformly for all three-block sizes, including exact nonconstant multiplicities and the topology of disjoint positive-dimensional Segre strata. It separates global smoothness criteria from the finer local analytic and multiplicity information needed for later resolution, intersection-theoretic or singularity calculations. The claim is a motivated family-wide classification, not a restatement of a known Gorenstein or smoothness test.
