# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS

The proof gives necessary and sufficient conditions for *every* feasible subset, with no assumption of maximum cardinality. The unique tree geodesic excludes all interior tree-layer vertices. Convexity of each leaf layer forces the corresponding \(H\)-selection to be feasible, and a shortest-path detour through a nonleaf tree layer constructs a visible geodesic for every pair having different tree coordinates. The rook prism has exactly two middle vertices for each distance-two pair; this yields the complete two-row-plus-vertical-edge complex. Two simplex collapses and an edge/vertex count give the integral circle-wedge base, and the join identity gives the final sphere wedge. Five independent shortest-path enumerations (\(12800\) leaf-boundary masks) and three mod-two homology computations passed. They are finite checks, not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality: PASS (provisional publication priority)

The closest primary result is Tian–Klavžar, arXiv:2212.07193, Theorem 4.5, which already proves the product formula for the **maximum number** of selected vertices and already identifies leaf-layer support. Proposition 4.3 handles the maximum for a product of complete graphs. The entire face-system identity, independence of nonmaximal layer assignments, facet polynomial and integral sphere-wedge homotopy formula are stronger statements and not supplied by that scalar theorem. The 2025 Bujtás–Klavžar–Tian general distance-two characterization is stronger in the sense of applicability to arbitrary graphs, but supplies a condition rather than the explicit all-parameter joins, enumerators, or integral homotopy. The Hamming-graph paper applies to products of complete graphs, not the nontrivial tree factors covered here. Other searched sources concern visibility variants, bounds, or different graph complexes. An undiscovered equivalent combinatorial factorization is a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value: PASS

The theorem identifies a universal exact factorization of a graph-visibility **complex**, not only its extremal size. The rook-prism specialization yields closed all-cardinality and inclusion-maximal counts and an infinite family of complexes with integral homology concentrated in the tunable dimension \(2|L(T)|-1\) and with independently tunable rank \((n-1)^{|L(T)|}\). The maximal-face spectrum and purity boundary show why scalar optimal values cannot recover this structure. This is a natural structural refinement of the published Cartesian-product problem rather than an arbitrary isolated parameter slice.
