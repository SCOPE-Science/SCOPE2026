## Correctness: PASS
The complete-intersection equations reduce on the two curve-covering charts to the integral cusp \(y^2+z^3=0\). The characteristic-three Jacobian equations are precisely \((y,z^3)\). Explicitly checking both remaining points in the coordinate hyperplane excludes hidden singular support. The overlap identity \(z'=a^{-4}z\) proves the *split* triple thickening rather than only generic multiplicity. Direct-sum cohomology computes \(P_J(m)=12m-9\), degree \(12\), and genus \(10\). The standalone checker reproduces the defining polynomial and overlap identities, but the geometric argument—not the finite computation—proves the theorem.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The Jacobian singular subscheme is a canonical, coordinate-independent invariant of a complete-intersection surface. In characteristic three the derivative of the transverse cubic term vanishes, leaving a length-three infinitesimal structure rather than the characteristic-zero generic length-two cusp Jacobian. The exact line-bundle twist of the nilpotent generator is needed to determine global degree, genus, and section spaces; none follows from the reduced singular locus alone. The example isolates a substantive wild-characteristic phenomenon in a classical rational-normal-curve construction.

## Scope and risks
The result is for the specified rational normal quartic in characteristic three. It is not a classification of higher-degree cases, nor an assertion about the full graded Jacobian-ideal Hilbert function. Historical priority and external validation remain open.
