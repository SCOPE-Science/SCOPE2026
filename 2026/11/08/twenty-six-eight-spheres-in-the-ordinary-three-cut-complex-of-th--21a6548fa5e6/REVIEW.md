# Scientific review of the Frucht-graph three-cut classification

**Correctness — PASS.** The twelve-vertex graph is defined by an explicit eighteen-edge list. Every one of its 4096 vertex subsets is tested in the standalone verifier. The Alexander dual has cardinality profile \((1,12,66,30,1)\), then admits exactly thirty checked elementary free-face collapses to a connected graph with twelve vertices and thirty-seven edges. This proves its integral homotopy type is \(\bigvee^{26}S^1\). Combinatorial Alexander duality gives unique nonzero reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z^{26}\) in degree eight. The complete six-skeleton, Hurewicz and Whitehead establish the stated wedge homotopy equivalence. Independently computed mod-two boundaries agree. All conclusions are for the one precisely specified Frucht graph; no finite-experiment extrapolation is used.

**Originality — PASS against inspected evidence.** The foundational cut-complex paper (arXiv:2304.13675) and its sequel (arXiv:2407.08158) were inspected for definitions, scope and possibly encompassing theorems. Neither states an exact three-cut classification for the Frucht graph. Related indexed results for triangle-bearing four-cycle-free graphs and for triangle-free edge-disjoint-four-cycle graphs fail their explicit hypotheses here. An accessible Frucht independence-complex calculation studies a different object. Searches for direct Frucht three-cut computations, Alexander dual formulations, numerical ranks and broader graph-family implications found no covering result. These searches are not proof of absolute novelty. Full text of some plausible indexed publications was inaccessible, leaving residual literature risk.

**Value — PASS.** The Frucht graph is the classical smallest asymmetric cubic graph; its three triangles and sole four-cycle place it at the intersection of two otherwise useful but inapplicable topological classification hypotheses. The exact integral homotopy type, not just a Betti number, gives a concrete boundary example with an explicit and reproducible simple-homotopy explanation. Its multiplicity twenty-six contrasts with the numerical value twenty-five suggested by the four-cycle-free count, which must not be applied outside its domain. The result is a natural isolated classification, not a general theorem for all cubic graphs.
