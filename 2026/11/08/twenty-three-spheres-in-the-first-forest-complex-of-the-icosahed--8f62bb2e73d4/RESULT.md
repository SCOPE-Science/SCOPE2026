# Twenty three-spheres in the first forest complex of the icosahedral graph

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the graph of the regular icosahedron, with twelve vertices and thirty edges. Let \(\mathcal F_1(I)\) be the simplicial complex on its vertex set in which a subset is a face if the induced graph is a disjoint union of isolated vertices and edges. Equivalently, this is the two-independence complex \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(I)\), where each induced connected component may have at most two vertices. Then there is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal F_1(I)\simeq\bigvee^{20}S^3.}
\]
In particular, \(\widetilde H_3(\mathcal F_1(I);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{20}\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. The complex is three-dimensional and pure, with 105 tetrahedral facets.

## Assumptions and scope
The object is a **vertex-subset** complex, not the edge-set matching complex or the usual independence complex. Its faces induce graphs of maximum degree at most one; no cycle can occur under this condition for a finite simple graph. An explicit labeling of the icosahedral graph is contained in `verify_icosahedron_forest.py` as `EDGES`. The script uses only Python standard-library modules. Every subset of the twelve vertices is included in the exhaustive proof below, and all integer orientations use increasing vertex labels. No claim is made for other Platonic graphs or higher filtration stages.

## Proof
Write \(K=\mathcal F_1(I)\). The two independent face predicates in the attached verifier check, respectively, induced vertex degrees and disjointness of induced edges. They agree over all \(2^{12}=4096\) subsets. Exactly 324 subsets, including the empty set, are faces. The nonempty face vector by cardinality is \((12,66,140,105)\), and all 105 inclusion-maximal faces have size four.

For either order \(0,1,\ldots,11\) or \(11,10,\ldots,0\), perform the following complete partial matching on the **nonempty** face poset. At a vertex \(v\), scan all currently unmatched faces \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\), and pair \(\sigma\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) whenever both remain unmatched. After processing all twelve vertices, each construction has 149 pairs and 25 critical faces: one vertex, two triangles and 22 tetrahedra. This is not an assumption of acyclicity: the verifier directs each unmatched covering incidence from the larger face to the smaller one and reverses each matched covering incidence. Kahn topological sorting visits all 323 nonempty faces and verifies acyclicity for each of the two matchings; each checked directed graph has 972 Hasse incidences.

Forman's discrete-Morse theorem therefore provides a homotopy-equivalent CW complex with one zero-cell, two two-cells and 22 three-cells, and no other cells. Its two-skeleton is a wedge of two two-spheres. To calculate the **signed integer** degree-three Morse boundary, write \(\partial\tau=\sum_j(-1)^j(\tau\setminus\{v_j\})\) for increasing vertices. For each paired lower cell \(\sigma\leftrightarrow\tau\), the projected chain of \(\sigma\) is computed recursively from \(\partial\tau\), by eliminating the coefficient \(\pm1\) of \(\sigma\) and projecting the other codimension-one faces. Projected matched upper cells vanish; projected critical cells form the corresponding basis. The acyclic matching makes this recursion well-founded. Applying it to the boundary of all 22 critical tetrahedra yields an explicit integral matrix \(B\) of size \(2\times22\), printed by the verifier and included in `verification_output.txt`.

For ascending order, the two columns indexed 3 and 11, counted from zero in the ordered critical-three-cell list, have determinant \(+1\); independently, the descending-order matrix has a unit minor in columns 1 and 3. Thus the integral homomorphism \(B:\mathbb Z^{22}\to\mathbb Z^2\) is surjective and its Smith invariant factors are both one. Since \(\pi_2(S^2\vee S^2)=\mathbb Z^2\), three-cell attaching maps are completely specified by the displayed degree vectors. Integral elementary changes of cellular bases correspond to homotopy equivalences and three-cell handle slides in this low-dimensional simply-connected CW complex. Two three-cells cancel the two two-cells, leaving one zero-cell and twenty three-cells attached trivially. Hence \(K\simeq\bigvee^{20}S^3\), proving the claimed integral homotopy type.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_icosahedron_forest.py`. It (i) reconstructs the icosahedral graph from 30 explicitly listed edges, checks degree five and exactly two common neighbors across each edge; (ii) independently checks all 4096 candidate faces with degree and edge-disjointness predicates; (iii) checks all 972 directed incidences for each of two Morse matchings; (iv) constructs both signed integral Morse boundary matrices and their unit minors; and (v) separately computes the unreduced simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). The latter are \((11,55,85)\) for the three consecutive boundary maps, consistent with one connected component and 20 top-dimensional homology classes. `verification_output.txt` records the complete critical-cell masks, matrices and determinantal witnesses.

## Relationship to prior work
Deshpande and Singh (2020) study \(r\)-independence complexes and determine homotopy types for complete multipartite graphs, fully whiskered graphs, paths, cycles and perfect rooted trees. The complex here is exactly their \(\operatorname{Ind}_2\), but the icosahedral graph is none of those families. Abdelmalek, Deshpande, Goyal, Roy and Singh (2021) study shellability and vertex decomposability in chordal graphs and trees; the icosahedral graph has induced cycles and is not chordal. Carnero Bravo (2023) independently places the same object at the first positive level of the forest filtration, calculating paths, cycles, double stars, cactus graphs and particular graph joins and products. The icosahedral graph is not covered by these hypotheses. No inspected primary statement implies the displayed homotopy type. This bibliographic comparison does not constitute a proof that no equivalent computation exists outside indexed literature.

## Limitations
The equivalence is nonequivariant; although the count of 20 spheres matches the number of triangular faces of the icosahedron, no canonical basis indexed by those faces, automorphism action, filtration-map computation, or infinite-family pattern is established. The complete finite certificate supports the stated twelve-vertex result only. Scientific review here is not independent validation.
