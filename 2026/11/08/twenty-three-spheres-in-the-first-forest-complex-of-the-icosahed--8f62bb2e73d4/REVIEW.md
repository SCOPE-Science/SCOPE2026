# Scientific review: first icosahedral forest complex

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns precisely the vertex sets inducing graphs of maximum degree one. Two independent face predicates agree on all 4096 subsets, yielding 324 faces. For each of two opposite vertex orders a complete acyclic matching has 149 pairs and critical-cell counts \((1,2,22)\) in dimensions \((0,2,3)\). Both signed Morse boundary matrices are constructed from the complete face poset by recursion along gradient paths, and each has an explicit determinant-one minor. This proves a free rank-twenty kernel and permits topological cancellation of both two-cells. The verifier separately checks ordinary simplicial boundary ranks over two finite fields. The critical computational certificate is included with the finding.

## Originality — PASS against inspected sources
The closest source is Deshpande–Singh's higher-independence theory, which defines precisely this complex and classifies different graph families. Carnero Bravo's forest filtration is equivalent at level one and likewise treats other families. The graph is neither a cycle, tree, cactus, complete multipartite graph, fully whiskered graph, nor chordal graph; no applicable theorem from the inspected texts implies the claimed icosahedral result. Search comparisons also covered icosahedral neighborhood, matching and cut complexes, which are distinct objects. An equivalent unpublished or unindexed calculation remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The icosahedron is the canonical regular five-valent triangulation of the sphere. Determining the *integral homotopy type* of its first nontrivial forest stage supplies a complete, natural and independently reproducible benchmark outside the previously classified graph families. The 20 summands suggest (but do not establish) a relationship with its 20 triangular faces. No equivariant interpretation, extension to all Platonic graphs, or general theorem is claimed.
