# Twist-parity duality for square-flip reconfiguration of ladder matchings

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge5\), let \(P_n=C_n\square K_2\) be the circular ladder (prism), and let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder with \(2n\) vertices. In each graph, join two perfect matchings by a move precisely when their symmetric difference is the four edges of one of the \(n\) designated rung squares. Write \(\mathcal R(P_n)\) and \(\mathcal R(M_{2n})\) for these square-flip graphs, and let \(\Lambda_n\) be the Lucas cube on independent subsets of the cycle \(C_n\), joined when exactly one element is toggled. Then
\[
\mathcal R(P_n)\cong
\begin{cases}\Lambda_n\sqcup2K_1,&n\text{ even},\\\Lambda_n,&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\qquad
\mathcal R(M_{2n})\cong
\begin{cases}\Lambda_n,&n\text{ even},\\\Lambda_n\sqcup2K_1,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
In every nontrivial component the shortest number of flips from the matching encoded by \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) to the matching encoded by \(J\) is exactly \(|I\mathbin\triangle J|\). Consequently this component is a median graph, has radius \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), diameter \(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), exactly \(L_n\) vertices, and \(nF_{n-1}\) edges, with \(F_0=0,F_1=1\) and \(L_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n+1}\). The two additional states, when present, are isolated. Thus one boundary twist reverses precisely which parity admits frozen matchings while preserving the entire connected moving sector and all its flip distances.

## Assumptions and scope
Put two vertices \(u_i,v_i\) in each column \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Include every rung \(u_iv_i\). For \(0\le i<n-1\), include the two longitudinal edges \(u_iu_{i+1}\) and \(v_iv_{i+1}\). For \(P_n\), close the two rows with \(u_{n-1}u_0\) and \(v_{n-1}v_0\); for \(M_{2n}\), close them crosswise with \(u_{n-1}v_0\) and \(v_{n-1}u_0\). The four-cycle \(Q_i\) between columns \(i\) and \(i+1\) uses their two rungs and their two corresponding longitudinal edges; the crosswise seam is part of \(Q_{n-1}\) in the twisted case. Only these \(n\) rung squares are allowed as moves. For \(n\ge5\), they are the square faces naturally arising from the ladder construction. Perfect matchings are sets of \(n\) edges covering every vertex once. An independent set \(I\) of \(C_n\) has no pair of adjacent elements, including the pair across the cyclic seam. A legal square flip exchanges the two opposite occupied edges of \(Q_i\) for the other two opposite edges.

## Proof
**1. Binary construction.** For an independent set \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), choose both longitudinal edges of \(Q_i\) for each \(i\in I\), and choose the rung \(u_jv_j\) for every column \(j\) not incident with any selected \(i\). Different selected indices cannot meet at a column, so this yields a perfect matching \(\Phi(I)\). Distinct independent sets have distinct matching edges.

**2. Exhaustion of matchings with a rung.** If a perfect matching contains at least one rung, cut the cyclic two-row ladder at the occupied rung columns. Between any two successive occupied rung columns the remaining longitudinal edges form two paths on the same number \(k\) of consecutive unoccupied columns; the possible seam cross-connection merely exchanges row labels at the end. A perfect matching of either path exists if and only if \(k\) is even, in which case the edges are forced: consecutive columns are joined in synchronized pairs. This determines a unique independent set of rung squares, and the matching is \(\Phi(I)\).

**3. Exhaustion without rungs.** In the prism, a matching without rungs consists of independent perfect matchings on the two \(n\)-cycles. None exist for odd \(n\); for even \(n\), four exist. Of these four, two use synchronized longitudinal pairs and equal \(\Phi(I)\) for the two alternating maximum independent sets; the other two use opposite row phases and cannot be written \(\Phi(I)\). In the Möbius ladder, the longitudinal edges form a single cycle of length \(2n\), so there are exactly two no-rung matchings. When \(n\) is even, both use synchronized longitudinal pairs and occur in the binary construction. When \(n\) is odd, neither does: a cyclic pairing of an odd number of columns is impossible. Therefore the two parity-exceptional perfect matchings in either model are the only unmatched states.

**4. Exact flips and freezing.** Within \(\Phi(I)\), the face \(Q_i\) is alternating precisely if the bit \(i\) may be toggled without creating adjacent occupied bits. The flip changes \(\Phi(I)\) to \(\Phi(I\mathbin\triangle\{i\})\). In an exceptional matching no rung is occupied and no face contains its two longitudinal edges together; it has no alternating rung square and is isolated. Hence the asserted isomorphisms hold with their full adjacency relations, not just their vertex counts.

**5. The metric and median property.** Each permitted flip changes exactly one bit, so any path between \(\Phi(I)\) and \(\Phi(J)\) has length at least \(|I\mathbin\triangle J|\). Delete the bits in \(I\setminus J\), then add those in \(J\setminus I\). Every intermediate set is independent, giving a path of exactly that length. The coordinatewise majority of any three independent-set bitstrings is again independent: if adjacent coordinates both had majority value one, two subsets of a three-element index set of cardinality at least two would intersect, forcing one original independent set to contain both adjacent coordinates. Therefore each triple has a unique median in the Hamming-isometric component.

**6. Sizes and extremal distances.** Independent subsets of \(C_n\) are counted by \(L_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n+1}\), by separating according to one fixed vertex. Each one-bit edge is uniquely obtained by selecting its added position \(i\) and an independent subset of the remaining path on \(n-3\) vertices, giving \(nF_{n-1}\) edges. Every independent set has size at most \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Two disjoint alternating independent sets of that size exist for all \(n\ge5\), so the metric diameter is \(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). The empty set has eccentricity \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), while the general radius–diameter inequality gives the reverse bound. The radius is therefore \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\).

## Verification
Two independently constructed executable checks accompany this result. The Python checker enumerates perfect matchings directly from the original edges and compares every nonexceptional state and its entire neighbor set with a separately generated Lucas-cube model for all \(5\le n\le14\), under both closures. It also checks the exact number and isolation of exceptional matchings. The C++17 checker independently enumerates matchings and square moves, then checks components, edge counts, and the radius and diameter of the nontrivial component for all \(5\le n\le13\). Both tests completed successfully. Exhaustive finite tests support, but do not replace, the all-order proof above. Reproduce with `python3 verify_binary_model.py` and `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_direct.cpp -o checker && ./checker`.

## Relationship to prior work
Cao and Ren (2016) give exact counts of perfect matchings and edge-colourings of Möbius ladders. Their counted matching states do not, from the inspected primary article, determine the local-move graph or its distances. Klavžar (2005) establishes the median property and edge enumerations of Lucas cubes; these abstract properties are **not** claimed as new here. Žigert Pleteršek and Berlič (2013), Theorem 4.2, obtain a Lucas cube with two isolated states for *cyclic fibonacene benzenoid graphs* under **hexagon** resonance moves. Their host graphs and moves are different; in particular their even-index exceptional parity is not an implication for square flips of Möbius ladders. Lekshmi and West (2020) count perfect matchings in both ladder families, including the same exceptional parity counts. The new claim is the specific simultaneous *square-flip adjacency and exact metric classification* for prism and Möbius ladders, with the parity reversal under a single twist. A counting identity alone does not determine these move graphs. These related sources are acknowledged explicitly to avoid claiming their enumerative consequences as novel.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns only designated rung-square flips, not arbitrary alternating-cycle exchanges or unrestricted matching switches. In disconnected cases, shortest paths between a frozen state and any other state do not exist; the quoted radius and diameter refer solely to the unique nontrivial component. No general statement about other ladder widths, hexagon resonance systems, or sampling-mixing times follows. The 2020 matching-count paper's full text was not obtainable for complete line-by-line comparison; while its abstract concerns counting, it may contain relevant intermediate constructions. Earlier unindexed work on dimer flips or equivalent reconfiguration descriptions may also exist. Neither historical uniqueness nor independent scholarly validation is asserted.
