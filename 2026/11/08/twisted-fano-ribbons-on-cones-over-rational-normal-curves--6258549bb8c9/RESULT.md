# Twisted Fano ribbons on cones over rational normal curves

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic, let \(d\geq 2\), and let \(X_d\subset\mathbb P^{d+1}_k\) be the projective cone over the degree-\(d\) rational normal curve \(\nu_d(\mathbb P^1)\subset\mathbb P^d\). Let \(F_d=F_1(X_d)\) denote the **full scheme** of lines on the cone, embedded in the Grassmannian. Then
\[
(F_d)_{\mathrm{red}}\cong\mathbb P^1,
\qquad
F_d\cong\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_{\mathbb P^1}
\bigl(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}\oplus\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(d-2)\bigr),
\]
where the second summand is a square-zero ideal. The retraction to \(\mathbb P^1\) comes from intersection of each line with the fixed base hyperplane. Thus the intrinsic nilradical satisfies
\[
\mathcal N^2=0,\qquad \mathcal N\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(d-2),
\qquad \dim_k H^0(F_d,\mathcal O_{F_d})=d.
\]
With the Plücker polarization, the Hilbert polynomial is
\[
P_{F_d}(n)=2dn+d.
\]
In particular, for every \(d\geq3\) the nonreduced scheme \(F_d\) is **not** abstractly isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^1\times\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\). The boundary case \(d=2\) is the trivial product.

## Assumptions and scope
The embedding is the standard complete-degree-\(d\) rational normal curve with coordinates
\[
\nu_d([s:t])=[s^d:s^{d-1}t:\cdots:st^{d-1}:t^d].
\]
The cone has coordinates \([V:X_0:\cdots:X_d]\) and ideal generated by the \(2\times2\) minors of the Hankel matrix with rows \((X_0,\ldots,X_{d-1})\) and \((X_1,\ldots,X_d)\). We assume \(d\geq2\) so the base curve contains no line. The assertion is over every algebraically closed field, with no restriction on characteristic; it concerns the entire Grassmannian Fano scheme, not just its reduced locus or an abstract parameter space of rulings.

## Proof
**1. Every geometric line is a ruling.** Let \(v=[1:0:\cdots:0]\) be the cone vertex and let \(H=\{V=0\}\). A line contained in \(X_d\) and disjoint from \(v\) would project from \(v\) to a line contained in the rational normal curve, which is impossible for \(d\geq2\). The same applies to a line contained in \(H\). Consequently every geometric line joins \(v\) to a unique point of \(\nu_d(\mathbb P^1)\). The intersection with \(H\) defines a morphism \(\rho:F_d\to\mathbb P^1\) on the entire scheme: lines contained in \(H\) are absent, so the Grassmannian intersection construction is defined everywhere; the intersection point belongs scheme-theoretically to \(X_d\cap H\). The ruling family gives a section of \(\rho\).

**2. Exact first affine chart.** On the open set of rulings whose base point has \(X_0\ne0\), write a line uniquely using the two spanning vectors
\[
P=[1:0:u:b_2:\cdots:b_d],\qquad Q=[0:1:t:t^2:\cdots:t^d].
\]
Here the value of \(t\) is fixed by the intersection with \(H\), and the normalization \(P_V=1\), \(P_{X_0}=0\) eliminates basis ambiguity. A line is contained in \(X_d\) if and only if all its restrictions of the affine graph equations \(X_j/X_0=(X_1/X_0)^j\), for \(2\le j\le d\), vanish coefficientwise in the line parameter \(z\):
\[
t^j+z b_j=(t+zu)^j.
\]
For \(j=2\), the linear and quadratic coefficients give \(b_2=2tu\) and \(u^2=0\). For all \(j\ge3\), the linear coefficient then gives \(b_j=j t^{j-1}u\), and the higher coefficients vanish because \(u^2=0\). This reasoning uses polynomial coefficients, not division by factorials, and holds in every characteristic. It proves the **entire local ideal**, yielding
\[
\rho^{-1}(X_0\ne0)=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,u]/(u^2).
\]

**3. Second chart and scheme-theoretic gluing.** On \(X_d\ne0\), use \(s=1/t\) and
\[
Q'=[0:s^d:s^{d-1}:\cdots:s:1],\quad
P'=[1:c_0:\cdots:c_{d-1}:0],\quad w=c_{d-1}.
\]
The same coefficient argument gives \(\operatorname{Spec}k[s,w]/(w^2)\). On the overlap, take \(Q'=t^{-d}Q\) and eliminate the final base coordinate of \(P\) by setting
\[
P'=P-\frac{b_d}{t^d}Q.
\]
Since \(b_{d-1}=(d-1)t^{d-2}u\) and \(b_d=dt^{d-1}u\), this gives the **exact transition**
\[
s=t^{-1},\qquad w=b_{d-1}-\frac{b_d}{t^d}t^{d-1}=-t^{d-2}u.
\]
These are precisely the transitions of a square-zero ideal sheaf \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(d-2)\). There is no nilpotent correction to the reduced coordinate \(s=t^{-1}\); therefore the two affine rings glue as the square-zero sheaf-algebra \(\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(d-2)\), with the claimed retraction. This proves the global scheme isomorphism, not merely an identification of completed local rings.

**4. Intrinsic and polarized invariants.** The nilradical is a characteristic ideal, so its restriction to the reduced \(\mathbb P^1\) is preserved by any abstract scheme isomorphism. For \(d\ge3\), \(\mathcal O(d-2)\) is not trivial. Moreover
\[
H^0(F_d,\mathcal O_{F_d})\cong k\oplus H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(d-2))
\]
as a vector space, so its dimension is \(1+(d-1)=d\), whereas a product with the dual-numbers point has dimension \(2\). The reduced ruling curve is embedded into the Grassmannian by Plücker coordinates as the degree-\(d\) rational normal curve. Applying Euler characteristic to the nilradical exact sequence, twisted by the Plücker bundle to the \(n\)-th power, gives
\[
\chi(F_d,\mathcal O_{F_d}(n))
=\chi(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(dn))
+\chi(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(dn+d-2))
=(dn+1)+(dn+d-1)=2dn+d.
\]
The proof is valid for every integer \(n\), as an Euler-characteristic identity; the Hilbert polynomial statement follows.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` checks the Hankel-minor identities after substituting the exact first-order direction, the coefficient of \(u^2\) in the first quadratic relation, the two-chart transition, and the Hilbert-characteristic arithmetic for degrees \(2\) through \(11\). It also independently constructs the full coefficient ideals of the Hankel minors, without assuming the chart description, and checks equality with the stated ideals via Gröbner bases for degrees \(3,4,5\) in characteristics \(0,2,3,5\). The remaining coefficient checks work over the integers and modulo \(2,3,5,7\). Reproduction: `python3 verify.py` using SymPy. The proof, not the finite computational range, establishes the theorem for all degrees and characteristics.

## Relationship to prior work
Ilten, *Fano Schemes of Lines on Toric Surfaces*, arXiv:1411.3025v1 (2014), Theorem 2.2(1), gives the product \(\mathbb P^1\times\xi_2\) for the cone \(S(d,0)\), \(d\ge2\), where \(\xi_2=\operatorname{Spec}k[z]/(z^2)\). Its proof in Section 3.4 determines the affine local form and then identifies the global gluing with a product. The local doubled-affine-line calculation agrees with the present theorem, but local form alone does not determine the global nilradical. The transition \(w=-t^{d-2}u\) distinguishes the two for every \(d\ge3\), and the discrepancy is independently visible in the intrinsic vector-space dimension \(h^0(\mathcal O_{F_d})\). The assertion agrees with the product for \(d=2\).

Ilten–Zotine, *On Fano Schemes of Toric Varieties*, arXiv:1605.05745v1 (2016), Section 9 and Remark 9.7, develops local combinatorial descriptions of potentially nonreduced Fano schemes and references the preceding classification. Its local description does not supply the specific global nilradical transition or the Hilbert polynomial above. The claim here is the global scheme and its intrinsic invariant, not the already known reduced ruling parameterization or local dual-number charts.

## Limitations
The full-text comparison is with the accessible arXiv version of the 2014 paper and the full 2016 preprint. The final publisher-hosted version of the first article was not accessible; it could contain revisions to the stated product formula. No claim of independent historical-priority certification is made. The result does not cover \(d=1\), arbitrary toric surfaces, or higher-dimensional Fano schemes. Exact symbolic checks in finitely many degrees do not establish the infinite-degree theorem without the proof given above.
