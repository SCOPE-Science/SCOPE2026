# Two adjacent sphere dimensions in ordinary three-cut complexes of Boolean hypercubes

## Finding
Let \(Q_d\) denote the graph on \(\{0,1\}^{d}\), with two vertices adjacent exactly when their Hamming distance is one. Put \(N=2^d\). For every integer \(d\ge3\), the ordinary three-cut complex of \(Q_d\) has the integral homotopy type
\[
\boxed{\Delta_3(Q_d)\simeq\bigvee^{A_d}S^{N-5}\ \vee\ \bigvee^{B_d}S^{N-4}},
\]
where
\[
A_d=2^{d-3}(d^2-5d+8)-1,
\qquad
B_d=2^{d-1}\sum_{j=3}^{d}\binom dj.
\]
The first instances are \((A_3,B_3)=(1,4)\), \((A_4,B_4)=(7,40)\), \((A_5,B_5)=(31,256)\), and \((A_6,B_6)=(111,1344)\). The case \(d=3\) was established previously; the claim of novelty is the infinite-dimensional extension \(d\ge4\), together with its uniform cubical-collapse explanation. For every \(d\ge3\), this pure \((N-4)\)-dimensional simplicial complex is not shellable.

More precisely, the combinatorial Alexander dual \(D_d=\Delta_3(Q_d)^\vee\) simplicially collapses onto the standard triangulated two-skeleton of the geometric \(d\)-cube, with one additional edge joining each pair of cube vertices at Hamming distance at least three. Consequently,
\[
D_d\simeq\bigvee^{B_d}S^1\ \vee\ \bigvee^{A_d}S^2.
\]
The explicit collapse is the main structural step; the cut-complex formula follows from integral combinatorial Alexander duality and a high-connectivity CW argument.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs and simplicial complexes are finite. For a graph \(G=(V,E)\), the ordinary three-cut complex \(\Delta_3(G)\) is generated by the facets \(V\setminus T\) for three-element sets \(T\subseteq V\) inducing a disconnected graph. This is distinct from the total three-cut complex, which uses independent triples only. The combinatorial Alexander dual on the *same* vertex set is \(K^\vee=\{F\subseteq V:V\setminus F\notin K\}\).

A cube square is a set of four words obtained by independently flipping two coordinates, with all other coordinates fixed. The full geometric \(d\)-cube has \(N=2^d\) vertices, \(M=d2^{d-1}\) edges, and \(Q=2^{d-2}\binom d2\) square two-faces. In the displayed formulas a zero-dimensional or negative-dimensional convention is unnecessary: \(d\ge3\) gives \(N\ge8\) and \(A_d,B_d>0\).

## Proof
**Step 1: Exact Alexander-dual face description.** A set \(F\subseteq V(Q_d)\) is in \(D_d\) if and only if *none* of its three-subsets induces a disconnected graph. Thus every singleton and every pair is a face. Since \(Q_d\) is triangle-free, the two-faces of \(D_d\) are exactly induced three-vertex paths (two-edge paths) in \(Q_d\). A four-set all of whose triples induce connected graphs necessarily induces a four-cycle: all its vertices must have degree at least two within the induced graph, and a triangle-free graph on four vertices with minimum degree two is a four-cycle. For five vertices, the condition that every triple contain at least two edges would require at least seven edges: count the incidences of edges with the ten triples, each edge occurring in three triples. Mantel's bound for a five-vertex triangle-free graph is six edges. Hence \(D_d\) has no faces with five or more vertices. Its three-simplices are exactly the cube squares.

**Step 2: A global elementary collapse.** Every two-edge path of the hypercube lies in precisely one square. Its endpoint vertices differ in exactly two coordinates, and their two common neighbors are precisely the two alternative intermediate vertices in that square. In particular a two-path triangle belongs to exactly one square tetrahedron of \(D_d\), and a diagonal joining vertices at Hamming distance two belongs to exactly one square.

For each square take cyclic vertices \(a,b,c,e\), so \(a,c\) and \(b,e\) are its two diagonals. First remove the tetrahedron \(\{a,b,c,e\}\) together with its free triangular face \(\{a,b,e\}\). Next remove the triangle \(\{b,c,e\}\) together with its now-free diagonal edge \(\{b,e\}\). The first triangle is a free face because it belongs to exactly this square tetrahedron. After that removal, the diagonal \(\{b,e\}\) has just one triangular coface, \(\{b,c,e\}\), and belongs to no remaining tetrahedron. No such removal interferes with another square because neither the relevant path triangle nor the diagonal belongs to a second square.

The surviving two-faces of this square are \(\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\{a,c,e\}\), the usual triangulation of its square face by diagonal \(\{a,c\}\). All cube graph edges survive. All edges at Hamming distance at least three survive but occur in no two-face, since endpoints of every induced two-edge path have Hamming distance two. Therefore the indicated collapses leave the geometric cube's triangulated two-skeleton \(C_d^{(2)}\), together with exactly one extra edge for every vertex pair at Hamming distance at least three. This proves the claimed simplicial collapse, with no finite-case extrapolation.

**Step 3: Homotopy of the dual.** Attaching cells of dimensions three and higher to \(C_d^{(2)}\) produces the contractible geometric \(d\)-cube and does not change \(\pi_1\). Hence \(C_d^{(2)}\) is simply connected. It is two-dimensional, so its second integral homology is free, of rank given by its Euler characteristic:
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_2(C_d^{(2)};\mathbb Z)
=Q-M+N-1
=2^{d-3}(d^2-5d+8)-1=A_d.
\]
Maps from two-spheres representing a basis of this homology induce a homology equivalence from \(\bigvee^{A_d}S^2\); as both sides are simply connected CW complexes, Whitehead's theorem gives a homotopy equivalence. Each extra edge between two already connected vertices adds a free circle. The number of unordered vertex pairs at Hamming distance at least three is
\[
\frac12\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^d}\sum_{j=3}^{d}\binom dj
=2^{d-1}\sum_{j=3}^{d}\binom dj=B_d.
\]
Thus \(D_d\simeq\bigvee^{B_d}S^1\vee\bigvee^{A_d}S^2\), integrally, and its integral cohomology is free in degrees one and two with these respective ranks and zero in every other positive degree.

**Step 4: Integral cut-complex homology and connectivity.** For a simplicial complex on \(N\) vertices, combinatorial Alexander duality gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(\Delta_3(Q_d);\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H^{N-i-3}(D_d;\mathbb Z).
\]
Consequently the only nonzero reduced homology groups have degrees \(N-5\) and \(N-4\), and are \(\mathbb Z^{A_d}\) and \(\mathbb Z^{B_d}\), respectively.

Every five-vertex subset of a triangle-free graph contains a disconnected three-subset, by the edge count in Step 1. Thus every subset of \(V(Q_d)\) with at most \(N-5\) elements is a face of \(\Delta_3(Q_d)\). This complex therefore contains the full \((N-6)\)-skeleton of the \((N-1)\)-simplex and is \((N-7)\)-connected. Since its reduced homology in degree \(N-6\) is zero, the Hurewicz theorem shows that it is in fact \((N-6)\)-connected. Set \(m=N-5\ge3\). We have a finite \((m-1)\)-connected CW complex of dimension \(m+1\), with free homology only in degrees \(m,m+1\). In this dimension range, the Hurewicz map in degree \(m\) is an isomorphism and the Whitehead exact sequence gives a surjection from \(\pi_{m+1}\) onto \(H_{m+1}\). Choose sphere maps realizing bases of both free homology groups. They define a map from the indicated wedge of \(m\)- and \(m+1\)-spheres to the cut complex which is an integral homology isomorphism. Both spaces are simply connected, so Whitehead's homology theorem makes this map a homotopy equivalence. This proves the displayed integral homotopy type.

**Step 5: Nonshellability.** The complex is pure of dimension \(N-4\), since all its facets are complements of disconnected triples. A pure shellable complex has reduced homology concentrated in its top dimension. But \(A_d>0\) gives nonzero reduced homology in the next-to-top dimension \(N-5\). Therefore \(\Delta_3(Q_d)\) is not shellable.

## Verification
The standalone scripts `artifacts/verify_collapse.py` and `artifacts/verify_cube.py` can be executed using Python 3's standard library alone. The first builds every face of \(D_d\), checks that both collapses at every square use free faces at the moment of removal, and compares the surviving complex to the explicitly triangulated cubical two-skeleton plus the predicted long edges, for \(d=3,4,5,6\). The second independently builds the full degree-one and degree-two boundary columns over \(\mathbf F_2\), confirms the predicted ranks and incidence identities for \(d=3,4,5,6\), and tests the exact Alexander-dual face characterization by exhaustive small-set enumeration for \(d=3,4\).

The recorded outputs are `artifacts/collapse_check.txt` and `artifacts/boundary_check.txt`. The scripts test finitely many dimensions and do not certify the infinite-family claim. That claim rests on the all-parameter free-face and connectivity arguments above. Both boundary and collapse scripts are included with all dependencies.

## Relationship to prior work
The ordinary cut-complex definition, combinatorial Alexander duality, shellability implications, and classifications for trees, cycles, complete multipartite graphs and prisms over cliques are from Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* (arXiv:2304.13675, Sections 1, 6–7). Their sequel treats disjoint unions, squared paths and rectangular grids, but does not state this hypercube-family theorem. Chandrakar et al. study planar rectangular \(2\times n\) and \(3\times n\) grids, not the \(d\)-fold cube product for unbounded \(d\).

A previously available calculation already establishes \(\Delta_3(Q_3)\simeq S^3\vee\bigvee^4 S^4\). This is the \(d=3\) check here and is not claimed as new. An existing theorem about edge-disjoint-square triangle-free graphs is not applicable to \(Q_d\): for \(d\ge3\), each cube edge lies in \(d-1\) squares. Another existing theorem identifies the same numerical rank \(A_d\) for one component of a *Lovász neighborhood complex* of \(Q_d\). That is a different simplicial complex; its statement neither identifies the Alexander dual of the ordinary three-cut complex nor yields the second rank \(B_d\). The new uniform square-tetrahedron collapse and two-degree homotopy classification are distinct mathematical content.

## Limitations
No statement about equivariant homotopy equivalence, integral automorphism representations, simple-homotopy type of the original cut complex, or higher cut parameters is made. The cubical collapse is elementary and explicit, but the duality-to-wedge step uses standard classical algebraic topology. Indexed searches and the inspected primary sources do not exclude an unindexed equivalent theorem stated in another framework. The novelty claim is restricted to dimensions \(d\ge4\); the initial cube case is prior work.
