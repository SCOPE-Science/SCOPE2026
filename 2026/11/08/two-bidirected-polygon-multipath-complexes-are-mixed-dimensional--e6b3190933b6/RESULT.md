# Two bidirected polygon multipath complexes are mixed-dimensional sphere wedges

## Finding
Let \(B_L\) be the bidirected simple cycle on the vertex set \(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\): every cycle edge is replaced by its two opposite directed arcs. Let \(X_L\) be its multipath complex, whose vertices are directed arcs and whose simplices are the arc subsets whose directed connected components are simple paths or isolated vertices. Then
\[
 X_5\simeq S^2\vee S^3\vee S^3, \qquad
 X_6\simeq S^2\vee S^4\vee S^4.
\]
Both equivalences are integral, ordinary homotopy equivalences. The five-vertex result additionally shows that the previously established zero-connectivity lower bound for the bidirected five-cycle is not sharp: its multipath complex is simply connected.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has exactly \(L\) vertices for \(L\in\{5,6\}\), with no chords or loops and with both directed arcs on every polygon edge. A multipath is a spanning directed subgraph whose connected components are directed simple paths or isolated vertices. All homology and cohomology groups use integral coefficients unless specified. The notation in Caputi–Collari–Smith (2026) is \(\mathrm{BP}_{L-1}\), so these are the cases \(\mathrm{BP}_4\) and \(\mathrm{BP}_5\). No other polygon length is asserted.

Label the arc \(i\to i+1\pmod L\) by \(i\), and its reverse \(i+1\to i\pmod L\) by \(L+i\), for \(0\le i<L\). An arc subset is a multipath exactly when (i) every source and target occurs at most once and (ii) the selected arcs contain no directed cycle. The equivalence follows because in-degree and out-degree at most one force each connected component to be a directed path or directed cycle. A directed-cycle-free arc set has at most \(L-1\) arcs.

## Proof
**Complete finite discrete-Morse reductions.** For each \(L\in\{5,6\}\), enumerate every nonempty multipath face. The exact \(f\)-vectors, by dimensions beginning at zero, are
\[
L=5:\ (10,30,30,10),\qquad L=6:\ (12,48,76,48,12).
\]
Apply the following deterministic partial matching to the nonempty face poset. For \(v=0,1,\ldots,2L-1\) in that order, pair every still-unpaired face \(F\) not containing \(v\) with \(F\cup\{v\}\) whenever both faces remain unmatched; scan available faces in increasing binary-bitmask order. Each face occurs in at most one pair. Reverse those paired cover arrows in the Hasse diagram and direct every remaining cover downward.

The standalone verifier checks *every* cover relation and shows this directed graph is acyclic: there are 190 and 576 Hasse incidences, respectively. For \(L=5\), 38 matched pairs leave precisely one critical zero-cell, one critical two-cell, and two critical three-cells. For \(L=6\), 96 pairs leave precisely one critical zero-cell, one critical two-cell, and two critical four-cells. By the acyclic-matching/discrete-Morse CW theorem, these are the exact cell dimensions of homotopy-equivalent CW complexes. This exhaustive verification is a finite proof, not a conjectural pattern extrapolated to other \(L\).

**The five-vertex case.** The exact rational ranks of the ordinary simplicial boundary maps \(\partial_2\) and \(\partial_3\), calculated by rational Gaussian elimination on the complete signed incidence matrices, are 21 and 8. Since there are 10 three-simplices, \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_3(X_5;\mathbb Q)=10-8=2\). The Morse CW model has two three-cells and one two-cell. Its degree map from the two free three-cell chains to the one two-cell chain must therefore be zero over \(\mathbb Q\), hence over \(\mathbb Z\). As its two-skeleton is \(S^2\) and \(\pi_2(S^2)=\mathbb Z\), the two degree-zero attaching maps are nullhomotopic, yielding \(X_5\simeq S^2\vee2S^3\).

**The six-vertex case and its integral Hopf obstruction.** The Morse CW model has one cell in dimension zero, one in dimension two, and exactly two in dimension four. It is therefore \(S^2\cup_{f_1}e^4\cup_{f_2}e^4\), with attaching maps \(f_j:S^3\to S^2\). As \(\pi_3(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z\) is generated by the Hopf map, the integers classifying these attachments are their Hopf invariants. These are exactly the coefficients of the square of a primitive integral degree-two cohomology generator on the two four-cells.

Define an integral simplicial two-cochain \(\alpha\) to have coefficient \(+1\) on the following increasing triples of the *clockwise-arc labels*, and zero on all other triangles:
\[
(0,1,3),\ (0,2,3),\ (0,2,4),\ (0,2,5),\
(1,2,4),\ (1,2,5),\ (1,3,4),\ (1,3,5).
\]
The signed simplicial coboundary \(\delta\alpha\) vanishes on all 48 tetrahedra, by exact integer verification. The three disjoint polygon edges numbered \(0,2,4\) give the embedded octahedral two-sphere cycle
\[
z=([0]-[6])*([2]-[8])*([4]-[10]),
\]
where \(*\) is oriented simplicial join. Each of the eight triangles belongs to \(X_6\); its boundary vanishes by alternating-sign cancellation. Direct evaluation yields \(\langle\alpha,z\rangle=1\). Since the Morse model has \(H^2(X_6;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), this proves that \([\alpha]\) is a primitive generator (rather than an unspecified multiple).

The Alexander–Whitney cup-square formula on an increasing four-simplex \((v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4)\) is
\[
(\alpha\smile\alpha)(v_0,\dots,v_4)=
\alpha(v_0,v_1,v_2)\alpha(v_2,v_3,v_4).
\]
Both factors cannot simultaneously be nonzero on any of the 12 four-simplices of \(X_6\); the verifier checks all of them, with integral signs and coefficients. Therefore the cup square is the **zero cochain**, not just zero modulo two. Both Hopf invariants vanish, so \(f_1,f_2\) are nullhomotopic in \(\pi_3(S^2)\), proving \(X_6\simeq S^2\vee2S^4\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using only the Python standard library. It reconstructs both face posets from the directed-graph definitions (rather than importing face tables), forms the deterministic matchings, enumerates every Hasse cover, verifies acyclicity by a complete topological sort, and checks the exact rational boundary ranks for \(L=5\). For \(L=6\), it checks the eight-triangle integral cocycle, the octahedral cycle and unit evaluation, and the zero Alexander–Whitney square on all top faces. `verification_output.txt` stores an actual successful run. The complete finite enumeration checks 80 and 196 nonempty faces, respectively. No fact about larger polygons is inferred from these finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work
Caputi, Collari, and Smith (2026, first publicly posted 2026-01-09) prove connectivity bounds and integral homology groups for bidirectional polygon multipath complexes but explicitly leave their sphere-wedge homotopy types unresolved. Their bound for \(\mathrm{BP}_4\) is connectivity at least zero, and the present calculation strengthens it to simple connectivity. The earlier Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith treatment (2022 preprint; 2024 journal version) proves sphere-wedge results for **oriented** polygons, explicitly excluding bidirectional edges, so that result does not imply either case here. Earlier calculations of rational or integral symmetry actions on bidirected polygon homology identify representations but, without attaching-map information, do not determine the two asserted homotopy types. The new ingredient in the hexagon is the explicit primitive integral cocycle with identically vanishing square, which resolves the potentially nontrivial Hopf attachments.

## Limitations
The result covers the five- and six-vertex bidirected cycles only; it does not establish sphere-wedge decompositions for arbitrary polygon lengths or describe equivariant homotopy. The proof of the finite acyclic-matching statements depends on the fully supplied executable enumerator. Independent checking of the computational certificate and the classical Hopf-invariant interpretation remains appropriate. Absolute historical originality cannot be guaranteed against inaccessible or unindexed literature.
