# Two-binomial phase reconstruction by centered Parikh-prefix vectors

## Finding

Let \(U=u_0u_1\cdots u_{p-1}\) be a nonempty finite word of length \(p\) over the alphabet \(\Sigma\) of letters actually occurring in \(U\). For each \(m\ge1\) and \(0\le i<p\), define \(F_i^{(m)}=(u_i\cdots u_{p-1}u_0\cdots u_{i-1})^m\). Write \(A_x=|U|_x\), \(P_i(x)=|u_0\cdots u_{i-1}|_x\), and

\[
H_i=\bigl(pP_i(x)-iA_x\bigr)_{x\in\Sigma}.
\]

**Theorem (all periods and all repeat counts).** For any \(m\ge1\) and \(0\le i,j<p\), the words \(F_i^{(m)}\) and \(F_j^{(m)}\) have identical scattered-subsequence multiplicities for every word of length at most two **if and only if** \(H_i=H_j\). Equivalently, for \(i<j\), the intervening ordinary factor \(V=u_i\cdots u_{j-1}\) must have its Parikh vector proportional to the full period:

\[
\forall x\in\Sigma:\quad p|V|_x=(j-i)A_x.
\]

Thus the number of two-binomial classes among the \(p\) length-\(mp\) phase factors is the number of distinct centered vectors \(H_0,\ldots,H_{p-1}\), independent of \(m\). This gives an expected \(O(p|\Sigma|)\)-arithmetic-operation algorithm for class enumeration using integer prefix counts and standard tuple hashing; sorting instead gives a deterministic \(O(p|\Sigma|\log p)\) comparison-operation bound.

**Arithmetic and structural consequences.** Put \(g=\gcd\{A_x:x\in\Sigma\}\). Two different phases can collide only when their separation is a positive multiple of \(p/g\). Hence \(g=1\) guarantees all \(p\) phases are two-binomially distinguishable. More strongly, for every \(q\ge3\), pairwise distinct letters \(c_0,\ldots,c_{q-1}\), and arbitrary positive run lengths \(a_0,\ldots,a_{q-1}\), the periodic word generated by

\[
U=c_0^{a_0}c_1^{a_1}\cdots c_{q-1}^{a_{q-1}}
\]

has **exactly \(p=\sum_ta_t\) two-binomial phase classes at every length \(mp\), \(m\ge1\)**. Its one-binomial phase complexity is one, so the least deck order that recovers its phase is exactly two. This last assertion holds even when \(g>1\): for example, the six phases of \(001122\) are distinguishable, whereas \(012012\), with the same Parikh vector, has only three distinct phases. These examples show that the gcd obstruction is necessary but not sufficient for a collision and that cyclic letter ordering matters.

## Assumptions and scope

Words are indexed from zero; alphabet letters can be given in any fixed order to form vectors. A scattered occurrence of a word \(v\) in a word \(w\), written \(\binom{w}{v}\), is a strictly increasing sequence of positions of \(w\) spelling \(v\). Two-binomial equivalence means equality of \(\binom{w}{v}\) for all \(|v|\le2\), including single letters and all ordered pairs. The period word \(U\) need not be primitive. The symbol \(m\) is an arbitrary positive integer, and the phase positions are \(0,\ldots,p-1\), even when some phases yield identical ordinary words. The theorem concerns **whole-period lengths \(mp\)** only; no claim is made here for lengths not divisible by \(p\), nor for minimal distinguishing order beyond two when phase collisions occur.

## Proof

Fix \(x,y\in\Sigma\) with \(x\ne y\), and let \(C_{xy}(w)=\binom{w}{xy}\). The full-period-length word \(F_{i+1}^{(m)}\) is obtained by moving the first letter \(z=u_i\) of \(F_i^{(m)}\) to its end. Each phase word contains exactly \(mA_x\) copies of \(x\), independently of \(i\). If \(z=x\), moving \(z\) deletes precisely \(mA_y\) occurrences of \(xy\); if \(z=y\), it creates precisely \(mA_x\) occurrences; otherwise nothing changes. Therefore

\[
C_{xy}(F_{i+1}^{(m)})-C_{xy}(F_i^{(m)})
=m\bigl(A_x\mathbf1_{u_i=y}-A_y\mathbf1_{u_i=x}\bigr).
\]

For \(0\le i<j<p\), summing over \(i,\ldots,j-1\), with \(v_x=|u_i\cdots u_{j-1}|_x\), gives the exact identity

\[
C_{xy}(F_j^{(m)})-C_{xy}(F_i^{(m)})
=m(A_xv_y-A_yv_x).\tag{1}
\]

For repeated-symbol pairs \(xx\), the count is always \(\binom{mA_x}{2}\); the empty word and the one-letter counts are also independent of phase. Thus two-binomial equivalence holds exactly when all the cross-products in (1) vanish. All \(A_x\) are positive, so this is equivalent to \(v_x=\lambda A_x\) for every \(x\). Summing the coordinates gives \(j-i=\lambda p\), and hence \(pv_x=(j-i)A_x\). Finally, \(H_j(x)-H_i(x)=pv_x-(j-i)A_x\), proving the stated centered-vector test and its independence of \(m\).

For the arithmetic consequence, write \(A_x=gb_x\) with \(\gcd\{b_x\}=1\), and set \(s=p/g=\sum_xb_x\). Any collision with separation \(d=j-i\) satisfies \(sv_x=db_x\). Since the \(b_x\) have gcd one, Bezout's identity yields \(s\mid d\). If \(g=1\), then \(s=p\), which is impossible for \(0<d<p\).

For the distinct-run consequence, suppose for contradiction that a proper nonempty factor \(V=u_i\cdots u_{j-1}\), \(i<j\), has \(pv_x=(j-i)A_x\) for all \(x\). Since \(0<j-i<p\), its common ratio \((j-i)/p\) lies strictly between zero and one. Every letter of \(U\) must occur in \(V\). But when \(q\ge3\) and each letter occupies a single contiguous run in the displayed linear order, any contiguous factor containing every letter contains at least one complete interior run, namely the run of \(c_1\) (and possibly more). For that letter, \(v_{c_1}=A_{c_1}\), contradicting the strict ratio bound. Hence all \(H_i\) differ. One-binomial equivalence holds for all phases because each has the same letter counts \(mA_x\), giving minimal order two. The class-count algorithm computes the \(p\) prefix vectors incrementally, forms the \(H_i\), and groups equal tuples; this requires expected \(O(p|\Sigma|)\) integer operations with hashing (or a deterministic sorting bound with an extra logarithmic factor) and does not depend on \(m\).

## Verification

A standalone stdlib Python program `verify.py` constructs the full scattered-one- and scattered-two-letter decks of each phase **directly**, without using the centered-vector formula in the deck calculation, and compares every pair with that formula. The command `python verify.py` outputs `VERIFY_OK 35116 432228 7416`. The first number counts tested input configurations, the second independent phase-pair comparisons across repeat counts, and the third observed collisions. Exhaustive loops include all words using at least two symbols over alphabets of sizes two, three and four, with maximum tested period sizes eight, seven and six respectively, and repetition counts one through three. Additional loops test distinct-run words on three to five letters with run lengths from one to four. These finite checks detect counterexamples in the tested domain; the proof above, not enumeration, establishes the infinite statements.

## Relationship to prior work

Lejeune, Rigo and Rosenfeld (2020), *On the binomial equivalence classes of finite words*, give the general criterion in their Lemma 7 that fixed Parikh vector and all pair counts determine the two-binomial class, together with general equivalence-class algorithms. Rigo, Stipulanti and Whiteland (2022), *Characterizations of families of morphisms and words via binomial complexities*, study complexity refinements, morphic families, and when binomial complexity reaches factor complexity. Poovanandran, Simpson and Teh (2021), *Counting Subwords in Circular Words and Their Parikh Matrices*, define averages of Parikh-matrix entries over an entire conjugacy class, deliberately discarding individual phase distinctions. Their Corollary 4.13 also uses the already-known binary weak-ratio relation between Parikh vectors to characterize a multiplication identity for **averaged binary circular** Parikh matrices; the proportionality relation itself is therefore not proposed as a new algebraic concept. None of the inspected relevant sections states the exact centered-prefix phase criterion, its power-independent equivalence partition, or the all-phase distinction for arbitrary distinct-run words on three or more letters. Their general definitions and preliminary combinatorial identities are prerequisites, not claimed as new.

The previously obtained binary two-run calculations for \((0^a1^b)^\omega\) determine special binary collision counts and third-order phase separation; those results do not imply the arbitrary-alphabet criterion or the three-or-more-distinct-run rigidity statement. Conversely the present two-binomial theorem makes no claim to recover third-order binary collision pairs. The distinction is between complete **linear phase-specific** subsequence decks and rotationally averaged circular invariants.

## Limitations

No statement of priority or peer-reviewed originality is claimed: equivalent formulations may exist outside inspected literature. The theorem treats only lengths divisible by the displayed period and records equivalence classes of phase factors, not all phases at other lengths or arbitrary optimal reconstruction schemes. The complexity bound counts integer-arithmetic operations; bit complexity also depends on integer magnitudes. Independent audit has not been performed.
