# Scientific review: two-binomial rigidity of geometric-marker words

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is completely quantified over integer bases \(B\ge2\), factor lengths \(n\ge1\), and all starting positions. The proof reduces the 2-binomial statistics to the number of markers \(r\) and their relative-position sum \(T\), identifies the consecutive geometric markers and proves that the possible \(T\)-intervals for distinct first-marker levels do not overlap. The critical inequality \(B^r-1\ge r(B-1/B)\) is proved from its base case and monotone increments, and the sole boundary exception \(B=r=2\) is explicitly enumerated. The complexity formula separately counts every admissible marker window using exact end-exclusive inequalities. Every singleton-marker factor and the all-zero factor is accounted for. The independent direct-scan implementation in `verify.py` corroborates the proof at 5,601,120 finite windows, but no finite enumeration is used as a substitute for proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk
Rigo–Stipulanti–Whiteland, *Characterizations of families of morphisms and words via binomial complexities* (arXiv:2201.04603, first public 2022-01-12), establishes 2-binomial faithfulness for Sturmian factors, examines binomial complexities under morphisms, and in §7.1 studies a sparse morphic word whose 2-binomial complexity is strictly below factor complexity. Its different sparse word requires binomial order three for full reconstruction. Neither that comparison nor the general morphism statements imply the geometric integer-power support classification here. Lejeune–Rigo–Rosenfeld's Tribonacci template theorem treats a primitive substitution word, not this nonrecurrent automatic family. Thue–Morse results concern a different word and often bounded binomial complexities. Relevant records for geometric-support words concern reflection complexity and sparse-marker bounds, not scattered-subword reconstruction. No equivalent or stronger coverage was located in the checked sources and mathematical records; an unindexed equivalent cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Binomial complexity is motivated by language-restricted subsequence reconstruction, and deciding when its order-two statistic recovers *all* factors is an explicitly studied structural question. This theorem answers it for every integer geometric base, shows that level two is sharp, and gives an exact computable formula for each length. Its aperiodic automatic examples form a distinct class from uniformly recurrent Sturmian/Tribonacci cases and contrast with the linear-gap sparse morphic example of Rigo et al., where order two fails. The value lies in a uniform, infinite, nontrivial reconstruction classification, not in a small numerical census or notation change.

## Remaining limitations and closest literature
The theorem does not classify all supports with two-binomial rigidity. The exact complexity uses a finite double sum, not a simpler digit formula. Its proof and program have not received independent scientific validation. The closest literature is arXiv:2201.04603 (Introduction, §7.1), the Tribonacci paper DOI:10.1016/j.aam.2019.101947 (definitions and template theorem), and the Thue–Morse study arXiv:1812.07330 (different automatic family). Search coverage cannot rule out obscure or unindexed equivalent proofs.
