# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The result concerns finite simple complete joins of at least two noncomplete graphs. For any nonedge inside a factor, every outside vertex is the internal vertex of a shortest path of length two. If a dual general-position set omits an entire factor, that path violates convexity of its complement. Since every factor therefore meets the chosen set, the same length-two obstruction forces both its selected and unselected parts to be cliques. The converse holds because cliques are simultaneously general-position and geodesically convex. Proper two-colorings of the complementary graph are exactly two-clique partitions. Connected bipartite components each have exactly two color orientations, including isolated vertices, proving the labelled cardinality polynomial and size bounds. Inclusion maximality follows by observing that only vertices isolated in the complement can be toggled without breaking proper two-coloring. These arguments use no unproved extrapolation or numerical assumptions. The attached verifier independently checked the exact defining geodesic condition on 1,085 join profiles and 139,216 subsets.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority

Tian and Klavžar, arXiv:2402.17338v1, Theorem 3.1, characterize dual general-position by convex complement, and their Proposition 3.5 proves the particular zero case for \(P_n\vee2K_1\), \(n\ge5\). Anand, Changat, Klavžar and Peterin (Graphs Combin. 28 (2012), Theorem 2.1) characterize proper convex sets of lexicographic products; their theorem supplies a strong pre-existing convexity ingredient, but not the full dual set-system classification, two-coloring polynomial, maximal-set formula, or the full complete-first-factor answer. Dokyeesun et al., arXiv:2408.15951v1, Section 5.2, treat dual general-position of lexicographic products but do not give the formula for \(K_m\circ H\). The journal version published online on 15 December 2025 explicitly identifies determining \(\operatorname{gp}_{\mathrm d}(K_m\circ G)\) as a remaining problem (Section 6). Targeted searches for equivalent formulations using joins, co-bipartite graphs, complement colorings, convex geodesic sets, and dual general-position did not reveal a statement covering this characterization. The closest published research-record comparison is a complete-multipartite case, which follows on taking edgeless factors but does not cover arbitrary noncomplete factors with internal edges. Absence of a search hit is not claimed as a proof of priority. The strongest risk is that the 2012 convexity theorem and the 2024 dual-position characterization may have been combined elsewhere without the specific terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result gives an exact all-set classification and complete weight polynomial for an arbitrary join of noncomplete graphs, together with the optimal size and its multiplicity, inclusion-maximal solution spectrum, and the complete one-vertex-toggle component structure. These are natural graph-geodesic invariants and solve the explicitly identified \(K_m\circ H\) complete-first-factor case, which was not determined in the closest paper. Its consequences are sensitive to the bipartite structure of the complement, not simply to graph order: \(K_m\circ P_5\) has no nonempty solutions, \(K_m\circ P_3\) has \(2^m\) maximum sets, and unbalanced complementary components yield different maximal sizes. The value lies in the unifying classification and the previously undetermined family, not in the formal act of multiplying independent two-coloring factors. Scope excludes complete factors in the general join theorem and infinite graphs.
