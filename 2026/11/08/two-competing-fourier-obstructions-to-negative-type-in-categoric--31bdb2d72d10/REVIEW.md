# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The full Fourier character decomposition reduces the powered-distance negative-type criterion to one scalar bipartition eigenvalue and one first-order Fourier block. Their roots, multiplicities, and the high-dimensional transition are proved by monotonicity, a one-turn derivative argument and a uniform Taylor expansion. Breadth-first graph distances and independent character sums were checked for 28 parameter/exponent cases; five endpoint multiplicities were also checked. The finite runs are corroboration only, not substitutes for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (bounded comparison)

Sánchez (2011), arXiv:1108.0451, gives general negative-type matrix methods and the complete-bipartite case. Murugan (2011), arXiv:1112.5657, provides an exact determinant criterion for vertex-transitive graphs. Neither inspected primary treatment states the present categorical-product roots, the two distinct critical eigenspaces, or the asymptotic constant. Targeted synonym and implication searches revealed no known covering result. The graph product and abelian Fourier diagonalization themselves are standard. A wider unlocated distance-regular graph theorem might contain special cases.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The complete structural answer concerns the natural Hilbert snowflake invariant of a bipartite categorical-product graph family, with explicit endpoint embedding dimensions. Its two spectral obstructions compete, and a sharp alphabet/dimension scaling transition changes the exponent by a factor of order the alphabet size. This gives geometric information across an infinite two-parameter family rather than an isolated calculation.

## Scope and limitations

Only equal alphabet sizes at least three and one binary factor are included. No claim is made for the critical asymptotic slope or for heterogeneous or weighted graph products. Historical priority remains subject to wider specialist verification.
