# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** In any semigroup with zero, a power-complete bijection must fix zero, and every nonzero element must have a nonzero prescribed power; this is verified directly from the injectivity of the product permutation. A zero sandwich entry produces nonzero square-zero elements. With full sandwich support, every nonzero Green group class has an explicitly computed group inverse; groupwise inversion is an involution and satisfies the defining two-complete identity. This proves both directions for all finite Rees zero-matrix semigroups, with a stronger sufficient assertion for arbitrary completely regular semigroups. Thirteen exact cases and four exhaustive order-five comparisons independently corroborate the boundary; no finite check is treated as a universal proof.

## Originality

**PASS under the investigated literature, with priority risk.** Araújo et al., arXiv:2608.25092v1, define higher-power complete mappings and leave their Rees zero-matrix classification as Problem 15.8. Their detailed Rees results in Sections 5–7 concern ordinary complete mappings; Proposition 6.5 determines ordinary Brandt mappings through Latin squares. The present two-complete if-and-only-if theorem is not implied by those existence conditions, since the source's sparse Brandt semigroups are ordinary-complete but fail the two-complete condition. Searches of relevant published mathematical research records and historical claims identified no statement with the same quantifiers, exponent, and Rees support condition. The inversion construction on groups is elementary and might have appeared separately; historical priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** This answers the first nontrivial exponent in an explicit recent classification problem for an entire standard class of completely zero-simple semigroups, with arbitrary finite groups and arbitrary regular sandwich patterns. It gives a sharp structural threshold: the transportation-pattern criterion for ordinary complete mappings collapses to full sandwich support at exponent two, and a universal explicit solution replaces the Hall–Paige complications on the positive side. The result also yields a natural constructive positive theorem for all completely regular semigroups. No claim is made about the general finite regular case or higher exponents.
