# Scientific review: complete-split stable-set covariance comparison

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns the standard-coordinate stable-set polytope \(\operatorname{STAB}(K_p\vee\overline{K_q})\) for \(p,q\ge2\). Its vertex set is exactly one \(q\)-cube and \(p\) extra clique-indicator vertices. For the volume law, the clique-coordinate sum has beta distribution with parameters \((p,q+1)\); conditional cube and simplex laws give the full covariance by total covariance. Both covariance matrices decompose into the two within-class contrast spaces and one explicit two-by-two constant-class block. Positivity and strict comparisons with the contrast eigenvalues identify the actual global extreme eigenvalues. Exact trace/determinant identities yield the two formulas, and their difference has the sign of the explicit cubic. The root-count proof uses three independent sign evaluations and the cubic degree; the limits use the exact two-by-two comparison expressions rather than unchecked numerical interpolation. The standalone exact-rational tests and independent low-dimensional volume triangulations support these calculations. The proof is analytical in all permitted dimensions.

The closest inspected foundational sources (Hu–Laurent, arXiv:1706.05496, and Aliniaeifard–Benedetti–Bergeron–Li–Saliola, arXiv:1804.00360) develop stable-set polytope geometry, facial constraints, edge structure, and extension complexity, rather than the comparison between vertex-uniform and volume-uniform covariance condition numbers. Related published geometric findings address minimum enclosing spheres and John ellipsoids of complete-multipartite stable-set polytopes; these are different extremal metrics and do not imply the two probability-measure covariances. A directly relevant earlier covariance comparison was not identified in the scientific literature and available indexed findings searched. The novelty claim is specifically the *exact two-measure comparison and its two sharply scaled reversals*, not the standard beta and Dirichlet moment identities separately. ## Mathematical value — PASS
Both sampling laws are canonical on the **same** naturally defined polytope. The exact comparison identifies a counterintuitive regime in which the volume law is more anisotropic despite the vertex law being exponentially concentrated on the cube component, flanked by two regimes of the opposite ordering. For each independent-class dimension at least four there are two algebraic transition roots, with nontrivial universal normalized limits \(1/3\) and \(3\). This supplies an invariant-level geometric distinction, not just a coefficient computation or a restatement of known stable-set counts. The metric specificity and historical risks are stated openly.
