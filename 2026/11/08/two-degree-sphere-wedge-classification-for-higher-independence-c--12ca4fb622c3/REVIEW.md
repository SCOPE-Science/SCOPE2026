# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The result follows from two explicit homotopy pushouts: first a double-star \(s\)-independence link is obtained by attaching the cone \((uv)*B\) along \(\partial(uv)*B\) to a contractible base, yielding \(\Sigma^2B\); second, deleting the middle vertex gives a join of two star complexes, and its link is the double-star complex with parameter \(r-1\). The inclusion from the link into the deletion is nullhomotopic by strict connectivity-versus-dimension inequalities, so the cofiber is their wedge after suspension. This proves both dimensions, multiplicities, boundary cases and integral torsion-freeness. An independently written full face/augmented boundary enumerator over \(\mathbb F_2\) checked 117 triples and 36,352 total nonempty faces with no mismatches. Finite tests alone do not prove the unrestricted claim.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Sawant (arXiv:2605.25150v1) proves vertex decomposability for all trees. Abdelmalek et al. (arXiv:2106.10863, Theorem 3.12) prove it for all caterpillars. Deshpande–Singh (arXiv:2001.05448, Theorems 3.8/3.10) give exact one-dimensional homotopy formulas for fully whiskered graphs; the middle core vertex here is unwhiskered, and the exhibited two-degree output for \(a,b\geq3\) is not covered. A similar-looking published directed-multipath formula for adjacent double stars has different simplicial vertices and is not an implication. The stated novel item is the explicit *once-subdivided double-star* integral two-degree homotopy formula, not vertex decomposability or the auxiliary sphere skeleton facts. Unindexed equivalent work remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The tree is the first symmetric three-core caterpillar obtained by subdividing the link between two leaf-bearing branching vertices. Its central unwhiskered vertex is exactly what prevents the existing fully-whiskered single-degree formula from applying. The exact result exposes a genuine two-degree sphere family, sometimes with widely separated dimensions, and gives closed Betti ranks and integral torsion-freeness for every parameter rather than only a qualitative wedge property; for instance \(\operatorname{Ind}_3(T_{3,3})\simeq S^2\vee S^5\). The natural subdivision perturbation has mathematically different topology and motivates this precise computation.

## Scope and limitations
The theorem does not extend automatically to longer core paths or to general caterpillars. The wedge splitting is ordinary, not equivariant. Exact finite-field computations are corroborative, while the homotopy proof is general. An equivalent publication not found by the investigated searches remains a possible priority risk.
