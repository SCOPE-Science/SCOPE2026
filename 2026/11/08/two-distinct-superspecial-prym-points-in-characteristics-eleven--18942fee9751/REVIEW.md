# Scientific review: two superspecial Prym points in characteristics eleven and thirteen

## Correctness — PASS
The genus-three model is smooth exactly when \(b(a^2-4b)\ne0\). The quotient by the sign involution has \(j(E)=0\), and the Prym/Jacobian splitting has degree a power of two, prime to both characteristics. The proof explicitly reconstructs the \(p^{-1}\)-linear Cartier coefficient extraction and all three basis images, rather than inferring geometric rank from finite point counts. At \(p=11\), the two Prym entries are proportional to \(a\) and the elliptic Cartier entry vanishes; at \(p=13\), the Prym entries share the factor \(a^2+5b\) and the elliptic entry is invertible. Superspeciality, Newton polygons and exact \(j\)-divisor orders follow. Both standalone Python checkers were executed successfully, with independent zeta calculations on all smooth prime-field coefficient pairs. Risk: the Hasse determinant is normalized in the specified differential basis; the stated divisor is the normalized coarse-parameter divisor, not an assertion about every compactification.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk
Laga–Shnidman (arXiv:2308.15297) already define the entire family, quotient and Prym, and record rational CM values \(j=1,-1\). Nourozi–Rahmati (arXiv:2306.07823) provide general Cartier machinery. Neither inspected source states the complete characteristic-eleven/thirteen Prym rank stratification and different normalized Hasse zero orders. As printed, Nourozi–Rahmati Theorem 3.1 claims constant \(a\)-number zero or one for such Picard curves with nonzero constant term, whereas our smooth examples give two and three; their general assertion therefore needs correction under its displayed hypotheses. Searches of published mathematical research records for the exact prime/family/coordinate descriptions returned no mathematically covering statements; nearby records concern unrelated genus-three monodromy or other varieties. An older Estrada-Sarlabous 1991 full text was inaccessible, so the result is not certified historically unique.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural marked bielliptic Picard family has quaternionic-multiplication Pryms. Locating exactly where these Pryms become superspecial at two good primes, identifying the corresponding distinct CM coordinates, and distinguishing simple/double Hasse zero orders from reduced matrix-rank loci are structural moduli/arithmetic facts, not arbitrary small finite enumerations. The two Newton-polygon descriptions give direct geometric consequences.
