# Two extra goods: the exact value-blind EFX lottery frontier

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge2\), consider \(m=n+2\) positive-value goods and \(n\) agents sharing one additive valuation. Among lotteries over complete recipient sequences that are independent of the goods' numerical values, the sharp worst-case exact EFX success probability is
\[
\boxed{R_n=\frac{1}{\binom{n+2}{3}+3\binom{n+2}{4}}=\frac{24}{(n+2)(n+1)n(3n+1)}.}
\]
It is achieved by choosing uniformly over all surjective recipient sequences (every agent receives at least one good). The lottery is irrevocable and ex-ante envy-free. In the first three cases \(R_2=1/7\), \(R_3=1/25\), and \(R_4=1/65\). In particular \(R_n\sim8/n^4\).

## Assumptions and scope
There are exactly \(n\ge2\) labeled agents and \(m=n+2\) labeled indivisible goods presented in a fixed order. Every agent values each good \(g\) at the same arbitrary strictly positive number \(v_g\), with additive bundle value. A complete allocation assigns every good irrevocably to exactly one agent. EFX means that for every two agents \(i,j\) and every good \(g\) in agent \(j\)'s bundle, \(v(A_i)\ge v(A_j\setminus\{g\})\). A value-blind lottery is an arbitrary probability distribution over recipient sequences of length \(m\), independent of the numerical vector \(v\). It may correlate recipients across arrivals; it may know \(n,m\) and previously chosen recipients, but does not condition decisions on revealed values. The adversary chooses the positive value vector before realization of this lottery. The comparison class includes all such lotteries, regardless of ex-ante fairness; the optimal one also has ex-ante fairness.

## Proof
**1. Only two occupancy shapes can succeed.** Because every good has positive value, EFX forbids any empty bundle: otherwise another bundle has at least two goods, and removing any one of them leaves positive value for the empty agent. With \(m=n+2\), the occupancy vector therefore consists of either one three-good bundle and \(n-1\) singletons (type T), or two two-good bundles and \(n-2\) singletons (type D).

**2. Exact EFX criterion for each shape.** Order the goods by nonincreasing values. Write the four smallest values as \(a\ge b\ge c\ge d>0\); all remaining goods, if any, have value at least \(a\). In type T, each singleton must have value at least the sum of the two largest goods of the triple, because the least-valued good may be removed. Therefore the triple must consist of the three smallest goods and the EFX condition is exactly \(a\ge b+c\), provided these values are distinct. For tied values there may be additional qualifying triples, but the particular bottom-three choice still works whenever the inequality holds. No other comparisons restrict EFX: a triple-holder does not envy a singleton after the singleton is removed, and singleton-to-singleton comparisons are vacuous after removal.

In type D, every singleton must have value at least each individual member of either double bundle. Hence, with distinct positive values, the four paired goods are precisely the four smallest. Each of the three unordered pairings is EFX precisely when both pair sums dominate the larger entry in the opposite pair. Of the pairings \((a,b)|(c,d)\), \((a,c)|(b,d)\), and \((a,d)|(b,c)\), their conditions reduce, respectively, to \(a\le c+d\), \(a\le b+d\), and \(a\le b+c\). The other direction of each comparison is automatic because \(a\ge b\ge c\ge d\). If \(a<b+c\), at least the last pairing is EFX. Again, ties can only create more successful patterns, not invalidate this chosen pairing.

**3. A universal lower bound.** There are \(n!\binom m3\) type-T surjections: choose the three-good subset and assign the \(n\) resulting unlabeled bundles bijectively to agents. There are \(3n!\binom m4\) type-D surjections: choose four goods, pair them in one of three ways, and assign the resulting \(n\) unlabeled bundles to agents. Let \(H=\binom m3+3\binom m4\). Thus there are exactly \(n!H\) surjective recipient sequences. If \(a\ge b+c\), the unique choice of the three lowest-valued goods as the triple yields at least \(n!\) EFX sequences (arbitrary agent permutation). Otherwise \(a<b+c\) and pairing \((a,d)|(b,c)\) among the bottom four yields at least \(n!\) EFX sequences. Consequently the uniform surjection lottery succeeds with probability at least \(n!/(n!H)=1/H\), for all positive value vectors. Symmetry among agents gives each good marginal recipient probability \(1/n\), establishing ex-ante envy-freeness. Sampling the recipient sequence first implements the rule online.

**4. Sharpness against every value-blind lottery.** Symmetrize an arbitrary lottery by independently applying uniform random permutations to good positions and agent labels. This cannot decrease its worst-case success probability: for each value vector the new success probability averages the original probabilities across all relabelings of that vector. Under the symmetrized lottery, let \(t\) and \(d_0\) denote the total probabilities of the T and D shapes. Other shapes never satisfy EFX and \(t+d_0\le1\). Conditional on shape, relabeling symmetry makes every corresponding surjection equiprobable.

Choose the four smallest values as \((a,b,c,d)=(10,4,3,1)\) and any remaining values as distinct numbers greater than \(10\). Here \(a>b+c\); exactly one type-T choice of goods succeeds and no type-D choice does. The success probability of the symmetrized lottery is \(t/\binom m3\). Next choose the four smallest values \((6,4,3,1)\), and any remaining values distinct and greater than \(6\). Then \(b+d<a<b+c\); no type-T choice succeeds, and exactly one of the three pairings of the four smallest goods succeeds. The success probability is \(d_0/(3\binom m4)\). Hence the worst-case success is at most
\[
\min\left\{\frac{t}{\binom m3},\frac{d_0}{3\binom m4}\right\}\le\frac{1}{\binom m3+3\binom m4},
\]
by \(t+d_0\le1\). The uniform surjection lottery attains equality by step 3. This also verifies both worst-case witnesses, the endpoint \(n=2\) (there are no singleton bundles in type D), and all equality conditions.

## Relationship to prior work
Neoh and Teh, *Understanding EFX Allocations: Counting and Variants* (arXiv:2504.03951, Theorem 3.13), prove a universal \(n!\) minimum count for EFX allocations under identical valuations, but do not optimize worst-case value-independent online lotteries with two extra goods. Cohen, Teh and Wooldridge, *Online Fair Division under an Oblivious Adversary* (arXiv:2609.28333, Theorem 5.4 and Appendix D.5), quantify randomized EFX success with one extra good and allow arbitrary heterogeneous valuations. They sample balanced allocations for their guarantee; their theorem does not state the two-extra-good minimax frontier or its sharp adversarial dual witnesses. Neoh and Teh, *Online Fair Division with Frequency Predictions* (arXiv:2610.34625), address online guarantees under additional information, not the value-blind stochastic minimax quantity here. The present proof explicitly uses their background notions and separates a narrower value-blind information model from their more general algorithms.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to common strictly positive additive values, exactly two extra goods, and decisions independent of all numerical values. It does not extend the sharp value to heterogeneous agents, valuations that can be observed and exploited online, zero-valued goods, or more extra goods. The exact finite formula is proved, but independent expert validation has not been performed. An equivalent unindexed formulation may exist. The proof is self-contained, while its scientific interpretation builds on prior EFX-counting and online-randomization work.
