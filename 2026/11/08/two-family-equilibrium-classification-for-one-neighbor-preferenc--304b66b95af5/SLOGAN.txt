For an anonymous cycle-allocation game in which every agent wants exactly one occupied neighbor, unrestricted-jump equilibria consist precisely of separated occupied dimers or isolated vacancies between occupied runs of length at least two, apart from the zero-, one-, and full-occupancy cases. This yields a complete binomial census and a sharp density \(2/3\) boundary: below it, every nontrivial odd-sized population has no equilibrium; at the boundary only three periodic occupancy patterns survive.
