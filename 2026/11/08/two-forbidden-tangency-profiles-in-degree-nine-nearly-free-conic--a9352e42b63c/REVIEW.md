# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The published lower bound on the minimal Jacobian syzygy degree and the standard near-freeness criterion force \(r=4\), \(\tau=47\) and precisely four numerically feasible tangency profiles. Bézout fixes component-pair multiplicities. For one tangency, the zero-node condition forces a six-cycle of conic triple incidences between two concurrent triples of secants; an exact chord-normalization calculation shows the forced matching line is a secant and cannot be the prescribed tangent. For two tangencies, line-pair incidence and parity forbid the unique possible distribution. Every implication is proved without replacing infinite geometry by a finite enumeration. A reproducible standalone verifier confirms the incidence constraints and exact cyclotomic identity.

## Originality
PASS under inspected-source and record comparisons, with residual historical-priority uncertainty. Gałecka's 2022 paper establishes the numerical constraints and leaves degree nine undecided, rather than excluding one- and two-tangency configurations. Măcinic–Pokora's defect-two plus-one-generated classification is not the defect-one near-freeness problem. Pokora's free ordinary-multiplicity analysis does not include the present tacnode restriction. The closest published mathematical-record example is a degree-eight nearly free arrangement and does not imply the universal degree-nine exclusion. A classical Pascal or involution reference could contain the geometric lemma in another language, but the application to the weak-combinatorics obstruction was not found. No claim of exhaustive priority certification is made.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The degree-nine existence/classification problem is explicitly left open in the motivating paper. The theorem eliminates two entire weak-combinatorics candidates that survive the standard numerical tests, cutting the one-conic, seven-line nearly free search from four patterns to two. The one-tangency obstruction uses a structural geometric mechanism, not a routine substitution or isolated coordinate example; the two-tangency obstruction is a sharp incidence decomposition. This does not solve the remaining cases and the reduction is not inflated into nonexistence.
