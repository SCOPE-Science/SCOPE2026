For smooth strictly convex ovals sampled with one boundary-density zero of order \(\alpha>0\), the worst Hausdorff error has an exact inhomogeneous-Poisson gap limit at scale \(n^{-2/(\alpha+1)}\). Missing area instead has a sharp transition at \(\alpha=1/2\), from a deterministic \(n^{-2}\) law through a logarithmic critical law to a Poisson cubic-gap sum, jointly coupled to the Hausdorff error.
