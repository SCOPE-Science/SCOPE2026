# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns the two exact very affine surfaces of the motivating construction, not arbitrary smooth toric charts. For both, the four deleted divisor curves are classified explicitly; each zero curve is isomorphic either to \(\mathbb C^*\) or to \(\mathbb C^*\setminus\{-1\}\). Every pairwise intersection is enumerated within the torus and there are no triple intersections. Inclusion–exclusion gives \(\chi=5\) and \(\chi=4\). The source's unimodular torus parameter change identifies generic Cox logarithmic forms with generic forms \(d\log(x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}A^{\gamma}B^{\delta}C^{\epsilon}H^{\zeta})\), so the established ML-degree theorem supplies the generic scattering counts. Exact saturated Gröbner calculations give an independent reduced \(5\)-point and \(4\)-point specialization. Assumptions include base field \(\mathbb C\), the full divisor complement, and generic parameters; no extrapolation from finite samples is made.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

Calvo Cortes–Telen, Example 4.2, give the shared fan and both charts, including their affine embedding degrees \(10\) and \(7\); their Section 5 gives a general \(\chi\)-to-scattering-count reduction, evaluating a *pentagonal* example, not the two hexagonal characteristics. The representation-theoretic predecessor (Arkani-Hamed–Frost–Plamondon–Salvatori–Thomas, Example 13.2) supplies the first chart's equations but does not state these counts. Huh's general theorem is a crucial cited dependency, not a new claim. Published-literature and indexed-result searches using the exact four polynomials, their aliases, degree/ML-degree wording, and both source identities did not reveal an equivalent \(5/4\) classification; this is evidence only, not an exhaustive historical-priority guarantee. A broader arrangement or matroid calculation under different terminology could preempt the specific values.

## Value — PASS

Both positive charts arise from the same six-ray smooth hexagonal fan, and the motivating paper explicitly notes that even their binary presentations have identical combinatorics. The actual generic scattering counts are nevertheless different. The numerical invariants are naturally selected by the source's algebraic moment-map problem, not by an artificial parameter slice. The result supplies a concrete obstruction to recovering scattering complexity from the fan alone, and distinguishes the two associated very affine surfaces intrinsically via their Euler characteristics. Its content goes beyond the source's two affine embedding degrees.
