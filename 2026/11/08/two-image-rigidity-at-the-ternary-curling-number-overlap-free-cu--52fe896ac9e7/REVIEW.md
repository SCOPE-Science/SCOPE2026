# Scientific review of the terminal curling-image theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is finite and completely enumerated. The suffix-overlap equivalence and cubefree square-suffix rule justify every extension and pruning operation. The complete labeled and symmetry-reduced searches produce identical terminal transform fibers of sizes 4032 and 2016; the latter regenerates and byte-compares all 1008 canonical rows. All terminal representatives have three symbols, so multiplication by six is exact. The computation establishes no unsupported infinite extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest primary result is Caveney–Dong–Shallit, arXiv:2608.15670v1, Theorem 7 and Appendix B: it gives the length-84 maximum, the total 6048 admissible sources, and an example, but no transform-image/fiber distribution. Its numerical total cannot imply the split into exactly two output images, and its infinite constructions address different alphabets or repetition constraints. Searches for the equivalent square-suffix indicator formulation and for distinct images/fiber sizes found no covering statement. Earlier curling-number literature concerns other recurrence statistics. There is a residual priority risk from unindexed work or supplementary data not presenting the result in the primary text.

## Value — PASS

The exact image/fiber spectrum is a natural measure of inverse ambiguity for the curling-number transform at a proven, non-arbitrary extremal boundary. The collapse of 1008 nonisomorphic valid sources into only two output traces with a nonuniform 2:1 fiber ratio is a qualitative rigidity result not conveyed by the already known count 6048. The finding is a self-contained complete finite classification, not a proposed generalization or a mere recount of source words.

## Scope and remaining limits

This is an exhaustive finite result at length 84. The proof does not characterize arbitrary lengths or alphabets. The reproducible enumerators are proof certificates subject to independent scrutiny, not independent formal verification.
