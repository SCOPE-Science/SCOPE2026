# Scientific review: two dyadic degenerations of the Klein twelve-line divisor

## Correctness (C): PASS

The claim concerns an explicit relative degree-12 Cartier divisor over the Dedekind ring \(R=\mathbb Z[a]/(a^2+a+2)\). All twelve factors have a unit coefficient, so no fiber of the defining polynomial is identically zero. Torsion-freeness over every localization at a nonzero prime gives flatness. Reconstructing all \(\binom{12}{3}=220\) determinants in the exact quadratic integer ring gives zero-norm count 12 and the nonzero counts \(56,84,64,4\) at norms \(8,16,32,64\). Every pair has a nonzero determinant with a third line; zero triples meet pairwise in at most one line. Since the nonzero determinants have 2-power norms, the twelve triples, thirty doubles and line distinctness persist at every odd prime, including seven. Direct substitution at both primes over two independently checks the two nonreduced fibers. The arithmetic genera and nilpotent Euler characteristics follow from the Cartier-divisor exact sequence. These statements were all checked against the actual attached source and the executed standalone script; neither finite sample counts nor an inaccessible algebra system are needed to establish the uniform claim.

## Originality (O): PASS, with priority uncertainty

The complex \((12_3)\) configuration and its \(12\) triple and \(30\) double points are Gévay–Pokora's Proposition 2.1, not a new result. Their complete accessible preprint does not assert the characteristic-uniform determinant certificate or classify the two different dyadic special fibers. The Bauer–Di Rocco–Harbourne–Huizenga–Seceleanu–Szemberg manuscript concerns the full twenty-one-line configuration and different characteristic-seven issues. The accessible sections of Innamorati's article discuss characteristic-seven Klein configurations and characteristic-two Ceva configurations, not these dyadic fibers; full-text access to this article was blocked. A separately documented thirty-three-line Galois-closure calculation has a different arrangement and a different bad-prime profile. It does not imply the twelve-line subarrangement has no odd bad prime; the present result separately calculates all 220 triple determinants. Targeted searches returned no equivalent statement. This evidence supports best-knowledge originality, not an exhaustive claim of historical priority.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

The chosen twelve-line Klein subarrangement was explicitly singled out as a natural symmetric geometric \((12_3)\) configuration; classifying *all* primes preserving that matroid and the exact nonreduced fibers at the exceptional primes is an intrinsic reduction question for its specified integral realization. The two primes over two yield inequivalent limits (three concurrent multiplicity-four lines versus one multiplicity-twelve line), a contrast not implied by the complex incidence basket. Constant arithmetic genus and differing reduced genera quantify the degeneration. The all-odd-prime guarantee, including the ramified prime seven, requires and is supported by the exact exhaustive norm certificate. The result does not claim a general theory of modular configuration degeneration or projective-model invariance.
