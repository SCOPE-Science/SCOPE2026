# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Smoothness follows from the smooth base conic, the étale power map off the coordinate axes, and distinct transverse axis intersections. A symbolic Hessian factorization identifies the full divisor; a nonzero discriminant of the explicit cubic gives six reduced base residual intersections and exactly \(6n^2\) ordinary flexes. Direct tangent restriction gives exact contact \(n\) at all \(6n\) axis points. The independent weight sum equals the classical Hessian intersection degree. The standalone exact-arithmetic verifier checks the identities and representative values.

## Originality
PASS, with historical-priority limitations. Arakelian–Borges, arXiv:1409.8549, Section 4, construct these conic pullback families and already establish existence of tangent contact \(n\) at some coordinate point: that antecedent is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. Their text does not classify the entire line-inflection scheme or two orbits. Hidalgo and coauthors, arXiv:1409.3063, Theorem 17, study hyperosculation in a distinct complete-intersection embedding of generalized Fermat covers and therefore do not imply the complete plane-embedding statement here. Targeted mathematical-record, exact-expression and primary-literature searches found no dominating statement. Undiscovered specialist accounts remain possible.

## Value
PASS. Complete inflection divisors and contact-order distributions are natural projective invariants; an unbounded family with arbitrarily high axis contact has a sharp, unusually simple two-orbit classification. The explicit residual cubic and absence of any other higher contacts give a structural calculation useful for studying automorphisms and dual-curve singularities. This value does not rest merely on rederiving the known existence of contact \(n\).

## Limitations
Characteristic zero and the single symmetric coefficient choice are essential to the stated scope. The classification is not claimed for arbitrary smooth conic pullbacks or other embeddings. The historical-priority comparison may miss equivalent earlier accounts. Finite symbolic test cases complement, rather than replace, the all-\(n\) proof.
