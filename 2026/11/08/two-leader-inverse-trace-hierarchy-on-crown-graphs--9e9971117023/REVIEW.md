# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem defines the unweighted crown graph, exactly two clamped leaders, and the unscaled grounded-Laplacian trace. Connectedness and grounding ensure invertibility. A singular-value decomposition of a two-block grounded Laplacian reduces all pair placements to three exact singular-value spectra. The matched, same-class and adjacent-cross-class formulas are obtained with algebraic denominators positive for every \(m\ge4\). The strict differences are explicit positive rational functions, proving all globally optimal leader sets and their count. A separate exact-rational matrix inversion script checks all 365 possible pairs on \(m=3,\ldots,8\), nine larger representatives and 148 gap identities. The infinite proof does not depend on the finite checks.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

The closest general works are Lin–Fardad–Jovanović, arXiv:1302.0450 (general grounded-Laplacian leader optimization), Fitch–Leonard, arXiv:1407.1569 (joint centrality, cycle/path special cases), and Patterson–McGlohon–Dyagilev, arXiv:1412.6595 (weighted paths/cycles). Their full accessible texts were inspected in relevant formulations, optimization sections, case studies and conclusions. None establishes the three exact crown-graph pair trace formulas or proves their strict ordering. The special six-cycle is excluded. A complete-bipartite formula does not imply a matching-deleted crown result, because the graph and grounded spectra change. Closest separately indexed crown-graph perfect-Roman and restrained-domination results select the same vertex-pair orbit for different combinatorial problems but do not imply the inverse-trace objective or its quantitative gaps.

Relevant searches in primary literature and mathematical-result indices were conducted for crown graphs, matched-pair leaders, bipartite-double-cover interpretations, and exact pairwise coherence. Search nonmatches do not prove novelty; inaccessible or unindexed specialist work remains a publication-priority uncertainty.

## Value — PASS

The two-leader budget is the smallest budget that distinguishes pair geometry in this vertex-transitive graph, where all individual vertices have equal standing. The result supplies a complete exact design rule, counts every optimizer, and distinguishes **three** performance classes despite equal vertex degrees: it is strictly better to ground both ends of a *missing edge* than two same-side vertices, and strictly worse to ground the ends of an actual cross-edge. The order-\(1/m\) penalties quantify the loss from the wrong pair geometry. This is a natural complete classification for a fixed, inexpensive sensing budget, not an arbitrary parameter slice or numerical census.

## Scientific limits

Only two unweighted noise-free leaders and \(m\ge4\) are treated. No claim is made for arbitrary budgets, weighted crowns or noisy-leader dynamics. Exact finite verification supplements rather than replaces the proof, and no external independent validation has occurred.
