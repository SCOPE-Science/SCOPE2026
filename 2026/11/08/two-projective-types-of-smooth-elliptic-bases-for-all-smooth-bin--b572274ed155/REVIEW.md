# Scientific review: smooth bases of binary quadric pencils

## Correctness
PASS. The finite state space is fully parameterized by ten coefficients of a binary quadric and the polar Pfaffian; the stand-alone verifier checks the smoothness predicate against independent matrix-rank arithmetic for all 1,024 forms. It enumerates all 14,336 eligible pencils in two ways, generates the complete rational projective group using the twelve standard transvections, and verifies all five orbit sizes and proves from explicit matrices that the two four-element stabilizers have distinct isomorphism types. Four affine-chart Gröbner unit ideals for each of the two candidate representatives prove geometric smoothness of both bases. Hasse's bound rules out smoothness of the remaining 0- and 6-point bases. Extension point counts independently support the genus-one Frobenius traces. Computation was rerun from the supplied verifier and completed with the PASS signature.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the documented residual literature risk. The motivating 2022 source constructs all-smooth pencils but does not give the two smooth-base projective orbits or their point counts and stabilizers. The highly relevant 1988 Bruen–Hirschfeld abstract describes a classification for **non-absolutely-irreducible** base curves, whereas the present main theorem addresses geometrically smooth, hence absolutely irreducible, genus-one bases. That source's full text was not accessible: this is an explicit priority risk rather than a claim that all its content was examined. The characteristic-zero classification of Fevola–Mandelshtam–Sturmfels and the finite-geometry material on disjoint elliptic quadrics have no direct implication for the two smooth-base orbit counts. Search comparisons found no exact or stronger published small-field table in the material inspected; negative search results alone do not certify uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Smooth complete intersections of two quadrics are a fundamental realization of genus-one curves. The natural question arising from the published existence construction is how many fully smooth pencils can have a smooth base and what arithmetic types those bases realize. The exact census proves a complete classification under rational projective equivalence, not merely isolated examples: the two orbits have different Frobenius polynomials and nonisomorphic order-four stabilizers (Klein versus cyclic). It also separates the smooth-base sector from the previously studied degenerate sector. This is a naturally bounded finite classification, not an arbitrary coefficient slice.
