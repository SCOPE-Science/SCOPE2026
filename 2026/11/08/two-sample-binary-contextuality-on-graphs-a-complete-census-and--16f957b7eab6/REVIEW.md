# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** At denominator two, a binary edge table with fair endpoints must be exactly the equal-pair or unequal-pair distribution; other tables are determined by endpoint marginal types. This bijection gives the first count. Balanced signings are exactly coboundaries and are simultaneously necessary and sufficient for a global probability distribution, yielding the second count, the binary contextual-fraction dichotomy, and the cycle/girth corollaries. The denominator-three four-cycle witness is explicitly normalized and no-disturbance, a mixture with local mass one third; global-support constraints prove the upper bound. The standalone checker enumerates all possible two-sample edge tables independently for five small graphs. The derivation applies to arbitrary finite simple graphs and handles isolated vertices algebraically; finite enumeration does not itself certify the unrestricted quantifier.

## Originality

**PASS within inspected evidence.** The 2026 polyteam paper (arXiv:2611.00814v1, Sections 1–3, including weighted-polyteam definitions and contextuality formulations) provides the mathematical motivation but not this complete two-sample all-graph census or the sharp denominator-three threshold. The earlier XOR/signed-graph source (arXiv:1511.05415v1, signed-graph discussion) already contains the balance/switching principle: this ingredient is credited and is not claimed new. The classic global-section account (arXiv:1102.0264) is broader in objects but does not state these finite-denominator counts. The quantitative contextual-fraction work (arXiv:2609.20281) studies different higher-arity relational forms and capacities. Targeted literature and research-record queries uncovered no equivalent complete statement. **Risk:** the enumeration may occur as a known specialization of a graphical partition polynomial or in less indexed Bell-contextuality tables; historical priority is not independently certified.

## Value

**PASS.** The exact count on every finite compatibility graph is a natural, reusable finite-resolution invariant for the database/weighted-polyteam approach. The structural dichotomy gives a sharp qualitative obstruction to intermediate contextuality and identifies the first sample precision where it disappears. The formulas capture topology through cycle rank and girth, and recover the exact CHSH finite two-sample census without assuming a special symmetry or relying on individual polytope vertices.

## Limits

This is a result for binary outcomes, pairwise graph contexts, normalized two-sample probability tables and ordinary probability-global-section noncontextuality, not a quantum-realizability theorem. Its signed-graph criterion is previously known; a prior source containing the entire count would defeat the originality claim. Independent mathematical and priority checks remain outstanding.
