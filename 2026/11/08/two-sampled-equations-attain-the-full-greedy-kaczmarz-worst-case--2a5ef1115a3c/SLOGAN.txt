For a regular-simplex system in every dimension, uniformly sampling just two distinct equations and projecting onto the larger residual achieves exactly the same sharp worst-case one-step squared-error contraction as checking every equation. In odd dimensions, even full greediness cannot improve that worst-case guarantee over one uniformly sampled equation; in even dimensions, the optimal decrement jumps from \(1/n\) to \((n+1)/n^2\) at sample size two and never increases afterward.
