# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** For every fixed \(0<q<1\), \(\theta\in\mathbb R\), and the stated mass scalings, the directed constant-rate zero-range ring has the canonical product weights and the exact two-defect convolution given in the result. Summing stationary inflows verifies these weights directly. The partition-ratio flux identity is exact; a geometric law of large numbers and central limit theorem, a local central limit theorem with controlled tails, and Riemann sums prove both limiting split densities and both signed current corrections. The checker independently enumerates small configurations and confirms the finite laws and directional numerical convergence. Normalization, integer rounding, the case \(\theta=0\), finite-size boundary occupations and positivity are explicitly covered; infinite results do not rely on enumeration. No dynamics or alternative scaling is asserted.

**Originality: PASS on examined scientific evidence, with historical priority risk.** Earlier zero-range work gives the product stationary measure and the canonical partition method. Angel–Evans–Mukamel (2004) use a single defect; Evans–Majumdar–Zia (2005) analyze factorized canonical condensation; Schwarzkopf–Evans–Mukamel (2008) obtain multiple condensates through occupancy-dependent cutoffs; Mailler–Mörters–Ueltschi (2015) study random disorder; the available Rafferty (2016) summaries mention equal-rate double defects and dynamical properties. These results do not, as inspected, state the joint finite-bias \(L^{-1/2}\) critical and \(L^{-1}\) supercritical splitting laws together with stationary-current corrections. The complete 2016 dissertation could not be reliably read, and unindexed equivalent work cannot be excluded. Absence from search results does not prove global priority.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** Competing defect sites in a conserving interacting-particle transport model raise an interpretable condensate-sharing question. The analysis finds two genuinely different and physically specified vanishing perturbation scales, an explicit two-dimensional non-Gaussian critical law, an extensive condensed tilted-uniform law, and the first current corrections. These identify a finite-size crossover that the unperturbed product formula alone does not present as an evaluated result. The contributions are narrow, stationary, and model-specific, but naturally motivated and checkable; no wider theorem is claimed.

All reviewed evidence is limited to the named scientific sources and reproducible finite computations. The historical residual risk is especially the incompletely accessible 2016 thesis and unindexed equivalent canonical analyses.
