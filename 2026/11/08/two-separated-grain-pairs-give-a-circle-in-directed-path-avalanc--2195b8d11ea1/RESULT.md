# Two separated grain-pairs give a circle in directed-path avalanche topology

## Finding

Let \(g\ge 1\) and \(t\ge 0\) be integers, and let \(n=g+4+t\). Consider the directed path on \(n\) firing vertices \(v_0\to v_1\to\cdots\to v_{n-1}\), followed by an absorbing sink \(s\), with initial binary sandpile configuration
\[
 c_0=(1,1,\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{g},1,1,\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{t}).
\]
Let \(\mathcal A(n,c_0)\) be the simplicial avalanche complex generated by all simultaneous firing sets, with the sink excluded. Then \(\mathcal A(n,c_0)\) admits a sequence of exactly \(7+5t\) elementary simplicial collapses onto a simple polygon on \(g+2\) vertices. In particular,
\[
\mathcal A(n,c_0)\simeq S^1,\qquad
\widetilde H_j(\mathcal A(n,c_0);\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z&j=1,\\0&j\ne1.\end{cases}
\]
The homotopy type is independent of both the separation \(g\) and the trailing-zero length \(t\).

As a consequence, a single extra grain can eliminate arbitrarily many independent integral first-homology classes: for the same path, changing the rightmost grain of the second adjacent pair from one to zero, so that the initial nonzero positions are only \(0,1,g+2\), gives a wedge of \(t+2\) circles by Riihimäki--Smith, Proposition 3.8. Replacing that zero by one yields the single circle proved here. The all-consecutive four-grain case \(g=0\) is contractible by their consecutive-block lemma, so the separation threshold is sharp.

## Assumptions and scope

The path edges all point right and the final sink absorbs grains without firing. Each non-sink vertex has out-degree one. At every discrete time step **all** currently unstable non-sink vertices fire simultaneously, where instability means having at least one grain. The simplex set is the downward closure of the nonempty firing sets; neither repeated firing times nor the empty set creates additional vertices. The convention agrees with Definitions 2.1--2.3 of the cited 2026 paper. The theorem concerns finite paths, binary initial configurations of the exact displayed form, and ordinary integral simple-homotopy type. No statement is made for more than two grains per block or for directed cycles.

Write \(L=g+2\), so \(L\ge3\) and \(n=L+2+t\). A vertex index \(j\) below abbreviates \(v_j\).

## Proof

**1. Firing sets.** Since there is exactly one directed incoming edge to each firing vertex except the first, and the initial entries are binary, the parallel dynamics simply translates the indicator vector one place right at each step, discarding the last grain into the sink. In particular the state remains binary. The complete list of nonempty firing sets is
\[
 \sigma_r^{(n)}=\{r+a:\ a\in\{0,1,L,L+1\},\ r+a<n\},\qquad 0\le r<n.
\]
Let \(K_n\) be their downward-closed simplicial complex. It is exactly \(\mathcal A(n,c_0)\).

**2. Uniform removal of trailing zeros.** For each \(m\ge L+3\), inspect the link of the last vertex \(v_{m-1}\) in \(K_m\). The only firing times containing that vertex are
\(m-L-2\), \(m-L-1\), \(m-2\), and \(m-1\).
After removing \(v_{m-1}\), their respective other vertices form
\[
\{m-L-2,m-L-1,m-2\},\quad
\{m-L-1,m-L\},\quad
\{m-2\},\quad \varnothing.
\]
The last two sets are contained in the first set or contribute nothing. Thus the link is the union of a triangle and an edge meeting at the **cone vertex** \(v_{m-L-1}\). A vertex whose link is a cone is simplicially dominated and can be removed by elementary collapses: pair every simplex containing \(v_{m-1}\) but not \(v_{m-L-1}\) with its coface obtained by adjoining that cone vertex, proceeding from larger simplices to smaller ones. The link above has three vertices and one edge not containing the apex, plus the empty face, so this uses exactly **five elementary pairs**.

Deletion of \(v_{m-1}\) leaves \(K_{m-1}\), since restricting each displayed firing set to the first \(m-1\) vertices produces precisely the corresponding firing set for the shorter path. Repeating for \(m=n,n-1,\ldots,L+3\) gives \(K_n\searrow K_{L+2}\) using \(5t\) pairs.

**3. The base complex and its polygon.** For \(n=L+2\) the maximal generating simplices are the tetrahedral simplex
\[
T=\{0,1,L,L+1\},
\]
the triangle \(U=\{1,2,L+1\}\), and the consecutive edges \(\{j,j+1\}\) for \(2\le j\le L\). (The terminal singleton is already a face.) Vertex \(0\) lies only in \(T\), and its link is a simplex with apex \(1\). Deleting \(0\) requires four elementary pairs and replaces \(T\) with its face \(\{1,L,L+1\}\).

The edge \(\{1,L\}\) is now free in the triangle \(\{1,L,L+1\}\); collapse that pair. Next \(\{1,2\}\) is free in \(U\); collapse that pair. Finally \(1\) is a leaf incident only to \(\{1,L+1\}\); collapse that vertex-edge pair. Exactly seven elementary pairs have been used in the base case. The remaining complex consists of the vertices \(2,3,\ldots,L+1\), their consecutive edges, and the edge \(\{2,L+1\}\), which is the boundary of an \(L\)-gon (also valid for \(L=3\)). Hence
\[
K_n\searrow C_L\simeq S^1,
\]
with precisely \(5t+7\) elementary pairs. All assertions are integral and apply for every stated \(g,t\). \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work

Riihimäki and Smith introduced avalanche complexes and classified several path and cycle configurations. Their consecutive-block lemma makes the zero-gap four-grain configuration contractible. Their Proposition 3.8 treats the three-grain support \(\{0,1,g+2\}\) and proves \(\bigvee^{t+2}S^1\) on a path of \(g+4+t\) firing vertices. The additional fourth grain changes the generating simplices, so neither statement implies the present uniform circle collapse. This result gives an explicit all-parameter simple-homotopy classification of two separated adjacent pairs, together with an arbitrarily large one-grain reduction in first-homology rank.

A published result on arithmetic transitions in residue-class avalanche complexes concerns **directed cycles**, not the absorbing directed paths and four-grain binary support here. No relevant source examined establishes or dominates the displayed two-pair theorem; unindexed equivalent work is a remaining literature risk.

## Limitations

The conclusion is restricted to the indicated right-directed path with a terminal sink, synchronous firing, two separated runs of exactly two initial grains, and arbitrary trailing zeros. It does not classify general binary strings, unsynchronized firing, undirected sandpiles, or persistent barcodes. The proof supplies an explicit simple-homotopy collapse but not an equivariant statement. Historical originality cannot be established solely by negative searches; independent review remains useful.
