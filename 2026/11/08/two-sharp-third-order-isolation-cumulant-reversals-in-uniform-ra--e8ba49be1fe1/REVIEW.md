# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Counting mutual avoidance of three marked key rings gives the exact triple moment, while centered-product expansion gives the cumulant. Strict concavity of the logarithm of the avoidance ratios guarantees the convex two-exponential reduction. The expansion in inverse pool size explicitly cancels constant and linear terms, leaving a factored quadratic coefficient with two distinct positive roots. The supplied checker passed 403 independent exact-rational and high-precision assertions. Finite tests are evidence of reproducibility, not infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** The primary 2008 Yağan–Makowski paper (sections V, VIII–X) gives first and second isolation moments and Poisson/zero-one laws. The later arXiv:0908.3644 connectivity paper examines the same model and uses first/second moments but does not state the present third distinct-indicator connected cumulant or two-scale sign reversal. Searches of prior mathematical research records found no equivalent evaluated polynomial profile or covering theorem. A generic higher-factorial-moment result could implicitly contain the identity; this remains a real priority risk and is not excluded by non-matching titles.

**Value — PASS.** The connected third-order dependence of three marked isolated vertices is a canonical statistic for understanding non-Gaussian fluctuations of the isolated-vertex count. Its sign reverses twice at natural proportional pool/vertex scales even though the first/second isolation moments are familiar. The sharp thresholds identify where the third-order contribution changes direction and constitute a structural phase classification, not an arbitrary finite computation. The theorem is about this joint cumulant, not the total-count skewness or connectivity transition.
