# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Two independent exhaustive constructions agree on the complete face sets. Acyclicity of every proposed Morse matching is checked by a topological sort of the complete directed Hasse graph, not inferred from a partial sample. The critical-cell patterns reduce each complex to a simply connected two-skeleton plus three-cells. Exact signed recursive cancellation of all matched two-three pairs gives a one-element Smith diagonal of 2. This proves the Moore-space splittings by the Hurewicz theorem and elementary changes of attaching-map bases. Independent simplicial field homology over characteristics 2, 3 and 5 agrees with the resulting integral torsion. A finite calculation is an exhaustive proof here because the claims concern only two fixed finite complexes.

## Originality
PASS on accessible evidence, with residual risk. The motivating 2026 bidirectional polygon paper covers paths and cycles, not multiply cyclic bidirected complete bipartite digraphs. The 2022 complete-bipartite Euler calculation is for one-way orientations, not these symmetric directed graphs. Indexed related records on double stars and on matching/Morse complexes do not imply this claim because their directed graph or face definitions differ. Specific exact-name, alias, torsion, and stronger-coverage comparisons found no covering theorem. The existence of torsion in multipath complexes in general was known and is not claimed here; equivalent unindexed work remains possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The two smallest studied widths exhibit the same degree-two Moore summand but different free-sphere dimensions and multiplicities. This gives concrete integral homotopy models beyond a field-Betti or Euler-characteristic table and demonstrates that bidirectionality plus overlapping four-cycles can create torsion in a natural complete-bipartite family. Both cases are motivated by the current study of multipath complexes of symmetric digraphs, and their exact attaching maps provide nontrivial structural calibration for any wider family classification.
