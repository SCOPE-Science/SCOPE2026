# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The counting formulas for cardinality, cubic torsion, and iterated squaring establish necessity with exactly two reusable variables. The sufficiency proof gives a concrete equitable partition indexed by each 2-primary element and three odd-coordinate categories, and verifies every atomic two-variable incidence multiplicity. Its formula-induction argument applies at unrestricted quantifier depth. The standalone verifier checks all 351 pairs of distinct finite abelian group types up to size 128 and selected larger examples. Finite tests are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (provisional).** Gomaa's accessible 2007 dissertation, Chapter 3 §§3.1, 3.3, 3.7, uses precisely the relational group signature and gives general first-order/at-most-five-variable resource bounds; these do not imply the specific \(C^2\) invariant. Kiefer–Schweitzer–Selman (2015) gives a general relational-structure refinement theory, not a group-classification formula. Kopczyński–Tan (2013) concerns \(C^2\) spectra, not these group equivalence classes. The odd-order specialization is not asserted as newly discovered. The newly classified even-order abelian case contains a nontrivial interaction between 2-power torsion and three-torsion not supplied by the inspected results. Specialized uncatalogued sources may change historical priority; the 2010 journal article by Gomaa was not fully accessible, although its earlier dissertation was read.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a complete invariant for all finite abelian groups in a standard restricted logical language, not a one-off small-group calculation. It exhibits a sharp prime asymmetry: the complete Sylow-2 type is recoverable but the odd-order direct factor is visible only through its size and three-torsion. It supplies infinite families of nonisomorphic indistinguishable even-order groups and identifies precisely what is and is not observable in this signature.

**Scientific limitations.** The result is relative to abelian groups, two-variable counting logic, and ternary relational multiplication with a named identity. The proof does not apply to binary multiplication-function signatures. The historical-priority check is substantial but not exhaustive. Independent validation remains unperformed.
