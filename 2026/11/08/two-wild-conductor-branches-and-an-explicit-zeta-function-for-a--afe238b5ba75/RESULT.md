# Two wild conductor branches and an explicit zeta function for a characteristic-two quartic scroll

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), and let \(C\subset\mathbb P^3_k\) be the twisted cubic. Write
\[
Q_0=X_0X_2+X_1^2,\quad Q_1=X_0X_3+X_1X_2,\quad Q_2=X_1X_3+X_2^2.
\]
For a general smooth conic \(\Gamma=\{H=0\}\subset\mathbb P^2_{[Q_0:Q_1:Q_2]}\), the quartic surface \(S_\Gamma=\{H(Q_0,Q_1,Q_2)=0\}\) is integral, is smooth away from \(C\), and has reduced singular support exactly \(C\). Its normalization \(\nu:Y_\Gamma\to S_\Gamma\) is \(Y_\Gamma\simeq\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\). The reduced preimage \(E=\nu^{-1}(C)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a smooth elliptic curve of bidegree \((2,2)\) in \(\Gamma\times C\simeq\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), and is exactly the conductor divisor on the normalization. The degree-two map \(E\to C\) is separable with exactly two branch points; at each, its ramification index and different exponent are both \(2\). The quadratic discriminant divisor on \(C\) consists of twice each of these two points.

There is an explicit member over \(\mathbb F_2\):
\[
S:\quad (Q_0+Q_1+Q_2)^2+Q_0Q_2=0.
\]
For every \(n\ge1\), set \(q=2^n\) and define integers \(a_0=2\), \(a_1=-1\), \(a_n=-a_{n-1}-2a_{n-2}\) for \(n\ge2\). Then
\[
\#S(\mathbb F_q)=(q+1)^2+a_n,
\qquad
Z(S,T)=\frac1{(1-T)(1-2T)^2(1-4T)(1+T+2T^2)}.
\]
These arithmetic identities concern this explicitly specified \(\mathbb F_2\)-member; the geometric conclusions hold on a nonempty open locus of smooth conics in characteristic \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field for the geometric statement is algebraically closed of characteristic \(2\). “General” means belonging to a specified nonempty Zariski-open subset of the parameter space of smooth conics: the universal incidence has balanced splitting, its double-curve preimage is smooth, and the middle coefficient of its quadratic equation over \(C\) has two distinct zeros. The explicit finite-field member belongs to this open locus after base change to \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\). Ramification statements concern the normalization's conductor map, not a proposed classification of analytic surface germs at those two points.

## Proof
**1. Incidence and normalization.** The twisted cubic is \(C=[u^3:u^2v:uv^2:v^3]\), with ideal generated by \(Q_0,Q_1,Q_2\). Their linear syzygies are
\[
X_2Q_0+X_1Q_1+X_0Q_2=0,\qquad
X_3Q_0+X_2Q_1+X_1Q_2=0.
\]
The projective graph of the quadratic-minor map \(\mathbb P^3\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^2\) is the incidence variety cut out by the corresponding two linear equations in \([X_0:X_1:X_2:X_3]\), with \([Q_0:Q_1:Q_2]\) now independent coordinates. This is the blow-up of \(C\). Its projection to \(\mathbb P^2\) is a \(\mathbb P^1\)-bundle: the two displayed independent linear equations define a rank-two kernel for each nonzero triple \((Q_0,Q_1,Q_2)\). Restriction to a smooth conic \(\Gamma\) gives a smooth ruled surface \(Y_\Gamma\). The map \(\nu:Y_\Gamma\to S_\Gamma\) is an isomorphism away from \(C\), since the minor map is regular there and its fibers are the secant or tangent lines of \(C\). Over each point of \(C\), the fiber of \(\nu\) is the intersection of \(\Gamma\) with a line in the minor plane, hence finite of length \(2\). Thus \(\nu\) is finite and birational and, since \(Y_\Gamma\) is smooth, is the normalization. It also follows that \(S_\Gamma\) is integral and smooth away from \(C\). Being a quartic in the square of the ideal of \(C\), it is singular at every point of \(C\), proving the reduced singular-support assertion.

**2. Balanced splitting is nonempty and open.** Identify \(\Gamma\simeq\mathbb P^1_{s,t}\). The dual of the incidence kernel has a resolution
\[
0\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)^{\oplus2}\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}^{\oplus4}\to\mathcal Q\to0,
\]
where \(\deg\mathcal Q=4\) and \(Y_\Gamma\simeq\mathbb P(\mathcal Q)\). Serre duality shows that \(H^0(\mathcal Q(-3))=0\) if and only if the corresponding map
\[
H^0(\mathcal O(1))^{\oplus4}\longrightarrow H^0(\mathcal O(3))^{\oplus2}
\]
is invertible. Its matrix is the two syzygy rows after substituting the conic parametrization. For the conic
\[
\Gamma_0=[s^2:s^2+st+t^2:t^2]
\]
its determinant is \(-1\) over the integers and therefore nonzero in characteristic \(2\) (an explicit eight-by-eight construction is in the verifier). The splitting theorem on \(\mathbb P^1\) then forces \(\mathcal Q\simeq\mathcal O(2)\oplus\mathcal O(2)\). Semicontinuity makes this true on a nonempty open set, so \(Y_\Gamma\simeq\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) there.

**3. Conductor curve.** For each \([u:v]\in C\), the two incidence equations become proportional, leaving the single binary-quadratic incidence condition
\[
Q_2u^2+Q_1uv+Q_0v^2=0.
\]
Its restriction to \(\Gamma\times C\) defines \(E_{\mathrm{inc}}\) with bidegree \((2,2)\). For \(\Gamma_0\) its equation is
\[
f(s,t,u,v)=t^2u^2+(s^2+st+t^2)uv+s^2v^2=0.
\]
The four partial derivatives in characteristic \(2\) are \(tuv\), \(suv\), \((s^2+st+t^2)v\), and \((s^2+st+t^2)u\). They have no simultaneous projective zero on \(f=0\): simultaneous vanishing forces \(uv=0\), then \(s^2+st+t^2=0\), while \(f=0\) forces \(t=0\) when \(v=0\), or \(s=0\) when \(u=0\), giving a contradiction. Therefore the incidence curve is smooth. Smoothness is open for the general conic, and adjunction gives genus \((2-1)(2-1)=1\). This incidence curve is the reduced preimage of \(C\) on \(Y_\Gamma\). On the balanced normalization its class is \((2,2)\): it has degree two on a ruling fiber, and genus one fixes the other coefficient.

The conductor ideal of a finite birational normalization of a Gorenstein hypersurface pulls back to an invertible ideal on a smooth normalization. Its effective divisor \(D\) satisfies the canonical/duality relation \(\omega_{Y_\Gamma}\simeq\nu^*\omega_{S_\Gamma}\otimes\mathcal O_{Y_\Gamma}(-D)\). Since \(\omega_{S_\Gamma}\simeq\mathcal O_{S_\Gamma}\) and \(\omega_{Y_\Gamma}\simeq\mathcal O(-2,-2)\), the class of \(D\) is \((2,2)\). Its support is the reduced incidence curve \(E_{\mathrm{inc}}\), also of class \((2,2)\). Consequently \(D=E_{\mathrm{inc}}\) as divisors. Write this smooth elliptic conductor curve as \(E\).

**4. Exactly two wild branches.** The map \(E\to C\simeq\mathbb P^1_{u,v}\) has degree two. Write a general conic parametrization as \(Q_i=A_i s^2+B_i st+C_i t^2\). The middle coefficient of the equation of \(E\to C\), regarded as a quadratic in \((s,t)\), is the binary quadratic
\[
\mathcal B(u,v)=B_2u^2+B_1uv+B_0v^2.
\]
For \(\Gamma_0\) this is \(uv\), with two distinct zeros. The property that \(\mathcal B\) has two simple zeros is open. Away from those zeros, the derivative of the quadratic in the parameter \(s/t\) is nonzero, so the cover is separable and unramified. At each zero of \(\mathcal B\), the binary quadratic has one double root because \(E\) is smooth, and hence gives one ramified point of index two. There are exactly two such points. Their ramification is wild because the characteristic equals the index. For a separable double cover of smooth projective curves, Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
\deg\operatorname{Diff}(E/C)=2g(E)-2-2(2g(C)-2)=4.
\]
Each wild index-two point has different exponent at least two, so these two points have exponent exactly two each. The determinant/discriminant of a binary quadratic \(As^2+\mathcal B st+Ct^2\) in characteristic two is \(\mathcal B^2\). Thus the discriminant divisor on \(C\) is twice each branch point. In the explicit example, at \((u:v)=(1:z)\) and \((s:t)=(1:w)\), the local equation is \(w^2+(1+w+w^2)z+z^2=0\), with \(z=w^2+w^3+O(w^4)\), directly exhibiting ramification index two and differential vanishing to order two. The second point is symmetric.

**5. Arithmetic of the explicit member.** The conic \(\Gamma_0\) is defined over \(\mathbb F_2\). The integral determinant \(-1\) in step 2 shows that its normalization is already the split surface \(Y=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Directly enumerating the three \(\mathbb F_2\)-points on each factor of \(\Gamma_0\times C\) shows \(\#E(\mathbb F_2)=4\). Since \(E\) is smooth of genus one, its Frobenius polynomial is \(z^2+z+2\). Let \(a_n\) be the sum of the \(n\)th powers of its two roots; then \(a_0=2\), \(a_1=-1\), and \(a_n=-a_{n-1}-2a_{n-2}\). Hence \(\#E(\mathbb F_{2^n})=q+1-a_n\). Over every finite extension, the normalization map is an isomorphism off \(C\) and its full rational-point correction is supported on \(E\to C\). Therefore
\[
\#S(\mathbb F_q)=\#Y(\mathbb F_q)-\#E(\mathbb F_q)+\#C(\mathbb F_q)
=(q+1)^2+a_n.
\]
The zeta identity follows from \(Z(S,T)=Z(Y,T)Z(C,T)/Z(E,T)\), giving precisely the rational function in the finding.

## Verification
Run \(\texttt{python verify\_scroll.py}\) with Python 3 and SymPy. The script checks the two syzygies, reduced singular containment, smoothness criterion, the separable middle coefficient, local expansion and different exponent, balanced bundle determinant, and independent point counts both on the quartic and on the conductor over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_8,\mathbb F_{16}\). The observed pairs \((\#E,\#S)\) are respectively \((4,8)\), \((8,22)\), \((4,86)\), and \((16,290)\). These finite checks corroborate the geometric proof and the zeta derivation; they are not used to infer an infinite point-count theorem merely by interpolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Polo-Blanco, van der Put, and Top (2010/2011, §3.2.4) give the general secant-scroll construction \(H(Q_0,Q_1,Q_2)\) and a classification of ruled quartics with twisted cubic singular locus; their quartic classification explicitly assumes characteristic zero or greater than three. The construction, the rational ruling curve, and the generic balanced bundle are therefore not claimed as new. Edge's classical geometric account explains four pinch points in the complex case. Jones (2026, §3.5) describes the complex normalization and genus-one double-curve conductor. None of these inspected discussions asserts the characteristic-two two-point wild ramification and different-exponent law, nor the displayed finite-field zeta function. These are the delimited contributions here; the possibility of unindexed older positive-characteristic treatments remains.

## Limitations
The geometric statement is generically quantified, not asserted for every possibly tangent or reducible conic. It does not classify the complete analytic local singularities of the surface at the wild branch points or all positive-characteristic quartic-scroll isomorphism types. Its zeta formula applies only to the displayed \(\mathbb F_2\)-model, not to every member of the family. The independence and historical novelty of the result have not been externally audited.
