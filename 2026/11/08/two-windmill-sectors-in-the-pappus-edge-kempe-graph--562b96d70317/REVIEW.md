# Review: Two windmill sectors in the Pappus edge-Kempe graph

## Correctness — PASS

The graph, palette quotient, moves and all quantifiers are explicit. The finite matching lemma is fully exhaustive: forty-two matchings, thirty-six Hamiltonian complements and six three-hexagon complements; the six exceptional matchings are listed by reproducible edge indices. Two direct implementations starting from different search spaces independently pass. The four factorizations per exceptional matching and the unique factorization per Hamiltonian complement account for all twenty states. Intersecting six complete move-cliques gives the asserted two cones over three triangles, and the distance conclusions follow without extra experiments. The full proof is finite and exact; numerical results have not been extrapolated.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

Abreu–Labbate–Sheehan (arxiv:1002.1891v1, posted 2010-02-09) established Pappus pseudo-2-factor isomorphism, hence parity restrictions on complementary cycle structures; we do not claim that property as new. The paper does not enumerate the switching adjacency graph. Belcastro–Haas (arxiv:1209.1730v1, first posted 2012-09-08) establishes general edge-Kempe equivalence theory. Goedgebeur–Östergård (arxiv:2105.01363v1, 2021) enumerates *numbers* of equivalence classes for cubic graphs covering Pappus order. That latter study already supplies stronger coverage of the bare class-count problem; merely saying “two components” would be subsumed and is not separately claimed novel. The inspected portions and aggregate tables do not provide the precise 20-state windmill isomorphism, six exceptional matchings, unique hubs or exact distances. Searches under Tait-coloring, one-factorization, Levi-graph and Kempe switching terms found no direct equivalent. Unindexed or unpublished per-graph state graphs remain a priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The full reconfiguration graph of the canonical eighteen-vertex Pappus cubic graph is naturally motivated by the edge-Kempe equivalence literature. Unlike a bare component count, the theorem identifies the six move-cliques, why three intersect at exactly one hub in each sector, and the entire shortest-path geometry; it supplies a reusable exact benchmark for edge-Kempe algorithms and a concrete structural counterpoint to planar bipartite connectivity. The graph is small, but the classification is complete and not a trivial reformulation or special case of the published aggregate count tables.

## Literature and limitations

The exact graph concerns unlabelled one-factorizations, not all named-color states. The finite matching census is provided with two standalone reproducible enumerators, rather than as a uniform theorem for every cubic graph. Inspection covered the relevant principal sections of three original preprints and the Pappus reference, but not unpublished computational archives. No historical priority or independent validation is asserted beyond the documented best-knowledge comparison.
