# Two-world collapse and finite witnesses for the Gödel S5 modal-depth hierarchy

## Finding
For every finite number of propositional letters, on the two-world universal reflexive Kripke frame every Gödel-valued modal formula is equivalent to one of modal depth at most one. With exactly one letter, the formula-equivalence algebra contains six depth-zero meanings and exactly 342 meanings in total. Across the family of all finite universal reflexive frames, however, the depth hierarchy is strict at *every* positive level: for each integer \(d\ge1\), a frame with \(2d+1\) worlds distinguishes a depth-\(d+1\) formula from all formulas of depth at most \(d\).

## Assumptions and scope
The truth set is the standard Gödel chain \([0,1]\); conjunction is minimum, disjunction maximum, and implication is \(a\to b=1\) when \(a\le b\) and \(a\to b=b\) otherwise. The sole constant is false \(0\) (true \(1\) is definable). On \(K_n\), the complete reflexive relation, \(\Box f=\min_{w\in K_n}f(w)\) and \(\Diamond f=\max_{w\in K_n}f(w)\), constant across worlds. Modal depth counts nested occurrences of either operator. Equivalence means equality of truth degrees at every designated world for every valuation of the indicated propositional variables. Finite universal frames are witnessed; no fuzzy accessibility, intermediate truth constants, or non-Gödel connectives are assumed.

## Proof
**Two-world exchange.** At a chosen world write \(x=p(w)\), \(y=p(v)\) for the other world. Set \(l=\Box p=\min(x,y)\) and \(h=\Diamond p=\max(x,y)\). The depth-one formula
\[
Q(p)=((p\to\Box p)\to\Diamond p)\wedge((\Diamond p\to p)\to\Box p)
\]
evaluates to \(y\). Indeed, if \(x\le y\), its first factor equals \(y\) and the second is \(1\); if \(x\ge y\), its first factor is \(1\) and its second is \(y\). These calculations include endpoint and equality cases. For each of \(k\) letters, the local and other-world values can therefore be represented by \(p_i\) and \(Q(p_i)\). By structural induction, the value of *any* modal formula at a world is a propositional Gödel term in these \(2k\) values: Boolean-Gödel connectives act pointwise, while \(\Box\) and \(\Diamond\) take respectively the minimum and maximum of the term at the two ordered world assignments. Substitution of the \(Q\)-formulas gives depth at most one. Conversely every \(2k\)-generated propositional term is realized by this substitution.

**Exact one-variable count.** Gödel terms in the ordered variables \(x,y\) are completely determined by their behavior on the weak-order types of \(0,x,y,1\): operations only select existing arguments or \(1\), and the selection is governed by comparisons. The 16 evaluations \((x,y)\in\{0,1/3,2/3,1\}^2\) include all such weak-order types. Exhaustive term closure, initialized by \(0,1,x,y\) and closed under minimum, maximum, and Gödel implication, yields exactly 342 distinct 16-entry vectors. The standalone checker enumerates the entire finite closure and asserts its stability. Starting from \(0,1,x\) on the four-point chain yields six vectors. These are complete term algebras, not finite sampling estimates, because of the weak-order representation lemma.

**Finite strictness.** Fix \(d\ge1\), put \(n=2d+1\), and assign \(p\) the distinct values \(0<a_1<\dots<a_n<1\) in the \(n\) worlds. Define \(t_0=p\) and \(t_{r+1}=t_r\vee(t_r\to\Box t_r)\). Inductively \(t_r\) has value \(1\) in the first \(r\) worlds and value \(a_j\) in world \(j>r\). Hence \(\Box t_d\), of depth \(d+1\), is the constant value \(a_{d+1}\) in every world.

For the lower bound put \(B_r=\{0,1,a_1,\ldots,a_r,a_{n-r+1},\ldots,a_n\}\) for \(0\le r\le d\). **Boundary-band lemma:** At every world \(i\), each formula of depth at most \(r\) evaluates to a Gödel propositional term in the local value \(a_i\) using constants from \(B_r\), with the same term at all worlds. For \(r=0\) this is immediate. Gödel propositional operations preserve the property. For its modal step, a one-variable term with fixed constants from \(B_r\) returns on each interval separated by those constants either the local variable or one of the constants; this is proved by induction using the formula for Gödel implication. As there are no valuation grades between consecutive low boundary values or between consecutive high boundary values, its minimum and maximum across the world set lie in \(B_{r+1}\): on the sole central interval they can only add \(a_{r+1}\) or \(a_{n-r}\). Thus a modal operator creates a constant term from \(B_{r+1}\), proving the lemma by induction. At depth \(d\), every formula at the first world has a value in \(B_d\); the set excludes \(a_{d+1}\). Therefore no depth-at-most-\(d\) formula can equal \(\Box t_d\) on all valuations of \(K_n\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_godel_s5.py` with Python 3 (standard library only). It exhaustively generates the 342 two-generator Gödel term functions on the complete order-type test grid and the six one-generator functions, checks the exchange term at each of the 16 pairs, and evaluates the known selection recurrence at the explicitly stated finite witnesses for \(1\le d\le9\). The unbounded strict hierarchy is established by the boundary-band lemma, not inferred from finite checking.

## Relationship to prior work
Ferrari, Fiorentini, Giardini and Rodriguez (2026) develop witnessed Gödel modal semantics and proof search for more general accessibility; they do not state this two-world or finite-size depth classification. Castaño, Cimadamore, Díaz Varela and Rueda (2020, Section 2) **already use exactly the selection recurrence** \(t_{r+1}=t_r\vee(t_r\to\Box t_r)\) on an *infinite* sequence to establish non-local finiteness of monadic Gödel algebras. That recurrence and the general phenomenon of unbounded generation are prior work, not new here. Their study of locally finite subvarieties and free algebras does not directly specify the two-world depth-one translation, its exact 342-element quotient, or the finite \(2d+1\)-world depth-separation certificate. The new result is restricted to these quantitative finite-frame statements and their proof.

## Limitations
The \(2d+1\) size is a proved sufficient witness bound, **not** asserted to be minimal. The exact 342 count is for one letter on the fixed two-world complete frame, not the global free algebra of all finite universal frames. Stronger published characterizations could subsume part of the result; the relevant papers and residual priority risk are documented in the separate review. The results do not cover arbitrary finite accessibility relations or additional truth-degree constants.
