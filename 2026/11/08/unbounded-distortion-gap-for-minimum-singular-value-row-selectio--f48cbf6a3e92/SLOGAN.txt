Even on an exact two-vector Krylov basis of a symmetric positive-definite matrix with condition number \(3\), every row pair maximizing the smallest sketched singular value—and the pairs chosen by both DEIM and Q-DEIM—has whitening distortion \(\sqrt N\), while another pair has the optimum distortion \(1\). Thus smallest-singular-value selection has no dimension-independent guarantee for the condition-number distortion it is used to control; this does not assert superior numerical performance of the tiny-amplitude alternative.
