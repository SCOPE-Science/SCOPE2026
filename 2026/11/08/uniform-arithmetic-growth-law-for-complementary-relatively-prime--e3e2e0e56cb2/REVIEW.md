# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For each possible gcd, division by the gcd identifies its subset count with \(f(\lfloor n/g\rfloor)\). Complementing the published gcd-divisibility and gcd-coprimality interpretations, with the empty subset counted separately, yields exact identities. The bounds \(2^{m-1}\le f(m)\le2^m-1\) hold by the subsets containing one and by the full set of nonempty subsets. The minimum admissible gcds are exactly \(a(n)\) and \(p(n)\), so lower-bounding by the leading term and upper-bounding by at most \(n\) terms proves the uniform ratio inequality. Parity gives exponents separated by at least \(n/6-O(1)\), dominating the linear factor. The standalone exact-integer verifier passed for \(4\le n\le400\), with direct subset comparisons through \(13\). The infinite statement follows from the proof alone.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Pongsriiam (2013) explicitly asks for these two limits in Question 3.3. The later 2015/2016 paper's Theorem 9 proves the alternating sign of \(D-\Phi\), which is stronger than the earlier sign question but does not state the arithmetic two-sided ratio bounds or evaluate the two limits of the distinct deficit ratio. The OEIS records the sequence \(D\), not this ratio-growth theorem. Direct searches for the ratio, for its least-nondivisor exponent, for aliases and for stronger asymptotics did not identify covering results. These are best-knowledge originality judgments, not proof of historical uniqueness; elementary derivability from known gcd partitions creates a residual priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The source expressly posed the limsup and liminf as a natural unexplained aspect of the near-\(2^n\) subset counts. The result supplies their exact values and a stronger, uniform pointwise arithmetic rate that identifies the least nondivisor and the least prime factor as the two controlling parameters. This goes beyond a parity sign test; the quantitative form explains the exponential separation for all integers with no unproved assumptions. Further partial sums in the question remain open here.
