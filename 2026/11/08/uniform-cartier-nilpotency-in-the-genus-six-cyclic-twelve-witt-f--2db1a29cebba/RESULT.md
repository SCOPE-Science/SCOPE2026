# Uniform Cartier nilpotency in the genus-six cyclic-twelve Witt family

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\). For every \(\lambda\in k\), let \(X_\lambda\) be the smooth projective curve obtained by adjoining \(x\) with \(x^3=z\) and a length-two Artin–Schreier–Witt vector \((y,w)\) satisfying
\[
(F-1)(y,w)=\left(\frac1z,\frac\lambda z\right).
\]
This is the genus-six cyclic-order-twelve family from Kazemifard–Tafazolian, Theorem 5.8. On \(H^0(X_\lambda,\Omega^1)\), the characteristic-two Cartier operator \(\mathcal C\) has the **parameter-independent rank profile**
\[
\boxed{\bigl(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C^j\bigr)_{j=0}^{5}=(6,4,3,2,1,0).}
\]
Consequently the \(a\)-number is \(2\), the \(2\)-rank is \(0\), and \(\mathcal C\) has nilpotency index \(5\). Moreover, at the unique point \(P_\infty\) above the pole of \(t=1/x\), the Weierstrass pole semigroup is \(\langle4,6,9\rangle\), with gaps \(1,2,3,5,7,11\).

## Assumptions and scope
All fields have characteristic \(2\); \(k\) is algebraically closed (hence perfect). The parameter \(\lambda\) is arbitrary, including \(0\). A curve defined by these affine equations means its smooth, projective, geometrically integral normalization. The ranks are \(k\)-dimensions of the images of **semilinear** iterates of the Cartier operator; no inappropriate linearity of \(\mathcal C\) is assumed. The pole semigroup concerns only the uniquely specified point \(P_\infty\).

## Proof
**Step 1: An Artin–Schreier tower.** Write \(t=1/x\), so \(1/z=t^3\). In characteristic \(2\), Witt-vector subtraction gives the two ordinary equations
\[
y^2+y=t^3,\qquad w^2+w=y^3+(\lambda+1)y^2+\lambda y.
\]
Choose \(c\in k\) with \(c^2=\lambda+1\), put \(v=w+cy\), and set \(b=\lambda+c\). Using \(y^2+y=t^3\), the equations become
\[
\boxed{y^2+y=t^3,\qquad v^2+v=y^3+by.}\tag{1}
\]
This change is invertible. In particular, the same tower describes every parameter of the cited cyclic-order-twelve family.

**Step 2: Genus, pole orders, and differentials.** Let \(E\) be the first double cover \(y^2+y=t^3\). It has one ramified point above \(t=\infty\), of conductor \(3\), and genus \(1\). At this point \(t\) and \(y\) have poles of orders \(2\) and \(3\). The second equation in (1) is an Artin–Schreier extension of \(k(E)\) with a unique pole, of odd order \(9\); it is therefore connected and totally ramified there. Its different exponent is \(9+1=10\), and Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
2g(X_\lambda)-2=2(2g(E)-2)+10=10,
\]
so \(g(X_\lambda)=6\). If \(P_\infty\) denotes the unique point above the pole of \(t\), the pole orders of \(t,y,v\) are, respectively, \(4,6,9\). The morphism to the \(t\)-line is unramified at finite \(t\), and the different exponent in the degree-four tower at infinity is \(10+2\cdot4=18\). Since \((dt)\) on the projective \(t\)-line is \(-2(\infty)\), its pullback divisor is
\[
\operatorname{div}_{X_\lambda}(dt)=(18-8)P_\infty=10P_\infty.
\]
The semigroup generated by \(4,6,9\) has precisely six positive gaps \(1,2,3,5,7,11\). A Weierstrass semigroup at a point of a genus-six curve has exactly six gaps; consequently the full semigroup is \(H(P_\infty)=\langle4,6,9\rangle\). Hence the six holomorphic differentials
\[
\boxed{dt,\quad t\,dt,\quad y\,dt,\quad t^2dt,\quad v\,dt,\quad ty\,dt}\tag{2}
\]
have respective nonnegative vanishing orders \(10,6,4,2,1,0\) at \(P_\infty\), no poles elsewhere, and distinct valuations. They form a basis of \(H^0(X_\lambda,\Omega^1)\).

**Step 3: Cartier on the basis.** The function \(t\) is a separating variable. For any functions \(A,B\), the characteristic-two rule is
\[
\mathcal C\bigl((A^2+tB^2)\,dt\bigr)=B\,dt.
\]
The first equation of (1) gives \(y=y^2+t^3\) and \(ty=(t^2)^2+t y^2\). Its cube satisfies
\[
y^3=(y+t^3)^2+t(ty)^2.
\]
Since \(c^2=\lambda+1\), equivalently \(c_0^2=b\) for some \(c_0\in k\), equation (1) also gives
\[
v=(v+y+t^3+c_0y)^2+t(ty+c_0t)^2.
\]
Thus, writing \(c_0^2=b\), the six basis images are exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\omega&dt&t\,dt&y\,dt&t^2dt&v\,dt&ty\,dt\\ \hline
\mathcal C(\omega)&0&dt&t\,dt&0&(ty+c_0t)\,dt&y\,dt.
\end{array}\tag{3}
\]
The first image spans the four-dimensional subspace generated by \(dt,t\,dt,y\,dt,ty\,dt\). Its successive images have spans
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{im}\mathcal C^2&=\langle dt,t\,dt,y\,dt\rangle,\\
\operatorname{im}\mathcal C^3&=\langle dt,t\,dt\rangle,\\
\operatorname{im}\mathcal C^4&=\langle dt\rangle,\\
\operatorname{im}\mathcal C^5&=0.
\end{split}
\]
Because \(k\) is perfect, taking square roots of coefficients does not affect these image dimensions. Thus (3) proves all six claimed ranks uniformly in \(b\) and \(\lambda\). The \(a\)-number is \(g-\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C=2\), and nilpotence implies \(2\)-rank zero.

## Relationship to prior work
Kazemifard and Tafazolian, Theorem 5.8, identify the genus-six characteristic-two \(C_{12}\) family through cubic and length-two Witt covers, and compute its ramification signature and genus. Their discussion does not state Cartier operator ranks, \(a\)-numbers, nilpotency indices, or the marked Weierstrass semigroup. Groen (2022/2024) gives general bounds for powers of Cartier on Artin–Schreier **double covers**. In the present tower the intermediate elliptic curve \(E\) is supersingular, so Groen's exact formula for Artin–Schreier covers of **ordinary** base curves is inapplicable; its general bounds do not alone assert the exact image filtration (3). The present result computes that filtration directly.

## Limitations
The theorem is for characteristic \(2\) and the specified one-parameter genus-six \(C_{12}\) family. It is not a classification of all genus-six curves, all cyclic-degree-twelve covers, geometric automorphism groups beyond the specified action, or full Ekedahl–Oort types. The Cartier rank profile does not by itself determine the full \(2\)-torsion group scheme. No independent mathematical audit has been performed; the priority comparison is not an exhaustive historical search.
