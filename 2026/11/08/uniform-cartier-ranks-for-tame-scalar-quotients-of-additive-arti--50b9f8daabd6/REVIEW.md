Tame differential descent and Cartier equivariance identify the quotient image with the invariant subspace of the cover's image. The critical nonstandard premise, the exact monomial image and invertible triangular minor for all additive coefficients, was checked directly against [2, Lemmas 6.1–6.5, Theorem 6.6]. The weight calculation for the paired source/target basis elements preserves the invariant minor. The uniform-block and odd-residue cases are disjoint and exhaustive. Finite exact direct matrices independently match the closed formula. The result does not claim an infinite proof from finite tests, nor any higher Cartier rank.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The natural target is the first Cartier rank and Jacobian \(a\)-number of the *tame cyclic scalar quotient*, which is a different curve from the additive cover of [2]. The new formula is obtained using [2]'s stronger cover-image theorem, so that dependence is explicit and the covering theorem itself is not claimed. The new content is the complete character congruence classification, closed \(p,h\) formula, and new arithmetic invariant for the automorphism-free maximal examples of [1]. Full primary material in [1], [2], and [3] was inspected for the same quotient invariant, including the original statements and relevant proofs; none states this formula. Multiple mathematically phrased search queries of available mathematical research records and literature found no equivalent statement. This is not proof of historical priority. A 2005 article by Pries on quotients of Artin–Schreier curves has a potentially relevant title but its available abstract describes a different universal family and Newton polygons; a complete text comparison was not obtained. The general cover-image theorem means the conclusion is a derived result rather than a logically independent theorem, a residual priority risk that does not invalidate its distinct, non-immediate quotient evaluation.

**Value — PASS.** An \(a\)-number detects the nonordinary local \(p\)-torsion of a Jacobian, not determined by the maximal point count, supersingularity or trivial automorphism group. The result completely computes this invariant for an infinite, explicitly defined family in every odd characteristic, and for a larger natural coefficient family. Its exact residual congruence term also quantifies why the quotient differs from simply dividing the cover's Cartier rank by the group order. The calculation requires both representation-theoretic descent and a parity-sensitive enumeration; it is not merely a change of variables or a restatement of the published covering-curve rank.
