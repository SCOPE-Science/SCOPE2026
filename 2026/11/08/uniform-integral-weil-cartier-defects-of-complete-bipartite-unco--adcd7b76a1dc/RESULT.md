# Uniform integral Weil–Cartier defects of complete-bipartite unconditional toric Fano varieties

## Finding
For integers \(a,b\ge1\) with \(d=a+b\ge3\), let \(G=K_{a,b}\), and let \(Q_{a,b}\subset\mathbb R^{a+b}\) be the unconditional stable-set polytope of \(G\). Let \(X_{a,b}\) be the normal projective complex toric variety with moment polytope \(Q_{a,b}\). Then \(X_{a,b}\) is a Gorenstein toric Fano variety with integral divisor groups
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(X_{a,b})\simeq\mathbb Z^{4ab-a-b}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\qquad \operatorname{Pic}(X_{a,b})=\mathbb Z[-K_{X_{a,b}}],
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(X_{a,b})/\operatorname{Pic}(X_{a,b})
\simeq\mathbb Z^{4ab-a-b-1}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
The anticanonical divisor is primitive among Cartier divisors (Fano index one). The anticanonical degree is
\[
(-K_{X_{a,b}})^d=2^d a!b!.
\]
In particular these varieties are not \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial, their Weil–Cartier free-rank defect grows with both part sizes, and their Weil–Cartier quotient retains two independent order-two classes.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). In mutually complementary coordinate lattices \(\mathbb Z^a\oplus\mathbb Z^b\), let \(e_i\) and \(f_j\) denote their standard bases. A stable set of the complete bipartite graph is supported entirely in one part. Its unconditional stable-set polytope is therefore
\[
Q_{a,b}=\operatorname{conv}([-1,1]^a\times\{0\},\ \{0\}\times[-1,1]^b)
=\{(x,y):\|x\|_\infty+\|y\|_\infty\le1\}.
\]
Here \(1\le i\le a\), \(1\le j\le b\), and both part sizes are positive. Use the standard full lattice; in particular do not replace it by the index-two lattice generated by facet normals. The toric variety is constructed from the normal fan of \(Q_{a,b}\) (equivalently from the face fan of its polar). The projective model uses every lattice point of \(Q_{a,b}\).

## Proof
**(1) Polytope, normality and Fano polarization.** The inequalities for \(Q_{a,b}\) are precisely
\[
sx_i+ty_j\le1\quad(1\le i\le a,\ 1\le j\le b,\ s,t\in\{-1,1\}).
\]
Each defines a facet: set the indicated coordinates to \(s/2,t/2\), and set all other coordinates to zero to see relative interior on that hyperplane. Thus there are exactly \(4ab\) distinct primitive facet normals \(r_{ij}^{s,t}=se_i+tf_j\). Their constants are all one, so \(Q_{a,b}\) is reflexive and the toric polarization \(H\) equals \(-K_{X_{a,b}}\).

The projective monomial model is normal. Indeed, if \((x,y)\) is an integral point of \(mQ_{a,b}\), put \(p=\|x\|_\infty\), \(q=\|y\|_\infty\). These are nonnegative integers with \(p+q\le m\). Distribute each coordinate \(x_i\) among \(p\) signed \(0,1\) entries of its sign, giving \(p\) lattice points of the first cube summing to \((x,0)\). Do the same in the second cube for \(y\) using \(q\) points. Add \(m-p-q\) zeros. This proves the integer-decomposition property in all dimensions.

The vertices of \(Q_{a,b}\) are the \(2^a\) sign corners \((\sigma,0)\) and the \(2^b\) sign corners \((0,\tau)\). Their normal cones have, respectively, the rays \(r_{ij}^{\sigma_i,t}\) for every \(i,j,t\) and \(r_{ij}^{s,\tau_j}\) for every \(i,j,s\). Each maximal cone has \(2ab>d\) rays when \(d\ge3\), proving non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality.

**(2) Picard lattice, including integrality.** For any integral torus-invariant Cartier divisor let \(h_{ij}^{s,t}\in\mathbb Z\) denote its coefficient at the ray \(r_{ij}^{s,t}\). Cartier compatibility at every cone over \((\sigma,0)\) means that there are integral vectors \(U^\sigma\in\mathbb Z^a\), \(V^\sigma\in\mathbb Z^b\) with
\[
h_{ij}^{\sigma_i,t}=\sigma_i U_i^\sigma+tV_j^\sigma.
\]
At every cone over \((0,\tau)\) one similarly has integral \(\widetilde U^\tau,\widetilde V^\tau\) with
\[
h_{ij}^{s,\tau_j}=s\widetilde U_i^\tau+\tau_j\widetilde V_j^\tau.
\]
If \(a\ge2\), independently varying two signs \(\sigma_i,\sigma_{i'}\) in the first relation shows that the half-difference in \(t\) is a common \(q_j\) independent of \(i,s\). If \(b\ge2\), the second relation similarly makes the half-difference in \(s\) a common \(p_i\) independent of \(j,t\). For \(a,b\ge2\), this gives \(h_{ij}^{s,t}=sp_i+tq_j+c_{ij}\). The first cone equations force \(c_{ij}\) to be independent of \(j\); the second force independence of \(i\). Thus \(c_{ij}=c\).

When \(a=1\), \(b\ge2\), the second cone equations still give a global \(p_1\), hence \(h_{1j}^{s,t}=sp_1+g_{j,t}\). At either first-part cone the relation gives \(g_{j,t}=c+tq_j\) with the *same* \(c\) for all \(j\), since the first-part slope is independent of \(j\). The case \(b=1\), \(a\ge2\) is symmetric. We have therefore proved in every allowed dimension that
\[
h_{ij}^{s,t}=sp_i+tq_j+c.
\]
The cone equations show directly that \(p_i\) is a coordinate of an integral slope on a second-part cone and \(q_j\) a coordinate of an integral slope on a first-part cone. Consequently \(p_i,q_j\in\mathbb Z\), and \(c=h_{ij}^{s,t}-sp_i-tq_j\in\mathbb Z\). Every integral Cartier support function is thus an integral character plus an integer multiple of the constant-one support function. Modulo principal divisors, \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_{a,b})=\mathbb Z[H]\), and \(H=-K\) is primitive.

**(3) Weil class group and its torsion.** The standard toric divisor sequence is
\[
0\longrightarrow M\stackrel{R}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb Z^{4ab}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(X_{a,b})\longrightarrow0,
\]
where the rows of \(R\) are the primitive normals \(r_{ij}^{s,t}\). The subgroup of \(N=\mathbb Z^d\) spanned by all rows contains \(2N\), since changing either sign subtracts twice the corresponding basis vector. Modulo two the rows are \(e_i+f_j\), whose span has dimension \(d-1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) because the complete bipartite incidence graph is connected. Hence the row subgroup has index two in \(N\). The Smith invariants of \(R\) are \(1\) repeated \(d-1\) times and a final \(2\), proving the claimed class group.

Because the Picard group is generated by \(H\), represented by the all-one divisor vector, the Weil–Cartier quotient is the cokernel of the augmented map \((m,c)\mapsto(\langle m,r_{ij}^{s,t}\rangle+c)_{i,j,s,t}\). Its transposed row vectors in \(N\oplus\mathbb Z\) are \((se_i+tf_j,1)\). Their span contains \(2(N\oplus\mathbb Z)\): sign differences supply twice every original basis vector and the sum of opposite rows gives \((0,2)\). Modulo two, these rows become \(e_i+f_j+z\) for a new last-coordinate basis vector \(z\). They span a subspace of dimension \(d-1\): a basis is the vector for \((i,j)=(1,1)\), the \(a-1\) differences in the first part, and the \(b-1\) differences in the second part. The augmented row lattice consequently has index \(2^{(d+1)-(d-1)}=4\), with quotient annihilated by two. Its Smith invariants are \(1\) repeated \(d-1\) times followed by \(2,2\). This proves the two independent order-two classes and the free-rank formula.

**(4) Anticanonical degree.** Since \(H=-K\) has moment polytope \(Q_{a,b}\), its top self-intersection equals \(d!\) times Euclidean lattice volume. Integrating over the first cube, or using the beta integral, gives
\[
\operatorname{Vol}(Q_{a,b})
=2^d a\int_0^1 u^{a-1}(1-u)^b\,du
=\frac{2^d a!b!}{(a+b)!}.
\]
Hence \(H^d=2^d a!b!\), as asserted.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` constructs all facet normals and maximal-cone incidences for six parameter pairs \((a,b)=(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,2),(2,3),(3,3)\), computes the rational Cartier-support rank, Smith normal forms of \(R\) and its all-one augmentation, and checks the normalized volumes independently by finite differences of exact Ehrhart counts. The checker is a finite sanity check; unrestricted quantifiers follow from the preceding lattice and integral-support-function proofs.

## Relationship to prior work
Mori–Motomura–Ohsugi–Tsuchiya (2026) define unconditional and symmetric stable-set polytopes and prove preservation of toric-ring normality and quadratic generation. Kohl–Olsen–Sanyal (2020) give the free-sum identity for unconditional polytopes of graph joins (specializing to the displayed free sum of cubes) and prove the unconditional-reflexive/perfect-graph correspondence, which already implies reflexivity for complete bipartite graphs. Neither the free-sum formula nor reflexivity is claimed as a new result here. He (2025 preprint, with later revisions) develops general Picard-rank bounds from affine dependences of polar reflexive polytopes; in its simple-polar cases such tools also imply Picard rank one. None of those cited statements supplies the present two-parameter integral class groups or the pair of torsion obstructions in the Weil–Cartier quotient. No general novelty theorem follows from this comparison: equivalent computations under free-sum or graph-polytope terminology remain a historical-priority risk.

## Limitations
The assertions are over the complex numbers and concern the normal toric variety associated with the standard full lattice and the *entire* symmetric stable-set polytope. The boundary case \(a=b=1\) is excluded: its toric Picard group has rank two, so the stated Picard formula does not extend there. The result does not classify the whole singular locus, Cox-ring relations, or all toric blowups. Independent mathematical validation and exhaustive priority confirmation have not been performed.
