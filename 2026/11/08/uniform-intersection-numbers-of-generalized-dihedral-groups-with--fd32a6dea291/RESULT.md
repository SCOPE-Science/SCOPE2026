# Uniform intersection numbers of generalized dihedral groups with odd abelian kernel

## Finding
Let \(A\) be a nontrivial finite abelian group of odd order, and write \(A=\prod_{p\in P}A_p\) for its Sylow decomposition. Define \(r_p=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(A_p/pA_p)\) and \(h_p=(p^{r_p}-1)/(p-1)\). Let \(G=\operatorname{Dih}(A)\) be the semidirect product by inversion. If \(i(G)\) denotes the smallest number of distinct maximal subgroups intersecting in the Frattini subgroup and \(ui(G)\) the smallest number such that **every** selection of that many distinct maximal subgroups has Frattini intersection, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(G)&=\prod_{p\in P}pA_p,\
|\mathcal M(G)|&=1+\sum_{p\in P}p h_p,\
i(G)&=1+\sum_{p\in P}r_p,\
ui(G)&=2+\sum_{p\in P}p h_p-\min_{p\in P}p^{r_p}.
\end{aligned}
\]
All four formulas hold for every such \(A\), including non-elementary and mixed-primary groups. In the elementary abelian case \(A=C_p^r\), they give \(i(G)=r+1\) and \(ui(G)=1+(p^r-1)/(p-1)\). Thus in fixed rank \(r\ge2\), the minimal/uniform gap grows without bound as the odd prime \(p\) increases.

## Assumptions and scope
Groups are finite. The kernel \(A\) has odd order greater than one; the complementary involution \(s\) acts by inversion. We use additive notation in \(A\) and denote by \(M(W,a)=W\cup ((a+W)s)\) the subgroup generated by an index-prime subgroup \(W<A\) and the involution \(as\), where \(a+W\) is a coset. The symbol \(\mathcal M(G)\) means the set of all maximal subgroups. For an abelian Sylow group \(A_p\), \(pA_p\) means its subgroup of \(p\)-multiples. The intersection of any family of maximal subgroups is compared with \(\Phi(G)\); the family is a set, without repetitions.

## Proof
**1. Complete list of maximal subgroups.** The kernel \(A\) is maximal of index two. Let \(M\) be any other maximal subgroup. As \(M\not\subseteq A\), its image in \(G/A\) is all of \(C_2\), and \(W=M\cap A\) is a proper subgroup of \(A\). If \(W\) were not maximal in \(A\), then a proper intermediate subgroup \(W<W'<A\) would be invariant under inversion; adjoining the same involution would yield \(M<W'\rtimes C_2<G\), a contradiction. Hence \(W\) is an index-\(p\) maximal subgroup of \(A\), for some prime \(p\in P\), and \(M=M(W,a)\) for exactly one coset \(a+W\). Conversely every such \(M(W,a)\) is maximal, of index \(p\). Distinct choices of the pair \((W,a+W)\) give distinct maximal subgroups.

For fixed \(p\), maximal subgroups \(W<A\) of index \(p\) are inverse images of hyperplanes in \(A_p/pA_p\), with all other Sylow factors contained. Their number is \(h_p\). Each admits exactly \(p\) distinct reflection cosets, hence \(|\mathcal M(G)|=1+\sum_p p h_p\). The intersection with \(A\) of all the maximal subgroups is \(\bigcap_W W=\prod_p pA_p\). Because \(A\) itself is maximal, the complete Frattini intersection is exactly this subgroup.

**2. Maximal number of maximal subgroups containing a non-Frattini element.** Put \(N=1+\sum_p p h_p\). Fix \(x\in A\setminus\Phi(G)\). Let \(S(x)\) be the set of primes \(p\) for which the image of the \(p\)-component of \(x\) in \(A_p/pA_p\) is nonzero. For each such \(p\), the number of hyperplanes of the \(r_p\)-dimensional quotient omitting this nonzero vector equals \(p^{r_p-1}\): indeed there are \(p^{r_p-1}\) projective covectors not orthogonal to the vector. Each omitted hyperplane supplies \(p\) maximal groups \(M(W,a)\), none of which contains \(x\). Thus
\[
\#\{M\in\mathcal M(G):x\in M\}=N-\sum_{p\in S(x)}p^{r_p}.
\]
This is largest when \(x\) is supported modulo \(\Phi(G)\) in exactly one primary component \(p\) minimizing \(p^{r_p}\); the value is \(N-\min_p p^{r_p}\).

Every element outside \(A\) is an involution of the form \(as\). For each \(W\), exactly one of the \(p\) maximal subgroups \(M(W,b)\) contains \(as\), namely the one with \(b+W=a+W\). The maximal subgroup \(A\) contains no reflection, so a reflection lies in exactly \(\sum_p h_p\) maximal subgroups. The rotation maximum dominates this count, because for a minimizing prime \(p_0\),
\[
(N-\min_p p^{r_p})-\sum_p h_p=\sum_{p\ne p_0}(p^{r_p}-1)\ge0.
\]
For any finite group, the maximum size of a family of maximal subgroups whose intersection properly contains \(\Phi(G)\) equals the maximum, over elements \(g\notin\Phi(G)\), of the number of maximal subgroups containing \(g\). This follows by choosing any such \(g\) in a bad intersection, and conversely taking all maximal subgroups that contain \(g\). Hence \(ui(G)=1+N-\min_p p^{r_p}=2+\sum_p p h_p-\min_p p^{r_p}\).

**3. Minimal intersection number.** Let \(R=\sum_p r_p\). Every maximal subgroup other than \(A\) imposes at most one linear hyperplane condition on the rotation quotient \(A/\Phi(G)=\prod_p(A_p/pA_p)\); \(A\) imposes no such condition. For fewer than \(R\) subgroups, the intersection contains a rotation outside \(\Phi(G)\). With exactly \(R\) subgroups, a family containing \(A\) still imposes fewer than \(R\) rotation conditions. A family avoiding \(A\) either has rank less than \(R\), again leaving a rotation outside \(\Phi(G)\), or has exactly \(R\) independent affine conditions on possible reflection coordinates \(a\in A/\Phi(G)\). In the latter case, because the linear conditions form a basis of the dual quotient, the affine system is solvable and a reflection belongs to all \(R\) subgroups. Thus \(i(G)\ge R+1\). On the other hand, choose \(A\) and, for each \(p\), \(r_p\) maximal subgroups \(M(W,a)\) whose hyperplane normals form a basis of the dual of \(A_p/pA_p\). Their rotation intersection is precisely \(\Phi(G)\), and intersecting with \(A\) excludes all reflections. Consequently \(i(G)=R+1\).

**4. Specializations.** When \(A=C_p^r\), only one prime occurs and \(p h_p=p(p^r-1)/(p-1)\), so the uniform formula reduces to \(h_p+1\). For cyclic odd \(A\), each \(r_p=1\), giving \(ui(G)=2+\sum_p p-\min_p p\), exactly the cyclic-kernel dihedral result of Choudhury–Khanra (2026), Theorem 2.6.

## Verification
A standalone exact Python checker enumerates every subgroup of four small odd abelian kernels directly from the additive group operation, extracts their index-prime maximal subgroups, constructs all maximal subgroups of the corresponding generalized dihedral groups, and calculates the intersection of *every* subset of maximal subgroups as an integer bitset. It checks the predicted Frattini subgroup, maximal-subgroup count, minimal intersection number, and uniform intersection number. Run `python3 verify.py`; the expected complete output is in `verification_output.txt`. The checker examines \(8{,}192+8{,}192+512+262{,}144=279{,}040\) subsets of maximal subgroups. These finite checks support, but do not establish, the arbitrary-group theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Archer–Bautista Serrano–Cook–Lauderdale–Perez–Villalobos (2019), Section 4, determines the minimal intersection number for the cyclic-kernel dihedral groups. Choudhury–Khanra (2026), Theorem 2.6, determines their uniform intersection number and motivates asking how the invariant depends on the Sylow-rank profile beyond cyclic kernels. The formulas here agree with both results in the cyclic case. Their inspected statements do not count maximal-subgroup containment fibers for arbitrary noncyclic abelian kernels, which is the essential additional step here. Related studies of generating and independence graphs of generalized dihedral groups employ the same Frattini quotient but address other invariants and do not directly determine the number of maximal subgroups in a worst-case intersection.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes an odd-order abelian kernel and an inversion action. It does not cover even-order kernels, nonabelian kernels, or other semidirect actions. It does not classify the maximal-intersection poset or the number of extremizing subsets. Complete historical priority beyond the inspected sources has not been independently certified; the proof synthesizes elementary finite-group and finite-geometry facts, and an earlier equivalent formulation remains possible.
