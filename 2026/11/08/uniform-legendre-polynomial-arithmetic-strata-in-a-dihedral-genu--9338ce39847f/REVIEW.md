# Scientific review — uniform Legendre-polynomial arithmetic strata

## Correctness: PASS
The source curve is a squarefree degree-eight hyperelliptic model precisely away from \(c=\pm2\), so the genus and parameter boundary are correct. Three degree-two elliptic quotients are obtained by explicit invariant rational functions, and the three differential pullbacks span the genus-three differential space. The Klein-four trace relation shows that the product map is a 2-power isogeny, so it preserves all geometric \(p\)-divisible-group invariants in odd characteristic. The Prym corresponds to the two isomorphic quotient factors. Direct multinomial coefficient extraction gives both quartic Hasse invariants; coefficient-ratio identities identify them with the appropriate Legendre polynomials up to nonzero scalars. The Legendre differential equation proves their separability away from \(\pm2\) in each characteristic, yielding the counts. Two exact, independent arithmetic programs replay the finite-characteristic specializations and the Euclidean-gcd example table. Their successful finite checks supplement, but are not used in place of, the all-prime proof.

## Originality: PASS, with priority risk
Ohashi (arXiv:2112.11152, Sections 2–4, Example 4.3) already proves genus-three Howe-curve elliptic decomposition, derives maximality/minimality **conditional on superspeciality**, and tabulates superspecial **isomorphism classes** in small characteristics. Borówka–Shatsila (arXiv:2308.07038, Sections 3–4) describes genus-three elliptic quotient and Prym moduli in characteristic zero, including the general two-parameter model. The established classical coefficient formula for elliptic Hasse invariants and Legendre-polynomial theory are likewise not claimed as new. The reviewed result instead explicitly classifies, in every odd characteristic and for every smooth geometric coefficient, the joint Prym, elliptic, and Jacobian \(p\)-rank/Cartier-number strata, with a uniform exact count of superspecial **Prym coefficient parameters** and a polynomial-gcd formula for simultaneous superspeciality. No inspected source states or supplies this complete parameter-wise classification; this conclusion is based on mathematical comparison, not on the absence of a matching title. It subsumes prior characteristic-seven/eleven calculations of the same pencil rather than treating them as independent novelties. Residual risk: the formula may have appeared in older specialized computational or modular-curve literature not found by the searches.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The one-dimensional dihedral genus-three pencil is a geometrically distinguished locus of hyperelliptic Howe curves with nontrivial Prym geometry. Determining its characteristic-dependent, geometric \(p\)-rank and \(a\)-number stratification **for all odd primes** supplies a complete arithmetic invariant classification without a finite-characteristic cutoff. It distinguishes an exactly quantified superspecial-Prym locus from the varying superspecial-Jacobian locus, with nontrivial common-zero behavior (for example gcd degrees \(0,2,3,7\) at characteristics \(11,17,31,47\)), and avoids treating parameter counts as isomorphism-class counts. The result is not an arbitrary parameter sample or a further isolated small-prime computation. It does not solve the remaining arithmetic problem of expressing the gcd degree solely in terms of a simple prime congruence.

## Closest sources and limitations
- R. Ohashi, *On the maximality of hyperelliptic Howe curves of genus 3*, arXiv:2112.11152, first public 2021-12-21: decomposition and conditional maximality; small-prime isomorphism classes, not complete all-prime coefficient strata.
- P. Borówka and A. Shatsila, *Hyperelliptic genus 3 curves with involutions and a Prym map*, arXiv:2308.07038, first public 2023-08-14: quotient and Prym geometry for the two-parameter family, not geometric positive-characteristic strata.
- T. Shaska, *Genus 3 hyperelliptic curves with (2, 4, 4)-split Jacobians*, arXiv:1306.5284: older general decomposition background.

Limits: a polynomial-gcd recipe is exact but not a closed congruence formula for every prime; different coefficient parameters may represent isomorphic curves; no full Newton-polygon or finite-field point-count assertion. Originality is a best-knowledge assessment and has not been independently certified.
