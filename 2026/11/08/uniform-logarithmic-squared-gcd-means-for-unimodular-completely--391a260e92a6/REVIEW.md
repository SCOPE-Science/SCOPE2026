# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Jordan identity for the GCD-square kernel gives an exact nonnegative square decomposition. Complete multiplicativity and unit modulus remove the divisor-dependent phase exactly. The factor \(J_2(d)/d^2=\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(e)/e^2\) converts the problem into a floor-divisor sum. In that sum, the floor-error is bounded by two harmonic sums and the omitted infinite tail by \(x\sum_{r>x}2H_r/r^2\); both are explicitly controlled. Absolute convergence of \(\sum\mu(e)/e^3\) establishes the coefficient and the uniform logarithmic-squared error. A classical Euler sum is reconstructed with a one-dimensional integral to give the unique maximum \(3\). The hypotheses, conjugation, endpoints and all infinite interchanges are specified. Finite exact checks corroborate the identities but are not used as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest source, Azimifard (arXiv:2611.00525v1), proves an asymptotic for a Möbius vector. The vector cannot be rewritten as a unimodular completely multiplicative function, and its required coprimality corrections prevent a direct specialization to the present family. This theorem has a different quantified class, absolute uniform logarithmic-squared remainder, and a global extremal-coefficient classification. The Jordan decomposition and the floor-block method are classical and explicitly credited. Searches of related GCD matrix literature and mathematical records showed no theorem stating or implying the complete new combination. Residual risk: an old general GCD-kernel identity or previously unindexed arithmetic-weights theorem may imply the result without naming these parameters; the 1998 original paper could not be inspected fully.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A recent result on Möbius-weighted GCD quadratic means motivates a direct structural comparison with completely multiplicative arithmetic weights. The present theorem settles the full unit-modulus multiplicative class, including the natural Liouville function and complex twist phases, with an absolute logarithmic error rather than just a power-saving remainder, and identifies the exact unique extremizer of the linear coefficient. This is a nontrivial analytic mean-value classification of a natural number-theoretic kernel, not an isolated substitution or a small finite improvement. It does not solve the Möbius case or unrelated GCD spectral extremal questions.

## Remaining scientific limitations
Historical priority is qualified by inaccessible older full text and possible equivalent unindexed formulations. The main theorem does not extend to weights with zeros and makes no exact optimality assertion for the logarithm power. No independent audit has been completed.
