# Uniform online dimension three for maximal cliques of line graphs and their complete-graph shattering polynomial

## Finding
For any finite simple graph \(G\), let \(L(G)\) be the line graph with vertices \(E(G)\): two distinct edges are adjacent exactly when they share an endpoint in \(G\). Let \(\mathcal C(G)\) contain the vertex sets of all nonempty inclusion-maximal cliques of \(L(G)\), interpreted as functions \(E(G)\to\{0,1\}\) by incidence. For the graph with no edges, put both dimensions equal to zero.

**Theorem.** For every finite simple \(G\),
\[\operatorname{VCdim}\mathcal C(G)\le\operatorname{Ldim}\mathcal C(G)\le3.\]
For every integer \(n\ge4\), both inequalities are sharp on \(G=K_n\). In fact every \(S\subseteq E(K_n)\) is shattered by \(\mathcal C(K_n)\) precisely when its edges pairwise intersect and \(|S|\le3\). Consequently, if \(s_k(n)\) counts shattered supports of size \(k\),
\[\Phi_n(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}s_k(n)z^k=1+\binom n2 z+3\binom n3 z^2+\left(\binom n3+n\binom{n-1}3\right)z^3.\]
Thus \(|\mathcal C(K_n)|=n+\binom n3\) grows cubically while the worst-case optimal realizable online mistake bound stays exactly three.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, loopless and without parallel edges. A clique is nonempty and inclusion-maximal, not necessarily maximum-cardinality. The query universe is the **edges of the underlying graph**, equivalently the vertices of its line graph; it is not the vertex-neighborhood concept class of either graph. A support is *shattered* if every binary assignment to it is realized by at least one concept. The Littlestone dimension is the maximum depth of a complete binary mistake tree with each internal node labeled by a query and every root-to-leaf binary answer pattern realized by a concept. This is the standard arbitrary-query adaptive model. If the concept class is empty, use dimension zero.

## Proof
**1. Pair codegree lemma.** Any two distinct vertices of \(L(G)\) belong to at most two inclusion-maximal cliques of \(L(G)\). Indeed, nonadjacent vertices belong to none. Otherwise the corresponding edges of \(G\) have the form \(ab\) and \(ac\) with \(a,b,c\) distinct. Any edge meeting both is either incident with \(a\), or it is precisely \(bc\). Thus any clique containing \(ab\) and \(ac\) is contained in the full edge-star at \(a\) or in the three-edge triangle \(\{ab,ac,bc\}\), if that triangle exists. At most one maximal clique of each form contains the pair. This gives codegree at most two without assuming regularity, connectivity or a minimum degree.

**2. Adaptive dimension upper bound.** Suppose a complete mistake tree of depth four existed. Write \(e\) for its root query and \(f\) for the second query on the positive branch. Because both answers to the second query must be possible after \(e=1\), we have \(f\ne e\). The branch answering positive to both queries must contain a complete subtree of depth two; its four leaves require four *distinct* concepts containing both \(e\) and \(f\). This contradicts the codegree-two lemma. So the Littlestone dimension is at most three. The general inequality \(\operatorname{VCdim}\le\operatorname{Ldim}\) follows by querying the points of a shattered support in a fixed order, yielding a complete adaptive mistake tree.

**3. Maximal cliques for complete underlying graphs.** For \(n\ge4\), every maximal pairwise-intersecting collection of edges of \(K_n\) is either the full star \(S_a=\{ab:b\in[n]\setminus\{a\}\}\), or the three edges \(T_{abc}=\{ab,ac,bc\}\) of a triangle. To see completeness, take two distinct intersecting edges \(ab,ac\); every further member belongs to their star or is \(bc\). If \(bc\) occurs, no additional edge can meet all three, so the family is the triangle. If it does not occur, all edges share \(a\), and maximality forces the full star. Each star is maximal since it has at least three spokes, and each triangle is maximal. The families are distinct, so there are \(n+\binom n3\) concepts.

**4. Exact shattered-support characterization.** A shattered support cannot contain disjoint edges because its all-positive pattern would require a pairwise-intersecting concept. It also cannot have size at least four by step 2. For sufficiency, every intersecting triple of distinct edges of a simple graph is either three spokes \(\{ab,ac,ad\}\) or a triangle \(\{ab,ac,bc\}\).

For the three-spoke support, the four possible positive-set sizes are all realizable: \(S_a\) realizes all three, \(T_{abc}\) realizes \(\{ab,ac\}\), \(S_b\) realizes \(\{ab\}\), and \(T_{bcd}\) realizes none. Permuting the leaves \(b,c,d\) realizes every pattern of each size. Here \(a,b,c,d\) are pairwise distinct, so these witnesses exist already for \(n=4\).

For the triangle support, \(T_{abc}\) realizes all three, \(S_a\) realizes \(\{ab,ac\}\), \(T_{abd}\) realizes \(\{ab\}\), and \(S_d\) realizes none, with any \(d\) outside \(\{a,b,c\}\). Relabeling its three vertices realizes all patterns. Every intersecting pair of edges can be extended to a three-spoke support, and every single edge can too (because \(n\ge4\)); shattering passes to subsets. The empty support is shattered because the concept class is nonempty. This proves the stated if-and-only-if condition and exhibits a shattered triple, giving both dimensions exactly three.

**5. Count.** There are \(\binom n2\) singleton supports. Two-element intersecting supports are uniquely given by a center and two other vertices, hence \(n\binom{n-1}2=3\binom n3\). Every intersecting three-element support is either a triangle (\(\binom n3\) choices) or a three-spoke set (\(n\binom{n-1}3\) choices). There are no shattered larger supports. Summing produces the claimed polynomial.

## Verification
The standalone pure-Python program `check_max_clique_learning.py` independently enumerates **all** edge subsets of \(K_n\) for \(n=4,5,6\), retains those that are maximal pairwise-intersecting, computes every shattered support and calculates Littlestone dimension by the exact two-branch recurrence. It obtains respectively \(8,15,26\) hypotheses, dimension three, and support-count vectors \((1,6,12,8)\), \((1,10,30,30)\), \((1,15,60,80)\). It separately enumerates every nonempty simple graph on \(2,3,4,5\) labeled vertices (1, 7, 63, and 1023 graphs) and checks both dimensions never exceed three: \(1094\) graphs total. These finite checks are not the proof for unbounded graphs, which is in steps 1–5.

Reproduce using Python 3 with `python check_max_clique_learning.py`. No libraries outside the Python standard library are needed.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical rank-two intersecting-family classification distinguishes full stars and triangles; the new invariant here is the complete family of **maximal** cliques considered jointly as binary concepts and, particularly, its uniform adaptive shattering bound. Frankl and Pach (2020) investigate shattering matchings by a *fixed* maximal intersecting hypergraph with middle-uniform sets: their quantifiers and query domain differ from the present family-of-families incidence problem. Bujtás, Davoodi, Győri and Tuza (2016/2020) study clique coverings of claw-free graphs and local clique-cover numbers, not VC or Littlestone dimensions of maximal-clique incidence. Hanneke, Livni and Moran (2021) establish general relationships in online learning rather than dimensions for this graph class. Manurangsi (2022/2023) gives hardness results for arbitrary explicit concept matrices, not the special pair-codegree bound or exact coefficients here. These papers motivate distinguishing uniform structural learnability from unrestricted computation; none of the inspected claims states or implies the theorem or polynomial by a documented specialization. Graph neighborhood incidence is not maximal-clique incidence, so dimension results for the former are not an immediate source of this theorem.

## Limitations
No exact formula for the two dimensions for each *individual* graph \(G\) is claimed: only the sharp universal upper bound and the exact complete-graph family are established. The theorem applies to **simple** graphs; multigraph line graphs may have different clique multiplicities. Historical priority beyond the inspected literature and indexed searches is not certified. The brute-force checks stop at the stated finite sizes. This proof does not claim a new maximal-clique structural classification independent of its classical star/triangle description.
