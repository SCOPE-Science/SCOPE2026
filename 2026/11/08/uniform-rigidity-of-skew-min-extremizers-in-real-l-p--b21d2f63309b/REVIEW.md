# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The argument uses the orthogonal scalar transform \(M_{r,s}\), exact inequalities between \(\ell_p^2\) and Euclidean norms, and the strict equality cases in those inequalities. The two-output minimax bound follows after integration, while the sharp witnesses show attainment. The near-equality equivalence is proved using the compact zero set of a homogeneous scalar defect on a fixed two-dimensional sphere, then integrating the pointwise distance to the equality cones against the input \(p\)-mass. The quartic identity is an exact binomial expansion. The standalone finite checker passed 5,514 assertions and is not used as an infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The 2026 skew-parameterized paper introduces the invariant. The 2026 Schäffer paper (Theorem 3.2) and an accessible two-coordinate generalized-James result supply matrix norm mechanisms that already cover the sharp *value* of the invariant; neither is presented as a novelty. Their stated results and proof passages do not classify all norming pairs or all near-maximizing sequences for the **minimum of two norms with independently Euclidean-normalized coefficients**. The focused new results are the uniform necessary-and-sufficient rigidity across varying real \(L^p\) spaces and the quartic defect decomposition. A differently worded broad stability theorem for equality in two-coordinate \(L^p\) operator inequalities could subsume these refinements; no absolute priority claim is made. Older original sources were checked where accessible, with an abstract-only limitation for a 2019 paper.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Extremal values do not determine whether the coefficient parameter must balance or which input-support structures can nearly attain the maximum. This theorem supplies the complete optimizer geometry and a dimension-free characterization of near-optimizing sequences, distinguishing disjointness and signed-mass balancing across the Hilbert exponent. Its exact quartic mismatch/bias formula provides a usable positive defect with a quantitative norm-distance consequence, not just a repetition of the known value. These data are natural for stability, extremal configurations and geometry of the recently defined skew invariant. The claim does not purport to give a general Clarkson stability theorem.

## Closest literature and limits

Xu–Liu, arXiv:2610.03050, Theorem 3.2, covers a power-mean extremum and matrix norm equality. Rao–Wu–Liu–Ni–Wang, arXiv:2504.00827, provides earlier skew James definitions. The accessible mathematical record *Two-coordinate operator-norm formula for a generalized James constant* covers the value reduction but does not contain the uniform sequence classification. The record *Exact skew geometric mean constant of ell_4* contains a related \(\ell_4^2\)-norm formula but no quartic minimum-defect identity. An older foundational journal article was accessible only in abstract/preview form. A publication-priority risk remains from stronger unstated consequences of general uniform-convexity stability literature.
