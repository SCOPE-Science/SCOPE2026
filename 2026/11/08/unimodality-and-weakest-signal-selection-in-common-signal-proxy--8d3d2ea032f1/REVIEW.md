# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact increment is derived by exposing the vote of one newly delegating nonexpert and using the two pivotal binomial point masses. The positive and negative parts of each ex-ante increment scale respectively by factors strictly below and strictly above two, proving single crossing across the entire signal mixture. For a unique weakest state, strict monotonicity of \(q(1-q)\) on \((1/2,1)\), plus a uniform finite upper bound on the number of optimal proxies, justifies the asymptotic log-odds formula. The adjacent rational example explicitly checks the competence boundary. The included exact-rational standalone checker reports 5,760 independent increment comparisons and 1,280 unimodality cases and confirms the rational example and rare-state selection. Finite checks are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The 2023 preprint and 2026 article of Dhillon et al. already prove the one-environment log-odds rule (Section 5.3.1); this specialization is explicitly excluded from novelty. Gilbert et al. 2026 introduce a common-signal model and delegation guarantees but the full six-page proceedings version contains neither a star-optimizer single-crossing result nor a weakest-state selection law. Targeted published-record searches for these statements, reductions and parameters returned unrelated voting-power or general majority results; comparison with accessible prior-claim text found no covering theorem. The longer 2026 preprint was unavailable to inspect: an unindexed equivalent remains a meaningful residual risk. The present theorem is about optimization under a finite mixture, not the componentwise optimization already known in the scalar model.

## Value — PASS

The result answers a natural inverse-design problem in a new signal-dependent delegation setting: whether proxying success can have several local maxima and how the optimal proxy mass scales with committee size. Its single-crossing theorem gives a globally reliable one-dimensional optimization method, while the rare-state result warns that vanishingly small but fixed environment priors can determine optimal delegation in large committees. The explicit sharp competence boundary clarifies exactly why conditional voter reliability matters.

## Scope and residual risk

The theorem covers a symmetric single-expert star, not strategic equilibria or arbitrary graphs. Literature comparison used the complete six-page 2026 conference version and the accessible full text of the older scalar model, but could not inspect the longer common-signal arXiv paper. Novelty should be independently verified before publication.
