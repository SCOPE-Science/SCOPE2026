When there are at least as many agents as goods and each agent needs a fixed set of at least two complementary goods, maximum complete envy-free welfare is exactly the packing number of requests made by just one agent; pair demands are polynomial-time solvable by graph matching, whereas every fixed request size of at least three is NP-hard. Under independent uniformly random requests, welfare undergoes a universal Poisson crowd-out transition at \(n/\binom mr=\log\binom mr+\log\log\binom mr+t\), even though adding agents initially appears to create more opportunities for satisfying demands.
