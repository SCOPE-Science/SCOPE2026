# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The explicitly affine epoch representation and exchangeable without-replacement covariances yield the exact variance identity. Strict rearrangement of the strictly ordered geometric weights proves uniqueness of reverse position pairing; AM–GM proves the negative covariance and strict factor-two gain; centered weight expansions and Riemann limits prove the two asymptotic regimes; independent fresh epochs give an exact AR(1) stationary law. The code's 460 finite checks are not substituted for any infinite argument. A ratio at zero center variance is deliberately excluded as undefined.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The motivating 2026 paper already introduced reversal averaging and the generic cancellation of the second-order order-dependent term, so those are NOT attributed as new. Comparison of its full text including Theorems 3.6, 3.9, and appendix, plus available full texts on reshuffling noise and optimal rates, did not reveal a complete all-fixed-pairing optimality and exact epoch-size crossover/steady-state variance law in the specified equal-curvature family. The strict rearrangement inequality itself is classical; novelty attaches to the combined algorithm-level exact statement and its scaling classification. More general antithetic-pairing theory or unindexed equivalent formula may affect priority. The recent centered-permutation paper was accessible only by abstract, and that limitation is not presented as a full-text exclusion.

**Value — PASS.** The paper motivates reduction of permutation-sensitive noise by paired reversal but its general bounds do not quantify the exact benefit or prove the choice best in the natural class of fixed order rearrangements. The result resolves precisely that policy choice and exhibits an effective-epoch-step crossover, identifying the regime where a two-pass implementation improves beyond an ordinary factor-two averaging intuition. It gives exactly the same gain in the stationary optimization error floor, which matters for constant-step shuffled SGD. Restricting the guarantee to its natural equal-curvature test family and recognizing the two-pass computational cost prevent overgeneralization.

**Closest literature and residual risks.** Nguyen–Phan–Kalagnanam (2026) establishes the symmetrization principle but not this complete sharp classification; Domingo-Enrich (2022) derives stationary shuffling estimates through noise-model spectral analysis but not the antithetic coupling optimum; Safran–Shamir (2020) establishes rates rather than exact coupling variance. The main risks are earlier unindexed applications of classical rearrangement to linear SGD and inaccessible appendices in later broad reshuffling literature. The proof is exact arithmetic and one-dimensional, with no guarantee of improvement per unit compute in general applications.
