## Correctness — PASS
The exact trace uses unitary-radial monomial normalization, the homogeneous kernel diagonal, and the positive commutator decomposition \(H_{\overline f}^*H_{\overline f}=M_f^*M_f-M_fM_f^*\). After homogeneous cross-terms drop, the initial and final degrees telescope to an exact boundary shell. Tilting the radial probability by increasing moments establishes \(m_{L+k}/m_L\to1\). The normalized shell is bounded by \(k\), so dominated convergence for finite boundary energy and Fatou for infinite boundary energy establish all quantifiers. The accompanying checker passed 930 finite exact/numerical assertions; it is corroboration and not a proof of the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
Clos (2026) proves a qualitative non-Hilbert–Schmidt theorem on smooth strongly pseudoconvex domains, not a first-power finite-cutoff trace law. Arazy–Fisher–Janson–Peetre (1991) provides Schatten ideal membership for conventional weighted balls but its accessible abstract does not state this formula; its unavailable full text is a declared priority risk, not presumed negative evidence. Engliš–Guo–Zhang (2007) and Engliš (2010) give critical-power Dixmier traces rather than ordinary squared-Hankel degree cutoffs; those do not yield this exact moment-shell identity for arbitrary singular radial measures. Tang–Wang–Zheng (2022) concerns traces in trace-class regimes and does not imply the divergent first-power cutoff law. Previous related mathematical records concerning radial spaces, Bergman traces and commutators were compared by actual semantic search; the most similar statements address different quantities. The familiar one-dimensional Hilbert–Schmidt identity is acknowledged and not claimed new.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exact, full-symbol, full-radial-measure finite-scale spectral identity. It identifies a universal leading coefficient that survives changing a smooth ball weight to boundary Hardy measure or to singular mixtures, and gives an explicit sharp growth law for the non-Hilbert–Schmidt divergence in dimension at least two. It quantitatively bridges the classical disk Hilbert–Schmidt identity and the known higher-dimensional obstruction. The result has natural relevance for truncation-based numerical Hankel energy and for comparison of Hilbert-function growth across radial analytic spaces.

## Limits and remaining risks
The statement excludes nonradial weights and symbols without bounded multiplier control. The cutoff is by homogeneous degree, not by singular-value order. An inaccessible 1991 full text and potentially equivalent older radial commutator calculations remain genuine originality risks. The statement has no independent priority or mathematical audit.
