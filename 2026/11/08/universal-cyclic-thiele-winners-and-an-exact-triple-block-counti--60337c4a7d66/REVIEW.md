# Scientific assessment: cyclic consecutive-block Thiele winners

## Correctness — PASS
The domain consists of all cyclic translates of one fixed approval block, with equal voter weights. Each selected candidate occurs in exactly \(r\) ballots, so the integer coverage vector has total \(rk\). Strictly decreasing Thiele increments make the sum strictly Schur-concave on this fixed-sum integer domain: any two coverage counts differing by at least two admit an improving Robin Hood transfer in the abstract count vector. This bounds every score and makes the upper-bound equality condition necessary. The explicit periodic floor-difference committee attains the bound for all \(n,r,k\), proving sufficiency, including endpoints. For triple blocks, a sparse winner has gaps at least three; the dense case follows by complement; and middle winners are exactly cyclic binary words with run lengths one or two. The directed-gap and marked-run enumeration arguments yield the exact formulas without a missing rotational factor. The standalone verifier passed exhaustive PAV tests through \(n=16\) and tests of other strictly concave weights and block lengths. The finite computations check, but do not replace, the infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Relevant articles were compared by actual implications. Janeczko and Faliszewski (arXiv:2305.01769) study general winner-counting hardness and random interval-profile ties but give no cyclic all-winner count. Ai (arXiv:2609.08884) treats complexity of PAV winner membership, uniqueness and counting in general elections rather than this exact rotating profile. Peters and Lackner (JAIR 2020), inspected in the relevant full-text algorithmic sections, produce an optimum for the more general circle single-peaked domain, without a formula for *all* optimal committees or the triple-block count. Manurangsi and Sornat (arXiv:2604.05953) and Lassota and Sornat (arXiv:2607.28575) develop structured Thiele optimization algorithms, not the asserted full winner-family/count. A research-index search found nearby voting/cyclic-combinatorics records but no implication covering the theorem. Its strict-concavity-plus-rotational-symmetry method and run enumeration may have independently known equivalents in combinatorics of balanced circular words: priority remains qualified, and no search absence is treated as proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exact structural solution for a natural symmetric interval-approval election, a special case sitting inside much broader optimization and counting-hardness studies. The theorem identifies every optimal committee simultaneously for every strictly concave Thiele weight rule, rather than merely one optimum. Its sharp three-regime binomial enumeration measures the full selection multiplicity and changes at one-third and two-thirds density. These are motivated quantities for deterministic tie resolution, winner-set diversity and efficient exact counting. The claim is limited to full equal-weight rotational profiles and a closed count at block length three; it does not solve general circle-domain voting.
