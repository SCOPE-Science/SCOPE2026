# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The packet identities are deterministic and valid for every composition of the deck. A coordinatewise cut monotonicity argument proves strict negative covariance on every finite product of nondegenerate Bernoulli variables, including heterogeneous gap probabilities. The regeneration proof uses i.i.d. geometric packet lengths, a two-dimensional renewal-reward central limit theorem, and uniform moments of the boundary packet. The covariance formulas have been reconstructed from first four geometric moments and the odd-part moment generating function; the limiting matrix is positive definite throughout \(0<p<1\). Independent exact rational checks are included. Finite checks are not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
The original 2026 paper focuses on iterated mixing and derives a one-card spectrum; the inspected main results and relevant proof sections do not provide one-pass fixed-point/inversion covariance. Earlier generic composition central-limit theory includes the Gaussian mechanism, so novelty is deliberately limited to the explicit model-specific joint covariance, strict sign under all independent heterogeneous cut laws, and monotone exact correlation map. Searches for the exact objects and their random-composition aliases found no explicit covering statement. Some older composition and shuffle literature remains a historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Fixed-point count and Kendall inversion count are canonical, complementary measures of how much a shuffle changes a deck. Their exact dependence matters when combining these observables rather than implicitly treating them as independent. The strict finite-size law is robust to position-dependent cut rates; the sharp homogeneous correlation curve quantifies an informative, nonconstant dependence across the entire parameter interval, with no residual unknown constant. This is more than a generic CLT: it gives a universal sign and an explicit correlation limit with strict monotonicity.
