# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For N=4d, all coefficient-pair terms of the intensity except the top diagonal have degree at most N−2; the top diagonal is |a_d|²(T²+T⁴)^d, so [T^(N−1)]I=0, including when degree(p)<d. For distinct sampling nodes any nonzero interpolation-kernel polynomial must be a constant times the monic node product with coefficient −sum(t_j) at T^(N−1). This proves invertibility under exactly the stated nonzero-sum condition for the intensity space and explains the positive-node result. Equal intensities for nonzero polynomials yield the rational identity p(phi)/q(phi)=q*(psi)/p*(psi). The two fiber reflections preserve the two sides and generate a translation by 2i. A rational function invariant under that translation has no finite poles and then must be constant, giving unimodular equivalence since phi is surjective. Zero data force the zero polynomial because the sites are distinct and outnumber its possible roots. At d=1 the Hermitian coordinates realize all four coefficients (1,T,T²,T⁴); at zero node sum the nonzero lifted kernel cannot contain a semidefinite form because the site vectors span C². A rank-one positive part minus a rank-one negative part gives the claimed ambiguous pair. The same lifted argument plus compactness establishes fixed-design stability; no uniform constant is asserted and higher-degree necessity is not proved.

## value

PASS

The exact deterministic design rule removes one sample from the prior every-node guarantee while letting a practitioner select all sites in an arbitrarily short prescribed positive interval without a genericity assumption or adaptation to the signal. Linear inversion recovers the entire intensity profile and identifies a real obstruction in the smallest degree; the result does not promise good conditioning for clustered samples or a global information-theoretic optimum.
