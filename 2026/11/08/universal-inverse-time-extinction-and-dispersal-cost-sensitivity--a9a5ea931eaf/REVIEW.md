# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement has explicit positive mixing, growth and competition parameters, strictly subunit dispersal-probability columns, the equality \(\rho(M)=1\), and normalized Perron vectors. The proof gives a strict positive linear Lyapunov decrement for every nonzero nonnegative state and proves global extinction without assuming monotone nonlinear transport. Uniform Taylor expansion on the compact normalized simplex, contraction on the non-Perron invariant subspace, and a reciprocal-increment argument establish the exact \(t^{-1}\) coefficient. The strict cost derivative follows from the displayed coefficient. The rational example gives \(\alpha=1/2\) versus \(5/4\), and independent arithmetic checks in `verify.py` reproduce it. Numerical tests are supporting evidence, not infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, residual priority uncertainty
The 2024 motivating manuscript treats its extinction theorem under \(\rho(A(0))<1\) and positive persistence under \(\rho(A(0))>1\); it does not state the critical global inverse-time limit. Kirkland–Li–Schreiber (2006) establish qualitative extinction for constant-dispersal models including critical cases. Cushing–Farrell (2020) provide general local bifurcation analysis and quadratic diagnostic coefficients; the Perron projection here is a standard ingredient and is explicitly not claimed to be a novel method. The composite global, initial-state-independent limit, its explicit all-patch coefficient with nonconstant density-dependent dispersal and the strict dispersal-cost comparison are not covered by those identified conclusions. A general critical nonlinear-map asymptotics theorem may subsume the result, and coverage outside inspected sources remains an actual risk.

## Value — PASS
At the extinction–persistence boundary, binary spectral tests alone do not quantify how long an ecological population persists near low density. This result fixes the precise leading extinction amplitude in every patch for a natural bounded-reproduction/costly-migration family and distinguishes landscapes with exactly the same low-density growth matrix. The positive derivative formula lets the density-dependent dispersal response be ranked quantitatively even when the nonlinear map is not monotone, a relevant model-level issue rather than a change of variables or an arbitrary finite enumeration. General parameter families and arbitrary nonzero initial states are covered; neither a global general center-manifold theorem nor unmeasured empirical ecology is asserted.

## Closest literature and limitations
McGrane-Corrigan, Mason and de Andrade Moral (arXiv:2405.04505, 2024) is the exact encompassing model; its full accessible manuscript was compared through the stability, critical-threshold, positive-equilibrium and persistence sections. Kirkland–Li–Schreiber (DOI:10.1137/050628933) is closest on critical qualitative extinction under constant dispersal. Cushing–Farrell (DOI:10.1080/10236198.2019.1699916) has a general nonlinear matrix bifurcation theorem; its author manuscript treats branches of equilibria rather than a global inverse-time trajectory limit. Related general nonlinear center-manifold asymptotics remain the highest priority risk. The theorem requires strictly positive critical mixing, positive local competition, and finite-dimensional deterministic dynamics. It does not imply an ordering at finite times or a stochastic mean-extinction-time law.
