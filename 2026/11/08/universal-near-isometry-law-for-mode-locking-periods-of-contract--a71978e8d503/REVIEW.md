# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The assertion is a single joint asymptotic law with an effective smooth-test bound and moment consequences. The original rational-plateau endpoints give the exact numerator-independent weight, including the zero-rotation atom; rational parameter exceptions are a null set by the source's theorem. Möbius inversion supplies a uniform reduced-residue discrepancy, and the weighted divisor-sum error is \(O(\tau\log^2(1/\tau))\). The explicit summatory-totient estimate and Abel summation produce the factorized limit. Two tail inequalities control the origin and infinity; uniform exponential tails justify moments. Telescoping the digit equation proves \(|\delta-\rho|\leq1-\lambda\). The exact finite-parameter weights, normalization and numerical moment constants were reproduced in the packaged checker. The proof does not infer infinite claims from finite checks.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Laurent--Nogueira's Theorem 3 (arXiv:1704.05130v3, Section 1) establishes rational rotation plateaus and their widths; Theorem 4 establishes a null singular parameter set. Those results determine finite-parameter probabilities but do not state the \(\tau q\) distribution, effective joint asymptotic independence from the rotation number, or convergence of all scaled moments. The derivation uses a new combined arithmetic averaging and reduced-fraction discrepancy argument beyond quoting that endpoint formula. Gaivão--Laurent--Nogueira (arXiv:2212.10944v1, Theorems of Section 5 and Section 8) concern parameter-to-rotation formulas for more general two-branch maps, not this near-identity statistical law; Guiraud and collaborators (arXiv:2501.16263, introduction and main results) address three-interval dynamics. Existing publicly indexed mathematical records about logistic smoothing of rational circle rotations, integer quantized affine contractions and rotated odometers address different systems and do not imply this theorem. Ding--Hemmer (1987), accessible only in abstract, and Bugeaud--Conze (1999), accessible bibliographically, are remaining priority risks, not represented as having been read completely. A positive search outcome is not a proof of absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The denominator of a rational rotation number is the least attracting period: its behavior is a natural quantitative invariant of mode locking. The near-identity limit is singular, with individual plateau widths tending to zero while the typical denominator diverges. The exact nontrivial universal density, finite mean constant, product-limit independence and explicit smooth-test rate quantify typical long-period mode locking, substantially beyond isolated rational plateau formulas. This information can be used to predict period statistics from detuning sampling, and it is not a mere normalization check or a fixed small-parameter special case.

## Limitations and sources
This is a one-slope, uniformly sampled parameter result. Test-function rates do not cover arbitrary discontinuous statistics, and no optimality of the rate is established. The reference family is detailed in Laurent--Nogueira (2017/2018); related primary comparisons include Gaivão--Laurent--Nogueira (2022), Guiraud et al. (2025), and the older works listed above. The exact checker supports but does not certify infinite assertions.
