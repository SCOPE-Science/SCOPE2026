# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is restricted to shared zero-one utilities and a bipartite complete resource graph, with explicit empty-after-deletion convention. The necessity proof applies all-one valuations and distinguishes whether balanced bundle sizes are all at least two or include singleton bundles. The two sufficiency constructions cover every binary profile in the permitted lower and upper ranges. The nonstandard removable-positive lemma is verified from the exact vertex-deletion geometry; the auxiliary matching argument uses the precise deleted-positive-positive bipartite graph. The exact EGF count follows from a complete classification of feasible bundle compositions and exclusion of the all-high duplicate case. Independent brute-force finite checks agree on 304 small profile/agent cases, and 252 all-one coefficient/threshold cases; these are corroboration, not proof of the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Bei–Igarashi–Lu–Suksompong prove ordinary EF1 connected allocations in a lower-agent regime, whereas outer-EF1 is strictly a different deletion requirement, and neither their statements nor the read relevant sections give the fixed-profile coefficient count and the complete two-interval universal binary spectrum. The more recent Bousquet–Connor–Totschnig–Zeitoun source discusses beyond-traceability fairness and binary spectra on other families; its accessible introduction and stated results were compared, but the remaining primary manuscript could not be fully inspected. Igarashi–Zwicker give the outer-EF1 framework, not this K-bipartite classification. General equitable partition work covers the all-one balanced-size boundary but not arbitrary binary valuations or the full four-type count. Publication priority remains uncertain if an equivalent argument appears elsewhere.

## Value — PASS

Graph-constrained fair allocation asks which graph families and numbers of agents support fair connected allocations for *every* valuation in a given class. Here the universal feasibility spectrum contains a potentially arbitrarily long interior gap, despite universal existence at both small and large agent counts; this is a structural boundary, not a numeric example. The exact fixed-profile count distinguishes merely finding one fair allocation from determining how many exist and permits quantitative reliability analysis and uniform sampling in the structured family. The work needs no speculative downstream theorem to make these precise questions natural.
