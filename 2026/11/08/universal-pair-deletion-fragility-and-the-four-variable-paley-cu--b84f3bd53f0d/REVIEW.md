# Review of universal pair-deletion fragility in Paley graphs

## Correctness — PASS

The exact three-pattern character expansion includes the contributions at the three base vertices, which must be excluded. Its one- and two-character sums respectively vanish and equal \(-1\), and its cubic character sum satisfies the Hasse bound \(|T|\le2\sqrt q\). Thus every pattern has at least three witnesses for allowable \(q\ge49\). The integer rounding at \(q=49\) is essential. A permanent four-pebble strategy proves that the three-extension property is equivalent to sharing the random graph's counting-free four-variable theory. For the exceptional orders, exhaustive integer computations establish a minimum witness count of two; independent six-pair certificates, combined with the two pair-orbits of Paley graph automorphisms, establish failure after **every** two-vertex deletion. The computations were rerun from their standalone programs; the general finite-field conclusion uses the proof, not extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Ananchuen and Caccetta (1993), DOI 10.1002/net.3230230404, introduced generalized witness-multiplicity properties of Paley graphs, establishing sufficiently-large-order results; only their abstract and bibliographic material could be read. Bonato, Holzmann and Kharaghani (2001), DOI 10.37236/1545, discuss the classical 3-extension threshold and other graph families. The full accessible text of *Existentially closed critical Cayley graphs* (2025), DOI 10.61091/ars164-02, notes one-deletion persistence for the order-29 Paley graph, without stating the sharp **universal pair-deletion dichotomy** in the inspected text. Equivalent-formulation, stronger-coverage and exact finite-table searches did not find this combined assertion. A stronger, inaccessible theorem in the 1993 study could subsume part or all of the large-order half; originality and historical priority therefore remain qualified, not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Three-extension multiplicity is a natural extremal invariant controlling resilience under adversarial vertex deletion. The finding gives a complete sharp phase transition for every eligible Paley order at least 29, rather than a sufficient asymptotic estimate, and simultaneously classifies the effect of arbitrary pair deletions on a four-variable logical theory. The small-order phenomenon is unusually strong: each individual vertex is harmless, yet **every** pair is fatal. The six compact witness certificates, automorphism reduction and uniform character argument explain the boundary structurally.

## Boundaries and unresolved checks

The work does not classify larger deletion sets, arbitrary finite graphs, enriched signatures, or full first-order equivalence. A 1993 primary full text remains inaccessible, and an unidentified stronger literature result cannot be excluded. Independently reviewing the proof and historical priority remains necessary.
