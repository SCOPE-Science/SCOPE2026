# Scientific assessment

**Correctness (PASS).** The claim concerns positive probability measures on a fixed bounded subset of the nonnegative half-line, dilated by a small positive scalar. The rank-one limiting operator has a simple isolated eigenvalue. Its leading eigenvector and the Szegő-kernel manifold agree through first order, but differ at second order by the moment variance. The compression onto the complement of the constant vector is of norm at most order two in the dilation parameter; the norm-testing deficit is therefore half the squared second-order distance, giving the exact quartic coefficient. The all-parameter quantifiers, normalizations, existence of kernel maximizers and point-mass boundary are covered. The 20 finite assertions in `verify.py` independently corroborate sample coefficients without replacing the proof.

**Originality (PASS, best-knowledge).** Dyakonov's 2026 paper, specifically its *version 3 addendum*, gives the qualitative complete classification of compact Hankel operators obeying the strong reproducing kernel thesis. That coverage is explicitly acknowledged. The new theorem is the universal quantitative quartic rate and variance-squared coefficient under concentration, not the already-known strict inequality. The 2013 positive moment-matrix paper establishes compactness and Schatten criteria, not this local norm-test stability law. Published finite-rank examples and Hilbert-matrix gaps likewise do not give the sharp uniform-in-measure coefficient. A general unlocated stability theorem may still pose a priority risk.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The exact coefficient is a natural local stability invariant at the rank-one boundary of the moment-Hankel cone. It explains why a first-order perturbation is invisible to kernel testing but a second-moment defect creates a fourth-order norm gap. The class includes every compactly supported positive probability measure, finite or continuous, and produces exact test cases \(1/32\) and \(1/288\). The result is substantially more informative than qualitative nonattainment and requires no proposed downstream theorem.
