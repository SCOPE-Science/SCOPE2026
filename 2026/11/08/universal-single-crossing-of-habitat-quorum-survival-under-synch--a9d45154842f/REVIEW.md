# Review of the single-crossing habitat-quorum theorem

## Correctness (PASS)

The proof is complete for all integers \(n\ge2\), \(1\le k\le n\), every rate \(e>0\), every ordered pair \(0\le g_1<g_2\le e\), and all positive times. The binomial-tail elasticity decreases strictly from \(k\) to zero for intermediate quorums. The exact integral for the normalized log survival ratio is strictly increasing in time and has opposite strict endpoint limits, establishing exactly one crossing. The endpoint quorum cases have strict constant sign. The exact three-patch mean and its unique interior maximizer follow from an exhaustive quadratic sign analysis. A separate uniformization computation reproduces 1,142 deterministic checks, and algebraic derivatives were independently re-evaluated. No numerical test is presented as a substitute for the infinite-parameter proof.

## Originality (PASS within accessible comparisons)

The Marshall–Olkin common-shock construction and its coherent-system survival formulas predate this result. The statement being compared is the **strict one-crossing classification with fixed per-patch hazard**, plus the sharp interior mean-time optimizer, not the already standard shock-model formula. Johst–Drechsler (2003) consider correlated disturbance with patch regeneration, rather than irreversible quota survival. Ozkut–Kan (2020) provide a general coherent-shock reliability treatment, whose inspected proof and examples do not establish the fixed-marginal universal crossing. The accessible published-finding corpus results closest to hazard and catastrophe questions use different objects and claims. Bayramoglu–Ozkut (2015) and the 2016 follow-up are particularly important residual historical-priority risks: only their abstracts could be inspected, and a generic theorem in either full text might cover some of the proposed comparisons. No claim of independent historical certification is made.

## Value (PASS)

The result completely distinguishes short-horizon risk and long-horizon resilience under the same one-patch mortality rates. A requirement for two of three patches is a natural minimal occupancy quorum, and its exact optimal mixture of common and independent mortality is a non-endpoint structural law. The theorem supplies a rigorous baseline for interpreting synchrony, not an empirical metapopulation prediction; it is more substantive than a normalization check or a finite table enumeration.
