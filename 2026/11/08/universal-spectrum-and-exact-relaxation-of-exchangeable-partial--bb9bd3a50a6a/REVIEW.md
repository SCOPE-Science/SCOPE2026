# Research review: exchangeable partial-permutation shuffles

## Correctness
PASS. Uniform averages over position-subgroup permutations are orthogonal projections. Conjugation averaging makes the kernel central. Young branching gives the exact invariant-subspace dimension, hence each Fourier eigenvalue and its multiplicity. A sharp uniform-linear-extension injection bounds every nontrivial tableau ratio, with equality for shape \((n-1,1)\). These steps establish the entire spectrum and exact gap for all \(n\ge2\) and arbitrary exchangeable size laws. The standalone checker passed 1752 independent exact-rational assertions, including direct convolution trace identities.

## Originality
PASS on the accessible comparison, subject to historical-priority uncertainty. The motivating Pittel paper treats one-step record counts rather than spectral iterates. The consecutive-block shuffle of Nestoridi–Priestley–Schmid is noncentral. The Hermon–Pymar paper establishes general hypergraph interchange *bounds*, not this exact uniform-complete-hyperedge spectrum and universal two-statistic gap. The one-sided transposition spectrum of Bate–Connor–Matheau-Raven uses a different probability kernel. Actual research-index queries for exchangeable subset reshuffling, skew-tableau spectral formulas and hypergraph interchange found related but non-covering titles. A more general historic central-random-walk theorem may imply this specialization and remains the most important novelty risk.

## Value
PASS. The full, finite-size Fourier spectrum of a natural repeated smoothed-permutation model quantifies all \(L^2\) relaxation modes and yields an unexpected compression of the exact gap to two sampling statistics. The result distinguishes exchangeable complete-hyperedge shuffling from consecutive-block or one-sided-transposition dynamics and provides an exact benchmark for randomized partial-scrambling schemes. It does not assert a total-variation cutoff.

## Limitations
The gap formula is tied to uniform permutation of the selected subset and exchangeable selection. There is no independence or external audit claim. Finite verification supports, but does not replace, the proof. Related classical central-measure and hypergraph-interchange results remain a potential source of earlier coverage.
