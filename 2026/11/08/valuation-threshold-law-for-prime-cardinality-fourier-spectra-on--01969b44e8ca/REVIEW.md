# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The argument uses two precise cyclotomic facts: reduction modulo \((1-\zeta_{q^k})\) forbids a zero sum of \(p\) many \(q^k\)-th roots when the primes differ, and \(\Phi_p\) remains irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{q^k})\). These force all prime-cardinality spectral sets to be transversals. Exact Fourier-zero analysis then gives the necessary and sufficient valuation threshold. Counting graph maps yields the stated spectrum multiplicities, stratum sizes and total count. Integer-polynomial enumeration independently confirms cases with both prime orientations, including every \(p\)-subset at cyclic orders \(6,12,18\), and checks additional full transversal-pair families. The finite computations are not used to prove the infinite claim.

**Originality — PASS relative to checked material.** The full 2017 Kolountzakis–Malikiosis paper proves spectrality/tiling for the same orders and discusses mask-polynomial vanishing and explicit examples of spectra, but does not supply the complete valuation-threshold spectral-pair incidence law or the closed multiplicity distribution. The 2022 Malikiosis full paper develops general spectral-pair structure without the present formulas. The available summaries of the 2026 Somlai and Tan–Zhang papers address square-free tiling/spectrality rather than the non-square-free multilevel incidence formulas. The most relevant publicly indexed neighboring count, on four-point sets in \(\mathbb Z_{4p}\), has a different cardinality and group-parameter problem. The recent Tan–Zhang full manuscript could not be inspected; broader hidden coverage or an older equivalent formulation is a residual risk, not a claim of certified priority.

**Value — PASS.** This is a natural complete quantitative classification of all prime-cardinality orthogonal character bases in the two-prime, one-prime-power cyclic setting. The arithmetic depth acts as a sharp, symmetric compatibility threshold; all \(k+1\) multiplicity levels genuinely occur and the extremal basis counts differ by the factor \(q^{k(p-1)}\). The exact total incidence count is not implied by mere knowledge that every transversal has at least one spectrum. The classification has direct use in selecting or enumerating distinct perfect Fourier sampling bases on small spectral sets.

**Limits.** No weighted-frame, higher-cardinality or arbitrary-composite-order result is asserted. Literature comparison was bounded by the accessible full texts and bibliographic records, with no claim of an independent novelty certification.
