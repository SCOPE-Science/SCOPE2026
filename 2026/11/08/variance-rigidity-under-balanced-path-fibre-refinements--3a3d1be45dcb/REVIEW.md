# Review of the sharp path-fibre spread theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The metric specification is consistent because any pair in a single fibre is at distance at most two, and an excursion via another fibre costs at least two. The independent-conditional-pair argument proves the adjacent-fibre squared-distance bound. The equal-mixture range-variance inequality proves the nonadjacent bound without any integrality assumption. Exact summation yields a nonnegative deficit identity for every admissible function when there are at least four fibres; the three-fibre algebra is separately exact. Equality arguments use strictly positive atomic weights to turn vanishing within-fibre variance into constancy. At four fibres, endpoint saturation forces both remaining fibres to a single value by intersecting metric intervals. For three fibres the equality equations are both necessary and sufficient, subject to the original internal metric constraints. A two-layer example witnesses the boundary. Exact rational tests corroborate, but are not used as universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The closest foundational papers are Alon–Boppana–Spencer (1998), which identifies the ordinary path as a maximal-spread graph, and Addario-Berry–Janson–McDiarmid (arXiv:0902.1156), which reviews graph spread and treats random graphs and Cartesian products. The present metric is a weighted-probability *fibre refinement* of a path, not a Cartesian product or an ordinary path: there may be arbitrarily many points per fibre, each fibre may have arbitrary internal distances bounded by two, and the conditional atom weights vary independently. Neither checked relevant treatment establishes sharp preservation across these refinements, the universal exact deficit, or the four-layer equality threshold. An indexed record on geodesic-subpath numbers for path blowups concerns a different invariant; indexed records on clustered Lipschitz-free projections also concern other operators. Statement-level comparisons and searches do not rule out unindexed equivalent work. The 1998 paper was materially read in the spread/path and integer-extremizer sections; the 2009 arXiv text was examined across its section structure and relevant statements. This is a qualified literature originality assessment, not a claim of exhaustive coverage.

## Value — PASS

A natural question about spread is whether refining each path level into a genuine metric fibre can increase optimal variance. The theorem settles that question in a nontrivial, infinitely parameterized class: preservation begins sharply at three fibres, full optimizer rigidity begins at four, and the exact nonnegative deficit controls all departures from equality. It handles arbitrary fibre cardinalities, internal geometry and conditional probability distributions simultaneously; the endpoint polarization classification explains why preserving the constant does not imply preserving the optimizer structure. These are structural and stability facts about a Lipschitz-to-centered-Hilbert embedding norm, not a reindexing of the path formula.

## Closest literature and remaining limitations

The uniform path extremum is prior work and is expressly not claimed as new. The metric-fibre theorem depends on equal *total* masses across layers and the exact prescribed cross distances; it does not cover other base graphs or weighted layer totals. Relevant source texts and indexing searches may miss equivalent results in metric-measure or transportation literature. Independent audit has not been performed.
