# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The crown vertex proof uses parity to distinguish shores and an explicit two-omitted-index obstruction within shores. The edge proof uses exact vertex-to-edge distances and a zero-sum role-vector argument: every distinct edge pair differs in at least two index roles. These steps prove the all-subsets equivalence for all \(n\ge3\). The two-row rook assertion follows from the exact necessary-and-sufficient Lemma 6.2 of Cáceres et al., with the exceptional \(n=3\) witness explicitly established. Counting and TAR paths are constructive. Independent definition-level exhaustive computations confirm all assertions for specified finite orders; they are not substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)

The known rook resolving-set criterion, crown vertex metric dimension and minimum crown edge metric bases **are not** claimed as new. Specifically, Cáceres et al. (2005) fully covered the rook characterization, Saputro et al. (2011) covered crown metric dimension, and a relevant earlier record counts and classifies crown minimum edge metric bases. An accessible earlier claim also gives the crown ordinary vertex-resolving polynomial. None of these is the present all-cardinality equivalence of crown vertex/edge resolving families and complete-prism vertex resolving families. Knor et al. (2020) provide contrasting general metric/edge-metric examples, while Hakanen et al. (2020) concern the stronger \(\{2\}\)-resolving rook property. The new claim is the stronger **literal identity of feasible-set systems** and the induced identical TAR state graphs, not pre-existing optimum numbers or the prior vertex polynomial.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Equivalence of vertex and edge landmark feasibility is strictly stronger than equality of their minimum dimensions: it identifies all successful sensor configurations, all inclusion-minimal configurations, every coefficient and the entire TAR reconfiguration graph. The unexpected extension to nonisomorphic complete prisms preserves this feasibility for arbitrarily many vertices even though both adjacency and distance structures differ, with a sharp \(n=3\) boundary. This gives an exact transfer principle between naturally studied locating tasks, rather than an isolated numerical recasting.

## Risk and scope

Novelty remains provisional without exhaustive global literature indexing or independent audit. The edge-metric-bases result supplies overlapping minimum information; it does not establish this unrestricted equivalence. The main proof is self-contained except for an explicitly identified and inspected published rook characterization; the prism implication also follows by directly verifying its stated conditions. The standalone finite checks cover \(n\le6\), not all orders.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
