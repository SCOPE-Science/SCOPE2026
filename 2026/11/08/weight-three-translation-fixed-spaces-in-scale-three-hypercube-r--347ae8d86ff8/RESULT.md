# Weight-three translation fixed spaces in scale-three hypercube Rips complexes

## Finding
Let \(Q_n=\{0,1\}^n\) carry the Hamming metric and let \(X_n=\operatorname{VR}_{\leq 3}(Q_n)\) be its inclusive Vietoris–Rips simplicial complex. Let \(v\in\mathbb F_2^n\) have Hamming weight exactly three, and write \(T_v=\langle x\mapsto x+v\rangle\). For \(n=3\), the geometric fixed-point space \(|X_3|^{T_v}\) is contractible. For every \(n\geq4\), put \(m=n-3\), \(E_m=m2^{m-1}\), and \(b_m=E_m-2^m+1\). Then

\[
|X_n|^{T_v}\simeq \bigvee^{b_m}S^1\ \vee\ \bigvee^{E_m}S^3.
\]

In particular, the fixed space is \(S^3\) for \(n=4\), is \(S^1\vee\bigvee^4S^3\) for \(n=5\), and is \(\bigvee^5S^1\vee\bigvee^{12}S^3\) for \(n=6\). This is an integral homotopy classification, not just a field-homology calculation.

There is also an exact finite simplicial description. Write coordinates as three coordinates supporting \(v\), followed by \(m\) other coordinates. The fixed-point simplicial model has vertices \((w,c)\) with \(w\in\mathbb F_2^m\) and \(c\in\mathbb F_2^3/\langle(1,1,1)\rangle\), a set of four cosets. Two different vertices \((w,c)\) and \((z,d)\) are adjacent precisely when either \(w=z\), or \(w\) and \(z\) differ in exactly one coordinate and \(c\ne d\). Its clique complex is PL homeomorphic to the geometric fixed-point space.

## Assumptions and scope
The integers satisfy \(n\geq3\); the translation vector has **exactly** three nonzero coordinates; the scale is **inclusive** Hamming distance at most three; and fixed points mean fixed points of the realized simplicial complex, not just simplices on individually fixed vertices. The coordinate permutation symmetry allows the support of \(v\) to be chosen as the first three coordinates. A wedge with zero circles is interpreted as just the remaining wedge. The case \(n=3\) is treated separately.

## Proof
**1. Geometric fixed-point simplices.** The involution \(x\mapsto x+v\) acts without fixed vertices. Its vertex orbits are the two-element sets \(\{x,x+v\}\). A point of a realized simplex is fixed exactly when its barycentric coefficients agree on every orbit. Hence every geometric fixed point has a unique expression in terms of barycenters of complete vertex orbits, and a collection of orbit barycenters spans a simplex exactly when the union of its constituent orbits spans a simplex of \(X_n\). This gives a PL simplicial model because the orbit barycenters are affinely independent on every such simplex.

**2. Exact adjacency.** Quotient the first three binary coordinates by the span of the all-one vector \(v\), and denote a quotient class by \(c\). Each orbit is then uniquely indexed by \((w,c)\). For two distinct orbits, let \(q\) be the Hamming distance between their last \(m\) coordinates and let \(h\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) be the Hamming distance between chosen representatives of their first three coordinates. Among the four cross-orbit distances, the two possible values are \(q+h\) and \(q+3-h\). All are at most three precisely when \(q+\max\{h,3-h\}\leq3\). If \(q=0\), all distinct classes are compatible. If \(q=1\), compatibility requires \(h\in\{1,2\}\), which is equivalent to \(c\ne d\). If \(q\ge2\), compatibility is impossible. Each individual orbit has diameter three and is therefore admissible. Since a Rips complex is flag, these pairwise tests determine *all* fixed-point simplices.

**3. Cross-polytope blocks.** For each \(w\in\mathbb F_2^m\), the four vertices \((w,c)\) span a tetrahedron \(D_w\). For every edge \(e=\{w,z\}\) of the ordinary cube graph \(Q_m\), the induced subcomplex \(B_e\) on its eight orbit vertices has all edges except the four disjoint pairs \(\{(w,c),(z,c)\}\). Thus \(B_e\) is the join of four zero-spheres, i.e. the boundary of a four-dimensional cross polytope, homeomorphic to \(S^3\). Both \(D_w\) and \(D_z\) are tetrahedral faces of \(B_e\), and they are disjoint. The graph \(Q_m\) has no triangles, so every clique in the global orbit graph lies either in one \(D_w\) or in some \(B_e\). Distinct \(B_e\)'s intersect exactly in the tetrahedron corresponding to a shared endpoint of their cube edges, and are disjoint if their edges are disjoint.

**4. Gluing lemma and homotopy.** We use the following standard graph-of-spaces observation, with a proof here for the particular setting: if connected CW edge-spaces \(B_e\simeq S^3\) are glued along *disjoint, contractible* vertex subcomplexes \(D_w\), each incident edge-space meeting the others only in its appropriate \(D_w\), then the total space is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee_{e}S^3\vee |G|\), where \(G\) is the underlying connected incidence graph. To see this, contract each disjoint tetrahedron \(D_w\) to a point. These contractions are homotopy equivalences by the cofibration property of subcomplex inclusions and contractibility. Each edge-block stays homotopy equivalent to \(S^3\), with its two endpoint tetrahedra replaced by distinct marked points. More explicitly, choose a path between the two marked points of each edge block. Map an interval with an attached copy of \(S^3\) to that edge block by sending the interval along the chosen path and the sphere by a based degree-one map. Both spaces are simply connected and the map is an integral homology isomorphism, hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead. It fixes the two endpoint points and is therefore a homotopy equivalence relative to them by the homotopy extension property for CW pairs. Doing this simultaneously for all edge blocks produces the cube graph \(G=Q_m\) with one three-sphere attached at each edge. A spanning-tree contraction of \(G\) gives the stated wedge. (The construction is also the homotopy-colimit decomposition of this graph of spaces.)

For \(m\ge1\), the graph \(Q_m\) has \(2^m\) vertices and \(E_m=m2^{m-1}\) edges, and is connected. Its first Betti number is therefore \(E_m-2^m+1=b_m\). Applying the gluing lemma yields precisely \(E_m\) three-spheres and \(b_m\) circles. When \(m=0\), there is only one tetrahedron \(D_w\) and no edge block; the fixed-point space is contractible. This proves all cases.

## Verification
The standalone script `verify_weight3.py`, requiring only Python standard-library modules, derives actual orbit-compatibility edges by comparing **every pair of original cube vertices** in each pair of translation orbits. It compares these edges with the independently stated four-label cube-graph model. It then enumerates all cliques, computes the complete reduced mod-two simplicial homology, and verifies the formula for dimensions \(n=3,4,5,6,7\). For dimensions \(n=3,4,5,6\), it also checks the total compatibility-edge count for **every** choice of three-coordinate support. Run `python verify_weight3.py`; a literal output record is supplied as `verification_output.txt`.

The unrestricted integral statement is proved by the combinatorial decomposition above. Finite calculations do not certify the infinite quantifier and are used solely as independent cross-checks. No integral computer Smith normal form is needed for the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Galetto, Montaño and Wellner (arXiv:2606.20784, first submitted 2026-06-18) determine the characteristic-zero **homology representations** of the hyperoctahedral group on ordinary hypercube Rips complexes at scales up to three; they motivate examining finer geometric fixed loci. Their accessible introduction and main theorem concern rational representation decomposition, which does **not** determine the homotopy types of pointwise geometric fixed spaces. The full 41-page source could not be inspected, and a stronger uninspected section remains a residual overlap risk.

Bendersky, Elia and Grbić (arXiv:2605.00705, first submitted 2026-05-01) study ordinary hypercube Rips connectivity, coconnectivity, and cohomological classes. Their openly accessible full article includes a cross-polytope construction for an ordinary cohomology class, but no geometric translation-fixed-space theorem. Adamaszek and Adams (arXiv:2103.01040, 2021) determine ordinary scale-two Rips homotopy types. Those nonequivariant results do not imply the fixed-space classification above. Related prior unpublished or unindexed statements remain possible; the result is not claimed to establish a full equivariant homotopy classification.

## Limitations
The classification is restricted to translations of weight exactly three and to inclusive Rips scale three. It neither classifies translations of other weights at scale three nor any nontranslation automorphism. The wedge equivalence is ordinary, not asserted equivariant with respect to a residual group action, and no compatibility among different subgroup-fixed spaces is asserted. The available first paper's main body was inaccessible, which leaves a literature-overlap risk. Self-review is not independent mathematical validation.
