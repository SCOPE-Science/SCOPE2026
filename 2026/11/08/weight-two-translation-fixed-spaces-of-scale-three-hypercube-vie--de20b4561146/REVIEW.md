# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every geometric fixed barycentric point has constant coordinates on original translation orbits, giving an exact orbit model. The calculated cross-orbit distances yield the flag graph with same-type adjacency at base distance one and cross-type adjacency at base distance at most two. Each clique is supported in a two-face of the smaller cube, as proved by the maximum-distance cases for one or two edge pairs. Each square contributes a sphere \(C_4*C_4\cong S^3\), with all distinct-block intersections confined to the thickened cubical one-skeleton. This one-skeleton and each square-boundary thickening explicitly retract onto their underlying cubical skeletons. The gluing maps into sphere blocks are null-homotopic; the homotopy-pushout splitting and the cubical two-skeleton's simply connectedness establish the **integral** wedge decomposition. A separate executable check compares actual orbit distances, face sets, and complete mod-two boundary ranks for seven cube dimensions. There is no inference from finite enumeration to the all-parameter result.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest first-public 2026 paper, Galetto–Montaño–Wellner (arXiv:2606.20784v1), proves characteristic-zero **ambient homology representations**, not geometric pointwise fixed-space homotopy. The earlier Feng theorem (arXiv:2305.07084v1) proves a **nonequivariant** homotopy decomposition in ambient dimensions four and seven; it does not imply the fixed two-/three-sphere wedge. A published related neighborhood-complex result coincides with the cubical two-skeleton's second Betti rank, but does not identify the present fixed-point complex or its three-sphere blocks. Searches by orbit, translation weight, aliases, and possible stronger equivariant formulations yielded no matching theorem. This is a best-knowledge scientific assessment, not an assertion of globally proven uniqueness. Residual risk includes unindexed equivariant Rips and cubical polyhedral-complex literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The question is motivated by the recently determined full hyperoctahedral homology representations at scale three, for which pointwise fixed topology is additional information. The result gives an exact infinite-family **integral homotopy classification** for a natural weight-two translation class, exhibits a structural decomposition into square-indexed three-spheres and the cube two-skeleton, and explains mixed-dimensional fixed homology. Neither the formula nor the block decomposition is a routine restatement of the known ambient homotopy or character. The precise fixed-point functor is relevant to symmetry-aware topology and explicit equivariant examples.
