# Review of wild conductor geometry of the binary-quartic discriminant in characteristic two

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim is a complete statement about the reduced characteristic-two binary-quartic discriminant, its normalization and conductor, the conductor branch map, and the generic repeated-root incidence. The integral discriminant identity is checked by a symbolic resultant and an independent invariant reduction. The map from the smooth Segre product is finite (proper with finite fibers) and birational (inverse recovered from the two middle odd coefficients away from their common zero), hence is the normalization. The calculation \(R=A\oplus bA\lambda\subset B=A\oplus A\lambda\) establishes the conductor \((b,d)\) exactly, not merely set-theoretically, on the two main coefficient charts; the remaining points have a transverse two-branch germ. The conductor restriction is the symmetric-square map, with étale locus \(c\ne0\) and diagonal Frobenius on \(c=0\). The repeated-root incidence is a distinct generically purely inseparable extension because the generic quotient \(d/b\) is not a square. Point-count computations over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_8,\mathbb F_{16}\) match the general proof. No finite experiment is used to establish an infinite statement.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty.** Gondim–Russo's first version (2013, arXiv:1312.1618) identifies the classical Perazzo cubic and its Segre projection in characteristic zero. That prior projection is enough to anticipate the normalization, so the isolated normalization is not claimed as novel. Du Plessis–Wall (2017), Section 6.2, explicitly identifies the characteristic-two cubic invariant \(ad^2+bcd+eb^2\), its repeated-root interpretation, and the square-quartic plane; these are likewise not new. The assessed advance is the exact scheme-theoretic conductor on this discriminant, the characteristic-two branch/Frobenius identification on its conductor cover, and the contrasting purely inseparable generic repeated-root incidence. The accessible relevant passages do not give that combined implication. A later Perazzo-threefold paper has an accessible abstract but could not be inspected fully; an older account may still contain a conductor computation. This is not independent certification of priority.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The universal discriminant of degree-four binary forms is a natural moduli boundary, not an arbitrary parameter slice. In characteristic two its Cartier equation becomes a square, while its reduced support remains a nonnormal cubic. The exact conductor describes the double locus as a finite flat symmetric-square quotient and singles out a wild Frobenius line; the generic repeated-root incidence exhibits the opposite separability behavior. These distinctions explain how characteristic-two derivative geometry alters standard intuition about discriminants and normalization. This is a structural boundary statement, not a restatement of the invariant formula or a routine rational-point count.

## Relevant literature and limits
Primary material inspected: Gondim–Russo (arXiv:1312.1618), especially Example 1 and Theorem 4.2, under an explicitly characteristic-zero hypothesis; du Plessis–Wall, *The moduli space of binary quintics*, Section 6.2, including the repeated-root and pole discussion. A relevant later article on Perazzo threefolds was identifiable from its abstract but its full text was not accessible through the available route. The result does not classify binary quartics up to automorphism, and the rational-point formula counts projective coefficients. Historical originality requires separate expert review.
