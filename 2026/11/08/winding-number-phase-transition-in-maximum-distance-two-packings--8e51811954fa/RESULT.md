# Winding-number phase transition in maximum distance-two packings of triangular cylinders

## Finding
For all integers \(n\ge5\), write \(T_n=C_3\square C_n\) and \(k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). A distance-two packing is a vertex set whose distinct vertices have graph distance at least three. The maximum packing size is
\[\rho(T_n)=k.\]
Form the reconfiguration graph \(\mathcal R_n\) on maximum distance-two packings, joining two if one vertex of the first set slides along an edge of \(T_n\) to an unoccupied vertex, leaving all other selected vertices fixed.

For odd \(n=2k+1\), there are exactly \(3n2^{k-1}\) maximum packings, and \(\mathcal R_n\) is connected.

For even \(n=2k\), there are exactly \(2(2^k+2(-1)^k)\) maximum packings. Define
\[I_k=\{p\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}:2p-k\equiv0\pmod3\}.\]
For every \(p\in I_k\), there are exactly **two** components, each of order \(3\binom{k}{p}\). If \(3\mid k\), there are in addition exactly twelve isolated states; otherwise there are none. No other components occur. In particular, local mobility alternates sharply with the parity of the cylinder length, and the even components have exact winding-number sizes.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected. Identify vertices of \(T_n\) with \((i,a)\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\). Edges join equal columns with unequal row labels, and equal row labels at consecutive columns. A legal slide changes exactly one chosen vertex by one such edge. Every configuration in the reconfiguration graph has **maximum** size, not merely maximal size. Different labeled graph-vertex subsets count as different configurations. The even-case cycle of row colors has \(k\ge3\) sites; the odd-case path has \(k\ge2\) sites.

## Proof
**1. Column structure and optimum.** Two chosen vertices in the same column are adjacent. Two chosen vertices in neighboring columns are at graph distance at most two, whether or not their row labels agree. Thus a distance-two packing uses at most one vertex in each column, and its occupied columns form an independent set on \(C_n\), giving at most \(k\) vertices. Choose occupied columns spaced by at least two, and choose row labels so that selections separated by exactly two columns have different labels. Such a choice exists for every \(n\ge5\) (for even order use a proper three-coloring of a cycle; for odd order use a proper three-coloring of a path). Hence \(\rho(T_n)=k\).

For a maximum packing, list its \(k\) occupied columns in clockwise order and write the positive cyclic gaps between them. Every gap is at least two. When \(n=2k\), every gap is exactly two, so the occupied columns have one of the two parities and their row labels form a proper three-coloring of \(C_k\). When \(n=2k+1\), exactly one gap is three, all others two, and their row labels form a proper three-coloring of \(P_k\). These descriptions are necessary and sufficient, since columns at cyclic distance three or more have graph distance at least three regardless of their row labels.

**2. State counts.** A proper three-coloring of \(C_k\) has \(2^k+2(-1)^k\) choices: its count is \(\operatorname{tr}((J-I)^k)\), where \(J\) is the all-ones matrix of order three; \(J-I\) has eigenvalues two, negative one, negative one. There are two occupied-column parities for even order. For odd order there are exactly \(n\) possible positions of the unique three-gap, and \(P_k\) has \(3\cdot2^{k-1}\) proper three-colorings. This proves both state counts.

**3. Even-order moves and invariant.** When \(n=2k\), every horizontal slide would create an occupied-column gap of one, so it is forbidden. Vertical slides are exactly single-site recolorings of proper three-colorings of \(C_k\). Encode the clockwise increments by \(\sigma_i\in\{-1,+1\}\), using \(a_{i+1}-a_i\equiv\sigma_i\pmod3\). If \(p\) signs are positive, consistency requires \(2p-k\equiv0\pmod3\). Conversely, every such sign word has exactly three compatible choices of its first row color.

A site is legally recolorable precisely when its neighboring colors agree, equivalently when its two adjacent signs are opposite. Its recoloring interchanges those signs. Hence \(p\) is invariant. For \(0<p<k\), adjacent swaps of opposite signs along the *linear* edges \((0,1),(1,2),\ldots,(k-2,k-1)\) connect every length-\(k\) sign word with \(p\) positives. Those swaps do not change the row color at site zero. To connect the three possible values of that row color, choose a sign word with positive sign at position zero and negative sign at position \(k-1\). Swap this opposite pair across the cyclic boundary \((k-1,0)\). This is a legal recoloring at vertex zero and changes its color by one modulo three. Restore the original sign word using only linear adjacent swaps, which leave its color at site zero unchanged. Repeating the closed sign walk visits all three phases. Thus every nonconstant sign sector, on each fixed occupied-column parity, is one component of size \(3\binom{k}{p}\). If every sign agrees, no recoloring is legal. The two constant sign words are cyclically consistent precisely when \(3\mid k\), yielding \(2\) parities times \(2\) sign words times \(3\) initial colors, or twelve isolated configurations.

**4. Odd-order connectivity.** Fix the position of the unique three-gap. The allowed vertical slides correspond to single-site recolorings of proper three-colorings of a path. These recoloring states are connected: induct on path length. A recoloring step on the path obtained by deleting an endpoint lifts to the full path by first changing that endpoint, when needed, to the third color different from both its current neighbor color and its intended new neighbor color. At the end, recolor the endpoint to its desired color. This proves connectivity within every fixed gap placement.

To transport the gap, consider three consecutive occupied columns \(A,B,C\) with clockwise column gaps \(A\to B=3\) and \(B\to C=2\). Recolor the selected vertex at \(B\), if needed, to a row distinct from both the rows at \(A\) and \(C\); the intermediate state is legal because \(B\) is currently required only to differ from \(C\). Slide the vertex at \(B\) one column backward into the old three-gap. The two gaps become two and three, respectively, so the distinguished three-gap moves two columns clockwise. Repeating gives every gap position because \(\gcd(2,n)=1\) for odd \(n\). Since the states for each position are connected, all maximum packings lie in one component.

These steps prove the claimed maximum size, exact counts, and complete component classification for every allowed \(n\).

## Verification
Two executable standard-library Python checkers accompany the result. `check_direct.py` enumerates maximum-size subsets of the **actual Cartesian product graph** and builds the one-vertex-slide graph for \(5\le n\le11\), verifying maximum packing sizes, state counts and component profiles. `check_gaps.py` independently generates maximum configurations from cyclic gaps and builds their complete transition graph for \(5\le n\le19\), verifying the same formulas. Their recorded outputs are in `direct.log` and `gaps.log`. These exhaustive finite checks corroborate the proof; the universal claims do not depend on finite extrapolation. Reproduce with `python3 check_direct.py` and `python3 check_gaps.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
Demaine and collaborators' 2014 work on sliding tokens on trees investigates reconfiguration of ordinary independent sets, not distance-two packings on triangular cylinders. Cereceda, van den Heuvel and Johnson's earlier work on paths between three-colorings establishes the classical winding/color-weight obstruction for proper three-colorings on cycles. **That invariant and general three-coloring reconfiguration are prior work**, not new claims here. The contributions here are the explicit reduction of *maximum distance-two packing slides* on \(C_3\square C_n\), its parity-dependent gap motion, the odd-case global connectivity, and the exact optimum-state and component census. General three-coloring results illuminate the even-order part but do not directly state the full packing mobility classification.

## Limitations
The theorem applies to the specified three-row Cartesian cylinders of order \(n\ge5\), maximum (not arbitrary-size) distance-two packings, and single-edge token slides (not arbitrary token jumps). It does not classify all packings or other cylinder widths. The general cyclic color-winding obstruction is known; only its specific packing-theoretic manifestation and the full exact phase transition are advanced here. Literature search and mathematical-record comparison cannot certify historical uniqueness, and independent scholarly validation has not been performed.
