# Self-review of the prescribed square-zero sum fibers

## Correctness — PASS

Every nonzero square-zero matrix in \(M_3(\mathbb F_q)\) has rank one. The rank-one target count is the common-image/common-row inclusion–exclusion \(2q^2-q\), and the rank-two target count is a bijective count of projectors on its image satisfying one trace equation. The projector count \(q+1-e(M)+qz(M)\) holds in all characteristics, and the exact similarity populations satisfy the weighted mass identity \((q^4+q^3-q)^2\). Two independent direct arithmetic approaches corroborate the proof over \(q=2,3,4,5\). No computational extrapolation is used as proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Botha's 2012 theorem and de Seguins Pazzis's 2016 full preprint establish *existence*, including characteristic-two nonrepresentability, so no novelty is attributed to that support criterion. The assessed advance is the positive multiplicity at each prescribed target and the resulting population census. The strongest source inspected in full, arXiv:1605.05131v1, discusses invariant-factor existence in §2.4 and Appendix A, not these finite-field counts. Targeted literature and published-results comparisons found no dominating count; the unavailable original Botha full text and other potential additive-convolution theorems leave a genuine priority risk.

## Value — PASS

Ordered decomposition multiplicities are a natural refinement of the classical question of which matrices are sums of two square-zero matrices. The result is a complete family-wide classification for dimension three: the positive fiber depends on split/nonsplit eigenline behavior or scalar/Jordan structure and exhibits a characteristic-two zero-fiber boundary. It also determines exactly how many targets have each positive value. This is substantive information not implied by the known representability statement alone.

## Limits and sources

The result is confined to \(3\)-by-\(3\) matrices, two ordered square-zero summands, and finite fields; it does not count conjugation-orbits of decompositions. The relevant papers are de Seguins Pazzis (arXiv:1605.05131v1, full relevant sections and appendix inspected), Botha (DOI:10.1016/j.laa.2011.07.011, abstract and restatement inspected), Song–Zheng–Cao (DOI:10.1155/2013/640350, abstract/introduction preview) and Mohammadian (DOI:10.1080/00927872.2011.613423, abstract). Full texts unavailable for the latter papers were not represented as fully checked. Historical priority is not independently certified.
