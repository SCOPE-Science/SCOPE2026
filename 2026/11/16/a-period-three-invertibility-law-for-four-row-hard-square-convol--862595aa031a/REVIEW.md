# Scientific review of the four-row grid convolution theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The complete claim has been reconstructed from an eight-state signed transfer representation of every independent vertex set. The checker verifies the three decisive exact integer certificates: the Cayley–Hamilton parity base identities for eight successive indices, an invariant span giving three-periodic zero multipliers, and an exact period-twelve signing orbit that attains multipliers of magnitude one. Direct full Walsh transforms of independently enumerated grid independent sets agree for lengths one through four. These checks establish the infinite quantifiers through algebraic recurrences, not finite extrapolation. The normalization of counting convolution and the Hilbert-space inverse norm are explicit.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
Bousquet-Mélou, Linusson and Nevo's *On the independence complex of square grids* (arXiv:math/0701890, especially sections 6–7) treats the alternating partition function at one **uniform** activity and records a four-row generating function. Oh and Lee's *Enumerating independent vertex sets in grid graphs* (arXiv:1609.00515, Theorem 1 and sections 3–4) treats the **univariate** independent-set polynomial. Neither statement entails all-character Walsh invertibility or the constructed periodic signings giving zeros and units. A closely related published result on graph-exclusion Fourier interpolation concerns a Fourier matrix restricted to independent-set indices, not the full-group convolution multiplier, so it is not a dominating theorem. Searches of other published mathematical records identified no direct equivalence, but database retrieval is not a guarantee of historical priority. A comparison text for the restricted-interpolation result was not available beyond its public summary.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an infinite, natural strip family from the hard-square model, with a complete and exact classification of which dimensions admit convolution recovery and a sharp uniform Hilbert inverse norm in every invertible dimension. The three-periodic singularity witness and the separate two-periodic unit witness are structural rather than a routine unsigned enumerative specialization. They determine behavior invisible to the classic univariate and uniform-activity counts, while avoiding an arbitrary finite census or an unsupported general-width claim.

## Remaining limitations
No exact singular nullities, complete multiplier distribution, or generalization to other widths or boundary conditions is proved. The novelty assessment has bounded source coverage and no independent priority certification. The proof and checker are self-contained, but external independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
