# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim applies to every nonzero real planar \(+1\) Fourier eigenfunction of Gaussian-polynomial degree at most four with zero value at the origin. The five-mode Hermite normal form is complete. Finiteness of the nonpositive-set area forces strictly positive angular leading coefficient; angular zeros yield a nonintegrable inverse-square radial threshold. Polar integration plus the identity \(A=2B+B''/2\) yields the exact sum of nonnegative terms and uniqueness of the radial extremizer. The embedded symbolic checker independently replays the five eigenfunction identities, but finite computations are not used as proofs.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked evidence

Edwin (arXiv:2611.08224v1, Sec. 1, Problem 1.6) formulates the broader definite-set uncertainty problem and provides a nonsharp universal area bound, not this degree-four two-dimensional sharp classification. Giraud–Peschanski (arXiv:math-ph/0504015, Secs. II–IV) develop Hermite/Laguerre positivity criteria and radial examples, not the minimization of negative area over the full nonradial fourth-order eigenspace. Gonçalves–Oliveira e Silva–Steinerberger (arXiv:1602.03366, Sec. 1) study last-sign radii and related Hermite sign-pattern phenomena, not negative-set area. Semantic searches for angular Fisher identities, negative-set minimizers, and the exact degree-four planar problem produced no covering statement; search absence alone does not establish priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The new definite-set problem lacks an immediate radialization argument. The result proves radial extremality over *all* nonradial modes in a natural first nontrivial complete planar Hermite band and gives an exact nonnegative deficit, not just a test example or routine pointwise bound. The coefficient \(1/4\) is the sharp local quadratic penalty for every independent nonradial mode in the natural normalization. It provides a tractable model for investigating whether geometric radialization principles persist at higher Hermite levels, without claiming any unrestricted theorem.
