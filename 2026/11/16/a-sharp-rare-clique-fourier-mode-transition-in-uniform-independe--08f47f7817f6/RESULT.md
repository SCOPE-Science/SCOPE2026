# A sharp rare-clique Fourier mode transition in uniform independent-set sampling of wheels and clique–cycle joins

## Finding
Let \(n\ge3\) and \(h\ge1\) be integers, and set \(G_{n,h}=K_h\vee C_n\), the join of an \(h\)-clique and an \(n\)-cycle. Uniformly sample an independent set \(I\) of this graph (including the empty set) and let \(X\in\{0,1\}^{n+h}\) be its vertex-indicator vector. Define \(L_n=\varphi^n+\psi^n\), \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), \(\psi=(1-\sqrt5)/2\), and \(Z=L_n+h\). Let \(p_n=F_{n-1}/L_n\), where \(F_m\) is the Fibonacci sequence with \(F_0=0,F_1=1\); let \(v_n\) be the variance per site of the total size of a uniformly sampled independent set of \(C_n\).

**Exact spectral decomposition.** The covariance of \(X\) has \(h-1\) eigenvalues equal to \(1/Z\), \(n-1\) eigenvalues equal to \((L_n/Z)\gamma_{n,k}\) for \(1\le k<n\), and two additional eigenvalues \(\beta_-\le\beta_+\) of
\[
B_{n,h}=\begin{pmatrix}
 L_n/Z^2 & -\sqrt{hn}\,L_np_n/Z^2\\
 -\sqrt{hn}\,L_np_n/Z^2 & (L_n/Z)v_n+hnL_np_n^2/Z^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here the explicit cycle Fourier eigenvalues are
\[
\gamma_{n,k}=\frac{(1-r^2)(1-r^n)}{5(1+r^n)(1-2r\cos(2\pi k/n)+r^2)},\quad r=(\sqrt5-3)/2,\quad1\le k<n,
\]
and
\[
v_n=\frac{1-r^n}{5\sqrt5(1+r^n)}+\frac{nr^n}{5(1+r^n)^2}.
\]
Every covariance eigenvalue is positive; moreover, \(\beta_-<1/Z\) whenever \(h\ge2\).

**Sharp asymptotic transition.** For any integer sequence \(h_n\ge1\) satisfying \(nh_n/L_n\to c\in[0,\infty)\), define \(p=(1-1/\sqrt5)/2\), \(v=1/(5\sqrt5)\), and \(S=1/\sqrt5\). Then
\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}L_n\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{Cov}(X))=\frac{v}{v+cp^2},\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{\max}(\operatorname{Cov}(X))=\max\{S,v+cp^2\}.}
\]
Thus the exact limiting squared-energy condition ratio \(\kappa_{n,h}=\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\) obeys
\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\kappa_{n,h_n}}{L_n}=\frac{v+cp^2}{v}\max\{S,v+cp^2\}.}
\]
The top limiting eigenmode changes from a high-frequency cycle Fourier mode to a clique-versus-rim aggregate eigenmode at the **unique positive critical scale**
\[
\boxed{c_*=\frac{S-v}{p^2}=2+\frac6{\sqrt5}.}
\]
Below \(c_*\) the rim Fourier top mode is eventually strictly maximal; above \(c_*\) the aggregate top mode is eventually strictly maximal. At \(c=c_*\), only equality of the two *limiting* top levels is asserted; finite-dimensional ties need not occur. The small-scale case \(c=0\), including every fixed clique size, gives \(\kappa_{n,h_n}\sim L_n/\sqrt5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple and undirected; the clique vertices are joined to every rim vertex. The law is **uniform over independent sets**, not generated by a random-order greedy algorithm. The covariance is probability-normalized in standard real indicator coordinates. The squared-energy condition ratio is the largest divided by the smallest positive covariance eigenvalue. All finite spectral identities hold for integer \(n\ge3,h\ge1\). The scaling limit assumes nonnegative finite \(c\); no assertion about \(nh_n/L_n\to\infty\) is made.

## Proof
**1. Exact distribution as a disjoint mixture.** Every independent set is either one of the \(L_n\) independent sets entirely on the rim, or one of the \(h\) singleton clique sets. Thus its partition count is \(Z=L_n+h\). Put \(\alpha=L_n/Z\), \(\epsilon=h/Z\), and let \(Y\in\{0,1\}^n\) be uniformly drawn from the independent sets of \(C_n\). Then on the rim branch the indicator vector is \((0,Y)\), and on the clique branch it is \((e_J,0)\), with \(J\) uniform on \(\{1,\dots,h\}\).

**2. Covariance blocks.** The clique mean is \((1/Z)\mathbf1_h\), and each rim mean is \(\alpha p_n\). Hub variances and covariances give the clique block \(I_h/Z-\mathbf1_h\mathbf1_h^T/Z^2\). The mixed block is \(-L_np_n\mathbf1_h\mathbf1_n^T/Z^2\). The rim block, by the law of total covariance, is \(\alpha C_n+\alpha\epsilon p_n^2\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^T\), where \(C_n=\operatorname{Cov}(Y)\). The rim covariance is circulant, with Fourier eigenvalues \(v_n=\gamma_{n,0}\) and \(\gamma_{n,k}\) for \(1\le k<n\). The clique contrasts, rim nonconstant Fourier modes, and the span of normalized clique and rim all-ones vectors are orthogonal invariant subspaces; restriction to the last span yields exactly the displayed \(B_{n,h}\). Direct cancellation shows \(\det B=L_n^2v_n/Z^3>0\). Its diagonal entries are positive, and its off-diagonal entry is nonzero. Thus \(\beta_-<B_{11}=L_n/Z^2<1/Z\) for \(h\ge2\). For \(h=1\), the comparison with a nonexistent clique-contrast sector is vacuous. The rim Fourier eigenvalues are positive, as follows from the positive definiteness of \(\operatorname{Cov}(Y)\): the empty set and every singleton are sampled with positive probability, so a nonconstant affine linear form cannot be almost surely constant. The same argument proves positivity for the full graph. The explicit expressions for \(\gamma_{n,k}\) and \(v_n\) follow by enumerating cycle independent sets using \(F_{d-1}F_{n-d-1}/L_n\) for their two-point probabilities and a discrete Fourier transform; alternatively they are the stated known cycle spectral identities.

**3. Asymptotic characteristic scales.** The Fibonacci and Lucas formulas give \(L_n\sim\varphi^n\), \(p_n\to p=(1-1/\sqrt5)/2\). Since \(|r|<1\), \(v_n\to v\), and the largest nonconstant rim Fourier eigenvalue tends to \(S=1/\sqrt5\) (the largest frequency has cosine tending to \(-1\)). All rim nonconstant eigenvalues remain bounded below by a positive constant. Under \(nh_n/L_n\to c<\infty\), one has \(h_n/L_n\to0\), so \(Z/L_n\to1\), \(\operatorname{tr}B_{n,h_n}\to v+cp^2\), and \(L_n\det B_{n,h_n}\to v\). The product and sum of the eigenvalues therefore imply \(\beta_+\to v+cp^2>0\) and \(L_n\beta_-\to v/(v+cp^2)\). Because the rim modes remain uniformly bounded away from zero, and clique contrasts (when present) exceed \(\beta_-\), the latter is the global least eigenvalue eventually. The full largest eigenvalue tends to \(\max(S,v+cp^2)\); clique-contrast eigenvalues tend to zero. Dividing the two limits yields the condition-number law. Solving \(v+cp^2=S\) yields \(c_*\), and strict inequality on either side gives the claimed eventual mode selection. No finite-size equality is inferred at the critical limit.

## Verification
Run the standalone dependency-free Python 3 program `verify.py`. It independently enumerates independent sets for small \(n,h\), constructs their covariance with rational arithmetic, compares its entries against the mixture-block formula, and tests the large-size limiting constants at several finite sizes. It also compares the cycle Fourier spectrum computed from independent-set combinatorics with the explicit formula. These finite calculations are consistency checks, not proofs of the infinite-family conclusions; the proof above supplies those.

## Relationship to prior work
Classical Fibonacci/Lucas transfer-matrix counts of cycle independent sets are not new. The published exact cycle vertex-covariance Fourier formula (The complete vertex-covariance spectrum of cycle stable-set polytopes) supplies the exact rim spectral input, including its largest-frequency mode. The published random-greedy result Rare-hub variance inflation in random greedy adsorption on clique-joined cycles analyzes the same graph operation but a fundamentally different sampling measure; its probability law and covariance cannot imply this uniform-independent-set spectral transition. Published covariance-anisotropy transitions for complete-split stable-set polytopes analyze the join \(K_p\vee\overline{K_q}\), with **no cycle edges** in the second part, and compare vertex-uniform to volume-uniform condition numbers; they do not imply the present cyclic Fourier edge-mode transition. The contribution here is the coupled exact spectrum and the unique sharp exponential clique-scale crossing for uniform independent-set sampling with an interacting cycle rim.

## Limitations
The parameter \(c\) describes a limit, not a finite integer threshold. We do not assert exact finite-dimensional crossings or a universal theorem for arbitrary residual graphs, nor do we analyze the regime \(nh_n/L_n\to\infty\). A mathematical novelty comparison is provisional: older structure-factor and exclusion-model literature may contain an equivalent covariance calculation in another normalization, and the 2021 combinatorial source was not read in its entirety. The published-finding corpus reports are publicly available mathematical statements, not independent validations of this finding. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
