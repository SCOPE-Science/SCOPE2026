# Mathematical review: five-cycle hexaphase cross-ambiguity

## Correctness — PASS

The claim specifies cyclic length five, two full-length sixth-root sequences, an unnormalized cross-STFT and the two independent global-phase normalizations. The proof first uses the degree-four cyclotomic extension to reduce nonzero-frequency nullity to a constant-shift test, then classifies exactly six five-term zero sums in the Eisenstein integer plane. Translation and autocorrelation arguments give structural descriptions and exact counts at support 5, 19, 20, and 21; the derivative parity obstruction proves the 120 special windows without floating point. Two independent exhaustive checkers test every one of the \(6^8=1,679,616\) normalized ordered pairs and every sorted row-zero profile, one directly reducing all Fourier coefficients by \(\Phi_{30}\). Their results agree exactly. Finite enumeration is exhaustive over the claimed finite domain, not a proof for other orders.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

Nicola's arXiv:2204.14176v1 (April 29, 2022), Theorems 1.1–1.2 and proof, classify the general support-5 equality case, which is **not** asserted as a new standalone equality theorem. They leave the intermediate support spectrum, six-phase discrete profiles and multiplicities open as far as the inspected text shows. Lam–Leung's root-of-unity theorem addresses admissible lengths of a vanishing sum, not simultaneous compatible cyclic shifts. Closest indexed mathematical records concern three-tap cross-ambiguity, auto-ambiguity lower gaps or proper-support Gabor lifting rank; their accessible summaries do not imply the claimed 19–25 classification. Their full texts and certain specialized historical radar tables were not available, so residual priority risk remains and no independent audit is claimed.

## Value — PASS

At cyclic length five, the alphabet of sixth roots is the first coprime-to-length composite phase alphabet that permits a five-term zero sum (the natural \(2+3\) collision). The resulting simultaneous time-frequency zero patterns exhibit a sharp gap from support 5 to 19 and only eight possible complete row signatures. These exact support and multiplicity constraints guide finite phase-alphabet waveform design and distinguish a real arithmetic obstruction from a generic uncertainty bound. The result is intentionally finite, mathematically natural, and does not claim a more general theorem or noisy stability.
