# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite current kernel is the exact second derivative of the weighted spanning-tree determinant; a full geometric Fourier inversion supplies every entry. Triangle exchanges establish the entire nullspace. The subtle distinction between a zero discrete Laplacian Fourier multiplier and the nonzero continuous limit is retained throughout, producing the positive \(1/(2n)\) small-frequency correction. The periodization error is uniformly exponentially small, and the two limiting bands have explicitly checked monotonic extrema. Rational exhaustive checks at sizes five through seven and further finite spectra corroborate the independent analytic derivation.

## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)

The 1985 square-cycle paper establishes the Fibonacci tree count and diagonalizes the host Laplacian, not the covariance of edge occupancy. The 2019 weighted determinant work provides general finite-graph methods, and the transfer-current literature gives exact edge-pair probabilities on arbitrary graphs. These imply a general recipe for covariance, but not the nontrivial small-frequency spectral-floor calculation nor the sharp numerical coefficient here without further analysis. Related published findings on wheel, ladder and multipartite tree polytopes concern different graphs and asymptotic laws. A prior unindexed periodic transfer-current structure-factor specialization may cover the claim and remains a real risk; no universal novelty certification is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural near-local two-channel observable of a uniform random spanning tree has a zero limiting long-wavelength band but only a \(1/n\), rather than \(1/n^2\), least nontrivial finite-circle eigenvalue. The explicit coefficient \(1/2\) identifies the finite-topology contribution, and the sharp conditioning coefficient measures recoverability of edge-class Fourier observations. This distinction is not a routine tree-count evaluation or an arbitrary numerical cutoff.

## Closest literature and limits

The exact tree-count formula for squared cycles is due to Baron–Prodinger–Tichy–Boesch–Wang (1985). The weighted determinant framework is in Klee–Stamps (arXiv:1903.03575). The transfer-current identity is classical, with a 2018 extension by Avena–Gaudillière. The result does not determine exact finite-frequency extremizers and requires further independent priority review.
