# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The half-period translation has two equal-dimensional parity eigenspaces. A direct, full quadratic-Gauss-sum calculation shows that opposite quadratic parities in the same eigenspace cannot be orthogonal, with squared magnitude exactly \(2N\). After fixed-chirp cancellation, each parity eigenspace is isometric to the lower-modulus restricted-character system, which gives both the exhaustive recursion and unique count. The separate \(N=2\) base case is checked. The checker confirms exact cyclotomic cancellation for moduli \(2\) through \(32\) and independently enumerates all bases through modulus \(8\). Numerical evidence is not substituted for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded historical comparison

The 2008 finite-field quadratic-surface restriction article studies bounds, not dyadic finite-ring spectral-basis enumeration. The 2015 cyclic prime-power and \(p\)-adic spectral-set article covers a one-coordinate group, not the two-coordinate quadratic graph. A published full-text result treats the same quadratic graph over odd prime-power rings, using invertibility of two; it neither contains the even-prime case nor implies its two-sector recursion. Semantic searches for dyadic quadratic-graph bases, Fourier spectra, quadratic Gauss sums, and equivalent periodization found no equivalent enumeration. A hidden or unindexed older classification remains possible; a negative search does not establish priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Classifying every exponential basis for a canonical quadratic graph over all dyadic rings is a natural full mathematical classification. The unusual two-sector recursion gives an exact doubly exponential count, not merely an existence construction; the failure of first-coordinate injectivity and its difference from the odd-prime case are structurally significant.
