# Review of the quarter-ball quadratic concentration result

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is an exact spectrum for complex total-degree-at-most-two polynomials on a uniform solid ball, in all dimensions at least three. The normalized gamma moment rule handles odd exponents in the first two coordinates on the wedge and kills odd exponents in residual coordinates. Conditional mean zero identifies the residual radial quadratic; residual trace-free harmonics and swap-parity sectors give an exhaustive orthogonal direct sum. Explicit evaluation of two-by-two Gram blocks and a finite four-by-four moment prescription establishes the spectral multiset, including all repetitions. The direct full-matrix and polar-quadrature program and a separate symbolic small-block checker passed; the analytic argument, not the finite runs, supplies generality. Residual risk: a human may wish to independently verify every gamma normalization and the all-dimensional invariance argument.

## Originality — PASS with priority uncertainty

The 2023 Gerhards–Huang paper supplies the polynomial-ball Slepian framework and high-band asymptotics, but not this fixed-band quarter-ball spectral decomposition in the full text examined. Khalid–Kennedy–McEwen study another bandwidth (Fourier–Bessel/Fourier–Laguerre). The indexed one-half-ball theorem handles one coordinate cut rather than two, and the indexed spherical quarter-wedge theorem handles exact-degree-two harmonics on a surface rather than all degree-at-most-two volume polynomials. Their respective spectral statements do not mathematically imply this one. A related prior full-orthant volume claim cuts all coordinates, not exactly two, and does not determine this spectrum. Full text of some indexed results was unavailable; unindexed equivalent formulas remain a historical priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The standard intersection of two orthogonal half-balls is a natural incomplete-volume observation region. The result replaces a polynomial space of growing quadratic dimension by explicit blocks of size at most four, identifies a protected high-multiplicity quarter-energy sector, and determines sharp best and worst concentration (hence observation stability) for every dimension. It is a complete intrinsic classification rather than a tiny numerical improvement or a finite census. No higher-degree or arbitrary-angle consequences are asserted.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
