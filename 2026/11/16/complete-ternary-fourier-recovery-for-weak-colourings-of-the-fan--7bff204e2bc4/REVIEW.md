# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The seven Fano lines are defined canonically by binary XOR. Inclusion-exclusion over the \(128\) sets of lines gives an exact block-character formula (equation (1) in RESULT.md) valid at every ternary frequency. An exhaustive \(168\)-element collineation-group orbit table evaluates that formula on all \(729\) balanced frequencies. A separate full \(2187\)-site exact Eisenstein-integer transform agrees on the total \(882\), every Fourier level and multiplicity, Parseval, and the least positive multiplier \(9\). Standard character diagonalization then proves the deconvolution statement. Computations have exhaustively finite domains; no infinite or probabilistic conclusion is asserted.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison

Cheilaris–Keszegh–Pálvölgyi, DOI 10.1137/120880471, establishes definitions and background for hypergraph conflict-free/nonmonochromatic colouring but does not provide the Fano Fourier spectrum. Henning–Löwenstein, DOI 10.1002/jgt.21993, studies the Fano plane's strong independence ratio, not character sums of its ternary weak colourings. Nearby indexed mathematical findings concerning Heawood graph proper colourings, Fano lean colourings, proper cycle Fourier sampling, and Fourier sampling on other hypergraphs have different domains or different invariants; none states a stronger result implying all six exact multipliers or nonvanishing on balanced frequencies. Indexed and web queries specifically testing aliases and the numerical levels found no covering result. The 2015 full article was not obtained; unindexed earlier tables and differing terminology remain genuine priority risks.

## Value — PASS

The Fano plane is the canonical smallest non-two-colourable Steiner triple system, while Fourier information under a named three-colour alphabet describes recovery under additive colouring noise. The complete orbit/multiplier table identifies the *only* missing character sectors and supplies exact sharp recovery stability, not merely a count of admissible colourings or a routine transform of a previously published spectrum. This natural small-case classification is independently useful as a benchmark for hypergraph-colouring Fourier recovery and for testing generalizations; no higher-order claim is made.
