# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem's probability law, Fourier normalization, limit order, and fixed-offset quantifiers are explicit. Counting marked cyclic configurations gives the exact deficit weights, and a factorial majorant permits a justified infinite-mixture limit. Conditional weak compositions with finitely many excess gap units converge to independent uniform defect locations; their Fourier phases give a bounded random integral. The binomial difference-count calculation gives the whole near-Bragg profile. Dirichlet spacing moments supply the nontrivial central simplification and endpoint expansions. An independent enumeration and Fourier-moment script verifies 111 finite cases, but the proof of the infinite limits does not rely on finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, conditional on accessible primary evidence

General hard-rod partition functions and correlations are established background, not the asserted advance. The closest studies inspected were Bakhti–Müller–Maass (2013), Jauslin–Lebowitz (2017), and Benmessabih–Bakhti–Chellali (2021). They derive broad density-functional, high-fugacity, or thermodynamic results; none of the inspected relevant passages states the cubic finite-ring activity window, the fixed-index Fourier line-shape limit, or the root-of-unity/Dirichlet central Bragg profile. A general joint-distribution result can supply starting weights but does not itself give the noncommuting mesoscopic limit. Published Fourier-sampling results for other constrained cyclic ensembles differ both in law and observables. An equivalent old lattice diffraction calculation is a documented residual historical-priority risk, not a proved absence of overlap.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The scale at which a near-crystalline hard-rod ring retains a finite random number of phase-slip defects is determined exactly, and the full fixed-offset Bragg-peak profile follows from their geometric limit. This is relevant to Fourier observation of constrained ensembles: the central covariance eigenvalue is order the circumference, while nearby normalized Fourier modes have explicit nontrivial line-shape limits. The central intensity interpolates sharply from a cubic-root onset to perfect-crystal strength. The result is not a generic finite matrix exercise or a restatement of a fixed-activity structure factor.
