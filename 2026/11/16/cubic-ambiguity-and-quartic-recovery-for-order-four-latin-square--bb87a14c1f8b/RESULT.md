# Cubic ambiguity and quartic recovery for order-four Latin-square distributions

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal L_4\) be the set of all \(576\) Latin squares on the four labelled rows, four labelled columns, and four named symbols \(\{0,1,2,3\}\). Embed each square \(L\) into the Boolean cube of \(64\) cell-symbol coordinates
\[
 X_{r,c,a}(L)={\bf1}_{\{L(r,c)=a\}},\qquad 0\le r,c,a\le3.
\]
For each nonnegative integer \(d\), let \(V_d\) be the real span of the functions on \(\mathcal L_4\) obtained by multiplying at most \(d\) distinct coordinates \(X_{r,c,a}\), including the empty product. Equivalently, \(V_d\) is the restricted degree-at-most-\(d\) Boolean Walsh-character space, since \((-1)^{X}=1-2X\).

**Theorem.** The cubic observation space has dimension \(\dim V_3=522\), while \(\dim V_4=576\). Its annihilator in the space of signed distributions on \(\mathcal L_4\) has dimension exactly \(54\), and is generated by \(54\) linearly independent signed, sixteen-square, four-block intercalate-switch cubes. Thus all marginals involving at most three specified cells leave exactly \(54\) linear degrees of ambiguity in a distribution; the collection of all four-cell marginals uniquely determines every distribution. Both bounds are sharp.

## Assumptions and scope

Every row and column is labelled \(0,1,2,3\), and every symbol is named. A Latin square uses each symbol exactly once in each row and column. The observations are the joint probabilities of any assignments to at most \(d\) cells, including inconsistent assignments (which have probability zero). Different choices of up to \(d\) one-hot Walsh coordinates yield the same real span. No assumption of independent cells or uniform distribution is made. The theorem concerns arbitrary real signed measures and, consequently, arbitrary probability distributions supported on the \(576\) squares. It is not an assertion about a single unknown Latin square being uniquely completed from four entries.

## Proof

**First, the cubic nullspace has dimension at least \(54\).** Partition the four rows into two unordered pairs, and similarly partition the columns. In a Latin square where all four resulting \(2\times2\) blocks are intercalates (each is \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\ b&a\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) with \(a\ne b\)), each block can be switched independently by exchanging \(a\) and \(b\) within that block. This preserves all Latin row and column conditions. The \(2^4\) resulting squares form a switch cube. Give the square with switch pattern \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^4\) coefficient \((-1)^{\sum_j\varepsilon_j}\). Any joint assignment involving at most three cells depends on at most three switch blocks; summing over the untouched fourth switch cancels it. Hence this signed measure annihilates \(V_3\).

The finite enumeration described below finds exactly \(54\) distinct switch cubes of this kind among the \(576\) squares. There are \(432\) squares lying in exactly one cube and \(144\) lying in exactly three; each cube contains exactly eight squares that belong to no other cube. A coefficient at one such private square witnesses independence of that cube's signed measure from the other \(53\). Therefore \(\dim V_3\le 576-54=522\), over the reals, without relying on modular arithmetic for this inequality.

**Second, exact lower-rank witnesses establish equality and recovery.** The included `verify_primary.py` enumerates all \(576\) Latin squares, forms the indicators of all consistent assignments to at most four distinct cells, and performs deterministic column elimination over \(\mathbb F_5\). It identifies \(522\) independent columns of degree at most three, and \(576\) independent columns of degree at most four. The resulting \(576\)-column list appears in `pivot_manifest.json`. Independence of an integral matrix's columns modulo a prime implies their independence over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb R\), so \(\dim V_3\ge522\) and \(\dim V_4\ge576\). The latter equals the entire function space. Combining both inequalities gives the asserted exact dimensions and identifies the \(54\) independent cube measures as a basis of the cubic annihilator.

Because each signed cube measure has total mass zero, taking its positive and negative parts and normalizing gives two different probability measures with identical marginals on at most three cells. This demonstrates actual probability-distribution ambiguity, not just formal signed-measure nonuniqueness.

## Verification

The primary verifier uses exact \(\mathbb F_5\) arithmetic, generates all squares by recursively choosing legal rows, and checks each of the \(54\) cube measures against the entire collection of three-cell assignment events. Its degree-by-degree modular ranks are \((1,28,246,522,576)\). Only the two highest rank values are needed for the theorem; earlier entries are certificate diagnostics rather than separately asserted rational ranks.

The independent verifier constructs all squares by choosing three mutually column-disjoint permutation rows and completing the last row, confirms the separately generated ordered-square digest, and recomputes independence of the selected \(576\) columns over \(\mathbb F_7\). It also checks a canonical four-switch construction from the addition table of \(\mathbb F_2^2\). Run from the directory containing the accompanying files:

```sh
python3 verify_primary.py pivot_manifest.json
python3 verify_independent.py pivot_manifest.json
```

These are reproducible exact finite calculations, not floating-point rank estimates. The scripts require Python 3 and NumPy. The complete range is exhaustive because every Latin square consists of four permutation rows and the generators explore each compatible choice.

## Relationship to prior work

Latin trades and intercalates provide classical methods of changing Latin squares while preserving Latin constraints; the present switch cubes make an alternating signed cancellation in low-degree Fourier/marginal observations. Published work on critical sets studies whether a partial Latin square has a unique individual completion; that is logically different from linear reconstruction of an arbitrary *distribution* from all its low-order marginals. The cited archival papers supply this combinatorial setting but the inspected material does not establish the present dimensions \(522\) and \(54\), the switch-cube nullspace basis, or degree-four distribution recovery.

## Limitations

The exact rank proof uses a finite-field elimination certificate, not a closed symbolic formula valid at larger Latin-square orders. No claim is made for order five or arbitrary order, for noisy reconstruction conditioning, or for the minimum size of a critical set. The literature comparison is bounded rather than a proof of global historical priority. Previously unindexed rank tables or equivalent algebraic-statistical formulations may exist.
