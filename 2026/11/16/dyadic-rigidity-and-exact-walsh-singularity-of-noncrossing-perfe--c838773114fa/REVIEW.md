# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Catalan factorial valuations, binary digit-sum subadditivity and a decomposition of prescribed noncrossing chord extensions establish the exact common \(2\)-power divisor of all Walsh coefficients. The coefficient at zero frequency makes the divisor sharp. The reflected-root cancellation argument constructs actual zero coefficients, with a recursive central-chord null factor when the half-length is odd; Catalan parity excludes zeros at the complementary lengths. Connectedness of the opposite-parity chord graph proves the zero-orbit lower bound. Exact computation independently checks the statement through all frequencies for four pairs and explicit longer witnesses. No finite test is treated as an all-length proof.

## Originality

**PASS under bounded historical comparison.** Postnikov–Sagan's Theorem 2.1 covers height-indexed Dyck weights satisfying stronger difference conditions, not arbitrary independently signed chords. Catalan enumeration and valuation themselves are acknowledged as classical. The published first-degree Fourier sampling statement for noncrossing matchings only classifies a degree-one Gram kernel; it does not imply existence or absence of zeros for *all* Walsh characters. Searches for equivalent signed Catalan/perfect-matching transform statements uncovered no stronger covering theorem. A classical signed-rook or weighted-Catalan identity not identified in this comparison remains a genuine priority risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Fourier zeros determine whether a concrete convolution operator can be inverted; its natural support has Catalan complexity but an exact binary-digit singularity threshold. The theorem also computes the best possible common \(2\)-adic divisor for the entire signed family and constructs an exponential family of null frequencies. These are global operator invariants, not a routine Catalan count or an arbitrary finite slice.
