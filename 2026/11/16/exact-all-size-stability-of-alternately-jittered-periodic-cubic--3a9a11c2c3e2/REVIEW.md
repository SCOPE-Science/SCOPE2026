# Review of the exact two-periodic cubic-spline collocation spectrum

## Correctness — PASS
The piecewise cubic cardinal function and its periodization are normalized explicitly. Evaluating at alternately shifted nodes produces a four-tap two-cell stencil. A unitary cell Fourier transform decomposes the full matrix into exact \(2\times2\) conjugate-symmetric blocks. Their singular values are \(|u|\pm|v|\); a strictly positive polynomial factor proves \(|u|>|v|\) away from node collision. Another explicit factor establishes that the lower singular band decreases strictly toward the zero cell frequency, and both spectral extrema occur uniquely there. The rank-half collision, monotonic condition number and blow-up constant are derived without asymptotic approximation. A separate direct-matrix Python script passed 1085 assertions. The numerical checks are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (best available evidence; historical priority provisional)
The motivating paper of Yu–Townsend (arXiv:2202.04722, first version 2022-02-09) proves stability statements for nonuniform *trigonometric polynomial* interpolation and quadrature, not a cubic B-spline coefficient-to-sample collocation spectrum. Its openly accessible paper text was read for the setup, matrix norms and stability results. Fageot–Aziznejad–Unser–Uhlmann's accessible 2020 article studies Hermite and cubic spline support/approximation theory, not this two-periodic square collocation singular spectrum in the read material. Publicly indexed classifications of alternating-knot **linear** spline projection norms and cubic cardinal B-spline **Gabor Zak zeros** address mathematically different operators. The exact all-size spectrum, extremal checkerboard mode, displacement-independent cycle-size condition number, and half-rank collision were not located as an already-stated theorem. Novaković, Singer and Singer (2010), *Estimates for the spectral condition number of cardinal B-spline collocation matrices*, https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/61876. Its accessible abstract concerns cardinal collocation at knot averages and numerical/estimated uniform-mesh conditioning; the linked primary PDF could not be fetched. It is a plausible older source, but the abstract does not claim the present two-periodic point-jitter singular spectrum. Older cardinal-spline Floquet theory may admit equivalent results; this is an explicit remaining risk, not a proof of historical uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
Alternating periodic jitter is a natural, structured perturbation of the standard cardinal spline sampling grid. A full all-grid stability classification is useful for separating sample collision from mere quadrature/interpolation perturbation, and the exact worst-case coefficient noise amplification determines the critical alternating mode. The result supplies a complete two-band singular spectrum, sharp constants and all critical boundary cases, rather than an isolated numerical invariant. It gives a dimension-independent bound throughout the noncolliding interval, a sharper structural statement than a generic small-perturbation estimate, without claiming arbitrary-jitter conclusions.
