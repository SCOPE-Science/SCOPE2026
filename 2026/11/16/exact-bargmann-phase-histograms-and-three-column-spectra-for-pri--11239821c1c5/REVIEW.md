# Review of the exact prime MUB Bargmann-phase law

## Correctness — PASS
The coefficient inner product is an exact quadratic Gauss sum. Both the all-chirp and computational-basis triples reduce to a quadratic expression whose coefficient sign equals the Legendre symbol of the exponent whenever nonzero. Each nontrivial linear form takes each value exactly \(p^2\) times and a nonsquare scaling exchanges basis-sign sectors. This derives every phase, its exact multiplicity, and the three-column Gram cubic. The distinct spectral type count follows by exact cosine/sine pairing, and exhaustive modular checks for primes 3, 5, 7, 11 independently match all counts. This excludes invalid extension of finite checks to the unbounded prime range.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The Gauss-sum MUB construction and third-order Bargmann invariant are known. Gültekin–Samuel–Gedik explicitly report numerical phase-frequency tables at dimensions 3 and 5 (arXiv:2604.04164, §5, Table 2); these values are **not** new. The all-odd-prime analytic formula, complete multiplicities, and exact Gram spectral-type census are stronger than those tabulated small examples. Fickus–Iverson–Jasper–Mixon (arXiv:2112.14267) supplies frame-theoretic Gauss-sum context, not the statement. Search comparison against relevant prior research records returned no implication-equivalent all-prime result. The main residual risk is an older equivalent result under finite-geometric triple-product or Clifford-orbit terminology; this is not a proof of priority.

## Value — PASS
The problem is motivated by exact gauge-invariant fingerprints of complete mutually unbiased bases and the conditioning of their three-column subframes. One all-prime formula replaces numerical histograms, identifies a sharp finite phase alphabet and multiplicities, and determines every unordered Gram spectral type. It is a natural complete invariant census, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Boundaries
The result is for canonical odd-prime quadratic-chirp MUBs only. It does not claim a classification of all complete MUB sets or arbitrary Gram-submatrix sizes. The Gauss sum is a classical input. No independent review is asserted.
