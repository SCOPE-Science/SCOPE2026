# Exact cap concentration on first complex-projective harmonics: three spectral levels and a universal crossover

## Finding
Let \(d\ge3\), let \(\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}\) carry normalized Fubini–Study probability measure \(\mu\), and fix the projective point \([e_1]\). Identify the first nonconstant Laplace eigenspace \(\mathcal H_1\), complexified, with \(\{f_A([u])=u^*Au:\operatorname{tr}A=0\}\), where \(u\in\mathbb C^d\) has unit length. For \(0<t<1\) define the projective geodesic cap \(C_t=\{[u]:|u_1|^2\ge t\}\) and the self-adjoint concentration operator \(T_t=P_{\mathcal H_1}\mathbf1_{C_t}|_{\mathcal H_1}\).

Its **complete spectrum**, including complex multiplicities, comprises
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\mathrm{ax}}&=(1-t)^{d-1}(1-2t+d^2t^2), &&\text{multiplicity }1,\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{cross}}&=(1-t)^d(1+dt), &&\text{multiplicity }2(d-1),\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{tan}}&=(1-t)^{d+1}, &&\text{multiplicity }d(d-2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here axial refers to the line generated by \(d|u_1|^2-1\), cross to \(u_1\overline{u_j}\) and its conjugate for \(2\le j\le d\), and tangential to traceless quadratic forms in \((u_2,\ldots,u_d)\). The strictly smallest eigenvalue is always \(\lambda_{\mathrm{tan}}\). The largest is \(\lambda_{\mathrm{cross}}\) for \(0<t<1/d\), the common axial/cross value at \(t=1/d\), and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{ax}}\) for \(1/d<t<1\). Thus the unique nontrivial top-mode exchange occurs at \(t=1/d\), where the top eigenspace has dimension \(2d-1\).

In particular, restriction to any positive-volume cap is injective on \(\mathcal H_1\) and has the **sharp squared condition number**
\[
\kappa_d(t)=\begin{cases}
(1+dt)/(1-t),&0<t\le 1/d,\\
(1-2t+d^2t^2)/(1-t)^2,&1/d\le t<1.
\end{cases}
\]
The cap volume at the switch is \(v_d=(1-1/d)^{d-1}\to e^{-1}\). More precisely, if cap volume \(v\in(0,1)\) is held fixed and \(y=\log(1/v)\), as \(d\to\infty\) the three eigenvalues converge respectively to \(v(1+y^2)\), \(v(1+y)\), and \(v\), while \(\kappa_d\to1+\max\{y,y^2\}\). The limiting top-mode switch is at \(v=e^{-1}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The dimension restriction \(d\ge3\) guarantees a nonzero tangential traceless sector. The functions are complex-valued and the concentration eigenvalues are defined relative to the global \(L^2(\mu)\) norm, not a conditional-on-the-cap norm. The cap parameter \(t\) is squared coordinate overlap (equivalently a Fubini–Study distance threshold in the usual normalization). No point-sampling or higher-band statement is asserted. At \(t=0\) the operator is the identity; at \(t=1\) it is zero, so the strict ordering assertions concern \(0<t<1\).

## Proof
**1. The harmonic model and orthogonality.** Traceless bidegree-\((1,1)\) polynomials \(u^*Au\) are invariant under the common circle phase and are harmonic in the ambient complex coordinates because their complex Laplacian is proportional to \(\operatorname{tr}A\). They give the first positive projective Laplacian eigenspace. Haar fourth moments give, for trace-zero matrices \(A,B\),
\[
\langle f_A,f_B\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(AB^*)}{d(d+1)}.
\]
Thus its complex dimension is \(d^2-1\).

**2. Disintegration.** Put \(n=d-1\), \(X=|u_1|^2\), and \(Y=1-X\). For uniform complex unit \(u\), \(X\) has the \(\mathrm{Beta}(1,n)\) law; \(Y\) has density \(n y^{n-1}\) on \([0,1]\). Conditional on \(X\), the last \(n\) coordinates are \(\sqrt{Y}\,v\), where \(v\) is independently Haar-uniform on the complex unit sphere in \(\mathbb C^n\), and the first-coordinate phase is independent and uniform. With \(s=1-t\), the cap event is \(Y\le s\), and for every integer \(j\ge0\),
\[
\mathbb E[Y^j\mathbf1_{C_t}]=\frac{n}{n+j}s^{n+j}.
\]

**3. Three mutually orthogonal invariant sectors.** Under the cap stabilizer \(U(n)\), the trace-zero matrices split orthogonally into the axial line \(A_0=\operatorname{diag}(d-1,-1,\ldots,-1)\), the two off-diagonal blocks joining coordinate \(1\) to its complement, and the traceless matrix block entirely in the complement. Their dimensions are \(1\), \(2n\), and \(n^2-1=d(d-2)\). Conditional uniform phase and the identity \(\mathbb E[(v^*Bv)\overline{(v^*Dv)}]=\operatorname{tr}(BD^*)/[n(n+1)]\) for traceless \(B,D\) show that these sectors do not couple and that \(T_t\) acts by a scalar on each. On the axial line the scalar is \(\mathbb E[(dX-1)^2\mathbf1_{C_t}]/\mathbb E[(dX-1)^2]\); on the cross blocks it is \(\mathbb E[XY\mathbf1_{C_t}]/\mathbb E[XY]\); and on the tangential block it is \(\mathbb E[Y^2\mathbf1_{C_t}]/\mathbb E[Y^2]\). Substituting the moment identity of Step 2 and simplifying gives exactly the three displayed eigenvalues. Since the sector dimensions sum to \(d^2-1\), the list exhausts the spectrum.

**4. Sharp ordering.** Direct subtraction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\mathrm{ax}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{tan}}&=(d^2-1)t^2(1-t)^{d-1}>0,\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{cross}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{tan}}&=(d+1)t(1-t)^d>0,\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{ax}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{cross}}&=(d+1)t(dt-1)(1-t)^{d-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These identities establish all ordering, multiplicity, and condition-number statements, including the sole top crossing. Positivity of the smallest eigenvalue proves cap injectivity, with equality in every sharp spectral bound obtained by the corresponding stated sector.

**5. Fixed-volume limit.** The cap measure is \(v=s^{d-1}\). For fixed \(v\in(0,1)\), put \(y=-\log v\), so \(s=e^{-y/(d-1)}\) and \(dt\to y\). Substituting in each exact eigenvalue yields the three fixed-volume limits. The condition number follows by taking their maximum divided by the tangential limit. At \(t=1/d\), \(v_d=(1-1/d)^{d-1}\to e^{-1}\). The limit also shows that the crossover is nondegenerate on either side of \(v=e^{-1}\).

## Verification
The included `check_projective_cap.py` is self-contained Python 3 using `fractions.Fraction`. It independently constructs cap and whole-space Gram matrices of all \(d^2-1\) trace-zero coordinate harmonics from second moments of a Dirichlet law, then tests the three spectral sectors, cross-sector orthogonality, multiplicities, trace, and all three eigenvalue-difference identities. It passed \(42\) exact-rational cases for \(3\le d\le8\), including the crossover \(t=1/d\). This finite check corroborates but does not replace the proof for arbitrary \(d\) and \(t\).

## Relationship to prior work
Jaming and Speckbacher (2020, arXiv:2004.02474) prove large-sieve concentration **bounds** for finite Laplace-eigenfunction expansions on compact two-point homogeneous spaces, including complex projective spaces. Their general inequalities and addition formula do not state this first-eigenspace cap concentration spectrum, the exact eigenvalue ordering, or the fixed-volume phase transition. Morales-Ramos, Quiroga-Barranco, and Sánchez-Nungaray (2017) study radial Toeplitz symbol algebras on **holomorphic section/Bergman spaces** of complex projective manifolds; those operators and spaces differ from the traceless \((1,1)\) Laplace harmonic concentration operator here. Classical spherical Slepian treatments concern real spheres. The formulas here use standard Haar/Beta fourth moments, but the complete three-sector comparison and limiting transition do not follow merely by quoting those moments without the indicated representation and extremal analysis. Historical priority is not conclusively established, particularly against differently named projective cubature or covariance literature.

## Limitations
The theorem addresses exactly the first positive projective Laplacian eigenspace, not arbitrary spectral bandwidth, arbitrary projective domains, or holomorphic Bergman sections. In particular, the stated \(d(d-2)\)-multiplicity and sharp worst-observability result do not apply to \(d=2\). No assertion of independent verification, numerical certification of the universal theorem, or exhaustive historical novelty is made.
