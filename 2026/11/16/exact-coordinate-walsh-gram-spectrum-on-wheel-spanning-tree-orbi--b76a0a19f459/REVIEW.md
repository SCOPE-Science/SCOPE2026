# Scientific review: wheel spanning-tree coordinate-Walsh spectrum

## Correctness — PASS

The asserted object is the uncentered probability-normalized Gram matrix of the \(2n\) first-degree Walsh characters, restricted to the uniformly weighted spanning-tree orbit of a simple wheel with \(n\ge3\) rim vertices. Weighted matrix-tree differentiation gives single- and double-edge inclusion moments without assuming independence. The reduced Laplacian \(3I-S-S^{-1}\) is inverted with a checked periodic Green kernel. Its three transfer-current block types yield the two-by-two Fourier decomposition; explicit finite geometric sums establish every displayed eigenvalue, with the constant mode handled separately. Positivity, forced one-dimensional kernel, the sharp least and greatest eigenvalue formulas, and the cubic conditioning asymptotic follow by exact algebra and monotonicity. The separate combinatorial `checker.py` reconstructs full integer Gram matrices by enumerating all spanning trees for every \(3\le n\le10\) and corroborates the spectrum. Finite enumeration is auxiliary, not a claim of proof for larger \(n\).

## Originality — PASS, with bounded historical priority

The decisive closest source is Burton–Pemantle's transfer-impedance result, which supplies joint spanning-tree edge probabilities for arbitrary graphs. The present theorem does **not** claim that general identity is new. Neither its inspected finite-graph theorem nor its transfer-impedance section presents the evaluated wheel coordinate-Walsh Gram eigenvalues or the exact low-frequency bottleneck. Benjamin–Yerger give the wheel spanning-tree count, including the reduced circulant Laplacian, but not coordinate-character joint moments or a sampling Gram spectrum. Dall–Pfeifle establish general weighted matrix-tree identities, not an explicit Fourier-mode spectral evaluation. Searches for wheel edge-covariance spectra, wheel Walsh sampling, and wheel Gram condition numbers did not identify a dominating earlier formula. A pre-existing specialized wheel covariance diagonalization remains possible and would require reevaluation if found; absence from the searched sources is not proof of global priority.

A related published calculation of wheel symmetric-edge-polytope ellipsoids addresses the geometry of edge-root polytopes, not probability-normalized coordinate Walsh characters on wheel spanning-tree incidence vectors. Existing coordinate-Walsh sampling calculations on finite quadratic sets concern unrelated sample orbits and do not imply the wheel formula. Earlier local computations on other graphic-matroid base sets do not include the wheel family.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sample set is the canonical uniform spanning-tree ensemble of a 3-connected, non-series-parallel family of graphs. Exact first-degree Fourier sampling stability measures whether coordinate edge observables can be reconstructed stably from tree samples. The full spectral law identifies the sole necessary dependence, an exact two-dimensional weakest oscillatory mode for \(n\ge4\), and an asymptotically sharp cubic condition-ratio blowup. These are specific quantitative invariants not given by the classical spanning-tree count, Kirchhoff's formula, or the bare general determinantal process rule. Both the evaluated closed forms and the sharp mode/asymptotic classification contribute to the mathematical value; a generic restatement of the transfer-current identity alone would not.

## Limits and closest literature

The theorem covers unweighted wheels, degree-one Walsh characters, and the orthogonal complement of one forced null direction. It gives no full Walsh spectrum of the spanning-tree indicator and no claim about arbitrary graphic matroids. The numerical checker uses floating-point eigenspectra after exactly counting all trees; exact symbolic proof controls the infinite family. The relevant primary sources examined were Burton–Pemantle (arXiv:math/0404048, especially Theorems 1.1 and 4.2), Benjamin–Yerger (wheel enumeration and reduced circulant Laplacian), and Dall–Pfeifle (arXiv:1404.3876, weighted matrix-tree context). Independent mathematical and exhaustive historical-priority audits have not been performed.
