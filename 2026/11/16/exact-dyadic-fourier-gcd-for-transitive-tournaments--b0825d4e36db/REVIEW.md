# Mathematical review: exact dyadic Fourier gcd for transitive tournaments

## Correctness — PASS
The statement fixes labeled orientations, the unnormalized Fourier convention and all positive \(n\). Complementary halves of a dyadic position block contribute a bracket in \(\{-2,0,2\}\), giving a factor two beyond the inductive factors; binary decomposition yields the full \(2^{n-s_2(n)}\) divisor. Fourier orthogonality at the identity order rules out odd gcd factors; the zero-frequency coefficient \(n!\) fixes the largest possible dyadic exponent. Order reversal forces the claimed half of the zeros. Exact integer experiments corroborate but do not prove the infinite statement.

Their inspected results concern different quantifiers and do not imply a common divisor simultaneously across arbitrary edge-sign assignments. Related published tournament sampling and acyclic-orientation summaries concern different operators or graph classes; their full underlying records were not accessible. ## Mathematical value — PASS
The gcd of every Walsh multiplier is a canonical, global spectral arithmetic invariant rather than a single signed evaluation. The result finds the exact maximal uniform divisor, proves exponential-in-\(n\) spectral quantization, and bounds nonzero-mode inversion for a natural family of labeled orientations. The dyadic complement pairing is nontrivial and uniformly handles all sign assignments. Full zero multiplicities and the smallest nonzero magnitude for arbitrary \(n\) remain open in this analysis.
