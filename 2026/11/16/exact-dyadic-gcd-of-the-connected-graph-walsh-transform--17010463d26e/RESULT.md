# Exact dyadic gcd of the connected-graph Walsh transform

## Finding

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \(E_n=\binom{[n]}2\), and identify a simple graph on the labelled vertex set \([n]\) with its edge subset \(G\subseteq E_n\). A graph with one vertex and no edges is regarded as connected. For every frequency \(S\subseteq E_n\), define the **unnormalized** Walsh multiplier

\[
W_n(S)=\sum_{\substack{G\subseteq E_n\\([n],G)\ \mathrm{connected}}}(-1)^{|G\cap S|}.
\]

**Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{\gcd_{S\subseteq E_n} W_n(S)=2^{\,n-1-\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}.}
\]

The gcd is the nonnegative gcd of the entire family, including any zero coefficients. Equivalently, all eigenvalues of unnormalized convolution by the connected-graph indicator on \(\mathbb F_2^{\binom n2}\) are divisible by the displayed power of two, and no higher integer divides them all.

There is also a constructive restricted witness family. Put \(L=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\) and \(t=n-2^L\). Among the frequencies whose *positive-sign edges* are a matching of size \(j\) with \(0\le j\le t\), at least one multiplier has exact two-adic valuation \(n-1-L\). For any such matching of size \(j\), its multiplier is the explicitly computable integer

\[
A_{n,j}=\sum_{a=0}^{j}\binom ja(-1)^{n-a-1}(n-a-1)!\,2^a.
\]

Here the positive-sign edges are \(E_n\setminus S\); all other edge coordinates receive sign \(-1\). Thus a search of at most \(t+1\) explicit integers supplies a sharp frequency for every \(n\). This witness statement is part of the same sharp-divisibility theorem, rather than a claim of a separately classified Fourier spectrum.

## Assumptions and scope

The graphs are **all** simple connected labelled graphs on the fixed complete edge set \(E_n\), with no restriction on edge count, degree, or topology. Walsh characters are \((-1)^{|G\cap S|}\), with no averaging factor. The graph-convolution operator is on the binary edge group, not a vertex-adjacency or Laplacian operator. All arithmetic is over the integers; the exponent of two is a statement about the common divisor of integer Fourier multipliers, not about the zero multiplicity or the smallest nonzero multiplier at a fixed order.

## Proof

**1. Component partitions.** Let \(\Pi_n\) be the lattice of partitions of \([n]\), ordered by refinement, with top partition \(\widehat1\). For \(\pi\in\Pi_n\), write \(k(\pi)\) for its number of blocks and

\[
E(\pi)=\bigcup_{B\in\pi}\binom B2,
\qquad e(\pi)=|E(\pi)|=\sum_{B\in\pi}\binom{|B|}{2}.
\]

The interval from a partition having \(k\) blocks to \(\widehat1\) is the partition lattice on \(k\) objects. Its Möbius number is \(\mu(\pi,\widehat1)=(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!\). If \(\pi(G)\) is the connected-component partition of graph \(G\), Möbius inversion yields

\[
\mathbf1_{\{G\ \mathrm{connected}\}}
 =\sum_{\pi\ge\pi(G)}(-1)^{k(\pi)-1}(k(\pi)-1)!.
\]

The constraint \(\pi\ge\pi(G)\) is exactly \(G\subseteq E(\pi)\). Summing edge signs over these subgraphs gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_n(S)
 &=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_n}(-1)^{k(\pi)-1}(k(\pi)-1)!
 \prod_{e\in E(\pi)}\bigl(1+(-1)^{\mathbf1_{e\in S}}\bigr)\\
 &=\sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Pi_n\\E(\pi)\cap S=\varnothing}}
 (-1)^{k(\pi)-1}(k(\pi)-1)!\,2^{e(\pi)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

**2. Integral zeta inversion preserves the common divisor.** For every edge set \(T\subseteq E_n\) define

\[
g_n(T)=\begin{cases}
 (-1)^{k(\pi)-1}(k(\pi)-1)!\,2^{e(\pi)},&T=E(\pi)\text{ for some }\pi,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

This is well defined: the connected components of the graph \(([n],E(\pi))\) recover precisely the blocks of \(\pi\), even for singleton blocks. Equation (1) becomes

\[
W_n(S)=\sum_{T\subseteq E_n\setminus S}g_n(T).
\tag{2}
\]

On the Boolean subset lattice, the map \(g\mapsto (U\mapsto\sum_{T\subseteq U}g(T))\) has an inverse whose coefficients are integers \((-1)^{|U\setminus T|}\). Complementing \(S\) is a permutation of coordinates. Consequently the ideal of \(\mathbb Z\) generated by all \(W_n(S)\) is **exactly** the ideal generated by all \(g_n(T)\). In particular,

\[
\gcd_S W_n(S)=\gcd_{\pi\in\Pi_n}
 \bigl((k(\pi)-1)!\,2^{e(\pi)}\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]

**3. Sharp valuation.** Put \(L=\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor\) and let a partition have \(k\) blocks of sizes \(b_1,\ldots,b_k\). Since \(\binom b2\ge b-1\) for every positive integer \(b\),

\[
e(\pi)\ge\sum_{i=1}^{k}(b_i-1)=n-k.
\]

Legendre's identity, with \(s_2(a)\) the number of ones in the binary expansion of \(a\), gives

\[
\nu_2((k-1)!)=k-1-s_2(k-1).
\]

Because \(0\le k-1\le n-1<2^{L+1}-1\), the integer \(k-1\) has at most \(L\) ones: the only \((L+1)\)-bit integer with \(L+1\) ones is \(2^{L+1}-1\). Thus

\[
\nu_2\bigl((k-1)!\,2^{e(\pi)}\bigr)
 \ge(k-1-L)+(n-k)=n-1-L.
\tag{4}
\]

To attain equality take \(k=2^L\) blocks. Because \(2^L\le n<2^{L+1}\), one can make \(t=n-2^L\) blocks of size two and \(2^{L+1}-n\) singleton blocks. Then \(e(\pi)=t=n-k\) and \(s_2(k-1)=L\), so equality holds in (4). No odd prime divides the gcd in (3): for \(n\ge2\) any two-block partition has weight of the form \(2^{e(\pi)}\), with factorial factor \(1\); for \(n=1\) the unique weight is \(1\). Equation (3) now proves the boxed theorem.

**4. Matching-complement sharpness.** Let \(M\subseteq E_n\) consist of the \(t\) disjoint edges used as doubleton blocks in the sharp partition above. Its weight \(g_n(M)\) has valuation exactly \(n-1-L\). Boolean Möbius inversion of (2) gives

\[
g_n(M)=\sum_{U\subseteq M}(-1)^{|M\setminus U|}W_n(E_n\setminus U).
\]

All summands are divisible by \(2^{n-1-L}\), so at least one is **not** divisible by \(2^{n-L}\). Such a set \(U\) is a matching of size \(j\le t\). By permutation symmetry its multiplier depends only on \(j\). In (1), partitions compatible with \(E(\pi)\subseteq U\) consist of a choice of \(a\) of those \(j\) doubleton blocks and the remaining singletons, giving precisely \(A_{n,j}\). This proves the restricted witness assertion. The case \(n=1\) is included: \(t=0\), \(A_{1,0}=1\).

## Verification

The proof is uniform in every positive integer \(n\). Two independent exact finite calculations corroborate the partition identity and its sharp divisor:

- `verify_connected_walsh.cpp` enumerates all labelled edge graphs and computes their connectivity indicators by disjoint-set merging, then performs an integer Walsh-Hadamard transform. Separately, it enumerates vertex set partitions and computes equation (2) by an integer subset-zeta transform, comparing the **entire** multiplier arrays through \(n=7\).
- `verify_witnesses.py` checks the exact integer matching-complement formulas and the valuation lower bound for \(1\le n\le200\).

In the full-spectral checks the verified numbers of zero multipliers for \(n=3,4,5,6,7\) were respectively \(3,0,230,7872,318647\); these are finite reproducibility diagnostics, not additional all-order claims. Reproduction commands are `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_connected_walsh.cpp -o verify && ./verify` and `python3 verify_witnesses.py`. The uniform result follows from the proof, not extrapolation from those computations.

## Relationship to prior work

Van der Hofstad and Spencer's 2005 analysis of labelled connected graphs studies positive edge-count enumeration and its asymptotics. Kostić and Yan's 2006 work relates spanning forests and the Tutte polynomial to multiparking functions. Goodall's preprint on finite Abelian Fourier methods reviews graphic flow/tension transforms and Tutte-polynomial evaluations. These provide the relevant enumerative and harmonic context but their inspected statements do not determine the exact common divisor over **all independently signed edge coordinates** of the complete-graph connectivity indicator. The proof here combines the standard partition-lattice Möbius identity with **integral inversion over the entire Boolean edge subset lattice** and a sharp binary-popcount minimization.

Generic Fourier inversion, positive connected-graph enumeration, and signed Tutte evaluations are distinguished from the quantified result in (3)–(4); this result is not asserted to be historically independent of every uninspected source.

## Limitations

The theorem determines the exact **common arithmetic divisor**, not the complete Fourier distribution, its number of zeros, individual smallest nonzero multipliers or optimal projection norms. The restricted matching-witness family guarantees at least one sharp sign choice but does not provide a closed formula for the first successful \(j\). The source comparison was bounded; full historical priority and independent mathematical review have not been established.
