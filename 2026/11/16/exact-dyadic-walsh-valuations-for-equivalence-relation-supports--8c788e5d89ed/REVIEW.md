# Scientific review: equivalence-relation Walsh valuations

## Correctness — PASS
For every integer \(n\ge2\) and every frequency graph \(H\), the signed character sum expands exactly as \(\sum_{F\subseteq H}(-2)^{|F|}B_{c(F)}\). Any two distinct simple edges impose exactly two equivalence identifications; truncating at two selected edges proves the congruence modulo eight with no probabilistic assumption. The eight-state Stirling recurrence yields the 24-periodic Bell residues by a finite, explicitly reproducible matrix calculation; direct substitution gives each valuation class. The frequency rank conclusion uses ordinary Fourier diagonalization with unnormalized convolution. The embedded checker independently corroborates 2,131,018 complete small-dimensional coefficients and 1,026 additional eight-vertex signed sums. The general result is proved algebraically, not inferred from enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, bounded comparison
The Bell-number congruence period is historically known and is explicitly *not* claimed as new. Duncan–Peele's 2009 full text describes graphical Bell counts and their insertion/contraction identities, while Hertz–Mélot's institutional full text describes non-equivalent colorings, graph Bell numbers and extremal enumeration; neither gives the signed all-frequency equivalence-relation indicator, dyadic valuation classification, or Boolean convolution invertibility theorem. Related indexed Fourier results on spanning trees and small binary supports concern different supports and do not imply the set-partition formulas. An exact specialized signed graph-partition statement was not found in the comparisons made, but an unexamined broader graph-polynomial identity could subsume the observation. The inaccessible 1979 congruence-paper text is a historical-access limitation, mitigated only for correctness by the independent modular-period proof. This assessment is not a claim of exhaustive worldwide priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Equivalence relations form a canonical, rapidly growing subset of the binary edge cube. Even Bell cardinality alone does not decide convolution invertibility. The theorem establishes a uniform spectral divisibility law that depends on the size but not the shape of the frequency graph, and resolves invertibility on eleven of twelve dimension classes, including three nontrivial even-Bell classes. The precise valuations give minimum spectral-gap bounds and the exceptional class retains a rigorous half-rank guarantee. The residual class is not claimed solved. These are natural harmonic-analytic invariants, not arbitrary numerical slices or a restatement of Bell-number parity.

## Closest literature and limits
- Duncan and Peele, *Bell and Stirling Numbers for Graphs* (2009), gives unsigned graphical partition counts and insertion/contraction, not signed character spectra.
- Hertz and Mélot, *Counting the number of non-equivalent vertex colorings of a graph* (public institutional version 2014), gives unsigned graphical Bell theory.
- Lunnon, Pleasants and Stephens, *Arithmetic properties of Bell numbers to a composite modulus I* (1979), concerns historical Bell congruences. The full text could not be inspected, so no full-text claim of noncoverage is made; the needed period is established independently here.
- Relevant published Fourier records concerning perfect matchings and small binary sets use different supports and do not provide an equivalence-relation signed Bell spectrum.

Scientific limits: all signed multiplier values remain undetermined; at \(n\equiv2\pmod{12}\), odd-edge frequencies might have zeros and the theorem asserts only their divisibility by eight. Independent validation is not performed.
