# Exact low-cyclotomic fourth moments on the cubic triangular Fourier aperture

## Finding
Let \(S=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:i+j\le3\}\) and, for \(q\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\), let \(\mu_q\) denote all \(q\)-th roots of unity. For every coefficient array \(a\in\mu_q^S\), form \(P_a(z,w)=\sum_{(i,j)\in S}a_{ij}z^iw^j\), and let \(M_4(a)=\int_{\mathbb T^2}|P_a|^4\,dm\), where \(m\) is normalized Haar measure.

The complete sharp minima are

| \(q\) | \(\min M_4\) | normalized minimizing arrays | all minimizing arrays | next attainable moment | normalized minimizing orbit sizes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 442 | 1 | 1 | none | 1 |
| 2 | 138 | 3 | 24 | 154 | 3 |
| 3 | 136 | 8 | 216 | 148 | 2, 6 |
| 4 | 138 | 45 | 2880 | 146 | 3, 6, 6, 6, 6, 6, 12 |
| 6 | 132 | 6 | 1296 | 134 | 6 |

A normalized array satisfies \(a_{00}=a_{10}=a_{01}=1\). The phase-orbit action on these arrays is generated by permutations of the three homogeneous barycentric coordinates \((i,j,3-i-j)\) and complex conjugation, each followed by this normalization. The indicated lists of sizes are its complete disjoint minimizing orbits. Every alphabet has sharp maximum \(442\), attained exactly by the \(q^3\) character-modulations of the constant array (one such array for \(q=1\)). In particular the fourth-moment optimum is nonmonotone in alphabet cardinality: \(\min_{\mu_3}M_4=136<138=\min_{\mu_4}M_4\), but \(\min_{\mu_6}M_4=132\).

These five alphabets are precisely the root-of-unity groups for which the cyclotomic field has degree at most two, including the trivial alphabet.

## Assumptions and scope
The ten frequencies form the full third-degree triangular aperture. Coefficient magnitudes are exactly one and phases lie in the specified *whole* root-of-unity alphabet. Torus Haar measure is normalized to mass one. There is no additional optimization over coefficient magnitudes, and no assertion of a sharp unrestricted complex-phase minimum or of other phase alphabet orders. The orbit sizes and normalized counts refer to distinct coefficient arrays, not continuous phase classes.

## Proof
**Fourth-moment identity.** Fourier orthogonality gives both finite nonnegative sums
\[
M_4(a)=\sum_{u\in S+S}\left|\sum_{s,t\in S\, :\,s+t=u}a_sa_t\right|^2
=\sum_{h\in S-S}\left|\sum_{s,t\in S\, :\,s-t=h}a_s\overline{a_t}\right|^2.
\]
These expressions include ordered pairs and therefore have no missing symmetry factors.

**Exhaustive gauge quotient.** The transformation \(a_{ij}\mapsto\alpha\beta^i\gamma^j a_{ij}\) for arbitrary \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mu_q\) leaves \(M_4\) unchanged (it multiplies the polynomial by a unimodular scalar and changes its torus arguments). Exactly one triple of such phases enforces \(a_{00}=a_{10}=a_{01}=1\), since those three coefficient values uniquely determine \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). Consequently every full array is represented uniquely by a gauge representative plus a triple of phases; there are exactly \(q^7\) gauge representatives, and each minimizing representative has exactly \(q^3\) full-array lifts.

**Exact finite certificate.** Enumerate all seven remaining exponents in \(\{0,\dots,q-1\}\), in the lexicographic support order stated in the executable certificate. Calculate the ordered-pair convolution on the 49 possible lattice sums and independently the ordered-pair autocorrelation on the 49 lattice differences. For \(q=1,2\), the sums are integers, and for \(q=4\) they are Gaussian integers, whose exact norm is \(x^2+y^2\). For \(q=3,6\), use the Eisenstein basis \((1,\omega)\), with \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\), where the exact norm is \(x^2-xy+y^2\). The embedded `verify.cpp` computes the resulting nonnegative integer for every gauge representative by *both* formulas, rejects any discrepancy, computes minima/maxima, counts the minimizers, computes the next attainable value and verifies the claimed exact distribution sizes. The enumeration is exhaustive rather than sampling: there are exactly \(1+128+2187+16384+279936=298636\) representatives across the five alphabets, each processed exactly once.

**Complete phase-orbit classification.** Affine permutations of the three barycentric coordinates preserve the triangular support up to a monomial and invertible torus reparameterization. Their induced coefficient permutations and coefficient conjugation therefore preserve \(M_4\). The separate embedded `orbit_check.py` parses the exact minimizing indices printed by the independent enumeration program, normalizes every transformed array, checks the generated orbits remain entirely within the minimum set, checks their disjointness and completeness, and verifies the exact sizes listed in the table.

**Maximum and equality.** The coefficient of every monomial in \(P_a^2\) is a sum of the same fixed number of unimodular terms. Its modulus cannot exceed that count, with equality for all sums when \(a_{ij}\) is a product of a common scalar and two coordinate characters. Summing squares gives \(M_4(a)\le442\). Conversely, if equality holds, equality in the triangle inequality for all coefficients of \(P_a^2\) forces in particular \(a_{s+t}a_{00}=a_sa_t\) for all \(s,t,s+t\in S\): the terms indexed by \((s,t)\) and \((s+t,0)\) share the same sum index. Hence induction on \(i+j\) forces \(a_{ij}=a_{00}(a_{10}/a_{00})^i(a_{01}/a_{00})^j\), exactly the stated character-phase arrays.

## Verification
The two implementations are self-contained: `verify.cpp` is standard C++17, and `orbit_check.py` uses Python 3 standard libraries. From a directory containing both, run:

`c++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify.cpp -o verify`

`./verify`

`python3 orbit_check.py`

The latter executes the former again and reconstructs each normalized minimizer group. Both were executed from the complete artifact package, with all asserted values verified. In particular the sum and difference moment formulas agreed in all 298636 cases. The loops use bounded exact 64-bit integer arithmetic; no numerical tolerance or unproved extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
The ten-site third-degree triangular aperture is a natural nested two-dimensional Fourier sensor and small planar array. Existing exact phase-only studies of the *six-site degree-two* triangular aperture, the nine-site rectangular square and related planar supports treat different support geometries and do not, through their stated theorems, determine this ten-site table. The array-design literature motivates fourth moments as a measure of integrated sidelobe energy and treats larger or rectangular layouts, often algorithmically rather than with exact finite phase-alphabet classifications. The present assertion is the complete exact classification of the degree-at-most-two cyclotomic phase-alphabet family for this specified ten-site support, not a general theorem about every planar array.

## Limitations
The result does not identify the unrestricted unit-circle minimum and does not classify alphabets of cyclotomic degree greater than two; numerical experiments cannot fill either gap. Completeness and equality assertions are certified by the included finite enumeration and orbit algorithm, and their mathematical reliability depends on those short, inspectable programs and the independent Fourier moment identity. The bibliography review does not exclude inaccessible historical design tables or unpublished earlier enumerations. The computational certificate and the accompanying proof have not undergone independent audit.
