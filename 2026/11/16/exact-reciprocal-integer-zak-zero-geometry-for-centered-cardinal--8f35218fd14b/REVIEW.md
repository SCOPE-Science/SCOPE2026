# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact centered B-spline refinement equation is obtained by subdividing the unit interval and convolving; it yields an identity for every order and every integer reciprocal scale, not a limit of sampled data. Finite reindexing gives the displayed Zak factorization. The mask roots are exactly the nontrivial roots of unity; the cited classical unit-scale theorem establishes a unique residual zero. Spatial quasi-periodicity resolves the parity boundary, and differentiating the geometric sum proves the asserted exact transverse order. The supplementary exact-rational checker verified 126 cases of the finite identity. No computational grid is used to exclude zeros.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The focal September 2026 paper proves horizontal lines at reciprocal integer scale, but its complete small-scale zero-set result is for spline order two; for higher orders it discusses selected symmetry fibers. The 2013/2014 Kloos–Stöckler paper and Schoenberg's theorem establish the unit-scale unique zero, not the reciprocal integer classification. Published indexed related findings on continuous scale intervals \(\lambda\ge1/2\) and specialized zero transitions do not imply the all-order family \(m\ge3\). This work gives the missing complete factorization and exact non-half-frequency multiplicity, while explicitly excluding already-covered order two. Residual risk is a formulation in older wavelet-refinement or exponential Euler-spline literature not identified by the searches.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The reciprocal-integer scales are canonical spline subdivision levels and precisely where entire horizontal Zak-zero fibers occur; their full geometry is a natural, unresolved-to-the-inspected-source harmonic-analysis classification problem. The theorem resolves that entire two-parameter family for all higher spline orders, distinguishes odd versus even subdivisions, and quantifies the order of every ordinary zero fiber. The fact that the proof unifies classical refinement and a classical unit-scale theorem is a structural explanation, not merely a computation of a single convenient parameter or an arbitrary finite table.

## Limits
The theorem neither covers irrational scales nor proves a new all-scale frame-set result. The novelty assessment is limited by the material examined; priority is provisional. Independent mathematical checking remains outstanding.
