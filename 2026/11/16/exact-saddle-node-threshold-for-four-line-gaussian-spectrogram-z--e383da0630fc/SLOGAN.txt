Four equally spaced independent Gaussian spectral lines have exactly one peak in their expected Gaussian-spectrogram zero intensity below the sharp adjacent-gap threshold \(2\sqrt{-\log[(2\sqrt3-3)/9]/8}\), and three peaks above it. The central peak persists: the two outer peaks are born in a saddle-node pair at a calculable square-root scale, unlike the symmetry-breaking transition of three-line spectra.
