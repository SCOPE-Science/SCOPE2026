# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Each admissible row alternates \(+1,-1,+1\), and the column partial-sum recursion is necessary and sufficient for an order-four alternating-sign matrix. It produces exactly \(42\) distinct matrices. Row/column sum constraints reduce Fourier analysis from \(3^{16}\) frequencies to \(3^9\); each reduced squared magnitude has exactly \(3^7\) lifts. All \(19,683\) exact Eisenstein norms and all \(512\) weight-one/two frequency candidates were evaluated. Independent monotone-triangle and row-recursion enumerators agree on the \(42\) matrices, while independent phase-count and Eisenstein-transform methods agree on the complete \(53\)-level spectrum and Parseval identity. The sharp norm follows from diagonalization, with an explicit attaining frequency.

## Originality

**PASS under bounded comparison.** Propp's survey and Ayyer–Romik's refined boundary correlations do not state or imply the evaluated full ternary Fourier nullity and first-zero geometry. Behrend's six-statistic refined generating functions treat boundary locations, generalized inversions, and number of negative entries, rather than independent additive-character coefficients of all signed matrix entries. The separate known binary nonzero-entry character spectrum concerns modulus two and does not furnish a ternary spectral formula directly. FindStat lists classical alternating-sign matrices and their statistics, but no inspected exact ternary Fourier spectrum. This is an exact finite evaluation rather than a claim to have introduced alternating-sign matrices or their known enumeration. Unindexed data and unexamined sources remain possible historical-priority risks.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Ternary reduction retains the three distinct possible matrix entries and is therefore the natural faithful finite-field encoding. The complete sharp spectrum identifies how much additive-character information is lost, the exact conditioning of recoverable components, and a rigid local cancellation geometry at two-coordinate support. Order four is the first alternating-sign matrix size with a total count divisible by three, making exact ternary phase balance arithmetically possible; the result supplies a concrete benchmark for further character-sum studies of square-ice combinatorics, without asserting a larger-order theorem.
