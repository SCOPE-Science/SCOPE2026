# Exact three-dimensional loss and the complete five-frequency Fourier rank census at order fifteen

## Finding
Let \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/15)\) and \(F=(\zeta^{rc})_{r,c\in\mathbb Z_{15}}\). For **every** pair \((R,C)\) of five-element subsets of \(\mathbb Z_{15}\), form the complex five-by-five sampling matrix \(F[R,C]\), with its rows and columns in increasing order. The exact distribution over the \(\binom{15}{5}^{2}=9,018,009\) ordered pairs is:

| Complex rank | Exact number of ordered row/column-set pairs |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 |
| 2 | **360** |
| 3 | **24,480** |
| 4 | **880,920** |
| 5 | **8,112,249** |

Moreover, **the rank is two exactly when one index set is a coset of** \(H=\{0,3,6,9,12\}\) **and the other intersects exactly two residue classes modulo five**. There are exactly 360 such ordered pairs. Thus exactly 360 sampling patterns lose three independent Fourier coordinates. The package also contains the **complete** 201-by-201 rank-incidence matrix under independent row and column translations; its entries specify the rank of all 9,018,009 restrictions without unresolved cases.

## Assumptions and scope
All subsets have **five distinct** residues modulo fifteen, and row and column subsets vary independently. Ranks are taken over \(\mathbb C\). The Fourier matrix is unnormalized, although any common nonzero scaling has the same rank. The original five spectral coefficients may be recovered from the five samples exactly when the corresponding sampling matrix has rank five. Rank two means that only two of the five Fourier-coordinate directions are observable, leaving a three-dimensional kernel. This is a statement about rank, not singular values or noise conditioning.

## Proof
**1. Translation reduction.** For residues \(s,t\),
\[
F[R+s,C+t]_{r,c}=\zeta^{(r+s)(c+t)}
=\zeta^{st}\zeta^{rt}\zeta^{sc}\zeta^{rc}.
\]
The extra factors are invertible row/column scalings, and reordering does not change rank. Hence it suffices to consider one representative from each translation orbit of the five-subsets, independently in rows and columns. Direct orbit generation (all starting subsets, all fifteen shifts) gives 201 disjoint orbits: 200 of size 15 and one of size 3, represented by \(H\). Their sizes sum to \(\binom{15}{5}=3003\). This step is exact and exhaustive.

**2. Exact cyclotomic rank test.** The minimal polynomial of \(\zeta\) is
\[
\Phi_{15}(X)=X^{8}-X^{7}+X^{5}-X^{4}+X^{3}-X+1.
\]
For any equal-cardinality sublists \(A=(a_i)\), \(B=(b_i)\), of size \(d\le5\), define
\[
P_{A,B}(X)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_d}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
X^{\left(\sum_i a_i b_{\sigma(i)}\right)\bmod 15}.
\]
The determinant is \(P_{A,B}(\zeta)\). Since \(\Phi_{15}\) is the **minimal** polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\), this determinant vanishes exactly when the remainder of \(P_{A,B}\) modulo \(\Phi_{15}\) has all eight integer coefficients zero. This equality is exact; no floating-point threshold is used.

The executable certificate evaluates each of the \(201^2=40,401\) orbit-representative matrix pairs. It first tests the five-minor, then all necessary four-minors, three-minors and two-minors until it finds a nonzero one. The largest nonzero minor size equals the rank. The resulting 201-by-201 incidence matrix, together with the orbit sizes, yields the displayed totals by the exact identity
\[
N_d=\sum_{i=1}^{201}\sum_{j=1}^{201}|\mathcal O_i|\,|\mathcal O_j|\,\mathbf1\{\operatorname{rank}F[R_i,C_j]=d\}.
\]
The certificate also verifies symmetry of the incidence matrix and that the total is 9,018,009.

**3. Characterization of the deepest defect.** If \(R\) is a coset of \(H\), all elements of \(R\) are congruent modulo three. On its rows, \(F[R,C]\) differs by invertible scalings from a five-point Fourier transform on \(\mathbb Z_5\), with columns depending only on the residues of \(C\) modulo five. Therefore its rank is precisely the number of distinct residues represented by \(C\) modulo five. In particular rank two results when that number is two. There are three cosets of \(H\). A five-subset of \(\mathbb Z_{15}\) meeting precisely two residue classes modulo five must take three members of one (three-point) class and two of the other: \(\binom52\cdot2\cdot\binom32=60\) such subsets. Counting both orientations yields \(2\cdot3\cdot60=360\) rank-two pairs. The exhaustive cyclotomic test establishes the converse: its only eight rank-two **translation-orbit pairs** are precisely these two orientations and the four translations-orbits of the sixty two-class subsets; all other representative pairs have rank at least three.

For an independent analytic lower bound, rank one would require \((r-r')(c-c')\equiv0\pmod{15}\) for every \(r,r'\in R\) and \(c,c'\in C\). The subgroups generated by the row and column differences would then annihilate one another, yet each contains at least five elements, while annihilating subgroups of \(\mathbb Z_{15}\) have cardinality product at most fifteen. Thus rank one is impossible.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_f15_five.py f15_five_incidence.json` in the extracted artifact directory. It requires only the Python standard library, regenerates all translation orbits, computes every exact cyclotomic rank and compares the resulting complete incidence matrix with the attached JSON data. Successful execution prints `EXACT_F15_FIVE_CERTIFICATE_OK`, the rank totals and `MODULAR_LOWER_BOUNDS_AGREE [31, 61]`.

As a **separate** check, the script reduces the Fourier matrices modulo the primes 31 and 61 using primitive fifteenth roots in those fields, and computes their ranks by modular Gaussian elimination. Modular ranks can only underestimate the characteristic-zero ranks, but the maximum of the two modular ranks agrees with the exact cyclotomic rank in every one of 40,401 representative cases. This is an independent lower-bound check; exact vanishing of higher minors comes from the integer cyclotomic method. The 201-by-201 JSON incidence table has representative row/column sets, translation orbit weights, and all rank values.

## Relationship to prior work
Caragea and Lee's initial September 2024 manuscript studies **principal** Fourier minors and proves nonvanishing of principal minors of sizes two and three at square-free orders, while conjecturing the general principal statement. Their result does not enumerate **all** five-row/five-column restrictions and does not distinguish rank two, three, four and five. The present census includes all principal choices as a subset but **does not claim the square-free principal conjecture as new**.

Osgood, Siripuram and Wu establish the general interpolation framework and prime-power universal sampling criteria; their prime-power results cannot be directly applied at square-free order fifteen. Malikiosis and Oussa study full-spark group-orbit frames, not this fixed Fourier rank-incidence table. An existing exact census at the **same order fifteen with four rows and four columns** supplies ranks at a *different minor size* and is credited. It cannot determine the present five-minor determinant pattern or its 360 three-dimensional kernel cases. Delvaux and Van Barel construct maximal rank-deficient blocks of Kronecker Fourier matrices, which explains subgroup-type defects; their work is conceptually important prior art. Their full 2007/2008 papers could not be checked here; see the limitations.

## Limitations
This is a finite, complete classification at one natural square-free order and one fixed sample size, **not** an all-order theorem or a spectral conditioning analysis. The exact numerical census is certified by a transparent exhaustive integer algorithm rather than a compact symbolic formula for every incidence entry. The rank-two *if-and-only-if* characterization has an analytic construction and counting argument, while its converse and the ranks three through five rely on the exhaustive certificate. Older rank-deficient Fourier-block papers may contain a stronger general classification from which some of the strata could be deduced, and historical priority is not independently certified. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
