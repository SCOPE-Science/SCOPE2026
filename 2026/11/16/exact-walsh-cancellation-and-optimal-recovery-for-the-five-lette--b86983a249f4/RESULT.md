# Exact Walsh cancellation and optimal recovery for the five-letter alternating permutation codebook

## Finding

Let \(G=M_5(\mathbb F_2)\), with entrywise additive pairing \(\langle A,B\rangle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{4}A_{ij}B_{ij}\), and let \(E\subset G\) be the incidence matrices of the sixty even permutations of five symbols. For every frequency \(A\in G\) define the **unnormalized** Walsh coefficient
\[
W(A)=\sum_{B\in E}(-1)^{\langle A,B\rangle}.
\]
Its **complete** absolute-multiplier census is:

| \(\lvert W(A)\rvert\) | 0 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 | 28 | 36 | 60 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Frequencies in \(G\) | 5,795,840 | 12,595,200 | 8,908,800 | 4,019,200 | 1,904,640 | 184,320 | 102,400 | 30,720 | 12,800 | 512 |

In particular, counting convolution \(Tg=\mathbf1_E*g\) has rank \(27,758,592\), nullity \(5,795,840\), least positive multiplier magnitude \(4\), largest multiplier magnitude \(60\), sharp Hilbert-space Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse norm \(1/4\), and positive-spectrum condition number \(15\).

**Sharp local cancellation.** No frequency matrix supported on fewer than three entries has \(W(A)=0\). Exactly \(400\) frequencies supported on three entries vanish; they are precisely the length-three paths (three corners of a \(2\times2\) submatrix) in the bipartite row–column graph \(K_{5,5}\).

Let \(P\) be the canonical Fourier projection onto the range of \(T\), whose multiplier equals \(\mathbf1_{\{A:W(A)\neq0\}}\). Its exact sharp endpoint norms are
\[
\boxed{\|P\|_{\ell^1(G)\to\ell^1(G)}=\|P\|_{\ell^\infty(G)\to\ell^\infty(G)}=\frac{266611}{4096}.}
\]
Among **all** linear projections from \(\ell^1(G)\) (respectively \(\ell^\infty(G)\)) onto this translation-invariant Fourier range, \(P\) has minimum possible operator norm.

## Assumptions and scope

All entries of the incidence vectors and Fourier frequencies are in \(\mathbb F_2\). Permanents and determinants of matrices whose entries are \(\pm1\) are evaluated as **integers**, not modulo two. Convolution is unnormalized counting convolution \((f*g)(x)=\sum_y f(y)g(x-y)\); the Fourier transform of a convolution is the pointwise product of unnormalized Fourier transforms. The inner product for the pseudoinverse statement is ordinary counting-measure \(\ell^2(G)\). The result concerns order five and does not extend these numerical constants to arbitrary orders.

## Proof

**1. Signed-even-permanent identity.** Given \(A\in G\), put \(B(A)_{ij}=(-1)^{A_{ij}}\). Directly from the Leibniz expansions,
\[
W(A)=\sum_{\pi\in A_5}\prod_{i=0}^{4}B(A)_{i,\pi(i)}=\frac{\operatorname{per} B(A)+\det B(A)}2.
\]
This identity is classical; the assertions above concern its exact distribution over all sign matrices and the associated Fourier range.

**2. Reduction from 25 to 16 frequency bits.** Adding the all-one mask of any row or column to \(A\) reverses the sign of every summand of \(W(A)\), since each permutation matrix has exactly one entry in that row or column. The binary subspace \(R\) generated by the five row masks and five column masks has dimension \(9\), as its only generator relation is the sum of all rows equal to the sum of all columns. Each frequency is therefore equivalent, up to an overall sign, to exactly one matrix with its last row and last column zero. These representatives are the \(2^{16}\) assignments of bits in the leading \(4\times4\) block; each represents \(2^9=512\) original frequencies. This proves that the full absolute-magnitude histogram is 512 times the reduced one.

**3. Finite exact spectrum certificate.** In the reduced \(4\times4\) frequency cube the coefficient array \(W\) has the following exhaustive absolute-magnitude histogram:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrrr}
t&0&4&8&12&16&20&24&28&36&60\\
\hline
\#\{|W|=t\}&11320&24600&17400&7850&3720&360&200&60&25&1.
\end{array}
\]
This is an **exact finite integer equality** with a reproducible exhaustive verification, not a numerical sample. In `check_even_permutation_walsh.py`, all sixty even permutations are embedded injectively by their leading \(4\times4\) incidence submatrices, then one sixteen-bit Walsh–Hadamard transform produces all reduced coefficients. Separately, `check_even_permanent.cpp` generates all 120 permutations and evaluates the integer permanent and determinant independently at every reduced sign matrix, then takes half their sum. Each algorithm independently verifies the displayed histogram, Parseval's identity \(\sum_A W(A)^2=2^{16}\cdot60\), the nonzero count \(54,216\), and the projection-kernel norm below. The two algorithms have different coefficient-evaluation paths and agree at the complete-histogram level. Exact execution steps appear in `VERIFICATION.md`.

**4. The first cancellation is a bipartite three-edge path.** If a prescribed set of \(k\le3\) permutation entries is a matching in \(K_{5,5}\), then its number of even extensions is \((5-k)!/2\). This is an integer for \(k\le3\), since exchanging two unused columns exchanges parity. It equals \(12,3,1\) for \(k=1,2,3\), respectively. Any incompatible prescribed set has no extension. For frequency edge set \(S\), expanding the sign product yields
\[
W(S)=\sum_{F\subseteq S}(-2)^{|F|}N(F),
\]
where \(N(F)\) counts even-permutation extensions. The constant coefficient is 60; every one-edge coefficient is \(60-2\cdot12=36\). The two-edge coefficients equal \(12\) when the edges share a vertex and \(24\) when disjoint. For three edges, let \(d\) be the number of disjoint pairs among them. If the three edges form a matching, the coefficient is \(60-72+4(3d)-8=16\). Otherwise the three-edge term is absent and \(W(S)=60-72+12d=12(d-1)\), which vanishes **if and only if** \(d=1\). In a simple bipartite graph, exactly one disjoint pair among three edges means that the three edges form a length-three path. There are \(4\binom52^2=400\) such paths. A separate direct scan of every one-, two- and three-entry frequency confirms this classification.

**5. Sharp range-projection norm.** Let \(m(A)=\mathbf1_{\{W(A)\neq0\}}\). The projection convolution kernel is \(K(x)=2^{-25}\sum_A m(A)(-1)^{\langle A,x\rangle}\). As \(m\) is constant on cosets of \(R\), \(K\) vanishes outside the annihilator \(R^\perp\), which is the sixteen-dimensional subspace of binary matrices having even sums in every row and column. A matrix in \(R^\perp\) is uniquely determined by its leading \(4\times4\) entries, and the pairing with the reduced frequencies is the standard sixteen-bit dot product. Hence the \(\ell^1\) norm of the full kernel equals the \(\ell^1\) norm of the sixteen-bit inverse transform of the reduced indicator \(m\). The exact integer inverse-transform calculation gives
\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_2^{16}}\left|\sum_{A\in\mathbb F_2^{16}}m(A)(-1)^{A\cdot x}\right|=4,265,776.
\]
Consequently \(\|K\|_1=4,265,776/65,536=266611/4096\). For convolution, a point mass and a phase-aligned bounded function attain the respective \(\ell^1\) and \(\ell^\infty\) upper bounds, proving sharpness.

To show minimum norm among **all** projections onto the same range, take any projection \(Q\) onto that range and average its conjugates by all translations of \(G\). This remains a projection onto the range, commutes with every translation, and has norm no larger than \(\|Q\|\). Since the range is a span of Fourier characters, its unique translation-invariant projection is \(P\). Thus \(\|Q\|\ge\|P\|\).

## Verification

The two standalone programs use exact integers and require no externally downloaded data. Run `python3 check_even_permutation_walsh.py` and `g++ -O2 -std=c++17 check_even_permanent.cpp -o check_even_permanent && ./check_even_permanent`. Both produce explicit `PASS` outputs after re-evaluating all \(65,536\) reduced frequencies. The Python program also directly checks the \(400\) three-edge cancellations in the full \(25\)-coordinate cube. The complete finite enumeration is part of the proof certificate; neither execution extrapolates to order six or beyond.

## Relationship to prior work

The even-permutation sum as half the sum of a permanent and a determinant is immediate from classical Leibniz formulas. Wanless establishes possible signed permanent values and vanishing criteria, but the examined material does not tabulate this **even-permanent** distribution or its Fourier projection norm. Tao–Vu prove asymptotic statements for random Bernoulli permanents, not the joint permanent/determinant distribution at order five. Dolinar–Guterman–Kuzma–Orel analyze permanent/determinant distributions for matrices over finite fields of odd characteristic, a different ensemble and arithmetic setting. A published order-five **full permutation-family** Walsh census gives the absolute-permanent distribution and projection constant \(33045/4096\), but it does **not** determine the parity-restricted distribution: the latter requires the unreported joint value of the permanent and determinant. Its Fourier projection is also a different spectral subspace. These comparisons concern mathematical implications, not just title matches.

## Limitations

This is a complete finite statement only for the even permutation orbit at size five. The extensive constants are established using exhaustive exact-arithmetic certificates, not a compact formula for their multiplicities. A previously unindexed equivalent enumeration may exist; the literature comparison was bounded. The result does not claim any general-dimension rank or nullity formula. Independent external mathematical validation has not been performed.
