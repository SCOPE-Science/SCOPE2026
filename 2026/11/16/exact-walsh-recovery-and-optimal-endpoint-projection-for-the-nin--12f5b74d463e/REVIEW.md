# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The definitions and normalization fix the precise twelve-dimensional binary edge cube. Exhaustiveness follows from all \(\binom{12}{8}=495\) potential tree masks and all \(4096\) character masks, with no unenumerated cases. The independent signed-Laplacian determinant code and spanning-tree/Walsh-butterfly code have both been run on the embedded source paths and passed matching exact histogram and kernel-sum assertions. Edge-inclusion counts certify fourfold divisibility; an explicit mask attains the bound. The projection optimality proof averages arbitrary projections over translations, making the sharp endpoint claim valid without assuming a translation-invariant competitor.

## Originality — PASS with bounded-priority risk
The archived finite-lattice spanning-tree literature treats unweighted numbers and lattice entropy. The general weighted matrix-tree formula gives an algorithm for evaluating signed coefficients, but the inspected original documents do not supply the exact square-grid Walsh histogram, 1,186-dimensional nullity, sharp gap, or optimal projection constant. Related already-existing spectral calculations for cube, wheel, ladder and other spanning-tree families involve nonisomorphic underlying graphical matroids and do not imply these exact values. Targeted source/title/parameter searches did not identify a corresponding complete table; that negative search is not taken to prove uniqueness. A buried signed-grid tabulation or stronger general result remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS within finite scope
The free-boundary \(3\times3\) square grid is the smallest square lattice with a true interior vertex, beyond two-rail ladders. Its complete Walsh support gives the exact recoverable dimension of arbitrary counting-convolution signals, its smallest positive multiplier gives optimal \(\ell^2\) deconvolution stability, and the globally minimal endpoint projection constant quantifies worst-case amplification. These three linked invariants form a canonical small two-dimensional benchmark for any subsequent grid-family theorem. This is not a claim of a new general matrix-tree identity or of an all-size lattice formula.

## Closest comparisons and limitations
Shrock–Wu (2000) covers unsigned lattice-tree enumeration; Klee–Stamps (2019) states weighted determinant machinery for other graph families. Exact spectra for ladders, wheels, and the three-dimensional cube concern different edge-sets and support classes, so their claims do not dominate the present result. The proof is finite and has no consequence for larger square grids. The originality assessment is bounded by access and indexing, and independent external audit remains to be done.
