# First Walsh-rank separation and endpoint stability contrast for \(123\)- and \(132\)-avoiding permutations

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal A_n(p)\) be the permutations of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) avoiding the classical pattern \(p\in\{123,132\}\), and embed each permutation as its binary pair-comparison (inversion) vector in \(G_n=\mathbb F_2^{\binom n2}\). Let \(T_{n,p}\) be counting convolution by the indicator of this embedded family. For every \(1\le n\le5\), the convolution ranks are exactly:

| \(n\) | Family size, each pattern | \(\operatorname{rank}T_{n,123}\) | \(\operatorname{rank}T_{n,132}\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 3 | 5 | 8 | 8 |
| 4 | 14 | 49 | 46 |
| 5 | 42 | 1024 | 817 |

Thus order four is the first rank separation of the classical Wilf-equivalent length-three avoidance families in this natural incidence embedding. At order five their convolution invertibility first differs: all \(1024\) Walsh multipliers of \(T_{5,123}\) are congruent to \(2\pmod4\), whereas \(T_{5,132}\) has exactly \(207\) zero multipliers. The sharp minimum \(\ell^1\)- and \(\ell^\infty\)-projection constant for the range of \(T_{5,132}\), taken over **all** real or complex linear projections, is
\[
\Lambda_1(\operatorname{ran}T_{5,132})=\Lambda_\infty(\operatorname{ran}T_{5,132})=\frac{3797}{512}.
\]
The range of \(T_{5,123}\) is the whole space and its corresponding projection constant is \(1\). In particular, neither order-four nor order-five avoidance support pair is related by an invertible affine change of coordinates of its binary incidence cube.

## Assumptions and scope

The classical pattern \(p\) occurs in a permutation \(\pi\) if some three increasing positions have values with relative ranks given by \(p\). For \(1\le i<j\le n\), define the inversion coordinate \(b_{ij}(\pi)=1\) when \(\pi_i>\pi_j\), and zero otherwise. Coordinates are ordered lexicographically as \((1,2),(1,3),\ldots,(n-1,n)\). The map \(\pi\mapsto b(\pi)\) is injective. The **unnormalized** Walsh transform is
\[
W_{n,p}(u)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal A_n(p)}(-1)^{\sum_{i<j}u_{ij}b_{ij}(\pi)},\qquad u\in G_n.
\]
Counting convolution is \((T_{n,p}f)(x)=\sum_{y\in G_n}1_{\{b(\pi):\pi\in\mathcal A_n(p)\}}(y)f(x-y)\). Fourier diagonalization makes its rank the number of nonzero values of \(W_{n,p}\). All assertions are for exactly \(1\le n\le5\); no formula for higher orders is asserted.

## Proof

**Two independent finite constructions.** Every permutation of each order is generated by lexicographic enumeration and rejected if any triple has the forbidden ranks. Independently, start from the empty permutation and insert the new maximum \(m\) into an avoiding permutation of length \(m-1\). For avoidance of \(123\), insertion is allowed precisely when the prefix before \(m\) has no increasing pair. For avoidance of \(132\), insertion is allowed precisely when every prefix value exceeds every suffix value. These equivalences follow because a newly inserted maximum can only be the last element of a new \(123\)-occurrence or the middle element of a new \(132\)-occurrence. Both constructions produce identical families for every order through five, with counts \(1,2,5,14,42\).

**Exact Walsh census.** For each family, directly sum \((-1)^{u\cdot b(\pi)}\) at every character. Independently apply integer Walsh–Hadamard butterflies to the binary incidence indicator. Both computations agree at every frequency, and satisfy Parseval
\[
\sum_{u\in G_n} W_{n,p}(u)^2=|G_n|\,|\mathcal A_n(p)|.
\]
Counting nonzero entries gives exactly the displayed ranks. The complete signed-multiplier histograms and order-five coefficient arrays are attached as `signed_histograms.csv` and `spectrum5.csv`; the standalone checker `check.py` regenerates and checks these files without nonstandard libraries.

**Independent modular explanation for order-five invertibility.** For each pair coordinate \(e=(i,j)\), let \(N_e\) be the number of \(123\)-avoiding permutations of length five with \(\pi_i>\pi_j\). In coordinate order these ten counts are
\[
(28,28,30,34,28,28,30,28,28,28).
\]
Every count is even. For any frequency supported on a set \(F\) of pair coordinates, expanding \(\prod_{e\in F}(1-2b_e(\pi))\) modulo four gives
\[
W_{5,123}(F)\equiv42-2\sum_{e\in F}N_e\equiv2\pmod4.
\]
Hence the transform is nowhere zero, proving invertibility without relying on a numerical inference from its exhaustive spectrum.

**Explicit order-five singularity witness.** For \(132\)-avoiders, take the two-coordinate frequency \(F=\{(1,3),(2,5)\}\). The exact four counts for \((b_{13},b_{25})=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\) are respectively \(4,6,15,17\), so
\[
W_{5,132}(F)=4-6-15+17=0.
\]
The full census gives \(207\) zeros and hence rank \(1024-207=817\).

**Sharp unrestricted projection constant.** Write \(E=\{u:W_{5,132}(u)\ne0\}\) and \(H(x)=\sum_{u\in E}(-1)^{u\cdot x}\). The integer transform of the support indicator, independently checked by direct character summation, gives
\[
\sum_{x\in G_5}|H(x)|=7594.
\]
The canonical Fourier projection onto frequencies \(E\) is convolution with \(H/1024\) and has \(\ell^1\)- and \(\ell^\infty\)-norm \(7594/1024=3797/512\). For any other real or complex projection onto the same range, average its conjugates over all translations of \(G_5\). This remains a projection onto the range, does not increase its endpoint norm, and commutes with translations. Such a translation-invariant projection is unique, since its Fourier multiplier must be one on \(E\) and zero off \(E\). Therefore no projection has smaller endpoint norm.

Finally, an affine automorphism of \(G_n\) permutes Fourier frequencies and multiplies their transforms by unit-modulus characters. It preserves the number of zero Walsh multipliers. The distinct zero counts at orders four and five prove affine inequivalence.

## Relationship to prior work

Classical Wilf equivalence identifies the two avoidance families' common Catalan cardinality. Robertson's 1999 paper studies joint \(123/132\) avoidance and pattern-occurrence refinements; Lewis's 2008 paper gives a binary-tree construction for \(132\)-avoiders. Katthän's 2011 preprint studies decomposition of permutation inversion sets. Their results describe the underlying sets and structural relations but do not supply the multivariate signed pair-coordinate transforms used here. Inv-Wilf enumeration retains only the total inversion number, a single specialization, and cannot alone determine the complete Fourier zero sets or endpoint projection constant. The present claim is the evaluated first separation and sharp finite-dimensional operator invariants, not the classical Catalan enumeration or the general Walsh–Hadamard algorithm.

## Limitations

The ranks and optimal projection constant are established only for orders one through five, not for arbitrary order. The modular proof concerns \(123\)-avoiders at order five; no all-order congruence is claimed. These computations do not classify nonlinear bijections between the two Catalan families. Historical-priority searches and inspections are bounded; independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
