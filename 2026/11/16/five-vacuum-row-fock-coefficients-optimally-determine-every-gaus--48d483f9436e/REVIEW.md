# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS

The normalized ellipse equation has trace-one negative quadratic part, so subtracting two candidate defining equations cancels the radial quadratic and leaves precisely the five measured harmonic features. On the symmetric difference, that difference has the sign of the indicator difference; strict Gaussian positivity proves uniqueness. Invariance of domain proves the five-real-count lower bound. The positive-part potential provides global inverse reconstruction, and its exact boundary-feature Gram has no kernel because a traceless quadratic cannot vanish on a whole nondegenerate conic unless zero. The unweighted boundary Gram determinant and resulting Gaussian-weight lower bound have been separately derived and numerically corroborated for three displaced anisotropic ellipses.

## Originality: PASS (provisional priority)

Jaming–Speckbacher (arXiv:1906.02964v1) give planar STFT sampling bounds, not inverse finite-vacuum-row identification. Abreu–Dörfler (arXiv:1202.5841) give an eigenfunction-based inverse theorem that forces disks, not five-entry tomography of ellipses. Di Dio (arXiv:1907.00790v1, §5) discusses general semialgebraic shape reconstruction from moments, but the cited general criterion uses higher-degree moments and does not imply this sharp degree-two Gaussian theorem. The relevant earlier circle/ball tomography and ellipse concentration claims do not recover arbitrary anisotropic ellipses from these five entries. Classic shape-from-moments literature (Gustafsson–He–Milanfar–Putinar 2000) establishes the larger inverse-moment context; its complete article was not fully accessible in the present inspection, so equivalent low-degree specializations remain a residual historical-priority risk. The general polynomial sign-separation technique itself is not claimed as novel.

## Mathematical value: PASS

This solves a natural finite-readout inverse problem for Gaussian time-frequency localization: unknown translation, two axis lengths and orientation are recovered with the topologically minimal five real observations, without any radial second-moment measurement. The unique convex inverse and strictly positive explicit boundary-Jacobian certificate characterize local identifiability beyond merely counting parameters. Unrestricted masks and uniform noise stability are not asserted.
