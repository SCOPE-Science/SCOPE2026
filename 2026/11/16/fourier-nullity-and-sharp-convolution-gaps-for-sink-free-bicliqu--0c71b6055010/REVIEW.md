# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact three-letter characterization enforces positive outdegree at all vertices. Inclusion--exclusion yields the coefficient formula, and the axial-zero exclusion follows from powers of three modulo eight. Nonaxial zeros are completely counted by parity, and evenness together with an explicit frequency proves the sharp positive spectral gap. The proof covers all \(n\ge3\) and directly treats \(n=1,2\). Integer Walsh verification through length eight and independent direct signed sums through length four corroborate the argument; computations are not substituted for infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
Cohn--Pemantle--Propp analyzes exact generation via sink popping, rather than additive-character convolution, and their complete arXiv text does not state a biclique Walsh spectrum. Anand and collaborators' sampling/approximate-counting work is likewise not a full Walsh analysis, with minimum-degree hypotheses not met by the degree-two side of \(K_{2,n}\). The nearest indexed spectral result concerns spanning trees of \(K_{2,n}\), a different support; an indexed result on acyclic orientations is incompatible with the sink-free property. No equivalent all-frequency rank and sharp-gap statement was identified in the scoped searches. Residual risk: a more general character-weighted count could imply the formula without stating these invariants.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Sink-free orientations are natural in exact sampling and graph combinatorics. Their entire convolution zero set exhibits a precise exponentially shrinking relative nullity despite singularity for every nontrivial length; simultaneously, the least nonzero Fourier magnitude remains exactly two. This is a complete infinite-family structural rank and stability classification, not an arbitrary small finite computation.

## Limitations
No formula for every nonzero multiplicity, no general biclique sizes beyond two hubs, and no independent external mathematical audit are claimed. Bounded source comparisons do not establish absolute historical uniqueness.
