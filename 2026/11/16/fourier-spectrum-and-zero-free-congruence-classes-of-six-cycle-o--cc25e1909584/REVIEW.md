# Scientific review: six-cycle Walsh orbit

## Correctness — PASS
The six-cycle-to-ordered-column-triple bijection has no orientation overcount. Inclusion-exclusion of three diagonal equalities gives \(XYZ-X^2-Y^2-Z^2+2n\). The four local sign types each have exactly two preimages among the three edge signs, giving the multiplicities and sharp finite norm. The mod-four parity argument uses the exact even incidence count per edge. The mod-sixteen argument treats both possible odd-coordinate parity patterns. The Gaussian limit uses the independent column vectors' identity covariance, uniform integrability, and an explicitly vanishing quadratic correction after \(n^{3/2}\) scaling. Exact direct Walsh computations provide independent finite corroboration through \(n=6\).

## Originality — PASS, bounded historical comparison
Actual semantic searches of published mathematical records, accessible prior findings, and the primary works below located no statement of the two exact uniform valuation laws **together with** the sharp endpoint-norm limit for the full six-cycle orbit in \(K_{3,n}\). Goodall provides general finite Fourier formalism. Schaefer–Zaslavsky give a general signed-cycle inclusion-exclusion identity and show the negative-cycle vector dimension for complete bipartite graphs; this covers the *existence of an algorithm* to recover a given signed-cycle count, so this general ingredient is not presented as new. Their all-graph inclusion-exclusion and dimension theorems do not state the evaluated valuation patterns or the limit constant. Schaefer's complete-graph setting is different. A source could contain a special treatment not indexed by these searches; exhaustive priority is not claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The six-cycle incidence orbit is the full shortest nontrivial Hamiltonian-length cycle family on the three-vertex side of a complete bipartite graph. The two infinite zero-free arithmetic progressions identify full-rank XOR convolution operators with exactly specified nonzero two-adic valuations, while the exact full coefficient multiplicities and sharp \(n^{3/2}\) endpoint-amplification constant quantify this canonical Fourier projection. This is a nontrivial joint harmonic invariant rather than a mere reformulation of cycle counting or a sample computation. Zero-rich examples at \(n=4,5\) show the inversion assertion is not automatic.
