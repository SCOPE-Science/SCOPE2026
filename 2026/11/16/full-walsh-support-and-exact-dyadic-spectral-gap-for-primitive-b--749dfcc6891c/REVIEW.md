# Review of the primitive-word Walsh-support theorem

## Correctness — PASS
Equation (1) is the pointwise Möbius inversion formula for least word period, Fourier-transformed using character orthogonality. A nonzero Möbius term has divisor \(d=mt\) with \(t\mid\operatorname{rad}(n)\), so all supports lie inside \(H_m\). On \(H_m\), the unique least divisor contributes the odd multiple \(\mu(\operatorname{rad}(n))2^m\), while all remaining contributions are divisible by \(2^{m+1}\). This proves exact nonvanishing and valuation. The explicit frequency \(e_0+e_m\) attains the sharp spectral gap. The maximal stabilizer follows by Fourier duality and the operator statements by diagonalization. The independent direct-period, integer-Walsh checker confirms every frequency through length sixteen; finite verification is not substituted for proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
Domaratzki–Rampersad, *Abelian Primitive Words*, Section 7, supplies the classical unsigned Möbius count, not an all-frequency binary Walsh support or valuation theorem. Vajnovszki, *Gray code order for Lyndon words*, studies selection and ordering of one representative from each rotation orbit, not signed sums over all primitive words. Goresky–Klapper–Washington study Fourier analysis of an individual periodic time sequence, an operator on a cyclic index group rather than the Boolean cube whose points are all length-\(n\) words. Named Boolean-Fourier, necklace, radical, equivalence, and published-finding corpus queries did not identify a stronger implying theorem; this is bounded evidence, not proof of worldwide novelty. The main residual risk is an unlocated character-valued Möbius formula for primitive necklaces that may already yield the result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Primitive (aperiodic) words are an established complete family in word combinatorics. The exact subgroup-shaped Fourier zero set for **every length**, the squarefree-radical threshold, the maximal nonobvious translation symmetry, and the exact inverse spectral gap answer natural classification/stability questions for convolution by a primitive-word indicator. Counts alone provide none of this multi-frequency information. The arbitrary-length rank jump between squarefree and prime-power lengths is structurally informative, not a parametrized toy example.

## Closest literature and limitations
The main input is the classical primitive-word Möbius enumeration. The 2010 article and the 2007 Gray-code paper concern the same primitive-word universe, but neither states or subsumes the sharp Fourier classification. The 2000 Fourier paper concerns a different transform, despite superficially similar keywords. Unlocated prior work on character sums over necklaces could diminish originality. The theorem is limited to labelled binary words, and it does not classify all nonzero magnitudes.
