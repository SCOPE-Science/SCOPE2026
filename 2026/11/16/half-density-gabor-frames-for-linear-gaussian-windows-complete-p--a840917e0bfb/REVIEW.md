# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For lattice product \(ab=1/2\), the Zak-frame multiplier is exactly the sum of two squared Zak entries. The Gaussian theta function has one simple zero in its period rectangle by the argument principle. A logarithmic-derivative difference is elliptic with two simple poles and hence two zeros; both are exhibited explicitly and force \(c=0\). The theta zero itself cannot be a common perturbed Zak zero. This proves the complete frame iff statement, for all parameters specified. At the canonical odd-window defect, the Zak multiplier has a unique nondegenerate zero on the smaller torus. Differentiation gives two independent real-direction tangent vectors, the implicit-function theorem gives an analytic minimizer, and reflection eliminates odd Taylor degrees. The moment determinant is strictly nonzero, giving the explicit positive anisotropic coefficients. A standalone checker passed 162 finite arithmetic/functional consistency tests. Its finite tests do not purport to prove the analytic infinite-dimensional conclusions.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

The closest direct text, Lemvig, arXiv:2611.05337, states a sufficient criterion requiring \(q-p>\deg P\), which excludes the present \(p=1,q=2,\deg P=1\) case. Liehr–Shafkulovska–Taylor, arXiv:2609.27846, Corollary 5.1, requires \(q\ge np+2\), likewise excluding this case. Ulanovskii–Zlotnikov, arXiv:2606.31450, prove an *existence* theorem for nonframe polynomial–Gaussian generators on this boundary, not a classification of all linear perturbations. Lyubarskii–Nes, arXiv:1108.2684, proves the pure odd-function nonframe obstruction; Faulhuber–Petersen, arXiv:2609.02610, classifies the pure first Hermite. All five primary texts were examined in relevant sections and no statement among them implies the nonzero-constant classification or phase-anisotropic lower bound. Searches under theta/Zak, linear Hermite combinations and half-density aliases also found no equivalent or stronger claim. Residual risk: specialized older Zak-frame and theta literature may contain the same elementary special-case argument or an implicit broader theorem, and independent novelty verification is outstanding.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This gives an exact sharp classification on the first exceptional rational density where the new polynomial–Gaussian sufficient criterion fails, rather than a routine extension inside its proved safe region. The odd first Hermite nonframe condition is destroyed by every nonzero complex Gaussian component, and its approach to the obstruction has two explicitly different sharp stability rates. The result resolves a natural qualitative boundary question and supplies quantitative frame conditioning for perturbations; it does not rely on an arbitrary finite table or a merely numerical observation.

## Limits of the assessment

The classification does not cover higher-order exceptional rational products or general polynomial windows. The leading sharp constants are tied to the unnormalized canonical lattice and rate; other dilations require corresponding rescaling. Numerical tests verify only finite evaluations; the proof supplies the infinite statements. Exact priority beyond the inspected literature is not certified.
