Both have exhaustive global minimization arguments, with all complex coefficients and phase boundaries covered. The nonframe completeness conclusion uses an almost-everywhere injectivity argument, not a mistaken implication from the frame bound. The singular normal form is obtained from nondegenerate global trigonometric extrema, and the odd/even Taylor orders were checked independently. The saved checker passed 3,536 finite consistency assertions; these are not substitutes for the proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected primary and indexed evidence.** The closest papers by Stangl–Frederick (2026) and Hasannasab–Lemvig (2026) classify Zak zeros and frames for *individual* hat splines, with variable lattice steps rather than a two-pulse filtered analysis window and fixed lattice. Christensen–Kim–Kim (2017), including its explicit Section 4 combination of shifted B-splines, uses those combinations as approximate-dual windows rather than proving their own sharp two-channel Gabor spectra. Related indexed results on sharp hat-spline bounds and on finite cyclic two-tap Gabor systems do not state or imply the continuous two-pulse delay-parity spectrum, completeness at singular parameters, and global optimal conditioning. A general Zak criterion is acknowledged as background and does not, by itself, state the classification. Remaining priority risk: possible older finite-impulse-response filtered-window Gabor theory.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem is an all-parameter classification of a minimal, naturally motivated coherent-pulse window: it distinguishes removable instability from loss of completeness, characterizes the optimal phase/delay design, and quantifies a parity-specific quadratic-versus-quartic instability. Such sharp conditioning and phase sensitivity are relevant to oversampled time-frequency systems using spline pulse shaping; they are not merely one hand-picked parameter evaluation.

**Limitations.** Only integer delays, the centered order-two spline, and the fixed redundancy-two lattice are treated. The odd-delay bound is an exact finite stationary-point minimization, not a universal radical expression. No independent review or publication-priority audit has been performed.
