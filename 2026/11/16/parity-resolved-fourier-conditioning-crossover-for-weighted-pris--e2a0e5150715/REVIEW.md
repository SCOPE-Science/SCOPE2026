## Originality

**PASS, with provisional historical priority.** The closest explicitly compared result on uniform prism matching covariance concerns the single activity value \(\lambda=1\) and the full edge-incidence covariance. That special case is not counted as an original contribution here. The new statement is the continuously weighted rung spectrum, exact even/odd rank and extremal modes, and especially the sharp distinct \(\lambda=c/n\) critical laws and near-alternating profiles. Wilkins–Powell (arXiv:2103.03260, §§ II–III, VI) analyzes the even-by-even periodic square-lattice dimer model and bulk/topological-sector correlations, not the simple finite prism's parameter-dependent rung Fourier ratio. Quitmann (arXiv:2309.13632, § 2 and Theorem 2.1) proves exponential decay for *fixed positive* monomer activity, not these exact finite spectra or nonuniform shrinking-activity limits. A prior specialized one-dimensional dimer structure factor remains a substantive priority risk; the searches were not a proof of global uniqueness.

## Value

**PASS.** This is a natural inverse-observation question: a rung count is a spatially sampled dimer observable, and the close-packed model has an exact parity conservation law. The theorem determines precisely which spatial modes are identifiable, where sharp instability occurs and how weak rung activity on the finite-size scale \(\lambda\asymp n^-1\) changes the order of conditioning from quadratic growth to finite parity-separated limits. The factor-of-four parity distinction and complete critical near-null profile cannot be inferred from merely knowing fixed-activity correlation decay. This is a meaningful all-size spectral classification and a motivated critical-window stability law, rather than an arbitrary parameter substitution.
