For every fixed cross-polytope dimension \(d\ge5\), the count of nonzero integer-lattice Fourier coefficients inside a frequency box has a sharp parity split: \(2^d\binom d2N^{d-1}+O_d(N^{d-2})\) in odd dimensions, but \(2^d\binom d3N^{d-2}+O_d(N^{d-3})\) in even dimensions. The reason is a Hermite-divided-difference collision law: a generic pair of matching absolute frequencies suffices in odd dimension, whereas even dimension requires a generic triple.
