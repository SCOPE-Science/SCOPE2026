# Scientific review of wheel spanning-tree Walsh nonvanishing

## Correctness
PASS. The claim follows from the weighted matrix-tree identity at arbitrary \(\pm1\) edge weights. The classical Lucas formula and its period-three parity prove that all coefficients are odd whenever \(3\nmid n\). When \(n=3k\), the displayed \((1,1,2)\)-periodic nullvector works for all \(4^k\) explicitly signed wheel Laplacians. The separate modulo-four argument uses the correctly evaluated spoke-inclusion count \(F_{2n}\), the parity period of Fibonacci numbers, and the Lucas companion-matrix identity modulo four. Exact finite enumeration and integer determinant calculations independently check the formulas in accessible dimensions.

## Originality
PASS under bounded prior comparison. The general determinant identity and the unweighted wheel-tree enumeration are acknowledged prior results. A full reading of Benjamin and Yerger's six-page wheel enumeration manuscript shows an unweighted bijection and count, not an all-character Walsh-zero classification. The relevant sections of Constantine and Buliga's full-text article provide colored-tree determinants, but no evaluated wheel Fourier support theorem. Related first-coordinate wheel spanning-tree Fourier Gram results concern low-degree correlations rather than all \(2^{2n}\) convolution eigenvalues. An earlier specialized signed-wheel theorem might still exist outside the inspected literature.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Wheel spanning trees are a natural graphic-matroid basis family. Invertibility of convolution by their indicator is a genuine all-character harmonic invariant, distinct from the classical total tree count or low-order moments. The exact modulo-three dichotomy, the singular periodic nullvector construction, universal fourfold divisibility in singular dimensions, and exponential explicit family of zeros give a complete qualitative classification with structural and arithmetic content, not a parameter-renaming or an isolated small example.

## Literature and scope limitations
The primary scientific inputs are Constantine–Buliga (2004) on weighted colored spanning-tree determinants and Benjamin–Yerger (manuscript 2004, article 2006) on Lucas wheel counts. The weighted cellular matrix-tree theory of Aalipour and collaborators (2015) is broader background but does not evaluate these Fourier zeros. Full exact zero multiplicities and norms remain open here. Novelty cannot be guaranteed from a bounded literature search.
