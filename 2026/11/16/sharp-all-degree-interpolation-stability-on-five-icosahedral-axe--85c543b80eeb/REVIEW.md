# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The addition theorem and the exact five-cycle inner products give the entire Gram spectrum without approximation. The two-term rational recurrence enumerates the finite degrees \(2\) through \(18\), with independent numerical harmonic evaluation checks. The published Legendre Bernstein inequality proves the strict infinite tail; the finite tests alone are not represented as an infinite proof. Dimension, measure normalization and limits of data interpolation are explicit.

## Originality
PASS under accessible evidence, with historical-priority risk. The motivating full paper on near-optimal spherical-harmonic reconstruction does not evaluate this deterministic five-axis problem. Hitchin's full paper contains the important *previously known* cubic rank obstruction and equiangular axis geometry, which are expressly not asserted as independently novel. The full Bernstein bound is credited. Nearby indexed dodecahedral-axis, root-system and icosahedral-rotation frame calculations use different sample sets/operators. No implication from their claims supplies the sharp all-degree five-axis bound or the four equality degrees; unindexed equivalence is a remaining risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is a natural minimal-erasure question for the six antipodal icosahedral axes. It gives a globally sharp, degree-uniform guarantee for interpolation of five arbitrary samples, identifies the cubic blind spot inherited from the underlying geometry, and classifies four unexpectedly repeated worst-conditioned degrees. It does not overclaim recovery of a whole high-degree harmonic from five observations.
