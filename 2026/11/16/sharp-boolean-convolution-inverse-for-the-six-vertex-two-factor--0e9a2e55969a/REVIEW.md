# Review of the six-vertex two-factor convolution theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The assumptions and counting normalization are explicit. Both simple-graph 2-factor classes account for all 70 factors. By symmetry each of 15 edges occurs in 28 factors; inclusion–exclusion modulo four gives the universal multiplier congruence. A three-edge frequency attains multiplier magnitude two. Complete exact enumeration, two independent descriptions of the support, all 32768 direct character sums, both integral Fourier transforms, a 512-class quotient cross-check, Parseval and every inverse-convolution identity establish the signed spectrum and sharp inverse norms.

## Originality
PASS, bounded. The checked mathematical-record searches for signed 2-factor enumerators, the precise K6 full-edge Walsh spectrum and the rational inverse norm returned no statement covering these parameters. The closest existing Fourier claim for undirected Hamiltonian cycles treats a strict 60-member family and does not imply the result after including the 10 disconnected factors. The inspected Alspach et al. and Pasotti–Pellegrini two-factorisation theorems address decompositions, not signed orbit sums; the Baldridge–Lowrance–McCarty polynomial works with prescribed matchings in planar cubic graphs. The 2005 Aldred–Holton–Sheehan abstract concerns existence with prescribed/proscribed edges, but its full text was not accessible. An unpublished or unindexed equivalent signed 2-factor table remains a possible priority risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Six vertices is the first order where spanning two-regular graphs include disconnected members, and the full family unexpectedly returns to Fourier invertibility after a simple cancellation at five vertices. The exact inverse norm is an intrinsically harmonic-analytic stability invariant; its six kernel amplitudes and 512-point support are neither implied by the number 70 nor by parity nonvanishing. It supplies a reproducible first mixed-cycle recovery benchmark without claiming a general higher-order theorem.

## Limits and comparison
No claim is made for larger complete graphs, independent external validation, or exhaustive priority. The exact norm depends on the exhaustive finite certificate, not the elementary congruence alone. Main comparisons: Aldred–Holton–Sheehan (2005), DOI 10.1002/jgt.20065; Alspach et al. (2014), arXiv:1411.6047; Pasotti–Pellegrini (2016), arXiv:1603.06469; Baldridge–Lowrance–McCarty (2018), arXiv:1812.10346. The first source's full text remains inaccessible, and the bounded search cannot rule out unindexed tables.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
