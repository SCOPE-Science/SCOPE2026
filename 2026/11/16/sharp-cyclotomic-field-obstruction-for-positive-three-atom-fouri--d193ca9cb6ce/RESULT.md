# Sharp cyclotomic field obstruction for positive three-atom Fourier zeros

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(p\ge5\) be an odd prime, let \(v_0,v_1,v_2\in\mathbb F_p^2\) be affinely independent, and let \(A,B,C\) be strictly positive real numbers. Put
\[
f=A\delta_{v_0}+B\delta_{v_1}+C\delta_{v_2},\qquad
\widehat f(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^2f(v_j)e^{-2\pi i\langle \xi,v_j\rangle/p}.
\]
Then its Fourier zero set is either empty or exactly \(\{\xi,-\xi\}\), with \(\xi\ne0\). If a zero exists, the number field generated by its two projective positive amplitude ratios is necessarily
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Q(B/A,C/A)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}),\quad [\mathbb Q(B/A,C/A):\mathbb Q]=(p-1)/2,}
\]
where \(\zeta_p=e^{2\pi i/p}\). In particular, positive amplitudes whose two ratios belong to a number field of degree strictly less than \((p-1)/2\) produce a zero-free Fourier transform. The degree threshold is attained for every \(p\ge5\).

There are exactly \(\boxed{(p^2-1)/8}\) distinct positive projective amplitude triples \([A:B:C]\) admitting any Fourier zero (each producing exactly two zeros). These rays have a complete explicit parametrization: take integers \(0<u<v<p\) with \(u\), \(v-u\) and \(p-v\) all at most \((p-1)/2\); form the positive triple
\[
\left[\sin\left(\frac{2\pi(v-u)}p\right): -\sin\left(\frac{2\pi v}p\right):\sin\left(\frac{2\pi u}p\right)\right].
\]
Include the coefficient triples obtained by interchanging the last two entries and identify triples that coincide; equivalently, index rays by oriented strictly containing triangles of three \(p\)-th roots with one anchored vertex, modulo complex conjugation. The count and degree assertions hold for every affine-independent choice of three spatial sites.

## Assumptions and scope

The ambient group is the additive group \(\mathbb F_p^2\) with the standard perfect pairing. The spatial sites are three distinct noncollinear vectors. Amplitudes are real and strictly positive; a common positive factor is immaterial. A projective amplitude triple means a positive ray in \(\mathbb R_{>0}^3\). For prime \(p\), the field \(K_p^+=\mathbb Q(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1})\) is the maximal real subfield of the \(p\)-th cyclotomic field and has degree \((p-1)/2\). There is no assertion for collinear triples, nonprime moduli, zero or sign-changing coefficients, or higher supports.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to two independent phases.** Set \(w_1=v_1-v_0\), \(w_2=v_2-v_0\). Because the spatial triple is affinely independent, \(\xi\mapsto(u,v)=(-\langle\xi,w_1\rangle,-\langle\xi,w_2\rangle)\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb F_p^2\). Removing the common nonzero factor \(e^{-2\pi i\langle\xi,v_0\rangle/p}\), the zero equation is
\[
A+B\zeta_p^u+C\zeta_p^v=0.
\]
No two phases may coincide: two coincident positive vectors cannot be cancelled by a single positive vector at any odd-order root of unity, since the opposing direction would require a root of unity of order two. Thus \(0,u,v\) are distinct.

**2. Complete positive cancellation rays.** Conjugating and, when necessary, swapping the two nonzero phases, we can study \(0<u<v<p\). The origin lies in the strictly positive cone generated by \(1,\zeta_p^u,\zeta_p^v\) precisely when each of the three cyclic angular gaps is less than a half-circle: the integer gaps \(u\), \(v-u\), \(p-v\) are then each at most \(m=(p-1)/2\). This follows either by separating hyperplanes in \(\mathbb R^2\) or by the positive unique kernel of the \(2\times3\) real matrix of these three unit vectors. The sine-of-difference identity gives the kernel vector
\[
(A,B,C)\ \propto\ (\sin(2\pi(v-u)/p),-\sin(2\pi v/p),\sin(2\pi u/p));
\]
all entries are strictly positive under the gap conditions. This establishes the parametrization and existence.

**3. Two-zero rigidity.** Divide by \(A>0\). Taking the squared modulus of \(A+B\zeta_p^u=-C\zeta_p^v\), the phase \(u\) obeys
\[
\cos(2\pi u/p)=\frac{C^2-A^2-B^2}{2AB}.
\]
Among \(p\)-th root phases this permits at most the two values \(u\) and \(-u\), and \(u\ne0\). For each such \(u\), the original equation determines \(\zeta_p^v\) uniquely. Conjugation produces the opposite solution. Therefore every nonempty zero set has exactly two antipodal characters.

**4. Arithmetic field rigidity.** Set \(S_j=\zeta_p^j-\zeta_p^{-j}\). The displayed sine ratios show that \(B/A=-S_v/S_{v-u}\) and \(C/A=S_u/S_{v-u}\) in the sorted orientation. These quotients lie in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) and are fixed by complex conjugation, so \(L=\mathbb Q(B/A,C/A)\subseteq K_p^+\). Suppose that this inclusion is proper. Since \(K_p^+/\mathbb Q\) is a finite Galois extension, there is an automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) fixing \(L\) and sending \(\zeta_p\) to \(\zeta_p^s\) with \(s\not\equiv\pm1\pmod p\). Applying it to the normalized zero equation gives another zero with phase pair \((su,sv)\). Step 3 says that this pair must equal either \((u,v)\) or \((-u,-v)\). As \(u\ne0\), this forces \(s\equiv\pm1\pmod p\), a contradiction. Thus \(L=K_p^+\), of degree \((p-1)/2\). If the ratios belong to any number field \(E\), the field \(L\subseteq E\) gives the stated degree obstruction. Conversely the positive configurations of Step 2 attain the degree bound for every prime.

**5. Exact ray count.** For sorted phases \(0<u<v<p\), let \(a=u\), \(b=v-u\), \(c=p-v\). The gaps are positive integers with \(a+b+c=p=2m+1\) and each at most \(m\). Replacing \(a,b,c\) with \(m-a,m-b,m-c\), or using inclusion-exclusion, yields exactly \(m(m+1)/2=(p^2-1)/8\) sorted phase pairs. Each has two phase assignments to the ordered coefficients \(B,C\); the two assignments together are paired by global complex conjugation. Step 3 proves there are exactly two phase pairs per positive ray, so the number of rays equals \((p^2-1)/8\). The identity of these rays does not depend on the spatial triple because of Step 1.

**Example.** At \(p=5\), the amplitude ray \([1:(1+\sqrt5)/2:1]\) has the zero \(1+((1+\sqrt5)/2)\zeta_5^2+\zeta_5^4=0\) and its conjugate; its coefficient field is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), of sharp degree two. There are three positive cancellation rays at this prime.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `checker.py` checks the finite cyclotomic identities using exact integer arithmetic modulo \(1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}\). It independently counts admissible positive rays, verifies all nontrivial Galois actions on the two normalized ratios, and checks the exact cancellation identities for primes through 53. A separate small-prime enumeration checks every Fourier phase pair. Execute `python3 checker.py` from the extracted file directory. Finite computation does not establish the all-prime assertions; Steps 1–5 above are the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Dongwei Li, *Parity-Sensitive Fourier Uncertainty and Zero-Set Rigidity on the Finite Parabola*, arXiv:2609.16030v1 (first public September 11, 2026), proves an upper bound of two zeros for nonzero three-point complex data on the prime finite parabola; the present positive-amplitude analysis gives an arithmetic field obstruction and exact rays for all noncollinear planar triples, not a claim of discovering the two-zero upper bound. The published indexed result *Exact three-sparse Fourier support spectra on elementary Abelian odd-prime groups* also treats the general attainable counts and hence already covers a zero-count statement. *Cyclotomic zero-line rigidity for rational finite-parabola Fourier data* addresses rational coefficients and the possible zero geometries, but its available summary does not state an exact minimal degree for positive algebraic ratios. Cyclotomic Galois theory and sine-weighted triangle cancellation are classical ingredients, not novel assertions.

## Limitations

The original 2026 motivating paper's full text could not be inspected; its public abstract was read. Relevant indexed repository records were inspected by semantic summaries, not by their full proofs or appendices. This leaves a real priority risk that the arithmetic field-degree statement, ray census, or an equivalent broader corollary is already written down. The field-degree conclusion is proved for **positive** real amplitudes and **noncollinear** triples only; it says nothing about generic conditioning near zeros, or about finite approximations to irrational ratios. No independent mathematical audit has yet been performed.
