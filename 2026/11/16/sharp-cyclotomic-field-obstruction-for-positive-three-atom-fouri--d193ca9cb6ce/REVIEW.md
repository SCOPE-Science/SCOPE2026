# Review of the cyclotomic field obstruction

## Correctness (PASS)

Affine independence makes the frequency-to-relative-phase map bijective. Strictly positive cancellation requires three phases whose cyclic gaps are each smaller than a half-turn, and the kernel of the real two-row phase matrix is uniquely the sine-of-difference vector. The squared-modulus identity fixes one nonzero phase modulo sign; the other phase is uniquely then determined. This proves exactly two antipodal Fourier zeros for any admissible positive weights, without a finite-enumeration assumption. Sine ratios are fixed by complex conjugation and lie in the maximal real cyclotomic field. Any nontrivial field automorphism fixing both ratios would produce a third cancellation, contradicting two-zero rigidity, hence the projective coefficient field is the full real field. Counting gap triples gives \((p^2-1)/8\) rays. An exact-arithmetic checker verifies the sine identities, Galois nonstabilizers and all small-prime Fourier zeros. The checks are corroborative, not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality (PASS relative to examined sources)

The closest sources are Li's 2026 general finite-parabola Fourier-zero theorem and the indexed *Exact three-sparse Fourier support spectra on elementary Abelian odd-prime groups*. Their known zero-count bounds and attainable counts already cover the bound of two zeros; this review does not assert novelty for that subclaim. The indexed *Cyclotomic zero-line rigidity for rational finite-parabola Fourier data* treats coefficients in \(\mathbb Q\) rather than the exact smallest number field of all **positive** algebraic ratio pairs; the present result yields an exact \((p-1)/2\) degree threshold. The classical Galois and triangle identities are not claimed as original. Searches for cyclotomic degree, three-positive-atom cancellation, rational and real algebraic Fourier zeros, and prior prime-parabola zero classifications found no statement implying this entire field obstruction. Full access to Li's paper and the most pertinent repository records was not obtained; priority remains provisional rather than independently certified.

## Value (PASS)

The theorem identifies a precise arithmetic obstruction to *exact spectral annihilation*: any amplitude-ratio field of degree smaller than \((p-1)/2\) is automatically Fourier zero-free, and the threshold is attained for every prime. This cleanly distinguishes positive algebraic amplitudes by number-field complexity, classifies every exceptional projective ray, and explains why rational coefficients cannot cancel for \(p\ge5\). The object is natural in sparse finite Fourier analysis and finite-parabola uncertainty; the quantified field threshold is a structural statement, not a small numeric refinement.
