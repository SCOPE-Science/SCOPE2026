Avoiding any unbordered binary factor of length \(k\) forces every unnormalized Walsh multiplier at word length \(n\) to be divisible by \(2^{n\bmod k}\), and an explicit character attains this sharp nonzero gap. Multiples of \(k\) have a completely nonvanishing odd Walsh spectrum, while at lengths congruent to one modulo \(k\), exactly half the characters have the minimum possible two-adic valuation.
