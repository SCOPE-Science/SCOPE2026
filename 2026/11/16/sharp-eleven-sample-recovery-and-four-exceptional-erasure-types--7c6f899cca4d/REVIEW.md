# Review of the sharp rhombic-dodecahedral erasure theorem

## Correctness (PASS)
The space has a nine-function harmonic basis. For the 14 specified spherical nodes, modular images of the exact evaluation matrix in characteristics 97 and 109 have rank nine on all 364 eleven-point subsets and on 956 of the 1001 ten-point subsets. Four symmetry orbits contain all 45 remaining ten-point subsets. Exact arithmetic in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\) supplies explicit vanishing quadratics on each orbit representative; their modular rank eight proves these are precisely one-dimensional failures in characteristic zero. The complete standalone checker reproduces all finite cases, not a random sample. No inference about floating-point conditioning is made.

## Originality (PASS, bounded priority assessment)
The closest same-node published analysis concerns single-degree harmonic sampling on the full fourteen points, not cumulative degree-at-most-two erasure ranks. The published cuboctahedral erasure matroid concerns a different twelve-point node set; planar/polyhedral duality does not turn its evaluation matrix into this one. Zandieh–Han–Avron prove general randomized recovery estimates without arbitrary survivor guarantees. The equiangular cubed-sphere paper was only accessible via abstract; this and unindexed historical tables remain priority risks. Comparison uses mathematical implication, not titles alone.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The 14-vertex rhombic dodecahedron is a natural two-orbit spherical sampling configuration. The theorem gives the sharp universal recovery threshold and resolves every exceptional ten-node erasure by four exact symmetry types with explicit quadratic witnesses. That geometric classification is a concrete, reusable guarantee for noiseless low-degree spherical reconstruction; it does not assert a noise-stability bound.
