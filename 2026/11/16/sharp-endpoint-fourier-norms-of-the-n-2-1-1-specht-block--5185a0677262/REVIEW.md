## Correctness — PASS
The exterior-square trace identity reconstructs the character from 1- and 2-cycle counts, and the real irreducible central idempotent has sharp endpoint norm equal to character degree times the Haar \(L^1\) norm. A direct exact enumeration of disjoint fixed points, transpositions, and a remainder with no short cycles yields the displayed positive-part sum with all factorial normalizations. The generating function \(e^{-z-z^2/2}/(1-z)\) is a standard exponential cycle-index identity. The compensating product \(H(z)\) is entire by a majorant \((R^2/2)e^{2R+R^2}\), so Cauchy bounds prove the uniform-for-each-radius geometric remainder and the limit. Independent exact computations from raw permutations and cycle-count formulas corroborate these identities at finite ranks. The theorem does not rely on an unverified numerical extrapolation.

The two-row \((n-2,2)\) character differs by the 2-cycle sign and has a distinct negative region; its exact norm does not imply the present sum, constant, or entire-error theorem. A historical equivalent under character \(L^1\) terminology remains possible.

The sharp \(\ell^1\) and \(\ell^\infty\) amplification constant of an irreducible central projector is a canonical harmonic invariant. The second-exterior-power hook is the first block where both short-cycle counts interact through an unbounded negative threshold, giving genuinely two-parameter signed character statistics. The exact all-rank norm and the faster-than-any-geometric limit give quantitative information beyond tabulated character values and ordinary weak Poisson convergence, and make this family available as an exact comparison case for nonabelian Fourier endpoint stability.
