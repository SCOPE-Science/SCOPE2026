# Scientific review: centered-coordinate Fourier-cross flatness

## Correctness — PASS

The exact fourth-moment formula follows from independent coordinate characters, their second and fourth moments, and a completion of the square. Its normalization is probability Haar measure, and the full claim assumes dimension at least two and unit magnitudes for all \(2d+1\) coefficients. A conjugate pair realizes the minimum in two dimensions; a distinct three-dimensional witness and cancelling pairs cover all higher dimensions. The upper equality case follows from a sharp triangle inequality. In the finite-alphabet subproblem, the defining phase products are roots of unity and therefore algebraic integers, excluding the rational value \(-1/2\) without any numerical approximation. Binary, fourth-root and sixth-root gaps are shortest-vector calculations in one- and two-dimensional integer lattices. Ternary gaps follow from an exact integer-square formula. The five-phase claim is certified by all 25 exact \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) comparisons. The packaged checker independently reconstructs Fourier convolution moments; finite checks corroborate but do not establish the all-dimensional conclusions.

## Originality — PASS, with a qualified historical-priority risk

The most directly comparable published theorem on **the same signed-coordinate frequency support** establishes an arbitrary-amplitude *upper* fourth-moment ratio and upper-extremizer phases. It gives neither this sharp *lower* equal-magnitude fourth-moment formula nor the all-finite-alphabet impossibility. The published five-frequency cross result optimizes the *upper sixth* moment with variable amplitudes. Published finite-abelian cross extension results optimize upper rather than lower moments on different supports. The full text of Das–Banerjee–Parampalli (2023) treats complementary four-array constructions and PMEPR upper bounds rather than this single sparse cross and its exact lower-moment phase geometry. Searches for equivalent phase-only cross formulas, moment minima, and finite-root obstructions did not locate a stronger theorem. Classical algebraic-integrality and Fourier orthogonality are not claimed as new. Earlier specialized radar, coding, or array-theory work may still implicitly contain an equivalent statement, so unconditional historical priority is not asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The centered coordinate cross is the canonical nearest-neighbor Fourier stencil on a product torus. Integrated fourth moment is exactly zero-shift autocorrelation energy plus total sidelobe energy, a natural flatness objective for sparse phase-coded apertures. The result settles a natural sharp minimization in every dimension and identifies **every** equality phase configuration. Its absolute finite-alphabet obstruction is structurally stronger than an isolated small numerical improvement: no fixed finite phase alphabet, however large, ever reaches the continuous optimum. The residue-dependent ternary gap and nonmonotone five-versus-six-phase onset in the smallest two-dimensional cross quantify meaningful phase quantization effects. These statements address an exact Fourier-analytic invariant; no unsupported practical signal-performance benefit is claimed.
