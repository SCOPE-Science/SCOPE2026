# Scientific review: sharp local stability of the Dirichlet-range Hardy–Littlewood contraction

## Correctness — PASS
The defining radial-mean functional agrees with Eq. (1.1) of Brevig–Kulikov–Seip–Zlotnikov, and the weighted derivative identity and monomial coefficients agree with their Eqs. (2.2)–(2.4). On the explicitly bounded nonvanishing neighborhood \(1+h\), the uniform estimate for \(|1+h|^{t-2}\) proves a genuine infinite-dimensional quadratic remainder, rather than a formal Taylor series in finitely many coefficients. The exact eigenvalues follow from the \(c_s(k)\) recurrence and the first-mode cancellation from the common conformal index. The second mode proves optimality. The gauge step uses the real implicit-function theorem and exact common isometries. Risks: this does not give a global quantitative rigidity theorem or uniform parameter bounds, and the local topology must retain the supremum-norm hypothesis.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The October 2025 source proves qualitative contraction and classifies equality but does not assert a sharp local remainder or its full transverse Hessian. Llinares (2024) treats the Dirichlet-to-Hardy special case as a global embedding; Kulikov (2021) proves a distinct Hardy coefficient inequality. Semantic searches of related published records found only Hessian results for different objects (e.g., equispaced Bergman poles and finite-field paraboloid extension). The claim-to-prior implication comparison is explicit: a qualitative nonnegative deficit and a classification of equality do not imply a uniform positive Hessian on the complement of symmetry directions, still less the exact sharp coefficient and uniform nonlinear remainder. Residual risk remains from older hypercontractive/Carleman stability literature under alternate terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The new result quantifies the sharp inequality's previously qualitative extremizer rigidity, identifies the precise obstruction from conformal symmetry, and supplies an explicit spectral stability law for every Dirichlet-range exponent and target exponent, rather than a single finite-dimensional example. The second Fourier mode is proved to be the unique weakest transverse direction, with an exact gap that can be used in local convergence/stability analyses. This is a motivated structural question attached directly to a new class of contractive inequalities, rather than a generic substitution into a known formula. The value is local, not a claim of a global stability theorem.
