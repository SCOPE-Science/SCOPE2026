# Sharp one-channel spectral sampling on the normalized pendant chain

## Finding
Let \(G\) be the infinite graph with vertices \((n,s),(n,p)\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), where every spine vertex has edges to its two neighboring spine vertices and its own pendant leaf. Let \(L\) be the symmetric normalized graph Laplacian and \(\mathcal H_\omega=\mathbf1_{[0,\omega]}(L)\ell^2(G)\). The following theorem simultaneously determines its one-complex-channel sampling threshold, the sharp maximum lower stability bound, and all tight local sensors.

For \(0<\omega\le4/3\), set
\[
\lambda(c)=\frac{c+\sqrt{c^2+3}}3,\quad u(c)=\frac{(\sqrt3\lambda(c),1)}{\sqrt{1+3\lambda(c)^2}},\quad c_\omega=\begin{cases}\frac{3(1-\omega)^2-1}{2(1-\omega)},&0<\omega<2/3,\\-1,&2/3\le\omega\le4/3.\end{cases}
\]
For any fixed \(w=(w_s,w_p)\in\mathbb C^2\) with \(\|w\|=1\), measure a signal by \(T_wf=(\overline{w_s}f(n,s)+\overline{w_p}f(n,p))_{n\in\mathbb Z}\). Then
\[
\sup_{\|w\|=1}\inf_{0\ne f\in\mathcal H_\omega}\frac{\|T_wf\|_2^2}{\|f\|_2^2}=\frac{1+\langle u(c_\omega),u(1)\rangle}{2}.
\]
The unique maximizing \(w\), modulo unimodular scalar, is \(w_*=(u(c_\omega)+u(1))/\|u(c_\omega)+u(1)\|\). The *sharp* lower and upper frame bounds for this sensor are the displayed number and \(1\). In particular for the full lower band \(2/3\le\omega\le4/3\), \(w_*=(1,1)/\sqrt2\) and the sharp lower bound equals \((2+\sqrt3)/4\).

The normalized sensors attaining exact tightness for every positive bandwidth in this range are *exactly* \(w=(1,\pm i)/\sqrt2\) modulo a common phase; their sharp frame bound is \(1/2\). Thus the single sequence \((f(n,s)\pm if(n,p))_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) preserves the whole signal norm on \(\mathcal H_\omega\). For \(\omega>4/3\), no fixed one-local-complex-channel sensor yields a lower frame bound, indeed no such sensor is injective.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is unweighted, simple, and infinite, and its spine and leaf degrees are respectively three and one. The signal Hilbert space is \(\ell^2(G)\) with counting measure, and \(L=I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}\) is bounded self-adjoint. Fourier transforms along the integer coordinate are unitary, with normalized Lebesgue measure on \([ -\pi,\pi]\). Frame bounds mean inequalities for every signal in \(\mathcal H_\omega\); no positivity or reality is assumed for signals. The same unit complex row functional is used in every cell with no temporal samples. We exclude the trivial zero-band spectral subspace \(\omega=0\).

## Proof
**Spectral fibers.** Fourier transforming only the cell coordinate sends \(I-L\) to
\[
M(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac23\cos\theta&\frac1{\sqrt3}\\\frac1{\sqrt3}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
With \(c=\cos\theta\), its eigenvalues are \(\lambda_\pm(c)=(c\pm\sqrt{c^2+3})/3\). Consequently the spectrum of \(L\) is \([0,2/3]\cup[4/3,2]\), with the lower Laplacian band generated by the real, positive, unit eigenvector \(u(c)\) displayed above. The upper band begins at \(4/3\) on a measure-zero fiber, so \(\mathcal H_\omega\) has almost-everywhere fiber dimension one exactly for \(0<\omega\le4/3\). Its active values of \(c\) form \([c_\omega,1]\), with positive-measure interior; the endpoint formula follows by inverting \(\lambda(c)=1-\omega\).

**Exact fiber frame inequality.** Each member of \(\mathcal H_\omega\) has Fourier transform \(\widehat f(\theta)=h(\theta)u(\cos\theta)\) on the active angular set and zero elsewhere. Plancherel gives
\[
\|f\|_2^2=\int|h(\theta)|^2\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},\quad\|T_wf\|_2^2=\int|h(\theta)|^2|\langle w,u(\cos\theta)\rangle|^2\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Thus optimal sharp frame constants are the essential infimum and supremum of that multiplier, and continuity turns these into ordinary minima and maxima on the closed active \(c\)-interval.

**Optimal lower stability.** Put \(v=u(c_\omega)\), \(q=u(1)\), and \(s=\langle v,q\rangle\in(0,1)\). For every normalized complex \(w\),
\[
\min\{|\langle w,v\rangle|^2,|\langle w,q\rangle|^2\}\le\tfrac12\langle w,(vv^*+qq^*)w\rangle\le\frac{1+s}{2}.
\]
The top eigenvalue of \((vv^*+qq^*)/2\) is \((1+s)/2\), simple because \(0<s<1\), with unique normalized top eigenvector proportional to \(v+q\). The directions \(u(c)\) traverse the minor positive quadrant arc from \(v\) to \(q\) monotonically: the slope of the first coordinate relative to the second is \(\sqrt3\lambda(c)\), increasing strictly in \(c\). The bisector \(w_*\) has overlap squared at least \((1+s)/2\) throughout that arc, with equality at the endpoints, and overlap one at its midpoint (attained by an interior \(c\) since the angle varies continuously and strictly). This proves both optimality and uniqueness of the sharp interval \([A_*,1]\). At \(c=-1,1\) the vectors are \((1/2,\sqrt3/2)\) and \((\sqrt3/2,1/2)\), so \(s=\sqrt3/2\) and \(A_*=(2+\sqrt3)/4\).

**All tight one-channel sensors.** For real \(u(c)=(x(c),y(c))\),
\[
|\langle w,u(c)\rangle|^2=|w_s|^2x(c)^2+|w_p|^2y(c)^2+2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{w_s}w_p)x(c)y(c).
\]
The active eigenvector angles vary on a nontrivial open interval. The linear independence of \(1,\cos(2t),\sin(2t)\) on that interval proves the multiplier is constant precisely when \(|w_s|^2=|w_p|^2=1/2\) and \(\operatorname{Re}(\overline{w_s}w_p)=0\). These are exactly the two quadrature sensors, modulo global phase, and the multiplier is identically \(1/2\). Removing the factor \(1/\sqrt2\) yields an isometry.

**Sharp bandwidth obstruction.** For every \(\omega>4/3\), the upper Laplacian branch belongs to \(\mathcal H_\omega\) on a positive-measure interval near \(\theta=0\); both Bloch directions therefore occur in the spectral subspace there. Any complex row from \(\mathbb C^2\) to \(\mathbb C\) has a nontrivial kernel. A measurable fiberwise kernel direction multiplied by a nonzero square-integrable scalar yields a nonzero bandlimited signal with identically zero samples. Thus injectivity already fails, irrespective of the chosen fixed row.

## Verification
The source file `checker.py` independently constructs the two-by-two Fourier fibers, diagonalizes them numerically, checks exact symbolic endpoint identities, verifies the bisector and quadrature multipliers on several bandwidths, tests random complex sensors against the endpoint bound, and checks full periodic-chain synthesized signals by unitary FFT. Running `python checker.py` with Python 3, NumPy and SymPy returned `PASS 5495 fiber and signal checks; exact endpoint identities`. These finite checks support normalization and arithmetic; the claims for infinitely many cells and all bandwidths follow from the preceding analytic proof, not finite experimentation.

## Relationship to prior work
Graph Paley–Wiener spaces and dynamical operator-orbit sampling are developed by Bulai, Cabrelli, Cordero, Molter and Saliani in their September 2026 preprint [1]; the accessible abstract discusses integer lattices, their products, and finite path experiments, but the full manuscript was inaccessible for detailed comparison. Giannoni [2] proves general infinite-graph vertex sampling criteria using Poincaré-type complements; these do not directly state the present two-site complex-mixing sensor, exact cutoff, or unique optimal stability. Tanaka and Eldar [3] provide general graph generalized-sampling machinery, rather than the exact normalized infinite pendant-chain fiber calculation. Classical work of Pesenson [4] supplies graph Paley–Wiener motivation. The contributions asserted here are the complete exact one-channel classification and optimization *for this graph and sensor model*, not invention of fiberwise graph Fourier sampling or generic frame theory.

## Limitations
The graph and local sensor family are fixed, and no result is claimed for arbitrary periodic decorations, spatially varying sensors, or time-varying measurements. At the band edge \(\omega=4/3\) an upper-branch eigenvector exists only on a measure-zero spectral fiber and does not produce an \(\ell^2\) obstruction. The exact formula is a finite-dimensional fiber reduction of classical spectral methods; it is not claimed as a new general theorem about all periodic graphs. The complete text of the most directly motivating 2026 manuscript could not be checked, so older or uninspected equivalent constructions remain a priority risk. Independent mathematical verification is not claimed.
