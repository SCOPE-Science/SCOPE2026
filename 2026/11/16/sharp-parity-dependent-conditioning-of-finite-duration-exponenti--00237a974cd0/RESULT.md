# Sharp parity-dependent conditioning of finite-duration exponential Gabor bases

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge2\) and \(\lambda>0\), the critical-lattice Gabor system generated by the finite-duration exponential \(g_{m,\lambda}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\mathbf{1}_{[0,m]}(t)\) is a Riesz basis for \(L^2(\mathbb R)\). Its optimal squared frame bounds are \(A_{m,\lambda}=e^{-2\lambda}\min_{|z|=r}|S_m(z)|^2\) and \(B_{m,\lambda}=((1-r^m)/(1-r))^2\), where \(r=e^{-\lambda}\) and \(S_m(z)=1+z+\cdots+z^{m-1}\).

When \(m\) is even, the entire sharp frame condition number is **independent of the duration**:
\[
A_{m,\lambda}=e^{-2\lambda}\left(\frac{1-r^m}{1+r}\right)^2,\qquad
\kappa_{m,\lambda}:=\frac{B_{m,\lambda}}{A_{m,\lambda}}
=e^{2\lambda}\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^2.
\]
The lower and upper extrema occur at respective Zak frequencies \(\xi=1/2\) and \(\xi=0\), and the lower-frequency minimizer is unique modulo integers.

At odd duration \(m=3\), a sharp localization transition occurs at \(r_*=2-\sqrt3\). Defining
\[
M_3(r)=\begin{cases}
(1-r+r^2)^2,&0<r\le2-\sqrt3,\\
\frac34(1-r^2)^2,&2-\sqrt3\le r<1,
\end{cases}
\]
the exact bounds are \(A_{3,\lambda}=e^{-2\lambda}M_3(r)\) and \(B_{3,\lambda}=(1+r+r^2)^2\). At \(r\le r_*\), the minimizing frequency is \(\xi=1/2\); at \(r>r_*\), there are exactly two conjugate minimizing frequencies, characterized by \(\cos(2\pi\xi)=-(1+r^2)/(4r)\).

For every **fixed** integer \(m\ge2\), the weak-damping condition number has the sharp limit
\[
\lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}\lambda^2\kappa_{m,\lambda}
=\begin{cases}4,&m\text{ even},\\4\cos^2(\pi/(2m)),&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus duration three has limiting coefficient \(3\), strictly less than the coefficient for every other fixed duration at least two. At zero damping \(\lambda=0\), no duration \(m\ge2\) produces a Gabor frame on the critical lattice.

## Assumptions and scope
The Hilbert space is complex \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) with Lebesgue measure. The unnormalized time-frequency atoms are \(e^{2\pi i\ell t}g_{m,\lambda}(t-k)\) for \(k,\ell\in\mathbb Z\). Bounds refer to \(A\|f\|_2^2\le\sum_{k,\ell}|\langle f,e^{2\pi i\ell\cdot}g(\cdot-k)\rangle|^2\le B\|f\|_2^2\); \(\kappa=B/A\) is the **frame-operator** condition number, not the synthesis-matrix singular-value ratio. The support endpoints are irrelevant up to null sets. The weak-damping limit fixes \(m\) before taking \(\lambda\downarrow0\); no uniformity as \(m\to\infty\) is claimed.

## Proof
**Step 1 (exact spectral reduction).** The unitary Zak transform is \(Zf(x,\xi)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}f(x+j)e^{-2\pi ij\xi}\), on \((x,\xi)\in[0,1)^2\). At the critical integer lattice the Gabor frame operator is multiplication by \(|Zg|^2\). For \(0<x<1\), exactly \(j=0,\ldots,m-1\) contribute, giving
\[
Zg_{m,\lambda}(x,\xi)=e^{-\lambda x}S_m(re^{-2\pi i\xi})
=e^{-\lambda x}\frac{1-r^m e^{-2\pi i m\xi}}{1-r e^{-2\pi i\xi}}.
\]
Since \(0<r<1\), neither numerator nor denominator vanishes on the frequency circle; continuity and compactness furnish a positive minimum and a finite maximum. Taking the essential extrema of \(e^{-2\lambda x}\) yields the stated exact Zak bound formula. The triangle inequality gives \(\max_{|z|=r}|S_m(z)|=S_m(r)=(1-r^m)/(1-r)\), with equality at frequency zero.

**Step 2 (the even-duration rigidity).** For even \(m\), for every \(\theta\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\left|\frac{1-r^m e^{im\theta}}{1-r e^{i\theta}}\right|
\ge\frac{1-r^m}{1+r}.
\]
The numerator bound is the reverse triangle inequality and the denominator bound is the triangle inequality. Both become equal at \(\theta=\pi\), since \(e^{im\pi}=1\) for even \(m\), and the denominator is \(1+r\). Equality in the denominator requires \(e^{i\theta}=-1\), proving uniqueness. Squaring and multiplying by the \(x\)-factor proves the even-length formula, with its cancellation of \((1-r^m)^2\) in \(B/A\).

**Step 3 (the complete three-cell transition).** For \(m=3\), writing \(c=\cos\theta\in[-1,1]\) gives the exact quadratic
\[
|1+r e^{i\theta}+r^2e^{2i\theta}|^2
=4r^2c^2+2r(1+r^2)c+1-r^2+r^4.
\]
Its vertex is \(c_*=-(1+r^2)/(4r)\), which lies in \([-1,1]\) exactly when \(r\ge2-\sqrt3\). Below this threshold, the minimum is at \(c=-1\), equal to \((1-r+r^2)^2\). Above it, completing the square gives the interior minimum \(3(1-r^2)^2/4\). The quadratic is strictly convex in \(c\), so these frequency locations and their multiplicities are exhaustive.

**Step 4 (sharp weak-damping limit for every fixed duration).** Put \(\theta_k=2\pi k/m\), \(1\le k<m\), the \(m-1\) zeros of \(S_m(e^{i\theta})\). As \(\lambda\downarrow0\), every minimizing frequency must approach this finite zero set: \(S_m(re^{i\theta})\to S_m(e^{i\theta})\) uniformly and its minimum tends to zero. Near such a zero, with \(\theta=\theta_k+u\) and \(u\to0\), the numerator of the rational expression satisfies
\[
|1-e^{-m\lambda+imu}|^2=m^2(\lambda^2+u^2)+o(\lambda^2+u^2),
\]
whereas the squared denominator tends to \(4\sin^2(\pi k/m)>0\). The competitor \(u=0\) shows that each minimizing offset is \(O(\lambda)\); any subsequential limit of \(u/\lambda\) then minimizes \(1+v^2\), hence is zero. Consequently
\[
\min_{|z|=e^{-\lambda}}|S_m(z)|^2
\sim\frac{m^2\lambda^2}{4\max_{1\le k<m}\sin^2(\pi k/m)}.
\]
The denominator's maximum is \(1\) for even \(m\) and \(\cos^2(\pi/(2m))\) for odd \(m\). Since \(B_{m,\lambda}\to m^2\) and \(e^{-2\lambda}\to1\), the displayed sharp limit follows. For odd \(m\ge3\), the coefficient \(4\cos^2(\pi/(2m))\) increases strictly with \(m\), starting at \(3\); every even duration has coefficient \(4\). Finally, when \(\lambda=0\), \(S_m(e^{i\theta})\) has roots on the unit circle, hence essential infimum zero; no lower frame bound exists.

The package checker completed 262 comparisons without assertion failure. Finite grids are supplementary numerical evidence, **not** a proof of global optimality or limiting behavior; those claims depend on Steps 1–4. Reproduce with `python3 CHECK.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
Pont, Gröchenig, Liehr, Shafkulovska and Taylor (first submitted August 5, 2026) characterize the Gabor frame set for continuous integrable totally positive windows. The present windows have a jump at their finite support boundary and their degree-dependent Zak polynomial is outside that continuous class; more importantly, the sharp length-parity condition numbers and damping coefficients are not consequences of a qualitative frame-set characterization. Christensen, Kim and Kim (2016) study continuous compactly supported windows and sufficient frame-set regions, not this discontinuous critically sampled exponential. Kloos and Stöckler (2013) study exponential B-splines, which are convolutions of first-order pieces, and their oversampled Zak zero geometry; the **unconvolved** duration-\(m\) cutoff here is a different family. Classical Zak diagonalization and the geometric-series identity themselves are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns the square critical lattice with unit time and frequency steps, integer durations \(m\ge2\), and real positive damping. No other lattice geometry, continuous duration, modified window, noninteger taper or simultaneous large-duration/weak-damping limit is classified. The all-duration exact condition-number formula is asserted only for even durations; among odd durations the complete finite-parameter formula is provided only for duration three. Literature search cannot exclude an older equivalent formulation under finite-polynomial, filter-bank or polyphase terminology; the 2026 motivating manuscript's complete PDF could not be inspected, only its abstract and introductory published excerpt. No independent mathematical validation has been carried out.
