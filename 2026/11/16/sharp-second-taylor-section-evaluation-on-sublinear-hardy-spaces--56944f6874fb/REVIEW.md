# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS (same-model).** The published outer-extremizer theorem [1] applies directly to the nonzero bounded sum of the first three Taylor coefficients. Passing to the analytic \(2/p\)-th power of a unit \(H^2\) root gives the exact four-term coefficient expansion. Parseval and the triangle inequality bound the full infinite-dimensional Hardy problem by the two-variable function \(F_t\); its interior maximum exists because \(t>2\), and its critical equations reduce by exact algebra to the quartic \(Q_t\). The quartic's coefficient signs have exactly one change and its endpoints have opposite signs, giving precisely one positive root. The root satisfies the real quadratic Schur–Cohn conditions; the normalized power is analytic and achieves the bound. Equality in both inequalities forces the claimed unique family. Exact rational and numerical supplemental checks were run through the attached `CHECK.py`. Continuous \(p\), infinitely many Taylor coefficients, and uniqueness are handled analytically, not inferred from finite tests.

## Originality

**PASS relative to searched and inspected evidence.** Queries for Taylor-section evaluation, three simultaneous Hardy coefficients, and mixed finite jets in published-finding corpus and scholarly search found the 2026 outer-function theorem, isolated-coefficient exact bounds, fixed-origin coefficient frontiers, and the related two-jet functional \(a_0+\tau a_2\), but no exact sharp \(a_0+a_1+a_2\) norm for all \(0<p<1\). The closest 2019 primary full text includes general Carathéodory–Fejér extremal structure but its explicitly evaluated functionals are isolated coefficients. That general structure does not determine the quartic or all equality cases here. The own current research ledger was searched for aliases and closest claims; its all-exponent second-jet finding again excludes \(a_1\). The main residual risk is undiscovered older equivalence in classical analytic extremal-functional literature, not direct support for a covering theorem from any inspected source.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Norms of finite Taylor-section evaluation are natural and finite-section truncations are central to Hardy coefficient estimates. The new result gives an exact closed algebraic characterization uniformly over the entire nonlocally convex exponent regime, and describes the unique optimizer rather than merely producing a lower bound or arbitrary numerical instance. The first three coefficients jointly present a genuinely coupled quadratic Hilbert-root optimization that is not an immediate specialization of isolated jet results. No general higher-section theorem is claimed or needed for the exact natural invariant.
