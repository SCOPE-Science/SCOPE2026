# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Invariance under coordinate sign flips and the parity decomposition reduce the full space of candidate degree-six harmonic polynomials to eight finite polynomial sectors. Their edge-vanishing ideals are exactly the coordinate-axis ideal. For six nontrivial parity sectors a displayed nonzero determinant proves injectivity; the triple-odd sector is zero by degree. The even sector has an explicit rank-nine certificate for ten basis vectors, and the known sextic spans the kernel. The standalone exact checker confirms every critical coefficient calculation, so no numerical sampling is mistaken for an all-degree proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence.** Vasilyev's arXiv:2506.24116 already constructs the sextic; this paper does not claim to rediscover it. Its theorem and remarks do not establish minimal degree six or uniqueness within that degree. The nearest published spherical-harmonic cube-vertex interpolation claims concern finite point evaluation, unlike whole-line vanishing. Older reflection-discriminant principles cover alternating homogeneous polynomials on reflecting hyperplanes, not all affine edge-vanishing harmonics. No inspected source implies the sharp unrestricted nonhomogeneous degree-onset theorem. Residual risk is older cubical ideal/classification literature not yet inspected in full.

**Value — PASS.** Wavre's cube-edge problem is a motivated classical existence question. Determining the **least degree** and **one-dimensional full optimizer space** measures the exact polynomial complexity of its first solution and proves its rigidity among all nonhomogeneous harmonic polynomials, including those without cube symmetry. The result is a natural sharp invariant, not an arbitrarily chosen coefficient slice.

**Limits.** No classification of higher-degree or nonpolynomial harmonic functions, and no independent priority check is claimed. Publication priority is provisional.
