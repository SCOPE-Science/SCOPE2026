# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The exact local joint-order probabilities for peaks and valleys use only orderings of at most five independent continuous labels. The resulting block-circulant covariance is exactly diagonalized into two bands. Both are monotone in cosine; the minus band has exactly one zero mode from the cyclic discrete-derivative identity. The threshold between constant-plus and first-nonzero-minus least eigenvalues occurs between lengths five and six. The asymptotic constant follows from elementary trigonometric expansions. The bundled standard-library program corroborates the proof by exhaustive exact covariance matrices at lengths five through eight, Fourier checks and a much wider finite extrema sweep; it is not treated as an infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS (priority provisional).** Fu's linear exterior-peak generating functions, Crane–DeSalvo–Elizalde's single-pattern overlap variance formula, and Hivert–Nechaev–Oshanin–Vasilyev's local-peak count statistics precede the present work. Importantly, Bandt (arXiv:2212.14386, Section IV) already gives the long-run six-pattern aggregate covariance for continuous i.i.d. observations, including turning-rate variance and the asymptotic peak/valley equality; those components are not claimed as new. An existing finite exact six-pattern count covariance theorem handles noncyclic **aggregate** count vectors, and a separate generalized ordinal-pattern covariance-nullspace result handles aggregate tie-aware patterns. These supply local moment identities and telescoping intuition, but do not state the full finite cyclic **position-resolved** peak/valley Fourier spectrum, its all-length nonzero mode structure, or the sharp conditioning ratio without additional construction and analysis. Searches did not identify an identical two-channel spectral theorem; an unindexed cyclic point-process specialization remains a priority risk.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The natural two-channel extrema observation problem has a topological conservation law and a previously unquantified near-null low-frequency contrast. A complete spectral classification, the sharp \(5/\pi^2\) quadratic conditioning law, and the contrast with uniformly bounded peak-only conditioning distinguish this from a cosmetic rewriting of a peak-count variance.

Limitations: Unrestricted uniform permutations, cycle length at least five, raw indicator covariance, and the positive-eigenspace condition number only. No independent priority certification or externally performed mathematical audit has been obtained.
