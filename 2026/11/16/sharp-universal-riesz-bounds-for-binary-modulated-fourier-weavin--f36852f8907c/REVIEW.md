# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The sole final claim is the exact sharp uniform two-modulation Fourier weaving theorem in `RESULT.md`. The cross Gram corner is bounded by the radius of an elementary unit-circle arc. On the opposite side, a one-sided shifted Hilbert matrix has limiting Rayleigh quotient \(\pi\) on finite power-law vectors, which certifies both sharp squared edges \(1\pm\sin(\pi|	heta|)\). Coercivity of the projected phase multiplication proves surjectivity in the positive regime. A holomorphic boundary witness proves incompleteness strictly above the half phase; regularized critical witnesses prove failure of every positive frame lower bound at equality. The proof fixes normalization, boundary phases and all quantified index sets. The independent numerical script completed 250 finite checks; these do not replace the infinite argument.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature

The 2026 motivating manuscript by Pai, Rocha and Yu states qualitative woven-completeness/frame results, including a Kadets example, and sufficient perturbation bounds. Cabrelli, Molter and Negreira (2024) provide general uniform central-compression criteria. Classical Kadets already establishes the qualitative subcritical cutoff after centering, so no originality is asserted for this consequence. The nontrivial claim is the **exact optimal uniform** \(1\pm\sin(\pi|	heta|)\) squared bound, with a one-sided Hilbert extremizing mechanism and sharp boundary conditioning. Search of equivalent all-subset binary shifts, partial Fourier compressions and Hilbert/Toeplitz corners found no equivalent indexed statement. Relevant periodic shifted-sinc research indexed results have different claims and parameters. Older quantitative Kadec perturbation results are the principal unresolved priority risk; lack of a search match alone is not a novelty certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Two individually orthonormal measurement banks need not remain uniformly well-conditioned after arbitrary indexwise switching. An explicit universal optimal conditioning formula, its extremizing partition and its phase-boundary scaling answer a natural quantitative stability question that general existence theorems do not settle numerically. The boundary witness also explains the sharpness of the qualitative transition without claiming that the transition itself is new.
