# The first cubic-resonance threshold for phase-only two-block Fourier polynomials

## Finding
Equip the circle \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\) with normalized Haar measure. For every integer \(m\ge3\) and every \(a_0,a_1,a_m,a_{m+1}\in\mathbb C\) of modulus one, define
\[
P_{m,a}(e^{it})=a_0+a_1e^{it}+a_me^{imt}+a_{m+1}e^{i(m+1)t},\qquad
M_{2k}(m,a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|P_{m,a}(e^{it})|^{2k}\,dt.
\]
The sharp *complete moment ranges* are
\[
\boxed{M_4(m,a)\in[20,36]\quad(m\ge3),}
\]
while the sixth moment detects the first spacing resonance:
\[
\boxed{M_6(3,a)\in[106,430],\qquad M_6(m,a)\in[112,400]\quad(m\ge4).}
\]
Every intermediate real value in each interval occurs, and all extrema are attained with binary phases. The exact phase classification below distinguishes a single normalized minimizer and maximizer for \(m=3\), but continuous families for all \(m\ge4\).

Normalize by a common unit scalar and a rotation of the circle so that the coefficients at frequencies zero and one are both one; write the remaining coefficients \(u=e^{i\alpha}\) and \(v=e^{i\beta}\). Put \(\phi=\alpha-\beta\). For \(m=3\), also put \(\psi=2\alpha-\beta\). Then the exact identities are
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_4&=28+8\cos\phi,\\
M_6(3,a)&=256+144\cos\phi+6(3+2\cos\phi)\cos\psi,\\
M_6(m,a)&=256+144\cos\phi\qquad(m\ge4).
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(m=3\), the sixth-moment minimum occurs exactly at \((u,v)=(1,-1)\), and the maximum exactly at \((u,v)=(1,1)\). For \(m\ge4\), the sixth-moment minimum occurs exactly when \(v=-u\), and the maximum exactly when \(v=u\), with arbitrary \(|u|=1\). The fourth-moment minimizing and maximizing conditions are respectively \(v=-u\) and \(v=u\) for every \(m\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The Fourier frequencies are exactly the four distinct integers \(\{0,1,m,m+1\}\), with \(m\ge3\); each coefficient has modulus *exactly* one, but is otherwise unrestricted complex. The circle is continuous, not a finite sampled grid. All reported moments are integrals of sixth or fourth powers, not their roots, and use probability-normalized integration. The two symmetries used in normalization are multiplication by a common unit scalar and rotation of the argument; no complex-conjugate or coefficient-reversal symmetry is silently imposed.

The study isolates the two-pair sparse phase-coded aperture: two adjacent tones separated by one integer gap. The sixth moment is the first even moment capable of seeing the extra cubic convolution collisions at separation \(m=3\), even though the entire fourth-moment range is independent of separation. This is a statement for the natural complete family of all such apertures with disjoint nonadjacent pairs, not a claim about all four-point Fourier supports or arbitrary coefficients.

## Proof
**Fourier normalization.** Given \(|a_j|=1\), multiplying by \(a_0^{-1}\) and rotating \(z\mapsto (a_0/a_1)z\) sets the coefficients at degrees zero and one equal to one. Thus it is sufficient, without loss, to examine
\[
P_m(z)=1+z+uz^m+vz^{m+1},\qquad |u|=|v|=1.
\]

**Convolution and the exact resonance threshold.** By orthogonality of circle characters,
\[
M_{2k}(m,a)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\big|[z^j]P_m(z)^k\big|^2.
\]
Each term of the third power has exponent \(mi+j\), where \(0\le i,j\le3\). If two such formal pairs produce the same exponent then \(m(i-i')=j'-j\), with \(|j'-j|\le3\). For \(m\ge4\), this forces \((i,j)=(i',j')\), so no *additional* cubic aliasing occurs beyond the formal two-variable convolution of \(1+x+uy+vxy\). At \(m=3\), the only additional possible aliases have \((i',j')=(i+1,j-3)\), i.e. collisions between \((i,3)\) and \((i+1,0)\) for \(i=0,1,2\). For the square, the same argument with \(0\le i,j\le2\) shows no additional aliases for every \(m\ge3\).

Expanding the second and third powers and pairing coefficient monomials with equal Fourier exponents gives exactly
\[
\begin{split}
M_4&=28+4(u\bar v+\bar u v),\\
M_6(m,a)&=256+72(u\bar v+\bar u v)\quad(m\ge4),\\
M_6(3,a)&=256+72(u\bar v+\bar u v)+3(u+\bar u)\\
&\quad+9(u^2\bar v+\bar u^2v)+3(u^3\bar v^2+\bar u^3v^2).
\end{split}
\]
These are exact integer Laurent identities; their complete monomial coefficients can be independently regenerated by the included verifier. Using \(u=e^{i\alpha}\), \(v=e^{i\beta}\) and \(\cos(\psi-\phi)+\cos(\psi+\phi)=2\cos\psi\cos\phi\) yields the stated real formulas.

**Sharp bounds and equality.** The fourth-moment and \(m\ge4\) sixth-moment claims follow immediately from \(-1\le\cos\phi\le1\). For \(m=3\), note that \(3+2\cos\phi\ge1>0\). The following *exact nonnegative deficit identities* hold:
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_6(3,a)-106&=132(1+\cos\phi)+6(3+2\cos\phi)(1+\cos\psi),\\
430-M_6(3,a)&=156(1-\cos\phi)+6(3+2\cos\phi)(1-\cos\psi).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both deficits are nonnegative and vanish exactly when \((\cos\phi,\cos\psi)=(-1,-1)\) and \((1,1)\), respectively. Since \(\alpha=\psi-\phi\), \(\beta=\psi-2\phi\) modulo \(2\pi\), these force exactly \((u,v)=(1,-1)\) and \((1,1)\). For \(m\ge4\), the corresponding deficits are \(144(1+\cos\phi)\) and \(144(1-\cos\phi)\), leaving one freely varying phase. All claimed extremal coefficient patterns are therefore necessary and sufficient. Finally, the phase torus \(\mathbb T^2\) is connected and the moment is continuous, so each full interval between its attained extrema is realized. This also proves the absence of any missing moment values.

## Verification
Run `python artifacts/verify.py` from the standalone extracted package. The standard-library script constructs the second and third convolution powers with three integer exponent coordinates, forms the exact integer Laurent coefficient dictionary of the moment, and compares it with the displayed formulas for all integers \(3\le m\le44\). It then checks another 160 fourth/sixth evaluations at Gaussian-integer phase points and the sharp equality examples, reporting `VERIFY_OK`.

The analytical argument using the bound \(|j'-j|\le3\) proves the formulas for *every* integer \(m\ge3\); the finite verifier is supplemental, not a substitute for that infinite-range step. No continuous-angle numerical optimization is used in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Pai, Liu, Li and Chen (2023, arXiv:2304.08583) construct sparse complementary pairs and study aperiodic auto- and cross-correlations, including sparse two-block configurations. Their explicit construction in Example 2 contains two adjacent nonzero pairs, but its theorems target paired autocorrelation cancellation and do not state the sharp *single-polynomial* sixth moments for this entire two-block family.

The published result *Exact sixth-moment flatness of four unimodular Fourier taps* treats the consecutive-frequency set \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), corresponding to the excluded overlapping-pair case \(m=2\), and yields a different, nonbinary sixth-moment minimizer. It neither implies the present integer-spacings \(m\ge3\) classification nor bounds these moments sharply. The published *First phase-frustrated low-multiplicity Fourier support occurs at six points* concerns fourth moments and progression-free supports; it cannot distinguish \(m=3\) from \(m\ge4\) at sixth order. The published *Sharp sixth-moment norm for one balanced triple-sum defect* optimizes a coefficient-\(\ell^2\)-normalized **maximum** for supports having exactly one special collision of size-three multisets; the present support is phase-only and has multiple formal rectangle relations and three additional cubic collisions at \(m=3\), so its hypothesis and extremal objective are different.

The general harmonic-analytic convolution identity is classical; the asserted contribution is the full phase-sensitive two-block classification, its sharp first resonance transition, all equality families, and the exact nonnegative rigidity deficits—not Parseval's identity itself.

## Limitations
This finite-tap result makes no claim for nonunit coefficient magnitudes, more than two blocks, nonadjacent tones within a block, or arbitrary even moments. It is not an asymptotic waveform performance claim. Although two 2023 primary texts and the closest publicly available mathematical findings were compared, older specialized sparse-sequence moment tables may contain equivalent constants, and historical priority has not been independently certified. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
