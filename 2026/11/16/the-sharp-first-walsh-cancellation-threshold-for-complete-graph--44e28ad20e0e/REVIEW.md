# Self-review

**Correctness: PASS.** The exact weighted matrix-tree identity reduces Fourier zeros to an ordinary graph-Laplacian eigenvalue. The critical bound \(\lambda_{\max}\le k+1\) for \(k\) negative edges is proved by connected-component sizes and the complementary Laplacian identity. At equality the component must be a tree with disconnected complement; a cut-edge counting argument identifies it uniquely as a star. Odd-order nonvanishing follows independently from parity and Cayley's count. The independently implemented small-order integer checkers agree on the threshold and extremizer counts; they are corroboration, not the infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS under bounded comparison.** Martin--Reiner and Duval--Klivans--Martin already provide the weighted tree enumerator and matrix-tree machinery, while Burton--Pemantle establishes much of the all-edge marginal determinant technology. These sources do not state the exact minimum-support vanishing character theorem or the star-only boundary. Earlier complete-graph Walsh nonzero-gap and invertibility claims concern a different minimum, not the support size of a zero multiplier. Searches for equivalent Laplacian eigenvalue and weighted-tree cancellation statements found no decisive covering source. The possibility of an implicit older reformulation remains a genuine historical-priority risk.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The minimum cardinality of a vanishing edge-parity character is an intrinsic obstruction scale in Fourier recovery on tree-incidence codes. Proving a sharp boundary, uniqueness up to labels, and an exact count at every even order is a structural result that does not follow from merely tabulating small Walsh spectra.

The proof is restricted to the first zero-support degree. Complete higher-degree Fourier nullity, sharp nonzero inverse norms and historical-priority exhaustion are not asserted.
