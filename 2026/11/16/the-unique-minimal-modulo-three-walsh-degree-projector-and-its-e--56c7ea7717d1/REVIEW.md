# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed result reduces the endpoint operator norm to the exact \(\ell^1\) norm of a Fourier projection kernel. The cubic roots-of-unity filter produces explicit kernel magnitudes by Hamming weight; the complete even/odd binomial sums establish both exact norm levels. The identity \(3B_n-A_n=2A_{n-1}\) proves strict optimality for every \(n\ge3\). The assertion that the kernel norm is the minimal projection norm follows by averaging any projection over the translation group, not by an unsupported norm estimate. Integer Walsh-transform replay independently checks low dimensions; it is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (bounded)

Tan's 2012 lecture notes provide the standard Walsh expansion but not modular-degree projection constants. Defant et al., arXiv:2302.00233, Theorem 3.1, provide the general mean-absolute-character-sum formula and are explicitly credited; Sections 3–4 concern arbitrary spectra and fixed-degree spaces, not the explicit three-residue formula or unique optimal class. A separate public note on modulo-three Walsh sampling concerns first-degree restriction Gram matrices rather than the norm of the projection onto all degrees in the residue. Recorded searches of equivalent residue, projection, and Krawtchouk formulations did not reveal the claimed exact two-level classification. Residual risk: an earlier equivalent roots-of-unity evaluation could exist under a different terminology; no complete historical-priority assertion is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The three modular Fourier spaces have dimensions differing by at most one, yet choosing the wrong residue raises the smallest possible projection norm by a strictly positive quantity growing exponentially with dimension. The theorem gives all exact norms, their unique design choice, and their sharp asymptotic ratio. This is a natural endpoint-stability invariant and a complete infinite-family classification, not a finite table, normalization restatement, or fixed-degree standard estimate.

## Limitations

Only the binary cube and degree residues modulo three are covered. Neither other residue moduli nor other selection constraints are classified. Earlier specialized literature may contain an equivalent calculation. The finite checker is not an independent audit.
