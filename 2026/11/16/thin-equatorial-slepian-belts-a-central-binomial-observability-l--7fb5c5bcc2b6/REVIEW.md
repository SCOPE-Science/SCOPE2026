# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claimed observability constant follows from orthogonal azimuthal decomposition and a proved shrinking-interval Rayleigh quotient lemma. Norm equivalence controls all rescaled polynomial coefficients, monic Legendre orthogonality fixes the unique limiting optimizer, and min–max separation establishes eventual simplicity. The theorem specifies that bandwidth is fixed and that all lower harmonic degrees are present. High-precision checks agree at five bandwidths, but the infinite statement rests on the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical-priority limitations.** The 2006 spherical polar-gap paper provides the geometric Slepian operator and a method of computing eigenpairs. The 2020 planetary application uses zonal concentration over a latitude belt. Neither inspected treatment states the fixed-degree shrinking-belt exponent with its exact central-binomial coefficient and scaled Gauss–Legendre nodal limit. The preceding operator formulas make the problem computable, but do not themselves give the stated singular asymptotic or optimizer limit. Earlier work on doubly orthogonal polynomials and small-interval concentration may contain an equivalent formulation; that remains a specific residual risk.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a motivated sharp observability limit for narrow latitude coverage, not an arbitrary numerical invariant. The exact power quantifies increasingly severe loss of stable recovery with harmonic cutoff, the constant removes normalization ambiguity, and the Gauss–Legendre root limit describes the geometry of the worst hidden mode. The all-degree family and eventual symmetry statement are structurally informative even though they do not solve the varying-bandwidth case.

## Scope and uncertainty
The result concerns a continuum belt at fixed cutoff and makes no uniform-in-bandwidth or sampled-data claim. The searched literature did not establish priority absolutely. The nodal statement concerns the minimizing eigenfunction for sufficiently thin belts, not every Slepian mode.
