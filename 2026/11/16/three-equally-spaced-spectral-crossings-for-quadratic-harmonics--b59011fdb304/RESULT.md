# Three equally spaced spectral crossings for quadratic harmonics on unbalanced spherical block caps

## Finding

Let \(p,q\ge3\) be **distinct integers**, put \(d=p+q\), and decompose \(\mathbb R^d=\mathbb R^p\oplus\mathbb R^q\). On the probability-normalized sphere \(\mathbb S^{d-1}\), write \(z=(u,v)\), \(T(z)=\|u\|^2\), and observe the block cap
\[
\Omega_t=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb S^{d-1}:\|u\|^2\ge t\},\qquad 0<t<1.
\]
Let \(\mathcal H_2\) be the complexification of the real homogeneous harmonic quadratic polynomials restricted to the sphere. The self-adjoint concentration operator \(K_t=P_{\mathcal H_2}\mathbf1_{\Omega_t}|_{\mathcal H_2}\) has the following **entire spectrum**, counting eigenspace dimensions:

| Sector | Eigenvalue | Multiplicity |
|---|---|---|
| radial \(R\) | \(\displaystyle\lambda_R=\frac{\mathbb E[(T-p/d)^2\mathbf1_{T\ge t}]}{\mathbb E[(T-p/d)^2]}\) | \(1\) |
| within \(u\), traceless \(U\) | \(\lambda_U=Q_{a+2,b}(t)\) | \(\frac{p(p+1)}2-1\) |
| mixed \(C\) | \(\lambda_C=Q_{a+1,b+1}(t)\) | \(pq\) |
| within \(v\), traceless \(V\) | \(\lambda_V=Q_{a,b+2}(t)\) | \(\frac{q(q+1)}2-1\) |

Here \(a=p/2\), \(b=q/2\), \(s=a+b=d/2\), \(T\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,b)\), and \(Q_{\alpha,\beta}(t)=B(\alpha,\beta)^{-1}\int_t^1 x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}\,dx\). In particular, every eigenvalue lies strictly between zero and one.

**Complete strict ordering and three crossings.** Set
\[
 t_- =\frac{p-2}{d},\qquad t_0=\frac pd,\qquad t_+=\frac{p+2}{d}.
\]
All three thresholds lie strictly between zero and one and are equally spaced by \(2/d\). The four eigenvalues are strictly ordered, from largest to smallest, as
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
0<t<t_-&U>C>V>R\\
t_-<t<t_0&U>C>R>V\\
t_0<t<t_+&U>R>C>V\\
t_+<t<1&R>U>C>V.
\end{array}
\]
At \(t_-\) only \(R=V\), at \(t_0\) only \(R=C\), and at \(t_+\) only \(R=U\), with the other inequalities strict. The respective coincident eigenspaces have dimensions \(q(q+1)/2\), \(pq+1\), and \(p(p+1)/2\). Therefore the least-observed mode switches at \(t_-\), and the most-observed mode switches at \(t_+\).

Writing \(\kappa(t)=\lambda_{\max}(K_t)/\lambda_{\min}(K_t)\) for the **squared-energy** condition ratio, its complete sharp formula is
\[
\kappa(t)=\begin{cases}
\lambda_U/\lambda_R,&0<t\le t_-,\\
\lambda_U/\lambda_V,&t_-\le t\le t_+,\\
\lambda_R/\lambda_V,&t_+\le t<1.
\end{cases}
\]
The endpoint formulas agree. In the thin-block-cap regime,
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{t\uparrow1}(1-t)^2\kappa(t)=\frac{q(q+2)(q+4)}{2pd}.}
\]
The result concerns **unbalanced** blocks \(p\ne q\) only; the balanced hypersurface trace problem has existing explicit spectral formulas, discussed below.

## Assumptions and scope

The sphere carries total measure one, as do the conditional angular measures on spheres within each block. All observation integrals use the **ambient spherical measure**, not normalized measure on \(\Omega_t\) and not intrinsic measure on the boundary product hypersurface. The harmonic degree is exactly two, not a low-pass band containing constants and linear harmonics. Coefficients may be complex; the same eigenvalues hold for real harmonics. All inequalities and coincidence claims are for every integer pair \(p,q\ge3\), \(p\ne q\), and every \(0<t<1\). The quotient \(\kappa\) is an eigenvalue ratio, whereas the singular-value condition number is \(\sqrt\kappa\). No finite point-sampling statement follows automatically.

## Proof

**1. Four complete orthogonal invariant sectors.** Every complex symmetric trace-zero \(d\times d\) matrix defines a harmonic quadratic \(z\mapsto z^TAz\). Relative to \(\mathbb R^p\oplus\mathbb R^q\), decompose these as (i) the radial line generated by \(T-p/d\); (ii) traceless symmetric forms only in \(u\); (iii) bilinear forms \(u^TBv\); (iv) traceless symmetric forms only in \(v\). Their dimensions are respectively \(1\), \(p(p+1)/2-1\), \(pq\), \(q(q+1)/2-1\), which add to \(d(d+1)/2-1=\dim\mathcal H_2\). The invariant groups \(O(p)\times O(q)\) and sign reflections of the blocks make these spaces mutually orthogonal both globally and on \(\Omega_t\). Within each sector, the conditional energy quotient is scalar: rotational invariance of the relevant traceless and bilinear forms reduces it to a single radial factor. Thus there are no further eigenvalue branches.

**2. Exact Beta-tail eigenvalues.** A uniform point on the sphere can be constructed by normalizing independent Gaussian vectors in the two coordinate blocks. Hence \(T\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,b)\), and conditional on \(T=x\) the normalized directions in the two blocks are independent and uniformly distributed on \(\mathbb S^{p-1}\) and \(\mathbb S^{q-1}\). The squared norm of a traceless \(u\)-quadratic is proportional to \(x^2\); for a mixed bilinear quadratic it is proportional to \(x(1-x)\); and for a traceless \(v\)-quadratic it is proportional to \((1-x)^2\). Dividing the cap integrals by their respective full-sphere integrals produces the three shifted-Beta tails displayed in the theorem.

The normalized radial quadratic is \(x-a/s\). Its full-sphere squared norm is \(\operatorname{Var}(T)=ab/[s^2(s+1)]\); this gives \(\lambda_R\). The normalization constants in all four branches make \(\lambda_J(0)=1\), \(\lambda_J(1)=0\). For an interior threshold both the cap and its complement contain open sets, and a nonzero quadratic cannot vanish on an open spherical set. Consequently each branch is in \((0,1)\).

**3. Exact factored differences.** Put \(G(t)=t^a(1-t)^b/B(a,b)>0\). The five necessary pairwise gap identities are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_R-\lambda_U&=\frac{s+1}{b(a+1)}G(t)\,[st-(a+1)],\\
\lambda_R-\lambda_C&=\frac{s+1}{ab}G(t)\,[st-a],\\
\lambda_R-\lambda_V&=\frac{s+1}{a(b+1)}G(t)\,[st-(a-1)],\\
\lambda_U-\lambda_C&=\frac{s(s+1)}{ab(a+1)}G(t)\,t,\\
\lambda_C-\lambda_V&=\frac{s(s+1)}{ab(b+1)}G(t)\,(1-t).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here is a direct universal proof. Write each normalized conditional squared-energy profile as a quadratic polynomial \(W_J(x)\) multiplying \(x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}/B(a,b)\). Every difference \(H=W_J-W_K\) has zero full-Beta mean. A quadratic \(H=h_0+h_1x+h_2x^2\) with that property is exactly the derivative of \(x^a(1-x)^b(A+Bx)\), divided by the unnormalized beta density, where \(A=h_0/a\) and \(B=-h_2/(s+1)\). Indeed differentiation yields \(h_0=aA\), \(h_1=(a+1)B-sA\), \(h_2=-(s+1)B\); the linear equality is forced by the zero-mean condition. Integrating from \(t\) to one gives \(-G(t)(A+Bt)\). Substituting each of the five explicit conditional profiles gives the identities above. The standalone symbolic check independently expands these polynomial equalities.

Since all prefactors are positive, the last two gaps show \(U>C>V\) throughout. The first three cross zero exactly at \((a-1)/s\), \(a/s\), and \((a+1)/s\), proving the four ordering regimes, all three unique coincidences, the two extremal switches, and the sharp condition-ratio formula. There can be no other crossing.

**4. Sharp thin-cap constant and trace consistency.** As \(t\uparrow1\), the Beta integral endpoint expansion gives
\[
\lambda_R\sim \frac{s+1}{aB(a,b)}(1-t)^b,\qquad
\lambda_V\sim\frac{s(s+1)}{b(b+1)(b+2)B(a,b)}(1-t)^{b+2}.
\]
Above \(t_+\), \(R\) and \(V\) are the extremal sectors, so their quotient yields \(b(b+1)(b+2)/(as)=q(q+2)(q+4)/(2pd)\), as asserted. The addition formula also implies the independent trace identity
\[\sum_{J\in\{R,U,C,V\}}m_J\lambda_J(t)=\left(\frac{d(d+1)}2-1\right)Q_{a,b}(t).\]
It follows either from the constant diagonal of the degree-two reproducing kernel or by summing the four normalized conditional profiles using the sector multiplicities.

## Relationship to prior work

Michel, Plattner and Seibert (2021) develop general spherical Slepian constructions and commuting operators for polar caps on \(\mathbb S^2\). Jaming and Speckbacher (2020) establish large-sieve concentration bounds for finite harmonic expansions on two-point homogeneous spaces. Neither inspected theorem states the exact four-sector unbalanced block-cap spectrum, its equally spaced crossings, or the sharp thin-cap ratio; these sources provide concentration motivation and general techniques.

A closely related published mathematical result, *Sharp quadrupole reconstruction from generalized Clifford hypersurfaces* (2026), computes the four restriction eigenvalues on a **single equal-block product hypersurface**, with normalized intrinsic trace measure, and optimizes over the hypersurface radius. It is directly applicable to balanced boundary shells, so **balanced blocks are excluded** from the new contribution. Integrating such shell formulas is a legitimate technique, but the cited theorem does not assert the unbalanced ambient-volume block-cap phase diagram or its exact three distinct crossings. Another result, *A sharp quadrupolar concentration-mode transition for antipodal caps in every sphere dimension* (2026), treats a **one-dimensional first block**, outside \(p\ge3\), and has three rather than four sectors. These two records, alongside classical Beta moments, are acknowledged as important antecedents rather than being relabelled as new results.

## Limitations

Novelty is restricted to the stated **unbalanced** all-dimensional block-cap spectral ordering, triple equally spaced crossings, and sharp stability law. The general Beta moment formulas, the finite-dimensional harmonic decomposition, the equal-block product-hypersurface trace spectrum, and the one-coordinate antipodal-cap spectrum are not claimed as new. Whether a broader unlocated general product-sphere harmonic restriction theorem implicitly entails the precise unbalanced cap ordering remains a historical-priority risk. No claims are made for unequal noninteger dimensions, other harmonic degrees, full low-pass bands, intrinsic boundary sampling, point samples, or optimization among arbitrary observation shapes.
