# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The all-dimensional assertion reduces to a pointwise cube-graph fact: if two corner-removal deficits are below the clipping depth, their binary addresses differ in one coordinate, so a triple is impossible. The pairwise overlap volume is exactly the elementary integral of a \((d-1)\)-simplex density; disjoint edge events yield the probability of two removals. The one-removal mass follows from the expected number of removed cube corners. Torus Parseval then identifies the Fourier-energy sum with the variance of the lattice coverage. The derivative jump and factorial upper limit follow by direct algebra. All quantifiers and boundary-null qualifications are explicit. Fifteen finite mesh checks corroborate, but are not invoked to prove, the general theorem.

## Originality: PASS, subject to historical priority
A directly related earlier planar result gives the dimension-two overlap distribution and Fourier energy but no theorem in dimensions three and higher. The new triangle-free cube-graph restriction and exact high-dimensional pair-volume integration yield all-dimensional structure and a smoothness order varying with dimension. Searches of relevant research records, alternate descriptions (corner-clipped hypercubes, lattice multicover, Fourier energy, polytopal periodization) and primary literature found no covering theorem that entails the stated distribution. Lev–Liu (2019) supplies polytope spectrality and Fourier-asymptotic obstructions, not this integer periodization; Lev–Matolcsi (2019) proves weak-tiling implications, not this geometric probability law. General polytope transform machinery and older specialized multicover classifications remain priority risks; the claim does not assert novelty for the Fourier transform identity or its planar case.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The result is a complete, exact probability law for every dimension in a natural nonseparable convex-polytope interpolation between a cube and its corner truncations. It reveals the unexpected dimension-free *number* of lattice multiplicity levels, a sharp universal onset depth, an exact factorial suppression in high dimension, and a dimension-dependent Fourier-energy differentiability transition. These are substantive geometric and harmonic-analytic invariants, not a single arbitrarily chosen parameter evaluation.

Independent historical exhaustiveness and third-party mathematical validation are not asserted. The paper-level formulation is not claimed as an independent contribution to spectral-set characterization; only the exact overlap/Fourier law is assessed.
