For three equally spaced Fourier tones with a phase-opposed center, Gaussian-window frequency intensity has an exact one-/two-/three-peak phase diagram, separated by the sharp curves \(a=2e^{-s/2}(1-s)\) and \(a=2e^{-s/2}\), where \(s\) is the squared frequency gap. The first transition splits the central peak with a square-root law; the second creates a central intensity peak from a fourth-order spectral null.
