# Two-facet Poisson covariance cones on the torus and graph-cycle singularities

## Finding
For fixed \(0<r,s<1\), put \(P_r(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}r^{|k|}e^{ikt}\), and for real \(a,b\) define the stationary real kernel on \(\mathbb T^2\) by
\[
F_{a,b}(x,y)=P_r(x)P_s(y)\bigl(1+a\cos(x+y)+b\cos(x-y)\bigr),
\qquad K_{a,b}(u,v)=F_{a,b}(u-v).
\]
Set \(U=r^{-1}s^{-1}+rs\) and \(V=r^{-1}s+rs^{-1}\), so \(U>V>0\). The kernel is positive semidefinite on **every finite collection** of points in \(\mathbb T^2\) if and only if
\[
2+aU+bV\ge0,\qquad 2+aV+bU\ge0. \tag{1}
\]
This is a sharp two-facet wedge in the full real \((a,b)\)-plane with unique vertex
\[
a=b=a_*= -\frac{2}{U+V}.
\]
On the wedge's relative interior all Fourier coefficients are strictly positive, and the kernel is \(C\)-universal on \(\mathbb T^2\). At any point on either boundary ray except the vertex, the kernel is strictly positive definite for every finite set of distinct points, but not \(C\)-universal. At the vertex, an arbitrary collection of \(E\) distinct sites has Gram nullity exactly
\[
\boxed{\dim\ker G=E-V_G+c_G}, \tag{2}
\]
where the associated bipartite graph has one vertex for each distinct first coordinate and each distinct second coordinate, one edge per site, \(V_G\) vertices altogether, and \(c_G\) connected components. In particular, all configurations of at most three distinct sites are nonsingular; a four-site Cartesian rectangle has a one-dimensional nullspace, generated by alternating corner coefficients.

## Assumptions and scope
The torus is \(\mathbb T^2=(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2\). The Fourier convention is \(F(x,y)=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb Z}\widehat F(m,n)e^{i(mx+ny)}\), with normalized Haar Fourier coefficients. Both \(r,s\) lie strictly between zero and one; \(a,b\) may be any real numbers, with no restriction to nonnegative physical-space modulation. Positive semidefiniteness means positivity of all finite Hermitian Gram matrices, allowing repeated sites; strict positive definiteness means positive definite Gram matrices at all pairwise distinct sites. \(C\)-universality means that the kernel's reproducing-kernel Hilbert space is dense in \(C(\mathbb T^2)\) in the uniform norm. All nullities are over \(\mathbb C\); the same nullities hold for real Gram matrices.

## Proof
**1. Fourier spectrum.** Write \(p_m=r^{|m|}\), \(q_n=s^{|n|}\). Uniform absolute convergence of the Poisson series and multiplication by the two cosines give, for every \((m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2\),
\[
\widehat F(m,n)=p_mq_n
+\tfrac a2\bigl(p_{m-1}q_{n-1}+p_{m+1}q_{n+1}\bigr)
+\tfrac b2\bigl(p_{m-1}q_{n+1}+p_{m+1}q_{n-1}\bigr). \tag{3}
\]
All baseline factors \(p_mq_n\) are strictly positive. Dividing (3) by that factor gives five exhaustive classes:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{indices}&\widehat F(m,n)/(p_mq_n)\\\hline
mn>0& H_+=1+(aU+bV)/2\\
mn<0& H_-=1+(aV+bU)/2\\
m=0,\ n\ne0& H_y=1+(a+b)r(s+s^{-1})/2\\
n=0,\ m\ne0& H_x=1+(a+b)s(r+r^{-1})/2\\
m=n=0& H_0=1+rs(a+b).
\end{array} \tag{4}
\]
(For the first two rows both coordinates are nonzero.) Compact-abelian Bochner positivity implies the necessity of \(H_+,H_-\ge0\). Conversely their sum gives
\[
a+b\ge -\frac4{U+V},\qquad U+V=(r+r^{-1})(s+s^{-1}). \tag{5}
\]
Using (5), the remaining three classes satisfy the strictly positive lower bounds
\[
H_y\ge\frac{1-r^2}{1+r^2},\qquad
H_x\ge\frac{1-s^2}{1+s^2},\qquad
H_0\ge1-\frac{4rs}{(r+r^{-1})(s+s^{-1})}>0. \tag{6}
\]
The last strict inequality follows from \(r+r^{-1}>2r\) and \(s+s^{-1}>2s\). Hence (1) is sufficient for all nonnegative Fourier coefficients and therefore for positive semidefiniteness. Since \(U-V=(r^{-1}-r)(s^{-1}-s)>0\), the two boundary lines meet only at the stated vertex.

**2. Universality and strictness away from the vertex.** On the open wedge both \(H_+\) and \(H_-\) are positive, so (4)-(6) show every Fourier coefficient is positive. A continuous translation-invariant kernel on a compact abelian group is \(C\)-universal precisely when it has positive Fourier coefficient at every character: positive coefficients make every character a member of the RKHS and trigonometric polynomials are uniformly dense; conversely a missing character cannot be approximated because its Fourier functional annihilates that RKHS.

At a nonvertex boundary point exactly one of \(H_+,H_-\) is zero, and the other is positive. Thus either **all** pairs \((m,n)\) with \(m,n>0\), or **all** pairs with \(m>0,n<0\), have positive Fourier coefficient. Let \((x_j,y_j)\) be finitely many distinct sites and suppose a coefficient vector has zero Gram quadratic form. Since the spectrum is nonnegative, its exponential sum vanishes for all characters in the positive quadrant. In the first case, fixing \(n\ge1\) and varying \(m=1,2,\ldots\), Vandermonde inversion over the distinct values of \(e^{ix_j}\) implies, for each first-coordinate group, \(\sum_{j:x_j=x}c_je^{iny_j}=0\). Varying \(n=1,2,\ldots\) and applying a second Vandermonde inversion within that group forces every \(c_j=0\). In the second case use \(n=-1,-2,\ldots\) instead. Thus every distinct-site Gram matrix is strictly positive definite. But one entire mixed Fourier quadrant vanishes, so such kernels are not \(C\)-universal.

**3. Exact graph nullity at the vertex.** Setting \(a=b=a_*\) makes \(H_+=H_-=0\). By (6), the axial and constant coefficients remain positive. Therefore the only supported Fourier characters are exactly the coordinate axes, and there is an additive decomposition
\[
F_{a_*,a_*}(x,y)=H_0+H_x(P_r(x)-1)+H_y(P_s(y)-1), \tag{7}
\]
with \(H_x=(1-s^2)/(1+s^2)\), \(H_y=(1-r^2)/(1+r^2)\), and \(H_0=1+2rs a_*\).

For a finite site set, the nonnegative Fourier-energy expansion gives
\[
c^*Gc=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb Z}\widehat F(m,n)
\left|\sum_j c_je^{i(mx_j+ny_j)}\right|^2. \tag{8}
\]
At the vertex, its zero set consists exactly of coefficient vectors satisfying all the first-coordinate and second-coordinate grouped-sum constraints
\[
\sum_{j:x_j=x}c_j=0\quad\text{for every occurring }x,
\qquad
\sum_{j:y_j=y}c_j=0\quad\text{for every occurring }y. \tag{9}
\]
Indeed the positive-axis Fourier modes imply (9) by finite Vandermonde inversion; conversely (9) annihilates all supported axial and constant modes. These are precisely the equations of the unoriented vertex-edge incidence matrix of the bipartite graph of the site set. Changing all signs in the second vertex class converts that matrix to an oriented incidence matrix. Each connected component contributes one row relation and no others, so its rank is \(V_G-c_G\). Rank-nullity yields (2). A simple bipartite graph has no cycles of length below four, which gives the last two assertions.

## Verification
The included standalone `checker.py` uses exact rational arithmetic to compare the coefficient formula (3) against all five classes in (4) for 1,452 combinations of rational parameters and frequencies. It checks the vertex axial values and exact rational Gram ranks and nonnegative eigenvalues for three complete \(2\)-by-\(3\) coordinate grids, each with nullity two. Reproduce by executing `python checker.py` with Python 3 and SymPy. These finite checks are supplements; the proof above establishes the assertions for all real parameters in the given range and all finite site sets.

For a concrete example \(r=1/2\) and \(s=1/3\), one has \(U=37/6\), \(V=13/6\), and the vertex is \(a=b=-6/25\). At that vertex the nonzero axial multipliers are \(H_x=4/5\), \(H_y=3/5\), and \(H_0=23/25\). The four sites \((0,0),(0,\pi),(\pi,0),(\pi,\pi)\) form a rectangle and have exactly one Gram null direction, with alternating signs.

## Relationship to prior work
Bochner's theorem for compact abelian groups provides the coefficientwise positivity criterion used in the proof. Guella, Menegatto and Peron (2015), Theorem 5.1, give a general Fourier-support criterion for strict positive definiteness of torus kernels, including nonseparately-isotropic complex-circle variants. Their theorem supplies an alternative route to the qualitative strictness conclusion once the present support is known; strictness alone is **not** claimed to be new. Said, Da Costa, Steinert and Mostajeran (2026) supply a general compact-group universality criterion; the \(C\)-universality step is likewise an application of an established principle. The specific two-parameter sharp wedge, its vertex decomposition and its exact arbitrary-configuration graph-nullity law are the classified content here. The original papers do not state those parameter and incidence classifications in the inspected relevant material.

## Limitations
The assertion is restricted to the product Poisson baseline, the two lowest mixed harmonics and \(0<r,s<1\); it does not classify all toroidal covariance modulations. Exact spectral support alone does not guarantee universality on the boundary. An equivalent classification might exist in specialized stationary random-field or toroidal covariance literature not located in the comparisons. No independent mathematical validation is represented; the review is same-model only. The finite checker does not certify the infinite theorem.
