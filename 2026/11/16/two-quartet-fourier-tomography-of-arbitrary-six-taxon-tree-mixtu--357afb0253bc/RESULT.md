# Two-quartet Fourier tomography of arbitrary six-taxon tree mixtures

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal T_6\) denote the \(105\) unrooted binary phylogenetic trees whose six leaves are labelled \(0,1,2,3,4,5\), with internal vertices unlabelled. Let \(\mathcal Q_6\) be the \(45\) resolved quartet queries \(ab\mid cd\), with four distinct leaves and with pairs unordered. Put \(X_q(T)=1\) if the paths connecting \(a\) to \(b\) and \(c\) to \(d\) in \(T\) are disjoint, and put \(X_q(T)=0\) otherwise. The binary Fourier coordinate is \(\chi_q(T)=1-2X_q(T)\). For \(d\ge0\), define \(V_d\) to be the real linear span, as functions on \(\mathcal T_6\), of all products of at most \(d\) such Fourier coordinates (with the empty product \(1\)).

**Theorem.** The exact sampling-rank hierarchy is
\[
\boxed{\dim V_0=1,\qquad \dim V_1=31,\qquad \dim V_2=105.}
\]
Thus the \(45\) individual quartet-resolution marginal probabilities determine only a \(31\)-dimensional space of functionals on arbitrary tree laws, leaving a \(74\)-dimensional signed ambiguity. By contrast, all pairwise joint quartet-resolution probabilities determine **every** probability distribution on the \(105\) trees uniquely. The unique degree-one linear relations, up to linear combination, are the \(15\) obvious three-resolution partition equations.

The degree-one failure has an explicit four-tree witness. Write each tree by its three nontrivial leaf splits; each pair below is mixed with equal weights:

- Mixture \(P\): the split systems \(\{045\mid123,05\mid1234,12\mid0345\}\) and \(\{045\mid123,04\mid1235,13\mid0245\}\).
- Mixture \(Q\): the split systems \(\{045\mid123,04\mid1235,12\mid0345\}\) and \(\{045\mid123,05\mid1234,13\mid0245\}\).

These distributions have the same marginal for each of the \(45\) resolved quartet events. Yet the joint event \(X_{02\mid13}=X_{04\mid15}=1\) has probability \(1/2\) under \(P\) and \(0\) under \(Q\).

## Assumptions and scope

All trees are **unrooted**, **fully binary**, and have the six specified labelled leaves; internal vertices and edge lengths are not observed. A resolved quartet query is a binary yes/no test of the specified four-leaf split, not a DNA-state pattern or a stochastic gene-evolution parameter. An arbitrary tree law is any probability distribution on all \(105\) labelled topologies, with no sparsity, uniformity, Markov, or independence assumption. The rank is over \(\mathbb R\) or equivalently \(\mathbb Q\); products of up to two Boolean characters and products of up to two corresponding \(0/1\) indicators span the same space by \(\chi_q=1-2X_q\).

## Proof

**Complete tree and query universes.** Starting from the three-leaf star, obtain every tree on \(k+1\) labelled leaves by inserting the new leaf into an edge of a tree on \(k\) leaves. Every target tree has a unique predecessor after deleting that new leaf and suppressing its degree-two neighbor. A tree with \(k\) leaves has \(2k-3\) edges, giving exactly \(3\cdot5\cdot7=105\) six-leaf trees. There are \(3\binom64=45\) quartet queries. Each tree resolves each four-leaf subset in exactly one of the three possible ways.

**Degree-one upper bound and its exact attainment.** For each of the \(15\) four-leaf sets \(\{a,b,c,d\}\),
\[
X_{ab\mid cd}+X_{ac\mid bd}+X_{ad\mid bc}=1.
\]
These \(15\) equations are independent as linear relations among the \(46\) formal constant and singleton columns: the three nonconstant columns of different four-leaf sets are disjoint. Consequently \(\dim V_1\le46-15=31\). Conversely, the supplied `CERTIFICATE.json` specifies \(31\) singleton-or-constant columns and \(31\) tree rows whose integer incidence submatrix has determinant \(243\pmod{1000003}\). This is nonzero, so its integer determinant is nonzero and \(\dim V_1\ge31\). It follows that \(\dim V_1=31\), and dimension counting shows that all degree-one dependencies are generated by the three-resolution equations.

**Degree-two full recovery.** Order all tree incidence vectors lexicographically, and order queries by increasing four-leaf set and then by the three canonical pairings \(ab\mid cd\), \(ac\mid bd\), \(ad\mid bc\) for \(a<b<c<d\). The supplied certificate specifies \(105\) distinct degree-at-most-two incidence monomials. Their \(105\)-by-\(105\) integer evaluation matrix on all \(105\) trees has determinant
\[
\det M\equiv582830\not\equiv0\pmod{1000003}.
\]
The finite-field nonzero determinant is a rigorous nonvanishing certificate for the original integer matrix. Hence \(\dim V_2\ge105\), and equality follows since functions on \(\mathcal T_6\) have dimension \(105\). The equivalent Fourier-character products also span the full function space. Therefore the observations evaluate every coordinate functional on an arbitrary signed distribution and, in particular, identify every probability law.

**Explicit failure of first-order identifiability.** The four split systems in the finding form two pairs of distinct valid binary trees. For every quartet query, the sum of its indicator values over the first pair equals its sum over the second pair. The two equally weighted tree laws are therefore indistinguishable from single-quartet marginal probabilities. The two indicated quartet queries jointly occur in exactly one tree of the first pair and in no tree of the second pair, establishing the probabilities \(1/2\) and \(0\). The full first-order nullspace has dimension \(105-31=74\); since the constant function is sampled, every signed vector in this nullspace has total mass zero, and sufficiently small perturbations of the uniform law also produce genuine probability-law ambiguities.

## Verification

The `CERTIFICATE.json` file stores the deterministic query ordering, selected row and column indices, both modular determinant residues, a hash of the sorted \(105\)-by-\(45\) incidence matrix, and the explicit four-tree witness. Run `python3 verify_insertions.py` and `python3 verify_splits.py` in the same directory as the certificate; neither script requires third-party libraries. The first re-enumerates trees by edge insertion, defines each quartet by disjoint leaf-to-leaf paths, and recomputes both certificate determinants and the mixture witness. The second independently enumerates all maximal compatible nontrivial split systems and determines quartet relations by their separating splits. It compares the complete incidence hash and independently obtains ranks \(31\) and \(105\) modulo the different prime \(1000033\). Both checks ran successfully.

The exhaustive proof concerns exactly the fully specified finite tree class; the modular determinants are exact certificates, not numerical approximations or extrapolations.

## Relationship to prior work

Grünewald, Humphries and Semple study compatibility and the number of quartets needed to identify a **single** phylogenetic tree, not the linear span of marginal and joint quartet indicators on all tree topologies. Matsen, Mossel and Steel analyze phylogenetic mixture models, including pair-of-tree identifiability based on restrictions and Fourier methods applied to sequence-site distributions; neither their two-tree theorem nor their polytope dimensions computes unrestricted six-taxon quartet-marginal tomography. Allman and Rhodes treat identifiability under parametrized molecular evolution models with limited classes, not an arbitrary categorical law on \(105\) tree shapes.

Published seven-taxon binary quartet-shattering results determine VC/Littlestone dimensions and shattering-support counts. Those are combinatorial trace properties of fixed query sets; they neither imply nor explicitly state the rank of all degree-two functions on the six-taxon tree class. The present finite-rank result is not a restatement of single-tree quartet identification or Boolean shattering. Published and indexed records have been compared under those distinctions; an unindexed equivalent rank calculation remains a historical-priority risk.

## Limitations

No rank formula for seven or more taxa, no exact spectral condition number for noisy inversion, and no inference guarantee under a mutation model are claimed. Repeated quartet observations on the **same** tree under a shared latent topology are essential; separately sampled independent gene trees would not supply these joint moments. The rank certificates establish mathematical correctness of this finite claim but are not an independent mathematical audit or proof of historical priority.
