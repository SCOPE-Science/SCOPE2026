# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The full-domain theorem follows from finite character orthogonality over image subspaces: the three subspace incidence sums recover the cumulative rank-zero, rank-one, and rank-two Fourier coefficient exactly. Gaussian-binomial simplification produces an integer-valued expression. The corank-zero, corank-one, corank-two, and all larger-corank cases are handled by explicit inequalities; the exceptional binary square factorization changes sign precisely at dimension five and has no integer zero. Fourier inversion plus exact rank-shell multiplicities gives the sharp endpoint norm and additive-convolution invertibility. Direct Walsh and ternary character enumeration validates small cases independently of the subspace-incidence derivation. The proof treats all prime powers without assuming prime order.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification
Delsarte (1978) derives the individual rank-relation character matrix, not the sign classification and sharp norm for their cumulative radius-two sum. Gadouleau–Yan (2007) give equivalent generalized Krawtchouk data. Brouwer et al. (2017) study individual rank-distance eigenvalue signs and extremal values, not these cumulative signs or the binary square exception. The present theorem is a nontrivial parameter-uniform consequence of earlier formulas, so those formulas and the rank counts are not claimed as discoveries. An unlocated earlier specialization remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The rank-at-most-two matrix set is the first nonlinear secant rank ball of the Segre determinantal variety and the first nontrivial cumulative ball in the bilinear-forms association scheme. Zero-freeness determines invertibility of its convolution operator, while the extra negative corank-two orbit for binary square matrices gives a sharp, structurally motivated boundary in its projection norm. The compact exact norm formula avoids an absolute-value sum over every rank and is valid in a uniform infinite family. This is more information than a restatement of the classical character table.
