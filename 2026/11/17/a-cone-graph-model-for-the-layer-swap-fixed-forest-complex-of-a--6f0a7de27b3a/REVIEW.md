# Scientific review: cone-graph layer-swap fixed forest complex

## Correctness — PASS

For every finite nonempty simple graph, the geometric fixed-point orbit complex is identified via explicit barycentric coordinates. An invariant edge selection is indexed uniquely by a set of base graph edges and a set of diagonal matching edges. If base edges contain a cycle, the double cover contains a lifted cycle (one doubled cycle for odd length, two copied cycles for even length). Otherwise the chosen base edges form a forest whose bipartite double cover has exactly two lifted trees per component. Each diagonal edge joins the corresponding two trees, with a cycle appearing exactly upon choosing a second diagonal within the same component. The same condition characterizes the forest edge sets of the graph cone. The orbit vertex bijection is therefore a simplicial isomorphism, compatible with all base-graph automorphisms. The no-coloop complete-graph corollary uses known matroid shellability and makes no new shellability claim. A standalone exact verifier checks 49,302 orbit-edge selections on 82 finite graphs; it is corroboration, not an all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected material

The complete text of Ardila–Castillo–Samper's 2014 *The topology of the external activity complex of a matroid* supplies a classical shellability benchmark and the known Tutte evaluation for independence complexes, but not a geometric fixed-space identification. The complete publicly available result on the full integral automorphism module of the \(K_{2,n}\) forest complex concerns an integral homology module of a different biclique family, not the involution-fixed forest complex of \(K_{n,n}\) or the all-graph natural cone identification. The full result on layer-exchange fixed points of matching complexes of arbitrary graph prisms identifies a different graph construction and a *matching* complex, with an orbit condition forbidding shared endpoints rather than cycles. Neither stronger or equivalent statement was found in targeted searches for cone forests, bipartite double covers, signed-graphic and rooted-forest formulations. Search non-detection is not definitive proof of priority. Kook's previously published homology of coned-graph graphic matroids is an important overlapping area, but an ordinary homology description does not imply an explicit PL homeomorphism for a different geometric fixed-point construction. Equivalent unindexed results remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A single natural fixed-point model identifies geometric fixed spaces of an entire graph-cover family with familiar, independently motivated graphic-matroid complexes, for *every* finite graph and equivariantly for every automorphism of the base. The cycle criterion is a structural lemma comparing lifted trees and rooted forests, not a finite lookup or mere relabeling. It transfers the cone's integral homotopy invariants and the full residual symmetry action to a fixed space that cannot be deduced from the ambient nonequivariant homotopy type or ordinary homology characters. It includes a uniform nontrivial infinite complete-bipartite example. The classical sphere counts are explicitly attributed to prior work.

## Closest literature and limitations

Ardila–Castillo–Samper, arXiv:1410.3870 (2014), matroid independence complexes and shellability. Public October/November 2026 papers on \(K_{2,n}\) integral forest modules and graph-prism matching fixed spaces were compared using their complete results. The theorem is confined to the diagonal-augmented bipartite double cover with specified layer swap; it is not a theorem about arbitrary graph coverings, all automorphism subgroup fixed loci, matching complexes, or new Tutte calculations. Independent mathematical validation and definitive literature priority assessment are outstanding.
