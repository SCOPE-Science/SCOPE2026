# A core-crossing law that determines the shape of a flat Möbius band

## Finding

Let \(M\) be a smooth, compact, connected intrinsic flat Riemannian Möbius band with nonempty totally geodesic boundary. Such a band has a unique core closed geodesic \(C\) whose homotopy class generates the fundamental group; write \(L>0\) for its length and \(2h>0\) for the transverse width of the band. Choose \(X,Y\) independently according to normalized Riemannian area, and let \(\gamma_{XY}\) be their length-minimizing geodesic, unique with probability one. Put \(w=2h/L\).

Then the core-intersection probability is exactly
\[
P_M=\Pr(\gamma_{XY}\cap C\ne\varnothing)=
\begin{cases}
\frac12-\frac{w^2}{8},&0<w\le1,\\[2pt]
\frac{4\log w+3}{8w^2},&w\ge1.
\end{cases}
\]
It decreases strictly and continuously from \(1/2\) to \(0\) as \(w\) increases from \(0\) to infinity. Consequently this *single probability* determines the similarity class of \(M\); if its area is also prescribed, it determines the full isometry class. Moreover, as a function of \(w\), \(P_M\) is twice continuously differentiable but not three times continuously differentiable at \(w=1\): its right third derivative minus its left third derivative equals \(4\).

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is intrinsic: the band need not possess a smooth isometric embedding in \(\mathbb R^3\). Flat means sectional/Gaussian curvature identically zero. Totally geodesic boundary means that the boundary's geodesic curvature vanishes. Any such Möbius band has a rectangular strip presentation
\[
M_{L,h}=([0,L]\times[-h,h])\big/\big((0,y)\sim(L,-y)\big)
\]
with the quotient of the Euclidean metric. Its area is \(2Lh\), its core is the image of \(y=0\), and its width is \(2h\). The dimensionless ratio \(w=2h/L\) is intrinsic. The event concerns the unique *globally minimizing* path between two area-uniform points, not an arbitrary geodesic and not its Euclidean chord in an embedding.

Ties between two or more minimizing paths occur only on a measure-zero set of endpoint pairs. Both endpoints lying on the core or on the boundary also have probability zero. Thus \(P_M\) is well defined without tie-breaking conventions.

## Proof

**1. Classification of the admissible metric.** Doubling \(M\) across its totally geodesic boundary extends the flat metric smoothly and produces a closed flat Klein bottle. The universal covering surface is the Euclidean plane. The lifts of the doubled boundary are pairwise disjoint complete straight lines, hence parallel lines. A connected lift of the original band is one of the strips between adjacent such lines. The cyclic deck group of this lift interchanges its two boundary lines and, since the quotient is nonorientable, is generated by a glide reflection. After an Euclidean coordinate change the glide is \((x,y)\mapsto(x+L,-y)\) on \(\mathbb R\times[-h,h]\), for some \(L,h>0\). This proves the stated presentation and also identifies the unique one-sided core \(y=0\). The metric classification is standard; the calculation below concerns a different probabilistic observable.

**2. The two possible shortest-path lifts.** Represent the independently uniform points by \((x_1,y_1)\), \((x_2,y_2)\) in the fundamental rectangle. Define \(t=|x_1-x_2|/L\in[0,1]\) and normalized transverse coordinates \(u=y_1/L\), \(v=y_2/L\). The Euclidean squared lengths divided by \(L^2\) of the shortest lifts in the two deck-parity classes are
\[
E=t^2+(u-v)^2,\qquad O=(1-t)^2+(u+v)^2.
\]
All other even deck translates have at least as great a horizontal displacement as \(t\); all other odd translates have at least as great a horizontal displacement as \(1-t\). Because the covering strip is convex, their straight joining segments project to admissible geodesics. Thus the globally minimizing geodesic uses the odd lift if and only if
\[
t>\frac12+2uv.
\]
A straight segment joining endpoints of transverse heights \(u\) and \(v\) crosses \(y=0\) precisely when \(uv<0\); the odd lift joins heights \(u\) and \(-v\), so it crosses precisely when \(uv>0\). In particular the crossing event is not determined by the distance \(\sqrt{\min(E,O)}\) alone.

**3. Elementary integration over the product of heights.** The variable \(t\) has density \(2(1-t)\) on \([0,1]\). The variables \(u,v\) are independent uniforms on \([-w/2,w/2]\). Define \(a=w^2/4\) and \(q=|uv|\). The sign of \(uv\) is fair and independent of \(q\), while \(q\) has density
\[
f_q(s)=\frac1a\log\frac as,\quad 0<s<a.
\]
For \(0<s<1/4\), conditioned on \(uv=s>0\), a core crossing requires the odd lift; its probability is \((\frac12-2s)^2\). Conditioned on \(uv=-s<0\), a crossing requires the even lift, of probability \(1-(\frac12+2s)^2\). Their sum is exactly \(1-4s\). For \(s\ge1/4\), both crossing probabilities vanish: in the positive-sign case the odd lift can never be shortest, and in the negative-sign case the even lift can never be shortest. Averaging the two signs gives the exact integral
\[
P_M=\frac1{2a}\int_0^{\min(a,1/4)}(1-4s)\log\frac as\,ds.
\]
The integral is proper as an improper integral, since \(s\mapsto\log(1/s)\) is locally integrable at zero. For \(a\le1/4\), evaluate using \(\int_0^a\log(a/s)\,ds=a\) and \(\int_0^a s\log(a/s)\,ds=a^2/4\):
\[
P_M=\frac12-\frac a2.
\]
For \(a\ge1/4\), use the same primitives at the fixed cutoff \(1/4\):
\[
P_M=\frac{2\log(4a)+3}{32a}.
\]
Substituting \(a=w^2/4\) yields the two stated formulas, with equality of their endpoint values \(P_M=3/8\) at \(w=1\).

**4. Strict inversion and sharp smoothness boundary.** Differentiation on each branch gives
\[
P_M'(w)=\begin{cases}-w/4,&0<w<1,\\-(4\log w+1)/(4w^3),&w>1.\end{cases}
\]
Both are strictly negative. The limiting values are \(\lim_{w\downarrow0}P_M=1/2\) and \(\lim_{w\to\infty}P_M=0\). Therefore the map \(w\mapsto P_M\) is a bijection \((0,\infty)\to(0,1/2)\), proving the asserted scale-free rigidity. The area identity \(\operatorname{area}(M)=L^2w\) then recovers \(L\) and \(h\) from \(P_M\) and area. The one-sided first derivatives at \(1\) both equal \(-1/4\), the second derivatives both equal \(-1/4\), but the left and right third derivatives are respectively \(0\) and \(4\). Hence \(P_M\) is \(C^2\) but not \(C^3\) at the sharp critical aspect ratio.

## Verification

A standalone script `verify_core_crossing.py` computes the displayed one-dimensional integral by independent numerical quadrature, enumerates the competing lifted Euclidean squared distances for random point pairs in fundamental rectangles, and compares the crossing frequency with the proposed closed formula. With NumPy and SciPy installed, run `python verify_core_crossing.py`. The calculation validates the formula at both branches, their matching point, and disparate aspect ratios. Monte Carlo checks are empirical corroboration only; the integral evaluation and derivative analysis above provide the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The flat Möbius band appears in Croke and Herreros' analysis of lens rigidity, and the extrinsic behavior of developable Möbius strips is discussed by Naokawa. Florit and Ziller discuss the structure of flat twisted cylinders with totally geodesic boundary. These treatments do not state the area-uniform shortest-geodesic core-crossing probability or its one-scalar inverse-shape law. In particular, results about the *marginal distribution of geodesic distance* do not directly specify which minimizing paths intersect the distinguished core. No novelty is claimed for the flat-strip classification, the deck-transformation distance formula, or elementary logarithmic-product integration separately.

## Limitations

The theorem applies to compact flat Möbius bands with totally geodesic boundary, not arbitrarily bent or curved bands, general nonflat Möbius metrics, or arbitrary embeddings into Euclidean three-space. The intersection event is almost sure, because on the null tie set the choice of a shortest geodesic could matter. The theorem proves an exact probability and its strict inversion, not a distribution of intersection angles or a general lens-data rigidity theorem. Historical priority for the combined probability-and-rigidity claim has not been independently established.
