# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The single final claim is the three-regime perimeter deficit theorem for equiangular radial polygons of the planar unit superellipse. The local graph normal form yields the exact variance cell; tangent oscillation bounds prove uniform tail summability. The borderline curvature square singularity produces coefficient \(1/12\) after all four axes, while the integrable regime gives a curvature integral. The congruence phases follow from the positions of the four axial angles. The \(p=2\) circle check and 44 numerical consistency checks corroborate the proof. Computations are not infinite verification.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority
Full mathematical comparison of Fodor–Minuță Theorem 5.5 and the published same-polygon Hausdorff theorem establishes a different non-implied invariant: a sum of arc-to-chord deficits, with a different critical exponent and three rather than two arithmetic profiles. Rodríguez–Montilla provide a series representation of the continuous curve perimeter according to the abstract, but the complete paper was not obtained. A specialized singular chordal quadrature result could still cover part or all of the claim; priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The sharp \(p=3/2\) integrability boundary for curvature-squared perimeter error, exact positive phase series, and nontrivial arithmetic behavior answer a natural discretization question left beyond qualitative polygon convergence. The result gives quantitative information for a standard convex-curve sampling algorithm, including a strict separation between maximum geometric gap and accumulated perimeter error.

## Scope and limitations
Fixed \(p>1\), unit semiaxes, fixed angular phase, and increasing number of samples only; no best approximation, no metric-projection convergence rate, and no uniform parameter crossover. The full primary source relevant to continuous perimeter representations was not available. Independent audit has not been conducted.
