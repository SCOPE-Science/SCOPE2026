Angular-root polygons inscribed in a superelliptic disk exhibit a sharp perimeter-loss threshold at \(p=3/2\): a phase-dependent \(n^{-(2p-1)}\) law below it, an \(n^{-2}\log n\) law at it, and a curvature-integral \(n^{-2}\) law above it. Below the threshold, the leading coefficient follows three congruence classes modulo four, which are genuinely distinct when \(p>1\) is sufficiently close to one.
