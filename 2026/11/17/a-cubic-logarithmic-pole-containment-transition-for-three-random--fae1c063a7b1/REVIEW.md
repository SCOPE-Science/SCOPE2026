# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is for three uniform surface points of a cap on the unit two-sphere. Writing their heights as independent uniform variables and azimuths as independent uniform angles is exact. The positive-cone condition reduces to one signed cotangent inequality on an angular simplex. The unconditional simplex density is \(1/(2\pi^2)\), which gives the exact loss integral (1). On the region where all sine-gap coefficients dominate the southern width, translating the three height variables produces a simplex with exact volume \(h^3 A^3/(6a_1a_2a_3)\). Its three edge singularities each integrate to \(8/3\) times the logarithmic scale, yielding \(4/\pi^2\) after angular normalization. Thin-boundary estimates exclude any additional leading contribution. Partitioning the local simplex volume by height sign gives the coefficient split \(1:1/3\) for two versus one southern points, and log-band integration supplies the independent uniform log-gap mark. The Cartesian determinant and independent numerical quadrature checks corroborate the proof. The proof, not those finite checks, supplies the infinite asymptotic.

## Originality — PASS, with historical risk

The \(1/4\) hemispherical baseline is not asserted as novel. The nearby established hemisphere fixed-pole margin problem addresses varying distance to the hull boundary with a hemisphere-supported sampling law; it does not permit the random vertices themselves to cross the equator, and its marginal-margin implication does not deliver the cubic-logarithmic coefficient or marked sign split. Wendel (1962) determines separability under symmetric laws. Bárány–Hug–Reitzner–Schneider (2015) derive expected facet, volume and approximation formulas for random hemispherical polytopes, with emphasis on growing sample size. Bürgisser–Cucker–Lotz (2010) derive *sphere-coverage* formulas for caps whose centers are sampled on the full sphere. None of those inspected sources contains or implies the stated one-sided three-vertex fixed-pole boundary law. Additional historical literature might contain a related calculation, so priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The problem quantifies stability of a fixed interior-pole capture event when the natural sampling domain passes from geodesically convex to reflex. The geometry is not governed by an ordinary Taylor expansion: rare nearly-semicircular azimuth gaps produce a cubic term amplified by a logarithm. The exact coefficient and the \(3:1\) mechanism split distinguish two-south from one-south configurations and clarify the scale of angular near-degeneracy. The statement is a natural boundary law for spherical convex hulls, not a renamed classical hemisphere probability.

## Limits and source comparison

The theorem is restricted to three samples on \(S^2\), is a one-sided asymptotic, and proves weak convergence for the marks only. The cited primary sources were read to the relevant results; a global priority claim is not made.
