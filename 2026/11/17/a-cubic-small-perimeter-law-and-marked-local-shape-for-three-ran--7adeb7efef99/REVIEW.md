# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The normal-coordinate measure is exactly product-uniform, planar spanning has probability \(1/4\), and the spherical cell admits an exact halfplane description under gnomonic projection. The planar perimeter is linear in supporting-line offsets, with coefficients proved by moving a single side. The event of small perimeter bounds all normal offsets and excludes nonspanning azimuths. Pointwise convergence plus an integrable fixed-cube bound proves the cubic tail and the joint total-variation limit; the beta and Dirichlet factors follow from a transparent Jacobian. The coefficient is independently integrated numerically and checked by true spherical intersections. No simulation is used as a proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority

The same fixed-ray conical zero-cell appears in Hug–Schneider (2015), but the accessible full paper develops first and second moments rather than a vanishing-perimeter probability or a joint rare-event offset–angle law. Le (1989) computes the shape distribution of three cap-uniform spherical vertices, which may relate to the dual of the current cell; because the rotation-quotiented shape law does not itself mark the pole-to-supporting-circle offsets, it cannot directly state the conditional offset-factorization theorem. Its full text could not be obtained, and a scalar consequence from its methods remains possible. Other older sources are noted as historical risks. Published indexed findings on spherical Voronoi cells, large-sample spherical zero-cell moments and spherical triangle perimeters concern different models or regimes; a separate three-circle cell-area-imbalance result concerns a different geometric invariant.

## Value — PASS

The exact cubic law gives the rare-event scale for unusually small cells generated by the minimum nontrivial great-circle arrangement. The stronger statement identifies how the three supporting circles approach the pole and factorizes their asymptotic geometric contributions into universal beta–Dirichlet marks with a nontrivial angular bias. This is information not supplied by moment formulas and is suitable for local conditioning, small-cell simulation, and comparison with large-sample spherical tessellations.

## Scope and residual risks

The claim is restricted to three isotropic great circles on the two-sphere; it is not a finite-threshold density or an explicit error bound. Source priority remains provisional: Le (1989), Arbeiter–Zähle (1994), and older spherical tessellation studies could contain related implicit distributions. Independent expert audit has not been performed.
