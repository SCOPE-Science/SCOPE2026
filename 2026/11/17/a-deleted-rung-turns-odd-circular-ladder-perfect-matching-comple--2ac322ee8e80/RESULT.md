# A deleted rung turns odd circular-ladder perfect-matching complexes into spheres

## Finding
For each odd integer \(n=2k+1\ge3\), define the **punctured circular ladder**
\[
G_n=(C_n\square K_2)-\bigl\{\{(0,0),(1,0)\}\bigr\}.
\]
For any graph \(G\) admitting a perfect matching, let \(\mathcal P(G)\) be the simplicial complex whose vertex set consists of graph edges lying in at least one perfect matching and whose faces are precisely the partial matchings extendible to some perfect matching of \(G\). Its facets are all the perfect matchings.

**Theorem.** For every such odd \(n\),
\[
\boxed{\ |\mathcal P(G_n)|\simeq S^{(n-3)/2}.\ }
\]
In fact, writing \(L_m=P_m\square K_2\) for the straight ladder with \(m\) columns, there is a two-cone decomposition with exact intersection \(\mathcal P(L_{n-3})\), and
\[
|\mathcal P(G_n)|\simeq\Sigma|\mathcal P(L_{n-3})|.
\]
For \(n=3\) the right-hand side is interpreted via the augmented empty complex (or directly as two disjoint simplices). The corresponding **intact** odd circular-ladder perfect-matching complexes are contractible, by Agarwal and Basak [1]. Thus a single-edge deletion produces a homotopy sphere of unbounded dimension.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices are \((a,j)\), for \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Horizontal edges join \((a,j)\) to \((a,j+1)\); vertical edges join \((0,j)\) to \((1,j)\), except that the edge at \(j=0\) is absent. Graphs are finite, simple and undirected. The cycle has \(n\ge3\) columns. All homotopy equivalences and (reduced) homology statements are integral, not field-specific. The case \(n=3\) has a nonstandard tiny horizontal-cycle geometry but the proof and statement remain valid.

## Proof
**1. A paired-horizontal-edge lemma.** In a perfect matching of a straight ladder, the leftmost column is covered either by its vertical rung or by **both** horizontal edges to column two. In the horizontal case, using only one of these two edges leaves the other vertex of the first column unmatched: its vertical edge is blocked by the first horizontal edge, so it must use the second horizontal edge. Removing the chosen first column or first two columns leaves a shorter straight ladder. Induction proves that every perfect matching of a straight ladder is tiled by vertical rungs (single columns) and paired horizontal edges (two consecutive columns).

For an odd circular ladder, a perfect matching with no rung would independently perfectly match each odd cycle of horizontal edges, which is impossible. Choose a rung. Removing all rung columns divides the remaining vertices on each horizontal cycle into identical disjoint path segments; a path of even order has exactly one perfect matching. Hence the horizontal edges of every perfect matching occur in pairs, one in each row over the same column interval. This statement remains true after deletion of the rung at column zero.

**2. The punctured circular ladder as two cones.** Since the rung at column zero is deleted, every perfect matching of \(G_n\) covers the two vertices in that column by either the pair of horizontal edges leading to column one (call this pattern \(H_+\)), or the pair leading to column \(n-1\) (call this \(H_-\)). The paired-horizontal-edge lemma excludes the mixed choice. Let \(A\) and \(B\) be the subcomplexes generated by the full perfect matchings containing, respectively, \(H_+\) and \(H_-\). Their generating facet sets are disjoint and cover the generating facets of \(\mathcal P(G_n)\). Every maximal face of \(A\) contains either edge of \(H_+\); thus \(A\) is a simplicial cone. Similarly \(B\) is a cone.

A face of \(A\cap B\) cannot contain an edge incident to column zero: perfect matchings from the two families use distinct edges there. Likewise it cannot contain an edge incident to column one, which is wholly covered by \(H_+\) in \(A\)'s defining perfect matchings, nor to column \(n-1\), wholly covered by \(H_-\) in \(B\)'s defining perfect matchings. Therefore it consists only of edges in the induced straight ladder on columns \(2,3,\ldots,n-2\). Choose one perfect matching containing that face. By the paired-horizontal-edge lemma, every horizontal edge appearing in the face forces its parallel horizontal partner in the corresponding complete matching. Thus its chosen edges specify a collection of mutually disjoint *column tiles*: one-column rungs or two-column pairs of horizontals, each supported entirely in columns \(2\) through \(n-2\). Filling every unused interior column with its vertical rung gives a perfect matching of the interior straight ladder extending the face. The forced tiles do not overlap because they came from a single complete perfect matching. Conversely, every face extendible to a perfect matching of the interior straight ladder extends to a full perfect matching of \(G_n\) using \(H_+\) and the rung at \(n-1\), and also to one using \(H_-\) and the rung at one. Hence the intersection, with its original simplicial face structure, is **exactly**
\[
A\cap B\cong\mathcal P(L_{n-3}).
\]
When \(n=3\), there are no interior vertices and the intersection is the augmented complex \(\{\varnothing\}\).

Since simplicial subcomplex inclusions are cofibrations, the ordinary union is a homotopy pushout. Contractibility of both cones yields \(|A\cup B|\simeq\Sigma|A\cap B|\), whenever the intersection has nonempty realization. At \(n=3\), the union consists of two disjoint full-matching simplices, so its realization is \(S^0\) up to homotopy.

**3. A self-contained calculation for straight ladders.** Let \(m=2j\ge2\). Partition perfect matchings of \(L_m\) into those using the rung in the first column (subcomplex \(U_m\)) and those using the two horizontal edges between the first two columns (subcomplex \(V_m\)). Both are cones, with those forced edges as cone vertices. The exact same restriction-and-extension argument proves
\[
U_m\cap V_m\cong\mathcal P(L_{m-2}).
\]
The base \(\mathcal P(L_2)\) consists of two disjoint edges: one is the pair of vertical rungs and the other the pair of horizontals, with no shared graph edges. It is homotopy equivalent to \(S^0\). Thus for every \(j\ge1\), repeated two-cone gluing gives
\[
|\mathcal P(L_{2j})|\simeq S^{j-1}.
\]
The empty ladder can equivalently be treated as the augmented \((-1)\)-sphere in this recurrence.

**4. Conclusion.** For \(n=2k+1\ge5\), the interior has \(n-3=2(k-1)\) columns, so its perfect-matching complex is \(S^{k-2}\) up to homotopy. Suspending proves \(|\mathcal P(G_n)|\simeq S^{k-1}=S^{(n-3)/2}\); the \(n=3\) case was treated directly. No field coefficients, computer extrapolation, or unproved global classification are used.

## Verification
The included standalone standard-library program `verify_perfect_matching_ladders.py` enumerates perfect matchings from graph edges, forms all simplicial faces, independently checks that the two cone-generated subcomplexes cover the entire complex and intersect **exactly** in the perfect-matching complex of the prescribed induced straight ladder, and calculates reduced mod-two Betti numbers by explicit boundary reduction. It checks odd punctured circumferences \(3,5,7,9\), and their structural decomposition also at \(11\). It checks straight ladders of lengths \(2,4,6,8\). The finite calculations corroborate, but do not replace, the proof for all odd \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak [1], Theorem 25 and Main Theorem 2, classify perfect-matching complexes of **intact** circular ladders, proving contractibility for every odd circumference. Their Section 5.2 concerns the intact ladder, and their classification does not give the one-rung-deleted case. Chandrakar and Singh [2] study perfect-matching complexes of straight \(2\)-row ladders and polygonal line tilings; the straight-ladder sphere calculation above is included as a self-contained intermediate lemma and is not asserted as the new object. The new result is the exact two-cone intersection created by a deleted circular rung and the resulting infinite-family change from a contractible intact complex to a sphere. Results on the **ordinary** matching complex or the **Möbius** ladder address different graphs or different simplicial complexes and do not imply this statement without additional arguments.

## Limitations
Only a single deleted **vertical** rung and **odd** circular circumferences are treated. Nothing is claimed for deletion of horizontal edges, multiple missing rungs, or even circumferences. The displayed homotopy equivalences are not claimed to be equivariant, simplicial collapses, or homeomorphisms. The finite verification tests are not an infinite proof, and lack of an identified duplicate is not proof of global priority.
