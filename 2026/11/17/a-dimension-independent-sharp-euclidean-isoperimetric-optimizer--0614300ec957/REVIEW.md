# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The chainlink volume trace is taken, with its exact parameter range and normalization, from the original paper's Proposition 6.8. Differentiating a moving supporting plane contributes its ordinary Euclidean facet area times reciprocal normal length. The vector area-balance identity proves that total lower-coordinate and upper-coordinate facet areas coincide, giving the exact general boundary-area formula. Uniform specialization and homogeneity give the derivative expression. The entire fixed-volume optimization is proved by strict Brunn–Minkowski concavity: different members have unit coordinate widths yet strictly different volumes, and hence cannot be homothetic. The calculated quotient derivative changes sign only at \(1-1/\sqrt2\). Independent convex hull calculations corroborate the exact boundary areas for small dimensions. Finite checks are not treated as proof of the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)

The principal closest source, Kantarcı Oğuz–Özel–Ravichandran, arXiv:2211.08382v1, establishes the general volume trace, not the full-dimensional ordinary Euclidean surface measure or the sharp scale-invariant surface minimizer. A related result on perimeters of complementary chainlink slices concerns fixed-section two-dimensional geometry, and related research on inballs and Minkowski asymmetry studies different observables. The volume identity, first-variation principle and Brunn–Minkowski theorem are all explicitly credited. Search did not reveal an equivalent all-dimensional isoperimetric optimization, but unindexed work or a hidden argument in related literature remains a risk. One related accessible summary could not be compared against its unavailable full text.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A dimension-independent **unique** optimizer is a structural isoperimetric theorem for a canonical, naturally parameterized family of full-dimensional polytopes; it is not just a re-expression of a volume formula. The result gives exact normalized Euclidean boundary area and identifies a real sharp volume-preserving improvement over cubes in all dimensions, with a nontrivial high-dimensional limiting gain. The family and precise metric are motivated by the primary source's cyclic fence / chainlink geometry.

## Scope and residual limitations

This is an intrinsic Euclidean, not lattice-normalized, measure. Optimality is only within the uniform family and the admissible parameter interval; no claim for arbitrary chainlink vectors or link values beyond the source's volume chamber is made. Some historically related sources are accessible only through summaries, so priority is provisional.
