The polynomial cofactor identity dot f=-2 x f for f=x^2+y^2-1 proves invariance. On the unit circle dot theta=c+a cos theta+b sin theta=c+R cos(theta-phi)>0. Standard circle integral gives exact original-time period T=2pi/sqrt(c^2-R^2). The normalized time density has E cos(theta)=-a/(c+Delta), so the transverse variational multiplier is exp(-2 integral x dt)=exp(4pi a/[Delta(c+Delta)]). With a=kappa sqrt(epsilon), c=sqrt(a^2+b^2)+epsilon, Delta/sqrt(epsilon)->sqrt(2b), giving the exact two-parameter period and multiplier limits. Independently numerically evaluated for epsilon=1e-3,1e-5 at b=1.3,kappa=2; scaled T tends 3.89666610975 and log multiplier tends 11.9897418762. At slowing angle u=0, physical-time density Delta/[2pi(epsilon+R(1-cos u))] rescales by sqrt(R/(2epsilon)) to Cauchy; a quadratic lower bound on 1-cos u gives tight tails. For kappa=0, J=(x^2+y^2-1)(c+by)^(2/b) is a local first integral and regular circle is part of a period annulus. Endpoint a=0,c=b has nilpotent Jacobian [[0,b+2],[0,0]] and quadratic angular zero, giving a homoclinic circle-minus-point. No off-circle global claim.
