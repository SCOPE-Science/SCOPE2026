# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. Five rational vertices, six supporting triangular faces, exact section areas and facet areas, exact axial distributions, and Gini integrals give a fully specified three-dimensional mean-distance reversal. The rational strict margin follows from elementary monotonicity and exact root bounds. Re-execution of the bundled standalone rational checker succeeds. Risks: Euclidean first-moment means are bounded rather than evaluated exactly, but this is sufficient for strict reversal.

## Originality

PASS, provisional. Shen's arXiv:2608.06470, section 4, establishes a six-vertex example and the longitudinal Gini technique; it does not furnish a five-vertex construction. The current example has a triangular end face rather than the source's rectangular end face and a different cross-section density. The five-vertex condition cannot be inferred merely from existence of a six-vertex example or by deleting a vertex, since both boundary and interior probability distributions change. Lotnikov's cited centrally symmetric construction cannot have five vertices; Tokmachev studies positive inequality under additional hypotheses. Targeted published-index searches found no equivalent pentavertical construction. Priority risk remains from unindexed or implicit equivalent work.

## Value

PASS. Five is the first non-simplex vertex count possible for a three-dimensional polytope. An all-triangular-face five-vertex counterexample strengthens the known six-vertex witness, pushing the failure into the simplest nonsimplicial spatial combinatorial class. The quantitative strict certificate is elementary and independently executable. The result does not claim the four-vertex case is impossible.

## Limits

No minimality proof, no exact spatial mean-distance values, and no independent audit. Source's axial-stretch lemma and Gini formula are duly credited.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
