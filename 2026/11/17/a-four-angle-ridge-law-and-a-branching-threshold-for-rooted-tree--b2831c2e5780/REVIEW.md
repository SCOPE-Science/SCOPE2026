# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The order-polytope inequality system is irredundant: precisely one root lower, one upper per leaf, and one inequality per cover edge. The proof treats all six types of facet pair, including the potentially misleading upper-leaf/own-cover case: this pair is a ridge only when the parent has exactly one child. Contraction of selected edges gives an independent proof of codimension two for all other pairs. Orthogonal unit-normal products give the four angles. The four multiplicities add to the separately derived ridge count, and the maximum-degree criterion follows from sharp quadratic upper/lower bounds on sibling-edge pairs. A finite checker independently verifies actual vertex-affine ranks and Euclidean cosine values for 748 rooted instances through nine vertices. Risks: no infinite computation is claimed; the generic-height construction is essential and is spelled out in RESULT.md.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

The general face-partition theorem in Ahmad–Fourier–Joswig, arXiv:2309.01626, §2, Theorem 2.1, already covers the *combinatorial adjacency* consequences through specialization. These are credited, not claimed as a stand-alone novelty. The added metric work is a complete all-tree Euclidean ridge-angle spectrum and an if-and-only-if asymptotic branching criterion, neither of which is asserted in the inspected source sections. Garver–Grosser–Matherne–Morales, arXiv:2001.08822, §1, studies linear extensions of rooted-tree posets. Fang–Fourier–Litza–Pegel, arXiv:1712.01037, treats marked-poset interpolation; only its abstract was inspected. Semantic searches compared “facet adjacency,” “dihedral,” “rooted tree order polytope,” “ridge angle spectrum,” “maximum branching,” and related mathematical aliases with indexed findings. Results on intrinsic volumes, boundary areas, covariance and difference-body inballs do not imply this Euclidean ridge-angle distribution. Historical priority remains susceptible to an unindexed or inaccessible equivalent computation.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The claim is a full metric classification over a canonical infinite family, not an arbitrary finite slice or raw facet-count substitution. It distinguishes sibling, consecutive-edge and boundary-edge geometry; shows the facet dual graph is almost complete despite highly varied rooted combinatorics; and supplies a sharp necessary-and-sufficient concentration criterion for typical ridge geometry that separates linear branching from sublinear branching. It is compatible with, but not subsumed by, previously studied surface area or intrinsic volume identities. Limitations: it does not imply ridge-volume distributions or extend to noncanonical Euclidean metrics.
