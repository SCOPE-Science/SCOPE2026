# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)

For the specified hub-first, clockwise rim scan on \(K_1\vee C_n\), each local operation is a binary linear involution. Writing the sweep equation with strictly triangular adjacency parts proves its characteristic determinant. The three-dimensional elimination, its explicit finite geometric sums and four residue cases yield the displayed reciprocal polynomial for all \(n\ge4\). The eigenvector-rank argument over an algebraic closure proves that the only possible noncyclicity occurs at eigenvalue one; the rank and first derivative of the reduced matrix together with the polynomial's multiplicity at one give precisely the stated invariant factors. The standard polynomial-module kernel calculation proves the fixed-power and primitive-orbit formulas for every positive exponent. The separately constructed verifier agrees with direct characteristic determinants for rim lengths 4–25, minimal polynomials for 4–80, 1,078 fixed-power ranks, and the full labelled functional graphs for 4–10. These are corroborating finite checks, not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)

Macauley–Mortveit (arXiv:0802.4412, original 2008-02-29) treats update-order equivalence for arbitrary graph systems, not a wheel-specific characteristic polynomial or primitive spectrum. Macauley–McCammond–Mortveit (arXiv:0808.1238, sections 2 and 5) analyzes Rule 150 on *cycle graphs* without a central hub. The publicly available complete result *Scan-order phase diagram and exact Rule 150 cycle census on binary rings* proves a stronger result about *scan orders on rings*, but contains no hub or wheel invariant-factor assertion. The complete result *A two-adic transient law and exact orbit census for XOR dynamics on wheel graphs* treats a **synchronous open-neighborhood XOR** wheel map and therefore has different invertibility and spectrum. These are not implications of the present hub-first sequential closed-neighborhood theorem. Equivalent-formulation, stronger-coverage, exact-table and implication searches did not reveal an earlier result with this combined map, size family, and spectral module classification. The possibility of unindexed historical Coxeter-type or graph-dynamics work remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

The classification exposes an all-parameter exceptional Jordan phenomenon: a central hub leaves the sweep cyclic unless the rim length is divisible by four, at which point two extra one-dimensional invariant summands appear. This is not merely a renaming of the ring Rule 150 census or a small finite table. The exact characteristic and invariant factors determine all primitive temporal orbits, including different long-period mixtures at the five-rim and eight-rim boundaries, and give a reusable description of coupling sequential local involutions around a short-cycle hub.
