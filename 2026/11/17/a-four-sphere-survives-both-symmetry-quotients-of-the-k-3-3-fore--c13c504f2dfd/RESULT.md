# A four-sphere survives both symmetry quotients of the \(K_{3,3}\) forest complex

## Finding
Let \(K_{3,3}\) be the graph with left and right vertex sets \(L=R=\{0,1,2\}\). Let \(\mathcal F\) be the simplicial complex whose nine vertices are the graph edges and whose faces are the **acyclic** subsets of graph edges (the graphic-matroid independence complex). The side-preserving group \(H=S_3\times S_3\) and the full graph-automorphism group \(G=(S_3\times S_3)\rtimes C_2\), including the exchange of the two shores, act simplicially.

The following are homotopy equivalences of ordinary geometric realizations and orbit spaces:
\[
 |\mathcal F|\simeq\bigvee^{20}S^4,
 \qquad |\mathcal F|/H\simeq S^4,
 \qquad |\mathcal F|/G\simeq S^4.
\]
In particular both quotients are simply connected, have \(\widetilde H_4(-;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), and have no other reduced integral homology. No claim of homeomorphism to a manifold or of an equivariant sphere equivalence is made.

## Assumptions and scope
An edge \((i,j)\) joins left vertex \(i\) to right vertex \(j\). A set of edges is a forest precisely if its graph has no cycle, with isolated vertices allowed. Both groups act by relabeling shores; the nontrivial shore-exchange element sends \((i,j)\) to \((j,i)\). The quotient means the topological orbit space, not the Borel construction or the orbit of an abstract complex formed by blindly identifying vertices. For homology all coefficients are integral unless specified.

## Proof
**1. Topology of the covering complex.** The forests of \(K_{3,3}\) form the independent sets of a rank-five graphic matroid. A graphic matroid independence complex is pure and shellable, hence homotopy equivalent to a wedge of four-spheres (Björner's matroid shellability theorem). Direct forest enumeration gives the numbers of nonempty faces by cardinality \(1,2,3,4,5\):
\[
(9,36,84,117,81).
\]
Its reduced Euler characteristic is \(-1+9-36+84-117+81=20\), giving twenty four-spheres. In particular the covering complex is simply connected.

**2. The genuine orbit chain complex.** Barycentrically subdivide \(\mathcal F\). Its \(d\)-simplices are flags \(F_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_d\) of nonempty forests. The action preserves the number of edges in each flag member. Thus any stabilizer of a flag fixes its vertices separately, and an orbit of flags is a regular simplex cell with its naturally increasing-rank orientation. Different flag orbits may have identical sets of vertex orbits, so these cells must not be merged. Their boundary is
\[
\partial[F_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_d]
 =\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^i[F_0\subsetneq\cdots\widehat{F_i}\cdots\subsetneq F_d],
\]
where brackets denote a whole group orbit of the indicated flag. Because deleted positions have distinct rank signatures, no two codimension-one faces of one flag are identified under the group. This is the integral cellular complex for the actual orbit space.

**3. Exhaustive exact calculation.** The attached independent Python checker enumerates all forest masks (by a union-find cycle test), all \(36\) or \(72\) group elements and all orbits of nonempty forest flags. It constructs boundaries from the preceding formula. An independent Burnside count of fixed flags under each permutation checks every orbit-cell count. Results, ordered by dimension \(0,1,2,3,4\), are

| Group | Order | Cells in dimensions \(0\) through \(4\) | Integral unit cancellations in degrees \(1,2,3,4\) |
|---|---:|---|---|
| \(H\) | 36 | \((20,162,504,630,270)\) | \((19,143,361,269)\) |
| \(G\) | 72 | \((14,92,260,315,135)\) | \((13,79,181,134)\) |

Each cancellation chooses a differential entry \(u=\pm1\) between generators in consecutive degrees, uses integral basis changes to isolate the corresponding contractible two-term summand, and removes it. Algebraically the remaining matrix is its Schur complement \(A-bu^{-1}c\). The checker carries out these operations over exact integers, verifies \(\partial^2=0\) before reduction, and ends with one generator in degree zero and one in degree four, and no remaining differential. Therefore, for **each** group,
\[
\widetilde H_i(|\mathcal F|/(-);\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&i=4,\\0,&i\ne4.\end{cases}
\]
These chain cancellations prove integral homology, not a topological simple collapse; the remaining argument is needed to determine homotopy type.

**4. Simple connectivity.** Armstrong's orbit-space theorem identifies the fundamental group of the quotient of a simply connected finite polyhedron by a finite simplicial group with the quotient of the group by the normal subgroup generated by elements having a fixed point. Each elementary transposition within one shore fixes a singleton forest edge based at the third vertex of that shore. These transpositions generate \(H\). The additional shore-exchange generator fixes every diagonal edge \((i,i)\) as a vertex of \(\mathcal F\). Thus the fixed-point elements generate both \(H\) and \(G\), and both orbit spaces are simply connected.

**5. Homotopy type.** Each orbit space is a simply connected finite CW complex with integral homology of \(S^4\). The low-degree Hurewicz theorem makes it three-connected and identifies \(\pi_4\cong H_4\cong\mathbb Z\). A representative \(S^4\to|\mathcal F|/(-)\) of a generator induces an integral homology isomorphism. Whitehead's theorem then makes this map a homotopy equivalence. This establishes the three asserted equivalences.

## Verification
Use a standard Python 3 interpreter from the directory containing the package:

```bash
python3 verify_k33.py
```

It enumerates \(327\) nonempty forests, checks the forest face counts, constructs both **genuine flag-orbit** chain complexes, checks every boundary composition, independently recomputes the cell numbers by Burnside averaging, and performs all exact integral unit cancellations. The expected complete output is in `verification_output.txt`. The checker is finite and exhaustive for this exact graph and these two groups; it is not evidence for any family of larger graphs.

## Relationship to prior work
Björner's 1992 matroid-shellability theorem supplies the covering wedge-of-spheres fact but does not identify the orbit spaces. Stones (2014), *Computing the number of h-edge spanning forests in complete bipartite graphs*, tabulates the same labeled forest counts \((1,9,36,84,117,81)\) for \(K_{3,3}\) (Figure 1, page 315) but computes counts, not symmetry-quotient topology. Hughes and Swartz (arXiv:1205.6387, first public 29 May 2012) study quotients of **linear spheres by tori**, in which a matroid encodes the torus representation; their Theorems 9 and 16 do not apply to finite graph automorphisms of a graphic-matroid independence complex. Armstrong's 1965 theorem concerns fundamental groups of orbit spaces, not integral higher homology.

Adjacent indexed findings address the \(K_{2,n}\) edge-forest complex, the **directed multipath** complex of \(K_{3,3}\), and the **Morse matching** complex of \(K_{3,3}\). Neither identifies these \(K_{3,3}\) graphic-matroid orbit spaces. The distinction is by object and implication, not title alone.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to the nine-edge forest matroid of \(K_{3,3}\) and the two stated natural groups. The result is a homotopy classification only; it does not show either quotient is a PL sphere, a topological manifold, or a homology manifold. It does not calculate the degree of the natural map between the two quotients, nor characterize quotients by every subgroup. The computational certificate uses no external packages but still requires checking its finite algorithm. Possible unindexed equivalent classifications are not excluded.
