# A gap-sensitive sphere-wedge transition for partially reversed cyclic permutation complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(P_n\) be the semisimplicial ordered-injective-word complex generated by all \(n\) cyclic rotations of \((0,1,\ldots,n-1)\). Choose a nonempty set \(T\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\) of \(t\) starting positions, and let \(R_T\) be generated by the reversed cyclic words
\[
 (s,s-1,\ldots,s-(n-1))\pmod n,\qquad s\in T.
\]
Set \(X_T=P_n\cup R_T\). For \(t\ge2\), the marked starts partition the reversed cyclic ordering into \(t\) nonempty consecutive runs of lengths \(g_1,\ldots,g_t\), with \(\sum_i g_i=n\). Then the integral homotopy classification is
\[
 |X_T|\simeq
 \begin{cases}
  \bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{2}-1}S^2,&t=1,\\[2pt]
  \bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{2}+\sum_{i<j}g_ig_j-2}S^2,&2\le t\le n.
 \end{cases}
\]
Zero sphere summands mean a contractible space. The genuinely new assertion is the classification for \(1\le t<n\), including the dependence on the gap pattern; the full two-orientation endpoint \(t=n\) was already known. For example, with \(n=6\), two selected starts at reversed-cyclic distance \(1\) or \(3\) yield respectively \(13\) or \(17\) two-spheres.

## Assumptions and scope
A cell is a nonempty ordered subword of a selected full permutation. Distinct words are distinct cells, even if they use the same vertex set. The cellular boundary of \((a_0,\ldots,a_k)\) is \(\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j(a_0,\ldots,\widehat a_j,\ldots,a_k)\). This is a regular semisimplicial CW complex, or equivalently one may work with its barycentric subdivision. All equivalences and homology statements are integral and nonequivariant. Starting positions and run lengths are taken cyclically, with every run nonempty. No claim is made about reversal subsets in which forward rotations are also removed.

## Proof
**1. Each orientation alone.** Let \(W\) be the word complex of all forward rotations. The following gives an explicit acyclic discrete-Morse matching for \(n\ge3\). If a word \(w\) omits \(0\), it consists of one increasing block or two increasing blocks separated by the unique cyclic descent. Match \(w\) with the word formed by inserting \(0\) at its descent, or at the beginning when there is no descent. The unmatched words are exactly \((0)\) and \((s_1<\cdots<s_k,0)\) for nonempty subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\). Next, whenever \(1\notin\{s_i\}\), match the latter word to \((1,s_1,\ldots,s_k,0)\). Only \((0)\) and \((1,0)\) remain critical. For acyclicity, every upward step of the first kind has all other lower facets containing \(0\); thus it can be followed only by a second-kind step. Every second-kind upward step has other lower facets either containing \(1\), and hence unmatchable, or the word \((1,s_1,\ldots,s_k)\), which makes one first-kind upward step to \((0,1,s_1,\ldots,s_k)\); after that step no further matching is possible. In particular no closed gradient path exists. Hence \(|P_n|\simeq S^1\). The same argument, reversing numerical order, applies to the full reverse-rotation family. For any two distinct reverse rotations, their two full simplices intersect in two disjoint nonempty simplices, so their union is \(S^1\). On adding another reverse rotation, the intersection with what was already present is the union of two full simplices on the two largest complementary initial/final intervals in the new rotation; since there were at least two old starting cuts, these intervals intersect in a nonempty simplex. The attachment is contractible, so \(|R_T|\simeq S^1\) whenever \(t\ge2\). For \(t=1\), \(R_T\) is a simplex.

**2. Intersection graph.** Write \(K_T=P_n\cap R_T\). Every two-letter ordered word occurs in \(P_n\). On any three distinct letters, forward and reversed cyclic orders are opposite, so no reversed three-letter word occurs in \(P_n\). Thus \(K_T\) is the connected, one-dimensional multigraph with one oriented edge per ordered pair occurring in \(R_T\). For \(t=1\), it is the complete graph with one edge per unordered pair. For \(t\ge2\), an unordered pair of letters has both orientations in \(R_T\) exactly when its endpoints lie in different runs between the selected reversed starts. Consequently
\[
 E(K_T)=\binom n2+\sum_{i<j}g_ig_j,
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}H_1(K_T;\mathbb Z)=E(K_T)-n+1.
\]

**3. Integral winding and nullification.** On forward ordered edges define \(\omega_+(a,b)=1\) if \(a>b\) and \(0\) otherwise; on reversed ordered edges define \(\omega_-(a,b)=1\) if \(a<b\) and \(0\) otherwise. For every allowed ordered triple in its respective orientation, the alternating sum of values on the three boundary edges is zero. Each class is a primitive integral generator of its orientation's \(H^1\): for the forward family evaluate on the cyclic directed polygon, and for \(t\ge2\) in the reverse family evaluate on a directed digon between vertices belonging to different runs. A reversed triangle has its entire boundary in \(K_T\), with winding pair \((\omega_+,\omega_-)=(1,0)\). For \(t\ge2\), a digon crossing two runs has winding pair \((1,1)\). Therefore the map
\[
 H_1(K_T;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow
 H_1(P_n;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(R_T;\mathbb Z)
\]
 is integrally surjective for \(t\ge2\); for \(t=1\), the reversed triangle alone surjects onto \(H_1(P_n;\mathbb Z)\). Applying van Kampen to the union shows \(\pi_1(X_T)=1\), since these loops kill the one or both fundamental-group generators, not merely their abelianizations.

**4. The sphere wedge.** The union \(X_T=P_n\cup R_T\) is a homotopy pushout of its two subcomplexes over \(K_T\). Mayer--Vietoris and the preceding integral surjectivity yield \(H_2(X_T;\mathbb Z)\cong\ker(H_1(K_T)\to H_1(P_n)\oplus H_1(R_T))\), free of the displayed rank; all other reduced integral homology vanishes. Since the union is simply connected, a basis of \(H_2\) is represented by maps from two-spheres; their wedge is a homology equivalence and therefore a homotopy equivalence by the simply connected Whitehead theorem. This proves the claimed formulas for every \(n\) and every selected reverse-start set.

## Verification
The embedded standalone checker `VERIFY_PARTIAL.py` enumerates all nonempty reverse-start subsets for \(3\le n\le7\), reconstructs their full ordered subword complexes, verifies that the forward/reverse intersection has no higher cells, checks the gap-edge formula, and computes all modular boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_{101}\). All \(243\) subsets pass. The separate `VERIFY.py` enumerates the complete face-poset Hasse graph for \(2\le n\le11\), verifies the specified optimal acyclic matching, the exact number of critical cells, the winding cocycle, and the face count. These tests are finite checks only; the proof above establishes the unbounded statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Govc, *Fundamental Groups of Permutation Complexes* (arXiv:2611.11362, first publicly posted 2026-11-08), proves freeness of fundamental groups for arbitrary permutation complexes but supplies no partial opposite-orientation rank formula. Chachólski--Levi--Meshulam, *On the Topology of Complexes of Injective Words* (arXiv:1908.03394, 2019), develops permutation complexes and the two-generator case, but does not classify this many-generator gap-sensitive family. An earlier classification of the **full** two-orientation rotation complex, \(t=n\), gives \(\bigvee^{(n-1)^2-2}S^2\); our formula recovers that established endpoint and is not claiming it anew. The new content is every proper nonempty reverse subset, the single-reversal transition, and the dependence of the resulting rank on its cyclic spacing. Pattern-avoiding injective-word complexes (arXiv:2610.07303) are a different selection rule and were checked at the abstract level only.

## Limitations
The result requires **all** forward rotations and a nonempty subset of reverse rotations. The underlying semisimplicial cells remember order; replacing them by unoriented subsets changes the object. The proof does not compute equivariant homology, attaching maps for arbitrary noncyclic permutation subsets, or simultaneous deletion of forward and reverse rotations. Published coverage beyond the inspected sources could still overlap; originality is a best-knowledge literature assessment, not independent priority certification.
