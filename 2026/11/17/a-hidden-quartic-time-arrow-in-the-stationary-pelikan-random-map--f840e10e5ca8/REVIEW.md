# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The hidden representation is exact: starting depth \(K_0\) geometric, independent uniform \(U\), and \(X_n=2^{-K_n}D^{L_n}U\). Geometric detailed balance makes the path reversal identity valid at each lag. The dyadic cylinder calculation establishes exact mixed-shift moments \(h_{2,1}=h_{1,2}\) and \(h_{3,1}-h_{1,3}=(2^{-m}-2^{-3m})/120\). Every fourth-moment contribution is nonnegative, the all-doubling event makes it positive at every positive lag, and a zero-to-zero event cannot occur from an initial depth at least the number of available steps. The analytical proof therefore has the stated all-integer scope. Independent direct-map one-step integration and 28 exact finite path evaluations corroborate, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality: PASS within inspected literature
Yan et al. (arXiv:2308.09269, especially their Section III on position correlations) supply the invariant density and second-order information; they do not evaluate the fourth-order time direction. Monthus (arXiv:2409.19999, Section VI.2 and VI.5) provides the earlier binary-stack method and a uniform-initial position correlation, not the stationary quartic minimality criterion. The full available text of a published published-finding corpus finding on Pelikan correlations proves ordinary stationary covariance, not fourth moments; another published-finding corpus result treats three-time correlations of a different class of deterministic full-branch maps. A separate own-history finding analyzes a Pelikan reflected-depth coupling, but not this fourth-degree all-lag time arrow. These objects and implications were compared rather than just titles. Residual risk: general existing binary-stack formulas may indirectly imply the same moment identity; older unindexed literature and Pelikan (1984) were not exhaustively inspected.

## Mathematical value: PASS
Stationary dynamical time-reversal asymmetry is a natural and structurally meaningful diagnostic. The theorem identifies the minimal total polynomial degree at which a two-time coordinate statistic detects the time arrow in a well-studied random map, although all second- and third-degree moments are symmetric. Strict positivity at every lag and an exactly computable finite path formula sharpen the contrast. It is neither a parameter relabeling nor a known covariance recomputation. It does not assert three-time or distributional symmetry.

## Closest literature and boundaries
The stationary invariant density and random map definition come from Yan et al., supported by earlier Pelikan work; the reversible hidden walk and bit-deletion mechanism relate to Monthus's representation. The result excludes the nonstationary regime, quenched averages, and the full process's time-reversal law. No priority certification is claimed.
