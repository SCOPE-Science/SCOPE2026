# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The stationarity assumptions and finite second moment are explicit prerequisites, identified as established in Matveev's equations (8)–(12) and (11). Reconstructing the differentiated generating identity produces the nonnegative-derivative differential equation \((1-H)H'''=B-AH''\). Monotonicity gives a uniform finite bound for \(\lambda>9/2\), forced logarithmic divergence for \(4<\lambda<9/2\), and the critical boundary law for \(\lambda=9/2\). An index-zero positive-coefficient Tauberian theorem converts the latter to the exact truncated third-moment coefficient. The closed-form finite branch and near-critical pole follow algebraically. Independent numerical finite-prefix checks corroborate only finite behavior; they are not the proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

Matveev (arXiv:2611.09537v1), sections 1–3, gives the steady recurrence and \(M_2\), but not the post-gelation third-moment boundary, its finite formula, or its critical logarithmic coefficient. The distinct critical case \(\lambda=4\) concerns a second-moment tail, not this higher-moment transition. A published study by Laurençot and van Roessel treats singular fragmentation, not constant-rate discrete monomer shattering. Broader aggregation/fragmentation reviews may contain analogous moment calculations; complete priority remains a risk rather than a claim of exhaustive coverage.

## Value — PASS

The finite second moment is the usual gelation diagnostic. A second, strict threshold at \(\lambda=9/2\), *inside the non-gelling phase*, distinguishes equilibria with unbounded higher size fluctuations from equilibria with a finite third moment. The exact boundary coefficient \(3\) and the pole \(9/4\) quantify both sides of this physically interpretable transition. This is a natural invariant for the model, not an arbitrary finite cutoff or a restatement of its known second moment.

## Scope and remaining risks

The proof is conditional on the published existence and moments of the stationary mass-one branch; it does not independently establish global convergence of all transient solutions. The complete 2026 review and every specialized pre-2026 monograph were not exhaustively inspected. A relevant older article uses a different fragmentation operator and was compared at abstract/metadata level only. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
