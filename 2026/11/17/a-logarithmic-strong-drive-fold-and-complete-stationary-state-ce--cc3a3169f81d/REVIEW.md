# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The normalized rates and currents are obtained directly from the source's homogeneous Glauber equations. A uniform \(C^2\) singular-limit expansion near each vertex leads to the scalar equation \(2u=e^{-c+3u}\). The extended fold equations have a nonsingular Jacobian, establishing the second asymptotic coefficient; a separate compactness argument forces **every** nonuniform equilibrium into one of the enumerated vertex neighborhoods, ruling out unexamined solutions elsewhere on the simplex. The limiting Jacobian has a stable-node branch and a saddle branch, while the disordered point is Hurwitz for \(k=b+O(\log b)\). The numeric checker is a finite consistency test, not the justification for the infinite statements.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

The 2026 Tateyama–Yin–Ito–Kitahata–Noguchi paper supplies these field equations, numerical finite-b saddle-node plots, and an exact Hopf boundary; its relevant Sections II–III, appendix, and supplement do not give the logarithmic high-drive fold constant, its next-order correction, or the exhaustive seven-versus-one fixed-point count. The 2024 Noguchi–van Wijland–Fournier paper was inspected in full; its continuum phenomenology is formulated differently and does not state this result. A separate 2024 Noguchi–Fournier paper was compared at abstract level only. Related mathematical records on discrete cyclic softmax feedback and other three-state models concern different systems and do not imply the theorem. The main remaining risk is an unlocated broader singular-perturbation theorem that may specialize to the calculation.

## Value — PASS

The known finite-b ordered/disordered bistability is physically motivated by competition between cooperative ferromagnetic ordering and cyclic driving. This result provides its previously unknown strong-drive scale, complete fixed-point census, stability types, explicit Lambert-function minority fractions, and a second-order threshold coefficient. The logarithmic fold is exponentially separated from the previously known Hopf boundary, quantifying a large stable-disorder/ordered-state coexistence region. These structural facts are not just changes of variables or routine finite checks.
