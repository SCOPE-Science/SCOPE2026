# Scientific review: marked random-ideal-quadrilateral cusp law

## Correctness — PASS

The proof fixes all sampling conventions, the cyclic Ptolemy cross ratio, and the range of each mark. The exact positive derivative of the angular \(z\mapsto q\) map ensures a unique inverse, and its explicit Jacobian gives the interior marked density. Martin's independently established scalar cross-ratio formula supplies the full denominator and excludes a missing boundary mass. The limit integrates to one, so Scheffé's lemma, not a simulation or informal tightness claim, upgrades pointwise density convergence to total variation. The absolute phase remains independent by rotation invariance. The checker validates the derivative, normalization and simulated finite-threshold comparisons but does not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

The full text of Martin (arXiv:1807.06202), especially Theorem 4.1, already gives the **entire scalar cross-ratio law**, including its logarithmic cusp. That statement is explicitly credited and excluded. The new claim concerns a four-variable conditional law of root phase, macroscopic angular separation, logarithmic small-gap exponent and normalized cross ratio. Deterministic earlier work (Vuorinen–Wang, arXiv:1203.6494; Izmestiev, arXiv:1408.2247) and later geometry (Wang–Vuorinen–Zhang, arXiv:1908.10389) do not assert it. A related published five-point joint-cross-ratio tail involves different conditioning and marks and does not imply this four-point law. Exact aliases, broader possible implications and previous related records were compared, but unidentified older literature is a genuine priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The most degenerate ideal quadrilateral configurations form two almost-coincident pairs, not one fourfold cluster. The theorem determines how the pair sizes split at random logarithmic scales, proves their asymptotic independence from normalized cross ratio, and identifies a nonuniform sine-square bias for the pair separation. This supplies the geometric mechanism hidden by the older scalar cusp law and a natural marked description of degeneration under uniform boundary sampling.

## Scope and risks

Only four independent boundary-uniform vertices and the fixed origin-based angular frame are addressed. No rates of total-variation convergence, extension to nonuniform sampling, or Möbius-invariant formulation of the angular marks is asserted. Historical priority remains provisional. Independent validation has not been performed.
