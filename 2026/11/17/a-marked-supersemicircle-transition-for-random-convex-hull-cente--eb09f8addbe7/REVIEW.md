# Review of the marked supersemicircle transition

## Correctness (PASS)

The center containment event is exactly the absence of a circular gap of length at least \(\pi\). The joint minimum/maximum order-statistic density is explicit, the unwanted internal-gap events have disjoint Dirichlet volumes, and both conditional endpoint density limits follow from \(L^1\) convergence. The nearest-facet identification relies on the explicit distance of a circle chord at gap \(g\), namely \(|\cos(g/2)|\), and vanishing probabilities for competing macroscopic internal gaps. The advertised clearance limit is a continuous image of the endpoint marks. The marked endpoint convergence is total variation, but clearance convergence is stated only weakly to avoid incorrectly equating laws supported on different curved surfaces. A separate numerical script checks the finite identity and convergence. Unproved uniformity in moving \(c\) is not claimed.

## Originality (PASS, with specified historical-priority risk)

Wendel's full-circle formula is classical; Jewell–Romano's arbitrary-distribution scalar coverage and disc-inclusion identities are stronger than the scalar normalization here. The scalar probability and scalar clearance are therefore explicitly not claimed as original. The single claim being assessed is the joint conditional **endpoint-witness geometry**, its two total-variation limits and the identity of the unique closest boundary facet under rare and critical conditioning. The full text of Jewell–Romano was unavailable, and this is a residual historical-priority risk; an inaccessible plausible paper is not taken as proof of originality. Reviewed published-finding corpus research on marked Wendel laws studies complete centrally symmetric ovals conditioned on *missing* their centers, not an incomplete arc conditioned on *capturing* its center. Searches and available claims did not identify a broader marked-witness theorem that implies the claim.

## Value (PASS)

The critical length \(\pi\) is the exact deterministic threshold for any positive inclusion probability. The marked law tells which two sample vertices create center inclusion and quantifies the microscopic geometric center clearance, not merely its probability. It gives a complete and dimensionally natural \(1/n\) crossover, with a zero atom and explicit nontrivial positive density, matching the fixed-sample Dirichlet opening on the appropriate rare scale. These are boundary-geometry phenomena useful for understanding near-degenerate random convex hulls.

## Limits

Independent verification is not performed. Possible detailed prior treatments of the marked arc-supported case, especially in the 1982 work, remain a priority risk. The circle radius is one, sampling is angular-uniform and on a connected arc, and the limits do not cover irregular densities or a parameter \(c\) diverging with sample size.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
