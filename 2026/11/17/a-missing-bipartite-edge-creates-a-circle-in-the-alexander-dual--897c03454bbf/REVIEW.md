# Scientific assessment: one-edge defects in bipartite three-cut complexes

## Correctness — PASS

The proof starts from the exact combinatorial characterization of disconnected triples after one edge deletion. Its dual decomposition is equality of abstract simplicial complexes, not a conjectural homotopy comparison. The use of integral Mayer--Vietoris requires injectivity on third homology for the intersection-to-piece map, checked directly by the injection of graph cycle lattices under induced inclusions. Two-connectivity, the dimension bound and integral homological Whitehead then prove the stated wedge type of the Alexander dual. Integral combinatorial Alexander duality proves, without torsion ambiguity, the two homology groups of the original cut complex. Independent finite enumerations and modular boundary-rank calculations agree for six cases, at primes 1009 and 10007. Mod-prime checks alone do not prove an integral theorem; the symbolic proof supplies that step.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The most relevant primary literature was inspected in its textual sections containing actual theorems. Bayer et al., Theorem 7.4, classify intact complete bipartite graphs, not single-edge deletions. Shen et al. (arXiv:2512.12933, Proposition 3.3) prove whether a deleted edge changes a three-cut complex, not its topology. Carnero Bravo (arXiv:2602.21427, complete-multipartite section) addresses total cut complexes of different graphs. The closest related published records for crown graphs remove an entire perfect matching, and the dissociation-complex records study a different simplicial construction. Statement-level comparisons under edge-defect and Alexander-dual aliases reveal no theorem implying the present two-degree integral homology plus dual homotopy classification. Residual risk: an equivalent statement may occur in unindexed or differently named combinatorial-topology literature. No claim of exhaustive publication coverage or externally certified novelty is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete infinite two-parameter classification at the first nontrivial edge-deletion boundary of the classical bipartite cut-complex family. The local graph modification produces a qualitatively different topology: a new circle appears in the dual and a new top-dimensional integral class appears in the cut complex, while precisely \((a-2)(b-2)\) lower-degree classes disappear. The proof identifies the controlling link/cycle-space mechanism and provides a family of pure, nonshellable complexes. These effects are not obtained merely by substituting parameters into the intact-graph theorem. The finding does not assert a homotopy decomposition of the original cut complex.
