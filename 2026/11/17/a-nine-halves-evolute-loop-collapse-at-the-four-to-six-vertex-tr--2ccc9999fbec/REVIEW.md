# Mathematical review

## Correctness
The curvature-radius derivative factors as an explicit quadratic in cosine, giving an exhaustive root count in the strictly convex domain. The evolute coordinates are exact; an even/odd split, a one-turning-point monotonicity comparison, and the derivative-angle identity show the small loop has no hidden self-crossings. The area coefficient follows from a \(C^1\) local blow-up and direct polynomial integration. The standalone computation is a check, not the proof.

## Originality
The closest classical sources establish lower vertex bounds, zero-count principles or general evolute/equilibrium topology. The full open-access 2021 treatment of plane convex equilibria and Musin's primary manuscript were compared by the mathematical objects, statements, and implications; neither provides the explicit mixed \(2,3\)-harmonic local loop, its closing point or its nine-halves area constant. Other indexed records treat Wigner caustics, which are different envelopes. A definitive historical priority claim is not made; the original 2006 survey was available in bibliographic/abstract form, and the separate 2021 article was read in its full HTML text.

## Value
The four-vertex theorem is qualitative. The finding identifies a concrete birth/death mechanism for two extra curvature extrema, locates the associated three-cusp evolute loop exactly, and measures its disappearance through a sharp noninteger exponent and coefficient. The chosen low Fourier harmonics form a natural minimal mixed-frequency test family and the loop area is a geometric invariant, not an arbitrary numerical sample.

## Scientific risks and limits
The loop conclusion requires fixed \(b>0\) and sufficiently small positive \(6b-a\). The model is not claimed generic in all multi-parameter oval spaces; unrelated prior singularity theory could classify its local germ without reproducing its exact normalized area coefficient. Numerical tests do not certify novelty or generality. Independent checking is outstanding.
