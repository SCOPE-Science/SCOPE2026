# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The result concerns the unique perimeter-Euclidean median, not the vertex Fermat point or an intrinsic boundary-path median. The edge calculus gives the exact \(G(h)\) displayed in `RESULT.md`; symmetry and strict convexity make its sign sufficient for global center comparison. The checker derives the analytic derivative symbolically, covers the entire rational interval by one hundred outward-rounded interval enclosures, and confirms the opposite endpoint signs. It also evaluates the original segment integrals independently. The certified claim is local uniqueness and crossing in \((7/10,4/5)\); it does not extend to all isosceles shapes.

## Originality — PASS, provisionally

Zhang–Carlsson (2014, arXiv:1403.3715v1) is the closest primary source. Its full eleven-page paper describes the general Euclidean perimeter Weber objective and numerical optimization, but not the present coincidence with the Spieker center or a non-equilateral simple crossing. The Spieker center itself and generic median convexity are old. Fekete–Mitchell–Beurer (2005) chiefly use the \(\ell_1\) metric, while Carmi–Har-Peled–Katz (2005) address uniform interior demand; their complete articles were not inspected. Searches of equivalent terminology and research-record results yielded no direct matching statement. An implicit derivation from the general formula or specialized triangle-center work remains a real priority risk; the finite search is not a uniqueness guarantee.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a shape-classification result about equality of two classical, conceptually distinct perimeter centers: the linear arclength centroid and the nonlinear Euclidean distance median. An isolated, non-equilateral coincidence with an accompanying strict order reversal shows why regularity-based intuition fails. The exact transcendental root and positive-derivative certificate give a reproducible geometric threshold, not a purely experimental example.

## Limitations

Only the isolated shape and reversal on the stated interval are established. No claim is made about other possible coincidences or scalene triangles, full historical novelty, or independent validation. The external papers are credited for their actual general formulas and definitions, not recharacterized as having proved this crossing.
