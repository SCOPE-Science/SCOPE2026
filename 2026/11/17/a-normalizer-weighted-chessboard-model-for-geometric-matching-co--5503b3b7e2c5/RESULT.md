# A normalizer-weighted chessboard model for geometric matching-complex fixed spaces

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite group, acting separately on nonempty finite sets \(R\) and \(C\). Let \(K_{R,C}\) be the complete bipartite graph with those shores, and \(\mathcal M(K_{R,C})\) its matching simplicial complex. For each conjugacy class \([H]\) of subgroups of \(G\), let \(a_H\) and \(b_H\) count the row and column orbits isomorphic, as \(G\)-sets, to \(G/H\), and let \(w_H=|N_G(H)/H|\). Let \(wK_{a,b}\) denote the bipartite multigraph obtained by replacing every edge of \(K_{a,b}\) by \(w\) distinct parallel edges, whose matching complex treats those edges as distinct simplicial vertices.

**Theorem.** There is a PL homeomorphism, induced by the geometric barycenters of eligible edge orbits,
\[
\left|\mathcal M(K_{R,C})\right|^G\ \cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\
\left|\mathop{\ast}_{[H]:\,a_H b_H>0}\mathcal M(w_H K_{a_H,b_H})\right|.
\]
The right side is interpreted as the empty geometric space when there is no contributing orbit type. In particular, for any subgroup \(J\le G\) the same formula applied to the restricted \(J\)-actions computes \(|\mathcal M(K_{R,C})|^J\). The theorem makes no claim of a canonical residual-normalizer action on the chosen labeled multigraph models.

**Torsion consequence.** For every finite group \(G\) of order at least two, let each shore consist of five disjoint regular \(G\)-orbits. Then the corresponding geometric fixed space is PL homeomorphic to \(|\mathcal M(|G|K_{5,5})|\). There are simplicial maps
\[
\mathcal M(K_{5,5})\xrightarrow{s}\mathcal M(|G|K_{5,5})
\xrightarrow{p}\mathcal M(K_{5,5}),\qquad p\circ s=\mathrm{id}.
\]
Consequently, \(\widetilde H_2(|\mathcal M(K_{R,C})|^G;\mathbb Z)\) has a **direct summand \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\)**: the summand comes from the established \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal M(K_{5,5});\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\), not from a new calculation of that ordinary chessboard complex.

For a single orbit type with exactly two row orbits and two column orbits and normalizer weight \(w\), the fixed complex is the disjoint union of two copies of the graph \(K_{w,w}\). Thus for \(w\ge2\) it has two connected components, each homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{(w-1)^2}S^1\); for \(w=1\) both components are contractible. This is an exact elementary illustration of the classification.

## Assumptions and scope
A matching is a set of bipartite edges without repeated endpoints; the empty matching is the empty simplex, not a geometric point. The graph has all \(|R||C|\) possible edges, and the group acts *diagonally* on its edge set by \(g(r,c)=(gr,gc)\), preserving each shore. There is no assumption of freeness, faithfulness, abelianness, or transitivity. Row and column orbit multiplicities \(a_H,b_H\) are finite nonnegative integers. A class is omitted when \(a_H b_H=0\). Each \(w_H\ge1\), since \(H\) is contained in its normalizer. All homotopy and homology groups are ordinary integral invariants of geometric fixed spaces, not invariants of sets of individually fixed simplicial vertices.

## Proof
**1. Fixed points and barycentric edge orbits.** In the geometric realization of any finite simplicial complex, a point has unique barycentric coordinates supported on a simplex. A point fixed by all of \(G\) has a union of edge orbits as support, with equal coefficients on each edge in a given orbit. An edge orbit \(O\subseteq R\times C\) is individually eligible precisely when its edges are pairwise disjoint. The barycenter \(b_O=|O|^{-1}\sum_{e\in O}e\) is then a fixed point. Sending the vertex corresponding to each eligible orbit to \(b_O\), and extending affinely across compatible collections, is a bijective PL homeomorphism from the simplicial orbit complex to the geometric fixed space. Indeed, supports of different orbits are disjoint edge sets, so barycentric coefficients uniquely recover the orbit weights.

**2. Eligible orbits are equivariant bijections.** Let an eligible orbit \(O\) contain \((r,c)\). Its first and second projections are the full transitive \(G\)-orbits \(G r\) and \(G c\), because \(O\) is itself an orbit. Since no matching repeats a vertex, both projections are injective. Thus \(O\) is the graph of a bijection \(f:G r\to G c\); invariance says \(f(gr')=g f(r')\). Conversely, the graph of any equivariant bijection between transitive \(G\)-orbits is a single eligible edge orbit. Such bijections exist exactly when the two stabilizers are conjugate. If the transitive sets have type \(G/H\), their number is \(|\operatorname{Aut}_G(G/H)|=|N_G(H)/H|=w_H\): an equivariant map is determined by the image of \(H\), and the possible images are cosets fixed by \(H\), namely normalizer cosets.

**3. Compatibility becomes a colored matching.** Every eligible orbit between a given row orbit and column orbit uses *all* vertices of both. Therefore two distinct eligible orbits can coexist in a matching exactly when they involve different row orbits **and** different column orbits. Hence the eligible orbits of each stabilizer type form the simplicial matching complex of the complete bipartite multigraph with \(w_H\) parallel edges per pair of row and column orbits. Different stabilizer types have disjoint row and column supports, so their orbit complexes combine by simplicial join. This proves the theorem, including the case of no admissible edge orbits.

**4. Regular-action torsion.** For a regular \(G\)-set, the stabilizer is trivial and \(w_{\{1\}}=|G|\). Five copies on each shore give the multigraph \(|G|K_{5,5}\). The simplicial map \(p\) sends every parallel edge to the underlying uncolored edge; it is well-defined because no matching contains two parallel edges. Choosing one particular color on every board square defines a simplicial section \(s\). Their composite is the identity. Thus \(s_*\) embeds integral homology as a split direct summand in every degree. The previously proved chessboard calculation \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal M(K_{5,5});\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\) gives the claimed split torsion in degree two.

**5. Two-by-two block.** For two row and two column orbit indices, a pair of compatible edges must use opposite corners of the board. There are precisely two opposite-corner patterns, each giving a complete bipartite graph \(K_{w,w}\) in the one-dimensional matching complex, with no connection between the patterns. Its cycle rank is \(w^2-2w+1=(w-1)^2\).

## Verification
The standard-library program `orbit_chessboard_verify.py` independently generates group actions, edge orbits, equivariant bijections, invariant matching supports, and proposed orbit-type faces. It compares the actual matching condition against the distinct-orbit matching model for **every subset of eligible orbits** in eight finite test actions: the trivial group; cyclic groups of orders two and three with repeated free and fixed orbits; and \(S_3\) with natural and regular actions. The largest full face-set check has twelve eligible orbit vertices, so it inspects all \(2^{12}\) subsets in that case.

A separate exhaustive construction of matchings for the five-by-five parallel-edge board with multiplicity two finds \((1,50,800,4800,9600,3840)\) faces of sizes zero through five (total 19,091), matching \(\binom 5k^2 k!2^k\) for each \(0\le k\le5\). On every enumerated matching it checks the forget-color/choose-color section-retraction identity. The replay ends with `ALL_ORBIT_CHESSBOARD_FIXED_CHECKS_PASS`. These finite checks do not establish the theorem for arbitrary groups, and they do not independently recompute Jonsson's integral torsion theorem; both are supplied by the mathematical proof and cited literature.

## Relationship to prior work
Björner–Lovász–Vrećica–Živaljević (1994) introduce and analyze matching/chessboard complexes and their connectivity. Shareshian–Wachs (2004 preprint) and Jonsson (2012 public preprint of an earlier journal paper) establish, among other results, the ordinary integral three-torsion used here. Bayer–Jelić Milutinović–Vega (2023, Proposition 11) establish a nonequivariant homotopy splitting when a parallel edge is added to an arbitrary graph; that is consistent with, but not an all-group geometric fixed-point classification of, the present orbit multigraphs. Jojić–Panina–Živaljević (2022, Remark 6.2) provide a weighted fixed-face description for a different multiple-chessboard action with row capacities. An indexed 2026 result, *Transitive bipartite symmetry forces discrete geometric matching-complex fixed spaces*, proves the separately-transitive special case; the present theorem is its genuinely nontransitive multiblock extension. These are statements of mathematical coverage, not claims of definitive publication priority.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes a shore-preserving action on a **complete** bipartite graph. It does not address actions exchanging shores, arbitrary missing board squares, generalized matching capacities, residual normalizer representations, or an equivariant homeomorphism of the original entire complexes. The PL identification requires choices of representatives and labels for the multigraph decomposition. The retraction proves a direct \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\) summand, not a complete computation of the remaining integral homology of the parallel-edge board. An equivalent formulation in uninspected group-action or graph-matching literature cannot be excluded without independent priority review.
