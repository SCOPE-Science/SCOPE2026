# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For each of the four explicitly defined bidirected one-leaf cycles, the complete face sets are reconstructed twice, with agreement. The deterministic pivot certificates yield full Forman-acyclic Hasse matchings and precisely stated critical cells. An integral signed Morse-chain reduction gives zero boundaries. The minimal CW models then split as the asserted wedges; dimensions 1, 2, and 3 are treated with the appropriate fundamental-group or Hurewicz argument. No infinite generalization is claimed.

## Originality
PASS subject to ordinary bibliographic uncertainty. The closest primary paper, Caputi–Collari–Smith (arXiv:2601.05670), studies bidirected polygons and a different T-operation, but not an additional two-way pendant leaf. The earlier framework paper (arXiv:2208.04656) similarly does not give these types. Published related calculations of unmodified or one-arc-punctured polygons do not determine the new cycle-plus-leaf incidence complexes; neither does a coherent-orientation sunlet classification. Exact claim/alias searches against available research records did not identify an equivalent result. Absence from search results does not prove absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The four cases are the first representatives of all four cycle-length residues modulo four, the natural periodicity already used in the motivating bidirected-polygon literature. They give a complete initial integral homotopy comparison under the smallest strongly connected pendant perturbation, including changes in connectivity, circle rank and mixed-dimensional sphere multiplicities. The full homotopy types serve as exact low-order boundary data for extension of bidirected-polygon topology, beyond a list of Betti numbers.

## Closest literature and limitations
The original bidirected polygon retains every cycle vertex at in/out degree two, while the faulted version introduces a new degree-one vertex with a bidirected 2-cycle and raises the distinguished cycle vertex's in/out degrees. This is not an arc deletion, and it is not the suspension T-operation described by Caputi–Collari–Smith. The result is only for cycle lengths three, four, five and six. No additional lengths, representations, or stability claims were proved. An unindexed equivalent computation remains possible.
