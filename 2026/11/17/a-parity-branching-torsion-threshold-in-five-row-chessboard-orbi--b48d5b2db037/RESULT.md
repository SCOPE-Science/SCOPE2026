# A parity-branching torsion threshold in five-row chessboard orbit spaces

## Finding

Let \(\Delta_{5,n}\) be the ordinary chessboard simplicial complex of nonattacking rook placements on five *labeled* rows and \(n\) labeled columns. Let \(A_5\) permute the rows, and let \(Y_n=|\Delta_{5,n}|/A_5\) denote the **ordinary geometric orbit space**, not a homotopy quotient. Write \(M(\mathbb Z/d,3)=S^3\cup_d e^4\) for the Moore space with degree-\(d\) attaching map. For every integer \(1\le n\le8\), the following is the complete integral homotopy classification:

\[
Y_n\simeq
\begin{cases}
\{\mathrm{point}\},&1\le n\le3,\\
S^3,&n=4,\\
\bigvee^4 S^3,&n=5,\\
\bigvee^9 S^3\vee S^4,&n=6,\\
\bigvee^{14}S^3\vee M(\mathbb Z/3,3)\vee\bigvee^6S^4,&n=7,\\
\bigvee^{14}S^3\vee\bigvee^6 M(\mathbb Z/3,3)\vee M(\mathbb Z/6,3)\vee\bigvee^{21}S^4,&n=8.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, \(n=7\) is the first width exhibiting integral torsion in the alternating-row quotient, and at \(n=8\) both two-primary and three-primary torsion occur in third homology:
\[
\operatorname{tors}H_3(Y_8;\mathbb Z)\cong(\mathbb Z/3)^6\oplus\mathbb Z/6.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All quotients are taken in topological spaces after geometric realization. A rook face is a partial injection from occupied columns to the set of five rows. Its vertices are ordered by increasing column index to orient each cell. There are no identifications of two distinct vertices within any group-translated simplex: a row permutation carrying a rook face to itself fixes every occupied row, because its columns are distinct. Therefore the orbit-face interiors form a regular-face CW quotient with ordinary integral incidence coefficients given by signed deletions.

For placements of \(k\) rooks, \(A_5\) has one orbit on row assignments if \(k\le3\), and two if \(k=4,5\). The latter are distinguished by the parity of the completed five-letter row permutation. Consequently the orbit-cell counts in dimensions zero through four, for \(n\ge5\), are
\[
\left(n,\binom n2,\binom n3,2\binom n4,2\binom n5\right).
\]
The full row-symmetry quotient \(|\Delta_{5,n}|/S_5\) is the four-skeleton of the standard simplex on the \(n\) columns. The alternating quotient is the natural parity double over its three- and four-dimensional cell interiors, while lower cells are unsplit. These observations motivate the precise torsion threshold.

## Proof

**1. Orbit cellular chains.** For each four-subset \(S\subset[n]\), let \(e_S^+\) and \(e_S^-\) denote the two degree-three orbit cells, and for each five-subset \(T\subset[n]\) let \(f_T^+\), \(f_T^-\) denote the two degree-four orbit cells. The lower-dimensional cells are the usual simplex cells on column subsets. Set \(u_S=e_S^+\), \(v_S=e_S^--e_S^+\); this is a unimodular change of basis. The degree-three cellular boundary satisfies
\[
\partial_3 u_S=\partial_{\mathrm{simp}}S,\qquad \partial_3 v_S=0.
\]
For \(T=\{t_1<\cdots<t_5\}\), the parity of the completed row permutation changes by \((-1)^{5-i}\) after deleting the \(i\)-th rook. Hence, with \(T_i=T\setminus\{t_i\}\),
\[
\partial_4 f_T^+=\sum_{i=1}^5(-1)^{i-1}u_{T_i}-v_{T_2}-v_{T_4},
\]
\[
\partial_4 f_T^-=\sum_{i=1}^5(-1)^{i-1}u_{T_i}+v_{T_1}+v_{T_3}+v_{T_5}.
\]
This proves the boundaries without assuming a free group action.

**2. Reduced integral presentation.** Denote by \(V_n\), \(W_n\), and \(Z_n\) the free abelian groups on respectively the four-, five-, and six-subsets of \([n]\). The difference \(f_T^--f_T^+\) has boundary \(\sum_i v_{T_i}\), giving a map \(U:W_n\to V_n\) with unsigned five-to-four incidence. The \(u\)-part of the other top-cell boundary is the ordinary simplicial boundary \(\partial:W_n\to\mathbb Z^{\binom n4}\). By exactness of the augmented full-simplex chain complex, every relation among those simplicial boundaries is generated by the boundaries of six-subsets. Lifting such a relation through the \(f^+\)-cells gives the map \(R:Z_n\to V_n\): for \(Q=\{q_1<\cdots<q_6\}\),
\[
R(Q)=-\sum_{j=1}^6(-1)^{j-1}
\left(v_{(Q\setminus\{q_j\})\setminus\{s_2\}}+v_{(Q\setminus\{q_j\})\setminus\{s_4\}}\right),
\]
where \(s_1<\cdots<s_5\) are the elements of \(Q\setminus\{q_j\}\). Thus the actual third homology has the **integral** presentation
\[
H_3(Y_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\operatorname{coker}\left([U\ R]:W_n\oplus Z_n\longrightarrow V_n\right),\quad n\ge5.
\]
This is a presentation of the homology lattice, not merely a rational rank identity.

**3. Exact finite Smith calculation.** The standalone file `a5_quotient_verify.py` builds the \(A_5\)-orbits from all sixty even row permutations, constructs full cellular boundaries from oriented rook faces, verifies \(\partial^2=0\), constructs \([U\ R]\) independently from its subset formula, and computes its integer Smith diagonal. The resulting complete nonunit diagonals, free third-homology ranks, and fourth-homology ranks are:

| \(n\) | \(\operatorname{rank}H_3\) | Nonunit Smith entries of \([U\ R]\) | \(\operatorname{rank}H_4\) |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 5 | 4 | none | 0 |
| 6 | 9 | none | 1 |
| 7 | 14 | \(3\) | 6 |
| 8 | 14 | \(3,3,3,3,3,3,6\) | 21 |

For \(n=4\), the two degree-three orbit cells share the same degree-two boundary, giving \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z\). For \(1\le n\le3\), the quotient is precisely a simplex. The lower reduced homology groups vanish for \(n\ge4\), because the two-skeleton is the standard two-skeleton of a simplex and the degree-three cellular boundaries surject onto its two-cycle lattice. The fourth homology is free; its rank follows from the exact cellular ranks or Euler characteristic.

**4. Homotopy, not just homology.** For \(n\ge4\), the quotient contains the full standard two-skeleton on the columns, so it is simply connected. By the cellular calculation \(H_2=0\), the degree-two Hurewicz theorem makes it two-connected. A simply connected two-dimensional CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres; the degree-three attachments whose boundaries generate its second homology primitively cancel that wedge, leaving a three-dimensional wedge of three-spheres. The remaining degree-four attaching maps are elements of the free group \(\pi_3(\bigvee S^3)\cong\bigoplus\mathbb Z\). Integral change of bases of degree-three and degree-four cells realizes the Smith diagonal by homotopy equivalences. Therefore the nonunit invariant factors give exactly the Moore summands stated above, the zero columns give four-spheres, and the unhit generators give three-spheres. This proves the claimed finite-width homotopy classifications.

## Verification

Execute `python3 a5_quotient_verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy 1.14 or a compatible version. It recomputes the finite parity orbits, quotient cellular boundaries and their zero compositions, independently compares cellular ranks modulo primes \(2,3,5,101\), and computes the exact integral Smith invariants of the reduced presentation for every \(4\le n\le8\). The checked output is given in `VERIFICATION.md`. The symbolic derivation of the presentation and the homotopy decomposition is supplied above; finite checks alone do not establish any statement for \(n>8\).

## Relationship to prior work

Björner, Lovász, Vrećica and Živaljević introduced and studied the chessboard complexes and their connectivity, including the partial-injection interpretation. Reiner and Roberts gave the rational symmetric-group homology representation of the original labeled chessboard complexes (their Theorem 1.3); this does not determine the integral homology of a geometric orbit space under a nonfree action. Jojić, Vrećica and Živaljević define *symmetrized* multiple chessboard complexes by taking **unions** of row-permuted subcomplexes (Definition 2.1), rather than taking orbit-space quotients. Limonchenko and Yu calculate integral homotopy types and torsion of underlying generalized chessboard complexes, again not the row-alternating quotient. The parity-branching orbit model, its reduced unsigned-incidence relations and the resulting width-seven/width-eight orbit-space torsion are the new statements tested here.

## Limitations

This classification covers exactly the integer widths \(1\le n\le8\). It asserts neither a uniform closed form for larger widths nor any result about Borel homotopy quotients. The checker depends on exact integer Smith reduction by SymPy; its output is computational evidence alongside the explicit chain-model proof. This is not independent validation. Despite searches of mathematical literature, equivalent unindexed work is a residual publication-priority risk.
