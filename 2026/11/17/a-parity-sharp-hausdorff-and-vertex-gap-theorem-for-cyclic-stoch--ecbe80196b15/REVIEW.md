# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The projection formula is exact because the cyclic shifts are pairwise Frobenius-orthogonal and the orthogonal coefficients of a doubly stochastic matrix are nonnegative with sum one. The residual-norm identity reduces the extremum to minimizing an integer displacement-histogram square sum. The modular sum constraint rules out all-distinct displacements at even orders and a single collision at odd orders. Explicit permutations attain both sharp levels; a Hilbert-space variance identity and a circulant-difference rigidity lemma exclude nonvertex farthest points. The finite checks independently reproduce the first two discrete levels through order eight and the explicit constructions through order \(1000\).

## Originality
**PASS, with historical priority not independently certified.** The nearest circulant-matrix projection and the cyclic-group complete-mapping existence/obstruction are known mathematics. Rajchel-Mieldzioć et al. (arXiv:2101.11288) treat circulant unistochastic spectra, Baumeister et al. (arXiv:1109.0191) cyclic permutation polytopes, Salahub and Uhlmann (arXiv:2310.15375) general structured approximation, and Bouthat et al. (arXiv:2310.14043) Birkhoff Chebyshev radii. Inspected discussions do not give the present exact *two-polytope Hausdorff distance*, rigidity locus, or second-largest vertex-distance law. The broad orbit-polytope and Latin-square literatures remain residual priority risks; nonmatching phrasing is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Circulant doubly stochastic matrices are precisely translation-invariant Markov kernels on a finite cycle, a natural low-dimensional approximation family within all bistochastic matrices. This result quantifies the worst Frobenius approximation loss in every order and explains an arithmetic parity defect by the impossibility of a cyclic Latin transversal at even orders. The first discrete stability gap and exact farthest-point locus are motivated structural refinements, not a parameter substitution or arbitrary slice. The pointwise projection component is explicitly credited as known rather than used as the sole novelty claim.

The theorem uses only Frobenius geometry. The second distance level is a discrete permutation-vertex statement. Counts of complete or near-complete cyclic mappings are not asserted. Unindexed historical coverage is possible; an independent mathematical audit is outstanding.
