# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Deletion of undamaged shore vertices is a strong simplicial collapse because each such vertex has at least the neighbors of a defective endpoint on its shore. The resulting path forcing argument excludes every full transversal; the cycle forcing argument leaves exactly two transversals per cycle. The two-nerve Alexander-duality proof yields one integral homology generator and no torsion. The full two-skeleton (or the explicit triangle base) proves simple connectivity, and the Hurewicz–Whitehead step upgrades homology to a sphere. Independently reconstructed simplicial boundary ranks agree in 57 finite tests. This finite check alone is not an all-size proof.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to literature-indexing risk.** The precise matching-defect/color-incidence statement and its consequences were compared with the unmodified multipartite classification and published clique-free shellability criteria, relevant indexed research claims. Published intact-graph formulas do not imply an edge-deletion dimension law. Previous forest-deletion formulas confined to one shore pair do not include a simple nontrivial color cycle. Search failures are not uniqueness proofs. The analysis does not claim newness of nerve methods, duality, or simplicial folding.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This natural complete classification on all degree-at-most-two color-incidence matching defects distinguishes contractible path cases from nonpure sphere cases, including arbitrarily large dimension increases. It explains why shore sizes disappear under compression and why every independent color cycle contributes an additional dimension shift. The bipartite one-shore-pair forest case alone cannot predict this behavior.
