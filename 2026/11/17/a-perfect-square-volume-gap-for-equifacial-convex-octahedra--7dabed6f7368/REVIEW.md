# Scientific review: exact volume minima for equifacial convex octahedra

## Correctness — PASS
The stated domain is acute scalene triangles and combinatorial six-vertex convex octahedra only. The published complete four-arrangement classification [2] supplies exhaustion and uniqueness for each geometric realization; the original construction [1] supplies all eight supporting facets. Direct facet-determinant formulas give the twisted volume \(2r^2(d+e)/3\) and coordinate volume \(4\sqrt{XYZ}/3\). The exact factorization
\[
 V_{\rm tw}^2-V_{\rm co}^2=\tfrac49(d-e)^2(r^2-2de)^2
\]
is checked algebraically in the proof and rationally by a standalone checker. Positivity is unconditional on the specified domain. With scalene faces, equality forces \(r^2=2de\) and the equatorial side to be largest, yielding exactly the parameterized equality curve. Symmetry and the balance of equal-area facet normals prove that the same center is the unique maximal inball center; volume and inradius differ by the same fixed factor \(8\Delta/3\). The finite matching census is exhaustive but does not substitute for the cited geometric rigidity classification.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
The closest source [2] classifies the four equifacial octahedral realizations by cube-edge-color arrangements, without a volume comparison, minimum theorem, perfect-square identity, tangential inradius classification, or exceptional equal-volume family. The original paper [1] constructs a universal example and determines minimum face count; neither conclusion implies an extremal volume ordering. Equivalent and broader formulations involving scalenohedra, equal-area facet solids, and fixed-face octahedra were searched in mathematical records and public literature. No directly covering statement was identified. Unknown older crystallographic treatments remain a priority risk, not a logical disproof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Equal triangular faces fix the surface area, but the four rigid six-vertex shapes generally have different volumes. The theorem identifies their sharp global minimum throughout the natural acute scalene domain and shows that the minimizer is usually unique, except for a nontrivial one-parameter family with two noncongruent minimizers. The same result exactly orders their inradii relative to the minimum, with a perfect-square deficiency certificate. The explicit acute triangle \((\sqrt5,2\sqrt2,3)\) yields two differently arranged polyhedra having identical surface area \(24\), volume \(8\), and inradius \(1\), making the equality phenomenon geometrically concrete. It is not a count of known arrangements or a tiny numerical improvement.
