# A positive high-frequency Dirichlet law for nonreversible periodic cooperative flows

## Finding

For an irreducible cooperative linear differential equation with a fixed, possibly nonreversible mixing matrix and periodic *diagonal* growth modulation, the first nonzero correction to the rapid-environment Floquet exponent is a **positive graph Dirichlet energy**. In particular, even for directed mixing networks with three or more habitats and any number of environmental phases, sufficiently short but positive environmental periods increase the leading growth exponent, unless all growth histories have identical temporal fluctuations.

Define \(\Lambda(T)\) as in the next section. The exact expansion is
\[
\Lambda(T)=\lambda_0+T^2\mathcal E+O(T^3),\qquad
\mathcal E=\frac12\int_0^1\sum_{i\ne j}u_iL_{ij}v_j\bigl(H_i(s)-H_j(s)\bigr)^2\,ds.
\]
Its coefficient is zero precisely when all relative local growth profiles are constant in time. Otherwise \(\Lambda'(T)>0\) for every sufficiently small positive period \(T\).

## Assumptions and scope

Fix an integer \(d\ge3\). Let \(L\) be a real \(d\times d\) irreducible Metzler matrix: \(L_{ij}\ge0\) whenever \(i\ne j\), and its directed positive-entry graph is strongly connected. Column sums need not be zero, although conservative migration matrices are included. Fix bounded real piecewise-continuous 1-periodic functions \(r_i:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\), with finitely many discontinuities, and periodic continuation. Consider
\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=A_T(t)x,\qquad A_T(t)=L+\mathrm{diag}\bigl(r_1(t/T),\ldots,r_d(t/T)\bigr),\qquad T>0.
\]
Let \(P_T\) be its principal fundamental matrix over \([0,T]\) and define \(\Lambda(T)=T^{-1}\log\rho(P_T)\). For \(T>0\), irreducible positivity makes its Perron multiplier simple. Put \(\bar r_i=\int_0^1r_i(s)\,ds\) and \(M=L+\mathrm{diag}(\bar r_i)\). Its simple dominant real eigenvalue is \(\lambda_0\), and its positive left and right eigenvectors are \(u,v\), normalized by \(u^\top v=1\). For each \(i\), define the unique absolutely continuous periodic function \(H_i\) satisfying \(H_i'=r_i-\bar r_i\) almost everywhere and \(\int_0^1 H_i=0\). Write \(H(s)=\mathrm{diag}(H_i(s))\) and \(V(s)=H'(s)\).

The theorem covers all such finite networks and waveforms; its strictness criterion is equivalent to the existence of indices \(i,j\) for which \(r_i-r_j\) is not essentially constant. The conclusion concerns \(T\downarrow0\) with all other parameters fixed; neither stochastic environments nor time-dependent off-diagonal dispersal are covered.

## Proof

**Analytic Floquet branch.** In rescaled phase time \(s=t/T\), the fundamental matrix is the time-ordered exponential generated by \(T(M+V(s))\). Its Dyson series is analytic in \(T\) near zero. Consequently \((P_T-I)/T\), with its removable value \(M\) at zero, is analytic. The simple Perron eigenvalue of \(M\) therefore continues analytically near \(T=0\); so does \(\Lambda(T)=T^{-1}\log\rho(P_T)\). A positive 1-periodic Floquet vector \(p_T(s)\) admits uniform power-series expansions in \(T\), and obeys
\[
 p_T'(s)=T\bigl(M+V(s)-\Lambda(T)I\bigr)p_T(s).
\]
Normalize it by \(\int_0^1u^\top p_T(s)\,ds=1\). Expand \(p_T=v+Tp_1+T^2p_2+O(T^3)\) and \(\Lambda(T)=\lambda_0+T\lambda_1+T^2\lambda_2+O(T^3)\). All the following equalities hold almost everywhere in \(s\); periodic integration remains valid with the stated finite jumps.

**First two orders.** Comparing the coefficient of \(T\) gives \(p_1'=Vv\); hence \(p_1=Hv+c\) for a constant vector \(c\). Average the equation at order \(T^2\) and apply \(u^\top\). The contribution from \((M-\lambda_0I)p_1\) vanishes, as does that from \(Vc\) because \(\int_0^1V=0\). Moreover, \(\int_0^1VH=\tfrac12[H^2]_0^1=0\), so \(\lambda_1=0\). Averaging the full vector equation then shows \((M-\lambda_0I)c=0\). The normalization and \(\int H=0\) force \(c=0\). Thus \(p_1=Hv\).

At order \(T^2\),
\[
 p_2'=(M-\lambda_0I)Hv+VHv=[M,H]v+\tfrac12(H^2)'v.
\]
Because \(\int_0^1[M,H]v=0\), let \(J\) be any periodic matrix primitive satisfying \(J'=[M,H]\). Then \(p_2=Jv+\tfrac12H^2v+c_2\), where \(c_2\) is constant.

**Second-order coefficient.** Average the equation at order \(T^3\) against \(u^\top\). The \((M-\lambda_0 I)p_2\) term vanishes. Since \(\int V=0\) and \(\int VH^2=\tfrac13[H^3]_0^1=0\), only \(VJv\) remains:
\[
\lambda_2=\int_0^1u^\top H'Jv\,ds
=-\int_0^1u^\top H[M,H]v\,ds
=\int_0^1u^\top(\lambda_0H^2-HMH)v\,ds.
\]
Here the integration by parts has no boundary term because both \(H\) and \(J\) are periodic. Using \(Mv=\lambda_0v\) and \(u^\top M=\lambda_0u^\top\) to symmetrize the last expression, one obtains
\[
\lambda_2=\frac12\int_0^1\sum_{i,j}u_iM_{ij}v_j\bigl(H_i(s)-H_j(s)\bigr)^2\,ds.
\]
The diagonal terms vanish and the off-diagonal entries of \(M\) are exactly \(L_{ij}\), proving the displayed formula \(\mathcal E=\lambda_2\). No detailed-balance identity is used.

**Strictness and boundary.** Each term of the energy is nonnegative because \(u_i,v_j>0\) and \(L_{ij}\ge0\) for distinct indices. Equality forces \(H_i=H_j\) almost everywhere on every directed edge of positive weight; strong connectivity forces all \(H_i\) to agree. Differentiation then says \(r_i-r_j\) is constant almost everywhere for all pairs. Conversely this condition makes all centered functions \(H_i\) identical. When this holds, write \(A_T(t)=M+c(t/T)I\) with a common zero-mean scalar \(c\); its monodromy is exactly \(e^{TM}\), so \(\Lambda(T)=\lambda_0\) for every positive \(T\). In the nonconstant-relative case \(\mathcal E>0\), the analytic expansion gives \(\Lambda'(T)=2\mathcal E T+O(T^2)>0\) for all sufficiently small \(T>0\).

## Verification

The standalone Python checker `checks/check_fast_switching.py` uses exact rational integrals of zero-mean temporal primitives and independently evaluates the resulting graph energy and the unsymmetrized commutator expression. For a directed three-cycle with two equal-duration phases, it establishes \(\mathcal E=1/48\) exactly. For an asymmetric nonreversible directed cycle with three distinct phases, it establishes \(\mathcal E=34/351\) exactly. It also compares direct matrix-exponential Floquet exponents for three small periods in each model. Execution output ends with `FAST_PERIOD_GRAPH_ENERGY_CHECK_OK exact_cases=2 numeric_horizons=6`. Numerical checks test the derived expression, not the all-network asymptotic theorem, which is proved analytically above.

## Relationship to prior work

Monmarché, Schreiber and Strickler (arXiv:2408.11179, first posted August 20, 2024) treat fast and slow periodic/environmental switching in cooperative systems. Their Proposition 1 derives an abstract second-order fast-switching coefficient for **two** environmental matrices, and their Theorem 3 with Proposition 6 proves vanishing of the first-order coefficient for general fixed-dispersal metapopulation systems. Those facts are credited rather than claimed as new. The present theorem supplies an explicit positive Dirichlet formula, strictness criterion and local positive-period monotonicity for arbitrary waveforms and numbers of phases on an irreducible, possibly nonreversible network with \(d\ge3\). In the two-phase specialization, its coefficient can also be extracted from the earlier general formula; that specialization is not asserted novel.

Benaïm, Lobry, Sari and Strickler (arXiv:2311.04706, first posted November 8, 2023) formulate a stronger global monotonicity conjecture for fixed nonsymmetric migration. Their follow-up arXiv:2407.07553 describes a counterexample to this global conjecture. The local small-\(T\) law above is compatible with this failure: the theorem makes **no** statement about monotonicity away from \(T=0\). Katriel (arXiv:2104.01589) and related periodic-eigenvalue work give stronger global results when migration is symmetric, but do not establish the claimed nonsymmetric coefficient for arbitrary periodic diagonal forcing.

## Limitations

The theorem does not cover time-varying off-diagonal interactions, reducible matrices, infinite-dimensional population operators, stochastic modulation, or global monotonicity over all periods. The \(O(T^3)\) constant depends on the fixed network and forcing functions. The high-frequency coefficient is a local asymptotic invariant, not a criterion for eventual survival at arbitrary periods. The literature comparison is based on inspected accessible texts and indexed research records; mathematically equivalent results in unindexed material remain a publication-priority risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
