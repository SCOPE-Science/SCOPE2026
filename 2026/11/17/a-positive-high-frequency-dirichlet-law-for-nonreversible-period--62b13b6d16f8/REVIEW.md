# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses an analytic Floquet branch at zero period and computes the first three perturbative equations. The first-order exponent coefficient is zero; the second-order coefficient is obtained by integration by parts and the left/right Perron eigenvector identities. This yields an explicitly nonnegative off-diagonal graph energy and a sharp equality condition by strong connectivity. Two directed, nonreversible three-patch examples have rational coefficients independently verified by the standalone checker, with six matrix-exponential small-period comparisons. The finite checks are supportive only; the analytic argument establishes the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

The 2024 two-regime Proposition 1 already provides a general second-order expression in that restricted setting, and the same paper proves first-order cancellation for fixed dispersal with arbitrary switching. These known facts are specifically excluded from novelty. Inspected text did not contain the multi-regime, arbitrary-waveform explicit positive graph-Dirichlet coefficient, its equality criterion, or the local strictness theorem for arbitrary nonreversible networks. The 2023 global conjecture is known to fail, so the present statement is explicitly local. An unindexed equivalent formula remains a publication-priority risk, not a proof of independence.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact coefficient resolves the sign and vanishing conditions of the first nonzero rapid-switching correction in the very asymmetric fixed-dispersal setting where global period monotonicity fails. It converts an abstract perturbative coefficient into a directly computable network functional with a sharp physical criterion. Its three-phase example demonstrates the part genuinely outside the earlier two-environment expansion.
