# A rank jump in regular-translation fixed spaces of bipartite graph complexes

## Finding
For a finite set \(V\), let \(\mathcal B(V)\) denote the simplicial complex on the edges of the complete simple graph on \(V\), whose faces are those edge sets that form bipartite graphs (isolated vertices are retained). Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group acting on its own elements by translation, hence simplicially on \(\mathcal B(A)\). Fix an integer \(a\ge1\) and any finite abelian group \(B\) of odd order, including the trivial group. Then
\[
\bigl|\mathcal B(C_{2^a}\times B)\bigr|^{C_{2^a}\times B}\simeq\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{a}\{\ast\},
\qquad
\bigl|\mathcal B(C_{2^a}\times C_2\times B)\bigr|^{C_{2^a}\times C_2\times B}\simeq\bigvee_{j=1}^{a}S^1.
\]
Both equivalences are integral homotopy equivalences. In the second family the fixed space is connected, with fundamental group free of rank \(a\), first integral homology \(\mathbb Z^a\), and no reduced integral homology in other degrees. The first family has exactly \(a\) path components, each contractible. Thus adjoining one independent involution changes a disconnected fixed space into a bouquet of an unbounded number of circles. The odd-order direct factor does not alter either homotopy type.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(C_m\) is a cyclic group of order \(m\). A graph is bipartite if its vertices admit a proper two-coloring; this includes the edgeless graph. Geometric fixed points refer to actual points of the polyhedron of the simplicial complex, not merely simplices fixed vertexwise or invariant chains. The action is the **entire regular translation group**, not an arbitrary subgroup. Set \(P=C_{2^a}\) or \(P=C_{2^a}\times C_2\) as appropriate.

For a simplicial action, group orbits of potential edges that individually form admissible faces are the vertices of a canonical geometric fixed-point complex. Collections of these vertices form faces exactly when the union of their edge orbits is bipartite. Indeed, a fixed point has coordinates constant on each edge orbit, and the rescaling of orbit weights gives a piecewise-linear homeomorphism with this orbit complex. An edge orbit generated by a nonzero difference \(d\in A\) consists of all unordered edges \(\{x,x+d\}\); its label is the inversion class \(\{d,-d\}\).

## Proof
**1. Bipartite Cayley criterion.** Given a set \(S\) of nonzero differences, the union of its translation edge orbits is the Cayley graph with steps \(S\cup(-S)\). Put \(H=\langle S\rangle\). Its connected components are cosets of \(H\). This graph is bipartite if and only if there exists a homomorphism \(\chi:H\to C_2\) satisfying \(\chi(s)=1\) for all \(s\in S\). The reverse implication colors cosets by \(\chi\); for the forward implication, choose the color of the identity and assign to a group element the parity of any step path from the identity. Bipartiteness makes that parity independent of the path, and concatenation makes it a homomorphism. The criterion is independent of the representative \(s\) versus \(-s\).

**2. Eliminating odd factors.** For \(A=P\times B\), where \(|B|\) is odd, any homomorphism from a subgroup of \(A\) to \(C_2\) vanishes on the kernel of its projection to \(P\). Hence it factors through the projected subgroup. By step 1, a nonempty selection of translation edge orbits is admissible exactly when its projected nonzero \(P\)-differences form an admissible selection for \(P\); a difference with zero \(P\)-component never occurs in any admissible nonempty face. On the orbit complex, identify all edge-orbit labels with the same projected inversion class. There is a simplicial retraction onto the representatives with zero \(B\)-component. Its composite with the inclusion is the identity, while its other composite is contiguous to the identity: the union of every simplex with its retracted image is still a simplex. Therefore the orbit complex for \(A\) strongly deformation retracts, after geometric realization, onto that for \(P\). In particular, only the two-primary group \(P\) matters for homotopy.

**3. A cyclic two-primary group.** For \(P=C_{2^a}\), a subgroup with a nonzero homomorphism to \(C_2\) is of the form \(2^kC_{2^a}\), with \(0\le k<a\). Its nonzero character sends \(2^k u\) to \(u\bmod2\). Every admissible nonempty set of differences therefore has one common exact two-adic valuation \(k\), and any set whose differences have that valuation is admissible. Thus the orbit complex is the disjoint union of \(a\) nonempty full simplices, one for each \(k\). Its realization has exactly \(a\) contractible components.

**4. A cyclic-by-two group.** Write \(P=C_{2^a}\times C_2\). Represent a difference as \((x,b)\), with \(x\) modulo \(2^a\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(v_2(x)\) denote the exact power of two dividing a nonzero representative \(x\) modulo \(2^a\). For each \(0\le k<a\), define the following inversion-invariant sets of nonzero differences:
\[
F_k=\{(x,b):x\ne0,\ v_2(x)=k\},\quad
G_{-1}=\{(x,1):x\in C_{2^a}\},\quad
G_k=\{(x,b):2^k\mid x,\ x/2^k+b\equiv1\pmod2\}.
\]
Read these as sets of inversion classes \(\{(x,b),(-x,b)\}\). Each set spans a simplex of the fixed-point orbit complex. For \(F_k\), use the character \((x,b)\mapsto x/2^k\bmod2\) on \(2^kC_{2^a}\times C_2\). For \(G_{-1}\), use \(b\); for \(G_k\), use \(x/2^k+b\bmod2\).

Conversely, let a selected set of differences be admissible and \(H\) its generated subgroup. The projection of \(H\) onto \(C_{2^a}\) is either zero or \(2^kC_{2^a}\). If \(H\) contains \((0,1)\), it is the whole product \(2^kC_{2^a}\times C_2\), and every nonzero character is one of the three characters displayed above; the corresponding differences lie in \(F_k\), \(G_{-1}\), or \(G_k\). If \(H\) does not contain \((0,1)\), projection is an isomorphism of \(H\) with \(2^kC_{2^a}\); its only nonzero character requires every selected difference to have valuation \(k\), so all lie in \(F_k\). If the projection is zero, all selected differences lie in \(G_{-1}\). Consequently the \(2a+1\) simplices on \(F_0,\ldots,F_{a-1},G_{-1},G_0,\ldots,G_{a-1}\) cover the fixed-point complex, and each is maximal.

**5. Nerve and homotopy type.** Every nonempty finite intersection of these simplex facets is again a simplex, so the nerve lemma identifies their homotopy type with that of their nerve \(N\). All \(G\)-facets contain the inversion class of \((0,1)\), so they span an \(a\)-simplex \(Q\) in the nerve. Distinct \(F\)-facets are disjoint. For any fixed \(k\), the intersections of \(F_k\) with the \(G\)-facets are exactly a cone over the simplex on \(G_{-1},G_0,\ldots,G_{k-1}\), together with the additional edge \(F_kG_k\). The reason is elementary: an element of \(F_k\) meets each earlier \(G_j\) precisely when \(b=1\), meets \(G_k\) precisely when \(b=0\), and meets no later \(G_j\). In particular, the extra edge is in no simplex containing an earlier \(G\)-vertex.

Let \(N_0\) consist of \(Q\) and all the cones \(F_k*\langle G_{-1},\ldots,G_{k-1}\rangle\). Each cone meets \(Q\) along a nonempty full simplex, and distinct cones have no common \(F\)-vertices. Hence \(N_0\) is contractible (indeed, it simplicially collapses onto \(Q\)). The remaining \(a\) edges \(F_kG_k\) join pairs of vertices already connected in \(N_0\), without adding higher faces. Attaching these \(a\) edges to the contractible connected complex produces precisely a wedge of \(a\) circles. Steps 2–5 prove both assertions for every integer \(a\ge1\) and every odd-order abelian \(B\).

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` constructs the translation edge orbits using inversion classes and independently tests bipartiteness by breadth-first graph two-coloring of the actual Cayley graphs. It compares the result with the facet cover, exhaustively for every edge-orbit selection for \(a=1,2,3,4\) in the cyclic-by-two family. The selection counts are respectively \(8,32,512,131072\). It separately computes the reduced mod-two homology of the predicted nerves, obtaining first Betti numbers \(1,2,3,4\). It exhaustively checks the odd-factor projection for \(C_4\times C_2\times C_3\) and \(C_2\times C_2\times C_3\), and tests all singletons, pairs, and 12,000 deterministic additional selections for \(C_4\times C_2\times C_5\). The saved `verification_output.txt` contains the actual run transcript of results, not a proof of the infinite statement. The general proof is given in the preceding section.

## Relationship to prior work
The complex of bipartite graphs is classical. Chari's theorem, as documented in Jonsson's *Simplicial Complexes of Graphs*, Chapter 14.1, classifies the nonequivariant homotopy dimension of bipartite subgraph complexes; Linusson and Shareshian's work on two-colorable graph complexes likewise studies ordinary homotopy and discrete Morse matchings. Those results do not imply the geometric fixed-space classification here: the fixed spaces have free first homology in a dimension very different from the ordinary complex. The present claim concerns regular translations, 2-adic subgroup structure, and an explicit nerve. Earlier elementary-abelian examples cover the boundary \(a=1\) but not the non-elementary infinite family \(a\ge2\). Du, Feng and Spiga study enumeration and automorphisms of bipartite Cayley graphs, not geometric fixed points of their monotone graph-property complex. A complete priority comparison with every older unpublished or inaccessible text is not asserted.

## Limitations
The theorem covers groups with two-primary direct factor \(C_{2^a}\) or \(C_{2^a}\times C_2\), and arbitrary odd-order abelian factors. It does not classify general two-primary groups of rank at least three or the full equivariant homotopy type. The first homology and fundamental group claims follow from the exhibited nerve, not from finite computer experiments. The original full text of the Linusson–Shareshian journal paper was inaccessible at the time of comparison; a synonymous earlier result cannot be conclusively excluded. Independent validation and formal publication-priority review have not occurred.
