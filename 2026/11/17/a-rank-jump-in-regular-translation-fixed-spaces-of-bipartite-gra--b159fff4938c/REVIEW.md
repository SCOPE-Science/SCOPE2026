# Scientific review: regular-translation fixed spaces of bipartite graph complexes

## Correctness — PASS
The claim has precise quantifiers: every integer \(a\ge1\), every finite odd-order abelian \(B\), and the **full regular** translation actions on the bipartite-graph complexes. Fixed points are modeled by invariant edge orbits via constant barycentric coordinates. Bipartite Cayley graphs are characterized using characters of the subgroup generated by the edge differences. An odd direct factor is killed by all such characters and yields a simplicial retraction. For the cyclic factor, valuations partition admissible faces into full simplices. For the cyclic-by-two factor, the classification of subgroups projecting to a cyclic factor proves the complete \(2a+1\)-facet cover. The explicit intersection nerve consists of a contractible subcomplex plus \(a\) free edges, giving exactly \(a\) independent circle generators. The proof handles inversion classes, subgroup kernels, empty selections and \(a=1\). The standalone verifier checks the actual graph predicate independently at many small parameters. The infinite result rests on the proof, not those computations.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The closest established theorem is Chari's nonequivariant sphere-wedge statement for bipartite graph complexes, documented in Jonsson (2008), Section 14.1. Linusson–Shareshian (2000/2003) treat the same underlying two-colorable graph complex and its ordinary homotopy properties; they do not provide the same group-fixed facet nerve in the material accessible for inspection. Du–Feng–Spiga (2020) concern bipartite Cayley graph enumeration and automorphism groups, not the topology of the invariant graph-property complex. Prior elementary-abelian fixed-space calculations include the \(a=1\) boundary case but do not imply the growing \(a\ge2\) family. Prior-claim and literature searches used statement-equivalent and broader-coverage terminology, without a matching stronger theorem found. Lack of an indexed match is not a proof of absolute novelty. The full Linusson–Shareshian article could not be read; this remains a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The claim links a classical monotone graph-property complex to the two-primary structure of the regularly acting group. Its homotopy changes qualitatively upon adjoining one involution: \(a\) components become a connected bouquet with unbounded first Betti number \(a\), while every odd direct factor is homotopically invisible. This is a natural infinite classification, not an arbitrary numerical slice or an Euler-characteristic-only result. The proof gives an explicit finite-facet nerve and integral fundamental-group information useful for further fixed-space computations. The small already-known elementary-abelian boundary is not its substantive contribution.

## Sources and residual risks

An equivalent older fixed-space calculation outside the accessible indexed literature remains possible. Neither the higher-rank two-primary case nor arbitrary subgroup fixed spaces are established here.
