# A seven-primary Moore summand in a dihedral noncrossing-partition quotient

## Finding
For the noncrossing-partition lattice on seven cyclically ordered points, remove its discrete and indiscrete partitions, take the order complex, and divide its geometric realization by all fourteen polygonal symmetries. The quotient \(Y\) has the integral homotopy type
\[
Y\simeq M(\mathbb Z/7,3)\vee\bigvee^{9}S^4.
\]
In particular, its nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_3(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/7\) and \(\widetilde H_4(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^9\). The seven-primary summand is invisible to rational homology.

## Assumptions and scope
Identify the vertices of a regular heptagon with \(\mathbb Z/7\). A partition is *noncrossing* when no four distinct vertices \(a<b<c<d\) have \(a,c\) in one block and \(b,d\) in another. The lattice \(\mathrm{NC}_7\) is ordered by refinement. Its proper part \(\overline{\mathrm{NC}}_7\) excludes only the all-singleton and the one-block partitions. The order complex \(X=\Delta(\overline{\mathrm{NC}}_7)\) has a simplex for every nonempty strictly increasing chain. The group \(D_7\), of order fourteen, acts through \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\) modulo seven. The claim concerns the **ordinary geometric realization quotient** \(Y=|X|/D_7\), not the order complex of a quotient poset, the Borel quotient, or an orbifold classifying space. Each group element preserves the number of blocks and hence the order of the vertices along any chain; no simplex is orientation-reversed by its stabilizer.

## Proof
**1. Connectivity before taking the quotient.** The lattice \(\mathrm{NC}_7\) is the noncrossing partition lattice of Coxeter type \(A_6\), of rank six. The classical EL-shellability theorem gives \(X\simeq\bigvee^{132}S^4\), in particular \(|X|\) is connected and simply connected. The number \(132\) also follows from the type-\(A\) Möbius number (the sixth Catalan number), and is checked by the original chain Euler characteristic \(133\).

**2. Fundamental group of the orbit.** Any reflection of the heptagon interchanges three unordered vertex pairs. Pick one exchanged pair and form the partition having that pair as a single two-element block and all other vertices singleton. This is a nontrivial proper noncrossing partition fixed by the reflection; therefore the induced action on \(|X|\) has a fixed vertex. Since the reflections generate \(D_7\), Armstrong's orbit-space theorem applies to the simply connected finite complex and gives \(\pi_1(Y)=1\).

**3. Exact integral quotient chain calculation.** Generate all set partitions via restricted-growth strings. Retain exactly the noncrossing partitions with between two and six blocks; their number is \(427\). Generate every nonempty strict chain in this proper part, take its orbit under the fourteen signed cyclic permutations of seven labels, and orient each orbit cell by increasing coarseness. Orbit-cell counts, in dimensions zero through four, are
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(47,542,1954,2680,1231).
\]
A separate fixed-chain enumeration yields, for the identity element, the counts \((427,6468,25382,36015,16807)\), for each of the six nonidentity rotations zero, and for each of the seven reflections \((33,160,282,215,61)\). Burnside's formula independently reproduces the orbit-cell counts. The signed boundary of a chain \((p_0<\cdots<p_d)\) is the sum of its vertex-deleted chains with coefficients \((-1)^j\), identified by their *whole-chain* orbits; multiple face contributions are added. Every composite boundary is checked to be zero.

Over \(\mathbb Z\), repeatedly remove any \(\pm1\) entry by an elementary chain contraction: use its column to eliminate the other entries of its row, then remove that row and column together with their corresponding adjacent generators. Every step is unimodular and preserves chain homotopy type. The attached `verify.py` deterministically performs exactly \(3221\) such contractions, leaving chain groups of ranks \((1,0,0,1,10)\). The only nonzero remaining differential is \(\mathbb Z^{10}\to\mathbb Z\), all ten entries being \(\pm7\) or \(\pm14\) with greatest common divisor \(7\). Hence its one nonzero Smith invariant is precisely \(7\), yielding
\[
H_0(Y)=\mathbb Z,\qquad H_3(Y)=\mathbb Z/7,\qquad H_4(Y)=\mathbb Z^9,
\]
with all other integral homology groups zero. The actual ordered pivot records, final boundary, and fixed-chain counts are encoded in `certificate.json` and can be regenerated from `verify.py` using only Python's standard library.

**Independent seven-local consistency check.** The subgroup \(C_7\) acts freely: an invariant equivalence relation for a nontrivial translation of the regular \(C_7\)-set is either the equality or indiscrete relation, both excluded. Thus the rotation quotient is a regular sevenfold cover of a complex with the homotopy type of a wedge of four-spheres. The Cartan–Leray spectral sequence gives \(H_3(|X|/C_7;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/7\). A reflection induces inversion on \(C_7\), which acts by \((-1)^2=+1\) on \(H_3(C_7;\mathbb Z)\). Taking the further involution orbit and localizing at seven therefore preserves this order-seven group by transfer. This checks the seven-primary class independently of the integer pivot sequence; it **does not** establish absence of two-primary torsion or the free fourth-homology rank, for which the integral reduction is essential.

**4. From homology to homotopy.** Since \(Y\) is simply connected and \(H_2(Y)=0\), the first Hurewicz theorem gives \(\pi_2(Y)=0\). It is a finite, two-connected CW complex of dimension four. Its three-skeleton is also two-connected and therefore homotopy equivalent to a wedge of three-spheres (by Hurewicz and the homological Whitehead theorem). Attaching the four-cells is described by an integral degree matrix on \(\pi_3\), and integral row/column changes are realized by homotopy equivalences of wedges of three-spheres and changes of four-cell characteristic maps. Smith reduction of this matrix, equivalently the computed cellular homology, leaves a single attachment of degree seven and nine unattached four-spheres. Thus \(Y\simeq M(\mathbb Z/7,3)\vee\bigvee^9 S^4\).

This finite exhaustive calculation proves the finite chain statement, not the classical theorems invoked in Steps 1, 2 and 4. The latter are used as stated with their hypotheses checked here.

## Relationship to prior work
Callan and Smiley (first publicly posted 20 October 2005) enumerate noncrossing partitions under rotation and reflection, including fixed-partition and polygon-pattern counts. Those are **vertex-orbit and combinatorial enumeration statements**, not homology or homotopy calculations for an orbit of the proper-part order complex. Bessis and Reiner determine cyclic-sieving fixed-point counts for noncrossing partitions, likewise not the chain-level quotient. Athanasiadis, Brady and Watt establish shellability of the *unquotiented* proper-part complex; it supplies simple connectivity of the covering space, not integral homology of \(Y\). Hersh studies orbit cell complexes for different posets, including the full partition lattice modulo the symmetric group; its conclusions do not identify this noncrossing heptagon quotient. Armstrong's theorem determines the fundamental group from stabilizers but does not determine the higher integral homology. A separate six-point cyclic noncrossing-partition quotient result has three-primary torsion in degree two; it does not imply the seven-point full-dihedral quotient. Similarly, a cyclic orbit of the prime-polygon noncrossing-**diagonal** complex is a different construction from this partition **order** complex. The computed seven-primary Smith factor and nine free four-dimensional classes are the new finite topological content.

## Limitations
The result concerns exactly the seven-point noncrossing-partition lattice and the **full** order-fourteen dihedral action. It does not classify all prime polygon sizes, all cyclic subgroups, or any homeomorphism type. The homotopy reduction uses exact finite integer computation; its source and pivot certificate should be replayed for external checking. Existing unindexed, differently named, or unpublished calculations may predate this formulation. Independent expert validation has not been performed.
