# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The coefficient identity follows by differentiating the finite geometric sum; Parseval then yields the exact finite error expression. The Riemann-sum integrand has a removable singularity with a jointly smooth extension in phase, proving uniformity. Absolute convergence of the smooth remainder's Fourier series gives uniformly small aliases. Strict positivity of the cosine defect proves the unique optimal phase. For an aligned midpoint convention, exact coefficient correction and \(x\cot x<1\) prove strict inferiority. The bundled numerical checker corroborates but does not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with specific priority risk.** Kolomoitsev (arXiv:2601.19012v1) addresses general sampling bounds and bounded-variation convergence rates, not the sharp shift-dependent mean-square limit. Prestin–Quak (2001) treat the same jump model with explicit trigonometric coefficients, but their principal conclusions concern graph arclength rather than a sharp \(L^2\) error coefficient or optimal half-cell phase. Their formulas could provide a short route to some of the present calculations; they are credited. Poghosyan (2013) studies shifted nodes and sharp pointwise error constants for corrected interpolants, not demonstrably the uncorrected \(L^2\) statement, but full text was inaccessible. Targeted research-record searches found no equivalent claim; absence from search results is not proof of absolute novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The placement of sampling nodes relative to a jump is a genuine approximation-design choice. The exact phase response, strict globally optimal placement and robustness to smooth remainders provide a quantitative answer beyond the previously available order \(N^{-1/2}\), while the aligned midpoint comparison rules out the obvious alternative of assigning the average jump value. The result does not claim multiple-jump or nonuniform-grid optimality.
