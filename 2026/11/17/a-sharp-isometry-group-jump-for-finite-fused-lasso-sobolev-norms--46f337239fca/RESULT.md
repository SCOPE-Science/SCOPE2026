# A sharp isometry-group jump for finite fused-lasso Sobolev norms

## Finding
For \(n\ge1\) and \(a,b>0\), equip real \(\mathbb R^n\) with the first-difference Sobolev (fused-lasso) norm
\[
N_{n;a,b}(x)=a\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|+b\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|x_{i+1}-x_i|.
\]
The exact number of surjective real-linear isometries is
\[
|\operatorname{Iso}_{\rm lin}(X_{n;a,b})|=
\begin{cases}
2,&n=1,\\
12,&n=2,\ a=b,\\
16,&n\ge3,\ a=b,\\
4,&n\ge2,\ a\ne b.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(n\ge2\) and unequal coefficients, the entire group is \(\{\pm I,\pm R\}\), where \((Rx)_i=x_{n+1-i}\). For \(n\ge3\) and equal coefficients, it is generated by \(-I\), \(R\) and the endpoint involutions
\[
(Lx)_1=x_2-x_1,\quad (Lx)_j=x_j\ (j\ge2),
\qquad
(Hx)_n=x_{n-1}-x_n,\quad (Hx)_j=x_j\ (j<n).
\]
It has structure \(C_2\times((C_2\times C_2)\rtimes C_2)\), of order \(16\). For \(n=2\) and equal coefficients, the isometry group is dihedral of order \(12\).

## Assumptions and scope
All scalar fields are real. The parameters \(a,b\) are strictly positive and constant across the respective coordinate and edge families; there is no regularity, nonnegativity or order-preservation requirement on an isometry. The groups concern surjective linear self-isometries, not affine translations (which are available for any norm). For \(n=1\) the edge sum is empty. The classification applies simultaneously for every finite \(n\), and does not claim a corresponding classification in infinite dimension or for nonuniform individual edge weights.

## Proof
**1. Exact rigidity of the absolute-linear-form decomposition.** Let \(u_1,\dots,u_m\) be pairwise nonparallel nonzero linear forms spanning the dual of a real finite-dimensional vector space, and \(N(x)=\sum_j|u_j(x)|\). Every linear isometry \(A\) satisfies \(N(Ax)=N(x)\). Its nonsmooth locus is the union of the distinct hyperplanes \(\ker u_j\). Since an invertible linear map permutes the irreducible hyperplane components of this finite union, \(u_j\circ A=c_j u_{\pi(j)}\), for a permutation \(\pi\) and nonzero scalars \(c_j\). Choosing a generic point on \(\ker u_k\) outside all other hyperplanes and differentiating across that hyperplane along a transverse line, the jump in the directional derivative of \(N\) is \(2|u_k(h)|\). For \(N\circ A\) it is \(2|c_{\pi^{-1}(k)}||u_k(h)|\). Therefore every \(|c_j|=1\). Thus \(A^T\) sends the set of the displayed weighted forms exactly onto itself, up to independent signs. Conversely, every invertible transformation inducing such a signed permutation is an isometry.

**2. Triangular circuits.** Write \(e_i\) for coordinate forms and set
\[
u_i=a e_i\quad(1\le i\le n),\qquad v_i=b(e_i-e_{i+1})\quad(1\le i<n).
\]
These \(2n-1\) forms are pairwise nonparallel for \(n\ge2\). They are an oriented incidence representation of the fan graph with vertex set \(\{0,1,\dots,n\}\), radial edges \(\{0,i\}\) and successive path edges \(\{i,i+1\}\), with the vertex \(0\) grounded. A set of edge-incidence forms is minimally dependent precisely when its graph edges form a simple cycle: a cycle gives the signed sum zero, whereas a forest has linearly independent incidence forms by successively removing leaves. The three-element circuits are therefore exactly
\[
C_i=\{u_i,u_{i+1},v_i\},\qquad 1\le i<n,
\]
and their unique linear dependence, up to scaling, is
\[
\frac1a u_i-\frac1a u_{i+1}-\frac1b v_i=0.
\]
No other three-element circuit exists.

**3. Classification for \(n\ge3\).** The intersection graph of these \(n-1\) three-circuits (adjacency means sharing a form) is the path on \(n-1\) vertices. It has only identity and reversal automorphisms. The coordinate reversal \(R\) realizes the latter, so compose an arbitrary isometry with \(R\) if necessary to fix every triangle as a set. The forms \(u_2,\dots,u_{n-1}\) are identified as the unique forms in two triangles. Hence they are individually fixed as unsigned forms. Each interior \(v_i\), for \(2\le i\le n-2\), is then individually fixed; the two boundary triangles leave only the possible exchanges \(u_1\leftrightarrow v_1\) and \(u_n\leftrightarrow v_{n-1}\).

If \(a\ne b\), the unique circuit dependence in Step 2 has two coefficient magnitudes \(1/a\) and one \(1/b\). A signed permutation preserving the dependency must take the unique singleton coefficient position \(v_i\) onto \(v_i\) in each fixed triangle. Hence neither endpoint exchange is possible. Once every form is fixed up to sign, \(A^T u_i=\varepsilon_i u_i\). Applying \(A^T\) to \(v_i=(b/a)(u_i-u_{i+1})\) and requiring \(A^T v_i=\pm v_i\) forces \(\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon_{i+1}\). Thus \(A=\pm I\), before possibly reinstating reversal. This proves exactly four elements.

If \(a=b\), each boundary exchange is realized by the concrete involutions \(L\) and \(H\) above. Indeed \(L\) interchanges the absolute terms \(|x_1|\) and \(|x_2-x_1|\), leaving the others unchanged; \(H\) has the analogous property at the right endpoint. The maps \(L,H\) commute and \(RLR=H\). Their products, together with \(R\), implement all at most \(2\cdot2\cdot2=8\) unsigned form permutations. After composing to make each form fixed, Step 3's common-sign argument gives just \(\pm I\). All \(16\) possibilities are distinct, since the unsigned form permutations are distinct and the sign factor is independent. This also identifies the stated semidirect-product group.

**4. Dimensions one and two.** When \(n=1\), the norm is \(a|x_1|\), giving \(\{\pm I\}\). For \(n=2\), the only circuit is \(\{u_1,u_2,v_1\}\). Its unique dependence has coefficient magnitudes \((1/a,1/a,1/b)\). If \(a\ne b\), only exchanging the two radial forms is allowed, up to an overall sign; this yields \(\{\pm I,\pm R\}\). If \(a=b\), all six permutations of the three forms are realized up to signs by the endpoint involution \(L\) and coordinate reversal \(R\), and there are exactly two choices for the common global sign after the unsigned permutation. This gives the order-twelve dihedral group, also visible as the full linear symmetry group of the central hexagonal norm ball.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python program `check_discrete_sobolev_isometries.py` enumerates exact signed permutations of all weighted forms and checks that the induced linear map permutes the entire form family, not merely a subset. It verifies \(n=1,2,3,4\) at coefficients \((a,b)=(1,1),(1,2),(2,1)\), plus \(n=5\) at equal weights: all 13 counts agree with the theorem. These are supplementary finite checks. Steps 1–4 prove the claim for **all** positive real parameters and all finite dimensions; no numerical extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Hamad's 2026 isometry theorem concerns continuous real Sobolev spaces on \(\mathbb R^d\), whose isometries are generated by coordinate symmetries, translations and global sign. The present finite graph-derivative norm exhibits two additional non-coordinate boundary moves exactly at equality of the two penalty coefficients. General zonotope theory explains the association of absolute-linear-form norms with segment generators but does not supply the complete dimension-uniform symmetry jump for this specific weighted path model. Standard fused-lasso literature studies estimation and TV regularization rather than the exact real-linear isometry group. The full natural equivalence of these statements, if any, is a remaining bibliographic question.

## Limitations
No claim is made about all possible graph-incidence norms, arbitrary nonuniform individual weights, nonlinear isometries, the classification of different values of \(n\), or quantitative near-isometries. The proof uses elementary finite-dimensional hyperplane rigidity and graphic circuits, and the stated novelty is the **explicit complete isometry classification and unique critical penalty ratio** for this concrete norm family. An older unindexed zonotope or matroid-symmetry formulation could potentially encode an equivalent result; independent priority confirmation is still desirable.
