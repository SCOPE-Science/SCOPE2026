The natural odd-cycle stable-set cutting plane has a sharp isoperimetric depth transition: for the pentagon a partial cut uniquely minimizes Euclidean boundary area divided by volume to the \((n-1)/n\) power, whereas for every odd cycle with at least seven vertices the complete integral cut uniquely minimizes it. Exact volume and surface-area formulas hold at every cut depth, and the fully tightened pentagon is less isoperimetrically efficient than its fractional relaxation even though the intermediate optimal cut is better than both.
