# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof reduces every noncontact component of a shortest polygon side to a geodesic subarc and an internally disjoint alternate arc in the remaining polygon. The theta topology gives three exhaustive configurations: an arc wholly on one arm; an arc crossing both poles; or an arc crossing one pole. Shortest-path inequalities and an exact two-branch distance maximization establish the bound for all real lengths. A fully specified geodesic-polygon construction attains it, including at equal-arm endpoints. A separate algorithmic route enumeration (3,128 exact mesh comparisons) corroborates but is not relied upon for the infinite assertion.

**Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk.** The initiating paper studies large-scale implications of uniform slimness, not exact constants on theta networks. Dragan–Mohammed compute triangle rather than uniform-polygon slimness. The most relevant complete publicly available theta manuscript determines a four-point hyperbolicity constant, which does not bound this sharp uniform-polygon constant by equality and only treats two equal arms; a second manuscript enumerates geodesic paths rather than their slimness. Statement-level searches found no equivalent formula. Older graph-metric literature and unindexed work remain residual risks.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A theta graph is the minimal two-terminal graph with three competing routes. Exact uniform-polygon slimness is a natural quantitative way to measure deviation from tree geometry, and it differs from the better studied triangle/four-point constants. The theorem supplies an all-positive-real-parameter classification and an unexpected sharp cutoff at a middle-to-shortest-arm ratio of three; a finite sharp polygon exists at every parameter.

**Limits.** No classification for four or more arms or arbitrary metric graphs; no claim about equality to triangle slimness; no independent audit or exhaustive historical-priority certification.
