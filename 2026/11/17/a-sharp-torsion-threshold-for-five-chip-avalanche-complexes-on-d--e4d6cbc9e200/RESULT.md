# A sharp torsion threshold for five-chip avalanche complexes on directed cycles

## Finding

Consider parallel sandpile firing on a consistently oriented cycle of length \(n\ge3\), starting from a binary chip configuration with support \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Let \(A(n,S)\) be the avalanche complex: the abstract simplicial complex generated by the sets of vertices firing together at each time.

**Theorem (finite-range integral classification).** For every \(3\le n\le15\) and every nonempty \(S\) with \(|S|\le5\), all integral homology groups of \(A(n,S)\) are torsion-free, **except** when \(n=15\), \(|S|=5\), and \(S\) is dihedrally equivalent to one of the following fourteen supports:

\[
\begin{gathered}
\{0,1,2,4,10\},\ \{0,1,2,5,8\},\ \{0,1,3,4,9\},\ \{0,1,3,5,12\},\\
\{0,1,3,6,7\},\ \{0,1,3,6,10\},\ \{0,1,3,7,10\},\ \{0,1,3,7,12\},\\
\{0,1,3,7,13\},\ \{0,1,3,10,12\},\ \{0,1,4,6,9\},\ \{0,1,4,6,12\},\\
\{0,1,4,7,9\},\ \{0,1,4,8,10\}.
\end{gathered}
\]

Every exceptional complex is connected and has exactly \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\) as the torsion subgroup of \(H_1\); no other degree carries torsion. For the four supports \(\{0,1,2,4,10\}\), \(\{0,1,2,5,8\}\), \(\{0,1,3,5,12\}\), and \(\{0,1,4,8,10\}\),
\[
H_1\cong\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^2,\qquad H_2=0.
\]
For the other ten supports,
\[
H_1\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^2,\qquad H_2\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
In each exceptional case \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\) and all homology groups in degrees at least three vanish. Thus, within the fully enumerated binary configuration range \(3\le n\le15\), \(|S|\le5\), integral avalanche torsion first occurs at length fifteen and chip count five. There are \(111\) dihedral five-chip classes at length fifteen, exactly \(14\) with torsion; altogether \(747\) dihedral classes are examined.

## Assumptions and scope

The directed cycle has arrows \(i\to i+1\pmod n\); every vertex has outdegree one. An initially occupied vertex has exactly one chip; all other vertices have zero. At every synchronous time step, a vertex with at least one chip fires, sends one chip forward, and loses one chip. The integral simplicial homology is unreduced, and the empty face is not counted among the enumerated nonempty faces. No statement is made for \(n>15\), for more than five initial chips, for nonbinary initial states, or for other digraphs.

A dihedral equivalence of supports means \(S\mapsto\{a+s\epsilon:s\in S\}\) for \(a\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\). Reflection need not be a directed-graph automorphism; it is nevertheless a simplicial isomorphism between the resulting cyclic-translate complexes.

## Proof

**Step 1 (exact reduction to cyclic translates).** Since each vertex has outdegree one and the configuration is binary, every occupied vertex fires and exactly one chip arrives at each successor. Hence the configuration at time \(t\) is the indicator of \(S+t\). There is no stabilization, and the firing sets repeat after finitely many shifts. Thus
\[
A(n,S)=\{\sigma:\sigma\subseteq S+t\text{ for some }t\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}.
\]
This formula eliminates dynamical simulation errors and proves the dihedral reduction.

**Step 2 (complete finite enumeration).** The attached `verify.py` enumerates all \(\binom{n}{k}\) supports for each \(3\le n\le15\) and \(1\le k\le\min(5,n)\), then retains the lexicographically least support in each full dihedral orbit. This generates precisely \(747\) representatives, including \(111\) with \((n,k)=(15,5)\). For every representative it builds every nonempty face, then removes only codimension-one free face/coface pairs. These elementary simplicial collapses preserve integral homotopy type. The complete computed outcomes are given in `census.csv`; it is regenerated by the attached code, not assumed as input.

**Step 3 (why the Smith computation gives *all* torsion).** Orient every simplex in increasing vertex order and form the integral cellular boundary matrix \(d_j:C_j\to C_{j-1}\). Since \(d_{j-1}d_j=0\), there is a short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow H_{j-1}\longrightarrow C_{j-1}/\operatorname{im}d_j\longrightarrow\operatorname{im}d_{j-1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The right-hand group is a subgroup of a finitely generated free abelian group, so it is free. Consequently this sequence splits, and the torsion of \(H_{j-1}\) is precisely the torsion among the Smith invariant factors of \(d_j\). The ranks are \(\operatorname{rank}H_j=\#C_j-\operatorname{rank}d_j-\operatorname{rank}d_{j+1}\). The program constructs exact integer boundary matrices and computes their Smith forms over \(\mathbb Z\) in every existing dimension; there is no inference from finite-field ranks to integral groups.

**Step 4 (exhaustive certificate).** The explicit closed loops in `verify.py` range over every \(n\), every chip count, and every support, with dihedral canonicalization and all relevant boundary operators. The executable tests assert the representative count, the exact fourteen exceptions, their Smith factors \((2,2)\), their two free-rank signatures, and the absence of torsion in all remaining cases. As a separate consistency test, the two displayed witnesses are recomputed from their original, **uncollapsed** full boundary matrices. The verifier runs to completion and writes the complete census reproducibly. Since these loops cover a specified finite range, the output certifies this finite theorem and makes no unbounded inference.

## Verification

Running `python verify.py` with the dependency in `requirements.txt` generated all \(747\) dihedral-class entries in `census.csv` and passed all assertions. Exactly fourteen entries had nonunit Smith factors, each \((2,2)\) in the degree-two boundary and nowhere else. The two independent full-complex witness checks agreed with their elementary-collapse reductions. For \(S=\{0,1,3,4,9\}\) the unreduced boundary calculation yields \((H_0,H_1,H_2)=(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z^2\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^2,\mathbb Z)\); for \(S=\{0,1,2,4,10\}\) it yields \((\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^2,0)\). The exact `census.csv` SHA-256 and command output are recorded in `VERIFICATION.md`.

## Relationship to prior work

Riihimäki and Smith introduced avalanche homology in arXiv:2606.26786 (first public June 25, 2026). Their directed-cycle results cover selected configurations, including consecutive occupied blocks and three-chip patterns; their discussion explicitly raises the question of integral torsion in avalanche complexes. The present statement is a finite, exact classification for sparse *binary directed-cycle* configurations, not an assertion that torsion in avalanche homology was previously unknown everywhere.

The September 2026 working paper *Universal Realization of Avalanche Complexes on Directed Trees* constructs prescribed homotopy types using directed trees. Its unrestricted existence theorem already permits torsion-bearing avalanche complexes; it does **not** imply this length-fifteen threshold or enumerate sparse directed-cycle configurations. Published results on two-pair four-grain directed-cycle avalanches give tori for suitable parameters, not the fourteen five-grain torsion signatures. A specific baseline \(S=\{0,1,3,4\}\) at length fifteen has torus homotopy type by that four-grain result, whereas adding a grain at position nine produces \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\) in first homology.

## Limitations

The cutoff \(n\le15\) is essential to the census theorem. This result does not show that all four-chip configurations on arbitrarily long directed cycles are torsion-free, that these complexes have a particular wedge or projective-plane homotopy type, or that order-two torsion is the only possible torsion in other avalanche complexes. Exact integer arithmetic relies on the stated SymPy Smith algorithm; independent mathematical review has not been performed. An inaccessible potentially relevant published manuscript is noted as a residual bibliographic risk in the review.
