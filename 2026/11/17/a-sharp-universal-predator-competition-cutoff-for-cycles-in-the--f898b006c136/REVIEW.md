# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The multiplier \(1/(xy)\) produces exact strict negative weighted divergence whenever \(\sigma\ge a/(4b)\), including equality. Conversely, for each smaller \(\sigma\), choosing sufficiently large carrying capacity and sufficiently small prey growth yields one positive equilibrium with positive trace and determinant, while both boundary equilibria have stable separatrices confined to their respective axes. Boundedness and the planar limit-set theorem force an interior periodic orbit. General symbolic identities and an exact rational witness were independently rederived using the bundled checker, which does not replace the planar arguments.

## Originality
**PASS within the available evidence, with residual priority uncertainty.** The 2026 primary source provides only a local Hopf-location restriction, whereas the new theorem is a necessary-and-sufficient **universal competition-only exclusion threshold** plus realization for every strictly lower coefficient. Wang and Yu (2021) use Dulac's method under different restricted hypotheses, and Liu (2005) treats predator growth without quadratic self-damping. The full Lu–Huang (2021) study of the same Bazykin equations could not be read; its potentially stronger coverage is the main historical-priority risk. Searches for the formula and equivalent all-parameter/no-cycle formulations returned no covering mathematical record.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is an exact, sharp parameter-uniform phase boundary in a classical ecological ODE. It rules out **all** finite-amplitude positive oscillations on one side, rather than merely ruling out Hopf instability; the converse is constructive for **every** smaller competition level. The result is quantitatively meaningful because competition strength can be varied independently of prey growth, enrichment, conversion and predator baseline mortality. Both directions are needed to make the cutoff sharp.

## Closest literature and limitations
Chan-López et al. (2026), Sec. 3, Theorem 8; Wang and Yu (2021), Theorems 2.1–2.2; Liu (2005), Theorems 1–3; Lu and Huang (2021), abstract only. Below the cutoff, the theorem does **not** assert that every parameter vector oscillates. It provides no uniqueness or orbital stability and is confined to planar continuous-time equations.
