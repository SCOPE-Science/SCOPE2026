# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The ball radius is constructed from the exact sublinearity modulus. The nonsmooth gauge identity is exact on either branch, and the next-coordinate smallness is checked before applying the modulus. This yields a uniform \(\mu\) increment and a first-exit time bound for every initial condition in the ball. The theorem does not claim an orbit remains defined on an invariant neighborhood after leaving it, or global divergence.

## Originality — PASS within the inspected evidence
The closest primary paper is Simpson's *Nonsmooth folds are tipping points* (arXiv:2406.04587). Section 3 proves the linear truncation result, and Appendix A Proposition 6 addresses \(g(y)=y^2\) under \(\delta_L>0\), \(\delta_R<0\). The present theorem supplies an independent absolute-value gauge proving the local-escape property for all determinant signs and every continuous sign-changing scalar feedback with \(g(y)=o(|y|)\). Simpson and Glendinning, arXiv:2111.12222, prove persistence of chaotic attractors in another setting, not this all-orbit escape. Search-index coverage does not establish absolute novelty; an unindexed stronger theorem remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The theorem addresses an explicitly motivated higher-order robustness question, and handles a full function class rather than merely another isolated polynomial choice. The quantitative escape bound is uniform in the detuning parameter and carries a genuine no-forward-invariant-set conclusion. It remains a restricted triangular-feedback result, not a solution of the full nonlinear nonsmooth-fold conjecture.

## Limitations
The first-exit estimate need not be sharp. Feedback may be arbitrary away from zero, hence no claim is made about global post-exit behavior. No external independent mathematical audit or literature-priority certification has been completed.
