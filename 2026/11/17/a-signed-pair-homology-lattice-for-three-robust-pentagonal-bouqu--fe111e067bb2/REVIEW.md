# Review of the pentagonal-bouquet three-robust classification

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact support obstruction restricts all admissible vertex sets to at most two private petals. Quotienting by the common contractible union of full-petal simplices splits the complex into unordered-pair blocks. The nine-vertex block has a complete acyclic discrete-Morse matching: 134 nonempty faces, 66 matched pairs, 374 oriented Hasse incidences, exactly one critical zero-cell and one critical three-cell. The supplied standalone program checks these face-poset facts and the four base symmetry traces. The quotient splitting and the base computation together prove the infinite homotopy formula; the signed two-petal traces prove the entire integral module. The standard rational-transfer argument establishes the quotient homology statement. There is no inference from finite enumeration to an unproved infinite claim.

## Originality
**PASS with stated residual risk.** The 2026 motivating article handles bipartite square sequences; the closest later all-bipartite theorem, by its hypotheses, excludes these odd-cycle bouquets. Published bouquet results treat four-cycles, not pentagons, and their statements do not compute the odd-petal reflection module. The original bounded-independence paper treats collapsibility bounds, not this family representation. The short-cycle-free ordinary three-cut complex has a different defining condition and different Alexander dual. Actual published-finding corpus semantic searches compared the graph family, threshold, forbidden-triple equivalence, rank formula, primitive module and quotient. The full relevant portions of the original articles and main claims of the nearest published mathematical records were inspected. ## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The claim determines a natural nonbipartite infinite family just beyond the current bipartite robust-clique classification and upgrades a sphere count to a primitive integral group representation with nontrivial reflection signs. The structural pair decomposition is uniform, and the difference between the permutation-only and full symmetry quotient distinguishes information unavailable from Euler characteristics or Betti numbers alone.

## Limits
Only unfilled five-cycle bouquets at threshold three, with full group action on homology, are addressed. The proof does not give a fully equivariant wedge decomposition, all odd girths, integral quotient homology or higher thresholds. The finite base proof and its code are supplied; no independent audit has been performed.
