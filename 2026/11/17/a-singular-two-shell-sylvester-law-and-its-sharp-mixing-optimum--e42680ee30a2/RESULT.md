# A singular two-shell Sylvester law and its sharp mixing optimum

## Finding
Let \(0<r<1\) and \(0\le p\le1\). Independently draw four planar points, each uniformly in angle on the unit circle with probability \(1-p\), and uniformly in angle on the concentric circle of radius \(r\) with probability \(p\). Let \(Q_r(p)\) be the probability that all four points are vertices of a strictly convex quadrilateral. Then
\[
\lim_{r\downarrow0}\sup_{0\le p\le1}|Q_r(p)-(1-p+p^3)|=0.
\]
Conditioning on exactly \(k\) points belonging to the smaller circle, the five limiting conditional probabilities for \(k=0,1,2,3,4\), respectively, are
\[
(1,\,3/4,\,1/2,\,1/2,\,1).
\]
Consequently
\[
\lim_{r\downarrow0}\min_{0\le p\le1}Q_r(p)
=1-\frac{2}{3\sqrt3},
\]
and any minimizer \(p_r\) satisfies \(p_r\to1/\sqrt3\). This singular radial-mixture limit is below \(2/3\), the classical minimum for four independent points uniform throughout a planar convex body, because the sampling measures here are not uniform on any convex body.

## Assumptions and scope
The four labels are independent and each label independently selects a circle and a uniform continuous angle. The limiting radius is approached through strictly positive values; setting \(r=0\) would coalesce small-circle points and is a different probability model. Strict convex position means all four points are extreme points of their convex hull, and its boundary has no collinear triples. The result applies for every fixed mixing probability \(p\), uniformly for all \(p\in[0,1]\). It does not assert an exact finite-radius formula or a convergence rate.

## Proof
Write \(q_k(r)\) for the conditional convex-position probability when exactly \(k\) of the four labels choose the inner radius. Symmetry under permutation of labels gives the exact Bernstein polynomial
\[
Q_r(p)=\sum_{k=0}^4 {4\choose k}p^k(1-p)^{4-k}q_k(r).
\]

**Zero or four inner vertices.** Four independent distinct points on a single circle form a strictly convex quadrilateral almost surely, giving \(q_0(r)=q_4(r)=1\) for every positive \(r\).

**One inner vertex.** The other three points are distinct points \(A,B,C\) on the unit circle. For almost every such triangle the origin is not on an edge and the radial point \(rU\) is either inside the triangle for all small \(r\), or outside it for all small \(r\). An outer point cannot lie inside the triangle of the other outer points and an interior disk point, since it is an exposed point of the unit disk. Therefore the limiting nonconvexity indicator is that the origin lies inside \(ABC\). For three independent uniform circular angles, the origin lies inside their triangle exactly when no open semicircle contains all three points. The probability that some semicircle contains them equals \(3/4\): specifying the counterclockwise first point of such a semicircle gives three pairwise-disjoint events of probability \(1/4\) each, up to zero-probability ties. Thus \(\lim q_1(r)=3/4\).

**Two inner vertices.** Let \(A,B\) be the two outer points, and \(rU,rV\) the two inner points. Almost surely \(A,B\) are not collinear with the origin; the two outer points remain extreme in every configuration. For sufficiently small \(r\), one of the inner points is nonextreme precisely if \(U-V\) belongs to the open cone spanned by \(A,B\) or its negative. The exact containment condition is that \(U-V\) belong to the positive cone generated by \(A-rV,B-rV\). If \(U-V=\alpha_r(A-rV)+\beta_r(B-rV)\), with positive bounded coefficients, then \(rU\) has convex barycentric coefficients \(r\alpha_r,r\beta_r,1-r(\alpha_r+\beta_r)\) relative to \(A,B,rV\). For configurations off the limiting cone boundaries, this membership agrees for all sufficiently small \(r\) with membership in the cone generated by \(A,B\). The same holds after swapping \(U,V\). The same reasoning applies with \(U,V\) interchanged. The angular width \(\Theta\in(0,\pi)\) of the smaller positive cone between \(A,B\) is uniform on \((0,\pi)\). By rotational invariance, the direction of \(U-V\) is uniform and independent of \(A,B\). The two opposite cones have total angular width \(2\Theta\), hence conditional nonconvexity probability \(\Theta/\pi\). Averaging gives \(\lim q_2(r)=1-\mathbb E\Theta/\pi=1/2\).

**Three inner vertices.** Let \(A\) be the outer point and \(rU_1,rU_2,rU_3\) the remaining points. Divide coordinates by \(r\) and rotate so \(A/r=(1/r,0)\). For almost every inner triple, their vertical coordinates are distinct; write \(U_-\), \(U_m\), \(U_+\) in increasing vertical order. The outer point and the two vertical extrema are extreme for sufficiently large \(1/r\). The remaining point \(U_m\) is internal exactly when it lies to the right of the segment \(U_-U_+\), since the other vertex moves off to the right. At the height of \(U_m\), this segment lies strictly inside the unit disk, hence its horizontal coordinate lies strictly between the two circle boundary coordinates \(\pm\sqrt{1-y_m^2}\). Therefore the median-height point is to the right of the segment exactly when its horizontal coordinate is positive. Conditional on all three vertical coordinates, the three horizontal signs are independent fair coins. In particular the median-height sign is fair. Hence \(\lim q_3(r)=1/2\).

Each indicator converges almost surely, and bounded convergence justifies all limits. Expanding the binomial mixture gives
\[
\sum_{k=0}^4{4\choose k}p^k(1-p)^{4-k}\lim q_k(r)
=1-p+p^3.
\]
Because the five Bernstein basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one,
\[
\sup_{p\in[0,1]}|Q_r(p)-(1-p+p^3)|\le\max_{0\le k\le4}|q_k(r)-\lim q_k(r)|\to0.
\]
Finally the limiting cubic has derivative \(-1+3p^2\), and its unique minimum on \([0,1]\) is at \(p=1/\sqrt3\), with value \(1-2/(3\sqrt3)\). Uniform convergence proves convergence of minimum values and of all minimizing sequences. Continuity of \(Q_r(p)\) (a polynomial in \(p\)) ensures minimizers exist. \(\square\)

## Verification
The five conditional limits are checked independently by the reproducible program `verify_two_shell.py` using direct orientation tests on random four-point configurations. Numerical sampling is diagnostic only. The proof is the almost-sure geometric classification plus bounded convergence; simulation is not used to infer the limiting probabilities.

## Relationship to prior work
The convex-position probability is a version of Sylvester's four-point question. Marckert (2014), *Probability that n random points in a disk are in convex position*, studies independent points uniform in a **filled disk**. Eisenberg and Sullivan (2011), *A Modification of Sylvester's Four Point Problem*, study an arbitrary-distribution **normalized triangle-area functional**, rather than the present two-shell four-point convexity limit. Neither inspected statement yields the conditional five-class law or the optimal inner-shell mixing probability. Classical center-containment of three independent circle points supplies the first nontrivial conditional coefficient; it is not claimed as new. Published results on thin continuous shells and rotated concentric regular polygons concern different random-sampling laws.

## Limitations
No finite-positive-radius rate, optimal mixing threshold at fixed radius, or universal bound over arbitrary rotationally symmetric measures is claimed. Priority is conditional on the inspected literature and indexed overlap comparisons; radial singular mixtures may have been considered under different terminology. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
