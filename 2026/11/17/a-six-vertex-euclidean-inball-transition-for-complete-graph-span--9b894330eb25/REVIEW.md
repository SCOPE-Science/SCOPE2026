# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact claim is intrinsic Euclidean, in the canonical edge-coordinate metric, for complete graphs with \(n\ge4\). Edmonds' halfspace presentation is quoted from primary literature; the projection norm for an \(a\)-edge indicator in the sum-constrained affine hyperplane is \(\sqrt{a(1-a/m)}\). Orbit-averaging reduces the maximum feasible radius to the centered facet distances. An explicit derivative proves that the closest induced-subgraph facet has size \(n-1\), after which the sign of \(n^2-7n+8\) yields the sharp threshold. The degree-equality argument and rank of the unsigned incidence matrix determine the *entire* incenter set, including affine dimensions, rather than merely a particular center. The included standard-library checker reported `VERIFICATION_OK 451620`; finite checks are corroboration, and no nonstandard computational assertion is used to prove the unlimited statement. The facet irredundancy premise is supported by the complete-graph specialization of Chaourar's facet classification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete-graph spanning-tree polytope is a canonical full-dimensional matroid base polytope in its natural affine span. Its insphere is a well-motivated metric quantity, not an arbitrary parameter slice. The theorem explains why the active obstruction changes from \(n\) co-singleton cut hyperplanes to all \(\binom n2\) coordinate hyperplanes at \(n=6\), with an abrupt disappearance of a high-dimensional family of optimal centers. The entire center set and contact orbit are determined, and the proof identifies the geometric mechanism (projected facet normals and summation constraints). The work is not a reparameterization of the known affine Minkowski-asymmetry problem.
