# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact finite probabilities follow from the primary fine-harmonic-triple enumeration and the separately published Euclidean shell formula. The proof independently establishes factorial-ratio symmetry, global estimates, three asymptotic regimes and the weak limit. The square-root transition uses a uniform logarithmic expansion with error tending to zero; the inverse-deficit regime has geometric endpoint contributions and controlled exponentially small middle. Finite exact and numerical tests are supplementary, not substituted for the infinite argument.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
Ardila–Escobar's original paper explicitly provides the combinatorial vertex count; the published Euclidean-shell result already provides all finite-radius multiplicities and is a stronger exact finite statement. The present claim does not reassert originality of those quantities. The newly assessed mathematical assertion is the explicit square-root crossover function and the nonconcentrating logarithmic-uniform deficit law; neither appears in the materially inspected sources. Proving these statements from the exact count requires nontrivial uniform asymptotics with a changing saddle/endpoint regime. A priority risk remains that an equivalent asymptotic analysis exists under record-statistic or permutation terminology. The finite formula logically determines these limits but does not itself contain the analytical limit statement.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result identifies a critical \(\sqrt n\) radial scale and shows that the logarithmic distance from a polytope's outer vertex sphere does not concentrate. The continuous limit and matching cutoff profile describe a natural global geometric statistic of an established high-dimensional polytope family; they are not obtained by changing a parameter or simply evaluating a single finite count.

## Sources and limitations
Principal full-text comparison: Ardila–Escobar, arXiv:2006.03078 (especially Propositions 3.5 and 3.6 and their proofs), and Ardila, arXiv:2008.02295. Published exact-shell result and harmonic-vertex covariance result were read through accessible primary records. Further comparisons concerned simple vertices/local degree and targeted exact/alias searches. This work is limited to canonical Euclidean vertex sampling; it establishes no finite-sample convergence rate or claim about volume-uniform sampling. Historical priority is not independently certified.
