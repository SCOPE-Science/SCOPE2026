## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
Hales's honeycomb theorem supplies a global lower bound and hexagonal equality case, not a sharp perimeter optimizer over the moving non-Bravais two-coset site family. Choe's flat-torus result supplies a distinct irregular-hexagon *fundamental-domain* minimum, not an exact Voronoi-site-shift profile or its quantization and covering opposition. Yates–Baker analyze transition rates for Voronoi compartments, and Du–Faber–Gunzburger analyze centroidal Voronoi optimization; neither inspected statement implies the three exact profiles and uniquely minimizing non-Bravais offset. Published mathematical finding searches used equivalent terms involving checkerboard cosets, staggered grids, Voronoi hexagons, perimeter minimization, covering and quantization, without identifying an equivalent or stronger result. This is a best-knowledge comparison, not a proof of priority; a wider two-site-basis tessellation reference could still cover the exact restricted calculation.

## Value — PASS
The quantity is a natural physical and geometric intercell-interface objective for a fixed-density nearest-site tessellation, and the competing covering and mean squared nearest-site objectives are standard performance measures. The example gives an exact, unique, nonlattice symmetry-breaking perimeter optimum and a provably opposite monotonic trend in both nearest-site quality measures. This is a structural non-equivalence of objectives under fixed density, not an arbitrary isolated polygon parameter or a claim of a large numerical improvement. The gain is only about 0.134%, which limits practical significance; the mathematical value is the complete sharp tradeoff inside a minimal two-coset periodic family.
