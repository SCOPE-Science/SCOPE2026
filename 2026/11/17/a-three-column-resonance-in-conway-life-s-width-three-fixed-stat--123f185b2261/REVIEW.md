# Review of the width-three Conway Life fixed-state theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The Moore neighbor count is exactly \(a+b+c-1\) at each live cell and \(a+b+c\) at each dead cell in a column with counts \((a,b,c)\), because a three-row Moore neighborhood contains every site of the adjacent columns and both other sites of the middle column. Examining all four middle occupancies gives conditions (3) with no missing boundary case. The weighted sixteen-state transfer counts each labelled periodic fixed configuration once. Its exact symbolic factorization is \(x^3(x^3-27)P(x)\). The three-state component contributes \(3^{n+1}\) if \(3\mid n\); the remaining ten-state irreducible aperiodic block has Perron root between \(2.70\) and \(2.71\), by a displayed positive rational vector. Both the factorization and the asymptotic claims follow from finite exact algebra and Perron–Frobenius theory, not extrapolated data. The independent direct Moore update checks all states up to width seven. Residual risk: independent human checking of the exact determinant and proof is outstanding.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Jen's general cylinder transfer technique supplies the background counting method, not the specifically computed sixteen-state Conway-Life matrix, its factorization or its two spectral scales. Chu–Stuckey–García de la Banda address a different boundary-value optimization question; Eppstein treats growth and decay generally. Compared published three-row results have different automaton rules or four-neighbor geometry. Focused indexed-finding searches for B3/S23 fixed counts on \(3\times n\) Moore tori and for equivalent characteristic polynomials returned no controlling exact formula. Full-text inspection covered Eppstein's accessible HTML, Chu and collaborators' PDF, and the closest two published related results; Jen's 1988 paper was available here through a detailed abstract only, which remains a concrete bibliographic risk. Absence of a found formula is not proof of absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The full exact fixed-point census of a canonical Life strip is a natural dynamical invariant. The three-cycle of column occupancies contributes a distinct larger exponential base only at resonant circumferences, while an irreducible ten-state subsystem governs nonresonant circumferences. This arithmetic split is not supplied by merely counting familiar isolated still-life objects. The result has an unrestricted recurrence, exact characteristic polynomial, and quantitative asymptotics without claiming a general theory for all widths.
