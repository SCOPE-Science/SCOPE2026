# A three-sphere homotopy quotient of the eleven-vertex wheel matching complex

## Finding
Let \(W_{11}=C_{10}*K_1\) be the wheel graph whose ten rim vertices are indexed by \(\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\), with rim edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and ten spokes from a distinguished hub. Let \(X=\mathcal M(W_{11})\) be its **ordinary matching complex**: vertices of \(X\) are the twenty graph edges, and a nonempty set of such vertices spans a simplex exactly when its graph edges have pairwise disjoint endpoints. Let \(D_{10}\), of order twenty, rotate and reflect the rim while fixing the hub. Then the **ordinary topological orbit space** satisfies

\[
\boxed{\ |X|/D_{10}\simeq S^3.\ }
\]

The claim is a homotopy equivalence, not a PL-homeomorphism or a claim that the orbit is a manifold. In particular, all integral homology groups of this quotient agree with those of a three-sphere, its fundamental group is trivial, and it has no integral torsion.

## Assumptions and scope
All spaces are geometric realizations of finite simplicial complexes; all quotients are ordinary orbit spaces, not homotopy/Borel quotients. The matching complex here is the degree-one matching complex, not the two-matching complex or the discrete-Morse complex. The dihedral action has generators \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\) on rim vertices; the hub is fixed. All homology and Smith normal forms use \(\mathbb Z\) coefficients. The statement concerns this canonical ten-rim wheel, selected as the first wheel whose full dihedral quotient has a nonzero rational homology group above dimension one according to the previously established twelve-periodic rational calculation.

The historical input motivating the cone decomposition is the cycle independence-complex cofibre approach of Adamaszek, first posted 2011-06-30. The nonequivariant wheel calculation and the rational dihedral character are known later inputs, not contributions claimed here.

## Proof
**1. Simple connectivity of the covering complex.** Write \(R\subseteq X\) for the simplices consisting only of rim edges. Their incompatibility graph is \(C_{10}\), so \(R=\operatorname{Ind}(C_{10})\simeq S^2\) by the standard path/cycle independence-complex calculation. For each rim index \(i\), let \(u_i\) denote its spoke, and let \(A_i\subseteq R\) be the rim matchings avoiding the two rim edges incident with \(i\). Then \(A_i\cong\operatorname{Ind}(P_8)\simeq S^2\), and there is an actual simplicial decomposition

\[
X=R\cup\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z}(u_i*A_i).
\]

Any matching uses at most one spoke. Thus distinct new cone interiors are disjoint, and attaching the cones sequentially identifies each cone with the existing space precisely along its path-complex base \(A_i\). Since \(R\) is simply connected and each \(A_i\) is path-connected, van Kampen shows inductively that \(X\) is path-connected and simply connected. Alternatively, Vega's previously known classification gives \(X\simeq\bigvee^9S^3\), which also implies simple connectivity; the cone argument avoids requiring a nonequivariant wedge theorem.

**2. Simple connectivity of the quotient.** Armstrong's orbit-space theorem applies to the finite simplicial \(D_{10}\)-action on simply connected \(|X|\): the quotient fundamental group is \(D_{10}/N\), where \(N\) is the normal subgroup generated by elements with nonempty fixed-point sets. The reflection \(s:i\mapsto-i\) fixes the spoke at \(0\), hence the corresponding vertex of \(X\). The reflection \(rs:i\mapsto1-i\) preserves the rim edge \(\{0,1\}\), hence fixes that matching-complex vertex. These two reflections generate \(D_{10}\), so \(N=D_{10}\) and

\[
\pi_1(|X|/D_{10})=1.
\]

There is no assumption that the group action is free.

**3. Correct integral chain model for the quotient.** Let \(P\) be the set of **nonempty** matchings of \(W_{11}\), ordered by inclusion. The first barycentric subdivision \(\mathrm{sd}\,X\) is the order complex of \(P\). Its simplices are strict chains \(F_0\subsetneq F_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_d\) of nonempty matchings. Since the group preserves matching cardinality, a group element stabilizing one such chain preserves each of its vertices, hence fixes the simplex pointwise. Therefore the chain orbits form a regular simplicial orbit-cell structure on \(|X|/D_{10}\), with one cell per chain orbit and boundary obtained by alternately deleting entries of each chain. In particular, the boundary coefficients are integers, and no orientation signs from a nontrivial permutation of chain vertices are omitted.

Enumeration gives the exact chain-group ranks, in increasing cell dimension,

\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=(43,343,882,894,312).
\]

The integer boundary matrices \(B_d:C_d\to C_{d-1}\) for \(1\le d\le4\) have ranks

\[
(\operatorname{rank}B_1,\operatorname{rank}B_2,\operatorname{rank}B_3,\operatorname{rank}B_4)
=(42,301,581,312).
\]

**Every nonzero Smith invariant factor of every matrix is \(1\).** These are exact integer computations, not modular or floating-point approximations. The complete finite enumeration, orbit action, boundary construction, boundary-squared test, independent Burnside counts, exact Smith computations, and matrix digests are reproducible by `code/verify_wheel_quotient.py`; the actual replay output is in `verification/output.txt`. No precomputed chain matrices are assumed.

The chain condition \(B_{d-1}B_d=0\) is checked over \(\mathbb Z\). The displayed ranks yield

\[
(\operatorname{rank}H_0,\ldots,\operatorname{rank}H_4)
=(1,0,0,1,0).
\]

The all-unit Smith factors imply each image \(\operatorname{im}B_d\) is a **primitive** sublattice of the corresponding free chain group. Thus \(C_d/\operatorname{im}B_{d+1}\) is torsion-free, and its subgroup \(\ker B_d/\operatorname{im}B_{d+1}\) is torsion-free. Consequently the displayed Betti ranks are the full integral homology groups; there is no hidden torsion. The quotient has no cells above dimension four, so higher homology vanishes too.

**4. From a homology sphere to a homotopy sphere.** Write \(Y=|X|/D_{10}\). The first two steps give \(\pi_1(Y)=0\), and the third gives \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\). The degree-two Hurewicz isomorphism therefore gives \(\pi_2(Y)=0\); the degree-three Hurewicz isomorphism yields \(\pi_3(Y)\cong H_3(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). A representative map \(S^3\to Y\) of a generator of \(\pi_3(Y)\) induces an isomorphism on all integral homology groups. Both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, so the homological Whitehead theorem makes this map a homotopy equivalence. This proves the result.

## Verification
Run `python3 code/verify_wheel_quotient.py` with Python 3 and SymPy, using exact integer arithmetic (tested with Python 3.13.5 and SymPy 1.14.0). The program enumerates the \(20,115,260,225,52\) nonempty matchings of respective sizes one through five. It independently counts chain orbits using Burnside's lemma, confirms \(B_{d-1}B_d=0\) coefficientwise over the integers, and computes the Smith normal form of each boundary matrix. All nonzero invariant factors are units. The exact four matrix SHA-256 digests and the complete output are recorded in `verification/output.txt`. The finite computation is exhaustive for this **single fixed graph**; no infinite-range inference is made from sample sizes.

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek's cycle-independence cofibre construction, and the earlier path/cycle calculations cited there, explain the rim-and-spoke decomposition but neither treats this wheel symmetry quotient. Vega's Theorem 5.2 determines the **unquotiented** homotopy type of all ordinary wheel matching complexes, in particular \(\mathcal M(W_{11})\simeq\bigvee^9S^3\), but does not determine their orbit-space homotopy types. The published all-wheel dihedral-character calculations already imply that this particular quotient has the **rational homology** of \(S^3\); that rational statement is explicitly prior work, not new. They do not establish the integral Smith factors, the orbit fundamental group, or the equivalence to a sphere. Published integral wheel homology-module calculations concern the covering complexes and similarly do not determine the integral chain complex of the orbit space. Armstrong's theorem supplies one general prerequisite but not the quotient's integral homology.

Thus the distinction is one of logical implication: a rational homology three-sphere need not be a three-sphere up to homotopy; a simply connected space can retain torsion invisible rationally. The present exact primitive integer quotient-boundary calculation, combined with simple connectivity, supplies the additional conclusion. Equivalent but unindexed special-case results remain a possible priority risk.

## Limitations
This is a homotopy classification of one specified finite quotient, not a general formula for all wheel sizes. It does not assert a PL homeomorphism, manifold structure, an equivariant sphere model for the covering complex, or an extension to higher matching complexes. The Smith computations are machine-reproducible exact finite evidence, not a handwritten list of unimodular transformations; the code and library implementing Smith normal form are therefore part of the necessary verification. No independent scientific audit has been performed. A literature search cannot establish absolute priority.
