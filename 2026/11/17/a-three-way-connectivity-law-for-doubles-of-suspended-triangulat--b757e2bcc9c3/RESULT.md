# A three-way connectivity law for doubles of suspended triangulated surfaces

## Finding
Let \(L\) be a finite simplicial triangulation of a connected closed surface, with \(n\) vertices, \(m\) edges, \(f\) triangular faces, and Euler characteristic \(\chi=n-m+f\). Let \(\Sigma L=S^0*L\) denote its simplicial suspension, and let \(D^2(\Sigma L)\) be the simplicial double: its vertices are edges of \(\Sigma L\), and two are adjacent exactly when they are disjoint and together span a tetrahedral facet. The double is a graph because \(\Sigma L\) has dimension three.

Define the **opposition multigraph** \(T(L)\) on \(V(L)\) by assigning to each edge \(ab\) of \(L\) one indexed multiedge between the third vertices \(c\) and \(d\) of the two triangles \(abc\) and \(abd\). Here \(c\ne d\); parallel multiedges are retained. Let \(C_1,\ldots,C_q\) be its connected vertex sets, \(v_i=|C_i|\), and \(e_i\) the number of its indexed edges internal to \(C_i\).

**Theorem.** There is a natural bipartite graph isomorphism from \(D^2(\Sigma L)\) to the graph with vertices \(E(T(L))\sqcup(V(T(L))\times\{+,-\})\) and incidence edges \((t,(x,\epsilon))\) whenever \(x\) is an endpoint of \(t\) and \(\epsilon\in\{+,-\}\). Consequently,
\[
|D^2(\Sigma L)|\simeq\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{q}\bigvee^{\,3e_i-2v_i+1}S^1,\qquad 1\le q\le3.
\]
The first Betti number of the \(i\)-th component can also be computed as \(\frac32\sum_{x\in C_i}\deg_L(x)-2v_i+1\), where the half-sum is integral. The total rank is
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_1(D^2(\Sigma L);\mathbb Z)=7n-9\chi+q.
\]
All integral homology is torsion-free; \(\widetilde H_0\cong\mathbb Z^{q-1}\), the indicated \(H_1\) is free, and all higher reduced homology vanishes.

Call \(S\subseteq V(L)\) an *exact triangle transversal* if every triangular face of \(L\) contains exactly one vertex of \(S\). Then the number of exact triangle transversals is **always exactly \(0\), \(1\), or \(3\)**, and it equals \(0,1,3\), respectively, when \(q=1,2,3\). In particular, \(q=3\) precisely when the one-skeleton of \(L\) is properly three-colorable; \(q=2\) precisely when there is an exact transversal but no proper three-coloring; and \(q=1\) precisely when there is no exact transversal. If \(L\) triangulates the two-sphere, \(q=3\) is equivalent to all vertex degrees of \(L\) being even.

## Assumptions and scope
The surface is connected, closed (no boundary) and triangulated as an abstract simplicial complex. Every edge belongs to exactly two distinct triangular faces, and the facet-adjacency graph is connected. No orientability assumption is made. The two new suspension vertices are distinct and have no edge between them. Parallel edges occur only in the auxiliary multigraph; all simplicial complexes themselves are abstract complexes without repeated faces. The wedge of circles is interpreted componentwise, not as a connected wedge across different components.

## Proof
**1. Face and edge counts.** Closed surface incidence gives \(3f=2m\). Hence \(m=3(n-\chi)\) and \(f=2(n-\chi)\). For each original edge \(ab\), its two incident triangles \(abc\), \(abd\) have different third vertices. This defines the opposition multigraph \(T(L)\). Its degree at \(x\) is exactly \(\deg_L(x)\): each triangle through \(x\) contributes the unique opposite edge to one incidence of \(x\) in \(T(L)\). Therefore \(2e_i=\sum_{x\in C_i}\deg_L(x)\).

**2. Exact graph identification.** The edges of \(\Sigma L\) are the original edges \(ab\) and the suspension edges \(+x,-x\). For each triangular face \(abc\), the tetrahedra \(+abc\) and \(-abc\) yield exactly the six opposite-edge pairs \((ab,+c),(bc,+a),(ca,+b)\) and their negative-apex counterparts. For a fixed original edge \(ab\), the two incident triangular faces supply the four neighbors \(+c,-c,+d,-d\). These are precisely the four incidence edges associated with the multiedge \(t_{ab}=cd\) of \(T(L)\). This proves the asserted graph isomorphism, including the multiplicity-sensitive subdivision vertices corresponding to original edges. The actual double has \(m+2n\) vertices and \(4m\) edges.

**3. Components and circle counts.** Subdividing edges of a multigraph and replacing each original vertex by two twin vertices with identical incidence neighborhoods does not change its components, provided each original vertex is incident with an edge. Every vertex of a closed triangulated surface has positive degree in \(T(L)\), so the components of the double are indexed by \(C_i\). The associated component contains \(e_i+2v_i\) vertices and \(4e_i\) edges. Any finite connected graph contracts a spanning tree to a wedge of exactly \(E-V+1\) circles, giving \(3e_i-2v_i+1\). Summing, and using \(\sum_i e_i=m\), yields \(3m-2n+q=7n-9\chi+q\). This is an integral homotopy result, not an inference from field-valued Betti numbers.

**4. At most three components.** Fix one triangular face \(abc\). Label each of its three vertices by its component in \(T(L)\). If triangles \(abc\) and \(abd\) share \(ab\), then \(c,d\) are joined by a multiedge of \(T(L)\), so replacing \(c\) by \(d\) preserves the multiset of component labels on the face. Since the dual graph of triangular faces is connected, the label multiset is the same on every face. Moreover every vertex occurs in some face, so every component occurs in the reference multiset. There are thus at most three components.

**5. Exact transversals and colorings.** If \(S\) meets each face exactly once, opposite vertices \(c,d\) across any edge \(ab\) have the same membership in \(S\): when an endpoint of \(ab\) lies in \(S\), neither does, otherwise both do. Thus \(S\) is a union of opposition-graph components. Conversely, the constant label multiset of Step 4 shows that when \(q=2\), exactly the component whose label occurs once on a face is an exact transversal, and when \(q=3\), each of the three components is one. No other unions can meet each face exactly once. For \(q=1\), no proper nonempty union exists. This gives exactly \(0,1,3\) transversals.

When \(q=3\), the three component labels give three different colors on each face, hence a proper three-coloring. Conversely, under any proper three-coloring, opposite vertices \(c,d\) across \(ab\) have the same color, so an opposition-graph edge never joins different color classes. Every triangle uses all three colors, forcing exactly three components. This proves the classification for every closed connected triangulated surface.

**6. Sphere degree criterion.** In a proper three-coloring, the neighbors in the cyclic link of a vertex alternate between the two other colors, so each vertex degree is even. Conversely suppose all degrees are even. Color one triangle with three distinct colors and propagate across the dual graph by giving the third vertex of each neighboring triangle the color missing at the shared edge. Around a primal vertex, propagating through its cyclic link induces the identity if the link length is even. The dual cell structure of the sphere is simply connected: closed dual walks are generated, up to homotopy, by the face-boundary walks surrounding primal vertices. Therefore the propagated coloring is path independent and gives a global proper three-coloring. This final equivalence relies on the sphere hypothesis; it need not hold for higher-genus surfaces.

## Verification
A standalone program, `check_suspension_doubles.py`, independently constructs the actual double from the suspension tetrahedra and the predicted graph from opposite third vertices. It checks exact graph component counts, circle ranks, surface incidence, triangulated vertex links, degree-sum identities, exhaustive exact triangle transversals and independent proper three-colorability. Its deterministic examples include the tetrahedral sphere \((q=1,\beta_1=11)\), a face-stellated tetrahedral sphere \((q=2,\beta_1=11+29)\), the barycentric subdivision of the tetrahedral sphere \((q=3,\beta_1=25+29+29)\), the icosahedron \((q=1,\beta_1=67)\), and the seven-vertex torus \((q=1,\beta_1=50)\). It additionally examines 96 deterministic edge-flip instances of triangulated spheres. A passing run ends with `SUSPENSION_DOUBLE_OPPOSITION_TRICHOTOMY_PASS`. Tests are corroboration; the general proof is symbolic and applies to every permitted finite triangulation.

Run with `python check_suspension_doubles.py`. Python 3.9+ is sufficient for all tests except the optional icosahedron example, which requires the `networkx` package as listed in `requirements.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work
The simplicial doubling operation and a general weak Cohen–Macaulay connectivity bound were introduced by Lesh, Schreiner and Wahl (arXiv:2506.10436, first posted June 12, 2025). That bound does not determine connectivity for a three-dimensional suspension, much less the exact circle ranks or the trichotomy. Work on recoloring triangulated spheres (Ito et al., arXiv:2210.17105) studies graph colorings rather than simplicial doubling. Earlier special homotopy classifications for doubles of joined polygons and stacked three-spheres do not imply the arbitrary-surface opposition-graph classification: the join-of-polygons example specializes to a suspension only when a polygon factor is a four-cycle. The present theorem gives a uniform criterion covering higher-genus surfaces as well as spheres, and ties component count to the exact-transversal \(0,1,3\) law. An equivalent statement in other graph-theoretic terminology remains a residual novelty risk.

## Limitations
Only finite connected closed simplicial surfaces are covered. The result is nonequivariant; it does not compute homology representations under automorphism groups or homotopy types of orbit spaces. For higher-genus surfaces, even vertex degrees alone do not imply three-colorability. The formulas require the exact opposition-graph components or an exact triangle transversal test, not just \(n\) and \(\chi\). No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
