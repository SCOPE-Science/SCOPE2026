# Scientific review of the invariant quadratic-period orbit

## Correctness

PASS. The claim concerns existence of one primitive orbit in a precisely defined finite sequential map for every integer parameter at least two. The proof checks the top-path, bridge, triangle, and lower-pulse local transitions on a finite symbolic family and identifies the resulting permutation as a coprime product-clock translation with one subdivided edge. Pairwise distinctness of the symbolic states and a single cycle prove primitivity and the exact count, without extrapolating finite experiments. Both independently written executable checks reproduce the transition description and the first four complete finite-state cases. There is no claim about attractors outside the displayed invariant family.

## Originality

PASS with documented historical risk. The most pertinent earlier paper, Kuhlman–Mortveit–Murrugarra–Kumar (2011), gives long cycles on an H-tree and discusses the effect of edge additions but does not give this specific unicyclic exact quadratic-period construction. The closest published tree-maximization result covers acyclic graphs through fourteen vertices and cannot imply the present all-parameter chorded-network theorem. The independent necklace result is on a plain cycle graph and also does not imply it. Actual indexed-record and external literature searches showed no equivalent transition law, explicit invariant set, or complete implication from a stronger theorem. Unindexed constructions could still predate it.

## Value

PASS. The source literature explicitly motivates the response of long periodic orbits to graph edits. One chord changes a tree into a unicyclic network and yields a transparent quadratic-period mechanism on linear graph size, with the exact delay \(\beta-2\) and independently advancing coprime clocks. This is a structurally motivated, parameter-uniform dynamical construction, not an arbitrary numerical example or a restatement of a generic long-period theorem.

## Scope and limitations

Only existence of the displayed orbit, in the specified closed-neighborhood \((1,3)\) permutation-sequential model and fixed order, is asserted. Exhaustive attractor classification, maximality, alternate scans, different thresholds, other chord placements, and independent external validation remain outside scope. Absolute originality is conditional on the searched accessible literature.
