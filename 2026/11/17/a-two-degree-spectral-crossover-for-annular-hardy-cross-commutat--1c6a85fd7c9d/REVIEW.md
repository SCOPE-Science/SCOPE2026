# Scientific review

## Correctness

The stated commutator is a single weighted bilateral shift, not a positive operator when the two degrees differ. The exact matrix coefficient follows from direct multiplication in the normalized Laurent basis and rational factorization. Since its output indices are distinct, the singular values are the positive coefficients themselves. Strict log-concavity yields interval superlevel sets; elementary integer counting gives an error of at most one; layer cake gives the full \(p>0\) Schatten inequality. Uniformly integrable exponential tails and local convergence prove the simultaneous annulus-width/degree limit. A direct integral representation and partial fractions give the unequal-degree Hilbert--Schmidt constant. Rational and numerical diagnostics checked 5,332 finite assertions, without being used as proof of infinite-dimensional limits.

## Originality

The strongest nearby published quantitative result already establishes the diagonal monomial self-commutator spectral law with the same one-count discrepancy and moment estimate. This paper explicitly credits that case. Another published result establishes the exact **mixed** matrix entry for fixed Laurent polynomial symbols and the fixed-degree thin-annulus spectral law; that coefficient is also credited. The proposed distinct contribution is the unequal, jointly growing degrees with a genuinely two-parameter nonnormal cross-commutator singular-value profile, globally degree-uniform counting and Schatten bounds, and a closed two-parameter Hilbert--Schmidt limit. The primary motivating 2026 Toeplitz paper develops algebraic product characterizations, not singular-value asymptotics. Historical equivalents remain possible; no priority certification is claimed.

## Mathematical value

Commutators between different analytic Toeplitz multipliers measure failure of adjoints to respect analytic multiplication, and the moving-degree/thin-annulus regime is intrinsic to boundary-layer scaling. Unlike the positive diagonal case, the crossed commutator is nonnormal; nevertheless its entire singular-value distribution has an exact sampling description and a sharp absolute finite-width error. The resulting two-dimensional crossover and explicit second moment are natural quantitative invariants, not a finite enumeration or arbitrary cutoff. The result does not extend to non-monomial or multi-band symbols, and neither coefficient algebra nor the general lattice-rounding lemma is claimed as new.
