Every nonregular ideal hyperbolic quadrilateral has exactly two, not one, largest inscribed disks; the regular quadrilateral uniquely merges them into a four-tangent disk. In normalized Klein coordinates \((\pm c,\pm\sqrt{1-c^2})\), their centers are \((\pm(2c^2-1)/c,0)\) and their common radius is \(\operatorname{arsinh}(1/\sqrt{4c^2-1})\).
