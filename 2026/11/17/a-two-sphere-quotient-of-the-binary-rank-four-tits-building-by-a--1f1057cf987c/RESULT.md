# A two-sphere quotient of the binary rank-four Tits building by a Singer normalizer

## Finding

Let \(V=\mathbb F_{16}\), regarded as a four-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(\mathcal B\) be the order complex of the poset of all nonzero proper \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear subspaces of \(V\), and let \(N\) be the normalizer in \(\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_2)\) of the Singer group \(\mathbb F_{16}^{\times}\). The natural action on subspaces gives an ordinary geometric quotient satisfying

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal B|/N\simeq S^2.}
\]

In particular, this orbit space is simply connected, its second integral homology is \(\mathbb Z\), and every other positive-dimensional integral homology group vanishes.

## Assumptions and scope

Write \(\mathbb F_{16}=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^4+t+1)\). The residue class \(t\) has multiplicative order fifteen. Let \(a\) act as multiplication by \(t\) and let \(f\) act as the field automorphism \(z\mapsto z^2\). Then

\[
N=\langle a,f\mid a^{15}=f^4=1,\;faf^{-1}=a^2\rangle
\cong C_{15}\rtimes C_4,
\qquad |N|=60.
\]

To see that this is the full normalizer, note that the centralizer of the irreducible multiplication operator \(a\) is \(\mathbb F_{16}^{\times}\), and conjugation by a normalizer element induces an \(\mathbb F_2\)-automorphism of \(\mathbb F_2[a]=\mathbb F_{16}\). The four Frobenius automorphisms all occur.

All actions here preserve the ranks of subspaces, hence act without simplex inversions. The space is the ordinary orbit space, not the Borel or homotopy quotient. The statement is for the specified binary four-dimensional vector space only; no assertion for arbitrary prime powers is made.

## Proof

**1. The covering building is simply connected.** A maximal chain of nonzero proper subspaces has dimensions \(1<2<3\), so \(\mathcal B\) is a connected two-dimensional simplicial complex. By the Solomon–Tits theorem, its realization is a wedge of two-spheres (in fact, sixty-four copies); in particular it is simply connected.

**2. The orbit fundamental group vanishes.** For a finite group acting simplicially on a simply connected polyhedron, Armstrong's orbit-space theorem identifies the fundamental group with the acting group modulo the normal subgroup generated by elements having fixed points. The automorphism \(f:z\mapsto z^2\) fixes the rank-one vertex \(\mathbb F_2\cdot1\). Therefore \(f\) is trivial in the orbit fundamental group. The relation \(faf^{-1}=a^2\) then implies \(a=a^2\), hence \(a=1\) in that group. Since \(a\) and \(f\) generate \(N\), the orbit fundamental group is trivial.

**3. Count the quotient cells.** There are fifteen rank-one subspaces, thirty-five rank-two subspaces, and fifteen rank-three subspaces: sixty-five nonempty vertices in all. Their nested pairs and full flags number 315 and 315, respectively. For every integer \(0\le k<4\), sum the numbers of flags of each dimension fixed by \(a^i f^k\) over \(0\le i<15\). Exact finite-field enumeration gives the following three-component totals, in dimensions zero, one, two:

| Frobenius exponent \(k\) | Sum of fixed vertex, edge, triangle counts |
|---|---|
| \(0\) | \((75,315,315)\) |
| \(1\) | \((45,45,15)\) |
| \(2\) | \((75,135,75)\) |
| \(3\) | \((45,45,15)\) |

Burnside's lemma, dividing column sums by sixty, gives exactly four vertex orbits, nine edge orbits, and seven triangle orbits. Since the action is rank-preserving, every simplex stabilizer fixes that simplex pointwise; these orbits define open cells of a finite two-dimensional CW quotient. Thus

\[
\chi(|\mathcal B|/N)=4-9+7=2.
\]

**4. Upgrade to homotopy type.** Let \(Y=|\mathcal B|/N\). It is connected and simply connected, hence \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\). As a finite two-dimensional CW complex, \(H_i(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(i>2\) and \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\) is free. The Euler characteristic forces \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). Hurewicz realizes a generator by a map \(S^2\to Y\), which is an integral homology equivalence between simply connected CW complexes. Whitehead's homology theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library programs `verify_orbits.py` and `verify_burnside.py` independently enumerate the sixty group elements and all flags. The first computes face orbits directly; the second applies fixed-face Burnside counts. Both reproduce orbit cell counts \((4,9,7)\) and Euler characteristic two. Their exact outputs are included as `orbit_output.txt` and `burnside_output.txt`.

The finite enumeration establishes the Euler characteristic only; simple connectivity is obtained separately from the group-theoretic fixed-point argument, and the homotopy classification uses the stated topological theorems. Neither computation alone would prove the sphere equivalence.

## Relationship to prior work

Capdeboscq, Rumynin and Thomas study Singer cycles and normalizers as vertex stabilizers of lattices acting on affine buildings, and explicitly describe their projective action; their result does not determine the ordinary quotient of the finite spherical rank-four link by the normalizer. The classical Solomon–Tits theorem determines the homotopy type before the quotient, but not the quotient. Armstrong's theorem supplies a general orbit fundamental-group mechanism without calculating the present orbit cells. The full group-orbit and integral homotopy statement above is thus a distinct question from the known group structure and from cyclic Singer-only orbit-space calculations.

## Limitations

The classification applies only to \(\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_2)\) and its explicitly specified Singer normalizer. It makes no claim about other fields, higher ranks, homotopy quotients, equivariant homotopy types or homology-module decompositions. Searches cannot exclude unindexed equivalent results. The full texts of some related older finite-geometry publications were not accessible for a definitive novelty comparison. No independent validation has been performed.
