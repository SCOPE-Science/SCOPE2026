# A two-stage sphere splitting for one-vertex punctures of ternary Hamming neighborhood complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge3\), write \(H_d=K_3^{\square d}\) for the graph of ternary words of length \(d\), adjacent when they differ in precisely one coordinate. For any vertex \(v\), write \(X_d=\mathcal N(H_d)\) and \(Y_d=\mathcal N(H_d-v)\), where \(\mathcal N\) is the **ordinary open-neighborhood complex**. The natural simplicial inclusion has an integral homotopy splitting, compatible with that inclusion:
\[
\boxed{X_d\simeq Y_d\vee\bigvee^{4d(d-2)+2}S^2.}
\]
Consequently the inclusion induces an isomorphism of fundamental groups and of integral first homology, and a split injection on integral second homology with free cokernel of rank \(4d(d-2)+2\). In combination with the known integral homology of the intact ternary Hamming neighborhood complex, this gives
\[
H_0(Y_d;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad
H_1(Y_d;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^d,\qquad
H_2(Y_d;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{3^d(d-1)(d-2)+d-1-4d(d-2)-2},
\]
with every higher reduced integral homology group zero. For example, the second Betti number drops from \(56\) to \(42\) at \(d=3\), and from \(489\) to \(455\) at \(d=4\). The homotopy splitting, rather than the previously known intact-complex computation, is the result asserted here.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix an integer \(d\ge3\). By vertex transitivity set \(v=0^d\). A face of \(\mathcal N(G)\) is a nonempty set of graph vertices with a common **adjacent** vertex; the center itself is not in its open neighborhood. The graph vertex deletion \(H_d-v\) is different from the simplicial vertex deletion \(\operatorname{del}_{X_d}(v)\). Let \(S=N_{H_d}(v)\), consisting of \(2d\) words \(a_{i,s}\) which have their only nonzero coordinate at \(i\in\{1,\dots,d\}\), with letter \(s\in\{1,2\}\). For words with disjoint nonzero coordinate sets, \(a\oplus b\) means their coordinatewise union, not addition in a finite field. Define \(\Lambda\) as the graph with vertex set \(S\) and an edge between words from distinct coordinate groups. It is the complete \(d\)-partite graph with two vertices per group. All homotopy statements are nonequivariant; homology uses integer coefficients.

## Proof
**1. The auxiliary graph.** The graph \(\Lambda\) has \(2d\) vertices and \(4\binom d2=2d(d-1)\) edges. It is connected, so
\[
\operatorname{rank} H_1(\Lambda;\mathbb Z)=2d(d-2)+1=:g_d.
\]
The flag clique complex of \(\Lambda\) is the join of \(d\) discrete two-point sets, hence is a sphere of dimension \(d-1\). Since \(d\ge3\), it is simply connected. Therefore the fundamental group of the *graph* \(\Lambda\) is normally generated by boundaries of its three-edge cycles with vertices in three distinct coordinate groups. This is the only place the lower bound \(d\ge3\) is needed.

**2. The first attachment: recover the missing center's simplex.** Let \(D=\operatorname{del}_{X_d}(v)\). Every face of \(D\) is witnessed by a center distinct from \(v\), or by \(v\) itself. It follows that
\[
D=Y_d\cup\Delta(S),\qquad Y_d\cap\Delta(S)=\Lambda
\]
where \(\Delta(S)\) is the full \((2d-1)\)-simplex and \(\Lambda\) here denotes its one-dimensional graph complex. To verify the intersection, two words in \(S\) from distinct groups have the additional common neighbor \(a\oplus b\ne v\), whereas two different symbols from the same group have only \(v\) as common neighbor. No three distinct words in \(S\) have any common neighbor other than \(v\): two from a group already force it, and three from different groups likewise force it. These observations use the ternary alphabet essentially. The complex \(Y_d\) is path-connected: the graph \(H_d-v\) is connected (one can bypass the removed all-zero word by keeping another coordinate nonzero), and its graph edges are edges of \(Y_d\) except for the \(d\) pairs \(\{a_{i,1},a_{i,2}\}\). Each such exceptional pair is connected in the one-skeleton of \(Y_d\) through \(a_{i,1}\oplus a_{j,1}\) for any \(j\ne i\), the first edge witnessed by \(a_{i,1}\oplus a_{j,2}\) and the second by \(a_{i,1}\), both surviving vertices.

Every triangle \(\{a,b,c\}\) with members of three distinct groups has a **nullhomotopic edge cycle in \(Y_d\)**. Put \(t=a\oplus b\oplus c\), which is nonzero. The three triangles \(\{a,b,t\}\), \(\{b,c,t\}\), \(\{c,a,t\}\) belong to \(Y_d\): their respective witnesses are \(a\oplus b\), \(b\oplus c\), \(c\oplus a\), none equal to \(v\). Together they form a simplicial disk on the three-cycle. By step 1 these cycles normally generate \(\pi_1(\Lambda)\). Thus the inclusion \(\Lambda\to Y_d\) induces the zero map on \(\pi_1\). Any map from a connected graph into a path-connected space with trivial induced fundamental-group homomorphism is nullhomotopic, so this inclusion is nullhomotopic. As \(\Delta(S)\) is contractible and the gluing is along simplicial subcomplexes, the homotopy pushout gives, **under the inclusion of \(Y_d\)**,
\[
D\simeq Y_d\vee\Sigma\Lambda\simeq Y_d\vee\bigvee^{g_d} S^2.
\]

**3. The second attachment: recover the missing vertex as a cone.** Let \(T=\operatorname{link}_{X_d}(v)\). Then \(X_d=D\cup(v*T)\) with intersection \(T\); the vertex star \(v*T\) is contractible. The link decomposes as a union of full simplices
\[
T=\bigcup_{a\in S}\Delta\bigl(N_{H_d}(a)\setminus\{v\}\bigr).
\]
Every such simplex lies in \(Y_d\), since its center \(a\) survives graph vertex deletion. If \(a,b\in S\) lie in distinct groups, the two simplex vertex sets intersect in the single word \(p_{ab}=a\oplus b\); if they belong to the same group, their intersection is empty. Intersections of three distinct pieces are always empty. Therefore the simplicial nerve theorem identifies \(T\) up to homotopy with precisely the graph \(\Lambda\). The nerve map can be computed on graph loops using vertices \(p_{ab}\) of pairwise intersections and edges contained in the individual simplex pieces.

In particular, a three-cycle of the nerve with vertices \(a,b,c\) in distinct groups is represented by the edge loop with corners \(p_{ab},p_{bc},p_{ca}\). This loop already bounds the simplex \(\{p_{ab},p_{bc},p_{ca}\}\) **inside \(Y_d\)**, because all three corners are neighbors of \(t=a\oplus b\oplus c\ne v\). As in step 1, the triangle boundaries normally generate \(\pi_1(\Lambda)\), and hence \(\pi_1(T)\). The inclusion \(T\to Y_d\), and therefore \(T\to D\), is nullhomotopic (\(T\) is homotopy equivalent to a graph). A second homotopy-pushout splitting gives
\[
X_d\simeq D\vee\Sigma T\simeq
Y_d\vee\bigvee^{2g_d}S^2
=Y_d\vee\bigvee^{4d(d-2)+2}S^2.
\]
Both pushout decompositions preserve the original inclusion of \(Y_d\) up to homotopy. They give the claimed integral split injection and fundamental-group isomorphism without any assumption about the higher homotopy of \(Y_d\).

**4. Absolute homology.** Existing published homotopy and homology calculations of the intact ternary Hamming neighborhood complexes give \(H_1(X_d;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^d\), \(H_2(X_d;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{3^d(d-1)(d-2)+d-1}\), and no homology above dimension two. Applying the natural splitting in step 3 proves the displayed groups for \(Y_d\), including their integral freeness. The same splitting identifies \(\pi_1(Y_d)\cong\pi_1(X_d)\cong\mathbb Z^d\).

## Verification
The standalone Python program `verify_ternary_puncture.py` reconstructs the two complexes directly from graph neighborhoods, computes mod-two boundary ranks, checks the critical local intersection formulas and verifies all explicit triangle-filling disks. For \(d=3,4,5,6\) it verifies the local graph/nerve data and counts \(g_d=7,17,31,49\), yielding sphere losses \(14,34,62,98\). It also computes complete face vectors and reduced mod-two homology at \(d=2,3,4\), obtaining second Betti ranks \(56\to42\) and \(489\to455\) for \(d=3,4\). The boundary \(d=2\) instead has first Betti ranks \(2\to3\) and second Betti ranks \(1\to0\). Re-run with `python3 verify_ternary_puncture.py`; the expected console output is recorded in `verification_output.txt`. Finite mod-two computations support—but do not substitute for—the all-dimensional integral homotopy proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Arana and Stehlík (first public 4 October 2025) investigate the relationship between Lovász and neighborhood complexes and give topological results for quadrangulations; they do not state this ternary-Hamming vertex-puncture splitting. Behal and Chih calculate Hamming graph fundamental groups; Matsushita describes fundamental groups of ordinary neighborhood complexes through even-walk graph groups. These account for the pre-existing intact fundamental-group information. Published calculations of the **intact** ternary Hamming neighborhood complexes establish the two-dimensional collapse, integral Betti numbers and, at three dimensions, an optimal discrete-Morse matching. They do not compare a punctured complex with its intact counterpart. The distinction is substantive: the present statement determines both missing sphere summands through two null attachments and gives the inclusion-induced maps. Comparisons with keyword, equivalent-formulation, and stronger-coverage searches revealed no complete same-object vertex-puncture splitting. An unindexed equivalent formulation may still exist.

## Limitations
The theorem requires the three-letter alphabet and at least three coordinates. It does not classify vertex punctures of quaternary Hamming graphs, multiple vertex deletions, equivariant homotopy or integral homology representations. The homotopy equivalence is inclusion-compatible but not asserted to be canonical or automorphism-equivariant. The \(d=2\) example is a finite calculation, not an all-dimensional extension. Existing published intact-complex calculations are explicitly used for the absolute homology corollary. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
