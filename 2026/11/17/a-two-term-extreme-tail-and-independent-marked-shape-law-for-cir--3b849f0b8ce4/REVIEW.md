# Scientific review: circular three-point spanning-tree extreme tail

**Correctness (PASS).** The global extremum follows from strict sine concavity and deleting the longest edge. Starting from the constant anchored-gap density, the near-maximal event localizes linearly in the deviations. In each of three wedges the MST is exactly \(4\sin(\pi/3-r/4)\cos(3ru/4)\), with Jacobian \(3r/2\). A uniformly invertible radial cutoff has expansion \(r_t(u)=2t-(\sqrt3/2)(1+9u^2)t^2+O(t^3)\), giving both sharp probability coefficients. Normalized conditional densities converge in \(L^1\), proving the marked total-variation limit. The separately claimed pairwise deficit convergence is weak and follows from Taylor expansions. The executable checker independently tests integrals, gap-grid volumes and iid-circle samples.

**Originality (PASS; priority provisional).** Full primary text from Koroleva–Nikitin (arXiv:1301.1360, especially Section 2.1), Lao–Mayer (DOI:10.1016/j.jmva.2008.02.001, Section 6), and Nikitin–Simarova (arXiv:2010.04460, especially Sections 3–4) was materially examined for the closest sampling model. Those theorems concern smooth perimeter kernels or smooth Hessian-based generalizations, not the nonsmooth MST sum of the two shortest edges. Relevant indexed records for a circle triangle's minimum width and ordinary graph-weight spanning trees use different observables or sampling models. Searches revealed no statement subsuming the marked wedge law or its two-term probability. Historical-priority risk remains because search is not exhaustive, and some less-related MST sources were available only as abstracts.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** A minimum spanning tree is a canonical network functional. At the regular triangle, deleting the longest edge creates a nonsmooth maximum, in contrast to the smooth perimeter extremum. The result quantifies the corresponding exponent change from first to second power, determines a nontrivial cubic correction, and identifies the joint conditional tree topology, angular marks, and perimeter fluctuation. These explain a natural geometric boundary effect, rather than merely renaming a known perimeter statistic.

**Limits.** This is a fixed three-vertex, unit-circle theorem, not a large-network or general-distribution result. Numerical experiments are corroborative; the argument for the infinite limit is analytic. No independent mathematical or historical validation has been carried out.
