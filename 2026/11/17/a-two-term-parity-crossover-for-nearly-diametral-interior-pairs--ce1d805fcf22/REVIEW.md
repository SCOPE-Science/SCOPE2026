# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact polar boundary of the regular polygon has the stated fourth-order radial expansion. The event of a nearly diametral pair forces the two radial deficits and angular separation into a fixed compact window at every bounded rescaled threshold. Expanding the Euclidean cosine rule to second order, integrating the radial simplex with its Jacobian, and using the exact polygon area yields the displayed two-term formula. A squared positive-part threshold is continuously differentiable with Lipschitz derivative; consequently the integrated error is uniform even at the two parity onsets. The two local minima in the odd case and the single periodic minimum in the even case supply the quartic coefficients. The conditional four-dimensional uniform limit follows by dominated convergence. Finite exact-geometry quadrature checks corroborate these steps but are not used as proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisionally
Bäsel (arXiv:1204.2707, posted 2012-04-12) gives the complete fixed-regular-polygon interior-point distance distribution, so finite probabilities and their consequences are not independently new. Pure, Durrani, Tong and Pan (arXiv:1903.07757) offer general polygonal distance methods. The published boundary-arclength parity crossover (identifier f64acf954997) gives a leading limiting profile; its full theorem and proof were inspected and its leading implication for the present area-profile is acknowledged by the second-derivative relation. Neither inspected text states or directly supplies by parameter substitution the explicit uniform two-term interior-area correction or the four-dimensional conditional law. The expansion extracts a nontrivial singular two-variable limit beyond the known fixed-sample formulas; the leading crossover alone is not presented as original. Broader prior results under alternative wording could exist, and historical priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The scaled distance deficit is of the same order as regular-polygon boundary scalloping, so the polygon-to-circle limit has two different parity phases. For interior-area sampling the rare-event mass is of order the fifth power of the angle spacing, and the explicit seventh-power probability contribution permits a finite-side approximation with ninth-power residual throughout a compact threshold interval. The sharp onsets and Dirichlet slack geometry explain which interior configurations dominate the rare event. The next-order law and its error control constitute a motivated quantitative stochastic-geometric refinement, not an arbitrary finite-parameter lookup.
