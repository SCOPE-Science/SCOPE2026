The domain is every fixed positive damping parameter and every nonstationary real initial state. The polynomial energy is coercive, with the exact strict derivative \(\dot E=-\varepsilon x^6-y^4\). The proof obtains a complete small-energy section return from continuous dependence on the regular quartic Hamiltonian oval and verifies its transversality. Its period asymptotic and energy defect are calculated from the original-time scaling, not guessed from dimensional analysis. The return difference for inverse-quarter energy, the Cesàro limit for crossing times, and within-cycle squeezing establish the two precise global-in-time limits for all initial conditions. The analytic proof includes eventual infinitely many crossings and every endpoint required by the stated hypotheses. The included numerical and symbolic tests corroborate, but are not substituted for, the proof.

The closest public note reusing this system applies a flat time change to compare formal Taylor jets; that statement neither implies the original-time decay constant nor the count. The related nilpotent and cubic weak-focus results treat different dynamical regimes, and the classical Barta study concerns a different velocity-damping class and does not give these constants. Structured mathematical-record comparisons found no matching or stronger claim. This is not proof of uniqueness; classical asymptotic-averaging reductions remain a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The polynomial is a natural recently published example in which a globally attracting equilibrium admits no smooth strict Morse Lyapunov function, yet the energy is explicit. Characterizing its physical relaxation is nontrivial because the nilpotent linearization supplies no fixed angular frequency: it determines an exact initial-condition-independent energy constant and the asymptotic count of turns. These two facts together distinguish amplitude decay from slowing phase accumulation. A single natural whole-family dynamical problem, rather than an arbitrary finite parameter instance, motivates the result; no new general Morse-existence theorem is claimed.
