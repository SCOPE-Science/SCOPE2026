# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact Cramer–determinant area formula was derived and cross-checked against independent Cartesian line intersections. The inequality reducing all large-area events to an angular pair-collision provides the needed domination. A single-collision blow-up produces the precise coefficient and the jointly marked limit; intersections of two collision tubes and projective seam events are quadratic-order. Integration of the tail proves the sharp moment threshold, and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the optimizer. The standalone checker passed; numerical sampling is secondary to the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
Finch's 2011 original tangent paper explicitly concerns a unit-circle inradius sampling model and a side-length distribution, not independent signed offsets. Published notes on three tangent triangles and curvature–width Pareto tails already contain the tail phenomenon in circumscribed-polygon models; those special laws are not claimed as new. Their offsets are tied to tangent support and their sampling is conditioned on halfplane boundedness. The present joint law, squared-offset size bias, arbitrary continuous angular density and variance-only coefficient are not implied by those statements. The publisher abstract of Daley–Ebert–Swift fixes a side to unit length in a distinct directional construction. Its full text was not available, which remains a priority risk. Search results cannot certify uniqueness; comparison is claim-based, not title-based.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural triple-line intersection triangle has infinite expected area under any nontrivial bounded offset noise, and the leading tail is unexpectedly insensitive to all offset distribution details beyond variance. The marked law identifies the geometric mechanism and conditional sampling biases rather than only a scalar tail. The exact angular optimizer clarifies how directional anisotropy amplifies extreme areas; the theorem applies to discrete offset laws as well as continuous ones.

## Scope and risks
The result assumes exactly three lines, continuous angular density, bounded independent offsets and first-order large-area asymptotics. An inaccessible plausible original full text prevents a definitive historical priority determination. Independent validation is outstanding.
