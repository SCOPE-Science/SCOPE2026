# Scientific review: width-variation far-field normal intersections

## Correctness — PASS
The exact support-function line equation is \(z\cdot t_\theta=h'(\theta)\). Solving two such equations gives \(a=[h'(\theta)\cos\delta-h'(\theta+\delta)]/\sin\delta\) in the first normal direction. The apparent singularity at coincident normals is removable; the only far-field singularity is near antipodal normal directions, where \(a=w'(\theta)/s+O(1)\) uniformly. The rescaling \(q=Rs\), bounded convergence and Scheffé's lemma prove both the exact tail constant and the joint total-variation limit. The bounded-support equivalence and moment cutoff follow without numerical assumptions. The ellipse coefficient and two distinct Fourier-support examples were independently checked algebraically and numerically.

## Originality — PASS, provisionally
The 2014 Bárány–Schneider full-text study supplies classical width and curvature foundations, not the two-normal random intersection distribution. Giblin–Holtom (1999) studies centre-symmetry-set envelopes, and explicitly relates common normals to constant width, but not independently sampled normal pairs or the \(R^{-1}\) asymptotic and marked probability density. Published results on random tangent polygons and average normal multiplicity involve different sampling and observables. A 2007 constant-width characterization study could not be inspected in full; this leaves a specific residual risk regarding the standalone deterministic boundedness criterion. Even if that criterion is classical, the principal joint distribution and width-variation coefficient are not implied by the results inspected.

## Value — PASS
The coefficient \(2\int f(\theta)f(\theta+\pi)|w'(\theta)|d\theta\) is a natural weighted total variation of width. It gives an exact diagnostic separating constant-width bodies (bounded intersections) from all others (a sharp Pareto tail and infinite expected distance), and the marked law identifies which normal directions produce distant crossings. Uniform-normal sampling on any ellipse yields the explicit invariant \(4(a-b)/\pi^2\), exhibiting sensitivity to departure from circularity. This is not merely a numerical curiosity or a restatement of constant width: it quantifies a natural global random-normal experiment.
