For Li's explicit intermittent map, estimating the mean first-return time from \(\lfloor e^{c/\delta}\rfloor\) independent uniform-base starts exhibits a sharp zero–half–one law: the sample mean divided by the true mean tends in probability to \(0\), \(1/2\), or \(1\) as \(c<1\), \(c=1\), or \(c>1\). The critical half comes from a gamma saddle bisected by the largest observable excursion.
