# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Summing the three triangle inequalities gives \(3S\le2D\), and equality at the lower bound forces equal vector norms and all three chord lengths. The six normalized chord directions form an affine-regular hexagon whose edge midpoints lie on the sphere. Supporting functionals at all six midpoints force the entire planar unit ball to equal this hexagon. The explicit reverse construction uses exact rational geometry; it was verified in the packaged checker. The argument covers every noncollinear zero-sum real triple without smoothness or completeness assumptions.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Fang and Liu's primary full text proves the inequality but does not classify its equality geometry. Published result searches and accessible historical claims include multiple distinct invariants of affine-regular hexagons and an already-established critical Fermat–Torricelli endpoint theorem; none of those statements implies an if-and-only-if classification of saturating three-vector configurations. The endpoint theorem is expressly excluded from the claim. Older geometric literature may contain an equivalent equality classification under another name; independent literature review remains necessary.

**Value — PASS.** The bound \(3/2\) is the exact geometric threshold of a radial-rescaling lemma in a new characterization of Hilbert norms. Identifying *all* saturating normed-plane geometries explains the obstruction to strictness, distinguishes the extremal affine-regular hexagon from every strictly convex norm and provides a natural boundary lemma for future parameter and stability analyses. This is a complete, intrinsic equality classification, not a numerical substitution or a routine special case of an endpoint theorem.
