# Scientific review: affine-subgroup quotients of a two-particle graph surface

## Correctness — PASS
The finite group has exactly six conjugacy classes of subgroups, verified both by an elementary semidirect-product argument and exhaustive subgroup enumeration. All 14 subgroups are covered. The explicit deleted-product cubical face-poset algorithm enumerates every cell/flag and, for each action, all orbits, all triangle-edge incidences, all links, boundary circles and orientation-sign constraints. Exact Burnside counts independently match orbit counts. The compact homeomorphism types then follow from the classification of surfaces, with no inference from rational homology alone. The continuous, noncompact orbit spaces are claimed only homotopy equivalent through the equivariant strong deformation retraction. The checker is complete for this finite domain.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources
Abrams and Ghrist determine the genus-six ordered covering surface; Barnett and Farber provide broader graph-configuration homology background. The inspected full published complete-graph result gives full symmetric and alternating quotients, not intermediate affine-group quotients. The inspected full published K5 character calculation determines rational representations but not boundary or orientability of the subgroup quotient surfaces. No equivalent six-class affine subgroup table was identified by targeted searches of published literature and research records. An equivalent unindexed result is still possible, and the Barnett–Farber full text was not inspected.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete classification for the natural 20-element five-point affine group, not a single arbitrary subgroup. The transition from closed nonorientable surfaces to a punctured Klein bottle, a Möbius band, and disks exposes how the same graph configuration surface behaves under different symmetry stabilizers. The order-five and order-two examples share first rational Betti rank but differ integrally and in boundary structure; these distinctions are not recovered by full-group rational homology alone.
