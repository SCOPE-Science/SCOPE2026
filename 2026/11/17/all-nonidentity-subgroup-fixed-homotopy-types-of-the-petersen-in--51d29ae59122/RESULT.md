# All nonidentity subgroup-fixed homotopy types of the Petersen induced-forest complex

## Finding
For the Petersen graph \(P=\operatorname{KG}(5,2)\), let \(\mathcal I\!\mathcal F(P)\) consist of the vertex subsets inducing forests. Under its natural \(S_5\)-action, every **nonidentity** subgroup has exactly one of five geometric fixed-point homotopy types:

| Condition on \(H\le S_5\), \(H\ne1\) | \(|\mathcal I\!\mathcal F(P)|^H\) up to homotopy | Subgroups | Conjugacy classes |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| \(H\) transitive on the five labels | Empty | 20 | 5 |
| \(H=\langle (12)(34)\rangle\) up to conjugacy | \(S^2\vee S^2\) | 15 | 1 |
| \(H=\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}\) up to conjugacy | \(S^1\vee S^1\) | 5 | 1 |
| \(H=A_4\) or \(S_4\) fixing one label, up to conjugacy | \(S^0\) | 10 | 2 |
| All other nonidentity \(H\) | Contractible | 105 | 9 |

The total is 155 nonidentity subgroups in 18 conjugacy classes. The trivial subgroup is deliberately not classified here: its nonequivariant homotopy type was studied previously.

## Assumptions and scope
The five ground labels are \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\). A vertex of \(P\) is a two-element subset; two vertices are adjacent precisely when their subsets are disjoint. The complex \(\mathcal I\!\mathcal F(P)\) has a simplex for each subset of these ten vertices whose induced graph has no undirected cycle. All fixed points are **geometric** points fixed simultaneously by the entire subgroup \(H\), not just individually fixed simplicial vertices. Homotopy equivalence is ordinary integral homotopy equivalence of each fixed space. The classification is exhaustive for \(1\ne H\le S_5\).

## Proof
**Fixed-simplex orbit model.** An invariant point has barycentric coordinates constant on every \(H\)-orbit of the ten simplicial vertices. It therefore lies in the orbit-barycenter simplex for a collection of orbits whose union is a face. Conversely each such barycenter simplex consists of fixed points. These simplices intersect in common orbit-face subsimplices, so they form a simplicial complex PL-homeomorphic to the geometric fixed set. Its vertices are \(H\)-orbits of two-subsets whose unions induce forests, with faces the collections whose total unions induce forests.

**Exhaustive finite classification.** The accompanying standalone `verify.py` enumerates the full permutation group on five labels by explicit tuples. Starting at the trivial group, it adjoins each outside element and takes subgroup closure. Every subgroup can be generated by successively adjoining its elements, so this exhaustive procedure reaches every subgroup. It produces 156 subgroups and partitions them into 19 conjugacy classes by all 120 conjugations. One class is trivial. For each of the other 18 classes, the verifier builds the orbit-face complex by an independent cycle-detection test and performs a sequence of elementary free-face collapses. Each proposed pair is explicitly checked at its deletion step. The file `census.json` records each representative, all faces left after collapse, every collapse pair, and class multiplicities. The remaining complexes are either empty, one vertex, two isolated vertices, a connected graph of four vertices and five edges, or a connected two-dimensional complex of six vertices, thirteen edges and ten triangles. Respectively these are empty, contractible, \(S^0\), \(S^1\vee S^1\), and the two-dimensional case below.

**The two-dimensional residual.** Only the double-transposition class yields the last residual. In a labeling of its six vertices, the five edges incident with vertex \(0\) form a spanning tree. Every other edge becomes nullhomotopic from one triangle whose other two edges are already nullhomotopic; eight such implications, encoded as triangle witnesses in `census.json`, eliminate all eight remaining edge generators of the fundamental group. Thus the residual is simply connected. Its Euler characteristic is \(6-13+10=3\). Since it is a connected two-dimensional simplicial complex, its second integral homology is free of rank two. A simply connected two-dimensional CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-dimensional spheres, so this residual is \(S^2\vee S^2\).

**Identification of group classes.** The transitive subgroups are the five conjugacy types of orders \(5,10,20,60,120\). The two-dimensional residual arises from the 15 double-transposition subgroups. The rank-two graph residual arises from the five normal Klein four subgroups of point stabilizers \(S_4\). The two-point residual arises from the five conjugates each of \(A_4\) and \(S_4\) fixing one point. The remaining nine nontrivial conjugacy classes (105 subgroups) collapse to one vertex. The class-by-class orbit and collapse certificates verify every distinction without assuming that subgroup order alone determines the answer.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py --certificate census.json` using Python 3 and only its standard library; compare the output with `output.txt`. The script generates all 156 subgroups without a hard-coded subgroup list, checks multiplication and inverses, independently checks all 1024 induced vertex subsets for cycles by union-find and graph traversal, enumerates the 19 conjugacy classes, and constructs the exact orbit complexes. It validates every free-face deletion, the fundamental-group elimination for the two-dimensional residual, and the stability of face counts under conjugation. This finite complete calculation is a proof certificate for the finite classification, not a sample extrapolation. The analytical fixed-orbit construction and fundamental-group argument explain why the certificate implies the stated homotopy types.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical Petersen graph labeling and \(S_5\) action are described in Zaslavsky's detailed study of Petersen automorphisms [2]. Independence-complex topology and simple-homotopy techniques were established by Ehrenborg–Hetyei [1]. The general clique-of-line-graph theorem of Goyal–Shukla–Singh [3] concerns the different complex of pairwise incident edges, **not** the induced-forest complex. Recent records [4] and [5] establish the underlying induced-forest homotopy and its automorphism homology representation, and a relationship with induced bipartite vertex sets; neither indexed claim provides the fixed-set types for all nonidentity subgroups. In particular, the fixed \(S^2\)- and \(S^1\)-wedges here are not recoverable merely by taking invariants in ordinary homology representations.

## Limitations
The identity subgroup is outside the claimed classification. The theorem determines individual fixed-space homotopy types but not inclusions between different subgroup-fixed spaces, normalizer actions, equivariant attaching maps, or homeomorphism types of the residual complexes. The closest recently indexed records were accessible as scientific summaries, but their complete hosted project files could not be inspected; an equivalent unpublished or unindexed enumeration remains a novelty risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
