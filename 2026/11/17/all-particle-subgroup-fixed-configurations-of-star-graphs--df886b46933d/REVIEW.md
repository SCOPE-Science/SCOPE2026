# Scientific review: all-particle fixed star configurations

## Correctness

PASS. Every invariant configuration has, on each nontrivial arm orbit, a common strictly positive and ordered finite radius list. This orbit-radial count cannot change in an invariant collision-free path because a simultaneous collision of two or more distinct orbit points at the center is forbidden. Restriction to the fixed-arm substar and reconstruction from radial lists yield continuous inverse homeomorphisms on finitely many open-and-closed strata. Contractibility of the radial list factors, the cited ordinary radial-star theorem, and binomial generating functions establish all homology formulas, including zero particles and no fixed arms. A separate finite graph-spine computation checks component and cycle ranks.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected sources. The closest ordinary graph-braid theorem gives the nonequivariant star rank, which is explicitly not claimed new. Existing indexed star-symmetry calculations determine rational homology characters or ordered arm-orbit quotients. A character does not specify the homotopy types of geometric subgroup fixed spaces: for a transposition of two arms on five arms with four unordered particles, the fixed space has three components and seven independent circles, more information than its Euler characteristic. The previously known two-particle tree classification covers the small-parameter specialization. No inspected full primary or indexed result states or mathematically dominates the all-particle fixed-locus product homeomorphism and its two rational series. An implicit more general equivariant configuration theorem or an unindexed prior specialization remains possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a natural, complete classification of collision-free fixed configurations for every finite arm subgroup and every particle number, not an arbitrary finite slice. It gives topological spaces (not just alternating traces), a uniform component and free-group-rank calculation, and a precise distinction between fixed arms and synchronized nonsingleton orbits. It predicts mixed disconnected graph homotopy types, arbitrary subgroup orbit profiles, and torsion-freeness; these are directly relevant to collision-free symmetric multi-agent motion on networks.

## Closest literature and limitations

Farley--Sabalka (arXiv `math/0410539v1`, 2004, Section 4) establishes the ordinary rank and graph homotopy; Rourke--Sanderson (2000) treat an equivariant configuration-space formalism for manifolds with labels and variable size; An--Maciążek (2021) treat graph braid presentations. Publicly available indexed records on star homology characters and on ordered quotient graphs were read in full and compared by implication. No independent audit or definitive publication-priority confirmation has been performed. The classification does not extend to general trees or symmetry orbit quotients.
