# Alternating-label quotients of injective-word complexes are Moore spaces

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge3\), let \(W_n\) be the regular CW complex of nonempty injective words over \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\): a word with \(k\) letters represents a cell of dimension \(k-1\), and its ordered faces are obtained by deleting one letter. The alternating group \(A_n\) acts by relabeling letters. The *ordinary geometric orbit space* has the following integral homotopy type:

\[
|W_n|/A_n\simeq
\begin{cases}
M(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,n-2),&n\ge3\text{ odd},\\
M(\mathbb Z/(n/2)\mathbb Z,n-2)\vee S^{n-1},&n\ge4\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]

Here \(M(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z,d)=S^d\cup_{q}e^{d+1}\) is the mapping cone of a map of degree \(q\) on \(S^d\); for \(d=1\), use the standard degree-\(q\) attaching loop. Therefore, for odd \(n\), the only positive-degree integral homology is \(H_{n-2}\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). For even \(n\), it is \(H_{n-2}\cong\mathbb Z/(n/2)\mathbb Z\) and \(H_{n-1}\cong\mathbb Z\). At \(n=4\), for example, this quotient is \(M(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,2)\vee S^3\); at \(n=5\), it is \(M(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z,3)\).

The torsion orders are unbounded, although the *unquotiented* complex is known to be a wedge of \((n-1)\)-spheres, with the derangement number of summands [1--3].

## Assumptions and scope
We use the standard injective-word regular CW complex, **not** the abstract simplicial complex on words with arbitrary face identifications. The order complex of the word poset is its barycentric subdivision, so both models have the same equivariant realization. Cell orientations come from the displayed position order. The action of \(A_n\) permutes letter labels but does not reorder positions; every element stabilizing a cell fixes that cell pointwise. Accordingly the ordinary orbit space has a CW structure with one cell for each orbit of words, and the integral cellular differential is obtained by identifying word faces, retaining their positional signs. We do not confuse this ordinary quotient with the Borel construction.

## Proof
**1. Orbit enumeration.** A word of length \(k\) determines a completion to a permutation of \([n]\) by appending its omitted labels in increasing order. When \(n-k\ge2\), any odd relabeling stabilizing its word can be achieved by swapping two omitted labels; hence there is exactly one \(A_n\)-orbit of \(k\)-letter words. When \(k=n-1\) or \(k=n\), the stabilizer contains no odd permutation and there are exactly two orbits, distinguished by the sign of the completed permutation. It follows that the quotient has one \(j\)-cell in each degree \(0\le j\le n-3\), and two cells in each of degrees \(n-2,n-1\).

**2. Cellular boundary in the one-cell range.** In degrees \(1\le j\le n-3\), the unique \(j\)-cell has boundary coefficient \(\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^i\), which equals zero for odd \(j\) and one for even \(j\). Thus the successive one-cell skeleta alternate between a sphere (odd upper dimension) and a contractible complex (even upper dimension). This follows inductively: attaching a cell of the next even dimension by degree one kills the preceding sphere, and attaching the subsequent odd-dimensional cell to a contractible space creates a sphere. The one-vertex zero-skeleton is contractible.

**3. The upper two differentials.** Write \(U,V\) for the two \((n-2)\)-cells, distinguished by positive/negative completion sign, and \(P,N\) for the two \((n-1)\)-cells. The lower differential sends both \(U,V\) to the sole \((n-3)\)-cell with coefficient \(\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}(-1)^i\), namely zero for odd \(n\) and one for even \(n\).

For the top cells, deletion of the letter in position \(i\), counted from zero, changes completion parity by \((-1)^{n-1-i}\). The oriented face has positional sign \((-1)^i\). If \(n=2m+1\) is odd, in the ordered bases \((U,V)\) and \((P,N)\), these signs give the integral matrix

\[
\partial_{n-1}=\begin{pmatrix}m+1&-m\\-m&m+1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is \(2m+1=n\), and the gcd of its entries is one; its Smith form is \(\operatorname{diag}(1,n)\). If \(n=2m\) is even, the matrix is

\[
\partial_{n-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-m&m\\m&-m\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\partial_{n-2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The kernel of the latter is generated by \(U-V\), and the image of the former is generated by \(m(U-V)\). Hence the integral homology groups in the finding, with no possible omitted homology in lower degrees.

**4. From cellular chains to homotopy.** For odd \(n\ge5\), the one-cell lower skeleton has even dimension \(n-3\), so is contractible. Attaching the two \((n-2)\)-cells therefore gives \(S^{n-2}\vee S^{n-2}\). As \(n-2\ge3\), attaching maps of the top cells are classified by their two degree coordinates in \(\pi_{n-2}(S^{n-2}\vee S^{n-2})\cong\mathbb Z^2\). Integral elementary basis changes of the degree matrix can be realized by homotopy self-equivalences of that wedge and by cellular attaching-cell slides. Its Smith form \(\operatorname{diag}(1,n)\) cancels a unit pair and leaves the degree-\(n\) Moore attachment.

For even \(n\ge4\), the lower skeleton has homotopy type \(S^{n-3}\). Two \((n-2)\)-cells attached with degrees one and one leave a space homotopy equivalent to \(S^{n-2}\). The top two attaching maps then have degrees \(m\) and \(-m\) (up to orientation). Sliding one top cell over the other yields a degree-\(m\) attachment and a null attachment. Hence the quotient is \(M(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,n-2)\vee S^{n-1}\).

For the small odd case \(n=3\), write \(a,b\) for the two oriented 1-cell orbits. The two triangular word orbits have attaching relators \(ab^{-1}a\) and \(ba^{-1}b\), respectively. The first relator contains the edge \(b\) exactly once and permits eliminating that edge together with its 2-cell; substituting \(b=a^2\) into the second yields \(a^3\). Thus the quotient is the degree-three Moore two-complex \(M(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,1)\). This completes the proof for all \(n\ge3\).

## Verification
The accompanying independent verifier constructs the actual \(A_n\)-orbit representatives of injective words, computes their incidence matrices directly from deleted-letter faces, and checks \(\partial^2=0\), orbital cell counts, complete lower boundaries, and the predicted top Smith forms for every \(3\le n\le9\). It also builds the full symmetric-group orbit-chain complex as a control. All tests pass. The computational interval is finite corroboration; the preceding arguments cover every \(n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
The standard injective-word complex and its top-dimensional wedge-of-spheres homotopy were established before this calculation. Hanlon--Hersh [1] explicitly study the oriented deletion differential and the full symmetric-group homology representation. Ragnarsson--Tenner [2,3] identify the same complete-graph Boolean complex and describe its nonequivariant spheres. Chacholski--Levi--Meshulam [4] investigate homotopy types of related injective-word and permutation complexes. Athanasiadis [5] studies rational symmetric-group modules of rank-selected word posets. These results are background, not independent novelty for the present statement: a rational module or an ambient wedge-of-spheres result does not determine the integral *ordinary* \(A_n\)-quotient or its Moore attaching degrees. Searches and substantive comparisons located no statement implying the formula above; this is a best-knowledge literature assessment, not a proof of priority.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the full injective-word complex on \(n\) symbols and the specific *label-relabeling* subgroup \(A_n\). It does not classify arbitrary permutation-complex subposets, arbitrary subgroups, relative equivariant spectra, or integral actions on the original derangement homology lattice. Claims of uniqueness in the full historical literature or of external independent verification are not made. The numerical verification stops at \(n=9\), with no inference from finite enumeration needed in the proof.
