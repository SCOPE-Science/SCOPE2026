# Scientific review: resistance-law reconstruction of metric bouquets

## Correctness — PASS

The same-loop law is the parallel resistance of two complementary arcs; different-loop resistances add through the unique junction. Uniform arclength sampling supplies the exact mixture weights, producing the displayed Laplace transform. The density of \(U(1-U)\) and its Taylor expansion give the precise Laplace coefficients with controlled fixed-order remainders. The crucial inverse step is a *strictly positive* coefficient \(D_k\): the full all-integer inequality is proved via an injection establishing \({n\choose j}\le{2n\choose2j}\); no finite experiment is used as an infinite proof. Recursive recovery of the first \(m\) reciprocal-length power sums and Newton identities is exact, with all repeated roots permitted. The first two coefficients recover total length and positive integer loop count. The actual standalone checker independently replayed 301 rational configurations, 12 numerical double-integral comparisons, and stochastic mean checks. No hidden genericity or injectivity assumption is needed.

## Originality — PASS (historical-priority provisional)

The comparison is at the *implication* level. Baker–Faber [1] provides metrized-graph resistance laws but not an unlabelled sampling-distribution inverse theorem. Baptista–Kennedy–Mugnolo [2] use geodesic distance rather than effective resistance and optimize mean distance; no complete resistance distribution is involved. Faught–Kempton–Knudson [3] calculate discrete resistance between identified vertices on cyclic-gluing flower graphs, not continuous arclength on a one-junction bouquet, and do not invert one scalar probability law. Weihrauch [4] uses *every pairwise labelled resistance* on a known point set, strictly more data than one randomly sampled unlabelled resistance law. Published research records closest in topic concern metric-star persistence, theta graph invariants, and finite-ladder resistance kernels; their statements cannot imply the claimed inverse law without substantive additional arguments. Relevant full texts were inspected, including definitions and main theorems. No equivalent Laplace finite-jet identification statement was found. Residual risk: specialized electrical-network tomography literature may have an equivalent formula or identifiability result not located in the searches; independent audit is pending.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete inverse classification for an intrinsic, practically motivated observable on arbitrary positively weighted finite loop bouquets: **one unlabelled two-point scalar distribution**, rather than a labelled resistance matrix, recovers all lengths and multiplicities. The result supplies an exact finite-dimensional signature and a constructive inversion with an explicit non-cancellation inequality, explaining why even coincident edge lengths cannot cause degeneracy. It links resistance geometry, integral transforms, and finite inverse moment problems without imposing unrealistic generic lengths. The theorem makes no claim of noise stability or identification beyond bouquets; that limitation matters for applications.
