# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For every finite positive length multiset, same-loop terminals see two complementary arcs in parallel, giving ℓU(1−U), while distinct-loop terminal-to-junction resistances add. Normalized arclength weights ℓ_iℓ_j/L² give the stated Laplace mixture. The density of U(1−U) is 2/(1−4z)^{1/2} on (0,1/4); a fixed-order Watson expansion has c_k=2(2k−2)!/(k−1)!, with an exponential tail controlled at the far endpoint. The s^−1 and s^−2 coefficients give L=2/A_1 and m=L√A_2/2. For k≥3, the coefficient of H_{k−2}=Σℓ_i^{−(k−2)} is D_k=c_k+2c_1c_{k−1}(m−1)−Σ_{p=2}^{k−2}c_pc_{k−p}. A binomial injection bounds D_k≥4k(2k−4)!/(k−2)!>0 for k≥4, while D_3=16m+8>0. Hence power sums H_1…H_m recursively exist and Newton identities recover all reciprocal lengths including repeated roots. Independently recomputed A_1…A_5 and H_1…H_3 for lengths (1,2,2), matching exact rational claims. This finite example supports—not proves—the all-length coefficient argument.

Best-of-knowledge PASS for the exact unlabelled effective-resistance-law inverse on finite metrized bouquets and its finite Laplace jet, not for series/parallel resistance or Newton inversion. Baker–Faber supplies resistance on metrized graphs; Weihrauch reconstructs finite labelled resistance metrics; Baptista–Kennedy–Mugnolo and the more structurally general published metric-rose theorem recover from *shortest-path distance* distributions, a different scalar observable. No full-text covering theorem emerged; this is not a universal priority certificate.
