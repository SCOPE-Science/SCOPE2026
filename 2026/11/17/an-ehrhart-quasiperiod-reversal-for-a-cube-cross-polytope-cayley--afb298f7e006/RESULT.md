# An Ehrhart quasiperiod reversal for a cube–cross-polytope Cayley polar

## Finding
For each integer \(d\ge1\), consider the rational \((d+1)\)-polytope
\[
K_d=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R:\ \|x\|_1+t\le1,\quad \|x\|_\infty-t\le1\}.
\]
It is the polar, in the standard pairing, of the symmetric-height Cayley hull of a coordinate cube and a cross-polytope:
\[
K_d=\operatorname{conv}((B_\infty^d,1),(B_1^d,-1))^\circ.
\]
Define \(L_d(n)=\#(nK_d\cap\mathbb Z^{d+1})\) for all integers \(n\ge0\). Set
\[
A_d(u)=(1-u)^{1-d}\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom dj
\frac{(1+u)^{d-j}(-2u)^j}{1-u^{j+1}}.
\]
Then the full Ehrhart series, for every \(d\ge1\), is the rational function in \(z=u^2\)
\[
\mathcal E_d(u^2):=\sum_{n\ge0}L_d(n)u^{2n}
=\frac{(1+u)^2A_d(u)+(1-u)^2A_d(-u)}{2(1-u^2)^2}.
\]
Every odd \(d\) has **no pole at** \(z=-1\), despite the potential \(1+z\) factors generated by fourth roots of unity. More strikingly, in the consecutive dimensions \(5\) and \(6\) (corresponding to \(d=4\) and \(d=5\)), the exact minimal periods of the Ehrhart quasipolynomials are respectively \(30\) and \(15\). Thus increasing ambient dimension removes a genuine period-two oscillation.

Write \(\Phi_3(z)=1+z+z^2\) and \(\Phi_5(z)=1+z+z^2+z^3+z^4\). The reduced series are
\[
\mathcal E_4(z)=\frac{P_4(z)}{(1-z)^6(1+z)\Phi_3(z)\Phi_5(z)},
\qquad
\mathcal E_5(z)=\frac{P_5(z)}{(1-z)^7\Phi_3(z)\Phi_5(z)},
\]
with exact, palindromic numerator polynomials
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_4(z)={}&1+8z+86z^2+191z^3+433z^4+505z^5+624z^6\\
&+505z^7+433z^8+191z^9+86z^{10}+8z^{11}+z^{12},\\
P_5(z)={}&1+8z+150z^2+223z^3+977z^4+409z^5+1584z^6\\
&+409z^7+977z^8+223z^9+150z^{10}+8z^{11}+z^{12}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their numerators are coprime to the corresponding denominators in \(\mathbb Q[z]\); in particular, the root orders force minimal periods \(\operatorname{lcm}(2,3,5)=30\) and \(\operatorname{lcm}(3,5)=15\).

Finally, the exact Euclidean volume in the standard coordinates is
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{d+1}(K_d)=\frac{2^{2d+1}}{(d+1)(d+1)!}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All coordinate norms are the usual real \(\ell_1\) and \(\ell_\infty\) norms. Polarity is with respect to the standard origin-centered pairing, and the lattice is the full \(\mathbb Z^{d+1}\), not a rescaled affine lattice. The integer \(d\) is unrestricted above \(1\); the exact period assertion concerns only \(d=4,5\), not a complete minimal-period classification in arbitrary dimension.

## Proof
**1. Bounded slices and exact lattice-point weights.** For \(x\in\mathbb R^d\), the admissible fiber is
\[
\|x\|_\infty-1\le t\le1-\|x\|_1.
\]
It is nonempty exactly when \(\|x\|_1+\|x\|_\infty\le2\). The body is bounded, full dimensional, and rational. At the integer dilation \(n\), each lattice vector \(x\in\mathbb Z^d\) contributes exactly
\(2n-\|x\|_1-\|x\|_\infty+1\) integer heights when the expression is positive, and zero otherwise. Consequently
\[
L_d(n)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z^d}\max\{0,2n-\|x\|_1-\|x\|_\infty+1\}.
\]
This identity also holds for \(n=0\).

**2. Complete generating series.** Put \(w(x)=\|x\|_1+\|x\|_\infty\) and \(A_d(u)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z^d}u^{w(x)}\). For an integer \(m\ge0\), let
\[
F_m(u)=\sum_{\|x\|_\infty\le m}u^{\|x\|_1}
=\left(\frac{1+u-2u^{m+1}}{1-u}\right)^d.
\]
Taking successive maximum-norm shells, with \(F_{-1}=0\), gives
\[
A_d(u)=\sum_{m\ge0}u^m(F_m(u)-F_{m-1}(u))
=(1-u)\sum_{m\ge0}u^mF_m(u).
\]
Expand the final \(d\)-th power and sum each geometric series in \(m\). This proves the displayed finite rational expression for \(A_d\), as an identity of formal power series. For any fixed integer weight \(w\ge0\),
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\max\{0,2n-w+1\}u^{2n}
=\frac{u^{2\lceil w/2\rceil}(2\lceil w/2\rceil-w+1+(w-2\lceil w/2\rceil+1)u^2)}{(1-u^2)^2}.
\]
Equivalently, first use the unrestricted generating function
\(\sum_{m\ge0}\sum_x\max\{0,m-w(x)+1\}u^m=A_d(u)/(1-u)^2\), then retain its even coefficients by averaging its values at \(u\) and \(-u\). This gives exactly the asserted Ehrhart series.

**3. Absence of the half-period pole in odd cross-dimension.** The only possible poles of \(A_d(u)\) at \(u=i\) come from denominators \(1-u^{j+1}\) with \(j\equiv3\pmod4\). In the numerator of the even extraction, the contribution of any such \(j\), up to a nonzero factor common to its two summands, is
\[
\frac{(1-u)^{d-j+2}}{(1+u)^{d-1}}
-\frac{(1+u)^{d-j+2}}{(1-u)^{d-1}}.
\]
At \(u=i\), the quotient \((1+u)/(1-u)\) equals \(i\). The ratio of the two displayed terms is a power of \(i\) with exponent \(2d-j+1\), which is divisible by \(4\) when \(d\) is odd and \(j\equiv3\pmod4\). Both terms therefore coincide and their simple poles cancel. The outer denominator \((1-u^2)^2\) is regular there. Since the final series is even in \(u\), this proves analyticity at \(z=u^2=-1\). (This does not assert cancellation of all other root-of-unity poles.)

**4. Sharp consecutive minimal periods.** Substitute \(d=4\) and \(d=5\) into the finite expression of Step 2. Clearing denominators and factoring over \(\mathbb Q[z]\) yields exactly the two reduced rational functions given above. Exact polynomial division shows each numerator is coprime to the corresponding cyclotomic denominator; the bundled checker independently performs these cancellations and finite integer comparisons. A degree-\(d+1\) quasipolynomial of period \(p\) has generating-function poles only at \(p\)-th roots of unity. Conversely, when a rational series has poles only at roots of unity whose orders divide \(p\), its partial-fraction coefficient formula is a quasipolynomial with period dividing \(p\). Thus primitive second-, third-, and fifth-root poles force the first minimal period to be \(30\), and primitive third- and fifth-root poles force the second to be \(15\).

**5. Volume normalization.** Let \(\Sigma_d=\{x:\|x\|_1+\|x\|_\infty\le1\}\). In each fixed sign/order chamber \(x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_d\ge0\), set \(y_j=x_j-x_{j+1}\) with \(x_{d+1}=0\). The determinant is \(1\), and the norm bound becomes \(\sum_{j=1}^d(j+1)y_j\le1\). Its volume is \(1/(d!\,(d+1)!)\). Multiplying by the \(2^dd!\) chambers gives \(\operatorname{vol}_d(\Sigma_d)=2^d/(d+1)!\). The length of the height fiber is \((2-\|x\|_1-\|x\|_\infty)_+\). Integrating its level sets gives \(\operatorname{vol}_{d+1}(K_d)=2^{d+1}\operatorname{vol}_d(\Sigma_d)/(d+1)\), the stated value.

## Relationship to prior work
Li (2026) studies cross-polytope geometry across support-size scales and contrasts it with its polar cube. Black and De Loera (2021) study the signohedron and signed-poset combinatorics of the cross-polytope monotone path polytope. The present lifted polytope preserves the cube/cross-polytope pair in a different geometric construction and addresses its arithmetic lattice-point enumeration, exact generating series, and cyclotomic period cancellation. The usual rational-polytope Ehrhart theorem guarantees quasipolynomiality but does not supply these formulas or the consecutive-period reversal.

## Limitations
The general minimal Ehrhart quasiperiod in arbitrary dimension is not classified. The formulas concern the specified standard lattice and polarization; different normalization and lattice embeddings can change the periods. The available literature search did not establish a definitive global priority determination, and the full text of the recent approximation preprint was not accessible for inspection; its available abstract addresses a different approximation problem. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
