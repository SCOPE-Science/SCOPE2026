# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the precise vertex family with \(1/2<r<1\), the explicit fourteen supporting facet inequalities are reconstructed from triples of actual vertices; Euler’s bound excludes unlisted facets. Averaging centers over the threefold rotation and horizontal reflection reduces the unrestricted largest-ball problem to the origin. The three distances are exactly \(h\), \(s\), and \(v\). Algebraic comparisons show the slanted orbit is never minimal, with one crossing \(h=v\). The contact normals determine all center dimensions, and a strict algebraic inequality confirms that the entire post-transition center segment is feasible. The fact that the largest ball of the volume optimizer has a two-dimensional center polygon follows because its radial parameter is strictly above the crossing.

The standalone verifier checks fourteen genuine facets, independently solves inball linear programs, and verifies contact counts and center candidates at eighteen admissible parameters. The exact polynomial identity and interval for the crossing were independently checked. The 466 finite assertions are corroboration, not a substitute for the proof. No computational exhaustion over all real parameters is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with historical limits

The nearest primary source, Hoehner–Ledford, *The maximum volume polytope with nine vertices inscribed in the sphere* (arXiv:2608.04392, August 5, 2026), proves the unrestricted nine-vertex **volume** optimum and gives the special vertex realization. Its accessible November 6 full-text revision was inspected in the theorem, geometric verification, optimization, and applications; it does not state an insphere transition or a full incenter locus for the present one-parameter family. A recorded stability result on the same nine-vertex shape concerns the second variation of **volume** under perturbations, not largest inscribed balls.

The familiar Johnson solid is the equilateral-face realization rather than the volume-maximizing realization. Its catalogued geometry does not give the one-parameter inradius maximizer or the \(2\)-to-\(0\)-to-\(1\) dimensional center transition. Actual synonym and theorem searches in the literature and mathematical records found no equivalent statement. A differently named or unpublished treatment remains possible; this does not constitute independent historical-priority certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The class is naturally selected by the new sharp classification of the volume-maximal nine-vertex spherical polyhedron. Fixing the cyclic symmetry leaves a one-parameter family with constant face structure. The exact competing distances yield a different inradius-optimal shape than the famous volume-optimal one, and a sharp eight-contact unique-center transition between continuous two- and one-dimensional families of optimal centers. These distinctions are geometric and are not consequences of volume values or face counts. Their natural coordination-polyhedron interpretation and exact algebraic parametrization make the invariant worth resolving even though the problem is restricted to a symmetric family.

## Scientific limitations

This result does not establish global maximal insphere radius over all nine-vertex spherical polytopes, and does not classify asymmetric realizations. The inequality determining the segment, strict in the interior parameter range, is shown exactly. The finite checker is not an infinite proof. Other formulations of the same geometric classification could have escaped indexing; comparison with the most closely related accessible full text supports, but cannot guarantee, historical priority.
