# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The explicit head-support poset map has a nonempty proper image for every nonempty directed-acyclic arc set. For any invariant proper head set the complete exiting cut is an invariant cone face, and adding that cut cannot create a cycle. Thus every subgroup-fixed lower fiber contracts. Applying Quillen's poset fiber lemma to each subgroup and finite-group equivariant Whitehead proves the asserted equivariant equivalence. Fixed spheres, orientation signs, quotient homotopy equivalence and rational quotient homology follow. All quantifiers and the empty transitive-fixed-space case are included. The verifier checks 55 separate small examples but is not treated as a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS with identified residual risk
The fully inspected seven-page Hultman (2004) article proves the nonequivariant sphere result for all strongly connected digraphs and uses the transitive-closure poset; it does not state or prove an all-subgroup fixed-space equivalence to the vertex-permutation sphere. The Björner–Welker (1999) result covers the nonequivariant complete-digraph case; its full text was inaccessible, and it may contain relevant special-case equivariant observations. Such a special case does not imply the claim for arbitrary strongly connected digraphs with arbitrary automorphism subgroups. The path-free complexes in Grinberg–Katthän–Lewis (2021) forbid a designated source–sink path instead of all cycles. Targeted literature and indexed-record searches found no stronger theorem that would imply the asserted universal equivariant equivalence. This is a best-evidence priority assessment, not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The finding makes Hultman's sphere phenomenon equivariantly rigid: changes to directed arcs do not affect the complete genuine automorphism-equivariant homotopy type so long as strong connectivity and the vertex action persist. It gives exact homotopy types for all geometric subgroup fixed spaces, including the empty/nonempty boundary, and exact integral orientation characters and rational quotient homology for arbitrarily large groups and graphs. These consequences cannot be read off from nonequivariant sphere counts and are natural input for equivariant topology of graph complexes.

## Closest literature and limitations
Hultman (2004), Theorem 3.3 (full article inspected), is closest. Björner–Welker (1999), DOI 10.1137/S0895480198338724, is a potentially relevant but full-text inaccessible precursor. Grinberg–Katthän–Lewis (arXiv:2102.07894) concerns a different path-free complex. The result excludes non-strongly-connected digraphs and does not identify integral torsion in arbitrary quotient spaces. Independent mathematical audit is outstanding.
