# Review of the random hyperbolic incircle-deficit boundary law

## Correctness — PASS

The hyperbolic chord identity and Horváth's Staudtian inradius equation are checked against the source. The proof factors the exact finite-radius expression and constructs a gap-uniform bound sufficient for dominated convergence. The single-small-gap chart gives a \(\delta(x)\) profile with two equal nonzero limit terms in the exponential inradius factors. The coefficient follows from a convergent integral, evaluated by substitution; the marked density converges pointwise to a normalized product law, hence in total variation by Scheffé. Independent hyperboloid and side-length computations agree, and direct quadrature plus simulation corroborate the constant. No numerical experiment is used as a substitute for proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

The preceding result with the same random hyperbolic-circle sampling scheme already states an almost-sure \(e^{2R}\) rescaled-deficit law and its heavy tail. That prior result is explicitly excluded: it does not supply the new \(e^{-R}\) mean rate, exact coefficient, or deficit-weighted angular distribution; its limiting random variable has infinite expectation. Horváth (2014) gives the classical deterministic formula, while Brody–Schettler (2014) treat rational fixed-inradius triangles. The closest indexed same-ensemble paper addresses acuteness instead. Exact constant, synonymous gap, mean, and marked-law searches found no broader controlling result. Historical priority is not independently established; unindexed literature and articles available only as indexed summaries remain risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first expected inradius deficit and the precise rare configurations causing it are natural complements to the typical fluctuation law. An exact elementary coefficient and a total-variation size-biased marked limit give a structural mechanism, not merely a small numerical improvement or an arbitrary finite parameter computation.

## Scope and limitations

Fixed curvature \(-1\); independent arclength-uniform points on a common growing circle; no finite-radius quantitative remainder, extension to general surfaces or independent audit. The supplementary code is corroborative.
