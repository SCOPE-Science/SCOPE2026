# An inclusion-minimal 13-step circulant family with integral three-torsion

## Finding
For each integer \(t\ge 109\), define the thirteen distinct positive steps
\[
S_t=\{1,2,3\}\cup\{t-47,t-44,t-36,t-30,t-22,t-15,t-10,t-5,t,t+11\}.
\]
For every integer \(n>2(t+11)\), give the directed circulant \(D_{t,n}=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,S_t)\) an edge \(a\to a+s\) for each \(s\in S_t\). Its **regular integral GLMY path homology** satisfies
\[
H^{\mathrm{path}}_1(D_{t,n};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]
The integer short-addition presentation producing this group is inclusion-minimal for the torsion: removal of any single step makes that presentation torsion-free. In particular, the example \(t=109\) uses the steps \(\{1,2,3,62,65,73,79,87,94,99,104,109,120\}\) and works for every \(n\ge241\), including prime moduli.

## Assumptions and scope
All chain groups have integral coefficients. A regular allowed path is a directed vertex sequence with no consecutive repeated vertices; \(\Omega_m\) comprises allowed chains whose boundary remains allowed. No claim about symmetric circulants, nonregular homology, higher degrees, or graph homotopy type is made. Because \(1\in S_t\), the digraph is strongly connected for each modulus. Since \(n>2\max S_t\), integer sums of at most two steps are not identified spuriously modulo \(n\).

## Proof
**1. Integral first-homology presentation.** For any finite positive set \(S\) containing \(1\), let \(F=\mathbb Z^S\) with generators \(e_s\), and let \(L\subset F\) be generated by
\[
e_a+e_b-e_c\quad(a+b=c\in S),\qquad e_a+e_b-e_c-e_d\quad(a+b=c+d;\ a,b,c,d\in S).
\]
Then, for every \(n>2\max S\),
\[
H_1^{\mathrm{path}}(\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,S);\mathbb Z)\cong F/L.\tag{1}
\]
Here is an integral derivation, including the coefficient issue. Over \(R=\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}]\) form the regular path chain complex of the infinite circulant \(\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z,S)\), with translation \(z\). Each degree is a free \(R\)-module: for each starting vertex the allowed words form a free finite module, and the forbidden-interior-face cancellation conditions cut out a kernel of a homomorphism between finite free abelian groups, which is itself free. The insertion-of-a-unit-step chain homotopy \(h\) satisfies \(\partial h+h\partial=z-1\) integrally (the alternating inserted paths telescope); it also preserves the \(\partial\)-invariant chain subcomplex. Hence \(K_n=(1+z+\cdots+z^{n-1})h\) has \(\partial K_n+K_n\partial=z^n-1\).

Multiplication by \(z^n-1\) and by \(z-1\) is injective in each free \(R\)-degree. The long exact sequences of their two quotient complexes split on homology by the displayed integer chain homotopies, giving isomorphic groups \(H_m(F_*/(z^n-1))\cong H_m(F_*)\oplus H_{m-1}(F_*)\cong H_m(F_*/(z-1))\). The first quotient is exactly the periodic digraph path chain complex when \(n>2\max S\); the second is the integral step-word complex. In the step-word complex, degree-one differential is zero; degree-two generators are legal two-step words and differences of two words having the same forbidden merged step. Their boundaries are exactly the displayed additive relations (commuted ordered pairs add no new relation). This proves (1). This argument uses no averaging or division by \(n\). It is the integer form of the chain constructions in Tang–Yau, Sections 4.1–4.4, here checked at the chain level instead of assuming a field-only proposition for integral coefficients.

**2. Stability of the exact relation matrix.** Write \(B=\{-47,-44,-36,-30,-22,-15,-10,-5,0,11\}\) and \(S_t=\{1,2,3\}\cup(t+B)\). For \(t\ge109\) the ranges of sums of two small steps, one small and one large step, and two large steps are separated: respectively \([2,6]\), \([t-46,t+14]\), and \([2t-94,2t+22]\). In fact \(6<t-46\), and \(t+14<2t-94\). The first two intervals and the step set account for every possible legal pair merge. Within each sum type, all equalities depend only on \(B\) and the three small steps; the coefficient \(t\) cancels. Therefore the entire integral relation matrix of \(S_t\), indexed by corresponding generators, equals that for \(S_{109}\) for all \(t\ge109\). No finite sweep is substituted for this inequality argument.

**3. Exact integral matrix.** Enumerate the pairs \(a\le b\) in \(S_{109}\); for each common sum add differences of pair vectors, also including a singleton vector when the sum is in \(S_{109}\). The reproducible code and matrix in `verify.py` and `CERTIFICATE.json` determine the complete 13-row, 13-column relation matrix. Its exact Smith invariant factors are ten copies of \(1\) followed by \(3\), with rank \(11\). Thus its cokernel is \(\mathbb Z^{13-11}\oplus\mathbb Z/3\), proving the theorem. The matrix has rank \(10\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) and rank \(11\) over \(\mathbb F_{101}\), independently confirming the three-primary defect.

**4. Inclusion-minimal short-relation torsion.** For each of the thirteen choices of one omitted step, regenerate **all** equalities of at most two remaining steps and calculate their integral Smith form. Each resulting cokernel is torsion-free; the exact thirteen tests are implemented as assertions in `verify.py`. Since the pair-sum pattern is stable in \(t\), this holds for every \(t\ge109\). This is minimality of the short-addition lattice obstruction, not a claim that the entire digraph's homology after deleting the step is unchanged or connected.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy. It constructs the integer matrix from the defining steps, verifies its exact Smith factors, checks ranks modulo two primes, tests the stability type on several parameters, and checks all thirteen one-step deletions. `CERTIFICATE.json` contains the exact matrix and expected invariant factors; `verify.py` compares this embedded matrix, not a separately downloaded log. The verification checks the finite arithmetic and the step-pattern evidence; the all-parameter theorem relies on the range separation and integral chain-homotopy proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Tang–Yau, *Path Homology of Circulant Digraphs* (first posted February 4, 2026), Sections 4 and 5 and Appendix A, develops the step-word reduction and the short-relation lattice over arbitrary fields. Its detailed three-step, rectangular, and doubling-string classifications do not state this thirteen-step three-primary family. Di–Ivanov–Mukoseev–Zhang, *On the Path Homology of Cayley Digraphs and Covering Digraphs* (2024), constructs the general Cayley covering framework, without this explicit integral odd-torsion classification. Known examples of two-primary torsion for other forward circulants are not equivalent to the three-primary invariant here: distinct torsion primes are not consequences of one another. No claim is made that the general group-homological method is new.

## Limitations
This construction is not claimed to use the minimum possible number or magnitude of connection steps among **all** circulants. Only deletion-minimality for its own thirteen-step short-relation presentation is proved. Neither higher path homology nor the path-homotopy type is determined. A future bibliographic discovery could affect priority even though the integer certificate is explicit.
