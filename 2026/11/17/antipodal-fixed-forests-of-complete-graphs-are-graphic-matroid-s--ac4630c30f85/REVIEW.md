# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every \(m\ge2\) the  \(m^2\) edge orbits are explicitly identified. A signed edge-orbit graph lifts to two disjoint copies of each tree component, and each fixed diameter connects corresponding copies, so the independent orbit sets are **precisely** the forests of the apex-doubled multigraph. This proves the PL fixed-point identification, including the geometry of barycenters. The latter multigraph is connected and coloopless of rank \(m\); ordinary independence-complex shellability gives the claimed sphere-wedge homotopy. The labeled-tree exponential formula counts all forests without duplication. The standalone exhaustive union-find checker agrees on every orbit subset for \(m\le4\). Boundaries and the special singleton component convention are explicit.

## Originality — PASS relative to examined literature
Ardila–Castillo–Samper (arXiv:1410.3870, submitted 2014-10-14) supplies generic matroid independence-complex shellability. That published result does not supply the novel reduction of antipodal fixed complete-graph forests to a doubled-edge apex graphic matroid. The closest published-finding corpus matches concern matching complexes and specific forest complexes of different graphs, which neither imply nor identify this fixed space. Searches included antipodal fixed forests, invariant graphic matroids, two-sheet covering forests, and alternative quotient terminology. The possibility of an older equivalent result in signed-graph or equivariant-graph-complex literature is an explicit residual publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural exact classification of geometric fixed-point topology for *every* size of the complete graph under its fixed-point-free involution. The result turns an equivariant problem involving edge-orbit constraints into an ordinary graphic matroid, gives a closed nontrivial sequence of sphere multiplicities, and controls complete integral homology and homotopy type. It is not a restatement of generic matroid shellability because identifying the fixed complex as a graphic matroid requires the covering-forest lemma.

## Limitations
The result is non-equivariant after passing to the fixed space; it does not determine residual-group homology representations. Finite brute-force checks verify the first cases only. An equivalent theorem under alternative signed-graph terminology has not been categorically excluded.
