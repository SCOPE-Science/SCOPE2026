## Mathematical value — PASS

The motivating inverse-Gaussian mapping analysis [1] highlights the dependence of Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup behavior on the Gaussian reference measure, drift and exponent. The classical invariant-Gaussian comparator exhibits a distinct, natural question: what happens to its complex-time boundedness region when two relaxation rates have incommensurable imaginary clocks? A later common-rate periodicity theorem [5] (subsequent comparison) does not address this question. The precise asymptotic exponent changes from one to two and the far-imaginary boundary remembers the classical Lagrange approximation constant; this yields a mathematically natural, quantitative stability obstruction to extrapolating common-period behavior to anisotropic product flows. The exact coefficient can distinguish badly approximable from other irrational ratios even when no exact nonzero period exists. The two-rate model and order of limits are essential, clearly stated restrictions.

## Limitations and status

The arithmetic theorem is a consequence of the established one-dimensional Gaussian boundedness criterion plus additional arguments; it does not create a new criterion for arbitrary second-quantization operators or non-diagonal Ornstein–Uhlenbeck drifts. The infinite claims depend on classical equidistribution and continued fractions. The original Epperson article was not available in full text; the accessible 1997 and 2018 primary treatments provide its relevant criterion.
