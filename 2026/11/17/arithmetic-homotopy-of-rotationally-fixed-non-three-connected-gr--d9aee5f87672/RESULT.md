# Arithmetic homotopy of rotationally fixed non-three-connected graph complexes

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 4\), let \(\Delta_n^{(3)}\) be the abstract simplicial complex on the \(\binom n2\) edges of the complete graph on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Its faces are exactly the edge sets whose corresponding spanning simple graphs are **not** three-vertex-connected. Let the rotation group \(C_n\) and the full polygon symmetry group \(D_n\) permute the graph vertices.

The geometric fixed-point spaces of these two groups are **equal as subspaces**:
\[
\bigl|\Delta_n^{(3)}\bigr|^{C_n}
=\bigl|\Delta_n^{(3)}\bigr|^{D_n}.
\]
They are PL-homeomorphic to the simplicial complex \(L_n\) whose vertices are the possible undirected cyclic edge lengths \(1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), and whose nonempty faces are precisely the subsets \(S\) satisfying
\[
\gcd(n,\{d:d\in S\})>1,
\]
**together with** every singleton \(\{d\}\) having \(\gcd(n,d)=1\). (The greatest common divisor of a set here includes \(n\).)

Write \(r=\omega(n)\) for the number of distinct prime divisors and \(u=\varphi(n)/2\) for half Euler's totient. Then the complete, integral homotopy classification is:

* When \(n\) is prime, \(L_n\) is a discrete set of \((n-1)/2\) points.
* When \(n\) is composite but not squarefree, \(L_n\) is homotopy equivalent to a discrete set of \(u+1\) points.
* When \(n\) is squarefree and composite, \(L_n\) is homotopy equivalent to the disjoint union \(S^{r-2}\sqcup\{u\text{ points}\}\). In particular \(r=2\) means that the sphere component \(S^0\) consists of two separate points.

Thus all reduced integral homology groups are free abelian. The **first** nonzero reduced homology in two distinct degrees occurs at \(n=30\):
\[
\widetilde H_0\bigl(|\Delta_{30}^{(3)}|^{D_{30}};\mathbb Z\bigr)
\cong \mathbb Z^4,
\qquad
\widetilde H_1\bigl(|\Delta_{30}^{(3)}|^{D_{30}};\mathbb Z\bigr)
\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
with all other reduced homology zero. The same statement holds with \(D_{30}\) replaced by \(C_{30}\). For every \(4\le n<30\), geometric fixed-point reduced homology is supported only in degree zero, if nonzero.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, undirected, simple, and have a prescribed vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Three-vertex-connected means that at least four vertices remain initially and deletion of any set of at most two vertices leaves a connected graph. The geometric realization uses the standard affine simplex realization; fixed points are geometric fixed points, **not** merely fixed vertices or fixed homology classes. The acting groups are the **entire** cyclic rotation group and the entire dihedral group, not arbitrary proper subgroups.

For \(1\le d\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), write \(E_d=\{\{x,x+d\}:x\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\), identifying undirected edges. For any distance set \(S\), its union \(G(n,S)=\bigcup_{d\in S}E_d\) is the corresponding simple circulant graph. When \(n\) is even and \(d=n/2\), the antipodal step contributes degree one rather than degree two.

## Proof

**1. Circulant connectivity criterion.** The subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) generated by the elements \(\pm d\), for \(d\in S\), is all of \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) exactly when \(\gcd(n,S)=1\). Hence \(G(n,S)\) is connected exactly under this condition. Its vertex degree is \(2|S|\), reduced by one if the antipodal distance \(n/2\) occurs. Therefore \(|S|\ge2\) implies degree at least three.

We apply the standard connectivity bound of Watkins: every connected finite vertex-transitive graph of degree \(\delta\) has vertex connectivity at least \(2(\delta+1)/3\). Every connected circulant is vertex-transitive, so degree at least three implies vertex connectivity at least three, as connectivity is integral. Conversely, if \(S=\{d\}\) and \(\gcd(n,d)=1\), then the corresponding circulant is an ordinary \(n\)-cycle and is not three-vertex-connected. Disconnected circulants are also not three-vertex-connected. Thus for nonempty \(S\),
\[
G(n,S)\in\Delta_n^{(3)}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\gcd(n,S)>1\ \text{or}\ (|S|=1\text{ and }\gcd(n,d)=1).
\]
This argument also covers \(n=4\) and all even antipodal cases.

**2. Actual geometric fixed-point identification.** Cyclic rotation is transitive on each distance edge class \(E_d\); these are all its edge orbits. Any point of the geometric realization fixed by every rotation has equal barycentric coefficients on edges in each \(E_d\). Its nonzero support is therefore a union of distance classes whose graph is a face. Sending the total coefficient mass of each orbit to the corresponding vertex \(d\) provides an affine PL homeomorphism from the geometric rotation-fixed set onto \(|L_n|\), with inverse distributing that mass equally among edges of \(E_d\). Every dihedral reflection preserves each \(E_d\) setwise. Consequently it fixes pointwise every vector of barycentric coefficients constant on \(E_d\), proving the **equality** of the two geometric fixed spaces.

**3. Prime-divisor nerve.** The nonunit-distance part \(K_n\subseteq L_n\) has face condition \(\gcd(n,S)>1\). For each prime \(p\mid n\), let \(\Sigma_p\) be the full simplex on those distances \(d\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) divisible by \(p\). Then \(K_n=\bigcup_{p\mid n}\Sigma_p\). An intersection \(\bigcap_{p\in T}\Sigma_p\) is a nonempty simplex exactly when the product of the distinct primes in \(T\) is at most \(n/2\). Because this product divides \(n\), the inequality is equivalent to the product being **strictly smaller than** \(n\).

If \(n\) is squarefree composite, every proper subset of its \(r\) prime divisors has product smaller than \(n\), whereas the full subset has product \(n\). The nerve is therefore the boundary of an \((r-1)\)-simplex, so the simplicial nerve lemma gives \(K_n\simeq S^{r-2}\). If \(n\) is nonsquarefree composite, the product of all distinct primes is \(\operatorname{rad}(n)<n\), so the full intersection is a nonempty simplex and the nerve is a full simplex; hence \(K_n\) is contractible. When \(n\) is prime, \(K_n\) has no vertices.

**4. Isolated unit distances and sharp onset.** A distance \(d\) with \(\gcd(n,d)=1\) is in a face only by itself, so it is an isolated vertex. For \(n\ge4\), the distances \(d\in[1,n/2]\) pair opposite reduced residue classes; exactly \(\varphi(n)/2\) distances are units. This proves the three cases and gives the stated integral homology groups by the disjoint-union formula. A nonzero reduced homology group in positive degree occurs exactly when \(n\) has at least three distinct prime divisors and is squarefree; the smallest such integer is \(2\cdot3\cdot5=30\). The four isolated unit distances at \(n=30\) are \(1,7,11,13\), while the nonunit subcomplex has homotopy type \(S^1\). Five connected components yield \(\widetilde H_0\cong\mathbb Z^4\), completing the sharp claim.

## Verification

Run `python verify_cyclic_fixed_not3.py` using Python 3.9 or later; only the standard library is required. The program constructs the actual circulant adjacency masks and directly checks connectivity after every pair of vertex deletions (reduced using the known cyclic vertex transitivity). It exhaustively compares this independent graph calculation against the gcd criterion for **6,117 nonempty distance sets** spanning all \(4\le n\le22\). It separately compares all prime-simplex intersection witnesses for \(4\le n\le120\), totaling 327 cases, and checks the numerical \(n=30\) boundary. The recorded output ends `CYCLIC_FIXED_NOT3_CONNECTIVITY_VERIFIED`.

These computations verify finite instances and the combinatorial intersection arithmetic; the universal mathematical conclusion uses the graph-connectivity bound and the nerve argument above. No homology torsion was inferred from finite numerical sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Babson, Björner, Linusson, Shareshian and Welker initiated the systematic topology of complexes of graphs lacking prescribed vertex-connectivity, including the ordinary nonconnected and non-two-connected cases. Jonsson later proved that the **ordinary**, nonequivariant complex \(\Delta_n^{(3)}\) is a wedge of \((n-3)(n-2)!/2\) spheres of dimension \(2n-4\). Those statements are prior results, not claimed here. The graph-connectivity input is likewise Watkins's known vertex-transitive bound. The present claim concerns the geometric fixed-point subspaces of the natural full polygon symmetry groups, distinguished from the topology and homology of the ambient complex. In particular, the equality of the cyclic and dihedral **geometric fixed sets**, the all-parameter prime-divisor homotopy description, and the sharp integral mixed-degree onset are the asserted refinements.

## Limitations

This statement concerns whole-group geometric fixed spaces, not fixed spaces for arbitrary subgroups or the quotient spaces. The sphere description is a homotopy equivalence from the nerve lemma, not a claim of PL-homeomorphism to a standard sphere. No stronger equivariant homotopy equivalence for the original ambient complex or integral \(S_n\)-module description is claimed. A related book chapter titled *Dihedral Variants of k-connected Graphs* was identifiable by its table of contents but its full chapter was not available in the consulted sources; an alternative formulation there or elsewhere is an unresolved publication-priority risk. The original Watkins article was not obtained in full, although its stated bound is documented in graph theory references and was checked by direct computation in the finite range given above. Independent mathematical review remains outstanding.
