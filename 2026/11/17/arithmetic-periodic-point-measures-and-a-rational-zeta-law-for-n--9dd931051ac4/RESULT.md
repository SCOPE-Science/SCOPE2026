# Arithmetic periodic-point measures and a rational zeta law for nonsofic frequency shifts

## Finding

For every integer \(k\ge1\), put \(L=k+1\) and consider the binary one-sided frequency shift \(\Sigma_k=\Sigma(k/(k+1))\), as defined in Reyes [1]. Denote the shift by \(\sigma\), its finite fixed-point set by \(\operatorname{Fix}_n=\{x\in\Sigma_k:\sigma^n x=x\}\), and its uniform fixed-point probability by
\[
\nu_n=\frac1{|\operatorname{Fix}_n|}\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}_n}\delta_x.
\]
Then the following **single periodic-sampling dichotomy** holds, with an exact periodic-point census and dynamical zeta function:
\[
|\operatorname{Fix}_n|=
\begin{cases}
2+L(2^{n/L}-1),&L\mid n,\\
2,&L\nmid n,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\zeta_{\Sigma_k}(z)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|\operatorname{Fix}_n|}{n}z^n\right)
=\frac{1-z^L}{(1-z)^2(1-2z^L)}.
\]
Write \(\mu_k\) for the unique measure of maximal entropy of \(\Sigma_k\), which exists by [1], and put \(\eta=(\delta_{0^\infty}+\delta_{1^\infty})/2\). Then
\[
\nu_n=\eta\quad\text{if }L\nmid n,
\qquad \nu_{Lm}\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\mathrm{weak}\text{-}*}\mu_k\quad(m\to\infty).
\]
Consequently, the **complete** set of weak-* accumulation measures of \((\nu_n)_{n\ge1}\) consists of precisely \(\{\eta,\mu_k\}\), with \(\eta\ne\mu_k\). More quantitatively, for each fixed one-sided cylinder \(C\),
\[
|\nu_{Lm}(C)-\mu_k(C)|=O_{k,C}(2^{-m/4}).
\]
If \([01]\) denotes the cylinder with first two symbols \(01\), its exact probabilities are
\[
\nu_{Lm}([01])=\frac{2^{m-1}}{2+L(2^m-1)},\qquad
\mu_k([01])=\frac1{2L},\qquad \eta([01])=0.
\]
This is a model-specific, arithmetic failure of full-sequence equidistribution of periodic points despite the uniqueness of the maximal-entropy measure. The statement concerns the mathematical one-sided frequency shifts and not a physical recurrence process.

## Assumptions and scope

The parameter is any integer \(k\ge1\), and \(L=k+1\). We use the **one-sided** shift of [1]; every periodic point has a unique two-sided periodic extension. The source's Theorem C identifies the coded subsystem \(X_k\) generated by the words \(0^g1^{kg}\), \(g\ge1\), and proves that every invariant Borel probability on \(\Sigma_k\) is supported on \(X_k\) (with \(\Sigma_1=X_1\)). Thus each periodic point of \(\Sigma_k\) is in \(X_k\). Theorem D gives uniqueness of \(\mu_k\), and Theorem C gives topological entropy \(\log2/L\). These are prior inputs, not newly established claims.

A length-\(n\) *fixed point* need not have least period \(n\). The formula counts distinct fixed points of \(\sigma^n\), not primitive orbits, and includes the two constant points. The zeta series is initially a formal power series, converging analytically for \(|z|<2^{-1/L}\).

## Proof

**1. Cyclic run form.** Let \(x\in\operatorname{Fix}_n\) be nonconstant. Its two-sided periodic extension has finitely many maximal zero-runs and one-runs in a cycle. In \(X_k\), every complete alternating run pair has the form \(0^g1^{kg}\), because each finite block containing the adjacent run boundaries is a factor of concatenated generators \(0^g1^{kg}\) (Lemma 5.3 and the coded-language characterization in [1]). Hence every nonconstant periodic point is a cyclic concatenation of these generators. Conversely, every such cyclic concatenation defines a periodic point in \(X_k\subseteq\Sigma_k\).

Every junction \(10\) is a boundary between consecutive generators and their lengths are multiples of \(L\). Therefore all such junction positions share one residue modulo \(L\). Since translation by the period \(n\) preserves junction positions, a nonconstant \(n\)-fixed point requires \(L\mid n\). If \(L\nmid n\), the only fixed points are the two constants.

**2. Exact finite census.** Let \(n=Lm\). Specify a residue \(r\in\{0,\dots,L-1\}\) and a nonempty subset \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). Place each \(10\) junction at the positions congruent to \(r+Lj\pmod n\) for \(j\in S\). Between consecutive junctions at distance \(Lg\), fill the first \(g\) positions with zeros and the last \(kg\) positions with ones. This produces a cyclic concatenation of the permitted generators. Conversely, the junction set and its residue are recovered uniquely from every nonconstant periodic word, even if that word has smaller least period. Hence there are exactly \(L(2^m-1)\) nonconstant fixed points, plus the two constants. Summing the resulting formula over \(n\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\zeta_{\Sigma_k}(z)
&=2\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{z^n}{n}
+\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{2^m-1}{m}z^{Lm}\\
&=-2\log(1-z)-\log(1-2z^L)+\log(1-z^L),
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves the rational zeta identity.

**3. Infinite marker measure.** Let \((B_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) be independent fair Bernoulli bits and let \(R\) be uniform on \(\{0,\dots,L-1\}\), independent of those bits. For each \(j\) with \(B_j=1\), place a \(10\) junction at \(R+Lj\), filling the gaps by the same run-pair rule. Almost surely there are infinitely many junctions in both directions and the construction defines a two-sided \(X_k\)-point. Its law \(\widetilde\mu_k\) is invariant under one-step shifting: shifting cycles the phase, and after a complete phase cycle shifts the Bernoulli index by one. The junction positions recover the phase and all bits almost surely, so the coding is a measure-theoretic isomorphism (modulo null sets) to the cyclic suspension of the fair two-sided Bernoulli shift. Thus
\[
h_{\widetilde\mu_k}(\sigma)=\frac{\log2}{L}=h_{\mathrm{top}}(\Sigma_k).
\]
Uniqueness of the maximal-entropy measure [1] forces \(\widetilde\mu_k=\mu_k\) after restriction to nonnegative coordinates. The asymptotic marker density is one half per \(L\) physical sites, which gives \(\mu_k([01])=1/(2L)\).

**4. Periodic sampling and convergence.** At \(n=Lm\), conditional on being nonconstant, a uniformly selected fixed point is exactly a uniformly selected nonempty subset of \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) together with an independent uniform phase. Unconditioning the subset makes its \(m\) markers independent fair bits, with an exceptional empty-subset probability \(2^{-m}\). For a fixed cylinder \(C\) of length \(q\), couple those \(m\) cyclic markers to the bi-infinite fair markers by making them agree on a contiguous interval containing the observation region and extending at most \(r=\lfloor m/4\rfloor\) macro positions in both directions. For all sufficiently large \(m\) (depending on \(q,L\)), the interval contains fewer than \(m\) distinct residues. The resulting physical cylinder agrees whenever a marker occurs in each bracketing interval of length \(r\). The probability that either bracket is empty is at most \(2^{1-r}\). Conditioning the cyclic marker set to be nonempty contributes at most \(2^{-m}/(1-2^{-m})\); the two constant points have total mass \(2/(2+L(2^m-1))\). It follows that
\[
|\nu_{Lm}(C)-\mu_k(C)|\le
2^{1-\lfloor m/4\rfloor}
+\frac{2^{-m}}{1-2^{-m}}
+\frac{2}{2+L(2^m-1)}
\]
for sufficiently large \(m\), proving the stated exponential bound and weak-* convergence.

**5. Separation and exact cylinder statistic.** The nonconstant \(Lm\)-fixed words have a total of \(L\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}|S|=Lm2^{m-1}\) occurrences of \(01\), counted across all \(L(2^m-1)\) indexed words and all physical positions. Translation invariance of the word ensemble shows that exactly \(2^{m-1}\) indexed words begin with \(01\). Adding constant words changes only the denominator and gives the displayed formula for \(\nu_{Lm}([01])\). When \(L\nmid n\), \(\nu_n=\eta\), and \(\eta([01])=0\). Since \(\mu_k([01])=1/(2L)>0\), the two measures are different. Every integer belongs either to the multiples or the nonmultiples of \(L\); these subsequences have the stated limits, establishing that no third accumulation measure exists. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying `checker.py` uses two independent finite descriptions: direct cyclic run tests and explicit junction-subset generation. It checks fixed-point counts for \(k=1,2,3,4\) and \(1\le n\le14\), checks that the two finite descriptions give identical sets for feasible \(n\le14\), verifies injectivity of the subset/phase construction, and checks exact \([01]\) probabilities. On the reviewed artifact it printed `PASS 67 finite parameter/length census and marker checks`. The unrestricted statement and the exponential convergence rate rely on the analytic proof, not finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Reyes [1] proves the coded-generator structure, nonsoficity, nonmixing, entropy, and uniqueness of the maximal-entropy measure, but does not state the above fixed-point counts, zeta function, or periodic-sampling limits. The coded-shift equilibrium theory of Kucherenko, Schmoll, and Wolf [2] gives broad methods for maximal-entropy measures, not a count of periodic points in this particular frequency shift. Known zeta calculations for periodic-finite-type shifts [3] and for Sturmian-decorated gap shifts [4] concern different symbolic constraints. In particular, neither the general zeta methodology nor nonmixing alone forces the exact two-limit phenomenon, which here depends on the coexisting constant fixed points and the arithmetic generator lengths.

## Limitations

The exact result concerns the rational family \(\Sigma(k/(k+1))\), not arbitrary frequency parameters or all coded shifts. It uses uniform weighting of **points** fixed by \(\sigma^n\), not uniform weighting of primitive orbits or empirical measures along a single trajectory. No claim is made about first-order mixing, spectral gaps of general transfer operators, or uniqueness among unrelated mathematical models. The originality comparison cannot rule out unpublished or unindexed calculations, and the verification code checks only finite instances. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
