# Scientific review of the periodic-point sampling dichotomy

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claim is restricted to positive integer \(k\) and to the one-sided frequency shift \(\Sigma(k/(k+1))\). The source's invariant-support theorem puts every periodic point in the uniquely decipherable coded core. Every nonconstant cyclic word has alternating run pairs \(0^g1^{kg}\); a junction residue and a nonempty set of macro-position cuts determine precisely one word. This proves the exact fixed-point census, from which the zeta identity follows as a formal logarithm. The Bernoulli macro-marker suspension is an almost-sure bijection with entropy \(\log2/(k+1)\), hence equals the unique maximal-entropy measure. A local marker coupling gives the claimed exponential fixed-cylinder convergence, and the exact \([01]\) count separates the two cluster measures. The finite checker reproduces 67 census and cylinder instances. Correctness does not rest on the finite checker alone.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical priority limitations.** The closest primary source, Reyes, arXiv:2611.10995v1, establishes generator structure, nonmixing, entropy and intrinsic ergodicity, not the uniform-periodic-point measure dichotomy or exact zeta. The general equilibrium results of Kucherenko–Schmoll–Wolf concern maximal-entropy measures and do not supply model-specific periodic measures. Published rational-zeta results for periodic-finite-type shifts and Sturmian-decorated gaps use different admissibility constraints. Indexed mathematical-record searches for exact formulas, aliases, periodic sampling and nonmixing arithmetic return measures did not reveal an identical result. The closed-form census alone is relatively elementary given the generator characterization; the mathematically distinctive conclusion is the sharp split between the MME and atomic sampling limits in a system with a unique MME. The decorated-gap full text was inaccessible, and unindexed calculations remain a risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Uniform periodic-point sampling is a natural method of approximating equilibrium statistics. In intrinsically ergodic systems it is important to know if these finite ensembles converge to the unique MME. This family exhibits a full-sequence failure caused by a precise arithmetic obstruction: at nonmultiples of \(k+1\), only two fixed atoms exist, while at multiples exponentially many nonconstant points dominate. The theorem gives every fixed-point count, an exact zeta, two and only two weak-* limits, and an exponential convergence rate on the ergodic subsequence. It sharply separates uniqueness of an equilibrium measure from convergence of a natural periodic approximation scheme, with exact finite-cylinder witnesses. This is not asserted to be a general theorem about all coded systems.

## Limitations

The statement is for this rational family and point-uniform fixed-period measures. The proof uses the source's Theorems C and D. No external audit or independent certification has been performed. Historical overlap comparisons are bounded by accessible published sources and indexed records, and the model-specific novelty assessment should be independently checked before external publication.
