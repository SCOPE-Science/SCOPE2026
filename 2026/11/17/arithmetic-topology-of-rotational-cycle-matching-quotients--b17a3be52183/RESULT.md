# Arithmetic topology of rotational cycle-matching quotients

## Finding
For an integer \(n\geq3\), let \(C_n\) be the undirected cycle with edges labeled by \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), and let \(K_n=\mathcal M(C_n)=\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\) be its ordinary matching complex. Thus a face is a subset of cyclic edge positions containing no pair of consecutive positions. Let \(r(i)=i+1\) act simplicially, and let \(Q_n=|K_n|/\langle r\rangle\) be the **ordinary geometric orbit space**, not the abstract complex obtained by identifying vertices.

**Theorem (one arithmetic quotient classification).** For every \(n\geq8\),
\[
\pi_1(Q_n)\cong H_1(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,&n=p^e\text{ for a prime }p\text{ and }e\geq1,\\
0,&n\text{ has at least two different prime factors}.
\end{cases}
\]
Write \(d_n=\lfloor(n-2)/3\rfloor\). Its complete positive-degree **rational** homology is
\[
\widetilde H_j(Q_n;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&j=d_n\text{ and }\gcd(n,6)=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Here \(H_0(Q_n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\). Moreover, at **every odd prime** \(p\geq5\), put \(m=\lfloor(p+1)/6\rfloor\), so \(d_p=2m-1\). The entire integral homology of the free cyclic quotient is
\[
H_j(Q_p;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0\text{ or }j=2m-1,\\
\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,&j\in\{1,3,\ldots,2m-3\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Its full integral cohomology ring is
\[
H^*(Q_p;\mathbb Z)\cong
\mathbb Z[u,v]/(pu,u^m,uv,v^2),
\qquad |u|=2,\quad |v|=2m-1.
\]
For \(m=1\), the relation \(u^m=0\) sets \(u=0\), as required. This is a homology-and-cohomology-ring comparison with a lens space, **not** a claim of homeomorphism or homotopy equivalence to a lens space.

## Assumptions and scope
The face complex is the ordinary undirected-graph matching complex, equivalently the independence complex of the line graph \(L(C_n)\cong C_n\). Vertices of the complex are the **edges** of the graph, not the graph's vertices, even though cyclic labels identify the sets. The action is full cyclic edge rotation, not the full dihedral action. The all-length fundamental-group and rational statements require \(n\geq8\), when \(K_n\) is simply connected; the integral prime statement applies separately to \(p\geq5\). Rational vanishing never asserts integral acyclicity at composite lengths, where uncomputed higher torsion may remain.

## Proof
**1. Covering-complex homotopy.** The classical cycle-matching calculation, established by Kozlov and reproduced by Adamaszek and by Donovan–Scoville, gives, for \(k\geq1\),
\[
K_{3k}\simeq S^{k-1}\vee S^{k-1},\qquad
K_{3k+1}\simeq S^{k-1},\qquad
K_{3k+2}\simeq S^{k}.
\]
Thus \(K_n\) is connected and simply connected whenever \(n\geq8\), and has reduced rational homology in exactly degree \(d_n\), with rank two for \(3\mid n\) and rank one otherwise.

**2. Fixed elements and fundamental group.** Consider \(r^a\) with \(0<a<n\) and \(g=\gcd(n,a)\). It partitions the \(n\) complex vertices into cyclic orbits consisting of congruent edge labels modulo \(g\). If \(g=1\), every nonempty invariant face would have to contain all \(n\) edges, contradicting the matching condition. Hence \(|K_n|^{r^a}=\varnothing\). If \(g\geq2\), the orbit of edge-position \(0\) consists of positions \(0,g,2g,\ldots,n-g\), which are pairwise nonconsecutive modulo \(n\). That orbit is a face, and its barycenter is fixed. A point of a simplicial realization is fixed precisely when its support face is invariant and its barycentric coordinates are constant on each vertex orbit. Therefore
\[
|K_n|^{r^a}\neq\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \gcd(n,a)\geq2.
\]
Armstrong's orbit-space theorem, applicable to a simply connected finite simplicial complex and a finite group action, gives \(\pi_1(Q_n)\cong C_n/N\), where \(N\) is generated by all rotations with fixed points. If \(n=p^e\), exactly the exponents divisible by \(p\) occur, so \(N=\langle r^p\rangle\) and \(C_n/N\cong C_p\). If distinct primes \(p,q\) divide \(n\), both \(r^p,r^q\) fix points; together they generate \(C_n\), so the quotient is trivial. Degree-one integral homology is the abelianization of \(\pi_1\).

**3. Exact rational homology.** The generator \(r\) has no fixed point. The ordinary Lefschetz number of \(r\) is zero, hence its reduced Lefschetz number is \(-1\). Only reduced degree \(d_n\) homology is nonzero, so
\[
\operatorname{tr}\left(r\mid\widetilde H_{d_n}(K_n;\mathbb Q)\right)=(-1)^{d_n+1}.
\]
If \(3\nmid n\), this is a one-dimensional rational representation. For \(n\) odd its generator acts as \(+1\), since its finite order is odd; for \(n\) even the displayed trace is \(-1\) (directly from the three-periodic formula for \(d_n\)). Thus the invariant rank is one precisely when \(\gcd(n,6)=1\). If \(3\mid n\), the representation has dimension two and the generator has trace \(+1\) or \(-1\), depending on \(n/3\)'s parity. A two-dimensional rational finite-order representation with trace \(+1\) (respectively \(-1\)) is the primitive sixth-root (respectively cubic-root) representation, and has no invariant line. Finally, for a finite group, barycentric subdivision makes rational cellular coinvariants of the action canonically equivalent to invariants by averaging; consequently
\[
H_*(Q_n;\mathbb Q)\cong H_*(K_n;\mathbb Q)^{C_n}.
\]
This yields the claimed complete rational homology.

**4. Prime-length integral cohomology ring.** For an odd prime \(p\geq5\), all nonidentity powers of \(r\) are fixed-point-free by Step 2. Hence \(K_p\to Q_p\) is a free regular \(C_p\)-cover. By Step 1, \(K_p\simeq S^{2m-1}\). Since \(p\) is odd, the orientation action on \(H^{2m-1}(K_p;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\) is trivial. The multiplicative integral Cartan–Leray (equivalently, Borel–Serre) cohomology spectral sequence for this free action has just rows \(b=0,2m-1\):
\[
E_2^{a,b}=H^a(C_p;H^b(K_p;\mathbb Z))\Longrightarrow H^{a+b}(Q_p;\mathbb Z).
\]
With trivial coefficients the base ring is \(H^*(BC_p;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[t]/(pt)\), where \(|t|=2\). The only possible nonzero differential joins these two rows:
\[
d_{2m}:E_{2m}^{0,2m-1}=\mathbb Z\longrightarrow E_{2m}^{2m,0}=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z.
\]
It must be surjective. Otherwise it is zero, and multiplicativity forces the entire \(t\)-tower in the base row to survive to arbitrarily high total degree, contradicting that \(Q_p\) is a finite-dimensional CW complex. As a module over the base ring, surjectivity makes \(d_{2m}\) an isomorphism between every pair of corresponding positive-even \(\mathbb Z/p\)-terms. The surviving classes are \(1\), the powers \(u^j\) for \(1\le j<m\), and the infinite-cyclic top class \(v\) in degree \(2m-1\). Their products yield exactly \(pu=u^m=uv=v^2=0\) and no further relation (the cup powers are detected by the filtration). Thus the stated integral cohomology ring holds. The integral homology follows from the universal coefficient theorem: free groups in degrees \(0,2m-1\), cyclic \(p\)-torsion in positive odd degrees below \(2m-1\), and zero elsewhere.

## Verification
The provided standard-library program `verify_rotation.py` enumerates all independent edge-position faces for \(8\le n\le18\), checks three-periodic Euler characteristics and generator Lefschetz numbers, computes each rotation's geometric fixed Euler characteristic by invariant matching-orbit enumeration, checks Burnside quotient Euler characteristics and the consequent rational Betti ranks, and exactly calculates stabilizer-generated subgroups. All tested cases pass. The separate `verify_prime_quotients.py` computes *integer* cellular boundary matrices for the free prime-rotation quotient, verifies consecutive boundary compositions vanish, and calculates integral Smith normal forms for \(p=5,7,11,13\). Their free ranks and torsion factors agree with the theorem. `check_output.txt` contains the actual execution output. These finite tests corroborate the proofs; they are not a substitute for the infinite arguments and do not independently check the multiplicative cohomology ring.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's cycle matching/independence-complex homotopy formula is an essential older input, also discussed in Waßmer's dissertation, proved by Adamaszek via splitting, and rederived by Donovan–Scoville using star clusters. The topological orbit-space theorem is due to Armstrong; the finite-group spectral sequence is classical. The published *Prime-power fundamental groups and unbounded torsion in bidirected-polygon orbit spaces* establishes a closely analogous prime-power isotropy mechanism but concerns the **different bidirected multipath complex**, not the cycle matching complex. Its partial prime-degree homology statement neither identifies this quotient nor implies its **full prime-length integral cohomology ring**. The other related published cyclic/dihedral results identified by semantic search concern cycle powers, higher independence complexes, or wheel matching complexes, not the ordinary cycle matching quotient. The inspected sources do not provide the combined all-length rational/fundamental-group and prime-length integral-ring classification in the theorem.

## Limitations
At composite lengths the result does **not** determine higher integral torsion, cohomology products, or homotopy type. At prime lengths the theorem specifies the cohomology ring but not a geometric lens-space homeomorphism or a classification of the attaching maps. An unindexed equivalent quotient theorem or a broader equivariant result subsuming the combined classification remains a publication-priority risk. The primary Kozlov paper's full text was not inspected; the cycle homotopy theorem was independently verified from later accessible full-text treatments. The use of standard group-action theorems is explicit; no original general fixed-point or spectral-sequence machinery is claimed.
