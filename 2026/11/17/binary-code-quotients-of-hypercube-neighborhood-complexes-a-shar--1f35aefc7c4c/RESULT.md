# Binary-code quotients of hypercube neighborhood complexes: a sharp freeness threshold and integral torsion

## Finding
Let \(d\ge4\), let \(Q_d\) be the graph on \(\mathbb F_2^d\) joining vertices at Hamming distance one, and let \(X_d=|\mathcal N(Q_d)|\) be the realization of its ordinary **open** Lovász neighborhood complex. Let \(C\le\mathbb F_2^d\) be any binary linear code, acting by translations on \(X_d\). Put \(E=C\cap\ker(\operatorname{wt}\bmod2)\), \(r=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}E\), and \(\mu_d=2^{d-3}(d^2-5d+8)-1\).

**Theorem.** The action of \(C\) on \(X_d\) is free **if and only if** \(C\) has no nonzero word of Hamming weight two. Under this sharp hypothesis, \(X_d/C\) has two connected components if \(C=E\), and one component otherwise. For every connected component \(Y\) of the quotient,
\[
\pi_1(Y)\cong E\cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^r,\qquad
H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^r,
\]
\[
H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{\,2^{d-3-r}(d^2-5d+8)-1},\qquad
H_j(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(j\ge3),
\]
with \(H_0(Y;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\). Moreover \(Y\) admits a finite two-dimensional CW spine. These formulas include \(C=0\) and codes containing odd-weight words. The theorem determines the fundamental group and **all integral homology groups**, not the complete homotopy type, of each quotient component.

The sharp boundary is natural: a weight-two translation fixes the midpoint of an edge of \(\mathcal N(Q_d)\). In the free range, the code's even subcode is recovered as the quotient's fundamental group, producing arbitrarily large ranks of integral first-homology two-torsion.

## Assumptions and scope
All vector spaces and code dimensions are over \(\mathbb F_2\). Hamming weight counts nonzero coordinates. The ordinary open neighborhood complex has vertices all binary strings and faces all nonempty subsets admitting a **common graph neighbor**; it is distinct from the radius-one closed-neighborhood Čech nerve and from a Vietoris–Rips complex. The quotient is a quotient of the **geometric realization**, not the clique complex of a quotient graph. The action of a code means coordinatewise addition; there is no coordinate-permutation action here. The theorem requires \(d\ge4\) and the explicitly stated sharp weight-two exclusion. It asserts nothing about the quotient homology when the action is not free.

## Proof
**1. Two components, explicit two-dimensional collapse, and simple connectivity.** Every face of \(\mathcal N(Q_d)\) is monochromatic for the parity of the vertex Hamming weights. Its even-vertex component \(K_d\) has facets \(F_y=\{y+e_i:1\le i\le d\}\), indexed by odd vectors \(y\). Distinct facets meet in at most two vertices: a pair of different cube vertices has two common graph neighbors exactly when their Hamming distance is two, and otherwise no common neighbors. In particular each face with at least three vertices lies in a unique facet.

Choose \(a_y=y+e_1\) in each facet. In each \(F_y\) collapse every pair \(\sigma\subset\sigma\cup\{a_y\}\) with \(\sigma\subseteq F_y\setminus\{a_y\}\), \(|\sigma|\ge3\), in decreasing order of \(|\sigma|\). All removed faces have at least three vertices and lie in their unique facet, so the local collapses concatenate. The retained complex \(L_d\) contains all edges and exactly the triangles
\[
\{x,x+g_j,x+g_k\},\quad g_j=e_1+e_j,\quad 2\le j<k\le d,
\]
for even \(x\). Translation by any vector carries a facet and its designated anchor to the corresponding facet and anchor; in particular this collapse matching is equivariant for all **even** translations.

The one-skeleton of \(L_d\) joins even vectors at distance two. Every edge whose difference is \(g_j+g_k\) is homotopic relative to endpoints to a path of two edges of types \(g_j\) and \(g_k\) through one retained triangle. Thus each loop can be expressed in the cube graph generated by \(g_2,\ldots,g_d\). The two retained triangles based at \(x\) and \(x+g_j+g_k\) fill every coordinate square. The ordinary cubical two-skeleton is simply connected (higher-dimensional cells of the contractible cube do not alter its fundamental group), so \(K_d\) is simply connected. The odd component is its translate and also simply connected.

The cell counts in \(L_d\) are
\[
 f_0=2^{d-1},\quad f_1=2^{d-2}\binom d2,\quad f_2=2^{d-1}\binom{d-1}{2}.
\]
Thus \(\chi(K_d)=2^{d-3}(d^2-5d+8)=1+\mu_d\). These collapse, simple-connectivity and cell-count premises can also be found in the previously published hypercube neighborhood-complex calculation cited below; they are reconstructed here to make the deduction self-contained.

**2. A sharp free-action criterion.** A nonzero translation by \(c\) can stabilize a point of a simplicial realization only if it stabilizes the point's unique supporting face setwise. Translation by \(c\) has order two, so any invariant nonempty face contains some pair \(\{x,x+c\}\). Such a pair has a common cube neighbor exactly when \(\operatorname{wt}(c)=2\). Conversely, if \(\operatorname{wt}(c)=2\), this pair is an edge of the neighborhood complex and its midpoint is fixed. This proves that the full code action is free exactly under the stated exclusion. Notice that nonzero odd-weight translations exchange the two parity components and cannot fix points.

**3. Component quotients and their fundamental groups.** The even translations \(E\) preserve each component, while each odd translation exchanges the two. Hence when the action is free, each component of \(X_d/C\) is homeomorphic to \(|K_d|/E\); there are two such components when \(C=E\), and one otherwise. The connected simply connected \(|K_d|\) is the universal cover of \(Y=|K_d|/E\), with free deck group \(E\). Therefore \(\pi_1(Y)\cong E\) and \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong E\), since \(E\) is abelian.

**4. Dimension and remaining integral homology.** The anchor-face collapse in step 1 commutes with every even translation. The absence of weight-two codewords makes the action free on all closed simplices: otherwise an invariant simplex would contain two vertices differing by such a codeword. Therefore the face-orbit matching descends to collapses of a subdivided quotient CW complex. In particular \(Y\) is homotopy equivalent to the finite two-dimensional quotient of \(L_d\). Its cellular chain complex has no groups above dimension two. Thus all higher homology vanishes, and \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=\ker(\partial_2)\) is a subgroup of a finite free abelian group, hence free abelian.

Since the action is free on cells, \(\chi(Y)=\chi(K_d)/|E|=2^{d-3-r}(d^2-5d+8)\). The group \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong E\) is finite, so its rational rank vanishes. Euler characteristic of the two-dimensional connected CW complex yields \(\operatorname{rank}H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=\chi(Y)-1\), as claimed. In particular the displayed exponent is a nonnegative integer whenever the code hypotheses hold.

**5. Unbounded torsion, including the first cases.** In dimension four, the repetition code \(E=\langle1111\rangle\) is free, and each of the two quotient components has \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) and \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^3\). In dimension six, \(E=\langle001111,111100\rangle\) has three nonzero words of weight four, and each component has \(H_1\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\) and \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{27}\). For every integer \(r\ge3\), the binary simplex code on \(d=2^r-1\) coordinates, whose generator columns are all nonzero vectors of \(\mathbb F_2^r\), has dimension \(r\) and every nonzero word has weight \(2^{r-1}\ge4\). The theorem consequently realizes \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^r\) as the fundamental group and first integral homology of these natural quotient spaces. In the first such case, \(d=7,r=3\), the second-homology rank is \(43\).

## Verification
The included Python standard-library program `artifacts/verify.py` independently constructs the even parity vertices and the retained two-complex, and forms cell orbits under selected binary linear codes in dimensions \(4,5,6,7\). It checks that the vertex/edge/triangle orbit counts are scaled exactly by the code order and builds the cellular boundary matrices **over** \(\mathbb F_2\), allowing multiple edges and triangles with the same projected vertex sets. This avoids the unsound shortcut of treating the quotient as an abstract simplicial complex. It calculates the mod-two Betti dimensions \((1,1,4)\), \((1,1,16)\), \((1,2,29)\), and \((1,3,46)\), respectively. The second mod-two dimension exceeds the free integral \(H_2\)-rank by \(r\), exactly as prescribed by the universal coefficient contribution \(\operatorname{Tor}(H_1,\mathbb F_2)\).

The code enumerations are finite corroboration. The argument above, not the finite tests, establishes the all-dimension, all-code result.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov (first public arXiv version, 2005) develops foundational neighborhood-complex formal deformations and the relationship with graph-Hom constructions, but does not determine this code-quotient homology. Matsushita (2012) proves that graph two-coverings induce neighborhood-complex coverings and analyzes fundamental groups; his general results do not supply a sharp weight-two binary-code quotient classification or the stated integral second-homology formula. In particular, graph two-coverings impose a stronger local-neighborhood condition than the geometric free action used here.

A more recent public result, *Neighborhood complexes of hypercubes collapse to explicit 2-sphere wedges* (2026), proves the underlying nonequivariant two-component sphere-wedge formula and the facetwise collapse. **That sphere-wedge theorem is prior work, not a new claim here.** Its stated ordinary homotopy classification does not determine actions of binary codes or quotient fundamental groups; the contribution here is the sharp freeness criterion, code-component reduction, equivariant descent, and all-code integral quotient calculation. Another nearby published calculation concerns hypercube radius-two **closed** Čech complexes and does not make a statement about the open-neighborhood quotient analyzed here.

## Limitations
When a code contains a weight-two word, the theorem identifies failure of freeness but does not classify the nonfree quotient. For free actions, it determines \(\pi_1\) and all integral homology groups, **not** the full homotopy or homeomorphism type of the quotient. The final formulas rely on the special topology and facet structure of binary hypercube open-neighborhood complexes; arbitrary graphs and nontranslation symmetries are outside scope. Finite verification tests only four representative code parameters and cannot establish the universal statement. Publication-priority comparisons are necessarily bounded: an unindexed older equivalent code-quotient result remains a residual risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
