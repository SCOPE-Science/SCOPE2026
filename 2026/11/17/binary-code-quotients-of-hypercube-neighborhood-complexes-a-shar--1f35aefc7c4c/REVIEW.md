# Scientific assessment: binary-code quotients of hypercube neighborhood complexes

## Correctness — PASS
The general assertion is proved for every \(d\ge4\) and binary code without a weight-two word. A translation can stabilize a geometric point only by stabilizing its support simplex; its involutive vertex pairs differ by the codeword. The existence of such a simplex is equivalent to weight two. A direct unique-facet collapse reconstructs a simply connected two-dimensional spine of each parity component and is translation-equivariant under the even subcode. The free quotient is therefore a two-dimensional finite CW complex with fundamental group exactly the even subcode. The integral second homology is free by cellular dimension, and its rank follows from exact Euler characteristic. The distinction between mod-two second Betti dimension and integral second-homology rank is explicitly handled by the universal coefficient theorem. Four standalone finite quotient-chain tests corroborate the proof but are not used to infer its generality.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The closest published result, *Neighborhood complexes of hypercubes collapse to explicit 2-sphere wedges* (2026), establishes precisely the nonequivariant sphere-wedge theorem and gives the facetwise collapse. Its complete RESULT.md was compared. The new assertion is not a restatement of that ordinary homotopy theorem: geometric code-action freeness, the sharp Hamming weight-two threshold, the even-code deck groups, the number of quotient components and all integral quotient homology groups require additional equivariant reasoning. Kozlov's 2005 formal deformations concern generic neighborhood/Hom complexes, not binary-code quotient topology. Matsushita's 2012 two-covering and fundamental-group results do not give the weight-four boundary or second-homology ranks. published-finding corpus searches for the code-quotient formulation and alternate aliases found no statement giving this result. The 2026 published result is credited, not repackaged as a discovery. An unpublished/unindexed equivalent and stronger theory of equivariant hypercube neighborhoods remains a publication-priority risk; searching alone cannot prove novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural binary linear coding data control exactly when the quotient becomes a genuine regular covering and then classify its integral homology and fundamental group for **all** codes in the free range. The cutoff is sharp and distinguishable from stronger graph-two-covering criteria. The first canonical cases have quotient first homology \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) and \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\); binary simplex codes give arbitrarily high finite two-torsion rank in degree one while the simply connected covering components have homology concentrated in degree two. These exact group-valued invariants and the weight boundary are motivated by the interaction of error-correcting code distance with covering topology; they are more than a generic nonequivariant sphere count. No classification of nonfree quotients or complete homotopy types is claimed.
