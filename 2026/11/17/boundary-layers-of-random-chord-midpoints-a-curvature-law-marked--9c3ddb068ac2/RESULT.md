# Boundary layers of random chord midpoints: a curvature law, marked limit, and optimal sampling

## Finding

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a compact strictly convex planar body with \(C^3\) boundary \(\Gamma\) and everywhere positive curvature \(\kappa(s)\), parametrized once by arclength \(s\in\mathbb T_L=\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z\), where \(L>0\) is the perimeter. Fix any continuous nonnegative boundary probability density \(f\) with \(\int_0^L f(s)\,ds=1\). Independently draw \(X=\gamma(S_1)\) and \(Y=\gamma(S_2)\) with this density. Put \(M=(X+Y)/2\) and \(D=\operatorname{dist}(M,\partial K)\). Define
\[
J_K(f)=\int_0^L\frac{f(s)^2}{\sqrt{\kappa(s)}}\,ds,
\qquad H_K=\int_0^L\sqrt{\kappa(s)}\,ds.
\]
Then, for every fixed such \(K,f\),
\[
\boxed{\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\Pr(D<\varepsilon)=4\sqrt2\,J_K(f).}\tag{1}
\]

There is also a conditional **total-variation** law. Outside a null set, let \(h\in(-L/2,L/2)\) denote the shortest *signed* arclength difference from \(S_1\) to \(S_2\), and put \(s=(S_1+h/2)\bmod L\), the mid-arclength coordinate. On the event \(D<\varepsilon\), define
\[
Z_\varepsilon=\frac{h\sqrt{\kappa(s)}}{\sqrt{8\varepsilon}}.
\]
The conditional joint law of \((s,Z_\varepsilon)\), relative to \(ds\,dz\) on \(\mathbb T_L\times\mathbb R\), converges in total variation to
\[
\boxed{\frac{f(s)^2\kappa(s)^{-1/2}}{2J_K(f)}\,\mathbf1_{\{-1<z<1\}}\,ds\,dz.}\tag{2}
\]
Thus the limiting location has density \(f^2\kappa^{-1/2}/J_K(f)\), independently of a signed uniform mark on \((-1,1)\). Moreover, the normalized inward distance satisfies the **weak** conditional limit
\[
\boxed{D/\varepsilon\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Beta}(1/2,1).}\tag{3}
\]

For each fixed \(K\), the leading coefficient (1) has the sharp sampling inequality
\[
\boxed{4\sqrt2\,J_K(f)\ge\frac{4\sqrt2}{H_K},}\tag{4}
\]
with equality **if and only if** \(f(s)=\sqrt{\kappa(s)}/H_K\) almost everywhere. Across all such bodies of fixed perimeter \(L\), even after optimizing \(f\),
\[
\boxed{4\sqrt2\,J_K(f)\ge\frac4{\sqrt{\pi L}},}\tag{5}
\]
with equality **if and only if** \(K\) is a Euclidean disk and \(f\) is uniform in arclength. For uniform arclength density, the range of the leading coefficient over smooth positively curved convex bodies of fixed perimeter is exactly \([4/\sqrt{\pi L},\infty)\): it is unbounded above and every value in that interval occurs already along a continuous one-parameter family of ellipses.

## Assumptions and scope

The body \(K\) is fixed as \(\varepsilon\to0\); its positive curvature has a fixed positive minimum, and \(f\) is fixed and continuous with total mass one. A **chord** here is specifically generated by *two independent boundary points*, not by an invariant random line or by a uniform midpoint. The event \(D<\varepsilon\) measures ordinary Euclidean distance of the chord midpoint to the original boundary. The domain of the marked limit is a circle coordinate and one real coordinate; the signed shorter difference is unique except on a set of probability zero. The result is not asserted uniformly over degenerating families of ovals or changing sampling densities.

## Proof

**1. Localization at the diagonal.** Let \(\gamma:\mathbb T_L\to\partial K\) be a positively oriented unit-speed parametrization, with inward normal \(n(s)\). Strict convexity implies that \(\frac12(\gamma(s_1)+\gamma(s_2))\in\operatorname{int}K\) whenever \(s_1\ne s_2\). Indeed, were this midpoint on the boundary, the supporting line there would contain both endpoints, violating strict convexity. Compactness therefore gives, for any fixed \(\eta>0\), a positive lower bound for midpoint-boundary distance when the shorter arclength difference has magnitude at least \(\eta\). Consequently \(D<\varepsilon\) forces \(|h|\to0\) uniformly as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

**2. Exact local normalization.** In the unique short-chord chart, write
\[
S_1=s-h/2,\qquad S_2=s+h/2\pmod L.
\]
The transformation has arclength Jacobian one. Since \(\gamma''(s)=\kappa(s)n(s)\), the uniform Taylor expansion gives
\[
\frac{\gamma(s-h/2)+\gamma(s+h/2)}2
=\gamma(s)+\frac{\kappa(s)h^2}{8}n(s)+o(h^2)
\qquad(h\to0),
\]
uniformly in \(s\). A compact positively curved \(C^3\) curve has a fixed inward normal tubular neighborhood in which the signed distance function is continuously differentiable with normal derivative one at the boundary. Therefore
\[
\boxed{D(s,h)=\frac{\kappa(s)h^2}{8}+o(h^2)}\tag{6}
\]
uniformly in \(s\). Positivity and compactness also give constants \(c,C,\eta_0>0\) with \(ch^2\le D(s,h)\le Ch^2\) for \(|h|<\eta_0\). Combined with step 1, this gives \(|h|\le C'\sqrt\varepsilon\) on \(D<\varepsilon\), for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).

**3. Total-variation density.** Set \(h=\sqrt{8\varepsilon/\kappa(s)}\,z\) in the short-chord chart. The unnormalized joint density of \((s,z)\) on the conditioning event is
\[
q_\varepsilon(s,z)=\sqrt{\frac{8\varepsilon}{\kappa(s)}}
\,f\!\left(s-\frac h2\right)f\!\left(s+\frac h2\right)
\mathbf1_{\{D(s,h)<\varepsilon\}}.
\]
Step 2 bounds its support in a fixed compact \(z\)-interval for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). Formula (6) yields \(D(s,h)/\varepsilon\to z^2\) uniformly for bounded \(z\) and \(s\), and continuity of \(f\) gives, almost everywhere,
\[
\varepsilon^{-1/2}q_\varepsilon(s,z)
\longrightarrow\frac{\sqrt8\,f(s)^2}{\sqrt{\kappa(s)}}\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}.
\]
The functions are uniformly bounded and supported in a common finite-measure region. Dominated convergence therefore gives \(L^1(ds\,dz)\) convergence. Integrating the limit gives
\[
\varepsilon^{-1/2}\Pr(D<\varepsilon)
\longrightarrow\sqrt8\cdot2\int_0^L f(s)^2\kappa(s)^{-1/2}\,ds=4\sqrt2\,J_K(f),
\]
proving (1). Since \(J_K(f)>0\), normalization of the same \(L^1\) convergence proves total variation in (2) (total variation is one half the \(L^1\) distance between probability densities).

**4. Depth mark.** On the conditioning event, (6) implies \(D/\varepsilon-Z_\varepsilon^2\to0\) uniformly as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Combining this with (2) and the continuous mapping theorem gives \(D/\varepsilon\Rightarrow U^2\), where \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}(-1,1)\). This is \(\operatorname{Beta}(1/2,1)\), establishing (3). **No total-variation claim for the exact depth variable is needed.**

**5. Optimal density and body.** Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
1=\left(\int f\,ds\right)^2
\le\left(\int\frac{f^2}{\sqrt\kappa}\,ds\right)
\left(\int\sqrt\kappa\,ds\right)=J_K(f)H_K.
\]
Equality requires \(f\propto\sqrt\kappa\), proving (4) and uniqueness. By total turning angle and Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
H_K^2\le L\int_0^L\kappa\,ds=2\pi L,
\]
with equality precisely at constant curvature, which for a closed positively curved planar curve means a disk. Combining gives (5) with its equality condition. For \(f=1/L\), Jensen independently yields the same disk minimum. To see that the uniform-density coefficient is unbounded above at fixed \(L\), take ellipses with semiaxes \(a>b>0\), scaled to perimeter \(L\). In eccentric-anomaly coordinates \(t\), let \(q(t)=\sqrt{a^2\sin^2t+b^2\cos^2t}\). Then
\[
\kappa(t)=\frac{ab}{q(t)^3},\qquad
\int_0^L\kappa^{-1/2}\,ds=\frac1{\sqrt{ab}}\int_0^{2\pi}q(t)^{5/2}\,dt.
\]
As \(b\downarrow0\) at fixed perimeter, \(a\to L/4\) and the last integral diverges at least like a positive constant times \(b^{-1/2}\). Continuous dependence of the coefficient on the axis ratio and the disk endpoint imply that every value above the sharp minimum occurs.

## Verification

For a circle of radius \(R\) with independent uniform endpoints, the shorter angle difference is exactly uniform on \([0,\pi]\) and the midpoint has boundary depth \(R(1-\cos(\Theta/2))\). Hence for \(0<\varepsilon<R\),
\[
\Pr(D<\varepsilon)=\frac2\pi\arccos(1-\varepsilon/R)
\sim\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi\sqrt R}\sqrt\varepsilon,
\]
matching (1) at \(f=1/(2\pi R)\). This is a **known circle special case** used as a normalization check, not claimed as new.

The self-contained `verify_midpoint.py` performs independent ellipse-curvature integration and directly samples chord endpoint pairs with both uniform and curvature-optimal densities, measuring the midpoint-to-boundary Euclidean distance by a high-resolution boundary nearest-neighbor computation. For semiaxes \((2,1)\) it gives perimeter \(9.68844822055\), predicted coefficients \(0.866155939587\) (uniform density) and \(0.774072242382\) (optimal density), and global fixed-perimeter lower bound \(0.725033283312\). With 220,000 samples per density at \(\varepsilon=0.001\), the observed event frequencies are \(0.0284864\) and \(0.0247000\), compared with respective leading predictions \(0.0273903\) and \(0.0244783\). Conditional mean squared signed marks are \(0.33311\) and \(0.33392\), close to the theoretical \(1/3\). The script reports `MIDPOINT_BOUNDARY_CHECKS_OK`. Finite-grid nearest-boundary approximation and sampling uncertainty are numerical diagnostics, not proofs of the infinite-domain statement.

## Relationship to prior work

Classical Bertrand-chord treatments distinguish independent uniform endpoints from invariant lines and uniform midpoints. Comets, Popov, Schütz and Vachkovskaia explicitly explain these competing laws and develop a **cosine-reflection** chord process in a full, publicly available 2006 preprint [1]. Their constructed chord has a different endpoint coupling; their stated results concern invariant measures, mean chord lengths, induced chords and billiards, not the independent-endpoint midpoint-boundary conditional law (1)--(3).

Ehlers and Enns's 1981 work [2] concerns lengths of random secants generated by surface randomness. Ren's 2014 paper [3] treats chord-length distributions under randomly intersecting lines via containment functions. The available abstracts do not state the Euclidean distance of a **midpoint** to the boundary, its marked conditional distribution, or optimal endpoint density. Their full texts were not available in the present examination, so no whole-document noncoverage inference is claimed.

The publicly accessible full text of *A curvature law and sharp circular minimum for visibility in thin inner-parallel shells* [4] proves a distinct Beer-visibility probability for **area-uniform points in a shrinking inner shell**, with coefficient \(8\sqrt2\int\kappa^{-1/2}/(3L^2)\). That common curvature integral and its circular Jensen minimum are credited. For independent **boundary endpoints**, (1) instead has coefficient \(4\sqrt2\int\kappa^{-1/2}/L^2\) when \(f=1/L\), and (2)--(4) give the conditional midpoint mark and the unique curvature-adaptive endpoint density; no implication from the published *shell-point sampling* statement to those new assertions was found. Related circle-only extremal work by Bortolotto and Ramos [5] optimizes intersections and radial chord measures, not smooth-oval midpoint boundary depth or curvature-adaptive sampling.

The new contribution is the **joint boundary-midpoint layer law and its density-optimal and shape-optimal characterization**; no novelty is claimed for the Frenet Taylor expansion, the circle chord formula, total turning angle, or Cauchy--Schwarz separately.

## Limitations

The boundary must be strictly convex and \(C^3\), with positive curvature; arbitrary polygonal boundaries, vanishing-curvature points, higher dimensions and ε-dependent densities are not covered. The total-variation claim applies to the mid-arclength and signed normalized gap, while the exact scaled depth is asserted to converge only weakly. No error rate uniform in shape is proved. Independent mathematical assessment and definitive historical priority verification have not been performed; in particular, two closely related older articles lacked accessible complete text.
