# Scientific review: boundary layers of random chord midpoints

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is formulated for independent boundary points on a fixed strictly convex \(C^3\) oval with everywhere positive curvature and a continuous probability density. Strict convexity forces any midpoint approaching the boundary to come from two nearby endpoints. In a mid-arclength and signed-gap chart the Euclidean boundary depth has the uniform quadratic expansion \(D=\kappa h^2/8+o(h^2)\); this gives a compact normalized-gap support. The exact sampling Jacobian is one, and dominated \(L^1\) convergence proves both the leading coefficient and conditional total-variation limit. The depth-mark limit is only weak, correctly distinguished from the joint total-variation statement. Cauchy--Schwarz and the turning-angle identity prove the two sharp minimizers, while fixed-perimeter thinning ellipses prove unboundedness and continuity gives the full coefficient range. The exact disk formula and direct ellipse computations reproduce the constants. There is no unproved infinite-dimensional step hidden inside the finite simulation.

## Originality — PASS, provisionally

The 2006 preprint on stochastic billiards and Bertrand-chord conventions concerns different chord measures, while the complete 2025 circle-only extremal-chord article treats intersections and expectations rather than this boundary-midpoint depth. A materially read published 2026 thin-shell visibility result contains exactly the same curvature integral and disk inequality, but it samples points inside an inner-parallel shell, not independently on the original boundary. Its scalar law does not state the conditional signed midpoint-gap law or the uniquely optimal \(\sqrt\kappa\) sampling density. Ehlers–Enns (1981) and Ren (2014) concern random-secants/chord-length distributions, respectively; their accessible abstracts do not decide overlap with the current marked midpoint result, and complete texts could not be obtained. No queried published result was found that implies the full combined claim; mathematical priority remains provisional rather than proved by search failure.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The random-midpoint boundary layer is a natural statistic for endpoint-generated chords, quantitatively connecting boundary curvature to extreme near-boundary chord geometry. The theorem gives both the complete conditional leading-order configuration and the unique sampling density \(f\propto\sqrt\kappa\) minimizing rare near-boundary midpoints, a different optimum from previously studied sampling laws for other convex-hull observables. It also establishes the rigid disk minimizer among shapes of fixed perimeter and the sharp unbounded range of the uniform-endpoint coefficient. The contribution is the combined geometric-probability and extremal-sampling theorem, not the textbook Frenet identity, Jensen inequality or disk case in isolation.
