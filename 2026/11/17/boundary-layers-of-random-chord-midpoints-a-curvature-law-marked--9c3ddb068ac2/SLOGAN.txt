For two independently sampled boundary endpoints of a smooth strictly convex oval, the probability that the chord midpoint lies within distance \(\varepsilon\) of the boundary is asymptotic to \(4\sqrt{2\varepsilon}\int f^2\kappa^{-1/2}\,ds\); its exceptional signed gap becomes uniform after curvature normalization, independently of a curvature-biased boundary location. The unique endpoint density minimizing this rare-event coefficient is \(f\propto\sqrt\kappa\), and among all bodies of fixed perimeter the disk with uniform endpoints is the unique global optimizer.
