# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The asserted theorem is an existence and maximal-attracting-count statement, *not* a global count of all period-four cycles. The log-odds conjugacy is exact, the two different seed intervals are rational, and the standalone verifier uses only integer-based outward interval arithmetic. Its truncated exponential series has a positive explicit tail bound. Each fourth-return derivative bound applies to the whole interval and the whole rectangle, not just the center parameter; fourth-return residuals are strictly dominated by the Banach contraction margin. The independent period-two and cross-phase disjointness inequalities give least period four and distinct cycles. The upper bound of two attracting periodic orbits uses the established negative-Schwarzian critical-basin theorem for this family; the Schwarzian sign is also reconstructed algebraically.

## Originality

**PASS within inspected evidence.** The 2021 originating article is the closest source: it reports bistability and two stable period-four cycles *numerically*, while proving the two-attractor upper bound. The 2024 replicator treatment cites that numerical example. The contribution is not an independent discovery of two stable cycles, but the first identified exact rational certificate on a fully specified open two-parameter rectangle, with uniform contraction inequalities and unambiguous inequivalence of the two orbits. The rotation-like large-coupling result of Bielawski et al. is not a certificate of the present two-cycle coexistence near the specified parameter point. Published results and mathematical-record comparisons did not reveal the stated certificate. A possible inaccessible or unindexed earlier rigorous certificate for the same rectangle is the principal publication-priority risk.

## Value

**PASS.** The question is directly motivated by a numerically detected *maximal bistability* window in a widely studied bimodal map: numerical orbit plots do not establish the existence of distinct attracting cycles at an exact parameter point, still less throughout a nonzero-area parameter region. The uniform rectangle and strong contraction margins provide a reproducible rigorous witness that this model actually attains the sharp negative-Schwarzian two-attractor bound on an open set. The numerical phenomenon itself and the general bound are not claimed as novel; the exact robust witness is the meaningful finite classification.

## Limitations and overlap risk

The rectangle is not optimized; numerical evidence covers wider regions. No complete repelling-cycle census, basin classification or differential-equation lifting is claimed. The first source's broader qualitative statement about bistability does not imply membership of every exact parameter pair in this rectangle without additional quantitative verification. Unindexed rigorous certificates and partially accessible later studies remain risks. Publication priority requires human literature review.
