# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For each fixed state of the collision–streaming map, the sitewise collision bit equals the XOR of corresponding transported and original channel bits. The north-channel equation forces the two row collision indicators to coincide columnwise, and the east-channel equation forces an even number of collision columns. A nonempty collision set determines opposite pure-pair masks in its columns and complementary horizontal lanes elsewhere; the remaining vertical choices are free, producing a fully reversible classification. A separate six-case count handles the collision-free sector. Adding these disjoint sectors yields the joint polynomial and all finite-size consequences without extrapolating enumeration. Exact particle-hole symmetry yields conditional half filling, while a unique dominant analytic generating-function term yields the specified bivariate Gaussian limit. The actual bundled C++ and Python programs reproduce complete bivariate histograms through width three and independent transfer counts at widths three and four, including the full width-four weighted histogram. Critical boundary conditions and counting conventions are explicit.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority qualified)

Hardy–Pomeau–de Pazzis (1973) gives the microscopic model and discusses invariant statistical states and time correlations; its 1976 continuation concerns hydrodynamic properties. The separate 1973 *Journal of Mathematical Physics* abstract addresses invariant probability measures without cross-velocity correlations, not the stationary finite-torus microstate census. The accessible description of the model and relevant publication abstracts do not present this joint collision-column and mass polynomial. Closest available mathematical-record results count jammed states in a different Kob–Andersen gas, or collisions of distinct inputs to traffic Rule 184, neither of which implies the HPP head-on collision classification. Exact-sequence searches found unrelated tables, not equivalent counts. Existing related enumerations for other cellular-automaton rules use different collision/update constraints and do not imply this formula. The original 1973 and 1976 primary full texts were not accessible; absence of this result throughout them is **not** asserted. Such inaccessible priority sources and unindexed preprints remain substantive but nondecisive risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The HPP lattice gas is a foundational reversible particle automaton. The smallest nontrivial two-lane periodic channel is a natural finite-geometry test of how its local head-on collisions constrain global stationary microstates. Exact parity rigidity, a complete occupation-by-collision polynomial, and an exact zero covariance combined with asymptotically independent Gaussian fluctuations are sharper information than a list of small-instance counts. The number of collision-free fixed states becomes exponentially negligible, despite strict stationarity. This resolves a meaningful microscopic ensemble observable and provides a reproducible benchmark for finite-channel lattice-gas dynamics. No claim about hydrodynamic equilibrium or higher-width asymptotics is made.
