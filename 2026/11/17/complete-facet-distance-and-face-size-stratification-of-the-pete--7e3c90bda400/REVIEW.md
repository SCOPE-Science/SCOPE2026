# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The polar-facet correspondence is exact in the inherited nine-dimensional Euclidean subspace. The extreme-point characterization by connected tight-edge subgraphs is both necessary (componentwise perturbation) and sufficient (rank-nine tight incidence equations). Because the Petersen graph has diameter two, integer extreme potentials reduce exhaustively to normalized ternary words. Two different complete parameterizations enumerate exactly the same 1410 polar vertices. Norms and facet vertex counts are integer expressions in each potential; the complete 11-shell joint table is verified by exhaustive integer arithmetic. Direct \(S_5\) action and antipodality split them into the stated 18 disjoint orbits. The gap follows from the top two shell values, with no numerical approximation.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
The closest directly relevant published result, “Exact inradius and contact-facet rigidity of the Petersen symmetric edge polytope,” already states the outermost norm \(28/5\), 30 tangent facets, and 1410 total facets; those facts are not treated as new. Its full manuscript and executable source were checked: they do not give the remaining ten norm shells, the joint distribution of facet distances with face vertex counts, or the complete 18-orbit classification. Earlier primary literature provides general facet labeling and graph-subgraph characterizations rather than the evaluated Petersen table. Specialized unpublished or differently named classifications remain a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result completely resolves a natural metric/combinatorial stratification of every facet of a distinguished highly symmetric nine-dimensional graph polytope. In particular, it determines all simplicial facets (280), all nonsimplicial facet-size classes, the first 180 non-tangent competitors to the maximal insphere, and the exact metric clearance. The orbit analysis shows that identical metric shells can split into distinct face-size and symmetry classes, so it is not a repetition of the known inradius or a single arbitrary example statistic. This is a finite, self-contained classification, not an assertion of a new general theorem.

## Limits
The invariant depends on the canonical Euclidean embedding. The finite certificate has two independently parameterized exhaustive enumerations but has not been validated by an independent mathematician. Specialist equivalences in graph-metric and polytope facet literature remain possible.
