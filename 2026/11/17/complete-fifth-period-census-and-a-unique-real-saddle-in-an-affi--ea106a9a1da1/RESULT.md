# Complete fifth-period census and a unique real saddle in an affine nilpotent-Jacobian map

## Finding
Consider the real polynomial map
\[
N(x,y,z)=\bigl(U,\ z-2\alpha xU,\ \alpha U^2+\nu\bigr),\qquad
U=p(y+\alpha x^2)+q,
\]
where \(\alpha,p\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\) and \(q,\nu\in\mathbb R\). Its Jacobian has characteristic polynomial \(\lambda^3\) at every state, and hence is everywhere nilpotent. Its fifth iterate has **exactly 16 distinct complex fixed points**, comprising the unique fixed point and exactly three primitive five-cycles. **Exactly six of these points are real**, comprising the fixed point and one primitive five-cycle. The two nonreal five-cycles are exchanged by complex conjugation. Every fifth-iterate fixed point is algebraically simple.

The unique real primitive five-cycle is a **hyperbolic saddle**: its three-dimensional fifth-return derivative has multipliers \(0,\lambda_+,\lambda_-\) with \(0<\lambda_+<1\) and \(\lambda_-<-1\). The entire census and its nonzero return multipliers are independent of \(\alpha,p,q,\nu\) in the stated domain. Approximate nonzero multipliers are \(0.7224123092\) and \(-225.599487660\); these decimals are illustrative, not used to certify the inequalities.

## Assumptions and scope
All polynomial maps and periodic points in the counting statement are over \(\mathbb C\); the reality assertion refers to real initial states. A point has *primitive period five* if its least positive iteration period is five. A fixed point of the fifth iterate has primitive period one or five, since five is prime. The nonvanishing hypotheses are necessary for the explicit invertible normalization; this finding makes no claim for degenerate parameters.

Set \(P(s)=ps+q\) and \(Y=y+\alpha x^2\). The polynomial automorphism
\[
\Phi(x,y,z)=\bigl(x-P(Y),\ Y,\ z-2\alpha xP(Y)+\alpha P(Y)^2\bigr)
\]
has the explicit polynomial inverse \(x=X+P(Y)\), \(y=Y-\alpha(X+P(Y))^2\), and \(z=Z+2\alpha(X+P(Y))P(Y)-\alpha P(Y)^2\). Direct substitution conjugates \(N\) to
\[
G(X,Y,Z)=\bigl(p(Y-Z),\ Z,\ \alpha p^2(Y-Z)^2+\nu\bigr).
\]
The additional invertible affine coordinates \(u=\alpha pX\), \(v=\alpha p^2(Y-\nu)\), \(w=\alpha p^2(Z-\nu)\) make this the parameter-free map
\[
G_0(u,v,w)=\bigl(v-w,\ w,\ (v-w)^2\bigr).
\]
The transformations hold over \(\mathbb C\) as well as \(\mathbb R\), preserve primitive periods, and conjugate derivatives of periodic returns.

## Proof
**Reduction to a planar periodic-point problem.** Every periodic state of \(G_0\) lies on \(w=u^2\). Its full cyclic orbit satisfies \(v_j=u_{j-1}^2\) and
\[
u_{j+1}=u_{j-1}^2-u_j^2.
\]
Hence its scalar word is generated by
\[
F(a,b)=(b,a^2-b^2).
\]
Conversely, \(L(a,b)=(b,a^2,b^2)\) satisfies \(G_0\circ L=L\circ F\). It is one-to-one on periodic points: if two periodic states have the same image under \(L\), they have the same image under \(F\), and periodic states of any deterministic map cannot have two distinct preimages that are themselves periodic. Thus periodic points of \(F\) and \(G_0\) are in period-preserving bijection.

**Exhaustive fifth-return elimination.** Starting with \(A_0=a,B_0=b\), recursively set \(A_{j+1}=B_j\), \(B_{j+1}=A_j^2-B_j^2\) for \(0\le j<5\). An exact lexicographic Gröbner reduction over \(\mathbb Q\) of \(A_5-a,B_5-b\), ordered \(a>b\), gives
\[
\langle a-R(b),\ bQ_{15}(b)\rangle,
\]
where \(R\in\mathbb Q[b]\) has degree fifteen and
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{15}(b)={}&1215b^{15}-8640b^{13}+2520b^{12}+19890b^{11}\\
&-9844b^{10}-16290b^9+8314b^8+5303b^7+1552b^6\\
&-2010b^5-4202b^4+2060b^3+1108b^2-340b-169.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Gröbner algorithm and the exact polynomial \(R\) are reproduced in `CHECK.py`; the script asserts the full two-polynomial basis, not just a necessary resultant. The square-free test \(\gcd(bQ_{15},(bQ_{15})')=1\) establishes sixteen distinct complex solutions, each with unique \(a=R(b)\). Only \((0,0)\) is fixed under \(F\), so the other fifteen points are primitive five-periodic and constitute three five-cycles.

**Real-root census.** An exact Sturm count gives five distinct real zeros of \(Q_{15}\) and no others. Each lies, respectively, in the disjoint rational intervals
\[
\begin{gathered}
(-2.033905,-2.033904),\quad(-1.646315,-1.646314),\\
(0.675684,0.675685),\quad(1.426417,1.426418),\\
(1.578117,1.578118).
\end{gathered}
\]
Each interval is verified to contain exactly one root, and the total number of real roots is five. The real coefficients of \(R\) make all five corresponding plane points real. They form one primitive five-cycle. The remaining ten complex points form two nonreal cycles; complex conjugation must swap them, since a nontrivial involution commuting with a rotation of prime order five cannot act nontrivially on a single five-cycle.

**Exact hyperbolicity inequalities.** At the fifth-periodic point whose second coordinate is the unique root in \((0.675684,0.675685)\), take \(a=R(b)\). The derivative of \(F\) is
\[
DF(a,b)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\2a&-2b\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Exact rational interval arithmetic, after Sturm isolation of that root to width less than \(10^{-18}\), bounds the fifth-return trace within \((-230,-220)\) and determinant within \((-170,-150)\). The checker additionally certifies \(1-\operatorname{tr}+\det>0\) and \(1+\operatorname{tr}+\det<0\). Therefore the real characteristic polynomial has opposite-sign roots, with one in \((0,1)\) and the other below \(-1\). Every point on the real cycle has the same two nonzero multipliers by cyclicity. The local immersion \(L\) has rank two along this orbit because all coordinates are nonzero; the third-dimensional return has the same tangent multipliers plus a zero normal multiplier, since \(G_0\) maps the entire space into \(w=u^2\) after one iterate. Polynomial conjugacy transfers the result to \(N\).

## Verification
Run `python3 CHECK.py` with SymPy installed (requirements in `requirements.txt`). The script recomputes both fifth iterates, the exact lexicographic ideal, the displayed eliminant, square-freeness, the global Sturm count, five rational isolating intervals, and the fifth-return trace and determinant inequalities using fractions. Successful output begins `FIFTH_PERIOD_UNIQUE_REAL_SADDLE_EXACT_OK`. The proof does not infer the total census from approximate roots or from numerical search.

## Relationship to prior work
Chamberland's treatment of the simplest nilpotent-Jacobian polynomial map gives its unique fixed point, three-cycle, and complicated basin geometry, but not a complete fifth-period census. Castañeda and Rebollo-Perdomo prove a general three-cycle theorem and report a **five-cycle already exists** for affine \(P\) (Remark 3.2), explicitly noting their Gröbner calculation; the mere existence of a cycle is therefore **not claimed here**. A separate published result excludes primitive four-cycles for affine \(P\) while constructing four-cycles at quadratic \(P\). That exclusion does not imply either the exact three-complex-cycle fifth census or the unique real saddle. The current claim uses an exact full-scheme elimination and real-root count rather than another existence witness.

## Limitations
The result is a complete census **only at period five** for the nonconstant affine-defining-polynomial family; it does not bound higher periods or classify the global basin of the saddle. It does not assert novelty of the original existence of the five-cycle, the map reduction, or the four-cycle obstruction. The computational certificate uses exact symbolic polynomial operations and rational Sturm isolation; no independent audit has been performed.
