# Complete higher-independence homotopy and rooted symmetry of two-level trees

## Finding
Let \(n\ge2\) and \(q_i\ge1\) be integers. Form the rooted tree \(T(q_1,\ldots,q_n)\) with root \(c\), distinct branch vertices \(a_i\) adjacent to \(c\), and sets \(B_i\) of \(q_i\) leaves each adjacent only to \(a_i\). Write \(Q=\sum_iq_i\). For every integer \(r\ge1\), the complex of vertex subsets inducing connected components with at most \(r\) vertices satisfies

\[\operatorname{Ind}_r(T)\simeq\bigvee^{U_r}S^{nr-1}\vee\bigvee^{V_r}S^{r-1},\qquad U_r=\prod_{i=1}^n\binom{q_i-1}{r-1},\quad V_r=\binom{Q-1}{r-n-1}.\]

Here \(\binom ab=0\) if \(b<0\) or \(b>a\), and a wedge with multiplicity zero is absent. Thus both terms vanish, and the complex is contractible, whenever their multiplicities are zero. The dimensions are distinct because \(n\ge2\). In particular, both sphere types occur exactly when \(n+1\le r\le \min_iq_i\); this is possible for branches with sufficiently many leaves. The higher-degree multiplicity depends on the individual branching profile while the lower-degree multiplicity depends only on its sum.

For the rooted automorphism group \(G\), let \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\), \(B=\bigsqcup_iB_i\), and let \(U_B=\ker(\mathbb Q[B]\xrightarrow{\mathrm{sum}}\mathbb Q)\). For \(n+1\le r\le n+Q\), the complete rational representation on lower-degree homology is
\[\widetilde H_{r-1}(\operatorname{Ind}_r(T);\mathbb Q)\cong\operatorname{sgn}(G\curvearrowright A)\otimes\bigwedge^{r-n-1}U_B.\]
For \(1\le r\le\min_i q_i\), the higher-degree integral homology is the join tensor product \(\bigotimes_i\bigwedge^{r-1}\ker(\mathbb Z[B_i]\to\mathbb Z)\), with each branch swap carrying the join Koszul factor \((-1)^r\). No integral lattice assertion is made for the lower-degree rational formula. Other reduced integral homology groups vanish.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple. The root and its direct children are distinguished; group actions preserve the root and permute children and their leaf sets compatibly. The parameter \(r\) ranges over all positive integers. No assumption of equal branch sizes is made. Homotopy equivalences are nonequivariant; rational and integral homology modules are separately specified.

## Proof
**Root deletion.** The deletion of the root from the complex equals \(D=\bigast_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Ind}_r(K_{1,q_i})\), since the remaining graph is the disjoint union of \(n\) stars. For a star with center \(a_i\), its higher-independence complex is the union of the full leaf simplex with the cone from \(a_i\) over the \((r-2)\)-skeleton of that simplex, whenever \(1\le r\le q_i\). The simplex skeleton is a wedge of \(\binom{q_i-1}{r-1}\) spheres of dimension \(r-2\) (with the usual augmented convention for \(r=1\)); thus the star complex is a wedge of the same number of spheres of dimension \(r-1\). For \(r\ge q_i+1\), the star complex is a simplex. The join formula gives \(D\simeq\bigvee^{U_r}S^{nr-1}\), or a point if \(U_r=0\).

**Root link.** The link \(L=\operatorname{lk}_{\operatorname{Ind}_r(T)}(c)\) consists exactly of subsets \(\tau\subseteq A\sqcup B\) satisfying
\[w(\tau):=\sum_{i:a_i\in\tau}\bigl(1+|\tau\cap B_i|\bigr)\le r-1.\]
Select one distinguished leaf \(b_i\in B_i\) from each branch. On the augmented face poset of \(L\), perform sequential elementary matchings by toggling \(b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_n\), always matching all remaining eligible pairs. The sequential-element-matching lemma (Deshpande–Singh, Proposition 2.10) makes their union acyclic. After the first toggle, the unmatched faces have \(a_1\) present, \(b_1\) absent, and weight exactly \(r-1\). Each later toggle eliminates exactly the faces whose corresponding branch vertex is absent. Indeed, absent branches have zero weight for all their leaves, so toggling that distinguished leaf remains possible; present branches are saturated and toggling changes the weight. The final unmatched faces are therefore precisely
\[A\cup C,\qquad C\subseteq B\setminus\{b_1\},\quad |C|=r-n-1.\]
There are \(V_r\) of them, all with \(r-1\) vertices (dimension \(r-2\)). If \(V_r=0\), the augmented matching is perfect and \(L\) is contractible. Otherwise the matching, which includes the empty face in a pair, gives \(L\simeq\bigvee^{V_r} S^{r-2}\). The only nonempty-wedge case has \(r\ge n+1\ge3\), so there is no ambiguity about reduced suspension in dimension zero.

**Null attaching map and full homotopy type.** The standard root-deletion decomposition is \(\operatorname{Ind}_r(T)=D\cup(c*L)\), with intersection \(L\). Its homotopy type is the homotopy cofiber of \(L\hookrightarrow D\). If either complex is contractible the cofiber gives the asserted sphere summand. If both are noncontractible, the link is a wedge of spheres of dimension \(r-2\) and \(D\) is a wedge of spheres of dimension \(nr-1\). Since \(n\ge2\), \(r-2<nr-1\); therefore each attaching map is nullhomotopic. The cofiber is consequently \(D\vee\Sigma L\), yielding the two displayed wedge summands and free integral homology in exactly those degrees. The case \(r=1\) has contractible \(L\) and is included.

**Symmetry.** The integral top homology of the \((r-2)\)-skeleton of a leaf simplex is the exterior power \(\bigwedge^{r-1}\ker(\mathbb Z[B_i]\to\mathbb Z)\), naturally under leaf permutations. The join Künneth isomorphism gives the higher-degree tensor module and the sign \((-1)^r\) for exchanging two factors (the corresponding reduced chains have suspension degree \(r\)). For the lower-degree module, apply the signed simplicial Lefschetz formula to the root link. For an automorphism \(g\), every branch-permutation cycle left unselected contributes zero to the signed sum, since its nonempty leaf-orbit family cancels. Thus all branches must be selected. If \(g\) has \(c_A\) cycles on \(A\), and \(\ell\) runs over its cycle lengths on \(B\), the signed generating polynomial of surviving invariant faces is \((-1)^{c_A}z^n\prod_{\ell}(1-z^\ell)\). Taking the partial coefficient sum up to weight \(r-1\), converting from reduced Lefschetz number to the unique nonzero homology character, and using
\[\det(I-zg\mid U_B)=\frac{\prod_{\ell}(1-z^\ell)}{1-z}\]
shows that the character is exactly that of \(\operatorname{sgn}(g\mid A)\otimes\bigwedge^{r-n-1}U_B\). Rational representations of a finite group are determined by characters. The root-deletion Mayer–Vietoris boundary is natural under the group action; because the two possible homology degrees differ, this identifies the claimed lower-degree representation.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone program `height_two_verify.py` independently enumerates induced connected components and all simplices for ten branch profiles and every positive threshold, computes complete mod-two boundary ranks for 75 threshold/profile cases, constructs and checks each full link face-incidence graph for acyclicity, and compares 1,118 exact signed symmetry traces with the exterior-power character formula. It also independently checks the mixed-dimensional profile \((4,4)\) at threshold \(3\), obtaining one two-sphere and nine five-spheres. These finite tests corroborate the symbolic proof; they do not establish the infinite theorem by extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Sawant (arXiv:2605.25150, first posted 2026-05-24) proves vertex decomposability for higher-independence complexes of every tree, but does not provide the present exact profile-dependent homotopy and rooted character formulas. Deshpande and Singh (arXiv:2001.05448) calculate fully whiskered graphs, graphs in which **every nonleaf vertex has a pendant neighbor**, and perfect regular \(m\)-ary trees. Here the root has no pendant neighbor, so their general whiskered theorem does not apply directly; their special perfect-height-two family overlaps the regular cases and those cases are not claimed as new. Abdelmalek et al. (arXiv:2106.10863) give qualitative caterpillar results, not this unrestricted two-degree classification. The discrete Morse elementary-matching lemma used here is standard and explicitly credited to the earlier work. No claim is made to a new matching principle.

## Limitations
The rooted symmetry modules do not automatically describe non-root-preserving automorphisms in degenerate small trees. The splitting is nonequivariant, and the lower-degree module identification is over \(\mathbb Q\), not an integral lattice classification. The proof uses classical simplicial Morse theory and cofiber methods; it does not establish new general vertex-decomposability criteria. Some equal-branch cases overlap previously classified perfect trees. No claim of exhaustive coverage of unindexed literature is made.
