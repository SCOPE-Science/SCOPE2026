# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The width identity follows from an explicit Minkowski segment decomposition; the complete list of maxima follows from the chamber-wise Cauchy equality classification. An ordered-step decomposition proves the sharp internal-variation lemma, including its equality cases. The tangent space at every two-level direction splits into two zero-mean blocks; before any block ordering changes, their cross contributions cancel and the sharp angular envelope follows. On every stratum with at least three coordinate levels the width is a linear functional on a positive-dimensional block-constant sphere, excluding local minima, while block splitting excludes all remaining nonglobal local maxima. The accompanying program corroborates the normalization and constants in finitely many ranks; the argument, not the program, proves the arbitrary-rank theorem.

## Originality
PASS with historical-priority qualification. The complete-graph zonotope description and pairwise absolute-difference functional are known from primary permutohedron literature and standard Gini-mean-difference references. The arXiv full text of Ardila–Castillo–Eur–Postnikov (2019) was inspected in its permutohedron, zonotope, fan and deformation sections; it establishes the underlying polytope but does not classify Euclidean-spherical local extrema or their angle-dependent stability. Ardila–Supina–Vindas-Meléndez (2019) concerns equivariant Ehrhart theory, not this width landscape. A published related result for the regular simplex uses the range functional rather than the sum of all pairwise differences, so its formulas and extremal count do not imply this result. No direct covering statement was identified in the database and broader literature queries; possible older graph total-variation spectral results remain a residual risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is a natural geometric optimization problem on a classical \((n-1)\)-polytope, not an arbitrary finite selection. It gives a complete nonconvex, nonsmooth spherical landscape with exponentially many strict nonglobal traps but factorially many global maximizers. The sharp angular coefficient and attained finite-angle profile measure the precise local conditioning of each trap. The contrast between singleton global minima and balanced highest-width local minima is structurally informative even without a separate downstream theorem.

## Limitations
Prior equivalence in older graph-total-variation or statistical-dispersion literature remains possible. The uniform angular formula stops before the explicit threshold; no generalization to weighted root zonotopes is claimed. An independent audit has not been performed.
