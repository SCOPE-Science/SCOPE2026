# Scientific review: complete orientation law for equilateral triangle containment in rectangles

## Correctness — PASS

The claim covers every pair of positive rectangle dimensions, every orientation, and the exact uniform-orientation distribution. The argument eliminates translation by coordinate spans, identifies the feasible complex similarity multipliers with an intersection of two six-strip regular hexagons, and computes their 6/12-vertex phase diagram. The six-triangle subtraction independently yields the claimed area and hence its exact angular mean square. A disjoint-forbidden-arc argument for radii strictly below the common vertex radius yields the complete survival function; the finite endpoint has zero angular mass. Fixing the rectangle area gives two elementary monotonicity problems with distinct equality cases. A standalone numerical checker independently enumerates the polygon by twelve halfplane clips and checks its area, radius and angular probabilities. These finite checks are corroboration, not substitutes for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Choi--Choi (arXiv:2611.08556v1; 2026-11-05) provides the general containment-polytope representation and studies silhouette algorithms, but its general-position hypotheses explicitly exclude parallel-sided rectangular targets, and its stated theorems do not give the all-aspect-ratio equilateral distribution, exact angular second moment or opposed shape optimizers. The classical square-fitting optimum and the best single-orientation fixed-area rectangle aspect ratio are already consequences of known bounding-rectangle geometry; neither is claimed as original. A published exact distribution for bounding-box **area** of rotated regular polygons (including triangles) was inspected in full: it determines the product of the horizontal and vertical widths, and its minimum recovers the classical best-case aspect ratio, but it does not imply the joint-threshold survival law or the unique-square optimum for its angular second moment. An earlier exact triangle-in-itself containment result uses a different target and does not imply the rectangle formulas. Targeted publication and mathematical-record searches did not identify the entire theorem. This is a best-knowledge statement, not a historical uniqueness certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The problem is the natural orientation-resolved version of placing the largest similar equilateral triangle inside a rectangle while permitting translation. Rather than a single illustrative fit, the theorem classifies all positive target aspect ratios, the complete law of scale under an unknown angle, and all sharp target-shape optima. A mathematically substantive consequence is that the target which maximizes the *average squared* achievable scale at fixed area is uniquely square, while the target which maximizes the *best individual* scale has aspect ratio \(2/\sqrt3\) or its reciprocal. The transition from the twelve-vertex feasible polygon to a hexagon is a geometric mechanism explaining that difference. The result requires neither an arbitrary finite parameter cutoff nor an unsupported downstream claim.

## Remaining limitations and priority risk

The source triangle is equilateral and the target is rectangular; arbitrary source triangles, different target polygons, nonuniform orientations and translation distributions are outside scope. The classical best-case fitting optima for a square or a freely chosen rectangle, generic containment-polytope construction and the bounding-box-area law are prior results. Unindexed historical work could overlap the new joint-width probability law. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
