# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact cycle count follows from the explicit polynomial-ring conjugacy, the even/odd local Smith normal forms for the fixed-point operator, and Möbius inversion. The odd-time local unit condition is checked at every factor dividing \(a^t+1\), including repeated factors; zero-primary noninvertible factors contribute no extra periodic points. On the two power-of-two rim families, the nilpotent factor is excluded from periodic states and the exact valuation-two unit perturbation yields the closed fixed-period populations. Independent matrix and state-based tests validate the new fixed-point and cycle counts for finite sizes; a third exact polynomial checker separately validates the infinite-family formula through \(v=256\). The proofs, not finite computation, establish the unrestricted formulas.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
A previously recorded theorem on precisely the same prism XOR system establishes the reversible/nonreversible dichotomy, zero-primary Jordan partitions and transient-entry populations. **All those statements are treated solely as known and are not submitted as discoveries.** They do not determine the number or lengths of primitive cycles, which require a separate calculation of the invertible summand. Martin–Odlyzko–Wolfram's general additive-automaton framework, Tadaki's scalar cyclic automata, and Ogihara–Uchizawa's complexity treatment do not present the evaluated two-layer prism all-period formula or its explicit closed power-of-two subfamily. Closest indexed results treat complete multipartite graphs and Boolean cubes; neither contains the prism orbit census. Search cannot prove global uniqueness; an undocumented specialized full-period calculation is the main priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Exact temporal orbit counts are a natural invariant not determined by the already known transience or recurrent-state dimension. The main theorem evaluates this invariant for every prism size and temporal length, while the two natural number-theoretic subfamilies admit a complete closed classification of which lengths occur and how many cycles attain each. The contrast between \(n=v\) (all states recurrent) and \(n=3v\) (nontrivial transients) with identical recurrent cycle statistics is a structural arithmetic consequence, not a sample table or arbitrary cutoff. No broader-graph theory or breakthrough priority is claimed.

## Closest literature and limitations
The general algebraic method is classical. The 1984 original full text, both Tadaki preprints, and the 2020 XOR graph-dynamics article were inspected for the relevant objects and conclusions. The earlier prism-specific transient result is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim; the irreducible new point is its distinct full temporal-cycle census. The result does not simplify the entire arbitrary-size period spectrum beyond its exact gcd formula, and it remains awaiting independent mathematical review.
