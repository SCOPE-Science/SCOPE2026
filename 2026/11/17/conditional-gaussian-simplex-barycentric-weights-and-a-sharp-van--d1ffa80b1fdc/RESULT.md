# Conditional Gaussian-simplex barycentric weights and a sharp vanishing-vertex crossover

## Finding

Let \(m\ge2\), let \(G_0,\ldots,G_{m-1}\) be independent standard Gaussian vectors in \(\mathbb R^{m-1}\), and let \(\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_{m-1}>0\). Put \(Y_i=\sigma_iG_i\), and write \(E_m\) for the event that the origin is in the interior of the simplex \(\operatorname{conv}(Y_0,\ldots,Y_{m-1})\). On \(E_m\), define its unique barycentric coordinates \(\lambda_i>0\) by
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\lambda_iY_i=0,\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\lambda_i=1.
\]
Then \(\Pr(E_m)=2^{1-m}\), independently of the positive scales, and, conditionally on \(E_m\),
\[
(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_{m-1})\ \stackrel{d}{=}\
\left(\frac{X_i/\sigma_i}{\sum_jX_j/\sigma_j}\right)_{i=0}^{m-1},
\quad X_0,\ldots,X_{m-1}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}|N(0,1)|.
\]
In the usual \((m-1)\)-dimensional barycentric coordinate chart \(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_{m-2}\), this gives the complete conditional density
\[
\boxed{f_{\boldsymbol\sigma}(\boldsymbol\lambda)=
\frac{2^{m-1}\Gamma(m/2)}{\pi^{m/2}}
\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\sigma_i}
{\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\sigma_i^2\lambda_i^2\right)^{m/2}},\qquad \lambda_i>0,\quad\sum_i\lambda_i=1.}
\]

The distinct feature appears if only vertex zero becomes concentrated at the origin as dimension increases. Let \(\sigma_0=\tau_m>0\) and \(\sigma_i=1\) for \(i\ge1\); write \(a_m=m\tau_m\) and \(\mu=\sqrt{2/\pi}\). Then, *conditional on origin containment*,
\[
\lambda_0\ \longrightarrow\
\begin{cases}
1&\text{in probability if }a_m\to0,\\
0&\text{in probability if }a_m\to\infty,
\end{cases}
\]
whereas if \(a_m\to a\in(0,\infty)\), then
\[
\boxed{\lambda_0\ \Longrightarrow\ Z_a:=\frac{X}{X+a\mu},\qquad
 g_a(z)=\frac{2a}{\pi(1-z)^2}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{a^2z^2}{\pi(1-z)^2}\right),\quad 0<z<1,}
\]
with \(X\sim|N(0,1)|\). In particular,
\[
\boxed{\Pr(Z_a>1/2)=\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt\pi).}
\]
The critical scaling is exactly \(\tau_m\asymp1/m\): the origin-containment probability remains exponentially small and independent of \(\tau_m\), but the conditional share of the distinguished vertex changes from nearly all to nearly none.

For the exact critical choice \(\tau_m=a/m\), the fluctuations have the following mixture-normal refinement under the natural common-half-normal coupling:
\[
\boxed{\sqrt m\left(\lambda_0-\frac{X}{X+a\mu}\right)
\ \Longrightarrow\ -\frac{aX}{(X+a\mu)^2}\,Z,\quad
 Z\sim N(0,1-2/\pi),\quad Z\perp X.}
\]
Finally, among the ordinary vertices, let \(w_i=\lambda_i/(1-\lambda_0)\), \(1\le i<m\). For every sequence \(\tau_m>0\), their conditional empirical distribution satisfies
\[
\boxed{\frac1{m-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\delta_{(m-1)w_i}
\Longrightarrow\mathcal L(X/\mu),\qquad
(m-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}w_i^2\longrightarrow\frac\pi2,}
\]
in probability. The bulk weights retain a diffuse half-normal profile even when the distinguished vertex captures nearly all total barycentric mass.

## Assumptions and scope

All Gaussians are independent and have covariance the identity in the indicated dimension. All scales are deterministic and **strictly positive**, even when they shrink with \(m\). The origin is required to lie in the *full-dimensional interior* of the random simplex. The stated density is relative to ordinary Lebesgue measure on the first \(m-1\) barycentric coordinates, not intrinsic Hausdorff measure in \(\mathbb R^m\). At each fixed \(m\), the conditional simplex is nondegenerate almost surely. All asymptotics concern integers \(m\to\infty\) and the conditional laws given \(E_m\).

## Proof

**1. Exact conditional representation.** Form the \((m-1)\times m\) matrix \(G\) with columns \(G_i\). It has rank \(m-1\) almost surely. Right orthogonal invariance of its standard Gaussian law implies that the unoriented one-dimensional kernel of \(G\) is Haar-uniform in projective \((m-1)\)-space. Thus a unit generator has the same law, up to a simultaneous sign flip, as \(N/\|N\|_2\) for an \(m\)-vector \(N\) of independent standard normals. The kernel of \(G\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i)\) is generated by \(\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i^{-1})N\).

Since all \(m\) vertices are affinely independent almost surely, the origin lies in their simplex interior exactly when this one-dimensional kernel contains a vector with *all strictly positive* entries. Since all \(\sigma_i\) are positive, this occurs precisely when all coordinates of \(N\) have the same sign. Independent fair Gaussian signs give probability \(2/2^m=2^{1-m}\). These signs are independent of the magnitudes \(X_i=|N_i|\), and normalizing the positive generator to sum to one proves the displayed conditional representation.

**2. Exact density and normalization.** The independent variables \(T_i=X_i/\sigma_i\) have half-normal densities \(\sigma_i\sqrt{2/\pi}\exp(-\sigma_i^2t_i^2/2)\), \(t_i>0\). Change variables \(t_i=s\lambda_i\) with \(s>0\) and \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\). The Jacobian in \((s,\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_{m-2})\) is \(s^{m-1}\). Hence the density of \(\boldsymbol\lambda\) is
\[
\left(\prod_i\sigma_i\right)\left(\frac2\pi\right)^{m/2}
\int_0^\infty s^{m-1}\exp\left(-\frac{s^2}{2}\sum_i\sigma_i^2\lambda_i^2\right)\,ds.
\]
The gamma integral equals \(2^{m/2-1}\Gamma(m/2)(\sum_i\sigma_i^2\lambda_i^2)^{-m/2}\), giving the stated coefficient. The invertible change of variables also proves the density integrates to one, without numerical extrapolation.

**3. Distinguished-vertex transition.** Under the exact conditional representation, take independent half-normals \(X_0,X_1,\ldots\), with \(S_m=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}X_i\). The distinguished coordinate is
\[
\lambda_0=\frac{X_0}{X_0+\tau_m S_m}=
\frac{X_0}{X_0+a_m(S_m/m)}.
\]
The strong law gives \(S_m/m\to\mu\) almost surely. It proves all three regimes, and for \(a_m\to a>0\) identifies \(Z_a\). Transform \(X=a\mu z/(1-z)\) to obtain \(g_a\), using the half-normal density \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\,e^{-x^2/2}\). Its survival at \(z=1/2\) is \(\Pr(X>a\mu)=\operatorname{erfc}(a/\sqrt\pi)\).

**4. Critical fluctuations.** For \(\tau_m=a/m\), the classical central limit theorem gives \(\sqrt m(S_m/m-\mu)\Longrightarrow Z\sim N(0,1-2/\pi)\), independent of \(X_0\). Applying the derivative of \(u\mapsto X_0/(X_0+au)\) at \(u=\mu\), conditionally on \(X_0\), yields the mixture-normal limit. Independence and joint convergence follow because \(S_m\) uses only \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\).

**5. Diffuse ordinary-vertex profile.** For every \(\tau_m\), cancellation yields \(w_i=X_i/S_m\) for \(1\le i<m\). The strong law implies \(S_m/(m-1)\to\mu\). The empirical law of \(X_i\) converges almost surely weakly to the half-normal law; its common normalization therefore gives the scaled empirical limit. Moreover,
\[
(m-1)\sum_iw_i^2=
\frac{(m-1)^{-1}\sum_iX_i^2}{((m-1)^{-1}\sum_iX_i)^2}
\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbb E X^2}{\mu^2}=\frac\pi2,
\]
again by the strong law. The coupling establishes the stated conditional convergence in distribution and in probability.

## Relationship to prior work

Wendel's 1962 absorption theorem and the 1967 treatment by Cover and Efron determine the probability that the origin belongs to the random convex hull. The present work credits that probability, rather than presenting it as original. Baryshnikov and Vitale (1994) relate Gaussian samples to random projections of regular simplices; their affine-equivalence statement does not by itself identify barycentric coordinates relative to a fixed ambient origin. Götze, Kabluchko and Zaporozhets (first circulated in 2019) express Gaussian-hull absorption in terms of Grassmann angles and identify rotationally invariant Gaussian kernels, but do not state the conditional weight density and the singular distinguished-vertex crossover. Moseeva (2020) gives weighted Gaussian-simplex **volume** distributions, not the location of the origin in its barycentric coordinates.

The specific claim here is the joint conditional barycentric distribution for arbitrary positive column scales and the precise \(m^{-1}\) crossover, including its fluctuation law and the ordinary-vertex bulk profile. The Gaussian nullspace symmetry and classical laws of large numbers are fundamental ingredients, not new results. Priority beyond examined sources is not independently certified.

## Limitations

The origin-containment probability \(2^{1-m}\) is exponentially small, so unconditioned high-dimensional sampling is inefficient. The critical limit holds only when scales are strictly positive; taking \(\sigma_0=0\) exactly places the origin at a vertex, and the full-dimensional interior containment event is then impossible. No extension to non-Gaussian column distributions or correlated columns is proved. The total-variation convergence of the high-dimensional conditional profile and a uniform rate in \(a\) are not established. The self-review is not independent mathematical certification. The historical literature comparison has residual risks for inaccessible full texts.
