# Conjugation quotients of the proper-subgroup complexes of \(A_5\) and \(S_5\)

## Finding

For \(G\in\{A_5,S_5}\), let \(L(G)\) be the finite poset of all subgroups \(H\) satisfying \(1<H<G\), ordered by inclusion. The group \(G\) acts on its order complex \(\Delta L(G)\) by simultaneous conjugation of every subgroup in a flag. The ordinary geometric orbit spaces have homotopy types

\[
\bigl|\Delta L(A_5)\bigr|/A_5\simeq S^1,
\qquad
\bigl|\Delta L(S_5)\bigr|/S_5\simeq S^2.
\]

The more precise claim is that the first quotient collapses by three elementary cells pairs to a **five-edge circle**, while the second collapses by twenty-six pairs to a **twelve-vertex triangulation of the two-sphere**, with thirty edges and twenty faces. These are homotopy equivalences, not asserted homeomorphisms of the original quotient spaces.

## Assumptions and scope

The acting groups are the naturally embedded even-permutation and full permutation groups of a five-element set. Quotients are ordinary geometric orbit spaces, not Borel constructions, homotopy quotients, or the order complex of the **poset of conjugacy classes** of subgroups. A cell of \(\Delta L(G)\) is a strict chain \(H_0<\cdots<H_d\), of length \(d\); quotient cells are simultaneous conjugacy orbits of such chains. Every stabilizer of a chain fixes its vertices individually, because subgroup orders strictly increase. Accordingly the orbit space has the indicated regular ordered-chain cell structure with faces formed by deleting one subgroup. Distinct chain orbits can have the same vertex-orbit sequence before collapsing; do not identify such cells.

## Proof

**Exhaustive subgroup construction.** Represent \(S_5\) by all one hundred twenty permutations of five letters and \(A_5\) by its sixty even permutations. Starting with the identity subgroup, repeatedly adjoin each ambient group element and close under multiplication until no new subgroup is found. Every subgroup is generated by a subset of the ambient finite group, and induction over a generating set proves this procedure finds every subgroup. Direct closure and inverse checks validate each enumerated subgroup. The resulting total counts, *including* the trivial and ambient groups, are fifty-nine for \(A_5\) and one hundred fifty-six for \(S_5\).

**Correct orbit cells.** Index the nontrivial proper subgroups. For each chain, choose the lexicographically least simultaneously conjugate chain as its representative. Enumerating every increasing chain and taking this canonical representative produces the following numbers of orbit cells, listed from dimension zero upward:

\[
A_5:\quad (7,8,1),\qquad S_5:\quad (17,49,41,7).
\]

A quotient chain has distinct subgroup orders, so no ambient group element permutes vertices of a simplex nontrivially while preserving it setwise; the quotient is therefore obtained from the chain-orbit cells by the usual face gluings. In every dimension the boundary is determined by the actual deleted-subgroup orbits, not merely their endpoint conjugacy classes. The verifier checks every two-step cellular boundary composition equals zero over the integers.

**Elementary collapse certificates.** The supplied \`certificate.json\` records each pair as \([d,u,v]\), where \(v\) indexes an active \(d\)-cell and \(u\) its \((d-1)\)-face. The companion \`verify.py\` reconstructs the whole cell structure and confirms at the instant of deletion that \(u\) is contained in exactly one active \(d\)-cell, namely \(v\), and that \(v\) has no higher-dimensional coface. Thus both cells are removed by a genuine elementary collapse. There are three pairs in the alternating case, leaving cell numbers \((5,5,0)\), and twenty-six pairs in the symmetric case, leaving \((12,30,20,0)\).

**Identification of the residual spaces.** For \(A_5\) the five remaining vertices are connected by five distinct edges; each vertex has degree two, and the graph is connected. It is a single five-cycle, hence homeomorphic to \(S^1\). For \(S_5\) the remaining edges have distinct two-vertex supports and the remaining triangles have distinct three-vertex supports, so the residual complex is an ordinary simplicial complex. Each of its thirty edges lies in exactly two triangles. The link of each of its twelve vertices is connected and two-regular, hence a circle, and the one-skeleton is connected. It is therefore a connected closed triangulated surface. Its Euler characteristic is \(12-30+20=2\), and the classification of compact surfaces identifies it with \(S^2\). Each elementary collapse preserves homotopy type, completing the proof.

## Verification

With Python 3 and no third-party packages, place \`verify.py\` and \`certificate.json\` in the same directory and run \`python3 verify.py certificate.json\`. The script regenerates the full multiplication and inversion tables, all subgroups, all chain orbits, face maps, collapse pairs, final graph and vertex links; compares the complete structured result to the recorded certificate; and prints \`VERIFY_OK\` only after all assertions pass. The exact run produced:

```text
VERIFY_OK
A5 59 [7, 8, 1] 3 [5, 5, 0] circle
S5 156 [17, 49, 41, 7] 26 [12, 30, 20, 0] sphere
```

This is an exhaustive finite calculation plus a topological surface-classification argument, **not** a finite sampling extrapolated to a general group family. The certificate includes each deleted pair and all residual cell indices.

## Relationship to prior work

Ramras (2002) studies connectivity and fundamental groups of the *unquotiented* proper-subgroup poset and of coset posets. Its Sections 6–7 distinguish these objects, and its \(A_5\) analysis in Section 5 concerns the **coset poset**. Neither the results inspected there nor the earlier coset-poset thesis identify the simultaneous-conjugation orbit spaces here. Shareshian–Woodroofe (2014) establish noncontractibility for *coset* complexes, a different construction and not a statement about these orbit quotients. In particular, topological quotients must retain chain-orbit incidences, so a tabulation of conjugacy classes of individual subgroups does not imply the claimed equivalences.

## Limitations

Only the groups \(A_5\) and \(S_5\) and their inner conjugation actions are treated. The result does not establish a formula for higher symmetric or alternating groups, nor does it describe the full nonequivariant subgroup-complex homotopy types. A published, equivalent calculation of these particular orbit spaces may exist outside the sources inspected; absence from a search is not a mathematical proof of novelty. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
