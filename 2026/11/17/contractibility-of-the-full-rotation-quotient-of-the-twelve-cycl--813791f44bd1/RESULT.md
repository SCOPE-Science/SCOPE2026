# Contractibility of the full rotation quotient of the twelve-cycle independence complex

## Finding
Let \(C_{12}\) be the simple graph on \(\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\), with consecutive residues adjacent. Let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(C_{12})\) be the abstract simplicial complex of all subsets containing no cyclically adjacent pair. Let \(r(i)=i+1\), and let \(C_{12}=\langle r\rangle\) act on the *geometric realization* of \(K\). Then the ordinary topological orbit space satisfies
\[
\boxed{|K|/\langle r\rangle\simeq\{\mathrm{point}\}.}
\]
In particular, all reduced integral homology groups and all homotopy groups of this quotient vanish. This concerns the ordinary orbit space, not the Borel homotopy quotient or the abstract simplicial complex obtained by identifying the original twelve vertices.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices are the residues \(0,1,\ldots,11\). A nonempty face \(F\) is an independent set if \(i,i+1\in F\) never holds, with indices modulo twelve. The quotient is formed *after* geometric realization. All chain groups, boundary coefficients, and homology groups use integral coefficients unless another coefficient ring is explicitly stated.

The theorem concerns this particular twelve-cycle and its complete order-twelve rotation group. No classification of other lengths or of proper rotation subgroups is claimed. The archived motivating source has the verified first-public date 2011-04-03 (arXiv:1104.0433, version 1), and the standard cycle homotopy theorem used below predates this computation.

## Proof
**1. The covering complex is simply connected.** The classical independence-complex calculation for cycles, recalled in Adamaszek's *Clique complexes and graph powers*, Example 6.3, says \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3m})\simeq S^{m-1}\vee S^{m-1}\). With \(m=4\), this gives \(K\simeq S^3\vee S^3\), hence \(|K|\) is path connected and simply connected. This is an ordinary, not equivariant, equivalence; no equivariant conclusion is assumed.

**2. Rotational isotropy kills the quotient fundamental group.** The independent face \(F_4=\{0,3,6,9\}\) is setwise fixed by \(r^3\), which has order four, and the independent face \(F_3=\{0,4,8\}\) is setwise fixed by \(r^4\), which has order three. Their barycentres are fixed points of those rotations in \(|K|\). Because \(\langle r^3,r^4\rangle=\langle r\rangle\), the subgroup generated by all elements of the rotation group with fixed points is the entire group. Armstrong's fundamental-group theorem for finite simplicial group actions on simply connected complexes therefore gives \(\pi_1(|K|/\langle r\rangle)=1\).

**3. A regular orbit CW structure.** Take the barycentric subdivision \(\operatorname{sd}K\). A \(d\)-simplex is a strict chain \(F_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_d\) of nonempty independent subsets. The action preserves cardinality and hence the order of this chain. Any rotation stabilizing a chain setwise fixes each of its faces and acts *pointwise* on its geometric simplex. Consequently the orbits of these flag simplices give a genuine regular cellular model (with distinct orbit cells retained, even when their vertex sets agree), and its oriented cellular differential is the alternating sum of the orbits of the deleted-face flags.

Exhaustive enumeration gives 321 nonempty independent faces and 38,347 barycentric flags. Counting flag orbits under twelve rotations produces the quotient cell vector, in dimensions zero through five,
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5)=(30,291,912,1190,660,120).
\]
An independent Burnside count using \(\frac1{12}\sum_{k=0}^{11}|\operatorname{Fix}(r^k)|\) agrees **in each dimension**. For each orbit flag \(v=(F_0,\ldots,F_d)\), the integral boundary is
\[
\partial[v]=\sum_{j=0}^d(-1)^j[F_0\subsetneq\cdots\widehat{F_j}\cdots\subsetneq F_d],
\]
where square brackets denote the rotational orbit of the indicated flag. The resulting exact matrices satisfy \(\partial^2=0\).

**4. Exact integral acyclicity certificate.** The attached `c12_reduction_certificate.json` records a specific finite sequence of 1,242 elementary free-face collapses, each deleting a cell and its *unique maximal* coface with signed incidence \(\pm1\). These are genuine CW collapses. On the residual integral chain complex it records 359 algebraic cancellations with signed pivot \(a=\pm1\). For such a pivot from a degree-\(d\) cell \(v\) to a degree-\(d-1\) cell \(u\), each remaining degree-\(d\) column \(w\) is replaced by
\[
\partial'(w)=\partial(w)-a^{-1}[u]\partial(w)\,\partial(v),
\]
and the pivot row and column are deleted. This is an invertible integral change of chain bases followed by splitting off an acyclic two-term chain complex; therefore it preserves integral homology, including torsion. The certificate ends with exactly one generator in degree zero and zero differentials. Thus \(\widetilde H_*(|K|/\langle r\rangle;\mathbb Z)=0\).

**5. Contractibility.** The quotient is a connected finite CW complex. By steps 2 and 4, it is simply connected and integrally acyclic. If some higher homotopy group were nonzero, the first such group would give nonzero homology by the Hurewicz theorem, contradiction. All homotopy groups vanish, and Whitehead's theorem implies contractibility.

## Verification
Run, in one directory, `python3 verify_cycle12_quotient.py c12_reduction_certificate.json` with standard Python 3. The program rebuilds the faces and all flags from the twelve-cycle adjacency condition, recomputes all orbit cells and signed integer boundaries, checks \(\partial^2=0\), checks every Burnside cell count, replays all elementary collapses, and then replays each exact integral unit-pivot cancellation from the supplied certificate. It checks that only one degree-zero generator remains and prints `CERTIFICATE_OK`. See `cycle12_verification_output.txt` for the recorded complete output.

The finite calculation is exhaustive for **this specified twelve-vertex complex**, not a numerical experiment extrapolated to arbitrary cycle lengths. The topological identification additionally requires the cycle independence-complex theorem and Armstrong's theorem as used explicitly in steps 1 and 2; chain data alone would not prove contractibility.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's cycle independence-complex theorem, in the account by Adamaszek (arXiv:1104.0433), gives the homotopy type of \(|K|\), not the full rotation quotient. The existing arithmetic calculation of cyclic-rotation quotients of cycle matching/independence complexes gives a trivial fundamental group and vanishing positive-degree **rational** homology at length twelve, but does not settle possible integral torsion. The present calculation resolves that remaining issue and proves the actual homotopy type.

The separate established theorem on rotation quotients of *critical polygon Vietoris--Rips complexes* concerns \(\operatorname{Cl}(C_{3k}^k)\); at twelve vertices it is \(\operatorname{Cl}(C_{12}^4)\), not \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_{12})\). The two coverings are not even homotopy equivalent: the former is a wedge of three two-spheres whereas the latter is a wedge of two three-spheres. A complete dihedral-quotient result for the cycle independence complex also does not determine the cyclic quotient, because taking a further reflection quotient need not preserve integral homotopy.

## Limitations
The result is a single natural exact-length classification, not a uniform theorem for all cycles. Algebraic unit cancellations prove integral acyclicity, not an elementary collapse of the entire quotient. No geometric homeomorphism classification or independent validation is claimed. The originality comparison is bounded by accessible indexed literature and prior recorded claims, and equivalent unindexed work remains possible.
