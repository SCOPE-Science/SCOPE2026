# Contractibility of the full-symmetry Petersen matching-complex quotient

## Finding
Let \(P=\operatorname{KG}(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph: its ten vertices are the two-element subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), with an edge between disjoint subsets. Let \(M(P)\) be its **ordinary matching complex**, whose simplicial vertices are the fifteen graph edges and whose nonempty faces are collections of pairwise endpoint-disjoint graph edges. The entire symmetric group \(S_5=\operatorname{Aut}(P)\) acts by permuting the five ground labels.

**Theorem.** The ordinary topological orbit space has the complete integral homotopy type
\[
\boxed{\,|M(P)|/S_5\simeq\{\mathrm{point}\}.\,}
\]
In particular all positive-degree integral homology groups and all positive-degree homotopy groups vanish. This is a contractibility claim about the full ordinary matching complex, **not** the different simplicial complex generated only by perfect matchings.

## Assumptions and scope
The quotient is the topological (geometric) orbit space, not the naive simplicial complex on vertex orbits. For the chain calculation, first barycentrically subdivide \(M(P)\). Its quotient is a regular \(\Delta\)-complex whose oriented cells are the \(S_5\)-orbits of strict inclusion chains of nonempty matching faces, with the vertices of each chain ordered by increasing face cardinality. Each group element preserves this rank ordering; therefore simplex stabilizers fix that simplex pointwise and cannot invert it. Consequently the quotient's integral cellular boundary is the signed alternating deletion of the barycentres, summing all incidences landing in the same orbit cell.

The statement concerns this one finite graph and the **full** symmetry group. It makes no claim about partial symmetry quotients, other graphs or equivariant contractibility of the original complex.

## Proof
**1. Simple connectivity before and after quotienting.** The graph \(P\) has fifteen edges. Enumeration of its pairwise disjoint edges gives 15 vertices, 75 edges and 145 triangles in the matching complex's two-skeleton. Choose the lexicographically indexed fourteen one-skeleton edges
```
72, 52, 29, 11, 47, 63, 37, 1, 69, 66, 32, 73, 14, 36
```
(the edge enumeration is reconstructed from first principles in the standalone verifier). These edges are a spanning tree. In the usual edge-path presentation of the fundamental group, tree edges are trivial and each triangle yields a relation among its three boundary edges. Repeatedly imposing a triangle relation with only one unresolved edge proves every one of the 75 edge generators trivial; the successive propagation counts are \(12,16,20,11,2\), alongside the original fourteen tree edges. Hence \(\pi_1(|M(P)|)=1\).

Any transposition of the five labels fixes a Petersen graph edge as a set (the verifier confirms a fixed graph-edge index for the swap of labels zero and one). Thus every transposition fixes a vertex of \(M(P)\). Transpositions generate \(S_5\). Armstrong's finite-group orbit-space theorem states that when a finite group acts simplicially on a simply connected connected complex, the fundamental group of its orbit space is the acting group modulo the normal subgroup generated by elements having a fixed point. Therefore \(\pi_1(|M(P)|/S_5)=1\).

**2. Exact integral quotient chain complex.** Exhaustively enumerate matching faces of cardinalities one through five: their counts are
\[
(15,75,145,90,6).
\]
Apply all \(120\) permutations of the five symbols to matching faces, then to inclusion chains. The orbit counts of strict nonempty face chains of lengths one through five are
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=(10,32,47,30,6).
\]
The 23,911 candidate inclusion-chain visits (with repetitions across containing faces) before orbit reduction are reproduced in `verify_petersen_full_quotient.py`. The alternating-deletion integer boundary matrices satisfy \(\partial^2=0\) exactly. The following **integral** cancellation is then carried out: if a boundary matrix has a coefficient \(a=\pm1\) in row \(i\), column \(j\), use the column containing that entry to eliminate row \(i\) from every other column; on the complementary rows and columns the new differential is the integer Schur complement \(D-ca^{-1}b\). Remove the corresponding rank-one domain and codomain generators, with the induced adjacent differentials restricted as required by \(\partial^2=0\). Each operation splits off a contractible two-term chain summand over \(\mathbb Z\), preserving integral homology.

An exhaustive deterministic sequence of unit operations gives numbers \(9,23,24,6\) in boundary degrees one through four, respectively, or exactly **62 unit cancellations** in total. The residual chain complex consists of one degree-zero generator and no other generators or differentials. Therefore
\[
\widetilde H_i(|M(P)|/S_5;\mathbb Z)=0\quad\text{for every integer }i.
\]
These are algebraic chain contractions, not claimed to be free-face simplicial collapses. They rule out **all** integral torsion, not only torsion at selected primes.

**3. From acyclicity to contractibility.** The orbit space is a finite connected CW complex. If a simply connected CW complex were to have a nonzero higher homotopy group, its first such nonzero group would be isomorphic, by Hurewicz, to a nonzero reduced integral homology group. There are none. The space is therefore weakly contractible and, by Whitehead's theorem, contractible.

It enumerates the graph and all matching faces, verifies a simple-connectivity presentation, exhausts all 120 label permutations, checks a fixed point for a transposition, lists every quotient cell orbit, verifies \(\partial^2=0\) before and after integer cancellation and checks the final residual chain complex. The actual output is supplied as `verification_petersen_output.txt`. No test over a finite field is used to infer an integral result; no infinite-range extrapolation is made.

## Relationship to prior work
Shareshian and Wachs (first public preprint September 3, 2004) define the matching complex of a general graph, developing connectivity and torsion theory principally for complete and complete-bipartite graphs. Zaslavsky (2012, Section 3.2) classifies matching orbit types in the Petersen graph under graph automorphisms, but that classification is of individual matchings, not the incidence-chain quotient topology. The closest identified directly matching result, *Rational \(S_5\)-homology of the Petersen matching complex* (2026), calculates the full rational \(S_5\)-representation and deduces **rational acyclicity** of the full quotient; it explicitly leaves integral quotient torsion undetermined. Rational acyclicity—even together with simple connectivity—does not imply contractibility: a simply connected Moore space with finite nonzero second homology is a counterexample. The integral unit-cancellation certificate is thus a genuinely stronger assertion.

A separate result, *An equivariant graph spine for the Petersen perfect-matching complex* (2026), proves contractibility of its full quotient, but the perfect-matching-generated complex is a **proper subcomplex** of the ordinary matching complex used here. Its equivariant deformation retraction is established only for that smaller space and does not entail this theorem. Armstrong's theorem establishes the necessary fundamental group but not integral acyclicity. No identified exact table or general theorem among the checked sources determines this ordinary full quotient's integral homotopy type.

## Limitations
This finite exact result does not exhibit a geometric free-face collapse of the quotient, an equivariant contraction of \(M(P)\), or a uniform theorem for other graphs. The cited 2012 Petersen-matching classification was accessible as substantial publisher-page material but not as an independently inspected complete PDF; an unindexed equivalent quotient result remains a novelty risk. The algebraic enumeration is reproducible, but no independent mathematical audit is represented.
