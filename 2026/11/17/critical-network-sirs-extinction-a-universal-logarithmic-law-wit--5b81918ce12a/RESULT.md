# Critical network SIRS extinction: a universal logarithmic law with a sharp waning-rate sign reversal

## Finding

For an irreducible finite directed network SIRS epidemic exactly at its spectral extinction threshold, every trajectory with initially nonzero infection has a universal network-shaped inverse-time extinction profile **and** a universal signed logarithmic correction. The two terms are determined by an explicit projected linear solve. On a specified irreducible two-node network, changing only the common immunity-waning rate reverses the sign of the logarithmic correction across one sharp algebraic threshold, while the reproduction number remains exactly one.

Specifically, under the definitions below, for all admissible initial conditions with \(y(0)\ne 0\),

\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t y(t)=\frac{v}{K},\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty}t z(t)=\frac{h}{K},\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{u(t)^{-1}-Kt}{\log t}=\frac{L}{K}.
\]

For the concrete two-node family, the correction is

\[
\frac{L}{K}=-\frac{8d^3+45d^2+27d-54}{9d^2(2d+3)}.
\]

It is positive below and negative above the unique positive root \(d_*\approx 0.795652212912595\) of \(8d^3+45d^2+27d-54=0\).

## Assumptions and scope

Fix an integer \(n\ge2\), a nonnegative irreducible matrix \(W\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\), and positive diagonal matrices \(\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_i)\), \(\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(\delta_i)\). Assume the *exact* critical condition \(\rho(\Gamma^{-1}W)=1\). The SIRS dynamics are

\[
\dot y_i=(1-y_i-z_i)(Wy)_i-\gamma_i y_i,\qquad
\dot z_i=\gamma_i y_i-\delta_i z_i.
\]

Initial data satisfy \(y_i,z_i\ge0\), \(y_i+z_i\le1\), and \(\sum_i y_i>0\). All vector products denoted \(\odot\) are componentwise. Put \(A=W-\Gamma\). Choose positive right and left nullvectors \(v,w\) of \(A\) with \(w^Tv=1\); their remaining common scaling is fixed arbitrarily but consistently (the statements transform covariantly under it). Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=w^Ty,\quad P=vw^T,\quad h=\Delta^{-1}\Gamma v,\\
b&=(v+h)\odot(Wv),\qquad K=w^Tb>0,\\
Ap&=b-Kv,\qquad w^Tp=0,\\
q&=\Delta^{-1}(\Gamma p+K h),\\
L&=w^T\!\left[(v+h)\odot(Wp)+(p+q)\odot(Wv)\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

The equation for \(p\) has a unique solution on \(\ker w^T\), because all eigenvalues of the restriction of the irreducible Metzler matrix \(A\) to that subspace have strictly negative real part. The scalar \(L/K\) is understood with exactly these normalizations. The theorem does not require symmetric contacts or identical recovery and waning rates.

## Proof

**1. Critical attraction and the slowly varying Perron coordinate.** The product of the population triangles \(\{(y_i,z_i):y_i,z_i\ge0,\ y_i+z_i\le1\}\) is forward invariant. Irreducibility makes every infected component positive at every positive time when the initial infection vector is nonzero. The Perron identities \(Av=0\) and \(w^TA=0\) give the exact equality

\[
\dot u=-w^TN,\qquad N=(y+z)\odot(Wy)\ge0.
\]

The nonnegative quantity \(u\) is nonincreasing. Any invariant trajectory on which \(\dot u\equiv0\) must have \(y\equiv0\): a nonzero infected trajectory is strictly positive in every component at positive times, and then every component of \(N\) is positive. On \(y=0\), \(z\) decays exponentially. LaSalle's invariance principle on the compact state space yields \((y,z)\to(0,0)\). Since \(w>0\), \(y=O(u)\) componentwise, so

\[
0\ge\frac{\dot u}{u}\ge-C(\|y\|+\|z\|)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Consequently, for any small \(\varepsilon>0\) and all sufficiently large \(t\ge s\), \(u(s)/u(t)\le e^{\varepsilon(t-s)}\). For each fixed \(r\ge0\), \(u(t-r)/u(t)\to1\).

**2. First-order stable slaving.** Write \(\eta=y-vu\), so \(w^T\eta=0\). Let \(Q=I-P\). The exact forced stable equations are

\[
\dot\eta=A\eta-QN,\qquad \dot z=-\Delta z+\Gamma(vu+\eta).
\]

On \(\ker w^T\), the semigroup generated by \(A\) decays exponentially. The inequality in Step 1 and variation of constants first imply \(z=O(u)\); hence \(N=O(u^2)\). Comparing exponentially weighted convolutions with the slowly varying denominator \(u\) gives

\[
\frac{\eta}{u}\to0,\qquad \frac{z}{u}\to h.
\]

This follows rigorously by splitting each convolution into a bounded recent-time interval and an exponentially dominated older-time tail: choose \(\varepsilon\) smaller than the stable spectral gaps, use \(u(t-r)/u(t)\le e^{\varepsilon r}\), and use pointwise convergence for fixed \(r\). It follows that \(N/u^2\to b\) and

\[
\dot u=-Ku^2+o(u^2),\quad K=w^Tb>0.
\]

Integrating \((1/u)'=K+o(1)\) yields \(t u(t)\to1/K\).

**3. Second-order stable slaving.** The same convolution argument, now dividing by \(u(t)^2\) and choosing twice the slow-ratio exponent below the stable gaps, applies to \(N/u^2\to b\). It gives

\[
\frac{\eta}{u^2}\to p,\qquad Ap=Qb=b-Kv,\quad w^Tp=0.
\]

For \(\zeta=z-hu\) the exact equation is \(\dot\zeta=-\Delta\zeta+\Gamma\eta-h\dot u\). Because \(-\dot u/u^2\to K\), another stable convolution yields

\[
\frac{\zeta}{u^2}\to q=\Delta^{-1}(\Gamma p+Kh).
\]

Thus \(y=vu+pu^2+o(u^2)\) and \(z=hu+qu^2+o(u^2)\). Componentwise multiplication now gives

\[
N=u^2b+u^3\left[(v+h)\odot(Wp)+(p+q)\odot(Wv)\right]+o(u^3).
\]

Consequently \(\dot u=-Ku^2-Lu^3+o(u^3)\), and therefore

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\frac1u=K+Lu+o(u)=K+\frac{L/K}{t}+o(t^{-1}).
\]

Integration from a fixed positive time to \(t\), divided by \(\log t\), proves the claimed logarithmic limit, uniformly in the sense that the same constants apply to each nonzero initial infection profile. The first two vector limits follow from Step 2. The argument makes no assertion that the residual after subtracting the logarithm converges to a finite constant.

**4. Sharp two-node reversal.** Take

\[
W=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\1/2&3/2\end{pmatrix},\quad
\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),\quad \Delta=dI,\quad d>0.
\]

Then \(v=(1,1)^T\), \(w=(2/3,1/3)^T\), \(\operatorname{spec}(A)=\{0,-3/4\}\), and \(\rho(\Gamma^{-1}W)=1\) at every \(d>0\). Substitution into the projected solve gives

\[
K=\frac{2(2d+3)}{3d},\qquad
p=\frac{d+3}{9d}\begin{pmatrix}4\\-8\end{pmatrix},\qquad
L=-\frac{2(8d^3+45d^2+27d-54)}{27d^3}.
\]

Because the derivative \(24d^2+90d+27\) of the cubic numerator is strictly positive for \(d>0\), that numerator has exactly one positive zero. The denominator of \(L/K\) is positive; hence its sign reverses exactly once. As exact checks, at \(d=1/2\) the logarithmic coefficient is \(113/36>0\), whereas at \(d=1\) it is \(-26/45<0\). Only the waning parameter varies; criticality is unchanged.

## Verification

The accompanying `checks/check.py` independently recomputes the two-node Perron normalization, the projected equations, the cubic infection expansion, the second-order invariance equations, the exact rational sign checks, and the strict positivity of the cubic derivative using symbolic arithmetic. Optional SciPy integrations at two waning rates compare finite-time logarithmic residual slopes to the predicted signs and constants. These trajectory checks are supplementary; the infinite-time result rests on Steps 1–4, not on finite simulation.

## Relationship to prior work

Gatti and Como (first public version April 22, 2026) establish the global disease-free threshold \(\rho(\Gamma^{-1}W)\le1\), along with the critical weighted infected functional used in Step 1. Their result does not determine the sharp critical decay profile or its logarithmic correction. Bonaccorsi and Turri analyze heterogeneous-network SIRS systems and equilibrium thresholds under related incidence assumptions without the present two-term directed-network critical law. Existing critical asymptotics for adaptive SIS and other epidemic models are mathematically related but do not imply the displayed \(p,q,L\) coefficients for the directed SIRS equations. No claim is made that inverse-time decay or logarithmic corrections as general dynamical phenomena are new.

## Limitations

This is a finite-dimensional deterministic network mean-field theorem, not a theorem for the finite stochastic epidemic. It applies only at the exact spectral threshold, with positive recovery and waning rates, irreducible contact matrix, and nonzero initial infection. The expansion controls the signed term at order \(\log t\) in reciprocal prevalence and does not establish a bounded next-order remainder. The sharp reversal is established for the stated two-node family; arbitrary networks need not display a reversal. Historical priority relative to less-accessible specialized network-SIRS asymptotic literature is not completely settled. This work has not undergone independent mathematical audit.
