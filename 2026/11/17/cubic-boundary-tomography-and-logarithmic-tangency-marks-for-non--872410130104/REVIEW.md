# Scientific review: cubic boundary tomography for nonuniform random chords

## Correctness — PASS

The line-normal/offset transformation includes ordered-endpoint multiplicity and the Jacobian. The two near-tangent/regular sectors contribute equally; the double-singular sector is bounded by transversality and vanishes after division by the logarithm. Integrating the resulting uniform angular asymptotic produces the crossing boundary law and cuberoot reconstruction. The isolated-label law is derived from the leading sectors, and its log-uniform normal offset has exact scale from the square-root radial cutoff. Scheffé gives total variation. The independent finite-angle FFT quadrature reproduces the known uniform formula and tracks the nonuniform boundary prediction. Numerical tests supplement but do not certify the limiting argument.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority

Poonen–Rubinstein (1995; finite regular-polygon intersections) and Bortolotto–Souza (2025; uniform-endpoint radial law) are structurally different or narrower. Bortolotto–Ramos (2025) addresses arbitrary sampling measures for global extremal probabilities but does not supply this pointwise local cubic coefficient, cube-root inverse or log-tangency marked limit. The 2019 random-disc-chord paper uses different sampling. Direct semantic research-record searches for the new coefficient and aliases returned near papers but no dominating claim. David–Fix (1964) was bibliographically located but its full text was unavailable; historical priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The proposed local boundary observation identifies the entire sampling density without directly observing endpoints and captures a sharp non-Gaussian rare-event mechanism (three near-colliding endpoints, one independently sampled isolated point, and a uniform logarithmic tangency exponent). Both the inverse characterization and conditional shape mechanism go beyond the old uniform marginal law.

## Limitations

Strictly positive normalized C1 angular sampling densities, four independent endpoints, and one circular boundary only. No rates of convergence, singular input measures or independent mathematical validation.
