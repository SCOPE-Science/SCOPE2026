# Scientific review: curvature-weighted small-inradius law

## Correctness — PASS
The sixfold rooted-gap measure is normalized exactly. The local area-to-semiperimeter calculation gives the \(\kappa ab/4\) cusp, whose hyperbolic sublevel area has the logarithmic coefficient. Noncorner strata contribute only order \(t\), while a differentiable inverse change of variables and Scheffé's lemma establish total-variation convergence of the location, logarithmic split and normalized deficit. Hölder and total curvature prove the two sharp optimizers. The standalone ellipse and circle tests passed; finite computation is supporting evidence only.

## Originality — PASS (with residual historical risk)
The closest public circle inradius distribution is a special case explicitly excluded from the new claim. The general smooth-curve small-area law has a different observable, scaling and curvature optimizer, so its full statement does not imply the present inradius asymptotic or spatial marked law. The previously known \(\sqrt\kappa\) sampler for expected Hausdorff polygon approximation does not give this new inradius functional. Relevant original affine polygon and random area/perimeter literature was compared. The 2023 isoperimetric triangle study has a different sampling model; its accessible summary was read, not treated as complete evidence against all possible overlap. Historical priority remains provisional.

## Value — PASS
The result identifies the curvature-sensitive coefficient of a natural triangle-degeneracy event, its rare-event location and scale law, and the unique best boundary sampling strategy. Its \(\sqrt\kappa\) density contrasts concretely with affine arclength \(\kappa^{1/3}\), and the sharp equal-perimeter circle bound gives a geometric extremal consequence.

## Limitations
