# Cyclic fixed-point persistence of evenly spaced circular Čech nerves

## Finding

For each integer \(n\ge2\), let \(\mathcal N(n,k)\) be the simplicial complex on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) generated by the clockwise intervals of \(k+1\) consecutive vertices, for \(0\le k\le n-1\). These are the nerves of \(n\) evenly spaced closed circular arcs of normalized length \(k/n\), or equivalently ambient Čech complexes for those samples at radius \(k/(2n)\).

Let \(d\ge2\) divide \(n\), put \(m=n/d\), and let \(H=C_d\) be the order-\(d\) subgroup of polygon rotations, generated by the shift \(i\mapsto i+m\). For every \(k\), the **geometric**, rather than merely vertexwise, fixed-point space is

\[
|\mathcal N(n,k)|^{H}\cong_{\rm PL}
\begin{cases}
\varnothing,&0\le k<(d-1)m,\\
|\mathcal N(m,k-(d-1)m)|,&(d-1)m\le k\le n-1.
\end{cases}
\]

All these homeomorphisms are compatible with the inclusions as \(k\) increases. They intertwine residual rotation and reflection, so they are equivariant for the induced action of the dihedral quotient \(D_n/H\) on the fixed space (with the natural possibly nonfaithful small-polygon action).

In particular, for \(m\ge2\) fixed points first appear as \(m\) isolated points, and for \(m\ge3\) the next stage is homotopy equivalent to a circle. The complete integral persistent-homology diagram of the fixed filtration is exactly that of the smaller \(m\)-vertex circular-arc nerve filtration, shifted by \((d-1)m\) parameter steps.

## Assumptions and scope

All complexes are finite, use closed intervals, and include faces of every maximal cyclic interval. The case \(k=n-1\) is the full simplex. A face means a **nonempty** subset whenever the geometric fixed space is discussed; the empty abstract face does not define a geometric point. The theorem covers every composite or prime \(n\), every nontrivial rotational subgroup, and every integer filtration level. For \(m=1\) the only nonempty fixed level is the terminal full simplex, whose fixed set is a point.

## Proof

**Orbit-barycenter lemma.** For a finite simplicial complex \(K\) with a group \(H\) permuting its vertices, form the orbit complex \(K_H\): its vertices are the \(H\)-orbits of vertices of \(K\), and a collection of orbits is a face precisely when their union is a face of \(K\). Define the map of realizations by sending the vertex corresponding to an orbit \(O\) to its barycenter \(|O|^{-1}\sum_{v\in O}v\). Barycentric coordinates of an \(H\)-fixed point in \(|K|\) are constant along every \(H\)-orbit; its positive-coordinate support is a union of whole orbits and is a face. Conversely every point in the orbit complex maps to such a fixed point. The maps and their inverses are linear on each simplex, giving a canonical PL-homeomorphism \(|K_H|\cong |K|^H\). This proof also establishes naturality under inclusions and commuting symmetries.

In our setting there are exactly \(m\) orbits
\[
O_a=\{a,a+m,\ldots,a+(d-1)m\},\qquad a\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z.
\]
Let \(\varnothing\ne B\subseteq\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), and put \(U(B)=\bigcup_{a\in B}O_a\). For a nonempty subset \(S\) of a cyclically ordered \(s\)-element vertex set, define \(g_s(S)\) as the largest forward cyclic gap (measured in integer steps) between consecutive selected vertices. For a singleton, the gap is \(s\). The elementary interval criterion is
\[
S\in\mathcal N(s,t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_s(S)\ge s-t.
\]
Indeed, \(S\) fits into \(t+1\) consecutive positions exactly when its complement contains a run of at least \(s-t-1\) missing consecutive positions.

The points of \(U(B)\) reproduce the same cyclic gap pattern in each of the \(d\) consecutive blocks of length \(m\). Hence
\[
g_n(U(B))=g_m(B)\le m.
\]
If \(k<(d-1)m\), then \(n-k>m\), so no nonempty orbit union is a face and the geometric fixed space is empty. Otherwise put \(j=k-(d-1)m\), where \(0\le j\le m-1\). Then
\[
U(B)\in\mathcal N(n,k)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 g_m(B)\ge n-k=m-j\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\in\mathcal N(m,j).
\]
Thus the orbit complex is **simplicially equal**, under orbit labels, to \(\mathcal N(m,j)\). Combining this with the orbit-barycenter lemma proves the PL-homeomorphism.

The same orbit barycenters are used at every filtration level, so the isomorphisms strictly commute with inclusions. A one-step original rotation maps \(O_a\) to \(O_{a+1}\), and reflection of labels \(i\mapsto-i\) maps \(O_a\) to \(O_{-a}\). These induce the usual smaller dihedral action and finish the equivariance proof.

**Homotopy and integral persistence.** By Theorem 3.6 of Adamaszek–Adams–Frick–Peterson–Previte-Johnson (2014), for \(m\ge2\), \(0\le j\le m-2\), and an integer \(\ell\ge0\), the smaller nerve is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{m-j-1}S^{2\ell}\) when \(j/m=\ell/(\ell+1)\), and to \(S^{2\ell+1}\) when \(\ell/(\ell+1)<j/m<(\ell+1)/(\ell+2)\). The stage \(j=m-1\) is contractible. These previously established nonequivariant results, combined with the **new strict filtered PL identification**, give the full isomorphism of integral fixed-space persistence modules without assuming that pointwise homotopy equivalences determine persistence maps.

## Verification

A standalone Python checker independently enumerates all orbit-saturated nonempty subsets and all cyclic intervals, and compares three exact tests: occurrence as a face of the original nerve, the largest cyclic-gap criterion, and occurrence as a face of the shifted smaller nerve. This was exhaustively performed for all \(2\le n\le24\), every nontrivial divisor \(d\mid n\), and all \(0\le k\le n-1\): **896 parameter triples and 195,110 nonempty orbit-subset tests**. An additional **382** direct checks verify the smaller rotation and reflection actions on the orbit labels.

For example, \(n=18\) and \(d=3\) give \(m=6\): the fixed filtration is empty through \(k=11\), then is respectively six points, a circle, a circle, \(\bigvee^2S^2\), \(S^4\), and a point for \(k=12,13,14,15,16,17\). These topology labels follow from the cited homotopy theorem, not merely the finite checker.

Run `python verify_circle_cech_fixed.py` with Python 3's standard library. Its expected exact output is provided in `verification_output.txt`. The exhaustive finite tests support but do not replace the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The 2014 paper *Nerve complexes of circular arcs* defines precisely these nerves (Definition 3.2), proves their nonequivariant homotopy types (Theorem 3.6), and describes the dihedral action on homology (Section 7). Its homology characters do not classify the actual geometric fixed spaces or the inclusion maps among them. The 2015 preprint *The Vietoris–Rips complexes of a circle* analyzes circular Čech complexes and related nonequivariant persistence, especially Section 9, but likewise does not give the all-subgroup filtered PL models here.

Published results on polygonal **Vietoris–Rips/clique** fixed filtrations concern a different complex: taking a clique completion changes the space. The distinction matters even in elementary examples; the Čech nerve \(\mathcal N(6,3)\) is a wedge of two two-spheres, while its clique completion is a simplex. Accordingly a polygonal Rips fixed-filtration theorem cannot imply the Čech fixed-space theorem by substituting parameters. The contribution here is the explicit *geometric* fixed-space and residual-equivariant identification of the whole nerve filtration, not the preexisting ordinary homotopy classification.

## Limitations

The result addresses finite regular samples and nontrivial **rotational** subgroups, not all dihedral subgroups containing reflections or arbitrary nonuniform samples. It uses closed arcs and integer levels; strict/open threshold conventions require endpoint adjustments. The established nonequivariant sphere classification is cited, not reproved. No canonical integral chain-level isomorphism between the *ambient* and fixed-space homology is asserted, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed. An unindexed equivalent earlier equivariant formulation cannot be excluded solely by search.
