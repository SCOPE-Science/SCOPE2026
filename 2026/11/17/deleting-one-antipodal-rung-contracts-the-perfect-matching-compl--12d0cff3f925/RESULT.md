# Deleting one antipodal rung contracts the perfect-matching complex of every even-width Möbius ladder

## Finding
For every even integer \(n\ge4\), form the Möbius ladder \(M_{2n}\) by adding the \(n\) antipodal edges \(\{i,i+n\}\) to the cycle on vertices \(0,1,\ldots,2n-1\), with indices on the cycle taken modulo \(2n\). Remove exactly one antipodal edge, namely \(\{0,n\}\), and call the resulting graph \(G_n\). If \(\mathcal P(G)\) denotes the simplicial complex with graph edges as vertices and graph perfect matchings as facets, then
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal P(G_n)|\simeq *\quad\text{for every even }n\ge4.}
\]
In contrast, the intact graph satisfies \(|\mathcal P(M_{2n})|\simeq S^{(n-2)/2}\vee S^{(n-2)/2}\) for even \(n\), as established by Agarwal and Basak. Thus deleting a *single* antipodal rung destroys both homology generators in every even width. The statement is an integral homotopy classification, not only a computation of field-valued Betti numbers.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected and simple; all complexes are ordinary finite abstract simplicial complexes including the empty face. The perfect-matching complex used here is *not* the ordinary matching complex: a matching is a face exactly when it extends to a perfect matching. The result covers all even \(n\ge4\), with no assertion about odd \(n\) or removal of a cycle edge. The homotopy equivalence is non-equivariant. The convention \(|K|\simeq *\) means contractible, not the empty complex.

Use the equivalent two-row presentation with column vertices \(a_i=i\) and \(b_i=i+n\), \(0\le i<n\). The edges of \(G_n\) are the two horizontal paths \(a_ia_{i+1}\) and \(b_ib_{i+1}\) for \(0\le i<n-1\), vertical rungs \(a_ib_i\) for \(1\le i<n\), and the two twisted seam edges \(a_0b_{n-1}\) and \(b_0a_{n-1}\). In particular the rung at column zero is absent.

## Proof
**Step 1: a perfect matching cannot use exactly one twisted seam.** Suppose a perfect matching uses precisely one seam edge. For each cut between the first \(j\) columns and the remaining columns, where \(1\le j<n\), the number of matching edges crossing the cut is even because the first \(j\) columns contain \(2j\) vertices. Exactly one seam edge crosses each such cut. Hence exactly one of the two horizontal edges between columns \(j-1\) and \(j\) must belong to the perfect matching. Within an intermediate column, the arriving and departing horizontal edges therefore use opposite rows, and no vertical rung is used. Across the \(n-1\) horizontal steps, the row alternates. For even \(n\), \(n-1\) is odd, so a seam edge joining opposite rows forces the first and final horizontal edges to use the same row; this would match the seam endpoint twice at the final column. Contradiction. Thus every perfect matching uses either zero or both seam edges.

**Step 2: an exact two-cone cover.** If neither seam edge is used, both vertices in column zero must be matched horizontally to column one; call those two forced edges \(F\). All remaining edges form an arbitrary perfect matching of the ordinary straight two-row ladder on columns \(2,\ldots,n-1\). If both seam edges are used, those two edges constitute a forced set \(S\), and the remaining edges form an arbitrary perfect matching of the straight ladder on columns \(1,\ldots,n-2\). Let \(A\) and \(B\) be the subcomplexes generated by these two respective sets of perfect matchings. Every facet of \(A\) contains the two edges in \(F\), so \(A\) is a simplicial cone. Likewise every facet of \(B\) contains both edges of \(S\), so \(B\) is a simplicial cone. Step 1 proves \(\mathcal P(G_n)=A\cup B\).

**Step 3: exact identification of the intersection.** Let \(T_m\) denote the perfect-matching complex of a straight \(2\)-by-\(m\) ladder. The common graph edges available to both families are exactly those of the middle ladder on columns \(2,\ldots,n-2\), whose length is \(n-3\). A matching of this middle ladder which extends to a perfect matching of either larger ladder also extends to a perfect matching of the middle ladder itself: in any extension, if the outermost added column is covered by a vertical rung, simply remove that rung; otherwise the outermost added column is covered by two horizontal edges from its adjacent middle column, and removing those two horizontal edges and replacing them by the vertical rung in that adjacent middle column yields a perfect matching of the middle ladder containing the original partial matching. The replacement is legitimate because neither of the two adjacent middle vertices was covered by the original partial matching. Conversely, every perfect matching of the middle ladder extends to a perfect matching in both larger ladders by using a vertical rung in the one extra column (and adding \(F\) or \(S\), respectively). Taking downward closures, one obtains the literal equality of simplicial subcomplexes
\[
A\cap B=T_{n-3},
\]
where the right-hand side is embedded on the middle ladder's edges.

**Step 4: every odd straight-ladder perfect-matching complex is contractible.** The one-column case \(T_1\) is a single vertex. For every \(m\ge3\), partition all perfect matchings of the straight \(m\)-column ladder according to their first column: either its two vertices are joined by its vertical rung, or they are each joined horizontally to column two. Let \(U\) and \(V\) be the associated facet-generated subcomplexes. These are cones, because the first class contains its vertical rung in every facet and the second class contains both first-pair horizontal edges in every facet. The same outer-column extension/replacement argument as in Step 3 identifies \(U\cap V\) with \(T_{m-2}\). Since the intersection is a subcomplex, the inclusion is a cofibration. The union of two contractible CW complexes along a nonempty contractible common CW subcomplex is contractible (for example by the homotopy-pushout gluing theorem). Induction on odd \(m\), beginning with \(T_1\), proves that all \(T_m\) with odd \(m\ge1\) are contractible.

**Step 5: final gluing.** For even \(n\ge4\), the length \(n-3\) is positive and odd. Hence \(A\), \(B\), and \(A\cap B=T_{n-3}\) are all nonempty and contractible finite CW complexes. The same cofibration gluing theorem shows that \(A\cup B=\mathcal P(G_n)\) is contractible. This proves the claimed uniform integral homotopy type. \(\square\)

## Verification
The included standard-library Python program `verify.py` generates all perfect matchings from the *graph edge list*, independently tests the dichotomy of the two seam states, compares the actual facetwise intersection with the middle-ladder perfect-matching complex, and checks both asserted exact cone parametrizations at \(n=4,6,8,10,12\). The counts of perfect matchings in those cases are respectively \(4,10,26,68,178\). For \(n=4,6,8,10\) it also generates every simplex and performs checked elementary free-face simplicial collapses to one vertex (\(26,233,2096,18863\) collapses, respectively). The finite tests corroborate Steps 1–3 and the conclusion, but only the symbolic arguments in Steps 1–5 establish the theorem for all even integers.

Reproduce with `python3 verify.py` in the folder containing `verify.py`. Compare stdout to `verification_output.txt`. The program uses the Python standard library and needs no external files, internet access or computer algebra system.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, *The Homotopy Types of the Independence and Perfect Matching Complexes of Möbius and Circular Ladder Graphs* (first publicly posted 2026-08-31; arXiv:2608.30601), classify the full, undeleted Möbius ladder. Their even-width perfect-matching result is the two-sphere wedge quoted in the Finding section. It does not give a simplicial restriction or a homotopy statement for deleting one antipodal edge, because removing a graph edge changes which partial matchings can extend to a perfect matching.

Chandrakar and Singh, *Perfect Matching Complexes of Polygonal Line Tilings* (first publicly posted 2024-07-08; arXiv:2407.05809), Theorem 3.3, already prove the odd-width straight-ladder contractibility used here. The proof above includes an independent cone-gluing derivation to keep the new result self-contained; the isolated straight-ladder theorem is **not** claimed as new. Their theorem does not classify the defective Möbius ladder, which requires the separate seam-state dichotomy and exact intersection identification.

## Limitations
No claim is made of elementary collapsibility for *all* widths (the program establishes it only for the finite instances reported); contractibility does not generally imply simplicial collapsibility. No generalization to odd widths, deletion of horizontal cycle edges, multiple edge defects, integral equivariant decompositions, or arbitrary cylindrical graphs is established. A literature search cannot exclude unindexed equivalent formulations; the mathematical statement merits independent examination. The auxiliary open-ladder contractibility result was known before this work.
