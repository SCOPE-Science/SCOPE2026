# Dihedral and layer-exchange modules in lower homology of circular-ladder perfect-matching complexes

## Finding
Let \(n=6k\) with \(k\ge2\). For the circular ladder \(L_n=C_n\square K_2\), let \(X_n=\mathcal M_p(L_n)\) be the simplicial complex whose faces are graph matchings extendible to a perfect matching. The group \(G_n=D_n\times C_2\) acts naturally. Put \(r(i,a)=(i+1,a)\), \(t(i,a)=(-i,a)\), and \(s(i,a)=(i,1-a)\), with indices modulo \(n\). Thus \(r\) is a column rotation, \(t\) a column reflection, and \(s\) exchanges the layers.

Let \(\rho_j\) denote the two-dimensional rational dihedral representation with rotation character \(\operatorname{tr}(r^a\mid\rho_j)=2\cos(2\pi ja/n)\) and trace zero on all reflections; here \(j=n/6\) or \(n/3\), so the representation is defined over \(\mathbb Q\). Write \(\mathbf 1\) and \(\varepsilon\) for the trivial and sign representations of the layer-exchange factor \(C_2\).

The lower, four-dimensional reduced rational homology has the **complete** equivariant decomposition
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_{2k}(X_n;\mathbb Q)\cong (\rho_{n/6}\boxtimes\mathbf1)\oplus(\rho_{n/3}\boxtimes\varepsilon).}
\]
Equivalently, for \(a\in\mathbb Z/n\) and \(e,f\in\{0,1\}\), its character on \(r^at^es^f\) is
\[
\chi_n(r^at^es^f)=
\begin{cases}
4\cos(\pi a/3),&e=0\text{ and } f\equiv a\pmod2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, \(s\) has two \(+1\) and two \(-1\) eigenvectors, one-step rotation has eigenvalues of orders six and three, and no nonzero class in this degree is invariant under all of \(G_n\). Thus \(\widetilde H_{2k}(|X_n|/G_n;\mathbb Q)=0\). No assertion is made about the other nonzero homological degree or the full quotient's homotopy type.

## Assumptions and scope
All simplicial homology is reduced; the empty simplex contributes in chain degree \(-1\). A perfect-matching complex is the downward-closed complex generated by perfect matchings, not the ordinary matching complex. Here \(n\) counts *columns* (rungs), so the graph has \(2n\) vertices. The theorem concerns exactly the widths \(n=12,18,24,\ldots\) and the specified natural group, with rational coefficients. The source's nonequivariant theorem provides
\[
X_n\simeq \bigvee^2 S^{3k-1}\vee\bigvee^4 S^{2k},
\]
so the lower homology lies in degree \(2k\), separated from the higher degree \(3k-1\). The wedge decomposition is used only nonequivariantly and is not asserted equivariant.

## Proof
**1. An invariant base and two exceptional simplices.** Name the rim edges \(b_i=\{(i,0),(i+1,0)\}\), \(c_i=\{(i,1),(i+1,1)\}\), and rung edges \(a_i=\{(i,0),(i,1)\}\), with all indices modulo \(n\). Let \(B_n\) be the simplicial complex of matchings that avoid all pairs \(\{b_i,c_{i+1}\}\) and \(\{c_i,b_{i+1}\}\). These are the two cyclic types of forbidden cross pairs. This condition is preserved by \(G_n\).

Let \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) be the full simplices on the two alternating all-rim perfect matchings: \(P_0\) uses the \(b_i\) with odd \(i\) and the \(c_i\) with even \(i\); \(P_1\) uses the opposite choice. Their edge sets are disjoint. The matching-extension analysis of Agarwal--Basak, Lemmas 22--24 and Theorem 25, gives the exact equality of simplicial complexes and their intersections
\[
X_n=B_n\cup P_0\cup P_1,\qquad
B_n\cap P_i\cong\operatorname{Ind}(C_n),\qquad
B_n\simeq S^{(n-2)/2}\vee S^{(n-2)/2}.
\]
The cycle in each intersection is on the \(n\) consecutive alternating rim edges: neighboring such edges constitute a forbidden cross pair. The last equivalence is an **ordinary** homotopy equivalence, not a claim of equivariant folding. In our range \((n-2)/2=3k-1>2k\), so \(\widetilde H_{2k}(B_n;\mathbb Q)=\widetilde H_{2k-1}(B_n;\mathbb Q)=0\).

**2. Natural relative homology.** The long exact sequence of \((X_n,B_n)\) therefore gives a canonical \(G_n\)-equivariant isomorphism
\[
\widetilde H_{2k}(X_n;\mathbb Q)\cong H_{2k}(X_n,B_n;\mathbb Q).
\]
Every face outside \(B_n\) is supported in exactly one of \(P_0\) and \(P_1\), so the *relative chain complex* is the direct sum of the two relative simplex chain complexes. The group permutes these summands transitively. Because each simplex is contractible, the natural connecting homomorphism gives
\[
H_{2k}(P_i,B_n\cap P_i;\mathbb Q)\cong
\widetilde H_{2k-1}(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n);\mathbb Q).
\]
Call the latter two-dimensional module \(W_n\), with the dihedral action inherited from its alternating-edge cycle.

The stabilizer \(H\le G_n\) of \(P_0\) has index two and is generated by \(R=rs\) and \(T=ts\). On the cyclically indexed alternating edges of \(P_0\), \(R\) moves an edge one position forward and \(T\) sends edge-index \(i\) to \(-i-1\). Thus \(H\cong D_n\), acting as the ordinary full dihedral group on \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\), and the natural relative-chain construction gives
\[
\widetilde H_{2k}(X_n;\mathbb Q)\cong \operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G_n}W_n.
\]
There is no implicit choice of an equivariant wedge splitting in this step.

**3. Complete character of the cycle module.** The standard cycle-independence homotopy calculation gives \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_{6k})\simeq S^{2k-1}\vee S^{2k-1}\). The character can be determined without choosing generators. For an automorphism \(g\) of a simplicial complex, a fixed simplex built from \(m\) vertex-permutation orbits contributes \((-1)^{m-1}\) to the reduced signed Lefschetz sum, including the empty face as \(-1\). Hence the reduced Lefschetz number is minus the independence polynomial at \(-1\) of the graph of admissible vertex orbits.

For \(R^a\), put \(d=\gcd(n,a)\), with \(d=n\) for \(a=0\). Its orbits are residue classes modulo \(d\), whose conflicts form \(C_d\) when \(d\ge3\), a single edge when \(d=2\), and a forbidden loop when \(d=1\). The signed independence sums in all these cases agree with \(2\cos(\pi d/3)\). This follows directly from the path recurrence \(p_m=p_{m-1}-p_{m-2}\) for independence polynomials at \(-1\), or from the two-state transfer matrix. As the only nonzero reduced homology degree \(2k-1\) is odd, its trace is
\[
\operatorname{tr}(R^a\mid W_n)=2\cos(\pi d/3)=2\cos(\pi a/3).
\]
The last equality holds because \(6\mid n\) and the two arguments have the same cosine among the six possible residue classes. If \(g\) is a vertex-axis reflection of \(C_{2m}\), its orbit-conflict graph is the path on \(m+1\) vertices. If it is an edge-axis reflection, the two paired adjacent-vertex orbits cannot be selected, leaving a path on \(m-2\) vertices. Here \(m=3k\), and both path lengths are congruent to one modulo three, so both signed independence sums are zero. Thus all reflection traces on \(W_n\) vanish. These traces identify \(W_n\) with the rational two-dimensional dihedral representation having rotation eigenvalues the primitive sixth roots of unity.

**4. Induction and splitting.** Since \(H\) is the kernel of the parity homomorphism \(G_n\to C_2\) sending \(r,t,s\) all to the nontrivial element, it is normal of index two. The induced character vanishes off \(H\). On an element \(R^aT^e\in H\), the two cosets contribute equal traces because the coset representative \(s\) is central. Reflection traces are zero, and rotation traces double to \(4\cos(\pi a/3)\), yielding the displayed formula.

Over \(\mathbb Q\), the index-two induced module splits into the two extensions of \(W_n\) distinguished by the scalar action of \(s\). In the \(+1\)-extension, \(r=R\) has primitive sixth-root eigenvalues, giving \(\rho_{n/6}\boxtimes\mathbf1\). In the \(-1\)-extension, \(r=-R\) has primitive third-root eigenvalues, giving \(\rho_{n/3}\boxtimes\varepsilon\). Both irreducible characters are rational and distinct. This proves the decomposition and the character formula. Finite-group rational transfer after barycentric subdivision identifies rational quotient homology with invariants and yields the stated vanishing in degree \(2k\). \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, first including circular ladders in their public 2026-09-14 version, prove the nonequivariant homotopy types and the underlying matching-extension decomposition. Neither their Main Theorem 2(c) nor Theorem 25 identifies the homology action of rotations, reflections or layer interchange. In particular, the rank-four lower degree does not determine the two contrasting rotation orders or the layer parity.

A separate published calculation of symmetry for *independence* complexes of circular ladders concerns a different complex on graph vertices, whereas the current object has graph edges as vertices and faces extendible to perfect matchings. A separate equivariant classification of *odd Möbius-ladder perfect-matching* complexes uses a contractible base and the symmetry of a different graph; it does not determine the surviving lower-degree module here, whose base has nonzero higher homology. These are implication comparisons, not merely a distinction between titles. No stronger same-object equivariant classification was found in the checked sources.

## Limitations
This result does not determine the \(G_n\)-module in the other nonzero degree \(3k-1\), an integral \(\mathbb Z[G_n]\)-lattice, a simultaneous equivariant wedge decomposition, or quotient homotopy type. The proof relies on the cited exact matching-extension and ordinary homotopy calculations, with independent direct finite checks but no claim of independent external validation. The accessible-source search cannot exclude unindexed equivalent work.
