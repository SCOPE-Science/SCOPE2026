# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The asserted integral lattice follows from the long exact sequence of a sphere and its graphical-arrangement hyperplane link. The essential detail is that the ambient sphere's oriented fundamental class injects as the *diagonal chamber sum* and that vertex relabeling acts with the orientation sign on each top-dimensional chamber class; the result is the coaugmentation **quotient**, not an erroneously substituted augmentation kernel. Coordinate fixed spheres and quotient-edge graphs give all element-fixed spaces. Reflection edge signs were checked by explicitly mapping every oriented reference edge, including both even-axis types. The rational quotient formula is character averaging, not an unsupported integral quotient computation. The standalone exact enumeration over all 150 elements for orders three to twelve and the independent fixed-space Euler calculation support these deductions.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature

The nonequivariant top Betti rank \(2^n-3\) is an immediate known consequence of the work of Jonsson and Hersh–Swartz, and is excluded from any novelty claim. The checked primary result in Hersh–Swartz identifies the underlying graphical-arrangement link and gives ordinary homotopy, but does not state the complete vertex-dihedral integral module, reflection-dependent fixed loci or quotient ranks. Macauley–Mortveit's dihedral operation on orientations is *source-to-sink flipping plus edge reversal*, not polygon automorphism by relabeling graph vertices. Braun–Crown Rundell discuss a hyperoctahedral action on partition-chain slots for signed graph coloring complexes, not the label-permutation \(D_n\)-module here. No equivalent claim emerged in indexed literature or the available prior-claim comparisons. The full texts of some older publications were not obtained, so this is not a claim of exhaustive publication novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The object is a standard classical coloring complex for the fundamental graph family of cycles. The theorem provides an explicit **integral** group module for the entire infinite family, a genuinely geometric description of each symmetry-fixed space, and closed integer rational quotient ranks; these are not encoded in the known nonequivariant sphere count. The two distinct reflection axis types at even polygon sizes fix \(S^{n/2-2}\) versus a wedge of \(2^{n/2}-1\) such spheres, and their difference persists for arbitrarily large sizes, giving a useful equivariant distinction. For eight graph vertices the full quotient retains 13 rational fifth-homology classes even though the original complex has 253. The coaugmentation presentation retains integral information omitted by rational characters.
