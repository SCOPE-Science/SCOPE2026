# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof gives the exact domain (every simple cycle with at least three vertices) and the complete \(D_n\)-character on rational top homology. Acyclic orientations of induced graphs parameterize commutation-class cells; the sign of a fixed cell is computed from its vertex-label permutation. The augmented cellular Lefschetz trace then becomes a finite signed partition sum. Independent rotation and reflection transfer-matrix arguments evaluate that sum for all integers, with the degenerate cyclic periods one and two explicitly accounted for. The original sphere-wedge theorem supplies the homology concentration premise. The accompanying verifier checks integral boundary identities, equivariance and every group element for the first six sizes, and corroborates top ranks for the first five.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature
The commutation-class model is established by Jonsson and Welker; the homotopy type and affine-cycle Lucas multiplicity by Ragnarsson and Tenner; and graph-dependent mod-two homology bases by Ragnarsson and Tenner. These are credited as prior results. The specific full rational dihedral character, including the reflection-axis distinction for even cycles, was not present in the inspected principal statements, nor found in the scientific database and literature searches. An alternative notation or unindexed equivariant paper could still cover it; unsuccessful searches are not a uniqueness theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result determines a complete representation invariant for an infinite natural family of topological cell complexes, not just its dimension. The signs and axis-dependent traces distinguish actions that are invisible to ordinary homology and determine the entire positive-degree rational homology of the full group quotient.

## Closest literature and limits
The closest object-identical sources are Jonsson–Welker (arXiv:0712.2143) and Ragnarsson–Tenner (arXiv:0806.0906 and arXiv:1005.4411). Babson–Reiner's Coxeter-like complex (DOI:10.46298/dmtcs.312) has different construction and group action in its publicly inspected abstract; its complete text was not read. The conclusion makes no assertion about integral quotient torsion, the integral group-ring representation, or uniqueness against inaccessible literature. The independent verification is restricted to finite cases, while the proof covers all sizes.
