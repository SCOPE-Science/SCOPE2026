# Dihedral parity and integral topology of three-row chessboard quotients

## Finding
For an integer \(n\ge5\), let \(X_n=|\Delta_{3,n}|\), where \(\Delta_{3,n}\) is the simplicial complex of nonattacking placements of at most three rooks on three labeled rows and \(n\) cyclically labeled columns. The dihedral group \(D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\;srs=r^{-1}\rangle\) acts on columns through \(r(c)=c+1\) and \(s(c)=-c\) modulo \(n\), keeping each row fixed. Quotients below are **ordinary geometric orbit spaces**, not Borel constructions or face-orbit posets. Write \(Q_n=X_n/D_n\), and use normalized Lusternik–Schnirelmann category (so a noncontractible wedge of spheres has category one).

**Theorem.** For odd \(n\ge5\),
\[
Q_n\simeq\bigvee^{(n-3)^2/2}S^2,\qquad\operatorname{cat}(Q_n)=1.
\]
More generally, if \(d\mid n\) and \(H\le D_n\) is any dihedral subgroup of order \(2d\), then
\[
X_n/H\simeq\bigvee^{\,[n(n^2-6n+8)/d+1]/2}S^2.
\]
For even \(n\ge6\),
\[
\pi_1(Q_n)\cong H_1(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2,\qquad
H_2(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{(n^2-6n+6)/2},
\]
all integral homology in degrees greater than two vanishes, and the canonical nonzero \(u\in H^1(Q_n;\mathbb F_2)\) satisfies \(u^2\ne0\). Consequently \(\operatorname{cat}(Q_n)=2\). In particular, passing from odd to even numbers of columns changes the quotient fundamental group and its cup-length despite the same natural family of simply connected covering complexes.

## Assumptions and scope
Every face is a set of squares with no repeated row or column; its ordering by increasing row supplies the cellular orientation. The row labels are never permuted. All groups are finite. The source condition \(n\ge5\) is essential to the proof because it gives simple connectivity of \(X_n\). For an odd number of columns, every reflection fixes exactly one column; for an even number, half of the reflections fix two columns and half fix none. Normalized category is the minimal number of categorical open sets minus one. This theorem does not assert the full homotopy type for even \(n\), only its stated groups, product and category.

## Proof
**1. Simple connectivity and dimension.** Ziegler proved that \(\Delta_{m,n}\) is vertex decomposable for \(n\ge2m-1\) (1994, Theorem 2.3, in a preprint dated September 22, 1992). Thus for \(m=3,n\ge5\), it is a pure shellable two-dimensional complex and is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres. In particular \(X_n\) is path connected and simply connected. Directly counting nonattacking rook placements yields
\[
f_0(X_n)=3n,\quad f_1(X_n)=3n(n-1),\quad f_2(X_n)=n(n-1)(n-2),
\]
so \(\chi(X_n)=n(n^2-6n+8)\). Its dimension is exactly two.

**2. Geometric fixed points.** A simplex uses at most one square in each row. Therefore if a column permutation stabilizes a simplex setwise, it fixes every one of its vertices; if it fixes a point of the realization, it fixes each vertex of the smallest face containing the point. Every nonidentity rotation fixes no column and hence has empty fixed locus. For odd \(n\), a reflection fixes one column, so its fixed locus consists of three distinct points and has Euler characteristic three. For even \(n\), the \(n/2\) vertex-axis reflections fix two columns and have fixed locus \(\Delta_{3,2}\), a six-edge circle with Euler characteristic zero; the other \(n/2\) gap-axis reflections fix no column and have empty fixed locus. This dichotomy is exact for all parameters.

**3. Fundamental groups.** By Armstrong's orbit-space theorem, since \(X_n\) is simply connected, \(\pi_1(X_n/G)\cong G/N_G\), where \(N_G\) is the normal subgroup generated by elements having fixed points. When \(n\) is odd, every reflection fixes points; reflections generate \(D_n\) and also any of its dihedral subgroups. The corresponding quotient is simply connected. When \(n\) is even, the fixed-point reflections of the full \(D_n\) generate \(\langle s,r^2\rangle\), a normal subgroup of index two. Consequently \(\pi_1(Q_n)=\mathbb Z/2\).

**4. Euler characteristics.** For a finite cellular action, the Euler characteristic of the quotient is the mean of fixed-set Euler characteristics; this follows here directly by counting simplex orbits, since face stabilizers fix their faces pointwise. For odd \(n\) and a dihedral subgroup of order \(2d\), the only nonidentity fixed-point elements are the \(d\) reflections, each contributing three. Hence
\[
\chi(X_n/H)=\frac{n(n^2-6n+8)+3d}{2d}.
\]
Since the quotient is a simply connected two-dimensional CW complex, its homology is free and concentrated in degree two, and the homological Whitehead theorem identifies it with a wedge of \(\chi-1\) two-spheres, proving both odd-column formulas. For even \(n\), every nonidentity element has fixed-set Euler characteristic zero (including the vertex-axis reflections), so
\[
\chi(Q_n)=\frac{n(n^2-6n+8)}{2n}=\frac{n^2-6n+8}{2}.
\]
The quotient is connected, two-dimensional, and has \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z/2\). Its \(H_2\) embeds in the free integral cellular two-chain group, hence is free. Euler characteristic now gives the asserted rank \(\chi-1=(n^2-6n+6)/2\).

**5. Cup square and category.** When \(n\) is even, the universal coefficient theorem gives a unique order-two summand in \(H^2(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\), the image of the integral Bockstein \(\beta:H^1(Q_n;\mathbb F_2)\to H^2(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\). Since the CW complex has dimension two, reduction from integral to mod-two degree-two cohomology is injective on this torsion summand. The identity \(\rho\beta(u)=\operatorname{Sq}^1(u)=u^2\) proves \(u^2\ne0\). Cup-length gives normalized \(\operatorname{cat}(Q_n)\ge2\), and the cellular dimension bound gives \(\operatorname{cat}(Q_n)\le2\). For odd \(n\), its positive wedge of spheres has category one.

## Verification
An independent finite construction in `verification/verify.py` enumerates the original nonattacking rook simplices, computes all \(D_n\)-orbits without relying on the closed orbit-count formulas, constructs the signed quotient boundary maps using row orientations, checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), and computes exact Smith normal forms. It verifies \(n=5,6,\ldots,12\), with odd cases having trivial first homology and even cases exactly one order-two first-homology summand. Each computed integral second Betti number matches the general expression. Its full recorded stdout is `verification/run_output.txt`. These finite checks support but do not replace the proof for arbitrarily large \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Ziegler's vertex-decomposability theorem establishes the topology of the **unquotiented** chessboard complexes in the stable range. Armstrong establishes the general relation between orbit fundamental groups and isotropy normal subgroups; neither work states the column-dihedral quotient classification or its parity-dependent cohomology products. A published explicit treatment of all column-subgroup quotients of \(\Delta_{3,4}\) concerns the smaller toroidal case where the cover is not simply connected, and cannot imply the displayed \(n\ge5\) formulas. Separate published cyclic-column quotient computations for \(\Delta_{4,5}\) and \(\Delta_{5,5}\) concern different dimensions and groups. The new scope is the combined infinite-family and odd-dihedral-subgroup classification, including the even-width obstruction and exact normalized category; the underlying shellability and orbit-space tools are credited, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
For even \(n\), no wedge or Moore-space decomposition is asserted, and the complete integral homotopy type is undetermined. The subgroup formula for the simply connected wedge decomposition is asserted only for odd \(n\). Other automorphisms permuting rows, other chessboard dimensions, and equivariant homotopy types are outside scope. Publication-priority searches cannot exclude unindexed equivalent work. The cited 1965 orbit-space article was accessible through its publisher extract rather than complete primary text; the published smaller case was read in full.
