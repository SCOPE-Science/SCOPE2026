# Review of dihedral three-row chessboard quotients

## Correctness
PASS. The stable-range shellability theorem makes every \(\Delta_{3,n}\), \(n\ge5\), simply connected. Since the action preserves row labels, a group element that stabilizes any simplex fixes its vertices; the fixed-point classification and the orbit-cell count follow. Armstrong then gives the parity-dependent fundamental group. The Euler calculation, two-dimensional homological Whitehead theorem, and mod-two Bockstein establish exactly the theorem stated in RESULT.md. The standalone finite checker independently reconstructs quotient chains for eight consecutive widths, including both parities. Finite checks are not extrapolated as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS relative to inspected evidence. The Ziegler paper supplies unquotiented shellability, and Armstrong supplies a general orbit-space theorem; neither directly classifies the stated quotient. A highly relevant published published-finding corpus record concerns only the \(3\times4\) torus and was read in full; its smaller case is not an implication for \(n\ge5\). Other searched cyclic chessboard quotients concern other board dimensions or group actions. Searches included equivalent formulations as chessboard, rook-placement, complete-bipartite matching, and deleted-join complexes. Exact previous equivalent work outside the searched sources remains possible; primary Armstrong full text was not accessible.

## Value
PASS. The three-row stable shellability threshold is a canonical point where quotient topology changes from the earlier exceptional torus. The infinite-family classification identifies a sharp odd/even fundamental-group and cup-length obstruction, determines all odd dihedral-subgroup quotient homotopy types and their exact sphere counts, and computes the normalized LS category for the full quotient. This gives precise integral and homotopy information not determined by ordinary Betti counts or the prior finite surface classification.
