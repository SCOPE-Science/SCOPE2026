# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Ziegler Theorem 2.3 gives vertex-decomposable, hence pure shellable 2D Δ_{3,n} for n>=2*3-1=5, implying simply connected wedge of 2-spheres. Every rook face uses a row at most once, so a column dihedral symmetry fixing a simplex setwise fixes each rook vertex; quotient orbit cells have ordinary signed row-ordered simplicial boundaries. Nonidentity rotations have no fixed rooks, odd n reflections fix 1 column and 3 discrete points (χ=3), even n vertex-axis reflections fix 2 columns yielding Δ_{3,2}, a hexagon χ=0, and gap reflections have empty fixed set. Armstrong isotropy-normal-closure then gives π1=1 at odd n and index-two subgroup quotient Z2 at even n; same for odd-n dihedral subgroups. Burnside Euler yields the displayed wedge/homology ranks; for simply connected 2D CW the homological Whitehead argument produces wedge S2. At even n, H2 embeds in free C2, H1=Z2 and UCT gives a nontrivial Bockstein βu; by H³=0 and absence of 4-torsion it reduces injectively, with Sq¹u=u²≠0; cat=2 by cup-length/dimension. Independently enumerated all geometric quotient simplex-orbits for n=5..12 and verified f-vectors, Euler characteristic and expected free second rank. Inspected exact source Smith checker reporting the single Z2 H1 torsion in even cases; infinite proof comes from Armstrong and topology, not the finite checker.

## originality

PASS

The strongest same-object sources are Ziegler shellability for the unquotiented chessboard and Armstrong general orbit π1 theorem. Neither specifies these column-label D_n quotient invariants, parity thresholds or LS cup square. A smaller published complete all-column symmetry classification for Δ_{3,4} concerns a torus, outside the simply connected stable n>=5 range. Closest Resultary three-row cyclic quotient rotates ROWS, not columns; an independently published 3x5 *cyclic column* quotient has a Z5 deck π1, which is genuinely different from adding reflections to form D5 (those isotropy reflections normally generate D5). Other 4-row and 5-row quotients change dimensions and groups. No equivalent all-n column-dihedral theorem surfaced. Accessible full text of Ziegler was inspected; Armstrong original only publisher extract (and cannot be used to rule out a special application), recorded as priority risk.

## value

PASS

The stable range of three-row chessboard complexes is a canonical natural family. The symmetry quotient undergoes a sharp odd/even change from simply connected 2-sphere wedges to π1=Z2 with nonvanishing cohomology cup square and normalized LS category 2. This is meaningful torsion and multiplicative structure invisible to rational Betti counts. The odd-n all-dihedral-subgroup wedge ranks are a nontrivial uniform homotopy classification, not arbitrary small-case computations.
