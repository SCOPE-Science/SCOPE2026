# Dihedral quotients of cycle matching complexes through homological dimension three

## Finding
Let \(C_n\) be the undirected \(n\)-cycle with edges indexed cyclically by \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), and let \(K_n=\mathcal M(C_n)=\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\) be its ordinary matching complex. Thus a nonempty face is a set of edge positions without two cyclically adjacent positions. Let \(D_n\) act by all rotations \(i\mapsto i+k\) and reflections \(i\mapsto k-i\), and let \(Y_n=|K_n|/D_n\) be the **geometric** orbit space.

**Theorem.** For every integer \(3\le n\le 13\),
\[
Y_n\simeq
\begin{cases}
S^3,&n\in\{11,13\},\\
\{\mathrm{point}\},&n\notin\{11,13\}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, \(Y_{11}\) and \(Y_{13}\) are integral homology three-spheres, and every other quotient in this range is contractible.

The endpoint \(13\) is natural: \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\) has no reduced homology above degree three for \(3\le n\le13\), whereas \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_{14})\) already has reduced homology in degree four. No assertion is made for larger \(n\).

## Assumptions and scope
All spaces are ordinary finite geometric realizations and all homology uses integral coefficients. The action on the original simplices may have nontrivial stabilizers. The quotient is **not** defined by simply identifying the vertices of \(K_n\): instead we take the barycentric subdivision and the orbits of chains of nonempty faces, which give the correct quotient regular CW structure. The result concerns all eleven integer values \(3\le n\le13\) and the full dihedral group of order \(2n\), not its cyclic subgroup.

## Proof
**1. Correct quotient CW complex.** A vertex of \(\operatorname{sd}K_n\) is a nonempty independent set \(F\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Its simplices are strictly increasing chains \(F_0\subsetneq F_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_j\). The group \(D_n\) sends each chain to another chain, preserving cardinalities. Consequently any group element stabilizing a chain fixes its vertices individually; there are no inversions. The geometric quotient is therefore a regular simplicial-cell complex with one \(j\)-cell per orbit of these chains. Ordered by increasing face cardinality, its boundary is
\[
\partial [F_0<\cdots<F_j]=
\sum_{t=0}^{j}(-1)^t[F_0<\cdots<\widehat{F_t}<\cdots<F_j],
\]
where brackets on the right mean **orbit classes**. This orientation is well-defined because group elements preserve all face cardinalities. The program `verify_dihedral_cycle.py` enumerates precisely these cells using nonempty bitmasks and takes the lexicographically least dihedral translate as a canonical orbit representative.

**2. Exact elementary collapses.** For each dimension, in descending order, the program repeatedly selects the least remaining codimension-one cell having exactly one remaining coface in that dimension. It deletes the free face and its unique coface. Higher-dimensional cells have already been processed, so each deletion is a genuine elementary collapse, not merely a chain cancellation. The complete initial orbit-cell counts, by ascending dimension, and remaining counts are:

| \(n\) | Initial orbit-cell counts | Counts after collapses |
|---:|---|---|
| 3 | (1) | (1) |
| 4 | (2,1) | (1,0) |
| 5 | (2,1) | (1,0) |
| 6 | (4,4,1) | (1,0,0) |
| 7 | (4,6,3) | (1,0,0) |
| 8 | (7,15,12,3) | (1,0,0,0) |
| 9 | (8,23,28,12) | (1,0,0,0) |
| 10 | (13,51,81,54,12) | (1,0,0,0,0) |
| 11 | (15,84,189,180,60) | (9,35,52,26,0) |
| 12 | (25,173,480,601,330,60) | (1,0,0,0,0,0) |
| 13 | (30,297,1095,1788,1320,360) | (14,64,100,50,0,0) |

For every value except \(11\) and \(13\), these collapses end at a single vertex, proving contractibility without relying on numerical homology computations.

**3. Integral boundaries at the two exceptional sizes.** For \(n=11\), the remaining boundary maps \(\partial_1,\partial_2,\partial_3\) have Smith normal forms with, respectively, \(8,27,25\) nonzero diagonal entries, **all equal to one**. For \(n=13\), their nonzero diagonal counts are \(13,51,49\), again all equal to one. Direct integer multiplication verifies \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) and \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\). The rank formula \(\operatorname{rank}H_j=c_j-\operatorname{rank}\partial_j-\operatorname{rank}\partial_{j+1}\), with the unit Smith factors, proves
\[
H_j(Y_n;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,3,\\
0,&j\ne0,3
\end{cases}
\qquad(n=11,13).
\]
These exact matrices are generated by the embedded verifier; there is no numerical tolerance or finite-field extrapolation.

**4. Fundamental groups and homotopy types.** The established cycle-independence recurrence gives \(K_{11}\simeq S^3\) and \(K_{13}\simeq S^3\), so these two covering spaces are simply connected. Every reflection of an odd cycle fixes a vertex of the cycle, hence fixes the corresponding singleton point of its independence complex. Reflections generate \(D_n\). Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem therefore gives \(\pi_1(Y_{11})=\pi_1(Y_{13})=1\). Since their second integral homology vanishes, the Hurewicz theorem makes them two-connected, and \(\pi_3\cong H_3\cong\mathbb Z\). A representing map \(S^3\longrightarrow Y_n\) is an integral homology equivalence; the simply connected homological Whitehead theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence. This proves the theorem.

## Verification
Run `python verify_dihedral_cycle.py` with the tested dependency `sympy==1.14.0`; compare with `verification_output.txt`. The program recomputes all nonempty independent faces, nested chains, dihedral orbits, signed quotient boundaries, elementary free-face pairings, integral Smith normal forms, and both consecutive boundary-composition identities for every \(3\le n\le13\). As a separate check, the program evaluates the orbit-independence polynomial at \(-1\) and computes Burnside's fixed-point Euler characteristic independently of the chain-orbit enumeration. The resulting values agree with the exact quotient-cell Euler characteristic in every case. The final output line is `EXACT_DIHEDRAL_CYCLE_QUOTIENT_CHECK_PASS_n=3..13`.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's classical cycle-independence homotopy classification, recalled and extended in Adamaszek's *Splittings of Independence Complexes and the Powers of Cycles* (first public preprint June 30, 2011), describes \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\) **before quotienting**. It does not supply the integral boundaries or homotopy types of the full dihedral orbit spaces. Armstrong's 1965 theorem supplies the fundamental-group step but says nothing about the quotient's integral higher homology. Previous integral calculations for **rotation-only** cycle matching quotients do not settle the effect of reflection stabilizers; even rational cyclic invariant dimensions alone do not determine integral dihedral homotopy type. Results about wheel matching complexes, two-independence complexes, and cyclic nonmatching complexes are on different simplicial objects and do not imply these eleven geometric quotient classifications.

## Limitations
The conclusion is finite, exhaustive and restricted to \(3\le n\le13\), with no periodic or all-width conjecture asserted. The exact integral chain calculation for \(n=11,13\) relies on a reproducible computer-assisted finite enumeration; manual reproductions of every Smith reduction are not provided. The proof establishes homotopy equivalence, not a homeomorphism to a particular smooth or PL sphere. Equivalent unpublished or unindexed prior calculations remain a novelty risk, and no independent mathematical validation is claimed.
