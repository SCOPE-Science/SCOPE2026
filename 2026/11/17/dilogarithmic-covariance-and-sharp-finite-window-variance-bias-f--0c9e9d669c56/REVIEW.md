# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is derived from exact inverse-branch integration. The telescoping logarithms leave the finite dilogarithm expression; a positive decreasing integral kernel gives strict covariance monotonicity. Absolute convergence, harmonic-number asymptotics, and stationary covariance pair counting yield the large-lag expansion and exact variance remainder. The computation checks branch integrals independently and finite-window rearrangements, but finite tests do not establish the theorem by themselves. Boundary cases \(m=1\), \(N=1\), and integer slopes at least two are included.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
The closest directly relevant source, Defaveri--Smith (arXiv:2610.21288v1), considers precisely \(-\log x\) for doubling and reports the variance numerically, but does not furnish the present exact finite dilogarithm covariance for all integer slopes or sharp signed finite-length correction. Nicol--Schindler (arXiv:2610.35221v1) proves deviation estimates and pressure regularity for logarithmic singularities, rather than these correlation identities. Searches for equivalent logarithmic covariance, Green--Kubo, polylogarithm, expanding-map and finite-window formulas did not locate dominating statements. Standard summation algebra is not claimed to be original. An unindexed older identity remains a live historical-priority risk.

## Value — PASS
A newly studied singular chaotic observable has a numerically reported variance. The exact all-lag formula converts this to an explicit, controllable Green--Kubo series, reveals a logarithmically enhanced decay rate, and determines the sign and coefficient of the finite-sample bias for every observation length. The integer-slope family gives a nontrivial monotonic interpolation toward independent-observation variance, relevant to numerical fluctuation estimates. This is a natural invariant and a useful precise fact, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Scientific limits
No claims about noninteger maps, other singularity locations, limit distributions or independent proof verification. Unindexed literature may contain an equivalent formula.
