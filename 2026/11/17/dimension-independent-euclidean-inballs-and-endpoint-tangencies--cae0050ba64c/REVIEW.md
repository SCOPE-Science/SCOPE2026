# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The full facet system is reconstructed from the polar inequalities, including irredundancy of the normals \(\mathbf1\) and \(\mathbf1_S-e_j\). Opposite ordered-difference facets force the maximum radius to be at most \(1/\sqrt2\) and all optimal centers to the diagonal. On the diagonal, the \(s=0\) and \(s=2\) facet barriers give the exact nondegenerate interval, and strict monotonicity in \(s\ge2\) settles every other facet. The all-ones facet is strictly inactive. Equality conditions give the three precise tangent-facet counts. The mathematical proof is all-dimensional; 79,635 finite checks only corroborate it.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

Tsuchiya (arXiv:1512.08332, full original text) establishes the twinned chain family and its facet normals; Mori–Mori–Ohsugi (arXiv:2511.22981, full original text) explicitly credits the mixed family's \(1+d2^{d-1}\) facet count. Those combinatorial facts are not claimed new. The inspected sources do not establish the exact Euclidean insphere, full optimal-center segment, or endpoint tangent-facet jumps. Results on enclosing balls of terminal simplices and on other signed chain or cube polytopes address different objects or invariants. An unindexed equivalent statement remains a residual historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural family with exponentially many known facets and rich poset combinatorics, yet its optimal Euclidean inball has a dimension-independent radius and a positive-dimensional center set with a completely classified combinatorial change at each endpoint. The exact \(\sqrt{d/2}\) growth in improvement over the canonical origin-centered ball explains why the standard reflexive center conceals the geometry. The result is a complete, non-routine Euclidean invariant/equality classification rather than a new facet census.

## Limitations

The theorem is for \(d\ge3\) in the fixed standard Euclidean coordinates; it is not invariant under arbitrary affine maps. The published facet input and any known terminal-simplex enclosing-ball results remain credited prior work. Finite verification does not establish the infinite theorem; independent review has not occurred.
