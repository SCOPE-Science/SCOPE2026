# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is local and quantified for every pair of analytic monotone half-potentials with nonzero leading coefficients and integer orders at least two. The energy ovals exist by monotonicity and conservation; the vector field is locally Lipschitz, since the left and right first derivatives tend to zero at the switching point. Original-time crossing and area integrals yield exact quadratures without changing clocks. Inverting the analytic potentials gives a differentiated Puiseux series with a remainder bounded uniformly after scaling energy \(u=hq\). This controls both integrable beta endpoint weights in \(L^1\). The factor \(2/k\) in the first correction comes from differentiating the second Puiseux term; normalizing then centers the \(q^{1/k}\) perturbation. The total-variation leading constant is positive when the analytic linear correction is nonzero. The beta recurrence identifies the universal rare-side time-versus-area ratio after exact stiffness cancellation. The harmonic/quartic example independently checks all normalization factors. The companion checker passed. Main limits: the statement does not extend to unverified merely continuous potentials, negative energies, or large ovals.

## Originality — PASS within inspected published evidence

The closest *same-object* source is Bakhshalizadeh, Liu and Rezende, arXiv:2311.10020 (2023), Section 3 and Theorem B. It establishes non-isochronicity and gives period decompositions and small-energy scaling of piecewise potential centers. The period exponent and the underlying one-dimensional mechanical quadrature are existing knowledge. Its inspected discussion and calculations do not assert conditional probability laws for potential-energy shares under two different fixed-energy samplings; nor the perturbative signed beta-density correction and exact total-variation coefficient; nor the joint coefficient-independent ratio of rare half-plane time and area shares. The new result requires both the boundary time distribution and the interior area measure, their separate normalization and common analytic inversion.

Chen and Dong, arXiv:2401.07332 (2024), analyze nondegenerate piecewise Hamiltonian centers with homogeneous nonlinearities, establishing non-isochronicity rather than the degenerate-side pair of fixed-energy beta laws. Giordano et al., DOI:10.1002/andp.202300294 (2023), derive a Gibbs-ensemble relation between thermally averaged statistical and deterministic frequencies for an anharmonic potential well; this is not the comparison of physical-time and *uniform microcanonical area* fractions of different switching half-planes. Published indexed comparable findings on asymmetrically switching Hamiltonian residence and beta distributions concern different systems or large-energy limits. Comparison with published three-zone Hamiltonian results concerns isolated-cycle Floquet multipliers rather than near-center fixed-energy statistics. An accessible full-text search is not a mathematical guarantee against equivalent unindexed derivations; elementary monomial beta laws are explicitly treated as prerequisites, not as a novelty claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The motivating qualitative gap is that one-sided degeneracy forces asymmetric period growth, but a period alone cannot specify how the same orbits are sampled geometrically. For fixed-energy oscillations the distinction between boundary physical time and interior Euclidean area is canonical, and the two exhibit different beta shapes even within the same half-plane. Their rare-side selection agrees only at zero-order concentration, with a strict, exact universal multiplicative bias that quantitatively identifies the two orders of flatness. The signed first correction gives a testable shape-level signature of the leading anharmonic perturbation; its nonzero total-variation constant makes the rate sharp. These results provide a natural orbit-statistics classification stable under analytic corrections, beyond qualitative non-isochronicity or mere numeric computation. They do not claim a global center classification or a new general beta-integral identity.

## Residual limitations and priority risk

Exact homogeneous-power beta formulas are classical computations. Some older mechanical-oscillator literature may contain equivalent fixed-energy conditional densities or a rearranged form of the universal half-plane ratio; publication priority has not been independently certified. A 2022 paper by Liu and Wang on piecewise polynomial potential centers is discussed in the primary source, but no directly inspected full-text comparison of its appendices was obtained. Its cited theme is isochronicity at infinity rather than the local two-measure law. General action–period differentiation alone does not provide the conditional beta probability distributions or their sharp first density corrections, although it explains why the area exponent is related to the period exponent. The claim is restricted to the stated hypotheses, and no independent audit has occurred.
