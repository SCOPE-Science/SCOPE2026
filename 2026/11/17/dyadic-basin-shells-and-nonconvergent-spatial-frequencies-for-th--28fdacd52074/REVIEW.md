It does not give the exact global equilateral capture strata or infinite-line basin statistics. The closest published exact-three-cycle theorem in published-finding corpus (`2026/10/11/SCOPE-exact-two-orientation-classification-of-primitive-three-cycles-f--2944d54e59d8`) proves that two period-three cycles exist in the equilateral case, which is explicitly acknowledged as prior; its scope excludes all basin questions and all other periods. Another analysis of this map in a right-isosceles triangle classifies a different period-four basin geometry; its coordinates and dynamical implications do not cover equilateral dyadic ray frequencies. Everett's 2021 extended angle-rule paper proves general convergence conditional on contraction but supplies no farthest-rule quantitative basin law. The general literature on piecewise contractions does not determine these explicit orientation frequencies. A differently indexed equivalent remains a publication-priority risk.

## Value — PASS
Everett's map is defined on complete supporting lines, not just its bounded triangle; therefore the large-distance basin selection and time to reach the triangle are natural unresolved quantitative observables. The theorem finishes the full equilateral recurrent-set classification, not merely the known number of three-cycles, and reveals the unusual contrast of certain eventual convergence with **no limiting one-sided spatial basin frequency**. The exact dyadic shell and reversed-geometric capture formulas give reproducible measures of how initial conditions at different distances select the two attractors. The numerical limits \(1/3\) and \(2/3\) are sharp and arise structurally from orientation reversals, not from an arbitrary chosen interval. The result does not generalize to non-equilateral triangles or establish perturbation persistence.
