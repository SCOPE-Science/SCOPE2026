# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof directly evaluates the augmented signed invariant-independent-set trace, including the crucial case in which a power of an automorphism exchanges the endpoints of an edge. On each edge orbit, internal-vertex choices contribute exactly minus one if the original vertices do not select both endpoints, and zero otherwise. Multiplication yields the claimed reduced Lefschetz identity. Csorba's independently established ordinary suspension theorem supplies the homological degree shift; character theory then yields the stated rational module isomorphism under the explicit single-degree condition. A separate verifier reproduces the signed identity in 251 finite actions, including 70 edge-inverting cases. These calculations are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected evidence, with publication-priority risk. Csorba (2009), Sections 3 and 5, establishes nonequivariant suspension of independence complexes when edges are replaced by four-edge paths. The relevant full text was inspected. It does not formulate an automorphism action, edge-sign twist, or full character formula. The closest additional indexed result concerning subdivided multitheta graphs computes characters for a restricted multi-arm family, not an arbitrary finite simple graph. Further broad comparisons with symmetric Kneser-complex homology and graph-subdivision searches did not identify a dominating theorem. A missing/unindexed equivariant treatment remains possible; the absence of a search hit is not itself evidence of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The edge-permutation sign is a structural and non-obvious correction to ordinary suspension: different automorphisms can reverse the homological orientation in ways not determined by the nonequivariant space. The formula settles the entire virtual character for every finite graph, and the actual top-degree rational representation for every base with single-degree homology. For fourfold-subdivided complete graphs it gives a closed symmetric-group module in every order, exhibiting a parity-dependent sign twist; neither the ordinary sphere-wedge count nor an isolated finite example supplies that information.

## Remaining limitations

The result concerns natural lifted automorphisms and rational characters. No equivariant homotopy equivalence, integral module classification, or independent audit is claimed. Publication priority may be affected by uncatalogued or implicit equivariant subdivision arguments.
