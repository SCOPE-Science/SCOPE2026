# Research review: Eight-cycle resonance in degree-two Lipschitz Fourier projection

## Correctness — PASS
The derivative map is an isometric identification of the real Lipschitz space modulo constants with a zero-sum coordinate-max-norm hyperplane. Group averaging forces the Fourier spectral projector to be a minimum-norm projection onto the first two real harmonic pairs. Quotient duality computes the exact operator norm as the median absolute deviation of the sampled five-term Dirichlet kernel. At eight sites the kernel values and a zero-sum sign-gradient give the exact attained value \(1+1/\sqrt2\). The continuum median \(1/2\) has integral absolute deviation \(10\) on one period, giving the limit \(5/\pi\). A bounded-variation derivative argument gives the all-dimensional error \(44\pi/(3n^2)\); an exact rational enclosure program confirms the fifteen remaining dimensions outside eight. The resulting strict global comparison is fully quantified.

The full \(\ell^\infty\) norms of finite Fourier-band projectors apply on a larger space and do not determine the median-quotient Lipschitz norm; the known five-band full-space bound at seven vertices exceeds the new universal eight-vertex constant. Exact three-frequency endpoint results are likewise outside the claimed simultaneous-band norm. Full available public results were compared by their implications, not titles. ## Mathematical value — PASS
The first two harmonics form the smallest genuinely multi-pair Fourier band on a cycle. The problem asks for the optimal projection among **all** real projections onto this natural finite-dimensional subspace, not just the coefficient of one chosen Fourier multiplier. Determining the unique worst cycle, the attained sharp universal norm, and a smaller continuum limit provides a useful finite-size stability distinction that is not a routine restatement of the single-pair formula.

## Limitations

The finite rational checker supplements, but does not replace, the analytic argument in large dimensions. Historical priority is qualified by possible unindexed equivalent statements.
