# Equal cell counts, different integral pentagon-Hom homology for the Shrikhande–rook twins

## Finding
Let \(R=K_4\square K_4\) be the four-by-four rook graph, and let \(S\) be the Shrikhande graph. Both are loopless, connected, strongly regular graphs with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\). For the **ordinary polyhedral graph-Hom complex** with source the unfilled five-cycle \(C_5\), one has
\[
H_1(\operatorname{Hom}(C_5,R);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{90}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2,
\qquad
H_1(\operatorname{Hom}(C_5,S);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{60}.
\]
Both complexes have exactly two connected components, each with 3840 vertices. More strongly, their **entire polyhedral face vectors agree**, with numbers of cells in dimensions zero through six respectively
\[
(7680,30720,45120,36480,20160,6720,960).
\]
In particular, both have Euler characteristic zero, but their first integral homology groups are not isomorphic. This is an exact comparison of two named graphs, not a classification of arbitrary strongly regular graphs or of higher-degree homology.

## Assumptions and scope
Index the sixteen graph vertices by pairs \((a,b)\in(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\), in lexicographic order. In \(R\), distinct pairs are adjacent when they agree in at least one coordinate. In \(S\), pairs are adjacent when their difference lies in \(\{\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1),\pm(1,1)\}\). The graph \(C_5\) has vertices indexed cyclically by \(0,1,2,3,4\).

A cell of \(\operatorname{Hom}(C_5,H)\), for either target \(H\), is a tuple \((A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4)\) of nonempty subsets of \(V(H)\) such that **every** element of \(A_i\) is adjacent to **every** element of \(A_{i+1}\), with indices interpreted cyclically. The cell is the product \(\Delta(A_0)\times\cdots\times\Delta(A_4)\), of dimension \(\sum_i(|A_i|-1)\). This is the standard unquotiented polyhedral graph-Hom construction; in particular it is not the clique complex of either target, the neighborhood complex, the space of graph homomorphisms considered as a discrete set, or any dihedral quotient.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive graph and cell descriptions.** The supplied `verify.py` constructs the adjacency relation directly from the displayed definitions and confirms that each graph has 16 vertices, degree six, and exactly two common neighbors for each distinct vertex pair. It enumerates all singleton graph homomorphisms \(f:C_5\to H\) by enumerating the five cyclic vertex assignments and testing the closing edge. There are exactly 7680 in each case. For a valid cell and a cyclic position \(i\), an additional graph vertex \(v\notin A_i\) can be inserted into \(A_i\) precisely when \(v\) is adjacent to all vertices in \(A_{i-1}\cup A_{i+1}\). Enumerating every such insertion in increasing cell dimension produces precisely the stated face vector.

This enumeration is **complete**: each nonempty valid cell contains a singleton graph homomorphism, obtained by choosing one vertex from each \(A_i\). Its additional members can then be inserted one by one in arbitrary order; every intermediate tuple is still a valid cell because the graph-Hom adjacency condition is hereditary under taking subsets. The algorithm stores sets of canonical five-tuples of sixteen-bit masks and deduplicates exact tuples. Exhaustion terminates after dimension six for both graphs.

**2. Integer cellular boundary.** Orient each simplex \(\Delta(A_i)\) by the increasing vertex order and the product cell by the order of the five factors. If a cell has dimensions \(d_i=|A_i|-1\), deleting the \(k\)-th ordered member of the \(i\)-th factor contributes sign \((-1)^{d_0+\cdots+d_{i-1}+k}\) to the cellular boundary. Thus every two-cell has either three boundary one-cells (one triangle factor) or four (two edge factors). `check_fields.py` independently constructs these signed boundaries and checks the integer chain identity \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) for all 45,120 two-cells of each complex.

**3. Reduction to the integral cycle lattice.** The zero-one skeleton is an ordinary finite graph. A spanning forest on its 7680 vertices and 30,720 edges has two components, each containing 3840 vertices, and leaves exactly
\[
30720-7680+2=23042
\]
non-tree edges. The integral kernel of the cellular incidence boundary \(\partial_1\) is freely generated by the fundamental cycles indexed by those non-tree edges: projection of a cycle to its non-tree edge coefficients is an isomorphism of free abelian groups. Therefore, to determine \(H_1\), it suffices to present the subgroup \(\operatorname{im}\partial_2\) by a \(23042\)-row integer matrix whose columns are the non-tree coefficients of the signed two-cell boundaries.

**4. Exact integer pivots and residual Smith invariants.** Each elimination step selects a matrix entry \(+1\) or \(-1\), uses an integer column operation to zero the other entries in that pivot row, and removes the pivot row and column. Such a unit pivot is an integral unimodular equivalence: it splits off a direct summand with Smith invariant one and leaves the presented abelian group unchanged. No modular-only pivot is used. The script carries out all operations with arbitrary-precision integers; every residual entry is explicitly retained rather than estimated from ranks.

For \(S\), 22,982 unit pivots remove all nonzero rows and columns; the resulting group is free of rank \(23042-22982=60\). For \(R\), 22,950 unit pivots leave two active rows. The gcd of all entries in the residual matrix is **two**, and the gcd of all its \(2\)-by-\(2\) minors is **four**. The residual matrix has rank two over \(\mathbb Q\); its two nontrivial Smith invariants are therefore \(2\) and \(2\). The free rank is \(23042-22950-2=90\). This proves both integral group isomorphisms.

For clarity, the Smith computation is exhaustive even though many columns are eliminated: gcds of entries and maximal minors are the determinantal divisors of the residual rank-two integer presentation, and these determinantal divisors are invariants under unimodular row and column operations.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` from a directory containing the accompanying files. It requires only the Python 3.10+ standard library, takes the graph definitions from the text, constructs all 147,840 polyhedral cells of each graph-Hom complex, forms the complete degree-two integer boundary presentation, and performs the unit-pivot cancellation and rank-two Smith calculation. The resulting machine-readable report is `verification_output.json`.

Run `python check_fields.py` for a second chain-level consistency check. It independently checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) on each two-cell and computes the first Betti dimensions over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_5\). For \(R\) these are \(92,90,90\), and for \(S\) they are \(60,60,60\), agreeing with the stated integral answers and the universal coefficient theorem. Its report is `fields_output.json`. These are exact exhaustive finite computations, not statistical tests.

## Relationship to prior work
The graph-Hom classification theorems of Fujii–Kimura–Nozaki (arXiv:2412.19039, first posted December 26, 2024) and Matsushita (arXiv:2412.19144, first posted December 26, 2024) address **square-free targets**. Both targets here have four-cycles: in fact, any nonadjacent pair has two distinct common neighbors, forming a four-cycle. Thus the classification hypotheses do not hold and neither theorem implies the present integral homology groups.

Cioabă–Guo–Ji–Mim (arXiv:2508.05871, first posted August 7, 2025) compare the clique-complex Laplacians of strongly regular graphs, including these two graphs, but their chain complex has target-graph cliques as cells, not five-cyclic multi-homomorphisms. Its spectral distinction and its graph-cohomology results do not calculate the present complex or its torsion.

The present comparison uses the **same ordinary polyhedral face vector** and goes beyond the common strong-regularity parameters: even complete counts of cells in all dimensions do not recover the first integral homology. A covering result with these exact graph-Hom targets and first integral homology was not identified in the completed literature comparisons; absence from the searched corpus does not prove priority.

## Limitations
The result gives \(H_0\) through connected-component counts and the full \(H_1\) over the integers, but not the higher integral homology groups, integral symmetry representations, simple-homotopy types, or fundamental groups. In particular it does **not** claim that the spaces are wedges of spheres or projective planes. The finite certificate is exhaustive for the stated sixteen-vertex graphs only, and no independent external mathematical audit has been conducted. Unindexed previous calculations of these exact Hom complexes remain a bibliographic priority risk.
