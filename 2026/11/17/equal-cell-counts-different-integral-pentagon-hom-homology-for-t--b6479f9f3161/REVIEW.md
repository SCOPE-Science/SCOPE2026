# Scientific review: first integral pentagon-Hom homology of the strongly regular twins

## Correctness
PASS. Both codomains are constructed from explicit sixteen-vertex coordinate formulas and the complete strong-regularity parameters are verified. The full face enumeration uses a proof of exhaustion from singleton homomorphisms and all legal one-vertex insertions. Product-simplex cellular orientations give the integral chain complex. A spanning forest identifies its degree-one cycle lattice with 23,042 integer coordinates. Every unit-pivot cancellation is a unimodular equivalence; the remaining two-row rook presentation has entry gcd 2 and maximal-minor gcd 4, while the Shrikhande presentation disappears after 22,982 unit pivots. A second checker verifies the signed chain identity and field-rank consequences.

## Originality
PASS, with residual bibliographic uncertainty. The 2024 graph-Hom classification theorems require square-free target graphs and cannot cover the two four-cycle-containing targets. The 2025 strongly regular graph paper compares clique-complex Laplacians rather than the cyclic graph-Hom chain complex. Searches of exact graph names, five-cycle Hom notation, torsion, and graph-Hom homology, together with comparisons to related topological results, identified no equivalent or dominating integral computation. Relevant earlier work on clique, neighborhood, dominance, and cut complexes of the same graph pair uses distinct complexes and does not imply the displayed groups. Unindexed or otherwise inaccessible direct computations remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. These are the canonical nonisomorphic strongly regular twins of parameters \((16,6,2,2)\). The precise choice of source graph is the foundational odd test graph \(C_5\), and the classification detects a torsion phenomenon in the natural topological graph-Hom functor. The pair has exactly the same complete cell vector, so Euler characteristics and even all face counts cannot detect the difference. The two order-two summands and the substantial free-rank separation are integral topological information of clear use in comparing non-square-free targets, rather than arbitrary numerology.
