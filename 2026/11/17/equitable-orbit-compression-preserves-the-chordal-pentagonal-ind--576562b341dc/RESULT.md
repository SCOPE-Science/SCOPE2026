# Equitable orbit compression preserves chordal–pentagonal independence-complex topology

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite simple graph with **no induced cycle of length \(4\) or length at least \(6\)**; thus its possible induced cycles have length \(3\) or \(5\). Let \(\Pi\) be an **equitable** vertex partition into independent sets: for every two blocks \(A,B\in\Pi\), the number of neighbors of a vertex of \(A\) in \(B\) is constant as that vertex varies in \(A\). Construct its simple quotient \(Q_\Pi\) with one vertex per block and an edge when two blocks have adjacent representatives.

**Structural theorem.** The quotient \(Q_\Pi\) also has no induced cycle of length \(4\) or at least \(6\).

For any finite subgroup \(H\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), let \(\mathscr O_H\) be the set of \(H\)-orbits on \(V\) that induce no edge of \(G\). Define \(Q_H(G)\) with vertex set \(\mathscr O_H\), joining two surviving orbits if there is an edge between them. Then \(Q_H(G)\) satisfies the same forbidden-cycle condition, and its independence complex is an exact simplicial model for the *actual geometric fixed points*:
\[
|\operatorname{Ind}(G)|^H \cong_{\mathrm{PL}} |\operatorname{Ind}(Q_H(G))|.
\]
Consequently the fixed-space model is **vertex-decomposable and nonpure shellable** by the established theorem for graphs whose induced cycles are only triangles and pentagons. Its realization is empty, contractible, or a finite wedge of spheres (spheres may occur in different dimensions); in particular all integral homology is torsion-free. These conclusions hold simultaneously for every subgroup, without restrictions on its order or action.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, loopless, undirected, and simple. An induced cycle has distinct vertices and no chords. The empty graph is permitted; the realization of the simplicial complex with only the empty face is taken to be empty. A block of an equitable partition must itself be independent in the structural statement. For group fixed points, non-independent vertex orbits are *discarded before* taking the quotient. The full action on the independence complex comes from permuting vertices. The word \(\mathrm{PL}\) refers to the natural orbit-barycenter triangulation of the fixed point set, not the unsubdivided ambient triangulation.

## Proof
**1. Equitable-partition preservation.** Suppose the quotient of an independent equitable partition has an induced cycle with \(\ell\ge4\) vertices, corresponding in order to blocks \(B_0,\ldots,B_{\ell-1}\). Let \(T\) be the subgraph induced by the union of these blocks. Because the blocks are independent and the quotient cycle has no chords, the map taking a vertex to its block index is a graph homomorphism \(T\to C_\ell\). Equitability and the existence of quotient edges imply that every vertex of each \(B_i\) has at least one neighbor in \(B_{i-1}\) and at least one in \(B_{i+1}\). These two neighbor sets lie in distinct blocks, so \(T\) has minimum degree at least two. Every finite graph with minimum degree at least two contains a cycle; a shortest cycle is induced.

If \(\ell=4\), then \(T\) is bipartite through the map to \(C_4\), so its shortest cycle is an induced even cycle of length at least four, forbidden by hypothesis. If \(\ell\ge6\), then the graph homomorphism to \(C_\ell\) rules out triangles and pentagons in \(T\): neither \(C_3\) nor \(C_5\) admits a graph homomorphism to \(C_\ell\) for \(\ell\ge6\). Thus a shortest cycle of \(T\) is induced and has length four or at least six, again forbidden. Hence no such quotient cycle exists. The case \(\ell=5\) is deliberately permitted.

**2. Apply the lemma to group orbits.** Vertex orbits of an automorphism subgroup form an equitable partition. Remove all orbits containing an internal edge, and take the induced graph on the union of the remaining orbits. The remaining orbit partition is still equitable because the action remains transitive on each individual orbit and preserves the induced graph. Every surviving orbit is independent, so step 1 applies and shows that \(Q_H(G)\) belongs to the same forbidden-cycle class.

**3. Geometric fixed points and barycentric coordinates.** A point of \(|\operatorname{Ind}(G)|\) has unique nonnegative barycentric coordinates \((t_v)_{v\in V}\), summing to one, with independent support. It is fixed by all \(H\) exactly if \(t_{hv}=t_v\) for all \(h\in H\) and \(v\in V\). Every orbit meeting its support must therefore lie entirely in its support, hence be independent; such an orbit is a vertex of \(Q_H(G)\). Two such orbits may receive positive total mass simultaneously exactly when they are nonadjacent in \(Q_H(G)\). Assigning to an orbit \(O\) of total mass \(s_O\) the common coordinate \(t_v=s_O/|O|\) for \(v\in O\) gives an affine bijection on each simplex, with affine inverse \(s_O=\sum_{v\in O}t_v\). These glue to a PL homeomorphism of the stated realizations.

**4. Topology.** Woodroofe's vertex-decomposability theorem applies to any graph having no chordless cycles other than triangles or pentagons. Apply it to \(Q_H(G)\), not merely to \(G\). A vertex-decomposable complex is nonpure shellable; the usual nonpure shelling attachments yield a wedge of spheres, possibly of several dimensions, unless the complex is contractible or has empty realization. Such a wedge has torsion-free integral homology. These are homotopy consequences; no assertion of PL sphere or representation-sphere structure is made.

**Boundary illustrations.** For the pentagon \(C_5\), the reflection \(i\mapsto-i\) has surviving orbits \(\{0\}\) and \(\{1,4\}\), while \(\{2,3\}\) is discarded. The conflict graph is \(K_2\), giving exactly \(S^0\) as the reflection-fixed realization. Both the pentagon and nontrivial subgroup action lie outside the forest-only case. Conversely, mixed sphere dimensions cannot be excluded: take \(F=K_2*(K_2\sqcup K_2)\), a chordal graph, and let the involution swap the two copies of \(F\sqcup F\). Its fixed complex is \(\operatorname{Ind}(F)=S^0\sqcup S^1\), a wedge model involving degrees zero and one.

## Verification
The included standard-library program `verify.py` exhaustively enumerates all eligible labeled simple graphs through five vertices. For each, it tests every independent equitable partition, and all cyclic automorphism subgroups obtained from every graph automorphism. It also tests four genuinely noncyclic generated actions. It constructs all invariant independent vertex sets and checks exact agreement with the orbit-quotient face model. It verifies absence of forbidden induced cycles in all quotient graphs.

Recorded results: **906** eligible graphs, **1,695** independent equitable partitions, **3,633** group-action cases (including **2,727** nontrivial cases), and **24,273** fixed/model face comparisons. All checks passed. Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3; no external library or network is needed. The general theorem is proved above; finite tests are not an infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Van Tuyl and Villarreal (first public preprint January 10, 2007) established nonpure shellability of independence complexes of chordal graphs. Woodroofe (first public preprint October 1, 2008) proved the larger vertex-decomposability theorem allowing induced pentagons, which is the exact topological input used here. Dochtermann and Engström (2008) independently treat vertex decomposability of chordal graph independence complexes. These papers treat the original graph, rather than proving preservation of the pentagon-allowed forbidden-cycle class under equitable orbit compression or identifying every geometric subgroup-fixed independence complex with one in that class. Previously known forest fixed-space statements are included as a strictly smaller subfamily; they do not establish this closure result for graphs containing triangles and pentagons. Searches did not locate a prior formulation equivalent to the equitable-quotient theorem; absence from those searches does not establish publication priority.

## Limitations
The graph must forbid **both** induced quadrilaterals and induced cycles of length at least six. The statement does not assert that every quotient of every vertex-decomposable graph is vertex-decomposable, nor that arbitrary set partitions preserve the forbidden-cycle class; equitability and independent blocks are essential in the proof. The theorem classifies the *allowed homotopy shapes*, not exact sphere multiplicities or dimensions for every input; these remain computable from the explicit quotient but depend on the graph and action. Equivalence to undiscovered earlier formulations is a residual literature risk. Independent mathematical auditing has not been performed.
