# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The structural lemma is proved by lifting an induced forbidden cycle in an independent equitable quotient to an induced subgraph of minimum degree two. The lifted graph maps to the quotient cycle. For quotient length four it is bipartite, and for quotient length at least six it has no triangle or pentagon. A shortest lifted cycle is therefore forbidden. The proof separately verifies that surviving independent automorphism orbits remain an equitable partition, and constructs mutually inverse affine barycentric-coordinate maps for the geometric fixed points. Woodroofe's existing theorem then supplies vertex-decomposability; nonpure shelling supplies the asserted wedge shape. The empty-orbit, disconnected-graph, and pentagon cases are included. The finite checker covers 906 labeled eligible graphs, 1,695 equitable partitions, and 3,633 automorphism actions, and compares 24,273 fixed/model faces. The checks do not replace the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to examined literature
The closest primary results are Van Tuyl–Villarreal (2007) for chordal shellability, Woodroofe (2008) for the full no-induced-4-or-6-plus class, and Dochtermann–Engström (2008) for chordal vertex decomposability. Their inspected results concern nonequivariant graph complexes and do not state the equitable-quotient closure theorem or the all-subgroup PL fixed-point consequence. The closely related existing forest fixed-space theorem concerns a proper subclass; it gives no implication for graphs containing pentagons. Broad published-result searches for equivariant chordality, equitable quotient closure and fixed independence complexes did not locate an equivalent statement. This is evidence of distinction, not proof of universal novelty. Residual risk: the independent-equitable quotient lemma may appear in unindexed algebraic graph theory or be treated as folklore.

## Value — PASS
The claim closes a substantial classical graph class under the exact quotient needed for **every** subgroup's geometric fixed points. It converts a nonequivariant vertex-decomposability theorem into a simultaneous equivariant shape restriction, proving torsion-freeness and sphere-wedge topology without group-order constraints. It strictly includes chordal graphs and pentagon-containing graphs, with a reflection-fixed pentagon example and a mixed-dimensional boundary example. The exact orbit model permits further computation of homology in applications; it is not just a rephrasing of the known nonequivariant result.

## Scope and limitations
No assertion of one sphere, purity, equivariant sphere, universal dimensions, or automatic preservation for arbitrary vertex partitions is made. Publication priority remains provisional because not every historical source can be ruled out. No independent audit was performed.
