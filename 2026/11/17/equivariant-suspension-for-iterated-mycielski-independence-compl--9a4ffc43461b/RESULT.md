# Equivariant suspension for iterated Mycielski independence complexes

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any nonempty finite simple loopless graph, and let \(A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) be any subgroup. Denote by \(\mu(G)\) its **ordinary** Mycielskian (one shadow layer and one apex), and by \(\mu^{r}(G)\) its \(r\)-fold iteration, for every integer \(r\geq1\). The action of \(A\) is lifted at each iteration by permuting original and shadow vertices in parallel and fixing the new apex. Then there is an \(A\)-equivariant homotopy equivalence
\[
\left|\operatorname{Ind}(\mu^{r}(G))\right|\simeq_A
\Sigma^{r}\left|\operatorname{Ind}(G)\right|.
\]
Here \(\Sigma\) is the **unreduced suspension**, with both suspension poles fixed by \(A\), and \(\Sigma\varnothing=S^0\) (two points). Thus, simultaneously for **every** subgroup \(H\leq A\),
\[
\left|\operatorname{Ind}(\mu^{r}(G))\right|^H\simeq
\Sigma^{r}\left(\left|\operatorname{Ind}(G)\right|^H\right),
\qquad
\left|\operatorname{Ind}(\mu^{r}(G))\right|/H\simeq
\Sigma^{r}\left(\left|\operatorname{Ind}(G)\right|/H\right).
\]
There is a completely combinatorial input for these formulas: form the **orbit-conflict graph** \(Q_H(G)\) whose vertices are the \(H\)-orbits of vertices of \(G\) that are themselves independent sets, and join two distinct orbits if an edge of \(G\) connects them. Then
\[
\left|\operatorname{Ind}(G)\right|^H\cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\left|\operatorname{Ind}(Q_H(G))\right|,
\]
so the suspension formula computes every geometric subgroup-fixed homotopy type from an ordinary finite graph. When \(Q_H(G)\) has no vertices, its independence-complex realization is defined to be empty.

As a concrete infinite chromatic-growth example, let \(G=C_5\) and \(A=D_5\). For every \(r\geq1\), the ambient independence complex is a sphere \(S^{r+1}\); the fixed space of the fivefold rotation is \(S^{r-1}\), each reflection fixes \(S^r\), and the full dihedral group fixes \(S^{r-1}\), all up to homotopy. The full dihedral orbit-space is contractible. The base graph has increasing chromatic number under iteration, while its natural subgroup-fixed spaces follow these distinct, exact sphere dimensions.

## Assumptions and scope
For a graph \(G=(V,E)\), the vertex set of \(\mu(G)\) is \(V\sqcup V'\sqcup\{w\}\). Its edges are: every original edge \(uv\); the two cross edges \(uv'\), \(vu'\) for every original edge \(uv\); and every \(wv'\). There are **no** edges among shadow vertices or between an original vertex and its own shadow. For an automorphism \(g\) of \(G\), the induced automorphism of \(\mu(G)\) sends \(v\mapsto gv\), \(v'\mapsto(gv)'\), and \(w\mapsto w\); the lift is iterated.

The independence complex \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) has faces the independent subsets of \(V\). Its realization uses normalized nonnegative barycentric coordinates. A fixed point of the *realization* may have positive coordinates on an entire vertex orbit; the fixed set is not necessarily the complex on pointwise fixed vertices. All graphs are finite, simple, undirected and nonempty. The theorem concerns the **natural lifted action**, not the full automorphism group of each iterated graph. The empty-realization convention \(\left|\{\varnothing\}\right|=\varnothing\) is used for orbit graphs with no vertices. For nonempty spaces the usual unreduced suspension agrees with the double-cone construction; by definition the double cone on the empty space is two isolated points.

## Proof
**1. A canonical equivariant contraction of the deleted-apex complex.** Put \(X=\operatorname{Ind}(\mu(G))\). Let \(B\subset X\) be the old-vertex copy of \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\), and let \(Y\subset X\) be the subcomplex of independent sets that do not contain the apex \(w\). Let \(S\subset Y\) be the full simplex on the shadow vertices. Define a simplicial map \(p:Y\to S\) by \(p(v)=v'\) and \(p(v')=v'\). If \(F\) is any independent face of \(Y\), then \(F\cup p(F)\) is independent: the shadows are mutually nonadjacent; an old vertex \(u\) and a shadow \(v'\) are adjacent exactly when \(uv\in E(G)\); this adjacency is already forbidden whenever the old labels \(u,v\) occur in \(F\). Thus the identity on \(Y\) is simplicially **contiguous** to the inclusion followed by \(p\). Linear interpolation on each simplex \(F\cup p(F)\) gives a strong deformation retraction \(Y\searrow S\), natural under the complete automorphism group \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Contract \(S\) linearly to its equally weighted barycenter, fixed by that group. Therefore \(Y\) is \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-contractible, not just ordinarily contractible.

**2. Equivariant suspension from the apex cofiber.** The faces of \(X\) containing \(w\) have only old vertices besides \(w\), so \(X=Y\cup(w*B)\) and \(Y\cap(w*B)=B\), both as equivariant simplicial subcomplexes. Inclusions of finite simplicial subcomplexes are equivariant cofibrations. This pushout computes the equivariant homotopy pushout of \(Y\leftarrow B\to(w*B)\). Replacing the \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-contractible \(Y\) with a fixed point is valid in the equivariant homotopy category, because the contraction in Step 1 is explicitly equivariant. The resulting double-cone homotopy pushout is \(\Sigma B\) with trivial action on the suspension coordinate. It follows that \(X\simeq_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}\Sigma B\). The argument also handles any nonempty edgeless input graph and any empty **subgroup-fixed** base realization.

**3. Iteration, all subgroup fixed spaces and all quotients.** Apply Step 2 to \(G,\mu(G),\ldots,\mu^{r-1}(G)\), restricting in each instance the full automorphism-equivariant equivalence to the naturally lifted original group \(A\). Composition gives the equivariant \(r\)-fold suspension. An equivariant homotopy equivalence restricts to an ordinary homotopy equivalence on \(H\)-fixed spaces for every subgroup and descends to an ordinary equivalence on the orbit spaces. Since \(H\) fixes the two suspension poles and acts only on the base, fixed points and quotients commute with this double-cone construction. This proves both displayed simultaneous formulas. In particular, for integral coefficients, the reduced homology of each nonempty base space shifts by \(r\) with its integral \(A\)-module structure unchanged; no rationalization is required.

**4. Exact finite orbit model.** An \(H\)-fixed barycentric point of \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) has coordinates constant on each \(H\)-orbit of graph vertices. Every orbit carrying positive mass must be an independent set; distinct positive orbits must have no connecting graph edges. Each such orbit contributes one normalized orbit mass. Sending each independent orbit to its uniform barycenter gives a simplexwise affine, globally compatible PL homeomorphism from \(\operatorname{Ind}(Q_H(G))\) to the fixed realization, with inverse recovering orbit masses. This is a genuine geometric-fixed-space identification, not an assertion about only pointwise-fixed original vertices.

**5. The five-cycle example.** \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_5)\) is itself a pentagon, hence an \(S^1\), with the natural dihedral action. The nontrivial rotation subgroup and the whole \(D_5\) have no fixed points on that pentagon. A reflection fixes exactly two points on it. The three asserted fixed sphere formulas follow from Step 3 and \(\Sigma^r\varnothing=S^{r-1}\), \(\Sigma^r S^0=S^r\) for \(r\ge1\). The quotient of the pentagon by \(D_5\) is a closed interval, whose iterated unreduced suspension is contractible. The ordinary ambient sphere \(S^{r+1}\) follows by suspending the pentagon without taking fixed points.

## Verification
The standalone Python program `verify.py` generates all simple graphs on up to four labeled vertices and all their graph-automorphism permutations, testing **293 automorphism/graph instances**. It constructs the ordinary Mycielskian independently, verifies on all independent faces that adjoining the corresponding shadows remains independent (the critical contiguity step), constructs the exact orbit-conflict graphs, and checks the required shifted reduced mod-two homology ranks. It additionally tests twelve second-iteration automorphism cases for the two-vertex complete graph and five-cycle and separately tests the full noncyclic dihedral action on the five-cycle through two iterations. Execute `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 standard library only. The recorded output is in `verification_output.txt`; the completion marker is `MYCIELSKI_FIXED_SUSPENSION_TESTS_PASS`.

Finite mod-two computations do **not** establish the infinite theorem, integral statements, or equivariant equivalence. These are consequences of the explicit contraction and equivariant homotopy-pushout proof, independent of the finite experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
The ordinary equivalence \(\operatorname{Ind}(\mu(G))\simeq\Sigma\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) was proved by Goyal, Shukla and Singh (2019, Theorem 4.1), and was recovered and iterated nonequivariantly by Carnero Bravo (2026, Theorem 5 and Corollary 6). **Neither nonequivariant formula is claimed as new here.** Barmak's (2010) star-cluster suspension machinery is an antecedent of the ordinary proof, while Csorba's (2008) Mycielski results concern Hom complexes rather than these independence-complex fixed spaces.

The public *Subgroup-fixed homotopy of complete-graph generalized Mycielskians* (2026) gives explicit fixed spaces for every label-permutation subgroup when the base is a **complete graph**, including higher generalized Mycielski levels; its ordinary-level special cases overlap, and are not claimed anew. *Reflection-axis dichotomy for generalized Mycielskians of the six-cycle* (2026) treats two specific reflection actions on a particular base cycle, also overlapping in its ordinary-level specialization. Neither result supplies the all-graph, all-iteration **global equivariant homotopy equivalence**, its uniform all-subgroup orbit-conflict reduction, or the all-quotient conclusion. The essential distinction is fixed-point and equivariant homotopy data, which do not follow from knowing the ordinary homotopy type. Publication priority is subject to further independent literature review.

## Limitations
This theorem applies to **ordinary**, iterated one-step Mycielski constructions, not every higher-level generalized Mycielskian, and to automorphisms lifted from the original graph, not arbitrary additional automorphisms of iterates. It is an equivariant homotopy equivalence, not a PL homeomorphism of the entire Mycielski independence complex to the suspension. The PL claim is only for the exact *base* subgroup orbit model. The theorem reduces arbitrary fixed-space calculations to the base graph's orbit-conflict graph; it does not claim all such base complexes are sphere wedges. The five-cycle conclusion is a worked infinite family, not an exhaustive classification of all Mycielski base graphs. Independent validation has not been performed, and a stronger equivalent statement in unindexed literature cannot be ruled out.
