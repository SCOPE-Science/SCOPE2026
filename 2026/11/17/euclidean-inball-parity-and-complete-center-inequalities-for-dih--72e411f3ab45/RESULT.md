# Euclidean inball parity and complete center inequalities for dihedral permutohedra

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge3\), let the **dihedral permutohedron** be the convex hull in \(\mathbb R^n\) of the \(2n\) permutations of \((1,2,\ldots,n)\) generated by cyclic rotation and reversal. Its affine span is the hyperplane with coordinate sum \(n(n+1)/2\), and all metric assertions below use the **ambient Euclidean metric restricted to that hyperplane**, not a lattice-coordinate metric. Its maximum inscribed Euclidean ball has radius
\[
 r_n=\begin{cases}\sqrt n/2,& n\text{ even},\\ n/\sqrt{4n-6},& n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
For odd \(n\), the **only** optimal center is \(m_n=((n+1)/2)\mathbf1\). For even \(n\), the set of optimal centers has affine dimension **exactly \(n-2\)**, and its affine hull is
\[
 m_n+\left\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_j=0,\ \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^j x_j=0\right\}.
\]
The centered optimal ball touches precisely \(2\) facets when \(n\) is even and precisely \(2n\) facets when \(n\) is odd.

There is also an **explicit finite inequality characterization of all optimal centers**. Put \(Y_n=\{y\in[-1,1]^n:\sum_j y_j=0,\ \text{at most one coordinate has }|y_j|<1\}\), indexed cyclically, and \(E(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(y_{j+1}-y_j)^2\), with \(y_n=y_0\). In this definition, \(Y_n\) is finite: when \(n\) is even it consists of balanced \(\pm1\) vectors, and when \(n\) is odd it consists of balanced \(\pm1\) vectors on all but one coordinate, which equals zero. Every optimal center, and no other point, is \(m_n+x\), where \(\sum_jx_j=0\) and
\[
 \left|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} x_j(y_{j+1}-y_j)\right|+r_n\sqrt{E(y)}\le n\qquad\text{for every }y\in Y_n.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Fix \(n\ge3\). Let \(H=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_jx_j=0\}\), \(s=(1,2,\ldots,n)-((n+1)/2)\mathbf1\), and let \(v_j\) be its cyclic shifts for \(j=0,\ldots,n-1\). Reversal of the increasing coordinate sequence is its negative after centering, so the centered polytope is exactly \(P_n=Q_n-m_n=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm v_j:0\le j<n\}\). All inballs are required to lie in the intrinsic affine span \(m_n+H\). A center may lie away from \(m_n\); the theorem optimizes over **all** such centers.

## Proof
**1. Polar model and Euclidean metric.** The vectors \(v_j\) span \(H\): their cyclic differences are \(v_{j+1}-v_j= n e_j-\mathbf1\) for cyclic index \(j\); these span \(H\). Also \(\sum_jv_j=0\). For any \(u\in H\), set \(y_j=\langle u,v_j\rangle\). Then \(\sum_jy_j=0\) and
\[
 y_{j+1}-y_j=n u_j,\qquad \|u\|_2^2=\frac{E(y)}{n^2}.
\]
Hence \(T:u\mapsto y\) is an invertible linear map \(H\to H\), with inverse \(u_j=(y_{j+1}-y_j)/n\). The polar of \(P_n\) is \(\{u\in H:|\langle u,v_j\rangle|\le1\ \forall j\}\); under \(T\), it is exactly the central cube section \(C_n=[-1,1]^n\cap H\). Its vertices are precisely \(Y_n\). They give **all** polar vertices and therefore all supporting facets of \(P_n\), each with equation \(\langle u_y,z\rangle=1\), where \(u_y=n^{-1}(y_{j+1}-y_j)_j\). The distance from the origin to this facet is
\[
 \frac1{\|u_y\|_2}=\frac n{\sqrt{E(y)}}.
\]

**2. Sharp energy maximization.** On the even-dimensional cube section (\(n\) even), each vertex \(y\in Y_n\) is a balanced \(\pm1\) string. Every squared adjacent difference is at most \(4\), so \(E(y)\le4n\); equality occurs exactly for the two alternating sign strings. When \(n\) is odd, each vertex has one zero and all remaining entries are balanced \(\pm1\). The two edges adjacent to that zero contribute exactly \(1\) each, and the other \(n-2\) edges contribute at most \(4\); therefore \(E(y)\le4n-6\). Equality holds if and only if the remaining nonzero entries alternate along the path, giving precisely \(2n\) maximizers (choose the location of the zero and the first sign). These are exhaustive, including \(n=3\). Thus the centered ball has the claimed radius and contact counts. Since \(P_n\) is centrally symmetric, for any ball of radius \(r\) centered at \(x\) also the reflected ball centered at \(-x\) is contained; their Minkowski midpoint is the radius-\(r\) ball centered at the origin. Consequently the centered ball achieves the **global**, unrestricted optimum.

**3. Complete locus of optimal centers.** A ball \(B(x,r_n)\) lies inside \(P_n\) if and only if, for each facet normal \(u_y\),
\(\langle u_y,x\rangle+r_n\|u_y\|\le1\). The antipodal facet is also among the inequalities. Substituting the explicit \(u_y\) gives exactly the claimed finite inequalities with the absolute value. This proves that the displayed system is both necessary and sufficient.

For every facet attaining the minimal distance, its antipodal facet is also tight at the centered ball. Thus any other optimal center must be orthogonal to **every** active facet normal. Conversely, because the facet set is finite and all inactive facets have positive strict slack at radius \(r_n\), sufficiently small perturbations of the origin in the common orthogonal complement of the active normals remain valid centers. Therefore the affine dimension of the incenter locus equals \((n-1)\) minus the rank of the active normals.

For even \(n\), the two extremal alternating sign patterns yield one antipodal pair of normals proportional to \(((-1)^j)_j\), hence rank one and affine dimension \(n-2\). For odd \(n\), the \(2n\) maximizing sign patterns are the cyclic shifts and negatives of \(z=(0,1,-1,1,-1,\ldots,1,-1)\). Their cyclic shifts span \(H\): for a nontrivial \(n\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\),
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}z_j\zeta^j=\frac{\zeta-1}{\zeta+1}\ne0,
\]
where the denominator is nonzero because \(n\) is odd. The nonzero Fourier coefficients imply the cyclic shifts span every nonconstant Fourier mode. Since \(T\) is invertible, the associated active facet normals also span \(H\). Thus their common orthogonal complement in \(H\) is zero and \(m_n\) is the unique optimal center.

## Verification
A standalone program `verify.py` independently constructs the \(2n\) original Euclidean vertices and their convex hulls for \(3\le n\le8\), recovers the distinct facet planes from numerical hulls, checks their Euclidean distances, counts the active facet planes and tests active-normal ranks. It also checks all \(4850\) vertices of the central cube sections for \(3\le n\le12\) by exact integer enumeration, and tests explicit noncentral optimal centers for even \(n\). Its completed output is `PASS: 4850 exact cube-section vertices and 305 independent Euclidean/facet checks`. These calculations are finite corroboration; the proof above handles all integers \(n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
C. Olairez, *Triangulation, Volume, and Symmetries of a Dihedral Group Analogue of the Permutohedron*, arXiv:2610.28672, first posted 2026-10-18, establishes the orbit polytope, its central symmetry, its facet combinatorics (Corollaries 4.11 and 4.13), triangulations, normalized lattice volume, and affine symmetry properties. The present Euclidean inradius and metric incenter classification use the **ambient** Euclidean norm and do not follow from the paper's normalized lattice-volume formula; no claim of novelty is made for the facet or orbit description. The parity in Euclidean facet distances comes from a new cyclic-difference-energy optimization, not from the known facet count alone.

A similarly named *Coxeter permutahedron* for the dihedral reflection group is a rank-two orbit polytope, whereas the object here has intrinsic dimension \(n-1\); standard rank-two insphere results do not cover this family. Previously recorded Euclidean inradius formulas for the symmetric edge polytope of a cycle concern a different canonical Euclidean embedding: the vector configurations are linearly related but not generally Euclidean-isometric, so those metric formulas do not imply this result.

## Limitations
This is a self-review, not an independent audit. The finite computations are not a substitute for the proof. The theorem is specific to the standard vector \((1,\ldots,n)\) and its ambient Euclidean metric; arbitrary dihedral orbit basepoints or alternative Euclidean metrics are not treated. The full incenter set has an explicit finite inequality description but no minimal irredundant face lattice is claimed. Historical priority is provisional: inaccessible or unindexed literature could contain an equivalent Euclidean specialization.
