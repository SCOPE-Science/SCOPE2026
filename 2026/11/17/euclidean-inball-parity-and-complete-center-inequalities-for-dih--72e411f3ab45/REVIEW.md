# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The ambient-Euclidean metric is fixed explicitly. The polar is linearly equivalent to the zero-sum cube section, and the exact inverse map differentiates the section coordinates cyclically. The finite vertex classification, sharp energy inequality, equality cases, contact-facet count, and incenter-rank argument are proved for every integer \(n\ge3\). The Fourier step uses only nontrivial odd-order roots of unity, so its denominator cannot vanish. The finite embedded verifier independently checks the vertex configurations, facet planes, and normal ranks in small dimensions; its finite scope is disclosed.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
Olairez (arXiv:2610.28672, 2026-10-18) defines the same orbit polytope and determines central symmetry and all facets, but investigates triangulations, normalized volume, and affine automorphisms rather than ambient-Euclidean inballs and complete optimal-center loci. Its facet description is prior work and is credited. Searches of mathematical records under dihedral permutohedron, inscribed Euclidean ball, orbit polytope, cube section, and incenter parity did not reveal the same metric statement. The cycle symmetric-edge polytope and dihedral Coxeter permutahedron are geometrically different embeddings or representations; matching parity language is not an implication. An unindexed stronger or equivalent general result remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete inradius and center classification concerns a newly studied, natural infinite family with a sharp and unusual parity transition: a unique incenter for odd order versus an affine family of dimension \(n-2\) for even order. The number of nearest facets jumps from \(2\) to \(2n\). The transparent cyclic-difference optimization produces a finite exact center system and separates Euclidean behavior from lattice-volume combinatorics; it is not a generic dimension-substitution exercise.
