# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The fixed-edge statement has a closed proof: the three possible pair-order sign correlations are computed from all six relative orders, zero total edge vector kills exactly the mixed triple term, and Cauchy–Binet converts the determinant sum into an invariant covariance determinant. The equality cases follow from a two-eigenvalue inequality. For the Haar–Stiefel specialization, the first-row squared norm follows a beta law, and the remaining sphere conditional moments yield the stated rational expression. The one-step variance ratio establishes the four-edge maximum without numerical extrapolation. Exact rational enumeration for 18 deterministic configurations and independent Haar sampling for five sizes corroborate the formulas. Scope: all integers \(n\ge3\), closed possibly self-intersecting planar polygons, signed area only.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical uncertainty

The principal antecedent, Cantarella–Deguchi–Shonkwiler (arXiv:1206.3161), gives the same Haar measure and averages **chord-squared** quantities over edge permutations, rather than area-squared. The inspected full PDF (especially Section 6) does not furnish the area-variance identity. Cantarella–Needham–Shonkwiler–Stewart (arXiv:1702.01027), inspected in full HTML, provides the sphere representation that already implies the \(n=3\) value, and studies quadrilateral convexity types, but not the general second moment. Bárány–Steiger (doi:10.1016/j.comgeo.2012.01.003) concerns convex hulls of independently sampled points. Searches for equivalent signed-area formulas and broader random-polygon variance results did not show an implication covering the fixed-edge theorem or the all-size Haar result. In particular, the triangle instance is not claimed as new. The exact same statement could have an earlier formulation in polymer or geometric-probability literature not recovered by these searches.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The random-edge-order variance becomes a simple anisotropy invariant of the unordered edge list, with sharp isotropic versus collinear equality classes. The result is not a mere substitution into a known chord-length theorem: orientation products require independent sign-correlation analysis. The Haar–Stiefel evaluation gives a closed finite-size law across every polygon order, including the nonmonotone universal four-edge maximum and its decay at large order. This complements established chord and gyration benchmarks for random closed polygon models.

## Closest sources and limits

The closest two full texts are arXiv:1206.3161 and arXiv:1702.01027. Other area-variance literature often uses unsigned convex hulls, not signed winding area. The full distribution, unsigned area moments, stability or topological restrictions are not covered. Historical uniqueness cannot be inferred solely from unsuccessful searches.
