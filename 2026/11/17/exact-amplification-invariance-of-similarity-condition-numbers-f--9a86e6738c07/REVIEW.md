# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Similarity of amplifications preserves the characteristic and minimal polynomials of complex two-by-two matrices, so nonsimilar originals cannot acquire a similarity. For similar scalar matrices the identity is optimal. For repeated-eigenvalue nonscalar matrices, two rank-one square-zero parts give the exact optimal norm ratio and an explicit diagonal intertwiner. For the distinct-eigenvalue case, conjugacy of the rank-one spectral idempotents forces the lower bound through \(g(P)=\|2P-I\|\); an explicit bisector eigenbasis and positive diagonal intertwiner attain it. These bounds hold unchanged under tensoring with any nonzero Hilbert-space identity. The \(2\times2\) nilpotent family attains the full amplification envelope \(\theta_{2,q}(t)=t\). The standalone finite checker ran 5,200 supplementary assertions successfully; the infinite-dimensional statement is proved analytically, not inferred from the tests.

**Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority risk.** Aouichaoui, *Similarity cancellation and primitive operator roots* (arXiv:2611.01298v1), investigates \(\theta_{n,q}\) through general dimension-dependent bounds and shows amplification can strictly improve conditioning for certain large dimensions, but the inspected introductory definitions and principal results do not assert the exact \(n=2\) equality. Jiang, Ma, Shi and Zhang, *The Ulam–Kaplansky problem in operator algebras* (arXiv:2607.20593v2), give general bounds, not this sharp classification. Demmel, *The Condition Number of Similarities that Diagonalize Matrices* (1983), gives the one-matrix spectral-subspace orthogonalization constant and a useful ingredient, but not the claimed optimal ratio between two distinct similarity realizations or Hilbert-amplification equality. Searches for analogous two-dimensional statements and equivalent eigenvector-angle formulations in mathematical records produced no covering statement. An older equivalent argument under different terminology remains possible; novelty is not independently certified.

**Value — PASS.** The result exactly determines the similarity-cancellation constant for a complete natural matrix size, without a dimension-dependent loss. It supplies a uniform formula in all similarity classes, finite and infinite Hilbert multiplicity, and attains the sharp envelope at every distortion parameter. This isolates a structural dimension-two rigidity boundary complementary to the known strict improvement in higher dimensions.

The result does not classify the earliest higher dimension permitting strict improvement, nor simultaneous or non-Hilbert-space similarities. Demmel's result is acknowledged rather than claimed as new.
