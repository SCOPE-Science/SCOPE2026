# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The hypotheses demand normalized strictly positive product masses, ordered sites, positive edge weights and globally additive affine regression. Orthogonality gives one coefficient per axis. Fiberwise integration by parts gives the exact positive prefix mass \(U_{j,k}\) and bounds each output slope by the corresponding input edge variation. A step at any chosen axis and position gives equality, proving the maximum without relying on sampled computations. The uniform-grid variance, prefix sums and edge sums simplify algebraically to the stated exact parity formula. An independent rational cut enumeration checks eight finite weighted and unweighted instances, including a nonuniform anisotropic design. No limit statement is inferred from enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The archival multivariate discrete least-squares study of Zhou–Narayan–Xu (arXiv:1401.0894v1) concerns stability of the sampling and polynomial approximation, not the graph-BV operator seminorm. Nochetto–Salgado (arXiv:1211.1069v1) study a local total-variation-diminishing interpolation map, not the orthogonal regression projector. The full available published result on affine cube projection treats a maximum-coordinate Lipschitz seminorm on continuous cubes, while the full result on simplex products treats the uniform \(L^\infty\) norm, neither implying the weighted discrete graph-variation constant. The accessible related one-dimensional complete-pair Gini variation result uses a different seminorm. Search and statement comparisons did not reveal a dominating theorem; differently phrased old tensorization statements remain a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result determines a sharp all-dimensional natural stability invariant, provides a linear-in-the-total-number-of-axis-sites algorithm despite exponentially many grid observations, and classifies exactly when affine regression preserves graph variation on rectangular grids. The first four-site instability, exact even/odd plateau, and optimal global \(3/2\) envelope clarify an approximation-stability phenomenon absent from Euclidean nonexpansivity. The fact that Boolean grids preserve variation even where the projector has a negative kernel demonstrates that this is not a disguised positivity assertion. No statistical estimation-rate consequences are claimed.

## Limitations and priority
The formula does not cover nonproduct masses, nonfactorized edge weights, multiaffine tensor products or higher polynomial degrees. Only abstracts/metadata were obtainable for some 1990s bounded-variation regularization literature; its penalty-problem formulation is different but uninspected full sections remain a priority risk. Published-theorem wording can differ from search terms, and indexing alone cannot establish global uniqueness.
