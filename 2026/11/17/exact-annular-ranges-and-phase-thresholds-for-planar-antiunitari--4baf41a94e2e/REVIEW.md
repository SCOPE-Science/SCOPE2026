# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The fixed-vector constrained pair formula is exact because the orthogonal complement of a unit vector in a complex Hilbert plane is one-dimensional. Its orthogonal coefficient has magnitude \(\sqrt{1-m^2}\) for an antiunitary. The self-overlap moduli cover precisely \([0,c]\): a zero exists by a quadratic-polynomial argument, the maximum by compactness, and the intermediate values by connectedness. Two one-variable optimizations give the claimed sharp endpoints, and the connected constrained-frame space plus rotational covariance fill all intervening moduli and circles. The two endpoint cases \(|q|=0\) and \(|q|=1\), and \(c=0\) and \(c=1\), were checked separately. The standard-library diagnostics exercise explicit representative antiunitaries, but are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest motivating source, Majumdar–Müller (arXiv:2610.16823v1), states that the two-dimensional \(q\)-numerical range is a centered closed annulus and exhibits a nonzero inner radius; it does not evaluate both endpoints for all antiunitaries or identify the two sharp parameter thresholds. Its three-or-more-dimensional disk result does not imply the two-dimensional hole radius. Kołaczek–Müller (2024) establishes ordinary antilinear numerical-range geometry, which is only the \(|q|=1\) slice. Their 2025 real-linear paper treats ordinary numerical ranges. The independently published higher-dimensional antiunitary disk profile likewise does not classify a two-dimensional annulus. Targeted literature and indexed-result searches found no directly equivalent statement; this is not proof of global priority. A possible hidden classification in broader antiunitary or quadratic-form literature remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The motivating work leaves the radii of the exceptional two-dimensional annulus unspecified. The result supplies the complete exact parameterized answer, including a sharp necessary and sufficient criterion for convexity and a different sharp threshold for the norm to be attained as the outer \(q\)-numerical radius. The thresholds can appear in either order, and every allowed numerical radius is realized by a concrete planar antiunitary. This is a motivated complete classification, not an arbitrary finite computation or an ordinary-range restatement.

## Scope and limitations
The claim is confined to antiunitaries on a complex Hilbert plane and \(|q|\le1\). General antilinear or real-linear maps may have different endpoint formulas. The exact planar statement does not determine boundary attainment for infinite-dimensional antiunitaries, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
