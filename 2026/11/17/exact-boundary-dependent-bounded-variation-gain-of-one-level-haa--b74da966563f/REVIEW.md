# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The coefficient is the maximum over single-jump inputs, by the exact telescoping representation of every real path signal and because the reflection fixes constants. For a jump internal to a block, reflection shifts the two half-blocks by opposite amounts; counting the input, midpoint and available block-edge jumps gives \(1+(2+c)a\), except at the true midpoint where the value is \(1+c\). Optimization over the integer jump position and over interior versus boundary blocks yields the formula. The proof has no asymptotic interpolation step. Exact rational checks cover 3,204 jumps over 72 parameter pairs; they are secondary verification rather than proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification

The closely relevant classical sources address BV wavelet coefficients (Cohen et al., 2003), restricted weak-type \(L^p\) bounds for Haar projections (Osȩkowski, 2013), and TV-related soft coefficient shrinkage (Steidl et al., 2004). These do not imply an exact coefficient for *sign reflection* at one level under open-path variation. Direct search and relevant published-result comparisons did not locate a covering expression. Similar finite-block computations could exist outside indexed literature, so historical priority is not certified.

## Value — PASS

The theorem gives a complete classification of a canonical linear Haar coefficient modification: boundary/interior block geometry changes the exact sharp constant from a limit of \(3\) or \(4\) to a limit of \(5\), despite exact preservation of Euclidean length. The finite constants are relevant to sharp BV stability budgets for wavelet coefficient editing and require the precise operator/geometry rather than a generic norm-equivalence estimate.

## Scope and limits

Uniform even blocks and a *single complete contrast level* only; not arbitrary multiscale sign patterns, weighted BV or periodic boundary conditions. The references are comparison evidence, not independent certification of priority.
