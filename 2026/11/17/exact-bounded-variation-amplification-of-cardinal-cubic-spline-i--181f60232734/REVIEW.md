# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The cubic matching recurrence and its two-sided bounded Green inverse give the unique interpolant. A unit step has an exactly monotone central transition and alternating geometric overshoot humps whose variation sums to \(1+4h/(1-q)\). Summable jump decomposition establishes the upper bound for *every* finite-variation sample sequence, and the same step attains it. Symbolic elimination checks the displayed quartic; finite natural-spline tests are supplemental, not a proof of the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk

The comparison included the full available text of Madani et al. (arXiv:1105.0011v1), Tzimbalario (1977), and Abergel–Moisan (2016), alongside published summaries on monotone cubic interpolation. The first optimizes frequency-domain approximation, the second evaluates \(\ell^\infty\)-to-\(L^\infty\) Lebesgue constants, and the third defines total variation of a different Shannon reconstruction. None of these inspected statements covers the exact \(\ell^1\) first-difference to \(L^1\) derivative norm of the bi-infinite cubic interpolant. Older inaccessible spline-norm articles and unindexed formulations leave genuine historical-priority uncertainty; noncoverage is not asserted for their full texts.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This gives an exact natural operator invariant for canonical cubic reconstruction from discrete samples: sharp 58.91-percent variation inflation, a geometric decomposition of interpolation ringing, and attained worst-case step data. It distinguishes smooth interpolation from variation preservation in a way that ordinary pointwise errors, positivity tests, or \(L^\infty\) Lebesgue constants cannot quantify. The result is all-dimensional (bi-infinite) and not a finite-test extrapolation.

## Scientific limitations

Only the real, unweighted, unit-spaced, bi-infinite, bounded \(C^2\) cubic cardinal interpolant is covered. Finite-boundary, nonuniform and higher-order interpolation variants are not claimed; historical novelty remains conditioned on unindexed prior literature.
