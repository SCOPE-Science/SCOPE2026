# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The weighted-difference map is a surjective isometry onto \(\mathbb C\oplus_1\ell^\infty\) (and onto the corresponding max direct sum), because every root-to-vertex path is finite. Direct expansion yields the explicit finite-supported multiplier row. Local finiteness makes truncations finite rank. Subtracting both the input and output prefixes gives an upper approximation with exact tail norm \(E_K\). To establish a lower bound against **arbitrary** compact operators, select phase-aligned norm-one path vectors in successive disjoint depth bands; disjoint bounded sequences in \(\ell^\infty\) are weakly null because finitely additive bounded-variation measures have summable variation across disjoint supports. Compactness then forces their images to be norm-null. The same root-zero witnesses work for both norms. This provides the full infinite-dimensional proof; 21,537 finite assertions are supplementary checks.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

López-Martínez (2026) gives a complete coordinate model and exact **operator** norms, but no essential-norm result. The primary 2026 graph multiplier paper (arXiv:2602.13534, Section 8) gives estimates rather than an exact essential-norm formula. The most relevant public exact theorem concerns **little** weighted tree Lipschitz spaces, modeled by \(c_0\), and expressly excludes the **big** \(\ell^\infty\) domain. The new formula deletes finite **input** as well as output prefixes and is mathematically non-equivalent to that little-space theorem: its weighted-ray compactness example cannot even act on the little space. Earlier tree multiplier papers give estimates; no accessible result was found that implies the stated sharp formula for arbitrary weights and the big Banach space. Full text of a relevant 2010 paper and some historical literature could not be inspected, leaving a real priority risk. An absence of search hits is not regarded as uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This fills a naturally posed quantitative question for a nonseparable function space, provides exact compactness/essential norm beyond prior bounds, and identifies why a tempting extension of the little-space coefficient formula is wrong. The vanishing essential norm of a multiplier with persistent weighted derivative shows an analytically substantive distinction caused by finite-prefix compact corrections. Arbitrary weights include both usual Lipschitz and weighted tree settings; no downstream theorem is assumed for its value.

## Limits and relationship to prior work

This is not a result for general graphs or unbounded multiplication. It uses standard finite-rank and weak-convergence technology, whose application to the weighted multiplier row yields the new operator-specific classification. The 2010 Colonna–Easley primary paper was bibliographically verified but its full text was inaccessible during review, and possible equivalent older triangular-matrix formulations remain a publication-priority risk.
