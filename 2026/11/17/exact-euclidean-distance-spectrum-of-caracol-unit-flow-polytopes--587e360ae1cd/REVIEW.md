A disjoint classification by two, three, or four distinct endpoints yields every coefficient in the displayed generating polynomial. A related result on Pitman–Stanley Euclidean diameters concerns a different polytope and is not a reduction of the edge-coordinate metric of caracol flows. A general inball result for directed-flow polytopes concerns distances to facets, not distances among path vertices. An exact beta law for sums of uniform order-statistic spacings is classical; it is explicitly treated as a proof ingredient rather than a separate discovery. Searches did not locate an equivalent full finite distance-spectrum formula. There remains a risk of unindexed work on path-incidence metrics or related order-polytope embeddings, and novelty has not been independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The canonical ambient metric of a natural lattice-polytope family has a complete all-dimensional vertex-pair distance histogram with only linear-many arithmetic terms rather than a quartic number of pair comparisons. It determines its exact mean, sharp diameter, every maximizing pair, and a limiting normalized distance law at a quantitative rate. This geometry is not determined by the already-known affine order-polytope equivalence because the map need not preserve Euclidean distances. The exact spectrum and equality classification are the substantive findings, not the routine beta-spacing calculation alone.

## Scope and limitations

The theorem applies only to standard Euclidean edge-coordinate embeddings of unit-flow caracol polytopes. It does not assert properties of all netflow vectors, arbitrary edge metrics, geodesic graph distances or uniform solid-volume point samples. The primary source comparison was focused on the graph definitions and relevant theorem sections, with full-text word searches for distance claims; it does not eliminate all unpublished or unindexed equivalents. The finite checker is not an independent audit.
