# Scientific review of mixed-arm spider Euclidean inballs

## Correctness

PASS. For a finite unweighted tree the polar Lipschitz ball is the image of an edge-gradient cube, so every independent edge-sign choice gives one facet normal. The centered-squared-norm calculation is exact. A one-arm replacement inequality gains at least \(\(4-8/N>0\)\), proving that returning length-two arms cannot maximize. The remaining discrete minimization \(\min|3a+b|\) is exhaustively solved by parity and explicit witnesses, including the exceptional odd-\(q\), single-short-arm case. Facet multiplicities use exact sign binomial counts; center-locus ranks follow from the group-orbit decomposition, with no computer-dependent lemma. Direct exact arithmetic independently checked 30 parameter profiles and 152,928 signed-edge potentials.

## Originality

PASS with historical-priority qualifications. The full text of Higashitani--Jochemko--Michałek, arXiv:1807.07678, §3.1, supplies a general integer facet criterion, not the canonical Euclidean norm maximum or contact rank. Alexander--Fradelizi--García-Lirola--Zvavitch, arXiv:1911.10642, §2 and §3, treats tree crosspolytope structure and volume products, not these formulas. Godard, arXiv:0904.3178, identifies tree metrics in isometric Banach-space terms, not a canonical Euclidean inball. Adjacent all-long-arm spiders and bistars do not meet the simultaneous constraints \(p\ge1,q\ge2\). Searches did not locate an exact general formula for heterogeneous one/two-edge spider canonical inballs, although absence of a search hit is not a proof of novelty; historical equivalents under graph-potential variance terminology remain possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. A partially subdivided star is the smallest heterogeneous extension of a star that separates the contribution of arm lengths from the abstract crosspolytope geometry. The full-height screening lemma, sharp integer discrepancy, exact number of tangent facets, and incenter-locus rank give structural information not implied by the common Banach-space isometry class. In the three-short-arm family, parity changes the affine dimension of optimal centers by two, providing a nontrivial and testable geometric distinction.

## Boundaries and limitations

The theorem is for real finite unweighted tree metrics with \(p\ge1,q\ge2\) in the canonical zero-sum Euclidean realization, not arbitrary weighted spiders. Computational checks are finite corroboration, not an infinite certificate. Equivalent historical formulations remain a modest residual risk. The optimal-center locus is classified by affine dimension, not by all its vertices.
