# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Each tree-edge gradient is an independent coordinate on the polar cube. Every facet normal is therefore uniquely represented by unit-magnitude signed edge increments. The variance identity on the \(L+1\) vertices of an arm proves \((L+1)(u-v)\ge w^2-s^2\). Replacing any nonmonotone arm by the full-height ramp opposite the sum of the remaining arms changes the squared centered norm by at least \((w^2-s^2)(1/(L+1)-1/N)>0\), because a spider has other vertices beyond that arm. This strict screening rules out **every** nonmonotone maximizing facet. For full-height arms the exact squared norm is \(\sum_i u_i-(\sum_i\varepsilon_iw_i)^2/N\); the signed-partition minimum proves the radius and counts contacts for all positive integer arm lengths. Central symmetry proves global rather than merely centered optimality. Contact constraints span the image of the winning-sign matrix under an injective map from arms into zero-sum potentials, so their orthogonal complement has exact affine dimension \(n-\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal E)\). Rational tests independently examined 68,904 polar cube vertices across 86 heterogeneous and homogeneous profiles, arm path variance for all 1,022 signed paths through length nine, and 1,113 signed-partition profiles. Finite verification is corroborative, not a substitute for the general proof.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** Full published primary texts on the extremal structure of finite-metric Lipschitz-free balls and tree fundamental polytopes supply the molecule/duality and graph-facet background, not the canonical Euclidean spider inradius. Already published path, one-edge-star and bistar results were inspected as statement-level comparisons. More recent prior special families with arm lengths only one or two are explicitly acknowledged, as is the twice-subdivided equal-arm case. None of the identified results implies a full signed-partition law for arbitrary arm lengths by simple parameter substitution: the strict exclusion of all nonmonotone arms of lengths greater than two is the needed missing step. No blanket claim of global priority is made. Equivalent max-variance formulas under alternate terminology remain a residual historical-priority risk; full text and public finding comparisons are stated in the structured audit.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The unrestricted lengths, rather than one fixed subdivision depth, are a natural invariant family of starlike metric trees. The theorem converts a \(2^n\)-facet Euclidean maximization to a \(q\)-variable triangular-weight partition, provides exact inradius and tangent count, and determines the affine dimension of *all* incenters. The strict path-variance screening lemma is substantive and applies to several distinct long-arm shapes. Exact computation of the partition minimum and contacts in polynomial time in the tree's vertex count further distinguishes the result from isolated numerical cases. The affine center-rank result makes a combinatorial property of optimal signs measurable as Euclidean geometry. Neither computation nor novelty by itself is taken as proof of value.

**Closest literature and residual limits.** Aliaga, arXiv:2102.01219; Alexander–Fradelizi–García-Lirola–Zvavitch, arXiv:1911.10642; D'Alì–Delucchi–Michałek, arXiv:1910.05193; Delucchi–Hoessly, arXiv:1612.05534. Published calculations for paths, stars, and bistars, together with limited results for two-edge arms and one-/two-edge mixtures, do not cover arbitrary lengths. A different-language graph-variance identity may have been overlooked. Weighted tree edges and center-polytope vertices remain unaddressed.
