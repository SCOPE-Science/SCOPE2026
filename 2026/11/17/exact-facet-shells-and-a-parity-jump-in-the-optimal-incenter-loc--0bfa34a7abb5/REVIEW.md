# Mathematical review: triangular-book facet-distance spectrum

## Correctness

**PASS.** The symmetric edge roots span the zero-sum hyperplane because the triangular-book graph is connected. Polar vertices are exactly the feasible potentials whose tight incidence normals span the hyperplane; anchoring a hub and following a tight spanning tree forces integral potentials. The hub-pair difference has only three possible values, giving a disjoint and exhaustive classification into two binomial families. Mean subtraction establishes the exact squared norms; tight-edge counts establish their facet sizes. The global inball bound follows from antipodal support inequalities. Exact ranks of balanced sign vectors plus strictly slack noncontact inequalities prove the dimensions of **all** maximum-inball centers. Two factored quadratic inequalities establish the second-shell ordering, including the exceptional low-order ties and parity asymptotics. The standalone exact-rational checker covers nine relevant orders; it corroborates but is not used as proof for the infinite family.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** The first-public 2019 work of D'Alì, Delucchi and Michałek treats symmetric edge polytopes including common-edge gluings, but its Proposition 4.21 for two glued odd cycles does not imply the all-page Euclidean facet metric. Chen–Davis–Korchevskaia and Braun–Bruegge give general tight-edge/facet characterizations without this evaluated family spectrum. Mori–Mori–Ohsugi bound join-graph facet counts; an upper or lower facet-count theorem has no information about the canonical Euclidean norm classes or the affine optimal-center dimension. A directly inspected mathematical record on fan graphs \(K_1*P_m\) overlaps the two-page case, but not the books with three or more pages, \(K_2*\overline{K_q}\); the graphs and metric normal sets differ. A directly inspected clique-windmill record shares a **vertex**, not an edge. Searches for equivalent graph-Lipschitz norm/variance formulations and exact precomputed norm tables did not locate stronger coverage. No absence-of-search-result argument alone establishes global priority.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Triangular books are a canonical infinite join/gluing family with increasing polytope dimension. The result is a complete quantitative face-support metric distribution (not only a facet count): it distinguishes simplicial from nonsimplicial facets, gives sharp positive separation of the first and second tangent-plane shells including the exceptional ties at two and three pages, and reveals a parity jump in the dimension of the full center set of optimal inscribed balls. Such complete radial/contact data answer natural stability questions about maximal inspheres and cannot be obtained simply by relabelling existing facet-count formulas. The existing all-graph facet criterion by itself requires the nontrivial classification and quantitative comparisons proved here.
