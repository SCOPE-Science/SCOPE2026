# Review of exact Floquet instability staircase

## Correctness — PASS

The rotating-frame substitution gives the exact two-real-component retarded linear equation and entire characteristic determinant. The proof establishes a stable baseline by factoring the characteristic determinant at zero radial forcing, showing that no nonzero imaginary root is available for \(0<\beta<\pi/2\), and tracking the initially double zero. The imaginary-axis equations force odd frequencies \((2j+1)\pi\), and derivative calculations give strictly positive crossing velocities. A uniform root-modulus bound for any bounded forcing interval rules out hidden entries from infinity. Hence the right-half-plane root count and the standard orbital stability implications follow without finite-root enumeration. A standalone script checks selected exact identities and Newton continuations; those finite experiments are corroboration, not the proof.

## Originality — PASS within available evidence

Motonaga–Takayasu (2026, arXiv:2611.04530v1), which treats exactly the same Hara delay equation, credits the circular orbit to Hara (1997), proves seven other periodic solutions, and states explicitly that the circular instability threshold remains open. Related published mathematical records concern different equations; Hara–Sakata’s piecewise-constant-argument model is not the unit-delay RFDE. Searches for the circular-wave Floquet spectrum, characteristic formula, equivalent rotating-frame statements, and possible stronger delay-stability classifications found no covering theorem. The original 1997 Hara paper was not accessible in full, so priority is provisional; this is a genuine source limitation, not evidence of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem gives the sharp local orbital stability interval and complete unbounded instability-index staircase, across the natural full range of positive rotation angles. It resolves a stability question explicitly left open by the primary motivating paper and supplies both the threshold and exact multiplicity of unstable modes. It does not claim a global basin or stability of newly found star-shaped waves.
