# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The real matrix decomposition into complex-linear and antilinear parts gives singular values \(|z|+|w|\) and \(\bigl||z|-|w|\bigr|\). The determinant-sign trichotomy classifies every real two-plane. A strictly monotone scalar ratio and the principal-angle graph formula yield all stated claims, with quasi-Banach exponents admitted without invoking triangle inequalities. The Taylor expansions have nonzero, explicitly checked leading coefficients. Supplementary computations independently reconstruct singular values and finite-dimensional samples; they are not used as proof of the universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The source arXiv:2611.08448 studies asymptotic Gelfand numbers, and arXiv:2011.06554 and arXiv:2103.13050 study the same embeddings and broad bounds. A previously available real two-by-two scalar Gelfand minimum is acknowledged, not claimed new. The presently evaluated statement is the *exact norm of every individual real two-plane*, including the rank-one boundary, its graph parameter, the exact Grassmannian projection gap, and the distinct quadratic/linear sharp stability orders. Targeted mathematical-result searches found other finite-dimensional Schatten geometry, but none stating or logically implying this complete objective and metric profile. Historical equivalent formulations cannot be entirely excluded.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A Gelfand number records only the best subspace, while quantitative matrix recovery and near-optimal width constructions require distinguishing all nearly optimal subspaces from rank-one-intersecting ones. This theorem identifies the entire two-plane norm landscape and converts the measured norm defect into an exact geometric distance. The change in sharp stability order between finite target exponent and the operator-norm endpoint is substantive and not visible in the scalar Gelfand value.
