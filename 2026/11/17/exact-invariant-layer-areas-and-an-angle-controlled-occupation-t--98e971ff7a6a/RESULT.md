# Exact invariant-layer areas and an angle-controlled occupation transition in translated cone exchanges

## Finding
For a four-cone translated-cone exchange with the specific middle-cone swap of Cockram, Ashwin and Rodrigues, the nested invariant polygonal layers have **an exact area spectrum**. It undergoes a sharp equal-area transition at \(\varphi=\pi/3\), and when the number of layers diverges it exhibits an explicit nondegenerate, angle-dependent continuum occupation law.

Set \(c=\cos\varphi\), \(s=\sin\varphi\), \(\eta=1-\lambda\), and
\[
N=\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda}{2\eta(1+c)}\right\rfloor.
\]
For \(N\ge1\) and \(1\le n\le N\), write \(Y^{(n)}\) for the invariant \(n\)-stepped pyramid from the source and \(Y^{(0)}=\varnothing\). Its exact area and the area of its \(n\)-th invariant layer are respectively
\[
A_n=\frac{n\eta(1+c)}s\left[c(1+\lambda)+n\eta(1+c)(1-2c)\right],
\]
\[
D_n=A_n-A_{n-1}=\frac{\eta(1+c)}s\left[c(1+\lambda)+(2n-1)\eta(1+c)(1-2c)\right]>0.
\]
If \(N\ge2\), then \(D_n\) is strictly decreasing in \(n\) for \(\varphi<\pi/3\), strictly increasing for \(\varphi>\pi/3\), and independent of \(n\) if and only if \(\varphi=\pi/3\). At the special angle,
\[
D_n=\frac{\sqrt3}2\eta(1+\lambda).
\]
For each fixed \(0<\varphi<\pi/2\), as \(\lambda\uparrow1\), \(N\to\infty\) and \(A_N\to(1+2c)/(4s)\). If a planar-area-uniform point of \(Y^{(N)}\) is assigned the index \(K\) of its invariant layer, then \(K/N\) converges in law with CDF and density
\[
F_\varphi(u)=\frac{4cu+(1-2c)u^2}{1+2c},\qquad
f_\varphi(u)=\frac{4c+2(1-2c)u}{1+2c},\quad 0\le u\le1.
\]
The limit is uniform if and only if \(\varphi=\pi/3\).

## Assumptions and scope
Take \(0<\varphi<\pi/2\), \(0<\lambda<1\), and the four-cone partition of the upper half-plane with consecutive cone apertures \((\pi/2-\varphi,\varphi,\varphi,\pi/2-\varphi)\). The cone permutation swaps only the middle two cones. Put \(\eta=1-\lambda\). On the left, inner-left, inner-right, right cones, respectively, the complex-map branches are
\[
F(z)=z-1,\quad F(z)=e^{i\varphi}z-\eta,\quad
F(z)=e^{-i\varphi}z-\eta,\quad F(z)=z+\lambda.
\]
As in the source, treat cone-boundary ambiguities and polygonal edges modulo planar Lebesgue-null sets. The source constructs nested \(F\)-invariant open polygons \(Y^{(n)}\), for every \(1\le n\le N\), with invariant measurable differences \(Y^{(n)}\setminus Y^{(n-1)}\). These source statements are assumptions already established in the literature; our results quantify the **area** of these regions and the induced index distribution. The asymptotic takes fixed \(\varphi\) and \(\lambda\uparrow1\), not a jointly varying angle.

## Proof
**Step 1 (reconstruct the polygon boundary).** For a fixed admissible \(n\), define
\[
Q=n\eta(1+c),\quad \ell=\lambda-2Q\ge0,\quad h=\frac{Qc}s.
\]
The source's vertices give an upper boundary connecting \((-1,0)\) to \((\lambda,0)\), above their common baseline. The first oblique edge has horizontal width \(Q\) and height \(h\), and is followed by a horizontal portion of length \(\ell\) and a further horizontal portion of length \(\eta\). From \((-Q,h)\), exactly \(n\) rising blocks consist of an oblique edge of horizontal width \(\eta c\), vertical rise \(\eta s\), then a horizontal edge of width \(\eta\); they terminate at \((0,Q/s)\). Next \(n\) descending blocks use the corresponding oblique decreases and horizontal edges, ending at \((Q,h)\). A final horizontal portion of length \(\ell\) and a right oblique edge of horizontal width \(Q\) reach \((\lambda,0)\). Equivalently, the explicit ordered vertices are generated by the standalone checker using only these increments. These are exactly the \(n\)-stepped boundaries of the source (up to zero-length edges at \(\ell=0\)).

**Step 2 (integrate horizontal strips).** The two exterior sloping edges contribute the combined triangle area \(Qh\). The middle horizontal base level contributes \(h(1+\lambda-2Q)\). Above that level, the rising and descending step structures contribute altogether \(n^2\eta^2s(1+c)=n\eta sQ\). Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_n&=Qh+h(1+\lambda-2Q)+n\eta sQ\\
&=h(1+\lambda-Q)+n\eta sQ\\
&=\frac Qs\left[c(1+\lambda)+Q(1-2c)\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Using \(Q=n\eta(1+c)\) yields the area formula; the case \(n=0\) is zero. This is an exact polygonal area identity, not a numerical extrapolation.

**Step 3 (positivity and sharp equal-area angle).** Subtract consecutive quadratic areas:
\[
D_n=\frac{\eta(1+c)}s\left[c(1+\lambda)+(2n-1)\eta(1+c)(1-2c)\right].
\]
If \(1-2c\ge0\), the bracket is positive. If \(1-2c<0\), then \((2n-1)\eta(1+c)<\lambda\), so the bracket is greater than \(c(1+\lambda)+\lambda(1-2c)=c+\lambda(1-c)>0\). Moreover,
\[
D_{n+1}-D_n=\frac{2\eta^2(1+c)^2}s(1-2c).
\]
For \(N\ge2\), this vanishes for all consecutive layers exactly at \(c=1/2\), equivalently \(\varphi=\pi/3\), and its sign gives the two strict regimes. Direct substitution at \(c=1/2\) gives the stated common area.

**Step 4 (complete layer-index distribution).** Nesting and zero-area boundaries imply that if \(Z\) is distributed by normalized planar area in \(Y^{(N)}\), then its unique layer index \(K\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb P(K\le k)=\frac{A_k}{A_N},\quad 0\le k\le N.
\]
For fixed angle, \(N\eta(1+c)\to1/2\), since \(\lambda-2N\eta(1+c)\in[0,2\eta(1+c))\). Hence for \(k=\lfloor uN\rfloor\), the quantity \(k\eta(1+c)\to u/2\), and the displayed exact ratio tends to
\[
\frac{(u/2)[2c+(u/2)(1-2c)]}{(1/2)[2c+(1/2)(1-2c)]}
=\frac{4cu+(1-2c)u^2}{1+2c}.
\]
The limit is continuous, so convergence of CDFs proves convergence in distribution. Differentiation yields the density. It is strictly positive on \([0,1]\), and constant exactly at \(c=1/2\). The total-area limit comes from the denominator calculation: \(A_N\to(1+2c)/(4s)\). All claims follow.

## Relationship to prior work
Cockram, Ashwin and Rodrigues construct the four-cone invariant \(n\)-stepped pyramids and prove their nested layer invariance (2023 preprint; 2025 journal, Theorem 4.2 and Corollary 4.4). Those are **prior results** and are not claimed as new. The present result calculates their exact planar area spectrum, the unique equal-area angle, and an explicit limiting layer-index law not stated in the inspected source portions. A different three-cone translated-cone construction has previously been studied for rational-parameter **period** spectra weighted by area; those periods and that trapezium are not these four-cone nested-shell areas. Related renormalization literature studies a different first-return phenomenon and does not directly imply this area law.

## Limitations
The law describes the finite family of invariant regions guaranteed by the source construction, not all invariant subsets or ergodic components in the full upper half-plane. The normalized random point is chosen by planar Lebesgue area **inside the outermost constructed pyramid**, not according to a unique natural invariant measure of the full dynamical system. No equidistribution of a single orbit across distinct invariant layers is asserted: such crossing is forbidden by layer invariance. Boundary points and zero-width edges require the usual null-set conventions. Exhaustive publication priority is not established; inaccessible or unindexed geometric derivations could duplicate an area identity.
