# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim quantifies polygons previously established as nested invariant sets. The polygon boundary is reconstructed from the source's given edge lengths; integrating its two exterior triangular sides, central base rectangle, and the triangular/horizontal step increments gives the exact quadratic area formula. Its consecutive differences give the unique angle transition; a positive-area inequality handles all parameter boundaries; an explicit area ratio and the floor-function remainder give the limiting probability law. The included checker exactly verifies 66 rational shoelace instances and samples 12,977 forward images; these corroborate, but do not replace, the mathematical proof. The boundary case with zero horizontal top edge is covered by continuity.

## Originality — PASS within checked evidence
The 2023 preprint and its later open-access journal article establish polygonal invariance and nested layers, not the area spectrum, critical angle, or continuum depth law in the inspected material. A closely related published result on a three-cone translated-cone map concerns period-count area weights in a different trapezium and does not imply the four-cone nested layer law. Another record calculates two-height return clocks on four-cone caps, not the areas of the bounded invariant pyramids. The original first-return renormalization article describes a different dynamical reduction. Searches for algebraically equivalent area expressions, relevant aliases, and dominant region-area formulas did not yield decisive prior coverage. The comparison is by implications, not titles. Inaccessible full-text variants and unindexed geometry may still represent a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The previously known barriers restrict motion to distinct invariant planar layers. Their relative Lebesgue sizes are a natural measure of the phase space allocated to those invariant components. A universal, parameter-independent angle separates decreasing from increasing shell areas, with exact equiareal shells at the transition. The critical continuum limit gives a concrete nonuniform-vs-uniform distribution as the number of layers diverges and is not a routine count of polygon vertices or an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest evidence and limits
The source constructs the four-cone nested polygons and proves almost-everywhere layer invariance (Cockram–Ashwin–Rodrigues, arXiv:2312.17412, 2023; journal version 2025, Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.4). Related work on translated-cone renormalization and other three-cone area/period spectra involves different objects. This result does not establish orbitwise mixing between invariant shells, uniqueness of invariant measures, or a global invariant-set census. The 2025 publisher page exposed the relevant theorem and proof by web-indexed text, but the direct full-page fetch was unavailable in this check.
