# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem quantifies over simple three-path theta graphs with all three path lengths at least two. Its proof reduces every nontrivial two-cycle to oscillating entire connecting paths; degree-two sites cannot flip in isolation. Frozen internal words are exactly those with no singleton runs, except that a one-vertex path interior is permitted only when the terminal opinions oppose each other. The degree-three terminal vote makes the one-active-path class possible only with opposed stationary boundary votes on *both* other paths. The disjoint support decomposition gives the three contributions without a symmetry or phase overcount. The generating functions and equal-arm asymptotics follow by run composition. Two independently implemented finite checks agree with the result on every stated finite case. The finite checks do not replace the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The general period-two property is already known (Goles–Olivos 1980, and its formulation in Benjamini et al., arXiv:1405.2486); one-dimensional no-singleton-run logic is also known (Tosic–Agha 2004). The 2004 primary paper's full text concentrates on one-dimensional regular CA rather than general three-path theta census. A directly relevant published record on singly chorded cycles has a degree-three terminal edge and two arc paths, not three paths of length at least two. Changing the chord to a subdivided edge changes the update map; its formula does not imply this one. A generalized-theta record concerns three-state excitation and period three, not binary majority and period two. Relevant record searches located no equivalent formula for the present exact domain. Risk: an unindexed or uninspected combinatorial classification could predate the observation; search failure is not a proof of priority.

## Value — PASS

The graph is the simplest 2-connected network with two independent cycles and two branching terminals. The exact three-parameter classification explains how an oscillation can be supported by only one alternating path even though each terminal needs a strict majority, because two *stationary* paths hold opposite boundary opinions. For equal arm lengths, single-channel oscillators dominate all primitive two-cycles at a quantified exponential rate, unlike the superficially expected two-channel support. The count and mechanism constitute a natural finite-graph dynamical invariant and a structural asymptotic, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice or mere enumeration.

The result counts initially periodic primitive two-cycles, not basins, convergence times, all recurrent states, or larger generalized-theta networks. No external independent audit was performed.
