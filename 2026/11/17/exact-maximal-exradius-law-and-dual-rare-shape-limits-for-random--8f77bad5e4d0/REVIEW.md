# Scientific review: maximal exradius of random cyclic triangles

## Correctness — PASS

The final result is the full one-dimensional CDF of the largest of three unit-circumradius exradii, with two explicit endpoint equivalents and conditional total-variation shape laws. The assumptions specify exactly three independent arclength-uniform circle points. The angle simplex density \(2/\pi^2\) follows from an **anchored** Dirichlet spacing argument. The exradius half-angle formula is independently supported by Wu–Yue–Chu (2015), §3.2, and follows from area divided by semiperimeter-minus-side. Sorting by the largest angle, the Jacobian \(1/2\), the three possible indices, and the section-length computation give the exact CDF with normalized total mass one. The deterministic support is \([3/2,4]\), with only the equilateral configuration giving the lower endpoint and only degenerate triangles approaching the upper endpoint.

The lower-end linearization is \(r_i=3/2+\sqrt3\delta_i+O(\|\delta\|^2)\); its limiting triangular sublevel region has area \(3/2\) after \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) angular scaling. The upper-end quadratic form is \(Q(B,C)=B^2+BC+C^2\); its positive-quadrant unit sublevel area is \(\pi/(3\sqrt3)\). These facts yield the sharp constants, and bounded-domain indicator convergence proves total-variation limits, not just weak convergence. The independently implemented Cartesian-distance-and-area checker agreed with the half-angle result and with the CDF at six thresholds; endpoint and marked-shape diagnostics passed. Finite checks are supporting tests, not substitutes for proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded historical priority

The classical support lower bound \(L\ge3/2\) is not new: it has been attributed to Erdős and is expressly excluded. Wu, Yue and Chu (2015), full HTML theorem/proof and §3.2 inspected, establish deterministic inequalities for **cyclic sums of exradius-to-side ratios**. Their exact trig formulas are inputs, but they do not derive a random maximal-exradius CDF. Edelman and Strang (2015), complete publicly available article examined, distinguish a uniform angle-simplex model from a Gaussian-triangle model, without the evaluated maximum-exradius distribution. Publicly available full proofs of *The full inradius law and two contrasting tails of random cyclic triangles* and *Excentral transport and universal shape ordering for acute random cyclic triangles* were also inspected. The former concerns the incircle radius; the latter concerns acute-conditioned angles and excentral shapes. Neither gives the CDF, two endpoint exponents, or marks for the largest original excircle radius. The identity \(\sum_i r_i-r=4\) does not determine the maximum exradius. Daley–Ebert–Swift (2014 issue) use independent side directions and unit-side normalization rather than independent circle points; available bibliographic material was examined, but their full text was not obtained.

Exact-claim and synonym searches covered maximal exradius, largest excircle, escribed radius, uniform-angle random triangle, endpoints, conditioned shapes and potential size-distribution analogues, and a local duplicate scan of accessible existing research statements found no equivalent. Published-record searches also checked stronger/nearby geometry results. These are substantive best-evidence comparisons, not exhaustive historical proof; unavailable older literature remains a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The largest exradius is a natural extremal triangle invariant with an established deterministic sharp inequality. An exact random law is a meaningful quantitative refinement: it characterizes how frequently random cyclic triangles approach equality and approach the degenerate extremum. The different powers \(\varepsilon^2\) and \(\varepsilon\) arise from distinct local geometries (a piecewise-linear maximum at the equilateral triple versus three quadratic simplex corners). The conditional Dirichlet and uniform-sector descriptions identify which triangle shapes generate the rare events, rather than merely supplying another moment or routine substitution. The full CDF permits exact probabilities at any intermediate threshold, and all constants are geometrically determined.
