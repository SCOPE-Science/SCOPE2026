# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** On the reducing subspace orthogonal to the fixed points, the residual bound is equivalent to a unit-circle spectral-arc gap. Every constant Ishikawa step is exactly a convex-coefficient quadratic polynomial. The two arc endpoints force a sharp one-dimensional minimax lower bound, and the symmetric trinomial attains it. The coefficient equality at the interior endpoint proves uniqueness. A planar rotation attains the rate at every horizon. Finite diagnostic tests support but do not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The motivating 2026 paper's indexed abstract gives general convergence under metric subregularity; its abstract does not supply the exact isometric minimax factor or unique parameters. The earlier 2017 inexact Krasnosel'skii–Mann work addresses residual estimates for a broader setting without an announced sharp isometric spectral filtering classification. Related published minimax results for extragradient and triangular projection means optimize different polynomials/domains. A full-text comparison with the 2026 paper and the 2017 manuscript was unavailable, so an equivalent unpublished or differently phrased classical result remains a material publication-priority risk. No absolute priority is claimed.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** An exact residual-error constant is converted into an attained, explicit best uniform convergence rate and unique implementable parameters for a natural infinite-dimensional class. The same optimal fixed parameters work for every finite horizon. Optimal Mann iteration is strictly slower, providing a concrete performance benchmark. The result does not claim optimality against time-varying parameters or outside orthogonal mappings.

**Important limitations.** The sharpness witnesses are planar rotations; the universal upper bound also holds in infinite-dimensional real Hilbert spaces. Publisher full text was inaccessible. Only a self-review has been performed.
