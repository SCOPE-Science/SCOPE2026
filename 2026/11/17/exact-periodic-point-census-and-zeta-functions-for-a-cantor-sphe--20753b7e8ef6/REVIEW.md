# Mathematical review: exact periodic census of the Cantor-sphere example

## Correctness

**PASS.** The precise hypothesis is the Fletcher–Martin three-dimensional construction with integers \(d,m\ge2\) satisfying \(1/d+1/m<1\). Its published conjugacy identifies the Julia restriction with a two-symbol shift over pillow-sphere fibre maps induced by integer linear torus maps. Every length-\(n\) word has a unique periodic symbolic base point. The return matrix has expansion \(Q=d^{n-r}m^r\) and reflection parity \((-1)^r\). The two torus kernels \(\ker(A-I)\) and \(\ker(A+I)\) are finite, and their intersection is exactly the involution-fixed subset of their union; Burnside counting therefore gives \(Q^2+(-1)^r\) geometric pillow fixed points, including the branch corners. Binomial cancellation gives \((d^2+m^2)^n\); the one additional Fatou fixed point is infinity. The zeta and primitive-period formulas follow by formal power series and Möbius inversion. The finite checker corroborates, rather than establishes, these infinite claims.

## Originality

**PASS within investigated sources; priority provisional.** Fletcher and Martin, arXiv:2611.08773v1, state the skew-product model, entropy, and density of repelling periodic points but not the all-iterate, itinerary-refined geometric fixed-point census or Artin–Mazur zeta. Their \(D^n\)-atom generating partition does not itself count branch-identified periodic points. The determinant and Burnside ingredients belong to established dynamics and finite-group counting; they are not asserted to be new. Li, arXiv:2611.07816v1, addresses geometric iterated Galois groups and fixed-point proportions of preimage-tree group actions, not this three-dimensional skew product's geometrical periodic orbit counts. Classical Lattès literature is relevant background and was only partially accessible. Further priority risk remains from literature on Lefschetz counting for branched expanding maps and general periodic-point quotients.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** An exact periodic census and zeta function are natural missing invariants of a newly constructed class of Julia sets with infinitely many Fatou components. The itinerary-resolved formula records distinct effects of orientation-preserving and orientation-reversing pillow return maps; their \(\pm1\) corrections disappear in the total for a structural reason rather than by an unjustified degree approximation. The result shows that a repeller with an uncountable family of connected spherical components has exactly the same periodic-point zeta data as a full shift on \(d^2+m^2\) symbols. The primitive-orbit formula gives every least-period population without finite enumeration.
