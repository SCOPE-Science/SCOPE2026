# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The lower bound is a direct conjugation-norm obstruction, applied not merely to the original matrices but to their separating polynomial: the spectral involution in the simple-spectrum case, and the nonzero nilpotent part in the repeated-root case. The exact two-dimensional orthonormal normal forms are derived from eigenvector bisectors or from the one-dimensional image/kernel of a square-zero matrix, respectively. A diagonal intertwiner attains the same lower bound. Tensoring it with the identity supplies the upper bound for every nonzero Hilbert multiplicity; the polynomial obstruction remains valid for any bounded invertible amplified intertwiner, including ones not decomposable as tensors. The scalar case and all boundary parameters are included. Exact rational and complex-phase checks from the supplied script passed; finite tests are supplemental to the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Jiang–Ma–Shi–Zhang, arXiv:2607.20593v1, Definition 4.1 and Remark 4.2, establish the quantitative question and suggest equality of the doubled optimum without deriving the two-dimensional formula. Aouichaoui, arXiv:2611.01298v1, Theorem 1.3 and Proposition 2.2, establishes general fixed-size boundedness and asymptotic continuity at one; Corollary 5.1 supplies growing-dimension counterexamples. None of these inspected theorems evaluates \(\theta_{2,q}\), classifies all two-dimensional pairs, or gives arbitrary Hilbert-multiplicity isometry of the optimum. Demmel's older projection-conditioning results concern diagonalization of one matrix into spectral blocks, not conditioning of a similarity between two arbitrary matrices with and without amplification. Marcoux–Sarkowicz–Zhang's related amplification work optimizes unitary-orbit distances, not invertible-intertwiner condition numbers. Targeted mathematical-record searches returned no directly covering result. These searches do not prove worldwide priority; an equivalent classical line-angle formula is the main residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The quantitative similarity-cancellation function was introduced explicitly in recent operator-algebra research, and the currently known general size-dependent bound permits strict drops after amplification in larger dimensions. The result determines its exact value in the first nontrivial size for all multiplicities, and simultaneously classifies the optimal conditioning of every similar planar pair (including defective Jordan cases), showing that arbitrary infinite multiplicity cannot improve it. This is a complete natural classification of a published optimization problem, rather than an isolated matrix substitution or a numerical improvement.

## Scope and caveats

No analogous formula is claimed for base dimension at least three or for joint intertwiners of families. An earlier theorem phrased in terms of principal angles or spectral projections could imply part of the result; this literature risk requires independent priority investigation. The computational checks do not establish the universal statement without the analytic proof.
