# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The Fermat–Torricelli first-order and vertex subgradient conditions [2] establish a unique minimizer and distinguish interior solutions from obtuse-vertex solutions. Three normalized uniform circular gaps give the exact \(1/3\) vertex probability. A global two-orientation coordinate map from an interior median, its three \(120^\circ\) directions, and their ray–circle intersection lengths is injective modulo angle seams. Differentiation gives the exact Jacobian \((\sqrt3/2)\sum_jt_j/\prod_jh_j\). The two charts and uniform sample-angle law yield the stated planar density. Four isolated angular singularities each contribute a controlled logarithm; the center expansion and the rim shell follow. The standalone checker numerically evaluates the radial law, independently solves sampled median problems, and tests the Jacobian; these diagnostics are corroborative, not proof.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

Materially inspected: the complete relevant text of Várilly's 2000 paper [1] (deterministic Fermat location relative to an Euler-line constraint); the full available text of Mordukhovich–Nam [2] (deterministic convex-analytic characterization and algorithms); and the full online RESULT statements for published research on the unit-circle triangle inradius, acute-angle excentral transport, and strongly obtuse counts. None gives an equivalent push-forward **spatial distribution** of the geometric median, a radial planar density, or the singular/continuous decomposition. published-finding corpus semantic searches for Fermat/Torricelli/geometric median, random inscribed triangles, center location, and radial distribution did not return direct prior coverage. Importantly, a previous random Steiner-saving **length** law for this same sampling model gives \(\Pr(Q=0)=1/3\), but cannot determine the interior median's location law. The general term "Fermat point" sometimes denotes the first isogonic center even for highly obtuse triangles; the paper defines the **distance minimizer** and does not equate the two outside their shared regime. Unindexed prior computations and alternate-terminology sources remain a genuine priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Fermat location is a fundamental Euclidean facility-location / Steiner-junction functional. Determining its complete distribution for an elementary invariant random triangle is a natural exact stochastic-geometric problem, not a nominal parameter specialization. The law identifies a previously hidden mixture of a positive singular boundary component and a bulk density with a logarithmic rim singularity, and supplies full radial probabilities and a sharp center estimate. These two qualitatively distinct mechanisms cannot be inferred from a known zero-saving probability or a known triangle-angle distribution without the nontrivial global Jacobian analysis.
