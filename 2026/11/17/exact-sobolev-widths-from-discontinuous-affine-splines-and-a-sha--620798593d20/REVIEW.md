# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The one-cell sharp estimate follows from a Neumann cosine expansion, the exact normalized affine moment vector, even-mode comparison, and a strictly positive completed-square bound on odd modes. Scaling, orthogonal summation and continuity give both the arbitrary-mesh constant and all equality functions. An exact global cosine reaches the uniform full-norm bound, and the Neumann singular spectrum supplies the matching \(2N\)-width. The finite diagnostics are supplementary and do not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Yang–Zhang (2026), Theorems A and B and Section 5, identify the \(\nu=2\) asymptotically unexcluded case but do not state an exact finite-\(N\) width equality or a maximal-cell formula with all equality functions. Floater–Sande (2017) establishes different spline-space optimality in prescribed boundary-condition regimes; Pinkus (1985) concerns general optimal spline existence. Published investigations of \(L^\infty\) spline-projection stability do not imply a derivative-energy-to-\(L^2\) width identity. A classical equivalent sharp approximation theorem may nevertheless predate this result, and old full-text coverage is not complete.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The recent analysis isolates two degrees of local freedom as a critical asymptotic exception. The exact finite-dimensional identity settles the natural first discontinuous case for every mesh size and every even dimension, while the nonuniform formula explains why mesh size, rather than merely a global average, controls worst derivative-seminorm approximation. Equality classifications identify all worst inputs and connect approximation-theoretic optimality with Neumann spectral modes. This is not a renamed textbook Poincaré inequality: the second moment constraint, extremizer patching and full-width identity are essential.

The full-norm claim is not extended to uniqueness across nonuniform meshes. The original 1978 optimal-spline literature and other older equivalences remain priority risks. No independent verification is claimed.
