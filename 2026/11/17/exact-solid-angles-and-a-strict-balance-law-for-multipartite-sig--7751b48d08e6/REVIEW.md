# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is restricted to the full-dimensional cone cut out by positive pair-sum root inequalities between all different classes. Every admissible sign pattern has either no negatives or negatives confined to one class. Conditional on \(k\) negatives in one class and all outside positive, the event is that their absolute values are the \(k\) smallest among \(k+n-a\) independent continuous magnitudes; exact order-statistic symmetry produces the stated binomial formula. The comparison of independent uniform-cube and Gaussian coordinate probabilities is valid for these sign/rank inequalities, and the volume normalization is \(2^n\). For fixed dimension, a strictly convex one-variable tail function gives all majorization comparisons and equality cases. A geometric-series domination establishes the balanced limit, and exact binomial symmetry gives the concentrated asymptotic. A standalone standard-library exhaustive enumeration tests the formulas and monotonicity for small dimensions, without being substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
Beck and Hlavacek supply the general signed-poset and type-B chamber framework, but the inspected sections do not evaluate the complete multipartite positive-root cone's total solid angle or prove strict part-size majorization. Godland and Kabluchko evaluate individual Weyl-chamber conic intrinsic volumes, not this signed-poset union. A known complete two-level signed-chain volume theorem imposes two-sided absolute-value bounds and thus describes a different region. A known complete-multipartite Lipschitz-dual volume theorem imposes cross-class difference bounds and works in an anchored space of dimension one lower; its exact formula and strict imbalance theorem do not imply the new cone identity. The complete-split polar-volume theorem similarly concerns a different polytope and metric normalization. The two-class, sign-switchable classical order-cone angle is explicitly credited and excluded. A generic signed-poset chamber-count algorithm could recover an evaluation in principle; no source inspected contains this closed form plus the strict balance law. Risk: an unindexed signed-graph angle/chamber-number formula may imply part of the result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The spherical angle is a coordinate-independent geometric measure for a prescribed Euclidean realization and the cube-intersection volume is a natural signed-order-polytope invariant. Their unexpected equality after scaling, exact finite formula, strict majorization classification, and sharp contrast between exponentially and polynomially small extremal angles describe all fixed-class compositions, rather than an arbitrary small example or a reparameterization of the two-class case. In particular, the asymptotic separation is a concrete geometric phenomenon invisible in the general existence of a type-B chamber triangulation.

## Limits and closest literature

Proof of novelty is not possible by keyword search; earlier equivalent signed-graph enumerations remain a genuine priority risk.
