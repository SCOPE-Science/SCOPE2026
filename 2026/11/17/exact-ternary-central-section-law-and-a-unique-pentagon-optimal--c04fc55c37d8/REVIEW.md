# Review of the triangular-prism central-section law

## Correctness — PASS

A plane not containing a prism vertex has one section vertex for each cut prism edge. With the equilateral base centered at the origin, the horizontal vertex dot products sum to zero. Their absolute magnitudes are therefore \(b,a,a+b\), where \(0<b<a\) outside a measure-zero set. The vertical threshold produces section counts \(4,5,4,3\) in the four consecutive intervals. Haar measure makes the absolute vertical normal component uniform and independent of the horizontal azimuth. The exact inverse-sine primitive gives all three probabilities, and a strictly decreasing scalar function proves uniqueness of the pentagon optimum. The proof covers every positive height; the standalone checker independently tests exact sign cells and numerical integration, not infinitely many cases.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

The motivating 2026 source develops the random-direction width and brightness theory of convex polytopes, not the vertex count of central prism sections. The 2023/2024 Konstantinova–Shchur research explicitly investigates random section angles for regular triangular prisms, and the earlier Ohser–Mücklich paper investigates stochastic prism stereology. Their accessible abstracts do not establish this specific central-Haar, variable-aspect-ratio three-/four-/five-gon law or its unique maximum. The relevant full texts could not be inspected; this is an acknowledged historical-priority risk rather than evidence of non-coverage. Mathematical-record searches returned central-section probability results for different solids and observables, including rectangular boxes and icosahedra. None of the accessible statements imply the present formulas by a reduction or duality: a rectangular box has a centrally symmetric vertex set and a different possible section spectrum, while an icosahedron has fixed combinatorial and metric shape.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A complete section-type probability law for **every** aspect ratio of a natural five-faced solid is a reproducible geometric-probability invariant, of the same stereological kind as section-side-count statistics. The strict unique aspect ratio optimizing pentagonal sections is not merely a random example or a normalization reformulation. The result gives an elementary exact counterpart to numerical section-distribution work and shows that the optimal prism is markedly shorter than the uniform prism of height one. This is a narrow, concrete result; it does not claim broader extremal inequalities for all prisms.
