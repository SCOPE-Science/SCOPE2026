# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The conclusion concerns every labelled wheel with \(n\ge3\), and does not generalize a finite experiment. For a zero hub, rim-majority is the AND of the two rim neighbors; for a one hub, it is their OR. Composing the alternating-hub updates gives exactly \(x_i=x_i(x_{i-2}\vee x_{i+2})\). The hub switches exactly when the numbers of occupied rim sites and occupied skip-two edges lie strictly on opposite sides of half the rim length. For a stationary hub, the corresponding AND–AND or OR–OR relation forces each skip-two component to be monochromatic, producing two nonconstant cycles only for even rim length. The no-isolated-one cyclic-run enumeration is justified by marked-run counting and tested also at the exceptional two-site component. The two independent executable approaches agree for all rim lengths \(3\)–\(17\); the generating polynomial is checked separately by enumerating cyclic words of lengths \(2\)–\(13\). No claim about untested transient basins is made.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification
The nearest direct primary predecessor is Agur–Fraenkel–Klein (1988), which enumerates cyclic strings and fixed points of self-inclusive one-dimensional majority. Its model has no global hub, and its formulas do not impose the two strict global cardinality tests essential to the wheel orbit count. Goles–Olivos (1980) establishes general period-at-most-two behavior but, in available material, supplies no wheel-specific orbit census; the original full text could not be read and remains a priority risk. Turau (2022) establishes structural results on **trees**; Nakar–Ron (2022) addresses fixed-radius one-dimensional automata; Aledo et al. (2020) concerns MAX/MIN Boolean maps. Search results for the wheel, the alternative cone-on-cycle description, no-isolated-one runs, and two-cycle count values yielded no equivalent or stronger coverage. A failure to find a formula is not itself proof of priority. The mathematical nonimplication of the cited published claims is the basis for the qualified assessment.

## Mathematical value — PASS
An exact primitive-orbit census on the entire wheel family is a natural missing invariant of majority dynamics, particularly because a hub couples local rim updates to a **global** strict-majority test. The answer separates two different mechanisms (stationary versus alternating hub), demonstrates that temporal two-cycles proliferate exponentially on infinite subsequences even while their proportion among states vanishes exponentially, and supplies a finite integer-coefficient formula for every rim size. These are structural findings, not routine substitution into a general period-two theorem.

## Limitations and closest literature
The underlying cyclic-run composition method is elementary and its one-dimensional combinatorial setting is classical; no ownership of that general counting idea or of the period-two theorem is asserted. The exact application to wheel two-cycles is the claim. The 1980 primary threshold-function article was not available in full; an exact antecedent in unindexed sources remains possible. No independent verification of the proof has occurred.
