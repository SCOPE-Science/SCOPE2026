# Exponent-four Moore summands in the binary affine-cube translation quotient

## Finding
Let \(V=\mathbb F_2^3\). Form the rank-four affine-geometry matroid \(AG(3,2)\) on these eight points, and let \(K=IN(AG(3,2))\) be its simplicial independence complex. The additive translation group \(T=V\) acts on the labels. For the **ordinary geometric quotient** \(Y=|K|/T\), not a Borel quotient, the complete homotopy type is
\[
\boxed{Y\simeq M(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,2)\vee\bigvee^{3}M(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z,2).}
\]
Here \(M(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z,2)\) is the three-dimensional Moore space obtained by attaching a three-cell to \(S^2\) by a map of degree \(d\). In particular,
\[
\pi_1(Y)=1,\quad H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^3,\quad H_i(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(i\geq1,\ i\ne2),
\]
and \(H_0(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). The order-four torsion is noteworthy because every nonidentity element of \(T\) has order two. In comparison, the covering independence complex has integral homotopy type \(\bigvee^{21}S^3\) and therefore has no integral torsion.

## Assumptions and scope
Identify the vertices with the integers \(0,1,\ldots,7\), using their three binary digits. A nonempty subset is a face of \(K\) precisely when it has at most three points, or it has four points whose bitwise XOR is nonzero. In particular the 14 affine planes, each a four-subset of XOR zero, are exactly the omitted tetrahedra; the face vector in dimensions \(0,1,2,3\) is \((8,28,56,56)\). A translation \(t\in T\) maps vertex \(a\) to \(a\oplus t\). The action is simplicial and the quotient is taken after ordinary geometric realization. No condition of free action is imposed.

For a finite simplicial complex, write a *flag* for a strictly nested chain of nonempty faces. Regard each face as an eight-bit subset mask. Order flags first by dimension and then lexicographically by their sequence of masks; for an orbit, choose the lexicographically least translated sequence as representative. Orient each barycentric simplex by strictly increasing dimension of the faces in its flag. These conventions determine all the integer boundary matrices without arbitrary software choices.

## Proof
**The covering complex and fundamental group.** Because every two- and three-point subset is independent, \(K\) contains the complete two-skeleton of the seven-simplex, hence is simply connected. Its three-dimensional chain complex has torsion-free homology concentrated in degree three, of rank \(21\): an elementary proof is that the 14 missing affine-plane tetrahedral boundaries already lie in the span of the 56 present tetrahedral boundaries, using an outside vertex and the boundary of a formal four-simplex. Thus the latter boundary image has the same rank \(35\) as for the full three-skeleton, leaving kernel rank \(56-35=21\). Equivalently, this agrees with the known matroid-independence sphere-wedge results.

Each nonzero translation \(t\) fixes the midpoint of the edge \(\{0,t\}\) in \(|K|\): the translation interchanges the two endpoints, and every edge is present. The orbit-space theorem of Armstrong gives \(\pi_1(|K|/T)\cong T/N\), where \(N\) is the normal subgroup generated by elements having a fixed point, because \(|K|\) is simply connected and the finite simplicial action satisfies the theorem's hypotheses. Every element of \(T\) has a fixed point, so \(N=T\) and \(\pi_1(Y)=1\).

**Exact quotient chains.** Barycentrically subdivide \(K\). Because translations preserve the cardinality of every face, no group element permutes the vertices of any flag simplex nontrivially while fixing that simplex; in particular the oriented orbit flags form a valid integral quotient CW chain complex. The face flags have counts \((148,1176,2352,1344)\). Choosing least translated representatives gives quotient cell counts
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(22,147,294,168).
\]
These counts are checked independently by Burnside averaging over all eight translations. Each column of the boundary \(\partial_d\) is obtained from the alternating sum of its \(d+1\) deleted-subflag orbits. Direct exact integer multiplication confirms \(\partial_{d-1}\partial_d=0\) for \(d=2,3\).

Canceling a unit entry \(\pm1\) in adjacent differentials is an integral change of chain bases plus deletion of a contractible direct summand; the prescribed algorithm pivots on the pair minimizing the product of its current row and column nonzero counts, then lexicographically. The exact chain reduction uses \(21\) degree-one, \(126\) degree-two and \(164\) degree-three unit cancellations. It leaves a single zero-cell, no one-cells, four two-cells and four three-cells. In the deterministic remaining order, the differential \(\partial_3\) is
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}
-2&-2&4&-2\\
-2&2&0&-2\\
-2&2&-4&2\\
0&0&-4&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The greatest common divisors of all minors of sizes one through four are respectively
\[
2,\quad8,\quad32,\quad128.
\]
Thus the Smith invariant factors of \(D\) are exactly \(2,4,4,4\), and \(D\) has rank four over \(\mathbb Q\). Consequently \(H_3(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\), \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=\operatorname{coker}(D)\cong\mathbb Z/2\oplus(\mathbb Z/4)^3\), and \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\). This computation is a chain calculation; individual algebraic cancellations are not claimed to be geometric free-face collapses.

**Upgrading homology to homotopy.** The two-skeleton \(Y^{(2)}\) is simply connected because attaching cells of dimension three does not change \(\pi_1\). A simply connected two-dimensional CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres. Since \(Y\) has no cells above dimension three, its three-cell attaching maps become elements of the free abelian group \(\pi_2(\bigvee S^2)\), and are completely determined by the integral cellular boundary matrix. Changes of bases of the two-sphere homotopy group and of the attaching three-cells realize the integral Smith reduction. The rank-four full boundary and invariant factors \(2,4,4,4\) therefore give exactly the displayed wedge of four Moore spaces, with no residual sphere summand. This upgrades the verified integral homology calculation to the full homotopy type.

## Verification
The accompanying pure-Python `verify.py` enumerates all independent subsets, all flags of nonempty faces and all eight translations. It verifies face invariance, the Burnside orbit counts, each signed quotient boundary, both chain-complex identities, every integral unit-cancellation update, the final matrix, and the gcd of every minor of the remaining four-by-four matrix. It also checks the four fixed edges of each nonzero translation, which is the key geometric condition needed in the separate fundamental-group argument. Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3.8 or later; its expected standard output is included in `verification_output.txt`. The exact finite calculation does not substitute for the topological facts about simple connectivity and three-dimensional CW attachment maps, which are proved above.

## Relationship to prior work
Merino, Ramírez-Ibáñez and Rodríguez-Sánchez (first public arXiv version March 1, 2012) identify the eight-point binary affine matroid and its fourteen circuit-hyperplanes as the unique Steiner quadruple system \(S(3,4,8)\), and compute its Tutte polynomial. Ardila, Castillo and Samper (October 14, 2014) give general matroid-independence shellings. These establish the natural object and its ordinary topology, not this geometric translation orbit space. Moci and Pezzoli study automorphism representations on homology of matroids; a representation or rational invariant dimension alone does not determine the integral torsion of a nonfree ordinary orbit space. Existing exact cycle bases for the unquotiented eight-point matroid likewise do not furnish this quotient chain calculation. The present theorem concerns four explicit integral Moore summands, including elements of order four in homology despite an exponent-two symmetry group.

## Limitations
This determines the ordinary quotient for the particular eight-point binary affine matroid under its full translation subgroup, not under its whole affine automorphism group. It is not a theorem for higher-dimensional affine geometries or arbitrary translation subgroups, and does not describe canonical Moore-space generators or the full integral equivariant homology module. Independently indexed equivalent work and uninspected specialized literature remain publication-priority risks. No independent mathematical audit is represented.
