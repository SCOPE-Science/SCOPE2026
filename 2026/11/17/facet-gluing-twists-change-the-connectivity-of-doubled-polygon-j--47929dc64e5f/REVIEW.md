# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The four-step proof reconstructs the exact tetrahedral incidence definition of the double. It identifies the complete bipartite and categorical-product pieces, proves connectedness of each after excising one facet with explicit detours, traces all six port identifications under an arbitrary tetrahedron bijection, and derives the graph cycle ranks from verified vertex and edge counts. The hypotheses \(m,n\ge4\) and at least one odd are necessary to the stated proof. Independent facet-based enumeration reproduces both forms for sixteen parameter pairs and exhausts twenty-four gluing bijections for the \((4,5)\) example. The verification runs establish finite checks; the symbolic argument proves the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The originating work on simplicial doubles proves a general connectivity *lower bound*, not the gluing-sensitive connected-sum dichotomy. The closest previously established polygon-join double classification treats a single, unpunctured factor and stops before facet deletion and cross-identification. Published comparisons covering doubles of cyclic four-polytope boundaries, iterated simplicial doubles, and joined simplicial spheres do not state a facet-gluing transition for two fixed three-sphere summands. This assessment is based on full material inspection of the originating paper, claim-level comparison with earlier polygon-join results, and targeted literature/database searches; it cannot rule out an unindexed or differently named equivalent result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result is a structural counterexample to an apparently natural expectation that the double's connectedness should depend only on the connected-sum factors, their PL homeomorphism types or the resulting face vector. It gives a uniform, nonconstant phenomenon with exact *integral homotopy types* for both gluing classes, and shows how the stabilizer of a tetrahedral perfect matching governs the transition. It can serve as a local test case for compositional or functorial theories of simplicial doubling.

## Scope
No general arbitrary-connected-sum functoriality theorem, no even-even classification, and no independent mathematical validation is asserted.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
