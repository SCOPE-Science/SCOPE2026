# Scientific review: finite polylogarithmic hyperbolic polygon mean areas

## Correctness

PASS. The proof uses the conformal disk's exact angle relation, the Dirichlet law for circular spacings, an absolutely convergent Fourier logarithm, finite integration-by-parts recurrence, and Gauss–Bonnet. The Klein metric supplies an independent monotonicity argument. The exact small-radius limiting checks and large-radius polylog expansions are consistent; the included standalone checker independently matches triangle angles from the hyperbolic law of cosines and tests higher polygon orders against the exact expectation. Numerical checks corroborate but are not the proof.

## Originality

PASS, subject to historical priority. Bao–Chen (2014) is about deterministic area formulas, not random cyclic polygon expectations. Horváth (2014) concerns triangle centers. A related reported result for spherical fixed-latitude random triangles has a negative-argument dilogarithm but does not establish a hyperbolic result, much less the arbitrary-vertex finite polylogarithm identity. The closest existing hyperbolic-circle work concerns acuteness and polygon-area growing-sample crossover, not a fixed-sample all-order mean formula. A more general constant-curvature expected-area identity might nevertheless subsume this result and remains the principal historical risk. No claim of priority proof is made.

## Value

PASS. The expected intrinsic area of a random inscribed polygon is a canonical geometric statistic. The result supplies an exact finite formula for every fixed vertex count, proves strict radius identifiability, and extracts the sharp logarithmically enhanced ideal-polygon correction. This is not merely a renamed or parameter-specialized identity, since the same calculation exposes an all-order finite even-polylogarithm structure absent from the deterministic source.

## Limitations and sources

The conclusion assumes curvature \(-1\), independent arclength-uniform sampling, fixed \(n\), and a common geodesic circle. The proof does not imply arbitrary sampling laws, complete distributions, or joint large-\(n\) convergence. Closest directly inspected primary articles: Bao and Chen, Journal of Mathematics (2014), DOI 10.1155/2014/838497; Á. Horváth, arXiv:1410.6735v1 (2014). Related published research records on spherical polygon mean areas and hyperbolic-circle acuteness were examined by title/summary only, not full text; comparisons with them retain residual uncertainty.
