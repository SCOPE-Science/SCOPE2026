# Five-primary Moore homotopy in the diagonal symmetric quotient of a chessboard complex

## Finding
Let \(\Delta_{5,5}\) be the abstract simplicial complex with vertices \((i,j)\in[5]\times[5]\), whose simplices are nonattacking rook placements: in each simplex no first coordinate and no second coordinate occurs twice. The symmetric group \(S_5\) acts **diagonally** by simultaneous relabeling, \(\sigma(i,j)=(\sigma(i),\sigma(j))\). The ordinary geometric orbit space satisfies
\[
\boxed{|\Delta_{5,5}|/S_5\simeq M(\mathbb Z/5,2).}
\]
Here \(M(\mathbb Z/5,2)=S^2\cup_{5}e^3\) is the simply connected Moore space obtained by attaching a 3-cell to a 2-sphere using a map of degree five. In particular,
\[
\pi_1(|\Delta_{5,5}|/S_5)=0,\qquad
\widetilde H_i(|\Delta_{5,5}|/S_5;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z/5,&i=2,\\0,&i\ne2.\end{cases}
\]
Thus simultaneous relabeling produces integral five-torsion in degree two. The result concerns the **full** five-by-five board, including diagonal squares, under the **diagonal** permutation group; it is not the quotient under independent row and column permutations, a cyclic column shift, or board transposition.

## Assumptions and scope
The complexes are finite and geometrically realized with their ordinary topology; quotienting is by the actual action on points, not merely by identifying vertices. All homology groups are integral unless stated otherwise. Faces of the first barycentric subdivision correspond to strict chains of nonempty rook placements. Since simultaneous relabelings preserve the number of rooks, they cannot permute vertices within any barycentric simplex. Consequently the quotient of the barycentric subdivision is an ordinary finite simplicial cell complex with cells given by group-orbits of strict face chains and the signed simplicial boundary induced by deleting chain members. Distinct quotient cells may have the same vertex-orbit data, which is why their full chain orbits, rather than just their dimension sequences, are enumerated.

## Proof
**1. Simple connectivity.** The classical Björner–Lovász–Vrećica–Živaljević connectivity theorem gives \(\Delta_{m,n}\) at least \((\nu-2)\)-connected, where \(\nu=\min\{m,n,\lfloor(m+n+1)/3\rfloor\}\). Here \(\nu=3\), so \(|\Delta_{5,5}|\) is simply connected. Armstrong's fixed-point theorem for the fundamental group of a simplicial orbit space identifies the fundamental group of the quotient with \(S_5/N\), where \(N\) is the normal subgroup generated by stabilizers of points. Every transposition fixes a diagonal-square vertex \((i,i)\) with \(i\) outside its support. Since transpositions generate \(S_5\), \(N=S_5\), proving \(\pi_1=0\).

**2. Exact quotient-chain model.** Represent each rook placement as a finite set of ordered pairs \((i,j)\) with no repeated first or second coordinate. For each \(k=1,\ldots,5\), enumerate all choices of \(k\) first coordinates and injective choices of \(k\) second coordinates. Each strict chain of nonempty placements is generated by a terminal placement \(F\), an ordering of its rooks, and a strictly increasing sequence of positive prefix cardinalities. Compute the orbit of each such chain under all \(120\) simultaneous permutations and retain one basis element for each orbit. For a length-\(d+1\) chain \(F_0\subset\cdots\subset F_d\), define
\[
\partial_d[F_0<\cdots<F_d]=\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^j[F_0<\cdots<\widehat{F_j}<\cdots<F_d].
\]
Orbits of chains are oriented by increasing face cardinality, so the formula is the genuine quotient cellular differential, with signs preserved by the group. The numbers of quotient cells in degrees zero through four are exactly
\[
(35,199,429,384,120).
\]
An independent Burnside computation gives the same five values. For a permutation \(\sigma\) stabilizing a terminal matching \(F\), let \(c_\sigma(F)\) be its number of cycles on the rooks of \(F\). The number of \(\sigma\)-fixed strict chains of length \(q\) ending in \(F\) is \(q!\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}c_\sigma(F)\\q\end{smallmatrix}\right\}\), since their successive differences must partition those cycles into \(q\) nonempty ordered stages. Averaging over all permutations checks the orbit counts independently of the orbit representatives.

**3. Integral chain contraction.** The accompanying `reduction_certificate.json` records \(582\) explicit elementary unit cancellations, replayed by the standalone `verify.py`. At each listed cancellation, the coefficient of a \((d-1)\)-cell \(a\) in the boundary of a \(d\)-cell \(b\) is \(u=\pm1\). For any other \(d\)-cell \(x\), replace its boundary column by
\[
\partial x\longmapsto\partial x-\frac{[a]\partial x}{u}\,\partial b.
\]
Delete the paired row \(a\), column \(b\), and their incident entries in adjacent differentials. These are integral unimodular splittings of a contractible two-term chain summand; hence they preserve integral homology, including all torsion. The verifier reconstructs the entire orbit complex directly from definitions, checks \(\partial^2=0\), validates every integer unit pivot and rechecks the final differentials. Exactly one generator survives in dimension zero, none in dimension one, one in dimension two, one in dimension three and none in dimension four. The sole nonzero residual differential sends the degree-three generator to **five times** the degree-two generator. Therefore \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\), \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z/5\), and all remaining integral homology vanishes. This is an exhaustive finite calculation for the fixed five-by-five complex, not numerical extrapolation.

**4. From homology to homotopy.** By step 1, the quotient \(X\) is simply connected. The degree-two Hurewicz map is an isomorphism \(\pi_2(X)\cong H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/5\). Choose a sphere map representing a generator; composing it with a degree-five self-map of \(S^2\) is nullhomotopic, so it extends over the three-cell of \(M(\mathbb Z/5,2)\). The induced map \(M(\mathbb Z/5,2)\to X\) is an isomorphism on every integral homology group. Both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, so the homological Whitehead theorem makes this map a homotopy equivalence.

## Verification
With Python 3, from the directory holding the two verification artifacts, run `python3 verify.py`. It requires only the standard library. The program reconstructs all rook placements, the diagonal action, orbit cells and boundary operators. In a separate check it uses Burnside's lemma and Stirling numbers to count all fixed chains, and verifies the same orbit vector independently. It replays all \(582\) recorded unit cancellations from the original integer matrices and checks the remaining differential has coefficient exactly five. The recorded execution ended with four `PASS` lines. No part of the argument depends on a floating-point approximation or on a calculation in a single finite characteristic. The topological steps use the stated classical connectivity, orbit-fundamental-group and homological Whitehead theorems.

## Relationship to prior work
Björner, Lovász, Vrećica and Živaljević established the connectivity of chessboard complexes, but not this diagonal-orbit homotopy classification. Shareshian and Wachs compute integral homology of **unquotiented** chessboard complexes and of diagonal-deleted boards, notably classical three-primary torsion, but their theorems do not identify the five-primary homology of this particular simultaneous-relabeling quotient. Previously published computations of the five-by-five **diagonal-deleted** chessboard under simultaneous label permutations treat a different complex; the full-board formula cannot be inferred from that calculation. Likewise, a published calculation of the **free cyclic column-shift quotient** concerns a different subgroup, has a nontrivial fundamental group, and does not imply a simply connected Moore type for the diagonal symmetric quotient. The known rational representations for independent row/column actions do not determine integral group-order torsion in this geometric orbit space. These comparisons are mathematical distinctions, not claims of exhaustive global priority.

## Limitations
The theorem is for exactly five symbols; no all-size formula or homeomorphism to a particular triangulation of a Moore space is claimed. The chain-cancellation certificate establishes chain homotopy and integral homology; the **separate** topological argument establishes the homotopy equivalence. The work has no independent mathematical audit. A relevant historical thesis on the original group-action interpretation of chessboard complexes and unindexed equivalent computations remain priority risks.
