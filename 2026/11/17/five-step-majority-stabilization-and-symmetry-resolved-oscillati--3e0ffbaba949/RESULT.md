# Five-step majority stabilization and symmetry-resolved oscillations on the truncated octahedron

## Finding
Let \(G\) be the 24-vertex truncated-octahedral graph, equivalently the four-letter permutohedron: its vertices are permutations of \((0,1,2,3)\), with an edge when two adjacent positions are interchanged. Let \(F\) be synchronous strict open-neighbor binary majority: every vertex takes the majority of its three neighbors. Then
\[
F^7=F^5,\qquad F^6\ne F^4.
\]
There are exactly \(208\) fixed configurations and \(43{,}056\) configurations of least temporal period two, hence \(21{,}528\) primitive two-cycle orbits. Under the full 48-element graph-automorphism group, the fixed configurations form exactly 20 classes and the primitive two-cycle orbits form exactly 717 classes. The smallest nonempty proper fixed-state support has four vertices: the six such supports are precisely the six square faces.

For every starting state, its *entry time* is the least \(t\ge0\) such that \(F^{t+2}(x)=F^t(x)\). The complete labelled entry-time distribution is:

| Entry time \(t\) | Eventually fixed | Eventually period two |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 208 | 43,056 |
| 1 | 27,808 | 3,314,528 |
| 2 | 185,520 | 8,819,280 |
| 3 | 137,856 | 3,471,744 |
| 4 | 35,424 | 350,880 |
| 5 | 37,728 | 353,184 |
| **Total** | **424,544** | **16,352,672** |

Here "eventually fixed" and "eventually period two" refer to the least eventual period; every one of the \(2^{24}=16{,}777{,}216\) initial configurations is counted once. For the geometric classes, the number of fixed-point classes of sizes \(1,3,4,6,8,12,24\) is respectively \(2,2,2,2,3,5,4\). The number of two-cycle classes of sizes \(1,3,4,6,8,12,24,48\) is respectively \(1,9,8,22,18,72,327,260\); sizes refer to graph-symmetry orbits of *unoriented temporal cycles*, with temporal phase not counted separately.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is the simple cubic undirected Cayley graph of \(S_4\) generated by the three adjacent-position transpositions. Vertices and configurations are labelled; no spatial quotient is taken in the state counts. Each site has three neighbors, so there are no voting ties and no self-loops. All updates are simultaneous. The spatial automorphism group may act on the collection of temporal cycles; it does not change temporal periods. The assertion is only about this graph, not the general sequence of permutohedra.

## Proof
**1. Explicit graph and update.** List the 24 permutations lexicographically, indexed \(0,\ldots,23\). For permutation \(p\), let \(p^{(j)}\) exchange positions \(j\) and \(j+1\), for \(j=0,1,2\). Encode a state by a 24-bit nonnegative integer \(x\). Its output bit at \(p\) is one precisely if at least two of the three input bits at \(p^{(0)},p^{(1)},p^{(2)}\) are one. The accompanying C++ verifier enumerates every integer from zero through \(2^{24}-1\) and applies this rule, with no sampling.

**2. Independent twelve-bit reduction.** Adjacent transpositions reverse permutation parity, so the graph has two parts \(L,R\), each of size twelve. Write a state as \((a,b)\) with \(a\in\{0,1\}^{L}\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}^{R}\). Denote the two one-step majority maps by \(f:\{0,1\}^R\to\{0,1\}^L\) and \(g:\{0,1\}^L\to\{0,1\}^R\). Then
\[
F(a,b)=(f(b),g(a)),\quad F^2(a,b)=(P(a),Q(b)),\quad P=f\circ g,\quad Q=g\circ f.
\]
Both \(P\) and \(Q\) act on exactly \(4096\) states. Direct exhaustive construction finds that their respective shortest fixed-entry-time distributions for \(k=0,1,2,3\) are identically \((208,3312,528,48)\); there are no longer tails and no nontrivial cycles. In particular every orbit of \(F\) ultimately has period at most two.

For a state \(a\) of \(P\), define \(u(a)\) as the least \(k\ge0\) for which \(P^{k+1}(a)=P^k(a)\), and \(r(a)\) as the least \(k\ge0\) for which \(g(P^{k+1}(a))=g(P^k(a))\). Define \(v(b)\) and \(s(b)\) analogously for \(Q\) and \(f\). The two-step recurrence condition first holds at the smaller of the earliest even and odd times:
\[
d(a,b)=\min\bigl\{2\max(u(a),v(b)),\ 1+2\max(r(a),s(b))\bigr\}.
\]
Indeed the even and odd states are respectively \((P^k(a),Q^k(b))\) and \((f(Q^k(b)),g(P^k(a)))\); equality of such a state with its successor two steps later gives the formula. Once equality occurs it persists. The endpoint on the even subsequence is \((a_*,b_*)=(P^{u(a)}(a),Q^{v(b)}(b))\), and the ultimate period is one exactly when \(f(b_*)=a_*\) and \(g(a_*)=b_*\). Summing these explicit tests over all \(4096^2\) pairs gives the six rows of the finding table. The independently generated Cartesian-coordinate verifier carries out this calculation and asserts an explicit graph isomorphism to the permutation model.

The count has positive mass at entry time five, and none later. A sharpness witness in lexicographic permutation indexing is \(x=9762\), whose successive states from times zero to seven, in hexadecimal, are \(0x2622,0x898,0x422,0x90,0x2,0x0,0x0,0x0\). Thus the least entry time is five, proving both iterate statements.

**3. Recurrent and geometric counts.** The immediate periodic states are those for which \(F^2(x)=x\); the fixed states additionally satisfy \(F(x)=x\). The resulting numbers are \(43{,}264\) immediate period-at-most-two configurations and \(208\) fixed ones, yielding \((43{,}264-208)/2=21{,}528\) distinct primitive temporal cycles. Every spatial automorphism sends a temporal cycle to a temporal cycle. The 24 relabellings of permutation values and the reversal of positions give 48 distinct graph automorphisms. At each vertex exactly two incident edges lie on one four-cycle and the third on none, so a graph automorphism is determined by the target of one vertex and at most one choice to interchange the two square edges; hence the full group has at most \(24\cdot2=48\) elements. The verifier explicitly checks these 48 edge-preserving permutations, forms their spatial orbits on fixed states and their spatial orbits on two-element temporal cycles, and counts the orbit-size distributions shown above. Their weighted sums are \(208\) and \(21{,}528\), respectively.

**4. Minimal nonuniform fixed supports.** On any cubic graph, a binary state is fixed under open-neighbor majority if and only if every vertex has at most one neighbor of the opposite color, i.e., its bichromatic edges form a matching. Therefore a proper nonempty one-color support must induce minimum degree at least two, as must its complement. The permutohedral graph is triangle-free, so any such support has at least four vertices. At size four it must be an induced four-cycle. The only four-cycles are the six cosets of the commuting adjacent swaps at positions zero and two. Each has exactly four distinct external neighbors, so its indicator is fixed. Thus the six square-face indicators are exactly the six proper fixed states of weight four.

## Verification
The full functional graph is enumerated by `majority_permutohedron.cpp`; a separately written verifier `verify_factorized.py` uses the Cartesian realization by all permutations of \((0,\pm1,\pm2)\), edges of squared Euclidean length two, a hardcoded 24-vertex isomorphism certificate, and the two 12-bit maps. Its counts agree exactly, including all six entry-time bins. The C++ checker also asserts that all claimed spatial symmetries preserve every edge and that all 48 are distinct. The captured outputs are `census.jsonl` and `factorized_output.json`. For the C++ program, compile with `g++ -O3 -std=c++17 majority_permutohedron.cpp -o verify_majority` and execute `./verify_majority`; for the independent program run `python3 verify_factorized.py`. Finite enumeration is exhaustive here because the entire state space is finite and both programs visit every relevant state; no statistical extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Goles and Olivos proved the general eventual-period-at-most-two theorem for symmetric threshold networks. Benjamini, Chan, O'Donnell, Tamuz, and Tan state this theorem and study majority dynamics on infinite transitive and random graphs. Kaaser, Mallmann-Trenn, and Natale study general voting-time bounds rather than the exact 24-vertex permutohedral census. Collares, Erde, Geisler, and Kang study *monotone* majority bootstrap percolation on high-dimensional permutohedra, a different rule in which infected vertices never revert. Earlier exact state censuses for the Heawood graph, dodecahedral graph, Clebsch graph, and cube concern different graphs; the permutohedral fixed-state and period-two counts do not follow from those graph-specific numbers. Results on perfect matchings of the four-letter permutohedron concern edge matchings, not the present vertex-majority fixed configurations or their transients.

## Limitations
The result determines all entrance-time populations and geometric classes but not the individual attraction-basin sizes of each of the 21,528 temporal two-cycle orbits. The result does not extend by itself to higher-order permutohedra, asynchronous majority, ties, or weighted votes. The general period-two property is prior work. No claim of absolute historical novelty or independent mathematical certification is made; older or unindexed graph-specific enumerations may exist.
