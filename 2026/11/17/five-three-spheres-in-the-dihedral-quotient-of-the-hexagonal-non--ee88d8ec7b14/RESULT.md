# Five three-spheres in the dihedral quotient of the hexagonal noncrossing-partition complex

## Finding

Let \(\mathrm{NC}_6\) be the lattice of noncrossing set partitions of six points in cyclic order, and let \(K=\Delta(\overline{\mathrm{NC}}_6)\) be the order complex obtained by deleting the discrete and indiscrete partitions. Let \(D_6\) act by all rotations and reflections of the six-point circle. Then the **ordinary geometric orbit space** satisfies

\[
\boxed{\,|K|/D_6\simeq\bigvee^{5}S^3\,}.
\]

In particular, \(\pi_1(|K|/D_6)=1\), its reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^5\) in degree three and vanishes otherwise. This is a homotopy classification, not a claim of homeomorphism or equivariant homotopy equivalence.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground set is \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\) with its fixed cyclic order. A partition is *noncrossing* when no \(a<b<c<d\) have \(a,c\) in one block and \(b,d\) in another. Ordered partitions are compared by refinement. A vertex of \(K\) is a proper noncrossing partition; a \(k\)-simplex is a chain of \(k+1\) distinct comparable proper partitions. Write \(r(i)=i+1\pmod 6\) and \(s(i)=-i\pmod 6\); these generate the indicated order-twelve dihedral group. The quotient is the topological orbit space of the geometric realization, **not** the clique complex of a quotient graph or an orbifold quotient with stabilizer weights.

Because each group element preserves partition rank, no nonidentity group element can permute the distinct vertices of any simplex while preserving that simplex setwise. Accordingly, the quotient of the simplicial realization has an ordinary oriented CW structure with one cell for each dihedral orbit of chains. Rank-increasing order gives compatible orientations on all representatives.

## Proof

**1. Exact combinatorial chains and incidence maps.** There are \(132\) noncrossing partitions of six elements. Removing the two extremal partitions leaves \(130\) vertices, distributed by ranks one through four as \((15,50,50,15)\). Exhaustively generating every strict chain gives \(4621\) nonempty simplices. For each chain \(c=(v_0<\cdots<v_k)\), take the lexicographically least image under the twelve transformations \(i\mapsto a\pm i\pmod 6\). The numbers of quotient cells in dimensions zero through three are

\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(22,114,204,116).
\]

Burnside counts on the complete sets of ordered chains reproduce these four numbers independently of orbit-canonicalization. For a representative chain, the quotient cellular differential is

\[
\partial_k[v_0<\cdots<v_k]=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\,[v_0<\cdots<\widehat{v_j}<\cdots<v_k],
\]

where each face is replaced by its orbit representative. There are no additional orientation signs because the action preserves ranks and therefore chain positions. The computed integer matrices satisfy \(\partial_{k-1}\partial_k=0\) identically for \(k=2,3\).

**2. Integral homology.** Exact integer elimination using only boundary entries \(\pm1\) successively cancels \(21\), \(93\), and \(111\) contractible algebraic two-term summands in degrees \(1\), \(2\), and \(3\), respectively. Each cancellation is an explicit unimodular change of bases followed by deletion of a unit differential block; the standalone checker updates neighboring matrices and verifies that no entries remain. Thus the reduced chain complex has ranks

\[
(1,0,0,5)
\]

in degrees zero through three and all zero differentials. Consequently the integral homology is \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\), \(H_1=H_2=0\), \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z^5\), and \(H_j=0\) for \(j>3\). These *algebraic* cancellations certify homology; they are not asserted to be elementary simplicial collapses or to prove homotopy type without the following argument.

**3. Simple connectivity.** The proper-part order complex of a noncrossing partition lattice is shellable and has the homotopy type of a wedge of three-dimensional spheres; in this six-point case its number of spheres is the fifth Catalan number, \(42\). Hence \(|K|\) is simply connected. For \(s_0(i)=-i\), the partition whose only nonsingleton block is \(\{1,5\}\) is a fixed vertex. For \(s_1(i)=1-i\), the corresponding partition with sole nonsingleton block \(\{0,1\}\) is a fixed vertex. The two reflections generate \(D_6\), as their product is a one-step rotation. Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem applies to the finite group acting on the simply connected, locally contractible polyhedron \(|K|\): the quotient fundamental group is \(D_6\) modulo the normal subgroup generated by stabilizer elements. Both generating reflections lie in this subgroup, so \(\pi_1(|K|/D_6)=1\).

**4. Homotopy type.** By simple connectivity and \(H_2=0\), the degree-two Hurewicz map identifies \(\pi_2\) with \(H_2\) and shows \(\pi_2=0\). The space is therefore two-connected. Hurewicz then gives \(\pi_3\cong H_3\cong\mathbb Z^5\). Choose five maps from \(S^3\) whose Hurewicz classes form an integral basis. The induced map \(\bigvee^5 S^3\to |K|/D_6\) is an integral homology isomorphism in every degree. The homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected CW complexes makes it a homotopy equivalence.

## Verification

Run the standalone, dependency-free file `verify_nc6.py` with `python3 verify_nc6.py` (Python 3 standard library only). It independently enumerates all noncrossing partitions and refinement chains; constructs the twelve permutations; computes actual orbit incidences; verifies Burnside counts and both consecutive boundary compositions; performs exact integral unit-pivot chain cancellation; and checks the existence of fixed proper-part vertices in both reflection classes. The expected final lines are `remaining chain modules in degrees 0 to 3: (1, 0, 0, 5)`, `all remaining differentials: zero`, `reflection classes: invariant partition vertices found`, and `PASS`. Assertions terminate execution if any stated check fails. The qualitative uses of shellability, Armstrong's theorem, Hurewicz and Whitehead are mathematical deductions explained in the proof, not outputs of the program.

## Relationship to prior work

Callan–Smiley's 2005 study gives exact *orbit counts* of noncrossing partitions. Its table records \(28\) cyclic orbits versus \(24\) dihedral orbits at six labels, and no discrepancy at sizes at most five. Deleting both bounds yields the independently computed \(26\) and \(22\) proper-part vertex orbits. This makes the hexagon the first naturally distinguished size where reflection genuinely identifies additional cyclic classes. Neither the published orbit counts nor their Burnside proof determines the quotient *order complex's* edges, higher cells, integral homology or homotopy type.

Athanasiadis–Brady–Watt prove EL-shellability of noncrossing partition lattices. That theorem supplies the connectivity of the labeled complex, but does not identify the homotopy type of a finite-group quotient. Armstrong's theorem determines the fundamental group once stabilizers are known, not the five higher-dimensional generators. The present statement combines the exact full-dihedral cellular computation with those topological theorems.

Related cyclic-only and seven-point symmetry-quotient calculations concern different quotient groups or different point sets, and their numerical homotopy types do not imply this six-point full-dihedral classification.

## Limitations

The result concerns precisely the ordinary geometric quotient of the proper noncrossing partition order complex on six cyclic labels. No assertion is made for other polygon sizes, for generalized Coxeter noncrossing lattices, for quotient posets, or for equivariant splitting. The algebraic cancellation routine computes chain homology only; its promotion to homotopy type requires the independent simple-connectivity and Whitehead argument above. Literature coverage cannot rule out unindexed or inaccessible prior equivalent results; no independent mathematical audit is represented here.
