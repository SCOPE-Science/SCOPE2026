# Scientific review: fourth-order four-cap coverage onset

## Correctness — PASS

The event is exactly origin-centered ball containment in a random inscribed tetrahedron. The unique optimizing shape is the regular inscribed tetrahedron, and the rigid motions have been quotiented out before the five-dimensional local integration. An explicit nonsingular chart, its symbolic Jacobian, and a transverse disphenoid Hessian establish the volume coefficient. The four facet constraints imply the required anisotropic localization of three height variables at order \(\delta\) and two deformation variables at order \(\sqrt\delta\). Dominated convergence gives the quartic coefficient, the Beta radial law, and the independent Dirichlet slack ratios. The checker repeats the critical Jacobian algebra and independently samples the original random geometric event. A finite sample is corroboration only; the proof is analytic.

## Originality — PASS with historical limitations

Finch, *Covering a Sphere with Four Random Circular Caps* (arXiv:1502.06931v1, 2015), poses the intermediate-radius probability problem and develops bounds without a formula close to the optimal tetrahedral threshold. The new result gives a sharp endpoint asymptotic and a joint rare-event geometry law, not the full distribution. Bürgisser–Cucker–Lotz (arXiv:0712.2816, 2007) derive an exact law at hemisphere size and beyond, and bounds below it, without this endpoint result. Brauchart et al. (arXiv:1512.07470, 2015) principally study the large-point-number regime. Published research-record searches for this fourth-order coefficient, its equivalent inscribed-tetrahedron formulation and Beta–Dirichlet scaling found no identical statement. General asymptotic techniques might imply the result, and early spherical-coverage works may contain unexamined special cases. Historical priority has not received an independent review.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The fixed-four-cap problem was explicitly highlighted as open for intermediate cap radii; previous lower estimates did not provide a nonzero lower estimate close to the optimal threshold. A sharp positive leading coefficient identifies the exponent and probability scale immediately above the tetrahedral optimum. The Beta–Dirichlet limit adds a structural and testable description of the rare covering configurations rather than another numerical approximation to the aggregate probability.

## Limits of review

The exact four-cap coverage function away from the endpoint, higher-order terms, and analogous formulas for other numbers of caps are not established. The archival comparison covers key available primary works but is not exhaustive. Numerical checks do not substitute for mathematical proof or historical priority verification.
