# Fourth-period reentrance and a renewal zeta for cyclic Rule 22

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(F_n\) be synchronous elementary cellular automaton Rule 22 on indexed binary configurations on the cyclic ring of length \(n\). Let \(C_4(n)\) be the number of **primitive temporal four-cycle orbits** of \(F_n\): spatial translations of an orbit are counted separately whenever they are different labelled configurations.

Set
\[
P(z)=1-z^8-2z^9-3z^{10}-2z^{11}-z^{12}.
\]
Then the complete all-circumference census is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle \sum_{n\ge1}C_4(n)z^n=-\frac{zP'(z)}{2P(z)}.}
\]
In particular, primitive temporal four-cycles exist **exactly** when
\[
\boxed{n\in\{8,9,10,11,12\}\ \cup\ \{n:n\ge16\}.}
\]
Thus the period-four phenomenon appears at circumference eight, disappears at circumferences thirteen, fourteen and fifteen, and is present at **every** circumference of at least sixteen. The growth of the exact orbit population is
\[
C_4(n)\sim\tfrac12\lambda^n,\qquad
\lambda=1.249851588864\ldots,
\]
where \(\lambda\) is the unique positive root of
\[
x^{12}-x^4-2x^3-3x^2-2x-1=0.
\]
The first nonzero counts are

| Circumference \(n\) | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 16 | 17 | 18 | 19 | 20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Four-cycle orbits \(C_4(n)\) | 4 | 9 | 15 | 11 | 6 | 4 | 17 | 45 | 76 | 95 |

## Assumptions and scope

The local map produces one exactly for neighborhoods \(001,010,100\), and zero for the other five binary triples. The sites are indexed modulo \(n\); the update is simultaneous and uses the previous time row only. A primitive temporal four-cycle consists of four distinct configurations \(x,F_nx,F_n^2x,F_n^3x\), with \(F_n^4x=x\). Neither transient attraction, other temporal periods, nor the nonspatially periodic infinite lattice is classified here.

## Proof

For a positive temporal height \(t\), encode one spatial column of a temporally \(t\)-periodic space-time diagram by an integer \(a\in\{0,\ldots,2^t-1\}\), with \(a_j\) its bit at time \(j\). Construct a directed graph \(D_t\) with vertices \((a,b)\) and an edge
\[
(a,b)\longrightarrow (b,c)
\]
precisely if
\[
\forall j\in\mathbb Z/t\mathbb Z:\quad
f(a_j,b_j,c_j)=b_{j+1},
\]
where \(f\) is the Rule 22 local map. A closed walk of spatial length \(n\), with its starting vertex distinguished, is equivalent to a labelled cyclic configuration fixed by \(F_n^t\): the temporal columns reconstruct the entire space-time diagram uniquely. Therefore \(\operatorname{Fix}(F_n^t)\) has cardinality \(\operatorname{tr}(D_t^n)\), where the graph symbol denotes its adjacency matrix.

An exact local-edge decomposition gives the cyclic strongly connected components of \(D_2\) of sizes \(4,2,1\), and those of \(D_4\) of sizes \(18,18,4,2,1\). Every other vertex lies outside a directed cycle. The small cyclic components of \(D_4\) are exactly the doubled-time embeddings of the three cyclic components of \(D_2\): the height-two column \(a\) becomes the height-four column \(a+4a\). One-time-step rotation of each four-bit column exchanges the two eighteen-vertex components, giving isomorphic adjacency matrices. Let \(A\) denote the adjacency matrix of either eighteen-vertex component. Removing the states of temporal period dividing two leaves exactly twice its closed-walk count, so
\[
4C_4(n)=\#\operatorname{Fix}(F_n^4)-\#\operatorname{Fix}(F_n^2)=2\operatorname{tr}(A^n).
\]

Here is a complete, checkable description of one eighteen-vertex component. Its vertices are the left entries in the following adjacency table; each row lists its outgoing vertices (integer pairs encode four-bit temporal columns):

```text
(0,1): (1,9)
(0,4): (4,6)
(0,6): (6,5)
(0,9): (9,5)
(1,0): (0,1)
(1,1): (1,9)
(1,9): (9,5)
(4,0): (0,4)
(4,4): (4,6)
(4,6): (6,5)
(5,6): (6,0) (6,4)
(5,9): (9,0) (9,1)
(6,0): (0,6)
(6,4): (4,0) (4,4)
(6,5): (5,9)
(9,0): (0,9)
(9,1): (1,0) (1,1)
(9,5): (5,6)
```

The nine directed simple cycles of this graph have length multiplicities \(1,2,3,2,1\) at lengths \(8,9,10,11,12\), respectively, and **every one passes through all four vertices** \((5,6),(5,9),(6,5),(9,5)\). This finite graph statement is directly reproducible by `certificate.py`, which regenerates the edges from the local truth table, enumerates every simple cycle, and checks the common vertices.

In the determinant expansion of \(\det(I-zA)\), a nonconstant term corresponds to a vertex-disjoint collection of directed simple cycles. Since every two simple cycles intersect, such a collection contains at most one cycle. Thus
\[
\det(I-zA)=1-z^8-2z^9-3z^{10}-2z^{11}-z^{12}=P(z).
\]
The formal identity \(-z\frac{d}{dz}\log\det(I-zA)=\sum_{n\ge1}\operatorname{tr}(A^n)z^n\) proves the generating function.

Every closed walk decomposes into directed simple cycles, hence its length belongs to the additive semigroup generated by \(8,9,10,11,12\). Conversely all nine simple cycles share a vertex, so concatenating their representatives realizes every length in that semigroup. That semigroup consists exactly of \(0\), the five lengths \(8\) through \(12\), and all integers at least \(16\): two generators already cover each length from \(16\) through \(24\), and adding an eight-cycle covers the remaining integers inductively. This proves the sharp gap and the all-large-length existence claim without relying on bounded computation.

Finally, the eighteen-vertex component is strongly connected, and its closed-walk lengths include both eight and nine; thus its adjacency matrix is primitive. Perron–Frobenius theory yields a simple strictly dominant positive eigenvalue \(\lambda\), with \(\operatorname{tr}(A^n)\sim\lambda^n\). The determinant formula identifies \(\lambda\) with the unique positive root of the displayed degree-twelve equation, proving the stated asymptotic and leading coefficient.

## Verification

`certificate.py` independently constructs the temporal height-one, -two, and -four local constraint graphs using only the explicit eight-entry rule table. It checks their complete recurrent-component size lists, the temporal-phase isomorphism, the lower-period component correspondence, the eighteen-vertex characteristic determinant using integer Newton identities, and the nine simple-cycle lengths and common vertices. It checks the semigroup characterization against the exact generating function through circumference one hundred. Run with `python3 certificate.py` from the directory containing the files.

`bruteforce.py` implements Rule 22 directly by a bit-parallel Boolean formula, with no transfer graph. It exhausts all \(2^n\) indexed initial configurations for **each** \(3\le n\le20\), compares second- and fourth-return tests, and checks the resulting four-cycle orbit counts against the generating function. Run with `python3 bruteforce.py`. Both programs use only the Python standard library. Their actual replay outputs are included as `certificate_output.txt` and `bruteforce_output.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work

Martínez, Adamatzky, Hoffmann, Désérable, and Zelinka investigate Rule 22 attractors and extended de Bruijn diagrams. Their 2019/2020 study includes representative short cycles through circumference twenty and notes the earlier finite circumference-thirty-four cycle tables of McIntosh. Such finite tables provide valuable checks, but neither a finite table nor the general method alone implies the present exact all-circumference renewal determinant, complete period-four support semigroup, or exponential rate. Dennunzio, Di Lena, and Margara provide general background on temporal versus spatial periodicity in cellular automata, rather than this nonlinear Rule 22 periodic-ring census. Existing Rule 18, Rule 54, Rule 62, and Rule 126 exact-orbit results concern different local maps and do not determine the Rule 22 invariant.

## Limitations

The exact equality is for **labelled** cyclic words and primitive **temporal** period four. It does not quotient out spatial shifts or mirror images and does not predict transient basin sizes or higher periods. Mathematical correctness is established by the finite exact graph argument; the independent direct enumeration only covers lengths through twenty. Historical priority is not guaranteed: the complete text of McIntosh's 2009 monograph, including its finite Rule 22 tables, was not available for a full comparison; accessible bibliographic previews show its Appendix A lists cycles through length thirty-four. This remains a specific source-inspection risk, not a claim that the source lacks a more general result.
