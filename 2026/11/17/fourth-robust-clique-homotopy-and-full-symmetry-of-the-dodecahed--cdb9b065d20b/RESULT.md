# Fourth robust clique homotopy and full symmetry of the dodecahedral graph

## Finding

Let \(D=GP(10,2)\) be the twenty-vertex graph of the regular dodecahedron. For a graph \(G\), define the fourth robust clique complex by
\[
\mathsf{Cliq}_{4}(G)=\{W\subseteq V(G):\alpha(G[W])\le 3\},
\]
where \(\alpha\) denotes ordinary graph independence number. The exact **integral homotopy type** is
\[
\boxed{\ |\mathsf{Cliq}_{4}(D)|\simeq\bigvee^{154}S^5.\ }
\]
In particular the only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_5\cong\mathbb Z^{154}\). The complex has six-dimensional maximal faces, yet collapses in the discrete-Morse sense to cells in dimensions zero and five.

The full automorphism group of the graph has order \(120\). Its rational homology character on \(\widetilde H_5\) is completely determined by the following table. Cycle types refer to the action on the twenty graph vertices.

| Vertex-cycle type | Number of elements | Trace on \(\widetilde H_5\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| \((1^{20})\) | 1 | 154 |
| \((2^8,1^4)\) | 15 | -10 |
| \((2^{10})\), noncentral | 15 | -6 |
| \((2^{10})\), central | 1 | 6 |
| \((3^6,1^2)\) | 20 | 4 |
| \((6^3,2)\) | 20 | 0 |
| \((5^4)\) | 24 | -1 |
| \((10^2)\) | 24 | 1 |

The sum of these \(120\) traces is zero, so no positive-degree rational homology class is invariant under all graph automorphisms. This is a statement about the rational representation, not a classification of the integral representation lattice or equivariant homotopy type.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set is \(V(D)=\{u_i,v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\}\) with edges \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(u_i v_i\), and \(v_i v_{i+2}\), indices modulo ten. In the verifier, labels \(0,\ldots,9\) represent \(u_i\) and labels \(10,\ldots,19\) represent \(v_i\). There are exactly thirty edges. The definition is identical to the bounded-independence complex \(I_4(D)\) in the notation of Kim and Lew and is Alexander dual to the *total* fourth cut complex, not the ordinary fourth cut complex. Every set of at most three vertices is a face. The empty set is included.

All computations below concern this explicitly defined finite graph and threshold exactly four. No extension to arbitrary cubic, planar, or regular graphs is asserted.

## Proof

**Step 1: exact face reconstruction.** Represent a subset by a twenty-bit mask \(M\). Let \(a(M)=\min\{4,\alpha(D[M])\}\), let \(i\) denote the least-labeled vertex in a nonempty \(M\), and let \(N[i]\) be its closed neighborhood. The exact recurrence
\[
a(\varnothing)=0,\qquad
 a(M)=\min\{4,\max(a(M\setminus\{i\}),1+a(M\setminus N[i]))\}
\]
follows from whether a maximum independent set includes \(i\). Applying this recurrence to *every* one of the \(2^{20}\) masks gives exactly \(12{,}217\) nonempty faces with face vector, in dimensions zero through six,
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6)=(20,190,1140,3335,4632,2660,240).
\]
These numbers and the complete masks are regenerated by the standalone verifier, rather than assumed as input.

**Step 2: an explicit acyclic face-poset matching.** Starting with the entire set of nonempty face masks, process the twenty vertex labels \(j=0,1,\dots,19\) in order. At stage \(j\), pair every remaining lower face \(M\) not containing \(j\) with its remaining coface \(M\cup\{j\}\), when both are still present; remove each pair immediately. Distinct pairs are disjoint because each stage uses a single coordinate. This deterministic rule produces exactly \(6{,}031\) incident lower/upper pairs and leaves exactly \(155\) critical faces: a single vertex and \(154\) faces of dimension five.

To verify acyclicity rather than merely count critical faces, orient the *entire* Hasse diagram of the nonempty face poset downward (coface to facet), except that the \(6{,}031\) matched cover relations are reversed. The program constructs all \(57{,}940\) cover relations, checks endpoints and reversals, computes indegrees, and runs a complete topological sort. Every one of the \(12{,}217\) faces is visited; hence the oriented diagram is acyclic. This constitutes an explicit, reproducible finite acyclic-matching certificate without reliance on the field used for homology.

By the standard acyclic matching theorem of Forman's discrete Morse theory, the complex is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with one zero-cell and \(154\) five-cells, with no other critical cells. All attaching maps target the zero-skeleton, so the CW complex is exactly a wedge of \(154\) five-spheres. This proves the stated integral homotopy equivalence, not just Betti numbers.

**Step 3: complete automorphism character.** The verification program performs adjacency-preserving backtracking over partial bijections of the twenty vertices. Every assigned adjacency and nonadjacency is checked, and the resulting enumeration has exactly \(120\) graph automorphisms. The program further identifies the unique central involution among the class whose trace is six by explicitly checking commutation with all \(120\) automorphisms.

For each automorphism \(g\), a nonempty simplex \(F\) contributes to the ordinary simplicial Lefschetz trace only if it is setwise fixed. Its contribution is \((-1)^{\dim F}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_F)\), where the sign is calculated by reordering the mapped vertices into increasing order. Including the empty-face term yields the reduced Lefschetz trace
\[
\widetilde L(g)= -1+\sum_{\varnothing\ne F\in K,\ gF=F}(-1)^{\dim F}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_F).
\]
The preceding homotopy classification shows that only reduced degree-five homology survives, so
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(g\mid\widetilde H_5(K;\mathbb Q)\bigr)=-\widetilde L(g).
\]
Evaluating this finite signed sum on every automorphism gives all table entries, not merely representative order classes. Averaging the traces gives invariant dimension zero. No claim of an equivariant CW splitting is used in the character argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Kim and Lew introduced the equivalent bounded-independence complex notation \(I_k(G)\) and proved general collapsibility and Leray bounds. Those bounds do not determine this exact dodecahedral integral homotopy type or its symmetry representation. Filakovský's 2026 treatment of fourth robust clique complexes explicitly addresses edge-glued square-sequence graphs, and its broader grid result concerns smaller thresholds; the dodecahedral graph has girth five and is not a square-sequence graph. Carnero Bravo's 2026 total-cut calculations concern cycle powers, complete multipartite graphs and certain Cartesian products rather than this graph and threshold.

Relevant previously published exact computations include the Heawood fourth robust clique complex, the dodecahedral *total third* cut complex (dual to the third robust clique complex), and the ordinary dodecahedral independence complex. These are different graph/threshold constructions; none of their stated results entails the fourth robust clique homotopy and the full 120-element character here. Likewise the ordinary fourth cut complex of a girth-five graph is not its total fourth cut complex. An unindexed or implicit prior computation may nonetheless exist.

## Limitations

This is an exact finite computer-assisted theorem about the specified graph only. It does not classify other robust parameters, arbitrary planar cubic graphs, or an equivariant homotopy splitting. The full rational character does not determine the integral representation lattice. Reproducibility requires execution of the supplied finite exhaustive checker; the finite proof's validity depends on the program's faithful implementation of the displayed recurrence and acyclic-matching theorem. The literature comparison is substantial but cannot establish global priority. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
