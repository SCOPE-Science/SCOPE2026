# Scientific review: global hypergeometric isochrons

## Correctness — PASS
The transformation \(z=y+w(x^2-h)x/(2h)\) of the **source's exact polynomial coefficients** yields \(\dot q=wq(1-q)\) and an angular Riccati equation. Its projective lift is the nonautonomous second-order equation \(\ddot\psi+(w/2)q(t)\dot\psi+\psi=0\), with an explicit Gauss hypergeometric basis. The two complex conjugate basis functions have a nonvanishing time Wronskian because their distinct exponents near the attracting circle differ by \(2i\Omega/w\), and Abel's identity carries independence across each radial component. This proves the exact full trajectory and the phase formula. Since \(c(t)=c(0)e^{-wt}\), the initial phase coefficient has exact drift \(-\Omega t\). The cycle approximation and continuous boundary extension follow from the hypergeometric function's power series around zero and a nonsingular limiting phase chart. The original coefficients and radial identity were independently checked symbolically, and phase covariance for both interior and exterior initial data was tested numerically by solving the physical-time ODE. Tests do not replace proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
The relevant primary paper, Belattar et al. (2023), Theorem 1.1 and proof (pp. 2–9), establishes the invariant oval, nonautonomous first integral and phase portrait. It neither states nor proves a Gauss basis for the off-cycle angular equation or a whole-basin asymptotic phase map. The relevant earlier published *exact physical-time statistics and arrest scaling of an algebraic limit cycle* covers the **same oscillator** but specifically gives on-cycle period, physical-time density and Floquet contraction; its full RESULT.md was inspected. It does not state the off-cycle hypergeometric flow reconstruction or its isochrons, and those are not deductions from the on-cycle stationary law alone. The 2024 related paper by the same authors is a *different degree-six polynomial field*, with different invariant curve and equations; its explicit solutions cannot serve as this model's isochron formula. A different three-dimensional analytic phase-gauge result is not an implication for this field. Actual published-finding corpus searches for the system, isochron aliases, hypergeometric Riccati reduction, and phase-gauge reductions found no equivalent statement. A general reduction of Riccati equations to second-order linear equations and the Gauss hypergeometric equation are classical methods, not originality claims. Residual priority risk: an equivalent solution might be implicit in older or unindexed integrable-oscillator literature, or independently recoverable from the source's radial formula; no universal novelty certificate is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A global asymptotic phase is the natural invariant needed for synchronizing or weakly forcing a stable oscillator: it determines which initial conditions converge to the **same phase** on the stable periodic orbit, whereas knowledge of the orbit and its physical-time density does not. A formula covering every non-equilibrium start inside and outside a nontrivial algebraic cycle supplies a substantive global dynamical classification beyond qualitative attraction. It also gives an exact nonlinear original-time solution by a special-function evaluation, allowing benchmark comparisons with phase-reduction and integration methods. The result does not claim a perturbation error theorem, elementary closed form, or uniform phase conditioning near critical parameters.
