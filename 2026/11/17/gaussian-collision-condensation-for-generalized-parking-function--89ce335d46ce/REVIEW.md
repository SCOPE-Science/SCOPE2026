Taking fixed integer \(r\)-th powers proves the complete finite simultaneous-collision sums, with the exact exceptional orbit correction. The nonstandard step compares each sum with \(\sum n^j/(j!)^r\): a log-concave discrete saddle at \(j=n^{1/r}\), a Gaussian local expansion, and a carefully controlled falling-factorial correction give the sharp constant \(e^{-1/2}\) only at \(r=2\), and prove the conditional Gaussian limit with variance \(1/r\). Exact finite chamber enumerations passed in 12 cases for \(r=2,3,4\), and numerical higher-dimensional checks support the asymptotics. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The probability of repeated independent random objectives selecting the same vertex is a natural measure of optimization stability. Although finite cone masses are already known, an all-\(r\) asymptotic classification is not just a renaming of them: collision dramatically conditions vertex rank from linear order to \(n^{1/r}\), with a sharp Gaussian fluctuation law, and the finite-population correction changes qualitatively between \(r=2\) and every higher fixed order. This reveals a meaningful hierarchy of effective vertices in an infinite natural family and sharp sensitivity to the Euclidean realization; it requires nontrivial local asymptotic analysis rather than table recomputation.
