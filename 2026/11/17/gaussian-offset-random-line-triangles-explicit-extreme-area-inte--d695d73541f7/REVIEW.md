# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The specified Gaussian-offset normal-coordinate model yields the exact identity \(A=Z^2/(2P)\) and conditional Gaussian variance \(S^2\). Uniform \(W\le1\) controls the rare-event expansion. The sparse dependency graph bounds two-triple overlaps sharing one line by \(O(h)\) and sharing two by \(O(h\log(1/h))\), giving an \(O_t(n^{-1})\) total-variation error after the scale \(h=t/n^6\). The conditional angular/spatial/signed-defect law follows from Gaussian orthogonal projection and a bounded-density change of variables. Independent direct-Cartesian computations, adaptive quadrature and simulations corroborate critical identities but do not substitute for proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

The exact classic source is Schulte–Thäle, arXiv:1201.5282, Theorem 2.6 / Corollary 2.7: it already proves Poisson/Weibull extremes for minimum-area triangles generated by stationary isotropic Poisson lines in a fixed convex window, with the same sixth-power scale and square-root exponent. These are explicitly not claimed as new. The present claim has a genuinely distinct sampling law (exactly \(n\) independent Gaussian signed offsets rather than stationary uniform line offsets in a fixed window), a model-specific explicit nonstationary coefficient, a quantitative fixed-size-dependence bound, and an exact conditional least-squares Gaussian location and asymptotically independent uniform signed defect. Decreusefond–Schulte–Thäle (arXiv:1406.5484) provides general Poisson approximation methods rather than this geometric calculation. Chenavier–Hemsley (arXiv:1502.00135) treats extreme inradii; Daley–Ebert–Swift (2014) fixes a side of a different random triangle. Full Daley–Ebert–Swift text was inaccessible; unindexed equivalents remain residual risk. No claim of the first Weibull result is made.

## Value — PASS

The near-concurrence mechanism in an inhomogeneous finite line arrangement has exact angular, location, and signed-defect marks, not merely a generic scalar extreme-value exponent. The sharp coefficient and \(O(n^{-1})\) bound permit finite ensemble calibration; the spatial Gaussian mixture localizes the exceptional intersections relative to the distinguished origin. These facts are meaningful for random line arrangements and extremal small triangular cells, and are not simply a restatement of stationary Crofton line results.

## Limits

The asymptotic rate is for fixed thresholds and iid Gaussian-offset lines. It does not assert results for arbitrary offset densities, finite-window stationary Poisson tessellations, or finite-sample estimation. Numerical trials are limited supporting checks; primary proof is analytic. Historical priority requires external expert inspection of potentially unindexed earlier articles.
