# Scientific review: Gaussian-optimal Crofton ball sections of higher-dimensional spherical tori

## Correctness — PASS
The result concerns affine hyperplanes conditioned on meeting a circular-profile solid, not centered sections. The meridian projection is a convex stadium and each radial fiber is an interval; a generic meridian section is therefore a disk with zero, one, or two boundary intervals on the rotation axis. Rotating yields precisely the three asserted homeomorphism types. The number of axis contacts is exactly the number of unit meridian disks met by a line, providing the nonnegative offset lengths and the beta-normalized Crofton probabilities. The optimizer proof explicitly differentiates \(N_d(R)=\mathbb E\min(RX,1)\), establishes strict negativity of its second derivative on \((1,\infty)\), and checks both endpoint signs. The bound \(R_d\le\mu_d^{-2}\), obtained from comparing the optimum to the limiting horn radius, supplies compactness for the Gaussian scaling. Convergence of the squared distinguished normal coordinate and a uniform-integrability argument give the limiting one-variable objective; its derivative has exactly one zero at the stated normal quantile. The packaged checker passed 106 finite assertions, including four sampling comparisons and a known three-dimensional formula. The finite checks are not claimed to prove the infinite-dimensional assertions.

## Originality — PASS with documented residual risk
Relevant full texts examined were Peng Peng's 2026 rotational torus existence paper (arXiv:2605.05022v3), Moroni's classical three-dimensional toric sections exposition, and the full research statements and proofs of the published three-dimensional convex-meridian Crofton theorem and the centered Villarceau area-cusp theorem. Their actual statements differ: existence of \(f\)-minimal tori; three-dimensional toric intersection curves; general three-dimensional probability laws; and centered three-dimensional area singularities, respectively. The formula at \(d=3\) is **not** claimed novel. None of these texts implies the exact generic topology in every \(d\ge4\), the unique radius maximizing the ball-section chance in every dimension, or the quarter-normal-quantile optimal asymptotics. Multiple semantic published-record searches and a claim-based scan of accessible existing research records returned no equivalent high-dimensional theorem. An unindexed stereological or geometric-probability source remains possible; search absence alone does not prove historical priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The source's new embedded toroidal hypersurfaces motivate the geometry of their simplest circular-profile representative, with the same \(S^1\times S^{d-2}\) topology, while not conflating the model with an \(f\)-minimal example. The all-dimensional Crofton classification yields an actual connectivity and fundamental-group transition between dimensions 3, 4 and 5. The unique shape that maximizes the chance of a contractible section is a natural optimization problem, and its dimension-dependent optimum is nontrivial: the radius ratio approaches the forbidden horn threshold on the sharp \(d^{-1}\) scale. The normal-quantile constant measures a geometric rare-event obstruction and is neither an arbitrary parameter slice nor a restatement of the published three-dimensional formula. These outcomes are useful benchmarks for geometric tomography and section topology, even without claims about all rotational shapes.

## Closest literature and limits
Peng Peng (arXiv:2605.05022, public 2026-05-06) constructs \(S^1\times S^{d-2}\) rotationally symmetric \(f\)-minimal hypersurfaces, but derives no invariant affine section law. Moroni (arXiv:1708.00803) concerns three-dimensional toric quartics. The published *A three-topology Crofton law and inverse support tomography for convex-meridian toroids* proves a stronger meridian generality in dimension three, including our three-dimensional formulas; it does not cover dimensions four or greater. *Logarithmic area cusp and Haar topology law at the Villarceau transition* treats only centered planar cuts and their areas. The present theorem does not apply to the general \(f\)-minimal hypersurface, arbitrary meridian, or central-plane sampling. Literature coverage is necessarily incomplete, especially among older stereological references, and no independent audit is claimed.
