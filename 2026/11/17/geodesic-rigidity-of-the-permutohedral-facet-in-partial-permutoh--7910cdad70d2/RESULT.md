# Geodesic rigidity of the permutohedral facet in partial permutohedra

## Finding
For integers \(m\ge2\) and \(n\ge m\), the canonical partial permutohedron \(\mathcal P(m,n)\) has an ordinary permutohedral facet \(F\) consisting of all permutations of the \(m\) largest labels. Give its one-skeleton the intrinsic length metric obtained by weighting each edge by its ambient Euclidean length. For any two facet vertices \(u,v\),
\[
 d_{\mathcal P}^{\rm edge}(u,v)=\sqrt2\,\ell(u,v),
\]
where \(\ell(u,v)\) is the minimum number of transpositions of consecutive **numerical values** among \(n-m+1,\ldots,n\) needed to change the coordinate word \(u\) into \(v\). Moreover, **every** shortest edge path from \(u\) to \(v\) stays in \(F\). In particular no detour through a rank-deficient partial-permutation vertex can even tie a shortest facet path.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(\mathcal P(m,n)\subset\mathbb R^m\) be the convex hull of vectors with pairwise distinct positive entries chosen from \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and all remaining coordinates zero; it suffices to use the actual exposed vertices. With \(n\ge m\), the vertices are exactly words of the form \((n,n-1,\ldots,n-k+1,0,\ldots,0)\) in arbitrary coordinate order, \(0\le k\le m\). A word has rank \(k\) when it contains \(k\) nonzeros. The rank-\(m\) vertices form the facet exposed by the sum of coordinates and hence a usual permutohedron. All metrics here are **weighted graph-path metrics**, not chord distances in the ambient Euclidean space; the two differ in general. In \(\ell\), adjacent refers to consecutive **values**, not consecutive coordinate positions.

## Proof
**1. Edge moves and their lengths.** For an objective \(c\cdot x\), order the positive coefficients of \(c\) decreasingly. The maximizing partial permutation assigns the descending labels \(n,n-1,\ldots\) to this ordered positive support and assigns zero to every nonpositive coefficient, with arbitrary tie-breaking. The normal-fan walls between generic maximizers correspond to (a) equality of two consecutive **positive** coefficients, interchanging consecutive occupied labels, and (b) the least positive coefficient crossing zero, inserting or deleting the smallest occupied label at a zero coordinate. Ties of coefficients that are not adjacent in this ordered positive list do not define a codimension-one wall. Since \(n\ge m\), every rank is available and there are no other boundary moves at maximum rank. This yields all edges. The first type has Euclidean length \(\sqrt2\). In the second type, passing from rank \(k-1\) to rank \(k\) introduces value \(t=n-k+1\), and the edge length is \(t\). The complete edge description also follows by the same normal-cone argument applied to any two adjacent chambers of the ordered-positive-coefficient fan.

**2. Completion measures.** Let \(\mathfrak S\) denote the set of all full-rank coordinate words containing the \(m\) largest labels. On \(\mathfrak S\) put the shortest-path metric \(d_{\rm Cox}\) for transposing consecutive numerical labels, each with cost \(\sqrt2\). For a rank-\(k\) partial-permutation vertex \(x\), define \(\mu_x\) as the uniform probability measure on words formed by filling its \(j=m-k\) zero positions with the absent smallest \(j\) labels \(n-m+1,\ldots,n-k\). At rank \(m\) this is the point mass \(\delta_x\). Use the Wasserstein-1 distance \(W_1\) generated by \(d_{\rm Cox}\) on probability measures over this finite space.

**3. Nonexpansion and strict insertion contraction.** If adjacent partial vertices differ by swapping consecutive occupied labels, perform the same swap in each completion. This couples \(\mu_x,\mu_y\) at cost \(\sqrt2\), equal to their edge length.

Suppose instead that \(x\) has rank \(k-1\), that \(y\) has rank \(k\), and that \(y\) is obtained by putting \(t=n-k+1\) in a formerly zero coordinate \(p\). Set \(j=m-k\), so \(x\) has \(j+1\) zero positions. Draw a completion of \(x\) uniformly. Its value at \(p\) is some \(u\in\{t-j,\ldots,t\}\), uniform on these \(j+1\) choices. Transform this word by the sequence of transpositions of consecutive *values* \((u,u+1),(u+1,u+2),\ldots,(t-1,t)\), in that order. The value at \(p\) becomes \(t\). Conditional on \(u\), the mapping bijects the permutations of the other missing labels onto the completions of \(y\), so the transformed marginal has exactly law \(\mu_y\). The transformation costs at most \(\sqrt2(t-u)\) in \(d_{\rm Cox}\), giving
\[
 W_1(\mu_x,\mu_y)\le \sqrt2\,\frac{j}{2}=\frac{j}{\sqrt2}<j+1\le t=\|x-y\|_2.
\]
The last inequality uses \(t=n-k+1\ge m-k+1=j+1\). It is strict even for \(j=0\). Thus on **every** edge, \(W_1\) of completion measures is no greater than its Euclidean length, and on a rank-changing edge it is strictly smaller.

**4. Endpoint bound and rigidity.** By the Wasserstein triangle inequality, any graph path \(x_0=u,x_1,\ldots,x_s=v\) has length at least
\[
 \sum_{a=1}^s W_1(\mu_{x_{a-1}},\mu_{x_a})\ge W_1(\mu_u,\mu_v)=d_{\rm Cox}(u,v)=\sqrt2\,\ell(u,v).
\]
Every path leaving \(F\) necessarily contains a rank-changing edge and therefore makes the **first** inequality strict. Conversely, any reduced sequence of consecutive-value transpositions between \(u\) and \(v\) is an edge path in \(F\) with exactly \(\ell(u,v)\) edges of length \(\sqrt2\). This proves the equality and the strict confinement of all shortest paths.

## Verification
The proof gives an explicit coupling for each of the two edge types and a quantitative strict margin at each rank-changing step: its edge length is at least \(j+1\), whereas the coupling costs at most \(j/\sqrt2\). Thus no enumerative extrapolation is required. For evaluation, encode a full-rank vertex as the coordinate word of its ranked labels; inversion count of the permutation on the **values** mapping one word to the other computes \(\ell\), using the conventional Coxeter adjacent-value generators. The normal-cone argument is the sole geometric input needed to establish that these are all one-skeleton edge types. Its scope requires \(n\ge m\); the smaller-label regime may have additional rank-saturation moves and is not covered.

## Relationship to prior work
Heuer and Striker [1], Proposition 5.7, describe the partial-permutation vertices. Behrend and collaborators [2] determine face structure of partial permutohedra. In particular the ordinary permutohedral top face and its combinatorial edges are not claimed as new. Previously recorded work on partial-permutohedron chord diameters, one-skeleton Laplacian spectra, and individual edge-length distributions determines different invariants and does not imply the ambient weighted-shortest-path equality, nor that *all* ambient geodesics avoid the other ranks. The contribution here is the global metric non-shortcut theorem and its strict form, obtained with a completion-measure transport obstruction.

## Limitations
Only full-rank endpoints are treated, and the hypotheses \(m\ge2,n\ge m\) are essential to the stated edge classification. These are weighted **edge** geodesics, not unrestricted paths through the interior of the polytope. This proof has not been independently audited. Possible equivalent theorems under alternative polytope-graph terminology remain a historical-priority risk.
