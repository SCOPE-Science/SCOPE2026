# Geodesic rigidity: scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The domain is integers \(n\ge m\ge2\); facet endpoints are rank-\(m\) permutations. The normal fan of the ordered positive-coordinate optimization has only consecutive occupied-label swaps and rank-changing insertion/removal walls. An explicit coupling between uniformly completed partial-permutation vertices bounds Wasserstein-1 change by the Euclidean edge length, with a strict gap on every rank-changing edge. Endpoint delta measures recover exactly the Coxeter word metric and an in-facet reduced-word edge path realizes the lower bound. The proof is analytic and does not infer an infinite statement from finite checks.

## Originality
PASS at best current historical knowledge, not a claim of exhaustive priority. The original vertex characterization in Heuer–Striker [1] and the facial description in Behrend et al. [2] supply the definition and graph moves but not the quantified weighted shortest paths between arbitrary top-facet vertices or rigidity of *all* minimizing paths. Related previous results treat Euclidean diameters, single-edge lengths and graph spectra, not these endpoint-to-endpoint intrinsic geodesics. Searches for equivalent isometric-facet, geodesic-convexity, and shortest-path formulations located no dominating result. A hidden equivalent formulation in polytope-skeleton literature remains possible.

## Value
PASS. Geodesics in a full polytope skeleton need not be determined by a proper face: exterior detours can reduce shortest-path length even when the face is a familiar permutohedron. This exact criterion identifies a natural infinite family where such shortcuts are impossible, yields an inversion-based exact algorithm, and proves strict confinement of every minimizing path using a structural metric certificate, beyond a routine single invariant computation.

## Closest literature and limits
[1] C. Heuer and J. Striker, arXiv:2012.09901, Proposition 5.7; [2] Behrend et al., arXiv:2207.14253, face description. The result is restricted to \(n\ge m\) and Euclidean edge-length paths. Potentially relevant wider results on polytope geodesics warrant later independent priority review.
