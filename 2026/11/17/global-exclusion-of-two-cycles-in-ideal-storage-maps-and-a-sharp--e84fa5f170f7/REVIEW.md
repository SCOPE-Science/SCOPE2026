# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the map \(F_{c,h}(s,r)=(s(1-r)+c,h(sr))\), write released quantities \(a=s_0r_0\), \(b=s_1r_1\) on a putative two-cycle. The two stock updates force \(a+b=2c\) and \(s_1-s_0=(b-a)/2\). The two rate updates force \(s_1-s_0=b/h(a)-a/h(b)\); if \(b>a\), nondecreasing \(h\) of range \((0,1)\) makes the latter strictly larger than \(b-a\), contradiction. Cyclic relabeling treats \(a>b\); equality yields a fixed point. Domain boundary points map into the strictly positive interior in one step. The decreasing-feedback witness is checked with exact rational stock identities and exact log-odds values. This is a complete algebraic proof, not an extrapolation from computation.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

The 2026 study uses the logistic map to exhibit Neimark–Sacker oscillation and spatial wave dynamics; it gives no global two-cycle classification. The 2011 full-text general-map paper supplies uniqueness and local linear/Neimark–Sacker analysis but does not state or imply this global prohibition, and it identifies dropping monotonicity as a source of richer behavior. The global all-parameter period-two exclusion with an exact smooth counterexample is different in quantifiers, conclusions and methods from those prior results. Searches for period-two, second iterates, no-flip results, and related model terminology found no exact or stronger overlapping result. The 1982 foundational article remains inaccessible in full; a possible older derivation is a material historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The 2026 motivation includes oscillatory regimes and spatial patterns, making the allowed periodic spectrum of the underlying local map a natural structural question. The result rules out the lowest nontrivial exact temporal locking universally, even after a Neimark–Sacker instability, without controlling or forbidding the observed oscillations. The smooth decreasing logistic construction pinpoints that the feedback-order property, rather than positivity or analyticity alone, drives the obstruction. This is a meaningful global dynamical restriction and sharp boundary rather than an isolated numerical computation.

Generic negative-feedback flow theorems operate in continuous time and do not imply a statement about this discrete map. The older Dress–Jaeger–Plath (1982) original article was located bibliographically, but its full text could not be inspected. No claim is made about longer periodic orbits, invariant circles, global convergence, or coupled sites.
