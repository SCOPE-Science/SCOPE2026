# Scientific evaluation

## Correctness

PASS. The graph-induced admissibility conditions separate all four possibilities for the hubs. The subcomplex omitting faces with both hubs is contractible because each of its two cone attachments meets the existing contractible complex in a contractible join with a nonempty simplex factor. The second join is a cone on the truncated leaf skeleton and meets the first subcomplex in exactly the suspension of that skeleton. Homotopy pushout and the augmented skeleton chain complex prove the unrestricted sphere count, including both endpoints. Natural Mayer--Vietoris and the join Künneth identification prove the full integral automorphism module, including the additional sign from a hub exchange. All exact small-case homology and signed-character calculations agreed with the formulas.

## Originality

PASS, at best knowledge with an explicit residual risk of an unindexed equivalent. The directly relevant full papers on higher-independence complexes of trees and chordal graphs prove vertex decomposability or unspecified wedge existence rather than the complete all-threshold double-star classification and integral automorphism representation. The previously classified perfect rooted trees, fully whiskered graphs, complete multipartite graphs and paths do not cover the stated general diameter-three family. A separately published multipath result on bidirected double stars concerns directed-arc path complexes, not induced-vertex higher-independence complexes. The path endpoint is known and is not a claim of novelty by itself.

## Value

PASS. Diameter-three trees are exactly the positive-leaf two-hub trees and form the smallest family of caterpillars with two genuine branching centres. A complete integral homotopy classification for every threshold reveals that the top dimension follows the threshold and the multiplicity depends only on the total number of leaves, not on the hub split. The integral exterior-power module and the hub-exchange sign give equivariant information that neither wedge existence nor Betti numbers imply. The result is a natural structural refinement of recent tree-complex work rather than an arbitrary finite instance.

## Scope and literature risk

The argument does not treat more general caterpillars or establish an equivariant sphere decomposition. The primary texts checked include Sawant (arXiv:2605.25150), Deshpande–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:2001.06775), Deshpande–Singh (arXiv:2001.05448), and Abdelmalek et al. (arXiv:2106.10863). Identical unindexed work remains possible. The finite script is corroborating evidence and is not an all-parameter proof.
