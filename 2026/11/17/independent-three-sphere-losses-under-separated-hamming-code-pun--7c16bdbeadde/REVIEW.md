# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(n\ge3\), the proof gives explicit maximal faces of each vertex link, a relative simplicial collapse of the link's contractible core, and an integral triangle-coordinate basis of rank \(\binom n3\). Each deleted word has \(\binom n3\) embedded octahedral-link three-cube cycles, all avoiding other deleted words because the code minimum distance is at least four. These furnish a signed identity matrix in relative integral homology, proving surjectivity and primitive splitting; the long exact sequence, van Kampen, Hurewicz, and Whitehead then establish the full claim and all inclusions. The verification script independently enumerates finite flag complexes, signed-irrelevant mod-two ranks, and local cubical pivots for dimensions three through six. Finite tests do not prove the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS, subject to the identified residual risks

The global \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(Q_n)\) sphere-wedge theorem and its ordered initial-segment deletion theorem were established by Adamaszek and Adams; the present work does not claim them as discoveries. A prior finite two-vertex classification through dimension five already determines the small pair cases, which are likewise not new. The new claim is for **every finite distance-four code, in every dimension, and every one of its deletion subsets**, including arbitrary nonlinear codes and full integral inclusion diagrams. The known results are not strong enough to imply this arrangement-independent multi-puncture statement: ordered initial segments do not cover codes of minimum distance four once multiple points are removed, and global symmetry-character calculations do not classify their induced deletions. Relevant indexed research and full-text literature were compared; unindexed work and the incompletely accessible full 2026 group-action article remain priority risks.

## Value — PASS

A minimum-distance-four binary code is a natural, not parameter-tuned, collection of sites whose radius-three embedded cube cycles cannot meet another removed site. The theorem gives an exact stability law for deleting multiple well-separated landmarks, including *integral* primitive inclusion maps rather than merely Euler characteristics or selected Betti numbers. It covers nonlinearly arranged codes and all intermediate puncture filtrations, extending a previously finite pair classification to an infinite structural family; the exact subtraction \(|T|\binom n3\) is useful when studying sparse metric samples and the local-to-global topology of cube Rips complexes.
