# Integral cohomology rings of diagonal cyclic quotients of real moment-angle complexes over \(K_{n,n}\)

## Finding

Let \(n\geq3\), let \(K_{n,n}\) denote the complete bipartite graph regarded as a one-dimensional simplicial complex, and write
\[
\mathcal R_n=(D^1,S^0)^{K_{n,n}},\qquad
Q_n=\mathcal R_n/C_n,
\]
where the generator of \(C_n\) simultaneously cyclically permutes the \(n\) coordinates in each bipartition class. Quotients throughout are ordinary *geometric* orbit spaces.

For every integer \(q\geq1\), put
\[
N_n(q)=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)q^{n/d},\quad
r_n=2^{n-1}-N_n(2)+1,\quad
s_n=(n-4)4^{n-1}+N_n(4)-1+2r_n.
\]
Then the complete integral homology is
\[
H_i(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
\mathbb Z^{2r_n},&i=1,\\
\mathbb Z^{s_n},&i=2,\\
0,&i\geq3.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, **all integral homology is torsion-free**, even though the cyclic action has nontrivial vertex stabilizers.

More precisely, the cohomology ring has degree-one generators
\(a_1,\ldots,a_{r_n},b_1,\ldots,b_{r_n}\) and degree-two generators
\(c_{ij}\) for \(1\leq i,j\leq r_n\), together with
\(s_n-r_n^2\) extra degree-two generators \(w_t\), such that
\[
a_i\smile a_j=0,\quad b_i\smile b_j=0,\quad
a_i\smile b_j=c_{ij}=-b_j\smile a_i.
\]
The displayed degree-two generators form an **integral basis**; all products of positive-degree elements not forced by these formulas vanish for dimensional reasons. In particular, the rank-\(r_n^2\) sublattice generated by degree-one products is *primitive* in \(H^2(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\). The ring description is noncanonical in its choice of bases.

For \(n=3,4,5,6\), respectively, \((2r_n,s_n)=(2,9),(6,75),(18,481),(38,2785)\). The ranks \(s_n-r_n^2\) of indecomposable degree-two summands are \(8,66,400,2424\) and grow without bound.

## Assumptions and scope

Use \(D^1=[-1,1]\), \(S^0=\{-1,1\}\), and the standard product CW structure. Every point of \(\mathcal R_n\) has non-boundary coordinates supported on a simplex of \(K_{n,n}\), i.e. at most one coordinate in each of the two parts. Write \(A_n\) for the one-skeleton of the \(n\)-cube \((D^1)^n\). Since \(K_{n,n}\) is the join of two discrete \(n\)-vertex complexes, the definition of polyhedral products supplies an equivariant homeomorphism
\[
\mathcal R_n\cong A_n\times A_n.
\]
The diagonal action rotates the coordinate positions in *both* cube graphs. It is not the independent \(C_n\times C_n\) action, whose quotient would be the much simpler product of two quotient graphs. No statement about the fundamental group beyond its abelianization, or about a homotopy splitting, is asserted.

## Proof

**1. A necklace quotient graph.** Put \(B_n=A_n/C_n\). Coordinate permutations preserve Hamming weight. Thus no cube edge can be reversed under the action, and each edge orbit has trivial stabilizer: an edge has a unique variable coordinate, which no nonidentity cyclic shift fixes. There are \(n2^{n-1}\) edges before division, so \(B_n\) has \(2^{n-1}\) edges. Its vertices are binary necklaces of length \(n\), hence there are \(N_n(2)\) vertices by Burnside's lemma. The graph is connected, so
\[
H_1(B_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{r_n},\qquad
r_n=2^{n-1}-N_n(2)+1.
\]
The all-minus-one vertex \(v\in A_n\) is fixed by every rotation. Every edge path in \(B_n\) beginning at \([v]\) lifts edge-by-edge to an edge path in \(A_n\) beginning at \(v\): rotate each chosen representative edge until its initial vertex equals the current lifted endpoint. Because the orbit of \(v\) is a singleton, a based loop ends at \(v\). Consequently the graph quotient map \(u:A_n\to B_n\) is surjective on \(\pi_1\), and therefore on \(H_1\).

**2. The first homology of the diagonal quotient.** Set \(X=A_n\times A_n\), let \(q:X\to Q_n\) be the diagonal orbit map, and let
\[
p:Q_n\longrightarrow B_n\times B_n,\qquad
p([(x,y)])=([x],[y]).
\]
The pair \((v,v)\) is fixed by every rotation. The quotient one-skeleton has one edge for each orbit of a horizontal or vertical product edge; no such orbit is inverted. The same edge-path lifting argument therefore proves that \(q_*:\pi_1(X,(v,v))\to\pi_1(Q_n,[(v,v)])\) is surjective. Since \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=H_1(A_n;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(A_n;\mathbb Z)\), the images of the two coordinate-axis copies generate \(H_1(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\).

The two maps \(j_1,j_2:B_n\to Q_n\) defined by \(j_1([x])=[(x,v)]\) and \(j_2([y])=[(v,y)]\) are well-defined, and their composition with \(p\) is the corresponding coordinate inclusion into \(B_n\times B_n\). Thus the induced map
\[
(j_1)_*\oplus(j_2)_*:\ H_1(B_n;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(B_n;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow H_1(Q_n;\mathbb Z)
\]
is both surjective (by the preceding paragraph and the surjectivity of \(u_*\)) and split injective (by \(p_*\)). Hence \(H_1(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{2r_n}\) integrally; this is not merely a rational invariant calculation.

**3. Exact quotient cells and second homology.** In the product cubical structure on \(X\), a vertex is a pair of binary strings of length \(n\). Its diagonal rotation orbit is a necklace on an alphabet of four paired bits, giving exactly \(N_n(4)\) quotient vertices. Every positive-dimensional cell contains an edge in at least one factor and has trivial cell stabilizer, because that edge's variable coordinate must be fixed. In \(X\) there are \(n4^n\) horizontal and vertical one-cells in total and \(n^2 4^{n-1}\) two-cells. Dividing by \(n\), the quotient therefore has the exact CW cell counts
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=\bigl(N_n(4),\,4^n,\,n4^{n-1}\bigr).
\]
No positive-dimensional quotient cell is inverted or internally folded. Consequently
\[
\chi(Q_n)=N_n(4)+(n-4)4^{n-1}.
\]
The quotient is a connected two-dimensional finite CW complex. Its integral second homology is \(\ker(\partial_2)\), a subgroup of the free cellular group \(C_2(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\), so it is free. Euler–Poincaré and Step 2 yield its rank \(s_n=\chi(Q_n)-1+2r_n\). Higher homology vanishes.

**4. Primitive cup products and the full ring.** The induced map
\[
p_*:H_2(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_2(B_n\times B_n;\mathbb Z)
\]
is surjective. Indeed, \(p\circ q=u\times u\); by Step 1, \(u_*\) is surjective on the free group \(H_1\). The integral Künneth isomorphism for products of graphs gives the surjectivity of \((u\times u)_*:H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\to H_2(B_n\times B_n;\mathbb Z)\), which factors through \(p_*\). Since both second-homology groups are free abelian, this epimorphism splits. Dualizing, \(p^*:H^2(B_n\times B_n;\mathbb Z)\to H^2(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\) is an injection with primitive image. The map \(p^*\) is already an isomorphism on \(H^1\) by Step 2 and the universal coefficient theorem. The integral cohomology ring of the product of two graphs has only the cross-factor degree-one products, with \(r_n^2\) independent degree-two generators. Their images under \(p^*\) are therefore primitive and independent in \(H^2(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\). Extend this primitive basis to the remaining \(s_n-r_n^2\) generators; dimensional considerations give all remaining products. This proves the stated full ring.

Finally, \(N_n(q)=q^n/n+O(q^{n/2})\) for fixed \(q\), giving \(r_n=2^{n-1}(1-2/n)+o(2^n)\) and \(s_n-r_n^2=(n-5+o(1))4^{n-1}\). Thus the number of additional, non-cup-generated degree-two classes tends to infinity.

## Verification

The included standalone `verify_diagonal.py` enumerates every binary-string vertex, one-cell, two-cell and simultaneous-rotation orbit for \(n=3,4,5,6\). It independently checks the formulas for each quotient cell count and Euler characteristic. For \(n=3,4\), it builds the signed quotient boundary operators, explicitly checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), restricts \(\partial_2\) to non-tree edges after choosing a spanning tree, and calculates the exact *integral* Smith form without rational approximations.

The resulting cells and homology ranks are \((24,64,48;2,9)\) for \(n=3\), \((70,256,256;6,75)\) for \(n=4\), \((208,1024,1280;18,481)\) for \(n=5\), and \((700,4096,6144;38,2785)\) for \(n=6\). At \(n=3,4\), all nonzero Smith factors are units, matching the torsion-free proof. The checks are finite calibrations; the arbitrary-\(n\) theorem follows from Steps 1–4, not finite extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work

Bahri–Bendersky–Cohen–Gitler (public arXiv version dated November 29, 2007) supplies the general polyhedral-product context and a stable suspension decomposition, but not this *ordinary geometric* diagonal permutation quotient or its integral ring. Fu–Grbić (2018) establishes equivariant stable splitting and representation stability for polyhedral products, including joins of simplicial complexes, but concerns representations of the covering space rather than the integral orbit-space cup lattice. Al-Raisi's 2014 thesis, first publicly available October 17, 2016, studies permutation symmetries and branched quotients of polygonal real moment-angle surfaces; the accessible detailed abstract identifies a different base complex. Its full thesis was not available for comparison, so a claim of complete noncoverage is not made. Public mathematical-record searches for simultaneous cyclic cube-graph product quotients, binary necklace formulations and complete-bipartite real moment-angle orbit cohomology did not identify the stated integral ring. The closest retrieved records concern other graph complexes and other symmetry actions; absence from search results alone is not proof of priority.

## Limitations

This theorem treats only the simultaneous cyclic shift on two *equal-width* bipartition classes, not arbitrary permutation subgroups, independent rotations, or more than two factors. It determines all integral cohomology groups and their products but does not determine the full fundamental group or homotopy type. A published or unindexed equivalent result could have escaped the available searches, especially within an inaccessible thesis or its appendices. No independent mathematical review is claimed.
