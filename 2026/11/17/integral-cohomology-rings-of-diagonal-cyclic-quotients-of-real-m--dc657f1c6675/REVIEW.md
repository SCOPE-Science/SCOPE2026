# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The object is explicitly identified with the product of two cube one-skeleta, the quotient graph ranks follow from exact necklace and free edge-orbit counts, and the vertex-fixed edge-lifting argument is given for both the factor and the product quotient. The first-homology isomorphism follows from a split left inverse and surjectivity; integral second-homology freeness is a subgroup-of-free-chain fact. The primitive cup-product statement follows from a split surjection on second homology, not from a rational approximation. Signed Smith checks at widths three and four and independent quotient-cell counts at widths three through six corroborate each critical finite arithmetic step. There is no assertion of a full fundamental-group presentation or of a space-level wedge splitting.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected evidence, not priority certification. The closest primary works are the foundational polyhedral-product decomposition of Bahri–Bendersky–Cohen–Gitler (arXiv:0711.4689), Fu–Grbić's equivariant stable splitting and representation-stability results (arXiv:1803.11047), and Al-Raisi's 2014 thesis (public October 2016). The first gives a stable additive decomposition and definition; the second studies representations on the original polyhedral product; the thesis's available detailed abstract treats polygonal moment-angle surfaces. These do not by their inspected statements yield the complete integral ring of this coupled, nonfree diagonal quotient. Searches also compared simultaneous rotation of cube-skeleton products, four-color necklace orbit cells, and complete-bipartite real moment-angle quotient cohomology with public mathematical records and accessible earlier claims. The thesis full text was not accessible, and equivalent unpublished or unindexed results remain a risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The diagonal action is distinguished from separate rotations of the two cube graphs, a natural question for symmetries of polyhedral products. The complete *integral cohomology ring* gives more information than Betti or rational-invariant counts: it rules out torsion despite nonfree stabilizers, establishes a primitive, nonzero cup-product lattice of unbounded rank, and exhibits exponentially many degree-two classes not generated by degree-one classes. This is an all-width classification of a canonical two-factor family, with structural cup-lattice information not available by merely counting orbit cells.

## Scope and residual risk

The finite checker proves only its stated small-size checks; the infinite statement relies on the included elementary topological argument. Full homotopy types, other permutation subgroups and the inaccessible 2014 thesis's uninspected contents remain outside the verified scope. No publication-priority or independent validation is claimed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
