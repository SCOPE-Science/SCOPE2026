# Integral dihedral homology of generalized Mycielskians of odd cycles

## Finding

For every odd integer \(n\ge 5\) with \(3\nmid n\), and every integer \(\ell\ge0\), the following formulas give **all** reduced integral homology groups of the independence complex \(X_{n,\ell}=\operatorname{Ind}(\mu_\ell(C_n))\), including their natural \(D_n\)-actions.

Let \(n=3r+\delta\), where \(\delta\in\{1,2\}\), and define
\[
d=\begin{cases}r-1,&\delta=1,\\r,&\delta=2,\end{cases}\qquad e=2r.
\]
Both \(d\) and \(e\) are nonnegative, with \(d\ge1\), \(d\) odd, and \(e\) even. Let \(\mathbb Z_\gamma\), for \(\gamma\in\{1,-1\}\), mean the rank-one integral \(D_n\)-module on which all rotations act as \(1\) and every reflection acts as \(\gamma\). Let \(\alpha_n,\beta_n\in\{1,-1\}\) be determined by

| \(n\pmod{12}\) | \(1\) | \(5\) | \(7\) | \(11\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\alpha_n\) | \(1\) | \(-1\) | \(-1\) | \(1\) |
| \(\beta_n\) | \(1\) | \(-1\) | \(1\) | \(-1\) |

For every integer \(k\ge0\):

* At \(\ell=3k\), the only nonzero reduced integral homology modules are
\[
\widetilde H_{k(e+1)}(X_{n,3k};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z_{\beta_n^{k}},\qquad
\widetilde H_{d+k(e+1)}(X_{n,3k};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z_{\alpha_n\beta_n^{k}}.
\]
* At \(\ell=3k+1\), the only nonzero reduced integral homology module is
\[
\widetilde H_{d+k(e+1)+1}(X_{n,3k+1};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z_{\alpha_n\beta_n^{k}}.
\]
* At \(\ell=3k+2\), the only nonzero reduced integral homology module is
\[
\widetilde H_{(k+1)(e+1)-1}(X_{n,3k+2};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z_{\beta_n^{k+1}}.
\]

All unspecified reduced homology groups vanish. In particular, **all reduced integral homology is torsion-free**, every nonzero group is rank one, and the action is completely determined by the displayed signs.

For the five-cycle, \(\alpha_5=\beta_5=-1\), \(d=1\), and \(e=2\). Hence the dihedral quotient \(|X_{5,\ell}|/D_5\) is rationally acyclic exactly when \(\ell\equiv1,2\pmod6\); otherwise it has precisely one nonzero reduced rational homology group of rank one. Here rationally acyclic means vanishing reduced rational homology, **not** contractible.

## Assumptions and scope

The cycle \(C_n\) has vertices indexed by \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with edges joining consecutive indices. The generalized Mycielskian \(\mu_\ell(C_n)\) has layers \(V_j=\{(i,j):i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\), for \(1\le j\le\ell+1\), and an apex \(w\). Consecutive layers carry all edges \((i,j)(i\pm1,j+1)\); the terminal layer \(V_{\ell+1}\) carries a copy of \(C_n\); and the apex is adjacent to every vertex of \(V_1\). There are no other edges. For \(\ell=0\), this is the graph-theoretic cone over \(C_n\).

The dihedral group \(D_n\) is generated by rotation \(\rho:i\mapsto i+1\) and reflection \(\tau:i\mapsto-i\); it acts in the same way on each layer and fixes \(w\). The independence complex contains all vertex sets with no graph edges among them. Only this natural \(D_n\) action is asserted, not necessarily the full automorphism group for every parameter.

## Proof

**1. Cycle homology and its dihedral orientation.** The standard cycle-independence calculation gives \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\simeq S^d\) and \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n\times K_2)\simeq\operatorname{Ind}(C_{2n})\simeq S^e\) for the stated odd \(n\) with \(3\nmid n\). The double cover is a single \(2n\)-cycle because \(n\) is odd. Since their top integral homology groups each have rank one, all rotations, which have odd order, act trivially.

To determine reflections, write \(F_m=I(P_m;-1)\), the alternating number of independent sets of the path on \(m\) vertices. Endpoint deletion gives \(F_0=1\), \(F_1=0\), and \(F_m=F_{m-1}-F_{m-2}\), so \(F_m\) has period six with successive values \(1,0,-1,-1,0,1\).

For an automorphism \(g\) of a graph, consider the graph whose vertices are \(g\)-orbits of original vertices, discarding any orbit containing an internal edge, and joining two orbit vertices when an original graph edge runs between the orbits. In the simplicial chain Lefschetz sum, an independent union of \(s\) orbits contributes \((-1)^{s-1}\). Consequently the *reduced* Lefschetz number equals minus the independence polynomial of this orbit graph evaluated at \(-1\). This includes the empty simplex through reduced chains and works for cycles of any orbit length.

For \(\tau\) acting on the odd cycle \(C_n\), the orbit \(\{(n-1)/2,(n+1)/2\}\) contains an edge and is discarded. The remaining orbit graph is \(P_{(n-1)/2}\), giving \(\widetilde L(\tau)=-F_{(n-1)/2}\). Since \(d\) is odd, the reflection action on \(\widetilde H_d(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n);\mathbb Z)\) is \(\alpha_n=F_{(n-1)/2}\).

For \(\tau\) acting on \(C_n\times K_2\) by \((i,a)\mapsto(-i,a)\), no vertex orbit contains an internal edge. The orbit graph forms two paths connected at their far endpoints, hence is exactly \(P_{n+1}\). Thus \(\widetilde L(\tau)=-F_{n+1}\). Since \(e\) is even, the reflection action on \(\widetilde H_e(\operatorname{Ind}(C_{2n});\mathbb Z)\) is \(\beta_n=-F_{n+1}\). The six-periodic path values yield precisely the four-column sign table above.

**2. Invariant fold inclusions.** Write \(A_\ell=\operatorname{Ind}(\mu_\ell(C_n)-w)\) and \(B_\ell=\operatorname{Ind}(\mu_\ell(C_n)-N[w])\). The elementary independence-complex fold says that if distinct graph vertices \(u,v\) satisfy \(N(u)\subseteq N(v)\) for *open* neighborhoods, then deletion of \(v\) induces a homotopy equivalence on independence complexes. Within \(A_\ell\), successively delete the complete layers \(V_3,V_6,V_9,\ldots\), using domination by vertices in the preceding retained layers. In \(B_\ell\), delete \(V_4,V_7,V_{10},\ldots\), again retaining the appropriate dominating vertices. Depending on the terminal layer, a last domination fold removes the terminal layer and leaves an isolated layer, making the corresponding independence complex contractible.

Each complete-layer deletion is the inclusion of a \(D_n\)-invariant induced subgraph. Although the individual vertex folds may be ordered non-equivariantly, their composite **invariant inclusion** is a nonequivariant homotopy equivalence, so it induces an isomorphism of integral \(D_n\)-homology modules. Put \(J=\operatorname{Ind}(C_n\times K_2)\). The surviving graphs therefore give the following \(D_n\)-module identifications on reduced homology:

| Level | Homology model for \(A_\ell\) | Homology model for \(B_\ell\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(3k\) | \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)*J^{*k}\) | \(J^{*k}\) |
| \(3k+1\) | Contractible | \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)*J^{*k}\) |
| \(3k+2\) | \(J^{*(k+1)}\) | Contractible |

For \(k=0\), the empty join \(J^{*0}\) is the augmented complex \(\{\emptyset\}\), with one reduced chain generator in degree \(-1\). This handles \(\ell=0\) by the separate observation \(\operatorname{Ind}(\mu_0(C_n))=\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\sqcup\{w\}\).

**3. The equivariant long exact sequence.** For \(\ell\ge1\), the exact decomposition \(X_{n,\ell}=A_\ell\cup(w*B_\ell)\) has intersection \(B_\ell\) and is \(D_n\)-invariant. Its reduced Mayer–Vietoris sequence is a sequence of integral \(D_n\)-modules. The join Künneth formula (there is no torsion in the sphere factors) puts the sole nonzero reduced homology of \(J^{*k}\) in degree \(k(e+1)-1\) with action \(\beta_n^k\), and that of \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)*J^{*k}\) in degree \(d+k(e+1)\) with action \(\alpha_n\beta_n^k\). No factor permutation occurs under the stated diagonal group action.

For \(\ell=3k\) with \(k\ge1\), these two homologies lie in distinct dimensions with a gap \(d\ge1\); therefore the inclusion maps occurring in the Mayer–Vietoris sequence vanish for degree reasons. The sequence gives isomorphisms in degrees \(k(e+1)\) and \(d+k(e+1)\) to the corresponding \(B_\ell\) and \(A_\ell\) modules. For \(\ell=3k+1\), \(A_\ell\) is contractible and the same sequence shifts the \(B_\ell\) homology up one degree. For \(\ell=3k+2\), \(B_\ell\) is contractible and the inclusion of \(A_\ell\) induces an isomorphism. The \(\ell=0\) decomposition was handled above. All these maps are integral and equivariant, proving every stated module and all vanishing claims.

**4. Quotients.** After barycentric subdivision, the finite \(D_n\)-action has no simplex inversions. Rational transfer identifies the rational homology of the geometric quotient with the invariants in rational homology. A rank-one module \(\mathbb Q_{\gamma}\) contributes an invariant class exactly when \(\gamma=1\). Applying this to the formulas yields the assertion for \(n=5\) and also determines every quotient's reduced rational homology from the sign table. No quotient homotopy equivalence is inferred.

## Relationship to prior work

The nonequivariant homotopy types of generalized Mycielskian independence complexes, including cycles, follow from Carnero Bravo's 2026 structural theorem. Earlier work of Goyal, Shukla and Singh treats complete-graph generalized Mycielskians. Existing symmetry calculations for other graph complexes, and those for generalized Mycielskians based on complete graphs, concern different actions or graph families. The present result determines the **integral dihedral homology action** for all the specified odd-cycle bases and every depth; an abstract nonequivariant homotopy type does not determine these reflection signs or the rational homology of symmetry quotients.

## Limitations

The hypothesis \(3\nmid n\) is essential to the rank-one cycle homology argument; lengths divisible by three have higher-rank cycle homology and are not classified here. Even cycle lengths are excluded because the double cover splits into two cycles. The theorem computes integral homology representations, **not** equivariant homotopy types, and the quotient statement concerns rational homology only. The exact dihedral action is restricted to the natural layer-preserving \(D_n\) subgroup. Bibliographic novelty cannot be guaranteed against unindexed literature or an unpublished equivariant reformulation of earlier work.
