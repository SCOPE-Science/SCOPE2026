# Review of the pentagonal Dutch windmill full-symmetry homology theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies the full graph automorphism group from the unique high-degree vertex, writes the non-spoke subcomplex as a join of \(n\) contractible-pair components, and identifies every spoke link as an inclusion that is an integral homology isomorphism. The resulting equivariant long exact sequence has boundary \(\theta\otimes\varepsilon\) with \(\theta=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\), yielding an exact integral augmentation kernel. Sign under blade permutation is the Koszul sign of exchanging degree-zero join generators; within-blade reversal fixes each local \(\widetilde H_0\) generator. All \(n\ge2\) cases are covered. Exact finite signed Lefschetz and mod-two boundary checks reproduced the formulas for small parameters, but finite checks alone do not prove integral claims.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
Donovan–Scoville's inspected Theorem 30 proves the nonequivariant wedge-of-spheres result, not the full automorphism module. The earlier public blade-permutation result proves \(\operatorname{sgn}_n\otimes(\mathbf1\oplus2\mathrm{Std}_n)\) on the restricted \(S_n\) subgroup, and states that individual blade reflections and the integral lattice are outside its scope. The new theorem determines both: the entire \((C_2)^n\rtimes S_n\)-action and its exact integral lattice, not merely the restricted character or rational dimension. Bate–Everitt–Ford–Ramos analyze complete-hypergraph matching complexes, which do not specialize to Dutch windmill graphs. Residual risks are an equivalent formulation under different terminology or unindexed work; no claim of exhaustive global originality is made.

## Value — PASS
The complete integral symmetry lattice distinguishes the internal reflection action invisible under the known \(S_n\)-restriction and yields a structural kernel presentation useful for fixed-point and representation calculations. The \((C_2)^n\)-fixed subspace has rank \(n-1\); full-invariance disappears sharply for \(n\ge3\). This is an exact, natural infinite-family symmetry classification, not a routine recomputation of homology ranks. No integral direct-sum splitting or equivariant homotopy classification is asserted.
