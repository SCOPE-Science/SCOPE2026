# Integral hyperoctahedral symmetry and unbounded coinvariant two-torsion in hypercube magnitude homology

## Finding
For every integer \(n\geq1\) and \(k\geq0\), let \(Q_n=K_2^{\square n}\) be the binary Hamming cube with shortest-path metric. Its full graph-automorphism group \(B_n=(\mathbb Z/2)^n\rtimes S_n\) acts on integral magnitude homology. There is a **complete integral signed-permutation description** of this action on \(M_{n,k}=\operatorname{MH}_{k,k}(Q_n;\mathbb Z)\), with the following consequence:

\[
(M_{n,k})_{B_n}\cong\mathbb Z^{g_n(k)}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{b_n(k)}.
\]

Here \(g_n(k)\) is the number of integer partitions of \(k\) into at most \(n\) positive parts in which **no odd part occurs twice**, and \(b_n(k)\) is the number of the remaining partitions (those with a repeated odd part). The empty partition counts once when \(k=0\). In particular, the original magnitude homology is free abelian, yet its full-symmetry coinvariants contain two-torsion in every degree \(k\geq2\) when \(n\geq3\). Already for \(n=3\), the number of independent order-two classes is unbounded as \(k\) increases; for every even \(k\geq2\),
\[
 b_3(k)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{k+2}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]

More precisely, an integral basis of \(M_{n,k}\) is indexed by a starting bit vector \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\) and a weak composition \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) of \(k\). Write its generator \(z_{\epsilon,a}\). For \(t\in\{0,1\}^n\) and \(\sigma\in S_n\), the automorphism \(v\mapsto t\oplus\sigma(v)\) acts by
\[
 (t,\sigma)z_{\epsilon,a}=(-1)^{\sum_{i<j,\,\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)}a_i a_j}\,z_{t\oplus\sigma(\epsilon),\,\sigma(a)}.
\]
This formula determines the integral representation and hence every rational character value. It is not merely a statement about its rationalization.

## Assumptions and scope
Use the ordinary integral magnitude chain complex of a finite, undirected graph, with chain group \(\operatorname{MC}_{r,\ell}\) generated by proper vertex sequences of \(r\) steps and total graph-metric length \(\ell\). The differential removes an interior vertex only when doing so preserves total length, with alternating signs. In this result \(\ell=r=k\); all edges in a generating \(k\)-chain are unit graph edges.

The group \(B_n\) is the full automorphism group of the cube, acting on chains by applying graph automorphisms to each vertex. The subscript \((-)_{B_n}\) means the algebraic **coinvariant module**, namely the quotient by all relations \(gx-x\), not the magnitude homology of an orbit graph and not the singular homology of a quotient space. All statements are over \(\mathbb Z\).

## Proof
**1. Integral shuffle basis.** For \(K_2\), in each degree \(j\geq0\), the two alternating \(j\)-step walks, one starting at each endpoint, form a basis of \(\operatorname{MH}_{j,j}(K_2;\mathbb Z)\). This homology vanishes off the diagonal. The natural magnitude-homology Künneth exterior product has no Tor term for these groups and, iterated across \(n\) factors, is an isomorphism over \(\mathbb Z\). Thus its tensor factors give an integral basis indexed by \((\epsilon,a)\). The generator \(z_{\epsilon,a}\) has the explicit signed-shuffle representative
\[
 z_{\epsilon,a}=\sum_{w}\operatorname{sgn}_{\mathrm{sh}}(w)\,[v_0(w),v_1(w),\ldots,v_k(w)],
\]
where \(w\) ranges over words containing exactly \(a_i\) occurrences of coordinate \(i\), \(v_0(w)=\epsilon\), and \(v_j(w)\) is obtained by flipping the first \(j\) coordinates specified by \(w\). The shuffle sign is \((-1)\) to the number of pairs of letters appearing in reverse coordinate order. These are cycles: whenever two consecutive steps flip different coordinates, removing their intervening vertex is canceled by interchanging the two steps; two successive flips in the same coordinate backtrack and do not yield a boundary term. There are no chains in \(\operatorname{MC}_{k+1,k}\), so none of these nonzero cycles is a boundary. The Künneth isomorphism proves completeness, not merely independence. The rank is \(2^n\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}\).

**2. Full symmetry action.** Flipping a target coordinate changes only the corresponding initial bit and introduces no sign. Interchanging two source coordinates exchanges the two blocks of \(a_i\) and \(a_j\) unit steps in each shuffle; precisely \(a_i a_j\) pairs reverse order. The resulting sign is \((-1)^{a_i a_j}\). Multiplying these signs along adjacent transpositions yields the displayed inversion-weight formula for every \(\sigma\). This is a calculation in integral chain representatives, so no undetermined sign or rational scaling remains.

**3. Integral coinvariants.** The normal translation subgroup \((\mathbb Z/2)^n\) acts freely and without signs on the initial-bit label \(\epsilon\). Its coinvariants therefore have one free generator for each weak composition \(a\). The residual \(S_n\) action permutes these compositions with precisely the signs of Step 2. Its orbits correspond bijectively to integer partitions of \(k\) with at most \(n\) positive parts, padded by zeros. For any such orbit, the coinvariant group is either \(\mathbb Z\) or \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\): transport relations identify every orbit generator with a signed chosen representative, while a negative loop forces exactly the relation \(2x=0\). The stabilizer consists of permutations of positions having equal parts. A transposition between two positions with common value \(r\) acts by \((-1)^{r^2}=(-1)^r\). Hence a negative stabilizer element exists exactly when some **odd** positive part appears at least twice. Every other stabilizer generator has positive sign. Distinct orbits give independent direct summands, proving the formula and the absence of higher or odd-primary torsion.

**4. Unbounded-rank consequence.** For \(n\geq3\) and \(k\geq2\), the partition \((k-2,1,1)\) (with zero omitted when \(k=2\)) supplies at least one negative orbit. If \(n=3\) and \(k\) is even, the partitions \((j,j,k-2j)\), with \(j\) positive odd and \(2j\leq k\), are pairwise distinct and each has repeated odd part. There are \(\lfloor(k+2)/4\rfloor\) such partitions. This proves the asserted unbounded two-rank with **fixed three-cube**.

For any \((t,\sigma)\), the signed-basis rule additionally gives the character as a signed count over the weak compositions fixed by \(\sigma\), multiplied by the number of initial-bit vectors fixed by \(\epsilon\mapsto t\oplus\sigma(\epsilon)\). The latter number is zero when the bit flips have odd parity on any cycle of \(\sigma\), and otherwise equals \(2^{c(\sigma)}\). This follows directly from one linear bit equation per permutation cycle.

## Verification
The embedded `VERIFY.py` enumerates all shuffle words, applies the actual magnitude differential on their vertex sequences, and checks that every resulting shuffle chain is a cycle for \(1\leq n\leq4\) and \(0\leq k\leq4\). It also checks each adjacent-coordinate transposition and one bit flip on the full signed chain, before passing to homology. The optional exact Smith-form check constructs the integer relation matrix of the generating hyperoctahedral actions and verifies the free and torsion invariant factors at \((n,k)=(2,2),(2,6),(3,2),(3,3),(3,4)\). Those checks are finite corroboration; the preceding four proof steps apply to every admissible \(n,k\).

In particular, the exact Smith reductions yield \((M_{2,2})_{B_2}\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\), \((M_{3,3})_{B_3}\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/2\), and \((M_{3,4})_{B_3}\cong\mathbb Z^3\oplus\mathbb Z/2\).

## Relationship to prior work
The diagonal hypercube magnitude-homology ranks and natural integral Künneth shuffle isomorphism are established prior inputs, not novel claims. The 2026 Koizumi–Liu arrangement paper (Example 2.12 and Section 6.3 of version 5) gives the nonequivariant hypercube ranks and uses shuffle generators in a proof about Boolean and non-Boolean arrangements. Liu's August 2026 paper studies mod-two magnitude **cohomology products** and explicit integral cycles in certain antipodal intervals, not the full signed-coordinate representation or its integral coinvariant invariant factors. Koizumi's July 2026 tope-graph paper broadly classifies magnitude homology; the accessible abstract concerns ranks and face-flag decompositions. Its full text was not accessible for direct comparison, so potential previously unstated equivariant coverage remains a priority risk. The new assertion is the complete **integral** symmetry action and its exact partition-indexed free/two-torsion quotient, not the earlier magnitude-homology ranks.

## Limitations
The result is confined to hypercube Cartesian powers and to the integral diagonal magnitude-homology groups. It does not classify equivariant magnitude homology for arbitrary tope graphs or calculate group homology in degrees above zero. Coinvariant two-torsion must not be described as torsion in the underlying magnitude homology, which is free, nor as torsion of the metric graph quotient. The exact priority comparison with the unavailable full text of arXiv:2607.11863 remains incomplete; independent mathematical review has not been performed.
