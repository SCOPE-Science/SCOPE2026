# Integral topology of Singer quotients of rank-four finite-field buildings

## Finding

For every prime power \(q\ge2\), the ordinary geometric quotient of the rank-four spherical Tits building by its projective Singer group has precisely determined fundamental group and integral homology. Write \(F=\mathbb F_{q^4}\), \(V=F\) as a four-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_q\), \(\Delta_q=\Delta(\{0\subsetneq U\subsetneq V\})\) for the order complex of nonzero proper vector subspaces, and \(G=F^\times/\mathbb F_q^\times\). Let \(Y_q=|\Delta_q|/G\), with the quotient topology. Then

\[
\pi_1(Y_q)\cong \mathbb Z/(q^2+1)\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_i(Y_q;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
\mathbb Z/(q^2+1)\mathbb Z,&i=1,\\
\mathbb Z^{q^2(q-1)},&i=2,\\
0,&i\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]

By the universal coefficient theorem, \(H^2(Y_q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{q^2(q-1)}\oplus\mathbb Z/(q^2+1)\mathbb Z\). In particular the quotients have unbounded first-homology torsion, although the underlying buildings have torsion-free homology.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(q\) ranges over **all** prime powers, including nonprime powers such as \(q=4\). The simplex vertices of \(\Delta_q\) are proper nonzero \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspaces of dimensions one, two or three. A simplex is a strict chain; its dimension is at most two. The Singer action is projective multiplication, not the full projective-linear action. The space being classified is the ordinary orbit space \(Y_q\), not the Borel/homotopy quotient. We do not assert a wedge decomposition of \(Y_q\), and the result does not compute its higher homotopy groups or the \(\pi_1\)-module \(\pi_2\).

## Proof

**1. Building and elementary field structure.** The Solomon–Tits theorem gives \(|\Delta_q|\simeq\bigvee^{q^6}S^2\). To see the multiplicity, the Möbius value of the finite-subspace lattice is \(\mu(0,V)=(-1)^4q^{\binom42}=q^6\), which is the reduced Euler characteristic. In particular the building is connected and simply connected, with \(\chi(|\Delta_q|)=1+q^6\).

The projective Singer group is cyclic of order
\[
N=\frac{q^4-1}{q-1}=(q+1)(q^2+1).
\]
There is exactly one proper intermediate field \(E=\mathbb F_{q^2}\) between \(\mathbb F_q\) and \(F\). Write \(H=E^\times/\mathbb F_q^\times\le G\), so \(|H|=q+1\).

**2. Exact isotropy and geometric fixed sets.** For any nonidentity projective multiplication \(g=[a]\in G\), a subspace \(U\) is fixed as a vertex if and only if \(aU=U\). Thus \(U\) is a vector space over the subfield \(\mathbb F_q[a]\). If \(g\in H\setminus\{1\}\), then \(\mathbb F_q[a]=E\), so the only proper nonzero invariant \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspaces are the one-dimensional \(E\)-subspaces of the two-dimensional \(E\)-vector space \(F\). There are \(q^2+1\) of them, all of \(\mathbb F_q\)-dimension two. If \(g\notin H\), then \(\mathbb F_q[a]=F\) and there is no proper nonzero invariant subspace. Every rank-two \(E\)-line has stabilizer exactly \(H\); the \(q^2+1\) such lines form one \(G\)-orbit. All other vertices have trivial stabilizer.

The group action respects subspace dimensions. No element can nontrivially permute vertices within a simplex because all dimensions in a strict chain are distinct. Thus it acts without inversions, and a fixed geometric point must lie in a simplex with pointwise fixed vertices. As two different fixed vertices of rank two cannot be comparable, for each \(g\in H\setminus\{1\}\) the fixed realization \(|\Delta_q|^g\) consists of exactly \(q^2+1\) isolated vertices. All other nonidentity \(g\) have empty fixed sets.

**3. Fundamental group.** Armstrong's orbit-space theorem for simply connected simplicial complexes identifies \(\pi_1(|\Delta_q|/G)\) with \(G/N_G\), where \(N_G\) is the normal subgroup generated by the elements that fix some point. Here these elements are precisely \(H\). Since \(G\) is abelian, \(N_G=H\), and
\[
\pi_1(Y_q)\cong G/H\cong\mathbb Z/(q^2+1)\mathbb Z.
\]
The Hurewicz theorem in degree one gives the same cyclic group as \(H_1(Y_q;\mathbb Z)\).

**4. Euler characteristic and the free top homology.** The fixed-point Euler formula for a finite cellular action (or Burnside's formula applied to orbit cells after equivariant subdivision) yields
\[
\chi(Y_q)=\frac{1}{(q+1)(q^2+1)}\left((1+q^6)+q(q^2+1)\right)
=q^3-q^2+1.
\]
Indeed, exactly \(q\) nonidentity group elements have a nonempty fixed set, each with Euler characteristic \(q^2+1\). The quotient has a two-dimensional finite CW structure: the rank-preserving action fixes any invariant simplex pointwise. Thus \(H_i(Y_q;\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(i\ge3\). Its degree-two cellular homology is a subgroup of a finitely generated free abelian chain group, so is free. As \(H_1\) is finite, the Euler characteristic forces
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_2(Y_q;\mathbb Z)=\chi(Y_q)-1=q^3-q^2=q^2(q-1).
\]
The universal coefficient theorem then gives the claimed \(H^2\) decomposition; this requires no assertion about the ring structure or the full homotopy type.

## Relationship to prior work

Drudge's 2001 analysis of Singer cycles gives a strong prior classification of **flat orbits and their stabilizers**, including the unique intermediate-field spread of projective lines in \(\mathrm{PG}(3,q)\). The stabilizer component of the argument is therefore not claimed as novel in isolation. The present statement adds the actual geometric orbit-space fundamental group, all its integral homology groups, and the resulting unbounded integral cohomology torsion. The comparison involves statements rather than titles: flat-orbit counts do not themselves specify the topology of the flag-complex quotient. Armstrong's orbit-space theorem and Solomon–Tits sphericality are classical ingredients. Arone–Dwyer's 2001 work relates partition complexes, buildings, and the Steinberg module via equivariant homological methods; its inspected constructions do not imply the ordinary Singer quotient formulas above.

## Limitations

No full homotopy decomposition, higher homotopy groups, integral cup-product structure, or general-rank building analogue is established. Standard stabilizer and building facts are credited as prerequisites. The originality comparison covers inspected primary material, targeted searches and accessible mathematical records, not all unindexed or inaccessible literature. The finite verifier covers only \(q=2,3\). This work has not undergone independent mathematical audit.
