# Scientific review: alternating-permutation word complexes

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a finite complete integral homology classification for the parity-generated word complexes on three, four and five letters. Its basis, face maps, signs, boundary matrices, integral reductions and degree ranges are explicit. Exhaustive enumeration produces 3, 12 and 60 top cells, and exact integer unit-pivot reductions prove top Smith factors \((1^3)\), \((1^{11},2)\) and \((1^{56},2^4)\). Every consecutive differential composition is verified to vanish. The lower homology vanishes by a shared-skeleton comparison with the full injective-word complex, and the free ranks follow from independently checked boundary ranks. A second Smith-normal-form implementation reproduced the exact invariant factors. No extrapolation to higher rank is made.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations

The closest inspected primary manuscripts concern full injective-word complexes, two-generator permutation complexes, complexes generated by non-derangements, and free fundamental groups. Chachólski–Levi–Meshulam's 15-permutation \(S_4\) torsion example is different from the twelve-element alternating subgroup (only four permutations are common), so that example cannot imply the present Smith invariants. Govc's theorem is about \(\pi_1\), not higher integral torsion. Current pattern-avoiding shellability results concern different defining restrictions. Direct queries of research findings and literature located no alternating-subgroup parity-complex homology table. The full Elizalde–Hanlon–Hersh manuscript was inaccessible; priority cannot be categorically certified against unindexed sources.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The alternating subgroup is the canonical index-two, highly symmetric family of permutation generators. Exact integral torsion beginning on four letters, increasing to four independent two-torsion classes on five, is a meaningful obstruction to extending torsion-free and shellability expectations from complete or pattern-restricted word complexes. It also separates free fundamental groups from higher homological torsion in a natural subgroup family. These are precise, reusable finite benchmark examples, rather than an arbitrary subset selection.

## Scientific limits

The result does not establish a homotopy decomposition, equivariant character, or infinite-family formula. Source access and unpublished work leave a residual novelty risk. The computational evidence is independently reproducible but no external mathematical audit has been performed.
