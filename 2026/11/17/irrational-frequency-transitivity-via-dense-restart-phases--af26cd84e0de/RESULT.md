# Irrational-frequency transitivity via dense restart phases

## Finding
For every irrational \(p\in(0,1)\), the one-sided frequency shift \(\Sigma(p)\) of Becker--Baraviera is topologically transitive. Precisely, for all words \(u,v\in\mathcal L(\Sigma(p))\) there exists a finite word \(w\) such that \(uwv\in\mathcal L(\Sigma(p))\). The proof gives an explicit bridge search based on a dense irrational rotation. No mixing, specification, or intrinsic ergodicity claim is made.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix irrational \(p\in(0,1)\) and \(\beta=p/(1-p)>0\), also irrational. For a finite binary word \(z\), define \(D(z)=\beta |z|_0-|z|_1\). A word is *admissible* if, whenever its digit in position \(i\) is \(1\) and there is a next digit, the next digit is \(1\) exactly when the discrepancy of the prefix ending at \(i\) is nonnegative. A zero may be followed by either digit. Let \(P(p)\) consist of sequences whose every prefix is admissible, and \(\Sigma(p)=\overline{\bigcup_{j\ge0}\sigma^j P(p)}\). Reyes, Lemma 2.7, proves that its language consists exactly of factors of admissible finite words. All statements apply to the one-sided language as defined, not to an alternative stationary discrepancy model.

## Proof
**1. Strictness at an initial admissible prefix.** For any nonempty prefix \(z\), if \(|z|_0>0\), the number \(D(z)=\beta|z|_0-|z|_1\) is irrational and hence not zero. If \(|z|_0=0\), then \(D(z)=-|z|_1<0\). Consequently every finitely many transition tests in an admissible prefix have a nonzero margin. More precisely, if a finite word \(W\) is admissible from discrepancy zero, there is \(\delta(W)>0\) such that all tests on positions after its first symbol preserve their prescribed signs under any discrepancy offset of absolute value less than \(\delta(W)\).

**2. A closing run with interior discrepancy.** Given any admissible prefix \(U\), append the forced ones (if its last symbol is one and its discrepancy is nonnegative) until the discrepancy becomes negative, then append a zero as required; if its last one already has negative discrepancy, append a zero directly. If its last symbol is zero, no preliminary change is needed. Append sufficiently many zeros to obtain a positive discrepancy \(d\) before a new run of ones, then append exactly \(\lfloor d\rfloor+1\) ones. Since the resulting prefix has at least one zero, \(d\) is irrational and the discrepancy after this final one is \(e=d-\lfloor d\rfloor-1\in(-1,0)\). The run can therefore be followed by a zero. The original prefix \(U\) is retained unchanged.

**3. Dense closing states.** Starting at such a last one with discrepancy \(e\in(-1,0)\), append \(m\) zeros, then exactly \(L_m=\lfloor e+\beta m\rfloor+1\) ones, with \(m\) any sufficiently large positive integer. Its closing discrepancy is
\[
 e_m=e+\beta m-L_m=\{e+\beta m\}-1\in(-1,0).
\]
Here the braces denote the fractional part, and none of the arguments is an integer because the total word includes a positive number of zeros and \(\beta\) is irrational. The irrational rotation theorem shows that \(\{e+\beta m\}\), for positive integers \(m\), is dense in \((0,1)\). Thus the closing discrepancy \(e_m\) can be chosen arbitrarily close to \(-1\) from above, or to \(0\) from below, and the chosen finite extension is admissible.

**4. Restart lemma for any admissible prefix.** Let \(W\) be a nonempty admissible prefix. If its first digit is zero, take \(e_m\) sufficiently close to zero from below and follow the new last one by the zero that begins \(W\), and then by the remaining digits of \(W\). The forced transition from that last one to zero is valid because \(e_m<0\); after the initial zero the discrepancy in this constructed word is \(\beta+e_m\), differing from the original prefix's \(\beta\) by only \(e_m\). Every later transition test is therefore the corresponding original strict test plus the same small offset \(e_m\), and remains valid. If the first digit of \(W\) is one, take \(e_m\) close to \(-1\) from above and regard the new last one itself as the first digit of \(W\). An admissible prefix beginning with one and having at least two digits necessarily begins with \(10\), since its first one ends with discrepancy \(-1\); the constructed last one has discrepancy \(e_m<0\), so the next zero is likewise forced. All later transition tests differ from those of the original \(W\) by the arbitrarily small offset \(e_m+1\), so they remain valid. For the one-letter word the conclusion is immediate. Hence \(W\) embeds after any preexisting admissible prefix, with a finite bridge.

**5. Transitivity.** Given \(u,v\in\mathcal L(\Sigma(p))\), select admissible prefixes \(U=a u\) and \(W=b v\), possible because every language word is a factor of an admissible word. Apply Steps 2--4 to extend \(U\) so that \(W\) occurs later. The result is an admissible finite word containing \(u\) before \(v\). The intervening segment is the required finite bridge \(w\), proving topological transitivity. \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone `checker.py` included in this package enumerates all admissible starting prefixes of lengths one through six and successfully constructs a connecting admissible prefix for every ordered pair, for four irrational test parameters \(\sqrt2\), \(\sqrt3/2\), \((1+\sqrt5)/2\), and \(\pi\). Its floating-point checks are finite corroboration only. The infinite theorem follows from the strict-sign and irrational-rotation argument above, not from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Becker--Baraviera (2023) introduced the frequency shift and proved basic finite-type and entropy properties. Reyes (2026), Sections 2 and 5, established the discrepancy formalism and determined transitivity at parameters \(k/(k+1)\); Question 7.1 explicitly asks for a general transitivity classification. Those rational parameters have no irrational rotation. The present theorem determines the uncountable complementary set of irrational parameters, without extending the rational classifications already in the literature. Its dense-restart mechanism is distinct from the integer-discrepancy renewal mechanism. A full text of Becker--Baraviera was not publicly obtainable in the present source inspection, so earlier independent priority cannot be completely excluded.

## Limitations
No rate or uniform bound for the connecting bridge is asserted; for badly approximable and Liouville parameters the required length may be very different. The theorem says nothing about topological mixing, synchronizing words, intrinsic ergodicity, or rational parameters. Its constructive search relies crucially on irrationality: at rational parameters, zero discrepancies may give non-robust boundary transitions and irrational rotation density is unavailable.
