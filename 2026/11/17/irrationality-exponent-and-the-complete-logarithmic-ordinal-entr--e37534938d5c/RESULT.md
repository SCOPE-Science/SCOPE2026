# Irrationality exponent and the complete logarithmic ordinal-entropy spectrum of rotations

## Finding
For an irrational number \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), let \(R_\alpha(x)=\{x+\alpha\}\) act on \([0,1)\) with Lebesgue probability. The order-\(n\) **raw ordinal-pattern entropy** \(H_n(\alpha)\) is the Shannon entropy, in nats, of the permutation which sorts the real representatives
\[
\{x\},\{x+\alpha\},\ldots,\{x+(n-1)\alpha\},
\]
with \(x\) uniformly distributed. Let \(\mu(\alpha)\in[2,\infty]\) denote its irrationality exponent. Then, with \(1/\infty=0\),
\[
\boxed{\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_n(\alpha)}{\log n}=\frac1{\mu(\alpha)-1},\qquad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_n(\alpha)}{\log n}=1.}
\]
More precisely, the **entire subsequential accumulation set** of \(H_n(\alpha)/\log n\), for integer \(n\ge2\), is the closed interval
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Acc}_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{H_n(\alpha)}{\log n}\right)
=\left[\frac1{\mu(\alpha)-1},1\right].}
\]
In particular, all irrational slopes of exponent two have \(H_n(\alpha)\sim\log n\); every Liouville slope has the full normalized accumulation interval \([0,1]\), despite its rotation having zero Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy. This describes raw, single-window pattern entropy, **not** the usual per-step entropy or factorial-normalized permutation entropy.

## Assumptions and scope
The phase \(x\) is Lebesgue-uniform, the rotation is irrational, the displayed orbit coordinates are ordered as real numbers in \([0,1)\), and logarithms are natural. No finite-type, bounded-partial-quotient, Diophantine, or mixing hypothesis is imposed. Distinct orbit coordinates never tie. The irrationality exponent is
\[
\mu(\alpha)=\sup\left\{v>0:\left|\alpha-\frac pq\right|<q^{-v}\text{ for infinitely many reduced }p/q\right\}.
\]
For every \(n\), \(H_n\) is the unnormalized Shannon entropy of a distribution with exactly \(n\) atoms, so \(0\le H_n\le\log n\). The theorem concerns a **specified geometric observation** of rotations: Shannon entropy at finite ordinal order is not invariant under arbitrary measurable conjugacy. In standard Sturmian coding, the same partition is the one generated by consecutive finite symbolic words after the appropriate index shift.

## Proof
**Step 1: ordinal patterns and rotation gaps.** Let \(P_n=\{-j\alpha\pmod 1:0\le j<n\}\). Between two consecutive circular points of \(P_n\), no observed real coordinate crosses zero, so its ordinal pattern remains constant. At a cut \(-j\alpha\), coordinate \(j\) passes from the largest to the smallest position of the real order. The circular order of the \(n\) distinct coordinate labels is fixed, and each gap has a different first-coordinate label in its linear ordering. Thus the \(n\) gaps give \(n\) distinct ordinal patterns. Denoting their lengths by \(g_{n,1},\ldots,g_{n,n}\),
\[
H_n=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{n,i}\log g_{n,i}.
\]
The reflected point set \(P_n\) has the same gap multiset as \(\{j\alpha\pmod1:0\le j<n\}\).

**Step 2: uniform lower entropy bound from convergents.** Write \(p_k/q_k\) for the reduced regular continued-fraction convergents of \(\alpha\), with \(q_k\to\infty\), and put \(q=q_k\), \(Q=q_{k+1}\). The standard bound \(|\alpha-p_k/q|<1/(qQ)\) implies that the first \(q\) rotation points lie within circular distance \(1/Q\) of a permuted regular \(q\)-point grid, because \(\gcd(p_k,q)=1\). Consequently their largest gap is at most \(1/q+2/Q\le3/q\). Additional points cannot increase the maximum gap. For every \(n\ge q\), each gap probability therefore satisfies \(g_{n,i}\le3/q\); in particular,
\[
\boxed{H_n\ge\log(q/3)\qquad(n\ge q).}\tag{1}
\]
The lower bound may be negative at the first few convergents, which causes no difficulty.

**Step 3: matching upper entropy bound before the next convergent.** For \(1\le n\le Q/4\), each of the \(n\) cut points \(-j\alpha\) lies within circular distance \(\delta=n/(qQ)\) of a grid point of \(q^{-1}\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z\). Partition \([0,1)\) into its \(q\) regular grid cells, and let \(I\) be the cell containing a uniform random phase. Outside the union \(E\) of the \(\delta\)-neighborhoods of the \(q\) grid boundaries, the ordinal-pattern label \(J\) is constant within each such cell. Moreover, \(\Pr(E)\le2q\delta=2n/Q=:\varepsilon\le1/2\). Given the cell and the indicator of \(E\), the label is deterministic when \(E\) does not occur and takes at most \(n\) values when it does. The entropy chain rule and binary-entropy function \(h(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t)\) give
\[
\begin{split}
H_n=H(J)&\le H(I)+H(J\mid I)\\
&\le\log q+h(\Pr E)+\Pr(E)\log n\\
&\le\boxed{\log q+h(2n/Q)+(2n/Q)\log n}.
\end{split}\tag{2}
\]
There is no assertion of independence between the grid cell and the boundary event.

**Step 4: lower and upper logarithmic limits.** The elementary upper bound \(H_n\le\log n\), together with (1) at \(n=q_k\), proves \(\limsup H_n/\log n=1\). For \(q_k\le n<q_{k+1}\), (1) and \(\log n<\log q_{k+1}\) yield
\[
\frac{H_n}{\log n}\ge\frac{\log q_k-\log3}{\log q_{k+1}}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(k\). Define \(\vartheta=\liminf_{k\to\infty}(\log q_k)/(\log q_{k+1})\), so \(\liminf H_n/\log n\ge\vartheta\). If \(\vartheta=1\), this and \(H_n\le\log n\) finish the proof of the lower limit. If \(0\le\vartheta<1\), choose convergents along which the displayed ratio tends to \(\vartheta\). Then \(Q/q\) grows superpolynomially in \(\log Q\), so
\[
n_k=\left\lfloor\frac{Q}{(\log Q)^2}\right\rfloor
\]
satisfies \(q\le n_k\le Q/4\) for sufficiently large selected indices, and \(\log n_k/\log Q\to1\). Applying both (1) and (2), with \(\varepsilon=O((\log Q)^{-2})\), gives
\[
H_{n_k}=\log q+O(1),\qquad
\frac{H_{n_k}}{\log n_k}\longrightarrow\vartheta.
\]
Thus \(\liminf H_n/\log n=\vartheta\) in all cases. The classical continued-fraction identity
\[
\mu(\alpha)=1+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{k+1}}{\log q_k}
\]
then gives \(\vartheta=1/(\mu(\alpha)-1)\). This identity follows from the upper and lower convergent error estimates together with the fact that every sufficiently accurate rational approximation (in particular, one with error smaller than \(1/(2q^2)\)) must be a convergent. Infinite irrationality exponent is included.

**Step 5: no gaps in the accumulation set.** Going from \(P_n\) to \(P_{n+1}\) splits exactly one circular gap of length \(g\) into two pieces. Its Shannon entropy gain is \(g\,h(t)\) for some \(t\in(0,1)\), so
\[
0\le H_{n+1}-H_n\le\log2.
\]
Using \(0\le H_n\le\log n\) gives
\[
\left|\frac{H_{n+1}}{\log(n+1)}-\frac{H_n}{\log n}\right|
\le\frac{\log2}{\log(n+1)}+\frac{\log(n+1)-\log n}{\log(n+1)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
A bounded real sequence whose increments tend to zero has a connected set of subsequential limits: whenever a level lies strictly between its liminf and limsup, infinitely many upcrossings pass arbitrarily close to that level. Its accumulation set is consequently the whole closed interval between the two proven endpoints.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` using Python 3 and the `mpmath` package. The standalone script calculates ordered rotation gaps with 90-decimal-digit arithmetic, confirms that all phase intervals for orders \(2\) through \(11\) represent distinct ordinal patterns, and checks finite gap and entropy bounds for selected convergent denominators, including a deliberately large partial quotient \(2000\). It also prints normalized entropies for golden rotation and a controlled near-rational example. These experiments test normalization and indexing but do not establish the infinite theorem; Steps 1--5 provide its proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Avhale, Diehl, Velankar, and Verri [1] develop global permutation entropy, contrasting ordinary consecutive ordinal patterns with nonconsecutive sampling. The observable here is **only ordinary, raw consecutive-pattern Shannon entropy**, not their global index. The motivation is to determine exactly what asymptotic arithmetic information ordinary finite-order ordinal patterns encode in zero-entropy rotations. An earlier exact golden-slope study [2] proves a Fibonacci staircase and \(H_n/\log n\to1\) at a **single quadratic irrational**; it does not cover the strict lower endpoint for slopes of irrationality exponent greater than two, the Liouville case, or the general accumulation interval.

Cheng, Ospina, Vinhage, and Zhai [3, Section 5.1] give all-slope three-gap cylinder frequencies and show a polynomial slow-entropy variational failure for Sturmian subshifts. These exact gap tables provide the classical frequency input used here; their slow entropy is a different invariant and they do not state the Diophantine log-Shannon spectrum. Another published study [4] identifies the same irrationality exponent in **cohomological transfer regularity**, using maximum gap lengths; its oscillation observable and conclusions do not give the entropy upper bound (2) or its cluster interval. Thus the shared exponent is not presented as a new Diophantine identity; the new result is its exact role in finite-order Shannon information of all irrational rotations. Unakafov and Keller [5] provide general ordinal conditional-/sorting-entropy foundations. The novelty claim is restricted to the unified exact Shannon information spectrum, not to continued fractions, the three-gap theorem, or polynomial slow entropy.

## Limitations
The result applies only to irrational rotations observed by increasing real representatives with a uniform initial phase, not to arbitrary observation functions, sparse/nonconsecutive ordinal samples, noisy empirical data, rational rotations, or arbitrary interval exchanges. The theorem is a finite-order, one-window Shannon statement, not a positive metric entropy assertion. The full gap law was already known; the additional insight is the strict arithmetic classification of its information growth and exact cluster set. Searches do not establish absolute priority: a prior result in older Sturmian-frequency or information-spectrum literature under different terminology could cover the asymptotic consequence. No independent scientific audit has been conducted.
