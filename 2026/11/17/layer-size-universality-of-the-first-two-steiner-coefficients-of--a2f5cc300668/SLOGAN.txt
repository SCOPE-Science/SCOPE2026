For a chain polytope built from \(r\ge3\) successive antichains, the surface-area/volume ratio is exactly \(n(r+\sqrt r)\) and the next Steiner coefficient also has a closed formula depending only on the dimension \(n\) and the number of layers \(r\), not their individual sizes. An exact three-class ridge census explains why exponentially different facet systems share these normalized Euclidean invariants.
