# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement is one coherent exact geometry theorem for a precisely defined family. The proof derives the complete slice and facet descriptions, proves facet irredundancy by relative-interior support faces, and obtains an inradius upper bound from two full-set facet types and all coordinate lower bounds. Equality fixes the unique center, while the strict subset-size barrier identifies the full tangent facet set. Complementary Boolean vertices give the exact diameter, minimum enclosing sphere and all contact pairs; integrating product slices gives the volume formula, with an analytic Laplace calculation for the planar base blocks. The standalone program, replayed from its actual file, reported 103232 finite checks, including independent convex-hull facet counts and linear programming. These checks are corroborative, not a replacement for the all-parameter proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The facet and Euclidean metric classification is motivated by a natural open structural question about the newly established counterexamples to universal graph edge expansion: do they rely on enormous facet complexity or extreme Euclidean thinness? In the original k=2 family the answer is sharply negative: the polytope has 10n+2 facets in dimension 4n+1, a unique inscribed ball of constant radius more than 80% of the unit cube's, and maximal Boolean diameter. The general block-dimension and volume laws quantify this geometry rather than merely recompute the source's graph invariant.
