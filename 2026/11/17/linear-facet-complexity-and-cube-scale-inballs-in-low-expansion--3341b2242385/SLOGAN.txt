The Boolean Cayley polytopes behind Yang's low-edge-expansion counterexample have a complete explicit facet system and an exact, unique Euclidean inball whose radius is independent of the number of blocks: \(r_k=(k+1)/(2(k+\sqrt{k^2-k+1}))\). In the original square–triangle case, the dimension-\((4n+1)\) counterexample has only \(10n+2\) facets, retains more than \(80\%\) of the unit cube's inradius, and still exhibits exponentially poor graph edge expansion by the source theorem.
