# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

Every input, interval family, and norm is defined. The universal upper bound uses only the oscillation of the perturbation. The finite lower bound has an explicit \(24q\)-site construction and an exact, disjoint odd/even output-branch analysis; the number of responding edges is \(4q-3\), and the ratio is at least \((4q-3)/2\). Zero extension retains those interior inequalities and changes perturbation variation from one jump to two, proving unbounded ratios on \(BV(\mathbb Z)\). Positive homogeneity moves the examples into every neighborhood of zero. Exact-rational finite checks test the critical branch inequalities independently of floating-point roundoff. Remaining limit: the exact finite-dimensional constants are not computed.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The original 2010 discrete Tanaka theorem concerns the variation of one maximal output, not the variation of the difference of two outputs. The 2017 theorem proves continuity of \(\mathcal M:BV(\mathbb Z)\to BV(\mathbb Z)\), without a local Lipschitz modulus. The sharp 2010 pointwise Lipschitz theorem and a later finite weighted-path result use the maximum adjacent slope of a single function, not two-input total variation. This makes their conclusions non-dominating rather than algebraic equivalents. A direct primary full-text comparison was made for the 2010 and 2017 maximal results, and the published weighted-path result was also read as full text. Priority caveat: no bibliographic search can exclude an unindexed equivalent example.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The growth-rate classification \(L_n=\Theta(n)\) is a structural dimension-dependent stability law for a canonical nonlinear operator and natural variation norm. More significantly, an explicit positive-cone counterexample separates one-input contraction and global \(BV\)-continuity from local Lipschitz regularity. This supplies a sharp *qualitative boundary* in a long-standing regularity problem; the exact finite constants are not required for that boundary statement.

## Scope and residual risks

The interval family must contain every contiguous interval, including those with even cardinality, rather than only centered graph-distance balls. Constants \(2\lfloor n/24\rfloor-3/2\) and \(n-1\) are lower and upper bounds, not asserted exact. The infinite-line result is for compactly supported nonnegative inputs and a local failure at the origin. An earlier unstated counterexample in general maximal-operator literature remains possible. The reviewed analytic inequalities are independent of the finite checker.
