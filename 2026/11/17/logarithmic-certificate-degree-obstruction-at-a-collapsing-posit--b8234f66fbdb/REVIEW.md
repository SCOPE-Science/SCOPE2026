# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof specifies the matrix polynomial, natural three-generator preordering, positivity, weighted-degree convention and asymptotic quantifiers. All certificate weights are uniformly positive on a fixed interior interval. A Bernstein–Walsh/Cauchy estimate gives uniform \(R^d\) derivative bounds for every certificate matrix. At the origin, positive semidefiniteness yields first-column vanishing, a precise off-diagonal jet inequality, and the bound \(1\leq C_R(\sqrt{\varepsilon R^d}+\varepsilon^2R^d)\). For each \(R>3+2\sqrt2\), this forces the claimed asymptotic lower degree; the limit in \(R\) proves the coefficient. Exact polynomial checks are supplementary. The source paper establishes existence of the certificates, so the minimum degree is finite.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Section 6 of Jindal–Zalar (arXiv:2611.02967v1) proves only \(d_\varepsilon\to\infty\) by subsequence compactness. The inspected source sections, earlier Zalar matrix Fejér–Riesz results, Sun–Zalar's isolated-point classification and recent general Putinar complexity work do not state or imply a numerical logarithmic lower growth rate for this particular moving set and matrix polynomial. The contribution is *not* a new counterexample; it is an effective quantitative refinement for the already-known example. Literature and research-record comparisons were performed on the statement and on potential aliases (weighted-degree SOS complexity, collapsing intervals, matrix Positivstellensatz, polynomial extrapolation). Hidden equivalent older results remain a residual priority risk; search failure is not proof of novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The motivating operator-theory paper highlights nonuniformity of certificate degrees when a positivity interval collapses. A divergence statement alone cannot predict the growth cost. The proved geometric coefficient supplies a usable lower bound for the complexity of exact matrix positivity certificates as geometry degenerates, with the conformal distance of the stable interval controlling the obstruction. This is a motivated asymptotic invariant of a source-defined natural family, not an arbitrary parameter slice. No paper-sized extension, tight upper bound or practical degree formula is asserted.

The closest 2026 source introduced the matrices and qualitative nonuniformity. The present result does not challenge that priority. A matching upper rate, optimality of the coefficient, and nonasymptotic constants are open here. Polynomial sup-norm bounds can be equivalent to classical approximation estimates, but the positivity-jet obstruction is additional. No independent review has been performed.
