For two independent arclength-uniform endpoints on a convex polygon boundary, the small nonzero area cut off by their chord has an explicit logarithmic dust law, corner weights proportional to reciprocal sines of interior angles, and an independent uniform logarithmic endpoint split. The scale-normalized leading coefficient is uniquely minimized by equiangular polygons and satisfies a sharp angular-variance stability inequality; triangles also have a complete exact nonzero fragment-area distribution.
