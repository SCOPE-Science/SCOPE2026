## Correctness — PASS

The polar Lipschitz polytope is full-dimensional modulo constants. A centered potential is extreme precisely when its tight-edge graph connects all vertices. Given two internally equilateral clusters, connectedness forces one cross-cluster tight edge; using its orientation fixes all potentials to one of the two antipodal families with levels \(\{0,s\}\) on the smaller cluster and \(\{1-t,1\}\) on the larger. Necessity and sufficiency are established, so the squared-norm maximization has no unexamined vertex classes.

The key nonstandard point is the strict screening lemma. For each configuration with at least one smaller-cluster point at level \(s\), either reducing that count or jointly reducing it and the larger cluster's count strictly increases centered squared norm. The proof checks separately the \(y=0\), \(s>t\), and \(t\ge s\) cases; every gap is strictly positive for all \(2\le p\le q\) and real \(0<s,t<1\). Once the smaller-cluster count is zero, the exact nearest-integer rule follows by completing a concave square. Contact-facet multiplicity is binomial. For every active norm, the central opposite facet forces orthogonality of possible inball centers; slack at all inactive facets establishes relative interior in the orthogonal complement. Contact normals have exact ranks one or \(q\). This proves the radius and both center-dimension alternatives globally, not merely at the origin.

The canonical Euclidean radius, tangent faces and set of all maximal-ball centers are intrinsic, reproducible invariants once the zero-sum embedding is specified. The main structural claim is that the entire smaller-cluster distance disappears from the optimal inradius and incenter affine hull, even though it affects the defining polytope; this is proved by a strict exchange argument. ## Scientific limitations

Only the canonical coordinate Euclidean realization is addressed; it is not the Banach–Mazur-optimal Euclidean structure. The theorem requires exactly two equilateral clusters, \(2\le p\le q\), distinct points, and strict \(s,t\in(0,1)\). No assertions are made about other cluster geometries, ambient rescalings, external certification or exhaustive global historical priority.
