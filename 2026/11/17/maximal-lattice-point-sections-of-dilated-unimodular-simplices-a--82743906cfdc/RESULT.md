# Maximal lattice-point sections of dilated unimodular simplices are exactly their faces

## Finding
Let \(d\ge 2\), let \(k\ge 1\) and \(1\le \ell\le d-1\) be integers, and let \(\Delta_d=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)\subset\mathbb R^d\). For each affine \(\ell\)-plane \(E\subset\mathbb R^d\), count its points in \(k\Delta_d\cap\mathbb Z^d\). Then
\[
\max_{\dim E=\ell}\bigl|E\cap k\Delta_d\cap\mathbb Z^d\bigr|
=\binom{k+\ell}{\ell},
\]
and equality holds **if and only if** \(E\) is the affine hull of an \(\ell\)-dimensional face of \(k\Delta_d\). Consequently, the number of different maximizing \(\ell\)-dimensional sections is exactly
\[
L^{(\ell)}_{\Delta_d}(k)=\binom{d+1}{\ell+1}
\qquad(k\ge1).
\]
The statement extends without alteration to every unimodular simplex by an integral-affine lattice automorphism. In particular, for \(d\ge3\) and \(2\le\ell\le d-1\), this *constant*, positive counting function has degree zero, rather than the positive degree \(d-\ell\) predicted literally for every lattice polytope in the higher-dimensional maximal-slice conjecture of Brose and collaborators. The result does not refute the weaker statement that the eventual quasi-polynomial degree is **at most** \(d-\ell\).

## Assumptions and scope
All sections are intersections \(E\cap k\Delta_d\) with unrestricted affine real \(\ell\)-planes \(E\), including boundary faces; the maximization counts *integer points* in these sections, not Euclidean section volumes or their vertices. Maximizers contain \(\ell+1\) affinely independent lattice points and therefore define a unique affine \(\ell\)-plane. Dilations \(k\) are positive integers. The reference simplex is unimodular, and the lattice is the standard \(\mathbb Z^d\). The original conjecture applies to \(2\le\ell\le d-1\), although the classification above also covers \(\ell=1\).

## Proof
**Coordinate-projection bound.** Let \(V\) be the \(\ell\)-dimensional direction space of \(E\). The restrictions of the \(d\) coordinate functionals span \(V^*\), so a set \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\) of exactly \(\ell\) coordinates restricts to a basis. The coordinate projection \(\pi_I\) is therefore one-to-one on \(E\), and it sends every integer point in \(k\Delta_d\) to an integer vector \(y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\ell}\) with \(\sum_{i\in I}y_i\le k\). By stars and bars, this target contains exactly \(\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}\) points. This proves the upper bound. Each \(\ell\)-face of the simplex is a unimodular \(\ell\)-simplex dilated by \(k\), so it contains exactly that many lattice points and attains the bound.

**Equality rigidity.** Suppose \(E\) attains the upper bound. Since \(\pi_I\) is injective, every integer \(y\ge0\) with coordinate sum at most \(k\) has a unique *integer* preimage on \(E\cap k\Delta_d\). Reorder the coordinates so that \(I\) comes first. Write \(a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{d-\ell}\) for the remaining coordinates of the point over \(y=0\); they satisfy \(\sum a_j\le k\). The preimage of \(y=k e_i\), for every \(i\in I\), must have all the remaining coordinates equal to zero, since its projected coordinate sum already equals \(k\). These \(\ell+1\) preimages are affinely independent, and hence span \(E\). Therefore the only possible coordinates on \(E\) above any \(y\) are
\[
z(y)=a\left(1-\frac{\sum_{i\in I}y_i}{k}\right).
\]
For \(k\ge2\), set \(y=e_i\). Then \(a_j(k-1)/k\) must be an integer for each component, so \(k\mid a_j\), because \(\gcd(k,k-1)=1\). With \(a_j\ge0\) and \(\sum a_j\le k\), it follows that either \(a=0\) or \(a=k e_j\) for a single coordinate outside \(I\). If \(k=1\), the same alternatives follow directly from \(a\ge0\), integral, and \(\sum a_j\le1\). In the first case \(E\) is the affine hull of the face with vertices \(0\) and \(k e_i\) (\(i\in I\)); in the second it is the affine hull of the face with vertices \(k e_j\) and \(k e_i\) (\(i\in I\)). Thus **every** equality plane is the affine hull of an \(\ell\)-face.

**Number of maximizers and dimension law.** The \(d\)-simplex has exactly \(\binom{d+1}{\ell+1}\) distinct \(\ell\)-faces, whose affine hulls are different. Each achieves equality, and there are no other maximizers. The count is constant in \(k\), of degree zero. The stated higher-dimensional conjecture prescribes a quasi-polynomial of degree \(d-\ell>0\) for these same unrestricted maximizers, so its exact-degree formulation fails already for \(d=3,\ell=2\): there are exactly four maximizing planes at every positive dilation. All steps are invariant under unimodular affine changes of coordinates.

## Verification
The included file `verify.py` is a Python standard-library-only exact checker. It enumerates lattice points of dilated simplices, constructs the affine flats generated by every affinely independent \((\ell+1)\)-tuple, canonicalizes each flat by rational reduced row echelon form, and aggregates all its integer points. It independently verifies the optimum, number of maximizers, and equality of maximizing flats with the affine hulls of all appropriate faces. Executing `python verify.py` checks 18 finite \((d,k,\ell)\) cases and 2,499 different affine-flat classes. These computations are corroboration only: they cannot establish the all-dimensions theorem without the coordinate-projection proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Brose, De Loera, Lopez-Campos and Torres introduced lattice-diameter segments and an Ehrhart-type maximal-line-counting question (arXiv:2508.20009, first released August 27, 2025). The revised paper with Averkov proves eventual quasi-polynomiality for **one-dimensional lattice-diameter segments**, and its higher-dimensional maximal-slice conjecture states degree \(d-\ell\) for \(2\le\ell\le d-1\). This work classifies the maximizing lattice-point slices of every dimension in one natural test family and shows why the *exact degree* cannot hold universally. It does **not** disprove eventual quasi-polynomiality, a formulation with degree \(\le d-\ell\), or a hypothetical different conjecture expressly restricted to sections passing through the interior.

Birkemeyer, Brose, Brandenburg and Prün (arXiv:2609.16195) study combinatorial data such as vertex counts of sections, rather than maximizing integer-point counts. Their public abstract was checked, but their full manuscript was not available for a complete claim-by-claim exclusion. Continuous-section-volume and Ehrhart-point-counting results also involve different extremal questions.

## Limitations
The theorem is for unimodular simplices and positive integer dilates, not arbitrary lattice polytopes. The proof makes no assertion about the eventual quasi-polynomial degree for non-unimodular examples or all polytopes. Its identification of a contradiction uses the usual **exact** meaning of polynomial degree; it does not contradict an intended *upper-degree bound* interpretation or any conjecture restricted to sections meeting the interior rather than allowing faces. Historical originality is provisional because an earlier equivalent lattice-slice maximization lemma may be unindexed. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
