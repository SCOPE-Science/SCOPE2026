# Mixed-dimensional homotopy and affine symmetry of the five-letter permutation complex

## Finding
Let \(\mathbb F_5=\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), and let \(G=\operatorname{AGL}(1,5)\) consist of the twenty permutations \(\sigma_{a,b}(x)=ax+b\) for \(a\in\mathbb F_5^\times\) and \(b\in\mathbb F_5\). Define \(X(G)\) to be the ordered-word permutation complex generated by the one-line words \((\sigma_{a,b}(0),\ldots,\sigma_{a,b}(4))\): a \(k\)-cell is each distinct ordered subword of length \(k+1\), with its alternating deletion boundary. Its geometric realization has integral homotopy type
\[
|X(G)|\simeq S^2\vee\bigvee^{17}S^3.
\]
Under relabeling by \(G\), its rank-one integral second homology is the multiplier quadratic-character lattice \(\mathbb Z_\chi\), where \(\chi(\sigma_{a,b})=1\) for \(a=1,4\), and \(-1\) for \(a=2,3\). Moreover, as rational \(G\)-modules,
\[
\widetilde H_3(X(G);\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q_\chi\oplus\mathbb Q[C_4]\oplus3V,
\]
where \(\mathbb Q[C_4]\) is the regular module of the multiplier quotient \(G/\mathbb F_5\cong C_4\), inflated to \(G\), and \(V=\ker(\mathbb Q^{\mathbb F_5}\to\mathbb Q)\) is the four-dimensional point-permutation augmentation representation.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is exactly \(\mathbb F_5\), multiplication and addition are modulo five, and every affine permutation with nonzero multiplier is included. Ordered subwords are cells: two different orders of the same vertex set give distinct cells. The semisimplicial realization has the homotopy type of the barycentrically subdivided order-complex convention for permutation complexes. Homology is unreduced in degree zero and reduced in positive degrees; all homotopy statements are nonequivariant unless an action is explicitly specified.

## Proof
**Cells and boundaries.** Enumerating the twenty progressions \((b,b+a,b+2a,b+3a,b+4a)\) and their subwords yields precisely \(5,20,60,80,20\) cells in degrees zero through four. There are all \(5\cdot4\cdot3=60\) possible ordered triples, so the two-skeleton agrees with the two-skeleton of the full injective-word complex on five letters. The latter is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of four-spheres by the Farmer–Björner–Wachs result, so its two-skeleton, and hence \(X(G)\), is simply connected.

On integral cellular chains, let \(\partial_k[w_0\ldots w_k]=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j[w_0\ldots\widehat{w_j}\ldots w_k]\). An exact Smith reduction of these explicit matrices gives the following complete nonzero invariant factors:

| Boundary | Matrix size | Nonzero Smith factors |
|---|---|---|
| \(\partial_1\) | \(5\times20\) | four \(1\)'s |
| \(\partial_2\) | \(20\times60\) | sixteen \(1\)'s |
| \(\partial_3\) | \(60\times80\) | forty-three \(1\)'s |
| \(\partial_4\) | \(80\times20\) | twenty \(1\)'s |

All other Smith factors are zero. In particular \(\partial_{k-1}\partial_k=0\), and the reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z\), \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{17}\), and zero in every other degree. The Smith computation is fully reproducible in `check.py`, which constructs the matrices from the definition, without reading any precomputed matrix or result.

**Homotopy, rather than just homology.** Every affine five-word has a free four-letter face obtained by deleting its second letter. To verify this uniformly, normalize an affine word to \((0,1,2,3,4)\); the four-letter word \((0,2,3,4)\) is a subword of no other affine five-word, as one sees by testing the twenty possible multiplier-translation pairs. Affine relabeling preserves this uniqueness. These twenty different free faces permit twenty elementary collapses, deleting every four-cell and leaving a three-dimensional complex \(Y\) with the same two-skeleton.

That simply connected two-skeleton is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres, with second homology of rank \(60-16=44\). The remaining sixty three-cells attach by elements of its second homotopy group, identified with this free second homology by Hurewicz. Their integral attaching matrix has forty-three unit Smith factors (the original third boundary modulo the twenty free four-cell pairs). Unimodular changes of sphere generators and of attaching maps cancel those forty-three sphere–cell pairs, leaving precisely one two-sphere and seventeen three-spheres. These changes preserve homotopy type. This proves the asserted wedge decomposition.

**Symmetry.** The indicated rank-one cohomology class can be represented by the integral two-cochain whose nonzero coefficients, on oriented three-letter words, are
\[
-[021]+[032]+[042]+[103]-[120]+[142]+[143]-[213]+[230]+[240]+[241]+[301]-[312]+[341].
\]
The explicit matrices verify that its coboundary vanishes, that its class is not a coboundary, and that translation by \(1\) fixes the class while multiplication by \(2\) negates it modulo coboundaries. The rank-one integral module therefore has character \(\chi\).

For \(g\in G\), the reduced cellular Lefschetz trace is \(-16\) for the identity, \(-1\) for each nonzero translation, and \(0\) for every affine element with nonidentity multiplier: these cases fix respectively all cells, no nonempty word, and just one vertex. As reduced rational homology exists only in degrees two and three, its alternating trace is \(\chi(g)-\operatorname{tr}(g|\widetilde H_3)\). The resulting third-homology character equals that of \(\mathbb Q_\chi\oplus\mathbb Q[C_4]\oplus3V\). Semisimplicity of rational finite-group representations proves the claimed module isomorphism.

## Relationship to prior work
Govc, *Fundamental Groups of Permutation Complexes* (first public 2026-11-08), proves that every permutation complex has free fundamental group; it does not classify this affine subgroup's higher homotopy or rational symmetry character. Chachólski–Levi–Meshulam, *On the topology of complexes of injective words* (arXiv 2019), prove the full-injective-word sphere-wedge theorem and realize sphere wedges using two arbitrary permutations; neither result determines the natural twenty-permutation family here. The present mixed-dimensional decomposition is an exact calculation for an intrinsically defined transitive subgroup, rather than an existential realization of the abstract wedge. Related previously computed cyclic/dihedral and alternating-group word complexes employ different permutation families, and their results do not imply this affine subgroup calculation.

## Limitations
The classification concerns exactly the degree-five action of \(\operatorname{AGL}(1,5)\). No formula for \(\operatorname{AGL}(1,p)\) at other primes is asserted. The third-homology module decomposition is rational; no complete integral decomposition of that sixteen-plus-one-dimensional representation is claimed. The calculation is not a classification of every sharply two-transitive permutation group. Literature indexing is incomplete, so an unindexed equivalent calculation could exist.
