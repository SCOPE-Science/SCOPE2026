# Scientific review: affine five-letter permutation complex

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns a completely specified finite regular semisimplicial cell complex. The exact face counts are \(5,20,60,80,20\); consecutive cellular boundaries compose to zero. All nonzero Smith factors of the four integral boundary maps are \(1\), of multiplicities \(4,16,43,20\). These imply free reduced integral homology of ranks one in degree two and seventeen in degree three. Twenty independent free face–four-cell collapses give a three-dimensional simply connected complex. Its simply connected two-skeleton is a wedge of two-spheres, and unit Smith factors of the third attaching matrix give the claimed homotopy wedge. The explicit integral cocycle and full affine fixed-word Lefschetz calculation prove the stated action. The embedded checker directly reconstructs and checks every finite cell and matrix.

## Originality — PASS, subject to the stated indexing risk
The closest primary material inspected is Govc (arXiv:2611.11362v1), whose theorem concerns the fundamental group only, and Chachólski–Levi–Meshulam (arXiv:1908.03394v1), whose general stable realization and two-generator results do not compute the natural affine subgroup. Searches for the exact group, ordered subwords, the rank pair, affine character, and subgroup equivalences found no dominating result. The abstract wedge type can be realized in other ways, but that does not determine the topology or group action of this specific permutation family. Unindexed calculations of this subgroup remain a bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS
The smallest nontrivial proper sharply two-transitive affine permutation family has homology simultaneously in degrees two and three, unlike the full injective-word complex, which has homology concentrated in top degree. Its rank-one integral affine character and seventeen-dimensional rational representation supply a structured benchmark for the group-generated permutation complexes considered in current work. These exact facts are natural to ask of this canonical subgroup and are not inferred from general fundamental-group freeness or the fact that arbitrary wedges can be realized.

## Scope and independent status
The theorem is for five letters; its third-homology equivariant decomposition is rational. No behavior for higher primes, general permutation groups, or full integral higher-homology modules is asserted.
