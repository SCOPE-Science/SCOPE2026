# Mixed-primary Moore summands in the alternating-row quotient of the first shellable four-row chessboard

## Finding
Let \(\Delta_{4,7}\) denote the ordinary chessboard complex of all nonattacking rook placements on four labeled rows and seven labeled columns. Let the alternating group \(A_4\) act by even permutations of the four **rows**, leaving columns fixed. For the ordinary geometric quotient
\[
Y=|\Delta_{4,7}|/A_4,
\]
the following is an integral **homotopy** classification:
\[
\boxed{Y\simeq M(\mathbb Z/6,2)\vee\bigvee^{5}M(\mathbb Z/3,2)\vee\bigvee^{15}S^3.}
\]
Here \(M(\mathbb Z/d,2)\) is the 3-dimensional Moore space obtained by attaching a 3-cell to \(S^2\) by a map of degree \(d\). In particular,
\[
\pi_1(Y)=1,\quad \pi_2(Y)\cong H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\oplus(\mathbb Z/3)^6,\quad H_3(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{15},
\]
with \(H_0(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) and all other reduced integral homology groups zero. By contrast, the unquotiented \(\Delta_{4,7}\) is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{225}S^3\); thus the quotient creates second-degree torsion at both primes dividing \(|A_4|=12\).

## Assumptions and scope
A vertex of \(\Delta_{4,7}\) is a pair \((r,c)\) with \(r\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) and \(c\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}\). A nonempty face is a set of such pairs with no repeated row or column. The action is \(g(r,c)=(g(r),c)\) for \(g\in A_4\). The quotient is of the geometric realization, not the abstract quotient of a vertex set, not a fixed-point complex, and not a homotopy-orbit space. Every simplex has distinct column labels, so no group element can permute the vertices of a simplex setwise without fixing them individually; accordingly its face-orbit CW structure has the ordinary signed simplicial cellular boundaries.

The width seven is the first width in the full-shellability range \(n\ge 2m-1\) for four-row chessboard complexes \(\Delta_{m,n}\). The theorem concerns this particular first stable-width quotient only; no claim about other widths or higher-row actions is made.

## Proof
**1. Exact orbit cells.** Order the occupied columns of each \(k\)-rook face increasingly, and view its rows as an injection from \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\) to \(\{0,1,2,3\}\). There is one \(A_4\)-orbit of such injections when \(k=1,2\). There are exactly two orbits when \(k=3,4\), distinguished by the sign of the four-permutation formed by the occupied rows in column order, followed for \(k=3\) by the sole unused row. The quotient has precisely \((7,21,70,70)\) cells in dimensions \(0,1,2,3\), respectively. The corresponding unquotiented face numbers are \((28,252,840,840)\).

**2. Integral cellular boundaries.** Let \(e_{ij}\) denote the edge orbit on columns \(i<j\); let \(f^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}\) denote either orientation-sign orbit for \(i<j<k\), and \(t^{\varepsilon}_{ijkl}\) either orientation-sign orbit for \(i<j<k<l\), with \(\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\). All cells are oriented by increasing column order. The differentials are
\[
\partial_1 e_{ij}=v_j-v_i,\qquad
\partial_2 f^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}=e_{jk}-e_{ik}+e_{ij},
\]
and, using \(S=(s_0,s_1,s_2,s_3)\) and \(S\setminus s_a\) in increasing order,
\[
\partial_3 t^{\varepsilon}_{S}=
\sum_{a=0}^3(-1)^a f^{\varepsilon(-1)^{3-a}}_{S\setminus s_a}.
\]
The last sign follows because deleting the row in position \(a\) and moving that row to the unused-row position entails \(3-a\) transpositions. Thus these displayed formulas determine the entire finite cellular complex over \(\mathbb Z\), with no appeal to floating-point computations.

**3. Exact integer reduction.** In the bases above, the three boundary matrices have sizes \(7\times21\), \(21\times70\), and \(70\times70\). Their nonzero Smith invariant factors, computed by exact integer algorithms from the displayed formulas, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_1 &: 1^{\times6},\\
\partial_2 &: 1^{\times15},\\
\partial_3 &: 1^{\times49},\ 3^{\times5},\ 6^{\times1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both consecutive boundary compositions are identically zero. Since the image of \(\partial_2\) is primitive (all nonzero Smith factors equal one), the torsion in \(\operatorname{coker}\partial_3\) is exactly the torsion in \(H_2\). The ranks give \(\operatorname{rank}H_1=21-6-15=0\), \(\operatorname{rank}H_2=70-15-55=0\), and \(\operatorname{rank}H_3=70-55=15\). The nonunit factors of \(\partial_3\) supply \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z/6\oplus(\mathbb Z/3)^5\), or equivalently \(\mathbb Z/2\oplus(\mathbb Z/3)^6\).

**4. Fundamental group.** The established shellability theorem for \(\Delta_{m,n}\) when \(n\ge2m-1\) makes \(\Delta_{4,7}\) a wedge of three-spheres, hence simply connected. The finite row action is simplicial, so Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem applies. Each three-cycle in \(A_4\) fixes every vertex whose row is its unique fixed row; the three-cycles generate \(A_4\). Therefore the normal subgroup generated by point stabilizers is all of \(A_4\), and \(\pi_1(Y)=1\).

**5. Homotopy type, not just homology.** Attaching 3-cells cannot change \(\pi_1\), so the connected 2-skeleton of \(Y\) is simply connected. Any simply connected 2-dimensional CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres: choose an integral \(H_2\)-basis of spherical representatives and apply the homological Whitehead theorem. The attaching maps of 3-cells consequently lie in \(\pi_2\) of this wedge, a free abelian group, and are determined by their integer degree columns. Unimodular changes of basis in the source and target wedges, followed by the Smith reduction above, change neither homotopy cofiber nor homotopy type. Unit entries give contractible cell pairs, factors \(3\) and \(6\) give the specified Moore spaces, and the fifteen zero columns give fifteen three-spheres. This proves the displayed homotopy equivalence. Finally the original face vector has Euler characteristic \(28-252+840-840=-224\), so its shellable three-sphere wedge has \(225\) spheres.

## Verification
Two independent, fully executable exact-integer procedures are included. `verification/verify_orbits.py` generates all twelve even row permutations, all \(28+252+840+840\) nonempty labeled faces, canonicalizes their actual row-group orbits, constructs each signed quotient boundary, checks both zero compositions, and computes the integral Smith forms. `verification/verify_parity.py` instead constructs the quotient only from the explicit parity-orbit formulas and obtains the same Smith factors. Run them using Python 3 with SymPy installed, e.g. `python verification/verify_orbits.py` and `python verification/verify_parity.py`. The recorded stdout is `verification/results.txt`.

These exact finite computations determine the complete three-dimensional cellular homology of the fixed geometric quotient, not a sample of a conjectural all-width pattern. The homotopy conclusion additionally uses the established shellability/Armstrong/Whitehead theorems and the explicit dimension bound.

## Relationship to prior work
Shareshian–Wachs (first arXiv posting 2004-09-03) studies integral chessboard homology and describes the earlier connectivity bound, including the representation-theoretic treatment of the **unquotiented** complex. Hersh (first arXiv posting 2008-09-14) supplies another proof of shellability of chessboard complexes in the stable range. Neither inspected full text computes the integral cellular boundary or homotopy type of this **nonfree alternating-row geometric quotient**. Armstrong's classical orbit-space theorem is used for the fundamental group, not claimed as new.

For comparison, the published finite quotient classification *All column-symmetry quotients of the \(3\times4\) chessboard torus* concerns a different board and its 2-dimensional surface quotients. The published *Integral homotopy classification of four-row \((1,1,1,2)\) multiple chessboard complexes* concerns an unquotiented complex with different row capacities. Neither of these determines the displayed \(4\times7\) ordinary-board \(A_4\) quotient. The novelty claimed is the exact mixed-primary integral **homotopy decomposition** of this specific first shellable-width quotient, not shellability, the original homology, the generic orbit-space theorem, or the classification of Moore spaces.

## Limitations
Only \(\Delta_{4,7}/A_4\) under row permutations is classified. The theorem does not classify all \(\Delta_{4,n}/A_4\), the full row-symmetric quotient, other group actions or any homotopy-orbit construction. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed. Although searches and relevant full-text comparisons found no covering statement, equivalent results in unindexed earlier literature cannot be excluded. The exact Smith data depend on the included executable finite enumeration and exact integer algorithms, which are not a proof of any unbounded family.
