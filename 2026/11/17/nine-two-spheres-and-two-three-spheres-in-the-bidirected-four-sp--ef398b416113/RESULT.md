# Nine two-spheres and two three-spheres in the bidirected four-spoke fan

## Finding

Let \(F=K_1\vee P_4\) be the five-vertex fan whose path is \(0-1-2-3\), with hub \(4\) adjacent to all four path vertices. Let \(D=\overleftrightarrow{F}\) denote the fully bidirected digraph (both arcs of every undirected edge). Its multipath complex \(X(D)\) has one vertex for each directed arc and a simplex for each set of arcs that is a disjoint union of vertex-simple directed paths (isolated vertices permitted). There is an **integral homotopy equivalence**

\[
|X(D)|\simeq\bigvee^{9}S^2\vee\bigvee^{2}S^3.
\]

Let \(\rho\) reverse the four path vertices, \(\rho(i)=3-i\) for \(0\le i\le3\), fixing the hub. Under its natural action on rational reduced homology, the \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspace dimensions are \((5,4)\) in degree two and \((1,1)\) in degree three. Equivalently,

\[
\widetilde H_2(X(D);\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q_+^{5}\oplus\mathbb Q_-^{4},\qquad
\widetilde H_3(X(D);\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q_+\oplus\mathbb Q_-.
\]

Here \(\mathbb Q_+\) and \(\mathbb Q_-\) denote the one-dimensional trivial and sign representations of the order-two reflection group. In particular, the only nonzero reduced integral homology groups are \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^9\) and \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z^2\), with no torsion.

## Assumptions and scope

This is the single specified five-vertex undirected fan with **both** orientations of each of its seven edges. The 14 directed arcs (simplicial vertices) are ordered lexicographically by sorted undirected edges, with each undirected edge contributing the forward and reverse arc. A finite arc subset is a multipath if and only if each graph vertex has selected indegree and outdegree at most one and the selected successor relation has no directed cycle. These conditions exclude oppositely oriented pairs (two-cycles), as required by the ordinary multipath definition. All homotopy conclusions are for the geometric realization with integral coefficients; only the reflection representation is asserted over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Proof

**1. Exact complex and boundary signs.** Enumerate the 14 arc subsets by bitmasks, checking at most one successor and predecessor at each vertex and ruling out repeated vertices along successors. This exhaustive definition gives exactly 160 nonempty simplices with face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)=(14,56,70,20),
\]
with no higher simplices. Every proper subface passes the same condition. The signed simplicial boundary is \([\sigma:\sigma\setminus\{j\}]=(-1)^{\#\{i\in\sigma:i<j\}}\), using zero-indexed arc order.

**2. A complete acyclic matching.** On the nonempty face poset, repeatedly pair each currently unpaired face not containing an arc \(j\) with its coface obtained by adding \(j\), where the successive arc indices are
\[
(13,6,10,0,8,4,3,11,1,9,2,5,7,12).
\]
At each stage the pairs are taken in increasing face-mask order, and both elements are removed. The 74 resulting pairs cover 148 faces. The unmatched cells consist of exactly one vertex, nine two-simplices and two three-simplices. Form the Hasse graph on all 160 nonempty faces, orient every unmatched covering relation downward and reverse the 74 matched covering relations. An exhaustive topological sorting visits all 160 nodes and all 402 covering incidences. Hence this is an **acyclic discrete Morse matching**, giving a homotopy-equivalent CW complex with precisely the stated critical cells.

**3. Exact integral attaching degrees.** To evaluate the cellular Morse differential, eliminate each matched lower face \(a\) and its upper mate \(b\) by the integral substitution \(a\mapsto-[b:a]^{-1}\sum_{c\subset b,\ c\ne a}[b:c]c\), recursively reducing noncritical faces. The incidence coefficient \([b:a]\) is \(+1\) or \(-1\), so no division by a nonunit occurs. The acyclic matching guarantees termination. Executing this formula on the two critical three-cells produces the **zero integral matrix** of size \(9\times2\). There are no critical one-cells, so the two-skeleton of the Morse CW complex is a wedge of nine two-spheres. Its second homotopy group is freely generated by those spheres by Hurewicz. An attaching map of a three-cell therefore vanishes in \(\pi_2\) exactly when its integral cellular boundary vector vanishes. Both attaching maps are nullhomotopic. It follows that the two three-cells contribute a wedge of two three-spheres and proves the stated homotopy type.

**4. Reflection action.** The reflection permutes arcs and therefore acts by signed permutations of oriented simplices. The exact simplicial boundary from 20 tetrahedra to 70 triangles has rational rank 18, so its two-dimensional kernel equals \(H_3(X(D);\mathbb Q)\) (there are no four-simplices). Exact rational Gauss–Jordan elimination gives a basis with two free tetrahedral coordinates. Applying reflection to this basis gives trace \(0\) on \(H_3\). Alternating signed fixed-face traces on the full simplicial chain groups, with the degree-zero reduction, then give trace \(1\) on \(\widetilde H_2\). Identity traces are the Betti ranks \(9\) and \(2\). Since the eigenvalues of an involution are \(\pm1\), the multiplicities are \((5,4)\) and \((1,1)\).

## Relationship to prior work

Caputi–Collari–Smith (first public preprint January 9, 2026) classify multipath complexes of **bidirected paths**, and compute connectivity and integral homology for **bidirected cycles**; their Theorems 2.8 and 2.10 do not cover the fan obtained by adjoining a hub to a path. Their T-operations require special in/out-degree restrictions not satisfied by the fully bidirected fan. Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith (2022 preprint, 2024 publication) treat various oriented graphs, but their linear-graph, polygon and tournament classifications do not imply this fan's exact homotopy or symmetry action. Caputi–Collari–Ramos (2024) obtain categorical finiteness and a matching-complex comparison for **digraphs without oriented cycles**; here every undirected edge yields a directed two-cycle, so that comparison theorem does not apply. Deshpande–Sawant (February 16, 2026) explicitly identify directed-linear-forest complexes with multipath complexes. Their vertex-decomposability criteria cover particular acyclic digraphs and underlying forests or cycles, while their general conflict-hypergraph representation is a reformulation, not an exact homotopy calculation for a fan with three triangles. The reported homotopy type of fully bidirected double stars is for trees, not a fan with triangles. The ordinary **matching complex** of the underlying fan is a different simplicial complex and cannot imply the arc-set multipath result. These comparisons distinguish statements and scope rather than infer novelty merely from different titles.

## Limitations

The homotopy theorem concerns exactly the five-vertex fully bidirected fan \(K_1\vee P_4\); it does not classify larger fans, other orientations, or arbitrary cone graphs. The reflection representation is rational: no canonical integral eigenspace decomposition or equivariant homotopy splitting is claimed. Verification is finite but exhaustive for this object and rests on the standard discrete Morse correspondence and the elementary homotopy description of three-cell attachments. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed. An unindexed equivalent computation remains a residual originality risk.
