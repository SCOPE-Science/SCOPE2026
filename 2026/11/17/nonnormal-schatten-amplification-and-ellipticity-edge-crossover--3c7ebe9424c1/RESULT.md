# Nonnormal Schatten amplification and ellipticity-edge crossover for planar inverse Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroups

## Finding

For any \(a,b>0\), choose a real shear \(\kappa\) satisfying \(|\kappa|<2\sqrt{ab}\). Put
\[
B_\kappa=\begin{pmatrix}-a&\kappa\\0&-b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q_\kappa=-(B_\kappa+B_\kappa^{\mathsf T})
=\begin{pmatrix}2a&-\kappa\\-\kappa&2b\end{pmatrix}>0.
\]
The inverse Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup \(H_{\kappa,t}\), \(t>0\), acts on \(L^2(\mathbb R^2,e^{|x|^2/2}\,dx)\) and is generated by
\[
\mathcal A_\kappa f=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(Q_\kappa D^2f)
-\langle B_\kappa x,\nabla f\rangle.
\]
For \(t>0\), set \(u=e^{-at}\), \(v=e^{-bt}\) and
\[
r(t)=\begin{cases}(u-v)/(b-a),&a\ne b,\\te^{-at},&a=b.\end{cases}
\quad F=u^2+v^2+\kappa^2r(t)^2,\quad
s_{\pm}=\sqrt{\frac{F\pm\sqrt{F^2-4u^2v^2}}2}.
\]
These are the two singular values of \(e^{tB_\kappa}\), with \(0<s_-\le s_+<1\). For **every** \(p>0\), including quasi-Schatten exponents \(0<p<1\),
\[
\boxed{\|H_{\kappa,t}\|_{\mathcal S_p}^{p}=
\frac{e^{-p(a+b)t}}{(1-s_+^p)(1-s_-^p)}.}
\]
At fixed \(a,b,t,p\), this quantity is strictly increasing with \(|\kappa|\). Its unique minimum occurs at \(\kappa=0\), and its sharp (unattained within the positive-definite diffusion family) supremum is the finite limit as \(|\kappa|\uparrow2\sqrt{ab}\). In particular,
\[
\boxed{\|H_{\kappa,t}\|_{\mathcal S_2}^{2}=
\frac{e^{-2(a+b)t}}{(1-e^{-2at})(1-e^{-2bt})-\kappa^2r(t)^2}.}
\]
Despite this strict Schatten amplification, the **entire eigenvalue multiset**, the trace, and the operator norm remain shear-independent:
\[
\operatorname{Eig}(H_{\kappa,t})=\{e^{-(a+b)t}u^jv^k:j,k\in\mathbb N_0\},\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}H_{\kappa,t}=\frac{e^{-(a+b)t}}{(1-u)(1-v)},\qquad
\|H_{\kappa,t}\|=e^{-(a+b)t}.
\]
All eigenvalues are counted algebraically; the formula allows \(a=b\) and nondiagonalizable one-particle drifts.

There is a sharp short-time crossover at the edge of ellipticity. Write \(\delta(t)=4ab-\kappa(t)^2>0\), and assume \(t\downarrow0\) with \(|\kappa(t)|<2\sqrt{ab}\) and \(\delta(t)/t^2\to\xi\in[0,\infty)\). Then
\[
\boxed{\lim_{t\downarrow0}t^4\|H_{\kappa(t),t}\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2=
\frac{1}{\xi+ab(a+b)^2/3}.}
\]
By contrast, for every **fixed** interior \(\kappa\),
\[
\lim_{t\downarrow0}t^2\|H_{\kappa,t}\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2=(4ab-\kappa^2)^{-1}.
\]
Thus the short-time exponent changes from two to four along a natural double-scaling window, while the inverse semigroup's eigenvalues and trace remain unchanged.

## Assumptions and scope

All Hilbert spaces and Schatten ideals are complexified. The inverse Gaussian measure is literally \(e^{|x|^2/2}dx\), without a normalizing factor. The stationary Gaussian probability measure of the ordinary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck system is \(d\gamma_+(x)=(2\pi)^{-1}e^{-|x|^2/2}dx\). The covariance identity \(B_\kappa+B_\kappa^{\mathsf T}+Q_\kappa=0\) makes the stationary covariance exactly \(I_2\). The restriction \(|\kappa|<2\sqrt{ab}\) is equivalent to \(Q_\kappa>0\). The parameter \(p\) may be any positive real number, and the time is strictly positive in each Schatten formula. For fixed \(t>0\), the supremum is taken only over strictly positive-definite \(Q_\kappa\), not over the singular endpoint itself.

## Proof

**One-particle reduction.** By the inverse/ordinary Gaussian semigroup unitary equivalence [1, Lemma 4.2], there exists a unitary \(U:L^2(\gamma_+)\to L^2(e^{|x|^2/2}dx)\), explicitly multiplication by \((2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-|x|^2/2}\), such that
\[
H_{\kappa,t}=e^{-(a+b)t}U(P_t)^*U^{-1},
\]
where \(P_t\) is the ordinary stationary Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup. Its Wiener-chaos decomposition identifies \(P_t\) with the bosonic symmetric second quantization of \(e^{tB_\kappa^{\mathsf T}}\) [2,3]. Therefore the nonzero singular values of \(H_{\kappa,t}\) are precisely
\[
e^{-(a+b)t}s_+^j s_-^k,\qquad (j,k)\in\mathbb N_0^2,
\]
including the vacuum \((j,k)=(0,0)\). Indeed the singular-value decomposition of the one-particle operator lifts on each symmetric tensor power; the tensor products form an orthonormal diagonal singular basis even when the original drift is not normal. Since
\[
I_2-e^{tB_\kappa}e^{tB_\kappa^{\mathsf T}}
=\int_0^t e^{sB_\kappa}Q_\kappa e^{sB_\kappa^{\mathsf T}}\,ds>0,
\]
all one-particle singular values are strictly below one. Summing the product-geometric series yields the claimed \(p\)-Schatten identity for all \(p>0\). (The general symmetric-second-quantization Schatten product formula for \(p\ge1\) is known [3, Corollary 3.8]; extending its finite product computation to \(0<p<1\) here is immediate.)

**Exact planar spectrum and Hilbert–Schmidt norm.** Direct exponentiation gives
\[
e^{tB_\kappa}=\begin{pmatrix}u&\kappa r(t)\\0&v\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its squared singular values are the two eigenvalues of \(CC^{\mathsf T}\), where \(C=e^{tB_\kappa}\). The trace and determinant of that matrix are \(F\) and \(u^2v^2\), respectively, giving the displayed \(s_\pm\). The identity
\[
(1-s_+^2)(1-s_-^2)=\det(I_2-CC^{\mathsf T})
=(1-u^2)(1-v^2)-\kappa^2r(t)^2
\]
proves the exact Hilbert–Schmidt formula. In each finite chaos, \(C\) is triangular with diagonal entries \(u,v\); its \(n\)-th symmetric power has algebraic eigenvalues \(u^jv^{n-j}\), \(0\le j\le n\). Since \(\|C\|<1\), direct-sum traces converge absolutely. Thus the eigenvalue multiset and trace follow by two geometric series; the vacuum block shows \(\|H_{\kappa,t}\|=e^{-(a+b)t}\).

**Strict extremal law.** Put \(q=uv\). For every shear, \(s_+s_-=q\) and \(s_+^2+s_-^2=u^2+v^2+\kappa^2r(t)^2\), which is strictly increasing in \(|\kappa|\) because \(r(t)>0\). Consequently the ratio \(s_+/s_-\) strictly increases. Write \(s_\pm=\sqrt q\,e^{\pm\eta}\), \(\eta\ge0\). For \(p>0\),
\[
(1-s_+^p)(1-s_-^p)=1+q^p-2q^{p/2}\cosh(p\eta).
\]
This is positive (both \(s_\pm<1\)) and strictly decreases as \(|\kappa|\) increases. Hence every \(\mathcal S_p\) quasi-norm is strictly amplified. The minimum at zero shear is
\[
\|H_{0,t}\|_{\mathcal S_p}^p=\frac{e^{-p(a+b)t}}{(1-u^p)(1-v^p)}.
\]
The boundary supremum is obtained by inserting \(\kappa^2=4ab\) in the explicit formula. It is finite because \(I_2-CC^{\mathsf T}\) remains strictly positive at this boundary: at \(\kappa_*=2\sqrt{ab}\), \(Q_{\kappa_*}=qq^{\mathsf T}\) with \(q=(\sqrt{2a},-\sqrt{2b})^{\mathsf T}\), and
\[
\det[q,B_{\kappa_*}q]=-2\sqrt{ab}(a+b)\ne0.
\]
Thus \(\{q,B_{\kappa_*}q\}\) spans \(\mathbb R^2\); the Gramian \(\int_0^t e^{sB_{\kappa_*}}qq^{\mathsf T}e^{sB_{\kappa_*}^{\mathsf T}}ds\) is positive definite for every \(t>0\).

**Sharp two-scale boundary.** Denote \(D(t,\kappa)=\det(I_2-CC^{\mathsf T})\), \(D_*(t)=D(t,2\sqrt{ab})\), and \(\delta=4ab-\kappa^2\). The exact identity
\[
D(t,\kappa)=D_*(t)+\delta\,r(t)^2
\]
separates the rank-one boundary and interior ellipticity. At the boundary, the Gramian can be written \(\int_0^t f(s)f(s)^{\mathsf T}ds\) with \(f(s)=e^{sB_{\kappa_*}}q\). The two-dimensional Gram determinant identity gives
\[
D_*(t)=\int_0^t\int_0^y \det[f(x),f(y)]^2\,dx\,dy.
\]
Taylor expansion, uniformly over the compact triangle \(0\le x\le y\le t\), yields
\[
\det[f(x),f(y)]=(y-x)\det[q,B_{\kappa_*}q]+O(t(y-x)).
\]
The time integral of \((y-x)^2\) equals \(t^4/12\). Therefore
\[
D_*(t)=\frac{\det[q,B_{\kappa_*}q]^2}{12}t^4+O(t^5)
=\frac{ab(a+b)^2}{3}t^4+O(t^5).
\]
Since \(r(t)=t+O(t^2)\), the hypothesis \(\delta(t)/t^2\to\xi\) entails
\[
\frac{D(t,\kappa(t))}{t^4}\to\frac{ab(a+b)^2}{3}+\xi.
\]
The Hilbert–Schmidt formula and \(e^{-2(a+b)t}\to1\) finish the crossover. For fixed interior shear, \(I_2-CC^{\mathsf T}=tQ_\kappa+O(t^2)\), whence \(D(t,\kappa)/t^2\to\det Q_\kappa=4ab-\kappa^2\), proving the contrasting exponent.

## Relationship to prior work

Bruno–Casarino–Ciatti–Sjögren [1] establish the inverse/ordinary semigroup intertwining and inverse-OU spectral description, but not the fixed-spectrum monotone Schatten optimization or its joint short-time ellipticity-edge limit. Van Neerven [2] develops the one-particle second-quantization spectral representation. Araki–Sinha–Sunder [3] already give the general product formula for Schatten norms of symmetric second quantizations; that general formula is not claimed as new. Li [4] gives analogous determinant and compactness formulas in the distinct setting of embedded Gaussian quantum Markov semigroups. Trélat–Zuazua [5] establish much broader rank-one controllability-Gramian small-time expansions, implying the rank-one \(t^4\) determinant mechanism; **the rank-one Gramian asymptotic alone is not claimed as new**. The specific joint rank-two-to-rank-one ellipticity crossover for the inverse Gaussian semigroup and strict shear optimization of all positive Schatten orders are the asserted contributions.

## Limitations

The result concerns exactly a two-dimensional upper-triangular drift with stationary covariance \(I_2\), correlated diffusion \(Q_\kappa=-(B_\kappa+B_\kappa^{\mathsf T})\), and a fixed pair of strictly positive diagonal damping rates. It does not prove analogous optimization for all drifts or all covariance choices. The rank-one endpoint is used only to calculate an unattained supremum and a limit; there the originally assumed diffusion is not positive definite. The supplied finite computations do not certify the infinite-dimensional claims. Older equivalent results under alternate terminology may exist, and no independent publication-priority determination has been made.
