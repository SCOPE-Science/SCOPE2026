# One-accumulation interval forests admit cyclic omega-limit realization

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge1\), and every prescribed cyclic tuple of compact sets as specified below, there is a continuous triangular map \(F:[0,1]^2\to[0,1]^2\) and a point whose omega-limit set has exactly the prescribed sections over an \(m\)-cycle of the base map. More precisely, the sections admit continuous surjections forming a chain-transitive cyclic map for which every exact forward orbit eventually reaches a single periodic orbit. The sections may have different finite or countably infinite numbers of isolated singleton components and need not be homeomorphic.

## Assumptions and scope
For \(i\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), let \(M_i\subset[0,1]\) be compact. Its connected components are exactly: one distinguished singleton \(\{p_i\}\); a countably infinite family \(I_{i,k}\), \(k\ge1\), of pairwise disjoint nondegenerate closed intervals; and an optional finite or countably infinite family \(E_i\) of isolated singleton components. Require that the interval components converge to \(\{p_i\}\) in the Hausdorff metric, and that each point of \(E_i\) is isolated in \(M_i\), with all accumulation points of \(E_i\) contained in \(\{p_i\}\). These conditions imply every interval component is relatively clopen in \(M_i\), all the listed components are disjoint, and the only accumulation point of the family of distinct components is \(p_i\). Empty \(E_i\) is allowed. No relation between the shapes or singleton counts of different sections is assumed.

A cyclic map acts on \(X=\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z}(\{i\}\times M_i)\), sending phase \(i\) to phase \(i+1\). An \(\varepsilon\)-chain is a finite sequence whose consecutive image discrepancies are smaller than \(\varepsilon\), using any metric separating the finitely many phases.

## Proof
**1. Ordered enumeration.** List \(E_i\) as \(e_{i,1},e_{i,2},\ldots\) when infinite (and use its finite list otherwise). Merge the ordered interval list \(I_{i,1},I_{i,2},\ldots\) with the singleton list, without changing the interval order, into one enumeration \(C_{i,1},C_{i,2},\ldots\) of all non-distinguished components. For example, alternate interval and singleton components while both lists remain, then enumerate the remainder. Since all but finitely many components lie in any prescribed neighborhood of \(p_i\), \(C_{i,t}\) tends to \(\{p_i\}\). If \(C_{i,t}=I_{i,k}\), then \(t\ge k\).

**2. Surjective maps.** Define \(\phi_i(p_i)=p_{i+1}\). Map each isolated point of \(E_i\) to \(p_{i+1}\), and map the whole first interval \(I_{i,1}\) to \(p_{i+1}\). For every \(k\ge2\), map \(I_{i,k}\) onto \(C_{i+1,k-1}\): use an affine surjection between intervals when this target is an interval, and use a constant map when it is a singleton. This is well-defined since all pieces are disjoint. Every target component \(C_{i+1,t}\) is the image of source interval \(I_{i,t+1}\), while \(p_{i+1}\) is the image of \(p_i\), hence \(\phi_i\) is onto. Restriction to any non-distinguished component is continuous. For continuity at \(p_i\), sequences approaching \(p_i\) through components with unbounded indices have images in components \(C_{i+1,t}\) with \(t\to\infty\) or at \(p_{i+1}\). The images therefore converge to \(p_{i+1}\). No other nontrivial continuity issue occurs.

**3. Eventual periodicity.** An image of a point in interval \(I_{i,k}\), \(k\ge2\), either belongs to a target singleton, or belongs to interval \(I_{i+1,j}=C_{i+1,k-1}\) with \(j\le k-1\). An isolated singleton maps directly to \(p_{i+1}\). Thus whenever a forward orbit remains in interval components its interval index strictly decreases; otherwise it reaches a singleton and then the distinguished phase cycle. Hence each point eventually enters the orbit \(\{(i,p_i):i\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\}\).

**4. Arbitrarily deep preimages near the distinguished points.** Fix \(y\in M_j\) outside \(\{p_j\}\). Its component is \(C_{j,t}\), and it has a preimage in interval \(I_{j-1,t+1}\). If this preimage lies in interval \(I_{j-1,k}\) (necessarily \(k=t+1\)), its component appears as \(C_{j-1,u}\) at some index \(u\ge k\), so its next chosen preimage lies in \(I_{j-2,u+1}\), with interval index at least \(k+1\). Inductively, backward preimages of any prescribed depth exist, with source interval indices tending to infinity. Their distances to the distinguished points of their respective phases converge to zero. When \(y=p_j\), simply take the distinguished preimage at every depth.

**5. Chain transitivity.** Fix phases and points \(x,y\in X\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). Follow the exact orbit of \(x\) until it hits a distinguished point, then, if needed, follow further exact steps to a desired distinguished phase \(i\). Choose a sufficiently long backward preimage \(z\) of \(y\), with length in the appropriate residue class modulo \(m\), so that \(z\) lies in phase \(i+1\) and \(d(z,p_{i+1})<\varepsilon\). Make one allowed \(\varepsilon\)-jump from \(p_i\) to \(z\), and thereafter follow exact forward iterates to \(y\). This gives an \(\varepsilon\)-chain from every \(x\) to every \(y\). For an \(\varepsilon\)-chain from a distinguished point to itself, choose a full cycle of positive length, if necessary. The cyclic map is chain transitive.

**6. Triangular realization.** Theorem A of García Guirao (arXiv:2611.06889v1) states that a cyclic tuple of compact sections is realizable over a periodic base orbit of a continuous triangular map if and only if its disjoint union admits a continuous chain-transitive cyclic map. Apply that theorem to the constructed maps. This produces the stated triangular realization. The proof does not require the actual omega-generating orbit to lie in the sections.

## Verification
The construction is explicit on every component. Critical checks are: every target component has source interval \(I_{i,t+1}\); the only limit point requiring continuity checking is \(p_i\); interval indices strictly decrease under any interval-to-interval forward transition; and backward predecessor indices strictly increase, producing every sufficiently deep phase-constrained preimage near the target phase's distinguished point. These observations prove the infinite claims directly rather than by finite sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
García Guirao, arXiv:2611.06889v1 (2026), proves the general cyclic chain-transitivity realization criterion (Theorem A), and establishes sufficient classes with uncountably many components or countably many with only finitely many nondegenerate components. Its explicit remaining class consists of sections with countably infinitely many nondegenerate components; it refers to a companion preprint for further partial results. The present result is an explicit sufficient construction in that remaining class, allowing isolated degenerate components that need not themselves be limits of nondegenerate interval components. The fundamental realization criterion is used as prior literature, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The result applies only to sections whose distinct components have a unique common accumulation point and whose nondegenerate components are isolated intervals. It does not resolve the general remaining countable-component class, nor does it provide a closed formula for the ambient triangular map; existence follows from the cited realization theorem. A companion preprint mentioned by the source could contain overlapping special constructions but was not accessible for full-text comparison. Independent mathematical validation and definitive historical-priority review have not been performed.
