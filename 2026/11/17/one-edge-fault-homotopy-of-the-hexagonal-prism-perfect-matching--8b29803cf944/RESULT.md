# One-edge fault homotopy of the hexagonal prism perfect-matching complex

## Finding
For the hexagonal-prism graph \(G=C_6\square K_2\), deletion of **any one graph edge** gives
\[
|\mathcal P(G-e)|\simeq S^2\vee S^2.
\]
Here \(\mathcal P(H)\) has the edges of \(H\) as simplicial vertices and has as simplicial faces exactly the partial matchings extendible to a perfect matching of \(H\). The intact hexagonal prism has \(\mathcal P(G)\simeq\bigvee^6 S^2\) by the existing circular-ladder theorem. Thus a single edge fault changes the rank of degree-two integral homology from six to two, independently of which of the two edge orbits contains the deleted edge. The horizontal-edge case has previously been established; the independently computed vertical-rung orbit completes the one-edge classification.

## Assumptions and scope
Use vertex labels \((r,i)\) with \(r\in\{0,1\}\), \(i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\). The 18 undirected graph edges comprise 12 horizontal edges \(\{(r,i),(r,i+1)\}\) and six vertical edges \(\{(0,i),(1,i)\}\). No edges are reweighted and graph edges are deleted *before* defining the extendibility complex. Under the geometric dihedral and layer-swap automorphisms, all horizontal edges lie in one orbit and all rungs in another; it suffices to check \(e_h=\{(0,0),(0,1)\}\) and \(e_v=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}\). Homotopy equivalence is ordinary nonequivariant topological homotopy equivalence.

## Proof
Enumerate perfect matchings by recursively selecting the least unused graph vertex and pairing it with each available neighbor. For each deleted-edge representative, take the downward-closed complex generated by the perfect matchings avoiding that edge. In the order of the 12 horizontal edges (first row zero, then row one), followed by the six rungs, perform the following deterministic face-poset matching: for successive edges \(f\), pair every as-yet-unmatched face \(\sigma\) not containing \(f\) with \(\sigma\cup\{f\}\), whenever both remain unmatched. Include the empty face as an augmented face. Orient every Hasse cover from the larger to the smaller face, except matched covers, which are reversed. Full topological sorting of the resulting oriented Hasse diagram establishes acyclicity, not merely algebraic rank.

For vertical-rung deletion the complex has 12 perfect-matching facets and 562 augmented faces; the algorithm creates 279 matched face pairs and leaves one critical one-cell and three critical two-cells. The integral Morse boundary from degree two to degree one, with the reproducible critical-cell ordering, is
\[
\partial_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
There are no higher critical cells. Since a unit entry identifies a cancellable one-/two-cell pair, the Morse CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two two-spheres.

For horizontal-rim deletion there are 14 perfect matchings and 598 augmented faces; the matching has 296 pairs, leaving one critical one-cell, four critical two-cells, and one critical three-cell. The signed Morse differentials are
\[
\partial_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&-1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\partial_3=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their composite vanishes. A primitive degree-one attaching map cancels the one-cell against a two-cell; the remaining simply connected two-skeleton is a wedge of three two-spheres. The residual three-cell has primitive degree vector (equivalently the image of \(\partial_3\) in the resulting integral degree-two chain group). Since \(\pi_2\) of that wedge is its integral second homology, a change of sphere basis makes this three-cell cancel one two-sphere. The remaining homotopy type is \(S^2\vee S^2\). Thus both graph-edge orbits have the stated homotopy type.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, *The Homotopy Types of the Independence and Perfect Matching Complexes of Möbius and Circular Ladder Graphs* (arXiv:2608.30601, 2026), prove the intact-prism classification in their Theorem 25; at six columns this gives six two-spheres. An existing horizontal-edge-deletion classification already implies the horizontal orbit above, so the distinct vertical-rung fault is the new case required for the complete all-edge statement. Published work on ordinary (not extendible) matching complexes computes a different simplicial complex and does not imply the missing-rung result.

## Limitations
The result is specifically the six-column circular ladder and one removed edge. It neither classifies general multi-edge faults nor asserts an infinite-family invariance theorem. Homology ranks alone cannot substitute for the acyclic-Morse and primitive-attachment checks carried out here. An unindexed stronger pre-existing result remains a bibliographic possibility. No independent external validation is claimed.
