# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The complete proof reduces the signed-root deletion to a single cap cut of a Cayley body using the fixed slice parameter and a product-simplex corner inequality. It identifies the cap base as a codimension-one face of the truncated body, computes the Euclidean projection exactly, and establishes the two regimes using a strictly convex projection inequality against every signed-root orbit. For volume, the proof parameterizes the cap face by its two simplex-difference slices, states the relevant mixed-volume identity, gives both Jacobian factors, and evaluates the two beta integrals. These steps determine both the exact metric and normalized-volume formulas for all integers \(p,q\ge2\). A standalone exact rational checker verified 256 parameter pairs against all surviving vertices; independent hull volume computations agreed in 11 low-dimensional instances. The finite checks do not substitute for the analytic proof. No scientific claim depends on numerical approximations.

## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)
Relevant primary literature establishes facet and original-volume formulas, but not the one-oriented-root deletion body, its contact-face threshold \(pq=2(p+q)\), or its cap-loss formula. HJM's Proposition 4.5 concerns facets of the original symmetric-edge polytope; the cap's base is a facet of the *truncated* polytope, not generally of the original. Awasthi–Kalantari–Zhang use the relevant distance as a robustness parameter but do not compute it for this family. The nearest indexed root-deletion result concerns a positive-root product after removing an entire matching diagonal, without the two signed layers, and does not imply the present theorem. Historical terminology and older special-case tables remain a residual risk; absence of a search hit alone is not treated as priority proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The signed-versus-undirected distinction matters geometrically: deleting just one orientation breaks central symmetry and cuts a single nonsimplicial cap. The theorem classifies a sharp transition in exposed-contact *dimension*, not merely a floating-point distance. Its factorial-sum cap volume makes this geometric perturbation quantitatively exact in every dimension, including an elementary but nontrivial \(1/[2(q+1)]\) relative loss in the two-vertex-shore case. This connects the robustness parameter for vertex recovery to a natural lattice-polytope family with existing facet/volume theory. The classification and complete parameter law are mathematically motivated rather than a parameter cherry-pick.

## Closest literature and limitations
Higashitani–Jochemko–Michałek (2018, arXiv:1807.07678), especially Sections 3–4, covers the undeleted symmetric-edge polytope. D'Alì–Delucchi–Michałek (2019, arXiv:1910.05193) connects symmetric-edge, adjacency, and metric fundamental polytopes. Chen–Davis–Korchevskaia (2021, arXiv:2107.12315) addresses face subgraphs. Awasthi–Kalantari–Zhang (2018, arXiv:1802.01515) motivates vertex-to-remaining-hull distance. Exact one-oriented-root truncation is the new object. The result does not supply the full face lattice or Ehrhart series of the truncated body. Older equivalent formulations might exist and independent review is outstanding.
