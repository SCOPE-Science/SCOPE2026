## correctness

PASS

The geometric quotient |K_9|/C9 is not a naive quotient of nine-gon diagonal vertex orbits. For a regular convex nonagon, the secondary associahedron GKZ affine space is the orthogonal complement to Fourier modes 0 and ±1 in R9, leaving real 6-dimensional C9 representation C_2⊕C_3⊕C_4; the invariant interior barycenter yields an equivariant sphere S5. Decompose U=C_2⊕C_4 (free C9 action on S3) and W=C_3 (kernel H=C3). The invariant closed halves retract onto lens L(9;2,4) and S1, and intersection E=(S(U)×S(W))/C9 is the mapping torus of lens F=S(U)/H. Wang gives H1(E)=Z⊕C3, H2(E)=C3, H3(E)=Z, H4(E)=Z. The map E→A embeds C3 into C9 on H1 and multiplies H3 fundamental class by 3, while E→B identifies the free H1 base generator. Mayer–Vietoris gives H1(Y)=C3, H2=H4=0, H5=Z and 0→C3→H3(Y)→C3→0, so |H3|=9 but NOT whether cyclic/split; stabilizers H along S(W) yield π1=C9/H=C3 by Armstrong/van Kampen. Independent exhaustive enumeration of all noncrossing faces gives (27,225,825,1485,1287,429) and rotational orbits (3,25,93,165,143,49), with 12 faces stabilized nontrivially, matching representation weights. The finite orbit checker alone cannot prove integral homology; the Mayer–Vietoris argument carries it.

## originality

PASS

PASS best-of-knowledge for a nonfree composite-nine rotation quotient. The relevant published prime-polygon equivariant lens-space theorem explicitly assumes prime p, giving free actions and C_p torsion, hence does not cover n=9 where weight 3 has C3 stabilizer. Classical weighted-sphere and secondary-polytope theory are acknowledged inputs; an older general composite-weight orbifold homology formula could cover the exact invariants and remains a priority risk.

The natural rotation action develops exceptional isotropy, making π1=C3 and a nontrivial 3-primary order-nine H3 extension unlike ordinary prime lens spaces. The exact order and nonfree homology information is worthwhile even though the internal structure Z9 vs C3² is deliberately left undetermined.
