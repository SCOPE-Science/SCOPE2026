# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact orthogonal-pair subset norm reduces exponentially many subset constraints to their cardinalities and the number of singly selected pairs. Both ranges have explicit analytic inequalities under the stated power threshold. The source-to-sink calibration lower bound proves global minimum cost, not merely stationarity. The contained verifier was run successfully for 10,772 finite checks; the proof is analytic and holds for every qualified dimension and exponent.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
Teplitskaya (2026), Question 1.2, asks for bounds. Cherkashin (2025), Theorem 2, proves an existential degree \(d+1\) benchmark. Volz and collaborators give a general optimal-star criterion. Actual published-finding corpus and literature searches for paired orthogonal directions, cross-polytope stars, and degree \(2d-1\) found no matching stronger outcome. An explicit construction plus the all-subset verification is not stated in the inspected sources. An older unindexed equivalent remains possible; a negative search is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a motivated explicit improvement in a named asymptotic extremal-degree problem, nearly doubling the known linear lower bound in the fixed \(p>1/2\) regime. Its strength is a global optimal network with finite terminals, rather than a first-order necessary condition. The coefficient two may be nonsharp and no general upper bound is established.

## Limitations and closest prior work
The sufficient dimension can be enormous near either endpoint of \(p\). Only \(1/2<p<1\) is covered. The strongest inspected prior existence theorem has degree \(d+1\), while the older criterion covers arbitrary feasible subset vectors in principle but does not spell out this family. Literature priority remains provisional and no independent audit has occurred.
