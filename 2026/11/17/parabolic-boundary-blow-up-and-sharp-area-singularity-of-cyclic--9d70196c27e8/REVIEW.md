# Scientific review: Parabolic boundary blow-up and sharp area singularity of cyclic polygons

## Correctness — PASS
The exact short-gap chord closure yields \(\varepsilon=(H/6)t^2+O(t^4)\), and the analytic inverse in \(t^2\) gives the radius law with controlled remainder. The exact oriented area identity gives \(A=(H/6)t+O(t^3)\). Independent chord-parameter vertex formulas establish \(x_j=u_j+O(t^2)\) and \(y_j=t\,u_j(S-u_j)+O(t^3)\), with correct fixed-\(n\) polygonal rather than smooth-curve convergence. Positivity \(H>0\) and compact positive side-length sets support all inverse and uniformity steps. Independent numerical checks passed across six side vectors, including the classical triangle and quadrilateral consistency examples. Numerical tests are not substituted for the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical uncertainty
Kouřimská–Skuppin–Springborn's 2015 full text proves existence and uniqueness but does not give the explicit boundary normal form, derivatives or rescaled vertex polygon. Connelly's 2009 full text gives algebraic area relations, which require branch selection and additional asymptotic analysis before the present geometric limiting law. The classical Heron/Brahmagupta cases \(n=3,4\) are excluded from the asserted new dimensional range. Actual semantic searches in mathematical research records and public literature did not identify a stronger theorem with these parameters and outputs. The main residual risk is an older unstated asymptotic consequence or overlooked specialized analysis of polygonal feasibility boundaries.

## Value — PASS
The classical polygon inequalities describe only binary feasibility, whereas this theorem identifies a natural *sharp boundary modulus* for the optimal area and radius throughout every higher side count. The explicit cubic moment \(H\) and all-vertex parabolic blow-up distinguish two kinds of geometric information (scalar size and shape) and quantify conditioning of cyclic-polygon reconstruction near collapse. The complete limiting polygon is a structural geometric classification along the codimension-one face of the length cone, not a numerical increment or arbitrary slice.
