# Parabolic-lens law for crossings of random chords in nearly tangent circles

## Finding
Take two circles of radius one with centers \(c_-=(-(2-\delta)/2,0)\) and \(c_+=((2-\delta)/2,0)\), where \(0<\delta<2\). Pick two independent arclength-uniform endpoints on each circle; the two endpoint pairs define independent chords \(L_-\) and \(L_+\). Let \(C_\delta\) be the event that their closed line segments intersect. Except for a null event, an intersection \(Z_\delta\) is unique. The sharp tangency law is
\[
\Pr(C_\delta)\sim\frac{8}{3\pi^3}\delta^{3/2}\log(1/\delta)\qquad(\delta\downarrow0).
\]
Moreover, conditioned on \(C_\delta\), the scaled intersection \(\bigl(Z_{\delta,1}/\delta,Z_{\delta,2}/\sqrt\delta\bigr)\) converges **in total variation** to the probability measure having planar density
\[
\frac34\mathbf1_{\{(x,y):y^2+2|x|<1\}}.
\]
The limiting vertical marginal has density \(\frac34(1-y^2)\) on \((-1,1)\), so its second moment is \(1/5\). Conditional on vertical coordinate \(y\), the limiting horizontal coordinate is uniform on \([-(1-y^2)/2,(1-y^2)/2]\).

## Assumptions and scope
All chords are generated by **two independent uniform circumference endpoints**, separately for each circle. This is not motion-invariant line sampling. The radii are equal to one; the center separation is \(2-\delta\). A crossing is an intersection of the **segments**, not merely of their supporting infinite lines. All the asymptotics are for \(\delta\to0^+\). The normalized intersection is defined on \(C_\delta\) only. Equality of endpoints, tangencies and parallel supporting lines are null events.

## Proof
**1. Exact intersection-density identity.** A chord formed from two uniform angles on a unit circle has a supporting line represented by \(n_\phi\cdot(z-c)=\rho\), where \(n_\phi=(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\), \(\phi\) is uniform in \([0,\pi)\), and the signed offset \(\rho\) is independent of \(\phi\) with the arcsine density \(\bigl(\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\bigr)^{-1}\) on \((-1,1)\). This follows by changing variables from the two independent endpoint angles to their mean and half-difference, accounting for the unoriented normal. For two lines of normals \(n_\phi,n_\psi\), the map from their offsets to their intersection \(z\) has absolute Jacobian \(|\det(n_\phi,n_\psi)|=|\sin(\phi-\psi)|\). A point lies on both actual chord segments exactly when it belongs to both disks. Thus the crossing-point subprobability density is precisely
\[
q_\delta(z)=\frac{\mathbf1_{K_\delta}(z)}{\pi^4}\int_0^\pi\!\int_0^\pi
\frac{|\sin(\phi-\psi)|\,d\phi\,d\psi}
{\sqrt{1-[n_\phi\cdot(z-c_-)]^2}\sqrt{1-[n_\psi\cdot(z-c_+)]^2}},
\quad K_\delta=B(c_-,1)\cap B(c_+,1).
\]
This is an identity for each \(0<\delta<2\); the square roots vanish only on angle-null sets (or boundary-null point sets).

**2. Blow-up of the lens.** Write \(z=(\delta x,\sqrt\delta y)\). The scaled lens \(D_\delta\) obeys the exact inequality
\[
y^2+(2-\delta)|x|+\delta(x^2+1/4)\le1.
\]
It tends, up to a null boundary, to \(D=\{y^2+2|x|<1\}\), whose area is \(\int_{-1}^{1}(1-y^2)dy=4/3\). The area Jacobian is \(\delta^{3/2}\). Put \(v_\pm=z-c_\pm\), \(a_\pm=1-|v_\pm|^2=\delta h_\pm\), where
\[
h_\pm=1-y^2\pm(2-\delta)x-\delta(x^2+1/4).
\]
For each fixed interior \((x,y)\in D\), both \(h_\pm>0\), and the normal directions of \(v_\pm\) approach the same projective horizontal direction, at angular separation \(O(\sqrt\delta)\).

**3. Angular logarithm.** Let \(I_\delta(x,y)\) denote the double angular integral in the expression for \(q_\delta\), after the above scaling. For any fixed interior point of \(D\),
\[
\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\frac{I_\delta(x,y)}{\log(1/\delta)}=2\pi.
\]
Indeed, the singular angular kernels have the form \(k_{a,\alpha}(\phi)=[1-(1-a)\cos^2(\phi-\alpha)]^{-1/2}\), with \(a=\delta h\) and \(\alpha=O(\sqrt\delta)\) modulo \(\pi\). Their integral on any fixed neighborhood of the singular projective angle is \(\log(1/a)+O(1)\), while the integral outside that neighborhood is bounded. Split the angular square into a neighborhood of \(\phi=0\) with \(\psi\) outside, the symmetric region, both angles near the singular projective direction, and neither. On the first region, \(k_\psi|\sin(\phi-\psi)|\to1\) at the singular value of \(\phi\), giving \(\pi\log(1/\delta)\) after the neighborhood shrinks; the symmetric region contributes the same. Where both angles lie within angular distance \(\eta\) of the singular direction, \(|\sin(\phi-\psi)|\le\operatorname{dist}_\pi(\phi,0)+\operatorname{dist}_\pi(\psi,0)\), so its normalized contribution is \(O(\eta)\). The remaining region is bounded. Taking first \(\delta\to0\) and then \(\eta\to0\) proves the coefficient \(2\pi\).

**4. Uniform integrability and conditional limit.** This pointwise asymptotic also holds in \(L^1\) on scaled space after setting \(I_\delta=0\) off \(D_\delta\). To see this, the elementary projective-angle triangle inequality gives
\[
|\sin(\phi-\psi)|\le |\sin(\phi-\alpha_-)|+|\sin(\psi-\alpha_+)|+C\sqrt\delta.
\]
The estimates \(\int_0^\pi k_{a,\alpha}\,d\phi\le C(1+|\log a|)\) and \(\int_0^\pi k_{a,\alpha}|\sin(\phi-\alpha)|\,d\phi\le C\) imply
\[
I_\delta\le C\{2+|\log a_-|+|\log a_+|+\sqrt\delta(1+|\log a_-|)(1+|\log a_+|)\}.
\]
On \(D_\delta\), the functions \(h_\pm\) are affine decreasing or increasing in the horizontal coordinate up to a uniformly small quadratic correction, with horizontal derivatives bounded away from zero. Horizontal integration shows that \(\int_{D_\delta}|\log h_\pm|^2\,dxdy\) is uniformly bounded: the logarithmic singularity at each boundary is integrable, and the domain is uniformly bounded. Consequently the terms with \(|\log h_\pm|/\log(1/\delta)\) tend to zero in \(L^1\); the product term is also negligible because \(\sqrt\delta\log(1/\delta)\to0\). The constant term supplies a uniform-integrability bound. Combining this with the interior pointwise limit yields
\[
\frac{I_\delta\mathbf1_{D_\delta}}{\log(1/\delta)}\longrightarrow 2\pi\mathbf1_D\quad\text{in }L^1(\mathbb R^2).
\]
The exact density identity therefore gives
\[
\Pr(C_\delta)=\frac{\delta^{3/2}}{\pi^4}\int_{D_\delta}I_\delta\,dxdy
\sim\frac{\delta^{3/2}\log(1/\delta)}{\pi^4}\,2\pi\,\frac43
=\frac8{3\pi^3}\delta^{3/2}\log(1/\delta).
\]
Dividing the scaled density by this probability yields \(L^1\) convergence to \(\frac34\mathbf1_D\), equivalent to total-variation convergence. Boundedness of the scaled domain gives convergence of its fixed continuous moments, including \(\mathbb E[y^2]=\frac34\int_{-1}^1 y^2(1-y^2)dy=1/5\).

## Verification
The accompanying `checker.py` directly samples independent endpoint angles in Cartesian coordinates, computes the crossing of the two *segments* rather than just their supporting lines, and tests the scaled location distribution. It separately applies Gaussian quadrature to the double angular integral at the center of the lens, comparing its logarithmic coefficient with \(2\pi\). Running `python checker.py` with NumPy and SciPy prints `NEAR_TANGENT_CHORD_CHECKS_OK`. Numerically, at \(\delta=0.02\), 3,500,000 independent pairs yielded 3,897 crossings, empirical probability \(0.00111343\), compared with leading asymptotic \(0.00095162\); the nonvanishing finite-gap correction is expected. The empirical scaled vertical second moment was \(0.21616\), versus limiting \(0.2\). These numerical tests are not a proof of the limit; the preceding density and uniform-integrability argument provides it.

## Relationship to prior work
David and Fix (1964) studied intersections of random chords of **one** circle. Bortolotto and Souza (2025, arXiv:2504.12866, especially Theorems 1–2 and Section 2) provide a detailed full-text treatment of same-circle chord intersection locations and rotationally invariant line laws. Their line ensembles have a **common center**, hence do not directly give the displaced-circle lens and its near-tangency singularity. Torres, Manrique-Mirón and Treviño-Aguilar (2019, arXiv:1911.05576) study crossing angles in one disk. A published result on nested **concentric** circles considers a different limit where center separation is identically zero and radii vary. These comparisons concern the statements, not merely their titles; no examined statement implies the present near-external-tangency coefficient or the conditional uniform parabolic-lens law. A complete search of all historical sources has not been performed.

## Limitations
The theorem holds only for equal unit radii, endpoint-uniform chords, and the near-external-tangency regime; it does not provide an exact probability at arbitrary separation or a quantified convergence rate. No assertion is made for motion-invariant random lines. The original David–Fix 1964 paper was located bibliographically but its full text was inaccessible here; an undiscovered older displaced-circle result remains a historical-priority risk. The result has not undergone independent expert audit.
