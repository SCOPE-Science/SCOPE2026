# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact line-ensemble density follows from independent uniform endpoint angles and an invertible Jacobian off the null parallel-line set. An exact scaled-lens equation, two logarithmically singular angular channels, and uniform \(L^1\) bounds give the sharp probability coefficient and conditional total-variation convergence. The standalone checker independently tests both angular quadrature and actual Cartesian segment intersections. Numerical experiments do not prove the limit; the analytical uniform-integrability argument does.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
David and Fix (1964) considered crossings in a single circle; only a bibliographic record could be read in this run. Bortolotto and Souza (arXiv:2504.12866) were examined in substantive full text: their Theorems 1–2 use two lines drawn from a common-center rotationally invariant line ensemble. The closest indexed circle-crossing record concerns nested concentric circles, while a lens-distance record concerns a different statistic. The two distinct centers in the present problem cause an anisotropically shrinking geometric lens, and the earlier radial laws do not imply the near-external-tangency asymptotic or conditional spatial law. Uninspected historical work remains a real priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem gives a complete limiting geometric description of a natural crossing event between two nearly externally tangent independent circular chord systems. The surprising \(\delta^{3/2}\log(1/\delta)\) rate and the explicit uniform parabolic-lens conditional law jointly identify the geometry of rare intersections, rather than merely a low-order correction or an arbitrary parametric statistic.

## Limitations
Only equal unit circles, independently endpoint-uniform chords, and the external-tangency limit are covered. There is no exact all-separation formula, quantitative rate of convergence, or independent expert validation; historical priority is not definitive.
