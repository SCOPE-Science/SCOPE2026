# Review: Parity-gated majority oscillations on matching-deleted complete graphs

## Correctness

PASS. Exact neighbor counts for nonadjacent pairs and universal vertices give the full update table for both parity classes of the graph order. The even case reduces to an involution on the balanced layer; the odd case reduces to two critical levels with an exact return classification. The formulas and the at-most-three-update entry bound follow for arbitrary integer sizes. Direct labelled enumeration and an independently implemented weighted-profile method corroborate the infinite proof on their stated finite domains. A finite check alone would not certify the infinite theorem.

## Originality

PASS with a historical-priority qualification. The period-at-most-two theorem of Goles and Olivos is strictly weaker than an exact enumeration; the inspected Benjamini et al. full-text results address different global questions. The closest later primary full-text census by Turau is restricted to trees. Published complete-multipartite parity automata obey a different XOR rule, and compared crown/prism/Möbius-ladder majority statements concern different graph families. Equivalent graph descriptions, broader results, exact-table searches and claim-to-prior implication comparisons were conducted; none returned a covering result. The full original Goles–Olivos paper remained inaccessible and absence of a search result does not prove novelty.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This supplies a complete fixed- and primitive-two-cycle census for an infinite family of near-complete graphs. The fine dependence on the parity and number of universal vertices leads to a sharp oscillation extinction/reappearance law and differs substantially from the high multiplicity of balanced oscillations in the even case. A simple exact finite-time convergence law accompanies the counts. These are natural dynamical invariants, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Boundaries

All proofs assume binary synchronous open-neighborhood majority with unchanged tie states on simple graphs obtained by deleting disjoint edges from a clique. Asynchronous, weighted, or more general deletion patterns remain outside the claim. Source priority has the specific access caveat above. There is no independent external mathematical review.
