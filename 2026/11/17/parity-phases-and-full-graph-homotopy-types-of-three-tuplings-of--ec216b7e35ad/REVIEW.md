# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof explicitly partitions the vertex triangles by their three-factor occupancy and identifies every allowed adjacent pair. The maximum face size six excludes all higher simplices. The graph-product walk criterion is proved by path parity, backtracking and odd cycles. Component equality follows from cycle-rotation automorphisms, including the asymmetric complete-bipartite factors. Independent exhaustive facet-based enumeration for eight length triples confirms literal graph equality and individual component Betti ranks, not merely aggregate Euler numbers. The proof is uniform for all polygon lengths at least four.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations
Definition 2.1 and Theorem A of Lesh–Schreiner–Wahl (arXiv:2506.10436v1, first public June 12, 2025) establish the construction and a connectivity lower bound, not this join-of-three-polygons graph decomposition. The full accessible primary article was checked, including the connectivity proof and applications. A related published-finding corpus record on cyclic-six-polytope three-tuplings treats a different simplicial five-sphere family and produces one or two components, unlike the four-to-ten-component phase diagram here. Other adjacent records concern doubles of polygon joins rather than triples, or iterated doubles rather than a single triple. The categorical-product connectivity result is classical and credited. Residual risk: a specialized or unindexed direct-product formulation might imply the same result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The join of three polygon boundaries is a natural infinite family of combinatorial five-spheres. The full decomposition resolves all path components and integral fundamental-group/homology ranks, exhibits a four-stage parity change, and gives a sharpness example for what can be inferred about connectivity of three-tuplings even in the sphere case. The all-square example yields ten nontrivial components, so the result is not simply a rephrasing of connectedness. The construction's topology has a concrete structural explanation.
