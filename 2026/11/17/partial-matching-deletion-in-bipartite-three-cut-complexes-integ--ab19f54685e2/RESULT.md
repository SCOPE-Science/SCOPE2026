# Partial-matching deletion in bipartite three-cut complexes: integral homotopy and two exceptional phases

## Finding
Let \(a,b\ge3\), \(1\le t\le\min(a,b)\), and \(G=K_{a,b}\setminus\{a_i b_i:1\le i\le t\}\), where all deleted cross-edges have distinct endpoints. Let \(\Delta_3(G)\) be the **ordinary** three-cut complex, and let \(D=\Delta_3(G)^\vee\) be its combinatorial Alexander dual. Define
\[
B(a,b,t)=\binom{a-1}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}-t(a-2)(b-2)+\binom{t}{2}.
\]
Then the **integral homotopy type** of the dual is completely determined:
\[
|D|\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigvee^4 S^1,&(a,b,t)=(3,3,3),\\
\bigl(\bigvee^4 S^1\bigr)\vee S^2,&(a,b,t)=(4,4,4),\\
\bigl(\bigvee^t S^1\bigr)\vee\bigl(\bigvee^{B(a,b,t)}S^3\bigr),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In the third line the integer \(B(a,b,t)\) is nonnegative; an empty sphere wedge is omitted. The reduced **integral** homology of the original cut complex is therefore, in the generic case,
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_3(G);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{B(a,b,t)},&j=a+b-6,\\
\mathbb Z^t,&j=a+b-4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The exceptions have \(\widetilde H_2(\Delta_3(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4\) for \((3,3,3)\), and \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z\), \(\widetilde H_4\cong\mathbb Z^4\) for \((4,4,4)\); all other reduced integral homology vanishes. No homotopy wedge decomposition of the original cut complex is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. Label shores \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_a\}\) and \(B'=\{b_1,\ldots,b_b\}\), and remove precisely \(a_i b_i\) for \(1\le i\le t\). The ordinary three-cut complex is generated by complements of disconnected induced triples. The Alexander dual is \(\Delta^\vee=\{F\subseteq V:V\setminus F\notin\Delta\}\). All homotopy and homology groups are of the geometric realization; integral cohomology uses ordinary constant coefficients. Our theorem includes full matching deletions for all shores, including two small exceptional cases, but the previously known one-edge and large balanced full-crown endpoints are acknowledged below.

## Proof
**1. Exact Alexander-dual decomposition.** For this graph, an induced triple is disconnected if and only if it lies in one shore or contains a deleted cross-edge. Indeed, a mixed triple not containing a deleted pair has both possible cross-edges and is connected. It follows that a face of \(D\) either contains at most two vertices from each shore and none of the \(t\) deleted pairs, or consists of one of the \(t\) deleted pairs itself (and its subfaces). Write \(L_t\) for the former complex. The edge interiors of these \(t\) exceptional one-simplices are absent from \(L_t\), while their endpoints lie in the connected one-skeleton of \(L_t\). Hence attaching them produces
\[
|D|\simeq |L_t|\vee\bigvee^t S^1.
\]
The connectivity of the one-skeleton follows because each same-shore pair is an edge and every vertex has a cross-neighbor when \(a,b\ge3\).

**2. Sequential forbidden-pair removal.** For \(0\le j\le t\), let \(L_j\) consist of subsets using at most two vertices of either shore and avoiding deleted pairs \(a_i b_i\) for \(i\le j\). Initially
\[
L_0=\operatorname{sk}_1(\Delta^{a-1})*\operatorname{sk}_1(\Delta^{b-1})
\simeq \bigvee^{\binom{a-1}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}}S^3.
\]
At step \(j\), put \(e_j=\{a_j,b_j\}\). The closed star of \(e_j\) in \(L_{j-1}\) is \(e_j*Q_j\), with link \(Q_j\) the graph on \((A\setminus\{a_j\})\sqcup(B'\setminus\{b_j\})\) whose edges are all cross pairs except the previous \(j-1\) removed matching pairs. In symbols, \(Q_j=K_{a-1,b-1}\setminus M_{j-1}\), regarded as a one-dimensional simplicial complex. The intersection of that star with \(L_j\) is \(\partial e_j*Q_j=\Sigma Q_j\). Consequently
\[
\widetilde H_k(L_{j-1},L_j;\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H_{k-2}(Q_j;\mathbb Z).
\]
These identifications come from the quotient \((e_j*Q_j)/(\partial e_j*Q_j)\simeq\Sigma^2Q_j\).

**3. Connectivity and cycle generation in the link.** Each \(Q_j\) is connected except when \((a,b,j)=(3,3,3)\), when it is the union of two disjoint edges. For a connected \(Q_j\), its integral first homology is free of rank
\[
\mu_j=(a-2)(b-2)-(j-1),
\]
by the graph Euler characteristic. Every integral cycle of a connected \(Q_j\) is generated by four-cycles unless \((a,b,j)=(4,4,4)\), for which \(Q_j\cong C_6\) is its own one-dimensional cycle generator and has no four-cycle. To prove the claim, decompose graph cycles into induced cycles using chords. Any chordless bipartite cycle of length at least eight would force one vertex to miss at least two vertices across the bipartition, impossible because \(Q_j\) deletes a matching. A chordless six-cycle uses three deleted nonconsecutive cross-edges as its missing chords. Unless the entire link has three vertices in each shore, there is an outside vertex adjacent to all three opposite-shore vertices of the cycle: the three missing partners are already on the cycle. Coning those three alternating two-edge segments to this outside vertex expresses the six-cycle as an **integral signed sum of three four-cycles**. On exactly three vertices per shore, a chordless six-cycle requires all three matching pairs deleted, which gives precisely the stated exceptional \(Q_j\).

**4. An explicit integral surjectivity witness.** Let \(u,u'\in A\setminus\{a_j\}\) and \(v,v'\in B'\setminus\{b_j\}\) form a four-cycle in \(Q_j\). Form the oriented simplicial join of triangle-boundary cycles
\[
\partial[a_j,u,u']*\partial[b_j,v,v'].
\]
This is an integral three-cycle of \(L_{j-1}\): every constituent four-vertex simplex has two vertices from each shore and avoids every previously deleted pair. Under the relative map \(H_3(L_{j-1};\mathbb Z)\to H_3(L_{j-1},L_j;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(Q_j;\mathbb Z)\), its image is, up to orientation, the corresponding four-cycle of \(Q_j\). Thus this map is surjective for all connected-link steps except \((4,4,4)\). The long exact sequence then proves, at each ordinary step, vanishing lower reduced homology, no torsion, and a decrement of \(\mu_j\) in the rank of \(H_3\).

**5. Homotopy type at ordinary steps.** Since \(e_j*Q_j\) is contractible and \(\Sigma Q_j\) is simply connected when \(Q_j\) is connected, van Kampen gives \(\pi_1(L_j)\cong\pi_1(L_{j-1})\). The initial \(L_0\) is simply connected, so all ordinary successive complexes are simply connected. Their only possible nonzero reduced homology is free in degree three, with rank
\[
\binom{a-1}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}-\sum_{j=1}^t\bigl((a-2)(b-2)-(j-1)\bigr)=B(a,b,t)=\binom{a-1}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}-t(a-2)(b-2)+\binom{t}{2}.
\]
Hurewicz supplies degree-three sphere representatives and Whitehead's theorem upgrades the resulting homology equivalence to the stated wedge of three-spheres (including the contractible case when the rank is zero). This proves the generic clause.

**6. Exceptional complete small matchings.** For \((a,b,t)=(3,3,3)\), the first two removals yield a contractible \(L_2\). In \(L_3\), there are exactly six triangles, each containing a unique edge between two same-shore vertices, and every such same-shore edge occurs in exactly one triangle. Collapsing these six free triangle-edge pairs leaves the off-diagonal six-cycle. Therefore \(L_3\simeq S^1\), and step 1 yields \(D\simeq\bigvee^4S^1\). For \((4,4,4)\), the first three removals yield contractible \(L_3\). The last link is \(Q_4=C_6\), whence the relative sequence gives \(\widetilde H_2(L_4;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), with every other reduced homology zero. Van Kampen still gives \(\pi_1(L_4)=0\). Hurewicz and Whitehead imply \(L_4\simeq S^2\), and adjoining the four exceptional edges gives \(D\simeq (\bigvee^4S^1)\vee S^2\).

**7. Integral homology of the original cut complex.** Combinatorial Alexander duality for a complex on \(N=a+b\) vertices gives
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_3(G);\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H^{N-j-3}(D;\mathbb Z).
\]
The computed duals have free integral cohomology and the indicated degrees, proving all statements of the finding. The homology conclusion for \(\Delta_3(G)\) does **not** assert a corresponding integral wedge decomposition for that original complex.

## Verification
A stand-alone Python 3 program, `artifacts/verify.py`, builds every graph from the adjacency relation, independently enumerates disconnected triples, constructs the dual by its defining forbidden triples, and compares it with the predicted \(L_t\) plus exceptional edges. It computes reduced mod-two simplicial homology by complete boundary-matrix elimination, checks the graph-cycle generation property of every relevant link over the same field, and compares the predicted Betti ranks. The complete test range is \(3\le a\le b\le7\) and \(1\le t\le a\), comprising 65 instances; the largest dual contains 793 nonempty simplices. The run output is recorded as `artifacts/verification_output.txt` and was reproduced from the packaged files. These checks corroborate the algebra and the two exceptional cases; **the universal integral conclusion is derived from steps 1–7, not from modular enumeration.**

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* [1], treat intact complete bipartite graphs. Shen et al., *Realizations and Uniqueness of Cut Complexes of Graphs* [2], investigate recognition and uniqueness for three-cut complexes, not this partial matching deletion homotopy spectrum. A published complete-crown result [3] establishes the equal-shore endpoint \(a=b=t\ge5\), including the two nonzero degrees; an earlier one-edge classification gives the \(t=1\) endpoint. Both are explicitly credited. Jojić, Vrećica and Živaljević's multiple-chessboard shellability theorem [4] helps explain the complete-column endpoint but does not determine the partially forbidden bipartite-board filtration or its two exceptional phases. The **new** material is the general partial-matching classification, the integral cycle-lattice surjectivity mechanism, and the sharp exceptional \(S^1\) and \(S^2\) phases. The known higher-independence/dissociation complexes for deleted matchings forbid connected induced triples instead of the disconnected triples defining the Alexander dual here, and are not equivalent.

## Limitations
The deleted edges must be vertex-disjoint, and both shore sizes must be at least three. Nothing is asserted for arbitrary forbidden bipartite subgraphs, larger cut parameters, equivariant homotopy types, or a sphere-wedge decomposition of the original cut complex. The finite checker is mod two and cannot, by itself, establish torsion-freeness or an infinite statement. The novelty comparison cannot exclude an equivalent unindexed formulation in the theory of subcomplexes of multiple chessboard complexes.
