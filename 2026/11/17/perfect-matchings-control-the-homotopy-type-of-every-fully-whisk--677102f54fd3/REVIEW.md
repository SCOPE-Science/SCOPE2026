# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Each matching of a fully whiskered graph has one original-edge matching \(M\) and a freely chosen subset \(T\) of pendant edges over vertices not covered by \(M\). Choosing the least uncovered vertex and toggling its pendant edge matches the entire Boolean fiber. The directed augmented Hasse graph cannot have a cycle: original-edge deletion strictly decreases \(M\), and within fixed \(M\) every reversed arrow adds only the distinguished pendant edge while all other downward arrows delete non-distinguished pendant edges. Critical cells correspond bijectively to perfect matchings of the base graph, all in dimension \(k-1\), yielding the asserted integral wedge. The nonempty one- and two-vertex edge cases are handled explicitly. The accompanying standalone checker tests 1,114 graphs, with 1,104 Hasse acyclicity and 1,103 independent mod-two homology checks. None of the finite checks substitutes for the proof for all graphs.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
Bayer, Jelić Milutinović and Vega, *Matching Complexes of Outerplanar Graphs*, arXiv:2411.04601v1, prove a general wedge-or-contractible theorem restricted to outerplanar graphs; no exact full-whiskering perfect-matching formula is stated in the inspected proof. Deshpande and Singh, arXiv:2001.05448, study higher independence complexes on the vertices of fully whiskered graphs, a different simplicial complex; their patchwork method is established prior work. Polygonal-line-tiling matching results, arXiv:2211.12559, assume a different restricted graph family. Indexed alias and statement searches disclosed no equivalence or stronger prior theorem that implies the all-base-graph formula. The explicit differences are the domain (all finite simple base graphs) and exact multiplicity \(p(G)\). A potentially overlooked folklore or unindexed corona result remains a material originality risk.

## Value — PASS
The classification unifies the homotopy types of an infinite class of generally nonplanar matching complexes in an explicit invariant, detects perfect matchability by contractibility, and establishes integral torsion-freeness. It gives a uniform structural proof rather than an arbitrary finite graph calculation. The result does not address equivariant homology, naturality of generators, or weighted persistent topology.
