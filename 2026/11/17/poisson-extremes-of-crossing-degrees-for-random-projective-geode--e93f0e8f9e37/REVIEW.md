# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The conditional projective intersection kernel is derived from uniform intersection locations on projective lines of circumference \(\pi\), followed by independent random phases. This yields an exact binomial conditional degree law. The rare-length Poisson process follows from independent regularly varying lengths. The vital nonstandard step checks that long arcs cannot produce anomalously small crossing degrees: a Chernoff bound multiplied by the sample size is negligible because the exponent exceeds one. For short arcs, a conditional variance estimate and a tight-candidate union bound prove simultaneous normalized closeness of degrees to lengths. The fixed-degree coefficient follows from a justified Stieltjes scaling integral. The executable direct-geometry and quadrature checks passed. Infinite convergence rests on the proof, not finite experiments.

## Originality — PASS, with historical risk

Acan's 2015 full accessible paper studies random combinatorial matchings of circular endpoints and includes degree and component results, but not the projective isotropic continuous-arc model or a lower-degree extreme process. The 2014 spherical crossing question establishes only a pairwise result. Bonamy–Mohar–Wesolek's 2020 full text covers spherical graphons of drawings with shared endpoints, not global low-degree extremes in independent projective arcs. The most relevant previously published iid spherical-arc theorem determines conditional binomial degrees, star moments and normalized degree convergence, and those are credited; its full result does not state the joint Poisson extremal process or the projective fixed-degree rare-event coefficients. No uncovered historical text is asserted not to contain the claim. Priority remains provisional.

## Value — PASS

The minimum degree is a natural global geometric-network observable, qualitatively different from typical normalized degrees. The theorem identifies a full Poisson spectrum of low-degree vertices and its dependence on the small-length power-law exponent, including a sharp Rayleigh specialization for projective geodesics joining uniform endpoints. The argument verifies that crossing dependencies cannot corrupt these extremes, rather than extrapolating the one-vertex degree mixture. The result provides a precise, motivated rare-network statistic and is not a mere numerical substitution.

## Limits and source comparison

The result is restricted to isotropic independent projective arcs of lengths below the cut threshold and exponents strictly greater than one. Critical exponents, anisotropic directions, rate estimates and definitive historical priority are open. The closest relevant sources are arXiv:1501.01489, arXiv:2008.10459, the January 2014 spherical crossing discussion, and the published 2026 geodesic-needle star-spectrum result.
