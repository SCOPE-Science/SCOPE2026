# Self-review: metric-graph biharmonic quartic envelopes

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is stated on the actual mean-zero operator domain of the self-adjoint Kirchhoff Laplacian; the norm is \(\int |f''|^2\) and not \(\int |f'|^2\). Cauchy–Schwarz with the *first-order* Green kernel establishes the squared evaluation norm \(Q(x)=\int_Gg(x,y)^2dy\), because \(f(x)=\langle Lf,g_x\rangle\); its extremal is \(L^{-1}g_x\). The interpolation identity for a moving source on an edge accounts for both the local tent Green function and the global zero-mean constant. Squaring and integrating gives the displayed quartic with exact endpoints. The fourth-degree coefficient is checked against the effective-resistance identity, and Foster's trace identity gives the stated topology law, including its bridge and positive-cycle consequences. A finite quartic necessarily attains its interval maximum at an endpoint or a cubic stationary root; flat edges are explicitly included. The proof handles all positive lengths, not just the tested rational samples. An independent-subdivision exact-rational diagnostic checked 140 pointwise values on 35 graph edges, including parallel-edge circles, random weighted graphs, trees and graphs with overlapping cycles. No infinite conclusion is inferred from that finite diagnostic alone.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest antecedent is Baker–Rumely's *Harmonic Analysis on Metrized Graphs* (arXiv:math/0407427, Sections 1, 6, 12–16). Their Green-kernel eigenfunction expansion provides the abstract squared-inverse kernel; its existence is **not** a novelty claim. Their abstract formulas do not supply the present graph-wide finite **quartic envelope**, complete cubic-critical-point optimizer classification, or the electrical leading-coefficient formula. Solomyak (arXiv:math/0206235, main introductory and Sobolev approximation sections) addresses first-order Sobolev approximation, not exact Kirchhoff \(H^2\) evaluation. Usman (2018) treats first-order/coarea inequalities. Borthwick–Jones–Harrell (arXiv:2204.06619, Introduction, heat-kernel and diagonal theory) studies the heat kernel on the diagonal rather than this explicit squared-Green polynomial. Accessible previous first-order graph results give an edgewise quadratic for \(\int|f'|^2\), but that is not the sharp constant for \(\int|f''|^2\). Semantic database searches using the exact invariant, alternative Laplacian terminology, fourth-order Green kernels and edge quartics returned no direct exact coverage. Their negative results cannot establish uniqueness; a classical spline-kernel equivalent, especially outside indexed papers, is the principal residual priority risk. The known Foster identity and the circle/interval spectral formulas are credited, not claimed new.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The object is the natural sharp Sobolev evaluation invariant of the standard **squared Kirchhoff Laplacian** on an arbitrary compact finite metric graph, rather than an arbitrary finite parameter table. The theorem converts infinitely many evaluation points and an infinite-dimensional variational problem into finitely many vertex linear solves and quartic/cubic evaluations, with a complete equality classification. It works for arbitrary topologies and positive edge lengths and detects the electrical cycle rank in the leading quartic coefficients. It is relevant to graph interpolation, spectral Green estimates, sharp evaluation bounds and exact norming-site computation. This is a structural theorem with a continuum of metric inputs, even though it uses elementary one-dimensional Green integration; no downstream generalization or independent certification is asserted.

## Limitations and closest coverage

The statement fixes real functions, unweighted arclength, the standard Kirchhoff operator domain and mean zero. General vertex couplings, vertex masses, potential terms, nonconstant densities and higher Laplacian powers are not covered. Abstract spectral theory already implies \(Q(x)=\sum_{j\ge2}|\varphi_j(x)|^2/\lambda_j^2\); the new formula is a finite exact evaluation that goes beyond this implicit expression. More complete inspection of historical interpolation splines, biharmonic Green functions and network theory could disclose earlier equivalent quartic formulas. The exact-rational checker is reproducible scientific corroboration rather than an independent mathematical audit.
