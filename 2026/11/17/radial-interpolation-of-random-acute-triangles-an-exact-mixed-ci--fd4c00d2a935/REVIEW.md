# Scientific review: mixed-circle random acute triangles

## Correctness — PASS
The complete domain is one pinned point at Euclidean radius \(0\le r\le1\) and two iid arclength-uniform points on the unit circle. The three obtuse-angle events are disjoint except null right-angle events. The boundary-vertex obtuseness integral reduces via the positive Fourier sine series of a circular arc to the odd dilogarithm. Polar visual angles of circle-boundary points have density \( (1-h_r')/(2\pi)\), and the obtuse angular-difference kernel yields \(q_C=1/2-2J(r)/\pi^2\). These identities give the claimed formula, exact endpoint limits, strict opposite monotonicities of its separate obtuseness probabilities, and the linear centered cusp. The independent angular quadrature and Cartesian grid checker passed on five radial parameters; its grid is corroboration, not a proof. The argument does not assert monotonicity of the total probability.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
The closest accessible full mathematical source is Edelman and Strang's 2015 *Random Triangle Theory with Geometry and Applications*, which analyzes a Gaussian/shape-space sampling model and not the mixed pinned-interior/circle-boundary vertex distribution. The 2015 three-uniform-circle discussion gives the boundary endpoint only. Hall's 1982 article concerns three random points from the ball's volume; its abstract was accessible, but its complete text was not. Related full-ellipse, hyperbolic-circle, and Gaussian acuteness results concern three random vertices drawn under different laws and do not imply this radial pinned-vertex formula without a new conditional calculation. A recent fixed-target containment law for three sampled boundary vertices also concerns a distinct event. Searches identified no direct implication, but absence from indexed results does not prove novelty; the inaccessible historical literature remains a risk.

## Value — PASS
This solves a natural interpolation between a center-pinned triangle (acute probability one half) and the classical all-boundary circular triangle (one quarter), identifies which vertex causes obtuseness across the interpolation, and proves a non-smooth spatial response at the center. The split obtuse-angle probabilities and radial cusp are structurally informative, not a change of symbols or a finite numerical tabulation. The result is limited to this specific sampling model.

## Limitations
No assertion is made about arbitrary convex boundaries, three area-uniform samples, total-probability radial monotonicity, or independent historical certification. Numerical and symbolic checks are not independent mathematical validation.
