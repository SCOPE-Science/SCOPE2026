# Reflection-fixed spheres and zero persistence in finite subset spaces of the circle

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge1\), write \(X_k=\exp_k(S^1)\) for the space of nonempty subsets with at most \(k\) points, and let complex conjugation act on subsets. Set \(Y_k=X_k^{\langle r\rangle}\). Then
\[
Y_k\simeq S^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
The interpretation at dimension zero is two points up to homotopy. Moreover every inclusion \(Y_k\hookrightarrow Y_{k+1}\) is zero on all reduced integral homology. Thus the nonzero integral persistent homology class at each filtration stage dies under its next inclusion.

## Assumptions and scope
The circle is \(S^1=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}\), the reflection is \(r(z)=\overline z\), and the topology on \(X_k\) is the quotient of \((S^1)^k\) by the map taking the underlying nonempty point set, including repeated coordinates. A fixed element of this quotient is a set preserved by conjugation, not a tuple with each coordinate fixed. The two individually fixed circle points are \(+1\) and \(-1\). No other hypotheses are imposed; all integer \(k\ge1\) are included. The result is an integral homotopy classification, not an assertion of homeomorphism with a sphere.

## Proof
**The finite CW structure.** Identify the quotient of the circle by reflection with the closed interval \([0,1]\), whose endpoints represent \(+1\) and \(-1\). A reflection-invariant nonempty subset is uniquely determined by integers \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\) indicating which endpoints it includes, together with \(j\) distinct interior orbit positions \(0<t_1<\cdots<t_j<1\). The cardinality is \(a+b+2j\). Hence \(Y_k\) has one open cell \(E_{ab}^j\cong\mathbb R^j\) for every triple with \(1\le a+b+2j\le k\). Its closure is the image of the closed simplex \(0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_j\le1\). At \(t_1=0\), the first orbit is absorbed into the left endpoint; at \(t_j=1\) it is absorbed into the right endpoint; when two consecutive positions collide they become one orbit. This yields a finite (not necessarily regular) CW structure. Compactness of the finite-subset quotient and the locally finite simplex charts identify the weak CW topology with the inherited topology.

Orient the parameter simplex by increasing coordinates. The integral cellular boundary is
\[
\partial E_{ab}^j=E_{1b}^{j-1}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(-1)^i\right)E_{ab}^{j-1}+(-1)^jE_{a1}^{j-1}\qquad(j\ge1),
\]
with nonexistent zero-point cells omitted and with repeated faces combined algebraically. This follows by listing the \(j+1\) codimension-one simplex faces \(t_1=0\), \(t_i=t_{i+1}\), and \(t_j=1\) in the standard alternating orientation order. The symbol \(E_{00}^{0}\) is absent.

**Integral cancellation.** For each available \(j\ge1\), pair the lower cell \(E_{10}^{j-1}\) with the upper cell \(E_{00}^{j}\). For each available \(j\ge1\), also pair \(E_{11}^{j-1}\) with \(E_{01}^{j}\). Both pairing coefficients in the displayed boundary are \(+1\). All remaining face terms lower the interior-orbit count; among gradient alternations this count strictly decreases, so the matching is acyclic even when zero-incidence faces are included. Elementary unit-pivot cancellation of finite free chain complexes therefore preserves integral homology. It leaves exactly two unmatched cells: \(E_{01}^0\) and, when \(k=2q+1\), \(E_{10}^{q}\), or when \(k=2q\), \(E_{11}^{q-1}\). For \(k=1\) the first choice is \(E_{10}^{0}\). Hence \(Y_1\) and \(Y_2\) each have two connected components; for every \(k\ge3\) the reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z\) in dimension \(\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor\), and zero elsewhere. This is an integral chain calculation, not an inference from a finite sample or merely from Euler characteristic.

**Upgrading homology to homotopy.** The first four spaces are explicit from the cells. The space \(Y_3\) is a triangular loop; the addition of \(E_{00}^{2}\) and \(E_{11}^{1}\) gives \(Y_4\), in which the triangle is filled and the new based loop survives. Thus \(Y_3\simeq Y_4\simeq S^1\). Passing to \(Y_5\) attaches two two-cells \(E_{10}^{2}\) and \(E_{01}^{2}\); each has cellular boundary \(E_{11}^{1}\) with coefficient \(+1\). Since \(\pi_1(Y_4)\cong\mathbb Z\) is generated by that single loop, either attaching map normally kills the fundamental group. Therefore \(Y_5\) is simply connected. Further stages attach cells only in dimensions at least two, so all \(Y_k\) with \(k\ge5\) remain simply connected. A simply connected CW complex with the reduced integral homology of one positive-dimensional sphere is homotopy equivalent to that sphere (Hurewicz and homological Whitehead), completing the classification.

**Vanishing of all inclusion maps.** For \(k=2q+1\), put \(Z_q=\partial E_{00}^{q+1}\), computed in the larger space \(Y_{2q+2}\). Its boundary is a cycle using only cells of \(Y_{2q+1}\); it has coefficient exactly \(+1\) on \(E_{10}^{q}\), the unique unmatched cell in the highest dimension of \(Y_{2q+1}\). As there are no \((q+1)\)-cells in that older space and its reduced degree-\(q\) homology is infinite cyclic, \(Z_q\) is a primitive generator (for \(q=0\), it is the difference of the two vertices and generates reduced zero-dimensional homology). In \(Y_{2q+2}\), the same cycle bounds the new cell \(E_{00}^{q+1}\), so the odd-to-even map is zero. For even-to-odd maps, the nonzero reduced homology degrees are different, and the target has no homology in the source degree, so these maps are zero too.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python program `verify_fixed_circle.py` reconstructs all cells, signed boundary matrices, and **all** face incidences (including cancellations invisible in the signed matrices), verifies \(\partial^2=0\), tests the exact prescribed matching and gradient acyclicity, computes rational chain ranks using exact fractions, checks the predicted Betti numbers, and checks the primitive odd-to-even bounding cycle. It exhausts \(1\le k\le16\) and prints `REFLECTION_FIXED_SUBSET_CW_MORSE_PASS`. The infinite conclusion is proved by the uniform cellular argument above, not by extrapolating these sixteen computations.

## Relationship to prior work
Tuffley, *Finite subset spaces of \(S^1\)*, arXiv:math/0209077 (first public version 2002-09-07), proves the ordinary finite-subset homotopy spheres, odd-to-even multiplication by two in ambient top homology, and degrees of induced circle maps; its full accessible text does not give the actual fixed-space homotopy types or the fixed-space persistence maps. Mostovoy's 1999 paper gives a lattice model for the special three-subset space but does not state a uniform fixed-space classification. Forman's 1998 cell-complex Morse theory is background for acyclic matchings; the present proof uses the matching for **integral chain cancellation** and separately proves simple connectivity, avoiding any assumption that the CW cells are all regular. Related studies of rotational fixed finite subsets of \(S^2\) concern a different domain and group action.

## Limitations
No claim is made about the complete equivariant homeomorphism type of the ambient spaces, arbitrary homeomorphisms of the circle, or fixed spaces of nonreflection subgroups. The matching has algebraic rather than necessarily regular CW collapse interpretations. The novelty search is a best-evidence literature comparison, not a guarantee against unpublished or unindexed equivalent formulations. The result is not independently audited.
