# Scientific review: layered chain-order boundary measure

## Correctness — PASS

The precise domain is every ordinal sum of positive antichain ranks and every rankwise chain/order interpolation. The 2023 Ahmad–Fourier–Joswig facet classification (Definition 2.2, Lemma 3.2) supplies a genuinely complete set of geometric facets. For each family, the volume of the relevant Euclidean hyperplane section is computed by a Dirichlet integral or a rank-contraction quotient. Gram factors are exactly \(1\), \(\sqrt2\), and \(\sqrt{k+1}\). Summing the rankwise budgets gives the displayed formula including singleton and endpoint cases; an exact concavity comparison gives the strict signs.

The independent finite-method cross-check enumerates feasible vertices, obtains their convex hulls and validates its Euclidean volume and surface area across 253 profile/interpolation combinations in ambient dimensions two through six, with 2204 listed inequalities independently checked as codimension-one faces. This does not substitute for the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

Ahmad–Fourier–Joswig (arXiv:2309.01626, initially public 2023-09-04) define exactly these rankwise interpolants and prove increasing face vectors, not a Euclidean surface-area interpolation formula. Fang–Fourier (arXiv:1508.02232) establish the interpolation and lattice geometry generally, without the checked area law. Freij-Hollanti–Lundström (arXiv:2312.13890) study face-vector monotonicity for broader ordinal sums. Existing separate surface-area formulas for the two endpoints are expressly not claimed as novel. Their statements do not determine the intermediate \(k\)-dependence or strict reversal across the full chain/order interpolation.

The closest checked metric literature concerns boundary-to-volume ratios for rooted-tree order polytopes, layered-order Steiner coefficients, layered-chain Steiner coefficients, or distinct width/inertia/Hausdorff quantities. No checked statement implies the all-intermediate surface profile for arbitrary positive rank sizes. Priority remains conditional on unindexed equivalent work and on the possibility of an unstated direct corollary of general marked-poset formulas.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural f-vector interpolation has equal volume at all steps but increasing face counts. Ordinary Euclidean surface measure has the opposite exact strict trend at every nontrivial interior step. The full profile collapses to just dimension, rank count and interpolation index, whereas the numbers and individual areas of facets depend on all rank sizes. The dimensional asymptotic yields an exact, parameter-independent maximal fractional area reduction approaching \(1-1/\sqrt2\). This is a structural metric counterpart to the studied combinatorial interpolation rather than a small numerical refinement.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to canonical Euclidean coordinates and maximal ranked posets. The first and last interpolation steps are equalities; for only one, two or three levels some or all intermediate cases reduce to previously studied endpoints. Independently established mathematical priority and independent audit are not claimed.
