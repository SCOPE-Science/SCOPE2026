# Scientific review — Rigidity of Schur-multiplier norming weights for projective-plane incidence masks

## Correctness — PASS
The exact incidence axioms give \(BB^*=B^*B=qI+J\). The factorization \(B=S^{1/2}S^{-1/2}B\), \(S=(BB^*)^{1/2}\), gives the upper bound for every pair of nonnegative probability weights. The Schatten-factorization equality condition forces \(S D_r S=B D_c B^*\). Each off-diagonal entry is the weight of a unique common line; three points on each line force row uniformity, and the same identity forces column uniformity. A polar unitary exists for singular matrices as well, so zero-weight boundaries are covered. Direct uniform substitution attains equality. Rank-one duality finishes the normer classification. The checker independently verifies incidence structure in the prime-field examples.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Levene (2026, arXiv:2611.08419v1, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.5) provides the general variational formula, concavity, and existence of symmetry-invariant maximizers; these do not imply **uniqueness**. The present extra equality equation and the unique-line axiom give uniqueness for every finite projective plane, independent of transitivity. Levene (2014) concerns norm gaps, a small-norm classification, and path mask formulas, not a stated projective-plane equality classification; only its publisher abstract and publication summary were inspected, so a full-text overlap is a remaining risk. Searches against available mathematical research records and web sources for projective-plane Schur multiplier optimal weights did not reveal an equivalent stronger statement; negative searches are not proof of priority. Earlier specialized Hadamard/Schur multiplier equality analyses might contain a different formulation.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A symmetric optimal weight may coexist with many non-symmetric optima, as demonstrated by all-ones bipartite masks. The theorem rules out this degeneracy for a natural infinite class of incidence geometries, completely classifies the row-column probability optimizers and rank-one trace-class witnesses, and distinguishes the norm-value problem from the harder equality problem. The argument uses the projective axiom of exactly one line through two points and works even without incidence-group transitivity. This exact structural characterization is potentially useful for stability and perturbation questions, though no quantitative stability theorem is claimed.

## Limitations and closest literature
Only rank-one trace-class normers are classified; not all operator-norm maximizing input matrices. The formula for the norm value follows earlier work and is contextual, not independently novel. Levene's 2026 paper was inspected in its accessible full arXiv HTML. The older 2014 Levene paper was checked only at its publisher abstract. A full independent literature review is still needed to establish publication priority.
