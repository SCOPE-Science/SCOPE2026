# Seven orbit-surface types for all graph symmetries of the two-particle \(K_{3,3}\) configuration surface

## Finding
Let \(B\) be the canonical **unordered discretized two-point configuration complex** of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{3,3}\): its cubical cells are unordered pairs of disjoint closed graph cells, each of which is a vertex or an edge. Let \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(K_{3,3})\cong(S_3\times S_3)\rtimes C_2\) act on \(|B|\), and let \(H\) range through **every subgroup** of \(G\). There are exactly 112 such subgroups, in 26 conjugacy classes. Their ordinary topological quotients \(|B|/H\) are compact connected surfaces, classified exhaustively by the following seven homeomorphism types:

| Surface | Number of subgroups | Number of subgroup conjugacy classes | Subgroup orders (number of subgroups) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Closed nonorientable genus five, \(N_5\) | 1 | 1 | 1 (1) |
| Closed nonorientable genus three, \(N_3\) | 2 | 1 | 3 (2) |
| Projective plane, \(N_1\) | 3 | 2 | 3 (2), 9 (1) |
| Once-punctured Klein bottle, \(N_{2,1}\) | 9 | 1 | 2 (9) |
| Möbius band, \(N_{1,1}\) | 36 | 5 | 2 (6), 4 (18), 6 (12) |
| Once-punctured torus, \(\Sigma_{1,1}\) | 6 | 1 | 2 (6) |
| Disk, \(\Sigma_{0,1}\) | 55 | 15 | 4 (9), 6 (16), 8 (9), 12 (12), 18 (5), 36 (3), 72 (1) |

Here \(N_{g,b}\) means the connected sum of \(g\) projective planes with \(b\) open disks removed, and \(\Sigma_{g,b}\) means an orientable genus-\(g\) surface with \(b\) boundary circles; \(N_g=N_{g,0}\). In particular the full 72-fold graph-symmetry quotient is a disk; the free diagonal three-cycle quotient is \(N_3\), whereas a three-cycle on only one bipartition class has projective-plane quotient. These distinguish genuinely different natural actions of cyclic groups of the same order.

## Assumptions and scope
Write the six vertices as \(\{0,1,2\}\sqcup\{3,4,5\}\), with all nine cross-edges and no within-class edges. The cubical complex \(B\) has 15 vertices (unordered pairs of distinct graph vertices), 36 edges (an edge together with a nonincident vertex), and 18 squares (an unordered pair of disjoint graph edges). Each graph automorphism permutes these cells. All quotients in the theorem are **geometric quotients of this specified finite cubical complex**; neither labeled configurations nor quotient spaces of the continuous configuration space are asserted to be identical by an equivariant deformation retraction.

## Proof
1. **Subgroup completeness.** An automorphism of \(K_{3,3}\) independently permutes the two three-element classes and may interchange them. Thus five explicit permutations (a rotation and transposition in each class, and the class swap) generate precisely all 72 group elements. Starting from the trivial subgroup, adjoining each element outside a known subgroup and taking multiplication closure enumerates every subgroup: any subgroup has a finite generating list, and each successive generator prefix occurs in this breadth-first closure. Exact enumeration gives 112 distinct subgroups, with group-order counts \(1:1, 2:21, 3:4, 4:27, 6:28, 8:9, 9:1, 12:12, 18:5, 36:3, 72:1\). Conjugation inside the 72-element group yields 26 classes.
2. **Faithful quotient triangulations.** Barycentrically subdivide the cubical face poset. Its nonempty-cell barycenters are 69 vertices; incidences of comparable distinct cells give 216 edges; strictly increasing chains of length three give 144 triangles. Since a group element preserves each cell dimension, no element can permute the vertices of a simplex nontrivially while fixing that simplex. Taking the exact orbits of these chains therefore produces a valid Δ-complex cellulation of the ordinary geometric quotient (retaining distinct orbit simplices even when they have the same quotient vertices).
3. **Surface recognition, not merely homology.** For every subgroup, the certificate reconstructs all quotient triangles, their three boundary edges and the vertex links. Every quotient edge belongs to precisely one or two triangles. At every quotient vertex the entire link is connected and is respectively an interval or a circle: it has two degree-one link vertices and all others degree two, or every link vertex has degree two. Consequently every quotient is a compact surface with possible boundary. The connectedness follows already from that of \(B\), which can also be verified from the face incidence graph.
4. **Homeomorphism invariants.** The certificate computes Euler characteristic from exact numbers of orbit cells, boundary circles from the graph of edges incident to one triangle, and orientability by solving the triangle-sign compatibility equations across each interior edge. These tests classify connected compact surfaces uniquely. The resulting seven types and their subgroup/conjugacy counts are precisely the table above. In particular the diagonal three-cycle has orbit subdivision \((23,72,48)\), Euler characteristic \(-1\), no boundary and is nonorientable, so its quotient is \(N_3\); the full group has \((4,5,2)\), Euler characteristic \(1\), one boundary circle and is orientable, so its quotient is a disk.

The exhaustive loops above are finite and the complete source `verify.py` is included. Every possible graph automorphism, subgroup, original face, face-chain orbit, quotient vertex link, edge incidence, boundary component and orientation constraint is considered; no statistical sampling or extrapolation is involved. The separate `orbit_census.json` records each subgroup by a reproducible group-element list and its own quotient counts; regenerate it with `python3 create_census.py`.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` (Python standard library only). It enumerates 72 automorphisms, all 112 subgroups, all 26 conjugacy classes, verifies every subgroup quotient's local surface links and edge incidences, computes the exact Euler/boundary/orientability signature, and asserts the seven-type census. The included `verification_output.txt` is an example of its exact categorical output. The final marker is `K33_TWO_CONFIGURATION_ALL_SUBGROUP_SURFACES_PASS`. The independently readable `orbit_census.json` gives the finer 112-row certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Crisp and Wiest (2004, Figure 1 and the adjacent discussion) establish that the underlying unordered two-point configuration cubical complex of \(K_{3,3}\) is the closed nonorientable surface of Euler characteristic \(-3\). That nonequivariant surface type \(N_5\) is **prior knowledge**. Barnett and Farber (2009, Section 1) supply the deleted-product cell framework and describe the ordered two-point \(K_{3,3}\) configuration space, which is a different cover. Prue and Scrimshaw (2009) discuss conditions relating the discretized and continuous graph-configuration spaces. The present result determines the complete 112-subgroup orbit **homeomorphism census** of the unordered discretized space; it does not re-claim the known unquotiented surface. A related finite orbit census for the three-by-four chessboard torus concerns another space and group action and does not imply this classification.

## Limitations
The classification records underlying topological surface types, not orbifold cone points, mirror-isotropy orders, induced maps on surface fundamental groups, equivariant homeomorphism types, or mapping-class conjugacy of subgroup actions. The subject is the discretized two-particle complex, not a claimed equivariant equivalence to the continuous configuration-space quotients. The proofs are an exact exhaustive finite calculation plus standard surface classification; an independent mathematical audit and a definitive exhaustive publication-priority search have not been performed. Published records and search indexes can omit equivalent work.
