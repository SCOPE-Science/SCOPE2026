# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The polynomial in the finding is expanded coefficient by coefficient, and the exact factorization follows from the sole quadratic equation for the positive root \(L\). Integrating proves the upper deficit; applying the transform twice proves the lower deficit. Both constants are attained by explicit unit functions on two disjoint supports, and the equality conditions follow from a nonnegative integrand. The pointwise distance constant follows from diagonalizing a traceless symmetric quadratic form. The packaged checker verifies symbolic identities and finite instances but is not substituted for a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Xu and Liu's general \(L^p\) operator-norm reduction already implies the constants through an unresolved maximization, and this is fully credited. Its accessible complete text does not give the explicit sixth-power radical, the two exact nonnegative remainder identities, or an all-extremizers classification. Rao et al. introduce the skew James invariant but lack this sextic identity. Closest indexed mathematical findings address fourth-power deficits and the high-\(p\) limit; they do not cover the finite sextic theorem. A previously unindexed sextic Clarkson refinement remains a real bibliographic risk. Novelty is asserted only for the evaluated sextic identities and rigidity, not for definitions or the established reduction.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The sextic case is the first even power beyond four where the full skew-transform expansion produces a sixth-degree defect and a nontrivial quadratic spectral optimization. It yields an exact previously unevaluated natural geometric constant, but more importantly an exact global equality and stability mechanism and a dual lower-endpoint certificate. The result separates the numerical evaluation from the more informative geometric norming-pair classification; it is not just a numerical specialization or a generic inequality restatement.

## Limitations and closest literature
Only real \(L^6\) spaces of dimension at least two; no complex extension, no all-exponent solution, and no claimed globally optimal unit-sphere distance coefficient. Main references: Xu–Liu arXiv:2610.03050v1, Rao et al. arXiv:2504.00827v1, and related published fourth-power and large-exponent mathematical findings.
