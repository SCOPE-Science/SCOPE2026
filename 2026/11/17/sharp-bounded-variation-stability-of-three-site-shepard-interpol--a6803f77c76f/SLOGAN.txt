For three arbitrary real interpolation sites, normalized inverse-distance Shepard interpolation has an explicit sharp total-variation amplification depending only on the power and adjacent mesh ratio: equal spacing uniquely minimizes it, while colliding neighbors drive it toward two. When the inverse-distance power grows and the smaller-to-larger gap ratio is \(\tau/p\), the exact amplification tends to \(1+2/(1+e^{2\tau})\), exposing a sharp mesh–power stability boundary layer even for a positive, range-preserving interpolant.
