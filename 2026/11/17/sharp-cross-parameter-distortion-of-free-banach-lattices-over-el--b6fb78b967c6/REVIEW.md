# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite-dimensional conclusion is reconstructed from the standard weak-summing free norm on \(\ell_1^n\). For each \(p\)-admissible dual array, its row maxima have \(p\)-sum at most \(n\). Rescaling rows by \(r_k^{p/q-1}\) produces a \(q\)-admissible array; Hölder gives \(\|f\|_p\le n^{1/p-1/q}\|f\|_q\). The maximum of coordinate evaluation moduli has exactly \(r\)-free norm \(n^{1/r}\), giving equality. The \(q=\infty\), zero-row and \(n=1\) cases are explicit. The resulting bounded inverse extends by completion. For infinite \(\ell_1(I)\), finitely supported versions show failure of a lower bound, and the cited canonical-map criterion implies non-order-continuity. No experimental step is needed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Pariente-Martín–Tradacete (arXiv:2611.00451v1), Section 5, proves qualitative equivalences and a coarse finite-dimensional norm estimate, not this sharp exponent/dimension constant. Jardón-Sánchez et al. (2021/2022), Sections 6–7, develop the norm formula and nonlinear summing inclusion, not the all-parameter inverse constant. Oikhberg et al. (2022 preprint/2026 journal), Section 9.6, establishes general automatic-convexity and isomorphism phenomena, not the claimed exact constant. Published research on fixed-point sublattices already identifies the coordinate-maximum norm, providing a lower bound; the unrestricted upper estimate is a separate statement. Targeted source/alias/parameter searches identified no dominating result. Equivalent older cross-norm interpolation estimates could exist, so publication priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The calculation supplies an exact, attained, dimension-sensitive norm invariant for the canonical morphism directly studied in recent literature, across all \(n,p,q\). It also quantifies failure of equivalence as \(n\to\infty\) and resolves the infinite \(\ell_1\) case for all sub-Hilbert cross-parameters, where the motivating paper exhibits the contrasting Hilbert behavior. The result is a complete classification for a canonical free-lattice family, not a general theory for other generators. Its proof is elementary but the sharp quantitative target and the low-parameter obstruction are intrinsically motivated.

## Limitations and previous literature
The proof does not establish an optimal comparison for other finite-dimensional Banach spaces. The cross-parameter order-continuity equivalence is cited, not new. The most relevant public fixed-point-lattice result was available by summary but not complete text; an undiscovered equivalent norm inequality remains a possibility.
