For bounded-slope isotonic least squares with any positive and spatially varying slope caps, the sharp two-input discrete-Sobolev amplification is \(1,\sqrt5/2,\sqrt{14}/3\) at lengths \(2,3,4\) and exactly \(\sqrt{(n-1)/2}\) at every length \(n\ge5\). Consecutive-block averaging explains the cap-independent law: small \(\ell_2\) data perturbations can produce two concentrated slope jumps even though the regression is Euclidean-nonexpansive.
