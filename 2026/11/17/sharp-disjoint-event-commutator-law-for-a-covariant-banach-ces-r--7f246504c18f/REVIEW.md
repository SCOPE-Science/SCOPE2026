# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof is self-contained after the two-band operator density from Dzhenzher's Example 2.5. The commutator is an exact scalar multiple of the difference of two orthogonal projections. For any event, the first Fourier moment is bounded by its circular arc value, including for ultrafilter limits. The area of three zero-sum complex vectors is the square root of a two-by-two Gram determinant. A positive supporting-plane certificate for the concave square-root determinant establishes the acute three-density branch; the large-density branch follows from two individual arc bounds. Explicit periodic partitions attain both branches. The equal-third global maximum follows from strict concavity of the logarithm of sine. All boundary cases and factors of two are treated explicitly. The supplementary finite checker passes 12,316 checks, but the proof, not the experiments, covers the infinite and nonperiodic cases.

## Originality — PASS (bibliographically qualified)

Dzhenzher's arXiv:2610.33821v1 supplies the covariant two-band measure and a nonzero commutator for overlapping events. It does not calculate the maximal commutator of disjoint events at prescribed densities. Pellonpää's arXiv:quant-ph/0108105v2 develops the Fourier-matrix representation and structural classification of covariant phase observables, not the specific three-color sharp area theorem. Related published findings about finite-frame commutators, circular Fourier concentration and Fourier sampling use different constraints; no source examined was shown to imply the result. An older equivalent circular moment extremal theorem or phase-observable application remains a real bibliographic risk, not ruled out by search failures.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Disjoint sets have commuting projection-valued-measure effects but need not have commuting positive operator-valued-measure effects. The result makes that obstruction quantitative across every possible density allocation in an explicit noncommutative covariant model. The transition at maximum density \(1/2\) changes the geometry of extremizing event partitions from three consecutive arcs to two orthogonal smaller arcs with a possibly disconnected complement; the unrestricted sharp maximum and unique equal-third density identify the worst disjoint-event incompatibility in the model. This is a complete natural optimization invariant, not an isolated numerical example.

## Closest literature and limitations

The source model comes from Dzhenzher, Example 2.5, arXiv:2610.33821v1. Pellonpää, arXiv:quant-ph/0108105v2, develops covariant phase effects and their Fourier coefficients but does not present the disjoint-event optimization in its inspected structural results. The theorem is restricted to a two-band measure; periodic rigidity is not asserted for arbitrary nonperiodic ultrafilter events. A later independent audit and broader bibliography check would remain valuable.
