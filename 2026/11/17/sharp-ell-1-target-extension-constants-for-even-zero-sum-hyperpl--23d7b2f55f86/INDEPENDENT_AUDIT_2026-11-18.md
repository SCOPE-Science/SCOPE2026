## correctness

PASS

The unit ball of the real ℓ1 zero-sum hyperplane is the convex hull of ±(e_i−e_j)/2; hence any R represented by n target vectors v_i has norm diam(v_i)/2. Any extension from ℓ1^n has columns v_i−c for a common center c and norm max_i||v_i−c||_1. Thus the universal loss is exactly 2r/D. The arithmetic mean gives r≤(n−1)D/n for every set of n points. For even n=2k, take target coordinates indexed by all C(n,k) balanced subsets and vectors ±1 depending on membership; each target coordinate has k plus and k minus entries, forcing max_i ||v_i−c||_1≥m at every center. The zero center attains r=m, while each pair has D=4 C(n−2,k−1)=nm/(n−1). Therefore the claimed sharp value holds simultaneously for all even n, including the n=2 factor 1, without finite extrapolation. Independent integer subset checks for n=2,4,6,8,10 verified pairwise distances, zero sums and exact ratios.

## originality

PASS

The POE-p definition and structural extension requirement are known (Pariente-Martín–Tradacete 2026, Theorem 1.3 and Section 4); the new claimed object is a sharp quantitative factor for this particular canonical hyperplane inclusion. Full text of Castillo–Papini (2024) treats the *unrestricted-cardinality* finite Jung constant, with no explicit n-point cardinality bound for arbitrary ℓ1 targets in the inspected discussion. Dolnikov (1987) gives the *dimension-m* Jung constant of ℓ1^m and a Hadamard equality criterion, not a universal target-dimension-independent fixed number-of-points n constant. These aliases and their different quantifiers were compared. Residual priority risk from older n-point radius/extension results remains.
