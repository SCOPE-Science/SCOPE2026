# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The signed boundary-incidence Gram identity \(B^{\mathsf T}B=3I-A_T\) follows from consistently oriented triangles and the dual tree. Diagonal dominance with strictness at leaves guarantees positive definiteness; its nonnegative adjacency resolvent is entrywise strictly positive. Via the complete facet-normal parametrization of the linear-image crosspolytope, its largest sign quadratic is attained exactly by the two constant sign vectors. Opposite supporting planes bound the radius for *every* center; strict inequalities at remaining facets give a relative-open optimal-center set of affine dimension \(n-1\). The strict global extremal comparison uses the published all-walk theorem on trees and a direct degree-square equality argument. The path formula comes from an explicitly solved tridiagonal difference equation. The included checker independently reconstructs the boundary matrix and checks 196 triangulations and 10,064 facets. Finite checks are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

The motivating manuscript [1] introduces the boundary-column polytopes and develops combinatorial faces, lattices and crosspolytope characterizations, but its inspected Sections 1–9 do not state a canonical Euclidean inradius formula or extremum over polygon triangulations. The walk extremal ingredient [2] is explicitly attributed rather than presented as new. Closest research-record comparisons concern path symmetric-edge polytopes (Gram diagonal \(2\) instead of \(3\)) and covariance/metric reconstruction for *closed* pseudomanifolds. None found implies the full intrinsic Euclidean inradius, the maximum precisely at dual paths, or the complete optimal-center dimension. A previous equivalent metric result under other terminology remains a residual risk; lack of search hits is not a proof of absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The paper's higher-dimensional homology-polytope construction naturally raises the issue of what geometry survives when all examples are combinatorially crosspolytopal. Here all polygon-triangulation examples share radius \(\sqrt3\) and face lattice, yet their inradii differ. A nontrivial transfer from all-walk tree extremality gives the exact **maximum over every triangulation** and its strict equality cases, with an explicit dimension-uniform value; the two-facet contact structure and \((n-1)\)-dimensional optimal-center locus add geometric information that does not follow from combinatorial equivalence. The four-triangle path–branching example demonstrates genuine separation.
