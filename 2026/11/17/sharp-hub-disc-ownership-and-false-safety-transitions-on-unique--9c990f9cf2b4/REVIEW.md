# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For any connected unweighted graph with a unique maximum-degree vertex of degree at least two, the similarity \(D^qJ_qD^{-q}=A-I\) is exact. Perron--Frobenius and the Rayleigh vector supported on a maximum-degree star give \(1<\sqrt\Delta\le\varrho<\Delta\). The hub radius is a strictly decreasing continuous sum of powers of ratios strictly between zero and one, yielding both unique thresholds and their strict ordering. The distance from \(\varrho-1\) to the disc center \(-1\) equals \(\varrho\), proving the exact ownership threshold, and the disc right edge gives the false-safe threshold. Star eigenvalues and the two equality boundaries are reconstructed directly. The embedded finite algebraic checker corroborates, but does not replace, these arguments. The theorem only concerns a specified exact linear flow.

## Originality — PASS, with stated priority limits

Patil--Meena (arXiv:2611.08692v1, full HTML Sections 3--6) already acknowledge empirical hub-disc misclassifications, but give neither the universal two-transition theorem on arbitrary uniquely maximum-degree graphs nor its sharp star thresholds and unbounded eigenvalue-versus-disc discrepancy along growing stars. Meena et al. (arXiv:2007.04890, supplementary Section 2.1) derive the star eigenvalue, and Dawn et al. (arXiv:2512.09765, Sections I--II) give diagonal symmetrization; both are explicitly treated as existing ingredients, not novelty. Targeted published mathematical-record searches for this source, the degree-conjugacy formulation, and the hub-disc false-safety statement returned no direct implication coverage. General diagonal-scaling/Gershgorin literature could contain the principle in another notation; full historical priority and independent validation are outstanding.

## Value — PASS

The recent network-dynamics classifier depends on a cheap hub-only stability proxy. This theorem supplies a necessary-and-sufficient pair of parameter boundaries and a graph-uniform mechanism by which its apparent margin of safety can be made arbitrarily large without any change in the actual eigenvalues. The exact star cutoffs exhibit complete transfer of spectral ownership from hub to leaves and unbounded discrepancy with network size. This is a motivated structural limit of the advertised approximation, rather than an isolated numerical misclassification. It does not invalidate the full-row theorem or establish performance frequencies on random graphs.
