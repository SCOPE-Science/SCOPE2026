# Sharp independent-block convexity and planar-shadow extremizers for cubic constant-width bodies

## Finding
Let \(m\ge2\), \(n\ge3m\), and let \(e_{j,a}\in\mathbb R^n\), \(1\le j\le m\), \(1\le a\le3\), be a mutually orthonormal family. For \(\rho>0\) and real parameters \(\beta_j\), define the smooth function on \(S^{n-1}\)
\[
h(u)=\rho+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_j\prod_{a=1}^{3}\langle u,e_{j,a}\rangle,\qquad B=\max_j|\beta_j|.
\]
**Sharp convexity domain.** The function \(h\) is the support function of a full-dimensional compact convex body \(K\) if and only if
\[
\boxed{B\le\frac{3\sqrt6}{8}\rho.}
\]
If the inequality is strict, its spherical curvature-radius operator \(\nabla^2_Sh+hI\) is positive definite at every normal. If equality holds with \(B>0\), it is positive semidefinite and singular at exactly \(12k\) normals, where \(k=\#\{j:|\beta_j|=B\}\). These are exactly the unit vectors supported on one maximizing block \(j\), with absolute block coordinates a permutation of \((2,1,1)/\sqrt6\) and coordinate-sign product equal to \(\operatorname{sgn}(\beta_j)\). At each, the nullspace in the tangent plane is one-dimensional. No other singular normals occur.

**Complete planar-shadow law.** For any two-dimensional plane \(P\), choose an orthonormal basis \(a,b\) and set
\[
z_{j,r}=\langle a,e_{j,r}\rangle-i\langle b,e_{j,r}\rangle,\qquad Z_P=\sum_j\beta_j z_{j,1}z_{j,2}z_{j,3}.
\]
The orthogonal projection of \(K\) onto \(P\) has threefold rotational symmetry after a translation, and has exact area
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{area}(K\mid P)=\pi\rho^2-\frac\pi4|Z_P|^2.}
\]
For \(B>0\), its entire sharp range over all planes is
\[
\boxed{\pi\left(\rho^2-\frac{2B^2}{27}\right)\le\operatorname{area}(K\mid P)\le\pi\rho^2.}
\]
The lower bound is attained *only* on planes lying in one maximizing three-dimensional block and whose unit normal within that block has coordinate absolute values \((1,1,1)/\sqrt3\); there are exactly \(4k\) such unoriented planes. The upper bound is attained, for example, by a plane generated by one axis from each of two different blocks. If \(B=0\), every projected area is \(\pi\rho^2\) instead. At the sharp nonzero convexity endpoint, the least shadow area is \(15\pi\rho^2/16\), independently of \(m\) and \(n\).

Every admissible \(K\) has constant Euclidean width \(2\rho\). In particular, each extra orthogonal cubic mode adds a genuine independent parameter without shrinking the sharp individual coefficient range. For instance, in ambient dimension six with two nonzero critical blocks, the endpoint has exactly 24 curvature-singular normals and eight least-bright projection planes.

## Assumptions and scope
All Euclidean distances and projection areas use the standard inner product on \(\mathbb R^n\). The triples are mutually orthogonal, not merely linearly independent. Every coefficient \(\beta_j\) may have arbitrary sign; unused ambient coordinates are allowed. The curvature-radius operator acts on \(u^\perp\) at each \(u\in S^{n-1}\). For \(B=0\), \(K\) is the ball of radius \(\rho\); all statements about a finite set of extremizing shadow planes are restricted to \(B>0\). The equality case in the convexity threshold is a support function but is not of positive curvature everywhere.

## Proof
**1. Exact great-circle decomposition.** For arbitrary orthonormal \(u,v\), put \(\gamma(\theta)=u\cos\theta+v\sin\theta\). Every cubic monomial in \(h(\gamma(\theta))\) has only frequencies one and three. Define \(w_{j,r}=\langle u,e_{j,r}\rangle-i\langle v,e_{j,r}\rangle\). Expanding each coordinate as \((w_{j,r}e^{i\theta}+\overline{w_{j,r}}e^{-i\theta})/2\) shows that the coefficient of \(e^{3i\theta}\) is exactly \(\beta_j\prod_r w_{j,r}/8\). Thus the combined third harmonic can be written as
\[
\frac14\operatorname{Re}\left(e^{3i\theta}\sum_j\beta_j\prod_r w_{j,r}\right).
\]
The first harmonic has no effect on \(p+p''\), where \(p(\theta)=h(\gamma(\theta))\). The spherical curvature form consequently obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle(\nabla^2_Sh(u)+h(u)I)v,v\rangle
&=p''(0)+p(0)\\
&=\rho-2\operatorname{Re}\sum_j\beta_j\prod_r w_{j,r}.
\end{aligned}
\]

**2. Orthogonal energy allocation.** Write \(s_j=\sum_{r=1}^3|w_{j,r}|^2\). Because the blocks are orthogonal and \(u,v\) are orthonormal, \(s_j\ge0\) and \(\sum_j s_j\le2\). The arithmetic--geometric mean inequality and strict convexity of the power \(3/2\) give
\[
\sum_j\prod_r|w_{j,r}|
\le3^{-3/2}\sum_j s_j^{3/2}
\le3^{-3/2}\left(\sum_j s_j\right)^{3/2}
\le\left(\frac23\right)^{3/2}.
\]
Hence every unit tangent curvature satisfies the sharp uniform lower bound
\[
\langle(\nabla^2_Sh+hI)v,v\rangle
\ge\rho-2B\left(\frac23\right)^{3/2}
=\rho-\frac{4\sqrt6}{9}B.
\]
A twice differentiable spherical function is a support function if and only if this curvature form is nonnegative everywhere (equivalently, its positively homogeneous extension is convex). This proves sufficiency for \(B\le3\sqrt6\,\rho/8\), and positive definiteness for strict inequality. Its convex body is full-dimensional: for any unit \(u\), with \(t_j=\sum_r\langle u,e_{j,r}\rangle^2\),
\[
\left|\sum_j\beta_j\prod_r\langle u,e_{j,r}\rangle\right|
\le\frac{B}{3\sqrt3}\sum_jt_j^{3/2}
\le\frac B{3\sqrt3}<\rho,
\]
so its support function is bounded below by a strictly positive constant.

**3. Sharpness and endpoint degeneracy.** Choose a block \(j\) with \(|\beta_j|=B\). Take \(u\) supported there with coordinates \((2,1,1)/\sqrt6\), choosing signs whose product agrees with \(\operatorname{sgn}(\beta_j)\), and take the corresponding tangent vector \(v\) supported on the two smaller coordinate axes, with their signed difference \((0,1,-1)/\sqrt2\). Substitution in the exact curvature formula yields \(\rho-(4\sqrt6/9)B\). Therefore any violation of the claimed bound produces a negative curvature direction, proving necessity.

At the endpoint, equality in the energy-allocation bound forces \(\sum_j s_j=2\), all the energy in a single maximizing block, and \(|w_{j,1}|^2=|w_{j,2}|^2=|w_{j,3}|^2=2/3\). This means that the plane \(\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}\) lies in one block and its unit normal there has three coordinate magnitudes \(1/\sqrt3\). On each of the four such unoriented planes, the restriction of the block cubic has a nonzero pure third harmonic after discarding first harmonics; the curvature reaches its negative extremum at exactly three points of the unit circle. Equivalently, these points are exactly the three signed-permutation normals described in the finding, giving \(4\cdot3=12\) singular normals per maximizing block. Strictness of the energy-allocation inequalities excludes all other normals, and the third harmonic has a unique null tangent line at each listed normal. Distinct blocks yield disjoint sets of singular normals.

**4. Projected area and rigidity.** For a plane with oriented orthonormal basis \(a,b\), its projected support function is the restriction \(h(a\cos\theta+b\sin\theta)\). Removing its first Fourier harmonic by a translation leaves the planar support function
\[
p(\theta)=\rho+\frac14\operatorname{Re}(Z_Pe^{3i\theta}).
\]
This has threefold rotational symmetry. The planar area identity \(\operatorname{area}=\tfrac12\int_0^{2\pi}(p^2-(p')^2)\,d\theta\) gives \(\pi\rho^2-\pi|Z_P|^2/4\). The same energy-allocation inequality yields \(|Z_P|\le B(2/3)^{3/2}\); if \(B>0\), equality occurs exactly in one maximizing block with all three squared projections equal to \(2/3\). Such a plane is perpendicular to a signed body-diagonal direction in that block, yielding exactly four unoriented planes per maximizing block. A plane spanned by coordinate axes in different blocks has \(Z_P=0\), and thus realizes the maximum area. Finally \(h(u)+h(-u)=2\rho\), proving constant width; substituting \(B=3\sqrt6\,\rho/8\) into the minimum-area expression gives \(15\pi\rho^2/16\).

## Verification
The self-contained accompanying `verify_blocks.py` checks exact symbolic tangent-curvature identities and the listed endpoint-nullspace witnesses in multiple blocks. Deterministic finite tests include ambient dimensions strictly larger than the number of used coordinates, signed coefficient choices, nonadmissible coefficients, and independent Fourier-quadrature area comparisons. The program completed successfully with **29,819 checks**. This is corroboration only: the proof above establishes all dimensions, coefficients, planes, and sharp equality cases without finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Myroshnychenko, Ryabogin, and Saroglou establish the exceptional threefold-symmetric projection phenomenon using a single cubic monomial, with convexity ensured for sufficiently small coefficients. Their full September 2026 manuscript gives the great-circle Fourier mechanism. A separate October 2026 public mathematical note derives the **sharp single-monomial** convexity threshold \(3\sqrt6/8\) and its twelve singular normals. Another public note computes the single-monomial brightness profile. These single-block cases are prior work, not separate original claims here.

The new theorem proves that *arbitrarily many mutually orthogonal cubic blocks do not interact in the sharp convexity constraint*, classifies every endpoint degeneracy for independently signed amplitudes, and identifies every least-bright projection plane of the combined high-dimensional body. The strongest general degree-three harmonic curvature bound located in the comparisons is dimension-independent but does not give the sharp blockwise bound. The multi-block assertion is not obtained by simply applying the single-block bound to each summand: a triangle inequality would instead impose a restrictive sum-of-amplitudes condition.

## Limitations
The exact independent-coefficient box relies essentially on mutually orthogonal coordinate triples; neither overlapping triples nor arbitrary linear combinations of cubic spherical harmonics are classified. A nonidentical positive constant part or different Euclidean metric also lies outside the theorem. Historical priority is provisional: an unindexed general norm result for orthogonally decomposable tensors might imply the energy-allocation lemma, although no covering sharp convexity-plus-shadow statement was identified in inspected sources. The finite checks cannot prove uniqueness or universality by themselves.
