# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The inverse problem is reduced exactly to a one-dimensional logit level-set equation with derivative \(1/[x(1-x)]-a\chi/(1-k)\), where \(\chi=1-k(1+cp_1)\). The proof checks strict monotonicity including the equality boundary, the two nondegenerate fold lines when the derivative changes sign, the three-inverse upper bound, and physical three-inverse witnesses sufficiently near onset. The instantaneous-belief case \(k=1\) is handled separately and cannot be inferred by dividing by \(1-k\). The cubic inverse regularity follows from an explicit third-order expansion. The included checker validates essential algebra and a high-precision witness; the quantified universal statements rely on the analytical proof.

## Originality — PASS (within inspected evidence)
The full text of Metaxas and Sapsis (2026), including its bifurcation discussion and appendix, presents the map, equilibria, time averages, and numerical attractor families, but does not present or imply the sharp global injectivity phase boundary or fold multiplicities. Chotibut et al. (2018) analyze a different multiplicative-weights map and an invariant scalar diagonal, not the adaptive-belief two-coordinate inversion problem. Adomaitis and Kevrekidis (1991) establish the general link between noninvertibility and basin structure in a distinct adaptive-control map. The new result is therefore not a rewording of known fold concepts; it supplies model-specific necessary-and-sufficient parameter boundaries and the cubic endpoint. Historical priority remains provisional because not every adjacent publication was available in full.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For the paper's model, the geometry of a complicated two-dimensional attractor landscape is known mainly from numerical bifurcation plots. Knowing exactly when every state has at most one predecessor, versus when physical fold curves and locally multiple predecessors become possible, is a natural topological classification that informs basin-boundary and inverse-iteration analyses. The sharply degenerate cubic boundary and three-predecessor onset provide more than a routine fixed-point stability calculation. For the original source's \(k=0.865\), \(cp_1=0.5\), the theorem places the numerically complex attractor diagrams on a globally invertible branch. The theorem does not assert chaos or a particular basin topology.
