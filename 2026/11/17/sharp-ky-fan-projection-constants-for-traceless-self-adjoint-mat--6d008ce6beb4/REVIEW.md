# Scientific review — sharp Ky Fan projection constants

**Correctness: PASS.** Diagonalization reduces the real self-adjoint problem to mean-centering in a real symmetric gauge space. The dual Ky Fan ball is an integer-cardinality cube/crosspolytope intersection with exactly sign-support vertices; its two norm branches and all cases of the mixed-sign quadratic are checked analytically. Explicit spectral spike and two-level diagonal witnesses attain the sharp upper bound, and compact-group averaging proves global minimality over all real-linear projections. Exact rational checks independently enumerate the dual vertices through order ten and verify the boundary classification through order fifteen. These checks do not claim to certify all dimensions.

**Originality: PASS, with bibliographic risk.** Foucart's Section 4.2 (2016) proves minimality under unitarily invariant norms and gives a rank-independent Ky Fan bound, but not the exact rank-dependent constant on self-adjoint matrices. The two-branch formula and \(n=3k-2\) switch do not follow numerically from that bound, and the bound is strictly nonsharp for intermediate ranks. published-finding corpus queries for trace-zero Ky Fan projection constants, top-\(k\) mean centering, and symmetric matrix projections found no identical formula. Nearest unrelated records concern quadratic Lorentz spaces and Schatten-Schur projections. Residual risk: an older symmetric-gauge Banach-space reference could contain the equivalent finite vector calculation; the full non-self-adjoint matrix problem is not solved here.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** This closes the explicit intermediate-rank sharpness gap for a canonical family of traceless self-adjoint matrix spaces, including optimality among all projections, exact extremizers, an arithmetically sharp crossover, a complete nonexpansivity classification, and a dimension-uniform optimal-rank limit. The choice of the Ky Fan rank is intrinsic to spectral truncation rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. Although the proof is elementary once the right dual polytope is used, the result answers a natural unquantified sharpness question left by the published uniform bound.

Scientific limits: the claim covers only self-adjoint matrices, not general square matrices or nonlinear matrix maps. The literature comparison establishes no historical priority guarantee.
