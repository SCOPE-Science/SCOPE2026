# Scientific review: latitude-design projection-body radius theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The normalized cubic design is checked by the vanishing first three nontrivial Fourier sums and the parity of the two heights; the fourth vertical moment excludes degree four. Minkowski existence and Cauchy's brightness formula are standard, with the surface-area normalization made explicit. The outer-radius bound uses the exact second moment and analyzes its equality cases in all dimensions of the stated three-dimensional family. A uniform Lipschitz quadrature bound transfers the limit of support functions to unrestricted inradii with an explicit numerical rate. The limiting one-variable minimization has a unique strict positive root of a concave transcendental equation, which also proves concentration of minimizing latitudes. A separate numerical implementation checked 1,948 finite assertions. The analytic proof, not the finite tests, establishes the all-order and limit claims.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
The motivating August 2026 primary article establishes equal-area Minkowski realizations, general brightness identities, and rates when design strength grows; it does not compute the sharp limit for the fixed-strength two-ring family. The September 2026 companion article studies polar vertices, slack matrices, and self-polarity, not this brightness extremum. The prior exact same-family finding 7cc0bd2c-fbd6-4315-9d63-3a19cd2582f2 was read for its mathematical claim: it already computes the original bipyramid metric and volume, but not the projection-body brightness extremum, its root-defined oblique minimizing cone, or the finite maximizing directions. The object is deliberately the same, but the optimized functional, limiting body, contact sets and nonlinear proof are new, and no old radius or volume conclusion is repackaged. A full published result on spherical covering by two regular latitude rings was read; its worst-empty-cap objective is not an absolute-projection average and cannot imply the present inradius law. Searches of published records and external literature for the two-ring cosine transform, sharp projected-area minima, the implicit root \(u=\sin(2u)\), and equivalent formulations found no covering theorem. An older differently worded calculation of the same latitude-mixture zonoid remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Spherical designs are used as normal data for convex bodies; the recent quantitative theory bounds non-sphericity as design strength increases. Here design strength is fixed at three while facet count diverges. The previously unresolved **projection-body** limiting least-bright radius, non-axial optimizing cone, and exact outer-contact transition provide a sharp quantitative obstruction to treating increasing cardinality as a substitute for increasing strength. The explicit transcendental minimizer is a natural radius invariant of a canonical two-latitude spherical measure, not an arbitrary parameter slice. This is a complete asymptotic answer for the chosen family, with an all-order error bound, not a claim of a general convergence theorem for arbitrary designs.
