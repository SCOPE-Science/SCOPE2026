# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The operator on the real Lipschitz quotient is conjugate, through cyclic differences, to the same Fourier idempotent acting on the zero-sum \(\ell^\infty\) hyperplane. A standard finite-dimensional duality identity converts its row norm to the sum of absolute deviations from a median. The proof evaluates these sums separately in the odd, divisible-by-four and two-modulo-four cases and treats the Nyquist rank-one exception. Exact elementary inequalities show that only effective period six attains \(4/3\), and the strict sign constraints force every maximizing six-period edge-increment pattern. The three expansions follow from convergent Taylor series. The independent finite cube-vertex checker and rational six-cycle identity corroborate the stated normalization, but universality depends on the derivation.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Relevant earlier work computes finite cyclic idempotent concentration and ordinary endpoint Fourier projection norms; the closest complete published results concern three or more consecutive frequencies and two-frequency *point-concentration* ratios, not the operator norm on the zero-sum increment hyperplane. The known absolute-cosine sums are reused and expressly credited. The current statement goes beyond those records by determining the real Lipschitz norm for every harmonic, treating the odd median correction, identifying the unique universal six-period maximizer, classifying its norming gradients and giving all three large-period phases. A previous equivalent formula hidden in classical finite-group multiplier or approximation theory remains a historical priority risk; a negative search cannot establish uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
Single-harmonic graph Fourier projections are natural bounded linear operators on finite cycle Lipschitz spaces. The dimension-independent sharp uniform bound \(4/3\), unique six-period equality, explicit norming gradients and three asymptotic discretization laws provide a complete answer to a motivated operator-norm problem, rather than a single small instance or a routine restatement of an ordinary Lebesgue constant. They quantify precisely how a spectral projection can amplify edge oscillations despite being orthogonal in \(L^2\).
