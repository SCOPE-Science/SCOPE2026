# Scientific review: sharp two-moment local rigidity

## Correctness — PASS
For positive weights, the moment operators are diagonal; taking logarithms converts the hypotheses *exactly* to \(d_j\ge-\alpha\) and \(\sum_{h=0}^{n-2}(n-1-h)d_{j+h}\le\beta\). Solving the latter weighted inequality using the lower bounds proves the universal upper constant and all equality conditions. A finite positive spike surrounded by sufficiently many \(-\alpha\) increments satisfies **every** translated moment inequality; an isolated negative increment attains the lower constant. The monotone ramp satisfies arbitrarily small defects while retaining distance \(1/4\) from scalar shifts. The independent finite rational checker passes; the mathematical proof covers every index and every integer \(n\ge3\). No assertion about finite tests certifying infinitely many cases is made.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The full text of Pietrzycki's 2026 paper was compared with the proposed quantitative statement, including its exact single-pair order characterization, hypotheses, and examples. The cited earlier 2015 weighted-shift paper covers failures of *single exact identities*, not perturbative simultaneous inequalities. Searches for quasinormality stability, approximate moment criteria, log weight ratios, and optimal weighted-shift bounds revealed related but non-dominating work on finite nilpotent moment defects, Aluthge asymptotics, and weighted-shift growth. Accessible prior finding claims were compared, and no identical assertion was found. An older but unindexed equivalence to a sharp scalar weighted-sum inequality cannot be excluded, and the 2015 source was checked at the abstract level only. Lack of a matching search result is not a novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
An exact operator characterization naturally raises the sensitivity question under nonzero, asymmetric multiplicative errors. The theorem gives a *scale-invariant*, sharp all-exponent local modulus for every adjacent weight and exact equality rigidity, while exhibiting a rigorous structural obstruction to translating that modulus into operator-norm stability. Both the positive result and the failure boundary are naturally parameterized by the original moment orders and errors, rather than arbitrary finite slices or routine reparameterizations. The conclusion is specific to positive unilateral shifts, not a general perturbation theorem.
