Moving just one bulk Chebyshev–Lobatto sample by a fixed fraction \(\tau\) of its angular mesh leaves interpolation logarithmically stable, but increases its **exact** leading Lebesgue coefficient to \(\frac{2}{\pi}\sin(\pi v_\tau)(1+\tau/v_\tau)\), with a unique scalar optimizer \(v_\tau\in(0,1/2)\). The new worst amplification concentrates on the side opposite the displaced sample, and its leading coefficient does not depend on where in the interior the sample is moved.
