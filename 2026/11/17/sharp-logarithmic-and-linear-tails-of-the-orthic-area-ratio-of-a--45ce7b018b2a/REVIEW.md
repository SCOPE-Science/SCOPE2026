# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For three angularly uniform circle points the half-gap angle triple is Dirichlet(1,1,1), with A,B density 2/pi^2. The signed orthic identity Q=2|cos A cos B cos C| correctly includes obtuse triangles. Product-to-sum gives E[cos A cos B cos C]=-1/4; the acute conditional simplex yields E[P 1acute]=3/(4pi^2)-1/16, and hence E Q=1/4+3/pi^2. Each small-ratio corner has local product Q=2|y(x+y)|(1+O(x^2+y^2)) in the wedge x>=0; same-sign wedges and an opposite-sign quadrant give t log(1/t)+O(t) angle area, times three corners and density 2/pi^2. Each near-flat corner has Q=2-2(u^2+uv+v^2)+O((u+v)^4) and area pi*t/(6sqrt3); summing three gives the upper slope 1/(pi sqrt3). Independent 700000-sample exponential-simplex numerical diagnostic gave mean 0.55453 versus 0.55396 and upper-tail ratios near 1; lower-tail ratios remained >1 due to a non-negligible O(t) correction, not interpreted as limit proof.

## originality

PASS

Classical pedal-triangle identities and random-cyclic polygon area extremes do not determine the full unsigned orthic area ratio statistic. The three-point exact mean and two distinct sharp endpoint laws are not mechanically inherited from original-triangle area statistics. Best-of-knowledge original with residual risk of unindexed stochastic orthic-triangle laws.

PASS

The orthic transform is a natural nonlinear triangle construction and its ratio differs sharply between flat and right-angle degeneracies. Exact mean and two asymptotically distinct endpoint tails resolve a motivated geometric probability question; the logarithmic corner-collision mechanism is a substantive feature.
