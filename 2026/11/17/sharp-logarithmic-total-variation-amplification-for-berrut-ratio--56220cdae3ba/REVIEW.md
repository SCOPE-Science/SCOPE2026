# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The result is a uniform sharp asymptotic, not an exact finite-size maximizer classification. The finite-dimensional variation norm is exactly the maximum over single jumps by telescoping data decomposition. On every cell, positive alternating sums and a reciprocal-denominator \(C^1\) estimate reduce each step's variation to a sine-wave contribution; summing the resulting two harmonic tails with parity yields the absolute \((2/\pi)\log n+O(1)\) upper bound. An endpoint jump gives the matching lower bound. The exceptional jump cell has uniformly bounded variation. Exact-arithmetic identities and numerical variation computations were separately reproduced by the included checker; finite experiments are not used for infinite claims.

## Originality — PASS with priority qualification
The closest inspected primary texts study Berrut or Floater–Hormann **Lebesgue sup-norm constants**, not the induced total-variation norm or the variation of step interpolants. The related variation-diminishing finite-element construction uses a different operator. Closest indexed 2026 results concern bounded monotone range and deleted-node Lebesgue factors: neither controls the sum of intra-cell oscillations or implies this theorem. Queries for synonyms (Jordan variation, BV seminorm, oscillation, step response, alternating barycentric rational interpolation) identified no covering theorem. Historical priority is not independently certified; original 1988 Berrut full text and possibly unindexed older analysis remain residual risks.

## Value — PASS
It resolves a natural stability question for a classical interpolation operator, distinguishing bounded pointwise range escape from **logarithmically divergent accumulated variation**. The sharp universal coefficient, all-grid uniform remainder, exact jump reduction, and explicit extremizing step family establish a structural result rather than a narrowly parameterized computation.
