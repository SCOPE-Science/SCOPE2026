# Scientific review: clique-joined biclique Poincaré constants

## Correctness — PASS
The metric upper bound follows from two independently valid complete sums of triangle inequalities: one indexed by the opposite shore and the other by all universal clique vertices. Their optimal interpolation is \(2(m-1)/(m+2c)\), and the supplied finite metric attains each equality. For the real \(\mathrm L^1\) bound, the cut formula has exact crossing-edge count, endpoint reduction in the clique cardinality, and a matched-pair increment identity that proves the cut maximum and all equality cases for every integer parameter. The denominator normalization is the average over distinct unordered vertex pairs divided by the average over graph edges. The closed gap identity is an exact polynomial factorization. The checker is supplementary, not a substitute for the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated priority risk
Targeted semantic research-record searches for the precise clique join, general complete multipartite Poincaré formulas, cut-cone equivalents, and the spectral-gap source returned no statement implying both sharp constants with the optimal cut classification. The comparison calculation for \(K_{a,b}\) applies to the no-clique boundary \(c=0\); it cannot yield the nonzero-clique equality conditions. Polavarapu's 2026 regular-expander theorem is not directly applicable because the joined graphs have two different vertex degrees. Research records on multipartite Lipschitz variance, negative type, and cut-space projection constants concern different invariants. The originating paper's complete text could not be inspected, and an older exact-cut theorem remains a possible priority risk; a missing full text is not being treated as evidence of absence.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete natural two-parameter classification of a nonlinear graph Poincaré functional for two target classes, with a strict, exact metric-vs-\(\mathrm L^1\) gap at every \(m\ge3,c\ge1\). A universal clique gives a clear structural perturbation: it removes all previously optimal multi-vertex balanced cuts while preserving the metric-target gap, which then vanishes quadratically as the clique grows. Explicit target witnesses, all extremal cut cardinalities, and a geodesic equality criterion supply useful structural information beyond numerical constants.
