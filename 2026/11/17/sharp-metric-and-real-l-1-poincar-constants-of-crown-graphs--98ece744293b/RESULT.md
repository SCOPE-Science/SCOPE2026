# Sharp metric and real \(L^1\) Poincaré constants of crown graphs

## Finding
For the connected crown graph \(\operatorname{Cr}_n=K_{n,n}\setminus M\), with a deleted perfect matching and \(n\ge3\), the sharp linear-distance Poincaré constant for **arbitrary metric targets** is
\[
\boxed{\gamma_{\mathrm{met}}(\operatorname{Cr}_n)=\frac32.}
\]
For **real \(L^1\) targets**, the exact constant is
\[
\boxed{\gamma_{L^1}(\operatorname{Cr}_n)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{n-1}{n-2},& n\text{ even},\\
\dfrac{n}{n-1},&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\]
The two constants coincide for \(n=3,4\), and the \(L^1\) constant is strictly smaller for every \(n\ge5\), tending to \(1\) while the unrestricted metric constant stays \(3/2\). The optimal \(L^1\) cuts are *exactly* the balanced opposite-pair cuts: choose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) with \(|I|\in\{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil\}\), and take
\[
S_I=\{a_i:i\in I\}\cup\{b_j:j\notin I\}.
\]
Every equality-achieving nonconstant map into a real \(L^1\) space has, almost everywhere in its coordinate-and-threshold layer-cake decomposition, only cuts of this form or their complements, apart from null or full cuts. Conversely this condition suffices.

## Assumptions and scope
For each integer \(n\ge3\), write the vertex classes of \(\operatorname{Cr}_n\) as \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_n\). An edge joins \(a_i\) and \(b_j\) precisely when \(i\ne j\). Thus \(|V|=2n\) and \(|E|=n(n-1)\). For a map \(f\) into a metric space \((X,d)\), put
\[
\operatorname{Pair}(f)=\frac1{(2n)^2}\sum_{u,v\in V}d(f(u),f(v)),\qquad
\operatorname{Edge}(f)=\frac1{n(n-1)}\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}d(f(u),f(v)).
\]
The constant \(\gamma\) is the least real number such that \(\operatorname{Pair}(f)\le\gamma\operatorname{Edge}(f)\) for every map of this kind. The graph is connected, so vanishing edge energy forces the map to be constant. The metric target in the first formula is unrestricted: the supremum ranges over all metric spaces and all maps. The \(L^1\) target in the second formula means any **real** \(L^1(\Omega,\mu)\), including finite-dimensional real \(\ell_1\), and does not assert the corresponding complex-valued assertion. Both inequalities use the exponent one, rather than squared distances.

## Proof
**1. Arbitrary metrics and the symmetry reduction.** For every admissible map, let \(d_f(u,v)=d(f(u),f(v))\) be the resulting pseudometric on the vertices. Average it under the automorphism group generated by simultaneous permutations of the \(n\) indices and the global exchange \(a_i\leftrightarrow b_i\). Pair and edge averages are invariant under these automorphisms; the averaged pseudometric has the same two averages as \(d_f\). Its off-diagonal entries fall into exactly three classes: \(a\) between distinct vertices of one bipartition class, \(b\) between matched vertices \(a_i,b_i\), and \(c\) between adjacent vertices. For \(n\ge3\), triangles using two edges imply \(a\le2c\), and triangles connecting a matched pair through a distinct same-class vertex imply \(b\le a+c\le3c\). Counting unordered pairs gives
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Pair}(f)}{\operatorname{Edge}(f)}
=\frac{(n-1)a+b+(n-1)c}{2nc}\le\frac32,
\]
when \(c>0\). Equality is realized by the intrinsic shortest-path metric of \(\operatorname{Cr}_n\): distinct same-class vertices have distance \(2\), matched cross-class vertices have distance \(3\), and adjacent vertices have distance \(1\). This is a legitimate metric target (and isometrically embeds into \(\ell_\infty^{2n}\) via the distance-coordinate construction), so \(\gamma_{\mathrm{met}}=3/2\).

**2. Reduction of the real \(L^1\) problem to cuts.** For each real-valued coordinate \(h\) and any vertices \(u,v\),
\[
|h(u)-h(v)|=\int_{\mathbb R}\big|\mathbf1_{\{h(u)>t\}}-\mathbf1_{\{h(v)>t\}}\big|\,dt.
\]
Integrate this identity over \(\Omega\) and use Tonelli's theorem for the finitely many pairs and edges. The Poincaré quotient for any real \(L^1\) map is a weighted average of quotients of nontrivial indicator cuts, with weights proportional to their positive edge boundary counts. Consequently the optimal \(L^1\) constant equals the maximum indicator-cut quotient. The same decomposition shows that equality holds exactly when almost every nontrivial layer cut is an optimal cut.

**3. Exact cut calculation.** Write a cut as \(S\subset V\), with \(k=|S\cap\{a_i\}|\), \(\ell=|S\cap\{b_i\}|\), \(s=k+\ell\), and \(t\) the number of matched pairs wholly contained in \(S\). Replacing \(S\) by its complement if necessary gives \(1\le s\le n\). The edge boundary is
\[
B=(n-1)s-2k\ell+2t,
\]
and the cut Poincaré quotient is
\[
R(S)=\frac{(n-1)s(2n-s)}{2nB}.
\]
Since \(k\ell\le\lfloor s^2/4\rfloor\) and \(t\ge0\),
\[
R(S)\le \frac{(n-1)s(2n-s)}{2n\big((n-1)s-2\lfloor s^2/4\rfloor\big)}\le H(s),
\qquad H(s)=\frac{(n-1)(2n-s)}{n(2n-2-s)}.
\]
The denominators are strictly positive for \(1\le s\le n\) and \(n\ge3\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
H'(s)=\frac{2(n-1)}{n(2n-2-s)^2}>0.
\]
If \(n\) is even, then \(R(S)\le H(n)=(n-1)/(n-2)\). Equality demands \(s=n\), \(k=\ell=n/2\), and \(t=0\), exactly the balanced opposite-pair cuts.

If \(n\) is odd, then for every \(s\le n-1\),
\[
R(S)\le H(n-1)=\frac{n+1}{n}<\frac{n}{n-1}.
\]
For \(s=n\), the exact floor-bound instead gives
\[
R(S)\le\frac{(n-1)n^2}{2n\left((n-1)n-2\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor\right)}=\frac{n}{n-1}.
\]
Equality demands \(k,\ell=(n-1)/2,(n+1)/2\) in either order, and \(t=0\), again precisely the stated cuts. Every such cut exists, so all estimates are sharp. Together with Step 2, this also proves the complete real \(L^1\) equality classification.

## Relationship to prior work
Polavarapu's 2026 theorem gives upper bounds on nonlinear spectral gaps of scalar expanders for cotype and unconditional-basis Banach targets, including Banach lattices, and introduces the precise ordered-pair versus undirected-edge normalization used here. It does not compute the constants or equality cases for crown graphs. Altschuler, Dodos, Tikhomirov, and Tyros develop general metric graph Poincaré theory and sharp estimates involving random regular graphs, rather than a closed formula for this family. Han and Wang (2026) study sharp \(L^1\) inequalities on Hamming cubes and Cayley/product settings; their abstract does not assert the crown-graph formulas.

An earlier published classification of generalized roundness for crown-graph shortest-path metrics concerns *negative type* and optimal Euclidean snowflake ranks. These properties are not the same as the exponent-one mean-pair/mean-edge Poincaré ratio, and do not imply the real \(L^1\) cut-value formula or its equality classification. Other published crown-graph projection results concern cut/cycle subspace operator norms, not the indicator-cut quotient used here. The layer-cake method and automorphism averaging are classical tools; novelty is claimed only for the sharp combined family-specific constants, strict target gap, and exact equality cuts, not for those tools.

## Limitations
The exact two-target classification concerns only unweighted crown graphs with deleted full perfect matching and exponent-one distances. It does not establish constants for \(L^p\) targets for \(p>1\), arbitrary irregular graphs, weights, complex-valued \(L^1\), or exponent-two spectral gaps. It does not characterize all maximizing arbitrary-metric pseudometrics: only the bound and an explicit sharp example are asserted. A prior equivalent graph-cut/isoperimetric formulation cannot be excluded solely by absence from searched records; this remains a publication-priority risk. No independent external audit has been carried out.
