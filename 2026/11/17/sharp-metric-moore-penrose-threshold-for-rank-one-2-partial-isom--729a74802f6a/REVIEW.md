# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The rank-one \(2\)-partial-isometry equation is \(z^2c^2-2z+1=0\). It gives the feasible range \(z\ge1/2\), and the reflexive generalized inverse yields exact idempotents \(P=ST\), \(Q=TS\). One positive metric must symmetrize both. The \(P\)-condition makes the metric diagonal; the \(Q\)-condition then forces \(2g_1=(2-z)g_2\). Hence it exists precisely for \(z<2\), with an explicit metric, and fails for \(z\ge2\). An exact \(z=3\) witness independently has negative product trace of the two idempotents. The algebraic checker passed; finite checks are supplementary, not an infinite proof. No additional hypotheses are silently used.

The motivating arXiv:2606.12865v1 paper expressly poses this metric Moore–Penrose question in §6, Question 6. The paper's §2 supplies the rank-one polynomial relation and canonical inverse but not the simultaneous-positive-metric norm threshold or negative witness. Prior published-record searches found no stronger or equivalent classification; nearby hits concern different inverse problems. Residual risk: a classical theorem on simultaneous symmetrization of idempotents could imply the criterion when combined with the source's rank-one formula; no exhaustive priority claim is made.

## Value — PASS
The negative answer resolves a precisely posed natural operator-theoretic question, not a merely arbitrary finite example. The sharp classification determines exactly when the proposed Moore–Penrose metric exists, covering every admissible norm in the smallest nontrivial rank-one family. It exposes the positivity obstruction separating generalized inverses from metric pseudoinverses.
