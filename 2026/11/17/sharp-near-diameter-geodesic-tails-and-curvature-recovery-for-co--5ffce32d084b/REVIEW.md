# Scientific assessment

**Correctness: PASS.** Exact curvature-dependent polar area measures and the hyperbolic cosine rule give the rare-event angular aperture. The aperture and boundary radial densities admit uniform relative errors of order \(O(\varepsilon)\), including near the degenerating boundary of the integration triangle; integrating \(\sqrt{\varepsilon-u-v}\) gives the verified \(4/15\) factor. The conditional coordinate Jacobian yields a normalizable \(W^{-1/2}\) density with constant \(15/8\), which is precisely the claimed Dirichlet law. Strict curvature recovery follows from a pointwise positive logarithmic derivative, not a numerical graph. Both hyperbolic and Euclidean boundary cases were checked in the analytic argument.

**Originality: PASS (self-review; historical priority provisional).** The inspected 2012 and 2014 primary papers analyze graph degree and random-graph path statistics, not the near-\(2R\) endpoint of ambient pair distances. A Euclidean-ball distance treatment provides a flat baseline but not curvature sensitivity. Related distance-tail work on Euclidean annuli, hyperbolic cusps, and expanding hyperbolic balls does not contain an implication for the fixed-radius hyperbolic coefficient and its marked Dirichlet limit. A general manifold endpoint theory not found by these searches remains a genuine priority risk; absence of search results is not considered a uniqueness proof.

**Value: PASS.** The result is a complete, natural rare-event classification for fixed-radius geodesic disks in nonpositive constant curvature. Beyond a noninteger endpoint exponent, it supplies the explicit curvature signal, an inverse rigidity theorem from one scalar distribution, and the full conditional geometric configuration law. These are motivated geometric invariants, not arbitrary finite computations.

Scientific limitations: fixed parameters, normalized intrinsic area, simply connected space forms; no variable-curvature theorem, noisy-inverse stability guarantee, or independent validation.
