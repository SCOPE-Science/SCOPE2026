# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Weighted atomic Bochner duality reduces all extensions to orthogonal coefficient perturbations, making norm and error formulas exact rather than estimates. For dual exponents below two, the probability-space power-mean inequality proves the radius \(\sqrt{R^2-1}\), with equality only for proportional coefficient magnitudes. At two the result is Pythagorean. Above two, the exact excess-cost transform \(F_u(d)\), its monotonicity in the original coefficient mass, and its increasing cost-efficiency establish the least-atom optimization and all equality cases. Central symmetry makes the diameter twice the radius. The finite-atom asymptotic follows from an ordinary binomial expansion at a positive budget. Deterministic supplementary tests give independent arithmetic stress checks, not a substitute for these arguments.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual priority risk.** The full available text of Karak–Paul, arXiv:2611.03203v1, Section 3 proves qualitative uniqueness transfer rather than any exact tolerance-dependent extension diameter. Daptari–Paul–Rao, arXiv:2009.09581v1, treats qualitative extension properties under a Radon–Nikodým hypothesis. Gayathri–Shunmugaraj–Thota (2024), Sections 2–3, explicitly define near-minimal extension sets and characterize qualitative diameter decay, but do not state the exact profile in the finite-atomic Hilbert setting, its least-mass localization, or its concentration threshold. The nearest retrieved mathematical records concern different weak-null perturbations, operator-valued extension constants, or different mixed-norm extremizers. Standard duality, Hilbert orthogonality, and scalar inequalities are credited as ingredients. Older quantitative Chebyshev-approximation formulations, especially Oja's 1988 work, remain a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The quantitative near-extension fiber is a natural and independently motivated invariant in the theory of strong and uniform Hahn–Banach extension properties. Its complete sharp formula identifies the *single* original-functional statistic controlling all atomic weights for conjugate exponent above two, the exact spatial form of extremizers, and a sharp change at the Hilbert exponent. Even the elementary equal-mass model has a nontrivial finite-size correction that quantifies how concentration approaches the worst uniform stability modulus. This is an exact motivated classification rather than a substitution into the qualitative uniqueness theorem.

## Limits and relevant literature

The theorem does not extend to arbitrary Banach targets or nonatomic spaces. Karak–Paul (2026) and Daptari–Paul–Rao (2020/2021) give qualitative transfer statements; Gayathri–Shunmugaraj–Thota (2024) makes the exact diameter question natural but studies its qualitative behavior. Oja (1988) is an older related source for which an equivalent formulation cannot be excluded. The result is not claimed to be independently certified.
