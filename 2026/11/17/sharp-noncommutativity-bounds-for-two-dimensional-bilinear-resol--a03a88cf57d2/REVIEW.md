# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
Tian's Theorem 4.1 gives the equivalence between a positive joint Hausdorff moment completion and \(A/\|A\|+B/\|B\|\succeq I\) for the exact noncommuting strictly positive two-dimensional domain. The proof rewrites both matrices as complements of two rank-one projections, computes the sum eigenvalues and the commutator norm exactly, optimizes the overlap under a necessary-and-sufficient determinant constraint, then obtains the sharp global bound by a quadratic discriminant inequality and AM–GM. Equality conditions and a rank-one-completion witness were checked independently; neither finite numerics nor a partial certificate is used to infer an infinite-domain theorem. The only nonstandard external premise, the moment-completion criterion, was inspected in the full original Section 4.

## Originality — PASS, with stated uncertainty
The motivating source's full Section 4 classifies feasibility and the unique completion but does not give a commutator optimum or the fixed-spectra transition. Projection norm identities and general commutator bounds are genuine antecedents for individual algebraic steps, not claims to a moment-completion spectral envelope. Closest published research-record entries on rank-one weighted commutators and crossed-Heinz inequalities optimize different quantities. The full texts of some older general commutator papers were not obtained; their precise theorem scope remains a priority risk, as does the possibility of an equivalent independent formulation. This review does not establish absolute novelty or independence.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A qualitative existence criterion allows a continuum of noncommuting moment-completable pairs but leaves their quantitative compatibility cost undetermined. The exact two-parameter spectral envelope describes the maximum noncommutativity under each pair of marginal spectral gaps, with a concrete transition between constraint-saturated and overlap-saturated extremizers. The universal attained constant and its rigid equality case give an explicit normalization-independent obstruction; a merely commutator-norm-bound exercise would not supply this combined classification. The work does not purport to solve the higher-dimensional moment problem.
