# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The geometric normalization is intrinsic to the zero-sum hyperplane, and cycle-closed step words enumerate vertices exactly. The discrete Green identity converts the Euclidean norm into a positive-weight pair energy; strict concavity of this energy in cyclic gaps reduces all nonmaximal configurations to capped-simplex extremal gap vectors. Exact deficit formulas then prove the even and odd minima. The odd zero-position endpoint lemma follows by a positive quadratic second difference and two explicit endpoint comparisons. Exact checker results across the full vertex sets for orders 4 through 15 and 1,334 further rational deficit identities corroborate (without replacing) the all-order argument. Boundary orders 4 and 5 and duplicate cyclic words are expressly accounted for.

## Originality — PASS, historically provisional
The closest public statement is *Cycle symmetric edge polytopes have a parity-split exact Euclidean inradius*, which already determines the largest polar-vertex norm, its maximizers, and the inball centers. That earlier maximum is explicitly credited. Knowing the maximum does not provide the next largest distinct norm, next-shell equality families, or next nearest facet clearance. The full cycle sections of D'Alì–Delucchi–Michałek and Jevtić–Timotijević–Živaljević treat facet combinatorics and weighted-cycle boundary structure rather than second Euclidean norm shells. No checked source implies the sharp quantitative second shell. A differently indexed discrete-walk variance stability result remains a bibliographic risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result is a complete sharp second-extremal classification over all facets of a natural infinite polytope family. It quantitatively refines a previously known maximal insphere theorem with an explicit strict clearance and every equality configuration. The new information is not an arbitrary sample invariant or a small numerical improvement: it identifies the precise nearest nontangent geometric obstructions after the complete set of inball contacts, relevant to stability and face-distance stratification.

## Closest literature and limits
The earlier inradius result is stronger for optimal-center geometry and weaker for next-shell separation. The 2019 facet paper furnishes the necessary combinatorial parametrization. The 2018 weighted-cycle paper concerns Bier-sphere boundary combinatorics. Neither finite enumerations nor unsuccessful searches prove historical uniqueness. The theorem is unweighted and root-Euclidean only, with no assertion about continuous interior radii.
