# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is restricted to finite polynomial coefficient vectors, integer Gaussian Fock–Sobolev orders and the strict compact total-degree regime. The monomial Hilbert norm uses the exact radial moment formula and the coefficient weights have leading constant \(1\), giving a finite Laurent band multiplied by \((1+k)^{-\delta/2}\), plus a smaller-Schatten-tail remainder. The weighted-band counting lemma is proved by closed-walk moments on mesoscopic index windows, polynomial approximation, threshold continuity, and separate low-rank/high-norm tail control. Ky Fan counting inequalities remove the remainder. Counting inversion and harmonic summation justify the Schatten cutoff and logarithmic residue. In the example \(I^3(zf+f')\), exact matrix weights and the integral yielding \(4/\pi\) have supplementary numerical confirmation. No finite computation is treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations

Dong–Hu arXiv:2611.01959v1, Theorems 1.3, 1.5–1.6 and proof equations (49)–(50), establishes total-degree compactness, critical essential norm and Fredholm data; it does **not** give the compact sums' ordered singular-value asymptotics or their angular coefficient. Mengestie (2017; 2015) studies the Schatten membership of related, but narrower/different, Volterra and companion operators. The most relevant mathematical research-record matches concern critical Fock integral **commutators**, Fock Toeplitz/Hankel operators or Volterra models on different spaces, none of whose statements implies the displayed all-polynomial Fock–Sobolev constant. General negative-order pseudodifferential Weyl theory and locally Toeplitz theory furnish a broader route to the weighted-band lemma. The novel element is the explicit identification and unified classification of the original Fock–Sobolev operator family, not a claimed new abstract Weyl principle. An unnoticed prior formulation or direct published specialization remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The classification supplies the exact spectral scale and critical weak-Schatten residue of an operator family for which the motivating 2026 study proved only compactness and critical essential-norm structure. Distinct derivative degrees produce nonorthogonal bands, so their coefficient magnitudes do not simply add; the exact phase-sensitive angular polynomial controls the leading constant. The example \(I^3(zf+f')\) has the nontrivial coefficient \(4/\pi\), rather than the coefficient \(2\) suggested by summing individual band magnitudes. This gives useful precise quantitative spectral information even where Schatten membership was qualitatively predictable. No second term, optimal remainder, or broader symbol class is claimed.
