# Scientific comparison and scope

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact conditional law uses the chord length density \(\ell/2\), the projected-sphere density \(1/(2\pi\sqrt{1-|q|^2})\), and the perpendicular-distance area formula. The small and large chord cutoffs contribute respectively at most \(O(\eta^{1/2}t^{5/3})\) and \(O(R^{-1}t^{5/3})\), showing that the cubic rescaling captures all leading mass. In the rescaled limit the singular integral reduces to two beta integrals, giving \(C_*>0\). Independent exact-slice quadrature and direct 3D samples provide numerical cross-checks, not proofs.

## Originality
**PASS, provisionally.** Finch–Jones (arXiv:1009.5329) concerns intrinsic **spherical excess** rather than Euclidean chord-triangle area, and its inspected full-text sections contain no such endpoint law. Akopyan–Edelsbrunner–Nikitenko (arXiv:2007.07783) supply the Blaschke–Petkantschin decomposition, size/shape independence and facet means, not the asserted fractional small-area exponent or constant. Related geometric-probability research records concern acute triangles, random perimeter, Voronoi cell spherical area or opposite (maximal) area tails. The full 1971 isotropic-simplex moment paper and the 1989 spherical-triangle shape-density paper could not be checked; whether those texts contain an equivalent explicit asymptotic is a residual historical-priority risk. Their bibliographic abstracts alone are insufficient to assert noncoverage.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** A canonical random geometric observable has an unexpected fractional zero-endpoint exponent \(5/3\), rather than a generic smooth-density power. The proof identifies the exact cubic collision geometry and supplies a normalized closed beta-product constant. This precise tail gives quantitative probabilities for arbitrarily small surface-sampled facets and is not a routine dimensional scaling or table lookup.

## Limitations
Only three independent surface-uniform vertices and ordinary Euclidean planar area are considered. The self-review does not establish independent certification, all-source historical priority, a second coefficient, or numerical error control for extrapolating finite-threshold data to zero.
