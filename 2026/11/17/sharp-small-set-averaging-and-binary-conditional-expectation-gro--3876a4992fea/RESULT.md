# Sharp small-set averaging and binary conditional-expectation growth in mixed Lebesgue spaces

## Finding

For all real exponents \(1<p,q<\infty\), put \(X_{p,q}=L^p([0,1]_x;L^q([0,1]_y))\). For every measurable \(B\subset[0,1]^2\) of planar Lebesgue measure \(t\in(0,1)\), let
\[
 P_Bf=\frac{\mathbf 1_B}{t}\int_B f,\qquad
 E_Bf=\frac{\mathbf 1_B}{t}\int_B f
       +\frac{\mathbf 1_{B^c}}{1-t}\int_{B^c}f.
\]
Thus \(E_B\) is conditional expectation onto the two-atom sigma-algebra generated by \(B\). Define the natural optimal amplification functions
\[
 K_{p,q}(t)=\sup_{|B|=t}\|P_B:X_{p,q}\to X_{p,q}\|,\quad
 H_{p,q}(t)=\sup_{|B|=t}\|E_B:X_{p,q}\to X_{p,q}\|.
\]
If \(p=q\), then \(K_{p,p}(t)=H_{p,p}(t)=1\) for every \(0<t<1\). If \(p\ne q\), set \(\Delta=|1/p-1/q|\) and
\[
 (\alpha,\beta)=
 \begin{cases}(1/q,1/p'),&p<q,\\(1/q',1/p),&p>q,\end{cases}
 \qquad C_{p,q}=\frac{\alpha^\alpha\beta^\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)^{\alpha+\beta}},
\]
where \(p'=p/(p-1)\) and \(q'=q/(q-1)\). Then both independent suprema have **the same exact leading constant**:
\[
 \boxed{\lim_{t\downarrow0}t^\Delta K_{p,q}(t)
  =\lim_{t\downarrow0}t^\Delta H_{p,q}(t)=C_{p,q}.}
\]
In particular, uniformly bounded averaging projections over arbitrary measurable sets exist in this mixed-norm family **if and only if** the two exponents coincide. For unequal exponents, the sharp divergence order is \(t^{-\Delta}\), with the explicit nontrivial coefficient \(C_{p,q}\).

## Assumptions and scope

All spaces are scalar real or complex mixed Lebesgue spaces on the unit square with product Lebesgue measure, and \(1<p,q<\infty\). The two norms are the usual iterated norms; changing their order changes the meaning of the exponents. The supremum ranges over *all* measurable subsets of planar measure exactly \(t\), not just rectangles. We do not assert attainment of the finite-\(t\) supremum or uniqueness of asymptotic maximizers. The claim includes neither infinite exponents nor variable measures.

## Proof

**1. Exact reduction to section sizes.** Set \(b(x)=|\{y\in[0,1]:(x,y)\in B\}|\), so \(0\le b\le1\) and \(\int_0^1 b(x)\,dx=t\). Mixed-norm duality and the norm identity for a rank-one map give
\[
 \|P_B\|=\frac{\|\mathbf1_B\|_{p,q}\|\mathbf1_B\|_{p',q'}}{t}
 =\frac{\left(\int_0^1 b^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}
             \left(\int_0^1 b^{p'/q'}\right)^{1/p'}}{t}.\tag{1}
\]
Both inequalities in this equality are equalities: iterated Holder gives the dual norm of the indicator, and its norming function can be chosen supported inside \(B\). Conversely, any measurable \(b:[0,1]\to[0,1]\) is represented by a set \(B=\{(x,y):0\le y<b(x)\}\). Thus (1) is an exact variational representation, not a relaxation.

**2. Sharp upper bound for \(p<q\).** Write \(\theta=p/q\in(0,1)\), \(\eta=p'/q'>1\), and \(d=1/p-1/q>0\). For \(0<t<1\) split \([0,1]\) into \(G=\{x:b(x)\ge\sqrt t\}\) and its complement \(L\). Define \(c=t^{-1}\int_G b\in[0,1]\). Concavity and the inequalities \(b^\theta\le t^{(\theta-1)/2}b\) on \(G\), \(b^\eta\le t^{(\eta-1)/2}b\) on \(L\), and \(b^\eta\le b\) everywhere imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 t^{-\theta}\int b^\theta
 &\le(1-c)^\theta+c\,t^{(1-\theta)/2},\\
 t^{-1}\int b^\eta
 &\le c+(1-c)t^{(\eta-1)/2}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{2}
\]
By (1), \(t^d\|P_B\|\) is the product of the left sides of (2), raised to \(1/p\) and \(1/p'\) respectively. The upper expression converges **uniformly** for \(c\in[0,1]\) to
\[
 (1-c)^{\theta/p}c^{1/p'}=(1-c)^{1/q}c^{1/p'}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \limsup_{t\downarrow0}t^d K_{p,q}(t)
 \le\max_{0\le c\le1}(1-c)^{1/q}c^{1/p'}=C_{p,q},\tag{3}
\]
where the maximizing fraction is \(c_*=(1/p')/(1/q+1/p')\).

**3. Matching sets.** For fixed \(0<c<1\), and every \(0<t<1\), put
\[
 h_{t,c}=\frac{t(1-c)}{1-ct},\qquad
 B_{t,c}=([0,ct]\times[0,1])\cup((ct,1]\times[0,h_{t,c}]).\tag{4}
\]
This set has measure \(t\); its vertical sections have lengths \(1\) on a strip of width \(ct\) and \(h_{t,c}\) on the remainder. The exact expression (1) becomes
\[
 \|P_{B_{t,c}}\|=
 \frac{(ct+(1-ct)h_{t,c}^{\theta})^{1/p}
       (ct+(1-ct)h_{t,c}^{\eta})^{1/p'}}{t}.\tag{5}
\]
Because \(\theta<1<\eta\), the first bracket divided by \(t^\theta\) tends to \((1-c)^\theta\), and the second bracket divided by \(t\) tends to \(c\). Therefore \(t^d\|P_{B_{t,c}}\|\to(1-c)^{1/q}c^{1/p'}\). Choosing \(c=c_*\) proves equality in (3).

**4. The reverse exponent order and equality case.** Since \(X_{p,q}^*=X_{p',q'}\) isometrically and \(P_B^*=P_B\) under the integral pairing, \(K_{p,q}(t)=K_{p',q'}(t)\). If \(p>q\) then \(p'<q'\), so Steps 2--3 applied to the dual exponent pair give \(\Delta=1/q-1/p\) and the stated constants \(\alpha=1/q'\), \(\beta=1/p\). When \(p=q\), both spaces are ordinary \(L^p\) and formula (1) gives \(K_{p,p}(t)=1\).

**5. Binary conditional expectations.** For each \(B\), a norming input for \(P_B\) may be chosen supported on \(B\). On such an input \(E_B=P_B\), so \(\|E_B\|\ge\|P_B\|\). Conversely, by the triangle inequality
\[
 \|E_B\|\le\|P_B\|+\|P_{B^c}\|\le K_{p,q}(t)+K_{p,q}(1-t).\tag{6}
\]
The elementary bounds \(\int b^\theta\le t^\theta\) and \(\int b^\eta\le t\) in the first regime (and the dual argument in the second) give \(K_{p,q}(u)\le u^{-\Delta}\) for every \(u\in(0,1)\). Thus \(K_{p,q}(1-t)\) stays bounded as \(t\downarrow0\), and multiplying (6) by \(t^\Delta\) proves the second limit. For \(p=q\), ordinary conditional expectation is a contraction fixing constants, hence has norm one.

These steps establish every quantifier in the theorem.

## Verification

Formula (5) allows independent numerical checks without discretizing an operator. The supplied Python 3 checker evaluates multiple exponent pairs and decreasing measures, verifies the exact area of (4), the dual pair symmetry, the general Jensen bound, and convergence toward the sharp coefficient. These are finite diagnostics. The proof of optimality for arbitrary measurable sets is the uniform moment splitting in (2), not the numerical tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Krasikova, Popov and Randrianantoanina (2026) introduce the averaging-friendly condition for Köthe-Banach targets and use indicator fundamental functions in disjointness-preserving and narrow-operator theory. The present theorem calculates a sharp small-set rank-one amplification profile for the canonical mixed-Lebesgue family, a different quantitative question from their operator-existence conclusions.

Lü and van Neerven (2016/2018) characterize boundedness of conditional expectations on mixed Lebesgue spaces and exhibit unbounded conditional expectations for a symmetric sigma-algebra when the exponents differ. That example neither is a binary-atom averaging family nor specifies the sharp growth exponent and leading constant as the smaller atom tends to zero. Albiac, Ansorena, Bello and Wojtaszczyk (2023) study bounded averaging onto ordered partitions in subsymmetric sequence spaces; their norm bounds do not imply this mixed-function-space extremum. These contributions are credited as prior context, and qualitative failures of boundedness are not asserted as a new discovery.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to product probability intervals, finite exponents strictly above one, and rank-one/binary conditional expectations. It does not determine the exact optimal norm for a prescribed nonzero \(t\), does not classify near-extremal measurable sets, and does not address general countably generated sigma-algebras. The literature search cannot guarantee publication priority under alternate terminology, especially for older mixed-norm conditional-expectation results.
