# Self-review of sharp metric laws for multipartite edge polytopes

## Correctness — PASS
The claim includes two exact Euclidean quantities and their equality sets, consistently stated for every complete multipartite graph with at least two classes. All vertices of the edge polytope have squared norm two. The finite-set fixed-norm minimax lemma is derived from a convex projection inequality and a barycentric lower bound; it gives a *unique* smallest-ball center, not merely an equidistant sphere. The reduction to bounded class masses is exact because class-edge incidence vectors form the rank-two hypersimplex. Strict convexity solves the class-mass minimization in the balanced and dominant regimes, and direct inner products classify every sphere contact. For each missing hypersimplex vertex, two Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities yield a sharp lower bound achieved by an explicit feasible average of cross-class edges. The final convex-combination argument and distinct equal-length projection residuals prove that no nonvertex point is equally far away. Equality at the threshold, the two-class case, the all-singleton case, and low-order degeneracy are expressly handled.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority
Higashitani--Matsushita, arXiv:2102.02349 (first public 2021-02-04), analyzes the **same canonical complete multipartite edge polytope** through edge rings and Gorenstein-type conditions. The full 16-page text was available and its main results and relevant sections were examined; whole-document searches found no Euclidean radius, Hausdorff-distance, or optimal-sphere theorem. Tran--Ziegler, arXiv:1307.6708, was read at the object definition and the face-count, neighborliness, and extremal-facet statements. It places all graph edge polytopes inside the rank-two hypersimplex but does not give the two metric equality classifications. The 2023 MathOverflow question and answer on whether the affine vertex sphere center lies in a matroid base polytope warns precisely why an arbitrary equidistant sphere is not automatically the minimum sphere; its counterexample is not a formula for either new invariant.

Semantic comparisons also covered published rank-two matroid inradius, Minkowski asymmetry, Gaussian cone angles, and general matroid-independence-polytope minimum spheres. These concern distinct quantities or different polytopes; the available indexed summaries do not dominate the present **joint** minimum-sphere and Hausdorff/farthest-point theorem. However, those summaries are not a substitute for complete full-text priority inspection, and the standard projection techniques could have yielded an equivalent result under unfamiliar terminology. Historical priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The edge polytope of a graph is a canonical subpolytope of the second hypersimplex, and complete multipartite graphs are the base graphs of rank-two partition matroids. The theorem identifies exact metric invariants for *all* positive class sizes, and especially a sharp geometric boundary at half the vertices: below it, deleting every forbidden within-part edge leaves the minimum sphere **unchanged**, even when the Hausdorff gap stays bounded away from zero; above it, the minimum sphere contracts and ceases to touch entire edge-orbits. Exact farthest points and their unique nearest points yield a complete global directed Hausdorff classification rather than a numerical approximation or a face-count restatement. This contrast is meaningful in geometric approximation and matroid-base geometry; it is not an arbitrary parameter slice. No claimed application beyond those geometric properties is necessary for value.

## Limitations
Standard Euclidean metric only; simple complete multipartite graphs and rank two only. The result does not classify general graphic edge polytopes, weighted deformations, or arbitrary-rank matroid base polytopes. Full historical priority and independent verification remain open.
