# Scientific review: successive-degree Bernstein stability

## Correctness

**PASS.** A shared Bernoulli sample couples the degree-\(n\) and degree-\(n+1\) binomial proportions. Its exact expected displacement is \(2x(1-x)/(n+1)\). Every displacement is saturated with the same sign by a piecewise-linear alternating-slope spline. All edges of the two interlacing grids occur with positive probability at an interior evaluation point, proving rigidity for arbitrary real Lipschitz inputs. An independent exact-rational cumulative-distribution calculation corroborates the transport coefficient for finite degrees; the infinite-degree claim follows from the analytic coupling, not the finite test set.

## Originality

**PASS, with historical-priority qualification.** Compared the claim and its transport reformulation with classical Bernstein single-degree Hölder estimates and preservation results in Gonska–Prestin–Tachev–Zhou (author manuscript, introductory Theorems A–E and main Theorem 1), the attributed Brown–Elliott–Paget Lipschitz-preservation lemma, and Aldaz–Kounchev–Render on generalized Bernstein operators. These imply neither the exact adjacent-degree norm nor simultaneous norming and rigidity for all evaluation points. Semantic literature and mathematical-result searches did not reveal covering statements. The 1987 original Lipschitz-preservation paper and certain older approximation sources could not be inspected in full; unindexed equivalent results remain a residual risk. No historical independence or exhaustive bibliographic coverage is asserted.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The difference between consecutive approximants is a canonical stability quantity for degree refinement. For a unit Lipschitz class, ordinary separate approximation estimates are of order \(n^{-1/2}\), whereas this theorem supplies the exact universal \(n^{-1}\) increment and pointwise parameter dependence. The rigid, single common spline extremizer is a further structural fact not supplied by individual-degree estimates, and the theorem identifies an explicit optimal transport between adjacent binomial-grid laws.

## Scope and limits

Only classical univariate Bernstein operators, real Lipschitz functions, and consecutive degrees are covered. The all-degree norm and equality classification are analytical. The exact finite verification is corroborative. The full 1987 paper was not available through accessible open sources or institutional access during this check.
