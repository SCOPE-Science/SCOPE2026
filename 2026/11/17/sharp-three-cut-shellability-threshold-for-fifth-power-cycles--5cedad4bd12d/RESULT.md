# Sharp three-cut shellability threshold for fifth-power cycles

## Finding
**Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(G_n=C_n^{(5)}\) be the graph on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) whose distinct vertices are adjacent precisely at cyclic distance at most five. Let \(\Delta_3(G_n)\) be the ordinary three-cut complex, generated by complements of three-vertex sets inducing a disconnected graph. Then:

\[
\begin{array}{rl}
3\le n\le12:&\Delta_3(G_n)\text{ is void};\\
13\le n\le20:&\Delta_3(G_n)\text{ is nonvoid and not shellable};\\
n\ge21:&\Delta_3(G_n)\text{ is shellable}.
\end{array}
\]

The positive range has the **integral homotopy classification**
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle \Delta_3(G_n)\simeq
\bigvee^{\binom{n-10}{2}-54}S^{n-4}\quad(n\ge21).}
\]

The new shellability certificates cover the six finite cases \(21\le n\le26\), which lie outside the previously proved large-order theorem. The published homology data and general large-order case are acknowledged as prior results. The new assertion is the sharp complete fifth-power shellability threshold, established by the six explicit shellings together with existing general theory and the lower-range obstructions.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are undirected and simple. A facet of \(\Delta_3(G_n)\) is exactly \(F_T=V(G_n)\setminus T\) for a three-element set \(T\) such that \(G_n[T]\) is disconnected. For three vertices this is equivalent to inducing **zero or one graph edges**, not two or three. Every nonvoid \(\Delta_3(G_n)\) is pure of dimension \(n-4\). We use ordinary pure shellability: a facet ordering has each new facet meet earlier facets in a pure codimension-one subcomplex. Homology groups and Alexander duality in the negative range are with \(\mathbb F_2\) coefficients. The positive homotopy assertion is integral and follows from simplicial shellability, not from mod-two calculations alone.

## Proof
**1. The void range.** For \(3\le n\le11\), every pair of cycle vertices has distance at most five and \(G_n\) is complete. At \(n=12\), each vertex has just one nonneighbor, its antipode. A disconnected induced triple needs an isolated vertex with two nonneighbors, impossible in either case. Thus no complement facet exists when \(n\le12\).

**2. An exact obstruction below the threshold.** For \(13\le n\le20\), let \(\mathcal B_n\) be the complete set of disconnected triples. The Alexander dual \(K_n=\Delta_3(G_n)^*\) has the following concrete description:
\[
K_n=\{\sigma\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z:\text{no }T\in\mathcal B_n\text{ is contained in }\sigma\}.
\]
This follows directly from the definition of the dual and the facet-complement relation. The independent exact finite verifier constructs every \(T\in\mathcal B_n\) from cyclic distances, enumerates all \(2^n\) subsets by a recurrence preserving the no-forbidden-triple condition, and computes **all** augmented simplicial boundary ranks by bit-exact Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_2\). The complete result is:

| \(n\) | Number of disconnected triples | Nonzero \(\widetilde H_d(K_n;\mathbb F_2)\) | Corresponding \(\widetilde H_{n-d-3}(\Delta_3(G_n);\mathbb F_2)\) |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 13 | 13 | \(\mathbb F_2\), \(d=5\) | \(\mathbb F_2\), degree 5 |
| 14 | 42 | \(\mathbb F_2\), \(d=5\) | \(\mathbb F_2\), degree 6 |
| 15 | 90 | \(\mathbb F_2^{21}\), \(d=3\) | \(\mathbb F_2^{21}\), degree 9 |
| 16 | 160 | \(\mathbb F_2^{101}\), \(d=3\) | \(\mathbb F_2^{101}\), degree 10 |
| 17 | 255 | \(\mathbb F_2^{52}\), \(d=3\) | \(\mathbb F_2^{52}\), degree 11 |
| 18 | 366 | \(\mathbb F_2^{19}\), \(d=3\) | \(\mathbb F_2^{19}\), degree 12 |
| 19 | 494 | \(\mathbb F_2\), \(d=3\) | \(\mathbb F_2\), degree 13 |
| 20 | 640 | \(\mathbb F_2^4\), \(d=2\) | \(\mathbb F_2^4\), degree 15 |

All other reduced mod-two groups of \(K_n\) vanish, as the complete rank computations show. The isomorphisms in the last column are simplicial Alexander duality over a field. In every row the original degree is strictly less than the top simplex dimension \(n-4\). A nonvoid pure shellable complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres in its top dimension and therefore has no such lower homology. Hence none of these eight complexes is shellable. The negative-side numerical ranks corroborate and partly reproduce entries of Chauhan–Shukla–Vinayak's previously published Table 2; **they are not asserted to be new homology calculations**.

**3. Six complete finite shellings.** Fix \(n\in\{21,22,23,24,25,26\}\). The supplied `shelling_orders.json` stores, for each \(n\), a complete permutation of the lexicographically enumerated disconnected triples \(T\), encoding the proposed order on the complementary facets \(F_T\). For any step with current triple \(T\) and set \(\mathcal P\) of earlier triples, put
\[
R(T)=\{b\in V\setminus T:\exists W\in\mathcal P
  \text{ with }|W\setminus T|=|T\setminus W|=1
  \text{ and }W\setminus T=\{b\}\}.
\]
The *pure shelling intersection criterion* is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{R(T)\cap(U\setminus T)\ne\varnothing
\quad\text{for every earlier }U\in\mathcal P.}
\]
To see this, \(F_T\setminus F_U=U\setminus T\). An earlier complementary facet \(F_W\) meets \(F_T\) in codimension one precisely when \(|W\setminus T|=1\); it covers the intersection \(F_U\cap F_T\) precisely when its unique extra omitted vertex \(b\) lies in \(U\setminus T\). Therefore the displayed condition checks the full defining shelling axiom, with no omitted earlier facets.

The included standalone verifier checks independently that each certificate is a permutation of **every** disconnected triple, reconstructs \(R(T)\) by explicit set differences, and checks that boxed condition against **every** earlier triple at every step. Each comparison is exact. A facet is spanning (its entire boundary already belongs to previous facets) precisely when \(R(T)=V\setminus T\). The exhaustive verified totals are:

| \(n\) | Complete facet count | Spanning facets | Top dimension |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 21 | 805 | 1 | 17 |
| 22 | 990 | 12 | 18 |
| 23 | 1196 | 24 | 19 |
| 24 | 1424 | 37 | 20 |
| 25 | 1675 | 51 | 21 |
| 26 | 1950 | 66 | 22 |

Thus all six orders are genuine shellings. The standard wedge theorem for pure shellable simplicial complexes gives one top-dimensional sphere per spanning facet (and none for the first facet). The six sphere counts agree with \(\binom{n-10}{2}-54\). The certificate is **complete**, not an incomplete search, sampling result or empirical extrapolation.

**4. The infinite tail.** Chauhan–Shukla [1, Theorem 1.4] proves for every \(p\ge2\) and \(n\ge6p-3\) that \(\Delta_3(C_n^{(p)})\) is shellable and has \(\binom{n-2p}{2}-(2p^2+p-1)\) top-dimensional sphere summands. For \(p=5\) this supplies **all \(n\ge27\)** and the claimed expression \(\binom{n-10}{2}-54\). Together with Steps 1–3, this completes the theorem for every integer \(n\ge3\).

## Verification
The complete certificate is `shelling_orders.json` and the standalone checking program is `verify_fifth_power.py`. From the directory containing both files run `python3 verify_fifth_power.py`. The script uses Python 3.10 or later and only its standard library. It independently reconstructs all disconnected triples, checks every shelling permutation and all previous-facet intersection conditions, counts all spanning facets, constructs all faces of the eight Alexander duals, computes every relevant augmented boundary rank over \(\mathbb F_2\), verifies Euler–Poincaré, and checks the void range. Its exact standard output is in `verification_output.txt`. The all-parameter step for \(n\ge27\) explicitly invokes a published theorem, not extrapolation from those certificates.

## Relationship to prior work
Chauhan–Shukla–Vinayak [2, Conjecture 4.2(ii)] predicts shellability exactly for \(n\ge4p+1\); its Table 2 includes small fifth-power integral homology data, so the new result **does not claim those numerical Betti counts**. Chauhan–Shukla [1] proves only the general range \(n\ge6p-3\), leaving at \(p=5\) precisely \(21\le n\le26\) unproved by that theorem. Earlier separate classifications establish sharp thresholds at powers three and four, but do not imply the fifth-power shellings or their facet-intersection certificates. Existing general nonshellability obstructions cover the negative side, not the six missing positive cases. The present six complete certified shellings establish Conjecture 4.2(ii) in its entirety **for the fifth power**, without claiming the conjecture for all powers.

## Limitations
The theorem is confined to ordinary three-cut complexes of fifth-power cycle graphs. It gives no new homotopy classifications for the nonshellable range \(13\le n\le20\), no claim for cut size other than three, and no proof of the conjectured sharp transition uniformly in all powers. A finite computational certificate proves only its explicitly enumerated cases; the infinite tail requires [1]. Although relevant prior literature and related published classifications were compared, an unindexed equivalent shelling is a residual bibliographic possibility. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
