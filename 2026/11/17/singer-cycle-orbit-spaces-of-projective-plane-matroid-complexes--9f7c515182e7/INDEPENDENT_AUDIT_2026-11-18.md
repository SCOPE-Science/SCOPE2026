# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The PG(2,q) rank-three matroid independence complex has all singletons/pairs and precisely the noncollinear triangular faces. Its matroid shellability makes it a wedge of two-spheres, hence simply connected. A Singer group of order m acts regularly on the points. A nontrivial stabilizer of any geometric point must permute a supporting simplex of size at most 3, so odd m precludes order 2 and forces element order 3, possible exactly when q≡1 mod3. The order-three element is multiplication by beta not in F_q, whose minimal polynomial has degree 3; thus 1,beta,beta² is an F_q basis and each of the m/3 vertex orbits is a noncollinear triangle with one barycenter fixed. Armstrong gives pi1(Q)=G/<stabilizers>=C_{m/3^epsilon}. f0=m, f1=C(m,2), f2=C(m,3)-m*C(q+1,3); the fixed-point Euler formula adds 2epsilon*(m/3) before division by m. Since Q has finite pi1 (b1=0) and 2-dimensional cellular H2 is a subgroup of free C2, H2 free rank chi-1, yielding the exact claimed formula. Independent substitution q=2,3,4,5,7,8,9 checked integrality and first values 1,12,44,110. Finite checker constructs cyclic difference sets for q=2,3,4,5; these samples are not an all-prime-power proof.

## originality

PASS

Armstrong supplies general pi1 for orbit spaces and matroid shellability supplies the simply connected covering complex; both methods are explicitly credited. A previously published integral Singer-module calculation for the Fano (q=2) covering complex gives coinvariant torsion but not homology of the geometric quotient for every q, notably the order-three isotropy transition. Thus the particular all-q orbit-space formula and stabilizer dichotomy are not a direct restatement of the prior q=2 module.

## value

PASS

Ordinary quotient topology of natural matroid complexes under a Singer cycle is a motivated finite-geometric invariant. Its exact fundamental-group order jumps by a factor 3 at q≡1mod3, while H2 remains explicitly torsionfree; identifying all stabilizer strata and integral homology for every prime power is a coherent natural classification, not an arbitrary numerical slice.
