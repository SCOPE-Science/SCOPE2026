# Singer-cycle orbit spaces of projective-plane matroid complexes: a threefold stabilizer dichotomy

## Finding
Let \(q\ge2\) be a prime power, let \(m=q^2+q+1\), and let \(K_q\) be the rank-three matroid independence complex of the points of the Desarguesian projective plane \(\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\). Thus a face is a set of at most three projective points, with three-point faces required to be noncollinear. Let \(G=C_m\) be a Singer cycle acting on those points, and form the **ordinary geometric orbit space** \(Q_q=|K_q|/G\).

Set \(\varepsilon_q=1\) when \(q\equiv1\pmod3\) and \(\varepsilon_q=0\) otherwise, and define
\[
b(q)=\frac{(m-1)(m-5)-6\binom{q+1}{3}+4\varepsilon_q}{6}.
\]
Then \(b(q)\) is a nonnegative integer and, for every prime power \(q\ge2\),
\[
\pi_1(Q_q)\cong C_{m/3^{\varepsilon_q}},\qquad
H_j(Q_q;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,\\
\mathbb Z/(m/3^{\varepsilon_q})\mathbb Z,&j=1,\\
\mathbb Z^{b(q)},&j=2,\\
0,&j>2.
\end{cases}
\]
All higher integral homology vanishes and \(H_2\) is free. The action is free on \(|K_q|\) if \(q\not\equiv1\pmod3\). When \(q\equiv1\pmod3\), the only nontrivial point stabilizer is the unique subgroup \(C_3\le G\); its fixed locus consists of exactly \(m/3\) isolated barycenters of projective bases. Those barycenters form one orbit under \(G\). In particular the quotient fundamental-group order falls by precisely a factor of three in the nonfree case.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground set consists of the one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspaces of \(\mathbb F_{q^3}\), considered as a three-dimensional \(\mathbb F_q\)-vector space. Distinct projective points span a projective line; every line contains \(q+1\) points, and there are \(m\) lines. A three-element subset is a face of \(K_q\) precisely when it is not contained in a line. The Singer generator is multiplication by a primitive element of \(\mathbb F_{q^3}^\times\), modulo the scalar subgroup \(\mathbb F_q^\times\); hence \(G\) has order \(m\) and acts freely transitively on the point set. All actions and quotients here are on the **geometric realization**, not on a set of face-orbits regarded as an abstract simplicial complex. The result concerns the independence complex, not the order complex of the proper lattice of flats and not the no-three-collinear cap complex allowing faces of size greater than three.

## Proof
**1. Simply connected covering complex.** The projective rank-three matroid has no loops or coloops. The standard shellability theorem for matroid independence complexes gives \(|K_q|\simeq\bigvee S^2\). In particular \(|K_q|\) is connected and simply connected. Every finite simplicial action becomes without inversions on barycentric subdivision, so the quotient is a connected finite CW complex of dimension two.

**2. Point stabilizers.** A point of the realization has a unique smallest simplex whose relative interior contains it. If \(g\in G\) fixes that point, then \(g\) preserves its support simplex. Since \(G\) is regular on projective points, no nonidentity element fixes a simplex vertex. Every nontrivial orbit of \(g\) on vertices therefore has length \(\operatorname{ord}(g)\), so the support simplex must have cardinality at least \(\operatorname{ord}(g)\). The maximal face size is three, and \(m=q^2+q+1\) is odd. Thus a nonidentity element can fix a geometric point only if it has order three and permutes the three vertices of a face cyclically. Such an element exists exactly when \(q\equiv1\pmod3\), since \(m\equiv0\pmod3\) exactly in that case.

Assume \(3\mid m\) and choose the unique order-three subgroup \(H\). Represent its generator by multiplication by \(\beta\in\mathbb F_{q^3}^\times\). It is projectively nontrivial, hence \(\beta\notin\mathbb F_q\). Since the extension has prime degree three, the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) has degree three. Therefore \(1,\beta,\beta^2\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_q\); multiplying by any nonzero \(v\) shows that every point orbit \(\{[v],[\beta v],[\beta^2v]\}\) is a noncollinear triple. Exactly \(m/3\) disjoint such triples occur. On each triangular face, the cyclic permutation fixes only its barycenter. No larger-order element fixes any point. Hence both nonidentity elements of \(H\) have exactly these \(m/3\) fixed points, while every other nonidentity group element acts without fixed points. Because \(G\) acts transitively on the points, it also acts transitively on these \(m/3\) triangles and their barycenters.

**3. Fundamental group.** Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem applies to the simply connected finite polyhedron and its finite simplicial action. It identifies \(\pi_1(Q_q)\) with \(G/N\), where \(N\) is normally generated by those elements with nonempty fixed-point sets. As \(G\) is cyclic, \(N\) is trivial when \(\varepsilon_q=0\), and equals \(H\cong C_3\) when \(\varepsilon_q=1\). Thus \(\pi_1(Q_q)\cong C_{m/3^{\varepsilon_q}}\). The first integral homology is its abelianization, the same cyclic group.

**4. Euler characteristic.** The numbers of vertices, edges and triangles of \(K_q\) are respectively
\[
f_0=m,\qquad f_1=\binom m2,\qquad
f_2=\binom m3-m\binom{q+1}{3}.
\]
Here each collinear triple belongs to a unique projective line, so subtraction has no multiplicities. The finite-group Euler-characteristic formula on a barycentric subdivision is
\[
\chi(Q_q)=\frac1m\sum_{g\in G}\chi(|K_q|^g).
\]
By step 2 all nonidentity summands vanish except, when \(\varepsilon_q=1\), the two elements of order three. Each has \(m/3\) isolated fixed points, so
\[
\chi(Q_q)=\frac{m-\binom m2+\binom m3-m\binom{q+1}{3}}m
+\frac{2\varepsilon_q}{3}
=1+b(q).
\]
Because \(Q_q\) is a connected two-dimensional CW complex, its second integral homology is a subgroup of its free cellular two-chain group and therefore has no torsion. Its first Betti number is zero because its fundamental group is finite. The Euler identity now shows \(H_2(Q_q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{b(q)}\); dimensionality gives all remaining groups. This also proves the integrality and nonnegativity of \(b(q)\).

## Verification
`verify_singer.py` is a standalone standard-library Python checker for the finite projective planes of orders \(2,3,4,5\). It constructs cyclic planar difference sets, verifies unique projective-line incidence for every point pair, enumerates all independent triples, tests all nonidentity rotations for invariant triangles, computes actual face-orbit counts, and recomputes quotient Euler characteristics independently by a barycentric-flag cell count and by the fixed-point formula. Recorded results are in `verification_output.txt`:

| \(q\) | \(m\) | Nonfree fixed triangles | Quotient Euler characteristic | \(b(q)\) | \(|\pi_1|\) predicted |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 7 | 0 | 2 | 1 | 7 |
| 3 | 13 | 0 | 13 | 12 | 13 |
| 4 | 21 | 7 | 45 | 44 | 7 |
| 5 | 31 | 0 | 111 | 110 | 31 |

The finite checks do **not** prove the all-prime-power theorem; that is established by the four steps above. The checker does not independently compute fundamental groups; that part is a consequence of the stated action hypotheses and Armstrong's theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
The existence of the Singer cycle and cyclic planar difference sets is classical; the first section of Huang–Schmidt (2008) gives the needed field and incidence model. The shellable, sphere-wedge homotopy type of matroid independence complexes is standard and is stated explicitly in Ardila–Castillo–Samper (arXiv first posted in 2014). Armstrong (1965) supplies the general theorem identifying the fundamental group of an orbit space.

A published Fano-matroid Singer-cycle calculation describes the **integral top-homology module of the seven-point covering complex**, \(\mathbb Z[C_7]\oplus I\); its coinvariants are \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/7\). That result concerns an integral representation, not a theorem for the **geometric orbit spaces** \(Q_q\) across all prime powers. A published Fano rational character calculation gives the invariant rational rank for the sevenfold subgroup, again only for \(q=2\). The present statement identifies a different topological object and the uniform order-three isotropy transition at \(q\equiv1\pmod3\). It does not claim a new method for matroid shellability or Armstrong's theorem.

## Limitations
This determines the fundamental group and complete additive integral homology of \(Q_q\), **not** its higher homotopy groups, its integral cohomology ring, or its homotopy decomposition. The action is specifically the Singer cyclic group on the Desarguesian plane, not the full projective collineation group, arbitrary cyclic projective planes, or a higher-rank projective geometry. A separate earlier source might state the same orbit-space formula; the described overlap searches did not identify one. Its originality assessment is a self-review, not an independent priority determination.
