# Six edge-orbit homotopy responses of the \(3\times4\) grid matching complex

## Finding

Let \(G=P_3\square P_4\) be the Cartesian rectangular grid, and let \(\mathcal M(G)\) be its ordinary matching complex. For every edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) of \(G\), write \(D_e=\mathcal M(G-e)\) and \(L_e=\mathcal M(G-\{u,v\})\), where the second operation removes both endpoints and their incident edges. The complete integral homotopy classification, by the six edge orbits, is

| Removed edge \(e\) | Orbit size | \(D_e\) | \(L_e\) |
|---|---:|---|---|
| \(v_{0,0}\) | 4 | \(\bigvee^5S^3\) | \(\bigvee^4S^2\) |
| \(v_{0,1}\) | 4 | \(\bigvee^7S^3\) | \(\bigvee^2S^2\) |
| \(h_{0,0}\) | 4 | \(\bigvee^7S^3\) | \(\bigvee^2S^2\) |
| \(h_{0,1}\) | 2 | \(\bigvee^5S^3\) | \(\bigvee^4S^2\) |
| \(h_{1,0}\) | 2 | \(\bigvee^7S^3\) | \(\bigvee^2S^2\) |
| \(h_{1,1}\) | 1 | \(\bigvee^8S^3\) | \(S^2\) |

Here \(v_{r,c}=\{(r,c),(r+1,c)\}\) and \(h_{r,c}=\{(r,c),(r,c+1)\}\). The orbit group is generated by row and column reversal. The intact matching complex satisfies \(\mathcal M(G)\simeq\bigvee^9S^3\). Moreover, for **every** edge, its natural deletion inclusion is a split homotopy summand:
\[
\mathcal M(G)\simeq D_e\vee\Sigma L_e,
\]
with the equivalence extending \(D_e\hookrightarrow\mathcal M(G)\) up to homotopy. In particular, the induced map on integral third homology is split injective, with cokernel \(\mathbb Z^{9-b(e)}\), where \(b(e)\in\{5,7,8\}\) is the indicated three-sphere count. Edge position therefore destroys exactly one, two, or four of the nine classes, according to its orbit.

## Assumptions and scope

The grid vertices are \((r,c)\) with \(0\le r\le2\) and \(0\le c\le3\). Edges join pairs whose coordinates differ by one in one position. A face of \(\mathcal M(H)\) is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint graph edges; the empty face is included in augmented enumeration. The parameter range is one edge deletion in this specific grid, not an asymptotic statement. All homotopy equivalences and homology calculations are integral, not just rational or mod two.

## Proof

**Exact enumeration and acyclic matchings.** The accompanying independently runnable `verify_grid34_defect.py` specifies the seventeen graph edges in a fixed order, recursively enumerates every face for the intact graph, each of the six representative deletions and the six corresponding endpoint-deletion links, and supplies one explicit vertex order for an augmented acyclic face matching on each complex. At each ordered vertex, it pairs each still-unmatched face not containing that vertex with its union with the vertex whenever both are present. The program *independently checks global acyclicity*, rather than assuming the greedy prescription is automatically acyclic: for every Hasse cover relation it reverses the matching covers and topologically sorts the complete resulting directed graph. The verification covers every face and cover. Every augmented matching pairs the empty face and leaves only the claimed number of critical faces, all of dimension three for the intact and deleted-edge complexes, and all of dimension two for the endpoint-deletion links.

Removing the pair containing the empty face creates one ordinary critical vertex and leaves the other critical faces unchanged; the empty face becomes a sink in the augmented orientation, so acyclicity is preserved. Forman's discrete Morse theorem now gives a CW model with one vertex and only the stated higher-dimensional cells. Every attaching map is constant up to homotopy, hence these CW complexes are the displayed wedges of spheres. The four-element row/column reflection group carries the six representative calculations to all seventeen edges. The program exhaustively verifies that their edge orbits have sizes \(4,4,4,2,2,1\), are disjoint and cover all edges.

**Inclusion and splitting.** A simplex of \(\mathcal M(G)\) either avoids \(e\), so belongs to \(D_e\), or contains \(e\), in which case its remaining edges form a simplex of \(L_e\). Therefore \(\mathcal M(G)=D_e\cup(e*L_e)\), with intersection \(L_e\). Since \(D_e\simeq\bigvee^{b(e)}S^3\), it is two-connected; as \(L_e\simeq\bigvee^{9-b(e)}S^2\), every map \(L_e\to D_e\) is nullhomotopic. The homotopy pushout therefore splits as \(D_e\vee\Sigma L_e\), respecting the inclusion of the first factor. In integral degree three, the inclusion is thus a split injection with the specified free cokernel.

**Numerical independence.** Separately from the face-cover acyclicity computation, the verifier calculates the signed matching polynomial of each input graph by an include/exclude recursion on graph edges and matches its Euler characteristic against the critical-cell count. This independently checks the alternating count, although the proof of the homotopy type uses the exact acyclic certificates.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_grid34_defect.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter. It uses no third-party dependencies, reconstructs the 13 simplicial complexes, verifies all 17 graph-edge positions by symmetry, counts each face and Hasse cover, and checks the global directed acyclic orientation and independent signed Euler-characteristic recursion. A recorded reference output is supplied as `verify_grid34_defect.out`. The concluding line is `GRID34_ALL_EDGE_DELETION_HOMOTOPY_PASS`. The verified augmented face counts are 823 for the intact complex; 625, 683, 605, 673, 691, 700 for the representative edge deletions; and 198, 140, 218, 150, 132, 123 for their links. The finite calculation is exhaustive for this fixed graph: no inference about infinite grid sizes is made.

## Relationship to prior work

Chauhan, Sharma and Shukla's preprint *Homology of matching complexes of \(3\times n\) grid graphs* (first public 2026-09-23, arXiv:2609.27366) establishes general connectivity and homology-vanishing information for **intact** three-row grids, not the exact edge-deletion profile or inclusion maps here. An earlier article of Goyal, Shukla and Singh concerning intact grids was retracted; none of its disputed global statements is a premise here. A published finite calculation, *Exact edge-defect homotopy of \(\mathcal M(P_3\square P_5)\) and \(\mathcal M(P_3\square P_6)\)* (2026-10-25), explicitly classifies widths five and six, not the width-four case. The width-four intact homotopy classification itself is background rather than the novel claim. Existing deletion–link cofibration machinery is also standard; the added work is the six precise link/deletion types and the resulting complete split-inclusion profile at width four.

## Limitations

Only single-edge deletions in the twelve-vertex \(3\times4\) grid are treated. No general formula for arbitrary width or multiple faults is claimed, and the splitting is not asserted to be canonically equivariant. The primary 2026 grid preprint was accessible at the abstract level, but its complete manuscript was not obtained during this comparison; an unindexed calculation or differently formulated result cannot be categorically excluded. The finite proof does not depend on that manuscript's results. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
