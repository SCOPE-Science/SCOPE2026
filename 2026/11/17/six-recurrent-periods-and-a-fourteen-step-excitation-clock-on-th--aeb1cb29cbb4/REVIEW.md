# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The model is specified by explicit Fano incidence coordinates and four-state synchronous transitions. Exhaustive forward-path and indegree-pruning classifiers agree on all sixty-four joint first-entry/primitive-period populations, and the table totals the entire \(4^{14}\) state set. Both source programs are embedded and replayable; no inference from a partial sample to all configurations is used. The proof is computer-assisted finite exhaustion, not a symbolic arbitrary-graph theorem.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Relevant historical Greenberg–Hastings sources treat lattice/threshold behavior or a three-color arbitrary-graph theory; a distinct three-state Heawood census has different state alphabet, different period set and a different transient cutoff. The inspected comparisons do not entail the four-state Heawood joint census. General historical full texts were not all accessible, leaving some unquantified priority risk; failed bibliographic searches alone are not used as novelty proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The Heawood graph is the smallest cubic graph of girth six, a canonical incidence graph. Exact orbit periods, sharp entrance depth, attracting populations and a full sixty-four-class finite dynamical decomposition yield a substantial, reproducible invariant. The contrast with three-state dynamics is nontrivial: the higher refractory level removes two primitive periods while extending the maximal transient from ten to fourteen.

**Limitations.** The graph is fixed, the update alphabet has exactly four states, and no universal graph-theoretic classification is claimed. A separate external mathematical audit remains to be done.
