In the canonical Euclidean symmetric-edge polytope of an \(n\)-cycle, deleting any one vertex has squared Hausdorff loss \(1/2,4/5,9/10\) for \(n=3,4,5\), then exactly \(11/12\) for every \(n\ge6\). At this sharp six-vertex cutoff, the nearest-point face stabilizes to a tetrahedron spanned by the four oriented roots on the two cycle edges nearest the deleted edge.
