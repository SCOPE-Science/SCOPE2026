# Sixteen \(2\)-spheres in the Coxeter-conjugation quotient of the type-\(H_4\) noncrossing complex

## Finding
Let \(W\) be the real Coxeter group of type \(H_4\), let \(c=s_1s_2s_3s_4\) be the Coxeter element in its standard simple-reflection system with edge labels \(5,3,3\), and put \(P=[1,c]_T\) for the absolute-reflection-order interval. Let \(X=|\Delta(P\setminus\{1,c\})|\). The cyclic group generated by \(c\) acts on \(P\) by conjugation, and hence on \(X\). The ordinary geometric orbit space is

\[
Q=X/\langle c\rangle\simeq\bigvee^{16}S^2.
\]

In particular, \(\pi_1(Q)=0\), \(\widetilde H_2(Q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{16}\), and every other reduced integral homology group vanishes. The statement concerns the ordinary quotient, not homotopy-orbit or Borel equivariant homology.

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficients and representations are exact. Write \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). In the ordered simple-root basis, \(s_i(e_i)=-e_i\), \(s_i(e_j)=e_j+\gamma_{ij}e_i\) for \(j\ne i\), where \(\gamma_{12}=\gamma_{21}=\varphi\), \(\gamma_{23}=\gamma_{32}=\gamma_{34}=\gamma_{43}=1\), and every other \(\gamma_{ij}=0\). These matrices lie in \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]^{4\times4}\), faithfully realize the order-\(14400\) type-\(H_4\) reflection group, and determine \(c\) unambiguously.

For \(w\in W\), its reflection length is \(\ell_T(w)=\operatorname{rank}(I-w)\), by the real-reflection-group reflection-length theorem. Thus
\[
P=\{w\in W:\ell_T(w)+\ell_T(c^{-1}w)=4\},\qquad
u\leq v\Longleftrightarrow\ell_T(\nu)+\ell_T(\nu^{-1}v)=\ell_T(v).
\]
The equality using \(c^{-1}w\) is valid because \(\ell_T(w^{-1}c)=\ell_T(c^{-1}w)\). The group \(\langle c\rangle\) has order \(30\) and acts effectively by conjugation through a group of order \(15\), since \(c^{15}=-I\) is central.

## Proof
**Exact interval and orbit count.** An exhaustive breadth-first traversal in the four displayed simple reflections constructs exactly \(14400\) distinct matrices, using the exact ring law \(\varphi^2=\varphi+1\). Applying the two rank tests defining \(P\), and the exact rank tests for all comparable pairs of unequal ranks, gives the rank distribution
\[
(1,60,158,60,1).
\]
Its proper-part order complex has \(278\) vertices, \(1395\) edges and \(1350\) triangles. The effective order-\(15\) conjugation action has four vertex orbits of size \(15\) in rank one; in rank two, ten orbits of size \(15\), one of size \(3\) and one of size \(5\); and in rank three, four orbits of size \(15\). Every edge and triangle orbit has size \(15\). All these enumerations and the arithmetic over \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\) are checked by the complete standalone script `verify_h4.py` included with this result.

**Simply connected covering complex.** The noncrossing lattice of every finite real reflection group is EL-shellable (Athanasiadis–Brady–Watt, Theorem 1.1), including type \(H_4\). Its proper-part order complex is therefore homotopy equivalent to a wedge of \(2\)-spheres. In particular, \(X\) is connected and simply connected. Direct cell counts give \(\chi(X)=278-1395+1350=233\).

**The quotient fundamental group.** The rank-two conjugation orbits of sizes \(3\) and \(5\) yield vertex stabilizers in the effective cyclic group \(C_{15}\) of orders \(5\) and \(3\). These stabilizers generate \(C_{15}\). Since the simplicial action on the finite, simply connected complex has no simplex inversions (ranks distinguish all vertices of any chain), Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem gives
\[
\pi_1(Q)\cong C_{15}/\langle C_5,C_3\rangle=1.
\]

**Euler characteristic and homotopy type.** Quotient cells are rank-respecting orbits of simplices and occur in numbers \((20,93,90)\), so \(\chi(Q)=20-93+90=17\). Independently, Burnside's fixed-point Euler formula gives
\[
\chi(Q)=\frac{233+4\cdot3+2\cdot5}{15}=17.
\]
Indeed, four nonidentity powers fix exactly three isolated vertices, two other nonidentity powers fix exactly five isolated vertices, and the remaining eight nonidentity powers fix none. Since \(Q\) is a simply connected finite two-dimensional CW complex, \(H_2(Q;\mathbb Z)\) is free of rank \(\chi(Q)-1=16\), with no other positive reduced integral homology. Hurewicz realizes a basis of \(H_2\) by maps from \(2\)-spheres; the induced map \(\bigvee^{16}S^2\to Q\) is an integral homology isomorphism between simply connected CW complexes and hence a homotopy equivalence by the homological Whitehead theorem.

## Verification
Run `python verify_h4.py` in an environment satisfying `requirements.txt` (SymPy is used only for rational ranks of the quotient boundary matrices). The script regenerates all group elements and intervals without external data and certifies \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), the rational boundary ranks \((19,74)\), the Burnside fixed-simplices identity, the effective-action period, and the displayed orbit and Euler counts. It terminates with `ALL_EXACT_CHECKS_PASS`. A record of an actual successful invocation is included in `execution_record.txt`.

The preliminary modular rank filter uses the ring homomorphism \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\to\mathbb F_{101}\) sending \(\varphi\mapsto23\), for which \(23^2-23-1=0\pmod{101}\). Reduction cannot increase matrix rank, so any true member of \(P\) passes the filter; every retained candidate is then independently checked by *exact* nonzero-minor rank over \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\). Thus the filter does not assume a finite-field rank equality and cannot discard a genuine interval element.

## Relationship to prior work
Athanasiadis–Brady–Watt prove the shellability and resulting wedge-of-spheres statement for the *unquotiented* proper part. Bessis–Reiner prove cyclic sieving for the conjugation action on the *elements* of the noncrossing interval. Reading counts chains of such intervals, and Reiner–Ripoll–Stump analyze Coxeter-element conjugacy and associated poset isomorphisms. Those results provide the structural input and context but do not state a simply connected quotient with exactly sixteen two-sphere summands. In particular, counts of fixed elements or unsymmetrized chains do not alone identify the quotient fundamental group and integral homotopy type.

## Limitations
This is a classification of the single exceptional Coxeter type \(H_4\); it is not an all-rank theorem for other noncrystallographic or exceptional groups. The finite enumeration is exact and exhaustive for the stated group, while source coverage does not rule out every unindexed equivalent historical formulation. No independent mathematical review is claimed.
