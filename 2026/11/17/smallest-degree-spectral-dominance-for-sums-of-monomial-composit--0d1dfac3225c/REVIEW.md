# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The monomial basis gives exact orthogonal columns for each individual composition map. For \(\gamma>1\), differentiating the explicit logarithmic weight ratio proves eventual ordering and its sharp decay. Noncompactness for \(\gamma\le1\) follows from a weakly null basis and exact norms of finite-sum columns. The nonorthogonality between columns of different maps is resolved by Ky Fan inequalities and a carefully chosen sublinear index \(n^\theta\), yielding a genuine two-sided singular-value asymptotic. A second-order logarithmic expansion proves the exact Schatten threshold and critical residue. Independent finite diagnostics supplement, but do not replace, these arguments.

## Originality: PASS (provisional publication priority)
The 2026 weighted Hardy-space paper characterizes equality of *sets of symbols* with compact/Schatten composition operators; it does not provide a spectral-dominance theorem for finite nonorthogonal sums on these weights. Earlier work on finite sums considers classical Hardy or Bergman/Orlicz norms, and available mathematical-record hits concern lacunary weights, two-tap **weighted** compositions in a different asymptotic regime, or monomial cross-adjoint commutators. Their statements do not imply the sharp smallest-degree singular-value law and the coefficient-independent critical residue in this setting. Absolute priority is not established; some relevant older full texts could not be inspected.

## Mathematical value: PASS
Finite linear combinations of composition operators and the distinction between compactness and Schatten membership are motivated research objects. The theorem describes a full natural scale of coefficient Hilbert spaces, gives an exact spectral asymptotic even though output degrees overlap, identifies distinct compactness and Schatten thresholds, and computes an explicit weak-trace residue insensitive to higher-degree terms. This is more than the diagonal monomial calculation: the singular-value dominance step requires a nontrivial comparison at sublinear counting scales.

## Scope and remaining risk
This is a complete classification only for fixed finite sums of monomial maps on the displayed logarithmic weights. A general theorem in older operator-ideal literature might subsume its Weyl-perturbation step; that remains a publication-priority qualification. No independent audit is claimed.
