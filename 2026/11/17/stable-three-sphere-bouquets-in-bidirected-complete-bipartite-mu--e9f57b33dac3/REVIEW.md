# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The four-slot encoding exactly characterizes directed linear forests for this graph: an allowed set has at most one incoming and one outgoing arc at each hub, cannot repeat the corresponding leaf ports, and has no directed two- or four-cycle. These are precisely the five exclusions in the proof. The stage-by-stage lexicographic matching is acyclic by the earliest-upward-pivot gradient-path argument. The fresh-tail lemma is proved simultaneously with an explicit bound of five extra leaf labels per processed type, yielding the unrestricted result for widths at least twenty. The remaining widths six through nineteen were exhaustively checked from the literal face predicate and matching; for six and seven a second graph-based face check and a full directed-Hasse acyclicity test passed. The reduced Euler calculation fixes the top critical-cell count. No experimental inference is used for unbounded widths.

## Originality: PASS (best knowledge; residual risk)
Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith (arXiv:2208.04656v1, Section 3.2) treat one-way complete bipartite digraphs, all arcs directed from the first part to the second. Their matching-complex interpretation does not cover this bidirected digraph. The closest more recent multipath papers concern bidirected paths/cycles (Caputi–Collari–Smith, arXiv:2601.05670) and directed-linear-forest conflict graphs under additional hypotheses (Deshpande–Sawant, arXiv:2602.14936). Neither supplies this formula for a fully bidirected two-by-many complete bipartite graph with directed four-cycles. Previously recorded finite widths four, five and six do not imply the infinite family, and the present result's novelty is specifically the uniform range beyond the known width-six calculation. The Morse complex of the underlying graph, ordinary matching complexes and two-matching complexes are different constructions. No equal or stronger full-range statement was located in accessible searches, but an unindexed equivalent or broader theorem could exist.

## Mathematical value: PASS
A natural two-hub complete-bipartite family is an elementary dense test class for multipath complexes, yet its low-width examples contain multiple homology dimensions and torsion phenomena. The theorem identifies a sharp stable three-dimensional sphere-wedge range with an explicit quartic multiplicity, giving a structural result for arbitrarily many leaves and explaining why finite examples stabilize. The value is the uniform homotopy classification rather than a numerical gain in a single finite table.

## Scope and limitations
The proof is nonequivariant and requires both orientations of every edge. No claim is made about other complete bipartite part sizes, missing arcs, or integral symmetry modules. The exact finite checks do not themselves establish the infinite case; the latter requires the fresh-tail lemma. A stronger unindexed prior classification remains possible.
