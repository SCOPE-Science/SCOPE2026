# Stochastic median-radius majorization and exact tomography of metric stars

## Finding
For any finite metric star with \(m\ge3\) strictly positive arms of lengths \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\), put \(L=\sum_i a_i\). Choose \(2k+1\) points independently and uniformly by intrinsic arclength, for any integer \(k\ge1\). Their unique geodesic median \(M\) minimizes the sum of distances to the sampled points. Write \(R=d(M,o)\), where \(o\) is the common hub, and
\[
B_k(q)=\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(2k+1,q)\ge k+1\}
=\sum_{j=k+1}^{2k+1}\binom{2k+1}{j}q^j(1-q)^{2k+1-j}.
\]
Then the **entire median-radius survival function** is
\[
\boxed{\Pr(R>t)=\sum_{i=1}^m B_k\!\left(\frac{(a_i-t)_+}{L}\right)\quad (t\ge0).}
\]
For fixed \(m,L,k\), majorization of the positive arm-length vector increases \(R\) in first-order stochastic order, with strict inequality at threshold zero for every nontrivial majorization. Thus equal arms uniquely minimize the median radius in stochastic order; equivalently, they uniquely maximize the probability that the median is the hub, whose sharp upper bound is \(1-mB_k(1/m)\).

The full law of \(R\) also **uniquely recovers the number and lengths of all arms**, even if their lengths coincide, without knowing the scale or arm count. Put \(c_k=(k+1)!\binom{2k+1}{k+1}\). At each distinct arm length \(s_j>0\), let \(J_j>0\) be the absolute right-minus-left jump in the \((k+1)\)-st derivative of the survival function. These and only these positive points are derivative jumps, and
\[
\boxed{L=\left(\frac{c_k}{\sum_j s_jJ_j}\right)^{1/k},\qquad n_j=\frac{J_jL^{k+1}}{c_k},}
\]
where \(n_j\) is the multiplicity of arm length \(s_j\). These results hold simultaneously for all odd sample sizes at least three; neither prior random two-point distance tomography nor deterministic tree median theory is claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
A metric star is obtained by identifying the zero endpoints of \(m\) Euclidean intervals \([0,a_i]\). Its distance is shortest-path arclength, and its only branch point is the hub \(o\). Every \(a_i\) is strictly positive, \(m\ge3\), and \(k\) is a fixed positive integer. Each of the \(2k+1\) sample points has probability mass \(a_i/L\) on arm \(i\) and conditional uniform arclength there. The median is the unique minimizer of the sum of intrinsic distances. The population survival function is assumed exactly known in the inverse assertion; no claim is made about noisy estimation.

For real vectors of equal sum, \(b\) majorizes \(a\) when its decreasingly rearranged partial sums are no smaller than those of \(a\). 'Strict first-order stochastic ordering' here means pointwise greater-or-equal survival functions with strict inequality at least at \(t=0\). Ordinary derivatives are interpreted piecewise and jumps of piecewise smooth derivatives by one-sided limits.

## Proof
**1. Median and majority components.** At any point \(x\) in a metric tree, the one-sided change in total sample distance when moving from \(x\) into a component of its complement is the number of samples outside that component minus the number inside it. Thus the unique median of an odd number \(2k+1\) of distinct points lies beyond distance \(t\) along arm \(i\) exactly when that arm contains at least \(k+1\) sampled points at radial distance greater than \(t\). These events are disjoint for distinct arms. Since a point belongs to that outer subarm with probability \(q_i(t)=(a_i-t)_+/L\), the number in the outer subarm is binomial, and summing the disjoint majority events gives the displayed exact survival function. Odd sample size ensures uniqueness, with probability one under continuous sampling.

**2. Stochastic majorization despite the binomial-tail inflection.** Differentiating the elementary binomial tail gives
\[
 B_k'(q)=\frac{(2k+1)!}{k!^2}\,q^k(1-q)^k,\qquad 0\le q\le1.
\]
Fix \(t\ge0\) and consider two arms whose lengths are unequal. During a transfer of a positive amount from the shorter to the longer arm, let \(q\ge r\ge0\) be their remaining outer-subarm probabilities. Always \(q+r\le1\). Where both are positive,
\[
 q(1-q)-r(1-r)=(q-r)(1-q-r)\ge0,
\]
so \(B_k'(q)\ge B_k'(r)\). Where the shorter outer subarm has disappeared, its derivative with respect to its length is zero while the longer one still has nonnegative derivative. Therefore every inequality-increasing transfer weakly raises \(\sum_iB_k(q_i(t))\) for every \(t\). Majorization is generated by such transfers, proving the global stochastic inequality. At \(t=0\), all arms remain positive and, because there is a third positive arm, \(q+r<1\). Every nontrivial transfer with unequal endpoints then raises the survival strictly. Consequently the equal vector is the unique stochastic minimum, and the hub atom is maximal exactly at equality. (For equal starting endpoints, strictness holds immediately after a positive transfer.)

**3. Positive-knot singularities and inverse reconstruction.** Near zero,
\[
 B_k(q)=\binom{2k+1}{k+1}q^{k+1}+O(q^{k+2}).
\]
Hence at the terminal point \(t=a_i\), the corresponding summand has exactly a nonzero jump of its \((k+1)\)-st derivative, with absolute size \(c_k/L^{k+1}\), where \(c_k=(k+1)!\binom{2k+1}{k+1}\). All arm contributions have the same sign of derivative jump; repeated lengths therefore add without cancellation. Between knots the survival function is polynomial and smooth. If \(s_j\) is a distinct length with multiplicity \(n_j\), its jump has magnitude \(J_j=c_kn_j/L^{k+1}\). Summing \(s_jJ_j\) and using \(\sum_j n_js_j=L\) gives
\[
 \sum_j s_jJ_j=\frac{c_k}{L^k}.
\]
This yields the scale formula and then each integer multiplicity exactly. The number of arms is \(m=\sum_j n_j\), so the unlabelled star is uniquely recovered.

**4. Sharpness and degenerate limits.** For every genuine \(m\)-arm star the hub atom is strictly positive. If one arm approaches total length while the others shrink to zero, the hub atom tends to zero. Thus zero is its unattained infimum over positive length vectors; the equal-arm upper bound is attained uniquely.

## Relationship to prior work
Dahl (2007) studies **majorization of deterministic vertex-distance vectors** and associated tree centers, rather than stochastic dominance of the **random odd-sample geodesic median radius** as arm lengths vary. Classical deterministic median theory explains the majority-component rule, which is used here rather than claimed as new. Wei, Walteros and Batta (2019) study forward distributions of **two-point distances** on networks; an existing star-graph inverse result reconstructs arm lengths from that different two-point metric statistic. Such reconstruction does not imply that an independently defined, one-dimensional random-median-radius distribution has the explicit same global injectivity. Existing metric-star stochastic distance-moment majorization results likewise concern two-point distances, not multi-sample median locations. The present simultaneous full-law stochastic order and exact inverse from the median-radius law are the distinct claims. Relevant published-result records and primary source materials were compared; historical completeness is not asserted.

## Limitations
The theorems require a finite star with at least three positive arms, independent *arclength-uniform* sampling, a known odd sample size and the complete exact population law. The strictness of stochastic order is not pointwise for all thresholds (both tails are zero above the largest arm), but is strict for at least the threshold zero under any nontrivial majorization. No conditioning-rate, finite-sample statistical stability or inference procedure under observation noise is proved. Priority against unindexed tree-median literature remains provisional; independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
