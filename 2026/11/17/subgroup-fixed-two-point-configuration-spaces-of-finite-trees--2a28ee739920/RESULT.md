# Subgroup-fixed two-point configuration spaces of finite trees

## Finding
Let \(T\) be a finite tree with at least one edge, let \(X=|T|\), and let \(H\leq\operatorname{Aut}(T)\) act on \(X\) by the canonical linear action on edges. Write \(\operatorname{UConf}_2(X)\) for the space of two-element subsets of \(X\) with the quotient topology from ordered distinct pairs. Put \(F=X^H\), which is a nonempty closed subtree, possibly one point. For each nontrivial homomorphism \(\chi:H\to C_2\), put \(K_\chi=\ker\chi\) and \(Y_\chi=X^{K_\chi}\setminus F\). If \(g\in H\setminus K_\chi\), its restriction to \(X^{K_\chi}\) is an involution, with fixed-point set exactly \(F\). It pairs the components of \(Y_\chi\), with no invariant component. Choose one component \(C\) from each pair \(\{C,gC\}\). Then there is a canonical decomposition into open-and-closed sectors, with homeomorphisms
\[
 \operatorname{UConf}_2(X)^H\ \cong\ \operatorname{UConf}_2(F)\ \sqcup\ \coprod_{\chi\ne1}\ \coprod_{\{C,gC\}} C.
\]
In particular, every component outside \(\operatorname{UConf}_2(F)\) is contractible. If \(F\) has at least two points, the first sector is connected and homotopy equivalent to a wedge of \(\beta(F)=\sum_{v:\deg_F(v)\ge3}\binom{\deg_F(v)-1}{2}\) circles. If \(F\) is a singleton, this sector is empty. Define \(b(H)=\frac12\sum_{\chi\ne1}\#\pi_0(Y_\chi)\). The entire fixed locus therefore has \(b(H)+1\) components if \(F\) is nondegenerate, and \(b(H)\) components if \(F\) is one point; all integral homology above dimension one vanishes, and \(H_1\) is free of rank \(\beta(F)\).

For example, on the five-arm star, let \(H=C_2\) swap exactly two arms and fix the other three. Then \(F\) is a three-arm star and the nontrivial character contributes one contractible component: \(\operatorname{UConf}_2(X)^H\simeq S^1\sqcup\{\mathrm{point}\}\). Thus even an involution-fixed locus need not be contractible or connected.

## Assumptions and scope
The tree is finite and nonempty, with any positive edge lengths invariant under the prescribed graph automorphisms (equal lengths along automorphism orbits). Vertices of degree two are harmless; geometric edge midpoints are allowed, including when an automorphism reverses an edge. The group \(H\) may be noncyclic or act nonfaithfully on a subdivided realization. All homomorphisms to \(C_2\) are considered, including those with empty corresponding sectors. The empty topological space is allowed when no configuration is fixed. The theorem concerns genuine point-set geometric fixed loci, not fixed homology classes or an invariant-cell subcomplex.

## Proof
**(1) Invariant-pair permutation sectors.** Every unordered pair \(p=\{x,y\}\) fixed by \(H\) determines a unique homomorphism \(\chi_p:H\to\operatorname{Sym}(p)\cong C_2\), recording whether each element fixes or interchanges its points. The map \(p\mapsto\chi_p\) is locally constant: choose disjoint small neighborhoods of \(x\) and \(y\), and use finiteness of \(H\) to restrict the configuration neighborhood until every element preserves its original interchange behavior. Its fibers are therefore open and closed in the fixed locus. The trivial fiber consists exactly of the unordered configurations in \(F=X^H\).

**(2) Nontrivial characters.** Fix \(\chi\ne1\), put \(K=\ker\chi\), and choose \(g\in H\setminus K\). Necessarily \(x,y\in X^K\), and the interchange by \(g\) forces \(y=gx\ne x\). Conversely, every \(x\in X^K\setminus F\) defines an invariant pair \(\{x,gx\}\) of character \(\chi\), since \(K\) fixes \(x\) and \(g^2\in K\). Thus the sector is homeomorphic to \((X^K\setminus F)/(H/K)\). The map is a homeomorphism, rather than merely a bijection, because the ordered-to-unordered two-point configuration map is a two-sheeted covering, and its pullback to the graph \(x\mapsto(x,gx)\) is precisely the free \(H/K\)-orbit covering.

**(3) Tree geometry and components.** Fixed points of each tree automorphism form a closed convex subtree: for fixed points \(a,b\), the unique arc \([a,b]\) is fixed pointwise by an isometry preserving its endpoints. Intersection over a subgroup is again convex. All automorphisms of a finite tree fix the central vertex or midpoint of the central edge, so \(F\ne\varnothing\). Let \(S=X^K\), a subtree invariant under \(g\). The induced involution on \(S\) has fixed set \(F\). Each component \(C\) of \(S\setminus F\) is a convex, contractible subtree. If \(gC=C\), the unique midpoint of \([x,gx]\subset C\) would be fixed by \(g\), contrary to \(C\cap F=\varnothing\). Hence every component belongs to a distinct exchanged pair \(\{C,gC\}\), and the quotient is homeomorphic to a disjoint union of copies of \(C\). This establishes the displayed homeomorphism and the component count, including the case \(F\) is a singleton.

**(4) The sole potentially cyclic sector.** When \(F\) is a nondegenerate finite tree, its ordinary two-point unordered configuration space is connected and homotopy equivalent to a graph. The critical-cell description and count in Farley–Sabalka's tree-configuration discrete Morse theorem, specialized to two points, give one critical zero-cell, no critical cells in degree two or higher, and \(\binom{d(v)-1}{2}\) critical one-cells for each degree-\(d(v)\) branch vertex. Hence it is a wedge of exactly \(\beta(F)\) circles. If \(F\) is one point, no distinct pair of its points exists. Since the other sectors are contractible, the assertions about integral homology follow.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python program checks the invariant-pair/character decomposition independently on the edge-midpoint subdivision of every unlabelled six-vertex tree. It constructs genuine graph automorphisms and generated subgroups; for each fixed unordered sample pair, it computes the actual transposition homomorphism and verifies that all such pairs are accounted for by the claimed fixed-subtree/paired-component sectors. It tests 194 group actions, 2,014 invariant sample pairs, and 213 nontrivially exchanged sample pairs. This finite test is only a check of the decomposition on sampled points; the topological statements for all finite trees and all points are proved above.

Reproduce with Python 3 and NetworkX version 3.6.1: `python verify_tree_fixed.py`. Expected stdout is stored in `verify_tree_fixed_output.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work
Farley and Sabalka (2004/2005) prove general nonequivariant graph braid-space discrete Morse results, including the tree two-strand wedge rank used in step (4). Rourke and Sanderson (2000) develop equivariant configuration spaces on manifolds and loop-space comparison theorems; their setting does not give this all-subgroup geometric fixed-locus classification for finite trees. A publicly indexed result titled *The full automorphism character of two-point configuration spaces of finite trees* gives a complete **rational homology representation** of the ambient configuration space. That representation does not imply the topology or number of components of individual subgroup fixed loci. The new statement is a point-set decomposition using index-two characters and complementary fixed-subtree branches; no new claim is made about the previously established ordinary tree-configuration Betti ranks.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to unordered configurations of **two** distinct points on finite trees. For a general graph, a component of \(X^{\ker\chi}\setminus X^H\) can be invariant under the involution and have nontrivial topology; the contractible paired-branch conclusion then fails. Higher particle counts admit nontrivial permutation actions beyond \(C_2\). The result determines fixed-space topology, not the full quotient space by \(H\) and not an equivariant homotopy equivalence of ambient spaces. The closest indexed general tree-automorphism character result was available through its public summary rather than a complete accessible manuscript; implicit equivalence in other literature remains a priority risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
