# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The isometric root-plus-weighted-difference identification is an explicit bijection onto real R ⊕_1 ℓ∞. The finite row expression yields exact operator norm and finite-rank upper bounds. The disjoint-support lower witnesses are weakly null in ℓ∞ (bounded partial signed sums prove this), hence every compact operator annihilates them asymptotically. Both inequalities give the unrestricted compact-operator essential norm. Polynomial α=1 is the divergent-harmonic boundary; α>1 has uniformly summable tails. The alternating symbols satisfy the exact limit identities; 90 independent finite-rational profiles corroborate row algebra but are not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (best knowledge, qualified).** The assessed claim is the unrestricted big-space essential norm for arbitrary positive edge weights and its α=1 summability/compactness transition, not the known c0 tail-row lemma, the 2013 α=1 compactness criterion, or a general row-coordinate substitution. The closest 2026 published little-space result explicitly excludes nonseparable big spaces. In the summable regime, the new compact big-space example fails to preserve the little space, so it is not a specialization of that theorem. Generic matrix essential-norm technology remains a genuine terminology/priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The exact distance to every compact operator answers a natural unresolved counterpart of the earlier multiplier estimates for a nonseparable function space. The summable-versus-nonsummable reciprocal-weight transition gives complete compactness tests across all polynomial weights, with a sharp qualitative boundary: for α>1 symbol decay suffices and compact multipliers may not preserve the little space, whereas α≤1 explicit decaying symbols have essential norm 2. This is structural rather than a single arbitrary parameter computation.

The closest literature is Allen–Colonna–Easley, *Multiplication Operators on the Weighted Lipschitz Space of a Tree* (2013), Section 8; Colonna–Martínez-Avendaño, *Composition Operators on the Little Lipschitz Space of a Rooted Tree* (arXiv:2410.14714, 2024); and the published 2026 little weighted Lipschitz multiplier theorem. The 2013 paper provides upper/lower bounds for the weight \(c_v=|v|\); the 2026 theorem is an exact **little**-space result, with an explicit exclusion of big-space claims. In the summable-weight example, the compact big-space multiplier does not preserve little, so the earlier little-space result cannot supply this case by specialization. The main remaining historical-priority risk is a general matrix-operator result equivalent to the new double-tail formula.

Limitations: real scalars, infinite locally finite rooted trees, arbitrary positive weights and the stated sum norm; no result for general graph metrics. Exact finite tests check row algebra, not infinitely many vertices. Mathematical proof is analytic; independent validation has not occurred.
