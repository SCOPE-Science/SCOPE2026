# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For \(n\ge5\), the Alexander dual of the ordinary three-cut complex contains exactly \(2n\) vertices, \(\binom{2n}{2}\) edges, \(6n\) connected-path triangles, and \(n\) square tetrahedra. Each of the \(6n\) triangles participates in a uniform valid elementary-collapse pair; this leaves a connected graph with \(2n(n-3)\) edges and first homology rank \(2n^2-8n+1\). Equivariant Alexander duality introduces the sign of the full vertex permutation. Signed simplex fixed-point counts establish the stated characters for all rotations and both reflection-axis types, including the parity dependence in the square-tetrahedron contribution. Averaging gives the invariant dimension \(\lfloor(n-3)/2\rfloor\). The standalone script checks the actual collapse property for 1,200 pairs, character formulas for 800 group elements, and directly constructed ordinary-cut-complex Lefschetz numbers for 72 group elements. These checks corroborate rather than replace the uniform proof. Neither high-dimensional integer lattice action nor nonspecified graph automorphisms are determined.

## Originality — PASS against inspected literature
The already known ordinary three-cut sphere-wedge homotopy type of the same Möbius-ladder family is acknowledged without novelty claims; it entails only the dimension, not the action of the dihedral group. Bayer et al.'s foundational and sequel papers concern cut complexes, but their inspected pertinent results do not provide this family-wide character. The general square-free and edge-disjoint-square results do not apply because the Möbius ladder has squares sharing antipodal edges, and they do not calculate this action. The known dihedral character for *circular* ladders concerns a different graph family; independence and perfect-matching homology also concern different simplicial complexes. Full texts of the two foundational papers and relevant public mathematical results were inspected where available; the later independence/perfect-matching preprint was accessible only at abstract level. No found published statement implies the exact four parity-dependent reflection formulas or the full rotational character; absence from searches cannot prove global novelty. The strongest residual risk is an unindexed equivalent character formula or a stronger overlooked representation theorem.

## Value — PASS
The Möbius ladder is a canonical twisted cubic graph family, and ordinary three-cut homology was already known in all widths. Classifying the natural entire dihedral action is a genuinely additional topological invariant: scalar Betti numbers cannot determine individual rotation/reflection traces or the rank of invariant homology. The precise fixed-subspace dimension distinguishes Möbius ladders from the corresponding previously classified circular ladders, particularly at even widths. The answer holds for every width \(n\ge5\), not an arbitrary finite sample. The result is limited to rational characters and makes no larger equivariant homotopy claim.
