# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The reflection acts simplicially on chords, and constant barycentric coordinates on chord orbits identify its geometric fixed space exactly with an orbit independence complex. Internal and pairwise chord conflicts are all tested, so no invariant faces are omitted. The complete degree-by-degree integer chain complexes are generated by exhaustive enumeration; all boundary squares vanish; exact Smith reductions give unit nonzero invariants and ranks \(20,61,38,10,1\) in the 12-gon side-axis case. This proves integral \(\widetilde H_1=\mathbb Z^4\) and \(\widetilde H_2=\mathbb Z\), and exhaustive enumeration of both dihedral reflection classes for each smaller polygon establishes first occurrence. Finite exhaustive verification suffices for the stated finite cutoff, but not for arbitrary larger polygons.

## Originality — PASS relative to the checked literature
Jonsson, *Simplicial Complexes of Graphs*, §16.3, Theorems 16.7–16.9 (2007 first public edition), establishes the ordinary topology and homology support of polygon noncrossing-matching complexes. Its statement does not determine any reflection-fixed-space homology. Braun–Ehrenborg's noncrossing-diagonal complex permits shared endpoints and is different. Indexed reflection-fixed results for wheel, cycle, grid and Möbius-ladder matching complexes use different underlying matching constraints and do not imply the dodecagon noncrossing-matching calculation. No checked source supplies an equivalent calculation, but unindexed equivariant treatments remain a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The noncrossing-matching complex is a natural, previously studied object and the polygon's two reflection axes are canonical symmetries. The exact first mixed-degree geometric fixed locus answers a boundary question about when symmetry-fixed topology becomes more intricate than a single homology degree. It distinguishes the fixed topology (degrees 1 and 2) from the ambient dodecagon's known homology support (degree 3), supplies the other-axis contrast, and is a reproducible natural cutoff rather than an arbitrary small example.
