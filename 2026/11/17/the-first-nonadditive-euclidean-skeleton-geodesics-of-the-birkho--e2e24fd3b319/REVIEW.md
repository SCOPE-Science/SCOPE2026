# Scientific review of the weighted Birkhoff skeleton threshold

## Correctness
PASS. The known single-cycle adjacency criterion converts each edge into a cycle factor of Frobenius length \(\sqrt{2k}\). A proved support-intersection lemma forces \(k+l\ge9\) for two-cycle factorizations of three disjoint transpositions. Parity, strict square-root comparisons and the three-edge cost floor establish the exact optimum. The equality case gives precisely \(2^3\cdot2\) ordered decompositions for each of two possible cycle-length orders. The at-most-five support statement follows from complete cycle-type enumeration, the Euclidean triangle inequality and parity. Finite checkers for orders six and seven passed, but the infinite claim rests on proof.

## Originality
PASS with priority risk. The primary accessible Huang et al. manuscript establishes adjacency and develops clique/independence structure; it does not formulate this Frobenius-weighted path metric. Cardinal–Steiner investigate a different (unweighted step-count) shortest-path problem for general matching polytopes, and the Cação et al. generalized graph work concerns random and quantum walks. Targeted searches for weighted cycle factorization and Birkhoff Euclidean skeleton metrics located no statement implying this six-point threshold or 32-path count. Failed search alone does not prove priority; equivalent terminology may exist.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Weighted graph geometry is distinct from both Euclidean chord distances and the familiar unweighted diameter-two structure. The result locates the smallest support at which direct disjoint-cycle moves cease to minimize geometric path length, supplies an exact nonadditive geodesic and counts every minimizing path, uniformly in dimension. This provides a concrete structural boundary for metric studies of assignment-polytope skeletons.

## Limits
No formula for arbitrary cycle types or weighted diameter; external independent priority and proof audit have not been conducted. Nonmatching weighted Cayley metrics and older factorization literature may contain equivalents.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
