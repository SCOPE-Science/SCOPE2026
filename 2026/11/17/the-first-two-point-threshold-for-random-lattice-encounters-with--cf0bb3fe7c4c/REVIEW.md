# Scientific review: first two-point occupancy of a random convex body

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact low-diameter distribution follows from the absence of unit-distance triangles in the square lattice, lattice-translation unfolding, and the first two factorial moments. The curvature coefficient follows from a nondegenerate quadratic radial maximum of the difference body, the local \(3/2\)-power contact-lens area, and integration over four angular windows. The normalized angular-window weight yields the claimed beta limit. The separate disk calculation uses the exact circle-overlap formula. Numerical quadrature of exact independent formulas corroborated the ellipse and disk coefficients; it was not used as a proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical uncertainty.** Bárány (2006) and Roldán-Pensado (2013) study lattice-free random rigid-motion bodies, with the inspected material focused on large-area/small-width bounds rather than the first pair threshold. Bäsel (2013) counts triangular **cells intersected**, rather than integer **points contained**. Classical covariogram identities and the elementary local lens power are ingredients, not claims of discovery. The comparison targeted equivalent formulations through the lattice-free probability correction, the all-direction covariogram, and collision-orientation conditional laws; no inspected primary statement supplies the exact curvature coefficient together with the universal conditional law. Wider covariogram literature and an incompletely accessible 2016 ellipse-hitting treatment remain historical-priority risks. A negative search is not a proof of global originality.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The model is a natural geometric-probability version of lattice-point avoidance, a problem studied in relation to integer programming. The theorem resolves the first onset of *two-point* occupancy, exhibits an explicit diameter-endpoint curvature invariant, and identifies a universal conditional collision-angle law. Its sharp exponent changes between noncircular ellipses and disks, so this is more than an arbitrary special-case calculation.
