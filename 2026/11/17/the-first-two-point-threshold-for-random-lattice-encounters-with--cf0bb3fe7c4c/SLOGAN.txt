At the first square-lattice two-point occupancy threshold, a randomly rotated and translated smooth convex body with a unique nondegenerate diameter has collision probability proportional to \(\varepsilon^2\), with an explicit curvature coefficient and a universal \(\operatorname{Beta}(1/2,5/2)\) collision-orientation law. A disk instead has exponent \(3/2\), revealing a sharp symmetry-dependent onset transition.
