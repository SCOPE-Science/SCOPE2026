# The full equivariant Euler character of the four-simplex Morse-matching complex

## Finding
Let \(K=\Delta^4\) be the abstract simplex on five labeled vertices, and let \(X=\mathcal M(K)\) be the *full*, non-pure simplicial complex of acyclic partial matchings of the Hasse diagram of its nonempty face poset. The natural action of \(S_5\) relabels the five vertices. Consider its reduced rational **virtual homology representation**
\[
\mathcal E(X)=\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^j[\widetilde H_j(X;\mathbb Q)]\in R_{\mathbb Q}(S_5).
\]
The exact character \(\chi_{\mathcal E}\), with conjugacy classes listed by cycle type, is

| Cycle type | \((1^5)\) | \((2,1^3)\) | \((2^2,1)\) | \((3,1^2)\) | \((3,2)\) | \((4,1)\) | \((5)\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Reduced Lefschetz trace | 212456 | -16 | 0 | -1 | -1 | 0 | 1 |

Consequently, in the rational representation ring,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E(X)={}&1769 S^{(5)}\oplus 7079 S^{(4,1)}\oplus 8851 S^{(3,2)}\\
&\oplus10623 S^{(3,1,1)}\oplus8854 S^{(2,2,1)}\\
&\oplus7084 S^{(2,1,1,1)}\oplus1772 S^{(1,1,1,1,1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The plus signs describe a **positive virtual character**, not a decomposition of any individual homology group: the full graded homology of \(X\) is not determined. In particular, the topological quotient satisfies
\[
\widetilde\chi(|X|/S_5;\mathbb Q)=1769,
\qquad \chi(|X|/S_5)=1770.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
A Hasse node is one of the \(31\) nonempty subsets of five labels. A primitive matching vertex is a cover pair \((a,b)\) with \(a\subset b\) and \(|b|=|a|+1\); there are exactly \(75\) such pairs. A set \(M\) of primitive pairs is a face of \(X\) when no Hasse node occurs twice and there is no nontrivial closed gradient \(V\)-path. Its cardinality may range from zero (the augmented empty face) to fifteen. All calculations use this *ordinary* Morse-matching complex, not the generalized complex that permits cycles and not the pure complex generated by optimal matchings. The rational Specht symbols are indexed by partitions of five; \(R_{\mathbb Q}(S_5)\) is the Grothendieck group of finite-dimensional rational representations.

## Proof
**Reduction to invariant orbit unions.** Fix \(g\in S_5\). Its action on the \(75\) primitive cover pairs has finite orbits. A face \(M\) is setwise fixed by \(g\) if and only if it is the union of some of these whole orbits. If \(M\) consists of \(k\) primitive pairs in exactly \(o\) selected orbits, then the action of \(g\) on its oriented simplex has sign \((-1)^{k-o}\). As this simplex has dimension \(k-1\), its contribution to the alternating *reduced* chain trace is \((-1)^{o-1}\). The augmented empty face contributes \(-1\). Accordingly, if \(P_g(z)=\sum_{o\ge0}a_o(g)z^o\) enumerates valid fixed matchings by the number of complete primitive-pair orbits, then
\[
\chi_{\mathcal E}(g)=-P_g(-1).
\]
This is an exact chain-level Lefschetz identity; no inference about individual homology degrees is involved.

**Exhaustive finite characterization.** Encode every nonempty face by a nonzero five-bit integer. Generate the \(75\) cover pairs exactly from subset containment and one-rank difference. Given a permutation representative, enumerate its primitive-pair orbits. An orbit can participate in a matching only if its pairs use disjoint Hasse nodes and contain no closed gradient path. The remaining orbits are considered by a binary include/exclude recursion, rejecting selections with repeated Hasse nodes or a closed gradient path. Each invariant face is visited exactly once, because it has a unique set of complete primitive-pair orbits.

To test acyclicity, form a directed graph whose vertices are the selected cover pairs. For matched pairs \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\), include an arrow from the first to the second exactly when \(c\ne a\) is another codimension-one face of \(b\). A directed cycle is precisely a nontrivial closed \(V\)-path. This gives an exhaustive, exact and terminating test without a homology matrix. Rejection of a cyclic partial matching is safe: a closed \(V\)-path uses only already matched nodes, so adding disjoint pairs cannot remove it. The implementation is `morse_equivariant_euler.py`.

The resulting **complete orbit-subset enumerations** for the six nonidentity classes are:

| Cycle type | Valid invariant matchings, including empty | Reduced Lefschetz trace |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| \((2,1^3)\) | 449440 | -16 |
| \((2^2,1)\) | 10480 | 0 |
| \((3,1^2)\) | 1029 | -1 |
| \((3,2)\) | 1 | -1 |
| \((4,1)\) | 32 | 0 |
| \((5)\) | 3 | 1 |

The exact coefficients of every \(P_g(z)\) are printed in `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt`. A *separate algorithm* in `crosscheck_morse_kahn.py` generates orbit unions without pruning by the gradient-path test, then reverses the matched edges of the entire \(31\)-node, \(75\)-edge Hasse diagram and applies an ordinary topological sort. It reproduces all six traces and all six valid matching totals; its raw matching-only counts are also recorded in `KAHN_CHECK_OUTPUT.txt`.

**Identity and representation decomposition.** The identity trace is the reduced Euler characteristic. Scoville's exact published complete \(f\)-vector of \(\mathcal M(\Delta^4)\) (Section 5.2 of [1]) has alternating sum \(212457\), hence reduced Euler characteristic \(212456\). Thus the seven displayed character values are established. For partitions \(\lambda,\mu\vdash5\), compute the Specht multiplicity by
\[
 m_{\lambda}=\frac1{120}\sum_{\mu\vdash5}|C_{\mu}|\,\chi_{\mathcal E}(\mu)\,\chi^{\lambda}(\mu).
\]
The seven irreducible characters may be reconstructed from the standard four-dimensional permutation-augmentation character, its symmetric and exterior squares, and sign twists. Exact integer evaluation yields the seven coefficients in the finding; their degree-weighted sum is \(212456\). In characteristic zero, reduced rational homology of the finite-group quotient is the invariant part of reduced rational homology of the original space, so the coefficient \(m_{(5)}=1769\) is the reduced Euler characteristic of the quotient.

## Verification
Execute `python3 morse_equivariant_euler.py` and `python3 crosscheck_morse_kahn.py` in the same directory. The first checker also exhaustively verifies the four-label tetrahedral case, enumerating its \(12821\) faces including the augmented empty face and reproducing the previously established reduced \(S_4\)-character \((99,1,-1,0,-1)\). It asserts the six new traces, all seven rational Specht multiplicities and the quotient Euler characteristic. The second checker uses full-Hasse topological sorting instead of the \(V\)-path digraph, including a raw fixed-matching search before acyclicity filtering. Both checks were executed, with actual output attached. All arithmetic is exact integer arithmetic.

## Relationship to prior work
Scoville [1] gives the full \(f\)-vector and reduced Euler characteristic for \(\mathcal M(\Delta^4)\), but explicitly leaves its homology unknown; the paper does not provide the character values for nonidentity label permutations. Lin and Scoville [2] determine the abstract automorphism group and its natural relabeling action, not its equivariant Euler characteristic. A published stabilizer census of *optimal* four-simplex matchings gives orbit counts at the top-dimensional stratum, but does not account for all lower-dimensional Morse-matching faces, so it does not imply this virtual character. A published tetrahedral calculation determines the rational \(S_4\)-homology character for the *four-label* complex; its different domain does not imply the five-label result. These are mathematical comparisons of results rather than title comparisons.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the **finite four-simplex** with five labels. It is a complete exact equivariant *Euler* classification, not a computation of the graded homology groups, integral representation lattices, or equivariant homotopy type. Positivity of the virtual Specht coefficients does not prove concentration in one dimension. The identity contribution uses the published exact face vector [1]; the nonidentity contributions are computed independently here. An equivalent but unindexed literature computation remains possible, and no independent expert audit has been performed.
