# Scientific evaluation

## Correctness — PASS
The source theorems identify the rational homology dimensions \(29\) and \(7\), with no other positive reduced rational homology. The known cross-polytope sphere basis determines the degree-nine character. An exact finite signed Lefschetz computation gives the degree-six character on all fifteen conjugacy classes; its decomposition is checked by the Murnaghan–Nakayama rule. Two unrelated finite enumeration algorithms return identical signed counts. The quotient claim follows from invariants under a finite group over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Originality — PASS
Feng–Wang (2024) already proves independent degree-nine cross-polytope cycles, and Feng (2026) proves the Betti numbers; neither is presented as new. A separate ninth-homology symmetry calculation also already covers that representation. The new result is the complete \(\mathfrak S_7\)-representation **in degree six**, its three irreducible summands, and the resulting rational homology of the quotient. Targeted published-record and primary-text comparisons found no equivalent sixth-degree character. A stronger unindexed result remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The \(35\)-vertex complex is the first nontrivial seven-point triple-Kneser case; rank \(29\) does not identify its symmetry representation. The decomposition into a fixed line plus conjugate 14-dimensional Specht modules is a motivated exact refinement, and the rational six-sphere quotient homology is a structural consequence not implied by the rank alone. No claim is made for higher parameters or integral torsion.

## Limits
The code checks combinatorial equivariant traces and character arithmetic. It does not re-prove the published homology concentration over \(\mathbb Q\), and neither a general integral result nor an independently audited certificate is asserted.
