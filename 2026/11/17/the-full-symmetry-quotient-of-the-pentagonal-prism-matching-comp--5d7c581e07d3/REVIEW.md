# Review of the pentagonal-prism matching-complex quotient

## Correctness — PASS

The matching faces, barycentric flags, twenty group actions, and flag-orbit chain groups are reconstructed directly. The quotient is modeled with actual chain orbits rather than an unjustified orbit-poset identification, and the integral differential squares to zero. Precisely 463 unit cancellations leave one generator in degree zero and one in degree three. Independent spanning-tree/triangle simplification eliminates all 61 generators of the original fundamental group; the fixed all-rung barycenter and Armstrong's theorem imply the quotient is simply connected. The integral homology sphere is therefore homotopy equivalent to a three-sphere by Hurewicz and Whitehead. Risks: the result is specific to one graph, with no extrapolation or external audit.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

Braun and Hough, arXiv:1606.01204 (2016), concern open two-row grids, not the periodic prism quotient. Published results on the same pentagonal-prism ordinary matching complex establish the labeled wedge decomposition and rational symmetry characters. In particular, the full-rational-symmetry article explicitly refrains from claiming integral homology or homotopy of the quotient. Direct comparisons of full RESULT.md texts establish that its rational sphere result does not imply an integral sphere; torsion and fundamental group would remain undetermined. Targeted indexed record and literature searches did not uncover a dominating integral quotient homotopy theorem. Risk: unindexed prior calculations are not ruled out.

## Value — PASS

This is the canonical first mixed-dimensional circular-ladder matching complex, previously identified as a wedge of spheres in two dimensions. Its twenty-element full symmetry collapses the quotient's complete homotopy to a single three-sphere. Integral homology, vanishing fundamental group and Whitehead's conclusion are strictly stronger than rational invariant calculations and answer a natural next question about the effect of symmetry on this known mixed-dimensional complex. The exact finite case has a motivated object rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.
