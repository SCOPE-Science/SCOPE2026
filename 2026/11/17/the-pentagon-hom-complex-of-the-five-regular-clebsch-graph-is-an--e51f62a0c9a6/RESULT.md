# The pentagon Hom complex of the five-regular Clebsch graph is an orientable surface of genus \(241\)

## Finding
Let \(C_5\) be the undirected simple cycle with vertices indexed cyclically by \(0,1,2,3,4\). Let \(H\) be the **five-regular**, sixteen-vertex Clebsch graph, namely the folded five-cube, NOT the complementary ten-regular graph occasionally bearing the same name. The ordinary polyhedral graph-homomorphism complex \(X=\operatorname{Hom}(C_5,H)\) is a connected closed orientable surface with cubical cell vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(1920,4800,2400),\qquad \chi(X)=-480.
\]
Therefore \(X\) is homeomorphic to the orientable genus-\(241\) surface \(\Sigma_{241}\); its fundamental group is the usual closed-surface group on \(482\) generators with one surface relation, and its integral homology groups are \(H_0(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{482}\), \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), and zero in degrees above two. Under the natural affine group \(E\rtimes S_5\), where \(E\) is the four-dimensional even-weight binary-vector group acting by translations and \(S_5\) permutes coordinates, the action on the integral fundamental class is the sign of the \(S_5\) permutation; every translation preserves orientation.

## Assumptions and scope
Represent \(H\) by the group \(E=\{x\in\mathbb F_2^5:\sum_i x_i=0\}\), with \(x\sim y\) exactly when \(x+y=g_j\), where \(g_j\) has a zero in coordinate \(j\) and ones elsewhere. Thus \(H\) has sixteen vertices, degree five and parameters \((16,5,0,2)\). A cell of \(X\) is a five-tuple \((S_0,\ldots,S_4)\) of nonempty subsets of \(E\), with complete adjacency between \(S_i\) and \(S_{i+1}\), cyclically. Its geometric cell is \(\prod_{i=0}^{4}\Delta(S_i)\) of dimension \(\sum_i(|S_i|-1)\); this is the ordinary **polyhedral** Hom complex, not an arbitrary graph-mapping clique complex.

## Proof
**1. The vertices are labelled closed walks.** The five generators \(g_0,\ldots,g_4\) satisfy exactly one nonzero binary linear relation: \(\sum_jg_j=0\). Indeed, the \(k\)-th coordinate of \(\sum_jc_jg_j\) equals \((\sum_jc_j)+c_k\); vanishing forces all coefficients equal. Consequently any closed walk of exactly five edges in \(H\) uses each generator once: the total number of uses of each generator is odd (a zero parity vector cannot sum to five), and hence is precisely one. A vertex of \(X\) is thus uniquely encoded by its initial image \(x\in E\) and a permutation \(\pi\in S_5\) of edge labels. Hence \(f_0=16\cdot5!=1920\). In particular the five vertices along any such walk are distinct, and the two neighbors of any source vertex have different, nonadjacent images.

**2. Square cells and local links.** Each pair of distinct nonadjacent vertices in \(H\) has exactly two common neighbors. For a graph map of the source pentagon, fixing four vertex images therefore leaves exactly one alternative image at the remaining position. In the generator-label description this is the elementary operation \(T_i\) that interchanges the two edge labels incident to source vertex \(i\) (positions cyclically adjacent in \(\pi\)). In any multi-homomorphism cell each coordinate set has size at most two. Simultaneously doubling positions \(i\) and \(j\) is possible exactly when they are nonadjacent on the source pentagon: in that case the two swaps affect disjoint pairs of edge-label positions and commute; for adjacent positions the two transpositions overlap and fail to commute, contradicting the four-corner condition. The largest independent sets of \(C_5\) have size two, so there are no higher-dimensional cells. Every vertex lies in five one-cells and in exactly five square cells, indexed by the five nonadjacent pairs of source positions. Therefore \(f_1=1920\cdot5/2=4800\) and \(f_2=1920\cdot5/4=2400\).

The link of every vertex has one vertex for each of its five recolorings and one edge for each of the five commuting nonadjacent pairs of recolorings. Thus the vertex link is the complementary five-cycle, itself a circle. Each one-cell lies in exactly two square cells. The complex is consequently a closed two-dimensional PL manifold without boundary.

**3. Connectedness and orientation.** The operations \(T_1,\ldots,T_4\) realize the adjacent transpositions of the five generator labels without changing the initial image, so all \(5!\) label orders at fixed \(x\) are connected. The operation \(T_0\) changes the initial image by \(g_a+g_b=e_a+e_b\) for some distinct indices \(a,b\), and these weight-two vectors generate \(E\). Hence the one-skeleton is connected.

For orientability, order the five local recoloring directions cyclically as \(0,2,4,1,3\), the cycle of nonadjacent source positions. At a vertex \((x,\pi)\), choose this link-circle orientation multiplied by the parity sign of \(\pi\). Passing across any one-cell transposes two distinct labels, reversing that sign; the two square corners on the edge agree with the resulting opposite local boundary directions. This defines a coherent orientation of every square, hence of the surface. Independently, the exact signed two-cell incidence traversal in the standalone program constructs an integral fundamental cycle supported with coefficient \(\pm1\) on each of the \(2400\) squares and verifies that its cellular boundary is zero.

**4. Genus and symmetry.** The Euler characteristic is \(1920-4800+2400=-480\). Classification of connected closed orientable surfaces gives \(2-2g=-480\), so \(g=241\), with the integral homology and fundamental group stated above. The translation subgroup of \(E\rtimes S_5\) leaves the generator order unchanged; a coordinate permutation \(\sigma\) changes the generator-label permutation from \(\pi\) to \(\sigma\circ\pi\), multiplying the chosen local orientation by \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\). The full orientability character is therefore \((t,\sigma)\mapsto\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\), also checked on all \(16\cdot120=1920\) affine automorphisms by cellular orientation transport.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify.py` using only the Python standard library. It reconstructs the target graph and checks degree, adjacency and common-neighbor properties, enumerates all \(1920\) closed walks in two different ways, generates every Hom cell by expanding single-valued graph maps, and verifies exactly \(1920\) vertices, \(4800\) edges and \(2400\) squares with no higher-dimensional cells. It checks each vertex link is a five-cycle, every one-cell is in exactly two squares, the one-skeleton is connected, and signs of all square incidences yield a coherent integral fundamental cycle. Finally it checks the induced orientation sign on all \(1920\) affine target symmetries. The accompanying `verification_output.txt` is a reproducible output record, not a replacement for the proof or code.

## Relationship to prior work
Fujii–Kimura–Nozaki, arXiv:2412.19039 (first public December 26, 2024), and Matsushita, arXiv:2412.19144 (same first-public date), classify Hom complexes with **square-free** target graphs. The five-regular Clebsch graph contains four-cycles, so neither theorem applies; their circle/point descriptions do not imply a closed surface with nonzero second homology. Csorba–Lutz, arXiv:math/0510177, introduce graph-coloring manifolds and analyze graph-Hom complexes whose targets are complete graphs, not this folded cube. The Stiefel-manifold classification of \(\operatorname{Hom}(C_5,K_n)\) likewise does not determine this target's Hom complex. A graph Hom calculation for the induced Grötzsch graph and another for the Chvátal graph concern different targets; inclusion into the Clebsch graph is not a homotopy equivalence by the cited theorems. No matching assertion was found in the recorded direct title, parameter, alias and implication searches, but that is not a proof of global bibliographic uniqueness.

## Limitations
This is a classification of the ordinary \(C_5\)-to-five-regular-Clebsch Hom complex only; it is not a general result for all triangle-free strongly regular graphs or all folded cubes. The complement, also sometimes called the Clebsch graph, is excluded. The group character concerns the natural affine target-automorphism action on top integral homology, not a decomposition of the entire integral first-homology representation. Independent mathematical and bibliographic audits have not been performed.
