# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the precisely defined endpoint-uniform unit-circle model, the normal-angle/signed-offset coordinates have independent uniform and arcsine laws. The intersection map has Jacobian \(\sin t\); integrating the first normal gives the marked density. The previously published and independently displayed radial density normalizes the conditional law. The two distinct logarithmic neighborhoods near the boundary each contribute \(L(r)/\sin t\). Pointwise convergence plus the exact unit mass gives total-variation convergence by Scheffé's lemma. This proof does not infer an infinite limiting law from simulations. Risks: the new result is conditional on \(0<r<1\); no endpoint-rate estimate is proved.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, provisional historical priority)

David–Fix (1964) is a directly relevant predecessor but could not be read in full. The accessible full text of Bortolotto–Souza (arXiv:2504.12866) proves the unmarked intersection-radius distribution, not the joint distribution with crossing angle or the conditional total-variation boundary law. Barahona Torres and coauthors (arXiv:1911.05576) use one chord with area-uniform disk endpoints and another horizontal chord with a uniform height, which is a different law. The separate ellipse crossing-angle study reports an unmarked squared-sine statistic and does not imply a radius-conditioned density. The inaccessibility of the historical paper is a genuine priority risk and is not represented as an inspection of its contents. Unconditional probability and angular/radial marginals are not claimed new.

## Value — PASS

The previously known radial concentration of chord intersections toward the circle boundary does not say which crossing orientations cause it. The exact marked joint law and the surprising uniform boundary-angle limit supply this missing directional description, with an explicit total-variation statement useful for angle-sensitive random crossing geometry and verification of anisotropic intersection statistics. This is a natural whole-range structural description, not an arbitrary numerical slice or a textbook probability value.

## Closest work and limitations

The central primary comparison is Bortolotto–Souza (2025), whose radial theorem supplies the normalization but not the marked law. The older David–Fix (1964) paper remains incompletely accessible and hence a historical-priority risk. The claim is restricted to uniform endpoints on one circle and intersections within the disk. Independent verification has not been performed.
