# The rational cohomology ring of bounded circle-subset shapes

## Finding

For every integer \(k\geq1\), let \(E_k=\exp_k(S^1)\) be the space of **nonempty, unordered, multiplicity-free** subsets of the Euclidean unit circle with at most \(k\) points, topologized as the quotient of \((S^1)^k\) by the map sending a tuple to its underlying set. Let \(\mathcal S_k=E_k/O(2)\), where the full orthogonal group acts by its usual rotations and reflections, and take the **ordinary topological quotient**. For a field \(F\) equal to \(\mathbb Q\), or to \(\mathbb F_p\) for an odd prime \(p>k\), there is a natural class \(v_k\in H^4(\mathcal S_k;F)\) satisfying

\[
H^*(\mathcal S_k;F)\cong F[v_k]/\bigl(v_k^{\lceil k/4\rceil}\bigr),\qquad |v_k|=4.
\]

The class \(v_k\) is the image of the first Pontryagin class of the universal two-plane bundle in the rational (respectively prime-to-stabilizer) Borel-to-orbit comparison. The classes are compatible with the inclusions \(\mathcal S_k\hookrightarrow\mathcal S_{k+1}\). Thus the ordinary rational Betti numbers are one in degrees \(0,4,\ldots,4(\lceil k/4\rceil-1)\) and zero elsewhere; \(\chi(\mathcal S_k)=\lceil k/4\rceil\). In particular, the first nonzero positive-degree rational cohomology occurs at \(k=5\), and new powers appear at \(k=9,13,\ldots\). Moreover, the integral cohomology groups have no \(p\)-primary torsion for any prime \(p>k\).

## Assumptions and scope

All spaces are the ordinary compact Hausdorff quotient spaces described above, rather than homotopy-orbit spaces. The coefficient-field qualification is essential: **no assertion about integral torsion at primes at most \(k\), or at the prime two, is made**. Cohomology is singular cohomology. The sequence includes \(k=1\) and \(k=2\) without exceptions. For a geometric shape consisting of exactly \(j\) points, write its successive normalized positive angular gaps as \((a_1,\ldots,a_j)\), with every \(a_i>0\) and \(\sum a_i=1\). These gaps are classified up to the action of the dihedral group \(D_j\) on the \(j\) indices. When a gap becomes zero the corresponding points merge, giving the lower-cardinality strata.

## Proof

**1. Finite stabilizers and finite-dimensional orbit spaces.** A rotation preserving a nonempty finite subset acts faithfully on that subset: a rotation fixing even one point is the identity. Every rotational stabilizer is therefore finite of order at most \(k\). In the full orthogonal group the isotropy is a finite extension by at most one reflection, hence has order at most \(2k\). The positive-gap description gives a finite stratification of \(E_k/SO(2)\) and \(\mathcal S_k\) by quotients of open simplices of dimensions \(j-1\), for \(1\leq j\leq k\). The compact spaces have finite triangulable dimension at most \(k-1\). For characteristic zero, or for an odd prime \(p>k\), all stabilizer orders are invertible. The map from the Borel construction to the orbit space then induces a cohomology isomorphism with coefficients \(F\): its fibers are the classifying spaces of the finite stabilizers, and they are \(F\)-acyclic in positive degrees. The finite equivariant stratification supplies the usual local-to-global comparison.

**2. The rotation-quotient ring.** Set \(m=\lceil k/2\rceil\). Tuffley's theorem states \(E_k\simeq S^{2m-1}\), nonequivariantly, integrally. The Serre cohomology spectral sequence of

\[
E_k\longrightarrow ES^1\times_{S^1}E_k\longrightarrow BS^1
\]

has base ring \(H^*(BS^1;F)=F[u]\), \(|u|=2\), and only rows \(q=0\) and \(q=2m-1\). Because the structure group \(S^1=SO(2)\) is connected, it acts trivially on the top-cohomology coefficient line. The only possibly nonzero differential is \(d_{2m}(\xi)=c u^m\). If \(c=0\), the bottom polynomial row survives in arbitrarily high degrees, contradicting the finite-dimensional orbit comparison in Step 1. Thus \(c\ne0\); over \(F\) it is a unit. The multiplicative spectral sequence and the Borel-to-orbit isomorphism yield

\[
H^*(E_k/SO(2);F)\cong F[u]/(u^m).
\]

There is no additive or multiplicative extension ambiguity: all surviving classes are generated by the pullback of the universal degree-two class \(u\), with the unique truncation relation displayed.

**3. The reflection involution and the full quotient.** The residual involution in \(O(2)/SO(2)\cong C_2\) conjugates a rotation \(z\mapsto\lambda z\) to its inverse. Its induced automorphism of \(H^2(BS^1;F)\) sends \(u\mapsto-u\). Naturality of the Borel construction and of the comparison in Step 1 shows that this is the actual action on \(H^*(E_k/SO(2);F)\). Since \(2\) is invertible in \(F\), transfer for the finite involution gives the cohomology **ring** of its ordinary quotient as the invariant subring:

\[
H^*(\mathcal S_k;F)=\bigl(F[u]/(u^m)\bigr)^{C_2}
=F[u^2]/\bigl((u^2)^{\lceil m/2\rceil}\bigr).
\]

Writing \(v_k=u^2\) and using \(\lceil m/2\rceil=\lceil k/4\rceil\) proves the theorem. Under \(BSO(2)\to BO(2)\), the universal first Pontryagin class restricts to \(u^2\), so it determines \(v_k\) and makes compatibility with cardinality inclusions automatic.

**4. Integral torsion bound.** The same argument works over \(\mathbb F_p\) for every odd \(p>k\). The \(\mathbb F_p\)-Betti dimensions then coincide with the corresponding rational Betti ranks in all degrees. Because the quotient is a finite triangulable space, integral (co)homology is finitely generated. The universal coefficient sequence therefore excludes \(p\)-primary integral cohomology torsion for such \(p\).

**5. Independent Euler-characteristic check.** The exactly-\(j\)-point shape stratum is \(\Delta_{j-1}^{\circ}/D_j\), the quotient of the positive-gap simplex by cyclic permutations and reversal. For a dihedral permutation \(g\), the fixed locus has \(c(g)\) gap-coordinate cycles and compactly supported Euler characteristic \((-1)^{c(g)-1}\). The finite-group Euler-characteristic average gives

\[
\chi_c(\Delta_{j-1}^{\circ}/D_j)=\frac{1}{2j}\sum_{g\in D_j}(-1)^{c(g)-1}
=\begin{cases}1,&j\equiv1\pmod4,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]

Indeed, for odd \(j\) every rotation contributes \(+1\), while each reflection contributes \((-1)^{(j-1)/2}\). For even \(j\), even and odd rotations cancel and the two reflection types cancel. This remains valid with the nonfaithful abstract dihedral action for \(j=1,2\). Summing over \(1\leq j\leq k\) yields \(\chi(\mathcal S_k)=\lceil k/4\rceil\), independently matching the cohomology computation.

## Verification

The standalone script `gap_euler_check.py` independently enumerates all \(2j\) dihedral permutations at each \(j\), counts their coordinate cycles, and computes the exact rational Euler average. It checks \(1\leq j\leq100\) and matches both the modulo-four closed form and the cumulative cohomology-ring Euler characteristic in every case. The captured output is `gap_euler_output.txt`. This finite check tests the combinatorial formula; it is not used as a substitute for the all-\(k\) proof. The Serre-spectral-sequence, transfer, and stabilization arguments are mathematical proofs, not numerical extrapolations.

## Relationship to prior work

Tuffley (2002, arXiv:math/0209077, especially Theorem 4) proves the nonequivariant sphere homotopy type of \(E_k\) and studies the special rotation action at \(k=3\). These are inputs, **not** new claims. Mostovoy (1999, arXiv:math/9911224) relates only the three-point finite-subset space to planar lattices and records rotation equivariance. Tuffley (2003, arXiv:math/0210315) treats homology of unquotiented finite-subset spaces of connected graphs and punctured surfaces. These results do not themselves state the full-isometry shape-quotient cohomology ring, the compatible characteristic generator, or the prime bound. The argument applies standard almost-free circle-action and finite-group transfer methods to this specific geometric filtration; it does not assert those general tools are new.

## Limitations

The theorem determines only the stated characteristic-zero and large-odd-prime cohomology rings, not the integral homotopy types or small-prime integral torsion. Since standard equivariant machinery is used, undiscovered equivalent applications to this particular filtration remain a novelty risk; an accessible summary of Ageev–Usimov (2013) concerns cohomology of subspaces of universal \(S^1\)-spaces with finite orbit types, but its full text was not accessible for a decisive specialized comparison. The proof does not identify the quotient with a complex or quaternionic projective space and makes no assertion about manifold structure.
