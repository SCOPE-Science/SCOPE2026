# Review of the eight-cell area-balance theorem

## Correctness — PASS

Each signed chamber's three spherical interior angles are obtained from the oriented plane normals. Girard's theorem gives the eight area expressions, from which the range equals twice the sum of the two largest absolute arcsine pairwise correlations. The joint Gram-correlation density is obtained directly using independent uniform polar cosines and an independent uniform relative azimuth. Its sign-symmetrized Taylor series has degree-zero and degree-four terms but vanishes at degrees one, two, three and five. Exact integration of the ordered positive octant gives polytope volume \(2\) and quartic moment \(7/120\). Scaling the density produces \(\varepsilon^3/(16\pi)+7\varepsilon^7/(122880\pi)+O(\varepsilon^9)\). Density convergence on a bounded polytope proves the claimed total-variation limit and hence the Beta range law. The symbolic integration and independent direct spherical geometry checks support, but do not substitute for, the proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

The deterministic Girard, trivariate Gaussian orthant, and Gram-density identities are explicitly treated as prior tools. Finch–Jones (arXiv:1009.5329, dual area section) supplies marginal spherical-triangle laws, not the joint max-minus-min of eight chambers. Hug–Schneider (arXiv:1508.07768, introductory results and second-moment theorem) supplies face expectation/moment identities, not the small-support polytope probability or its seventh-order term. Kabluchko–Thäle (arXiv:2005.01055) studies combinatorial counts and expectations. The published finding `2026/11/2/SCOPE-sharp-two-step-finite-size-law-for-squared-areas-of-great-circle--901e9aec2ff6` treats high-\(n\) squared cell-area moments, not a three-circle joint rare event. An online benchmark asks for the **mean** area spread, showing the invariant is natural but not supplying the present tail. A comprehensive priority audit of historical stereological integral formulae remains outstanding.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Nearly equal area is the natural regularity criterion for a partition into eight antipodal spherical triangles. A sharp cubic probability distinguishes independent orthogonality constraints; the unexpectedly absent intermediate corrections and the positive seventh-order coefficient measure higher-order anisotropy. The marked total-variation limit describes which perturbations of orthogonal circles generate almost balanced octants, rather than reporting a stand-alone numerical constant. This is a motivated complete local probability calculation, not a renaming of a known marginal law.

## Residual risks and scope

The proof covers exactly three isotropic circles in \(S^2\), not the entire distribution or arbitrary tessellations. The literature may contain earlier equivalent special-function integral evaluations or unindexed area-spread calculations. Finite numerical tests cannot independently certify a geometric probability theorem.
