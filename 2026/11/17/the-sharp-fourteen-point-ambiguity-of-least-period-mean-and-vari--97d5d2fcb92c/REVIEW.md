# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For uniform sampling of points in a finite permutation, the chance of seeing an orbit of length \(k\) is proportional to \(k\) times its number of cycles. Consequently, the first two moments are equivalent to the cycle-length power sums of degrees two and three at fixed cardinality. The exact multivariate partition recurrence has been reconstructed and matched coefficient by coefficient to an independent exhaustive partition generator. It has no repeated signature for any size below fourteen and exactly one double fiber at fourteen; the two partitions have disjoint period sets and precisely the stated rational moments. Adjoining fixed points proves noninjectivity at every larger size by identity, rather than numerical extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty

The closest original research by de Jong classifies the supports of least-period spectra; the open-access text gives orbit-length realizability and does not state the minimal two-moment classification cutoff. The Dold-sequence survey treats complete orbit-count sequences, a strictly richer observation than the two moments. Searches by the equivalent squared/cubed partition sums and by both explicit cycle profiles found no dominating published cutoff theorem. Partition-moment coincidences may independently occur in older combinatorial tables, and novelty cannot be proved from search absence alone.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The least-period distribution is a natural observational statistic of a finite dynamical system, while the support and full counting sequence are important studied invariants. Determining exactly when the first two moments cease to recover cycle structure is a well-motivated inverse problem. This theorem supplies a sharp full small-size classification, a unique first obstruction with disjoint period supports, and a structural mechanism that works at every larger size—not a single arbitrarily chosen example.

## Scope and remaining risks

The unique threshold applies at fixed cardinality and uniform measure. No claim is made for cross-size comparison or nonuniform measures, and the result has not undergone external independent validation.
