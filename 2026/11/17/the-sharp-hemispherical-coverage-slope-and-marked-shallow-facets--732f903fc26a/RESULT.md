# The sharp hemispherical coverage slope and marked shallow facets of random inscribed tetrahedra

## Finding
Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_4\) be independent surface-uniform points on the Euclidean unit sphere \(S^2\), and let \(K=\operatorname{conv}\{X_1,\ldots,X_4\}\). On \(E=\{0\in\operatorname{int}K\}\), let \(H_j\) be the perpendicular distance from the origin to the affine plane through the three vertices other than \(X_j\), and define the *origin-centred clearance* \(R=\min_{1\le j\le4}H_j\). Denote by \(p(\omega)\) the probability that the four geodesic caps with centres \(X_j\) and common angular radius \(\omega\) cover \(S^2\). Then, as \(t\downarrow0\),
\[
 \Pr(E,\,R<t)=\frac32t+o(t),\qquad
 \Pr(R<t\mid E)=12t+o(t),
\]
and equivalently, as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\),
\[
 p\left(\frac\pi2-\varepsilon\right)=\frac18-\frac32\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon).
\]
There is also a marked limit. Condition on \(E\) and \(R<t\); select the (almost surely unique) nearest face, whose omitted-vertex index is \(J\). Let \(u,v,w\) be the half-angular gaps between its three vertices around the face circumcircle, in cyclic order, with \(u+v+w=\pi\) and a uniformly randomly chosen starting vertex among the three. In total variation, the joint law of \(\bigl(J,R/t,u,v\bigr)\) converges to the product of: a uniform index on \(\{1,2,3,4\}\); a uniform variable on \((0,1)\); and a density
\[
 f(u,v)=\frac{16}{3\pi}\sin u\sin v\sin(u+v)
 \mathbf1\{0<u<\pi/2,\,0<v<\pi/2,\,u+v>\pi/2\}.
\]
Thus a near-loss of coverage at the hemispherical threshold is caused by exactly one nearly central facet, and that facet is acute in its limiting shape.

## Assumptions and scope
All vertices are independently distributed according to normalized rotation-invariant surface area; all four cap radii are equal. The convex hull is a nondegenerate tetrahedron almost surely. The clearance is a radius about the *fixed origin*, not the tetrahedron's freely movable inradius. Only positive-side coverage thresholds \(t=\cos\omega\in(0,1)\) are used. An index tie for the nearest facet has probability zero. The limit holds with four vertices in three dimensions, with a fixed sampling distribution.

## Proof
**1. Three-vertex plane law and the face shape.** For three independent surface-uniform sphere points, write the affine plane as \(x\cdot n=h\), where its unsigned distance is \(H=|h|\in(0,1)\), and let \(r=(1-h^2)^{1/2}\). Parametrize the three points in that plane by \(x_i=hn+r(\cos\theta_i e_1+\sin\theta_i e_2)\), with orthonormal \((e_1,e_2,n)\). The spherical Blaschke–Petkantschin coarea Jacobian is, apart from a constant, \(r^2\left|\det((1,\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i)_{i=1}^3)\right|\). The rotational and shape factors therefore separate from the \(h\)-factor \(r^2=1-h^2\). Normalizing on \(0<h<1\) gives the exact marginal
\[
 f_H(h)=\frac32(1-h^2),\quad 0<h<1.
\]
The three intrinsic planar triangle angles (equivalently, three consecutive half-angular gaps) have a density proportional to \(\sin u\sin v\sin(u+v)\) on \(u>0,v>0,u+v<\pi\), independent of \(H\). More precisely,
\[
 J_0=\int_0^\pi\int_0^{\pi-u}\sin u\sin v\sin(u+v)\,dv\,du=\frac{3\pi}{8}.
\]
The projected triangle contains its circumcircle centre precisely when all three angles are acute, i.e. \(u,v,w<\pi/2\). Its corresponding integral is
\[
 J_A=\int_0^{\pi/2}\int_{\pi/2-u}^{\pi/2}\sin u\sin v\sin(u+v)\,dv\,du=\frac{3\pi}{16}.
\]
Both identities follow by product-to-sum integration, and hence the area-biased face-shape probability of acuteness is exactly \(J_A/J_0=1/2\). This marginal density and acuteness probability also appear in Finch's treatment of randomly inscribed spherical triangles; neither is asserted as a new result.

**2. The opposite vertex near a central face.** Fix a nondegenerate triple with plane \(x\cdot n=h\), \(h>0\). A fourth point \(y\) makes \(\operatorname{conv}\{x_1,x_2,x_3,y\}\) contain zero if and only if \(y\) lies in the negative cone generated by \(x_1,x_2,x_3\). This follows by taking the positive barycentric weights in a zero-sum convex combination. As \(h\downarrow0\), for almost every limiting triple and fourth point \(y\), the indicator of this event tends to \(\mathbf1\{\text{projected triple contains }0\}\mathbf1\{n\cdot y<0\}\): if the projected triangle contains zero strictly, its cone approaches the corresponding closed half-space; if not, its limiting cone has zero solid angle. The exceptional collinear, right-triangle, and equatorial sets have probability zero. Since \(y\) is independent surface-uniform, the conditional limiting probability over its location is \(1/2\) in the acute case and zero otherwise. Coarea and bounded convergence now give, for any fixed omitted index \(j\),
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{\Pr(E,H_j<t)}{t}
 =f_H(0)\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac38.
\]

**3. Distinct shallow facets cannot contribute to first order.** Fix two distinct indices \(j\ne k\). Condition on the triple forming facet \(j\) and let its plane distance tend to zero, using the preceding coarea coordinates. Except on a measure-zero set of limiting triples and fourth vertices, the affine plane of facet \(k\) does not contain the origin, so its limiting distance is strictly positive. By dominated convergence, its conditional probability of having distance below \(t\) tends to zero, uniformly after integration over \(0<h<t\). Hence
\[
 \Pr(H_j<t,H_k<t)=o(t).
\]
This unconditional bound also bounds overlaps restricted to \(E\). Inclusion–exclusion over the four facets therefore yields
\[
 \Pr(E,R<t)=\sum_{j=1}^4\Pr(E,H_j<t)+o(t)
 =4\left(\frac38t+o(t)\right)+o(t)=\frac32t+o(t).
\]
By Wendel's symmetric-point theorem, \(\Pr(E)=1/8\), proving the conditional coefficient \(12\).

**4. Cap duality and marked convergence.** The four caps cover exactly when \(\max_i x\cdot X_i\ge\cos\omega\) for every \(x\in S^2\), equivalently when \(B_{\cos\omega}(0)\subset K\) for \(\cos\omega\ge0\). Thus \(p(\arccos t)=\Pr(E,R\ge t)=1/8-3t/2+o(t)\). The identity \(\cos(\pi/2-\varepsilon)=\sin\varepsilon\sim\varepsilon\) gives the angular expansion.

For a fixed face \(j\), the coarea integrand of the event \(E,H_j<t\), written with \(h=ts\), is bounded by an integrable constant times the angular shape density and converges almost everywhere to the constant-halfspace selection rule in Step 2. Scheffé's lemma, after normalizing the probability, proves total-variation convergence of \((H_j/t,u,v)\) to independent \(\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\) and the area-biased acute face density \((2/J_0)\sin u\sin v\sin(u+v)\). Overlaps of facet selections have probability \(o(t)\), so replacing the distinguished shallow face with the unique minimum changes the conditioned law in total variation by \(o(1)\). Symmetry makes its label exactly asymptotically uniform. This proves the asserted product law. \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone `checks/verify.py` (Python with NumPy) independently quadratures the two normalized face-shape integrals and simulates the actual four-face geometry with a fixed seed, checking the Wendel containment mass and the approach to the two predicted small-distance coefficients. One replay produced `FOUR_CAP_HEMISPHERE_CHECKS_OK`, with integral ratio \(1/2\), center-containment frequency approximately \(0.124662\), and at threshold \(t=0.01\) an unconditioned probability-to-threshold ratio approximately \(1.40958\), compared with the proven limiting coefficient \(1.5\). These finite tests are diagnostic only. The analytic coarea factorization, zero-solid-angle limiting cone, and negligible two-facet overlaps establish the infinite-limit claims.

## Relationship to prior work
Finch (2015) studies this precise four-cap coverage model, states that a full exact formula below a hemisphere is unknown, and derives a triangular face-height density and acute-angle density and lower bounds. The face-height and angle facts used in Step 1 are background, not claimed new. Bürgisser, Cucker and Lotz (2007 preprint; 2010 journal) obtain exact coverage probabilities for cap radii at least a hemisphere and bounds below; their coverage bounds do not assert the sharp one-sided slope or marked shallow-facet law. Wendel's containment probability is classical. General results on facets of spherical random polytopes concern facet marginals or different sample-size regimes. An earlier investigation of four caps at the *minimal* covering radius proves a quartic onset near \(\arccos(1/3)\), a distinct endpoint from the present \(\pi/2\) limit. No claim is made to solve the full four-cap coverage distribution.

## Limitations
This is a first-order, one-sided local law; no exact probability for intermediate cap radii, explicit second-order error, or extension to nonuniform vertices or dimensions other than three is established. Total variation is proved for the specified finite-dimensional marks, not every geometric observable. Related historical work could contain an equivalent endpoint asymptotic not identified by the literature search. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
