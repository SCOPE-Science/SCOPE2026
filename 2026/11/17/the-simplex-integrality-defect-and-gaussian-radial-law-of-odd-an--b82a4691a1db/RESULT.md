# The simplex integrality defect and Gaussian radial law of odd-antihole clique relaxations

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n=2m+1\ge5\), let \(G_n=\overline{C_n}\), with cyclic vertex indices, and distinguish the *clique relaxation* from the ordinary edge relaxation:
\[
P_n=\operatorname{STAB}(G_n)=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_S:S\text{ is stable in }G_n\},\qquad
Q_n=\operatorname{QSTAB}(G_n)=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i\in K}x_i\le1\ \forall\text{ cliques }K\subseteq G_n\}.
\]
Put \(a_n=m^{-1}\mathbf1\) and \(v_i=e_i+e_{i+1}\), using cyclic indices. Then the following assertions hold simultaneously.

**Exact removed simplex.** The difference \(Q_n\setminus P_n\), up to its boundary of zero volume, equals the simplex above its opposite facet:
\[
Q_n\cap\left\{x:\sum_i x_i\ge2\right\}
 =\operatorname{conv}(a_n,v_1,\ldots,v_n),\qquad
P_n=Q_n\cap\left\{x:\sum_i x_i\le2\right\}.
\]
Its ordinary \(n\)-dimensional Euclidean volume is exactly
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{vol}_n(Q_n\setminus P_n)=\frac1{m\,n!}.}
\]
In particular, the cap is a full-dimensional simplex with \(n+1\) vertices, and the only fractional vertex of \(Q_n\) is \(a_n\).

**Exact radius moment and Gaussian limit.** Let \(X_n\) have the normalized Euclidean-volume distribution on \(Q_n\setminus P_n\), and put \(R_n^2=\|X_n-a_n\|_2^2\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E R_n^2=\frac{n\,(2-2/m+2/m^2)}{(n+1)(n+2)}.}
\]
As odd \(n\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{nR_n^2\xrightarrow{\mathsf P}2,\qquad
\sqrt n\,(nR_n^2-2)\xrightarrow{\mathsf d}N(0,20).}
\]
Thus the missing-volume mass concentrates at Euclidean distance asymptotic to \(\sqrt{2/n}\) from its unique fractional vertex, with an explicit Gaussian second-order fluctuation.

## Assumptions and scope
All spaces use their standard coordinate Euclidean metric and ordinary ambient volume, not graph-weighted or lattice-normalized volume. The graph is the **complement** of a chordless odd cycle; an odd hole itself has different cap geometry. The clique relaxation includes *all* clique constraints, not merely pairwise edge inequalities. There are no added vertices, edge weights, translations, or extra graph components. Uniformity of \(X_n\) is conditional on lying in the removed cap, not uniformity on all of \(Q_n\). The Gaussian limit is along odd integers only. Boundaries can be included or omitted in the sampling event without affecting its law.

The classical minimally-imperfect-graph theorem (Padberg, as presented and reproved by Koster and Wagler) supplies two explicitly identified inputs: \(Q_n\) has exactly one nonintegral vertex, \(a_n\), and \(P_n\) is obtained by imposing the full rank inequality \(\sum_i x_i\le2\). The remaining simplex, determinant, and probability-law claims are proved below rather than attributed to that theorem.

## Proof
**1. The simple fractional vertex and its neighbors.** Every clique of \(G_n\) is a stable set of \(C_n\). The maximum cliques have size \(m\), and there are exactly \(n\) of them, the cyclic translates of
\(I=\{0,2,\ldots,2m-2\}\). Indeed, placing \(m\) selected vertices around a cycle of length \(2m+1\) requires \(m\) intervertex gaps of length at least two; their total excess above two is precisely one, so one gap is three and the others are two.

Let \(H\) be the \(n\times n\) incidence matrix of these maximum cliques. It is circulant. For every nontrivial \(n\)-th root of unity \(z\), its generating polynomial satisfies
\[
1+z^2+\cdots+z^{2m-2}=\frac{1-z^{2m}}{1-z^2} = \frac{1-z^{-1}}{1-z^2}\ne0,
\]
and at \(z=1\) it equals \(m>0\). Hence \(H\) is nonsingular. At \(a_n\), all \(n\) maximum-clique inequalities are equalities. Every smaller clique has strict slack and every coordinate is positive. Therefore \(a_n\) has exactly \(n\) independent incident facets and is a simple vertex of \(Q_n\).

For each cycle edge \(\{i,i+1\}\), the vector \(v_i=e_i+e_{i+1}\) is the indicator of a stable set of the *antihole*. Exactly \(m\) maximum cliques contain \(i\), and exactly \(m\) contain \(i+1\); none contains both adjacent cycle vertices. Thus exactly \(2m=n-1\) of the \(n\) maximum-clique facets contain \(v_i\), and precisely one does not. The \(v_i\) are vertices of \(Q_n\). Since \(a_n\) is simple and the exceptional maximum clique is different for different edges, its \(n\) neighboring vertices are precisely \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\).

**2. The exact cap.** The cited minimally-imperfect theorem implies that the only vertices of \(Q_n\) are the stable-set indicators and \(a_n\), and that
\(P_n=Q_n\cap\{\sum_i x_i\le2\}\). The tangent cone at the simple vertex \(a_n\) is generated by the rays \(v_i-a_n\), so every \(x\in Q_n\) can be written
\(x=a_n+\sum_{i=1}^n t_i(v_i-a_n)\) with \(t_i\ge0\). Since
\(\sum_j(a_n)_j=n/m=2+1/m\) and \(\sum_j(v_i)_j=2\), membership in the halfspace \(\sum_jx_j\ge2\) forces \(\sum_i t_i\le1\). This is exactly the convex-combination condition for the simplex with vertices \(a_n,v_1,\ldots,v_n\); the reverse inclusion is immediate.

**3. The exact volume.** Let \(B\) be the \(n\times n\) matrix with columns \(v_i\). For an odd cycle \(B=I+T\), where \(T\) is cyclic shift, hence \(\det B=2\) and \(B^{-1}\mathbf1=\tfrac12\mathbf1\). The matrix determinant lemma gives
\[
\det\left(B-\frac1m\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\right)
=2\left(1-\frac{n}{2m}\right)=-\frac1m.
\]
Its columns are precisely the differences \(v_i-a_n\). Dividing the absolute determinant by \(n!\) gives the stated Euclidean simplex volume.

**4. Exact moment.** A volume-uniform point of the cap has barycentric form \(X_n=a_n+\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i(v_i-a_n)\), with the \(n+1\) barycentric coordinates \((\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_n)\) uniformly Dirichlet \((1,\ldots,1)\). Their exact second moments are \(\mathbb E\lambda_i\lambda_j=(1+\mathbf1_{i=j})/((n+1)(n+2))\). The difference columns \(r_i=v_i-a_n\) satisfy
\[
\sum_i r_i=-\frac1m\mathbf1,\qquad
\sum_i\|r_i\|^2=n\left(2-\frac4m+\frac n{m^2}\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E\|X_n-a_n\|^2
=\frac{\|\sum_i r_i\|^2+\sum_i\|r_i\|^2}{(n+1)(n+2)}
=\frac{n(2-2/m+2/m^2)}{(n+1)(n+2)}.
\]

**5. Gaussian fluctuations.** Generate independent mean-one exponential variables \(E_0,E_1,\ldots,E_n\), with cyclic convention \(E_{0}^{\mathrm{cyc}}=E_n\) for the indexing of the variables \(E_1,\ldots,E_n\); the separate apex variable \(E_0\) is not part of this cycle. Let \(S=\sum_{j=1}^n E_j\), \(T=S+E_0\), \(\bar E=S/n\), and
\(M=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n(E_j+E_{j-1}^{\mathrm{cyc}})^2\), where \(E_{j-1}^{\mathrm{cyc}}=E_{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\) and \(E_0^{\mathrm{cyc}}=E_n\). The standard exponential representation of uniform Dirichlet barycentric coordinates gives
\[
(X_n-a_n)_j=\frac{E_j+E_{j-1}^{\mathrm{cyc}}-S/m}{T}.
\]
Expanding the square and using \(n=2m+1\) yields the *exact* identity
\[
nR_n^2=
\frac{M-4\bar E^2}{(\bar E+E_0/n)^2}
+\frac{4\bar E^2}{(n-1)^2(\bar E+E_0/n)^2}.
\]
As \(n\to\infty\), \(\bar E\to1\) and \(M\to6\) in probability by the law of large numbers for a one-dependent sequence. Thus \(nR_n^2\to2\) in probability; furthermore
\[
nR_n^2=\frac{M}{\bar E^2}-4+O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1}).
\]
For \(Y_j=(E_j+E_{j-1}^{\mathrm{cyc}})^2\), standard exponential moments \(\mathbb E E^k=k!\) give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(E_j)=1,\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(E_j,Y_j)=\operatorname{Cov}(E_j,Y_{j+1})=6,\quad
\operatorname{Var}(Y_j)=84,\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_j,Y_{j+1})=40,
\]
with all remaining cross-covariances zero outside the one-dependent range. The cyclic wrap modifies only a bounded number of summands; it does not change the limit. The joint one-dependent central limit theorem therefore gives
\[
\sqrt n\binom{\bar E-1}{M-6}
\xrightarrow{\mathsf d}N_2\!\left(0,
\begin{pmatrix}1&12\\12&164\end{pmatrix}\right).
\]
For the smooth map \(f(u,v)=v/u^2-4\), the gradient at \((1,6)\) is \((-12,1)\), and its limiting variance is
\[
(-12,1)\begin{pmatrix}1&12\\12&164\end{pmatrix}\binom{-12}{1}
=144-288+164=20.
\]
The delta method and the displayed \(O_{\mathsf P}(n^{-1})\) remainder establish the asserted Gaussian limit.

## Verification
The standalone script `antihole_verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. Running `python3 antihole_verify.py` performs exact-integer/rational checks for odd \(n=5,7,9,11,13\) of maximum-clique incidence, the two determinant identities, the cap-volume identity, and the exact second moment. It independently checks the exponential-moment covariance and the variance \(20\). These were run successfully, with 122 checks. Separate polyhedral halfspace enumeration for \(n=5,7,9\) found exactly \(2n+2\) clique-relaxation vertices, with no missing or extra vertices relative to the predicted incidence vectors and fractional apex. Those finite checks are diagnostic, not substitutes for the cited minimal-imperfection theorem or the analytic central limit theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Koster and Wagler (2006, Section 3.2), recalling Padberg's theorem, identify the unique fractional extreme point and the full-rank cut for every minimally imperfect graph. These structural premises are credited explicitly; they do not give the antipodal family’s determinant, ordinary cap volume, exact radius moment, or Gaussian law. Higashitani, Padrol, and Sanyal (2026) develop Minkowski indecomposability of \(0/1\) polytopes, including stable-set constructions; this motivates studying intrinsic quantitative geometry, which is not an indecomposability question. A separately published odd-*cycle* cap calculation yields a Brownian-functional, nonconcentrating radius limit. It studies the complementary graph's **edge** relaxation, not the present odd-*antihole* **clique** relaxation: graph complementation does not preserve the coordinate Euclidean geometry or turn its stated Brownian limit into the Gaussian law here. The pentagon is common to both families, but dimensions \(n\ge7\) are geometrically different.

## Limitations
No full finite-dimensional radial density, Berry–Esseen rate, or volume of the entire clique relaxation is claimed. The theorem uses known polyhedral structure of minimally imperfect graphs and does not claim that structure is new. The cap law concerns volume conditioned on the removed region, not typical unconditional points. Historical originality is contingent on relevant sources identified in the review and cannot be guaranteed by searches alone.
