# The stable Vietoris–Rips circle of the Möbius ladder, including the critical threshold

## Finding
For every pair of integers \(r\ge2\) and \(n\ge3r\), let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder with vertices \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), unit edges between consecutive residues and between antipodal residues. Use intrinsic shortest-path distance, and let \(K_r=\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(V(M_{2n}))\) be the **inclusive** flag complex of vertex subsets of diameter at most \(r\). Then
\[
K_r\simeq S^1,\qquad \pi_1(K_r)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad\widetilde H_i(K_r;\mathbb Z)=\begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&i=1,\\0,&i\ne1.\end{cases}
\]
For fixed \(n\ge6\), every persistence inclusion \(K_r\hookrightarrow K_s\) with \(2\le r\le s\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor\) is an isomorphism on fundamental groups and on integral homology. At scale one, the graph has cycle rank \(n+1\). Its map to scale two is surjective on integral first homology, with kernel free of rank \(n\). Consequently exactly \(n\) degree-one integral persistence intervals are \([1,2)\), and one further degree-one interval is born at scale one and survives through scale \(\lfloor n/3\rfloor\); its death scale is not asserted here.

The equality case \(n=3r\) is essential: the twisted ladder **retains its circle**, unlike the untwisted graph \(C_n\square K_2\), whose scale-\(r\) Rips complex at \(n=3r\) is a nontrivial wedge of two-spheres (for \(r\ge2\)) [3].

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(L=\mathbb Z\square K_2\) be the infinite ladder with vertices \((i,a)\), \(i\in\mathbb Z\), \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and distance
\[
d_L((i,a),(j,b))=|i-j|+\mathbf1_{a\ne b}.
\]
The fixed-point-free graph automorphism \(\tau(i,a)=(i+n,1-a)\) generates an infinite cyclic deck group. The graph quotient \(L/\langle\tau\rangle\) is exactly \(M_{2n}\), via the orbit representatives \((i,0)\mapsto i\) and \((i,1)\mapsto i+n\) modulo \(2n\). The quotient shortest-path metric satisfies
\[
d_{M_{2n}}([x],[y])=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}d_L(x,\tau^k y).
\]
All assertions concern the finite **vertex set** of the unweighted graph; no points in edge interiors are included. For \(r\ge2\), the bound \(n\ge3r\) also implies \(n>2r\).

## Proof
**Step 1: contractibility of the infinite-strip Rips complex.** Let \(\widetilde K_r=\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(V(L))\). For every finite integer interval \([a,b]\), let \(D_{a,b}\) be the full subcomplex on the two rows in those columns. Assume \(r\ge2\). If \(a<b\), the vertex \((a,0)\) is dominated by \((a+1,0)\): every vertex in \(D_{a,b}\) at distance at most \(r\) from \((a,0)\) is also at distance at most \(r\) from \((a+1,0)\). Indeed the column cannot be less than \(a\), and moving the base one column right can only increase the distance to a vertex in column \(a\); the only such vertex of the opposite row is now at distance two, which is allowed. All other distances weakly decrease. Removing \((a,0)\), the same domination holds for \((a,1)\) by \((a+1,1)\). Dominated-vertex deletion is a simplicial collapse in the associated flag complex: every maximal face containing the deleted vertex also contains its dominator. Repeating this process removes the leftmost columns until only the edge connecting the last two row vertices remains. Thus every \(D_{a,b}\) is contractible. Every map of a compact sphere into the locally finite simplicial complex \(\widetilde K_r\) has image in a finite strip and is null-homotopic there. Consequently \(\widetilde K_r\) is connected and weakly contractible, hence contractible by Whitehead's theorem for CW complexes.

**Step 2: unique lifting of Rips edges.** A vertex downstairs is an orbit under \(\tau\). Distinct deck translates of one lifted vertex differ horizontally by at least \(n\), hence lie at distance at least \(n>2r\). For any Rips edge \(\{[x],[y]\}\) downstairs, a chosen lift \(x\) therefore has **exactly one** lift \(\tau^k y\) of its other endpoint within distance \(r\). Existence follows from the quotient-distance formula, uniqueness from the triangle inequality and \(n>2r\).

**Step 3: no triangular deck winding, even at equality.** Take any Rips triangle downstairs and lift its three oriented edges consecutively. The final vertex is \(\tau^k(x)\) for some integer \(k\). Each edge changes the horizontal coordinate by at most \(r\), so \(|k|n\le3r\le n\). If \(k\ne0\), necessarily \(k=\pm1\), \(n=3r\), and all three horizontal changes have absolute value \(r\) with the same sign. But an edge of total ladder distance at most \(r\) and horizontal displacement exactly \(r\) cannot change row. Therefore three such edges preserve the initial row, while \(\tau^{\pm1}\) swaps rows—a contradiction. Hence \(k=0\): every downstairs Rips triangle has a unique compatible lift once one vertex is lifted.

**Step 4: higher simplices, covering, and homotopy.** Choose one vertex of any downstairs Rips simplex and a lift. Step 2 gives unique distance-\(r\) lifts of every other vertex. Every triangle with the chosen vertex lifts compatibly by Step 3, so every pair of chosen lifts also has distance at most \(r\). Thus the entire simplex lifts, and its lift is unique once the initial vertex is fixed. In particular the deck group has no simplex inversions and the simplicial quotient \(\widetilde K_r/\langle\tau\rangle\) is exactly \(K_r\); the geometric-realization map is a regular covering. Its total space \(\widetilde K_r\) is contractible by Step 1, with deck group \(\langle\tau\rangle\cong\mathbb Z\). Thus \(K_r\) is an Eilenberg–Mac Lane space \(K(\mathbb Z,1)\). The standard circle is another CW model of \(K(\mathbb Z,1)\), so \(K_r\simeq S^1\), integrally.

**Step 5: persistence maps.** For fixed \(n\), the same deck transformation and contractible covering space work for every \(2\le r\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor\), and the inclusions of the lifted complexes commute with \(\tau\). Under the deck-group identification of all fundamental groups with \(\mathbb Z\), the induced maps are the identity. Hence all stable-scale homology maps are isomorphisms. At scale one, the cubic graph has \(2n\) vertices and \(3n\) edges, so its first integral Betti number is \(n+1\). The path along \(n\) horizontal edges from \((0,0)\) to \((n,0)\), followed by a single rung to \((n,1)=\tau(0,0)\), projects to a closed graph loop whose lift represents the deck generator. Its image generates \(H_1(K_2;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), proving surjectivity and a rank-\(n\) free kernel. All stated persistence conclusions follow.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_twisted_ladder.py` with the standard Python library. The verifier independently constructs shortest-path distances in the quotient graph, checks the deck-distance formula for every vertex pair, tests unique edge lifts and compatible triangle lifts exhaustively at all cases \(2\le r\le5\), \(3r\le n\le3r+2\), and enumerates all simplices and reduced Euler characteristics for ten of these cases. In two strict-lower-bound samples, exact binary boundary ranks give noncircle homology: at \((n,r)=(5,2)\) the only nonzero reduced mod-two Betti number is \(\beta_2=1\), and at \((8,3)\) it is \(\beta_3=1\). The recorded terminating line is `TWISTED_LADDER_STABLE_RIPS_CHECKS_OK`. These computations are finite cross-checks, **not** an infinite proof; the argument in Steps 1–5 is uniform.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak [1] classify independence and perfect-matching complexes of Möbius and circular ladders; those complexes have different faces and do not classify the graph-metric Rips construction studied here. Adams and collaborators [2] study square toroidal grids, not Möbius ladders, and their homotopy stability addresses a two-dimensional torus rather than a one-dimensional twisted graph quotient.

A previously indexed classification [3] states that the *untwisted* clique-fiber cycle \(K_m\square C_n\), at \(r\ge2\), has type \(S^1\) for \(n>3r\) but a wedge of \((mr-1)\) two-spheres at \(n=3r\). It does not imply the critical-equality Möbius statement: the graph \(M_{2n}\) is a deck **glide** quotient, not a Cartesian-product quotient, and its triangle-winding obstruction has opposite behavior at equality. An indexed finite circular-ladder scale-two classification [4] also concerns the untwisted prism. The twisted square-grid classification [5] assumes both grid widths at least seven and gives a Klein-bottle homotopy type at radius two, not the width-two glide quotient or arbitrary radius. Classical metric-gluing work [6] studies continuous metric graphs and specific gluing regimes, not these finite graph vertices with antipodal identification. No checked statement supplies this result as a special case. Absence of a match is not proof of novelty; see the limitations.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify the complexes for \(n<3r\), noninteger or weighted metrics, graph edge interiors, or equivariant homotopy types. The degree-one interval surviving through the stable range may die later; its death is not determined. The exact finite boundary examples establish only mod-two homology for those two small cases. An equivalent theorem in unindexed work or a stronger applicable graph-covering theorem could still exist. No independent validation has been performed.
