# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a complete nonexpansivity threshold, not a purported exact finite-time norm. Cut reduction is proved via layer cake for constant-preserving linear maps. The generator derivative is computed pairwise including within-cut collisions. Rational enumeration of **all 502 cut representatives** for sizes 2–9 verifies the complete finite set needed for the \(n\le9\) assertion; each certificate value is cross-checked against independent direct Euler-step variation. Exponential-product submultiplicativity establishes all-time contraction. The neutral nine-site case is strictly contractive by analyticity and ergodic convergence. For all larger sizes, the chosen singleton has derivative \((n-9)/2\), and a uniform sup-norm Taylor remainder yields the explicit positive-time interval. The analytic argument, not the 10–100 test, establishes the unrestricted large-dimension quantifier.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority qualified)

Relevant primary full texts were checked: Fill–Kahn on birth–death comparison and mixing, and Gaubert–Qu on Hopf oscillation/Dobrushin dual variation. Neither claims spatial complete-pair Gini contraction of the degree-normalized path semigroup or gives the nine/ten cutoff. Published statements concerning graph-edge total variation on weighted trees and graph-metric Lipschitz amplification on paths concern different norms and do not imply this all-pairs threshold directly. The closest indexed titles were compared by the mathematical statement and norm, not merely by name. The complete texts of the nearest indexed graph-edge-variation items were not retrievable; possible unindexed equivalent calculations remain a genuine residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first nine-to-ten vertex boundary is a natural size classification for a canonical Markov averaging process in a natural statistical-dispersion seminorm. It identifies the precise point at which unequal stationary degree weights can defeat naive ``smoothing always reduces spread'' intuition, with an actual witness and interval, while the nine-vertex borderline has a zero logarithmic norm yet strictly contracts for each positive time. Neither a norm renaming nor a general trivial property of stochastic matrices produces the threshold: the path's endpoint rates and complete-pair variation interact in a nonlocal way. The closed classification has independent value even without a closed formula for every finite-time norm.

## Relevant boundaries

Only real signals, standard unit paths, the specified degree normalization, continuous-time semigroups, and complete-pair unweighted Gini are covered. Graph-edge total variation, oscillation, total variation between probability measures, and alternative Markov generators are distinct. Literature search coverage is not a uniqueness proof.
