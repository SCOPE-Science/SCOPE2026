# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For every integer \(c\ge1,m\ge2\), the stable-set convex hull equals the explicit \(c+2m\)-inequality body. The proof enumerates the six real codimension-two facet-pair types, with the diagonal \(B_j,C_j\) intersection correctly excluded as lower-dimensional. Each ridge measure is obtained from a Dirichlet integral and a Euclidean affine-Jacobian factor. Scalar products of three normal classes determine exactly the three distinct exterior dihedral angles. Summing with the correct \(1/(2\pi)\) Steiner normalization gives the area and \(V_{N-2}\) expressions. An independent affine-hull/convex-hull checker passed seven parameter profiles with actual facet/ridge measurements; those finite experiments support, but are not the all-parameter proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority limits.** The 2018-04-02 primary stable-set-polytope paper by Aliniaeifard et al. discusses the defining geometry, edges and faces but the inspected sections do not give angle-weighted Euclidean ridge measures or a second Steiner coefficient. Hu and Laurent's 2019 full-text discussion gives the perfect-graph clique inequality characterization, not these metric evaluations. The closest indexed results on complete-split stable-set polytopes give isotropic-constant and covariance information, which cannot imply boundary curvature without additional facet integration. The earlier order-polytope curvature results concern a different coordinate realization and a different class of antichain layers; Stanley's transfer is not an isometry. Relevant indexed and primary sources were searched by object, facets, curvature, parallel-body coefficients, possible aliases and formula. A published but unindexed explicit curvature calculation remains a residual risk; an unavailable historical source was not used as proof of absence.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The theorem gives a complete ridge-area-and-angle classification for the natural complete-split stable-set family, including the first two geometric parallel-body coefficients in arbitrary dimension. It exhibits a cancellation not apparent from the combinatorial model: the normalized angle-class ridge areas are independent of the independent-set size even though the number of polytope vertices is \(c+2^m\). Exact natural boundary invariants with a complete classification are useful separately from any conjectured downstream theorem. A single facet count or volume integral would not have supported the same value judgment; the all-parameter, angle-resolved second coefficient does.

**Sources and limitations.** Aliniaeifard et al., arXiv:1804.00360 (2018-04-02, especially introduction, §§2.3 and 7.3); Hu–Laurent, *European J. Combin.* 80 (2019), §§1–2.2; Stanley (1986) for the classical chain-polytope context. Also compared were indexed findings on stable-set isotropic constants, bulk covariance, and prior order-polytope boundary coefficients. The general Steiner ridge formula is standard and is credited as an input, not a new assertion. This review is limited to the indicated sources, exact proof and finite checker; no external independent audit was performed.
