# Three-column total three-cut complexes of complete-clique paths

## Finding
For each integer \(m\geq2\), let \(G_m=K_m\square P_3\), with three consecutive columns \(A,B,C\), each a complete graph on the row set \(\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}\); adjacent columns have edges exactly between corresponding rows. Let \(\Delta^t_3(G_m)\) be the simplicial complex generated by complements of independent three-vertex subsets of \(G_m\). Then there is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\Delta^t_3(K_m\square P_3)\simeq\bigvee^{(m-1)^2}S^{3m-6}.
\]
For \(m=2\), the right side is \(S^0\), interpreted as two points. In particular the complete reduced integral homology is free of rank \((m-1)^2\) in degree \(3m-6\), and zero in all other degrees. This establishes the three-column, arbitrary-complete-factor case of the conjectural total-three-cut homology pattern for \(K_m\square P_n\) formulated in [1, Conjecture 4.3], with the stronger conclusion of an integral homotopy classification.

## Assumptions and scope
All complexes are finite, abstract, and realized geometrically. In the graph \(G_m\), write the vertices \(A_i,B_i,C_i\) for \(0\leq i<m\). Every pair within one column is adjacent; between successive columns the only edges are \(A_iB_i\) and \(B_iC_i\); there are no edges between \(A\) and \(C\). A triple is independent if and only if it is \(\{A_i,B_j,C_k\}\) with \(i\ne j\) and \(k\ne j\). Thus, for \(\sigma\subseteq V(G_m)\),
\[
\sigma\in\Delta^t_3(G_m)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\;i,j,k\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}:\ i\ne j,\ k\ne j,\quad
\{A_i,B_j,C_k\}\cap\sigma=\varnothing.
\]
In particular the complex is nonvoid for all \(m\geq2\), and has \(m(m-1)^2\) facets, each of cardinality \(3m-3\). Its pure dimension \(3m-4\) is generally **two greater** than its homotopy dimension. The dimension of the final spheres is \(3m-6\), not the facet dimension.

## Proof
We give a uniform acyclic face-poset matching. Faces, including the empty face, are processed in the following coordinate order:
\[
A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{m-1},\quad B_0,B_1,\ldots,B_{m-1},\quad C_0,C_1,\ldots,C_{m-1}.
\]
At a coordinate \(v\), pair every two remaining faces \(\tau\) and \(\tau\cup\{v\}\), with \(v\notin\tau\), whenever both remain; remove these pairs before moving to the next coordinate. The sequential element-matching lemma [1, Lemma 2.5] proves acyclicity: each single-coordinate matching is acyclic, and successively adjoining such matchings on the remaining face family preserves acyclicity. We now identify the survivors for every \(m\), not just for sampled widths.

**First, the \(A\)-coordinates.** Fix the selected \(B\)-vertices and \(C\)-vertices. Let \(Q\) and \(R\) be the sets of *missing* row indices in columns \(B\) and \(C\), and define
\[
J(Q,R)=\{j\in Q:R\setminus\{j\}\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
The allowable selected \(A\)-sets \(S\) satisfy \(\exists j\in J(Q,R)\) and \(\exists i\notin S\) with \(i\ne j\). If \(J\) is empty there are no faces in this fiber. If \(|J|=1\), say \(J=\{j\}\), the fiber is the cone
\(\partial\Delta(\{0,\dots,m-1\}\setminus\{j\})*\Delta(\{j\})\): membership is invariant under toggling \(A_j\). Previous-coordinate matchings respect this free cone coordinate, so the \(A_j\)-matching eliminates its entire fiber. If \(|J|\ge2\), the fiber consists of all **proper** subsets of the \(m\) row indices: any missing index \(i\) can be paired with some \(j\in J\setminus\{i\}\). Thus it is the boundary of the \((m-1)\)-simplex. Matching at \(A_0\) pairs every face not containing \(A_0\) of size at most \(m-2\), leaving only \(\{A_1,\dots,A_{m-1}\}\). No later \(A\)-matching changes this survivor. Hence after the \(A\)-block, all and only the faces
\[
\{A_1,\dots,A_{m-1}\}\cup S_B\cup S_C
\quad\text{with }|J(Q,R)|\ge2
\]
remain.

**Next, the \(B\)-coordinates.** Fix \(S_C\), hence \(R\). If \(|R|\ge2\), then \(J(Q,R)=Q\) because for each \(j\) at least one different missing \(C\)-index exists. The condition \(|J|\ge2\) is \(|Q|\ge2\), i.e. \(|S_B|\le m-2\). This fiber is exactly the \((m-3)\)-skeleton of the full simplex on the \(m\) \(B\)-vertices (with \(m=2\) interpreted as its empty-face skeleton). Matching at \(B_0\) leaves precisely the \(B\)-sets of size \(m-2\) contained in \(\{B_1,\dots,B_{m-1}\}\), numbering \(m-1\). If \(R=\{k\}\), then \(J(Q,R)=Q\setminus\{k\}\) and the fiber condition is \(|Q\setminus\{k\}|\ge2\). It is invariant under toggling \(B_k\), so its entire fiber is matched at \(B_k\). If \(R\) is empty no faces remain. Hence after the \(B\)-block, the surviving faces have an arbitrary \((m-2)\)-subset of \(\{B_1,\dots,B_{m-1}\}\), together with an \(S_C\) satisfying \(|R|\ge2\).

**Finally, the \(C\)-coordinates.** The remaining \(C\)-sets are exactly those of size at most \(m-2\). Matching at \(C_0\) leaves the \((m-2)\)-subsets of \(\{C_1,\dots,C_{m-1}\}\), numbering \(m-1\). No later matching can change them.

It follows that the complete list of unmatched faces is
\[
\begin{split}
&\{A_1,\dots,A_{m-1}\}\\[-1mm]
&\qquad\cup\bigl(\{B_1,\dots,B_{m-1}\}\setminus\{B_b\}\bigr)\\[-1mm]
&\qquad\cup\bigl(\{C_1,\dots,C_{m-1}\}\setminus\{C_c\}\bigr),
\qquad 1\le b,c\le m-1.
\end{split}
\]
There are exactly \((m-1)^2\) such faces. Each has \((m-1)+2(m-2)=3m-5\) vertices and hence dimension \(3m-6\). The empty face is matched: for instance the empty face pairs with \(\{A_0\}\) in the first stage because both belong to the complex. By the discrete-Morse cell theorem (with the empty-face convention in [1, Theorem 2.7]), the resulting CW model has one additional base vertex and exactly these \((m-1)^2\) cells, all in the single dimension \(3m-6\). For \(m\ge3\) this is a wedge of \((m-1)^2\) spheres, as there are no intermediate-dimensional attaching cells. For \(m=2\) the model has exactly two vertices, hence \(S^0\). This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

## Verification
The self-contained standard-library Python program `verify.py` independently constructs the Cartesian-product graph, enumerates all independent triples using adjacency tests (rather than the proof's fiber classification), builds all faces from the complements of those triples, applies the specified sequential matching, and checks the directed Hasse graph for cycles by topological sorting. For \(m=2,3,4,5\), it confirms the **exact identities** of all critical cells and checks every incidence, not only homology ranks. Reproduce with `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing the standalone script. The captured run output is in `verify_output.txt`. These four finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the symbolic proof for all \(m\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Chauhan, Shukla and Vinayak [1] classify total **two**-cut complexes of products of complete graphs with paths and cycles, but leave total **three**-cut complexes of \(K_m\square P_n\) as Conjecture 4.3, giving the predicted integral homology. Setting the path length parameter in their conjecture to three predicts \((m-1)^2\) spheres in degree \(3m-6\); their table treats finite instances rather than supplying the uniform homotopy conclusion. The present proof supplies a direct all-\(m\) acyclic matching and an integral homotopy classification for that first three-column slice. Carnero Bravo [2] develops Alexander-duality methods for total cut complexes and classifies total two-cut complexes of certain Cartesian products; its Cartesian-product results do not establish this total three-cut family. The object is not the ordinary matching complex or the ordinary three-cut complex; those are distinct constructions.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to exactly three path vertices and a complete graph in the other factor; it does not prove the conjectural formula for arbitrary \(n\ge4\), or determine automorphism actions or inclusion maps. Discrete-Morse acyclicity is proved symbolically; no claim of independent review is made. An unindexed equivalent or stronger published classification remains a possible literature overlap.
