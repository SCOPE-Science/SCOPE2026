# Three consecutive sphere homotopy types for generalized Petersen two-matching complexes
## Finding
For the generalized Petersen graphs at the first three indices beyond the ten-vertex Petersen graph, their degree-two matching complexes are **single spheres**:
\[
\boxed{M_2(\operatorname{GP}(6,2))\simeq S^8,\quad M_2(\operatorname{GP}(7,2))\simeq S^{10},\quad M_2(\operatorname{GP}(8,2))\simeq S^{12}.}
\]
All statements concern integral homotopy types, not only rational homology or finite-field Betti numbers.

## Assumptions and scope
For an integer \(n\in\{6,7,8\}\), define \(G_n=\operatorname{GP}(n,2)\) with vertices \(\{u_i,v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\) and the \(3n\) edges \(u_iu_{i+1}\), \(u_iv_i\), \(v_iv_{i+2}\). All subscripts are read modulo \(n\). Each \(G_n\) is a finite, simple cubic graph on \(2n\) vertices. The simplicial complex \(M_2(G_n)\) has one complex vertex for each graph edge. Its faces are exactly the edge sets \(F\subseteq E(G_n)\) for which every graph vertex has at most two incident edges in \(F\). The empty face is included in enumerations, while geometric realization uses the usual nonempty face poset. The theorem does not assume any quotient by graph automorphisms.

## Proof
**Complete face enumeration.** Number the graph edges in this order: all \(u_iu_{i+1}\), then all \(u_iv_i\), then all \(v_iv_{i+2}\), each group with \(i\) increasing from zero. Represent an edge subset by a \(3n\)-bit integer. Recursively branch on omission/inclusion of each edge; allow inclusion only when both endpoint degrees remain below two. Each graph-edge subset has exactly one such branch sequence, so this recursively enumerates all faces without sampling or omitted cardinalities. The augmented face counts for \(n=6,7,8\) are respectively \(69{,}415\), \(447{,}641\), and \(2{,}873{,}983\). Their full \(f\)-vectors, all face-poset covers, and the checks of every endpoint-degree constraint are regenerated by the embedded verifier.

**Acyclic Morse matching.** Start with all nonempty faces unmatched. For each complex vertex in the displayed graph-edge order, toggle that vertex: pair each still-unmatched face not containing it with its one-vertex extension whenever that extension is a still-unmatched face. Remove both members before the next toggle. The procedure gives \(34{,}704\), \(223{,}819\), and \(1{,}436{,}990\) matching pairs. The critical-cell counts indexed by dimension are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
n & \text{critical Morse cells}\\ \hline
6&0:1,\quad8:3,\quad9:2\\
7&0:1,\quad10:1\\
8&0:1,\quad12:1.
\end{array}
\]
For every pair the verifier confirms a genuine codimension-one cover. It then checks acyclicity of the complete gradient graph: for a paired upper face, follow each alternate codimension-one lower face to its matched upper face, if any. A directed cycle in the reversed-matching full Hasse diagram must contain matched upward incidences and therefore projects to a directed cycle in this graph. Conversely, a completed topological ordering rules out all such cycles. The verifier checks every such transition and visits all paired upper faces. Thus discrete Morse theory provides genuine homotopy-equivalent CW reductions, not a heuristic simplification.

**Attaching maps and integral conclusion.** For \(n=7\) and \(n=8\), the resulting CW complex has exactly one vertex and one cell in dimension ten or twelve, and no other cells. Its realization is exactly the corresponding sphere. For \(n=6\) the Morse CW model has one vertex, three eight-cells, and two nine-cells. The eight-skeleton is \(\bigvee^3 S^8\); because it is seven-connected, its eighth homotopy group is \(\mathbb Z^3\) by the Hurewicz theorem. Thus the homotopy classes of both attaching maps are completely determined by their **integral** degrees. With critical eight-cell masks \(58834,107986,231114\) and critical nine-cell masks \(114066,224100\), signed integer Morse elimination gives the full boundary matrix
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\-1&-1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Elementary integer row/column operations reduce this matrix to \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1)\), with one zero row. The operations are unimodular and realized by homotopy equivalences on the relevant sphere wedges; they cancel two eight/nine CW pairs, leaving exactly one eight-cell attached to the vertex. Therefore \(M_2(G_6)\simeq S^8\) integrally. This is stronger than a calculation of its Euler characteristic or homology ranks.

**Independent matching-order cross-check.** Reverse the complete edge order and reconstruct **every** face and match anew. The reversed matchings leave critical cells \((0:1,8:2,9:1)\), \((0:1,9:1,10:2)\), and \((0:1,11:1,12:2)\), respectively. Their corresponding signed integral differential matrices are \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\end{pmatrix}\), and \(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}\). Each contains a unit pivot and reduces to exactly one cell in degree eight, ten, or twelve, independently corroborating the result.

## Verification
Run the embedded Python-standard-library-only checker in two separate modes, from its extracted actual path:
```
python3 verification/verify_gp_m2.py
python3 verification/verify_gp_m2.py --reverse
```
The two outputs are reproduced in `verification/forward.json` and `verification/reverse.json`. Each invocation enumerates all faces, independently checks the cubic graph construction and every retained face, constructs a complete matching, checks that matching pairs are codimension-one incidences, proves gradient acyclicity by exhaustive directed topological sorting, and performs exact signed integer Morse elimination for the necessary adjacent-dimensional cells. The total numbers of nonempty face-poset covers checked per graph are \(488{,}208\), \(3{,}678{,}829\), and \(26{,}998{,}440\). The reduced Euler characteristic is \(+1\) for all three graphs, independently matching the Euler characteristic of an even-dimensional sphere. Checks use exact integers and no network, random seeds, external packages, or cached certificate paths.

## Relationship to prior work
Vega (arXiv:1909.10406) defines two-matching complexes and proves homotopy results for selected clawed, wheel, and caterpillar graph families. Singh (arXiv:2006.13632) analyzes higher-matching complexes of complete and complete-bipartite graphs. Neither inspected theorem includes these three step-two generalized Petersen graphs. Agarwal–Basak (arXiv:2608.30601, first posted 2026-08-31) motivates homotopy classifications for highly symmetric cubic graphs, but their independence and perfect-matching-extendability complexes of Möbius ladders are not bounded-degree-two edge-subset complexes.

The previously determined Petersen case \(M_2(\operatorname{GP}(5,2))\simeq\bigvee^4 S^7\) is an adjacent lower boundary, **not** a general theorem implying the three subsequent cases. Another reported result gives \(M_2\) of the fourteen-vertex Heawood graph as \(\bigvee^6S^{10}\), showing that the fourteen-vertex cubic graph size does not determine the sphere multiplicity. Work on \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(\operatorname{GP}(n,2))\) classifies vertex-subset higher-independence complexes, an essentially different functor from \(M_2\), whose vertices are graph edges. Research-record, alias, parameter and implication searches found no covering exact three-case classification. This negative search result is not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Limitations
The theorem is **only** for the three finite indices \(n=6,7,8\). It does not prove a formula for larger generalized Petersen graphs, assert symmetry-module decompositions, establish shellability, or classify arbitrary cubic graphs. The inductive-looking sequence of sphere dimensions is evidence for a potential structural study, not a proved infinite pattern. Numerical enumerations alone do not imply homotopy types; the complete acyclic matchings and the exact integer attaching-degree calculations supply those implications. An unindexed equivalent computation may exist. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
