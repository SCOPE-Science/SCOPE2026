# Three-degree integral homology of two-path visibility complexes of balanced complete tripartite graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), the one-fault-tolerant mutual-visibility complex \(V_n\) of \(K_{n,n,n}\) has reduced integral homology
\[
\widetilde H_j(V_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{3(n-1)(2n-1)},&j=2n-1,\\
\mathbb Z^{3(n-1)^2},&j=3n-5,\\
\mathbb Z,&j=3n-4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
All groups are free abelian. The three indicated degrees are distinct for \(n\ge5\). In particular, \(n=5\) gives ranks \(108,48,1\) in degrees \(9,10,11\); \(n=6\) gives ranks \(165,75,1\) in degrees \(11,13,14\).

## Assumptions and scope
Let the vertex set be the disjoint union \(P_1\sqcup P_2\sqcup P_3\), with \(|P_i|=n\). Two vertices in different shores are adjacent. A subset \(S\) is allowed if every two **nonadjacent** vertices in \(S\) have at least two internally disjoint shortest connecting paths with no internal vertex in \(S\). Adjacent pairs impose no condition, following the one-fault-tolerant convention of Cicerone–Di Stefano–Klavžar–Zhang. Subsets of an allowed set remain allowed, so they form the simplicial complex \(V_n\). The integer bound \(n\ge5\) ensures the three homology degrees do not coincide and that the auxiliary high-dimensional joins have no homology through degree two.

## Proof
**1. Geodesic criterion and Alexander dual.** If \(u,v\in P_i\) are distinct, every shortest \(u,v\)-path is \(u,w,v\) with \(w\in P_j\sqcup P_k\), where \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\). Such paths have distinct internal vertices. Consequently, a set \(S\) is a face precisely when, for each \(i\), either \(|S\cap P_i|\le1\), or at least two vertices outside \(P_i\) are unselected.

Write \(T=P_1\sqcup P_2\sqcup P_3\setminus S\) and let \(Y_n=V_n^*\) be the combinatorial Alexander dual. The criterion implies
\[
Y_n=A_1\cup A_2\cup A_3,\qquad
A_i=\{T:|T\cap P_i|\le n-2,\ |T\setminus P_i|\le1\}.
\]
This is an equality of simplicial complexes, not just equality of facets. Thus
\[
A_i=\operatorname{sk}_{n-3}\Delta^{n-1}*D_{2n},
\]
where \(D_{2n}\) is the discrete \(2n\)-vertex complex. The simplex skeleton has reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z^{n-1}\) in degree \(n-3\), and no other reduced homology; equivalently it is a wedge of \(n-1\) spheres of that dimension. The join therefore has
\[
\widetilde H_{n-2}(A_i;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{(n-1)(2n-1)},
\]
with all other reduced homology zero. For \(n\ge5\), in particular \(H_1(A_i)=H_2(A_i)=0\).

**2. Intersections.** For distinct shores \(i,j,k\), the complex \(A_i\cap A_j\) consists of the complete bipartite graph on \(P_i\sqcup P_j\) and the \(n\) isolated vertices of \(P_k\). It has \(n+1\) connected components and free first homology of rank \((n-1)^2\). For \(U=A_1\cup A_2\), the integral Mayer–Vietoris sequence gives
\[
H_1(U)\cong\mathbb Z^n,\quad H_2(U)\cong\mathbb Z^{(n-1)^2},\quad
H_{n-2}(U)\cong\mathbb Z^{2(n-1)(2n-1)},
\]
and no other positive-degree homology. The description of \(H_1(U)\) is the reduced component group of \(A_1\cap A_2\), freely generated by the \(n\) isolated \(P_3\)-vertices relative to its single bipartite component.

**3. The decisive integral map.** The intersection \(W=U\cap A_3\) is precisely the connected graph \(K_{2n,n}\), with parts \(P_1\sqcup P_2\) and \(P_3\). Its first homology has rank \((2n-1)(n-1)\). The inclusion map
\[
\phi:H_1(W;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_1(U;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{P_3}
\]
has image exactly the primitive augmentation sublattice
\[
\ker\bigl(\mathbb Z^{P_3}\stackrel{\Sigma}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb Z\bigr).
\]
Indeed, a four-cycle in \(W\) using a vertex of \(P_1\), one of \(P_2\), and two vertices \(x,y\in P_3\) maps, under the Mayer–Vietoris connecting homomorphism for \(U\), to the difference \([x]-[y]\). These differences generate the augmentation sublattice. Every cycle in the complete bipartite graph \(W\) is generated by four-cycles; a four-cycle whose two non-\(P_3\) vertices lie in the same \(P_i\) lies wholly in \(A_i\) and maps to zero. Thus no other image is possible. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{coker}\phi\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad
\ker\phi\cong\mathbb Z^{2(n-1)^2}.
\]
The latter rank follows by subtracting \(n-1\) from \((2n-1)(n-1)\), and the subgroup is primitive because it is the kernel of the sum map.

**4. Final Mayer–Vietoris and duality.** Apply integral Mayer–Vietoris to \(Y_n=U\cup A_3\). Both terms and \(W\) are connected. The preceding exact groups imply
\[
\widetilde H_1(Y_n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,\qquad
\widetilde H_2(Y_n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{3(n-1)^2},\qquad
\widetilde H_{n-2}(Y_n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{3(n-1)(2n-1)},
\]
and all other reduced homology vanishes. The short exact sequence for degree two splits because its quotient \(\ker\phi\) is free; no torsion is concealed by the sequence. Since the three homology groups are free, universal coefficients give reduced cohomology of the same ranks and degrees. Combinatorial Alexander duality for the \(3n\)-vertex ground set states
\[
\widetilde H_j(V_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\widetilde H^{3n-j-3}(Y_n;\mathbb Z).
\]
The degrees \(1,2,n-2\) in \(Y_n\) correspond respectively to \(3n-4,3n-5,2n-1\) in \(V_n\), proving the displayed result integrally for every integer \(n\ge5\).

## Verification
The included `verify.py` independently enumerates all \(2^{3n}\) vertex subsets for \(n=5,6\), tests the two-clear-geodesic condition using graph-neighbor intersections, and compares the resulting dual face set with the cover \(A_1\cup A_2\cup A_3\). For both sizes it computes every dual simplicial boundary rank over \(\mathbb F_2\). There are respectively \(750\) and \(2076\) nonempty dual faces. The resulting reduced mod-two Betti vectors agree in all degrees with the integral formula: at \(n=5\), \((\widetilde\beta_1,\widetilde\beta_2,\widetilde\beta_3)=(1,48,108)\); at \(n=6\), \((\widetilde\beta_1,\widetilde\beta_2,\widetilde\beta_4)=(1,75,165)\). These are finite corroborations, not substitutes for the unrestricted integral proof. Run `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3; output should match `verification_output.txt` exactly.

## Relationship to prior work
Cicerone, Di Stefano, Klavžar and Zhang introduced one-fault-tolerant mutual visibility, where a nonadjacent selected pair must have two clear internally disjoint shortest paths. Their 2025 preprint studies extremal cardinalities, complexity and graph families including grid-like and product networks, not the topology of the entire tripartite family of allowable sets. Combinatorial Alexander duality and simplex-skeleton homology are standard established tools; the contribution here is their explicit multipartite union-and-intersection computation, including the primitive lattice map that yields three separate homology degrees. Prior calculations of **bipartite** redundancy-two visibility complexes do not determine the tripartite case. Results on *total mutual visibility* use a different selection predicate and do not supply the displayed homology formula.

## Limitations
Only balanced, three-shore complete multipartite graphs with shore size \(n\ge5\) and two required clear geodesics are classified. No claim is made for other fault-tolerance levels, unequal shores, arbitrary graphs, cup products, fundamental groups, or a sphere-wedge homotopy decomposition. Published or unindexed equivalent homology classifications may exist; the present search cannot certify global uniqueness. The finite verifier works over \(\mathbb F_2\) and cannot alone exclude integral torsion; torsion-freeness comes from the explicit free-lattice Mayer–Vietoris proof.
