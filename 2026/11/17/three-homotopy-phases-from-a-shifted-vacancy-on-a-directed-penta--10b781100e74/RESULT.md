# Three homotopy phases from a shifted vacancy on a directed pentagonal rose

## Finding
Let \(R\) be the nine-vertex directed rose formed by the two consistently oriented pentagons
\[
0\to1\to2\to3\to4\to0,\qquad 0\to5\to6\to7\to8\to0.
\]
For each \(h\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), put one grain at each private vertex except vertices \(1\) and \(5+h\), and put zero grains at the common vertex \(0\). Thus all four initial configurations have six grains. Let \(A_h\) denote the avalanche complex generated by the successive parallel firing sets, with a vertex firing whenever its grain count is at least its outdegree. Then the **complete vacancy-shift classification** is
\[
|A_0|\simeq S^1,\qquad |A_1|\simeq S^2\vee S^2,\qquad
|A_2|\simeq *,\qquad |A_3|\simeq *.
\]
All equivalences are integral homotopy equivalences, rather than merely rational or mod-two homology assertions. The symmetric case \(h=0\) is an instance of the pre-existing synchronized-cycle result; the asymmetric cases \(h=1,2,3\) are the substantive new part.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple, has the specified nine vertices and ten directed edges, with no sink. The common vertex has outdegree two; every other vertex has outdegree one. A firing step simultaneously fires **every** vertex whose current number of grains is at least its outdegree, sends one grain along each outgoing arc, and does not use any additional stabilization or random choice. The complex \(A_h\) is the downward closure of all firing sets from the initial state through its first recurring state. The four shifts exhaust all possible locations of the second private-arm vacancy while the first vacancy remains at vertex \(1\); rotations moving the common vertex are not permitted.

## Proof
**1. Exact dynamics.** Write \(c^t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{9}\) for the chip vector at time \(t\), and \(\sigma_t=\{v:c^t(v)\ge \deg^+(v)\}\). Then
\[
c^{t+1}(v)=c^t(v)-\deg^+(v)\mathbf 1_{v\in\sigma_t}
+\sum_{u\to v}\mathbf1_{u\in\sigma_t}.
\]
The total number of grains remains six. Iterating this nine-coordinate recurrence with exact nonnegative integers proves the following exhaustive recurrence data; the stated first-repeat times are obtained by comparing the whole chip vectors, not merely firing-set labels:

| Shift \(h\) | Start of periodic orbit | Number of states before first repeat | Number of maximal firing sets |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 5 | 5 |
| 1 | 4 | 9 | 8 |
| 2 | 4 | 9 | 7 |
| 3 | 4 | 9 | 6 |

The recurrence is finite and exhaustive because the initial vector, all directed arcs, the firing rule, and each repeat comparison are implemented directly in `verify.py`. Once a chip vector repeats, determinism makes every later firing set recur as well.

**2. Valid nerve replacement.** Let \(\mathcal F_h\) be the set of maximal firing sets, and let \(N_h\) be the nerve of the family of their full simplices: a nonempty index set is a face exactly when the corresponding firing sets intersect. Each nonempty finite intersection is itself a simplex and hence contractible. The finite simplicial nerve lemma therefore gives \(|A_h|\simeq|N_h|\). The nerve face totals in increasing shift order are \(20,129,75,39\). Every nerve face is enumerated by testing the indicated actual vertex intersections, not inferred from homology.

**3. Constructive simplicial collapses.** In each nerve, repeatedly take a nonempty face contained in exactly one immediate coface, preferring the greatest coface dimension and then lexicographic order; remove that free face and its unique coface. The resulting deterministic elementary collapses use respectively \(7,45,37,19\) face pairs. For \(h=0\) the remainder is a three-cycle on the nerve vertices \(1,3,4\). For \(h=2\) and \(h=3\) the remainders each consist of one vertex. These prove three of the four homotopy statements.

**4. Exceptional shifted phase.** When \(h=1\), the \(45\) elementary collapse pairs leave exactly eight vertices, eighteen edges, and thirteen triangles. In the zero-based nerve-vertex indexing induced by chronological first appearance of the maximal firing sets, the following eleven edge–triangle pairs form a partial acyclic discrete-Morse matching:
\[
\begin{gathered}
(56,256),(07,037),(14,147),(27,257),(67,267),\\
(15,157),(16,156),(36,356),(35,357),(06,067),(47,467).
\end{gathered}
\]
Here `56` abbreviates the edge with vertices \(5,6\) and `256` the triangle with vertices \(2,5,6\), and similarly for the other pairs. The seven remaining edges are
\[
\{37,46,03,57,17,26,25\};
\]
they form a spanning tree on all eight vertices. Root this tree at vertex \(0\) and match each other vertex to its unique parent edge. The resulting matching is globally acyclic: `verify.py` constructs the directed full Hasse diagram with paired incidences reversed and performs a complete topological sort. Its unmatched cells are precisely one vertex and two triangles. Discrete Morse theory therefore gives a CW model with one zero-cell, no one-cells, and two two-cells. Each two-cell attaches to a point, so the CW model is \(S^2\vee S^2\). This proves the exceptional phase **integrally**.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` using the Python 3 standard library. The script checks every chip state through the first repeat, all actual maximal firing sets, complete direct and nerve face enumerations, and deterministic elementary free-face pairs. For the exceptional shift it checks all eleven explicitly listed edge–triangle pairs, the complementary seven-edge spanning tree, an acyclic directed Hasse diagram, and the three critical cells. Independently it computes all reduced mod-two Betti numbers from direct simplicial boundary matrices **and** from the nerve; these agree for all four shifts. Its terminal marker is `PENTAGON_ROSE_PHASE_CLASSIFICATION_PASS`. The mod-two computations corroborate the constructive integral homotopy proof; they are not substituted for it.

## Relationship to prior work
Riihimäki and Smith introduce avalanche complexes and parallel firing and prove the symmetric two-cycle wedge statement (Proposition 3.25) in *Avalanche homology of digraphs via sandpile dynamics*, arXiv:2606.26786, first posted June 25, 2026. They explicitly identify asymmetric initial configurations on wedges of cycles as a source of potentially different homotopy types. Their symmetric proposition accounts for \(h=0\), not the three asymmetric classes here. Related published-finding corpus records classify near-critical directed roses, directed cycles, or *disjoint* synchronized cycles; their stated results do not imply the six-grain asynchronous two-pentagon phase diagram. The proof here fixes one minimal natural rose and enumerates the complete shift orbit, rather than extrapolating to arbitrarily long roses.

## Limitations
The result concerns exactly two directed pentagons sharing one vertex and precisely six grains in the specified positions. It makes no claim about unequal petal lengths, other chip totals, arbitrary initial vectors, persistent barcodes, or a uniform infinite-family formula. External results indexed only by short summaries may conceal an equivalent formulation; no independent audit has been carried out.
