# Three independent two-torsion classes in the full octahedral forest quotient

## Finding
Let \(G=K_{2,2,2}\), with vertices \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\) in three distinguished antipodal pairs \(\{0,1\}\), \(\{2,3\}\), and \(\{4,5\}\). Let \(\mathcal F(G)\) be the simplicial complex whose vertices are the 12 graph edges and whose faces are acyclic edge sets, and let \(W=C_2\wr S_3\) act through all 48 automorphisms of \(G\). For the **ordinary geometric quotient** \(Y=|\mathcal F(G)|/W\), integral homology is

\[
H_i(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3,&i=3,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&i=4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Moreover \(Y\) is 2-connected. The original labeled complex is a wedge of 133 four-spheres; the three order-two classes in the quotient are therefore a new integral effect of identifying the full graph symmetry.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has all edges between distinct antipodal pairs and none inside a pair. Forests include every acyclic subset of these edges, including the empty face, and the displayed realization is the ordinary, **not homotopy**, orbit space. Each group element permutes pairs and may independently interchange the two vertices of each pair. All statements are for this particular 6-vertex graph and the entire 48-element group, not a family or a subgroup. The simplicial complex has top dimension four.

## Proof
The graphic-matroid independence complex is pure and shellable by the matroid independence-complex theorem. Exhaustive enumeration gives its nonempty face counts \((12,66,212,408,384)\) in dimensions zero through four; hence its Euler characteristic is \(12-66+212-408+384=134\). Shellability yields \(\mathcal F(G)\simeq\bigvee^{133}S^4\), in particular simple connectivity.

Take the barycentric subdivision of \(\mathcal F(G)\). Its simplices correspond to strict chains of nonempty forest faces. Because graph automorphisms preserve the rank (cardinality) of every forest face, an automorphism stabilizing a flag cannot invert the flag, so the flag-orbit incidence complex computes the ordinary quotient's integral cellular chain complex. Enumeration of all forest faces, flags and graph automorphisms gives 48 group elements and quotient-cell counts \((38,441,1594,2148,960)\). In each dimension, delete one member of an orbit-representative flag with the usual alternating incidence sign and look up the image face orbit. The resulting differentials satisfy \(\partial^2=0\) exactly over the integers.

Next perform 2,586 elementary integral cancellations, each about a coefficient \(+1\) or \(-1\), updating all incident differential entries via the integer Schur-complement rule. These are chain-homotopy equivalences over \(\mathbb Z\) and preserve integral homology. The residual cellular chain ranks in dimensions zero through four are \((1,0,0,3,5)\). Its sole nonzero differential is \(d_4:\mathbb Z^5\to\mathbb Z^3\), represented in the recorded residual bases by

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
4&-4&0&2&0\\
0&0&2&-2&0\\
2&-2&0&0&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The greatest common divisor of its nonzero entries is 2; the gcd of its two-by-two minors is 4, and the gcd of its three-by-three minors is 8. Thus its Smith invariant factors are exactly \(2,2,2\), yielding \(\ker(d_4)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and \(\operatorname{coker}(d_4)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^3\). The stated homology follows.

For simple connectivity of the quotient, use Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem: when a finite group acts on a simply connected finite polyhedron, its quotient's fundamental group is the group modulo the normal subgroup generated by elements fixing points. The within-pair transpositions and the adjacent pair swaps generate \(W\). Each fixes at least one edge of \(G\) as an unordered pair (an edge between two other pairs for a within-pair flip, and an edge joining the swapped pairs for a pair swap), and therefore fixes a vertex of the forest complex. It follows that \(\pi_1(Y)=1\). Since \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\), the degree-two Hurewicz theorem yields \(\pi_2(Y)=0\).

## Relationship to prior work
Jonsson's *Forests and Matroids* explains standard matroid-complex shellability and the labeled-complex topology; these facts alone do not determine the integral homology of a quotient by a nonfree automorphism action. Moci and Pezzoli's *Representations of automorphism groups on the homology of matroids* develops equivariant homology representations; such rational or representation-theoretic information does not supply the three integral quotient torsion classes here. Other known octahedral constructions involving **directed-linear-forest**, **discrete Morse**, or **matching** complexes use different simplices and have no direct implication for this undirected graphic-matroid independence quotient.

## Limitations
This gives integral homology and 2-connectivity, **not** a complete homotopy decomposition or higher homotopy groups. It neither asserts a formula for arbitrary complete multipartite graphs nor excludes unindexed equivalent literature. The 2007 chapter's publicly accessible publisher introduction was inspected, not its inaccessible full subscriber-only text. All finite enumerations are exact and exhaustive for this specified graph, and the homological theorem uses these finite calculations in addition to standard topological results.
