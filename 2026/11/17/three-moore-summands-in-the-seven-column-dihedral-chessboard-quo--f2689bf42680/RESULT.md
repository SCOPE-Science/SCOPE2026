# Three Moore summands in the seven-column dihedral chessboard quotient

## Finding

Let \(\Delta_{4,7}\) be the simplicial complex of nonattacking rook placements on four labeled rows and seven cyclically labeled columns. The dihedral group \(D_7\), of order fourteen, acts on column labels by \(j\mapsto j+t\) and \(j\mapsto -j+t\) modulo seven, fixing every row. Its **ordinary geometric orbit space** is

\[
Q=|\Delta_{4,7}|/D_7\simeq
\bigvee^{3}M(\mathbb Z/2,2)\ \vee\ \bigvee^{15}S^3.
\]

Here \(M(\mathbb Z/2,2)=S^2\cup_{2}e^3\), with the three Moore summands attached at a common basepoint. Therefore \(\pi_1(Q)=0\), \(\pi_2(Q)\cong H_2(Q;\mathbb Z)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^3\), \(H_3(Q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{15}\), and all other positive-degree reduced integral homology groups are zero. In contrast, \(|\Delta_{4,7}|\simeq\bigvee^{225}S^3\), so its homology is torsion-free.

## Assumptions and scope

Each vertex of \(\Delta_{4,7}\) is a pair \((i,j)\), with \(0\le i\le3\) and \(j\in\mathbb Z/7\). A face is a set of such pairs with no repeated first or second coordinate. The action is *only* on columns; no row permutation is included. The quotient is of the **geometric realization**, not an incorrectly identified simplicial quotient with vertex-orbit identification. Barycentric subdivision gives an equivariant triangulation without simplex inversions, yielding a regular orbit-of-flags cellular chain complex.

This result concerns this particular first fully shellable width, seven columns. It does not claim a classification for other widths or for subgroups other than the full column-dihedral group.

## Proof

**1. Connectivity and orbit fundamental group.** The standard chessboard connectivity bound of Björner–Lovász–Vrećica–Živaljević gives \(\Delta_{m,n}\) connectivity at least \(\min\{m,n,\lfloor(m+n+1)/3\rfloor\}-2\). For \((m,n)=(4,7)\) this is two, so the original complex is simply connected (indeed two-connected). Each reflection of the seven column labels has a unique fixed column, and hence fixes the four vertices in that column. All reflections together generate \(D_7\). Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem, applicable to a finite simplicial group action, gives \(\pi_1(Q)\cong D_7/N\), where \(N\) is the normal subgroup generated by elements having fixed points. Consequently \(\pi_1(Q)=0\).

**2. Exhaustive, correctly normalized quotient chains.** List the nonempty faces by their rook placements. The numbers in face dimensions zero through three are \((28,252,840,840)\). Every barycentric simplex is an increasing chain of nonempty faces. Order its vertices by strict face cardinality. Under the fourteen column symmetries, this order is preserved. Taking one representative for each orbit of chains produces exactly

\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(142,1236,2520,1440)
\]

cells. Boundary signs are the alternating face-deletion signs; a deleted chain is mapped to its canonical orbit representative. Since chain vertices have distinct cardinalities, no stabilizer reverses their order. The resulting differentials have integer entries, and the explicit verifier checks both compositions \(\partial_1\partial_2\) and \(\partial_2\partial_3\) are zero.

**3. Exact integral reduction.** Repeatedly cancel a unit entry \(\pm1\) in a boundary matrix by integral row/column operations, removing the resulting contractible adjacent-generator pair. A pivot \(u=\pm1\) with column \(b\) and row \(a\) replaces every remaining coefficient \(d_{h\ell}\) with \(d_{h\ell}-d_{hj}d_{i\ell}/u\); the neighboring differentials restrict under the induced basis changes because \(\partial^2=0\). These are exact chain homotopy equivalences over \(\mathbb Z\), not modular rank approximations.

The deterministic reduction makes \(141,1095,1422\) cancellations in degrees one, two and three, respectively, leaving chain-group ranks \((1,0,3,18)\). The only nonzero remaining boundary is a \(3\)-by-\(18\) integer matrix \(\partial_3\). The greatest common divisors of its nonzero minors of orders one, two, and three are exactly \(2,4,8\). Its three nonzero Smith invariant factors are therefore precisely \(2,2,2\); its kernel has rank fifteen. Thus

\[
\widetilde H_2(Q;\mathbb Z)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^3,
\qquad
\widetilde H_3(Q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{15},
\]

with no other reduced homology. These integer determinantal-divisor checks exclude all higher exponents and all odd-prime torsion.

**4. Upgrade from homology to homotopy.** The quotient is a simply connected finite CW complex of dimension three. Its two-skeleton is simply connected, hence is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres: by Hurewicz, a basis of its free \(H_2\) is represented by maps of spheres, and Whitehead's theorem gives the equivalence. The attaching maps of the three-cells are elements of \(\pi_2\) of that wedge, classified by an integer matrix. Integral changes of bases of these two- and three-cell groups induce homotopy equivalences and put the attaching matrix in Smith normal form. The three degree-two attachments give three \(M(\mathbb Z/2,2)\) summands; the fifteen zero attachments give fifteen \(S^3\) summands. This proves the displayed homotopy equivalence.

**5. Independent Euler check.** A nonidentity rotation cannot stabilize a face, since every orbit of columns has seven members but each face has at most four rooks. A reflection has precisely four fixed vertices (one per row in its unique fixed column) and no invariant higher-dimensional faces. Hence \(\chi(\Delta_{4,7})=28-252+840-840=-224\) and the finite-action Euler formula gives \(\chi(Q)=(-224+7\cdot4)/14=-14\). The computed homology likewise gives \(1-15=-14\). Since \(\Delta_{4,7}\) is two-connected and three-dimensional, it is itself a wedge of \(225\) three-spheres by the same cell-dimensional argument.

## Relationship to prior work

The definition, connectivity and original homology theory of chessboard complexes are classical. The width-seven two-connectivity follows from the published Björner–Lovász–Vrećica–Živaljević theorem, and shellability in this range follows from Ziegler. The full 2004/2007 Shareshian–Wachs treatment studies torsion and representations in the unquotiented complexes. More recent work on generalized and multiple chessboard complexes likewise addresses their intrinsic homotopy and homology, rather than the geometric quotient under reflections of seven columns. The present result identifies new **quotient-created two-primary torsion** and the full corresponding three-dimensional homotopy type. Standard fixed-point fundamental-group and Smith-normal-form methods are cited as tools, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The explicit Smith computation is for one natural pair of parameters only. The proposed novelty is relative to the specifically compared available sources, not a proof that no equivalent unindexed result exists. Independent mathematical review has not been performed. No statement concerning the topological quotient for all board widths, or an equivariant homotopy decomposition before quotient, is asserted.
