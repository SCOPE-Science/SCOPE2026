# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The stated group action is the order-fourteen dihedral action on seven columns, not a permutation of rows. The classical connectivity theorem gives two-connectivity of the three-dimensional covering complex. Every reflection fixes four vertices, and reflections generate the group, so Armstrong's fixed-element theorem makes the geometric quotient simply connected. The exhaustive flag-orbit enumeration has cell counts \((142,1236,2520,1440)\) and exact chain cancellations leave \((1,0,3,18)\); integer determinantal divisors \((2,4,8)\) give Smith factors \((2,2,2)\). The theorem on simply connected three-dimensional CW complexes then upgrades these data to the asserted Moore-space-and-sphere wedge. These checks use the supplied executable, not an external unverified numerical table. Independent Euler characteristic agrees at \(-14\).

The 2026 generalized-chessboard classification studies changes in capacities, not orbit spaces of the ordinary \(4\)-by-\(7\) chessboard. Closest publicly indexed findings on cyclic chessboard quotients use a different board or group action. Prior calculations for the alternating row quotient of the same board and the dihedral column quotient of three-row boards do not imply this result: they concern distinct, incomparable actions or different covering complexes. Search nonappearance alone does not establish originality; non-indexed equivalent results remain the main residual risk.

Seven columns is the first width at which the standard four-row chessboard connectivity bound reaches two-connectivity and shellability holds. The result gives the full integral three-dimensional homotopy classification of its full column-reflection quotient, including three previously unaccounted order-two Moore summands, rather than a routine rational-rank calculation. The contrast between a wedge of three-spheres upstairs and nonzero degree-two torsion downstairs motivates the exact invariant and the choice of group.
