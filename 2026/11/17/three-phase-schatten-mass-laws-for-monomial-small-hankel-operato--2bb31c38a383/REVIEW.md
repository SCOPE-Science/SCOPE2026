# Scientific review of the monomial small-Hankel spectral law

## Correctness — PASS

The one-variable weighted Besov normalization is copied literally from Li and Wang's exponent-two definition. Its monomial squared norms are exact beta integrals. Expanding the weighted Bergman reproducing kernel and using the angular-selection rule gives one anti-diagonal entry for each input degree up to the symbol degree, with the output-to-input monomial norm ratio included. These entries give the entire nonzero singular multiset, with multiplicity and rank. Uniform two-sided gamma-ratio bounds justify the bulk, critical logarithmic, and dominated-edge limits for every fixed positive Schatten exponent. The operator norm follows by maximizing the bulk beta profile or the boundary sequence. The kernel-admissibility condition from the general-symbol theorem is explicitly **not** assumed in the finite-rank monomial statement. The supplementary numeric checker was run successfully; no finite check is treated as proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Li and Wang's full-text theorem and final arguments establish boundedness tests rather than an exact sequence of singular values or growing-degree Schatten asymptotics. The classical weighted Bergman small-Hankel literature treats Schatten membership, but the accessible Janson bibliographic material alone cannot decide whether an older specialized monomial singular-value theorem subsumes these precise leading constants. The closest located degree-asymptotic results concern **big** Bergman Hankel operators or harmonic Bergman functional-calculus defects, neither of which has this anti-diagonal small-Hankel matrix or the separate Besov/kernel indices. Their statements do not imply the three branches by direct specialization. An alternative older matrix formulation remains a real priority risk. No claim of independently certified novelty is made.

The primary MSC classification 47B35 is assigned to the new theorem's small-Hankel operator content; Li and Wang's arXiv version does not itself print an MSC assignment. A related published small-Hankel paper by Sehba (2008) explicitly lists 47B35 as its primary classification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural question left quantitatively open by the Hilbert specialization of the recent boundedness theory is how large the full singular spectrum is as a polynomial symbol's degree increases. The answer is a genuine bulk-to-edge transition, with an exact logarithmic boundary and a sharp coefficient equal to one, **including** the kernel-parameter range excluded from the general-symbol boundedness hypothesis. The discrete limiting constant on the edge cannot be read off from a beta integral. This is a finite-rank family at each degree but a nontrivial, uniform-in-degree spectral asymptotic and not a statement about general symbols.

Janson (1988), Arazy--Fisher--Peetre (1988), and later small-Hankel studies provide substantial trace-ideal context; Janson's full text was inaccessible, so a publication-priority comparison remains partially unverified.
