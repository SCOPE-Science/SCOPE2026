# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
For \(\kappa>0\) and spatial dimension \(d\ge2\), Euclidean projection is explicitly obtained from a meridian-plane constrained minimization. Its middle-region Jacobian interpolates affinely between two endpoint matrices; convexity of the induced coordinate maximum norm reduces the exact global bound to their row sums. Cauchy--Schwarz sharply optimizes each row for fixed distinguished-coordinate magnitude, and local directional perturbations give the required two-point lower bounds. The maximum of \(a\sqrt{1-a^2}\) and uniform bounded remainders prove the three aperture regimes. The standalone checker was executed successfully on deterministic sample cases, but is not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest exact prior all-dimensional coordinatewise result, *Sharp coordinatewise Lipschitz transition for Euclidean Lorentz-cone projection*, treats the **standard aperture** \(\kappa=1\); its full research proof and limitations were compared. *Exact planar stability and nonexpansivity rigidity for \(\ell_p\) projections onto scaled Lorentz cones* measures nearest-point projection in the **ambient \(\ell_p\) metric**, not the present Euclidean projector in the coordinate maximum metric. The primary full texts of Németh (2012), Németh--Zhang (2014) and Bello Cruz et al. (2016) were also inspected for dominating results and equivalent formulas. None of their inspected assertions supplies the variable-aperture exact modulus or the \(\kappa=1/2\) and \(\kappa=2\) large-dimensional transitions. Historical priority remains provisional, including one related earlier paper accessible only by abstract.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Euclidean conic projection is nonexpansive in Euclidean norm but its sharp coordinate-error amplification depends strongly on cone aperture. The new finite one-variable formula quantifies that dependence for every spatial dimension; the asymptotic classification reveals three qualitatively different worst-coordinate mechanisms, with exact structural transition apertures. This is directly relevant when conic feasibility is measured in entrywise error budgets rather than Euclidean distance.

## Limitations and references
The scalar maxima are implicit, and the equality-aperture finite-dimensional phase competition is not resolved. The theorem excludes spatial dimension one, arbitrary ellipsoidal cones, weighted error norms and non-Euclidean projection maps. Source coverage may be incomplete; independent specialist verification is needed for firm priority.
