# Translation quotients of affine-plane matroid complexes: a characteristic-two homotopy transition

## Finding

For every prime power \(q=p^e\ge 2\), let \(V=\mathbb F_q^2\) and let \(K_q\) be the rank-three affine-geometry matroid independence complex on the \(q^2\) points of \(V\): its nonempty faces are single points, pairs of distinct points, and noncollinear triples. Let the additive group \(T=(V,+)\) translate the vertices, and let \(Q_q=|K_q|/T\) denote the **ordinary geometric orbit space**, not a homotopy quotient.

Set
\[
 b_q=\begin{cases}
 \dfrac{q(q-1)^2(q+1)}6,&p=2,\\[3pt]
 \dfrac{(q-1)(q+1)(q^2-q-3)}6,&p\ne2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then, for every prime power \(q\ge2\), the complete integral homology is
\[
 H_i(Q_q;\mathbb Z)\cong\begin{cases}
 \mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
 0,&i=1,\ p=2,\\
 (\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{2e},&i=1,\ p\ne2,\\
 \mathbb Z^{b_q},&i=2,\\
 0,&i\ge3.
 \end{cases}
\]
The fundamental group has a sharp characteristic dichotomy:
\[
 \pi_1(Q_q)\cong\begin{cases}
 1,&p=2,\\
 (\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{2e},&p\ne2.
 \end{cases}
\]
In characteristic two the stronger complete homotopy classification holds:
\[
 Q_q\simeq\bigvee^{b_q}S^2.
\]
For odd characteristic, the translation action is free on the whole geometric complex; in characteristic two, every nonidentity translation fixes exactly \(q^2/2\) isolated midpoints of edges. In particular the odd-characteristic quotients have finite first-homology torsion of arbitrarily large prime exponent, while every even-characteristic quotient is a wedge of two-spheres.

## Assumptions and scope

The finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) is arbitrary, including nonprime orders \(q=p^e\); the translation group is **additive** and should not be confused with a cyclic group of order \(q^2\). The action is the diagonal translation \((x,y)\mapsto(x+a,y+b)\). An independent triple is exactly a triple having nonzero affine determinant. The quotient is formed after geometric realization, equivalently from the barycentric subdivision and its orbit cells. All homology groups are integral. The empty face is not regarded as a geometric point. No statement about non-Desarguesian translation planes or the complete odd-characteristic homotopy type is made.

## Proof

**1. Simple connectivity of the covering complex.** The complex \(K_q\) is the independence complex of the rank-three affine-geometry matroid, with all maximal independent sets of size three. It has no coloop: given any point, there exist three noncollinear points avoiding it (also for \(q=2\)). The matroid-shellability theorem yields that \(|K_q|\) is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres, and therefore is connected and simply connected. The argument uses the usual rank-three independence complex, not the larger cap complex of all no-three-collinear point sets.

**2. Stabilizer classification.** Every nonzero \(t\in T\) has prime order \(p\) and no fixed vertex. If a point of \(|K_q|\) is fixed by \(t\), then \(t\) preserves its smallest carrier face (the unique simplex containing that point in its relative interior). That face has at most three vertices, so a free action of the cyclic group of order \(p\) on that finite face forces \(p\le3\). For \(p=3\), a three-point orbit is \(\{v,v+t,v+2t\}\), which lies on the affine line \(v+\mathbb F_q t\) and is not an allowed two-simplex. Hence odd-characteristic translations fix **no** geometric point. For \(p=2\), the only nontrivial invariant faces are pairs \(\{v,v+t\}\). The geometric fixed points are exactly their midpoints, and there are \(q^2/2\) of them. No nonzero translation stabilizes an allowed triangle in characteristic two. Thus the normal subgroup of \(T\) generated by elements with geometric fixed points is trivial for odd \(p\), and all of \(T\) for \(p=2\).

**3. Fundamental group.** Apply the orbit-space fundamental-group theorem for a finite simplicial group acting on a simply connected complex (after barycentric subdivision to eliminate inversions). It identifies \(\pi_1(|K_q|/T)\) with \(T/N\), where \(N\) is the normal subgroup generated by the point stabilizers. Step 2 gives \(N=1\) for odd \(p\) and \(N=T\) for \(p=2\). Since \(T\) is the \(2e\)-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_p\), this proves the displayed \(\pi_1\) formula. Its abelianization gives the integral first homology.

**4. Euler characteristic.** Put \(n=q^2\). There are \(n\) point vertices, \(\binom n2\) edges, and
\[
 f_2=\binom n3-q(q+1)\binom q3
\]
triangles: the affine plane has \(q(q+1)\) lines, each containing \(q\) points, and each collinear triple lies on exactly one line. Therefore
\[
 \chi(|K_q|)=n-\binom n2+\binom n3-q(q+1)\binom q3
 =\frac{q^2(q^4-q^3-4q^2+q+9)}6.
\]
For odd characteristic the action is free, so \(\chi(Q_q)=\chi(|K_q|)/q^2\). For characteristic two use Burnside's Euler-characteristic formula for the barycentric subdivision. Every nonidentity element fixes \(q^2/2\) isolated barycenters, whence
\[
 \chi(Q_q)=\frac{\chi(|K_q|)+(q^2-1)q^2/2}{q^2}.
\]
Subtraction of one yields exactly the two stated expressions for \(b_q\).

**5. Integral homology and even-characteristic homotopy type.** The quotient has a finite two-dimensional CW structure. Its first homology is finite or zero by step 3, and \(H_i(Q_q;\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(i\ge3\). Moreover \(H_2\) is a subgroup of the free abelian cellular two-chain group, so it is torsion-free. Since the rational first Betti number vanishes, \(\operatorname{rank}H_2=\chi(Q_q)-1=b_q\). This proves the complete integral homology, with no hidden extension problem. If \(p=2\), the quotient is simply connected. The Hurewicz theorem realizes a basis of \(H_2\) by maps of two-spheres, producing a homology equivalence \(\bigvee^{b_q}S^2\to Q_q\); the homology version of Whitehead's theorem for simply connected CW complexes upgrades this to a homotopy equivalence.

## Verification

## Relationship to prior work

The matroid shellability and sphere-wedge premise is classical, with a full account and further shelling refinements in Ardila–Castillo–Samper (2014/2015). Armstrong's orbit-space theorem provides the fundamental-group mechanism. Moci–Pezzoli (2020/2021) develops automorphism representations on matroid homology, but determines representations of the covering complex rather than the ordinary orbit space's integral homology, and does not provide this affine translation parity classification. Literature on affine-plane incidence-graph Rips complexes and on cap complexes concerns different face systems; neither gives the displayed fundamental group or integral orbit-space classification. The result here combines stabilizer geometry and nonfree quotient topology rather than deducing quotient homology from rational invariants.

## Limitations

The uniform argument determines integral homology, fundamental group, and the full homotopy type in characteristic two. It does not identify the complete odd-characteristic two-type, Postnikov data, or homotopy equivalence class, nor a general cohomology ring. The finite verifier supports only \(q=2,3,4\); the infinite statement rests on the theoretical proof. No claim of exhaustive coverage of unindexed mathematical literature is made.
