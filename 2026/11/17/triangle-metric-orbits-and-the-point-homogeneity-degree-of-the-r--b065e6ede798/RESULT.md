# Triangle metric orbits and the point-homogeneity degree of the regular 600-cell

## Finding
Let \(V\subset\mathbb R^4\) be the \(120\)-vertex set of a regular 600-cell, scaled to circumradius \(1\), and let \(G\) be its full Euclidean symmetry group. Among the \(\binom{120}{3}=280840\) three-element subsets of \(V\), there are exactly **56 Euclidean congruence classes** and exactly **56 orbits of \(G\)**. Thus any congruence between unordered vertex triangles can be realized by a symmetry at the level of the underlying sets.

The stronger statement for a *specified bijection* fails in exactly one congruence class. For every triple \(T\subset V\) other than a mutually orthogonal triple, every intrinsic distance-preserving permutation of \(T\) is induced by a symmetry stabilizing \(T\). If the three vertex vectors of \(T\) are pairwise orthogonal, its intrinsic isometry group is \(S_3\), whereas the permutations induced by the setwise stabilizer in \(G\) form exactly \(A_3\cong C_3\). The orthogonal class consists of exactly **2400 unordered triangles** and one \(G\)-orbit.

Consequently the regular 600-cell is **2-point homogeneous but not 3-point homogeneous**, so its point-homogeneity degree in the sense of Berestovskii–Nikonorov is exactly \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
A congruence or isometry of a three-element subset preserves all three Euclidean distances; its extension means a single symmetry of the *whole vertex configuration* whose restriction equals the specified bijection. These statements include all triangles, not only two-dimensional faces of the 600-cell, and do not identify a setwise congruence with an arbitrary matching of its labeled vertices. All vertices are distinct and lie on the unit sphere in \(\mathbb R^4\).

Write \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). The explicit normalized vertex model is obtained by dividing the following \(120\) four-tuples by \(2\): the eight signed coordinate vectors with one coordinate \(\pm2\), the sixteen sign vectors with all coordinates \(\pm1\), and all 96 independently signed *even coordinate permutations* of \((0,1,\varphi,\varphi-1)\). Their exact coordinates lie in \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]/2\). In the checker, the doubled coordinate vectors are used throughout, with multiplication performed by the identity \(\varphi^2=\varphi+1\).

## Proof
**1. The complete symmetry group.** All doubled vertex vectors \(a\) have exact squared norm \(4\). Reflection in the hyperplane perpendicular to such a vector acts on doubled coordinates by
\[
  s_a(x)=x-\frac{\langle x,a\rangle}{2}a.
\]
Select four of these roots whose consecutive exact inner products are \(-2\varphi,-2,-2\), and whose nonconsecutive inner products vanish. Direct substitution into the explicit coordinates verifies that their four reflections permute the \(120\) vertices. Closing their induced permutations under composition produces \(14400\) distinct permutations; the attached checker performs this construction over the ring \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\), without numerical tolerances.

To see that no symmetry is missing, any symmetry sends a vertex to one of \(120\) vertices. The \(12\) nearest neighbors of a fixed vertex form a regular icosahedron in the perpendicular affine hyperplane; their three-dimensional symmetry group has order \(120\). A symmetry that fixes that vertex and fixes all twelve neighbors is the identity on the spanning four-dimensional vertex space. Hence the full group has order at most \(120\cdot120=14400\), attained by the displayed reflections.

**2. Two-point homogeneity.** For a fixed vertex \(u\), the stabilizer has order \(120\). Every orbit of this stabilizer is contained in a level set of \(\langle u,v\rangle\). Exact orbit enumeration finds precisely eight nontrivial stabilizer orbits, and exact inner-product evaluation finds precisely eight distinct values for \(v\ne u\). Thus the stabilizer is transitive on each distance sphere about \(u\); transitivity of the whole group on the vertices follows from the group order and stabilizer order. Every distance-preserving ordered pair therefore extends to a global symmetry.

**3. All unordered triangle orbits.** Form all \(\binom{120}{3}=280840\) unordered triples. Apply the four reflection generators to each sorted triple and take the orbit closure by breadth-first traversal. The orbit sizes are \(400\) (one orbit), \(720\) (four), \(1200\) (three), \(1440\) (four), \(1800\) (one), \(2400\) (three), \(3600\) (ten), \(7200\) (twenty-nine), and \(14400\) (one). Their sizes sum to \(280840\), and there are exactly \(56\) orbits. For each orbit representative, compute the multiset of its three pairwise inner products as three exact pairs of integers \((a,b)\), representing \(a+b\varphi\) for doubled coordinates. The \(56\) resulting signatures are **all distinct**. Since every vertex has equal norm, two unordered triples are Euclidean congruent exactly when these three inner products match as a multiset. This proves the equality of intrinsic congruence classes and global setwise symmetry orbits. The attached CSV lists all \(56\) representatives, signatures, orbit sizes, and stabilizer data.

**4. Every allowed labeling, with one exception.** The intrinsic isometries of a triple are the permutations of its three labels preserving all pairwise inner products. For each of the \(56\) representatives, compute the image in \(S_3\) of the complete setwise stabilizer within the \(14400\)-element group. For \(55\) representatives, its image equals the full intrinsic isometry group. The sole exception is the class with all three pairwise inner products zero: the intrinsic group has order \(6\), but the image of the stabilizer has order \(3\), namely the cyclic subgroup of three-cycles. Its orbit has size \(2400\), and its pointwise stabilizer has order \(2\). These computations use only integer-pair arithmetic and finite permutation equality, and the complete checker and orbit catalogue are attached.

There is also a direct geometric obstruction. The three unit coordinate vectors \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) form an orthogonal vertex triangle. Any orthogonal transformation swapping \(e_1,e_2\) while fixing \(e_3\) must be the corresponding odd coordinate permutation on their span, with either sign on \(e_4\). Both possibilities carry the 96-vector even-permutation subset of the vertex model into the disjoint odd-permutation subset, so neither is a symmetry of the 600-cell. Conversely a three-cycle of the first three coordinates is an even permutation and is a symmetry. Thus exactly the cyclic, not the transposition, label symmetries are realized for this class.

Finally, any congruence from \(T\) to \(T'\) is the composition of one setwise global symmetry (Step 3) with an intrinsic isometry of \(T'\). Step 4 consequently gives the complete extension classification. Step 2 proves degree at least \(2\), and the orthogonal transposition counterexample proves degree strictly less than \(3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Berestovskii and Nikonorov, *On \(m\)-point homogeneous polytopes in Euclidean spaces* (first public arXiv version June 27, 2022), introduce the point-homogeneity degree and explicitly state in Section 5 that they do not know that degree for the 600-cell. Their results on other polytopes do not settle this case. The exact degree \(2\), the \(56\)-class triangle classification, and the uniqueness of its orthogonal labeling obstruction are the present conclusions.

Waegell and Aravind, *Critical noncolorings of the 600-cell proving the Bell–Kochen–Specker theorem* (2009), already establish the 75 orthogonal four-ray bases, the \(14400\)-element symmetry group, and its **even-permutation restriction on orthogonal bases**. These facts are credited here: in particular the parity phenomenon behind the nonextendible orthogonal swap follows from their symmetry description. That prior work does not give the complete 56-class unlabeled triangle census or the exact point-homogeneity degree. Berestovskii and Nikonorov's 2023 work on perfect homogeneous polytopes describes the 600-cell as the binary icosahedral vertex configuration and proves a different property, perfectness with respect to quadratic hypersurfaces, which does not imply the present triangle-extension classification.

## Limitations
The result concerns Euclidean distances among triples of vertices of the **regular** 600-cell, not arbitrary points of the solid, perturbed embeddings, the dual 120-cell, or higher-cardinality configurations. The 56-class enumeration is computer-assisted but exact and reproducible with a standalone finite verifier. The existence of a short conceptual nonenumerative proof of the full orbit count is not established. Historical priority is provisional: an equivalent triangle-orbit enumeration could occur in older Coxeter or association-scheme literature that was not fully located. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
