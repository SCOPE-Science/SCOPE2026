# Twenty-four three-spheres in the Morse-matching complex of a three-triangle disk

## Finding

Let \(D\) be the pure two-dimensional simplicial complex on vertices \(0,1,2,3\) with maximal faces \(012\), \(013\), and \(023\). Thus \(D=0*\partial\Delta^2\), a cone on the boundary of a triangle, and also the tetrahedral boundary after deleting its facet \(123\) while retaining all proper faces. Let \(\mathfrak M(D)\) denote the **ordinary** complex of discrete Morse matchings, not the strong or pure version: its vertices are Hasse cover incidences between nonempty faces of \(D\), and a set of incidences is a simplex exactly when no base face occurs twice and reversing the chosen incidences in the downward-directed Hasse diagram produces no directed cycle.

**Theorem.** The complete integral homotopy type is
\[
\boxed{\ |\mathfrak M(D)|\simeq\bigvee^{24}S^3.\ }
\]
The same holds for a tetrahedral boundary with any one triangular facet removed, by symmetry. In particular, \(\widetilde H_3(\mathfrak M(D);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{24}\), all other reduced integral homology vanishes, and \(\pi_1(\mathfrak M(D))=0\).

## Assumptions and scope

The vertices of \(D\) are four distinct labeled points. The complex includes all nonempty subfaces of its three facets and the empty face; its ordinary Morse complex uses only **nonempty** Hasse incidences. The empty gradient matching is an empty simplex, not a cone vertex. The theorem concerns the full Morse-matching complex, rather than the subcomplex generated by maximum matchings or the different strong-Morse construction. No asymptotic or arbitrary-cone statement is asserted.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive base and gradient-field census.** The nonempty faces of \(D\) consist of four vertices, six edges, and three triangles. They have exactly 21 codimension-one Hasse incidences. Index the 13 base faces by increasing cardinality and lexicographic order, and then enumerate the 21 Hasse pairs \((i,j)\) by increasing lower-face index and increasing upper-face index. A subset \(W\) of these pairs determines a gradient field precisely when (i) its pairs have disjoint endpoints in the base face poset and (ii) the directed graph obtained by reversing those pairs in the downward Hasse graph is acyclic. The complete recursion in `verify.py` tests both conditions. It produces the full face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(21,162,560,846,468,64),
\]
so there are 2,121 nonempty simplices of \(\mathfrak M(D)\). Its reduced Euler characteristic is \(-24\), consistent with but insufficient to establish the theorem.

**2. An explicit perfect discrete Morse matching on the resulting complex.** The file `certificate.json` lists 1,048 ordered pairs of nonempty simplices of \(\mathfrak M(D)\) and 25 critical simplices. Every simplex is encoded by an integer bitmask on the ordered 21 Hasse incidences. Each listed pair \((a,b)\) satisfies \(a\subset b\) with exactly one additional vertex, and no simplex occurs in more than one pair or also appears as critical. The complete face-poset cover graph of \(\mathfrak M(D)\) is built independently by `verify.py`; its unmatched arcs are oriented from higher-dimensional to lower-dimensional simplices, and exactly the 1,048 matched incidences are reversed. The resulting directed graph is verified acyclic by complete topological sorting; **all 2,121 vertices of this directed graph are visited**. This is a finite, exhaustively checkable certificate of a Forman acyclic matching, not a heuristic assertion based on a successful optimizer.

**3. Determination of the homotopy type.** The certificate partitions all nonempty simplices into exactly 1,048 disjoint matched pairs and 25 unmatched simplices. The unmatched simplices consist of exactly one vertex and 24 three-dimensional simplices, with no unmatched cells of any other dimension. The discrete Morse theorem therefore supplies a CW complex with one zero-cell, 24 three-cells, and no cells of dimensions one, two, four, or five. Every three-cell attaches to the unique zero-cell along the constant map, since there is no positive-dimensional lower skeleton. Hence this CW complex is the wedge of 24 copies of \(S^3\), proving the theorem **integrally**, without inferring homotopy from field-valued Betti numbers.

## Verification

Place `verify.py` and `certificate.json` in the same directory and run `python verify.py certificate.json` with Python 3. The verifier uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs the base simplices and all 21 Hasse incidences; exhaustively enumerates every Morse-matching simplex using a directed-acyclicity test; compares the face vector; validates every listed matching pair and critical simplex; checks that all 2,121 nonempty simplices occur exactly once; and independently tests acyclicity of the **entire reversed Hasse graph of the Morse complex**, rather than trusting the original construction order. It separately calculates all reduced mod-two simplicial Betti ranks from the full incidence matrices, obtaining only \(\beta_3=24\). See `verification_output.txt` for the exact output of the executable replay. The integral homotopy conclusion is provided by the acyclic matching itself, not by the auxiliary mod-two calculation.

## Relationship to prior work

Chari and Joswig introduced the ordinary complex of Morse matchings and determined properties for one-dimensional graph inputs and small simplices. Scoville calculated the full homotopy types for the tetrahedral boundary and the tetrahedron, including
\[
\mathfrak M(\partial\Delta^3)\simeq\bigvee^{21}S^3\vee\bigvee^{24}S^4,
\]
and established that a base inclusion induces a null-homotopic inclusion of Morse complexes for any simplicial cone. Neither result determines \(\mathfrak M(0*\partial\Delta^2)\): the general cone theorem controls an **inclusion map**, not the cone's Morse-complex homotopy type. The present 24-sphere calculation therefore quantifies the sharp effect of removing a single triangular facet from the tetrahedral boundary. Knudson and Owens-White study a different, strong Morse-matching construction and do not imply the ordinary result here.

## Limitations

This is an exact finite classification for the unique isomorphism type of a tetrahedral boundary missing one triangular facet, and not a theorem for all triangulated disks or all cones over cycles. The matching certificate proves nonequivariant integral homotopy only; it does not determine the full permutation action on the 24-dimensional homology lattice, nor the map from this complex into the tetrahedral-boundary Morse complex. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
