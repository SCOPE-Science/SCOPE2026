# Two- and seven-primary torsion in the full dihedral quotient of the seven-cycle cyclohedron

## Finding

Let \(C_7\) be the simple cycle whose vertices are \(0,1,\ldots,6\), regarded modulo seven. Let \(\mathcal T(C_7)\) be the simplicial complex of tubings: its vertices are nonempty proper connected vertex subsets of \(C_7\), and its simplices are compatible collections of such tubes. Write \(D_7=\langle r,s\mid r^7=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\), acting on labels by \(r(i)=i+1\), \(s(i)=-i\). For the **ordinary geometric orbit space** \(Y=|\mathcal T(C_7)|/D_7\),

\[
\pi_1(Y)=1,\qquad
H_j(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,\\
\mathbb Z/7,&j=3,\\
\mathbb Z/2,&j=4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus this natural symmetry quotient is rationally acyclic, yet contains two distinct torsion primes in adjacent homological degrees.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph-associahedron \(P_{C_7}\) is the six-dimensional convex Minkowski sum \(\sum_{I\in\mathcal B(C_7)}\operatorname{conv}\{e_i:i\in I\}\subset\mathbb R^7\), where \(\mathcal B(C_7)\) consists of all nonempty subsets with connected induced cycle subgraph, including the entire vertex set. All summands have weight one. It lies in a translated copy of the hyperplane \(V=\{(x_0,\ldots,x_6):\sum_i x_i=0\}\). Its face structure is dual to \(\mathcal T(C_7)\), equivariantly under graph automorphisms. The action is nonfree: reflections have nonempty fixed-point sets. The claim concerns the quotient of the geometric realization, not a quotient simplicial complex obtained by identifying vertex orbits without subdivision, and not a homotopy quotient.

## Proof

**1. Equivariant convex reduction.** The connected-set Minkowski construction is preserved by every graph automorphism, since a label permutation maps each coordinate simplex \(\Delta_I\) to \(\Delta_{gI}\). Choose the group-average of an interior point; this is a group-invariant interior point \(c\). Radial projection from the unit sphere in \(V\) gives a \(D_7\)-equivariant homeomorphism \(S(V)\to\partial P_{C_7}\). The standard six-simplex \(\Delta^6=\operatorname{conv}\{e_0,\ldots,e_6\}\) has the same coordinate-permutation action and an invariant barycenter, so its boundary is also \(D_7\)-equivariantly homeomorphic to \(S(V)\). The equivariant dual-face/barycentric identification therefore gives

\[
Y\cong\partial\Delta^6/D_7.
\]

**2. An integral orbit-cell complex.** Let \(B\) be the poset of the \(126\) nonempty proper subsets of the seven labels. Its order complex \(\Delta B\) is the barycentric subdivision of \(\partial\Delta^6\), and a simplex is a strictly nested chain \(I_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq I_k\). A label permutation preserves every subset cardinality; thus it cannot permute the vertices inside such a chain. There are no inversions, and each orbit of chains is a regular quotient cell with the orientation inherited from increasing cardinality. For any oriented chain, the cellular boundary is the alternating sum of its deletion faces, each replaced by its orbit representative; multiplicities and signs are retained.

Exhaustive chain generation gives the original cell counts in dimensions zero through five as

\[
(126,1806,8400,16800,15120,5040).
\]

Burnside's formula gives the quotient cell counts

\[
(16,147,612,1200,1080,360).
\]

Indeed every nonidentity rotation fixes no proper nonempty subset, and every reflection fixes exactly \((14,36,24,0,0,0)\) chains in those dimensions. The resulting exact integer boundary maps obey \(\partial_{k-1}\partial_k=0\) entry by entry.

**3. Integral chain reduction.** Apply cancellation only at unit incidence entries \(\pm1\), with integer Schur-complement updates. Such a cancellation splits off a contractible two-term free chain summand, preserving **integral** homology, not merely rational or modular ranks. The reproducible sequence of cancellations, counted by boundary degree one through five, is

\[
(15,132,480,719,359).
\]

The remaining free chain groups are \(C_0=\mathbb Z\), \(C_3=\mathbb Z\), \(C_4=\mathbb Z^2\), \(C_5=\mathbb Z\), and zero otherwise. In the surviving integral bases, the only nonzero maps are

\[
\partial_4=\begin{pmatrix}7&7\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\partial_5=\begin{pmatrix}-2\\2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \partial_4\partial_5=0.
\]

Hence \(H_3\cong\operatorname{coker}\partial_4\cong\mathbb Z/7\). The kernel of \(\partial_4\) is generated by \((1,-1)\), and \(\operatorname{im}\partial_5\) is twice that subgroup. Thus \(H_4\cong\mathbb Z/2\), \(H_5=0\), and there are no other positive-degree groups.

**4. Fundamental group.** The covering sphere \(S(V)\cong S^5\) is simply connected. Each reflection has three swapped vertex pairs and one fixed vertex; its fixed subspace in \(V\) has dimension three, so its unit sphere of fixed points is \(S^2\), nonempty. Reflections generate \(D_7\). Armstrong's orbit-space theorem therefore identifies \(\pi_1(S(V)/D_7)\) with the quotient of \(D_7\) by the normal subgroup generated by fixed-point elements, which is trivial. This proves simple connectivity.

## Verification

The independent, self-contained standard-library program `verify.py` and its full pivot transcript `certificate.json` are part of this package. Run `python verify.py --verify certificate.json` from a directory containing both. The script regenerates all subset chains, group permutations and quotient boundary maps, independently checks Burnside orbit counts and every \(\partial^2=0\), checks every one of the 1,705 certified unit pivots, and verifies the final integer matrices. It separately computes modular boundary ranks for primes two, three, seven and eleven. The supplied `verifier_output.txt` records a successful reproducible execution. In particular, modular checks are supplementary; the integers seven and two in the reduced differential, not finite-field ranks alone, certify integral torsion.

## Relationship to prior work

Carr and Devadoss's graph-associahedron construction identifies the cyclohedron and its tubing faces (arXiv:math/0407229). Postnikov, Reiner and Williams's graphical nestohedron construction supplies the invariant connected-subset Minkowski realization (arXiv:math/0609184). These establish the convex representation but not the integral homology of this dihedral orbit. Armstrong's theorem supplies the quotient fundamental group. Hambleton's work on dihedral actions concerns pseudofree actions on even-dimensional spheres, whereas the present representation sphere has dimension five and has reflection-fixed two-spheres (arXiv:0907.2641). The standard cyclic subgroup quotient is a lens-space case; its seven-primary information alone does not determine the two-primary contribution from the additional nonfree reflections. The asserted new content is the **complete paired-prime integral calculation for the full dihedral quotient**, not the general radial-representation method.

## Limitations

The calculation is proved only for the seven-vertex cycle with the **full** dihedral group. No general formula for arbitrary cycle lengths or arbitrary subgroups is asserted. Simple connectivity and integral homology do not by themselves establish a wedge-of-Moore-spaces homotopy classification, which is not claimed. The proof is exact but partly computer-assisted through explicitly specified finite integer matrices and a replayable complete elimination certificate. Searches cannot establish bibliographic uniqueness; an unindexed equivalent calculation is a residual publication risk. No external independent audit has been performed.
