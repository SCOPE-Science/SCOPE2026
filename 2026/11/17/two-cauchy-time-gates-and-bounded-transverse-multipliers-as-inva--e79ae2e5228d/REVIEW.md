# Self-review

## Correctness (PASS)
The proof reconstructs Cao–Llibre's quartic vector field and restrictions to all three circle orbits. The *exact* circle-distance factors give the nondegenerate quadratic gate, and the standard Cauchy integral fixes the constants and normalization. Physical-time sampling, not uniform angle, is used. To control transverse multipliers, the exact invariant-curve cofactor identities are combined with reflection pairing that cancels odd singularities; the paired quotient has an integrable uniform bound. The finite symbolic and quadrature checks are supplementary, not proofs of infinite limits. The result excludes degenerate angular factors, third-circle contacts, and the singular boundary itself.

## Originality (PASS, provisional)
The primary full text (Cao and Llibre, arXiv:2611.05696v1, Theorem D and Section 6) classifies periodic and hyperbolic *disjoint* invariant circles but does not give a near-tangency physical-time period law, joint Cauchy time distributions, or the bounded transverse log-multiplier comparison. The proof is not just rephrasing the source's periodicity inequalities: it analyzes a singular family of time measures and cancellation in the cofactor integral. Earlier work on the Yablonskii oval and a cubic torus supplies comparable bottleneck mechanisms but does not imply these model-specific two-circle formulas; the general square-root gate mechanism itself is not claimed as novel. Search-based priority is not a certificate of uniqueness. Full text for those related repository findings was not accessible, leaving residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
This is a natural boundary question for a recent complete classification which explicitly excludes tangency. It quantifies a *pair* of diverging physical-time clocks, their nontrivial ratio, the full normalized statistical residence profile, and the contrasting finite transverse multipliers. These distinguish a near-collision of algebraic cycles from loss of hyperbolicity and permit meaningful comparison of two simultaneously slowing periodic signals. The exact constants and universal profile are useful even without a classification of the limiting singular flow.

## Limits and next checks
The result is restricted to regular external contacts and uniformly nonvanishing angular factors; simultaneous tangencies and singular angular factors are unproved. Independent scientific assessment and a broader literature priority search remain open.
