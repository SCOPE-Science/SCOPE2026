# Two circle-and-sphere wedges for open neighborhoods of even \(C_4\square C_n\) tori

## Finding

Let \(n\ge 6\) be even and \(G=C_4\square C_n\). Each parity component of the open neighborhood complex admits an explicit elementary simplicial collapse onto a cyclic union of \(n\) embedded PL two-spheres: the sphere at position \(c\) is the tetrahedral boundary on the four vertices of that parity in columns \(c,c+1\). Consecutive spheres intersect in the edge on their common column and nonconsecutive spheres are disjoint. Moreover, there is an explicit strong deformation retraction onto this embedded subcomplex, equivariant for all rotations and reflections of each of the two cyclic coordinates, including those that exchange the components. Equivariance is asserted for the deformation, not for an ordering of the elementary simplicial collapses.

Consequently
\[
\mathcal N(G)\simeq
\left(S^1\vee\bigvee^n S^2\right)\sqcup
\left(S^1\vee\bigvee^n S^2\right).
\]
In addition, under the full group \(D_4\times D_n\) of coordinate rotations and reflections, the **ordinary orbit space of the embedded union** is homeomorphic to a closed disk. Thus the ordinary orbit space of \(|\mathcal N(G)|\) strongly deformation retracts onto a disk and is contractible. The assertion specific to this family is the coordinate-equivariant embedded sphere model and its disk quotient. The abstract homotopy formula is already covered in part by prior circulant results through a reduction described below.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph has vertices \((r,c)\in \mathbb Z/4\times\mathbb Z/n\), adjacent if one coordinate is unchanged and the other changes by \(1\) or \(-1\). In the open Lovász neighborhood complex a nonempty vertex set is a face if it has a common graph neighbor. A column has exactly two vertices of each parity \(r+c\bmod 2\). The graph is connected and bipartite, so the two parity classes are exactly the two complex components: faces have constant parity, and an even graph walk gives a path in the one-skeleton of the complex. All homotopy statements refer to geometric realizations.

## Proof

**Tetrahedra and their unique free edges.** Fix one of the parities. Write \(R_c\) for its two vertices in column \(c\), regarded as an edge. Each vertex of the opposite parity in column \(c\) gives a maximal simplex of the form
\[
T=R_c\cup\{a,b\},
\]
where \(a,b\) are in columns \(c-1,c+1\), respectively, on the row of the opposite-parity vertex. The two choices of opposite-parity centers give one such simplex for each possible \(a\) in its column and for each possible \(b\) in its column. The edge \(\{a,b\}\) belongs to no other maximal simplex. Indeed, their columns have the unique common neighboring column \(c\) in \(C_n\) for \(n\ge6\), and a common graph neighbor must be in their common row. This also distinguishes all the displayed tetrahedra.

Write \(R_c=\{u,v\}\). First collapse \(T\) through its free triangle \(\{u,a,b\}\), then collapse the triangle \(\{v,a,b\}\) through its now free edge \(\{a,b\}\). Both pairs are legal because no other tetrahedron contains the flank edge \(\{a,b\}\). These pairs in the different tetrahedra are disjoint and may be performed successively in any order of the tetrahedra. The two triangles left from \(T\) are precisely \(R_c\cup\{a\}\) and \(R_c\cup\{b\}\), with their faces. At consecutive columns \(c,c+1\) the retained triangles consist of the edge \(R_c\) with either vertex of \(R_{c+1}\), and the edge \(R_{c+1}\) with either vertex of \(R_c\). These are exactly all four faces of \(\partial [R_c\cup R_{c+1}]\). The boundaries share precisely their stated column edges, which are pairwise disjoint. The total number of elementary collapse pairs is \(8n\) in the two-component complex.

**Deformation compatible with all coordinate symmetries.** In a tetrahedron with the above labels use barycentric coordinates \((w_u,w_v,w_a,w_b)\) and write \(m=\min\{w_a,w_b\}\). At time \(t\in[0,1]\) send these coordinates to
\[
(w_u+tm,\ w_v+tm,\ w_a-tm,\ w_b-tm).
\]
The coordinates stay nonnegative and sum to one. At time one at least one flank weight vanishes, which is exactly the retained two-triangle subcomplex in this tetrahedron. Points there are fixed at every time. A point in the intersection with another tetrahedron cannot have both flank weights positive, by uniqueness of the flank edge, so the formulas agree on overlaps and form a continuous global strong deformation retraction. The formula is unchanged by permuting the two rung vertices or the two flank vertices, and every rotation or reflection of either coordinate takes a rung to a rung and flanks to flanks. This proves the asserted equivariance, even for rotations interchanging the parity classes.

**Ordinary homotopy type.** In each tetrahedral boundary choose one edge joining its two opposite column edges. The two column edges with this chosen edge form a three-edge arc. The sphere has a CW structure whose one-skeleton is this arc, with one two-cell: cutting the sphere along the arc gives a disk whose boundary runs along both sides of the arc. These CW structures agree on the shared column edges. The resulting one-skeleton of the cyclic union has \(2n\) vertices and \(2n\) edges and is connected, so has cycle rank one. Each two-cell attaches by a loop in its own contractible arc. Hence all \(n\) attaching maps are nullhomotopic, and the component is homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\vee\bigvee^n S^2\). In particular the original complex has reduced integral \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\), \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{2n}\), with all higher homology zero and no torsion.

**Disk quotient.** The coordinate group is transitive on the spheres in the two components together: column rotations move the slab and a row rotation by one changes parity. Fix one sphere \(S\) on columns \(c,c+1\). Its stabilizer acts on its four vertices by all permutations preserving the partition into the two column pairs. With columns fixed, the row reflections \(r\mapsto-r\) and \(r\mapsto2-r\) independently exchange one of the two row-parity pairs. The column reflection interchanging \(c,c+1\), combined with the row reflection \(r\mapsto1-r\), exchanges the two column pairs. Conversely a stabilizer element must preserve or swap the columns. The global orbit space of the spine equals the orbit space of \(S\) by this stabilizer: if points of \(S\) with support in both columns are equivalent, each belongs to just one sphere, forcing the transporting element to stabilize \(S\). Points with support in a single column can only be transported to vertices or edge points with the same barycentric weights up to permutation, and all such identifications within \(S\) are already made by the stabilizer. This yields a continuous bijection of compact Hausdorff quotients, hence a homeomorphism.

Radial projection from the tetrahedron barycenter identifies \(S\) equivariantly with the unit sphere of the space \(x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4=0\), with the two pairs numbered \((1,2)\) and \((3,4)\). In the orthonormal coordinates \(X=(x_1-x_2)/\sqrt2\), \(Y=(x_3-x_4)/\sqrt2\), \(Z=(x_1+x_2-x_3-x_4)/2\), the two independent swaps flip \(X\) and \(Y\), respectively. Their quotient is the closed sector \(X,Y\ge0\) in that unit sphere. Swapping the pairs acts there by \((X,Y,Z)\mapsto(Y,X,-Z)\). Put \(A=(X+Y)/\sqrt2\), \(B=(X-Y)/\sqrt2\); the sector is exactly \(A\ge |B|\) with \(A^2+B^2+Z^2=1\). Projection to \((B,Z)\) identifies it with the closed ellipse \(2B^2+Z^2\le1\) since \(A\) is uniquely the nonnegative square root. The remaining involution induces \((B,Z)\mapsto(-B,-Z)\). After rescaling the ellipse to a disk its quotient is a disk, by retaining the radius and doubling the angle. The strong deformation already constructed descends to this orbit space.

## Relationship to prior work

Shukla's Theorems 6(B) and 13 and the proof of the latter give sphere garlands for suitable four-regular circulants; in particular, their collapse to tetrahedral boundaries is the closest prior structural analogue. There is also an indirect overlap with the even family here. If \(n=2m\) with \(m\ge3\) odd, projection onto \(H=C_4\square C_m\) identifies each parity component's vertices bijectively with \(V(H)\). The opposite-parity lifts of neighbors of any vertex of \(H\) are exactly its chosen lift's four neighbors upstairs, so each parity component is isomorphic to \(\mathcal N(H)\). The Chinese remainder parametrization makes \(H\) the circulant \(C_{4m}(4,m)\). This meets Theorem 13: \(4t=4m\) for \(s=4,t=m\), and odd \(m\ge3\) avoids all listed exceptions. In its sphere garland the boundaries have indices modulo \(2m\), with neighboring indices separated by \(4+m\), which is relatively prime to \(2m\); there are \(2m=n\) spheres. The ordinary homotopy formula for \(n\equiv2\pmod4\) is therefore not an independent claim. In contrast, the local deformation proved here uses the two coordinate factors directly, covers also \(4\mid n\), and specifies the embedded target and a deformation compatible with the full coordinate-dihedral action, not just an abstract homotopy type.

The square-lattice quotient theorem of October 11, 2026 assumes all nonzero periods have Manhattan length at least five. The period \((4,0)\) excludes this graph family. Another calculation on the same graphs, *A signed integral sphere lattice at the four-cycle boundary of toroidal three-cut complexes*, computes the square filling of the graph and the topology and integral symmetry lattice of its three-cut Alexander dual. The square filling is a related model with the same ordinary homotopy type as one parity component; it is built from the original graph edges and square cells rather than a subcomplex of open neighborhoods. The scale-two Vietoris–Rips complex of the same graph has a different consecutive-column collapse, to three-spheres. The local two-pair collapse is also used for the neighborhood complex of \(K_3\square C_n\), where it gives a torus. The barycentric minimum-transfer deformation itself was used for square-cycle neighborhood complexes, where the invariant target is a torus with a sphere quotient by its dihedral group. Here the local methods yield a different gluing pattern: the two-column sphere stabilizer has two independent within-column swaps and a column exchange, giving a disk quotient. Neither the earlier square-cycle result nor the nonequivariant circulant homotopy theorem gives this action on the embedded spheres.

## Verification and limitations

The free-edge uniqueness and barycentric formula certify the collapse and deformation for all the stated parameters. The original standard-library `verify_even_tori.py` and `EXPECTED.txt` provide a separate finite check of the homotopy consequence: they use a different Morse matching with a winding cocycle for even \(n=6,8,\ldots,20\), and mod-two ranks for the first four of those values. They do not check or stand in for the equivariant deformation proof. No conclusion is asserted for width other than four, \(n=4\), odd lengths, or twisted periods. The abstract wedge equivalence need not be equivariant, and no claim about comprehensive publication priority is made.
