# Review: Frucht perfect-matching homotopy

## Correctness — PASS
The object is defined by the canonical twelve-entry LCF list, not an ambiguous picture. An independent labeled graph check confirms it is isomorphic to the standard Frucht graph. Recursion on the least uncovered vertex and a separate exhaustive six-edge spanning-set enumeration independently yield exactly eight perfect matchings. Intersections of the resulting facet simplices are simplices, justifying the ordinary nerve theorem. Two nerve constructions agree on all 256 potential faces. Each of the 26 recorded elementary collapses is verified by examining **all** remaining cofaces, and the remaining connected graph has five vertices and six edges. Thus the full integral homotopy equivalence, fundamental group, homology and nonshellability assertions follow. No finite experiment is extrapolated to an infinite family.

The Franklin perfect-matching result concerns a different twelve-vertex cubic graph. The 2026 Möbius-ladder paper studies another graph family. Actual searches with the exact object, aliases, eight facet count, homotopy type, and possible stronger classifications found no implication of the target claim. This is a defensible best-knowledge priority assessment, not an exhaustive literature guarantee.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Frucht graph is a canonical smallest-order cubic asymmetric graph, an especially natural contrast with the highly symmetric graph families in which perfect-matching complexes have been calculated. Its perfect-matching complex is pure five-dimensional but has nontrivial free nonabelian \(\pi_1\), not merely different rational Betti numbers. The transparent eight-simplex nerve and 26-collapse proof explain the complete homotopy type and isolate precisely 29 missing matching pairs, while the graph's ordinary matching complex is a wedge of twenty three-spheres. A complete exact calculation of this canonical benchmark is a meaningful finite topological classification rather than a parameter substitution.
