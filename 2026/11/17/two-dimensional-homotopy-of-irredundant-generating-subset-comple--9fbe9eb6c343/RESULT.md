# Two-dimensional homotopy of irredundant generating-subset complexes of dihedral groups of squarefree rotational order

## Finding
Let \(p\ne q\) be primes, including the possibility that one is \(2\), put \(n=pq\) and \(h=(p-1)(q-1)\), and let \(D_{2n}=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\). Its **group independence complex** \(\mathcal I(D_{2n})\) has the nonidentity elements as vertices; its faces are the finite subsets \(F\) satisfying \(x\notin\langle F\setminus\{x\}\rangle\) for every \(x\in F\). This is not the graph-theoretic independence complex of a Cayley graph, and no face is required to generate the whole group.

Then there is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
|\mathcal I(D_{2pq})|\simeq\bigvee^{\beta_1}S^1\ \vee\ \bigvee^{\beta_2}S^2,
\]
with
\[
\beta_1=\frac{(p-1)(q-1)(3pq-2)}{2},\qquad
\beta_2=2pq(p-1)(q-1)-\frac{3pq(p+q-2)}2-(p-1)(q-1)+pq+p+q-3.
\]
All reduced integral homology is torsion-free, supported only in dimensions one and two, with the indicated ranks. In particular, the fundamental group is free of rank \(\beta_1\). The result holds for every pair of **distinct** primes, not only for odd primes.

For example, \((p,q)=(2,3)\) gives \(\bigvee^{16}S^1\vee\bigvee^3S^2\), and \((p,q)=(3,5)\) gives \(\bigvee^{172}S^1\vee\bigvee^{117}S^2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The dihedral group has exactly \(2pq\) elements, with \(n\) reflections \(sr^i\), \(0\le i<n\). Partition the nonidentity rotations into sets \(P,Q,H\) according as their order is \(p,q,pq\), so \(|P|=p-1\), \(|Q|=q-1\), \(|H|=h\). The orientation of the reflection exponent is immaterial: the criterion for two reflections to generate a subgroup containing another is congruence modulo \(\gcd(n,i-j)\). Homotopy equivalence means ordinary nonequivariant homotopy of geometric realizations.

## Proof
**1. Complete face classification.** A subgroup of rotations is cyclic. Thus the only independent pair of rotations consists of one order-\(p\) and one order-\(q\) element. A rotation of order \(pq\) is independent only as a singleton or together with one reflection. Any two distinct reflections are independent. The only independent triples are (i) an order-\(p\) rotation, an order-\(q\) rotation and a reflection; (ii) one order-\(p\) rotation and two reflections whose exponents agree modulo \(p\); and (iii) one order-\(q\) rotation and two reflections whose exponents agree modulo \(q\). The two reflection exponents must be distinct. No triple of reflections is independent: identifying a reflection exponent with its pair of residues modulo \(p\) and \(q\), two reflections generate the whole group if they differ in both coordinates; otherwise their generated subgroup contains all reflections in their shared row or shared column. Three distinct points with each pair sharing a row or a column must all share one row or all share one column, so one reflection lies in the subgroup generated by the other two. Finally, each of the three listed kinds of independent triple generates the entire dihedral group: the first uses the two coprime rotation orders and one reflection, while in the second and third cases the difference of the reflection exponents generates the complementary prime-order rotation subgroup. Thus no independent four-element face exists.

For clarity, the group-theoretic independence test above can also be verified from a single subgroup formula: for a subset with rotation exponents \(a_1,\dots,a_u\) and reflection exponents \(b_1,\dots,b_v\), let \(d=\gcd(n,a_1,\dots,a_u,b_2-b_1,\dots,b_v-b_1)\), omitting unavailable terms. The generated subgroup contains exactly the rotations with exponent divisible by \(d\), and, if \(v\ge1\), the reflections with exponent congruent to \(b_1\pmod d\). This formula proves the classification by testing removal of each chosen element. In particular, all faces and all boundary cases with \(p=2\) are covered.

**2. A simply connected two-dimensional core.** Let \(K_0\) be the subcomplex on reflection vertices and the rotations \(P\sqcup Q\), keeping precisely the triangles classified above. Its edges comprise every reflection-to-\(P\) and reflection-to-\(Q\) edge, every \(P\)-to-\(Q\) edge, and precisely those reflection pairs agreeing modulo \(p\) or modulo \(q\). There are
\[
f_0=n+p+q-2,\quad f_1=\frac{3n(p+q-2)}2+h,\quad f_2=2nh.
\]
Indeed, the reflection pairs contribute \(n(q-1)/2\) edges for agreement modulo \(p\) and \(n(p-1)/2\) for agreement modulo \(q\), and these two classes are disjoint. Triangles contribute \(nh\) of type (i) and \(nh/2\) of each of types (ii) and (iii).

To compute \(\pi_1(K_0)\), fix \(P_0\in P\), \(Q_0\in Q\). The edges from \(P_0\) to every reflection, the edges from \(P_0\) to every element of \(Q\), and the edges from \(Q_0\) to \(P\setminus\{P_0\}\) form a spanning tree. Every remaining edge is homotopic relative to endpoints to its tree path: the \(P_0,Q,R\) triangles handle arbitrary \(Q\)-reflection edges; the \(P,Q_0,R\) triangles handle \(P\)-reflection edges; a \(P,Q,R\) triangle handles each \(P\)-\(Q\) edge; the triangles \(P_0,R_i,R_j\) for equal residues modulo \(p\), and \(Q_0,R_i,R_j\) for equal residues modulo \(q\), handle all reflection-reflection edges. These relations kill every fundamental-group generator, so \(\pi_1(K_0)=0\).

The reduced Euler characteristic is \(\widetilde\chi(K_0)=f_0-f_1+f_2-1\). Since \(K_0\) is finite, two-dimensional and simply connected, integral cellular homology gives \(H_2(K_0;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{\beta_2}\) with \(\beta_2=f_0-f_1+f_2-1\). Hurewicz yields based maps from \(\beta_2\) two-spheres giving an integral homology isomorphism; the homology version of Whitehead's theorem for simply connected CW complexes makes their wedge a homotopy equivalence to \(K_0\).

**3. Attach the remaining edges.** The \(h\) order-\(pq\) rotation vertices have exactly \(n\) edges each, one to each reflection, and are in no triangle. Adjoining one such vertex and its \(n\) edges to the connected core adds \(n-1\) circles. The reflection pairs differing modulo both primes comprise \(n(n-1)/2-n(p+q-2)/2=nh/2\) further edges; they also lie in no triangle and add one circle each. Thus
\[
\beta_1=h(n-1)+\frac{nh}{2}=\frac{h(3n-2)}2.
\]
Graph edge attachments to a connected CW complex are homotopy equivalent to wedging these circles onto the core. Combining with step 2 proves the stated integral homotopy type and free fundamental group.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_dihedral_pq.py` uses only Python's standard library. It reconstructs generated subgroups from gcd congruences, independently enumerates all independent edges and triangles, checks no independent four-subsets, verifies the exact predicted face sets, and computes the mod-two ranks of the full simplicial triangle boundary matrix. Parameter pairs \((2,3),(2,5),(3,5),(3,7)\) all passed; the recorded complete output is `verification_output.txt`. This finite verification corroborates the proof but is not itself a proof for arbitrarily large primes. The all-prime statement is proved by the subgroup classification and the simply connected core argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Pinckney (2021, Chapter 8, p. 97) explicitly discusses unpublished preliminary structural results for the same dihedral group family with distinct **odd** primes and identifies their classification as a natural future direction. Those unpublished results were not presented in the thesis, and no homotopy classification or Betti formulas were found in its relevant text; overlap with the uncirculated preliminary work cannot be excluded. Pinckney introduces this precise group independence complex and studies other finite group families. Cameron (2024) and Lucchini–Stanojkovski (first publicly posted March 25, 2025) study independent generating subsets and related complexes in general, but do not supply the present complete two-dimensional dihedral homotopy decomposition in the passages examined. This result treats the group independence complex, not the Frattini-core non-generating complex of a dihedral group, which has a different face predicate.

## Limitations
The primes must be distinct. The proof does not cover arbitrary composite rotational order, products with repeated prime factors, equivariant homotopy types, higher group actions on integral homology, or the full-group generating-subset-only subcomplex. A relevant 2021 thesis discloses unpublished preliminary structural results for the odd-prime portion; lack of access to those unpublished results leaves an originality risk despite no identified published equivalent. The verification code computes mod-two homology only; the claimed integral homotopy type uses the separate explicit topological proof.
