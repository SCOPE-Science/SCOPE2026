# Mathematical review: two incommensurable proximity thresholds

## Correctness — PASS

The proof checks the exact domain and both directions of each implication. Density makes open threshold relations closed under positive addition via two-step paths. The key normed-space inclusion identity, \(B_s(y)\cap A\subseteq B_r(x)\cap A\iff\|x-y\|\le r-s\) for \(r>s>0\), is verified using an outward ray and density. Positive integer combinations generate open thresholds, and their positive differences generate closed thresholds. Irrationality makes the resulting subgroup dense, recovering all distances. Completion and Mazur–Ulam justify the affine extension. The explicit rational-ratio homeomorphism is nonisometric but preserves both thresholds. Supplementary exact-arithmetic computations are not substituted for these arguments.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest directly inspected manuscript, Wang–Tang–Luo (arXiv:2611.09463v1, Sections 1, 5–6), treats one-scale step-isometries, geometry-dependent rigidity, and the whole-space closed-integer-ball lemma. Its nonlinear real-line example makes the new two-scale phase transition substantive, rather than a consequence of one-scale rigidity. Balister et al. (arXiv:1504.05324v1, Introduction and Theorems 4–5) and Balogh–Walters–Zsák (arXiv:2409.04237v1, Introduction and Theorem 3) treat one-scale rigidity in different settings. A published two-radius metric-frame result deals with arbitrary metric realizations and finite distance alphabets, not uniqueness of normed-space distances; its summary was available, not its complete manuscript. Targeted implication comparisons with related published results and primary literature did not identify a covering two-threshold theorem. Search absence is not proof of absolute novelty; unindexed equivalent proximity-graph formulations remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A single threshold admits nonlinear step-isometries even on the real line; two irrationally related threshold relations eliminate them in **every** normed space, including nonreflexive and nonsmooth examples, and even on dense rather than full domains. This is a structurally sharp qualitative transition for graph-based recovery of normed metrics. The proof exposes a reusable relation-theoretic reconstruction lemma and provides explicit commensurable-radius counterexamples. Its value lies in the general rigidity dichotomy, not the supplementary finite check.

## Scope and remaining risk

The conclusion requires bidirectional comparisons, bijection, density, and real normed-space geometry. It is not a theorem about arbitrary metric spaces or finite samples. The closest historical works have been checked at relevant statements and constructions, but a previously unindexed simultaneous two-radius theorem could supersede this claim.
