# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The labeled complex is a wedge of six five-spheres by Babson et al., Theorem 3.1. Each of the five reflections stabilizes a star-graph simplex, so the stabilizer normal closure is the entire dihedral group; Armstrong's theorem proves simple connectivity of the quotient. Direct signed Lefschetz counts \((-5,0,3)\) give character values \((6,1,-2)\), averaging to zero, hence rational acyclicity. The independent executable constructs every labeled graph mask and every barycentric quotient chain; checked ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) are \((0,96,2190,13488,31440,30238,10080)\), all square compositions are zero, and the Betti vector is \((1,0,0,0,2,2,0)\). Rational acyclicity plus the integral universal coefficient theorem implies exactly two cyclic 2-primary summands in fourth homology and none elsewhere; no exponent is inferred.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected material
Babson et al. determine the **labeled** complex's sphere-wedge type and rational character, not the mod-two homology of its nonfree dihedral orbit space. Jonsson's Chapter 21 concerns polygon-representation graph properties, not orbit spaces of the original complex. The closest dihedral quotient search result concerns a five-color pentagon graph-Hom complex, which has different cells and no implication here. Searches for aliases, cyclic-to-dihedral reductions and finite tables and inspection of accessible historical claim texts did not disclose a stronger or equivalent result. This is evidence of no found overlap, not proof of global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
This is a natural exact finite invariant at five labels, the smallest odd prime case where the classical not-2-connected graph complex is already highly connected and a full pentagonal symmetry quotient is possible. It gives a concrete simply connected, rationally acyclic but noncontractible symmetry quotient, with precisely two independent two-torsion homology classes. It supplies integral information absent from the labeled homology character and from free cyclic quotients; computing exact torsion exponents is a separate open limit rather than a hidden assertion.

## Closest literature and limitations
See Babson et al. (1997/1999), Jonsson (2007), Shareshian (2001), and Armstrong (1968). The package does not determine odd torsion, 2-primary exponents, or the complete homotopy type.
