# Mathematical review: two parabolic Fermat–Torricelli transitions

## Correctness — PASS

For three ordered parameters the exact chord slopes are their pair sums; the three Euclidean interior angles therefore reduce to three differences of principal arctangents. The deterministic Fermat–Torricelli criterion converts those angles into mutually exclusive network topologies. The middle-angle range is globally maximized only at the symmetric boundary triple, yielding \(a_0=1/\sqrt3\). The endpoint-angle range is globally maximized, at the second onset, at a unique tangent-chord boundary triple; minimizing its exact angular inequality yields \(a_1=\sqrt3/2+2\sqrt6/3\). Both rare-event coordinates are localized by a unique strict maximum and a negative quadratic transverse curvature. Their limiting regions have computable finite volume, and ordered uniform parameters have density \(3/(4a^3)\). These facts prove both \(\delta^{5/2}\) constants and, via \(L^1\) convergence of normalized indicators, both conditional total-variation statements. Direct exact-angle numerical integrations and threshold-identity checks in the package passed. Numerical checks are independent corroboration, not the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk

The 120-degree Fermat–Torricelli criterion is classical and expressly excluded from novelty. Zachos (2014) provides the deterministic floating/absorbed classification and weighted constructions; Jahn et al. (2014) extend point-location optimization to convex sets. Inspection found no results for independently parameter-uniform triples on a symmetric parabolic arc or for the two explicit topology onset constants and marked distributions. A 2015 parabola-triangle discussion considers a 120-degree triangle with one fixed parabola vertex but not the stochastic phase diagram. The closest indexed comparisons concern parabolic random convex-hull area, random angular phenomena on other curves, or fixed triangle optimization; their statements do not imply the proposed double-threshold probability law. Result-index searches cannot establish uniqueness; unindexed or inaccessible historical work remains a genuine risk.

## Value — PASS

The geometry has a natural network interpretation: the optimal Euclidean three-terminal network changes first from a sampled middle hub to an interior junction, and later allows a sampled endpoint hub. The distinct finite arc-width thresholds are not arbitrary numeric slices; they separate all three possible network topologies. The exact leading-order rare-event probabilities and full marked conditional laws explain *how* each transition occurs, supplying a quantitative geometric phase diagram rather than merely testing the classical angle criterion.
