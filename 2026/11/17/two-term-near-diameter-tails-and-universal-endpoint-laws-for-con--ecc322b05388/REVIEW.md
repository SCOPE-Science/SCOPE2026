# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof identifies every maximizing pair as a diametral vertex pair, shows that diameter supporting directions are strictly positive on both nonzero tangent cones, and localizes all near-diameter pairs to bounded cone products. The exact squared-distance inequality and a uniformly valid radial boundary expansion yield the leading and next coefficient with an \(O(\varepsilon^{2d+2})\) absolute error. The coarea calculation produces the beta and Dirichlet laws; total-variation convergence follows from an \(O(\varepsilon)\) symmetric-difference bound on compact regions. For the box corollary the ray volumes and \(\operatorname{Dirichlet}(2,\ldots,2)\) moments are independently evaluated. Potential incorrect shortcuts—including treating ordered pairs as unordered, confusing the normalized distance deficit with slack, and extrapolating to smooth bodies—are excluded. The stored checker tests only finite cases and is not a substitute for these arguments.

## Originality — PASS with historical limitations

Searches of published mathematical records and accessible own prior claims compared the precise Euclidean pair-distance endpoint event, vertex-cone mixture, second-order coefficient, and Dirichlet conditional law against aliases and stronger statements. The nearest published 2013 and 2016 polygon references provide exact or algorithmic **marginal** distance distributions, possibly implying polygon-level marginal tails on expansion, but not in their available abstracts/sections the all-dimensional joint endpoint law. The 2012 maximum-interpoint-distance paper studies a growing sample from spherically symmetric distributions, not two points from arbitrary polytopes. Published records on polygon **short** pair distances and difference-body **gauge** distance address different ends or norms. Residual priority risk is real because the complete 2016 polygon paper and the 2002 sample-diameter paper were not materially inspected in full; no claim of exhaustive literature coverage is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The universal exponent \(2d\) and positive first correction quantify exceptionally long pair distances in polytopes in every dimension, in terms of intrinsic local cone geometry. The conditional endpoint theorem adds shape information entirely absent from a marginal tail, including a geometrically invariant cone-pair mixture, a universal beta law for the normalized distance deficit, and universal Dirichlet patterns of vertex displacements. The box coefficients and sharp fixed-volume cube extremizer provide interpretable exact tests and an optimization consequence. This is not a routine restatement of one polygon's distance-density formula.
