For two uniform boundary points of a smooth convex oval, the probability that their chord cuts off area at most \(t\) has a two-term \(t^{1/3}\)-plus-\(t\) expansion whose second coefficient is exactly a negative curvature-gradient energy correction. The rare-cut location reconstructs arclength-labelled curvature, and an explicit harmonic family exhibits a sharp positive-to-negative second-coefficient transition.
