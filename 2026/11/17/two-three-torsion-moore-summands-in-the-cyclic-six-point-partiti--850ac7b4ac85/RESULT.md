# Two three-torsion Moore summands in the cyclic six-point partition quotient

## Finding
Let \(\bar\Pi_6\) be the poset of set partitions of \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), excluding the discrete and indiscrete partitions, ordered by refinement. Let \(X=|\Delta(\bar\Pi_6)|\), and let \(C_6\) act by the rotation \(i\mapsto i+1\pmod6\). The **ordinary geometric orbit space** \(Q=X/C_6\) has integral homology
\[
 H_j(Q;\mathbb Z)\cong
 \begin{cases}
 \mathbb Z,&j=0,\\
 (\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^2,&j=2,\\
 \mathbb Z^{20},&j=3,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Moreover its fundamental group is trivial, and the full homotopy type is
\[
 Q\simeq M(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,2)\vee M(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,2)
       \vee\bigvee^{20}S^3,
\]
where \(M(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,2)=S^2\cup_{3}e^3\) is the degree-three Moore space. The three-primary classes occur in degree two, even though the original partition complex is a torsion-free wedge of 120 three-spheres.

## Assumptions and scope
This is the *six-point* partition lattice and the *full regular cyclic* subgroup generated by a six-cycle; it is not a claim about every composite order, other subgroup actions, homotopy-orbit spaces, or equivariant stable topology. Every simplex is a strictly increasing chain of partitions, with the alternating orientation induced by its chain order. The quotient is an ordinary topological orbit space, not a homotopy quotient. All homology has integral coefficients unless specified.

## Proof
**1. Original topology and simple connectivity of the quotient.** The classical partition-lattice calculation gives \(X\simeq\bigvee^{120}S^3\). In particular \(X\) is simply connected. Let
\[
 P=\{\{0,2,4\},\{1,3,5\}\}.
\]
Rotation by one relabels these two blocks, hence fixes the vertex \(P\) of \(X\). Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem says that if a finite group acts on a simply connected locally path-connected space, the quotient fundamental group is the acting group divided by the normal subgroup generated by elements with fixed points. Here a generator of \(C_6\) fixes \(P\); therefore \(\pi_1(Q)=1\).

**2. An exact orbit-cell complex.** Every partition has a distinct rank within any strict chain. Thus a cyclic element preserving a simplex must fix each of its vertices: there are no inversions. The geometric quotient therefore has one oriented cell for each cyclic orbit of strict chains. For an orbit represented by a chain \([p_0<\cdots<p_k]\), its cellular boundary is the signed sum of the face orbits,
\[
 \partial[p_0<\cdots<p_k]_{C_6}
 =\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j[p_0<\cdots<\widehat p_j<\cdots<p_k]_{C_6}.
\]
This is the actual integral cellular chain complex of the quotient; no freeness assumption or division by stabilizer orders occurs. Exhaustive recursive generation gives 201 proper partitions, 201/1865/4245/2700 original simplices in dimensions 0/1/2/3 and 41/326/720/454 cyclic orbit cells in those dimensions. The latter numbers give \(\chi(Q)=41-326+720-454=-19\).

**3. Exact integral chain contractions.** The accompanying standard-library verifier enumerates every chain and every boundary, checks \(\partial^2=0\), and performs 758 elementary cancellations at coefficients \(+1\) or \(-1\). An elementary unit-pivot cancellation is a chain-homotopy equivalence over \(\mathbb Z\), not a rational row reduction. At termination the surviving chain groups have ranks \((1,0,2,22)\) in degrees 0 through 3, and the only nonzero differential is a \(2\times22\) matrix \(A\) from degree 3 to degree 2. In the verifier's deterministic row/column ordering its nonzero entries are
\[
 \begin{split}
 A_{1,*}:&\quad (1,-3),(2,-3),(4,3),(6,3),(7,3),
 (9,3),(11,3),(14,-3),(17,-3),(20,3),\\
 A_{2,*}:&\quad (14,3),(15,-3),(18,3),(19,3),
 \end{split}
\]
where the displayed column indices start at zero. All unspecified entries vanish. Every entry is divisible by three, and columns 1 and 15 have determinant \(+9\) in the two-row minor. Because there is at least one entry of absolute value three and the gcd of all two-by-two minors is nine, the integral Smith diagonal is exactly \((3,3)\). Thus \(H_2(Q)=\operatorname{coker}(A)\cong(\mathbb Z/3)^2\) and \(H_3(Q)=\ker(A)\cong\mathbb Z^{20}\). The other degrees follow from the contracted complex. The computation is finite and exhaustive over the explicitly specified six-element object.

**4. Passage from homology to homotopy.** A simply connected three-dimensional CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of degree-two Moore spaces and three-spheres with multiplicities given by the Smith form of the degree-three attachments. Indeed, its simply connected two-skeleton is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres by Hurewicz and Whitehead; the three-cell attaching maps are classified by their integral \(\pi_2\)-coordinates. Integral basis changes give the Smith form of those maps. Applying this to \(Q\) with \((3,3)\) and a kernel of rank 20 yields the claimed homotopy equivalence. No assertion of a canonical or equivariant splitting is made.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_partition_quotient.py` from the directory containing that file; no third-party packages or external data are needed. The verifier regenerates all partitions and strict chains, computes their cyclic orbits and integer boundary matrices, checks initial and contracted boundary compositions, performs all 758 integral unit-pivot cancellations, and checks the displayed residual matrix and its Smith invariants. It also reproduces the known prime-order \(n=5\) homology \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z/5\), \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^4\) as a calibration. Expected output is given verbatim in `check_output.txt`. The Python checker validates the finite integral calculation; the separate fundamental-group and homotopy deductions use the mathematical arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
The homology and homotopy type of the unquotiented partition complex are classical. Donau's *Quotients of the topology of the partition lattice which are not homotopy equivalent to wedges of spheres*, publicly posted February 20, 2012, studies regular **prime-order** cyclic actions; its Proposition 4.2 gives first homology of order \(p\) and the free top homology for \(p\ge5\). Its proof exploits the absence of stabilizers, whereas the present six-cycle fixes a partition vertex. Donau's earlier result for subgroups of a Young stabilizer is inapplicable because this regular cyclic action is transitive and fixes no individual ground-set element. Here the two degree-two classes of order three and the full Moore-space wedge are the additional information. These distinctions were checked at the level of hypotheses and implications, not solely titles.

## Limitations
The classification is proved only for the ordinary quotient by the particular regular six-cycle. It does not establish an all-composite formula, canonical integral equivariant generators, or quotient-space multiplicative cohomology operations. The orbit and Smith computations are reproducible, but an independent external mathematical audit has not been performed. A mathematically equivalent, unindexed treatment of this specific six-cycle action could remain in the literature.
