In a sinusoidally perturbed three-echelon supply-chain flow, increasing the feedback amplitude creates arbitrarily many distinct attracting equilibria: their number grows as \(p^{1/3}/(\pi C^{1/3})\), while the total equilibrium count is asymptotically twice as large. Explicit, strictly ordered scalar fold thresholds explain the new stationary branches, and a full three-dimensional Hurwitz test distinguishes genuinely attracting branches from merely existing roots.
