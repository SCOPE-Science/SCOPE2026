# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every strictly positive parameter quintuple, the unique positive equilibrium follows from a quadratic whose root product is negative. Its Jacobian has a cubic characteristic polynomial with coefficients \(( -\alpha,\omega,-\alpha)\), \(-1/2<\alpha<1/2\), and \(0<\omega<1\). The complete strict Jury–Schur inequalities follow. Distinct positive alternating states are forbidden by an exact positive-factor identity. For fixed \(u,v,a,b\), simple-root perturbation around \(\lambda(\lambda^2+1)\) establishes the spectral radius asymptotic; the leading term is independent of \(a,b\). The independent symbolic checker and sample spectral calculations passed. No nonlinear global-convergence claim is made.

## Originality — PASS within available evidence
AlSharawi, Cánovas and Kallel (2024), Example 4.3(v), contain the normalized rational map. Their Theorem 4.7 supplies global-attractivity conditions in some coefficient regions; those regions therefore already imply local stability and are credited. The complete 21-page accessible source was compared against the large-inhibition spectral claim and the universal positive two-cycle obstruction; neither appears in the inspected statements. Targeted indexed-record and literature searches identified distinct systems and standard general stability machinery, but no prior statement implying the full spectral-gap law. A general global theorem for all coefficient values or a prior asymptotic treatment could subsume portions of this result; publication priority is not guaranteed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The sharp law \(1-\rho(r)\sim v/(2\sqrt{ur})\) identifies a natural and singular loss of uniform relaxation speed without a finite parameter destabilization. It quantitatively explains long-lived near-period-four oscillations when delayed inhibition becomes strong, with a leading coefficient independent of both same-parity coupling strengths. The all-parameter sink and no-two-cycle results constrain interpretations of such transients. This is a structural stability statement across a full positive family, not a numerical threshold or arbitrarily selected finite slice.

## Limits and closest work
The original global theorem is stronger than local stability where its sufficient hypotheses apply; we do not claim the latter there as novel. Standard Jury and simple-root perturbation results are used, not rediscovered. This work proves no global attraction outside published parameter regions, excludes no period \(\ge3\), and does not estimate long-time nonlinear transient behavior uniformly over the positive phase space. Relevance of other older third-order rational recurrence literature has been screened but not exhaustively settled.
