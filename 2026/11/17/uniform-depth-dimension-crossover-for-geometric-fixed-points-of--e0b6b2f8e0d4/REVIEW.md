# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite-level Lattès counting identity is applied under the required characteristic-zero, scalar-isogeny, geometric-generic hypotheses. Each signed permutation cycle has a uniform fair sign product and a kernel of the stated exact cardinality. The classical cycle polynomial yields the exact level and limiting fractions. The logarithmic bound follows from elementary harmonic-sum inequalities, uniformly in dimension and depth. The proof explicitly handles the diverging-parameter case without an unproved convergence assumption. The executable verifier independently recomputes finite cases using the signed permutations rather than reusing the formula.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk
Li (2026) gives the general fixed-dimensional Lattès formula, while Boston et al. (1993) already give the general wreath product fixed-point formula (as fully presented in Arumugam, Dietrich and Glasby (2022)). In consequence, the exact gamma quotient and its fixed-dimensional limits alone are previously implied and are not presented as the novel assertion. The assessed result is the sharp, uniform simultaneous depth–dimension approximation and the explicit critical window, including its necessity. Searches against related mathematical findings and available literature did not identify this two-parameter law for geometric symmetric Lattès powers. The original Boston paper's archival copy was identified, but its complete text could not be read in this inspection, leaving a specific historical risk.

## Value — PASS
A finite-depth monodromy computation may accurately approximate the infinite-depth proportion for any fixed projective dimension yet fail by a finite or unbounded relative factor as projective dimension increases. The theorem resolves the required iteration depth quantitatively and proves a sharp logarithmic-logarithmic crossover. It also reveals polynomial survival under full symmetric powering versus exponential survival under independent Cartesian powering, an important distinction when comparing natural high-dimensional Lattès constructions. This is a meaningful uniformity and scaling question, not a claim to rediscover the classical fixed-point counting formula.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to symmetric powers of the multiplication-by-integer elliptic Lattès map over algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields. It does not determine arithmetic fixed-point proportions or non-scalar isogeny cases. The combination of the classical cycle identity and Li's formula makes the finite-parameter identity prior-covered; originality is limited to the uniform two-parameter statement and requires independent historical validation.
