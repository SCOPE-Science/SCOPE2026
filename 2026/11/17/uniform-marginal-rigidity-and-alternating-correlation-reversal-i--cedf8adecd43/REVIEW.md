# Scientific review: phase-resolved uniform laws for a threshold neuron

## Correctness

**PASS.** The push-forward density of each sign-threshold branch proves that exactly the endpoint gains preserve normalized Lebesgue measure on the invariant interval. The identities \(B^2=\mathrm{id}\), \(AB=A\), and \(A=\mathrm{id}+B\), interpreted off a null set, reduce the alternating dynamics to the signed binary shift. Transfer-operator evaluation gives all parity-resolved covariances. Exact covariance summation and a uniformly bounded binary-digit recoding error give the variance and central limit theorem. The standalone rational interval-refinement checker confirms the stated finite examples; it is not used to infer infinite-time assertions.

## Originality

**PASS against inspected accessible evidence.** The original 2023 Bula–Radin article develops deterministic chaotic-interval and periodic-orbit properties; the accessible full-text sections inspected did not supply a classification of two-phase uniform stationary marginals, phase-dependent voltage covariances, or the paired-sum finite-window formula. The earlier 2020 article addresses eventual periodicity, not these exact statistical observables. Transfer methods and signed binary expansions are standard and are credited as such. The 2015 paper on periodic internal decay could not be inspected in full; related unindexed results or a general transfer theorem remain a substantive novelty risk. No absolute uniqueness beyond the checked evidence is claimed.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A natural complete endpoint classification answers when an alternating threshold neuron's one-time distributions completely hide its periodic forcing. The all-lag opposite-sign correlation formulas show explicitly how the forcing remains observable through time dependence, and the paired-sum exact variance and central limit give a benchmark for time-aggregated signals. These are linked structural statements about a single nonautonomous dynamical system, not an arbitrary isolated numerical parameter computation.

## Scientific limitations

The theorem requires uniform phase-marginals and gains in \([1,2]\). It does not establish a general central limit theorem for all gains, nor classify all invariant measures. Exceptional threshold itineraries are excluded as a null set. The inaccessible 2015 source remains a publication-priority comparison. Independent mathematical audit remains outstanding.
