# Uniform monogenic rigidity in the canonical strictly finite Heyting family

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge1\), let \(H_n=X_n^*\) be the Heyting algebra of clopen upsets of the Esakia space \(X_n\) from the canonical strict-finiteness construction. Put
\[
W=|L_n^i|=2^n+1.
\]
For \(U\in H_n\), write \(\langle U\rangle\) for the Heyting subalgebra generated by \(U\), with the constants \(0,1\) included. Then
\[
\boxed{\{\,|\langle U\rangle|:U\in H_n\,\}=\{2,3,5,9\}}.
\]
The multiplicities are exact:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
|\langle U\rangle|&\#\{U\in H_n:|\langle U\rangle|\text{ has this size}\}\\ \hline
2&2\\
5&2^W-2=2^{2^n+1}-2\\
9&W-1=2^n\\
3&\text{all remaining elements (infinitely many).}
\end{array}
\]
The size-9 generators are precisely
\[
L_n^0\setminus\{x_0^j\},\qquad 1\le j\le W-1=2^n.
\]
Consequently the sharp monogenic bound is the constant \(9\), independent of \(n\ge1\).

There is a sharp threshold at \(n=0\): the source identifies \(X_0^*\) with the Rieger--Nishimura lattice, the free Heyting algebra on one generator, so its one-generated subalgebra can be infinite.

## Source geometry
Definition 4.1 of the source gives
\[
X_n=\{x_i^l:l\le2^n,\ i<\omega\}\cup\{x_\infty\},
\]
with minimum \(x_\infty\), levels \(L_n^i=\{x_i^l:l\le2^n\}\), and
\[
x_i^l\le x_{i'}^{l'}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 i\ge i'+2\quad\text{or}\quad(i=i'+1\text{ and }l'\ne l+1),
\]
apart from equality. The topology is the one-point compactification topology. Thus every proper clopen upset is finite and omits \(x_\infty\). In the Esakia dual,
\[
A\to B=X_n\setminus\downarrow(A\setminus B).
\]

## Proof
Let \(L_0=L_n^0\), \(L_1=L_n^1\), and write \(q_j=x_0^j\), \(r_j=x_1^j\).

### 1. Every generator reaching level at least 2 is dense
If a finite upset \(U\) contains a point on level \(r\ge2\), then that point lies below every point of \(L_0\). Hence \(L_0\subseteq U\). Every point of \(X_n\) lies below some maximal point in \(L_0\), so \(\downarrow U=X_n\), i.e. \(\neg U=0\). Any nonzero proper dense element generates exactly \(\{0,U,1\}\). Therefore every such \(U\) generates a 3-element subalgebra.

Only upsets whose deepest occupied level is \(0\) or \(1\) can be exceptional.

### 2. Rank-1 upsets
For \(0\le l<W-1\), the point \(r_l\) lies below every maximal point except \(q_{l+1}\); the point \(r_{W-1}\) lies below all of \(L_0\). Hence a rank-1 upset that does not contain all of \(L_0\) must be one of
\[
E_j=(L_0\setminus\{q_j\})\cup\{r_{j-1}\},\qquad 1\le j<W.
\]
All rank-1 upsets containing \(L_0\) are dense and generate size 3. For the exceptional ones,
\[
\neg E_j=\{q_j\},\qquad \neg\{q_j\}=E_j,
\]
and \(E_j\vee\{q_j\}\) is dense. Thus \(E_j\) generates exactly five elements.

There are \(2^W-1\) rank-1 upsets containing all of \(L_0\), one for every nonempty subset of \(L_1\), and exactly \(W-1\) exceptional \(E_j\)'s. Hence the rank-1 contribution is
\[
(2^W-1)\text{ generators of size }3,
\qquad W-1\text{ generators of size }5.
\]

### 3. Rank-0 upsets
Every nonempty subset \(S\subseteq L_0\) is a rank-0 upset. If \(|S|\ge2\), no point of \(L_1\) avoids every member of \(S\), so
\[
\neg S=L_0\setminus S.
\]
For a singleton \(\{q_j\}\) with \(j\ge1\), there is one additional point that does not see it:
\[
\neg\{q_j\}=(L_0\setminus\{q_j\})\cup\{r_{j-1}\}=E_j,
\]
whereas \(\neg\{q_0\}=L_0\setminus\{q_0\}\).

These identities give all cases. The full \(L_0\) is dense and generates size 3. Every nonempty proper \(S\subseteq L_0\) generates size 5 except
\[
a_j=L_0\setminus\{q_j\},\qquad 1\le j<W,
\]
which generate size 9. Thus among the \(2^W-1\) nonempty rank-0 upsets there are
\[
1\text{ of size }3,
\quad 2^W-W-1\text{ of size }5,
\quad W-1\text{ of size }9.
\]

For completeness, the nine-element closure of \(a_j\) is uniform in \(W\) and \(j\). Put
\[
b=\neg a_j,\quad c=a_j\vee b,\quad d=\neg b,\quad e=b\vee d,
\quad f=d\to a_j,\quad g=d\vee f.
\]
Then
\[
\{0,1,a_j,b,c,d,e,f,g\}
\]
consists of nine distinct clopen upsets and is closed under \(\wedge,\vee,\to\). One can see the only apparent boundary distinction explicitly:
\[
f=L_0\cup(L_1\setminus\{r_{j-1}\})\cup K_j,
\]
where \(K_1=\varnothing\) and \(K_j=\{x_2^{j-2}\}\) for \(j\ge2\); and
\[
g=L_0\cup L_1\cup K_j.
\]
The resulting nine-element operation table is the same for all \(W\ge3\) and \(1\le j<W\).

### 4. Total multiplicities
Adding the two constants \(0,1\), each of which generates the two-element algebra, gives
\[
\#(2)=2,
\]
\[
\#(5)=(2^W-W-1)+(W-1)=2^W-2,
\]
\[
\#(9)=W-1=2^n.
\]
Every other element is a nonzero proper dense upset and generates size 3. This proves the theorem.

## Relation to the source
The source constructs \(X_n^*\), proves it infinite and \((n+1)\)-generated, and proves existence of a finite bound on its \(n\)-generated subalgebras. It does not state this exact one-generator spectrum. The result above shows that the entire canonical family has a much stronger first-arity rigidity: despite the rapidly increasing level width \(2^n+1\), the maximum singly generated subalgebra never exceeds 9.
