# Research review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof starts from the exact level order in Definition 4.1 and the standard Esakia implication formula. Every upset reaching level at least 2 is proved dense uniformly, so the infinite portion of the algebra is handled without finite truncation. Rank 0 and rank 1 admit complete symbolic classifications for arbitrary width \(W=2^n+1\). The only nine-element cases have an explicit uniform nine-term closure. The replay script independently checks all low-rank upsets at \(W=3,5,9\) and all operations inside the maximal closures.

## Originality
PASS, with a meaningful residual caveat. The construction paper proves only existence of a finite bound for \(n\)-generated subalgebras and contains no occurrence of “monogenic” or “one-generated.” Exact and semantic searches found no all-\(n\) spectrum or multiplicity formula. A previously derived \(n=2\) specialization gives the spectrum \(\{2,3,5,9\}\) with multiplicities \(2,30,4\), but explicitly did not determine the all-\(n\) spectra. The present result supplies the missing uniform theorem, exact multiplicities for every \(n\ge1\), and the sharp \(n=0\) threshold. Because the proof is short once the pattern is visible, unindexed folklore remains the principal originality risk.

## Value
PASS. The source's central problem is quantitative control of finitely generated subalgebras. This result shows an unexpectedly strong uniform phenomenon one arity below the source's main threshold: while the level width grows doubly exponentially in the displayed size-5 multiplicity, the largest monogenic subalgebra remains exactly 9. The exact multiplicity formulas and the contrast with the infinite one-generated \(n=0\) endpoint make the statement both structural and sharply testable.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is the canonical construction itself, especially Definition 4.1, Corollary 4.4, and Proposition 4.10 of DOI 10.1017/jsl.2024.48. Classical Rieger--Nishimura theory explains the \(n=0\) endpoint but does not identify this internal spectrum for \(X_n^*\). The exact two-generated spectrum is not addressed here.
