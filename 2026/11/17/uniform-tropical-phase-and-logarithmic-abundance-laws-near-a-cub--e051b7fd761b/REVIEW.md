## Correctness — PASS
For positive coefficients, the conserved weighted product and simplex constraint give one periodic oval per energy level. On each vertex passage, two logarithmic derivatives differ from their constant vertex rates by errors integrable in original physical time with an energy-independent bound. This yields a **uniform, not solely endpoint**, affine logarithmic orbit approximation with additive \(O(1)\) error before division by \(L=\log(1/h)\). Bounded travel time along three connecting boundary-edge arcs and \(T_h=PL+O(1)\) establish the global phase error \(O(1/L)\). Coupling on the same uniform phase gives the Wasserstein bound and explicit conditional edge measures; the projected coordinate laws follow from constant-speed parametrization. The numerical checker confirms normalization at two coefficient triples, but finite numerical data are not used as a proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected coverage
The directly relevant 2023 primary text of Benny–Jana–Sarkar classifies cubic sphere-flow phase portraits. A closer published quantitative result for this **same** flow, *Sharp long-period tails on cubic Kolmogorov sphere flows*, supplies the period law and the sharp exponential tail under **random initial spherical area**. The present single-orbit uniform logarithmic phase waveform and its explicitly weighted physical-time coordinate distributions do not follow as stated from its period or ensemble-tail conclusions; full new uniform-in-time passage error controls are proved here. A published logarithmic phase theorem in a planar cubic global center treats a different dynamical system, rather than a reduction of this three-coordinate sphere flow. Searches for tropical log-abundance, heteroclinic simplex and cyclic replicator occupancy equivalents returned no decisive covering result. This lack of search hits does not establish absolute novelty; generic heteroclinic scaling and unindexed equivalent work remain the chief priority risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A period asymptotic only specifies how long a nearly heteroclinic periodic trajectory takes. The new theorem answers where it spends that time in **logarithmic minority-abundance scales**: the complete oriented phase profile, each edge's duration fraction, and each coordinate's explicit point-mass-plus-uniform distribution. These are natural observables of cyclic dominance, distinguishing rare-species duration and intensity in a way neither the ordinary boundary orbit limit nor a scalar period can. The theorem provides quantitative Wasserstein control for every positive fixed triple without claiming a stronger but invalid total-variation statement.

## Boundaries and residual risks
The small-energy limit holds at fixed positive coefficients. The source model's qualitative classification, conserved product, and earlier sharp scalar period/tail theorem are not original assertions. The Wasserstein rate is not proved optimal. Generic singular-cycle literature could contain an equivalent log-profile theorem under another normalization; no publication priority or independent replication is certified. The present self-review is limited to the exact analytic proof and its cited inspection, plus a finite numerical corroboration.
