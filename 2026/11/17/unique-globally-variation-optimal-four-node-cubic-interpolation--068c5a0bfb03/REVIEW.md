# Scientific review of the variation-optimal cubic interpolation theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The exact domain is all strictly ordered four-node grids with endpoints fixed at -1 and 1, and arbitrary real four-vector samples with positive adjacent variation. The Lagrange basis and partition-of-unity property give a complete decomposition into three one-jump interpolants; consequently the global seminorm is exactly the maximum of their three total variations. All three are cubic polynomials with exactly two critical points positioned by Rolle's theorem, and the derivative-root discriminant identity yields the closed norm formulas. A strict symmetrization inequality and oppositely monotone scalar branches establish the unique global minimizer. The symbolic checker verifies those identities; rational interval arithmetic encloses its location. The finite tests do not stand in for the all-parameter proof. Residual mathematical limitation: endpoint positions are assumed fixed; no wider node-design optimum is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
An exact statement-level comparison was made against the full text of Rack–Vajda's 2015 cubic Lebesgue-constant theorem (which optimizes uniform-norm stability, interior parameter approximately 0.4177913013), the published four-node monotone-range defect theorem (parameter approximately 0.4458209225), Berrut–Trefethen's 2004 survey and formulae, and Nochetto–Salgado's 2012 averaged low-order TV-diminishing interpolation. None asserts or implies the exact arbitrary-grid variation norm and its strict global endpoint-fixed node minimizer. The monotone-range paper uses the same cubics and jump patterns, so formula-level proximity is a substantive residual priority risk; a complete derivative variation accounts for both extrema, whereas the range defect selects only the worst boundary excursion. Searches of relevant mathematical research records and published literature did not reveal an equivalent claim. This is evidence of noncoverage in checked sources, not a proof of first discovery.

## Value — PASS
This solves a natural finite-dimensional **optimization over every interior node pair**, rather than reporting an isolated numerical constant. It identifies the unique sampling configuration minimizing how badly cubic polynomial interpolation can amplify an arbitrary sequence's total variation. Unlike the classical Lebesgue-optimal spacing and the distinct monotone-range-optimal spacing, this design is specialized to bounded-variation stability. The exact all-grid formula and three simultaneous norming jumps provide a structural description and practical stability tradeoff. The four-node cubic case is a natural first nontrivial degree beyond linear variation-preserving interpolation, not an arbitrary selected table cell.
