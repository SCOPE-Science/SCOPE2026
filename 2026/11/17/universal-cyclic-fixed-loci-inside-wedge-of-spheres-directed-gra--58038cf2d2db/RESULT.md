# Universal cyclic fixed loci inside wedge-of-spheres directed-graph complexes

## Finding
For every finite abstract simplicial complex \(L\) on a nonempty vertex set, with every vertex contained in a face, there is a finite connected symmetric circulant digraph \(G_L\) such that its complex of non-strongly-connected spanning arc-subsets is a wedge of spheres, while the geometric fixed points of its full vertex rotation are homotopy equivalent to \(|L|\):

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{NSC}}(G_L)\simeq\bigvee^{b_L}S^{2N-4},\qquad
|\Delta_{\mathrm{NSC}}(G_L)|^{C_N}\simeq |L|.
\]

Here \(N=|V(G_L)|\) and \(b_L\geq1\) is the nonzero number supplied by the chromatic-polynomial formula of Hultman [1]. In particular, every finite simplicial homotopy type, including any prescribed finite complex with integral torsion, is realized as the full cyclic fixed-point space of a graph complex whose underlying space has torsion-free reduced integral homology concentrated in one degree.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(L\) have actual vertices \([r]=\{1,\ldots,r\}\), with \(r\ge1\), and let \(\mathcal{F}\) denote its set of nonempty inclusion-maximal faces. Choose \(r+1\) distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_r,q\), with \(q\) larger than each \(p_i\). Define
\[
N=q\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_i,\qquad d_F=\prod_{i\in F}p_i\ (F\in\mathcal F),\qquad
S=\{1,-1\}\cup\{d_F,-d_F:F\in\mathcal F\}\pmod N.
\]
Let \(G_L\) be the loopless directed Cayley graph on \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), with all arcs \((x,x+s)\) for \(s\in S\). These are distinct step residues: \(1<d_F<N/2\), the facet products are distinct, and \(d_F\) and \(N-d_E\) cannot coincide. Opposite arcs are both present. Let \(C_N\) act by translation on graph vertices and hence on the arc-subset complex. For any directed graph \(G\), define \(\Delta_{\mathrm{NSC}}(G)=\{A\subseteq E(G): (V(G),A)\text{ is not strongly connected}\}\); its empty face is included and its realization is taken in the ordinary simplicial sense.

## Proof
**1. Orbit-barycenter fixed-space triangulation.** The translation group acts freely and transitively on the arcs of each fixed step difference \(s\in S\); distinct differences yield distinct arc orbits \(O_s\). A point in the realization is invariant exactly when its barycentric coefficients are constant on each \(O_s\). Such a coefficient may be positive only if the entire orbit \(O_s\) occurs in a face. More generally the support is a union of orbits, and the orbit barycenters give a genuine PL triangulation of the fixed set by step subsets \(T\subseteq S\) for which \(\bigcup_{s\in T}O_s\) is not strongly connected. This argument works directly on the geometric realization; it does not confuse fixed vertices of the original complex with fixed points in interiors of simplices.

**2. Arithmetic connectivity criterion.** The union of step orbits \(O_s\) for \(s\in T\) is the directed Cayley graph of the finite cyclic group with generators \(T\). Its reachable set from zero is the subsemigroup generated by \(T\), which is exactly the subgroup they generate because each element has finite order. Consequently it is strongly connected precisely when \(\gcd(N,\{s:s\in T\})=1\). In particular, no step orbit of difference \(1\) or \(-1\) belongs to the fixed-space triangulation. The remaining fixed-space triangulation \(K\) has vertices \(\{\pm d_F:F\in\mathcal F\}\) and a subset \(T\) is a face if and only if a prime dividing \(N\) divides all steps of \(T\).

**3. Prime-support good cover.** The prime \(q\) divides no \(d_F\). For each \(i\in[r]\), let \(\Sigma_i\) be the full simplex on the step vertices \(\pm d_F\) with \(i\in F\). Squarefreeness of \(N\) gives \(K=\bigcup_{i=1}^r\Sigma_i\). Each \(\Sigma_i\) is nonempty because every vertex of \(L\) occurs in a facet. Any intersection \(\bigcap_{i\in I}\Sigma_i\) is either empty or a full simplex, and it is nonempty precisely when some facet \(F\) contains \(I\), equivalently precisely when \(I\in L\). Thus the nerve of the cover \(\{\Sigma_i\}\) is simplicially isomorphic to \(L\). By the finite simplicial nerve lemma, \(|K|\simeq |L|\).

**4. Ambient wedge-of-spheres theorem.** The presence of the steps \(\pm1\) makes \(G_L\) strongly connected and makes its underlying undirected graph connected. Because every directed arc has its reverse in \(G_L\), every directed cycle has two vertices that form a directed two-cycle in \(G_L\): the graph is 2-dense in the exact sense of Hultman [1, Section 4]. Applying [1, Theorem 4.3] gives \(\Delta_{\mathrm{NSC}}(G_L)\simeq\bigvee^{b_L}S^{2N-4}\). The corresponding undirected graph is connected, so its chromatic-polynomial linear coefficient is nonzero and \(b_L\ge1\). The preceding fixed-point identification completes the proof.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_universal_fixed_nsc.py` reconstructs the step sets for five finite models: a filled triangle, an edge disjoint from a point, the boundary of a triangle, the boundary of a tetrahedron and a four-cycle. It independently tests the cyclic Cayley strong-connectivity condition by direct breadth-first search on every step subset in the listed tractable cases, verifies the prime-simplex cover equality, verifies the cover nerve equals the original full face set, and computes reduced mod-two Betti numbers of the fixed model. All five models pass. The theorem itself rests on the preceding all-parameters argument, not on finite computation.

For an example with visible nontrivial homology, take \(L\) to be the boundary of a triangle. Choose \(p_1=2,p_2=3,p_3=5,q=7\). Then \(N=210\), \(\mathcal F=\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\}\) and \(S=\{\pm1,\pm6,\pm10,\pm15\}\). The fixed complex has six vertices and reduced first homology \(\mathbb Z\), while the ambient complex is a wedge of spheres in dimension \(416\).

## Relationship to prior work
Hultman [1] established the wedge-of-spheres homotopy type of the non-strongly-connected digraph complex for 2-dense strongly connected digraphs. Björner and Welker [2] handled complete digraphs. Ayzenberg, Beketov and Magai [3] placed such full digraph complexes in a framework of hemisphere-cover nerves. Those theorems supply nonequivariant foundations; none of their relevant inspected statements classifies full rotational fixed spaces of variable symmetric circulant digraphs or realizes arbitrary prescribed finite homotopy types in such fixed spaces. The new result is the combined explicit prime-facet Cayley construction and identification of its **actual geometric** cyclic fixed space with a simplicial nerve of prescribed type. The underlying wedge theorem, cyclic gcd criterion and simplicial nerve lemma are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The theorem provides a homotopy equivalence, not a PL identification with \(L\) itself; the fixed space is PL-homeomorphic to the explicitly defined arithmetic step complex \(K\). The construction can use an exponentially large number of graph vertices and is not asserted to minimize that size. It excludes the void abstract complex and intentionally does not prescribe the entire cyclic equivariant homotopy type of the ambient space. Independent verification and a definitive global priority determination have not been performed; alternative terminology in unindexed literature remains a risk.
