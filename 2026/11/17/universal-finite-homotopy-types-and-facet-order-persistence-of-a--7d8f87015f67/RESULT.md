# Universal finite homotopy types and facet-order persistence of avalanche complexes on rooted directed trees

## Finding

**Theorem (rooted-tree avalanche realization).** For every nonempty finite abstract simplicial complex \(K\), choose any ordering \(F_0,\ldots,F_{m-1}\) of its nonempty facets. There exist a finite simple directed graph \(G\), whose underlying undirected graph is a tree, with exactly one sink, and a binary initial chip configuration \(c_0\) such that, under deterministic parallel sandpile firing with the sink excluded from firing sets, the avalanche complex \(\mathcal A(G,c_0)\) simplicially **strong collapses** onto a subcomplex canonically isomorphic to \(K\).

More precisely, writing \(K_r=\langle F_0,\ldots,F_r\rangle\) and \(\mathcal A_r\) for the avalanche complex generated by firing sets in rounds \(0,\ldots,r\), the natural inclusions \(K_r\hookrightarrow\mathcal A_r\) are homotopy equivalences for each \(0\le r<m\), and they commute strictly with the filtration inclusions. Thus, over any coefficient ring, the persistence modules \(H_q(K_r)\) and \(H_q(\mathcal A_r)\) are naturally isomorphic in every degree \(q\). The dynamics stabilizes after round \(m-1\), and no non-sink vertex has outdegree other than one.

Consequently every nonempty finite simplicial homotopy type occurs as the avalanche homotopy type of a binary initial configuration on a directed in-tree. In particular, finite simplicial complexes with integral torsion occur. An explicit six-vertex triangulation of \(\mathbb{RP}^{2}\) below produces an avalanche complex with \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\).

## Assumptions and scope

A finite simple digraph has no loops or parallel arcs. At each round every nonsink vertex holding at least its outdegree in chips fires once, sending one chip along every outgoing arc; sinks absorb chips and do not appear in firing sets. Its avalanche complex is the simplicial complex generated by all nonempty firing sets. Every facet \(F_t\) is nonempty. No connectedness assumption is made on \(K\). Only the digraph \(G\), not the avalanche complex, is required to be connected.

Let \(V\) be the vertex set of \(K\). Introduce one vertex \(v\) of \(G\) for each \(v\in V\), and a unique sink \(s\), with arrows \(v\to s\). For each \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\) and each \(v\in F_j\), add exactly \(j\) new vertices \(p_{j,v,0},\ldots,p_{j,v,j-1}\), with the directed chain
\[
p_{j,v,0}\longrightarrow p_{j,v,1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow p_{j,v,j-1}\longrightarrow v.
\]
All the new chains are internally disjoint. Initially place one chip at each chain's first vertex and one chip at each target vertex \(v\in F_0\), and zero elsewhere. This is a \(\{0,1\}\)-valued configuration. The graph has precisely
\[
1+|V|+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j|F_j|
\]
vertices, including its sink, and exactly \(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|F_j|\) initial chips.

## Proof

**1. The graph is a rooted directed tree.** Every nonsink vertex has exactly one outgoing arc; every such arc eventually reaches \(s\). There are exactly \(|V(G)|-1\) arcs and the underlying graph is connected, so it is a tree. No edge is repeated because the chains are internally disjoint and terminate at individually specified target vertices.

**2. Exact firing times.** Every auxiliary chain initially has one chip at its first vertex, with no chips at later vertices. All its vertices have outdegree one, and each is fed only by its immediate predecessor. Hence the chip moves one edge toward its target per parallel round: \(p_{j,v,t}\) fires exactly at round \(t\), for \(0\le t<j\), and never again. At any round \(t\), each target vertex \(v\) receives at most one grain from these chains, namely from the unique chain indexed \((t,v)\) when \(v\in F_t\). The initial target grains trigger precisely \(F_0\) at round zero. Thus, by induction, target vertices fire at round \(t\) exactly when they belong to \(F_t\), and no chips remain after round \(m-1\). For every \(0\le t<m\), the full firing set is therefore
\[
\sigma_t=F_t\ \cup\ \{p_{j,v,t}:j>t,\ v\in F_j\},
\]
where the displayed \(F_t\) refers to its target vertices in \(G\). All firing sets after \(m-1\) are empty.

**3. The target-induced complex.** Intersecting each generator \(\sigma_t\) with the target vertex set gives exactly \(F_t\). Hence the full subcomplex of \(\mathcal A_r=\langle\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_r\rangle\) induced on the target vertices present up to round \(r\) is \(K_r=\langle F_0,\ldots,F_r\rangle\).

**4. Explicit stagewise homotopy equivalence.** For each \(t\), fix an anchor \(b_t\in F_t\). Define a simplicial map \(\rho\) from the final avalanche complex to \(K\) by fixing each target vertex and sending each auxiliary \(p_{j,v,t}\) to \(b_t\). This is well defined because the auxiliary vertex occurs in exactly one firing set, namely \(\sigma_t\). Its image on every generating simplex \(\sigma_t\) is exactly \(F_t\), so it restricts to \(\rho_r:\mathcal A_r\to K_r\) for every \(r\). The composite \(\rho_r\circ\iota_r\) is the identity on \(K_r\), where \(\iota_r\) is the target-subcomplex inclusion. For every simplex \(\tau\subseteq\sigma_t\), its union with \(\iota_r\rho_r(\tau)\) is contained in \(\sigma_t\). Thus \(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal A_r}\) and \(\iota_r\rho_r\) are contiguous simplicial maps, proving a homotopy equivalence at every stage.

In fact, each auxiliary vertex belongs to the unique maximal simplex \(\sigma_t\), which also contains its anchor \(b_t\). It is therefore dominated by \(b_t\); deleting all auxiliary vertices successively is a simplicial strong collapse onto \(K_r\). These choices are compatible with the filtration: the inclusions \(K_r\hookrightarrow\mathcal A_r\) commute exactly with the two sets of filtration inclusions. Taking homology over any coefficient ring yields isomorphic persistence modules.

**5. A concrete torsion witness.** Let \(K\) have vertices \(0,1,2,3,4,5\) and the ten triangular facets
\[
012,\ 013,\ 024,\ 035,\ 045,\ 125,\ 134,\ 145,\ 234,\ 235.
\]
Here each unordered edge occurs in exactly two triangles and every vertex link is a five-cycle. Thus \(K\) is a closed connected triangulated surface, with Euler characteristic \(6-15+10=1\), hence the projective plane. Alternatively, use the ten non-tree edges \((i,j)\) for \(1\le i<j\le5\) as an integral basis of one-cycles of its complete one-skeleton. The ten signed triangle boundaries form a \(10\times10\) integer matrix with Smith diagonal \((1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,2)\). Hence \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) and \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)=0\). The construction produces a directed tree with \(142\) vertices, including its single sink, and \(30\) binary initial chips; its avalanche complex has the same integral homology and homotopy type as \(K\).

## Verification

The standalone script `verify_universality.py` constructs every arc and initial chip, independently runs synchronous parallel firing, checks the exact formula for each firing set, verifies each auxiliary's unique appearance and the stagewise retractions, and computes the projective-plane Smith factors over \(\mathbb Z\). It passed five named cases (including the six-vertex projective plane) and all \(166\) nonempty antichains of nonempty subsets of four labels, as recorded in `replay_output.txt`. The unrestricted result is proved above; the finite computations are corroboration, not an extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work

Riihimäki and Smith introduced avalanche complexes and parallel-firing homology in [1]. Their Definition 2.3 and Section 2.2 give the precise model and its nerve interpretation, while their Section 5, Question 5.1 asks whether every wedge of spheres and torsion-bearing homology can arise. Their path/cycle classifications (Section 3 and Table 1) do not supply a general realization construction. The present result gives a positive answer to the finite-simplicial homotopy-type version, already on directed trees with binary chips. Existing published special examples of torus-shaped avalanches and bidirected-star classifications do not logically imply this universality or stagewise realization theorem.

## Limitations

The result is existential and the constructed graph can be large: the auxiliary-vertex count depends on the selected ordering of the facets. It uses synchronous parallel firing, excludes the sink from firing sets, and requires a finite nonempty target complex. It does not claim to preserve the original graph structure, to minimize the number of chips or vertices, to realize arbitrary simultaneously batched filtration timestamps without refinement, or to realize degree-zero torsion (which no ordinary simplicial complex has). The proof does not establish any probabilistic robustness to altered chip configurations.
