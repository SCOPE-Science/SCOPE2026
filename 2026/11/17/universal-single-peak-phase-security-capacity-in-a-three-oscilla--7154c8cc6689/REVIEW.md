# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The zero-frequency third node enforces \(b\sin\phi=\sin\psi\); under the principal edge caps all three phase differences have the sign of the injected power, so the entire secure class is parametrized by one difference \(\delta\). The principal phase formula and its strictly increasing power map are derived directly. The first active security cap changes exactly at \(b_*=\sin\eta/\sin(\gamma-\eta)\), establishing both analytic capacity branches. The derivative signs are strict for all \(0<\eta<\gamma<\pi/2\). Weighted-Laplacian positivity proves exponential attraction modulo rotation. Endpoint limits and monotonicity give the complete finite coupling window and exact interval-uncertainty criterion. A separate principal-branch argument shows stable phase locking persists at sufficiently strong coupling after security has failed. The accompanying checker supplies finite tests, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected literature

The full texts of Bačić et al. arXiv:2611.06466v1 and arXiv:2610.24595v1 were compared at their relevant statements and examples. The former proves uniform **sufficient** conditions using edge-flow error bounds, not a necessary-and-sufficient threshold for this explicit three-bus family. The latter's general convex minimizer equivalence is stronger as a structural characterization and is acknowledged; its later a-posteriori necessary and sufficient **bounds** do not themselves give the sharp closed parameter dependence, single peak, two-sided coupling window, or the all-interval endpoint rule. The old general theorem could of course be used to characterize individual parameter points numerically, but does not state or imply these exact parameter conclusions without additional nontrivial analysis. Witthaut–Timme (2012) prove/show stronger-edge Braess effects on the existence of ordinary phase locking in larger systems; that is not the same as preserving a hyperbolic lock while violating a smaller security cap. The closest mathematical record for three-clock networks establishes Braess-free *unconstrained* locking under path completion, compatible with this different constrained claim. Priorities for the general model and general theory are explicitly retained.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A universal, sharp all-angle-pairs phase diagram answers a motivated robustness question about nonuniform edge safety limits. A single increase in coupling simultaneously improves a first link's transfer and worsens a different bottleneck, yielding a mathematically rigorous interval of acceptable equipment strength and an exact uncertainty worst-case rule. The conclusion is structural for every unequal cap pair, not a hand-selected numeric example. Distinguishing operational phase-security loss from destruction of a locally attracting lock is a useful boundary counterexample to monotone-coupling intuition.

## Closest literature and limits

The general robust-existence result in arXiv:2611.06466v1 and the convex criterion in arXiv:2610.24595v1 are the strongest relevant predecessors. A full 2012 open-access study of geometric-frustration and Braess locking was compared. No covering formula or stronger published implication for the **two edge limits and varying weight** was identified in inspected texts or mathematical-record searches, but a negative search does not establish worldwide novelty. The result is confined to fixed positive weights, a three-cycle, zero winding and stationary equilibria; it does not solve switched-weight or higher-dimensional networks. Independent audit was not conducted.
