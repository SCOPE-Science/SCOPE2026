# Vertex-deletion homotopy of halved-cube clique complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(E_n\) be the even-cardinality subsets of \([n]\), let \(H_n\) be the graph on \(E_n\) joining distinct \(A,B\) when \(|A\mathbin\triangle B|=2\), and write \(K_n=\operatorname{Cl}(H_n)\) for its ordinary clique (flag) complex. Choose **any** vertex \(v\in E_n\) and let \(K_n^{-v}=\operatorname{Cl}(H_n-v)\), the induced vertex-deleted clique complex. Set
\[
 B_n=\sum_{i=4}^{n}(-1)^{i-4}2^{n-i}\binom{n}{i}.
\]
Then the **complete integral homotopy type** of the punctured complex is
\[
 \boxed{\quad |K_n^{-v}|\simeq\bigvee^{\,B_n-\binom{n-1}{3}} S^3\quad (n\ge4).}
\]
Here a wedge with zero summands means a point. The unpunctured complex has \(|K_n|\simeq\bigvee^{B_n}S^3\), and the natural inclusion induces a split (not naturally split) short exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\widetilde H_3(K_n^{-v};\mathbb Z)
 \longrightarrow\widetilde H_3(K_n;\mathbb Z)
 \longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{\binom{n-1}{3}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
In particular, no torsion appears, and deletion of one vertex kills exactly \(\binom{n-1}{3}\) independent integral three-dimensional classes. For \(n=4,5,6,7\) the punctured sphere counts are respectively \(0,5,39,189\), compared with \(1,9,49,209\) before deletion.

## Assumptions and scope
The metric is the inherited Hamming/symmetric-difference metric on the **even-parity** class, and the Rips threshold is **inclusive two**. This is the flag complex of the halved-cube graph, not the scale-two Rips complex on all \(2^n\) Boolean vertices. Deletion is on the graph's vertex set **before** taking the clique complex; equivalently it is induced simplicial deletion. Every \(n\ge4\) and every even-parity vertex is covered. Translating by any even-parity vector is a graph automorphism, so the proof may assume \(v=\varnothing\).

## Proof
**1. Existing integral homology input and low-dimensional topology.** The halved-cube clique complex is the \(k=4\) instance of the half-cube subcomplexes studied by R. M. Green [G, Proposition 2.2.6 and Theorems 3.3.2, 4.1.2]. That work proves that the reduced **integral** homology of \(K_n\) is free, vanishes outside degree three, and has rank \(B_n\). We use that established homology theorem, not claim its rank formula as new.

The complex \(K_n\) is simply connected. Identify the even subsets with the elementary abelian group generated by \(s_i=\{1,i\}\) for \(2\le i\le n\). Every other distance-two edge, toggling \(\{i,j\}\) with \(i,j>1\), is homotopic rel endpoints through a 2-simplex to a path consisting of the \(s_i\)-edge followed by the \(s_j\)-edge. The presentation of the elementary abelian group has only involution relations and commuting-pair relations. Involution loops are backtracks; each commutator square is the union of two triangles with diagonal toggling \(\{i,j\}\). Thus all edge loops are nullhomotopic in \(K_n\).

**2. Vertex link.** After translating the deleted vertex to \(\varnothing\), its neighbors are the two-element subsets of \([n]\). Two of these neighbors are adjacent exactly when they intersect in one element. Hence
\[
 L_n:=\operatorname{lk}_{K_n}(\varnothing)
 =\operatorname{Cl}(J(n,2)).
\]
The clique complex of the line graph of \(K_n\) consists of the \(n\) star simplices \(A_i\) on pairs containing coordinate \(i\), together with the extra triangles \(\tau_{ijk}=\{\{i,j\},\{i,k\},\{j,k\}\}\). Two star simplices intersect at the single vertex \(\{i,j\}\); triple star intersections are empty. Their union is therefore homotopy equivalent, by the nerve lemma, to the complete graph on \([n]\). Under this equivalence each extra triangle attaches along the corresponding triangular cycle \(i,j,k\). Attaching all such triangles gives the two-skeleton of the \((n-1)\)-simplex. Consequently
\[
 L_n\simeq (\Delta^{n-1})^{(2)}\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{3}}S^2.
\]
The last equivalence is the standard homotopy type of the two-skeleton of a simplex. A convenient integral basis for \(\widetilde H_2(L_n)\) comprises the octahedral two-sphere cycles supported on the six two-subsets of each four-element set containing a chosen reference coordinate. They correspond to the boundaries of the reference-coordinate tetrahedra in \((\Delta^{n-1})^{(2)}\).

**3. Every link generator bounds after deletion.** For a four-element coordinate set \(T\subseteq[n]\), let \(Z_T\subset L_n\) be the induced octahedral boundary on its six two-subsets. The four-element vertex \(T\) is itself an even subset different from \(\varnothing\), and it is adjacent in \(H_n\) to **each** of the six two-subsets of \(T\), since their symmetric difference is a two-subset. Thus \(T*Z_T\) is a simplicial cone contained in \(K_n^{-\varnothing}\). Every integral generator of \(\widetilde H_2(L_n)\) therefore bounds in \(K_n^{-\varnothing}\):
\[
 \widetilde H_2(L_n;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow
 \widetilde H_2(K_n^{-\varnothing};\mathbb Z)
 \quad\text{is zero}.
\]

**4. Exact integral homology and the inclusion.** Decompose \(K_n=K_n^{-\varnothing}\cup(\varnothing*L_n)\) with intersection \(L_n\). Since the cone is contractible, the Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence, Green's full-complex homology result, and the zero map just established imply \(\widetilde H_j(K_n^{-\varnothing};\mathbb Z)=0\) for all \(j\ne3\), and the short exact sequence
\[
 0\to\widetilde H_3(K_n^{-\varnothing};\mathbb Z)
 \to\mathbb Z^{B_n}\to\mathbb Z^{\binom{n-1}{3}}\to0.
\]
The terminal group is free, so the sequence splits abstractly and the middle injection has primitive image. The rank of the kernel is precisely \(B_n-\binom{n-1}{3}\).

**5. From homology to homotopy.** The punctured graph is connected: any nonempty even subset can be reduced by two-at-a-time coordinate deletions to a two-subset, without passing through the empty set, and the two-subsets form the connected Johnson graph \(J(n,2)\). Its link \(L_n\) is simply connected for \(n\ge4\). The cone-gluing van Kampen theorem therefore identifies \(\pi_1(K_n^{-\varnothing})\) with \(\pi_1(K_n)=0\). Since its second integral homology vanishes, Hurewicz implies \(\pi_2(K_n^{-\varnothing})=0\).

Finally the punctured clique complex simplicially collapses onto a complex of dimension at most three. Indeed Green's clique classification shows that all simplices with at least five vertices, and every four-vertex simplex of the *odd-center type*, lie in a unique odd-centered simplex \(\Delta(N(o)\setminus\{\varnothing\})\), where \(N(o)\) is the even-parity distance-one neighbor set of odd \(o\). The other four-vertex simplices come from three-coordinate subcubes and already have dimension three. In every odd-centered simplex choose a surviving apex. In descending face-size order, pair every face of size at least four not containing the apex with the face obtained by adding it. These are elementary free-face collapses: their high-dimensional faces are not shared across odd centers or with the subcube tetrahedra. What remains has dimension at most three. The same argument works before vertex deletion.

A simply connected three-dimensional CW complex with \(H_2=0\) and free \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z^r\) is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of \(r\) three-spheres: Hurewicz realizes a homology basis by maps of three-spheres, and the simply connected homology Whitehead theorem makes their wedge map a homotopy equivalence. Apply this first to \(K_n\) and then to \(K_n^{-\varnothing}\), concluding the two stated integral homotopy types and the split-inclusion assertion.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_halfcube.py` builds each complex in two independent ways for \(n=4,5,6,7\): direct adjacency clique enumeration and the odd-center/three-coordinate-subcube clique classification. It verifies equality of all enumerated faces, computes exact simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\), checks the predicted reduced Betti numbers, and checks for every four-coordinate set that the eight-triangle octahedral sphere is coned by its four-subset vertex in the deleted complex. The matching output is in `verification_output.txt`; the final line is `PUNCTURED_HALFCUBE_FINITE_CHECKS_PASS`.

These finite mod-two tests are only corroborating computations. The proof of the unrestricted **integral** result is the argument above: Green's integral theorem, link generators, explicit integral cone fillings, Mayer–Vietoris, fundamental-group control, and dimension-three collapse. The code does not attempt to certify Green's independent theorem or all integers experimentally.

## Relationship to prior work
R. M. Green [G] already classifies the **unpunctured** halved-cube clique complex's integral homology and cliques and supplies the sphere-rank formula \(B_n\). Green's sequel [G2] studies full-complex group representations. The newer hypercube Rips work of Galetto, Montaño, and Wellner [GMW], first public on June 18, 2026, concerns the full Boolean cube's Rips homology under symmetry, not the induced removal of a point from a parity component. None of these full-space statements, by itself, specifies the link-to-deletion map: the essential new step is showing that every octahedral link generator bounds via a four-coordinate vertex **away from the deleted point**. This determines both the punctured integral homotopy type and the rank and primitiveness of the inclusion.

## Limitations
Only deletion of **one** parity-class vertex at inclusive Hamming scale two is classified. No claim is made about multiple deleted vertices, higher Rips scales, equivariant homotopy representations of the stabilizer, or the full Boolean-cube Rips complex after deletion. The result relies on Green's established unpunctured integral homology theorem. A comprehensive uniqueness claim is not made; additional unindexed work may exist. Independent mathematical audit is not included.
