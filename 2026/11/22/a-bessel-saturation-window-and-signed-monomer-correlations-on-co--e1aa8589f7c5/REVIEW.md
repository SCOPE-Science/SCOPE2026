# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For each deficit \(j\), choose unmatched subsets on both shores and count the perfect matchings between the remaining sites; this reconstructs the exact finite distribution. The tilted Bessel series is bounded in total variation by the missing mass of the falling-factorial factor, giving the stated constant. Classical Laguerre orthogonality gives simple positive zeros and thus an exact Poisson–binomial representation; Cauchy–Schwarz proves strict negative same-shore covariance, while conditioning on the shared deficit proves strict positive cross-shore covariance. Dominated factorial moments yield the three limiting coefficients; the strict sign of \(f_2-\mu^2\) follows from a decreasing-function covariance. Activity-ratio comparisons prove both off-window regimes. A separately implemented finite checker passes 728 assertions. The analytical argument, not the checker, covers all sizes.

The possibility of an unindexed, equivalent, or more general monomer-correlation theorem is a residual risk; this is not a priority certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Nearly perfect matchings form a natural saturation regime in an equilibrium dimer gas. At the \(n\)-scale of the dimer activity, the order-one monomer defects have a non-Poisson Bessel distribution; the explicit uniform finite-size error quantifies that boundary layer. The same finite matching measure also displays rigorously opposite spatial correlation signs, with all three rescaled coefficients computed. These are motivated finite-volume and large-system observables, rather than arbitrary evaluations of a partition polynomial or a recomputation of a known table.
