## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is for all integers \(n\ge3\), \(N\ge1\), with separate treatment of the zero-Poisson-parameter case. Its exact identity \(2N-D=2T\) is deterministic, because each cycle occupancy is included in two edge endpoints. The maximal-variation count follows by partitioning on the occupied support, counting independent vertex sets with a circular-gap argument and onto allocations with Stirling numbers. For the Poisson bound, the adjacent sampled-pair proxy has marginal probability \(2/n\); its local-dependence neighborhoods contain exactly \(2N-3\) pair indices, and all distinct overlaps have probability \(4/n^2\). The cited Arratia–Goldstein–Gordon Theorem 1 is used with its total-variation norm correctly converted by a factor of two into \(\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|\). Triple-occupancy coupling supplies the additional error. The \(o(n^{2/3})\), central-limit and full sharp-threshold consequences then follow with precise quantifiers; the latter uses monotonicity and an intermediate sample size of \(\lfloor n^{7/12}\rfloor\). ## Originality — PASS against accessible evidence; priority risks retained

The motivating full text, arXiv:2611.14735v1, proves moment and large-sample fixed-bin formulas; it does not derive a distribution for \(N-\tfrac12D\), a Poisson approximation in the regime \(N\asymp\sqrt n\), or the independent-support exact saturation count. Part 1, arXiv:2610.13366v1, gives an exact mean and refers to a separate sparse fixed-sample study; its accessible account does not resolve whether the jointly growing deficit law appears elsewhere. Arratia–Goldstein–Gordon's full text gives a general birthday-event Poisson theorem, an essential antecedent fully acknowledged rather than presented as a new lemma. Applying that theorem to the *adjacent pair proxy* is routine; the unquoted statement for the cyclic roughness deficit additionally requires the triple-collision coupling and occupied-support characterization, neither explicitly supplied by the compared sources. A published variance result for multinomial histogram roughness in the \(N/n\to\lambda>0\) regime is only available here as a synopsis; it does not imply the claimed \(N\asymp\sqrt n\) distributional law on its stated face. ## Value — PASS

For order-sensitive multinomial histogram statistics, the event that cyclic discrete total variation attains its maximum is a natural extreme calibration event, and its loss is an observable integer-valued statistic. The explicit finite-size probability can be used without simulation, while the theorem exhibits a nondegenerate birthday-scale Poisson limit with a quantified finite-law error, the limiting no-loss probability, and a Gaussian fluctuation crossover for larger but still sparse samples. This complements known mean and fixed-bin variance formulas, which do not determine extreme-event frequencies. The result is a motivated, complete natural statistic-specific classification of the first saturation transition, not merely a small numerical example or an arbitrary parameter slice. Its reliance on classical local-dependence Poisson approximation limits its methodological novelty, and this limitation is material to the claimed value.
