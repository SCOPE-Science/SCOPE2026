The loss from maximal cyclic total variation in a uniform multinomial histogram has a quantitative Poisson law when the sample size is below the \(n^{2/3}\) scale, with a sharp first-collision transition when the sample size is of order \(\sqrt n\). The probability of attaining maximum variation at every finite size is an explicit Stirling-number sum over independent vertex sets of the cycle; at growing intermediate scales the deficit crosses to Gaussian fluctuations.
