# A Boolean–partition congruence lattice for prime-square unipotent Alexander quandles

## Finding
Let \(p\) be any prime. On \(Q_p=\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z\) define
\[
x\triangleright y=(1+p)x-py\pmod{p^2},\qquad
x\triangleleft y=(1-p)x+py\pmod{p^2}.
\]
Both binary operations are part of the quandle structure. Let \(\operatorname{Con}(Q_p)\) be the lattice of equivalence relations compatible with both operations, ordered by inclusion. Let \(\mathcal B_p\) be the Boolean lattice of subsets of \(\mathbb F_p\), and \(\Pi_p\) the lattice of set partitions of \(\mathbb F_p\), with equality at its bottom and the one-block partition at its top. Then
\[
\operatorname{Con}(Q_p)\cong \mathcal B_p\oplus\Pi_p,
\]
where the ordinal sum \(\oplus\) here **identifies the top of** \(\mathcal B_p\) **with the bottom of** \(\Pi_p\) (and makes no duplicate element). In particular
\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Con}(Q_p)|=2^p+B_p-1},
\]
where \(B_p=|\Pi_p|\) is the \(p\)-th Bell number. Exactly three of these congruences are induced by additive subgroups of \(\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z\); hence \(2^p+B_p-4\) are not subgroup-induced. For \(p=2,3,5,7\), the congruence counts are respectively \(5,12,83,1004\).

## Assumptions and scope
The result holds for every prime \(p\ge2\), without any unproved asymptotic or computational cutoff. Two operations are used, so this is a quandle congruence result, not merely a classification of equivalences preserving one operation. A congruence induced by an additive subgroup \(H\le\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z\) means the equivalence \(x\equiv y\pmod H\). All subgroups are automatically invariant under the Alexander multiplier \(1+p\).

Write each residue uniquely as \(x=r+ps\), where \(r,s\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\) and arithmetic in the second coordinate is modulo \(p\). This makes \(Q_p\) the rank-one unipotent Alexander quandle on \(\mathbb F_p^2\) with
\[
(r,s)\triangleright(c,t)=(r,s+r-c),\qquad
(r,s)\triangleleft(c,t)=(r,s-r+c).
\]
The orbits of the inner action are the \(p\) fibers \(R_r=\{(r,s):s\in\mathbb F_p\}\). Put \(\rho=\{((r,s),(c,t)):r=c\}\), the equivalence that collapses each entire fiber and does not mix fibers.

## Proof
**Step 1 — fiber-preserving congruences.** Suppose \(\theta\in\operatorname{Con}(Q_p)\) and \(\theta\subseteq\rho\). Fix a fiber \(R_r\). Right multiplication by any \((c,t)\) translates the second coordinate by \(r-c\). Since \(c\) runs over \(\mathbb F_p\), congruence compatibility implies the restriction of \(\theta\) to \(R_r\) is invariant under every translation of the additive group \((\mathbb F_p,+)\). Any translation-invariant equivalence on a group is congruence modulo a subgroup, by considering the equivalence class of zero. Since \(\mathbb F_p\) has no proper nonzero additive subgroup, the restriction is either equality or the universal relation on \(R_r\). For any chosen subset \(S\subseteq\mathbb F_p\), collapse exactly the fibers with \(r\in S\) and keep the remaining fibers pointwise separate. The displayed operation and its inverse preserve this equivalence: output fiber always equals the first input fiber, and a change within the second input fiber does not change the result at all. Thus the full interval below \(\rho\) is precisely \(\mathcal B_p\).

**Step 2 — every cross-fiber identification forces the whole fiber kernel.** Suppose instead that \(\theta\not\subseteq\rho\). There exist \((r,s)\mathrel\theta(c,t)\) with \(r\ne c\). For every \(u,v\in\mathbb F_p\), compatibility in the second input gives
\[
(u,v+u-r)=(u,v)\triangleright(r,s)
\mathrel\theta
(u,v)\triangleright(c,t)=(u,v+u-c).
\]
The two elements of fiber \(R_u\) differ by the nonzero translation \(r-c\). Compatibility with right translations by all operands makes the restriction on this fiber translation invariant. The subgroup generated by \(r-c\) is all of \(\mathbb F_p\), so \(R_u\) is collapsed. As \(u\) was arbitrary, \(\rho\subseteq\theta\).

**Step 3 — classification above the fiber kernel.** If \(\rho\subseteq\theta\), the congruence is completely determined by which fibers are identified. The quotient \(Q_p/\rho\) has operation \(r\triangleright c=r\), and similarly \(r\triangleleft c=r\). On this projection quandle every set partition is a congruence. Hence the full interval above \(\rho\) is precisely \(\Pi_p\). Steps 1–3 are exhaustive and establish the identified ordinal sum and the cardinality formula.

**Step 4 — subgroup congruences.** The additive cyclic group of prime-square order has exactly three subgroups, \(\{0\}\), \(p\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z\), and the whole group. They yield equality, \(\rho\), and the universal relation, respectively. The remaining \(2^p+B_p-4\) congruences are therefore genuinely non-module congruences. \(\square\)

## Verification
A standalone `verify.py` implements both operations from the definition, generates all set partitions for \(p=2\) (four elements) and \(p=3\) (nine elements), and tests preservation in both argument positions for both operations. It yields respectively \(5\) and \(12\) congruences. A separate constructive check validates all \(32\) fiber-selection congruences for \(p=5\). The finite calculations test the algebraic statement but do not substitute for the general proof above. Run with Python 3 using `python verify.py`; no third-party packages are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Burrows and Tuffley (2023, arXiv:2308.11852, first public version August 23, 2023) establish the correct two-operation congruence convention, treat connected Alexander quandles, and exhibit non-module congruences in nonconnected cases. Their result motivates a full finite nonconnected classification, but the examined congruence section does not provide this Boolean–partition lattice or its Bell-number enumeration. Burrows's 2022 thesis gives broader background and non-module examples; the inspected relevant chapter concerns different infinite Alexander examples. Nelson's classification addresses isomorphism classes of Alexander quandles, not the congruence lattice of a fixed one. Jedlička, Pilitowska and Zamojska-Dzienio (2015), Theorem 3.2, already classify the congruences *below* the orbit relation for general medial quandles using compatible submodules, while Proposition 3.1 identifies the canonical orbit quotient. Those structural facts explain two intervals of this lattice; the new step proved here is that every cross-fiber identification automatically contains the entire orbit relation, so there are no intervening congruences. An available published classification of embedded subquandles of the same rank-one unipotent family counts subalgebras, not congruence relations: its result does not directly imply the present partition classification.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns specifically the cyclic prime-square unipotent multiplier \(1+p\), not arbitrary prime-power Alexander quandles or general finite medial quandles. A broader preexisting universal-algebra theorem may imply this special case; historical priority is not established exhaustively. The 2015 medial-quandle classification was inspected in full at its relevant Section 3, including Theorem 3.2; it explains the lower interval but not the special cross-fiber rigidity proved here. The indexed same-object subquandle study was not accessible to complete full-text inspection. An independent audit has not been performed.
