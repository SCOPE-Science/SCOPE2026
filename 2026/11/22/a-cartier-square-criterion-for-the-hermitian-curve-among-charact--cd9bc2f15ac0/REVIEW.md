# Mathematical review: Cartier-square characterization in a cyclic-quintic family

**Correctness (PASS).** The projective quintic is smooth exactly under the stated squarefreeness assumption. Adjunction provides six explicit regular differentials. The characteristic-two mixed-partial Cartier formula produces rank three for every smooth member; the second power has image generated by the two scalar coefficients \(a^{1/4}\) and \(b^{1/4}\), and the third power vanishes. No finite enumeration is used to establish generality. The square-zero condition forces an additive quartic, which transforms to the Hermitian equation. The reverse branch-set implication follows from the uniqueness of the tame equal-exponent Kummer cover on \(\mathbf P^1\). The executable independent mixed-partial computation and an exact \(\mathbb F_{16}\)-point count support the proof.

**Originality (PASS, with priority risk).** Bouw's published cyclic-cover Hasse--Witt formulas provide a dominating *general computational method*, not a worked classification of this five-branch-point family or its Hermitian-characterization locus. Pries--Weir already establish the Hermitian curve's geometric \(2\)-torsion type and thereby its special-member Cartier invariants; those results are acknowledged and excluded. The claim concerns the iff-converse within the entire two-dimensional geometric branch-moduli family and the exact rank on its complement. Searches comparing equal branch-exponent covers, cyclic-quintic aliases, linearized quartics, Hermitian Ekedahl--Oort theory and accessible indexed results found no statement implying the full family characterization. An unindexed specialization is a genuine unresolved historical risk, not a proof of novelty from silence.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The branch-divisor moduli of five equally ramified points is natural. A constant \(2\)-rank and \(a\)-number fail to distinguish its members, but the next Cartier iterate singles out precisely the maximally symmetric five-point configuration and its Hermitian curve. This is a complete two-stratum classification with a structural geometric boundary and a known arithmetic extremal representative, not an arbitrary fixed-coefficient calculation. It does not purport to solve all Newton polygons or the full Ekedahl--Oort stratification.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Bouw, *The p-Rank of Ramified Covers of Curves*, Section 5, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1017513122376; Pries--Weir, *The Ekedahl--Oort Type of Jacobians of Hermitian Curves*, arXiv:1302.6261. The precise limit is tame degree-five covers with five equal local exponents in characteristic two. Only geometric isomorphism and geometric \(2\)-rank are claimed. Prior generic cyclic-cover algorithms may make the formula computationally accessible, and earlier unindexed calculations could reduce the priority claim.
