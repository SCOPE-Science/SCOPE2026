# Review of the chromatic-cycle theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The forward law is physically positive on its stated open domain. Reciprocal depth makes the epoch-to-band and band-to-epoch propagation exactly affine; every edge has a nonzero slope. For a connected component, one spanning-tree root parameter generates every candidate solution. Each cycle imposes an affine return map whose slope is the product of the observed reciprocal-depth ratios. Since an admissible solution exists, a nonunit slope fixes that root uniquely; if all slopes are one, the maps are identities, and strict inequalities preserve a whole local interval of distinct admissible parameters. The derivative of the holonomy at a constant-coverage point is explicitly nonzero on any mixed-contrast cycle, establishing an algebraic exceptional set. The graph edge/vertex count and a bichromatic four-cycle with pendant edges prove the sharp measurement bound. A complete rational four-observation example and exact arithmetic supplementary checks support, but do not replace, this proof. Scope excludes noise, unknown contrasts, and boundary parameters.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, residual priority risk
The closest astrophysical source, Chakraborty et al. (arXiv:2311.16864), provides the stellar contamination forward model and studies realistic spot retrievals, but does not identify an arbitrary sparse epoch–band graph by a chromatic holonomy criterion. Rosich et al. (A&A 641 A82, 2020) recover spot maps from independent multiband photometric time series and compare the homogeneous spot formula with limb-darkened models; they do not give the precise transit-depth-only sparse-graph identification theorem. Rathcke et al. (arXiv:2412.16541) use a reference planet for contamination correction rather than known contrasts and a single planet's sparse multi-epoch depths. Ling and Strohmer (arXiv:1611.04196) develop general bilinear self-calibration and random-sensing guarantees but study three sensing architectures different from the constrained chromatic epoch–band graph; the full accessible article was compared at its setup, theorem and proof sections. Targeted scientific and mathematical searches and accessible research summaries did not reveal a dominating theorem. Known bilinear inverse techniques might still imply the exact result after an unrecognized specialization; this remains an explicit historical-priority risk rather than proof of uniqueness in the literature.

## Value — PASS
The observation graph is the real scheduling object when transit epochs are not observed in every wavelength band. The theorem gives an exact, physically interpretable decision rule for whether missing bands can be recovered without assuming a spotless reference epoch. It identifies the unique exceptional-data obstruction and supplies a sharp experimental measurement budget: one scalar depth measurement per unknown coverage or intrinsic band depth. This is more informative than simply counting parameters, since an equally sized acyclic or monochromatically cycled schedule cannot recover the unknowns. The result is a motivated analytical measurement-design boundary, not a claim of empirical superiority or robust noisy recovery.

## Literature and limitations
Sources inspected include Chakraborty et al. (2023), https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16864; Rosich et al. (2020), https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202037586; Rathcke et al. (2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16541; and Ling and Strohmer (2016/2017), https://arxiv.org/html/1611.04196v3. The astrophysical forward model and the generic idea of bilinear self-calibration are prior art. The new proposed classification is restricted to a homogeneous unocculted cool-spot model, exact known contrasts, constant intrinsic spectrum and exact noiseless depths; nearly unit holonomies are susceptible to severe noise amplification. Full historical coverage is not certified.
