# A coefficient-characteristic phase transition for cyclic Hasse path cohomology of nonorientable manifolds

## Finding
Let \(M\) be a connected, closed, nonorientable \(d\)-manifold, \(d\ge 2\), with a finite regular CW decomposition and face poset \(P\). Form the cyclic Hasse digraph \(G_P^{\mathrm{cyc}}\) by orienting incidence arrows from every cell to its codimension-one cofaces and adding every arrow from a top cell to a vertex. Let \(\mathbb K\) be any field, and let \(A_{\mathbb K}=PH^*(G_P^{\mathrm{cyc}};\mathbb K)\) denote the GLMY path-cohomology algebra.

**Theorem (coefficient-characteristic phase transition).** If \(\operatorname{char}\mathbb K\ne2\), then \(A_{\mathbb K}\) is finite-dimensional and its positive-degree ideal has square zero. More precisely, put \(V=H^*(M;\mathbb K)\) and \(b_j=\dim_{\mathbb K}H^j(M;\mathbb K)\), with \(b_j=0\) outside \(0\le j<d\). There is an isomorphism of graded unital algebras
\[
A_{\mathbb K}\cong\mathbb K\oplus W,\qquad W^2=0,
\]
where, as graded vector spaces, \(W^1\cong\mathbb K\) and for \(k\ge2\),
\[
W^k\cong (V\otimes_{\mathbb K} V)^{k-1}/\{1\otimes x-x\otimes1:x\in V^{k-1}\}.
\]
The braces denote a linear subspace, **not** an ideal of the ordinary tensor-product algebra. Thus
\[
\dim A_{\mathbb K}^0=\dim A_{\mathbb K}^1=1,\qquad
\dim A_{\mathbb K}^k=\sum_{i+j=k-1}b_i b_j-b_{k-1}\quad(k\ge2),
\]
and \(A_{\mathbb K}^k=0\) for \(k\ge2d\).

By contrast, if \(\operatorname{char}\mathbb K=2\), the **same digraph** has an infinite-dimensional path-cohomology algebra containing a nonnilpotent homogeneous element of degree \(d+1\). Thus positive-degree nilpotence and finite-dimensionality each change at characteristic two.

In particular, for the nonorientable surface \(N_h=\#_h\mathbb{RP}^2\), \(h\ge1\), the odd-characteristic Hilbert series is
\[
1+t+(h-1)t^2+(h-1)^2t^3,
\]
with all products of positive-degree elements zero. In characteristic two its Hilbert series is
\[
1+t+ht^2+\frac{h^2t^3}{1-t^3},
\]
and the degree-three matrix class has nonzero powers in all positive multiples of degree three.

## Assumptions and scope
All manifolds are connected, compact without boundary, and nonorientable, of dimension at least two. Every face poset comes from a finite **regular** CW structure; the cyclic Hasse diagram has all return arrows between top cells and zero-cells, not an arbitrary selection. \(PH^*\) is the path-cochain differential graded algebra cohomology, not ordinary simplicial cohomology of the directed graph. The theorem is about field coefficients. Integer-coefficient assertions are not made. A closed nonorientable manifold has \(H^d(M;\mathbb K)=0\) when \(\operatorname{char}\mathbb K\ne2\), while in characteristic two it has a fundamental class and satisfies field-valued Poincaré duality.

## Proof
Write \(\Omega=\Omega^\bullet(G_P)\), \(\bar\Omega=\Omega^\bullet(G_P^{\mathrm{cyc}})\), and \(R,L\) for the right and left differential ideals generated by the nonminimal and nonmaximal vertex idempotents, respectively, as defined in [1, Definition 5.2]. Let \(\rho\) be the sum of all return arrows, and \(I=(\rho)\) its differential two-sided ideal.

**Step 1 (the two acyclic ideals).** For a regular cellulation of a closed manifold, each proper interval of the face poset has reduced cohomology concentrated in its top degree: lower intervals arise from cell boundaries, upper intervals from links, and bounded intervals from incidence spheres. The interval hypotheses used in [1, Proposition 3.9] therefore apply to \(P\), its least-element extension and its greatest-element extension without any global orientability requirement. The latter two posets are contractible. The contracting homotopy established in [1, Lemmas 5.8–5.9] consequently proves \(H^*(R)=H^*(L)=0\). Moreover, [1, Proposition 3.9 and Corollary 4.6] identify \(H^*(\Omega)\cong V\). Importantly, these arguments use local links and contractible cones, **not** top-dimensional Poincaré duality.

**Step 2 (vanishing of paths with two returns in odd characteristic).** For any finite ranked poset of rank \(d\), [1, Lemma 5.6] proves an isomorphism of complexes
\[
\Omega/(R+L)\cong H^d(P;\mathbb K)[-d].
\]
In odd characteristic (and characteristic zero), \(H^d(P;\mathbb K)\cong H^d(M;\mathbb K)=0\), hence \(\Omega=R+L\) in every degree, as a chain-level equality. The path-algebra relations imply \(\rho R=L\rho=0\), and more generally \(\rho x\rho=0\) for every \(x\in\Omega\), by [1, Lemma 7.2]. Any path containing two return arrows has an ascending segment between two successive return arrows. That segment is in \(\Omega\), so \(I^2=0\) **as a differential graded ideal**, not merely on cohomology. These statements also give \(\bar\partial\rho=0\) and
\[
\bar\partial x=\partial x+\rho x-(-1)^{|x|}x\rho\qquad(x\in\Omega\text{ homogeneous}).
\]
The defining quadratic relations are homogeneous in return-arrow count. Thus \(\bar\Omega=\Omega\oplus I\) as graded vector spaces, with the restriction map \(\bar\Omega\to\Omega\) a morphism of differential graded algebras.

**Step 3 (identify the ideal's cohomology).** The one-return case of the explicit path-factorization argument in [1, Lemma 7.3, proof equations (7.26)–(7.28)] does **not** require an orientation class: it gives an isomorphism of complexes
\[
(\Omega/L\otimes\Omega/R)[-1]\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }I/I^2=I,
\quad (x+L)\otimes(y+R)\longmapsto(-1)^{|x|}x\rho y.
\]
Since both ideals \(L,R\) are acyclic, Künneth over a field identifies \(H^k(I)\cong(V\otimes V)^{k-1}\).

**Step 4 (connecting map and the square-zero algebra).** Consider the short exact sequence of cochain complexes \(0\to I\to\bar\Omega\to\Omega\to0\). For a homogeneous cocycle \(x\in\Omega\), the explicit differential in Step 2 identifies its connecting morphism with
\[
\delta([x])=[\rho x-(-1)^{|x|}x\rho]
\ \longleftrightarrow\ 1\otimes[x]-[x]\otimes1\in V\otimes V.
\]
For \(x=1\) this is zero. For each positive degree \(p\), it is injective: the two summands lie in the disjoint direct-sum components \(V^0\otimes V^p\) and \(V^p\otimes V^0\). The long exact sequence therefore gives \(H^0(\bar\Omega)=\mathbb K\), \(H^1(\bar\Omega)=\mathbb K\), and, for \(k\ge2\), exactly the asserted quotient of \(H^k(I)\) by \(\delta(H^{k-1}(\Omega))\). Every positive-degree cohomology class is represented in \(I\) (because positive-degree restriction to \(H^*(\Omega)\) is zero). Since \(I^2=0\), all its pairwise products vanish. This proves the graded algebra statement and dimension formula; maximal possible degree is \(2(d-1)+1=2d-1\).

**Step 5 (characteristic two).** Over a field of characteristic two, every closed manifold is orientable over that field, and \(H^*(M;\mathbb K)\) is a Poincaré duality algebra. Thus [1, Theorem 6.3 and Corollary 6.4, whose proof uses only field-valued Poincaré duality] apply verbatim. Nonorientability makes the first Stiefel–Whitney class nonzero in \(H^1(M;\mathbb F_2)\), which remains nonzero after extension to \(\mathbb K\). Therefore the interior cohomology \(H^{(0,d)}(M;\mathbb K)\) is nonzero. The matrix summand \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb K}(H^{(0,d)}(M;\mathbb K))\otimes t\mathbb K[t]\), with \(|t|=d+1\), contains \(e\otimes t\) for any nonzero homogeneous idempotent \(e\) projecting onto a one-dimensional degree-one summand. All powers \((e\otimes t)^n=e\otimes t^n\) are nonzero. Hence the algebra is infinite-dimensional.

**Step 6 (surface specialization).** For \(N_h\) in characteristic other than two, \(V^0\cong\mathbb K\), \(V^1\cong\mathbb K^{h-1}\), and all higher cohomology vanishes. Substitute the Betti numbers into the formula to obtain degrees \(0,1,2,3\) of dimensions \(1,1,h-1,(h-1)^2\). In characteristic two, \(H^0\cong H^2\cong\mathbb K\) and \(H^1\cong\mathbb K^h\). Applying [1, Corollary 6.4] gives the displayed rational Hilbert expression, with an \(h\)-by-\(h\) matrix algebra in degrees divisible by three.

## Verification
The bundled `check_projective_plane.py` enumerates a complete six-vertex triangulation of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\), checks all edge incidences and circular links, computes its full simplicial Betti vector by exact finite-field elimination for characteristics two, three and five, constructs all 31 vertices and 120 nondegenerate arrows of its cyclic Hasse diagram, and computes its first GLMY path-cohomology dimension from the full degree-two quadratic relation quotient. The verified output gives simplicial Betti vectors \((1,1,1)\) in characteristic two and \((1,0,0)\) in odd characteristic, with \(\dim PH^1=1\) in all three fields. It independently verifies the local coefficient behavior and low-degree prediction only. **The general algebra and arbitrary-degree claims are proved in Steps 1–6, not inferred from finite experimentation.** Reproduce with `python3 check_projective_plane.py` using only the standard library.

## Relationship to prior work
Ivanov and Zhang [1] define cyclic Hasse diagrams and prove an all-degree path-cohomology algebra classification under an **orientation/Poincaré duality** hypothesis, in particular for closed oriented manifolds (Theorem 6.3 and Corollary 6.4). Their paper does not state a nonorientable, characteristic-dependent classification. Its internal Lemmas 5.6, 5.9, 7.2 and 7.3 supply machinery that remains available when the top cohomology vanishes; the present proof uses this machinery to establish a different square-zero regime and combines it with characteristic-two field orientability. Original GLMY theory [2] supplies the foundational cochain algebra, but not this newly defined cyclic Hasse face-poset construction. This comparison distinguishes the present conclusion from simply substituting a nonorientable manifold into a theorem whose stated hypotheses do not apply in odd characteristic.

## Limitations
No claim is made for nonregular CW structures, manifolds with boundary, disconnected spaces, arbitrary return-arrow subsets, integral coefficients, or twisted-coefficient theories. Characteristic-two matrix nonnilpotence relies on the nonzero interior mod-two class; the result makes no assertion about a universal ring structure for all digraphs. Novelty assessment is subject to unindexed or unpublished equivalent work, and independent mathematical audit remains to be performed.
