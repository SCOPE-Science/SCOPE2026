# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The quartic Lucas-balancing sequence has an exact Binet expression in the unramified extension \(\mathbb Z_5[\sqrt2]\). Its three residue-class functions have distinct first-order slopes \((0,2,3)\) modulo five, forcing periods beyond modulus five to preserve residue classes modulo three. A unit-derivative isometry in the middle branch proves the exact period \(3\cdot5^{a-1}\). A five-adic geometric-series calculation gives a congruence \(3\cdot5^s\pmod{5^{s+1}}\) for every block of length \(3\cdot5^s\). Sliding-window differences and this mean congruence together prove the formula at every length and prime-power exponent. Independently executable finite calculations agree; the infinite proof does not rely on finite enumeration.

## Originality

**PASS within inspected evidence.** The 2023 paper of Mbirika, Schrader and Spilker addresses squared windows and motivates related GCD questions, while Rayaguru and Panda (2019) supply quartic summation identities without the evaluated five-adic GCD/least-period law. Related published findings concern prime seventeen for arbitrary balancing-number powers, and primes two and three for fourth powers of Pell companions. Different sequences/primes and the square-window specialization do not logically cover the stated five-adic quartic classification. A possible implicit equivalent in broader summation or Lucas-recurrence theory remains a material priority risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Exact all-length prime-power divisor spectra of an infinite translated-window GCD are natural arithmetic invariants, motivated by an established family of open sliding-window questions. The theorem resolves the entire five-primary component for quartic Lucas-balancing windows, identifies the least periods at all prime powers, and exhibits a sharp nontrivial dichotomy between lengths divisible by three and other multiples of five. It does not claim the full GCD or a general result for all powers.
