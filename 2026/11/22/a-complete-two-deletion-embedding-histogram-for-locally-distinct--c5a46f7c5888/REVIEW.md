# Scientific review: two-deletion embedding histograms for locally distinct words

## Correctness (C): PASS
The claim defines indexed subsequence embeddings over every finite labeled alphabet of size \(q\ge3\) and every word length \(m\ge1\) satisfying distinctness at offsets one and two. Every embedding of the received word has at most two displaced host indices. The disjoint candidate-position sets have at most two surplus positions, giving the exhaustive multiplicity cases of one triple position or two doubled positions. The triple and adjacent alternating-four-letter cases count exactly \(2m-1\) multiplicity-three hosts; two distinct adjacent duplications count exactly \(\binom m2\) multiplicity-four hosts. The standard greedy supersequence count and insertion-history sum determine the remaining histogram, followed by exact Bayes normalization. The finite independent host-DP and insertion-history enumerations agreed on 1,226 patterns and the encoded boundary counterexamples; they support but are not the infinite proof.

## Originality (O): PASS against inspected sources, with stated residual risk
Ma–Zhang, arXiv:2611.01064 (2026), gives a complete positive embedding histogram for **binary alternating** words, and an entropy maximum over binary received words; neither statement specializes to the nonbinary distance-two-distinct family. Singhvi–Sabary–Bar-Lev–Yaakobi, arXiv:2407.10026 (2024), develops insertion/deletion entropy and embedding formulas but not the claimed host-multiplicity partition. Tavakoli–Nguyen–Bose, arXiv:2607.19559 (2026), gives general nonbinary embedding recurrence/normalization and capacity bounds; its all-word normalization identity does not identify the special multiplicity-three/-four hosts. Menon–Singh, arXiv:2306.07870 (2023), gives binary low-frequency subsequence counts, a different alphabet and a different family. Inspecting these statements and their implications found no dominating, equivalent, or tabulated formula for all \(q\ge3\). An unpublished, unindexed, or implicitly derivable equivalent case remains possible; the check does not prove global priority.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
The structural restriction is natural: all consecutive triples have distinct letters, a standard locally rainbow condition available only for nonbinary alphabets. It allows a *complete all-length* occurrence spectrum for a fixed received word in the two-deletion channel, whereas normalization alone yields only its zeroth and first moments. The nontrivial conclusion that no source has more than four compatible embeddings, together with exact counts at multiplicities three and four, resolves the posterior uncertainty and provides a uniform closed entropy correction of \(4/q\) bits asymptotically. This is a motivated channel invariant rather than a small numerical table.

## Scope and limitations
No claim of global entropy optimality, arbitrary-input-distribution behavior, or radii beyond two. The verified test grid is finite. The strongest subject-matched primary source is binary; other nonbinary work is chiefly on aggregate channel entropy. Potential undiscovered nonbinary specializations are the remaining priority risk. The output has received no external independent assessment.
