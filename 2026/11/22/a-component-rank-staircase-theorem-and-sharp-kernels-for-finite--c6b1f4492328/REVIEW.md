# Scientific review: component-rank composition and exponential chain staircase

## Correctness — PASS

The general result treats the exact finite one-point-extension rank for independently partitioned hereditary relational structures, not first-order quantifier rank. Every one-point extension lies in one occupied component or creates a new component. An induction tracks local thresholds in occupied components and forces the sorted unused-component ranks to dominate a descending staircase; the converse recursively selects a local response or consumes a highest-rank unused component. It follows that any principal local embedding kernels transfer to one principal componentwise kernel. The strict-chain specialization separately proves every gap requirement and the extremal census. For each marked position, the proof establishes the exhaustive alternative of a new incomparable chain versus insertion into a chosen gap of an occupied chain. An induction gives **necessity** because a successful gap insertion must leave two successor-rank gaps, and a successful new-chain insertion must consume one appropriately large unused component while leaving a descending capacity ladder. It gives **sufficiency** by choosing a point with the required margins or taking the largest unused chain. The scalar formula, kernel embedding equivalence and minimum/near-minimum census are algebraic consequences with boundary cases checked. A separate direct-game enumerator agreed with the closed criterion on every marked subset of all hosts through ten vertices, at thresholds zero through four (66,963 marked host configurations), and a partition check confirmed unique minimum and the next-layer counts through threshold four. A separate fully recursive test on small independently partitioned simple graphs corroborated the general composition theorem on 838 marked positions. Both finite computations only corroborate the induction.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

The contribution includes a general componentwise composition theorem, not only a linear-order substitution. López-Callejas and Navarro-Castillo's arXiv:2604.14461v1, sections 2 and 6, establishes the rank recursion and sharp single-chain threshold \(2^r-1\), but the simultaneous *ordered gap* and *new component* constraints are not covered by its single-order result. The general structural survey by Macpherson identifies the disjoint-chain homogeneous context but does not develop this rank calculation. Two closely indexed results on exactly the same objects address unrestricted-depth fixed-variable first-order equivalence and first-order rank-three theories, respectively, not the one-host extension-game rank. The earlier general evolution-rank work defines the rank and gives a finite-list embeddability characterization, while the present result identifies one explicit induced-embedding kernel in every finite rank layer. Full text of the two indexed same-object results was not accessible, so priority is provisional and no comprehensive exclusion of unknown literature is claimed.

## Value — PASS

The compositional lemma is useful whenever a relational class is assembled from independent named-equivalence components, transferring local game-rank invariants and principal witness kernels. Its exact ordered-chain application additionally gives a complete all-size, all-marking answer for a canonical homogeneous-poset age, together with a sharp exponential staircase and a **principal** induced-embedding test for every rank threshold. This is stronger than a minimum-size example: it describes every host of sufficient rank and every relative marked-position rank. New-component pressure forces successive long chains, while insertion pressure requires exponential gaps, a genuine interaction absent in either single-chain or pure-equivalence settings. The first two extremal order layers have complete structural and labelled counts, providing natural sanity checks and witnesses for rank universality.

## Closest literature and limitations

The direct antecedents are arXiv:2604.14461v1 (15 April 2026), Macpherson's survey of homogeneous structures (2011), and Kubiś–Shelah's *Evolution systems — an ordinal rank measuring universality* (2024). Closest indexed finite-chain comparisons have descriptions but not accessible full text. The general lemma assumes finite hosts, a hereditary base class with a unique singleton type, and a named independent equivalence partition. Its numerical census concerns finite strict disjoint-chain posets only, and the enumerative checker is not a general proof. An independent external audit is still required.
