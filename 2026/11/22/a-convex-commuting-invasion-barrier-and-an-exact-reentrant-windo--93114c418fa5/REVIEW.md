# Scientific review: A convex commuting-invasion barrier and an exact reentrant window in a two-city epidemic network

## Correctness — PASS

The proof starts from the published disease-free invasion linearization and its fixed positive protection matrix. A positive diagonal similarity reduces the matrix to a symmetric affine family; Rayleigh–Ritz yields the convexity theorem with no irreducibility hypothesis. For constant within-city row sums, the positive lift of the two-city quotient Perron vector establishes equality of *full* matrix and quotient radii; elementary differentiation yields exact curvature, a unique possible minimum, and the precise parameter condition. The ten-node graph has four nodes in the smaller city and six in the larger, nonnegative symmetric binary contacts, \(\tau=2/3<1\), and true probabilities \(\mu=1/5\), \(\beta=8/75\). The two exact crossing fractions are roots of the derived characteristic quadratic. The information-free equilibrium is locally stable for strictly positive active-removal and awareness-decay probabilities. The accompanying checker confirms symbolic identities and explicit full-matrix calculations. No finite enumeration is used to prove the general theorem.

## Originality — PASS, literature-bounded

The source paper [1] provides the disease-free eigenmatrix and numerical dependence on commuting, but not a convexity restriction, a complete exact regular-class phase classification or a binary-network instance where both commuting extremes admit invasion while an interior window does not. Li et al. [2] report nonmonotone thresholds in a movement–interaction–return home/destination model; Bliman et al. [3] provide general commuter reproduction-number analysis. Aronna and Moschen [4] and Rapaport and Mimouni [5] give other commuter optimizations, some with two threshold crossings, but their next-generation matrices involve population/time averaging or demographic compartment groups rather than the affine fixed-awareness adjacency matrix of [1]. The general convexity principle itself is standard matrix analysis, and broad commuter reentrance is known. The assessed claim is the *joint model-specific characterization and the exact admissible witness*, not a first discovery of nonmonotone commuting thresholds. Unindexed identical specializations remain an unresolved priority risk; they are not assumed absent.

## Value — PASS

The source explicitly notes that the commuting effect on epidemic thresholds varies with network structure. The convexity barrier gives a graph-independent policy-relevant impossibility of intermediate invasion when both extremes are safe; the equitable classification identifies the precise structural condition for a protective intermediate commuting interval. The exact sparse ten-node construction demonstrates the phenomenon within all of the source model's asymmetric-city constraints, rather than in an unrelated surrogate or outside the probability domain. This is a motivated finite-network threshold question; the claim does not extrapolate linear invasion to global epidemic control.

## Closest literature and limits

The closest literature comprises the original invasion matrix and numerical study [1], the home/destination nonmonotonicity analysis [2], the general commuter framework [3], and two previously characterized commuter optimizations [4,5]. Those establish precedent for nonmonotonic mobility but do not imply the matrix identity and exact thresholds of the assessed system. The theorem requires a fixed disease-free information equilibrium; its witness has no endogenous information spreading at equilibrium. No independent review is claimed. Full nonlinear outcomes, multiple-city commuting and global optimality in policy settings are not addressed.
