# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The comparison is a local spectral perturbation theorem, not an empirical extrapolation. At homogeneous coefficients both positive generalized eigenfunctions are constant and their principal eigenvalues are simple. The first-order eigenvalue coefficients vanish under the centered perturbation hypotheses. The common first-order eigenfunction solves the same zero-mean Neumann Poisson problem. Integrating the second-order equations leaves the same geometric resolvent term in both and one extra Fokker–Planck factor involving spatial recovery covariance. Its exact cancellation produces the claimed coefficient. Both strict threshold reversals follow from explicitly nonzero rational quadratic coefficients; positivity and zero-flux conditions persist for small amplitudes. The numerical cosine-Galerkin implementation independently reproduces the sign and coefficient limits. Smoothness, simplicity, and the bounded connected Neumann domain are necessary for the stated local argument.

The 2026 Liu–Wang–Zhang primary article supplies both eigenvalue definitions and expressly notes a difficulty comparing their reproduction numbers outside the constant-recovery case (Section 3, after Proposition 3.4). The 2022 Wang–Wang–Kim full primary article supplies variational spectral theory and numerical threshold differences, including one sign in particular simulations, but not the derived centered-heterogeneity covariance comparison or the symmetric pair of opposite invasion decisions. The 2023 Liu–Wang–Zhang nonlinear-incidence article gives reproduction-number and segregation studies; available indexed text was inspected but complete contiguous access was limited. Indexed published mathematical findings and the accessible current research ledger were compared by source, operator type, threshold claim and perturbative equivalence; none of the examined claims establishes this spectral comparison. The 2018 particle-transport study concerns microscopic derivations of diffusion laws, not reaction-driven SIS invasion eigenvalues. Historical priority beyond these inspections remains unproved.

## Value — PASS

The 2026 source identifies ordering its two invasion thresholds as difficult. The covariance law answers that natural model comparison locally, exposes a nontrivial geometry-free leading correction despite separate geometric contributions, and provides constructive opposite epidemic invasion outcomes without changing the diffusivity law between a paired comparison. These are structural epidemiological conclusions, not a generic textbook eigenvalue expansion or an arbitrary isolated numerical example.
