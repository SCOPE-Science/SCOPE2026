# Review

## Correctness
PASS. For diameter-one centrally symmetric polygons, the lattice triangle obstruction gives at most two points when the dilation remains below \(\sqrt2\). Only antipodal diameter vertices contribute as the diameter threshold is crossed. Each local overlap is an intersection of opposite polygonal tangent cones, an exact parallelogram with explicitly integrated area; four unoriented-direction/alignment branches per diameter pair produce the claimed factor \(1/(3\pi)\). The normalized angular density is exactly \(6u(1-u)\) at leading order. The derivation is uniform on the finite angular windows and yields \(O_P(\varepsilon^4)\). A standalone numerical test confirms the local geometry, but is not an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS, provisional. Schlie's November 2026 survey concerns randomized lattice counts mostly in the large-dilate regime. The closest published smooth-body threshold theorem proves a different exponent under hypotheses excluding polygon corners; its exact three-atom covariogram identity is explicitly credited. De Bruijn's long thin rectangle and Rosen's inclined rectangle are relevant but do not, on inspected material, imply the universal polygon coefficient plus its angle law. Rosen's full note remains an access-related residual risk. Independent historical priority review is still needed.

## Value
PASS. The polygonal critical exponent is a genuine boundary-regularity contrast with smooth body and disk onset laws; the coefficient is a natural complete finite invariant of all diameter tangent wedges, and the universal conditional angular law permits direct comparisons across shape families. The exact regular even polygon subfamily displays a nonuniform polygon-to-disk limit. This is a class-wide sharp onset theorem rather than a single isolated threshold calculation.

## Scope and risks
Fixed, centrally symmetric convex polygons only; no uniform near-circle limit, no general lattice, and no free-orientation optimization. Nearby special cases may exist in older stochastic geometry literature. Numerical checks cannot establish the theorem.
