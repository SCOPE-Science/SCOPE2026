# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness

PASS. The claim is exactly restricted to \(\mu=0\) and \(h=1/2\), where the source map reduces identically to \(w'=w-u\), \(u'=u\cos w\). The positive pre-crossing region is ordered, so the crossing initial amplitudes form an upper open set with a positive boundary. The boundary trajectory must approach zero, and smaller amplitudes retain a positive phase gap. The Riemann-sum identity provides two-sided bounds, a quintic threshold expansion, and a direct proof of the universal critical power law. The standalone diagnostics passed, but are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS within inspected evidence. Peng et al. (2024), https://doi.org/10.3390/sym16080990, specify the same map and provide numerical phase portraits and local equilibrium analysis; they do not state this zero-gain one-sided boundary or the \(n^{-3/2}\) law. A distinct already-accessible same-map result at fixed positive input gain concerns the count and distribution of equilibrium lines as amplification grows, which cannot cover a zero-input-gain transient separatrix. Parabolic-dynamics background uses different complex skew-product hypotheses. Indexed semantic searches and direct publication searches found no implication covering the full assertion. Risk remains from unindexed or differently formulated real-sine-memory literature.

## Value

PASS. The critical coupling \(h=1/2\) and maximally neutral phase \(z=\pi/2\) are natural stability boundaries of the published memristive coupling. The result identifies a complete, nonlinear basin separator on a two-dimensional invariant phase section, distinguishes exponential from algebraic synchronization, and supplies two sharp threshold coefficients, including the discrete fifth-order correction. It is more than a stability-eigenvalue restatement or a parameter substitution.

## Boundaries

This is not a theorem about arbitrary positive input gain, all real initial conditions, or long-term behavior after a crossing. No independent validation has been performed. Published background on the neutral fixed-line problem could still contain equivalent local material, although no inspected source gives the same global one-sided theorem.
