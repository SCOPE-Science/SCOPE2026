# Scientific review: degree-marked common-edge Poisson process

## Correctness — PASS

The exact conditional generating function is established by contracting a prescribed matching and applying weighted Prüfer coding. The multiplication of independent fair endpoint lifts is valid for every quotient-tree incidence, and the quotient weights sum to the original vertex count. For a fixed set of prescribed disjoint common edges, the conditional four-degree distribution in two independent trees factors into two copies of that one-tree law. Its limiting Poisson/Bernoulli distribution and independence across prescribed edges follow directly from the displayed finite pgf. Every overlapping-edge factorial-moment contribution is bounded by a uniform forest-inclusion estimate, while disjoint tuples give the claimed spatial intensity and independent degree marks. The proof covers all fixed-degree classes and spatial windows with boundary-null endpoints, not just leaf marks.

Exact arithmetic checks reproduce the conditional joint degree laws for eight small matching configurations and the pendant-overlap expectations by exhaustive pairs of labeled trees for three orders. These are finite checks, not a substitute for the limiting proof. The independent audit remains unperformed.

## Originality — PASS with historical qualification

The unmarked common-edge count converging to a Poisson law of mean two was established in classical work and is *not* claimed as a contribution. The full texts inspected for Bender–Canfield (1999) and Bóna–Wagner (2025) treat unmarked overlap statistics, exact forest inclusion, and related graphs; their mathematical conclusions do not state or imply the joint conditional endpoint-degree distribution across every matched prescribed edge. The independent four-degree marks, their spatial Poisson limit, and the nine-way pendant-type classification require the additional weighted Prüfer argument. A separate publicly indexed note on common-edge counts among multiple independent Cayley trees also treats overlap counts and their cross-tree cumulants rather than these degree marks.

An earlier Janson (1986) paper supplies the original unmarked law. Its accessible abstract was checked, but full text could not be obtained in this research. This and the possibility of an equivalent more general marked-Poisson theorem remain historical priority risks. The first exact-day public source date verified for the archival context is 1999-09-26; the earlier 1986 source is known at the year/month level and is not assigned an invented day.

## Value — PASS

The result determines the complete local structure attached to a sparse global overlap: a common edge is biased toward high-degree endpoints, and its two endpoint degrees in a given tree remain negatively correlated through the single obligatory external incidence. Yet different common edges have independent limiting degree profiles. This distinguishes genuinely new structural information from the classical unmarked overlap count, and leads to explicit probabilities for aligned versus opposite pendant ends. The exact conditional pgf is usable at every finite order for arbitrary fixed degree configurations, not merely an isolated limit constant.

## Limitations and references

The host is the complete graph, the trees are independent and uniform, prescribed edges form a matching, and limit marks refer to fixed finite degrees. No extension to arbitrary random spanning trees or growing degree thresholds is asserted. Priority is a best-knowledge assessment, not exhaustive historical certification.
