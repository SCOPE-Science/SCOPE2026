# The integral dihedral character of the Wagner graph's two-matching complex

## Finding

Let \(W\) have vertex set \(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\), the eight rim edges \(\{i,i+1\}\), and the four rungs \(\{i,i+4\}\). Let \(X=M_2(W)\): its simplicial vertices are the edges of \(W\), and its faces are edge subsets in which the graph degree at every vertex is at most two. Its automorphism action here is the action of **graph** automorphisms, generated by \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\). This group has order sixteen. The integral action on the only nonzero reduced homology group, \(\widetilde H_5(X;\mathbb Z)\cong \mathbb Z\), has character
\[
\chi(r^k)=(-1)^k,\qquad \chi(sr^k)=(-1)^{k+1}.
\]
In particular the ordinary geometric quotient \(|X|/G\), for a subgroup \(G\leq D_8\), has zero reduced rational homology if \(G\) contains a character-reversing element, and otherwise has reduced rational homology \(\mathbb Q\) in degree five only. The full graph symmetry quotient lies in the first case. The quotient conclusion is an application of the character, not an additional novelty claim.

The underlying nonequivariant homotopy type \(|X|\simeq S^5\) is background: it is given explicitly in the four-ladder computation cited below and also follows from the known vertex-independence computation as shown here. It is **not** presented as an original finding.

## Proof

**The group.** A four-cycle must use two rungs: no four consecutive rim edges form a cycle, three rim steps do not connect an antipodal pair, and rungs form a matching. The cycles are \((i,i+1,i+5,i+4)\) for \(i=0,1,2,3\). Every rim edge lies in one of these cycles and every rung lies in two. Thus graph automorphisms preserve the rim, and all of its dihedral symmetries preserve the rungs.

**Two dualities and an equivariant nerve.** For a complex \(K\) on a specified ground set \(S\), its combinatorial Alexander dual \(K^*\) comprises all \(B\subseteq S\) with \(S\setminus B\notin K\). The integral combinatorial Alexander isomorphism, with its permutation sign made explicit, is
\[
\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\widetilde H^{|S|-i-3}(K^*;\mathbb Z)\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_S.
\]
Here the cohomology action is the left action by inverse pullback. To see the sign, pass from reduced chains to relative chains of the full simplex modulo \(K\), shifting degree up by one. Complement each oriented relative face to the dual cochain, with the sign of the two oriented faces wedged to the ground-set volume. This intertwines boundary and coboundary up to degree-dependent signs (absorbed by rescaling in each degree). Reordering the ambient volume under a permutation contributes exactly \(\operatorname{sgn}_S\).

Write \(E=E(W)\), \(V=V(W)\), and \(I=\operatorname{Ind}(W)\), the ordinary **vertex** independence complex. Because \(W\) is cubic, the forbidden stars of three edges give a cover of \(X^*\) by simplices on \(E\setminus\operatorname{star}(v)\), for \(v\in V\). The nerve is \(I^*\): a set \(A\) of indices has nonempty geometric intersection exactly when there is a graph edge in \(V\setminus A\). The equivalence induces an equivariant isomorphism on homology and cohomology. Indeed, the following mutually reversing maps of nonempty face posets induce simplicial maps of their subdivisions:
\[
B\longmapsto\{v\in V: \text{no edge in }B\text{ meets }v\},
\qquad A\longmapsto E(W[V\setminus A]).
\]
Both composites contain their input faces, so they are homotopic to the identity by the order homotopy for comparable poset maps, and the original maps commute with graph automorphisms. Applying the duality first on the 12 elements of \(E\), and then on the eight elements of \(V\), gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(X;\mathbb Z)\cong
\widetilde H_{i-4}(I;\mathbb Z)\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_{E}\otimes\operatorname{sgn}_{V}
\]
in the degrees at issue. In particular \(I\simeq S^1\) by Agarwal–Basak, Main Theorem 1(a) at \(n=4\), so the only nonzero reduced homology of \(X\) is in degree five. For completeness, \(X\) has a complete 1-skeleton. The boundary of any missing triangle is the boundary of a full star at one graph vertex; it is filled by the three triangles obtained by coning that boundary with any edge outside the star. Any two edges of the star have just its center in common, and the new edge is not at that center. Thus all triangular loops in the 1-skeleton are null, so \(X\) is simply connected. Its homology now gives the background sphere equivalence, by Hurewicz and the homological Whitehead theorem.

**Compute the integral sign.** The reduced Lefschetz trace on \(I\) is minus the trace on its only reduced homology, in degree one. For \(r\) no nonempty face is fixed setwise, since \(r\) is an eight-cycle on \(V\). The augmentation contributes \(-1\), so the degree-one trace is \(+1\). For \(s\) the fixed vertices are \(0,4\); the setwise-fixed independent pairs are the swapped pairs \(\{1,7\},\{3,5\}\). The independent triples fixed setwise are \(\{1,4,7\},\{0,3,5\}\), also with an internal swap. No larger fixed independent set is possible: \(0,4\) are adjacent, \(2,6\) are adjacent, and the two allowed swapped pairs cannot coexist because \(1,5\) and \(3,7\) are adjacent. The alternating contributions in nonnegative chain degrees are \(2,+2,-2\), respectively, so the reduced trace is \(-1+2+2-2=1\) and the degree-one trace is \(-1\).

On \(V\) the permutation signs of \(r,s\) are both negative (an eight-cycle and three transpositions, respectively). On \(E\), \(r\) is an eight-cycle times a four-cycle and has positive sign. The reflection swaps four rim pairs and one rung pair and has negative sign. After both sign twists, \(r\) and \(s\) both act by \(-1\) on \(\widetilde H_5(X;\mathbb Z)\). The whole character follows because they generate the group.

**Ordinary quotient.** Pass to the barycentric subdivision so that stabilizers fix a simplex pointwise. The quotient rational chain complex identifies with the coinvariant chain complex (one simplex-orbit generator in each degree, with orientations inherited from face dimensions). Averaging for the finite group identifies coinvariants with invariants and is exact over \(\mathbb Q\). Therefore reduced rational homology is the invariant part of the one-dimensional character restricted to \(G\), proving the criterion.

## Relationship to prior work and limits

- Julianne Vega, [*2-Matching Complexes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10406), treats this bounded-degree complex and repeated Morse toggling, including sphere results for other graph classes. Neither that terminology nor the method is claimed as new.
- Anshu Agarwal and Biplab Basak, [*The Homotopy Types of the Independence and Perfect Matching Complexes of Möbius and Circular Ladder Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30601), Main Theorem 1(a) and Theorem 12, supply the vertex-independence homotopy input. The two Alexander dualities explain exactly how that input implies the nonequivariant result. Their nonequivariant theorem by itself supplies no generator signs.
- [*Two-matching homotopy of the first four nondegenerate Möbius ladders*](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/blob/e36f0e8ddfc25c4e15c7280f7b72b9078cfbf234/2026/11/22/SCOPE-two-matching-homotopy-of-the-first-four-nondegenerate-m-bius-lad--e40e4a95f116/RESULT.md) explicitly calculates the Wagner \(S^5\) case by discrete Morse theory. It does not calculate the integral graph-action character. No relative priority within that shared public snapshot is asserted.
- The scope is the homology character for this particular Wagner complex. No equivariant homotopy equivalence to a geometric sphere with a specified action, integral quotient homology, or classification of larger ladders is claimed. The novelty assessment is limited to inspected sources, not a proof of historical priority.
