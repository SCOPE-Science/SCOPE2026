# Review of the dimension-four quenched time-average law

**Correctness — PASS.** A reset-killed backward voter ancestry gives the exact conditional stationary mean as the known resolvent-weighted sum of independent fixed original spins. An exponential conditional covariance bound proves convergence of the local physical-time average to that mean. The semigroup identity gives the exact disorder variance; local central limit estimates yield the distinct \(d<4\), \(d=4\), and \(d>4\) asymptotic regimes. The maximum-coefficient Lindeberg criterion proves Gaussian convergence for \(d\le4\), while an \(\ell^2\) Green-kernel limit and strictly negative fourth cumulant exclude Gaussianity for \(d\ge5\). At the critical dimension, the Green-function \(\ell^4\) estimate and the exact fourth cumulant prove the claimed coefficient. A separate exact finite-state generator computation, Green/Fourier identities, and one-dimensional closed forms passed 231 deterministic checks. The finite checker does not certify the infinite-limit theorem.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Grange (2022, arXiv:2207.08590) provides the *same model* and the one-point mean resolvent, initial-state memory, and annealed correlation equations: those prerequisites are explicitly credited, not presented as new. The previously published finite-cycle fixed-versus-fresh reset comparison (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/blob/main/2026/11/2/SCOPE-fixed-opinion-memory-strictly-enhances-voter-correlations-on-eve--6fc6c892fbed/RESULT.md) was inspected in full and concerns finite-ring annealed pair correlations and domain walls rather than disorder-law limits of a stationary time average on the infinite lattice. Pymar–Rivera (2021, arXiv:2112.01478) use fresh rather than fixed-original resetting. Actual searches for stationary voter time averages, quenched initial fields, dimension-four criticality, Rademacher resolvent series, and equivalent Green-function formulations found no directly dominating statement in the inspected sources or indexed records. The assessed new claim is the *sharp Gaussian-to-non-Gaussian quenched law* and the dimension-four logarithmic-squared kurtosis coefficient, not the mean resolvent or general CLT technique. A broader unindexed random-field convolution theorem could still imply this result, so publication priority remains qualified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The distinction between annealed spin correlations and the distribution across original opinion fields of a single-site stationary time average is structurally meaningful: every rate has a nonzero sample-dependent long-time average, but the standardized weak-reset law is Gaussian only below a sharp dimension. The transition coincides with the change from divergence to square summability of the random-walk Green kernel, and the critical fourth cumulant has an explicit nontrivial logarithmic asymptotic. It describes a natural fixed-memory effect and an interpretable critical dimension, not a parameter substitution or a finite-table exercise.

**Closest literature and limitations.** The one-point resolvent is known in Grange (2022). The finite-ring resolvent and correlation comparisons are known in the published result cited above, and fresh-noise noisy voter results are not directly applicable. Classical random-walk and independent-series theorems are used. Assumptions are iid fair fixed original signs, independent local Poisson resets, nearest-neighbour copying, infinite \(\mathbb Z^d\), conditional stationary time limit before \(r\downarrow0\). Claims do not cover fixed-environment Gaussianity, joint time/reset limits, or arbitrary graphs. Independent scientific priority and correctness audits remain outstanding.
