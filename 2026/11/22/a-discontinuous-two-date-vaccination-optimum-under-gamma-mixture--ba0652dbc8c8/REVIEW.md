# Scientific assessment: discontinuous optimal dose timing in two-wave exposure

## Correctness — PASS
The exact first-event survival law is obtained by integrating the post-dose nonhomogeneous Poisson hazard, not by approximating an epidemic final size. Two independent algebraic reconstructions confirm the explicit objective derivative and total baseline hazard. Four analytic derivative-sign certificates force three distinct stationary points. The exponential-polynomial Rolle bound excludes all additional extrema, including extraneous tangencies. An envelope derivative proves that the difference between the early and late local maxima increases strictly with the fast-wave amplitude; elementary bounds establish opposite signs at the two endpoints. The result is therefore an infinite-parameter proof, not an inference from the numerical checker. The standalone reproducibility checker passes 5,199 assertions.

## Originality — PASS within searched evidence; priority provisional
Primary literature relevant to waning and seasonal vaccination was inspected at the level available: the complete open-access Xiao–Moghadas 2013 article models vaccinated and unvaccinated compartments, epidemic-season pulses and immunity across strains, but assumes vaccines before each season; the open Chao–Dimitrov 2015 article studies seasonal interruption and optimal *coverage*, not variable within-season individual dose times. Werker et al. (2013 online) pose a directly related vaccine-date optimization and report a Markov-cohort optimum, but its full manuscript was not accessible after open-access and institutional-access attempts. Its abstract does not settle the claimed exact three-extremum classification or unique transition. Semantic published-result searches for exponential-waning one-dose timing, two-wave hazards, gamma-mixture vaccination dates and equivalent switch formulations returned related but not covering statements. The result uses classical exponential-polynomial zero-counting and the envelope theorem; those tools are not new. The exact two-wave parameter family, proven two-local-maximum regime and unique global crossing are the narrow originality claim. Residual risks include a broader pre-existing theorem on mixture-response optimal stopping, and comparison to the unavailable Werker manuscript.

## Value — PASS
When waning protection must cover two widely separated exposure peaks, a single-date optimum need not move continuously as the relative waves change. The result provides a rigorous and explicit counterexample with exactly two competing date optima, exhaustive stationary-point count, and a unique policy crossover. This is a motivated qualitative boundary for time-varying preventive coverage, rather than a generic calculation of a single optimum or an experimentally fitted vaccine recommendation. It is useful as a benchmark for numerical timing models and for checking any algorithm that assumes unimodal benefit. The exact hazard and vaccine-response assumptions are stylized; no conclusion is claimed about a real disease's optimal vaccination month, coupled transmission or health-system policy.
