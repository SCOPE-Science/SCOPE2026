The Boolean identity \(g^2(x)_i=x_i\lor(x_{i-2}x_{i+2})\) proves that an initially nonzero rim state cannot become zero after its first nonzero NOR output, while \(g^3=g\) gives the one-step recurrent cutoff. The case split counts the oscillator basin without overlap. Distance-two cyclic adjacency has one component for odd length and two components for even length. The 2-state transfer trace is Lucas, including the short boundaries \(n=3,4\). Both independent checker scripts pass, with finite checks clearly distinguished from the all-size proof. **Value — PASS.** A universal hub is a natural, graph-theoretic intervention in inhibitory Boolean networks. The precisely identified empty/full two-cycle has more than half the entire state space as its basin for every rim size, despite the existence of other fixed states and oscillators. The exact probability exhibits a hidden odd/even and mod-four Lucas correction while tending to one-half. The corresponding bare-cycle oscillator basin has size \(T_n+1\), a vanishing fraction of the bare cycle state space, whereas adding one hub makes the selected oscillator attract over half the new state space. The observable is therefore a natural basin-selection probability over a scalable graph family, not an arbitrary finite slice. Full classification of other attractors is not needed for this dominant-basin theorem.
