## Originality — PASS on inspected literature

The cited 2024 full-text experimental articles by Boos and colleagues and Munz and colleagues examine flow-dependent bed retention and downstream particle transport, not optimization of two controlled exposure-regime orders. The complete Rasmussen et al. mathematical article develops nonautonomous compartmental transit-time formulas, not this two-stage export-order classification. The published *Sharp three-stage stormwater exposure ordering with continued pollutant supply* concerns a scalar stock and integrated exposure with continuing supply. The published *A sharp exchange-rate optimum for an unequal-loss two-box tracer* concerns optimizing a constant rate for a receiver-state endpoint. Neither mathematically implies the explicit two-regime commutator law, sharp initial-storage composition threshold, all-duration sign invariance, and high/low-flow reversal in this source-free two-state objective. The 2022 Drummond et al. full article was not accessible: no claim of whole-document noncoverage is made. General matrix-commutator algebra is classical and is not itself claimed as new; an unknown stronger scheduling theorem remains a residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS

A field-motivated reversible mobile/bed storage model makes two countervailing mechanisms relevant to hydraulic intervention: initial mobile mass can be exported promptly, whereas bed-stored mass must first be remobilized. The complete theorem identifies which composition determines the preferred order, and proves the counterintuitive robustness that increasing either stage duration cannot change that ranking. The positive-rate example makes the reversal nonempty even when high flow raises export, attachment, and remobilization rates simultaneously. This gives a falsifiable structural benchmark for interpreting two-stage control and initial-state uncertainty, although it cannot prescribe field operations without spatial transport, new input, heterogeneous sediments, and calibration.
