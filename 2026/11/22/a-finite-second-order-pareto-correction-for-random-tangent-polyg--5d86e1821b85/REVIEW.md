# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The geometric formula \(A_n=\sum_i\tan(\pi Y_i)\) follows from tangent-segment decomposition and the inradius-one area–perimeter identity; it was also checked independently via direct line intersections and shoelace area. For \(n\ge4\), the conditional residual after one almost-semicircular gap has a finite first moment. Its beta-coordinate tail is \(O(r^{-(n-2)})\). A residual truncation at \(t^{2/3}\) makes double-edge or large-residual configurations \(o(t^{-2})\), while the moving threshold expands with averaged error \(o(t^{-2})\). The beta marginal then yields the two coefficients with all conditioning factors. The displayed quadrilateral integral is evaluated by integration by parts.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
Fodor, Hug and Ziebarth (2014, arXiv:1409.8105, primary MSC 52A22) analyze large-side-count circumscribed-polytope expectations and variance rather than fixed-sample extreme-area tails. Finch (2011, arXiv:1101.3931) gives three-tangent single-side laws. The full 2023 Wang–Li–Xu article treats large-side-count Dirichlet extrapolation. The accessible Xu (2015) abstract describes the same tangent-polygon model and unbounded possible area, but its full article could not be inspected; that is a real unresolved priority risk. Published exact Pareto constants and conditional rare-shape laws for fixed Dirichlet polygons give only the leading \(1/t\) term, not \(D_n\), and the published logarithmic second-order result is restricted to \(n=3\). Actual published-record searches for four-sided aliases, beta-integral coefficients and equivalent statements found no dominating result. No claim of exhaustive historical uniqueness is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The second coefficient completes a natural fixed-sample next-order geometric tail question left open by the previously known universal leading Pareto term. The entire family \(n\ge4\) has a finite, positive correction, while \(n=3\) exhibits a different logarithmically amplified correction: the proof identifies the exact integrability threshold causing this change. The closed quadrilateral constant involving \(\log2\) makes the effect independently testable; the result can quantify finite-threshold tail bias even though the mean area diverges.

## Limitations
The model is the fixed-size unit-circle tangent ensemble under boundedness conditioning. No uniformity in the number of sides, no nonuniform angular law and no independent mathematical audit are asserted. Priority against the inaccessible full Xu (2015) article remains provisional. Numerical quadrature corroborates rather than proves the infinite-threshold statement.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
