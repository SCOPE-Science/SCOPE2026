## Correctness — PASS
The proof works on the compact nonnegative fixed-sum simplex for every integer \(m\ge2\) and every real \(S>0\). Boundary optima are impossible since \(f'(0)=1>f'(x)\) for all positive \(x\). Equal marginal returns restrict asymmetric candidates to exactly one high-density patch \(b>2\), with all other patches at \(1<a<2\); a second high-density patch is excluded by its strictly positive split-direction curvature. The scalar branch \(S_m(d)=m+d+md/(e^d-1)\) is strictly convex, and the descending stationary branch is a saddle in its redistribution direction. On \(S_m(d)<2m\), the envelope comparison \(H'_m(d)=[f'(a)-f'(S_m/m)]S'_m(d)\) has the prescribed sign. At \(S=2m\), the positive cubic perturbation for \(m\ge3\) proves that the increasing branch beats equality. These facts force exactly one global jump before the equal point loses local stability. For \(m=2\), the scalar branch starts continuously at \(S=4\). Numerical checks, including independent constrained optimization, are supplementary; they are not an infinite-domain proof.

## Value — PASS
The result resolves a motivated conflict between equal-density ideal-free-like placement and collective recruitment maximization when the Ricker curve is declining at high density. Its single discontinuous \(m\ge3\) transition occurs while the equal allocation is still a strict local maximum, whereas the two-habitat transition is continuous. The exact scalar construction gives every optimal vector for all stocks, including an actionable model benchmark for detecting local optimization traps and quantifying forced-allocation overcrowding. This is a self-contained model result, not a real-world stocking recommendation.
