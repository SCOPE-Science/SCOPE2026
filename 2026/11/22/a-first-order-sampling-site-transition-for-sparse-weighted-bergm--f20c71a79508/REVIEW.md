The reduction from one-kernel orthogonal projection to maximization of \((1+a|w|^m)^2(1-|w|^2)^\beta\) is exact, and equality in angular alignment determines all phases. The derivative for \(m=2\) is affine. For every \(m\ge3\), the radial derivative has a unique interior hump, while the global crossing threshold is the unique minimum of a coercive scalar function; the critical root is unique by strict concavity. The spinodal and crossing are strictly separated, and the limiting laws follow from the root equation. Neither inspected complete paper supplies the \(1+a z^m\) global center/orbit phase boundary, metastable window or all-degree asymptotics. Qu–Dang (arXiv:1803.04609) concerns adaptive approximation. Closest indexed Hankel binomial tests and two-kernel pure-quadratic approximation are different extremal problems. The full Qu–Dang 2019 algorithmic article was inaccessible, leaving its experiments and unindexed earlier statements as residual historical-priority risks. No claim of exhaustive novelty is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is the exact complete geometry of optimal single-site sampling of a natural high-frequency signal with a constant background, with an unexpected universal split between continuous degree-two selection and discontinuous higher-degree selection. The distinct spinodal and global thresholds quantify metastability, and their separating asymptotics provide a meaningful high-degree regime. Neither general existence nor pure-monomial circle degeneracy yields these observations.

## Scientific limitations

The argument treats a single kernel atom, power-weighted analytic Bergman disks and binomial targets only. Generic sparse polynomials, simultaneous multiple atoms, algorithmic attainment, and absolute historical priority are outside the claim. Finite diagnostics are not infinite proofs.
