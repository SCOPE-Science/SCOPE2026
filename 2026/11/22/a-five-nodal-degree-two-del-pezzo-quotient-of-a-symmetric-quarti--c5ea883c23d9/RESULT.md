# A five-nodal degree-two del Pezzo quotient of a symmetric quartic K3 surface

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let
\[
X=V(x^3y+xy^3+z^3w+zw^3)\subset\mathbb P^3_k.
\]
Let \(G\) be the eight-element permutation group generated by \((xy)\), \((zw)\), and \((xz)(yw)\); thus \(G\cong D_8\), the order-eight dihedral group. The normal quotient \(Y=X/G\) is the normal double plane
\[
Y\cong V\!\left(Z^2-A(A-4B)(A^2-4AB+8AC-16BC-16C^2)\right)
\subset \mathbb P_k(1,1,1,2),
\]
where \(A,B,C\) have weight one and \(Z\) has weight two. The natural morphism \(Y\to\mathbb P^2_{[A:B:C]}\) is the anticanonical double cover, branched along two lines and a smooth conic in pairwise transverse position with five distinct crossing points. Consequently \(Y\) is a Gorenstein del Pezzo surface of degree two, with exactly five ordinary double points of type \(A_1\) and no other singularities. Its minimal resolution is a weak del Pezzo surface of degree two with five disjoint exceptional \((-2)\)-curves.

The five branch crossings are
\[
[0:0:1],\quad [0:1:0],\quad [0:1:-1],\quad
[4:1:0],\quad [4:1:1].
\]
All formulas have rational coefficients and the five resulting singular points are rational in the indicated double-plane model; the classification is geometric over \(k\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero. The stated quotient is the **full coordinate-permutation quotient** by the order-eight group \(G\), not the quotients by diagonal symmetries studied in Berglund–Hübsch–Krawitz constructions. A line of the branch curve has ramification order two; the five singular points are the full singular locus of the normal quotient. We do not classify all automorphisms of \(X\), all quotient presentations, or the arithmetic splitting of exceptional curves.

## Proof
**1. Smooth source, group and invariant map.** The polynomial is invariant under each of the three stated permutations. They generate \((C_2\times C_2)\rtimes C_2\), which has order eight. The quartic is smooth: simultaneous vanishing of the two partial derivatives in \(x,y\) forces \(x=y=0\), since for \(xy\ne0\) the equations \(3x^2+y^2=0\) and \(x^2+3y^2=0\) are incompatible; similarly \(z=w=0\). Hence \(X\) is a smooth K3 surface with hyperplane class \(H\) and \(H^2=4\).

Set \(s=x+y\), \(t=z+w\), \(p=xy\), \(q=zw\), \(U=s+t\), \(d=s-t\), and \(e=p-q\). Put
\[
A=U^2,\qquad B=st,\qquad C=p+q,\qquad Z=4Ude-U^2d^2.
\]
The polynomials \(A,B,C\) are invariant quadrics, and \(Z\) is an invariant quartic: the two within-pair transpositions leave all six quantities unchanged; the interchange of coordinate pairs negates \(d,e\) simultaneously. These give a morphism
\(f:X\to\mathbb P^2_{[A:B:C]}\) because the three quadrics have no common zero on \(X\). Indeed, \(A=B=C=0\) implies \(U=0\), \(st=0\), thus \(s=t=0\) and \(y=-x,w=-z\). Then \(C=-(x^2+z^2)=0\) and the quartic equation becomes \(-2(x^4+z^4)=-4x^4=0\); all four homogeneous coordinates would vanish, a contradiction.

The pullback of a projective line by \(f\) is \(2H\), which is ample; the morphism is therefore finite. Its degree satisfies
\[
\deg f=(2H)^2=16.
\]
The generic orbit of the faithful finite group \(G\) has eight points. Consequently the finite induced map \(\bar f:Y=X/G\to\mathbb P^2\) has generic degree two.

**2. Exact quadratic equation and identification of the quotient.** Direct expansion in \(k[x,y,z,w]\) gives the polynomial identity
\[
Z^2-A(A-4B)(A^2-4AB+8AC-16BC-16C^2)
=-16(x^3y+xy^3+z^3w+zw^3)U^2d^2.
\]
Thus the invariant \(Z\) satisfies the stated weighted quartic equation on \(X\). Write \(Q=A^2-4AB+8AC-16BC-16C^2\). The product \(A(A-4B)Q\) is not a square in \(k(\mathbb P^2)\) because its valuation along the line \(A=0\) is odd. Hence \(Z\) gives a quadratic subextension of \(k(X)/k(\mathbb P^2)\), necessarily the full degree-two extension \(k(Y)/k(\mathbb P^2)\). The weighted double-plane hypersurface is normal, since it is a degree-two flat cover of the smooth plane with reduced branch divisor having only ordinary crossings (see Step 3). It therefore equals the normalization of \(\mathbb P^2\) in \(k(Y)\), and is the quotient \(Y\). This proves the claimed global, not merely generic, presentation.

**3. Branch curve and singularities.** The three reduced components of the branch quartic are
\[
L_1:\ A=0,\qquad L_2:\ A-4B=0,\qquad Q:\ A^2-4AB+8AC-16BC-16C^2=0.
\]
The symmetric matrix of \(Q\) is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&-2&4\\-2&0&-8\\4&-8&-16\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is \(128\ne0\). Thus \(Q\) is a smooth conic. The lines meet at \([0:0:1]\), where \(Q=-16\ne0\). On \(L_1\), \(Q=-16C(B+C)\), yielding the two distinct transverse crossings \([0:1:0]\) and \([0:1:-1]\). On \(L_2\), \(Q=16C(B-C)\), yielding the distinct transverse crossings \([4:1:0]\) and \([4:1:1]\). There are no triple intersections. The corresponding local double-cover equations are analytically \(z^2=uv\) at the five crossings; elsewhere the reduced branch cover is smooth. Each singularity is \(A_1\), so there are exactly five of them.

**4. Canonical class and minimal resolution.** The canonical bundle formula for a normal double plane with quartic branch gives
\[
K_Y=\bar f^*(K_{\mathbb P^2}+2H_{\mathbb P^2})=\bar f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1).
\]
Therefore \(-K_Y\) is ample and \((-K_Y)^2=2\). Since every singularity is Du Val, the minimal resolution is crepant. Its anticanonical class is nef and big of square two; the five exceptional components are disjoint \((-2)\)-curves. This is a weak del Pezzo surface of degree two.

## Verification
The included \(verify.py\) uses exact symbolic arithmetic to check: invariance under the three group generators; the quartic-polynomial identity; nonsingularity of the conic; the two line restrictions; and the nonintersection of the third branch component with the line-line crossing. These checks are finite identities, not substitutes for the geometric arguments concerning generic degree, normality and canonical class. Execute \(python verify.py\) with the version of SymPy specified in \(requirements.txt\).

As an independent topological consistency check, the smooth degree-two weak del Pezzo resolution has Euler characteristic \(10\), while contracting the five \((-2)\)-curves gives Euler characteristic \(5\). The Lefschetz fixed-locus averaging for the order-eight permutation group also gives \(5\): two transpositions each fix a smooth genus-three curve of Euler characteristic \(-4\), three even double transpositions each have eight isolated fixed points, and the two four-cycles have no fixed points on \(X\), so \((24-8+24)/8=5\). The explicit double-plane proof does not rely on this extra check.

## Relationship to prior work
Barrett–Clayton–DeVries–Priddis (2026) introduce the quartic and explicitly list its order-eight coordinate-permutation group; they study diagonal and individual nondiagonal automorphisms, invariant lattices, and mirror-symmetry questions. The present calculation addresses the **full** permutation quotient, a different invariant, providing the complete anticanonical equation and exact singularity basket. The literature already contains general double planes branched in two lines and a conic, including five-nodal degree-two del Pezzo examples in Fallucca–Pignatelli (2023/2024); novelty is **not** asserted for that abstract branch type. Their covering groups and source surfaces differ. Uludağ (2005) gives broad classifications of K3 Galois covers of the plane but does not identify this specific non-Galois sixteen-sheeted invariant map or its intervening order-eight quotient in the inspected material. Searches of indexed mathematical findings and literature did not locate an exact equivalent of this identification; older explicit Fermat-quartic invariant calculations may be unindexed.

## Limitations
The result concerns the specified coordinate-permutation subgroup, not the entire quartic automorphism group. The singularity basket and del Pezzo structure are geometric; arithmetic resolution curves are not classified. The finite symbolic checker verifies identities and simple intersection calculations, not an exhaustive historical novelty claim. Earlier results on double planes with the same branch combinatorics are expressly acknowledged. No independent external mathematical audit is claimed.
