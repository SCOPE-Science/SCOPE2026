# Review of the explicit dihedral quotient

## Correctness — PASS
The three invariant quadrics have no common zero on the smooth quartic, and their map has degree sixteen because the pullback of a line is twice the quartic hyperplane section. Quotienting by the faithful order-eight group therefore produces a double plane. The full polynomial relation, obtained by direct expansion and independently checked by the included symbolic verifier, identifies the quadratic extension. The conic matrix has determinant \(128\), and direct restrictions to the two branch lines exhibit exactly five pairwise transverse crossings; normality, local double-cover singularity models, and double-plane adjunction give precisely five \(A_1\) points and anticanonical degree two. Code tests are supplementary, not a proof of geometric normality.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The exact quartic and eight-element coordinate-permutation group are from Barrett–Clayton–DeVries–Priddis, arXiv:2611.15986v1, §3.1.3. Their results concern mirror polarizations and individual automorphisms, not the full quotient. Fallucca–Pignatelli, arXiv:2305.04545v2, §5.7 has degree-two del Pezzo double planes branched over two lines and a conic with five nodes in *different* constructions. This prior result means the numerical configuration is known generally: the new contribution is this specific K3 quotient's invariant equation and its proof of identification. Uludağ (2005) classifies Galois K3-to-plane orbifolds, a different map than the non-Galois sixteen-sheeted invariant map constructed here. Targeted statement/alias and indexed-finding comparisons yielded no dominating result. A classical Fermat-quartic invariant presentation remains a genuine possible publication-priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The question naturally extends the initiating paper's permutation-symmetry analysis from individual fixed loci to the entire nonabelian quotient. The complete anticanonical double-plane presentation and five-point singularity basket make the quotient's birational type and resolution explicit. No wider automorphism classification or new abstract del Pezzo branch type is claimed.

## Limits
Geometric classification over algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields; no claim about arithmetic splitting of exceptional curves. Finite computer algebra checks identities and branch geometry, with global quotient and canonical statements justified mathematically. Publication priority is best-knowledge, not exhaustive historical certification.
