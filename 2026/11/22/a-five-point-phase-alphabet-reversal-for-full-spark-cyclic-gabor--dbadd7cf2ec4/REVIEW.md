# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim concerns a fully specified finite domain of \(979\) normalized windows across five alphabets and all \(53{,}130\) five-atom selections for each tested candidate until an exact singular witness. Every complex determinant lies in a cyclotomic integer ring of degree at most eight; its integer norm is bounded by \(5^{20}\). Evaluations modulo two explicitly verified completely splitting primes, whose product exceeds that bound, give an exact complex zero oracle. An independently implemented integer polynomial expansion reproduces both extreme outcomes: five normalized binary full-spark windows, zero normalized fifth-root full-spark windows. The program outputs and all 709 singular witnesses are embedded.

## Originality
PASS under bounded comparison. Lawrence–Pfander–Walnut establish open-dense generic full-spark behavior, and Malikiosis gives existence and high-order cyclotomic constructions; these do not classify small phase alphabets. The closely matched fifth-root cross-STFT support census tests phase-quantized input *and* window, while full spark requires independence for arbitrary complex inputs. The flat-sign-line capacity classifies a different vector family in real dimension five. Searches for phase-alphabet, finite Gabor-minor, and full-spark equivalents revealed no exact \(q=1,\ldots,5\) census. The possibility of an earlier unindexed equivalent polyphase-code enumeration cannot be excluded.

## Value
PASS. Full spark is the sharp all-erasure-pattern recoverability condition for an overcomplete finite Gabor frame. The first nontrivial odd prime dimension admits a complete short phase-alphabet design classification: all five symmetric binary sign-pattern designs succeed, while *all* 625 normalized pentaphase designs fail. The counterintuitive reversal is genuinely about the natural, nonnested root-of-unity alphabets, not a mislabeled monotonicity claim. Both explicit designs and sharp obstructions are useful finite design benchmarks.

## Closest literature and limitations
The 2005 prime-order generic theorem and 2013 general cyclic construction prove neither the small-alphabet counts nor the fifth-root impossibility. The 2016 noncyclic obstruction has different hypotheses. Two materially examined exact records (pentaphase cross-STFT support and flat sign-line capacity) do not imply the present 25-atom Gabor full-spark statement. The theorem is finite, says nothing about dimension other than five or \(q>5\), and has no independently performed audit.
