For every fixed Gauss–Legendre node budget, the tangent and algebraic transforms for inverse-square and inverse-cube near-singular kernels attain vanishing relative error as the source distance tends to zero, whereas the usual sinh transformation loses the entire leading singular mass. For nonconstant smooth numerators, the absolute-error advantage grows sharply as \(d^{-1}\) or \(d^{-2}/\log(1/d)\), respectively; this is a fixed-budget limit, not a contradiction of fixed-distance exponential convergence.
