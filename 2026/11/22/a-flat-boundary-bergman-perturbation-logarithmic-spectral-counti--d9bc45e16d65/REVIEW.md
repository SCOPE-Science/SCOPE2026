# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
On the monomial basis, the normalized self-commutator is exactly a signed Jacobi quadratic form. The weight integral follows from integration by parts; its fixed-parameter saddle yields the exact logarithmic coefficient, and an included rank/block argument proves counting rather than inferring it from finite experiments. At small parameter a Bessel K0 scaling, a uniform two-tail bound, and long constant-like test blocks give the exact norm coefficient. The checker independently reconstitutes moments and finite matrix identities (135 numerical assertions). The unbounded form congruence is expressly restricted to finite vectors.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Le–Simanek (arXiv:2003.07330) already establishes a *general* Jacobi norm criterion, so that criterion is not claimed as new. The new subject is its infinitely flat radial symbol and two sharp asymptotic quantities: the fixed-parameter logarithmic spectral counting coefficient and the singular vanishing of the hyponormal coupling bound as the flatness parameter tends to zero. Pushnitski (arXiv:1710.02652) treats power-decaying banded matrices; Koita–Kupin–Naboko–Touré (arXiv:2006.02586) treats logarithmic decay. These results do not directly yield the stretched-exponential counting coefficient. The 2021 positive Toeplitz trace result has a different operator and assumptions. Other Bergman hyponormal symbol papers concern polynomial symbols. No checked source was found to state or imply both asymptotic laws for this Jacobi family. The literature search is not an exhaustive priority proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The quantity is not an arbitrary spectral ornament: it is the exact positivity-control operator for a natural Bergman hyponormality problem. The result distinguishes infinite-order boundary flatness from genuinely small hyponormal perturbations and quantifies the escaping high-degree obstruction through a universal Bessel maximizer. The counting law refines qualitative compactness to an exact spectral scale for all Schatten classes; the coupling collapse reveals a nonuniform strong-operator limit.

## Scope and limitations
The two limits (rank and flatness parameter) are taken separately. Numerical checks establish consistency, not the infinite theorem. The compact Jacobi operator has no asserted elementary closed norm for finite positive parameter. Priority remains subject to specialist review.
