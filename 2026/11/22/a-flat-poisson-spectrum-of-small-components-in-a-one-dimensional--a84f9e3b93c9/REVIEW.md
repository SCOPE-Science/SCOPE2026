# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For independent uniform points on an interval, the augmented vector of order-statistic spacings has an exchangeable uniform-simplex law. Exactly the internal spacings exceeding the radius create component boundaries. Conditional on their number, the boundary locations are a uniform subset, yielding an exact uniform positive-composition law. Counting marked component slots proves the displayed mixed factorial-moment identity, including all degenerate conventions. If the component count is asymptotic to \(a\sqrt n\), its fixed finite-size component counts converge via all mixed factorial moments to independent \(\operatorname{Poisson}(a^2)\) variables. At \(r_n=(\log n+x)/(2n)\), exact one-gap and two-gap probabilities and negative pairwise covariance yield concentration of the component count at \(e^{-x/2}\sqrt n\), giving the claimed unconditional independent Poisson spectrum. Every quantifier and fixed-size limitation appears in the result. Exact code verified 5,345 finite assertions; finite checks do not substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk

The inspected arXiv full text of Decreusefond–Ferraz (2010, arXiv:1008.5140) develops a Poissonized graph's cluster length and total-count distributions, not this fixed-\(n\) conditional size-moment law or a simultaneous fixed-size rare-component spectrum. The accessible full-text transcription of Zhao–Yağan–Gligor (2015, arXiv:1502.00404, **withdrawn**) establishes an isolation threshold but no vector Poisson count limit for every finite component size. Penrose (2015/2018, arXiv:1507.07132), Theorems 2.2–2.3, provides general one-component-size Poisson approximations but requires a connection function with supremum vanishing or uniformly below one, which excludes the present deterministic hard-radius kernel. An accessible published comparison record for a circular geometric graph studies the isolate count and Gaussian total-count fluctuation; that does not establish independence across all component sizes on an interval. A distinct high-retention finite-range **bond-percolation** model has a flat Poisson size spectrum, but not under the present uniform-position geometric mechanism or the same scale. The exact composition count and the conditional cut exchangeability themselves are classical and not independently claimed as new. Targeted searches did not expose equivalent stronger fixed-sample geometric coverage. Older random-composition or renewal-process literature may subsume portions; historical priority is not certified. One plausible full-article PDF could not be inspected in PDF format, although its accessible transcription was reviewed.

## Value — PASS

The well-studied isolation threshold is not the whole failure-mode distribution: at that threshold a fixed number of connected fragments of *every* bounded size persist, with the same Poisson intensity and asymptotically independent frequencies. This is a natural cluster-size census for one-dimensional sensor connectivity, and the finite conditional factorial-moment formula gives exact calibration at any radius rather than an arbitrary numerical instance. The coexistence of order-\(\sqrt n\) total components and order-one counts at each fixed size is a structural, nontrivial scaling distinction. No promise of relevance to higher dimensions or a growing number of size categories is made.

## Limitations and closest literature

The primary papers establish important marginal or Poissonized information; the theorem's historical originality remains subject to independent literature and mathematical review. Its finite-sample formula is conditional, not a claim that component sizes are unconditionally independent. The proof is for iid uniform samples on an interval; the cut characterization does not transfer unchanged to a circle or to nonuniform positions. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.5140, https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07132, https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00404 (withdrawn), and related published research on circular geometric-graph isolation and on finite-range cycle percolation.
