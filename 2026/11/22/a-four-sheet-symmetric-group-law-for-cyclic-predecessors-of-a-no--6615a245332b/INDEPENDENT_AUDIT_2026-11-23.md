# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For indexed y, a fixed state at the cut before site 0 extends by the displayed backward maps to an n-periodic sequence of overlapping four-bit states. Taking first coordinates of those states recovers a unique predecessor, and overlap enforces wraparound even for n<5. Independent enumeration directly from the polynomial gives 491 maps (ranks 16,10,8,6,4: 1,2,2,6,480); all sixteen length-four products have rank four. Left composition preserves the kernel when rank stays four, and the 480 maps divide into two closed kernel classes of 240. Each has ten four-point images, each image meets each kernel block once, and each fiber contains every one of the 24 possible block-to-image bijections. A fixed point must lie in the image, on which the map acts as a permutation, so three fixed points are excluded. The n=1,2,3 count vectors in order (0,1,2,4) are (1,0,1,0), (3,0,0,1), (1,6,1,0). Both classes are strongly connected and have coprime return walks of lengths 2 and 11. A block relabelling commutes with the left-composition transition and is transitive on each image fiber; uniqueness of the stationary distribution in a class forces uniformity conditional on the image. Each fiber gives (9,8,6,1)/24. Therefore the possibly unequal stationary class weights do not matter, and primitive finite-chain convergence gives an O(rho^n) error for some rho<1. The reverse evaluation order does not alter sampling for iid uniform letters. The result is for period dividing n, not least period n or rotation classes; finite direct checks alone do not prove the limit.

## originality

PASS

Best of checked knowledge, the contribution is the specific four-sheet periodic lifting law, not the polynomial, full-shift four-to-one result, or the general principle of group-extension equidistribution. Earlier group and automaton work does narrow the novelty to the rule-specific monoid and permutation calculation.

## value

PASS

The rule is a previously distinguished smallest-diameter, non-bipermutive four-to-one LEO example. Its same-period lifting spectrum and limiting attainable fraction 5/8 answer questions not settled by the known infinite degree or by a finite set of ring examples. The finite certificate gives a concrete case where the full symmetric-group distribution, rather than just a mean count, is visible, with an all-length prohibition on multiplicity three.
