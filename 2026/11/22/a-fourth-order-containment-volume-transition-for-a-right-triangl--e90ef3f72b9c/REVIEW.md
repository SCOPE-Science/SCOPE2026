# Review of the triangle-rectangle containment-volume theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim uses the ordinary four-real-parameter Lebesgue measure, not scale-angle Haar measure. The twelve linear inequalities (3) are exact for vertexwise containment. Their independent translation intervals have the widths in the proof; the four sign quadrants reduce the integral to two elementary double integrals. All three regimes of the auxiliary integral agree algebraically at their boundaries. The formula is positive, homogeneous of degree four in target dimensions, symmetric under interchange of the two rectangle sides and gives the independent values \(5/12\) at the square and \(11/8\) at aspect two. The combinatorial statement is covered by exhaustive exact rational intersection/feasibility arithmetic over the *entire* parameter intervals, not a sample search. An independent numerical four-dimensional hull implementation corroborated both claims. No statement about arbitrary triangles is asserted.

## Originality
**PASS, provisional historical priority.** Choi and Choi (2026) analyze silhouettes and maximal placements for triangle–polygon containment polytopes, while Agarwal–Amenta–Sharir (1998) and Chan–Hair (2024) give placement-computation algorithms and complexity statements. Their inspected statements do not give a four-volume spline or this aspect-ratio stratification. The closest explicit volume result in the public mathematical-record database, *A sharp equilateral extremum and a universal beta law for triangle self-containment polytopes* (2026, identifier 0f26f49ae237), assumes that the source and target are the **same triangle**; its triangular fiber homothety does not apply to a rectangular target. The exact formulas and their implication comparison were separately searched. General construction algorithms are not themselves the stated explicit phase law. Residual risk: an older unindexed containment-volume computation might cover this special family.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a complete, scale-covariant evaluation of a natural configuration-space invariant for the simplest genuinely non-self-containment triangle–rectangle family. It simultaneously classifies all vertex-count phases and isolates an exact fourth-order loss of smoothness at ratio two. The symmetric square has an exceptional vertex collapse without a volume singularity; this separates combinatorial phase changes from volumetric transitions in a concrete four-dimensional polytope family. The result is not merely a single parameter substitution, arbitrary numerical sample or an isolated volume constant. It does not claim a general theorem for every pair of polygons.
