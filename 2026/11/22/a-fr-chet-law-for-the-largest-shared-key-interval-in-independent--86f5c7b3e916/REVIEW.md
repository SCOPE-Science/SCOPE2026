# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim is about a uniformly randomized binary search tree on keys with fixed order. Every fringe-subtree key set is consecutive. The boundary-priority lemma gives exact single-interval probabilities. The finite sum of their squares gives the expectation. For the Poisson step, overlapping intervals must be laminar, adjacent intervals are mutually excluded, separated neighborhoods are independent, and the only non-nested remaining dependence has the stated exact beta integral. The nested and one-key-separated pair sums vanish at the \(n^{1/3}\) scale. The dependency-graph Poisson theorem plus tail first moments proves the Fréchet law. Exact rational priority-permutation checks passed 1,622 assertions for sizes two to seven. These checks are finite corroboration, not the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Classical studies by Feng–Mahmoud–Panholzer, Fuchs, Dennert–Grübel and Holmgren–Janson concern fringe sizes in a *single* random search tree. Their fixed-size Poisson critical window is \(\sqrt n\), not the two-independent-tree same-labelled-key extreme window of \(n^{1/3}\). A detailed prior survey covers the one-tree background; neither its theorem nor the closest indexed independent-fringe-size result supplies the joint spatial dependency bounds or resulting Fréchet law. Text and semantic searches for shared-key, common-fringe, Cartesian-tree and treap equivalents returned no decisive covering statement. A publication-priority risk remains because older complete texts or differently named results may contain equivalent two-tree laws.

**Value — PASS.** Shared consecutive-key fringe subtrees measure the size of an exactly reusable key block between independently built search-tree indexes. Unlike shape-isomorphic fringe subtrees, key-set agreement has an operational and probabilistic interpretation. The theorem identifies the correct nontrivial extreme scale and a complete limiting distribution, in contrast with the familiar one-tree scale. The finite expectation and Poisson limit together calibrate how much substructure can agree by chance.

**Closest sources and limits.** Holmgren and Janson, arXiv:1406.6883 (2014-06-26), and their 2016 preprint survey (Section 7.2, Theorem 7.10) establish the single-tree comparison. Feng–Mahmoud–Panholzer (2008) and Dennert–Grübel (2010) address other subtree-size regimes. These do not directly compare identical key sets in two independent trees. Some older full texts could not be inspected; no claim of exhaustive literature coverage or independent verification is made.
