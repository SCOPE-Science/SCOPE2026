# Scientific review: a fractional short-chord cusp law for the astroid

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof starts with the exact unit-speed astroid quadrant parameterization and circumference \(6\), giving the exact intrinsic-nearby baseline \(\varepsilon/3\). It controls same-quadrant extrinsic shortcuts by a monotone tangent-angle estimate with total variation \(\pi/2\), yielding only \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) unnormalized product-arclength mass. Across each cusp, the exact two-branch squared distance, bounded rescaled support, and an ordinary dominated-convergence argument give the sharp \(\varepsilon^{5/3}\) term. Four cusps and both ordered branch orientations yield the coefficient \(2^{1/3}B(1/3,3/2)/9\). The same \(L^1\) argument yields total variation convergence, and the beta/uniform change of variables gives the independent marks. An executable deterministic numerical check reconstructs finite-radius distances without replacing them by the limiting geometry. Neither the numerical checks nor the finite thresholds are used as a proof of the asymptotic.

## Originality

**PASS (provisional historical priority).** The 2012 full text by Uwe Bäsel studies motion-invariant random-line chords and **interior-uniform points in regular polygons**. The 2005 Gille study concerns invariant-line chords in a wedge and rod; only its abstract was available. The 2015 García-Pelayo paper concerns invariant-line chords in convex/concave polygons; only its abstract was examined. A related published ellipse-boundary analysis has independently sampled arclength endpoints, but treats **upper near-diameter tails**, not **lower near-zero cusp excess**. A related smooth-curve midpoint-depth result studies another observable and explicitly requires a smooth, positively curved oval. Thus these precise published statements neither cover the \(5/3\)-power astroid correction nor imply its conditional marked law. The title/alias/parameter searches revealed no equal or stronger formula, but a negative search cannot establish absolute priority. Unindexed geometric correlation papers and unavailable full texts are residual risks. The present result is not positioned as a completely verified universal novelty claim.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The exact leading short-arc contribution alone hides singularity geometry. The positive fractional-order next term detects the semicubical separation of tangent cusp branches and is sharply distinguishable from the usual smooth-arc correction scale. Its conditional uniform-region, beta and independent-uniform marks identify *how* the exceptional close pairs form, not merely their probability. This is a natural geometric invariant of a classical fixed algebraic curve, not an arbitrary numerical slice or a renaming of the intrinsic arclength law. A potential use is recognizing cusp order from short-pair correlations; no general inverse theorem or quantitative estimation guarantee is claimed.
