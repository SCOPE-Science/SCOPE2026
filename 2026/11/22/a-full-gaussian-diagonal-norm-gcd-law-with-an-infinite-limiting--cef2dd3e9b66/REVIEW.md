# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact prime-power root counts include both parity cases, all split/nonsplit odd primes, and higher prime powers. Reduction to the square-root equation has invertible change of variables at odd primes and the roots are Hensel-simple. CRT supplies all composite moduli. For the random box, finite-prime convergence and the explicit large-prime bound together establish full convergence; the infinite product is almost surely finite. The moment calculation is an exact convergent/divergent Euler-product argument relying on the standard divergence of reciprocal primes in the progression \(1\pmod4\). A standalone checker verifies direct congruence counts, with no extrapolation from tests to infinity.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
The general convergence theorem of Kabluchko and Marynych supplies a framework and is explicitly acknowledged. Bodin and Dèbes provide broader coprimality-density theory. The closest Gaussian-norm result is a deterministic distribution along a *fixed* primitive Gaussian line, and a related homogeneous-resultant theorem has incompatible inhomogeneous hypotheses. The inspected results do not specify this complete two-coordinate distribution and sharp infinite-mean boundary. Residual risk is an unindexed implicit or more general theorem yielding these explicit formulas.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete prime-power and global probability distributions answer a natural arithmetic question about adjacent Gaussian-lattice norms. Their exact moment frontier reveals a mathematically significant infinite-mean phenomenon not seen from coprimality density alone. This is an intrinsic structural statistic, not an arbitrary small computation.

## Verification and limits
The executable checker tests every modulus through 129 and samples three squares. Those finite tests support, but do not replace, the symbolic proof. No general rate for distributional convergence or asymptotic of finite-box means is asserted. The originality assessment relies on inspected primary literature and accessible research records, and does not prove uniqueness of publication.
