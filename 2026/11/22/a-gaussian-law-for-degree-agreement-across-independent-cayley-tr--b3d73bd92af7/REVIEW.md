# Scientific review

## Correctness
The claim specifies independent uniform labelled Cayley trees and fixed \(r\ge2\). The Prüfer encoding yields a complete finite pgf, exact first and second marked moments, and a first-order joint occupancy correction. Conditioning independent Poisson columns on their totals proves the Gaussian limit with variance equal to the covariance-matrix Schur complement; finite moment asymptotics independently identify the same variance. The strict sign and positivity arguments are explicit. A standalone exact checker passed 231 assertions. Tail estimates and a conditional Fourier limit require conventional analytic arguments; small enumeration is not an infinite proof.

## Originality
The full finite pgf, fixed-total covariance correction, and r-tree Gaussian variance concern labelled vertex-by-vertex degree agreement. Classical Prüfer/multinomial degree counts, single-sample occupancy variance, one-variable Poisson coincidences, random-tree isomorphism, and edge-set overlaps are nearby but do not state this same result in the inspected material. Searches under degree collisions, labelwise agreement, equal counts in independent multinomial occupancy, and equivalent Hamming agreement revealed no clearly dominating indexed claim. Generic conditional occupancy central limit methods can imply parts of the framework; historical or unindexed full coverage is a residual risk, not evidence of uniqueness.

## Value
Degree agreement at matching labels is a natural graph-comparison statistic when several independent labelled random trees are observed. Its finite pgf is a complete null law; the explicit, strictly negative covariance correction determines a nontrivial Gaussian sampling variance and prevents a naive independent-vertex uncertainty estimate. The r-fold extension supplies a coherent family rather than arbitrary isolated parameters, and the result is directly usable for calibration of degree-signature similarity.

## Limitations and closest sources
Frieze–Karoński supplies the classical random-tree encoding; Bogachev–Gnedin–Yakubovich studies occupancy variance; Nijimbere studies unconditioned Poisson coincidences. The results do not address correlated trees or growing numbers of trees. A publisher chapter was inspected through its available excerpt rather than unrestricted full text, and an earlier general coincidence theorem could cover the specific limit without using its terminology. No independent audit was undertaken.
