# Review of the shared-risk quorum reversal

## Correctness
PASS. Both schemes have identical expected intervention rates, so their prescribed expected costs cancel. The exact count distributions give the two achievement tails and the risk-weighted expected payoff formula. For every positive count, the public-to-independent likelihood ratio is strictly increasing, implying strict monotonicity of the conditional tail ratio. Strict Jensen convexity gives the lower endpoint, and the upper endpoint is explicit. The binomial law of large numbers and central limit theorem locate the two distinct thresholds, yielding the positive \(\sqrt{N}\) disagreement band. An exact six-agent example proves finite non-vacuity. The checker confirms finite identities, not the infinite theorem.

## Originality
PASS within the inspected literature and recoverable record. Zhou et al. (arXiv:2611.07627v1) study a common uncertain risk and adaptive risk-threshold actions, but do not give the two-protocol comparison, strict quorum ordering, or separated Gaussian cutoffs in the inspected main text. Fernández Domingos et al. (arXiv:2610.33895v1) study information about an uncertain quorum, which is a distinct information environment. De Jaegher's threshold-public-good work studies incentive dynamics, not this pair of protocol orderings. Literature on sums of Bernoulli mixtures gives broad distributional background; generic likelihood-ratio tools are recognized as prior mathematical ingredients. Priority remains conditional on unindexed results, especially broader shared-shock and dependent-Bernoulli theorems; no exhaustive originality certification is asserted.

## Value
PASS. A threshold public good under a common, observable disaster risk naturally raises two different evaluation questions: how often the quorum is met and how much risk-weighted loss is avoided. The exact construction proves that these objectives can rank two equally costly protocols oppositely, and quantifies the entire nontrivial, growing set of quorums where the disagreement occurs. It clarifies why success-frequency alone is an inadequate welfare proxy for risk-responsive collective action.

## Limitations
The independent benchmark is intentionally uninformative; comparing it with common observed risk does not isolate correlation as the cause, and does not establish superiority over equally informative independent signals. The result is one-shot and Bernoulli-group, not an evolutionary equilibrium or population-level behavioral prediction. The asymptotics require fixed interior parameters. Historical priority is literature-bounded; the general dependent-Bernoulli and information-design literature is extensive.
