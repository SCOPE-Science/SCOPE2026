# Review of the two-bridge conjunctive-cycle enumeration

## Correctness: PASS
The proof specifies a synchronous AND map on two labelled directed cycles, the two incoming arcs, and the exact meaning of a recurrent phase, phase fiber, orbit basin and temporal period. Backtracking the Boolean ancestry of every downstream vertex for \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\) steps gives precisely two residue classes modulo \(g=\gcd(a,b)\), proving the projection formula. Once projected, the gate never deletes a permitted downstream bit, so each ring rotates independently and every orbit is periodic by \(L\). The count by active upstream residue classes leads to a genuine two-state transfer matrix on \(h=\gcd(g,s)\) residue cycles, giving both recurrent-phase multiplicities and their individual inverse-image fiber sizes. Restricting the projection image to states invariant under \(t\) rotations, and collapsing their repeating words to lengths \(\gcd(a,t)\) and \(\gcd(b,t)\), proves the fixed-iterate transfer expression. Möbius inversion counts primitive periods, and the fiber cardinality yields the orbit-level basin formula. No infinite statement is based only on finite experiments.

Two separate self-contained Python verifiers passed: direct full functional-graph computations on \(115\) small triples and \(76{,}280\) original states, supplemented by complete \(65{,}536\)-state checks at each of three eight-by-eight offsets; and independent symbolic predecessor-set checks on \(8{,}450\) parameter triples. The three offsets yield recurrent counts \(2207\), \(2209\) and \(2401\), agreeing with the theorem.

## Originality: PASS, with a substantial close-prior qualification
Jarrah, Laubenbacher and Veliz-Cuba (arXiv:0805.0275, 2008), especially their Example 2.9 and Section 7, establish one-bridge examples, generic component-cycle bounds and fixed-point formulas. Chen, Gao and Başar (arXiv:1708.01975, 2017), **already prove the two-bridge Hadamard-product projection** in Section IV-B, Lemma 9, and an eventual-projection algorithm for general weakly connected networks in Theorem 2. Projection, periodic eventuality and generic component methods are explicitly credited as prior work, not discoveries here. Neither inspected text supplies the closed two-arc transfer polynomial, its full fixed-iterate and primitive-orbit population law, phase-fiber and orbit basin sizes, or the gcd-class exact invariance for moving the second bridge. These precise population invariants require a separate weighted directed-residue-cycle count and periodicity reduction. Actual searches for equivalent presentations, broader coverage and exact tabulations found no covering result. A historical equivalent and the proximity to the already-published projection remain genuine limitations, not proof of priority.

## Value: PASS
The number and position of incoming arcs matter for recurrent activity in coupled regulatory Boolean networks, so a closed all-order orbit and attraction-basin distribution is a natural dynamical invariant. The two-bridge interaction reduces to an arithmetic residue graph whose cycle decomposition explains why moving a single arc changes exact populations only at gcd thresholds. At equal eight-vertex cycle lengths it produces three distinct recurrent populations, while increasing only the downstream length by one switches the asymptotic survival probability from one to zero. The classification is infinite-family and quantitative rather than a restatement of the existing generic projection or a one-off example.
