# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** From the cited stochastic-update law, an \(O(\varepsilon)\) perturbation of each monopoly generates exactly two classes of one-event departures. Their zero-quality absorption probabilities determine the inter-monopoly rates. The transient fundamental matrix exists by the source's absorption theorem. A singleton absent incumbent engagement propagates along directed contact walks: the graph's cyclic classes prevent simultaneous absence at period above one, while positivity of a suitable matrix power makes a total blackout possible for primitive graphs. This proves the sharp \(B=0\) criterion and the quality-sum bias identity. The attached complete-state checker corroborates all terms on a three-node path and triangle. Its finite numerical checks are not substituted for an all-network proof.

**Originality — PASS (within accessible literature).** The original 2026 stochastic popularity paper addresses uniqueness of stationary behavior at positive quality and concentration on monopoly states as quality vanishes, but does not determine the relative low-quality stationary monopoly weights, nor their graph-period distinction. Related deterministic coupled-popularity and stochastic competitive-attention models use different state spaces/transition laws. The finite Markov perturbation and Perron–Frobenius arguments are standard; the sharp application linking period, total engagement blackout and quality-sum reversal is the assessed claim. Searches in published mathematical research records for the model, rare-quality fixation, equivalent stochastic perturbation and graph period returned no implication covering this statement. Incompletely indexed and access-restricted studies could still affect priority.

**Value — PASS.** The result answers a natural question left open by a theorem giving only the *support* of the small-quality stationary limit: how much time is spent in each dominant channel. It establishes a qualitative graph-theoretic criterion (connected bipartite versus odd-cycle networks for undirected contacts) and a quality-sensitive sign law across all fixed finite sizes. The direct consequence is not merely a recomputation of a Markov invariant: the support period precisely controls a formerly unsuspected route through the completely empty engagement state.

Closest source: Ravazzi, Frasca and Cocca, *Ergodic and Metastable Behavior of Stochastic Popularity Dynamics in Social Networks*, arXiv:2610.20021 (2026), sections defining the transition law and establishing zero-quality absorption and low-quality stationary concentration. Related earlier model papers: arXiv:2503.15170 and arXiv:2507.03448. The same source's results are required as premises, not contradicted.
