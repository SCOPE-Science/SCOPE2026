# Review

## Correctness (PASS)

The Poisson-clock embedding and shared label-sequence coupling give a finite-\(n\) mismatch error bounded by \(2(1+e)\sqrt{H_n/n}\) per other collector. Independent exponential thinning yields the exact beta-binomial mixture, while a conditional Poisson approximation and a deterministic shared-rate coupling contribute \(2/n\) and \(1/n\) per coordinate. The proof checks the parameter ranges \(n,q\ge2\), correctly preserves common-rate dependence and uses the total-variation convention. The finite exact-rational checker corroborates the component identities but does not replace the all-size argument.

## Originality (PASS, with risk)

The simultaneous independent two-collector results of Myers–Wilf (2003) concern finishing ties and ballot-like paths, not a joint missing-category vector or a uniform dimension-growing approximation. The Doumas–Papanicolaou (2014) sibling process transfers repeated coupons sequentially; its marginal deficit is not the independent-album statistic here. The negative-multinomial mixture is a classical distribution, and the elementary Poissonization ingredients are not separately claimed. The closest inspected primary material does not imply the explicit synchronized-collector joint total-variation bound; unindexed equivalent quantitative couplings remain a priority risk.

## Value (PASS)

The number of simultaneous reference albums can increase with the coupon alphabet while still yielding an explicit full-vector null model. Fixed-number marginal convergence would not establish the needed joint approximation for many collectors. The mixture quantifies common stopping-time dependence despite independent drawing and supplies a finite-size error budget. The result gives a usable mathematical dependence law, without claiming a sharp threshold.
