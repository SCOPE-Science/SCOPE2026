# Research review: Gumbel consensus and terminal shells in Boolean-cube OR dynamics

## Correctness — PASS
The update is precisely open-neighbor OR on the dimension-\(d\) binary cube. Exact-length walk reachability gives the full recurrence classification; alternating Hamming parity and the one-Lipschitz covering-radius maxima establish the first-entry clock. Counting singleton and Hamming-neighborhood-star supports with the double-counted pair correction gives both exact late shells for every integer \(d\ge3\). For the stochastic law, neighborhoods of distinct cube sites overlap only at Hamming distance two. Fixed factorial moments therefore converge to a Poisson law with mean \(e^{-2a}\), and the radius-two ball union bound gives two-step consensus with probability approaching one. Scripts independently exhaust small states, enumerate covering radii, and check bit-parallel simulations and factorial moments. No finite calculation is substituted for an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with qualified historical priority
General graph-walk and height results of Jarrah, Laubenbacher and Veliz-Cuba (arXiv:0805.0275) do not give the labelled terminal population formulas or the Bernoulli Gumbel critical window. The closest specialized available result, *Exact maximal-transient census for disjunctive dynamics on halved Boolean cubes* (published 2026-10-26), concerns the distance-two adjacency graph, whose parity components stay invariant per update, rather than the nearest-neighbor graph whose parity components swap. Its proof and principal result were inspected, not merely its title. Weichsel's perfect dominating sets concern an exact domination criterion, and Balogh–Bollobás–Morris use a different persistent high-threshold process. Weber's 1987 Poisson paper remains a real historical-priority risk: its available abstract addresses isolated structures in randomly thinned cubes, but full text was inaccessible. No definitive uniqueness or novelty certification is claimed.

## Value — PASS
The one-update consensus threshold \(p_d=1/2+a/d\) has a nondegenerate Gumbel profile rather than a deterministic step; by two updates consensus is overwhelmingly likely even at the window. This separates random typical dynamics from the sharp \(d-1\) worst-case clock. The final two rare-shell formulas give exact natural finite-size invariants and a structural explanation in terms of singleton and overlapping Hamming-star supports. These are motivated ensemble and extremal questions on a canonical graph family, not arbitrary parameter slices.

## Limits
The equations are for binary sites on the open-neighbor cube, not persistent bootstrap percolation or XOR dynamics. Earlier entrance-time populations are not fully enumerated. Some older, potentially equivalent probabilistic literature remains inaccessible; independent mathematical validation has not occurred.
