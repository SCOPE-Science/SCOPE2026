# A hexagon correction for ordinary five-cut complexes of girth-six graphs

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite simple undirected graph with \(n\ge10\) vertices and girth at least six. Forests have infinite girth; disconnected graphs are allowed. Write \(c_5(G)\) for the number of five-element vertex subsets inducing a connected subgraph of \(G\), and \(h_6(G)\) for the number of distinct unoriented six-cycles. Define
\[
B(G)=\binom{n-1}{4}-c_5(G)+h_6(G).
\]
Let \(\Delta_5(G)\) be the ordinary five-cut complex generated by the complements of disconnected induced five-vertex sets, and let \(D=\Delta_5(G)^\vee\) be its combinatorial Alexander dual. Then \(B(G)\) is a nonnegative integer, and the **complete integral homotopy types** are
\[
D\simeq\bigvee^{B(G)}S^3,\qquad
\Delta_5(G)\simeq\bigvee^{B(G)}S^{n-6}.
\]
The zero-fold wedge denotes a contractible point. All reduced integral homology groups vanish outside degree \(n-6\), where \(\widetilde H_{n-6}(\Delta_5(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{B(G)}\). The six-cycle term remains valid when hexagons intersect in edges or in paths of up to three edges.

## Assumptions and scope
Girth is the minimum length of a simple cycle. All complexes are abstract finite simplicial complexes with the empty face, and homotopy equivalence means ordinary topological homotopy equivalence of their geometric realizations. For \(U\subseteq V(G)\), the induced graph is denoted \(G[U]\). A vertex subset \(F\) is a face of the dual \(D\) exactly when every five-subset of \(F\) induces a connected graph; when \(|F|<5\), the condition is vacuous.

By girth at least six, every connected induced five-vertex subgraph is a tree. Every six-cycle is chordless. A connected five-set contained in a six-cycle consists of the path obtained by deleting one cycle vertex. The lower bound \(n\ge10\) is used to ensure that \(\Delta_5(G)\) has a complete two-skeleton, allowing the integral homology classification to be promoted to an actual homotopy equivalence.

## Proof
**1. Classify the dual faces.** The dual \(D\) contains all vertex subsets of size at most four. Its five-vertex faces are precisely the connected induced five-sets. Suppose a six-set \(W\) belongs to \(D\). Deleting any vertex from \(G[W]\) leaves a connected graph. Thus \(G[W]\) has no cut vertex and no isolated vertex; in particular, it has minimum degree at least two and contains a cycle. The girth assumption forces a cycle on all six vertices. There can be no additional edges, since they would be chords creating shorter cycles. Hence \(G[W]\) is an induced six-cycle. Conversely, each six-cycle has every five-vertex deletion connected and is a face of \(D\).

Two different induced six-cycles cannot share a five-set: that common induced five-set would be a path, and the two distinct completing vertices would each be adjacent to its two endpoints, producing a four-cycle. If \(D\) contained a seven-set, two of its different six-subsets would have to be six-cycles sharing five vertices, an impossibility. Consequently, \(D\) contains precisely the complete three-skeleton of the \((n-1)\)-simplex, the \(c_5(G)\) additional four-simplices on connected five-sets, and the \(h_6(G)\) five-simplices on induced hexagons, with no faces of larger size.

**2. The two-connector lemma.** Let \(F\) be a disconnected four-set. If \(F\cup\{x\}\) and \(F\cup\{y\}\) are two different connected five-sets, both \(x\) and \(y\) have a neighbor in every component of \(G[F]\). Choose two components and connect the corresponding two neighbors of \(x\) and \(y\) inside each component. Together with \(x\) and \(y\), those disjoint paths form a cycle involving at most six vertices. Girth at least six forces it to be an induced six-cycle using **all** four vertices of \(F\), and \(F\cup\{x,y\}\) is that six-cycle. A third connecting vertex \(z\) would similarly produce two different six-cycles sharing the connected five-set \(F\cup\{x\}\), contradicting Step 1. Therefore every disconnected four-set has at most two connected five-set extensions; if it has two, they are exactly the two relevant five-faces in one induced hexagon.

**3. Explicit elementary simplicial collapses.** First consider a connected induced five-set \(T\) not lying in any six-cycle. Its induced graph is a tree, so choose a nonleaf vertex \(v\). The four-set \(F=T\setminus\{v\}\) is disconnected. If \(F\) had another connected five-extension, Step 2 would put \(T\) in a six-cycle, contrary to the choice of \(T\). Hence \(F\) is a free codimension-one face of the four-simplex \(T\), not contained in any other maximal simplex, and the pair \((F,T)\) admits an elementary collapse.

For each induced six-cycle \(C\), write \(T_v=C\setminus\{v\}\) for the six distinct path-shaped five-faces. None is shared with another hexagon. Choose a root vertex \(r\in C\). The pair \((T_r,C)\) is an elementary collapse: \(T_r\) lies in no other five-simplex. The graph on the six vertices of \(C\) connecting **nonadjacent** cycle vertices is connected (it is the complement of the six-cycle). Choose any rooted spanning tree of this graph. Traverse its edges \(u\to v\) from an already visited vertex to an unvisited one. The four-set \(F_{uv}=C\setminus\{u,v\}\) is disconnected, and by Step 2 its only connected five-extensions are \(T_u\) and \(T_v\). Since \(T_u\) was already removed, \(F_{uv}\) is now a free face of \(T_v\), giving the elementary collapse \((F_{uv},T_v)\). All these collapses are disjoint between hexagons by Step 2; the private-face collapses of the remaining five-sets are also disjoint. Each hexagon contributes one \((5,4)\)-dimensional collapse and five \((4,3)\)-dimensional collapses. Each of the other \(c_5(G)-6h_6(G)\) connected five-sets contributes one \((4,3)\)-collapse. Thus \(D\) collapses onto a three-dimensional simplicial complex \(K\), removing exactly \(c_5(G)-h_6(G)\) three-faces while preserving every face of dimension at most two.

**4. Determine \(D\)'s integral homotopy type.** The full three-skeleton of a simplex is two-connected and has no reduced integral homology except in dimension three. Since every face of dimension at most three originally belongs to \(D\), its full two-skeleton makes \(D\) simply connected, and the presence of the full three-skeleton makes \(\widetilde H_2(D;\mathbb Z)=0\). The collapse in Step 3 gives a three-dimensional, two-connected \(K\). Its reduced Euler characteristic, computed directly from the face description, is
\[
\widetilde\chi(K)=\widetilde\chi(D)
=-\binom{n-1}{4}+c_5(G)-h_6(G)=-B(G).
\]
Hence \(\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{B(G)}\), with all other reduced integral homology zero; freeness also follows because \(K\) has no four-cells. The Hurewicz theorem gives spherical representatives for a basis of degree-three homology. Their wedge map is an integral homology equivalence between simply connected CW complexes and hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem. This proves \(D\simeq\bigvee^{B(G)}S^3\) and also \(B(G)\ge0\).

**5. Transfer to the ordinary cut complex.** Combinatorial Alexander duality gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(\Delta_5(G);\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H^{n-i-3}(D;\mathbb Z).
\]
Since the dual is a wedge of three-spheres, the only nonzero reduced integral group on the left is \(\mathbb Z^{B(G)}\) in degree \(i=n-6\). Step 1 shows that no set of seven or more vertices belongs to \(D\). Every vertex set of cardinality at most \(n-7\) consequently belongs to \(\Delta_5(G)\). In particular, for \(n\ge10\) the original cut complex has a full two-skeleton and is simply connected. It is pure of dimension \(d=n-6\). Its reduced homology vanishes below \(d\); induction with the Hurewicz theorem makes it \((d-1)\)-connected. A wedge of \(B(G)\) degree-\(d\) spherical representatives induces a homology isomorphism and is therefore a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem. The theorem follows.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using only Python 3's standard library. The script independently enumerates induced connected five-sets, six-cycles, all possible six- and seven-vertex dual faces, the complete disconnected-four-set extension relation, the actual private faces, and all hexagon-block collapses. It checks eight examples: two long cycles, a single hexagon with isolates, two disjoint hexagons, a three-hexagon theta graph with overlaps, the Heawood graph, the Möbius–Kantor graph, and the Pappus graph. The output in `verification_output.txt` was produced by executing the included code. A separate `independent_mod2.py` reconstructs the ordinary cut complexes directly from disconnected five-set complements and computes mod-two simplicial boundary ranks for five graphs of orders ten through twelve; `mod2_output.txt` records the exact agreeing Betti ranks. These exhaustive **finite** checks reproduce the published Heawood rank \(491\) and Pappus rank \(2074\), and compute rank \(1101\) for the Möbius–Kantor test; they do not substitute for the infinite-family proof in Steps 1–5.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* (arXiv:2304.13675, first public 2023-04-26), defined the ordinary cut complex and gave general face-lattice and Euler-characteristic results, but not this girth-six five-cut homotopy classification. Their sequel (arXiv:2407.08158) considers disjoint unions, squared paths, and grids, not this general family.

The publicly recorded *High-girth cut complexes are wedges of top-dimensional spheres* establishes the \(k\)-cut formula for girth at least \(k+2\). With \(k=5\), it only covers graphs of girth at least seven, where \(h_6(G)=0\). The publicly recorded *Pentagon correction for ordinary four-cut complexes of girth-five graphs* determines a parallel boundary phenomenon for \(k=4\), but neither its argument nor its statement covers these five-cut complexes. The independent finite Heawood and Pappus five-cut computations agree with the present formula, but classify two individual graphs, not all finite graphs of girth at least six. The present statement is a uniform exact hexagon correction with no edge-disjointness assumption. These comparisons are about logical coverage, not titles alone.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify graphs containing triangles, squares, or pentagons; it concerns ordinary five-cuts, not total cuts, and does not establish shellability, equivariant sphere splittings, or homotopy classifications for smaller orders. The elementary collapses on the dual provide a nonequivariant homotopy classification. No assertion of bibliographic uniqueness beyond inspected accessible literature is made; an unindexed equivalent statement remains possible. The provided finite checks are not a machine proof for arbitrary vertex count.
