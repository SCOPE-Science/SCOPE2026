# Integral homotopy types of ordinary five-cut complexes at the hexagonal girth boundary

## Finding

Let \(G\) be a finite simple undirected graph on \(n\ge10\) vertices with girth at least six; forests and disconnected graphs are allowed. Let \(c_5(G)\) count induced connected five-vertex subsets and \(h_6(G)\) count distinct unoriented six-cycles. Write \(\Delta_5(G)\) for the ordinary five-cut complex whose facets are the complements of disconnected induced five-vertex subsets. Let \(D=\Delta_5(G)^\vee\) be its combinatorial Alexander dual on the same vertex set.

Set \(B(G)=\binom{n-1}{4}-c_5(G)+h_6(G)\). Then \(D\) admits elementary simplicial collapses to a three-dimensional complex with its full two-skeleton intact, and
\[
D\simeq\bigvee^{B(G)} S^3,\qquad \Delta_5(G)\simeq\bigvee^{B(G)} S^{n-6}.
\]
Here \(B(G)\ge0\) and the empty wedge is a point. In particular there is no reduced integral homology in other degrees and no torsion. The collapses work even when distinct hexagons share edges or paths.

The face lattice and the hexagon term \(+h_6(G)\) in the signed reduced Euler characteristic were already established by Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes II*, Propositions 2.17–2.18 and Eq. (4), at \(k=5\). The assertion here is the explicit dual collapse and the full wedge homotopy types for the entire family, not the Euler correction itself.

## Proof

**Faces of the dual.** A subset is in \(D\) if all its five-subsets are connected. In particular \(D\) contains the entire three-skeleton of the simplex, and its four-simplices are the connected five-sets. Its five-simplices are precisely the induced hexagons. Indeed, if deleting any vertex from a six-set leaves a connected graph, that graph has minimum degree at least two, so by the girth assumption it is a six-cycle and has no chords. Conversely every hexagon has this property. Two hexagons cannot share a five-set: that common set would induce a path, and the two extra vertices adjacent to its endpoints would create a four-cycle. Therefore there is no seven-set in \(D\). This is the dual form of the face description in Bayer et al., and the verification also includes the case with no hexagons.

**A restriction on extensions of disconnected four-sets.** Suppose \(F\) is a disconnected four-set and both \(F\cup\{x\}\) and \(F\cup\{y\}\) induce connected graphs, with \(x\ne y\). Each of \(x,y\) meets each component of \(G[F]\). In each of two components choose a path between a neighbor of \(x\) and a neighbor of \(y\), allowing a path of length zero if they coincide. The two paths are disjoint and, with \(x,y\), form a simple cycle on at most six vertices. The girth bound forces it to use all six vertices, and hence \(F\cup\{x,y\}\) is an induced hexagon. A third connector \(z\) would force distinct hexagons sharing the five-set \(F\cup\{x\}\), which is impossible. The two five-set extensions of such an \(F\) therefore both belong to its unique hexagon.

**Elementary collapses.** For a connected five-set \(T\) not in a hexagon, delete a nonleaf of the induced tree to obtain a disconnected four-set \(F\). By the preceding restriction, \(T\) is its only connected five-extension. Since \(T\) has no larger coface, \(F\) is free and the pair \((F,T)\) collapses.

For each hexagon \(C\), let \(T_v=C\setminus\{v\}\). Choose a root \(r\in C\) and collapse the pair \((T_r,C)\), since no other hexagon contains \(T_r\). Choose a rooted spanning tree in the complement of the six-cycle graph on \(C\), which is connected. Traverse the tree in parent-before-child order. For each edge \(u\to v\), the four-set \(F_{uv}=C\setminus\{u,v\}\) is disconnected because \(u,v\) were not adjacent on the cycle. Its only connected five-extensions are \(T_u,T_v\); after the parent is removed, \((F_{uv},T_v)\) is a free pair. There is no conflict with another hexagon: a disconnected four-face used in this way has precisely the two extensions within \(C\), so cannot occur as a disconnected four-face of any other hexagon. A four-face private to a nonhexagonal five-set cannot be used in a hexagon either. Different tree edges in one hexagon give different four-faces. These facts give a globally valid elementary-collapse sequence.

Each hexagon removes one five-simplex with one four-simplex and then five more four-simplices with five three-faces. The nonhexagonal five-sets each remove one three-face. The result is a complex \(K\) of dimension at most three with every face of dimension at most two preserved. Exactly \(c_5(G)-h_6(G)\) three-faces were removed.

**Homotopy classification.** The full two-skeleton of \(D\) makes it simply connected, and its full three-skeleton gives \(\widetilde H_2(D;\mathbb Z)=0\). Thus \(K\) is two-connected by Hurewicz. It has no homology above degree three or below it in reduced degrees, and its degree-three homology is free, being the kernel of an integer boundary map with no incoming boundary. The sign and rank follow from the face count: the complete three-skeleton has reduced Euler characteristic \(-\binom{n-1}{4}\), and the remaining faces change it by \(c_5(G)-h_6(G)\). Hence the rank is \(B(G)\). Choose representatives of an integral basis via Hurewicz and take their wedge map; it is a homology equivalence of simply connected spaces and thus a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem.

Integral combinatorial Alexander duality gives \(\widetilde H_i(\Delta_5(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{n-i-3}(D;\mathbb Z)\). Thus \(\Delta_5(G)\) has free reduced homology only in degree \(d=n-6\) with rank \(B(G)\). Because \(D\) has no seven-set, \(\Delta_5(G)\) contains every set of size at most \(n-7\), including its full two-skeleton since \(n\ge10\). It is simply connected. Applying Hurewicz successively gives \((d-1)\)-connectivity; representing the degree-\(d\) basis by spheres and applying the homological Whitehead theorem now gives the claimed wedge. If the rank is zero, the point map gives the same conclusion. This argument uses homology concentration and connectivity, not only the Euler characteristic.

## Prior work and scope

- M. Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs*, [arXiv:2304.13675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13675), gives the ordinary cut-complex definition. Their sequel, [*Topology of Cut Complexes II*, arXiv:2407.08158v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08158v1#S2.Thmtheorem18), Propositions 2.17–2.18, gives the boundary-girth face lattice and signed Euler count, without any hexagon-disjointness hypothesis. That count does not establish the homology concentration or the collapse proved here.
- The high-girth wedge result for ordinary \(k\)-cuts assumes girth at least \(k+2\); at \(k=5\) this excludes hexagons. The ordinary four-cut results at the pentagonal boundary concern a different cut parameter. The previously recorded Heawood and Pappus five-cut wedge results are individual instances of the present classification. The comparisons to these related findings are at the level of indexed mathematical statements.
- The theorem is about ordinary five-cuts, not total cuts. It makes no claim about smaller girth, smaller orders, shellability, or equivariant homotopy. The comparison is limited to the literature inspected.
