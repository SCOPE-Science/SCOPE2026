# Scientific assessment of the integral homotopy classification

**Correctness.** Two connected five-extensions of a disconnected four-set force a unique hexagon through its four vertices and the two connectors. Thus each nonhexagonal connected five-set has a private disconnected four-face, and the six five-faces of a hexagon collapse in spanning-tree order in the complement of the cycle. The local sequences do not interfere at overlaps. The remaining dual is two-connected and three-dimensional at most, so its Euler characteristic determines the rank of its free top homology. Integral Alexander duality supplies the homology of the original complex; its complete two-skeleton is what allows the wedge conclusion via Hurewicz and Whitehead. Finite examples verify the local moves and counts without standing in for this proof.

**Originality.** Bayer et al. already prove the complete codimension-two skeleton, missing-face classification, and signed Euler formula at girth \(k+1\), including its \(k=5\) specialization with overlapping hexagons. The contribution is restricted to the explicit collapse and integral homotopy classification. No covering theorem for that conclusion was identified in the inspected sources. The girth-at-least-seven five-cut theorem and the pentagonal four-cut classification are related but do not imply this boundary case as stated. Individual Heawood and Pappus homotopy types are prior special cases.

**Value.** The theorem uses the known signed Euler count to specify an integral wedge only after proving the missing homology concentration and connectivity. It classifies a graph family at the first boundary outside the higher-girth wedge theorem, with an explicit construction valid at overlapping hexagons. It replaces a collection of individual wedge computations with a general argument; it is not a new enumeration of hexagons or Euler characteristics.
