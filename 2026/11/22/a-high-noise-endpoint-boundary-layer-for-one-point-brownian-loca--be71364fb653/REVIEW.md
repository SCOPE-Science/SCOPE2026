# Scientific assessment

**Correctness: PASS.** The full calculation starts from the occupation-density normalization and Gaussian regression, derives a nonnegative and convergent information series with explicit coefficients, and uses derivative signs plus uniform \(C^2\) asymptotics to prove eventual uniqueness. A separate numerical integral checks the central nonstandard coefficient identity. The two-term sampling expansion is obtained by a controlled perturbation of the leading first-chaos equation. No uniqueness at arbitrary small noise is asserted.

**Originality: PASS (qualified).** The earlier conditional-mean literature studies high-frequency grids and stable-process local-time errors, and does not imply the noisy one-point placement law. Relevant historical mathematical results on local-time covariance and Brownian bridges have different processes and quantities. The present exact rank-one information series and high-noise location boundary are not direct restatements of those high-frequency results. The comparisons do not establish universal historical priority; older one-point design sources may still exist.

**Value: PASS.** Brownian local time is a natural occupation-density functional for which exact data are unavailable under sparse observation. The result gives an explicit all-noise design objective and the sharp interior-to-endpoint boundary layer with a next-order correction and explained-variance scale. These facts govern exactly what a single expensive noisy measurement can recover; they are not merely an arbitrarily selected numerical constant or a textbook polynomial substitution.

Closest literature: Ivanovs–Podolskij (2020), Altmeyer–Le Guével (2021), Amorino–Jaramillo–Podolskij (2022). Limits include fixed-known Brownian dynamics, one nonadaptive point, Gaussian noise, and high-noise uniqueness only. The standalone tests are supportive finite checks, not proof certificates.
