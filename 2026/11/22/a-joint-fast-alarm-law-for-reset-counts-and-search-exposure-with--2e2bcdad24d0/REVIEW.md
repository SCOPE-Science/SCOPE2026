# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact marked renewal identity follows by partitioning every independent search/return cycle into direct search success, return success and restart. The killed half-line Brownian resolvent and OU boundary-value equations give exact one-cycle kernels and the one-sided derivative data at home. Their first boundary-layer moments rigorously produce the joint Laplace transform, with \(s=q=0\) continuity and positive normalization. The Poisson compensator shows that the normalized alarm count and accumulated active-search time become identical in probability. Differentiation of the limiting transform with OU integrating factors gives a strictly positive finite covariance at positive trap stiffness. The included numerical program independently tests exact identities, boundary-layer coefficients, marked transforms and covariance derivatives. No finite computation substitutes for the proof.

The closest indexed items instead concern strong-reset mean corrections, weak-return trap stiffness, or local time of *instantaneously* reset processes. ## Value — PASS

The finding distinguishes physical progress from the apparent number of interruption events: infinitely frequent alarms correspond to a diverging count but vanishing active-search exposure. It supplies a full joint law and proves *strictly positive* correlation with completion time, making the alarm counter an informative but non-independent diagnostic. The harmonic-trap scale function gives its exact exponential scale, with clear relevance to switching-trap trajectory measurements. This is more than a restatement of the known limiting mean or a generic geometric-count identity.

## Scope and independent status

The covariance is for the limiting pair only; no finite-rate covariance convergence or uniform parameter extension is asserted. The zero-stiffness boundary has infinite mean completion time. Stronger unindexed prior work could affect novelty attribution.
