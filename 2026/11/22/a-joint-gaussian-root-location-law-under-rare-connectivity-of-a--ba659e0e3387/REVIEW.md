# Review of the conditional nearest-neighbor root-location theorem

## Correctness

**PASS.** Every missing interior edge is a strict local maximum of three consecutive spacings, and the no-peak event is exactly a valley permutation. There are \(2^{n-2}\) eligible rank permutations, yielding the exact probability and independent fair placement-bit representation. The independent spacing-rank decomposition is valid for the symmetric Dirichlet law. Conditional moments follow from the explicitly proved minimum-spacing survival function and ordinary Dirichlet first/second moments. The conditional Lindeberg argument controls the largest weight, limiting empirical sums of weights and squared weights, and the negligible exterior spacing contribution. The exact-rational independent verifier passes all cases \(n=2,\ldots,9\). No finite test substitutes for the analytic infinite-\(n\) proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with historical-priority qualification.** The principal primary sources address the structure of nearest-neighbor components (Eppstein–Paterson–Yao), on-line nearest-neighbor total weighted length and ancillary standard directed-graph length moments (Penrose–Wade), or generic/typical connected-component behavior (Linderman et al.). None of their inspected arguments gives the specified rare-connectivity conditional distribution of both the reciprocal-pair index and physical midpoint, nor its explicit joint Gaussian covariance. Semantically related published results on count–length covariances and mutual-neighbor counts were located and compared; these statistics do not determine the conditional bivariate root location. Some related historical works were not fully accessible. The distinction between mathematical nonimplication and incomplete historical coverage is maintained.

## Value

**PASS.** A connected one-nearest-neighbor graph is a naturally extreme connectivity configuration, and its unique reciprocal pair is the root toward which all directed branches flow. The exact \(2^{n-2}/(n-1)!\) rarity, independent binomial root rank, exact bivariate finite moments and strongly correlated joint spatial/rank Gaussian limit jointly characterize this geometry. In particular, conditioning produces midpoint variance \(1/(2n)\), twice the conventional order-statistic variance scale \(1/(4n)\) near the interval center. This is a motivated joint location law for a standard graph rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest primary papers are Penrose–Wade, arXiv:math/0603561v1, and Eppstein–Paterson–Yao, *On Nearest-Neighbor Graphs*. The later open-access analysis by Linderman et al. treats general \(k\)-nearest-neighbor component formation. Adjacent nearest-neighbor covariance results found in mathematical records address different observables, and their inaccessible full text remains a residual priority risk. No independent review, general-density extension, or rate of joint normal approximation is asserted.
