# Review: joint-rank commuting census

## Correctness
**PASS (self-review).** The foundational normal form and four multiplication cases are explicitly attributed to the primary work. For fixed rooted kernel fibers, a direct choice of image set, involution on that image, and one-step attachments gives each local count. An exhaustive type-by-type commutation comparison determines the bivariate summands. The partition exponential formula follows by rooted labeled block counting, not by extrapolating computed values. Exact independent implementations of the finite-sum and exponential-formula recurrences agree through degree eight; actual endomorphism evaluations check all standard-form pairs through four letters and sampled pairs at five letters. These computations supplement, rather than replace, the general proof.

## Originality
**PASS relative to checked primary literature and accessible prior mathematical records.** The Schein--Teclezghi classification and Gould--Grau--Johnson multiplication/Green results cover individual semigroup endomorphisms and their ranks, but the inspected relevant primary sections do not tabulate commuting ordered pairs stratified by both ranks. Previously existing work on commuting *idempotent* endomorphisms is strictly narrower: at degree five the present census includes \(2535\) additional nonidempotent singular endomorphisms. The thesis chapter was examined as well as the full text of the 2023 arXiv source. Historical-priority uncertainty remains for the complete 1998 article and unindexed variants of this enumeration.

## Value
**PASS.** The subject is the canonical singular ideal of a central finite semigroup family, rather than an arbitrary special parameter slice. The new complete two-rank census identifies how its even, odd and nonpermutation strata jointly affect commutativity and yields a reusable all-degree generating function. At degree five it provides a \(3\times3\) exact joint-rank matrix, rather than only a marginal count. This is a structural enumeration of nonregular endomorphisms, with natural future applications to commuting probabilities and centralizer statistics.

## Limits
Unit automorphisms of the endomorphism monoid are deliberately excluded, and the dimension-four exceptional family is not covered. The 1998 original full text was not confirmed to have been inspected in entirety, so full historical priority is not claimed. Numerical checks alone are not infinite proofs.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
