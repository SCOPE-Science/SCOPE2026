# A Jordan-edge logarithmic–logarithmic tipping delay in a planar nonsmooth fold

## Finding
Consider the planar, continuous, two-branch affine map
\[
F_\mu(x,y)=\begin{cases}
\big((1+\delta-\alpha)x+y+\mu,-\delta x\big),&x<0,\\
\big((1+\delta+\alpha)x+y+\mu,-\delta x\big),&x\geq0.
\end{cases}
\]
Fix \(0<\alpha<1\), \(h>0\), and \(0<\delta\leq(1-\sqrt\alpha)^2\). Start at \((x_0,y_0)=(-h,0)\), and for \(\mu>0\) define the **actual first-passage index**
\[
\mathcal T_\mu=\min\{n\geq0:x_n\geq h\},\qquad
L_\mu=\log\frac{\alpha h}{\mu}.
\]
Define
\[
\tau_-=1+\delta-\alpha,\quad\tau_+=1+\delta+\alpha,
\qquad\gamma=\frac{\tau_++\sqrt{\tau_+^2-4\delta}}2>1.
\]
For strict overdamping, \(0<\delta<(1-\sqrt\alpha)^2\), put
\[
\lambda=\frac{\tau_-+\sqrt{\tau_-^2-4\delta}}2\in(0,1).
\]
Then, as \(\mu\downarrow0\) with \(\alpha,\delta,h\) fixed,
\[
\boxed{\mathcal T_\mu=\left(\frac1{|\log\lambda|}+\frac1{\log\gamma}\right)L_\mu+O(1).}
\]
At the **critical damping boundary** \(\delta=(1-\sqrt\alpha)^2\), write \(t=1-\sqrt\alpha\in(0,1)\). The left-branch characteristic multipliers coalesce into the defective double root \(t\), and the passage law changes to
\[
\boxed{\mathcal T_\mu=\left(\frac1{|\log t|}+\frac1{\log\gamma}\right)L_\mu+\frac{\log L_\mu}{|\log t|}+O(1).}
\]
These are two-term, bounded-remainder asymptotics. The extra positive \(\log\log(1/\mu)\) delay exists precisely at the Jordan boundary of the stated parameter range. For all sufficiently small \(\mu\), the trajectory switches from the negative to the nonnegative half-plane exactly once, then increases strictly in the first coordinate until passage.

## Assumptions and scope
The parameters \(\alpha\), \(\delta\) and \(h\) are fixed while \(\mu\downarrow0\); all maps are considered on the whole real plane. The left and right matrices are \(A_-=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+\delta-\alpha&1\\-\delta&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(A_+=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+\delta+\alpha&1\\-\delta&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). The inequalities \(0<\delta\leq(1-\sqrt\alpha)^2\) guarantee two positive stable left multipliers or one repeated stable left multiplier. The right matrix has positive eigenvalues \(\gamma>1\) and \(\zeta=\delta/\gamma\in(0,1)\). The branchwise affine fixed points have respective first coordinates \(+\mu/\alpha\) and \(-\mu/\alpha\), both virtual, so the map exhibits a nonsmooth-fold collision at \(\mu=0\).

The result concerns one physically distinguished incoming section \((x,y)=(-h,0)\), not every initial condition and not general nonlinear perturbations. No expansion uniform in \(\alpha\), \(\delta\), or \(h\) is asserted.

## Proof
Put \(q=\mu/\alpha\). On the left branch the second coordinate obeys \(y_n=-\delta x_{n-1}\) for \(n\geq1\). Because \(y_0=0\), the formal left-branch recurrence starts with \(x_{-1}=0\) and has the exact solution of
\[
x_{n+1}=\tau_-x_n-\delta x_{n-1}+\mu,
\quad x_{-1}=0,\quad x_0=-h.
\]
Its affine fixed point is \(q>0\). If \(\delta<(1-\sqrt\alpha)^2\), let \(0<\rho<\lambda<1\) be the roots of \(z^2-\tau_-z+\delta\). The solution is
\[
x_n=q-A\lambda^n-B\rho^n,\quad
A=\frac{\lambda(h+q)-\delta q}{\lambda-\rho}>0,\quad
B=\frac{\delta q-\rho(h+q)}{\lambda-\rho}<0.
\]
For each fixed \(n\), the limit as \(q\to0\) is \(-h(\lambda^{n+1}-\rho^{n+1})/(\lambda-\rho)<0\). Thus the first nonnegative index \(N_\mu\) tends to infinity. The terms \(A\lambda^n+B\rho^n\) are positive and asymptotic to \(A\lambda^n\), with \(A\to h\lambda/(\lambda-\rho)>0\). At the first crossing, \(A\lambda^{N_\mu}+B\rho^{N_\mu}\leq q\) and the preceding value exceeds \(q\). The successive ratio tends to \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), so
\[
N_\mu=\frac{L_\mu}{|\log\lambda|}+O(1),\quad
x_{N_\mu}=O(q),\quad x_{N_\mu-1}=O(q).
\]
The constants in the last two estimates are for fixed parameters.

At critical damping, the left characteristic polynomial is \((z-t)^2\), since \(\tau_-=2t\) and \(\delta=t^2\). The **exact** left-branch solution is
\[
x_n=q-\big[h+q+n\big(h+(1-t)q\big)\big]t^n.
\]
It is negative at each fixed \(n\) as \(q\downarrow0\), and tends to \(q>0\) as \(n\to\infty\). The positive subtracted term is \((hn+O(h+qn))t^n\). Let \(a=|\log t|\). Its first crossing of level \(q\) has, by comparing adjacent terms of asymptotic ratio \(t\),
\[
aN_\mu-\log N_\mu=L_\mu+O(1).
\]
As \(N_\mu\sim L_\mu/a\), this gives
\[
N_\mu=\frac{L_\mu+\log L_\mu}{a}+O(1),
\quad x_{N_\mu}=O(q),\quad x_{N_\mu-1}=O(q).
\]
The \(\log L_\mu\) term is generated by the factor \(n\) in the Jordan-block transient; it is absent when the left multipliers are distinct.

It remains to control the **actual** trajectory after crossing, rather than extend an inadmissible affine branch. For \(n=N_\mu\), \(x_n\geq0>x_{n-1}\). Whenever the right branch is used,
\[
x_{n+1}-x_n=\alpha x_n+\delta(x_n-x_{n-1})+\mu>0.
\]
Induction proves \(x_{n+1}>x_n\geq0\) thereafter. In particular the switching line is never recrossed. On this right branch the virtual fixed first coordinate is \(-q\); let \(u_k=x_{N_\mu+k}+q\) for \(k\geq-1\). Then
\[
u_{k+1}=\tau_+u_k-\delta u_{k-1},\qquad
u_k=C_\mu\gamma^k+D_\mu\zeta^k,
\]
where
\[
C_\mu=\frac{\gamma u_0-\delta u_{-1}}{\gamma-\zeta}.
\]
The crossing estimates yield \(u_0\in[q,2q]\), \(u_{-1}=O(q)\) and \(u_{-1}<q\); hence
\[
\frac{\gamma-\delta}{\gamma-\zeta}q
\leq C_\mu\leq C_1q,\qquad |D_\mu|\leq C_2q
\]
for positive constants independent of sufficiently small \(\mu\). Since \(\gamma>1>\zeta>0\), the additional number \(K_\mu\) of iterates required to reach \(x\geq h\) satisfies
\[
K_\mu=\frac{L_\mu}{\log\gamma}+O(1).
\]
Finally \(\mathcal T_\mu=N_\mu+K_\mu\), which establishes both formulas with bounded, not merely sublinear, remainders. \(\square\)

## Verification
The algebraic identities for the two left-branch solutions, the right-branch root decomposition and the inequalities governing the unique crossing constitute the proof. The accompanying standalone Python checker uses exact rational orbit arithmetic, verifies the critical Jordan solution at every pre-switch index, and checks the predicted bounded remainders for two determinant values and four displacement scales each. Execute `python3 CHECKER.py` with the Python standard library. These eight finite trajectories support reproducibility but do not substitute for the above all-parameter argument.

## Relationship to prior work
The map is the equal-determinant subfamily of the canonical planar continuous piecewise-linear map given by Simpson in section 3, equation (3.6), of *Nonsmooth folds as tipping points* [1]. Theorem 1 of that paper proves divergence when both branch fixed points are virtual, without an asymptotic passage law. A prior study of nonlinear scalar feedback in the same normal form establishes a universal local finite-time exit upper bound of order \(\mu^{-1}\); that bound neither implies the sharp logarithmic scale nor the critical Jordan logarithmic–logarithmic correction. The present result isolates a spectral singularity at the stable double multiplier and compares it with strictly overdamped evolution; it is not a proof of the conjecture on arbitrary nonlinear higher-order perturbations from [1].

Lamba and Budd [2] concern Lyapunov-exponent scaling at nonsmooth bifurcations, not the fixed-initial-condition first passage studied here. Simpson's repeated-unit-multiplier normal-form work [3] concerns accumulation of periodic solutions, with a different multiplier value and parameter problem. The available evidence therefore does not imply the present two-term exit laws; absence of an indexed match is not proof of priority.

## Limitations
No claim is made for oscillatory left multipliers, the endpoint \(\delta=0\), varying initial points, parameter-dependent target sections, general nonlinear perturbations, or a uniform joint limit in \(\mu\) and \(\delta\). The \(O(1)\) terms can oscillate with logarithmic parameter phase and are not claimed to converge. The right-hand flight time is controlled only up to bounded error. Literature inspection cannot exclude unindexed or inaccessible equivalent statements.
