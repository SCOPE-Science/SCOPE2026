# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof establishes an exact all-tail finite-difference criterion by a signed but absolutely summable geometric moment expansion, then shows strict threshold monotonicity. Uniform coefficient comparison and a double-sided geometric-saddle bound give the second-order log-log asymptotic; no finite test is treated as an infinite proof. The hypotheses \(b<\min(1,p^2a)\) and \(a<p^{-1}\) ensure the asserted coefficient signs and convergence. Exact-rational diagnostics and independently evaluated finite differences were executed with `check.py`.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Benhida–Curto–Exner (arXiv:2309.05888; arXiv:2405.15000) already supplies the geometric family, complete-hyperexpansivity sector and atomic sign patterns. Those premises and the qualitative non-CHE implication are not claimed. The assessed contribution is the global exact finite-order threshold and its sharp logarithmic plus log-log divergence. The related safe-quotient article (arXiv:2312.06390) studies different properties. Searches for aliases, charge inequalities, equivalent moment formulations, stronger sector results and order cutoffs found no precise quantitative coverage, but a non-indexed or partially inaccessible historical source might.

## Mathematical value — PASS

How many orders of complete alternatingness survive a small crossing of a natural sector boundary is a direct quantitative question motivated by difficult finite-order testing in operator theory. The extra positive log-log term is structurally forced by a geometric saddle over atomic charge locations and is missed by the naive first-negative-atom estimate. The result is valid for a continuum of weighted shifts, not a handpicked finite list.

## Limits

The remainder is bounded but not resolved to a constant; the proof applies with \(p,a\) fixed and \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Finite diagnostics are auxiliary and independent historical mathematical validation has not occurred.
