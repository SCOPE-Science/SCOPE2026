# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The free propagator is transformed exactly to a Gamma random variable, stationary resetting contributes an independent exponential age, and moments follow from Gamma identities. A Weierstrass-product factor has an explicitly positive derivative on the whole parameter interval. The log-product limit follows from weak convergence of the Gamma shape, with characteristic function and the fourth-moment coefficient checked independently. The accompanying executable confirms polynomial identities and finite numeric cross-checks; these are not substitutes for analytical arguments.

## Originality

**PASS, with publication-priority uncertainty.** The exact stationary position law, propagator, and qualitative bimodality of the motivating paper are prior results. The located primary text does not establish the centered non-Gaussian strong-sluggishness layer or monotone full-range inverse kurtosis theorem. A subsequent paper studies window maxima and last-passage times, not the stationary one-position boundary layer. The proposed finding is the joint universal boundary-layer law and strict calibration property, not the known Bessel density. Algebraic consequences of the published density remain a natural priority risk, as are unindexed equivalent Gamma-product asymptotics.

## Value

**PASS.** The full stationary law can look almost two-point at strong sluggishness, making variance-scale measurements singularly insensitive to internal fluctuations. The exact logarithmic boundary layer retains non-Gaussian fluctuations and quantifies their width, while the strictly monotone kurtosis supplies an observable calibration over the full exponent range. This is a motivated structural characterization of fluctuation shape and identifiability, beyond listing moments or plotting a stationary density.

## Closest literature and limits

Frimpong, Bresque and Roldán (arXiv:2610.21962) prove the fixed-parameter Bessel density and qualitative sharpening; Frimpong (arXiv:2610.34568) addresses maxima and global times. The proof does not extend to other origin conditions, stochastic calculi, or imperfect measurement models. Search coverage is not a proof of global publication priority, and model-specific inverse consequences may be implicit in existing Mellin-transform literature.
