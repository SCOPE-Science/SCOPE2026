# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The sparse-range facet theorem and explicit exposed-vertex description supply all facets and all vertices. At each vertex precisely \(m\) different facets are tight, making the polytope simple. The proof checks the necessity and sufficiency of all five possible facet-pair incidence types, calculates squared normal cosines exactly, and enumerates each ridge once. Dominance estimates use \(n=o(m)\) and \(n\to\infty\); the right-to-obtuse weight is \(2^{n-1}/m\). For the conditional CLT, a random lower upper-facet subset is asymptotically binomial, and the angle map has derivative one at one half. All edge cases \(n=2\) and \(m=n+2\) remain covered by the finite proof. A fresh executable checker verified 14 parameter cases, 17,218 vertices and 6,789 facet-adjacency ridges with exact integer incidence and squared-cosine comparisons; this is supportive, not a substitute for the all-order proof. Principal caveat: the *completeness* of the established sparse facet list depends on the cited full published proofs, not just the listed inequalities.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
The two original primary papers establish polytope definition, facets, vertices, and face-incidence combinatorics, but not an explicit complete Euclidean angle measure or a logarithmic critical transition. The closest published generalized-parking ridge-angle theorem was inspected in full: it assumes positive parking parameters and provides a different Gaussian fluctuation of variance \(3/8\); its partial-permutohedron correspondence is restricted to \(n\ge m-1\), whereas the theorem here requires \(n\le m-2\). Separately inspected partial-permutohedron edge-spectrum and mean-width records concern different observables. Searches also included full-claim aliases, subset-chain and truncated-permutohedron terminology. None of the inspected statements implies this log-scale two-atom law. Residual risk: a nonindexed specialized source or a strong general angle-geometry theorem could subsume this conclusion; absence from search is not proof of global priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The transition is not a routine finite table. It is a sharp infinite-family geometric effect caused by competing numbers of coordinate–upper and nested-upper ridges: its critical scale is logarithmic, and its two limiting angles remain macroscopically separated. The conditional \(1/4\)-variance normal law also distinguishes the sparse geometry from its neighboring parking-type regime. The invariant is natural for Euclidean polytope curvature and ridge-angle statistics; it is not an arbitrary sliced statistic. Known face classification is openly used as a starting point. The novelty and utility lie in the exact angle-weight law and the proved new asymptotic competition.
