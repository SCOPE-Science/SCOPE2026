# Scientific assessment

## Correctness (PASS)

The prior bijective length formula is applied with its zero-length exception. Cayley–Hamilton supplies an exact order-two recurrence with independently specified initial conditions for every phase; strict Perron positivity gives ordered distinct dominant constants, including the last-to-first wraparound. Since the subordinate eigenvalue has modulus below one, the perturbed thresholds differ from ideal geometric thresholds by less than half an integer eventually. This yields the finitely many exceptional integers per period, the sharp branchwise extrema, and, after harmonic logarithmic-phase equidistribution, the stated absolutely continuous law. Geometric-annulus integration provides the ordinary-sampling phase family; its nonconvergent mean follows from a differential identity. A direct palindromic-closure oracle and 360,000 integer prefix-count checks exercise the normalization and finite-index boundaries; their finite coverage is not treated as a proof.

## Originality (PASS against inspected evidence)

Hamoud, *Lengths and Counting of Palindromic Prefixes of Standard Sturmian Words* (arXiv:2611.20221v1), proves the precise palindromic-length indexing and a logarithmic count with an \(O(1)\) error, and explains why that error is not \(o(1)\). Its full text does not give the phase-dependent error law, an exact pushed-forward probability density, or the sharp two-sided error extrema for every periodic directive. Fischler, *Palindromic prefixes and episturmian words* (2006), addresses ratio-growth invariants rather than these harmonic statistics. De Luca–Fici, *Open and Closed Prefixes of Sturmian Words* (2013), classifies a different prefix property. A disguised general geometric-renewal theorem may cover the distributional mechanism, and an unindexed word-specific equivalent remains a risk. The first-order counting result and the input length theorem are expressly attributed, not claimed as new.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

The 2026 logarithmic counting theorem leaves a genuine, explicitly noted bounded but nonconvergent remainder. The new law resolves its *complete statistical profile*, phase-dependent ordinary-sampling nonconvergence, and exact attainable asymptotic endpoints for every periodically directed Sturmian word, distinguishes the Fibonacci uniform case from nonuniform multi-branch cases, and converts all far-out palindromic-prefix lengths to finite algebraic data and nearest-integer operations. These quantify where the count gains or loses individual palindromic prefixes at exponentially separated scales; this is more informative than the textbook observation that a bounded sawtooth error oscillates.

Finite reproducibility checks cannot alone establish the infinite theorem.
