# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite first-moment integral follows from a direct two-endpoint density and the exact probability \(2rs\) of a third interval bridging a fixed disjoint pair. An exact beta integral and one-dimensional integration reduce it to a checked finite rational formula. Comparison to an exponential-integral expression proves the specified \(\gamma+\log2-1\) constant. Disjoint corner point-process convergence, control of the full untruncated count by an integrable large-coordinate envelope away from zero, and Campbell's formula establish the weak limit, finiteness almost surely, and infinite mean. A high-probability two-bridge construction proves that obstruction pairs have graph distance three. An extracted checker enumerated all matchings through twelve endpoints (11,478 assertions). Finite checks support but do not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

Searches of the mathematical research record and primary sources did not locate a statement of the all-pair distance-three statistic, its logarithmic mean including the exact constant, or its finite-but-infinite-mean limiting count. Diaconis–Holmes–Janson and Iliopoulos discuss the same sampling model, clique behavior and the previously known universal-interval event. An earlier related derived law for the *universal vertex point process* concerns only the single minimum-extrema rectangle, equivalent to whether the obstruction count is zero; it neither counts all obstruction pairs nor determines the divergent mean. The 1990 *Random Intervals* paper was accessible only through its abstract and secondary coverage, so priority remains non-exhaustive. No searched result implied the stronger present claim.

## Value — PASS

The number of distance-three vertex pairs measures a natural failure of two-hop communication in a graph whose diameter is at most three with high probability. Its weak limit is finite but has infinite expectation, whereas its finite-sample expectation grows like \(\log n\); this is a non-routine, mathematically motivated example of extreme-value non-uniform integrability. The explicit triple-corner Poisson representation explains the mechanism and supports future quantitative tail and robustness work.

## Limitations

The full positive-mass distribution and total-variation rate remain unproved; the argument concerns a single iid atomless endpoint law, and no independent priority or proof audit has been performed. One closely related 1990 primary article was not available in full.
