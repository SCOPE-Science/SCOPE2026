# Scientific review: logarithmic slow-vertex crossover

## Correctness — PASS
The one-slow-vertex SI process is a finite Markov chain with positive rates. Before slow infection, the ordinary infection count is exactly the \(k(m-k)/N\) pure-birth process. The exact exponential race for marked-last yields a gamma ratio; independently, conditional hazard yields the slow-infection-time asymptotic. Concentration of the ordinary birth chain before and after \(\log N\), coupled with its two-logarithm full-infection time, proves the mixed limit. A half-infection hitting-time bound supplies uniform integrability for the expected completion-time limit. The mathematical argument explicitly distinguishes the completion time from the slow vertex's own infection time. A 44-case exact-rational finite-state computation checks the closed form and full-chain mean recurrences; these tests are not substituted for infinite-size proof.

## Originality — PASS on the examined evidence, with priority risk
Janson (1999) covers independent identically distributed complete-graph edge weights and the homogeneous two-logarithm baseline. The full text assumes identical edge distributions, so its main theorem cannot directly give a marked incoming rate of order \(1/(N\log N)\) or the mixed atom/exponential tail. Aalto and Leskelä (2015, arXiv 2014) allow heterogeneous *transmitters*, not the receiver-specific slowdown: targets are chosen uniformly by each active sender. Peterson (2010 preprint, 2011 journal) considers complete-graph rank-one vertex-dependent infection rates, but his model is an SIS contact process with recovery at rate one, and the results quantify extinction and quasi-stationarity rather than irreversible SI completion. Leskelä and Ngo (2018 preprint) establish sparse-graph first-passage results with additive node boundary delays, not weakened complete-graph incoming edge rates. A related indexed double-star rumor finding displays a mixed last-vertex phenomenon but on a different network and contact mechanism, with no implication for the exact gamma product or this threshold. Other indexed clique-bouquet and biclique flooding results have different graph families and scaling hypotheses. Complete-graph susceptibility heterogeneity may have been studied under alternate names; priority is not established conclusively.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Heterogeneous susceptibility is a natural deviation from homogeneous epidemic flooding. The result identifies a critical logarithmic rate scale, an atom-plus-tail limiting completion law, the asymptotic chance a specified resistant vertex finishes last, and an explicit expected-time penalty. The two boundary regimes demonstrate a genuine three-way transition, not simply an arbitrary finite-size statistic. This provides a precise benchmark for how one slow target can dominate an otherwise rapidly saturating dense network.

## Limits
Assumes independent exponential SI transmission, exactly one slow target, no recovery and no external seeding. It provides neither constant-order fluctuations nor an analogous general heterogeneous-network theorem; historical priority requires further external scrutiny.
