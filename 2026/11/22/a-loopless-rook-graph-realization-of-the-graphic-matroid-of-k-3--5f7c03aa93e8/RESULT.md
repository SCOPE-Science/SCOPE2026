# A loopless rook-graph realization of the graphic matroid of \(K_{3,3}\)

## Finding

Let \(R=K_3\square K_3\), the simple, loopless rook graph with vertices \((i,j)\in\mathbb F_3^2\), and let \(H=K_{3,3}\) with its two shores both labelled by \(\mathbb F_3\). Label each vertex of \(R\) by an **edge** of \(H\) through
\[
\phi(i,j)=(i+j,i-j),
\]
where the arithmetic is modulo \(3\). Then for **every** initial blue set \(S\subseteq V(R)\), its complete skew-zero-forcing closure is exactly the inverse image of the graphic-matroid closure:
\[
\operatorname{scl}_R(S)=\phi^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{cl}_{M(H)}(\phi(S))\bigr).
\]
Consequently the rook graph is a **simple, loopless** realization of the rank-five graphic matroid \(M(K_{3,3})\). Its minimum skew-zero-forcing sets are exactly the \(81\) inverse images of spanning trees of \(K_{3,3}\); each has five vertices.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(R\) has one vertex for each pair \((i,j)\in\mathbb F_3^2\). Distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when their first coordinates agree or their second coordinates agree. It is a nine-vertex four-regular simple graph with no loops or multiple edges. The skew color-change rule allows **either blue or white** vertex to force its only white neighbor; repeated applications until no force is possible define \(\operatorname{scl}_R\). In \(H=K_{3,3}\), an edge \((u,v)\) connects the left vertex \(u\) to the right vertex \(v\). The closure \(\operatorname{cl}_{M(H)}\) contains precisely those edges whose endpoints belong to one connected component of the selected-edge subgraph of \(H\). All assertions refer to these fixed finite labelled objects and all subsets \(S\).

## Proof

**1. An invertible affine edge correspondence.** The linear map has coefficient rows \((1,1)\) and \((1,-1)\), hence determinant \(-2=1\) in \(\mathbb F_3\). Therefore \(\phi\) bijects the nine rook vertices with the nine edges of \(H\).

**2. The neighborhoods are precisely the four-cycle edge sets.** Fix \((i,j)\) and put \(u=i+j\), \(v=i-j\). The four rook neighbors are \((i,j+1)\), \((i,j-1)\), \((i+1,j)\), and \((i-1,j)\). Their images are, respectively,
\[
(u+1,v-1),\quad(u-1,v+1),\quad(u+1,v+1),\quad(u-1,v-1).
\]
They are the four edges of the rectangle between the two left endpoints \(u+1,u-1\) and the two right endpoints \(v+1,v-1\). This rectangle is a four-cycle in \(H\). Conversely, each four-cycle of \(K_{3,3}\) is obtained by omitting one left and one right vertex from the cycle, so the nine rook neighborhoods, as \((i,j)\) varies, are **exactly all nine four-cycles** of \(H\).

**3. Closure from four-cycles equals graphic closure.** Let \(\operatorname{cl}_4\) be the closure generated by repeatedly adjoining the fourth edge of any four-cycle whenever its other three edges have been selected. Every such addition belongs to graphic-matroid closure, since the three selected edges already connect its endpoints. Thus \(\operatorname{cl}_4(A)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{M(H)}(A)\).

For the reverse inclusion, suppose an edge \(e\notin A\) belongs to the graphic closure. The endpoints of \(e\) are joined by a simple path in \(A\), so that path together with \(e\) forms a simple cycle. Every simple cycle in \(K_{3,3}\) has length \(4\) or \(6\). In the four-cycle case, \(e\) can be added immediately. In the six-cycle case, there is a chord between opposite vertices of the six-cycle; this chord splits it into two four-cycles. Exactly one of those four-cycles avoids \(e\). Its three cycle edges already belong to \(A\), so first adjoin the chord. The other four-cycle then has its other three edges present, and \(e\) can be adjoined. Hence \(\operatorname{cl}_{M(H)}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_4(A)\), proving equality for arbitrary \(A\).

**4. Skew forcing is precisely four-cycle completion.** A rook vertex can force a white vertex exactly when the other three elements of its four-element neighborhood are blue. By Step 2, these are precisely the four-cycle completion rules on \(H\). By Step 3, repeated skew forcing is exactly graphic-matroid closure under \(\phi\).

**5. Bases and the minimum count.** A set spans the cycle matroid exactly when its chosen edges connect all six vertices of \(H\); a minimum such set is a spanning tree and has \(6-1=5\) edges. The classical matrix-tree formula gives \(\tau(K_{m,n})=m^{n-1}n^{m-1}\); at \(m=n=3\), this is \(3^4=81\). Under \(\phi^{-1}\), these are precisely the \(81\) minimum skew-zero-forcing sets of size five. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone Python program `verify.py` uses exact integers, explicitly builds the rook adjacency and the affine edge bijection, and computes **both** closures independently for all \(2^9=512\) initial subsets. The rook closure is computed from the skew color-change rule; the graphic closure uses a six-vertex union-find connectivity calculation. It also checks the nine neighborhood/rectangle identities and independently enumerates the \(81\) five-element spanning bases, ruling out any smaller forcing set. Run `python verify.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter. Finite exhaustive verification is corroboration; Steps 1–5 provide the mathematical proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Hall, Pfeil, and Taylor (arXiv:2611.15741, first public November 11, 2026), Proposition 4.6 and Figure 12, already show that \(M(K_{3,3})\) is skew-zero-forcing realizable. **Their exhibited graph contains loops.** The present statement is a different and stronger *realization-property* result: the explicit, symmetric, four-regular graph \(K_3\square K_3\) is loopless and still realizes the same matroid. Their Proposition 2.10 gives a sufficient hypothesis in which every matroid circuit is a graph neighborhood; that hypothesis does **not** apply here, because the matroid has six-edge circuits while the rook neighborhoods have four vertices. Step 3 supplies the missing six-cycle implication.

Cooper and Fickes (arXiv:2303.17419) define the skew-zero-forcing matroid and prove SZF completeness of the **original complete bipartite graph** (Proposition 3.16). That does not imply the assertion for the **line graph** of \(K_{3,3}\). DeAlba (arXiv:1404.1618) treats extreme skew forcing numbers and certain bipartite cases, not this nine-vertex graphic-matroid realization.

## Limitations

The theorem asserts an explicitly labelled isomorphism for the **three-by-three** rook graph only. It does not classify all simple or loopless skew-zero-forcing realizations of \(M(K_{3,3})\), nor does it assert a corresponding identity for larger rectangular rook graphs. The literature comparison supports provisional historical priority, not independently certified novelty. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
