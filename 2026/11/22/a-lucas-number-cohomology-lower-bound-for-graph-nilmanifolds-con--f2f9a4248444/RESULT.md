# A Lucas-number cohomology lower bound for graph nilmanifolds containing induced cycles

## Finding
Let \(L_0=2\), \(L_1=1\) and \(L_{n+2}=L_{n+1}+L_n\) be Lucas numbers. For every integer \(n\ge3\), the compact Dani–Mainkar nilmanifold associated to the cycle graph \(C_n\) has total rational Betti number satisfying
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\dim_{\mathbb Q}H^k(N(C_n);\mathbb Q)\ \ge\ B_n
:=2^n L_n+\frac{2^n-4(-1)^n}{3}.
\]
For any finite graph \(G\) containing an **induced** copy of \(C_n\), the same inequality holds for \(N(G)\). The initial bounds for cycle lengths \(3,4,5,6,7\) are respectively \(36,116,364,1172,3756\). As \(n\to\infty\), \(B_n\sim(1+\sqrt5)^n\). These are **lower bounds**, not claims of exact total Betti numbers.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, simple and undirected; \(C_3\) denotes the triangle, with three distinct graph edges. The rational Betti numbers of a compact graph nilmanifold agree with the dimensions of Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology over a characteristic-zero field, by the standard nilmanifold cohomology identification. To avoid normalization ambiguity, give the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential explicitly: for the cycle with vertices indexed in \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), its cochain algebra is the exterior algebra generated in degree one by \(x_i,y_i\), with
\[d x_i=0,\qquad d y_i=x_i\wedge x_{i+1}.\]
An orientation change of an edge replaces a \(y_i\) by its negative and does not change the result. In a general graph the same construction uses a generator for each edge. No condition on the lattice beyond existence of the corresponding compact nilmanifold is needed for the Betti-number inequality.

## Proof
**1. The preserved multiplicity grading.** For exterior monomials \(x_Ay_E\) with subsets \(A,E\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), define their multidegree \(w\) coordinatewise by
\[w_i={\bf1}_{i\in A}+{\bf1}_{i-1\in E}+{\bf1}_{i\in E}\in\{0,1,2,3\}.\]
The exterior differential replaces \(y_i\) by \(x_i\wedge x_{i+1}\); therefore each \(w_i\) is preserved. The entire cochain complex is a finite direct sum of subcomplexes \(C_w\), indexed by \(w\in\{0,1,2,3\}^n\). Let \(\chi_w\) be the alternating sum of their cochain dimensions. For any finite cochain complex, the alternating sum of dimensions is the alternating sum of its cohomology dimensions, so
\[\sum_k\dim H^k(N(C_n);\mathbb Q)\ge\sum_w|\chi_w|.\]
The direct-sum decomposition is essential: unlike one ungraded Euler sum, absolute Euler sums cannot cancel between different weights.

**2. The signed transfer product.** Write \(a_i\in\{0,1\}\) for whether \(x_i\) occurs and \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\) for whether \(y_i\) occurs. Then \(w_i=a_i+b_{i-1}+b_i\), and the cochain-degree parity is \((-1)^{\sum_iw_i-\sum_i b_i}\). Define a two-by-two matrix indexed by \(b_{i-1}\) (row) and \(b_i\) (column) whose entry is \((-1)^{b_i}\) when \(w_i-b_{i-1}-b_i\) equals zero or one, and is zero otherwise. Its four possible values are
\[
M_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
M_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
M_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
M_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Summing over the cyclic binary variables yields
\[\chi_w=(-1)^{\sum_iw_i}\operatorname{tr}(M_{w_0}\cdots M_{w_{n-1}}).\]
Consequently \(\sum_w|\chi_w|=S_n\), where
\[S_n:=\sum_{w\in\{0,1,2,3\}^n}|\operatorname{tr}(M_{w_0}\cdots M_{w_{n-1}})|.\]

**3. A complete finite integer certificate.** The multiplicative monoid generated by the four displayed matrices and the identity \(I\) contains exactly twenty-five matrices. This is an exhaustive statement: start at \(I\), close under multiplication on the right by each of the four displayed matrices, and stop only when every right product is in the enumerated set. Let \(T\) be the twenty-five by twenty-five integer matrix counting these four right transitions, and let \(q(A)=|\operatorname{tr}(A)|\) be the twenty-five-vector indexed by its elements. Then
\[S_n=e_I^{\mathsf T}T^nq.\]
The accompanying executable `verify.py` independently rebuilds the entire transition graph with exact integer arithmetic and verifies every coordinate of the identity
\[T\left(T^4-3T^3-4T^2+8T+8I\right)q=0.\]
This is a finite exact identity, not an extrapolation from initial values. It yields for all \(n\ge1\)
\[S_{n+4}=3S_{n+3}+4S_{n+2}-8S_{n+1}-8S_n.\]
Direct evaluation from the identity state gives \((S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4)=(4,12,36,116)\). The proposed expression \(B_n\) satisfies the same recurrence, since its characteristic roots are \(1+\sqrt5\), \(1-\sqrt5\), \(2\), and \(-1\), and
\[z^4-3z^3-4z^2+8z+8=(z^2-2z-4)(z-2)(z+1).\]
The first four values agree, proving \(S_n=B_n\) for every positive \(n\), in particular all genuine cycle graphs \(n\ge3\). Combining steps one through three proves the cycle bound.

**4. Induced-subgraph extension.** The ordinary Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of any graph is graded by the *vertex support* of a monomial: an edge variable has support equal to its endpoints. This grading is preserved by the differential, so summing the summands supported entirely on the vertices of an induced copy of \(C_n\) gives precisely the cycle cochain complex. Because the embedding is induced, there are no extra edge generators between these vertices. Its cohomology is a direct summand of the cohomology of the full graph nilmanifold. Thus the same lower bound applies to any such \(G\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 and only the standard library. The program enumerates the complete twenty-five-state monoid, checks every coordinate of the annihilating matrix identity, compares the recurrence with the closed form for lengths one through eighteen, and independently enumerates exterior monomials to check \(S_n\) at cycle lengths three through six. `verify_output.txt` records one replay. The proof for unbounded \(n\) rests on the exact finite-state identity and the grading argument, not on the finitely checked examples.

## Relationship to prior work
Aldi, Bevins, Chatzitheodoridis, Da Silva, Frias and Mejía Gómez (arXiv:2611.17014, first public 12 November 2026) obtain explicit Poincaré polynomials for **paths** and a lower bound from graph diameter (their Section 6). The present bound is for **cycles** and is exponential even for wheel graphs with diameter two, where the diameter bound cannot grow with rim size. Aldi and coauthors (arXiv:2402.14188, especially their decomposition theorem) establish the general vertex-support decomposition used in step four; they do not give this refined multiplicity-Euler evaluation for cycles. The paper on independence polynomials of two-step nilpotent Lie algebras (arXiv:2504.19836) concerns algebraic independence parameters, not a closed total-cohomology lower bound. Those established results are credited rather than claimed anew. The new step is the exact twenty-five-state cycle transfer bound and its extension through the already known support decomposition.

## Limitations
The inequality is one-sided; it does not establish equality of total Betti numbers, individual Betti numbers, cup products, or the full Poincaré polynomial of a cycle nilmanifold. An induced cycle must be genuinely induced; arbitrary non-induced cycles are not addressed by step four. While the comparison covers the identified primary full texts and targeted indexed queries, unindexed equivalent bounds could remain; independent mathematical validation and exhaustive publication-priority review have not been performed.
