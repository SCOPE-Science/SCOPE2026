The killing reveal is at forward rank n+1−T_v: T_v counts the hidden vertices immediately before that reveal, and T_v−1 immediately after. The formula in the theorem uses the former convention throughout, as in the previous scalar last-isolated time. Under independent reverse priorities, the maximum of the 2d neighbor priorities has CDF t^{2d}; the achieving signed offset is uniform and independent of the maximum by continuity and exchangeability. The center priority is also independent, and the spatial grid converges to uniform arc measure. This gives the exact one-center box probability and the stated fourfold intensity. In fixed-order joint factorial moments disjoint closed neighborhoods give product terms; deleting their O(n^{q−1}) exceptional placements from the independent approximation loses o(1). Among dependent configurations count components of the open-neighborhood overlap graph. With c components and h nonsingletons they use at least 2dc+h distinct small-priority sites: the equal-size neighborhoods are distinct for all sufficiently large n. The number of placements is O(n^c); their contribution is O(n^{−h/(2d)}). If only closed neighborhoods intersect, the open-neighbor priority events still factor and the O(n^{q−1}) placements contribute O(1/n).
