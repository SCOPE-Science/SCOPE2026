# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed residual operator norm follows exactly from products of rank-one complementary projections in complex dimension two. The arithmetic convergence criterion uses the orbit of the expanding circle map and a proof that convergence of the orbit to zero forces eventual exact zero. The almost-everywhere rate is obtained from the pointwise ergodic theorem for an integrable logarithmic observable and Jensen's integral. Dense preimages of the two fixed phases yield the opposing Baire root rates. A separate symbolic-digit cylinder count proves the zero Hausdorff dimension, while controlled digit-block frequencies realize all intermediate unbalanced rates. The packaged finite checker tests 537 identities and representative finite averages; its numerical scope is explicitly not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest directly inspected primary article is Berner–Herr–Jorgensen, *Frame properties of effective stationary sequences* (arXiv:2611.03358, 2026), which studies full consecutive Fourier observations, unconditional reconstruction and adjoint frame properties. Kwapień–Mycielski (2001) provides stationary effectiveness, not the stability of this particular geometrically thinned subsequence. Czaja–Tanis (2014) addresses general effective sequences and frames. Publicly searchable related findings on finite-atomic sequential Fourier Kaczmarz convergence and regular-polygon perturbations use cyclic or consecutive schedules, rather than geometric gaps and the associated expanding-map arithmetic. Searches for lacunary Kaczmarz and phase-dependent category rates did not identify a theorem containing this joint five-part classification. An older general theorem on products of nonstationary projections or ergodic cocycles could imply parts of it; a failed search alone is not a proof of bibliographic uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact arithmetic success criterion answers whether the ordinary effective Fourier sequence remains effective after a natural exponentially sparse observation schedule: it does not at a countable dense family of resonances. More strongly, the same concrete approximation process exhibits a sharp measure-versus-category clash, while its maximally slow upper-rate set has Hausdorff dimension zero. The attainment of every root rate between the two extremal contraction values for unequal masses makes this a complete and motivated phase diagram, rather than a numerically chosen finite slice. This offers a tractable benchmark for stability of severely undersampled stationary measurement systems.
