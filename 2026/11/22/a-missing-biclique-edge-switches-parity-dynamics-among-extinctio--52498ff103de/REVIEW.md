# Assessment of the missing-edge biclique XOR classification

## Correctness — PASS

The four-cell update is derived directly from the graph adjacency rule and all four input functionals are independent for \(m,n\ge2\). The exact invariant block has one of three matrix types, and explicit power ranks establish the rational/Jordan decompositions. The Fitting decomposition turns those into genuine all-parameter claims about exact least periods, entry-time shells and per-orbit attraction basins. An independent state-tracing implementation agrees with the matrix implementation; binary tests cover 225 size pairs for the matrix identities, and 15 exhaustive state-space cases over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_4\) cover 15,376 states. Exhaustion of small examples alone is not used to prove the theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The closest published graph-XOR calculations are the one-edge-defective Johnson and hypercube families, which already exhibit the same even-even *abstract* six-periodic block. That block by itself is therefore not advanced as new. The Johnson and hypercube support graphs do not yield the three shore-parity regimes or the biclique-specific nilpotent filtration. The known one-edge-defective biclique majority model has a different, nonlinear rule, while the checked prior biclique XOR model instead adds an intra-shore edge. Inspected finite-field and XOR-network primary literature gives general methods but not the present exact fault law. No indexed exact covering result was found. An obscure historical equivalence could still exist.

## Value — PASS

One faulty bond in a natural two-population complete network can have three opposite dynamical effects: it destroys all nonzero cycles for odd-odd sizes, creates two-cycles for mixed parity, and creates six-cycles where the unmodified even-even network extinguishes in two updates. Exact orbit counts, entry-time probabilities and individual attraction basin sizes make this a structural fault-response classification across all admissible sizes and characteristic-two fields, not an arbitrary finite slice.

## Boundaries

The proof assumes one missing cross-edge, both shore sizes at least two, synchronous additive updates, and a finite field of characteristic two. It does not treat odd characteristic, multiple missing edges or arbitrary nonlinear feedback. All finite computational checks are reproducible but independent external validation has not occurred.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
