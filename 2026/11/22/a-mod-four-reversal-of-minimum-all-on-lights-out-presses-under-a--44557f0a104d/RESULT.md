# Half-weight rigidity for all-on Lights Out on Möbius ladders

## Finding
Let \(n\ge 4\) be divisible by four. On the cubic Möbius ladder \(M_{2n}\) with vertices \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\) and neighbors of \(i\) at \(i-1,i+1,i+n\), play binary Lights Out in which a press toggles its vertex and its three neighbors. There are exactly four labelled press subsets clearing the all-on position. Each chooses one entire residue class from \(\{0,2\}\) modulo four and one entire residue class from \(\{1,3\}\) modulo four. In particular **every** solution presses exactly half the vertices: the minimum is \(n\) and the full all-on enumerator is \(4z^n\).

## Proof
Write \(x_i\in\mathbb F_2\) for the press at \(i\). The complete all-on system is
\[
x_{i-1}+x_i+x_{i+1}+x_{i+n}=1
\qquad(i\in\mathbb Z/(2n)).
\]
Let \(u_i=x_i+x_{i+n}\). Adding the equations at \(i\) and at \(i+n\) gives \(u_{i-1}+u_{i+1}=0\), or \(u_{i+2}=u_i\). Now \(x_{i-1}+x_{i+1}=1+u_i\); compare the same equation at \(i+2\) to obtain \(x_{i-1}=x_{i+3}\). Thus every solution is four-periodic. Since \(4\mid n\), this forces \(x_{i+n}=x_i\). The original system on four-periodic words now reduces precisely to
\[
x_0+x_2=1,\qquad x_1+x_3=1.
\]
Conversely these two equations imply the original system on the repeated four-word, so no solutions are missed and no additional constraints are needed. The two independent choices yield four subsets, each with two pressed vertices per four consecutive positions. This proves both the stated rigidity and the weight count for every allowed \(n\), including \(n=4\).

## Relationship to known results
This finding concerns **inhomogeneous** closed-neighborhood parity. Minevich's diagonal-image theorem and Berman–Borer–Hungerbühler's inverting-set theorem prove existence, not which weights occur. The Möbius neighbor-XOR fixed-state result already supplies the quiet kernel. In the ring with modulus \(H=z^{2n}+1\), its gcd computation for \(4\mid n\) gives \(g=(z+1)^2=1+z^2\). The kernel is the ideal generated by \(H/g=1+z^2+\cdots+z^{2n-2}\), so the quiet vectors are exactly the two-periodic words. This describes differences of all-on solutions, but does not place the inhomogeneous coset; the two complementary residue pairs above do so.

For comparison, in a cubic graph every closed neighborhood must contain at least one pressed vertex for an all-on solution. Counting incidences gives \(4|S|\ge 2n\), and equality characterizes perfect codes (efficient dominating sets). The perfect-code classifications for cubic circulants cover this equality problem, including the efficient \(n\equiv2\pmod4\) Möbius ladders. They do not determine the smallest *larger* odd-neighborhood solution when equality is impossible. For \(4\mid n\), the conclusion here excludes **every** weight from \(n/2\) up to \(n-1\), not just perfect codes. The known prism quiet code and its affine translate by a whole rail are comparative background, not part of the contribution claimed here.

## Scope and reproducibility
The result concerns the stated simple cubic Möbius graph, binary self-inclusive switching, and the all-on target at \(4\mid n\). It does not concern an arbitrary position, other ladder conventions, or symmetric-difference orbits of strategies. The proof is exact and covers all these sizes; `verify_balance.py` provides finite checks by reducing the actual graph's binary column syndromes. The prior comparison is bounded and does not certify absolute priority.
