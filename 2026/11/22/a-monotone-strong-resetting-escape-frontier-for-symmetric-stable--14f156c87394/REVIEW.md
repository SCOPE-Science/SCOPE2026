# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The short-time two-boundary Lévy exit intensity and the Poisson restart renewal identity yield the strong-reset mean. The published free stable-flight mean supplies the denominator. Gamma reflection and duplication give the cosh ratio, while an Euler-product argument proves strict all-index monotonicity and endpoint expansions independently. The supplied finite checker completes 6,359 consistency assertions; it is not evidence for infinite-domain proof by itself.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The 2026 Żbik–Palmowski article already proves the high-rate mean (including off-center starts) and the 2024 Żbik–Dybiec work addresses the weak-reset coefficient-of-variation region. Those ingredients and the direct pointwise ratio they imply are expressly acknowledged. The contribution assessed here is the sharp **strictly increasing** all-index strong-reset frontier, proved with Euler's sine product, its limiting constants, and the distinction from the weak-reset regime. Closely related published results address marked large-interval escape laws and high-rate clock fluctuations, without an identified matching index-order theorem. Their complete text was not always accessible; implicit equivalent statements or broader results remain a priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The theorem gives a precise boundary for whether *sufficiently frequent* resetting helps a heavy-tailed bounded-interval search and orders that boundary through every stability index. Its reverse index trend relative to weak-reset tests clarifies the choice of asymptotic regime. No claim about optimizing intermediate finite reset rates, noninstantaneous reset costs, or the Brownian endpoint is made.
