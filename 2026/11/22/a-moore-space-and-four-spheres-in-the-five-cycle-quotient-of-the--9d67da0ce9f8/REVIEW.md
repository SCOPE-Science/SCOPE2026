# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A complete five-element partition enumeration produces 50 proper partitions, 205 chains of length two and 180 chains of length three. Every five-cycle action is free on cells, giving 10 vertices, 41 edges and 36 triangles after quotienting. The self-contained verifier checks signed cellular boundary compatibility, contracts a 9-edge tree, then records and checks 31 legal homotopy-preserving generator/two-cell eliminations. Its terminal attaching maps have degrees \(-5,-5,0,0,0\) on a single generator. The second degree-five map becomes nullhomotopic after the first, giving the stated Moore-space-and-four-sphere wedge. The finite enumeration is exhaustive for the specified five-label domain; it is not a general-prime proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature

The closest exact-object published source is Donau (arXiv:1202.4368), Proposition 4.2. It explicitly establishes the fundamental group, first-homology torsion and rational top-homology rank, but does not present or imply the full attaching-map decomposition. Later primary work by Arone and Arone–Brantner covers extensive Young-subgroup fixed-point/quotient phenomena; the transitive five-cycle is not a proper Young subgroup, and the inspected relevant material does not yield the claim. Actual searches over a mathematical findings index returned no direct identical theorem. Residual priority risk includes a related dissertation whose full text could not be inspected and unindexed equivalent work. Search failure alone is not novelty proof.

## Value — PASS

The complete homotopy type resolves the first five-label example in a classical prime-cyclic partition-quotient family. Homology and the group \(\mathbb Z/5\) do not determine its attaching words; the degree-five and null-attachment splitting is a structural refinement with a fully repeatable certificate. The classification is deliberately confined to this natural first case and does not claim a general theorem for all primes.
