## Originality — PASS with historical-priority risk

The original cospectral switching theorem and its fourteen-vertex cubic examples are already established in Godsil–McKay (1982), but it does not classify majority periods and basins. Goles–Olivos (1981) supplies the general eventual period-one/two theorem, not these exact counts. The full accessible arXiv text of Kaaser–Mallmann-Trenn–Natale (2015), including the computational appendix, addresses general voting times rather than this pair. A public sixteen-vertex Shrikhande–rook result establishes the phenomenon of dynamically distinguishable cospectral graphs, but not the explicit smallest-order cubic example. Searches for alternative descriptions, stronger implications and exact tables located no source determining these values. No uniqueness beyond the inspected sources is asserted. Unindexed exact graph-census tables are a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The first possible order for nonisomorphic cospectral cubic graphs is a natural minimal test of which dynamical invariants the adjacency spectrum can and cannot encode. This pair separates not just attractor counts but sharp voting times and fixed-point basin volume, with a reversal between equilibrium abundance and equilibrium attraction. The full finite data are reusable as a benchmark and counterexample to a spectral-determinacy expectation. The value does not rely on a claim to have invented the switching construction.
