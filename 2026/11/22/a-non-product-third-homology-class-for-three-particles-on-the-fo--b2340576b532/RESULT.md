# A non-product third-homology class for three particles on the four-cube graph

## Finding

Let \(Q_4\) be the graph whose vertices are the sixteen binary four-tuples and whose edges change one coordinate. Let \(B_3(Q_4)\) denote the space of unordered configurations of three pairwise distinct points on its **one-dimensional geometric realization**. For every three pairwise vertex-disjoint simple cycles of \(Q_4\), their Cartesian product determines a geometric three-torus in this configuration space. Let \(L\) be the subgroup of integral third homology generated by *all* such product tori.

**Theorem.** The top integral homology and product-torus rank satisfy
\[
\boxed{H_3(B_3(Q_4);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{129},\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}L=128.}
\]
Consequently, the free rank of \(H_3(B_3(Q_4);\mathbb Z)/L\) equals one: there is a non-product third-dimensional homology class that cannot even be rationally expressed as a combination of disjoint-cycle product tori. A specific integral cycle with 1,475 nonzero cellular coefficients, each in \(\{\pm1,\pm2\}\), witnesses this strict rational non-generation. No assertion is made about torsion in the quotient lattice.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is the one-skeleton of a hypercube, **not** its filled cubical body. The points are distinct, move continuously on graph edges, and are unordered. The graph has sixteen vertices of valence four and thirty-two edges. Homology is unreduced singular homology with integral coefficients. A geometric product three-torus is formed using three simple closed cycles whose images in the graph are pairwise disjoint. Orientation affects the signs of generators, not the subgroup they generate. All claims concern the three-particle space of this particular graph; no homotopy type, fundamental group, or uniform formula for other hypercube dimensions is asserted.

## Proof

**1. Integral chain model and upper bound.** Use the reduced Świątkowski chain model for graph configuration spaces [1]. Label graph vertices by integers from zero through fifteen (their four-bit binary encodings). Enumerate the thirty-two unoriented graph edges lexicographically, and order the four incident edge variables at each vertex increasingly. At each vertex \(v\) take local degree-one generators \(h_{v,k}=H_{v,k}-H_{v,0}\), for \(k=1,2,3\), and set
\[
\partial h_{v,k}=e_{v,k}-e_{v,0}.
\]
An edge variable has degree zero and weight one, while each \(h_{v,k}\) has degree one and weight one. A degree-three, weight-three basis is therefore an ordered wedge of three such generators at distinct vertices; its rank is
\[
\binom{16}{3}3^3=15{,}120.
\]
The degree-three differential is the signed Koszul differential. The supplied `verify.py` constructs every column and carries out exact Gaussian elimination modulo three, finding rank **14,991**. Therefore the differential has rational rank at least 14,991 and its rational kernel has dimension at most 129. The comparison theorem of [1] identifies this chain kernel with third homology, as there are no weight-three chains in degree four.

**2. Complete enumeration of geometric products.** Every graph cycle has even length at least four. Any three vertex-disjoint cycles therefore have total length at most sixteen; their length multisets can only be
\[
(4,4,4),\quad(4,4,6),\quad(4,4,8),\quad(4,6,6).
\]
Exhaustive canonical traversal gives 24 four-cycles, 128 six-cycles, and 696 eight-cycles. Testing pairwise vertex-disjoint triples gives precisely 152, 576, 240, and 144 product tori for the four length types respectively, for **1,112 distinct geometric tori**. A cycle is represented in the chain model by the sum of its local incoming-to-outgoing half-edge differences. The threefold graded product of these cycle chains yields an integral degree-three cycle: its boundary vanishes by the Leibniz rule because each factor is a cycle. The verifier also directly checks every signed boundary.

**3. Exact rank of all product tori.** Represent each of the 1,112 cycle products in the fixed 15,120-coordinate top-chain basis. Fraction-free integer column elimination with primitive-gcd normalization gives rational rank **128** for the 152 square-triple products. Every other product vector reduces to zero against this exact rational basis. Consequently, all simple-cycle product three-tori together have rational span of dimension exactly 128. This argument checks the complete cycle-length classification above, not a selected sample or a finite-field surrogate.

**4. The extra integral class and sharpness.** The file `nonproduct_cycle.json` records a signed integer chain as pairs of top-basis column index and coefficient, in the canonical increasing-basis order fixed by `verify.py`. It has 1,475 nonzero terms, coefficient magnitude at most two. Exact multiplication by the full integral differential gives zero. Fraction-free rational reduction against the 128-dimensional product-torus basis leaves a **nonzero remainder** (pivot coordinate 13,278), proving that this class is not in the rational span of any geometric cycle-product torus. Independently, the 128 square-torus vectors and the witness are linearly independent modulo three. Hence 129 integer cycles are linearly independent over the rationals. Combined with step 1's upper bound, this proves the integral kernel is torsion-free of rank exactly 129. The class belongs to the integer kernel, and rational independence is stronger than non-membership in the integral torus lattice. We do not claim that the witness is a primitive generator of the quotient lattice.

## Verification

Place `verify.py` and `nonproduct_cycle.json` in the same directory and execute `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 and only its standard library. The checker constructs all 15,120 top cells and their signed differentials, verifies the 14,991 rank modulo three, exhaustively enumerates all 1,112 geometric tori and their zero boundaries, performs exact fraction-free integer elimination to prove their rational rank is 128, and checks both the witness's integral boundary and its exact rational non-membership. It prints `Q4_THREE_PARTICLE_NONPRODUCT_H3_OK` only on successful completion. A modular computation alone would not prove rational non-membership in a potentially nonsaturated torus lattice; the exact rational remainder is indispensable and is checked separately.

## Relationship to prior work

The reduced Świątkowski comparison theorem [1] supplies the chain complex used here and makes no claim about this particular hypercube computation. Świątkowski's earlier graph-configuration framework [2] establishes historical context. Barnett and Farber [3] treat two particles, with planar cycle-intersection methods, not three on the nonplanar four-cube graph. Maciążek and Sawicki [4] discuss geometric product cycles and non-product classes on other graphs, giving a motivation to test whether three-particle classes can all be toric. Closely related published finite computations determine the **two-particle** second-homology torus lattice of the same four-cube graph [5], the **three-particle** homology of the smaller eight-vertex cube [6], and the three-particle third-homology torus basis of a truncated tetrahedral graph [7]. None of the inspected statements implies the present 129-versus-128 top-homology obstruction. Literature and mathematical-record searches using the four-cube, three-particle, product-torus, and non-toric aliases found no exact covering statement; this is evidence of a gap, not a proof of publication priority.

## Limitations

The free rank of the quotient by geometric product tori is determined, but its possible finite torsion and the full integral saturation of \(L\) are **not** classified. Other homological degrees, cup products, the fundamental group, and equivariant representations are outside scope. The supplied certificate uses finite exhaustive computations of the stated 15,120-column model; its use as singular homology relies on the published chain-model comparison theorem. An unindexed equivalent prior theorem remains possible. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
