# Scientific review: two-level light and water-column optical depth

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is precise for the instantaneous Steele rate, uniform biomass, two strictly positive surface levels, two positive weights, and Beer attenuation. The elementary integral and the Jensen gap \(D(t)=p e^{-xt}+(1-p)e^{-yt}-e^{-mt}\) are independently derived. Its derivative is a three-exponential sum; after multiplication by \(e^{xt}\), a further derivative has at most one real zero. The known simple zero at the origin and the negative limit prove exactly one further positive zero. This yields the strict sign diagram and equality cases, not just sampled crossings. The finite-depth logarithmic test and asymptotic expansions follow with controlled positive denominators. A standalone reproducible checker independently verifies thousands of finite cases and limiting regimes. Numerical checks are supporting diagnostics, never a substitute for the theorem. Risk: the theorem does not cover zero lower light or a physiological memory model.

## Originality — PASS with residual historical priority risk

Steele's 1962 paper (equation (2) and the triangular-daylight average in equation (9)) supplies the light-response kernel and a diurnal average. Bannister (1974, equation (16)) gives the depth-integrated exponential kernel inside a time integral. The same ingredients make the numerical gain computable, but neither source states or directly proves the quantified single-cutoff law for *every* unequal-duty pair, its necessary and sufficient threshold, or the high-mean asymptotic. Litchman's 2000 full article reports two-level observations and the limits of steady-state averaging, without this Steele/Beer phase diagram. The published result *A sharp optical-depth optimum for a uniformly mixed photoinhibited water column* derives the same depth-mean \(M_s(h)\) and optimizes it for *fixed* surface light; its result does not imply the one-cutoff behavior of the three-term Jensen comparison, and it does not classify duty weights. Thornley (1974) and Walsby (1997) are highly relevant but were not available in complete text; neither an abstract nor a failed query is taken as a proof of noncoverage. The work is provisionally original relative to the material actually compared.

## Value — PASS

The exact mathematical question is natural: at the same mean incident photosynthetically active radiation, can alternating light increase modeled water-column instantaneous gross production, and can greater optical depth reverse that assessment? The sharp theorem shows a single reversal or none, gives the exact boundary from measured inputs (two levels, duty fraction, extinction-depth product), and demonstrates distinct high-light and near-onset scales. It is a substantive parameter-wide classification, not a numerical example or a textbook single-point curvature test. Its potential role is as a reproducible benchmark for light-averaging approximations in depth-resolved aquatic production calculations. It does **not** support management predictions or biological growth claims for dynamic photosynthesis; model limitations are explicit.
