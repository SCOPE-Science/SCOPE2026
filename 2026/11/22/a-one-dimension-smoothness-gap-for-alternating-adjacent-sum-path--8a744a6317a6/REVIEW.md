# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The definitions fix the parity of the initial edge, avoiding the reversal ambiguity. For \(s\ge2\) all coordinate and edge inequalities are facets. Tight-edge components are paths: a vertex is exactly a feasible point whose components each have at least one zero coordinate. An excess of incident facets occurs precisely when a component has two zeros. The forced alternating recurrence from a zero reaches the next zero only after \(2s+1\) tight edges beginning with a low-capacity edge. Hence the low-first and high-first first-failure dimensions are \(2s+2\) and \(2s+3\); the critical vertices are uniquely enumerated. Total unimodularity of the signed path-incidence matrix makes every simple vertex smooth. The embedded exact checker independently confirms the first singular vertices for \(\(s=2,3\)\) and checks 57 explicit witnesses through \(\(s=20\)\). Finite checks are not the proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Smoothness is a basic combinatorial/toric property not determined by Ehrhart counts. The theorem gives a full infinite-parameter classification, identifies sharp dimension walls, finds the exact first singular vertices, and reveals an endpoint-orientation effect that delays nonsmoothness by one dimension. It provides a structural complement to the arithmetic invariants of the motivating papers. It does not claim a general theorem for arbitrary weighted graphs or a complete face-vector formula.
