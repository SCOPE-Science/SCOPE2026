# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The theorem is verified for the precisely stated compact planar cross times the middle-thirds Cantor set. The covering dimension is one by the product inequality and an embedded interval. Connected components are the cross fibers because the Cantor projection of a connected set is a singleton. For each two fibers, the real two-coordinate fixed-point map has coordinate sup-norm at most twice the perturbation radius, strictly less than one. Brouwer supplies an intersection of horizontal and vertical image arms, so all connected fiber images meet pairwise; their union is connected. The spectrum of a continuous function is its compact range. The Cantor-coordinate function has infinitely many spectral components. These steps establish the whole theorem without statistical or computational inference.

## Originality: PASS, with literature risk

Bračič and Kandić's 2026 *On operators with many spectral components* proves nonemptiness by components (Proposition 4.5), density for totally disconnected compact spaces (Proposition 4.6), and nondensity for finite or countably many two-dimensional disk fibers (Example 4.9). The disk argument establishes a common planar disk in images and does not yield the dimension-one cross-bundle result: a cross has no planar interior. Their companion *On operators with infinitely many spectral components* revisits the disk example and cardinal depth, not this dimension-one obstruction. Relevant targeted literature and indexed prior-result searches returned no implication covering the compact one-dimensional crossing construction. Classical Brouwer crossing and dimension theory are credited as ingredients rather than original claims. Residual bibliographic risk: an older general theorem on stable intersection of planar continuum images might imply the crossing lemma; an older Banach-function-algebra treatment might already have described this exact spectral application.

## Mathematical value: PASS

The work locates the sharp universal covering-dimension boundary of a new density phenomenon: dimension zero guarantees density of every spectral class for infinite compact metric spaces, while dimension one permits nonempty classes of arbitrary order all to be non-dense. An explicit compact counterexample is important because the motivating infinite-depth counterexample is noncompact with two-dimensional disk fibers. The proof exposes a reusable mechanism—stable pairwise intersection of images, not a shared open planar disk—and it yields a quantitative open neighborhood without extra spectral assumptions.

## Scope and uncertainty

The radius is not asserted optimal; not every one-dimensional space is claimed to obstruct density. Generalizations beyond this product and a full topological characterization are not proved. Independent validation is absent. The originality assessment is based on examined primary sections and current searches, not a formal proof of priority.
