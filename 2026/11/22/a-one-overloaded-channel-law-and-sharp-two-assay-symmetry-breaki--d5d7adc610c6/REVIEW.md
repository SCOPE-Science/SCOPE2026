# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The Poisson event calculation yields the channel failure function exactly. The proof derives the log-failure curvature sign from a strictly increasing polynomial-power expression, excludes boundary allocations through a strict derivative comparison, and applies feasible perturbations plus stationarity to classify all global optima. The fixed-volume, two-channel matched-rate statement is reduced to exact minimization of a quadratic in the sum of two fixed-product variables; its threshold, two exhaustive optimal branches and square-root splitting are proved algebraically. The large-volume statement follows from the general structural classification, uniqueness of the single-channel failure minimum, and a first-order derivative expansion. The bundled standard-library checker separately recomputes exact polynomial identities and 40,212 supplementary assertions. Numerical checks are not offered as proofs of the universal statements.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk

The scientific comparison distinguishes the present mandatory-volume probability-of-at-least-one-positive objective from experimental studies of inhibitor suppression and volume (Jane et al., Herder et al., Mächler et al.), general S-shaped resource allocation (Vicentini), and earlier work on integer PCR replicate counts, three-level assay identifiability, and unrelated ecological survey allocations. The generic allocation principle could be covered by older operations-research theory; novelty is claimed for the complete *specific* Poisson target-plus-inhibitor optimum, the limiting law, and the exact global two-channel threshold, not for S-shaped functions generally. The full text of Vicentini and Mächler was unavailable for definitive implication comparison. Search absence does not certify historical priority.

## Value — PASS

The target-copy versus assay-inhibition tradeoff is documented as a real design consideration; the model gives an exact counterexample to unconditional equal splitting and a natural complete global classification of a fixed-channel toy benchmark, including a sharp nonanalytic optimizer transition and a quantifiable severe failure of naive equal loading. That exact structure can guide future robustness studies of imperfect inhibition functions. Its value is mathematical and methodological, not a claim of optimal practical field protocols; the compulsory full-volume constraint is crucial and must not be hidden.

## Scientific boundaries

Independent homogeneous target and inhibitor arrivals, fatal inhibitors, common rates, mandatory all-volume processing and fixed channel count. The model has not been fitted to eDNA data, and richer sampling or mitigation actions can overturn its policy conclusion. Related S-shaped allocation literature remains a priority risk.
