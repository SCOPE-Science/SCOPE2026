# A one-parameter separation window for the five random-graph reducts

## Finding

Let \(R\) be the countable Rado graph and let \(G_0,G_C,G_S,G_B,G_{\mathrm{all}}\) denote, respectively, its automorphism group, its complement-extended group, its Seidel-switching group, the group generated jointly by switching and complementation, and the full symmetric group. For any pointwise named finite set \(A\subset R\) of \(n\geq0\) vertices, let \(T_G(n,r)\) denote the number of complete types over \(A\) of ordered \(r\)-tuples of pairwise distinct, nonparameter vertices in the corresponding reduct, for \(r\geq0\). These counts are independent of the isomorphism type of the induced graph on \(A\).

For \(k\geq0\), define integer rank functions
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_0(k)&=0,\\
d_C(k)&=\begin{cases}0&k\leq1,\\1&k\geq2,\end{cases}\\
d_S(k)&=\max(k-1,0),\\
d_B(k)&=\begin{cases}0&k\leq1,\\1&k=2,\\k&k\geq3.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(j\in\{0,C,S,B\}\) and all \(n,r\geq0\),
\[
\boxed{T_{G_j}(n,r)=2^{\binom{n+r}{2}-\binom n2-d_j(n+r)+d_j(n)}};
\qquad T_{G_{\mathrm{all}}}(n,r)=1.
\]

These formulas exhibit a sharp *nonmonotonic numerical separation window* for the five distinct reduct groups. With no named parameters, their complete ordered tuple type counts first become pairwise distinct at arity four, where the five counts in the displayed group order are \((64,32,8,4,1)\); at arity three they are \((8,4,2,1,1)\). With exactly one named parameter, the five counts first become pairwise distinct already at arity three, again with \((64,32,8,4,1)\), while at arity two they are \((8,4,2,1,1)\). However, for every \(n\geq2\) and every \(r\geq0\),
\[
T_{G_0}(n,r)=T_{G_C}(n,r),
\]
and for every \(n\geq3\) and every \(r\geq0\),
\[
T_{G_S}(n,r)=T_{G_B}(n,r).
\]
These equalities are explained by equality of the respective pointwise stabilizer subgroups, not merely accidental numerical coincidences. Hence one named point permits complete five-way numerical separation, but naming two or more points destroys it at every arity.

## Assumptions and scope

The reducts are understood through their automorphism groups as classified by Thomas. Tuples consist of *ordered, pairwise distinct vertices outside the named base*, and types are complete first-order types, not merely quantifier-free types in a chosen language. All formulas hold for every finite base and both \(n=0\) and \(r=0\). No randomness or sampling measure on the infinite graph is used. The field \(\mathbb F_2\) is used only for finite edge-incidence encoding.

## Proof

**Finite orbit interpretation.** For a fixed ordered labelled \(k\)-tuple of distinct vertices, record its induced graph as a vector in \(V_k=\mathbb F_2^{\binom{k}{2}}\), with one coordinate for each unordered edge. Every vector occurs in \(R\) by universality. Homogeneity identifies graph automorphism orbits with individual vectors. A switching at a subset \(U\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\) toggles the cut vector \(\delta U\), and complementation toggles the all-ones edge vector \(\mathbf1\). The extension property ensures that such finite changes are exactly the finite restrictions of transformations from the relevant reduct group, and no others arise from the group generators and closure. Consequently, tuple orbits of \(G_j\) are precisely cosets of a binary translation subgroup \(H_{j,k}\leq V_k\), generated by the appropriate cut and/or all-ones vectors.

**The ranks.** The cut map sends each vertex-label bit vector to the edge vector \((u_i+u_j)_{i<j}\). For \(k\geq1\) its kernel is precisely the constants, hence its image has dimension \(k-1\). For \(k\geq3\), the all-ones vector is not a cut: cuts have even parity on each triangle, while \(\mathbf1\) has odd triangle parity. At \(k=2\), the unique edge-vector is both a cut and the all-ones vector, and for \(k\leq1\) it is zero. These observations give exactly the four functions \(d_j(k)=\dim H_{j,k}\) above.

**Pointwise base.** Set \(k=n+r\). Fix any labelled graph pattern on the first \(n\) coordinates. The remaining \(\binom{k}{2}-\binom n2\) binary edge coordinates are freely selectable and every choice is realizable. Two extensions belong to the same complete tuple type over those named parameters if and only if their difference belongs to \(H_{j,k}\) and restricts to zero on the old-old edge coordinates. The restriction map \(H_{j,k}\to H_{j,n}\) is onto: every cut and the all-ones vector on \(n\) old vertices extend to the \(k\)-vertex set. Thus its kernel has dimension \(d_j(k)-d_j(n)\). Since this kernel acts freely by translation on the extension edge vectors, the quotient has size
\[
2^{\binom{k}{2}-\binom n2-d_j(k)+d_j(n)}.
\]
For the full symmetric group, all ordered injective tuples over a fixed named base constitute a single orbit. Because the reducts are \(\omega\)-categorical, these orbit classes are complete first-order types, establishing the formula.

**Sharp separation and stabilizers.** The formula directly yields the stated arrays at arities three and four without parameters and at arities two and three with one parameter. At smaller positive arities, at least two group profiles coincide, so each separation arity is minimal. A complementing permutation cannot fix two distinct old vertices because it would reverse the edge/nonedge status of their pair; therefore the pointwise stabilizers of \(G_0\) and \(G_C\) are equal when \(n\geq2\). Likewise, a switching-plus-complementation permutation outside \(G_S\) reverses the edge parity on every triple; it cannot fix three named vertices. Thus \(G_S\) and \(G_B\) have equal pointwise stabilizers for \(n\geq3\). The remaining numerical equalities follow, proving that no base of size at least two separates all five groups by any collection of tuple-type counts.

## Verification

The accompanying `check.py` enumerates labelled finite edge-pattern quotient orbits directly, without using the rank formula, for all \(0\leq n+r\leq5\), all splits of the total vertex count and all five reducts: 105 exact comparisons. It separately checks the finite translation subgroup dimensions through seven vertices in 32 cases, plus the separation arrays and the persistent spectral identities for a further range of parameters. Run `python3 check.py` using Python 3; its exact recorded output is in `checks.txt`. These finite checks corroborate, rather than replace, the proof for all \(n,r\).

## Relationship to prior work

Thomas (1991) classified exactly these five closed supergroups; that group classification is **prior work**, not part of the finding. Bodirsky and Pinsker (2009) give an accessible proof and explicitly describe complementation, switching and their generated groups. Cameron's account (2001) identifies switching equivalence through the parity of edge counts on triples. None of the inspected passages states the complete finite-base orbit-count formula together with its one-parameter separation and later permanent loss of numerical distinction. The formula follows by an additional labelled binary-translation kernel calculation; it must not be mistaken for a new classification of reducts. A historical source may contain an equivalent enumeration under group-profile terminology, so priority remains provisional.

## Limitations

This result counts *injective ordered* tuples; repeats introduce equality-pattern sums not asserted here. The claim is about numerical complete-type spectra and pointwise stabilizers, not recovering the full reduct from a sufficiently large parameter set. The original 1991 article was accessible only in abstract/metadata during the literature check, although the full 2009 article restates and proves its classification. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
