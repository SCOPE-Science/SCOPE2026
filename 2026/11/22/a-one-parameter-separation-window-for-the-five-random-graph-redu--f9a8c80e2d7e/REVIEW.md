# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every labelled graph is a free binary edge vector. Cut spaces have rank \(k-1\); the all-ones complement vector is independent of cuts precisely when \(k\geq3\). The kernel of edge-pattern restriction onto \(n\) old vertices therefore has exact dimension \(d(n+r)-d(n)\), giving the formula. For complete types the justification is the \(\omega\)-categoricity of the five reducts; pointwise stabilizer arguments separately establish the infinite-range collisions. The standalone enumerator checks 105 small full quotient cases and 32 finite group-rank cases. Main scientific risk: identifying orbit equivalence from switching/complementation requires the standard extension and closure argument, supplied in the proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Thomas (1991) classifies the five groups, not the claimed finite-base counts; Bodirsky–Pinsker (2009, full arXiv text, Theorems 2 and 4) state the generators and identifying relations but no complete numerical parameter-stabilizer table in the inspected material. Cameron (2001, switching/reduct sections) treats the parity character of switching, not the sharp one-parameter profile window. Published calculations for random bipartite reducts and generic digraph reducts use related orbit methods but concern different groups and objects; neither statement implies the fixed-base counts for the five random-graph reducts. Searches of these alternatives found no exact coverage. The original Thomas full text could not be accessed and an older equivalent orbit enumeration remains possible. The group classification itself is explicitly not claimed as new.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The new formula completely determines a natural finite-base oligomorphic invariant for all five classical reducts. In particular, a sharp failure of the apparent monotonicity of numerical distinguishability under naming parameters occurs: no parameters need four new coordinates, one parameter needs only three, but every base of two or more makes complete five-way separation impossible. The nonrecoverability is explained by equality of pointwise stabilizers at sharp cardinalities, not merely by finite numerical coincidence. This is a motivated structural boundary on what type-count signatures can detect.

## Scientific limits

The outcome does not distinguish reducts at bases of size at least two by numerical tuple counts, does not classify reduct groups afresh, and does not assert results for repeated-coordinate tuple counts. Publication priority is subject to inaccessible older enumerations and independent checking.
