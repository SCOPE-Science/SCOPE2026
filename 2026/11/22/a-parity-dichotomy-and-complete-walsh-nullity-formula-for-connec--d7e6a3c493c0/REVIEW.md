# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof is a complete integer-character evaluation for every multipartite shape. The excluded subsets consist precisely of the empty set and the nonsingleton within-part subsets. The full character multiplier formula follows from Boolean orthogonality. Its parity yields nonvanishing when \(N+r\) is even; when it is odd, a frequency meeting every part has exactly zero multiplier. The omitted-part coefficient formula is exhaustive and nonoverlapping. A unit multiplier exists in the invertible case, establishing optimal singular conditioning. The asymptotic follows by a union bound and central-binomial Stirling expansion. The standalone checker passed 751 exact assertions over 136 shapes; it is only supplementary finite evidence.

## Originality — PASS under bounded literature comparison
Tittmann–Averbouch–Makowsky (arXiv:0812.4147), Theorem 18 and Corollary 19, already provide the ordinary connected-subset polynomial for joins and complete bipartite graphs. Liao–Hou (arXiv:1311.6856), Theorem 4.5, establishes reconstruction of complete bipartite graphs from the component polynomial. Those results are credited, not reclaimed. Their ordinary size-counting polynomial does not index individual signed vertex characters. The closest indexed graph-exclusion Fourier interpolation finding studies a different restricted Fourier matrix. The sign-resolved convolution-rank and parity dichotomy were not found in inspected sources. The principal residual novelty risk is an unindexed multivariate vertex-weighted connected-set specialization that could imply the result.

## Value — PASS
Connected induced vertex sets are canonical objects in graph reliability and component-polynomial enumeration. The classification explains a complete/incomplete recovery transition for the full Boolean convolution problem, including exact nullities and sharp conditioning, for every multipartite shape. The universal asymptotic loss along singular parity is a structural consequence with natural interpretation; this is not an isolated finite computation.

## Boundaries
Only complete multipartite graphs, binary XOR convolution and unweighted connected-set indicators are addressed. No independent validation or claim of publication priority is made. Finite verification does not certify the infinite theorem.
