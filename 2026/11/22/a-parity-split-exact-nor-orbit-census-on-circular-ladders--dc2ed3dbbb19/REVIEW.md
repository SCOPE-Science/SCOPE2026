# Scientific review: A parity-split exact NOR orbit census on circular ladders

## Correctness — PASS

The only dynamical identity needed is \(F^3=F\) for an undirected open-neighborhood NOR relation, derived rigorously from its symmetric antitone Galois correspondence. Recurrent states are in one-to-one correspondence with maximal independent sets of the canonical bipartite double graph, and the even/odd cover classification is established by an explicit vertex-level isomorphism. The three-symbol cyclic dominance constraints and nine-state transfer produce the displayed characteristic polynomial. The two independent verification programs check the claimed count in every configuration for six sizes and check the graph-cover and transfer calculations separately. The code does not purport to certify infinitely many sizes; the structural proof supplies that step.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Veliz-Cuba–Laubenbacher (2011/2012), Aracena–Richard–Salinas (2014), and Aledo–Martinez–Valverde (2015) establish relevant fixed-point/maximal-independent-set relationships and broad period constraints, which are fully credited. Aledo et al. (2020) study general maxterm/minterm periodic-point bounds. None of the inspected accessible full-text portions gives the specific open-NOR circular-prism formula, its connected-versus-disconnected double-cover reduction, or its Lucas/cubic-root correction. Published statements involving self-inclusive NOR do not cover the present rule simply by substituting parameters. Closest archived related results concern a single NOR two-cycle's attraction basin on cubic ladders, a different invariant, and NOR periodic counts on rook graphs rather than prisms. Searches do not establish absolute novelty; the 2012 paper's inaccessible full text and possible unindexed exact counts are residual risks.

## Value — PASS

Cubic circular ladders are a standard, uniform graph family with a genuine bipartiteness transition. The complete fixed/two-cycle orbit census for an open inhibitory cellular network is a motivated exact natural invariant, not an arbitrary finite sample. The parity split is mechanistically explained through canonical double-cover topology, and the closed Lucas-plus-period-three formula gives both counts and a decay rate for recurrence. These are nontrivial global outputs even though the general polarity principle itself is elementary.

## Limits

No basin-size classification is claimed. Prior literature on generic graph dynamical systems is not re-proved or relabelled as new. Full-text comparison with Aledo et al. (2012), DOI `10.1016/j.mcm.2011.08.040`, was unavailable; an accessible 2015 survey by the same research group and bibliographic abstract were inspected. Further historical comparison and independent validation would be useful.
