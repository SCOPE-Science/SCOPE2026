## Correctness — PASS

The derivation differentiates the exact two-target mean-time formula, shows its quartic derivative factor is strictly increasing in the exponentiated right distance, and evaluates the factor at exactly one third of the left distance. The resulting factorization reduces the sign to the strictly increasing polynomial \(y^3-y-1\) for \(y>1\). Both asymptotics follow by leading balances in the same quartic, and continuity plus analyticity proves attainment of at least one ratio minimum. The source formula and physical first-hit-reset convention have been checked against the primary full text. The checker independently verifies symbolic identities and selected values. **No uniqueness claim is made for the ratio minimum.**

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Calvert–Evans (arXiv:2111.02335) prove the mean and optimal-position quartic, including the two limiting ratios, but do not state an all-intermediate-parameter crossing threshold, the plastic constant, or a nonmonotone optimum ratio. Pal–Prasad (arXiv:1812.03009) treat first absorption in an interval, not two-target completion and second-target design. The compared published two-target reset-site allocation theorem is for one hidden target, not two targets both to be found. This exact crossing is **derived from a prior published implicit relation**, posing a genuine implicit-coverage and publication-priority risk; originality is limited to its sharp phase classification and forced reentrance, not the original optimizer.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The optimal physical placement of a second target is a natural design parameter in multi-lesion Brownian search. The two previously known asymptotic ratios suggest an interpolation but do not tell whether the optimum can fall below the large-distance factor. The all-distance exact crossing proves it does, and gives the precise boundary via a universal algebraic constant. That structural sign reversal is relevant even when finding the global ratio-minimum location is unnecessary. The result does not claim experimental cost savings beyond the idealized model.
