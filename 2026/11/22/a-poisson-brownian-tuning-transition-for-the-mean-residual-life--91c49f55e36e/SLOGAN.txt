For the mean-fitted exponentiality test of Baringhaus–Henze, diverging exponential weights have a sharp \(a_n\asymp n\) calibration boundary: the scaled null statistic changes from a Brownian quadratic functional to a compensated-Poisson quadratic functional of exact variance \(1+c/2\), then collapses in probability. Above that boundary its expected scaled value nevertheless tends to one, an explicit non-uniform-integrability effect caused by rare observations near zero.
