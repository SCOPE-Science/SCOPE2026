# Scientific review: a two-sex Poisson founding-quorum allocation theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is established for arbitrary positive expected founding count and integer female quorum. Poisson thinning gives independent sex counts, differentiation gives the exact positive stationary equation, and a beta-distribution MGF gives a globally strictly monotone stationarity function. The same representation establishes strict budget and quorum comparative statics. Elementary Taylor and exponential-tail calculations prove both asymptotic regimes. All boundary cases and the one-female special case are addressed. The included standard-library checker independently reproduces the scalar numerical results and completed 13,569 diagnostic assertions, but numerical checks are not substituted for proofs.

## Originality — PASS relative to the investigated literature
The closest 2004 and 2010 mating-system papers establish the relevance of sex-ratio stochasticity and mating-capacity functions, but address population outcomes under fixed size and alternative mating functions; their mathematical assertions do not imply a complete Poisson threshold-quorum optimum, its strictly increasing male allocation with total founder intensity, its strict inverse relation with quorum size, or its large-intensity logarithmic displacement. The closest examined mathematical founder-group result concerns a fixed-size binomial model maximizing expected mating female output and a separate any-pair probability. The new objective requires \(r\) females rather than maximizing expected offspring or merely obtaining a mate. The Poisson-splitting identity and beta MGF are not claimed as new.

Searches also considered broader independent-Poisson resource-allocation, reliability optimization, sex allocation and mating system models. Derman, Lieberman and Ross (1974), DOI 10.1287/mnsc.21.3.241, discuss an allocation problem for a system of homogeneous components, whereas the present components have unequal threshold reliability responses; only the 1974 abstract and a Stanford technical-report catalog could be inspected, so a more general covered case in its full text cannot be excluded. No directly dominating statement was established in inspected material. Historical priority remains provisional: a general series-system reliability allocation theorem or inaccessible older mating-model result may imply the new theorem. Absence of a search hit cannot certify originality.

## Value — PASS
An explicit initial minimum breeding-group requirement gives a concrete nonlinear objective for release planning. The strict, all-budget female-biased-to-balanced transition and the opposite dependence on quorum size are not obtainable simply by assuming a fixed sex ratio. The exact one-dimensional equation, global uniqueness and sharp success-failure asymptotics provide benchmarks for designing or evaluating sex-stratified founder arrivals and for deciding when added founder effort changes the preferred mix. The result is not a claim about evolutionary equilibrium or indefinite population survival; applying it requires validating independence, quota biology, costs and survival.
