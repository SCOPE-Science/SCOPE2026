# Review of the prime-field structure-graph six-cycle theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** Over the integers, exact polynomial arithmetic gives \(g^3(X)-X=-X^2P(X)\) and the coprimality certificate \(\operatorname{Res}(P,X^2-2X+2)=1\). The reduction \(\Phi^2(-1,y)=(-1,g(y))\) then produces genuine least-period-six orbits at every qualifying prime. Modulo \(5\) and \(11\), the explicitly verified factorization patterns give a full two-block cyclic-wreath Galois group of order \(18\), rather than a numerical guess. Its unique quadratic subfield follows from the discriminant and the unique index-two subgroup. The intersection with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{24})\) is exactly that quadratic field by the degree gcd, giving a compositum of order \(72\). Exactly \(4,4,1\) compatible Frobenius elements fix zero, three, six roots, respectively. Chebotarev proves the asserted density and conditional distribution. The packaged checker has been executed on its actual file. Limitation: the density relies on the classical Chebotarev theorem and cannot be proved by inspecting finitely many primes.

## Originality
**PASS under the checked literature, with a declared historical-priority risk.** Chernev–Chernev (2026), Section 5, contains the crucial two-iterate reduction but explicitly leaves the infinite class of primes \(7\pmod{12}\) unresolved after finite computation. Lipkovski (2012, 2017) describes the same structure digraph, small cycles, and low-period equations, not the congruence-restricted infinite density or certified orbit count distribution. Bridy–Garton (2017) develops general Galois and fixed-point-proportion machinery for dynatomic polynomials, including wreath products; the present calculation of the *particular specialization* and its forced quadratic intersection with the \(24\)th cyclotomic field yields the ring-graph statement not contained in their stated results. All these constructions are acknowledged rather than claimed as new. A previously unnoticed specialized arithmetic-dynamics computation may still imply the final exact density; no independent priority certification is asserted.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a natural new infinite-subfamily result inside the specific prime congruence class still open in the motivating ring-graph classification. It gives more than existence: exact natural densities \(5/72\) for at least one certified six-cycle, and conditional frequencies \(4/9,4/9,1/9\) for zero, one, or two canonical six-cycles. The distribution is an arithmetic invariant tied to the quadratic field carried by the sextic discriminant. The theorem carefully does not purport to solve the entire open class or count every possible six-cycle.
