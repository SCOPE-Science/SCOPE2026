# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The coefficient reduction is derived directly from the multinomial blocks, and positivity and boundedness of the binomial matrix are established. Fourier inversion and a nondegenerate torus Laplace expansion yield the collision asymptotic, including the remainder needed for the logarithmic trace formula. The trace thresholds follow from positive diagonal summation; the two-prime Schatten sufficiency uses a convergent dyadic \(\mathcal S_{2p}\) decomposition of the positive square root, with necessity from non-trace-class membership. The one-prime noncompactness is established by weakly null block tests. Exact and numerical finite checks support rather than replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Bayart–Kouroupis provides general Schatten theory for individual Dirichlet composition operators but not ideal membership of these critical limiting Hankel operators. Fang–Guo–Mishra–Muthukumar identifies and reduces the limit, and proves multiprime compactness; the cited results do not determine sharp two-prime Schatten membership, its logarithmic trace coefficient, or the three-prime trace-class onset. The separately inspected published two-prime fourth-Schatten cusp concerns fixed-amplitude boundary translation, not the present critical scaling. published-finding corpus meaning searches for weighted-binomial Hankel, collision, and prime-support Schatten criteria yielded related but non-dominating results. The remaining risk is specialized, unindexed weighted-Hankel ideal literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

At the exact critical boundary the number of active primes changes the operator ideal: noncompact to compact-but-nonnuclear to nuclear. The two-prime case is a natural sharp threshold \(p=1\), not an arbitrary numerical slice; its exact logarithmic trace coefficient measures the failure of nuclearity in the canonical total-degree cutoff and quantifies how the two masses interact. The conclusions sharpen a qualitative compactness theorem and support spectral estimates for multi-frequency Hardy–Dirichlet limits.
