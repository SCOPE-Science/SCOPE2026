# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Reconstructed the all-parameter argument. For a feasible signed winding on each petal the nonnegative step counts are (ℓ_j±qw_j)/2; every arbitrary hub color and compatible collection of words gives one map. An internal move exchanges adjacent opposite signs, and a hub move exchanges first and last opposite signs on every petal, so winding is cellwise constant. The absence of a target square prevents adjacent source vertices from both varying in a cell; target degree two gives cubes, with disjoint adjacent-step exchanges at fixed hub. Identify the words with monotone paths in the rectangular grid, so the exchanges toggle ideal elements and a cube toggles an antichain of available elements. Deleting a maximal element of the poset removes a product strip attached along one end over the subcomplex containing all its predecessors; retracting each strip proves contractibility, including the degenerate one-word and prescribed-end cases. A hub cell with colors h,h+2 fixes all neighboring vertices at h+1, requiring both signs in each petal. Its nonempty fixed-end interface is contractible and the cell piece is precisely that interface times an interval. Thus a boundary winding sector splits into q contractible fixed-hub fibers; in strict sectors the vertex fibers and edge interfaces replace by points by CW homotopy gluing. The resulting step-two graph has one cycle for odd q and two for even q≥6. These facts establish the products and the empty case integrally. A direct independently written bounded enumeration for (6,4)→C₆ gave 792 maps: the (0,0) sector has 720 vertices in the two hub-parity components, with mod-2 b₁=2 in total, and each (±1,0) sector has 36 vertices in six fixed-hub components, with b₁=0. Each frozen fixed-hub component has six vertices, six edges and one filled square; hence frozen here does not mean isolated. Bounded b₁ and component counts corroborate, not replace, the proof.

## originality

PASS

Prior work gives the one-petal counts and general per-component types, and gives relative-displacement counts for a distinct two-terminal family. The individual multiplicities for a one-hub bouquet involve a simultaneous hub-movability condition across all petals; they are not products of unbased cycle Hom component counts. The specific products of feasible and strict windings and the hub parity split survive the inspected comparisons.

## value

PASS

This is a natural cut-vertex extension of cycle-to-cycle reconfiguration, with explicit integer counts for arbitrary petal number and lengths. The hub can move only when all petals can move it at once, even though nonhub recoloring can persist when the hub is frozen. The formula captures that distinction and even/odd hub-phase connectivity without enumerating maps, and gives the homotopy type rather than just a test on individual components.
