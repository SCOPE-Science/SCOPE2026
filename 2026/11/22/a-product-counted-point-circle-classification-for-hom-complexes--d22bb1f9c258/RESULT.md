# A product-counted point–circle classification for Hom complexes of cycle bouquets

## Finding
Let \(r\ge2\), \(q\ge5\), and \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r\ge3\) be integers. Form the bouquet \(B(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r)\) by identifying exactly one vertex of each simple cycle \(C_{\ell_j}\), with no other identifications or added edges. For each petal put
\[
 W_j=\{w\in\mathbb Z: |qw|\le\ell_j,\;qw\equiv\ell_j\pmod 2\},\qquad
 W_j^\circ=\{w\in W_j: |qw|<\ell_j\},\quad a_j=|W_j|,\quad b_j=|W_j^\circ|.
\]
Set \(s_q=1\) for odd \(q\) and \(s_q=2\) for even \(q\). Then the ordinary graph-homomorphism complex has the **complete integral homotopy classification**
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(B(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r),C_q)
 \simeq\left(\coprod^{s_q\prod_{j=1}^r b_j}S^1\right)
 \sqcup\left(\coprod^{q(\prod_{j=1}^r a_j-\prod_{j=1}^r b_j)}\{\mathrm{pt}\}\right).
\]
An empty coproduct means an empty space. In particular the complex is empty if any \(a_j=0\). The *individual component counts*, not merely a point-or-circle dichotomy, are asserted. For example, \(\operatorname{Hom}(C_7\vee C_7,C_5)\simeq\coprod^4 S^1\), while \(\operatorname{Hom}(C_5\vee C_7,C_5)\simeq\coprod^{20}\{\mathrm{pt}\}\).

## Assumptions and scope
Every graph is finite, simple and loopless; \(C_m\) denotes the chordless \(m\)-cycle on \(m\ge3\) vertices, and the source cycles have exactly one common hub \(v\). Each petal is separately oriented, beginning and ending at \(v\). The codomain's vertices are \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) with consecutive residues adjacent. \(\operatorname{Hom}(G,H)\) is the standard prodsimplicial complex whose cells are multihomomorphisms assigning a nonempty set of target colors to every source vertex, with every choice preserving adjacency. The restriction \(q\ge5\) makes each target vertex's two neighbors distinct and nonadjacent, and prevents four-cycles in the target.

The sets \(W_j\) are finite, with \(|w|\le\ell_j/q\); the count is a closed product expression requiring no enumeration of target colorings. The equality cases \(|qw|=\ell_j\) are included in \(W_j\) but excluded from \(W_j^\circ\).

## Proof
**1. Invariant signed winding sectors.** Write the colors along the \(j\)-th oriented petal as \(h=c_{j,0},c_{j,1},\ldots,c_{j,\ell_j}=h\) modulo \(q\). Each edge has a unique signed increment \(\varepsilon_{j,k}\in\{+1,-1\}\), since \(q\ge5\). Closure gives \(\sum_k\varepsilon_{j,k}=q w_j\) for a unique \(w_j\in W_j\), and conversely every such signed word and hub color defines a graph homomorphism. If one nonhub vertex changes color in the one-skeleton of the Hom complex, its two adjacent increments exchange \((+1,-1)\leftrightarrow(-1,+1)\); hence the total winding is fixed. If the hub changes color by \(+2\) or \(-2\), the first and last increments of **each** petal flip simultaneously, changing their sum by \(0\). Thus the winding vector \(w=(w_1,\ldots,w_r)\) is constant on every cell and connected component; different winding sectors are disjoint subcomplexes.

**2. Contractible fixed-hub fibers.** Fix a feasible winding vector and the hub color \(h\). On petal \(j\), the words have \(p_j=(\ell_j+qw_j)/2\) positive and \(m_j=(\ell_j-qw_j)/2\) negative increments. Two words are adjacent precisely when an adjacent pair of opposite increments is exchanged. More generally a cube corresponds to simultaneously exchanging disjoint adjacent opposite pairs. This is the standard cube complex \(K(P_j)\) of order ideals of the \(p_j\)-by-\(m_j\) rectangular poset: words correspond to monotone lattice paths (equivalently, ideals), and commuting elementary squares correspond to independent toggles. We give the needed contractibility argument. For any finite poset \(P\), choose a maximal element \(x\). The cubes toggling \(x\) form \(K(R)\times[0,1]\), where \(R\) is the subcomplex of ideals in \(P\setminus\{x\}\) containing all predecessors of \(x\); its attachment is the \(K(R)\times\{0\}\) face in \(K(P\setminus\{x\})\). Retracting the interval to zero gives \(K(P)\simeq K(P\setminus\{x\})\); iteration leaves a point. Therefore the fixed-hub fiber \(F_h(w)=\prod_jK(P_j)\) is nonempty and contractible. The same argument applies when the first and last steps on any petal are prescribed: the interior is another (possibly empty) fixed-sum word complex, hence contractible whenever nonempty.

**3. Exact hub transitions.** A cell in which the hub can take two colors must give all its neighboring vertices the unique common neighbor of those colors in \(C_q\). Because \(q\ge5\), the two hub colors are exactly \(h\) and \(h+2\) for some \(h\), and all neighbor colors must equal \(h+1\). In signed-word language this means every petal starts with \(+1\) and ends with \(-1\). Such a word exists with sum \(q w_j\) exactly when \(p_j,m_j\ge1\), i.e. \(|q w_j|<\ell_j\). Thus an \(h\to h+2\) hub edge exists precisely when **all** petal windings are strict. Its interface with either fixed-hub fiber is the product of the fixed-end word complexes, so is contractible. The cells over this hub edge are a product of that interface with \([0,1]\). There are no cells with three possible hub colors (a degree-two target cycle has no common neighbor of three distinct colors), and every cell with variable hub belongs to exactly one such edge piece.

**4. Homotopy of each winding sector.** If some \(|qw_j|=\ell_j\), no hub edge exists; the winding sector is the disjoint union of the \(q\) contractible fixed-hub fibers. If every \(|qw_j|<\ell_j\), replace the contractible vertex fibers and contractible hub-edge interfaces in the above graph-of-spaces decomposition by points, by the homotopy-pushout property for CW cofibrations. The resulting homotopy type is that of the graph with vertices \(h\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) and edges \(h\to h+2\) (one for each \(h\)). For odd \(q\), this graph is a single \(q\)-cycle; for even \(q\ge6\), it is two disjoint \(q/2\)-cycles. Its components are respectively one or two circles.

**5. Sum over sectors.** Independent winding choices give exactly \(\prod_j a_j\) feasible sectors; exactly \(\prod_j b_j\) have only strict windings. Each strict sector yields \(s_q\) circles and each remaining sector yields \(q\) contractible components. This proves the displayed formula, including the empty case, with no finite-range restriction.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `bouquet_hom_checker.py` constructs independently all signed-word graph homomorphisms, the entire single-recoloring graph, and its quadrilateral two-cell boundaries. For 13 parameter instances across both parities of \(q\), including three-petal bouquets, it checks component counts and dimension-one homology ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) against the formula. Its recorded output ends `ALL_BOUQUET_HOM_TESTS_PASS cases=13`. These checks exercise both circle and isolated-point cases, and the empty case, but do not replace the uniform proof. Exact integral homotopy follows from the graph-of-contractible-spaces argument, not from field computations.

## Relationship to prior work
Čukić and Kozlov's 2004/2006 work [1] computes the Hom complex when **the source is one cycle**. Fujii, Iwamasa, Kimura, Nozaki and Suzuki [2] prove the general point-or-circle dichotomy for **any connected source**, together with a directed-cycle test for the type of an individual component. Neither result provides the multiplicative *number* of circular and point components for bouquets of an arbitrary number of cycles. Fujii, Kimura and Nozaki [3] establish a broader square-free-codomain component-type theorem and algorithm, but do not give this product count. A separate published classification [4] of Hom complexes of generalized theta sources has two shared terminals and odd-cycle targets, not one-hub bouquets and arbitrary \(q\ge5\); its winding arithmetic is therefore not this theorem. An algorithm that could calculate each finite case is not the same mathematical statement as a closed classification for all lengths and petal counts.

## Limitations
The source must be a *one-hub* cycle bouquet, and the codomain a cycle of length at least five. No formula is claimed for more general cactus graphs, noncyclic targets, the exceptional four-cycle target, or equivariant homology representations. The family formula is mathematically established here; bibliographic uniqueness beyond the inspected literature and searchable records is not independently certified, and an equivalent unindexed formulation remains possible. The cited point-or-circle theorem is prior work and is not claimed as a new result.
