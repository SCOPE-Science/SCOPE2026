# Review of the bouquet-to-cycle Hom component formula

## Correctness — PASS
The winding increments are unique for targets with at least five vertices. Recoloring a nonhub vertex swaps consecutive opposite increments, while a hub recoloring reverses the first and last signs on every petal, preserving all winding numbers. Fixed-hub word complexes are cubical ideal complexes and explicitly contractible by maximal-poset-element retractions. A hub transition exists exactly when every winding is strict, and its attaching interface is itself contractible. The resulting graph-of-spaces reduces to the step-two cyclic color graph, yielding exactly one or two circles depending on parity; otherwise there are exactly as many contractible components as hub colors. This proves all admissible integers, including parity obstructions and exact equality boundaries. Thirteen exhaustive finite examples corroborate component counts and mod-two first homology.

## Originality — PASS against available evidence
The full text of the 2024 graph-Hom-to-cycle paper establishes the point/circle component dichotomy and the orientation criterion, not an all-bouquet numerical count. The 2004 cycle-to-cycle paper has only one source cycle. The square-free target extension supplies general component types and an algorithm but not a closed product expression. A closely related two-terminal generalized-theta classification is not a one-cut-vertex bouquet and is restricted to odd targets. Directly querying published mathematical records for bouquet, cactus, wedge of cycles, winding and Hom components revealed these related but non-covering results; own accessible findings containing related words were checked at claim level. A potential unindexed equal-strength classification remains a bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS
The natural passage from one cycle to arbitrarily many cycles attached at a cut vertex produces a new simultaneous compatibility problem: each petal is locally deformable, but the hub can move only when all windings are nonextremal. The exact multiplicative counts and odd/even target split describe graph-coloring reconfiguration topology without brute-force enumeration. These are complete infinite-family invariants and a sharp hub-freezing boundary, not a parameter substitution or a finite curiosity.

## Limits and follow-up risk
The source is only a one-hub bouquet, the target is a cycle of length at least five, and there is no claim about target length four, general cactus graphs or full equivariance. The literature comparison is substantive but is not a guarantee that every equivalent preprint is indexed. The symbolic proof controls integral homotopy; GF(2) experiments alone would not.
