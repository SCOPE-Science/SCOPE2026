# A projective-plane wedge law for cyclic shore quotients of two-particle bipartite configurations

## Finding
Let \(n\geq3\) be an integer, let \(G_n=K_{3,n}\), and let
\[
B_2(|G_n|)=\bigl((|G_n|\times|G_n|)\setminus\Delta\bigr)/\mathfrak S_2
\]
be the collision-free **unordered** configuration space of two points in the geometric graph. Let \(C_3\) cyclically permute the three vertices of the size-three bipartition class and fix the other \(n\) vertices. For the **ordinary geometric orbit space**, there is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\boxed{B_2(|K_{3,n}|)/C_3\ \simeq\ \mathbb{RP}^{2}\vee\bigvee^{\,n(n-3)/2}S^{2}\qquad(n\geq3).}
\]
In particular its fundamental group and first integral homology are \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\); its second integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^{n(n-3)/2}\); and all higher integral homology groups vanish. The \(n=3\) projective-plane case has already been determined in a finite subgroup classification; the contribution is the uniform classification and sphere growth for all \(n\geq4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices are \(a_0,a_1,a_2\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_n\). Every \(a_i\) is adjacent to every \(b_j\), and there are no other edges. Each edge is realized as a closed unit interval; particles are disallowed from coinciding even at interior edge points. The generator \(g\) of \(C_3\) sends \(a_i\) to \(a_{i+1\pmod3}\) and fixes all \(b_j\). The action on configurations is induced diagonally before forgetting particle labels. No free-action or Borel/homotopy-orbit interpretation is used. The integer lower bound \(n\ge3\) is essential to the displayed final reduction, since it supplies a pair of indices distinct from the pivot index.

## Proof
**1. An equivariant two-dimensional cubical model.** Write \(X=|G_n|\). Triangulate \(X\times X\) by retaining product vertices \((v,w)\) and coning the four boundary edges of every product square \(e\times f\) to its center \(c_{ef}\). The geometric diagonal \(\Delta\) is the *full induced subcomplex* on exactly the vertices \((v,v)\) and \(c_{ee}\): within \(e\times e\) its two diagonal half-edges join \((u,u)\) to \(c_{ee}\) and \(c_{ee}\) to \((v,v)\). Let \(K\) be the full subcomplex on all other vertices. For any point off the diagonal, the sum \(s\) of the barycentric weights of vertices outside the diagonal is positive. Replacing these weights by their values divided by \(s\), and setting diagonal-vertex weights to zero, is a strong deformation retraction onto \(|K|\). Indeed the straight-line barycentric homotopy keeps the total non-diagonal weight positive and fixes \(K\). This construction commutes with every graph automorphism and with particle exchange.

If distinct graph edges \(e,f\) meet at one endpoint, exactly two triangles of \(K\) involve \(c_{ef}\). Their exposed center spokes are free edges, so these two triangles can be collapsed along those spokes, followed by the remaining dangling center edge. Centers for disjoint \(e,f\) retain the centrally subdivided product square; for \(e=f\), the center lies in the deleted diagonal. The operations for different centers have disjoint center supports. The group generated by \(g\) and particle exchange acts freely on centers of ordered adjacent distinct-edge squares: an order-three permutation cannot fix an edge, nor interchange two distinct edges. Consequently the collapses can be performed equivariantly, simultaneously in full center orbits. After particle interchange the resulting finite model is the ordinary Abrams unordered cubical complex \(U\!D_2(G_n)\), whose cells are unordered pairs of closed graph vertices/edges with disjoint endpoints. Therefore the required orbit space is homotopy equivalent to \(|U\!D_2(G_n)|/C_3\). This explicit equivariance is needed: a nonequivariant configuration-space retraction would not alone imply the corresponding orbit-space statement.

**2. All quotient cells.** Orient each graph edge \(e_{ij}=[a_i,b_j]\) from its left endpoint to its right endpoint. The group \(C_3\) acts freely on positive-dimensional cells of \(U\!D_2(G_n)\), so the ordinary quotient has the following honest oriented two-dimensional CW structure (all index pairs satisfy \(1\le i<j\le n\)):

- Zero-cells: \(T=[\{a_0,a_1\}]\), \(Q_j=[\{a_0,b_j\}]\), and \(P_{ij}=\{b_i,b_j\}\).
- One-cells: \(L_j^+=[\{a_1,e_{0j}\}]\), \(L_j^-=[\{a_2,e_{0j}\}]\), and \(R_{i,j}=[\{b_i,e_{0j}\}]\) for \(i\ne j\).
- Two-cells: \(A_{ij}=[\{e_{0i},e_{1j}\}]\) and \(B_{ij}=[\{e_{0i},e_{2j}\}]\).

The symbols in square brackets denote the \(C_3\)-orbit, not a quotient in homology. The respective cell numbers are
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2)=\left(1+n+\binom n2,\ n(n+1),\ n(n-1)\right).
\]
The orbit representatives are exhaustive: every pair of graph edges has different left and right endpoints; applying a rotation makes the first left endpoint \(a_0\); and choosing the smaller right index as its right endpoint leaves exactly the two other-left-endpoint options \(A,B\). For vertex/edge cells the fixed right-vertex index and the other left endpoint give the complete list. Because no nontrivial rotation stabilizes a one- or two-cell, all attaching orientations are inherited without folding.

With the product-square orientation and these edge orientations, the cellular boundaries are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial L_j^+&=Q_j-T,&\partial L_j^-&=Q_j-T,\\
\partial R_{i,j}&=P_{\min(i,j),\max(i,j)}-Q_i,\\
\partial A_{ij}&=L_i^++R_{i,j}-R_{j,i}-L_j^-,\\
\partial B_{ij}&=L_i^-+R_{i,j}-R_{j,i}-L_j^+.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, the *attaching words*, not merely their abelianized boundaries, are the boundary words of the indicated product squares.

**3. A complete reduction of attaching words.** The edges \(L_j^+\) together with \(R_{i,j}\) for \(i<j\) constitute a maximal tree: the former join each \(Q_j\) to \(T\), and the latter join each \(P_{ij}\) to \(Q_i\). Collapsing the tree leaves the circle generators \(x_j=L_j^-\) and \(y_{ij}=R_{j,i}\) for \(i<j\). Traversing the four sides of each product square gives the reduced two-cell boundary words
\[
A_{ij}:\ y_{ij}^{-1}x_j^{-1},\qquad
B_{ij}:\ x_i y_{ij}^{-1}.
\]
For each \(i<j\), the \(B_{ij}\)-cell permits an elementary \(1\)-cell/\(2\)-cell cancellation of \(y_{ij}\), substituting \(y_{ij}=x_i\). The remaining cells give one relation \(x_i x_j=1\) for each \(i<j\), up to inversion and cyclic permutation of an attaching word. Next, for each \(j\ge2\), the cell corresponding to the pair \((1,j)\) cancels \(x_j\), replacing it by \(x_1^{-1}\). Only the circle \(x_1\) and the \(\binom{n-1}{2}\) cells indexed by \(2\le i<j\le n\) remain, and every attaching map has degree \(-2\) on that circle.

As \(n\ge3\), there is at least one such two-cell. Orient it so its map has degree \(2\). By sliding each further two-cell over the first, replacing its degree-two attaching map by the difference of the two, the other \(\binom{n-1}{2}-1\) two-cells become trivially attached spheres. These are ordinary finite two-dimensional CW homotopy-preserving cell cancellations and handle slides; no homology-only argument is substituted for the attaching-map calculation. A single degree-two cell on a circle is the standard CW structure on \(\mathbb{RP}^{2}\). Since
\[
\binom{n-1}{2}-1=\frac{n(n-3)}2,
\]
the asserted wedge decomposition follows, for every integer \(n\ge3\). \(\square\)

## Verification
The self-contained Python standard-library program `verify.py` enumerates all unordered zero-, one- and two-cells of \(U\!D_2(K_{3,n})\), constructs the cyclic orbits, verifies there is no positive-dimensional stabilizer, and checks the displayed orbit counts, every oriented boundary, the chosen spanning tree and the exact reduced two-cell words for \(3\le n\le9\). It prints the cell numbers, the claimed number of extra spheres, and `THREE_SHORE_CYCLIC_CONFIGURATION_ORBITS_OK`. Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing the program; the expected complete output is supplied as `verification_output.txt`. The finite computation corroborates the uniform orbit formulas but does not replace the all-\(n\) equivariant retraction and attaching-map proofs in the preceding section.

## Relationship to prior work
Crisp and Wiest prove that the two-particle unordered discretized configuration of \(K_{3,3}\) is the nonorientable closed surface of Euler characteristic \(-3\). A separate complete subgroup-orbit census for that same \(K_{3,3}\) configuration complex already gives a projective plane under a three-cycle acting on **only one shore**. Thus the \(n=3\) instance here is prior knowledge, not claimed as a discovery. The census is restricted to \(K_{3,3}\) and does not imply the \(n\ge4\) formula.

Farley and Sabalka, building on the Abrams discretization theorem, describe the finite cubical model for graph configuration spaces under suitable graph-length hypotheses; this fact alone does not establish that a particular deformation can be descended through graph automorphism quotients. The direct symmetric coned-square argument in this proof supplies precisely that step. Farber and Hanbury give graph-configuration homology methods for complete bipartite graphs, but unquotiented Betti groups do not determine the ordinary \(C_3\)-quotient's attaching maps or integral homotopy type. A general disk-retraction result for quotients by the **entire** automorphism group of \(K_{m,n}\) concerns a larger symmetry group: the contractibility of that further quotient does not imply contractibility or the displayed projective-plane wedge for the proper \(C_3\) quotient. Published integral homology symmetry modules for ordered two-point complete bipartite configurations similarly do not determine this nonfree unordered quotient.

## Limitations
The result covers exactly two **unordered**, noncoincident geometric particles, the specified three-cycle on **only** the size-three vertex class, and every integer \(n\ge3\). It does not classify other subgroup actions, labeled configurations, the Borel quotient, higher-particle spaces, higher homotopy groups, or all maps induced by inclusion \(K_{3,n}\hookrightarrow K_{3,n+1}\). The explicit cell counts are for a finite equivariant retract, not a homeomorphism with the original noncompact continuous configuration space. The first member \(n=3\) is already known. A finite search cannot rule out all equivalent unindexed earlier research; independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
