# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a complete adjacency characteristic polynomial for the ordinary simple zero-divisor graph of \(\mathbb F_{2^e}[(C_2)^3]\), \(e\ge1\), with nonsingularity as a corollary. The proof establishes that every nonunit is in the seven-dimensional augmentation ideal, decomposes product-zero equations by squarefree degree, and checks exact block sizes. Its nontrivial step is the identification of degree-three incidence with the point-line pairing of \(\operatorname{PG}(2,q)\). Internal clique differences, repeated point-block differences, and the incidence singular-value identity \(NN^\top=qI+\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\) account for every eigenvalue. The determinant sign is proved for all \(q=2^e\), not inferred from examples. The extracted checker verified 8,001 exhaustive unordered pairs over \(\mathbb F_2\), seeded comparisons over \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_8\), projective-incidence identities, and all 127 direct eigenvalues over \(\mathbb F_2\). The finite tests are supporting evidence only.

## Originality — PASS, with qualified historical priority
Aliniaeifard–Li (2014), whose relevant full-text sections were inspected, proves group-ring zero-divisor graph diameter/girth and isomorphism-recognition theorems, not the displayed spectrum. Mönius (2020), whose relevant full-text sections were inspected, develops spectral machinery for finite rings and evaluates direct products of residue-class rings, without a formula for this non-chain local ring. The relevant published square-zero induced-graph result concerns a different nine-dimensional algebra; the modular exponent/minimum-degree result is a scalar graph invariant, and the squarefree-radical zero-product-determination result studies bilinear-map quotients. Those three published results were inspected in full as publicly available result documents. No searched database or literature record supplied an equivalent or stronger characteristic-polynomial statement. Residual risk: a more general, unlocated spectral classification or compressed-graph treatment could implicitly imply this calculation. No absolute publication-priority certification is claimed.

## Value — PASS
The ordinary zero-divisor graph is a standard, motivated invariant of finite modular group rings. This result does more than calculate a small graph: it gives the complete graph decomposition and spectral polynomial for every characteristic-two extension field, identifies a projective-plane incidence operator inside group-algebra multiplication, and proves universal real adjacency nonsingularity. These assertions cannot be read off from vertex counts, diameter, minimum degree, or the related induced square-zero graph in a different algebra. The exact all-field structure and nonsingularity are natural, reusable spectral facts rather than arbitrary isolated numerical parameters.
