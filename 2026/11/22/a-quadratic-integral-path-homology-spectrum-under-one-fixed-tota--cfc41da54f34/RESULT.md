# A quadratic integral path-homology spectrum under one fixed total-order reachability topology

## Finding
For every integer \(m\geq1\), on one fixed labeled set of \(4m\) vertices there is a family of \(2^{m^2}\) distinct simple directed acyclic graphs whose left and right vertex-path topologies are **identical**, respectively, to the upper-set and lower-set Alexandrov topologies of a total order. Nevertheless their **first integral GLMY path-homology groups** realize every isomorphism type
\[
H_1^{\mathrm{path}}(G;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^k,\qquad 0\leq k\leq m^2.
\]
In fact the family is canonically indexed by subsets of an \(m\times m\) grid of possible forward arcs, and its first path-homology rank is exactly the cardinality of that subset. The conclusion holds for both the regular and non-regular GLMY complexes. By contrast, their reachability homology is identical and has zero positive-degree groups. The quadratic scale is asymptotically order-optimal among simple acyclic digraphs on the same number of vertices, although **no optimal leading constant is asserted**.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(V_m=\{0,1,\ldots,4m-1\}\). Include all chain arcs \(i\to i+1\), for \(0\leq i<4m-1\). Define the separated parity sets
\[
A_m=\{0,2,\ldots,2m-2\},\qquad B_m=\{2m+1,2m+3,\ldots,4m-1\}.
\]
For any subset \(S\subseteq A_m\times B_m\), let \(G_m(S)\) have exactly the chain arcs and additional chords \(a\to b\), for \((a,b)\in S\). Edges are simple, loops are excluded, and all vertices are distinct integer labels. We use the ordinary integral path homology of Grigor'yan–Lin–Muranov–Yau: \(\mathcal A_p\) is the free abelian group on allowed directed \(p\)-paths; \(\Omega_p=\{c\in\mathcal A_p:\partial c\in\mathcal A_{p-1}\}\), with the alternating vertex-deletion differential. In particular \(\Omega_1=\mathcal A_1\). Regular versus non-regular makes no difference for these graphs, since every directed path has strictly increasing vertex labels.

The induced left-path topology is generated by all reachable-vertex sets \(\{j:j\geq i\}\), and the right-path topology by all predecessor sets \(\{j:j\leq i\}\), as in the construction of Muranov–Muranova, Section 5. These two topologies are distinct from one another but **each is invariant when \(S\) changes**.

## Proof
**Identical path topologies.** Every arc is directed from a smaller to a larger label. The chain arcs \(i\to i+1\) give a directed path from \(i\) to every \(j\geq i\). Thus the reachability relation for every choice of \(S\) is precisely the same total order. The left and right path-topology bases from the collection of all directed walks are its upper and lower principal sets, respectively. These finite Alexandrov spaces have a minimum or maximum, hence contractible order complexes. Normalized reachability homology is the integral homology of the nerve of the same total order and is therefore independent of \(S\), with positive-degree homology zero.

**No allowable two-chains.** Every directed two-step path belongs to one of exactly three disjoint types: (i) two consecutive chain arcs \(i\to i+1\to i+2\), whose missing endpoint arc would go across a gap of two; (ii) a chain arc then one chord \((a-1)\to a\to b\), whose endpoints are both odd and differ by at least four; (iii) one chord then a chain arc \(a\to b\to(b+1)\), whose endpoints are both even and differ by at least four. Some paths in (ii) or (iii) are absent at the boundary; this does not affect the argument. Two successive chords are impossible because all chord targets lie in \(B_m\), outside the set of chord sources \(A_m\).

No two distinct allowed two-step paths have the same ordered pair of endpoints: the three types are distinguished by the endpoint gap and parity, while within each type the intermediate vertex is uniquely determined by the endpoint pair. Their shortcut arcs are always absent: type (i) has gap two, type (ii) begins at an odd label, and type (iii) ends at an even label, whereas every chord begins at an even label and ends at an odd label. Consequently the projection of the formal boundary \(\partial:\mathcal A_2\to\Lambda_1\) onto the free abelian group generated by **missing ordered arcs** sends each allowed two-step basis path to minus a *different* missing-arc basis vector. This projection is injective over \(\mathbb Z\). In particular,
\[
\Omega_2(G_m(S);\mathbb Z)=0
\]
for both regular and non-regular complexes. This is also a direct application of the established double-edge/triangle/long-square generating theorem for two-chains, but the injective projection proves the assertion independently.

**Entire first homology, integrally.** The underlying unoriented graph is connected via its spanning path. With \(n=4m\) vertices and \(n-1+|S|\) edges, the integral incidence map \(\partial_1:\mathbb Z^E\to\mathbb Z^V\) has rank \(n-1\), and its kernel is a free abelian group of rank \(|S|\). An explicit basis indexed by the chords is
\[
z_{ab}=e_{ab}-\sum_{j=a}^{b-1}e_{j,j+1},\qquad (a,b)\in S.
\]
Each \(z_{ab}\) has zero boundary. The chord coefficient of \(z_{ab}\) is one, and all its other chord coefficients are zero; eliminating every chord coefficient from a one-cycle leaves a cycle supported on a tree, necessarily zero. These are therefore an **integral** basis for \(\ker\partial_1\), not merely a rational basis. Since \(\Omega_2=0\), no class is killed by a two-boundary. Hence
\[
H_1^{\mathrm{path}}(G_m(S);\mathbb Z)
=\ker(\partial_1)/\partial\Omega_2
=\bigoplus_{(a,b)\in S}\mathbb Z[z_{ab}].
\]
The two size-\(m\) sets yield \(m^2\) independently selectable chords. Choosing exactly \(k\) of them realizes each integer \(0\leq k\leq m^2\), without changing either finite vertex topology.

**Quadratic-order comparison.** Any simple \(n\)-vertex acyclic digraph has at most \(\binom n2\) arcs. If its reachability relation is a total order, the underlying graph is connected, and the first path-homology rank is bounded above by its graph cycle rank, at most \(\binom n2-(n-1)=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2\). Our family reaches \(m^2=n^2/16\) for \(n=4m\); thus the possible fixed-topology discrepancy already has quadratic order. This comparison is a growth-rate statement, **not** an extremal solution for the optimal constant or exact maximum.

## Verification
A separate standard-library Python checker `verify_quadratic_path_topology.py` re-enumerates all allowed chord subsets at \(m=1,2,3\) and one explicit graph of every admissible first Betti rank at \(m=4,5,6,7,8\): **725 distinct graph instances**. It computes reachability by transitive closure, enumerates every directed two-step path and checks distinct missing endpoint pairs, then verifies every written integral cycle's signed incidence and unique chord pivot. The reproduced summary is `PASS: 725 graph instances`. This is bounded corroboration only; the unbounded assertion rests on the proof above.

Run with a standard Python 3 installation: `python3 verify_quadratic_path_topology.py`. There are no optional dependencies. The file reproduces the complete finite experiment from the graph-definition formula in this document rather than loading external logs or certificates.

## Relationship to prior work
Muranov and Muranova (2026, Sections 5–6) introduce the left/right vertex-path Alexandrov topologies and the associated digraph relationships; they do not state the fixed-total-order \(m^2\)-rank-spectrum theorem. Hepworth and Roff (2023, Introduction and Example 2.7) already **explicitly demonstrate** that different directed graphs with the same reachability relation can have different path homology. Thus mere failure of reachability to determine path homology is **not** claimed as new. Their examples have indiscrete reachability preorders, whereas the present family has a fixed antisymmetric *linear* order and fills every integer first Betti rank in a quadratic-size interval.

Chaplin (2022, Proposition 3.17, citing Grigor'yan et al. 2014 Proposition 2.9) recalls the stronger general theorem that integral regular second path-chains are generated by reciprocal double edges, directed triangles and long squares. It independently implies our vanishing \(\Omega_2\) after one verifies the absence of these motifs. This known theorem is not claimed as original. The contribution is the explicit parity-isolated family with complete quantitative rank spectrum, fixed labeled \(T_0\) path topologies, integral cycle bases and quadratic-order comparison. Targeted searches have not established exhaustive publication uniqueness.

## Limitations
The theorem determines **degree-one** GLMY path homology, not all higher groups or the entire chain-homotopy type. It does not claim that all graphs with a given total-order reachability relation belong to this family, nor an exact global extremal first Betti number. The spectrum is exhibited for the vertex counts divisible by four. Existing qualitative reachability/path-homology counterexamples and the general degree-two motif criterion are expressly credited. Unindexed equivalent constructions remain a literature-priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
