# Scientific review of the narrowed result

## Correctness — PASS

Direct differentiation of the log of the second log difference yields a strict negative-curvature estimate. Its exact triangular integral over the observation interval gives the stated lower bound, with the minimum attained by one of two endpoints. The relaxing-hazard log curvatures are geometric and have zero log-ratio. The proof assumes exact homogeneous spacing, positive rates with distinct pool rates, and the two specified functional forms. The decimal example is an illustration, not proof. An independent proof of the classical observation-count context follows from the discrete moment representation and the two-node quadrature argument, with positive weights and strictly positive reconstructed rates.

## Originality — PASS for the quantitative bound only

Classical Hankel rank covers qualitative separation at five points. Two-node Gauss quadrature on the positive relaxing-hazard moment measure gives a stronger four-sample overlap than merely finding one witness. Neither observation-count claim is represented as new. The specific lower bound on the log-curvature ratio depends on mixture balance and rate contrast, and does not follow from moment rank or degree-three exactness. Closely related indexed decay results concern other observables or inverse problems. No examined comparison supplies this quantitative bound, but an equivalent older inequality, including in the inaccessible Faddy–Jones full text, remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The bound supplies a scale-invariant separation margin for exact data, making explicit its dependence on rate contrast and balance. It gives more information than rank-based distinguishability and is usable as a theoretical sensitivity diagnostic. It is not a uniform noise guarantee, a parameter-free lower bound, or a general inference of loss mechanism from measurements.
