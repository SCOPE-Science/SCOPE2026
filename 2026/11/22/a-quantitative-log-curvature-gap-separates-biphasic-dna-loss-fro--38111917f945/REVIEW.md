## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The published ecological material establishes the practical relevance of biphasic loss and decaying removal, and classical positive exponential-sum rank theory already implies the qualitative five-observation distinction. Those are acknowledged as existing coverage. The new claim is the particular strict *log-curvature ratio inequality*, its explicit lower bound dependent on the rate contrast and latent mixture balance, and a positive four-point alias against the relaxing-hazard class. Searches for these exact or equivalent inequalities, quantifiers and competing model pair found no covering statement in the inspected primary literature or queried published mathematical records. The 1999 Faddy–Jones paper is a plausible relevant source whose abstract was inspected but full text could not be accessed; its exact coverage is unresolved, so historical priority is provisional.

## Value — PASS
Biphasic environmental-DNA survival and declining effective removal are both motivated in the literature, but those trends can look similarly convex on log concentration plots. The inequality gives a scale-independent, model-specific local diagnostic from five evenly spaced measurements, with a parameter-dependent positive margin rather than only a qualitative rank condition. It identifies a finite minimum for universal separation within the explicitly defined sampling protocol and explains where the margin can become weak. The result is useful as a theoretical experiment-design benchmark, not a ready-to-deploy statistical test.

Augustine et al. (2024, DOI 10.1101/2024.03.27.586987) compare environmental-DNA removal models in a hierarchical framework, without the exact discrete discriminator. Zanni et al. (2026, DOI 10.1002/ece3.72987) simulate a genuine fast/slow-particle biphasic model but do not establish the bound. Faddy and Jones (1999, DOI 10.1111/1467-9876.00151) compare mixed-exponential and differential-equation decay models, and full text was inaccessible here; it remains a priority risk. Classical Prony/Hankel rank arguments cover the qualitative five-point distinctness, not the explicit log-domain lower bound asserted as new here. Exact, equally spaced, no-background concentrations and nondegenerate parameters are essential; empirical noise can overwhelm the contrast.
