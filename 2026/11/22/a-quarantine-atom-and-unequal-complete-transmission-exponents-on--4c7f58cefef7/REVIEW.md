# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof conditions correctly on the single shared exponential hub-recovery time, recovers the atom-plus-exponential stopped occupation law by a two-state first-step calculation, and derives the independent-edge survival kernel by the two eigenvalues of a killed generator. A dominated-convergence argument establishes the power-law full-transmission asymptotic and a small-time scaling establishes the zero-offspring asymptotic. The strict exponent inequality is checked algebraically for all positive rates. The attached checker independently solves absorbing finite-state systems in exact rational arithmetic for degrees two through five at unit rates and for three additional non-symmetric positive-rate vectors and compares analytic integrals at several large degrees. The convex-order observation is established with the nonnegative discrete second differences of the binomial expectation. No finite enumeration substitutes for the full proof.

## Originality — PASS (bounded)

The main scientific comparison is to Moraes–Ferreira arXiv:2610.00429v1: its all-node intermittent SIS/SIR simulations and star SIS persistence motivate the question, but the exact hub-only SIR offspring atom and two degree-tail exponents are not among its claims. The original publicly accessible HTML model and main results, including the detailed star section, were inspected; appendices and full references were not retrieved. Giordano–Martinucci–Paraggio (2026) gives two-state switched-Poisson distributions and long-time count asymptotics, not the exponentially killed shared-hub/independent-link *all-neighbor* transmission comparison, so its general matrix-analysis machinery is acknowledged as established. Hilton–Hall (2023) already studies beta-Poisson and zero-inflated offspring models, showing that the general mixture form and zero inflation are prior art, not the original claim. Ball–Britton–Leung–Sirl (2019) distinguishes independent edge dropping and shared recovery, but not reversible source activity and this strict pair of exponents. Published research-index searches found close findings on convex order of random individual transmissibility and on star epidemic correlation, but these cover only the ancillary Jensen effect, not the specified exact occupation atom and contrasting degree-exponents. The closest matching published record's full code could not be retrieved; priority remains qualified rather than proven unique.

## Value — PASS

A contact budget or a one-edge infection probability does not determine whether a highly connected index case infects nobody or everybody. The theorem identifies both extremes quantitatively: shared quarantine leaves a nonzero zero-offspring atom and a slower polynomial all-infected tail, whereas independently cycling links have a vanishing zero-offspring probability and a strictly faster all-infected tail. This is a motivated structural boundary for epidemic tail-risk approximations, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The all-neighbor exponent is specifically relevant to hub-source outbreaks and to evaluating correlated versus independently organized contact suppression.

## Scope and remaining risks

The model is one hub with initially susceptible permanent-availability leaves; the source's fully node-intermittent network differs. Exponential switching and recovery, stationary clocks and fixed positive rates are essential to the explicit formulas. No extension to a full network's endemicity is claimed. An unindexed older exact comparison or general theorem may exist. Scientific correctness is reviewed here, but no independent mathematical audit has occurred.
