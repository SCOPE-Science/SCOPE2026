# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed strict sign follows from an exact forest/permutation count, factorial-moment integral and a pointwise strictly negative integrand when \(s\) is odd and \(2s\ge n\). The endpoint \(s=n\) is removable, not a singularity. Independent enumeration of mappings through six vertices and exact polynomial identities support the algebra. The claim does not extrapolate finite experiments to an infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison

The classical cyclic-root enumeration and forest-plus-permutation representation are expressly prior work. Hansen–Jaworski (2010), inspected across its 19 pages, analyzes conditional component sizes and limiting distributions, not the all-order Walsh sign assertion. Published results about the cyclic-vertex joint law, negative dependence and pairwise correlation control distinct quantities; they do not alone imply the sign of high-order alternating parity moments. Relevant indexed material was compared at the level of implications, not just titles. Risk: an older unindexed signed-transform inequality or a direct special-function reformulation could contain the sign result.

## Value — PASS

The result establishes a uniform spectral obstruction for every dimension: at least a quarter of Boolean Fourier modes asymptotically have negative eigenvalues. It is a structural, quantitative inertia theorem for a dependent, naturally occurring combinatorial distribution; no general invertibility claim is needed for the stated value.

## Scope and uncertainty

No exact inertia or zero set is claimed. The evidence does not establish absolute historical priority. The 2010 record supplies the verified exact public date for the archival source; earlier related literature is acknowledged without invented day dates.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
