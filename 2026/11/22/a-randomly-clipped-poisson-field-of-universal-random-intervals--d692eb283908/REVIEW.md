# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The universal-interval identity follows by comparing each left endpoint to every other right endpoint, and each right endpoint to every other left endpoint. Each of the three local endpoint clouds is an iid rare-event point process with uniform intensity two; disjoint source regions give independent Poisson limits. The two extremal thresholds are the first points of triangular Poisson clouds, so their tails are exactly \(e^{-t^2}\). A tight random-rectangle clipping argument proves convergence of the *marked* measure, not just its count. The mixed-Poisson integral gives the claimed factorial formula and moments. Exhaustive finite matching checks corroborate the identity and classical absence probability. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Justicz–Scheinerman–Winkler (1990) and the fully inspected degree chapter of Iliopoulos (2015) establish and credit \(\Pr(U_n>0)=2/3\), so the classical event probability is expressly excluded. A 2026 Sarkar article explicitly concerns the unmarked number of universal intervals; its accessible abstract gives no marked-spatial theorem, but its full text was unavailable. The new statement compared here is the joint three-corner limit with two independent Rayleigh boundaries and a conditionally uniform Poisson *point field* of scaled endpoint locations. Neither the known event probability nor a formula for marginal counts implies this joint spatial distribution. Searches for aliases under universal/full-degree intervals, endpoint extremes, marked point processes and mixed Poisson laws found no directly covering statement in accessible materials. Residual risk: an older or inaccessible work might already contain the marked theorem; this is a self-review rather than certified priority.

## Value — PASS

Universal vertices are the full-degree vertices of a standard interval-intersection random graph. Prior work determines whether they exist, but a count alone cannot describe where the intersect-all intervals lie, their conditional endpoint deficits, or how randomness of the two opposite boundary extremes shapes their multiplicity. The theorem supplies that natural conditional geometry at its critical \(n^{-1/2}\) scale, as well as a reproducible distribution-free marked limit; it is neither a parameter substitution nor a table recomputation.

## Closest literature and limits

Iliopoulos, *Random Interval Graphs*, arXiv:1507.06275v1, Section 4.3, and its 1990 source Justicz–Scheinerman–Winkler, *Random Intervals*. Sarkar (2026), *How Many Random Intervals Intersect All Others?*, is a plausible full-count-law source, not a verified marked-process source; full text was unavailable. No convergence rate or nonquantile Euclidean universality is established.
