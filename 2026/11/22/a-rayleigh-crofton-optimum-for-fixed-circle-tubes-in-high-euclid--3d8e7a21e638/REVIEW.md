# Scientific review: fixed-circle tube Crofton optimization

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is specified as the unit tube about a *fixed two-dimensional circle* in \(\mathbb R^d\), for integer \(d\ge4\) and radius ratio \(r>1\), with motion-invariant affine-hyperplane measure conditioned on intersection. A rotation of the normal leaves only its two-dimensional projection length \(S\). Shrinking the \(d-3\) unused orthogonal coordinates defines an explicit strong deformation retraction of every generic section onto a classical three-dimensional torus slice, so the claimed three types are precisely homotopy classes. The meridian offset lengths are \(2\min(rS,1)\), \((rS-1)_+\), and \((1-rS)_+\), summing to \(1+rS\). Integrating them against the \(\operatorname{Beta}(1,(d-2)/2)\) normal-projection law proves the exact probability formulas, including normalization.

Differentiating the truncated first moment yields \(P_d'(r)=2\mu_d[I_{r^{-2}}(3/2,b)-(1-r^{-2})^b]/(1+r\mu_d)^2\), so strict monotonicity of the two bracketed terms proves a unique finite optimizer. At \(d=4\), the condition reduces to the plastic-constant cubic. Gaussian representation of a uniform direction proves the Rayleigh limit, while two elementary envelope bounds keep the finite-dimensional optimizers in a compact \(\sqrt d\)-scale window. Strict concavity of the limiting truncated-moment numerator establishes a unique limiting maximizer. These steps prove the infinite-dimensional assertions independently of computation. A bundled independent numerical formula implementation passed 246 finite checks, including direct quadrature and the known dimension-three consistency value. Risk: finite checks do not verify every possible dimension or all geometric degeneracies; the proof explicitly excludes zero-measure transition sections.

## Originality — PASS against accessible evidence
Finch, *Slicing a Doughnut at Random* (arXiv:2611.15546v1, 2026-11-11), was inspected through its theorem, sections on invariant plane measure, the complete meridian proof and its references. It treats \(\mathbb R^3\), where the present law reduces to his formula; that specialization is credited and not asserted as new. The published *A three-topology Crofton law and inverse support tomography for convex-meridian toroids* treats planar cuts of solids of revolution only in \(\mathbb R^3\) and does not imply the fixed-core \(d\ge4\) normal-projection law or Rayleigh asymptotic. The published *Gaussian-optimal Crofton ball sections of higher-dimensional spherical tori* treats a geometrically distinct core \(S^{d-2}\) instead of a fixed \(S^1\), producing an optimizer approaching one rather than diverging like \(\sqrt d\). That entire published result was inspected, including its definition of the solid, probability law, proof and limitations. Standard Crofton theory concerns the invariant measure, but the cited general text does not provide this shape-dependent topological classification. Searches for equivalents using the body, aliases, incomplete-beta optimizer, plastic constant, and Rayleigh limit did not reveal covering prior results. Risk: incomplete indexing and historical stereology literature could contain an equivalent classification; absence from a search is not proof of absolute priority.

## Value — PASS
A stable, fixed circular spine is a natural higher-dimensional extension of the three-dimensional random-slicing problem, geometrically distinct from the previously treated family with an expanding spherical spine. The result determines the entire generic topological probability law, its unique finite-dimensional optimal tube ratio, the exact first nontrivial algebraic optimizer in \(\mathbb R^4\), and a sharp high-dimensional Rayleigh regime. It identifies a structural contrast: one natural toroidal embedding has optimal success tending to one with radius near the horn boundary, while the fixed-circle embedding has a diverging optimal radius and a nontrivial success ceiling. This is not merely renaming the original three-dimensional formula: the dimension-dependent two-coordinate Gaussian projection and compactness argument create a different limit. The fact is useful for understanding topology under isotropic stereological sampling of high-codimension tubes; no applications outside this model are asserted.
