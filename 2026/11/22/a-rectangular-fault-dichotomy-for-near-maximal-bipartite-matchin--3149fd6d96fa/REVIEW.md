# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The only forbidden faces are the full edge stars at vertices whose graph degree remains \(n\). A surviving edge internal to the bounding fault rectangle is in no such star and hence is a cone apex. For a complete fault rectangle, every surviving edge occurs in an intact star, while explicit edges incident to a deficient opposite-shore vertex furnish a private element for every intact star. A proved suspension lemma, using a conical vertex deletion and induction on the nonprivate elements, gives exactly one sphere of dimension \(n^2-|R||C|-2n+|R|+|C|-1\), with integral coefficients and the \(S^0\) boundary handled. The extracted verifier exhausts all 524 nontrivial faults at orders two and three, plus 15 canonical rectangle types at order four. Finite mod-two checks support, but do not replace, the all-order proof.

## Value — PASS
The answer is a complete, exact structural classification for arbitrary edge-fault sets, not a numerical special case. It reveals an abrupt transition controlled by the *shape* of the failures: two adjacent faults preserve a sphere while two disjoint faults destroy every homotopy group. When faults form a rectangle, the exact dimension decrease is a product of both nontrivial fault dimensions. This makes topology of degree-constrained edge selections explicitly sensitive to clustering even when the host graph and the degree constraint remain fixed.
