They do not state or imply the four-jump absorbing-chain obstruction, the low-step probability transition, or the nonstochastic ensemble mean. Different numerical integrators exhibit related five-state positive-cone failures, but their stability functions do not determine this method's exact thresholds. Classical SSP and absolute-monotonicity theories govern broader worst-case properties without supplying the sampled five-state phase law. **Value (PASS).** Markov transition positivity is a natural structural property of numerical semigroups, distinct from spectral or algebraic stability. Here the completed time step is row-sum preserving yet fails to be stochastic with asymptotically 50% probability for arbitrarily small steps. There is nevertheless a sharp finite interval in which *every* random tableau draw becomes stochastic. This complete and reproducible sign phase diagram supplies a benchmark for randomized stiff solvers and shows why decreasing the step does not monotonically improve an entrywise invariance property.

The closest sources are the 2026 randomized SDIRK paper, two published exact analyses of its scalar and two-state Markov behavior, and nearby five-state analyses for distinct rational methods. The result is restricted to a five-state, unit-rate absorbing birth process; there is no claim of a generic Markov-chain or nonlinear positivity theorem. The finite verification checker is not an infinite certificate. Older specialist equivalences remain a residual originality risk.
