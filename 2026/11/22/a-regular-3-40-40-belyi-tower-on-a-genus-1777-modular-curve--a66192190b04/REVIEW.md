# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The subgroup of \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/120\mathbb Z)\) defining the curve has local factors \(Z_8\), the six-element upper-triangular subgroup modulo \(3\), and \(\{\pm I\}\) modulo \(5\). Both scalar factors are central, proving normality in \(\Gamma_0(3)\). The finite quotient has order \(5760\) and group \(Q_8\times A_5\). The unique elliptic order-three point and two cusps of \(X_0(3)\) have respective inertia \(3,40,40\); this is proved with conjugated cusp translations, not guessed from the genus. Riemann--Hurwitz yields genus \(1777\). Taking the normal core of the level-\(120\) subgroup gives \(Q_8\times A_4\times A_5\) of order \(69120\) and a regular closure of genus \(5473\). The included checker confirms exact local orders, orbit counts and arithmetic formulas. The established source genus and Jacobian factorization are not relabeled as new.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations

Auffarth's arXiv:2611.16963v1, Sections 1–2, supplies the subgroup, its index and 288 cusps, but not a regular three-point quotient, its deck group, the two 40-fold relative cusp ramifications, the full canonical \(j\)-monodromy, or its Galois-closure genus. Paulhus–Sutherland's arXiv:2502.16007 concerns a preceding search for completely decomposable modular Jacobians and does not contain the new genus-1777 quotient. A separately indexed regular Belyi construction for Fermat power-sum curves has a different signature \((2,4,3d)\) and does not dominate this exact subgroup result. The inspected mathematical literature did not contain an equivalent genus-1777, index-23040, order-5760 or order-69120 Belyi computation. The construction is a consequence of the published subgroup presentation combined with classical congruence-curve methods; unpublished or unindexed historic modular group tables could predate this particular calculation.

## Value — PASS

The object is the record genus-\(1777\) completely decomposable modular Jacobian curve. Its canonical finite automorphism quotient is a natural intrinsic geometric structure to calculate: it exhibits the curve as a regular three-point cover (quasiplatonic), supplies a nonabelian deck group with an \(A_5\) direct factor, and fixes the exact geometric monodromy and Galois closure of the modular \(j\)-map. This is a complete, checkable automorphism-cover invariant for a named current curve, not a small improvement or an arbitrary numerical slice. It does not attempt to claim the full automorphism group or a new Jacobian decomposition.
