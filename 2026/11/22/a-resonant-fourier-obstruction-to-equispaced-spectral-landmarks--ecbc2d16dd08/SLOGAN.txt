For every even landmark count \(m\ge6\), a strictly positive-definite real-analytic circle kernel has a leading three-mode space on which both a regular grid and a staggered two-coset grid form tight sampling frames, yet the regular grid has spectral alignment loss at least \(1/3\) while the staggered grid has loss at most \((5/2)\varepsilon(1-2^{1-m})\). Thus equispacing can be arbitrarily worse for spectral landmarks when a high-frequency Fourier resonance aliases into the leading eigenspace.
