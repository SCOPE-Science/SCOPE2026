# Review of the second-Greene-width signed-chain outer-sphere theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The all-poset proof is reconstructed from signed-chain inequalities, not from integer enumeration: positive and negative coordinate parts individually lie in Stanley's chain polytope, so the signed-chain body is exactly its Minkowski difference. Antichain indicator generators give the sharp squared-norm bound by the second Greene width. The equality case is strict and forces a single disjoint two-antichain indicator; antipodal contacts force the unique center. Proper two-colorings of each maximizing induced comparability graph yield the complete contact count. Exact finite verification covers 407 naturally labeled posets on at most five points, with 36,947 checked integer points and 3,158 contact occurrences; this does not prove the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

The original Beck--Hlavacek 2023 Section 6 states the signed-chain inequalities and integer-point structure, not the Euclidean outer sphere or the real-polytope identity with Stanley's difference body. Stanley's 1986 result provides antichain indicator vertices, and Greene--Kleitman 1976 owns the second-width combinatorial invariant; neither is claimed as new. The closest identified published geometric results treat the enriched chain polytope (single signed-antichain hull), the order-polytope difference body (inballs), and the symmetrically twinned chain polytope (convex hull rather than Minkowski difference). Their full available proofs were compared. These do not imply the claimed new body-specific radius/contact classification without additional arguments. An unindexed equivalent observation remains a residual historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is an exact natural invariant of an entire infinite poset-polytope class, recovering the Greene--Kleitman second width from Euclidean geometry. Its complete contact count depends on two-colorings of maximal two-families. The sharp enclosing-radius comparison with enriched chain polytopes separates constructions with similar names, with equality criteria; chain and antichain families have substantially different contact multiplicities. These structural statements are more than computation of a special small example.

## Limits

Only the ordinary-poset specialization of the signed-chain polytope is classified; arbitrary genuinely signed posets and affine-distorted coordinate embeddings are excluded. The 1976 width invariant, 1986 antichain-vertex theorem, and 2023 signed-chain definition remain credited as prior work. Self-review does not substitute for independent proof checking or external historical-priority verification.
