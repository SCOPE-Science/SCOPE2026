# Scientific review of the rooted-tree order-ideal Fourier transition

## Correctness

PASS. The indicator is defined on the full binary group; every ideal has the root-decomposition required for \(W=1+\epsilon\prod W_i\). Parity of the ideal count gives odd-height invertibility. Counting the unit coefficients at odd heights and cancelling products at even heights yields the exact nullity recurrence, including the single-vertex base case. Exact rational Bernstein coefficients prove the finite branching inequalities for \(d=2\) through \(6\). A Bernoulli inequality and a one-turning-point logarithmic ratio argument prove the two positive fixed points and positive limiting null density for every \(d\ge7\). Actual standalone Walsh and symbolic verification programs pass; numerical limits are illustrative only.

## Originality

PASS under bounded comparison. Exact searches for order-ideal Fourier support, rooted-tree Walsh convolution, branching-seven nullity, equivalent tree-poset signed enumerators, and published work revealed no direct predecessor. Restrepo et al. (arXiv:1007.2255) work with hard-core independent sets and Markov-chain relaxation, not the additive Fourier spectrum of ancestor-closed sets. O'Donnell's Fourier-convolution treatment provides general formalism, not the explicit rooted-poset recurrence or branching threshold. The closest indexed items about tree colourings, tree-poset ideal-lattice counting, and graph-exclusion Fourier interpolation involve different observables. The accessible order-ideal theorem for a product of two chains has a modulo-four rank law and zero limiting null fraction; the present rooted-tree families are not such products, and the branching-seven positive limiting fraction is not implied. Unindexed historical signed-enumerator results remain a residual originality risk.

## Value

PASS. The complete regular rooted tree is a canonical hierarchical poset. The result gives an exact finite-height loss-of-information count, a whole infinite family of invertible operators at alternating heights, and a sharp, unexpected branch-six/branch-seven phase boundary for the fraction of Fourier modes that are irrecoverable. It requires no arbitrary frequency slice and differs materially from mere tree-ideal enumeration; it gives the rank of the full operator on the ambient Boolean group.

The theorem does not cover irregular tree shapes, reconstruction under noise, or independent-set mixing dynamics.
