# Scientific evaluation: positive-box direction normalization

## Correctness — PASS
For each positive input, the exact derivative of the Euclidean direction map is \(S^{-1/2}(I-xx^{\mathsf T}/S)\); its induced coordinate-maximum norm equals the maximum absolute row sum. The positive orthant fixes the signs of every Jacobian entry, so the row formula in RESULT.md has no unrecorded cancellations. Holding the sum of tail squares fixed, Cauchy–Schwarz shows that an equal tail maximizes its sum and preserves coordinate feasibility. Homogeneity forces the smallest permissible common scale, leading to two one-variable functions; their derivative signs are analyzed on the complete allowed intervals. On ratios no greater than one, monotonicity is nonnegative for all dimensions; above one the sole critical point is \((\sqrt{8n+1}-3)/4\), giving the exact dimension-six/seven boundary. Segment integration gives the upper global bound and signed two-sided perturbations at interior approximations give the matching sharp lower bound. The singleton and one-dimensional exceptional cases are separate. ## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
The original RMSNorm source, Zhang–Sennrich (arXiv:1910.07467), Section 4, gives the rescaling-normalization map and its standard projected-direction Jacobian, but not the sharp positive-box coefficient or contrast extremization. The related source, Singh–Krishnan (arXiv:1911.09737), introduces uncentered second-moment normalization and derives its projected gradient; its actual operator includes a regularization offset, scale/shift and an activation, without a sharp positive-box coordinatewise two-input coefficient. Dasoulas–Scaman–Virmaux (arXiv:2103.04886) establish attention-module Lipschitz controls for different operators and metrics. Qi–Wang–Zhang (arXiv:2306.09338), especially Table 2 and Appendix A.2, do display the same foundational Euclidean normalization Jacobian and a general spectral-norm bound for regularized RMSNorm, but not the present sharp maximum-entry box optimization, its dimension-seven change in extremizing input, or its contrast saturation threshold. The published all-dimensional radial-retraction cross-norm result concerns a different map and unrestricted directions; its unconstrained formulas do not imply the exact restrictive positive-box value. Search and full-text comparison did not reveal dominating prior statements, but earlier unindexed coordinate-conditioning calculations could contain an equivalent result. This is a qualified originality assessment, not a historical priority certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a structural sharp robustness law for the widely used mathematical operation of Euclidean direction normalization. It determines the precise two-input coordinate noise amplification from a physically meaningful dynamic-range constraint, identifies the counterintuitive threshold where homogeneous inputs stop being worst, and gives two different high-dimensional regimes (fixed contrast versus contrast growing with dimension). These are natural input-domain parameters, not arbitrary low-dimensional slices. The result can be applied directly to error budgets for the zero-offset normalization component but does not establish stability of an entire network, centered/signed activation case, or stabilized epsilon-positive normalization. The elementary derivative alone is prior knowledge; the equality-attaining restricted nonlinear optimization is the substantive contribution.
