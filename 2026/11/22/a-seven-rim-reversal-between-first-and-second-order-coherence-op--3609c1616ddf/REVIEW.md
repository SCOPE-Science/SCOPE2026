# Scientific review: wheel inverse-spectral-moment allocation reversal

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a strict, all-size comparison between the unique unrestricted unit-budget conductance optimizers of \(\operatorname{tr}(L^+)\) and \(\operatorname{tr}((L^+)^2)\) on wheels. Strict convexity of the two spectral traces on the common connected-support domain proves uniqueness against independently weighted competitors. Rotation symmetry then yields equal spoke conductances and equal rim conductances, and Fourier diagonalization gives the exact one-dimensional objectives. A nonzero negative derivative at the zero-rim boundary and a divergent hub term at the disconnected boundary prove interior optimization. For rim sizes 4 through 10, the stated rational separating points have exact-rational derivative signs; `verify.py` reproduces these with fraction arithmetic. For all rim sizes at least 11, explicit Poisson-kernel identities and the strict inequalities at \(u=1/3\) prove the ranking analytically. The three-rim graph is \(K_4\). Uniform Riemann-sum convergence and uniquely signed limiting derivatives prove both asymptotic optima, and the second-order cost ratio follows by substitution. Every step supports the single final claim; no finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification

Ghosh, Boyd and Saberi's 2008 expanded full text (section 5, wheel example) explicitly covers the first inverse moment on weighted wheels and provides the spectral reduction. It does not treat the squared inverse moment or compare its optimizer with the first-order optimum. Their 2006 conference version presents the general conductance allocation framework but only develops the barbell example in its explicit-design section. Patterson and Bamieh's original 2013 arXiv text establishes the second-order coherence spectral functional and studies fractal families; it neither optimizes wheel conductances nor asserts a crossover in rim allocation. Other inspected wheel results on published mathematical records optimize price-weighted first-moment resistance or discrete grounded-leader placements and differ in both feasible variables and objectives; neither implies the new conclusion by a stronger-parameter specialization. An inspected second-order star design uses inverse-squared coherence but concerns a tree without a rim cycle and heterogeneous edge prices. Keyword/semantic searches over accessible records and literature did not identify an equivalent theorem, but indexing gaps and uninspected work remain a real priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The graph and the two spectral moments are standard, motivated choices in continuous network design and noisy-consensus control. The new conclusion is not that second-order coherence merely changes an allocation: its **direction of change reverses exactly once**, with a sharp finite cutoff between six and seven rim vertices, while the infinite-size first- and second-order allocations approach two different algebraic fractions. The disagreement has a rigorously computed nonvanishing relative penalty of approximately \(4.594\%\) for using the first-order design under the second-order objective. The cutoff and limiting penalty are meaningful guidance about when cross-objective conductance reuse becomes suboptimal; they are not claims of a universal rule outside wheels.

## Limits and nearest coverage

The contribution relies on the precise unnormalized Laplacian, homogeneous unit conductance budget, connected support, and first versus second inverse powers. It is not a new general convexity theorem. The first-order wheel optimizer, spectral eigenvalues, and the general second-order trace metric are known antecedents. Directly related published wheel-leader and priced-resistance results were compared at the level of objective, variables, and implications, including their full RESULT texts. Unindexed equivalents and independent validation remain unresolved.
