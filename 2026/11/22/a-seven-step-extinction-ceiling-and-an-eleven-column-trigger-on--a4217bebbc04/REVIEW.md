# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed rule is exactly the three-state, two-contact synchronous Greenberg–Hastings update on the open Cartesian strip. The proof separates a mathematical lightcone reduction from its finite exact certificate. The ternary ordered BDD implements the Boolean recurrence for all three initial values, canonically merges equal functions, and proves zero excitation at time six on each of the thirteen necessary finite widths. The separate recurrence and direct trajectory replay establish the maximum-extinction relation, including refractory-state clearance one step later. Every lower-bound width has a positive BDD witness; the eleven-column witness persists by the radius-five cone lemma. The exact eleven-column event count and independent-block probability argument are reproducible. A separately constructed libz3 encoding obtained the same thirteen maxima. An independent external audit remains unperformed.

## Originality — PASS, with risk

The original Greenberg–Hastings literature treats planar excitation and asymptotic spiral behavior, not the three-row finite-length, threshold-two cutoff or exact eleven-column onset. The arbitrary-graph three-color analysis uses threshold one, so its single-contact mechanism cannot be substituted to obtain this theorem. Related published threshold-two exact results for two-row ladders and wheels use different adjacency structures and do not imply the width-three eleven-column trigger. Results about cyclic threshold automata are also distinct from the refractory Greenberg–Hastings dynamics. Targeted indexed research searches for the named graph family, its Cartesian-product aliases, bounded-time nilpotency and eleven-column onset found no dominating claim. Older or unindexed equivalent literature remains a genuine priority risk; search absence is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

An exact uniform extinction horizon on a natural infinite graph family is more informative than an isolated finite census. The sharp dependence on length has a nontrivial four-step sequence of onset thresholds and establishes a width-dependent causal locality phenomenon: a bounded window decides every trajectory in arbitrarily long strips. The exact critical-window count gives a quantitative and initially counterintuitive finite-time concentration statement: seven-step extinction becomes typical although permanent activity is impossible. The result connects deterministic nilpotency, finite-state symbolic certificates and a probabilistic block argument without claiming a general grid-width theorem.

## Limits

The exact finite BDD identity is necessary for the current proof and is provided in reproducible source code; no short purely symbolic simplification has been established. The random-length inequality is a lower bound, not an exact distribution beyond the eleven-column case. Broader widths and other color counts are outside scope. The earliest reviewed archival seed is Durrett–Griffeath (22 March 1993). The accessible text of that source is partly garbled, and some close published reports could not be opened in complete full text, leaving historical-priority risk.
