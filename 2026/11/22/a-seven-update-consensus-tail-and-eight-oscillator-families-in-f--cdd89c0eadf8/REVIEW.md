# Scientific review of the Folkman majority census

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem has a finite, completely specified domain of \(2^{20}\) labelled configurations and an explicit degree-four local tie-retaining map. An exhaustive integer functional-graph computation determines the first periodic-entry time and least period of every configuration. A separately implemented vectorized iterate-identity checker obtains the identical 12 nonzero time/period populations without using the functional-graph method. The sharp witness and equal split of 960 time-seven states between unanimous outcomes are directly checked. The 44/8 symmetry-class classification uses the full graph automorphism generators, whose completeness follows from the five duplicate-neighborhood classes, and is independently checkable with the supplied orbit-enumeration program. All counting is finite and exact; no asymptotic extrapolation is claimed.

The 1967 Folkman work addresses graph construction and semisymmetry, not a published majority census in the accessible abstract. The complete Kaaser–Mallmann-Trenn–Natale article treats general convergence and graph-family contraction, not this graph's 4036/351 exact recurrent populations, 44/8 symmetry classes or seven-step attraction tail. Closest published exact computations concern distinct McGee, Nauru, Tutte–Coxeter, and other graphs, not the Folkman graph; their counts do not imply the present classification. Searches for aliases, constructions, numbers and coverage found no stronger same-object result. Priority cannot be guaranteed: Folkman's original full article was inaccessible, and historical computational tables may be unindexed.

## Value — PASS

The Folkman graph is the smallest semisymmetric graph: a canonical finite test case where an edge-transitive network has two genuinely different vertex types. An exact classification of its majority attractors and basin-entry times resolves a natural dynamical invariant rather than a chosen random subfamily. The separation between oscillatory attraction ending by time three and consensus-only maximal seven-step delays is a substantive feature of the full state-space dynamics. The quotient by the full 3,840-element automorphism group exposes eight oscillatory types despite 351 labelled temporal cycles. This concrete benchmark can be used to test symmetry reductions and voting-time estimates; no unproved broader consequence is asserted.
