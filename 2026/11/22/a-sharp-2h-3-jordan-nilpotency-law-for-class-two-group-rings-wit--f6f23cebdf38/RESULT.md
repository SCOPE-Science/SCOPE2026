# A sharp \(2h+3\) Jordan nilpotency law for class-two group rings with cyclic commutator of order four

## Finding
Let \(R\) be a unital commutative ring of characteristic exactly \(2^h\), for an integer \(h\ge1\), and let \(G\) be a group with central derived subgroup \(G'\cong C_4\). For the Jordan product \(x\circ y=xy+yx\) on \(RG\), define \(J_2(x_1,x_2)=x_1\circ x_2\) and \(J_{n+1}=J_n\circ x_{n+1}\). Then the least integer \(n\ge2\) such that \(J_n\) vanishes for every input is exactly
\[
\boxed{n=2h+3.}
\]
Thus all such group rings of characteristic four have Jordan nilpotency index seven, regardless of the size of \(G\) or the particular coefficient ring of characteristic four.

## Assumptions and scope
The group may be infinite, but its commutator subgroup is central and cyclic of order four. Write \(G'=\langle s\rangle\). The coefficient ring is commutative, has an identity, and \(2^{h-1}\cdot1_R\ne0\), \(2^h\cdot1_R=0\). The Jordan index concerns **left-nested** Jordan products; no assertion about every parenthesization is made. The case \(h=1\) agrees with the known characteristic-two index-five case; the newly resolved range here is \(h\ge2\).

## Proof
**(1) Central factorization.** Given \(g_1,\ldots,g_n\in G\), write \(w_i=g_1\cdots g_i\). Since every group commutator is central, \(g_{i+1}w_i=w_i g_{i+1}c_i\) for some \(c_i\in G'\). Induction on \(n\) gives
\[
J_n(g_1,\ldots,g_n)=w_n\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1+c_i).
\]
Multilinearity reduces arbitrary group-ring inputs to this case.

**(2) Central nilpotence.** Work first in \(\mathbb Z[C_4]\), put \(u=1+s\), \(v=1+s^2\). The four factors \(1+s^j\) lie in \(K=(2,u)\): this is obvious for \(j=0,1\), while \(1+s^2=u^2-2s\) and \(1+s^3=u(1-s+s^2)\). As \(v^j=2^{j-1}v\) for all \(j\ge1\) and \(u^2=2s+v\), the binomial theorem yields, for every integer \(m\ge1\),
\[
u^{2m}=2^{m-1}\left(2s^m+v(u^m-s^m)\right).
\]
Consequently \(u^b\) has coefficients divisible by \(2^{\lfloor(b-2)/2\rfloor}\) for every \(b\ge2\). For each monomial \(2^a u^b\) with \(a+b=2h+2\), either \(b\le1\) (so \(a\ge h\)) or \(a+\lfloor(b-2)/2\rfloor\ge h\). Therefore
\[
K^{2h+2}=0\qquad\text{in }(\mathbb Z/2^h\mathbb Z)[C_4].
\]
The same holds inside \(R[G']\subset RG\). The factorization above implies \(J_{2h+3}=0\) for all inputs.

**(3) A universal nonzero witness.** Since \(G'\) is generated by commutators and is cyclic of order four, there are \(a,b\in G\) for which \(ba=ab\,s^{\epsilon}\), with \(\epsilon=1\) or \(3\) (replace the chosen generator \(s\), if needed). For every \(n\ge2\), repeated application of the Jordan product gives
\[
J_n(a,b,\ldots,b)=ab^{n-1}(1+s^{\epsilon})^{n-1}.
\]
The element \(1+s^{\epsilon}\) differs from \(u\) by a central unit, so it has the same nilpotency index. In the formula for \(u^{2m}\) above, take \(m=h\) and multiply by \(u\). After dividing the integer coefficient vector by \(2^{h-1}\) and reducing modulo two, its value is
\[
u(1+s^2)(u^h-s^h)=u^{h+3}-s^h u^3=-s^h u^3\ne0\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[C_4],
\]
because \(u^4=0\) but \(u^3=1+s+s^2+s^3\ne0\) in characteristic two. Therefore \(u^{2h+1}\ne0\) modulo \(2^h\), and the chosen left-nested product \(J_{2h+2}\) is nonzero in \(RG\), since group elements are an \(R\)-basis and \(2^{h-1}\ne0\) in \(R\). Combining both directions proves exact index \(2h+3\). \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses exact cyclic convolution in the four-dimensional group ring over \(\mathbb Z/2^h\mathbb Z\). It checks, for every \(1\le h\le8\), that the \((2h+1)\)-st power of \(1+s\) is nonzero, the next power vanishes, and every monomial of total degree \(2h+2\) in \((2,1+s)\) vanishes. It additionally evaluates nested Jordan products directly in the class-two order-\(64\) Heisenberg group over \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) and checks the index-seven transition. Run `python3 verify.py`. These finite checks confirm examples but do not replace the symbolic proof for all \(h\) and groups.

## Relationship to prior work
Cristo, Cornelissen Hoyos and Rodrigues (arXiv:2611.06587v1, November 4, 2026) establish Jordan index classifications through six, prove the characteristic-two cyclic-derived-of-order-four index-five case, and give a sufficient criterion for elementary abelian derived groups (Theorems 3.1, 4.3, 5.1). Their results do not provide this arbitrary-characteristic-power exact law for a cyclic derived group of order four. Goodaire and Polcino Milies (2014) established early low-index results; their 2013 presentation records the index-three classification. The present proof isolates the two-adic central-augmentation mechanism and constructs a sharp witness for every \(h\), rather than invoking low-index Lie classification.

## Limitations
This theorem assumes \(G'\cong C_4\) is **central**, so it does not cover class-three groups with the same abstract derived subgroup. It does not classify all Jordan nilpotent group rings of a given index. Historical priority remains conditional on unavailable or unindexed literature, particularly the full 2014 article. The numerical checker tests only finite examples; the infinite assertion uses the integral divisibility argument above.
