# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. A derivation of a free nilpotent Lie algebra is determined by its two generator images; the degree filtration reduces invertibility to the induced graded maps. Every positive two-letter multidegree contains an integral Lyndon basis element, giving a complete and characteristic-independent weight test even for nondiagonalizable generator matrices. The multidegree \((p,p)\) proves the universal obstruction at class \(2p\); irreducible quadratic matrices with nonzero trace establish existence below it. The finite-field enumeration separates irreducible, split-distinct, and repeated-eigenvalue cases with verified conjugacy-class sizes. Two standalone computational tests agreed with every covered instance.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected evidence. The 2019 derivation-parametrization and 2024 computational study provide structural background but no inspected finite-field all-class enumeration or sharp \(2p\) obstruction. The 2019 almost-inner theorem concerns a different operator subspace, while the 2016 quadratic classification concerns invariant forms. The class-two count is a covered boundary case, and its prior enumeration is expressly credited; the new claim concerns the class-three-and-higher weights and the all-class modular cutoff. Closest indexed derivation results study class-two or other Lie families; none of the checked statements implies the all-class modular threshold and normalized density plateau. An unlocated general Lie-power eigenvalue theorem could subsume the formula, so independent publication-priority examination remains necessary.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a natural unbounded family with an exact existence threshold, a full labeled finite-field count, and a class-independent density interval followed by a sharp modular collapse. The forbidden-ratio formulation explains the phenomenon and relates it to the multigrading of free Lie algebras; it is not merely a small numerical example or a reformulation of the known derivation parametrization.

The main risk is incomplete coverage of the broader Lie-power literature. The result does not cover arbitrary quotients of the free algebra, automorphism orbits, or skew-adjoint derivations. Finite experimental agreement does not replace the proof.
