# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The residue-class order statistic is exact: each \((y,z)\) base gives exactly one representation at a later matching residue, and the \((p+1)\)-st base marks its first value exceeding \(p\). Explicit row lengths and starting values give an all-residue upper bound throughout the affine interval. The extremal residue \(r=a-1\) attains the upper bound; at the first successor, \(r=1\) attains the corrected upper bound. The proof explicitly handles even and odd \(a\), row interleaving and the strictness of \(a-2d\). Finite checks: 977 coprime parameter pairs, 40,466 interior levels, 977 first-break levels, and 20 independent order-statistic spotchecks.

**Originality — PASS on the material inspected, with residual risk.** Komatsu–Ying's 2022 source [1, Theorem 1] ends at \(p=\lfloor a/2floor\), whereas the claimed interval begins at \(p=\lfloor a/2floor+2\). Subwattanachai's theorem [2, Theorem 1] needs a generator-divisibility hypothesis that fails for all admissible triples. The public exact three-phase result for consecutive denominations [3] concerns \(d=1\) only. Woods's broad eventual results [4] do not state a uniform two-parameter formula in the intermediate, linearly growing \(p\) range or the exact first phase change. An implicit equivalent in wider denumerant identities, unindexed published tables, or noninspected sources cannot be eliminated by searches alone.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The interval extends the recognized \(p\)-Frobenius arithmetic-progression problem by a number of levels growing with the base size and step, and identifies its precise endpoint and first nontrivial deviation. The result is a complete sharp structural phase for an infinite, two-parameter numerical-semigroup family rather than a numerical specimen or reformulation of the known small-level law.
