For the Brière-I insect-development curve, equal time spent above and below a fixed mean can increase the averaged development rate only when the lower-to-upper temperature-threshold ratio is below \(2/3\) and the normalized half-amplitude is below \((3-9a+\sqrt{9+2a-3a^2})/14\). Below that sharp amplitude, precisely one feasible mean-temperature crossover separates benefit from harm; above it, every feasible mean incurs a strict developmental-rate penalty.
