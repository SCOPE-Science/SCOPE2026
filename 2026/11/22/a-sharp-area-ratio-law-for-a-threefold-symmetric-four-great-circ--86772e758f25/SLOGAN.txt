Four explicitly threefold-symmetric great circles always cut the unit sphere into two triangles of one area, six triangles of another, and six quadrilaterals of a third; their exact largest-to-smallest area ratio is uniquely minimized by tetrahedral normals, with sharp value \((4\arccos(-1/3)-2\pi)/(2\pi-3\arccos(-1/3))\). A second, previously distinct phase transition occurs at the unique parameter \(t_*=0.5192000606114\ldots\), when the largest-area cell changes from a quadrilateral to a triangle; no unrestricted four-circle optimum is claimed.
