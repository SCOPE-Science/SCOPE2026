# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The source's two-group SEIR final-size equations reduce exactly to the \(F\) fixed point, while the continuous-time exponential-removal branching extinction probabilities reduce to the \(G\) fixed point. Their matrices are transposes. For arbitrary positive \(2\times2\) matrices, an order lemma is proved using strict subhomogeneity, continuation from the Perron eigenvector and a noncrossing argument. Its hypothesis holds for all allowed assortativities under the stated supercritical scale \(c\ge1\). The two row-sum expressions and reciprocal roots give the sharp threshold. Code verifies symbolic identities and sampled fixed-point signs. No numerical calculation substitutes for the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS, literature-bounded

The initiating age-structured SEIR article supplies the two equations and numerically compares age-group risk rankings, but not the exact one-switch law for the symmetric balanced two-group family in the inspected main text or appendices. Earlier two-group deterministic final-size results, homogeneous trait-dependent epidemic outcomes, and multitype proportionate household epidemic results do not, by their inspected statements and different hypotheses, imply the joint cross-metric switching law. Searches for direct formulations, row-sum aliases, assortativity ranking thresholds and published mathematical records revealed no identified dominating statement. Prior work establishes the standard positive fixed-point and branching-process foundations, which are not claimed as new. An older or differently parameterized specialization might still exist and should be checked independently.

## Value — PASS

The initiating epidemiology question is whether the group suffering the highest final attack fraction is also the most likely epidemic seed. The sharp two-group classification gives a complete answer over every nonsegregated assortative contact probability when susceptibility and infectiousness advantages are opposed. The exact boundary is unchanged by transmission strength and the exposed-stage waiting times, reducing a nonlinear pair of risk computations to a single structural contact test. Such a direct criterion is useful for interpreting why the two types of epidemic risk can disagree, even though it is not a validated empirical policy rule.

## Limits

Assumptions include equal population sizes, equal contact budgets, common exponential removal, two types, factorized infectivity and susceptibility and no exposed removal. Results are for the macroscopic positive-outbreak final-size branch and the infinite-population branching approximation; they are not finite-population exact stochastic claims. No exhaustive priority claim is made.
