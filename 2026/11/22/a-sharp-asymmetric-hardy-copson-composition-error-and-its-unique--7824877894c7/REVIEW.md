# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The step response is obtained by direct integration. Its exact dilogarithmic error has a strictly unique maximizing jump, proved through a positive derivative on the initial fifth of the interval and a strictly decreasing derivative thereafter. Distributional BV representation reduces the all-input sharp inequality to these jumps; the unique optimizer characterizes equality. Reflecting the interval transfers the theorem to the reversed composition. The packaged standard-library checker passed 59 formula/averaging comparisons and 65 signed BV tests. Finite tests are corroboration rather than infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The archival full-text iterated Hardy/Copson paper of Gogatishvili and Mustafayev (arXiv:1503.04079v1, 2015) treats weighted positive-operator inequalities on monotone cones. The later full-text superposition study (arXiv:2109.03095) likewise focuses on positive weighted Hardy–Copson estimates. The Nekvinda–Pick preprint studies optimal Hardy domains and duals. Compared results on output-variation norms of mixed products, identity-error norms of single-operator powers, and convex mixtures concern different targets or operators, and do not imply the exact coefficient here. Equivalence/alias, broader implication, exact-value and direct claim-comparison searches were performed. An unindexed historical equivalent remains possible; novelty is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS

The forward/backward composition is a canonical, noncommuting two-step average. Its exact sharp \(BV\)-to-\(L^1\) identity error and all extremizers give a natural stability test for discontinuous data. The strict comparison \(0.41050749\ldots>1/e\) shows that adding an oppositely directed averaging step can *increase* the worst normalized approximation error, despite positive constant-preserving kernels.

## Boundaries

Only real, unweighted, distributional BV inputs on the unit interval and one application of each operator are covered. The checker does not replace the proof; an unindexed exact older result cannot be excluded by search.
