# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a fixed five-Fourier-mode, two-disjoint-arc sharp small-total-length asymptotic with a necessary geometric characterization. The proof normalizes Haar measure and five coefficient coordinates explicitly. Its key upper bound is a double-zero Hermite trial polynomial corrected to the monic quadratic Legendre residual on each arc; the coefficient norm is the explicit positive quadratic polynomial \(34+32c+4c^2\). The derivative identity proves unique antipodal separation for the leading bound, and fifth-power convexity forces a vanishing fraction of the total length onto the short arc. The matching construction uses an antipodal narrow half-width \(a^{3/2}\). The lower bound projects an actual least-eigenvalue vector onto the quadratic Legendre residual on the wide arc, after showing its four value/derivative jets converge to zero. Distinctness of the two interpolation centers, and the separate colliding-center trial bound, cover all moving center sequences. High-precision Gram quadrature and symbolic checks corroborate normalizations; no finite grid substitutes for the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS within the comparisons performed
The closest published equal-width two-arc theorem supplies the sharp Legendre coefficient for fixed equal widths and proves asymptotic antipodal placement; it does not optimize the allocation of the two half-widths or obtain a vanishing-width companion. The published arbitrary-two-window fixed-index Fourier theorem expressly fixes both windows in its bandwidth asymptotic, hence does not imply uniform singular-window optimization. The open-access Marceca–Romero–Speckbacher article proves eigenvalue counting estimates on irregular Fourier domains, not the sharp five-dimensional smallest-eigenvalue allocation. Asipchuk–Drezels treats Riesz bases on unions of intervals; only its abstract and public metadata were accessible and specialized hidden content remains a priority risk. Earlier three-/four-mode equal-arc designs do not imply the five-mode width-allocation result. No claim of exhaustive historical priority is made.

## Value — PASS
Two observation arcs with a fixed total length are a natural measurement geometry for finite-band signals. The result identifies an unexpected singular allocation law and a sharp sixteen-fold gain relative to balanced antipodal allocation, while proving that nearly optimal center separation and length distribution are necessary. This is a nonroutine conditioning/design phenomenon: an extremely narrow second observation component removes a near-null direction while almost all observation length is concentrated into the other component. The exact sharp coefficient and geometric rigidity are useful for assessing and placing finite-band observation windows.

## Limits and evidence
Only asymptotic optimum and necessary limiting geometry are proved; the finite-width optimum and unique width-decay exponent are not. The numerical checker is independent of the Hermite proof but is not an infinite certificate. Specialist historical priority beyond inspected and indexed sources remains unresolved.
