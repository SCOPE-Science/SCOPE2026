# Scientific assessment: sharp Bernstein–Bézier integrated BV error

## Correctness — PASS
The operator is defined with nonnegative tail-power sampling weights and canonical BV traces. Every real BV input has a signed jump-measure representation, so triangle inequality gives the sharp norm by single jumps, including a one-sided supremum at sampling nodes. Every jump-error profile is strictly convex *within its sampling cell*, giving the finite exact maximum over two endpoints per cell. For fixed parameter and interior jump, binomial CLT gives a Gaussian transition; explicit binomial Bernstein tails dominate both tails uniformly and control boundary jumps. This proves the all-degree limit, localization, and the claimed leading constant. The ratio of two positive Laplace-type integrals has strictly positive logarithmic derivative because its two logarithmic support intervals are separated by \(\log2\), proving a unique parameter optimum. A direct Gaussian identity gives equal leading errors at parameters one and two; the strict parameter ordering follows. The degree-one error is computed exactly. Independent finite checks are recorded in VERIFICATION.md and check.py.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest directly inspected primary paper is Lian (2013), DOI 10.7153/jmi-07-59, particularly its operator definition and pointwise BV estimates (Theorems 1–2) and proof. These concern local one-point convergence, not the global sharp integrated variation norm, its finite-cell formula, or the optimization of its Gaussian leading constant. Ren–Zeng (2015), DOI 10.1186/s13660-015-0940-9, recalls the classical family but studies a different beta-modified operator via moduli of smoothness. Prior nearby results on the classical Bernstein sharp integrated BV error and a distinct Stancu drift–noise transition involve different weights and do not imply a tail-power parameter optimization.

Historical-priority risk remains material: Zeng–Piriou (1998), DOI 10.1006/jath.1997.3227, and Zeng (2000), DOI 10.1006/jath.2000.3451, concern the same Bernstein–Bézier family and asymptotically optimal *pointwise* BV estimates. Their accessible abstracts and citation records were compared, but full text could not be obtained. Those records do not state the present integrated operator-norm and unique parameter-optimum theorem, but this limited inspection cannot exclude an equivalent result in their bodies. These are a declared residual risk, not a claim that the full texts were cleared.

## Value — PASS
The power parameter is an intrinsic control in this established positive-approximation family. The theorem answers which parameter minimizes the sharp worst integrated error for discontinuous BV signals, not just whether individual signals converge. It produces a complete strict improvement window, an exactly tied but asymptotically different classical/twofold pair, a unique motivated optimum with about five-percent improvement, and a provable low-degree reversal. This supplies a quantitative tuning rule and shows why optimizing a fixed low degree can mislead asymptotic choices. The finite formula makes any fixed-degree benchmark reproducible. It does not determine the first improved finite degree or address variable parameters or weighted norms.

## Scope and priority limitations
Real-valued, canonically represented \(BV(0,1)\); point sampling at equispaced grid nodes; Lebesgue \(L^1\) approximation error; fixed \(\alpha>0\). Numerical root values are approximations and finite computations are not used as infinite proofs. Priority remains subject to independent historical inspection of the inaccessible 1998 and 2000 originals.
