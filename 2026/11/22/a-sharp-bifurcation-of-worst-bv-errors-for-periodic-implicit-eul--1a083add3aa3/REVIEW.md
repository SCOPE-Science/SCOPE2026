# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The signed derivative of a real periodic BV function has total mass zero, so its Jordan pieces have equal mass and can be coupled. The residual operator is convolution with \(-tG_t'\) applied to that derivative; the coupling triangle inequality is sharp for two atoms. Equality rigidity follows because all nonzero Fourier multipliers of \(-tG_t'\) are nonvanishing. The explicit periodic Green function solves the distributional equation and gives the derivative of the squared sharp arc-error functional. The strictly increasing function \(u\coth u\) proves a unique global optimizer and unique transition for every positive parameter. The small-time and critical expansions are algebraic consequences of the same root equation. The supplementary script compares 36 finite Fourier sums with rigorous tail bounds; it is not substituted for the continuum proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated historical risk
The closest inspected archived primary article, Miranda–Pallara–Paronetto–Preunkert, proves heat-semigroup \(L^1\)-gradient characterization of BV and uses resolvent regularity, not an exact sharp periodic \(L^2\) resolvent residual norm. Kovács–Larsson–Lindgren analyzes backward Euler for stochastic Allen–Cahn in Sobolev-based convergence regimes, and similarly does not imply the exact two-jump geometry or critical equation. Accessible previous sharp results for heat, fractional heat, Steklov and boxcar smoothing concern different operator multipliers; comparison of complete statements gives no covering implication. Semantic searches for the resolvent, Bessel potential, periodic Green, two-jump pulse and critical equation produced no matching assertion. An unindexed equivalent derivation remains a genuine residual risk.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete exact sharp-constant and equality-case theorem for a standard first-order implicit diffusion step on discontinuous data. The time threshold at which the worst discontinuity switches from an interior short pulse to a semicircle is a structural prediction not supplied by ordinary BV contractivity or numerical time-stepping error estimates. The exact short-time constant and square-root critical law provide benchmarks for studying how one implicit step treats pairs of nearby jumps.

## Scientific limits
The formula does not cover multiple steps, varying diffusivity, boundary-value intervals, dimensions above one, or nonlinear resolvents. Finite checker evaluations are corroboration rather than an infinite-dimensional proof. Historical priority remains subject to inaccessible/unindexed sources.
