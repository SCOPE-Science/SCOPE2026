# Scientific review: competing bulk and surface reaction allocation

## Correctness — PASS
The discounted surface-capture transform is derived from the explicitly normalized Robin boundary-value problem. Its exact hyperbolic formula is independently recovered by a second-order finite-difference boundary solve. Differentiation gives a quadratic numerator \(P(a)\), strictly decreasing on the entire reactivity budget domain for all off-midpoint launch sites, with \(P(0)>0\) and \(P(K/2)>0\). The endpoint sign is exactly \(\delta\sinh(k)-B_1K-B_2K^2\), having one positive zero. These global signs prove the unique boundary/interior optimizer and include equality cases. The midpoint case follows from symmetry and the strictly unique derivative zero; reflected starts follow by exchanging walls. The numerical checker is additional evidence, not the proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
Actual semantic searches of the public mathematical research index and primary literature identified the no-bulk, same-geometry mean-time allocation theorem and a related drift-with-resetting point-trap allocation switch. Both were inspected in complete available research statements and implication-compared. The former implies a strictly interior mean-time optimum, not the positive-budget corner phase for the *discounted* surface-vs-bulk objective; the latter uses an unbounded resetting process and different capture-time objective, with no direct parameter specialization to confined boundary killing. Ye's 2026 bulk/surface paper motivates the joint stopping mechanism but its complete primary text was inaccessible. Thus a strong specialized-precedence risk remains, and independent verification is needed.

## Value — PASS
Allocation of limited boundary reactivity when a molecule may disappear in the bulk is a naturally motivated, exact design question. The nontrivial phenomenon is a boundary-to-interior phase change absent from the corresponding no-bulk mean-time optimization, with one explicit threshold for every start off the midpoint, full uniqueness, and the especially simple physical scale \(\sqrt{Dp}\,\sinh(L\sqrt{p/D})\) at a boundary start. The result is self-contained, testable, and directly distinguishes how the competition of death clocks changes optimal targeting; it does not assert general design optimality in unrelated models.

## Checked literature and limitations
The closest named works are Ye (arXiv:2609.05247, public abstract), Nicolaou–Mulder (2023, accessible problem-formulation extract), *Optimal allocation of two reactive Brownian boundaries* (complete research statement), and *A sharp reactivity-budget transition for two drift-biased resetting traps* (complete research statement). Main limitations: the 2026 source full text was not available, the geometry is one-dimensional with constant diffusion, the bulk clock is exponential, and the reactivity budget is a simple sum, not a cost functional. Numerical comparisons do not certify the continuum theorem independently.
