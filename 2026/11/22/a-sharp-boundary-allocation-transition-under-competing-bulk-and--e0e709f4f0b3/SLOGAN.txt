For a reflected Brownian particle competing with an exponential bulk-death clock, optimally dividing a fixed surface reactivity budget between two interval endpoints undergoes a unique sharp transition: below an explicit positive threshold, all reactivity belongs on the nearer end; above it, both ends are active. From the launch boundary the dimensional critical budget is \(\sqrt{Dp}\,\sinh(L\sqrt{p/D})\), in contrast to the always-interior optimal allocation for ordinary mean surface capture time without bulk mortality.
