The equilibrium equation follows from the balanced row sums and the stage-vector identities. ## Originality — PASS, bounded by inspected literature
Garmire and O'Dwyer, arXiv:2610.23557v1 (2026), specify the generalized two-stage Lotka–Volterra dynamics and a demographic-speed-dependent stochastic stability analysis, but not the deterministic arbitrary-network finite-demography all-eigenvalue criterion or the identical-absolute-interaction reciprocal-versus-circulating counterexample. Giménez-Romero et al. (2025), Ecology Letters e70272, provide a structured community matrix and interaction-type stability comparisons, not the stated exact theorem. The public note *Supercritical oscillations from zero-mean stage interactions in a two-species community* proves a different exchange-symmetric nonlinear Hopf theorem and does not cover this spectral condition for arbitrary balanced networks. The public note *Uniform global entropy decay under arbitrarily switching stage-antagonistic ecological interactions* proves stronger global stability for a **different dissipative interaction structure**; neither its entropy cancellation nor the fact that reciprocal cases can be stable is presented as novel here. The public note on *four-patch directed-cycle delayed logistic instability* uses discrete patches with time-delay rather than the specified instantaneous two-stage dynamics. Classical Schur complements and normal matrix spectral facts are acknowledged as prior tools. Exact equivalence in unlocated linear matrix theory remains a historical-priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The initiating literature asks how finite demographic turnover and stage-varying interactions shift community stability. The finding replaces an averaged or ensemble-only picture by a sharp finite-rate, arbitrary-network criterion. It compares two strongly interacting ecological communities with the **same row-balanced stage aggregate, same habitat-edge set, same magnitudes, and same turnover rate**, yet opposite local stability at the same parameters. It identifies both a sharp exchange-controlled threshold and the first dimension permitting an oscillatory spectral loss. This is a structural ecology distinction, not merely restating a well-known general stability formula or inventing an unmotivated parameter slice.

## Scope and residual risks
The matrices have strict signed constraints and equal demographic transfer, so the theorem makes no empirical claim for random ecology, global persistence, or nonlinear Hopf existence. Linear criticality at equality does not determine nonlinear stability. The exact quadratic-pencil factorization is elementary enough that an older equivalent linear-systems theorem or specialized ecological example may exist; published and searched comparisons did not locate one.
