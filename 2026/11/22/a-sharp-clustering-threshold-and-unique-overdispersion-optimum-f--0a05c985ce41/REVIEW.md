# Review: A sharp clustering threshold and unique overdispersion optimum for scramble recruitment

**Correctness — PASS.** At fixed mean, the negative-binomial probability generating function differentiates exactly to the recruitment expression. The sign of its log derivative is the sign of \(H(z/k)-z\). The proof independently establishes that \(H\) strictly increases from \(2\) to infinity using a positive derivative, proving exhaustive global uniqueness and boundary behavior. It separately proves both optimizer asymptotics and a one-crossing law for every pair of fixed shapes, including the Poisson endpoint. Re-executing the bundled Python checker yielded 263 deterministic assertions. The finite checks corroborate, but are not substituted for, analytic quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority.** Brännström and Sumpter (2005), equations (2.3), (3.3), and (3.4), already give negative-binomial scramble recruitment and the Hassell functional form; those facts are explicitly credited and excluded from the novelty claim. Their accessible text does not present the sharp optimization over real dispersion at a fixed mean or its pairwise crossings and optimizer asymptotics. Anazawa (2009) reprises the model derivation; Anazawa (2010) likewise studies mechanistic aggregation, but its full text remained inaccessible after public-source search, leaving a documented priority risk. The full Beckman–Neuhauser–Muller-Landau (2012) methods and results study a different density-dependent enemy process where clumping can help through predator satiation. Accessible published Hassell seasonal-sorting and negative-multinomial cell-mode theorems were read at the statement/proof level and concern different optimizations, parameters, and outputs. Broader generic probability-transform and ecological allocation literature could yield an equivalent theorem; a search is not a uniqueness certificate.

**Value — PASS.** The fixed-mean formulation isolates aggregation from the trivial effect of adding seeds. A complete nonmonotone response classification, rather than only an illustrative parameter fit, gives the exact threshold at which moderate overdispersion becomes superior to Poisson arrivals, the finite optimal shape, universal pairwise ranking reversal, and practically interpretable critical and strong-crowding scales. This is a naturally motivated ecological question because aggregation and density-dependent mortality both materially change realized recruitment. The theorem does not presume that dispersal behavior is freely optimizable or that any field population follows this exact one-step rule.
