# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies every graph-edge compatibility relation and partitions faces by the at most two endpoint-incident edges. Each fiber over those edges is a simplex on the remaining middle edges, with a fixed first-available-element toggle. The outer-edge projection is monotone along the directed gradient graph, making the fiberwise acyclic matching globally acyclic. The three possible critical-cell patterns yield the complete integer homotopy classification. The one-dimensional Morse CW type needs no torsion assumption. Independently, a standalone verifier enumerates all matchings from graph endpoints in 40 parameter instances, checks that every proposed face pairing is legal, topologically sorts each directed Hasse graph, and compares mod-two ranks.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
The 2022 polygonal-line-tiling study classifies cycle *chains* joined along edges, whereas here cycles arise from multiple internally disjoint paths joining two shared poles. The 2024 outerplanar theorem is qualitative and inapplicable when there are at least three arms, since the graphs contain subdivisions of the nonouterplanar graph \(K_{2,3}\). A public quadrilateral-book matching-complex result already implies the homotopy types of the special subfamily \(a=0\): its graph adds an edge between the common terminals, which cones off a contractible simplex of middle edges. That known subfamily is explicitly credited, as are the cycle and chessboard boundary cases. The quadrilateral-book result does not cover the arbitrary positive short-arm counts or yield the displayed \(2a-1\) first-homology rank law. Four-edge theta matching complexes and perfect-matching or directed-multipath complexes concern different objects or graph families. The new claim is the combined two-parameter classification with explicit fiberwise Morse collapses. Residual risk: an unindexed or more general collapse theorem could cover the result.

## Value — PASS
The exact two-parameter classification gives a sharp, natural transition: an arbitrarily large number of short arms is topologically irrelevant once three long arms are present, and the complex simplicially collapses. Before the transition, the precise circle ranks behave in three qualitatively different ways. This adds explicit integral homotopy information for an infinite family of planar graphs outside the outerplanar graph class, rather than merely proving that some wedge decomposition exists.

## Limits
Only arm lengths two and three are treated. The finite test does not itself prove the unrestricted theorem; the matching argument does. No independent audit or complete bibliographic exclusion is claimed.
