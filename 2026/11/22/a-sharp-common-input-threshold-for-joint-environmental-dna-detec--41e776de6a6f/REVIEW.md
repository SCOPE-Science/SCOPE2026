# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every finite \(A,b,C>0\), strict log-concavity of the joint success probability is established by explicit derivatives and strict composition inequalities. Existence follows from both endpoint limits, and the positivity of its logarithmic derivative before the inhibited-only optimum forces the global maximizer beyond \(1/b\). Evaluating the derivative at \(2/b\) and invoking the strictly decreasing function \(t/(e^t-1)\) gives all threshold and tie cases. The large-\(A/C\) asymptotic follows from an exact stationary equation and logarithmic bounds. Reproducibility code independently checks stationary brackets, comparisons and selected limiting regimes; computation does not establish the theorem.

Actual comparisons included indexed mathematical findings, recoverable earlier model claims and the primary full texts of Acharya-Patel et al. (2024), Uchii et al. (2019) and Brys et al. (2023). These sources address detection protocols, chemical inhibition resistance, and multiple markers, but none of their inspected experimental or theoretical claims entails the precise common-dose Poisson model's global uniqueness plus its \(C=Ae^{-2}\) threshold. A broader indexed result on Hill-law inhibitory allocation has a distinct optimization variable and probability objective, and does not dominate the present statement. The novelty assessment is evidence-limited: older optimal-dilution and generalized log-concavity literature may contain an equivalent theorem.

## Value — PASS

The sharply testable dimensionless threshold gives a concrete mathematical warning: optimizing inhibitor-sensitive detection alone necessarily sacrifices joint detection, and a ratio of two target-abundance coefficients decides whether the needed common dose more than doubles. This distinguishes the biological objective (confirming two targets) from routine single-target amplification and motivates multi-marker assay planning. The exact exponential inhibition and independence assumptions are uncalibrated, so no laboratory prescription is inferred.
