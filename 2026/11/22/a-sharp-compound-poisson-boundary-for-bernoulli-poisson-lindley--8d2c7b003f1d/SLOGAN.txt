The Bernoulli–Poisson–Lindley count law is compound Poisson exactly when \(\alpha\le1/(\theta+2)\); the jump intensities are explicit and strictly positive on the permitted side. Just beyond this boundary, the first negative logarithmic PGF coefficient occurs at an even index \(K\) satisfying \(K\log(\alpha(1+\theta)/(1-\alpha))\to\log 2\), so finite-order checks can conceal non-divisibility.
