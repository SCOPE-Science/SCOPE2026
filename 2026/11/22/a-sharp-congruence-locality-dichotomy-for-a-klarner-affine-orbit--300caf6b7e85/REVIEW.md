# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The classification is proved for all positive moduli, not extrapolated from experiments. The key nontrivial points are: (i) modulo any \(m\) coprime to \(6\), the invertible maps \(A\) and \(B\) generate translation by \(2\) via \((A\circ B)(B\circ A)^{-1}\), because inverses of finite permutations are positive powers; (ii) when \(3\nmid m\), the images under \(A\) and \(C\) lift surjectivity from \(m\) to \(2m\); (iii) when \(2\nmid m\), the images under \(C\), \(A\circ B\), and \(B\) lift to \(3m\); and (iv) no generator outputs \(1\pmod6\). All modulus factorizations and the \(m=1\) boundary are covered. The packaged checker exhaustively verifies residues for \(1\le m\le2000\) but the stated all-modulus conclusion is justified by the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Shamazov–Talambutsa, arXiv:2507.06875v1, has been inspected in relevant full-text sections. It explicitly names the exact Klarner tuple \((0,2,3)\), but its results concern the growth of integer orbits, freeness and exact covering systems rather than an all-modulus congruence projection classification. The exact covering theorem cannot be applied to this distinct-slope family. Searches for equivalent modular reachability, aliases and prime-power surjectivity in indexed research claims found no close implication; these searches do not certify novelty. The most plausible gap is an elementary observation recorded in older semigroup or arithmetic-recursion literature, including the historical work of Klarner and Rado, whose full text was not assessed here.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result answers a complete natural congruence question for a specific, previously highlighted unresolved arithmetic orbit. Its sharp threshold distinguishes the lack of any prime-power obstruction from a genuine mixed-prime obstruction modulo six. The two lifting lemmas are a structural method that could be useful for other affine-generated orbit sets. This does not address the much harder ordinary density problem, and its proof is elementary; the value lies in the classification and the local-versus-global boundary rather than technical complexity.
