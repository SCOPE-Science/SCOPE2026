# Scientific review

**Claim.** For the single classical unit-circle involute, the curved boundary of the finite-arc convex hull changes at the first positive nonzero solution \(\alpha\) of \(\tan\alpha=\alpha\). Beyond that point exactly one terminal arc survives, with a unique tangent closing chord. Its exact area and perimeter have respectively cubic and quartic corrections to their precritical analytic continuations.

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces global support of every smooth parameter point to the sign of an explicit trigonometric function. Its derivative exhausts all possible support extrema, and the strictly increasing contact function gives a unique postcritical cut point for every height of the parameter interval. The prior turns cannot regain boundary exposure because their tangent normal has an exact later-turn competitor. A single arc and a supporting chord account for the full hull. Direct Green's theorem and arc length yield exact formulas, and differentiation with respect to the starting cut yields both singularity constants with controlled remainders. The provided standalone checker passed 44 supplementary tests; finite polygon samples do not establish a continuum theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks

The classical involute parametric formula and the general support methods are prior art. Longinetti–Manselli–Venturi's 2016 open-access paper analyzes involutes and self-distancing path bounding regions and was inspected in publisher-hosted full text. It does not provide the same contact equation and exact area/perimeter critical classification within the inspected material. The accessible older geometric research claims for logarithmic and Archimedean spiral convex hulls use different parametrizations and do not imply the involute's cusp-mediated onset. Searches for contact roots, convex-hull formulas, and alternate involute terminology did not find stronger or equal coverage. Nonindexed historical engineering sources and the uninspected full text of the less relevant polygon-involute literature remain risks. Neither search absence nor separation of object classes alone establishes priority; the stated conclusion is based on explicit implication comparisons.

## Value — PASS

The first loss of exposure of the involute cusp is a geometrically natural completeness threshold, and the exact finite-memory cut point specifies the whole convex hull for arbitrary unwinding angle. The different cubic and quartic singularity orders are sharp and make the threshold measurable through two classical intrinsic volumes. The formula extends the geometric description beyond textbook involute parametrizations without requiring a broader conjectural consequence.

## Sources and limitations

The principal background source is M. Longinetti, P. Manselli and A. Venturi, *Bounding Regions to Plane Steepest Descent Curves of Quasiconvex Families*, Journal of Applied Mathematics (2016), doi:10.1155/2016/4873276, first publicly published 27 July 2016. The MacTutor *Involute of a Circle* entry gives the classical parametrization. The result applies to a single Euclidean circular-involute branch; it is not asserted for other spirals or general involutes. Analytic comparison functions beyond the threshold are signed/formal, not additional convex-hull regions. Exact historical priority is unconfirmed.
