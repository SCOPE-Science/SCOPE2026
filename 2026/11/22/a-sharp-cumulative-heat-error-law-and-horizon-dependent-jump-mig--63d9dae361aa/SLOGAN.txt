The sharp cumulative Neumann heat-approximation error of real bounded-variation data is attained by one jump: for short observation horizons the two worst jump locations approach \(0.772397284434\ldots\sqrt T\) from the ends, giving the new squared constant \(0.257188139594\ldots\,T^{3/2}\), whereas for all \(T\ge1/2\) the unique worst jump is central. The cumulative optimum is genuinely different from optimizing the heat error independently at each observation time.
