# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The normalized degree-five moment polynomial produces a bounded strictly expansive six-isometric shift, while its Cauchy dual has moment sequence given exactly by the reciprocal polynomial. An inverse Laplace transform gives an integrable signed Berger density. Its positivity is equivalent to positivity of \(F_\rho\), which has alternating stationary points at the roots of \(\tan z=z\). The explicit stationary-value formula reduces necessity to the first nontrivial minimum; a proved spatial-monotonicity bound makes that minimum sufficient for every later minimum. The resulting threshold is unique. The standalone standard-library checker passed 344 finite tests; no infinite conclusion is based on finite sampling alone.

## Originality

**PASS, with priority qualification.** Buchała (arXiv:2407.17233) supplies the general six-isometry moment-polynomial framework; Reza–Zhang (arXiv:1903.00116) supplies the rational Hausdorff-moment and inverse-transform context. The latter's degree-five same-vertical-line obstruction and general pole-dominance restriction do not determine this positive-damping repeated-complex-pole region. A separate toral three-isometry study (arXiv:2310.04785) does not cover a scalar degree-five polynomial Cauchy dual. Searches for dual-shift subnormality and repeated-conjugate-pole complete monotonicity did not reveal this exact transition. Absence from searched sources is not a global originality proof; a historically unindexed equivalent remains possible.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A natural sharp slice of the degree-five rational Hausdorff-moment problem, motivated by strict six-isometric weighted shifts, receives a complete three-parameter criterion and an exact critical Berger-density geometry. The threshold distinguishes subnormal from non-subnormal Cauchy duals where pole dominance alone is insufficient, with an explicit universal constant not predetermined by the standard moment-polynomial construction.

## Boundaries and verification

The theorem addresses only the stated repeated-pole factorization, not arbitrary quintics, higher multiplicities or operator-weighted shifts. A unique interior zero of the density at criticality is proved, and the stated decimal constants are illustrative approximations. Numerical checks are not a substitute for the all-parameter proof. Historical priority and correctness still require independent scrutiny.
