# Scientific review: sharp three-season migration-benefit threshold

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed fitness ratio is \(r>1\), the direct exchange fraction is \(d\in[0,1]\), and the model has exactly three deterministic fitness phases with two identical phases. Exact matrix multiplication yields the stated trace and determinant. The characteristic polynomial evaluated at the sedentary Perron eigenvalue \(r^2\) factors as a positive multiplier times a single quadratic in \(d\). The second eigenvalue lies strictly below \(r^2\) for the whole closed parameter interval, so that factorization gives an *if and only if* rather than a one-sided test. The quadratic discriminant and vertex prove the full threshold, rates, tie cases and discontinuity. The half-exchange result follows from strict decrease of the quadratic on that subinterval. Cyclic similarity of matrix-product characteristic polynomials and patch-label conjugacy cover all nonconstant binary three-season calendars. A standalone exact-symbolic and finite-grid checker passed 721 diagnostic assertions, which are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The nearest openly indexed mathematical results establish either a distinct example of a three-season interior advantage using a neutral season, or a three-season *equal-pool* endpoint theorem with the exchange rate restricted to at most one half. The present theorem gives the full one-parameter binary-calendar classification, exact two distinct thresholds \(2+\sqrt3\) and \(2+\sqrt5\), exact winning-rate interval, and the discontinuity at onset. Neither previously inspected statement implies these quantitative conclusions. Schreiber and Li (2010) address evolutionary selection and periodic dispersal; Katriel (2021/2022) studies a continuous-time periodically driven system. These are important broader contexts, not recorded equivalents to the current exact discrete-time claim. Remaining risk: earlier work in positive-matrix products or dispersal theory may contain an equivalent parameterized result not identified by indexed searches. McPeek and Holt (1992) was available only as a publisher abstract, so it cannot certify full-text noncoverage.

## Value — PASS
A three-season calendar with one favorable patch for one phase and the other for two phases is the shortest imbalanced binary seasonal environmental pattern. All its nonconstant calendar orderings are classified, rather than selecting an arbitrary numerical witness. The result identifies a specific change in what kinds of movement can benefit population growth: at intermediate fitness contrast, intentional direct exchange can improve long-run growth while equal-pool dispersal cannot. The jump of optimal fractions away from zero is a structural warning against local perturbation analysis of dispersal. The claim has mathematical value as a complete benchmark under explicit restrictions; it does not claim ecological field calibration, survival probabilities, or an evolutionary optimum.

## Closest literature and limits
- Schreiber and Li (2010), arXiv:1007.5267v1: periodic unconditional dispersal and evolutionary selection, not the claimed three-season parameter classification.
- *Two-season endpoint optimality and the first interior dispersal optimum* (2026): a three-season witness involving one neutral phase, not this complementary binary calendar's sharp threshold.
- *Four seasons are necessary and sufficient for interior-optimal dispersal in a two-patch periodic model* (2026): full redistribution has exchange probability at most one half; compatible with, but not equivalent to, the present direct-exchange classification.
- Katriel (2021/2022), arXiv:2104.01589: a continuous-time periodic migration model rather than discrete post-reproduction exchange.
