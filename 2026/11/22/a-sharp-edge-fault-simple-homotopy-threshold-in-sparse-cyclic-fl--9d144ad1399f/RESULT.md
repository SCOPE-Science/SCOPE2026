# A sharp edge-fault simple-homotopy threshold in sparse cyclic flag complexes

## Finding
Let \(p\ge2\), \(n\ge4p+2\), and let \(C_n^p\) be the graph on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) joining any two distinct vertices of cyclic distance at most \(p\). For an edge \(e\) of cyclic length \(d\), \(1\le d\le p\), put \(X=\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^p)\) and \(Y=\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^p-e)\). Then
\[
|Y|\simeq
\begin{cases}
S^1\vee S^1,&p=2\text{ and }d=1,\\
S^1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In every case except \((p,d)=(2,1)\), **the actual inclusion** \(Y\hookrightarrow X\) is a simple-homotopy equivalence: there is a sequence of elementary simplicial collapses \(X\searrow Y\). The intact complex always has a separate collapse \(X\searrow C_n\). Consequently \(p=3\) is the first power for which every edge fault is harmless to the flag-complex simple-homotopy type throughout this stated sparse range.

## Assumptions and scope
All complexes are finite abstract flag (clique) complexes: a simplex is a vertex subset spanning a complete graph. Deleting a graph edge retains both endpoints and deletes exactly those simplices containing that edge. A simplicial elementary collapse removes a face together with its unique codimension-one maximal coface; \(X\searrow Y\) denotes a finite sequence of such operations. The graph is the **cycle power as a one-skeleton**, not the scale-two Rips complex for the newly recomputed metric on the graph after deletion. Thus no graph distances are recomputed following a fault. The uniform size bound \(n\ge4p+2\) keeps every relevant common-neighbor graph in a single unwrapped interval; no sharpness claim in \(n\) is made.

## Proof
**1. The intact clique complex collapses to the original cycle.** Fix an integer \(q\) with \(2\le q\le p\) and consider \(\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^q)\). For each \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), the distance-\(q\) edge \(\{i,i+q\}\) belongs to the **unique** maximal simplex \(A_i=\{i,i+1,\dots,i+q\}\). Indeed, because \(n\ge4p+2>3q\), every common neighbor of its endpoints lies strictly on the short arc between them, and that arc is a clique. Every face containing that edge is a face of \(A_i\). Choose one vertex \(w_i\in A_i\setminus\{i,i+q\}\), and for each \(T\subseteq A_i\setminus(\{i,i+q\}\cup\{w_i\})\), pair \(\{i,i+q\}\cup T\) with \(\{i,i+q\}\cup T\cup\{w_i\}\), in descending order of \(\lvert T\rvert\). At each step the upper face is maximal among remaining faces and the lower one has no other remaining coface, hence this is a sequence of elementary collapses. These intervals for distinct distance-\(q\) edges do not overlap: an arc of \(q+1\) consecutive vertices has only one pair at cyclic distance \(q\). Performing all such collapses leaves precisely \(\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^{q-1})\). Iterating for \(q=p,p-1,\ldots,2\) yields \(X\searrow \operatorname{Cl}(C_n)=C_n\simeq S^1\).

**2. The fault edge's flag link is a sparse interval clique complex.** Rotation and reflection let us take \(e=\{0,d\}\), with \(1\le d\le p\). Let \(L=\operatorname{lk}_X(e)\). Its vertices are exactly the common graph neighbors of \(0\) and \(d\). Using integer representatives on the short arc, this set is
\[
S_{p,d}=\{d-p,\ldots,-1\}\;\cup\;\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\;\cup\;\{d+1,\ldots,p\},
\]
where an interval with reversed endpoints is empty. The bound \(n\ge4p+2\) ensures that two distinct vertices \(a,b\in S_{p,d}\) are adjacent if and only if \(|a-b|\le p\); there are no extra wraparound edges. Hence \(L\) is the clique complex of this one-dimensional threshold graph.

**3. A complete link collapse when \(p\ge3\).** Order the representatives in \(S_{p,d}\) increasingly. Every consecutive gap is at most \(3\): ordinary gaps are one, gaps around either deleted endpoint are two, and a gap of three is possible only for \(d=1\). For \(p\ge3\), every consecutive pair is adjacent. If \(v\) is the least current vertex and \(w\) is its successor, then every current graph neighbor of \(v\) is a graph neighbor of \(w\): all current vertices lie to the right, so \(u-v\le p\) implies \(0\le u-w\le p\) for \(u>w\), while \(v\) itself is adjacent to \(w\). Thus the closed neighborhood of \(v\) is contained in the closed neighborhood of \(w\). The link of \(v\) in the current clique complex is a cone with apex \(w\), so all simplices containing \(v\) collapse away by elementary face/coface pairs. Repeating removes every vertex but one, proving \(L\searrow\{w_0\}\).

When \(p=2,d=2\), the common-neighbor graph has exactly one vertex, so the same conclusion holds. For \(p=2,d=1\), it instead has exactly two nonadjacent vertices, so \(L\cong S^0\).

**4. From a collapsible edge link to an elementary collapse \(X\searrow Y\).** The simplices of \(X\) missing from \(Y\) are exactly the faces of the form \(e\cup\sigma\), where \(\sigma\in L\), including \(\sigma=\varnothing\). For any elementary collapse pair \((\sigma,\tau)\) of nonempty faces in \(L\), lift it to the pair \((e\cup\sigma,e\cup\tau)\). These are genuine elementary collapse pairs in \(X\) relative to \(Y\): every coface of a simplex containing \(e\) also contains \(e\), and its other vertices lie in \(L\). After lifting the collapse \(L\searrow\{w_0\}\), just \(e\) and \(e\cup\{w_0\}\) remain outside \(Y\). Collapse that final pair. Hence \(X\searrow Y\) whenever \(L\) is collapsible, proving both the explicit inclusion result and \(Y\simeq S^1\).

**5. The exceptional squared-cycle fault.** For \(p=2,d=1\), write \(e=\{0,1\}\). In \(Y\) the triangles \(\{-1,0,1\}\) and \(\{0,1,2\}\) are absent. Every remaining distance-two edge except \(\{-1,1\}\) and \(\{0,2\}\) still has exactly one incident triangle, and each such edge/triangle pair collapses. The remaining connected graph has all \(n\) vertices, the \(n-1\) consecutive edges other than \(e\), and two surviving distance-two chords, for \(n+1\) edges in total. Its cycle rank is \((n+1)-n+1=2\). Thus \(Y\simeq S^1\vee S^1\). This proves the complete classification and the sharp threshold in \(p\).

## Verification
The standalone `VERIFY.py` independently reconstructs the graph from cyclic distances, exhaustively enumerates all flag simplices in moderate instances, computes each common-neighbor link directly from the graph, compares it to the stated integer-interval model, verifies the closed-neighborhood domination reduction, and **replays elementary face/coface collapses with free-face tests**. It verifies the link model and domination for \(p=2,3,4,5,6\) and \(4p+2\le n\le4p+4\); 29 nonexceptional fault instances also receive a full relative-collapse replay; three exceptional squared-cycle cases are checked separately, and the intact complex collapses are independently checked for each \(p\) from two to six. The exceptional squared-cycle graph cycle rank is checked directly. All checks passed. This finite computation corroborates but is not the proof of the unbounded statement; the preceding five steps are uniform arguments for every permitted integer.

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek and Adams (arXiv:1503.03669, 2015), Theorem 4.3, classify **intact** clique complexes of cycle powers and provide Lemma 4.8 on edge removal when both directed graphs remain *cyclic*. Their Lemma 4.8 can cover deletion of an outermost distance-\(p\) edge under its winding-fraction hypotheses, but it does not directly cover interior distances \(d<p\): deleting the arc \(0\to d\) while retaining \(0\to(d+1)\) breaks the cyclic-graph closure condition. The full-text comparison was to the exact hypotheses of that lemma, not merely its title. Adams, Coldren and Willmot (arXiv:1812.03374, first public 2018-12-08) develop persistence algorithms for filtrations whose members are cyclic graphs; those hypotheses likewise do not cover interior-edge-faulted graphs. The published *A sharp Alexander-dual collapse for ordinary three-cut complexes of cycle powers* treats the unmodified clique complex as a subcomplex of a different Alexander dual, without an edge-fault classification. The published *One-edge homotopy stability of demihypercube flag complexes* studies a different underlying graph family and a different homology response. These statement-level comparisons do not establish global bibliographic uniqueness.

## Limitations
The graph fault concerns a single one-skeleton edge; it is not equivalent to recomputing graph shortest-path distances and then taking a graph-metric Rips complex. The bound \(n\ge4p+2\) is sufficient and not asserted minimal. No simultaneous-fault theorem, equivariant simple-homotopy classification or all-scale persistence barcode is established. The result follows from a local simplicial-collapse argument and does not imply stability of other graph complexes. A later paper on induced subcomplexes of cycle-power flag complexes may be relevant, but a spanning edge-deleted complex is not an induced vertex subcomplex; its complete text was unavailable for an exhaustive comparison. Unindexed equivalents remain possible.
