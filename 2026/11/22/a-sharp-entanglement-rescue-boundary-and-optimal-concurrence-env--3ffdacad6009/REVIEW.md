# Scientific review: collective resetting versus spontaneous emission in an entangling qubit pair

## Correctness — PASS
The analytic claim specifies a four-dimensional Lindblad evolution with an explicit coherent pair-conversion Hamiltonian, two nonunital local amplitude-damping channels, and joint Poisson re-preparation. For any strictly positive reset rate, a completely positive resolvent establishes existence, uniqueness, and positivity of the stationary state. The exact stationary invariant sector satisfies three scalar balance equations, explicitly solved in `RESULT.md`; substitution into the established two-qubit \(X\)-state concurrence identity gives the phase boundary. Strict monotonicity of the threshold quadratic classifies all positive resetting rates. The derivative of the concurrence with respect to the coupling has exactly one positive zero inside the entangled region, proving global optimality and the sharp envelope. The envelope's monotonicity and both endpoint limits follow from an explicit one-variable derivative.

An independently constructed sixteen-dimensional Liouvillian solver in `CHECK.py` checks 85 parameter triples plus stationary residuals, partial transpose, extrema, phase boundaries, and scaling, totaling 782 assertions. No numerical test is used in place of a quantified proof. Limitations: strict positivity of emission and resetting for the principal claims; joint, not independent, reset; homogeneous spontaneous-emission rates; no thermal excitations or detuning.

## Originality — PASS, with residual publication-priority risk
Kulkarni–Majumdar (2023) establish quantum-reset entanglement in a **closed** transverse-field Ising pair; Hartmann–Dür–Briegel (2007) show entanglement under emission plus **local single-qubit** replacements, and analyze a related unreset dissipative pair. The physical mechanism of reset-assisted entanglement, stationary renewal resolvents, and standard concurrence are established prior knowledge. Their claims neither supply nor directly imply the sharp stationary boundary and coupling-optimized envelope under the present **joint** ground reset plus nonunital spontaneous emission. A published dephasing model with joint pair resetting has fixed odd-parity populations, not the spontaneous-emission transfer terms; a published local bit-flip model has unital rather than amplitude-damping channels. These differences alter the stationary equations and their optimization conditions, not merely their names. Search comparisons covered coherent pair conversion, two-qubit XX aliases, local spontaneous emission, joint re-preparation, rate thresholds, and optimized concurrence. The closest published results are listed in `AUDIT.json`.

Residual risk: an exact formula for this special stationary Lindbladian could appear as an implicit case of a previously solved general two-qubit open-system model, including a model expressed with \(\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x\) on a symmetric stationary invariant sector. The search and inspections do not prove world-wide novelty. The unreset golden-ratio bound is a limiting **comparison**, not an independent new assertion about unreset physics.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The combined sharp boundary and optimizing envelope answer natural, quantitatively distinct physical questions: which coherent pair-pump strengths can be entangled at a given emission/reset rate, how much reset is required to rescue a pair whose unreset steady state is separable, and how large stationary concurrence can become if the coherent pump strength is tunable. The result distinguishes control at fixed coupling from coupling-optimized control, including the striking difference between a vanishing fixed-coupling large-reset limit and a strictly increasing optimized envelope. It supplies a complete global phase diagram, rather than a parameter-renaming, elementary normalization check, or arbitrary finite enumeration. The result is restricted to the stated minimal model and does not claim a general quantum-control theorem.
