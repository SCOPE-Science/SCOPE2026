# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact annular Gaussian moment spectrum follows by angular orthogonality. The known adjacent-difference formula makes the finite spectrum unimodal, so the smallest eigenvalue of the complete first-\(N+1\)-mode compression is attained at degree zero or degree \(N\). The ratio of their captures increases strictly in the inner squared radius and has one crossing. Before the crossing the last-degree capture increases; after the crossing the vacuum capture decreases. This gives the unique global annular minimax placement and equality modes. Stirling expansion, a bounded truncated-exponential integral and the exact crossing equation give the \(N/e\) radius and minimax-energy equivalents; a separate Taylor argument covers thin annuli. The standalone checker passed 1,319 numerical and identity tests. Finite tests do not prove the all-parameter result; the complete proof is in RESULT.md.

## Originality — PASS under accessible comparisons
The Fock/Gaussian annular spectrum and its unimodality were previously known, and are explicitly acknowledged. Full published comparisons with the published-finding corpus Hermite-mode staircase and Gaussian-annulus eigenmode crossing works concern the **largest** spectral value, while this theorem solves maximization of the **smallest** value over annulus placement for a consecutive band with a unique balancing radius and a new \(N/e\) placement law. The published two-state Fock mask problem treats general masks and vanishing area; its one-annulus limit is compatible with our thin-annulus law but does not state the finite-area all-band optimum. Galbis and Dewage–Ólafsson provide individual spectral estimates/diagonalization; Knutsen studies porous-mask operator norms. Residual risk: an equivalent all-band E-optimal design may exist in unindexed sampling literature. The classical adjacent recurrence is a prerequisite, not claimed as novel; this makes originality a narrower, best-knowledge conclusion.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For reconstructing arbitrary superpositions of the first \(N+1\) Hermite modes with one connected fixed-area annulus, the least eigenvalue is the exact worst-case energy fraction. The theorem determines both the globally optimal sensor placement for every area and bandlimit and its sharp exponential high-bandwidth deterioration, useful for selecting measurement radii and quantifying conditioning. It is a natural complete constrained classification rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. Disconnected/nonradial observation masks are outside scope.
