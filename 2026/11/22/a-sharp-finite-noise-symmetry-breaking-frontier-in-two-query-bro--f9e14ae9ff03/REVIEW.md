# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Gaussian conditioning yields an exact four-variable quadratic risk. For both nonnegative optimal regression coefficients, strict convexity of site placement followed by a sum/difference algebraic identity forces the symmetric pair; for a negative coefficient, concavity in its corresponding site forces an endpoint or collision, giving precisely the correlated-noise-reference geometry. Strict one-variable monotonicity proves the unique threshold and exact equality cases. The independent symbolic verifier reproduced the critical algebraic identities and exact-rational endpoint reductions; its numerical probes only supplement the proof. The singular correlation boundary is excluded.

**Originality — PASS (subject to historical priority review).** The independent-noise symmetric pair is prior work. The broader literature on noisy Bayesian quadrature (Cai–Lam–Scarlett, arXiv:2202.10615), correlation-aware experimental design (Attia–Constantinescu, arXiv:2007.14476), and Brownian-bridge kernel interpolation (Santin, arXiv:2210.15959) does not directly yield a global finite-noise result for two positively correlated measurements of the *integral*. Earlier mathematical records show a high-noise correlation threshold and an isolated finite-noise asymmetric witness; those do not prove the present all-noise classification, uniqueness, or critical variance. Search and accessible-full-text comparisons did not identify direct or stronger coverage. An undiscovered special-case experimental-design result remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** This resolves a natural decision problem in correlated-noise quadrature: when to sacrifice one observation of the signal to obtain a correlated noise reference. It supplies an exact global phase diagram, a nontrivial finite-noise threshold, discontinuous optimal placement, and a sharp transition between interior quadrature and endpoint noise estimation, without asserting an arbitrary-Gaussian-process theorem.

**Residual risks and limits.** The result assumes nonnegative correlation less than one, identical marginal error variance, predetermined two-query sites, and pinned Brownian endpoints. Its priority has not been independently validated; some historical discussions of correlated design could use different terminology.
