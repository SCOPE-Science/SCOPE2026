**Correctness — PASS.** The displayed companion recurrence is defined for arbitrary real initial data. On a bounded lag window, sine is strictly contractive in amplitude; a block-maximum argument proves global convergence. Strictly positive histories obey inverse-square-root upper and lower envelope bounds. The companion linearization is primitive with one simple unit multiplier and all transverse multipliers strictly contracting. The fifth-order center-manifold jet follows from an explicit inverse-iterate calculation, and the inverse-square clock has a summable error after subtracting its linear and logarithmic terms. The sharp four/five-lag cutoff is an exact nonnegative-moment inequality. The standalone checker supports algebra and finite numerical diagnostics but is not substituted for proof.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** The 2024 generating paper studies a third-order sine map through fixed-point stability, bifurcation graphics and control; it does not state the memory-second-moment logarithmic law. The open 2018/2019 delay-difference Morse-decomposition article studies qualitative global dynamics, not this clock. Related indexed continuous-time Ikeda and Thomas sine delay results support the background theme of critical algebraic decay but are not equivalent to finite-memory discrete recurrences and do not cover the signed coefficient or sharp minimum lag. Searches for the same moments, arbitrary delay distributions and normal-form equivalences found no decisive covering theorem. The residual risk is an unindexed general result in discrete parabolic center-manifold asymptotics.

**Value — PASS.** The precise next-order coefficient separates delay kernels sharing the same mean and leading extinction rate. Its possible signs have a natural, sharp structural boundary: no negative correction for active delays at most four, exact cancellation first at four, and negative drift at five. These are motivated, nontrivial parameter-independent facts about the speed of global relaxation, not a new notation for scalar sine iteration or a computation of a single arbitrary numerical orbit.

**Limitations.** The fine asymptotic is restricted to strictly positive/negative bounded histories. General mixed-sign histories are proved to tend to zero but need not share this clock. The result is for deterministic finite memory; it does not provide global convergence rates uniform over all histories or weights.
