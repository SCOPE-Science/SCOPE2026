**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual priority risk.** The motivating simulation paper studies spectroscopic cadence and reports empirical optima; it does not state the analytic fixed-template limiting law. Existing idealized matched-template Doppler-autocorrelation theory already gives uniqueness and large-overhead square-root scaling for the **broadened, fully matched** filter, and those facts are deliberately treated here as prior comparison benchmarks. The fixed unbroadened template replaces the full signal norm by one squared projection; a matched-template theorem and Cauchy–Schwarz alone do not imply the fixed optimum, its finite saturation or its sharp loss \(R_*=0.6293963793\ldots\). Relevant Gaussian radial-velocity precision literature addresses other estimator objectives. **Value — PASS.** The distinction is motivated by a concrete decision in transit cross-correlation analysis: retain an unbroadened template or adapt it to within-exposure Doppler smearing. The closed-form phase behavior supplies a universal mathematical benchmark. The two optimizers agree to leading cube-root order at small overhead but diverge qualitatively at large overhead, and optimal fixed-template use loses about 37.06% of idealized information in that limit. Such a sharp, nonvanishing effect is more informative than a local numerical cadence optimum. Its physical scope remains intentionally restricted to a stationary weak-line, white-noise model.
