# Scientific review: inverse dual-curvature measure on rectangles

**Correctness — PASS.** With the standard planar \(q\)-th dual curvature normalization, integration over the four radial edge cones yields the exact positive formulas \(A=rb^q I_x(r)\), \(B=rb^q I_y(r)\). Every planar realizer must first be an axis-parallel rectangle, because the four-atom dual measure has the same normal support as ordinary surface-area measure. A strict positive derivative of opposite-edge mass in the corresponding support number forces central symmetry for \(q\ge1\); below that threshold the same derivative can change sign, and a continuous two-endpoint argument produces explicit off-center four-atom competitors. A strict pointwise comparison of two log-derivative integrands proves the ratio map's monotonicity for all \(q>0\) other than the critical index, without relying on numerics. Dominated convergence and a change of variables establish the endpoint ranges; the mass equation fixes scale uniquely except at \(q=2\). The standalone checker independently compares cone and angular definitions and exact low-degree identities. Its numerical observations do not substitute for the analytic proof.

**Originality — PASS (historical-priority qualification).** Monahan's main existence theorems require normal configurations excluding the four coordinate antipodal directions (the planar additional theorem has an odd number of normals at least five). Xiong–Yang's uniqueness theorem among origin-symmetric bodies at \(0<q<2\) already implies restricted symmetric uniqueness; the symmetric subspace-concentration existence theorems of Böröczky–Lutwak–Yang–Zhang–Zhao and Shan also characterize abstract solvability there. We do **not** claim that as new. The complete all-convex-body rigidity at \(1\le q<2\), its sharp failure for \(q<1\), the explicit construction, and the \(q>2\) global inverse are the assessed new aspects. Their \(q>2\) nonuniqueness and total-quermassintegral rectangle calculations do not determine the present four prescribed facet masses. Existing subspace-concentration bounds for higher \(q\) give some necessary ratio constraints, not the full explicit realizability, rigidity, and inverse theorem. Tao's planar dual-quermassintegral inequalities and the previously published planar cone \(L_p\) inverse computation study different functionals or data. Statement-level comparisons against nearby indexed results and published earlier geometry findings did not locate an implication-equivalent complete rectangular inverse image. Unindexed or older special-case treatments, especially of cone-volume data, remain a real priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Four antipodally symmetric normal atoms are the smallest natural family for testing a discrete even planar dual Minkowski inverse in a fixed coordinate fan. The theorem supplies the sharp global image and reconstruction for every exponent, a complete global fiber classification for \(q\ge1\), and a sharp new symmetry-breaking phenomenon below \(q=1\). It combines nonattained obstructions for \(q>1\), a full positive cone of allowable ratios for \(q\le1\), and a one-dimensional critical fiber at \(q=2\). For \(q<2\), the symmetric solvability range is also implicit in known general subspace-concentration criteria; those results do not supply the explicit reconstruction, the origin-symmetry rigidity, or its sharp breakdown. For \(q>2\), known concentration obstructions alone do not provide sufficiency or the complete global fiber. The result supplies a concrete boundary example for measure-realizability beyond generic-position hypotheses. The global rigidity theorem uses the exact four-normal support; no result for arbitrary normal sets or complete off-center solution fibers at \(q<1\) is asserted.
