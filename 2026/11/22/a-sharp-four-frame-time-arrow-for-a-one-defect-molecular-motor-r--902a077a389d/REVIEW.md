# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite four-state chain is irreducible and aperiodic throughout the stated parameter domain. The exact stationary distribution \((1,s,s,s)/(1+3s)\) satisfies balance, giving edge current \(s(p-q)/(1+3s)\). Symbolic multiplication of the four-frame law gives four nonzero reversal pairs, each of absolute discrepancy \(pqs|p-q|(1-s)/(1+3s)\), hence the claimed total variation. A separate enumerator checks every microscopic path with exact rational arithmetic for 46 admissible parameter settings. Global reversibility at the three zero surfaces follows from detailed balance, a label-preserving reflection, or stationary alternating-renewal symmetry. Any deterministic binary observation of a Markov chain with at most three states has a singleton label class and the same alternating-renewal symmetry. Differentiating the separated defect and bias factors proves the unique joint optimum; no infinite proof rests on finite numerics.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Arola-Fernández and Lacasa (arXiv:2303.11868, Section III.1) prove that stationary binary words shorter than four cannot reveal irreversibility. This is prior work, not the claimed novelty. Harunari et al. (arXiv:2203.07427, Section V.2, Eq. 57-58 and Appendix B) recover ring-cycle affinity from selected directed transition events, which the present two-half sampled occupancy detector does not record. Ertel and Seifert (arXiv:2312.08246, Sections III-V) derive information inequalities for blurred transition classes and intertransition times, not the exact four-snapshot binary-state total variation or its maximizers. Degünther et al. (2024) derive a general coarse-grained trajectory framework, not this parameter-specific observable phase diagram. The published homogeneous-ring classification on binary invisibility excludes site-dependent departure probabilities, which are essential here. Exact overlap searches in the published result database and primary literature returned related themes but not an implication covering the one-defect \(4\)-state iff theorem plus the sharp \(1/3\) and \(2+\sqrt3\) design points. The risk of implicit coverage in general hidden-Markov computations or uncatalogued models remains, and publication priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The four-state system is a minimal nontrivial model of a molecular motor observed through coarse spatial resolution. The theorem makes a meaningful physical distinction between hidden current and *observable* time direction, including the counterintuitive invisibility of wholly one-way transport. It also computes and jointly optimizes the full distributional four-frame distinguishing signal at fixed activity, giving exact sensor-model design benchmarks rather than a routine single probability. Its proof identifies why a site defect and bidirectional dynamics are both necessary for this observation, and why four hidden states and four observation frames are sharp lower limits. Application beyond this model and finite-data statistical power are deliberately not claimed.

## Limits and relationship to literature
The finite-state formulas apply only to the one-defect discrete-time ring with two adjacent sites per binary label and positive stay probabilities. Source results involving arbitrary observation times, directed transition records, general network entropy bounds, or homogeneous rings cannot simply be treated as equivalent. The narrow scope is mathematically motivated by the minimality of the hidden-state and observation counts. A broader algebraic specialization of existing general theory may still settle publication priority, which needs independent review.
