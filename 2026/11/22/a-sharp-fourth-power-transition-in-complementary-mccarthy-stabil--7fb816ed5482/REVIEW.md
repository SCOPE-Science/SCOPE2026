# Scientific review of the projection-pair trace-stability finding

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is an explicit two-sided sharp inequality for every real exponent \(3\le p\le5\), in arbitrary finite rank. Principal-angle decomposition exactly reduces both trace deficit and commutator Hilbert–Schmidt energy to the same generic two-dimensional blocks. The scalar quotient is a right-anchored secant of a function whose second-derivative sign is controlled by generalized even binomial coefficients and a single power term. Signs reverse at \(p=4\). Exact boundary derivatives give both constants and two-dimensional almost-orthogonal/almost-coincident witnesses prove global optimality. Endpoint and equality cases have been checked. The standalone code provides diagnostics only, not infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk
Zhang's arXiv:2611.00459v1, Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 3.3, already contains the general nonnegative complementary trace deficit and the scalar expression. The published result *A sharp commutator remainder in the quartic complementary McCarthy inequality* establishes the stronger quartic identity for arbitrary positive operators and is expressly credited. Neither inspected source gives the noninteger-exponent sharp two-sided Hilbert–Schmidt commutator comparison, angular monotonicity, or exchange of endpoint controls across four. Source and implication searches found no theorem dominating this nonquartic projection statement. Another source under classical principal-angle, trace-stability or functional-calculus terminology may still supply an equivalent inequality, so priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complementary McCarthy inequality alone yields only a nonnegative deficit. This result identifies how much deficit measures noncommutativity uniformly in dimension, supplies both best coefficients over a continuous exponent interval, and exposes a sharp change of worst-case principal-angle geometry at the natural quartic exponent. The nonquartic parameter ranges add material mathematical content beyond the known quartic identity, even though the proof can be reduced to a transparent scalar convexity argument.

## Scope and risks
Finite-rank orthogonal projections only; no assertion for general positive matrices at nonquartic powers or for exponents outside the proved interval. Sharp coefficients are limiting, not noncommuting equality cases, away from four. Quantitative two-projection inequalities may be indexed by another terminology. Independent refereeing, exhaustive global literature certification and independent audit have not been performed.
