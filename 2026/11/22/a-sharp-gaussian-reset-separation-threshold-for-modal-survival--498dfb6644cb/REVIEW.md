# Scientific review of the Gaussian-reset mode classification

**Correctness — PASS.** The invariant density is derived directly from the independent exponential reset-age formula; the Laplace Green kernel and Gaussian variance-mixture formula agree. The ratio of the normal-Laplace kernel to the Gaussian source is strictly increasing on the positive half-line and crosses one once, establishing the unique and nondegenerate inflection beyond the single-Gaussian threshold. The piecewise sign sequence of the derivative of a symmetric pair of Laplace kernels, combined with the standard Gaussian variation-diminishing theorem, bounds stationary critical points globally. The quartic boundary, peak-opening coefficient and two asymptotic limits follow from exact derivatives, an implicit-function expansion and explicit inequalities. The checker independently passed 213 finite diagnostics. It does not replace the infinite mathematical proofs.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Toledo-Marín and Boyer (arXiv:2206.14387) and Besga et al. (arXiv:2004.11311) analyze Gaussian random reset positions primarily for absorption and first-passage outcomes. Reed's normal-Laplace distribution supplies a known single-component building block. The classical symmetric two-normal mixture supplies only the zero-smoothing threshold. Closely related published results for two-point resetting under an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck drift and for smoothed active-particle distributions have different stationary kernels and do not state this exact free-Brownian two-Gaussian threshold or the logarithmic rare-reset asymptotic. The standard Gaussian variation-diminishing theorem is acknowledged. Priority may still be affected by unindexed mixture-mode literature or implicit consequences of existing resolvents; the literature comparison is not a global novelty certificate.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A bimodal reset-location mechanism can yield a unimodal stationary observation because Brownian excursions erase the spatial separation. The theorem identifies the entire physically motivated modal phase diagram, the exact finite-rate gap beyond the two-Gaussian source threshold, its continuous critical bifurcation and the unexpectedly slow logarithmic separation required for two modes at weak resetting. It provides a natural quantitative boundary for deciding when two spatial populations remain distinguishable in stationary resetting experiments, without asserting statistical sampling guarantees.

The result applies to two equally weighted Gaussian populations, exact Poisson resetting and free one-dimensional Brownian motion. The stationary theorem and limits do not extend automatically to unequal source weights or non-Poisson clocks.
