# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The rational Taylor expansion is obtained by the negative-binomial identity, the coefficient sequence crosses zero exactly once because its ratios are strictly below one for positive degree, and the leading off-diagonal Laplacian power is exactly the positive weighted geodesic sum after sign cancellation. Strict negativity at the first negative coefficient therefore holds for all sufficiently small steps on every graph of adequate diameter. The connected spectral projector proves eventual strict positivity. Stirling bounds establish the precise asymptotic distance, and the exact symbolic verifier checks coefficients and a rational five-vertex witness. Finite checks are not invoked as a proof of infinite claims.

The complete published note on scalar Richardson sign reversal proves a scalar positive-real stability-function threshold, not a geodesic, matrix-entrywise or small-step obstruction. The published network self-transition theorem explicitly restricts its claim to diagonals at two substeps; the directed five-state pure-birth classification likewise fixes two substeps and one generator. Their exact statements, not their titles, were compared with the result here. The fully read Zlatev–Faragó–Havasi and Mona–Lagzi–Havasi papers establish the extrapolation construction and stability/order contexts but do not contain the all-refinement geodesic coefficient classification in the inspected relevant sections. The uninspected remainder of the vast classical monotonicity/extrapolation literature remains a publication-priority risk, not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The theorem is a structural counterexample to assuming a positive backward-Euler building block or increasing refinement will ensure a discrete maximum principle. It applies to every connected positive-weight graph of adequate diameter and every integer refinement level, with a sharp leading-distance rule, explicit minimal path witness for that rule, and a quantitative logarithmic-over-logarithmic limitation on improvement by refinement. It is useful when selecting extrapolated diffusion schemes for network concentrations or probability distributions. The assertion does not encompass all positivity failures in shorter graphs or arbitrary timesteps.
