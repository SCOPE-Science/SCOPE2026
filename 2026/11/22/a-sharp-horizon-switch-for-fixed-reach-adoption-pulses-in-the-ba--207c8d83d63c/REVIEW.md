# Scientific review: fixed-reach adoption pulses

## Correctness

PASS. The flow conjugacy \(Y=(p+qx)/(1-x)\), strict monotonicity of the Bass trajectory, the sign of the state-impulse timing derivative, and the rational comparison of the two endpoint transformed states jointly give a global theorem for every permitted positive parameter and every time in the closed horizon. The cap at one is treated separately and proves that the saturation plateau consists of every and only time at or beyond the explicit first-saturation date. The symbolic factorization yields a positive gap between the phase-switch and saturation thresholds, and the proportional-reach derivative is strictly negative. The exact rational checker is a supporting diagnostic, not the proof.

## Originality

PASS under best-knowledge priority review. The original 1969 Bass paper establishes the underlying diffusion equation. The full-text 2011 generalized Bass optimal advertising analysis controls continuous expenditure and profit rather than an instantaneous fixed-adopter-reach jump and terminal adoption. The full-text 2006 two-segment product-seeding model of Lehmann and Esteban-Bravo holds seeding at the initial date and optimizes a discounted-profit objective, leaving the continuous impulse-time terminal-adoption classification unresolved. Delre et al.'s 2007 timing study uses heterogeneous agents, with only its abstract available here. Relevant already-indexed results on phase-ordering of innovation/imitation rates and attrition-bearing Bass flows do not imply the fixed absolute pulse horizon phase diagram or its relative-reach contrast. Standard scalar Riccati and impulse comparison techniques could still cover the result in a less obvious formulation; historical priority is provisional, not certified.

## Value

PASS. The claim gives a complete natural scheduling classification for the smallest nontrivial discrete marketing intervention under an important diffusion model, including a genuine sharp first-to-last horizon switch, equality boundary, saturated late-pulse plateau, and a contrasting always-early result for proportional remaining-market reach. These statements distinguish two commonly conflated intervention resources and constrain interpretation of an intervention's timing; no empirical or profitability benefit is asserted.

## Main limitations and residual risks

An abstract-only 2007 agent-based timing article and unexamined general nonlinear hybrid impulse-control theory leave room for hidden mathematical equivalents. The assumptions exclude changing innovation/imitation rates, discount, money cost, finite agents, competitor effects, and repeat purchases. An independent audit and comprehensive bibliography search remain outstanding.
