**Correctness — PASS.** The assertion quantifies over every real data vector, all positive fidelity masses and penalties, every finite star, and the full regularization ray. The proof eliminates leaves by exact soft thresholding, derives the strictly monotone scalar hub KKT equation, proves an affine-intercept no-exit lemma for all clipping cells, handles initial ties, and shows necessity with a positive-length fused interval that is followed by separation. The weight-design condition follows by a normalized mass-budget identity. A standalone exact-rational checker corroborates the KKT and explicit examples, but the proof carries the universal quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** Tibshirani–Taylor, arXiv:1005.1971 Section 6, already show that row diagonal dominance is sufficient for dual boundary persistence, so neither that implication nor the general KKT apparatus is presented as new. Hoefling, arXiv:0910.0526 Section 3, allows splitting and supplies a general flow algorithm without the necessary-and-sufficient weighted-star test. Chiquet–Gutierrez–Rigaill, arXiv:1407.5915 Section 5, analyze data-dependent complete-pair fusion weights and do not imply the fixed-star converse. The new content is necessity for each spoke, an explicit unfusion interval upon failure, and existence of a stabilizing weight system exactly at the hub-mass budget. Nearby historical star Huber and Gini results concern two-input variation sensitivity rather than regularization-path fusion persistence. Residual priority risk: specialized unindexed converses and follow-up literature may overlap.

**Value — PASS.** Whether a path algorithm may permanently merge groups is an operationally central mathematical property of fusion regularization. A full necessary-and-sufficient criterion on a fundamental branching topology identifies a sharp four-leaf obstruction at equal masses and tells which hub masses admit *any* stable penalty design; it is not merely a numerical case or a rephrasing of a sufficient bound. Scope remains star graphs and positive quadratic masses, not general networks.
