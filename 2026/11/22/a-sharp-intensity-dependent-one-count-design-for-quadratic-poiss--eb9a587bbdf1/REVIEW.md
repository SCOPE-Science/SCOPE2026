# Review of the quadratic Poisson-path one-count design

## Correctness — PASS
The exact posterior polynomial follows by conditioning on the observed count, using a binomial bridge on the observed prefix and independent Poisson increments afterward. Its explained variance follows from third and fourth Poisson moments, and the target variance follows from an independently derived bivariate covariance. The derivative has a degree-four intensity-polynomial decomposition. Strict positive/negative signs outside \((2/3,\sqrt{3/5})\) and strict derivative negativity inside that interval establish one **global** minimizer for every positive intensity, including both endpoint checks. The implicit differentiation shows that the unique optimizer increases strictly with intensity; the perturbation limits and high-intensity risk comparison are algebraic consequences. The bundled symbolic and exact-moment checks, with independent seeded simulation, supplement the analytic reasoning. No finite calculation is used as an infinite-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified by historical-priority risk)
The closest classical exact theorem is Cressie (1978), following Samaniego (1976): an optimal time of \(2/3\) for predicting the *linear* time integral of any stationary-independent-increment process. The full text of Cressie's article was inspected around the statement, conditional predictor, risk computation and minimization: it applies to \(\int X_t\,dt\), and its result does not imply the quadratic-Poisson-energy optimizer. Przybyłowicz (2017) is the motivating jump-diffusion numerical-information source (arXiv:1701.08311, first publicly posted 2017-01-28), but its exact approximation-rate and optimal-grid assertions concern global SDE paths, not this one-count Bayes functional. The primary abstract and beginning of Przybyłowicz–Sobieraj–Stępień (2021) concern a different SDE approximation problem. A published Poisson-path random-stratum quadrature theorem was inspected in full and treats a *linear* path integral under randomized numerical evaluation. Claim-level searches for the quadratic functional, bridge conditional moments, aliases and optimizer endpoint \(\sqrt{3/5}\) revealed no direct implication. These comparisons support a provisional novelty judgment, not a complete historical uniqueness certificate. The specialist Beentjes (2021) Poisson-bridge paper was inaccessible in full text and remains a disclosed residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The nonlinear counting-path energy \(\int N_t^2\,dt\) measures time-integrated occupancy-squared exposure, a natural congestion or concentration cost not captured by the classical linear-integral design. A single count is a common severe sampling-budget constraint. The exact intensity-dependent Bayes-optimal observation time is practically implementable without approximating the observed path, with a sharp monotone transition from \(2/3\) to \(\sqrt{3/5}\). At high intensity, switching designs improves the leading squared-error coefficient by \(24.158\ldots\%\), which is substantial rather than a purely cosmetic parametrization. The proof supplies the exact risk for arbitrary positive rates, not just a fitted cutoff. The claim is specific to known-rate homogeneous Poisson paths, noiseless one-time counts, squared loss and a quadratic integrated target; it does not prove analogous results for other powers, noise, unknown rates, or multiple samples.
