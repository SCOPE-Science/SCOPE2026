# A sharp isoperimetric inequality for anisotropic isocanted zonotopes

## Finding
Let \(d\ge 2\), let \(b_1,\ldots,b_d>0\), let \(a>0\), and set
\[
Z_d(\mathbf b,a)=\sum_{i=1}^d[0,b_i e_i]+[0,a\mathbf1]\subset\mathbb R^d.
\]
Write \(V_d\) for Euclidean volume, \(S_d=\mathcal H^{d-1}(\partial Z_d)\) for total Euclidean facet area, and \(\mathcal I_d=S_d^d/V_d^{d-1}\) for the scale-invariant isoperimetric quotient. Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal I_d\ge (\sqrt2\,d)^d\left[1+d(\sqrt2-1)\right].}
\]
Equality holds if and only if there exists \(b>0\) with \(b_1=\cdots=b_d=b\) and \(a=(\sqrt2-1)b\). These conditions are unique modulo simultaneous positive scaling and coordinate permutation. The exact volume and surface area, valid for all positive parameters, are
\[
V_d=\prod_{i=1}^d b_i+a\sum_{i=1}^d\prod_{j\ne i}b_j,
\quad
S_d=2\sum_{i=1}^d\prod_{j\ne i}b_j+2\sqrt2\,a\sum_{1\le i<j\le d}\prod_{k\notin\{i,j\}}b_k.
\]
For the symmetric isocanted cube parameterization \(\ell=b+a\), the equality ratio is \(a/\ell=1-1/\sqrt2\). This coincides with the previously studied inball contact transition; neither that transition nor the equal-width face-volume formulas are claimed as new. Compared with the cube's isoperimetric quotient \((2d)^d\), the sharp constrained ratio is \(2^{-d/2}[1+d(\sqrt2-1)]\).

## Assumptions and scope
All coordinates and measures use the ambient standard Euclidean metric (not an affine-normalized metric). Each generating segment is closed. All \(d+1\) segment lengths are strictly positive, so the zonotope is full-dimensional and its \(2d+d(d-1)\) facets are genuine. The lower bound is for this *prescribed direction family*: \(d\) coordinate axes and their positive all-ones diagonal, with **arbitrary unequal positive segment lengths**. It is not an inequality for unrestricted \((d+1)\)-generator zonotopes, whose global minimizer is already known.

## Proof
**Facet geometry and intrinsic volume.** The \(d+1\) generator directions are \(e_1,\ldots,e_d,\mathbf1\). Every facet normal is proportional either to one coordinate normal \(e_i\) or to one difference normal \(e_i-e_j\), with both signs present. The two coordinate facets perpendicular to \(e_i\) have area \(\prod_{j\ne i}b_j\). For each unordered pair \(\{i,j\}\), the two facets perpendicular to \(e_i-e_j\) are translates of the parallelepiped generated by \(a\mathbf1\) and all \(b_k e_k\), \(k\notin\{i,j\}\). The orthogonal component of \(a\mathbf1\) to the span of the remaining generators has length \(\sqrt2 a\), so each facet has area \(\sqrt2\,a\prod_{k\notin\{i,j\}}b_k\). These exhaust the facets because a facet normal must be orthogonal to \(d-1\) linearly independent generators, and no other possible hyperplanes occur. This proves the displayed surface formula (including \(d=2\), when the empty product is one). The zonotope determinant formula, or the disjoint sweep of the orthotope by its diagonal segment, gives the stated volume formula.

**Dimensionless reduction.** Set \(x_i=1/b_i\), \(\mu=\frac1d\sum_i x_i\), \(y_i=x_i/\mu\), \(p=a\mu\), \(P=\prod_i y_i\), and \(E=\sum_{i<j}y_i y_j\). Thus \(\sum_i y_i=d\), \(0<P\le1\), and \(0<E\le\binom d2\). Writing \(B=\sqrt2 E\), direct substitution into the exact face measures gives
\[
\mathcal I_d=\frac{2^d}{P}\,\frac{(d+Bp)^d}{(1+dp)^{d-1}}.
\]
For fixed \(\mathbf y\), the logarithmic derivative with respect to \(p\) has the sign of \(B(1+p)-d(d-1)\). Since \(0<B\le\sqrt2\binom d2<d(d-1)\), this sign changes exactly once from negative to positive, at the strictly positive point
\[
p_*(\mathbf y)=\frac{d(d-1)}{B}-1.
\]
The function tends to infinity as \(p\to\infty\), so this is its unique global minimum. Substitution yields
\[
\mathcal I_d\ge\frac{2^d}{(d-1)^{d-1}}\frac{(d^2-B)B^{d-1}}{P}.
\]

**Eliminating all anisotropy.** Let \(E_j=e_j(\mathbf y)/\binom dj\) be the normalized elementary symmetric means, with \(E_1=1\), \(E_2=E/\binom d2\), and \(E_d=P\). Newton's inequalities imply the ratios \(E_j/E_{j-1}\) are nonincreasing. Thus \(E_d\le E_2^{d-1}\), equivalently
\[
\frac{B^{d-1}}{P}\ge\left(\sqrt2\binom d2\right)^{d-1}.
\]
Moreover \(B\le B_0:=\sqrt2\binom d2\) by the elementary identity \(2E=d^2-\sum_i y_i^2\). As \(d^2-B>0\), these two inequalities multiply to give
\[
\mathcal I_d\ge\frac{2^d}{(d-1)^{d-1}}(d^2-B_0)B_0^{d-1}=(\sqrt2\,d)^d[1+d(\sqrt2-1)].
\]
Equality in \(B\le B_0\) forces \(y_1=\cdots=y_d=1\) by the strict arithmetic-quadratic mean inequality; this holds also for \(d=2\). Consequently all \(b_i\) coincide, and equality in the fixed-\(\mathbf y\) minimization then forces \(p=\sqrt2-1\), or \(a=(\sqrt2-1)b\). Conversely these parameters attain equality. This proves the theorem for every integer \(d\ge2\), without extrapolating from finite computations.

## Verification
The included `check_zonotope.py` independently constructs the full vertex set of the Minkowski sum, uses Qhull via SciPy to calculate Euclidean volume and boundary measure, and compares these to the claimed facet formulas for deterministic unequal parameters in dimensions two through six. It additionally tests the claimed sharp minimizer against perturbed parameters and checks the algebraic formula in dimensions two through ten. Execute `python check_zonotope.py` with Python, NumPy, and SciPy installed. Numerical experiments check normalization and low-dimensional geometry only; the all-dimensional result rests on the preceding proof and Newton's inequalities.

## Relationship to prior work
De la Puente and Clavería (2020) define the equal-coordinate isocanted alcoved polytopes and their combinatorial geometry. Their 2024 work gives the equal-coordinate volume. De la Puente (2025, Theorem 3.14) computes lower face measures of the *equal-width* family, and its Section 7 explicitly raises the surface-to-volume comparison; those known formulas are not claimed as new. Joós and Lángi (2022/2023, Theorem 3) prove that the *unrestricted* class of zonotopes generated by \(d+1\) segments is isoperimetrically minimized by the regular rhombic-dodecahedral zonotope. That unrestricted minimum is not the sharp larger lower bound for the specified axes-plus-diagonal direction class and does not determine its unique constrained optimizer. Here the independent contribution is optimization over all unequal positive axis lengths together with the diagonal generator, using a sharp Newton-inequality reduction to the symmetric case. The optimum's match with the known inball phase transition is a comparison, not a claim of priority for that transition.

## Limitations
This theorem does not optimize all possible generator directions, does not treat zero-length generators as equality cases, and does not assert a quantitative stability constant away from equality. Computation in a finite set of dimensions is not a substitute for the all-dimensional proof. Historical priority is provisional: an unindexed equivalent formulation of the constrained inequality could exist. The proof uses standard Newton inequalities and the elementary zonotope facet/volume formulas; there is no machine-checked formal proof.
