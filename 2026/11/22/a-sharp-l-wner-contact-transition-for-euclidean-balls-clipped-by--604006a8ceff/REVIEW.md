# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Existence/uniqueness and the rotational symmetry reduce the *unrestricted* enclosing-ellipsoid problem to two positive reciprocal squared semiaxes. Exact linear containment inequalities suffice because the squared-radius envelope has two affine pieces. Strict log-det concavity identifies the unique optimizer; independent isotropic contact distributions certify it. Both branches join continuously with zero first derivative and a second-derivative jump of exactly \(-2d^2\). The lower-dimensional slab case is excluded.

## Originality
PASS on the accessible sources, subject to bibliographic uncertainty. Todd (1982), full text inspected, determines optimal ellipsoids for one/two linear cuts of a ball/ellipsoid, not transverse round-cylinder clipping in dimension at least three. Published summaries about two genuinely unbounded crossing cylinders and orthogonal finite slab systems do not directly imply this intersection. Search for the alternate descriptions (ball cut by cylinder; convex hull of two antipodal caps) found no identical formula, sharp threshold, or contact statement. Full text of some potentially adjacent 2026 work was unavailable; this is residual priority risk, not a proof of novelty from a failed search.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is a natural all-dimensional, nonpolyhedral intersection body with a complete *unrestricted* exact Löwner classification, a genuine change in contact-set dimension, and a sharp nonanalytic volume response. The threshold can diagnose the loss of contact at the spherical poles in bounding-ellipsoid approximations; it is not an arbitrary numerical example. The general Löwner principle is credited.

## Closest prior work and limits
M. J. Todd, *On Minimum Volume Ellipsoids Containing Part of a Given Ellipsoid* (1982), DOI:10.1287/moor.7.2.253. The present result does not cover other cylinder orientations, unequal quadratic axes, or dimensions below three.
