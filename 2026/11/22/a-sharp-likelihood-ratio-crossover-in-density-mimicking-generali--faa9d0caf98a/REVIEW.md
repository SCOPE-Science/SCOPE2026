# Review of the generalized density-sampled half-logistic rate-order theorem

## Correctness: PASS

The proof begins from the exact, normalized count model in the 2024 originating article and establishes geometric summability. Adjacent log probabilities give strict log-concavity and all modal cuts. For any pair of rates, normalization cancels and the ratio increment is governed by a single continuous forward-difference function. An explicit hyperbolic-cosine curvature comparison proves a unique inflection of the governing function and a strictly decreasing positive forward-difference regime; hence there can be only one positive-level crossing. The first ratio step is consequently a necessary and sufficient global condition, including the exact equality/tie boundary. An integral average of a strictly decreasing logistic kernel yields uniqueness of the second-rate restoration threshold. The rational witness checks a genuine likelihood-ratio order failure without approximations. The standalone finite reproducer was executed successfully, but its grids are secondary corroboration rather than proof.

## Originality: PASS, with residual priority risk

The originating primary full text (Barbiero–Hitaj, *Journal of the Indian Society for Probability and Statistics*, 2024, §5, https://doi.org/10.1007/s41096-024-00185-w) proposes precisely the two-parameter density-sampling PMF and qualitatively notes its nonzero-mode flexibility, but does not state the present sharp rate likelihood-ratio phase diagram or one-turn classification. The related 2024 Barbiero–Hitaj *Annals of Operations Research* work (https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-023-05807-3) is an explicit alternative survival-preserving discretization, and its generalized mass formula is not algebraically identical. General likelihood-ratio order theory supplies definitions but not the evaluated boundary. Targeted title, synonym, formula, shape/modal and rate-order searches gave no identified stronger equivalent. This supports provisional originality, not a claim of exhaustive uniqueness; a sufficiently general unlocated sampling-order theorem remains a research-priority risk.

## Value: PASS

The 2024 source explicitly motivates allowing positive modes but does not classify how the two-parameter law behaves under changes of rate. The sharp and complete pairwise monotone-likelihood-ratio order classification distinguishes a robust one-sided comparison regime from an exactly single-peaked, nonmonotone regime. For shapes below one, a unique critical *larger* rate restores the stronger order even after small rate changes break it. This nontrivial structural separation matters to monotone tests and rate-driven modeling; exact mode thresholds and ties describe all possible modal configurations. The result is a mathematical characterization, not a demonstrated empirical improvement.
