# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a fixed-mean existential theorem, not an assertion of negativity for every clock below the threshold. The exact equilibrium-renewal age identity gives the first two photon transmissivity moments. Direct Laguerre expansion produces a strictly convex quadratic Wigner polynomial with an interior vertex. Its minimum is \(1-\Phi(A,B)/\mu\); the Hessian of \(\Phi\) is positive semidefinite, and the scalar transformed function \(\varphi\) has a strictly negative second derivative on all positive intervals. Two Jensen inequalities then yield a globally sharp bound with deterministic equality. Strict concavity proves the unique threshold and handles equality, nondegenerate clocks, and unbounded finite-mean waits. ## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The closest existing two-photon Poisson-reset theorem covers only exponential clocks and is recovered as a special case rather than claimed anew. The Chabaud et al. negativity witnesses concern static quantum states; Malpani et al. establish photon loss without arbitrary-clock optimization; Liu's semi-Markov theory provides broader renewal counting machinery but not the specific Wigner-sign extremal bound; Li et al. examine continuous autonomous photon-addition stabilization. A targeted search of published claims involving renewal-clock Wigner negativity, deterministic re-preparation and attenuation returned relevant adjacent theories but no dominating threshold. The published-source comparison was performed on available full texts; an unindexed universal-optimization result might still imply the new sharp bound. Publication priority has not been independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Wigner negativity is a precise nonclassical optical resource and finite mean re-preparation time is a natural experimental budget. The result answers the all-clock feasibility question with an exact critical budget and identifies the unique optimal protocol, distinguishing a collective stationary mixture from the instantaneous survival of negativity. The nontrivial double-Jensen inequality adds an ordering principle not conveyed by calculating the Poisson case. It is not a general quantum-resource optimality result and does not incorporate resetting implementation costs.
