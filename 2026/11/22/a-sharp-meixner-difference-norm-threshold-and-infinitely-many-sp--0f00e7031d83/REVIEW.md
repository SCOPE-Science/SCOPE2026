# Review: Meixner difference-norm threshold

**Correctness — PASS.** The weight conjugation produces an explicitly specified positive-offdiagonal Jacobi matrix. A compact tail perturbation fixes the essential interval. For \(\beta\ge1\), the shift triangle bound agrees with its upper essential edge. For \(0<\beta<1\), the sine-block Rayleigh lower bound has a positive \(K^{-1}\) contribution dominating an \(O(K^{-3/2})\) finite-block deficit; infinitely many orthogonal separated blocks establish infinitely many genuine discrete eigenvalues. An irreducible second-order recurrence makes them simple. The analytic generating-function argument proves polynomial density without assuming completeness of a special-function system. Strict parameter monotonicity follows from a strictly positive top eigenvector and entrywise coefficient ordering. No finite experiment substitutes for these arguments.

**Originality — PASS (with a historical-priority qualification).** The primary full text is Dimitrov–Nikolov, arXiv:2007.04061, especially Theorems 1.1, 1.3 and 4.1, Sections 5–7. It proves the \(\beta=1\) limiting norm, finite-degree monotonicity, general upper bounds, and finite Jacobi reductions, but not the stated strict/flat limit phase or infinite outlier family for every \(\beta<1\). Sasaki, arXiv:2310.04968, concerns Meixner birth-and-death eigenpolynomials, not the spectral norm of this forward-difference operator. General weighted-shift background concerns the shift spectrum, which does not directly imply the bound-state count of \((T-I)^*(T-I)\). Database and literature searches found no exact equivalent; these failures do not establish uniqueness. The polynomial-density lemma may already be classical and is not the novelty claim.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The source paper leaves the general limiting sharp constants bounded but unclassified, with an exact answer only at one shape parameter. The result establishes a natural sharp parameter boundary for the very same inequality and a stronger dichotomy between an essential spectral edge and an infinite discrete ladder. The plateau despite strict finite-degree monotonicity is not a mere relabeling or one-degree numerical computation. It gives a structural explanation for why shape parameters below one differ from those above one.

**Residual limitations.** A specialist spectral-theory result on Jacobi matrices with Coulomb-order tails could subsume the outlier phenomenon; an exhaustive historical priority determination is not claimed. Eigenvalue asymptotics and exact subcritical norms remain unproved. Independent reproduction is not supplied.
