At Fibonacci cutoffs with digit length \(8t+7\), the Fibonacci–Thue–Morse parity signs have exact modulo-three biases \(((2q_t+b_t)/3,(b_t-q_t)/3,(b_t-q_t)/3)\), where \(q_0=3\), \(q_1=23\), \(q_{t+2}=8q_{t+1}-q_t\) and \(b_t\) is bounded and periodic. A persistent surplus on multiples of three grows with the sharp Fibonacci-cutoff exponent \(\log(4+\sqrt{15})/(8\log\varphi)>1/2\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), even though the global signed sum stays within one of zero.
