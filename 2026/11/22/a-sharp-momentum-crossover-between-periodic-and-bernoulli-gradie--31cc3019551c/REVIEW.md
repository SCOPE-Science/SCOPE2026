# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The final claim has two exact, noninterchangeable convergence notions: geometric deterministic convergence for a periodic orbit and convergence in mean square for independent Bernoulli delivery. The one-period companion matrix has explicit trace and determinant; the strict Jury inequalities prove both periodic boundaries. The iid covariance map is exact for all deterministic initial pairs; its strictly dissipative positive-definite quadratic form proves sufficiency, and the nondecaying covariance eigenmode proves necessity, including boundary exclusion. The difference of the two ceilings factors by one strictly monotone crossover polynomial. Bernoulli's inequality and a logarithmic calculation establish strict monotonicity in the delivery period and the boundary-layer asymptotic. Algebraic identities and supplementary finite instances were checked by the supplied standalone code. Risks: no claim of equal pathwise and mean-square convergence notions, and no extension beyond fixed scalar coefficients.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations

Exact iid Bernoulli heavy-ball mean-square ceilings are already covered by prior scalar stochastic-two-step work and by the published *A three-objective conflict for Bernoulli-erased heavy-ball optimization*. Exact two-component periodic heavy-ball matrix calculations are also anticipated by *A sharp disconnected stability diagram for cyclic two-component heavy-ball descent*. Neither previously published statement inspected gives the **matched-frequency sharp crossover** of these two stability ceilings as a function of momentum for each integer delivery period, nor its strictly increasing root and logarithmic long-period asymptotic. Bollapragada, Chen and Ward (arXiv:2206.07553) analyze quadratic minibatch momentum with independent sampling and concentration, while Qiu and Milzarek (arXiv:2404.18452, original 2024 version) analyze reshuffling momentum through asymptotic convergence and complexity. Tran, Tran-Dinh and Nguyen (arXiv:2403.03180) analyze an epoch-anchored shuffling momentum algorithm different from the raw heavy-ball recursion here. The standard stability methods, iid criterion, and simple two-period special case are not asserted new. Residual risk: a classical periodically switched random-coefficient recurrence analysis, or another unlocated comparison of deterministic versus iid gradient delivery, may subsume the result.

## Value — PASS, with limitations

The frequency of gradient acquisition can be a binding resource constraint. The final theorem demonstrates a strict design reversal: independent random delivery tolerates a larger active gradient coefficient at small positive momentum, but periodic guaranteed delivery tolerates a larger one beyond an exact unique threshold. This is a meaningful exact schedule-correlation boundary for every finite delivery period, rather than an arbitrary parameter-table entry; the monotone and logarithmic period laws explain how much momentum is needed as delivery becomes sparse. It does **not** show that either design minimizes computation, iteration count, risk, or convergence time, and it does not optimize over every possible correlated delivery law. A sharp ceiling comparison is a robustness statement rather than a universal recommendation of the largest allowed stepsize.
