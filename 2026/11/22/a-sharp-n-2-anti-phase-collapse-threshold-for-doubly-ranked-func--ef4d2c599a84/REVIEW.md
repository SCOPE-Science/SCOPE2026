# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact simultaneous collapse of the arithmetic and logit summaries follows from complementarity and strict monotonicity; it holds at each finite sample without asymptotics. Each pair's reversal probability has first-order coefficient \(2\sigma\int pq/\sqrt\pi\). A fixed-graph factorial-moment argument excludes overlapping events at scale \(N^{-2}\); a second-moment bound covers the supercritical regime. The normal-mixture coefficient is checked algebraically and the packaged standard-library checker passes 457 assertions. Limits require bounded continuous fixed densities and vanishing Gaussian noise, which are stated explicitly.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The closest source on doubly ranked functional data provides the two scores but not their exact anti-phase collapse or noise phase diagram. The noisy-permutation paper proves fixed-dimension small-noise asymptotics and an exact general probability expression, but no simultaneous \(N\to\infty\), \(N^2\sigma\to c\) limit for both functional scores. Classical Poisson U-statistic results cover the abstract rare-pair mechanism; this is acknowledged rather than presented as original. The contribution is the coupled score-collapse identity, sharp three-regime threshold, explicit separated-Gaussian constant, and resulting nonconsistency upper bound. Searches found no directly covering result, not a proof of global novelty.

**Value — PASS.** Summing pointwise ranks is a natural reduction for functional comparisons, and anti-phase trajectories are a directly motivated boundary where group differences change sign over time. The quantified crossover says exactly how measurement noise restores unequal summaries, including a persistent all-tied atom under fixed, strongly different Gaussian groups. This informs the choice of time weighting and tie handling. The claim does not assert improvements to tests using other preprocessing or alternative score aggregation.

**Closest literature and unresolved priority risk.** Meyer (2023), arXiv:2306.14761, Section 3, gives both scoring transformations; Jeong–Dytso–Cardone (2021), arXiv:2105.03015, studies a related noisy ordering and its sequential small-noise limit. General Poisson U-statistic theorems by Silverman–Brown and Decreusefond–Schulte–Thäle encompass much of the limit-method toolkit. Other unpublished applications of marked Poisson close-pair theory to doubly ranked tests may exist. The result is unvalidated independently.
