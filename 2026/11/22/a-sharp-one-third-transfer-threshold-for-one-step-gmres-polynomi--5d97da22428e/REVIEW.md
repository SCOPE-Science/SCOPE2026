# Review: triangular-path one-step GMRES polynomial transfer

## Correctness — PASS

The claim has three quantifier levels: an exact finite-dimensional all-training/all-deployment equivalence, two explicit small-dimensional boundaries, and a uniform-in-dimension threshold. For any invertible triangular factor, the fitted coefficient is a Rayleigh quotient of the symmetrized inverse evaluated on the transformed training vector. The test residual is exactly \(\alpha(\alpha-2\beta)\|Tc\|_2^2\) above its initial squared norm. Since both quotient extrema are attained independently, the condition \(M\le2m\) is necessary and sufficient including equality. The derived symmetric inverse is half the identity plus the Kac–Murdock–Szegő matrix, and its Toeplitz symbol yields a strict finite-dimensional spectral bracket; the constant and alternating Rayleigh quotients attain the two symbol endpoints in the large-dimension limit. Thus the one-third threshold is not extrapolated from finite experiments. The explicit spectra in sizes two and three establish their larger exact thresholds, and a rational two-site witness proves actual amplification. The delivered verifier passed 961 finite symbolic, exact-rational and numerical assertions. No floating-point experiment is used to prove the infinite-dimensional statement.

## Originality — PASS, with recorded priority risks

The full accessible Dargaville–Pain manuscript (arXiv:2611.00552v1), especially its GMRES-polynomial Section 5 and its method comparisons, defines construction from a single random training vector and reuse but supplies no all-training/all-deployment threshold on uniform bidiagonal factors. Embree–Henningsen–Jackson–Morgan (arXiv:2502.18317), especially Sections 2 and 4 and Theorem 4.1, examine cross-right-hand-side polynomial approximations and give bounds requiring diagonalizability; a nontrivial Jordan triangular path is defective, so this result is neither their theorem nor a direct corollary. Classical Kac–Murdock–Szegő eigenvalue theory implies the symbol bounds used in the proof and is credited, not represented as new. The published weighted three-site GMRES stagnation result studies two iterations fitted on the *same* right-hand side, a different quantifier and algorithm from transfer of a fixed one-step polynomial. Searches did not locate the precise combined threshold, but a search does not establish priority. The full earlier Joubert analysis was not accessible for a statement-level comparison; its available abstract concerns an adaptive hybrid preconditioner, a different setup, but remains a nondecisive literature risk. An equivalent treatment in unindexed older GMRES and Toeplitz literature remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The object is not an arbitrary numerical slice: a single training right-hand side followed by fixed-polynomial reuse is the explicitly proposed approach to parallel triangular ILU solves, and the constant-subdiagonal path is the minimal family that admits unbounded chain length and exact spectral analysis. The dimension-uniform one-third threshold answers a practical safety question: can the frozen polynomial avoid making *any* new right-hand side's residual worse? The answer is sharp, distinguishes all-pairs robustness from individually successful GMRES fitting, and reveals that the two- and three-site safe thresholds are misleading for large systems. Although the polynomial has only degree zero, the infinite-family classification and precise quantifier reversal provide structural information that cannot be read off a single GMRES training residual. No performance guarantee for high-degree polynomials or real hardware is inferred.

## Scientific limits and priority risks

The proof covers exact Euclidean residual norms and one trained scalar coefficient on a constant-weight bidiagonal path. It does not address high-degree polynomial preconditioners, arbitrary sparse triangular factors, roundoff or repeated-iteration rates. Classical Toeplitz spectral theory, elementary Rayleigh-quotient arguments and known diagonalizable transfer bounds are used or compared with explicit attribution. No exhaustive novelty claim is made beyond the inspected primary sources and available records.
