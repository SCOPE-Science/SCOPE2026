# A sharp order-six transition for fourth-norm Fourier synthesis on four-point progressions

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite abelian group of order \(N\), with character group \(\widehat G\) and the unitary Fourier transform
\[
\widehat f(\chi)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\overline{\chi(x)}.
\]
Let \(d\in G\) have order \(m\ge4\), fix \(x\in G\), and set \(S=\{x,x+d,x+2d,x+3d\}\). All Fourier norms below use counting measure. Define the exact fourth-norm synthesis constant
\[
\mathcal C_4(S)=\sup_{0\ne f,\ \operatorname{supp}f\subseteq S}\frac{\|\widehat f\|_{\ell^\infty(\widehat G)}}{\|\widehat f\|_{\ell^4(\widehat G)}}.
\]
Write \(b=\frac12-t\), and define the four real quartic polynomials
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_4(t)&=32t^4-32t^3+12t^2-2t+\tfrac38,\\
P_5(t)&=20t^4-20t^3+9t^2-2t+\tfrac38,\\
P_6(t)&=30t^4-24t^3+9t^2-2t+\tfrac38,\\
P_{\ge7}(t)&=28t^4-24t^3+9t^2-2t+\tfrac38.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(m\ge7\), interpret \(P_m=P_{\ge7}\). The exact result is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_4(S)=\bigl(NP_m(t_m)\bigr)^{-1/4}},
\]
where \(t_4=t_5=\frac14\), \(t_6\) is the unique real root of
\[
60t^3-36t^2+9t-1=0,
\]
and, for every \(m\ge7\), \(t_m=t_\infty\) is the unique real root of
\[
56t^3-36t^2+9t-1=0.
\]
The two nontrivial roots lie strictly between \(\frac14\) and \(\frac12\). Numerically, \(t_6=0.274888829595677\ldots\) and \(t_\infty=0.310460416962326\ldots\).

All nonzero equality cases, with no omissions, are precisely
\[
f(x+jd)=\lambda\chi_0(x+jd)w_j\quad(0\le j\le3),\qquad
(w_0,w_1,w_2,w_3)=(t_m,\tfrac12-t_m,\tfrac12-t_m,t_m),
\]
with \(f=0\) outside \(S\), \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\), and \(\chi_0\in\widehat G\). All four optimal weights are strictly positive. At orders four and five, the normalized indicator \(\frac14\mathbf1_S\) is optimal. Beginning **exactly at order six**, equal weights cease to be optimal, and the unique normalized optimizer assigns strictly greater weight to the two endpoints. The small-order constants are \(\mathcal C_4(S)=(4/N)^{1/4}\) for \(m=4\) and \(\mathcal C_4(S)=(64/(13N))^{1/4}\) for \(m=5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The group is finite abelian; no cyclicity of \(G\) is assumed. The four support points are distinct because \(m\ge4\). They need not generate \(G\). The order \(m\) refers to \(d\), not to the ambient group. The constant measures the full class of complex-valued functions supported in \(S\), including functions vanishing at one or more sites; no positivity or reflection symmetry is imposed in the optimization. The equality statement allows modulation by every character and nonzero complex scaling. Counts and powers refer to \(N=|G|\), not to a chosen cyclic presentation.

## Proof
**1. Reduce the sharp ratio to a unique convex optimization.** Select \(\chi_0\in\widehat G\) at which \(|\widehat f|\) is largest and modulate by \(\overline{\chi_0}\). A nonzero scalar then makes the resulting function \(h\) satisfy \(\sum_{z\in S}h(z)=1\). Modulation permutes Fourier frequencies and hence preserves both norms; scaling does not change their ratio. For unnormalized convolution \((h*h)(z)=\sum_yh(y)h(z-y)\), Fourier orthogonality gives
\[
\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}|\widehat h(\chi)|^4=\frac1N\sum_{z\in G}|(h*h)(z)|^2.
\]
Since \(|\widehat h(1)|=N^{-1/2}\), the fourth power of the ratio is \(1/(N\Phi(h))\), where \(\Phi(h)=\sum_z|(h*h)(z)|^2\). Equivalently \(\Phi(h)=N\sum_{\chi}|\widehat h(\chi)|^4\), which is a strictly convex function of the complex coefficient vector, considered as a real vector. Indeed \(|u|^4\) is strictly convex in \(u\in\mathbb C\) and the Fourier transform is injective. On the nonempty affine hyperplane \(\sum h=1\), \(\Phi\) is coercive and therefore has one and only one minimizer.

**2. Force reflection and reality, without assuming them.** Conjugating coefficients preserves both \(\sum h\) and \(\Phi(h)\). So does the affine reflection \(z\mapsto 2x+3d-z\), which reverses the four sites and preserves Fourier absolute values. Uniqueness implies the minimizer equals its conjugate and its reflection. Consequently its values at the four sites are \((t,b,b,t)\) with real \(t\) and \(b=\frac12-t\). This argument also proves that asymmetric complex coefficients or lower-support competitors cannot have better ratios.

**3. Exact convolution arithmetic and all torsion cases.** Form the ordinary square of the coefficient polynomial \(t+bz+bz^2+tz^3\). Before reduction modulo \(z^m-1\), its seven coefficients, from degrees zero through six, are
\[
(t^2,\ 2tb,\ b^2+2tb,\ 2(t^2+b^2),\ b^2+2tb,\ 2tb,\ t^2).
\]
Because the support lies in the cyclic subgroup generated by \(d\), its convolution coefficients are precisely these coefficients aggregated by residue modulo \(m\). Their squared sum equals \(P_m(t)\). For \(m\ge7\) no aggregation occurs. At \(m=6\), the degree-six coefficient merges with degree zero; at \(m=5\), degrees five and six merge with zero and one; at \(m=4\), degrees four, five and six merge with zero, one and two. Expanding the resulting squared sums gives exactly the four displayed quartics, with no additional cases.

**4. Minimize each quartic globally.** Differentiation yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
P'_4(t)&=2(4t-1)^3,\\
P'_5(t)&=2(4t-1)(10t^2-5t+1),\\
P'_6(t)&=2(60t^3-36t^2+9t-1),\\
P'_{\ge7}(t)&=2(56t^3-36t^2+9t-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The quadratic in \(P'_5\) has negative discriminant and positive leading coefficient. The derivatives of the cubics in \(P'_6/2\) and \(P'_{\ge7}/2\) are, respectively, \(180t^2-72t+9\) and \(168t^2-72t+9\); each is positive on the entire real line because its discriminant is negative and its leading coefficient is positive. Each cubic therefore has one real root, at which the corresponding coercive quartic achieves its unique global minimum. The cubic is negative at \(t=\frac14\) and positive at \(t=\frac12\). The first two quartics have unique minima at \(\frac14\), where \(P_4=\frac14\) and \(P_5=\frac{13}{64}\). In the last two cases \(t_m>\frac14\), \(b>0\) and \(P_m(t_m)<P_m(\frac14)\) strictly.

**5. Equality.** Strict convexity shows that after normalization and modulation the minimizer must equal the unique real symmetric vector found above. Conversely, that vector has all coefficients positive and sum one, so the trivial Fourier character is a maximizing character by the triangle inequality; its ratio attains the claimed constant. Undoing the modulation and scaling yields exactly all equality cases. This also proves validity for every finite abelian ambient group, not just a cyclic group.

## Verification
Run \(\texttt{python3 artifacts/verify.py}\) from the directory containing this document. The standard-library script independently constructs the convolution polynomials using exact rational arithmetic for periods four through nineteen, compares them to the four claimed quartics, checks the torsion-threshold derivatives exactly, and tests the roots and inequality witnesses with independent direct finite Fourier transforms. Its finite random tests are supplementary stress tests, **not** substitutes for the global strict-convexity and cubic-monotonicity proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Iosevich, Li and Yu's 2026 spectral synthesis study defines exactly the ratio \(\mathcal C_r(S)\), proves general bounds, and gives the sharp value from the indicator for supports admitting a transitive affine symmetry group (Theorem 4.1). Its Section 9 explicitly raises the need for information about supports with little affine symmetry, where the indicator need not be extremal. The present claim determines \(\mathcal C_4\) and every extremizer for the particular nontransitive four-point progression when the difference has order at least six; the older affine-transitivity theorem does not supply those constants or the strict departure from the indicator. Its small-order cases are included as natural boundary values, not claimed as a new consequence of the affine theorem.

Bhowmik, Deodhar and Iosevich's earlier synthesis paper gives support-size and support-indicator estimates, but these are upper bounds and do not establish the exact nonuniform four-point optimum. The published exact four-character Fourier projection calculation concerns the \(\ell^1/\ell^\infty\) endpoint norm of an idempotent convolution operator, not the \(\ell^\infty/\ell^4\) norm of **arbitrarily weighted supported functions**. The published sharp \(\Lambda(4)\) calculations concern maximization of \(\|\widehat f\|_4/\|f\|_2\), whereas this theorem minimizes \(\|\widehat f\|_4\) at fixed point evaluation; neither optimization implies the other. Related sixth-moment calculations likewise fix a different exponent and normalization.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite-support theorem, not a general classification of four-element supports or of arbitrary \(\ell^r\) exponents. The numerical improvement in the ratio over the normalized indicator is about \(0.2257\%\) when \(m=6\) and about \(1.2565\%\) when \(m\ge7\); its significance lies principally in the sharp change of equality geometry, not in a large numerical gain. The proof does not claim that all sets without transitive affine symmetry have nonuniform optimizers. Older low-degree Nikolskii, Christoffel-function or Banach-space tables could conceivably contain an equivalent continuous four-frequency extremal problem under different notation; bibliographic priority is not certified.
