# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The unitary finite-group Fourier transform obeys \(\sum_\chi|\widehat h(\chi)|^4=N^{-1}\sum_z|(h*h)(z)|^2\). Modulation and complex scaling reduce the ratio to minimization under the single linear constraint \(\sum h=1\). The resulting fourth-power Fourier energy is strictly convex and coercive as a function of four complex variables; hence the minimizer is unique. Conjugation and affine reflection force a real palindromic coefficient profile. Polynomial convolution, exact aggregation modulo the step order and rational expansion give four quartics, whose derivatives admit unique global minima as stated. Every equality case follows from uniqueness and modulation. The included reproducibility code independently checked the 16 period-polynomial identities, critical-root inequalities, 14 finite-group cases and 720 randomized complex inequalities (774 assertions). These finite checks supplement, rather than certify, the infinite quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources
Iosevich–Li–Yu (arXiv:2609.25404, Theorem 4.1 and Section 9) establish the affine-transitive indicator extremum and discuss the less symmetric case; that theorem does not imply the explicit endpoint-heavy order-six optimizer. Bhowmik–Deodhar–Iosevich (arXiv:2508.12194) give general synthesis bounds rather than this sharp four-site law. Previously published four-frequency projection formulas optimize a different endpoint convolution operator, and \(\Lambda(4)\) results optimize the reverse direction, \(L^4/L^2\). The older even-moment symmetrization result is useful context but gives no minimization at fixed Fourier evaluation. An equivalent classical low-degree Nikolskii or Christoffel formula remains a nonzero residual risk; no claim of exhaustive priority is made.

## Value — PASS
The new result completely solves a natural finite Fourier synthesis ratio in the first four-point progression family where transitive symmetry no longer forces the indicator, and identifies the exact arithmetic threshold separating uniform from nonuniform optimal weights. The improvement in the numerical constant is small, but the rigorously proved change in optimizer geometry, its uniqueness and stabilization at order seven answer a specifically motivated question about nontransitive support, rather than a routine bound tightening. The result includes all finite abelian ambient groups and all coefficient phases.
