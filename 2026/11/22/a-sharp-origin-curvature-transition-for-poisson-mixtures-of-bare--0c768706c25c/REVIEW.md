# Review of the origin-curvature theorem

**Correctness: PASS.** The normalized packet and reset-age integral are exact. The core proof is the moving-age scaled integral, the sharp \(u^{-3a}\) tail, a beta integral in the integrable regime, the logarithmic exponential integral at \(\sigma=1\), and dominated convergence for \(\sigma>1\). All coefficients are positive; the finite classical origin curvature is given explicitly. The numerical checker reproduces normalization and representative asymptotics. The universal statements rely on the analytic proof, not on sampling.

**Originality: PASS (best-knowledge, not independently certified).** Chełminiak (2021) establishes the exact renewal mixture and separately evaluates \(\sigma=1,2\), but does not state the all-parameter origin curvature threshold. The \(\sigma=1\) logarithm is implicit in its exact special function, so that individual case is prior-implied. Lenzi et al. (2023) instead solve a nonlinear stationary source-PDE and do not dominate this different renewal-mixture statement. Earlier global-moment formulas cannot imply pointwise curvature, and available searches found no directly covering whole-domain classification. Residual priority risk: a more general asymptotic analysis could subsume the result.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The reset point is singled out naturally by the protocol, making central differentiability and curvature physically motivated observables. The classification supplies a sharp nonlinearity threshold and a distinct logarithmic boundary, separating three kinds of contact regularity. It does not conflate nonlinear source dynamics with a renewal mixture.

**Limits.** Only the one-dimensional, independent exponential-age mixture is covered. The theorem does not establish dynamics of the nonlinear source PDE, all higher derivative thresholds, or experimental validity. Independent scientific validation remains outstanding.
