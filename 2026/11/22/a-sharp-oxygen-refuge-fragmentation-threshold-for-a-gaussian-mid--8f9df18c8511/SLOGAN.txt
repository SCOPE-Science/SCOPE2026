For a finite water column with a Gaussian oxygen deficit and exponential thermal compensation, the physiological viability profile can split into two positive-width depth bands if and only if the deficit exceeds an explicit sharp threshold determined by the thermal gradient and the oxygen-minimum depth. The metabolic bottleneck then lies shallower than the oxygen minimum; at interior onset, the gap's viability-score range opens like \(\varepsilon^{3/2}\) with an exact coefficient.
