# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The fixed-budget mixing matrix reduces by positive diagonal similarity to an affine symmetric matrix. The all-size minimum follows from a Rayleigh quotient equal to one, with strictness from the failure of the population-share square-root vector to be an eigenvector when shares are unequal. Convexity of the largest eigenvalue gives strict one-sided order. A two-dimensional Krylov compression produces the stated explicit global lower bound, with equality characterized by a quadratic-versus-linear interpolation obstruction at three distinct shares. Exact two-community rational and radical calculations prove the subcritical-to-supercritical comparisons. The packaged checker reproducibly evaluates 7,616 finite tests; it is not the proof of the universal statements.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual historical risk

Gupta–Landry (2026) presents the two-community heterogeneous-mean-field construction and observes changes in its reproduction number as community sizes and contact strength change. The present theorem does not claim those source observations. Its original proposed content is the all-finite-community sharp moment envelope and exact equality class, together with the associated global random-mixing optimum and curvature. Landry–Restrepo (2023) defines the two-community total-edge-budget normalization for an opinion/hypergraph SIS setting, while Hill–Glasser–Feng (2023) treats positive preferential mixing with each group's activity fixed; neither covers the exact envelope or equality rigidity on the present budgeted planted-partition family. Search of published research records found related commuter-optimization and reciprocal-contact-class threshold results but not a covering equivalence. A general unindexed theorem for largest eigenvalues of affine diagonal-plus-rank-one matrices could overlap; this remains a genuine historical-priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The sharp formula uses only the second through fourth moments of community shares, despite arbitrarily many communities and group-level asymmetries. Its exact equality class clarifies when that low-dimensional information suffices to recover the full invasion threshold, rather than merely approximate it. The result isolates the risk created by population-size imbalance alone, without differences in biological infection products or increases in total contact volume. The exact two-sided invasion example shows that replacing some cross-community contacts by within-community contacts, and doing the opposite, can both destabilize a disease-free state relative to neutral mixing. This directly informs interpretation of fixed-budget mixing interventions. The model's predictive relevance is limited to mean-field invasion and does not establish empirical epidemic outcomes.
