# A sharp real-to-log-periodic relaxation crossover in a history-reset dephasing qubit

## Finding

A driven, dephasing qubit with uniform-history resetting has two algebraically decaying polarization sectors. The slowest sector switches exactly once between a nonoscillatory polarization and a log-periodically oscillatory pair of polarizations if and only if the coherent drive exceeds a sharp multiple of the dephasing strength.

Precisely, put \(H=(\omega/2)\sigma_x\), let the Lindblad generator be
\[
\mathcal L(\rho)=-i[H,\rho]+\kappa(\sigma_z\rho\sigma_z-\rho),
\qquad \kappa>0,\quad \omega>\kappa,
\]
and introduce a uniform-history resetting rate \(q>0\):
\[
\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}=\mathcal L(\rho(t))-q\rho(t)+\frac q t\int_0^t\rho(s)\,ds,
\qquad \rho(0)=\tfrac12(I+x_0\sigma_x+y_0\sigma_y+z_0\sigma_z).
\]
Assume the physical Bloch vector satisfies \(0<x_0^2+y_0^2+z_0^2\le1\), with \(x_0\ne0\) and \((y_0,z_0)\ne(0,0)\), so both sectors are observable. Define
\[
\beta_x(q)=\frac{2\kappa}{q+2\kappa},\qquad
\beta_{yz}(q)=\frac{\kappa q+\omega^2}{q^2+2\kappa q+\omega^2},\qquad
\nu(q)=\frac{q\sqrt{\omega^2-\kappa^2}}{q^2+2\kappa q+\omega^2}>0.
\]
For every \(q>0\), the transverse \(x\) polarization is a nonzero constant times \(t^{-\beta_x(q)}(1+O(t^{-1}))\). The \((y,z)\)-polarization is \(t^{-\beta_{yz}(q)}\) times a nonzero real vector-valued sinusoid of \(\nu(q)\log t\), up to a relative \(O(t^{-1})\) correction. The latter is genuinely log-periodic for every nonzero initial \((y,z)\) vector.

If \(\kappa<\omega\le\sqrt2\,\kappa\), the oscillatory sector is strictly slower than the nonoscillatory one for every \(q>0\). If \(\omega>\sqrt2\,\kappa\), there is exactly one switching rate
\[
\boxed{q_c=\frac{\omega^2}{\kappa}-2\kappa>0}.
\]
For \(0<q<q_c\), the nonoscillatory sector strictly dominates the leading asymptotic deviation from the maximally mixed state; for \(q>q_c\), the log-periodic sector strictly dominates; at \(q=q_c\), both contribute at exactly the same algebraic order. No other positive switching rates exist. The transition concerns the dominant *asymptotic polarization sector*, not a change in the stationary state, and is valid only for strictly positive resetting rates.

For instance, when \(\kappa=1\) and \(\omega=2\), the unique threshold is \(q_c=2\), the common exponent at the threshold is \(1/2\), and the oscillatory log-angular frequency there is \(\sqrt3/6\).

## Assumptions and scope

Time is continuous and the Pauli matrices have eigenvalues \(\pm1\). We take \(\hbar=1\). Between resets, the density matrix follows the time-homogeneous, completely positive, trace-preserving Lindblad semigroup generated by \(\mathcal L\). At each point of an independent Poisson clock of rate \(q\), the system is returned to its state at a past time selected uniformly from \([0,t]\), with the entire history retained. The equation above defines the resulting ensemble-averaged density matrix. It has a unique continuous trace-one solution. Positive semidefiniteness follows from the causal mild equation: each iteration combines completely positive Lindblad evolution with a positive temporal average, and the trace weights sum to one.

The theorem covers all \(\kappa>0\), all \(\omega>\kappa\) and all \(q>0\). The initial Bloch vector may be pure or mixed but must have nonzero projections onto both spectral sectors for the asserted observable dominance switch. If one sector is absent initially, it remains absent and its exponent still exists formally but cannot dominate the state. The overdamped and exceptional-point regimes \(\omega\le\kappa\), nonuniform memory, and nonlinear or interacting open systems are outside the claim. The boundary \(\omega=\sqrt2\,\kappa\) is included in the no-positive-crossover regime.

## Proof

**1. Bloch generator.** Writing \(\rho=\tfrac12(I+x\sigma_x+y\sigma_y+z\sigma_z)\) gives the real matrix system
\[
\dot{\mathbf b}=B\mathbf b,\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}-2\kappa&0&0\\0&-2\kappa&-\omega\\0&\omega&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its spectrum is the simple real eigenvalue \(\lambda_x=-2\kappa\) and the conjugate pair \(\lambda_\pm=-\kappa\pm i\Omega\), where \(\Omega=\sqrt{\omega^2-\kappa^2}>0\). The real eigenline is precisely the \(x\)-axis, and the conjugate eigenpair spans the real \((y,z)\)-plane. The unique stationary Bloch vector is zero.

**2. Scalar uniform-history response.** For any eigenvalue \(\lambda\), the component starting at one satisfies
\[
f_\lambda'(t)=(\lambda-q)f_\lambda(t)+\frac q t\int_0^t f_\lambda(s)\,ds,\qquad f_\lambda(0)=1.
\]
Taking the derivative after multiplying by \(t\), one obtains
\[
t f_\lambda''+[1+(q-\lambda)t]f_\lambda'-\lambda f_\lambda=0.
\]
With \(Z=(q-\lambda)t\) and \(f=e^{-Z}g\), this is Kummer's equation \(Zg''+(1-Z)g'-ag=0\) with \(a=q/(q-\lambda)\). The unique solution regular at zero is
\[
f_\lambda(t)=e^{-(q-\lambda)t}\,{}_1F_1\!\left(\frac q{q-\lambda};1;(q-\lambda)t\right).
\]
For all three eigenvalues, \(\Re(q-\lambda)>0\), \(\Re(a)>0\), and \(\Gamma(a)\) is finite and nonzero. Standard Kummer asymptotics in the open right half-plane therefore yield
\[
f_\lambda(t)=C_\lambda t^{\lambda/(q-\lambda)}(1+O(t^{-1})),\qquad
C_\lambda=\frac{(q-\lambda)^{\lambda/(q-\lambda)}}{\Gamma(q/(q-\lambda))}\ne0,
\]
where complex powers use the principal logarithm. The exponentially small secondary term does not affect this leading law.

**3. Real and complex exponents.** For \(\lambda_x=-2\kappa\), the exponent \(\lambda_x/(q-\lambda_x)=-\beta_x(q)\) is real, and its coefficient is strictly positive. For \(\lambda_+=-\kappa+i\Omega\), direct multiplication gives
\[
\frac{\lambda_+}{q-\lambda_+}=-\frac{\kappa q+\omega^2}{q^2+2\kappa q+\omega^2}
+i\frac{q\Omega}{q^2+2\kappa q+\omega^2}
=-\beta_{yz}(q)+i\nu(q).
\]
The \(\lambda_-\) contribution is its conjugate. If \(P_+\) denotes the complex spectral projector of the \((y,z)\) block onto \(\lambda_+\), the leading real \((y,z)\) vector is
\[
2t^{-\beta_{yz}(q)}\operatorname{Re}\left(C_{\lambda_+}e^{i\nu(q)\log t}P_+\binom{y_0}{z_0}\right).
\]
Because the block has nonreal eigenvalues, no nonzero real initial vector has a zero \(P_+\) projection; the real and imaginary parts of its eigenvector are linearly independent. The leading vector-valued sinusoid is therefore nontrivial (indeed its norm is bounded away from zero over a logarithmic period).

**4. Exhaustive sign calculation.** Subtracting the two decay powers gives the exact identity
\[
\beta_{yz}(q)-\beta_x(q)=\frac{q(\omega^2-2\kappa^2-\kappa q)}{(q+2\kappa)(q^2+2\kappa q+\omega^2)}.
\]
The denominator is positive throughout the domain. If \(\omega^2\le2\kappa^2\), the numerator is strictly negative for every positive \(q\). If \(\omega^2>2\kappa^2\), the numerator changes sign exactly once, at the stated \(q_c\). Smaller exponent means slower algebraic decay. Since the only nonstationary modes are these three, this proves the complete phase diagram and all strict dominance assertions, including the equality case.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `check.py` checks the Bloch characteristic polynomial and the exact rational identities for both exponents and the logarithmic frequency using symbolic algebra. Independently, it integrates the original *real* six-variable Volterra moment system (Bloch vector plus historical mean) and compares the result to the spectral Kummer solution at 36 distinct parameter/time combinations (108 real components). It checks the strict dominance classification on a further grid. Every asserted check passed when executed from the packaged file contents.

These finite numerical comparisons verify transcription and normalization only; the all-parameter and long-time claims follow from the analytic argument in the Proof section, not from numerical sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

De Mauro, Kulkarni and Majumdar introduced uniform-history resetting for **closed** quantum systems and gave the scalar Kummer response for purely imaginary Bohr frequencies [1]. Their source paper's equations, gapped analysis and concluding outlook were inspected; it explicitly identifies open quantum systems as a future direction. The present result inserts a noncommuting Lindblad dephaser and identifies an explicit crossing between a real decay mode and a complex-conjugate mode. It credits the scalar-response method rather than claiming it as new.

Boyer, Evans and Majumdar [2] treated uniform and general preferential relocation for a confined diffusion with real nonnegative Fokker–Planck relaxation eigenvalues. The same separation-of-variables principle supports the analytic method here, but real Fokker–Planck modes cannot yield the competing coherent log-periodic tail. Rose, Touchette, Lesanovsky and Garrahan [3] studied fixed-state resetting of Markovian open quantum dynamics, whose shifted generator yields exponential decay rather than history-sampled algebraic relaxation. Carollo and Wald [4] studied discrete-time reset-related quantum non-Markovianity with different state-renewal rules. Neither these protocols nor the compared research-index results imply the specific positive-rate mode crossover and its all-parameter threshold.

## Limitations

This is an exact, self-reviewed two-level mathematical result, not an experimental claim or a theorem for arbitrary Lindbladians. It does not determine finite-time crossing times of polarization magnitudes, distinguishability-backflow properties, or any exception-point behavior. The order \(q\downarrow0\) and \(t\to\infty\) is singular: the theorem holds for every fixed \(q>0\) and does not assert the same dominant tail at \(q=0\). The asymptotic oscillations describe the averaged Bloch-vector observables, not individual stochastic quantum trajectories. The originality comparison remains vulnerable to an equivalent unindexed open-system history-reset calculation; independent external validation has not yet been performed.
