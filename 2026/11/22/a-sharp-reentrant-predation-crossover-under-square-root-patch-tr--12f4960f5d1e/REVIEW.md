# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The precise domain is \(m,K>0\), \(0\le d\le m\), and the fixed square-root redistribution weights. The closed form in \(t=\sqrt{m^2-d^2}\) follows from an algebraic identity, the critical polynomial is strictly decreasing in \(t\), and the factorization of \(F(d)-F(0)\) proves the exact crossover including endpoints. All cases \(m<K\), \(m=K\), \(m>K\) are separated. The broader exponent statement is limited to local sign and endpoint existence, not uniqueness. `checker.py` is reproduced by standard-library arithmetic and passed 12,081 supplementary assertions; this is diagnostic, not a replacement for proof.

**Originality: PASS (subject to historical priority).** A published aggregate-predation model by Nachman (2006) is the strongest close precursor: it incorporates spatial prey heterogeneity, predator aggregative response and Holling-type local feeding, and it motivates the weighted-response formulation. Its inspected main text and appendices do not present the exact two-deterministic-patch square-root reentrant sign change, the single crossover at \(\sqrt{m^2-K^2}\), or the unique interior minimum. A 2006 empirical counterpart and Hossie–Murray (2016) examine different population responses. Indexed mathematical findings on generalized Type III prey allocation and logistic-crowding competition optimize different models/objectives. The broad prior framework could yield the starting expression as a specialization, so unlocated earlier analyses of this specific specialization remain a residual priority risk.

**Value: PASS.** The problem isolates the opposing effects of within-patch saturating intake and sublinear predator tracking in a fixed-total-prey two-patch assay. The closed-form threshold predicts when small clumping and extreme clumping have opposite effects at the *same* mean prey density, identifies the exact minimum, and gives an experimental benchmarking variable \(m/K\). A non-square-root power-law local-to-extreme reversal establishes the mechanism is not restricted solely to one chosen exponent. No field applicability, optimal foraging behaviour or population-scale conservation benefit is claimed.

Scientific limitations include no prey depletion, no predator interference, perfect instantaneous redistribution, and the absence of a unique-crossover theorem for other power exponents. The general antecedent literature is acknowledged; absence of an explicit matching theorem in inspected materials is not a guarantee of historical priority.
