When predators track prey across two patches in proportion to the square root of local prey abundance, saturating feeding creates a sharp reentrant effect of prey clumping: at mean density above the half-saturation level, moderate clumping decreases per-predator intake, but extreme clumping increases it. The unique minimum and the single crossover with uniform prey density have exact formulas, and the reversal survives as an existence result for general sublinear power-law tracking.
