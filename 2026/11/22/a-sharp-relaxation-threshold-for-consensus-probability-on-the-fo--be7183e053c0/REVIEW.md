# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof exactly identifies the weighted-median response for two equal-weight neighbors as a clamp and then as a three-entry median. Adjacent low pairs are permanently separated by pairwise interval invariance. Opposite low pairs have a four-scalar affine recurrence for \(\gamma\le1/2\) with decreasing widths, a nonnegative shrinking interpair gap, and conserved sum of the two interface opinions. This proves convergence to the midpoint of the second and third initial order statistics. The six possible lowest-rank position pairs give the exact probability \(1/3\). A parameter-dependent first-step algebraic witness establishes a nonempty open polarizing region at every \(\gamma>1/2\), not merely at sampled parameter values. The executable verification is supplementary; the infinite statement rests on the displayed inequalities and recurrence.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk
Cheng and Mei, arXiv:2610.16406, Theorem 11, characterize universal convergence-to-consensus through disjoint type-dependent cohesive sets. Their result entails the existence of persistent dissensus from some initial conditions on the four-cycle, but does not determine probability under independent continuous initial opinions, the exact selected consensus opinion, or the sharp transition at \(\gamma=1/2\). The proof here is based on initial rank geometry and time-step overshoot, not simply a restatement of the cohesive-set condition. Mei et al., arXiv:2212.08808, study asynchronous weighted-median updates; their probability and convergence setting differs. An indexed publicly available record about directional median/averaging agents on rings has a different strictly direction-biased mechanism, but its full text was inaccessible. Additional unindexed or inaccessible literature remains a priority risk; this is not an independent certification of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The originating mixed-median model distinguishes universal consensus from agreement for individual initial conditions and points to dependence on adjustment rates. The result gives an exact probability and closed-form selection for a smallest simple ring exhibiting an adjustment-rate-sensitive consensus probability (the three-cycle always reaches consensus), and proves that making an otherwise identical simultaneous adjustment more aggressive than one-half causes a strict loss of consensus reliability. The parameter transition is not a change in the network, the number of people, or the local influence weights. It offers a mathematically precise boundary for a speed-versus-consensus design trade-off, within the stated toy model.

## Limits and closest literature
The exact probability above the threshold is not calculated; only strict inequality is proved. The theorem applies to one four-person ring, equal neighbor weights and homogeneous relaxation, with no empirical behavioral inference. The closest primary literature is Cheng–Mei (2026), Theorems 9 and 11, and Mei et al. (2022) on asynchronous weighted-median dynamics. The indexed directional-ring result could not be fully inspected; its model comparison is limited to its indexed summary.
