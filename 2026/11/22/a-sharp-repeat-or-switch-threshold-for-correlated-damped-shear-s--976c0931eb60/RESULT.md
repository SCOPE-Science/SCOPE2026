# A sharp repeat-or-switch threshold for correlated damped-shear splitting

## Finding
Consider the deterministic two-mode evolution generated by
\[
A=A_1+A_2,\qquad A_1=-aI+bE_{12},\qquad A_2=-aI-bE_{21},
\]
where \(a,b,h>0\), \(E_{ij}\) denotes the matrix unit, and \(h\) is a fixed time step. On every step use the exact, probability-corrected subflow \(M_i=\exp(2hA_i)\). Group consecutive steps into independent two-step blocks. Within each block choose \((I_1,I_2)\in\{1,2\}^2\) by **any** joint law with each coordinate uniform on \(\{1,2\}\); the selected pair is independent of the state entering the block. Write \(q=\mathbb P(I_1=I_2)\) and \(k=2bh\).

For every deterministic initial state \(v\in\mathbb R^2\), the expected squared Euclidean norm after one block is exactly
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\|M_{I_2}M_{I_1}v\|_2^2
=e^{-8ah}\left[1+2qk^2+\frac{1-q}{2}k^4\right]\|v\|_2^2.}
\]
The globally minimizing joint law is **unique** and always switches (\(q=0\)) if \(0<k<2\), and is **unique** and always repeats (\(q=1\)) if \(k>2\). At \(k=2\) every coupling has the same expected squared norm. The sharp optimal factor is
\[
\boxed{R_*(a,b,h)=e^{-8ah}\left[1+\min\left\{2k^2,\frac{k^4}{2}\right\}\right].}
\]
After \(N\ge0\) independent blocks, the exact mean-square energy equals \(R_q^N\|v\|_2^2\), with \(R_q\) the bracketed factor above including damping. Thus \(R_q<1\), \(R_q=1\), and \(R_q>1\) are respectively the **necessary and sufficient** strict mean-square decay, conservation, and growth regimes for every nonzero initial state. For any fixed \(k>0\) other than \(2\), a nonempty interval of positive damping strengths makes the optimal policy mean-square stable while the other endpoint policy is unstable.

## Assumptions and scope
The state space is exactly \(\mathbb R^2\), the norm is Euclidean, and \(a,b,h>0\) are constant. At each time step only one of the two subgenerators is evaluated, with reciprocal-probability scaling of two. The coupling is state-independent, has both one-step marginals exactly uniform, and is renewed independently between consecutive two-step blocks. No independence is required between the two choices inside a block. The deterministic sum generator \(A=-2aI+b(E_{12}-E_{21})\) has the exact decay \(\|e^{At}v\|_2=e^{-2at}\|v\|_2\). Each individual \(A_i\) is Hurwitz, but need not be Euclidean-dissipative. This is a stability-design theorem, not a consistency, accuracy, or optimal-control guarantee.

## Proof
**Parameterization of all admissible laws.** Uniform marginals force
\[
\mathbb P(1,1)=\mathbb P(2,2)=q/2,\qquad
\mathbb P(1,2)=\mathbb P(2,1)=(1-q)/2,
\]
with \(0\le q\le1\). Thus the displayed scalar parameter exhausts the feasible couplings; there are no hidden asymmetrical or non-Markovian choices within a block.

**Exact moment matrices.** Since \(E_{12}^2=E_{21}^2=0\), the exact subflows are
\[
M_1=e^{-2ah}U,\quad M_2=e^{-2ah}L,
\quad U=\begin{pmatrix}1&k\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-k&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives the *matrix identities*, valid for every real \(k\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(U^2)^\mathsf TU^2+(L^2)^\mathsf TL^2}{2}&=(1+2k^2)I,\\
\frac{(LU)^\mathsf T(LU)+(UL)^\mathsf T(UL)}{2}&=(1+k^4/2)I.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(LU\) and \(UL\) are the possible chronological products for switching. Their convex combination with weights \(q\) and \(1-q\), together with \(e^{-8ah}\), yields the claimed expectation for **every** vector, not merely on average over starting directions.

**Global optimality and uniqueness.** For fixed positive \(k\), the repeat-minus-switch undamped factor is
\[
(1+2k^2)-(1+k^4/2)=\frac{k^2(4-k^2)}2.
\]
Because the objective is affine in \(q\), it has a unique optimal endpoint whenever \(k\ne2\). At \(k=2\) the affine slope vanishes, so every law is optimal. The algebra is exhaustive because the preceding marginal constraints parameterize all laws.

**Exact infinite-block behavior.** The expectation of the squared norm conditional on any state entering a new block is the same scalar \(R_q\) times its squared norm. Block independence and the tower property give \(\mathbb E\|v_{2N}\|_2^2=R_q^N\|v\|_2^2\) for every integer \(N\ge0\). The strict and boundary stability statements follow immediately; no step from a finite computation to an infinite horizon is used.

**Both directions of stability separation.** If \(k=1\), the undamped repeat and switch factors are \(3\) and \(3/2\). Set \(h=1\), \(b=1/2\), \(a=1/8\). The repeat factor is \(3/e>1\) and the switch factor is \(3/(2e)<1\), using \(2<e<3\). If \(k=3\), set \(h=1\), \(b=3/2\), \(a=3/8\). The repeat factor is \(19/e^3<1\), whereas the switch factor is \(83/(2e^3)>1\). Indeed \(27/10<e<3\) gives \(e^3>19\) and \(e^3<27<83/2\). More generally, strict inequality between the two endpoint undamped factors means that the open interval between their logarithms divided by \(8h\) supplies positive dampings with precisely one stable policy. Both eigenvalues of either split generator remain \(-a\) throughout.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` performs 440 exact-rational checks, including complete enumeration of all pair outcomes and all sequences of up to three independent blocks for eight rational shear strengths, five exact coupling probabilities, and three initial vectors. It verifies the full matrix identities (not just traces), the finite-block scalar recursion, endpoint comparisons, and the rational inequalities used by the two stability witnesses. Run `python verify.py` in the directory containing this file and the script; its final line begins `VERIFICATION_OK`. Finite enumeration is a diagnostic; the exhaustive coupling parameterization and exact matrix algebra above establish the all-parameter and all-block assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Veldman and Zuazua [1] formulate reciprocal-probability one-component randomized time splitting, with independent component choices at each step, and prove finite-horizon approximation estimates. The new result keeps their two-component selection scheme but **optimizes within-block dependence**, rather than applying the independent-step estimates. A published exact one-step planar-shear benchmark [3] derives the independent-step expected-energy factor \(e^{-4ah}(1+2b^2h^2)\); in the present notation its square is the special coupling \(q=1/2\), not the optimal two-step dependence law.

Sturman and Thiffeault [2] study random products of positive and opposite-sign shear matrices and bound asymptotic Lyapunov exponents. Their negative-shear analysis already identifies the hyperbolicity boundary \(|\alpha\beta|=4\), coinciding numerically with \(k=2\) here. This hyperbolicity observation is not claimed as new, and does not establish the exact worst-initial-state **mean-square** objective or the globally optimal uniform-marginal coupling. A published four-step study [4] gives spectral stability thresholds for *deterministic* balanced words containing two copies of each shear; its fixed-multiplicity Floquet comparison neither covers repeated two-step random blocks nor the mean-square minimization here. The matrix-moment technique itself is standard and not claimed as new. A broader two-step switching result not identified in the inspected literature remains a historical-priority risk.

## Limitations
The exact selection classification applies only to this two-dimensional pair of opposite triangular shears, a common step length, exact subflows, uniform one-step marginals, and independence between blocks. It does not imply stability for every coupled schedule across blocks, minimize pathwise Lyapunov exponents, guarantee good approximation for large \(h\), or give a practical speedup. In the strongly sheared regime the optimal repeating policy omits one subgenerator within each two-step block, although the two subgenerators remain equally likely marginally. Critical equality \(R_q=1\) conserves **expected squared norm**, not individual trajectories. Previous literature on shears and switching is substantial; independent publication-priority and mathematical review have not been performed.
