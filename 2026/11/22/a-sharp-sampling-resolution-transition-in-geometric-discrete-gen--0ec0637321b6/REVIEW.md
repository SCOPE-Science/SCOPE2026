# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem uses the exact geometric-maximum survival formula, proves the continuous hazard has at most one sign change of derivative by a generalized-polynomial root bound, then proves integrated hazards cannot introduce extra reversals under sampling. The sharp resolution condition follows from the exact one-step sign identity and the fact that the derivative of the resolution comparison has one sign reversal, while its limit is log(alpha/theta). The complete parameter partitions, boundary tie and explicit example were checked. Numerical checks cover 378 parameter settings and 2,584 exact rational sign identities, but are not substitutes for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification.** The 2018 UGDGE paper gives the model and discrete hazard formulas. The 2015 continuous Marshall–Olkin GE paper already classifies continuous hazard phases, and this earlier theorem is explicitly treated as known. The exact discrete one-step discriminator, no-extra-reversal classification and unique p-dependent resolution transition are not stated by the inspected primary sources, nor were they located in searched mathematical research records and available published comparisons. The combination of existing continuous and flooring results is a significant overlap risk; the new implication requires the additional integrated-hazard and resolution-crossing arguments, rather than a direct restatement.

**Value — PASS.** The threshold answers a natural reliability question for discrete measurements: whether the decreasing-then-increasing failure pattern of a geometric random parallel lifetime system is observable after grouping time into integer bins. It proves that coarser resolution can erase an actual bathtub exactly in one parameter regime, with a unique calibration threshold and a direct finite expression for classification. This is a structural property of the full published model, not an arbitrary restricted subfamily.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Kundu–Nekoukhou (2018, arXiv:1802.06715) defines the UGDGE law; Ristić–Kundu (2015, DOI:10.1007/s40300-014-0056-x) covers the continuous hazard and is the closest strong prior. The result applies only to the independent geometric-maximum construction with positive interior parameters and integer-time sampling. It does not assert new parameter estimators, empirical power guarantees, or priority established beyond accessible sources.
