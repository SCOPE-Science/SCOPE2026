# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Independent Poisson counts yield exactly the product of the two zero-avoidance probabilities. The logarithm of this product has a strictly negative second derivative in the weighted seasonal index, giving a unique global optimum. At the upper phase boundary, a strictly increasing ratio of exponential increments has exactly one crossing if and only if \(\beta(1-u)>\alpha(1+u)\). The implicit derivative of the optimum is positive whenever interior; endpoint saturation and both asymptotic regimes follow from the same stationarity equation. The exact rational witness \(z^4-4z^3+3=0\) has a root in \((39/10,4)\), and the reproducibility script checks illustrative computations. Finite tests alone are not the proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

Closest inspected primary literature: Rounds et al. (2024), https://doi.org/10.1111/fwb.14320, reports seasonal species-specific detection and monitoring tradeoffs, but no exact two-taxon product-optimal concentration boundary; Moore and McCarthy (2016), https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12564, provides general Poisson survey-effort theory principally optimizing detection for one species across sites and travel costs, not the common-season joint-detection objective. Flitcroft et al. (2025), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-19083-7, was inspected through its complete 16-page USDA-hosted open-access PDF; it reports temporal differences, water-body-specific richness and replicated multi-species sampling, not this exact opposing-hazard Poisson optimization theorem. Search summaries of related Poisson detection and ecological survey results did not establish the specific necessary-and-sufficient phase law, and historical priority remains provisional. Lack of a matching search result is not a uniqueness or novelty proof.

## Value — PASS

The proof supplies a complete budget-dependent policy classification for the natural event of detecting both potentially invasive taxa under opposing phenologies, including a genuinely sharp dividing line between persistent compromise and eventual concentration, a monotone optimum and the no-benefit-of-splitting equivalence. It distinguishes objectives aimed at joint detection from identifying the most easily detectable taxon. It can guide controlled model comparisons but does not recommend real-world schedules without estimated and validated hazard functions.

## Scientific limits

Perfectly opposed deterministic hazards and independent Poisson counting are idealizations; no field calibration, correlated detectability, survey overhead, or occupancy uncertainty is covered. Related general search and optimization literature could contain a mathematically equivalent case not located in the inspected material; an independent priority and applicability assessment is outstanding.
