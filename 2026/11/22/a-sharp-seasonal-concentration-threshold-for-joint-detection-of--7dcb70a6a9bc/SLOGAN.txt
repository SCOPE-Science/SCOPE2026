For two aquatic taxa with opposing seasonal Poisson detection rates, maximizing the chance of detecting both gives one uniquely optimal effective season that moves monotonically toward the rarer taxon's peak as sampling effort grows. Entire-season concentration occurs at a finite budget exactly when \(\beta(1-u)>\alpha(1+u)\); otherwise the compromise remains strictly optimal for every finite budget, and no split-season plan can outperform an appropriately selected single season under the stated model.
