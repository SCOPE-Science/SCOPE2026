# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The equilibrium equation is reduced exactly to a horizontal-level problem for a positive ratio. Its derivative has a unique sign transition (or no transition below the sharp separation threshold), proving all equilibrium counts, folds, hyperbolicity and critical nonhyperbolic cases without numerical root counting. The rational five-equilibrium witness is proved by exact inequalities. The packaged verifier checks the identities and representative regimes independently; numerical diagnostics are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

The general five-equilibrium/three-stable-equilibrium possibility for sums of logistics is explicitly prior (Wainwright 2020). This finding does **not** reassert that as novel. Its distinct claim is the exact necessary-and-sufficient threshold separation, unique maximal-ratio equation, open gain interval, and full boundary phase count for a physically motivated symmetric dual-sediment logistic feedback model. That criterion was not provided by the full text of Wainwright (2020), Carpenter (2005), Zeng et al. (2017), or Duncan et al. (2021), nor by the accessible text of Ding et al. (2023), and no equivalent criterion appeared in the current mathematical-result searches. Historical priority remains provisional: an exact threshold could occur in broader older mixture-logistic theory; the full motivating article was inaccessible. This is not a claim of certified worldwide novelty.

## Value — PASS

The precise question is whether two distinct pH-response sediment pools can, under a simple positive feedback, create three simultaneous stable water-quality outcomes rather than the two-state picture commonly used as a benchmark. The answer includes the nontrivial exact minimum threshold separation and all allowable feedback gains, identifies center-stability reversal at the upper boundary, and distinguishes folds from simple roots. These are natural parameters of the specific two-pool mechanism, not arbitrary coefficient slices. The result is mathematical and does not establish empirical tri-stability.

## Closest literature and limitations

Wainwright (2020), section 3.4, covers the general five-root bound and stable-root pattern, but not the present explicit separation/gain frontier. Ding et al. (2023) supplies the experimentally motivated logistic pH dependence, not the dual-pool feedback ODE; its methods/full text could not be read. Carpenter (2005) and Zeng et al. (2017) cover conventional lake-bistability models. Duncan et al. (2021) treats network stability for steep sigmoids. Unverified priority in broader logistic mixture theory is the main originality risk. Dynamic pH, unequal amplitudes and environmental validation remain open.
