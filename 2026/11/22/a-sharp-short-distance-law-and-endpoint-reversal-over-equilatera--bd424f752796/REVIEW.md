# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For a diameter-equilateral base, every one-point Katětov vector has either no distance below half the diameter or exactly one, with explicitly forced values at other named points. A four-case partition of two such vectors yields the exact ordered-pair count; each case satisfies the cross-triangle conditions via the stated indicator. Strict sub-square bounds prove the sharp rare-short-distance asymptotics. The upper-channel expansion and a per-pair inequality prove endpoint reversal for every even diameter. The standalone checker independently enumerated 78 complete finite metric-diagram instances and verified 66 endpoint identities; finite examples are not used to infer the infinite parameter claims. Residual correctness risk is ordinary independent proof scrutiny, not an unproved computational extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations

Sauer (arXiv:1010.4212, Sections 1–2) provides the one-point Katětov criterion and Urysohn extension framework, not the exact conditional two-point distribution over growing equilateral bases. Mubayi–Terry (arXiv:1502.01212, Introduction and Sections 2, 3, 6) prove global finite-metric enumeration and an even-diameter zero-one law; global sizes and limiting structures do not imply the stated fixed-base two-point counts, sharp exponential constant, or endpoint sign reversal. Published one-point metric type-polynomial results and three-distance computations are different alphabets or arities. Targeted meaning searches identified no dominating all-even-diameter conditional pair-type result. Older equivalent formulations under Katětov enumeration terminology remain a priority risk; absence from searches does not establish absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The paper isolates a natural conditional counterpart of global even-diameter metric concentration, obtains an exact all-parameter distribution instead of an asymptotic-only assertion, and demonstrates a genuine finite-size sign reversal between equally dominant upper distance channels. The universal tail constant, uniform upper limit, and quantitative crossover bound are useful features of the concrete logical extension model, not arbitrary recomputations of a small finite table. The result has no claim for odd diameters, general old bases or growing diameter.

## Closest literature and boundaries
