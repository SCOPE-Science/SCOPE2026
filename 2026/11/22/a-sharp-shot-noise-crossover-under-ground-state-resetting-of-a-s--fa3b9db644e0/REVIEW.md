# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The emitted-photon process is a renewal count because each recorded \(E\to G\) event regenerates the same ground state, and the reset mechanism is memoryless. The explicit transient resolvent produces a rational waiting-time transform with finite all-order moments. Differentiating yields a Fano numerator equal to a strictly positive prefactor times \(ps-(u+r)^2\), so the all-rate classification follows analytically. A third derivative of the same transform proves the strict normalized third-count-cumulant deficit exactly at equality. A separate tilted-generator characteristic equation has been differentiated for nine exact rational parameter combinations; all comparisons agree. There is no numerical-exhaustiveness claim.

## Originality — PASS with publication-priority caveat

The most directly relevant full texts are Liu (2023, arXiv:2308.02214) and Lachman et al. (2024, Phys. Rev. Research 6, 033254, Appendix B). The former derives general counting frameworks with resetting and tests a coherent two-level system. The latter establishes the reset-free incoherent shelved three-state model, including photon-correlation expressions, but does not add ground-state Poisson resets or state the present all-rate shot-noise crossing. The closest relevant indexed research record on reset fluorescence addresses detuning-dependent brightness in a two-level coherent emitter, not this noise sign theorem. General machinery may implicitly contain the claim after an unreported specialization; broader publication priority has not been independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The problem is motivated by electron shelving: a dark state creates bunching and excess count noise, while ground-state repumping removes long dark pauses but interrupts excitation. The exact criterion separates all physically allowed parameters, shows that the critical reset rate does not depend on radiative decay, and cautions that Fano equality does not yield Poisson emission by giving an exact third-count-cumulant witness. The calculation is a meaningful complete phase boundary, rather than an arbitrary numerical slice or a claim about generic emitters.

## Scope and residual risk

Ideal detection, finite stationary counting rates, exponential intrinsic transitions and reset times, and an incoherent rather than coherent three-level emitter are assumed. Broader photon-counting formulas might imply the same result; independently verifying exhaustive publication priority and practical experimental relevance remains open.
