# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS

The claim applies to every strictly positive pulse size, Hill half-saturation, decay rate, and finite horizon; the second equal pulse may be at either endpoint. Direct piecewise integration gives a four-logarithm objective, with an exact rational derivative. The positive-polynomial ratio argument rules out all extra local and global optimizers, establishes both boundary equalities, and yields the monotone finite large-horizon limit. The companion code checks symbolic algebra, 1,500 integrations, 1,500 optimizer instances, and 6,000 additional timing comparisons. These finite tests supplement rather than prove the global result.

## Originality: PASS on inspected evidence

The primary preprint by Ma and coauthors studies observed rainfall responses, not this deterministic optimization. Feldman and coauthors analyze pulse rehydration and growth dynamics; Ogle and Reynolds develop an ecological pulse-threshold framework. A general pharmacological response review covers schedule dependence but not the exact Hill-two equal-input timing frontier. Relevant published mathematical findings include cyclic runoff gaps, Green–Ampt infiltration sequencing, Hill resource allocation across independent mechanisms, and the one-exponent (Michaelis–Menten) two-impulse problem: their objectives or structural hypotheses differ and do not imply this finite upper lag, amplitude obstruction, and horizon threshold. Historical priority uncertainty persists because some broader nonlinear impulse-control literature was not exhaustively examined, and the ecological literature is not necessarily complete.

## Value: PASS

The postulated cooperative response is convex at low moisture and concave at high moisture, making the tension between pulse overlap and dilution mathematically natural. The theorem identifies a nontrivial amplitude floor below which separation is never useful, an exact observation-horizon transition above it, and the otherwise unexpected saturation of the optimal lag even as the available horizon grows. This supplies a transparent benchmark for pulse-timing experiments and for testing richer ecological models; it is not a calibrated field intervention.

## Scientific limits

No feedback of plant uptake on moisture, unequal pulse sizes, random rainfall, nonlinear drainage or physiological parameter fitting is covered. Supplementary computations do not establish an infinite theorem, and no exhaustive literature-priority certification is claimed.
