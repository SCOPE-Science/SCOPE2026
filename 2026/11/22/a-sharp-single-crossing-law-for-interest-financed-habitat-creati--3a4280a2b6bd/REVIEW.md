# Scientific review: interest-financed creation of a recolonizing habitat patch

## Correctness: PASS

The proposed theorem is about a single, initially absent patch created empty at a deterministic time \(s\) before a fixed census \(H\); the post-creation empty-to-occupied hazard is frozen at \(a e^{rs}\), and the occupied-to-empty hazard is \(\mu\). Exact solution of its two-state forward equation gives the stated terminal occupancy probability. Differentiating with respect to creation time yields the decision sign \(r\mathcal B(ae^{rs},\mu,H-s)-1\). The identity for \(\mathcal B_t\mathcal B-q\mathcal B_q\) is strictly positive at all positive parameters and all positive remaining horizons. Therefore every interior stationary point crosses from increasing to decreasing, which excludes multiple stationary points and proves global optimality and uniqueness. The endpoint and equality cases and the zero-extinction comparison are evaluated separately. The integral representation also proves the strict comparative statics of the interest threshold. A standalone deterministic check independently solves the forward equation numerically and exercises the sign identities, but the universal quantifiers rest on the analytical proof.

## Originality: PASS, with specified historical risk

The nearest published mathematical finding on timed habitat construction could be hidden in the broader restoration and investment literature, particularly Southwell, Heard and McCarthy's biodiversity-offsetting study (DOI 10.1002/eap.1666): its accessible abstract describes compound-interest funding and dynamic metapopulation planning, but its full primary text could not be read. This unresolved access limitation is explicitly retained; the abstract alone is not taken as evidence that its entire manuscript omits the present theorem. Thomson et al. (DOI 10.1890/08-0915.1) was examined in its full available PDF: its revegetation objective ranks landscape sites in a multispecies Zonation analysis, without this exact fixed terminal two-state Markov law. Iacona, Possingham and Bode (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1702111114) was examined in full open-access text: its optimization uses species-area/extinction-debt loss and a distinct deterministic protection model. Westphal et al. (DOI 10.1890/1051-0761(2003)013[0543:TUOSDP]2.0.CO;2) was compared via its accessible abstract and methods summary only. Searches of indexed mathematical results for aliases including restoration delay, Markov occupancy, investment growth and interest thresholds did not yield a result implying the explicit one-crossing classification. A lack of a match is not a universal novelty guarantee; the genuine primary-paper and broader Markov decision-literature risks remain.

## Value: PASS

For a basic and interpretable conservation investment/occupation model, the result supplies a **complete**, parameter-uniform decision rule rather than another particular numerical schedule. It establishes a unique optimum, a sharp transition between immediate and delayed action, and exact monotonic effects of colonization, extinction and deadline. The nontrivial structural step is the strict one-crossing identity; without it, stationary-point searches do not certify the unique global optimum. The distinction from a no-extinction patch is concrete: even when the appreciation rate satisfies \(rH\leq1\), local extinction can make delay strictly better. This may inform the design and benchmarking of more realistic stochastic restoration schedulers but should not be mistaken for a field recommendation.

## Limitations and closest literature

The externally sustained colonization source, exponential appreciation, investment-to-colonization conversion, frozen post-construction hazards and terminal-only objective are idealizations. The result does not solve coupled habitat networks, extinction debt, spatially distributed decisions, uncertainty in colonist supply, or general investment planning. The closest inaccessible primary paper (Southwell et al., DOI 10.1002/eap.1666) is a priority risk; the full available work of Thomson et al. (DOI 10.1890/08-0915.1), Iacona et al. (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1702111114) and accessible Westphal et al. summary were compared on mathematical hypotheses and objectives rather than titles.
