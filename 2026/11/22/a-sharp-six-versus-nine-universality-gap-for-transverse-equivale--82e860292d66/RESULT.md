# A sharp six-versus-nine universality gap for transverse equivalence relations

## Finding
Let \(X\) be a finite nonempty set with two **named** equivalence relations \(E,F\) such that \(E\cap F=\Delta_X\) (the identity relation). A complete ordered quantifier-free three-variable type records, for \((x,y,z)\), all atomic equalities and all instances of \(E\) and \(F\) between these variables. Call \(X\) **three-type universal** if every complete three-variable type realized in *some* finite structure satisfying these assumptions also occurs in \(X\).

**Theorem.** There are exactly \(25\) realizable ordered three-variable types. The smallest three-type universal structure has \(6\) elements, and there are exactly **four** isomorphism types of minimum structures, with the names \(E,F\) fixed. If, in addition, the two equivalences commute as relations (\(E\circ F=F\circ E\)), the sharp minimum is \(9\) elements, with exactly **three** isomorphism types. Every larger finite cardinality is attainable in both classes. Thus requiring commutation imposes a sharp three-element universality cost.

Under the standard correspondence with simple bipartite incidence graphs (described below), the four six-element extremizers are made of a left-centered three-star and a right-centered three-star sharing their central edge, plus one additional edge not incident to either center. This extra edge can meet zero, one, or both of the pre-existing noncentral endpoint sets, yielding four named-side-preserving isomorphism types. The three commuting nine-element extremizers have incidence graphs

\[
K_{3,3},\qquad K_{2,3}\sqcup K_{3,1},\qquad K_{1,3}\sqcup K_{3,2},
\]

where the first index of each complete bipartite component refers to \(E\)-classes (left shore). These three graphs are distinct when shore names are fixed.

## Assumptions and scope
All structures are finite and nonempty; no colors, constants, functions, other relations, or quantifiers are present in the types. The \(E\)- and \(F\)-class partitions may be arbitrary subject to \(E\cap F=\Delta_X\). This is **not** an assertion about all pairs of equivalence relations, which can have multi-element intersections. Relational composition means \((a,c)\in E\circ F\) when some \(b\) satisfies \(E(a,b)\) and \(F(b,c)\). Isomorphisms must preserve each named relation, not exchange them.

Make a bipartite graph \(G\): its left vertices are nonempty \(E\)-classes, its right vertices are nonempty \(F\)-classes, and each element \(a\in X\) gives an edge joining its two classes. The meet condition makes \(G\) **simple**, and conversely every finite simple bipartite graph without isolated vertices gives such a structure, with elements identified with edges. Isomorphism preserving \(E,F\) is precisely graph isomorphism preserving both shores. In particular, \(|X|=|E(G)|\); graph vertices (classes) are not elements of \(X\).

## Proof
**1. Enumeration of types.** On three *distinct* variables, each equivalence determines a partition of the index set \(\{1,2,3\}\). There are five partitions: the discrete partition, three partitions joining exactly one pair, and the indiscrete partition. Since \(E\cap F=\Delta_X\), their common refinement must be discrete. If the \(E\)-partition is discrete there are five possible \(F\)-partitions; if it joins exactly one pair there are three possible \(F\)-partitions; if it is indiscrete there is one. Hence the number of distinct-variable types is \(5+3\cdot3+1=15\). If exactly two variables agree, there are three choices of the agreeing pair, and the remaining distinct element can be \(E\)-related only, \(F\)-related only, or related by neither, giving \(9\) further types. The all-equal type contributes one, for \(15+9+1=25\). Each consistent pair of partitions has a finite incidence graph realization, so this counts possible, not merely formally consistent, types.

For three distinct variables, the fifteen ordered types fall under variable permutations into **six** edge-configuration classes: a three-edge matching; a three-edge star centered on either shore (two classes); a two-edge star plus an edge disjoint from its endpoints, centered on either shore (two classes); or a three-edge path (one class). The orbit sizes are respectively \(1,1,1,3,3,6\). Thus a graph contains all distinct-variable types exactly when it contains all six configurations as *edge-selected subgraphs*: extra edges between their endpoints do not alter the relations among the three selected elements. Any such graph also realizes every repeated-variable type, since it contains the required incident, nonincident, and disjoint pairs.

**2. Unrestricted lower bound and equality classification.** Universality requires both a three-edge star centered at a left vertex \(u\) and a three-edge star centered at a right vertex \(v\), and it also requires three pairwise disjoint edges (a matching). The two stars share at most the edge \(uv\). If they are edge-disjoint, they account for six edges and no three edges among them form a matching, because all edges meet \(u\) or \(v\). If they share \(uv\), their union accounts for five edges and likewise has matching number at most two. Consequently fewer than six edges never suffice.

On exactly six edges, the two stars must share \(uv\) and there must be exactly one further edge. To obtain a three-edge matching, this edge must meet neither \(u\) nor \(v\); conversely **any** such extra edge suffices. Indeed, of the two noncentral leaves of the left star, choose one not used by the extra edge, and likewise choose an unused leaf of the right star. The edges from these leaves to \(u\) and \(v\), together with the extra edge, form a three-edge matching. The shared stars contain a three-edge path. The two stars and the extra edge also supply both kinds of two-star-plus-disjoint-edge patterns. Thus all six edge configurations and all \(25\) types occur.

The extra edge's left endpoint is either one of the two old left leaves or a new left vertex; its right endpoint is either one of the two old right leaves or a new right vertex. Each choice gives one isomorphism type, since within each pair of old leaves permutation is possible. There are four choices. They are inequivalent under shore-preserving isomorphism, as witnessed by their counts of left and right class vertices, respectively \((3,3),(3,4),(4,3),(4,4)\). The six-point classification is complete.

**3. Commutation and rectangular components.** In a simple incidence graph, commutation \(E\circ F=F\circ E\) holds exactly when every connected component is complete bipartite. To see the nontrivial direction, if three cells \((u,r),(u,s),(v,s)\) occur, commutation implies the fourth cell \((v,r)\): the first and third elements are linked by \(E\circ F\) through the second, and a witness for \(F\circ E\) must occupy that fourth cell. Repeatedly completing the corners along any connected alternating path fills all pairs in its connected component. Conversely, unions of complete bipartite components plainly have commuting class relations.

If a commuting universal graph has its left three-star and right three-star in the **same** component, that rectangular component has at least three vertices on each shore and hence at least \(9\) edges; at equality it is \(K_{3,3}\) and no other component can be present.

If the two stars lie in **different** complete bipartite components, these components contribute at least \(3+3\) edges. A three-edge path requires a component having at least two vertices on each shore. If it is one of the two star components, that component must have at least \(2\cdot3=6\) edges, yielding at least \(6+3=9\) overall. At equality the two possibilities, preserving the shore names, are exactly \(K_{2,3}\sqcup K_{3,1}\) and \(K_{1,3}\sqcup K_{3,2}\). If the path uses a third component, it costs at least four additional edges, so at least \(3+3+4=10\) edges. This proves the commuting lower bound and exhausts all nine-edge equality cases.

Each displayed nine-edge graph has a left three-star, a right three-star, a matching of three edges, a three-edge path, and both two-star-plus-disjoint-edge patterns, so it realizes all \(25\) types. Finally, adding an element alone in a new \(E\)-class and a new \(F\)-class adds a disconnected single-edge component, preserving all previously realized types, the meet condition and, when present, commutation. Therefore every cardinality above the corresponding minimum is also realizable.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 program `verify_profiles.py` computes complete equality, \(E\), and \(F\) truth vectors on three ordered variables without assuming their count. It obtains \(25\) target types, checks every simple bipartite incidence graph of one through six edges inside a \(4\)-by-\(4\) class grid, and confirms that universal graphs occur first at six edges with exactly four shore-preserving isomorphism classes. It also enumerates every multiset of complete-bipartite components of total edge count at most nine, checking all commuting equality cases. Its output is:

`VERIFY_OK types=25 unrestrained_min=6 unrestrained_classes=4 commuting_min=9 commuting_classes=3 labelled_4x4=1296`

The finite enumeration is a reproducibility check, **not** the proof for arbitrary class sizes; Steps 2 and 3 provide the unrestricted all-size arguments.

## Relationship to prior work
Fiuk's *The Guarded Fragment with Nested Equivalences* (first public May 14, 2026) studies satisfiability and finite-model properties when named equivalences form a nested chain, and shows that removing nesting fundamentally changes computational behavior. Here the relations are instead transverse, and we determine a quantitative local realization invariant and its sharp change under permutability; no decidability or general satisfiability complexity claim is made. Kieroński and Otto investigate two-variable logic with multiple arbitrary equivalences, not universal simultaneous realizers of complete three-variable quantifier-free types. A published analysis of pointed rank-one types for two named equivalences studies a different invariant (unrestricted overlaps and rank-one formulas), with an eight-type maximum and twelve-element minimum; neither statement implies the present twenty-five-type, transverse, three-variable classification.

## Limitations
The result concerns precisely two named equivalences with trivial intersection and ordered quantifier-free types of at most three variables. The commuting classification requires actual relational commutation, not merely a bound on the number of equivalence classes. The theorem does not bound smallest models for formulas with arbitrary quantifiers or auxiliary relations. Full historic priority cannot be inferred from search results alone; specialized incidence-design terminology may conceal earlier equivalents. The algorithm checks finite instances only.
