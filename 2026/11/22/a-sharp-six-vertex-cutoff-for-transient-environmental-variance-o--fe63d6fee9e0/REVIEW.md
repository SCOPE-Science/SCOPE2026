# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a finite Markov-process theorem with explicit, universally quantified parameters. The one-edge centered moment evolves by factor \(a\); conditional independence of distinct edge updates given the sampled vertex gives the exact two-edge factors \(b_0\) and \(b_1\). Summing covariances yields the variance formula for all simple graphs and times. The tree-extremal combinatorial inequalities are sharp, and parity plus the bound \(\omega s(8-s)<12\) proves the minimal network size six. The rational witness satisfies the primary source's strict environmental inequalities and is independently checked by exact enumeration. No numerical test is used as a substitute for the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded-priority risk

The full arXiv source text of Betz–Fu–Masuda, 2611.11029, sections 2–6 and appendix, supplies the model and approximate pair closure (equation 78), but not this exact finite-time variance, the graph wedge invariant, or the sharp parity-dependent five/six-vertex tree ordering. Earlier independent-edge evolving-graph models use a different activation mechanism; generic random-scan Markov-chain results do not in the inspected material state this exact tree classification. Searches included the edge-environment model, incident-edge covariance, degree-wedge variance, random-block update covariance, and equivalent star/path orderings. Record overlap searches did not expose a logically stronger result on this process. A general unindexed theorem on random block-product chains could subsume the calculation, and independent historical verification is still needed.

This is a finite-network quantitative structural result, not merely a change of notation or a simulated anecdote. Limits: unanimity, homogeneous environmental initialization and the source's exact update protocol.
