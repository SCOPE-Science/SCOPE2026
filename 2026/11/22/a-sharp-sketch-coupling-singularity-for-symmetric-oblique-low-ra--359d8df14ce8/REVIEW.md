# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed observable is the Frobenius norm of the symmetric part of an exact two-sided oblique tangent projection at a fixed positive rank-one matrix. The proof reconstructs the \(2\times2\) matrix without approximations, identifies the conditional bivariate Gaussian distribution of the two sketch ratios, and uses local integrability of a strictly positive joint Gaussian density to obtain the complete moment cutoffs on both sides of perfect correlation. At perfect correlation, the two ratios coincide and the square of a Gaussian/chi ratio determines the sharper cutoff and the explicit power-tail constant. The independent and shared squared-risk formulas use exact chi-square inverse moments. An integral Laplace determinant identity, evaluated explicitly at three and four sketch rows, yields sharp near-sharing divergence orders. The standalone verifier passes finite exact-rational identities, moment arithmetic, numerical integration, and deterministic sketch experiments; infinite-support conclusions rest on the written analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations
Carrel and Grigori (2026, arXiv:2607.03402v1) already derive the geometric oblique tangent projector and warn of errors under perpendicular forcing. Lam, Ceruti and Kressner (2024, arXiv:2409.06384v1) bound moments for a different generalized Nyström reconstruction with independent Gaussian sketches. The published *Residual-spectrum convex ordering and the exact Gaussian sketch failure law* already obtains inverse-Wishart moment thresholds for one-sided randomized reconstruction; *Sharp inverse-moment thresholds and logarithmic tails for two Gaussian tensor sketches* already handles independent Gaussian/chi products in regression. Classical Gaussian-ratio calculations are not the discovery. The assessed new increment is the coupled/same-sketch phase diagram for the specific rank-one *symmetric oblique tangent force*, with its exact threshold distinction, endpoint loss, sharp tail exponent and singular near-perfect-correlation rates. The inspected primary results and mathematical-record searches do not supply these combined conclusions, but unindexed equivalents or a more general correlated-projector theorem remain plausible.

## Value — PASS
Symmetric matrix dynamics make reuse of the same sketch on both sides tempting. The theorem demonstrates a materially different risk regime: with three or four sketch rows, independent sketches followed by symmetric-part correction retain finite mean-square perpendicular-forcing contamination while identical sketches have infinite mean-square contamination. It also quantifies the singular degradation when the sketches are nearly identical, rather than asserting only an isolated counterexample. The exact row threshold and coupling singularity are directly relevant to sketch reuse and symmetry-preserving numerical design at fixed rank. This finding is not claimed to characterize all symmetric low-rank integrators.

## Limits and literature
The proof is exact-arithmetic and one-state/one-force only. Neither long-time integration stability nor all-rank guarantees follow. The most relevant earlier Gaussian ratio results cover distinct constructions; possible specialized covariance/oblique-projector work outside the inspected texts remains a priority risk. Additional sources inspected include the full relevant sections of the motivating oblique DLRA article, the independent-Gaussian generalized Nyström moment study, and the published mathematical statements and proofs listed in RESULT.md.
