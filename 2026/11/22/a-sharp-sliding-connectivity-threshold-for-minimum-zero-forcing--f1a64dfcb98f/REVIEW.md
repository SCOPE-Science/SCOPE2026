# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The all-order proof establishes an exact local witness criterion for every minimum forcing set, counts states without duplication, enumerates transitions using the unique common white pair, and proves a sharp connectivity threshold using connected fibres and a cross-parity bridge. The boundary \(n=5\) is enumerated explicitly; the \(n=6\) fibre exception is dealt with separately. Original-graph and structural checker implementations reproduce the formulas at finite orders. Their finite checks support rather than replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The key prior coverage is the previously established minimum forcing number and count for path complements. Those are explicitly excluded from the new contribution. The 2020 zero-forcing reconfiguration paper establishes the exchange framework, and the 2022 complement-forcing paper establishes the scalar parameter for tree complements, but their inspected full HTML texts do not yield the present path-complement slide connectivity theorem or the exact exchange/slide polynomial counts. Indexed mathematical results for path complements treat forcing polynomials and forts, not this relocation classification in their accessible summaries. Full historical priority cannot be guaranteed where indexed-source full text was unavailable; no claim of comprehensive uniqueness is made.

## Value — PASS
The result answers a natural optimum-preserving movement question for a standard dense graph family rather than merely recomputing an already known optimization number. The change from \(K_2\sqcup3K_1\) to connected movement at the sharp first threshold, the all-order constructive connectivity proof, and the exact cubic cost of local movement give usable structural and quantitative information. The number of forbidden exchanges is quadratic although all exchanges are cubic, so local movement asymptotically loses only a vanishing fraction of possible transitions.
