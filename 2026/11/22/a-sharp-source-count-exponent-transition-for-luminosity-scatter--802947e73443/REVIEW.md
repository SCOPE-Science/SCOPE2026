# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The radial selection identity follows directly from a capped inverse-square visibility radius. A Gaussian truncated-moment calculation gives the exact detection fraction and derivative. The decisive uniqueness argument is an integrating-factor proof in which a single-sign-change quadratic forces exactly one maximum when the radial count exponent exceeds two and the fixed mean luminosity is below the survey-edge threshold. The limiting law follows from a controlled Laplace expansion. Numerical quadrature and derivative tests in `verify.py` supplement, but do not replace, this proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to examined sources and accessible records.** The 1996 primary paper derives the general finite-volume selection integral and power-law number-count exponent; these are explicitly treated as prior material, not new contributions. The 2005 Malmquist analysis and the 2007 lognormal cluster-selection study develop the relevant bias and selection frameworks. No inspected relevant source established the complete sharp scatter-variance phase diagram, unique interior maximizer, or its faint-mean asymptotic under fixed arithmetic mean. Searches of accessible mathematical records and prior findings did not identify a dominating result. Residual historical risk remains for general capped-power lognormal volatility theory and uninspected literature.

**Value — PASS.** The result isolates a measurable structural threshold (effective radial count exponent two) between cases where increasing luminosity heterogeneity never aids catalog inclusion and cases where an exact finite optimum exists. It clarifies how a finite survey boundary can reverse the effect of intrinsic scatter even with a fixed arithmetic mean, and supplies a sharp analytic benchmark for interpreting idealized flux-selection simulations. It is not a recommendation to increase physical scatter and does not model empirical survey completeness.

**Closest antecedents and limits.** Sylos Labini et al. (1996), arXiv:astro-ph/9605135 (general power-law counts and finite-volume selection); Butkevich et al. (2005), DOI:10.1111/j.1365-2966.2005.09306.x (Malmquist bias); Nord et al. (2007), arXiv:0706.2189 (lognormal flux-limited cluster selection). The proof requires independence of luminosity and radius, a sharp flux limit and a single exact radial power law. Neither priority nor empirical applicability has been independently certified.
