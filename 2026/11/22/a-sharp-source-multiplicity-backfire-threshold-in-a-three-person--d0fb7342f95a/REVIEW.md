# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The weighted neighbor update matches the explicitly stated model and the primary source's neighbor-only convention. Symmetry reduces the process to two state variables; the first-step opinion and gaps are exact. For one and two sources the stated gap rectangles are forward invariant, and their positive gap matrices become strict contractions after two iterations once the ramp ends. For three or more sources, the ordinary bridge gap after one step is at least \(61/200>3/10\), while the source gap remains below the confidence threshold. Induction gives permanent separation and the exact limiting mean. An independently coded exact-rational checker passes six distinct source counts over 1100 updates. Neither a finite experiment nor a numerical tolerance substitutes for the infinite-time proof.

## Value — PASS

The precise open problem is whether *adding identically aligned, steadily moving influence sources* necessarily improves final persuasion in a static attractive-only confidence-gated network. The answer is decisively negative with an exact, large \(31/50\) ordinary-mean loss and a sharp integer cutoff: one or two sources succeed completely, a third fails by forcing an immediate permanent separation. This isolates the causal confidence-break mechanism from source placement, changing ordinary edges, repulsion, random sampling, and source-speed changes. It complements earlier group-averaging results suggesting a larger manipulative group improves reach. It is a meaningful, fully solved small-network boundary case rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limitations and comparison risks

The claim is not a minimum-size theorem over all networks, does not address human behavior, and makes no universal assertion for heterogeneous graphs. Source schedule, topology and weights are particular rational choices. The mechanism of premature segregation and the general possibility of backfire are preexisting. Mathematical priority awaits independent literature review, especially literature outside indexed records.
