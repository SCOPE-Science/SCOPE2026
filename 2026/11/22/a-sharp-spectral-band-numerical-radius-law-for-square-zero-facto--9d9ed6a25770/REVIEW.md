# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The rank-one numerical-radius identity is exact, and the reduction from arbitrary square-zero second factors preserves their numerical radius. The spectral inequality \(A^2\le (M+m)A-mMI\) bounds both moments; approximate endpoint eigenvectors certify sharpness even for continuous spectral endpoints. The scalar profile is strictly concave, with a unique optimizer. Endpoint and boundary cases have been checked separately. The reproducibility script corroborates, but does not replace, the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The sharp universal constant \(3\sqrt3/4\) and an extreme rank-one nilpotent example occur in Benabdi et al. (2022). The new statement is a spectral-minimum-dependent **optimal fixed-positive-factor law** valid for every square-zero factor, with an attainment criterion and limiting coefficients. The Gau–Huang–Wu 2008 work requires commutation and a square-zero *first* factor; the Bhunia–Paul 2021 and Bhunia–Jana–Paul 2021 results give different general numerical-radius and product estimates. No retrieved statement gives the same all-condition-number sharp curve, but an older specialized inequality or a reduction through dual numerical-radius theory remains a historical-priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The unrestricted universal product bound does not quantify the improvement forced by uniform positivity of the first factor. Determining the optimal amplification for the natural, large square-zero class gives a dimension-free spectrum-only law, detects exactly which positive operators have an extremizer, and separates the singular and nearly scalar regimes by sharp endpoint asymptotics. It answers a structural extremal question rather than computing a freely chosen isolated example.

## Literature comparison and limitations
The 2022 Filomat paper establishes a universal constant but no spectral-band refinement; the 2008 square-zero paper assumes commuting factors; the 2021 operator estimates treat different products and norm data. Exact overlap checks against the available record and indexed claims found no equivalent or broader fixed-spectral-band classification. The theorem does not extend to general \(B\) without separate proof. Historical priority could be affected by an inaccessible or unindexed exact inequality; independent mathematical audit is not performed.
