# Scientific review: stationary covariance curvature in heterogeneous telegraph resetting

**Correctness — PASS.** The signed Lamperti coordinate reduces the position-dependent-speed process to a homogeneous two-velocity telegraph process. Its regenerative stationary coordinate distribution is bilateral exponential. Stationary second-moment balance gives the first covariance jet; differentiating the no-reset Markov semigroup twice and integrating explicit Gamma moments gives the second jet. Local integrability at the origin follows from the specified exponent \(0<\alpha<2\), hence \(p>1\). Every asserted rate boundary and inverse formula is algebraically sharp. A standalone checker independently evaluates Markov-generator powers in exact rational arithmetic for three polynomial cases and tests nonpolynomial Gamma formulas, passing 1,343 assertions. No finite numerical check is used as proof of the continuum statement.

**Originality — PASS, qualified by literature coverage.** The directly motivating full text, Sandev et al. arXiv:2211.01909v1, Sections II–V, obtains heterogeneous telegraph one-time densities and mean-square displacement, but not the physical stationary two-time covariance curvature or three-parameter short-lag inverse map. The full relevant published published-finding corpus result on homogeneous run-and-tumble covariance treats constant-speed linear position and therefore does not contain the \(\alpha>0\) curvature sign reversal. The 2024 fractional heterogeneous telegrapher paper studies stationary one-time densities and MSDs; its indexed full PDF was inspected for model and observable overlap, without finding this two-time statement. The broad generator methodology is established prior art, so novelty is asserted for the specific sharp physical observable and inference claim, not for semigroup calculus. Related unpublished/unindexed work remains a priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A measurable stationary covariance switches initial right curvature precisely at \(\alpha r=4\nu\), a transition absent from the homogeneous model, and its first two jets plus a static variance identify all positive rates and speed at a fixed known exponent. The statement is a natural finite-velocity heterogeneous transport diagnostic rather than a renaming of an established one-time marginal. The model's origin crossing convention and exact-measurement assumptions are substantive limitations.
