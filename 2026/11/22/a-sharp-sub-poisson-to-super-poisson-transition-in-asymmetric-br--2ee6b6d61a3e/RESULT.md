# A sharp sub-Poisson to super-Poisson transition in asymmetric Brownian reset counts

## Finding

Consider a reflected Brownian particle on the half-line, with diffusion generator \(D\partial_{xx}\) for \(D>0\). Fix a threshold \(L>0\). While the position is strictly above \(L\), alarms of intensity \(r>0\) send the particle instantaneously to \(L\); alarms below \(L\) are ineffective. Start at \(L\) immediately following an effective reset and let \(T\) denote the next effective reset time. Let \(N_t\) be the total number of effective resets by time \(t\), starting with \(N_0=0\).

Set \(a=L\sqrt{r/D}\). Then the full inter-reset Laplace transform is
\[
\mathbb E e^{-sT}=\frac{r}{r+s+\sqrt{s(s+r)}\tanh(L\sqrt{s/D})},\qquad s\ge0.
\]
The mean and variance are
\[
\mathbb ET=\frac{1+a}{r},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}T=\frac{1+a+a^2+\frac23a^3}{r^2}.
\]
The asymptotic reset-count Fano factor exists and is exactly
\[
\mathcal F(a)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Var}N_t}{\mathbb EN_t}
=\frac{1+a+a^2+\frac23a^3}{(1+a)^2}.
\]
Consequently the effective-reset count is asymptotically **sub-Poisson** for \(0<r<3D/(2L^2)\), has unit Fano factor at exactly \(r=3D/(2L^2)\), and is **super-Poisson** for \(r>3D/(2L^2)\). The asymptotic Fano factor has precisely one global minimum. It occurs at \(r_*=D a_*^2/L^2\), where \(a_*>0\) is the unique root of
\[
2a_*^3+6a_*^2+3a_*-3=0.
\]
Numerically, \(a_*=0.4756865177957207\ldots\) and \(\mathcal F(a_*)=0.8145118671977507\ldots\). The mean effective reset frequency, in contrast, increases strictly with \(r\) and equals \(r/(1+a)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The process is the continuous-path one-dimensional diffusion \(dX_t=\sqrt{2D}\,dW_t+dK_t\) on \([0,\infty)\), where \(K_t\) is the minimal reflection regulator at zero. Conditional on the path, effective reset events occur with intensity \(r\mathbf 1_{\{X_t>L\}}\), and at an effective event the position becomes \(L\). The post-reset Brownian driving noise and clock are fresh by the strong Markov and exponential-memoryless properties. The target quantity counts **effective position-changing resets**, not all underlying alarm proposals. Equality at \(X_t=L\) is immaterial to the integrated intensity. The exact statements hold for every \(L,D,r>0\). The count asymptotics refer to \(t\to\infty\) at fixed positive parameters, not to finite-time Poisson statistics.

## Proof

**1. Regeneration.** At each effective reset the state is the same point \(L\). Independence of future Brownian increments and of the Poisson clock makes successive waiting times independent and identically distributed with law \(T\). The first event is almost surely finite: reflected Brownian motion has infinite accumulated occupation of \((L,\infty)\) almost surely, and a rate-\(r\) Poisson alarm measured in that occupation time therefore eventually rings. The Laplace transform derived below is analytic in a neighborhood of \(s=0\), and in particular its first two moments are finite and positive.

**2. Killed boundary-value problem.** For \(s>0\), define \(u_s(x)=\mathbb E_x[e^{-sT}]\). Feynman–Kac and the Markov property give
\[
D u_s''-s u_s=0\quad (0<x<L),\qquad
D u_s''-(s+r)u_s=-r\quad(x>L).
\]
Reflection gives \(u_s'(0)=0\). The solutions must be bounded as \(x\to\infty\), while \(u_s\) and \(u_s'\) are continuous at \(L\) because no singular local-time killing is present. With \(k=\sqrt{s/D}\) and \(q=\sqrt{(s+r)/D}\), these conditions give
\[
u_s(x)=A\cosh(kx)\ (x\le L),\quad
u_s(x)=\frac{r}{s+r}+B e^{-q(x-L)}\ (x\ge L),
\]
where \(A k\sinh(kL)=-qB\) and \(A\cosh(kL)=r/(s+r)+B\). Therefore
\[
 u_s(L)=\frac{r}{s+r}
 \frac{\cosh(kL)}{\cosh(kL)+(k/q)\sinh(kL)},
\]
which simplifies to the stated transform. Boundedness and elementary comparison yield its uniqueness for \(s>0\).

**3. Moment expansion.** Write \(z=s/r\) and \(a=L\sqrt{r/D}\). Then
\[
\sqrt{z(1+z)}\tanh(a\sqrt z)
=az+\left(\frac a2-\frac{a^3}3\right)z^2+O(z^3).
\]
Thus
\[
\mathbb E e^{-sT}=1-(1+a)z+
\left((1+a)^2-\frac a2+\frac{a^3}3\right)z^2+O(z^3).
\]
Using the coefficients of \(s\) and \(s^2\) yields the asserted mean and variance.

**4. Renewal counting noise.** Classical finite-variance nonlattice renewal asymptotics give \(\mathbb EN_t\sim t/\mathbb ET\) and \(\operatorname{Var}N_t\sim t\operatorname{Var}T/(\mathbb ET)^3\). The interarrival law is nonlattice because it is generated by a continuous diffusion with an absolutely continuous exponential alarm. Taking the ratio proves the Fano formula. As a separate consistency check, the previously established stationary density for this asymmetric model is constant on \([0,L]\) and exponential above \(L\); integrating its upper tail against \(r\) gives the reset rate \(r/(1+a)=1/\mathbb ET\).

**5. Sharp classification.** The exact identity
\[
\mathcal F(a)-1=\frac{a(\frac23a^2-1)}{(1+a)^2}
\]
has precisely one zero on \(a>0\), namely \(a=\sqrt{3/2}\), and gives the three claimed regimes. Furthermore,
\[
\mathcal F'(a)=\frac{2a^3+6a^2+3a-3}{3(1+a)^3}.
\]
The numerator is strictly increasing from \(-3\) to \(+\infty\) on \(a>0\), so there is exactly one positive critical point, a strict global minimum. The mean count rate \(r/(1+L\sqrt{r/D})\) has strictly positive derivative for all \(r>0\); hence the noise minimum is not a count-rate maximum.

## Relationship to prior work

Plata, Gupta and Azaele (2020, arXiv:2007.12417) introduce precisely this threshold-activated asymmetric resetting model. They derive its steady-state density for a reflecting wall, and first-passage statistics to an *absorbing* wall. The present result does **not** claim the model, stationary density, or general backward generator as new. It instead computes the effective inter-catastrophe clock and proves a sharp fluctuation transition for the regenerative count process, an observable not given by those one-time results.

Roldán and Gupta (2017, arXiv:1703.10615) already give a general path-integral/Feynman–Kac framework for Brownian systems with arbitrary space-dependent reset rates, including a representation of the first-reset-time density. The exact step-hazard half-line Laplace transform here is an evaluated specialization of that general theory, not a new underlying method; the claim is the full explicitly parameterized reset-count noise classification, which is not supplied by their different worked examples.

Godrèche and Luck (2023, arXiv:2309.06997) develop general renewal counting theory, including stochastic reset and dressed renewal intervals for a distinct nested-renewal construction. Classical renewal asymptotics supply the final count-variance step here, but do not evaluate this spatially gated interarrival transform or its parameter-dependent Fano factor. Closely related published work on threshold-*hitting* resets and occupation fluctuations has a different activation mechanism (absorption at a boundary rather than a Poisson hazard on a spatial half-line) and does not directly imply the stated sharp threshold.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns an ideal reflected Brownian diffusion with an instantaneous displacement reset and a perfect Heaviside spatial hazard; finite return durations, boundary drift, threshold noise, and random reset locations are not covered. The Fano factor is a long-time limit and is not asserted equal to the finite-window count dispersion. The exact closed forms are special to the stated model. Although relevant primary and public research results were searched and compared at the level of mechanisms and implications, the possibility of unlocated earlier derivations from general occupation-time resolvents remains a publication-priority risk; this is not an independently verified novelty certificate.
