# A sharp ten-edge integral torus threshold for three-particle dodecahedral homology

## Finding

Let \(D=\operatorname{GP}(10,2)\) be the 20-vertex, 30-edge dodecahedral graph, and let \(B_3(D)\) be the configuration space of three *unordered, mutually distinct* points on its geometric realization. For each positive integer \(d\), define \(L_d\) to be the subgroup of \(H_3(B_3(D);\mathbb Z)\) generated by the fundamental classes of all geometric product three-tori formed from three pairwise vertex-disjoint simple graph cycles, **each** of length at most \(d\).

Then

\[
H_3(B_3(D);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{26},\qquad
L_5=L_8\cong\mathbb Z^{20},\qquad
H_3(B_3(D);\mathbb Z)/L_8\cong\mathbb Z^6,\qquad
L_{10}=H_3(B_3(D);\mathbb Z).
\]

In fact, the twenty disjoint triples of pentagonal cycles and the six triples containing a ten-edge cycle give **twenty-six integral basis elements**. All thirty other triples (those requiring an eight-edge cycle) already belong to the pentagon-torus lattice; each is an integer combination of exactly two pentagon triples. Thus ten is the **sharp maximum allowed individual cycle length** for integral generation: allowing eight-edge cycles introduces no new homology, whereas allowing ten-edge cycles supplies six primitive missing generators.

The numerical rank \(26\) of the ambient top homology was determined previously; the new claims are the integral geometric basis, the primitive deficient lattice, all disjoint-cycle triple relations, and the sharp length cutoff.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set of \(D\) is \(\{u_i,v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\}\), with edges \(\{u_i,u_{i+1}\},\{u_i,v_i\},\{v_i,v_{i+2}\}\) for every \(i\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\). This is the ordinary one-dimensional CW graph, not the solid polyhedron. The three particles are unordered and cannot coincide. A simple cycle has no repeated vertices except its closing point; cycles are identified up to rotation and reversal. A triple of cycles must have **pairwise disjoint vertex sets**, not merely disjoint edges. Reversing a cycle changes a torus orientation sign but not the generated lattice.

The smallest simple cycle has length five, and by definition each of the three cycles in a generator of \(L_d\) has at most \(d\) edges. No assertion concerns arbitrary or knotted three-tori, other particle numbers, other graphs, lower homology, cup products, or geometric-quotient spaces.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive cycle and torus enumeration.** Order the graph vertices \(u_0,\ldots,u_9,v_0,\ldots,v_9\) and the 30 undirected edges lexicographically. Depth-first enumeration starting each cycle at its least vertex and retaining exactly the orientation with second vertex smaller than the final vertex yields **1,168** unoriented simple cycles. Enumerating unordered triples of pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles gives exactly **56** triples: **20** with maximum constituent length five, **30** with maximum length eight, and **six** with maximum length ten. No other disjoint-cycle triple exists, since the exhaustive test considers all 1,168 cycles. The standalone `verify.py` regenerates the sets and checks the counts and all disjointness constraints, without reading a precomputed certificate.

**2. Integral chain model and signed product cycles.** By the published graph-configuration comparison theorem, the reduced weight-three Świątkowski complex computes the integral homology of \(B_3(D)\). At every cubic vertex \(v\), order the three incident half-edges \(h_0(v),h_1(v),h_2(v)\), and use local degree-one generators \(a_j(v)=h_j(v)-h_0(v)\) for \(j\in\{1,2\}\). Their differential is \(\partial a_j(v)=e_j(v)-e_0(v)\), where \(e_j(v)\) denotes the corresponding graph-edge variable. The all-vertex reduced model in weight three has free chain groups

\[
\operatorname{rank} C_3=\binom{20}{3}2^3=9120,\qquad
\operatorname{rank} C_2=\binom{20}{2}2^2\cdot30=22800,\qquad C_4=0.
\]

For a cyclically oriented embedded graph cycle \(C\), let \(z_C\) denote the degree-one chain whose contribution at each visited vertex is the difference between the outgoing and incoming half-edges, expressed using \(a_1(v),a_2(v)\). If \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) are vertex-disjoint, their graded product \(z_{C_1}z_{C_2}z_{C_3}\) is an integral degree-three cycle. This chain represents the actual product torus in the configuration space through the comparison theorem. The verifier constructs all 56 signed products in the ordered 9,120-dimensional basis and computes their *complete integral boundaries* using the Leibniz rule: each is zero. No mod-two sign convention is substituted for this integral check.

**3. Exact ambient top-homology rank.** An independent binary elimination of the fully regenerated \(22,800\)-by-\(9,120\) boundary \(\partial_3\) has rank **9,094** over \(\mathbb F_2\). This establishes a *lower bound* of 9,094 on its rank over \(\mathbb Q\), hence an *upper bound* of 26 on the free rank of its kernel. All twenty pentagonal torus chains together with the six ten-cycle torus chains possess a **26-by-26 integral coordinate minor with determinant \(+1\)**, so they are rationally independent. Consequently the rational kernel rank is exactly 26. Since \(C_4=0\), the integral top homology is precisely \(\ker\partial_3\), a free abelian group of rank 26.

**4. The decisive integrality argument.** The determinant-one minor proves that the rank-26 lattice generated by those 26 tori is a direct summand of the *ambient* free group \(C_3\). The kernel of an integer map between free groups is also primitive in \(C_3\). As the torus lattice lies in that kernel and has the same rational rank, their inclusion has finite index; primitivity in the ambient group forces that index to be one. Thus these specific tori are an **integral basis**, rather than merely a rational basis.

The twenty pentagon triple tori likewise have an integral **20-by-20 coordinate minor with determinant \(+1\)**. They therefore span a primitive rank-20 sublattice even in \(C_3\), hence also in the rank-26 kernel. Consequently their homology quotient is \(\mathbb Z^6\), with no hidden finite-index torsion. Inverting that 20-by-20 unimodular minor gives integral coordinates for each of the thirty octagon-involving tori. The verifier checks equality of each reconstruction in **all 9,120 coordinates**, not just the twenty pivot coordinates; every reconstruction has exactly two coefficients of absolute value one. It follows that the octagon tori add nothing, so \(L_8=L_5\). The remaining six length-ten tori complete the basis, giving \(L_{10}=H_3\) and proving the sharpness of ten.

## Relationship to prior work

The existing published calculation *Twenty-six top-degree integral cycles in the three-particle dodecahedral configuration space* establishes the rank 26 of this **same** homology group, not a geometric product-torus basis, the integer saturation of pentagon triples, or the ten-edge cutoff. It explicitly leaves geometric and equivariant structure outside its claim. The published result *Integral cycle-length torus lattices in the dodecahedral two-point configuration space* treats this **same graph but only two particles** and classifies degree-two product-torus lattices; neither its 36-element pentagon-pair basis nor its two-particle length-pair table determines the present degree-three lattice. In particular, a change of configuration weight alters both chain differentials and disjoint-cycle constraints.

An, Drummond-Cole and Knudsen provide the homology-computing graph-configuration chain model, not this evaluated geometric torus lattice. An and Knudsen's integral generating theorem for planar graph braid-group **second** homology does not imply the exact top-degree degree-three threshold or its six primitive complement classes. A thorough search of indexed mathematical records, equivalent terminology, and accessible relevant full texts did not uncover a stronger theorem settling these exact statements, but the absence of an indexed record cannot certify priority.

## Limitations

This is a finite calculation for the exact graph \(\operatorname{GP}(10,2)\) and three **unordered** particles. It does not establish any uniform generating theorem for larger planar graphs, and it does not compute the full homology ring or any lower-degree homology. Cycle enumeration and modular elimination would not alone prove the claimed integral basis; the unit-minor and whole-chain identities provide the necessary additional certificates. The reduced Świątkowski comparison and geometric interpretation of product cycles are external published inputs. The result has not undergone independent mathematical review, and equivalent unindexed work is a residual bibliographic risk.
