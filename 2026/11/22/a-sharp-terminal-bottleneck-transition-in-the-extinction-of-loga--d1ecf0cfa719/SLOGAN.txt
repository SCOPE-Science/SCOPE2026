In an irreversible Ising ring seeded with equally spaced permanent plus spins, the extinction time of \(m\) minus domains of length \(n\sim(\log m)/\alpha\) changes mechanism at the sharp threshold \(\alpha=1-\log2\). Above that threshold the last surviving domain spends a macroscopic fraction of its lifetime waiting for its final spin, while below it the slowdown is collective erosion; both regimes have an exact scaling law.
