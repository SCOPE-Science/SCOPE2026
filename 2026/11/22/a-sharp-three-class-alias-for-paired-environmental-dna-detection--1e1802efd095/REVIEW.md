# Scientific review: a three-class paired-detection alias

## Correctness — PASS

The binary Poisson zero probabilities are correctly expressed as three finite Laplace transforms. Equality of exponential mixtures for all positive effort determines exactly the exponent multisets (including multiplicities). With two distinct host and parasite rates, the two possible matchings have different squared-sum totals by the nonzero difference \(2(a_1-a_2)(b_1-b_2)\). Two stated three-class rate-pair sets share both marginals and the paired-sum multiset \(\{5,7,9\}\), but their signed within-class host–parasite contrasts differ. An off-diagonal ratio outside a finite explicit set makes every candidate sum unique. The symbolic proof covers all positive rates in the stated domain; the executable integer/rational checks corroborate the witness but do not substitute for the general argument.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

The general nonuniqueness of matching marginals and a sum law is known from Griffiths and Hamza (2019/2020), so this result does not claim that general phenomenon. The inspected primary texts and published mathematical research comparisons did not reveal the *sharp two-versus-three latent-class distinction for full equal-effort binary Poisson assay curves*, this distinct-positive-rate witness, or the finite-forbidden-ratio remedy. The strong Griffiths–Hamza antecedent and older discrete-tomography switching-components literature leave historical-priority risk; independent bibliographic verification has not been performed.

## Value — PASS

Paired host–parasite eDNA surveys often retain presence/absence across jointly sampled markers. The exact counterexample shows that arbitrarily fine variation of a *single common effort* can fail to recover the latent host–parasite shedding association even when marginals are fully identifiable. The smallest possible number of latent classes and the design repair obtained by one separate effort-ratio family give actionable structural boundaries to that inferential limitation, while avoiding empirical claims beyond the idealized model.

## Sources and scope

Specialized discrete tomography could contain the finite combinatorial counterpart; that remains a residual originality risk. The original field data were not reanalyzed, and full statistical uncertainty is unaddressed.
