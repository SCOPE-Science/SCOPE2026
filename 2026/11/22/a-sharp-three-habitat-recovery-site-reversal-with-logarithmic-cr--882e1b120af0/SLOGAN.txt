For linear population recovery on a three-habitat path, choosing the locally faster-growing endpoint is better at every finite horizon unless the remote habitat's growth exceeds the sharp threshold \(r_1+m(r_1-r_2)/(m+r_1-r_2)\); above it, the initially better endpoint loses after exactly one time crossover. Near the threshold that crossover is logarithmically delayed, making the reversal mathematically real but potentially invisible over short monitoring horizons.
