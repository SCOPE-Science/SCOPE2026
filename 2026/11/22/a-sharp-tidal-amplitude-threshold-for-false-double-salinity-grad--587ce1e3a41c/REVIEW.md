# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is global in the real depth variable and quantifies all amplitudes \(A\ge0\), widths \(\sigma>0\), and midpoints \(m\in\mathbb R\). Direct differentiation of the Gaussian CDF gives the Gaussian–arcsine convolution. Its central curvature is an explicit Bessel combination, and the Bessel Riccati identity proves exactly one zero between one-half and one. Gaussian variation diminution limits the derivative to three sign changes, ensuring that the central curvature sign determines the **complete** one-/two-mode classification, rather than just local behavior at the midpoint. At equality the exact fourth derivative is negative and rules out hidden outer modes. The exact Bessel-series interval test is reproduced by `CHECK.py`. The finite-depth observation qualification and the non-hydrodynamic translation assumption are explicit.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

Huang et al. (2024, DOI 10.1029/2024WR037244) use a fitted van-Genuchten-based profile to identify salinity-interface features but do not derive a one-front, sinusoidal phase-average Gaussian–arcsine modal transition. Laurel-Castillo and Valle-Levinson (2023, DOI 10.1029/2023JC019813) analyze two-way exponential estuarine propagation, not this shifted-diffusive-front averaging. The older Jeju field-monitoring result establishes actual tidal salinity variability, not the mathematical curvature threshold (only its abstract was accessible). Genda et al. (2024, DOI 10.1002/zamm.202300432) already prove a general periodic-motion average as convolution with its phase distribution and derive Bessel moments for the arcsine distribution; these are prior art for the reduction, but not the sharp Gaussian-blurred bimodality classification. The classical Gaussian variation-diminishing principle, as in Evans and Williams (1998, arXiv:math/9801146), is openly acknowledged and covers the **mode-count tool**, but not the explicit Bessel-ratio crossing or its hydrological interpretation without further calculation. A published research-result index, accessible previously published claims, and targeted scientific searches did not reveal the same conclusion. This is provisional originality, not a claim of exhaustive historical priority.

## Value — PASS

The result isolates a consequential observation artifact: two extreme salinity-gradient zones in a cycle-averaged profile can arise from **one** moving front, while the unique 50%-salinity depth remains unchanged. The precise \(1.7776\)-width threshold and the exact critical quartic degeneracy distinguish the artifact from ordinary vague broadening. This provides a falsifiable synthetic benchmark for profile-fitting diagnostics and the interpretation of repeated conductivity-depth records. It does not imply actual groundwater profiles follow the surrogate, and no calibration or real-data validation is claimed.

## Closest evidence and residual limitations

The source studies use more complicated physical processes or different constitutive profiles. The full text of Huang et al. and Laurel-Castillo et al. was materially read, including methods and results; the 2008 Kim et al. article was inspected via its abstract. Gaussian–arcsine mixture analysis could be present in signal-processing or probability literature under different terminology. General cross-correlation averaging and arcsine Bessel moments are prior art, as is the classical sign-variation theorem rather than claimed novelty. The benchmark assumes uniform full-cycle phase sampling, a rigid sinusoidal shift and an error-function instantaneous profile.
