# Review of the trace-nine two-pole Hankel theorem

## Correctness

PASS. The Hankel operator is a sum of two positive rank-one operators with independent geometric columns. Its positive spectrum is exactly the spectrum of the displayed two-by-two Gram matrix. Algebraic elimination reduces its inverse fibers to the rational map (1), and the admissible product interval is proved by a reversible quadratic reconstruction. The derivative quadratic has discriminant \((T-1)(T-9)\), yielding strict uniqueness up to trace nine and the three monotone branches above nine. The cubic equation bounds all fibers by three. A standalone rational checker supports the formulas but is not substituted for the proof. Boundary cases \(a=0\), \(a\uparrow b\), and \(T=9\) are addressed.

PASS, with historical-priority uncertainty. Pushnitski–Treil (arXiv:2504.18707) proves injectivity for a more informative Cauchy spectral map that includes eigenvector weights; arXiv:2503.22189 concerns a cyclic spectral measure of integral Hankel operators. The exact trace-nine threshold and threefold interval were not found in the inspected sources or relevant mathematical-record searches. A specialized equivalent parameterization may exist outside the checked material.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The equal-mass normalization is intrinsic to a two-exponential response with known unit amplitudes, so the question asks whether spectral measurements alone recover two decay parameters. The result gives a complete transition from injectivity to threefold ambiguity, an exact reconstructing cubic, and a sharp critical flattening rather than merely a sample of nonuniqueness. This characterizes a natural failure mode of inverse spectral identification despite known masses.

## Limits

The statement concerns real nonnegative distinct poles below one and fixed unit coefficients. It does not claim independent validation or completeness across all historical literature. Stronger data such as a shifted spectrum or cyclic spectral weights can restore information.
