# Scientific scope of the corrected finding

The exact criterion classifies when the optimal policy has two positive-width curtailment bands separated by a positive-width operating interval. At the rated-minimum tie, the two strict superlevel pieces meet in their closures and there is no operating interval between them. When a lower peak is exactly on the cutoff, its isolated level tie contributes no band. The budget, objective value, and essential bands are independent of choices at such points.

The exchange principle selecting the risk-to-power ratio threshold is classical. The contribution is the sharp band geometry from the elasticity crossings and the rated-power kink, with an exact illustration within that regime. A feasible single cut-in policy has strictly less modeled power there. This does not say that a single cut-in policy is optimal outside that regime: under the baseline assumptions, the optimizer always operates on a sufficiently low-speed interval and so cannot be represented by a feasible single cut-in rule. The separate physical-cut-in observation compares policies at a budget reduced by the risk previously spent in the unavailable interval.

The example illustrates an idealized optimization, not a prediction of wildlife fatalities or field energy gains. Prior environmental-curtailment and bat-risk optimization methods are acknowledged. The full RENEBAT II technical report was not independently compared; no exhaustive priority or formal-verification claim is made.
