# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact branch maps give a strictly increasing two-step return \(G_{q,r}(u)=r\cos[(\pi q/2)\cos(\pi u/2)]\). Its global orbit convergence follows from monotonicity; the critical derivative shape follows from strict logarithmic concavity of \(C_q'\). Explicit differentiation gives the unique cusp \(s_*\tan s_*=1\). Return multipliers determine sink/repeller/fold stability. The diagonal \(q=r\) proves an open coexistence region. Direct Taylor composition yields the cubic normal form and a signed \(n^{-1/2}\) limit for all nonfixed critical returns. The self-contained checker verifies the original-return identity, an explicit rational benchmark, local expansion and finite orbit samples. It is not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS within the inspected material
The 2023 source article [1] defines the same cosine map and reports numerical dynamics, without an exact cusp or complete strict-alternation two-cycle census. The closest indexed exact cosine theorem [2], inspected at full RESULT and proof, concerns the disjoint range \(b=1/2\), \(1/4\le a<1/2\), corresponding to normalized gain \(q>1\), and counts at most two alternating cycles; it does not imply the present \(q<1\) three-cycle transition. A prior polynomial cusp theorem [3] is about different branches and does not imply the transcendental cosine threshold. Accessible historical statements for the same family are likewise of polynomial, rather than cosine, type. The 2024 related work [4] was available only in abstracts, leaving a residual equivalence risk. Searches establish no proof of universal novelty.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete and sharp global classification on a natural full-dimensional parameter region of the published no-equilibrium cosine map. In addition to the existence of attracting cycles, it locates a unique cusp, establishes the entire one/two/three orbit phase diagram, resolves the global attracting basins and invariant probabilities, and computes a universal nonhyperbolic relaxation constant. In particular it identifies robust bistability that the original numerical plots do not characterize analytically.
