# Review: a sharp two-harmonic switch in phase-robust ecological sampling

## Correctness — PASS

The stated domain is explicit: \(a>b+c>0\), aligned first and second diel harmonics with \(b,c>0\), two equal-effort conditionally independent Poisson observations, one fixed unknown common phase, and all two-visit separations available within a periodic cycle. The sum of Poisson intensities, not a sample-mean or marginal approximation, controls conditional zero detections. Reflection and translation symmetries reduce the full design to a scalar angular gap. Direct minimization in the unknown phase becomes a quadratic on \([-1,1]\), with all endpoint and vertex branches included. A strictly concave reciprocal expression on the interior branch and strictly convex boundary expression prove the unique low-ratio and high-ratio designs, with exactly two ties at \(r=4(\sqrt2-1)\). The exact identities for \(r=1\) and \(r=2\) agree with a runnable deterministic checker. The finite numerical experiments are not used as global proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority

The nearest ecological work studies actual variability of eDNA and the need to account for time of sampling: West et al. (2024) explicitly report continual day–night detection; Jensen et al. (2022) report changes across hourly samples; Bedwell and Goldberg (2020) compare timing, sample volumes, and spatial designs. The inspected material does not determine a two-harmonic, phase-adversarial Poisson zero-detection optimum. Kay and Gabriel (2002) address unknown-parameter sinusoid detection in Gaussian noise rather than this Bernoulli event probability induced by two Poisson counts, and Dilmaghani et al. (2007) address harmonic reconstruction on irregular grids, not the fixed-effort minimax detection problem. The exact threshold and discontinuous design switch are not claims about universally optimal experimental design or a new trigonometric identity. Searches of published mathematical finding descriptions for equivalent phases, harmonic sampling, Poisson counts, and competing design objectives found close but none whose hypotheses and conclusion imply the full statement. Limited access to older general robust harmonic-design monographs and abstract-only signal-detection papers is a residual historical-priority risk; failed queries do not certify uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

Time-of-day eDNA variation and substantial replicate requirements motivate an exact distinction between maximization at a known favorable phase and **guaranteeing detection under an unknown phase**. Two harmonics are the smallest aligned Fourier enrichment allowing a double-peaked daily intensity, so their relative strength has a clear ecological interpretation. The robust-design switch at \(4(\sqrt2-1)\) occurs strictly before the two-peaked waveform disappears at ratio \(4\), making the optimization boundary distinct from a basic modality boundary. The theorem proves a previously unquantified, sharp operational boundary: the optimum changes discontinuously from a short intraday separation to a half-day separation. This gives a reproducible benchmark for evaluating whether the common maxim “sample half a cycle apart” is always robust; it is not, even in a positive two-frequency Poisson model. This is not a claim of field-calibrated performance, and its applicability must be assessed against transport, imperfect independence, unaligned harmonics and resource constraints.
