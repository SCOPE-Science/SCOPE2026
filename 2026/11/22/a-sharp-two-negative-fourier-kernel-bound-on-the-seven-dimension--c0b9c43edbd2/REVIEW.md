# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS (same-model).** Fourier Bochner theory on the compact torus gives a nonnegative, absolutely summable integer-frequency measure, and at the specified \(\pi\)-shift nodes its quadratic form depends only on 128 parity aggregates. The two test averages are nonnegative because the kernel is nonnegative pointwise. The explicit Krawtchouk binomial formulas give all fourteen rational dual inequalities, yielding a global lower bound. The displayed cosine polynomial has nonnegative Fourier coefficients and 14 nonnegative symmetry-reduced cube-vertex values, and its eight-node matrix realizes the lower bound exactly. The independent standalone rational checker executed 510 assertions; computation corroborates identities but does not replace the compact-group argument.

## Originality

**PASS relative to examined sources, with a residual bibliographic risk.** The original 2011 conjecture concerned a universal matrix lower bound, whereas the 2026 counterexample and optimization on the same eight nodes explicitly use one negative contrast coefficient. Here there are two negative entries and an exact sharp infimum over *every* continuous doubly-positive periodic kernel; no permutation, simple sign reversal, or specialization of the published one-sign objective yields this statement. The published one-negative witness evaluates to \(82/35>2\) in the present contrast, so a distinct construction is necessary. The 2019 Schur-product variant proves a positive lower bound for squared positive-definite kernels, a proper subclass. A recent lacunary single-circle compression of the earlier counterexample was located in bibliographic indexing, but only its summary was accessible and its stated result concerns an odd-base threshold, not the present optimum. An earlier equivalent Walsh/Krawtchouk extremal formulation remains possible.

## Value

**PASS.** The eight-node torus simplex and matrix inequality are the natural fixed geometry of the recent counterexample. The two-negative direction is the first structurally different sign pattern after its one-negative witness; determining the sharp extremum is a natural finite-dimensional invariant of an infinite function cone. The result gives both a quantitative stronger violation \(-2/7\) (versus \(-1/10\) for the previous, different direction) and a compact global dual certificate that may inform other sign patterns. It does not claim that this is globally worst among all coefficient vectors or sampling sets.
