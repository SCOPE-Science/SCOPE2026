# Scientific review: four-class selective filtration

## Correctness — PASS

The mathematical claim is conditional on positive constant filtration rates and independent class losses. Quotient differentiation gives an exact six-pair signed exponential sum. For three classes, the six possible rate orderings yield at most one sign change. For four ordered classes satisfying the single-peak and pair-sum hypotheses, the six ordered pair terms have signs \((-,-,-,+,+,-)\); the generalized Descartes/Rolle argument yields at most two positive derivative zeros for every choice of positive abundances and ordered sizes. The specified strictly admissible integer example has exact derivative factor \(50e^{-4t}Q(e^{-t})\), where \(Q(1)=-25\), \(Q(1/2)=5/2\), and \(Q(1/4)=-479/128\). This proves exactly two simple interior zeros. Direct verification ran the included standalone checker. Limitations of the finite checks and the mathematical assumptions are explicit.

## Originality — PASS on the inspected scope, with priority risk

The two fully readable 2024 primary studies on size-dependent riverbed microplastic filtration establish experimental class-dependent behavior, but neither states an upper bound on turning points of a normalized surviving size profile under single-peaked attenuation with an additional rate-gap condition. Classical sign-variation principles are accepted prior mathematics rather than claimed discoveries. The closest mathematical record, *Four mortality classes are necessary for two hazard-rank reversals under strict survival ordering*, concerns two competing exponential-mixture hazards. Because a normalized weighted mean can be written as a ratio of two mixtures, its unconstrained four-class lower bound could follow from that work. The submitted statement instead supplies a uniform **order-constrained upper bound**, a strict within-cone constructive witness, and its perturbed persistence, not merely the four-class count. The other close record on reversible microplastic exchange addresses two conserved classes and switching environmental phases, not the present single-peak four-class filtration theorem. Full text of the mortality-classes record was not accessible, so priority for mathematical reductions and stronger historical coverage remains provisional.

## Value — PASS

The result gives an exact diagnostic of what a pair of sign-changing trends in mean *transmitted* particle size can and cannot signify under a simple class-filter model. It identifies a sharp minimum of four distinct attenuation rates, a natural rate-order regime with a uniform two-turn cap, and an open positive parameter set attaining two turns without particle creation. This is useful when judging whether composition reversals must be attributed to changing hydrodynamics, fragmentation, mixing or resuspension, rather than to fixed heterogeneous loss rates alone. It is not a field-validated prediction: the motivating homogeneous experiments found size-monotone fitted rates, whereas the witness is hypothetically single-peaked. The contribution is a self-contained theoretical model benchmark, not a general ecological mechanism claim.
