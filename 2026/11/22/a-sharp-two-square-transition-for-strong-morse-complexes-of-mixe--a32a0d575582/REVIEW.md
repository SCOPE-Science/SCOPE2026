# Scientific review: mixed triangle–square strong-Morse bouquets

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem's domain is all integers \(p,q\ge1\), one-dimensional unfilled petals, and the full filtration-generated strong-Morse complex. The proof independently identifies the exact no-hub join \(X=D_4^{*p}*C_6^{*q}\), the precise hub-apex links, and their attachment maps. When \(q\ge2\), a collapsible square-path factor in *every* hub link gives actual elementary collapses relative to \(X\). When \(q=1\), triangle apices still collapse; the two square-apex links each have type \(\bigvee^2 S^p\) and attach trivially because \(X\) is \(p\)-connected. This proves the two wedge formulas over the integers, not just numerical homology. The exact face polynomial follows from a disjoint cone-star enumeration. The standalone checker verifies exact full face-set equality on all exhaustively enumerated test profiles, including three mixed profiles, verifies F2 chain ranks and face polynomials, and performs 256 explicit elementary collapse pairs for the mixed \((p,q)=(1,2)\) profile. Finite tests corroborate rather than prove the unrestricted result.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Complete public mathematical reports on friendship graphs, all-square bouquets, the triangle-plus-four-cycle and triangle-plus-five-/six-cycle cases, and two-triangle-plus-square have been inspected at theorem and proof level. The previously known special cases \((1,1)\) and \((2,1)\) are expressly not counted as new. The searched statements do not entail the all-parameter \(q\ge2\) collapse or the unrestricted one-square multiplicity formula. Targeted semantic searches for mixed bouquets, arbitrary triangle/square numbers, and alternative descriptions found no covering theorem. Not finding one in an index cannot rule out an unindexed or differently described result. Distinct notions (ordinary Morse, pure strong, disjoint unions, square books) were excluded by direct comparison of objects and hypotheses.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural complete two-parameter homotopy classification answering how two elementary cycle lengths interact at a shared hub, where the available disjoint-union join theorem fails. The new structural observation is a sharp one-square versus at-least-two-squares phase change: the sphere rank drops by exactly four when the second square is added, while the sphere dimension advances by two. A uniform link-collapse mechanism explains the transition, including simple-homotopy information in the stable square range, and a closed face enumerator allows reproducible topological and combinatorial checks. The mathematical interest is the sharp parameter-independent link threshold and the all-parameter classification, not the recomputation of previously known special cases.

## Closest literature and limitations
Knudson–Owens–White (arXiv:2609.06844v1) provides the strong-Morse definitions and single-component context. The published friendship-graph and square-bouquet calculations treat the two boundary axes; finite triangle–square reports treat \((1,1)\) and \((2,1)\). The new theorem concerns exactly one common hub, all petals unfilled, and full strong matchings, not arbitrary cacti, filled clique complexes, ordinary Morse complexes, other cycle lengths, or equivariant representation lattices. Checker profiles are finite, while the proof is uniform.
