The numerator factors as a conjugate-root pair, yielding strictly positive compound-Poisson intensities at every count. The classical canonical monotonicity criterion is applied with its necessity and constructive sufficiency reproduced, not merely quoted. The uniform bound closes all positive weights at or above \(4/9\), while a growing-index asymptotic produces a strict negative canonical difference for every lower positive weight at sufficiently small rate. Endpoints \(p=0\), \(p=1\), \(q\in(0,1)\), and the positive-mixture restriction are accounted for. A separate exact-rational polynomial-division check confirms the first 13 canonical signs for one nontrivial example.

The canonical characterization of discrete self-decomposability is due to Steutel and van Harn (1979). Closest public research records concerning a Bernoulli-plus-negative-binomial convolution, a shape-five geometric/negative-binomial mixture, and a different modified Lindley count family do not imply the shape-three \(4/9\) frontier. Searches for alternate names, mixture equivalences and possible stronger results did not reveal an exact pre-existing classification. This does not prove global priority: other compound-Poisson mixture literature and inaccessible/unindexed manuscripts remain risks.

**Value — PASS.** The shape-three case is the first integer-shape extension of geometric/negative-binomial mixing in which the generating-function numerator has a conjugate-root pair. Its exact parameter boundary determines which observed overdispersed count marginals can be stationary under arbitrary Bernoulli-retention INAR dynamics, a prerequisite that mere infinite divisibility does not ensure. The isolated pure negative-binomial point and the need for a moving-index obstruction reveal a genuine structural distinction, rather than a low-order coefficient test or a fitted numerical cutoff.
