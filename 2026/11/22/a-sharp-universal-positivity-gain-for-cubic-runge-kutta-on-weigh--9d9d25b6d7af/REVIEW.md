# Review of the cubic RK3 weighted-bipartite positivity theorem

## Correctness — PASS

For every finite connected symmetric positive-weight bipartite network, direct expansion of the degree-three stability polynomial separates entries by graph distance. The only potentially negative entries are diagonal cubics and distance-two expressions; the latter have exactly one positive zero. Edge entries have a strictly positive quadratic factor because their discriminants are negative, and distance-three entries are positive. Each diagonal is a positive spectral mixture of strictly decreasing scalar cubic Taylor factors, with some positive-eigenvalue weight at every vertex; hence it decreases through exactly one positive root. This gives necessity, sufficiency and permanent loss beyond the minimum. The stochastic convex-combination identity gives the universal forward-Euler lower bound; the maximum-degree diagonal and the positive weighted degree moments prove a strict upper bound. Unit four-cycles and growing stars certify sharpness. Re-execution of the bundled exact-fraction checker passed 486 weighted graphs and 178505 entry checks. The proof, rather than finite checking, covers the universal quantifiers.

## Value — PASS

The theorem gives an exact local timestep test for heterogeneous heat/Markov diffusion using vertex degrees, squared weights and two-step paths, with no generic matrix-polynomial positivity search. It also establishes the best possible gain range over the classical forward-Euler limit, with explicit extremizing graph geometries. The distinction between diagonal-limited and distance-two-limited failures is important for deploying higher-order time stepping on irregular networks. It does not claim better accuracy or all-stage positivity. The universal constant is not an arbitrary tiny numeric improvement: it is the sharp network-independent limit of allowable cubic RK endpoint positivity.
