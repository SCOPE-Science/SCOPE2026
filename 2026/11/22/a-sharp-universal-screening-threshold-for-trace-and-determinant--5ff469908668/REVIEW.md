# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim follows from the exact Schur-complement error and complementary-minor determinant identities. The explicit continuants distinguish the two free-end tail blocks from Dirichlet interior blocks. Strict determinant-ratio ordering forces endpoint selection at all positive screening strengths. For screening at least one-half, two explicit inequalities place every tail trace increment strictly above every interior trace increment; strict discrete convexity then forces exactly balanced interior gaps. Below one-half, a finite eventual inequality shows the endpoint determinant maximizer can be improved in trace error. Exact rational enumeration checks the claimed complete equality cases through eleven vertices and reproduces the six-vertex counterexample. The infinite quantifiers are justified by the displayed analytical inequalities, not the finite code.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual risk

Belabbas–Wolfe (arXiv:0906.4582v1, Sections 3.2 and 4.1, complete accessible HTML read) supplies the general Nyström error problem but no screened path classification. The related 2010 Kriging prediction-and-entropy study concerns the stationary exponential-correlation precision, whose endpoint diagonal differs from the screened path's free boundary diagonal, so its equidistant-design statement does not imply this exact universal threshold. Its full text was not accessible, a genuine priority risk. Later complex Ornstein–Uhlenbeck optimal-design literature has related objective comparisons but different stationary kernels. The 2001 Mukherjee product-covariance path-sampling thesis gives general optimality conditions and is a significant possible conceptual predecessor. Its publicly available abstract does not report the precise discrete screened-Neumann universal threshold, but the complete thesis could not be inspected. Previously studied cycle designs lack free-end tails, and a one-landmark half-line threshold does not imply the all-budget finite-path classification. Searches of indexed mathematical findings found no direct matching statement; failure of retrieval alone cannot establish priority.

## Value — PASS

This is a structural all-budget classification for a standard discrete Green's kernel with a sharp parameter boundary. It shows exactly when maximizing sampled covariance volume guarantees a minimum trace-error Nyström design and supplies a counterexample for every weaker screening strength. The endpoint effect is precisely the feature that disappears in periodic or stationary-covariance design models. The result is useful as a benchmark for covariance landmark selection and as a warning against treating determinant and posterior-trace criteria as interchangeable.

## Limits and comparison risk

The theorem excludes one-landmark budgets, nonuniform weights or screening, noisy landmarks, and the exact classification of weak-screening trace optimizers. The full 2001 dissertation and 2010 Kriging study could not be inspected. The review establishes an internally reconstructed proof and evidenced originality assessment, not an independent priority or mathematical certificate.
