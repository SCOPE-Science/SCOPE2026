# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The pointwise three-pair dual potential reduces the continuum of joint laws to ten explicit inequalities valid for every \(t>1\). Two Latin-square distributions attain both branches. A complementary-slackness and two-way-margin argument proves uniqueness below the transition and forces the all-top mass above it. The symbolic algebra and finite exact-rational verifier support the proof; the finite verifier is not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority
The 2024 Bernard–Chen–Vanduffel zero-correlation study motivates the stricter pairwise-independence constraint but does not establish this variable-spacing ternary branch formula; a later dyadic-score study also concerns a different score class. The 2018 continuous-uniform multistochastic cost results and the 2023 unrestricted-dependence coskewness bounds have different feasible sets. Results allowing zero correlation without pairwise independence are also not equivalent. A stronger unpublished or differently formulated finite-table result remains possible.

## Value — PASS
This is the first nonbinary equal-mass discrete test family of a natural pairwise-independent triple-product optimization with a nontrivial sharp parameter transition, an exact dual certificate, two different extremal combinatorial couplings, and rigidity of a natural three-way upper-tail event. The phase change makes the distinction between pairwise and full dependence operational, rather than merely changing a numerical constant.

## Limitations and related literature
The conclusion is restricted to three equally weighted atoms and pairwise independence, and gives a maximum rather than both ends of the feasible coskewness range. Relevant comparisons are Gvozdev–Kolesnikov (2026; arXiv:2611.20365), Gladkov–Kolesnikov–Zimin (2018; arXiv:1803.10447), Bernard et al. (2023; arXiv:2303.17266), and Bernard–Chen–Vanduffel (2024; arXiv:2412.13362). Exact priority under alternate discrete-transport formulations is not guaranteed.
