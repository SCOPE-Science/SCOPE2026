For two pooled Poisson introduction pulses in a binary fluctuating environment, requiring at least two survivors creates exactly one budget threshold: every optimal release is concentrated at one date below it, while equal splitting alone wins above it, with exactly three tied allocations at the threshold. The sharp threshold solves \(\beta B\coth(\beta B/2)=1+\alpha B\) and is independent of environmental correlation whenever the two dates can differ in state; a one-survivor objective instead always favors equal splitting.
