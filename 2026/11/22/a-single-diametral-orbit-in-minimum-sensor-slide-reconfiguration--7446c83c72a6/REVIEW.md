# Review of the Nauru minimum sensor-slide theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The finite domain is precisely the 24 vertices of the explicit graph \(\operatorname{GP}(12,5)\), the \(2324\) candidate one-, two- and three-vertex subsets, and all \(165600\) unordered pairs among the 576 successful triples. Simultaneous bit-mask forcing and sequential set-based forcing use different graph constructions and agree on exactly 0, 0, and 576 successful subsets. All-pairs shortest-path searches independently agree on 6660 jump edges, 1152 slide edges, the complete distance spectra, and the sharp penalty of seven. A third exhaustive action check proves the explicit 144-element symmetry subgroup and the single orbit of 72 diametral pairs. The bipartition also has a direct parity proof. Verification is exhaustive for the stated finite graph, not an extrapolation.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical limitations.** The first-public 2022 reconfiguration framework treats generic jump and addition/removal rules and several standard graph families; its pertinent sections do not contain the Nauru triple configuration or slide metric. The 2022 cubic power-domination paper proves bounds, not exact Nauru configuration metrics. The closest published same-graph findings study edge-colouring Kempe moves, matching anti-forcing, and non-matching homology. Their states are edge-colourings, matchings, and simplicial faces, not three-sensor vertex subsets, and their statements do not entail the claimed jump/slide graphs or extremal-pair orbit. Full historical exclusion of differently indexed or unpublished work is impossible; that residual risk is explicit.

## Value
**PASS.** This is an exact, reproducible classification for the canonical arc-transitive cubic graph on 24 vertices. The unusual fact is not just a finite monitoring optimum: the minimum sensor-slide graph is connected, four-regular and bipartite, yet has a unique automorphism orbit of 72 worst-case relocation pairs, with a sharp seven-move locality surcharge. The result gives a natural benchmark for optimum-preserving relocation and symmetry analysis and separates reachability from shortest-path obstruction. It does not claim a general-family advance.
