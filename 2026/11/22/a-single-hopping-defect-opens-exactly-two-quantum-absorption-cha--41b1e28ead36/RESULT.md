# A single hopping defect opens exactly two quantum absorption channels in complete bipartite networks

## Finding
For every pair of integers \(m,n\ge3\), changing one hopping amplitude in a complete bipartite coherent transport network from the common value produces a rank jump of exactly two in the one-site absorber's bright space. This changes the uniformly mixed initial-state capture fraction discontinuously from \(3/(m+n)\) to \(5/(m+n)\) for arbitrarily small nonzero defects. The complete, exact sitewise capture probabilities and projectors are given below.

## Assumptions and scope
Take orthonormal single-particle states \(|a_i\rangle\), \(0\le i<m\), and \(|b_j\rangle\), \(0\le j<n\). Let \(H\) be the symmetric adjacency Hamiltonian with \(\langle a_i|H|b_j\rangle=J\) for all cross-part pairs other than \((i,j)=(0,0)\), for which the hopping is \(Jw\), where \(J,w>0\). Every within-part matrix element vanishes. Fix a sole irreversible absorber at \(a_1\) with \(\kappa>0\):
\[
G=-\mathrm{i}H-\frac{\kappa}{2}|a_1\rangle\langle a_1|,
\qquad
p_{\rm abs}(\psi)=1-\lim_{t\to\infty}\|e^{tG}\psi\|^2.
\]
For every normalized pure initial state, or density operator \(\rho\) with \(p_{\rm abs}(\rho)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho P_w)\), the exact orthogonal bright projector is
\[
P_1=|a_1\rangle\langle a_1|+
\frac{|u_A\rangle\langle u_A|}{m-1}+
\frac{|u_B\rangle\langle u_B|}{n},
\quad
u_A=\sum_{i\ne1}|a_i\rangle,\quad u_B=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|b_j\rangle,
\]
and, for every \(w\ne1\),
\[
P_w=|a_0\rangle\langle a_0|+|a_1\rangle\langle a_1|+
\frac{|r_A\rangle\langle r_A|}{m-2}+
|b_0\rangle\langle b_0|+
\frac{|r_B\rangle\langle r_B|}{n-1},
\]
where \(r_A=\sum_{i=2}^{m-1}|a_i\rangle\), and \(r_B=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|b_j\rangle\). All terms in each displayed sum are mutually orthogonal projectors. The rank is respectively \(3\) or \(5\).

The eventual absorption probabilities from single-vertex initial states are, for \(w=1\): \(1\) at \(a_1\), \(1/(m-1)\) at every other \(A\)-vertex, and \(1/n\) at every \(B\)-vertex. For \(w\ne1\): \(1\) at \(a_0,a_1,b_0\); \(1/(m-2)\) at every remaining \(A\)-vertex; and \(1/(n-1)\) at every remaining \(B\)-vertex. Consequently the average over the maximally mixed initial state is \(3/(m+n)\) for \(w=1\) and \(5/(m+n)\) otherwise.

## Proof
For any finite Hermitian \(H\), the dark subspace orthogonal to the absorber's cyclic span \(\mathcal K=\operatorname{span}\{H^j|a_1\rangle:j\ge0\}\) is \(H\)-invariant and undergoes unitary norm-preserving evolution under \(G\). Its orthogonal complement \(\mathcal K\) is also invariant. On \(\mathcal K\), no eigenvector of \(G\) can have an imaginary-axis eigenvalue: the real part of its eigenvalue forces zero absorber amplitude, and the remaining eigenvalue equation makes it an \(H\)-eigenvector orthogonal to \(a_1\), contradicting cyclicity. Since the space is finite-dimensional, every mode on \(\mathcal K\) decays in norm. Therefore \(p_{\rm abs}(\psi)=\|P_{\mathcal K}\psi\|^2\), regardless of \(\kappa>0\).

For \(w=1\), the biadjacency block is the all-ones matrix of rank one. Direct application of \(H\) gives precisely the three-dimensional span of \(|a_1\rangle\), \(|u_A\rangle\), and \(|u_B\rangle\), proving the first formula.

For \(w\ne1\), the five-dimensional orbit-sum subspace
\[
U=\operatorname{span}\{ |a_0\rangle, |a_1\rangle, |r_A\rangle, |b_0\rangle, |r_B\rangle\}
\]
is invariant under \(H\) and contains the sink. The orthogonal complement consists of zero-sum vectors on the two ordinary-vertex groups; it is annihilated by \(H\) and is dark. The nonzero left-singular block on \(U\), in the orthonormal left basis \(a_0\) and \((a_1+r_A)/\sqrt{m-1}\), has Gram matrix (with \(J^2\) removed)
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
w^2+n-1 & \sqrt{m-1}(w+n-1)\\
\sqrt{m-1}(w+n-1)&n(m-1)
\end{pmatrix},
\quad \det M=(m-1)(n-1)(w-1)^2.
\]
Its positive determinant for \(w\ne1\) gives two nonzero singular values. The strictly positive off-diagonal entry makes them distinct, and each left singular vector has nonzero overlap with \(|a_1\rangle\). The remaining one-dimensional left kernel in \(U\) is generated by \(|a_1\rangle-|r_A\rangle/(m-2)\), which also overlaps the sink. Thus the five distinct energy eigenspaces on \(U\) (four nonzero energies and one zero) are all seen by \(|a_1\rangle\). Its cyclic span is exactly \(U\). Orthogonalizing the five orbit sums yields the claimed projector \(P_w\). Taking its diagonal or trace proves all sitewise and averaged capture assertions.

## Verification
The standalone, dependency-free Python 3 script `verify.py` independently constructs every rational weighted adjacency and all Krylov vectors, then compares the exact orthogonal projectors by rational row reduction. It checks \(m,n\in\{3,4,5,6\}\) and \(w\in\{1,1/2,3/2,2\}\): 64 exact cases. Run `python3 verify.py`; the expected marker is `EXACT_BRIGHT_PROJECTOR_CHECKS_PASSED 64`. This finite test is supplementary: the determinant-and-cyclicity argument above proves the result for all parameters in scope.

## Relationship to prior work
The bright/dark Krylov criterion and its relationship to trapping are established in quantum-transport literature, including Krapivsky, Luck and Mallick (2014) and Razzoli, Paris and Bordone (2021). Razzoli and coauthors give the unweighted complete-bipartite capture law, which matches the \(w=1\) case and is not claimed as new. Bassereh and coauthors (2015) demonstrate qualitative improvement of coherent capture through a single-edge perturbation of a *complete* network; the new assertion here is a distinct quantitative, all-size, exact projector and rank-two defect law for a *complete bipartite* network. Hillery and coauthors (2012) examine discrete-time scattering walks on complete bipartite graphs with an extra within-part connection, not this across-part weighted-bond lossy continuous-time model. Exact equivalence to an unlocated historical result cannot be excluded.

## Limitations
This is a finite single-particle, single-absorber, zero-dephasing, adjacency-Hamiltonian statement. The jump refers to the \(t\to\infty\) capture probability for each fixed defect: the limits of time and defect strength need not commute; no finite-time discontinuity is asserted. Nothing here implies an infinite-volume thermodynamic transition, or the same formula for multiple defect bonds, arbitrary site energies or alternative dissipators. The proof does not establish historical priority beyond the inspected literature. Independent validation has not been performed.
