# Scientific review: near-minimal random widths of two perpendicular disks

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is precisely scoped to two unit filled disks in perpendicular planes, symmetric separation along their common line, Haar-uniform caliper normals, and three distinct limiting regimes. The exact support expression and its normalization were independently checked against direct two-disk support maximization. The equality set of four oriented minimizing normals follows from a global lower bound, not a numerical search. The uniform local expansion yields the two separate scalings, and a compactness argument licenses the probability and conditional-law limits. The crossover constant comes from exact area of its tangent domain. A 1,071-assertion deterministic checker confirms independent direct-width and exact-quadrature diagnostics; finite tests alone are not proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with historical caveat
The earliest contextual source, Finch (2012), concerns the same convex hulls and their mean widths. Bäsel (2016) evaluates the single oloid mean width, not the Haar lower endpoint. A published mean-width derivative result already contains the complete support identity used as an input here; that identity and average-width conclusions are therefore **not** claimed as new. A separately published three-coordinate-disk random width result treats another, centrally generated body and has no two-disk separation parameter. The claimed addition is the sharp *distributional* lower endpoint at each fixed separation, the discontinuity of its exponent at zero, the full crossover profile, and the conditional angular limit. These require a two-scale local normal-form analysis not stated in inspected primary material. Residual priority risk includes unindexed classical oloid width-distribution calculations and any implicit equivalent tail derived elsewhere.

## Value — PASS
The oloid is an established geometric object, and random caliper widths are directly motivated geometric measurements beyond their mean. The sharp distinction between exponent \(2\) for every positive separation and exponent \(3/2\) at coincident centers identifies an unstable endpoint geometry. The explicit one-parameter matching law and conditional rare-direction shape resolve the transition, providing useful quantitative benchmarks for integral geometry and orientation sampling rather than a routine single-parameter numeric increment.

## Relationship and limitations
Closest comparisons: Finch, *Convex Hull of Two Orthogonal Disks*, arXiv:1211.4514; Bäsel, *The mean width of the oloid and integral geometric applications of it*, arXiv:1604.07245; Dirnböck and Stachel, *The Development of the Oloid*, Journal for Geometry and Graphics (1997); *A one-dimensional derivative law for orthogonal-disk mean width* (2026); and *Exact random widths of the orthogonal three-disk hull and sharp hyperdisk endpoint laws* (2026). The theorem does not provide the full finite-separation width distribution, simultaneous arbitrary-path asymptotic uniformity, or noisy-data inference. A definitive historical-priority check would require broader specialist literature scrutiny.
