# Scientific review: random centered parallelograms in circular lenses

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem fixes the sample distribution (two independent directions uniformly distributed modulo \(\pi\)), the radial function, and the normalization \(M_c=2(1-c)\sqrt{1-c^2}\). The enclosing-ellipse inequality is an exact algebraic identity with equality at four axial contacts for positive center separation. It proves that the two maximizing *ordered* angle pairs exhaust the equality cases. The local angular expansion has a strictly positive quadratic coefficient in one direction and a strictly positive absolute-value coefficient in the other; integrating the resulting mixed-parabolic region gives the \(3/2\)-power law and its coefficient. The circle case follows from a uniform difference angle. The critical separation window is derived by uniform first-order radial expansion and bounded convergence. Endpoint and conditioning claims are supported by the same arguments. The accompanying standalone standard-library numerical checker passed 66,052 deterministic diagnostics; those finite tests supplement rather than replace the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
Kratky (1978) computes disk-intersection areas. Kallus (2013) considers maximal inscribed triangles in convex domains, and Barba et al. (2015) study extreme polygons sampled on circles. The published *Exact John and Löwner ellipsoids of equal-ball lenses in every dimension* contains the crucial enclosing ellipse and its contact set, which are treated as **known**, not as the original claim. The published lens maximal-triangle theorem is also distinct. Searches for fixed-center angle-uniform random inscribed parallelograms, equivalent area/determinant formulations, extremal-ellipse implications, probability-tail exponents, and coalescing directional maxima found no source covering the combined precise distributional endpoint, critical crossover, and marked conditional law. Historical references under a different sampling nomenclature could exist; the literature check does not establish exclusive priority.

## Value — PASS
Centrally symmetric parallelograms are natural four-vertex affine approximants of a centrally symmetric convex lens. The angle-uniform random construction is geometrically canonical and its near-extremal area measures how restrictive the optimal ellipse's contacts are. At any positive separation, near-optimal sampled directions concentrate on two isolated configurations with scales \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon\); for the disk, the maximizing directions have an entire rotational degree of freedom. The result resolves this geometric change quantitatively, including the exact separation-to-deficit crossover, and gives a reproducible conditional geometry rather than only a new finite constant.

## Limitations and closest coverage
No assertion is made about arclength-uniform or area-uniform point sampling, unequal disks, all arbitrary noncentered random parallelograms, noisy observations, or full historical priority. The exact deterministic optimal ellipse and its contacts are prior coverage. The fixed-positive-separation estimate is not uniform at zero separation, while the stated crossover explicitly handles finite \(c/\varepsilon\).
