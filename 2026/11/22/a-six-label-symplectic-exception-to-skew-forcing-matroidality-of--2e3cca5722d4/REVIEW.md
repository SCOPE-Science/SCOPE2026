# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof identifies a matroid in each positive case through *all* its circuit neighborhoods (three complementary four-circuits for \(T_4\); fifteen projective hyperplane complements for \(T_6\)) and verifies the closure identity using the standard circuit rule. In every excluded order the given initially unfilled edge graph is stalled, filling one prescribed edge completes forcing, and filling another prescribed edge leaves the first unfilled. The degree identity checks these conditions for the infinite double-star family. All subset closures were independently compared with binary-rank dual-matroid closure for the fifteen-vertex exceptional case.

**Originality — PASS, provisional as to historical priority.** Hall–Pfeil–Taylor (2026, arXiv:2611.15741v1) develops matroidality of skew zero forcing and investigates whether *cycle matroids of graphs* are realizable on some possibly different graph; that statement does not determine the closure of the *specific line graph* \(L(K_n)\). Cooper–Fickes (2023, arXiv:2303.17419v1) establishes fundamental SZF closure results for other graph families. A published record about corank-two SZF matroids covers the small complete-multipartite cases but neither implies the corank-four projective exception nor settles the negative orders. Searches for triangular-graph SZF matroid exchange, projective duals and related aliases did not uncover the claimed classification. An indexed search cannot exclude all earlier equivalent work.

**Value — PASS.** The complete all-order classification answers a natural structural question for a classical family of line graphs, explains why a nonuniform binary projective matroid occurs at exactly six labels, and supplies infinite explicit nonmatroidality witnesses. The exceptional minimum-set count \(840\) follows without a table lookup. This is more than an isolated finite graph calculation.

**Limitations and residual risks.** The identification relies on the conventional skew rule allowing either color to force, without loops or multiplicities. Unindexed matroid and algebraic-combinatorics literature might contain the same symplectic identification under alternative terminology. Finite verification is not an infinite proof and no external mathematical audit has occurred.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
