# A square-zero commutator ideal for ray Lipschitz multipliers and its sharp oscillatory witnesses

## Finding

Let \(\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\). On the rooted ray, let \(C\) be parent composition, \((Cf)(0)=f(0)\) and \((Cf)(n)=f(n-1)\) for \(n\ge1\). For \(|q|=1\), define \(\psi_q(0)=0\), \(\psi_q(n)=q^n/n\) for \(n\ge1\), and let \(M_q f=\psi_q f\).

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the norm-closed unital operator algebra generated by \(C\) and **all bounded multiplication operators** on either of these Lipschitz spaces. In its Calkin image \(\pi(\mathcal A)\), let \(\mathcal J\) be the closed two-sided ideal generated by the commutators \(\pi([M_\psi,C])\). Then
\[
 \boxed{\ \mathcal J^2=\{0\},\quad \mathcal J\ne\{0\},\quad \pi(\mathcal A)/\mathcal J\ \text{is commutative}.\ }
\]
This is a square-zero extension of a commutative operator algebra even though the Calkin image itself is noncommutative. The following critical family gives exact nonzero witnesses in \(\mathcal J\).

For every integer \(m\ge1\), define the single higher commutator family
\[
 B_m(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^j\binom mj C^jM_qC^{m-j}.
\]
On **both** the full and little complex Lipschitz spaces of the ray, with the root-plus-edge-supremum norm, these operators are bounded and satisfy
\[
 \boxed{\ \|B_m(q)\|_{\mathrm e}=|1-q|^{m+1},\qquad B_m(q)^2\ \text{is compact}.\ }
\]
Consequently, for \(q\ne1\), the image of \(B_m(q)\) in either Calkin algebra is a **nonzero element of nilpotency index exactly two**. At \(q=1\), the commutators themselves are compact.

The exponent one is forced by the multiplier boundary: for \(q\ne1\) and \(\psi_{q,\alpha}(n)=q^n n^{-\alpha}\) for \(n\ge1\), with \(\psi_{q,\alpha}(0)=0\) and \(\alpha>0\), multiplication is bounded precisely for \(\alpha\ge1\). For \(\alpha>1\) it is compact, so all the corresponding commutators are compact. Thus \(\alpha=1\) is the unique admissible positive decay exponent in this family yielding the noncompact square-zero Calkin phenomenon.

## Assumptions and scope

The full Lipschitz Banach space \(\mathcal L\) is the set of complex sequences \(f=(f(n))_{n\ge0}\) satisfying
\[
 \|f\|_{\mathcal L}=|f(0)|+\sup_{n\ge1}|f(n)-f(n-1)|<\infty.
\]
The little space \(\mathcal L_0\) consists of the sequences in \(\mathcal L\) with \(f(n)-f(n-1)\to0\), carrying the same norm. The essential norm is the operator-norm distance to compact operators **on the same specified space**. The Calkin algebra is the quotient of its bounded operators by the compact ideal. The operator algebra \(\mathcal A\) and its closed Calkin commutator ideal are defined in Finding. No Hilbert-space norm, alternative max-norm, weighted tree or arbitrary graph is claimed.

## Proof

**1. Boundedness and the critical exponent.** Put \(x_n=f(n)-f(n-1)\), so \(f(n)=f(0)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_k\). The coordinate map \(D:f\mapsto(f(0),(x_n)_{n\ge1})\) is an isometry onto \(\mathbb C\oplus_1\ell_\infty\), and maps \(\mathcal L_0\) onto \(\mathbb C\oplus_1c_0\). Write \(\Delta a_n=a_n-a_{n-1}\). Then
\[
 \Delta(\psi f)_n=\psi_nx_n+(\Delta\psi_n)\left(f(0)+\sum_{k<n}x_k\right).
\]
For the indicated symbols \(\psi_{q,\alpha}\), boundedness follows whenever \(\alpha\ge1\) from \(\sup_n|\psi_{q,\alpha}(n)|<\infty\) and \(\sup_n n|\Delta\psi_{q,\alpha}(n)|<\infty\). For \(x\in c_0\), the Cesàro mean \(n^{-1}\sum_{k<n}x_k\to0\), so this multiplier also preserves the little space. Conversely, for \(q\ne1\) and \(0<\alpha<1\), taking \(f(n)=n\) gives an unbounded output edge difference, whose leading term is \((1-q^{-1})q^n n^{1-\alpha}\). For \(\alpha>1\) the multiplier's derivative row \(\ell_1\)-norm is \(O(n^{1-\alpha})\to0\); finite-output-row truncations therefore converge to it in norm. It is compact on both spaces.

**2. The universal commutator ideal.** A general bounded multiplier \(M_\varphi\) has \(\sup_n|\varphi_n|<\infty\) and \(\sup_n n|\Delta\varphi_n|<\infty\): these follow by testing separately the single-edge increment and the \(n-1\) earlier aligned increments in the derivative-row formula above. Conversely, the same row formula implies boundedness under those two conditions, and the Cesàro argument proves invariance of the little space.

Call a multiplier symbol **first-order decaying** if \(a_n=O(1/n)\). Such a symbol is automatically a bounded multiplier because \(\Delta a_n=O(1/n)\). A product of two first-order decaying symbols satisfies \(a_nb_n=O(1/n^2)\), hence the corresponding multiplier is compact: its derivative-row norm is bounded by \(|a_nb_n|+(n-1)|\Delta(ab)_n|=O(1/n)\) in high rows. The parent-shift identity \(CM_\varphi=M_{\varphi\circ\pi}C\) shows every finite operator word in the generators is a bounded multiplier followed by a nonnegative power of \(C\). Shifting any bounded multiplier by finitely many parent steps preserves boundedness, and shifting a first-order decaying symbol preserves its decay.

Thus any finite product containing one commutator \([M_\varphi,C]=M_{\varphi-\varphi\circ\pi}C\) is a finite sum of operators \(M_aC^k\) with first-order decaying \(a\). A product containing **two** commutators is therefore compact, since the two decaying coefficients multiply to \(O(1/n^2)\). This property survives norm closure, giving \(\mathcal J^2=\{0\}\). Since the generators commute modulo \(\mathcal J\), the quotient \(\pi(\mathcal A)/\mathcal J\) is commutative. Nontriviality of the ideal follows from Step 4's critical witness (below).

**3. Finite-difference commutator identity.** For \(m\ge1\) and \(n\ge m\), expanding the binomial commutator and using \((C^jf)(n)=f(\max\{n-j,0\})\) yields
\[
 (B_m(q)f)(n)=\Delta^m\psi_q(n)\,f(n-m).
\]
Consequently, for \(n>m\),
\[
 \Delta(B_m(q)f)_n=\Delta^m\psi_q(n)x_{n-m}
 +\Delta^{m+1}\psi_q(n)\left(f(0)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-m-1}x_k\right).
\]
All finitely many exceptional initial output rows are finite-rank contributions. Since \(m\) is fixed and \(|q|=1\), for \(n\to\infty\)
\[
 \Delta^j\psi_q(n)=\frac{q^n(1-q^{-1})^j}{n}+O_j(n^{-2}),\qquad j\ge0.
\]
Define \((Hx)_n=n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}x_k\), \((U_qx)_n=q^nx_n\), and \(a_m=(1-q^{-1})^{m+1}\). In the increment coordinates, the row of \(B_m(q)-a_mU_qH\) restricted to the \(\ell_\infty\) (or \(c_0\)) summand has absolute coefficient sum \(O_m(n^{-1})\). Indeed, the \(n-m-1\) early coefficients each differ by \(O_m(n^{-2})\); at most \(m\) remaining coefficients are \(O_m(n^{-1})\). The root scalar gives only a rank-one input contribution. Hence
\[
 \boxed{\ B_m(q)\equiv a_mU_qH\pmod{\mathcal K}\ }
\]
under either increment-coordinate identification. This also proves boundedness of every commutator.

**4. Exact essential norm of the Hardy model.** Its \(n\)-th row is \((q^n/n,\ldots,q^n/n,0,\ldots)\), with \(n-1\) nonzero entries, so its \(\ell_1\)-norm is \((n-1)/n\to1\). Truncating finitely many output rows gives \(\|U_qH\|_{\mathrm e}\le1\). For the reverse inequality take any compact \(K\) on either increment space; compactness of \(K^*\) gives a norm-convergent subsequence of the output-coordinate functionals \(K^*e_n^*\). Restricting their limit to \(c_0\) yields an \(\ell_1\)-vector \(a\). The rows of \(U_qH\) converge pointwise to zero on every fixed coordinate, and their \(\ell_1\)-norms tend to one. Splitting coordinates into a fixed finite set containing almost all of \(a\) and its complement proves \(\liminf\|r_n-a\|_1\ge1\). Thus \(\|U_qH-K\|\ge1\). This works for operators on \(\ell_\infty\) as well, because restriction to its \(c_0\) subspace controls the norm. Therefore \(\|U_qH\|_{\mathrm e}=1\) on both spaces and \(\|B_m(q)\|_{\mathrm e}=|a_m|=|1-q|^{m+1}\).

**5. Cancellation and square-compactness.** For \(q\ne1\), the \(n\)-th row of \(HU_qH\) has coefficient at \(x_k\), \(k<n-1\),
\[
 b_{nk}=\frac1n\sum_{j=k+1}^{n-1}\frac{q^j}{j}.
\]
Abel summation and the bounded geometric partial sums imply \(\left|\sum_{j=k+1}^{n-1}q^j/j\right|\le C_q/(k+1)\). Hence
\[
 \sup_{\|x\|_\infty\le1}|(HU_qHx)_n|
 \le\frac{C_q}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\frac1{k+1}
 =O_q\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Finite-output-row truncations converge in operator norm, so \(HU_qH\) is compact on both \(c_0\) and \(\ell_\infty\). It follows that \((U_qH)^2=U_q(HU_qH)\) is compact. The congruence in Step 2 proves \(B_m(q)^2\) compact. At \(q=1\) the scalar \(a_m=0\), so Step 2 already shows \(B_m(1)\) compact. For \(q\ne1\), Step 3 shows its Calkin class is nonzero, yielding exact nilpotency index two.

The included `verify.py` checks the finite commutator identity and coordinate-row norm algebra over exact rational inputs for \(1\le m\le3\) on truncated rays, and tests finite Abel-sum bounds for alternating and quarter-turn modulations. The observed checker output is `RAY_CALKIN_COMMUTATOR_CHECK_OK 17484`. ## Relationship to prior work

Kong and Liu (arXiv:2611.11737v1, submitted 9 November 2026) classify Fredholm weighted composition operators on the Lipschitz space of a tree; parent composition and multiplication are natural special operators, but their results do not state a Calkin nilpotence law for commutators. Allen, Colonna and Easley (arXiv:2207.12616v1) characterize weighted Lipschitz multipliers and provide two-sided essential-norm estimates, rather than the cancellation phenomenon of a squared commutator. The square-zero ideal theorem is a claim about the whole norm-closed algebra, not simply the norm of one multiplier. The sharp multiplier essential-norm formulas and a periodic-modulation Cesàro square-zero example available in other operator settings are important related techniques; they do not directly imply this full/little Lipschitz Calkin statement for all unimodular phases. Square-zero Calkin elements themselves are not asserted to be new in general.

## Limitations

Only the unweighted rooted ray, specified root-plus-supremum norm, unimodular \(q\), and indicated critical-decay symbols are classified. The result does not determine singular values or Schatten membership (these Banach spaces are not Hilbert spaces). Literature overlap with an older endpoint Hardy-modulation theorem that covers the same \(\ell_\infty\)/\(c_0\) operator would remove originality; source access and semantic comparisons are recorded separately. The proof is analytical, not an independent formal certification; the algebraic square-zero ideal is an assertion about the indicated norm-closed operator algebra only.
