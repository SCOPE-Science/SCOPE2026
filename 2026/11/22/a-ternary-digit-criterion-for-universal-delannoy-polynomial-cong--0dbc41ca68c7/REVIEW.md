# Scientific review: universal Delannoy-polynomial congruences modulo nine

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns all nonnegative indices and every integer polynomial argument, with an exact ternary-language criterion and finite-block count. The proof first uses the polynomial Lucas identity to rule out digit one. For admissible indices it reduces all nine argument residues to a single Legendre constant term \(A_m=(-1)^m4^{-m}\binom{2m}m\), applies an all-index binomial modulo-nine scaling identity and exact admissible-digit recurrences, and evaluates a six-state counting transfer by rational functions. The detailed formulas in RESULT.md prove unrestricted index and digit-length quantifiers. A self-contained verifier passed 9,282 exact diagnostic assertions, including 6,561 indices times all nine arguments modulo nine. Those finite checks support but do not replace the proof. Rational denominators are prime-to-three units, and the universal congruence is not conflated with coefficientwise polynomial congruence.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest full mathematical record, *A noncyclic base-13 fixed-divisor spectrum for Delannoy polynomials*, establishes the universal-in-argument problem only modulo the **first** power of an odd prime, including the necessary digits zero/two at three. It does not establish the adjacent-digit residue \(c(u)\), all-values congruence modulo nine, or the exact \(N_r\) count. Guo (arXiv:1412.7724), Sun (arXiv:1101.5386), and Pan (arXiv:1508.02046) study Delannoy/Legendre congruences but not this all-index fixed-divisor mod-nine classification. Rowland–Yassawi (arXiv:1310.8635) proves a broad automaticity framework; its general computability theorem is not itself an exact six-state classification of the present object. A complete \(3\)-adic *value image* for the central Delannoy sequence \(D_n(1)\) has a different quantifier. Searches of published records and primary literature did not reveal a statement covering the new congruence and exact count. This is not an exhaustive historical-priority certification; an unstated specialization of a general automatic congruence remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is the first natural prime-square layer of the universal fixed-divisor question for Delannoy polynomials, not an arbitrary numerical residue slice. At the first prime, the admissible set is described by independent forbidden digits, whereas the prime square creates a **neighbor interaction** in the input digit language. The theorem provides a compact necessary-and-sufficient test for every index, a complete simultaneous argument-value law, and an unexpectedly bounded, exact six-periodic fluctuation in the count below each power of three. This is useful to higher-order divisibility investigations of these classical polynomials even without a full prime-power classification.

## Literature comparisons and limitations
The prime-level fixed-divisor result, the complete ternary value image, and the cubic-modulus sum-of-fourth-powers congruence were read in full; the first is the nearest predecessor, the second treats one argument with arbitrary index, and the third concerns weighted polynomial sums. Full-text PDF sections of Sun 2011, Guo 2014, Pan 2015, and Rowland–Yassawi 2013 were examined. The theorem treats only modulus nine, not all \(3^k\), and does not compute the entire fixed divisor. Earlier equivalent results may exist outside the examined sources. The proof and verifier are available in RESULT.md and verify.py.
