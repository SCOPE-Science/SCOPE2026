With equal read budgets, sequencing two sites separately can improve detection of **every taxon individually** yet be worse than 50–50 pooling at detecting **all three taxa together**. For a three-taxon two-site family, the joint advantage of pooling changes sign exactly when the shared taxon's first-site fraction crosses \(1/3\); this cannot occur with fewer than three taxa, and pooling never beats the best freely optimized split.
