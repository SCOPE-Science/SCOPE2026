# A topological shattering transition in connected maximum-independent-set extremizers

## Finding
For integers \(a\ge3\) and \(n>2a\), let \(F(n,a)\) be the connected graph of Mohr and Rautenbach obtained from \(a\) balanced disjoint cliques by joining one distinguished vertex \(x\) of a largest clique to one distinguished vertex in each other clique [1]. This is the unique (up to isomorphism) connected \(n\)-vertex graph with independence number \(a\) maximizing the number of *maximum-cardinality* independent sets [1]. Let \(\mathcal C(n,a)\) be its family of maximum independent sets as Boolean vertex-membership concepts, and let \(\Delta(n,a)\) be the simplicial complex of shattered query supports.

**Theorem (all \(n>2a\)).** Write the clique orders as \(r_0,r_1,\ldots,r_{a-1}\), with \(r_0=\lceil n/a\rceil\) and the other orders equal to \(\lfloor n/a\rfloor\) or \(\lceil n/a\rceil\). Let \(B_0\) contain \(x\), and let \(y_i\in B_i\) be its unique neighbor outside \(B_0\), for \(1\le i<a\). Then a support \(S\) is shattered if and only if (i) it contains at most one vertex from each \(B_i\), and (ii) when \(x\in S\), every other queried vertex lies in \(B_i\setminus\{y_i\}\) for a block with \(r_i\ge3\). In particular, the entire shattered-support polynomial is
\[
\Phi_{n,a}(z)=\bigl(1+(r_0-1)z\bigr)\prod_{i=1}^{a-1}(1+r_i z)
+z\prod_{\substack{1\le i<a\\r_i\ge3}}\bigl(1+(r_i-1)z\bigr).
\]
The empty product is one. For every such \(n,a\), both \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal C(n,a))\) and \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal C(n,a))\) equal \(a\).

**Topology and the sharp transition.** For \(2a<n<3a\), put \(t=n-2a\), so \(1\le t<a\). Then
\[
\Phi_{n,a}(z)=(1+2z)^{a-t+1}(1+3z)^{t-1}+z(1+2z)^{t-1},
\qquad
|\Delta(n,a)|\simeq\Bigl(\bigvee^{2^{t-1}}S^{a-1}\Bigr)\vee S^{t-1}.
\]
Here \(S^0\) and its wedge have their usual pointed interpretation, so at \(t=1\) the complex has two connected components, one equivalent to \(S^{a-1}\) and one isolated point. In this interval the facets have **exactly** cardinalities \(a\) and \(t\). In particular, the reduced integral homology has two nonzero degrees, \(a-1\) and \(t-1\), each with the indicated free ranks (these degrees are distinct because \(t<a\)).

For \(n\ge3a\), every clique has at least three vertices. The complex is pure with every facet having cardinality \(a\), and
\[
|\Delta(n,a)|\simeq\bigvee^{\beta_{n,a}}S^{a-1},\qquad
\beta_{n,a}=(r_0-2)\prod_{i=1}^{a-1}(r_i-1)+\prod_{i=1}^{a-1}(r_i-2).
\]
Thus \(n=3a\) is the **exact purity threshold** within the domain \(n>2a\); for \(2a<n<3a\) the mixed-dimensional sphere wedge is intrinsic. The lower-boundary case \(n=2a\) is already covered by the earlier fully-whiskered-star theorem [2] and is not claimed as a new special case here.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs and set families are finite, simple and labeled for enumeration; isomorphism is used only to identify the extremal graph. The source theorem [1] supplies the extremal-graph characterization for \(a\ge3\) and \(n\ge2a\). A maximum independent set has exactly \(a\) vertices, in contrast to an inclusion-maximal independent set. A support \(S\subseteq V(F(n,a))\) is shattered if all \(2^{|S|}\) possible membership-label patterns occur as traces of members of \(\mathcal C(n,a)\), including the empty trace. The shattered supports form an abstract simplicial complex \(\Delta(n,a)\) and \(\Phi_{n,a}(z)=\sum_{S\in\Delta(n,a)}z^{|S|}\). The realization \(|\Delta|\) denotes its geometric realization. Littlestone dimension is the maximum depth of a complete binary adaptive query tree, with each root-to-leaf label sequence realized by some concept.

For later use, a finite discrete space of \(d\) points is denoted \(D_d\), and a join is denoted \(*\). The wedge-of-spheres homotopy assertions are integral: no field coefficients or unproved torsion assumptions are used.

## Proof
**1. Exact optimizer family.** The \(a\) clique blocks form a partition and any independent set meets each block at most once. Conversely, choose one non-\(x\) vertex from \(B_0\) and an arbitrary vertex from every other clique. The resulting independent transversal has cardinality \(a\); hence the independence number is exactly \(a\). Every maximum independent set is a transversal and its only additional constraint is to avoid selecting both \(x\) and any connector \(y_i\). This is an exact equivalence, not merely an enumeration bound. Since \(n>2a\), the largest block has \(r_0\ge3\), hence at least two vertices of \(B_0\setminus\{x\}\).

**2. Necessary support conditions.** Two queried vertices of one clique can never both have positive membership labels in a maximum independent set; therefore any shattered support has at most one queried vertex in each clique. If a shattered support contains \(x\), it cannot contain \(y_i\), since the joint-positive pattern is prohibited by an edge. Suppose that it contains \(x\) and a vertex \(v\in B_i\setminus\{y_i\}\) in a two-vertex block. In the pattern \(x=1,v=0\), every maximum independent set selecting \(x\) must choose the only safe vertex of \(B_i\), namely \(v\); this pattern is impossible. Thus all vertices other than \(x\) in a support containing \(x\) must be safe vertices of blocks of order at least three.

**3. Sufficiency.** Let \(S\) satisfy both conditions, and fix arbitrary requested labels. If \(x\notin S\), choose in \(B_0\) a non-\(x\) vertex matching the requested label at its at most one query. For a negative label there, an alternative non-\(x\) vertex exists because \(r_0\ge3\). Select in each remaining block a vertex matching its one optional query; any such choice is compatible with the selected non-\(x\) vertex. If \(x\in S\) but its requested label is zero, choose any non-\(x\) vertex in \(B_0\) and proceed identically. If \(x\in S\) and its requested label is one, choose \(x\) in \(B_0\). In every other block avoid \(y_i\); if a safe vertex \(v\) is queried, choose \(v\) when its label is one, and a distinct safe vertex when its label is zero. Such a distinct safe vertex exists because the block has at least three vertices. Every zero-or-one pattern is therefore realized, proving the criterion.

**4. Polynomial and learning dimensions.** Supports avoiding \(x\) are unrestricted partial transversals of \(B_0\setminus\{x\},B_1,\ldots,B_{a-1}\), giving the first product of the displayed polynomial. Supports containing \(x\) have the second product, with only safe vertices in blocks of order at least three. Supports counted by these two products are disjoint. A partial transversal choosing one non-\(x\) vertex of \(B_0\) and one vertex of every other block is shattered and has \(a\) vertices, while no shattered set can exceed \(a\). Thus VC dimension equals \(a\). A full nonadaptive decision tree on those \(a\) queries proves \(\operatorname{Ldim}\ge a\). In any full adaptive decision tree, follow the all-positive branch. Queries along this branch must be distinct: repeating a query would make the opposite child unrealizable. A concept realizing its leaf must contain every queried vertex, so the branch has length at most \(a\). This yields \(\operatorname{Ldim}\le a\).

**5. Homotopy decomposition.** Let \(A\) be the subcomplex of supports not containing \(x\). It is the join
\[
A=D_{r_0-1}*D_{r_1}*\cdots*D_{r_{a-1}}.
\]
Let \(K=\bigast_{i:r_i\ge3}D_{r_i-1}\), where the vertices in each factor are the safe vertices \(B_i\setminus\{y_i\}\). The supports containing \(x\) form the cone \(x*K\), and \(\Delta=A\cup_K(x*K)\). Every vertex of \(K\) is compatible in \(A\) with any fixed \(u\in B_0\setminus\{x\}\); hence the inclusion \(K\hookrightarrow A\) factors through the cone \(u*K\subseteq A\) and is nullhomotopic. If \(K\) is nonempty, the homotopy-pushout formula gives \(|\Delta|\simeq|A|\vee\Sigma|K|\), with a harmless choice of basepoint. If there is no eligible other block, \(x\) is isolated and \(|\Delta|=|A|\sqcup\{x\}\).

A join of \(s\) discrete sets with respective cardinalities \(d_1,\ldots,d_s\ge2\) is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{\prod_{j=1}^s(d_j-1)}S^{s-1}\): use \(D_{d_j}\simeq\bigvee^{d_j-1}S^0\) and the standard join–suspension identity, or its cellular chain proof. If all \(r_i\ge3\), this applied to \(A\) and \(K\) gives the stated \(\beta_{n,a}\) and a pure \((a-1)\)-dimensional sphere wedge. If \(2a<n<3a\), there are exactly \(t=n-2a\) three-vertex cliques, including \(B_0\), and \(a-t\) two-vertex cliques. Consequently \(A\simeq\bigvee^{2^{t-1}}S^{a-1}\), while \(K=(D_2)^{*(t-1)}\simeq S^{t-2}\) when \(t\ge2\), giving \(\Sigma K\simeq S^{t-1}\). For \(t=1\) the cone apex \(x\) is isolated, giving the asserted wedge with \(S^0\). All stated homology groups follow integrally from these homotopy equivalences.

**6. Facet sizes and threshold.** Every facet of \(A\) selects one vertex from each of the \(a\) blocks, so has cardinality \(a\). Every facet containing \(x\) selects one safe vertex from each block of order at least three, so has cardinality \(1+\#\{i\ge1:r_i\ge3\}\). For \(2a<n<3a\) this is \(t<a\), while for \(n\ge3a\) it equals \(a\). All these candidates are indeed maximal: a support with \(x\) cannot extend into a two-vertex block, and a full transversal cannot extend within any block. This proves the exact purity transition and facet-size assertion.

## Verification
Run `python3 check.py` with Python 3. The independently executable script enumerates **all** independent vertex subsets directly from graph edges, extracts all maximum sets, enumerates **every** query support and all its traces for the stated finite test cases, and checks the structural criterion and every coefficient. It recursively evaluates the adaptive Littlestone dimension from concept partitions. For the smaller instances, it additionally computes simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) from the actual face lists and compares reduced Betti numbers. The recorded output is `check_output.txt`. Tested block-order vectors are \((2,2,2)\), \((3,2,2)\), \((3,3,2)\), \((3,3,3)\), \((3,3,2,2)\), \((4,3,3)\), \((4,4,3)\) and \((2,2,2,2)\). The first and last are historical-boundary cross-checks, not new novelty claims. These finite computations verify examples, not the quantified infinite theorem; the latter relies on steps 1–6.

## Relationship to prior work
Mohr and Rautenbach [1] prove the sharp maximum-independent-set count and identify the connected extremal graphs \(F(n,a)\). Their theorem and optimizer parametrization are premises here, **not new findings**. Their paper does not state the shattering criterion, support polynomial, adaptive dimension or sphere-wedge classifications obtained here. A previously published result [2] fully classifies shattering for maximal independent sets of fully whiskered graphs, including \(F(2a,a)=W_1(K_{1,a-1})\) as a covered special case: the polynomial \((1+2z)^{a-1}+2z\) and its nonpure support complex at \(n=2a\) are explicitly prior art. That result does not imply the present \(n>2a\) theorem: the larger clique blocks contain edges between nonconnector vertices, so the graph is not a graph with private degree-one whiskers and the concepts are *maximum*, not merely maximal, independent sets. Its proof is inapplicable to these clique blocks. Generic joins, cones and sphere-wedge calculations are familiar topology; the claimed contribution is the exact support identification and resulting threshold for the specific extremal family, not the topological identities themselves. Related maximal-independent-set shattering results on forests and cycles concern different concept classes or graph families.

## Limitations
The main theorem assumes \(a\ge3\) and \(n>2a\), and makes no claim about the exceptional connected extremizers with \(a=2\) or the nonunique extremal regime \(n<2a\). The \(n=2a\) boundary is prior covered and is not represented as an original component. The homotopy classification is an intrinsic nonequivariant classification; no automorphism-module decomposition is asserted. The source theorem [1] is used only for the extremality identification; steps 1–6 independently establish the new learning-theoretic and topological claims. Independent validation and a definitive historical priority determination have not been performed, and equivalent results could exist outside indexed literature.
