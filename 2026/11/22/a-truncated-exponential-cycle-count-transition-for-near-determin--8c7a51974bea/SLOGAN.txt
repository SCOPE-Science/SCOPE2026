For stable-rank permutations of i.i.d. letters with one least letter of probability \(1-\varepsilon_n\), the total cycle count differs from the initial run of least letters by at most the number of exceptional letters. When \(n\varepsilon_n\to\lambda\), its fraction of all positions has an explicit truncated-exponential law, with an \(O_\lambda(n^{-1})\) Wasserstein rate at \(\varepsilon_n=\lambda/n\), rather than the usual logarithmic Gaussian regime.
