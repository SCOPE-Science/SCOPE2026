# Scientific review: integral-spectrum ceiling for critical excitation

## Correctness — PASS

The critical lemma is proved by a closed contact-edge budget on an arbitrary recurrent trajectory: the cardinalities of excitation sets cannot increase; periodicity forces equality of every local degree constraint and the exact identity \(Au_t=r(u_{t-1}+u_{t+1})\). A temporal Fourier mode has eigenvalue \(r(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})\). With an integral adjacency spectrum, rational algebraic-integrality forces the cosine sum into \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\), so all modes have periods dividing twelve. Two-step refractory exclusion eliminates the remaining one- and two-period nonzero states when \(\operatorname{spec}(A)\cap\{-r,0,r\}=\varnothing\). The finite witness \(C_3\sqcup C_4\) attains period twelve; \(C_5\) exhibits a five-cycle when integrality is dropped. This is an infinite mathematical proof, not inference from finite enumeration. The three supplied standard-library verifiers independently check small trajectories, the pointwise contact lemma, and strongly regular graph spectral arithmetic. Risks: an overlooked eigenvector normalization would affect the theorem, but full pointwise counting and the explicit Fourier normalization have been checked.

## Value — PASS

The integral-spectrum condition translates a nonlinear infinite family of finite-state dynamical systems into a rigid universal recurrent-period bound, sharp at twelve. A simple spectral exclusion then proves **global extinction at a critical threshold** for the natural triangle-free Gewirtz and Higman–Sims graphs, where a prior triangle-coverage classification and the strict threshold-above-half contraction criterion do not apply. The theorem is not a renaming of the earlier three-cycle result: it resolves all eventual periods for every integral regular graph and gives a separately motivated critical spectral extinction criterion. No uniform relaxation-time estimate or existence of all listed periods is claimed.
