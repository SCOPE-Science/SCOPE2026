# A two-area law and a thin-side square limit for complete-bipartite graphical zonotopes

## Finding
Let \(p,q\ge2\) be integers. In the standard orthonormal-coordinate Euclidean realization
\[
Z_{p,q}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{q}[e_i,e_{p+j}]\subset\mathbb R^{p+q},
\]
every two-dimensional face is a parallelogram of area either \(\sqrt3\) or \(2\). Both areas occur. Define, for nonnegative integers \(a,b\) and positive integer \(s\),
\[
B_s(a,b)=(-1)^{a+b}\chi_{K_{a,b}}(-s)
=\sum_{j=0}^{a}(-1)^{a+j}\left\{\begin{matrix}a\\j\end{matrix}\right\}
\left(\prod_{t=0}^{j-1}(s+t)\right)(s+j)^b,
\]
where \(\chi\) is the chromatic polynomial, the braces are Stirling numbers of the second kind, and empty products have value \(1\). The convention \(K_{0,0}\) means the empty graph. The exact multiplicities are
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{\sqrt3}(p,q)&=p\binom q2 B_2(p-1,q-2)+q\binom p2 B_2(p-2,q-1),\\
N_2(p,q)&=4\binom p2\binom q2 B_3(p-2,q-2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(N_2\) counts square faces (side length \(\sqrt2\)); \(N_{\sqrt3}\) counts non-square rhombi (also of side length \(\sqrt2\)). In particular, for each fixed \(p\ge2\) and \(q\to\infty\), a uniformly selected two-face has limiting area distribution
\[
\Pr(A=2)\longrightarrow\frac{p-1}{p},\qquad
\Pr(A=\sqrt3)\longrightarrow\frac1p.
\]
Thus its limiting mean is \(2(p-1)/p+\sqrt3/p\). For the thin two-vertex side, the exact formulas simplify to
\[
N_2(2,q)=4\binom q2 3^{q-2},\qquad
N_{\sqrt3}(2,q)=4\binom q2 3^{q-2}+q2^{q-1}.
\]
For example, \(K_{2,2}\) has four squares and eight rhombi; \(K_{3,3}\) has 432 squares and 324 rhombi. Areas are intrinsic Euclidean two-dimensional measures, not lattice-normalized measures.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(K_{p,q}\) is unweighted and simple, with a fixed labeled bipartition of sizes \(p,q\ge2\). The canonical realization uses the ambient standard inner product and the segments \([e_i,e_{p+j}]\); translating the zonotope or reversing its segments does not change face areas. Its affine dimension is \(p+q-1\). The probability is uniform over distinct geometric two-faces, not over flags, normals, or incidence pairs. These formulas do not assert any analogous result for weighted graphical zonotopes or graphs containing triangles.

## Proof
**1. The only two supporting generator types.** For a linear functional \(u\), the maximizing face is a translate of the zonotope generated by precisely the root vectors \(e_i-e_{p+j}\) on edges where \(u_i=u_{p+j}\). Its dimension is the rank of this set of graph-incidence vectors. A rank-two edge flat of a simple triangle-free graph has exactly two edges: a third edge in the same rank-two closure would complete a triangle, while a disjoint third edge would raise the rank. Thus a two-face is a parallelogram generated by two root segments, with one of two patterns: incident edges (a wedge) or vertex-disjoint edges (a matching).

Every root has squared length \(2\). The absolute inner product of two distinct roots is \(1\) if their graph edges meet and \(0\) if they are disjoint. The squared area of the corresponding parallelogram is the Gram determinant \(2\cdot2-1^2=3\) or \(2\cdot2-0^2=4\). It follows that all two-faces are exactly of the two asserted Euclidean areas; since \(p,q\ge2\), both graph patterns exist.

**2. Count the faces over a fixed flat.** Fix an unordered pair of graph edges \(F\). The set of functionals vanishing on those two roots is the subspace of potentials constant on each connected component of \((V,F)\). For \(K_{p,q}\), no further graph root vanishes identically there. Its open chambers, indexed by acyclic orientations of the contracted graph \(K_{p,q}/F\) after parallel edges are identified, correspond bijectively to distinct faces whose free generator set is exactly \(F\). Consequently, each such flat produces exactly \(a(K_{p,q}/F)=|\chi_{K_{p,q}/F}(-1)|\) two-faces. This follows directly by the normal fan of the zonotope, or by the connected-partition face correspondence.

For a wedge centered in the \(p\)-vertex side, contraction merges one vertex on that side with two from the other, leaving a universal contracted vertex attached to \(K_{p-1,q-2}\). The resulting underlying simple graph is \(K_1\vee K_{p-1,q-2}\), whose chromatic polynomial is \(t\chi_{K_{p-1,q-2}}(t-1)\). Hence the number of acyclic orientations is \(B_2(p-1,q-2)\). There are \(p\binom q2\) such wedges. Interchanging sides gives \(q\binom p2\) wedges, each with \(B_2(p-2,q-1)\) faces.

For two disjoint edges, the contracted graph is \(K_2\vee K_{p-2,q-2}\), whose chromatic polynomial is \(t(t-1)\chi_{K_{p-2,q-2}}(t-2)\). Its number of acyclic orientations is \(2B_3(p-2,q-2)\). There are \(2\binom p2\binom q2\) unordered disjoint pairs of edges. Multiplication gives exactly the two formulas. No face is counted twice, since its maximal vanishing-edge flat is uniquely determined by its supporting directions.

**3. Evaluate the chromatic factors.** First color the side with \(a\) vertices, using exactly \(j\) distinct colors. There are \(\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}a\\j\end{smallmatrix}\right\}(t)_{\underline j}\) ways. The \(b\) other vertices may each use any of the remaining \(t-j\) colors, so
\[
\chi_{K_{a,b}}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{a}\left\{\begin{matrix}a\\j\end{matrix}\right\}(t)_{\underline j}(t-j)^b.
\]
Substituting \(t=-s\) gives the displayed finite Stirling formula and fixes the boundary conventions. This is an exact identity of integer polynomials, so its use at negative integers is legitimate.

**4. The thin-side limit.** For each fixed nonnegative \(a\) and positive \(s\), the largest exponential base in this sum is \(s+a\), with positive coefficient \(s(s+1)\cdots(s+a-1)\). Thus, as \(b\to\infty\),
\[
B_s(a,b)\sim s(s+1)\cdots(s+a-1)(s+a)^b.
\]
At fixed \(p\ge2\), the wedge count centered on the fixed side is asymptotic to
\[
p\binom q2\,p!\,(p+1)^{q-2},
\]
whereas the other wedge family is exponentially smaller (base \(p\) rather than \(p+1\)). The square count is asymptotic to
\[
2\binom p2\binom q2\,p!\,(p+1)^{q-2}
=p(p-1)\binom q2\,p!\,(p+1)^{q-2}.
\]
Dividing these leading terms proves the limiting square and rhombus frequencies. Their mean-area limit follows immediately because the random area has only two possible values. The displayed two-row formulas follow by inserting \(B_2(1,b)=2\cdot3^b\), \(B_2(0,b)=2^b\), and \(B_3(0,b)=3^b\).

## Verification
The standalone `check_faces.py` enumerates all vertices as distinct exposed endpoint-sum vectors, generates all rank-two supporting objectives by identifying the two selected edge endpoints, and deduplicates the resulting exposed vertex sets. It directly computes each four-vertex face's exact squared area from its integer-coordinate Gram determinant, then independently compares the complete histogram to the Stirling formulas. Exact cases: \((p,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,3),(3,4),(4,3)\). All matched; the output ends `BICLIQUE_AREA_FACE_EXACT_PASS`. This finite checker supports but does not replace the all-parameter proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The lead setting is Chapoton and Pilaud's complete-bipartite graphical zonotope family in their shuffle construction, first public January 18, 2022. Their work includes face-poset descriptions and vertex/facet tables, but not the Euclidean two-area classification or its thin-side limit. Padrol, Pilaud, and Poullot supply the general graphical-zonotope realization and ordered-partition face lattice. Grujić establishes for every graphical zonotope the general flat/contraction formula for the ordinary face vector, but does not evaluate this complete-bipartite area-refined two-face distribution. The present result specializes established face correspondence while supplying the exact *metric-refined* counts and limiting geometric frequencies.

## Limitations
Novelty is subject to potential unindexed equivalent area refinements. The general flat/contraction framework is prior work, not claimed as new. The exact frequencies concern uniform sampling over faces; they should not be confused with solid-angle-weighted or volume-weighted surface sampling. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
