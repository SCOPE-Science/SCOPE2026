# Review of the Galois-line stabilizer theorem

## Correctness

**PASS.** The exhaustive line list and group labels are taken from Higashine's main theorem. The proof independently establishes that the common concurrence point and the unique large plane are intrinsic, recovers the finite affine group from a smooth dual conic, forces the exact two free parameters by comparing all members of the infinite line family, and proves both label preservation and distinctness of the resulting embedded curves. The projective matrix determinant is nonzero for every stated \(a\ne0\). An exact finite-field checker confirmed the displayed action in two characteristics-two extension fields; the infinite theorem relies on the general proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual priority risk.** The initiating paper classifies individual lines and the finite automorphism group of the curve, not the setwise stabilizer of their arrangement, its label-preserving subgroup, or the existence of distinct embedded curves sharing every labelled Galois line. Other inspected/located articles analyze different quotient or Artin–Schreier–Mumford curves. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches found no equivalent or stronger statement about this concrete line configuration. The original line equations are sufficient *input* for the new calculation; the rigidity and non-reconstruction statements require additional arguments. No absence claim is made for unindexed or inaccessible literature.

## Value

**PASS.** Whether all Galois projection centers determine the ambient embedding is a natural reconstruction question after an exhaustive line classification. The full symmetry calculation exhibits an exact, positive-dimensional discrepancy between the automorphism group of the curve and that of its line data, including the richer version in which lines are decorated by their Galois-group types. The dimension-two non-reconstruction family is an explicit geometric boundary phenomenon, not a mere numerical restatement of the known classification.

## Closest literature and limitations

Higashine, arXiv:2611.08810v1, main theorem, Fact 6, and proof §§2–3, is the decisive same-object comparison. Fukasawa, arXiv:2207.03759v1 and arXiv:2005.10073v1, concerns related but different curves; only their accessible metadata/abstracts were inspected. This result does not classify infinitesimal nonreduced stabilizer schemes or produce abstractly inequivalent curves.
