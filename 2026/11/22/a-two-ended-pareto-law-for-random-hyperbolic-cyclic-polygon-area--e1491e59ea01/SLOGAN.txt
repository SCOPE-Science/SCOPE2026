For a fixed number of arclength-uniform points on an expanding hyperbolic circle, the polygon's deficit from the regular inscribed polygon has an explicit \(e^{-R}\)-scale cotangent-spacing limit. This limit has a sharp \(t^{-1}\) tail with coefficient \(4n(n-1)/\pi\), a sharp near-zero exponent \((n-1)/2\), and independent uniform collision marks; its infinite mean explains why previously known finite-radius mean deficits decay on the larger \(Re^{-R}\) scale.
