In the two-site boundary-driven symmetric exclusion process, a nontrivial Jordan block exists exactly when the exchange rate is the harmonic-contact value \(ab/(a+b)\) and the reservoir densities differ. The resulting \(t e^{-(a+b)t}\) relaxation is invisible to single-site means but appears explicitly in joint occupation, making two sites the minimal nonequilibrium exclusion example with this defect.
