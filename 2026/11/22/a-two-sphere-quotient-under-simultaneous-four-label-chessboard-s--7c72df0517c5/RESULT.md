# A two-sphere quotient under simultaneous four-label chessboard symmetry

## Finding
Let \(\Delta_{4,4}\) be the simplicial complex of nonattacking rook placements on the four-by-four board, with a vertex for each ordered pair \((i,j)\in\{0,1,2,3\}^2\). The diagonal copy of \(S_4\) acts by simultaneous relabelling of rows and columns, \(\sigma\cdot(i,j)=(\sigma(i),\sigma(j))\). Then the **ordinary geometric orbit space** is homotopy equivalent to a two-sphere:
\[
\boxed{\big|\Delta_{4,4}\big|/S_4\simeq S^2.}
\]
Consequently the quotient is simply connected and its reduced integral homology consists of exactly one free rank-one group in degree two; there is no integral torsion.

## Assumptions and scope
A nonempty set of squares is a simplex if it uses each row and column at most once. The group is **diagonal**: a single permutation acts simultaneously on both labels. In particular this differs from independent row and column permutations, transposition of the square board, and geometric fixed-point spaces. All orbit spaces mean geometric quotients of the realization, rather than Borel homotopy quotients. The proof is for exactly four rows and four columns; it does not infer a formula for larger sizes.

## Proof
**1. Simply connected source and common fixed point.** The classical chessboard connectivity theorem states that \(\Delta_{m,n}\) is \((\nu-2)\)-connected for \(\nu=\min\{m,n,\lfloor(m+n+1)/3\rfloor\}\). At \(m=n=4\), \(\nu=3\), hence \(|\Delta_{4,4}|\) is simply connected. The four diagonal squares \(\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)\}\) constitute a three-simplex whose barycenter is fixed by **all** elements of diagonal \(S_4\). Armstrong's finite-group orbit-space fundamental-group theorem identifies the fundamental group of the quotient of a simply connected polyhedron with the acting group modulo the normal subgroup generated by point stabilizers. The global fixed point makes this normal subgroup the entire \(S_4\), so the orbit space is simply connected.

**2. Exact quotient-cell complex.** Enumerate each nonempty rook-placement face, in which rows are increasing and columns are any injection into the four column labels. The numbers of faces of dimensions zero through three are \((16,72,96,24)\). The barycentric subdivision has a cell for each strict chain of nonempty faces. There are \((208,1056,1440,576)\) such cells, respectively. Its diagonal \(S_4\)-action is without inversions because a simplex is a strict chain of faces with distinct cardinalities. Thus quotient cells are exactly orbits of face chains, with no need for unlicensed cancellation of cell stabilizer multiplicities. The orbit counts are
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(19,59,66,24).
\]
For a chain \(F_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_k\), define its boundary as the alternating sum of the \(k+1\) chains obtained by deleting one entry, and take the corresponding chain orbit. This gives explicit signed integer matrices \(\partial_k\); exhaustively computing them verifies \(\partial_1\partial_2=\partial_2\partial_3=0\).

**3. Complete integral contraction.** The attached `verify.py` is a standalone exact finite certificate. It constructs every face and all its ordered-partition chains; enumerates all twenty-four simultaneous label permutations and every chain orbit; and constructs the three boundary matrices. On the integer chain complex, process the boundaries in descending degree and repeatedly choose any matrix coefficient of absolute value one. If the coefficient at row \(i\), column \(j\) is \(u\in\{-1,1\}\), eliminate the corresponding generator pair using integral row and column changes of basis: the surviving entries of that boundary become \(a_{rc}-a_{rj}a_{ic}/u\), with the pivot row and column deleted; in the adjacent boundaries remove respectively the corresponding basis row and column. This is the elementary cancellation of an acyclic two-term free chain summand, so it preserves integral homology. Each step also verifies the surviving boundary compositions vanish.

The exhaustive deterministic procedure cancels \(18\) unit pairs in degree one, \(41\) in degree two, and \(24\) in degree three. It leaves free chain-group ranks \((1,0,1,0)\) and zero differentials. Therefore the entire integral homology is
\[
H_0\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad H_2\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad H_1=H_3=0.
\]
The space has no cells above dimension three, so there is no higher homology. All computed counts are exhaustive for this finite complex, not samples of a larger family; the verifier is the reproducible proof of the integer arithmetic.

**4. Homotopy recognition.** Since the quotient is simply connected, its degree-two Hurewicz homomorphism is an isomorphism. Choose a map \(S^2\to|\Delta_{4,4}|/S_4\) representing a generator of the free second homology. It is an isomorphism on all integral homology groups by step 3, hence a homotopy equivalence by the homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected CW complexes. This proves the claimed complete homotopy type.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in a Python 3 environment using only the standard library. The program independently regenerates the \((16,72,96,24)\) source face counts, the \((208,1056,1440,576)\) barycentric counts, and the \((19,59,66,24)\) quotient counts. It validates both integer boundary-square identities after building quotient matrices and after **every** integral unit cancellation. Its expected output ends with `VERIFY_OK` and reports the surviving chain ranks \((1,0,1,0)\). A unit cancellation is integral and preserves homology, unlike a rational rank-only or floating-point computation. The simply connected and Hurewicz arguments are mathematical deductions from cited theorems; they are not consequences of finite computation alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Björner, Lovász, Vrećica, and Živaljević proved the general connectivity bound used in step 1 and analyzed small labelled chessboard complexes. That bound and those labelled-complex calculations do **not** give the diagonal \(S_4\) quotient homotopy type. The known complete classification of the column-permutation quotients of the \(3\times4\) chessboard torus treats a different complex and a different group action (columns only); its sphere cases do not imply the result above. Published fixed-point calculations for transpose-invariant rook placements and symmetric-label representations of *diagonal-deleted* chessboards likewise concern different constructions. The novelty asserted here is only the integral homotopy classification of the stated ordinary orbit space, not any of the cited background theorems or the enumeration method itself.

## Limitations
Only \(\Delta_{4,4}\) and the simultaneous-label copy of \(S_4\) are addressed. No general size or subgroup formula is claimed. The finite certificate supplies an exhaustive proof of the cellular homology calculation, not an independent expert audit; an equivalent older source not indexed in the searches remains possible. The quotient is classified up to homotopy equivalence, not claimed to be a manifold or homeomorphic to a sphere.
