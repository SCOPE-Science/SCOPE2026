# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The absorption boundary is accessible for every stated exponent. The explicit positive Green recursion produces finite first and second moments for every finite starting position. Centering cancels the square of the mean and yields an exact variance derivative; a change to incomplete-Gamma coordinates proves the three sharp large-distance regimes and the Gamma–Beta plateau. Positive-integrand Fubini and the spatial strong Markov property support the plateau and the centered martingale limit, respectively. The executable checks validate special-case ODEs and numerical identities but are not used as infinite-distance certificates.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Its main text and appendices were inspected for variance and second moments. Bansaye et al. (arXiv:1711.08603) give a broad hitting-time moment recurrence but impose a bounded first-passage mean, while Radice (arXiv:2307.12699) treats pure logarithmic potentials and related time-functionals. The proposed evaluated \(\alpha=2/3\) absolute-variance boundary, precise coefficients, Gamma–Beta plateau, and centered limit were not identified as covered statements. A standard Green formula can facilitate their derivation, so implicit-equivalence and publication-priority risk remain; this is not an independent novelty certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The precise question is whether mean-first-passage landscape design guarantees unbounded *absolute* timing uncertainty at large launch distance. The answer has a nontrivial integrability transition, a marginal logarithmic law, an exact finite constant, and a distributional consequence. This offers a motivated reliability boundary for single-particle arrival timing beyond prescribing its mean.
