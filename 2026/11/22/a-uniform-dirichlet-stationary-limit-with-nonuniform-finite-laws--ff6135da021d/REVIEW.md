The global drift is exactly \((1-rP_a)/(rn)\); a stationary carré-du-champ estimate uniformly bounds all part/global discrepancies in mean square by \(C_r/n\). Its stationary polynomial moment recursion has strictly positive leading coefficient at every positive degree and determines the unique Dirichlet(1,...,1) law on the compact simplex. No finite test is used as an infinite proof.

The multi-state noisy-voter work of Khalil–Galla (2020) uses complete interactions with intrinsic mutation. Related published public research results on two-party zealots address a Beta, not a Dirichlet simplex, and the star opinion-compatibility result is a rare-event absorption law, not invariant zealot copying. ## Mathematical value — PASS
The result isolates a natural, strongly structured network for which deleting every within-community edge invalidates the known finite flat law but leaves a universal non-self-averaging uniform simplex limit. It derives exact finite first moments, an exact smallest-instance distribution that separates finite and asymptotic behavior, and a full multivariate stationary limit rather than only a scalar variance. Complete multipartite networks and zealot-driven competing consensus are standard interacting-particle objects. The exact specified invariant distribution is pertinent to how topology affects stationary diversity.
