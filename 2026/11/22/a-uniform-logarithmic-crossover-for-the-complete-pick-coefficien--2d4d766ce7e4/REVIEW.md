# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A positive Stieltjes representation makes the radial kernel zero-free off its standard slit. The explicit upper-cut jump yields an exact positive integral for every reciprocal coefficient and its total mass. The fixed-parameter asymptotics follow by Laplace scaling, and the proposed uniform crossover is obtained by a jointly uniform gamma–zeta cancellation and dominated convergence, with both the small-variable and exponential-tail regions controlled. The diagnostic computations check, but do not replace, these arguments.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Luo and Rydhe's 2026 manuscript proves a qualitative complete-Pick implication, not the uniform coefficient crossover. Chalmoukis and Hartz explicitly discuss reciprocal-coefficient positivity in Dirichlet-type kernels; that known result is credited. The fixed endpoint belongs to classical reciprocal-logarithm theory, and polylogarithm singularity expansions are standard. Research comparisons considered equivalent renewal coefficients, alternative polylogarithm notation, stronger uniform Tauberian results, related published complete-Pick studies, and three related finite-defect kernel investigations. The particular sharp two-parameter scaling profile was not found, but a general theorem could implicitly contain it.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The canonical complete-Pick feature map has squared monomial weights given by the coefficients in this theorem. Their heavy-tail scaling changes singularly as the Dirichlet exponent approaches its critical value. The exact parameter-uniform law resolves the natural critical window, rather than providing an arbitrary isolated coefficient or a routine special case. It is not claimed as a general theory of all complete-Pick spaces.

**Closest literature and limits.** Luo–Rydhe 2026, Chalmoukis et al. 2022 (§2.1), NIST polylogarithm identities, classical Gregory coefficients. Potential prior uniform renewal-theory formulations remain to be compared by independent specialists. No conclusion about \(\alpha>1\) or nonradial kernels.
