When exactly one Poisson brood-pollinator visit preserves a flower and repeated visits destroy it, balancing a fixed visit budget is optimal up to \(B=4\) for two flowers, then breaks symmetry continuously. For any \(n\geq3\) flowers, there is exactly one earlier-than-\(2n\) and discontinuous global allocation switch: beyond it the best distribution deliberately overloads one flower while keeping all the others equally close to one expected visit.
