# Scientific review: reset dichotomous-acceleration kurtosis

## Correctness — PASS

The model and reset conditions specify one regenerative stationary law. Its exponential reset age bounds all position moments by the corresponding polynomial moments of the age. The weighted-degree generator recursion yields exact second and fourth moments, independently checked by ordered telegraph two-point and four-point correlation integrations. A rational derivative with a strictly increasing numerator cubic proves the unique interior minimum. Exact polynomial factorization proves the Gaussian-benchmark crossing and the inverse two-preimage interval. The packaged checker reproduces the identities and numerical root approximations. No finite computation substitutes for any all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Santra, Ajgaonkar and Basu, *Dichotomous acceleration process in one dimension* (arXiv:2304.11378, original 2023-04-22) explicitly compute first and second moments in the no-reset model and give a general moment representation. The general representation and the stationary second moment are not claimed as new. Their results inspected at their model definition, moment section, appendix, and discussion do not state the complete-reset fourth-moment nonmonotonicity, unique exact threshold, or moment-inverse fold. Singh (arXiv:2007.05576) treats complete-reset Gaussian acceleration, not telegraph acceleration; Montero, Palassini and Masoliver (arXiv:2312.13968) treat level crossing statistics of resetting inertial processes. The closest indexed research records on velocity-only resets, harmonic telegraph forcing, and full-reset Gaussian acceleration do not imply the particular all-rate fourth-moment fold. An unindexed exact higher-moment analysis could still affect publication priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Stationary kurtosis is a natural measurable diagnostic of intermittently accelerated transport. The result is not merely a fourth-moment tabulation: it proves a global shape theorem and a sharp model-family identifiability failure, where the second and fourth moments cannot uniquely recover the force reversal rate and amplitude even with the reset frequency known. This gives a precise boundary for calibrating a non-Gaussian acceleration mechanism from stationary observations.

## Scientific limits and nearest precursors

The result is restricted to a completely restarted and symmetrically sign-resampled telegraph acceleration. The two indistinguishable parameter pairs are not asserted to give equal full laws or temporal correlations. Strong general formulas for reset-free moments and for Gaussian reset ages are explicitly acknowledged; unindexed equivalents remain a source risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
