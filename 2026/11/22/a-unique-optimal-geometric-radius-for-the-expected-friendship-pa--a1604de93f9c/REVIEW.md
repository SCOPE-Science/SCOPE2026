# Scientific review: unique optimum of geometric friendship bias

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact expectation is attributed to Yuan (arXiv:2611.09765v1). The new proof verifies the two full-radius derivative signs through positive-coefficient elasticity and a strictly increasing geometric penalty, proves strict sample-size ordering by an exact binomial identity, and supplies uniform endpoint bounds and Taylor expansions for the two limit regimes. The baseline example is exact. The included standard-library checker passed 563 finite assertions; no finite computation is treated as a substitute for the infinite-parameter proof. The domain is uniform maximum-norm torus graphs with at least three vertices.

**Originality — PASS against inspected evidence.** The motivating full-text paper supplies the exact expected bias but does not state the unique maximizing radius, strict \(n\)-ordering, exponentially precise large-\(n\) displacement, or fixed-\(n\) high-dimensional boundary-layer optimizer. A separate 2026 full-text weak-law analysis assumes shrinking radii. Searches of relevant published mathematical records found nearby results on geometric graph degree structures and edge fluctuations, not these statements. Historical-priority uncertainty remains, especially from the incompletely accessed 2024 paper; inspection is not comprehensive certification.

**Value — PASS.** The canonical spatial connection radius controls sampling density and triangle closure in opposite directions. This theorem characterizes their unique extremal balance for every size, quantifies the very small finite-size offset from the geometric kink, and shows the dimensional and population limits target different ends of the radius range. It is a motivated structural optimization theorem rather than a routine evaluation of the known mean.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Mingao Yuan, *Limit laws for the friendship paradox in random geometric graphs*, arXiv:2611.09765v1 (the exact mean; Section 2.2) is the closest and is explicitly credited. The 2026 weak-law work applies to shrinking radii. The 2024 asymptotic-distribution article was only checked at abstract/bibliographic level. No claims are made for nonuniform points, Euclidean-distance graphs, realized graph optimizers, or simultaneous growth of vertex count and dimension.
