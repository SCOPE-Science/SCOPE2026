# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For every positive heat time, strict positivity and symmetry reduce the exact counting-\(\ell^1\) error relative to path variation to three cut responses. Mirror symmetry leaves two branches, whose spectral formulas follow from the even and odd two-dimensional blocks of the four-site weighted Laplacian. An exact Laplace-transform factorization expresses their difference as \(2[(r-1)g+rh]\), with positive \(g\) and positive convolution \(h\). Strict log-concavity of \(g\) yields a strictly increasing \(h/g\), proving the complete one-crossing classification. The scaling limits follow from controlled small-time and weak-bottleneck asymptotics. Independent numeric matrix exponentials and random BV vectors corroborate the algebra; they do not prove the universal statement.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The full mathematical statements of Chafaï–Joulin (2010; birth–death intertwining and Lipschitz/Wasserstein bounds), Castro–Grünbaum (2013; birth–death spectral representations), and a published weighted four-path sharp Lipschitz heat-norm result were compared. None of those inspected assertions computes the counting-\(\ell^1\) identity defect relative to unweighted input variation or the weak-middle-link unique cut migration. The previously established unweighted four-path cut theorem covers only \(r=1\), where no positive crossover occurs. Equivalent formulas in unindexed literature cannot be ruled out, so this assessment is not an independent historical-priority certificate.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem locates a sharp dynamical change in the worst discontinuity for a concrete nonuniform diffusion network, even though the underlying smoothing semigroup stays positive and symmetric. The exact weak-bottleneck and near-uniform scaling laws quantify when the dominant approximation error moves from locally fast outer links to a cardinality-dominant central cut; this is relevant to parameter selection and diagnostics of diffusion smoothing under inhomogeneous conductances. It is not a claim for general graphs.

**Closest literature and limits.** The primary birth–death papers address different operator norms; the published four-vertex Lipschitz result has distinct hypotheses and norm. The finite four-site symmetric-weight classification does not extend automatically to longer or asymmetric paths. Self-review is not independent verification.
