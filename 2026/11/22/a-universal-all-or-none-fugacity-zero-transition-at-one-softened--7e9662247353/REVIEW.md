# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The central-bond identity is a direct conditioning calculation. Weighted-path matching polynomials, realized as characteristic polynomials of irreducible real symmetric tridiagonal matrices, prove all negative-real root and simplicity assertions for repulsive bond weights. Coprimality of consecutive path recurrences prevents factor overlap and proves strict positivity throughout the real fugacity axis for every attractive central-bond weight. Local implicit expansions account for every collision, and coefficient comparison establishes the extra far-negative root in odd half-lengths. Exact independent configuration enumeration and Sturm root counts pass the supplied finite checks. The analytic proof, rather than finite numerical experiments, handles every length.

**Originality — PASS on examined evidence, provisional.** The principal precursor about nearest-neighbor one-dimensional lattice gases is Vavro (2000; https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0011401), and Scott–Sokal (2003; https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0309352) establish general repulsive independent-set and soft-gas identities. Wagner (2008; https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.1659) supplies the general weighted-matching root-location mechanism. The examined source sections do not derive the one-centered-link all-or-none coalescence theorem, the full classification for attractive central weights, or the odd-half-length singular root. The statement is not implied by homogeneous soft-bond results without the nontrivial factorization and coprimality comparison. Unindexed earlier equivalent manipulations of path polynomials remain a genuine priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A local bond defect is a natural exactly solvable impurity in one-dimensional lattice gases. The theorem gives a complete all-size zero-geometry classification, identifies the precisely localized root boundary between repulsive and attractive interactions, and resolves a singular degree change at zero central occupancy weight. These facts quantify finite-size analytic structure rather than simply reproducing a known constant-size table. There is no claim of a thermodynamic transition or general heterogeneous-chain theorem.
