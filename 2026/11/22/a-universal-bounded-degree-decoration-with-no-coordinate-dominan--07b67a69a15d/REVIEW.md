# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem defines a countably infinite simple graph with degree uniformly at most the base-degree bound plus four, so its adjacency operator is bounded selfadjoint. At \(\lambda=0\) the short leaves force every base amplitude to vanish and all long-arm amplitudes vanish; only short-leaf antisymmetry remains. At \(\lambda=\pm1\), the long-arm equations force every base amplitude to vanish and only long-arm antisymmetry remains. These are exhaustive eigenspace classifications, not merely exhibited eigenvectors. The resulting exact singleton spectral masses are \(0,1/2,1/4\) as appropriate. Each vertex misses at least one globally nonzero atom, which proves lack of dominance. An exact finite counterpart checker passed 208 assertions; the infinite argument is independent of that computation.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

The closest primary reference is Bruchez–de la Harpe–Nagnibeda (arXiv:2205.12819), which gives a finite seven-vertex tree motif and explicitly leaves the connected infinite bounded-degree case unresolved. The present claim does **not** rename their finite tree; it proves a uniform grafting statement for arbitrary infinite bases with full eigenspace support classification. Sabri–Youssef (arXiv:2304.06465) supplies a general flat-band framework but does not assert the spectral-measure dominance classification. Hirschler–Woess (arXiv:2507.21733) discusses finite-host substitutions and identifies infinite hosts as future interest, not this theorem. Prior-priority risk: an uninspected older decorated-graph construction could make this consequence known. A failed keyword search does not establish uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result answers a specific existence question formulated in the motivating primary paper and strengthens mere existence to a base-independent construction. Its content is a complete operator-theoretic analysis of the *point spectral measures*, not just a finite enumeration or a generic eigenvector example. It applies to arbitrary bounded-degree connected infinite cores and includes a one-ended tree with degree at most six. No optimality or full-spectrum formula is asserted.
