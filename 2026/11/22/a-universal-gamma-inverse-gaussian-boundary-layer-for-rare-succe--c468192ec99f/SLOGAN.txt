In an absorbing-target Brownian search with visible stochastic harmonic returns, a very stiff return reaches the target with probability asymptotic to \(rqD/(\kappa^2L^2)\) per cycle, where \(q\) is the probability of search-phase success before the alarm. Conditioned on this rare event, the scaled distance of the alarm position from the target has an exact \(\operatorname{Gamma}(2,1)\) limit, coupled to a drifted-Brownian inverse-Gaussian hitting time through the universal joint Laplace transform \(\bigl(u+(1+\sqrt{1+4s})/2\bigr)^{-2}\).
