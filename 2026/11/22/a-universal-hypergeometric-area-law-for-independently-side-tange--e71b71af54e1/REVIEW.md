# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The contact points uniquely determine a positive-definite inscribed ellipse through its center and covariance. The normalized area simplifies to the exact rational-square-root formula. The beta-series integral is nonnegative for every stated exponent and has convergent parameter excess, giving all moments without exchanging conditionally convergent terms. AM–GM identifies the unique maximizing contact pair. The evaluated two-by-two Hessian is negative definite, and the local ellipse-volume argument yields the stated sharp \(9/4\) tail. All reported finite tests passed. No claim of computer proof of an infinite statement is made.

## Originality: PASS, with residual priority risk
Horwitz (2015) gives the deterministic two-contact parameterization; the classical Steiner inellipse gives its maximal area. Neither supplies the side-contact-uniform pushforward probability law, its complete hypergeometric moment transform or the sharp near-maximum coefficient. Published stochastic triangle results considered in the comparisons use different observables. Uniform-center inellipses reduce instead to a broken-stick Dirichlet model and have a different distribution. Semantic database searches and recoverable relevant claims did not identify this exact law. Such searches cannot prove historical uniqueness, and a formula may occur in other classical probability literature.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The experiment is specified by physically choosing two independent tangent points on adjacent sides; there is then exactly one inscribed ellipse. Determining its random area is a natural finite-dimensional geometric-probability problem that differs structurally from choosing the ellipse center uniformly. The shape-independent all-moment law and exact near-extremal coefficient provide quantitative information unavailable from the known deterministic maximal-area theorem.

## Limitations
The result neither evaluates an elementary cumulative distribution nor addresses nonuniform or correlated tangency selection, eccentricity laws, or nontriangular hosts. Sources give only best-knowledge originality, and independent validation is outstanding.
