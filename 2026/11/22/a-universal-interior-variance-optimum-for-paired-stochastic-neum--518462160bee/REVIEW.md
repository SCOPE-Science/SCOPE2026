# Assessment of the paired-Neumann mirror-strength theorem

## Correctness (PASS)

The stated estimator is an exact-reference, equal-weight two-branch Neumann product with independent factor samples across depth. Its convergence proof uses the strict second-moment contractivity \(a_i^2+s_i<1\); neither almost-sure single-factor contraction nor higher noise moments are assumed. Centered perpetuity recursions give all three scalar second moments, including the negative cross-branch covariance \(-\tau s/( (1-a)^2(1-a^2+\tau s))\). Summing coordinate risks is valid with arbitrary contemporaneous cross-coordinate dependence because the loss is squared Euclidean norm. Differentiation of the stated rational expression yields strict convexity, opposite endpoint derivative signs, the unique interior root, its scalar quartic, and its golden-ratio boundary. Uniform \(L^2\) tail bounds justify convergence of finite-depth minimizers. The exact rational checker passes 109 specified checks, including an asymmetric centered noise-support case and a correlated two-mode case. All infinite claims rest on analytic proof, not on finite checking.

## Value (PASS)

The newly proposed antithetic Neumann family has an intrinsic tunable reflection strength. Exact odd-degree cancellation recommends full reflection for a third-moment objective, but a squared-error objective produces a different, universal structural answer. The unique interior minimizer is nontrivial even with perfectly centered Hessian-factor noise, is robust to the noise's higher-moment distribution and cross-mode dependence in commuting problems, and persists as the limit of practical finite-depth optima. The sharp high-noise golden-ratio strength and the \(1/4\) versus \(1/2\) risk constants provide concrete calibration and benchmark values. The example improves variance by about \(20.5\%\) against the full-mirror choice in the identical paired family. The result is not a guarantee for noncommuting matrices, unknown-reference bias, runtime, or end-to-end training.
