# Scientific assessment: integrated-Wiener base-observation design

## Correctness — PASS
The central domain is all integers \(p\ge0\), all independent Gaussian measurement-noise variances \(\eta>0\), and an unrestricted single measurement of \(W(t)\) on \([0,1]\). Gaussian conditioning gives the exact risk. An all-order binomial identity reduces the second derivative of the risk-reduction numerator to a polynomial with one positive constant coefficient and only negative positive-degree coefficients. This proves a single inflection, from which the strictly unimodal objective and all-noise uniqueness follow. The endpoint expansion and stationary equation yield the sharp order-dependent displacement and excess risk constants. The 99 exact and supplementary finite-order checks replay successfully, but all-order correctness rests on the algebraic proof rather than enumeration. Potential error modes checked: factorial indexing at order zero, covariance normalization, boundary derivatives, direction of the risk-versus-gain inequality, and the high-noise remainder.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual uncertainty
Schober–Duvenaud–Hennig (2014, arXiv:1406.2582) work with integrated-Wiener priors and derivative observations, but their stated results concern matching Runge–Kutta posterior means and not minimization of integrated posterior path variance over one noisy observation time. Ji–Müller (2016, arXiv:1604.05375) give a general trajectory-reconstruction objective for directly observed process values. Their available text and relevant sections do not supply the mixed base-process/iterated-integral covariance optimization, unique inflection structure, or \(1/(p+1)\) scaling. A historical dissertation by Bhramar Mukherjee addresses related optimal path-sampling problems but could not be inspected beyond its public catalog; its content is therefore an explicit priority risk, not asserted to be irrelevant. Related indexed Brownian quadrature and stochastic-design records concern different observation models or multiple sample points. Earlier results on noisy single observations of \(X_1(t)\), rather than \(W(t)\), do not imply this theorem; the large-noise endpoint behavior is structurally different. Search coverage and index matches do not certify global novelty.

## Value — PASS
Integrated Wiener priors and derivative evaluations are standard in probabilistic trajectory reconstruction and numerical ODE methods. The practical design question of where a single noisy base-process or derivative evaluation should be placed is therefore natural. The result is not only an evaluation of a fixed sensor: it establishes the exact global uniqueness and monotone placement law for every integration order and every positive noise level, and a sharp order-dependent noise boundary layer with an explicit risk penalty for taking the intuitively appealing endpoint observation. Those structural statements inform high-noise sampling and provide analytically reproducible reference cases. No unproved extension to arbitrary covariance classes or multiple sensors is suggested.
