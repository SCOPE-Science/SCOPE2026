# Review of the fixed-ball occupancy fragmentation theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The identity \(C=K-E\) holds pointwise for occupied induced subgraphs of trees. Three-vacancy inclusion–exclusion yields the exact mixed covariance with every edge, hence the claimed shape-independent five-term formula. For orders \(m\ge4\), the ordered exponential bases give at most three distinct real roots by generalized Descartes, exhausted by roots at zero, one and after two; the \(m=3\) case has only two possible positive real roots, similarly exhausted. Direct Taylor expansion gives the limiting sign profile and the additive constant. The independently structured exhaustive checker passed 1738 assertions. The proof, not finite checks, addresses unrestricted tree order and all sample sizes.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The relevant published component polynomial includes induced component-count coefficients, but does not supply fixed-ball multinomial mixed covariance or an exact one-crossing/additive cutoff theorem. Published multinomial-occupancy normal approximation results concern occupancy-frequency counts rather than clusters. Related published forest fragmentation results use independent site retention, or third cumulants instead of this joint statistic. Direct implication comparisons found no stronger claim covering the precise result. This is a best-knowledge research comparison, not a guarantee of exhaustive historical priority. The exact graph-polynomial framework remains a meaningful residual risk, especially for unstated specialization calculations.

## Value — PASS

Fixed-size sampling induces dependence absent in Bernoulli percolation; occupancy richness and spatial fragmentation are both central and naturally measurable. The theorem establishes a *structural*, all-tree invariant, a globally unique change in its sign with sample size, and a nontrivial order-one displacement beyond the leading \(\log 2\) load transition. The parameter and observable are natural rather than arbitrarily chosen, and the sharp cutoff is not an immediate covariance computation.

## Limits and literature

The result assumes connected acyclic host, homogeneous iid allocations and unconditional component counts. The comparison includes Tittmann–Averbouch–Makowsky (2008), Bartroff–Goldstein (2012), Allard et al. (2015), and related publicly available Bernoulli-tree fragmentation claims. An undiscovered equivalent formula remains possible.
