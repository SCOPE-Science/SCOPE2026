# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The stated general high-noise theorem has explicit hypotheses and quantifiers, and its global location-limit conclusion follows from uniform inversion of the positive-definite noise covariance and a strictly convex quadratic over the complete signal-covariance square. The Brownian-bridge specialization uses exact conditional covariances; the finite-noise counterexample is a strict rational inequality against *every* reflection-symmetric pair. The closed-form gain identities are independently reconstructed and symbolically tested by `verify.py`; its 501 grid and 1,200 seeded rational tests corroborate, but do not replace, the universal analytic argument. The critical \(\rho=1/2\) case is intentionally not assigned a unique limiting placement.

**Originality: PASS, with historical-priority risk.** The inspected full accessible arXiv texts by Attia–Constantinescu (2020) and Cai–Lam–Scarlett (2022) give, respectively, a general correlation-aware design framework and noisy numerical-integration rates. Neither presents the universal \(\rho=1/2\) two-query location transition or the explicit Brownian-bridge all-symmetric-design exclusion. The independent-noise exact Brownian-bridge design is a special noise model and does not imply the positive-correlation endpoint-reference regime; correlated erasures are a different observation law. Relevant published mathematical index entries on correlated-noise design, Brownian-bridge quadrature, and correlated erasures were compared for implications. This is best-knowledge originality, not an exhaustive priority certificate.

**Value: PASS.** This is a motivated structural classification in noisy goal-oriented numerical integration: with two correlated sensors, asymptotically optimal allocation changes qualitatively from replicate the most informative observation to measure a *zero-target-covariance* location to calibrate shared noise. Its threshold is independent of the latent continuous kernel under explicit hypotheses; the finite-noise Brownian-bridge example demonstrates a genuine loss of symmetry and provides an exact quantitative improvement over an entire symmetric class. It is useful for identifying when symmetry-restricted design optimizers are unsound. It is not a generic finite-noise optimizer for every kernel.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Attia and Constantinescu, arXiv:2007.14476 (correlated observation errors, general OED); Cai, Lam and Scarlett, arXiv:2202.10615 (kernel quadrature under noisy independent observations); Naslidnyk, Gonzalez and Mahsereci, arXiv:2112.01578 (symmetry-oriented quadrature priors). The classic Gaussian posterior-variance identity is not asserted as new. No result at singular error correlation, no classification at exactly \(\rho=1/2\), no full finite-noise phase diagram, and no higher-query extension is claimed.
