# A universal open-and-closed shattering law for triangle-free strongly regular graphs of codegree two

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite, simple, nontrivial strongly regular graph with parameters \((v,k,\lambda,\mu)=(v,k,0,2)\) and \(k\ge4\). Use all open neighborhoods \(N_G(w)\), or all closed neighborhoods \(N_G[w]\), as Boolean concepts on the vertex set. Define the shattered-support polynomial \(\Phi_{\mathcal C}(z)=\sum_{S\text{ shattered by }\mathcal C} z^{|S|}\). Then the exact complete polynomials are
\[
\Phi_{\mathrm{open}}(z)=1+vz+\frac{v(v-k-1)}2z^2+v\binom{k}{3}z^3,
\]
\[
\Phi_{\mathrm{closed}}(z)=1+vz+\binom v2z^2+v\left(\binom{k}{3}+\binom{k}{2}\right)z^3.
\]
Both VC dimensions are exactly \(3\). The shattered open triples are precisely the three-vertex independent sets with a common neighbor. The shattered closed triples are exactly these independent triples and the three-vertex induced paths. There are no shattered supports of cardinality at least four.

There is also a canonical, nonzero cohomology class \([\alpha_G]\in H^1(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{closed}};\mathbb F_2)\), where \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{closed}}\) is the closed-neighborhood shattering complex: set \(\alpha_G(\{u,w\})=1\) if \(uw\in E(G)\), and zero otherwise. The class is nonzero for every graph in the specified family.

The Clebsch graph \((v,k)=(16,5)\) yields
\[\Phi_{\mathrm{open}}(z)=1+16z+80z^2+160z^3,\qquad \Phi_{\mathrm{closed}}(z)=1+16z+120z^2+320z^3.\]
Its reduced mod-two Betti ranks are \((\widetilde\beta_1,\widetilde\beta_2)=(0,95)\) for open shattering and \((1,216)\) for closed shattering. The same universal formulas predict the exact face polynomials for the Gewirtz graph \((56,10,0,2)\): \(1+56z+1260z^2+6720z^3\) open and \(1+56z+1540z^2+9240z^3\) closed.

## Assumptions and scope
A strongly regular graph with parameters \((v,k,0,2)\) is a simple graph with \(v\) vertices, regular degree \(k\), no triangles, and precisely two common neighbors of each nonadjacent vertex pair. The usual parameter identity is \((v-k-1)\mu=k(k-\lambda-1)\), so here \(v=1+k+k(k-1)/2\). A finite support \(S\subseteq V(G)\) is shattered by the concept class \(\mathcal C\) when every subset of \(S\) occurs as \(S\cap C\) for some \(C\in\mathcal C\). Both graph-neighborhood classes have exactly one concept for each center. They need not exclude the center from the query support. Cohomology is simplicial cohomology over \(\mathbb F_2\). For the general results, no assumption is made that the graph is vertex-transitive or that all graphs with these parameters have been classified.

## Proof
**1. Elementary counting identities.** Every two adjacent vertices have no common open neighbor; every two nonadjacent vertices have exactly two. A pair of distinct closed neighborhoods always intersects in exactly two vertices: for adjacent centers, the centers themselves, and for nonadjacent centers, their two open common neighbors. For \(k\ge4\),
\[
v-2k=\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}2>0,\quad v-3k+2=\frac{(k-2)(k-3)}2>0,\quad v-3k+5=\frac{k^2-5k+12}2>0.\]

**2. Points and pairs.** A singleton is shattered by either class because each vertex occurs in some but not every neighborhood. For an open pair, the all-positive pattern is available if and only if the pair is nonadjacent. In that case the remaining three patterns occur: each single-positive pattern has \(k-2\) witnessing neighbors of the positive endpoint, and the all-negative pattern has at least \(v-2k+2>0\) witnesses. For a closed pair, the all-positive pattern always occurs (an endpoint witnesses an adjacent pair, or one of two common neighbors witnesses a nonedge). The two single-positive patterns occur because each closed neighborhood has \(k-1>0\) vertices outside the other, while there are \(v-2k>0\) all-negative witnesses. Thus exactly the stated pairs are shattered.

**3. Independent triples.** Suppose an independent triple \(T=\{a,b,c\}\) has a common neighbor \(w\). Its common neighbor is unique: a second such neighbor would be nonadjacent to \(w\) (since both meet \(a\)) and would share three neighbors with \(w\), contradicting \(\mu=2\). For each pair of \(T\), its second common neighbor consequently misses the third vertex, realizing each two-positive trace. To realize a single positive vertex \(a\) by an open neighborhood, choose among the \(k-3>0\) neighbors of \(a\) that are neighbors of neither \(b\) nor \(c\); inclusion–exclusion uses the three pair-intersections of size two and the unique triple intersection. The all-negative open trace has \(v-(3k-5)>0\) witnesses, since the union of the three open neighborhoods has size \(3k-5\). For closed neighborhoods, the query vertex itself realizes each singleton trace, while \(w\) and the second common neighbors realize the other nonempty traces. The union of the three closed neighborhoods has size \(3k-2\), hence \(v-3k+2>0\) witnesses realize the all-negative closed trace. Thus every independent triple with a common neighbor is shattered in both classes.

**4. Induced paths in the closed class.** Suppose \(T=\{a,b,c\}\), with edges \(ab\) and \(bc\), but not \(ac\). The center \(b\) realizes all-positive; \(a\) and \(c\) realize the adjacent-pair traces \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{b,c\}\). The second common neighbor of nonadjacent \(a,c\), distinct from \(b\), is nonadjacent to \(b\) by triangle-freeness and realizes \(\{a,c\}\). For singleton \(b\), select another neighbor of \(b\) distinct from \(a,c\); it is nonadjacent to both endpoints by triangle-freeness. For singleton \(a\), select a neighbor of \(a\) other than \(b\) and the second common neighbor of \(a,c\); this vertex meets neither \(b\) nor \(c\). The \(c\) case is symmetric, and \(k-2>0\) ensures choices. The three closed neighborhoods again have union size \(3k-2\), so an all-negative witness exists. Every induced three-point path is therefore closed-shattered.

**5. Exhaustive triple classification and the four-point obstruction.** An all-positive open trace forces three vertices into an open neighborhood, which is independent because the graph is triangle-free. An all-positive closed trace forces the three vertices into a closed neighborhood, where they induce either an independent triple or an induced path (two leaves and possibly the center). Steps 3–4 classify the shattered triples. For any hypothetical shattered four-point set \(S\), its all-positive trace forces \(S\) inside one open or closed neighborhood centered at \(w\). Select three distinct query points in \(S\cap N(w)\); they are independent and their only common open or closed witnessing center is \(w\), by the uniqueness in Step 3. But the fourth point of \(S\) is also in that center's chosen neighborhood. Hence the trace with precisely those three points positive and the fourth negative is impossible. Downward closure excludes all larger sets.

**6. Exact counting.** Each independent triple with a common neighbor has a unique witnessing center, and every vertex has \(\binom{k}{3}\) triples in its independent open neighborhood, giving \(v\binom{k}{3}\). Each three-vertex induced path has its unique middle vertex and is counted by choosing two of its \(k\) independent neighbors, giving \(v\binom{k}{2}\). Nonadjacent pairs number \(v(v-k-1)/2\). Together with Steps 2–5 this proves both polynomials, including the exact VC dimensions.

**7. The cohomological witness.** The closed shattering complex has every vertex and every edge as a face. Define the one-cochain \(\alpha_G\) to be one on edges of \(G\) and zero on nonedges. By the triple classification, a two-face induces either zero or exactly two graph edges, so its \(\mathbb F_2\) coboundary vanishes. Choose adjacent \(a,b\) and \(c\) adjacent to neither; such \(c\) exists because the number of vertices outside \(N[a]\cup N[b]\) equals \(v-2k>0\). The one-cycle formed by the three edges of the complete one-skeleton on \(a,b,c\) pairs to \(1\) with \(\alpha_G\). Coboundaries pair trivially with cycles. Hence \([\alpha_G]\ne0\) in first cohomology.

## Verification
The independent executable `verify_field.py` defines the sixteen-vertex graph as an additive Cayley graph of \(\mathbb F_{16}\), with the order-five subgroup of nonzero cubes as the generating set. It reconstructs all adjacency and strongly regular parameters, enumerates every support through cardinality four, checks the claimed structural classification for every triple, and computes the exact mod-two boundary ranks and cocycle pairing. The independent C++17 executable `verify_folded.cpp` uses the even-parity length-five binary words, adjacent exactly at Hamming distance four; it independently reconstructs all supports and mod-two boundary ranks. Both verify the same face counts and reduced Betti ranks. The source files and actual outputs are included with this report. The all-parameter theorem is proved in Steps 1–7 and is not inferred from finite computation.

## Relationship to prior work
Adler–Benediktsson–Macpherson (2020/2022) treat the VC dimensions and densities of neighborhood concepts in Hamming and Johnson graphs, not the exact open/closed trace complex of all triangle-free strongly regular graphs with codegree two. Pham–Senger–Tait–Nguyen Thu-Huyen (2023) address VC dimension in pseudorandom graphs and bounds rather than this exact profile. Coudert–Csikós–Ducoffe–Viennot (2024) give general graph VC-dimension algorithms and bounds; their statements do not yield the full coefficientwise parameter law.

Byrne–Byrne–Cioabă (2026) directly investigate VC dimensions of strongly regular graphs and determine dimensions for small orders, potentially including the Clebsch graph. Their abstract establishes no complete parameter-level shattering polynomial. The full source could not be inspected in this comparison, so an unrecognized implication from its full text is an explicit residual originality risk; no first discovery of the scalar VC dimension is claimed. Public mathematical records computing the *full* open and closed neighborhood complexes of the Clebsch graph already imply corresponding homological information for their two-skeleta in some cases, and that prior topological work is not claimed as new. A prior 2026 record computes different open/closed shattering polynomials for the Shrikhande and rook graphs, which have parameters \((16,6,2,2)\), not \((v,k,0,2)\). The present contribution is the general **exact face-by-face incidence criterion for both neighborhood classes**, the uniform counting formulas and their structural cohomological interpretation, not a fresh computation of known Clebsch homotopy types.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes \(\lambda=0\), \(\mu=2\) and \(k\ge4\); it does not extend by assertion to other strongly regular parameters, all triangle-free graphs, graph classes with three common neighbors, or to an exact integral homotopy type of the shattering complexes. The Gewirtz polynomial is a theorem-based substitution of its known parameters; only the Clebsch instance has an independent exhaustive verifier in the package. The mod-two Betti numbers are exact for the Clebsch instance only, and the nonzero universal first cohomology class is not a complete homology classification. Literature and historical-index coverage is not exhaustive; particularly the complete 2026 strongly-regular VC paper was not accessible for full-text inspection. No independent external mathematical assessment has been performed.
