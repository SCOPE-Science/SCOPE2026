# A universal Poncelet-polygon count for conics with two double contacts

## Finding

Let \(q\) be any odd prime power, and let \((C,D)\) range over **ordered pairs of distinct smooth plane conics** over \(\mathbb F_q\) for which the geometric intersection divisor on \(C\) is \(2P+2Q\), with \(P\ne Q\). Form the undirected *rational tangent-chord graph* on \(C(\mathbb F_q)\): two distinct vertices are adjacent precisely when their joining line is tangent to \(D\). An unoriented simple rational Poncelet polygon means a cycle of this graph, counted once up to rotation and reversal; different defining pairs count separately.

For every integer \(n\ge3\), the number \(\mathcal P_n(q)\) of such \(n\)-gons, counted jointly with their ordered conic pair, is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal P_n(q)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac{|\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_q)|\,\varphi(n)}{4n},&n\mid(q-1)\text{ or }n\mid(q+1),\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}}
\qquad
|\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_q)|=q^3(q^3-1)(q^2-1).
\]

Here \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient function. More precisely, a pair has a contact-splitting sign \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\): \(\sigma=+1\) if both geometric contact points are rational, and \(\sigma=-1\) if they form a conjugate quadratic pair. It also has a *step sign* \(\delta\in\{+1,-1\}\), and a unique step order \(n\ge3\) dividing \(q-\delta\). If \(\sigma=\delta\), its tangent-chord graph is \((q-\sigma)/n\) disjoint cycles of length \(n\), plus two isolated contact points when \(\sigma=+1\). If \(\sigma\ne\delta\), the graph has no edges. Thus every pair has either only isolated vertices or cycles all of the same length.

For any extension \(\mathbb F_{q^m}\), the number of cycles of this same length \(n\) is

\[
\begin{cases}
(q^m-1)/n,&m\text{ even},\\
(q^m-\sigma)/n,&m\text{ odd and }\sigma=\delta,\\
0,&m\text{ odd and }\sigma\ne\delta.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, a pair with no base-field rational polygon always acquires such polygons over the quadratic extension, and degree two is the least possible extension degree.

The projective classes number \(2(q-2)\), and each class of sign \(\sigma\) has size \(|\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_q)|/[2(q-\sigma)]\). The total number of ordered pairs of this contact type is \(q^4(q^3-1)(q-2)\). The proportion admitting any simple rational Poncelet polygon is exactly

\[
\boxed{\frac12-\frac{1}{2q(q-2)}.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The characteristic is odd. Conics are geometrically smooth and embedded in the fixed projective plane over \(\mathbb F_q\); the contact scheme has two *distinct geometric* support points, each with intersection multiplicity exactly two. Both conics and their order are part of the data. Graph edges require distinct rational endpoints and geometrically tangent chord lines. The counts are of rational cyclic polygons, not merely geometric closure conditions, not directed traversals, and not isomorphism classes of conic pairs.

The step order and contact sign are invariant under rational projective equivalence. Contact points may fail to be rational; the incidence type is nevertheless defined over the base field. A simple cycle has at least three vertices, so the excluded step orders one and two do not produce polygons.

## Proof

**1. The complete pencil and its contact sign.** Let \(\ell=0\) be the unique rational line joining the two geometric contact points. The restriction of the defining quadratic form \(F_D\) to \(C\) and the restriction of \(\ell^2\) both have divisor \(2P+2Q\). Since their degree-four sections of \(\mathcal O_C(2)\) have the same divisor, they differ by a nonzero scalar; since the kernel of restriction of plane quadratic forms to the smooth conic is generated by \(F_C\), the pair belongs to the pencil spanned by \(F_C\) and \(\ell^2\). For a fixed \(\ell\), writing \(F_D=F_C+\lambda\ell^2\) gives \(q\) finite pencil parameters; the value \(\lambda=0\) repeats \(C\), and exactly one other finite value makes \(D\) singular. Thus precisely \(q-2\) distinct smooth conics \(D\) remain for each non-tangent rational line \(\ell\).

An \(\mathbb F_q\)-line meets a fixed smooth conic at two rational points (\(\sigma=+1\)), two conjugate quadratic points (\(\sigma=-1\)), or one double rational point (tangent, excluded). The respective counts are \(q(q+1)/2\), \(q(q-1)/2\), and \(q+1\). The group \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\), acting on the conic, is transitive on lines of each non-tangent sign.

**2. Normal form and intrinsic parameter.** Over \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), select projective coordinates sending the two contacts to the endpoints of the standard rational parametrization

\[
C(t)=[t^2:t:1],\qquad
C:\ XZ-Y^2=0,\qquad
D_a:\ XZ-aY^2=0,
\]

where \(a\in\mathbb F_q\setminus\{0,1\}\). For split contacts this normalization is over the base field. For nonsplit contacts, Frobenius interchanges the endpoints and the parameter coordinate can be scaled so that \(t^q=1/t\) on rational points. The pencil coefficient \(a\) descends because the normalizer of the contact pair acts as \(t\mapsto ct\) or \(t\mapsto c/t\), preserving the ratio of the two displayed quadratic forms; thus the same \(a\) is intrinsic for the ordered pair. The two signs together with \(a\) give exactly \(2(q-2)\) rational projective-equivalence classes. Each class of sign \(\sigma\) has an ordered-pair stabilizer of order \(2(q-\sigma)\) in \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_q)\), namely the normalizer of the split or nonsplit contact torus. Its orbit therefore has size \(|\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_q)|/[2(q-\sigma)]\).

**3. Rational tangent chords and step orders.** For nonzero finite parameters \(u\ne v\), the chord through \(C(u)\) and \(C(v)\) has equation \(X-(u+v)Y+uvZ=0\). By substituting into the dual conic of \(D_a\), this line is tangent precisely when

\[
(u+v)^2=4auv.
\]

Equivalently, \(v=ru\) or \(v=r^{-1}u\), where

\[
r^2-(4a-2)r+1=0,\qquad
r+r^{-1}=4a-2,
\qquad \delta=\chi_q(a(a-1)).
\]

The discriminant is \(16a(a-1)\). For \(\delta=+1\), \(r\in\mathbb F_q^\times\); for \(\delta=-1\), Frobenius acts by \(r^q=r^{-1}\), so \(r\) belongs to the norm-one torus of order \(q+1\). Since \(a\notin\{0,1\}\), one has \(r\ne\pm1\); its multiplicative order \(n\) is at least three and divides \(q-\delta\).

When \(\sigma=+1\), the two contact points correspond to \(t=0,\infty\), while the remaining \(q-1\) rational vertices have parameters in \(\mathbb F_q^\times\). When \(\sigma=-1\), the \(q+1\) rational vertices are exactly the norm-one parameters \(t^{q+1}=1\). If \(\sigma\ne\delta\), multiplication by \(r\) does not preserve this rational parameter group, and there are no rational graph edges. If \(\sigma=\delta\), multiplication by \(r\) has \((q-\sigma)/n\) orbits, each an undirected simple \(n\)-cycle. In the split-contact case the two contact points are isolated. This proves the graph classification.

**4. All possible orders, with multiplicities.** The parameter identity \(a=(r+1)^2/(4r)\) is invariant under \(r\leftrightarrow r^{-1}\). Conversely, every \(a\notin\{0,1\}\) corresponds to exactly one inverse pair of roots. Thus for each divisor \(n\ge3\) of \(q-\delta\), precisely \(\varphi(n)/2\) values of \(a\) have step order \(n\). No integer \(n\ge3\) can divide both \(q-1\) and \(q+1\), because \(q\) is odd.

There are \(q^2(q^3-1)\) smooth conics over \(\mathbb F_q\), by dividing the order of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_q)\) by that of the conic stabilizer \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\). For the matching sign \(\sigma=\delta\), the number of ordered pairs whose polygon length is \(n\) therefore equals

\[
q^2(q^3-1)\cdot\frac{q(q+\sigma)}2\cdot\frac{\varphi(n)}2.
\]

Each contributes \((q-\sigma)/n\) distinct unoriented polygons. Multiplying and simplifying gives \(|\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_q)|\varphi(n)/(4n)\), proving the universal count.

**5. Extensions and density.** Over an even-degree extension, both geometric contacts and the two roots \(r,r^{-1}\) split; every rational non-contact vertex belongs to the multiplicative group of size \(q^m-1\), yielding \((q^m-1)/n\) cycles. Odd-degree extensions retain both signs and the preceding argument gives the remaining cases. Finally, the standard quadratic-character sum \(\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_q}\chi_q(a(a-1))=-1\) shows that among \(a\ne0,1\), exactly \((q-3)/2\) have \(\delta=+1\) and \((q-1)/2\) have \(\delta=-1\). Combining these with the respective secant and exterior line counts yields \(q(q^2-2q-1)/2\) polygon-admitting \(D\)'s per fixed \(C\), out of \(q^2(q-2)\) eligible \(D\)'s. Their quotient is the asserted proportion.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library scripts `verify_prime.py` and `verify_f9.py` compute rational tangent-chord adjacency via the dual quadratic form, rather than by using the parametrized step formula. `verify_prime.py` exhaustively enumerates every smooth second conic for a fixed standard \(C\) over \(\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7,\mathbb F_{11},\mathbb F_{13}\), isolates the exact \((2,2)\) contact locus using square binary quartics, and compares the entire graph spectrum against the predicted totient formula. `verify_f9.py` separately enumerates the full \((2,2)\) pencil family over \(\mathbb F_9\) and checks the quadratic-extension behavior for all nine base-field members over \(\mathbb F_3\). Exact machine outputs are in `prime_test_results.jsonl` and `f9_test_results.jsonl`.

For example, over \(\mathbb F_3\) there are nine eligible \(D\)'s per fixed \(C\), of which exactly three contribute one rational tetragon; over \(\mathbb F_9\), there are 567 eligible \(D\)'s, classified into 279 polygon-admitting and 288 polygon-free members. The calculations check small fields, including a nonprime extension, but the proof above establishes all odd prime powers and all extension degrees independently of finite sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Jow and Liang, *Poncelet's theorem for conics in any position and any characteristic* (arXiv:2112.11770, first posted 22 December 2021), classify the geometric incidence curve at tangency types including \((2,2)\) and prove the relevant geometric Poncelet porism. Their result does not provide these finite-field rational graph spectra or total all-period polygon counts.

Hungerbühler and Kusejko, *A Poncelet Criterion for Special Pairs of Conics in* \(PG(2,p^m)\) (2020), already prove an all-length totient distribution for a special **nested** pencil. That corresponds to the nonsplit-contact, rationally accessible portion of the picture; its \(\varphi(n)/2\) count is substantive prior coverage, not a new statement here. The present result adds the split-contact case, the inaccessible rational graph cases, Frobenius parity across all extensions, a joint enumeration across **all** ordered smooth conic pairs of type \((2,2)\), and the resulting universal total polygon formula.

Radnović and Ragas, *Poncelet Triangles and Tetragons over Finite Fields* (arXiv:2511.06347, first posted November 2025), analyze fixed-pencil conditions for \(n=3,4\) across all pencil types, including nontransverse ones. Their results overlap the low-period restrictions but not the entire all-length global count. Wang, *Counting pairs of conics over finite fields that satisfy the Poncelet n-gon condition* (arXiv:2309.16978, first posted September 2023), works in the transversely intersecting case and therefore does not imply the \((2,2)\) count.

## Limitations

The polygon count concerns finite fields of **odd** characteristic and exactly the two-distinct-double-contact stratum. Other intersection types and characteristic two are not treated. The formula counts rationally realised simple cycles rather than abstract geometric closure; many geometrically periodic pairs have no rational edges over the base field. The extension statement does not identify projective isomorphism classes over extensions beyond the given sign/parameter description. The same-model correctness and originality review is not independent verification; an equivalent universal enumeration in older or unindexed finite-geometry literature cannot be excluded definitively.
