# A universal reflection-transfer law for modular-difference lifts

## Finding

Let \(Y\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}\) be any nonempty two-sided subshift. Fix integers \(q\ge3\) and \(h\ge1\), and let
\[
X=X_{q,h}(Y)=\big\{x\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^{\mathbb Z}:(x_{i+h}-x_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}\in Y\big\}.
\]
Write \(\rho_Z(n)\) for the number of length-\(n\) factors of a subshift \(Z\), and \(r_Z(n)\) for the number of their equivalence classes under ordinary word reversal (classes are intersected with the factor language). For integers \(n>h\), put
\[
 f_h(n)=\begin{cases}\gcd(2,h),&\gcd(2,h)\mid(n-1),\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
Then \(\rho_X(0)=r_X(0)=1\), and for \(1\le n\le h\),
\[
\rho_X(n)=q^n,\qquad r_X(n)=\frac{q^n+q^{\lceil n/2\rceil}}2.
\]
For every \(n>h\), with \(m=n-h\), the **exact universal reflection-transfer law** is
\[
\boxed{\rho_X(n)=q^h\rho_Y(m),\qquad
r_X(n)=q^h\rho_Y(m)-\mathbf1_{\{0^m\in\mathcal L_m(Y)\}}
\frac{q^h-q^{(h+f_h(n))/2}}2.}
\]
In particular, the entire reflection-class correction depends on the base language *only* through whether it admits the all-zero word of the relevant length. Every length-\(n>h\) factor with a reversal also in \(X\) is \(h\)-periodic, and this occurs exactly when \(0^m\) is a base factor.

**Strictly ergodic specialization.** Let \(Y_p\) be the noble-means Sturmian subshift generated by \(0\mapsto0^p1\), \(1\mapsto0\), with any integer \(p\ge1\). Then \(\rho_{Y_p}(m)=m+1\) for \(m\ge1\) and \(0^m\) is a factor exactly when \(1\le m\le p+1\). Hence for \(n>h\),
\[
\boxed{r_{X_{q,h}(Y_p)}(n)=q^h(n-h+1)-
\mathbf1_{\{n\le h+p+1\}}\frac{q^h-q^{(h+f_h(n))/2}}2.}
\]
The existing strict-ergodicity theorem of Kang and Jäger applies to all these lifts, so this gives strictly ergodic, zero-entropy systems with fully explicit, eventually maximal reflection complexity. For \(h\ge3\) the last length having a *distinct* reflected pair is exactly \(h+p+1\); for \(h=1\) no distinct reflected pair exists at any length; for \(h=2\) distinct reflected pairs occur exactly at even lengths \(n\) with \(4\le n\le p+3\).

For example, \(p=1,q=3,h=3\) gives \(r_X(n)=1,3,6,18,45,72,108,135,162,\ldots\) at lengths \(n=0,1,\ldots,8\). Its two length windows \(n=4,5\) each contain exactly nine distinct reversal pairs; all lengths \(n\ge6\) contain none.

## Assumptions and scope

The base \(Y\) is a nonempty **two-sided** closed shift-invariant subset of \(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}\); it need not be minimal, ergodic, or reversal-closed. The modulus \(q\) is an integer at least three, not necessarily prime; \(h\) is any positive integer. Arithmetic on symbols is in \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\). All words are contiguous, and ordinary reversal means reversing symbol order *without* a relabeling or additive inversion. Reversal equivalence is taken on the subset of words actually occurring. No assertion about the excluded modulus \(q=2\) is made by the universal formula.

## Proof

**1. Exact lifting of finite words.** Fix any bi-infinite base point \(y\in Y\) and any starting vector \((a_0,\ldots,a_{h-1})\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^h\). The recurrence \(x_{i+h}=x_i+y_i\) determines a unique bi-infinite lift \(x\), by running the equation forward and backward separately along every residue class modulo \(h\). Thus the map sending any length-\(n>h\) lift factor to its initial \(h\) symbols and its length-\(m=n-h\) difference word is a bijection
\[
\mathcal L_n(X)\longleftrightarrow (\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^h\times\mathcal L_m(Y).
\]
For \(1\le n\le h\), the same construction realizes every word, yielding \(q^n\) factors. This establishes the ordinary factor-complexity claims.

**2. Reversal obstruction.** Let \(w=w_0\cdots w_{n-1}\in\mathcal L_n(X)\) for \(n>h\), and put \(v_i=w_{i+h}-w_i\), \(0\le i<m\). The difference word of the reversed word is exactly \(-v^R\). Since both \(v\) and any legal difference word use only the residues \(0,1\), and since \(q\ge3\) makes \(-1\notin\{0,1\}\), both \(w\) and \(w^R\) are legal if and only if \(v=0^m\). When this is the case, \(w_{i+h}=w_i\) throughout its length: it is a finite \(h\)-periodic word. Exactly \(q^h\) such factors exist if \(0^m\in\mathcal L_m(Y)\), and none otherwise. All their reversals remain legal.

**3. Count the reversal-fixed factors.** An \(h\)-periodic word of length \(n>h\) is specified by one symbol for each residue \(j\pmod h\). It is palindromic precisely when the residues \(j\) and \(n-1-j\pmod h\) are assigned equal symbols. The induced involution on \(\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z\) has exactly \(f_h(n)\) fixed residues: solving \(2j\equiv n-1\pmod h\) yields \(\gcd(2,h)\) solutions when that gcd divides \(n-1\), and none otherwise. The involution therefore has \((h+f_h(n))/2\) orbits. Consequently, exactly \(q^{(h+f_h(n))/2}\) of the \(q^h\) reversible factors are palindromes.

**4. Quotient by reversal.** Every distinct reflected pair saves exactly one class relative to counting its two words separately. There are \((q^h-q^{(h+f_h(n))/2})/2\) such pairs when the all-zero difference word occurs, and zero otherwise. This proves the displayed formula. For \(n\le h\), reversal acts on the full \(q\)-ary length-\(n\) language, with \(q^{\lceil n/2\rceil}\) fixed palindromes; the same orbit count gives the short-length formula.

**5. Noble-means application.** The binary noble-means language is Sturmian, hence has exactly \(m+1\) factors of length \(m\). Its runs of zeros have lengths \(p\) or \(p+1\): every occurrence of \(1\) in an inflated word arises as the final letter of \(0^p1\), and the intervening preimage letters have no adjacent \(1\)'s; both gap lengths occur. Therefore \(0^m\) is legal precisely for \(m\le p+1\). Substitution in the universal formula proves the specialized expression. For \(h\ge3\), the nonfixed-pair count is positive for every \(n>h\) with \(m\le p+1\). For \(h=2\), it is positive exactly when \(n\) is even; for \(h=1\) it vanishes. This proves the sharp-boundary statements.

## Verification

The standalone `check_reflection.py` explicitly constructs lifted factors, forms reversal equivalence classes, and compares against the claimed formulas. It passed all \(18\) combinations \(p\in\{1,2,3\}\), \(q\in\{3,4\}\), \(h\in\{1,2,3\}\), over lengths \(1\) through \(8\), using finite noble-means substitution words. A second check uses exact cyclic languages of five periodic base words for \(30\) further parameter triples, again over lengths \(1\) through \(8\). These are consistency tests only; the unrestricted formula is proved by the four preceding algebraic steps.

## Relationship to prior work

Kang and Jäger, arXiv:2609.01140 (first public version September 1, 2026), establish the finite prefix-lift/skew-product structure and strict ergodicity of noble-means lifts. Those assertions are inputs, not new claims. Their work does not furnish an exact reflection-complexity or reversal-pair formula for the modular-difference lifts. Allouche, Campbell, Li, Shallit and Stipulanti, arXiv:2406.09302, define reflection complexity, provide the general orbit-count identity, and determine the binary complementation-symmetric Rote case. Their Rote formula concerns modulus two and does not imply the sign-obstruction mechanism or its exact orbit counts for all moduli at least three and all memory lengths. Prior examples of finite reversal cutoffs in unrelated substitutions likewise do not imply this general transfer formula.

## Limitations

The theorem is about ordinary symbol reversal, not simultaneous reversal and group inversion. At modulus two the obstruction \(-1\ne1\) fails, and a separate theory is needed. The noble-means zero-run bound and Sturmian factor complexity are classical; novelty is claimed only for the precise reflection-transfer statement and its lifted consequences. The new result has not been independently audited, and unindexed prior treatments of cyclic group extensions may conceal equivalent formulations. Finite tests by themselves do not certify an infinite formula.
