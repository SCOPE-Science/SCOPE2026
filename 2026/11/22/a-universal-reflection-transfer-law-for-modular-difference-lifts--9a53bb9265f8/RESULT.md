# A reflection-transfer law for modular-difference lifts

## Finding

Let \(Y\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}\) be a nonempty two-sided subshift. For integers \(q\ge3\) and \(h\ge1\), form
\[
 X=X_{q,h}(Y)=\{x\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^{\mathbb Z}:(x_{i+h}-x_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}\in Y\}.
\]
For a subshift \(Z\), let \(\rho_Z(n)\) count its length-\(n\) factors and let \(r_Z(n)\) count their classes under ordinary word reversal. The classes are restricted to the factor language; a factor whose reversal is absent is in a singleton class. At \(n=0\) both counts for \(X\) are one. For \(1\le n\le h\),
\[
 \rho_X(n)=q^n,\qquad r_X(n)=\frac{q^n+q^{\lceil n/2\rceil}}2.
\]
For \(n>h\) put \(m=n-h\), and set \(f_h(n)=\gcd(2,h)\) if \(\gcd(2,h)\mid(n-1)\), or zero otherwise. Then
\[
 \boxed{\rho_X(n)=q^h\rho_Y(m),\qquad
 r_X(n)=q^h\rho_Y(m)-\mathbf1_{\{0^m\in\mathcal L_m(Y)\}}
 \frac{q^h-q^{(h+f_h(n))/2}}2.}
\]
Every length-\(n>h\) factor whose reversal also occurs has zero \(h\)-difference word. Such factors exist exactly when \(0^m\) occurs in \(Y\). The correction to ordinary factor complexity therefore depends on the base language only through zero-run availability.

## Noble-means lifts

Let \(Y_p\) be the noble-means Sturmian subshift generated by \(0\mapsto0^p1\), \(1\mapsto0\), with \(p\ge1\). Its length-\(m\) factor complexity is \(m+1\) for \(m\ge1\) and \(0^m\) occurs exactly for \(1\le m\le p+1\). Thus for \(n>h\),
\[
 r_{X_{q,h}(Y_p)}(n)=q^h(n-h+1)-\mathbf1_{\{n\le h+p+1\}}
 \frac{q^h-q^{(h+f_h(n))/2}}2.
\]
For \(h\ge3\) the last length having a distinct reversal pair is \(h+p+1\). For \(h=1\) there is no distinct reversal pair at any length. For \(h=2\), the lengths with a distinct reversal pair are **exactly the even lengths \(2\le n\le p+3\)**: length 2 comes from the full short language, and the other even lengths come from the zero-difference factors.

The lifts have zero entropy by their linear factor complexity. Their strict ergodicity is an existing result: identify the code in Kang–Jäger, arXiv:2609.01140v2, Theorem 1.6, by taking \(\kappa=q\) and \(\ell=h+1\). The reflection-complexity proof does not use strict ergodicity. For example, \(p=1,q=3,h=3\) gives \(r_X(n)=1,3,6,18,45,72,108,135,162\) at lengths \(0,1,\ldots,8\); at lengths \(4\) and \(5\) there are nine distinct reversal pairs each, and none at \(n\ge6\).

## Proof

**Lift the finite words.** A base point \(y\in Y\) and arbitrary residues \(a_0,\ldots,a_{h-1}\) determine a unique bi-infinite lift with \(x_j=a_j\) for \(0\le j<h\) by solving \(x_{i+h}=x_i+y_i\) in both directions on each residue class modulo \(h\). For \(n>h\), this gives a bijection between length-\(n\) lift factors and pairs consisting of an arbitrary \(h\)-letter prefix and an allowed length-\(m\) base factor, since each base factor can be shifted to start at index zero in a two-sided base point. When \(n\le h\), the same construction realizes all \(q^n\) words.

**Identify the reversible factors.** For \(n>h\), write \(v_i=w_{i+h}-w_i\), \(0\le i<m\). The \(h\)-difference word of the reversed word is \(-v^R\). Since \(q\ge3\), the only common residue of \(\{0,1\}\) and \(\{0,-1\}\) is zero. Thus having both words legal forces \(v=0^m\); conversely, if \(v=0^m\) is legal then the reversed word with that same difference word is legal by the lifting bijection. There are then exactly \(q^h\) reversible words, specified by arbitrary symbols on the \(h\) residue classes.

**Count fixed words.** On a zero-difference word, reversal acts on the class labels by \(j\mapsto n-1-j\pmod h\). The congruence \(2j\equiv n-1\pmod h\) has exactly \(f_h(n)\) solutions, so this involution has \((h+f_h(n))/2\) orbits. A palindrome is constant on each orbit, yielding \(q^{(h+f_h(n))/2}\) palindromes among the reversible words. Each distinct pair saves one class. For \(n\le h\) the full language has \(q^{\lceil n/2\rceil}\) palindromes and the same pair count gives the short-length formula.

**Apply to the substitution.** The noble-means language is Sturmian. The word has no adjacent ones, and both preimage factors \(00\) and \(010\) occur: for \(p=1\), \(00\) is already present in the third iterate of \(0\); for \(p\ge2\) it occurs in the first iterate. The presence of \(1\) and absence of \(11\) force \(010\) in the two-sided language. Consecutive zeros in the preimage or zeros separated by a single one produce gaps of \(p\) or \(p+1\) zeros, respectively, between consecutive ones in the inflated word. These are the only types of gap. This proves the zero-run criterion. For \(n>h\), the pair count is positive when the zero run is allowed and \(f_h(n)<h\); this is automatic for \(h\ge3\), impossible for \(h=1\), and equivalent to \(n\) even if \(h=2\). The short-length formula contributes \((q^2-q)/2>0\) distinct pairs at \(n=2\) for \(h=2\), and none at \(n=0,1\).

## Verification and relationship to prior work

The included `check_reflection.py` is a finite consistency checker. It constructs lifted factors for sampled noble-means cases and exact languages of several periodic bases, and compares their reversal classes with the formula, including short lengths. The lifting and orbit-count arguments, not these finite samples, establish the all-base theorem.

Kang and Jäger, *Permutative lifts of strictly ergodic subshifts*, arXiv:2609.01140v2, establish the relevant lift structure and strict-ergodicity result; those are not claimed as new. Allouche, Campbell, Li, Shallit and Stipulanti, *The reflection complexity of sequences over finite alphabets*, arXiv:2406.09302v4, give the general reflection orbit identity in §3 and treat mod-2 Rote differences in §6. Those frameworks do not compute the exact zero-run and affine residue correction for \(q\ge3\). The same-day *Parity dichotomy in the reversal Hamming spectrum of Sturmian integral lifts* obtains an asymptotic Hamming separation from reversals for the memory-one Sturmian lift, which implies eventual absence of reversal pairs when \(q\ge3\), not the exact all-length counts or arbitrary-memory transfer formula.

The law allows composite \(q\) and does not require that \(Y\) is minimal or reversal-invariant. It uses ordinary reversal, not reversal followed by symbol inversion. At modulus 2 the sign obstruction disappears. The transfer law is not a new result on the dynamical ergodicity of these lifts.
