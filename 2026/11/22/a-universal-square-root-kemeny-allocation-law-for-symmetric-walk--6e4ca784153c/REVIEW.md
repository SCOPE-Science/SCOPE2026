# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof works for every connected, simple, vertex- and edge-transitive core of order at least two and degree at least one, and each positive integer leaf multiplicity. It identifies strict matrix inverse-trace convexity on the feasible symmetric chain domain, reduces unrestricted optima by group averaging, derives the precise spectrum including the leaf-difference multiplicity, and solves a strictly convex scalar minimization. The zero-target normalization is explicit. The delivered program recomputes exact inverse traces on independent graph constructions and validates the radical formulas (775 assertions). Finite tests do not establish the infinite theorem, which is supported by the analytic steps. The proof excludes disconnected boundary choices through divergence and retains all core and leaf stochastic constraints.

Durastante–Gnazzo–Meini (2026), inspected in full HTML, treats small-norm structure-preserving perturbations and general optimization rather than the same full-family classification. Boyd–Diaconis–Xiao and the closest public leaf-corona research record concern SLEM/fastest mixing, not the inverse-spectral-sum objective. The latter public record was accessible in search-summary form only, and its complete document was not accessible; that access limit remains a priority risk. No high-confidence domination by a stronger prior claim was found in actual searches. The archived family calculations are new relative to the accessible same-category claim list, but a search result is not a proof of worldwide novelty.

## Value
**PASS.** The exact global solution covers every vertex- and edge-transitive core and every fixed positive private-leaf multiplicity, retaining unrestricted edge-by-edge competition and uniqueness. The core's entire influence compresses to one natural spectral invariant. Two canonical graph families exhibit qualitatively different optimization regimes: vanishing pendant weights with a fixed penalty for cycles, versus positive pendant weights and an unbounded maximum-degree heuristic penalty for cliques. This is an exact network-design classification with interpretable performance consequences, not an arbitrary finite benchmark.
