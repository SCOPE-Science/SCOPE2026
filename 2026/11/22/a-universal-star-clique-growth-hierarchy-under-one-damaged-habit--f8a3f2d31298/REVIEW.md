# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The three killed-migration operators are explicitly defined for every \(n\ge3\), \(m>0\), \(\delta>0\) and \(r\in\mathbb R\). Symmetry reduces the Perron eigenvalue problem to two quadratic equations and one cubic, and the positive eigenvector interpretation selects the correct roots. Substituting the clique root gives two exact, strictly signed polynomial identities, establishing \(\Lambda_L>\Lambda_K>\Lambda_H\) without parameter sampling. Implicit differentiation and bounded positive roots justify the endpoint limits. The bundled Python checker independently matched full-matrix eigenvalues for selected cases and passed 21,516 supplementary assertions; this does not substitute for the analytic proof. Limits: no nonlinear or stochastic-population comparison follows.

**Originality (PASS on inspected evidence).** Suzuki and Economo (2024) analyze stochastic competitive metacommunity variability with degree-normalized dispersal, not the one-degraded-site eigenvalue hierarchy. Nakagiri and coauthors (2024) had already observed the adverse effects of corridor addition in three-patch metapopulations, so neither that qualitative effect nor the three-patch motivation is presented as novel. The evaluated statement establishes a parameter-uniform three-way ranking for every star size, and rigorous weak- and strong-damage limiting laws. Aldous and Fill provide classical star/clique walk properties, including stationary measures, but the inspected chapter does not state the positive finite-killing comparison. Indexed related studies concern stochastic extinction times, fixed corridor budgets, symmetric edge-wise dispersal or different objective functions; none inspected implies the uniform all-size theorem. **Residual risk:** The complete 2024 hub-effect article was inaccessible and may contain additional analytic cases; general killed-walk spectral results beyond the inspected sources could imply the theorem. No claim of historical priority beyond inspected evidence is made.

**Value (PASS).** This is a complete natural comparison, not a selected numerical parameter slice. It resolves a conservation-network modeling question for an entire family: converting a star to a fully connected corridor network *strictly reduces* asymptotic low-density growth if the damaged patch was a leaf, but *strictly raises* it if the damaged patch was the hub, regardless of migration intensity, damage severity or star size. The exact limits quantify the mechanism in weak- and strong-damage regimes. Practical consequences require calibration and nonlinear or stochastic analysis; spectral growth must not be called extinction probability.
