# A universal threefold high-adhesion mixing-clock ratio for two riffle release rules

## Finding

For every fixed integer \(n\ge4\), let \(B_{1-q}\) denote binomial-cut, block-weighted adhesive riffle shuffling and \(I_{1-q}\) the same cut-and-bond model with midpoint-key block release (definitions of Jiang, arXiv:2611.02092v1). Put \(q=1-p\downarrow0\). For every fixed real \(s>0\), the full-deck total-variation profiles from an ordered deck obey

\[
\lim_{q\downarrow0} d_{B,1-q}\!\left(\left\lfloor s/q\right\rfloor\right)=D_n(s),\qquad
\lim_{q\downarrow0} d_{I,1-q}\!\left(\left\lfloor s/q\right\rfloor\right)=D_n(3s).
\]

Here \(D_n(s)\) is the nonconstant, continuous, strictly decreasing distance-to-uniform profile of an explicitly specified irreducible continuous-time chain on \((n-1)!\) cyclic-rotation cosets. In particular, for every fixed
\(0<\varepsilon<1-1/(n-1)!\),

\[
\lim_{q\downarrow0}q\,t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{B,1-q}(\varepsilon)=\tau_n(\varepsilon),\qquad
\lim_{q\downarrow0}q\,t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{I,1-q}(\varepsilon)=\tfrac13\tau_n(\varepsilon),
\]

where \(D_n(\tau_n(\varepsilon))=\varepsilon\). Consequently, the ratio of the two mixing times tends to \(1/3\) for every such fixed \(n,\varepsilon\). The universal factor is in the *strong-adhesion, fixed-deck limit*, not a statement that the finite-adhesion chains are time changes of each other.

## Assumptions and scope

All permutations act on the right by their one-line output-order lists, so the current deck list \(x\) is transformed to \(xg\). Let \(G=S_n\) and let \(H\le G\) be the order-\(n\) cyclic-rotation subgroup generated by \(r=(2,3,\ldots,n,1)\). For a nonempty binomial cut \(1\le k\le n-1\), the left packet has \(k\) cards, the right packet \(n-k\), and the cut has probability \(w_k=2^{-n}\binom nk\). Each internal packet contact survives independently with probability \(p=1-q\); the two blocks from different packets never have a mutual original contact.

For a particular cut, break exactly one internal contact. Let \(A,C\) be the early and late subblocks of that packet and \(B\) the intact opposite packet. All three blocks are nonempty. Write \(g_{k,j}\in G\) for the one-line permutation of the nonrotation output \((A,B,C)\), where the index \(j\) distinguishes the internal bond and the broken packet. There are exactly \(n-2\) choices of \(j\) per nonempty cut. Repeated output permutations across cuts or bonds are counted with multiplicity.

Define the nonnegative measure on \(G\setminus H\)

\[
\nu(g)=\frac1{3\,2^n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom nk\sum_{j\,\in\,\mathcal J_k}\mathbf 1\{g_{k,j}=g\},
\qquad
\lambda_n=\sum_{g\notin H}\nu(g)=\frac{n-2}{3}\bigl(1-2^{1-n}\bigr).
\]

The index set \(\mathcal J_k\) consists of \(k-1\) internal left-packet and \(n-k-1\) internal right-packet gaps. Define \(X=G/H\), the **right-coset space**, of cardinality \((n-1)!\), and on real functions \(f\colon X\to\mathbb R\) define

\[
(Lf)(xH)=\frac1n\sum_{h\in H}\sum_{g\notin H}\nu(g)
\bigl[f(xhgH)-f(xH)\bigr].
\]

Let \(U_X\) denote uniform probability on \(X\), and put
\[
D_n(s)=\left\|\delta_H e^{sL}-U_X\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}},\quad s\ge0.
\]

The claim concerns full-deck uniform mixing from an ordered deck and each fixed deck size. No assertion concerns a simultaneous deck-size and adhesion limit, arbitrary initial distribution in a nonuniform coset, or the third card-weighted cut-first interleaver.

## Proof

**1. First-order departures from the rotation subgroup.** For \(q=0\), each nonempty cut gives two intact packets. Both release rules order their two full packets by a fair coin (the two midpoint keys coincide at \(1/2\)), hence their common increment distribution is supported on \(H\). Every rotation and identity has positive probability, so the resulting walk on each coset of \(H\) is irreducible and aperiodic.

For one broken internal contact in a nonempty cut, the packet with the break contributes consecutive blocks \(A,C\), while the other contributes \(B\). The three order-preserving block interleavings are \((A,C,B)\), \((B,A,C)\), and \((A,B,C)\). The first two are rotations, regardless of which packet was split, while the third is not a rotation because it separates the consecutive cards of \(A,C\) by the intact opposite packet. Under block-weighted release, the three arrangements have probability \(1/3\) each.

For midpoint release, a block occupying packet positions \(a,\ldots,b\) of a packet of length \(m\) has key \((a+b-1)/(2m)\). If \(A\) ends at position \(j\), its key is \(j/(2m)<1/2\) and the following block \(C\) has key \((m+j)/(2m)>1/2\); the intact opposite packet has key exactly \(1/2\). Thus midpoint release deterministically outputs \((A,B,C)\). A specific one-break event has probability \(w_k q(1-q)^{n-3}\). Its off-rotation first-order coefficient is therefore \(w_k/3\) under block release and \(w_k\) under midpoint release, even if several events yield the same permutation. The probability of two or more breaks is \(O_n(q^2)\). Accordingly, on \(G\setminus H\), the first-order increment measures are \(\nu\) and \(3\nu\) respectively. Summing over \(n-2\) possible gaps per cut gives the stated \(\lambda_n\).

**2. Finite-state averaging.** Let \(P_{F,q}\) act on functions on \(G\) by \((P_{F,q}F)(x)=\mathbb E[F(xg)]\), and let \(P_0=P_{B,0}=P_{I,0}\). Put \((\Pi F)(x)=n^{-1}\sum_{h\in H}F(xh)\). Since \(P_0\) is an aperiodic irreducible right-\(H\) walk, \(P_0\Pi=\Pi P_0=\Pi\), and on \(\ker\Pi\) its powers have a geometric norm bound \(\|(P_0-\Pi)^k\|\le C_n\rho_n^k\) for some \(\rho_n<1\). Finiteness of the model gives the uniform operator expansion \(P_{F,q}=P_0+qA_F+O_n(q^2)\).

In the decomposition \(\operatorname{im}\Pi\oplus\ker\Pi\), its four blocks are \(I+q\Pi A_F\Pi+O(q^2)\), two cross-blocks of order \(q\), and a strictly contractive fast block plus \(O(q)\). For \(k\le S/q\) the fast component contributes only \(O_n(q)\) to the accumulated slow component: its initial transient has geometrically summable length, and its thereafter quasi-stationary size is \(O_n(q)\). Euler convergence of the slow block implies, for each fixed \(s>0\),

\[
P_{F,q}^{\lfloor s/q\rfloor}\longrightarrow
\Pi\exp\bigl(s\Pi A_F\Pi\bigr)
\]

in finite-dimensional operator norm (with the exponential acting as identity on the orthogonal complement after the left projection). Under the identification of right-\(H\)-invariant functions with functions on \(X\), \(\Pi A_B\Pi=L\) and \(\Pi A_I\Pi=3L\): the first-order terms supported inside \(H\) annihilate coset functions, whereas the off-\(H\) coefficient is averaged over the uniformly distributed rotation before the rare departure. Taking the adjoint acting on point masses, the limiting full-deck distribution is uniform within each coset, with coset masses \(\delta_H e^{sL}\) and \(\delta_H e^{3sL}\), respectively. Summing absolute deviations coset by coset gives the asserted total-variation limits.

**3. Irreducibility and strict crossing.** The cut after card two and a break after the first left card has nonzero coefficient for \(g=(1,3,4,\ldots,n,2)\), the cycle \((2\ 3\ \cdots\ n)\). Together with the cyclic rotation \(r=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ n)\), it generates \(S_n\), since \(rg^{-1}=(1\ 2)\) and conjugating yields adjacent transpositions. Therefore the jump support of \(L\), after freely averaging over \(H\), connects all right cosets. The generator is irreducible. Left translation of \(G\) preserves the kernel, so the uniform coset measure is stationary; irreducibility makes it unique. Each positive-time kernel \(e^{sL}\) has strictly positive entries, implying strict total-variation contraction for any nonzero signed measure of total mass zero. Consequently \(D_n\) decreases strictly, with \(D_n(0)=1-1/(n-1)!\) and \(D_n(s)\to0\). The distance remains positive at every finite \(s\), since the invertible semigroup cannot map \(\delta_H\ne U_X\) exactly to \(U_X\).

**4. Mixing-time limits.** For every \(\varepsilon\) in the stated range, a unique \(\tau_n(\varepsilon)>0\) satisfies \(D_n(\tau_n)=\varepsilon\). For any \(s_-<\tau_n<s_+\), the pointwise profile convergence at the two positive times and the monotonicity of discrete-time total-variation distance trap the rescaled block-weighted mixing time between \(s_-\) and \(s_+\). The same argument for the midpoint profile \(D_n(3s)\) gives \(\tau_n/3\). Sending the brackets to their common endpoints proves the limits and their ratio.

## Verification

A standalone standard-library Python checker `verify.py` verifies the exact one-bond classifications and rational off-rotation coefficients for every deck size from four through eight. It constructs all right-coset quotient generators for five and six cards with rational entries, checks exact row and column sums, verifies reachability of all cosets, and evaluates example continuous-time profiles by Poisson uniformization. Run `python3 verify.py` from the extracted package. The all-deck assertion and convergence of mixing times are analytical theorems above, **not** conclusions drawn from the finite computations.

For instance the exact total off-rotation rates are \(\lambda_4=7/12\), \(\lambda_5=15/16\), and \(\lambda_6=31/24\) for block-weighted release, and three times these values for midpoint release. These rates alone do not determine mixing times; the complete quotient generator is essential.

## Relationship to prior work

Jiang (arXiv:2611.02092v1, Theorem 2.5 and Sections 2 and 10) defines these specific cut-first processes, proves fixed-deck mixing-time order \(\Theta((1-p)^{-1})\), and identifies single-contact-break insertion moves, including a relative factor three for certain named insertions. Neither an all-deck quotient generator nor the exact full-deck limiting total-variation profile and universal mixing-time ratio appears in the inspected primary text. The present model-specific computation of **all** one-break departures plus finite-state averaging yields the sharper results. A previously available four-card exact-profile calculation already gives the ratio for that single deck size, so the four-card special case is not claimed new. The extension to every fixed deck size is the claimed contribution.

Palomares (2025) and Jonasson--Morris (2015) study a different correlated-label or Markov-word riffle model whose cut is not binomial at nontrivial adhesion. Their results therefore do not imply this cut-first comparison. Generic finite-state singular-perturbation and Markov-chain averaging arguments are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The limits require first fixing \(n\), then letting adhesion tend to one; no uniformity in deck size or finite-adhesion threefold time-change identity is established. At observation time zero, the full-deck distance is \(1-1/n!\), while the positive-time rescaled profile approaches \(1-1/(n-1)!\) as \(s\downarrow0\): the fast intra-coset equilibration creates a boundary layer, hence the restriction \(s>0\). The exact quotient generator is explicit but computing \(D_n\) on \((n-1)!\) states may be expensive for large \(n\). The work does not settle any large-deck cutoff question. Historical priority beyond the inspected sources remains a risk. Independent assessment has not been performed.
