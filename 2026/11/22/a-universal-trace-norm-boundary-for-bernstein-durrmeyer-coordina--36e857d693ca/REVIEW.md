# Scientific review: Bernstein–Durrmeyer coordinate commutators

## Correctness
PASS. The operator normalization is fixed explicitly on unweighted \(L^2(0,1)\). The known selfadjoint Legendre eigenvalues and coordinate three-term recurrence give an exact finite skew-Jacobi representation, including the terminal coefficient and zero tail. The product formula supplies both the Gaussian square-root-frequency profile and a uniform summable exponential tail. Blockwise constant-weight Jacobi spectra and rank-controlled perturbations establish the normalized all-Schatten trace limit, including quasi-Schatten exponents below one. A separate Toeplitz principal block proves the sharp operator-norm lower bound. Gamma integration yields the trace-norm, Hilbert–Schmidt, and stable-rank constants. Exact-rational kernel-versus-matrix checks independently test the finite representation but are not used as proof of the limiting theorem.

## Originality
PASS, with a residual historical-priority risk. The closest examined primary literature treats Bernstein–Durrmeyer kernels, classical eigenstructure, approximation to the identity, and commutativity with differential or companion approximation operators; none of the inspected statements implies the normalized \(\sqrt n\)-window Schatten law for \([M_n,X]\). The closest retrieved published mathematical record analyzes \(\operatorname{tr}(M_n^s)\) on simplices; it uses the same eigenvalue mechanism, but the coordinate commutator is instead off-diagonal and its spectral mass is governed by Legendre recurrence weights. The Bernstein–Kantorovich nonnormality result concerns a different nonselfadjoint smoother. Generic full-matrix locally Toeplitz spectral theorems can return a zero distribution at their usual \(1/n\) normalization; extracting the present \(1/\sqrt n\) distribution is a distinct nontrivial analysis. A stronger unlocated semiclassical trace result might imply this special case; complete historical priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The coordinate multiplier is the natural position observable and the Bernstein–Durrmeyer operator is a canonical positive approximant of the identity. Their commutator measures the spectral cost of noncommuting finite smoothing with position. The exact nonzero trace-norm limit, vanishing sharp operator norm, general Schatten exponents and diverging stable rank demonstrate a genuine critical spectral transition that ordinary strong or operator-norm approximation cannot see. The exact coefficients are invariant features of a natural operator family rather than an arbitrary isolated numerical computation.

## Closest literature and limitations

Berdysheva–Jetter (2010) covers related eigenstructure. Published records on Dirichlet Bernstein–Durrmeyer spectral-power traces and Bernstein–Kantorovich nonnormality have distinct claims. Scope is the classical unweighted one-dimensional degree-\(n\) smoother, a fixed positive exponent, and leading-order laws only; no individual fixed-rank singular-value formula or higher-order correction is established. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
