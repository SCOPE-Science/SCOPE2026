When an equal mixture of infected and healthy cells is randomly split and daughters above one-half infection are removed, a two-cell offspring is strictly worse than both one- and three-cell offspring for the immediate expected number of surviving infected cells, surviving infected–healthy contacts, and the infected share of expected surviving cells, for every even parent size at least six. Exact hypergeometric formulas expose the parity effect: an evenly infected two-cell daughter lets both offspring survive, whereas odd-size daughters cannot do so at exactly one-half parental prevalence.
