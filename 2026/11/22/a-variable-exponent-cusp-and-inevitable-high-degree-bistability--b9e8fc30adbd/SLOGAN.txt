The fixed-point-free power map has an exact real-exponent cusp threshold \(q_d=(d+1)^{d-1}/d^d\): its strictly alternating dynamics support one, two, or three primitive two-cycles, with two distinct attractors inside the sharp bistability wedge. For every fixed strict-alternation parameter triple, sufficiently large power exponents force bistability, and the attracting left coordinates approach \(0\) and \(c\) at explicit exponential rates.
