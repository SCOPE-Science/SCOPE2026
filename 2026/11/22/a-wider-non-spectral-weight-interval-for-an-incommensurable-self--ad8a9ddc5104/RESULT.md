# A wider non-spectral weight interval for an incommensurable self-similar measure

## Finding
For the probability measure \(\nu_p\) on \([0,1]\) generated by the two contractions
\[
g_1(x)=x/2,\qquad g_2(x)=(x+2)/3,
\]
with weights \(p\) and \(1-p\), define \(\alpha\in(1/4,13/50)\) as the unique root of
\[
3163277[\alpha(1-\alpha)]^9=1,
\]
and \(\beta\in(6/7,9/10)\) as the unique root on \((11/16,1)\) of
\[
92381\beta^{11}(1-\beta)^5=1.
\]
**Theorem.** For every \(p\in(\alpha,\beta)\), the measure \(\nu_p\) is not spectral: \(L^2(\nu_p)\) has no orthonormal basis of real-frequency exponentials. Numerically \(\alpha\approx0.254168573754829\) and \(\beta\approx0.857524036121288\). In particular, the rigorously excluded interval contains the previously known \([1/3,5/6]\) strictly in its interior. The conclusion is open at \(\alpha,\beta\).

## Assumptions and scope
For every \(0<p<1\), \(\nu_p\) is the unique Borel probability measure with
\[
\nu_p=p\,(g_1)_\#\nu_p+(1-p)\,(g_2)_\#\nu_p.
\]
Its compact attractor lies in \([0,1]\). Since \(g_1([0,1])=[0,1/2]\) and \(g_2([0,1])=[2/3,1]\), the first-level pieces, and hence distinct finite-word cylinder pieces, are disjoint. For a word with \(m\) symbols 1 and \(n\) symbols 2, its affine contraction has scale \(1/(2^m3^n)\) and its cylinder has probability mass \(p^m(1-p)^n\). The relevant field is the Fourier analysis of fractal measures; spectrality means a complete orthonormal exponential system in \(L^2(\nu_p)\).

## Proof
**1. Equal-scale and equal-mass translations.** For nonnegative integers \(m,n\), let \(A_{m,n}\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be recursively defined by
\[
A_{m,0}=A_{0,n}=\{0\},\qquad
A_{m,n}=A_{m-1,n}\cup\bigl((2^m-1)3^{n-1}+A_{m,n-1}\bigr).
\]
Prefix decomposition by the first symbol proves inductively that the affine maps for the \(\binom{m+n}{m}\) words having these symbol counts are exactly
\[
x\longmapsto\frac{x+3^n-1+2a}{2^m3^n},\qquad a\in A_{m,n}.
\]
They are distinct because their compact cylinder images are disjoint; each has the common mass \(M=p^m(1-p)^n\). In particular \(|A_{m,n}|=\binom{m+n}{m}\).

**2. A finite-cylinder spectral obstruction.** Suppose \(\nu_p\) admits an orthonormal exponential basis \(\{e^{2\pi i\lambda x}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}\). By translating frequencies, take \(0\in\Lambda\). For the equal-count cylinders above, normalize their indicators by \(M^{-1/2}\). Pairwise orthogonality and Parseval give, for \(a,b\in A_{m,n}\),
\[
\delta_{a,b}=M\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
\left|\widehat{\nu_p}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2^m3^n}\right)\right|^2
\exp\left(-\frac{4\pi i\lambda(a-b)}{2^m3^n}\right).
\]
The nonnegative coefficients on the right sum to one when \(a=b\), so they define a probability measure \(\sigma\) on the circle by pushing forward the corresponding discrete measure under \(\lambda\mapsto\exp(4\pi i\lambda/(2^m3^n))\). Its Fourier moments at every nonzero element of \(A_{m,n}-A_{m,n}\) vanish. The \(\lambda=0\) term contributes an atom of mass at least \(M\) at the unit point.

If \([-L,L]\cap\mathbb Z\subseteq A_{m,n}-A_{m,n}\), the square of the Dirichlet polynomial \(\sum_{j=0}^L z^j\) has integral \(L+1\) against \(\sigma\), but has value \((L+1)^2\) at the unit point. Positivity therefore forces
\[
(L+1)M\le1.
\]
This proves the obstruction directly from Parseval and a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial.

**3. The two new exact certificates.** The program `verify_differences.py` implements the stated finite-set recurrence using arbitrary-precision integers. Its binary-set representation proves, without floating point arithmetic,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|A_{9,9}|&=48620,& [-3163276,3163276]\cap\mathbb Z&\subseteq A_{9,9}-A_{9,9},\\
|A_{11,5}|&=4368,& [-92380,92380]\cap\mathbb Z&\subseteq A_{11,5}-A_{11,5}.
\end{aligned}
\]
In fact, the next positive integer after each displayed radius is absent from its respective difference set. The checker verifies exact cardinalities, maximal translation values, first missing differences, and rational sign certificates for the boundary tests. Hence spectrality would require simultaneously
\[
3163277[p(1-p)]^9\le1,\qquad 92381p^{11}(1-p)^5\le1.
\]

**4. The weight interval.** The first left-hand side increases on \((0,1/2)\), decreases on \((1/2,1)\), equals one at \(\alpha\), and is strictly greater than one at \(p=2/3\). It therefore exceeds one throughout \((\alpha,2/3]\). The second left-hand side increases on \((0,11/16)\), decreases on \((11/16,1)\), exceeds one at \(p=2/3\), and equals one at the uniquely specified high-branch root \(\beta\). It exceeds one throughout \([2/3,\beta)\). Thus for every \(p\in(\alpha,\beta)\), at least one of the spectrality necessary conditions is violated. This proves the theorem.

For exact root location the checker verifies
\[
\begin{gathered}
3163277(\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac34)^9<1<3163277(\tfrac{13}{50}\cdot\tfrac{37}{50})^9,\\
92381(\tfrac67)^{11}(\tfrac17)^5>1>92381(\tfrac9{10})^{11}(\tfrac1{10})^5.
\end{gathered}
\]
The required comparison at \(p=2/3\) is also checked exactly.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_differences.py` with Python 3 and its standard library; no external packages, floating point arithmetic, or downloaded data are used. The program computes every difference via bit operations on exact integers and checks the first absent positive difference for both specified finite translation sets. It additionally uses `fractions.Fraction` for every endpoint inequality. Each finite certificate is independently reproducible, but this finite computation alone does not prove the spectral obstruction; the Parseval–Dirichlet-polynomial argument above supplies that logical step.

## Relationship to prior work
Mao and Wu, *On the spectrality of non-homogeneous self-similar measures*, arXiv:2611.18106v1 (13 November 2026), prove non-spectrality for this same system on \([1/3,5/6]\) using the equal-count pairs \((5,5)\) and \((9,5)\). Their general finite-cylinder obstruction and recurrence motivate the present calculation. The new pairs \((9,9)\) and \((11,5)\), their exact difference-set radii, and the resulting algebraic endpoint interval are not stated in that paper. Its theorem does not imply the new intervals \((\alpha,1/3)\) and \((5/6,\beta)\). The earlier work of Mao and Wu, arXiv:2609.04701, is about the distinct golden-mean system with contraction ratios \(1/2\) and \(1/4\).

## Limitations
No claim is made at either algebraic boundary, outside \((\alpha,\beta)\), or about the existence of Fourier frames (rather than orthonormal exponential bases). This is a substantial but incomplete extension of a non-spectrality range, not a proof of the all-weight conjecture. The stated endpoint interval is the union certified by these two selected finite-cylinder obstructions, not an assertion that they are optimal among all possible obstructions. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
