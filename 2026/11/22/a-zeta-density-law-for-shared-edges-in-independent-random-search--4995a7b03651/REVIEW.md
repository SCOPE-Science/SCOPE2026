# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The mathematical object is the *key-labelled direct parent–child edge intersection* of two independent uniform-permutation binary search trees. For each ordered pair of keys, an explicit nearest-lower-priority criterion gives two disjoint oriented edge events. Their beta-integral probabilities are verified by exhaustive permutation enumeration through seven keys. Squaring and summing the resulting exact edge kernel proves the expectation and its second-order constant. The infinite version of the same priority model defines a bounded stationary ergodic local edge count; restriction monotonicity and an explicit harmonic-number expectation bound prove convergence in \(L^1\). The proof covers all \(n\ge2\) and does not confuse finite checks with the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with stated historical risk

The closest exact comparison is Seidel and Aragon's classical priority/ancestor theory: ancestor events are broader than direct parent–child events and do not determine the two-copy labelled edge overlap without the additional kernel calculation. Holmgren–Janson derive one-tree fringe-subtree limit laws, not coincident key-labelled edges across independent copies. Chu–Cleary study common **leaf-split** edges of **uniform Catalan shape trees**, which are not the same random model or edge event. A published Cayley-tree edge-overlap result concerns another ensemble with a vanishing shared-edge fraction. Distinct source statements and their implications were checked, rather than relying on absence of matching words. Full historical uniqueness is not proved; a general treap local-limit or edge-kernel theorem could subsume the density statement.

The theorem gives both a reproducible exact finite calibration and the limiting retained fraction \(\pi^2/3-3\), which is unexpectedly positive under independent construction. The explicit second-order boundary term distinguishes finite datasets from a translation-invariant bulk approximation. This answers a natural structural-stability question rather than selecting an arbitrary statistic solely to generate a constant.
