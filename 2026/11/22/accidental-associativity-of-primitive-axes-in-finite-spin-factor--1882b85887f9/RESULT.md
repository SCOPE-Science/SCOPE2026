# Accidental associativity of primitive axes in finite spin-factor Jordan algebras

## Finding
Let \(q\) be any odd prime power, let \(d\ge2\), and let \(B\) be a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on \(V=\mathbb F_q^d\). The spin-factor Jordan algebra \(J=\mathbb F_q1\oplus V\) has multiplication
\[
(a,u)\circ(b,v)=(ab+B(u,v),\;av+bu).
\]
Its proper primitive idempotents are precisely \(e_u=(1/2,u/2)\) with \(Q(u)=B(u,u)=1\). Count **ordered** triples of these axes satisfying just the one associator equation
\[
(e_u\circ e_v)\circ e_w=e_u\circ(e_v\circ e_w).
\]
Write \(D=\det B\) modulo squares and \(\chi\) for the quadratic character of \(\mathbb F_q\), with \(\chi(0)=0\). Define the integers \(S,T\) by the following explicit formulas.

If \(d=2m\) is even, set \(\epsilon=\chi((-1)^mD)\). Then
\[
S=q^{2m-1}-\epsilon q^{m-1},\qquad
T=q^{2m-2}+\chi(-1)\epsilon q^{m-1}.
\]
If \(d=2m+1\) is odd, set \(\epsilon=\chi((-1)^mD)\). Then
\[
S=q^{2m}+\epsilon q^m,\qquad T=q^{2m-1}-\epsilon q^{m-1}.
\]
For every form in the stated scope, the **complete zero-associator count** and probability are
\[
\boxed{H_q(B)=S\bigl(T^2+2(S-T)\bigr),\qquad
\Pr(\text{ordered primitive-axis triple associates})=\frac{T^2+2S-2T}{S^2}.}
\]
Here \(S\) is the number of proper primitive idempotents, and \(T\) is the number of primitive axes whose vector coordinates are perpendicular to a fixed primitive axis.

There are exactly \(2S^2\) associating triples whose two outer vectors are linearly dependent. The remaining
\[
\boxed{H_q^{\mathrm{acc}}(B)=S\,T(T-2)}
\]
are **accidentally associating triples**: the outer vectors are linearly independent, all three axes are distinct, the indicated parenthesization agrees, but the subalgebra generated by the triple is nonassociative.

A sharp existence boundary follows. Accidentally associating triples exist for every nondegenerate form when \(d\ge4\). In dimension \(d=3\) they exist except in the single small-field case \(q=3\) and \(\chi(-D)=1\). In dimension \(d=2\) they never exist. This boundary is valid in all odd characteristics, including characteristic three.

## Assumptions and scope
All counts concern the **single displayed ordered associator equation**, not the simultaneous vanishing of every associator in the generated subalgebra. Both discriminant classes of nondegenerate symmetric forms are included. The field cardinality may be any odd prime power, not only a prime. The scalar \(1/2\) exists because the characteristic is odd. The two trivial idempotents \(0\) and \(1\) are excluded. Only the zero fiber of the primitive-axis associator is counted; no formula for its nonzero fibers is asserted.

## Proof
**Primitive axes.** Squaring an element gives \((a,u)\circ(a,u)=(a^2+Q(u),2au)\). An idempotent with nonzero vector coordinate therefore has \(a=1/2\), \(Q(u)=1/4\); writing that coordinate \(u/2\) yields \(Q(u)=1\). The vector-zero idempotents are \(0\) and \(1\). Every proper idempotent is primitive because its Peirce \(1\)-eigenspace is one-dimensional, as can also be checked directly from the multiplication formula.

**Associator calculation.** For arbitrary imaginary parts \(u,v,w\), a direct expansion yields
\[
((a,u)\circ(b,v))\circ(c,w)-(a,u)\circ((b,v)\circ(c,w))
=(0,B(u,v)w-B(v,w)u).
\]
Thus for the idempotents \(e_u,e_v,e_w\), the defect is \(1/8\) times the right-hand imaginary vector. This scalar is invertible. If the outer vectors \(u,w\) are linearly dependent, then, since both have norm one and the characteristic is odd, \(w=u\) or \(w=-u\). In either case the defect vanishes. There are exactly \(2S\) such ordered outer pairs for every choice of \(v\).

If the outer vectors are independent, the equation \(B(u,v)w-B(v,w)u=0\) implies \(B(u,v)=B(v,w)=0\), and this condition is sufficient. Fix any norm-one middle vector \(v\). The orthogonal complement \(v^\perp\) is a nondegenerate space of dimension \(d-1\), and its determinant square class equals \(D\) because \(V=\mathbb F_qv\perp v^\perp\) and \(B(v,v)=1\). Let \(T\) count its norm-one vectors. Exactly \(T^2\) ordered outer pairs belong to \(v^\perp\), of which precisely \(2T\) are dependent. The complement of this set of dependent pairs contributes \(2(S-T)\), giving \(T^2+2(S-T)\) for **each** middle vector, independently of \(v\). Multiplying by \(S\) proves the formula for \(H_q(B)\). The dependent and independent counts give the separate formula for \(H_q^{\mathrm{acc}}(B)\).

**Finite-field quadratic counts.** For any nondegenerate \(n\)-dimensional form of determinant \(\Delta\), the number of norm-one vectors is
\[
R_n(1)=\begin{cases}
q^{n-1}-\chi((-1)^{n/2}\Delta)q^{n/2-1},&n\text{ even},\\
q^{n-1}+\chi((-1)^{(n-1)/2}\Delta)q^{(n-1)/2},&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
For completeness, diagonalize the form and let \(\psi\) be any nontrivial additive character. Character orthogonality expresses \(R_n(1)\) as \(q^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_q}\psi(-t)\prod_i\sum_x\psi(t\Delta_i x^2)\). For nonzero \(t\), each inner sum is \(\chi(t\Delta_i)G\) for a quadratic Gauss sum \(G\) with \(G^2=\chi(-1)q\). The remaining \(t\)-sum is \(-1\) if \(n\) is even and \(\chi(-1)G\) if \(n\) is odd. Simplification gives the displayed formulas in every odd characteristic. Applying them to \(V\) and \(v^\perp\) gives exactly the stated \(S,T\).

**Why accidental triples do not generate an associative algebra.** If \(u,w\) are independent norm-one vectors, the generated subalgebra contains \(1,u,w\). Its associator at \((u,u,w)\) has vector part \(B(u,u)w-B(u,w)u=w-B(u,w)u\), which is nonzero by independence. Thus a single accidentally vanishing associator is strictly weaker than associativity of the generated algebra.

**Sharp boundary.** In dimension two, \(v^\perp\) is a line, so \(T\) is either zero or two and \(T(T-2)=0\). In dimension three, \(T=q-\chi(-D)\), which equals two precisely when \(q=3\) and \(\chi(-D)=1\). In dimension four, \(T\ge q^2-q\ge6\); in every higher dimension, the displayed quadratic-count formulas give \(T>2\). Hence the accidental count is positive exactly in the cases claimed.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify_associator.py` with standard Python 3. It builds prime fields of orders three and five as well as the quadratic extension field \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^2+1)\), implements the Jordan product rather than reusing the closed associator formula, and checks all middle axes and all outer pairs in the small exhaustive configurations. In larger cases it checks complete outer-pair tables at several middle axes. There are 18 tested field/dimension/discriminant configurations, with direct counts of \(S\), \(T\), the associator-zero fiber per middle axis, and the predicted total where exhaustiveness is enabled. The captured result is in `verification_output.txt` and ends in `VERIFY_OK 18 configurations`. Finite checks are corroboration; the all-prime-power theorem follows from the proof above.

For example, over \(\mathbb F_3\) in dimension three, the two determinant classes yield \((S,T,H_q,H_q^{\mathrm{acc}})=(6,4,120,48)\) and \((12,2,288,0)\); in dimension four they yield \((24,6,1728,576)\) and \((30,12,5400,3600)\).

## Relationship to prior work
Hall, Rehren and Shpectorov (2014, Section 3.5) establish the primitive-axis model and its orthogonal Peirce decomposition; these foundational ingredients, and classical norm-one quadratic-form counts, are not claimed as original. A published result entitled *Associating tuples of finite spin factors recover Witt type at two generators* counts tuples **generating an associative subalgebra**, which requires all relevant associators to vanish. The present calculation instead counts **one ordered ternary associator at primitive axes**, and its accidentally associative stratum is expressly excluded by the stronger generated-subalgebra property. Known finite-spin-factor square-root and two-axis product fiber laws similarly do not count the three-axis associator constraint. The new assertion is the exact arithmetic primitive-axis single-associator census and its sharp exceptional existence boundary, not a new definition of spin factors or new quadratic norm-count identities.

## Limitations
The result does not classify nonzero associator targets, does not treat characteristic two or degenerate bilinear forms, and does not claim an all-arity associativity theorem. The proof and computation have received no independent mathematical validation. The exact simple count might have appeared implicitly in unlocated older treatments of Jordan associators and finite polar geometries; no priority certificate is claimed.
