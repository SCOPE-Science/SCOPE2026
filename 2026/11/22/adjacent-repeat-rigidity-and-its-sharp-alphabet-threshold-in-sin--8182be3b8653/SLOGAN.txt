For three-symbol words decoded with lists of size two after one insertion, the code consisting of words with an adjacent repeated symbol is uniquely optimal for alphabets of sizes two and three, with exact maxima \(6\) and \(15\). Four-symbol local exchanges and a disjoint-block lift give \(2q^2-q+\lfloor q/4\rfloor\) codewords for every \(q\ge4\), proving that size four is the sharp alphabet threshold at which this natural run-based code ceases to be optimal.
