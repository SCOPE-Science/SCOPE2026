# Affine Frobenius splitting laws for odd-radical Swinnerton–Dyer polynomials

## Finding

For an odd prime \(p\ge3\), an integer \(n\ge2\), and pairwise distinct rational primes \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n\) different from \(p\), write \(r_j=\ell_j^{1/p}>0\), fix \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/p)\), and set

\[
F(X)=\prod_{t\in\mathbb F_p^n}\left(X-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta^{t_j}r_j\right).
\]

This monic polynomial has integer coefficients, is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) of degree \(p^n\), and its splitting field has Galois group \(\mathbb F_p^n\rtimes\mathbb F_p^\times\), in the natural affine action on \(\mathbb F_p^n\).

For every rational prime \(q\nmid\operatorname{disc}(F)\), its complete squarefree factorization pattern over \(\mathbb F_q\) is exactly one of the following:

1. If \(q\equiv1\pmod p\) and each \(\ell_j\) is a \(p\)-th power modulo \(q\), there are \(p^n\) distinct linear factors.
2. If \(q\equiv1\pmod p\) but at least one \(\ell_j\) is not a \(p\)-th power modulo \(q\), there are \(p^{n-1}\) distinct irreducible factors, each of degree \(p\), with **no linear factors**.
3. If \(q\not\equiv1\pmod p\) and \(d=\operatorname{ord}_p(q)>1\), there is **exactly one linear factor** and exactly \((p^n-1)/d\) further irreducible factors, all of degree \(d\).

The natural densities among rational primes are, respectively,

\[
\frac{1}{p^n(p-1)},\qquad
\frac{p^n-1}{p^n(p-1)},\qquad
\frac{\varphi(d)}{p-1}\quad(d\mid p-1,\ d>1).
\]

The sum of these densities is one. Importantly, even at primes excluded from the squarefree classification, **every irreducible factor of \(F\bmod q\) has degree at most \(p\)**. Hence \(F\) is reducible modulo **every** rational prime, although irreducible over the rationals.

## Assumptions and scope

The assumptions are exactly those in the first paragraph. Every prime \(q\) considered for the squarefree pattern satisfies \(q\nmid\operatorname{disc}(F)\); no implicit unramified assumption is made for the universal factor-degree bound. The symbol \(d=\operatorname{ord}_p(q)\) is defined only when \(q\ne p\), and \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient. The displayed exponent-style pattern \(d^a\) means \(a\) distinct irreducible factors of degree \(d\), not a polynomial exponent or multiplicity. Density is natural density within all rational primes. The bound and its proof also work for \(n=1\), but the all-prime reducibility conclusion requires \(n\ge2\).

The construction is an odd-prime radical specialization of the Swinnerton–Dyer-type product of Messerschmidt [1], whose main results describe coefficient recursion and symmetry.

## Proof

**1. Independent radicals and integrality.** Put \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), \(L=K(r_1,\ldots,r_n)\). Since the only ramified rational prime in the cyclotomic field \(K\) is \(p\), each \(\ell_j\ne p\) has valuation one at every prime of \(K\) above \(\ell_j\), and the other \(\ell_i\)'s have valuation zero there. If \(\prod_j\ell_j^{e_j}\) is a \(p\)-th power in \(K\), these valuations give \(e_j\equiv0\pmod p\) for every \(j\). Consequently the classes of the \(\ell_j\) are linearly independent in \(K^\times/(K^\times)^p\). The Kummer extension theorem gives

\[
[L:K]=p^n,\qquad\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\simeq\mathbb F_p^n.
\]

The monomials \(\prod_j r_j^{e_j}\), with \(0\le e_j<p\), form a \(K\)-basis of \(L\). In particular the \(r_j\) are linearly independent over \(K\), so all the numbers \(\alpha_t=\sum_j\zeta^{t_j}r_j\) for \(t\in\mathbb F_p^n\) are distinct. Each is an algebraic integer, and their collection is stable under the full absolute Galois group of \(\mathbb Q\); its elementary symmetric functions are both rational and algebraic integers. Thus \(F\in\mathbb Z[X]\), monic and squarefree of degree \(p^n\).

**2. Precise Galois action.** Every automorphism in \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\) sends \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^u\) with \(u\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), and \(r_j\mapsto\zeta^{v_j}r_j\) with \(v_j\in\mathbb F_p\). All such choices occur: Kummer translations give all \(v\), and the cyclotomic automorphisms extend by fixing the real \(r_j\), because their defining equations have rational coefficients and the Kummer monomials give a free \(K\)-basis. Therefore

\[
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\simeq\mathbb F_p^n\rtimes\mathbb F_p^\times,
\qquad \alpha_t\longmapsto\alpha_{ut+v}.
\]

The translation subgroup is transitive on the \(p^n\) distinct roots, so \(F\) is irreducible. The affine action is faithful (a map fixing all \(t\) must have \(u=1\), \(v=0\)); hence the field generated by the roots of \(F\) is all of \(L\), not merely a subfield.

**3. Frobenius cycle types and the residue test.** For a rational prime \(q\nmid\operatorname{disc}(F)\), the degrees of the distinct irreducible factors of \(F\bmod q\) are the lengths of the cycles of a Frobenius element acting on the roots. Its scalar component is \(u\equiv q\pmod p\). When \(u\ne1\), translation conjugates the affine map \(t\mapsto ut+v\) to \(t\mapsto ut\). It has one fixed point; every other vector has orbit length exactly \(d=\operatorname{ord}_p(u)\), giving the third pattern.

For \(u=1\), Frobenius acts as a translation by \(v\in\mathbb F_p^n\). If \(v=0\), all vectors are fixed; if \(v\ne0\), every orbit has length \(p\), with precisely \(p^{n-1}\) such orbits. To identify the cases arithmetically, note that \(q\equiv1\pmod p\) makes \(\zeta\) rational in \(\mathbb F_q\). At a prime of \(L\) over good \(q\), Frobenius fixes each \(r_j\) exactly when its reduction lies in \(\mathbb F_q\), equivalently when \(\ell_j\) is a nonzero \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_q\). Thus \(v=0\) exactly when all these residue tests succeed; otherwise \(v\ne0\). This proves the first two patterns.

**4. Densities.** The group order is \(p^n(p-1)\). There is one identity, \(p^n-1\) nonidentity translations, and, for each \(d>1\) dividing \(p-1\), exactly \(\varphi(d)p^n\) affine maps whose scalar component has order \(d\). These are conjugacy-stable unions, so Chebotarev's theorem assigns the claimed natural prime densities. Their sum is

\[
\frac{1+(p^n-1)}{p^n(p-1)}+\frac{\sum_{d\mid p-1,\ d>1}\varphi(d)}{p-1}=1.
\]

**5. Uniform bound at every prime, including bad reduction.** Fix any rational prime \(q\). If \(q=p\), reduction of each \(r_j^p-\ell_j\) gives \((X-\ell_j)^p\) and the \(p\)-th roots of unity coalesce to one; consequently \(F\bmod p\) is a power of one linear factor. If \(q\not\equiv1\pmod p\) (and \(q\ne p\)), the map \(x\mapsto x^p\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb F_q\). Each \(\ell_j\) has its \(p\)-th roots in \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\), with \(d=\operatorname{ord}_p(q)\mid p-1\), because \(\zeta\in\mathbb F_{q^d}\). All reduced roots of \(F\) therefore lie in \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\), so every irreducible factor has degree at most \(d\le p-1\). Finally, if \(q\equiv1\pmod p\), \(\zeta\in\mathbb F_q\) and every element of \(\mathbb F_q\) becomes a \(p\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_{q^p}\): indeed \(1+q+\cdots+q^{p-1}\) is divisible by \(p\). Hence every reduced root of \(F\) lies in \(\mathbb F_{q^p}\), and every factor has degree at most \(p\). Since \(\deg(F)=p^n>p\) for \(n\ge2\), an irreducible reduction is impossible at **any** prime. This includes repeated-root primes and completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone script `VERIFY.py` reconstructs exact integer polynomials by iterated Sylvester resultants and checks factor-degree multisets by finite-field factorization. It tests five concrete choices of \((p,\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n)\), including degrees \(9,27,25,49\) and the one-radical boundary case of degree \(3\). For all rational primes \(q<251\), it verifies the factor-degree bound, and for squarefree reductions it independently checks the exact three-way splitting prescription via modular power-residue tests and \(\operatorname{ord}_p(q)\). Its exact run reported 243 good-prime checks and 22 repeated-root-prime checks, total 265, all passing. These finite tests **do not** establish the theorem for arbitrary primes or parameters; the Kummer and Frobenius arguments above do.

For example, with \(p=3\) and \((\ell_1,\ell_2)=(2,5)\), the degree-nine polynomial is

\[
F(X)=X^9-21X^6-123X^3-343.
\]

At \(q=13\) there are three cubic factors; at \(q=11\) there is one linear and four quadratic factors; at \(q=127\) there are nine distinct linear factors. All three reductions are squarefree.

## Relationship to prior work

Messerschmidt [1] introduced the general product and proved an integer-coefficient recurrence and symmetry. The present statement concerns a specific odd-prime-radical specialization and determines its **entire prime-factor-degree spectrum, all associated densities, and an all-prime reducibility property**; these are not stated in [1]. The classical square-root Swinnerton–Dyer construction [2] has an elementary abelian two-group and factors of degrees at most two. Here a nontrivial cyclotomic quotient creates a different, nonabelian affine Frobenius action, with one-root behavior for primes outside the residue class \(1\pmod p\), and the sharp upper degree bound \(p\). Standard Kummer theory and Chebotarev's theorem [3] supply the tools; the specialized family and the resulting combined classification are the object of the finding.

## Limitations

The result is stated for **odd prime** exponents and **pairwise distinct rational prime radicands not equal to the exponent**. It does not claim the same Galois group for dependent radicands or arbitrary composite exponents. The explicit squarefree factorization pattern excludes finitely many primes dividing \(\operatorname{disc}(F)\); at those primes, only the bound on irreducible factor degrees is asserted. No effective error bound for prime densities is proved. The proof does not establish that the exact classification is absent from all unindexed literature, and equivalent consequences of general Kummer/Chebotarev statements could have been recorded elsewhere.
