For intersections of two rotated copies of any regular Euclidean \(n\)-gon, the affine isotropic invariant decreases strictly from the regular \(n\)-gon to the regular \(2n\)-gon as the relative angle reaches a half-step. At three rotations, a rigorous cubic-order counterexample for every \(n\ge3\) shows that the corresponding Schur-convex angular-gap order fails, unlike the known Schur-concavity of the Mahler product.
