# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The point-localization lemma is proved by exhausting all four endpoint-shore combinations, including length-four shortest paths between parallel line-vertices. It gives \(q^2\le a+qb\), a sharp bound whose equality forces full parallel classes of lines. The converse uses the length-four path between any two selected parallel lines to cover every point and every transverse line. For sets of size \(q+1\), this same inequality forces at least \(q\) selected lines for \(q\ge3\); missing-line arguments and the absence of internal line-vertices between intersecting selected lines then establish the complete near-optimum classification. The exceptional plane of order two is separately classified. Two independent exhaustive implementations verify \(q=2,3\), and a separate finite-field construction exhausts \(q=4\). The general proof uses only affine-plane axioms and has no unproved computational inference.

## Originality — PASS, with acknowledged residual risk

Actual semantic research-record searches for affine-plane geodetic bases, geodetic intervals in affine designs, and inclusion-minimal size gaps returned a projective-plane geodetic classification as the closest same-parameter literature, not the affine theorem. The published projective result requires the line at infinity: its optimum has \(2q\) point/line vertices and a flag-based form, whereas the affine optimum has \(q\) line vertices and all bases are parallel classes. Deleting the line at infinity changes shortest-path distances; the projective result is not a corollary implying the affine result. The general graph-geodetic original of Harary–Loukakis–Tsouros and the minimal-set paper of Chartrand–Harary–Zhang have been compared; their accessible content concerns definitions, general bounds and realizability rather than affine-plane incidence graphs. The archive-dated Hon et al. preprint concerns geodetic algorithms in tree-cographs and related graph classes, not this incidence family. A relevant prior statement about equivalence of vertex-geodetic and edge-geodetic sets for diameter-three four-cycle-free bipartite graphs does not cover affine incidence graphs, which have diameter four. General resolvable-design results under different terminology or unindexed historical tables could still exist. No absolute priority is certified.

## Value — PASS

The complete minimum-basis classification applies to every finite affine plane, including non-Desarguesian planes, and pinpoints the structural effect of parallelism on geodesic coverage. It yields the exact count \(q+1\), an all-or-nothing sharp relocation threshold \(q\), an exact \(2(q+1)q^2\) near-minimum count, and a missing cardinality in the **inclusion-minimal** spectrum for \(q\ge3\) with an explicit six-configuration small-order exception. This naturally links incidence geometry, geodesic covering and optimal-configuration rigidity and is not a purely numerical substitution. It also sharply contrasts the projective-plane flag classification. The theorem makes no claim about the full higher minimal-set spectrum or strong geodetic numbers.

No independent scholarly validation was performed.
