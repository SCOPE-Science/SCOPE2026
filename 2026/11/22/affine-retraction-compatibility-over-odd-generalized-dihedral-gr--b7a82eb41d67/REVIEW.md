# Scientific assessment: affine-retraction compatibility over odd generalized dihedral groups

## Correctness — PASS

Group endomorphisms are exhaustively parametrized by the additive endomorphism of the odd-order kernel and the image of the reflection. Composition and self-idempotence are computed directly. A commuting affine family has a common fixed point by composition of its idempotents; distinct common-point choices are counted modulo the common image. The joint Boolean decomposition is valid for finite abelian groups without a field or semisimplicity assumption. The orbit-stabilizer formula, group-order normalization, mixed-exponent specialization and maximal-clique uniqueness have been rederived from the definitions. The standalone finite-group checker enumerates the dimension-two mixed-exponent case, including direct multiplication on the full group, and checks the resulting integer formulas. No finite experiment is used as a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked evidence

The 2019 work by Arvasi–Odabaş–Wensley addresses ordinary prime dihedral-group endomorphisms, already covered and not claimed new. The published elementary generalized-dihedral commuting-retraction result supplies the field-vector-space boundary, and the published all-arity finite abelian \(p\)-group idempotent census supplies the *unweighted* direct-decomposition count, including its exponent-invisibility theorem. Neither, read as a statement, asserts the arbitrary odd-kernel affine weight \(|A|/|A_{\mathbf1}|\) or the explicit reversal of exponent invisibility in the generalized dihedral extension. This original contribution is a full compatible group-retraction census, not rediscovery of commuting linear projections. Broader affine-module and semigroup literature could contain an equivalent previously unstated specialization; historical priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The finding determines all compatible retraction tuples and maximal families across non-elementary odd generalized dihedral groups, not just one numerical example. Its concrete comparison with a known exponent-invisible invariant is mathematically motivated: affine fixed-point constraints restore a missing cyclic-exponent parameter for every tuple length. This distinguishes the extension structure from the additive kernel by exact, computable group-endomorphism statistics.

## Closest literature and limits

The principal closest items are Arvasi–Odabaş–Wensley (arXiv:1911.12799, Section 4.4); *Commuting retractions of elementary generalized dihedral groups* (2026); and *All-arity idempotent clique spectra of finite abelian \(p\)-groups and an invisible exponent* (2026). The first describes a one-dimensional boundary; the second is elementary abelian; the third is the additive rather than affine group case. The complete proof assumes odd-order kernels, and the finite checker covers \(C_9\oplus C_3\), not every group. Independent priority confirmation and mathematical validation are outstanding.
