# Affine triangles and a sharp three-edge exchange threshold for Pappus minimum maximal matchings

## Finding
Let \(P\) be the eighteen-vertex Pappus incidence graph with points \(\mathbb F_3^2\) and the nine lines in the three parallel classes \(x=c\), \(y=c\), and \(y-x=c\), where \(c\in\mathbb F_3\). A matching is *maximal* if no further graph edge can be added to it. For every integer \(t\ge1\), let \(\mathcal R_t(P)\) have one vertex per smallest maximal matching of \(P\); two different matchings are adjacent when one can be obtained from the other by deleting and replacing at most \(t\) edges, while preserving cardinality and maximality.

The minimum size of a maximal matching is six, and all thirty-six minimum matchings are obtained as follows: choose one of eighteen affine triangles
\[
T_{x,y,a}=\{(x,y),(x+a,y),(x,y-a)\},\qquad x,y\in\mathbb F_3,\quad a\in\{1,2\},
\]
omit these three point vertices and the unique three line vertices avoiding them, then take either of the two perfect matchings on the twelve remaining vertices. These are all the minima, with no duplications.

The complete maximal-matching cardinality polynomial is
\[
36z^6+567z^7+648z^8+42z^9.
\]
Distinct minima share at most three edges; therefore \(\mathcal R_1(P)\) and \(\mathcal R_2(P)\) are each thirty-six isolated vertices. In contrast, \(\mathcal R_3(P)\) is a connected, vertex-transitive seven-regular graph with thirty-six vertices and 126 edges, radius and diameter four, exactly 108 triangles, and uniform distance spheres of sizes \((1,7,12,12,4)\).

## Assumptions and scope
All edges are undirected, all graph vertices and graph edges are labelled as in the construction, and the three lines in each parallel class are distinct. A maximal matching is inclusion-maximal, not a matching of maximum cardinality. The reconfiguration move replaces *up to* \(t\) existing matching edges simultaneously; intermediate partial matchings are not required to be maximal, but both endpoints of a move must be minimum maximal matchings. The enumerations concern this fixed eighteen-vertex graph, not an arbitrary affine design or all generalized Pappus configurations. The Pappus graph has eighteen vertices, 27 edges, and maximum matching size nine; the present minimum-maximal problem is different.

## Proof
**Reduction to independent uncovered sets.** The graph is bipartite with nine point vertices and nine line vertices. A matching with \(k\) edges leaves \(9-k\) points and \(9-k\) lines uncovered. It is maximal exactly when no uncovered point lies on an uncovered line. For four distinct points, the total point-to-line incidences is twelve. Two points lie together on at most one line in the selected three directions. If a line contains \(r\) of the four points, its contribution of \(r-1\) to repeated incidences is at most \(\binom r2\). Summing gives at most \(\binom42=6\) repeated incidences, hence at least six different lines meet these four points. At most three of the nine lines avoid them. Thus no maximal matching can leave four or more vertices uncovered on each side, proving \(k\ge6\).

**Classification at the bound.** Three points have nine incidences with the nine line vertices. At least six distinct lines meet them. Exactly six lines meet the triple if and only if each of its three point-pairs lies on a different line from the three permitted directions. The alternative of three collinear points repeats only two incidences, and a missing-direction point pair contributes no repetition; either alternative leaves at most two avoiding lines. Consequently a maximal six-edge matching must uncover exactly a noncollinear triangle whose three sides have the three distinct permitted directions. The vertical/horizontal corner is unique, and these triangles are precisely \(T_{x,y,a}\) as displayed above, giving \(3\cdot3\cdot2=18\) point triples. Each hits exactly two lines of each parallel class, so the three lines it avoids are forced. There is no other admissible uncovered line triple.

**Exactly two completions per triangle.** Translations of \(\mathbb F_3^2\) and simultaneous negation move any displayed triangle to \(T_{0,0,1}=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,2)\}\), preserving the chosen line directions. Label the lines by direction/intercept pairs \((d,c)\), with \(d=0\) meaning \(x=c\), \(d=1\) meaning \(y=c\), and \(d=2\) meaning \(y-x=c\). For the displayed reference triangle, the omitted line vertices are \((0,2),(1,1),(2,1)\). Among the six remaining points, the vertices \((0,1),(2,0),(2,1)\) have respectively only the available line neighbors \((0,0),(1,0),(2,2)\); these matching edges are forced. The remaining induced graph is a six-cycle between points \((1,1),(1,2),(2,2)\) and lines \((0,1),(1,2),(2,0)\), with exactly two perfect matchings. Hence there are exactly \(18\cdot2=36\) minima.

**Three-edge exchange graph.** The exhaustive certificate `verify_affine.py` enumerates *all* maximal matchings by a first-free-vertex recursion, independently verifies the eighteen uncovered triples, and compares all \(\binom{36}{2}=630\) pairs of minimum matchings. It finds no pair with fewer than three exchanged edges and precisely 126 pairs with three exchanged edges. Every minimum has seven such neighbors. Breadth-first search from each of the 36 minima yields the same sphere profile \((1,7,12,12,4)\), establishing connectivity, radius and diameter four. The certificate counts exactly 108 triangles in the exchange graph. Its separate affine-collineation test constructs the 108 invertible affine transformations preserving the three directions and verifies that their orbit on one minimum comprises all 36 configurations; hence the exchange graph is vertex-transitive. The completely independent `verify_lcf.py` builds the Pappus graph from its Hamiltonian LCF representation and enumerates minima by uncovered independent pairs and perfect matchings. It reproduces the optimum and entire exchange distance profile. Each finite assertion above is covered by an exhaustive loop over explicitly bounded finite sets; the printed logs by themselves are not used as proof.

**Complete polynomial.** The first-free-vertex recursion has two disjoint choices at each vertex: leave that vertex uncovered, which is permitted only when no previously uncovered neighbor exists, or match it to one of its still-free neighbors. This uniquely generates every maximal matching. Counts by size are six:36, seven:567, eight:648, nine:42. There are no other sizes because of the lower bound and the nine-per-shore matching upper bound. The recursion and its explicit source constitute a finite exhaustive proof of the displayed polynomial, with the separate LCF verifier independently confirming the minimum layer.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_affine.py` and `python3 verify_lcf.py`, both requiring only Python 3 standard-library modules. The first program generates all 27 edges from the coordinate incidence relation, outputs the maximal-matching histogram, checks the geometric classification and the affine symmetry orbit, and determines the complete three-edge exchange graph. The second independently constructs 27 edges using the LCF offsets \([5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3\), finds its bipartition, enumerates all independent uncovered four-by-four and three-by-three pairs, and reconstructs their complementary perfect matchings. Both check all 36 breadth-first distance profiles. The printed outputs, `verify_affine.out` and `verify_lcf.out`, are included solely as convenient replay comparisons. The lists `data/optimum_masks.txt` and `data/three_swap_edges.txt` provide the exact indexed configurations and exchange-edge certificate; edge indices follow lexicographic order in `verify_affine.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
Yannakakis and Gavril's edge-domination work (1980) supplies the classical connection between edge domination and smallest maximal matchings, not this specific Pappus classification. Dejter's 2009 Pappus–Desargues study treats oriented short-cycle and ultrahomogeneity geometry and anchors the archival graph context, not minimum maximal matchings. Adriaensen, Mattheus, and Spiro's 2023 work treats edge domination in incidence structures and the general incidence-free approach, but its principal symmetric-design completion theorem assumes large block size; the Pappus configuration has block size three. Its introductory matching equivalence is acknowledged here. General perfect-matching reconfiguration literature treats cycle flips between perfect matchings, rather than three-edge batch exchanges among nonperfect *maximal* matchings. Searches of indexed related mathematical records found finite matching and induced-matching results on Pappus and Heawood graphs, but no record was found that implies the present 36-state three-exchange graph. This is a qualified coverage assessment, not a proof of historical uniqueness.

## Limitations
This is a complete finite-graph theorem, not a general infinite-family bound. The distance profile is established by a reproducible finite exhaustive certificate, not a closed-form distance formula for arbitrary incidence structures. The number 36 and the matching-size polynomial could be independently computable from graph-software databases; the substantive claim here is the exact geometric optimum classification and the sharp connected three-edge mobility graph. Historical-priority claims remain conditional on the inspected sources and searchable records; unindexed or unavailable work may contain equivalent statements. No independent external audit is asserted.
