# Scientific review: Airy transverse dichotomy and linearized departure

## Correctness
PASS. Along \(x=y=0\), the third equation gives \(z=z_0-bt\), and the two transverse derivatives form a nonautonomous linear system. Eliminating the second variable gives an exact linearly drifting second-order oscillator. The exponential gauge \(u=e^{-ht}w\) and time shift transform it into the standard Airy equation without an approximation. The nonzero Airy Wronskian makes the coefficient map invertible and proves there is exactly one nonzero stable line of directions. Classical positive-argument Airy asymptotics establish the exact positive/negative \(t^{3/2}\) logarithmic exponents; the derivative term determines the component ratios. Explicit inversion of the Airy growth logarithm establishes the full three-term linearized first-threshold asymptotic. Independently run computations verify the identities and two large-logarithm first-passage cases but are not the proof. The divergence is constant \(-a-c\); Liouville's identity is exact.

## Originality
PASS within inspected material, not an assertion of global priority. The primary 2022 Wang–Dong–Li open-access article supplies the exact flow and discusses finite attractors, synchronization, divergence, bifurcations and coded periodic orbits, but none of its inspected analysis computes an Airy solution along the unbounded invariant axis or a three-term variational first-passage law. The 2012 Wei–Pehlivan original generalized Sprott-C article, inspected in its four-page full text, has the same axial variational equation when \(k=0\), so the Airy law is *not* attributed to the extra cross-product coupling. That source likewise does not prove the direction-splitting or threshold inversion. The closest checked indexed result, *Exact recurrence shell in the Wang–Dong–Li variable-boostable flow*, restricts compact invariant measures using a polynomial coboundary, hence its premise does not apply to the escaping axial trajectory. The seemingly analogous proximity theorem for a different planar transient-center flow has a Gaussian distance law and \((\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1/2}\) first passage, not the Airy law. More distant indexed polynomial-flow escape theorems involve different nonlinear escape mechanisms. Only public summaries, not full source texts, could be inspected for those indexed results, which is an explicit residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The published flow's constant negative divergence and finite hidden/coexisting attractors do not capture its transient sensitivity along a globally defined unbounded solution. A complete, parameter-uniform Airy splitting determines both how a codimension-one set of transverse perturbations decays and how generic perturbations are amplified superexponentially. The sharp three-term \((\log(1/\varepsilon))^{2/3}\) clock, including a parameter-dependent delay and finite constant, is a mathematically interpretable timescale for **linearized** sensitivity near this natural escaping axis. It is not inferred from ordinary frozen-time eigenvalues and requires the nonautonomous Airy analysis.

## Closest literature and limits
The governing model is Wang–Dong–Li, DOI 10.3390/fractalfract6120740 (2022-12-14), with the 2012 Wei–Pehlivan generalized Sprott-C antecedent. The Airy asymptotic functions are classical, not novel. The result does not establish a nonlinear stable manifold, nonlinear nearby-orbit departure times, nonlinear global attraction, an invariant probability or properties of compact chaotic attractors. Full source text was inspected for both directly relevant model papers; other mathematical-record comparisons were restricted to their available indexed summaries. Equivalent older or unindexed results remain possible.
