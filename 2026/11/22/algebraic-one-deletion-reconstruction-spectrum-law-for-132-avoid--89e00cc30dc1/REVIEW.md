# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The deletion-pattern equivalence classes are runs of adjacent positions connected by consecutive-value bonds. A unique maximum-element decomposition of \(132\)-avoiders gives the two-series equations and the claimed quadratic by elimination. Formal differentiation establishes the exact mean; the simple discriminant root and its first two derivatives give a nondegenerate quasi-powers Gaussian law. The packaged `verify.py` checks all avoiding sources through length eight, and polynomial totals and means through length twelve. Finite checks supplement, rather than replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Kitaev–Remmel (2013, arXiv:1305.6970) give a complete enumeration of positions incident to a bond (the one-box pattern), not bond edges; the two statistics differ even within the avoidance class. Bevan–Homberger–Tenner (2016, arXiv:1608.06931) study prolific permutations and maximum distinct deletion patterns rather than the present full sphere-size spectrum. Kitaev–Remmel–Tiefenbruck (2012, arXiv:1201.6243) analyze quadrant mesh patterns rather than this statistic. An OEIS numerical column is not claimed new. The new combination is the all-length algebraic deletion-sphere-volume distribution with exact mean and asymptotic variance and Gaussian limit. Search of related indexed mathematical findings did not identify equivalent coverage; unindexed implicit equivalence remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result answers the natural all-blocklength question of how many *distinct* deletion patterns are presented by a Catalan-constrained permutation source, giving all coefficients, sharp finite expectations, and a limiting law. This is directly relevant to constrained permutation reconstruction and is structurally stronger than an arbitrary isolated coefficient. It does not claim a reconstruction code capacity, independent auditing, or a novel Catalan count.

## Scientific limits

No claim is made for more than one deletion, pairwise sphere intersections, maximal code size, or unrestricted-permutation sphere distributions. The known one-box and prolific-permutation theorems remain prior art. Novelty remains qualified pending broader independent literature review.
