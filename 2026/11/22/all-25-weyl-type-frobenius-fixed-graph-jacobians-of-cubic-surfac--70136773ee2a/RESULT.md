# All 25 Weyl-type Frobenius-fixed graph Jacobians of cubic-surface lines and tritangents

## Finding

Let \(\Gamma\) be the *abstract line–tritangent incidence graph* with twenty-seven vertices representing geometric lines, forty-five vertices representing tritangent planes, and one edge for each line contained in each tritangent plane. It is connected and bipartite with seventy-two vertices and 135 edges. Its finite graph Jacobian is \(K(\Gamma)=\operatorname{Div}^0(\Gamma)/\operatorname{im}L\), where \(L\) is the integral graph Laplacian. Arithmetic Frobenius acts on the lines and planes and therefore on \(K(\Gamma)\).

Classes 6 and 8 have the same nine three-cycles on the geometric lines, but their five-primary fixed ranks are zero and two, respectively; therefore the splitting cycle type of lines does not determine this arithmetic graph-Jacobian invariant. For the identity element, the already-known underlying critical group is \(C_3^{10}\oplus C_9^5\oplus C_{27}^8\oplus C_{135}^6\); it is not the new contribution.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is an abstract incidence graph, *not* an assertion that all seventy-two vertices represent the components of a nodal divisor on the cubic surface. The classification is of fixed points of its finite critical group, for the indicated abstract Weyl conjugacy class; not every type is asserted to arise over every chosen finite field. Only ordinary geometric lines, the forty-five tritangent triples, and their incidence are used. No exceptional characteristic, nonreduced line scheme, or Néron component-group statement is asserted.

Use six standard blowup classes \(H,E_1,\ldots,E_6\) with intersection matrix \(H^2=1\), \(H\cdot E_i=0\), and \(E_i\cdot E_j=-\delta_{ij}\). The twenty-seven line classes are the six exceptional divisors, fifteen strict transforms \(H-E_i-E_j\), and six conics \(2H-\sum_{j\ne i}E_j\). A tritangent is precisely a triple of distinct line classes whose sum is \(-K_S=3H-\sum E_i\), giving forty-five triples.

## Proof

**Incidence and graph presentation.** The forty-five triples comprise thirty of the form \(\{E_i,H-E_i-E_j,2H-\sum_{k\ne j}E_k\}\) for \(i\ne j\), and fifteen of the form \(\{H-E_i-E_j,H-E_k-E_\ell,H-E_m-E_n\}\) for partitions of six labels into three unordered pairs. Each line belongs to five triples and each triple has three lines. Define the symmetric 72-by-72 integral Laplacian by adding \(+1\) to both diagonal incidences and \(-1\) to each pair of off-diagonal incidence positions. Exact construction gives the Schläfli line-intersection graph with parameters \((27,10,1,5)\).

**Group presentation.** Choose a root vertex \(r\) and the degree-zero basis \(b_v=e_v-e_r\) for every other vertex. In this basis the subgroup of principal divisors is represented by the 71-by-71 reduced Laplacian \(L_r\). For a graph automorphism \(g\), let \(G_r\) be the matrix whose \(v\)-th column is the coefficient vector of \(e_{g(v)}-e_{g(r)}\). Hence the coinvariant group admits the integral presentation

\[
K(\Gamma)_g\cong\operatorname{coker}[\,L_r\mid G_r-I\,].
\]

The symmetric graph Laplacian induces the canonical perfect, automorphism-invariant monodromy pairing \(K(\Gamma)\times K(\Gamma)\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\). Indeed, using \(L_r^{-1}\) modulo integers gives a nonsingular pairing on the quotient. Therefore the annihilator of \((g-1)K(\Gamma)\) is exactly \(K(\Gamma)^g\), and Pontryagin duality gives \(K(\Gamma)^g\cong\operatorname{Hom}(K(\Gamma)_g,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\) abstractly. The coinvariant Smith invariants are consequently the desired fixed-group invariants.

**All Weyl types.** Generate the Weyl action on line classes by five adjacent transpositions of six point labels and the quadratic Cremona involution in the root \(H-E_1-E_2-E_3\). Exact permutation closure has 51,840 elements. Conjugation by the six generators yields exactly twenty-five classes, whose sizes sum to 51,840; every line permutation uniquely induces a permutation of the forty-five tritangents. For each class choose one representative and form the displayed 71-by-142 presentation matrix. Its Smith reductions modulo \(3^4\) and \(5^2\) determine the full elementary divisors because the original group has order \(3^{62}5^6\), and the calculations show every fixed-group elementary divisor is among \(1,3,9,27,135\). Run the same calculations independently deleting vertex zero or vertex 71: the two reduced presentations agree for all twenty-five rows. Sorting the computed invariant-factor decompositions gives the displayed table and twenty-three isomorphism types.

**Determinantal boundary.** The 27-by-45 incidence matrix \(B\) satisfies \(BB^t=5I+A\) for the \((27,10,1,5)\) line-intersection graph \(A\). Its eigenvalues are \(15\) once, \(6\) with multiplicity twenty, and \(0\) with multiplicity six, giving the matrix-tree determinant \(\det L_r=3^{62}5^6\). A separate exact fraction-free determinant verifies this integer, and the identity-case prime-local Smith data give \(K(\Gamma)\cong C_3^{10}\oplus C_9^5\oplus C_{27}^8\oplus C_{135}^6\).

## Verification

The companion standalone `CHECKER.py` (Python 3.10+, no external packages) constructs all twenty-seven Picard classes, all forty-five tritangent sums, the incidence Laplacian and the Weyl generators from definitions. It performs an exact integer determinant calculation, enumerates all 51,840 group elements and twenty-five conjugacy classes, and calculates the Smith elementary valuations of the full coinvariant presentation over \(\mathbb Z/81\mathbb Z\) and \(\mathbb Z/25\mathbb Z\) for both deleted-root choices. It asserts that twenty-three distinct groups result, that class sizes sum to 51,840, and that both \(3^9\)-cycle classes have different five-primary ranks. Replay with `python3 CHECKER.py`; its expected full stdout is supplied in `CHECKER_OUTPUT.txt` and its terminal marker is `CUBIC_TRITANGENT_WEYL_25_FROBENIUS_CRITICAL_GROUPS_OK`.

The proof of completeness is the exact finite enumeration of the group generated by the six certified reflections combined with the classical identification of the line-incidence automorphism group with \(W(E_6)\). Small-case trials alone would not imply the table. The computations are exact integral and prime-power calculations, not floating-point or probabilistic estimates.

## Relationship to prior work

The 27-line/45-tritangent geometry and Schläfli incidence are classical and used as input. The prior ordinary (nonequivariant) critical-group decomposition is not claimed as new. Armstrong–Povero–Salamon survey the cubic geometry; Banwait–Fité–Loughran tabulate the twenty-five finite-field Weyl classes, geometric line splitting and Picard-cohomology information; Rybakov–Trepalin investigate those Weyl types and their realizability. The respective geometric, line-cycle, and Picard-cohomology results do not specify the twenty-five 71-by-142 integer coinvariant Smith decompositions or the two inequivalent critical groups for equal line-cycle types. The graph monodromy pairing is standard. No claim is made to have established historical priority exhaustively; unindexed prior computations remain possible.

## Limitations

Only the abstract graph critical group and its arithmetic permutation action are classified. A full classification of surfaces realizing every Weyl type over each individual field, geometric stable smoothings, monodromy of families, arbitrary edge thicknesses, or Néron component groups is outside the claim. This calculation is self-contained but has not undergone independent mathematical audit. A previously unknown equivalent integral classification could alter the priority assessment.
