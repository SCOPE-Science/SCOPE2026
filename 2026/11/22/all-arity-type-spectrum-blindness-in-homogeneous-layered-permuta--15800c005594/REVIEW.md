# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The relation of reversed pairs forms the old layer equivalence relation. Each new coordinate is either inserted into an existing old layer or into one of the \(k+1\) outside gaps. The first possibility has exact EGF \((1-z)^{-a_i-1}\); the second is an arbitrary sequence of nonempty decreasing blocks, with EGF \((1-z)/(1-2z)\). Their labeled product yields \((1-z)^{1-n}/(1-2z)^{k+1}\). Algebraic expansion gives both unary and binary counts and the exact integer recovery of \((n,k)\), while the first nonisomorphic collision is verified by the ordered compositions at size three. Independent labeled-diagram and direct pattern-popularity enumerations tested 48 configurations, with three-method exact agreement. Finite checks supplement, not substitute for, the proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk
Cameron's full 2002 article classifies the layered Fraïssé class and lexicographic model, but does not enumerate all finite-base tuple-type counts or characterize equality of numerical spectra. Mansour–Vainshtein 2000 count a different avoidance class; Albert–Homberger–Pantone 2014 classify popularity in the larger class of all separable permutations, not in the layered subclass; Vatter 2006 reconstructs an ordered composition from a set of deletion patterns, which is a strictly richer invariant than the numerical cardinalities of extension types. Searches of indexed prior research, including alternatives using wreath stabilizer orbits, did not locate an implication of the rational EGF or two-count inverse law. An equivalent result under a different orbit-counting terminology may be unindexed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The all-arity EGF (equivalently, the complete equipopularity classification within the layered permutation class) and sharp two-count reconstruction theorem provide a complete natural classification of *numerical logical-extension equivalence* for this Fraïssé class. Its strongest contrast is structural: every arity completely forgets the order and individual sizes of old layers even though the total size and number of layers are recoverable. The first concrete collision and the sharp unary-versus-binary distinction separate it from a routine total-age enumeration.

## Limits
The theorem concerns ordered, pairwise-distinct new-coordinate types in the layered class, not multisets, reducts or full pattern-deletion data. Historical indexing coverage and mathematical review remain non-independent.
