# All dihedral-subgroup homotopy types of the five-point noncrossing partition complex

## Finding
Let \(\mathrm{NC}_5\) denote the lattice of noncrossing set partitions of the vertices \(0,1,2,3,4\) of a convex pentagon, with the discrete partition \(\hat 0\) and indiscrete partition \(\hat 1\) removed. Let \(K=\Delta(\mathrm{NC}_5\setminus\{\hat0,\hat1\})\) be the order complex. The dihedral group \(D_5=\langle r,s:r^5=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\) acts by \(r(i)=i+1\pmod5\) and \(s(i)=-i\pmod5\). Ordinary geometric quotient spaces by every one of its eight subgroups are completely classified:

\[
|K|/H\simeq\begin{cases}
\bigvee^{14}S^2,&H=\{1\},\\
\bigvee^{7}S^2,&H\text{ is any of the five reflection subgroups},\\
M(\mathbb Z/5,1)\vee S^2\vee S^2,&H=\langle r\rangle\cong C_5,\\
S^2,&H=D_5.
\end{cases}
\]

The Moore space \(M(\mathbb Z/5,1)\) is the two-dimensional CW complex obtained from \(S^1\) by attaching one 2-cell by a degree-five map. In particular the full cyclic quotient has \(\pi_1\cong\mathbb Z/5\), while every reflection-containing quotient is simply connected. The integral deck-group lattice of the cyclic covering has an explicit description, with \(R=\mathbb Z[C_5]\) and \(I=\ker(R\to\mathbb Z)\) the augmentation ideal:
\[
\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong_R I\oplus R^2.
\]
The last isomorphism is as \(R\)-modules, not only as abelian groups.

## Assumptions and scope
A partition is noncrossing if it has no four vertices in cyclic order \(a<b<c<d\) whose equivalence relation satisfies \(a\sim c\), \(b\sim d\), and \(a\not\sim b\). Refinement defines the order; an order-complex simplex is a strict chain of nontrivial, nonuniversal noncrossing partitions. The construction includes every such chain, not only maximal chains. The group is the geometric pentagon dihedral group, of order ten. The quotients are ordinary topological orbit spaces, not homotopy-orbit or Borel constructions. All statements apply to exactly five ground points.

## Proof
**1. Dimension and simply connected cover.** The five-point lattice contains the Catalan number \(C_5=42\) partitions. Excluding its two extrema leaves forty vertices of ranks one, two and three. Its proper-part order complex therefore has dimension two. The established EL-shellability theorem for noncrossing partition lattices [1] implies that this connected proper-part complex is a wedge of two-spheres. The chain counts are forty vertices, 150 edges, and 125 triangles, yielding \(\chi(K)=40-150+125=15\). Consequently \(K\simeq\bigvee^{14}S^2\), and \(K\) is simply connected.

**2. Exact orbit-cell Euler characteristics.** Every dihedral element preserves partition rank, so a chain cannot be setwise stabilized with its vertices permuted. Consequently the quotient has a regular orbit-cell decomposition indexed by orbits of chains. Explicit enumeration of all chains gives the following counts in dimensions zero, one and two:

| Acting subgroup | Orbit vertices | Orbit edges | Orbit triangles | Euler characteristic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\{1\}\) | 40 | 150 | 125 | 15 |
| \(\langle s\rangle\) | 24 | 81 | 65 | 8 |
| \(\langle r\rangle\) | 8 | 30 | 25 | 3 |
| \(D_5\) | 8 | 21 | 15 | 2 |

All five reflection subgroups are conjugate and have the same orbit counts. The accompanying standard-library checker independently constructs these four orbit sets using the defining noncrossing condition and checks every count; thus these are exhaustive enumerations, not sampled estimates.

**3. Fundamental groups for reflection-containing subgroups.** For each of the five reflections, an invariant proper partition exists: the reflection swaps some adjacent vertex pair, and the partition with exactly that pair as a nontrivial block is fixed. Every nontrivial rotation fixes no proper partition: invariance under a translation by a generator of \(\mathbb Z/5\) would make the equivalence classes cosets of a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/5\), hence either all singletons or one block, both removed. Distinct ranks in a chain imply the same statements for geometric fixed points. Armstrong's orbit-space theorem [4] applies to the simply connected simplicial complex \(K\) and identifies \(\pi_1(|K|/H)\) with \(H\) modulo the normal subgroup generated by elements having fixed points. Thus the cyclic quotient has \(\pi_1\cong C_5\), and the reflection quotient and full dihedral quotient are simply connected (the reflections normally generate \(D_5\)).

A connected, simply connected, two-dimensional CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres: its second cellular homology is free, Hurewicz gives a map from a wedge of spheres realizing a basis, and Whitehead's theorem applies. Using the orbit Euler counts, the reflection quotient is \(\bigvee^7S^2\), and the full dihedral quotient is \(S^2\).

**4. The cyclic quotient, including attaching maps.** Because nonidentity rotations fix no vertices, the order-complex rotation action is free, so its orbit is an honest two-dimensional CW complex with 8 vertices, 30 edges and 25 triangles. Choose a spanning tree of seven quotient edges and contract it. This leaves 23 oriented one-cells and 25 triangular attaching words. The standalone verifier constructs the oriented quotient chain incidences, verifies \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) over the integers, forms the triangular boundary words with their signs, and performs 22 successive geometric one-cell/two-cell cancellations. Each cancellation chooses a 2-cell whose attaching word contains one 1-cell exactly once with exponent \(+1\) or \(-1\), slides the other attaching maps by the resulting substitution, and eliminates that 1/2-cell pair; these operations preserve the CW homotopy type, not merely its fundamental-group presentation. The surviving complex has exactly one one-cell and three two-cells, with attaching words, up to orientation and order, \(t^5\), the empty word and the empty word. This is precisely \(M(\mathbb Z/5,1)\vee S^2\vee S^2\). The finite cancellation trace is deterministically reproduced from the complete orbit enumeration by `verify_nc5.py`.

**5. Integral deck-group module.** Let \(R=\mathbb Z[C_5]\), with generator \(t\). The cyclic quotient found above has fundamental group \(C_5\), and \(K\) is its universal covering space. Lift the quotient homotopy equivalence from the presentation in step 4 to the corresponding universal covers (based homotopy equivalences preserve fundamental groups up to the chosen generator). The universal cover of \(M(\mathbb Z/5,1)\) has cellular complex in dimensions two and one with differential multiplication by the norm \(N=1+t+t^2+t^3+t^4\); hence its second homology is \(\ker(N:R\to R)=I\). The two additional quotient two-spheres lift to two free rank-one \(R\)-summands. This proves the asserted integral \(R\)-module isomorphism; its underlying rank is \(4+2\cdot5=14\), consistent with step 1.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_nc5.py` from the same directory as this file; the checker needs only the Python 3 standard library. It reproduces the 42 noncrossing partitions, forty proper partitions, every strict chain, the group actions, orbit-cell counts, Euler characteristics, reflection-fixed vertices, free rotation action, boundary compositions, seven spanning-tree contractions, 22 cellular one/two cancellations, and the final degree-five/empty/empty attaching words. It terminates with `VERIFY_OK` if and only if all its assertions pass. The higher-dimensional homotopy conclusions also invoke the cited shellability, Hurewicz–Whitehead, and Armstrong theorems; the executable checks their finite combinatorial inputs, not those general published theorems.

## Relationship to prior work
Athanasiadis–Brady–Watt [1] establish the required shellability of the underlying noncrossing partition lattice, not the four orbit homotopy types. Callan–Smiley [2] study rotation/reflection orbit counts and invariant partitions, not the topology or attaching maps of geometric quotients. Ding [3] analyzes dihedral fixed sublattices and homology characters, rather than the complete integral homotopy classification of these eight quotient spaces. Armstrong [4] supplies a general orbit fundamental-group theorem, used rather than claimed as new. The new content lies in the full five-point orbit homotopy list, the explicit integral cyclic quotient and the deck-module decomposition, and the reflection-versus-rotation contrast. No assertion is made that mere absence from bibliographic search proves publication priority.

## Limitations
Only the five-point noncrossing partition lattice and the indicated natural dihedral action are classified; general \(\mathrm{NC}_n\) subgroup quotients are not addressed. The conclusion describes ordinary homotopy types, not equivariant homotopy types of the entire group action. Priority may be affected by equivalent unpublished or unindexed calculations. The standalone code exhaustively verifies finite combinatorics and cellular cancellations but does not substitute for the classical theorems cited in the proof.
