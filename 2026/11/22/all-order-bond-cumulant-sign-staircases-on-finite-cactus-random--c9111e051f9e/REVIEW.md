# Scientific review: all-order cactus bond cumulants

## Correctness — PASS
The statement fixes finite simple cactus graphs, the free-boundary random-cluster distribution, strictly positive edge activities, a real positive cluster weight, and distinct marked edges. The cyclomatic-number decomposition rigorously factors every cycle block and bridge. Mixed differentiation of the exact moment-generating function reduces the cumulant to \((b\partial_b)^r\log(1+b)\) with a positive factor. A polynomial recurrence and an interlacing proof establish exactly \(r-2\) simple positive Eulerian sign roots; monotonicity of the activity product proves every threshold and the strict inequalities. The proof handles \(0<q<1\), \(q=1\), all \(q>1\), equality at thresholds and independent-block zeros. Direct rational configuration enumeration yielded 2,154 matching comparisons, including mixed cycles and bridges; finite verification is supplementary, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with priority risk
The classical random-cluster definition and the exact cactus cycle-block factorization are acknowledged as prior material. In particular, the closest examined cactus negative-association study (record `3f0dfb35e019`) proves a rank-one pair-covariance identity for \(0<q\le1\), not an all-order sign classification for \(q>1\). The 2026 Çiçeksiz–Ravichandran paper analyzes pairwise negative correlations, not these higher mixed cumulants. Grimmett's 2002 random-cluster survey supplies foundational definitions, not the claimed threshold staircase in the sections inspected. Indexed searches for cycle cumulants, Eulerian roots, higher Potts bond cumulants and cactus \(q>1\) fourth-cumulant reversals identified no covering result. The possibility of an unindexed equivalent or an earlier elementary derivation from the known cycle law remains and warrants independent priority review.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The claim answers a natural all-arity nonlinear response question for a canonical finite statistical-mechanics model, rather than tabulating a small graph. It identifies an exact support criterion, a universal dependence on the complete-cycle product, and an unbounded hierarchy of \(r-2\) sharp, simultaneous sign reversals at explicit Eulerian thresholds. Fourth- and fifth-order cumulants exhibit two and three reversals already on the smallest feasible cycles. The theorem is a meaningful structural complement to the known pairwise association results; it is limited to cactus graphs and does not establish a thermodynamic transition.
