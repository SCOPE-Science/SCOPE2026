# Ordinary orbit-space homotopy types for all subgroups of the seven-point Fano matroid

## Finding
Let \(K\) be the rank-three Fano matroid independence complex on \(\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\}\), and \(G=\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\). For **every** subgroup \(H\le G\), the ordinary geometric quotient \(|K|/H\) is classified by the following table. The last column counts distinct subgroups of each type in \(G\).

| Subgroup isomorphism type | Homotopy type of \(|K|/H\) | Number |
|:--|:--|--:|
| trivial | \(\bigvee^{13}S^2\) | 1 |
| \(C_2\) | \(\bigvee^5S^2\) | 21 |
| \(C_3\) | \(\bigvee^5S^2\) | 28 |
| \(C_4\) | \(\bigvee^3S^2\) | 21 |
| \(C_2\times C_2\) | \(S^2\) | 14 |
| \(S_3\) | \(S^2\) | 28 |
| \(C_7\) | \(M(\mathbb Z/7,1)\vee S^2\) | 8 |
| \(D_8\) | contractible | 21 |
| \(A_4\) | \(S^2\) | 14 |
| \(C_7\rtimes C_3\) | \(S^2\) | 8 |
| \(S_4\) | contractible | 14 |
| \(G\) | contractible | 1 |

Here \(M(\mathbb Z/7,1)\) is a circle with a degree-seven two-cell attached, and \(D_8\) is the dihedral group of order eight. The eight Singer subgroups \(C_7\) are exactly the subgroups with nonsimply-connected quotients. For every other subgroup, the two-sphere multiplicity is \((14-2n_2+2n_3+2n_4)/|H|-1\), where \(n_j\) counts elements of order \(j\) in \(H\). The contribution is the complete classification across the subgroup lattice; the Singer row itself follows from earlier Fano cap-complex attaching data.

## Assumptions and scope
Write the seven points as nonzero binary triples, identified with integers 1 through 7. A three-element set is a face precisely when its binary XOR is nonzero. The independence complex has all seven vertices, all 21 edges, and exactly 28 triangles. The group consists of the 168 invertible binary linear transformations. The quotient is the ordinary geometric orbit space, not the Borel construction. The homotopy types are integral and unlocalized, and the classification includes every actual subgroup, not merely representatives of isomorphism types.

## Proof
**The complex is simply connected.** The one-skeleton is complete, and triangle loops generate its fundamental group. Every missing triangle is a Fano line, and its boundary loop is nullhomotopic in \(K\): cone the three boundary edges to any point off that line to obtain three triangles of \(K\). Adding the present triangles already kills all other triangle loops. Hence \(K\) is simply connected. Its Euler characteristic is \(7-21+28=14\).

**Subgroup census.** The accompanying exact computation constructs all 168 invertible matrices, with element-order counts \((1,21,56,42,48)\) for orders \((1,2,3,4,7)\). It enumerates subgroups, starting with all two-generated subgroups and then checking that adjoining any group element to any enumerated subgroup gives another subgroup in the list. This closure and the presence of the trivial group imply that the 179 subgroups found are exhaustive. For each subgroup the computation also counts the orbits of all barycentric flags of nonempty faces. Before quotient there are \((56,210,168)\) such cells. Any transformation stabilizing a flag fixes all its vertices, because the ranks in a flag are distinct. Thus cell-orbit counts give the Euler characteristic of the quotient.

The computed subgroup orders and order profiles identify the rows of the table. Orders four, six and eight are distinguished by their counts of involutions and elements of order four (there is no order six). At order twelve there are four Sylow three subgroups, and the action on them embeds the group in \(S_4\): the normalizer of each has order three, and their intersection is trivial. The image is \(A_4\). At order twenty-four the four Sylow three normalizers have order six, each with a unique Sylow three subgroup. Their intersection has order dividing six and no element of order three, so a nontrivial kernel of the action would have order two. Its involution would be central in the subgroup, which is impossible: the ambient involutions are a single conjugacy class of size 21 (the unique nonidentity Jordan type of order two), hence have centralizer of order eight. Thus the image is \(S_4\). At order twenty-one the Sylow seven subgroup is normal and the Sylow three acts nontrivially since there is no element of order twenty-one. This gives the stated semidirect product.

**Fundamental groups.** The point cycle types for element orders two, three and four are \(1^3 2^2\), \(1^1 3^2\), and \(1^1 2^1 4^1\). All these elements fix a point. Order-seven elements are seven-cycles, so they preserve no nonempty face and fix no point of the realization. Armstrong's theorem for an action on a simply connected complex identifies \(\pi_1(|K|/H)\) with the quotient of \(H\) by the normal subgroup generated by elements fixing geometric points. For orders not divisible by seven this kills all of \(H\). For the order-21 group, the commutator of a nontrivial acting element of order three with a generator of \(C_7\) generates \(C_7\), so the normal closure of that element of order three is the whole group. For \(G\), use simplicity of \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathrm{PSL}_2(7)\). For \(H=C_7\), the action is free and the fundamental group is \(C_7\). These are all cases from the census.

**Euler characteristic and simply connected quotients.** The Euler characteristics of the fixed spaces for element orders \(1,2,3,4,7\) are \(14,-2,2,2,0\). Equivalently, the traces on \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\) are \(13,-3,1,1,-1\). The admissible barycentric action gives these Lefschetz numbers by alternating fixed flag counts; these counts also give the quotient Euler characteristic by Burnside averaging:
\[
 \chi(|K|/H)=\frac{14-2n_2+2n_3+2n_4}{|H|}.
\]
The verifier checks this expression against direct flag-orbit counts for every enumerated subgroup. Each simply connected quotient is a connected CW complex of dimension at most two with free integral second homology of rank \(\chi-1\). Hurewicz realizes a homology basis by maps of two-spheres; taking their wedge gives a homology equivalence between simply connected CW spaces, hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem. This also gives contractibility when the rank is zero.

**Singer row from the cap quotient's two-skeleton data.** All Singer subgroups are Sylow seven subgroups and are conjugate. Identify \(\mathbb F_2^3\) with \(\mathbb F_8\), choose a primitive element \(\alpha\) satisfying \(\alpha^3=1+\alpha\), and label \(\alpha^i\) by \(i\bmod 7\). The Fano lines are the seven translates of \(\{0,1,3\}\). The independence complex is the two-skeleton of the cap complex, since both allow exactly the same subsets of size at most three, and the free Singer actions agree on that two-skeleton. The cap-complex calculation in reference 5 gives the quotient before its equivariant collapse: one vertex, three oriented edges \(a,b,c\) of step sizes one, two and three, and four triangles in its two-skeleton. The first, second, third and fourth representatives are \((0,1,2),(0,1,4),(0,1,5),(0,2,4)\), with boundary words
\[
 a^2b^{-1},\qquad ac^2,\qquad ac^{-1}b,\qquad b^2c.
\]
These are also the attaching words of \(|K|/C_7\). Use the first cell to homotope the loop \(b\) to \(a^2\), and the third to homotope \(c\) to \(ba=a^3\). More explicitly, before canceling each loop and its defining cell, replace other attaching maps by homotopic ones that substitute the defining word so those maps no longer use the canceled loop. The two remaining words are then both \(a^7\). After attaching one such disk, the other attaching map is nullhomotopic, so that cell splits off as a two-sphere up to homotopy. This recovers \(M(\mathbb Z/7,1)\vee S^2\), with the extra sphere coming from the triangle orbit removed along with the tetrahedron orbit in the cap-complex proof.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_fano.py` using Python 3 and its standard library. It constructs the group and all simplices, certifies the exhaustive subgroup list by closure under generator adjunction, calculates flag orbits and Euler characteristics for every subgroup, and reconstructs the four oriented Singer attaching words. The recorded output is in `verification_output.txt` and ends `VERIFY_OK`. The finite computation checks the enumerations and identities, not the general topological deductions or bibliographic priority.

## Relationship to prior work
Cameron describes the characteristic-two Fano representation, and Ardila, Castillo and Samper discuss independence-complex shellability. Moci and Pezzoli study matroid automorphism representations on homology. For this same complex the rational homology character and integral Singer homology lattice have already been calculated. The rational character mechanically provides the rational invariant multiplicities; those numbers and the integral Singer lattice are not claimed as new. The Fano cap-complex result of reference 5 supplies the same four attaching maps and the same loop eliminations. The Singer homotopy type in this table follows from that calculation by retaining all four two-cells and omitting the three-cell, and is not an independent novelty claim. The distinct content is the integral ordinary orbit-space classification across all 179 subgroups: determining where the quotient fundamental group is trivial, and classifying the resulting ordinary quotients, not merely extracting rational homology ranks or computing the cyclic case.

## Limitations
Only the seven-point rank-three Fano independence complex is treated, not higher-rank projective matroids, larger fields, equivariant stable homotopy spectra, or Borel constructions. The assignment of novelty refers to inspected sources and is not an absolute priority claim.
