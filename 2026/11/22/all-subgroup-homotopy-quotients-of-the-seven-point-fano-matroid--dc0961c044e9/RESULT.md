# All subgroup homotopy quotients of the seven-point Fano matroid

## Finding
Let \(K\) be the rank-three Fano matroid independence complex on \(\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\}\), with simplices the linearly independent subsets of size at most three, and \(G=\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\). For **every** subgroup \(H\le G\), the ordinary geometric quotient \(|K|/H\) is classified by the following table; the third column gives the number of subgroups of that type in \(G\):

| Subgroup isomorphism type | Homotopy type of \(|K|/H\) | Number |
|:--|:--|--:|
| trivial | \(\bigvee^{13}S^2\) | 1 |
| \(C_2\) | \(\bigvee^5S^2\) | 21 |
| \(C_3\) | \(\bigvee^5S^2\) | 28 |
| \(C_4\) | \(\bigvee^3S^2\) | 21 |
| \(C_2\times C_2\) | \(S^2\) | 14 |
| \(S_3\) | \(S^2\) | 28 |
| \(C_7\) | \(M(\mathbb Z/7,1)\vee S^2\) | 8 |
| \(D_8\) | contractible | 21 |
| \(A_4\) | \(S^2\) | 14 |
| \(C_7\rtimes C_3\) | \(S^2\) | 8 |
| \(S_4\) | contractible | 14 |
| \(G\) | contractible | 1 |

Here \(M(\mathbb Z/7,1)\) is a Moore space formed from a circle by a degree-seven two-cell, and \(D_8\) means the dihedral group of order eight. Equivalently, the Singer subgroups \(C_7\) are the **only** subgroups with nonsimply-connected quotients; each has fundamental group \(C_7\). All other quotients are simply connected, and for \(H\not\cong C_7\), their two-sphere multiplicity is \(\beta(H)=(14-2n_2+2n_3+2n_4)/|H|-1\), where \(n_j\) counts elements of order \(j\) in \(H\). This is a complete integral homotopy classification of ordinary quotient spaces, not merely a rational homology computation.

## Assumptions and scope
Write the seven points as nonzero binary triples, identified with integers 1 through 7; a three-element set is a face precisely when its binary XOR is nonzero. The independence complex has all 7 vertices, all 21 edges, and exactly 28 triangles. The group consists of the 168 invertible binary linear transformations. The quotient is the ordinary geometric topological orbit space, rather than the Borel construction. The homotopy types refer to integral, unlocalized spaces. Every subgroup is included, not merely one representative of each abstract isomorphism type.

## Proof
**Simply connected covering complex.** The one-skeleton is the complete graph. Every missing triangle is one of the seven lines, and its boundary is the sum of the three present triangle boundaries obtained by adjoining any point off that line, with compatible orientations. More strongly, its boundary loop is nullhomotopic via those three two-simplices (the cone on that line's boundary). Every loop in a complete-graph one-skeleton decomposes into triangle loops; hence \(K\) is simply connected. Standard matroid shellability, or the exact face counts and the absence of one-dimensional homology, gives \(K\simeq\bigvee^{13}S^2\).

**Exhausting subgroup possibilities.** Enumerating the 168 invertible binary three-by-three matrices gives element-order counts \((1,21,56,42,48)\) in orders \((1,2,3,4,7)\). The accompanying standalone program enumerates the **179 distinct subgroups**, validates closure of the list under adjoining each of the 168 group elements (thus establishing completeness without assuming two-generator classification), and groups them into the twelve rows of the table. For each subgroup it directly constructs the orbits of all barycentric flags of nonempty faces. There are \((56,210,168)\) barycentric cells in dimensions zero, one, two before quotient. Rank-strict flags make the barycentric action admissible, so orbit counts are genuine cellular Euler counts. For all 179 subgroups these agree with the closed Euler expression below.

**Fundamental groups.** For every element of order two, three or four, the permutation on the seven points has a fixed point: its cycle type is, respectively, \(1^3 2^2\), \(1^1 3^2\), or \(1^1 2^1 4^1\). Thus it fixes a geometric point of \(K\). Elements of order seven act as seven-cycles on vertices and preserve no nonempty face, since every face has size at most three. Armstrong's orbit-space theorem identifies \(\pi_1(|K|/H)\) with \(H\) modulo the normal subgroup generated by its elements having fixed geometric points, because \(K\) is simply connected. For groups whose order is not divisible by seven, every element is of order at most four, so the quotient is simply connected. The only groups whose orders are divisible by seven are \(C_7\), \(C_7\rtimes C_3\), and \(G\). In the order-21 group the normal closure of an element of order three contains the order-seven commutator subgroup, so it is the whole group. For \(G\), simplicity of \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\cong\operatorname{PSL}_2(7)\) makes the normal closure of any involution equal to \(G\). For \(C_7\) the action is free, giving fundamental group \(C_7\). Hence the Singer groups are exactly the exceptions.

**Euler characteristic and the simply connected quotients.** The signed Lefschetz trace on \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\) is \(13,-3,1,1,-1\) for element orders \(1,2,3,4,7\). Consequently the Lefschetz Euler characteristic of fixed spaces is \(14,-2,2,2,0\). Finite-group averaging yields
\[
\chi(|K|/H)=\frac{14-2n_2+2n_3+2n_4}{|H|}.
\]
Every quotient is connected and has cellular dimension two. When its fundamental group is trivial, its integral \(H_2\) is a free abelian group, of rank \(\chi-1\). The Hurewicz theorem realizes a basis by two-sphere maps; the resulting map of sphere wedges induces a homology equivalence between simply connected CW spaces and hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem. The twelve enumerated subgroup profiles yield exactly the displayed sphere multiplicities, including the contractible cases.

**The singular Singer exception is a Moore space plus a sphere.** Choose a Singer generator and label the points in its cyclic orbit by \(0,1,\ldots,6\). It acts freely on the entire realization because no nonempty face is invariant. The quotient has a single vertex, three directed edge-orbits labelled \(a,b,c\) by positive differences one, two and three, and four triangle-orbits represented by \((0,1,2)\), \((0,1,4)\), \((0,1,5)\), \((0,2,4)\). Reading their oriented boundaries gives the exact group relators
\[
 aaB,\qquad acc,\qquad aCb,\qquad bbc,
\]
where capital letters denote the inverses of the corresponding lowercase edges. The first relator eliminates \(b=a^2\), and the third eliminates \(c=ba=a^3\). The two remaining attaching words both become \(a^7\). These are elementary two-cell/one-cell Tietze collapses of a two-dimensional CW complex, not only a group-presentation calculation. Finally, two two-cells attached to one circle along the same degree-seven loop are homotopy equivalent to one degree-seven Moore attachment wedged with a two-sphere: use one disk as a nullhomotopy for the attaching map of the other. Thus the Singer quotient is \(M(\mathbb Z/7,1)\vee S^2\), completing all cases.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_fano.py` (Python 3 standard library). The program constructs all nonzero binary vectors and 28 admissible triangles, enumerates the complete matrix group, computes and certifies the complete 179-subgroup list by closure under arbitrary extra generators, and checks the cell-orbit and Euler formulas for every subgroup, the twelve subgroup profiles and the four oriented Singer attaching words. Its terminal marker is `VERIFY_OK`; recorded output is in `verification_output.txt`. Symbolic topological deductions above are part of the proof and are not inferred from a numeric sampling experiment.

## Relationship to prior work
The Fano matroid's independence sets and characteristic-two representation are described in Cameron (2015), while matroid independence-complex shellability is a classical subject and is reviewed and strengthened by Ardila, Castillo and Samper (2014 preprint). Moci and Pezzoli (2020 preprint) develop general **homology representations** of matroid automorphism groups, not homotopy classifications of their ordinary orbit spaces. Two especially close Fano calculations already establish the full rational \(G\)-homology character \((13,-3,1,1,-1,-1)\), and the integral Singer module \(\mathbb Z[C_7]\oplus I\). Those character and lattice results are used and expressly not claimed as new. Neither determines the singular-versus-simply-connected dichotomy, the Singer Moore-space attaching maps, or all 179 quotient homotopy types. The earlier character does determine the numerical rational multiplicities: these counts alone are therefore not the new result.

## Limitations
Only the seven-point rank-three Fano independence complex is treated, not higher-rank projective matroids, larger fields, integral equivariant stable homotopy spectra or Borel/homotopy quotients. Results for the rational character and Singer homology lattice are prior results. Full novelty is assessed relative to inspected sources and accessible indexed research, not a proof of absolute publication priority. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
