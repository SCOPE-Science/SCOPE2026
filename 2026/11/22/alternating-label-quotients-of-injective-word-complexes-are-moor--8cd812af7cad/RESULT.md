# Alternating-label quotients of injective-word complexes are Moore spaces

## Finding

For each integer \(n\ge3\), the quotient of the ordinary injective-word cell complex by the alternating group on the letters has the following integral homotopy type:

\[
|K_n|/A_n\simeq
\begin{cases}
M(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,n-2),&\text{if }n\text{ is odd},\\
M(\mathbb Z/(n/2)\mathbb Z,n-2)\vee S^{n-1},&\text{if }n\text{ is even}.
\end{cases}
\]

The Moore-space notation \(M(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,d)\) means \(S^d\cup_m e^{d+1}\), attaching a single \((d+1)\)-cell by a map of degree \(m\). In odd widths the only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_{n-2}\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\); in even widths \(\widetilde H_{n-2}\cong\mathbb Z/(n/2)\mathbb Z\) and \(\widetilde H_{n-1}\cong\mathbb Z\).

## Assumptions and scope

Let \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). The finite regular CW complex \(K_n\) has one open cell of dimension \(k-1\) for each ordered injective word \(w=(w_1,\ldots,w_k)\), with \(1\le k\le n\). The characteristic map on a cell has the ordered-face prescription

\[
\partial(w_1,\ldots,w_k)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}(w_1,\ldots,\widehat{w_i},\ldots,w_k).
\]

This is the standard semisimplicial realization of injective words; its barycentric subdivision is the order complex of the nonempty injective-word poset. The group \(A_n\) acts by relabeling, preserving word positions. The result concerns the **ordinary geometric orbit space** \(Y_n=|K_n|/A_n\), not the homotopy-orbit or Borel construction.

The action preserves each cell's orientation whenever it stabilizes the cell, because it does not permute word positions. Therefore the orbit space inherits a CW structure whose cells are word orbits, and its integral cellular differential is the signed sum of the deletion faces, grouped by orbit. All claims hold without a primality assumption for the integer \(n\ge3\).

## Proof

**Orbit cells.** For an injective word of length \(k\le n-2\), the stabilizer of any chosen extension to a bijection has two unused letters that can be transposed. Consequently an arbitrary permutation taking one such word to another can be adjusted by this unused transposition to become even. Thus \(A_n\) is transitive on these words. For \(k=n-1\), append the unique missing letter; for \(k=n\), use the word itself. The resulting full permutation parity is an \(A_n\)-invariant, with exactly two possible orbits, denoted \(e\) and \(o\). Hence there is one quotient cell in each dimension \(0,\ldots,n-3\), and two cells in each dimension \(n-2,n-1\).

**Cellular differential.** The sole differential between one-cell-orbit dimensions \(j\) and \(j-1\) is multiplication by \(\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}(-1)^{i-1}\): it equals \(0\) for odd \(j\) and \(1\) for even \(j\). The differential from degree \(n-2\) into degree \(n-3\) is \((0\quad0)\) when \(n\) is odd and \((1\quad1)\) when \(n\) is even.

To determine the top boundary, delete position \(i\) from a full word and append its deleted letter to the end to complete the shorter word. The completed permutation has parity multiplied by \((-1)^{n-i}\), while the oriented deletion face has coefficient \((-1)^{i-1}\). With the \(e,o\) orbit bases this yields, for \(n=2k+1\),

\[
\partial_{n-1}=\begin{pmatrix}k+1&-k\\-k&k+1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\partial_{n-2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\end{pmatrix};
\]

for \(n=2k\), it yields

\[
\partial_{n-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-k&k\\k&-k\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\partial_{n-2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

These signed matrices satisfy \(\partial^2=0\), and no nonorientable-cell or stabilizer multiplicity is omitted because every cell stabilizer acts trivially on that cell.

**Even case.** Let \(n=2k\ge4\). The alternating degree-zero and degree-one attaching sequence through dimension \(n-3\) gives a sphere \(S^{n-3}\) up to homotopy. Both \((n-2)\)-cells attach to that sphere with degree one. Cancel one of these cells against the sphere; the remaining \((n-2)\)-skeleton has homotopy type \(S^{n-2}\). The two top-cell attaching degrees on this surviving sphere are \(k\) and \(-k\), as their boundary columns lie in the kernel generated by \(e-o\). A unimodular basis change of the two top attaching spheres gives degrees \(k\) and \(0\). As \(\pi_{n-2}(S^{n-2})\cong\mathbb Z\), their cofiber is

\[
M(\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z,n-2)\vee S^{n-1}.
\]

The elementary cancellations used here are homotopy equivalences of CW complexes, not merely reductions of homology matrices.

**Odd case, dimensions at least three.** Let \(n=2k+1\ge5\). The one-cell-orbit skeleton through dimension \(n-3\) is contractible: its final even-dimensional attaching map has degree one. The two \((n-2)\)-cells then give \(S^{n-2}\vee S^{n-2}\). The two top-cell attachments are maps from \(S^{n-2}\) into this wedge. Since \(n-2\ge3\), each such map is completely classified by its two integral degrees. The displayed top matrix has Smith normal form \(\operatorname{diag}(1,n)\): its determinant is \(n\) and its entries have greatest common divisor one. Elementary row and column operations are realizable by self-homotopy equivalences of the two sphere wedges. Their homotopy cofiber therefore contains one cancelling degree-one cell pair and the degree-\(n\) Moore attachment, yielding \(M(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,n-2)\).

**The first odd case.** For \(n=3\), the quotient has one vertex, edges \(a,b\), and two triangles whose oriented attaching words are \(ab^{-1}a\) and \(ba^{-1}b\). A cellular one–two cancellation gives \(b=a^2\) and reduces the remaining relation to \(a^3\), leaving the standard two-cell presentation of \(M(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,1)\). This avoids using degree matrices alone to classify a two-dimensional fundamental group.

## Verification

The accompanying independent finite orbit enumerator `verify.py` builds all injective words for \(3\le n\le8\), constructs the actual \(A_n\) orbits using adjacent three-cycle generators, and forms the signed orbit-cell boundary maps without assuming the parity formulas. It checks the predicted cell counts, every relevant \(\partial^2=0\), the low-dimensional boundary pattern, and the exact integral Smith factors. The computed top Smith diagonals are \((1,3),(0,2),(1,5),(0,3),(1,7),(0,4)\) for \(n=3,4,5,6,7,8\), respectively. Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy installed. The symbolic parity-and-CW argument above, not finite enumeration, proves the assertion for every \(n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work

The injective-word complex and its top sphere-wedge homology are classical. Kerz describes the standard semisimplicial complex; Jonsson–Welker analyze related injective-word complexes; Reiner–Webb determine the symmetric-group module of its top homology, and Athanasiadis studies symmetric-group characters of rank selections. Those statements concern the original complex or its homology representations. Rational invariant theory alone does not determine the integral attaching degrees in the **alternating-group orbit space**, nor its Moore summands. The proof here calculates those quotient attaching maps directly; it does not assert a new classification of the original injective-word complex.

## Limitations

This classifies the ordinary \(A_n\)-orbit spaces for the full injective-word complex only, not arbitrary word subcomplexes, other subgroups, or equivariant homotopy types. It does not determine the homotopy-orbit construction. The finite checker covers only \(3\le n\le8\). Independent mathematical review and publication-priority verification have not been performed; an equivalent formulation in unindexed literature remains possible.
