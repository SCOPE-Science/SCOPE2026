# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The result quantifies over positive capacity, strictly interior starting water, unequal positive pulses and positive finite integrated proportional loss. The pulse recurrence gives the displayed four-positive-part identity. Two exterior cases follow respectively from positive-part monotonicity and its 1-Lipschitz bound. When only the larger initial pulse saturates, the sign proof separates retention below, at, and above the uniquely determined ratio \(s/(s+B)\); it checks positivity of the relevant branch and equality at the boundary. An exact rational checker passes 46,683 supplementary grid cases, plus independent boundary and invariance tests. These finite tests do not prove the universal claim; the analytic proof does.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
The closest mathematical comparators are the full publicly accessible 2026 results on three-position **periodic** storm ordering and on equal-pulse **periodic** spacing. Their premises and conclusions differ: neither allows a freely chosen initial state in a two-event transient with inter-event drying, nor asserts a crossover at an initial-wetness-dependent integrated loss. The Green–Ampt pulse-rearrangement result studies infiltration without drying. The full Bartlett et al. (2015) treatment provides the pure saturation-threshold one-event rule and stochastic runoff distributions, not this order comparison. The full Zheng et al. (2023) study motivates the significance of rainfall depth and initial moisture through observed runoff characteristics, not deterministic permutation classifications. No inspected source was found to imply this exact claim. Residual risk: an equivalent finite-buffer transient input-swap theorem may exist under dam, inventory or queueing terminology; historical priority has not been independently determined.

## Value — PASS
Runoff from two unequal storms and a moisture-limited storage is a natural hydrological benchmark motivated by observed controls of storm depth and antecedent moisture. The theorem is a complete qualitative phase classification, not merely a computation at chosen parameters. Its strict-reversal window and capacity- and large-depth-independent crossover reveal why a universal large-first or small-first heuristic fails after accounting for leakage and starting wetness. The integrated-loss invariance makes the result interpretable for nonconstant drying rates. It supplies an exact check for richer catchment models while making no unsupported field prediction.

## Scope and uncertainties
The result concerns one homogenous saturation-excess store with impulse rainfall and proportional loss. It does not establish a universal ordering rule for nonlinear infiltration, stochastic storm arrivals, spatial reservoirs, more than two storms, or flood peaks. Priority among historical finite-buffer rearrangement results remains provisional.
