# Scientific review — aperiodic NOR seam response

## Correctness — PASS

The graph dynamics is exactly specified as synchronous **open-neighborhood** NOR, not self-inclusive NOR. The proof reduces the distinguished two-cycle's basin to the total dominating sets, then uses an explicit diagonal-incidence graph to obtain the parity-dependent decomposition into one doubled cycle or two separate cycles. The ordinary cycle domination transfer has a stated explicit matrix. Newton identities and elementary Galois-field arguments prove the signed cubic difference, nonzero values, and sign reversals on every arithmetic progression. Finite exhaustive NOR evolution on each graph through nine rungs and a separate transfer trace through forty rungs match the exact expressions. A finite check is not claimed to prove the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority limitations

The ordinary prism total-domination sequence is known in OEIS A296102 (2018), and the public 2026 prism-domination record states the precise parity splitting. Those contributions are not new here. The survey of Aledo–Martinez–Valverde (2015) gives a general NOR context and periods, and the indexed Möbius minimum/minimal total-domination records count different populations. A majority-dynamics prism/Möbius seam law concerns a different update rule and a modulo-six response. The exact **Möbius universal-NOR-basin population**, signed topology-difference cubic, nonvanishing and progression-wise aperiodic reversals were not found in the checked sources. The most relevant prism record's full text could not be accessed and historical equivalents may exist; the search does not certify absolute uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

A single topological seam changes the attraction population of a distinguished, natural dynamical orbit despite preserving the size and degree of every graph. The sign of this change is not controlled by congruence classes: it alternates indefinitely within each residue class, while the relative discrepancy vanishes exponentially. This is an exact structural result for an unbounded natural family, not an isolated computational count.
