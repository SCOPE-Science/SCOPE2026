# Scientific review: equal-weight Fock radial-shell obstruction

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem applies to every odd \(n=2m-1\ge3\) with finitely many full-Fock Gaussian-compensated evaluations, all having the same positive external weight. Testing normalized monomials gives a degree-\(2m-1\) exact Laguerre-weight quadrature identity. Fewer than \(m\) radii contradict positivity of the squared vanishing polynomial. At exactly \(m\), the nodal polynomial solves a uniquely invertible integer-moment Hankel system; all nodes are algebraic and the uniquely determined radial weights are algebraic. Their ratio cannot equal a positive integer multiplicity ratio times an exponential of a nonzero algebraic radius difference, by Hermite–Lindemann. The converse construction uses positive Gauss–Laguerre quadrature weights and angular root-of-unity cancellation, giving a Parseval frame on exactly \(m\) radii with variable positive weights. These deductions hold with no angular restrictions on the equal-weight obstruction. The extracted checker passed 106 finite exact arithmetic assertions, which are not substituted for the proof of the all-degree result or transcendence theorem.

This remains a best-knowledge comparison, not an exhaustive priority certification; older exact Gaussian cubature or coherent-state literature could express the same obstruction under another name.

## Value — PASS
The natural baseline for an exact Gaussian-coherent sampling rule of degree \(2m-1\) is the \(m\)-node Gaussian radial quadrature rule. The theorem shows that enforcing **identical physical measurement weights** destroys that minimum: at least one additional radius is mandatory regardless of how many angular measurements are made. It also proves that variable positive weights achieve the minimal \(m\) radii exactly, giving a mathematically motivated contrast in the design parameter that cannot be resolved by angular oversampling. The result is a structural impossibility over every odd degree, not a tiny numerical improvement or an arbitrary finite slice. Its limitations are explicit: no claim about attaining \(m+1\) equal-weight radii, quantitative near-tightness, even degrees, or the minimum individual sample count.
