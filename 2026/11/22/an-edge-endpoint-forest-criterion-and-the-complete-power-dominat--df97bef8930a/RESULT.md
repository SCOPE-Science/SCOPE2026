# An edge-endpoint forest criterion and the complete power-domination landscape of the Petersen line graph

## Finding

**Theorem (endpoint–forest criterion).** Let \(H\) be any finite simple undirected graph with minimum degree at least \(2\). For a set \(S\subseteq E(H)\), interpreted as a vertex set in the line graph \(L(H)\), let \(C(S)\) be the endpoints of the selected edges and \(U(S)=V(H)\setminus C(S)\). Under ordinary power domination (one closed-neighborhood observation step followed by repeated unique-unobserved-neighbor forcing),

\[
S\text{ power dominates }L(H)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H[U(S)]\text{ is a forest}.
\]

**Exact Petersen corollary.** Write \(P=\mathrm{KG}(5,2)\), with vertices the two-subsets of a five-element set and adjacency for disjoint pairs. Let \(G=L(P)\), with \(|V(G)|=15\). Then the inclusion-maximal non-power-dominating subsets of \(V(G)=E(P)\) are precisely the ten three-edge stars of \(P\) and the twelve edge sets of its five-cycles. In particular, the complete power-domination polynomial is

\[
\mathcal P_G(z)=\sum_{S\text{ power dominates }G}z^{|S|}=(1+z)^{15}-(1+15z+90z^2+130z^3+60z^4+12z^5).
\]

The power-domination number is \(\gamma_P(G)=2\). There are exactly \(15\) minimum power-dominating pairs and exactly \(140\) inclusion-minimal three-element power-dominating sets. No inclusion-minimal power-dominating set has any other size.

Label an edge of \(P\) by the unique symbol omitted from the union of its two disjoint two-subset endpoints. Each of five symbols labels exactly three edges of \(P\). The optimal pairs are exactly the two-edge selections with a common omitted symbol. Therefore the token-jumping reconfiguration graph on optimum pairs is the disjoint union \(5K_3\).

For token-addition/removal (TAR), restrict intermediate power-dominating sets to at most \(k\) vertices. At \(k=2\) the graph has \(15\) isolated vertices; at \(k=3\) it has \(140\) isolated vertices and five connected components of \(40\) vertices each; at \(k=4\) its \(1645\) vertices form one connected component. Thus \(4\) is the sharp permanent-connectivity budget.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple, undirected. No connectedness assumption is needed for the endpoint–forest criterion, but \(\delta(H)\ge2\) is essential to the stated peeling proof. The selected sensors are **vertices of the line graph**, which correspond to edges of the underlying graph; this is not vertex power domination of the Petersen graph itself. A power-dominating set is any initial selection whose closed neighborhood becomes completely observed after iterating the rule that an observed vertex with exactly one unobserved neighbor observes that neighbor. Power domination is monotone under adding sensors. A forest includes isolated vertices and the empty graph. The Petersen enumeration is for the labelled fifteen-vertex graph, without identifying subsets under automorphism. Token jumping replaces one selected vertex while retaining the optimum cardinality; \(k\)-TAR changes exactly one selected vertex by addition or removal and permits any power-dominating intermediate set of size at most \(k\).

## Proof

**Step 1: endpoint–forest equivalence.** The closed neighborhood of the selected line-graph vertices comprises exactly those original edges having at least one endpoint in \(C(S)\). Thus the initially unobserved line-graph vertices are exactly the edges of \(F=H[U(S)]\).

If \(F\) contains a cycle, consider the first edge of this cycle that could become observed after the initial step. Any observed edge sharing an endpoint with it has at least two unobserved neighbors, namely the two incident cycle edges at that endpoint (or, if the edge considered is itself on the cycle, it cannot already be observed). Consequently no edge of the cycle is first forced, proving necessity.

Conversely, assume \(F\) is a forest. Take any nontrivial tree component of \(F\), choose a root, and process vertices other than the root in an order in which every child precedes its parent. For a leaf \(u\) of the remaining rooted tree, let \(e=uv\) be its as-yet-unobserved parent edge. Because \(\deg_H(u)\ge2\), there exists an edge \(f=uw\ne e\). Either \(w\in C(S)\), in which case \(f\) is already observed and \(w\) has no unobserved incident edges, or \(w\) belongs to a previously processed child subtree, in which case \(f\) has already been forced and \(w\) again has no unobserved incident edges. Thus the line-graph vertex \(f\) is observed and has exactly one unobserved neighbor, \(e\), so it forces \(e\). Iterating clears every edge of \(F\). Isolated vertices require no forcing. This proves sufficiency.

**Step 2: complete Petersen obstructions.** The simple-cycle enumeration for \(P=\mathrm{KG}(5,2)\), using every possible simple vertex path and identifying a cycle by its edge set, gives exactly \(12\) five-cycles, \(10\) six-cycles, \(15\) eight-cycles and \(20\) nine-cycles, with no cycles of any other length. Each five-cycle leaves an induced five-cycle on its five complementary vertices. Each six-cycle leaves an induced three-edge star on the four complementary vertices. Each eight-cycle leaves two vertices; its complement can contain at most one original edge, and every Petersen edge occurs in a five-cycle. Each nine-cycle leaves one vertex and therefore permits only the empty sensor set in its complement. Hence a set \(S\) fails precisely when it is contained in a five-cycle edge set or in a three-edge star. The converse follows because each such obstruction leaves, respectively, an induced five-cycle or six-cycle among the original vertices untouched by its endpoints. None of these \(22\) obstruction sets is contained in another (a three-edge star is not contained in any five-cycle). This completes the maximal-failure classification.

**Step 3: complete cardinality enumeration.** Take the union of the downward-closed families of all subsets of the \(22\) obstruction sets. Their numbers at cardinalities \(0,1,2,3,4,5\) are exactly \(1,15,90,130,60,12\), and there are no failures at larger cardinalities. The reproducible finite subset sieve in `verify_kneser.py`, using explicit lists of all ten stars and twelve simple five-cycles, checks every one of the \(2^{15}=32768\) masks, without any sampling. Subtracting the six counts from \((1+z)^{15}\) yields the polynomial.

**Step 4: minimum and inclusion-minimal sets.** The symmetric group \(S_5\) acts on the Kneser representation of \(P\). Its fifteen edges form five three-edge classes by omitted symbol. The orbits of two-edge sensor selections have sizes \(30,15,60\), and the successful orbit is exactly the \(15\)-element same-symbol orbit; no singleton works. There are \(\binom{15}{3}-130=325\) successful triples. Exactly \(5(3\cdot12+1)=185\) triples contain a successful pair, so the other \(140\) successful triples are inclusion-minimal. Every successful four-set containing a pair is nonminimal. For a four-set with four distinct omitted symbols, six complete \(S_5\)-orbits have sizes \(60,120,15,120,60,30\). Their numbers of successful three-subsets, taken on each orbit representative, are respectively \(1,3,4,2,0,4\), and the only orbit with none is the failed orbit. Therefore no successful four-set is minimal. To exclude sizes five through fifteen, the exhaustive all-mask certificate independently tests every successful subset for the success or failure of each one-element deletion and finds no additional inclusion-minimal sets. This is a complete finite check, not an inference from the absence of size-four minima alone.

**Step 5: exchange and TAR geometry.** Each same-symbol class yields three optimal pairs, with any two differing in one sensor, and no single replacement connects different classes. Hence the optimum token-jumping graph is \(5K_3\). At TAR budget \(2\), all optimum pairs are isolated because singletons fail. At budget \(3\), each three-sensor set containing an optimum pair lies with that pair in one of five components. For each symbol, the component contains its three optimum pairs, its one full three-class set and its \(3\cdot12=36\) sets with two class members and one sensor from outside: \(40\) states. The remaining \(140\) minimal triples are isolated. At budget \(4\), a minimal triple uses three distinct omitted-symbol classes; adding any unselected sensor from one of its represented classes creates a successful pair contained in the enlarged set. All other feasible sets of size at most four contain a successful pair or such a triple, because there are no minimal sets of size four. Distinct optimal pairs can be connected within budget four via their union (at most four sensors), by additions followed by removals. Thus the entire \(15+325+1305=1645\)-state graph is connected.

## Verification

The included `verify_kneser.py` constructs the Petersen graph as \(\mathrm{KG}(5,2)\). It exhausts all \(32768\) line-graph sensor subsets, comparing direct synchronous propagation, an edge–endpoint disjoint-set forest test, and the \(22\)-obstruction test. It enumerates all simple original cycles, calculates the full polynomial and both minimal-set cardinalities, and computes every component of the optimum token-jumping graph and the TAR graphs at budgets \(2,3,4\). It also constructs all \(120\) label permutations and checks complete orbits up to four sensors for the critical minimality exclusion; its exact output is `kneser_verification.json`.

The separately written `verify_petersen.cpp` constructs the graph through outer-cycle, inner-star and spoke edges (a different model). It directly tests power domination and original-graph acyclicity for all \(32768\) masks and reproduces the optimum, minimal triple and reconfiguration component counts. Compile with `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic verify_petersen.cpp -o checker` and run `./checker`; run the Python check with `python3 verify_kneser.py`. Both programs were run after extraction from the final packaged contents. The computational work is finite and exhaustive only for this explicit graph; the general endpoint–forest result rests on the proof above, not on these computations.

## Relationship to prior work

Benson and collaborators' initial October 2015 manuscript supplies ordinary power-domination definitions and relates the process to zero forcing. It establishes general lower bounds, not the endpoint–forest equivalence for line graphs or this exact Petersen optimizer census. Bjorkman and collaborators' work introduces TAR and token-jumping reconfiguration for power domination; it does not specialize to the line graph of the Petersen graph. Girish and collaborators discuss lifting monitoring configurations to line graphs and explicitly use the Petersen graph as a simulation example, but their six-line-vertex configuration and fault-tolerance statements neither enumerate the fifteen optimum pairs nor characterize the full set family. Kameri and collaborators examine inverse power domination and include the Petersen graph itself and several elementary line graph families, not minimum-power-dominating-set enumeration of \(L(P)\). Related previously published descriptions of power domination on \(L(K_n)\) concern a different underlying family and do not imply the Petersen counts; conversely, the endpoint–forest equivalence here applies more widely under its degree assumption. The historical priority of the stated joint structural and finite result is not independently certified.

## Limitations

The endpoint–forest criterion is proved only for original graphs of minimum degree at least two; graphs with leaves need separate analysis. The named reconfiguration census concerns the single fifteen-vertex Petersen line graph and does not establish a universal connectivity threshold for arbitrary line graphs. Exact finite enumeration certifies this labelled graph, not larger unknown graph families. An equivalent formulation in unindexed prior literature remains possible. No external independent scholarly audit has yet been performed.
