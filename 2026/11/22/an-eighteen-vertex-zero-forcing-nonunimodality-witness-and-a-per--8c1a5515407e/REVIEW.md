# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact 12-block quotient and its expansion are completely specified. Every zero forcing set may omit at most one member of a twin block. At most one missing member per block makes blue availability and unique-white-neighbor forces wholly determined by a 12-bit state. Both a direct 18-vertex exhaustive test and an independent 12-bit enumeration reproduce the full base polynomial; the latter gives mask counts and exact multiplicity slopes. The expanded block remains of size at least two, so its eligibility and all successful-mask statuses are independent of the expansion parameter. The affine formulas and two strict inequalities prove nonunimodality for every nonnegative integer parameter. The proof does not claim a global minimum order or infer an infinite theorem solely from finite samples.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

Newton–Brimkov (2026-11-08, arXiv:2611.11352v1) supplies a nonunimodal family and poses the smallest-order problem, but gives no order-18 witness. The AFTD note (2026-11-11) provides 20 vertices and mentions a 19-vertex, non-formalized witness; its reported results do not cover 18. Searches for ordinary zero-forcing nonunimodality, exact coefficients, 18-vertex examples, twin classes, and published-finding corpus mathematical records found no statement implying this bound or parameterized family. Databases cannot guarantee unique priority and there may be unknown independent records.

## Value — PASS

The smallest-order question is explicitly motivated in the primary 2026 paper. The finding lowers the presently identified upper bound from the independently reported 19 to 18 and supplies a fully reproducible 18-vertex coefficient census, together with a stable infinite construction and a structural reason for the affine degeneracies. The result is not just a numerical substitution in the earlier 81-vertex construction. It remains a bound, not a solution of the exact minimum-order problem.
