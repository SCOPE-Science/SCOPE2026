# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The weight-four even-vector graph is the five-regular graph, and the common-neighbor pair at a base vertex distinguishes its ten nonneighbors, proving the full affine-group bound is sharp. A flag stabilizer fixes each barycentric vertex, and different subflags have different rank sequences. Hence no two points of a closed flag simplex are identified: identified open subfaces would have the same rank sequence and thus be the same subface, with vertices fixed. The closed quotient cells are embedded. An independent reconstruction reproduced 392 labeled faces, 19,992 strict flags, 39 quotient cells and the incidences, and confirmed unique active cofaces and face closure throughout the 19 listed collapses to one vertex. There is also an analytic check: translate a member of a facet to zero; other members are pairwise-intersecting 2-subsets of five coordinates. The maximal types are a full star (five vertices counting zero) and a triangle on three coordinates (four counting zero). On the star facet {e_a+e_i}, coordinate permutations followed by translation by e_a+e_{p(a)} realize every permutation of its five vertices. On the triangle facet, the even translations on its three coordinates together with S3 realize S4. Each facet type therefore contributes one simplex of rank-signature cells. Their images meet on the full rank-{1,2,3} triangle: all triples are equivalent by translating one member to zero and mapping the resulting intersecting pair of 2-subsets, and the two rank-four face types are distinguished by extendibility to rank five. Thus the quotient is a 4-simplex and a 3-simplex glued along a common triangle, giving exactly the asserted f-vector and a collapse to a point (12 and 4 pairs to the shared triangle, then 3 pairs). This is the ordinary geometric quotient, not the labeled complex or a homotopy quotient.

The same full-group geometric quotient already had a rational homology computation. The reviewed contribution is its regular cell structure and elementary collapse, which resolve stronger integral and simple-homotopy questions. These are not established by the inspected results.
