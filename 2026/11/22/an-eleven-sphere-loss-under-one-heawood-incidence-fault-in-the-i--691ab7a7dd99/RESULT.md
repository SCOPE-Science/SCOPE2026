# An eleven-sphere loss under one Heawood incidence fault in the intrinsic Čech nerve

## Finding
Let \(H\) be the fourteen-vertex Heawood graph, the point–line incidence graph of the Fano plane. For any edge \(e\), remove \(e\), recompute the unit-edge intrinsic path metric on \(H-e\), and let \(\mathcal N(G,3)\) be the nerve of closed radius-\(3/2\) balls centered at vertices in the geometric realization of \(G\). Then
\[
|\mathcal N(H-e,3)|\simeq\bigvee^{37}S^2,
\qquad |\mathcal N(H,3)|\simeq\bigvee^{48}S^2.
\]
Moreover the natural inclusion of the nerve after edge deletion into the intact nerve is a wedge-summand inclusion up to homotopy: there is a homotopy equivalence of pairs
\[
\bigl(|\mathcal N(H,3)|,|\mathcal N(H-e,3)|\bigr)
\simeq\bigl(|\mathcal N(H-e,3)|\vee\bigvee^{11}S^2,|\mathcal N(H-e,3)|\bigr),
\]
understood as a homotopy equivalence under the included subspace. In particular the induced map on integral second homology is a primitive injection with free cokernel of rank eleven; no other positive-degree reduced integral homology occurs.

## Assumptions and scope
Label points \(p_0,\ldots,p_6\) and lines \(\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_6\), taking their indices modulo seven. Join \(\ell_i\) to \(p_i,p_{i+1},p_{i+3}\). All edges have length one, endpoints of metric balls are included, and the graph metric is recomputed after edge deletion. The nerve is not a graph-metric Vietoris–Rips complex and is not the open Lovász neighborhood complex. The assertion concerns one deleted graph edge, not an arbitrary set of faults.

## Proof
**1. A finite, exact facet description.** At integer scale three, a vertex-centered intrinsic Čech nerve is generated by the sets
\[
F_{uv}(G)=\{x\in V(G): \min(d_G(x,u),d_G(x,v))\le1\},\quad uv\in E(G).
\]
This is the odd-threshold ball-nerve formula: vertices in a common radius-\(3/2\) intersection have a witness at a vertex or an edge midpoint, and each vertex witness is contained in an adjacent edge-midpoint witness for a connected graph. This is also Lemma 2.2 of Adams–Shukla–Singh [1]. Since deleting an edge can only increase the shortest-path distances on the surviving graph, every face of \(\mathcal N(H-e,3)\) is a face of \(\mathcal N(H,3)\).

**2. Edge transitivity without a black-box symmetry test.** Translation \(p_i\mapsto p_{i+1}\), \(\ell_j\mapsto\ell_{j+1}\) preserves incidence. So does multiplication \(p_i\mapsto p_{2i}\), \(\ell_j\mapsto\ell_{2j+6}\), because the difference set \(\{0,1,3\}\) is stable under \(a\mapsto 2a-6\) modulo seven. Applying three multiplier powers and seven translations to the edge \(p_0\ell_0\) yields all twenty-one incidence edges. It therefore suffices to check deletion of \(p_0\ell_0\).

**3. Complete integral discrete-Morse certificates.** Index \(p_i\) by \(i\) and \(\ell_j\) by \(7+j\). Generate the full face poset from the above facets, and in successive vertex order \(0,1,\ldots,13\), match each still-unpaired nonempty face \(\sigma\) not containing the current vertex \(v\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\), whenever both remain. All pairs differ by exactly one vertex. The complete oriented Hasse-graph acyclicity checks in the supplied standalone verifier show:

- The intact nerve has 847 nonempty faces, 399 matching pairs, exactly one critical vertex and forty-eight critical triangles.
- The faulted nerve has 714 nonempty faces, 338 matching pairs, exactly one critical vertex and thirty-seven critical triangles.

By Forman's discrete-Morse theorem, each nerve is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with one zero-cell and precisely the stated number of two-cells. No one-cells remain, so all the two-cell attaching maps are null, establishing both sphere-wedge formulas over the integers.

**4. The primitive inclusion and pair splitting.** Apply the same vertex-order matching procedure to the relative face set \(\mathcal N(H,3)\setminus\mathcal N(H-p_0\ell_0,3)\), leaving all faces of the faulted nerve untouched. There are exactly 133 relative faces. The construction gives 61 pairs and precisely eleven critical relative triangles, with no critical relative cells in any other dimension. The verifier checks acyclicity both within the relative face poset and in the complete intact face poset with all base faces left unmatched. Relative discrete Morse theory therefore constructs the intact nerve from the faulted nerve, up to homotopy under the base, by attaching exactly eleven two-cells. Since the faulted nerve is simply connected by Step 3, every such attaching circle is nullhomotopic. Thus the pair has the claimed wedge-summand homotopy type. The induced second-homology map splits over \(\mathbb Z\) with cokernel \(\mathbb Z^{11}\).

## Verification
The dependency-free Python file `verification/verify.py` reconstructs all graph edges, verifies both all-pairs distance matrices independently with breadth-first search and Floyd–Warshall, proves the explicit two-generator edge transitivity, generates every facet and subface, constructs all three exact matchings, and topologically sorts their full oriented Hasse graphs. It independently computes the full mod-two boundary ranks of both nerves, including checks of dimensions and face vectors. To replay, run `python3 verification/verify.py` from the package root; successful output ends in `VERIFY_OK`. The three matching verifications are exhaustive finite certificates for this fixed graph, not sampled checks or extrapolations. Their integral topological conclusions follow from the acyclic Morse matchings, not from mod-two ranks alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams–Shukla–Singh [1] define the metric graph Čech filtration and prove its facet description, with principal homotopy calculations for hypercubes rather than this edge-deleted Fano incidence graph. The published calculation [2] identifies the intact Heawood nerve as a wedge of forty-eight two-spheres; that baseline is used here, not claimed as new. Neither result computes the recomputed-metric edge deletion or its primitive inclusion. The existing projective-plane incidence-graph vertex-fault calculations concern radius-two Vietoris–Rips *clique* complexes, not radius-\(3/2\) Čech nerves under graph-edge deletion [3]. In particular, a shared graph or similar sphere multiplicities do not yield the asserted relative homotopy splitting. Equivalently worded or unindexed work remains a bibliographic risk.

## Limitations
The conclusion covers one deleted edge in the Heawood graph at scale three, with unit original edge lengths. It does not give other Čech scales, simultaneous faults, a uniform projective-plane theorem, or a canonical symmetry-equivariant splitting. Independent external verification has not been performed. Access to the full text of [1] was limited; its public abstract and the independently recorded precise facet formula were examined, but no whole-document negative coverage assertion is based on the abstract alone.
