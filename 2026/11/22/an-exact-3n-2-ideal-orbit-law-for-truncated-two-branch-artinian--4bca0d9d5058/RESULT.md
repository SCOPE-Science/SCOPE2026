# An exact \(3N-2\) ideal-orbit law for truncated two-branch Artinian nodes

## Finding
For any field \(k\) and integer \(N\ge3\), consider the commutative unital Artinian algebra
\[
 A_N(k)=k[x,y]/(x^2,xy,y^N).
\]
Its full ideal set has **exactly \(3N-2\) orbits** under \(k\)-algebra automorphisms, independently of the field. More precisely, put \(K_s=(x,y^s)\) and \(G_{s,c}=(y^s+cx)\). The orbits are

1. The singleton ideals \(0\) and \(A_N\), and each \(K_s\), \(1\le s\le N-1\).
2. One orbit \(\{G_{1,c}:c\in k}\).
3. For every \(2\le s\le N-2\), a singleton \(G_{s,0}\), and a second orbit \(\{G_{s,c}:c\in k^\times}\).
4. The singleton \(G_{N-1,0}=(y^{N-1})\), and a second orbit consisting of \((x)\) and every \(G_{N-1,c}\) with \(c\ne0\).

The middle range is empty at \(N=3\). Over \(\mathbb F_q\), the number of individual ideals is \((N-1)q+N+2\), and the complete orbit-size multiset consists of \(2N-1\) orbits of length \(1\), \(N-3\) orbits of length \(q-1\), and two orbits of length \(q\). The numerical formulas are
\[
 \boxed{\#\big(\operatorname{Id}(A_N)/\operatorname{Aut}_k(A_N)\big)=3N-2,\quad |\operatorname{Id}(A_N)|=(N-1)q+N+2.}
\]
For \(q=2\), some orbit lengths coincide and their multiplicities are added.

## Assumptions and scope
Every ideal is a \(k\)-linear two-sided ideal (equivalently an ordinary ideal), including zero and the whole ring. All automorphisms fix \(k\) pointwise. No restriction on characteristic or algebraic closure is needed. The finite-field count holds for every prime power \(q\). The ideals are *embedded* ideals of this specified algebra; no classification of abstract quotients or arbitrary two-branch algebras is claimed.

## Proof
Write \(\mathfrak m=(x,y)=kx\oplus yk[y]/(y^N)\). Every proper ideal lies in \(\mathfrak m\), which is a module for multiplication by \(y\): one length-one Jordan block \(kx\) and one length-\(N-1\) block generated by \(y\). Projection of an ideal \(I\) onto the latter block is either zero or \((y^s)\), \(1\le s\le N-1\). Its intersection with the former block is either \(0\) or \(kx\).

If the projection vanishes, \(I\) is \(0\) or \((x)\). If it is \((y^s)\) and \(kx\subset I\), then \(I=K_s\). Otherwise the projection is injective, so \(I\) is the graph of a \(k[y]\)-module map from the cyclic module generated by \(y^s\) to the trivial module \(kx\). Every such map vanishes on \(y^{s+1}\) and is uniquely specified by \(y^s\mapsto cx\) for \(c\in k\); its graph is exactly the ideal \(G_{s,c}\). These forms are disjoint and exhaustive. Therefore for finite \(k=\mathbb F_q\) the \(N\) ideals \((x),K_1,\ldots,K_{N-1}\), plus \((N-1)q\) graphs, plus \(0,A_N\) give \((N-1)q+N+2\) total ideals.

Every \(k\)-algebra automorphism is of the form
\[
 \phi(x)=u x+v y^{N-1},\qquad
 \phi(y)=a y+\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}a_jy^j+b x,
 \qquad a,u\in k^\times,\quad v,b,a_j\in k.
\]
To see necessity, square-zero elements in \(\mathfrak m\) have no \(y\)-linear term when \(N\ge3\), since the leading square term would be a nonzero multiple of \(y^2\). The condition \(\phi(x)\phi(y)=0\) and invertibility on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) force \(\phi(x)\) into \(kx\oplus ky^{N-1}\) with \(u\ne0\), and \(\phi(y)\) to have nonzero coefficient \(a\) on \(y\). Conversely every displayed assignment preserves \(x^2=xy=y^N=0\) and has invertible cotangent action, hence is an automorphism by the finite-dimensional local Nakayama criterion. This includes characteristic two, as \(y^2\ne0\) whenever \(N\ge3\).

Such automorphisms preserve each \(K_s\). For \(s=1\), they send \(G_{1,c}\) to \(G_{1,(b+uc)/a}\), so the affine parameter is transitive. For \(2\le s\le N-2\), the terms \(b x\) and the off-branch socle shear do not affect \(\phi(y)^s\) modulo \(y^{s+1}\), so
\[
 \phi(G_{s,c})=G_{s,uc/a^s}.
\]
Here nonzero parameters form one orbit and zero is fixed. At the socle \(\operatorname{Soc}(A_N)=kx\oplus ky^{N-1}\), the action is upper triangular with respect to the basis \((x,y^{N-1})\): \(x\mapsto ux+vy^{N-1}\), \(y^{N-1}\mapsto a^{N-1}y^{N-1}\). Its projective-line action fixes only the line \(ky^{N-1}\) and is transitive on the other projective points, consisting of \((x)\) and all \(G_{N-1,c}\) with \(c\ne0\).

All asserted orbits are disjoint: the projection exponent \(s\), the presence of \(kx\) for \(K_s\), the vanishing of graph parameter for the middle range, and the distinguished socle line are invariant as stated. Summation gives
\[
 (N-1)+1+2(N-3)+2+2=3N-2
\]
orbits. Over \(\mathbb F_q\), the two size-\(q\) orbits are the affine graph orbit and the projective socle complement; the \(N-3\) middle nonzero graph orbits have size \(q-1\); all others have size one. This proves the precise size multiset.

## Verification
The included standalone Python checker independently enumerates **all reduced-row-echelon subspaces** of the \(N\)-dimensional radical for \(N=3,4,5,6\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(N=3,4,5\) over \(\mathbb F_3\), tests invariance under multiplication by \(y\), and calculates the orbit partition under explicit algebra automorphisms (the affine \(y\mapsto y+x\) shear, the \(x\mapsto x+y^{N-1}\) shear, and nonzero scalings). It checks the exact numbers of proper ideals and their predicted orbit count for every test. The captured output ends with `ARTIN_IDEAL_ORBIT_TESTS_PASSED`. The entire ring itself supplies one extra fixed orbit. These finite tests are checks, not a replacement for the field-uniform proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Homs and Winz (2023) study deformation cells of plane local Artinian ideals and justify studying ideal moduli, but do **not** classify automorphism orbits of the particular quotients here. Existing multi-branch fibre-product research gives a complete normal form and dimension count of individual ideals in \(A_N\), which the proof specializes and credits. Separate work gives the full automorphism group of mixed-length coordinate crosses. Those two ingredients are stronger than the corresponding individual lemmas here but do not themselves state, count, or separate the resulting **embedded-ideal automorphism orbits**. Another nearby paper determines endomorphism-variety components of two-branch Artinian nodes, not ideal orbits. Work on the different ring \(k[x,y]/(x,y)^3\) determines twelve ideal orbits but does not contain the unbounded family \(A_N\) with its \(3N-2\) law. These are statement-level distinctions, not a claim that shared objects or parameters prove novelty.

## Limitations
The theorem is a single asymmetric two-branch family, not a general multi-branch ideal-orbit formula. It concerns ordinary \(k\)-rational automorphism orbits, not the nonreduced automorphism group scheme or quotient-stack geometry. In finite fields, the orbit length \(q-1\) becomes one for \(q=2\), as explicitly accounted for. General module-orbit theory or unpublished earlier work could imply the result; historical priority is not independently certified. There has been no independent mathematical audit.
