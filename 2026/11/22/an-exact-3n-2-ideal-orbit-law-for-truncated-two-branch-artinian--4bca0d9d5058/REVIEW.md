# Review of the ideal-orbit classification

## Correctness — PASS
The field-independent classification reconstructs all ideals using the radical module and then computes the complete action of the triangular automorphism normal form. Projection exponent, graph parameter, and socle projective line distinguish every asserted case. The finite-field counts sum correctly and the independent RREF enumeration over seven small field/length combinations matches the theorem. The proof applies uniformly to all fields; finite checks do not by themselves establish infinity.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources
Homs–Winz (arXiv:2309.06871, first public 2023-09-13) treats Hilbert/Betti strata and local Gröbner cells of plane ideals, not embedded ideal orbits of this finite quotient. The closest prior results separately classify all ideals of a multi-branch fibre product and the automorphism group of such an algebra, but neither computes the action-orbit partition. A distinct six-dimensional short local algebra has twelve ideal orbits, with different multiplication. The exact \(3N-2\) orbit law, representatives, and orbit-size distribution were not located in the checked literature or mathematical records. An unindexed general orbit theorem could subsume the result; an independent priority audit has not been performed.

## Value — PASS
The quotient by algebra automorphisms of an ideal parameter space is a natural moduli problem, not a presentation-dependent finite count. For each \(N\), this gives every embedded ideal-equivalence class and its population, explaining how the growing \(q\)-parameter families collapse to only \(3N-2\) classes. The distinction between affine graph parameters and projective socle lines, and the exceptional characteristic-two size-one collapse, is structural. The contribution is the complete orbit moduli for a non-Gorenstein Artinian family, not a routine recomputation of the already known number of ideals.

## Scientific boundaries
No extension to arbitrary mixed-length fibre products or claims about nonreduced group schemes are made. Exact historical priority, particularly against general module-embedding orbit theory, remains subject to independent verification.
