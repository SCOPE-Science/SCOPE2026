# An exact basin-width law and two sink-density transitions in an incommensurate sine map

## Finding
For every real gain \(\kappa>0\), set
\[
F_\kappa(x)=x+\kappa\sin(2\pi x)\sin(2x),\qquad x\in\mathbb R.
\]
This is the natural gain extension of the product-sine map studied at \(\kappa=0.7\) by Liu, Sun and Wang (2024, their equation (10)). We establish one unified spatial stability theorem.

**Exact weak-gain global dynamics and basin-width distribution.** The map is strictly increasing if and only if
\[
0<\kappa\le\frac1{2\pi}.
\]
For every gain in this interval and every initial \(x_0\in\mathbb R\), its forward trajectory converges to a fixed point. Aside from the one-sided neutral fixed point at zero, each attracting equilibrium is a hyperbolic sink and its full basin is the open interval between the neighboring fixed points. If each sink in \([-R,R]\) is assigned the length of its full basin, the empirical basin-width distribution converges weakly as \(R\to\infty\) to
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac1{\pi+1}\,\delta_{1/2}
+\frac{\pi-2}{\pi+1}\,\delta_1
+\frac4{\pi+1}\,\mathbf1_{(1/2,1)}(w)\,dw.
\quad}
\]
Thus this deterministic map has a nontrivial mixture of two atoms and a continuous uniform component in its basin sizes, independent of the gain throughout the entire increasing regime. The solitary neutral equilibrium at zero has a one-sided basin; it is not included in the empirical sink distribution.

**All-gain stability spectrum and sharp sink-density transitions.** Every fixed point lies in the two incommensurate lattices
\[
E=\tfrac12\mathbb Z\cup\tfrac\pi2\mathbb Z,
\]
whose only intersection is zero. The normalized empirical distribution of the multipliers \(F'_\kappa(p)\) over \(p\in E\cap[-R,R]\) converges weakly, as \(R\to\infty\), to the mixture
\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac\pi{\pi+1}\,\mathrm{Arc}(1,2\pi\kappa)
+\frac1{\pi+1}\,\mathrm{Arc}(1,2\kappa).
\quad}
\]
Here \(\mathrm{Arc}(c,a)\) is the probability law with density \(\mathbf1_{|s-c|<a}/[\pi\sqrt{a^2-(s-c)^2}]\), with \(a>0\). Consequently, the number \(N_\kappa(R)\) of hyperbolic fixed-point sinks with \(|p|\le R\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{\quad
\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{N_\kappa(R)}{2R}
= D(\kappa):=\frac2\pi\arcsin\min\!\left(1,\frac1{\pi\kappa}\right)
+\frac2{\pi^2}\arcsin\min\!\left(1,\frac1\kappa\right).
\quad}
\]
The density is exactly \(1+1/\pi\) for \(0<\kappa\le1/\pi\). At the first threshold it develops the nonanalytic law
\[
D(\kappa)=1+\frac1\pi-\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi}\sqrt{\pi\kappa-1}
+O((\pi\kappa-1)^{3/2})\quad(\kappa\downarrow1/\pi\text{ from above}).
\]
A second square-root singularity occurs at \(\kappa=1\):
\[
D(\kappa)=D(1)-\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi^2}\sqrt{\kappa-1}+O(\kappa-1)
\quad(\kappa\downarrow1\text{ from above}).
\]
For large gains, \(D(\kappa)=4/(\pi^2\kappa)+O(\kappa^{-3})\). For the published gain \(\kappa=7/10\), the exact density is
\[
D(7/10)=\frac2\pi\arcsin\frac{10}{7\pi}+\frac1\pi
=0.6188372299438\ldots\quad\text{sinks per unit real length}.
\]
There are \(2+2/\pi\) fixed points per unit length, so about \(23.4709\%\) of them are hyperbolic sinks at this gain.

The first loss of global order, at gain \(1/(2\pi)\), therefore precedes the first decrease in sink density, at \(1/\pi\). At every positive gain, sink multipliers approach \(+1\) along infinitely many distinct sinks, so their local contraction factors are never uniformly bounded away from one. At the first threshold no point of the half-integer lattice is flip-neutral, and at the second threshold no point of the \(\pi/2\)-lattice is flip-neutral: each singularity is generated by the accumulation of thresholds within the newly affected lattice.

## Assumptions and scope
The map acts on the entire real line, with no modular identification and exact real arithmetic. Gains are strictly positive. A **hyperbolic sink** is a fixed point \(p\) with \(|F'_\kappa(p)|<1\); the neutral fixed point \(p=0\), where \(F'_\kappa(0)=1\), is excluded from sink counts. Basin width means the Lebesgue length of \(\{x:F_\kappa^n(x)\to p\}\), which is finite for every sink in the increasing regime. The empirical width measure assigns equal mass to each sink whose *location* lies in \([-R,R]\), including the full basin even if part of it lies outside the window. It is not a distribution obtained by sampling initial states uniformly. The limiting spectral and sink-density laws hold for all gains; basin-width and global-convergence claims require \(\kappa\le1/(2\pi)\). No claim is made about all higher-period attractors outside this range.

## Proof
**1. Fixed-point set, multipliers, and the one neutral exception.** The fixed-point equation is \(\sin(2\pi x)\sin(2x)=0\), whence
\[
E=\{k/2:k\in\mathbb Z\}\cup\{j\pi/2:j\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Irrationality of \(\pi\) makes their only overlap zero. All other zeros are simple. Direct differentiation gives, for nonzero integers \(k,j\),
\[
F'_\kappa(k/2)=1+2\pi\kappa(-1)^k\sin k
=1+2\pi\kappa\sin((1+\pi)k),
\]
\[
F'_\kappa(j\pi/2)=1+2\kappa(-1)^j\sin(\pi^2j)
=1+2\kappa\sin((\pi^2+\pi)j).
\]
At zero the multiplier is one and \(F_\kappa(x)-x=4\pi\kappa x^2+O(x^4)\), giving attraction from the negative side and repulsion from the positive side in a sufficiently small neighborhood.

**2. The sharp increasing threshold.** Put \(G(x)=\sin(2\pi x)\sin(2x)\), \(u=2\pi x\), \(v=2x\). Then
\[
G'(x)=(2\pi\cos u,2\sin u)\cdot(\sin v,\cos v),
\qquad |G'(x)|\le2\pi.
\]
Equality requires \(u\in\pi\mathbb Z\) and \(v\in\pi/2+\pi\mathbb Z\), which cannot hold at the same real \(x\), because \(\pi\) is irrational. Thus \(F'_\kappa(x)>0\) everywhere when \(\kappa\le1/(2\pi)\). Conversely the linear orbit \(x\mapsto(2\pi x,2x)\bmod2\pi\) is dense in the two-torus (the frequency ratio is irrational), so \(\inf_{x\in\mathbb R}G'(x)=-2\pi\). For any \(\kappa>1/(2\pi)\), some point therefore has \(F'_\kappa(x)<0\), and \(F_\kappa\) is not increasing. This proves the exact threshold.

For an increasing \(F_\kappa\), every real point lies between two adjacent half-integer fixed points. The corresponding interval is invariant because its endpoints are fixed. The sequence of iterates is monotone: if the first iterate is above its starting point, all later iterates are nondecreasing, and similarly if it is below. It is bounded in that invariant interval, hence converges, and continuity makes the limit a fixed point. At a simple zero, the sign of \(F_\kappa(x)-x\) switches. The simple fixed points therefore alternate in their local attracting or repelling direction on either side of the sole exceptional double zero. In the increasing regime an inward crossing has multiplier in \((0,1)\), hence is a hyperbolic sink; the complete basin of each such sink is exactly the interval between its two nearest fixed-point neighbors.

**3. Spatial multiplier law and sink counts.** Irrationality of \((1+\pi)/(2\pi)\) and \((\pi^2+\pi)/(2\pi)\) implies that, separately, the sequences \(k(1+\pi)\) and \(j(\pi^2+\pi)\) are equidistributed modulo \(2\pi\). Weyl's theorem applied to any continuous function of the multiplier gives an arcsine law in each family. The two lattices have spatial densities \(2\) and \(2/\pi\). Consequently their normalized union has the stated arcsine mixture with weights \(\pi/(\pi+1)\) and \(1/(\pi+1)\).

For a multiplier \(1+a\sin\Theta\), with \(\Theta\) uniform on \([0,2\pi)\), the strict-sink event is \(-2/a<\sin\Theta<0\), of probability \(\pi^{-1}\arcsin(\min(1,2/a))\). The event boundary has zero angular measure, so equidistribution applies to the indicator too. Multiplication by the lattice densities gives the displayed formula for \(D(\kappa)\), including gains where an isolated individual multiplier might equal \(-1\). Taylor expansion of \(\arcsin(1/(1+\varepsilon))=\pi/2-\sqrt{2\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon^{3/2})\) yields the two singularity coefficients. The small-argument expansion of \(\arcsin\) gives the large-gain law.

At gain \(\kappa=1/\pi\), flip neutrality in the first lattice would require \(\sin((1+\pi)k)=-1\), which would force an integer \(k\) to equal a nonzero half-integer multiple of \(\pi\); impossible. At gain \(\kappa=1\), flip neutrality in the second lattice would require \(\sin((\pi^2+\pi)j)=-1\), which would imply an integer plus one-half equals \(j(\pi+1)\), impossible for nonzero integer \(j\). No assertion is made about possible isolated neutral points in the other lattice at this second threshold. Irrational rotation also produces arbitrarily small negative sines for infinitely many points of each lattice, proving the asserted absence of a uniform contraction factor over all sinks.

**4. Complete weak-gain basin-width law.** Rescale space by \(t=2x\), so the zero set becomes \(\mathbb Z\cup\pi\mathbb Z\). Each point \(j\pi\), \(j\ne0\), lies strictly inside a unique integer unit interval \((m_j,m_j+1)\), with fractional offset \(u_j=\{j\pi\}\in(0,1)\). Because successive such points are separated by \(\pi>3\), no two affected integer intervals are adjacent, and neither integer endpoint of a given affected interval is adjacent to another \(\pi\)-lattice zero.

At \(j\pi\), the multiplier sign correction has the same sign as
\[
(-1)^j\sin(\pi^2j)
=(-1)^{j+m_j}\sin(\pi u_j).
\]
Since \(\sin(\pi u_j)>0\), it is a sink exactly when \(j+m_j\) is odd. Restrict \(j\) to either parity class. Irrational rotation by \(2\pi\) modulo \(2\) makes \(j\pi\bmod2\) equidistributed on \([0,2)\) on that class. The parity condition on \(m_j\) selects one of the unit halves \([0,1)\) or \([1,2)\), within which \(u_j\) remains uniform on \((0,1)\). Therefore exactly half the \(\pi\)-lattice zeros are sinks and half are repellers, and the fractional offsets in either class are uniformly distributed.

When \(j\pi\) is a sink, its adjacent integer zeros are repellers and its basin has width \(1\) in the \(t\) coordinate. When \(j\pi\) is a repeller, its neighboring integers \(m_j,m_j+1\) are sinks, whose basin widths are \(1+u_j\) and \(2-u_j\) in the \(t\) coordinate. Every remaining integer-lattice sink has two integer-lattice repeller neighbors, hence basin width \(2\) in \(t\). The latter sink density is \(1/2-1/\pi\) per unit \(t\), because integer-lattice sinks have density \(1/2\) and the pairs flanking repelling \(\pi\)-lattice points have density \(1/\pi\). The \(\pi\)-lattice sinks have density \(1/(2\pi)\), and the integer sinks flanking repelling \(\pi\)-lattice points have density \(1/\pi\). Their sum is \(1/2+1/(2\pi)\), the total sink density per unit \(t\).

Normalize these three densities and divide widths by two to return to \(x\). This produces, respectively, the atom \(1/(\pi+1)\) at width \(1/2\), a uniform continuous component of mass \(2/(\pi+1)\) on \((1/2,1)\), and the atom \((\pi-2)/(\pi+1)\) at width \(1\). Boundary effects and the single exceptional fixed point at zero have zero limiting frequency. This proves the claimed weak convergence.

## Verification
The standalone `check.py` (Python 3 with NumPy) evaluates both multiplier formulas at sample integer-lattice points, compares spatial sink counts in a window \([-50000,50000]\) for seven gains with the exact density formula, tests both square-root coefficients and the increasing threshold numerically, and checks the exact weak-gain basin-width mixture using a sorted union of the two zero lattices. All finite diagnostics passed. The mathematical proof is in Steps 1–4; finite counts, phase sampling and numerical error thresholds do not certify the infinite limits or the all-real global convergence claim.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu, Sun and Wang (2024), DOI 10.1063/5.0219361, introduce the fixed-gain product-sine example \(\kappa=0.7\) in their equation (10) and demonstrate heterogeneous multistability numerically (Section V, Figure 19), mentioning stable fixed points and long transients. Their full-text analysis does not derive a basin-width probability law, a spatial stability-spectrum law, the gain-dependent exact sink density, or its two square-root transitions. Martins and Gallas (2008), DOI 10.1142/S0218127408021294, study counts of coexisting attractors in a *two-dimensional dissipative kicked rotor* and motivate quantitative prevalence, but their map and state-space parameter asymptotic are different. A public 2026 result titled *Uniform phase-independent fixed-point density in a two-frequency sine map* concerns an **additive two-sine map** \(T_A(x)=A(\sin(\omega_1x)+\sin(\omega_2x))\), with fixed-point counts proportional to the amplitude \(A\) and a vanishing fraction of nonrepelling points as \(A\to\infty\). It neither covers nor implies our product-sine translation map's nonzero spatial sink-density law or basin mixture. Weyl equidistribution and monotone interval dynamics are classical ingredients, not new theorems; the contribution is the exact combined stability and basin-statistical classification for this incommensurate system.

## Limitations
The global-convergence and basin-width laws cover only the exactly identified increasing range; no global basin classification is claimed for \(\kappa>1/(2\pi)\). Beyond that threshold, the sink formula counts **hyperbolic fixed points only**, not attracting cycles, chaotic attractors, physical measures or basin-weighted probabilities. A spatial frequency is not the probability that a uniformly sampled initial state converges to a sink. The limiting distributions have no effective finite-window error rate; slow irrational rotations can create long finite-window discrepancies. The singularity coefficients arise from dense families of multiplier thresholds, not from a single local fold or flip bifurcation. Equivalent unindexed literature and general almost-periodic zero-statistics results remain publication-priority risks. No independent mathematical verification has been performed.
