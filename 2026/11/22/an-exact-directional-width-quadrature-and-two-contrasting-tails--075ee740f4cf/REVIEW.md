# Scientific review — tricylinder directional widths

## Correctness (PASS)
The normal-cone proof of the full support function treats both the two-active-cylinder regime and the three-active-constraint vertex regime; the branches agree at the triangle-inequality boundary. The exact CDF partitions the sphere into 48 congruent chambers and integrates its monotonically inverted support value. The high-width interval follows from eight disjoint diagonal spherical caps, and the minimum-width logarithm from six axial product neighborhoods, with a controlled remainder away from the axes. The independent numerical optimizer and CDF checker replayed 251 assertions, but these finite tests do not replace the proof.

## Originality (PASS; historical-priority risks remain)
Angell–Moore (1987; DOI 10.1107/S0108767387099501) gives volume, surface, and orthographic geometry of intersecting cylinders, not an exact random-width law. Published results 2026/10/18/SCOPE-exact-twelve-sector-sections-and-cubic-directional-anisotropy-of--428486a75562 and 2026/11/1/SCOPE-exact-central-section-area-and-six-eight-arc-law-of-the-orthogon--3c351c7fe62f give tricylinder central sections and arc counts, distinct from support planes. Finch (arXiv:1110.0671v1) and 2026/10/11/SCOPE-exact-directional-width-law-of-the-cube-and-its-first-spherical--c1e6aa54ecb2 give regular-polyhedron/cube widths; only the generic cap-integration method overlaps, not the pointwise tricylinder support formula, quadrature, or six-axis logarithm. The 1974 Moore article was not available in full; comprehensive historical uniqueness is not asserted. Previously studied bicylinder widths concern a different convex intersection.

## Value (PASS)
This computes a natural but previously unreported exact width statistic for the classical equal-radius orthogonal tricylinder, revealing a linear upper tail and logarithmically enhanced low-width probability governed by qualitatively distinct classes of extremizing directions. The global support formula itself is useful for projection and random-orientation geometry. No need for an invented downstream theorem is assumed.

## Boundaries and verification
Equal-radius, orthogonal, concurrent infinite-cylinder intersections and isotropic directions only. No elementary closed antiderivative for the entire CDF, no unequal radii, and no general higher-dimensional assertion. Numerical tests support but cannot certify the entire continuum. Independent external mathematical validation and historical-priority audit remain outstanding.
