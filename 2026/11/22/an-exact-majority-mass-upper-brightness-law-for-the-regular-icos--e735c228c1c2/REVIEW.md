# Scientific assessment of the icosahedral upper brightness law

## Correctness (C): PASS
The object is the edge-length-two regular icosahedron, the probability measure is normalized area on the viewing sphere, and brightness is orthogonal projection area. The proof reduces Cauchy's exact facet-normal identity to ten antipodal normal generators. A separate standard-library certificate enumerates all twenty true supporting facets, all 1024 sign sums in exact \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\) arithmetic, the complete eighteen-level norm spectrum, and the twenty exact global maximizers; the analytic steps then convert the top two levels into disjoint spherical caps. Exact angular inequalities give both disjointness and containment in six-silhouette sign chambers. The spherical-cap area calculation gives the claimed law for every threshold, not just sampled thresholds. The 65,536-point quadrature is a diagnostic only. The equality endpoint may contain isolated additional sign-sum support directions; they have zero measure, so the probability statement is unaffected.

## Originality (O): PASS, with explicit historical risk
The same icosahedron's published silhouette-count law is a three-atom law for the number of shadow vertices; it does not determine the area law. The full published first area-weighted moment calculation resolves three conditional means but explicitly leaves conditional brightness distributions open. The published Platonic variance table gives only a second moment. An icosahedral upper-tail result for **diameter** uses a different projection statistic; the previously recorded dodecahedral high-brightness law concerns a different ten-generator spectrum and twelve, not twenty, cap centers. None of these inspected statements, or their combination, implies the present numerical top-two icosahedral sign levels and the exact majority-mass constant-density interval without additional analysis. Search evidence is not treated as definitive novelty proof. The 2026 general polyhedral-density study was identifiable by abstract but not readable in full; a possible broader geometric framework is a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
Projected area is a canonical convex-geometric invariant rather than an arbitrary coordinate statistic. The exact upper-band law covers a majority (about 55.94%) of viewing directions, identifies its sharp algebraic threshold and exact constant density, and marks every extreme event as a hexagonal shadow from one of twenty independent face directions. These facts are not first- or second-moment consequences and supply a useful stringent benchmark for geometric tomography, directional brightness simulation, and type-conditioned projection questions. The finite zonotope sign-spectrum certificate is required to make the threshold global. This is not represented as a universal projection theorem.
