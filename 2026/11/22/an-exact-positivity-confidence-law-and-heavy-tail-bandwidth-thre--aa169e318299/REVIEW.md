## Value — PASS
Unconstrained Gaussian-process quadrature can generate negative integration weights, potentially destroying positivity on nonnegative data. For the natural smallest nontrivial rule—two independently drawn Gaussian nodes—the result supplies an **exact, dimension-dependent reliability calculation** and, by inverse beta quantiles, an exact positivity-confidence tuning rule. Its sharp polynomial-tail exponent and failure of the mean critical bandwidth in dimension one quantify rare, extreme node configurations; the dimension-six 95% cutoff is a nontrivial finite parameter boundary rather than a routine numerical instance. These quantitative statements address a gap explicitly left to numerical exploration by the closest primary literature, without claiming an error-optimal bandwidth or a general theorem for arbitrary numbers of nodes.
