# Review of the binary complement-scattered-factor ambiguity law

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim fixes the alphabet, deleted scattered pattern, multiplicity convention, and uniform source. The previously published run-pair theorem reduces the unique-residual count to \(\binom{T+1}{2}-A\), where \(T\) and \(A\) count contiguous `01` and `0101`. A unique maximal-run decomposition gives the complete joint generating function by an explicit two-state transfer; differentiating it twice gives exact moments. The uniform one-dependent pattern-count central limit theorem and control of lower-order terms give the stated Gaussian limit. Two standalone, genuinely different exact enumeration methods agree through length 26, and direct deletion-output enumeration agrees through length 12. A finite check alone would not establish the theorem; the formal-series and probabilistic arguments establish the unbounded cases.

**Originality — PASS, subject to priority uncertainty.** Adamson, Fleischmann and Huch (arXiv:2603.20790v1) introduce the set and develop general algorithms, but do not give this random-source distribution or variance. The public result “A run-pair formula for two-letter complement scattered factors” (8 November 2026) contains the exact cardinality identity and the uniform-source mean, both explicitly credited. Its full result was inspected: it does not give the complete joint \((T,A)\) law, cubic variance, or normal limit. Other indexed results on unrestricted two-deletion balls, alternating hosts, and unrelated motif counts concern different objects or distributions. A preexisting general pattern-matching transfer-matrix framework can derive the law when specialized, but no previously verified explicit equivalent or stronger covering statement was found. Unindexed equivalent work remains possible; this is not a historical priority certificate.

**Value — PASS.** Counting the possible outputs of a fixed *ordered* deletion pattern gives the ambiguity faced by a reader who knows which error pattern occurred, unlike an unrestricted two-deletion ball. The exact distribution distinguishes words with identical mean ambiguity but different output fan-out. The sharp variance has order \(n^3\), and the central limit identifies the natural \(n^{3/2}\) fluctuation scale; these are useful precise probabilistic characteristics of a newly studied string operation, not an arbitrary finite parameter. The earlier mean alone does not imply the higher moments or the limit theorem.

**Scope and risk.** The model uses uniformly sampled binary words and deletion of prescribed scattered `01`; it is not a theorem for all patterns or nonuniform sources. Competing unindexed results in shuffle languages or joint motif statistics may exist. The established pattern-count limit theorem is invoked with hypotheses verified. No independent external validation or publication-priority certification has been performed.
