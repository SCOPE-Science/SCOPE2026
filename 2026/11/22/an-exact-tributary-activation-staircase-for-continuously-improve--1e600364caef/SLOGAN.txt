In an idealized branching river, continuously improving fish-passage barriers yields an exact staircase: weaker tributaries enter the optimal restoration plan at strictly ordered, explicit budget thresholds, and after diversification the shared downstream barrier always receives more effort than any tributary barrier. For geometrically declining tributary habitat quality, the number of funded tributaries grows only like \(\sqrt{2B/(-\log q)}\) as effort \(B\) increases.
