# An explicit primitive section and quadratic constant-field descent for a characteristic-five elliptic surface

## Finding
Let \(k_0=\mathbb F_5\), let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5\), and let \(E/k_0(t)\) be the generic fiber of the relatively minimal rational elliptic surface
\[
 E:\quad y^2=4x^3+3t(t+2)^3x+3t^4(t+2)^2.
\]
Choose \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_{25}\) with \(\alpha^2=2\), and define
\[
 P=(t(t+2),\alpha t(t+2))\in E(\mathbb F_{25}(t)).
\]
Then the **full geometric Mordell–Weil group**, with its canonical height pairing, is
\[
 E(k(t))=\mathbb Z P,\qquad \langle P,P\rangle=\frac1{30}.
\]
The arithmetic Frobenius of \(\mathbb F_5\) sends \(P\) to \(-P\), hence for every integer \(m\ge1\),
\[
 E(\mathbb F_{5^m}(t))=\begin{cases}
 \{O\},&m\text{ odd},\\
 \mathbb Z P,&m\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Equivalently, the constant quadratic twist \(2Y^2=4x^3+3t(t+2)^3x+3t^4(t+2)^2\) has full \(\mathbb F_5(t)\)-Mordell–Weil group generated by \((x,Y)=(t(t+2),t(t+2))\). The exact generator and constant-field descent are the new assertions; the geometric rank and abstract lattice type are consistent with known general classification and an earlier related geometric-height calculation.

## Assumptions and scope
All coefficients and equalities in the displayed Weierstrass equation are interpreted in characteristic \(5\). The ground field \(k\) is the algebraic closure of the prime field, not an algebraic closure of \(k_0(t)\). The group \(E(k(t))\) denotes rational sections after extension of constants only. The zero section is denoted \(O\). The surface is the rational elliptic surface of Burek–Chmiel–Cynk–Oczko, with Kodaira fibers \(\mathrm{III}\), \(\mathrm{IV}\), \(\mathrm I_5\) over \(t=0,3,\infty\), respectively. There are no other singular fibers. Its minimal regular model is used for all section intersections.

## Proof
**1. Exact section and local models.** Put \(u=t+2\), \(A=3tu^3\), \(B=3t^4u^2\). In \(\mathbb F_5[t]\), direct expansion gives
\[
 4[t u]^3+A[t u]+B=2t^2u^2.
\]
Since \(\alpha^2=2\), this proves that \(P\) belongs to \(E(\mathbb F_{25}(t))\). The discriminant in the indicated Weierstrass normalization is
\[
 -A^3-27B^2=t^3(t+2)^4.
\]
As a separate consistency check, \(j(E)=4(t+2)^5\), which is nonconstant, so the elliptic surface is nonisotrivial. The indicated equation and singular-fiber types were established in the initiating source.

**2. The section meets the nonidentity components of the additive fibers.** At \(t=0\), the lowest-degree part of the local Weierstrass surface equation at \((t,x,y)=(0,0,0)\) is \(y^2-4tx\). This is a nondegenerate quadratic surface singularity; its resolution introduces the nonidentity component of the \(\mathrm{III}\) fiber. The section has \(x\sim 2t\), \(y\sim2\alpha t\), and therefore passes through the singular point and meets that exceptional component. Its local height correction is \(1/2\).

At \(t=3=-2\), put \(s=t+2\). The leading local equation is \(y^2-3s^2-4x^3=0\); it is an \(A_2\) surface singularity whose two exceptional curves are the nonidentity components of the \(\mathrm{IV}\) fiber. Along \(P\), \(x\sim3s\) and \(y\sim3\alpha s\), and \((3\alpha)^2=3\) in characteristic five. The tangent lies on exactly one branch of the reduced tangent cone, so the strict transform of the section meets a nonidentity component, contributing \(2/3\).

**3. The multiplicative fiber.** At infinity, with \(s=1/t\) and regular Weierstrass coordinates \(X=s^2x\), \(Y=s^3y\), the section is
\[
 X=1+2s,\qquad Y=\alpha s(1+2s).
\]
The fiber at \(s=0\) is \(Y^2=4X^3+3X+3\), whose singular point is \((X,Y)=(1,0)\). Consequently \(P\) passes through the Weierstrass singularity, so its strict transform meets a nonidentity component of the minimal \(\mathrm I_5\) fiber. The possible correction terms are \(4/5\) (component index \(1\) or \(4\)) and \(6/5\) (index \(2\) or \(3\)). The section \(P\) is disjoint from \(O\): it has finite affine coordinates for every finite \(t\), and \((X,Y)=(1,0)\), distinct from \(O\), at infinity. Shioda's height formula gives
\[
 \langle P,P\rangle=2\chi(\mathcal O_S)+2(P\cdot O)-\frac12-\frac23-\operatorname{contr}_{\mathrm I_5}(P)
 =2-\frac12-\frac23-\operatorname{contr}_{\mathrm I_5}(P).
\]
The \(6/5\) option yields a negative height and is impossible because the height is nonnegative on sections of a nonisotrivial elliptic surface. Thus the correction is \(4/5\), and
\[
 \langle P,P\rangle=2-\frac12-\frac23-\frac45=\frac1{30}.
\]

**4. Primitivity and absence of torsion.** The Néron–Severi lattice of a rational elliptic surface over \(k\) has rank ten and absolute determinant one. The reducible fibers have root lattices \(A_1\), \(A_2\), and \(A_4\), of ranks \(1,2,4\) and determinants \(2,3,5\). Shioda–Tate therefore gives geometric Mordell–Weil rank \(10-2-(1+2+4)=1\). The trivial lattice has absolute determinant \(30\). If \(T\) is the geometric Mordell–Weil torsion subgroup, the discriminant formula gives the height of a primitive free generator \(G\) as \(|T|^2/30\). Since \(P=nG+T_0\) for some nonzero integer \(n\) and torsion point \(T_0\), comparison with the computed height of \(P\) gives \(n^2|T|^2=1\). Thus \(T=0\), \(|n|=1\), and \(E(k(t))=\mathbb Z P\).

**5. Frobenius descent and the twist.** Since \(2\) is a nonsquare in \(\mathbb F_5\), the root \(\alpha\) lies in \(\mathbb F_{25}\setminus\mathbb F_5\) and \(\alpha^5=-\alpha\). Frobenius fixes \(x(P)\) and negates \(y(P)\), so it acts as \(-1\) on the entire torsion-free cyclic group. The subgroup fixed by its \(m\)-th power is zero when \(m\) is odd and all of \(\mathbb ZP\) when \(m\) is even. The twist statement follows from the isomorphism over \(\mathbb F_{25}(t)\) sending \((x,Y)\) to \((x,\alpha Y)\); the twisted Frobenius action is the negative of the original action.

## Verification
The standalone script `check.py` (included with this note) performs exact arithmetic in \(\mathbb F_5[t]\), confirming the section identity, discriminant, \(j\)-formula, additive-fiber tangent relations, infinity-node equation and all candidate height values. It also checks the finite lattice and Frobenius arithmetic. Its tests passed. The finite computation is a check of identities, **not** a substitute for the geometric component and Shioda–Tate arguments in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Burek–Chmiel–Cynk–Oczko (arXiv:2611.15943v1, first public November 11, 2026) supply this characteristic-five surface and its three Kodaira fibers in the construction of a non-liftable projective Calabi–Yau threefold. Oguiso–Shioda's classification and standard Shioda height theory describe geometric Mordell–Weil lattices from fiber root lattices, but do not specify the coordinates of the primitive section or arithmetic Frobenius for this particular equation. The contribution here is precisely that explicit primitive section and the resulting classification over **every finite constant field**, including the quadratic twist. The geometric abstract rank, height determinant and other generic classification consequences are credited to existing theory, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The statement concerns the Mordell–Weil group over the constant-field function fields \(\mathbb F_{5^m}(t)\) and \(k(t)\), not sections after arbitrary nonconstant extensions of \(k(t)\). It does not make claims about arithmetic ranks after base changes of the parameter curve, about Tate–Shafarevich groups, or about the cohomology or lifting obstruction of the associated threefold. Although the explicit arithmetic assertion was not identified in the inspected literature, older and unindexed computations remain possible. A related earlier preprint about crepant resolutions (arXiv:2609.12522v1) was available as an abstract but its full text could not be inspected in this investigation; it is retained as a priority risk.
