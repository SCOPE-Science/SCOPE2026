# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The six-dimensional spline space has the explicit monomial plus two truncated-power basis. Exactness reduces to six moments. Positivity and all-node location follow from rational factor inequalities with \(q'(s)=4(3s-4)^2\), \(q(1/2)=-11/2\), a positive discriminant, and a positive two-node residual moment variance. A reflection plus a residual-variance/root-straddling contradiction eliminates the opposite node pattern. Two nodes are impossible by their forced truncated-power weights summing to \(16/27\); knot-locked three-node quadrature forces the central knot. Exact differentiation of the explicit root supplies the distinct one-sided second derivatives. The packaged checker independently evaluates 1,130 symbolic/rational/high-precision assertions. The proof does not rest on the finite sample.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

Ait-Haddou, Bartoň and Calo (arXiv:1410.7196, 2014) treat symmetrically stretched knot sequences and include the symmetric baseline. Bartoň and Calo (arXiv:1505.04391, 2015) treat general knot deformations by numerical homotopy and show changing nodal patterns, not an explicit all-asymmetry two-cell formula or a \(C^1\)-but-not-\(C^2\) knot crossing. Related indexing and result-level searches did not expose an equivalent specialization. Residual priority risk: older spline Gaussian-quadrature tables or non-indexed derivations may contain it. Database search alone is not evidence of global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

This is the first nonsymmetric two-cell case of optimal \(C^1\) cubic spline quadrature: the theorem removes root tracking, determines every positive three-node configuration, and exactly describes a genuine change in node–knot incidence. The non-\(C^2\) crossing quantifies a regularity limit of knot-position continuation, rather than a merely numerical curiosity. No general rule for many knots is asserted.
