# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The defining polytope is full-dimensional in the native matrix metric. The complete facet list has normal lengths \(1\), \(\sqrt2\), and \(2\). Two independent telescoping paths through the ball-margin inequalities prove the upper bound \(r_m(t)\le\min\{[2+(m-1)\sqrt2]^{-1},(1+t)/(2m+\sqrt2)\}\); one explicit center attains both applicable bounds. Saturation of the corresponding paths yields *all* centers in three parameter regimes, with necessary and sufficient inequalities. At zero second supply the center-polytope map to uniform Pitman–Stanley coordinates is explicit, the Euclidean Jacobian is \((\sqrt2 c)^{m-1}\), and the published parking-function volume formula gives the claimed intrinsic volume. The original zero-supply polytope has \(\binom{m+2}{2}\) vertices by direct order-ideal enumeration. Symbolic exact fraction checks and an independent numerical LP replay support, but do not replace, the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

The original 2023 generalized Pitman–Stanley paper supplies the inequalities, flow representation, and face/vertex enumeration; its inspected sections do not derive Euclidean inball center sets. The 1999 Pitman–Stanley work and the 2023 Hanada–Lentfer–Vindas-Meléndez work supply the classical parking-function polytope, volume and its binary vertex structure; they do not show it emerges as the center locus of a higher-dimensional grid polytope. An indexed prior result on the all-ones generalized Pitman–Stanley staircase already gives the row-controlled radius for that special input; that covered special case is not claimed as new. The present threshold from competing row and total-supply barriers, its center-locus collapse and reopening, and the boundary parking-function identification do not follow from its stated all-ones scope. An uninspected adjacent full text or unindexed broader theorem remains a historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Euclidean inball centers are a natural geometric invariant of a canonical grid-flow/polytope family. The exact irrational phase boundary changes the dimension of their complete solution set from \(m-1\) to zero to \(m\). The zero-supply center set is itself a classical parking-function polytope with \(2^{m-1}\) vertices and a Cayley-tree volume coefficient, while the object whose inballs it parametrizes has only quadratic vertex count. This is structural geometry and an exact all-dimensional classification, not a recomputation of enumerated finite data.

## Sources and limitations

The primary full HTML material inspected was Dugan–Hegarty–Morales–Raymond, *Generalized Pitman–Stanley polytope: vertices and faces* (arXiv:2307.09925), including its definition, flow-equivalence proof, vertex conditions and final discussion. The related open-access Hanada–Lentfer–Vindas-Meléndez article (DOI 10.1007/s00026-023-00671-1), §4, establishes the weakly increasing parking-function connection, volume and binary vertex structure. The original Pitman–Stanley 1999 abstract/technical report establishes the parking-volume antecedent. This review is limited to the stated two-row one-parameter family in its native metric and does not assert independent certification or historical priority.
