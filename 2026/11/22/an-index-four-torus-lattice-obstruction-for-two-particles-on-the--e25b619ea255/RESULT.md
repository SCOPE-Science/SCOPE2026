# An index-four torus-lattice obstruction for two particles on the nine-vertex rook graph

## Finding

Let \(G=K_3\square K_3\) be the graph with vertex set \(\{(i,j):i,j\in\{0,1,2\}\}\) and edges between distinct vertices sharing a row or column. It is also the nine-vertex Paley graph and the line graph of \(K_{3,3}\). Write \(Y=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|G|)\), the space of unordered pairs of distinct geometric points of this graph.

Each pair of vertex-disjoint simple cycles \(\gamma,\delta\) determines an embedded product torus in \(Y\). There are exactly eighteen such pairs with lengths \((3,4)\), consisting of a full row or column triangle and a square in the two remaining rows or columns. They form one orbit under \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)=(S_3\times S_3)\rtimes C_2\). Let \(L_{3,4}\) be the integral sublattice generated by their fundamental classes.

The exact classification is

\[
H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{18},\qquad
H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)/L_{3,4}\cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2.
\]

In addition, any one torus made from two disjoint row triangles and any one made from two disjoint column triangles, together with all eighteen triangle-square tori, generate \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\) integrally. The quotient displayed above is **a lattice quotient**: it does not mean that \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\) itself has torsion.

## Assumptions and scope

All spaces use the ordinary geometric realization of the simple, loop-free graph. The two particles are unordered and forbidden to collide anywhere on the graph, including edge interiors. Homology has integral coefficients. A cycle-pair torus requires vertex-disjoint cycles, so its geometric product lies in the collision-free space; its orientation is induced by arbitrarily choosing orientations on the two cycles. Reversing an orientation changes a generator's sign, not its generated lattice. No assertion is made for larger rook graphs or for more than two particles.

The first public primary source used for the established graph-configuration framework is Barnett--Farber, arXiv:0903.2180, initially posted 12 March 2009 (not its later revision date).

## Proof

**1. Faithful finite model.** Prue--Scrimshaw's stable-equivalence theorem applies with two particles: all nine graph vertices are essential of valence four, every path between two distinct essential vertices has at least one edge, and every noncontractible closed edge path has at least three edges (the girth is three). Consequently the ordinary unordered configuration space deformation retracts onto its Abrams cubical complex \(D\). Its cells are unordered collections of pairwise closure-disjoint vertex and edge cells of \(G\). There are \(36\) vertices \(\{u,v\}\), \(126\) edges \(\{e,v\}\), and \(99\) squares \(\{e,f\}\); no higher cells occur. Indeed \(G\) has nine vertices, eighteen edges, and every vertex has degree four; thus the number of unordered disjoint edge pairs is \(\binom{18}{2}-9\binom42=99\).

**2. Integer boundary operator and top rank.** Number vertices \((i,j)\) by \(3i+j\), orient each graph edge from its lower to its higher number, and order edges lexicographically. Index the square \(\{e,f\}\) with \(e<f\) and orient it by the product \(e\times f\). If \(e=(u,v)\), \(f=(s,t)\), both positively oriented, the cellular boundary is

\[
\partial_2[e,f]=[f,v]-[f,u]-[e,t]+[e,s].
\]

Here \([f,v]\) denotes the one-cell consisting of edge \(f\) and vertex \(v\). This is a completely specified integer \(126\times99\) matrix. Its Smith diagonal has eighty entries equal to \(1\), one equal to \(2\), and eighteen zeros on the domain side. Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=81\). Since \(D\) has no three-cells,

\[
H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z^{99-81}=\mathbb Z^{18}.
\]

The checker regenerates the matrix from the graph definition, verifies \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), and computes this Smith form exactly. Nothing is inferred about infinite families from finite enumeration.

**3. The eighteen product tori.** A canonically oriented simple cycle is represented by its vertex sequence starting at the smallest vertex, choosing the lexicographically smaller traversal direction. For every vertex-disjoint pair \(\gamma=(a_0,\ldots,a_{r-1})\) and \(\delta=(b_0,\ldots,b_{s-1})\), expand their tensor product in the square basis: the coefficient at \([e,f]\) is the product of the cycle-edge orientation signs, multiplied by \(-1\) if the two edges must be swapped to be lexicographically ordered. This constructs a closed integral two-chain, hence a genuine torus class. Enumerating all elementary cycles gives six triangles and nine squares. Exactly eighteen vertex-disjoint triangle-square pairs exist. Let \(T_{3,4}\in M_{99\times18}(\mathbb Z)\) have their chain vectors as columns, sorted according to the reproducible ordering in the accompanying checker. Its Smith invariant factors are

\[
\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{16\text{ times}},\ 2,\ 2.
\]

The eighteen torus classes are independent over \(\mathbb Q\) and their generated lattice has two independent parity defects in the ambient primitive top-cycle lattice. For a compact independently checkable maximal-minor certificate, the square-coordinate rows numbered

`(0,2,3,5,8,11,13,19,22,23,25,27,44,50,53,55,69,82)`

have determinant \(-4\) on the full eighteen-column matrix; the Smith calculation verifies that every maximal minor is divisible by four and the invariant factors are exactly as displayed. Indices are zero-based in the lexicographic square-cell basis.

**4. Two geometric torus corrections suffice.** There are precisely six disjoint triangle pairs: three pairs of rows and three pairs of columns. Adjoin their six columns to obtain \(T_{24}\in M_{99\times24}(\mathbb Z)\), where the first eighteen columns are the triangle-square classes. An explicit eighteen-by-eighteen minor, using columns

`(15,5,18,23,13,10,17,11,14,16,1,21,6,9,2,4,7,8)`

and square-coordinate rows

`(21,28,96,49,90,25,1,81,39,9,16,78,86,26,52,6,32,62)`

has determinant \(+1\). Thus the indicated eighteen columns span a primitive rank-eighteen sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^{99}\) inside \(\ker\partial_2\). Since \(\ker\partial_2\) has rank eighteen, these lattices coincide. Direct Smith reduction also verifies that the twenty columns consisting of the original eighteen plus **one** row-triangle-pair and **one** column-triangle-pair torus are already primitive, so all six extra tori are unnecessary.

Finally, because the image of \(\partial_2\) is free abelian, the exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow \ker\partial_2/L_{3,4}
\longrightarrow \mathbb Z^{99}/L_{3,4}
\longrightarrow \operatorname{im}\partial_2\longrightarrow0
\]

shows that the torsion subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^{99}/L_{3,4}\), computed by the Smith form of \(T_{3,4}\), is exactly the finite index quotient of interest. It is \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), completing the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The graph-configuration framework and its integral intersection form are foundational in Barnett--Farber (2009), and mature-graph Betti formulas and related exact sequences are developed by Farber--Hanbury (2010). Prue--Scrimshaw (arXiv:0909.5511, Theorem 3.2) justifies using the unsplit nine-vertex cubical complex. Maciążek--Sawicki (2019, §5) discusses general product cycles and relations for multiple graph families. None of the inspected relevant statements supplies the **specific** lattice inclusion of the eighteen vertex-disjoint triangle-square tori for \(K_3\square K_3\) nor its two parity defects. Graph-Hom complexes and two-particle configuration spaces are different constructions even when defined from the same nine-vertex graph. General second Betti number formulas alone cannot determine this integral generator index.

## Limitations

The proof gives an exact finite-graph classification, not a general formula for all rook graphs. It does not classify the full cohomology ring or graph braid group. An as-yet-unindexed equivalent lattice computation may exist. The Smith calculations and small determinant certificates are reproducible but are not a substitute for independent external verification. No assertion of independent auditing is made.
