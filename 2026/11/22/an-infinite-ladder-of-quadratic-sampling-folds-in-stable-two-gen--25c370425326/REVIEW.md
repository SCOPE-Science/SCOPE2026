## Correctness — PASS

The theorem fixes the initial observed coordinate and varies only the hidden coordinate, so the two-snapshot map is a one-variable scalar function. The first variation is the damped rotation \((-e^{-\gamma t}\sin(at),e^{-\gamma t}\cos(at))\). The second variation forced by the curvature of both regulation laws integrates exactly at every half-rotation, giving \(P_{T_k}''(0)=r_k(1-r_k)K/D\) with \(|r_k|<1\). If \(K\ne0\), the standard implicit function theorem produces a nondegenerate observation fold for every nearby sampling time. The logistic specialization has independently verified equilibrium jets \(F''(1)=-2\), \(G''(1)=0\), and strictly positive bounded regulation. Its global attraction follows from a unique sink, bounded positive trajectories, constant negative divergence and inward boundary fields. The standalone checker verified four resonances and one nonresonant alias; it is supporting evidence, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected literature

Belgacem's 2026 initiating work treats reconstruction from the instantaneous first coordinate and its derivative and convergence of continuous-time observers, rather than injectivity of a two-snapshot map. Baranowski's 2026 sampled-observability work provides general higher-order singularity geometry, but its main theorems require a cubic no-fold jet, and its explicit pendulum model has a zero resonant quadratic term. That paper does not give the displayed equal-decay negative-feedback curvature formula, its all-positive-integer resonance classification, or the globally attracting asymmetric logistic realization. Aeyels's generic multi-sample theorem is not a two-sample result. An existing general second-variation fold theorem could streamline the method but would not itself supply the exact present model-specific resonance/curvature classification; a previously unlocated older model-specific calculation remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The result identifies a concrete structural failure mode of sparse measurements in a natural strictly monotone regulatory network, quantifies its curvature and fold drift at *every* half-rotation, and proves persistence on open intervals of both resonant and nonresonant measurement times. It separates continuously available derivative information from the information in only two exact time samples, providing a mathematically motivated caution for state-reconstruction experiments. This is not presented as a new general singularity theory or as an improved continuous-time observer.

## Scope and outstanding limitations

The local family requires equal decays, a focus with symmetric opposing slopes, and a nonzero explicit curvature combination. It gives no uniform size over all resonances, no assertion about three or more samples, and no universal extension to arbitrary feedback laws. An independent priority and proof audit is still required.
