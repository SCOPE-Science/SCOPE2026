# An integral three-sphere quotient of the icosahedral dissociation complex

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the twelve-vertex icosahedral graph and let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}_2(I)\) be the simplicial complex of vertex sets \(U\subseteq V(I)\) whose induced connected components all have at most two vertices. Equivalently, every vertex of \(I[U]\) has degree at most one. The full graph-automorphism group \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(I)\), of order \(120\), acts simplicially on \(K\).

**Theorem.** The ordinary geometric orbit space has the **integral homotopy type of a three-sphere**:
\[
\boxed{|K|/G\simeq S^3.}
\]
In particular, this is a nonequivariant homotopy equivalence of spaces, not a claim that the orbit space is a topological manifold or is homeomorphic to the standard sphere.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is the one-skeleton of the regular icosahedron, with twelve vertices and thirty edges. Its complete adjacency list is the immutable `ADJ` constant in `verify.py`; no external graph data are required. Simplices are **induced vertex subsets**, not matchings of graph edges. The condition of induced maximum degree at most one is equivalent to excluding all three-vertex subsets that span two or more edges, and to the usual dissociation-set condition. The group is the **complete** graph-automorphism group, not just its order-\(60\) rotation subgroup. The orbit space is formed from the geometric realization, not by identifying vertices first and subsequently taking a naive abstract simplicial quotient.

## Proof
**1. Finite complex and complete group.** The graph has exactly \(80\) connected triples whose three vertices span at least two graph edges. Enumerating the \(4096\) vertex masks by both this forbidden-triple predicate and the independent induced-degree predicate gives exactly \(323\) nonempty faces, with \(12,66,140,105\) faces in dimensions zero through three. These predicates coincide on **every** vertex mask, so there are no higher simplices. Two explicit vertex permutations `X` and `Y` in `verify.py` each preserve the graph and fix a vertex. Their permutation-group closure contains \(120\) different automorphisms. An automorphism is determined by its image of one vertex and its permutation of that vertex's pentagonal neighbor cycle: the vertex's remaining five distance-two vertices are uniquely determined by their pairs of neighbors in that cycle, and the antipode is unique. Thus there are at most \(12\cdot10=120\) automorphisms, so the exhibited group is all of \(G\).

**2. A simply connected covering complex.** Every pair of vertices spans a one-simplex of \(K\). Choose the star at vertex zero as a spanning tree of this complete one-skeleton. There is one presentation generator for each of the \(55\) non-tree edges. The two-simplex relations directly kill \(35\) generators corresponding to allowed triples containing vertex zero. Every one of the remaining \(20\) edge generators occurs in an allowed triple with two already killed edge generators, so all vanish. The verifier checks this exhaustive two-round elimination against all \(140\) allowed triangles. Hence \(\pi_1(|K|)=1\).

**3. Fundamental group of the geometric quotient.** For a finite group acting on a connected simply connected polyhedron, Armstrong's orbit-space theorem identifies the quotient fundamental group with \(G\) modulo the normal subgroup generated by elements that have fixed points. The displayed graph automorphisms `X` and `Y` fix vertices of \(|K|\), and they generate the entire group of order \(120\). Thus the normal subgroup is all of \(G\), and
\[
\pi_1(|K|/G)=1.
\]
This argument concerns ordinary orbit spaces with stabilizers, not Borel homotopy quotients.

**4. Exact integral cellular homology.** Barycentrically subdivide \(K\). A barycentric simplex is a strictly increasing nonempty chain of faces. Its vertex ranks are distinct, so no group element can permute its vertices nontrivially; the quotient has a regular orbit-cell decomposition, whose oriented cells are group orbits of strictly increasing chains. The verifier exhaustively enumerates \(9905\) such flags, groups them into exactly \(118\) orbit cells, and obtains the cell vector
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(10,35,49,24).
\]
The cellular boundary of a chain deletes one constituent face at a time, with its usual alternating sign. The program reconstructs all incidences and checks that both consecutive boundary compositions vanish. It then uses **integer** row additions, column additions, sign changes and swaps—each unimodular—to reduce the three nontrivial boundary matrices to diagonals containing respectively \(9,26,23\) entries equal to \(+1\), all other entries zero. This is an exact Smith-normal-form certificate: in particular, no unobserved integral torsion remains. The chain ranks yield
\[
H_j(|K|/G;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,3,\\
0,&j=1,2\text{ or }j>3.
\end{cases}
\]
The quotient is connected by construction.

**5. Homotopy classification.** Since the quotient is simply connected and its second homology vanishes, the Hurewicz theorem gives \(\pi_2=0\) and \(\pi_3\cong H_3\cong\mathbb Z\). A representative of a generator of \(\pi_3\) gives a map from \(S^3\) that is an integral homology equivalence. The homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected CW complexes makes that map a homotopy equivalence. This proves the theorem.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3.10 or newer, using only the standard library. The independent graph/face predicates cover all \(4096\) masks; the explicit generators and a complete group closure certify the automorphism group; the triangular-relator elimination verifies simple connectivity of the original complex; and the quotient boundary matrices are reconstructed from all orbit flags. Integer reductions use only determinant-\(\pm1\) transformations. No numerical eigenvalues, finite-field approximations, or precomputed homology tables enter the proof. The deterministic successful output in `verification_output.txt` is included for comparison, but the checker, not the saved output alone, supplies the finite certificate. A separate construction with a graph-isomorphism enumerator and an integer Smith-normal-form library independently reproduced the orbit-cell vector and boundary ranks and unit factors.

## Relationship to prior work
Conant and Thistlethwaite (2008, arXiv:0808.0739) give the hypergraph-independence framework (Alexander dual to their theta complex) and group-action results for different hypergraphs. Taylan (2015, arXiv:1501.06754) studies devoid complexes and matching trees, including path- and cycle-family cases. Orlovich and coauthors (2011) investigate dissociation sets as induced-degree-at-most-one vertex subsets, but not their orbit-space topology. Deshpande and Singh (2020, arXiv:2001.05448) classify higher-independence homotopy for complete multipartite, whiskered, cycle and tree families, not the icosahedral graph.

The closest published same-graph calculations, *The icosahedral dissociation complex: twenty three-spheres and full symmetry* and *Twenty copies of \(S^3\) in the icosahedral two-independence complex, with full symmetry* (2026), show \(K\simeq\bigvee^{20}S^3\) and determine its full rational symmetry character. In particular they imply only that the full orbit space has the **rational** homology of \(S^3\); one explicitly makes no integral quotient assertion. A further published shelling theorem identifies all \(105\) tetrahedral facets of the original complex. Those results do not rule out torsion or a nontrivial quotient fundamental group. The present theorem supplies both missing integral and homotopy information. The equivalences with dissociation and forbidden-triplet complexes were included in searches; no matching or stronger quotient classification was identified in the inspected sources.

## Limitations
The conclusion treats one particular icosahedral graph and one induced-component threshold, and concerns homotopy rather than homeomorphism or a general equivariant wedge decomposition. The certificate is finite and exhaustive for this graph; it does not prove a family-wide assertion for arbitrary polyhedral graphs. Literature comparison cannot exclude unindexed or independently obtained equivalent work. This package contains a self-review, not an independent mathematical audit.
