# Scientific review: signed toroidal integral path homology

## Correctness — PASS

The integral path-product identification over a PID is an explicit theorem of Fu–Yau (arXiv:2603.09406, Theorem 2.7). The needed one-dimensional chain contraction is proved directly: only alternating paths survive for a bidirected cycle of length at least five; their degree-at-least-two differentials have unit entries and split, compatibly with cyclic translation. The anti-invariant cellular incidence matrix has integer Smith diagonal \(1,\ldots,1,2\). Tensoring with the remaining free cellular cycle complexes yields one \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) summand per subset of the remaining directions. Boundary signs, dimensions and integral Smith invariants were checked by the bundled verifier. The all-parameter proof is symbolic; finite enumeration alone would not suffice.

## Originality — PASS with residual uncertainty

Fu–Yau provide the integral shuffle construction but no exact computation for integral negative-seam toroidal chain anti-invariants; Bai–Li–Yau work with real signed homology; Grigor'yan–Muranov–Yau give general digraph product results. The closest integral signed-path work establishes degree-zero order-two torsion but restricts its degree-one classification to graphs without squares, whereas toroidal grids contain squares. Signed cactus results use real coefficients and edge-disjoint cycles. Full accessible texts and actual semantic searches were compared by implication and by coefficient convention. A different presentation in classical local-system topology could imply this computation and remains a priority risk; the exact identification with integral regular path homology is not supplied as a stated result by the identified sources.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The computation classifies every integral group in every degree for a natural infinite family of signed cycle-product networks. It exhibits order-two classes in arbitrarily high homological degrees despite complete real acyclicity and a torsion-free unsigned cover. Its degree-one and higher conclusions are not furnished by the known low-degree signed result, and the exact binomial multiplicities make the phenomenon testable and structurally useful.

## Limits and context

The chain-level versus homology-level anti-invariant distinction is essential. This result does not cover arbitrary edge signs, nonsquare-balanced holonomy, other digraph orientations, higher-torsion orders or Borel-equivariant path homology. Independent audit is not yet performed.
