# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns a single cyclic one-deletion output distribution for **every** \(q\)-ary de Bruijn cycle with \(q\ge2\) and \(n\ge2\), not just existence of an example. The proof separates deletion symbols by Parikh vectors. For each symbol, its unique length-\(n\) constant run supplies a unique anchor after any other same-symbol deletion, while a deletion within the anchor destroys the only occurrence of the corresponding constant factor. Equality of the two rooted outputs forces their deleted indices into the same constant run. Every output therefore corresponds bijectively to a source run. Short run counts follow from complete factor multiplicity, and the terminal counts from the \(q\) occurrences of the constant length-\(n-1\) factor. The case \(n=2\) uses the empty-sum convention. The likelihood is exactly run length divided by \(q^n\). The finite proof is independently reconstructible from the displayed definitions, without computational assumptions. The standalone checker reproduces 16 parameter pairs and every binary de Bruijn cycle through order five; this evidence is corroborative rather than an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Sloane's 2002 single-deletion work establishes **linear-word** run counts and deletion descendants, not circular rotation-classes of the surviving outputs. Liron–Langberg give more general linear subsequence analysis but do not assert this cyclic distribution. Sawada–Williams–Wong construct de Bruijn sequences but do not give a cyclic one-deletion descendant probability law. The closest publicly indexed circular result gives the **maximum** single-deletion output count among arbitrary binary necklaces; a maximum cannot imply the exact count for each de Bruijn necklace, and it does not imply the arbitrary-alphabet multiplicity distribution. Queries also sought output distributions, trace degrees, and exact constants under de Bruijn constraints; no equivalent statement was identified in the checked material. Because cyclic deletion has a broader unindexed literature, priority remains conditional rather than established by absence of search results.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This supplies a complete source-independent output probability law for every de Bruijn circle, including arbitrary alphabet size and every order. In general cyclic words, quotienting by rotation can collapse deletion outputs from different runs, and exact shadow sizes vary. Here maximal local-factor coverage prevents all such extra collisions. Both the support cardinality and the entire frequency-weighted distribution are directly relevant baseline invariants for cyclic deletion observations of universal test sequences, with no reliance on an arbitrary chosen construction. The quantitative formula, rather than mere correctness of an elementary fact, is the object of the claim.

## Residual scientific limitations
The theorem is restricted to a single deletion from a complete de Bruijn source. Its simple structural proof may already be known under a different description such as marked-run trace rigidity or cyclic deletion code degree. It does not determine recovery of an unknown source, two-deletion traces or optimum codebooks. The computations are finite and no independent audit has been carried out.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
