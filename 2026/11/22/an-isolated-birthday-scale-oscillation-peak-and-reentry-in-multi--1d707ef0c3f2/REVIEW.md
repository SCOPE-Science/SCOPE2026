# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The update map is defined with a uniform unique-winner decision per opposite shore and incumbent retention on *all* ties. A full three-case partition of initial states (two unique winners, one, or none) yields an exhaustive five-row first-periodic-entry/least-period table whose integers total \(q^{m+n}\), and directly establishes the sharp two-update universal bound. The number of uniquely winning words is obtained by exact bounded-occupancy enumeration. A one-pair birthday formula and a triple-collision bound prove the critical-scale limit; pairwise collision-indicator independence plus a variance estimate proves the intervening suppression, and a fixed-palette tie probability bound proves the low-palette reentry. Two independently coded finite-state enumerators, a separate exact occupancy checker and their actual outputs passed upon execution. Risk: the asymptotic proof concerns the stated regular scaling regimes only; finite checks cannot certify the infinite statements.

## Value — PASS

The contribution is not merely the elementary two-shore swap observation. It couples a complete all-alphabet dynamical entry-time/basin enumeration with a sharp, nonmonotone scale law: for balanced communities the positive oscillation peak \(e^{-2}\) is attained at palette size asymptotic to half the squared community size, disappears in a collision-tie valley, and reappears for every fixed palette. It isolates which mechanism creates cycles (independent unique winners with different colors), and shows how increasing opinion diversity can alternately create and destroy sustained oscillation. Exact finite census and asymptotic phase diagram are natural observables of a symmetric deterministic voting model. The precise limitations on tie convention and scaling are explicit.
