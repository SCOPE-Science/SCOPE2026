# An odd-cycle exception to Hall-cut shattering of matching-covered vertex sets

## Finding
For every simple cycle \(C_n\), \(n\ge3\), let \(\mathcal C_n=\{V(M):M\text{ is a matching of }C_n\}\), where the empty matching is allowed and a query asks if a vertex is covered by the selected matching. Let \(S\subseteq V(C_n)\), and let \(\Gamma(S)\) be the bipartite subgraph of cycle edges with exactly one endpoint in \(S\).

**Exact shattering theorem.** The set \(S\) is VC-shattered if and only if either

1. \(\Gamma(S)\) contains a matching saturating every vertex of \(S\), or
2. \(n=2m+1\) is odd and \(S\) is one of the \(n\) minimum vertex covers of \(C_n\), each of size \(m+1\).

The second case is equivalently the cyclic support indicator alternating \(1,0,1,0,\ldots,1\), with exactly one adjacent \(11\) edge and no adjacent \(00\) edge. These \(n\) supports are all the shattered supports larger than the ordinary maximum matching number of an odd cycle. For every \(n\ge3\),
\[
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal C_n)=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal C_n)=\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil.
\]
In particular, for \(n=2m+1\), both dimensions equal \(m+1\), strictly exceeding the graph matching number \(m\). For even cycles the crossing-matching case alone suffices, and the dimensions equal \(n/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, undirected cycles with distinct indexed vertices \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\). A matching is any pairwise vertex-disjoint subset of cycle edges, not necessarily maximal or maximum; its covered set consists of incident vertices. Shattering allows a different matching for each requested labeling on a fixed query support. Littlestone dimension is the maximum depth of a complete adaptive binary vertex-membership query tree, with each root-to-leaf labeling realized by some matching-covered set. The theorem applies to both odd and even cycles from the triangular boundary \(n=3\).

## Proof
**1. Crossing-matchings are sufficient.** If a matching \(Q\) in \(\Gamma(S)\) saturates \(S\), each edge of \(Q\) has exactly one endpoint in \(S\). For any requested positive trace \(A\subseteq S\), retain precisely the \(Q\)-edges incident to \(A\). This is a matching and its covered-vertex trace on \(S\) is exactly \(A\).

**2. Necessity except for one odd-cycle configuration.** Decompose \(\Gamma(S)\) into maximal alternating paths (and, when every cycle edge crosses the cut, one even cycle). Each alternating path has either equal numbers of vertices on both shores or one extra vertex on the shore containing its two endpoints. A matching saturating \(S\) exists exactly when no component has an excess vertex on the \(S\)-shore: each balanced or \(S\)-deficient alternating component is saturated by pairing consecutive vertices, independently of the other components.

Suppose \(\Gamma(S)\) has a deficient-for-matching component \(P\) with one more vertex in \(S\) than outside \(S\). Its endpoints are both in \(S\). If \(P\) does not span all of \(C_n\), the original cycle neighbors just outside the two ends of \(P\) lie in \(S\), because the boundary edges were removed from the crossing graph. Label every queried vertex in \(P\) positive and every queried vertex outside \(P\) negative. Any matching realizing this assignment cannot use boundary edges from \(P\) to the rest of the cycle, since their outside endpoints are queried negative. Nor can it cover more than one of the \(|P\cap S|\) positive vertices with each of the \(|P\setminus S|\) outside-support vertices internal to \(P\): the only internal edges alternate between the two shores. The positive trace is impossible. Therefore a shattered support with no crossing matching has a deficient component spanning all vertices.

For such a spanning alternating path, \(n\) is odd, its two endpoints lie in \(S\), and the *only* cycle edge absent from \(\Gamma(S)\) is the edge between these endpoints. The indicator of \(S\) has one adjacent \(11\) pair and all other adjacent pairs alternate; hence \(|S|=(n+1)/2\) and \(S\) is a minimum vertex cover. There are exactly \(n\) cyclic rotations of this support, one for the position of its \(11\) edge. This proves necessity.

**3. The exceptional supports are shattered.** Label a canonical exceptional support \(S=\{1,3,\ldots,2m+1\}\) along cycle vertices \(1,\ldots,2m+1\), where the exceptional edge joins the first and last vertices. For each desired trace \(A\subsetneq S\), choose \(s\in S\setminus A\). Delete \(s\) from the linear path \(1-2-\cdots-(2m+1)\). Because \(s\) has odd index, both remaining path components have even cardinality and possess the consecutive-edge perfect matching. That perfect matching pairs every other member of \(S\) with a vertex outside \(S\). Keep only edges incident with \(A\); the resulting trace is exactly \(A\). For \(A=S\), select the exceptional edge \(\{1,2m+1\}\) together with \(\{2,3\},\{4,5\},\ldots,\{2m-2,2m-1\}\), leaving vertex \(2m\) uncovered. This matching covers all of \(S\), proving sufficiency including \(n=3\).

**4. The static dimension.** In the crossing-matching case, \(|S|\le\nu(C_n)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). The exceptional class for odd \(n\) has size \(\lceil n/2\rceil\). For even \(n\), take one endpoint from every edge of a perfect matching, obtaining a crossing matching and a shattered support of size \(n/2\). Thus the VC dimension is \(\lceil n/2\rceil\).

**5. The adaptive dimension.** We give an explicit reduction to the established matroid-basis equality \(\operatorname{Ldim}=\operatorname{VCdim}\) for finite matroid basis-incidence classes. For a bipartite graph \(G=(L\sqcup R,E)\), choose generic, algebraically independent real indeterminates on the entries of a matrix \(A\) at graph edges and zero elsewhere. In the column matroid of \([I_L\mid A]\), a basis of size \(|L|\) consists of columns \((L\setminus X)\cup Y\), with \(X\subseteq L\), \(Y\subseteq R\), exactly when \(X\) and \(Y\) have a perfect matching in \(G\). This follows from the determinant expansion and absence of cancellation between distinct matching monomials. Hence the basis-indicator class, after complementing all coordinates in \(L\), is precisely \(\{V(M):M\text{ a matching of }G\}\). Complementing a fixed subset of query labels preserves both dimensions. The matroid-basis equality and the standard Hall-cut criterion therefore give \(\operatorname{Ldim}=\nu(G)\) for every finite bipartite \(G\).

Fix any root-query vertex \(v\) in a hypothetical full adaptive tree for \(\mathcal C_n\) of depth \(d\). The zero-answer version space consists exactly of the covered-vertex sets of matchings in the induced path \(C_n-v=P_{n-1}\). This is bipartite and has \(\operatorname{Ldim}=\nu(P_{n-1})=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\), as just established. A full depth-\(d\) tree has a depth-\((d-1)\) zero subtree, so \(d\le1+\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor=\lceil n/2\rceil\). The VC lower bound supplies an adaptive tree of the same depth by querying one fixed shattered support in sequence. Therefore equality holds.

## Verification
The included standalone Python 3 checker `verify_cycles.py` independently enumerates every matching of \(C_n\), compares actual traces of every support with the crossing-matching-or-exception criterion, checks the exact exceptional support count, and computes both shattering dimensions, including an exhaustive version-space recursion for the adaptive dimension. It verifies every order from \(n=3\) through \(n=11\). The recorded output `verification_output.txt` is reproducible using `python3 verify_cycles.py`. This finite verification corroborates but does not replace the unrestricted proof.

## Relationship to prior work
A published result on matching-covered vertices of **bipartite** graphs gives exactly the crossing-matching criterion and VC dimension equal to the graph matching number; the same work explicitly notes that the triangle is a nonbipartite counterexample. These established results are used, not claimed anew. A separate established matroid-basis theorem gives the equality of VC and Littlestone dimensions for basis-incidence concept classes, enabling the bipartite-path bound above; that equality is not asserted as a new contribution. The 2019 matroid-intersection literature provides the classical min–max and 2024 matching-delta-matroid literature identifies the matching-covered sets as feasible sets and studies representations, not their odd-cycle shattering behavior. The new result here is the exact classification of the **only** failures of the Hall-cut criterion on a single odd cycle, their number and size, and the sharp all-order adaptive boundary. Searches by object, aliases, and implication did not identify a previously published equivalent odd-cycle theorem, but a negative search cannot certify historical priority.

## Limitations
The characterization is for vertex coverage by unrestricted matchings of simple cycles; it does not cover general nonbipartite graphs, maximum-only matchings, edge-membership concepts, or weighted matching families. Its adaptive proof invokes the established finite-matroid basis equality, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed. Although all small cases have been exhaustively checked, finite tests alone cannot prove the all-order claim. Unindexed equivalent work remains a potential priority risk.
