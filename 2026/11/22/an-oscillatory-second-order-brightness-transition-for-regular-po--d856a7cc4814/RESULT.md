# An oscillatory second-order brightness transition for regular polygonal pyramids

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(P_{n,h}\) be the right pyramid whose base is a regular \(n\)-gon of circumradius one in the horizontal plane and whose apex is at height \(h>0\). Write \(B_{n,h}(u)\) for its ordinary orthographic shadow area in the plane normal to a unit vector \(u\), and \(M_n(h)=\max_{u\in S^2}B_{n,h}(u)\). Put \(\alpha_n=\pi/n\) and \(s_n=\sin\alpha_n\cos\alpha_n\). Then
\[
\boxed{M_n(h)^2=\max_{\lceil n/2\rceil\le k\le n}\{h^2\sin^2(k\alpha_n)+k^2s_n^2\}.}
\]
Every maximizing axis is generated by the sum of the oriented area vectors of \(k\) consecutive lateral triangular faces, for an active maximizer \(k\) of the displayed expression; these, and their opposites, are the **complete** maximizing directions. For \(k=n\), the only such axis is vertical; otherwise each active \(k\) contributes \(n\) distinct unoriented axes. Thus the extremal viewing configurations and every height-phase boundary are given by the upper envelope of finitely many explicitly specified affine functions of \(h^2\).

The first loss of the vertical direction's global maximality occurs precisely at \(h=h_n\), where
\[
\boxed{h_n^2=\min_{\lceil n/2\rceil\le k<n}\frac{(n^2-k^2)s_n^2}{\sin^2(k\pi/n)}.}
\]
The vertical axis is the unique unoriented maximizing axis for \(0<h<h_n\); at equality it ties with at least one nonvertical orbit, and it is not maximizing for \(h>h_n\).

The approach to the circular-cone threshold has a **nonconvergent, fully classified second-order term**. Let \(\xi\) be the unique minimizer on \([1/2,1)\) of
\[
R(x)=\frac{1-x^2}{\sin^2(\pi x)},
\]
equivalently the unique solution in \((1/2,1)\) of \(\xi+\pi(1-\xi^2)\cot(\pi\xi)=0\). Define
\[
\tau=\pi^2R(\xi),\quad C=\frac{\pi^2R''(\xi)}2>0,\quad A=-\frac{4\pi^2}3\tau,
\quad d_n=\operatorname{dist}(n\xi,\mathbb Z).
\]
Then, uniformly as integer \(n\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{n^2(h_n^2-\tau)=A+C d_n^2+O(n^{-1}).}
\]
Moreover \(\xi\) is irrational, hence the complete set of accumulation values of the scaled transition correction is precisely
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Acc}\{n^2(h_n^2-\tau)\}=[A,A+C/4].}
\]
Numerically \(\xi\approx0.58898107634912\), \(\tau\approx6.97700744205075\), \(C\approx52.5031358619\), \(A\approx-91.8137378087\), and the right endpoint is approximately \(-78.6879538432\). These are rounded values of exact analytic expressions, not interval-certified decimal claims. In particular, \(h_n\to\sqrt\tau\approx2.64140255206410\), with a strictly negative order-\(n^{-2}\) squared-height correction for all sufficiently large \(n\), and **no single second-order constant**.

For any fixed \(n,h\), let \(m\) denote the number of distinct unoriented maximizing axes described above. There is an explicitly constructible threshold \(t_*<M_n(h)\) such that a Haar-uniform viewing direction \(U\) satisfies the exact terminal law
\[
\boxed{\Pr(B_{n,h}(U)>t)=m(1-t/M_n(h)),\qquad t_*<t<M_n(h).}
\]
Thus the linear upper-tail coefficient jumps at the discrete height-phase transitions.

## Assumptions and scope
The base polygon is regular, centered at the origin and inscribed in the unit circle; its apex is directly above the center. The height is a positive real number and \(n\) is an arbitrary integer at least five. Oriented unit directions are antipodally identified for counting projection planes; random viewing directions are Haar-uniform on \(S^2\). The first-transition parameter \(h_n\) is defined independently for each integer \(n\), and the asymptotic range is the full sequence of integers tending to infinity, without a parity restriction. The \(O(n^{-1})\) error is for the already rescaled expression and has an absolute implied constant for sufficiently large \(n\).

## Proof
**1. Polygonal projection-body identity.** The outward area vector of lateral face \(j\) is
\[
a_j=(h\sin\alpha_n\cos((2j+1)\alpha_n),\ h\sin\alpha_n\sin((2j+1)\alpha_n),\ s_n),\quad 0\le j<n.
\]
The base's outward area vector is \(a_0=(0,0,-ns_n)\). As \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_j=-a_0\), Cauchy's projection formula and the elementary positive/negative-part identity give
\[
B_{n,h}(u)=\tfrac12\bigl(|a_0\cdot u|+\sum_j|a_j\cdot u|\bigr)
=\max_{S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n-1\}}\left|u\cdot\sum_{j\in S}a_j\right|.
\]
Consequently \(M_n(h)\) equals the largest Euclidean norm among all lateral-face subset sums.

**2. Exact maximizer classification.** Fix a subset size \(k\). If \(e_j\) denotes the equally spaced planar unit vector in the first two coordinates of \(a_j\), then
\[
\left\|\sum_{j\in S}a_j\right\|^2
=h^2\sin^2\alpha_n\left|\sum_{j\in S}e_j\right|^2+k^2s_n^2.
\]
The sum of any \(k\) distinct regular-\(n\)-gon unit vectors has maximum possible modulus \(\sin(k\alpha_n)/\sin\alpha_n\), with equality only for cyclically consecutive indices. Indeed, project the vector sum onto its own direction; the \(k\) largest projections among the equally spaced unit vectors are obtained at consecutive directions, and the geometric-series identity gives the claimed modulus. Since the full sum of the \(e_j\) vanishes, a subset of size \(k<n/2\) has a complementary subset of size \(n-k>n/2\) with the same planar modulus and strictly larger positive vertical component. It therefore cannot maximize. These observations prove the finite maximum formula and exhaust all its maximizing subsets. For each winning \(k<n\), the consecutive subsets give precisely \(n\) different axes; the all-face subset \(k=n\) gives the vertical axis. All their antipodes maximize by symmetry.

**3. Sharp first threshold and terminal tails.** The \(k=n\) squared norm is \(n^2s_n^2\), whereas each \(k<n\) competitor is the affine function \(k^2s_n^2+h^2\sin^2(k\alpha_n)\) with positive slope. Their earliest crossing is exactly the displayed minimum for \(h_n^2\), and no crossing can reverse, proving the vertical-to-oblique threshold in both directions.

For the tail, construct the finite set \(\mathcal V\) of all nonzero lateral-face subset sums. Its maximum norm is \(M=M_n(h)\), and the normalized vectors of norm \(M\) occur in \(2m\) distinct oriented directions with minimum angular separation \(\delta>0\). Put
\[
L=\max\{\|v\|:v\in\mathcal V,\ \|v\|<M\},\qquad
 t_*=\max\{L,M\cos(\delta/2)\},
\]
with \(L=0\) if the set in its definition is empty. For \(t_*<t<M\), only the maximizing vectors can satisfy \(|u\cdot v|>t\), and their antipodal spherical caps of radius \(\arccos(t/M)<\delta/2\) are pairwise disjoint. Each cap occupies \((1-t/M)/2\) of the sphere; their total probability is \(m(1-t/M)\). This construction is finite and proves \(t_*<M\).

**4. Uniqueness of the continuum transition.** Let \(f(x)=x+\pi(1-x^2)\cot(\pi x)\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
R'(x)=-\frac{2f(x)}{\sin^2(\pi x)}.
\]
For \(y=\pi(1-x)\in(0,\pi/2)\), a second direct differentiation, followed by integration by parts, yields
\[
f'(x)\sin^2(\pi x)
=-4\int_0^y(\pi-v)\sin^2v\,dv<0.
\]
Moreover \(f(1/2)=1/2\) and \(\lim_{x\uparrow1}f(x)=-1\). Thus \(f\) has exactly one zero \(\xi\), and \(R\) has exactly one minimum there. The derivative formula also gives \(R''(\xi)=-2f'(\xi)/\sin^2(\pi\xi)>0\). This proves that every constant in the finding is well-defined.

**5. Second-order lattice-grid expansion.** The first-transition formula can be rewritten identically as
\[
h_n^2=n^2\sin^2(\pi/n)\cos^2(\pi/n)
\min_{\lceil n/2\rceil\le k<n}R(k/n).
\]
Taylor expansion of the prefactor gives
\[
n^2\sin^2(\pi/n)\cos^2(\pi/n)
=\pi^2-\frac{4\pi^4}{3n^2}+O(n^{-4}).
\]
The unique strictly quadratic interior minimum of \(R\) implies, by comparing its value with the nearest integer to \(n\xi\), that every minimizing grid point lies a bounded number of grid steps from \(n\xi\). A uniform third-order Taylor expansion over those finitely many nearby indices therefore gives
\[
\min_k R(k/n)=R(\xi)+\frac{R''(\xi)}{2n^2}d_n^2+O(n^{-3}).
\]
Multiplying these two expansions proves the asserted rescaled correction with remainder \(O(n^{-1})\).

**6. Irrationality and the full accumulation interval.** If \(\xi\) were rational, then \(e^{2\pi i\xi}\) would be a root of unity, so \(\cot(\pi\xi)\) would be a nonzero algebraic number. But \(f(\xi)=0\) would then force \(\pi=-\xi/((1-\xi^2)\cot(\pi\xi))\) to be algebraic, contradicting the transcendence of \(\pi\). Thus \(\xi\) is irrational. The fractional parts of \(n\xi\) are dense in \([0,1]\), and hence \(d_n^2\) has precisely \([0,1/4]\) as its set of accumulation values. The uniform vanishing remainder proves the sharp interval \([A,A+C/4]\).

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` (Python standard library) independently constructs orthographic convex-hull areas and checks them against the Cauchy facet formula for 2,800 randomly selected projection directions across eight values of \(n\) and seven heights. For small \(n\), all lateral-face subsets are also enumerated and their maximum norm independently compared to the closed formula. Further tests compare exact finite thresholds against the derived second-order expansion. The execution prints `PASS 2840 numerical checks` and the critical constants. Numerical comparisons corroborate the formula but do not replace the all-\(n\) geometric and analytic proofs given above.

## Relationship to prior work
Cauchy's projection-area formula and the elementary root-of-unity sum are classical inputs, not new results. The published finding *A sharp global brightness-direction switch for right circular cones* establishes the **continuum** threshold \(\sqrt\tau\approx2.64140255206409\), which is explicitly credited here: the equality of constants is a required limiting consistency check, not a new cone theorem. A published *Sharp full-vertex shadows of right regular polygonal pyramids* theorem treats the probability that every vertex remains visible in a projection, rather than maximizing its **area**; the same underlying pyramid family does not imply a brightness-phase formula. Omri Abas's 2026 preprint gives a general random-brightness framework and exact densities for other specific polytopes, but its publicly accessible abstract does not state the finite-\(n\) transition or its oscillatory correction. What is asserted here is the sharp all-\(n\) brightness classification and the specifically quantified, nonconvergent second-order polygon-to-cone transition law.

## Limitations
The theorem does not give the entire random-shadow area distribution away from its exact terminal interval, nor does it cover irregular bases, off-center apices or perspective projection. The numerical decimals are rounded approximations rather than independently certified intervals. The complete source text of Abas's relevant preprint was not accessible at this review; its potentially stronger applications constitute a residual historical-priority risk. An unrestricted historical novelty claim or independent mathematical certification is not made.
