# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The two-disk overlap lemma follows from the standard sector-minus-kite formula even in the obtuse case; independent arccosine lens evaluations on 900 deterministic convex test polygons matched the formula. Fan triangulation partitions polygon area and splits each interior angle exactly. Weighted square completion proves the unique interior minimizer with global identity, not just a local Taylor approximation. The special triangle interpretation follows from a positive barycentric weighted average ruling out any triple-overlap point except the common endpoint. The numerical checks do not substitute for this exact argument.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
Pavlík's motivating polytope-coverage theorem supplies the relevant Thales disks and power diagrams, but gives no formula for adjacent lens areas or their optimizer. Closest published regular-polygon disk-multiplicity and mean-depth records fix the pole at the center and vary the spatial sampling point, so they do not imply this all-convex-polygon, variable-interior-pole quadratic identity. Searches for the formula, angle-weighted barycenter and aliases found no equivalent. Classical two-circle overlap formulas are credited background. The two closest published records were accessible as indexed scientific summaries, not fully inspectable primary texts; undiscovered equivalent identities remain a real risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The quantity measures local edgewise redundancy in an established diameter-ball covering of convex polygons. A priori it is a sum of nonlinear circular lens areas involving inverse trigonometric functions. An exact global cancellation yields a canonical angle-weighted center and a shape-independent sharp stability law for all convex polygons, with the stronger real twofold-coverage interpretation for every triangle. This is structural rather than an isolated routine numerical evaluation; no analogous assertion is made for clipped overlap or unrestricted overlap distributions.
