# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The graph is specified by an explicit set of five generators on sixteen binary four-vectors. A pigeonhole argument rules out functions of weight at most five. The strongly regular intersection computation reduces every weight-six optimum to a disjoint ordered pair of three-sets \((A,F(A))\); transitivity and two explicit representatives establish the involution. Elementary triple enumeration gives exactly \(160\) independent and \(160\) induced-path triples. Two source-distinct exhaustive programs verify the whole weight-at-most-six configuration space and the exact two-token graph, including all 320 BFS distance-shell profiles.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Three independent primary texts were compared by their actual mathematical claims. The 2009 Clebsch design paper determines maximum independent five-sets and ordinary four-vertex dominating sets, not rainbow-optimizing three-set pairs. The 2012 rainbow paper establishes the definition and Cartesian-product reduction but no Clebsch optimization. The 2015 paper studies general weak Roman comparisons. The closest indexed prior antineighborhood result concerns Petersen pairs, not Clebsch triples. Earlier archival claims on binary four-cube two-rainbow optima (also 320, but weight seven on a different graph), ordinary Clebsch domination, and Clebsch locating domination were checked for implication rather than mere number matching. Searches for both names of the graph, the equivalent Cartesian prism domination problem, and the 320-state exchange graph located no identical result. Unindexed or inaccessible work may still exist.

## Value — PASS
This is a natural complete classification of optimum resource placements for a canonical highly symmetric graph, not a renaming of a known minimum. The involutive matching between independent and path triples is a structural explanation of all optima. The sharp contrast between no valid single-copy relocation and globally connected exact two-copy relocation, together with the uniform finite distance profile, provides precise mobility information absent from the optimum weight alone.

## Scope and evidence limits
The distance metric is independently exhaustive rather than symbolically derived. No priority claim follows merely from absence of indexed hits; no extension to general strongly regular graphs is asserted. Independent validation remains outstanding.
