# Arithmetic descent of the eight primitive Hessian three-cycles

## Finding

On the characteristic-zero moduli line with coordinate \(x=j/1728\), the Hessian map is \(h(x)=(4-x)^3/(27x^2)\). The exact-period-three locus consists of 24 reduced geometric points in eight unordered cycles. The action of the absolute Galois group of \(\mathbb Q\) on the cycles has orbits of sizes \(2,2,4\). In each size-two orbit the field of definition of either cycle is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\). In the size-four orbit each cycle is defined over a quartic field generated by a root of
\[
G(z)=z^4+420z^3+57456z^2+174528z+269568.
\]
These quartic fields have normal closure with dihedral Galois group of order eight. No cycle descends as an unordered set to \(\mathbb Q\). The individual periodic points form three irreducible blocks of degrees \(6,6,12\).

The field of definition of an unordered cycle here is the fixed field of its setwise stabilizer. It need not contain the three coordinates separately. The claim concerns the moduli map, not plane cubic models or twists realizing its orbits.

## Proof

**Point polynomials.** Use homogeneous iteration \([U:V]\mapsto[(4V-U)^3:27U^2V]\), starting with \([x:1]\), and write \([U_i:V_i]\) for the result after \(i\) steps. The polynomials in each pair are coprime. The finite fixed-point polynomial is \(U_1-xV_1=-4(x-1)(7x^2+4x+16)\). Multiplication gives
\[
U_3-xV_3\ \doteq\ (x-1)(7x^2+4x+16)F_1(x)F_2(x)F_3(x),
\]
where \(\doteq\) denotes equality up to a nonzero scalar and
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_1(x)&=x^6+84x^5+11040x^4-47936x^3+204288x^2-33792x+4096,\\
F_2(x)&=19x^6+912x^5+18240x^4-54272x^3+47616x^2+3072x+4096,\\
F_3(x)&=37x^{12}+15540x^{11}+2026416x^{10}-30549568x^9+86526720x^8\\
&\quad+656409600x^7+378470400x^6-4591042560x^5+6717505536x^4\\
&\quad-4462477312x^3+1588592640x^2+25165824x+16777216.
\end{aligned}
\]
The product is squarefree. Each \(F_i\) has degree-preserving irreducible reduction modulo five: for its reduction \(F\) of degree \(n\), repeated fifth powers give \(x^{5^n}\equiv x\pmod F\), with \(\gcd(F,x^{5^{n/\ell}}-x)=1\) for every prime \(\ell\mid n\). These are the finite-field irreducibility tests replayed in the accompanying code. The point at infinity is fixed. The roots of the three \(F_i\) are therefore precisely the exact-period-three points, because any period dividing three is either one or three.

**Cycle sums and descent.** Let \(s=x+h(x)+h^2(x)\). In \(\mathbb Q[x]/(F_i)\), evaluate \(s\) by inverting \(V_1V_2\) and multiplying by \(xV_1V_2+U_1V_2+U_2V_1\). Exact remainders give
\[
  s^2+84s+10512=0\;(i=1),\qquad
  s^2+48s+1008=0\;(i=2),\qquad G(s)=0\;(i=3).
\]
The discriminants of the first two polynomials are \(-3\cdot108^2\) and \(-3\cdot24^2\), and \(G\) is irreducible by the same test at five. The three minimal polynomials are distinct, so their root sets are disjoint. Each \(F_i\) is one Galois orbit and each cycle sum occurs at exactly three roots of its \(F_i\) (the ratio of the point and sum degrees); these three points form a cycle since the sum is invariant under iteration. Thus fixing the unordered cycle is equivalent to fixing its sum. This proves exactly the two quadratic orbits and one quartic orbit, and excludes rational cycles.

**Quartic normal closure.** The discriminant and cubic resolvent of \(G\) are
\[
\operatorname{disc}(G)=2^{24}3^{20}7^2\,37\,211^2,\qquad
R(w)=(w-288)(w^2-57168w+55759104).
\]
The rational resolvent root fixes a pairing of the four roots, putting the Galois group inside the dihedral group of order eight. Irreducibility modulo five gives a Frobenius four-cycle. Use the good prime **13** for the transposition:
\[
G(z)\equiv z(z-5)(z^2-4z+2)\pmod{13}.
\]
The quadratic discriminant \(8\) is a nonsquare modulo 13 and the two linear factors are distinct, so this reduction is squarefree of type \((1,1,2)\). Frobenius at 13 is a transposition. A proper subgroup of the dihedral group containing an element of order four must be its cyclic rotation subgroup, which has no transposition in the action on four letters. This proves the claimed group. The reduction at seven, by contrast, is not squarefree and is not used for a Frobenius cycle type.

## Prior work

The Hessian map and the count of eight geometric three-cycles are not new. Kettinger, [*The dynamics of the Hesse derivative on the j-invariant*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04117), §§3.1–3.2, gives the rational map and the general complex orbit-count formula. Mula, Pintore and Taufer, [*The Hessian of elliptic curves as a Lattès map*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17042), give its rigid Lattès realization and broader dynamical framework. The result here specifies the Galois action and fields for unordered exact-period-three orbits on the rational moduli line. In particular the complex count does not settle descent, and individual point degrees alone do not specify the quartic normal closure. No conclusion is made about full point-field Galois groups or historical priority beyond the sources compared.
