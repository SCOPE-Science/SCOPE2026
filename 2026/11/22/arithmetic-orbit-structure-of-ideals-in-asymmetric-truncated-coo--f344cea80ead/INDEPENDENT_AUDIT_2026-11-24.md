## correctness

PASS

Intersect a proper ideal with each branch to obtain two tails. Multiplication by x and y traps the residual quotient in the two preceding boundary coordinates; neither coordinate line can occur by definition of the tails. This gives the monomial and mixed normal forms over any finite field. In the degree-two product of the images of x,y the independent nonzero x²,y² force the cotangent matrix to be monomial, including in characteristic two. A swap contradicts x^m=0 because n>m. The off-branch terms then annihilate a generator of their branch and hence are socle terms; conversely the prescribed choices preserve the relations and invertibility follows from the invertible cotangent action on the nilpotent radical. For a mixed ideal the two tails are stable. Off-branch terms disappear in the boundary quotient except at (1,n−1) and (m−1,1); in each exceptional pair the free socle coefficient translates the slope, including the pure branch endpoint, giving q ideals. On every other pair the only surviving action is multiplication by b^v/a^u, whose image in the cyclic group of order q−1 has index gcd(u,v,q−1). This proves the whole partition, including mn−1 stable monomial/unit ideals. A nonexceptional mixed pair is pointwise fixed exactly when both exponents are multiples of q−1; the two exceptional pairs meet that divisibility rule only for q=2 and must be excluded there. The tail dimensions plus one for a mixed boundary yield the two polynomial formulas. Dividing the fixed count by 1+mn+(q−1)(m−1)(n−1) gives the claimed limit for fixed q as both lengths grow. The q=2 exceptional count is four, and the small cases (3,4,2) and (3,4,3) agree with the supplied log by hand.
