# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
On the Hermite polynomial core, the anisotropic Gaussian generator has eigenvalues \(aj+bk\). The one-dimensional Epperson domain determines boundedness of each coordinate multiplier; the necessity via restriction to coordinate-only functions rules out cancellation outside the intersection. Fubini gives contractivity inside and the constants give exact norm one. The density law uses irrational linear-flow equidistribution with null discontinuity boundaries, and the no-period conclusion follows by invariance of proper circle windows under translations. In the rational case, an explicit lower bound on distinct zero separations makes the short-time formula exact. Boundary issues at saturation are explicitly treated as boundedness (not necessarily interior holomorphy) claims. The attached diagnostic passes, but the proof is analytic.

## Originality — PASS (subject to bibliographic risk)
Epperson's boundedness region, the isotropic periodicity, classical product tensorization and torus equidistribution are established tools, not new results. Perelmuter's arXiv:2611.15455v1 treats periodic covariance and one-rate examples, not the two-rate arithmetic phase diagram. The anisotropic two-rate factorization alone is not the novelty claim. The new identified assertion is the sharp change in the imaginary-time admissibility-density exponent for rational versus irrational rate ratios, together with the exact aperiodic-to-periodic-to-unrestricted section transitions. Semantic literature and record searches did not find this combined result, but an earlier anisotropic Gaussian analysis could imply it implicitly.

## Value — PASS
The invariant is the asymptotic fraction of imaginary times at a fixed real smoothing depth for which a natural anisotropic Gaussian diffusion has a bounded complex-time evolution. A single Gaussian rate has periodic geometry, but two relaxation rates produce a sharp arithmetic bifurcation in both global period structure and density exponent. This fully determines a motivated, intrinsic geometric question for the smallest anisotropic instance and separates commensurability from mere inequality of relaxation rates. It is not presented as a new method for deriving one-dimensional Epperson bounds or as a broad nonnormal-generator theorem.
