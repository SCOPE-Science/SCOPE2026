# Arithmetic rigidity and a popcorn law for subtracting circular tent covariances

## Finding

Let \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\), let \(d(\theta)=\min_{j\in\mathbb Z}|\theta+2\pi j|\), and for \(0<a\le\pi\) define the triangular geodesic taper
\[
T_a(\theta)=\left(1-\frac{d(\theta)}a\right)_+.
\]
Fix \(0<b<a\le\pi\). The signed two-scale stationary kernel is
\[
K_{a,b,\lambda}(x,y)=T_a(x-y)-\lambda T_b(x-y),\qquad\lambda\ge0.
\]
Write \(\Lambda(a,b)\) for the largest \(\lambda\ge0\) such that \(K_{a,b,\lambda}\) is positive semidefinite on **every** finite circle configuration. Define \(z=e^{ia}\), \(w=e^{ib}\).

**Theorem (exact subtraction criterion and threshold).** The threshold is positive if and only if \(w\in\langle z\rangle\), equivalently if and only if there exists an integer \(k\) with
\[
b\equiv ka\pmod{2\pi}.
\]
If this condition fails, \(\Lambda(a,b)=0\). If it holds, the kernel is positive semidefinite exactly for \(0\le\lambda\le\Lambda(a,b)\); in particular \(0<\Lambda(a,b)<1\). The positive threshold is as follows.

* If \(a/(2\pi)\) is irrational, there is a unique such integer \(k\), necessarily \(|k|\ge2\), and
\[
\boxed{\Lambda(a,b)=\frac{b}{a k^2}.}
\]
All Fourier coefficients of \(K_{a,b,\lambda}\) are strictly positive throughout \(0\le\lambda\le\Lambda(a,b)\), including equality. Hence these kernels are strictly positive definite on any finite set of distinct circle points and their translation-invariant kernel spaces are dense in \(C(\mathbb T)\).

* If \(a/(2\pi)=A/Q\) in lowest terms, then \(1\le A\le Q/2\), and admissibility requires \(b=2\pi B/Q\) with an integer \(1\le B<A\). In that case
\[
\boxed{\Lambda(a,b)=\frac{B}{A}\left(\max_{1\le r\le Q-1}\frac{\sin^2(\pi rB/Q)}{\sin^2(\pi rA/Q)}\right)^{-1}.}
\]
The displayed denominators never vanish. None of the positive-semidefinite members, including \(\lambda=0\), is strictly positive definite on all finite distinct-site configurations.

**Arithmetic discontinuity.** For every fixed irrational \(a/(2\pi)\), the function \(b\mapsto\Lambda(a,b)\) on \((0,a)\) is positive on a countable dense set and zero on its dense complement. It is continuous at every zero and discontinuous at every positive point, a Thomae-type (popcorn) law. If \(a/(2\pi)\) is rational, it has finitely many positive points.

**Explicit family.** For every odd integer \(Q\ge5\), choosing \(a=4\pi/Q\), \(b=2\pi/Q\) gives
\[
\Lambda(a,b)=1-\cos(\pi/Q)\sim\frac{\pi^2}{2Q^2}.
\]
For \(a=\pi/\sqrt2\) and \(b=2\pi-2a\), the irrational-order-two example has \(\Lambda(a,b)=(\sqrt2-1)/2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The statements concern real nonnegative subtraction weight \(\lambda\), angular geodesic radius \(0<b<a\le\pi\), the full continuous circle, and positive semidefiniteness of Gram matrices for **arbitrary** finite sampling locations. They are not assertions about a single fixed polygon, non-geodesic support, or higher-dimensional spheres. The zero-scale coefficient is included. Positive definiteness means nonnegative Hermitian quadratic forms; strict positive definiteness means positivity for every nonzero coefficient vector on distinct sample sites. Fourier coefficients use \(\widehat f(n)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(\theta)e^{-in\theta}\,d\theta\).

## Proof

**1. Exact Fourier reduction.** Direct integration on \([-a,a]\) gives
\[
\widehat T_a(0)=\frac a{2\pi},\qquad
\widehat T_a(n)=\frac{1-\cos(na)}{\pi a n^2}
=\frac{2\sin^2(na/2)}{\pi a n^2}\quad(n\ne0).
\]
This is nonnegative and summable. The Fourier series therefore represents the continuous kernel. The elementary positive-type criterion on \(\mathbb T\) says that every finite Gram matrix is positive semidefinite if and only if all Fourier coefficients are nonnegative. The forward implication also follows by approximating trigonometric integral quadratic forms with discrete Gram forms. Thus for \(n\ne0\) the sharp constraint is
\[
\lambda\frac ab\left|\frac{1-w^n}{1-z^n}\right|^2\le1
\]
whenever \(z^n\ne1\), and \(w^n=1\) is necessary if \(z^n=1\) and \(\lambda>0\). The zero-mode constraint is \(\lambda\le a/b\).

**2. The irrational character-rigidity lemma.** Suppose \(z\) has infinite order and all the nonzero-frequency constraints hold for some \(\lambda>0\). For some finite \(C>0\) they imply
\[
|1-w^n|\le C|1-z^n|\qquad(n\in\mathbb Z).
\]
Define \(F(z^n)=w^n\) on the dense cyclic subgroup generated by \(z\). This is a well-defined homomorphism, and for any \(m,n\), multiplying by unit scalars turns the displayed inequality for \(n-m\) into
\[
|F(z^n)-F(z^m)|\le C|z^n-z^m|.
\]
Consequently \(F\) extends uniquely to a continuous group homomorphism \(\mathbb T\to\mathbb T\). Every continuous circle character has the form \(F(\zeta)=\zeta^k\) for a unique \(k\in\mathbb Z\). This follows directly by lifting its argument to a continuous additive function on \(\mathbb R\), whose integer winding determines \(k\). Hence \(w=z^k\). Conversely, if \(w=z^k\), the finite geometric-series identity gives
\[
|1-z^{kn}|\le |k|\,|1-z^n|.
\]
Since \(z^n\) is dense and accumulates at 1, the best constant is exactly \(|k|\), so \(\Lambda=b/(a k^2)\) subject to the zero-mode check below. Because \(0<b<a\le\pi\), \(k\) cannot be \(0,1,-1\). For \(|k|\ge2\), the triangle inequality for a sum of consecutive unit powers is *strict* unless \(z^n=1\), which never occurs; thus at the critical threshold every nonzero Fourier coefficient remains strictly positive.

**3. The rational case.** When \(z\) has order \(Q\), the frequency \(n=Q\) has \(\widehat T_a(Q)=0\). For \(\lambda>0\), this forces \(w^Q=1\), equivalently \(b=2\pi B/Q\) for an integer \(1\le B<A\). Conversely, that relation ensures all zeros of the wider-tent coefficients are also zeros of the narrower-tent coefficients; the remaining inequalities are periodic in \(n\) modulo \(Q\). Taking their finite sharp maximum gives the displayed rational formula. Its denominators cannot vanish for \(1\le r<Q\) because \(\gcd(A,Q)=1\).

**4. The zero Fourier mode never binds.** In either admissible case let \(M^2\) be the finite supremum of \(|(1-w^n)/(1-z^n)|^2\) over \(n\ne0\) where the denominator is nonzero. The nonzero Fourier constraints yield \(\Lambda=(b/a)/M^2\). Summing the pointwise majorization \(\widehat T_b(n)\le(a/b)M^2\widehat T_a(n)\) over all \(n\ne0\) and using Fourier inversion at zero gives
\[
1-\frac b{2\pi}\le\frac ab M^2\left(1-\frac a{2\pi}\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
\Lambda\le\frac{1-a/(2\pi)}{1-b/(2\pi)}<1<\frac ab.
\]
So the zero Fourier coefficient of the signed kernel is strictly positive at the sharp positive threshold, and the stated classifications follow without an omitted restriction.

**5. Strictness and arithmetic topology.** In the irrational admissible case all Fourier coefficients are positive, as just shown. For any distinct sites \(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_m\), a vanishing Gram quadratic form would force \(\sum_j c_j e^{in\theta_j}=0\) for every integer \(n\). Taking \(n=0,1,\dots,m-1\) and using the invertible Vandermonde matrix of distinct \(e^{i\theta_j}\) forces every \(c_j=0\), contradiction. Positivity of all Fourier modes also ensures \(C(\mathbb T)\)-universality, since their characters lie in the associated RKHS and trigonometric polynomials are uniformly dense.

If \(a=2\pi A/Q\), all Fourier coefficients at nonzero multiples of \(Q\) vanish whenever the subtraction is admissible (and already for \(\lambda=0\)). On the \(2Q\) equally spaced sites \(\theta_j=\pi j/Q\), the nonzero vector \(c_j=(-1)^j\) has Fourier sum supported only at indices congruent to \(Q\) modulo \(2Q\), so its Gram form is exactly zero.

For a fixed irrational \(a/(2\pi)\), the fractional parts of \(ka/(2\pi)\) are dense, yielding density of the countably many admissible \(b\). Their associated integer \(k\) is unique. If a sequence of distinct admissible widths \(b_j\to b_0\in(0,a)\), the corresponding integers obey \(|k_j|\to\infty\); otherwise a bounded subsequence would yield a constant width. Thus \(\Lambda(a,b_j)=b_j/(a k_j^2)\to0\). This proves continuity at each zero and jump discontinuity at each positive threshold.

Finally, for odd \(Q\ge5\), \(A=2,B=1\) gives
\[
\max_{1\le r<Q}\frac{\sin^2(\pi r/Q)}{\sin^2(2\pi r/Q)}
=\frac1{4\sin^2(\pi/(2Q))},
\]
with the maximizers \(r=(Q\pm1)/2\). Substitution gives \(\Lambda=2\sin^2(\pi/(2Q))=1-\cos(\pi/Q)\), including its stated asymptotic. The irrational example has winding number \(k=-2\).

## Verification

The proof above treats every integer Fourier mode and all finite circle configurations; no finite spectral search substitutes for the character-rigidity argument. The standalone supplementary script `checks/check_tent.py` uses Python 3 with NumPy, SymPy, and mpmath and checks rational families, sharp-frequency failures beyond the boundary, rational Gram null vectors, a symbolic cyclotomic identity, and a high-precision irrational example. Its executed result was `PASS: 84177 supplementary checks`. Numerically tested nonadmissible radii illustrate, but do not prove, the infinite impossibility assertion. The mathematical proof does not rely on package-installation or floating-point tolerances.

## Relationship to prior work

The triangular kernel, its squared-sinc Fourier coefficients, and its use in periodic approximation are classical. Babenko, Kryakin, and Staszak (2013, Section 2, equations (2.5)–(2.6), and Section 3, equations (3.3)–(3.6)) explicitly present periodized tent convolution squares and alternating combinations of such scales; their results concern Jackson–Stechkin inequalities rather than a sharp positive-type classification of a free two-scale subtraction. Thus no novelty is claimed for the Fourier transform or for ordinary tent positive definiteness.

Emery and Alegría (first public 23 January 2021) study compactly supported Euclidean covariance families, including truncated powers, and derive multivariate covariance conditions. Their sphere-free Euclidean setting and positive-mixture constructions do not imply the irrational circle-character obstruction, rational finite-threshold arithmetic, or dense discontinuity law proved here. Buhmann and Jäger (first public 6 May 2021, especially their circle section) give general coefficient-structural criteria for strict positive definiteness of spherical convolution kernels, consistent with but not quantitatively resolving this signed two-radius problem.

The substantive claimed gap is the **full arithmetic classification and sharp signed subtraction threshold**, including the distinct rational/irrational strictness and the pointwise discontinuity structure. This is an implication-level distinction, not an assertion that these named works are exhaustive. Additional historical sources may contain an equivalent formulation, particularly within the older Schoenberg, dilation-order, and periodic-spline literature.

## Limitations

The classification applies to first-power compact tents on the continuous one-dimensional torus, only \(\lambda\ge0\) and \(0<b<a\le\pi\). It does not classify arbitrary polynomial or smooth compact tapers, general signed combinations of three or more ranges, higher-dimensional spheres, or positive definiteness restricted to one fixed discretization. Rational thresholds are exact finite maxima, not claimed to have a simpler universal closed form. Distinct-circle strict positive definiteness and universality are mathematical conclusions of the positive spectral support, not statements of numerical conditioning near irrational resonances. Historical priority remains subject to independent scholarly review.
