# Arithmetic splitting of fourfold-contact Poncelet conics

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), where \(q=p^r\) and \(p\) is an odd prime. Let \((C,D)\) be an **ordered** pair of distinct smooth plane conics defined over \(k\), such that their scheme-theoretic geometric intersection is supported at one point \(P\), with intersection multiplicity four. A *rational simple Poncelet \(p\)-gon* is a cyclic arrangement of \(p\) pairwise distinct \(k\)-rational points on \(C\), each consecutive chord tangent to \(D\), modulo cyclic rotations and reversal.

There are exactly two \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\)-orbits of such ordered conic pairs. Every pair admits coordinates
\[
C:\;XZ-Y^2=0,\qquad D:\;XZ-Y^2+\lambda X^2=0,\qquad \lambda\in k^\times,
\]
with contact point \(P=[0:0:1]\), and the two orbits are distinguished by the sign \(\varepsilon=\chi_q(-\lambda)\in\{+1,-1\}\) of the quadratic character. The sign is independent of all choices of normalized coordinates.

Define the **rational tangent-chord graph** with vertex set \(C(k)\), joining two distinct rational points precisely when their chord is tangent to \(D\). For \(\varepsilon=-1\) this graph is edgeless and the pair supports **no** rational simple Poncelet polygons. For \(\varepsilon=+1\), the graph is a disjoint union of one isolated vertex \(P\) and exactly \(q/p\) cycles of length \(p\). Consequently its number of rational simple Poncelet \(p\)-gons is
\[
\boxed{N_q(C,D)=\frac{q}{2p}(1+\chi_q(-\lambda)).}
\]
In particular, the split case supports exactly \(q/p=p^{r-1}\) polygons, including **one over the prime field**, unlike the triple-contact case. For every \(n\ge1\), the same conic pair after extension to \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\) supports precisely
\[
\boxed{N_{q^n}(C,D)=\frac{q^n}{2p}\bigl(1+\chi_q(-\lambda)^n\bigr).}
\]
Each projective orbit has cardinality \(q^2(q^3-1)(q^2-1)/2\). Hence, among all ordered smooth conic pairs with fourfold contact, exactly half lie in each arithmetic class.

## Assumptions and scope

All conics are projective, geometrically smooth, and defined over the same finite field of odd characteristic. Intersection multiplicity four means the length-four intersection subscheme is supported at one geometric point, not four distinct points, two doubled points, or type \((3,1)\). Smoothness of the conics and the odd-characteristic hypothesis are essential. The pair is ordered: the outer conic \(C\) supplies polygon vertices and the inner conic \(D\) supplies tangent edges. The graph allows no loops; tangency at a vertex without a second distinct vertex is not counted as an edge. Polygon counts identify cyclic rotation and reversal, but not other projective equivalences of polygons. All vertices, edges, and tangency points must be rational over the field being counted. The empty case includes possible geometric polygons not defined over \(k\).

## Proof

**1. Ground-field normal form and square-class invariant.** By uniqueness, the geometric support \(P\) of the intersection subscheme is fixed by arithmetic Frobenius and is therefore \(k\)-rational. The smooth rational conic \(C\) has a \(k\)-rational parametrization and is projectively equivalent over \(k\) to \(XZ-Y^2=0\), with \(P=[0:0:1]\). Parametrize it as \([s:t]\mapsto[s^2:st:t^2]\). A quadratic form restricted to \(C\) is a binary quartic; the condition that \(D\) meet \(C\) only at \([s:t]=[0:1]\), with multiplicity four, forces that restriction to be a nonzero multiple of \(s^4=X^2|_C\). The kernel of the restriction from plane quadrics to \(C\) is the line generated by \(XZ-Y^2\). Thus the equation of \(D\) is \(a(XZ-Y^2)+bX^2\) with \(a,b\ne0\). If \(a=0\), the equation is a doubled line, while \(b=0\) would give \(D=C\). Dividing by \(a\) gives the displayed normal form with \(\lambda=b/a\ne0\). The symmetric coefficient matrix
\[
B_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0&1/2\\0&-1&0\\1/2&0&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
has determinant \(1/4\), so every such \(D\) is smooth.

Any projectivity preserving \(C\) and \(P\) induces, on its affine parameter \(w=Y/X\), an affine map \(w\mapsto aw+b\) with \(a\in k^\times\), \(b\in k\). It is implemented by the Veronese-coordinate transformation
\[
(X,Y,Z)\mapsto (X,\;bX+aY,\;b^2X+2abY+a^2Z).
\]
The defining equation \(XZ-Y^2\) is multiplied by \(a^2\), while \(X^2\) is unchanged. Thus the normal-form coefficient \(\lambda\) transforms by multiplication or division by \(a^2\), according to coordinate direction. It follows that \(\chi_q(-\lambda)\) is a projective invariant and that any two nonzero coefficients with equal sign are projectively equivalent. There are exactly two orbits.

**2. Tangency criterion for each rational chord.** The \(q\) affine rational points of \(C\) are
\[
P_w=[1:w:w^2]\quad (w\in k),\qquad P=[0:0:1].
\]
For distinct \(u,v\in k\), their chord has homogeneous line coefficients \((uv,-u-v,1)\); the chord through \(P\) and \(P_w\) has line coefficients \((-w,1,0)\). A line with coefficients \((\ell_0,\ell_1,\ell_2)\) is tangent to a smooth conic with coefficient matrix \(B_\lambda\) precisely if \(\ell^\top\operatorname{adj}(B_\lambda)\ell=0\). Here direct calculation gives
\[
4\ell^\top\operatorname{adj}(B_\lambda)\ell
=4\ell_0\ell_2-\ell_1^2-4\lambda\ell_2^2.
\]
It follows that a chord joining two *distinct finite points* is tangent to \(D\) if and only if
\[
\boxed{(u-v)^2=-4\lambda.}
\]
For a chord through \(P\), the displayed dual expression equals \(-1\), so no such chord is tangent. Every rational tangent line to a smooth conic has a rational tangency point, since its unique geometric tangency point is fixed by Frobenius. Hence this graph captures precisely the allowed rational Poncelet edges.

**3. Complete graph decomposition and polygon count.** If \(\chi_q(-\lambda)=-1\), the equation for a chord has no solution in \(k\); the graph has no edges. If \(\chi_q(-\lambda)=+1\), choose \(h\in k^\times\) with \(h^2=-4\lambda\). The exact neighbors of \(P_u\) are \(P_{u+h}\) and \(P_{u-h}\), and \(P\) is isolated. Since \(k\) has characteristic \(p\), the additive subgroup generated by \(h\) is \(h\mathbb F_p\), of order \(p\). Thus each additive coset \(u+h\mathbb F_p\) gives one simple undirected cycle of length \(p\). These \(q/p\) cosets partition \(k\), and no additional edges exist. Because \(p\ge3\), a simple \(p\)-gon using graph edges must traverse exactly one of these cycles. Every cycle admits only the two orientations already identified by reversal. The isolated point belongs to no polygon. This proves the formula for \(N_q\) and its sharp prime-field assertion.

For a fixed \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_q^\times\), its quadratic character in \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\) is \(\chi_{q^n}(-\lambda)=\chi_q(-\lambda)^n\). The preceding graph argument with \(q^n\) in place of \(q\) proves the extension-field formula. In particular the nonsplit class first acquires rational edges and polygons over the quadratic extension, while the split class already has them over the ground field.

**4. Orbit sizes and the census of pairs.** The stabilizer of a normalized ordered pair inside \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\) must preserve \(C\) and the unique contact point. In the affine parameter transformations above it must satisfy \(a^2=1\), with arbitrary \(b\in k\). Since \(p\) is odd, its order is exactly \(2q\). As
\[
|\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_q)|=q^3(q^3-1)(q^2-1),
\]
each of the two orbits contains \(q^2(q^3-1)(q^2-1)/2\) pairs. Independently, the number of smooth conics over \(k\) is \(q^2(q^3-1)\), and each has \(q+1\) rational points available as fourfold-contact points. For every fixed \((C,P)\), the nontrivial pencil \(Q_C+\lambda L_P^2\) has precisely \(q-1\) distinct smooth other conics, where \(L_P\) denotes the tangent to \(C\) at \(P\). Hence the total is \(q^2(q^3-1)(q+1)(q-1)=q^2(q^3-1)(q^2-1)\), consistent with the two equal orbits.

## Verification

The symbolic verifier `verify_symbolic.py` checks the conic determinant, the full adjugate chord identities including the contact-point case, the affine coordinate transformation and the quartic restriction using SymPy. The standalone file `verify_contact4.py` reconstructs the finite fields \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_5\), \(\mathbb F_7\), \(\mathbb F_{11}\), \(\mathbb F_9\), \(\mathbb F_{25}\), and \(\mathbb F_{49}\) without third-party dependencies. For every nonzero coefficient in each field, it constructs all \(q+1\) rational points on the first conic, builds every distinct-point chord by cross products, checks tangency using the dual quadratic form, and independently tests the predicted connected-component structures and the equality of the two quadratic-character frequencies. It also enumerates all projective quadratic forms over \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_5\) to check the smooth-conic counts \(234\) and \(3100\). Execute from this file's directory using `python3 verify_symbolic.py` and `python3 verify_contact4.py`. All these checks passed.

The executable computation verifies the geometric graph and pair-count formulas in representative prime and extension fields; it is **not** a proof for infinitely many fields. The general assertions follow from the symbolic projective normal form, dual tangency identity, and finite-field additive-coset argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Jow and Liang's study of Poncelet's theorem in arbitrary position and characteristic proves the **geometric** periodicity in characteristic \(p\) for conic pairs with fourfold contact. Specifically, their classification of the \((4)\) intersection case identifies the incidence correspondence after geometric splitting and a translation of order \(p\). That result is used as motivation, not claimed as new. The present theorem identifies its exact arithmetic obstruction over finite fields, the complete rational tangent-chord graph and its polygon counts, all extension-degree behavior, and the two rational projective orbits. None of these specific statements follows just from geometric order-\(p\) periodicity over an algebraic closure.

The previously published rational \(p\)-gon count for **triple-contact** pairs \((3,1)\) is \((q-p)/(2p)\), and its full result explicitly excludes the \((4)\) case. The difference is structural: quadruple contact has two arithmetic square classes, with either \(q/p\) rational cycles or none, whereas triple contact has one geometric boundary coset removed and no such two-class dichotomy. Radnović and Ragas count conic pairs satisfying Poncelet triangle/tetragon conditions defined over an algebraic closure, rather than simple rational cycles and their full-contact rational graph. Hungerbühler and Kusejko treat special nested disjoint conic pencils; those cannot have fourfold intersection contact.

## Limitations

The theorem addresses only odd-characteristic *ordered* smooth conic pairs with intersection divisor \(4P\); it does not classify the characteristic-two case, reducible conics, other intersection multiplicity types, or arbitrary polygons with nonrational vertices. The graph records tangent *chords between distinct rational vertices*, not all incident tangent flags, and the isolated contact vertex may have a tangent counted only as a limiting flag. Orbit counts concern coefficient-free geometric pairs over \(\mathbb F_q\), not pairs modulo switching the two conics. The result does not assert novelty of the classical geometric Poncelet porism or of geometric \(p\)-periodicity. Equivalent arithmetic calculations may exist in older projective finite-geometry literature under different terminology.
