# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)

On normalized analytic Bergman space, monomial orthogonality gives the exact shift weights. Their self-commutator has an explicitly nonnegative diagonal. The real extension of its tail eigenvalues has a derivative with a shifted cubic numerator; at the isolated critical cubic root, the linear term in the lattice expansion vanishes. The derivative cubic localizes all potential maximizers to a fixed finite window, allowing uniform expansion and a finite quadratic maximum. On the floor sequence, the strict negative gap beyond the first two lattice sites proves the two-degree rule. Irrational rotation supplies infinitely many instances of either outcome. The exact algebra and finite example tests were checked by `verify.py`, with the proof of infinite behavior given separately.

## Originality (PASS, with residual risk)

Fleeman–Liaw (arXiv:1707.09677, 2017; full journal PDF) establish the weight and commutator framework. Revilla–Simanek (arXiv:2211.00760, 2022; full preprint) analyze the existing macroscopic degree ratio and explicitly pose the finite maximizing-index question as their Problem 6.2. Those works are not claimed as new; the assessed claim is the second-order lattice correction and the arithmetical infinite switching of two norming degrees in the critical window. Kim–Lee (2023; full open article) concerns combinations of nonharmonic symbols rather than this special two-index switching. A search among closely related published results identified harmonic Bergman and mixed commutator work on different settings, not the present critical-window theorem. Unindexed or inaccessible historical asymptotic work could affect priority.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

The two adjacent candidates are a genuinely motivated finite-index refinement of a stated open norming-mode problem. The original macroscopic transition alone cannot decide between the indices because the difference is of order \(n^{-2}\); the algebraic threshold and equidistribution force repeated, non-eventually-constant switching. This is a structural endpoint phenomenon in a natural Toeplitz operator family, not merely a preselected numerical example.

## Scope and limitations

No classification is asserted for all finite \(m,n\); no winner is asserted within an \(O(n^{-1})\) fractional-part boundary layer. The exact eigenvalues and first-order transition are prior work; only the specified second-order arithmetic refinement is assessed. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
