# Self-review

**Correctness: PASS.** For any absolutely summable atomic measure on finitely many disjoint Jordan curves that annihilates all analytic polynomials, polynomial isolation separates the maximal filled Jordan disks, harmonic balayage and the general-Jordan F. and M. Riesz theorem remove each outer atomic layer, and finite forest induction removes all layers. The proof derives the diagonal commutant and the equivalence of WOT and \(\sigma(\ell^\infty,\ell^1)\), giving the asserted operator identity. No finite computation substitutes for the infinite argument.

**Originality: PASS, with bibliographic residual risk.** The closest diagonal paper, Wang–Yao (arXiv:2611.22802v1), was inspected in its HTML full text. Its abstract moment criterion, single-Jordan criterion, convergent-inner-sequence case and concentric-circle lemma do not state the arbitrary finite nonconcentric Jordan forest result. The nested two-circle case also lies outside its norm-polynomial-separation direct-sum criterion. The 2025 analytic Toeplitz treatment, read in full HTML, has a different operator class. The 2026 composition-operator paper was checked at abstract/review level; its full text was inaccessible. Search and comparison cannot prove bibliographic uniqueness, and an older analytic-measure result could entail the claim.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** Finite nesting of Jordan boundaries is a natural spectral geometry, and this completes a previously nontrivial class: no smoothness, regular point spacing, radial structure or harmonic-separation condition is imposed. The proof removes the obstacle that norm-polynomial separation cannot isolate nested loops, while retaining WOT minimal commutants. This is a structural geometric criterion, not a finite example or numerical sharpening.

The proof's reliance on classical F. and M. Riesz, Carathéodory and Mergelyan results is explicit. The result does not treat countably infinite curve families or intersections. The original source full-text access limitation and the possibility of implicit earlier coverage remain recorded.
