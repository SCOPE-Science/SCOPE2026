# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Audited by splitting according to the image and generator types. Any order-two image subgroup has retractions classified by the homomorphisms to C2 that restrict to the identity on that image. For a reflection r^c s this requires c chi(r)+chi(s)=1 in F2; the two choices of chi(r) give exactly the two distinct reflection types. For the central half-turn it requires (n/2)chi(r)=1, giving exactly two maps at n≡2 mod 4. More generally if f(r) is a reflection, fixing it forces f(s)=f(r)^(1-c), so there are no higher-order images of that type. If both images are rotations, inversion forces f(r)^2=1 and idempotence leaves just the central pair and zero. For f(r)=r^a and f(s)=r^b s, idempotence is a²=a and ab=0 modulo n. The CRT gives precisely the unitary-divisor families with d choices and image order 2n/d. Every automorphism has affine parameters (u,v) since the rotations are generated by elements of order n>2 and hence characteristic. For the CRT family it fixes a and sends b to ub+(1-a)v; the latter linear term surjects onto admissible b because 1-a is one modulo d and zero modulo n/d. It sends the reflection index c to uc+v for either reflection type; the type is preserved by f(r) being rotational versus reflective. For the central pair, parity of v toggles chi(s) and every u fixes the half-turn. The trivial map is separate by image order. Hence the enumerator and orbit-size multiset follow, including coincident sizes of distinct orbits. At n=4 the two reflection orbits both have size four with no central pair; at n=6 the CRT contributions for d=1,2,3,6 total 12 and adding the alternate reflection orbit, central pair and trivial map gives 21. The supplied verify_dihedral.py and verify_output.txt were inspected, not executed; the latter reports all even n=4 through 32, not an unbounded proof.
