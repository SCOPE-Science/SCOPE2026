# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The generator-pair classification is exhaustive for all even \(n\ge4\), including the often missed case in which a rotation generator maps to a reflection. In the rotation–reflection case, the idempotence equations reduce to CRT idempotents and one annihilator congruence; in the rotation–rotation case the central-involution alternative exists only when \(n/2\) is odd. Explicit formulas for automorphism conjugation yield transitive orbits and the stated sizes. The independent enumeration script checks every map and every automorphism orbit through \(n=32\); this is supplementary to the proof, not a substitute for it.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected material.** Johnson's all-endomorphism count establishes the surrounding space but does not give the idempotent locus, image-order polynomial, or automorphism-conjugacy quotient. The full dihedral section of Madhusudanan–Sudhakara–Seth (2023) concerns descending homomorphisms, which are only a restricted class, and their element graph. Related odd-kernel generalized dihedral retraction results have different hypotheses. Recorded searches found no matching even-dihedral all-retraction orbit classification. This is not an assertion of exhaustive historical priority: general retract/normal-complement literature, and the inaccessible related book chapter, remain residual risks.

**Value — PASS.** Group retracts are the fixed-point images of composition-idempotent endomorphisms, so their image sizes and symmetry types are intrinsic invariants. This is an all-parameter classification, not a numerical specialization: the index-two reflections create an additional orbit, the prime-power CRT structure controls the remaining orbit sizes, and a sharp \(n\equiv2\pmod4\) exception creates exactly one more orbit. These provide reusable structural information beyond unrefined counts.

**Limits.** Even integer \(n\ge4\) only. No enumeration of commuting retractions or noncyclic even generalized dihedral groups. Finite code checks are not an all-\(n\) proof; no independent external mathematical audit has been performed.
