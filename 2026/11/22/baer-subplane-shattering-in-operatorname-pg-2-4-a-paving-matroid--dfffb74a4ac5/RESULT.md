# Baer-subplane shattering in \(\operatorname{PG}(2,4)\): a paving matroid and an adaptive gap

## Finding
Let \(P\) be the 21 points of \(\operatorname{PG}(2,\mathbb F_4)\), and let \(\mathcal B\) be the 360 point sets of its Baer subplanes \(\operatorname{PG}(2,\mathbb F_2)\). For the Boolean concept class \(\{1_B:B\in\mathcal B\}\), a support \(S\subseteq P\) is shattered exactly when \(|S|\le4\) and \(S\) is **not a four-point subset of one projective line**. In particular,
\[
\Phi_{\mathcal B}(z)=1+21z+210z^2+1330z^3+5880z^4,
\qquad \operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal B)=4,
\qquad \operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal B)=5.
\]
The shattered supports form the independence complex of a rank-four paving matroid whose rank-three hyperplanes of cardinality five are precisely the 21 projective lines. Consequently its integral homotopy type is \(\bigvee^{4740}S^3\), by matroid-complex shellability and the face counts. The one-round static/adaptive gap is exact: a depth-five complete mistake tree is provided, whereas an exhaustive independent decision recurrence excludes depth six.

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient field is \(\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^2+t+1)\). Points are one-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_4^3\). A projective line contains five points; each Baer subplane is the seven-point copy of \(\operatorname{PG}(2,2)\) generated by a quadrangle. Only **point membership** is queried: a query is a point \(p\), and its label under a hidden Baer subplane \(B\) is \(1\) precisely when \(p\in B\). Every query tree may choose a point depending on earlier labels, and must realize all \(2^d\) paths at depth \(d\). No assertion about arbitrary larger finite projective planes is made.

## Proof
**Incidence counts.** Four points with no three collinear form a projective frame; its projective joins and meets generate a unique seven-point Baer subplane. There are \(2520\) such ordered-free quadrangles: \(\binom{21}{4}-21\binom54-3360=2520\). Every Fano subplane contains exactly seven quadrangles, hence there are \(2520/7=360\) subplanes. The projective collineation group is transitive on point pairs, collinear triples, noncollinear triples, quadrangles, and the configurations made of three collinear points plus one off the line. Therefore the numbers of Baer subplanes containing these respective supports are \(36,12,9,1,3\). A given point lies on \(120\) Baer subplanes; the empty support belongs to all \(360\). These numbers also follow by double-counting the corresponding configurations inside the Fano subplane.

**Four queries.** Every four-point support that is not collinear is either a quadrangle or a collinear triple with one off-line point. In those two cases the number of subplanes containing all four points is \(1\) or \(3\), respectively. For positive-trace sizes three, two and one, inclusion-exclusion gives nonempty realizations regardless of which other positions are negative. More explicitly, a triple belongs to \(9\) or \(12\) subplanes, more than the \(1\) or \(3\) containing the fourth; any chosen pair belongs to \(36\), and the two excluded points remove at most \(24\); a chosen point belongs to \(120\), and three excluded points remove at most \(108\). The completely negative trace has \(360-4(120)+6(36)-4(9)+1=61\) realizations for a quadrangle, or \(360-4(120)+6(36)-(12+3\cdot9)+3=60\) for the other type. Thus all \(16\) traces occur. A collinear four-set cannot yield the all-positive trace, because a Baer subplane meets any projective line in one or three points. Every support of size at most three extends to a shattered four-set.

**Five-query obstruction.** Suppose five points are shattered. Their all-positive trace requires containment in a Baer subplane \(B\). Every five-point subset of the Fano plane contains a quadrangle: the two omitted Fano points determine a Fano line, and its four-point complement is in the five-set. That quadrangle determines \(B\) uniquely; consequently the label assigning its four points positive and the fifth point negative cannot be realized. Hence no support of size at least five is shattered, and the coefficient of \(z^4\) is \(\binom{21}{4}-21\binom54=5880\).

**Matroid and topology.** Let the bases be precisely the four-point subsets not lying on one projective line. They satisfy basis exchange: upon deleting an element from one base, the remaining three points either are noncollinear (then every replacement is allowed), or are collinear on a unique line; in the latter case any other base omitting the deleted point must contain a replacement outside that line. Thus these bases define a rank-four paving matroid, and its independent sets are exactly the shattered supports. The standard shellability theorem for matroid independence complexes implies a wedge of three-spheres; the face polynomial gives \(\widetilde\chi=-1+21-210+1330-5880=-4740\), hence exactly \(4740\) summands.

**Adaptive dimension.** The attached `witness_tree.json` supplies one full depth-five point-query decision tree. Its 32 leaves have nonempty, path-consistent subplane sets; this is checked by `verify_python.py`. For the upper bound define \(T(C,d)\) to mean that a full depth-\(d\) binary query tree is possible using the nonempty concept subset \(C\). For each point \(p\), let \(C_{p,1}=\{B\in C:p\in B\}\) and \(C_{p,0}=C\setminus C_{p,1}\). Then \(T(C,0)\) is true exactly for nonempty \(C\), and, for \(d>0\),
\[
T(C,d)=\bigvee_{p\in P}\bigl(T(C_{p,0},d-1)\wedge T(C_{p,1},d-1)\bigr).
\]
If \(|C|<2^d\), the predicate is false. Exact finite evaluations on the enumerated 360 subplanes give \(T(\mathcal B,5)=\mathrm{true}\) and \(T(\mathcal B,6)=\mathrm{false}\). Both implementations independently reconstruct all subplanes before evaluating the recurrence.

## Verification
From a directory containing the embedded files, run `python3 verify_python.py` and `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_cpp.cpp -o verify_cpp && ./verify_cpp`. The Python version constructs the subplanes by projective-frame matrices and verifies the depth-five tree; the independent C++ version constructs them by iterative projective joins and meets. Both exhaustively check traces on all supports through size five and implement the exact decision-tree recurrence. In the completed runs, both obtained 360 distinct Baer subplanes, the coefficient vector \((1,21,210,1330,5880,0)\), and exact online dimension five. Python memoized 1625 states (across depths at most six), and C++ memoized 24182 states with opposite query ordering. The tree uses the zero-indexed, lexicographically sorted normalized projective points listed inside the certificate. All enumerations are finite and exhaustive, not statistical.

## Relationship to prior work
Szőnyi and Weiner (2021) study blocking-set stability and the geometry of Baer subplanes. Their theorems concern near-blocking configurations, predominantly at large field orders, not a Boolean point-incidence shattering family. Fitzpatrick, Iosevich, McDonald and Wyman (2021) study fixed-radius finite-field spheres, a different concept family. Coudert, Csikós, Ducoffe and Viennot (2024) develop algorithms for graph-neighborhood VC dimension, not for Baer-subplane point membership or its adaptive dimension. Classical incidence results determine the 360 subplanes and the \(2\)-design parameters but do not supply the stated trace criterion or depth-five versus depth-six separation. Closely related records on affine-line unions, Grassmann graphs, and a quartic whose rational points form a Baer subplane concern mathematically different classes; no claim of absolute historical priority is made.

## Limitations
The online upper bound is a rigorous finite exhaustive evaluation of a transparent recurrence, not an all-orders conceptual bound. This result concerns only the unique Desarguesian projective plane of order four. The homotopy conclusion uses the classical matroid shellability theorem, rather than an independently enumerated integral boundary matrix. An unindexed earlier incidence or learning-theoretic computation might contain an equivalent result; external independent mathematical review has not been performed. The public availability of the 2021 Baer-subplane stability article was established at month precision, not an exact initial day; the precise dated motivating VC reference was publicly posted on 2021-08-30.
