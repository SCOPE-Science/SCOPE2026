# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite law conditions on the first suffix observation exceeding the removed-prefix maximum; its probability is exactly \(m/[j(j-1)]\), with no-exceedance mass \(m/n\). Conditional suffix ranks are uniformly random, giving the unsigned-Stirling record distribution. The exposure identity distinguishes newly visible records from records that remain visible. Independent exact enumeration of all permutations through length eight and rational moment calculations checked these identities. The infinite law is obtained by applying the standard exponential transform to record *values*, not by incorrectly assuming independent record *times*. The resulting Poisson mixture factors into independent geometric counts. The sublinear and macroscopic limit statements follow from explicit variance estimates and a negligible short-block event. Boundaries \(m=2\), \(m=n-1\), and the censoring atom are covered.

## Originality — PASS, with identified prior coverage

Wilf (1995) and Myers–Wilf (2007) give the classical record-count and independent-relative-rank tools. Naiman–Torcaso (2022) and a later exact one-arrival analysis cover the \(m=1\) marginal through time reversal; the new finding explicitly excludes that marginal from its novelty claim. Comparisons of statements, not titles alone, identified no inspected theorem providing the *joint* marked batch law for \(m\ge2\), the independent-geometric batch factorization, or the independent Gaussian–Pareto marked scaling for \(m/n\to\alpha\in(0,1)\). The literature search cannot establish absolute priority. Broader unindexed record-process results remain the main residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The problem describes a natural reliability failure in sequential maximum/record monitoring: removing an early window can expose a random number of spurious retrospective maxima, and the time at which original and restarted histories coincide is a separate operational quantity. The exact joint law provides their dependence and the probability of no recovery. The sublinear and macroscopic regimes behave differently; in the macroscopic regime the recovery clock retains a Pareto tail and a censoring atom even though the record-gain fluctuations are Gaussian and asymptotically independent. These are more informative than the known one-arrival geometric marginal or the classical harmonic expectation.

## Limitations and nearest literature

The setting is one-dimensional, i.i.d., continuous, and restricted to an initial contiguous deletion block. The one-arrival law, classical Stirling counts, and record-value Poisson construction are credited rather than claimed as new. Some historical record-process surveys and differently indexed results may imply part or all of the batch extension. The finite verifier supports but cannot establish infinite limits.
