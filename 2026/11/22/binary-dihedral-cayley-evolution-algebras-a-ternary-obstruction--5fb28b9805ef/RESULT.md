# Binary dihedral Cayley evolution algebras: a ternary obstruction and extension-stable automorphisms

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let
\[
D_{2n}=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle.
\]
Define a commutative, generally nonassociative evolution algebra \(E_n\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) with a basis \(\{e_g:g\in D_{2n}\}\). Its defining products are \(e_ge_h=0\) when \(g\ne h\), and
\[
e_g^2=e_g+e_{gr}+e_{gs}.
\]
For every field extension \(K/\mathbb F_2\), its \(2n\times2n\) structure matrix \(M_n\) satisfies the exact rank-defect dichotomy
\[
\dim_K\ker M_n=\begin{cases}0,&3\nmid n,\\2,&3\mid n.\end{cases}
\]
Moreover, if \(3\nmid n\), then \(E_n\) is **absolutely simple**, and its full ordinary automorphism group is constant under all field extensions:
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_K(E_n\otimes_{\mathbb F_2}K)\cong D_{2n}.
\]
The group acts faithfully by left translations of the natural basis. If \(3\mid n\), the stated three-support algebra is singular and not simple. This last assertion does not exclude other constructions of simple algebras for the same dihedral groups.

## Assumptions and scope
Write group elements uniquely as \(r^a s^b\), where \(0\le a<n\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\). The three support elements \(1,r,s\) are distinct for \(n\ge3\), and all their coefficients are one. All ranks are over the displayed extension field \(K\), of characteristic two; no characteristic-zero rank transfer is assumed. “Simple” means nonzero and without nonzero proper two-sided ideals. An algebra is absolutely simple if its scalar extension to every field extension is simple. Automorphisms are all ordinary invertible \(K\)-linear maps respecting multiplication, not only permutations supplied in advance. The finite group \(D_{2n}\) has order \(2n\).

## Proof
**1. A characteristic-two rank calculation.** Identify the coordinate space indexed by \(D_{2n}\) with the group algebra \(K[D_{2n}]\). Right multiplication by
\[
w=1+r+s
\]
has matrix \(M_n\) up to the row/column convention, so its rank and nullity agree with those of \(M_n\). Set \(R=K[r]/(r^n-1)\), write \(K[D_{2n}]=R\oplus Rs\), and let \(a=1+r\). Define the involution \(u^*=u(r^{-1})\) on \(R\). The dihedral relation is \(su=u^*s\), hence for \(u,v\in R\),
\[
(u+vs)(a+s)=(au+v)+(u+a^*v)s.
\]
This is the linear map \((u,v)\mapsto(au+v,u+a^*v)\). In the kernel \(v=au\); substitution into the second coordinate gives
\[
(1+a^*a)u=(1+r+r^{-1})u=0.
\]
Consequently the full matrix nullity equals the nullity of multiplication by \(1+r+r^{-1}\) on \(R\). Multiplication by \(r\) is invertible, so this equals the nullity of multiplication by \(1+r+r^2\). In the cyclic quotient \(K[x]/(x^n-1)\), the kernel dimension of multiplication by a polynomial \(h\) equals \(\deg\gcd(h,x^n-1)\): for \(d=\gcd(h,x^n-1)\), the kernel ideal is generated by \((x^n-1)/d\), and has dimension \(\deg d\). This remains valid with repeated factors. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(1+x+x^2\) is squarefree and has roots of multiplicative order three in \(\mathbb F_4\). Thus it divides \(x^n-1\) if and only if \(3\mid n\), and otherwise its gcd with \(x^n-1\) is one. Since gcd degree does not change under field extension, the claimed zero-or-two nullity holds over every \(K/\mathbb F_2\).

**2. Connectivity and simplicity.** The directed natural-basis graph has arcs \(g\to g\), \(g\to gr\), and \(g\to gs\). It is strongly connected because right multiplication by \(r\) and \(s\) reaches every group element, using only positive powers of \(r\) and involutive \(s\). For an evolution algebra whose squares form a basis, strong connectivity implies simplicity: a nonzero ideal contains \(e_g^2\) for some \(g\) after multiplication by a suitable basis vector; multiplying \(e_g^2\) by each basis element attached to an outgoing edge places every corresponding basis square into the ideal, and strong connectivity propagates this to all squares. They form a basis, hence the ideal is the entire algebra. Conversely, when the square matrix is singular, the algebra square is a nonzero proper ideal, so the algebra is not simple. Step 1 is stable under all extensions, proving absolute simplicity when \(3\nmid n\) and nonsimplicity for this construction when \(3\mid n\).

**3. Every automorphism is monomial in the simple cases.** Work over any extension field \(K\). Since the squares \(e_g^2\) are independent when \(3\nmid n\), an algebra automorphism \(\phi\), written \(\phi(e_g)=\sum_h p_{hg}e_h\), satisfies for distinct \(g,k\)
\[
0=\phi(e_g)\phi(e_k)=\sum_h p_{hg}p_{hk}e_h^2,
\]
so each matrix row contains at most one nonzero entry. Invertibility forces the matrix to be monomial: \(\phi(e_g)=\lambda_g e_{\pi(g)}\), with \(\lambda_g\in K^\times\) and \(\pi\) a permutation. Each square has self-loop coefficient one. Comparing the coefficient of \(e_{\pi(g)}\) in \(\phi(e_g^2)=\phi(e_g)^2\) yields \(\lambda_g=\lambda_g^2\), hence \(\lambda_g=1\) in every field. Thus the whole algebra automorphism group is exactly the automorphism group of the uncoloured directed support graph, even over the algebraic closure.

**4. The graph automorphism group is left regular.** Remove the distinguished self-loops. The edge \(g\to gs\) is reciprocal because \(s^2=1\). The edge \(g\to gr\) is not reciprocal: its inverse is not an \(s\)-edge and \(r^2\ne1\) for \(n\ge3\). Therefore every digraph automorphism preserves separately the uniquely determined reciprocal and nonreciprocal outgoing neighbours. If \(f\) is such an automorphism, then
\[
f(gr)=f(g)r,\qquad f(gs)=f(g)s
\]
for all \(g\). Induction on words in the generators implies \(f(g)=f(1)g\). Conversely every left translation respects all three outgoing edges. Consequently the full graph automorphism group is exactly the left-regular copy of \(D_{2n}\), of order \(2n\), and so is the algebra automorphism group from Step 3.

The standalone `verify.py` constructs the group multiplication and binary structure matrices independently, computes exact binary ranks for every \(3\le n\le60\), checks the cyclotomic gcd condition in \(\mathbb F_2[x]\), and reconstructs coloured arcs from uncoloured adjacency. It additionally enumerates **all** graph automorphisms via an independent directed-graph isomorphism engine for \(3\le n\le6\). The successful replay message is `BINARY_DIHEDRAL_EVOLUTION_VERIFIED n=3..60; graph_aut=2n; corank=2*indicator(3|n)`. These checks are finite corroboration only; Steps 1–4 prove the result for all \(n\ge3\) and every extension field.

## Relationship to prior work
Costoya, Muñoz, Tocino and Viruel introduce Cayley evolution algebras, their graph realization and absolute-simplicity framework. Their Theorem A constructs a given finite group as the extension-stable automorphism group under the sufficient hypothesis \(\lvert K^\times\rvert\ge2\lvert G\rvert\), which is **not satisfied by** \(K=\mathbb F_2\). Their original construction and general observations are credited, not claimed as new. A published complete three-term dihedral-convolution nullity census concerns the regular representation **over \(\mathbb C\)** and in particular gives a zero-or-two nullity for a mixed triple there. The binary rank defect proved in Step 1 is therefore related but is not itself claimed as a previously unknown characteristic-zero observation. The substantive combined statement here is the explicit characteristic-two, all-extension absolute-simplicity criterion and exact nonabelian automorphism group for the constant-coefficient support \(\{1,r,s\}\). Another published result on maximal automorphism-group orders of simple binary evolution algebras supplies the monomial/graph reduction but not this dihedral realization. General permutation-representation constructions supply perfect evolution algebras, without implying this explicit absolutely simple binary family. No literature search establishes unconditional first discovery, and the possibility that a general theorem implies this specialization remains open to independent scrutiny.

## Limitations
The positive realization is restricted to \(n\ge3\) with \(3\nmid n\), and the mod-three nonsimplicity conclusion concerns only the displayed three-support construction. The theorem does not classify all evolution algebras with dihedral automorphism group, prove a dimension minimum, or claim uniqueness. The binary obstruction is an exact matrix statement, not evidence that other supports fail. The published complex convolution census covers an analogous component, and further implicit consequences of existing general Cayley-evolution results are a bibliographic risk. Finite computations do not certify the infinite proof; no independent mathematical audit is claimed.
