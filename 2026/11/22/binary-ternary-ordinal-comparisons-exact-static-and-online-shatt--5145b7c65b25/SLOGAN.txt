For one-sided atomic feature comparisons on an independent product of binary and ternary ordered alphabets, every palette with at least two coordinates has identical VC and Littlestone dimensions, exactly \(\lfloor\log_2(2a+b+2)\rfloor\), where \(a\) and \(b\) count ternary and binary coordinates. A perfect pairing of nontrivial Boolean subsets proves the static lower bound, whereas arbitrary balanced cuts prove the online bound; two four-valued coordinates already break equality.
