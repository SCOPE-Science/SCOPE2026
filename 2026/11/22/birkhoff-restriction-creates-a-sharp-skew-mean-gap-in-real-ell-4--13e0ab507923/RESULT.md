# Birkhoff restriction creates a sharp skew-mean gap in real \(\ell_4^2\)

## Finding
Let \(X=(\mathbb R^2,\|\cdot\|_4)\), with \(x\perp_B y\) denoting Birkhoff–James orthogonality. For every \(t>0\) define the restricted and unrestricted skew arithmetic means
\[
A_t^B=\max_{\substack{\|x\|_4=\|y\|_4=1\\x\perp_B y}}
\frac{\|x+ty\|_4+\|tx-y\|_4}{2},\quad
U_t=\max_{\|x\|_4=\|y\|_4=1}
\frac{\|x+ty\|_4+\|tx-y\|_4}{2}.
\]
The following global equality classification is exact:
\[
\boxed{A_t^B<U_t\ \text{for every }t>0,\ t\ne1;\qquad A_1^B=U_1=2^{3/4}.}
\]
Here the unrestricted value, known from prior work, is
\[
U_t=\left[1+3t^2+t^4+t\sqrt{(1+2t^2)(2+t^2)}\right]^{1/4}.
\]
More precisely, put \(c=(4/27)^{1/4}\) and \(u_0=2-\sqrt3\). The restricted constant has the sharp small-parameter profile
\[
\boxed{A_t^B=1+\frac c2t+\frac1{\sqrt3}t^2+
\frac{7\sqrt3-9}{18}c\,t^3+O(t^4)\qquad(t\downarrow0).}
\]
For all sufficiently small positive \(t\), every maximizing ordered pair is in one orbit of simultaneous signed-coordinate permutations of the unique representative
\[
x_t=\frac{(1,u_t^{1/4})}{(1+u_t)^{1/4}},\qquad
 y_t=\frac{(u_t^{3/4},-1)}{(1+u_t^3)^{1/4}},\qquad
 u_t=u_0+(6\sqrt3-9)c\,t+O(t^2).
\]
Thus the sharp loss caused by the Birkhoff restriction begins linearly:
\[
\boxed{U_t-A_t^B=\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{4}-\frac c2\right)t+O(t^2),\quad
\frac{\sqrt2}{4}-\frac c2>0.}
\]
The unrestricted formula and the leading reverse-orthogonality residual \(c\) are prior ingredients, not asserted as new standalone results. The global strictness, the restricted higher-order law, and the moving extremizer are the joint finding.

## Assumptions and scope
All vector components and parameters are real. The norm is \(\|(a,b)\|_4=(|a|^4+|b|^4)^{1/4}\). The maxima exist by compactness. The relation \(x\perp_B y\) means \(\|x+\lambda y\|_4\ge\|x\|_4\) for every real \(\lambda\), equivalently \(x_1^3y_1+x_2^3y_2=0\) for a unit \(x\). The global statement is for each real \(t>0\), whereas the expansion and uniqueness classification hold only for all \(0<t<\epsilon\) for some \(\epsilon>0\). The \(O(\cdot)\) bounds are uniform in that interval. The term 'orbit' concerns ordered pairs under a common signed permutation; it does not mean independently changing the two signs.

## Proof
**1. The unrestricted top-eigenline and its equality cases.** For the linear map \(M_t(a,b)=(a+tb,ta-b)\), set \(R=a^2-b^2\), \(Q=2ab\). The fourth powers satisfy
\[
a^4+b^4=\tfrac12(2R^2+Q^2),\qquad
\|M_t(a,b)\|_4^4=\tfrac12\left[(1+t^2)^2(R^2+Q^2)+((1-t^2)R+2tQ)^2\right].
\]
Consequently \(m_t^4=\|M_t\|_{4\to4}^4\) is the largest generalized eigenvalue of
\[
K_t=\begin{pmatrix}2(1+t^4)&2t(1-t^2)\\2t(1-t^2)&1+6t^2+t^4\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{relative to}\quad \operatorname{diag}(2,1).
\]
Its characteristic equation is
\[
\lambda^2-2(1+3t^2+t^4)\lambda+(1+t^2)^4=0,
\]
so \(m_t=U_t\) is the displayed known unrestricted value. Indeed, putting \(P=\|x+ty\|_4\), \(Q'=\|tx-y\|_4\) and applying the scalar power-mean inequality and the matrix-norm inequality separately to both coordinate rows \((x_i,y_i)\) gives
\[
\frac{P+Q'}2\le\left(\frac{P^4+(Q')^4}2\right)^{1/4}
\le m_t\left(\frac{\|x\|_4^4+\|y\|_4^4}2\right)^{1/4}=m_t.
\]
The upper bound is attained by the standard two-row construction \(x=(a,b)/\|(a,b)\|_4\), \(y=(b,-a)/\|(a,b)\|_4\) whenever \((a,b)\) is a maximizing input for \(M_t\); its two output norms are equal. This also directly establishes the unrestricted formula without assuming equality in an unknown inequality.

For \(t\ne1\), the off-diagonal entry \(2t(1-t^2)\) is nonzero. The generalized top eigenvalue is simple and its \((R,Q)\) eigenvector has both components nonzero. As \((R,Q)=(a^2-b^2,2ab)\), the norming inputs \((a,b)\) form exactly two perpendicular real lines generated by \((a,b)\) and \((b,-a)\), with \(ab(a^2-b^2)\ne0\). If unit \(x,y\) attain \(U_t\), equality in the coordinatewise norm bound forces each nonzero row \((x_i,y_i)\) onto one of these lines. If both rows lie on the same line, then \(y\) is proportional to \(x\) with a factor of modulus different from one, contradicting the two unit norms. Therefore the rows lie on different lines; after relabeling, write
\[
(x_1,y_1)=h(a,b),\qquad(x_2,y_2)=k(b,-a).
\]
The unit norms imply \((|h|^4-|k|^4)(a^4-b^4)=0\), hence \(|h|=|k|>0\). But then
\[
x_1^3y_1+x_2^3y_2=|h|^4ab(a^2-b^2)\ne0.
\]
No unrestricted maximizer is Birkhoff-orthogonal, so compactness gives \(A_t^B<U_t\). At \(t=1\) take \(x=2^{-1/4}(1,1)\), \(y=2^{-1/4}(1,-1)\), for which \(x\perp_By\) and the common objective is \(2^{3/4}\). This proves the global assertion and all boundary cases \(t>0\).

**2. Exact one-dimensional restricted reduction.** The isometry group reduces any ordered Birkhoff pair to \(x=(1,s)/(1+s^4)^{1/4}\) with \(0\le s\le1\), and one of the two signs of \(y=(s^3,-1)/(1+s^{12})^{1/4}\). Set \(u=s^4\) and \(q(u)=1-u+u^2\). Define
\[
J(u)=\frac{u^{1/4}(1-u)}{q(u)^{3/4}},\qquad
B(u)=\sqrt{\frac{u}{q(u)}}.
\]
For the indicated sign of \(y\) one computes
\[
\|x+ty\|_4^4=1+6B(u)t^2-4J(u)t^3+t^4,\quad
\|tx-y\|_4^4=1+4J(u)t+6B(u)t^2+t^4.
\]
Thus the pair objective is the half-sum of the positive fourth roots on the right. Replacing \(y\) by \(-y\) sends \(J\) to \(-J\) while preserving \(B\). The formulas are exact for every \(u\in[0,1]\) and \(t>0\).

**3. Uniform Taylor expansion and the unique extremizer.** Uniformly over \(u\in[0,1]\) as \(t\downarrow0\), the indicated orientation has expansion
\[
F(u,t)=1+f_1(u)t+f_2(u)t^2+f_3(u)t^3+O(t^4),
\]
where
\[
f_1=J/2,\quad f_2=3B/2-3J^2/4,\quad
f_3=-J/2-9JB/4+7J^3/4.
\]
The opposite orientation has leading linear term \(-J/2\). Since \(J\ge0\), with strict positivity in the interior, and
\[
J(u)^4=\frac{u(1-u)^4}{(1-u+u^2)^3},\qquad
\frac{J'(u)}{J(u)}=-\frac{(u+1)(u^2-4u+1)}{4u(u-1)(u^2-u+1)},
\]
the sole global maximum of \(J\) lies at \(u_0=2-\sqrt3\). It is strict and nondegenerate. Uniform convergence shows all maximizing ratios tend to \(u_0\), with the favorable orientation. On an interior neighborhood of \(u_0\), the exact objective is analytic in \((u,t)\); its second \(u\)-derivative has the negative leading term \(f_1''(u_0)t\). Consequently the analytic implicit-function theorem and local strict concavity give one maximizing ratio \(u_t\), and no other maximizing orbit, for every sufficiently small \(t>0\).

At \(u_0\), exact differentiation gives
\[
J(u_0)=c,\ B(u_0)=1/\sqrt3,\quad
f_1''(u_0)=-\frac{7+4\sqrt3}{4}c,\quad
f_2(u_0)=1/\sqrt3,\quad f_2'(u_0)=1+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
The stationarity equation \(\partial_uF(u_t,t)=0\) therefore yields
\[
u_t-u_0=-\frac{f_2'(u_0)}{f_1''(u_0)}t+O(t^2)=(6\sqrt3-9)c\,t+O(t^2).
\]
Substituting back, the third-order optimized coefficient is
\[
f_3(u_0)-\frac{f_2'(u_0)^2}{2f_1''(u_0)}
=\frac{7\sqrt3-9}{18}c.
\]
This proves the restricted expansion and the extremizer motion. Taylor expanding the explicit unrestricted value gives \(U_t=1+(\sqrt2/4)t+(9/16)t^2+O(t^3)\). Subtraction produces the sharp first-order penalty, whose coefficient is positive since \(c<1/\sqrt2\).

The standalone `verify.py` (with `requirements.txt`) checks the exact quartic identity, the generalized eigenvalue characteristic polynomial, the leading critical-point polynomial and all coefficients at the algebraic stationary ratio. It also performs 3,300 deterministic supplementary checks of Birkhoff orthogonality, exact norm reductions and pointwise upper bounds at positive parameters. Run `python verify.py` after `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt`. These finite samples are not a proof of any all-parameter or asymptotic assertion. The proof above establishes those assertions by strict equality conditions, compactness, uniform analytic expansion and the implicit-function theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Qi et al. (2026, Section 2) define \(A_t^B\), give universal estimates and characterize Banach-space phenomena; their published treatment does not determine the present sharp real-quartic-plane gap or its asymptotic maximizer. Ni, Liu and Zhou (2025, Sections 3–4) study the unrestricted skew arithmetic mean and a separate *isosceles*-restricted variant, not the Birkhoff-restricted quartic plane. The exact unrestricted \(\ell_4\) formula and its matrix-eigenvalue derivation already occur in the public research record cited below. A different public result gives the sharp small-skew expansion of the *difference* constant \(D_t^B\) on the same plane, with a quadratic rather than linear optimizer displacement. Although both use the same leading Birkhoff support-functional residual, that difference result does not imply the present sum objective, full global strictness or its higher-order coefficients. Existing orthogonality-angle and other Banach invariants concern different functionals. No statement of bibliographic uniqueness is made beyond the sources compared.

## Limitations
The classification concerns the real two-dimensional quartic plane, not general \(\ell_p\), complex spaces or higher dimensions. A closed expression for \(A_t^B\) at arbitrary positive \(t\) is not asserted, only its exact strict comparison with the known unrestricted value. The local optimizer statement has no explicit numerical radius \(\epsilon\); existence is proved. Uniform remainder bounds are local. The unrestricted formula and the leading coefficient \(c\) have prior formulations. An equivalent but unindexed published result remains a bibliographic risk; no independent scientific audit is represented.
