# Scientific review: image-block entropy condensation

## Correctness — PASS
The finite weight \(W_n(\phi)=\prod_i J_{s_i}(n)\) follows by choosing fixed image points and independently specifying every other image. This is the same exact enumeration underlying Dolinka and East, Proposition 3.1, not a new count. Equation (6) is an exact logarithmic rewriting of each positive summand. Its correction term is globally bounded and uniformly vanishes on compact positive intervals; the coercive strictly concave exponent \(s\log t-t\) proves both the logarithmic saddle and the rank concentration. Finite summation over the \(m\)-block idempotents gives (1), strict convexity of \(s\log s\) gives (2), and (3)–(4) follow by finite mixtures. The explicit direct-composition checker passed for five finite configurations including 9028 maps at three blocks of size three and 2073 maps at four blocks of size two. Finite tests corroborate but do not establish asymptotic statements.

## Originality — PASS, with risk
The exact idempotent count and classification in Dolinka and East (2014, Proposition 3.1 and 3.2) are expressly prior work, including their direct dependence on block-fiber sizes. The original claim is not that one can enumerate the idempotents, nor a finite specialization; it is the full **exponential-rate probability law for every induced block-idempotent pattern**, the sharp rate hierarchy by active block number, and the simultaneous rank scaling for fixed block count and growing point block size. These need a separate uniform Laplace principle and a discrete entropy maximization. Dolinka, East and Mitchell (2015) treat a more general nonuniform finite enumeration, but not this limiting law. The 2026 partial-idempotent result treats partial maps and finite coefficient enumerations. Krattenthaler and Müller (2003) analyze a different wreath-product scaling regime with a fixed finite group base. A general unpublished probability-law result or an implicit saddle derivation from known exact sums remains possible; exact finite formulas determine the probabilities, so this is an asymptotic rather than enumerative contribution.

## Value — PASS
The large-block behavior is structurally surprising: almost every retraction collapses all source blocks into **one** target block even though all \(k\)-block image patterns exist. Exact exponential suppression costs describe every rare number of active image blocks and single out one large rooted-star component. The point-image rank remains of order \(mn/\log n\) even in each fixed rare block pattern. This supplies interpretable probabilistic structure and a sharp entropy hierarchy for a canonical finite transformation monoid, not a marginal restatement of a known total.
