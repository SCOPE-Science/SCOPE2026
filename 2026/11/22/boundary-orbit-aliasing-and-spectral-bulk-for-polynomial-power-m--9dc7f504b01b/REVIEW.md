## correctness

PASS

Direct integration on the normalized monomial of degree n gives each coefficient a_l times n/(kn+l) times b_n/b_(kn+l). For a fixed l this weight tends to k^(-(alpha+3)/2), including the separate Hardy calculation 1/k. For finitely many l the errors are compact diagonal-weighted shifts; the constant input is only a finite-rank discrepancy. The mutually orthogonal residue ranges reduce the Gram of the model to the sum of the Hardy multiplication Grams T_|g_r|^2. The real continuous symbol then gives exactly the interval between the minimum and maximum in the Calkin spectrum. The radial derivative identity and analyticity give a one-dimensional kernel, so zero in the essential Gram spectrum rules out closed range, and a positive essential lower bound implies it. A symbol zero is equivalent to the vanishing of g at every point of that boundary k-fiber. The first kN output coefficients impose an upper bound N on the dimension of the projected range, forcing infinite orthogonal defect. Finite-band moments of Toeplitz finite sections differ from the bulk moment only near their ends. For a polynomial test function, a telescoping expansion controls normalized trace differences by a constant times rank/N for a fixed finite-rank difference; a compact difference follows by norm approximation. Uniform polynomial approximation then proves the continuous-test limit for the positive Gram, not for the nonnormal companion.

## value

PASS

The proof identifies which geometric obstruction matters: one boundary zero can leave the range closed, while a full preimage fiber of zeros destroys it even with the same finite-dimensional kernel and infinite defect. For instance, the unweighted k=2 examples g=1-z and g=1-z^2 have respectively a singleton positive essential Gram spectrum and an interval touching zero. The single explicit residue-energy model predicts both the range dichotomy and the distribution of singular-value squares in natural monomial truncations; it adds information not recoverable from an exact upper-edge norm alone.
