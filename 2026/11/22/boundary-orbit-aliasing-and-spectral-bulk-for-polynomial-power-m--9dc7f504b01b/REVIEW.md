The exact normalized monomial coefficient identity, Gamma-ratio tail, compact diagonal-shift error, orthogonal residue decomposition and Hardy Toeplitz Gram identity reconstruct the entire Calkin formula. The finite-band symbol determines the essential spectrum and closed-range criterion; direct differentiation gives the one-dimensional kernel, and coefficient projection dimension proves non-Fredholmness. Finite-section moments and compact-error approximation prove the Szegő bulk. The standalone code passed 2368 supplementary finite assertions; infinity is handled analytically, not by extrapolation.

The supported additional claim is the unified \(k\)-channel positive Calkin Gram classification, complete root-orbit closed-range condition, and normalized finite-section eigenvalue distribution. Source retrieval for the earlier antipodal text and full general weighted-composition comparison was incomplete; a broader theorem could subsume this claim and remains the main risk. A failed exact search is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result explains when polynomial-symbol boundary zeros actually destroy stable inversion: the obstruction is a whole boundary preimage orbit rather than one zero. Alongside the exact essential spectral interval and bulk distribution this offers a complete, motivated stability picture of a natural family rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. The examples \(g(z)=1-z\) and \(g(z)=1-z^2\), with \(k=2\), show closed and nonclosed range respectively even though both symbols have boundary zeros. The classification has concrete uses for selecting polynomial weights and interpreting finite-section Gram spectra.

Only polynomial symbols, monomial composition and fixed Hilbert weights are treated. Full earlier slant primary text remained inaccessible, leaving an implication-based originality risk. No ordinary nonnormal operator spectrum or edge rate is asserted.
