# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The null-current identity follows from the explicit inverse covariance of two correlated standard Gaussian blocks. Gaussian-square-integrability justifies marginal weighted-divergence identities by cutoff integration. Mixed derivatives force affine fields when both canonical correlations are nonzero; the marginal Gaussian equations force the affine parts to be skew rotations. Unequal singular values kill both rotations, while equal positive singular values leave only the common rotation. The rank-one boundary is handled separately using one-dimensional Gaussian divergence and integrability, so there is no unjustified division by a zero canonical correlation. For independent blocks, smooth compactly supported radial rotations give infinite dimension. The known fixed diffusion covariance and a stable Ornstein–Uhlenbeck construction establish nonvacuous stationary-drift identification and ambiguity. Exact rational polynomial nullity checks agree for degrees through three, but are not substituted for the general analytic argument.

## Originality

**PASS against the inspected literature, subject to residual historical priority uncertainty.** Schwank and Drton, arXiv:2611.11288v1, Theorems 1.1 and 3.4, explicitly assume no directed reciprocal two-cycles; both blocks of the new theorem contain one. Dettling et al., arXiv:2209.03835, Theorem 32 and Appendix A Example 42, discuss reciprocal cycles and correlated known noise, including a three-node linear counterexample to the diagonal-noise obstruction. That is a close mechanistic precursor; it does not classify all square-integrable nonlinear block-local null fields or derive the two-canonical-correlation resonance. Garcez Vicente and Salehkaleybar, arXiv:2610.31310, Theorem 1, concern linear drift matrices, generic identification and known diagonal diffusion, so their hypotheses do not imply the present pointwise nonlinear rigidity statement. Published-record semantic searches and accessible prior claims did not identify an equivalent statement; failure to find one is not a proof of historical uniqueness.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Reciprocal feedback pairs are a central obstruction to equilibrium-only drift identification. This theorem gives an exact, complete four-dimensional exception criterion in terms of observable Gaussian canonical correlations: one rank-one correlation already eliminates every nonlinear local current, unequal positive canonical correlations enforce rigidity, but repeated nonzero canonical correlations retain exactly one coordinated irreversible circulation. This is stronger than showing an isolated example and locates a sharp symmetry boundary for a natural two-block feedback system.

## Scope and residual risk

The covariance is restricted to standard-Gaussian two-dimensional marginal blocks with arbitrary admissible cross-covariance; the noise covariance is known and may have cross-block correlations. The conclusion does not address unknown diffusion, larger blocks, non-Gaussian laws or finite-sample estimation. A broader specialized source not found by the documented searches could affect historical priority. The full-statement proof is analytical; exact polynomial checks only diagnose finite-degree cases.
