# Canonical ideal representatives of right-ideal isomorphism classes in rooted-forest incidence algebras

## Finding
Let \(P\) be any finite rooted-forest poset: the ancestors of each vertex form a chain. Let \(k\) be an arbitrary field and \(A=I(P,k)\) its incidence algebra. A profile is an integer assignment \(r=(r_v)_{v\in P}\) with \(0\le r_v\le d(v)\), where \(d(v)\) is the number of ancestors of \(v\) including itself, and \(r_{p(v)}\le r_v\) for every nonroot vertex, with \(p(v)\) its parent and root-parent values defined as zero.

The following three sets are naturally in bijection:

1. Isomorphism classes of right ideals of \(A\), considered as right \(A\)-modules;
2. Two-sided ideals of \(A\), which give **unique** representatives of those isomorphism classes;
3. Admissible profiles \(r\) as above.

Explicitly, number the ancestors of \(v\) from the root to \(v\). The unique representative of profile \(r\) is
\[
B_r=\operatorname{span}_k\{e_{uv}:u\le v,\ \operatorname{position}_v(u)\le r_v\}.
\]
Every right ideal \(J\) has the unique profile \(r_v=\dim_k(Je_{vv})\), and \(J\cong_A B_r\). Hence the regular right \(A\)-module is iso-multiplication. It is a classical multiplication module precisely when \(P\) is an antichain.

The embedding order on right-ideal isomorphism classes coincides with coordinatewise order on profiles and with inclusion of canonical ideals. It is therefore a finite distributive lattice. Write \(m_v=r_v-r_{p(v)}\), interpreting root-parent values as zero; then
\[
J\cong_A\bigoplus_{v\in P}(e_{vv}A)^{\oplus m_v},\qquad
\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_A(B_r,B_s)=\sum_{v\in P}m_v s_v.
\]
Over \(k=\mathbb F_q\), the precise automorphism-group order and number of distinct embedded right ideals of profile \(r\) are
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}_A(B_r)|=q^{\sum_{u<v}m_um_v}\prod_{v\in P}|\operatorname{GL}_{m_v}(\mathbb F_q)|,
\qquad
N_r(q)=\prod_{v\in P}{d(v)-r_{p(v)}\brack r_v-r_{p(v)}}_q,
\]
where the first sum is over strictly comparable vertices, Gaussian coefficients use their standard convention, and \(\operatorname{GL}_0\) has order one. The number of isomorphism classes depends only on the rooted forest, not the field; for a chain with \(n\) vertices it is the Catalan number \(\frac{1}{n+2}\binom{2n+2}{n+1}\), while for a star consisting of one root and \(s\) leaves it is \(3^s+2^s\).

## Assumptions and scope
The poset \(P\) is finite and may be disconnected. Each nonroot vertex has a unique parent and exactly one saturated chain from its root. The incidence-algebra basis consists of \(e_{uv}\) for \(u\le v\), with \(e_{uv}e_{xy}=\delta_{vx}e_{uy}\). Right ideals and two-sided ideals are subspaces of the *fixed*, labeled algebra, not merely abstract modules. An iso-multiplication right module \(M\) means that each submodule \(J\) is isomorphic to \(MB\) for some two-sided ideal \(B\), not necessarily equal to \(MB\). The projective decomposition is an isomorphism of right modules; it is not asserted to respect the embedding in \(A\). The group-order and embedded-ideal counts require a finite field; the classification and Hom formula hold over any field.

## Proof
**1. Right ideals are compatible column flags.** The orthogonal diagonal idempotents give \(J=\bigoplus_{v\in P}Je_{vv}\). Regard \(U_v=Je_{vv}\) as a subspace of \(k^{\operatorname{Anc}(v)}\) by the coefficients of \(e_{uv}\), \(u\le v\). For \(v\le w\), right multiplication by \(e_{vw}\) identifies the coordinate vector in \(U_v\) with that same vector supported on the ancestors of \(v\), now in column \(w\). Thus \(U_v\subseteq U_w\) in the natural coordinate inclusion. Conversely such compatible subspaces define a right ideal, because the diagonal idempotents and matrix units generate \(A\). The profile \(r_v=\dim U_v\) is necessarily nondecreasing along the rooted forest with \(r_v\le d(v)\).

**2. The rooted-forest splitting.** On each rooted-tree component choose a basis for \(U_v\) recursively: at a root choose any basis; when processing a child, retain its parent's basis and extend it to a basis of the child's space. For each vertex \(v\), the \(m_v=r_v-r_{p(v)}\) new basis vectors appear in \(U_v\) and persist, unchanged, in all descendant spaces. The right-module summand generated by each such vector has exactly one basis vector at every descendant vertex and no vector elsewhere, hence is isomorphic to the indecomposable projective \(e_{vv}A\). Distinct basis-vector labels give a direct sum at every vertex. Therefore
\[
J\cong_A\bigoplus_v(e_{vv}A)^{\oplus m_v}.
\]
The profile is invariant under right-module isomorphism because \(\dim_k(Je_{vv})\) is invariant. The splitting proves that equal profiles also imply isomorphism.

**3. Classification and uniqueness of two-sided representatives.** If \(B\) is a two-sided ideal, its column space \(Be_{vv}\) is invariant under each left diagonal idempotent \(e_{uu}\), so it is spanned by a subset of the coordinate vectors \(e_{uv}\). If it contains \(e_{uv}\), left multiplication by \(e_{tu}\) forces \(e_{tv}\) whenever \(t\le u\). As the ancestors of \(v\) are totally ordered, this subset must be an initial segment of length \(r_v\). Right multiplication along an edge forces \(r_{p(v)}\le r_v\). Conversely an initial-segment family with these inequalities is closed under all left and right matrix-unit multiplications, giving exactly \(B_r\). Its profile is \(r\). Every admissible profile is realized, and no two two-sided ideals share a profile. Combining this with Step 2 gives the asserted unique representative and the iso-multiplication property.

**4. Embeddings, Hom spaces and automorphisms.** If \(B_r\) embeds in \(B_s\), its induced maps on the components at each vertex are injective, hence \(r_v\le s_v\). Conversely these inequalities imply \(B_r\subseteq B_s\) by the explicit initial-segment definition. Profiles are closed under coordinatewise minimum and maximum, which are the corresponding meets and joins. The usual projective representability identity \(\operatorname{Hom}_A(e_{vv}A,B_s)\cong B_se_{vv}\) has vector-space dimension \(s_v\), proving the Hom formula from Step 2. Over a finite field the endomorphism algebra of \(\bigoplus_v(e_{vv}A)^{m_v}\) has diagonal blocks \(M_{m_v}(k)\) and one off-diagonal block of dimension \(m_um_v\) for each strict pair \(u<v\), and no other blocks. These off-diagonal blocks form its nilpotent radical; an endomorphism is invertible exactly when all diagonal blocks are invertible. Counting its invertible elements yields the stated automorphism order.

**5. Finite-field counts and special shapes.** To choose column \(U_v\) extending an already chosen parent space of dimension \(r_{p(v)}\), choose an \((r_v-r_{p(v)})\)-dimensional subspace of the quotient of dimension \(d(v)-r_{p(v)}\), giving the corresponding Gaussian coefficient. Branch choices are independent once their parent is fixed; multiply over all vertices. For counts of profiles, write \(F_v(t)\) for the number of assignments in the descendant subtree when the parent profile value is \(t\). Then
\[
F_v(t)=\sum_{a=t}^{d(v)}\prod_{w:\,p(w)=v}F_w(a),
\qquad \#\mathrm{Types}(P)=\prod_{v:\,v\text{ root}}F_v(0).
\]
For a chain, nondecreasing sequences with the rank bound \(r_i\le i\) are counted by Catalan \(C_{n+1}\), by the standard ballot-path correspondence. For a rooted star with \(s\) leaves, the root profile is zero or one; these give respectively three or two independent leaf choices, hence \(3^s+2^s\). Finally, when \(P\) has an ancestor pair \(u<v\) with \(v\) maximal, \(ke_{vv}=e_{vv}A\) is a right ideal but not left-stable because \(e_{uv}e_{vv}=e_{uv}\). If \(P\) is an antichain, \(A\cong k^{|P|}\), and every right ideal is automatically two-sided. This proves the final dichotomy.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library Python program `verify.py` enumerates admissible profiles, canonical two-sided ideals and exact Gaussian counts for chains and branched forests. It independently exhausts right-ideal subspaces in reduced-row-echelon form for a three-vertex chain over \(\mathbb F_2\), and a three-vertex rooted fork over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), testing closure under the actual matrix-unit products. It also checks the Hom formula by solving finite-field linear equations for the module-action intertwining constraints. Recorded outputs include 14 module types and 40 embedded right ideals for the three-vertex chain over \(\mathbb F_2\), and 13 types and 29 or 40 embedded right ideals for the three-vertex fork over \(\mathbb F_2\) or \(\mathbb F_3\), respectively. The checker terminates with `ROOTED_FOREST_ISO_MULTIPLICATION_CHECKS_OK`. These are finite corroborations of the all-forest, all-field proof, not substitute evidence for its universal quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work
Tuganbaev, Tekir, Koc and Ulucak (arXiv:2611.20073, first posted 2026-11-15) introduce and study iso-multiplication modules. Their Theorem 2.11 proves it for triangular two-block rings with an arbitrary bimodule dimension; it does not classify regular modules over arbitrary rooted-forest incidence algebras, their unique ideal representatives, or the embedding lattice. A published study entitled *Gaussian flag formula for the complete left-ideal dimension spectrum of triangular matrix algebras* derives field-size-dependent left-ideal counts for a **chain** (the upper-triangular algebra). That formula already implies an embedded right-ideal enumeration for chains by the transpose anti-isomorphism, so the chain Gaussian-count subcase is not claimed as new. Shapiro (1975) counts two-sided ideals in upper-triangular rings using Catalan numbers; that alone does not identify the isomorphism classes of arbitrary embedded right ideals. The new statement is the all-field rooted-forest *unique isomorphism-representative theorem*, together with its embedding lattice, Hom and automorphism formulas, and finite-field branched extension. The proof uses standard facts about projective representations of a directed forest; novelty is not asserted for that background machinery.

## Limitations
The unique-representative theorem requires every ancestor set to be a chain. When directed branches merge, compatible subspace systems need not admit simultaneous basis extension, and no corresponding classification is asserted. A claim of historical priority remains subject to whether earlier specialized representation-theoretic work states an equivalent rooted-forest classification or its precise ideal-realization consequence. The source-comparison literature search is not a proof of global uniqueness of publication. The finite tests cover only the listed small fields and forest shapes. No independent mathematical audit is asserted.
