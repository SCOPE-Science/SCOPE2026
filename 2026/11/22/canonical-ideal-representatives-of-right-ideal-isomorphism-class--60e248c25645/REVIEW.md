# Review

**Correctness — PASS.** Diagonal idempotents give a complete column-subspace representation of every right ideal. The ancestor-chain hypothesis is exactly what makes recursive basis extension possible, yielding a projective direct-sum classification. Left matrix units force column supports of two-sided ideals to be initial segments, so each right-ideal isomorphism class has one and only one ideal representative. The embedding, Hom and automorphism formulas then follow from exact module maps and the nilpotent off-diagonal endomorphism radical. The packaged checker independently verifies finite RREF enumerations, ideal closure, counts and Hom dimensions. These experiments do not establish infinite validity by themselves.

**Originality — PASS, with explicit priority risk.** The primary 2026 iso-multiplication study proves the regular two-block triangular case, not the branched rooted-forest class or unique-representative lattice. Its full HTML text was examined, including Theorem 2.11 and its module classification section. The closest published explicit triangular-ideal census treats left ideals of a chain; by anti-isomorphism it already covers **embedded right-ideal counts for chains**, but not the stated rooted-forest module-isomorphism classification. Shapiro's earlier Catalan ideal study concerns two-sided ideals. Searches included rooted-tree incidence ideals, canonical two-sided representatives, triangular-module equivalence and relevant finite-field counts. Standard hereditary-quiver theory may implicitly derive the main result, so historical novelty is qualified pending a specialist literature review.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The theorem supplies a natural exact classification of when representatives of all right-ideal isomorphism types can be chosen uniquely among two-sided ideals across arbitrarily large branched incidence algebras. It computes the embedding lattice, Hom dimensions, automorphism groups and finite-field populations in one framework and concretely distinguishes iso-multiplication from equality-based multiplication. Chains yield Catalan type counts; stars have a different exponentially branched law. The underlying projective decomposition is standard in spirit, but the canonical ideal-realization theorem and combined invariants are not merely a rephrased chain ideal count.
