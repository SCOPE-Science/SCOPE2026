# Castelnuovo-extremal Chebyshev space curves with semigroup \(\langle n-4,n-2,n\rangle\)

## Finding

**Theorem.** Fix an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic zero, an integer \(m\ge2\), and \(n=2m+1\). Let \(T_j\in\mathbb Z[u]\) be the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, defined by \(T_j(\cos\theta)=\cos(j\theta)\). Let \(C_n\subset\mathbb P^3_k\) be the reduced projective closure of the affine parametrized curve
\[
u\longmapsto (T_2(u),T_4(u),T_n(u)).\]
Use homogeneous coordinates \([W:X:Y:Z]\) so that the affine chart \(W\ne0\) has coordinates \((X/W,Y/W,Z/W)\). Then:

1. \(C_n\) is integral, nondegenerate, of degree \(n\), and its normalization is \(\mathbb P^1\). It lies on the rank-three quadric cone \(Q:YW=2X^2-W^2\).
2. Its affine singular locus consists of exactly \(m\) ordinary nodes, located at \(Z=0\) over the distinct roots of the degree-\(m\) polynomial \(h_m(X)\), characterized by \(T_n(u)=u h_m(T_2(u))\).
3. Its only point at infinity is \(v=[0:0:0:1]\). Its unique analytic branch has value semigroup
\[
\Gamma_v=\langle n-4,n-2,n\rangle=\langle 2m-3,2m-1,2m+1\rangle,
\]
with
\[
\delta_v=m(m-2),\qquad \operatorname{cond}(\Gamma_v)=(m-2)(2m-1).
\]
For \(m=2\), the point \(v\) is smooth; for every \(m\ge3\), it is singular.
4. The arithmetic genus is
\[
p_a(C_n)=m(m-1)=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-2)^2}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]
Thus every member attains the Castelnuovo upper bound for an integral nondegenerate degree-\(n\) curve in \(\mathbb P^3\).

In particular, the curve with frequencies \((2,4,7)\) has precisely three affine nodes, an infinity branch of semigroup \(\langle3,5,7\rangle\) and delta invariant three, and arithmetic genus six.

## Assumptions and scope

All geometric assertions are over algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields; the special-functions description gives the zero-phase trigonometric Lissajous curve over \(\mathbb C\). The ordinary projective closure is taken in \(\mathbb P^3\), **not** in a weighted projective space. The term value semigroup means the set of orders of nonzero functions in the completed local ring inside the complete discrete valuation ring of its normalization. The conductor of a numerical semigroup \(S\) is \(\min\{c\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:c+\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\subseteq S\}\); it is one more than the last gap if there are gaps, and it is zero for \(S=\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). The arithmetic genus is \(1-\chi(\mathcal O_{C_n})\), not the genus of its normalization.

## Proof

**1. Affine description and all finite singularities.** As \(T_2(u)=2u^2-1\) and \(T_4(u)=2T_2(u)^2-1\), the parametrized affine curve satisfies \(Y=2X^2-1\). Since \(n\) is odd, there is a unique polynomial \(h_m\) of degree \(m\) satisfying
\[
T_n(u)=u\,h_m(2u^2-1).
\]
Consequently the coordinate ring of the affine image is
\[
k[X,Y,Z]/\bigl(Y-2X^2+1,\ Z^2-\tfrac{X+1}{2}h_m(X)^2\bigr).
\]
This ideal is prime: \((X+1)/2\) is not a square in \(k(X)\), and the right-hand side of the second equation has a nonsquare factor \(X+1\). For \(n=2m+1\), the zeros of \(T_n(u)\) are distinct. Exactly one is zero and its derivative is nonzero; the other \(2m\) occur in opposite nonzero pairs. Their images under \(u\mapsto2u^2-1\) are \(m\) distinct roots of \(h_m\), none equal to \(-1\); equivalently, \(h_m\) is squarefree and \(h_m(-1)\ne0\).

For a plane double cover \(Z^2=F(X)\) in characteristic zero, singularities occur precisely at repeated roots of \(F\); here each root of \(h_m\) is a double root of \(F(X)=(X+1)h_m(X)^2/2\) with nonzero quadratic coefficient, and hence is an ordinary node. The simple root \(X=-1\) is smooth. The eliminated \(Y\)-coordinate creates no further singularities. The generic inverse \(u=Z/h_m(X)\) shows that \(u\) is a rational parameter on the curve; normalization is therefore \(\mathbb P^1\). Every node has two distinct inverse images and delta invariant one.

**2. Degree and the cone.** Homogenize the four functions \(1,T_2,T_4,T_n\) to the same degree \(n\) on \(\mathbb P^1\). They have no common zero: on the affine parameter chart the homogenized first coordinate is one, and at \(u=\infty\), the leading coefficient of \(T_n\) is nonzero. Thus they give a morphism \(\nu:\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^3\) with \(\nu^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^3}(1)\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(n)\). The map to its image is birational by the preceding inverse, so its image has degree \(n\). Because the functions of degrees \(0,2,4,n\) are linearly independent, the image is nondegenerate. The identity \(T_4=2T_2^2-1\) homogenizes to \(YW=2X^2-W^2\), a quadric cone with vertex \(v=[0:0:0:1]\). At infinity \(u=\infty\), the same homogeneous map takes the value \(v\), and this is the only point on \(C_n\) with \(W=0\).

**3. Arithmetic genus.** The Weil divisor class group of the rank-three projective quadric cone is generated by a ruling line \(L\), with \(\deg L=1\) and hyperplane class \(2L\). Because \(C_n\) is an integral Weil divisor on the normal surface \(Q\) of degree \(n\), its divisor class is \(n[L]\). Let \(D\) be the union of \(n\) distinct ruling lines, likewise of class \(n[L]\). Their divisorial ideal sheaves \(\mathcal O_Q(-C_n)\) and \(\mathcal O_Q(-D)\) are isomorphic, so the exact sequences of structure sheaves on \(Q\) imply that \(C_n\) and \(D\) have the same Hilbert polynomial.

The divisor \(D\) is the projective cone over \(n=2m+1\) distinct points on a smooth plane conic. The degree-\(j\) Hilbert function of those points is \(\min(n,2j+1)\): sections of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(2j)\) evaluate with maximal rank at distinct points. Thus, for \(d\ge m\), the Hilbert function of the cone is
\[
H_D(d)=\sum_{j=0}^d\min(n,2j+1)
=m^2+(d-m+1)n=nd+1-m(m-1).
\]
The Hilbert polynomial of an integral projective curve of degree \(n\) is \(nd+1-p_a\). Hence \(p_a(C_n)=m(m-1)\). The Castelnuovo bound in \(\mathbb P^3\) is \(p_a\le\lfloor(n-2)^2/4\rfloor\), and for odd \(n=2m+1\) this equals \(m(m-1)\).

**4. The singular branch at infinity.** Put \(s=1/u\). On the target chart \(Z\ne0\), local coordinates at \(v\) are \((W/Z,X/Z,Y/Z)\). Their pullbacks to \(k[[s]]\) have respective valuations
\[
\operatorname{ord}_s(W/Z)=n,\quad
\operatorname{ord}_s(X/Z)=n-2,\quad
\operatorname{ord}_s(Y/Z)=n-4.
\]
Thus the numerical semigroup \(S=\langle n-4,n-2,n\rangle\) is contained in the local value semigroup \(\Gamma_v\). The normalization has precisely one point above \(v\), so the local ring is unibranch. The genus-defect formula gives
\[
\delta_v=p_a(C_n)-\sum_{\text{affine nodes}}\delta
=m(m-1)-m=m(m-2).
\]

**5. Exact semigroup and conductor.** Write \(h=m-2\) and \(a=n-4=2h+1\). Then \(S=\langle a,a+2,a+4\rangle\), including \(a=1\) when \(m=2\). For each \(0\le j<a\), the element
\[
w_j=a\left\lceil\frac j2\right\rceil+2j
\]
is the least member of \(S\) in its residue class modulo \(a\). Indeed, each sum of \(a+2\) and \(a+4\) can be indexed by \(j=r+2s\), and for fixed \(j\) the minimum number of generators is \(\lceil j/2\rceil\); replacing \(j\) by \(j+a\) strictly increases its value. Because \(a\) is odd, the residues \(2j\bmod a\) cover all classes. Thus \(\{w_0,\dots,w_{a-1}\}\) is the Apéry set of \(S\) with respect to \(a\). Since \(\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}\lceil j/2\rceil=h(h+1)\), its number of gaps is
\[
\#(\mathbb N\setminus S)
=\frac1a\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}w_j-\frac{a-1}{2}
=h(h+2)=m(m-2).
\]
For a residually rational unibranch curve singularity, the delta invariant equals the number of gaps of its value semigroup. The inclusion \(S\subseteq\Gamma_v\) and equality of their gap counts therefore force \(S=\Gamma_v\). The largest Apéry element is \(w_{a-1}=h(a+4)\), so the conductor is \(w_{a-1}-a+1=h(2h+3)=(m-2)(2m-1)\). When \(m=2\), the semigroup contains one and the branch is smooth; when \(m\ge3\), its multiplicity is \(n-4\ge3\), so it is singular. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact symbolic identities (SymPy) and elementary numerical-semigroup computations for \(2\le m\le16\). It checks the Chebyshev factorization, squarefreeness and absence of the exceptional root, explicit Apéry sets, gap counts, conductors, Castelnuovo equality and the Hilbert-function formula. Run it as `python verify.py`. These are finite consistency checks; the proof above establishes the uniform statement for every integer \(m\ge2\) without a finite-bound assumption.

## Relationship to prior work

Mascarin, *Intersections, Equations, and Singularities of Lissajous Varieties* (2026), Section 5 and Example 5.8, identifies the **three affine singular points** in the specific \((2,4,7)\) zero-phase curve; the present theorem supplies their node types and the projective point-at-infinity invariants uniformly for all odd final frequencies. Mascarin and Telen, *Lissajous Varieties* (2025), study affine Lissajous varieties. Bel-Afia, Meroni and Telen, *Chebyshev Varieties* (2024), Section 3.2, investigate affine Chebyshev space curves and smoothness. Freudenburg and Freudenburg, *Curves Defined by Chebyshev Polynomials* (2009), Sections 3–4, treat affine nodes and line embeddings. The Castelnuovo bound and quadric-cone divisor theory are classical inputs. The abstract Apéry representatives, gap count and Frobenius number for \(\langle a,a+2,a+4\rangle\) with odd \(a\ge3\) also appear in *Isolated gaps of the three-term progression* below. The geometric conclusion here is that this semigroup is the actual value semigroup of the unique branch at infinity of the stated Chebyshev curve, and that the branch and the affine nodes account for the full genus defect. No equivalent computation of that projective singularity data was located in the sources compared; this is not an assertion of exhaustive historical priority.

## Limitations

The statement is for the zero-phase family with first two frequencies exactly \((2,4)\), odd final frequency at least five, ordinary projective closure, and characteristic zero. It does not treat arbitrary phase shifts, other frequency triples, positive characteristic, the Jacobian scheme at infinity, the full projective automorphism group, or the embedded resolution graph. A survey of all older projective-parametric-curve literature was not exhaustive.
