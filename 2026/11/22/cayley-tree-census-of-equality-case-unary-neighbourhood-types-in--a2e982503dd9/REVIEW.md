# Scientific review — Cayley-tree census of equality-case unary neighbourhood types

**Correctness — PASS.** For a finite forest and a marked set of size \(m\), contract the marked induced components and keep the outside vertices adjacent to at least two different marked components. A contracted incidence forest gives \(h\le k-1\). Counting singleton, marked-vertex nonsingleton and outside-vertex nonsingleton traces gives \(|\mathcal T|\le 2m+c\le 2m+\lfloor m/3\rfloor\). At \(m=3q\), equality forces \(q\) disjoint marked three-vertex paths and exactly \(q-1\) two-neighbour unmarked connectors whose quotient is a tree. Conversely, all such choices are realized by adding private leaves and one empty-profile vertex, using \(7q\) vertices. Distinct labelled families recover all block and tree data from their triples and pairs, giving the factorial/Cayley formula. The executable standard-library checker exhausts forests on at most six vertices and the complete \(q=1,2\) family census; only the proof supports unbounded \(q\).

**Originality — PASS on examined evidence; residual priority risk.** Berthe, Foucaud, Lehtilä and Parreau (arXiv:2607.16889v1, 18 July 2026), Section 3, already prove the sharp \(\lfloor7m/3\rfloor\) bound and provide the \(P_3\)-block/tree construction, so neither is claimed. Their inspected full Section 3 does not state the necessary-and-sufficient classification of every equality trace family or count distinct named-parameter families. Beaudou et al. (arXiv:2501.08895v1) study neighbourhood/profile bounds and not this exact census in the inspected introductory scope. Searches against mathematical records found related open-neighborhood classifications and closed-neighborhood shattering, but no stronger implication covering the stated trace-family census. Bertrand, Charon, Hudry and Lobstein (2005, *1-identifying codes on trees*, Theorems 1–2) prove the precursor identifying-code inequality and give extremal examples, but do not classify closed-neighbourhood trace families or count them. The cited constructions make this result a close extension; independent priority verification is needed.

**Value — PASS.** The sharp bound specifies a number but not which labelled Boolean one-types can be simultaneously realized at equality. The rigidity theorem provides a complete normal form and a closed all-size labelled-family census: \(3,810,1\,837\,080\) at the first three levels. It distinguishes realizing structures from distinct profile families, shows a canonical minimum-realization size and connects saturated sparse-graph profile systems to Cayley's labelled trees. This is a natural classification at a sharp extremal boundary, not merely a restatement of the already-known upper bound.

**Scientific limitations.** The residue classes one and two mod three, and classification of all realizing ambient forests up to isomorphism, are not treated. Historical priority remains provisional; the finite checker and literature search do not constitute independent validation.

(2005), *1-identifying codes on trees*, Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 31, 21–35 (Theorems 1–2); Cayley (1889), classical labelled-tree count.
