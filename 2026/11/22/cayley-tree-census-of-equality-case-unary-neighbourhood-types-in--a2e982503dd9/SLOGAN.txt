At the zero-slack multiple-of-three boundary of the known forest neighbourhood-complexity theorem, every saturated one-variable closed-neighbourhood profile family is uniquely encoded by a partition into marked triples, one centre per triple, and a tree whose edges choose endpoints inside their incident triples. For \(3q\) individually named parameters the number of distinct saturated families is exactly \(\frac{(3q)!}{2^q q!}9^{q-1}q^{q-2}\) (with the one-block convention), and every family has a minimum \(7q\)-vertex tree realization.
