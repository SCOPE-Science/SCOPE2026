# Scientific review: stationary OU quadratic-energy observation design

## Correctness — PASS
The exact conditional Gaussian formula converts reduction in Bayes risk into twice the trace of the square of an inverse-covariance-weighted integrated rank-two Gram matrix. The three-interval covariance integration and matrix multiplication yield the fixed-gap identity. Because fixed-gap margin exponentials have constant product and the only variable sum enters by a strictly decreasing quadratic, centering is *globally necessary*, not merely stationary. Exact centered matrix eigenvalues and strict endpoint derivatives exclude both coincident and boundary observation sites. The infinite-horizon excess has a unique maximizer by strict log-concavity, with a coefficientwise positive power-series certificate for the nonstandard hyperbolic lemma. A compactness/escape argument establishes convergence of **every** finite-horizon optimal gap; no unproved finite-horizon uniqueness is asserted. The actual standalone `verify.py` passed symbolic identities and 7,958 independent sampled placement comparisons, in addition to numerical asymptotic checks. These finite tests do not replace the infinite-domain proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Sikolya–Baran's 2018 primary paper optimizes integrated pointwise trajectory-prediction loss and observation entropy for a complex OU process. The present nonlinear scalar-path-energy criterion is a *second-Gaussian-chaos* objective involving the squared trace of conditional Gram eigenvalues, not the integrated posterior-pointwise variance or observation entropy. Ji–Müller treat sparse trajectory and linear-response prediction; Grebenkov optimizes the form of observed Gaussian quadratic estimators rather than the locations of two samples for inferring unobserved integrated energy. A closely related published OU quadrature result addresses the **linear** path integral rather than the integrated square. Related Brownian and Brownian-bridge two-site quadratic-energy results concern nonstationary covariance and do not imply the stated stationary OU exponential-kernel identity or asymptotic separation. Searches for equivalent observation-design formulations did not reveal an exact covering result. Publication priority is not proved; the broader historical literature may contain an equivalent treatment.

## Value — PASS
Quadratic path energy is a natural observable of stochastic motion, and a two-sample budget is the smallest nontrivial choice that captures spatial/temporal correlation in a nonlinear Gaussian functional. Centering, *strict* interiority and the universal correlation-length separation are structural consequences absent from the familiar linear OU integral prediction criterion. The result supplies exact finite-horizon one-dimensional design reduction and a unique limiting geometry. No large-sample convergence rate, data-adaptive claim, noisy-measurement extension or universal process-family conclusion is implied.

The related works of Ji–Müller, arXiv:1604.05375v1, and Grebenkov, arXiv:1307.0185v1, were inspected for their objectives and compared by mathematical implication.
