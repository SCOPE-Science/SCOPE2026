# Scientific review: finite substitution-monoid centralizers

## Correctness — PASS
The parameterization by \(at+bt^2+ct^3\) is exhaustive because the image of the nilpotent generator must lie in the principal nilpotent ideal. Direct composition gives the two exact centralizer equations. In characteristic two the roots of \(u-u^2\) and \(u-u^3\) are \(0,1\). In odd characteristic the latter has exactly the roots \(0,1,-1\), and the only extra resonant multiplier is \(-1\). The six odd and three even structural strata are mutually disjoint, cover exactly \(q^3\) maps, and their weighted cardinalities yield the stated ordered-pair polynomials. The explicit degree-two and degree-four noncommutativity witnesses give the sharp all-length cutoff. Replaying `verify.py` gives exact centralizer histograms for six fields, including two nonprime fields; these are checks rather than infinite proofs. Risk: conventional function-composition order must be fixed, as it is in the statement, to interpret the coefficient formulas.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
A comparison of actual claims rather than names separates the full noninvertible substitution monoid from the finite Nottingham *group*, its conjugacy classes, and the invertible fourth-order jet group. Enochs–Jenda–Ozbek (arXiv:1510.05984v1, §2 and §5, complete text inspected) classifies exponent-support submonoids and units, but does not supply this finite-field complete centralizer census. Jennings (1954) and Johnson (1988), full article texts inspected, study substitution groups and their filtrations or quotients, not noninvertible endomorphisms of the truncated algebra. A publicly available full-group conjugacy/centralizer-spectrum result uses the unit group of a *different* truncation and assumes characteristic at least five; it does not imply the present monoid spectra for characteristic two or odd characteristic. Semantic searches of the mathematical result registry for finite jet monoids, substitution centralizers, aliases, and the exact polynomial did not return a dominating result. Residual risk: an older source under jet semigroup, composition monoid, or transformation semigroup terminology could contain the same formulas, and negative search evidence cannot establish uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The complete distribution of centralizer sizes is a natural finite-semigroup invariant, not merely an example or a partial table. It reveals a characteristic-dependent leading commuting-probability constant \(5\) versus \(3\), and the exceptional order-two monoid with full commutativity at length four. The sharp cutoff at lengths \(4\) versus \(2\) quantifies when higher jet coefficients first force noncommutativity. The distinction is meaningful precisely because group-only treatments omit noninvertible substitutions, which materially change both the formulas and the cutoff. Limitation: higher-length centralizer distributions remain open here, and this analysis does not assert external independent certification.
