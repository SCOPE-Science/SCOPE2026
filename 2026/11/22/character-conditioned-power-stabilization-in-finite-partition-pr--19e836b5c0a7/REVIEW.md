# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof independently identifies the exact obstruction at the character level, proves that finite stabilization is equivalent to a rooted functional forest, counts fixed-block rooted forests by the labelled-tree correspondence, derives the sharp gap-free endpoints constructively, and obtains the full height spectrum by finite formal coefficient extraction. The accompanying verifier separately enumerates small maps and confirms 2,441 coefficients, the product total and every permissible index support. Boundary cases include singleton blocks, identity characters, and characters with nontrivial cycles. Finite enumeration supports but does not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked sources

The closest paper, Sarkar–Singh 2023 (arXiv:2310.19414), studies exactly this character-restricted semigroup and proves idempotent criteria (Proposition 3.9), regularity and Green relations; its inspected exposition does not give all-power stabilization counts or their gap-free support. Sarkar–Singh 2020 (arXiv:2006.04242) enumerates idempotents for a block-surjective subsemigroup and does not yield the multi-height result as stated. Existing generic forest species and Prüfer results provide the enumeration machinery, so some ingredients are known; the character-conditioned sharp index interval, factorized eventual-stabilization law and all-index lift classification are the assessed contribution. The risk of an older unindexed equivalent application of general functional-digraph methods remains.

## Value — PASS

The character map is a standard structural quotient of these transformation monoids. The new result gives a complete and computable invariant of the power-stabilization filtration in every character fiber, a sharp obstruction to finite stabilization, no-gap index support, and a surprising conditional probability independent of all transient block sizes. It applies across arbitrary finite partition profiles and arbitrary allowed character subsemigroups, beyond isolated finite cases or a straightforward restatement of the idempotent criterion.

## Scope and residual risks

The theorem is for finite total maps, not nontrivial periodic powers or partial maps. Elementary Cayley and multitype-tree tools are classical; prior overlap could arise from a general counting theorem specialized to these character constraints. The proof and finite computations have not been independently reviewed.
