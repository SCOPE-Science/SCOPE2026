# Chow-motive rigidity and unbounded irregularity for characteristic-five Igusa surfaces

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_5\) and let \(E/k\) be the smooth ordinary elliptic curve
\[
E:\quad y^2=x^3+3x+2,
\]
with rational point \(P=(1,1)\) of exact order \(5\). For every integer \(m\ge2\) not divisible by \(5\), let \(X_m/k\) be the smooth projective curve with function field \(k(x,z)\) given by
\[
z^5-z=x^m,
\]
let \(G=\langle P\rangle\simeq \mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\), and define the smooth projective surface
\[
S_m=(E\times X_m)/G,
\]
where the generator acts as \((e,x,z)\mapsto(e+P,x,z+1)\).

**Theorem.** All these examples have the same **rational Chow motive over \(\mathbb F_5\)** as the ruled surface \(E\times\mathbb P^1\):
\[
\mathfrak h(S_m)\simeq\mathfrak h(E\times\mathbb P^1)
\qquad\text{in }\operatorname{ChowMot}(\mathbb F_5;\mathbb Q).
\]
Consequently, for every \(r\ge1\),
\[
\#S_m(\mathbb F_{5^r})=(5^r+1)\#E(\mathbb F_{5^r}),
\]
and their common zeta function is
\[
Z(S_m,T)=\frac{(1-T+5T^2)(1-5T+125T^2)}{(1-T)(1-5T)^2(1-25T)}.
\]
Nevertheless, their coherent irregularities and geometric genera satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
h^1(S_m,\mathcal O_{S_m})&=\left\lfloor\frac{4(m+1)}5\right\rfloor,\\
h^2(S_m,\mathcal O_{S_m})&=\left\lfloor\frac{4(m+1)}5\right\rfloor-1,
\end{aligned}
\]
which are unbounded. The geometric numerical Néron–Severi lattice, and also the numerical lattice generated by divisors defined over \(k\), is the hyperbolic plane
\[
\operatorname{Num}(S_m\otimes\overline{k})\simeq U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In a natural basis, the numerical canonical divisor is \(K_{S_m}\equiv(4m-6)R\).

## Assumptions and scope

All schemes and quotients are over \(\mathbb F_5\). The integers \(m\) range over all \(m\ge2\) with \(5\nmid m\). The elliptic curve \(E\) has exactly five rational points, so its nonzero point \(P\) generates a constant étale subgroup \(G\) of order five; its Frobenius trace is one. The action on \(E\) is free, hence the diagonal quotient of the smooth projective product is smooth projective and the projection \(E\times X_m\to S_m\) is a finite étale \(G\)-torsor. The Artin–Schreier cover \(X_m\to\mathbb P^1_x\) is separable, totally ramified only above infinity, with lower jump \(m\), and has genus \(g(X_m)=2m-2\).

The isomorphism asserted is of **rational Chow motives**, not of varieties, integral motives, integral cohomology rings, or coherent-cohomology complexes. The lattice assertion concerns numerical equivalence; it does not assert that the full Néron–Severi group is torsion-free.

## Proof

**1. A torsion translation has the identity Chow correspondence.** Let \(d:E\times E\to E\) be \(d(a,b)=b-a\). The graph of translation by \(P\) is the divisor \(d^{-1}(P)\), while the diagonal is \(d^{-1}(O)\). Their difference in \(\operatorname{CH}^1(E\times E)=\operatorname{Pic}(E\times E)\) is \(d^*\mathcal O_E(P-O)\). Since the divisor class \(P-O\) has order five in \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)\), its pullback is five-torsion. Thus the graph of translation by \(P\), and likewise by every member of \(G\), equals the diagonal in \(\operatorname{CH}^1(E\times E)\otimes\mathbb Q\).

**2. Transfer for the two quotients.** For the finite étale quotient \(q:E\times X_m\to S_m\) of degree five, the standard graph/transpose-graph correspondence identities yield the rational projector
\[
\frac15\,{}^t\Gamma_q\circ\Gamma_q
=\frac15\sum_{g\in G}\Gamma_{g,E\times X_m}.
\]
Therefore \(\mathfrak h(S_m)\) is the image of this idempotent acting on \(\mathfrak h(E\times X_m)\). By step 1 the idempotent is \(\Delta_E\otimes e_X\), where
\[
e_X=\frac15\sum_{g\in G}\Gamma_{g,X_m}.
\]
For the separable Galois cover \(\pi:X_m\to\mathbb P^1\), the proper correspondences satisfy
\[
{}^t\Gamma_\pi\circ\Gamma_\pi=\sum_{g\in G}\Gamma_{g,X_m},\qquad
\Gamma_\pi\circ{}^t\Gamma_\pi=5\Delta_{\mathbb P^1}.
\]
The first equality holds as an equality of one-cycles on \(X_m\times X_m\): the fiber product \(X_m\times_{\mathbb P^1}X_m\) has exactly the five generic graph components, each with multiplicity one, and no additional one-dimensional vertical component. The equations identify the image \((\mathfrak h(X_m),e_X)\) with \(\mathfrak h(\mathbb P^1)\). Tensoring with \(\mathfrak h(E)\) gives the stated Chow-motive isomorphism over \(k\), for every admissible \(m\).

**3. Frobenius and zeta.** Prime-to-five étale realization of the rational motivic isomorphism identifies the graded Frobenius representations with those of \(E\times\mathbb P^1\). The elementary count \(\#E(\mathbb F_5)=5\) gives trace \(a_1=1\); hence
\[
Z(E,T)=\frac{1-T+5T^2}{(1-T)(1-5T)}.
\]
The product formula \(Z(E\times\mathbb P^1,T)=Z(E,T)Z(E,5T)\) yields the displayed rational function and all extension-field counts.

**4. Coherent cohomology.** Theorem C of Fong–Maccan–Modin applies to the unique ramification jump \(m\), and gives the precise Picard-scheme defect
\[
\delta_I(S_m)=\left\lfloor\frac{4(m+1)}5\right\rfloor-1.
\]
The motive computation gives \(b_1(S_m)=2\). By the defining identity \(\delta_I=h^1(\mathcal O)-b_1/2\), the claimed \(h^1\) follows. Their general formula \(\chi(\mathcal O_{S_m})=0\), or the Noether formula for this smooth isotrivial elliptic surface, then gives \(h^2=h^1-1\). In particular \(\operatorname{Pic}_{S_m}\) is nonreduced for all admissible \(m\), and the unbounded \(h^1\) shows infinitely many \(S_m\) are mutually nonisomorphic even though their rational Chow motives are all isomorphic.

**5. Integral numerical intersection lattice.** The unique point \(\infty_m\in X_m\) above \(\infty\in\mathbb P^1\) is rational and fixed by \(G\). Let \(R\subset S_m\) be the reduced image of \(E\times\{\infty_m\}\), an elliptic curve \(E/G\), and let \(D\) be a fiber of the smooth morphism \(S_m\to E/G\) induced by the first projection. The latter morphism has fiber \(X_m\), and \(R\) is its section. Thus \(R\cdot D=1\) and \(D^2=0\). Since \(q^*R=E\times\{\infty_m\}\) under the étale quotient, \(R^2=0\). The motive isomorphism gives \(b_2(S_m)=2\), so \(\rho(S_m\otimes\overline k)\le2\), while the two divisor classes are independent. They have Gram matrix \(U\) of determinant \(-1\), hence generate the whole rank-two numerical Néron–Severi lattice: any larger finite-index overlattice would have nonintegral discriminant. Both classes are defined over \(k\). Finally adjunction on \(R\) (genus one) and \(D\) (genus \(2m-2\)) gives \(K\cdot R=0\) and \(K\cdot D=4m-6\), proving \(K\equiv(4m-6)R\).

## Verification

A standalone standard-Python checker, `igusa_five_checker.py`, verifies that the explicit elliptic curve is smooth and has five points over \(\mathbb F_5\), thirty-five over \(\mathbb F_{25}\), and Frobenius trace one. For all twenty-three admissible \(m\) between two and thirty it checks the Artin–Schreier Riemann–Hurwitz arithmetic and the twisted-Frobenius first-field count \(\#S_m(\mathbb F_5)=30\). The executable does not verify motive identities or infinitely many values of \(m\); those conclusions follow from the correspondence and ramification proofs above.

## Relationship to prior work

Katsura–Ueno introduced contracted-product Artin–Schreier elliptic surfaces, and Liedtke established that their Picard defects can be arbitrarily large. Fong–Maccan computed the Betti numbers of general isotrivial contracted-product surfaces. Fong–Maccan–Modin (2026), Theorem C, gives the ramification-jump formula used here; its Lemma 3.1 supplies the vanishing coherent Euler characteristic. Those pieces of the irregularity and the underlying construction are **not** claimed as original. The asserted additional finding is the **uniform isomorphism of rational Chow motives over the specific finite field**, together with the exact Frobenius zeta function and saturated integral numerical Néron–Severi lattice, tied to the unbounded coherent invariants. The proof establishes these through explicit transfer and intersection arguments rather than inferring them from Betti numbers alone.

## Limitations

This does not identify the infinitesimal Picard group scheme, nor integral Chow motives or full torsion of the Néron–Severi group. The nontrivial new conclusions are obtained by relatively short known correspondence techniques; the construction and increasing Picard defect were known. The original 1985 Katsura–Ueno full text was not available for line-by-line comparison, so older priority for an equivalent motivic or numerical statement cannot be excluded. Search nonmatches are not proof of priority; independent mathematical verification has not been performed.
