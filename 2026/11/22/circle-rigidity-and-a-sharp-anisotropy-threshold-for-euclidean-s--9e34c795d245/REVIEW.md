**Correctness (PASS).** The four-point Kruskal lemma explicitly compares all six distances of the inscribed parallelogram, accounting for the duplicated alternating boundary-edge lengths. The change from independent antipodal pair angles to a uniform mid-angle and half-separation is measure-preserving; the exact conditional probability follows from two arctangent thresholds. The determinant identity gives the sharp \(\sqrt3\) bifurcation. Coordinatewise monotonicity of the two arctangent arguments and a uniform near-circle expansion prove the global qualitative assertions. Circle rigidity uses the first-order radial gradient on an arbitrary smooth noncircle. The independent code enumerates actual complete-graph MST edges for a deterministic sample; it does not replace the proof.

**Originality (PASS, historical risk retained).** The closest classical primary treatment is Toussaint's relative neighborhood graph: its inclusion of Euclidean MSTs in a proximity graph is strictly broader in graph class but does not imply the ellipse antipodal-pair distribution or sharp \(\sqrt3\) phase. Bose et al. treat constrained proximity graphs and algorithms, not fixed geometries with two randomly chosen antipodal pairs. The available indexed literature and published research records inspected using alternate descriptions (ellipse diagonal shortcuts, four-point parallelogram MST, relative-neighborhood edge tests, and circle rigidity) did not yield an equivalent or dominating theorem. This is a best-knowledge originality assessment, not proof of uniqueness; older, unindexed treatments of parallelogram MSTs could contain the elementary four-point lemma or the rigidity observation. Such lemmas are treated as inputs rather than the sole value claim.

**Value (PASS).** The invariant question is motivated by whether Euclidean shortest connecting networks of sampled convex-boundary vertices stay within the visible polygon. Central symmetry makes the smallest sample nontrivial, namely two antipodal pairs, and the circle rigidity gives a geometric characterization. The exact anisotropy response and the positive-measure all-separation regime at a sharp aspect ratio quantify a genuine, nonlocal network-topology transition, rather than an arbitrary finite enumeration.

**Limitations.** Only uniform eccentric-anomaly antipodal-pair sampling is covered quantitatively; other densities and larger configurations are not treated. Ties are excluded from strict event characterizations. The primary source comparison establishes no claim of independent validation or exhaustive historical priority.
