# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
Both involutions preserve the displayed genus-two equation, and the degree-two fixed-field quotient equations follow by direct rational-function substitution. The differential pullbacks \((x-d)dx/y\) and \((x+d)dx/y\) span the two-dimensional cotangent space. The induced isogeny has degree \(4\), prime to every characteristic under consideration; this is the essential point ensuring **the complete \(p\)-torsion group scheme**, not only the Newton polygon, agrees with two copies of the elliptic quotient. The quotient cubic has three distinct roots for \(p\ne2,5\). The exact \(j\)-invariant reduces to the Hilbert class polynomial of discriminant \(-20\), and CM reduction gives the split/inert dichotomy. The symbolic and finite-field checker passed. Mathematical risks: applying a prime-to-\(p\) argument in characteristics \(2\) or \(5\) would be invalid; both are expressly excluded.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations
The initiating automorphism paper (Kazemifard–Tafazolian, arXiv:2611.10255) supplies the reciprocal involution but not the two quotient equations or \(p\)-torsion classification in the inspected relevant sections. Tafazolian–Top (arXiv:2611.14198, Theorems 1.1–1.2 and sections 3–6) treats the distinct criterion of supersingularity and maximal extension fields; supersingular genus-two Jacobians need not be superspecial, so it does not imply the new group-scheme conclusion without an additional argument. Takei (2014, Chapter 5, §5.2) determines the possible Cartier–Manin matrix shapes for an entire one-parameter family, but does not evaluate the central parameter for all primes in the passages inspected. The CM polynomial itself is already known and credited. Searches comparing the exact central quintic, its elliptic quotients, the class-polynomial value, and all-prime Cartier ranks did not locate this complete statement. Risk: an older specialization or computation not indexed in the searched material could cover the result.

## Value — PASS
A natural singularity-free central member of the Chebyshev genus-two family has an arithmetic dichotomy sharper than supersingularity: **every inert characteristic is superspecial**, and every other good odd characteristic is ordinary. Identifying explicit elliptic quotient morphisms and a prime-to-characteristic isogeny explains the whole \(p\)-divisible group, not simply an observed finite Hasse–Witt pattern. The restriction to one highly symmetric natural curve is motivated by its reciprocal involution and CM structure. This does not settle general Chebyshev families.
