# Coarse-ray index splitting and exact nuclear dimension of uniform Roe coronas

## Finding

**Theorem (ray-index and corona dimension).** Let \(X\) be a countably infinite metric space of bounded geometry. Assume there are distinct points \(x_0,x_1,\ldots\in X\) and \(R<\infty\) such that \(d(x_n,x_{n+1})\leq R\) for every \(n\geq0\). Put
\[
 A=C_u^*(X),\qquad H=\ell^2(X),\qquad Q=A/\mathcal K(H),
\]
with quotient map \(\pi\). Then there is an explicit finite-propagation isometry \(S\in A\) such that \(u=\pi(S)\) is a unitary with Fredholm index \(-1\). Consequently:

1. The quotient map induces a **canonical** isomorphism \(K_0(A)\xrightarrow{\cong}K_0(Q)\).
2. The index sequence is split exact,
   \[
   0\longrightarrow K_1(A)\xrightarrow{\pi_*}K_1(Q)
   \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Ind}}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0,
   \]
   and therefore \(K_1(Q)\cong K_1(A)\oplus\mathbb Z\). The splitting uses the chosen ray; it is not asserted canonical.
3. The corona has the **exact** nuclear dimension
   \[
   \dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}Q=\begin{cases}
       1,&\text{if }X\text{ has Property A},\\
       \infty,&\text{if }X\text{ does not have Property A}.
     \end{cases}
   \]

Every infinite connected graph of uniformly bounded vertex degree, with its path metric, meets the ray hypothesis. The theorem also applies to countable bounded-geometry spaces whose fixed-radius Rips graph is infinite and connected.

## Assumptions and scope

A metric space has *bounded geometry* if for every radius \(r>0\) there is a finite number \(N(r)\) bounding the cardinality of every closed \(r\)-ball. An operator on \(H\) has propagation at most \(r\) if its matrix entry at \((x,y)\) vanishes whenever \(d(x,y)>r\). The uniform Roe algebra \(C_u^*(X)\) is the operator-norm closure of all bounded finite-propagation operators on \(H\). Matrix units have finite propagation, so the compact operators \(\mathcal K(H)\) form an ideal in \(A\). Their quotient is the uniform Roe corona \(Q\).

Property A means that for every \(r,\varepsilon>0\) there exist \(s>0\) and probability vectors \(\xi_x\in\ell^1(X)\), supported in the \(s\)-ball about \(x\), such that \(\|\xi_x-\xi_y\|_1<\varepsilon\) whenever \(d(x,y)<r\). The assertion requires only one *bounded-step simple ray*; it does not assume a geodesic or a quasi-isometric embedding of \(\mathbb N\).

We use the standard Fredholm-index convention \(\mathrm{Ind}(T)=\dim\ker T-\dim\operatorname{coker}T\). For any unitary in \(Q\), its index is the connecting map in the extension by the compact operators, evaluated on its \(K_1\)-class. All \(K\)-groups are complex topological \(K\)-groups. No separability assumption on the operator algebra is needed.

## Proof

**1. A controlled index-one defect.** On the canonical basis of \(H\), define
\[
 S\delta_{x_n}=\delta_{x_{n+1}}\quad(n\geq0),
 \qquad S\delta_y=\delta_y\quad
 \bigl(y\notin\{x_n:n\geq0\}\bigr).
\]
The images form an orthonormal family with orthogonal complement precisely \(\mathbb C\delta_{x_0}\). Thus \(S^*S=1\) and \(SS^*=1-p\), where \(p=|\delta_{x_0}\rangle\langle\delta_{x_0}|\) is rank one. Since every nonzero matrix entry of \(S\) joins vertices at distance at most \(R\), or lies on the diagonal, \(S\) has propagation at most \(R\) and belongs to \(A\). Modulo the compact ideal, \(u=\pi(S)\) is unitary. The lift \(S\) is Fredholm, with zero kernel and one-dimensional cokernel, hence \(\mathrm{Ind}(u)=-1\).

**2. The map from the compact ideal on \(K_0\) vanishes.** The projections \(SS^*=1-p\) and \(S^*S=1\) are Murray–von Neumann equivalent within \(A\), by the partial isometry \(S\). Orthogonality of \(p\) and \(1-p\) gives
\[
 [p]_{K_0(A)}=[1]_{K_0(A)}-[1-p]_{K_0(A)}=0.
\]
Since \(K_0(\mathcal K(H))\cong\mathbb Z\) is generated by a rank-one projection and \(K_1(\mathcal K(H))=0\), the inclusion-induced map \(K_0(\mathcal K(H))\to K_0(A)\) is identically zero.

**3. Both \(K\)-groups.** Apply the ordinary six-term exact sequence to
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal K(H)\longrightarrow A
\xrightarrow{\pi}Q\longrightarrow0.
\]
The preceding zero map, together with \(K_1(\mathcal K(H))=0\), implies that \(\pi_*:K_0(A)\to K_0(Q)\) is both injective and surjective. The other half reduces to the short exact sequence displayed in the Finding. Its boundary homomorphism is the Fredholm index and takes \([u]\) to \(-1\), so it is surjective. The homomorphism \(m\mapsto-m[u]\) from \(\mathbb Z\) to \(K_1(Q)\) is a right inverse of the index map. This proves the asserted splitting, with no use of Property A.

**4. Sharp dimension with Property A.** Guo, Li, Zhang and Zhu [1, Theorem 1.1] establish \(\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}A\leq1\) for all countable bounded-geometry Property A spaces. Nuclear dimension does not increase under quotients [4], hence \(\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}Q\leq1\). The split \(\mathbb Z\)-summand from Step 3 shows \(K_1(Q)\ne0\). A unital C*-algebra of nuclear dimension zero is locally approximately finite dimensional [5]; every locally AF algebra has trivial \(K_1\), because a unitary at any matrix level is arbitrarily closely approximated by one in a finite-dimensional subalgebra, and the latter unitary group is path-connected. Thus \(Q\) cannot have nuclear dimension zero, proving its exact value \(1\).

**5. Failure of Property A.** For countable bounded-geometry spaces, Property A is equivalent to nuclearity of \(C_u^*(X)\), as recalled in [1]. If \(X\) lacks Property A, then \(A\) is not nuclear. If \(Q\) were nuclear, the short exact sequence in Step 3 would exhibit \(A\) as an extension of the nuclear ideal \(\mathcal K(H)\) by a nuclear quotient, and thus \(A\) would be nuclear, a contradiction. Therefore \(Q\) is nonnuclear and its nuclear dimension is \(\infty\).

**6. Graph corollary.** The graph connecting points at distance at most \(R\) has finite vertex degrees by bounded geometry. If it is infinite and connected, König's infinity lemma, applied to a spanning tree rooted at any point, yields a one-sided simple infinite path. Its consecutive metric distances are bounded by \(R\), so Steps 1–5 apply. In particular this covers infinite bounded-degree connected graphs with \(R=1\).

## Verification

The operator identities can be checked entry-by-entry: the range of the displayed isometry omits only \(\delta_{x_0}\), its matrix support lies within propagation \(R\), and its Fredholm index is \(-1\). The two \(K\)-group conclusions then follow from the six-term exact sequence with the explicitly computed map out of \(K_0(\mathcal K(H))\). The dimension bound and the distinction between dimensions \(0\) and \(1\) are separately justified in Steps 4–5. No finite enumeration or numerical experiment is used as an infinite-dimensional proof, and no external computational certificate is required.

## Relationship to prior work

Guo, Li, Zhang and Zhu [1] prove the **upper bound for the full uniform Roe algebra**, and their trichotomy for that algebra; the present theorem transfers the upper bound to the corona and obtains its sharp lower bound from a controlled Fredholm-index class. Braga, Farah and Vignati [2] define and study uniform Roe coronas, with an emphasis on rigidity and liftings. Li and Willett [3] study low-dimensionality and \(K\)-theory of the full uniform Roe algebra, rather than the compact quotient. Winter and Zacharias [4] provide nuclear-dimension permanence and the original framework. The six-term exact sequence and Fredholm-index boundary are standard; the geometric ray witness, its zero map on compact \(K_0\), and the combined corona consequences are the specific statement isolated here. Search comparisons with the cited primary sources did not identify this exact combination, but bibliographic completeness is not claimed.

## Limitations

The theorem does **not** compute \(K_0(A)\) or \(K_1(A)\) numerically for an arbitrary space; it identifies the corona groups relative to them. The \(K_1\) splitting depends on the choice of a ray, and no result is asserted for spaces without a bounded-step simple ray. The sharp nuclear-dimension classification uses [1], a publicly available November 2026 preprint, as a previously established input; an independent refereeing outcome is not assumed. Equivalent formulations may exist in older extension or coarse-index literature, and this possibility remains a bibliographic risk.
