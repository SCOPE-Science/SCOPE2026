# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The support formula follows from the unique cyclic completion of each oriented old arc when the new point is adjacent to both endpoints. On a complete bipartite base, this forces a mixed support to span a monochromatically oriented rectangle; single-shore supports have independent two-way arc choices. Coefficientwise discrete convexity of \((1+z)^m-1\), the six paired-row groups and nonnegative triple groups prove the sharp lower bound. Strict convexity at \(z=1\) recovers every equality condition. A direct mixed-support inequality proves the upper bound and uniqueness. The symbolic arguments work for all \(b\ge1\), and the checker exhaustively confirms all \(8^b\) orientations through \(b=6\), with independent ternary-state enumeration through \(b=3\).

## Originality — PASS with historical priority risk

The initiating 2014 paper concerns the typical global structure of \(T_3\)-free oriented graphs; it does not state the graded finite-base one-point profile. The 2015 free-amalgamation paper supplies an ambient framework, while the 2015 bipartite colouring paper treats path and cycle covering rather than all monochromatic subrectangle counts. The claimed result is the full all-coefficient minimax theorem on oriented \(K_{3,b}\), including the mod-six equality classification and exponential mixed-type separation, not the background extension correspondence or elementary convexity. Related specialized matrix-enumeration literature may use different terminology and remains a priority risk; search failure is not a proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS

The theorem determines the entire graded logical extension spectrum in a natural, variable-width family and simultaneously classifies every extremizing orientation. It makes the structural effect of direction visible even though all bases have the identical underlying complete bipartite graph: universal balance over six signatures is optimal at every degree, and coherent orientations produce an exponentially larger population of mixed extensions. This supplies a reusable extremal principle and a sharp boundary family rather than a routine substitution or isolated small count.

## Scope and verification limits

Exactly the three-by-\(b\) complete bipartite family is covered, with all parameters individually named. The finite checker is corroboration, not a proof of the infinite family. Possible equivalent older matrix statements have not been independently ruled out.
