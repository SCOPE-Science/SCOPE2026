The finite bipartite Hölder argument and strict equality analysis prove coefficientwise dominance; directed mixed two-stars give the exact cubic difference. **Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority).** Hatami's weak Hölder inequality for complete bipartite graphs supplies the non-strict step, which is credited as prior work. The equality/strictness mechanism, cubic directed-two-path deficit and balanced random-orientation expectation and collapse were not covered by the primary texts inspected or targeted published-finding corpus results. Galvin–Tetali treat weighted homomorphisms in a different extremal setting; Conant supplies free amalgamation context. Unindexed equivalents remain possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result gives a sharp all-degree reconstruction of the loss of finite-base one-point type freedom, identifies the local obstruction and proves a large exponential contrast between coherent and randomly oriented named bases. These are connected structural facts beyond the preexisting weak inequality.

The scope is restricted to three-sorted directed-triangle-free generic graphs with complete bipartite old bases, third-sort new points and fair independent random orientations. Neither higher-arity types nor biased orientations are treated.
