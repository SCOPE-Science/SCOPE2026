# Scientific review: bipartite KMP cold-pair covariance sign reversal

## Correctness — PASS

The assumptions, generator, bath normalization, and domain \(n\ge3\), \(g,T,\Delta>0\) are explicit in the result. The degree-one generator equations and all eight independent degree-two equations have been reconstructed. Their stated determinant is positive over the entire domain. Eliminating those variables gives the exact normalized \(QQ\) and \(PQ\) covariance formulas and the \(PP\)-to-\(PQ\) relation. An exact symbolic elimination certificate proves the formulas as polynomial identities. Full-state-pair moment equations, not using the orbit reduction, reproduced these formulas in twelve rational tests with nonzero background temperature. Descartes' rule gives a unique positive quartic root, and the factored large-size limit yields the stated threshold. Finite computations are corroborative, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence

The heat-exchange dynamics is classical. The original chain model and Bertini–Gabrielli–Lebowitz (2005) concern boundary-driven linear heat transport; de Masi–Ferrari–Gabrielli (2023) give a general invariant mixture for designated-boundary graph dynamics with no edges joining two boundary vertices. These are not the all-vertex-refresh biclique model. Published comparison material on local mass-exchange covariance reversals and graph-based exclusion resetting involves different generators. A same-model complete-graph impurity calculation shows strictly positive cold–cold covariance at every rate; this cannot imply the opposite-shore negative covariance here. The examined sources did not yield the displayed finite-size quartic, unique threshold, or full cold-pair sign classification. A broader unlocated exact moment formula remains a possible priority risk; this does not constitute global novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural homogeneous two-shore geometry, a single local temperature impurity, and a physically interpretable bath-versus-exchange rate produce a qualitative sign inversion *between identically thermostatted cold vertices*. The other cold–cold pair classes remain positive at every rate. This is more than computing one finite graph or giving a routine perturbative coefficient: the exact classification holds for an infinite family, with a sharp transition and nontrivial large-degree constant \((1+\sqrt3)/2\). It also provides a controlled graph-topology counterpoint to the complete-graph impurity calculation.
