# Scientific review: one-rung majority prism response

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem uses the labelled graph \(C_n\square K_2\) with the vertical edge in one chosen column removed and an explicitly specified degree-two incumbent tie rule. The two-time column transfer represents precisely \(\operatorname{Fix}(F^t)\), for \(t=1,2\), with the lone defective column represented by a distinct transition matrix. The certificate reconstructs both 16- and 256-state transfer matrices, computes their cyclic-component determinant factors using exact integer Newton identities, and tests the complete rational generating-function identities up to the full Cayley–Hamilton bounds. These finite matrix identities, not bounded simulation, imply the results for all \(n\ge3\). A second, unrelated simulator enumerates every binary state through nine columns. The leading golden coefficients are verified by exact polynomial reductions, and the general period-one/two restriction is supplied by a symmetric-energy argument. The conclusion does not count basins or transient times.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The nearest existing result on **minority** dynamics with one deleted rung already establishes the same equilibrium-amplification constant \(3/\sqrt5\); the present work does not claim that numerical constant by itself as new. On even prisms the minority fixed-state formula in fact implies the majority fixed-state formula by bipartite spin switching. That covered subcase is explicitly credited. On odd prisms the majority and minority corrections differ. More importantly, the published minority work is restricted to fixed configurations and has no two-time return census or opposite two-cycle response. The intact-prism Lucas census gives the required baseline, not the defective two-step numerator. The primary papers by Goles–Olivos, Agur–Fraenkel–Klein and Jen give respectively a general period restriction, a one-dimensional different neighborhood, and a general transfer technique, not this evaluated two-rail one-bond defect theorem. The evaluated period-two rational function and complementary relative-response identity survived statement-level comparisons. An obscure older exact defect calculation remains a genuine priority risk.

## Value — PASS
A circular two-rail cubic strip is a standard finite spin-network test family. Removing one rung is its minimal local failure, and forces the tie-retention boundary convention at exactly two vertices. The result is not merely a finite transfer construction: it evaluates the entire all-circumference primitive-two spectrum and demonstrates an exact, countervailing golden-ratio amplitude response to that one defect, despite unchanged spatial entropy. The majority fixed-state six-residue law makes the odd-versus-even switching boundary explicit. It provides a reproducible quantitative comparison of metastability and oscillation on a natural infinite graph family, without claiming downstream applications or unproved basin behavior.

## Scope and remaining risks
The theorem concerns already recurrent labelled states, not attraction basins. At two-neighbor ties it requires incumbent retention. The supplied finite integer certificate is reproducible but has not been independently mathematically audited; earlier unindexed literature might evaluate the same defect. Goles–Olivos full text was unavailable, although its accessible abstract states the general period-two restriction and not an exact prism count. The precise first-public day used for dating is the verified 2014-05-11 arXiv version; older publications have less precise verified public dates.
