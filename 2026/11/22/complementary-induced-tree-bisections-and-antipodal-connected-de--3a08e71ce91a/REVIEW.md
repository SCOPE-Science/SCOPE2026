# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The general cubic edge count proves every connected feedback vertex set of the Heawood graph has at least seven vertices and that a size-seven optimum is exactly one half of a bipartition into two induced trees. The Fano incidence verifier exhausts all 3,432 seven-subsets, constructs 336 incidence automorphisms, and checks every breadth-first exchange distance; an independently implemented Hamiltonian/LCF C++17 verifier exhausts all 16,384 vertex masks and every exchange distance. Both return 770 optimal sets, 6,888 edges, 5 orbit sizes (the first verifier), and exactly 5,460 detour pairs. A fixed 14-vertex isomorphism checks the two models describe the same graph. The proof and executable membership predicates expose all finite boundaries, not merely recorded output.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historic priority risk.** The 2015 connected-feedback-vertex-set paper investigates general prices of connectivity in forbidden-induced-subgraph classes; the inspected theorem statements are not this exact Heawood classification. The 2014 induced-forest partition paper gives general existence and equitable partition results, not the count or structure of complementary *trees*. The closest published same-object feedback result determines 70 unconstrained optimum four-sets; its full RESULT text was inspected, and no implication yields the constrained seven-set number or exchange geometry. The Heawood induced-forest-complex result proves a homotopy equivalence and character identity, not complementary connected-tree bisections or their exchange graph. Related cubic decycling edge-count identities are acknowledged as known. Search did not locate equivalent counts under connected FVS, complementary induced-tree bisection, decycling or exchange aliases. The 1993 framed-tree paper and 2022 cyclic-connectivity paper were only accessible in abstract; unindexed prior computations remain a residual risk, not a claim of universal novelty proof.

**Value — PASS.** The classical, highly symmetric cubic 6-cage provides a natural test case for the cost of connected cycle breaking and equitable induced-tree partitions. The exact increase from four to seven, the complete 770-set/385-bisection enumeration, five group orbits, and the sharp one-detour exchange metric distinguish optimization from *mobility*; uniqueness of the complementary antipode is a nontrivial structural feature that is not an automatic consequence of connectedness. The precise invariant is motivated independently of the computation, and has potential use as a small high-symmetry benchmark.

**Limitations and nearest coverage.** The claim concerns one 14-vertex graph and is an exact finite certificate, not a uniform theorem. The earlier published unconstrained Heawood feedback-set classification is a necessary comparator, not a prior proof of the current constrained result. Source access for the two older, title-relevant papers remains limited, and independent scholarly validation remains to be performed.
