# Scientific review: complete balanced-cut entanglement census of the Heawood graph state

## Correctness — PASS

The finding concerns all \(\binom{14}{7}=3432\) labelled balanced cuts of the exact fourteen-qubit graph state and no larger family. From the normalized state amplitudes, the reduced-state spectrum is flat with binary entropy equal to the cross-adjacency rank. Reordering the point–line cut yields two exact square incidence blocks. Direct elimination of all \(3432\) cross-cut matrices agrees with independent cyclic-Fano block elimination on rank multiplicities \(100,980,1680,672\) at entropy \(4,5,6,7\), respectively. The first script also checks the exact mean \(5021/858\), point–line rank four and the two maximal-entanglement crossing-edge classes, each with 336 oriented cuts. Both programs are finite exhaustive certificates for this graph; no general-size inference is made.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence

Hein, Eisert and Briegel (2003) already prove the universal graph-state cut-rank/Schmidt-spectrum formula, which is credited. Cosentino and Severini (2009) develop related binary-rank tools. A closely related public result on projective-plane incidence-matching faults already covers the canonical four-bit point-versus-line baseline and omitted-gate responses; it **does not** claim a census across the other mixed seven-versus-seven vertex bipartitions. A Heawood linear-arrangement result exhausts edge-boundary extrema but not entropy ranks. The examined graph-state database states coverage only through twelve qubits. Neither those primary scientific results nor the other examined published and indexed records imply the complete \(3432\)-cut histogram and the 11-or-13-crossing-edge refinement. An unlocated equivalent rank-invariant table remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Heawood graph is a natural cubic incidence graph rather than an arbitrarily sliced dataset; complete balanced-cut entanglement is a physically interpretable quantum resource invariant. The exact rank histogram identifies every balanced entanglement class and quantifies the sharp distinction between the canonical maximum-edge cut (21 gates but four ebits) and the 672 maximum-entropy cuts (seven ebits but only 11 or 13 crossing gates). The all-cut invariant is not just a reformulation of the general rank identity without graph-specific work.

## Scientific limitations

The result applies only to this ideal fourteen-qubit pure graph state and balanced vertex cuts. It does not imply a classification for larger finite projective planes or for noisy or weighted preparations, and it is not independently validated or certified as historically unique. The two separately implemented algorithms constitute a reproducibility cross-check, not an external audit.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
