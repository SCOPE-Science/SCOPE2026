# Review: complete Cartier rank profiles for the genus-three Chebyshev octic

## Correctness — PASS

The polynomial is squarefree in every characteristic \(p\ge5\), with discriminant \(2^{31}\). The tame involution on \(w^2=z^{16}+1\) has invariant quotient \(y^2=\varphi_8(x)\), and invariant cohomology is three-dimensional. The monomial Hasse–Witt coefficient support is nonzero precisely on the residue arrows \(pi\equiv j\pmod{16}\); complementary binomial coefficients agree, so the restriction has no cancellation. The eight residue diagrams prove the entire Cartier-rank table for all primes. A direct original-curve checker passed on 44 primes below 200. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The initiating 2026 Chebyshev automorphism classification [1] does not give Hasse–Witt ranks. The 2019, 2025 CM-theoretic, and 2026 maximality papers [2,7,3] do imply the already-known superspecial \(p\equiv15\pmod{16}\) case, but not the other seven rows or their exact iterated ranks and \(a\)-numbers. A full comparison with the closest existing modulus-14 Dickson Cartier profile [6] shows different equations and different nonmaximal behavior: its rank always stabilizes at zero except in the ordinary class, whereas this octic has rank-one and rank-two stable classes. The 2009 genus-three Fermat-quotient examples [4] are different curves. General Fermat Hasse–Witt work [5] and Kodama–Washio hyperelliptic calculations (1990), available only in part, remain priority risks; absence of a search hit is not a proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS

The classification supplies the full arithmetic rank stratification of a natural genus-three Chebyshev curve. In particular, it distinguishes Jacobians of identical \(p\)-rank by \(a\)-number and separates rank-zero nonsuperspecial reductions from the known superspecial class. The exact periodic table and its structural proof are not mere restatements of a maximality criterion.

## Limitations

The result is degree-specific and makes no assertion on complete Newton slopes, full Ekedahl–Oort types, or varying Chebyshev degrees. The last table row has prior coverage; the contribution is the remaining nonmaximal profile and its complete uniform classification. Independent verification remains to be performed.
