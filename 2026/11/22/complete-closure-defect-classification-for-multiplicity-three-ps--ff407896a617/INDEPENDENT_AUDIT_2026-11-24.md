# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

A multiplicity-three semigroup is exactly three residue rays, with Apéry representatives 0, 3a+1, 3b+2 and a,b>=1. The two same-residue cross-closure inequalities are b<=2a and a<=2b+1; genus a+b and F=max(3a+1,3b+2)−3 follow by counting. For even F no pair x,F−x can both be present, and F/2 is absent; equality 2g=F+2 is therefore equivalent to pseudo-symmetry. With the two possible larger Apéry representatives it gives b=2a−1 or a=2b. Both families satisfy the inequalities, with the stated F. The two candidate pseudo-Frobenius gaps are the last gaps in the two nonzero residues, and their difference is the smaller gap itself, so neither dominates the other in the semigroup order; hence PF={F/2,F}. Under the exact row-deletion operation from Theorem 4.2, a member below F/2 is untouched and a member above it shifts down by one. Applying this to the three rays gives all three claimed residues in each family, including the entire tail. For A_a the new ray starts in residues 0,2,1 are respectively 0,3a−1,6a−2, precisely the semigroup generated by 3 and 3a−1. For B_b adding any multiple of three to a nonzero residue stays on its ray; sums of types 1+1 and 2+2 reach at least the required opposite ray starts, and 1+2 is at least 6b. A high multiple of three is harmless. Thus only the low positive multiples 3u and 3v contribute, with 1<=u,v<=b−1 and u+v>=b. Their sums fill the asserted progression, and counting v for each u gives 1+...+(b−1)=b(b−1)/2 ordered pairs. At b=1 the defect is empty and the residue sets give exactly <2,5>. At b=2 the source <3,8,13> gives {0,3,5,7,8} union [10,infinity), with sole defect 3+3=6. The supplied finite checker was inspected, not executed; the unrestricted calculation does not depend on its reported a,b<=30 coverage.

## value

PASS

Multiplicity three is the first possible multiplicity for pseudo-symmetric numerical semigroups. A precise explanation of when a symmetry construction preserves closure in this entire first family is useful beyond known nonclosed examples. The two exact output descriptions expose qualitatively different branches: uniform closure on one branch and unbounded distinct missing sums with quadratic ordered-pair growth on the other. Those counts are informative about the construction, not merely about a single small Frobenius bound.
