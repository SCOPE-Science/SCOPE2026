# Complete closure-defect classification for multiplicity-three pseudo-symmetric symmetrization

## Finding
Let \(\Phi\) be the numerical-set symmetrization defined by deleting the Young-diagram rows indexed by the nonmaximal pseudo-Frobenius numbers of an almost symmetric numerical semigroup, as in Delgado, Süer and Yeşil, Theorem 4.2. Every pseudo-symmetric numerical semigroup of multiplicity three is in exactly one of the families
\[
 A_a=\langle3,3a+1,6a-1\rangle\quad(a\ge1),\qquad
 B_b=\langle3,3b+2,6b+1\rangle\quad(b\ge1).
\]
Their respective Frobenius numbers are \(6a-4\) and \(6b-2\). For every \(a\ge1\),
\[
 \Phi(A_a)=\langle3,3a-1\rangle,
\]
which is additively closed. For every \(b\ge1\), the image \(T_b=\Phi(B_b)\) is exactly the cofinite numerical set described by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x\equiv0\pmod3 &: x\le3b-3\ \text{or}\ x\ge6b,\\
 x\equiv1\pmod3 &: x\ge3b+1,\\
 x\equiv2\pmod3 &: x\ge3b-1,
\end{aligned}
\]
where each condition is an equivalence for nonnegative integers \(x\). It is a numerical semigroup exactly when \(b=1\), in which case \(T_1=\langle2,5\rangle\). If \(b\ge2\), the complete set of positive missing sums is
\[
 D(T_b)=\{x+y:x,y\in T_b\setminus\{0\},\ x+y\notin T_b\}
 =\{3b,3b+3,\ldots,6b-6\}.
\]
Thus \(|D(T_b)|=b-1\). Exactly \(b(b-1)/2\) ordered positive pairs violate additive closure, namely \((x,y)=(3u,3v)\) with \(1\le u,v\le b-1\) and \(u+v\ge b\).

## Assumptions and scope
A numerical semigroup is a cofinite additively closed subset of the nonnegative integers containing zero. The *multiplicity* is its least positive element. Its gaps, genus, Frobenius number, and pseudo-Frobenius set are denoted \(G(S)\), \(g(S)\), \(F(S)\), and \(\operatorname{PF}(S)\). A semigroup is *pseudo-symmetric* when \(F(S)\) is even and \(G(S)\setminus\{F(S)/2\}\) is complementary under \(x\mapsto F(S)-x\); equivalently its type is two, \(\operatorname{PF}(S)=\{F(S)/2,F(S)\}\), and \(2g(S)=F(S)+2\). The source construction is fixed to the original-row deletion in Theorem 4.2 of the cited 2026 paper. Its output need not be closed under addition. The defect set records distinct missing sum *values*; the separately stated pair count is for ordered pairs. No assertions concern arbitrary higher multiplicities or injectivity/surjectivity of \(\Phi\) on all numerical sets.

## Proof
**Step 1: complete source classification.** For any multiplicity-three numerical semigroup, let its Apéry set with respect to three be \(\operatorname{Ap}(S,3)=\{0,3a+1,3b+2\}\). Both coordinates satisfy \(a,b\ge1\). The two nontrivial residue-addition tests give exactly \(b\le2a\) and \(a\le2b+1\). Conversely these inequalities make the specified three residue rays closed under addition. Its genus and Frobenius number are
\[
 g(S)=a+b,\qquad F(S)=\max(3a+1,3b+2)-3.
\]
Pseudo-symmetry forces \(2(a+b)=F(S)+2\). If \(3b+2\) is the larger Apéry element, solving gives \(b=2a-1\); if \(3a+1\) is the larger, solving gives \(a=2b\). Both families satisfy the Apéry inequalities for all positive parameters. In the first case the two Apéry elements minus three are \(3a-2\) and \(6a-4\), and in the second they are \(3b-1\) and \(6b-2\); neither pair is comparable under the semigroup order. Therefore these are exactly the two pseudo-Frobenius gaps, the smaller being half the Frobenius number, verifying pseudo-symmetry also in the reverse direction.

**Step 2: the output arithmetic.** For either family \(\operatorname{PF}(S)=\{k,2k\}\), the source construction deletes just the row at \(k\). The literal membership transformation is: retain each source element below \(k\); replace each element above \(k\) by that element minus one; and include the whole eventual tail. In family \(A_a\), \(k=3a-2\). The original residue-zero ray splits into \(0,3,\ldots,3a-3\) and \(3a,3a+3,\ldots\), the latter shifting to a residue-two ray beginning at \(3a-1\). The residue-one ray beginning at \(3a+1\) shifts to a residue-zero ray beginning at \(3a\), and the residue-two ray beginning at \(6a-1\) shifts to a residue-one ray beginning at \(6a-2\). These are precisely the three residue rays generated by \(3\) and \(3a-1\), so the claimed equality follows even at \(a=1\).

In family \(B_b\), \(k=3b-1\). The residue-zero ray below \(k\) is \(0,3,\ldots,3b-3\); the original residue-zero tail at \(3b,3b+3,\ldots\) shifts to residue two starting at \(3b-1\). The original residue-two ray starts at \(3b+2\) and shifts to residue one starting at \(3b+1\). The residue-one ray starts at \(6b+1\) and shifts to residue zero starting at \(6b\). This proves every entry of the stated membership criterion. In particular, the new Frobenius number is \(6b-3\), in agreement with the general symmetrization theorem.

**Step 3: exhaustive description of defects.** All sums above \(6b-3\) automatically belong to \(T_b\). Among positive elements not beyond that bound, adding a residue-one or residue-two member to any positive member cannot leave the corresponding residue ray: the mixed-residue thresholds are met, or the result exceeds the Frobenius number. A residue-zero member from the high tail is similarly harmless. Thus a missing sum, if any, consists of two low positive residue-zero members \(3u\) and \(3v\), with \(1\le u,v\le b-1\). Their sum lies in a forbidden residue-zero gap precisely if \(u+v\ge b\). As \(u+v\le2b-2\), the distinct possible defects are exactly \(3b,3b+3,\ldots,6b-6\), and every such sum occurs (choose \(u=b-1\) and \(v\) from \(1\) to \(b-1\)). Among the \((b-1)^2\) ordered pairs, precisely \((b-1)(b-2)/2\) have \(u+v\le b-1\). Hence the violating-pair count is \((b-1)^2-(b-1)(b-2)/2=b(b-1)/2\). The set is closed exactly for \(b=1\), when its residue formula gives \(\langle2,5\rangle\). This completes the proof for all positive parameters.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library checker `verify_symmetrization.py` enumerates every valid Apéry-coordinate pair \(1\le a,b\le30\), constructs source memberships and gaps directly, computes pseudo-Frobenius gaps from their definition, performs original-row deletion, and compares the complete output up to the conductor with the two formulas. It independently enumerates all ordered positive missing-sum pairs and checks the exact values and counts. The checker returns `VERIFY_OK` and records 30 pseudo-symmetric input cases. This bounded test is independent evidence for small instances; the preceding argument, rather than finite enumeration, proves the unrestricted result.

## Relationship to prior work
Delgado, Süer and Yeşil (2026), Theorem 4.2, introduce the symmetrization, prove symmetric output, and enumerate finite examples of nonclosed targets (including the first example at source Frobenius number ten). Those facts are not asserted as new here. Existing work classifying fibers with multiplicity-two outputs already covers the two boundary outputs \(\langle2,3\rangle\) and \(\langle2,5\rangle\); the present result does not claim those special cases as original. The contribution is the uniform classification of **all** multiplicity-three pseudo-symmetric inputs under the exact new map and the infinite, sharp closure-defect spectrum. An independent source establishes that the image of the general symmetrization omits the number one; that global obstruction is compatible with these outputs and does not imply this classification. Standard Apéry-coordinate facts are used as known tools, not new results.

## Limitations
No claim is made about other multiplicities, the full image or full fibers of the symmetrization, or global source uniqueness. This is a self-review only, not an independent mathematical audit. Unindexed prior results may predate the explicit formulas; the literature comparison therefore supplies a qualified originality assessment, not a guarantee of historical priority. Verification bounds are not substituted for an infinite proof.
