# Complete complement–reversal census of primitive binary six-window universal cycles

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal P_6\) denote the binary words of length six that are **primitive**: they are not repetitions of a shorter word. A cyclic binary word \(W\) is a primitive six-window universal cycle if its circular length-six windows list every member of \(\mathcal P_6\) exactly once and no other word.

There are exactly \(54\) primitive windows. **Exactly \(389120\) universal cycles exist modulo cyclic rotation.** Among them exactly \(320\) are invariant under complementation of every bit, up to rotation. None is invariant up to rotation under reversal or under reversal followed by bit-complementation. Therefore the action generated by bit-complementation and reversal has exactly \(97360\) orbits: \(160\) orbits of size two and \(97200\) orbits of size four.

## Assumptions and scope
All words are over \(\{0,1\}\). A length-six word is imprimitive precisely when it has a proper period dividing six, hence period one, two, or three. A cyclic \(54\)-bit host is identified only up to rotation for the first count; complementation and reversal are then additional operations on the set of such rotation classes. Windows wrap cyclically. The claims concern exactly six-window words, not all block lengths and not a shortened or reversal-unoriented covering model.

## Proof
**1. Edge set and balance.** Write a six-bit word as an integer \(e\) between \(0\) and \(63\), most significant bit first. It is primitive precisely when, for each \(d\in\{1,2,3\}\), at least one index \(j\in\{0,\ldots,5\}\) has bit \(j\) different from bit \(j\bmod d\). There are two words of least period one, two of least period two, and six of least period three, leaving \(64-2-2-6=54\) primitive words. Construct a directed graph \(G\) on the \(32\) binary five-letter vertices with the edge \(e\) from its first five bits to its last five bits, for each primitive \(e\). Rotation of a primitive six-letter word remains primitive. Rotating the first bit to the end bijects incoming edges at every five-bit vertex with outgoing edges, so \(G\) is balanced. Its out-degree multiset comprises \(22\) twos and \(10\) ones. In particular, vertex `00000` has the unique outgoing edge `000001`.

**2. Exact circuit count.** For this graph let \(L\) be the integral out-degree Laplacian, with \(L_{u,u}\) increased and \(L_{u,v}\) decreased once for each directed edge \(u\to v\). Deleting the row and column of vertex `00000` gives a \(31\)-square matrix \(L_0\). An exact integer determinant is
\[
\det L_0=389120=2^{12}\cdot5\cdot19.
\]
The matrix-tree theorem counts this many in-arborescences toward `00000`. Positivity, with balance, implies that all positive-degree vertices lie in one strongly connected component, hence \(G\) admits Euler tours. By the BEST theorem, cyclic Euler tours number \(\det L_0\prod_v(\deg^+(v)-1)!\). Every out-degree is one or two, so this product is one. Moreover, every tour contains edge `000001` once, giving a canonical rotation root. Thus there are exactly \(389120\) primitive six-window universal cycles modulo rotation. There are no omitted vertices or parallel-edge multiplicities hidden in this count.

**3. Complement-fixed cycles.** Bit-complementation \(C\) pairs every vertex with its five-bit complement and every edge with its six-bit complement. The quotient digraph \(H=G/C\) has \(16\) vertices and \(27\) directed edges, with out-degree multiset eleven twos and five ones. Represent each quotient vertex by the integer between \(0\) and \(15\) for the five-bit string beginning with zero. Represent each quotient edge by its primitive six-bit member \(e\) beginning with zero (so \(0\le e<32\)); its start is \(e\gg1\), and its endpoint representative is the smaller of \(e\bmod32\) and its five-bit complement. This specifies the quotient Laplacian without choices. Its cofactor rooted at the vertex pair containing `00000` has exact determinant
\[
\det L_H^{(0)}=320.
\]
The quotient cover is a two-sheeted voltage graph. The voltage of an edge is one if the last five bits of its chosen representative begin with one. Exactly thirteen of the \(27\) quotient edges have voltage one. Every Euler tour of \(H\) therefore has odd total voltage and lifts to one \(54\)-edge Euler tour of \(G\), rather than to two shorter disjoint lifts; the lift is exchanged with itself after \(27\) edges by complementation. Conversely, if a primitive universal cycle is fixed by complementation up to rotation, its binary length-\(54\) word must satisfy \(C(W)=\rho^{27}(W)\): any other shift is incompatible with the free occurrence of each six-window and the involutive nature of \(C\). Its first \(27\) edge classes form a quotient Euler tour. These are inverse operations once edge `000001` fixes the root. Consequently the number of complement-fixed rotation classes equals the quotient Euler-tour count. The BEST local factorial product is again one, so this is \(320\).

**4. Reversal exclusions and orbit count.** The primitive six-bit words fixed by reversal are precisely `001100`, `011110`, `100001`, `110011`: four palindromes. The primitive six-bit words fixed by reversal followed by bit-complementation are `000111`, `001011`, `011001`, `100110`, `110100`, `111000`: six antipalindromes. Any reflection of a \(54\)-cycle induces an involution on its \(54\) window-start positions having at most two fixed points. If a universal cycle were invariant under either reversed operation up to rotation, every corresponding palindrome or antipalindrome window would have to occupy a fixed start position. Four or six such windows cannot occupy at most two positions. Hence both fixed sets are empty.

Burnside's lemma for the four-element group generated by \(C\) and reversal \(R\) now yields
\[
\frac{389120+320+0+0}{4}=97360
\]
orbits. The \(320\) complement-fixed cycles have stabilizer of order two, producing \(160\) size-two orbits; the other \(388800\) have trivial stabilizer and give \(97200\) size-four orbits.

## Verification
The included `verify.py` is standard-library-only. Run `python3 verify.py` to reconstruct both graphs from the primitive-word predicate, compute both determinants using fraction-free integer elimination, verify all degree counts and voltages, and check the palindrome, antipalindrome and orbit arithmetic. It prints `VERIFY_OK`. Independently, compile the supplied exhaustive edge-walk enumerator with `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 enumerate.cpp -o enumerate` and run `./enumerate`. Anchoring every tour at edge `000001`, it enumerates all Euler tours directly and tests the four symmetry actions on the resulting cyclic strings; its output is `total=389120 F_C=320 F_R=0 F_CR=0 orbits=97360`. The determinant formulas and involution arguments prove exhaustiveness; the finite computations evaluate fully specified finite matrices rather than sample a larger conjecture.

## Relationship to prior work
The de Bruijn/BEST/matrix-tree framework is classical. Ruskey, Sawada and Williams (2012) construct cycles for fixed-content encodings and establish a relevant binary universal-cycle setting; Sawada, Williams and Wong (2013 preprint, 2014 journal) use Lyndon/aperiodic prefixes for *weight-restricted* universal cycles. Using aperiodic prefixes in a construction is not the same as requiring every window to be primitive. Champlin, Godbole and Tomlinson (2013) establish existence for complementary word classes, but neither a primitive-six-word edge set nor its counts. Related aperiodic *orientable* sequences select windows up to reversal and are a different covering problem. The new finite result is the exact mixed-period-six whole-class count and its complement/reversal fixed-point census, not the general existence or counting theorems already known.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite-length result at the first binary length with both proper nontrivial divisor periods two and three. No formula for arbitrary composite window lengths or longer universality is claimed. The historical priority assessment is based on the identified sources and overlap searches, not a proof that no earlier unpublished, unindexed, or differently formulated enumeration exists. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
