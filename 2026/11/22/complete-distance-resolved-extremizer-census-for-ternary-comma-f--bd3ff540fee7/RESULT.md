# Complete distance-resolved extremizer census for ternary comma-free triple codes

## Finding
Let \(\Sigma=\{0,1,2\}\). A *comma-free triple code* is a subset \(C\subseteq\Sigma^3\) such that, for all \(x,y\in C\), neither of the two length-three splices \(x_2x_3y_1\) and \(x_3y_1y_2\) belongs to \(C\). Here codewords are literal indexed triples and the alphabet labels are fixed. For \(d\in\{2,3\}\), let \(M_d\) be the maximum cardinality of such a code with pairwise Hamming distance at least \(d\), and let \(E_d\) be the family of all labeled codebooks attaining that maximum.

The exact capacities and equality-case counts are
\[
\boxed{M_2=4,\quad |E_2|=132;\qquad M_3=3,\quad |E_3|=12.}
\]
Under the order-twelve group generated by simultaneous permutations of \(\Sigma\) and reversal of every triple, the respective numbers of equivalence classes are \(12\) and \(3\). The class-size multisets are \(\{6,6,12,12,12,12,12,12,12,12,12,12\}\) at distance two, and \(\{2,4,6\}\) at distance three.

## Assumptions and scope
The code length and alphabet size are exactly three. The Hamming distance condition applies only to distinct *aligned codewords*, whereas the comma-free condition excludes exact equality to a misaligned splice. In particular, distance two here means detection of one substitution when the word boundary is known; it does **not** claim correction of a substitution at unknown boundaries or comma-free index at least two. No linearity, constant composition, cyclic closure, or additional channel condition is imposed. Both the codebooks and the alphabet are labeled; symmetry is used only for the supplementary classification.

## Proof
**Reduction to eight primitive rotation classes.** Any constant triple is forbidden: it reappears as an internal window when concatenated with itself. If both \(abc\) and one of its two nontrivial cyclic rotations belonged to \(C\), concatenating \(abc\) with itself would reproduce that rotation at a forbidden offset. Thus every admissible code uses at most one word from each nonconstant cyclic rotation orbit. There are exactly \((3^3-3)/3=8\) such orbits, each containing three words. Consequently **every possible** comma-free triple code is represented uniquely by an eight-digit choice vector with entries in \(\{0,1,2,3\}\): zero omits the orbit and the other three digits select one rotation. There are exactly \(4^8=65536\) choice vectors. This is an exhaustive finite parameterization, not a sampling argument.

**Exact optimal sizes and labeled counts.** For any selected set, test every unordered pair of codewords for the required Hamming distance. Then, independently, test every ordered pair \((x,y)\), including \(x=y\), by constructing both splices and requiring that neither is selected. These two elementary predicates are logically equivalent to the stated hypotheses. The complete eight-orbit exhaustive evaluation yields the following histograms, in which each entry counts distinct labeled *admissible* subsets of the indicated cardinality:

The complete literal loops appear in `verify_rotations.cpp`. The code enumerates exactly the mathematically described \(4^8\) selections, checks the predicates directly, and asserts every histogram entry. Since all candidate sets are represented, this proves the asserted finite counts and cardinality bounds. Separately, `verify_subsets.py` tests all unordered subsets of the entire \(27\)-word universe up to size five for distance two and up to size four for distance three; it finds \(132\) valid four-word codes but no valid five-word code at distance two, and \(12\) valid three-word codes but no valid four-word code at distance three. Every larger code would contain a forbidden five- or four-element subcode, respectively, so this independently proves the upper bounds without relying on the rotation reduction. The direct enumeration includes \(\binom{27}{5}=80730\) five-element subsets and \(\binom{27}{4}=17550\) four-element subsets.

**Equality-class classification.** The simultaneous alphabet-permutation action preserves both conditions. Word reversal interchanges the two forbidden splice positions after swapping the concatenated word order; it also preserves Hamming distance. Thus \(S_3\times C_2\) acts on all maxima. The direct checker evaluates all twelve transformations of each maximum, forms its orbit, and verifies that its orbit lies entirely in the computed maximum family. It finds the representatives and orbit sizes displayed above; their totals are \(2\cdot6+10\cdot12=132\) and \(2+4+6=12\). As a second count at distance two, the identity fixes all \(132\) maxima, each of the three nonidentity transpositions of symbols fixes four, and the remaining eight symmetries fix none, so Burnside's lemma gives \((132+3\cdot4)/12=12\) classes. At distance three, the complete listed orbit decomposition gives three classes. This finishes the finite, exact classification.

## Verification
The two independently implemented exhaustive programs are `verify_subsets.py` (standard-library Python) and `verify_rotations.cpp` (C++17). Both were compiled or executed directly from these exact source files and passed their embedded assertions. Run `python3 verify_subsets.py`, or compile with `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify_rotations.cpp -o verify_rotations` and run the resulting binary. The first program reconstructs all qualifying subsets from the \(27\)-word universe without using necklace reduction and checks all listed orbit classes. The second reconstructs all eight rotation classes and all \(65536\) possible choices, including the empty code. These are exact finite certificates, not statistical experiments or claims about unbounded block lengths.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical comma-free code theory of Golomb, Gordon and Welch and the later construction of Eastman establish maximum sizes for odd lengths when no aligned Hamming-distance constraint is imposed. Direct reading of Levenshtein’s 2004 full survey, Sections 2–4, confirms that its displayed theorems treat the unrestricted primitive-necklace upper bound, splice-to-word comma-free index and without-overlap constructions rather than the exact ternary three-letter joint distance-two/three census here. The 2009 study by Churchill discusses comma-free dictionaries and their rotation-orbit upper bounds, but it does not provide the simultaneous aligned-error-detection equality census above. A published classification of *length-three two-symbol-overlap-free codes* gives \(42\) maximal ternary eight-word dictionaries; it imposes a different (stronger) boundary-overlap condition, and its maxima all have aligned Hamming distance one. Thus it cannot imply the present exact distance-two or distance-three optima or their enumerations. A separate exact classification of ternary length-three *comma-free-index-two* codes gives nine optimal four-word dictionaries, each with aligned Hamming distance one. Its requirement of Hamming separation from splices is not the present requirement of Hamming separation among aligned codewords; neither equality census implies the other. These distinction checks use the actual word predicates, not merely different article titles.

## Limitations
Only ternary triples and aligned pairwise distances two and three are classified. The capacity for general alphabets, block lengths, or stronger noisy misalignment models is not asserted. The exhaustive certificates are finite complete proofs because the universe itself is finite; they do not yield asymptotic statements. Historical equivalence under another coding-theory formulation remains possible, especially in older non-digitized surveys or specialized code tables. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed.
