## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority risk

Cooper and Frieze (1995) define precisely the independent-edge nearest-neighbor graph and give an approximately \(3n/4\) edge count at selection order one; their main component-structure theorems concern selection orders two or higher. Berikkyzy et al. (2016) identify the forest-building process and total-component laws. The published *Exact mutual-nearest-neighbor component law in symmetric random-link graphs* (2026) derives the **number** of components via matching probabilities, not their size-resolved frequencies. A total-component distribution does not determine the component-size profile or prove the mass-sum identity. Kozakova et al. (2006) treat the different Euclidean Poisson geometric model; its title is not an implication of the independent complete-edge model. Direct searches for profile / Yule / nearest-neighbor aliases found no covering statement. ## Value — PASS

The known total number of components does not reveal whether the random-link forest has predominantly pairs, heterogeneous finite trees, or a macroscopic component. The exact positive size densities answer that natural fragmentation question and identify the component-size law for both vertex-sampling and component-sampling, with complete mass normalization and a no-giant conclusion. The result has a nontrivial marked growth mechanism and a family of explicit constants, rather than a re-expression of the known component count. It is directly relevant to nearest-neighbor network fragmentation and random forest growth.

## Scope and limitations

The weights are independent, identically distributed, atomless, and undirected on a complete graph. The exact order of the largest component, shape distribution within a fixed component size, and extensions to spatial or nonidentical weights are not proved.
