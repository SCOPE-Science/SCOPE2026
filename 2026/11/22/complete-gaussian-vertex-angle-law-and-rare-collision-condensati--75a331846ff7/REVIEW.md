# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reconstructs the normal fan from graphical arrangements, establishes a bijection between vertices and gap assignments, counts strict total-order extensions exactly, and derives collision moment formulas by counting multiplicities. The factorial-composition convolution estimate is bounded uniformly with the explicit \(4/s\) reciprocal-binomial bound. The three sampling limits and the one-edge deletion comparison follow algebraically. The standalone exhaustive permutation checker tests ten finite split graphs and four bipartite comparisons, but finite enumeration is not used as the proof of asymptotic claims.

## Originality — PASS (best available evidence, priority provisional)

The 2021 graphical-zonotope source treats normal fans and deformations, not measured spherical angles. The 2023 complete-multipartite source establishes orientation encodings and counts rather than normal-cone probabilities. The closest available angular-collision research concerns complete bipartite graphs, with no edges within their two color classes; the complete split graph contains a full clique and its angular fan is strictly refined. A separate complete-split result treats degree distributions, not angles. Indexed summaries of those closest prior research records were accessible; their complete primary files could not be retrieved here. The exact evaluated angle spectrum, all collision orders and three distinct conditioning limits were not found. Generic linear-extension formulas are background and do not themselves evaluate this family. An unindexed equivalent remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Angular exposure probabilities measure geometric stability under random linear optimization. The theorem provides a complete exact spectrum for an infinite nonsimplicial normal-fan family, with a polynomial rather than exponential effective collision scale, a sharp collision-conditioned concentration phenomenon, and a quantifiable \(3/5\) effect of a single added edge. These facts distinguish geometric sampling from uniformly sampling vertices; they are not the ordinary number of acyclic orientations.

## Scope and limitations

Fixed clique size in all asymptotic statements; the exact formulas cover all integer \(r,s\ge2\). The canonical Euclidean embedding matters. Historical priority remains provisional until broader human literature verification.
