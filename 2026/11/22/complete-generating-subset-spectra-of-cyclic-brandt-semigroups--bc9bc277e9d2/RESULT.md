# Complete generating-subset spectra of cyclic Brandt semigroups

## Finding

Let \(n\ge 2\), \(m\ge1\), and let \(B_n(C_m)\) be the Brandt semigroup whose nonzero elements are \((i,g,j)\) with \(i,j\in[n]\) and \(g\in C_m=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). Define its *nonzero generating-subset polynomial*
\[
G_{n,m}(z)=\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq B_n(C_m)\setminus\{0\}\\\langle A\rangle=B_n(C_m)}}z^{|A|}.
\]
Let \(S_n(t)\) count labeled strongly connected directed graphs on \([n]\), where each ordered pair (including loops) is allowed at most once, by the number of arcs. Then
\[
\boxed{G_{n,m}(z)=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)(m/d)^{n-1} S_n((1+z)^d-1).}
\tag{1}
\]
This is a fully explicit finite formula: put \(\mathcal C=\{U:\varnothing\ne U\subsetneq[n]\}\), and for \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal C\) put \(E_{\mathcal F}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal F} U\times([n]\setminus U)\). Then
\[
S_n(t)=\sum_{\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal C}(-1)^{|\mathcal F|}(1+t)^{n^2-|E_{\mathcal F}|}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, the minimum generating-subset size is \(n\) and its exact multiplicity is
\[
\boxed{[z^n]G_{n,m}(z)=(n-1)!m^{n-1}\varphi(m).}
\tag{3}
\]
No coefficient below degree \(n\) is nonzero; the leading coefficient \([z^{n^2m}]G_{n,m}(z)\) is one. If generating subsets are permitted to contain the zero element, their polynomial is \((1+z)G_{n,m}(z)\).

For example, direct expansion for \(n=2,m=2\) yields
\[
G_{2,2}(z)=2z^2+16z^3+39z^4+44z^5+26z^6+8z^7+z^8.
\]
There are 136 generating subsets not containing zero, distinguished by cardinality.

## Assumptions and scope

All generated subsemigroups are generated by **positive, nonempty words** in their given generators (not by adjoining inverses). For \(C_m\) we use additive notation. Multiplication is
\[
(i,g,j)(k,h,l)=\begin{cases}(i,g+h,l),&j=k,\\0,&j\ne k,\end{cases}
\]
and zero annihilates every element. The formula concerns labeled subsets of this semigroup, not ordered generating tuples or generating multisets. For \(m=1\) the cyclic group is trivial and \(\mu(1)=\varphi(1)=1\). The restriction \(n\ge2\) is essential for the automatic generation of zero from a strongly connected nonzero support: the one-index group-with-zero case needs an extra zero-generation convention.

## Proof

**Step 1: a criterion for generation.** For \(A\subseteq B_n(C_m)\setminus\{0\}\), draw one directed arc \(i\to j\) with label \(g\) for every \((i,g,j)\in A\); multiple labels on an ordered pair are permitted. Products of generators are nonzero precisely when their index sequence forms a directed walk; the group entry of the product is the sum of its arc labels. Therefore the directed support must be strongly connected to produce every ordered pair of indices.

Conversely, suppose the support is strongly connected, fix a root \(r\), and let \(H(A)\le C_m\) be the set of gains of all **positive closed walks at \(r\)**. Concatenation makes this a nonempty additive subsemigroup of the finite group \(C_m\); it is hence a subgroup. The zero-gain closed word is available as a positive power of any closed walk. For every \(i,j\), choose paths from \(i\) to \(r\) and from \(r\) to \(j\). Inserting a closed walk at \(r\) proves that all entries in a coset of \(H(A)\) occur at position \((i,j)\). If \(H(A)=C_m\), every possible labeled matrix unit occurs. The zero element also occurs: with \(n\ge2\), a strong support contains a nonloop arc, whose square is zero. Thus \(\langle A\rangle=B_n(C_m)\) exactly when the support is strongly connected and \(H(A)=C_m\).

**Step 2: potential classification of proper loop-gain subgroups.** Fix the unique subgroup \(H_d\le C_m\) of order \(d\mid m\), and let \(Q_d=C_m/H_d\), of size \(m/d\). Suppose the support is strongly connected and \(H(A)\subseteq H_d\). Every root-to-vertex path then has the same gain modulo \(H_d\): for two such paths, append a common return path to the root and compare the resulting closed walks. Denote the unique residue by \(p_i\in Q_d\), normalized by \(p_r=0\). For every selected arc \((i,g,j)\), we consequently have \(g+H_d=p_j-p_i\). Conversely, if every selected arc obeys these equations for some vertex potentials, every directed closed walk has gain in \(H_d\). Strong connectivity makes the normalized potentials unique.

There are \((m/d)^{n-1}\) choices of potentials. For each ordered pair \((i,j)\), the allowed group labels form **one coset with exactly \(d\) elements**. One may select any nonempty subset of those labels with polynomial \((1+z)^d-1\), or select none. Summing over the strongly connected support graphs therefore gives the exact weighted number of subsets whose closed-walk gain subgroup is contained in \(H_d\):
\[
F_d(z)=(m/d)^{n-1}S_n((1+z)^d-1).
\tag{4}
\]
There is no overcount: an actual strongly connected labeled subset determines its potentials uniquely.

**Step 3: subgroup Möbius inversion.** Write \(E_e(z)\) for the generating polynomial of strongly connected labeled subsets with \(H(A)=H_e\), where \(e\mid m\). By definition \(F_d(z)=\sum_{e\mid d}E_e(z)\). The subgroup lattice of \(C_m\) is the divisor lattice, so arithmetic Möbius inversion yields
\[
E_m(z)=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)F_d(z).
\]
By Step 1, \(E_m(z)=G_{n,m}(z)\), proving (1).

**Step 4: explicit connectivity polynomial.** A directed graph fails to be strongly connected if and only if some nonempty proper vertex set has no outgoing arc to its complement (choose a sink component of the condensation digraph). Avoiding this failure for every \(U\in\mathcal C\) and applying inclusion–exclusion leaves \(n^2-|E_{\mathcal F}|\) available ordered pairs, each contributing \(1+t\). This proves (2), including the loops correctly.

**Step 5: extremal coefficients.** A strongly connected digraph on \(n\) vertices has at least \(n\) distinct arcs (each vertex must have positive outdegree). With exactly \(n\) arcs it consists of a single directed Hamiltonian cycle, with \((n-1)!\) choices. Consequently \([t^n]S_n(t)=(n-1)!\). For \(d\mid m\), \([z^n]S_n((1+z)^d-1)=(n-1)!d^n\). Thus
\[
[z^n]G_{n,m}(z)=(n-1)!\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)(m/d)^{n-1}d^n
=(n-1)!m^{n-1}\varphi(m),
\]
using \(\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)d=\varphi(m)\). This coefficient is positive. The only subset of all \(n^2m\) nonzero elements is the complete set, which certainly generates the semigroup, proving the leading coefficient assertion. Finally, adding zero to any nonzero generating subset changes its size by one but not its generation status, proving the optional-zero factor.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `CHECKER.py` uses only Python's standard library. It computes \(S_n\) by enumerating all support graphs; evaluates (1) using an entirely integer binomial expansion and divisor Möbius function; and **independently** enumerates subsets and closes them under the actual Brandt multiplication to test generation. All coefficient vectors agree exhaustively for \((n,m)=(2,1),(2,2),(2,3),(2,4)\). The total nonzero-generating-subset counts are respectively \(4,136,3124,57312\). For \((n,m)=(3,2)\), it independently verifies **all coefficients** by exhaustive positive-word reachability on the gain-expanded digraph (189,504 generating subsets), the strong-graph histogram \((2,15,39,48,30,9,1)\) at arc counts \(3,\ldots,9\), and 512 seeded random multiplication closures. These computations test finite cases only; the general theorem is established in the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

The minimum-cardinality result \(\operatorname{rank} B_n(G)=n-1+\operatorname{rank}G\) is classical; Gray and Ruškuc explicitly reproduce it as Corollary 9.4, crediting Gomes and Howie. Gray's 2013 article gives it as Corollary 9 and treats general Rees-semigroup rank. For \(G=C_m\) this gives the lower endpoint \(n\), but neither inspected work tabulates the entire size distribution of **all** generating subsets. The present theorem refines that endpoint through an explicit gain-subgroup census and divisor Möbius transform. The trivial-group case \(m=1\) reduces to the classical strong-directed-graph inventory and is not claimed as a new graph enumeration theorem. The potential/coboundary criterion is related to standard voltage-graph language; what is asserted here is the complete cyclic-Brandt generating-subset polynomial, not the separate novelty of graph potentials or arithmetic Möbius inversion.

## Limitations

The formula is for cyclic maximal subgroups, whose subgroup lattice is the divisor lattice; a general nonabelian group requires a different group-theoretic count. The result enumerates labeled generating subsets by size, not their automorphism orbits. Formula (2) is explicit but generally exponential time to evaluate as written. The historical-priority comparison covers the inspected rank literature and related records; it does not prove the absence of an implicit equivalent in older graph-reliability, gain-graph, or Rees-semigroup literature. No independent external audit has been performed.
