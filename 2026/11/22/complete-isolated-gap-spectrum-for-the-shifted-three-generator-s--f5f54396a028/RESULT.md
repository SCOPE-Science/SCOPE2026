# Complete isolated-gap spectrum for the shifted three-generator semigroups \(\langle a,a+3,a+4\rangle\)

## Finding
For each integer \(a\ge6\), let \(S_a=\langle a,a+3,a+4\rangle\), the additive numerical semigroup generated by these three integers. An isolated gap is an integer \(h\ge1\) with \(h\notin S_a\) and \(h-1,h+1\in S_a\). Write \(I(S_a)\) for the set of all such gaps and \(m=\lfloor a/4\rfloor\). Then
\[
\boxed{I(S_a)=\{ta+5:2\le t\le m+2\}\ \cup\
\begin{cases}
\{(m+1)a-1\}, & a\equiv2\pmod4,\\
\{(m+2)a+2\}, & a\equiv3\pmod4,\\
\varnothing, & a\equiv0,1\pmod4.
\end{cases}}
\]
The displayed union is disjoint. In particular,
\[
\boxed{|I(S_a)|=\left\lfloor\frac{a+2}{4}\right\rfloor+1.}
\]
This determines every isolated gap for every integer \(a\ge6\), not just the largest or the number of gaps. For example, \(I(S_{10})=\{25,29,35,45\}\) and \(I(S_{11})=\{27,38,46,49\}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The parameter \(a\) is an integer at least six, so the three displayed generators are positive and minimal, as all lie below \(2a\). Since the two larger generators are consecutive, the three generators have greatest common divisor one and the semigroup has finite complement. Here \(0\in S_a\), and gaps are positive. The theorem is not claimed for arbitrary three-generated semigroups, other offset pairs, or the excluded smaller parameters. No claim is made about the entire gapset, genus, or pseudo-Frobenius set.

## Proof
**1. Exact fixed-coin bands.** Represent a semigroup element as a sum of exactly \(t\ge0\) generators. If \(y\) of those generators equal \(a+3\) and \(z\) equal \(a+4\), its value is \(ta+3y+4z\), where \(y,z\ge0\) and \(y+z\le t\). Consequently its possible values form the band \(B_t=ta+C_t\), where \(C_0=\{0\}\), \(C_1=\{0,3,4\}\), and, for every \(t\ge2\),
\[
C_t=\{0,3,4\}\cup[6,4t]\cap\mathbb Z.
\]
Indeed, using exactly \(j\) nonzero offsets (each three or four), for \(2\le j\le t\) we attain every integer in \([3j,4j]\); the intervals for consecutive \(j\ge2\) overlap or are adjacent because \(3(j+1)\le4j+1\). This proves the displayed description, not merely an inclusion. Thus for \(t\ge2\), the only internal holes of \(B_t\) between its smallest and largest elements are the offsets \(1,2,5\); its terminal interval runs from \(ta+6\) to \(E_t=t(a+4)\). For \(t=1\), the last band member is \(E_1=a+4\), and the maxima \(E_t\) increase strictly with \(t\).

**2. Exhaustive possible isolated positions.** Since the previous bands have maximal element \(E_{t-1}\), no earlier band can reach beyond \(E_{t-1}\), while no later band begins below \( (t+1)a\). For \(t\ge2\), every isolated gap inside the small-hole region or immediately preceding the \(t\)-coin band must therefore be one of the following: \(ta+5\) (between \(ta+4\) and \(ta+6\)); \(ta+2\) (between a possible preceding-band endpoint and \(ta+3\)); or \(ta-1\) (between the preceding band and \(ta\)). The offset \(ta+1\) cannot be isolated: when an earlier band contains \(ta+2\), its terminal interval also contains \(ta+1\), and otherwise its right neighbor is absent. All remaining gaps lie in runs of length at least two: before the initial generator, beyond a terminal interval before the next band, or within the listed uncovered offsets. The exceptional \(t=1\) band creates no other isolated position for \(a\ge6\); a possible gap at \(2a-1\) is already included among the \(ta-1\) cases with \(t=2\).

**3. The permanent offset-five family.** For \(t\ge2\), the two neighbors of \(ta+5\) are in \(B_t\). This candidate itself is a gap precisely when \(E_{t-1}<ta+5\): whenever \(E_{t-1}\) reaches the candidate, the previous terminal interval contains it (the case \(t=2\) cannot reach it for \(a\ge6\)). The inequality is \(4(t-1)<a+5\), equivalent for integral \(t\) to \(2\le t\le\lfloor a/4\rfloor+2\). This gives exactly the first family.

**4. The two exceptional boundary gaps.** The integer \(ta-1\), for \(t\ge2\), is an isolated gap exactly when the preceding terminal interval stops at \(ta-2\), or
\[
E_{t-1}=ta-2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad4t=a+2.
\]
Thus this occurs if and only if \(a\equiv2\pmod4\), at \(t=(a+2)/4=m+1\). Similarly, \(ta+2\) is isolated exactly when the preceding interval ends at \(ta+1\) (the left neighbor is present, but the candidate remains missing):
\[
E_{t-1}=ta+1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad4t=a+5.
\]
This occurs precisely if \(a\equiv3\pmod4\), at \(t=(a+5)/4=m+2\). For \(a\ge6\) each possible \(t\) is at least two and the band endpoint criteria apply without hidden earlier or later representations. These three disjoint alternatives exhaust every isolated gap.

**5. Cardinality.** The permanent family has \(m+1\) elements. Exactly one extra element occurs when \(a\equiv2,3\pmod4\) and none otherwise. Therefore its total size is \(m+1+\mathbf1_{a\bmod4\in\{2,3\}}=\lfloor(a+2)/4\rfloor+1\), proving the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Moreno-Frías and Rosales (arXiv:2305.14967, first released 2023-05-24) introduce the perfect-semigroup covariety and characterize isolated gaps via their absence and perfect closures. The inspected sections on the covariety, its algorithms, and perfect closure do not classify the isolated gaps of this explicit three-generator offset family. Singh and Ram (arXiv:2406.09848, released 2024-06-14) classify isolated gaps of two-generator semigroups, so their all-parameter statements do not imply an answer for a minimal three-generator family. Smith (2022) supplies general isolated-gap/Apéry techniques and covers the full embedding-dimension-two case; its complete PDF was inaccessible in this review, leaving a bibliographic risk. Two relevant published classifications cover respectively the distinct arithmetic-progression family \(\langle a,a+2,a+4\rangle\) (for odd \(a\)), and the threefold-quotient family \(\langle2k+1,3k+1,3k+2\rangle\). The latter contains the particular instance \(a=7\), so that single case is already covered; it does not cover the infinite general family. The present claim is only the exact all-parameter isolated-gap set and its periodic boundary structure. General Apéry criteria, isolated-gap definitions, and general perfect-closure procedures are credited to prior work.

## Limitations
The result is a deliberately specified natural family, not a classification of all embedding-dimension-three semigroups. It uses elementary band arithmetic; possible equivalent formulas implied implicitly by an uninspected journal theorem or an unindexed enumeration remain the main priority risk. Smith's journal PDF was access-restricted at the attempted publisher endpoint; its abstract and the full arXiv treatments citing it were examined, but its entire content was not verified. There has been no independent scientific audit.
