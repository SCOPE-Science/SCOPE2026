# Complete left-action cycle types and sharp exponent jumps in triangular radical braces

## Finding
Let \(q=p^e\) be any prime power, \(n\ge2\), and let \(A=\mathfrak n_n(\mathbb F_q)\) be the nilpotent associative algebra of strictly upper-triangular matrices. Its radical-ring brace has circle law \(a\circ b=a+b+ab\) and canonical left actions \(\lambda_a(b)=b+ab\). For each \(1\le r<n\), write \(a_r\) for the northwest \(r\times r\) corner of \(a\), and put \(D=\binom n2\).

**Complete individual cycle law.** For every \(a\in A\) and \(s\ge0\), define
\[
F_s(a)=q^{\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}(r-\operatorname{rank}(a_r^{p^s}))}.
\]
The permutation \(\lambda_a\) of the \(q^D\) additive elements of \(A\) has precisely \(F_0(a)\) fixed points, and for every \(s\ge1\) it has precisely \((F_s(a)-F_{s-1}(a))/p^s\) cycles of length \(p^s\). There are no cycles of any other length. This is an explicit finite algorithm for the entire cycle type of every canonical brace left action, over every finite field.

**Faithful quotient and sharp extremum.** The permutation group generated by all \(\lambda_a\) is canonically isomorphic to \(\mathrm{UT}_{n-1}(\mathbb F_q)\). Every \(\lambda_a\) has at least \(q^{n-1}\) fixed elements. Equality holds exactly when all \(n-2\) entries \(a_{i,i+1}\), \(1\le i\le n-2\), are nonzero. The proportion of brace elements realizing the minimum is \((1-q^{-1})^{n-2}\). More generally, for each \(s\) the minimum over all \(a\) of \(F_s(a)\) is exactly \(q^{\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\min(r,p^s)}\), with a common minimizer given by the same nonzero-adjacent-entry locus. On this locus \(\lambda_a\), as a linear operator over \(\mathbb F_q\), has Jordan blocks of sizes \(1,2,\ldots,n-1\), exactly once each, and hence
\[
F_s(a)=q^{\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\min(r,p^s)}.
\]

**Arithmetic order jump.** For every \(s\ge0\), set \(n=p^s+2\). The largest possible order of a canonical left action is \(p^{s+1}\), and exactly \((q-1)^{p^s}q^{D-p^s}\) brace elements attain this largest order. Equivalently, the exact maximal-order proportion is \((1-q^{-1})^{p^s}\). The nonzero-superdiagonal condition above characterizes all of them. These statements include \(s=0\) and all characteristics, without genericity assumptions.

## Assumptions and scope
All matrices are over \(\mathbb F_q\) and multiplication is ordinary matrix multiplication. The additive group of \(A\) has \(q^D\) elements. The brace law is the standard one associated to any nilpotent associative ring. The actions \(\lambda_a\) are the canonical left permutations appearing in the set-theoretic Yang--Baxter solution attached to this brace; the theorem concerns these permutations individually, not all braid-solution orbit equivalence classes. A power of a zero matrix is defined in the usual sense; for \(a_r=0\), its positive powers are zero. The prime \(p\), not the field order \(q\), determines all possible cycle lengths.

## Proof
For each \(1\le r<n\), let \(V_r\subseteq A\) be the \(r\)-dimensional coordinate subspace consisting of matrices supported in column \(r+1\). These columns give a direct-sum decomposition
\[
A=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{n-1}V_r.
\]
Left multiplication \(L_a:b\mapsto ab\) preserves each \(V_r\): in the basis \(E_{1,r+1},\ldots,E_{r,r+1}\), it acts by the principal corner \(a_r\). Thus \(L_a\cong\bigoplus_{r=1}^{n-1}a_r\), and \(\lambda_a=I+L_a\) acts as \(\bigoplus_r(I_r+a_r)\). Because the characteristic is \(p\), the binomial identity and associativity imply
\[
\lambda_a^{p^s}=I+L_a^{p^s}=I+L_{a^{p^s}}.
\]
Its fixed space therefore has dimension \(\sum_r(r-\operatorname{rank}(a_r^{p^s}))\), proving the displayed fixed-point formula.

Each block \(I_r+a_r\) is unipotent. Thus \(\lambda_a\) has order a power of \(p\), and all permutation cycles have powers of \(p\) as their lengths. The number of points fixed by the \(p^s\)-th power is the sum of the numbers of points on cycles of lengths at most \(p^s\). Subtracting consecutive fixed-point counts and dividing by the cycle length proves the individual cycle formula, including integrality.

To identify the action group, the homomorphism \(a\mapsto\lambda_a\) from the circle group \((A,\circ)\) has kernel exactly those \(a\) with \(a_r=0\) for all \(r<n\), equivalently those supported in the final column. This is a subgroup of cardinality \(q^{n-1}\). The circle projection \(a\mapsto a_{n-1}\) is surjective onto the strictly upper-triangular \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) radical; adjoining the identity gives the unitriangular group. The direct-sum action is faithful on its principal \((n-1)\)-block, proving the claimed isomorphism.

Every strictly upper-triangular \(r\times r\) matrix has rank of its \(t\)-th power at most \(\max(r-t,0)\), so the kernel of that power has dimension at least \(\min(r,t)\). Thus the displayed \(F_s\)-formula attains its smallest possible value simultaneously on regular corners. In particular, every strictly upper-triangular \(r\times r\) matrix has nontrivial kernel, so \(\dim\ker a_r\ge1\) and \(F_0(a)\ge q^{n-1}\). Equality holds precisely when all principal corners have one-dimensional kernel. The largest corner \(a_{n-1}\) has one-dimensional kernel if and only if it is regular nilpotent, which for a strictly upper-triangular matrix is equivalent to every adjacent entry \(a_{i,i+1}\ne0\) for \(1\le i\le n-2\): indeed the \((1,n-1)\)-entry of \(a_{n-1}^{n-2}\) is the product of those entries. In this situation every smaller corner is also regular, so the condition is sufficient. The coordinates of \(a\) are otherwise unconstrained, giving \((q-1)^{n-2}q^{D-(n-2)}\) matrices. Each regular corner \(a_r\) has a single nilpotent Jordan block of size \(r\), hence \(\dim\ker(a_r^{p^s})=\min(r,p^s)\). This proves the sharp fixed-point, Jordan-type and regular-locus laws.

Finally let \(n=p^s+2\). Then \(a_{n-1}\) has size \(p^s+1\), so \((a_{n-1})^{p^s+1}=0\) for every \(a\), while its \(p^s\)-th power is zero except possibly at the upper-right entry, which equals \(\prod_{i=1}^{p^s}a_{i,i+1}\). Because \(\lambda_a^{p^s}\) acts by \(I+(a_{n-1})^{p^s}\) on the last nontrivial column module, it is nonidentity exactly when that product is nonzero; its next \(p\)-power is always identity. Thus the largest order is \(p^{s+1}\), attained exactly under the stated nonvanishing conditions. Counting those independent constraints yields \((q-1)^{p^s}q^{D-p^s}\).

## Verification
A standalone checker `check.py` enumerates every brace element, constructs the permutations \(b\mapsto b+ab\), traces every actual permutation cycle, and independently compares against the corner-rank fixed-point formula over prime fields. Exhaustive tests passed for \((n,p)=(2,2),(3,2),(4,2),(5,2),(2,3),(3,3),(4,3)\). In addition, exhaustive small and seeded sampled checks of the sharp order jump were performed at \(n=p^s+2\) for \((p,s)=(2,0),(2,1),(2,2),(3,0),(3,1)\). The captured output is in `check_output.txt`. Reproduce with `python check.py`. Only the proof, not these finite checks, establishes the all-prime-power theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
The radical-ring brace construction and its Yang--Baxter role are classical, described by Rump (2007), Smoktunowicz (2015), and Guarnieri--Vendramin (2015). Smoktunowicz studies nilpotence and adjoint groups of braces; Guarnieri--Vendramin study the general brace construction and small examples. Those foundational properties explain why the canonical \(\lambda\)-permutations exist. The present result singles out all individual cycle types in the strictly triangular family, the faithful unitriangular quotient, the optimal fixed-point stratum, and the prime-power dimension thresholds. Fuchs--Kirillov (2022) study the distribution of Jordan types of individual strictly upper-triangular matrices. Those ambient-matrix counts do not give the family of simultaneous principal-corner Jordan types, the canonical brace left-action cycles, or the exact threshold population here. The finite-dimensional Jordan-block and binomial identities used in the proof are standard and are not claimed new independently. No equivalent triangular-brace permutation formula was identified in the inspected primary sources and published-result comparison; this is a bounded novelty assessment, not a claim of exhaustive priority.

## Limitations
The claim concerns canonical \(\lambda\)-actions, not full set-theoretic Yang--Baxter orbit statistics or a classification of all finite braces. The exact individual cycle law is given algorithmically by principal-corner ranks rather than by a closed distribution of cycle types as the brace element varies; the extremal and jump strata have closed counts. Computational checks use prime fields and finite dimensions only; the proof handles arbitrary finite fields. Historical priority is subject to unindexed or inaccessible algebra/brace literature. No independent expert audit has been performed.
