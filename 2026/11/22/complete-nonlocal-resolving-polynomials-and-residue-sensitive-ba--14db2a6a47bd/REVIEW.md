# Review of the nonlocal wheel enumerator

## Correctness — PASS

The exact nonlocal signature criterion reduces feasibility to cyclic zero-gaps of lengths zero through four, at most one gap of length three or four, and no adjacent gaps of length at least two. The weighted two-state transfer matrix gives the complete all-size polynomial. An independently stated binomial selection formula gives the five minimum-basis residue classes after bounded algebraic coefficient extraction. Ordinary resolution additionally prohibits a four-gap, and the positive-root analysis yields the limiting total-count ratio. Direct graph-distance exhaustive checks were executed for all 130,944 rim subsets at lengths 7 through 16, with and without a hub, and exact rational-series coefficients and basis formulas were checked through 220. This is corroboration of the symbolic argument, not finite-case extrapolation to all orders.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Klavžar and Kuziak (2022, arXiv:2211.10614, Section 4) prove the scalar nonlocal dimension and essentially the gap-feasibility constraints. These elements are explicitly prior art. Sooryanarayana, Suma and Chandrakala (2021, Theorem 7.11) describe a related ordinary resolving gap test. *Exact resolving-set and metric-basis enumerators of wheel graphs* (2026) already derives the ordinary all-cardinality polynomial and exact ordinary basis counts. The assessed new claim instead supplies the nonlocal all-cardinality enumerator, its five exact nonlocal minimum-basis count polynomials, and the exact positive nonlocal-only correction with a limiting ratio. The published ordinary polynomial does not count nonlocal-only configurations. Searches found no matching or dominating statement, but absence from accessible material does not establish exhaustive priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The known minimum nonlocal dimension does not determine how many optimal landmark placements exist or how many larger sets satisfy the weaker resolution condition. The new formula answers both natural enumeration questions for the entire wheel family; notably its minimum-base multiplicities vary from quintic to linear growth according to circumference modulo five. The exact correction and positive limiting fraction separate the two physically meaningful location-information requirements quantitatively, rather than offering a routine recomputation of the known minimum.

## Scope and risks

Graphs are finite, simple, labelled wheels with at least seven rim vertices. The cyclic feasibility lemma is not new, and the residue formula uses its combinatorial consequences. There is no orbit census, no all-graph extension, and no independent mathematical validation. The source comparison is bounded by the three closest accessible primary texts and targeted indexed searches; a presently unindexed equivalent enumerator remains possible.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
