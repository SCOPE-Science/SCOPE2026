# Complete periodic-orbit spectra and a sharp two-cycle gap for finite crown dynamics

## Finding
For each integer \(N\ge2\), let \(\mathfrak C_N\) be the \(2N\)-point crown poset, with vertices \(j\in\mathbb Z/(2N)\) and relations \(2i<2i-1\) and \(2i<2i+1\). For any order-preserving self-map \(f:\mathfrak C_N\to\mathfrak C_N\), write \(a(f)\) for its number of fixed points, \(b(f)\) for its number of primitive period-two orbits, and define its Artin–Mazur zeta function by
\[
\zeta_f(t)=\exp\left(\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|\operatorname{Fix}(f^k)|}{k}t^k\right).
\]
Then **the complete set** of zeta functions, with every listed function attained, is the union of
\[
\boxed{\left\{(1-t)^{-a}:1\le a\le N+1\right\}},
\qquad
\boxed{\left\{(1-t)^{-1}(1-t^2)^{-b}:1\le b\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor\right\}},
\]
\[
\boxed{\left\{(1-t^p)^{-2N/p}:p\mid N\right\}},
\qquad
\boxed{\left\{(1-t)^{-2}(1-t^2)^{-(N-1)}\right\}}.
\]
The first two families come from nonbijective degree-zero maps, the third from rotations, and the fourth from orientation-reversing crown automorphisms. In particular, a nonbijective map has **at most** \(\lfloor N/2\rfloor\) primitive two-cycles, attained sharply, whereas a reversing automorphism has \(N-1\) two-cycles (a strict numerical gap for \(N\ge3\), with equality at \(N=2\)). Every period of length at least three occurs only in a globally rotating automorphism, and its least period necessarily divides \(N\).

## Assumptions and scope
The order on \(\mathfrak C_N\) is exactly the height-two order generated by adjacent even minima and odd maxima. The associated finite Alexandrov \(T_0\) topology has continuous maps precisely the order-preserving maps. Distinct vertices are labeled; no quotient by automorphisms or homotopy is taken. A primitive two-cycle is an **unordered** orbit of exactly two vertices, and the fixed points of iterates count **vertices**, not periodic orbits. For the finite set, the zeta function is a formal power series (and the formulas are rational functions) with variable \(t\). No measure-theoretic or differentiable hypotheses are assumed.

## Proof
**1. Winding dichotomy and a short image arc.** A continuous map sends every comparable pair to either one vertex or another comparable pair. Hence it is nonexpanding in the undirected cycle graph metric. Choose an integer lift \(F\) of its vertex values along one turn of the source crown: \(F(j+1)-F(j)\in\{-1,0,1\}\), and \(F(j+2N)=F(j)+2Nd\) for an integer winding degree \(d\). The absolute sum of the \(2N\) increments is at most \(2N\), so \(d\in\{-1,0,1\}\). If \(d=1\), every increment is \(+1\). If \(d=-1\), every increment is \(-1\). In either case every source edge has nonzero image, and preserving the even-minimum/odd-maximum order forces the starting lift offset to be even. Thus these maps are exactly \(f(j)=j+2r\) and \(f(j)=2r-j\) modulo \(2N\), respectively.

If \(d=0\), the lifted walk \(F(0),\ldots,F(2N)=F(0)\) has total variation at most \(2N\). Write \(A_0=\min F\), \(B_0=\max F\). Traveling from minimum to maximum and back uses at least \(2(B_0-A_0)\) steps, hence \(B_0-A_0\le N\). The image \(f(\mathfrak C_N)\) is therefore contained in the proper contiguous cycle arc \(I=[A_0,B_0]\), whose graph-path length is at most \(N\). Moreover, \(f(I)\subseteq I\) since \(I=f(\mathfrak C_N)\) after using that the walk takes every intermediate integer, and the vertex image is actually all of \(I\). For points inside this short arc, the cycle shortest-path metric agrees with integer distance, including when the arc has length exactly \(N\). Therefore the restriction \(g=f|_I:I\to I\), represented in this integer coordinate, satisfies \(|g(x)-g(y)|\le|x-y|\).

**2. Periodic points of a finite nonexpansive interval map.** Let \(P\subseteq I\) be the nonempty eventual periodic set. It is nonempty because iteration on a finite set eventually repeats. For two points in \(P\), choose a common iterate that is the identity on every periodic orbit. The distances between their successive images cannot increase but return to their initial value, so \(g|_P\) is an isometry. Every isometry of a finite subset of the real line is either the identity or reflection about the midpoint of its extreme elements.

If \(g|_P\) is the identity, \(P\) consists only of fixed points. If \(u<v\) are fixed, the two nonexpansion bounds \(|g(j)-u|\le j-u\) and \(|g(j)-v|\le v-j\) force \(g(j)=j\) for every integer \(u\le j\le v\). Hence the fixed-point set is a contiguous interval of \(a\) vertices, \(1\le a\le N+1\), and there are no other periodic points.

If \(g|_P\) is a nontrivial reflection, let \(u=\min P<v=\max P\). The endpoints swap, so for every integer \(u\le j\le v\), the two Lipschitz bounds from \(u\) and \(v\) force \(g(j)=u+v-j\). Consequently all vertices of \([u,v]\) are periodic, and \(P=[u,v]\). This reflection strictly changes adjacent vertices, hence order preservation forces \(u+v\) to be even: the middle vertex is integral and fixed. Put \(v-u=2b\). Then \(1\le b\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor\) and \(P\) comprises exactly one fixed vertex and \(b\) primitive two-cycles. This exhausts degree zero.

**3. Automorphisms.** A rotation \(f(j)=j+2r\) has every vertex of the common least period \(p=N/\gcd(N,r)\) and therefore \(2N/p\) primitive \(p\)-cycles. Every divisor \(p\mid N\) is realized by choosing \(r=N/p\). A reversing automorphism \(f(j)=2r-j\) has \(f^2=\operatorname{id}\), and \(f(j)=j\) exactly when \(2j\equiv2r\pmod {2N}\), giving exactly two fixed vertices; the other \(2N-2\) vertices form \(N-1\) two-cycles.

**4. Explicit realization of every nonbijective case.** On integer representatives \(0,1,\ldots,2N\), choose \(0\le \ell\le N\) and set
\[
F_{\ell}(j)=
\begin{cases}
j&0\le j\le\ell,\\
2\ell-j&\ell\le j\le2\ell,\\
0&2\ell\le j\le2N.
\end{cases}
\]
The endpoints agree, every nonzero unit step preserves even-to-minimum and odd-to-maximum parity, and this defines an order-preserving crown map of degree zero. Its image is \([0,\ell]\), and it fixes precisely those \(\ell+1\) vertices, realizing every \(a=1,\ldots,N+1\).

For each \(1\le b\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), instead put
\[
G_b(j)=
\begin{cases}
2b-j&0\le j\le2b,\\
j-2b&2b\le j\le4b,\\
2b&4b\le j\le2N.
\end{cases}
\]
Again the endpoints agree and each nonconstant step preserves crown order. The image \([0,2b]\) is invariant and reverses under the map. It contains exactly one fixed point and \(b\) primitive two-cycles, and no other periodic points exist. Thus all advertised zeta families are realized.

**5. Zeta conversion.** If the primitive periodic orbits have lengths \(p_1,\ldots,p_s\), the elementary formal-series identity \(\exp(\sum_{k\ge1}|\operatorname{Fix}(f^k)|t^k/k)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1-t^{p_i})^{-1}\) follows by collecting the iterates divisible by each \(p_i\). Substitution from steps 2–4 yields exactly the four displayed families.

## Verification
A dependency-free script `artifacts/verify.py` independently enumerates every order-preserving endomap for \(N=2,3,4\) by complete length-\(2N\) unit-step lift words and checks order preservation directly on all covering relations. It computes exact primitive orbit decompositions, verifies every case of the stated spectrum and all possible degree-zero types, and independently checks both explicit realization families. The exhaustive finite cases have respectively \(36\), \(234\), and \(1544\) maps. Run `python3 artifacts/verify.py`; it prints `CROWN_DYNAMICS_VERIFY_OK`. These finite computations are diagnostics, not a proof for all \(N\); the preceding argument covers all integers \(N\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Chocano's paper on the infinite alternating fence proves the absence of period three for ordinary continuous single-valued dynamics on the **infinite line**, along with interval restrictions on its two-cycles. The finite crown is instead a cyclic quotient admitting nontrivial rotating maps with longer periods. The new classification separates that winding phenomenon from all degree-zero periodic behavior and determines the full zeta spectrum and sharp two-cycle gap.

Earlier published mathematical work on finite crowns classifies their unbased homotopy classes by degree and divisibility; it distinguishes degree-zero maps from extremal winding maps but does not classify periodic points of a single map. Work on endomorphisms of **ordinary graph cycles** concerns maps preserving edges without collapse, unlike the order-preserving crown maps here. Enumeration of onto bounded morphisms between crown Kripke frames imposes a back-lifting property not assumed here and counts maps, not temporal periodic spectra. Classical enumerations of monotone maps between fences and crowns address total-map counts rather than the stated dynamical classification; their inaccessible full texts leave a residual historical-priority risk.

## Limitations
The theorem is for finite height-two crown posets with an even number of vertices; it does not cover arbitrary finite spaces, infinite crown limits, multivalued dynamics, or complete transient-time distributions. The novelty comparison is literature-limited: a close equivalent in unindexed work or inaccessible classical enumeration papers cannot be excluded. The computational verifier covers only \(N\le4\) and is not an infinite proof. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
