# Complete power-map fibers and the exceptional square-only cone in finite rectangular-band algebras

## Finding

For any positive integers \(m,n\), any finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\), and any integer \(h\ge2\), let \(A=k[B_{m,n}]\) be the \(mn\)-dimensional (usually nonunital) semigroup algebra of the rectangular band \(B_{m,n}=[m]\times[n]\), with \((i,j)(a,b)=(i,b)\). Regard its elements as \(m\)-by-\(n\) coefficient matrices. For such a matrix \(Z\), let \(\rho(Z)=Z\mathbf1_n\), \(\gamma(Z)^\mathsf T=\mathbf1_m^\mathsf TZ\), and \(t(Z)=\mathbf1_m^\mathsf TZ\mathbf1_n\); these are respectively row sums, column sums and total sum.

Set \(d=(m-1)(n-1)\), \(g_h=\gcd(h,q-1)\), and \(\mathcal R_h=\{s^h:s\in k^\times\}\). Define \(N_h(Z)=\#\{X\in A:X^h=Z\}\), where powers use the *semigroup-algebra multiplication*, not ordinary matrix multiplication. **Every prescribed-target fiber** is

\[
\boxed{
N_h(Z)=
\begin{cases}
q^d(q^{m-1}+q^{n-1}-1),& Z=0,\ h=2,\\
q^{mn-1},&Z=0,\ h\ge3,\\
(q-1)q^d,&h=2,\ Z\ne0,\ \operatorname{rank}(Z)=1,\ \rho(Z)=0,\ \gamma(Z)=0,\\
g_hq^d,&Z\ne0,\ \operatorname{rank}(Z)=1,\ t(Z)\in\mathcal R_h,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]

The two nonzero-target cases are disjoint because \(t(Z)=0\) in the exceptional \(h=2\) case. In particular the exact image cardinalities are

\[
\boxed{
|A^2|=1+\frac{(q^{m-1}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{q-1}+\frac{q-1}{g_2}q^{m+n-2},
\qquad
|A^h|=1+\frac{q-1}{g_h}q^{m+n-2}\quad(h\ge3).}
\]

Here \(A^h=\{X^h:X\in A\}\) denotes the set of individual \(h\)-th powers, not the linear span or the ideal generated by products. For \(m,n\ge2\), precisely
\[
\boxed{\frac{(q^{m-1}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{q-1}}
\]
nonzero rank-one matrices with both row and column sums zero are **squares but never powers of exponent at least three**. This exceptional cone disappears if and only if \(m=1\) or \(n=1\).

## Assumptions and scope

All elements are labeled coefficient matrices in the full nonunital algebra \(A\), and \(q\) is any prime power including even and nonprime fields. Matrix rank means ordinary rank of the coefficient array. The symbol \(X^h\) means the associative repeated product in \(A\). The formula covers every \(m,n\ge1\), every integer \(h\ge2\), and **every** \(Z\in A\). It does not assert an analogous formula for arbitrary higher-rank sandwich matrices or arbitrary semigroup algebras.

## Proof

**1. Multiplication and its sufficient statistics.** Bilinearity and the basis law show that
\[
(XY)_{ib}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{m}x_{ij}y_{ab}
=\rho_i(X)\gamma_b(Y),\qquad XY=\rho(X)\gamma(Y)^\mathsf T.
\]
Let \(s=t(X)\), \(r=\rho(X)\), and \(c=\gamma(X)\), so \(\mathbf1_m^\mathsf Tr=\mathbf1_n^\mathsf Tc=s\). The elementary row-column sum map \(X\mapsto(r,c)\) is surjective onto the \((m+n-1)\)-dimensional space of pairs with equal total sum: prescribe all entries in the first column and first row, adjusting the overlap, and set the remaining entries to zero. Its kernel therefore has dimension \(d=mn-(m+n-1)\), so **each compatible \((r,c)\) admits exactly \(q^d\) preimages**.

The square and higher powers are
\[
X^2=rc^\mathsf T,\qquad
X^h=s^{h-2}rc^\mathsf T\quad(h\ge2),
\]
where for \(h=2\) the factor is \(1\), including \(s=0\). Indeed, \(\rho(rc^\mathsf T)=sr\), \(\gamma(rc^\mathsf T)=sc\) and induction through the product law gives the formula. Its total coefficient sum is \(t(X^h)=s^h\).

**2. Targets with nonzero total sum.** Suppose \(Z=X^h\ne0\) and \(t(Z)\ne0\). Then \(Z\) has rank one and \(t(Z)=s^h\in\mathcal R_h\). Conversely, let \(Z\) have rank one and \(t(Z)=s^h\ne0\). The row and column sums of a root are forced to be
\[
r=\frac{\rho(Z)}{s^{h-1}},\qquad
c=\frac{\gamma(Z)}{s^{h-1}}.
\]
Their totals are both \(s\). For any rank-one \(Z\) we have
\(\rho(Z)\gamma(Z)^\mathsf T=t(Z)Z\); substituting shows \(s^{h-2}rc^\mathsf T=Z\). Thus each of the \(g_h\) solutions of \(s^h=t(Z)\) in the cyclic group \(k^\times\) gives exactly \(q^d\) roots, and distinct \(s\) give disjoint fibers.

**3. Nonzero targets with zero total.** For \(h\ge3\), \(X^h\ne0\) forces \(s\ne0\): if \(s=0\), the factor \(s^{h-2}\) vanishes; if \(s\ne0\), then \(r,c\ne0\), hence their outer product is nonzero. Therefore a nonzero \(Z\) with \(t(Z)=0\) has no root for \(h\ge3\). For \(h=2\), a nonzero target requires \(Z=rc^\mathsf T\) with \(r,c\ne0\) and both sums \(s\). Since \(t(Z)=s^2=0\), necessarily \(s=0\); hence \(\rho(Z)=\gamma(Z)=0\). Conversely, any nonzero rank-one \(Z\) with these two vanishing margins has \(q-1\) ordered outer-product factorizations \(rc^\mathsf T\), all with sum-zero factors, and each factorization admits \(q^d\) lifts.

**4. Zero fibers.** Since \(rc^\mathsf T=0\) exactly when \(r=0\) or \(c=0\), its compatible sum-pair count is \(q^{n-1}+q^{m-1}-1\); multiply by \(q^d\) for squares. For \(h\ge3\), \(X^h=0\) exactly when \(s=0\). This is one nontrivial linear equation on \(mn\) matrix coordinates, hence has \(q^{mn-1}\) solutions.

**5. Count image points and the sharp boundary.** The number of nonzero rank-one matrices with \(\rho(Z)=\gamma(Z)=0\) is
\[
\frac{(q^{m-1}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{q-1},
\]
because their column space lies in \(\ker\mathbf1_m^\mathsf T\), their row space lies in \(\ker\mathbf1_n^\mathsf T\), and each nonzero outer product has \(q-1\) presentations. For any fixed \(t\in k^\times\), the number of rank-one \(Z\) with total \(t\) is exactly \(q^{m+n-2}\): uniquely write \(Z=uv^\mathsf T\) with \(\sum u_i=1\), \(\sum v_j=t\). There are \((q-1)/g_h\) nonzero \(h\)-th power values of \(t\). These disjoint strata, together with zero, give both image formulas. The exceptional cone is nonempty exactly when both sum-zero vector spaces have positive dimension. In particular, \(X^2\ne0\) but \(X^3=0\) holds for exactly \(q^d(q^{m-1}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)\) algebra elements.

## Verification

The standalone \(power_checker.py\) independently performs coefficient-wise semigroup-algebra multiplication by the original basis rule; its evaluator for \(X^h\) does *not* use the power formula. It enumerates every input and every target for selected \(m,n,q,h\), comparing observed fibers to the asserted formula. It checked \(q\in\{2,3,4,5,9\}\), including prime extensions defined by irreducible quadratic moduli, one-dimensional edge cases, and exponents \(2,3,4,5\). All 29 configurations matched; see \(power_checker_output.txt\). These finite checks are corroboration only; the preceding argument is the proof for all parameters.

As an independent arithmetic check, the fiber-weighted sums are \(q^{mn}\) in both regimes: for \(h=2\) the three nonzero/zero fiber strata contribute \(q^d[(q-1)q^{m+n-2}+(q^{m-1}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)+q^{m-1}+q^{n-1}-1]=q^{mn}\); for \(h\ge3\) they contribute \((q-1)q^{mn-1}+q^{mn-1}=q^{mn}\).

## Relationship to prior work

Dolinka and East's 2015 preprint (updated 2016) gives the general rectangular-sandwich monoid framework, regularity and Green relations, semigroup rank and idempotent-generated structure, but not this prescribed-target power-map fiber distribution. Nagy and Zubor (2015) analyze ideal/congruence structure in rectangular-band semigroup algebras and do not classify individual power fibers. Related published findings on the **same algebra** give a two-input commutator-rank spectrum, a rank-one Rota–Baxter operator census, and deformation/cohomology classes. Those invariants do not imply the \(h\)-power fibers: none fixes prescribed targets in the non-linear single-variable word map. In particular, knowledge that \(X^2=0\) for a class of operator images is not the complete fiber function \(Z\mapsto\#\{X:X^2=Z\}\). The new content claimed here is the complete all-exponent fiber law and the exceptional nonzero square-only cone, not the already-known sandwich multiplication or classical cyclic-group root count.

## Limitations

This is a finite-field theorem for a rank-one all-ones sandwich matrix, and it does not assert generality for higher-rank sandwiches. The arithmetic \(g_h\) is standard cyclic-group theory. The finite computations do not prove the uniform claim independently; that is supplied by the algebraic proof. Closely related older general Munn-ring texts were not all examined in full, so historical priority is not independently certified. There is no external mathematical audit.
