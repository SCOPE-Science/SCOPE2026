# Scientific review: prescribed commutator fibers in abelian wreath products

## Correctness — PASS

The semidirect-product multiplication gives the commutator coordinate \((I-\sigma^t)x+(\sigma^s-I)y\) with zero complement component. A Cayley-graph component argument, valid over every finite abelian lamp group, identifies the image with coset-sum-zero vectors; the fiber count follows from the first isomorphism theorem. Counting pairs of cyclic-complement exponents by their gcd gives the Jordan-totient formula. The primality criterion is established by an explicit pair of nonzero targets for every composite \(n\). Eleven exhaustive small-group checks and four divisor-form checks passed. No finite computation is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The closest full-text source, Erovenko–Sury (2008), Theorem 1.1 and Section 3, establishes and explains the **zero** fiber, not the pointwise nonzero fibers or the cyclic primality test. The new result explicitly credits this case. Riedl (2015) concerns wreath-product commutator calculus and upper central series; its full text was inaccessible for final exclusion. Cox (2016) considers an infinite cyclic complement, and Shomroni (2025) treats word measures with symmetric-group complements. Related publicly indexed findings on Engel words and other associative or group commutators do not imply this stated finite regular wreath-product classification. An unknown historical equivalent or a formula implicit in unpublished extensions of the older linear-system method is a residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The natural pointwise commutator word distribution is stronger than the previously known commuting probability. It identifies exactly which lamp configurations can arise for each chosen pair of complement exponents, remains valid for all finite abelian lamp groups (not only vector spaces), and detects primality of a cyclic complement through a uniformity property of nonidentity commutator fibers. The composite example at ten positions exhibits arithmetic collisions that a zero-fiber count misses.
