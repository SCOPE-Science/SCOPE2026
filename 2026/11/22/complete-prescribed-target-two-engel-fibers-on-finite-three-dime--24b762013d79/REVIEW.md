# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The direct trace-zero Cayley–Hamilton identity produces the exact square-adjoint formula, whose kernel/image dimensions split into zero, nonzero isotropic, and nonisotropic first arguments. Orthogonal-plane counts prove each of the four target fibers in all odd finite fields. In characteristic two the derived Lie algebra is central, making every second Engel value zero. A separate standalone enumeration uses actual matrix products, including nonprime finite fields, and confirms every individual target at \(q=2,3,4,5,7,9\). Finite tests are supporting evidence, not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Existing simple-Lie-algebra Engel-map studies establish image and surjectivity assertions, not the complete number of ordered pairs over finite fields landing at *each prescribed target*. The 2015 Lie-polynomial image study does not quantify fibers. The 2026 local-ring Engel-map paper defines fibers and establishes existence of witnesses and lifting, but its inspected Section 3 does not supply the exact four-class finite-field census. The related two-Engel fiber census for free class-three nilpotent Lie algebras is not a specialization or covering theorem for the nonnilpotent \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) object. A potentially undiscovered broader finite-field Lie-word distribution theorem remains a genuine priority risk; absence from searches alone is not proof of priority.

## Value — PASS

Surjectivity reveals only whether a Lie-polynomial fiber is empty. The full target-by-target fiber law quantifies strong output biases on the natural three-dimensional simple Lie algebra: the exceptional nilpotent fiber, the split/anisotropic imbalance, and a surprisingly exact equal-mass law after aggregating the two semisimple square classes. It gives a canonical finite-field word-distribution invariant and a characteristic-two boundary, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Prior art and limitations

Bandman–Gordeev–Kunyavskii–Plotkin, *Equations in simple Lie algebras* (arXiv:1012.4106), Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4, addresses Engel surjectivity; Kanel-Belov–Malev–Rowen (arXiv:1506.06792) classifies possible Lie-polynomial images; Roy–Singh (arXiv:2607.21171), Section 3, constructs nonempty fibers and studies lifting. The exact four-way finite-field multiplicity claim is not contained in the inspected results. This remains a self-review only and has not been independently validated.
