# Complete rational dihedral cohomology of the pentagonal graph nilmanifold

## Finding
Let \(G=C_5\) be the five-cycle and let \(N(G)\) be the compact ten-dimensional Dani–Mainkar nilmanifold with its natural dihedral action. Write \(D_5=\langle r,s\mid r^5=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\), where \(r\) rotates graph labels and \(s\) reverses them. Over \(\mathbb Q\), let \(\mathbf1\) be the trivial representation, \(\varepsilon\) the reflection-sign representation, and \(W\) the four-dimensional irreducible representation on \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)\), with \(r\) acting by multiplication by \(\zeta_5\) and \(s\) by complex conjugation.

**Theorem.** For each integer \(0\le k\le10\), the complete graded rational cohomology representation is
\[
H^k(N(C_5);\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbf1^{\oplus a_k}\oplus\varepsilon^{\oplus b_k}\oplus W^{\oplus c_k},
\]
with multiplicities below. The fifth column is the ordinary Betti number \(\beta_k=a_k+b_k+4c_k\).

| Degree \(k\) | \(a_k\) | \(b_k\) | \(c_k\) | \(\beta_k\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 20 |
| 3 | 5 | 4 | 8 | 41 |
| 4 | 9 | 5 | 14 | 70 |
| 5 | 9 | 9 | 18 | 90 |
| 6 | 5 | 9 | 14 | 70 |
| 7 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 41 |
| 8 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 20 |
| 9 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 5 |
| 10 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 |

In particular the total rational Betti number is \(364\). The ordinary geometric rotation quotient has rational Betti numbers \((1,1,4,9,14,18,14,9,4,1,1)\), and the full dihedral quotient has rational Betti numbers \((1,1,2,5,9,9,5,4,2,0,0)\), in degrees \(0\) through \(10\). These are Betti-number statements, not claims about quotient smoothness or the multiplicative cohomology rings.

## Assumptions and scope
Use the graph with vertices \(0,1,2,3,4\) and unoriented edges \(\{0,1\},\{0,4\},\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{3,4\}\). Let \(\mathfrak g\) be the rational two-step nilpotent Lie algebra with basis \(X_0,\ldots,X_4\) and \(Z_{ij}\) for each listed edge with \(i<j\); the only nonzero brackets are \([X_i,X_j]=Z_{ij}\) for \(i<j\) joined by an edge. Let \(L\) be the simply connected corresponding Lie group. Take \(\Gamma\) to be the lattice generated by all \(\exp(X_i)\) and \(\exp(Z_{ij})\), and set \(N(C_5)=\Gamma\backslash L\). The group generated by the graph rotation \(r(i)=i+1\bmod5\) and reflection \(s(i)=-i\bmod5\) preserves \(\Gamma\), as its induced action sends each \(X_i\) to \(X_{g(i)}\) and each oriented central generator to the appropriately signed generator. Hence this finite group acts on the nilmanifold, with right/left quotient conventions not changing its cohomology representation. All cohomology and group representations here have rational coefficients.

## Proof
**Step 1: exact cochain model.** The rational Chevalley–Eilenberg cochain algebra is the exterior algebra on \(e_0,\ldots,e_4\) and \(f_{01},f_{04},f_{12},f_{23},f_{34}\), with
\[
de_i=0,\qquad df_{ij}=e_i\wedge e_j.\]
Order these ten degree-one generators as written. For an ordered exterior word \(g_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge g_{i_k}\), the differential is the graded derivation
\[
d(g_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge g_{i_k})=\sum_{p=1}^k(-1)^{p-1}g_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d(g_{i_p})\wedge\cdots\wedge g_{i_k}.\]
It is implemented by signed exterior bitmask replacement in `reproduce.py`; the script tests \(d^2=0\) on every one of the \(2^{10}=1024\) exterior monomials. Nomizu's theorem, applied to the rational nilpotent lattice, identifies its cohomology with \(H^*(N(C_5);\mathbb Q)\), compatibly with automorphisms induced by the graph.

**Step 2: exact graded ranks.** Write \(R_k=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}(d:C^k\to C^{k+1})\). The complete exact rank vector for \(0\le k\le9\) is
\[(R_0,\ldots,R_9)=(0,5,20,59,81,81,59,20,5,0).\]
The cochain dimensions are \(\binom{10}{k}\), so the identity \(\beta_k=\binom{10}{k}-R_{k-1}-R_k\), with boundary ranks zero, gives the fifth column of the table.

**Step 3: signed orbit subcomplexes.** The rotation and reflection act by permuting the vertex generators and by signed permutation of the oriented edge generators; the latter signs are mandatory when an edge's ordered endpoints reverse. The script tests \(r^5=s^2=srsr=1\) and \(dr=rd\), \(ds=sd\) on all \(1024\) monomials. Over characteristic zero, invariants of a finite group are an exact functor. A signed orbit in degree \(k\) contributes one invariant cochain precisely when its stabilizer acts with positive sign. The rotation-invariant cochain dimensions and differential ranks (degree \(0\) through \(10\) and degree \(0\) through \(9\), respectively) are
\[
(1,2,9,24,42,52,42,24,9,2,1),\quad(0,1,4,11,17,17,11,4,1,0),
\]
and hence the dimensions of rotation-fixed cohomology are
\[(q_0,\ldots,q_{10})=(1,1,4,9,14,18,14,9,4,1,1).\]
For a reflection the cochain dimensions are
\[(1,5,20,60,110,126,100,60,25,5,0),\]
its differential ranks are
\[(0,2,8,31,42,39,28,12,3,0),\]
and therefore its fixed cohomology dimensions are
\[(u_0,\ldots,u_{10})=(1,3,10,21,37,45,33,20,10,2,0).\]
Finally, a separate two-generator signed-orbit traversal calculates the **full** \(D_5\)-invariant subcomplex with cochain dimensions
\[(1,1,2,12,26,26,16,12,7,1,0)\]
and differential ranks
\[(0,0,0,7,10,7,4,4,1,0),\]
producing the trivial-multiplicity vector \((1,1,2,5,9,9,5,4,2,0,0)\) directly.

**Step 4: group-theoretic reconstruction.** The three rational irreducible representations of \(D_5\) are \(\mathbf1\), \(\varepsilon\), and \(W\), with dimensions \(1,1,4\). Every nonidentity rotation has trace \(1\) on \(\mathbf1\) and \(\varepsilon\), and trace \(-1\) on \(W\); reflections have traces \(1,-1,0\), respectively. The trace of a reflection on \(H^k\) equals \(t_k=2u_k-\beta_k\). Consequently
\[
a_k=(q_k+t_k)/2,\qquad b_k=(q_k-t_k)/2,\qquad c_k=(\beta_k-q_k)/4.\]
Substituting the exact ranks yields the stated table; all entries are nonnegative integers. Poincaré duality twisted by the reflection orientation character exchanges \(a_k\) and \(b_{10-k}\) and identifies \(c_k=c_{10-k}\), as the computed values confirm.

**Step 5: quotients.** For a finite group acting cellularly on a compact triangulable space, rational cohomology of its ordinary orbit space is naturally the invariant part of rational cohomology. Apply this with the rotation subgroup and with \(D_5\) to obtain the two quotient Betti vectors. The full-dihedral vector also agrees with the direct full-invariants rank calculation in Step 3.

## Verification
Run `python3 reproduce.py` with Python 3 and only the standard library. It creates every exterior monomial in every degree; constructs each signed differential column by the displayed derivation; eliminates sparse columns by normalized exact `fractions.Fraction` pivots; and constructs invariant subcomplexes by signed orbit enumeration, including an independent full-group orbit traversal. The complete output is embedded in `reproduce_output.txt`. It verifies every column of \(d^2=0\), both chain-automorphism identities, all three invariant rank computations, the table, group relations, Poincaré duality, and Euler/Lefschetz consistency. The computation is exhaustive for this finite Lie algebra, not an extrapolation from sample graphs.

## Relationship to prior work
Aldi, Bevins, Chatzitheodoridis, Da Silva, Frias, and Mejía Gómez (arXiv:2611.17014v1, first public 12 November 2026) compute Poincaré polynomials for **path graphs** and give general lower bounds. Their theorems do not determine pentagonal cyclic-graph cohomology or graph-automorphism representations. Aldi, Butler, Gardiner, Grandini, Lichtenwalner, and Pan (arXiv:2402.14188v2, first public 22 February 2024) give a general induced-subgraph decomposition, explicit low-degree cohomology, and full star-graph results; their general technique can serve as an alternative route for computations, but the inspected article contains no graded pentagonal \(D_5\)-module table. An existing cycle-nilmanifold total-Betti **lower bound** specializes to \(364\) at the pentagon; it neither establishes the equality nor specifies any graded dihedral character, so it is not counted as this finding. The novelty claim concerns the *complete graded rational dihedral representation* and the resulting quotient Betti vectors, not the definitions or existing general methods.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns this canonical ten-dimensional graph nilmanifold and its rational cohomology as a \(D_5\)-module. It is not a classification of all cycle lengths, integral cohomology lattices or torsion, cohomology cup products, quotient singularities, or quotient homotopy types. Exact calculation of a finite chain complex is a proof of the stated finite-dimensional result, not a proof of any conjectural infinite-family recurrence. An unindexed equivalent computation may exist; publication priority and the mathematical argument have not been independently audited.
