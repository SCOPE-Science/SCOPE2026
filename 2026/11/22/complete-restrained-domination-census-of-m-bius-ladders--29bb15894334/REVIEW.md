# Review of the complete Möbius-ladder restrained-domination census

**Correctness — PASS.** The outside-vertex test is exactly the selected-neighbor count of one or two in a cubic graph. Twisted boundary conditions give a one-to-one correspondence between admissible selected subsets and closed weighted walks in the sixteen-state transfer. The row-swap involution and the explicit determinants reduce all cardinalities to a formal logarithmic derivative. A two-order series expansion establishes all first nonzero coefficients, including the nine-set small case. The exact symbolic determinant verifier and the adjacency-based exhaustive verifier both passed. The finite tests support, rather than replace, the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Knor and Potočnik (2012) previously proved the efficient-domination existence condition for the same graphs; that boundary is credited. Hemalatha and collaborators (2023) discuss Möbius-ladder restrained-domination *numbers* but do not enumerate all feasible sets or give the multiplicity of minimum sets. Published one-limited-domination results constrain selected vertices rather than unselected vertices, so their cardinality polynomials do not imply the present one. The earlier exact circular-ladder problem has a different, untwisted closure and does not determine the sign change between transfer sectors. The closest primary paper on the identical object and predicate was examined at its relevant theorem and proof. Unindexed historical work and incompletely available older primary texts are residual risks; a search result is not a uniqueness certificate.

**Value — PASS.** The restrained-domination minimum is a natural graph invariant, but its numerical value alone does not measure the redundancy of optimal placements. Here the full cardinality distribution is exact for every order, and the optimum multiplicity changes between constant, quadratic, and linear regimes under a nontrivial twisted boundary. The especially large quadratic family quantifies how topological closure affects optimal solution degeneracy. This is not a one-off numerical table or an automatic implication of a known minimum-size theorem.

**Scope and limitations.** All statements concern labelled simple Möbius ladders of order at least six, with no claims about optimal-set automorphism orbits, arbitrary cubic graphs, or algorithmic hardness. The published 2023 numerical theorem's printed cases were not assumed as proof. Priority outside the materials actually inspected is not certified. Included finite computations are not independent external validation.
