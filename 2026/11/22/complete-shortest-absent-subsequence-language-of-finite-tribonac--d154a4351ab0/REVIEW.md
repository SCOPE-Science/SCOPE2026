# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The complete proof starts with the published exact arch factorization and independently checks its rank specialization. The exact last-nine-letter suffix recurrence proves the trailing-tail cases and the double-one condition in the last full arch. The near-universality lemma splits all ternary words into a binary-to-two adjacency case and a terminal-binary-run case and constructs actual subsequence embeddings in both. The three listed missing words cannot occur because they require more copies of two or a letter after the last two; no finite test is treated as an infinite proof. Independent dynamic programming corroborates the boundary on substitution ranks four through ten.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risk)
Targeted searches compared full statements for the multinacci arch result, shortest absent scattered subsequences of Tribonacci words, missing powers of the rare letter, generalized period-doubling and paperfolding exceptions, equivalences, and subsequence-automaton formulations. The closest exact arch result supplies the count of complete arches but not the language of all shortest missing words. Known classifications for period-doubling, paperfolding, binary Cantor and repeated ternary permutations use different words and are not implication-dominating results. Possible unstated consequences of existing return-word or subsequence-automaton theory remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
An exact three-letter *language* at an exponentially growing forbidden length is a stronger invariant than an arch/universality count. It establishes exceptional almost-universality: of the exponentially many words of the first impossible length, all but one or three occur, with a sharp rank-dependent phase transition. The constructive pairing argument is a reusable structural lemma about arch families, not an arbitrary finite enumeration.

## Limits
The theorem is for unshifted finite Tribonacci iterates and full alphabet. It is not an assertion for all epistandard words. The known arch theorem is explicitly credited; independent validation has not been performed.
