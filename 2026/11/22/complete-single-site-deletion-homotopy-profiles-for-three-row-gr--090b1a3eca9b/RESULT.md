# Complete single-site-deletion homotopy profiles for three-row grid matching complexes at widths \(4,5,6\)

## Finding

Write \(G_n=P_3\square P_n\) for the ordinary rectangular grid with three rows and \(n\) columns. For every \(n\in\{4,5,6\}\) and every graph vertex \(v\in V(G_n)\), the ordinary matching complex of the induced vertex deletion \(G_n-v\) has an exact integral sphere-wedge homotopy type. Row and column reversal reduce all \(3n\) deleted vertices to the following **sixteen orbit representatives** (row \(r\in\{0,1\}\) and column \(0\le c\le\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\)). The entries are sphere multiplicities except the explicitly marked dimension-two exception:

| Width | Delete \((0,0)\) | Delete \((0,1)\) | Delete \((0,2)\) | Delete \((1,0)\) | Delete \((1,1)\) | Delete \((1,2)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(n=4\) | \(3S^3\) | \(S^3\) | — | \(S^2\) | \(2S^3\) | — |
| \(n=5\) | \(6S^4\) | \(4S^4\) | \(4S^4\) | \(2S^4\) | \(4S^4\) | \(4S^4\) |
| \(n=6\) | \(14S^5\) | \(9S^5\) | \(11S^5\) | \(6S^5\) | \(11S^5\) | \(8S^5\) |

For example, deleting a vertex \((1,0)\) on the middle row of the \(3\times4\) grid lowers the homology dimension from the unpunctured degree three to degree two. At width six, all deleted-vertex complexes have homology in degree five, with five distinct nonzero sphere multiplicities \(6,8,9,11,14\). These are exact homotopy classifications, not solely mod-two Betti numbers.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set of \(G_n\) is \(\{(r,c):0\le r<3,\ 0\le c<n\}\), and two vertices are adjacent precisely when their Manhattan distance is one. For each \(v\), the induced subgraph \(G_n-v\) removes \(v\) **and all incident graph edges**, not just a single grid edge. The matching complex \(\mathcal M(H)\) has the edges of \(H\) as its vertices and all collections of pairwise vertex-disjoint graph edges as faces. The notation \(kS^d\) in the table means the wedge \(\bigvee^k S^d\), not a disjoint union. The result is restricted to precisely the three given finite widths; no recurrence or all-width claim is made.

## Proof

**Exhaustion.** For each representative \(v\), the verifier constructs the complete graph-edge list of \(G_n-v\) by checking all pairs of retained grid vertices. Each edge is assigned an index, and all nonempty matchings are generated by recursively including or excluding successive edges, with inclusion allowed only if their endpoints are unoccupied. Induction on the remaining edge indices proves that every matching is produced once and no nonmatching is produced.

**Morse construction.** Identify each matching with the integer bitmask of its edges. For each nonempty face \(\tau\), list every codimension-one face \(\sigma\subset\tau\); these are all its immediate lower Hasse neighbors. Maintain an active subset of faces and, for each active face, the number of its immediate lower faces that remain active. Whenever an active face \(\tau\) has exactly one remaining lower neighbor \(\sigma\), pair \(\sigma\) with \(\tau\) and remove them both. Choose these pairs in increasing lexicographic order of \((\dim\tau,\mathrm{mask}(\tau))\). When none exists, mark the active face with smallest \((\dim,\mathrm{mask})\) critical and remove it. Continue to exhaustion. Each face is removed exactly once, each pair is a Hasse cover, and this is an explicit deterministic matching prescription, without reliance on the claimed output.

**Acyclicity, independently checked.** Rather than assuming the prescription is acyclic, the verifier reconstructs the complete Hasse diagram from all enumerated faces, directs matched covers from lower to upper and unmatched covers from upper to lower, and topologically sorts the resulting directed graph by Kahn's exact algorithm. In all sixteen cases every Hasse vertex is sorted; thus there is no directed cycle, establishing the discrete-Morse condition. The critical faces have exactly one critical zero-face and the stated number of critical faces all in the stated single dimension, with no other critical dimensions. Every claimed dimension is at least two. Forman's theorem therefore gives a CW model with exactly one zero-cell and no cells in dimensions one through one less than the top dimension, so all top cells attach constantly and its homotopy type is exactly the indicated wedge. This is an integral homotopy argument, not an inference from a field computation.

**Symmetry.** The two grid automorphisms \((r,c)\mapsto(2-r,c)\) and \((r,c)\mapsto(r,n-1-c)\) map each vertex to its unique representative in the table. They induce simplicial isomorphisms on matching complexes of the corresponding deleted graphs. The verifier also checks that the listed representative orbits form a disjoint partition of all \(3n\) grid vertices.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify_grid_vertex_punctures.py` re-generates all sixteen simplicial complexes and their matchings, checks the dimension and number of every critical cell, checks coverage of faces by pairings/critical cells, and performs a complete Hasse DAG check. Separately, it computes reduced mod-two homology from the full simplicial boundary maps by exact bitset Gaussian elimination, and independently checks each reduced Euler characteristic directly from the face counts. Its output is `CERTIFICATE_OUTPUT.txt` and it concludes `PASS cases=16 faces=87378 covers=419433 pairs=43636`. These statistics cover all 87,378 nonempty simplices and all 419,433 Hasse-cover relations in the sixteen orbit-representative complexes. The script reconstructs its data from definitions; no stored binary certificate, external solver, randomness, or previous-run evidence is needed.

## Relationship to prior work

Chauhan, Sharma, and Shukla (arXiv:2609.27366, first publicly posted 2026-09-23) establish connectivity and homological vanishing for the ordinary **unpunctured** three-row grids, rather than the sixteen vertex-deleted complexes here. Published finite perfect-discrete-Morse classifications likewise concern the unpunctured grids; their relevant full result was compared. A distinct published 2026 classification treats deletion of a **single graph edge** in widths five and six, and gives different edge-position ranks. Graph vertex deletion eliminates two, three, or four incident edges simultaneously, corresponding to an intersection of multiple vertex-deletion subcomplexes in the matching complex; neither the unpunctured homotopy type nor the one-edge deletion types determine the homotopy type of this intersection. In particular, the dimension drop at a four-column middle-row endpoint and the complete six-column vertex profile are additional local facts.

## Limitations

This is a finite exhaustive classification for three-row nonperiodic grids of widths four, five, and six, and exactly one graph vertex removed. It says nothing about wider grids, simultaneous deletions, relative inclusion homomorphisms, or general puncture recurrences. The September 2026 primary paper could be read at abstract/indexed-result level, but its full text was not retrievable during this comparison. Despite targeted searches and inspection of closely related published results, an unindexed equivalent finite computation cannot be excluded. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
