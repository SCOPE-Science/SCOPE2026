# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The asserted theorem is for integers \(p\ge2\), \(1\le m\le p\), injected character indices, all \(p\) subgroup translations and an arbitrary subset of modulations. The explicitly constructed orthonormal basis \(v_{u,a}\) verifies every coefficient and normalization. Orthogonality of the cyclic characters in the translation index yields the exact operator identity \(S_L=p^2\sum_ac_aP_a\). It implies the complete eigenvalue multiplicities, canonical dual eigenvector scaling, rank and tightness assertions. Independent direct-matrix tests of 1,281 parameter/mask instances and 63 exhaustive cardinality/count comparisons passed. Combinatorial isospectral counts and the strict minimum/maximum inequalities prove the optimum classifications without reliance on finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS with proximity caveat

Asipchuk–De Carli–Rodriguez, *Block-equivalent finite Gabor frames*, arXiv:2605.16139, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 5.1, provide block equivalence for every subgroup translation set. Their Corollary 4.3 and the example after it display one particular interlaced-character tight frame. The present theorem does **not** claim discovery of that framework or example. The new evaluated statements concern every arbitrary mask: occupancy-only eigenvalues with exact multiplicities and phase-label independence; each atom itself an eigenvector with explicit scalar canonical dual; full isospectral-class counts; exact globally optimal, enumerated cardinality designs. They are not stated by the source's block-decomposition or example, although both are essential structural antecedents.

Indexed related findings about translation erasures of a one-defect window, geometric windows and two-channel two-tap windows concern different modulation/translation patterns or window families. Their titles and summaries were compared by mathematical object and implication, not by wording alone. The complete text of the primary motivating work and the original result texts of the two closest indexed translation-mask papers were examined. An earlier equivalent evaluation derived from the general block formula remains the principal priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Arbitrary deleted frequency labels occur naturally in structured subsampled finite Gabor analysis. The theorem determines every mask's sharp stability and exact, no-matrix-inversion canonical reconstruction; this is stronger than merely knowing a block decomposition. It classifies and enumerates all tight and all optimally conditioned designs for every feasible cardinality, and quantifies guaranteed whole-frequency erasure survival. These results settle a natural finite design problem over all composite or prime subgroup orders, without inventing a general infinite-dimensional frame claim.

## Limits

The family is restricted to full subgroup translations and phase-interlaced characters with distinct character labels. Other window families and independent deletion of individual time-frequency atoms are not addressed. The finite computations are corroborative, not a proof of the all-parameter theorem. Priority remains subject to additional bibliographic review, and no external independent mathematical audit has occurred.
