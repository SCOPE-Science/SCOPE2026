# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The digit-sum/power-sum equivalence is proved by separating exponents modulo the exact multiplicative order. The exceptional finite census is exhaustive: every weak five-part composition of each weight \(1\le t\le8\) is tested, using a canonical residual fifth coordinate. Explicit zero-sum multiplicity vectors furnish weights \(5,7,9,10,11,12,13\), and concatenation with a weight-five sum generates all larger weights. The lifting-the-exponent identity proves that \(H_k\) is the inverse image of \(H_2\), so each modular zero-sum length transfers to every higher power. Checks use exact arithmetic and do not replace the infinite lifting proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected sources
Jacob–Külshammer (arXiv:2204.09403v1) already give the **minimum** lengths \(3\) and \(5\) and a lifting principle, which are acknowledged. Clokie et al. (arXiv:2002.02731v1) provide digital weight/power-sum equivalence and general algorithms. Neither inspected account gives the *complete* exceptional spectrum \(\{5,7\}\cup\{t\ge9\}\), particularly the holes at weights six and eight. The general digit-sum literature and semantically related published-finding corpus records were also checked. Existing but unlocated exact classifications remain possible, and independence is not claimed.

## Value — PASS
The smallest three-adic Wieferich phenomenon for base three occurs at \(11\): \(3^5\equiv1\pmod{121}\) before the multiplicative order starts growing. At this natural exceptional prime power, the shortest vanishing-sum invariant hides nontrivial *interior* omissions: weight six and eight fail despite weights five and seven succeeding, while every weight from nine onward succeeds. A complete local digit-sum spectrum answers a strictly richer, natural representation question than the known minimum-length computation.
