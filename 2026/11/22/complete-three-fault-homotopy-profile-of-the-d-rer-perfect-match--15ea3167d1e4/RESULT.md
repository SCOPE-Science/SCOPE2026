# Complete three-fault homotopy profile of the Dürer perfect-matching complex

## Finding
Let \(D=\operatorname{GP}(6,2)\) be the Dürer graph, and let \(\mathcal P(G)\) be the simplicial complex generated by the perfect matchings of the finite graph \(G\). For every edge set \(F\subseteq E(D)\) with \(|F|\leq 3\), the complex \(\mathcal P(D-F)\) is either void or has the integral homotopy type of a connected graph (hence a wedge of circles). The exact **complete** census is:

| Deleted edges \(|F|\) | Void | Contractible | \(S^1\) | \(\bigvee^2 S^1\) | \(\bigvee^3 S^1\) | Total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 0 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 0 | 18 |
| 2 | 0 | 78 | 63 | 12 | 0 | 153 |
| 3 | 20 | 662 | 132 | 0 | 2 | 816 |

The two three-edge fault sets yielding three circles are precisely the alternating outer-rim triples \(\{u_0u_1,u_2u_3,u_4u_5\}\) and \(\{u_1u_2,u_3u_4,u_5u_0\}\). Among single-edge faults, deleting an outer edge gives \(S^1\), deleting a spoke gives \(\bigvee^2S^1\), and deleting an inner triangle edge gives a contractible complex. There are exactly twenty three-edge fault sets with no perfect matching. For every nonvoid case the reduced integral homology is free and concentrated in degree one, of rank equal to the indicated number of circles.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices are \(u_i,v_i\), with \(i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\). The 18 undirected edges are precisely \(u_i u_{i+1}\) (outer, IDs 0–5), \(u_i v_i\) (spokes, IDs 6–11), and \(v_i v_{i+2}\) (inner, IDs 12–17), listed in increasing \(i\). For nonempty perfect-matching sets, \(\mathcal P(G)\) contains every subset of every perfect matching (including the conventional empty face); if there are none, its geometric realization is defined to be void. The theorem quantifies over **all** \(1+18+153+816=988\) graph-edge fault sets with at most three deletions; it does not assert formulas for larger faults.

## Proof
**All matchings.** Perfect matchings are enumerated by taking the least unmatched graph vertex and pairing it with each still-unmatched neighbor, until all twelve vertices are covered. The resulting ten perfect matchings (in the specified edge-index system) are

```
(0,2,10,11,12,13) (0,3,8,11,13,16) (0,4,8,9,16,17)
(1,3,6,11,13,14) (1,4,6,9,14,17) (1,5,9,10,12,17)
(2,4,6,7,14,15) (2,5,7,10,12,15) (3,5,7,8,15,16)
(6,7,8,9,10,11)
```

This list is independently verified by scanning all \(\binom{18}{6}\) graph-edge six-subsets and testing that every vertex is covered exactly once.

**A facet nerve.** For a fixed fault set \(F\), retain only the perfect matchings disjoint from \(F\). Each surviving matching \(P\) supports a full simplex of dimension five; these simplices cover \(\mathcal P(D-F)\). An intersection of such simplices, when nonempty, is itself a full simplex on the edges common to all the corresponding perfect matchings. Thus every nonempty finite intersection is contractible, and the simplicial nerve theorem gives a homotopy equivalence with the intersection nerve \(N_F\). Its nonempty faces are exactly the nonempty collections of surviving perfect matchings having at least one common graph edge.

**Complete finite collapse certificates.** `CERTIFICATE.json` contains a separate ordered list of elementary free-face collapses and a final residual face set for every one of the 988 fault sets. The standalone `VERIFY.py` reconstructs the graph and its ten perfect matchings, reconstructs every nerve, then for each certificate pair checks that the proposed lower-dimensional face has **exactly one proper coface in the current entire complex**, that this coface differs by one vertex, and that both faces are removed. Thus these are genuine simplicial collapses, not inferred cancellations. In all nonvoid cases the remaining complex is a connected graph; in the twenty void cases no perfect matching survives. The certificates contain a total of 2,632 individually checked elementary collapse pairs. Every graph's circle rank is its number of graph edges minus its number of vertices plus one. These integer graph ranks give precisely the table, proving the displayed **integral homotopy** classification without rational-only assumptions. The verifier separately computes the Euler characteristic from the original graph-edge faces, confirming the graph rank in each nonvoid case.

**Exceptional fault configurations.** Checking all \(\binom{18}{3}=816\) three-edge sets against the complete matching list proves that exactly twenty strike every perfect matching. Precisely two other three-edge sets give a three-circle graph after collapse; their edge IDs are `(0,2,4)` and `(1,3,5)`, which are exactly the alternating outer-rim triples stated above. Every surviving case at all four fault levels is connected.

## Verification
Place `VERIFY.py` beside `CERTIFICATE.json` and run `python3 VERIFY.py`. No third-party libraries are needed. `VERIFY_OUTPUT.txt` records the matching output. Two independent perfect-matching enumerators agree on the ten facets. For all 988 fault sets, the script checks each exact free-face operation, every residual graph, all multiplicities, and an independent Euler computation from the original complex rather than the nerve. Its proof is a finite exhaustive certificate: it neither extrapolates to greater fault sizes nor relies on mod-prime ranks to assert integral homotopy.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Jelić Milutinović, and Vega introduced the perfect-matching complex framework and used nerves and discrete Morse theory for honeycomb graphs [1]. Chandrakar and Singh classified perfect-matching complexes of grid strips and polygonal **line** tilings [2]. Neither treats the Dürer graph's graph-edge fault lattice. A previously established calculation identifies the intact Dürer perfect-matching complex as collapsible [3]; a further result establishes its dihedral equivariant contractibility [4]. Those **intact-case facts are not re-claimed**, and neither determines homotopy after edges are deleted. A published calculation of the Dürer graph's **ordinary matching complex** [5] concerns all matchings, including nonextendible ones, rather than perfect-matching extendibility. The present result instead classifies every fault set of sizes one, two and three, including its connected wedge ranks, vanishing cases, and exceptional triples. No priority or general uniqueness claim is made beyond the inspected evidence.

## Limitations
The theorem is finite and specific to \(\operatorname{GP}(6,2)\), not an assertion for all generalized Petersen graphs. It does not determine maps induced by nested fault sets, nor higher-size fault topology. The matching enumeration, nerve construction and collapse certificates are exact, but publication priority against unindexed work and independent mathematical audit are not established.
