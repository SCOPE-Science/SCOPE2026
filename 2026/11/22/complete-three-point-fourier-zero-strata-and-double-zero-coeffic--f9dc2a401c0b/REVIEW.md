# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies the annihilator-coset lifting factor \(N/h\), establishes the two-zero bound by a real-line/unit-circle intersection, and counts all two-zero projective coefficient rays by rank-two Fourier-row kernels and explicit character-annihilator inclusion-exclusion. It gives a full argument that the only no-double-zero case is the Klein-four difference subgroup, including the nontrivial finite-group cover inequality. One-zero/no-zero realizability uses proper complex hyperplanes. The global minimum follows from an explicit subgroup-order argument. Exact rational-phase finite checks cover 4,605 anchored supports; they are corroboration, not the proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to searched evidence
The full inspected public statements for all-cyclic minimum, odd-prime elementary groups, and fourth-power cyclic products cover meaningful special cases of the possible support sizes. This result does not re-label those as new. The all-finite-abelian fixed-support theorem and precise projective coefficient-ray count are stronger, and neither follow by a formal implication of those published restricted hypotheses. Massoulié gives a general quantitative near-extremizer structure theorem but not a sharp three-site stratum or its count. A prior unindexed three-term Fourier-minor classification remains an explicit bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS
The object is the first nontrivial sparse Fourier support problem after two impulses on an arbitrary finite abelian group, where mixed torsion changes the outcome. This is a natural, complete classification for a fixed three-point support with a sharply identified Klein-four boundary and a parameterized integer count of all maximally vanishing complex coefficient rays. The count distinguishes the support-only uncertainty problem from the geometry of extremizing coefficient choices and can benchmark sparse Fourier designs over composite-exponent groups. It is not an arbitrary finite slice or a routine recomputation of known tables.

## Limitations and literature
The result does not handle larger supports or restrict coefficient alphabets. Special cases from previously published cyclic and prime-power results are explicitly acknowledged. Independently complete bibliographic coverage is not asserted.
