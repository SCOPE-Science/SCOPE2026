# Scientific review: complete primitive two-cycle census

## Correctness

**PASS.** The exact return-point equations force either an origin/fixed-point case, a central symmetric two-cycle, or a strictly unequal-squared-coordinate solution. In the latter case the equations are equivalent, in both directions, to a prescribed coordinate product and a prescribed sum of squares. Their positive-root discriminant gives an if-and-only-if existence criterion and exactly two reflection-paired primitive cycles. Direct return-Jacobian expansion gives \(\det(P-I)=2r(a^2-b^2)^2/(1+r)>0\), which proves a real Floquet multiplier greater than one. The two thresholds cross precisely at \(r=1/3\). The exact symbolic/rational checker reproduces the determinant identity and representative cycles, but exhaustive classification relies on the algebraic proof, not finite tests.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to the source-access limitations below.** The 2023 original quadratic-map paper introduces the setting and qualitative bifurcations, the September 2024 continuation discusses discrete Lorenz attractors and primary origin bifurcations, and the open-access 2024 detailed treatment provides the normal form, second-iterate duality, origin stability triangle, and a qualitative flip/pitchfork scheme. These prior statements do **not** logically imply the global noncentral real prime-two orbit count, a pair of necessarily unstable off-symmetry cycles, or the complete ordering transition at \(r=1/3\) without the new calculation. No equivalent theorem was found in the checked published mathematical summaries or relevant accessible full-text sections. This is a source-bounded, not an absolute priority, judgment.

The most relevant accessible full text is A. S. Gonchenko, *On discrete Lorenz attractors of various types*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.18500/0869-6632-003140, in particular Section 2.1 (normal form and second-iterate duality) and Section 2.2 (stability triangle, primary period-doubling and pitchfork). The 2023 paper, DOI https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0172243, and the September 2024 paper, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.padiff.2024.100904, were compared using their accessible abstracts and indexed article sections, rather than represented as wholly inspected when full documents could not be obtained.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A complete natural first nontrivial periodic-orbit census distinguishes isolated saddles outside the central flip regime from the usual switching two-cycle. The sharp damping ratio determines whether the system admits a whole open parameter interval with two *asymmetric* prime-two cycles but no symmetric cycle. The forced positive expanding Floquet multiplier forbids these additional cycles from being stable attractors and changes the interpretation of low-period attractor diagrams. The square-root collision law describes the precise local birth geometry. These are structural facts for the published normal form, not arbitrary parameter samples or the enumeration of known tables.

## Limits and closest comparisons

The originating 2023 paper and two 2024 continuations have the strongest model overlap. A recorded two-cycle census for a generalized three-dimensional Hénon map, an unrelated three-dimensional parabolic map, and a cubic neuron map has similar vocabulary but different defining equations and cannot cover this theorem by a name match. The 2023 and September 2024 full texts remained unavailable through the lawful checked access routes; their uninspected results, plus unindexed independent derivations, are the principal residual originality risks. No external mathematical validation is asserted. The theorem does not address basins or higher periods.
