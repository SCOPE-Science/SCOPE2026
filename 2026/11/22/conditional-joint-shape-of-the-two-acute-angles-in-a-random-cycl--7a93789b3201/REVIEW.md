# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The inscribed-angle and Dirichlet-gap representations reduce the two-acute configuration to a unique adjacent pair. The exact three-gap density and unit-Jacobian transformation give the conditional joint density, while triangular integration gives an independent beta–uniform representation and the gamma limit. The reproducibility checker passed 1,251 rational and sampled assertions; the analytic integrals, not finite experiments, prove the all-size and limiting assertions. Boundary cases are covered for every integer \(n\ge4\) and tie events are null.

**Originality — PASS, with historical risk.** Putnam's December 3, 2005 A6 solution gives the event classification and scalar probability. Finch's 2016 *Random Cyclic Quadrilaterals*, Section 2, covers the four-vertex adjacent-angle joint density, and Section 4 treats the five-vertex angle law. The n=4 special case is not claimed as new. Actual research-record searches using acute-angle defects, conditional adjacent Dirichlet exceedances and beta–uniform factorization returned no all-size equivalent. Known scalar event counts and fixed-size angle laws do not directly state the general conditional shape hierarchy. Older general Dirichlet linear-combination literature remains a residual priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem describes the full marked geometry of a naturally rare polygon event rather than only its frequency. The independent deficit ratio, beta maximum, positive exact correlation and two-dimensional gamma-type scaling identify interpretable dependence that can inform conditioning, rare-event simulation and shape inference.

**Limitations.** Uniform i.i.d. points on a fixed circle are required, conditional on exactly two acute vertices. The result is not established for general boundary distributions or interior disk samples. No independence from unobserved geometric shape quantities is asserted. The review is not independent and does not establish absolute historical priority.

**References.** Putnam 2005 A6 and Richard Lockhart's solutions, https://www.sfu.ca/~lockhart/richard/Putnam2005Sol.pdf; Steven R. Finch, *Random Cyclic Quadrilaterals*, arXiv:1610.00510 (2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.00510.
