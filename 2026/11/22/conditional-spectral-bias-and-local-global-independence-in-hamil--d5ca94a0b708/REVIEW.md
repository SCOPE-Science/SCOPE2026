# Review of the conditional spectral and meander law

## Correctness — PASS
The graph-to-walk translation follows Reilly–Scheinerman's strict-positive reversed-word Hamiltonicity criterion, Laplacian/minimum-degree identity, and creation-word edge and clique formulas. Reflection yields the exact initial-up-run law; central binomial estimates give its limiting shifted size-biased geometric distribution (one plus a size-biased geometric) and exponential tails. The finite-horizon meander invariance principle, applied after an arbitrary *fixed* forced prefix, yields the same process limit and therefore full local–global asymptotic independence. The complete-word boundary is handled separately. Independent finite exhaustive graph and path computations cross-checked the algebra, ballot counts and exceptional configurations. Infinite process convergence uses published theory, not finite experiments.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The 2009 primary graph study supplies the creation-sequence model, Hamiltonicity criterion and unconditioned gap, but no conditional algebraic-connectivity law or joint local–global limit was located in the inspected original sections. Earlier meander papers provide the global area/endpoint limit; these are explicitly not claimed as new. The new subject is a conditional *graph* spectral law, its precise finite distribution, and independence from the global process. This separation is neither the meander area/endpoint law alone nor the unconditioned graph gap distribution. The 1974 source was accessible as an abstract only; a differently phrased prior result, including one inferred by an expert from general meander theory, is a residual originality risk. No claim of priority certainty or independent certification is made.

## Value — PASS
Hamiltonicity is a rare structural event in this random graph model. The result specifies how conditioning on it changes the small-scale algebraic connectivity from a geometric law with mean one to a one-plus-size-biased geometric law with limiting mean four, while preserving a separate macroscopic meander geometry for clique/edge fluctuations. Precise independence across the two scales is useful for conditional network inference and spectral–combinatorial asymptotics. The statement is a natural complete structural limit theorem, not a single arbitrary numerical calculation. It concerns a fixed model and does not assert a general graph universality principle.

## Scope and residual risks
The classical graph and meander theorems are independently credited, and the older full Iglehart paper was not retrieved. An earlier result could implicitly encompass the local-run law. The short proof depends on a fixed-start version of the conditioned meander invariance principle, which is a classical extension and not independently reproved from first principles here. Independent validation remains outstanding.
