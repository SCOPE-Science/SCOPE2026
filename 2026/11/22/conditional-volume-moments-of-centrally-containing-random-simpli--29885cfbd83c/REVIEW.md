# Mathematical review of conditional random-simplex volumes

## Correctness (PASS)

The event that an independent centrally symmetric \((d+1)\)-tuple contains the origin is characterized by common-sign alternating maximal minors. Each sign orbit has exactly two successful patterns, while every unsigned maximal minor is orbit-invariant. On the successful patterns the augmented determinant reduces to the sum of unsigned minors, giving the stated marked distributional identity without sampling approximations. Isotropic determinant and overlapping-minor expectations follow from Gram–Schmidt, spherical coordinate moments and covariance of projections. In three dimensions, the three types of cubic minor correlations are evaluated by an explicit two-angle integral, yielding the complete first-three-moment formula. The centered cubic is strictly negative by rational bounds on \(\pi\), not by a numerical sign test. The packaged checker independently verifies the symbolic identities and finite-sample consequences.

## Originality (PASS, provisional priority)

Wendel's 1962 origin-absorption probability and its sign-orbit proof are background; the all-sign reduction cannot be claimed as a fundamentally new principle. Miles's 1971 spherical random-simplex moment formula and modern simplex-volume formulas concern unconditional moments, whereas the new evaluations explicitly condition on the central-containment event and require correlations between maximal minors. Previously published work on Gaussian conditional barycentric weights, nearly coplanar random spherical tetrahedra, and shallow facets of centrally containing tetrahedra treats different quantities or conditioning events. The particular evaluated third conditional moment, its nontrivial three-minor correlation, and its strictly negative centered sign were not found in the inspected material or research records. This is a best-knowledge assessment, not a guarantee of independent historical priority. The original 1971 article's complete text was unavailable; an overlooked conditional variant there remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

Conditional Euclidean volume is a natural metric invariant of spherical simplices selected to surround the reference origin, a geometrically constrained event with probability \(2^{-d}\). Its first moments measure the size of centrally covering configurations, complementing the classical absorption probability. The exact third moment and negative skewness cannot be inferred from the first two moments or from the usual unconditional moment formula; their derivation requires a genuine three-minor angular contraction. The complete first and second moment laws across dimensions and radial distributions also identify precisely which radial moment assumptions control conditional size. These are natural invariants rather than arbitrary parameter substitutions.

## Sources and remaining limitations

Foundational comparisons include Wendel (1962), Cover–Efron (1967), Ruben–Miles (1980), Miles (1971), Grote–Kabluchko–Thäle (2017 preprint), and previously published conditional Gaussian and sphere-tetrahedron geometric results. The direct published full text and abstract inspected for Wendel and later results differ in their statements. The unavailable full text of Miles (1971) leaves unresolved historical-priority risk but no known contradictory statement. General angular anisotropy, higher exact moments, and the complete conditional volume distribution are outside the established theorem. Finite computations corroborate but do not substitute for the analytic argument.
