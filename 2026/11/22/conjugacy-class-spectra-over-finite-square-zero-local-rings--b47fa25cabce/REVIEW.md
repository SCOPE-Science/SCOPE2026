# Scientific review: conjugacy-class spectra over finite square-zero local rings

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is proved for every finite commutative local unital ring whose maximal ideal is nonzero and square-zero, with arbitrary finite residue field and arbitrary radical vector-space dimension. Reduction modulo the radical separates cyclic nonscalar matrices (which admit polynomial commuting lifts) from scalar matrices (whose centralizers are governed by coefficient tuples over the residue field). A direct exhaustive classification of two-dimensional commutative centralizer algebras produces the complete eight-row element census. Orbit-stabilizer gives the class number and the exact asymptotic complement. The separate direct-multiplication checker matches every claimed centralizer multiplicity for five finite ring configurations, including both equal- and mixed-characteristic rings of size eight. No finite test is used to replace the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual uncertainty
Prasad–Singla–Spallone, arXiv:1212.6157, supplies the fundamental general extension-orbit framework for square-zero local rings (Section 10) and explicitly states that the nonprincipal matrix-tuple orbit problem is not pursued there. Its principal length-two \(\operatorname{GL}_2\) count \(q^4-q\) is recovered here, not attributed to this finding. Sharma, arXiv:1409.2698, counts orbits of pairwise-commuting matrix tuples, whereas the predominant new stratum consists of noncommuting radical coefficients. The quantitative eight-row class-size census, its explicit nonprincipal class number, and the concentration law do not occur in the inspected texts. A derivation from the more general orbit reductions is possible and fully acknowledged; an undiscovered explicit historical derivation remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
Conjugacy class sizes are a standard structural invariant of finite groups and encode the full centralizer-size distribution. Beyond the principal case, the cited reduction theorem leads to a general tuple-orbit problem that can be difficult. For degree two, the present classification resolves the complete distribution in a uniform way across all radical dimensions and mixed/equal characteristics, rather than only exhibiting a few examples. Its noncommuting coefficient stratum appears exactly when the radical has at least two independent directions, and an exact formula shows that this stratum asymptotically accounts for almost all conjugacy classes. This describes a mathematically natural boundary and an independently usable invariant, not a reparameterization or an arbitrary finite slice.

## Scope and remaining risk
The result is restricted to two-by-two invertible matrices and square-zero maximal ideals. Identically sized centralizers from different source rows must be combined when interpreting the table. The closest prior article gives a broad orbit-reduction theorem but not the evaluated invariant, so implicit derivability should not be mistaken for historical absence. Unindexed or otherwise inaccessible publications could contain a comparable explicit count.
