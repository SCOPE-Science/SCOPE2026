# Review

## Correctness — PASS

A connected forcing set must include the common hub, occupy at least one cyclic neighbor, and occupy initial segments of all but at most one pendant arm. Each condition is necessary by an unbreakable pair of initially white neighbors, and sufficient by an explicit force sequence along the cycle, seeded arm suffixes, and the final unseeded arm. Counting independent nonempty cycle intervals and the arms proves all coefficients. The one-subdivided-arm factorization is exact; the quadratic discriminant proves the four-arm transition, and Newton's coefficient inequality excludes every longer cycle. An independently implemented checker exhaustively enumerates 170 parameter cases and 1,301,942 subsets; the proof, not the tests, establishes all orders.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

The full primary paper of Mukherjee–Ray (arXiv:2611.10985v1, November 7, 2026), including its definitions and Problems 5.2 and 5.4, concerns the tree polynomial and requests unicyclic and general-graph study; it does not provide the cycle-spider formula. A publicly indexed result on perfect edge domination studies the same one-hub cycle-spider shapes but a different property, hence cannot count connected zero-forcing vertex subsets. Earlier classical zero forcing of bouquets is for another graph family and ordinary rather than connected forcing. Related connected-forcing density work gives general constraints, not an identified product-plus-sum formula or sharp real-rootedness transition. Targeted published-index comparisons found no equivalent exact result. Inaccessible older full text and unindexed tables remain residual priority risks, not a proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The result is a full cardinality distribution on a natural infinite family of unicyclic graphs with arbitrary cycle length, number of branches, and branch lengths. The structural all-but-one-arm constraint gives a sharp change from one pendant path and yields the exact multiplicity of minimum forcing sets, not merely a scalar minimum. The separate, sharp real-rootedness threshold at four arms is a nontrivial polynomial-shape transition, showing how one extra pendant edge affects analytic properties of a graph enumeration polynomial.

## Scope and limitations

No arbitrary-unicyclic or arbitrary-cactus theorem; the real-root criterion covers one particular nontrivial arm-length pattern. Computational checks are finite. Novelty is a reasoned literature comparison, not an independent priority certificate.
