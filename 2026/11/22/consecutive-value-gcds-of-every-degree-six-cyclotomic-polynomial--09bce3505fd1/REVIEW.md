# Scientific review of the consecutive degree-six cyclotomic GCD classification

## Correctness

PASS. The domain is every integer argument, and the four indices are exactly those whose cyclotomic degrees equal six. Explicit determinants of two \(12\times12\) Sylvester matrices restrict all common prime divisors to \(19,29,37\), since the only other candidate is \(2\) and all four polynomial values are odd. Exhaustive local root tables and unequal unique Hensel lifting digits eliminate common prime squares. Reflection supplies the other two indices. Primewise residue independence gives the histogram, with least period proved from nontrivial indicator sets on cyclic prime groups. The included independent integer-GCD checks and determinant checker were run from their actual standalone path. The proof does not extrapolate a finite sample to infinitely many arguments.

## Originality

PASS on accessible primary and published evidence, not a priority guarantee. De Nápoli's 2020 full HTML manuscript describes index identities of cyclotomic polynomials, including results known from 1995; it does not assert this fixed-index neighboring-argument GCD classification. Cohen and Sonn's 2015 PDF studies fixed bases and growing exponent with conditional lower-bound theorems, a different direction whose conclusions neither cover nor imply the present bounded periodic GCD profiles. The relevant published third-cyclotomic shifted-GCD and eighth-cyclotomic LCM-window findings concern degrees two and four, not all degree-six indices. Relevant searches also looked for neighboring cyclotomic GCDs, ninth-cyclotomic evaluations and the exact primes and period. A generic resultant argument gives only a finite bound; the root and prime-square tests, full four-index census, least period, and all frequencies require further specific calculations. An unindexed equivalent exact table remains the residual risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a complete classification of a natural first higher cyclotomic layer selected by \(\varphi(m)=6\), not an arbitrary list of input arguments. It exhibits a three-prime collision support with mutually exclusive roots between paired indices, a sharp composite maximum, and an exact joint CRT distribution. These local overlap data furnish a test case for studying cyclotomic evaluations at adjacent bases, where generic resultant bounds alone are much weaker. The claim is finite in polynomial degree but infinite in integer arguments; no stronger general theory or downstream application is claimed.

## Limits

The theorem addresses shift one and cyclotomic degree six only. The finite certificate is reproducible, not a formal or independent certification. Some potentially equivalent historical tabulation may not have been indexed in the checked sources.
