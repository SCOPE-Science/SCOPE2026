# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement is about total partition-preserving transformations whose square is a fixed constant map, with exactly specified block images. Fixing the target point and its block, every such root is a depth-at-most-two functional digraph with a unique fixed root. The proof independently counts (i) root-block children, (ii) immediate nonroot-block children, and (iii) their depth-two preimages. Their choices give the displayed finite product-sum without overlaps. A direct exhaustive composition checker matched every induced-block-map fiber in the stated small cases. No computational extrapolation is used to prove the all-size theorem. The unique-root classification is proved by explicit constructions in each remaining case.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty

The 2014 partition-preserving monoid reference studies generating rank, and the 2015 non-uniform-partition paper enumerates idempotents; neither material inspected determines the root-fiber formula. Snowden's 1979 full-transformation root work and Higgins's 1986 all-roots construction are substantial antecedents; they encompass the **unrestricted** parent-root structure and imply the one-block specialization. A previously considered full-transformation exact root count for arbitrary idempotent targets likewise covers that one-block restriction. None of the compared statements gives the closed prescribed-induced-block-map fibers for arbitrary block-size profiles or the sharp restricted uniqueness criterion. An unlocated weighted/colored-root version of the earlier full-transformation constructions remains a genuine priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The semigroup of maps preserving a finite equivalence relation is a natural finite transformation monoid; prescribed fibers of its power map are intrinsic multiplication data not determined by generator rank or idempotent counts. Refining by the exact quotient/induced block map is important because different block maps can have markedly different root counts. The all-shapes finite sum, universal support characterization, simple two-block specialization and sharp rigidity boundary are a complete and reusable answer for a motivated target family, rather than a small isolated example.

## Scope and limitations

The result does not count arbitrary-target square roots or higher-power fibers. All displayed identities have an elementary structural proof; small enumeration is corroborative only. An independent external audit has not been performed.
