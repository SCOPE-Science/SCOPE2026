# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The one-dimensional Hankel determinant and rational recurrence are checked algebraically. The separate cases for two recovered axes, a common coordinate, fully cancelled axial data, and a single atom reconstruct every model configuration. The only nontrivial matching ambiguity is resolved by the nonzero mixed-moment difference; when an axial marginal cancels its common coordinate is recovered from the cross moment divided by a nonzero first axial moment. The exact feasibility test checks all eight equations and the torus constraints. A standard-library supplementary program passed 700 random and 5,355 structured two-atom examples plus the zero case.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge).** Diederichs–Kolountzakis–Papageorgiou (arXiv:2107.10348, Theorem 9, Corollary 10) already establish the eight-index **uniqueness** result. They explicitly distinguish it from their constructive Theorem 11, which uses twelve fixed frequencies for two atoms in two dimensions. The present assertion is solely that an explicit algorithm and exact compatibility test work on the smaller eight-index set, including all cancellation branches. Sauer (arXiv:1603.03944) uses shifted Hankel queries, Cuyt–Lee (arXiv:1610.06329) use projection shifts with additional sampling to resolve degeneracies, and Kunis et al. (arXiv:1506.00450) use multilevel moment matrices. Bounded searches found no dominating fixed-eight all-degeneracy formula. Incomplete inspection of older work remains a priority risk; an independent literature audit has not occurred.

**Value — PASS.** The mathematical motivation is the gap between an economical, published uniqueness sample set and a larger constructive guarantee. The result closes that gap for the first nontrivial two-dimensional two-spike case and proves that a mixed sample is indispensable relative to all axis-only data. It is useful as a small exact benchmark and highlights total marginal cancellation, which invalidates naive axis pairing. This is not claimed as a general sparse inverse algorithm, globally minimum sample count, or stable noisy inversion.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
