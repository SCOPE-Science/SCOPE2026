# Scientific review

## Correctness
The minimax center follows from the Euclidean projection of zero onto the normal triangle. Positive support weights yield a bounded three-sided planar blow-up, while two positive weights yield an anisotropic parabola. The exact Gram inequality identifies every case; at the zero-weight contact transition, the third side removes half the parabola. The area Jacobians determine sharp exponents and coefficients. The included checker compares all three regimes with independently integrated finite-deficit spherical areas. These numerical checks do not establish the limits; the proof uses uniform local expansions and dominated convergence.

## Originality
The inradius contact classification itself predates this work: Bezdek–Lángi, Section 2/Lemma 1, explicitly observes possible two-contact maxima. The two-contact \(3/2\) exponent is compatible with and locally reducible to the existing lune-clearance law. The new comparison targets are the *single exact Gram-test theorem spanning all three regimes*, the zero-weight three-contact half-coefficient, the three-positive-weight sharp coefficient, and the conditional uniform triangle/parabola/half-parabola laws. Inspected Lassak, Fillmore–Fillmore and Bezdek–Lángi full texts do not supply these stochastic assertions. A more general spherical parallel-set theorem might imply some cases; historical priority remains provisional.

## Value
The Euclidean intuition that a triangle's maximal inscribed disk always has three active sides fails on the sphere. This theorem quantifies the resulting distributional change, identifies the delicate equality boundary, and describes where rare deep-interior points concentrate. Both the exact coefficients and the marked conditional limits are natural geometric invariants, not arbitrarily selected parameter values. The normal Gram criterion makes the phase transition testable on any fixed spherical triangle.

## Risks and limits
The theorem assumes the triangle is proper, geodesically convex, nondegenerate, and fixed as the deficit tends to zero. The comparison is not an exhaustive historical-priority determination, and no independent audit has been performed.
