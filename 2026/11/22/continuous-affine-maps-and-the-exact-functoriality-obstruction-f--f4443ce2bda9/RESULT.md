# Continuous affine maps and the exact functoriality obstruction for join-Grothendieck spaces

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal S\) be the join monoid of isomorphism classes of finite abstract simplicial complexes, and let \(G=K(\mathcal S)\) carry the signed-divisor Alexandrov topology of Chocano and Prieto-Martínez. Write \(\mathcal P\) for the set of join-prime isomorphism classes. The known unique join-factorization identifies \(G\) with \(\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal P}\mathbb Z e_p\).

**Complete affine-map criterion.** An algebraically affine self-map \(F(x)=b+T(x)\), with \(b\in G\) and \(T:G\to G\) a homomorphism, is continuous if and only if for every output prime \(q\), the coordinate \(F(x)_q\) is either a constant \(b_q\), or \(a_q x_{p(q)}\) for one input prime \(p(q)\) and a nonzero integer \(a_q\). In the second case the constant term is necessarily zero. These descriptions are subject only to the natural finite-support requirement: each input prime influences finitely many output primes, and only finitely many constant outputs are nonzero.

**Functoriality criterion.** For a join-monoid homomorphism \(\varphi:\mathcal S\to\mathcal S\), the induced group-completion map \(K(\varphi):G\to G\) is continuous if and only if for all *distinct* join-primes \(P,Q\), their images \(\varphi(P)\) and \(\varphi(Q)\) have no join-prime factor in common (equivalently, their join-greatest-common-divisor is the unit). Every join-monoid homomorphism is continuous on the original divisibility Alexandrov spaces, but its group-completion map need not be.

In particular no nonzero translation of \(G\) is continuous. Every continuous affine bijection is a signed permutation of the join-prime coordinates, with no translation. The emphasis is the full classification of *noninvertible* affine maps and the exact criterion for continuity after group completion; the corresponding finite-rank homeomorphism-group rigidity was previously recorded separately.

## Assumptions and scope
The join unit is the complex consisting of the empty face. The monoid and group are taken on *isomorphism classes*, not on labeled representatives. Every group element has finite prime support; there are countably many join-prime classes. The unique factorization of the finite-simplicial-complex join monoid, and the minimal-pair signed-divisor Alexandrov topology, are inputs from Chocano and Prieto-Martínez (2026), not claims of this result.

Precisely, for \(x,y\in G\), define \(x\preceq y\) when at each prime coordinate \(p\), either \(x_p=0\), or \(x_p\) and \(y_p\) have the same strict sign and \(|x_p|\le |y_p|\). This is the coordinate translation of divisibility of both factors in the unique reduced numerator-denominator pair. The Alexandrov topology has open sets that are downward closed in this order, so continuity is equivalent to order preservation. An algebraic affine map need not be continuous or an additive group action.

## Proof
**Step 1: reduction to coordinates and verification of the factorization input.** On the vertex set of a finite complex, form the hypergraph whose hyperedges are the inclusion-minimal nonfaces. The connected components of this hypergraph determine its unique join-factor partition: a vertex bipartition yields a join precisely when no minimal nonface crosses it. Hence the components are irreducible join factors; this independently reconstructs the cited unique factorization, including singleton vertices of a simplex as separate factors. In the group completion, write each coordinate as its positive exponent minus its negative exponent. The unique reduced numerator-denominator pair consists of these disjoint positive and negative supports; divisibility of both halves is exactly the stated signed-coordinate relation. A homomorphism \(T\) is determined by the integer matrix \((a_{qp})\), where each column has finite support. For a fixed output prime \(q\), write \(F(x)_q=b_q+\sum_p a_{qp}x_p\), a finite sum for each finitely supported input \(x\).

**Step 2: two distinct input coordinates cannot feed one output.** Suppose \(a_{qi}\ne0\) and \(a_{qj}\ne0\) for \(i\ne j\). Choose an integer \(t\) so that \(c=b_q+t a_{qi}\) has \(|c|>|a_{qj}|\). Both \(t e_i\preceq t e_i+e_j\) and \(t e_i\preceq t e_i-e_j\). Choosing the sign of the added \(e_j\) to oppose the sign of \(c\) produces an output with absolute \(q\)-coordinate \(|c|-|a_{qj}|<|c|\). Hence the image relation is not an order relation: \(F\) is not continuous. Thus each row has at most one nonzero matrix entry.

**Step 3: offsets cannot coexist with a variable output.** If the only nonzero row entry is \(a_{qi}\ne0\) and \(b_q\ne0\), compare \(0\preceq t e_i\) for a large integer \(t\) chosen so that \(b_q+t a_{qi}\) has the opposite sign to \(b_q\). The images cannot be ordered. Therefore a nonconstant coordinate must have zero offset.

**Step 4: sufficiency.** A constant coordinate is order preserving. A coordinate \(x\mapsto a x_i\), even with \(a<0\), preserves the signed-divisor order: equal signs remain equal (possibly reversed together) and magnitudes are scaled by \(|a|\). Coordinatewise order preservation implies continuity of the whole map. Column finiteness and finite support of \(b\) ensure that the map indeed lands in \(G\).

**Step 5: functoriality.** Any monoid homomorphism \(\varphi\) sends a join-prime \(P\) to a finite join of primes and hence determines a column-finite matrix with nonnegative integer coefficients. By Steps 2–4, its group completion is continuous exactly when each output row contains at most one nonzero entry. This means that no prime divides the images of two distinct input primes: precisely the pairwise join-coprimality criterion. Conversely, on \(\mathcal S\) itself every monoid homomorphism preserves join divisibility and is continuous for the original Alexandrov order.

For example, choose distinct join-primes \(P,Q\), put \(\varphi(P)=P*Q\), \(\varphi(Q)=Q\), and fix every other prime. This defines an injective monoid homomorphism and an algebraic automorphism after group completion. In coordinates \((P,Q)\), the latter is \((u,v)\mapsto(u,u+v)\); it sends \((0,-1)\preceq(1,-1)\) to \((0,-1)\) and \((1,0)\), which are not ordered. Thus group completion does not preserve continuity even for this injective example.

**Step 6: bijections and translations.** A translation by \(b\) has both offset and nonzero identity coefficient in any coordinate where \(b_q\ne0\); Step 3 excludes continuity. In a bijective affine map no row can be constant, by surjectivity. Step 2 then says each row uses one input. To achieve an output supported at a single prime, surjectivity forces that input to appear in no other row and its coefficient to be \(\pm1\). Injectivity excludes unused inputs. Thus the affine bijections are precisely signed coordinate permutations.

## Verification
The standalone `check_affine.py` enumerates all \(729\) two-input/two-output affine maps with integer coefficients and offsets in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). For each it tests all \(121\) comparable ordered pairs in \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^2\) against the row criterion. Every comparison agrees. It additionally checks the explicit noncontinuous, algebraically invertible substitution in Step 5. Run with `python3 check_affine.py` (standard library only).

The finite computation is not used to infer the general statement. Arbitrary prime-cardinality, all integer coefficients, and infinite exponents are handled by Steps 1–6.

## Relationship to prior work
Chocano and Prieto-Martínez establish join-prime unique factorization and the signed-divisor Alexandrov topology, including continuous inversion but noncontinuous addition. Those results provide the setting; they do not state the complete classification of continuous affine maps or the precise join-coprimality criterion for induced group-completion maps. General Alexandrov-space theory supplies the fact that continuity is equivalent to monotonicity. Earlier calculations of the topology and full homeomorphism groups of finite-rank punctured subspaces concern invertible *topological* self-maps rather than arbitrary, potentially noninvertible, algebraic affine maps or the functoriality obstruction.

## Limitations
The result classifies only algebraically affine maps, not every continuous set map of \(G\). It concerns the authors' minimal-representation signed-divisor Alexandrov order; it need not hold for other group-completion topologies. The classification is elementary once the unusual order is translated into prime coordinates, and uniqueness against unindexed literature has not been independently established. No claim is made that \(G\) is a topological group; indeed the group operation is discontinuous.
