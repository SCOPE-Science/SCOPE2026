# Contractibility of first-critical polygonal Rips quotients under all dihedral symmetries

## Finding

For every integer \(m\ge 2\), let \(C_{3m}^{m}\) be the graph obtained by connecting each pair of vertices at circular distance at most \(m\) in the cycle on \(3m\) vertices, and let \(K_m=\operatorname{Cl}(C_{3m}^{m})\) denote its flag complex. The dihedral group \(D_{3m}\) of order \(6m\) acts by rotating and reflecting the vertex labels. Then the **ordinary geometric orbit space**, not the Borel homotopy quotient, is contractible:

\[
|K_m|/D_{3m}\simeq *\qquad (m\ge 2).
\]

More precisely, write \(A_m\) for the subcomplex generated by all cyclic intervals of \(m+1\) vertices. The inclusion induces a strong deformation retraction
\[
|K_m|/D_{3m}\longrightarrow |A_m|/D_{3m},
\]
and the target is homotopy equivalent to the closed interval. In contrast, the unquotiented complex has the established homotopy type \(\bigvee^{m-1}S^2\); the rotation-only quotient has previously been identified as a Moore space with \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) torsion in first homology. The reflection symmetries eliminate the entire ordinary-quotient homotopy, not just its rational homology.

## Assumptions and scope

The parameter \(m\) is an integer at least two, \(n=3m\), and the vertex set is \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Circular distance means \(d(i,j)=\min((i-j)\bmod n,(j-i)\bmod n)\). The group \(D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\) acts through \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\). All realizations and orbit spaces are ordinary geometric realizations and topological quotients. In particular, the nonfree action is handled geometrically, not by dividing simplicial chain groups naively.

Let \(F_i=\{i,i+1,\ldots,i+m\}\) (indices modulo \(3m\)) and \(A_m=\bigcup_{i=0}^{3m-1}\Delta(F_i)\). Let \(T_j=\{j,j+m,j+2m\}\) for \(0\le j<m\).

## Proof

**1. Exhaustion of the extra simplices.** We have the exact decomposition
\[
K_m=A_m\cup\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}\Delta(T_j),
\qquad A_m\cap\Delta(T_j)=\partial\Delta(T_j),
\]
with no extra higher-dimensional simplices involving the interior of any \(\Delta(T_j)\). To prove this, rotate any nonempty clique so it contains vertex \(0\). Every remaining vertex lies either clockwise in \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\), or counterclockwise in \(\{-1,\ldots,-m\}\). Let \(x,y\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) be the greatest respective distances in these two directions. If both are positive, the two extreme vertices are adjacent precisely when \(x+y\le m\) or \(x+y\ge2m\). In the first case all vertices lie in the cyclic arc from \(-y\) to \(x\) of length at most \(m\), hence form a face of \(A_m\). In the second case the bounds \(x,y\le m\) force \(x=y=m\), so the clique contains the equilateral triple \(\{0,m,2m\}\). No fourth vertex can be adjacent to all three of these vertices: a vertex strictly between \(0\) and \(m\) is too far from \(2m\), and the other two sectors are symmetric. Thus the only cliques outside \(A_m\) are the \(m\) distinct equilateral triangles. Each of their three edges lies in \(A_m\).

**2. Equivariant circle model for the short-arc subcomplex.** Consider a geometric circle of circumference \(3m\), and choose a real number \(\varepsilon\) with \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\). At integer position \(i\), take the open circular arc \(U_i\) of length \(m+\varepsilon\) centered at \(i\). These arcs cover the circle. A finite family has nonempty intersection exactly when its integer centers fit in one circular interval of length at most \(m\). Therefore their nerve is exactly \(A_m\). Since the arcs are shorter than half the circle, every nonempty finite intersection is a single open arc. Its setwise stabilizer in \(D_{3m}\) is either trivial or generated by a reflection: no nonidentity rotation preserves a proper short arc. The reflection, when present, has a fixed midpoint, and the intersection contracts equivariantly to that midpoint. The finite-group equivariant nerve lemma, proved by the usual nerve-incidence correspondence with these stabilizer-equivariant contractions, therefore gives a \(D_{3m}\)-homotopy equivalence
\[
|A_m|\simeq_{D_{3m}}S^1.
\]
Passing to orbit spaces yields \(|A_m|/D_{3m}\simeq S^1/D_{3m}\cong[0,1]\), hence \(|A_m|/D_{3m}\) is contractible. The stabilizer check is essential here: an ordinary, nonequivariant nerve equivalence alone would not justify passing to orbit spaces.

**3. Every equilateral triangle vanishes after the full dihedral quotient.** The group \(D_{3m}\) acts transitively on the set of \(m\) equilateral triangles. The setwise stabilizer of \(T_0\) has order six and induces **all** permutations of its three vertices, so its action is the full \(S_3\) on \(\Delta(T_0)\). Consequently \(\Delta(T_0)/S_3\) is homeomorphic to a closed triangular chamber (the ordered-barycentric-coordinate chamber), while \(\partial\Delta(T_0)/S_3\) is one entire boundary edge of that chamber. The triangular chamber strongly deformation retracts onto that edge, fixing it pointwise. Because the distinct triangles meet the short-arc subcomplex only along their boundaries, the orbit-space pushout identifies
\[
|K_m|/D_{3m}=(|A_m|/D_{3m})\cup_{\partial\Delta(T_0)/S_3}(\Delta(T_0)/S_3).
\]
Extending the chamber retraction by the identity on \(|A_m|/D_{3m}\) gives the asserted strong deformation retraction onto \(|A_m|/D_{3m}\). Step 2 now proves the theorem for every \(m\ge2\).

## Verification

The accompanying standalone Python program `verify_critical_dihedral.py` uses only the standard library. Run `python3 verify_critical_dihedral.py`. For \(m=2,3,4,5\), it exhaustively compares the described list of faces against all cliques of \(C_{3m}^{m}\), checks the order-six stabilizer of an equilateral triangle, constructs the barycentrically subdivided orbit-chain complex (so stabilizer-induced cell identifications are treated correctly), and computes the quotient Betti numbers over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). In every case, the only nonzero Betti number is \(b_0=1\); the quotient Euler characteristic is one. These are finite sanity checks, **not** the proof for arbitrarily large \(m\); Steps 1–3 supply the uniform proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Adamaszek's 2011 work determines the nonequivariant homotopy types of clique complexes of powers of cycles. Adamaszek, Adams, Frick, Peterson and Previte-Johnson's 2014 work introduces the short-arc nerve model, computes the dihedral action on its homology, and describes the exceptional triangles in the nine-point example. Adamaszek and Adams' 2015 preprint develops the wider cyclic Rips winding-fraction classification. These results do not identify the **ordinary full-dihedral orbit space** at the first critical scale. A separately published calculation of the rotation-only quotient as a Moore space and a calculation of individual dihedral fixed spaces give useful comparisons, but neither determines the integral homotopy type of the full orbit space: an equivariant attaching-space argument is required. The new point is that the equilateral triangles become removable *only after their full dihedral stabilizer is retained*.

## Limitations

This theorem treats only the full dihedral group at \(n=3m\), at the inclusive graph-distance threshold \(m\), with \(m\ge2\). It does not classify smaller dihedral subgroups, nearby threshold values, or homotopy/Borel quotients. The proof uses a standard equivariant nerve lemma together with an explicit clique classification and quotient-chamber retraction. The finite computational tests are illustrative and do not replace the all-parameter argument. The originality assessment is limited by the possibility of unindexed equivalent orbit-space results or unstated consequences inside inaccessible records; no independent audit has occurred.
