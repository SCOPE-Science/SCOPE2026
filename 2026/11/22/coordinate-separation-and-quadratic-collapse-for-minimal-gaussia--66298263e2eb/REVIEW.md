# Scientific review: Gaussian multiwindow coordinate separation and collapse

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a continuous-domain, all-dimensional statement for \(d+1\) unnormalized translates of a positive tensor Gaussian on the integer time-frequency lattice. The proof fixes the Zak convention and shows the frame-operator multiplier is the sum of the \(d+1\) squared Zak moduli. The Jacobi triple product proves that the scalar Gaussian Zak transform has exactly one zero per fundamental cell and that the spatial and frequency derivatives there are nonzero and linearly independent over the real numbers. This gives the exact coordinate-cover argument, with both directions and all boundary cases. The explicit lower bound follows by isolating the unique potentially vanishing triple-product factor and applying a circle-spacing pigeonhole argument; its upper bound follows by summing Gaussian tails. For the planar collision, the common-zero fiber at the endpoint is unique, the local Hessian is positive definite, and global compactness forces the optimal lower-frame minimizer into that neighborhood, giving the exact coefficient. The standalone numerical script passed four finite classification examples, twelve collision asymptotic diagnostics and four product–derivative comparisons; these diagnostics are not relied upon as infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
The closest general existence theorems are the 2026 results of de Dios Pont–Liehr–Taylor (arXiv:2606.26052, Theorem 1.2 and Section 4) and Caragea–Pfander (arXiv:2609.06255, Theorem 1.5). They establish minimal counts/existence of *some* regular windows, including broad Gaussian-combination constructions, but do not state a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for every prescribed configuration of \(d+1\) **single tensor-Gaussian translates**, nor the explicit uniform bounds or the present planar optimal quadratic-collapse coefficient. Luef–Testorf–Wang (arXiv:2405.08415, Sections 1–2 and 4–6) treat Gaussian/Hermite systems with different lattice or window constraints. Classical one-dimensional Gaussian Zak-zero facts supply a necessary ingredient but not the multidimensional center classification or collapse. Indexed published-finding corpus searches of exact center residues, multiwindow Gaussian collapse, equivalent common-zero formulations and Gaussian theta identities produced related but non-covering single-window, discrete cyclic, different-window and two-coset results. Accessible prior records were checked by recoverable claim/title for Gaussian/multiwindow collisions; no implication covering this exact combined statement was found. Search absence is not a proof of global novelty. An unindexed older construction remains a real bibliographic risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The 2026 minimal regular multiwindow existence theorem does not itself tell a designer whether an explicit chosen collection of only translates of a single localized Gaussian will give a frame. The result replaces existential selection by a complete coordinatewise classification for all dimensions and widths, an easy-to-implement explicit family, and an a priori quantitative stability bound. Its sharp planar collapse coefficient describes a specific genuine loss of frame stability at the exact admissibility boundary. This is not a claim about general lattices, optimized global frame constants for arbitrary designs, or a new multiwindow existence threshold.

## Source and verification limits
Source material inspected: the full available texts of arXiv:2606.26052, arXiv:2609.06255, arXiv:2405.08415, and the abstract-level description of Janssen's Gaussian critical-density study; related indexed statements and available prior-record claims were compared. The single-Gaussian zero fact is independently proved from the triple product. There is no independent replication of the theorem, and earlier specialized Gaussian-translate frame designs may exist outside the inspected corpus.
