# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The monomial moments are exact gamma ratios, and the polynomial Gram operator reduces into \(m\) explicit half-line Jacobi matrices. Their coefficient convergence establishes the essential interval by compact perturbation. At \(0<c<1\), alternating-sign conjugation yields the exact quadratic-form identity and a negative Coulomb-type coefficient \(-\beta(1-c)/j+O(j^{-2})\). Disjoint sine trial vectors in a dyadic interval give \(\Omega(\sqrt N)\) independent directions below the edge by \(\Omega(N^{-1})\). The min–max principle establishes the infinite multiplicity count and the lower bound. Direct pointwise multiplier bounds handle \(c\ge1\); \(c=0\) is diagonal. The standalone exact-rational finite diagnostic checks a subset of coefficients and trial vectors, not the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Santana-Rivero and Seco, arXiv:2611.18044v1, prove general essential spectral information, nonexistence on a specified spectral locus, and eigenspace dimension bounds for quadratic Bergman symbols. They explicitly leave existence questions open. The present positive infinite-discrete-spectrum assertion for binomials in every congruence sector and the quantitative edge count are not supplied there. Monsalve-López and Seco, arXiv:2501.12298v1, settle degree one; a higher-degree split has different induced weights and needs new comparisons. Classical Jacobi oscillation theorems may imply the qualitative accumulation after explicit reduction. No older identical analytic Bergman binomial spectral/count statement was located, but the 2001 Luef–Teschl general criterion was not inspected in full; the claim is not a literature-certified novelty statement.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The existence of one-dimensional point eigenspaces and the degree-greater-than-two case are expressly named as open directions in the 2026 source. This result gives **infinitely many** discrete eigenvalues in every natural reducing sector of a whole family of weights and powers, identifies the sharp coefficient threshold and quantifies their edge proliferation. These are natural spectral invariants of the exact multiplier Gram operator, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a finite computation.

## Closest literature and limits

The earlier degree-one classification and general Jacobi/Kneser criteria remain important antecedents. The essential band and compact-perturbation technique are standard. Exact outlier locations, complete embedded point spectrum, and sharp counting asymptotics are not claimed. Independent external review was not performed.
