# Scientific review: coupled extremal degrees of random interval graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The final claim specifies the paired-endpoint model, the probability-integral normalization, the exact two-sided degree and ratio marks, and a coupled construction from three independent Poisson processes. The proof establishes the underlying corner-process convergence by rare-event factorial moments, excludes every other potential low-degree vertex using a uniform binomial Chernoff bound, replaces corner degrees by twice their rescaled distal endpoints with a quantitative conditional variance bound, and identifies universal vertices as the third corner process restricted by the two short-corner minima. The mapping boundaries have zero probability mass. This proves the joint—not merely marginal—limit, its pushforward intensity, the order statistics, and the strict dependence statement, including the exact limiting covariance obtained by polar integration. The standalone finite checker passed 11,491 assertions from six-interval and smaller exhaustive endpoint pairings plus exact endpoint identities, a deterministic intensity test, and seeded numerical sanity checks. The asymptotic result relies on the analytical proof, never on finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk
Scheinerman's 1988 random interval-graph paper supplies a Rayleigh limit for the *smallest* degree, not an inspected full side- and endpoint-ratio-marked Poisson degree spectrum. Justicz–Scheinerman–Winkler (1990) gives the exact probability that at least one universal vertex exists. The accessible 2015 Iliopoulos thesis, examined at the model and degree sections, presents those earlier individual results but not their joint low/high extreme process. A prior marked Poisson theorem on universal intervals establishes the marginal universal process and its dependence on its two endpoint gaps; this is **credited** and the present result adds the complete low-degree spectrum and joint coupling to that process. Published Poisson-extreme results for permutation graphs and projective-geodesic crossing graphs have other objects and do not imply the paired-endpoint interval result. The 2026 Sarkar article's abstract treats the number of universal intervals; this count is explicitly not claimed as a new result here. Targeted searches for equivalent degree-spectrum, order-statistic, geometric-corner, and coupled-extreme formulations did not find a dominating source. The full primary texts of the 1988, 1990, and 2026 articles were inaccessible, and a more general unlocated theorem may exist. The originality assessment is qualified rather than certified as globally unique.

## Value — PASS
The statistic is not an arbitrary slice: the ordered smallest degrees identify the least-connected vertices in a dense geometric intersection graph. Their Rayleigh minimum had been known, but the multiplicity, two-sided localization, endpoint shape marks, and all-order lower tail were not determined by that one-dimensional theorem. The universal vertices provide an equally natural opposite degree extreme. The joint construction demonstrates a nontrivial *shared boundary obstruction*: a small degree on either side lowers the conditional Poisson rate of universal vertices, yielding a strictly positive correlation between the limiting universal count and the smallest degree, with an exact closed-form covariance constant. This mechanism cannot be read from the separate marginal laws and provides a concrete, reusable model of coupled extremes.

## Scope and residual limits
The limit is for fixed bounded degree windows and a fixed number of lowest degrees, not a uniform approximation over all vertices. No finite-sample covariance limit is claimed. The test suite validates finite identities only. The complete full texts of the earliest primary papers could not be inspected; their exact extent remains an access-related priority risk, not a substitute for the proof. Independent mathematical validation is pending.
