# Crepant birational dichotomy for two isolated Nash counterexamples

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field. Consider the normal affine toric fourfolds
\[
X_{11}=\mathbb A^4_k/\mu_{11}(1,2,3,5),\qquad
X_{9}=\mathbb A^4_k/\mu_{9}(1,2,7,8),
\]
where the notation means the diagonal action of the indicated finite diagonalizable group scheme with the listed character weights. These are the two explicit normal isolated Gorenstein sources used in Chen and Zuo's nontermination construction for normalized Nash blowups: \(X_{11}\) in characteristics other than two (including the characteristic-three construction), and \(X_9\) in characteristic two.

**Theorem (crepant dichotomy).** The following statements hold over any algebraically closed base field, using the indicated quotient's toric structure.

1. \(X_9\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and terminal. Its singular closed orbit has minimal log discrepancy \(2\). It has no nonidentity crepant toric subdivision and, more strongly, **no projective crepant resolution**, toric or otherwise.
2. \(X_{11}\) is canonical but not terminal, with minimal log discrepancy \(1\) at its unique singular point. Up to isomorphism over \(X_{11}\), there is exactly one nonidentity crepant toric birational morphism from a normal toric variety, the projective star subdivision at the ray generated by
   \[
   v=\frac1{11}(1,2,3,5).
   \]
   Its source \(Y\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and terminal, and its exceptional divisor is \(\mathbb P_k(1,2,3,5)\). The singularities of \(Y\) are exactly three isolated cyclic quotient points, of respective toric types
   \[
   \frac12(1,1,1,1),\qquad \frac13(1,1,2,2),\qquad
   \frac15(1,2,3,4).
   \]
   Consequently \(X_{11}\) has no smooth **toric** crepant resolution.

The uniqueness statement in part 2 is strictly toric; it does not assert uniqueness or nonexistence of non-toric crepant birational models.

## Assumptions and scope

The four variables are affine coordinates. In characteristic dividing the group order, \(\mu_r\) denotes the diagonalizable group *scheme*, not necessarily a reduced cyclic group. The invariant ring is the toric semigroup ring for
\[
N_{r,w}=\mathbb Z^4+\frac1r(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4)\mathbb Z,
\qquad \sigma=\mathbb R_{\ge0}^4\subset N_{r,w}\otimes\mathbb R.
\]
All toric fans, discrepancy computations, and assertions about terminal toric singularities below are characteristic-independent. The claim about the absence of projective crepant resolutions for \(X_9\) also applies in characteristic two, in which its \(\mu_9\)-action is tame. Neither assertion depends on the particular Nash-chart itinerary in the motivating paper.

A crepant toric birational morphism here means a proper birational toric morphism inducing the identity on the dense torus, with equality of canonical divisors under pullback. A terminalization means a projective crepant birational morphism whose source is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and terminal. Minimal log discrepancy is taken at the closed torus orbit.

## Proof

**1. The integral height-one slice.** For both pairs \((r,w)=(11,(1,2,3,5))\) and \((9,(1,2,7,8))\), every weight is coprime to \(r\) and \(\sum_i w_i\) is divisible by \(r\). Thus the functional
\(h(u)=u_1+u_2+u_3+u_4\) lies in the dual lattice and takes value one on the four primitive rays \(e_i\) of \(\sigma\). Therefore both quotient varieties are Gorenstein. The cone is simplicial of lattice index \(r>1\), so each quotient has a singular closed orbit and is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial. Every proper face is regular: each nontrivial residue class has all four coordinates nonzero, as the weights are units modulo \(r\), so the singularity is isolated.

Every lattice point \(u\in\sigma\cap N_{r,w}\) has a unique expression
\[
u=b+\frac1r(\langle kw_1\rangle_r,\ldots,\langle kw_4\rangle_r),
\quad b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^4,\quad 0\le k<r,
\]
where \(\langle t\rangle_r\) is the residue in \(\{0,\ldots,r-1\}\). This is because subtracting the coordinatewise fractional parts of \(u\) leaves a nonnegative integral vector. Hence every nonvertex point of height one arises among the nonzero fractional representatives satisfying
\[
\operatorname{age}_w(k):=\frac1r\sum_{i=1}^4\langle kw_i\rangle_r=1.
\]
The toric discrepancy of the divisor from a primitive new ray \(u\) is \(h(u)-1\). In particular, a cone is terminal precisely when its height-one lattice slice has no lattice point besides its primitive rays; the minimal log discrepancy at the closed orbit equals the least height of an interior primitive lattice point.

**2. The order-nine source.** For \(w=(1,2,7,8)\), the residues of \((k,8k)\) and of \((2k,7k)\) each sum to nine for every \(1\le k\le8\), because all these residues are nonzero and the weights in each pair are negatives modulo nine. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{age}_{(1,2,7,8)}(k)=2\qquad(1\le k\le8).
\]
No new height-one rays exist; all nonray lattice points have height at least two. The singularity is terminal, with minimal log discrepancy two. Since \(\sigma\) is simplicial, no nontrivial toric subdivision without a new ray is possible, proving the toric assertion.

Suppose a projective crepant resolution \(f:Z\to X_9\) existed. Terminality makes every exceptional divisorial discrepancy strictly positive, so crepancy forces \(f\) to be small (there are no exceptional divisors). Since \(X_9\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and \(Z\) is smooth, a small projective birational morphism is impossible: an \(f\)-ample Cartier divisor \(H\) on \(Z\) is the strict transform of a Weil divisor \(D\) on \(X_9\); some positive multiple of \(D\) is Cartier. As there are no exceptional divisors, \(H=f^*D\) as \(\mathbb Q\)-divisors, so \(H\) has degree zero on every contracted curve, contradicting relative ampleness if a positive-dimensional fiber exists. Thus \(f\) would be finite birational, hence an isomorphism by normality, contrary to singularity. This proves the stronger nonexistence assertion.

**3. The order-eleven source.** For \(w=(1,2,3,5)\), direct residue arithmetic gives the complete age sequence as \(k=1,\ldots,10\) varies:
\[
(1,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,3).
\]
Thus the unique nonvertex height-one lattice point is \(v=(1,2,3,5)/11\). It is interior to the junior tetrahedron. This proves canonicity, nonterminality, and minimal log discrepancy one.

Any crepant toric subdivision may add only height-one rays. Without \(v\), the simplicial cone admits no nontrivial subdivision. With \(v\), the only possible fan subdivision is the star fan with its four maximal cones
\[
\sigma_i=\operatorname{Cone}(v,e_1,\ldots,\widehat{e_i},\ldots,e_4),\qquad 1\le i\le4.
\]
Indeed, \(v\) is strictly interior to the original tetrahedron and a triangulation using its unique interior lattice vertex must consist of the four tetrahedra adjoining the original facets. The star subdivision is coherent and hence projective over the affine toric base. Since \(h(v)=1\), its morphism is crepant, and every maximal cone is simplicial, making its source \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial.

To calculate the local quotients, choose the lattice basis \((v,e_2,e_3,e_4)\) for \(N_{11,w}\). Then
\(e_1=11v-2e_2-3e_3-5e_4\). The cone \(\sigma_i\) has lattice index \(w_i\), namely \(1,2,3,5\). More precisely, the relation
\[
w_i e_i=11v-\sum_{j\ne i}w_j e_j
\]
gives the local cyclic-quotient action in its four ray coordinates, with weights
\[
\frac1{w_i}\left(11,(-w_j)_{j\ne i}\right).
\]
Reducing residues yields one smooth chart and the three quotient types in the theorem. Their nontrivial age sequences are, respectively, \((2)\), \((2,2)\), and \((2,2,2,2)\). All characters are coprime to their cyclic order. Thus the three singularities are isolated and terminal, and there are no other singularities. The ray relation for the exceptional fan is
\(e_1+2e_2+3e_3+5e_4=11v\), so the exceptional toric divisor is the weighted projective threefold \(\mathbb P(1,2,3,5)\). Since the only possible crepant subdivision is this singular star fan, no smooth toric crepant resolution exists.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `checker.py` uses exact integer arithmetic and no external libraries. It enumerates all ten nontrivial order-eleven residues and all eight order-nine residues, checks their ages, calculates determinants of the four star cones in an explicit lattice basis, and checks the local weights and terminal ages for the indices \(2,3,5\). It prints `All exact integer checks passed.` on success. These exhaustive finite group-residue checks cover the complete finite age and chart datasets; they are not a substitute for the general lattice decomposition, toric discrepancy criterion, projectivity argument, or nonexistence proof, which are supplied above.

## Relationship to prior work

Chen and Zuo construct the two source quotient singularities and prove persistence of singular charts under normalized Nash iteration. Their Sections 5.1–5.3 verify the source lattices, isolatedness and Gorenstein property, but do not analyze terminality, junior rays, crepant terminalizations or absence of projective crepant resolutions. These latter statements are the contribution here; nontermination of Nash blowups is credited entirely to their paper.

Dais, Henk and Ziegler develop general junior-simplex and Hilbert-basis criteria for toric crepant resolutions. The theorem here uses those classical principles, not a new discrepancy criterion. Dais and Henk classify a different cyclic series with at least three equal weights in dimension four; neither tuple here is equivalent to that series under a permutation and multiplication by a unit modulo the group order, because all four weights of each tuple are distinct. The precise order-nine terminal obstruction, the order-eleven unique extraction, and the residual three singularity types do not appear as such in the inspected primary works. General toric machinery remains a substantial antecedent and an important originality limitation.

## Limitations

The uniqueness and no-smooth-resolution statements for the order-eleven case are **toric only**. No assertion is made about arbitrary non-toric crepant resolutions or other non-toric minimal models of \(X_{11}\). For the order-nine case the obstruction rules out **projective** crepant resolutions, but not merely proper nonprojective morphisms. The Nash iteration's failure is a result of Chen and Zuo, not reproved here. The proof concerns these two explicit sources, not every Gorenstein toric isolated singularity or every Nash counterexample.
