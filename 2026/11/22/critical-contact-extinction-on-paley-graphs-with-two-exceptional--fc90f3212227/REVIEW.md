# Scientific review: critical-contact excitation on Paley graphs

## Correctness — PASS

The all-order claim follows from a finite-period contact-count inequality that is tight because the graph degree equals twice the excitation threshold. Tightness forces the exact vector recurrence \(Au_t=\theta(u_{t-1}+u_{t+1})\). Its temporal Fourier modes must produce adjacency eigenvalues whose ratios to \(\theta\) are algebraic integers. The Paley nonprincipal eigenvalue ratios fail this test for every admissible order \(q\ge13\), separately in the square and nonsquare cases. For the exceptional orders, the allowable Fourier frequencies restrict recurrent excitation to respectively period five and period three; elementary phase constraints count two and four primitive cycles. Two independently implemented complete finite-state enumerators verify every small-order basin and sharp finite transient count, with all states checked. No infinite theorem relies on finite sampling.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification

The closest public graph half-degree result establishes balanced proper three-coloring equivalence only for *initially period-three* states, and an exact count on squared cycles. It does not exclude all non-three periodic activity in arbitrary Paley orders or provide the P(5)/P(9) basin dichotomy. Published one-contact graph results do not directly apply to a half-degree contact threshold. An existing order-thirteen finite computation already gives extinction in that one case, which is explicitly excluded from the claim of novel finite verification; the new comparison is a complete theorem across all admissible prime powers. Targeted source, alias and implication searches found no equivalent full-order statement, but an unindexed earlier spectral argument or full finite calculation remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The result gives a complete natural infinite-family classification of whether periodic excitation is possible at a critical threshold, rather than another isolated graph enumeration. The exactly two exceptional graph orders distinguish a robust extinction regime from isolated or attracting wave families. The arithmetic transition at square order nine, where a rational adjacency eigenvalue ratio permits precisely twelve period-three states, connects finite-field graph spectra to recurrent dynamical patterns. Its exact basins and sharp entry cutoffs provide independently reproducible small-model benchmarks.

## Closest sources and limitations

Durrett–Griffeath (1993) motivates contact-threshold excitable media; Gravner–Lyu–Sivakoff (2016/2018) and Doba (2018) analyze the one-contact graph model; the public half-degree rigidity result concerns period-three states on general regular graphs and squared cycles. The present theorem does not bound the settling time uniformly for Paley orders at least thirteen, does not classify other thresholds and does not assert independent verification. Historical-priority uncertainty remains.
