# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The argument states the original and thinned circular-gap objects and the independent deletion mechanism explicitly. The exact finite identity follows by the Dirichlet spacing law and the Bernoulli mark pattern. For each fixed deletion-run length, mixed factorial moments yield independent marked Poisson exceedances. Longer runs are controlled uniformly by the exact exponential/geometric mixture \(q e^{-qu}\), not by truncating unexamined probabilities. The iid-exponential representation transfers the result to uniform circle spacings because \((n/S_n-1)\log n\to0\). The stability probability is the probability that the largest point of two independent tail-intensity processes comes from the unchanged process. The high-noise conclusion uses the conditional uniformity of survivors and the classical Gumbel tightness. Finite checks passed, but no simulation is treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The prior single-sample circular longest-gap theorem (Schlemm, arXiv:1405.6114), fixed-\(k\) spacing extrema (Mijatović–Vysotsky, arXiv:1508.00944), and general Poisson splitting characterization (Nehring, arXiv:1407.1700) do not state the joint noise-coupled Gumbel distribution or the exact asymptotic stability probability \(e^{-\tau}\). Searches using thinning, longest-gap, correlated maxima, deletion-noise and equivalent geometric/renewal language did not reveal a directly covering theorem. This is a best-knowledge comparison, not a proof of unique historical priority; a broader marked-extreme-process theorem is a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The longest gap measures worst-case uncovered arc length, a natural quantity in uniform coverage and sample-spacing diagnostics. The result distinguishes perturbations with a vanishing deleted fraction that nevertheless alter the extreme geometry, quantifies the exact chance of retaining the original longest gap, and provides a simultaneous rather than merely marginal calibration. The finite block-count identity also makes the source of the transition explicit.

## Limits and sources

The theorem is for uniform circular points and independent deletion, with no claimed error rate or adversarial/general-density extension.
