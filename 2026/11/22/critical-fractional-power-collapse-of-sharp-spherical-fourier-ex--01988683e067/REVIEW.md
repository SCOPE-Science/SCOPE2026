## Correctness — PASS
The theorem fixes the Fourier normalization, the surface measure, the full \(d\ge3\) domain, and the critical exponent. Fixed-order Hankel asymptotics show that both divergent moments share the same residue \(B_d\), while cancellation of two \(\pi\)-periodic phase profiles with equal mean gives a uniform \(O(1/R)\) tail for their difference at and just above the critical exponent. This proves a finite limit, rather than illegally subtracting two divergent integrals. The strict positivity is supplied by the positive-cell theorem of Xu (Theorem 3.9, flux and reflected-Wronskian proof), whose hypotheses remain valid at \(q=q_c>2\). The fractional power then follows from an explicit integral form of the mean-value identity. Only the supplementary finite numerical probes were machine-tested; they are not infinite certificates.
