For the two-sample OVL-\(q\) test, cut counts near the sample size create a sharp null transition: at \(q_n=\lfloor n+t\sqrt{n/2}\rfloor\), the scaled overlap \(\sqrt n\,\rho_{q_n,n,n}\) converges to \((Z-t)_+/(2\sqrt2)\) and its exact zero probability approaches \(\Phi(t)\). A run-boundary argument and isolated singleton patterns give the exact near-saturation cost with high probability, explaining this unexpected atom-plus-continuum law.
