For a negatively sloped affine integer iteration with unbiased rounding, tuning the fixed point to \(\theta_\varepsilon=1/2+c\varepsilon\) while the contraction factor is \(1-\varepsilon\) yields an explicit, finite-support stationary birth-death law rather than the previously known fixed-phase Gaussian limit. Its support expands by two sites at every sharp detuning wall \(|c|=1/4,3/4,5/4,\ldots\), and the first nontrivial limiting law has weights \(1:6:6:1\).
