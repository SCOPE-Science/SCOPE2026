# Scientific assessment: critical sign reversals in batch-renewal gaps

## Correctness — PASS

The ordinary-origin renewal representation exactly identifies a positive individual gap with the event that the preceding individual count is a batch boundary. For two distinct positive gaps the associated batch waits are independent; the exact joint-boundary probability is the product of two renewal masses. Consequently the covariance identity and the clock-independence of all signs are exact, with no asymptotic approximation. The renewal generating function factors into a constant equilibrium term plus a complex-root transient, yielding the displayed closed-form covariance. At equal one/three probabilities the conjugate-root angle is an irrational multiple of \(\pi\), proved by an algebraic-integer obstruction, so the transient changes sign infinitely often. A compactness argument supplies uniform bounded-gap visits to both arcs under nearby parameters and proves the logarithmic last-reversal law. All parameter ranges, integer boundaries and the separate maximum-two-burst formula are explicit. The standalone finite checker passed 1,310 assertions, but finite checks are not used as proofs of infinite phenomena.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority uncertainty

The closest primary work is Tathe and Ghosh, *Time-changed Compound Poisson Process* (https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16676v1), Sections 3 and 6.4, which gives the general model and first-passage marginal representations, not ordinary-origin *joint* adjacent individual-gap covariance or transient sign phases. Fretwell and Kouvatsos, *Modelling LRD and SRD Traffic with the Batch Renewal Process* (https://pws.npru.ac.th/sartthong/data/files/Network_Performance_Engineering__A_Handbook.pdf), Section 3, derive generic **stationary** interval-covariance generating functions; the eventual sign and general batch covariance principle are therefore not claimed new. The present result determines the nonstationary individual-index transient, its oscillatory critical point, the exact logarithmic cutoff and the minimal support where this happens. Meerschaert, Nane and Vellaisamy, *The fractional Poisson process and the inverse stable subordinator* (https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.5051), already give the general inverse-clock renewal representation. Available research-record searches and claim comparisons did not identify a theorem implying the full transient phase classification. A previously unlocated treatment of elementary renewal mass oscillations could overlap; this remains a genuine priority risk, not a proof of uniqueness. The batch-renewal handbook chapter was read as parsed text, while its PDF page images were not available.

## Value — PASS

The question is motivated by clustered-event data: arrival-gap correlations can vary with the number of customers observed since startup, rather than only with physical lag or an equilibrated observation point. The exact boundary where stationary covariance cancels nevertheless supports infinitely many nonstationary sign reversals; near cancellation the memory of startup persists for an explicitly logarithmic number of individual arrivals. Clock universality separates this effect from waiting-time distribution, including non-exponential inverse-subordinator clocks. The maximum-burst-two obstruction shows that three-customer bursts are the first natural setting supporting the phenomenon. These structural statements go beyond recomputing a stationary covariance, but do not classify arbitrary burst laws.
