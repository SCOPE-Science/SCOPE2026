# Scientific review: polygonal boundary-chord mean stability

## Correctness — PASS

The two asserted asymptotic formulas were reconstructed from independent geometry and angular integration. The polygon radial function and arclength density are exact: on an angular half-gap \(\alpha\), they are \(\cos\alpha\sec t\) and \(\cos\alpha\sec^2t\). Their side integrals give the cubic gap coefficient \(1/(24\pi^3)\) after perimeter normalization; the singular difference quotient is uniformly integrable with a \(\delta^4\log(1/\delta)\) bound. In the regular case, the chord Taylor expansion isolates the only logarithmically enhanced quadratic term. A periodic mean-square-difference identity shows that its logarithmic coefficient is the variance \(\alpha^4/45\) divided by \(\pi\), leading to \(-\pi^2/90\) after normalizing by perimeter. Fourier decay makes the unperturbed-kernel angular-density covariance only \(O(\alpha^6)\), so it cannot alter that coefficient. The code checks direct side-pair integrals, not the asymptotic kernel expansion, and all its finite numerical assertions passed. Remaining scientific limitations: no exact finite-size optimizer is claimed, and the general error is not known to be sharp.

Shen's 7 November 2026 paper proves the sharp lower bound and does not cover upper-end polygonal saturation. The MathBench finite-\(n\) regular-polygon identity is much closer: it provides exact mean values for the regular subclass and may permit the logarithmic term to be derived with further asymptotic analysis; it does not cover nonuniform angular partitions. Indexed near-diameter tail results concern probabilities under a rare-event scaling, not expectations over the entire chord distribution. An accessible earlier smooth local second-variation formula applies to sufficiently small \(C^2\) curvature deformations, while polygons have curvature atoms and angular-derivative jumps: its hypotheses do not include the present family or guarantee uniform remainders. No searched source states or immediately implies the principal mesh-uniform cubic-gap theorem. Residual risk remains from unindexed literature and uninspected full text of one indexed near-diameter result. This is a priority assessment, not a proof of historical uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sharp Tokmachev inequality gives an endpoint but not a quantitative rate for arbitrary polygonal approximations of its unique optimizer. The new coefficient is shape-sensitive: it is a convex cubic energy of all angular spacings, and is uniform over severely nonuniform meshes, so it gives a concrete leading-order quality measure for geometric discretization. Its strict Jensen minimizer provides a precise design criterion in the fine-mesh regime without overclaiming exact finite-size optimization. The regular \(n\)-gon exhibits an explicit logarithmic boundary interaction at order \(n^{-4}\log n\), distinguishing the singular Euclidean chord kernel from an ordinary even-power expansion. Those are motivated geometric invariants rather than arbitrary samples or routine table recomputations.
