# Cubic fiber collapse and exact Fourier nullity on reciprocal-power curves

## Finding
Let \(p\) be an odd prime such that
\[
p\equiv4\text{ or }7\pmod9,\qquad 2^{(p-1)/3}\equiv1\pmod p.
\]
Set \(m=p-1\) and
\[
k=\begin{cases}m/3,&p\equiv4\pmod9,\\2m/3,&p\equiv7\pmod9,\end{cases}\qquad
 d\equiv-k-1\pmod m,\quad 1\le d<m.
\]
On the reciprocal-power curve \(C=\{(x,x^{-d}):x\in\mathbb F_p^\times\}\), consider the unnormalized square sampling matrix for the ambient additive Fourier transform,
\[
M_{x,y}=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{p}\bigl(xy+x^{-d}y^{-d}\bigr)\right),\qquad x,y\in\mathbb F_p^\times.
\]
Let \(t=\operatorname{ord}_p(-2)\) be the multiplicative order of \(-2\) modulo \(p\). Then \(t\mid m/3\), and the **exact complex nullity and rank** are
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{nullity}_{\mathbb C} M=\frac{2m}{3t},\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C} M=m-\frac{2m}{3t}.}
\]
More precisely, choose any primitive cube root \(\omega\in\mathbb F_p\), and let \(\widehat{\mathbb F_p^\times}\) denote the group of complex multiplicative characters. The right kernel is exactly
\[
\ker M=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
\bigl\{(\chi(y))_{y\in\mathbb F_p^\times}:\chi(-2)=1,\ \chi(\omega)\ne1\bigr\}.
\]
The conditions are nonvacuous. For \(p=31\), the exponent is \(d=19\) and the nullity is \(2\); for \(p=43\), the exponent is \(d=13\) and the nullity is \(4\).

## Assumptions and scope
All field operations, inverses, exponents and multiplicative orders are taken in \(\mathbb F_p\). The first coordinate parametrizes the curve injectively. The number of rows and columns is \(p-1\). The roots of unity in the matrix are complex, and the rank concerns complex-linear recovery; normalization of the ambient Fourier transform does not change it. Only primes satisfying the stated arithmetic conditions are covered. The existence of infinitely many such primes is not asserted.

## Proof
**1. Multiplicative Fourier reduction.** Write \(\zeta_p=e^{2\pi i/p}\). Since \(-d\equiv k+1\pmod m\), the matrix is \(M_{x,y}=\zeta_p^{f(xy)}\) for
\[
f(u)=u(1+u^k),\qquad u\in\mathbb F_p^\times.
\]
For a multiplicative character \(\chi\), put
\[
T_\chi=\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_p^\times}\chi(u)\zeta_p^{f(u)}.
\]
Then \(M(\chi(y))_y=(\chi(x)^{-1}T_\chi)_x\). As all multiplicative characters form a basis of \(\mathbb C^{p-1}\), the nullity is exactly the number of characters with \(T_\chi=0\), and those characters give a basis of the right kernel.

**2. Three-to-one phase fibers.** The congruence condition on \(p\) makes \(k\) even, \(k\equiv1\pmod3\), and \(\gcd(k,m)=m/3\). Thus
\[
H=\{h\in\mathbb F_p^\times:h^k=1\}
\]
is the index-three subgroup of size \(m/3\); it contains \(-1\) and \(2\). The primitive cube root \(\omega\) satisfies \(\omega^k=\omega\), and its three multiplicative cosets classify the possible values of \(u^k\). On these cosets the phase map multiplies \(u\) respectively by \(2\), \(-\omega^2\), and \(-\omega\). Each factor carries its coset bijectively onto \(H\). In particular, \(f(\mathbb F_p^\times)=H\), and for every \(h\in H\) the complete inverse fiber is exactly
\[
f^{-1}(h)=\{h/2,-\omega h,-\omega^2h\}.
\]
These three elements are distinct, since they lie in distinct cosets of \(H\).

**3. Character-sum factorization and nonvanishing of the common factor.** Summing each fiber gives
\[
T_\chi=\chi(2)^{-1}G_H(\chi)
\bigl(1+\chi(-2)[\chi(\omega)+\chi(\omega^2)]\bigr),
\quad G_H(\chi)=\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h)\zeta_p^h.
\]
Every \(G_H(\chi)\) is nonzero. Indeed, all its coefficients lie in the cyclotomic field \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\). The polynomial \(P(X)=\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h)X^{\widetilde h}\in K[X]\), using integer representatives \(1\le\widetilde h\le p-1\), has degree at most \(p-1\), constant coefficient zero, and nonzero coefficient at each \(h\in H\). Since \(\gcd(p,m)=1\), the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta_p\) over \(K\) remains \(\Phi_p(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}\). If \(P(\zeta_p)=0\), then \(P\) would be a constant multiple of \(\Phi_p\), and its zero constant coefficient would force \(P=0\), a contradiction.

**4. Exact nullity count.** If \(\chi(\omega)=1\), the final bracket in Step 3 equals \(1+2\chi(-2)\), which cannot vanish because \(|\chi(-2)|=1\). Otherwise the nontrivial cube-root values satisfy \(\chi(\omega)+\chi(\omega^2)=-1\), and the bracket equals \(1-\chi(-2)\). Therefore
\[
T_\chi=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \chi(-2)=1\text{ and }\chi(\omega)\ne1.
\]
The element \(-2\) lies in \(H\), hence \(t=\operatorname{ord}_p(-2)\mid m/3\); as \(3\nmid m/3\), also \(3\nmid t\). The annihilator of \(-2\) in the character group has \(m/t\) members. Of these, exactly \(m/(3t)\) are also trivial on \(\omega\), since the subgroup generated by \(-2\) and \(\omega\) has order \(3t\). Subtraction leaves \(2m/(3t)\) vanishing modes. Step 1 supplies their explicit kernel vectors and the claimed rank.

## Relationship to prior work
Multiplicative character sums and discrete Fourier transforms on finite fields are established subjects. Xi's work (2021 preprint, 2022 journal) studies moments and limiting distributions of multiplicative analogues of Kloosterman sums, not the square matrix on the present reciprocal-power curve. Ayad, Belghaba and Kihel (2012 preprint) study when binomial polynomials permute a finite field; they do not determine these Fourier sampling nullspaces. The classic restricted Fourier viewpoint is developed by Mockenhaupt and Tao, while the cyclotomic coefficient argument uses standard prime-cyclotomic field disjointness. Previously examined prime-field reciprocal-hyperbola rank results concern the exponent \(d=1\), with at most a quadratic-character obstruction, while quadratic-parabola sampling concerns a different phase and different index set; neither statement implies the present cubic-coset nullity formula. The precise arithmetic family and full character-mode count are the assessed contribution.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to prime fields and this cubic-resonant family of reciprocal powers. It does not classify all exponents, all frequency curves, higher prime-power rings, complex singular-value conditioning, or the infinitude of qualifying primes. Historical originality has been assessed against selected related work, not exhaustively certified; independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
