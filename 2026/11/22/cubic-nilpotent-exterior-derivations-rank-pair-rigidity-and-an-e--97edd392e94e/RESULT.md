# Cubic-nilpotent exterior derivations: rank-pair rigidity and an exact finite-field census

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an arbitrary field of characteristic \(3\), let \(V\) be an \(n\)-dimensional vector space, \(n\geq1\), and let \(E=\bigwedge_k V\). Every **exterior-degree-preserving ordinary derivation** \(D:E\to E\) is uniquely induced by its restriction \(M=D|_V\in\operatorname{End}_k(V)\). Such a derivation satisfies \(D^3=0\) if and only if \(M^3=0\). Suppose the Jordan partition of \(M\) is \((1^a,2^b,3^c)\), where \(a,b,c\geq0\) and \(a+2b+3c=n\). Define the nonnegative integers

\[
 A=2^{a+c}\frac{3^b+1}{2},\qquad
 B=2^{a+c}\frac{3^b-1}{2},\qquad
 C=\frac{2^n-A-2B}{3}.
\]

Then **the entire Jordan partition of the induced operator on the full exterior algebra** is

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Jord}(D)=(1^A,2^B,3^C).}
\]

In particular, letting \(r_1=\operatorname{rank}D\) and \(r_2=\operatorname{rank}D^2\), one has \(r_1=B+2C\) and \(r_2=C\). Conversely, **the ordered pair \((r_1,r_2)\) and \(n\) recover the complete generator Jordan partition uniquely**: first compute

\[
 C=r_2,\quad B=r_1-2r_2,\quad A=2^n-2r_1+r_2;
 \qquad 2^{a+c}=A-B,\quad 3^b=\frac{A+B}{A-B},
\]

and then, writing \(s=\log_2(A-B)=a+c\), recover \(c=(n-s-2b)/2\) and \(a=s-c\). Thus distinct generator Jordan partitions cannot share an induced rank pair.

Over \(k=\mathbb F_q\), \(q=3^e\), set \(m_1=a,m_2=b,m_3=c\) and

\[
 d=(a+b+c)^2+(b+c)^2+c^2,\qquad
 G_n(q)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(q^n-q^i).
\]

There are precisely

\[
 \boxed{F_{a,b,c}(q)=\frac{G_n(q)}{q^d\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{3}\prod_{j=1}^{m_s}(1-q^{-j})}}
\]

cubic-nilpotent generator maps of this Jordan type, and hence precisely that many degree-preserving cubic-zero derivations with the corresponding \((r_1,r_2)\). The complete **joint rank polynomial** is

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal J_{n,q}(X,Y)=\sum_{a+2b+3c=n}\!F_{a,b,c}(q) X^{B+2C}Y^C.}
\]

Each admissible pair occurs; no other pairs occur. Each similarity class of generator maps produces exactly one orbit under the graded algebra automorphisms induced by \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\).

For example, over \(\mathbb F_3\) with \(n=4\), the four pairs \((\operatorname{rank}D,\operatorname{rank}D^2)\) are \((0,0),(4,0),(6,1),(8,4)\), with respective counts \(1,1040,6240,74880\), totaling \(82161\) cubic-zero homogeneous derivations.

## Assumptions and scope

The exterior algebra has dimension \(2^n\), multiplication is the usual alternating wedge product, and \(D\) obeys the **ordinary** Leibniz identity \(D(uv)=D(u)v+uD(v)\), not the graded (Koszul-sign) differential rule. Exterior-degree preserving means \(D(\bigwedge^jV)\subseteq\bigwedge^jV\) for each \(j\). In particular, constant and higher-degree terms are excluded from the images of generators. All fields in the structural theorem have characteristic exactly \(3\); the finite-field census applies to all \(q=3^e\), without restrictions on \(e\).

There is no classification claim for arbitrary (nonhomogeneous) derivations, exterior multiplication operators, or the multiplicative group action of \(I+M\) on \(E\). The operator here is the additive Lie-algebra action induced by \(M\) on each exterior power; this distinction matters in modular characteristic. A Jordan symbol \((1^A,2^B,3^C)\) counts blocks of the indicated lengths, rather than entries of a vector-space grading.

## Proof

**1. Restriction to the generators and the characteristic-three power.** A linear operator \(M:V\to V\) extends to an ordinary derivation of the tensor algebra by Leibniz. It preserves the exterior relations because differentiation of \(v\wedge v\) gives \(Mv\wedge v+v\wedge Mv=0\), and differentiation of an anticommutator is another anticommutator. It therefore descends to a well-defined derivation on \(\bigwedge V\); uniqueness follows because \(E\) is generated by \(V\). Conversely, a degree-preserving derivation has \(D(V)\subseteq V\). In characteristic three, the triple composition of an ordinary derivation is again a derivation: the binomial coefficients \(\binom31\) and \(\binom32\) vanish. Therefore \(D^3\) is the derivation induced by \(M^3\), and \(D^3=0\iff M^3=0\), as claimed. A nilpotent map with cube zero has a Jordan form consisting only of sizes one, two and three over its base field, without requiring algebraic closure.

**2. Exterior algebras of the elementary generator blocks.** View \(D\) as an action of the one-dimensional restricted Lie algebra with enveloping algebra \(k[t]/(t^3)\), where \(t\) acts as \(D\). Write \(J_s=k[t]/(t^s)\) for the \(s\)-dimensional indecomposable module (\(s=1,2,3\)). Direct exterior-basis computation yields

\[
 \bigwedge J_1\cong2J_1,\qquad
 \bigwedge J_2\cong 2J_1\oplus J_2,\qquad
 \bigwedge J_3\cong 2J_1\oplus2J_3.
\]

For \(J_2\), its degrees zero and two are fixed and degree one is \(J_2\). For \(J_3\), degrees zero and three are fixed, degree one is \(J_3\), and on the basis \(e_1\wedge e_2,e_1\wedge e_3,e_2\wedge e_3\) of degree two, \(D\) forms a chain of length three. The exterior algebra of a direct sum is the graded tensor product. Since \(D\) has degree zero, its tensor action is the **ordinary Kronecker sum** \(D\otimes I+I\otimes D\), with no extra Koszul sign.

**3. Tensor products in characteristic three.** The following identities hold as \(k[t]/(t^3)\)-modules:

\[
 J_1\otimes J_s=J_s,\qquad
 J_2\otimes J_2=J_1\oplus J_3,\qquad
 J_3\otimes J_s=sJ_3\quad(s=1,2,3).
\]

For the second identity, in \(k[x,y]/(x^2,y^2)\), multiplication by \(x+y\) has square \(2xy\neq0\) and rank two, giving one three-chain and one one-chain. For the third, the module on \(J_3\otimes J_s\) is \(k[x,y]/(x^3,y^s)\) under multiplication by \(t=x+y\). Because \(\operatorname{char}k=3\) and \(s\le3\), the change of variables \(x=t-y\) gives \(k[t,y]/(t^3,y^s)\), a free \(k[t]/(t^3)\)-module of rank \(s\). This proves all tensor identities without a characteristic-zero semisimplicity assumption.

**4. Extracting the complete Jordan spectrum.** Multiplying the representations from Step 2 in the tensor-product ring gives

\[
 [E]=(2J_1)^a(2J_1+J_2)^b(2J_1+2J_3)^c.
\]

The span of \([J_3]\) is an ideal by Step 3. In the quotient of this ring by that ideal, put \(u=[J_2]\); then \(u^2=1\), so

\[
 [E]\equiv 2^{a+c}(2+u)^b\pmod{[J_3]}.
\]

Taking even and odd powers of \(u\), the coefficients of \(J_1\) and \(J_2\) are respectively \(2^{a+c}(3^b+1)/2\) and \(2^{a+c}(3^b-1)/2\). The quotient here is an additive quotient of the Green ring by the projective-module ideal; because Jordan indecomposables form a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis, it preserves the **actual** multiplicities of \(J_1\) and \(J_2\), not just a dimension congruence. The total dimension is \(2^n\), so the remaining \(J_3\)-multiplicity is \(C=(2^n-A-2B)/3\), necessarily a nonnegative integer. This establishes the partition and rank formulas.

**5. Uniqueness of the inverse rank map.** For any cube-zero operator on a \(2^n\)-dimensional space, its two power ranks determine its Jordan multiplicities as \(C=r_2\), \(B=r_1-2r_2\), \(A=2^n-2r_1+r_2\). The formulas from Step 4 give \(A-B=2^{a+c}\) and \((A+B)/(A-B)=3^b\). Since these are integer powers of two and three, they uniquely determine \(s=a+c\) and \(b\). Then \(n=s+2b+2c\), so \(c=(n-s-2b)/2\) and \(a=s-c\). This reconstruction is purely arithmetic, remains valid for all \(n\), and proves distinct generator Jordan types yield distinct rank pairs.

**6. Finite-field orbit and counting formula.** Matrices \(M\) with fixed nilpotent Jordan partition form a single \(\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)\)-conjugacy class. The centralizer algebra is \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_q[t]}(J_1^a\oplus J_2^b\oplus J_3^c)\), whose dimension is

\[
 \sum_{i,j=1}^3m_i m_j\min(i,j)=(a+b+c)^2+(b+c)^2+c^2=d.
\]

Its semisimple quotient is \(M_a(\mathbb F_q)\times M_b(\mathbb F_q)\times M_c(\mathbb F_q)\); its Jacobson radical has dimension \(d-a^2-b^2-c^2\). Thus its unit group has size \(q^d\prod_{s=1}^3\prod_{j=1}^{m_s}(1-q^{-j})\). Orbit-stabilizer yields \(F_{a,b,c}(q)\). The correspondence \(M\mapsto D\) is bijective and equivariant under the natural \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\)-action, and Step 5 makes the rank-pair strata disjoint, proving the polynomial and exact spectrum.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify_exterior3.py` uses only Python's standard library. It builds \(D\) directly from the signed exterior Leibniz formula on wedge-monomial bitmasks, computes ranks of \(D,D^2,D^3\) by modular Gaussian elimination, and compares with the proposed entire Jordan partition. It directly checks \(D^3=D_{M^3}\) for all \(3^{n^2}\) matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\) for \(n=1,2,3\), including every cube-zero case (one, nine, and seven hundred twenty-nine, respectively), and verifies forty canonical partitions over \(1\le n\le8\). Its terminal marker is `EXTERIOR_CUBIC_JORDAN_VERIFIED`. The small-field computation is a reproducible check of the **original** identities and does not replace the arbitrary-dimension proof.

For \(n=4\) and \(q=3\), evaluating the proven centralizer formula gives the four rank-pair multiplicities \(1,1040,6240,74880\), summing to \(82161\).

## Relationship to prior work

Korhonen [1] determines Jordan forms on the second exterior and symmetric powers, principally in characteristic two, for nilpotent and unipotent actions. The present statement concerns the **whole exterior algebra**, the additive action of a cube-zero derivation in characteristic three, an inverse theorem from two global ranks, and a complete finite-field distribution. A previous published characteristic-two calculation for **multiplication by a homogeneous bivector** [5] likewise addresses an even-degree multiplication operator, not the degree-preserving derivation here.

Briggs and Rubio y Degrassi [2] study restricted Lie structure of integrable derivations, providing contextual support for characteristic-power phenomena, but do not assert the exterior-algebra Jordan spectrum or rank-pair reconstruction. Armstrong and Sigg [3] study exterior differentials in nilpotent Lie cohomology in characteristic zero, another distinguished setting. Norman [4] establishes powerful modular Jordan algorithms for Kronecker sums of nilpotent Jordan blocks; those are **prior work** relevant to the tensor identity in Step 3, not a result about the full exterior functor, the inverse rank theorem, or this exact finite-field census. The proof explicitly checks the necessary tiny tensor factors rather than claiming a new general Kronecker-sum theory. The previous exterior-multiplication and linear-operator results [5–7] are not subsumed as new claims. Historical publication priority is not certified; a broader representation-theoretic theorem or an uninspected older full text could imply this assembled statement.

## Limitations

The result classifies **homogeneous degree-zero derivations with \(D^3=0\)** only. A nonhomogeneous derivation may have scalar, higher exterior-degree or other terms on generators and is not covered. Fields of characteristic other than three, higher nilpotence exponents, graded-odd differentials, and group actions \(\bigwedge(I+M)\) are not covered. The full nilpotent Kronecker-sum literature was not exhaustively inspected; general tensor product techniques may imply parts of the proof. Exact counts are labeled derivations, not counts of conjugacy classes beyond the specified graded \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\)-action. Finite computational verification is not an infinite proof, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
