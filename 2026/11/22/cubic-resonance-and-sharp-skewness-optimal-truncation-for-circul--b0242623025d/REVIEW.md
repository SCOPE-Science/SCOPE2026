# Review: cubic resonances and circular Sobolev cutoff skewness

## Correctness — PASS
The independent uniform-angle model and finite positive-integer frequency support are explicit. Haar-character integration removes every three-edge pattern with a degree-one vertex, leaving one edge repeated three times (the additive-frequency resonance) and a sampled triangle (three equal frequencies). Their exact multiplicities yield the stated all-weight third central moment. A finite exact-character checker recomputed a broad grid of mixed moments and nonlinear cutoff cases with 13,229 assertions passed. The proof, not finite enumeration, covers arbitrary positive sample sizes and cutoffs. The strict minimizer follows from an exact sign comparison of consecutive cutoffs and the absence of two consecutive positive integer squares.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority uncertainty

Jammalamadaka, Jiménez-Gamero and Meintanis (2019), DOI 10.2436/20.8080.02.90, investigate related trigonometric-moment tests under composite circular nulls. Fernández-de-Marcos and García-Portugués (arXiv:2112.01808) investigate stabilizations of goodness-of-fit distributions. The inspected material and indexed record searches did not locate the claimed exact all-weight cubic moment, its additive resonance and its strict finite-integer minimum. Generic character orthogonality, kernel diagrams and asymptotic normality are prior methods, not the new claim. Earlier equivalent identities may remain uncatalogued; priority is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS
The harmonic count is a real calibration decision for the cited Sobolev goodness-of-fit test. Exact skewness makes third-moment behavior computable in finite samples and identifies a unique natural cutoff of order sample size, rather than leaving calibration to second moments or asymptotics. The formula also identifies the qualitative cubic scale change near the square of sample size, which is not visible from diagonal variance alone.

## Limitations and source-inspection scope
The optimal cutoff minimizes only standardized third moment; no optimal power, Gaussian distance, tail size or confidence calibration is claimed. The result assumes an exact iid uniform circular null and finitely many nonnegative weights. The 2020 source's cited definition, growing-frequency theorem and appendix were examined, with selective sections of the 2019 and 2021 comparison texts. Complete historical priority and independent mathematical validation remain open.
