# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact identity \([C_t,C_s]=(t-s)B R(SB-BS)R\), with \(R=[(I-tS)(I-sS)]^{-1}\), is verified by resolvent algebra and exact rational finite sections. The singular-value coefficient follows from the proved weighted analytic Toeplitz counting lemma and an explicit \(O(n^{-4})\) singular-value error, not from finite data. The lemma's polynomial case is justified by block localization, finite-section singular-value equidistribution, boundary-rank control and tail operator-norm bounds; uniform polynomial approximation extends it to the rational symbol. The positivity of the coefficient, Schatten transition, and logarithmic limit follow from the resulting sharp asymptotic. Quasinilpotence follows from triangularity and compactness; injectivity follows from the factored operators. No infinite-rank assertion is based only on a finite test.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The relevant full-text primary sources [1] and [2] treat compactness, one-operator spectrum and dynamics of generalized Cesàro operators, not their cross-parameter commutators or ordered singular-value laws. The 2024/2025 Rhaly source [3] studies different spectral and invariant-subspace questions, while the 2025 Rhaly Schatten work [4] is about row-constant matrices and a different exponent range. The closest published diagonal-commutator result [5] includes the special operator \([C_t,C_0]\) only in a Hilbert–Schmidt norm/cusp theorem: it does not supply the \(n^{-3}\) coefficient or the nonzero cross-parameter calculation. Published self-commutator and shift-commutator laws involve different pairs of operators and do not imply the claimed formula. Historical symbolic-calculus and specialized operator-ideal coverage remain possible; these are priority risks, not evidence of matching coverage.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The family \(C_t\) is a standard, noncommuting parameter deformation of discrete averaging. It is mathematically natural to ask how much smoother its order-defect is than either averaging map. The theorem gives the exact all-rank asymptotic, optimal low-rank error scale, sharp quasi-Schatten threshold, and normalized critical logarithmic trace. In particular, each \(C_t\) has \(n^{-1}\)-order singular values, while the cross-parameter commutator has a previously nontrivial cubic law with an explicit coefficient. This distinguishes order dependence of two sequential smoothings from one-map compactness or Hilbert–Schmidt energy, and the particular coefficient is needed for sharp approximation estimates.
