# Cubic square-root fibers and the first square-image obstruction in finite Nottingham quotients

## Finding
For every finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\) of characteristic two, where \(q=2^e\) and \(e\ge1\), let \(G_N(k)\) be the group of formal substitutions \(f(t)=t+\sum_{j=2}^{N}a_jt^j\) modulo \(t^{N+1}\), with composition as multiplication. In \(G_5(k)\), let
\[
H=\{t+A t^4+B t^5:A,B\in k\}.
\]
Then \(H\) is a central elementary abelian subgroup of order \(q^2\), and the subgroup generated by all squares in \(G_5(k)\) is exactly \(H\). Nonetheless, not every element of \(H\) is a square.

Precisely, the numbers of targets \(g\in H\) with each prescribed number \(r(g)=|\{f\in G_5(k):f\circ f=g\}|\) of square roots are

| Number of square roots \(r(g)\) | Number of targets |
|---|---:|
| \(0\) | \((q^2-1)/3\) |
| \(q^2\) | \((q-1)(q+2)/2\) |
| \(3q^2\) | \((q-1)(q-2)/6\) |
| \(q^3\) | \(1\) |

These are distinct fiber sizes for every \(q\ge2\), including the zero fiber. The complete power-image cardinality is
\[
\bigl|\{f\circ f:f\in G_5(k)\}\bigr|=\frac{2q^2+1}{3}.
\]
A target \(t+A t^4+B t^5\) with \(A\ne0\) is a square if and only if the separable cubic \(U^3+BU+A^2\) has a root in \(k\); its number of square roots is \(q^2\) times the number of roots of that cubic. All targets with \(A=0\) are squares. This is the **first truncated Nottingham quotient in characteristic two** in which the square map fails to be onto the subgroup generated by squares: for \(N\le4\), it is onto that subgroup, whereas for \(N=5\) the fraction attained is \((2q^2+1)/(3q^2)\), tending to \(2/3\) as \(q\) grows.

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient field is a finite field of characteristic exactly two; no assumption that \(q\) is prime is made. The group \(G_N(k)\) has precisely the coefficients of degrees \(2\) through \(N\), and equality and composition are always modulo \(t^{N+1}\). The statement concerns square roots *inside this finite group*, not square roots in the full formal-power-series group. Fiber sizes count labeled substitutions, not conjugacy classes. The term "square-generated subgroup" denotes the subgroup generated by the set of squares; it is not presumed identical to that set.

## Proof
**Step 1: determine every square.** Write
\[
f(t)=t+at^2+bt^3+ct^4+dt^5.
\]
In characteristic two the following truncated powers hold:
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(t)^2&\equiv t^2+a^2t^4,\\
f(t)^3&\equiv t^3+at^4+(b+a^2)t^5,\\
f(t)^4&\equiv t^4,\qquad f(t)^5\equiv t^5\pmod{t^6}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting into \(f\circ f=f+a f^2+b f^3+c f^4+d f^5\) and collecting coefficients yields
\[
f\circ f\equiv t+a(a^2+b)t^4+b(a^2+b)t^5\pmod{t^6}.
\]
Thus every square lies in \(H\), and the coefficients \(c,d\) are arbitrary and invisible to the square.

**Step 2: the square-generated subgroup.** Members of \(H\) compose by adding their pairs \((A,B)\), because all cross terms have degree beyond five. They commute with every element of \(G_5(k)\) (this also follows by expanding compositions with an arbitrary \(f\)); hence \(H\) is central and elementary abelian. For any \(B\in k\), taking \(a=0\) and \(b=\sqrt B\) realizes the square \((0,B)\). For any \(A\in k\), taking \(a=1\), \(b=A+1\) realizes \((A,A(A+1))\); composing it with a previously realized square \((0,A(A+1))\) produces \((A,0)\). Therefore the squares generate all of \(H\).

**Step 3: prescribed fibers.** Put \(u=a^2+b\). The square coefficient equations are \(A=au\), \(B=bu\). When \((A,B)=(0,0)\), necessarily \(u=0\): if \(u\ne0\), \(a=b=0\) and then \(u=0\), a contradiction. Thus \(a\) is arbitrary, \(b=a^2\), and \(c,d\) are arbitrary, giving \(q^3\) roots. When \(A=0\) but \(B\ne0\), necessarily \(a=0\), so \(u=b\) and \(B=b^2\). Frobenius squaring is bijective in \(k\), so there is a unique \(b\) and exactly \(q^2\) roots.

When \(A\ne0\), both \(a\) and \(u\) are nonzero, and \(a=A/u\), \(b=u+(A/u)^2\). The remaining equation \(B=bu\) is exactly
\[
u^3+Bu+A^2=0.
\]
Conversely every root \(u\in k\) of this equation is nonzero and determines a unique pair \((a,b)\); independently there are \(q^2\) choices for \((c,d)\). The derivative of the cubic is \(u^2+B\). At a hypothetical multiple root \(u^2=B\), substitution into the cubic leaves \(A^2\ne0\), so every such cubic is separable and has \(0\), \(1\), or \(3\) rational roots.

**Step 4: count the cubic root strata.** For a cubic with three distinct rational roots in characteristic two, those roots must be \(\{r,s,r+s\}\) for distinct nonzero \(r,s\in k\), since the \(u^2\) coefficient vanishes. Conversely, such a triple determines precisely
\[
B=r^2+rs+s^2,\qquad A^2=rs(r+s),
\]
with a unique nonzero \(A\) because Frobenius is bijective. There are \((q-1)(q-2)/2\) unordered pairs of distinct nonzero elements, and each triple is counted by its three unordered pairs; thus exactly \((q-1)(q-2)/6\) of the \(q(q-1)\) targets with \(A\ne0\) have three roots.

The total number of root incidences among targets with \(A\ne0\) is \((q-1)^2\): choose any nonzero \(a,u\), then \(A=au\) and \(B=(u+a^2)u\), and conversely every target-root incidence yields such a pair. Consequently the number of one-root targets is
\[
(q-1)^2-3\frac{(q-1)(q-2)}6=\frac{q(q-1)}2.
\]
Subtracting the one- and three-root counts from \(q(q-1)\) gives \((q^2-1)/3\) zero-root targets. Adding the \(q-1\) targets with \(A=0\), \(B\ne0\) gives \((q-1)(q+2)/2\) targets having \(q^2\) group-square roots. There is one identity target with \(q^3\) roots, giving the displayed complete histogram. Its entries sum to \(q^2\), and the sum of target-count times root-count is \(q^4\), the order of \(G_5(k)\). The attainable-target count is \(q^2-(q^2-1)/3=(2q^2+1)/3\).

**Step 5: prove minimality.** At truncation levels \(N\le3\), every square is the identity. At \(N=4\), every square has form \(t+A t^4\), where \(A=a(a^2+b)\); every \(A\in k\) occurs, for instance by choosing \(a=1\), \(b=A+1\). The square-generated subgroup there is exactly the set of squares. The strict obstruction at \(N=5\) follows because \((q^2-1)/3\ge1\) for every \(q\ge2\).

## Verification
The self-contained standard-library Python checker `nottingham_square_check.py` implements finite fields \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_4\), \(\mathbb F_8\), \(\mathbb F_{16}\) with polynomial-basis arithmetic. Crucially, it computes every element's actual self-composition using polynomial convolution, rather than assuming the square formula, then compares every one of the \(q^2\) prescribed target fibers with the cubic criterion and the four-row histogram. It fully enumerates \(q^4\) group elements in each test field. Run `python3 nottingham_square_check.py`; the captured output is in `nottingham_square_check_output.txt`. All four exhaustive tests passed. Finite enumeration is a reproducibility check, not the proof for all field orders; the general proof above is algebraic.

## Relationship to prior work
The finite substitution groups and their power filtrations are classical. York's 1990 full paper determines exponents of truncated substitution groups over commutative rings; this determines the possible order of an element but not the individual square-value images, their fiber sizes, or the sharp first failure of square-value surjectivity onto the square-generated subgroup. Pálfy's 1998 full paper studies conjugacy classes of selected Nottingham quotients; even a complete conjugacy class-size census cannot infer this exact power-word fiber histogram. Chinburg and Symonds (2010) construct a genuine order-four formal series in characteristic two, a different existence problem. Szechtman's later power-commutator presentation addresses finite Nottingham groups, primarily over prime fields, but does not state the extension-field cubic fiber histogram.

Two especially close published calculations concern the exact same degree-five quotient's *conjugacy-class* spectrum and the geometry and lifting of depth-one *involutions*, respectively. The latter controls part of the identity square fiber; neither covers nonidentity square fibers or the first nonsurjectivity result. Ordinary unitriangular-group square roots are not interchangeable with Nottingham-substitution-group roots. The novelty assessment is consequently about these precise statements and their implications, rather than the novelty of the elementary cubic-counting technique alone.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to characteristic-two fields and truncation degrees at most five for the minimality assertion. It does not classify higher-depth square maps, the full infinite group's power images, or root fibers for characteristic different from two. Its historical originality remains exposed to possibly unlocated power-word-map computations and full-text material not found by the literature searches; no claim of absolute priority or independent mathematical validation is made. The cited published classifications were compared for their exact scopes, not interpreted as novelty guarantees.
