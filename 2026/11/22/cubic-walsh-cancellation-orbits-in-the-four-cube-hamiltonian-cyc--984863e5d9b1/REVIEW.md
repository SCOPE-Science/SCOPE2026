# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness (PASS)

Every relevant object, the Walsh normalization, and the three finite support ranges are explicitly specified in `RESULT.md`. The first complete enumerator yields 2688 anchored directed traversals, paired two-to-one into 1344 Hamiltonian edge sets; the independent perfect-matching decomposition recovers exactly the same 1344 edge sets from 272 perfect matchings. Both check every one-, two-, and three-edge signed coefficient by exact integer sums, yielding identical complete histograms. The first program checks all 384 geometric automorphisms, all 24 triple orbits, and every proper vertex cut. All assertions passed on the included programs. No inference to higher-degree Fourier spectra or higher dimensions is made.

## Originality (PASS; bounded historical comparison)

The closest primary literature is Parkhomenko's 2001 full-text four-cube change-number enumeration and Chebiryak–Kroening's 2008 full-text classification. Their published result that 1344 cycles exist is deliberately not claimed as new. Neither inspected result states the full signed one-, two-, three-edge Walsh distributions or the six zero-orbit classification. Dvořák and Caha–Koubek give prescribed-edge **existence** theorems; those claims are not signed multiplicity identities. Searches of published indexed records, aliases (cyclic Gray codes, Hamiltonian 4-cube, signed edge-incidence correlations), and prior accessible results yielded related results for different underlying codebooks or complete graphs but no logically stronger result giving this exact classification. Search non-retrieval alone does not prove priority: older unindexed or inaccessible derivations remain possible, especially because the 2001 classification provides an alternative route to re-enumeration. The full texts of the two prescribed-edge articles were not available for inspection; their accessible theorems have a different quantifier and target.

## Value (PASS)

This is a complete low-degree Fourier-observability boundary for a canonical codebook of cyclic binary Gray codes rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. It distinguishes a ubiquitous singleton cancellation, nonvanishing of all pair characters, and genuinely additional cubic cancellation beyond vertex-cut parity. The six geometric orbits give reusable exact test cases for cycle-edge correlation and constrained sampling. It is a finite classification, not a claim about global Fourier recovery, and remains useful without generalization to every dimension.
