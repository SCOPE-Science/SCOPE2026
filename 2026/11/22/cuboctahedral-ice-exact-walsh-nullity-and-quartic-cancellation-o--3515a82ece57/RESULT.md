# Cuboctahedral ice: exact Walsh nullity and quartic cancellation orbits

## Finding
Let \(G=L(Q_3)\) be the cuboctahedral graph (the line graph of the three-dimensional cube). Its vertices are the twelve edges of \(Q_3\); two vertices of \(G\) are adjacent when their underlying cube edges meet. There are twelve vertices and twenty-four edges. Encode every Eulerian orientation of \(G\) by its binary edge-direction vector \(x\in\mathbb F_2^{24}\), using the labelling specified below. Let \(\mathcal E\) denote the set of these vectors, and define the unnormalized Walsh coefficients
\[
W(S)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal E}(-1)^{\sum_{e\in S}x_e},\qquad S\subseteq E(G).
\]
The following are exact:

1. \(|\mathcal E|=600\). Among the \(2^{24}=16{,}777{,}216\) characters, precisely \(9{,}500{,}672\) have \(W(S)=0\), and precisely \(7{,}276{,}544\) have \(W(S)\ne0\). Thus convolution on \(\mathbb F_2^{24}\) with the indicator of \(\mathcal E\) has rank \(7{,}276{,}544\).
2. All \(8{,}388{,}608\) odd-cardinality characters vanish by reversal symmetry. Of the even-cardinality characters, another \(1{,}112{,}064\) vanish. No even character of cardinality zero or two vanishes, whereas precisely \(864\) characters of cardinality four vanish. Under the full order-48 automorphism group of \(G\), these \(864\) supports form exactly \(21\) orbits, fifteen of size \(48\) and six of size \(24\).
3. The smallest absolute value of a nonzero multiplier is \(4\); therefore the sharp Hilbert-space condition number on the convolution range is \(600/4=150\), and the pseudoinverse has \(\ell^2\) operator norm \(1/4\).
4. Over all real or complex linear projections (not necessarily translation invariant) onto this convolution range, the minimal \(\ell^1\)- and minimal \(\ell^\infty\)-operator norms are both
\[
\frac{29471}{2048}.
\]
In particular, the first cancellation among even-cardinality frequency supports is quartic, not quadratic. The complete signed coefficient histogram is reproducibly generated by the accompanying exact-integer verifier; the theorem above singles out the rank, first blind modes and optimal recovery constants.

## Assumptions and scope
Number the cube vertices by \(0,\ldots,7\), viewed as three-bit words, and list its twelve undirected edges \((i,j)\), with \(i<j\) and Hamming distance one, in lexicographic order. These twelve edges are the labelled vertices \(0,\ldots,11\) of \(G\). List the twenty-four edges \((a,b)\) of \(G\), with \(a<b\), lexicographically; they join exactly pairs of cube edges sharing a cube vertex. For \((a,b)\), set \(x_e=1\) when its arrow runs from \(a\) to \(b\) and \(0\) otherwise. An orientation is admissible exactly when all twelve vertices have outdegree two (and hence indegree two). Every frequency, not only symmetry representatives, is included in the statement. Counting convolution is unnormalized: \((Tf)(y)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal E}f(y+x)\).

## Proof
**Graph and state-space completeness.** The two source programs construct \(Q_3\) directly from three-bit Hamming distances and form its line graph by incidence, obtaining twelve vertices and twenty-four edges, each of degree four. The first verifier visits all and only balanced edge orientations by a binary depth-first branching over all twenty-four edges. For a partially assigned vertex, an outgoing count greater than two, or unable to reach two with the remaining incident edges, is pruned. Every unpruned leaf therefore has exactly two outgoing edges per vertex; every legal orientation has exactly one unpruned branch sequence. The exact leaf count is \(600\).

**An independent exhaustive parametrization.** At a vertex \(v\), parity of the outdegree condition is the affine binary equation
\[
\sum_{e\ni v}x_e\equiv \#\{(a,v)\in E(G):a<v\}\pmod2.
\]
The unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\) has rank \(11\): its only row dependence is the sum of all twelve rows, since \(G\) is connected. Choose a spanning tree of its eleven edges; the other thirteen edge bits are freely specified and determine the tree bits uniquely by leaf elimination. Thus every balanced orientation lies in a fixed affine thirteen-dimensional subspace and can be recovered without the first program's search logic. The second verifier exhausts all \(2^{13}\) free-bit assignments, solves the tree bits, applies the exact outdegree-two rule, and independently obtains the same \(600\) states. An exact integer length-\(8192\) Walsh transform gives \(4639\) zero and \(3553\) nonzero reduced characters. Fourier character values on the full twenty-four-coordinate group differ across cosets of the eleven-dimensional affine annihilator only by a fixed sign; every reduced character therefore appears \(2^{11}\) times with the same absolute multiplier. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{rank}T=3553\cdot2^{11}=7{,}276{,}544,
\quad \operatorname{nullity}T=4639\cdot2^{11}=9{,}500{,}672.
\]
The independent reduced transform and the direct full transform both have minimum surviving absolute value \(4\), maximum \(600\), and satisfy Parseval's exact integer identity \(\sum_S W(S)^2=2^{24}\cdot600\).

**First blind characters and symmetry.** Reversing every arrow complements all twenty-four bits and permutes \(\mathcal E\), proving \(W(S)=(-1)^{|S|}W(S)\) and hence annihilation for odd \(|S|\). The second verifier checks the exact signed sum directly for every two-element and four-element support, using the independently recovered \(600\) states: it obtains respectively zero and \(864\) vanishing supports. The zero-weight multiplier equals \(600\). A separate full-bit Walsh transform gives \(1{,}112{,}064\) additional zeros at even weights, hence the stated parity decomposition. The orbit checker generates all \(3!2^3=48\) cubical automorphisms by coordinate permutation and bit translation and transports them to cuboctahedron edges. Their action partitions the \(864\) quartic supports into precisely fifteen orbits of size \(48\) and six of size \(24\); the orbit check verifies the images exhaust the zeros, without assuming there are no other graph symmetries. MathWorld independently states the full automorphism-group order is \(48\).

**Sharp projection constant.** Walsh diagonalization identifies the range \(\mathcal R\) as the span of characters with nonzero \(W\). Its translation-invariant Fourier range projection \(P\) has symbol \(1_{\{W\ne0\}}\). Any other linear projection \(Q\) onto \(\mathcal R\) can be averaged over all Boolean-group translations, preserving its action on \(\mathcal R\), the range and a non-increased \(\ell^1\) or \(\ell^\infty\) norm. Every translation-invariant projection with range \(\mathcal R\) equals \(P\); therefore \(P\) is norm-minimizing even among non-invariant projections. If \(p\) is the inverse Walsh transform of \(1_{\{W\ne0\}}\), then \(\|P\|_{1\to1}=\|P\|_{\infty\to\infty}=\sum_g|p(g)|\). Both the full \(2^{24}\)-character transform and the independent \(2^{13}\)-character transform return \(\sum_g|p(g)|=29471/2048\), with integer arithmetic. On the lower-dimensional quotient, the equality of norms follows because the Fourier-support indicator is constant on annihilator cosets and the projection kernel is supported on the corresponding thirteen-dimensional subspace. The pseudoinverse assertion follows from the minimum nonzero multiplier \(4\).

## Verification

Run `python3 cubo_ice_independent.py` for independent thirteen-coordinate parity elimination, Parseval, all two- and four-edge signed sums, and the reduced projection norm. Run `python3 cubo_orbits.py` for the complete symmetry-orbit census. The included output files record the exact results. All arithmetic is integer; the largest intermediate values are within the chosen integer types. The three procedures completed and passed their numerical assertions and agreement checks. Source-code loops, not merely their log messages, are included to make completeness inspectable.

## Relationship to prior work
The general finite ice model and Eulerian orientations of planar graphs are classical. Bleher and Fokin (first public version 8 October 2005) study the six-vertex model with square-domain-wall boundary conditions; Creed (first public version 7 March 2007) studies sampling Eulerian orientations on triangular planar lattices and their complexity; Eloranta treats ice on Archimedean lattices and long-range boundary effects. Those objects and objectives differ from the binary edge-direction Fourier recovery problem on this twelve-vertex graph. The standard cuboctahedral graph is documented by Weisstein, including its line-graph realization and automorphism group. Related public finite Fourier calculations for the octahedral graph's degree-one frame and the nine-vertex periodic square-ice torus concern different graphs and/or different frequency orders; their claims do not imply the exact twenty-four-coordinate rank, the twenty-one quartic cancellation orbits or this projection constant. A computationally equivalent but unindexed result cannot be ruled out by search alone.

## Limitations
This is a complete *finite* theorem about the specific cuboctahedral graph, not an all-lattice formula or a classification of all four-regular graphs. The orbit classification here concerns the first nontrivial even-weight blind characters; no separate geometric characterization of every higher-weight zero is asserted. The exhaustive source programs are computational certificates requiring execution and inspection; their correctness is supported by independent parametrization, exact transforms and proof of coverage, but not by an external independent audit. Literature priority remains provisional.
