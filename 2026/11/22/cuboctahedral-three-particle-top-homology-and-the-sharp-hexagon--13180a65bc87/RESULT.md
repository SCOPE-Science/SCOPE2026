# Cuboctahedral three-particle top homology and the sharp hexagon threshold

## Finding

Let \(G=L(Q_3)\) be the twelve-vertex, twenty-four-edge cuboctahedral graph, the line graph of the ordinary three-dimensional cube. Let \(B_3(G)=\operatorname{UConf}_3(|G|)\) be the space of unordered triples of distinct points on its geometric realization. Then
\[
H_3(B_3(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{24}.
\]
Exactly sixty triples of pairwise vertex-disjoint simple cycles of \(G\) occur. Their geometric product three-tori integrally generate this entire group, and twenty-four of them form an integral basis. The sixty cycle-length triples are distributed as
\[
(3,3,3):8,\qquad(3,3,4):12,\qquad(3,3,5):24,\qquad(3,3,6):16.
\]
Let \(L_{\leq d}\) denote the subgroup of \(H_3\) generated by product tori whose three constituent cycles all have length at most \(d\). Then \(L_{\leq4}=L_{\leq5}\) is a primitive free subgroup of rank twenty, and
\[
H_3(B_3(G);\mathbb Z)/L_{\leq5}\cong\mathbb Z^4,
\qquad L_{\leq6}=H_3(B_3(G);\mathbb Z).
\]
Thus six is the sharp maximum simple-cycle length needed to generate top integral homology by geometric product tori. In particular, pentagon-containing product tori do not enlarge the integral lattice already generated by triangle-and-square product tori, whereas hexagon-containing tori supply four missing directions.

## Assumptions and scope

The cube has vertices indexed by the binary triples identified with integers from zero through seven; its twelve edges are the unordered pairs at Hamming distance one. Each cube edge is a vertex of \(G\), and two such vertices are adjacent precisely when the cube edges share an endpoint. All graph cycles are simple undirected cycles, and disjointness means no common graph vertex (not merely edge-disjointness). A product torus is the image of the collision-free map \(S^1\times S^1\times S^1\to B_3(G)\) that places one independently moving point on each of three disjoint cycles. Each such map is an embedding. Orientations are fixed algorithmically by the lexicographically smaller of the two directed cycle words with their least vertex first.

The theorem is only about the top integral homology of three unordered particles and its explicitly defined geometric product-torus sublattices. It does not assert a complete homotopy type or determine lower-degree integral homology.

## Proof

**An integral chain model.** Use the fully reduced Świątkowski complex over \(\mathbb Z\) in particle weight three. For each vertex \(v\), order its four incident edges as \(e_{v,0},e_{v,1},e_{v,2},e_{v,3}\), and write \(h_{v,a}\), for \(a=1,2,3\), for the local degree-one difference between half-edges \(a\) and zero. Its differential is \(\partial h_{v,a}=e_{v,a}-e_{v,0}\). Edge variables have degree zero and weight one; the local generators \(h_{v,a}\) have degree and weight one. The integral homology of the weight-three complex is the homology of \(B_3(G)\).

In degree three, all three local generators must belong to distinct vertices. Hence the free chain group has
\[
\binom{12}{3}3^3=5940
\]
basis elements. In degree two the chain group has \(\binom{12}{2}3^2\cdot24=14256\) generators; exactly 14040 occur as nonzero rows of the degree-three boundary. The signed Leibniz differential is built independently from the graph and incident-edge indices, without loading a precomputed matrix. Ordered integer column elimination computes **5916 pivots, every one of coefficient \(\pm1\)**. Because all elimination steps are unimodular, the remaining twenty-four free column positions give exact integral coordinates on the kernel. Since weight three has no degree-four chains, this kernel is exactly \(H_3\), proving \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{24}\).

**All geometric cycle products.** A direct exhaustive depth-first enumeration, taking the smallest vertex first and identifying reversal, gives 1422 simple cycles. Testing pairwise disjoint vertex masks gives exactly sixty three-cycle collections and the four cycle-length counts above. For an oriented cycle \(C\), its local chain is the sum over cycle vertices of outgoing minus incoming half-edge differences. This is an integral one-cycle. Multiplying three such one-cycles on disjoint vertex supports with the appropriate Koszul permutation sign gives the top-dimensional product-torus chain. Direct substitution into the signed boundary independently checks that all sixty are cycles.

**Integral basis and sharp cutoff.** Project these sixty cycles to the twenty-four free integral kernel coordinates. Select the lexicographically first twenty-four vectors independent modulo two using binary Gaussian elimination. The resulting square integer coordinate matrix has exact determinant \(-1\), computed by fraction-free Bareiss elimination. Hence those twenty-four geometric tori are an integral basis of the entire homology group.

There are twenty product tori with no constituent cycle longer than four, and twenty-four additional product tori involving a pentagon. Exact rational elimination shows all forty-four short tori have rank twenty, and a twenty-by-twenty minor among the twenty triangle-and-square vectors has determinant \(+1\). Consequently the triangle-and-square subgroup is a primitive direct summand, and every integer vector in its rational span lies in that same integral subgroup. The pentagon vectors therefore contribute nothing new, and the short-torus quotient is free of rank four. Finally, the full sixty tori include the above twenty-four-element unimodular basis, all of whose remaining necessary directions must be supplied by hexagon-containing tori. This proves the sharp cutoff and the asserted integral quotient.

All enumerations are exhaustive because they range over every simple cycle on the twelve explicit graph vertices and every signed basis generator in weight three. The integer-unit eliminations and determinants are exact; finite sampling or numerical rank estimates are not used as infinite-family arguments.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_cuboctahedral.py` using Python 3.8 or newer, with no third-party libraries. The script reconstructs the graph and both chain groups, computes all 5916 unit pivots, enumerates all 1422 simple cycles and sixty disjoint triples, checks each of their signed boundaries, verifies the rank-twenty cutoff, and computes the two integral minors. The accompanying `checker_results.json` gives the actual free-coordinate positions, selected torus indices, determinant, cycle counts and results. ## Relationship to prior work

Farley and Sabalka developed discrete Morse methods for unordered graph configurations, supplying the classical 2004 starting point for graph braid topology. The integral reduced Świątkowski complex and geometric product-cycle chains are described in Maciążek and Sawicki's treatment of graph configuration-space homology. Their paper explicitly studies certain graphs such as wheels and complete bipartite graphs, not the three-particle cuboctahedral graph or the exact hexagon cutoff established here.

Related computations for three-point configuration spaces on the dodecahedral, Heawood, truncated-tetrahedral and octahedral graphs have different underlying graphs, and results about the cuboctahedron's cut or independence complexes have different underlying topological objects. Neither type gives the present 24-dimensional integral lattice or its twenty-dimensional short-cycle sublattice. These are statement-and-implication distinctions, not uniqueness claims based only on parameter matching.

## Limitations

No generalization to other polyhedral graphs, other particle numbers, or the full homotopy type is asserted. The result does not determine a preferred automorphism-equivariant basis or a complete presentation of all sixty torus relations. The published-literature comparison cannot rule out unindexed or inaccessible equivalent work. The proof assumes the standard integral reduced Świątkowski model for graph configuration spaces; all nonstandard, graph-specific integer computations are supplied in the verifier.
