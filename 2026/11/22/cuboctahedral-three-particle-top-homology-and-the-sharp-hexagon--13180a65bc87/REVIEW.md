# Scientific assessment: cuboctahedral three-particle top homology

## Correctness — PASS

The claim follows from an explicit full weight-three reduced Świątkowski chain model for the twelve-vertex, twenty-four-edge cuboctahedral graph. There are 5940 degree-three generators and 5916 exact unit pivots, so the integral top kernel is free of rank 24. All 1422 simple cycles and 60 vertex-disjoint triples are exhaustively constructed. Every product chain is checked to have zero signed boundary. An explicit 24-by-24 torus-coordinate determinant is -1. Of 44 tori using only cycles of length at most five, the rank is 20; a determinant-one 20-by-20 minor of the 20 triangle-and-square tori proves that subgroup saturated. The exact verifier reproduces every claim without preloaded certificates. Risks are the usual trust in the established reduced chain model and verifier correctness; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.

## Originality — PASS, relative to checked sources

The graph itself is canonical, but the searched literature did not supply this exact three-point cuboctahedral degree-three homology rank, product-torus integral generating set, or sharp maximum-cycle-length threshold. Farley–Sabalka (arXiv:math/0410539) provides the general discrete Morse framework, while Maciążek–Sawicki (DOI 10.1007/s00220-019-03583-5) discusses the reduced model, product tori and certain other graph families, without the present graph-specific calculation. An–Knudsen (arXiv:2008.10371) treats second homology, which does not imply the third-homology statement. The nearest indexed computations involve three particles on the truncated tetrahedron, dodecahedron, Heawood graph, octahedron or cube, not the line graph of the cube. Other results on the cuboctahedron's cut and independence complexes concern different complexes. Unindexed equivalent work is the principal residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The obstruction is structural and geometrically motivated: top-degree particle homology is generated by independent circular particle motions, but allowing all triangles, squares and pentagons leaves precisely four primitive integral directions unaccounted for. The need for hexagons in this symmetric twelve-vertex Archimedean graph is not implied by the top Betti rank, and the quotient is determined integrally rather than just rationally. This gives a reproducible, natural cycle-length boundary in a topological generating problem, without requiring a broader classification.

## Limits

Only third integral homology for three unordered particles and the two indicated torus sublattices are determined. Other homology groups, full homotopy type, natural symmetry module and all lattice relations remain open. The source comparison is a good-faith mathematical implication comparison rather than a claim that unpublished or inaccessible work is absent.
