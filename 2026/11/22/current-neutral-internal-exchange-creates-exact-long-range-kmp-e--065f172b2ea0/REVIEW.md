# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Common marks give a monotone coupling with the two effective baths at T_max. The product of independent Exp(T_max) variables is invariant for the latter: on a pair the sum has gamma shape two and its independent uniform split restores the product, including when just the interior output is retained at a reservoir. Starting the lower process at zero gives tightness and bounded second moments by domination; finite-rate continuous jump kernels give a Feller process and a stationary subsequential time-average limit. Under the same marks two initial states have expected ℓ¹ distance at most e^(−t) times the original distance: internal splits are nonexpansive and the two bath clocks shrink the associated coordinate difference by mean factor 1/2. Thus the stationary law is unique (also for arbitrary initial distributions by truncation against bounded Lipschitz tests), and is exchangeable. Writing c=(T_++T_−)/2, Δ=T_+−T_−, the first-moment bath drift is c−m, so m=c. For diagonal q and off-diagonal z the generator gives 0=−4q+4c²+2(T_+²+T_−²)+(n−1)v(−q+2z) and 0=6c²−6z+v(q−2z), each multiplied by three. The other internal edges cancel in the mixed equation by exchangeability. Substitution gives z−c²=vΔ²/(2[(3n+1)v+12]); the determinant is nonzero at all allowed rates. Internal currents vanish by equal means, and each plus bath contributes Δ/4 per unit time. The derivative is 6Δ²/[(3n+1)v+12]² and n(z−c²) has denominator 2[(3+1/n)nv+12] with numerator nvΔ², verifying both limits and the equilibrium and uncoupled cases.

## originality

PASS

The distinct claim is an evaluated finite-size covariance and its internal-rate response at fixed mean current in the symmetric two-bath complete interior graph. The KMP dynamics, its dual description and the general stationary representation are established. Their application here still requires a network-specific two-particle or moment calculation; the explicit one-dimensional covariance problem in the published work is not this calculation.

## value

PASS

The complete-graph symmetric bath geometry is a natural test of what exchange can change when mean currents cannot detect it. The exact covariance separates that effect from reservoir flux, and locates the internal-rate crossover at order 1/n with an explicit scaling function. This gives a useful finite-size benchmark for second-moment or mean-field methods; neither the symmetry argument for currents nor the general stationary law supplies the benchmark.
