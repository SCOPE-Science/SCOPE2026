# Mathematical review

## Correctness: PASS

The normalized coherent-state trace identity, support-point uniqueness, boundary normal coordinates, quadratic curvature expansions, rigorous dominated convergence, and four elementary one-dimensional integrals reconstruct the complete proof. The resulting constant is \(1/(4\pi\sqrt{\kappa_A\kappa_B})\) and the fixed transverse-offset penalty is \(e^{-\eta^2}\). The standalone disk checker independently tests normalization and several asymptotic ratios. Finite numerics are explicitly not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality: PASS (best-knowledge)

Nicola–Riccardi (arXiv:2401.04659) treats self-window Hilbert–Schmidt concentration, and Feichtinger–Halvdansson–Luef (arXiv:2308.04985) develops the broader convolution language. Neither inspected treatment gives the fixed-shape distant-window boundary curvature coefficient. The closest indexed work on *contact-order universality* takes the different expanding-window, fixed-gap limit; the two-distant-disk work gives top-eigenvalue splitting, not a mixed-trace law. The coherent-state overlap and the Laplace method themselves are not claimed original. Specialized older localization-products literature remains a priority risk.

## Value: PASS

The mixed trace measures cross-interference of spatially separated Gaussian localizers. Its exact exponential gap, cubic prefactor, curvature dependence and vertical-offset factor quantify the leading nonlocal interaction for an entire class of smooth convex windows, beyond heuristic Gaussian decay or numerics for one disk pair.

## Scientific limits and closest literature

Strict convexity and positive curvature only near the two support points, with fixed domains, are essential. The theorem does not address eigenvalue splitting, other spectra, uniform estimates or flat/corner regimes. References: arXiv:2401.04659; arXiv:2308.04985; Cordero–Gröchenig (2003).
