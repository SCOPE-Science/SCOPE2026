# Scientific review: Cycle-length ordering of observed entropy under stochastic resetting

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem concerns the observed-vertex entropy rate of the stated discrete-time Markov chain, not action-label entropy or entropy production. Its proof independently derives the absolutely convergent line Green function, periodizes it to the cycle, calculates the entropy loss caused by merged reset/hop destinations, and obtains a positive closed-form finite-size correction. The ratio of consecutive geometric deficit factors is strictly smaller than one for all admissible parameters. The optimized-entropy statement uses a controlled small-reset expansion, existence of interior maximizers and pointwise ordering; it does not assume uniqueness. The standalone checker independently solves the rational stationarity equations and passed 1,048 assertions. Finite checks support arithmetic, not the infinite-size argument.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Wang and Chen (arXiv:2208.05665) derive the reset Markov kernel and entropy-rate framework. The published *Sharp regular-graph entropy envelope for random walks with stochastic resetting* supplies the essential collision correction and finite-regular-graph high-girth envelope, so neither is claimed here. Its general upper bound, and convergence to the infinite-tree entropy, do not give the exact ring-size-by-ring-size ordering or the explicit finite-cycle correction and leading coefficient. A published exact clique entropy optimization treats a different graph family. Zelenkovski et al. (2023) explicitly merge reset and ordinary jumps but optimize hitting time rather than entropy. The strong monotonicity is not a coefficientwise consequence of unreset returns: three- and four-step return probabilities for triangles and squares cross. Focused literature and published-finding corpus searches produced no equivalent published theorem; incompleteness of search and possible implicit derivability from broader work remain risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The cycle is the canonical finite periodic one-dimensional geometry and the full family of connected simple two-regular graphs. Strict path-entropy ordering at fixed local degree and reset probability isolates a nontrivial network-size effect and supplies its exact finite-size error, improving on a general qualitative high-girth limit. The all-rate theorem and strict ordering of optimal entropies are usable in finite-size uncertainty/information benchmarks, without presupposing a unique optimizing probability. The statement is narrower than a general graph theorem and does not address physical dissipation.
