# Cycle-rose sliding puzzles are subdivided discrete tori with an exact spanning-tree product

## Finding

Let \(r\ge2\) and let \(m_1,\ldots,m_r\ge3\) be integers. Form the simple *cycle rose* \(X\) by taking cycles of lengths \(m_i\), pairwise disjoint except for one common vertex \(o\). Set
\[
N=1+\sum_{i=1}^r(m_i-1),\qquad d_i=m_i-1,\qquad L=\prod_{i=1}^r d_i.
\]
Let \(Y=K_{1,N-1}\) be a star with a distinguished center label (the *blank*). A vertex of \(\operatorname{FS}(X,Y)\) is a bijection of labels to board vertices, and an edge swaps the blank with a label at an adjacent board vertex.

**Theorem.** Every connected component of \(\operatorname{FS}(X,Y)\) is isomorphic to the graph formed from
\[
C_{d_1}^{(\mathrm{multi})}\mathbin{\square}\cdots\mathbin{\square} C_{d_r}^{(\mathrm{multi})}
\]
by subdividing *every edge in coordinate direction* \(i\) into a path of exactly \(m_i\) edges. Here \(C_d^{(\mathrm{multi})}\) is the ordinary cycle when \(d\ge3\), and \(C_2^{(\mathrm{multi})}\) consists of two parallel edges (not one). The displayed Cartesian product is a multigraph; after subdivision it is a simple graph. In particular, all components have \(NL\) vertices, \(L\sum_i m_i\) edges and integral cycle rank \(1+(r-1)L\). There are precisely
\[
\frac{N!}{NL}=\frac{(N-1)!}{L}
\]
components.

**Spanning-tree formula.** Write \(\tau\) for the number of ordinary undirected spanning trees in any one component. Then
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\tau=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^r m_i^{L}}{L}
\prod_{\substack{0\le k_i<d_i\quad (1\le i\le r)\\ (k_1,\ldots,k_r)\ne(0,\ldots,0)}}
\left(\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{4}{m_i}\sin^2\frac{\pi k_i}{d_i}\right).}
\]
The full expression is an integer. When every petal is a triangle, \(d_i=2\), \(L=2^r\), and this specializes to
\[
\tau=3^{(r-1)2^r+1}\,2^{2^{r+1}-2-r}
\prod_{j=1}^r j^{\binom{r}{j}}.
\]
For two triangles, there are six components on 20 vertices each, and every component has exactly \(7776\) spanning trees. For a triangle and a quadrilateral there are 20 components on 36 vertices each, and each has \(1620000\) spanning trees. For three triangles there are 90 components on 56 vertices each, and each has \(6347497291776\) spanning trees.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite. Every petal has length at least three. The rose consists solely of its cycles, and distinct petals share exactly the hub \(o\), with no other edges. Labels are all distinct, including the distinguished blank; the star has center blank and \(N-1\) leaves. Every allowed move exchanges the blank with one adjacent board token. The word *component* refers to a connected component in the full \(N!\)-configuration graph, not a quotient by token relabeling. Count graph edges without orientation and parallel edges in the *core multigraph* with multiplicity. For \(d_i=2\), both core parallel edges are separately subdivided; all resulting edges of a component are simple.

## Proof

**1. Hub monodromy and the component vertex set.** Orient each petal cyclically, starting and ending at \(o\), and denote by \(P_i\) the length-\(m_i\) closed board walk that goes once around petal \(i\). Start with the blank at \(o\). Sliding the blank along \(P_i\) returns it to \(o\) and rotates the \(d_i=m_i-1\) ordinary tokens in that petal by a single cyclic step. It moves no token in any other petal. The rotations therefore commute, and the hub-return action of these loops is
\[
H=\mathbb Z/d_1\mathbb Z\times\cdots\times\mathbb Z/d_r\mathbb Z
\]
acting freely on the labels occupying the nonhub vertices. The fundamental group of a rose graph is freely generated by the oriented petal loops. Every closed board walk based at the hub is obtained from these loops and their inverses by inserting immediate reversals; an immediate reversal swaps the same token back and has no net effect. Consequently the reachable configurations with blank at the hub are exactly the \(L=|H|\) distinct configurations in this commuting orbit. This conclusion does not assume that the fundamental group itself is abelian; only its action on tokens is.

For any board vertex \(v\ne o\), choose the unique forward segment of its petal from \(o\) to \(v\) that does not traverse a complete cycle. Reversing this segment moves the blank from \(v\) to \(o\). Therefore every reachable configuration with blank at \(v\) arises by starting from one of the \(L\) hub configurations and following the chosen forward segment. These \(L\) configurations are distinct because reversing the same segment would otherwise identify two different hub configurations. Each vertex \(v\) thus supports exactly \(L\) component states. Every connected component has \(NL\) vertices; since the full permutation graph has \(N!\) vertices, and all components are isomorphic by permuting the ordinary token labels, their count is \(N!/(NL)\).

**2. Explicit graph isomorphism.** Label the hub configurations by \(h=(h_1,\ldots,h_r)\in H\), where taking petal \(i\) once in its oriented direction sends \(h\) to \(h+e_i\), with addition modulo \(d_i\). For every \(h\) and \(i\), take the length-\(m_i\) path in the configuration graph obtained by moving the blank once around petal \(i\) from its hub configuration \(h\). Its endpoint is hub state \(h+e_i\). Its \(m_i-1\) interior states have blank at the successive nonhub vertices of petal \(i\), so no two such interior states are equal unless they come from the same initial hub state \(h\), petal \(i\), and step number: reversing the unique chosen forward segment recovers \(h\). Every nonhub configuration is one of these interiors by the preceding paragraph. At an interior state the blank has exactly two board neighbours, and both are precisely the preceding and succeeding vertices on its path; at a hub state it has exactly \(2r\) neighbours, the endpoints of the paths leaving it and entering it from the \(r\) oriented petals. The resulting paths account for every permitted exchange. Hence this is an exact edge-preserving bijection with the edge-subdivided Cartesian product of the cyclic *multigraphs* \(C_{d_i}^{(\mathrm{multi})}\). When \(d_i=2\), the forward paths from \(h_i=0\) and \(h_i=1\) give the two distinct parallel core edges and have distinct interiors. The core has \(L\) vertices and exactly \(L\) edges in each of the \(r\) directions; after replacing each type-\(i\) edge by \(m_i\) edges, the stated vertex and edge counts and cycle rank follow.

**3. Spanning-tree subdivision identity.** Let \(B\) be any connected loopless multigraph with positive integer edge lengths \(\ell_e\), and let \(B^{\mathrm{sub}}\) replace every edge \(e\) with a path of \(\ell_e\) edges. In a spanning tree of \(B^{\mathrm{sub}}\), each subdivided path is either fully present or has exactly one omitted segment: omitting two or more would isolate an interior vertex. The fully present base edges form a spanning tree of \(B\), because contracting their paths preserves connectivity and cannot create a cycle. Conversely, given a base spanning tree \(T\), include every segment of its edges and choose one segment to omit in each other subdivided path. Thus
\[
\tau(B^{\mathrm{sub}})
=\sum_{T\text{ spanning tree of }B}\prod_{e\notin T}\ell_e
=\left(\prod_{e\in E(B)}\ell_e\right)
\sum_{T\text{ spanning tree of }B}\prod_{e\in T}\ell_e^{-1}.
\]
The final sum is the weighted spanning-tree polynomial of \(B\) with edge conductances \(\ell_e^{-1}\). This argument remains valid for the two parallel edges of \(C_2^{(\mathrm{multi})}\), because the edges and their subdivided paths are distinct.

Apply the identity to our core \(B=\square_i C_{d_i}^{(\mathrm{multi})}\) with each direction-\(i\) edge assigned length \(m_i\). The prefactor is \(\prod_i m_i^L\). The weighted Laplacian is the sum of coordinate cycle Laplacians with conductance \(1/m_i\). On \(C_d^{(\mathrm{multi})}\) the discrete Fourier modes have eigenvalues \(4\sin^2(\pi k/d)\), for \(k=0,\ldots,d-1\); this also holds for the two-parallel-edge cycle \(d=2\), whose nonzero eigenvalue is \(4\). Tensor-product Fourier modes on \(H\) therefore have eigenvalues \(\sum_i (4/m_i)\sin^2(\pi k_i/d_i)\). Exactly the all-zero mode has eigenvalue zero. The weighted matrix-tree theorem gives the weighted tree sum as \(1/L\) times the product of all nonzero eigenvalues. Combining this with the subdivision identity proves the product formula. The integer character follows independently from its interpretation as a spanning-tree count.

**4. All-triangle specialization.** If \(m_i=3\) for every \(i\), then \(L=2^r\), and a Fourier mode with precisely \(j\) nonzero coordinates has eigenvalue \(4j/3\) and multiplicity \(\binom{r}{j}\). Substitute these modes in the product formula, use \(\sum_{j=1}^r\binom{r}{j}=2^r-1\), and simplify to obtain the stated all-triangle product.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library `verify.py` constructs the rose board, all hub configurations and the entire explicitly subdivided core. It independently explores a connected component of the *literal blank-swap graph* by breadth-first search and compares its complete vertex and edge sets with those from the proposed isomorphism. It then obtains spanning-tree numbers in two independent ways: the integer Kirchhoff cofactor of that component, and the subdivision identity applied to the conductance-weighted Cartesian core after clearing denominators. Fractional rounding is never used in these checks: determinants use exact Bareiss integer elimination.

The checked petals are \((3,3)\), \((3,4)\), \((4,3)\), \((4,4)\) and \((3,3,3)\). Their exact spanning-tree counts are \(7776\), \(1620000\), \(1620000\), \(12230590464\) and \(6347497291776\), respectively. These finite checks test implementation and key boundaries (including the two-parallel-edge core factors); the proof above, not the finite checks, establishes all parameter values.

Reproduce with `python3 verify.py` in an ordinary Python 3 standard-library environment.

## Relationship to prior work

The recent paper by Yang [1], publicly posted November 1, 2026, proves a local-connectivity equality for star friends-and-strangers graphs when the board is 2-connected. A rose with at least two petals has a cut vertex, so the board here falls outside that hypothesis; the paper does not give the explicit component shape or any spanning-tree formula. Krishnan and Li [2] prove the vertex connectivity of each component equals its minimum degree when the board is connected. That result applies to these roses but does not determine their component isomorphism types, integral cycle ranks or spanning-tree numbers. Defant and Kravitz [3] explicitly determine the one-cycle case and record a general cut-vertex lower bound on component counts; neither result determines the multi-petal subdivision and its spanning-tree product. Jeong [4] studies the girth of star friends-and-strangers graphs; Defant–Dong–Lee–Wei [5] study connectedness and cycle spaces for other board/friendship regimes. None of these inspected results establishes the present formula.

The underlying hub-monodromy principle belongs to classical sliding-puzzle theory; the contribution asserted here is the *combined exact multi-petal component graph and its all-parameter spanning-tree enumeration*, not the previously studied general fact that slide-puzzle components may be disconnected or that the single-cycle case is cyclic. An older, potentially relevant treatment of sliding-puzzle monodromy is Wilson's 1974 article [6], for which a complete direct-text comparison was not obtained. This limits claims of historical priority; it does not enter the proof.

## Limitations

The statement concerns a pure wedge of simple cycles, not arbitrary cactus graphs with cycles joined by paths or attached trees. The formula concerns ordinary spanning trees of each connected component, not directed trees or graph spectra of the full \(N!\)-vertex disjoint union. The exact classification is proved internally, but the historical-originality assessment is based on inspected and searchable literature, not an independent priority certification. The result has not undergone an independent mathematical audit.
