# Cyclic fixed loci of wheel-forest complexes are smaller wheel-forest complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 3\), let \(W_n\) be the wheel consisting of a cycle on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), a central hub, and the \(n\) hub spokes. Let \(\mathcal F(W_n)\) be the simplicial complex of cycle-free subsets of its \(2n\) edges. Let \(r\) rotate rim vertices by one position, fixing the hub. For every \(0\le k<n\), put \(d=\gcd(n,k)\), with \(\gcd(n,0)=n\).

There is a canonical piecewise-linear homeomorphism
\[
\left|\mathcal F(W_n)\right|^{\langle r^k\rangle}\cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\left|\mathcal F(W_d)\right|.
\]
Here \(W_2\) has two parallel rim edges and two spokes, and \(W_1\) has a rim loop and one spoke; the forest complex excludes loops. Consequently
\[
\left|\mathcal F(W_n)\right|^{\langle r^k\rangle}\simeq
\begin{cases}
\{\mathrm{pt}\},&d=1,\\
\bigvee^{2^d-2}S^{d-1},&d\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, for every \(n\ge3\), all nonidentity rotations have contractible fixed loci if and only if \(n\) is prime.

## Assumptions and scope
The action is the **geometric** simplicial action, not the action on the set of fixed vertices, the ordinary orbit-space quotient, or a homotopy quotient. Its fixed points can lie in the interiors of simplices. The PL homeomorphism uses the natural edge-orbit coordinates. The case \(k=0\) is included and reduces to the identity homeomorphism. For \(d\in\{1,2\}\), the graph notation is interpreted literally as a multigraph; a loop cannot be included in a forest and two parallel edges form a two-edge cycle.

## Proof
Label the spokes \(s_i=\{h,i\}\) and rim edges \(a_i=\{i,i+1\}\), with indices modulo \(n\). The subgroup generated by \(r^k\) is the subgroup of rotations by multiples of \(d\). Thus each of the \(2d\) edge orbits is exactly one of
\[
A_j=\{a_i:i\equiv j\pmod d\},\qquad S_j=\{s_i:i\equiv j\pmod d\},\quad 0\le j<d.
\]
A fixed point in the interior of a simplex of \(\mathcal F(W_n)\) has an invariant supporting forest, and its barycentric coordinates are constant on each edge orbit. Therefore the entire fixed space is a simplicial realization on the orbit-barycenters; a collection of orbit-barycenters forms a face precisely when the union of the corresponding edge orbits is a forest. This gives a canonical simplexwise-linear homeomorphism from the fixed space to this orbit-forest complex.

Identify the orbit vertices \(A_j,S_j\) with the rim and spoke edges of \(W_d\). We prove that a union of orbits is a forest in \(W_n\) if and only if its corresponding edge set is a forest in \(W_d\). If all rim orbits are selected, the lift contains the entire \(n\)-cycle and the projected set contains the complete \(d\)-rim cycle (a loop when \(d=1\), two parallel edges when \(d=2\)); both are dependent. Otherwise the selected rim graph in \(W_d\) is a disjoint union of paths and isolated vertices. The inverse image of each path under the cyclic covering of rims \(C_n\to C_d\) consists of \(n/d\) disjoint copies of that path. After adding spokes, a cycle occurs in the lifted graph exactly when one of these paths has at least two selected spoke endpoints, i.e. when its projected component has at least two spokes attached to the hub. This is precisely the cycle criterion in the projected wheel. The equivalence also holds with no rim edges and for \(d=1\), for which the rim loop is forbidden. Thus the orbit-forest complex is exactly \(\mathcal F(W_d)\), proving the PL assertion.

For \(d\ge3\), the graphic matroid of \(W_d\) has rank \(d\), no coloops, and a shellable independence complex. The classical matroid-independence theorem identifies it as a wedge of \(T_{W_d}(0,1)\) spheres of dimension \(d-1\). The wheel is planar self-dual, so \(T_{W_d}(0,1)=T_{W_d}(1,0)\). The chromatic polynomial
\[
P_{W_d}(q)=q\big((q-2)^d+(-1)^d(q-2)\big)
\]
then gives \(T_{W_d}(1,0)=(-1)^dP'_{W_d}(0)=2^d-2\). This agrees with the explicit wheel homology count in Kook's coned-graph calculation. For \(d=2\), the forest complex is the four-vertex graph with all five permitted pairs, hence \(S^1\vee S^1\). For \(d=1\), only the spoke is a legal nonempty face, so the realization is a point.

Finally, if \(n\) is prime, every nonidentity rotation has \(\gcd(n,k)=1\). If \(n\) is composite, take a nonzero \(k\) with \(d=\gcd(n,k)\ge2\), and the fixed locus has nonzero reduced homology. This establishes the arithmetic criterion.

## Verification
The accompanying Python 3 program `verify_wheel.py` checks the orbit-forest criterion on **every orbit subset** for all rotations with \(3\le n\le9\) (42 rotation patterns). It separately tests the odd-rim reflection cone property in five finite cases and verifies the Lefschetz Euler sums and binary-coloring characters for four odd rim sizes. Execution returned `WHEEL_FOREST_ROTATION_FIXED_LOCI_AND_ODD_DIHEDRAL_CHARACTER_OK 42 5`. These computations are supplementary checks; the unrestricted quantifiers follow from the covering/path argument and the matroid theorem above, not from finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Kook's 2006 prepublication/2007 article proves the wheel-forest complex's ordinary homology count and describes the natural equivariant homology representation through edge-rooted forests; those results are not claimed here as new. Ardila–Castillo–Samper's 2014 preprint/2016 article establishes shelling information for matroid independence complexes. Moci–Pezzoli's 2020 preprint/2021 article relates matroid homology representations to other combinatorial representations and includes coned graphs. None of those cited results states a PL identification of the geometric fixed spaces of wheel rotations with smaller-wheel forest complexes. A related published published-finding corpus result handles free regular graph covers, whereas the rotation action here fixes the hub vertex and is not a graph covering at that vertex. Previously recorded calculations for the five-rim wheel concern ordinary orbit-space quotients, not geometric fixed loci. The distinction between a fixed space and an orbit space is essential.

## Limitations
This theorem classifies fixed spaces for cyclic **rotation** subgroups only; it makes no claim about geometric fixed spaces for reflection-containing subgroups or integral orbit-space homology. Its ordinary nonequivariant wheel homology calculation is standard and included solely to identify the fixed-space homotopy types. The literature comparison is best-knowledge, not a guarantee that no equivalent unpublished or unindexed classification exists; some closely relevant indexed published-finding corpus full texts could not be inspected.
