# Mathematical review: cyclic Frobenius line-divisor Picard tori

## Correctness — PASS
The proof gives an integral chord-elimination basis for the complete graph cycle lattice and an explicit antipodal residual module. The even residual module is cyclic with relation \((s-1)(s^h+1)=0\), and the invariant perimeter class has no equivariant integral retraction. Evaluating Frobenius coinvariants produces the exact finite abelian groups; independent normalization node and branch counts give both zeta cases. A direct Vandermonde construction establishes geometric existence in every finite characteristic. The standalone exact integer tests cover \(4\le n\le11\) and several finite fields; they support, but cannot replace, the uniform proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
Full-text comparisons covered Busonero–Melo–Stoppino on nodal degree class groups, Casalaina-Martin–Kass–Viviani on compactified Jacobian local rings, Paul–Traves–Wakefield on hyperplane arrangements, and the closest three published arithmetic Picard-torus classifications. Existing structural results motivate normalization but do not supply this evaluated \(K_n\) cyclic integral module or the even signed-edge gluing. The Igusa, del Pezzo and Heawood formulas address different graphs and do not imply this claim. Searches cannot certify global historical uniqueness; an unindexed equivalent graph-module result remains a specific priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The uniform exact classification determines genuine algebraic-torus isomorphism types and rational-point groups, not just isogeny factors. For even degree the index-two extension is arithmetically detectable and its antipodal nodes produce a branch-reversal factor in the zeta function. The natural geometric family has no arbitrary extra incidence restrictions beyond general position and cyclic Galois transitivity.

## Limits
Only transitive cyclic Frobenius actions on ordinary-node line divisors are covered. This is the identity component of the curve Picard scheme, not the surface Picard group. No external independent mathematical review has been performed.
