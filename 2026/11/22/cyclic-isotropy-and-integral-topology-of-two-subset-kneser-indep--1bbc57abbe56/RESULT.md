# Cyclic isotropy and integral topology of two-subset Kneser independence quotients

## Finding

For every integer \(n\geq5\), let \(G_n=\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\), the graph on the two-element subsets of \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with adjacency meaning disjointness. Let \(X_n=|\operatorname{Ind}(G_n)|\), and let \(C_n\) translate all ground-set labels simultaneously. Put \(Y_n=X_n/C_n\), the ordinary geometric quotient, and write \(\epsilon_q(n)=1\) when \(q\mid n\), and zero otherwise. Define

\[
r_n=\frac{n^2-6n+5+3\epsilon_2(n)+4\epsilon_3(n)}6.\]

Then, without any coprimality assumption,

\[
\pi_1(Y_n)\cong C_{n/\gcd(n,6)},\qquad H_1(Y_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/(n/\gcd(n,6))\mathbb Z,
\]
\[
H_2(Y_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{r_n},\qquad H_i(Y_n;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(i>2),\qquad H_0(Y_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

In particular, the **first singular width** omitted from the previously available free-action classification has a complete integral homotopy classification:

\[
|\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{KG}(6,2))|/C_6\simeq S^2\vee S^2.
\]

For even widths not divisible by three, the fundamental group has order \(n/2\); for widths divisible by three but odd, it has order \(n/3\). At widths coprime to six, the formulas agree with the previously established Moore-space-wedge classification, and novelty is claimed only for the singular-action regime.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is the ordinary two-subset Kneser graph, not a stable Kneser graph. Its independence simplices are families of pairwise-intersecting ground-set edges. The translation group is the entire cyclic group of ground-set label additions. A geometric quotient is intended; the Borel quotient is a different space. All assertions concern integral homotopy and homology of the orbit space, and all integers \(n\geq5\), including those divisible by two or three.

## Proof

**1. Equivariant two-dimensional model.** The independence complex is the clique complex of \(L(K_n)\), since pairwise intersection of ground-set edges is incidence in the complete graph's line graph. The equivariant star-collapse theorem for line-graph clique complexes (reference 3) gives an \(S_n\)-equivariant PL subdivision and elementary collapse to the edge-midpoint subdivision of the two-skeleton \(K_n=(\Delta^{n-1})^{(2)}\). Thus for the subgroup \(C_n\) it gives an equivariant homotopy equivalence and, after passing to ordinary geometric orbit spaces,

\[Y_n\simeq |K_n|/C_n.\]

The underlying combinatorial mechanism can also be reconstructed: every pairwise-intersecting family of ground-set edges is either contained in the star at a common ground vertex or is the three-edge boundary of a ground triangle. Collapse each star simplex equivariantly to its incidence tree, fixing pairwise star intersections. The exceptional three-edge faces then fill precisely the subdivided ground triangles. No freeness hypothesis is used in this construction. In particular, the target has dimension two and is simply connected before quotienting, since it is the two-skeleton of a simplex with all triangular relations.

**2. Stabilizer calculation and fundamental group.** A nonidentity translation \(g\in C_n\) of order \(d\) acts freely on ground-set labels with all label-orbits of length \(d\). A point of the geometric realization of a simplicial complex is fixed exactly when its unique positive-support simplex is invariant as a set. Every simplex of \(K_n\) has one, two or three vertices. Consequently \(|K_n|^g\ne\varnothing\) if and only if \(d=2\) or \(d=3\). The order-two element, when present, fixes the midpoints of its \(n/2\) invariant edges. Each order-three element, when present, fixes the barycenters of its \(n/3\) invariant triangles. No elements of larger order have fixed points.

Armstrong's fundamental-group theorem for a simply connected simplicial complex modulo a finite simplicial group action (reference 4) identifies the quotient fundamental group with \(C_n/N\), where \(N\) is the subgroup generated by the elements having fixed points. Here \(N\) is generated by the unique order-two element when \(2\mid n\) and the unique order-three subgroup when \(3\mid n\); hence \(|N|=\gcd(n,6)\). This gives

\[\pi_1(Y_n)\cong C_{n/\gcd(n,6)}.\]

The first homology is its abelianization, which is the same cyclic group.

**3. Integral homology from the fixed-point Euler characteristic.** The equivariant barycentric subdivision is a genuine finite simplicial \(C_n\)-complex. The Euler characteristic of its ordinary orbit space is the Burnside average of the Euler characteristics of the fixed sets. The identity contributes

\[\chi(K_n)=n-\binom n2+\binom n3.\]

When \(2\mid n\), the sole element of order two contributes \(n/2\); when \(3\mid n\), each of the two elements of order three contributes \(n/3\). All remaining nonidentity elements contribute zero. Consequently

\[\chi(Y_n)=\frac1n\left(n-\binom n2+\binom n3+\frac n2\epsilon_2(n)+\frac{2n}3\epsilon_3(n)\right)=1+r_n.\]

The quotient is a connected finite two-dimensional CW complex. Because its fundamental group is finite, its first integral Betti number is zero. Its second integral homology is a subgroup of the free abelian degree-two cellular chains, hence is torsion-free. The Euler characteristic determines its rank as \(r_n\), and dimension gives all higher integral homology groups zero. This proves the entire integral homology formula.

**4. The simply connected first singular width.** The formula above shows that for integers \(n\geq5\) the fundamental group is trivial exactly when \(n=6\). At this width, the quotient is simply connected, two-dimensional and has \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^2\). Hurewicz supplies two spherical homology generators, and the homological Whitehead theorem identifies the resulting map \(S^2\vee S^2\to Y_6\) as a homotopy equivalence.

## Verification

The accompanying \(\texttt{verify.py}\) constructs the complete barycentric flag complex of \(K_n\), enumerates cyclic translation orbits, builds signed integral cellular boundaries and checks the boundary-square identity. For widths \(n=5,6,7,8,9\), an exact Smith reduction of the 2-cell attaching matrix after collapsing a spanning tree verifies the predicted abelianization, free degree-two ranks and absence of hidden first-homology torsion. For \(n=10,12\), it checks the cell counts, Euler formula and chain condition. The first singular width \(n=6\) produces 8 vertices, 25 edges, 20 two-cells and Euler characteristic 3; its exact Smith factors are all units. Execute \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\) after installing the version of SymPy specified in \(\texttt{requirements.txt}\). The program reports \(\texttt{CYCLIC_KNESER_SINGULAR_QUOTIENT_CHECKS_OK}\).

These finite checks are independent corroboration, not the proof for arbitrary widths. The unrestricted result follows from the equivariant two-skeleton model, Armstrong's theorem and the fixed-point Euler calculation.

## Relationship to prior work

Linusson–Shareshian–Welker (reference 1) first studied the bounded-matching complex \(\mathrm{NM}_2(n)\), which equals the present Kneser independence complex, and established its nonequivariant sphere-wedge topology. Goyal–Shukla–Singh (reference 2) gave a nonequivariant line-graph clique-complex model. The later equivariant star-collapse result (reference 3) already supplies the group-equivariant model used in the proof; that reduction is **not** claimed as new.

The closest directly overlapping result (reference 5) completely determines the ordinary cyclic quotient homotopy type for \(\gcd(n,6)=1\). It deliberately excludes widths with order-two or order-three stabilizers. Those free-action cases and their Moore-space homotopy types are prior coverage. The present statement instead calculates the fundamental groups and integral homology **at all previously excluded singular widths**, and the complete homotopy type at the initial width \(n=6\). The only additional topology input is the classical fixed-point normal-subgroup formula and the Euler characteristic of a finite quotient; neither theorem is claimed as new.

## Limitations

A full homotopy decomposition for arbitrary singular widths other than \(n=6\) is **not** proved. In particular, for \(n\geq12\) divisible by six, one must not infer a wedge of two-spheres from the integral homology because the quotient has fundamental group \(C_{n/6}\). The formulas concern two-subset Kneser graphs only and no homotopy-orbit construction. Source comparisons cannot exclude all unindexed equivalent derivations; no independent external audit has been performed.
