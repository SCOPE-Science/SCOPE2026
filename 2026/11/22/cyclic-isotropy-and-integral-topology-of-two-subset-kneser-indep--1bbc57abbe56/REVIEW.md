# Review of cyclic isotropy in Kneser independence quotients

## Correctness — PASS
The previously established equivariant star collapse identifies the Kneser independence complex with the simply connected two-skeleton of a simplex. For its ordinary translation quotient, the fixed-point criterion identifies only elements of orders two and three, so Armstrong's orbit-space theorem yields the cyclic fundamental group of order \(n/\gcd(n,6)\). Burnside's Euler formula, the two-dimensional cellular bound and torsion-freeness of second cellular homology give all stated integral groups. At width six, Hurewicz and homological Whitehead identify the quotient with two two-spheres. The accompanying finite verifier corroborates the chain complexes and integral presentations through width nine; finite cases are not substituted for the general argument.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The directly overlapping public classification already proves the full Moore-space-wedge homotopy type when \(\gcd(n,6)=1\), but explicitly excludes singular widths; those coprime cases are attributed and not claimed as new. An earlier full equivariant star-collapse result supplies the geometric model and is likewise attributed. The original bounded-matching source and later clique-complex literature establish unquotiented topology, not the singular cyclic orbit fundamental groups. Checks of equivalent terms and stronger coverage found no statement implying the width-six two-sphere wedge or the full singular-width formula. Unindexed equivalent work remains possible.

## Value — PASS
This resolves a natural excluded parameter regime rather than a manufactured small case. The isotropy thresholds at divisibility by two and three control the entire fundamental-group family, and the first singular quotient changes from torsion fundamental group to simply connected, with exact integral homotopy type. The rank formula and infinite-family group calculation make the phenomenon reproducible and useful for comparing nonfree quotient topology. No general homotopy decomposition is asserted at other singular widths.
