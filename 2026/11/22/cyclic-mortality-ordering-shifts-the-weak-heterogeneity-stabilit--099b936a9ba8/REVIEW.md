# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem refers only to the locally unique analytic coexistence branch, not to all fixed points. At the equal-rate, all-pairwise reference state, the simplex Jacobian has determinant \((2p-1)^2/3>0\); hence the equilibrium branch is analytic. At zero triadic share the equilibrium is explicit and the exact trace equals \(S_2\varepsilon^2/3+V\varepsilon^3/[3(2p-1)]\). The homogeneous Jacobian has trace \(-\alpha(2p-1)^2/3\). Cyclic relabeling symmetry on the zero-sum mortality plane forbids an \(\alpha\varepsilon\) term. The analytic implicit-function theorem, negative trace derivative, and positive determinant prove the local existence, uniqueness, sign and two-term expansion of the stability threshold. The packaged symbolic test reproduces the algebraic identities, with numerical checks serving only as consistency tests. This does not prove nonlinear stability at the pure-imaginary crossing.

## Originality — PASS, subject to accessible literature

The initiating paper arXiv:2501.09172 (v4) supplies the exact model, trace at zero triadic share, homogeneous Jacobian and a numerical heterogeneous threshold. Its Appendix C explicitly describes the heterogeneous equilibrium threshold as generally unavailable analytically. The claimed additional result is specifically the **orientation-sensitive cubic term**, and the strict difference between permutations of an identical mortality multiset, not the published leading variance dependence. The 2017 Grilli et al. tournament framework and the 2024 Gibbs et al. general Lotka–Volterra study do not, in the material read, give this exact three-way mixture threshold under permuted mortalities. Semantic searches of previously published mathematical findings found nearby ecology and cyclic-Hopf results, but not the same statement or a stronger one. Major residual risk: the source cites supplemental *small-species-difference scaling* whose complete contents were not available for comparison; it may contain relevant asymptotic details. No search result establishes absolute priority.

## Value — PASS

Variance or an unordered distribution is a natural summary of demographic heterogeneity, and the source uses such a summary to study higher-order stabilization. This theorem demonstrates quantitatively why it is insufficient even near equal species: the orientation of mortality along the competitive cycle changes the required fraction of higher-order interactions. The cubic term is the earliest order at which this directional effect appears; its sign and an explicit reversible example give a structural ecological stability diagnostic, not merely a numerical refinement of a constant. Applicable only to the stated mathematical model and local regime.
