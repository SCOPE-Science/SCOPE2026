# Cyclic-quintic symmetry and quartic Frobenius branching of the two exceptional genus-four curves

## Finding
The two known genus-four curves over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\) of gonality five have full **ground-field** automorphism groups
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_2}(C_2)\cong C_5,
\qquad \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_3}(C_3)\cong C_5.
\]
In each case, the corresponding quotient is a tame cyclic cover of degree five of \(\mathbb P^1\) with genus-zero target and exactly four geometric totally ramified points. The four points are conjugate under arithmetic Frobenius; their images give one closed branch point of degree four. Thus the two exceptional curves have the same unusually rigid ground-field symmetry and ramification-descent pattern.

## Assumptions and scope
Put \(k=\mathbb F_q\) for \(q\in\{2,3\}\). All projective equations below are in \(\mathbb P^3_k\) with coordinates \([x:y:z:w]\). Define \(C_q=V(Q_q,F_q)\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_2&=xy+z^2+zw+w^2,&F_2&=x^3+y^3+z^3+y^2w+xzw,\\
Q_3&=xy+z^2+w^2,&F_3&=x^3+y^3+y^2z+x^2w+xyw-y^2w-yzw+z^2w.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are the original Faber--Grantham smooth, geometrically connected genus-four models. They are the canonical complete intersections of the unique nonsplit quadric containing each curve with a cubic; in particular they are nonhyperelliptic. Every automorphism over \(k\) extends uniquely to a projective transformation preserving the quadric. The claims above concern these full arithmetic automorphism groups and the quotient by them, not the complete automorphism groups over an algebraic closure.

## Proof
**(1) Complete rational projective symmetry enumeration.** Let \(K=\mathbb F_{q^2}\), and let \(\theta\in K\) satisfy \(\theta^2+\theta+1=0\) when \(q=2\), and \(\theta^2+1=0\) when \(q=3\). Set
\[
H(x,y,z,w)=\begin{pmatrix}x&z+\theta w\\z+\theta^qw&-y\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(-\det H=Q_q\). The projective rational automorphism group of the nonsplit smooth quadric is the group generated by \(H\mapsto M H M^{(q)\mathsf T}\), for \(M\in\operatorname{PGL}_2(K)\), together with coefficient conjugation \(H\mapsto H^{(q)}\). The two cosets have \(q^2(q^4-1)\) transformations each, giving exactly \(120\) for \(q=2\) and \(1440\) for \(q=3\). This parametrizes **every** \(k\)-rational projective quadric automorphism, not a selected subgroup.

For each resulting four-by-four matrix \(T\) over \(k\), the curve-preserving criterion is
\[
F_q(Tv)=a F_q(v)+Q_q(v)(b_xx+b_yy+b_zz+b_ww),
\qquad a\in k^\times,\quad b_\bullet\in k,
\]
where \(v=(x,y,z,w)^{\mathsf T}\). This is exactly the condition that the cubic section be preserved modulo the unique quadratic equation. Checking all twenty degree-three monomial coefficients against the complete linear span gives precisely five transformations for each \(q\). The accompanying program performs all \(120+1440\) tests without interpolation or sampling. 

**(2) Explicit order-five generators.** In the displayed coordinate bases, representatives are
\[
T_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\1&1&1&1\\0&1&1&0\\0&0&1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
T_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2&0\\1&1&1&1\\2&0&2&0\\1&2&2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct coefficient calculation in the respective characteristic gives
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
Q_2(T_2v)=Q_2(v),&F_2(T_2v)=F_2(v)+(x+y)Q_2(v),\\
Q_3(T_3v)=Q_3(v),&F_3(T_3v)=F_3(v)+(x+y+z)Q_3(v).
\end{array}
\]
Moreover, \(T_q^5=I_4\) and \(\det(tI_4-T_q)=t^4+t^3+t^2+t+1\), which is irreducible over each \(k\) because \(2\) and \(3\) each have multiplicative order four modulo five. Thus the five enumerated automorphisms form exactly \(\langle T_q\rangle\cong C_5\).

**(3) Arithmetic branch orbit.** Since \(q\ne5\), the order-five action is tame. Write \(g_0\) for the genus of the quotient and \(r\) for the number of geometric fixed points (each has ramification index five). Riemann--Hurwitz yields
\[
2\cdot4-2=5(2g_0-2)+4r.
\]
The only nonnegative integer solution is \(g_0=0,\ r=4\). A genus-zero smooth curve over a finite field has a rational point and is therefore \(\mathbb P^1_k\). As the characteristic polynomial of \(T_q\) is separable, the projective action has exactly four geometric fixed eigenlines; all four must lie on the curve by the fixed-point count. Because that characteristic polynomial is irreducible of degree four over \(k\), arithmetic Frobenius acts transitively on the four eigenlines, and hence on the corresponding branch values. The branch divisor is consequently one closed point of degree four. No claim is made about ramification or automorphism groups over other characteristics or arbitrary twists.

## Verification
The standalone script `verify_excessive_aut.py` uses exact polynomial arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_4\), and \(\mathbb F_9\). It lists all projective two-by-two matrices over the relevant quadratic extension, forms all two quadric-automorphism cosets, tests equality of all twenty cubic coefficients, verifies the two displayed generators, and checks geometric smoothness via the unit ideal of all Jacobian minors on four affine charts. It confirms no rational point on either model. Running `python verify_excessive_aut.py` with SymPy installed prints `EXCESSIVE_GENUS_FOUR_AUTOMORPHISMS_VERIFIED`. The finite enumeration is exhaustive because the full quadric automorphism group is enumerated; the ramification conclusion follows from the theoretical Riemann--Hurwitz argument and irreducible characteristic polynomials, not extrapolation from experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
Faber and Grantham originally provided the canonical models and established gonality five and pointlessness, for \(\mathbb F_2\) in their 2020 preprint and for \(\mathbb F_3\) in their 2020 preprint. The later maximum-gonality work of Faber, Grantham and Howe establishes that these are the two genus-four exceptions up to ground-field isomorphism. These existence, uniqueness, and gonality facts are not claimed anew. The added invariant is their **complete ground-field automorphism group**, together with the cyclic-quotient branch and Frobenius classification.

## Limitations
The result determines arithmetic automorphisms over the two stated base fields. It does **not** classify full geometric automorphism groups over algebraic closures, their enlargement after extension, or isomorphism types of other cyclic-quintic curves. Search evidence cannot rule out earlier unindexed characterizations of the same automorphisms or quotient in another language. The computational certificate requires a functioning standard Python/SymPy environment; its numerical output is supported by the full enumeration argument, not treated as an independent audit.
