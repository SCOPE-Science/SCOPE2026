# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement's quantifiers concern every pair in the exact complex cubic harmonic eigenspace on the probability-measure two-sphere and arbitrary finite positive normalized node weights. The pointwise Laplace product identity turns mixed value/gradient energy into the degree-lowering operator \(I+\Delta_S/42\); the degree-six product component is annihilated, leaving only degrees zero, two and four. The converse uses the verified Legendre identity with two nonzero coefficients and orthogonality of harmonics in the variable defining the zonal center. The icosahedral spectra follow from six-axis dot products, the addition theorem, and the strict two-subspace decomposition. A packaged exact arithmetic checker verified all 49 independent matrix entries, idempotent-up-to-scalar identities and traces. The analytic argument proves the infinite quantification over cubic functions; computation only checks the indicated finite algebra.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty

The main claim is the if-and-only-if characterization for arbitrary weighted node sets and a mixed intrinsic-gradient Parseval form. The closest full text, Xiao–Zhuang, arXiv:2303.05365, treats value-cubature framelets and derivatives of the node-configuration objective, not the sampled gradients of harmonics. Allasia–Cavoretto–De Rossi, arXiv:1705.01032, discuss Hermite–Birkhoff interpolation by different cardinal bases without this exact moment characterization. Hitchin, arXiv:0706.0088, Proposition 1, already gives the four-dimensional nullspace of cubic point values at icosahedral vertices; that component is not presented as new. A related published icosahedral cap-concentration spectrum can imply the special vertex eigenvalues in a shrinking-cap limit; the general iff theorem is not a corollary of that single-geometry analysis. Indexed comparisons of discrete harmonic interpolation, spherical designs and Sobolev-gradient norms gave no encompassing identical result. Risks: unindexed derivative cubature formulations or results in the numerical quadrature literature may be equivalent; historical priority cannot be established exhaustively.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact characterization determines precisely when lower-order polynomial moments suffice to repair a potentially singular degree-three value frame using intrinsic gradients, without requiring degree-six cubature. This is a natural completeness criterion useful for choosing finite observation nodes: the condition is finite, checkable and is both necessary and sufficient. The icosahedral example realizes a strict, fully quantified complementarity: neither values nor gradients alone determine every cubic, whereas the weighted joint frame is perfectly conditioned and its weights are unique. These structural conclusions are more than an isolated scalar computation or normalization check.
