# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The argument uses the positive stationary edge-current recurrence, not a finite-size conjecture: iteration gives a strictly positive denominator and makes current orientation equal to the sign of the full clockwise/anticlockwise transition-product ratio for every integer ring size at least three. All destination-only preference factors cancel exactly over the cycle. Positive Gamma density formulas reduce the log ratio to \(C+A\log t-Bt\), with a derivative having at most one positive root, leading to an exhaustive exact classification including all degenerate coefficients, tangencies and branch boundary cases. The homogeneous matched-mean Gamma witness gives two simple roots using an exact elementary inequality \(4/e>1\). A standalone independent stationary-linear-system test passed for 2,590 cases. Finite tests do not prove the theorem and are not used as proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
Valle et al. (2024) provide the likelihood-times-selection construction but do not analyze stationary circulation under varying observation intervals. Prima et al. (2024) empirically examine sampling-frequency bias without the analytical cycle result. Continuous-time habitat-selection diffusion (Michelot et al. 2019) and published current-count theorems for physical switching ratchets concern different families. Classical Kolmogorov cycle balance and the fact that two Gamma density curves may intersect twice are explicitly credited: the narrower contribution is the preference-invariant *entire ring current orientation*, all-size sharp reversal bound, and equal-mean ecological counterexample for route-specific Gamma likelihoods. A broader mathematical statement could subsume this elementary-looking combination; historical priority is therefore provisional, not independently established.

## Value — PASS
The article-motivated distinction between observation-conditioned likelihood and true movement motivates a specific mathematical risk: at fixed estimated habitat preferences and even equal clockwise/counterclockwise mean transit times, an inferred cycle circulation can exhibit a counterclockwise–clockwise–counterclockwise sequence solely when the conditioning interval changes. The complete theorem also proves that altering any destination-only habitat preferences cannot eliminate or cause those two orientation changes, whereas common-rate Gamma travel models forbid two reversals. This gives an exact, model-specific interpretation boundary rather than a mere numerical artifact or unmotivated special-value computation. It is not a field prediction or a recommendation about habitat management.
