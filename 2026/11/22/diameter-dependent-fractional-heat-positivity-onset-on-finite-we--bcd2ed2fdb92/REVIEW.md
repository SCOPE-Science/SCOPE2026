# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Finite spectral calculus is analytic in the exponent near one, and the subtracted heat-kernel integral establishes that each nonedge \((L\log L)_{ij}\) is strictly positive. Shortest-path Taylor coefficients are positive. A uniform two-parameter entry expansion separates the negative \(\varepsilon t\) perturbation from positive \(t^d\) classical transport; local rescaling and its derivative establish exactly one crossing, while compact-time and uniform large-time positivity eliminate later crossings. Diagonal and edge entries are checked separately. Twelve finite graphs and 97 nonedge pairs pass 30,176 supporting numerical checks. The path constant has an explicit spectral calculation. Numerical checks are not proofs of infinite quantifiers.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual historical priority risk.** The close full-text comparisons are the 2014/2015 Riascos–Mateos \(0<\alpha<1\) graph random-walk study; the 2015 Daners–Glück–Kennedy qualitative eventual-positivity results; and the 2019 Benzi et al. subunit-fractional graph decay study. Their theorems do not entail entrywise uniqueness and universal diameter-dependent onset for \(\alpha\downarrow1\) from above. A published full-text complete-multipartite result covers the diameter-two unweighted specialization, and is explicitly acknowledged rather than presented as a novelty; its fixed diameter-two formulas do not imply the higher-diameter exponent. Related graph-heat numerical integrators have different scalar filters. Unindexed or differently formulated perturbative results could reduce historical priority.

## Value
**PASS.** The exponent \(1/(D-1)\) distinguishes graph metric distance as a stability mechanism for almost-Markovian fractional diffusion: a four-vertex path has square-root onset despite complete multipartite networks having linear onset. The positive \(L\log L\) integral connects global heat transport to local geodesic weights and provides explicit, computable sharp leading coefficients on arbitrary finite weighted graphs, not just a numerical example or an unmotivated parameter choice.

## Limitations
The small-exponent interval depends on the fixed graph; neither a graph-uniform interval nor global-in-exponent uniqueness is claimed. The numerical tests do not certify infinite graph classes, and full historical priority beyond the inspected sources is not established.
