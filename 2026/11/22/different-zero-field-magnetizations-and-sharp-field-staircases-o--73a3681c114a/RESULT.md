# Different zero-field magnetizations and sharp field staircases of the two Blanuša snarks

## Finding
For the two original 18-vertex, 27-edge Blanuša cubic snarks, the zero-temperature uniform antiferromagnetic Ising model distinguishes the graphs sharply. Write \(M=\sum_v\sigma_v\), and use the Hamiltonian specified below. Their exact positive-field profiles are:

| Field | First snark: ground magnetization (degeneracy) | Second snark: ground magnetization (degeneracy) |
|---|---|---|
| \(h=0\) | \(M=\pm2\) (one state each) | \(M=0\) (four states) |
| \(0<h<J\) | \(M=2\) (one state) | \(M=0\) (four states) |
| \(h=J\) | \(M=2\) (one state) | \(M=0,2,4\) (total 82 states) |
| \(J<h<3J\) | \(M=2\) (one state) | \(M=4\) (36 states) |
| \(h=3J\) | Coexistence of \(M=2,4,\ldots,18\) (2495 states) | Coexistence of \(M=4,6,\ldots,18\) (2473 states) |
| \(h>3J\) | \(M=18\) (one state) | \(M=18\) (one state) |

The exact ground-energy curves for \(h\ge0\) are
\[
E_1(h)=\min\{-21J-2h,\;27J-18h\},\qquad
E_2(h)=\min\{-19J,\;-15J-4h,\;27J-18h\}.
\]
In particular, the first graph has a nonzero infinitesimal-positive-field magnetization, while the second requires a finite field to magnetize.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(G_1\) and \(G_2\) be the labelled first and second Blanuša snark graphs generated by the exact edge lists in `verify.py`. On each of their 18 vertices put \(\sigma_v\in\{-1,+1\}\), and consider
\[
H_G(\sigma;h)=J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E(G)}\sigma_u\sigma_v-h\sum_v\sigma_v,
\qquad J>0,\quad h\ge0.
\]
Each graph has 27 edges and degree three at every vertex. The theorem concerns finite, labelled spin configurations at exactly zero temperature. The term “first” refers to `BlanusaFirstSnarkGraph` and “second” to `BlanusaSecondSnarkGraph` in Sage's small-graph generator definitions.

## Proof
Represent the set of down spins by \(S\subseteq V(G)\), with \(r=|S|\) and \(e_G(S)\) edges induced by \(S\). Since the graph is cubic, the number of cut edges is \(3r-2e_G(S)\). Hence, identically for every configuration,
\[
\frac{H_G}{J}=27-6r+4e_G(S)-\frac hJ(18-2r),\qquad M=18-2r.
\]
For nonnegative fields, spin reversal shows that it suffices to minimize over \(0\le r\le9\), with both signs restored at zero field. For fixed \(r\), minimize only \(e_G(S)\); no approximation is involved.

Here are the **complete exact finite counts**. Each entry \((f_r,a_r)\) gives the minimum number of induced edges over all \(r\)-element subsets and the number attaining that minimum:

| \(r\) | First \((f_r,a_r)\) | Second \((f_r,a_r)\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | (0, 1) | (0, 1) |
| 1 | (0, 18) | (0, 18) |
| 2 | (0, 126) | (0, 126) |
| 3 | (0, 438) | (0, 438) |
| 4 | (0, 801) | (0, 801) |
| 5 | (0, 748) | (0, 746) |
| 6 | (0, 316) | (0, 307) |
| 7 | (0, 46) | (0, 36) |
| 8 | (0, 1) | (1, 42) |
| 9 | (2, 20) | (2, 4) |

The table is an exhaustive finite proof certificate: `verify.py` specifies the two 27-edge graphs and iterates over **all** \(2^{18}=262144\) subsets of each graph, not a sample. Two independently implemented evaluators—direct per-edge counting and a recurrence that removes the least significant set bit—compute the same table. Every subset is accounted for once, and each edge contribution is an exact integer. The finite loop bounds, invariant and literal assertions allow independent reproduction without external packages. The edge sources and graph labels are also specified there.

Inserting these exact minima into the displayed energy identity gives the two claimed lower envelopes. For the first graph, the only strictly exposed positive-field magnetization sectors are \(M=2\) and \(M=18\), meeting at \(h/J=3\). For the second, they are \(M=0,4,18\), with crossings at \(h/J=1,3\). Every other sector lies strictly above the envelope except those tied at the stated crossing fields. At \(h=J\), second-snark counts \(4+42+36=82\). At \(h=3J\), all ground states are independent sets, with counts \(2495\) for the first and \(2473\) for the second, obtained by summing the table's \(f_r=0\) entries. At \(h=0\), spin-flip symmetry doubles the first graph's unique positive-magnetization minimizer; all four second-graph minimizers have zero magnetization. This completes all fields and all degeneracies.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the extracted package. The program verifies both 27-edge 3-regular graphs, enumerates each graph's complete \(2^{18}\)-subset state space in two arithmetic implementations, asserts every table entry, and checks the critical-field multiplicities and ground energies. It prints `ALL_EXHAUSTIVE_CHECKS_PASSED` upon success. No finite-size extrapolation is used or needed.

## Relationship to prior work
Orbanić, Pisanski, Randić and Servatius (2004) compare the graph-theoretic structure of the original Blanuša pair, including their different embedding genera; their accessible abstract does not present Ising ground-state counts. Strečka, Karľová and Madaras (arXiv 2015) develop the exact induced-subgraph method for magnetization of **regular polyhedra** and enumerate their field plateaux. That method is prior mathematics, not a claimed contribution here. The present calculation evaluates two different, nonpolyhedral 18-vertex snarks and extracts their complete field- and magnetization-resolved contrast. The Sage graph generator supplies independently documented labelled graph constructions.

## Limitations
This is a finite zero-temperature classification of classical Ising spins, not a bulk phase transition, quantum-spin statement, or nonzero-temperature result. The new numerical counts are established by exhaustive finite computation with a transparent all-state checker, not by closed-form counting formulas in a growing graph family. Earlier equivalent graph-table calculations could exist outside the examined sources; no claim of independently certified historical priority is made. The 2004 source's PDF was not readable through the available retrieval channel, so the originality comparison uses its accessible abstract and bibliographic record, explicitly leaving a source-access risk.
