# Dihedral first Hochschild cohomology of cyclic distance algebras

## Finding

Let \(n\geq 3\), let \(k\) be any field, and let \(A_n(k)\) be the distance algebra of the ordinary undirected cycle \(C_n\), defined below. Write \(D_n\) for the dihedral group of order \(2n\), acting by cycle symmetries; \(\varepsilon\) is its reflection-sign character, trivial on rotations and equal to \(-1\) on reflections. Let \(E_n\) denote the set of unoriented cycle edges.

**Theorem.** The first Hochschild cohomology \(\operatorname{HH}^1(A_n(k))=\operatorname{Der}_k(A_n(k))/\operatorname{Inn}(A_n(k))\), with its natural \(k[D_n]\)-module structure, satisfies:

1. If \(n\) is odd, there is a canonical \(D_n\)-equivariant quotient onto the edge permutation module,
   \[
   0\longrightarrow k_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\operatorname{HH}^1(A_n(k))\xrightarrow{s}k^{E_n}\longrightarrow0.
   \]
   This exact sequence splits equivariantly whenever \(\operatorname{char}k\neq2\). When \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), it does **not** split, even upon restriction to the order-two subgroup generated by any reflection fixing an edge.
2. If \(n=2m\) is even, there is a natural dihedral-module isomorphism
   \[
   \operatorname{HH}^1(A_{2m}(k))\cong k^{E_{2m}/\langle\tau\rangle},
   \]
   where \(\tau\) pairs each edge with its antipodal edge.

Thus, over every field,
\[
\dim_k\operatorname{HH}^1(A_n(k))=\begin{cases}n+1,&n\text{ odd},\\ n/2,&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]
The Lie bracket on \(\operatorname{HH}^1\) is zero. The main additional content beyond a dimension computation is the full symmetry-module description and the characteristic-two nonsplitting obstruction.

## Assumptions and scope

Index the vertices of \(C_n\) by \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with shortest-path distance \(d\). The unital associative \(k\)-algebra \(A_n(k)\) has one basis element \(u_{ij}\) for every ordered vertex pair \((i,j)\), with product
\[
u_{ij}u_{\ell h}=\begin{cases}
u_{ih},&j=\ell\ \text{and}\ d(i,h)=d(i,j)+d(j,h),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Its identity is \(\sum_i u_{ii}\). Associativity follows because a concatenation of three composable metric segments is geodesic exactly when the sum of their three lengths equals the total endpoint distance; in that case both parenthesizations give the same basis element, and otherwise both vanish. This is the shortest-path, or geodesic-composition, distance algebra of the graph; its multiplication depends on the full cycle metric, not just the adjacency matrix. Every isometry acts on basis elements by \(u_{ij}\mapsto u_{g(i),g(j)}\), and hence acts on \(\operatorname{HH}^1\) by conjugating derivations. All field characteristics, including two and characteristics dividing \(n\), are included. The result does not compute higher Hochschild cohomology, the magnitude (co)homology groups, or deformations themselves.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to diagonal edge data.** Put \(e_i=u_{ii}\). A derivation \(\delta\) can be adjusted by an inner derivation to satisfy \(\delta(e_i)=0\) for every \(i\): with \(h=\sum_i\delta(e_i)e_i\), the standard orthogonal-idempotent identities give \([h,e_i]=\delta(e_i)\). Thereafter
\[
\delta(u_{ij})=e_i\delta(u_{ij})e_j=\lambda_{ij}u_{ij},
\]
since \(e_iA_n(k)e_j\) is one-dimensional. The Leibniz rule for a nonzero product is precisely
\[
\lambda_{ih}=\lambda_{ij}+\lambda_{jh}\quad\text{whenever}\quad d(i,h)=d(i,j)+d(j,h).
\]
Conversely, these scalar equalities define a derivation; zero products impose no conditions. Residual inner derivations fixing all \(e_i\) come from \(\sum_i c_ie_i\), and therefore replace \(\lambda_{ij}\) by \(\lambda_{ij}+c_i-c_j\) (or the negative gradient under the opposite commutator convention). The quotient by residual inner derivations is the first cohomology as a dihedral representation.

For edge \(\{i,i+1\}\), introduce the clockwise and counterclockwise scalars
\[
a_i=\lambda_{i,i+1},\qquad b_i=\lambda_{i+1,i},\qquad s_i=a_i+b_i.
\]
The quantity \(s_i\) is unchanged by inner gradients. Rotations shift edge indices, and reflections permute them while interchanging the \(a\)- and \(b\)-directions. Consequently \(s=(s_i)\) is a canonical dihedral-equivariant invariant.

**2. Odd cycles.** When \(n\) is odd, each ordered pair of vertices has a unique shortest path. Its \(\lambda_{ij}\) is forced to be the sum of the corresponding oriented edge values; every assignment of \((a_i,b_i)\) is consistent. Thus the space of normalized derivations is \(k^{2n}\). The inner-gradient subspace has dimension \(n-1\), since \(C_n\) is connected. The map \(s:k^{2n}\to k^{E_n}\) is surjective, and its kernel has \(b_i=-a_i\). In this kernel, two assignments differ by an inner gradient if and only if the clockwise circulations \(\sum_i a_i\) agree. Hence the kernel on \(\operatorname{HH}^1\) is one-dimensional. A rotation preserves circulation, while a reflection negates it. This identifies it with \(k_{\varepsilon}\), including in characteristic two. If two is invertible in \(k\), the assignment \(a_i=b_i=s_i/2\) defines a \(D_n\)-equivariant section, proving the splitting.

When \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), fix the reflection \(r:i\mapsto-i\), which fixes the edge \(e=\{(n-1)/2,(n+1)/2\}\). Let \(v\in k^{E_n}\) be supported on that edge with value one. The vector \(v\) is \(r\)-fixed. For any lift represented by oriented edge scalars \((a,b)\) with \(a_i+b_i=v_i\), the class of \((1+r)(a,b)\) lies in the kernel of \(s\) and has clockwise circulation
\[
\sum_i\bigl(a_i+(ra)_i\bigr)=\sum_i(a_i+b_i)=\sum_i v_i=1.
\]
It is therefore a **nonzero** class, since inner gradients always have zero circulation. Thus no lift of \(v\) is \(r\)-invariant in \(\operatorname{HH}^1\), and no equivariant section can exist. All edge-fixing reflections are conjugate when \(n\) is odd, proving the full nonsplitting claim.

**3. Even cycles.** Write \(n=2m\). Only antipodal vertex pairs admit two different shortest routes. Equality of the sums along those routes gives, for each starting vertex \(x\),
\[
R_x=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_{x+j}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}b_{x-j-1}=0,
\]
where all subscripts are modulo \(2m\). Subtracting successive equations yields
\[
R_{x+1}-R_x=s_{x+m}-s_x.
\]
Therefore the entire system is equivalent to the \(m\) antipodal equalities \(s_{i+m}=s_i\) together with the single equation \(R_0=0\). These \(m+1\) conditions are independent over every field: after choosing any antipodally constant \(s\), write \(b_i=s_i-a_i\), so \(R_0=\sum_i a_i-\sum_{i=m}^{2m-1}s_i\), which can always be set to zero by choosing one \(a_i\). Hence the image of \(s\) is exactly the permutation module on antipodal edge pairs.

When \(s=0\), we have \(b_i=-a_i\) and \(R_0=\sum_i a_i=0\). Such assignments are precisely the cyclic gradients, because the only obstruction to solving \(a_i=c_{i+1}-c_i\) is the total circulation. Thus the induced map on \(\operatorname{HH}^1\) is injective as well as surjective, proving the natural dihedral-module isomorphism for even cycles.

**4. Lie structure.** Normalized derivations are simultaneously diagonal on the standard basis, hence commute pairwise. Every cohomology class has such a representative, so the Gerstenhaber degree-one bracket is zero.

## Verification

The standalone standard-Python checker `verify_distance_hh1.py` tests all cycle lengths \(3\le n\le16\) over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7\). It computes ranks of the full antipodal geodesic constraint system and the gradient space, constructs random admissible edge-weight derivations and checks the Leibniz rule on every ordered triple of vertices. It also checks rotation and reflection preservation, antipodal edge symmetry, odd-characteristic split lifts, and the characteristic-two edge-reflection obstruction. Its completion marker is `CYCLE_DISTANCE_HH1_DIHEDRAL_CHECKS_OK`; the actual recorded run checks 224 sampled geodesic cocycles plus all associated multiplication triples. The all-\(n\), all-field assertions follow from the preceding proof, not from finite enumeration.

Reproduce with `python verify_distance_hh1.py` using Python 3.9 or later, with no third-party packages. The checker and this manuscript are bundled together.

## Relationship to prior work

A recent study of magnitude homology for real hyperplane arrangements treats tope graphs as natural metric objects and explicitly discusses even and odd cycle metrics (Liu, arXiv:2604.03718v1, first public April 4, 2026). Liu's subsequent characteristic-two magnitude-cohomology-ring study treats rank-two arrangements whose tope graphs are even cycles (arXiv:2608.24869v1, first public August 25, 2026). Those works motivate the precise cyclic shortest-path algebra and modular comparison, but they compute *magnitude* invariants, not the first Hochschild cohomology or its dihedral-module extension. In particular, this result does not equate magnitude cohomology with Hochschild cohomology.

Asao and Ivanov introduced distance algebras in the Tor/Ext interpretation of magnitude (co)homology (arXiv:2402.14466, first public February 22, 2024). Cibils, Redondo and Solotar's Hurewicz theorem for Schurian categories (arXiv:1010.6296, Theorem 4.6) gives a broad abstract description of first Hochschild cohomology via additive characters of a fundamental group. That theorem is consistent with and can illuminate the nonequivariant dimension computation, but does not itself specify the present natural dihedral representation or its non-split modular extension. Published papers on Hochschild cohomology of special biserial algebras provide relevant comparison for dimensions but have not been verified to provide this precise equivariant theorem. The new claim is the complete family-level dihedral-module computation and the odd-cycle characteristic-two non-splitting, not the general Hurewicz theorem or the definition of distance algebras.

## Limitations

The result concerns first Hochschild cohomology of the explicitly defined geodesic-composition algebra, not arbitrary graded cohomology of the graph, higher Hochschild groups, or an identification of first-order deformations (which ordinarily belong to degree two). Some existing abstract Schurian or special-biserial results may imply the dimensions; the special-biserial literature has not all been accessible in full text. Independent mathematical review and historical-priority verification have not been performed. Computational checks are finite and are not a substitute for the symbolic proof.
