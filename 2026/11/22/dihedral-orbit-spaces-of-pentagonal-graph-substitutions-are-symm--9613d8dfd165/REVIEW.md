# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The argument lists all five maximal outer independent supports and proves the orbit map from one chosen join is both surjective and injective after division by its precise two-element setwise stabilizer, including the one-support overlap strata. Compact-Hausdorff topology upgrades the map to a homeomorphism. The crosspolytope-boundary sphere model is equivariant, the orthogonal change of variables conjugates factor exchange to a sign action, and the standard integral projective-space cellular boundary gives every claimed group. Exhaustive finite orbit and cellular-homology cross-checks are reproducible using `VERIFY.py`. No finite experiment is used as proof for arbitrary graphs or dimensions.

## Originality — PASS against inspected literature, with residual risk

The prior 2023 polyhedral-join work proves nonequivariant homotopy decompositions, not the genuine outer-dihedral quotient homeomorphism or its torsion. The 2023 forest-polyhedral-join theorem has a different outer-graph hypothesis. Related published reflection-quotient and projective-suspension examples concern path independence complexes or bounded-independence cycles, not these pentagonal graph substitutions. A related published wreath-symmetry result describes integral homology actions when the fiber has the homotopy type of a circle and a rational special-case quotient; it does not calculate the orbit space for the crosspolytope-sphere fibers in dimension at least two, or imply their two-torsion. A known general theorem on symmetric joins could conceivably subsume the standard last step, but the pentagonal geometric orbit-space reduction and resulting concrete graph family were not found in the checked material.

## Value — PASS

The orbit-space theorem is natural for the five-cycle, whose independent pairs form a single dihedral orbit, and applies to every finite nonempty fiber graph. It gives an exact infinite family of geometric quotients with dimensions and torsion groups determined uniformly and exposes new two-torsion in quotients of torsion-free graph independence complexes. The result is stronger than enumerating a single finite complex or repeating nonequivariant sphere-wedge counts.
