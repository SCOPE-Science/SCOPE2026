# Dihedral orbit topology of phylogenetic tree links through seven leaves

## Finding

Let \(K_n\) denote the simplicial link of the cone point in the space of unrooted phylogenetic trees with \(n\) individually labeled leaves: vertices are nontrivial unordered bipartitions (splits), and a simplex is a family of mutually compatible splits. Label the leaves cyclically by \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and quotient by the ordinary geometric action of the polygon group \(D_n\) of order \(2n\). Then, for all integers \(n\) with \(4\le n\le7\),

\[
|K_n|/D_n\simeq
\begin{cases}
S^0,&n=4,\\
*,&n=5,6,\\
\bigvee^{13}S^3,&n=7.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, the seven-leaf orbit space has \(\pi_1=0\), \(H_1=H_2=0\), and \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{13}\), all with integral coefficients. No quotient-orbifold, Borel construction, or equivariant fixed-point complex is substituted for this ordinary geometric orbit space.

## Assumptions and scope

For \(n\ge4\), put \(E=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). A split is an unordered pair \(\{A,E\setminus A\}\) with \(2\le |A|\le n-2\). Two splits \(A|A^c\) and \(B|B^c\) are compatible exactly when at least one of the four intersections \(A\cap B\), \(A\cap B^c\), \(A^c\cap B\), or \(A^c\cap B^c\) is empty. The complex \(K_n\) contains one simplex for each nonempty finite mutually compatible family. The group \(D_n\) acts by \(i\mapsto i+t\) and \(i\mapsto -i+t\), where \(t\in E\), on leaf labels, and hence simplicially on unordered splits. Its **underlying topological quotient** is the space classified above. The phrase “phylogenetic tree link” means the normalized all-split link, not the planar noncrossing subcomplex corresponding to a single circular arrangement.

## Proof

### 1. A genuine cellular model of the orbit space

Barycentrically subdivide \(K_n\). A vertex of the subdivision is a nonempty split family, and a \(d\)-simplex is a strict chain \(F_0\subsetneq F_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_d\). Since the group preserves the cardinality of a family, no nonidentity group action can permute different vertices within one chain while preserving that chain. Thus the subdivision is without inversions; its chains modulo \(D_n\), oriented by increasing family size, are regular orbit cells. Their cellular boundary is the exact alternating sum of the orbits of the chains obtained by deleting one entry. This constructs the chain complex of the ordinary topological quotient, not merely a complex of coinvariant chains before subdivision.

The complete face enumerations of the original complexes \(K_n\), in successive simplex dimensions, are

- \(n=4\): \((3)\);
- \(n=5\): \((10,15)\);
- \(n=6\): \((25,105,105)\);
- \(n=7\): \((56,490,1260,945)\).

The successive barycentric-orbit cell counts are

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
n & (c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)\text{ with trailing zeroes suppressed}\\\hline
4&(2)\\
5&(5,4)\\
6&(37,95,59)\\
7&(232,1660,3072,1656).
\end{array}
\]

These are obtained by exhaustive generation of compatible split families, all their strict chains and exact orbit representatives, as implemented in the accompanying standard-library verifier. Every consecutive boundary composition vanishes over the integers.

### 2. The small quotients

The three vertices of \(K_4\) fall into exactly two dihedral orbits, giving \(S^0\). For \(n=5\), an explicit sequence of four free-edge/free-vertex collapses takes the four-edge quotient tree to a single vertex. For \(n=6\), the orbit complex has 37 vertices, 95 edges and 59 triangles. Exactly 59 successive free-edge/triangle elementary collapses remove every triangle; the remaining graph undergoes 36 further free-vertex/edge collapses to one point. Each step is checked against actual surviving cofaces; these are homotopy-preserving CW elementary collapses. Hence both spaces are contractible.

### 3. Simple connectivity in the seven-leaf case

The classical tree-complex homotopy theorem identifies the seven-leaf link \(K_7\) with a wedge of 120 three-spheres, and in particular makes it simply connected (Billera–Holmes–Vogtmann; Devadoss–Huang–Spadacene). Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem gives \(\pi_1(|K_7|/D_7)\cong D_7/N\), where \(N\) is normally generated by elements having fixed points in \(|K_7|\). Every reflection on seven labels fixes a unique leaf \(f\) and interchanges three disjoint pairs of other leaves. Choosing one such pair \(\{a,b\}\) gives a fixed split \(\{f,a,b\}|(E\setminus\{f,a,b\})\), a vertex of \(K_7\). All reflections therefore lie in \(N\); since they generate \(D_7\), \(N=D_7\), so \(\pi_1(|K_7|/D_7)=0\).

### 4. Complete integral homology, including torsion exclusion

For \(n=7\), the cellular matrices obtained in Step 1 have ranks

\[
(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1,\operatorname{rank}\partial_2,\operatorname{rank}\partial_3)
=(231,1429,1643)
\]

both over \(\mathbb F_2\) and over \(\mathbb F_7\). Thus \(H_1=H_2=0\) with coefficients in each of these two fields. To check primes other than 2 and 7, note that for any field \(F\) of characteristic prime to \(14\), the averaging idempotent on the barycentrically subdivided chain complex identifies the quotient homology with \(H_*(|K_7|;F)^{D_7}\). As \(|K_7|\simeq\bigvee^{120}S^3\), this gives \(H_1=H_2=0\) over every such \(F\) as well. The integral quotient homology is finitely generated, so vanishing in degrees one and two over all prime fields and over \(\mathbb Q\) excludes any free or finite-primary summands in these degrees.

The seven-leaf quotient's Euler characteristic, directly from its orbit cells, is

\[
\chi=232-1660+3072-1656=-12.
\]

It follows that the only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{13}\); the top cellular homology is free because there are no cells above dimension three. An independent signed Burnside–Lefschetz check gives the traces \(-119\) at the identity, zero at the six nontrivial rotations, and \(-7\) at each of the seven reflections, whose average is also \(-12\).

### 5. From homology to homotopy type

The quotient is a simply connected three-dimensional CW complex with trivial second homology, so Hurewicz gives \(\pi_2=0\) and \(\pi_3\cong H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{13}\). Choose thirteen maps \(S^3\to |K_7|/D_7\) representing a homology basis. Their wedge induces an isomorphism on all homology groups; the homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected CW complexes makes it a homotopy equivalence. This proves the claimed wedge of thirteen three-spheres.

## Verification

Run, with Python 3 and no third-party packages:

```bash

python3 verify_tree_burnside.py
```

The first verifier enumerates all splits, compatible faces, chains and dihedral orbit cells for \(4\le n\le7\), constructs exact integer cellular boundaries, checks each \(\partial^2=0\), checks every small-case elementary collapse on the current active complex, and independently computes the two modular rank triples for seven leaves. Its terminal success marker is `DIHEDRAL_PHYLOGENETIC_SPLIT_QUOTIENT_CERTIFICATE_OK`. The second verifier independently enumerates the ordinary seven-leaf faces and computes signed fixed-face Lefschetz traces for all fourteen group elements; it checks that their Burnside average is \(-12\). Its terminal success marker is `BURNSIDE_7_TREE_LINK_EULER_OK`. Exact finite enumeration, rather than sampling or extrapolation, underlies all enumerated assertions. The general deductions additionally use the cited classical topology theorems.

## Relationship to prior work

Billera, Holmes and Vogtmann introduce the relevant tree space and its normalized split link. Ardila and Klivans identify its Bergman-complex/partition-lattice structure, and Devadoss, Huang and Spadacene derive its nonequivariant sphere-wedge homotopy type. These furnish the underlying object and known topology **before quotienting**. Hultman studies tree spaces and subspaces subject to symmetry conditions; taking fixed loci of trees is not the same as taking the geometric orbit space of all trees. Armstrong supplies a general fundamental-group method, but neither the rank, two- and seven-primary torsion exclusions, nor the small cases' collapses. A six-label **proper partition** complex with hexagon symmetry and a seven-label **noncrossing partition** complex with heptagon symmetry are not these seven-leaf unrooted split complexes with full \(D_7\)-action. In the published literature inspected for this calculation, no prior statement was found that entails all the specified ordinary dihedral orbit homotopy types. An unindexed equivalent result may still exist.

## Limitations

The classification is only for four, five, six and seven leaves; it does not claim a stable formula for larger leaf counts. The computation is exact but uses finite exhaustive enumeration; the argument's conceptual topological steps rely on established external theorems. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed. Publication-priority uncertainty remains, especially for unindexed sources treating automorphism quotients of tree complexes.
