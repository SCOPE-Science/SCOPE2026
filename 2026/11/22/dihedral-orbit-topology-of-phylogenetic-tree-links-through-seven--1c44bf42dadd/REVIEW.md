# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Barycentric simplices are strict chains of split families; distinct vertices have distinct family sizes, which the group preserves. In particular no closed simplex is folded onto itself, and deletion of different ranks cannot identify two boundary faces of the same simplex. The chain-orbit cells of the ordinary geometric quotient are regular and have the alternating deletion boundary. Independent enumeration reproduced cell counts (2), (5,4), (37,95,59), (232,1660,3072,1656), checked consecutive boundaries and the free-face collapses at n=5,6 (four edge–vertex steps at n=5; 59 triangle–edge and 36 edge–vertex steps at n=6), and reproduced the n=7 rank triple (231,1429,1643) over both F₂ and F₇. For n=7, root at an outgroup leaf: compatibility becomes nested-or-disjoint for the sides omitting that leaf, giving the unquotiented rooted link on six leaves and its known wedge of 120 three-spheres. It is simply connected. Every reflection fixes a split formed by its fixed leaf and one interchanged pair on one side. For a direct simple-connectivity check, edge loops in the quotient lift edge by edge to paths from x to gx by choosing each representative at the current lift. A path from x to a reflection's fixed vertex followed by the reflection of the reversed path has the same projection as a backtrack and ends at the reflected x. Concatenations of translates of such paths give a null-projecting path from x to gx for any g; simple connectivity upstairs makes it homotopic rel endpoints to the original lifted path. Thus the quotient is simply connected. Away from characteristics 2,7 the exact invariant-chain functor gives zero low homology from the unquotiented link. Together with the two reproduced modular ranks, UCT and finite generation exclude all integral H₁ and H₂. The orbit Euler characteristic −12 gives free H₃ of rank 13 (the dimension is three). Hurewicz represents a basis by spheres, and the resulting wedge map is a homology isomorphism of simply connected CW complexes, hence a homotopy equivalence. At n=4 the two orbit vertices give S⁰.

The complete small-order classification locates the first noncontractible quotient above the disconnected four-leaf case and distinguishes a clean integral 13-sphere wedge at seven leaves from possible bad-prime torsion. The topology is supported by a short explicit geometric model and reproducible exact certificates rather than numerical inference.
