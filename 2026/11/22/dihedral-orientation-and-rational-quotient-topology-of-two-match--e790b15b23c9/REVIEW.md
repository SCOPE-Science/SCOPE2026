# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every forbidden set has the form \(\{e_{i-1},e_i,f_i\}\). Removing the unique full-rim facet produces a subcomplex with a complete acyclic toggle matching: a proper rim selection always leaves a freely togglable pendant edge, while monotonicity of the rim selection and the matched-edge direction forbid closed gradient paths. The resulting invariant quotient is an ordinary homotopy equivalence to an \((n-1)\)-sphere with the actual rim-edge permutation action. Reflection fixed-edge counts give the exact integral orientation signs. Rational orbit homology follows from finite-group averaging after barycentric subdivision. The standalone finite verifier was replayed and passed for \(n=3,4,5,6,7,8\); the proof holds for every \(n\ge3\).

**Originality — PASS against inspected evidence.** Vega (arXiv:1909.10406v2, §§2–3) already proves the underlying sphere homotopy; it is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. The result adds the integral dihedral representation, an invariant contractible complement with an equivariant homology-equivalence map, the mod-four rational orbit-space dichotomy, and the rotation fixed set. Searches of comparable cycle independence, wheel matching and orbit-space results found no theorem whose statement implies these assertions for the fully whiskered-cycle two-matching complex. The indexed database is not exhaustive, and related thesis material could contain an unindexed equivalent.

**Value — PASS.** The core question is what remains of a known natural sphere under its full graph symmetry. The answer distinguishes cycle lengths whose **nonequivariant** homotopy types have exactly the same pattern but whose full-symmetry quotient retains or loses its top class. This is a complete integral character for every cycle length, together with a simple invariant-facet mechanism and a concrete geometric fixed-point calculation, not a recertification of a published sphere count.

The closest source is Vega, *2-Matching Complexes*, arXiv:1909.10406; forest bounded-degree results by Singh and published characters of other graph complexes do not cover the same equivariant object. Limits: no integral orbit-space torsion calculation, no classification of all subgroup fixed sets, and no independent external verification.
