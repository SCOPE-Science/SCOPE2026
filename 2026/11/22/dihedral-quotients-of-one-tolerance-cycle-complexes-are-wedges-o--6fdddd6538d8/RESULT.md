# Dihedral quotients of one-tolerance cycle complexes are wedges of two-spheres

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(n\ge4\), and let \(K_n\) be the **one-dimensional** simplicial complex with vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and exactly the cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) (no filled input triangles). Define
\[
X_n=\mathcal T_1(K_n)=\{\sigma\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z:\ \exists\tau\in K_n,\ \tau\subseteq\sigma,\ |\sigma\setminus\tau|\le1\}.
\]
Let \(D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\) act simplicially by \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\). Its **geometric orbit space** has the homotopy type
\[
\boxed{|X_n|/D_n\simeq\bigvee^{\lfloor(n-4)/2\rfloor}S^2.}
\]
For \(n=4,5\) the quotient is contractible. For \(n\ge6\) it is simply connected, has free integral second homology of the stated rank, and has no other positive-degree integral homology. In particular, no torsion or higher homotopy-type ambiguity is left in this classification.

## Assumptions and scope

All spaces are geometric realizations of finite abstract simplicial complexes with their usual topology. A tolerance face arises by adjoining at most one vertex to a face of the **unfilled** edge complex. This convention distinguishes \(K_n\) from the clique complex of any graph whose cliques might include triangles. The group action is by graph symmetries; the quotient is the genuine topological quotient with stabilizer identification, not a quotient of the chain groups formed by ignoring stabilizers. The theorem holds for every integer \(n\ge4\) without a parity exception.

Every singleton and every pair of vertices belongs to \(X_n\). Its two-dimensional faces are exactly the unordered triples containing at least one cyclically adjacent pair; there are no simplices of larger dimension. This gives \(n\) vertices, \(\binom n2\) edges and \(n(n-3)\) triangles.

## Proof

**1. The orbit one-skeleton is a tree.** Write \(Y_n=|X_n^{(1)}|/D_n\). Rotations identify all vertices. Two unoriented edges lie in the same dihedral orbit if and only if their cyclic distances are equal, yielding exactly \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) edge orbits. For each edge \(\{a,b\}\), the reflection \(j\mapsto a+b-j\) swaps its endpoints and fixes its midpoint in the geometric edge. Consequently the orbit of this edge is a closed interval with endpoints the unique vertex orbit and its midpoint orbit. Distinct edge orbits meet only at the vertex orbit. Thus \(Y_n\) is a star-shaped tree, hence contractible.

**2. Quotient attachments of triangle orbits.** The geometric quotient can be built from \(Y_n\) by attaching, for each \(D_n\)-orbit of triangular faces, a copy of the triangle divided by its setwise stabilizer. This follows from the finite simplicial action (or, to obtain a strict CW subdivision, from the barycentric subdivision). Every relevant triangle has stabilizer either trivial or generated by one reflection. Indeed a nontrivial rotation preserving a three-element subset must act there as a three-cycle; such a triple has three equally spaced vertices and, for \(n\ge4\), contains no cycle edge, so it is not a face of \(X_n\).

For **trivial stabilizer**, the triangle orbit supplies one ordinary two-cell whose entire attaching loop lies in the contractible tree \(Y_n\); it therefore contributes one wedge summand \(S^2\). For a **reflection stabilizer**, the orbit triangle is a half-triangle disk. Its two boundary arcs inherited from original edges lie in \(Y_n\), whereas the mirror segment from the fixed vertex to the midpoint of the opposite edge is a new interval joining two points of \(Y_n\). The half-triangle two-cell cancels this new one-cell: adjoining them changes neither homotopy type nor integral homology. Mirror segments from distinct triangle orbits occur in disjoint triangle interiors, so these cancellations do not interfere. All remaining attaching loops factor through \(Y_n\); collapsing this contractible CW subcomplex therefore identifies the quotient up to homotopy with a wedge of one two-sphere per triangle orbit with trivial stabilizer.

**3. Counting exactly those orbits.** Every triangle face either consists of three consecutive cyclic vertices, or has a *unique* cycle edge. Each consecutive triple admits the reflection exchanging its endpoints while fixing its middle vertex, and therefore contributes no sphere.

For the second type, use dihedral symmetry to normalize the unique cycle edge to \(\{0,1\}\). The third vertex is an integer \(k\in\{3,4,\ldots,n-2\}\), a set of cardinality \(n-4\) (empty when \(n=4\)). The only nonidentity dihedral transformation preserving that edge is its reflection \(j\mapsto1-j\), taking \(k\mapsto n+1-k\) modulo \(n\). For even \(n\) this involution has no fixed allowed \(k\); for odd \(n\) it fixes exactly \(k=(n+1)/2\), whose triangle has a reflection stabilizer and contributes no sphere. Every remaining two-element orbit describes precisely one triangle orbit with trivial stabilizer. The number of such orbits is therefore
\[
\frac{n-4-\mathbf1_{\{n\text{ odd}\}}}{2}=\left\lfloor\frac{n-4}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
Combining Steps 1–3 proves the asserted homotopy equivalence, including its integral homology and fundamental-group consequences. \(\square\)

## Verification

A standalone verifier, `verify_cycle_tolerance_quotient.py`, reconstructs the actual simplicial face set and the complete quotient of its barycentric subdivision by \(D_n\). It takes orbits of face flags, forms exact integer boundary matrices in dimensions one and two, checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), eliminates a spanning tree by unit operations and computes the Smith normal form of the remaining matrix. For every \(4\le n\le20\), it obtains zero first homology, torsion-free second homology of rank \(\lfloor(n-4)/2\rfloor\), and a separate direct stabilizer-orbit count agreeing with the same rank. The verifier uses only finite integer calculations; **the all-parameter proof is Steps 1–3**, not an inference from these tests.

Run the checker with `python verify_cycle_tolerance_quotient.py 20` (Python 3 and SymPy). The included checker itself is the complete reproducible artifact; no downloaded paper, external dataset or unstated private file is required.

## Relationship to prior work

The tolerance construction and its Leray-number motivation appear in Kim–Lew (2023). Selvaraja (first posted 10 November 2026) relates tolerance complexes to squarefree symbolic powers and proves collapsibility and Leray results, including top-dimensional nonvanishing criteria. These do not determine the actual dihedral quotient homotopy type for the unfilled cycles considered here. A previously obtained nonequivariant graph-wide one-tolerance classification already gives, as a specialization,
\[
|X_n|\simeq\bigvee^{(n^2-3n-2)/2}S^2\qquad(n\ge4).
\]
This nonequivariant statement is **not** the new claim. It cannot by itself determine the orbit-space homotopy type, which depends on cell stabilizers. The new result gives a complete *integral* homotopy classification of a natural symmetry quotient and contrasts quadratic underlying sphere growth with linear quotient sphere growth. No claim of independent external certification or exhaustive historical priority is made.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the one-tolerance complex of an unfilled simple cycle and its *full* dihedral quotient; it does not classify quotients under proper rotation subgroups, tolerance parameters greater than one, or general graph automorphism groups. The mathematical argument proves the result for all permitted orders; finite Smith computations are corroborating evidence only. An unindexed equivalent quotient construction remains a priority risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
