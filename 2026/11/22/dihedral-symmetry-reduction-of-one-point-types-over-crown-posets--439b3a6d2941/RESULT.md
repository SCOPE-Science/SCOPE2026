# Dihedral symmetry reduction of one-point types over crown posets

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge3\), define the finite height-two **crown** \(C_n\) on minima \(a_i\) and maxima \(b_i\), indexed modulo \(n\), with precisely \(a_i<b_i\) and \(a_{i+1}<b_i\). Embed \(C_n\) into the countable universal homogeneous poset \(\mathbb P\). Each old vertex is initially an individually named parameter. Let \(Q_n\) denote the number of orbits of the finite **parameter automorphism group** \(\operatorname{Aut}(C_n)\) on complete nonalgebraic one-point types over that named base. Equivalently, \(Q_n\) counts one-point extensions of the **unlabeled** crown up to isomorphism fixing the distinguished new point.

Write \(F_0=0,F_1=1\) and \(L_0=2,L_1=1\) for the Fibonacci and Lucas sequences, and \(\varphi\) for Euler's totient. The exact count is
\[
\boxed{Q_n=2+F_{n+3}+\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(n/d)L_{2d}.}
\]
The first values at \(n=3,4,5,6,7,8,9,10\) are respectively \(18,30,50,96,180,376,794,1764\). In contrast, the individually named number of nonalgebraic one-point types is \(2L_{2n}-1+4n\).

The exact orbit decomposition consists of two one-sided families governed by order-ideal orbits, with the all-incomparable type shared between them, and **three additional orbits of two-sided types**. For the one-sided family, nontrivial stabilizers are unavoidable at \(n=3\), while exactly eight ideals have trivial stabilizer at \(n=4\), the sharp first occurrence. In the full one-point type space, the proportion whose parameter-automorphism stabilizer is nontrivial is \(O(n\phi^{-n})\), where \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Consequently \(Q_n\sim L_{2n}/n\).

## Assumptions and scope
An admissible new point is distinct from every old vertex. No extra relation or linear extension is named. The group \(\operatorname{Aut}(C_n)\cong D_n\) has order \(2n\), acts by rank-preserving rotations and reflections on the old vertices, and acts on pointed extension types by permuting parameters. Each finite pointed extension pattern occurs in \(\mathbb P\) and determines a complete nonalgebraic one-point type by homogeneity and the one-point extension property. The terms “one-sided” and “two-sided” concern whether the new point has old predecessors and successors, respectively. For \(n\ge3\) the old comparability graph is an even simple cycle of length \(2n\); the two-vertex degenerate crown is excluded.

## Proof
**1. Extension profiles and the mixed sector.** For a new point \(x\) define \(D=\{p\in C_n:p<x\}\) and \(U=\{p\in C_n:x<p\}\). Necessarily \(D\) is an ideal, \(U\) is a filter, and \(d<u\) for every \(d\in D\), \(u\in U\). Conversely, these conditions define an induced one-point extension without creating new comparisons among old elements; the universal homogeneous poset realizes it. These conditions are invariant under every parameter automorphism.

If \(D\) and \(U\) are both nonempty, they can contain only minima and maxima, respectively. Since each minimal vertex has exactly two maximal neighbors and vice versa, compatible nonempty rectangular pairs \(D\times U\) are exactly: (i) a single comparable pair \(\{a_i\},\{b_j\}\), of which there are \(2n\); (ii) \(\{a_i\}\) with its two upper neighbors, of which there are \(n\); and (iii) the two lower neighbors of \(\{b_j\}\), of which there are \(n\). No larger rectangle occurs because two distinct minimal vertices share at most one upper neighbor. The dihedral group is transitive separately on each of these three families (in the first, it is transitive on cycle edges). Their profiles have distinct lower/upper sizes, so there are precisely three mixed orbits and \(4n\) mixed profiles.

**2. Reduction to order-ideal orbits.** Write \(J_n\) for the number of ideals of \(C_n\), and \(B_n\) for their number of dihedral orbits. Profiles with \(U=\varnothing\) correspond equivariantly to ideals \(D\), and profiles with \(D=\varnothing\) correspond to filters \(U\). Complementation identifies filters equivariantly with ideals. The all-incomparable profile belongs to both families, so these yield \(2B_n-1\) orbits. Adding the three mixed orbits gives \(Q_n=2B_n+2\). Without taking orbits the same argument gives \(2J_n-1+4n\) pointwise types.

**3. Rotations.** Every ideal is uniquely determined by its antichain of maximal elements. The comparability graph of \(C_n\) is exactly the undirected cycle \(C_{2n}\), so ideals correspond equivariantly to independent vertex subsets of that cycle. The number of independent sets of \(C_m\), for \(m\ge3\), equals the Lucas number \(L_m\), by the standard binary independent-set recurrence (with the two-vertex quotient interpreted as two mutually adjacent vertices). Hence \(J_n=L_{2n}\). A rotation advancing \(k\) indices fixes an independent set precisely when it repeats with period \(d=\gcd(n,k)\). The quotient incidence graph is a \(2d\)-cycle, or a single edge if \(d=1\), giving exactly \(L_{2d}\) fixed ideals. There are \(\varphi(n/d)\) rotations with this gcd.

**4. Reflections.** A rank-preserving reflection of the even cycle \(C_{2n}\) has two fixed old vertices; its orbits on old vertices form a **path with \(n+1\) quotient vertices**. Because the reflection preserves the bipartition, no quotient vertex has an edge to itself. Reflection-invariant independent sets therefore correspond bijectively to independent sets of this quotient path. A path with \(n+1\) vertices has \(F_{n+3}\) independent subsets. All \(n\) reflections consequently fix exactly \(F_{n+3}\) ideals, including both possible reflection axes when \(n\) is even.

Burnside's lemma gives
\[
B_n=\frac1{2n}\left(\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(n/d)L_{2d}+nF_{n+3}\right),
\]
and step 2 yields the displayed formula for \(Q_n\).

**5. Sharp one-sided symmetry breaking.** The six-element crown has precisely eighteen ideals. Directly classifying them by their maximal-element antichains shows that each has a nonidentity dihedral symmetry; equivalently, the direct exhaustive case check in `verify.py` finds no free ideal. In \(C_4\), the ideal containing minima \(\{a_0,a_1,a_2\}\) and the single maximum \(\{b_0\}\) has trivial stabilizer. The full finite enumeration finds exactly eight ideals with trivial stabilizer at \(n=4\). This proves sharpness of the first one-sided free action.

**6. Asymptotic free action.** Put \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). For every nonidentity rotation, \(d=\gcd(n,k)\le n/2\), so its number of fixed ideals is at most \(L_n=O(\phi^n)\). Each reflection fixes \(F_{n+3}=O(\phi^n)\) ideals. The two one-sided sectors thus give at most \(O(n\phi^n)\) total nonidentity group-element incidences with types; the mixed sector contributes at most \(O(n^2)\). The union bound shows that at most \(O(n\phi^n)\) types have any nontrivial symmetry, versus \(2L_{2n}-1+4n=\Theta(\phi^{2n})\) types overall. This gives the stabilizer fraction \(O(n\phi^{-n})\). From the same Burnside expression, the identity contributes \(L_{2n}/n\), all other contributions are \(O(\phi^n)\), so \(Q_n\sim L_{2n}/n\). This concludes the proof.

## Verification
The independent Python standard-library checker `verify.py` generates every crown ideal directly from the predecessor condition through \(n=10\), computes each group action, checks rotation and reflection fixed-point formulas, and groups ideals into explicit dihedral orbits. For \(n=3,4,5,6\) it separately enumerates all three-status comparison profiles of a prospective new point against the \(2n\) old parameters, checks transitivity without using the closed formula, and groups every admissible profile into dihedral orbits. It checks the exactly three mixed orbits and the eight asymmetric ideals at \(n=4\). An additional integer check covers the formula for every \(3\le n\le200\). Recorded deterministic output appears in `verification_output.txt`. Finite experiments corroborate, not replace, the all-order Burnside proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The existence of the countable universal homogeneous poset and its finite one-point extension property is classical, and the dihedral automorphisms of crown posets and Fibonacci/Lucas enumerations of independent sets are established inputs. The new object compared here is the setwise-stabilizer quotient of complete singleton extension types: it differs from both ordinary pointwise named type counts and enumeration of all Heyting endomorphisms of crown ideal algebras. Earlier published work on cyclic three-incidence posets counts **named** extension profiles, not their dihedral orbits; the cyclic triple-incidence graph is also different from the ordinary crown. The previously published crown endomorphism census uses Lucas numbers for total ideals, but does not compute the exact action of the parameter automorphism group on pointed one-point extensions. Published work on marked random-poset reducts explicitly studies pointwise rather than setwise stabilizers. The new formula refines the classical Lucas count by retaining symmetry, with identical fixed-ideal counts for every rank-preserving reflection and a precise three-orbit mixed correction.

## Limitations
The formula applies to ordinary crowns \(n\ge3\), to nonalgebraic singleton types and to the **rank-preserving automorphism group** of the old crown; adding order-reversing symmetries or additional named relations would alter the count. This does not classify pointed extensions over arbitrary height-two posets or higher-arity types. The Fibonacci and Lucas identities and Burnside's lemma are classical tools. Older enumerative work on cyclic-poset ideal orbits could conceivably contain an equivalent formula not found in the inspected sources. The novelty assessment is a self-review, not independent priority certification.
