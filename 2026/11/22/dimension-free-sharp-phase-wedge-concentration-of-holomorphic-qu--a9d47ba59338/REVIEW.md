# Scientific review: dimension-free phase-wedge concentration

## Correctness — PASS

The object is the orthogonal compression of multiplication by the relative-phase indicator to exact-degree holomorphic polynomial spaces on the complex unit sphere. The measure is Haar probability surface measure and the aperture is strictly between zero and \(\pi\). The proof derives every block entry from normalized Dirichlet moments and a uniform relative-phase Fourier coefficient; it accounts for the spectator multiindex multiplicities, including the \(d=2\) boundary. Multiplication by the first two coordinates makes each degree-\(k\) Rayleigh quotient a convex combination of degree-\(k+1\) quotients. This independently establishes the all-degree sharp extremal reduction. An explicit three-by-three diagonalization provides all quadratic eigenvalues, exact multiplicities, half-aperture coalescence, and sharp condition number. Strict positivity is proved analytically, and the exponential high-degree trial-polynomial estimates have geometric bases below one. A checker supplies independent finite quadrature, symbolic factorization and finite-dimensional matrix tests, but is not substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated priority risk

The strongest general antecedent inspected is Quiroga-Barranco, *Toeplitz operators, torus invariance and quasi-homogeneous symbols* (arXiv:2106.01980, Sections 1 and 7): it explicitly supplies monomial and representation-theoretic Toeplitz block methods. The generic blocking principle itself is not claimed new. The inspected source does not present this relative-phase spherical mask, evaluate its exact quadratic coupled spectrum, or determine the universal sharp recovery number and all-degree extremal comparison. Pau–Perälä (arXiv:1805.04339) concerns Hardy-ball Toeplitz-type boundedness; Michel–Plattner–Seibert (arXiv:2103.14650) concerns Slepian constructions on the real sphere. Closely related previously recorded results for two-coordinate **modulus** caps were read in full: these have independently phase-invariant masks and diagonal monomial spectra, so their conclusions do not imply the present off-diagonal relative-phase spectrum. Earlier angular Toeplitz formulas may allow the claim to be derived without a separately stated theorem. Consequently historical priority remains uncertain, despite the concrete negative implication comparisons made against the consulted texts.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Relative-phase windows are a natural observation geometry for phase-sensitive complex signals and a meaningful alternative to modulus-only caps. A complete quadratic spectrum with sharp extremizers is nontrivial because the compressed operator couples monomials. The result shows that the exact worst-case stability is independent of all inactive complex coordinates (a structural statement extending across every fixed degree), gives an explicit sevenfold squared observation condition number for a half-phase aperture, and proves why no fixed-angle lower bound persists uniformly over growing degrees. These conclusions are potentially useful for choosing finite-degree phase-sensitive measurements. No claim of a new generic Toeplitz decomposition or general real-harmonic statement is made.
