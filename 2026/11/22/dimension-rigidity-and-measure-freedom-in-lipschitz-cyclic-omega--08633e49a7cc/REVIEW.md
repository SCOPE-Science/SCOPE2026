# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem has two logically distinct parts. For arbitrary Lipschitz triangular maps with periodic base limit, the exact cyclic surjections between compact limit sections follow from invariance of the omega-limit; Lipschitz monotonicity applies separately to the four stated dimensions, and the cycle makes every inequality an equality. For the converse, the binary prefix construction embeds a minimal adding machine as a bi-Lipschitz map on a strongly separated Cantor set for each dimension below one. The finite-level lower gap bound is uniform because the relative contraction parameters remain bounded away from one half. Standard self-similar cylinder and mass-distribution bounds supply positive finite critical Hausdorff measure for positive dimensions. At dimension zero, the displayed superexponential cylinder scales force upper box dimension zero. A full-interval tent model realizes dimension one. Independent Lipschitz extensions on finitely many fibre knots, followed by linear interpolation in the base, yield a global Lipschitz triangular map; a dense return orbit then gives exactly the prescribed cyclic omega sections. Similarity scaling realizes arbitrary *ratios*, not arbitrary absolute measures. The code checks 32,688 finite rational statements but is not a proof of limiting dimensions or dense orbits; those appear in RESULT.md. No infinite proof step is delegated to the checker.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
García Guirao's 2026 theorem gives a topological characterization for *continuous* triangular maps and continuous cyclic surjections. Its examples permit nonhomeomorphic and differently dimensioned sections in the continuous class; it does not provide a complete metric-dimension realization theorem for *Lipschitz* maps or arbitrary Hausdorff-measure ratios. Dimension nonincrease along each Lipschitz surjection is elementary background, so the isolated equality-of-dimensions direction is not claimed as new. The distinct contribution is the sharp simultaneous realization, including all dimensions zero through one, minimal zero-entropy Cantor models below one, and independently variable critical-measure ratios in one global Lipschitz construction. Balka and Keleti's Lipschitz-image theorems study metric surjections but not cyclic limit-set dynamics. Pawelec studies recurrence of the Cantor adding machine, whose dynamics is not new. Jiménez López and Smítal address topological omega-limit realizability of Peano continua. An earlier related result concerning cyclic interval forests addresses continuous maps with a different section class and does not imply this quantitative Lipschitz spectrum. Residual risk: broad fractal skew-product literature may contain an equivalent constructive package, and the entire 2001 article was not available for section-by-section comparison. The finding does not rely on absence of a search hit as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The motivating periodic-base omega-limit classification makes geometry of the individual sections natural: continuous cyclic surjections impose topological restrictions, while Lipschitz regularity adds metric restrictions. The theorem answers a complete, objectively defined spectrum question, showing that dimension equality is the only restriction on the attainable common four-dimensional value and that critical Hausdorff measure is independent data up to arbitrary ratios. The zero-entropy minimal realizations show large geometric complexity can coexist with simple return dynamics. All finite periods are covered and the two endpoint dimensions are treated, so this is not an arbitrary finite sample or a routine numerical increment. Its limits are explicit: prescribed arbitrary shapes and fixed Lipschitz constants remain unclassified.
