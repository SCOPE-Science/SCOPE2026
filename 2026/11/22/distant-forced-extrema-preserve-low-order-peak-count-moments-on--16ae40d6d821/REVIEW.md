# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Atom-free iid ranks give a uniform permutation; fixing extrema inserts them at the ends of an otherwise uniform permutation. Under each of the four rank laws, the relative permutations on disjoint subsets of unconstrained vertices are independent, and any forced vertices in a subset merely add fixed lowest or highest positions. Thus rank comparisons with disjoint supports remain independent. For an ordered k-tuple of local indicators, index edges connect pairs of intersecting closed neighborhoods, including repeated vertices. Disconnected tuples have zero mixed cumulant in all four laws. If a connected tuple includes both forced sites in its neighborhood union, choose the shortest simple index path between neighborhoods containing the two sites; at most k-1 hops of graph length at most two and the two end edges give D<=2k. Hence under D>2r at least one forced site is outside the support for k<=r, yielding termwise equality of the two-extremum mixed cumulant with the sum of the one-extremum cumulants minus the unconditioned one. Summing is valid by multilinearity. Transitivity makes each single forced location's peak-count law equal the unconditioned mixture, and moment–cumulant identities give ordinary moment equality. On Q_d the local peak probability is 1/(d+1), adjacent joint probability zero, distance-two joint probability 1/[d(d+1)] by integrating the two center scores with two common neighbors, and the rest are independent. With Nd/2 and N*binom(d,2)/2 pairs this gives the stated variance. In exact boundary checks Q2 has conditional K=1 versus unconditional mean 4/3; Q4 has the same mean 16/5 under both laws but conditional variance 6/7 versus 24/25. The proof's index path need not visit all k indices.

## originality

PASS

The model, the peak-count mean and the connected-support property for dependency-graph cumulants are not individually new. The supported claim is an exact finite-distance cancellation for simultaneous fixed extremal rank locations, for arbitrary matching order below D/2 on vertex-transitive graphs. Actual peers in the same rank model, nearby same-day covariance records and broader cumulant methods do not give that two-constraint result; the plausible Manukyan–Eppstein–Buzas source could not be assessed in full and is left as a residual risk.

## value

PASS

This is exact robustness of a global nonlinear count to a natural pair of rank constraints, with a quantitative distance range that applies to every finite vertex-transitive graph and grows with dimension for antipodal cubes. It explains why low-order statistics can tolerate forcing both extrema even though full invariance need not follow. The equality-boundary failures at the first two orders confirm that the separation condition has real content, and the argument does not rely on nearby indicators becoming independent.
