# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The statement has explicit finite-simple-graph, vertex-transitivity, atom-free iid ranking, positive-integer moment order and strict distance \(D>2r\) hypotheses. Prescribing one extremum has no effect on the invariant total peak-count law by transitivity. For an ordered tuple of at most \(r\) peak indicators, dependency graph disconnection forces cumulant zero; connection forces the union of closed neighborhoods to have diameter at most \(2r\). Hence both distinguished endpoints cannot affect one connected tuple simultaneously. The joint cumulant contributions therefore separate into the two one-endpoint effects, each canceled by vertex transitivity. The moment–cumulant identity supplies the all-orders finite theorem. Exact ranking and probability checks validate specified finite boundaries; the proof does not infer an infinite theorem from finite enumeration.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual priority risk.** The closest primary full texts are Hegarty–Martinsson (2012, House-of-Cards/constrained accessible paths) and Neidhart–Szendro–Krug (2014, local-peak mean and rough-Mount-Fuji topography). Neither inspected statement provides the distance-indexed *conditional cumulant/moment invariance*. The unconditional peak mean and variance have been excluded from the novelty claim. Published-record searches for constrained House-of-Cards peak moments, distant fixed extrema, and local cumulant superposition found no directly covering result. A generic dependency-graph theorem specialized to this problem, or an older fitness-landscape note, may establish equivalent coverage and remains an unresolved historical-priority risk.

## Value

**PASS.** For random fitness landscapes, peak-count fluctuations are a natural ruggedness statistic, and fixing opposite global fitness extrema is a natural constraint when studying adaptive paths. The new result separates sensitivity of global path structure to endpoint constraints from exact insensitivity of *every low-order* peak-count moment, with the number of protected moments growing linearly in separation. Its general vertex-transitive formulation reveals a locality principle rather than a parameter substitution. The \(Q_2\)/\(Q_4\) boundary examples expose the limits and rule out unqualified moment independence.
