# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact rank-one resolvent criterion and elementary cosine bounds show strictly negative growth for every positive rate up to the explicit crossover. A global derivative estimate proves precisely one positive crossover in the entire quantified gap neighborhood. Taking logarithms of the hyperbolic root equation and inverting an increasing map proves the double-log expansion. The finite root checker passes, and is supplementary rather than an infinite certificate.

## Originality
**PASS (literature-bounded).** The known critical habitat equation and the numerically plotted threshold belong to Menon, de Castro and Anteneodo (arXiv:2505.03727v1, §III.1, Fig. 3). The additional statements are rigorous small-gap uniqueness and the explicit two-term asymptotic rate. Prior work on stochastic resetting, reset-location dispersion, and first-passage optimization was compared by claim and assumptions. The initiating implicit equation contains the needed root information, so independent mathematical priority evaluation remains important.

## Value
**PASS.** The distance to the absorbing boundary is a physically meaningful singular parameter. The exact logarithmic and double-logarithmic intensity requirements reveal a severe limit on rescue by relocation almost at the habitat edge and quantify the numerical phase transition.

## Limits
This is a linearized, one-dimensional, perfectly absorbing habitat with a fixed reset point and no claim about finite-population survival. The sufficient gap neighborhood is not sharp. Historical equivalence cannot be excluded solely by literature searches.
