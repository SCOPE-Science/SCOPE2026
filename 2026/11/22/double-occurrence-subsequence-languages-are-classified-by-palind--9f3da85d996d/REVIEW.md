# Review: two scattered occurrences and palindromic triples

## Correctness (C): PASS

Two indexed embeddings form two elements in the coordinatewise lattice of increasing position tuples. Their coordinatewise minimum and maximum must also be embeddings; a mixed-prefix-and-suffix construction shows that exactly two embeddings differ at exactly one coordinate. For adjacency-distinct patterns, ordinary inter-letter gaps exclude both neighboring pattern symbols and the fork gap excludes the fork letter and its existing pattern neighbors. These conditions are necessary, and the leftmost/rightmost greedy embeddings prove they are sufficient: every embedding lies between the two extremes, whose only variable coordinate has two admissible positions. The resulting disjoint, independently filled gaps give the claimed rational series. Counting the interior neighbors equal to each other proves the dependence on the length-three palindrome count, while direct coefficient extraction and a double-pole calculation establish the classification and asymptotics. No unresolved lemma or numerical assumption is needed for the infinite claim.

The standalone verifier uses a logically separate capped subsequence DP, complete index-tuple backtracking, and a product-of-series coefficient calculator. It checked 534 parameter-pattern-length instances and 1,472,158 host words, with all 147,576 positive two-occurrence witnesses reconstructed and fork-classified. These finite tests corroborate rather than establish the proof. The assumption that adjacent target letters differ is essential to the stated gap restrictions; the theorem makes no claim outside that class.

## Originality (O): PASS against accessible literature, with a priority risk

Bruda, arXiv:2607.02578 (first posted 2026-06-30), was inspected through its introduction, Section 4.1 and Appendix A.2. It gives all-frequency structural statements in the binary case and the exact one-occurrence general-alphabet problem; neither implies an all-length formula for two occurrences in arbitrary alphabets with an explicit length-three-palindrome statistic. Menon and Singh, arXiv:2306.07870, Section 3.1 Proposition 3.4, were inspected in full HTML: they give the two-occurrence formula on a binary alphabet, where all adjacency-distinct words are alternating and have no choice of the new statistic. The general-pattern publication by Mansour, Rastegar and Roitershtein, arXiv:1905.05646, was compared at its definition and main statements: its order-isomorphic occurrence notion differs from literal symbol equality in indexed scattered subsequences. An already-established special case \(q=3,p=012\) is recovered and explicitly not claimed as new. The present claim is the two-parameter, adjacency-distinct language classification, including equal/non-equal two-step returns; this classification is not implied by the special case.

Relevant full papers were read where accessible. The record search included aliases such as scattered-subsequence multiplicity, fixed-pattern embeddings, and generalized subsequence-frequency enumerators, with comparisons of mathematical hypotheses rather than title alone. Residual priority risk: an unindexed equivalent formula, especially in an as-yet-unavailable related general-alphabet manuscript, could cover the new classification. This is not a uniqueness proof or an independent literature audit.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

A natural exact multiplicity class of the subsequence channel has an unexpectedly short disjoint language decomposition. The resulting partition of **all** adjacency-distinct patterns by the number of palindromic length-three factors is complete and sharp: for each fixed \(q,m\), exactly \(m-1\) different two-occurrence count sequences exist, and all classes have an exact labeled population. Already at host length \(m+2\), the class invariant changes the exact count by one per palindromic triple. For instance, the ternary patterns `012` and `010` have respectively 20 and 21 five-letter hosts with exactly two embeddings, despite having the same length and no adjacent repetitions. The full rational series and sharp asymptotic coefficient explain the persistent distinction, rather than reporting isolated small counts.
