# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is on the bilateral binary full shift. Exactness is converted into surjectivity of the restriction of the finite-dimensional kernel of the update to a finite set of probe sites. At dyadic powers the kernel is precisely the periodic configurations of that dyadic period, so injectivity of probe residues is both necessary and sufficient; no finite simulation is used to prove this equivalence. The exact counting law follows by selecting distinct residue classes with one site per class. The sparse birthday asymptotic uses a falling-factorial product and an error term \(O(k^3/2^{2m})\) that vanishes on the asserted scale. For three sites, the complete list of nonzero binary subset relations consists of odd-cardinality terms of valuation zero and the three pairs; the displayed dyadic valuation law is therefore exhaustive. The two moment formulas follow from the exact three-point CDF using finite tail sums. All integer and rational examples checked by the included independent program agree. Risk: the birthday limit does not cover the dense sampling regime, and no result for non-dyadic unrounded times at \(k\ge4\) is claimed.

## Value — PASS

Local eventual onto gives existence of a finite image-covering time, but a practical quantitative question is how probe geometry controls that time and what delays occur for random sparse observation patterns. The complete all-mask dyadic equivalence turns a dynamic covering problem into a transparent arithmetic collision invariant. The exact finite-size birthday law locates a sharp sparse-probe transition around the square of the probe count and identifies dyadic-phase behavior. The exact unrounded three-probe calculation reveals a mathematically meaningful separation between a bounded typical clock, a logarithmically divergent mean and a linearly growing variance. This is a natural, all-size dynamic observable, not a selected numerical instance or an arbitrary improvement in a constant.
