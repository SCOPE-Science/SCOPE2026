# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Positivity and equality at the Rayleigh quotient of 1 imply that 1 is an eigenfunction, since the positive operator ||T||I-T has vanishing quadratic form there. The discarded disks contribute exactly r_j^2 c_j^n to each analytic moment; the centered outer disk contributes zero for n>=1. The first q nonconstant moments for q distinct nonzero centers give an invertible shifted Vandermonde system, contradicting their positive weights unless there are no nonzero centers. Disjointness then permits at most one positive-radius hole. For the concentric annulus, the ratio of its nth eigenvalue to b-a is S_n=sum_{j=0}^n b^{n-j}a^j, with S_{n+1}<= (a+b)S_n, strictly for n>=1 since a>0. The n=1 mode gives necessity of a+b<=1 and the recurrence gives sufficiency, including exactly two top modes at equality. Without holes the disk eigenvalues are b^{n+1}. The normalized constant/linear block has diagonal m and b^2-a^2-2a|c|^2 and off-diagonal modulus sqrt(2)a|c|. Its larger eigenvalue gives the strict bound. Along signed real displacement t, the disk moment integrand extends using factors (u+t)^j and (conj(u)+t)^k. In a complex t-neighborhood with r+|t| bounded by a fixed rho<1, the resulting matrix series converges absolutely in trace norm (geometric decay times sqrt((j+1)(k+1))). This justifies isolated analytic perturbation with compact positive real operators. In the simple case the constant diagonal is fixed and the only first-order coupler is -sqrt(2)a to e1 with gap m(1-a-b), yielding the stated second-order coefficient; unitary rotation by pi makes the branch even, so the next possible term is fourth order. At equality the derivative on the double eigenspace has slopes +sqrt(2)a and -sqrt(2)a, and the max gives exactly the stated cusp. Rotation handles complex displacements. These are exact operator arguments, not extrapolations from a finite compression.
