# Edge-fault homotopy and primitive homology loss in Möbius-ladder total three-cut complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge4\), let the Möbius ladder \(M_{2m}\) have vertices \(\mathbb Z/(2m)\), rim edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and antipodal chords \(\{i,i+m\}\). For a finite graph \(G\), define its **total three-cut complex** \(\Delta^t_3(G)\) as the simplicial complex generated by complements of independent three-vertex sets of \(G\). Write \(X_m=\Delta^t_3(M_{2m})\) and \(Y_{m,e}=\Delta^t_3(M_{2m}-e)\).

**Theorem.** For every \(m\ge5\), the homotopy type after deletion of any one graph edge is
\[
Y_{m,e}\simeq
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\bigvee^{1+\binom m2}S^{2m-6},&e\text{ a rim edge},\\
\displaystyle\bigvee^{2+\binom m2}S^{2m-6},&e\text{ an antipodal chord}.
\end{cases}
\]
The inclusion \(X_m\hookrightarrow Y_{m,e}\) induces a surjection in integral reduced homology of degree \(2m-6\) whose kernel is a primitive free abelian group of rank \(m\) for rim deletion and \(m-1\) for chord deletion. No other positive-degree reduced integral homology is present.

The first Möbius ladder is exceptional: \(X_4\simeq\bigvee^3S^2\); deleting one rim edge gives a complex that **simplicially collapses back to** \(X_4\), while deleting one antipodal chord gives \(Y_{4,e}\simeq\bigvee^2S^2\). In the chord case four elementary collapses relative to \(X_4\) leave one three-simplex attached along its entire boundary, so the induced homology map has one primitive generator in its kernel.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are simple, finite and undirected. The complex is the **total** cut complex, not the ordinary cut complex, the independence complex, or a graph-edge matching complex. The edge \(e\) may be any rim edge or any antipodal chord; cyclic automorphisms act transitively on each of those two edge classes. The theorem concerns all \(m\ge4\), with the distinct \(m=4\) boundary stated separately. Integral homotopy type here means nonequivariant homotopy equivalence over the integers; the primitive-kernel conclusion is about the natural inclusion, not a canonical basis or equivariant splitting.

## Proof
**1. Bounded independence and duality.** Define \(B(G)=\{U\subseteq V(G):\alpha(G[U])\le2\}\). Since a set \(U\) lies in \(\Delta^t_3(G)\) iff \(V(G)\setminus U\) contains an independent triple, \(\Delta^t_3(G)\) is exactly the combinatorial Alexander dual of \(B(G)\). Deleting a graph edge enlarges the total cut complex but decreases the bounded-independence complex: \(B(G-e)\subset B(G)\).

**2. Connectivity lemma.** For every connected graph \(G\), \(B(G)\) is two-connected. Take a spanning tree \(T\subseteq G\). The subcomplex \(B(T)\) is contractible by successively removing a leaf \(v\) adjacent to \(u\): any face containing \(v\) can be enlarged by \(u\), because if adding \(u\) created an independent triple, replacing \(u\) by \(v\) would have already created one, a contradiction. Hence \(v\) is dominated by \(u\) and strong collapses reduce \(B(T)\) to one vertex. Both \(B(G)\) and \(B(T)\) contain every two-vertex subset, so their one-skeleta coincide. Adjoining cells in dimensions at least two to the simply connected \(B(T)\) cannot create fundamental-group generators.

For second homology, consider a two-simplex \(\{a,b,c\}\in B(G)\setminus B(T)\). It contains some edge \(ab\) of \(G\) that is not an edge of \(T\). Starting at \(c\), traverse the unique \(T\)-path toward \(a\) until reaching the first vertex \(c_s\) adjacent in \(T\) to \(a\) or \(b\). For every step \(c_i c_{i+1}\), the four vertices \(a,b,c_i,c_{i+1}\) are distinct and contain disjoint graph edges \(ab\) and \(c_ic_{i+1}\), and hence lie in \(B(G)\). In the relative chain group \(C_2(B(G),B(T))\), the signed boundary of this tetrahedron is the difference of the triangles \([a,b,c_i]\) and \([a,b,c_{i+1}]\); the other faces contain a tree edge and vanish relatively. These boundaries telescope from \([a,b,c]\) to \([a,b,c_s]\), which belongs to \(B(T)\). Thus every relative two-chain generator bounds, \(H_2(B(G),B(T);\mathbb Z)=0\). The long exact sequence gives \(H_2(B(G);\mathbb Z)=0\). Hurewicz now gives \(\pi_2(B(G))=0\), as required.

**3. Exact faces for the perturbed ladders.** For \(m\ge5\), \(G=M_{2m}\) and \(G-e\) are connected, triangle-free, and contain no five-cycles. Indeed, in a closed walk of odd length at most five, an even number of antipodal steps leaves an odd nonzero rim displacement smaller than \(2m\); an odd number of antipodal steps needs at least \(m\) rim steps, impossible within four rim steps. Edge deletion preserves these girth restrictions. In a triangle-free graph, an induced five-vertex subgraph of independence number at most two would have to be a five-cycle (minimum and maximum degree two); by \(R(3,3)=6\), no six-vertex such subgraph exists. Thus \(B(G-e)\) is at most three-dimensional. Its two-connectivity proved above implies a wedge of three-spheres, of rank given by the negative reduced Euler characteristic.

For a triangle-free graph on \(N\) vertices with \(q\) edges, vertex degrees \(d_v\), and \(c_4\) four-cycles, the bounded-independence face counts through dimension three are
\[
f_0=N,\quad f_1=\binom N2,\quad
f_2=q(N-2)-\sum_v\binom{d_v}{2},\quad
f_3=\binom q2-\sum_v\binom{d_v}{2}-c_4.
\]
Every allowed triple contains an edge, with the repeated counts coming from incident edge pairs; every allowed four-set contains two disjoint edges, with multiple perfect-matchings arising precisely from four-cycles. In the intact ladder, \(q=3m\), \(\sum_v\binom{d_v}{2}=6m\), and \(c_4=m\). Deleting a single edge gives \(q'=3m-1\) and \(\sum_v\binom{d'_v}{2}=6m-4\). Each rim edge lies in exactly one of the \(m\) four-cycles, while each antipodal chord belongs to exactly two; therefore \(c'_4=m-1\) or \(m-2\), respectively. Substituting into \(1-f_0+f_1-f_2+f_3\) yields
\[
\operatorname{rank}\widetilde H_3(B(M_{2m}-e);\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}1+\binom m2,&e\text{ rim},\\2+\binom m2,&e\text{ chord}.\end{cases}
\]
Two-connectivity and dimension three imply each bounded-independence complex is a wedge of exactly that many three-spheres (Hurewicz and the simply-connected homological Whitehead criterion).

**4. Pass to total cuts.** Integral combinatorial Alexander duality on the common \(2m\)-vertex ground set reads
\[
\widetilde H_i(Y_{m,e};\mathbb Z)\cong
\widetilde H^{2m-i-3}(B(M_{2m}-e);\mathbb Z).
\]
It gives free reduced homology of the stated rank only in degree \(2m-6\). The total-cut complex has the complete two-skeleton: deleting any three vertices leaves an independent triple in the ladder. For \(m\ge6\), this follows from \(\alpha\ge\lceil(2m-3)/4\rceil\ge3\) for any graph of maximum degree three; for \(m=5\), \(M_{10}\) is bipartite with five vertices per color class, and at least one color class has four survivors. The edge-deleted graph only increases independent sets. Hence \(Y_{m,e}\) is simply connected, and its sole free reduced homology group is in degree at least four. By Hurewicz and Whitehead it is homotopy equivalent to the asserted wedge of spheres.

**5. Primitive homology lost by the inclusion.** Because the bounded-independence complexes have dimension three, \(H_3(B(G);\mathbb Z)\) is literally the kernel of the signed boundary \(\partial_3:C_3\to C_2\). The inclusion \(B(G-e)\subset B(G)\) embeds \(H_3(B(G-e))\) as the intersection of \(H_3(B(G))\) with the coordinate sublattice spanned by three-faces of \(B(G-e)\). Such an intersection is saturated: if a nonzero integer multiple of a cycle is supported in that coordinate sublattice, the cycle itself is supported there. Thus its quotient is free, of rank \(m\) after a rim fault and \(m-1\) after a chord fault (using the known intact rank \(1+\binom{m+1}{2}\)). Both complexes have vanishing second integral homology, so integral universal coefficients turn this saturated injection into a **surjection** on third cohomology with primitive free kernel. Naturality of Alexander duality identifies the map with \(\widetilde H_{2m-6}(X_m)\to\widetilde H_{2m-6}(Y_{m,e})\), proving the inclusion statement.

**6. First-order boundary \(m=4\).** The eight-vertex Wagner graph has eight independent triples, and its intact total three-cut complex has homotopy type \(\bigvee^3S^2\) (previously established; this is a cited baseline, not a new intact-graph claim). Independently, the enclosed deterministic face-poset matching has one critical vertex and three critical triangles for the intact complex and for a rim-edge deletion, and one vertex and two critical triangles after a chord-edge deletion. A full Hasse-diagram topological-sort check proves each matching acyclic, so the stated wedge types are integral.

For the explicit rim edge \(\{0,1\}\), the twelve faces added to the intact complex collapse away in six elementary free-face pairs; hence this deletion is a simple-homotopy equivalence relative to the intact complex. For chord \(\{0,4\}\), the nine new faces admit the four relative free-face pairs
\[
(1237,12357),\ (3567,13567),\ (123,1235),\ (567,1567),
\]
where digit strings denote vertex subsets. The sole remaining new face is the tetrahedron \(1357\), whose four triangular facets already belong to the intact complex. This is a single attached three-cell, so the long exact sequence of the pair combined with the wedge-rank calculation shows the inclusion induces an integral surjection with a primitive rank-one kernel. Graph automorphisms transfer the two representative calculations to every edge in the same orbit.

## Verification
The accompanying dependency-free Python program `artifacts/verify.py` independently constructs each listed graph, enumerates bounded-independence faces, verifies the exact face formulas and four-cycle counts for \(4\le m\le8\) (with the five-face-free restriction checked for \(5\le m\le7\)), and checks the wedge multiplicities for \(5\le m\le8\). At \(m=4\), it enumerates the full total cut face posets, constructs all three deterministic simplicial Morse matchings, and exhaustively checks the complete directed Hasse graphs for cycles. It also verifies all claimed relative elementary-collapse pairs and the remaining tetrahedron. Running `python3 artifacts/verify.py` finishes with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the uniform structural proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced total cut complexes and studied broad graph classes, without this Möbius-ladder single-edge-fault classification. Carnero Bravo developed the bounded-independence Alexander duality framework and graph classes to which it applies, but does not enumerate the two edge orbits here. Prior published calculations establish the **intact** Möbius-ladder total three-cut sphere formula for \(m\ge5\), and the intact Wagner graph three-sphere wedge; these are explicitly used as baselines, not claimed as new. Other published computations about *ordinary* three-cut complexes, independence complexes or perfect-matching complexes do not imply the total three-cut single-edge deletion theorem. The additional content here is the edge-orbit-dependent integral homotopy types, their primitive inclusion maps, and the exceptional eight-vertex relative collapse geometry.

## Limitations
The general result concerns exactly **one graph-edge deletion** and the total three-cut parameter. It does not determine multi-edge fault patterns, equivariant homotopy representations, or a canonical basis for the primitive kernels. It does not claim that a simplicial collapse exists for every higher-order inclusion. An unindexed equivalent calculation cannot be categorically excluded. The reported computer checks are finite; the all-order result rests on the structural connectivity, face-count, and duality arguments in the proof.
