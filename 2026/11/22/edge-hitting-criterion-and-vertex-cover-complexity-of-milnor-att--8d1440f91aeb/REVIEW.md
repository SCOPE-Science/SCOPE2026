# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The graph-homeomorphism necessity follows because every essential open edge has a periodic iterate; every omega-limit from that edge meets its compact closure. The realizing edgewise maps are strictly increasing bijections with explicit normalized formulas and uniform displacement control. Only countably many midpoints and finitely many essential vertices can have limits outside the target; nonatomicity makes these exceptions null. Every target point is either in the closure of an open fixed portion of the target or approachable by an attracting complementary interval endpoint, which gives essential support and proves the *smallest* closed full-basin set, not just a full basin. Replacing finitely many edge-interior choices by vertices proves the exact vertex-cover correspondence. The independent rational checker reports 903 edge-coordinate checks and six graph-cover comparisons. Infinite statements are analytic, not extrapolations of finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
The primary 2026 source proves a root-contraction sufficiency theorem, interval/circle realization, and one tripod counterexample. Its general technique underlies the constructive half of the result; we do not relabel that technique as novel. The complete finite-graph equivalence and reduction to vertex-cover optimization do not appear in the inspected relevant portions. The comparison with classical Euclidean global-attractor literature distinguishes a different attractor notion, and the cubic vertex-cover hardness is explicitly credited to Uehara. Targeted overlap searches found no covering statement. A previously unnoticed graph-specific classification or an implicit deduction from broader attractor theory remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The source explicitly leaves realization on branched continua open after its tripod example. Finite metric graphs are a natural first complete branched class. The sharp criterion separates obstruction from construction for *every* closed target and converts the minimum-cardinality dynamical realization problem into a classical vertex-cover invariant, including a concrete complexity boundary. This is stronger than merely constructing another special singleton attractor.

## Limits
Results assume a finite connected graph, a nonatomic full-support reference measure and existential choice of a homeomorphism. They do not solve realization on arbitrary dendrites or infinite branched continua. The prior literature comparison is limited to the identified sources, and independent validation remains unperformed.
