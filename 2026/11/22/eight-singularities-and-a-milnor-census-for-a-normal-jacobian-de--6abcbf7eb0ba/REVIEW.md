# Scientific review: eight singularities of a normal Jacobian decic

## Correctness — PASS
The assertion is restricted to the genus-two Jacobian cyclic-cover construction of Chen–Lazarsfeld–Moretti and general choices of plane projection center and line. Their resolution incidences imply five distinct three-branch points, with intersection multiplicities \(2,1,1\), on the quadric branch divisor. Arithmetic genus excludes further branch intersections. Projection creates two ordinary fivefold points on the plane branch decic. The derivative \(\partial_W(W^5Q^5-F_{10})=5W^4Q^5\) then proves completeness of the surface singular locus. At the five triple contacts, an exact analytic normal form has Jacobian length \(24\); the smooth homogeneous principal parts of the two fivefold points have Jacobian length \(64\); the isolated weighted principal part at the vertex gives \(324\). The independent global Euler computation returns the same sum \(572\). The exact checker runs on each local model; its finite calculations are not substituted for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risks
The initiating 2026 paper proves existence and normality of the model and establishes the component-incidence geometry. It neither enumerates the eight projective hypersurface singularities nor gives their individual Milnor numbers. The related Artal Bartolo–Cogolludo-Agustín–Libgober cyclic cover of a genus-two Jacobian uses a different branch curve and is not an implication of the present complete basket. Classical weighted Jacobian-length formulas are openly credited and are not independently new. Targeted record-index, publication, and alias searches did not locate an equivalent explicit eight-point \(24,64,324\) classification; absence of a search match is not a uniqueness proof. The geometric configuration is special to the cited construction rather than a general cyclic-cover theorem.

## Value — PASS
A natural and mathematically consequential invariant of a newly constructed normal projective model is its complete exceptional singularity basket: the type and number of singularities describe the cost of replacing a smooth abelian surface by a normal hypersurface in \(\mathbb P^3\). The result connects the resolution-incidence geometry on the quadric to exact analytic local invariants and independently reconciles those invariants with the Euler characteristic of a smooth projective decic. The 572 vanishing cycles and the decomposition among three geometric strata are not just a recomputation of the known degree or normality. The result does not determine resolution graphs, special-center degenerations or Tjurina numbers.
