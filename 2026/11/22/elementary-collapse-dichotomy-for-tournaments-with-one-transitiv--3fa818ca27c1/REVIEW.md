## Correctness — PASS

The forbidden-triangle equivalence follows from the transitive ordering outside the hub. The nested-neighborhood fold proof gives explicit free-face pairs in the independence complex, and lifting each pair through the hub is an elementary collapse of the original complex, not merely a homology argument. The end complex is verified to be either a singleton or the three-edge triangle boundary, establishing the integral conclusion for all orders. An independent exhaustive face-level checker replayed 4,092 instances (orders three through nine); every asserted free-face condition and target complex was verified. This computational check is illustrative only; the universal proof is structural.

Govc's tournament fundamental-group freeness and finite directed-flag census do not entail all-order elementary collapsibility; the Burzio–Demaria nontrivial-pi1 characterization already supplies some first-dimensional information, explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. Deniz's accessible sectionable-tournament treatment uses a different structural class and does not state the all-feedback-one free-face construction. The first 2015 Deniz paper was accessible only by abstract, so a directly relevant uninspected potential specialization is acknowledged. published research records and earlier-claim comparison did not locate a statement that subsumes elementary collapses of **all** tournaments admitting a transitive-deletion vertex. Noncoverage by a search is not a uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS

The condition that one vertex deletion makes a tournament transitive is a natural sharp structural parameter (feedback vertex number at most one). The result provides actual integral simple-homotopy certificates, rules out higher-dimensional homology and contractible-but-noncollapsible complexes for the entire family, and gives a complete orientation criterion. In particular, the explicit strongly connected five-vertex contractible example shows the distinction is not just strong versus non-strong connectivity. Unlike small-order census work, the theorem is uniform in the number of vertices.
