# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is explicitly conditional on a smooth six-point blowup and absence of Eckardt points. In that setting the 27 (-1)-curve intersection graph has 135 *distinct* ordinary nodes and 270 branches; normalization therefore supplies the zeta formula over all extension fields. The graph's integral cycle lattice supplies the torus order via an exact equivariant sequence, without assuming a rational-to-integral lifting. The standalone finite checker reconstructs the intersection graph in two ways, enumerates all 720 six-point permutations, reproduces eleven orbit profiles, and cross-checks twelve extension degrees and two numerical bases. No experiment is used as a proof of the universal normalization theorem.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
The 27 lines, their intersections, and the ordinary/special real splitting types are classical; the rational split row \(27q-108\) is explicitly credited to the known Hirschfeld count. Polo-Blanco--Top's inspected full text classifies real involutions, rather than eleven general finite-field Frobenius types on the 135 nodes and 270 normalization branches. The inspected full published Clebsch-cubic calculation concerns an Eckardt-rich fixed surface, and the published Igusa fifteen-line theorem concerns a different nodal/triple-point incidence curve. published-finding corpus searches under the 27-line-boundary, 135-node and six-point Frobenius aliases did not locate the full eleven-row 27-line spectrum. The chapter by Hirschfeld was only accessible as its detailed publisher summary, which limits priority certainty; web and database absence are not uniqueness proofs.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete classification records arithmetic of the *reduced 27-line divisor* rather than simply the Frobenius splitting type of its components. In particular it detects fixed nodes with exchanged branches, six rational nodes on no rational line in type \((2,2,2)\), and two point-free boundary types. The Picard-torus order formula integrates these signed orbits into a natural genus-109 degeneration invariant. These are complete, natural finite-field invariants of an important geometric boundary across eleven nontrivial classes, not a chosen arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limitations and closest work
The no-Eckardt assumption, S6 blowdown subgroup, and conditional existence of configurations are essential. The result does not classify the complete torus isomorphism class. Prior split formulas and rational-line involution counts are excluded from novelty. Historical priority and all independent verification remain open.
