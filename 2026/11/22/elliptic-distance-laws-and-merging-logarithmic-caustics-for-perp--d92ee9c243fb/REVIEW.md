# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The probability model is specified by two independently uniform arclength parameters and exactly perpendicular unit-circle planes. Conditioning on one angle gives an arcsine density; averaging gives the two elliptic-integral branches. The integral representation proves normalization and exact support, and the classical elliptic-log expansion supplies the sharp local constants and collision scaling. The change of variables, moments, and endpoint laws were rederived analytically. An embedded checker independently compares both density branches to direct angular integration and tests sharp asymptotics and moments (61 checks). Numerical tests alone are not used as proof.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional
Actual literature and indexed searches were compared by object and implication, not titles alone. Finch (arXiv:1211.4514) computes convex-hull mean widths, volume, and surface area for perpendicular disks; Bäsel–Dirnböck (arXiv:1503.07399) analyzes oloid developable geometry. Previous published width results for similar perpendicular disks concern a random projection normal, rather than independent random boundary points. Same-circle line picking gives a different density. No stronger covering theorem was identified. Generic elliptic-integral and convolution tables are a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The cross-distance law is a natural geometric-probability invariant for orthogonal circular wires. It classifies every center separation in a canonical one-parameter family, identifies the exact two-to-one logarithmic caustic transition at circle intersection, provides a uniform critical crossover profile and recovers the geometric offset from one distance moment. There is no claim of generality for tilted/noncongruent circles.
