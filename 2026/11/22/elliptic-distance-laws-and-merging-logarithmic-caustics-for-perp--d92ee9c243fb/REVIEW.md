# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Expanding the squared distance gives Z=d cos t+sin t cos s. The phase shift in t conditional on s leaves a uniform angle; its conditional density is arcsine with amplitude sqrt(d²+cos²s). This proves absolute continuity (the zero amplitude at d=0 occurs on a null set), symmetry and normalization. The integrated denominator is sqrt(A−sin²s), A=1+d²−z². For A>1 factor out sqrt(A); for 0<A<1 the supported angular interval truncates at arcsin sqrt(A), and sin s=sqrt(A) sin u gives K(A). Endpoints give K(0)=π/2. With h=|d²−z²|, the elliptic parameter gap is h or h/(1+h), so both branches give π⁻² log(16/h)+O(h log(1/h)). This yields all sharp constants and a uniform O(d² log(1/d)) remainder on the specified compact λ sets. The monotone square-root distance transform has Jacobian r, gives endpoints M_d±1, and at d=0 gives the small-distance probability r²/(2π). Direct averaging gives E[Z]=0 and E[Z²]=d²/2+1/4, confirming the two moments and identifiability. Independent CDF quadrature plus central differencing matched eight density probes to 1.7e−22 at working precision; collision errors over five λ probes decreased from 6.8e−5 at d=.01 to 1.4e−8 at d=.0001. These samples supplement rather than establish the analytical claims.
