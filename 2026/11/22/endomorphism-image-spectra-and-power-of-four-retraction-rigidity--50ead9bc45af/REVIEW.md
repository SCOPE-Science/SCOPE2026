# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
For every integer \(r\ge1\), the quandle operation in binary parity coordinates is exact. The homomorphism identity forces a unique affine-linear parity action and an arbitrary fiber-shift function. The linear-rank, quotient-coset occupancy, and projection-kernel arguments prove both coefficient formulas with no field-size or parity exceptions. The conditions on \(L,c,b\) for idempotence have been derived by composition and rechecked elementwise. The bounded program exhausts \(256\) maps at \(r=1\), then exhausts all \(16{,}384\) uniquely parametrized homomorphisms at \(r=2\), checking operation, image-size and idempotence directly. Formula totals agree with normal-form counts. The experiments do not prove universality; the written combinatorial argument does.

## Originality (O): PASS with priority uncertainty
The closest 2010 and 2016 primary articles concern automorphism groups, which measure invertible endomorphisms, not the singular image-cardinality distribution or idempotent ranks. The 2016 paper explicitly requires no two-torsion for its Takasaki automorphism theorem; the present groups have two-torsion. Singh (arXiv:2311.03708) proves residual finiteness of finite-generated quandle endomorphism monoids, which does not supply enumerative coefficients. Recent published same-family research determines the full automorphism group and fixed-point laws and is credited as a constraint, but its indexed theorem does not assert the singular endomorphism census. The semisimple Alexander-quandle retraction theorem assumes a semisimple action over a prime field, whereas multiplication by \(2\) is nilpotent on the present exponent-four group; it cannot be applied to the nonsemisimple case. Search for equivalent formulations, strong prior implications, and exact numerical tables found no decisive covering theorem. The full text of the closest later indexed results was not retrievable, so undiscovered equivalent work remains a material, disclosed priority risk.

## Value (V): PASS
The endomorphism image-size polynomial gives a complete invariant across all ranks, rather than isolated samples or the already-known automorphism count. Retractions obey the unexpected sharp restriction that every image has a power-of-four cardinality and is itself an exponent-four core quandle, although ordinary endomorphisms attain sizes such as \(2,3,6,8\) already at rank two. This supplies a natural homomorphic-image/retract boundary in a family where nonaffine maps proliferate; it can be used in the study of subquandle retracts and finite quandle homomorphisms.
