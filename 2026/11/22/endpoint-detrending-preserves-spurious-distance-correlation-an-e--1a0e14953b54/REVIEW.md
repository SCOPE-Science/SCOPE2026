## Correctness — PASS

The single claim concerns two independent, endpoint-linearly-detrended random walks, their path-occupation distance correlation, an exact Brownian-bridge numerator mean, an exact Gaussian finite-grid mean, and the behavior of unrestricted permutation calibration. The proof states the innovation moment assumptions and the sample normalization, derives the functional central limit using the endpoint-pinning map, establishes strictly positive numerator by nondegenerate conditional Gaussian time covariance, and excludes unit correlation through the one-dimensional isometry implication. Full support follows from pinned triangular-wave test paths and Brownian-bridge support. The central constant comes from the cyclic symmetry of the bridge's expected absolute-increment distance and the exact beta integral. The finite Gaussian formula uses a circulant expected-distance kernel; the unrestricted-permutation conclusion uses an exact combinatorial expectation followed by Markov inequality. `CHECKER.py` passed 6,155 finite assertions, including independently centered finite-grid computations, exact permutation enumeration, and an illustrative 400-replicate simulation. Neither the numerical experiment nor the checks are presented as proof of limiting claims.

Its primary full text and proofs were compared with the endpoint-pinned case: the independent-increment theorem does not apply literally to globally detrended levels; it does not give the bridge expectation \(1/(3\pi)-\pi/32\) or the all-grid exact Gaussian expression. Its continuity and permutation lemmas are acknowledged as enabling prior ingredients. The foundational Székely–Rizzo–Bakirov distance-correlation theory is also acknowledged. Searches of related mathematical findings and the primary literature found no direct covering theorem. A sufficiently general, unindexed Gaussian-process distance-covariance calculation could subsume parts of this claim; no worldwide originality certificate is asserted.

## Value — PASS

Endpoint detrending is a natural operation on integrated data: each path is forced to return to its observed initial level, so a practitioner could plausibly expect that the spurious nonlinear time association would decrease to zero. The theorem rules this out sharply, and its full-support limit plus asymptotic permutation failure quantify the persistent inferential risk. The exact positive covariance constant and its complete Gaussian finite-sample formula provide a reproducible benchmark distinct from the previously calculated Brownian-motion value. This structural cyclic-distance simplification is motivated by the underlying bridge law, not an arbitrary parameter choice or a numerical slice.
