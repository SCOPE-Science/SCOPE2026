# Endpoint laws and quadratic variance suppression for ballistic threshold-reset averages

## Finding

Consider repeated ballistic descents from a fixed position \(a>0\) to zero, with instantaneous resetting to \(a\) and independent resampling of a positive velocity at each reset. For every integer \(n\ge 2\), let the sampled velocity have density
\[
\phi_n(v)=\frac{v_0^{n-1}}{\Gamma(n-1)}v^{-n}e^{-v_0/v},\qquad v>0,\quad v_0>0.
\]
For any bounded Borel observable \(f:[0,a]\to\mathbb R\), write
\[
\bar f=\frac1a\int_0^a f(x)\,dx,\quad
F_f(x)=\int_x^a(f(y)-\bar f)\,dy,\quad
\widehat f_t=\frac1t\int_0^t f(X_s)\,ds.
\]
In the unique stationary renewal version of this process, the exact identity
\[
t(\widehat f_t-\bar f)=\frac{F_f(X_t)}{V_t}-\frac{F_f(X_0)}{V_0}
\]
holds for every \(t>0\), including windows crossing arbitrarily many resets. Let \(U\) and \(W\) be independent, with \(U\) uniform on \([0,1]\) and \(W\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(n,1)\), and put \(Z_f=W F_f(aU)/v_0\). For every finite family of bounded observables, jointly and with convergence of first and second moments,
\[
\left(t(\widehat f_t-\bar f)\right)_f\ \Longrightarrow\ (Z_f'-Z_f)_f,
\]
where the primed vector is an independent copy of the unprimed vector (each vector uses one shared \(U,W\)). In particular,
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^2\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat f_t,\widehat g_t)
=\frac{2}{v_0^2}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}a\int_0^a F_f(x)F_g(x)\,dx
-\frac{n^2}{a^2}\left(\int_0^a F_f(x)\,dx\right)\left(\int_0^a F_g(x)\,dx\right)\right].
\]
The same process **started at a reset at time zero**, rather than from stationarity, has the limiting vector \((Z_f)_f\), and its limiting covariance constant is precisely one half of the stationary constant. For the physical position observable \(f(x)=x\), this reads
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^2\operatorname{Var}_{\rm stationary}(\widehat X_t)
=\frac{a^4 n(n+6)}{360v_0^2},\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^2\operatorname{Var}_{\rm fresh}(\widehat X_t)
=\frac{a^4 n(n+6)}{720v_0^2}.
\]
The fresh-start mean has a nonzero leading bias,
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t\bigl(\mathbb E_{\rm fresh}\widehat X_t-a/2\bigr)
=\frac{a^2n}{12v_0}.
\]
For \(n=3\), \(a=v_0=1\), the respective variance coefficients are exactly \(3/40\) and \(3/80\). For every bounded \(f\) that is nonconstant almost everywhere, the corresponding variance coefficient is strictly positive, so the exponent \(t^{-2}\) is sharp. The rescaled fresh-start position error is nonnegative, whereas the stationary limiting position error is symmetric about zero; neither is Gaussian.

## Assumptions and scope

The process follows \(dX_t/dt=-V_t\) between resets, with \(X\) instantaneously changing from \(0\) to \(a\) on hitting zero. At each reset a new velocity is sampled independently from \(\phi_n\). Values at the instantaneous reset times do not affect time integrals. The theorem assumes an integer \(n\ge 2\), \(a,v_0>0\), bounded Borel \(f,g\), and the stated stationary or fresh-start initialization. It holds for each fixed finite collection of observables. The Gamma distribution uses shape \(n\), unit scale. The velocity \(V_t\) is the speed of the current descent. There is no diffusive noise, holding time at the boundary, or velocity resampling within a descent.

## Proof

**1. Exact coboundary.** Since \(F_f(a)=F_f(0)=0\), the function \(H_f(x,v)=F_f(x)/v\) is zero immediately before and after every reset, even though the speed jumps. Between resets, \(F_f'(x)=-(f(x)-\bar f)\) almost everywhere and \(\dot X=-v\), giving \(dH_f(X_t,V_t)/dt=f(X_t)-\bar f\) almost everywhere. Integration across each continuous piece and cancellation at all resets prove the finite-window identity. Thus the integrated centered reward has no contribution from any complete descent.

**2. Equilibrium endpoints.** Let \(T=a/V\) be a complete descent duration. Under \(\phi_n\), the variable \(v_0/V\) is \(\operatorname{Gamma}(n-1,1)\), so \(T\) is Gamma with integer shape \(n-1\) and rate \(v_0/a\). Equilibrium renewal sampling selects a descent with probability proportional to its duration, then samples its phase uniformly. Duration bias changes the Gamma shape to \(n\). Thus at a stationary observation time, \(X=aU\) and \(v_0/V=W\), with \(U,W\) independent as claimed. The stationary marginal of \(X\) is consequently uniform, consistently with the earlier ballistic-reset result.

**3. Endpoint decorrelation.** Put \(k=n-1\) and \(\lambda=v_0/a\). An Erlang-\(k\) renewal process can be generated by retaining every \(k\)-th event of a rate-\(\lambda\) Poisson process with a uniformly randomized initial stage label. The label chain on \(\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z\) is a continuous-time irreducible finite-state Markov chain (trivial if \(k=1\)); its nonconstant Fourier modes decay as \(\exp[\lambda t(e^{2\pi ij/k}-1)]\). The endpoint state at a time depends on the Poisson events back to the previous retained event and forward to the next retained event. Restrict this dependence to an interval of radius \(R\): the omitted probability tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\), because the length-biased Erlang duration has an exponentially decaying tail. For two windows centered at times \(0\) and \(t>2R\), Poisson increments in the windows are independent, and the intervening stage label becomes asymptotically uniform by the displayed Fourier decay. First let \(t\to\infty\) and then \(R\to\infty\). This proves joint weak convergence of stationary endpoint observables to two independent equilibrium copies.

For fresh-start initialization, the same Poisson-stage construction has deterministic stage zero at time zero. The finite-state stage converges to uniform, while the truncated local Poisson increments converge to their stationary law. Hence the current \((X_t,V_t)\) converges in distribution to the equilibrium endpoint state. For moment convergence, \(|H_f|\le \|F_f\|_\infty T/a\). The current cycle length at any fresh-start time is bounded in distribution, for positive moments, by a sum of at most \(2k+1\) independent exponential holding times: at most \(k\) completed Poisson-stage intervals since the last reset, the interval straddling the observation time, and at most \(k\) future intervals before the next reset. Exponential moments of this dominating sum are finite. This gives uniform integrability of all fixed polynomial moments of \(H_f\). In stationarity the size-biased Gamma distribution also has all moments. Thus all stated first- and second-moment limits follow.

**4. Exact limiting covariance and concrete constants.** Since \(\mathbb EW=n\) and \(\mathbb EW^2=n(n+1)\), direct integration of \(Z_f=W F_f(aU)/v_0\) gives the covariance formula. The stationary difference of two independent copies doubles this covariance; the fresh-start limit has one copy. For \(f(x)=x\), \(F_f(x)=x(a-x)/2\). The uniform integrals \(\int_0^1u(1-u)\,du=1/6\) and \(\int_0^1u^2(1-u)^2\,du=1/30\) yield
\[
\mathbb EZ_x=\frac{a^2n}{12v_0},\quad
\mathbb EZ_x^2=\frac{a^4n(n+1)}{120v_0^2},\quad
\operatorname{Var}(Z_x)=\frac{a^4n(n+6)}{720v_0^2}.
\]
The fresh-start integrated error is exactly \(H_x(t)\ge0\). Its limiting law has Laplace transform
\[
\mathbb E e^{-s Z_x}=\int_0^1\left(1+\frac{s a^2}{2v_0}u(1-u)\right)^{-n}du,\quad s\ge0.
\]
The stationary limiting characteristic function is the product of this transform at imaginary arguments of opposite signs. The Gamma-mixture law is nondegenerate and one-sided, so cannot be Gaussian. The independent-difference law is symmetric but not Gaussian either: choose an interior interval of phases where \(U(1-U)\) is uniformly positive; the Gamma tail then gives a strictly exponential lower bound on the positive tail of \(Z_x\), and restricting the second independent copy to a bounded interval transfers that lower bound to \(Z_x'-Z_x\). A nondegenerate Gaussian has a faster-than-exponential tail. More generally, if \(f\) is nonconstant almost everywhere, then \(F_f\) is nonzero on a set of positive measure, and the nonzero conditional variance of \(W\) proves \(\operatorname{Var}(Z_f)>0\).

## Relationship to prior work

Das, Majumdar and Pal (2026) establish the model's uniform position marginal, exact transient propagators, and spatially dependent relaxation-rate freezing. Their paper does not state a finite-window coboundary for spatial observables, a non-Gaussian endpoint-difference law for their time averages, or the two explicit preparation-dependent variance coefficients above. General renewal-reward results, including Patch, Nazarathy and Taimre (2014), cover renewal-epoch rewards and standard covariance corrections. Those results explain why a centered complete-cycle reward can give a degenerate ordinary central limit theorem, but do not themselves state this threshold-ballistic all-observable endpoint limit or its Gamma-mixture coefficients. This work exploits full deterministic traversal of every location per descent, not just a generic renewal-reward approximation.

## Limitations

All results concern the prescribed velocity-resampled, noiseless descent and no other resetting protocol. The proof of endpoint decorrelation explicitly covers integer Gamma shape \(n-1\); analogous real-shape extensions are not asserted. Time averages of nonlinear observables are covered only when they are functions of position, not of velocity or jump count. No claim is made about equal-time multibody statistics, the exact finite-time covariance, or noisy flights. The broad renewal literature may contain unindexed equivalent formulations; independent novelty adjudication has not occurred.
