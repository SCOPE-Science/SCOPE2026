# Review of Engel-word fiber plateaus over finite chain rings

## Correctness — PASS
For every finite commutative chain ring, the identity \(\operatorname{ad}_{ah+be}^{m}(ch+de)=a^{m-1}(ad-bc)e\) is an induction in the defining Lie bracket. For fixed \((a,b)\), the target map is an \(R\)-linear map whose image ideal and kernel cardinality are computed without assuming equicharacteristic. Valuation counts yield the stated all-depth multiplicities; exact increment formulas prove the plateau set and sharp depth threshold. Independent finite arithmetic enumeration across ring characteristics matched the complete fiber distribution, but proof rather than enumeration supports generality.

## Originality — PASS (with priority risk)
The closest archival research on Engel Lie polynomial images is [1]; its hypotheses and conclusions concern Chevalley/simple algebras over fields, not finite-chain-ring affine Lie brackets or full target fibers. The broad survey [2] discusses related word maps and gives contextual motivation, while [3] concerns finite simple groups. Semantic database searches for valuation fibers, affine Lie rings, iterated adjoint maps, and stabilization returned closest records about star-graph two-Engel probabilities, affine-Lie Reynolds operators, and coadjoint Smith strata, none providing this law or logically covering it. A broad, not-yet-identified theorem on polynomial maps over principal ideal local rings remains a genuine literature risk.

## Value — PASS
The all-parameter finite-ring target census answers a natural word-map counting question rather than an arbitrary finite slice. The exact set of missing and present target-fiber jumps identifies an arithmetic phenomenon that changes with word depth, and the equality cutoff \(m=\ell+1\) is sharp. The ring-characteristic independence separates these fiber laws from more sensitive representation invariants and supplies a practical test case for general word-map measures on Lie rings. The short proof is a strength for reproducibility, not independent evidence of originality.
