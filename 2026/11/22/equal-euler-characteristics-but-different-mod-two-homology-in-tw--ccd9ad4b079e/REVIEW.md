# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The complete twenty-vertex graph adjacency lists are derived from the generalized Petersen construction and checked via the involution quotient. The bad-triple test is equivalent to shortest-path collinearity for all distinct triples. Face enumeration is exhaustive and downward closed. Both row- and column-oriented exact mod-two Gaussian elimination independently return the four boundary ranks for each graph; the chain-complex identity and Euler identity both pass. Therefore the finite mod-two homology claim follows, with no inference about integral torsion or wedge homotopy types. The standalone verifier was executed against the exact embedded code, not a different package.

## Originality
PASS against accessible inspected evidence. The full 2017 geodesic general-position source develops maximum cardinalities, while the 2024 general-position-polynomial source counts faces and treats the ten-vertex Petersen graph rather than the two covering graphs' homology. Published results for the two covers' matching and higher-independence complexes have different defining forbidden faces; their statements do not imply the displayed exact general-position boundary ranks. Direct searches for the two graph names, geodesic complexes, Betti vectors and mod-two homology located no equivalent or dominating result. Unindexed work remains a residual risk, not evidence of coverage.

## Value
PASS. These two canonical twenty-vertex cubic graphs share a natural two-sheeted Petersen quotient and have the same Euler characteristic and exactly sixty maximal four-simplices. Their sharply different homology distributions show that neither the shared quotient, Euler characteristic nor maximal-dimensional face count captures this geodesic-topology distinction. The finite complete pair calculation supplies a reusable benchmark for comparing visibility complexes; this value does not depend on claiming an infinite theorem.

## Closest literature and scope
Manuel–Klavžar (2017), arXiv:1708.09130v1, establishes the graph-geodesic notion; Iršič–Klavžar–Rus–Tuite (2024), arXiv:2401.05696v1, supplies face-counting methods but not these homology groups. Related published results on other simplicial complexes of these graphs are not direct mathematical implications. Only mod-two homology is proved, and an unindexed equivalent computation could exist.
