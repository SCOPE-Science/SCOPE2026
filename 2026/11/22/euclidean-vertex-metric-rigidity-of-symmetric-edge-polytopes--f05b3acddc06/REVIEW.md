# Scientific assessment

The distance-table decoder uses four squared-distance shells. Each signed incidence root is extreme and its unique distance-squared-eight mate is its opposite. Two distinct opposite pairs are adjacent in the recovered line graph exactly when their cross-distances squared take the values two and six. Whitney's triangle–claw ambiguity is resolved by the ranks of the **centered Gram matrices**, two and three; the raw squared-distance matrix ranks are instead three and four. Exact shell recovery under entrywise squared-distance error strictly below one follows from nearest-even rounding. Isolated vertices do not appear in the data.

The graph-isomorphism versus Euclidean-congruence equivalence for connected graphs is established in Cuchilla, Hound, Plepel, Vindas-Meléndez and Ye, *Unitary Actions and Equivariant Volumes of Symmetric Edge Polytopes*, arXiv:2507.18846v2, Theorem 3.8. It is not a novelty claim here. The result supplies a direct inverse on complete metric data by line-graph reconstruction without first realizing coordinates or reconstructing an ambient lattice, including a rank test and a deterministic noise condition. The congruence consequence is included for context. Unimodular equivalence, which only determines the graphic matroid, is a different comparison.

The finite checker corroborates the metric shells and intrinsic rank distinction but does not prove the all-graphs statement. The decoder requires all vertices in the canonical unweighted realization and a complete, consistently indexed squared-distance table; a distance histogram alone is not the input.
