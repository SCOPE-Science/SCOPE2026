# Scientific review: even-cycle obstruction to support-rigid bracket fibers

## Correctness — PASS
For an oriented graph with \(n\) vertices and \(m\) edges, the prescribed bracket character sum has Fourier transform \(q^{2(n+m)-\operatorname{rank}A_\lambda}\). In graphs with no even cycle the Pfaffian of any matched principal minor has exactly one nonzero matching monomial, giving \(\operatorname{rank}A_\lambda=2\nu(\operatorname{supp}\lambda)\); grouping Fourier frequencies by support yields the stated complete fibers. An even cycle allows two Pfaffian monomials that cancel or do not cancel at fixed support for every \(q>2\), proving the converse by Fourier invariance. The bowtie's missing-target formula is separately derived by a constructive determinant argument. All eleven configurations of a standalone brute-force checker agree with the matching expression, including characteristics two and three and the extension field of four elements. This is a general proof rather than an inference from finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, with disclosed risk
Goze–Remm (2015), Mainkar (2013), and Cavers et al. (2012) provide the archived Lie-algebra and skew-adjacency background, and the odd-cycle/unique-perfect-matching criterion is explicitly credited as known. Rossmann (2022) counts centralizers and conjugacy classes, which aggregate zero-bracket behavior. Previously recorded forest and bare odd-cycle prescribed-bracket formulas do not imply the full odd-cactus prescription as stated; their domains are proper subclasses. The inspected unicyclic Pfaffian result counts aggregate alternating-matrix rank frequencies, not the Fourier phases required for every target. The reviewed claim is the finite-field all-graph support-rigidity equivalence, matching formula for every odd-cycle cactus, and the exact bowtie image obstruction, not a claim of priority for the underlying spectral matching lemma or known cycle cases. Residual risk: a prior finite-field graph word-map theorem may implicitly contain this synthesis; the general proof is quite short given its known ingredients.

## Value — PASS
A finite-field Lie-bracket word-map invariant can reveal graph parity that aggregate commuting or centralizer counts alone do not characterize: for every fixed \(q>2\), dependence only on the target's zero pattern occurs precisely in the no-even-cycle class. This defines a sharp structural boundary across **all** finite simple graphs, not just a small census. The universal matching-number expression determines every target fiber for all odd cacti, including nontrivial zero fibers. The bowtie example quantifies a natural failure of surjectivity even though each triangle alone is surjective, a relevant distinction for bracket images and commutator width. The result requires neither downstream theorem nor exhaustive graph enumeration to have intrinsic value.

## Relationship to close literature and limitations
Cavers et al. (2012), Theorems 4.2–4.3, establish the known odd-cycle orientation-spectral phenomenon and induced unique-perfect-matching property, and are a substantial ingredient, not an unacknowledged novelty. Mainkar's graph-Lie classification has a characteristic assumption in its isomorphism theorem; the alternating bracket construction and present counting proof independently cover characteristic two. The 2022 graphical-group paper is not a prescribed nonzero-fiber theorem. The publicly accessible forest theorem explicitly restricts to forests; the unicyclic rank-spectrum theorem concerns ranks aggregated over frequencies. The general formula may take exponential time; for even-cycle graphs no complete fiber law is claimed. Historical priority is assessed, not independently certified.
