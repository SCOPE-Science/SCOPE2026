# Review of the two-level oxygen harmonic-response theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** A unique positive periodic oxygen solution follows from the strict one-period contraction and a positivity barrier. The periodic adjoint identity converts average oxygen into an explicitly calculable Fourier coefficient. Integrating both exponential adjoint segments yields a complex rational form whose putative zero would equate a unit-modulus phase with a complex fraction of modulus strictly smaller than one: the decisive inequality is \(\nu_u-\nu_v=(m_H-m_L)(1-E_H)(1-E_L)>0\). This proves nonvanishing for every positive integer harmonic, not just tested frequencies. Independent numerical integration of the periodic oxygen trajectory and explicit adjoint kernel agreed across 220 random parameter samples; additional equal-duty tests passed. The checker is supporting reproducibility, not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** The motivating 2023 lake hypoxia paper directly relates wind-driven mixing and temperature/solar forcing to bottom-water oxygen, but its spatial numerical model does not state the all-harmonic noncancellation theorem for the new scalar switched-rate model. The 2016 pond reaeration study and 2023 ice-covered lake study provide independent physical context but not the exact phase classification. The 2003 sediment turbulence study documents phase lag; only accessible abstract material was examined, so no whole-paper negative claim is made. Closely retrieved mathematical results address oxygen sags, climate-system phase lag and two-box tracer exchange, with different governing equations and conclusions. Standard Fourier analysis explains the method but does not itself guarantee nonzero response at *every* harmonic. Historical mathematical priority remains provisional.

**Value — PASS.** The exact noncancellation statement is a structural benchmark for temporal oxygen budgets: changing relative demand phase changes the average even without changing mean demand or the fixed high/low mixing schedule. Its especially informative boundary is equal duty, where direct harmonic matching to the raw mixing rate would miss every even-harmonic response. This provides a clean analytic test of methods that approximate intermittent mixing and heat/demand forcing by matching only Fourier coefficients of the external rate. No lake-specific prediction, management recommendation or numerical spatial-model validation is claimed.

**Limits.** Constant oxygen saturation, one well-mixed store, additive prescribed demand, fixed two-rate switching, and exact periodic forcing are assumed. The result does not determine the duration of oxygen hypoxia or generalize without proof to arbitrary multi-level switching.
