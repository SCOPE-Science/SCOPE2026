# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The result specifies all quantifiers, the precise finite-field and distinct-vertex domain, and the multiplicity convention. The projective normalization, explicit Pascal equation, quadratic-character sums, and orbit-stabilizer division together prove each formula over all odd prime powers of characteristic at least five. Independent all-line brute force for primes \(5,7,11,13\), normalized triple enumeration through prime \(29\), and a quadratic extension-field check at \(25\) agree with the formulas. The numerical checks are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (with stated priority risk)

Chipalkatti (2023, arXiv:2303.10319), especially Proposition 2.1, already constructs and parametrizes a complex four-dimensional Pascal-line fiber. This is a **stronger geometric framework**, but it does not give the precise rational-fiber cardinalities over finite fields, their dependence on \(\chi_q(-3)\), or the universal rational-line surjectivity threshold. Chipalkatti–Da Silva (2022) treat collision degenerations instead. A related six-point statement over \(\mathbb F_5\) covers only one edge case. A separate published Pascal–Kirkman Frobenius classification fixes one generic sextuple and hence does not aggregate across all rational hexagons. This comparison uses the full accessible 2023 text (including Proposition 2.1), not just titles or search snippets. An older unindexed finite-geometry count might be equivalent, and that priority risk remains.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Pascal morphism is a classical natural object. Its rational fibers over finite fields are not constant across rational lines: their exact distinction into three geometric orbits and a quadratic-character correction give a uniform arithmetic specialization of the geometric fiber picture. The threshold at which every rational line is achieved is sharp within the stated domain, contrasting the ten-line degeneration at \(q=5\) with complete line coverage at \(q=7\). These data can inform further finite-field Pascal-incidence and coincidence studies. The theorem is not about repeated vertices or the entire fixed-hexad mysticum.
