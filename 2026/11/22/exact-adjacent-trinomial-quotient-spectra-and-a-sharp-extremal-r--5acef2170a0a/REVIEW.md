# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The quotient \(M(a-1,b+1,c)/M(a,b,c)=a/(b+1)\) forces equal prime valuations at its numerator and denominator when both integer multinomial coefficients are units. The unique decisive base-\(p\) digit and the forced lower-digit pattern establish the count \(N_p(u)S_r\). The lower extremum follows from a positive-part displacement identity; the upper extremum follows from the exact crossing bound for the footrule of multiplication permutations and a nonzero half-interval sum modulo \(p\). Exact integer enumeration checked complete spectra for several small primes and depths. Risks: finite checks cannot replace the infinite proof; all needed infinite assertions are proved independently of the checker.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest direct two-coordinate result gives the uniform nonexceptional neighbor-binomial quotient law and the exceptional maximum at \(-1\). Its statement does not settle the three-coordinate weighted simplex fibers. Deutsch–Sagan (DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2005.06.005) establishes relevant multinomial Lucas machinery for central trinomial coefficients and sequences; the inspected Section 4 does not give joint adjacent quotient counts. Meštrović (arXiv:1409.3820) surveys Lucas-type congruences, while Garfield–Wilf (DOI 10.1016/0022-314X(92)90078-4) concerns individual binomial residue counts. Searches for trinomial-neighbor unit-ratio spectra and multiplicative-permutation footrules did not identify an equivalent stronger theorem. Risks: not every historical source was available in full; the binomial-neighbor result is especially close conceptually; hidden implicit equivalence remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is the entire local quotient distribution for naturally adjacent nonzero entries in the three-part multinomial simplex, for all odd primes and depths, with a nontrivial unique-minimum/unique-maximum classification. The opposite extremal behavior from the two-part case is explained by a weighted digit simplex and a sharp finite-field permutation footrule bound, not by replacing a parameter in a known formula. It gives a reproducible arithmetic statistic relevant to the local geometry of multinomial arrays. No claim of downstream applications is needed or made.
