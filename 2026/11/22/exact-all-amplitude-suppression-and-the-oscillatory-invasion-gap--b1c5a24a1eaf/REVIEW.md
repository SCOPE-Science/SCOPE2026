**Correctness — PASS.** The ecological first integral proves periodicity and exactly unit mean vector abundance. The positive Floquet eigenfunction yields an exact scalar Riccati identity; its nonnegative variance forces a strict invasion gap on every nonconstant cycle, while monotone cooperative comparison proves uniqueness of the crossing and nonlinear extinction below it. The equality case at the ecological center is handled separately by an invariant-set argument. Numerical monodromy tests and a computed small-cycle coefficient support (but do not replace) this analytic proof. No unproved endemic persistence is asserted above the threshold.

The 2023 full-text seasonality study and the 2007 perturbative work already show small-density-seasonality suppression, so that qualitative observation is not the claimed contribution. The full-text 2014 predator–host–vector study concerns different epidemic and ecological terms. Related mathematical findings on periodic infection and ecology were compared without locating equivalent or broader coverage; this is not a uniqueness certificate.

**Value — PASS.** Orbit-extrema bounds may become uninformative on large cycles, even when the baseline disease reproduction ratio is at most one. The theorem closes that conceptual gap by showing complete clearance at and below the constant-environment threshold for every positive ecological amplitude, and explicitly describes the additional supercritical buffer before infection can invade an individual cycle. This gives a sharp, ecologically interpretable regime separation, not just a routine perturbation or one numerical example.
