# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The full affine dimension, vertex orbits, orbit-covariance eigenvalues, and strictly concave determinant objective were reconstructed. At the unique stationary mixture weight, every vertex has Mahalanobis gauge equal to dimension; a trace–determinant inequality proves the resulting ellipsoid globally optimal and unique without restricting competitors by symmetry. The three asymptotic scaling laws follow from the same exact derivative equation, with the critical-scale integer assumption stated explicitly. The included checker independently enumerates the vertices in 39 finite configurations and passes nine separate asymptotic tests. Finite tests do not prove the infinite assertions.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)
Hanely et al. (arXiv:2201.12442) and Deligeorgaki et al. (arXiv:2311.01640) study the same panhandle base polytopes, but prove Ehrhart and combinatorial statements rather than the minimum-volume outer ellipsoid. A previously indexed rank-two clipped-matroid ellipsoid result does not contain the rank-at-least-three formula. Earlier panhandle metric records concern width, Euclidean inballs, uniform-vertex covariance, or distances, not this optimized two-orbit determinant with its sharp contact-mass saturation threshold. Unindexed equivalent results remain possible; the comparison is not a priority guarantee.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The object is an established matroid-polytope family. The exact, unrestricted minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid, its full contact set and unique center, and a three-regime asymptotic transition in optimal contact design are natural global affine-geometric invariants. In particular, saturation of design weight occurs even while every vertex remains tangent, separating contact support from optimal mass allocation. The result is not merely a finite computation or a special-case numeric table.
