# Exact all-dimensional extendibility and integer frailty for the unit-Lomax-distorted independence copula

## Finding

For real parameters \(\alpha>0\), \(\theta>0\), let
\[
T_{\alpha,\theta}(z)=1-\left(\frac{1-z}{1-(1-\theta)z}\right)^{\alpha},\qquad 0\le z\le1,
\]
and put
\[
\psi_{\alpha,\theta}(t)=T_{\alpha,\theta}(e^{-t}),\qquad t\ge0.
\]
The canonical Archimedean construction
\[
C_d(u_1,\ldots,u_d)=\psi_{\alpha,\theta}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\psi_{\alpha,\theta}^{-1}(u_j)\right)
\]
is a copula for **every** integer dimension \(d\ge2\) if and only if
\[
\boxed{0<\alpha\le1,\qquad0<\theta\le\frac{2}{1+\alpha}.}
\]
Here the inverse is the decreasing inverse on \([0,1]\); all quantities at zero are interpreted by continuity. In the admissible region the generator is the Laplace transform of an *explicit positive-integer-valued* mixing variable \(K\), so that the construction has an exact shared-frailty representation.

For \(0<\theta\le1\) the positivity of the mixing probabilities follows by multiplying two positive binomial series. A three-term coefficient recurrence proves positivity throughout the additional region \(1<\theta\le2/(1+\alpha)\), which is nonempty whenever \(\alpha<1\). Outside the boxed region the canonical construction fails in at least one finite dimension.

## Assumptions and scope

All parameters \(\alpha,\theta\) are real and strictly positive. The subject is the **canonical** Archimedean extension generated by the 2024 unit-Lomax distortion of the independence copula, not arbitrary multivariate copulas having its bivariate distribution as a margin. The function \(T_{\alpha,\theta}\) is strictly increasing from zero to one; hence \(\psi\) is continuous, strictly decreasing from one to zero and has an inverse. The theorem includes both equality boundaries \(\alpha=1\) and \(\theta=2/(1+\alpha)\); it does not include limiting zero parameters.

The known general criterion says that a strict Archimedean generator defines a copula in every finite dimension if and only if it is completely monotone on positive arguments, equivalently if it is a probability Laplace transform. That criterion is applied here to a *specific* 2024 distortion and is not claimed as new.

## Proof

**1. Power-series reduction.** Set \(s=\theta-1\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
T'_{\alpha,\theta}(z)=\alpha\theta f(z),\qquad
f(z)=(1-z)^{\alpha-1}(1+s z)^{-\alpha-1}.
\]
Write \(f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n\) near zero, where \(a_0=1\). Integration from zero yields
\[
T_{\alpha,\theta}(z)=\sum_{k\ge1}w_kz^k,
\qquad w_k=\frac{\alpha\theta}{k}a_{k-1}.
\]
In particular,
\[
w_1=\alpha\theta,\qquad
w_2=\frac{\alpha\theta}{2}\left[2-(1+\alpha)\theta\right].
\]

**2. Sufficiency for \(0<\theta\le1\).** When \(0<\alpha\le1\), the factor \((1-z)^{\alpha-1}=(1-z)^{-(1-\alpha)}\) has nonnegative Taylor coefficients. Since \(s\le0\), the factor \((1+s z)^{-\alpha-1}=(1-(1-\theta)z)^{-\alpha-1}\) also has nonnegative Taylor coefficients. Their product therefore has \(a_n\ge0\) at every index. This also covers the endpoint \(\alpha=1\), at which the first factor is one.

**3. Sufficiency for the additional parameter strip.** Suppose \(0<\alpha<1\) and
\(1<\theta\le2/(1+\alpha)\). Thus
\[
0<s\le\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}<1,
\qquad A=1-\alpha-(1+\alpha)s=2-(1+\alpha)\theta\ge0.
\]
The logarithmic derivative of \(f\) gives the exact identity
\[
(1-z)(1+s z)f'(z)=(A+2s z)f(z).
\]
Equating coefficients yields
\[
a_0=1,\qquad a_1=A,
\]
and, for every integer \(n\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{(n+1)a_{n+1}=\left[A+(1-s)n\right]a_n+s(n+1)a_{n-1}.}
\]
Every coefficient on the right is nonnegative; induction proves \(a_n\ge0\) for every \(n\). At the upper boundary \(A=0\) the only missing positive coefficient is \(a_1\), while the recurrence ensures the subsequent \(a_n\) are strictly positive. Since \(s<1\), the power series is analytic for \(|z|<1\). Since \(T(1)=1\), monotone convergence of its nonnegative Taylor series gives \(\sum_{k\ge1}w_k=1\). Thus the weights form a probability mass function, and
\[
\psi_{\alpha,\theta}(t)=\sum_{k\ge1}w_ke^{-kt}=\mathbb E[e^{-Kt}].
\]
This is completely monotone, proving copula validity in every dimension.

**4. Necessity when \(\alpha>1\).** As \(t\downarrow0\), the defining formula and its twice-differentiated expansion yield
\[
\psi_{\alpha,\theta}(t)=1-\theta^{-\alpha}t^{\alpha}(1+O(t)),\qquad
\psi_{\alpha,\theta}''(t)=-\alpha(\alpha-1)\theta^{-\alpha}t^{\alpha-2}(1+O(t)).
\]
For any fixed \(\alpha>1\) the second derivative is negative for sufficiently small positive \(t\). A completely monotone function must have nonnegative second derivative, so no all-dimensional extension is possible.

**5. Necessity when \(0<\alpha\le1\) and \(\theta>2/(1+\alpha)\).** In this region \(w_1=\alpha\theta>0\) but \(w_2<0\). Suppose for contradiction that \(\psi\) were completely monotone. By Bernstein's theorem there is a probability law \(\mu\) on \([0,\infty)\) with \(\psi(t)=\int e^{-tx}\,\mu(dx)\). Since the analytic expansion gives \(e^t\psi(t)\to w_1\) as \(t\to\infty\), \(\mu\) cannot charge \([0,1)\). Dominated convergence on \([1,\infty)\) then gives \(\mu(\{1\})=w_1\). Positivity of the remaining measure forces
\[
\psi(t)-w_1e^{-t}=\int_{(1,\infty)}e^{-tx}\,\mu(dx)\ge0.
\]
But the Taylor expansion at \(z=e^{-t}\downarrow0\) implies
\[
\psi(t)-w_1e^{-t}=w_2e^{-2t}+O(e^{-3t})<0
\]
for all sufficiently large \(t\), a contradiction. This proves necessity, including nonexistence of any probability mixing measure with noninteger support.

Combining Steps 2–5 with the general all-dimensional Archimedean criterion proves the theorem.

**6. Constructive frailty sampling.** In the valid region generate \(K\) with probabilities \(w_k\) above and independent unit exponential variables \(E_1,\ldots,E_d\); set
\[
U_j=T_{\alpha,\theta}(e^{-E_j/K}),\qquad 1\le j\le d.
\]
Conditionally on \(K\), the probability of \(U_j\le u_j\) is \(e^{-K\psi^{-1}(u_j)}\). Taking the expectation of the joint conditional probability produces exactly \(C_d(u_1,\ldots,u_d)\). Thus this sampler has uniform margins and the asserted joint copula.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify_coefficients.py` uses only Python's standard library and exact rational arithmetic. It independently constructs the Taylor coefficients of \(f\) by generalized-binomial convolution and compares them with the differential-equation recurrence for nine rational parameter pairs through degree 26. For valid examples it also checks the first 81 recurrence coefficients nonnegative; for invalid examples it verifies an exact negative second mass coefficient. At \((\alpha,\theta)=(1/2,4/3)\) the first three mixing weights are exactly
\[
\Pr(K=1)=\frac23,\qquad\Pr(K=2)=0,\qquad\Pr(K=3)=\frac2{27}.
\]
The proof's positivity for *all* indices rests on induction in Step 3 (or binomial-series positivity in Step 2), not the finite script. The impossibility conclusion rests on the analytic Laplace-transform argument, not on a finite truncation.

To reproduce: `python3 verify_coefficients.py` from the directory containing that script; no external dependencies. The script's expected output begins with `PASS`.

## Relationship to prior work

Aldhufairi and Sepanski, *New Bivariate Copulas via Lomax Distribution Generated Distortions* (2024), define exactly \(T_{\alpha,\theta}\) in their Example 1 and its bivariate independence-copula distortion in Section 3.2. Their Lemma 1 proves convexity on the sufficient rectangle \(0<\theta\le1\), \(0<\alpha\le1\). Their derivative formula makes it possible to check convexity of \(T\) on a wider region, but the article does not address the all-dimensional complete-monotonicity criterion, the integer-frailty law, or the coefficient-induction proof above; its conclusion expressly identifies higher-dimensional distortions as future work. Extending the elementary convexity domain alone is **not** presented as an independent novelty claim.

The earlier paper Aldhufairi, Samanthi and Sepanski, *New Families of Bivariate Copulas via Unit Lomax Distortion* (2020), uses \(T(z)=[1+b(z^{-1}-1)]^{-a}\), a mathematically different transformation despite the similar name. Its independence-copula family is therefore not an equivalent formula for the present full two-parameter family. The 2024 paper identifies \(\theta=1\) as a Joe copula and \(\alpha=1\) as an Ali–Mikhail–Haq copula; these known one-parameter subfamilies are not claimed as new. Their known multivariate restrictions do not establish the mixed interior strip \(0<\alpha<1\), \(1<\theta\le2/(1+\alpha)\).

McNeil and Nešlehová (2009) establish the general equivalence between all-dimensional Archimedean validity and complete monotonicity. That foundational criterion is used, not extended. The specific exact frontier and integer-valued mixing distribution require the family-level argument in this result. Historical priority beyond the inspected sources remains open to independent bibliography review.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns the simultaneous validity of the *canonical Archimedean formula* in every finite dimension, and does not identify the maximum valid finite dimension outside the admissible region. It does not exclude non-Archimedean multivariate extensions with the same bivariate margins. The analytic weights give an exact distribution and an implicit sampling procedure; computational sampling of an unbounded discrete law may require truncation control. Results have received a self-review but no independent audit. Literature comparisons establish a reasoned originality assessment, not a proof of historical priority.
