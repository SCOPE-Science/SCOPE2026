# Scientific review: exact excitation-wave count on chorded cycles

## Correctness — PASS
For integers \(k\ge3\), \(n\ge4\) and \(2\le a\le n-2\), the claim counts initially primitive temporal \(k\)-orbits of threshold-one \(k\)-state Greenberg–Hastings dynamics on a labelled single-chord cycle. The proof establishes an all-or-nothing rotating condition for immediate temporal \(k\)-states on a connected graph, reduces every rotating coloring to a directed cycle of successor edges, and uses the three simple cycles of a chorded ring. Complementary-path words have an exact normal form and an explicitly proved modular count; pairwise overlaps are counted and triple intersection is arithmetically impossible for \(k\ge3\). All boundaries (simple chord, path lengths at least two, \(k\ge3\)) are explicit. Independently organized endpoint-transfer checking passed 10,266 parameter tests, and direct synchronous-map enumeration agreed on 30 graphs covering 1,301,418 initial states. No finite test is offered as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest source is *Exact two-terminal source census for three-color excitable theta networks* (2026-11-08). Its complete public scientific text counts three-state waves on three-or-more paths each of length at least two and gives a two-terminal source-multiplicity polynomial. It is methodologically related, and the path-word technique is credited. The present result concerns arbitrary palette \(k\ge3\), a graph having a length-one terminal path, unmarked orbit counts and the explicit three-cycle-overlap formula; its four-state congruence and fixed-span-three amplification do not appear in that text. The all-colour lattice paper by Fisch and Gravner (1995), inspected through its setup and results sections, analyzes one-dimensional ranges, thresholds and stable objects, not finite singly chorded rings. Gravner, Lyu and Sivakoff (2016) establish general three-color limiting behavior, not this all-palette count. The 2026-11-17 four-color CCA ring result uses a different update rule on a chord-free graph. Their claims do not imply the new exact formula as a stated corollary. Closest open questions concern historical unindexed formulas or generic cycle-polynomial specializations; prior work's path method limits methodological novelty.

## Value — PASS
Adding a *single* edge to a ring is a canonical local perturbation of a finite excitable medium. The theorem specifies exactly which chord placements create a self-sustaining wave at a given excitation clock length and how many individual phase cycles survive, including overlapping competing supports. This is more than a renamed transfer recurrence: it derives an explicit all-palette modular overlap formula and, at four states, an interpretable four-class linear amplification law valid for an infinite family. For ordinary rings wave existence is restricted to circumferences divisible by the palette length; a span-three chord causes four-phase waves on **all** rings of circumference at least five. The precise formula can be reused in comparisons of localized network defects and phase-wave support. It makes no basin, global period-spectrum, or quantitative experimental significance claims.
