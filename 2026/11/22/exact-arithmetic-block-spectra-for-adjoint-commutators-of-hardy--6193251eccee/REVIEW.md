# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The isometry and two-sided unitary block-decomposition claims follow coefficientwise, with exact finite overlap counts rather than asymptotic approximation. Intersecting two interval-partition step-function spaces supplies the gcd multiplicity of the unit singular value, and a triangular submatrix gives the finite-block rank for all integer bases. For adjacent bases, the explicitly computed Gram complement is a symmetric quadratic-rate tridiagonal difference operator; polynomial-degree triangularization proves all \(m\) eigenvalues exactly. Riemann sums and a beta integral establish the limiting distribution and its moment constants. The finite exact and numerical checker is supplementary evidence only; the infinite claims are analytic.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
Noor (2018) introduces the same weighted-composition semigroup but treats cyclicity and a Hilbert-space criterion, not the mixed-adjoint commutator. Manzur–Noor–Santos (2021) treat individual isometries, adjoints and invariant-space questions; the passages inspected do not assert the cross-adjoint finite-block spectrum. Sasaki (2021) gives classical Hahn/birth-and-death difference operators, so no novelty is claimed for \(j(j+1)\) as a standalone Laplacian eigenvalue. The published published-finding corpus findings on bare monomial compositions use \(f(z^n)\) without the geometric multiplier. Their abstract-level conclusions do not imply the present weighted mixed-adjoint formula; their full text was not retrievable and is a residual priority risk. A more general earlier theorem about canonical correlations of uniform interval partitions might imply the exact spectrum. Failed searches alone would not prove novelty; the assessment rests on comparing different mathematical objects and their implications.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Although the original isometries commute, their adjoints do not doubly commute. The arithmetic gcd precisely controls whether their cross-adjoint commutator attains norm one, and adjacent bases admit a complete, distinct nontrivial spectral hierarchy and a limiting cell distribution. This links natural Hardy weighted compositions to conditional expectations and a discrete Hahn operator, providing an exact quantitative obstruction in a semigroup already studied for cyclicity and invariant subspaces.

## Scientific limitations
All claims concern standard Hardy space and integer bases. Infinite-direct-sum singular levels have infinite multiplicity; no compact singular-value decay is claimed. Priority is provisional pending independent literature validation, particularly older partition-correlation work. No mathematical implication for the Riemann hypothesis is proposed.
