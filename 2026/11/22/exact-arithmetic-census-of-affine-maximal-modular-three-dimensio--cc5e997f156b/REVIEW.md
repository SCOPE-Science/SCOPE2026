# Scientific review of the affine three-dimensional modular-queen census

## Correctness

PASS. The cardinality \(n^2\) forces a unique queen in each vertical line; any affine subgroup translates to a graph of an additive homomorphism and so to unique coefficients \(a,b,c\). A direction-line attack is equivalent to a nonzero annihilator of one of twelve ring elements; over \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), its absence is exactly the unit criterion. The 12-line rational arrangement has a verified intersection profile of five quadruple, four triple, and twelve double points, persistent for every field of characteristic at least seven. Inclusion-exclusion gives \((p-5)(p-7)\); prime powers and arbitrary composites follow by unit lifting and Chinese remaindering. The small characteristics have explicit exclusion arguments. The independent direct-direction finite checks agree, while the unrestricted statement depends on the proof.

PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty. Older work on alternative three-dimensional attack rules is not automatically comparable.

## Value

PASS. The subclass is canonical, not chosen by an arbitrary numerical bound: maximum cardinality and the affine condition together produce a unique representation. The exact count quantifies a constructive family inside a published open total-count problem, distinguishes maximum-placement existence from multiplicity, and reveals a multiplicative arithmetic structure useful for modular lower bounds and geometric symmetry studies. The result is an all-moduli theorem rather than a small exceptional computation.
