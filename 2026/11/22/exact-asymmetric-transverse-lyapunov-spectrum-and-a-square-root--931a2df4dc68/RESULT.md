# Exact asymmetric transverse Lyapunov spectrum and a square-root instability cusp in a cyclic Lotka–Volterra map

## Finding

For the cyclic three-species quadratic map of Muni (2024), set the intrinsic growth parameter to \(R=3\) and let both competition coefficients \(\alpha,\beta\) be strictly positive. The invariant equal-population ray supports a canonically normalized, ergodic logistic-four arcsine probability \(\mu_{\alpha,\beta}\). Relative to this measure, the entire three-dimensional Lyapunov spectrum can be computed in closed form, including genuinely unequal cross-species interactions.

Write \(s=\alpha+\beta\), \(\delta=\alpha-\beta\), and
\[
B=\frac{2}{1+s}\left(s-2+i\sqrt3\,\delta\right), \qquad
H=\sqrt{B},\quad \operatorname{Re}H\ge0.
\]
For negative real \(B\), take either purely imaginary square root; the answer is independent of this choice. The Lyapunov spectrum (with multiplicities) is
\[
\boxed{\left\{\log 2,\ \Lambda_\perp,\ \Lambda_\perp\right\}},
\qquad
\boxed{\Lambda_\perp=2\log\left|1+\frac{H}{2}\right|.}
\]
Equivalently, without a branch convention, with \(u=\operatorname{Re}B\), \(h=|B|\),
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_\perp=\log\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{h+u}{2}}+\frac h4\right).}
\]
In the entire open quadrant \(\alpha,\beta>0\), the two transverse exponents are strictly positive except at the **unique neutral point** \((\alpha,\beta)=(1,1)\), where they vanish. No parameter in this quadrant gives typical infinitesimal transverse attraction of the invariant chaotic ray.

On the balanced line \(\alpha+\beta=2\) (necessarily \(|\delta|<2\)), the exact expression simplifies to
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_\perp=
\log\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{|\delta|}{\sqrt3}}
+\frac{|\delta|}{2\sqrt3}\right).}
\]
In particular, as unequal interactions approach equality along this line,
\[
\boxed{\lim_{\delta\to0}\frac{\Lambda_\perp}{\sqrt{|\delta|}}
=3^{-1/4}.}
\]
This is a nonanalytic square-root onset in an antisymmetric coupling direction, whereas the pre-existing symmetric one-parameter formulas concern \(\alpha=\beta\).

## Assumptions and scope

The phase space is \(\mathbb R^3\), with physically positive invariant diagonal states. For every \(\alpha,\beta>0\), define
\[
F(x,y,z)=\bigl(x+3x(1-x-\alpha y-\beta z),\quad
 y+3y(1-\beta x-y-\alpha z),\quad
 z+3z(1-\alpha x-\beta y-z)\bigr).
\]
Define \(d=3(1+\alpha+\beta)/4\). On the diagonal \((q,q,q)\), the normalized state \(w=dq\) ranges in \([0,1]\) and follows \(w\mapsto 4w(1-w)\). The measure \(\mu_{\alpha,\beta}\) is the diagonal pushforward of the probability density \(dw/[\pi\sqrt{w(1-w)}]\) on \((0,1)\). All exponent assertions are \(\mu_{\alpha,\beta}\)-almost-everywhere ambient derivative cocycle statements, **not** assertions about a positive-volume physical attractor, global boundedness, positive off-diagonal orbits, or attractors generated by generic initial states.

## Proof

**1. Exact diagonal dynamics and ergodic probability.** At \(x=y=z=q\), all components equal \(4q-3(1+s)q^2\), hence \(w=dq\) obeys the full-chaos logistic map \(L(w)=4w(1-w)\). The substitution \(w=\sin^2(\pi t)\) semiconjugates \(L\) to the doubling map \(t\mapsto2t\pmod1\). Pushing Lebesgue measure forward yields the arcsine density. Doubling-map ergodicity proves its ergodicity, and \(\int \log|L'(w)|\,d\mu=\log2\), giving the longitudinal exponent.

**2. One fixed splitting for every derivative.** Along the diagonal,
\[
DF(q,q,q)=\begin{pmatrix}
4-3(2+s)q&-3\alpha q&-3\beta q\\
-3\beta q&4-3(2+s)q&-3\alpha q\\
-3\alpha q&-3\beta q&4-3(2+s)q
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every such matrix is real circulant, so the fixed discrete Fourier basis decomposes \(\mathbb R^3\) into the diagonal line and its orthogonal two-plane. The line eigenvalue is \(4-6(1+s)q=4(1-2w)\). On the two-plane, the real matrix acts as multiplication by a complex number with either orientation of imaginary part:
\[
\eta(w)=4(1-w)+Bw,
\qquad B=\frac{2(s-2+i\sqrt3\delta)}{1+s}.
\]
Products of these transverse blocks are complex multiplications. Thus both transverse singular-value Lyapunov rates equal \(\int_0^1\log|\eta(w)|\,dw/[\pi\sqrt{w(1-w)}]\). This logarithm is integrable even when \(\eta\) has an isolated interior or endpoint zero. Birkhoff's theorem determines the common exponent for almost every initial diagonal state; exceptional precritical orbits carry no mass.

**3. Exact complex logarithmic average.** We prove, for any \(B\in\mathbb C\), the identity
\[
\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\log|4\cos^2\theta+B\sin^2\theta|\,d\theta
=2\log\left|1+\frac{\sqrt B}{2}\right|,
\]
where the square root has nonnegative real part (and either sign for negative real \(B\)). To see this first when \(\operatorname{Re}\sqrt B>0\), put \(v=\sqrt B/2\), \(\rho=(1-v)/(1+v)\), so \(|\rho|<1\) and the exact factorization is
\[
4\cos^2\theta+B\sin^2\theta=
(1+v)^2(1+\rho e^{2i\theta})(1+\rho e^{-2i\theta}).
\]
The two logarithmic factors combine, after changing variable \(\phi=2\theta\), into one full-circle average \((2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|1+\rho e^{i\phi}|\,d\phi=0\) by Jensen's elementary formula for \(|\rho|<1\). Both sides extend by dominated local logarithmic estimates (or radial limits in Jensen's identity) to \(|\rho|=1\); zeros then have only integrable logarithmic singularities. Substituting \(w=\sin^2\theta\) in the arcsine integral proves the first boxed formula. Squaring its modulus gives
\[
\left|1+\frac H2\right|^2=1+\operatorname{Re}H+\frac{|B|}{4},
\qquad \operatorname{Re}H=\sqrt{\frac{|B|+\operatorname{Re}B}{2}},
\]
proving the second.

**4. Strict sign and the transverse square-root cusp.** The right side of the squared-modulus identity is at least one; it is exactly one precisely when \(B=0\), equivalent to \(s=2\) and \(\delta=0\), hence \(\alpha=\beta=1\). Otherwise it is strictly greater than one. On \(s=2\), \(B=2i\delta/\sqrt3\), so \(\operatorname{Re}\sqrt B=\sqrt{|\delta|/\sqrt3}\) and \(|B|/4=|\delta|/(2\sqrt3)\). The displayed balanced-line law follows. Expanding \(\log(1+t+O(t^2))\sim t\) with \(t=3^{-1/4}\sqrt{|\delta|}\) gives the sharp one-sided asymptotic from both signs of \(\delta\). All asserted quantifiers include \(\delta=0\) as the neutral endpoint.

## Verification

The included standalone `verify.py` symbolically reconstructs the diagonal normalization, Fourier eigenvalues, equivalence of the complex and real-radical formulas, and the symmetry of the exponent in exchanging the competition coefficients. It independently integrates the arcsine logarithm at nonsingular and interior-zero examples, checks the balanced-line reduction at several parameter values and its limiting constant, and prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. These calculations are diagnostics; the Fourier/Jensen proof above establishes the exact theorem for the continuum of parameter values.

## Relationship to prior work

Muni's 2024 paper introduces the map, computes parameter-plane Lyapunov diagrams for specific regimes, and reports torus doubling and hyperchaotic attractors. Its two-parameter numerical chart does not give a formula for the invariant-diagonal cocycle at \(R=3\). An earlier exact symmetric-interaction result already determines the full diagonal spectrum and neutral composition foliation **when \(\alpha=\beta=a\)**, including a square-root law in the symmetric \(a>1\) direction. Substituting \(\alpha=\beta=a\) in the present formula reproduces that known law and is **not itself new**. The claim here is the exact asymmetric two-parameter extension, the unique neutral point in the whole positive quadrant, and the distinct antisymmetric \(\sqrt{|\alpha-\beta|}\) cusp on \(\alpha+\beta=2\). General Fourier diagonalization and Jensen's identity are established tools; no novelty is attributed to them separately. Other literature on periodic synchronous orbits and continuous-time balanced May–Leonard populations addresses different cocycles or flow regimes and does not imply this logarithmic average without new calculation.

## Limitations

This is an invariant **singular diagonal probability** and its ambient infinitesimal Lyapunov spectrum, not a full invariant measure classification, a transversely attracting chaotic attractor, or a positive-Lebesgue-basin hyperchaotic attractor. Positive transverse exponents express infinitesimal typical instability, not necessarily uniform transverse expansion or a basin statement. The case \(\alpha=\beta=1\) has neutral exponents; no transverse stability conclusion follows merely from neutrality. The formula presupposes exact \(R=3\); it does not describe the published paper's typical \(R=1.4\) attractor chart. Literature comparisons cannot rule out equivalent unindexed calculations. Independent validation remains outstanding.
