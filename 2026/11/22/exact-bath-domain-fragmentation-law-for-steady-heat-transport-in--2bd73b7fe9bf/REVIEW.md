# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement fixes every temperature, clock rate, graph size, and hot-site multiplicity. Applying the generator to two distinct spin coordinates gives an exact linear identity. Both-hot covariances vanish, so each cold run is a finite tridiagonal Dirichlet problem with boundary correlations one and zero. The continued fraction gives the stationary flux exactly. The iterative map is strictly contracting on a controlled invariant interval and its increments strictly decrease; splitting, balancing, and equality cases follow. Stationary existence and uniqueness follow from all strictly positive spin-flip rates. Independent full-state rational stationarity for selected rings and rational two-point enumeration of all 456 patterns up to eight sites agree with the formula. The finite checks are corroboration rather than an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence
The foundational Ising model and its pair-correlation generator are not new. Rácz–Zia (1994) and the 2002 Schmittmann/Schmüser papers treat the translationally alternating bath pattern; the known alternating heat-current formula is explicitly credited. Lavrentovich (2012) treats one macroscopic hot–cold junction, not arbitrary finite hot-site arrangements and their extremizers. Bauer–Cornu (2017) use alternating baths, even while varying kinetic parameters. Distinct mathematical claims are the arbitrary-run exact flux, strict fragmentation monotonicity, and all-size fixed-count optimizer classification. Searches of scholarly records and mathematical finding indexes found no statement implying all three; failure to locate a statement does not establish absolute historical novelty. The closest general pair-equation formalism is acknowledged and does not itself state the optimizer theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For a physically standard nonequilibrium Ising chain, the result gives a complete rigorous answer to a transport-design problem: at a fixed number of each reservoir type, arrangement alone determines and can sharply optimize heat current. The exact composition law distinguishes interface count from finite cold-domain length; its diminishing length increments make the design extremizers solvable at every size. It is a structural all-size result, not a selected numerical case, and provides benchmarks for patterned thermal contacts.

## Limits and residual risks
The infinite-hot limit is a structural simplification that makes hot–hot correlations vanish; analogous claims at finite hot temperature are not proved. The formula uses two distinct hot sites and nonzero strictly subcritical \(\gamma\). Research-paper comparisons were detailed for the most directly related freely available preprints, but priority beyond searchable/inspected materials is not independently certified.
