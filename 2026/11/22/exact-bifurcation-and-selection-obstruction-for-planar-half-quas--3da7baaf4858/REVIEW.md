# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The full positive quadrant boundary is parametrized by two complementary squares. The squared objective reduces identically to a strictly convex quadratic in \(v=u(1-u)\), where \(0\le v\le1/4\). Its clipped unique scalar minimizer yields every global nearest point and every transition. Direct differentiation establishes the regularity statements, and the swapping/compactness argument establishes nonexistence of continuous local selections. The packaged checker separately replays rational algebra and finite numerical instances; no finite sampling is used as a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (with residual priority risk)

Yang–Wang–Wang (arXiv:2101.01350, full text, introduction, optimality and numerical sections) treat general nonconvex quasi-ball projections and note possible multiple minimizers; they do not state the global two-coordinate equal-target phase thresholds, separation exponent or continuous-selection obstruction. Won–Lange–Xu (arXiv:2203.00564, full text, sections covering dual projection and empirical tests) treat general computation and duality gaps, without this exact diagonal minimizer classification. The general necessary conditions could lead a reader to derive the two-coordinate answer, but do not already imply the four-case classification without an additional global minimization. Targeted prior-database searches returned different convex-projection, geometric and unrelated bifurcation findings; no exact covering theorem was located. This is best-knowledge originality, not a claim that no earlier equivalent treatment exists.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The half-quasinorm constraint is a standard sparsity-promoting nonconvex model, and two coordinates are the first dimension in which projection nonuniqueness can arise. The complete equal-signal phase portrait identifies two exact transitions after boundary contact: symmetry breaking with a sharp square-root exponent, followed by support collapse. It proves a genuine local selection obstruction to treating this nonconvex projection as a continuous operator. These concrete structural facts are motivated by robustness of sparse projection steps rather than an arbitrary finite computation.
