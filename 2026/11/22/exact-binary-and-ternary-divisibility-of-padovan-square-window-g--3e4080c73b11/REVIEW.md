# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The precise claim is the two local valuations of the gcd of all positive-length Padovan square windows, equivalently the two prime-power period formulas. The sixth-order annihilator has \(Q(1)=-1\), which proves the nontrivial converse from periodicity to vanishing window sums. The matrices \(A^7\), \(A^{14}\), and \(A^{13}\) have been reconstructed, including the binary witness at offset seven and exact sharpness witnesses at indices zero and two. The proof handles every positive window length, all binary and ternary precisions, and the low-precision boundary separately. The standalone checker ran and passed; finite tests are corroboration rather than an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
The April 25, 2021 arXiv version of Guyer and Mbirika and the 2021 journal text treat sums of unsquared order-two Fibonacci recurrences, not Padovan squares. The finite-group Padovan paper describes ordinary recurrence behavior, not this gcd. Closest indexed results on primitive Padovan modulo two, Jacobsthal-square sliding gcds, and other Pell and Tribonacci relatives do not state the squared Padovan two-prime valuation law; the transition from ordinary periods to squared periods is not an immediate identity. Searches for alternate terms and implications were performed. Inaccessible full texts and implicitly applicable general results remain nonzero originality risks; no universal novelty claim is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural arithmetic refinement of the published problem of gcds of sliding recurrence windows. It determines two entire local prime-power components over every window length, and exposes a structural binary/ternary asymmetry: a squared third-order recurrence has a sharp binary period shortening, not a mere substitution in a known second-order formula. The exact local law can be used to determine divisibility of all windows without calculating any window sums.

## Scope and risk
The theorem does not classify other prime factors. There is no independent audit, and an unindexed equivalent remains possible.
