# Mathematical review of the affine-plane Boolean Fourier result

## Correctness

PASS. The finite geometry has 12 lines, four through each point, and one through each pair. Subtracting the signed line enumerator from the degree-three Krawtchouk sum yields the closed Walsh-multiplier formula. The formula at cardinalities zero through four, together with complement antisymmetry, proves the complete spectrum and zero-character classification. The nonzero-frequency set and Steiner pair-incidence formula give a closed expression for the projection kernel. The average of arbitrary projection conjugates over the translation group establishes unrestricted endpoint optimality. The standalone exact-integer checker passed 3,123 assertions involving independent line generation, all 512 Fourier characters, a full Walsh projection transform, and the kernel formula. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof; the object and all quantifiers are finite and completely covered.

## Originality

PASS under bounded historical comparison. The 2012 affine-matroid Tutte-polynomial paper identifies and enumerates the object but does not give the characterwise signed basis transform or its exact global projection constant. A pointwise signed-spectrum formula for the containing thirteen-point projective matroid does algebraically imply the affine multipliers after four-coordinate averaging, so the affine multiplier table alone is not regarded as an independent discovery. The 2008 finite-Fourier exposition supplies general methodology for graph-polynomial invariants, not the specialized computation. Publicly indexed mathematical findings on Steiner-system bases with point counts congruent to five or seven modulo eight and on cubic sampling of all 840 labelled affine planes have different hypotheses or signal/observation spaces; their searchable statements do not dominate this fixed nine-point claim. The full texts of those two indexed findings were inaccessible; that limits the historical coverage assertion. An unindexed equivalent finite Walsh computation remains a plausible priority risk.

## Value

PASS. The nine-point affine matroid is a canonical nonbinary rank-three representable matroid and the unique nine-point Steiner triple system. Its 72 bases have a natural incidence distribution on the Boolean group. The 240-dimensional information-loss space is characterized geometrically and the sharp constant among arbitrary, including nonsymmetric, endpoint projections is a nontrivial extremal property of the natural range. This is a complete natural finite invariant rather than an arbitrary parameter slice or a re-tabulation of a Tutte polynomial.
