# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every fixed positive integer \(m\), the uniform-product/Gamma identity yields an explicit nonnegative single-jump defect. Its integral is \(e^{-u}\sum_{k=1}^{m}u^k/k!\) and differentiation gives exactly \(e^{-u}(1-u^m/m!)\), so the maximizing jump is unique. The signed-derivative representation of arbitrary real \(BV(0,1)\) functions gives the upper bound and complete equality classification without assuming smoothness. The deficiency identity follows by splitting the exponential series at order \(m\); Stirling and dominated geometric convergence establish the asymptotic. The standalone checker corroborates these steps numerically but does not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical risk
Galaz Fontes–Solís (2008) treat convergence of Cesàro iterates to a boundary constant; Adell–Lekuona (2009) prove sharp sup-norm rates toward that limit and give the Gamma representation; Albanese–Bonet–Ricker (2013) analyze spectral/ergodic properties; Birou (2021) proves different sharp convergence-to-constant inequalities. None of these inspected full texts states or dominates the exact \(BV\)-to-\(L^1\) identity-defect norm or its unique optimizing jump. Indexed finite-Cesàro and commutator findings concern different norms and domains. Unindexed sources or alternate Hausdorff-operator formulations may contain a precedent, and only the indexed descriptions of some nearby published findings were available. Absence from search is not a proof of historical novelty.

## Value — PASS
Repeated prefix averaging is a canonical positive operator family on discontinuous signals. An exact, attained all-iteration error norm calibrates unavoidable loss relative to total input variation and exposes the unique worst discontinuity. The factorial-root localization and exponential approach of the coefficient to one quantify a nontrivial singular boundary regime invisible in general convergence-to-constant estimates. This natural exact invariant is useful independently of broader operator theory.

## Limits and evidence
The result applies to real distributional \(BV(0,1)\), ordinary Cesàro powers and unweighted integrated error. No extension is asserted to all \(L^1\), weighted averaging or noninteger powers. Scientific verification includes the complete analytical proof plus a standalone numerical cross-check of twelve orders, signed multijump tests and large-order behavior. Priority remains subject to independent historical literature review.
