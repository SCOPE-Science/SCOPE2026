# Self-review: homopolymer-free fixed-burst deletion fluctuation law

**Correctness — PASS.** The deterministic equality of two burst outputs is equivalent to period-\(b\) equality on all intervening positions; this makes the distinct-sphere count an additive return statistic. Under the uniform proper-word source, increments are independent uniform nonzero residues. The complete-graph walk spectral calculation yields both the single-return probability and all nontrivial overlapping two-return covariances. Their signs and finite-length contribution are explicit. A standard independent-block argument proves the fixed-parameter central limit. Exhaustive rational checks separately construct output sets and verify 108 bounded parameter strata; no finite search is invoked as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Schoeny et al. (arxiv:1602.06820) explicitly state a classical deterministic burst-deletion sphere formula and a full histogram for *unrestricted binary* words; neither is new. Their inspected Sections IV–V and Appendix A do not supply moment/covariance laws for uniform proper \(q\)-ary words. Chen, Sima and Milenkovic (doi:10.3390/e27040366) study Kautz constraints for universal sequences rather than burst deletion. Closest searchable related findings concern binary-flip sources, multiple bursts, or deleted reads, and their implications do not cover the proper-word nonbinary covariance kernel. Residual risk is an earlier unindexed finite-state source specialization or a stronger general theorem not located by searches.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Homopolymer avoidance is a concrete constrained-coding model. Exact finite-length variance and limiting dispersion determine typical ambiguity, rather than only the expected or maximum burst ball. A sharp structural change occurs at deletion length two: all return indicators become mutually independent and the sphere count is binomial, while every fixed larger burst has strictly positive local covariance and an explicitly higher long-run variance. The result does not claim new code capacity, multiple-burst correction, or a complete finite-length histogram for all burst sizes.
