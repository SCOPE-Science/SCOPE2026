## Correctness — PASS

The statement is about exactly one candidate-index map, not the true analytic pole set. The source's equation (2.3) identifies the multiplicative ratio for each index. The cubic factorization and reciprocal-root pairs turn this ratio into five explicit exponent vectors. To exclude extra collisions, the proof uses irreducibility of the first cubic over the quadratic field and a conjugate-modulus obstruction to root-of-unity phase. This proves injectivity on exponent pairs; their free nonnegative multiplicities produce the five-factor generating function. The diagonal asymptotic follows from its unique maximal-order singularity. Independent integer code tests irreducibility modulo eleven and all distributions through weight forty-five; experiments are supporting checks, not an infinite proof.

Serrano Holgado (2023), Sections 2–4, establishes the general pole-index map, fibre finiteness and the multiplicative-relation criterion. Section 6 already identifies the exact sextic and states its relation module rank four; those are **not** claimed as new. The same section explicitly counts fibres in a *quartic Salem* example, but not for this sextic. The earlier Serrano Holgado–Navas Vicente (2023) paper gives meromorphic continuation and a candidate pole expression but no complete fibre enumerator for this example. The present complete bivariate sextic fibre series, the exact count of distinct sites on every real line and the quartic-growth law require an additional exponent-pair identification and enumeration. No matching result was found in the targeted searches; absence of search hits is not treated as proof of uniqueness. Residual risks are an unindexed equivalent treatment and the proximity of this computation to the published relation module.

## Value — PASS

The original mathematical question concerns repeated indexing of zeta singular candidates. General finite fibres and the criterion for noninjectivity do not quantify the collision severity. The exact five-factor distribution provides every site multiplicity on an infinite family of lines and reveals an unbounded cubic average fibre size despite there being only linearly many distinct sites. This is a natural sharp quantitative refinement of an expressly studied noninjective sextic example, not an arbitrary parameter slice.
