# Exact census of maximal ternary span-three negative-avoiding cycles

## Finding
On the labeled alphabet \(\mathbb Z_3=\{0,1,2\}\), consider cyclic words whose length-three cyclic windows are pairwise distinct and never contain both \((a,b,c)\) and \((-a,-b,-c)\), with negation modulo three. The maximum period is \(13\), as previously established. **Exactly \(288\) distinct maximal cycles exist modulo cyclic rotation**, or \(3744\) with indexed starting positions. Under the four operations generated by whole-word negation and whole-word reversal, these \(288\) rotation classes form **exactly \(72\) orbits**, each of size four.

More precisely, the \(13\) orbits of eligible triples under coordinatewise negation give \(2^{13}=8192\) ways of selecting exactly one triple per pair. Exactly \(72\) selections have balanced overlap graphs, and exactly \(60\) of these have connected nonisolated support. The numbers of connected selections possessing \(1,2,4,6,8,24\) Euler circuits modulo cyclic edge rotation are respectively \(16,16,8,8,8,4\). One maximal cycle is `0010110211121`.

## Assumptions and scope
A cyclic word \(w=(w_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z}\) has cyclic length-three windows \(e_i=(w_i,w_{i+1},w_{i+2})\). It is *negative-avoiding of span three* when the \(e_i\) are distinct and \(e_i\ne -e_j\) for all positions \(i,j\). In particular, the self-negative word `000` cannot appear as a window. All symbols are labeled. The primary count identifies words only up to rotation, not reversal or negation. The secondary count further identifies a word with its reversed word, its negated word, and its negated reversal. No arbitrary alphabet relabelings are used.

The span is fixed at three, the alphabet has precisely three symbols, and maximality means that the cycle uses all \(13\) non-self-negative triple-pair orbits. There is no assertion for other spans, alphabets or nonmaximal periods.

## Proof
**Upper bound and orbit choices.** Of the \(3^3=27\) ternary triples, only `000` is fixed by coordinatewise negation. The remaining \(26\) triples partition into exactly \(13\) two-element orbits. The negative-avoidance condition uses at most one triple per orbit, so every cycle has length at most \(13\). A cycle of length \(13\) must use exactly one triple from every orbit. The earlier existence construction and the explicit witness above show attainability; the witness is also independently checked by the programs supplied here.

**Bijection with Euler circuits.** Order the \(13\) pairs lexicographically by their smaller members. For each pair choose its first or second triple. Interpret a selected triple \((a,b,c)\) as a directed edge from vertex \((a,b)\) to vertex \((b,c)\) in the order-two ternary de Bruijn graph. Every admissible length-\(13\) cycle gives a connected balanced selected-edge digraph and an Euler circuit. Conversely every connected balanced such digraph admits an Euler circuit, whose cyclic vertex labels form a length-\(13\) negative-avoiding cycle. Distinct circuits modulo edge rotation give distinct words modulo symbol rotation because each triple appears exactly once. Thus the correspondence is bijective. Loops are retained throughout, and only vertices incident with edges participate in the Euler criterion.

**Exact finite count.** The file `graph_enumeration.py` enumerates all \(8192\) binary selections without sampling. It compares in- and out-degree at all nine possible pair vertices, tests connectedness of nonisolated vertices, builds the directed outdegree Laplacian with its loop entries cancelled by the adjacency matrix, and evaluates a rooted matrix-tree cofactor by integer fraction-free elimination. For each Eulerian graph the BEST theorem gives the number of cyclic edge-distinguished Euler circuits
\[
\tau_r\prod_{v\in V^+}(d^+(v)-1)!,
\]
where \(\tau_r\) is the number of in-arborescences to any chosen active root \(r\), and \(V^+\) is the set of vertices with positive degree. All edges have distinct labels, so there is no additional multiplicity or periodicity quotient.

|---|---:|
| Not balanced | 8120 |
| Balanced but disconnected | 12 |
| Balanced and connected | 60 |

Adding the final column yields \(288\). The active-degree patterns are also completely checked: there are \(32\) graphs with sorted nine-vertex degree multiset \((1,1,1,1,1,2,2,2,2)\), \(24\) with \((0,1,1,1,1,2,2,2,3)\), and \(4\) with \((0,0,1,1,1,2,2,3,3)\). These data provide additional exact counting certificates.

**Independent word enumeration and symmetry.** The separate `word_enumeration.py` performs a direct exhaustive test of all \(3^{13}=1,594,323\) indexed ternary words, compares their \(13\) length-three cyclic windows with the \(13\) negation orbits, and canonicalizes successful words under rotation. It finds \(3744\) valid indexed words and \(288\) rotation classes, independently confirming the graph count. It then applies the four transformations generated by reversal and coordinatewise negation and finds \(72\) distinct four-member orbits, with no fixed rotation class under any nonidentity transformation. This is a complete finite check of the symmetry claim. Every valid word has least period \(13\), since a smaller period would repeat a length-three window; hence each rotation class has exactly \(13\) indexed representatives.

## Verification
Using only Python 3's standard library, run `python3 graph_enumeration.py` and `python3 word_enumeration.py` from the package directory. The expected full outputs are stored in `graph_output.txt` and `word_output.txt`; both programs contain exact assertions for the census totals and symmetry counts. The graph algorithm enumerates all \(2^{13}\) orientation vectors; the logically separate word algorithm enumerates all \(3^{13}\) indexed words. Both were executed from the delivered package and their output was compared byte-for-byte with the included output files. Neither method extrapolates from a proper subset of the finite state space.

## Relationship to prior work
Mitchell and Wild, *Negative Avoiding Sequences* (first public version 2026-03-26, arXiv:2603.25286), establish the period bound, the Eulerian-edge correspondence and maximal-period existence for all alphabets and spans. In particular they exhibit a maximal ternary span-three cycle in Example 4.1. Those results and the method are prior; this finding claims only the **complete count of all maximal ternary span-three cycles and their reversal/negation symmetry classification**, which is not stated in the inspected full text (Definitions 1.3 and 3.3, Theorems 3.2 and 4.1, Examples 4.1 and 7.1, and Sections 5–8).

The publicly available *Sharp length of involution-avoiding cyclic two-window words* proves maxima for span two and arbitrary alphabet involutions, explicitly without counting optimizers or treating span three. The publicly available *Complete classification of maximum ternary order-three orientable cycles* counts only eight length-nine rotation classes under pairing with **reversal**, not with coordinatewise negation; its edge-pair orbits, maximum period and counted objects are different. Neither result entails the present length-thirteen count. Classical directed matrix-tree and BEST theorems are used as tools, not claimed new.

## Limitations
This is an exact, finite computer-assisted census at a natural first higher-span instance, not an all-span formula or a general enumeration algorithm of proven efficient complexity. The numerical result depends on complete execution of the supplied small exhaustive programs and on the standard matrix-tree and BEST theorems. The two implementations independently agree, but no independent human audit is claimed. Searches and inspection of the most relevant accessible material do not prove worldwide novelty; a previously unindexed equivalent count remains a historical-priority risk. No practical positioning-system performance or error robustness is established.
