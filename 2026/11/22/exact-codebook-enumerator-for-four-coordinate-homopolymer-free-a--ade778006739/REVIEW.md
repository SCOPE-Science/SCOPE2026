# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** Every codeword and every allowed received word are defined explicitly. Multiset conservation gives an exact product decomposition into three and only three canonical components. Each component's complete conflict graph is included as incidence data and independently regenerated by the standalone checker. Its independence polynomial is verified by two different exact algorithms, including a literal scan of all size-six subsets of the 24-vertex case. The local primal and dual fractional certificates coincide; arbitrary-alphabet formulas follow algebraically from component counts. Risk: this is computer-assisted finite enumeration, not a short hand derivation of its 24-vertex coefficient table; checker and certificate are part of the proof package.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** The closest full inspected work on Kendall codes establishes the permutation metric and known small-distance obstructions, not the complete word-language codebook polynomial. The closely related four-coordinate limited-permutation result allows *simultaneous* disjoint swaps, accepts equal-neighbor inputs, and counts optimal size rather than all codebooks: its theorem does not imply the stated result. Searches tested the alternative formulations as independent-set graph polynomials, permutation Kendall codes, restricted run-length word codes, and overlapping prior capacity bounds. The maximal size-five distinct-symbol component is not claimed separately as novel. Risk: a previously computed unlabeled independence polynomial or unindexed exact capacity might supply the factorization implicitly; only inspected sources, not global uniqueness, are asserted.

**Value (PASS).** This is a complete natural finite classification under an orientation/adjacency constraint, not an arbitrary code sample: it counts *all* codebooks by size for every alphabet, identifies the exact threshold where the fractional-output relaxation becomes loose, and determines the full equality-layer population. At four letters, the optimal code size is 35 and there are 51,539,607,552 labeled maxima; as the alphabet grows the fractional overestimate approaches 20% of the integer optimum. The statements are structural and give precise small-block design and enumeration benchmarks. No longer-block capacity is asserted.

Closest literature: Barg–Mazumdar (arXiv:0908.4094); Buzaglo–Etzion (arXiv:1408.4963, arXiv:1310.5515); Langberg–Schwartz–Yaakobi (2015); the published four-coordinate limited-permutation channel result (2026). Their channel/assumption/implication distinctions and residual historical coverage uncertainty are set out in `RESULT.md` and `AUDIT.json`.
