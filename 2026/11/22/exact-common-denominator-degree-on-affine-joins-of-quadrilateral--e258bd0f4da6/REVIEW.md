# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Every vertex of the affine join is incident to precisely \(4k-2\) facets in dimension \(3k-1\), so the published common-denominator vanishing-order theorem forces order at least \(k-1\). Degree \(k-1\) is impossible at an affinely spanning collection of vertices by translation invariance of its nonzero top homogeneous form. Homogenized quadrilateral Wachspress coordinates give a matching degree-\(k\) upper construction. The exact-rational checker passed 336 illustrative cases, without substituting finite tests for the proof. The cited nonstandard vanishing-order lemma was examined in its original source.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
The most relevant 2026 paper gives the vertex-excess lower bound \(k-1\), the general Wachspress upper bound \(k\), and a degree-zero classification, but not the missing bound \(k\) for arbitrary common denominators. The 2025 paper's adjacent result concerns Wachspress adjoint degrees and products/pyramids, not the minimum across arbitrary rational systems. The older projective adjoint work and published planar polygon minimum-degree research do not cover the (\(3k-1\))-dimensional joins. Searches for joins, free sums, facet excess, and product dualities did not identify a stronger covering statement. Historical priority remains a residual risk; no uniqueness or independence is certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact invariant holds for every shape of the quadrilateral factors and in arbitrarily high dimension. It also gives an unbounded separation between two natural operations on the same polygon factors: a product of parallelograms has denominator degree zero, while their affine join has degree \(k\). The intermediate spanning-excess obstruction is reusable for further polytope families. This is a motivated structural classification rather than an arbitrary finite parameter slice.
