# Scientific review: connected zero forcing on three-path theta graphs

**Correctness (PASS).** The three terminal-occupation regimes are exhaustive. With neither terminal selected, two entirely white paths cannot be entered. With exactly one terminal, the selected vertices must be endpoint prefixes, and success is equivalent to at least two seeded paths. With both terminals, connectivity requires at least one completely selected internal path and all selected inner vertices to meet a terminal; successful forcing is then equivalent to at most one empty path. The corresponding endpoint-union counts are \(j+1\) at every proper size \(j<r_i\) and one at full size. Substitution gives the displayed generating polynomial. The standalone checker tested all vertex subsets in 68 theta graphs (1,886,304 masks) and returned `VERIFY_OK`. The infinite claim follows from this constructive case analysis, not finite extrapolation.

**Originality (PASS, provisional).** The principal November 2026 source proves the connected zero-forcing polynomial for trees, and states a general-graph problem without an all-coefficient theorem for theta graphs. The ordinary zero-forcing polynomial does not impose connected initial sets. Prior bounds on the connected forcing number do not imply the complete coefficient enumeration. Targeted comparisons in published mathematical-record search, indexed literature and the accessible existing findings found results for different theta invariants but not an equivalent or stronger formula; absence from indexing is not a proof of world-wide priority. The closest other forcing polynomials concern cycle bouquets, sunlets and single-cycle attached paths, which are not this 2-connected bicyclic family.

**Value (PASS).** The recently posed all-graph coefficient question motivates extending a sharp tree census to natural non-tree families. Three-path theta graphs are a canonical 2-connected series-parallel family with two degree-three terminals, and their all-size connected forcing polynomial is a natural, exactly specified invariant. The surprising uniform count of six minimum connected forcing sets and a closed formula over unrestricted path lengths provide a useful benchmark for connectivity-constrained forcing beyond the zero-forcing number alone, not a renamed calculation of a known table.

**Limits and risks.** Excludes terminal edges of length one, extra chords, attached branches and four-or-more arms. No claim about general graphs, uniqueness of zero-forcing polynomials, or automorphism orbits. Primary novelty comparison is provisional; an unindexed prior exact theta enumeration could cover the statement. Finite checker results are corroboration, not infinite proof.
