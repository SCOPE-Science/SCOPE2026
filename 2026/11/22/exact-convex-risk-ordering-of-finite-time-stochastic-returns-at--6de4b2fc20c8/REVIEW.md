# Scientific review: exact convex-risk ordering of stochastic returns

## Correctness

**PASS.** The comparison concerns the same exploration skeleton and return-start positions at every return diffusivity. The Brownian first-return time with fixed inward drift has the explicit inverse-Gaussian density, equal mean across diffusivities, and a positive–negative–positive difference of densities with exactly two crossings. The equal-mean stop-loss argument establishes a strict convex-order comparison for each excursion, including all positive strikes. Conditional independent convolution transports this comparison to the entire geometrically stopped search, without assuming that return distances are independent of exploration durations. Conditional mean/variance and the law of total variance give the exact affine variance formula. All return distances are bounded and the number of returns is geometric, ensuring the needed finite moments. The killed Green representation proves strict positivity of the slope. The included numerical checker passed 38 independent finite-parameter assertions; these supplement the mathematical proof and do not prove the continuum theorem by enumeration.

## Originality

**PASS, with priority risk.** Chen, Tang, and Li's 18 November 2026 bounded-domain paper gives the full mean, work, split probabilities and a general completion-time transform, along with return-time Laplace data. It does **not** state or prove the global all-convex-loss ordering in the return diffusivity, its strict stop-loss comparison, or the positive affine variance sensitivity in terms of expected work. The latter is derivable from the full transform by differentiating and reorganizing, so it is treated as supporting structure rather than a standalone novelty claim. Singh's 2024 paper has a general joint work–time renewal formula for a semi-infinite search and examines mean cost–time Pareto fronts; the broader formula is fully credited. The previously available framework plus classical inverse-Gaussian laws do not give the convex-order comparison without the two-density-crossing and conditional convolution arguments. A latent equivalent in stochastic orders, renewal reward or stochastic-return literature could still emerge from additional priority checks. The general stop-loss criterion and single-return inverse-Gaussian moments are established mathematics, not claimed as new.

## Value

**PASS.** A protocol designer who can change only the strength of return-phase thermal noise at fixed return drift sees no signal whatsoever in mean completion time, mean energy cost, the complete switching-work law or target-selection probabilities. Nevertheless, all convex time-risk objectives, including mean-square uncertainty and expected overrun above every positive deadline, rise strictly. This is a global, exact diagnostic; it exposes a hidden design degree of freedom and relates its variance sensitivity to a readily interpretable expected work observable. The result does not require a new general renewal transform and does not claim experimental recovery from noisy data.

## Sources and scope

The most relevant sources are Chen, Tang, and Li (2026), arXiv:2611.23790v1 (especially the model, return transform, mean-time derivation, splitting and total-work sections); Singh (2024), arXiv:2407.02071 (especially its joint work–time renewal formula and mean Pareto analysis); and Gupta, Plata, Kundu, and Pal (2020), arXiv:2004.11679 (foundational external-trap stochastic returns). The first source fixes the exact bounded-domain model, and the second covers a general earlier transform. The principal residual risk is an implicitly equivalent convex-order comparison in less directly titled stochastic-order or renewal literature. Numerical checks do not certify global originality. In particular, the return diffusivity is varied independently of the drift coefficient and the boundaries are inactive throughout return.
