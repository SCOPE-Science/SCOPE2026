# Scientific review: subgroup word-subtraction coset and state-complexity theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The deletion condition \(p_j^{-1}p_n\in H\) is exactly equality of left cosets \(p_jH=p_nH\); ordering successive visits to each left coset converts the legal predecessor Grundy values into the consecutive integers below the visit index. The resulting induction is valid for nonnormal subgroups. For uniform independent group-valued letters, successive prefix products are independently uniform by a bijection, and the empty-prefix match is an independent Bernoulli term; this establishes every binomial coefficient, not only moments. With \(H\) normal, a normalized visited-set/freshness automaton has \(2^{[G:H]}\) states; the full alphabet makes every state reachable, and empty- or one-letter suffixes distinguish all states. Boundary cases \(H=G\) and the empty word are accounted for. Direct recursive Grundy enumeration and independent quotient-DFA minimization passed, with exact outputs in the package.

## Originality
PASS against inspected evidence, with residual bibliographic risk. Inoue and Inazu's *Word Subtraction Games*, arXiv:2611.09157v1 (2026), Section 3.4, proves regularity for fixed Grundy-level languages using a general powerset construction, while Problem 3.1 asks for structural understanding of group languages. Neither its general regularity theorem nor its Exercise 6.6 on realizing identity group languages by a *complementary* subtraction set implies the new coset-visit law, exact binomial counts, or the minimal \(2^{[G:H]}\) automaton for the identity-*subgroup* subtraction set. Hetyei's *Enumeration by kernel positions for strongly Bernoulli type truncation games on words*, arXiv:0912.0573v1 (2009), Theorem 2.9, uses strong prefix closure not enjoyed by the present subgroup rule in general and does not claim the present quotient-automaton state complexity. Direct scholarly searches found no equivalent exact theorem. An independently indexed equivalent or a folklore treatment remains a risk; absence from searches is not proof of novelty.

## Value
PASS. The work gives a complete all-word Grundy formula for a natural family of regular subtraction languages, a full exact distribution across every finite group and subgroup (including nonnormal examples), and a sharp, parameterized exponential minimal-automaton size. This resolves a substantive structural special case of the regular-language Grundy framework and quantitatively demonstrates how a small quotient description can lead to an exponentially larger P-language recognizer. The all-index classification and minimality witnesses provide more than a routine isolated small example or a constant-factor computational improvement.

## Scope and limitations
The exponential minimal-state result assumes a normal subgroup and the full group alphabet. For arbitrary subgroups, the coset and binomial results remain valid. Uniform inputs are essential to the stated distribution. Existence of equivalent unindexed literature has not been excluded. Computational certificates check only finite examples; infinite parameters follow from the proof.
