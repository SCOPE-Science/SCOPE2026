# Exact cubic time-arrow and universal total memory in twisted baker maps

## Finding
For every integer \(M\ge2\), let \(F\) be the dimension-\(M\) twisted baker map defined below, equipped with stationary normalized Lebesgue measure. Let \(X_n\) be the first coordinate of \(F^n(Z_0)\), where \(Z_0\) is sampled from that measure. For all integers \(n\ge0\), define the ordered cubic moments
\[
U_M(n)=\mathbb E[X_0^2X_n],\qquad V_M(n)=\mathbb E[X_0X_n^2].
\]
Then \(V_M(n)=0\) for **every** \(M\ge2\) and \(n\ge0\), while the full forward-moment generating function is explicitly rational. With
\[
t_M(z)=(1-z/2)^{M-1},\qquad
D_A(z)=(1-z/4)t_M(z)+z^M/2^{M+1},\qquad
D_Q(z)=(1-z/8)t_M(z)-z^M/2^{M+2},
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{n\ge0}U_M(n)z^n
=-\frac{z\,t_M(z)\left[t_M(z)/4+z^{M-1}/2^{M+1}\right]}{3D_A(z)D_Q(z)}.}
\]
In particular the **first dimension-dependent correction** occurs exactly at lag \(n=M\):
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
U_M(n)=-\frac23\left(4^{-n}-8^{-n}\right)<0\quad(1\le n<M),}
\]
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
U_M(M)=-\frac23\left(4^{-M}-8^{-M}\right)-\frac1{3\cdot 2^{M+1}}<0.}
\]
The integrated time-reversal asymmetry is nevertheless independent of dimension:
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb E[X_0^2X_n]-\mathbb E[X_0X_n^2]\right)=-\frac29.}
\]
Thus the stationary scalar coordinate process is non-reversible already in its two-time cubic statistics, even though all ordinary autocovariances are symmetric by definition. The exact initial law persists for \(M-1\) lags; the full spatial twist first adds a negative correction at lag \(M\).

## Assumptions and scope
For each fixed \(M\ge2\), define \(\mathcal X=[-1,1]\times[0,1]^{M-1}\) and
\[
F(x_1,\ldots,x_M)=
\begin{cases}
(1+2x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_M),&x_1<0,\\
(1-2x_M,x_1,\ldots,x_{M-1}),&x_1\ge0.
\end{cases}
\]
Let \(m=\tfrac12\operatorname{Leb}_M\) on \(\mathcal X\). Each branch maps its half-domain bijectively onto the full domain with Jacobian modulus two, so \(m\) is invariant. The first coordinate \(X_n\) is stationary and uniform on \([-1,1]\). Statements are exact in the dimension, lag, and stationary law; no nonstationary or parameter-perturbed claim is made. Points on branch boundaries are irrelevant to \(m\)-almost-sure statements. The principal subject is dynamical correlations of the specified expanding, noninvertible map (MSC2020 37A25).

## Proof
**1. Exact affine and quadratic transfer.** The two inverse branches are
\[
G_Ly=((y_1-1)/2,y_2,\ldots,y_M),\qquad
G_Ry=(y_2,\ldots,y_M,(1-y_1)/2).
\]
Hence the transfer adjoint is \(P\phi=(\phi\circ G_L+\phi\circ G_R)/2\). Define centered functions \(h_1=x_1\), \(h_j=x_j-1/2\) for \(2\le j\le M\), and \(q_1=h_1^2-1/3\), \(q_j=h_j^2-1/12\) for \(2\le j\le M\). Exact substitution gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Ph_1&=\tfrac14h_1+\tfrac12h_2,\\
Ph_j&=\tfrac12h_j+\tfrac12h_{j+1}&&(2\le j<M),\\
Ph_M&=-\tfrac14h_1+\tfrac12h_M,\\
Pq_1&=\tfrac18q_1+\tfrac12q_2-\tfrac14h_1+\tfrac12h_2,\\
Pq_j&=\tfrac12q_j+\tfrac12q_{j+1}&&(2\le j<M),\\
Pq_M&=\tfrac18q_1+\tfrac12q_M.
\end{aligned}
\]
These formulas remain valid for \(M=2\). Thus the \(2M\)-dimensional polynomial space generated by the displayed functions is invariant under \(P\), with block row-coefficient matrix
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}Q&R\\0&A\end{pmatrix},
\]
where the rows of \(A\) and \(Q\) are the respective \(h\)- and \(q\)-coefficients above, and \(R\) has only \(R_{11}=-1/4\), \(R_{12}=1/2\) nonzero.

**2. Directional asymmetry.** Because the \(h_j\) are mutually independent centered uniforms under \(m\), every cubic integral \(\int h_jh_1^2\,dm\) vanishes, including \(j=1\). Since \(P^nh_1\) is an affine combination of the \(h_j\), the transfer adjunction gives
\[
V_M(n)=\int (P^nh_1)h_1^2\,dm=0.
\]
On the other hand \(\int h_jh_1\,dm=\mathbf 1_{\{j=1\}}/3\), while \(\int q_jh_1\,dm=0\). Hence
\[
U_M(n)=\int(P^nq_1)h_1\,dm=\frac13(B^n)_{q_1,h_1}.
\]
The equality includes \(n=0\), when both sides vanish.

**3. Resolvent and exact rational expression.** The cyclic sparse matrices \(A,Q\) have characteristic resolvent determinants
\[
\det(I-zA)=D_A(z),\qquad \det(I-zQ)=D_Q(z).
\]
This follows directly by selecting either all diagonal factors or the unique closing cycle in the determinant expansion. Their relevant inverse entries are
\[
[(I-zA)^{-1}]_{11}=\frac{t_M(z)}{D_A(z)},\quad
[(I-zA)^{-1}]_{21}=-\frac{z^{M-1}}{2^M D_A(z)},\quad
[(I-zQ)^{-1}]_{11}=\frac{t_M(z)}{D_Q(z)}.
\]
For the middle inverse entry, the unique directed path from row two to column one has \(M-1\) edges with product \(-2^{-M}\); the cofactor gives the displayed rational function. The upper-right block of \((I-zB)^{-1}\) equals \((I-zQ)^{-1}zR(I-zA)^{-1}\). Taking its \((q_1,h_1)\) entry and multiplying by \(1/3\) gives precisely the asserted \(U_M\) generating function, as an identity of formal series.

**4. Absolute convergence and integrated identity.** The eigenvalues of \(A\) and \(Q\) satisfy, respectively,
\[
(\lambda-1/4)(\lambda-1/2)^{M-1}=-2^{-(M+1)},\qquad
(\lambda-1/8)(\lambda-1/2)^{M-1}=2^{-(M+2)}.
\]
If \(|\lambda|\ge1\), the left sides have modulus at least \(3\cdot2^{-(M+1)}\) and \(7\cdot2^{-(M+2)}\), respectively, which contradict their right sides. Both blocks have spectral radius strictly below one. Therefore the series for \(U_M(n)\) is absolutely summable, and evaluating its generating function at \(z=1\) is legitimate. There, \(t_M(1)=2^{-(M-1)}\), \(D_A(1)=t_M(1)\), and \(D_Q(1)=3t_M(1)/4\). The stated rational expression simplifies to \(-2/9\). Since \(U_M(0)=V_M(n)=0\), this is the integrated time-arrow sum.

**5. Sharp first return to the initial coordinate.** In the coefficient graph of the block matrix, the only way to close a coordinate-index excursion that starts at index one is to traverse all \(M\) indices. Below degree \(M\), the rational generating function therefore agrees with its no-wrap expansion
\[
-\frac{z}{12(1-z/4)(1-z/8)}
=-\frac23\sum_{n\ge1}(4^{-n}-8^{-n})z^n.
\]
The earliest additional path contributing to \(U_M(n)\) is \(q_1\to h_2\to h_3\to\cdots\to h_M\to h_1\), whose \(M\) edge weights multiply to \(-2^{-(M+1)}\). Its covariance normalization \(1/3\) adds exactly \(-1/(3\cdot2^{M+1})\) at \(n=M\). All other winding paths have greater length. This proves both sharp formulas and the strict negative sign through lag \(M\).

## Relationship to prior work
Saiki, Takahasi and Yorke [1] introduced this map and proved Bernoulli coding, periodic-orbit spectral properties and mixing. Their results do not provide cubic ordered two-time moments. Two later calculations [2,3] determine the same map's **first-coordinate second-order autocovariances**, including the first negative lag and variance cancellation; the affine transfer matrix there does not contain the quadratic-to-affine coupling \(Pq_1\) required here. An additional dimension-two polynomial calculation [4] treats the quadratic *energy autocovariance* and its eigenvalue; it determines neither the all-dimensional ordered cubic moments nor their first-wrap correction. A general exact cubic-correlation result for one-dimensional full-branch interval maps [5] has a different dynamical setting and does not directly specialize to the present spatially twisting, multi-coordinate map. The direction-sensitive cubic formulas are not an assertion of a new general theory of non-reversibility.

## Limitations
The theorem is for the first physical coordinate, stationary normalized Lebesgue sampling and the specified map. Its time arrow is a two-time distributional asymmetry detected by particular third moments; it is not a claim of thermodynamic entropy production. The signs of later coefficients \(U_M(n)\) beyond lag \(M\) need not stay negative. No uniform-in-dimension mixing rate, nonstationary limit, or perturbative robustness is proved. The two-dimensional specialization partly intersects the already established polynomial transfer framework in [4]; undetected equivalent computations in the literature remain a publication-priority risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
