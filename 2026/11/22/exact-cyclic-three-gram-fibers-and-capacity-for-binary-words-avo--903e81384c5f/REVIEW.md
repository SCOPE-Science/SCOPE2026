# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The gap-profile identities are direct and invertible, the Burnside fixed-point count is exact with divisibility of the period, and the singleton classification divides all feasible parameters into constructive singletons and explicit distinct counterexamples. The two rational generating functions follow from independent nonnegative slack summations. Exhaustive reconstruction through circumference fifteen verified every fiber and both counting series. Edge cases, including constant and alternating cycles, are handled separately. Limits: finite checks supplement, but do not replace, the all-length proofs.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The published 2013 work introducing \(k\)-Abelian equivalence addresses finite/infinite word complexity and not this cyclic constrained inverse-fiber classification. Genome read reconstruction methods provide context but not the stated formulas. Ranked prior records include full binary three-gram *profile* counts, no-`11` four-gram fibers, and three-gram fibers under the different prohibition of isolated ones. These do not by their stated results imply the exact no-`11` cyclic **three**-gram fibers or the singleton spectrum; the read length and input language genuinely change the inverse equivalence relation. The most relevant records' complete files were inaccessible, so equivalent derivations hidden there remain a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The read model is a natural low-complexity cyclic sequencing channel on the Fibonacci (no-adjacent-one) constraint. The result classifies every observation fiber, yields exact zero-error capacity and uniquely reconstructible profiles, and identifies the first failure of uniqueness and a quadratic-versus-linear asymptotic separation. It is not merely a numerical table or an arbitrary finite slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
Karhumäki–Saarela–Zamboni (2013), arXiv:1301.5104, develops general \(k\)-Abelian equivalence; Bresler–Bresler–Tse (2013), arXiv:1301.0068, treats read-based assembly. Related published records on unrestricted binary cyclic three-gram profile counts, four-gram spectra under no adjacent ones, and three-gram fibers forbidding isolated ones provide comparison but do not establish the present source-restricted statement from accessible evidence. The inaccessible records remain a risk. The theorem does not address noisy readouts, linear strings, or reconstruction without the `11`-free prior.
