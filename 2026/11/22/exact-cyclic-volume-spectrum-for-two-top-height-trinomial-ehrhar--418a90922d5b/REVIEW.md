# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The upper-height subgroup equals the whole simplex group by a coordinate-image averaging inequality. Every high element has Euler totient of order at most four, so the cyclic group has order at most sixty. The arithmetic checker then visits every unordered pair of distinct high indices for all orders four through sixty: 34,219 total. Carry restrictions are necessary coordinatewise, and any faithful candidate is tested for rational inconsistency using an augmented matrix with certified full column rank modulo a prime. It excludes 2,352 of 2,366 faithful candidate pairs and retains fourteen pairs only at orders 4,5,6,8,10. A second prime reproduces the classification. Five explicit faithful residue generators, each checked exactly, attain all values for every exponent by coordinate replication. Finite testing is not substituted for the proved universal cutoff.

## Originality — PASS (subject to historical priority)

The close 2026 full-text general bound gives normalized volume at most twelve for all groups and an example attaining twelve, but it does not distinguish cyclic full-simplex groups or classify their attainable volume spectrum. Tsuchiya (2026), Proposition 4.3 bounds the volume in terms of the top coefficient and includes a cyclic intermediate-subgroup subcase with an additional support condition, not the theorem's unconditional full-group cyclic spectrum. Batyrev–Hofscheier (2013) supplies the group-to-simplex correspondence, not this classification. Targeted published-record, published-finding corpus, and own-history searches found no statement implying the five-value spectrum. Exact historical priority remains open to independent investigation.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Cyclic simplices are a natural group-theoretic subclass, and sharp cyclic volume ten is strictly smaller than the already known global bound twelve. More strongly, the full normalized-volume spectrum has precisely five permitted numbers, all realized in every exponent, answering a structural refinement of the dimension-free trinomial problem. The arithmetic obstruction to orders seven, nine, eleven and twelve is not implied by a generic volume bound.
