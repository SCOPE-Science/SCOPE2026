**Correctness — PASS.** For every unoccupied vertex set the next successful edge is uniform on the currently feasible edges. The resulting exact vertex-state recurrence evaluates \(94940\) reachable states. A second derivation enumerates the complete set of \(3781\) maximal matchings and sums the exact probability of each via \(111376\) matching-prefix evaluations. The independently implemented rational laws agree coefficient by coefficient, sum to one, and yield strictly positive exact cumulative-distribution differences at all four nontrivial thresholds. The graph is constructed directly as \(\operatorname{GP}(10,2)\). The finite exhaustive scope is a proof for this graph, not a simulation or a claim for arbitrary graphs.

**Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic limits.** Penrose–Sudbury give the dimer-adsorption framework, Gamarnik–Goldberg give general regular-graph greedy results, and Andova–Kardoš–Škrekovski establish fullerene saturation bounds and the already-known minimum six for this graph. Published dodecahedral matching-complex calculations supply unweighted matching multiplicities, and an exact Petersen-graph kinetics calculation concerns a different graph. Direct statement-and-implication comparisons and targeted source/database searches did not locate the evaluated C20 distribution or its strict stochastic comparison. This is not a uniqueness proof: older unindexed tables and the inaccessible complete text of Pippenger remain priority risks.

**Value — PASS.** The unique smallest fullerene provides a canonical finite trivalent substrate with twelve pentagonal faces. Full exact terminal probabilities quantify a genuine nonuniform sampling bias among naturally defined maximal coverages. The comparison is mathematically substantive, not universal: the six-vertex graph with edges \(\{01,12,14,15,23,24,25\}\) reverses the relevant lower-tail inequality, so the strong dodecahedral stochastic order is graph-dependent. A \(7.761\)-fold perfect-matching selection bias and a fully reproducible complete distribution give a benchmark for finite dimer-jamming studies.

**Limits.** One finite graph; no thermodynamic limit, transient kinetics, graph-universal theorem, or empirical material claim. The exact matching counts and minimal matching size are acknowledged prior facts. Independent external validation has not been conducted.
