# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The pair triangles supply a necessary-and-sufficient finite diagram test, and the countable finite-distance Urysohn structure realizes every such metric diagram. The three unary normal forms and the three distance-dependent local pair rules are derived explicitly. The inclusion-exclusion and position-of-one partitions provide proofs for arbitrary positive base size; the exact finite enumeration in `artifacts/verify.py` independently checks all named sizes through five and does not replace the proof. The asymptotics follow from exact leading terms.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The closest direct literature is Sauer (2010, arXiv:1010.4212), which gives the universal metric-space and Katětov setup and a finite extension property but does not state any equilateral ordered-pair distance census. The published 2026 Widom–Rowlinson type-polynomial result gives unary spectra and graph homomorphism counts on a related parameter slice but not the three distance-resolved ordered pair formulas or conditional limiting laws. Searches for these formulas and their Katětov, equilateral and metric-type aliases found no exact matching result. An equivalent finite-alphabet enumeration might appear elsewhere; absence from search results is not a proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural question is how the old parameter spacing changes the possible mutual distances in the next complete ordered pair type, not merely how many unary types exist. The theorem gives the entire all-size distribution across the three possible old spacings and shows a sharp qualitative distinction: middle spacing concentrates at mutual distance two, while the two extreme spacings retain three nonzero limiting masses with different weights. Exact distributions and mean compatibility multiplicities are useful finite-model invariants and benchmarks for general non-equilateral bases, although the finite-alphabet proof is short and no downstream theorem is claimed.

## Limits of review
Only equilateral bases and two new points are classified. A general type-function criterion by itself does not supply these explicit counts, but the proof is an elementary specialization of that known criterion. Historical priority and independent correctness have not been certified.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
