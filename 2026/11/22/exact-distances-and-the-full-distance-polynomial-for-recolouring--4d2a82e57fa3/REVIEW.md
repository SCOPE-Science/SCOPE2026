# Scientific assessment of the boundary result

**Correctness.** The swap obstruction gives the extra move in the lower bound. For two or more swapped leaves the spare-color count holds even at \(k=n+1\), giving a path with two center moves and exactly one move per differing leaf. The one-swap case uses a temporary leaf color instead. Counting independent leaf transitions gives the ordered-pair polynomial; the explicit \(k=n\) example proves sharpness.

**Relationship to prior work.** Cambie, Cames van Batenburg and Cranston (arXiv:2204.07928, §5, Theorem 17) already imply the complete star metric for \(k\ge n+2\), not merely its diameter. The bidirected color-shift edges are the swaps; their matching number is zero or one. The new scope assessed here is the extension to \(k=n+1\), which violates that theorem's center list-size assumption. The larger-palette counting formulas are corollaries of the known metric. Historical priority at the boundary is provisional.

**Value and limits.** At the smaller palette the result classifies every pair and its metric shell, and the \(k=n\) witness shows why reducing the palette further changes the law. The theorem concerns named colors on stars under proper single-vertex recolorings and does not claim the same boundary for arbitrary trees or arbitrary lists. Finite breadth-first checks corroborate the proof but do not replace it.
