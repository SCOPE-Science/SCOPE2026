# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The four-family Pansiot–Brlek classification of Thue–Morse square factors and its alignment lemma, inspected in Brown et al. (2006, Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.2), justify the exact square-suffix recursion. The derived adjacent-equality and alternating-triple recurrences are proved for all indices and lead to a closed dyadic count; an arbitrary-prefix recurrence proves natural density with an effective squared-logarithmic error. Independent direct all-period checks at 2048 endpoints, a published 32-term match, and exact dyadic checks to exponent 20 agree. The finite checks are corroboration, not an infinity certificate.

## Originality
PASS against checked literature and available mathematical records. The 2007 introduction of the transform and the 2017 OEIS annotation leave the density as an observed pattern. The 2006 Thue–Morse square paper analyzes squares beginning at positions; it supplies a foundational classification but not the exact square-ending prefix-count law. The 2026 curling-transform paper concerns power avoidance, not the exact frequency of ones for the Thue–Morse transform. Similar published Abelian-square statistics describe a different equivalence notion. A derivation implicit in unindexed earlier work remains a priority risk, and search cannot establish uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The theorem proves an explicitly posed natural density observation for a canonical automatic sequence and supplies a stronger exact all-dyadic-level formula with parity-sensitive logarithmic bias. The object and observable are independently motivated by the established curling-number transform, not a chosen artificial slice.

## Scope and remaining limits
The result is limited to the binary Thue–Morse word, and the error order for arbitrary lengths is not asserted sharp. Its proof uses the published square-factor classification as a cited premise. Independent external audit has not been carried out.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
