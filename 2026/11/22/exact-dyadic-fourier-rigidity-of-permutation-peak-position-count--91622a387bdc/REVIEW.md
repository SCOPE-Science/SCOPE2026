# Scientific review: exact dyadic Fourier rigidity of permutation peak-position counts

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is reconstructed for all \(n\ge1\) and every frequency subset of the interior peak positions, for the unnormalized counting convolution. Joint peak-event probabilities either vanish for adjacent required peaks or factor over disjoint alternating blocks. The odd-length zigzag probabilities are coefficients of \(\tan z\); its differential equation proves that they lie in the localization with odd denominators. Character expansion is therefore a \(2\)-adic unit after division by \(n!\). Inversion of the Walsh matrix modulo odd primes together with the exactly \(2^{n-1}\) peak-free permutations gives the gcd. The independently constructed descent enumerator and rational-moment algorithm agree on all computed spectra through length thirteen; direct permutations are checked through length eight. Risk: no computational finite cutoff is substituted for the proof; the main all-size inference is purely algebraic.

## Originality — PASS (bounded)
The most relevant primary texts are Stanley, *A Survey of Alternating Permutations* (2009); Billey–Burdzy–Sagan, *Permutations with given peak set* (2012); Billey–Fahrbach–Talmage, *Coefficients and roots of peak polynomials* (2014); and Zhuang, *Counting permutations by runs* (2015). Their inspected definitions, theorem statements and relevant enumeration sections provide the underlying peak and tangent machinery, not the asserted nonvanishing of every arbitrary peak-set Fourier character with the exact common dyadic valuation and gcd. A related exact Fourier theorem for permutation descent indicator patterns treats a different observable and has many vanishing characters; the nonlinear peak map does not let its support theorem imply this result. Risks: a less discoverable signed peak-set transform or a general multivariate generating-functional theorem might imply the conclusion. No absolute uniqueness or independent priority certification is claimed.

## Value — PASS
This theorem completely classifies Fourier information loss for a natural, multiplicity-weighted peak-position statistic in every dimension, in contrast to finite enumerations or single statistics. It also establishes the strongest uniform power-of-two divisibility shared by all characters, exactly rather than by a bound. The alternating-block lemma connects local pattern probabilities to finite-group harmonic recovery in a reusable way. It does not claim the exact smallest magnitude or inverse operator norm, and those are separate questions.
