# Scientific review: edge-correlation fixation theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The object is the sixteen-profile, four-site death–birth Markov chain with symmetric, static, two-quality edge benefits and explicitly specified payoff/linear-fitness normalization. Positive fitness on the stated domains makes all transition probabilities well defined; connectedness yields almost-sure absorption. Exact matrix inversion, symmetry quotienting and rational cancellation establish the cost-free identity for every nonnegative benefit pair and positive selection intensity. A separate exact parameter elimination verifies both positive-cost rational formulas, the strict comparison and the sole neutral crossing. The coefficients after the bijective substitution \(c=2t/(1+t)\) prove all asserted signs and root uniqueness for the *entire* stated parameter interval. The independent full-state calculation checks the symmetry quotient against the original fourteen transient states. The numerical root is only a displayed approximation to a uniquely characterized algebraic number. Boundary cases with vanishing fitness are explicitly excluded.

## Originality — PASS, subject to literature-priority limitations

The novelty claim is **not** that spatial heterogeneity changes cooperation, nor that the first weak-selection coefficient can be arrangement-independent: those phenomena appear in earlier literature. Kargaran, Kaveh and Chatterjee (2026) prove and investigate checkerboard-versus-segregated behavior for *node-dependent donor* benefits, including exact checkerboard and approximate segregated expressions on cycles. They do not supply the *symmetric edge-dependent benefit* zero-cost all-strength identity or the displayed four-cycle positive-cost polynomial threshold. Zhang and colleagues (2026) treat changing edge games and weak selection; Pi and colleagues (2026) study dynamic variable games and weak-selection optimization. A direct statement/implication comparison distinguishes frozen, symmetric edge-pair donation benefits and finite-strength fixation from these results. Classical regular-graph fixation methods provide general machinery but not the displayed algebraic classification. Search records on four-cycle habitat patterns concern mutant-only site fitness rather than benefits delivered over colored edges. Residual risks include incompletely accessible full text for Pi (2026) and a finite-model special case possibly hidden in unrelated supplements; absence from search results does not prove novelty.

## Value — PASS

The theorem answers a motivated inverse-comparison problem: with a completely fixed topology, a fixed multiset of interaction payoffs, and a uniform initial mutant, how much can arrangement alone alter takeover? In the exact four-edge motif, alternating benefits are *perfectly neutral* at zero cost for arbitrary contrast and selection intensity, while clustering is strictly superneutral. At strictly positive cost, the result gives an exact threshold where clustering changes from superneutral to subneutral, while maintaining strict superiority over alternating. This is a structural law, not an unmotivated single table entry; the positive-cost example shows a qualitative decision difference under realistic nonzero contribution costs. It is restricted to one finite topology and does not by itself imply population-level welfare, equilibrium selection or a general spatial-correlation law.
