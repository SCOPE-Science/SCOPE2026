# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
The necessity follows from invariance of the finite essential-edge permutation and a positive-measure, nonmeagre open edge obstruction. For sufficiency, each closed target has an explicit increasing fixed-endpoint interval homeomorphism on every incident edge, with a countable fixed-point exceptional set. The derivative and continuity bounds justify gluing. Every target point is approached by convergence from an open subset, proving minimality for both full-measure and residual basins. The circle case is obtained by a target-point cut. Finite exact tests check algebra and examples only; the written construction supplies the infinite proof.

## Originality (O): PASS within checked evidence
The closest source, Hantáková–Mlíchová (arXiv:2611.15353v1, Sections 4–6), establishes universal realization on manifolds and one negative tripod example, expressly leaving the branched-continuum case open. The result here solves the entire finite-graph subcase and adds generic limit sets with the same witness. Mai–Shao (2007) studies individual omega-limit sets of fixed graph maps; Robinson–Sánchez-Gabites (arXiv:1306.5959) studies topological global attractors in Euclidean spaces. No inspected theorem implies the edge-incidence classification. Specialized earlier graph-homeomorphism literature may contain an equivalent result; the search cannot exclude that.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
The problem is a natural structural existence question motivated by a published open direction: precisely which closed targets are attainable on a branched one-dimensional continuum? On every finite connected graph, the condition is reduced to meeting each essential edge closure; moreover one map realizes both measure-theoretic and category-theoretic attractor definitions. The branching obstruction and the exact arc/circle universality dichotomy are useful consequences not limited to a constructed example.

## Scope and risks
This is not a theorem about all branched continua or general continuous graph maps. It requires a probability measure equivalent to arclength, only produces a particularly simple pointwise-fixed target, and has not undergone independent validation. Older unpublished or unindexed equivalences remain a priority risk.
