# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed sequence is exactly the width-three negative-index poly-Bernoulli sequence. Deriving its exponential generating function gives the three-term power expression. The ten residue branches at eleven give nine unit-slope scaled isometries and one branch divisible by \(121\) in its first differences. Its normalized analytic function reduces to \(7t^2+10t\) modulo \(11\); the simple derivative root yields a unique critical center and a global quadratic coordinate. This establishes the value-support theorem and the full square-root fiber formula for all \(k\ge1\), with an independent proof of least period. The actual standalone checker verifies all targets through \(11^5\) and tests least periods through \(11^4\). These finite checks are corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Kim–Krotov–Lee (2011) supplies the negative-index poly-Bernoulli/lonesum identification and Stirling formula. Bényi–Hajnal (2015) supplies related combinatorial interpretations. Ohno–Sakata (2024) supplies general negative-index congruences and eventual periodicity, but not this value set and critical fiber law. OEIS A027650 contains the exact exponential formula and initial terms, rather than the all-depth residue distribution. Targeted searches of published mathematical findings and research literature covered those equivalent descriptions and other p-adic image literature. No known source examined states or logically implies the full result. Historical priority is qualified, especially as some wider p-adic exponential-polynomial theorem may imply a special case implicitly; historical sources are not an exhaustive search.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact prime-power image and fibers of a classical counting sequence form a natural invariant of lonesum matrices with three fixed columns. The exceptional branch is a translated square and creates unbounded spikes in output multiplicities even though the seven residue balls are already covered by the other nine branches. This provides genuine information beyond a period bound or a list of terms. The theorem does not assert analogous results for other widths or primes.
