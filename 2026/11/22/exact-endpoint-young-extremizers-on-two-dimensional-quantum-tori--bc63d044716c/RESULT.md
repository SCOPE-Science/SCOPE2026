# Exact endpoint Young extremizers on two-dimensional quantum tori

## Finding

Fix real deformation parameters \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\), put \(\alpha=\alpha_1+\alpha_2\), and let \(x\) and \(y\) be nonzero finite Fourier polynomials on the corresponding two-dimensional quantum tori. For coefficientwise quantum-torus convolution, the sharp endpoint Young bound
\[
\|x\star_{\alpha_1,\alpha_2}y\|_\infty\leq \|x\|_2\|y\|_2
\]
is an equality **if and only if** both conditions below hold.

1. The two coefficient supports agree, say \(S\subset\mathbb Z^2\), and their coefficient moduli are proportional: \(|a_n|=\lambda|b_n|\) for all \(n\in S\), for one \(\lambda>0\).
2. Choose any \(n_0\in S\) and set \(H=\langle n-n_0:n\in S\rangle_{\mathbb Z}\). The commutator obstruction vanishes on \(H\): \(\alpha\det(h,k)\in\mathbb Z\) for every \(h,k\in H\). Moreover, there is a map \(\chi:H\to\mathbb T\) with
   \[
   \chi(h)\chi(k)=e^{\pi i\alpha\det(h,k)}\chi(h+k)
   \]
   such that \(a_nb_n\chi(n-n_0)/|a_nb_n|\) is constant on \(S\).

The map \(\chi\) is a character of the commuting *twisted* Fourier system (not necessarily a character for ordinary addition). Every such map is explicitly parameterized by one phase per lattice basis vector, as proved below. The criterion makes no restriction on the individual input deformations beyond their sum.

In particular, if \(\alpha\) is irrational, equality forces \(S\) to lie in an affine lattice line. If \(\alpha=p/q\) is rational in lowest terms with \(q>0\), equality permits a rank-two support lattice precisely when its basis determinant is divisible by \(q\), provided the stated phase condition also holds. For a support consisting of exactly three noncollinear points, **every** nonzero coefficient-phase pattern can be aligned: in that case equality holds exactly when the two input modulus profiles are proportional and \(q\) divides the signed area determinant (and no such triple can be an extremizer for irrational \(\alpha\)).

## Assumptions and scope

Write \(n=(n_1,n_2)\), \(m=(m_1,m_2)\), and \(\det(n,m)=n_1m_2-n_2m_1\). For real \(\beta\), the standard Weyl unitaries \(W_n^{\beta}\), \(n\in\mathbb Z^2\), of the quantum torus satisfy
\[
W_n^{\beta}W_m^{\beta}=e^{\pi i\beta\det(n,m)}W_{n+m}^{\beta},\qquad
(W_n^{\beta})^*=W_{-n}^{\beta},\qquad
\tau_\beta(W_n^{\beta})=\mathbf1_{n=0}.
\]
The canonical trace \(\tau_\beta\) is faithful, normalized, and makes these unitaries orthonormal in \(L_2\). Thus every finite sum \(x=\sum_{n\in S_x}a_nW_n^{\alpha_1}\), \(y=\sum_{n\in S_y}b_nW_n^{\alpha_2}\), with all listed coefficients nonzero, satisfies
\[
x\star_{\alpha_1,\alpha_2}y=\sum_{n\in S_x\cap S_y}a_nb_nW_n^{\alpha},\qquad
\|x\|_2^2=\sum_{n\in S_x}|a_n|^2,\quad
\|y\|_2^2=\sum_{n\in S_y}|b_n|^2.
\]
Here \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\) is the operator norm in the canonical tracial representation. All claims concern **nonzero finite Fourier polynomials** and exact equality at exponents \((p,q,r)=(2,2,\infty)\). There is no claim about infinite Fourier series or other Young exponents.

## Proof

**Step 1 (the two forced equalities).** Put \(c_n=a_nb_n\), assigning zero to unlisted coefficients. The triangle inequality for unitary Weyl terms and Cauchy–Schwarz give
\[
\left\|\sum_n c_nW_n^\alpha\right\|_\infty
\leq \sum_n|c_n|
\leq \left(\sum_n|a_n|^2\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_n|b_n|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Equality in the second inequality for two nonzero finite nonnegative sequences is equivalent to proportionality of all corresponding magnitudes, **including zero locations**. Thus \(S_x=S_y=S\), with \(|a_n|=\lambda|b_n|\). It remains to classify equality in the first inequality with \(c_n\ne0\) on \(S\).

**Step 2 (common-eigenvector lemma, necessity).** For any finitely many unitaries \(V_j\) in a unital \(C^*\)-algebra and nonzero complex coefficients \(c_j\), equality \(\|\sum c_jV_j\|=\sum|c_j|\) implies that in some unital GNS representation there is a unit vector \(\xi\) for which all vectors \(c_j\pi(V_j)\xi\) point in exactly the same direction. Indeed, choose a norm-attaining state for \((\sum c_jV_j)^*(\sum c_jV_j)\); its GNS cyclic vector realizes the squared norm, and equality holds in the Hilbert-space triangle inequality. A norm-attaining state exists by the state-space maximum principle for a positive algebra element.

Fix \(n_0\in S\) and left-multiply the output by \((W_{n_0}^{\alpha})^*\). Let
\[
V_h=(W_{n_0}^{\alpha})^*W_{n_0+h}^{\alpha}
=e^{-\pi i\alpha\det(n_0,h)}W_h^\alpha.
\]
Then \(V_0=1\) and
\[
V_hV_k=e^{\pi i\alpha\det(h,k)}V_{h+k},\qquad
V_hV_k=e^{2\pi i\alpha\det(h,k)}V_kV_h.
\]
Because the term indexed by \(n_0\) is the identity, equality in Step 2 forces \(\pi(V_{n-n_0})\xi=\chi(n-n_0)\xi\) for \(n\in S\), with \(\chi(n-n_0)\in\mathbb T\), and forces the products \(c_n\chi(n-n_0)\) to have a single phase. Products and adjoints extend the eigenvalue map to the subgroup \(H\) generated by the differences. The displayed product law forces the twisted-character identity on \(H\). Since both \(V_h\) and \(V_k\) act by scalars on the same nonzero vector, their commutator scalar must equal one: \(e^{2\pi i\alpha\det(h,k)}=1\), equivalently \(\alpha\det(h,k)\in\mathbb Z\). This proves necessity without assuming the operator norm is attained by a vector in the original Hilbert-space representation.

**Step 3 (twisted characters and sufficient norm attainment).** Suppose the subgroup \(H\) is isotropic for the commutator, meaning the integrality condition above. Since \(H\) is free abelian of rank \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(h_1,\dots,h_r\). For rank one every twisted character has \(\chi(kh_1)=u_1^k\) with \(u_1\in\mathbb T\). For rank two set \(A=\det(h_1,h_2)\); necessarily \(\alpha A\in\mathbb Z\), and for arbitrary \(u_1,u_2\in\mathbb T\) the formula
\[
\chi(kh_1+\ell h_2)=u_1^ku_2^\ell e^{-\pi i\alpha A k\ell}
\]
satisfies the twisted-character law. Indeed, the ratio between its left and right sides reduces to \(e^{2\pi i\alpha A k'\ell}=1\) for two pairs of integer coordinates. Conversely any such map is determined by \(u_1=\chi(h_1)\), \(u_2=\chi(h_2)\). Rank zero has the unique map \(\chi(0)=1\).

The unitaries \(V_{h_j}\) commute. Their joint spectral support is all of \(\mathbb T^r\): for any nonzero integer multi-index \((k_j)\), their product of powers is a scalar multiple of \(V_{\sum k_jh_j}\), whose canonical trace is zero because the basis is independent. Therefore their tracial joint spectral measure has all nonconstant Fourier coefficients zero and is normalized Haar measure on \(\mathbb T^r\). Its support is the whole torus. Equivalently, the commuting subalgebra they generate is faithfully represented as continuous functions on \(\mathbb T^r\). Every choice of \((u_j)\) consequently occurs as a character in the spectrum of that subalgebra, assigning \(\chi(h)\) to \(V_h\). If the coefficients obey the phase-alignment condition, evaluation at that character gives
\[
\left\|\sum_{n\in S}c_nV_{n-n_0}\right\|_\infty
\geq \left|\sum_{n\in S}c_n\chi(n-n_0)\right|=\sum_{n\in S}|c_n|.
\]
The unitary triangle inequality gives the opposite bound. Combined with Step 1, this proves sufficiency and the full equivalence.

**Step 4 (arithmetic and three-point consequences).** If \(\alpha\) is irrational and \(H\) has rank two, any lattice basis has nonzero integer determinant \(A\), so \(\alpha A\notin\mathbb Z\), a contradiction. Rank at most one is exactly containment of \(S\) in an affine lattice line. For reduced rational \(\alpha=p/q\), isotropy in rank two is equivalent to \(q\mid A\). When \(S=\{n_0,n_1,n_2\}\) consists of three noncollinear points, the differences themselves form a basis of \(H\); the two free phases \(u_1,u_2\) can align the two non-base coefficients with the base coefficient, regardless of the original coefficient phases. Thus the exact three-point criterion follows. For collinear arithmetic triples \(\{n_0,n_0+h,n_0+2h\}\), by contrast, the phase condition is that \(c_{n_0}c_{n_0+2h}/c_{n_0+h}^2\) be a positive real number.

## Verification

The result is an exact analytical equivalence. Reproducible finite checks in `VERIFY.py` test the rank-two cocycle identity on bounded integer coordinates and check arithmetic for the explicit half-deformation example below. They are consistency diagnostics, **not** exhaustive verification of arbitrary inputs or a replacement for Steps 1–4.

For the half-deformed torus put \(U=W_{(1,0)}^{1/2}\) and \(V=W_{(0,1)}^{1/2}\). The anticommuting unitaries have central squares \(U^2,V^2\) with full joint spectrum by the trace moment argument of Step 3. In an irreducible representation they have the form \(e^{ix}Z,e^{iy}X\), where \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), \(X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), and the squares determine \(x,y\) modulo \(\pi\). All square phase pairs occur: a state extending evaluation at a point of the central spectrum has a nonzero GNS representation on which both squares are scalar, because the cyclic vector is an eigenvector for the center. After scaling, the two anticommuting self-adjoint involutions generate the Pauli matrix algebra, so each such central quotient gives the stated norm calculation.

For \(M=I+e^{ix}Z+e^{iy}X\), the larger eigenvalue of \(M^*M\) is \(3+2\sqrt{R}\), where \(R=\cos^2 x+\cos^2 y+\sin^2(y-x)\). Indeed, its traceless part is \(2\cos x\,Z+2\cos y\,X+2\sin(y-x)\,iZX\), a sum of three pairwise anticommuting involutions with real coefficients. Writing \(v=|\cos(x-y)|\) gives \(R\le 2+v-v^2\le 9/4\); equality holds at \(x=\pi/6,\ y=-\pi/6\). Hence \(\|1+U+V\|=\sqrt{6}<3\); the particular fiber \(x=y=0\) has norm \(1+\sqrt{2}\), not the norm of the element in the torus. For the isotropic support, \(\|1+U^2+V\|=3\) by the equality criterion (or evaluation at the Pauli fiber \(x=y=0\)).

## Relationship to prior work

Sukochev, Tastankul, Tulenov and Zanin introduce the coefficientwise deformation-addition convolution, establish the general sharp Young inequality with constant one (their Theorem 4.1), and note equality for constant inputs. Their Proposition 3.5 supplies the exact finite Fourier formula used here. The present classification addresses the missing equality structure at one endpoint and reveals an arithmetic dependence on the **sum** of the two deformations.

Liu and Wu characterize extremal pairs for convolution on **unimodular Kac algebras**, particularly from equality at an interior Young triple. That operation is governed by a fixed quantum-group coproduct and is not the deformation-addition map between two quantum-torus algebras used here; the characterization does not directly state this finite-support endpoint lattice-and-phase criterion. Huang, Liu and Wu's Frobenius-von-Neumann framework studies other quantum convolution and entropic extremizers, likewise not this explicit Fourier polynomial endpoint classification.

The separate three-mode result *Magnetic-flux rigidity and a half-flux norm transition for three-mode quantum-torus convolution* gives the same criterion for three affinely independent frequencies, and its weighted half-flux norm formula includes the equal-amplitude value \(\sqrt6\) computed above. Neither the three-point criterion nor this example is a distinct contribution relative to that result. The distinction here is the criterion for arbitrary finite supports, including the compatibility of phases across additive relations among support differences.

## Limitations

The classification assumes finite Fourier support and excludes zero inputs. It does not classify all \(L_2\) extremizers, claim strict quantitative stability for near-equality, or characterize equality for other Young exponents. The endpoint criterion may be viewed as a special case of a more general result on sums of unitaries in twisted group algebras; a completely exhaustive historical priority comparison is not asserted. The arithmetic condition alone is insufficient for supports with additive relations: the twisted phase condition is also necessary.
