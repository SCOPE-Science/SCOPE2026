# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The proof isolates the unique nonunit primary factor \(X^q+1\), proves it is coprime to the complementary factor modulo \(p^2\), and uses exact binomial valuations to obtain \(Y^q=pY^{q/p}W(Y)\) with \(W\) invertible. Explicit image-module cardinalities before and after time \(q\) yield the stated kernel law, including both endpoints and the sharp final population. A dependency-free checker independently tests local and whole-system kernels and exhaustively partitions all \(9^6\) states for one example. Risk: tests are finite; validity for arbitrary \(p,n,t\) depends on the algebraic proof.

**Originality (PASS on inspected evidence).** The 2019 Dular paper treats periodic populations for the prime-field case; the 2024 Lewis–Tefft manuscript treats maximal first-entry length, not the depth-by-depth histogram; the broader publicly indexed exact-maximum theorem does not imply all intermediate kernel sizes. The two-phase odd-prime-square law is neither the same invariant nor a direct corollary of those statements. Risk: Breuer's 2010 relevant full text was unavailable although the abstract describes a separable regime, and unindexed equivalents can exist.

**Value (PASS).** The source literature specifically develops the cyclic-sum map and its prime-power obstructions. Instead of a maximum alone, the theorem determines a natural complete invariant—how many configurations enter a cycle at each iteration—for every even length carrying an odd-prime repeated factor. Its phase transition from \(p^2\)-fold to \(p\)-fold population growth exposes the prime-square nilpotent extension structure, and the formula gives exact basin-depth proportions for infinitely many systems.
