# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. The fixed-position word length is forced by six wildcard-covered factors. The prefix-balance lemma gives exactly six nonconstant period-three prefixes at order at least five. The number of completions equals a rooted Euler-trail/arborescence count in a residual binary de Bruijn digraph. The rank-six directed matrix-tree formula is derived from a finite-horizon Markov Green kernel; the three-residue determinant identities are obtained by summing periodic overlap indicators. The small orders are handled separately. The independent whole-graph determinant checker reproduces the counts at orders four through six; the symbolic checker verifies the rational determinant identities.

## Originality

PASS, with historical-priority qualification. Chen, Kitaev, Mütze and Sun (arXiv:1601.06456, Sections 2–3) establish binary single-joker existence, nonexistence and a de Bruijn graph construction, but not all-order counts for a joker at position three. The closest identified exact enumerations concern joker positions one and two; third-position deletion paths have a distinct three-periodic prefix and determinant. Work on other alphabets, cyclic upwords and universal word-patterns has different objects. Available earlier linear/cyclic-word records were compared for implication, not just titles. A prior unindexed equivalent formula cannot be ruled out absolutely.

## Value

PASS. The new exact all-order count settles a natural fixed-location refinement of universal partial-word existence, identifies a nontrivial exceptional order five, and extracts a period-three finite-size effect with a precise limiting coefficient. The parameter is motivated by the known role of joker placement in universality and is neither an arbitrary finite slice nor merely a routine recomputation of a known table.

## Limitations

The result does not cover all joker locations, cyclic words, multiple wildcards or larger alphabets. Historical originality has not been independently validated. Reproducible exact arithmetic supplements but does not replace the mathematical proof for unbounded order.
