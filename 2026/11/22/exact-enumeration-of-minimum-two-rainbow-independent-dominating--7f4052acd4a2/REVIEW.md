# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof constructs exactly seven valid column words, 35 compatible column pairs, 70 locally legal transitions, and an integer potential certifying all transition costs at least one in closed walks. Optimal configurations correspond bijectively to cycles in the 46-transition tight matrix. The integer characteristic polynomial and two recurrent-block factors were recomputed exactly; taking power traces proves the formula for all cycle lengths. Direct C++ whole-graph enumeration at lengths three through seven and an independent Python column DP at lengths three through eleven agree with the computed coefficients. The small instances supplement, but do not replace, the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, provisional.** The 2017/2018 original invariant and the complete available 2023 article establish the model and existing minimum-weight law, including a transfer methodology, but not the optimum assignment multiplicities. In the 2023 article, Sections 2–4, especially Theorem 1.6, concern minimum weight, not counts of minimisers. Results on ordinary singleton rainbow domination, minimum two-rainbow independent labels of rook graphs, and three-colour functions of cyclic prisms have different hypotheses, so their conclusions neither state nor imply the current two-colour count. A related 2021 conference precursor is known bibliographically but was not available for full comparison; priority remains provisional, and unseen catalogues could contain relevant counts.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The previously known scalar minimum \(n\) does not determine even the order of magnitude of its multiplicity. The exact count exhibits leading growth \(2^n\), a non-obvious conjugate-eigenvalue correction of scale \(2^{n/2}\), and a sharp four-divisibility correction traced to an isolated periodic recurrent component. These quantify deployment diversity for a natural infinite graph family and expose structural information absent from a minimum-weight formula.

**Closest literature and limits.** Gabrovšek, Peperko and Žerovnik (2023), DOI:10.1007/s10100-023-00840-w, is the most directly relevant predecessor. Kraner Šumenjak, Rall and Tepeh (2017), arXiv:1709.08966, supplies the invariant. The 2021 conference precursor is acknowledged as an unresolved priority risk. The theorem is limited to two named colours and \(n\ge4\); it does not count every nonoptimal function.
