# Exact enumeration of shortest binary reverse-complement five-mer covers

## Finding

Write \(\rho(u)=\overline{u^{\mathrm{rev}}}\) for reverse complementation of a binary word, where complementation exchanges 0 and 1. A word \(w\) *covers the five-mer classes* if, for every \(u\in\{0,1\}^{5}\), either \(u\) or \(\rho(u)\) is a contiguous factor of \(w\). There are sixteen such classes.

**Theorem.** The minimum length of a covering linear word is twenty (the length bound and attainability are previously known). Among words of that minimum length there are **exactly 512 distinct linear words**. Each has the same four-symbol prefix and suffix. Identifying linearizations that arise by cutting the same cyclic sixteen-symbol word gives **exactly 32 circular words**, and these form **exactly eight classes** under bitwise complementation and reversal of the circle. Each of the eight classes contains four circular words, hence sixty-four linear words.

The eight lexicographically least circular representatives, written as sixteen literal bits with cyclic wraparound, are:

```
0000010001101011
0000010010100011
0000010011100101
0000010011101011
0000010100011011
0000010100100011
0000010100111011
0000011010111011
```

The new contribution is the complete optimizer count and classification, not the already known minimum covering length or mere existence of one covering word.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is exactly \(\{0,1\}\), with fixed-point-free bit complementation. Factors are **contiguous**, not scattered, and occurrences are oriented: a read is covered when one of its two reverse-complement orientations appears. Linear words are distinguished literally, without quotienting by their complement or reversal. A circular word is a binary period-sixteen string modulo rotation, with each five-symbol circular factor defined using wraparound. The symmetry group used after rotation is generated by bitwise complementation and word reversal. No alphabet-size, random-read, or longer-order capacity claim is made.

## Proof

**Minimum length and the covering criterion.** No binary five-symbol word is fixed by \(\rho\), since the middle bit would need to equal its complement. The thirty-two words therefore form sixteen paired classes. A length-\(m\) linear word has \(m-4\) indexed five-symbol windows, so a cover must have \(m\ge20\). The length-twenty word `00000100011010110000` covers all sixteen classes, as verified directly by the orbit rule below. Thus length twenty is optimal; this already follows from the odd-order construction of Orenstein and Shamir (2013). Moreover, every minimum word contains exactly one five-mer from each reverse-complement pair, with neither repeats nor duplicate orientations.

**Why every minimum word closes into a cycle.** This part has a useful general form: whenever \(k\ge3\) is odd, every binary length-\(2^{k-1}+k-1\) covering word has matching prefix and suffix of length \(k-1\). For the proof, use the directed binary de Bruijn graph whose vertices are the length-\((k-1)\) words and whose edges are the length-\(k\) words, with the usual prefix-to-suffix incidence. Let \(F\) be the edge trail of a shortest covering word, selecting exactly one edge from each reverse-complement pair, and let \(\rho(v)\) also denote reverse complementation of a vertex. Define \(d_F^+(v)\) and \(d_F^-(v)\) and their difference \(b(v)=d_F^+(v)-d_F^-(v)\). Reverse complementation interchanges the endpoints of an edge, and \(F\) together with its reverse complement uses each binary graph edge once. Because the full graph has in- and outdegree two at every vertex,

\[
d_F^+(v)+d_F^-(\rho(v))=2,\qquad
 d_F^-(v)+d_F^+(\rho(v))=2.
\]

Consequently \(b(v)=b(\rho(v))\). If \(v=\rho(v)\), the same equations give \(d_F^+(v)+d_F^-(v)=2\), hence \(b(v)\) is even. Were the trail open, there would be exactly one vertex with imbalance \(+1\) and exactly one with imbalance \(-1\). A vertex of odd imbalance cannot be fixed by \(\rho\), but its distinct reverse-complement partner has the same imbalance, a contradiction. Thus the trail is closed. For \(k=5\), the first and last four bits coincide.

**Exact finite Euler-tour count.** Label a five-mer by its standard integer value from zero to thirty-one, with its first bit most significant. The sixteen reverse-complement pairs, ordered by their smaller member, are

```
(0,31) (1,15) (2,23) (3,7) (4,27) (5,11) (6,19) (8,29)
(9,13) (10,21) (12,25) (14,17) (16,30) (18,22) (20,26) (24,28)
```

For a sixteen-bit mask \(a\), take from the \(i\)-th pair the smaller member if bit \(i\) of \(a\) is zero, or the larger member otherwise. Interpret each chosen five-mer \(e\) as the directed edge from \(\lfloor e/2\rfloor\) to \(e\bmod16\) among the sixteen length-four vertices. Testing the explicit indegree-equals-outdegree equations over the \(2^{16}\) masks gives exactly **52 balanced choices**; precisely **18** of these have a strongly connected non-isolated edge support. This enumeration is completely specified by the sixteen pairs and the incidence rule and can be repeated by the included C++ program.

For each surviving mask, distinguish the selected edge in the first pair \((0,31)\). Its tail and head are \(r\) and \(h\). The number of cycles containing the selected edges is the number of Euler tours with that uniquely distinguished edge first. Let \(E\) be the selected edge set and define the finite recurrence

\[
T(v,S)=\begin{cases}
\mathbf1_{\{v=r\}},&S=E,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{e\in E\setminus S\\\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}}T(\operatorname{head}(e),S\cup\{e\}),&S\ne E.
\end{cases}
\]

The required count for the mask is \(T(h,\{e_0\})\), where \(e_0\) is its distinguished edge. The full nonzero certificate is:

| Choice mask | Tours | Choice mask | Tours | Choice mask | Tours |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2048 | 4 | 8408 | 1 | 10512 | 2 |
| 14555 | 1 | 25320 | 1 | 25524 | 2 |
| 27424 | 2 | 31467 | 1 | 31671 | 2 |
| 33864 | 2 | 34068 | 1 | 38111 | 2 |
| 40011 | 2 | 40215 | 1 | 50980 | 1 |
| 55023 | 2 | 57127 | 1 | 63487 | 4 |

There are eight masks contributing one tour, eight contributing two, and two contributing four; hence the number of circular words is \(8+16+8=32\). Every possible optimal circular word determines exactly one of these directed tours and an orientation mask, and the first-pair edge occurs exactly once. The count therefore has neither omissions nor duplications.

Each of the sixteen cyclic five-mer windows belongs to a different reverse-complement class. The period of the circular word is therefore exactly sixteen (a smaller period would repeat a five-mer), so its sixteen cuts give sixteen distinct length-twenty linear words. Thus \(32\cdot16=512\).

Finally, complementing or reversing a cycle maps a valid cover to another. Direct application of these two involutions to the thirty-two cycles, followed by canonical lexicographic rotation, partitions them into the eight four-element orbits displayed in the Finding. The included second exhaustive method independently constructs all 512 literal length-twenty covers and checks both the closure condition and these symmetry orbits.

## Verification

Compile the included `verify.cpp` as `c++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify.cpp -o verify` and execute `./verify` in the same directory. The program runs two differently organized complete searches: all \(2^{20}=1{,}048{,}576\) linear binary words, and all \(2^{16}=65{,}536\) choices of one directed five-mer edge from each reverse-complement pair. The first recovers 512 covering words, zero open trails, thirty-two circular classes and eight complement/reversal classes; the second recovers 52 balanced edge selections, 18 connected selections and thirty-two canonical Euler tours. Both checks are exact integer computations; the recorded output is `verify_output.txt`. The infinite odd-order closure lemma is proved above and is not extrapolated from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Orenstein and Shamir (2013) proved that odd-order reverse-complement universal covering sequences attain the elementary orbit-count lower bound and explicitly posed counting the different optimal sequences as an open enumerative question. That paper therefore covers the length-twenty statement and the existence of optimal words, not the exact 512/32/eight-class census here. Chang and Wang (2024, arXiv:2408.01794) investigate full length-thirty-two complement-reverse-invariant de Bruijn sequences and exhibit a particular sixteen-symbol half-cycle, `0000010001101011`, which is the first representative listed above. Their constructions and divisibility statements concern those full de Bruijn sequences and do not enumerate all reverse-complement universal half-cycles or imply the claimed exact count. Yamano and Shibuya (2026, arXiv:2611.16573) investigate approximation algorithms for general shortest-common-superstring instances with reverse complements; their approximation guarantees are compatible with, but do not settle, this exact all-five-mer optimizer classification.

The reverse-complement equivalence relation must not be confused with ordinary reversal alone: results enumerating unoriented binary five-mer coverings are about a different family.

## Limitations

The optimizer census is proved only for the **binary, five-mer, literal contiguous-factor** model. The closure argument holds for binary odd orders, but no general optimizer-count formula is asserted. The known minimum covering length is not presented as novel. Exact exhaustive checking proves the stated finite census because the search spaces and arithmetic recurrence are exhaustive; it is not evidence for untested larger orders. A previously unindexed equivalent enumeration, or a reduction from more extensive results on complement-reverse de Bruijn sequences, remains a priority risk. Independent validation has not been performed.
