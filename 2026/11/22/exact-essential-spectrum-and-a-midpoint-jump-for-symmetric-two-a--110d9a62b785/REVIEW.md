# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The matrix is the sum of the weighted composition operator \(T_t\) and its Hilbert-space adjoint. An explicit Cayley--Paley--Wiener transform and logarithmic change of variables yield a two-sided weighted translation \(r(M_wU_L+U_L^*M_w)\), with \(w\to1\) at the negative end of the real line. Norm bounds enclose its spectrum. Weakly-null oscillatory packets supply singular Weyl sequences at every point of the entire signed interval when the log translation length is nonzero. At zero translation length the operator becomes multiplication by \(4e^{-x}\), with spectrum \([0,4]\). Strong continuity and the norm-discontinuity lower bound follow. The proof covers all \(0<t<1\); finite-section calculations are supplementary only. Check the exact Cayley and Laplace normalizations and whether the finite interval Weyl construction satisfies Fredholm singularity (it does for bounded self-adjoint operators).

## Originality: PASS, subject to historical-priority risk
Bellavita--Chalmoukis--Daskalogiannis--Stylogiannis (2023) already prove the defining adjoint relation, the general positive-measure norm formula, and noncompactness. The full signed spectrum is **not** deduced from their norm equality. The published midpoint-support obstruction already proves nonpositivity of off-midpoint symmetric atomic operators and does not classify their negative spectrum; its complete theorem, proof and limitations were inspected. The current conclusion determines all positive and negative essential spectral values and a strong-topology spectral discontinuity. Related two-atom Hausdorff spectra on BMOA/Bloch concern a distinct, generally nonselfadjoint, triangular matrix. A more general prior functional model might imply the result; independent literature audit is warranted.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The exact signed Fredholm spectrum distinguishes infinite negative spectral content from mere failure of positive definiteness, while the midpoint jump shows how strong operator convergence can hide an entire sharp negative spectral band. This is a natural stability and sign problem for a canonical two-atom member of a family already studied in Hardy-space operator theory. The result is a complete spectral classification in that natural model, not merely a finite negative minor or generic positivity counterexample.
