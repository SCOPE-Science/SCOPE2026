# Scientific review: cube–simplex Cayley facet geometry

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact inequality system follows from the positive-part decomposition of a cube-plus-simplex slice, and explicit dimensions certify that every stated supporting hyperplane is a facet. The section-volume polynomial and beta integral fix the volume normalization. A direct tangential-gradient/coarea computation yields each subset-facet Euclidean area with the \(\sqrt{k^2-k+1}\) normal factor; the remaining facets follow by coordinate restriction. Absolute convergence and factorial bounds establish the limiting law. The supplied independent convex-hull check recomputes the facet system and hull area for dimensions \(2\) through \(7\) (parameters \(1\le n\le6\)), with only finite diagnostic force.

Its statements and inspected sections do not supply the Euclidean area spectrum or the dimension-independent, factorially tight subset-rank boundary distribution. Semantic research-index searches returned neighboring Cayley and facet-area results for different objects, notably cube–crosspolytope Cayley bodies, Cayley sums of boxes and complete-bipartite graphical zonotopes; their claims do not imply the stated cube–simplex subset-facet areas. An exponentially large facet system has unexpectedly bounded total Euclidean boundary area; the exact full facet spectrum provides a natural geometric refinement of the already known combinatorial and volume invariants. The factorially tight area-weighted rank is sharply different from the binomial rank of an equally counted facet, with quantitative polynomial-complexity boundary approximations. This is a natural infinite-dimensional family result, not an arbitrary numeric slice or restatement of the known volume identity.
