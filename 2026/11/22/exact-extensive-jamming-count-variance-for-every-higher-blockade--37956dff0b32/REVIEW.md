# Scientific review of the higher-blockade ladder variance theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed coefficient is attached to the original dynamic priority process on the open square ladder, not an auxiliary boundary process. The first-split identity extends the published auxiliary **first-moment** recurrence to conditional variances, including all endpoint cases and singleton \(B_0\). Averaging the two geometries gives the displayed exact generating function. Entire-function coefficient extraction from the published mean generating function controls endpoint forcing, proving absolute convergence and linear asymptotic variance; a strictly positive root-choice variance at size \(b\) excludes a degenerate coefficient. A finite-range increasing-priority influence-path argument transfers the slope from the staggered auxiliary geometry to the ordinary open ladder. All quantities are finite for fixed integer \(b\ge2\).

Exact-rational independent greedy enumerations and first-split probability laws agreed for both auxiliary shapes at small sizes for \(b=1,2,3\). Independent high-precision moment recursion yielded \(\nu_2=0.0267945787085631864907625185535\ldots\). A second random-priority implementation on actual ordinary ladders produced consistent, non-proving finite-sample fluctuations. The checker and proof are included. Full infinite correctness has not been independently validated.

## Originality — PASS, with remaining priority risk

The closest original paper, Došlić–Puljiz–Šebek–Žubrinić (arXiv:2312.02747), establishes the same model, the same two auxiliary graph shapes and a **first-moment** density formula, alongside static configurational entropy. It does not give the dynamical jamming-count variance per rung. Chern–Hwang–Tsai (arXiv:1406.0614) establish a detailed distribution and local limit theorem for **only nearest-neighbor blockade \(b=1\)**, which is outside this theorem's new domain. General finite-range RSA fluctuation/CLT literature establishes central limit phenomena without this exact higher-blockade coefficient. Transfer-matrix counts of equally weighted jammed states treat the non-equivalent *static* probability distribution. Published-claim searches found an otherwise closely connected comparison of dynamic versus equilibrium **mean** densities, not the variance coefficient. Thus the asserted higher-blockade coefficient is not an immediate implication of inspected prior formulas. Broader or unindexed exact fluctuation results remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The previously available exact mean does not determine the count variance. The new exact positive coefficient measures sample-to-sample fluctuations of the final jammed population in the same two-row finite-range model, without substituting static combinatorial weighting for its true deposition law. The blockade-two result shows that the variance-to-mean ratio is only about \(0.0702\) in the large-ladder limit. A general convergent coefficient for all fixed \(b\ge2\) supplies a reproducible quantitative benchmark for strongly anticorrelated deposition and for later study of higher moments and fluctuations. The finding is an extensive natural observable rather than a decorative finite-size correction.

## Closest references and limits

- Došlić, Puljiz, Šebek and Žubrinić, 2023 first public preprint, arXiv:2312.02747: mean recursion, mean coefficient and static ensemble, but not dynamic higher-blockade variance.
- Chern, Hwang and Tsai, arXiv:1406.0614: full dynamic distribution and Gaussian fluctuations at blockade one, not blockade two or above.
- Coletti et al., *Fluctuations of the Occupation Density for a Parking Process* (2024): broader fluctuation existence and Gaussian context, not the coefficient for this distance-blockaded ladder.
- Spahn and Zeilberger, arXiv:2401.12430: static seating enumeration under social distancing, distinct sampling law.
