For two uniformly sampled points on a finite circular cylinder's sidewall, the intrinsic-to-ambient distance ratio has an exact one-quadrature distribution whose extreme tail is proportional to \(\varepsilon^{3/2}\). When the height shrinks like the radius times \(\sqrt\varepsilon\), this changes to an explicit \(\varepsilon\)-order crossover, with a sharp \(C^2\)-but-not-\(C^3\) transition; the same ratio law uniquely identifies the cylinder's aspect ratio.
