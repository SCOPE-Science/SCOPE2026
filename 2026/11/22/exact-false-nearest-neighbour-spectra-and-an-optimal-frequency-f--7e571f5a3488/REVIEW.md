# Scientific review

## Correctness
The smooth embedding is immediate from the first two independent sinusoidal measurements, and uniform density of an irrational circle orbit forces nearest-neighbour phase gaps to shrink uniformly. Exact cosine-chord cancellation reduces every finite difference ratio to a shifted tangent ratio. The empirical absolute-distance criterion vanishes uniformly. A rank-one generalized eigenvalue calculation then yields the exact finite-threshold angular law, with zero-measure boundary, and the parameter optimum follows from a one-variable derivative. The symbolic and six finite-orbit checks in `CHECK.py` passed; these experiments do not replace the infinite-time argument. The polynomial irreducibility argument ensures the optimal frequency lies in the irrational class.

## Originality
The nearest primary result, Śpiewak (arXiv:2611.18087v1), gives typical-observable, almost-everywhere asymptotic FNN results and a large-tolerance conclusion, but not the explicit phase-independent distribution for a fixed cosine measurement on irrational rotations or its exact global frequency optimum. Kennel–Brown–Abarbanel (1992) gives the FNN procedure, and secondary FNN analyses emphasize parameter sensitivity without these formulas. Published results about harmonic sampling or periodic phase aliasing address different quantities and do not logically imply the stated limit. An unlocated older calculation of the same sinusoidal test case remains a possible priority risk.

## Value
This complete parameter-dependent example shows rigorously that a smooth embedded attractor can retain a nonzero asymptotic false-neighbour fraction at small tolerance, and quantifies precisely where it disappears. The exact optimal sampling-angle condition and its irrational attainment offer a sharp, reproducible benchmark for selecting tolerance and cadence in one of the simplest nonmixing systems. This is a model-specific classification, not a universal algorithmic bound.

## Scope and limits
The mathematical result is restricted to a single noiseless cosine observable, three adjacent delays, Euclidean one-nearest-neighbour selection, and a fixed relative and absolute cutoff. No convergence rate for general irrational rotation numbers, noisy observations, or different observables is claimed. General priority requires independent literature review.
