# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim specifies independent uniform hub–leaf interactions, mean-zero real disagreement, the squared Euclidean error, and the full integer horizon. An elementary leaf-permutation symmetry splits the centered space into one hub–leaf-mean contrast and an \((m-1)\)-dimensional leaf-contrast space. Direct projection calculations give the stated two-by-two covariance operator. Its discriminant and the positive gap recurrence prove the exact finite-horizon optimizer, while its characteristic polynomial proves the closed eigenvalues and strict log-concavity. Expansion of the radical yields the logarithmic \(4/3\) asymptotic comparison. The accompanying checker separately reconstructs the full rational covariance recursion on ten star sizes. No infinite assertion is based solely on finite tests.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual risk

Boyd, Ghosh, Prabhakar and Shah (CDC 2004; IEEE TIT 2006) already supply the gossip model and the standard one-step spectral contraction bound, and the 2006 paper supplies a general lifted second-moment criterion. Mo (2014) computes the exact \(5/8\) factor for the three-vertex path, which is explicitly treated as a previously known boundary case. The available passages do not give the explicit general-star two-mode formula, exact worst-case initial subspace for all finite horizons, strict log-concavity, or sharp \(4/3\) limit. Sirocchi–Bogliolo (2022) instead study topology-based predictors and simulations. Public-record and literature searches did not identify a dominating all-star calculation, but an unindexed equivalent may exist, and a fully exhaustive bibliography is not claimed.

## Value — PASS

General spectral bounds for asynchronous averaging can be conservative in the precise squared-error observable that they bound. On the natural hub-and-leaves stress-test network, the result gives both an exact expression at every finite horizon and a sharp asymptotic \(4/3\) improvement in logarithmic contraction over using the standard per-interaction spectral rate as an equality. The complete extremal subspace and strict decrease of per-horizon contraction ratios clarify why the one-step factor does not persist. This is a quantitative structural benchmark for gossip convergence, not a cosmetic restatement of a textbook spectral estimate.
