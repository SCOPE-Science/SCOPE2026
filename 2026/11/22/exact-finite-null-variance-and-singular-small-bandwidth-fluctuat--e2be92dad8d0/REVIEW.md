# Scientific review — exact Cauchy Fourier–Stein variance

## Correctness (C): PASS
The theorem specifies the known-null Cauchy law, all integers \(n\ge2\), all positive \(a\), and the statistic's weight and normalization. The real-score representation is algebraically equivalent to the integrated complex modulus. Direct Cauchy Fourier transforms give the centered covariance kernel \(K(s,t)\); the off-diagonal kernel is degenerate, so distinct unordered-pair terms and diagonal terms have vanishing cross-covariance. Exact Laplace integrations on all four frequency quadrants yield the stated rational \(J(a)\) and complete variance. As \(a\downarrow0\), the off-diagonal terms vanish for almost every fixed sample with distinct observations, while the diagonal gives the bounded endpoint. The exact variance diverges as \(a^{-3}\) after centering and scaling; therefore the a.s. limit cannot be an \(L^2\) limit. Reproducible `checker.py` passed 402 exact rational assertions. The finite checker alone is not the infinite proof. Risk: the statistic's conclusions would not carry automatically to estimated parameters.

## Originality (O): PASS, qualified
The main original target is the explicit all-\(n\), all-\(a\) rational variance and the sharp non-\(L^2\) small-bandwidth boundary for the known-parameter null. Ebner–Eid–Klar (2021 preprint, published online 2022) introduce the statistic, derive its integration-free form and the fixed-sample almost-sure endpoint, and study estimated-parameter Hilbert-space asymptotics; the pointwise endpoint is **not** new. The exact finite-sample rational formula is not stated in the primary material inspected. The standard ECF Cauchy tests of Gürtler–Henze (2000) and Matsui–Takemura (2005) concern other statistics and parameter estimation, rather than this Fourier–Stein null variance. Searches for an equivalent variance, Laplace spectral-kernel integral and nonuniform integrability returned no dominating theorem in the examined records. Risk remains that a general known-parameter Gaussian-kernel variance theorem or separate Cauchy Stein calculation implies the formula without the same wording; absence from a database is not a uniqueness certificate. Matsui–Takemura's first page and Gürtler–Henze's abstract were read, not their complete proofs.

## Value (V): PASS
The full bandwidth-dependent finite-null variance is a useful quantitative calibration object for a named Fourier–Stein test. The contrast between a bounded almost-sure endpoint and variance blowing up as a precise inverse cube reveals severe nonuniform integrability missed by pointwise convergence and flags a nontrivial order-of-limits issue. This is not simply recalculating a known moment of a classical distribution. It does not purport to determine power, optimal bandwidth, or estimated-parameter calibration. The isolated endpoint limit itself was previously known and does not constitute the new claim.
