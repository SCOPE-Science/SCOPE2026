# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The new claim has a fully specified real finite-path domain, normalized counting \(\ell^1\) and adjacent variation, a self-contained proof of the exact spectral norm and all equality cases, and a dominated-convergence proof of its continuum limit. The nonstandard central-localization lemma is proved by writing the Dirichlet diagonal as a periodic kernel difference and showing strict cycle radial monotonicity through an irreducible positive-system evolution. Matrix and exact-integer tests independently check multiple intermediate identities. Risk: finite tests alone would not prove the theorem; the analytical argument supplies all-size quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Published comparisons include the full-text sections of Dowker's discrete heat kernels, an exact stationary-barycenter envelope for a different fixed-time functional, and a later BV heat-semigroup characterization of a different limit. The time-resolved operator error norm, central-cut rigidity and complete equality classification are not direct consequences of a quoted theorem in those inspections; they require a new joint optimization and proof of strict edge localization. The uniform-path equilibrium coefficient and the reflecting-interval continuum limit are recognized as related known or previously treated facts, not independent originality claims. Residual risks include older unindexed discrete heat-content identities and incomplete full-document review of broader BV literature.

## Value — PASS

The result is an exact all-size/all-time stability calibration for finite-difference reflecting diffusion applied to jump data. It identifies the uniquely worst locations, distinguishes the fixed-time sharp error from transport/gradient semigroup norms, and supplies a rigorous discrete-to-continuum bridge. Those facts are useful when diffusion is discretized on short or long paths: a generic convergence-rate bound does not identify exact finite coefficients or their extremizers. The main contribution is this structural combination, not the routine spectral diagonalization alone.

## Literature and scope

Dowker, arXiv:1207.2096 (2012-07-09), contains the relevant discrete Neumann, Dirichlet and cyclic heat formulas. Alonso Ruiz–Baudoin, arXiv:2010.12131 (2020), concerns short-time heat oscillation characterization of BV on smooth manifolds. The published stationary-barycenter result (2026-10-27) concerns Lipschitz norms optimized over variable conductances, not the fixed-conductance approximation error. The result is restricted to unweighted finite unit paths and real-valued signals, with no quantitative uniform scaling error rate.
