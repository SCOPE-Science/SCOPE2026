# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The object and normalizations are explicit: a length-\(2m\) core cycle with \(2m\) leaves, unit core dimer weights, alternating positive pendant activities, and the standard matching-polynomial map to the physical fugacity. The cycle-Sachs permutation expansion gives the magnetic determinant, the leaf Schur complement gives a two-step determinant-one transfer, and the Chebyshev trace yields all \(4m\) roots. The root quadratics have strictly positive, unequal solutions for every \(m\ge2\), \(a,b>0\). The smallest Chebyshev-trace parameter determines the exact inner finite-size gap. An independently written deletion/contraction enumerator was compared with the formula in 12 rational parameter instances and two exact magnetic determinants. All analytic statements are proved for arbitrary allowed parameters, not inferred from those computations.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence; residual priority risk

The general Heilmann–Lieb zero-line theorem and Wagner's weighted framework imply negative physical-activity zeros, but do not specify the sunlet's two-branch finite gap. The quarter-flux matching-polynomial representation is explicitly prior art under the complex-gain theorem of Samanta and Kannan (2023) and is not counted as new. Existing pendant-graph molecular spectral work is close and may contain an equivalent evaluated formula: Mandal's 2008 paper was accessible only as an abstract because the full text requires access. Alternating-cycle characteristic-polynomial formulas are related but concern different spectral objects or boundary phases; a hidden explicit specialization cannot be excluded. Published-record comparisons of sunlet packing, unweighted quantum mixing, matching-root leaf corona domination and alternating-rate kinetic dimers did not identify the present finite-size matching-zero gap. Absence from search is not asserted as proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The precise gap is a natural spectral observable: it measures the separation between the two ordered groups of monomer–dimer matching roots in a periodic system with two inequivalent pendant activities. The exact sum-of-squares law distinguishes a robust macroscopic dimerization gap from an \(m^{-1}\) quantization gap, determines the sharp crossover as the activities merge, and is supported by a complete all-size zero classification. It is not presented as a general phase-transition theorem, nor as a novelty claim for routine spectral real-rootedness.

The general gain determinant equivalence is fully acknowledged as established by Samanta and Kannan (2023), and the weighted matching framework by Wagner (2008).
