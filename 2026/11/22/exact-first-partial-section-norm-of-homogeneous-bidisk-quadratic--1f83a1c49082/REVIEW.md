# Scientific review: exact quadratic monomial initial section

## Correctness — PASS

For every nonzero complex Banach target, homogeneous degree-two polynomials on the closed bidisk reduce on its distinguished boundary to \(P(\zeta)=A+B\zeta+C\zeta^2\). The two nodes \(\xi_\pm=e^{\pm i\pi/3}\) and weights \(\lambda_\pm=1/2\mp i/(2\sqrt3)\) satisfy the exact three-moment system \(\sum\lambda=1\), \(\sum\lambda\xi=1\), and \(\sum\lambda\xi^2=0\). The total absolute weight is \(2/\sqrt3\), yielding the claimed universal upper bound from the Banach triangle inequality without using convexity or smoothness of the target. For the scalar polynomial \(P_*(\zeta)=1/2+\zeta-\zeta^2/4\), the exact boundary profile is \(27/16-(\cos t-1/2)^2/2\), so the full and projected suprema are \(3\sqrt3/4\) and \(3/2\), respectively. Hence equality holds. The scalar equality classification is derived from sharpness in both node inequalities and the stationary condition, giving \(b=2a\), \(c=-a/2\) after rotations. Boundary cases with a vanishing retained coefficient cannot attain the norm. The standalone exact-identity checker and 384 finite tests supplement, but do not replace, this proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The 2026 Grando manuscript, arXiv:2608.14926v1, motivates monomial coordinate sections and announces a universal degree-dependent bound \(c_{K,n}\le n+1\). That bound alone is not an equality theorem for this three-term quadratic block and does not imply the exact \(2/\sqrt3\) constant or its equality cases. Grando's full manuscript was inaccessible, however, so possible implicit coverage by its uninspected sections remains an explicit risk. Geddes–Mason (1975) studies Taylor sections of the full disk algebra and asymptotic projection growth, not an identified sharp restriction to inputs of degree two; only its abstract could be read. The fully inspected published-finding corpus records about quadratic Hardy-to-Bergman diagonal restriction, Euclidean-ball quadratic coefficient norms, and sublinear-Hardy Taylor-section evaluation use different operators and normed spaces and do not imply this claim. Actual synonym, numeric-constant, parameter, primary-source, and targeted literature comparisons found no statement dominating it; lack of indexing is not a uniqueness proof. Residual risk: classical approximation literature might already contain the restricted degree-two constant under another term.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is the smallest genuinely noncontractive monomial initial-section operator in a natural degree-graded holomorphic basis problem. It exactly sharpens the general degree-dependent constant at a canonical test case and proves that this sharp constant is **uniform across all complex Banach-valued coefficients**. The two-atom interpolation construction is reusable as a dual certificate for other coordinate cutoff problems, while the scalar equality classification identifies the unique extremal geometry rather than merely a numerical norm. The result is a motivated exact finite block, not a random numeric specialization; it does not claim an unproved higher-degree consequence.
