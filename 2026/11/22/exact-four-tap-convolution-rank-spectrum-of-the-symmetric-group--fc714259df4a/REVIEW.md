# Scientific review: four-tap convolution on \(S_4\)

## Correctness — PASS
The claim quantifies over all 10,626 unordered supports of cardinality exactly four with unit coefficients. The finite exhaustive C11 proof enumerates them by strictly increasing lexicographic permutation indices. For each, it computes the rank of a 24-by-24 integral left-regular convolution matrix over the prime field with modulus 1,000,000,007. The Hadamard estimate \(|\det M|\le2^{24}\) for **every** square minor makes equality of modular and complex rank rigorous. Generated subgroup orders are computed from difference generators by finite closure, not guessed from cycle types. The exact rational SymPy program partitions every support under two-sided translation/inversion into 34 disjoint orbits and recovers all rank totals from a separately implemented algorithm, while a third program recovers the full Fourier-block rank profiles from explicit permutation representations. Both verification paths were executed on the packaged source and agreed. The seven nonsingular-or-singular alternatives and all exclusions are exhaustively decided; this is not statistical sampling.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded historical-priority risk
The nearest general theorem is Alagic–Russell's 2006 rank-weighted uncertainty principle, which yields only the lower bound six and covers subgroup equality cases. Pinner's 2018 comprehensive \(S_4\) group-determinant paper supplies block determinant factors and attainable integer determinant values, but no all-support four-indicator rank or nullity distribution; the determinant is zero for all seven singular ranks, so those ranks cannot be read from the determinant value alone. Siemons–Zalesski (2018) restrict the Cayley singularity analysis to conjugacy-invariant inverse-closed sets and do not enumerate unrestricted directed supports. Targeted research-record comparisons found three-tap group spectra, dihedral four-tap spectra, and symmetric-group standard-block sampling, but no statement implying the exact \(S_4\) four-tap census. The possibility of an unindexed equivalent computation or a previously tabulated Cayley-group-algebra rank spectrum remains.

## Value — PASS
The question has a canonical object (the first symmetric group with irreducible representation dimensions \(1,1,2,3,3\)), a canonical coefficient restriction (four equal taps), and a natural complete domain (every four-subset). The exact eight-rank spectrum, 15 nonzero subgroup/order combinations, 34 two-sided/inverse symmetry orbits and 18 Fourier-block rank profiles show how irreducible matrix-block rank defects combine, a distinction absent from the general uncertainty inequality and a determinant-zero test. The exceptional rank-20 stratum and the exact invertible count are useful small-group benchmarks for convolutional inverse problems and Cayley-operator algebra. There is no claim of a universal larger-group theorem or arbitrary-coefficient classification.
