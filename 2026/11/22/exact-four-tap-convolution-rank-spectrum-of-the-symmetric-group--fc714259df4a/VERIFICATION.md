# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The regular convolution matrix has four distinct ones in each row. Every k-minor has magnitude at most 2^k, so the large prime in the frozen C certificate makes its modular decision exact for complex rank. I independently enumerated all 10,626 supports via the three small permutation-action sums (on the three pairings, on the letters, and on the letters with sign twist), not by running the frozen regular-matrix or orbit-rank programs. Removing the trivial or sign summands gives the 2-, 3-, and 3-dimensional irreducibles; the regular multiplicity of each is its dimension. For each of the small full-action matrices the row 1-norm is at most four, and every minor has magnitude at most 4^4<1009; reduction modulo prime 1009 is exact. Closing the normalized support differences computes H(S); changing base element gives the same subgroup because pair differences are products of normalized generators and inverses. All eight ranks, all 15 nonzero joint cells and all 18 block profiles were reproduced. Burnside on the 1,152 left-right/inversion actions gave a fixed-four-subset sum of 39,168, hence 34 orbits, also verified by independent construction of the representative table. The rank-six row has |H|=4, hence S is a whole coset. The seven subgroups are three cyclic groups from pairs of inverse 4-cycles, one double-transposition Klein group, and three Klein groups generated by disjoint transpositions. On each of the six right coset fibers the normalized full-subgroup average has rank one. All counts refer to distinct unweighted taps over C, not numerical rank or variable coefficients.

## originality

PASS

Known representation structure, a general sharp rank floor, a full group determinant, and narrower Cayley singularity theorems do not already give this fixed-coefficient four-support rank and difference-subgroup census. Distinct nearby Resultary records vary coefficients or treat different groups or support sizes; the exact indexed match is the frozen record itself. Credit the known methods and lower bound, not the full census, to the antecedents.

## value

PASS

This is a complete uniform-support census on a canonical small nonabelian group with irreducible blocks of sizes 2 and 3. The equal coefficients make the support itself the variable, so weighting the full orbit census answers a natural finite inverse question: which supports produce which rank defects, including invertibility. The joint table separates actual rank behavior from the intuitive expectation that generation predicts rank: rank 20 requires full generation, but both full and proper difference subgroups occur among invertibles. This information is absent even from a complete determinant-value classification and provides exact test cases for small-group convolution.
