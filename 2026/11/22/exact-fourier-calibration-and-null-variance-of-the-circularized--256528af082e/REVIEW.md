# Scientific review: circularized Anderson–Darling spectral calibration

## Correctness — PASS

For the full nonlinear average of Liu's modified logarithmic score, the proof begins with the exact Dirichlet(1, …, 1) spacing model, not a Gaussian surrogate. A normalized-exponential functional central limit theorem produces a periodic Brownian bridge. An interior Taylor expansion is justified uniformly. The otherwise delicate endpoint ranks are removed using exact beta-logarithmic expectations and the uniform bound \(\mathbb E h\leq4/m\). The resulting nonnegative Brownian quadratic functional has a complete Fourier decomposition into independent \(\chi_2^2\) modes with explicitly integrated weights. Parseval and differentiated beta integrals give the exact variance. The third cumulant is strictly positive. The tail estimates follow from the cosine-integral expansion and integral comparison. These steps verify all limits under the specified null, rather than extrapolating a finite experiment. The auxiliary script passed 39 assertions; those checks corroborate computations but do not substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with identified residual risks

Liu, arXiv:2209.08050 (16 September 2022), equation (4.8), defines the statistic, while Theorems 6–7 and equation (6.11) give circulant Gaussian approximations. Section 6.1 explicitly describes rigorous weak convergence of circularized statistics as requiring additional study; the available main text and appendix do not supply a proved weak limit for the original nonlinear logarithmic statistic with boundary-rank control, the explicit special-function coefficients, the elementary exact variance, or the logarithmic cutoff law. The separate arXiv:2209.08150 abstract announces a generic chi-square series but its full body was inaccessible, so stronger overlapping coverage cannot be excluded categorically. The 2023 journal version's accessible abstract similarly does not resolve that risk. The local prior result on exact finite-sample expectation of the same circularized statistic is distinct: it says nothing about the weak distribution, explicit Fourier mode weights, or exact variance. Research-record searches by source/title/weighted chi-square/Fourier aliases and nearby published covariance results identified no directly covering theorem. The general Fourier-and-beta techniques are classical and are not claimed as new. An independent historical-priority review remains needed.

## Value — PASS

A large-sample *distribution* is necessary for calibrating critical values of this proposed circularized test. Replacing a heuristic Gaussian kernel and numerical matrix diagonalization by a rigorous limit with explicit, positive, strictly ordered Fourier weights gives a directly reproducible null law. The closed variance \((42-4\pi^2)/9\) quantifies the reduction in null dispersion under circular pooling relative to classical unpooled Anderson–Darling, and sharp error tails permit controlled finite spectral evaluation. These are substantively motivated facts about an existing testing method rather than an arbitrary additional statistic or a mere title change. No claim about empirical power improvement or fitted-parameter validity follows.

## Main source inspections and limitations

Full mathematical main text and appendix were inspected at https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08050, especially sections 2, 4, 6.1 and 6.3, and the discussion of Theorems 6–8. The abstract of the parallel same-author arXiv:2209.08150 was accessible but not its complete manuscript, and the article at https://doi.org/10.1080/10485252.2023.2213782 returned an access restriction beyond its public bibliographic description. The latter sources are specific residual historical-priority risks. The proof covers only independent observations and a fully specified continuous null, the particular modified score, and average pooling. It does not prove a finite-sample null distribution or calibrated testing under estimated parameters.
