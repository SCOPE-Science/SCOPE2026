# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The normalization relations for the three generators give an exact rectangular Apéry set, whose residue thresholds have only two upward jumps per interior block. Each jump of height \(n\) gives exactly \(n\) distinct starts; descending thresholds and residue boundaries add none. Summation over the Apéry set yields the genus and Frobenius number. Membership-transition coefficients prove polynomial sparsity. Independent full-interval dynamic programming for every \(2\le n\le80\) agreed with the predicted starts, gaps, Frobenius number and coefficient signs. The finite checks are corroborative; the proof covers all integers \(n\ge2\).

## Originality: PASS on available source comparisons
Shor and Sim (2025) treat the same full geometric-sequence family and explicitly quote the known rectangular Apéry set and genus, but determine modular equidistribution of gaps rather than maximal gap-block starts or coefficient locations. The 2008 geometric-sequence work treats Frobenius numbers rather than gap-run start progressions. The full 2023 Apéry paper studies different numerical-semigroup families. Two highly relevant published exact gap classifications have different generator families (binary semigroups and consecutive triangular numbers) and do not imply the claimed three-generator progression-support formula. The 2017 Pascadi source may contain a general computation from which a special case could be extracted, but only an abstract could be inspected; historical priority has not been independently established.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The exact locations of all maximal missing-integer intervals and the sparse coefficient support of their semigroup polynomial sharpen the usual Frobenius/genus invariants for a simple, natural infinite three-generator family. They can be used to locate each missing-number run directly without computing a semigroup table. The claim is a complete parametric classification, not a small sample or a routine restatement of a known Frobenius number.

## Limits
The result does not classify arbitrary three-generated semigroups or give a separate closed frequency spectrum of gap-block lengths. Full bibliographic coverage remains incomplete, especially for general Hilbert-series formulas. Independent mathematical and historical validation remain outstanding.
