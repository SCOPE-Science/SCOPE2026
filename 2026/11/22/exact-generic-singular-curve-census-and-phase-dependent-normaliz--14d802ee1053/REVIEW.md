# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The morphism from the smooth torus coset is finite by the monic quadratic equation for each Laurent coordinate. Complete classification of all nontrivial coordinate-inversion subsets produces the displayed curves. Pairwise coprimality makes each fixed-coordinate torus slice irreducible. The rank-drop locus of the differential is contained in the specified curves, and the generic two branches have independent normal covectors. At symmetric phase the finite quotient by global inversion is normal and has exactly four isolated \(A_1\) fixed-point quotients. Exact symbolic elimination for weights \((1,1,2)\) supplies an additional finite sanity check, with proof independent of this experiment.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

The 2025 *Lissajous Varieties* paper establishes the finite Laurent-parametric construction and its degree; the 2026 *Intersections, Equations, and Singularities of Lissajous Varieties* paper supplies a general sandwich description and characterizes hypersurface singular loci directly in ambient dimension at least four. Its three-variable example treats the special phase of one weighted circuit, not the uniform generic-phase curve count. Exact claim/alias searches identified no general weighted three-variable census. Previously studied equal weights cover only a strict subfamily. An equivalent unindexed classification remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The theorem exactly classifies the codimension-one nonnormal locus of every generic-phase three-variable Lissajous hypersurface with pairwise coprime circuit weights, and determines its transverse singularity type. The count grows with the weights, distinguishing two different geometric origins of its curves. It also identifies a sharp normalization transition at symmetric phase. These are structural invariants directly motivated by the originating paper's incomplete three-variable singularity classification, not arbitrary numerical examples.
