# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The sole reviewed claim is the sharp **greedy two-step** inertia reduction and the exact optimizing second axes for an equilateral triangle. The all-directions first-step argument uses the exact triangular projection-width/chord-moment law; its equality occurs only along three side-parallel axes. It produces the explicitly stated nonsquare rhombus. The second-step proof integrates exact edge-defined chord lengths to obtain both complete rational angular profiles. A strictly increasing quintic has one interior critical root, and a 13-coefficient positive Bernstein certificate proves the competing branch is globally inferior. The resulting angle, uniqueness, multiplicity and contraction follow algebraically. The two standalone scripts passed exact symbolic/rational identities and independent numerical polygon-section comparisons. Finite numerical comparisons are checks, not substitutes for the analytic all-real-angles proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
Basit–Lángi (arXiv:2206.04323v1) uses Steiner symmetrization for polygonal extremal geometry but does not optimize the second polar-inertia step for an equilateral starting triangle. The published triangular \(\lambda\)-Steiner angular-response result gives a full one-step triangle fiber identity and an angular expectation, thereby accounting for much of the first step here; its claims do not cover the optimal second axis of the resulting unequal-diagonal rhombus. The existing exact rectangle angular law (including its square optimum) does not imply the rhombus profile: the body is a nonsquare rhombus, and Euclidean inertia and ordinary Steiner symmetrization are not invariant under anisotropic linear maps. Published convergence/rate results do not provide this exact conditional two-step algebraic classification. The novelty claimed is strictly the second-stage optimized two-branch comparison, algebraic critical angle and sharp conditional contraction, not the classical Steiner operation, elementary chord identity, or triangle-to-rhombus geometry. An unindexed equivalent specialized angle calculation remains possible; this review does not establish independent historical priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact optimizing direction for the second step of the most symmetric polygonal test case provides a nontrivial, reproducible greedy-symmetrization benchmark. It captures how a first symmetry-inducing step changes the optimal later direction and proves a global strict comparison between two entire angular regimes, rather than merely finding a numerical local optimum. The natural extremal invariant is sharp and its optimizing axes are characterized uniquely by a quintic. No unjustified extrapolation to optimal simultaneous two-step choices or eventual convergence rates is made.

## Scope and limitations
This is a deterministic two-step conditional greedy theorem for ordinary centroid-preserving Steiner symmetrization of a planar equilateral triangle. It neither handles arbitrary starting triangles nor determines non-greedy jointly optimized pairs. General qualitative symmetrization results and the complete one-step triangular chord identity are acknowledged as existing inputs; only the remaining specialized exact optimization is offered as new. Some older mechanical-inertia and symmetrization literature may be incompletely indexed. Independent external validation has not been performed.
