# Scientific review: the finite-slope drift dimension spectrum

**Correctness — PASS.** The normalized finite-approximant slopes are sums of half-circle signs. Their exact child multiplicities are independently proved using the elementary interval tree; the weighted two-state multigraph has Perron root \(a\cosh t+\sqrt{a^2\sinh^2t+c^2}\). A direct exponential covering argument yields the upper Hausdorff bound; a tilted stationary edge-Markov measure with entropy \(P_b(t)-tu\) gives the matching lower bound for interior drift; all-same-sign Cantor branches and a sparse-switch argument resolve both endpoints. The checker verified 756 exact small-cylinder counts and 30 Perron computations, but finite tests are not proof of the limiting dimension.

**Originality — PASS within checked literature, not an independence guarantee.** Allaart (2014, Section 3) already establishes exactly the required sign Markov law. Nakano and Takei (2025) already establish correlated-walk asymptotic results; neither finite-slope Markov behavior nor its Gaussian limit is claimed as novel. The October 2026 paper by Jiang, Ying and Zhang defines the same slope counts but addresses local level sets. The proposed contribution is the evaluated *full Euclidean Hausdorff spectrum for prescribed normalized slope drift*, including boundary dimensions and the odd/even pressure distinction. Finite-state multifractal formalism provides a general route, so the contribution is a concrete exact specialization, not a new general method. Searches for equivalent slope-frequency, derivative-drift, and correlated-walk dimension formulations found no exact competing evaluation. Remaining priority risk: an earlier unindexed or differently named multifractal calculation, including unpublished results.

**Value — PASS.** The object is a natural multiscale roughness diagnostic for a classical nowhere-differentiable family that was used explicitly in recent local-level-set work. The complete invariant separates the prevalent zero-drift set (full dimension) from extreme slope-drift sets whose dimension can remain positive, and it exhibits exact arithmetic dependence on base parity. This is a motivated all-parameter classification rather than an arbitrary finite table. It does not resolve differentiability or height-level-set dimensions.
