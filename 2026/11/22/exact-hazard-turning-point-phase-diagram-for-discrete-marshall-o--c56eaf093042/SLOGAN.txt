The discrete Marshall–Olkin length-biased exponential law has a sharp, two-candidate cubic threshold for globally nondecreasing hazard, and no parameter choice permits more than two hazard-direction reversals. For \(\beta=2\) and \(\gamma=1/2\), its hazard rises, falls twice, and rises forever; as \(\beta\to\infty\), the critical tilt converges to \(0.5204120459\ldots\) and the two turning points separate on the scale \(\beta\).
