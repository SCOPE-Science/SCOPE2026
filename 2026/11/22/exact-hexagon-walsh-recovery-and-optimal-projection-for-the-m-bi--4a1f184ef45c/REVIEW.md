# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is the binary edge cube of the connected \(24\)-edge Möbius–Kantor graph \(\operatorname{GP}(8,3)\). The proof defines each simple six-cycle exactly once and establishes the cut-space quotient of dimension nine. An exhaustive degree-characterized enumeration of all \(\binom{24}{6}=134{,}596\) six-edge subsets agrees with independent DFS cycle enumeration. An explicit isomorphism to the eight-point affine configuration checks the underlying graph in a second model. Independent direct and fast-Walsh integer methods agree on every one of the \(512\) quotient multipliers; inverse transform computes the projection numerator distribution, proving the claimed exact rank and \(123/16\) norm. The translation-averaging argument proves global optimality among arbitrary linear projections. These are finite exhaustive assertions; no incomplete numerical sampling is treated as proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded literature comparison

The same graph, its embeddings, its automorphisms and some classes of consistent cycles are extensively studied; none of the fully inspected primary works states the signed hexagon Walsh histogram or sharp convolution-range projection norm. The prior published analytic results for other codebook supports do not imply the numerical constants for this support without an additional complete enumeration. Related indexed results on the Möbius–Kantor independence complex concern a distinct vertex-subset topology rather than an edge-hexagon counting kernel. The 2004 embeddings paper was available only as an abstract, and hidden equivalent calculations in it or unindexed sources are a residual priority risk. The scientific claim of originality is bounded by that access limitation; a search miss is not taken as a uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS

The shortest cycles are a canonical, symmetry-invariant source of binary graph-code measurements. Character cancellation is exactly the obstruction to recovering signals from their convolution measurements. The seven-level signed spectrum gives the complete recoverable range and a sharp Hilbert inverse bound; the explicit \(123/16\) norm gives the least endpoint amplification even among nonsymmetric recovery projections. The quotient to the nine-dimensional graph cycle space makes this finite calculation portable and independently reproducible. These invariants are not consequences of just the graph's adjacency spectrum or the number of hexagons. The scope remains this one exceptional graph, not a claimed graph-family theorem.
