# Scientific assessment

## Correctness (C) — PASS
The result is a finite-geometry conditional law for any fixed site set in general position with iid atomless heights. Rank invariance reduces to independent uniforms; overlapping, disjoint and endpoint-obstructing blocker sets are exhaustively partitioned into four cases. The no-incidence integral is evaluated by explicit order regions, the one-way cases by a nested uniform integral, and mutual endpoint blockage is impossible. The degree variance follows by an exact indicator expansion. Exact-rational enumeration on all admissible abstract blocker pairs through seven labels and integer-coordinate geometrical sign examples corroborates the argument, without replacing its proof.

## Originality (O) — PASS, subject to historical priority risk
The closest paper is Muñoz–Nuño (arXiv:2611.16336v1, 2026-11-12), particularly its Proposition 5: it proves the marginal probability of a single edge and the conditional **mean** degree. It does not state the incident-edge joint probability or conditional variance, and single-edge marginals cannot algebraically determine pair dependence. Luque et al. (2009), inspected in its original article, establish time-series horizontal visibility degree laws; Lacasa–Iacovacci (2017), inspected in its arXiv full text, work with fixed directions on regular grids. Neither provides the arbitrary crossed Voronoi cell incidence formula. A semantic mathematical-record search over exact variance, shared blockers and rank-equivalence aliases found no dominating record; this cannot establish absolute priority. The remaining risk is an unindexed or differently named formula for overlapping common-endpoint visibility events.

## Mathematical value (V) — PASS
The motivating study proposes geometry-conditional mean degrees as randomness statistics for irregular sites but notes large degree fluctuations. The new exact conditional variance is needed to standardize a single-site degree against an iid-height null at actual irregular sampling positions, while the opposite-sign covariance examples show why replacing the variance by a sum of independent-edge Bernoulli variances is unreliable. The finite all-site theorem avoids arbitrary slice restrictions and supplies a distribution-free second-order observable without pretending to solve Poisson-site variance or dependent-height problems.

## Scope and limits
No absolute novelty or independent scientific validation is certified. Source comparison is bounded by inspected accessible primary literature and searched records; a historical equivalent may exist. All formulas are conditional on fixed sites and iid continuous heights, for horizontal rather than natural visibility. The proof is analytic; finite checks are supplemental.
