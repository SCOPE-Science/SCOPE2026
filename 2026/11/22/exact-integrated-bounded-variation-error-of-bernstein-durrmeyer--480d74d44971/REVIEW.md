# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The proof derives a symmetric normalized Markov kernel, an exact crossing-mass formula for a unit jump, and its equality with a binomial mean absolute difference. The derivative with respect to the paired-Bernoulli disagreement probability is strictly positive, uniquely locating the maximizing jump at the midpoint. An exact central-binomial telescoping identity evaluates the maximum; the signed-derivative decomposition proves the global BV norm and equality rigidity. The checker corroborates low-degree formulas and signed finite-jump inequalities. Risk: the strict monotonicity and BV measure argument are analytic; numerical checks alone would not suffice.

## Originality — PASS, with historical risk
Wang and Zhou (2009), extending Zeng and Chen (2000), prove pointwise BV estimates for Durrmeyer–Bézier kernels, not the exact integrated scalar operator norm and equality classification. Öksüzer–Karsli–Taşdelen Yeşildal (2016) study image variation and convergence in variation, not \(\|M_nf-f\|_1\). Berdysheva–Dyn–Farkhi–Mokhov (2022), section 6.2, provide nonsharp integrated bounds for generalized integral operators; Liu (2014) gives pointwise rates for another modification. An existing sharp point-sampled Bernstein integrated norm concerns a different operator. Searches for binomial-absolute-difference, integrated-BV and alias formulations did not expose a dominating result. The complete 2000 Zeng–Chen and 1987 Guo papers were not inspected; their accessible descriptions do not resolve all possible historical overlap. Search absence is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
Integrated BV error gives an exact operator-independent calibration for arbitrary jump-bearing inputs. The full-degree formula, unique worst discontinuity, and \(\sqrt2\) asymptotic contrast with point-sampled Bernstein approximation connect signed-jump reduction, symmetric kernels and the mean absolute deviation of a binomial law. This is a complete natural worst-case invariant, rather than a chosen numerical instance or routine restatement of variation diminution.

## Limits
Classical real, unweighted one-dimensional Bernstein–Durrmeyer approximation only; no independent replication or historical-priority guarantee.
