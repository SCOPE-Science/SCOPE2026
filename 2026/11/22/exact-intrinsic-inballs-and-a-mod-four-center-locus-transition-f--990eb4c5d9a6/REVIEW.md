# Review of the intrinsic inball theorem for cycle edge-midpoint polytopes

## Correctness — PASS
The asserted metric is explicitly the Euclidean metric of the affine span. The odd/even rank split follows from the eigenvalues of the cyclic incidence matrix. The proposed facets are all facets: a simplex has one facet per omitted vertex; an orthogonal free sum of two simplices has one facet per ordered pair of omitted parity-class vertices. Their oriented support values are fixed at \(1\) and \(1-n\) (odd case), or \(1\) and \(1-m\) (even case). Direct cyclic recurrence gives the odd normal norm; conjugation by the parity diagonal gives the even norm through a cycle-Laplacian inverse whose required identities have explicit short proofs. Symmetry-averaging certifies the *global*, not merely centered, maximum. The tangent normals sum to zero; their simultaneous equality constraints and the strict slack at the centroid prove the unique-versus-\((m-1)\)-dimensional center-locus classification. Substitution into every facet inequality establishes the full difference-constrained center polytope. The independent exact-rational checker passes 1,022 finite statements over \(3\le n\le26\); it is not offered as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The general midpoint extremal-set setting is from Yi (2026); its inspected introduction and theorem statements do not supply cyclic subpolytope inradii. Tran–Ziegler (2014), Example 3, expressly supplies the cycle edge-polytope combinatorial type; that part is *not new*. A directly relevant public full result, *Exact Euclidean chord-distance shells of cycle edge polytopes inside the second hypersimplex* (2026), was inspected: it computes nearest distances from other hypersimplex vertices to the same cycle edge polytope, whereas this theorem concerns distances of intrinsic supporting **facets from the centroid and all largest interior ball centers**. Neither assertion implies the other by specialization or convex duality. Separate work on ordinary wheel edge polytopes, symmetric cycle root polytopes, and cycle graphical zonotopes has different polytopes. No matching exact metric spectrum or mod-four center locus was identified by the recorded statement-level comparisons. Historical priority may still be affected by unindexed older work; this is a nonindependent assessment, not a novelty certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A Hamiltonian cycle is the simplest spanning selection of the regular-simplex edge-midpoint arrangement, and its odd/even convex-hull types were long known. Their exact intrinsic roundness is an independent geometric invariant, and its complete facet-distance spectrum exposes a non-obvious order-mod-four transition: there is one maximal inball center at even orders divisible by four but a full positive-dimensional polytope of centers at even orders congruent to two. The difference inequalities explicitly quantify all the centers instead of merely counting them. The statement is an all-size structural classification, not a single computed small example, a reparameterization of a known formula, or an unmotivated numerical improvement.

## Closest coverage and limitations
The strongest same-object published comparison calculates an **outer approximation error** relative to the second hypersimplex; the new theorem calculates **inner-ball geometry** of the cycle hull in its own affine span. The precise regular-simplex circumradius normalization matters. Results for other graph polytopes do not transfer under mere affine equivalence. The finite checker is corroborative; the proof bears the infinite claim. Search and accessible primary-text comparisons may not locate every previous equivalent theorem, and no independent audit was performed.
