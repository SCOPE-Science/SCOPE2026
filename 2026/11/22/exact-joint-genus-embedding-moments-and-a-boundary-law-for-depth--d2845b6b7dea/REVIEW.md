# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite family is exhaustive because the Frobenius number lies below twice the multiplicity, making all optional lower elements independent under addition. Counting generators in Apéry residue classes gives \(e=m-r+1+|B|-\mathbf1_{\{1\in B\}}+U_r\). Distinct summand pairs for a fixed target form a matching, proving the exact expectation and coefficient formula without independence assumptions across different targets. The total-variation coupling has an exponentially summable error tail, and tightness of \(U_r\) permits a valid binomial CLT for \(e\). The checker reconstructs actual semigroups and generator decomposability rather than only repeating the displayed formula. There is no unproved infinite enumeration step.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

The closest 2026 multiparameter counting paper enumerates genus at prescribed multiplicity and Frobenius number but does not state conditional embedding-dimension moments. The 2017 generator-classification paper gives algorithms for all dimensions, not these explicit moments or boundary limit. The 2025 maximal-embedding-dimension paper and 2022 depth-two counts restrict to a special value or count the ambient semigroups. These statements are neither equivalent to nor stronger than the claim here. A separate earlier theorem on pseudo-Frobenius type in the same short-conductor family does not determine the embedding dimension: for \(r=5\) and \(m=7\), the subsets \(B=\{1,3,4\}\) and \(B=\{2,3,4\}\) have equal genus and pseudo-Frobenius type but embedding dimensions \(5\) and \(6\). The residual historical risk is an unindexed formula or table for the joint distribution, so priority is not an independent certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Genus and embedding dimension are two basic, independently studied invariants of numerical semigroups, relevant to minimal presentation and Wilf-type questions. The result bridges a recent exact genus count to a second invariant, supplies an exact expression for every fixed-genus mean, identifies a universal finite-size \(5/2\) correction, and proves the unexpectedly tight joint law \(e+g-2m\), in contrast with Gaussian marginal fluctuations. These are natural, parameter-uniform statistical features, not an arbitrary finite slice. It does not claim Wilf's conjecture or any consequences outside the stated domain.
