The elementary lifting congruence \(\sum_{j=1}^{p^e}(a+j)^{p-1}\equiv-p^{e-1}\pmod{p^e}\) induces isometric bijections on the \(p\) index branches. On the exceptional branch \(n\equiv-1\pmod p\), the Faulhaber value vanishes at \(n=-1\), and the consecutive increment has strictly greater valuation than the value. The remaining branches reduce to the exact character-pair count \((p-4-\chi_p(-1))/4\) plus two singular residues. These steps derive all four finite-precision counts and the limiting covariance. The extracted standalone verifier independently enumerated 84,031 indices over 23 prime-power cases; it does not certify the infinite theorem.

Earlier quadratic-character pattern theorems could imply the result by a non-obvious reduction; that possible broader coverage remains a residual risk. Database search absence alone was not treated as a novelty certificate.

The family is canonical: the power exponent \(p-1\) is exactly where the Fermat power-sum congruence changes behavior. The theorem computes a complete natural two-point invariant at every odd prime and precision; the sign of local-square dependence reverses by the arithmetic class of the prime, a phenomenon not recoverable from one-point value distribution or periodicity alone. This is a mathematically motivated joint arithmetic law, not a single unmotivated numeric slice.
