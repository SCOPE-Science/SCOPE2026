# Exact Jordan spectra of two-branch Artinian Gorenstein connected sums

## Finding

Let \(q\) be any prime power, let \(a,b\ge 2\), and put
\[
 A_{a,b}=\mathbb F_q[x,y]/(xy,x^a-y^b),\qquad
 \mathfrak m=(x,y)\subset A_{a,b}.
\]
This finite local complete-intersection algebra has dimension \(a+b\) and one-dimensional socle generated by \(s=x^a=y^b\). The following explicit finite double sum determines **the number of elements of \(\mathfrak m\) of every nilpotent multiplication Jordan type**, including all zero-multiplicity types, over every finite characteristic.

For \(\ell\in\mathfrak m\) write \(r_t(\ell)=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\ell^tA_{a,b}\), \(r_0=a+b\), and extend \(r_t=0\) past the nilpotency index. Define
\[
 e_d(\ell)=r_{d-1}-2r_d+r_{d+1}\quad(d\ge1),\qquad
 \Phi(\ell;\mathbf z)=\prod_{d=1}^{a+b}z_d^{e_d(\ell)}.
\]
Then \(e_d\) is the number of multiplication Jordan blocks of length exactly \(d\), and the full Jordan census is
\[
 \mathcal J_{a,b,q}(\mathbf z)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathfrak m}\Phi(\ell;\mathbf z).
\]
We give a sum with at most \(2ab\) nonzero terms that evaluates this polynomial without enumerating field elements.

For any finite \(i\in\{1,\ldots,a-1\}\), \(j\in\{1,\ldots,b-1\}\), or missing branch indicated by \(\infty\), define a rank profile \(R(i,j)=(r_t)\), using the four-case formula in the next section. All profiles are independent of the coefficients of \(\ell\), **except** when
\[
 i,j<\infty,\qquad a/i=b/j=k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}.
\]
At this one possible resonance exponent, they split into \(R^-(i,j)\) with \(r_k=0\), and \(R^+(i,j)\) with \(r_k=1\). Let \(\Phi(R)=\prod_d z_d^{r_{d-1}-2r_d+r_{d+1}}\). Define
\[
 \rho_q(k)=\#\{t\in\mathbb F_q^\times:t^k=-1\}.
\]
For \(d=\gcd(k,q-1)\), this number is \(d\) if \((-1)^{(q-1)/d}=1\) in \(\mathbb F_q\), and zero otherwise. In particular, in even characteristic it always equals \(d\).

The exact distribution polynomial is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J_{a,b,q}(\mathbf z)
={}&z_1^{a+b}+(q-1)z_2z_1^{a+b-2}\\
&+q\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}(q-1)q^{a-1-i}\Phi(R(i,\infty))\\
&+q\sum_{j=1}^{b-1}(q-1)q^{b-1-j}\Phi(R(\infty,j))\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}\sum_{j=1}^{b-1}q^{a+b-1-i-j}\,\Psi_{i,j},
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
\Psi_{i,j}=\begin{cases}
(q-1)^2\Phi(R(i,j)), & a/i\ne b/j\text{ or the common ratio is not an integer};\\
(q-1)\rho_q(k)\Phi(R^-(i,j))
+\bigl((q-1)^2-(q-1)\rho_q(k)\bigr)\Phi(R^+(i,j)),
& a/i=b/j=k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the coefficient of \(\prod_d z_d^{m_d}\) gives the exact multiplicity of the partition with \(m_d\) blocks of size \(d\). Coincident profiles from different index pairs are automatically added. The coefficients sum to \(q^{a+b-1}\), the number of elements of the maximal ideal.

**Arithmetic transition in the balanced case.** For \(a=b=n\ge3\), the only elements with either of the two partitions \((n+1,n-1)\) or \((n,n)\) are those having both linear coefficients nonzero. Their respective exact counts are
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \#\{\ell:P_\ell=(n,n)\}&=q^{2n-3}(q-1)\rho_q(n),\\
 \#\{\ell:P_\ell=(n+1,n-1)\}
 &=q^{2n-3}(q-1)\bigl(q-1-\rho_q(n)\bigr).
\end{aligned}}
\]
Consequently the balanced type \((n,n)\) occurs over \(\mathbb F_q\) precisely when \(-1\) is an \(n\)-th power. For odd \(q\), this is equivalent to \((q-1)/\gcd(n,q-1)\) being even; for even \(q\), it always occurs. Conditional on both linear coefficients being nonzero, the probability of \((n,n)\) is exactly \(\rho_q(n)/(q-1)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The field may have arbitrary characteristic, including characteristic two and characteristics dividing either branch length. The integers \(a,b\) are at least two; equality is not assumed. No separability, algebraic closure, or genericity hypothesis is used. Only the action by multiplication of an element in \(\mathfrak m\) on the **regular module** \(A_{a,b}\) is being counted; multiplication on unrelated modules and Jordan degree types are not claimed.

The vector-space basis
\[
 1,\ x,x^2,\ldots,x^{a-1},\ y,y^2,\ldots,y^{b-1},\ s
\]
contains \(a+b\) elements. Write each radical element uniquely as
\[
 \ell=f(x)+g(y)+cs,\qquad
 f=\sum_{h=1}^{a-1}u_hx^h,\quad
 g=\sum_{h=1}^{b-1}v_hy^h,\quad c\in\mathbb F_q.
\]
Let \(i\) be the smallest index with \(u_i\ne0\), or \(\infty\) when \(f=0\); define \(j\) similarly. For finite \(i,j\), put \(u=u_i\), \(v=v_j\). The number of choices of \(f\) with specified finite \(i\) and specified leading \(u\ne0\) is \(q^{a-1-i}\), and analogously for \(g\).

For each integer \(t\ge1\), take \(ti=\infty\) when \(i=\infty\), and similarly for \(tj\). The profile formula is
\[
 r_t=\begin{cases}
 a+b-ti-tj,&ti<a,\ tj<b;\\
 a+1-ti,&ti<a,\ tj\ge b;\\
 b+1-tj,&ti\ge a,\ tj<b;\\
 \mathbf 1_{\{\gamma_t\ne0\}},&ti\ge a,\ tj\ge b,
\end{cases}
\]
where
\[
 \gamma_t=\mathbf1_{\{ti=a\}}u^t
 +\mathbf1_{\{tj=b\}}v^t
 +\mathbf1_{\{t=1\}}c.
\]
A term involving a missing branch is zero. This last case includes \(f=g=0\) and the socle-only elements. In every other case, the value of \(c\) and the higher branch coefficients are irrelevant to the Jordan partition. When both branches are finite the only possible coefficient sensitivity is the indicated common terminal exponent.

## Proof

First, \(xy=0\) annihilates all mixed monomials. The relation \(x^a=y^b=s\), together with \(xs=ys=0\), gives the stated basis, confirms that the unique socle line is \(\mathbb F_qs\), and shows that \(A_{a,b}\) is a zero-dimensional complete intersection.

Next, for \(t\ge2\), the cross-branch products and all products with \(cs\) vanish, so \(\ell^t=f(x)^t+g(y)^t\). The lowest term of \(f(x)^t\) is \(u^t x^{ti}\) for \(ti<a\), is \(u^ts\) for \(ti=a\), and is zero for \(ti>a\); these statements follow directly by multiplying the lowest nonzero monomial \(t\) times. Exactly the same holds on the other branch. The formula also holds for \(t=1\) after adding \(cs\).

Suppose \(ti<a\) and \(tj<b\). Multiplying \(\ell^t\) by positive powers of \(x\) spans all branch monomials above degree \(ti\), and multiplying by positive powers of \(y\) spans all branch monomials above degree \(tj\). In each case the coefficient of the first such monomial is a nonzero power of the corresponding leading coefficient, so triangular elimination supplies every tail monomial. Both tails reach the same socle line \(\mathbb F_qs\). Modulo their span, \(\ell^t\) contributes the independent leading vector \(u^tx^{ti}+v^ty^{tj}\). Hence \(\ell^tA\) has dimension \((a-ti-1)+(b-tj-1)+1+1=a+b-ti-tj\). If only the \(x\)-branch is below its terminal degree, the same argument gives the full \(x\)-tail from degree \(ti\) through the socle, with dimension \(a+1-ti\); similarly for the other branch. If both are at or past their terminal degree, the whole principal ideal is either zero or \(\mathbb F_qs\), according to \(\gamma_t\). This proves every case of the rank-profile formula, including vanishing cases and endpoints.

For any nilpotent linear operator, the number of Jordan blocks of length at least \(t\) is \(r_{t-1}-r_t\); taking successive differences gives \(e_t=r_{t-1}-2r_t+r_{t+1}\). This reconstructs the full Jordan partition from the proved principal-ideal dimensions.

It remains to count elements. The zero and nonzero socle-only choices contribute respectively \(1\) and \(q-1\). With exactly one nonzero branch, the specified valuation contributes \((q-1)q^{a-1-i}\), or its \(y\)-analogue, and the socle coefficient varies freely in \(q\) ways. With two branches nonzero, each specified ordered pair of leading coefficients has \(q^{a+b-1-i-j}\) lifts, including the free socle coefficient. If \(a/i=b/j=k\) is an integer, cancellation of the terminal socle coefficient occurs exactly for \(u^k+v^k=0\). For each nonzero \(u\), there are \(\rho_q(k)\) possible ratios \(v/u\), hence exactly \((q-1)\rho_q(k)\) cancelling leading pairs. There are \((q-1)^2-(q-1)\rho_q(k)\) others. If no such common integer ratio exists, no cancellation affects any rank. The displayed finite sum follows, and its total mass is \(q\cdot q^{a-1}\cdot q^{b-1}=q^{a+b-1}\).

Finally, when \(a=b=n\ge3\) and \(i=j=1\), ranks at \(1\le t<n\) are \(2n-2t\), while the rank at \(n\) is either zero or one according to \(u^n+v^n\). This yields exactly \((n,n)\) or \((n+1,n-1)\). Conversely, these two partitions have first-power rank \(2n-2\); the rank formula forces \(i=j=1\) for \(n\ge3\). Counting the cancelling leading coefficients and their \(q^{2n-3}\) lifts yields the asserted exclusive counts. The cyclic multiplicative group of \(\mathbb F_q\) supplies the expression for \(\rho_q(k)\).

## Verification

The standalone Python file `connected_sum_checker.py` independently constructs the algebra multiplication table and obtains each power rank by field Gaussian elimination. It compares every radical element's rank sequence with the ideal-dimension formula, and separately compares the complete brute-force Jordan histogram with the weighted finite-double-sum histogram. Thirteen exhaustive configurations were checked, over fields of cardinalities \(2,3,4,5,7,9\) and branch lengths from two to four. The fields of cardinalities four and nine use distinct irreducible quadratic presentations. See `connected_sum_checker_output.txt` for the actual output.

For example, at \((a,b,q)=(3,3,3)\) there are \(243\) radical elements. The types \((4,2)\) and \((3,3)\) each occur \(54\) times, while the remaining types and counts are \((4,1,1):108\), \((2,2,1,1):24\), \((2,1,1,1,1):2\), and \((1,1,1,1,1,1):1\). At \((a,b,q)=(3,3,2)\), the type \((4,2)\) does not occur, while \((3,3)\) occurs eight times. These examples are exact finite checks, not the proof of the general formula.

## Relationship to prior work

Connected sums of Gorenstein local rings and their defining quotients were established by Ananthnarayan–Avramov–Moore and analyzed further by Ananthnarayan–Celikbas–Laxmi–Yang. The present family is a socle-identification of two truncated one-variable algebras; that construction and its Gorenstein nature are **not new**. Iarrobino–McDaniel–Seceleanu study Lefschetz properties and generic Jordan types of connected sums; their Example 5.17 also emphasizes base-field sensitivity in the quadratic case. Altafi–Iarrobino–Macias Marques survey the Jordan-type invariant and its open classification questions. These sources provide context but their inspected statements do not give the all-branch-length, all-element, finite-field multiplicity polynomial above.

The new claim concerns the exact distribution of *all* regular-module multiplication Jordan forms, including higher-order radical elements, and its arithmetic resonance multiplicities. It is not a claim that connected sums, generic Lefschetz types, valuation methods, or roots of unity are new.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to the two-branch socle-glued complete intersections displayed here. It does not address arbitrary connected sums, higher embedding dimension, noncommutative algebras, other modules, or the scheme-theoretic multiplicities of the Jordan strata. The historical novelty assessment is based on inspected accessible primary texts and mathematical searches; it is not a proof that no earlier equivalent census exists. A general Hall- or Lefschetz-theoretic identity might reproduce the same counts after specialization and remains a literature-priority risk.
