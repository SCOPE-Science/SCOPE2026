# Scientific review of equal-arm-spider Kemeny design

## Correctness

PASS. The exact resistance identity reduces the unrestricted symmetric stochastic design to a strictly convex sum of edge reciprocal weights. Arm automorphisms then justify symmetry without assuming it. The proposed root and alternating probabilities have strict first-interior-junction slack, all remaining interior constraints tight, and an explicit nonnegative KKT multiplier certificate. The positive pair-ratio monotonicity and decreasing coefficient sequence certify the multiplier signs for all arm counts and lengths; the finite eigenvalue verifier independently checks the normalization in sample networks. The large-length cost and comparison follow from exact coefficient sums.

## Originality

PASS on inspected evidence, with a stated priority risk. Agharkar–Patel–Bullo's general SDP includes the design problem but does not state this all-length optimal-support and edge-probability law. Kirkland–Zeng study Kemeny for ordinary graph random walks and edge addition; Zeng studies fixed weight-multiset weighted-tree arrangements with degree-normalized stationary masses. Published depth-three spider fastest-mixing formulas optimize a different spectral objective. Earlier explicit Kemeny path and broom designs are not stronger theorems for equal-arm spiders with at least three long arms, although the single-junction phenomenon is a related motif. No exhaustive publication-priority claim is made.

## Value

PASS. Equal-arm spiders are a natural model of multi-route transport with a common bottleneck. The theorem completely classifies a non-obvious and all-size holding-probability pattern, supplies the exact optimum rather than a numerical program, and quantifies a sharp \(k/2\) asymptotic cost penalty for a standard uniform-edge comparator. For high hub degree this penalty is unbounded as a function of arm count, making the precise edge allocation relevant to network design.

## Limits

The statement concerns irreducible real symmetric stochastic walks with uniform stationary distribution, equal arm lengths and non-return Kemeny spectral sum. Different objectives, varying stationary distributions, unequal arms and directed walks are not covered. An unindexed equivalent treatment remains possible, and the work has no independent mathematical audit.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
