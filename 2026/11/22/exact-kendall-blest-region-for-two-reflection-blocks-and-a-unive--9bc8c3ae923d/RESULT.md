# Exact Kendall–Blest region for two reflection blocks and a universal block lower envelope

## Finding
Let \(\tau\) denote Kendall's population rank correlation and \(\eta\) the symmetrized Blest rank coefficient. Let \(\mathcal B_2\) be the class of copulas generated by measure-preserving maps of \([0,1]\) which equal the identity except on at most two disjoint intervals, on each of which the map is reflection about that interval's midpoint. Define the cubic
\[
P(r)=r^3-2r^2-3r+2,
\]
and let \(r_*\) be its unique root in \((0,1)\). Define
\[
t_*=-\left(\frac{1-r_*}{1+r_*}\right)^2,\qquad
F_* = \frac{1+2r_*^3+r_*^4}{(1+r_*^2)^2}.
\]
Put \(s=\sqrt{(1-t)/2}\) and define
\[
L(t)=1-2s^3(2-s).
\]
For **every finite or countable ordinal sum of identity and reflection blocks**, \(\eta\ge L(\tau)\), with equality if and only if the graph map reflects one initial interval \([0,s]\) and is the identity elsewhere (with the degenerate identity when \(t=1\)). In particular this is the sharp lower boundary for all finite or countable reflection-block ordinal sums.

For every \(t\in[-1,1]\), the **exact**, attained maximum \(H_2(t)=\max\{\eta(C): C\in\mathcal B_2,\ \tau(C)=t\}\) is
\[
H_2(t)=\begin{cases}
\tfrac12\bigl(1+\sqrt{-t}+3t-(-t)^{3/2}\bigr),&-1\le t\le t_*,\\[2pt]
1-\tfrac12F_*(1-t)^2,&t_*\le t\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently, the **complete attainable fixed-Kendall section** over \(\mathcal B_2\) is exactly the closed interval
\[
\{\eta(C):C\in\mathcal B_2,\ \tau(C)=t\}=[L(t),H_2(t)],\qquad -1\le t\le1.
\]
In the nontrivial range \(-1<t<1\), every upper-endpoint optimizer, up to null sets, has exactly two adjacent countermonotone blocks at the upper end of the unit interval, with the shorter block preceding the longer one. For \(t>t_*\), a nonempty lower identity interval remains; for \(t<t_*\), the two blocks cover all of \([0,1]\). At \(t=t_*\) the blocks first fill the interval. The maximizing block lengths are
\[
(a,b)=\begin{cases}
\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{-t}}2,\frac{1+\sqrt{-t}}2\right),&-1<t\le t_*,\\[4pt]
\left(\frac{r_*s}{\sqrt{1+r_*^2}},\frac{s}{\sqrt{1+r_*^2}}\right),&t_*\le t<1,
\end{cases}\qquad s=\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{2}}.
\]
At the lower endpoint, the unique optimizer for \(-1<t<1\) has one initial reflection interval \([0,s]\) and is the identity on the remainder. The endpoints \(t=1\) and \(t=-1\) correspond to the identity and full reflection, respectively.

There is also a strict **refinement obstruction**: for every finite ordinal sum of identity and countermonotone intervals with at least one positive countermonotone interval and total countermonotone length strictly below \(1\), a finite ordinal sum with one additional countermonotone interval has the *same* Kendall coefficient and a strictly greater symmetrized Blest coefficient. Therefore no such unsaturated finite-block construction can maximize \(\eta\) over all finite-block ordinal sums. In particular, each sharp two-block optimizer with \(t_*<t<1\) is strictly improved by a three-block construction; no corresponding refinement assertion is made for the saturated regime.

The best one-countermonotone-block construction has \(\eta=1-(1-t)^2/2\). Hence the optimum above supplies a strictly larger, explicitly attainable lower bound on the as-yet-unresolved unrestricted \((\tau,\eta)\)-region for every \(-1<t<1\). In particular, for \(t\ge t_*\) the improvement is exactly
\[
H_2(t)-\left(1-\frac{(1-t)^2}{2}\right)
=\frac{1-F_*}{2}(1-t)^2>0.
\]
Numerically \(r_*\approx0.5293165801\), \(t_*\approx-0.0947246494\), and \(F_*\approx0.8390650012\); the exact algebraic definitions, not these decimals, are the theorem.

## Assumptions and scope
A copula is a probability law on \([0,1]^2\) having two standard-uniform marginals. Its rank functionals are defined by
\[
\tau(C)=\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}((U-U')(V-V'))],\qquad
\eta(C)=1-6\mathbb E[(U-V)^2(2-U-V)],
\]
where \((U,V)\) and \((U',V')\) are independent with law \(C\). Ties have probability zero in either coordinate. Given disjoint closed intervals \(I_i=[h_i,h_i+\ell_i]\subseteq[0,1]\), \(1\le i\le k\le2\), with disjoint interiors, the defining transformation of \(\mathcal B_2\) sends \(u\) to \(2h_i+\ell_i-u\) on each \(I_i\), and to \(u\) elsewhere. Endpoint choices do not matter. Each such map preserves Lebesgue measure and is an involution, so its graph defines an exchangeable copula. Zero-length blocks are allowed as degeneracies.

The full sharp fixed-Kendall **two-block** region is asserted only for \(\mathcal B_2\). The lower envelope extends to arbitrary finite or countable reflection-block ordinal sums, but not to arbitrary copulas. The claim does not assert that the upper two-block envelope reaches the unrestricted copula boundary, nor does it classify upper envelopes for families with three or more countermonotone blocks. The source first published 2026-11-05 posed the unrestricted Kendall–Blest region as an open problem.

## Proof
**1. Block identities.** For a reflection block \([h,h+\ell]\), let \(u=h+x\) and \(v=h+\ell-x\) for \(0\le x\le\ell\). Then \(u+v=2h+\ell\), \(u-v=2x-\ell\), and
\[
6\int_0^\ell (u-v)^2(2-u-v)\,dx
=6(2-2h-\ell)\frac{\ell^3}{3}
=2\ell^3(2-2h-\ell).
\]
Outside the reflection blocks \(U=V\), and thus no penalty is contributed. Two independently sampled points are discordant exactly when both belong to the same reflection block; the probability of this event is \(\sum_i\ell_i^2\). Therefore
\[
\tau=1-2\sum_i\ell_i^2,
\qquad
\eta=1-2\sum_i\ell_i^3(2-2h_i-\ell_i).
\]
These are direct integral/probability identities; they coincide with the general block observations in Section 6.2 of Rockel (2026).

**2. Sharp lower envelope for arbitrary finite or countable blocks.** Each block left endpoint satisfies \(h_i\ge0\), so by Step 1
\[
1-\eta\le2\sum_i\ell_i^3(2-\ell_i)=2\sum_i\ell_i^2 g(\ell_i^2),\quad g(x)=2\sqrt{x}-x.
\]
For \(0\le x\le1\), \(g\) is strictly increasing (its derivative is \(x^{-1/2}-1>0\) for \(0<x<1\)). Since \(\ell_i^2\le s^2=\sum_j\ell_j^2\),
\[
1-\eta\le2g(s^2)\sum_i\ell_i^2=2s^3(2-s).
\]
The argument extends to countable blocks by monotone convergence. For \(0<s<1\), equality requires exactly one positive-length block and its left endpoint zero; it is the initial interval \([0,s]\). For \(s=0\) or \(s=1\), the only possible configurations are the identity or full reflection respectively. This proves the universal sharp lower boundary and its equality classification.

**3. Optimal upper placement and ordering.** Fix two positive lengths \(a,b\), writing \(a\) for the lower interval's length. The \(\eta\)-penalty is strictly decreasing in either interval's left endpoint, as long as the block length is held fixed. Thus both blocks must be pushed together against the right endpoint: their intervals are \([1-a-b,1-b]\) and \([1-b,1]\). If only one interval is present, it is similarly right-packed. For the two right-packed intervals, the penalty is
\[
2\bigl(a^4+2a^3b+b^4\bigr).
\]
Reversing their order changes the penalty by \(4ab(a^2-b^2)\). Hence the smaller positive block must come first for strict optimality, and we may assume \(0<a\le b\). The feasibility condition is \(a+b\le1\).

**4. Reduction to a one-variable exact minimization.** Fix \(t\in(-1,1)\) and set \(s=\sqrt{(1-t)/2}\). The Kendall constraint is \(a^2+b^2=s^2\). Define \(r=a/b\in[0,1]\). Then
\[
a=\frac{sr}{\sqrt{1+r^2}},\quad b=\frac{s}{\sqrt{1+r^2}},\quad
\eta=1-2s^4 f(r),\quad
f(r)=\frac{r^4+2r^3+1}{(1+r^2)^2}.
\]
Direct differentiation yields
\[
f'(r)=-\frac{2r\,P(r)}{(1+r^2)^3},\qquad P(r)=r^3-2r^2-3r+2.
\]
On \([0,1]\), \(P'(r)=3r^2-4r-3<0\), while \(P(0)=2\) and \(P(1)=-2\). Thus \(P\) has one root \(r_*\) and \(f\) strictly decreases up to \(r_*\) and strictly increases afterward. Its global minimizer in \([0,1]\) is uniquely \(r_*\).

**5. The length constraint and transition.** The constraint \(a+b\le1\) reads
\[
s\frac{1+r}{\sqrt{1+r^2}}\le1.
\]
The factor \((1+r)/\sqrt{1+r^2}\) is strictly increasing for \(0\le r<1\). Hence \(r_*\) is feasible exactly when \(s\le\sqrt{1+r_*^2}/(1+r_*)\), which is exactly \(t\ge t_*\). There the maximizing ratio is \(r_*\). If \(t<t_*\), the feasible ratios form \([0,r_{\max}]\) with \(r_{\max}<r_*\); strict decrease of \(f\) forces \(r=r_{\max}\), so \(a+b=1\). Solving \(a+b=1\) and \(a^2+b^2=(1-t)/2\) with \(a\le b\) gives
\[
a=\frac{1-\sqrt{-t}}2,\qquad b=\frac{1+\sqrt{-t}}2.
\]
Inserting these lengths into \(1-2(a^4+2a^3b+b^4)\) yields \((1+\sqrt{-t}+3t-(-t)^{3/2})/2\). On the other side inserting \(r_*\) gives \(1-F_*(1-t)^2/2\). The two expressions agree at \(t=t_*\), since both arise from the same ratio and block lengths. Strict placement, order and ratio inequalities also establish the uniqueness of each nondegenerate maximizing graph, modulo null sets. The endpoint cases follow directly from \(\sum_i\ell_i^2=0\) or \(1\) and \(\sum_i\ell_i\le1\).

**6. Filling the region and comparison with one block.** For a single block, right packing minimizes the loss, and its length must equal \(s\), yielding \(\eta_1=1-2s^4\). Sliding this block continuously from \([0,s]\) to \([1-s,1]\) realizes every \(\eta\) between the lower bound \(L(t)\) and \(\eta_1(t)\). Next set the right-packed two-block length ratio to vary continuously from \(r=0\) (the same single block) to the constrained optimal ratio derived above. Its \(\eta=1-2s^4f(r)\) increases strictly throughout that feasible ratio interval and realizes every \(\eta\) from \(\eta_1(t)\) to \(H_2(t)\). Thus every point in the announced closed vertical section is attained and none outside it can arise from \(\mathcal B_2\). For every \(r\in(0,1)\),
\[
1-f(r)=\frac{2r^2(1-r)}{(1+r^2)^2}>0.
\]
The maximizing ratio is strictly between zero and one whenever \(-1<t<1\). Consequently \(H_2(t)>\eta_1(t)\) throughout that open interval, with the stated exact difference for \(t\ge t_*\). This is a constructive inequality for the unrestricted copula problem, not a claim of unrestricted sharpness.

**7. Strict refinement when residual identity length remains.** Consider any finite number \(k\ge1\) of positive reflection blocks, with lengths ordered increasingly \(a_1\le\cdots\le a_k\) and placed adjacently at the top. (Right packing and sorting cannot decrease \(\eta\), by the same translation argument and adjacent-swap calculation from Step 3.) Their total loss divided by two is
\[
F_k=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i^4+2\sum_{1\le i<j\le k}a_i^3a_j.
\]
Suppose their total length is strictly below one. Write \(a=a_1>0\), \(B=\sum_{j=2}^k a_j\). Choose \(0<c<d\) such that \(c^2+d^2=a^2\) and \(c+d-a\) is less than the unused length; such a choice exists by taking \(c\) small. Replace the lowest block \(a\) by two successive blocks of lengths \(c,d\), and right-pack all blocks. The Kendall value is unchanged and all lengths fit. The decrease in the loss divided by two is
\[
F_k-F_{k+1}=2c^2d(d-c)+2B\bigl(a^3-c^3-d^3\bigr)>0,
\]
since \(a^3=(c^2+d^2)^{3/2}>c^3+d^3\). For any original finite configuration with gaps, first right-pack and sort; if that improves \(\eta\), the desired strict superiority already follows, and otherwise this splitting step gives it. This proves the refinement assertion. In particular, for \(t>t_*\) the two-block optimizer has an unused identity interval and therefore admits a strict three-block improvement.

## Verification
`verify.py` is a standalone Python 3 program using only the standard library. It verifies the polynomial differentiation and factorization identities by exact integer-polynomial arithmetic; rational brackets around the unique cubic root; the block integral identities; the two possible block orders; the right-packing comparison; and the constrained formulas at exact-rational test parameters. Running `python3 verify.py` exits successfully only if every assertion passes. These finite checks and polynomial identities supplement the complete analytic proof above; they are not used to extrapolate finite testing to all real \(t\).

## Relationship to prior work
Rockel (2026, arXiv:2611.08026v1, Section 6.2) gives the exact block-loss identity and shows that splitting a single top reflection block into two unequal blocks strictly increases \(\eta\) at fixed \(\tau\). That construction and the fact that the unrestricted \((\tau,\eta)\)-region is open are **prior work**. The addition here is an exact **complete** joint region over the two-block class (including its lower endpoint, vertical filling, upper endpoint, all equality cases, and a new length-saturation transition), a sharp universal lower envelope across every finite or countable collection of reflection blocks, and the finite-block unused-length refinement theorem. This result is not an unrestricted solution. The all-block lower envelope, two-block upper envelope, complete two-block vertical sections, and finite-block strict-refinement mechanism are a single coherent extremal classification for this canonical ordinal-sum model.

Rockel (2026, arXiv:2609.27634) treats the different exact regions \((\rho,\nu)\) and \((\eta,\nu)\). The earlier article on Kendall's tau and other concordance measures (DOI 10.1007/s00009-023-02350-0) determines relations with Spearman's footrule and Gini's gamma rather than the displayed Kendall–Blest two-block envelope.

## Limitations
The two-block class is strictly smaller than all copulas and all ordinal sums. Its upper envelope is therefore a **lower bound** on the unrestricted upper boundary, not an upper bound on arbitrary copulas. The universal lower envelope is restricted to finite or countable reflection-block ordinal sums, not arbitrary copulas. The strict refinement result concerns only copulas with a positive unused identity interval; it does not prove optimality or nonoptimality of saturated finite-block constructions. The exact optimum when three or more countermonotone blocks are allowed, and the unrestricted \((\tau,\eta)\)-region, remain open. The main comparison and source dates were checked against accessible primary materials; an equivalent or stronger historical specialization not found in those sources remains possible. The analysis is a self-review, not independent validation.
