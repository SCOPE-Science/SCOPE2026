# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The accepted claim is the complete kinetic law of irreversible rate-one dimer deposition on the twelve-site icosahedral graph. The proof uses the finite edge-choice Markov chain, a graph-independent-set bound, and an exact triangular Kolmogorov system. The complete terminal law, exact mean time, 17-term completion CDF and five distinct individual-perfect-matching weights have all been independently checked using rational arithmetic. The provided checker reconstructs the two data tables and validates each exact formula. The finite recurrence is exhaustive; it is not inferred from finite-time simulations.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with residual risk
The dimer arrival model is not new: Penrose and Sudbury (2005) formalize it and develop general equations, and Gamarnik and Goldberg (2008) analyze equivalent greedy matchings. The inspected regular-graph theorem requires girth at least four, unlike the icosahedron. Existing static perfect-matching counts (125) do not imply the kinetic weighting or jamming-time distribution. Relevant record searches for the graph name, its aliases, exact numerators and denominator, and competing processes did not establish prior stronger coverage. Pippenger's relevant 1989 full text was unavailable; priority against unindexed exact tables is not guaranteed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The icosahedron is the canonical twelve-site five-regular Platonic graph and a natural finite curved surface network for deposition kinetics. The complete terminal geometry and time law are useful exact finite benchmarks. In particular, the 125 static perfect matchings acquire five distinct nonuniform probabilities under random sequential deposition, providing a concrete kinetic-versus-static distinction. This finite exact result claims neither a universal theorem over polyhedra nor an asymptotic phase transition.

## Scope and remaining uncertainty
All clocks are independent rate-one exponentials and used once; the terminal law, but not the completion-time law, extends to other identically distributed continuous priority clocks. There is no independent scientific certification and no conclusion concerning adsorption on larger graphs.
