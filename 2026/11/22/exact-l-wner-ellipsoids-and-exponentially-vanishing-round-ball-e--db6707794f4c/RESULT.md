# Exact Löwner ellipsoids and exponentially vanishing round-ball efficiency of even-cycle root polytopes

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge2\), the canonical edge polytope of the unweighted cycle on \(2m\) vertices has a unique intrinsic minimum-volume enclosing Euclidean ellipsoid, with an exact Fourier spectrum and full vertex contact. Its ratio to the volume of the unique minimum enclosing Euclidean ball is
\[
\boxed{\frac{\operatorname{Vol}_{2m-2}(\mathcal E_m)}{\operatorname{Vol}_{2m-2}(\mathcal B_m)}=\frac{m}{2^{m-1}}.}
\]
The ratio is one only for the four-cycle and decreases strictly to zero, exponentially, along longer even cycles. Nevertheless every vertex touches both the optimal ellipsoid and the smallest enclosing ball. The ratio of the longest to shortest ellipsoidal semiaxis is exactly \(\cot(\pi/(2m))\), which is asymptotic to \(2m/\pi\).

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(n=2m\) and index the standard orthonormal basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_{n-1}\) of \(\mathbb R^n\) cyclically. Define the **ordinary unsigned graph-edge polytope**
\[
P_m=\operatorname{conv}\{v_j=e_j+e_{j+1}:j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}.
\]
This is Euclidean-congruent, by negating the coordinates in one bipartition class and optionally applying an overall sign, to the standard-orientation bipartite root polytope of the even cycle. In particular, it is **not** the centrally symmetric polytope generated by both signs of every edge root.

Its affine center, translation space, and dimension are
\[
c=\frac2n\mathbf1,\qquad
H=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_{j\ \mathrm{even}}x_j=\sum_{j\ \mathrm{odd}}x_j=0\},\qquad d=n-2.
\]
All ellipsoids, volumes, covariances, and Euclidean balls below are intrinsic to the affine Euclidean space \(c+H\). Define the positive-definite operator on \(H\)
\[
C=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(v_j-c)(v_j-c)^{\mathsf T}\bigm|_H.
\]
Write \(\kappa_d=\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(1+d/2)\), the volume of a Euclidean unit ball in dimension \(d\).

**Complete explicit assertion.** The unique minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid and minimum enclosing ball are respectively
\[
\mathcal E_m=\{c+x:x\in H,\ \langle x,C^{-1}x\rangle\le d\},
\qquad
\mathcal B_m=\{c+x:x\in H,\ \|x\|^2\le 2-2/m\}.
\]
Their contact sets with \(P_m\) consist of all \(n\) vertices \(v_j\) and no other points of \(P_m\). Every ellipsoid principal semiaxis occurs among
\[
a_k=2\sqrt{\frac{m-1}{m}}\cos\!\frac{\pi k}{2m},\qquad 1\le k\le m-1,
\]
with **multiplicity two for each** \(k\). Consequently
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_d(\mathcal E_m)=\kappa_d\,m\left(1-\frac1m\right)^{m-1},
\qquad
\operatorname{Vol}_d(\mathcal B_m)=\kappa_d\left[2\left(1-\frac1m\right)\right]^{m-1}.
\]

## Proof
**1. The exact affine geometry.** The linear map from the \(n\) standard edge labels to \(\mathbb R^n\) that sends the \(j\)-th edge label to \(v_j\) is the cyclic incidence matrix \(A=I+S\), with \(S e_j=e_{j+1}\). The kernel of \(A\) has dimension one, generated by the alternating-sign vector, because \(n\) is even. Its image consists of vectors whose even-coordinate sum equals their odd-coordinate sum. Every \(v_j\) has one coordinate equal to one in each bipartition class, so its affine span is precisely \(c+H\), with dimension \(n-2\). Rotation acts transitively on the \(v_j\), whose arithmetic mean is \(c\). Every \(v_j\) is exposed by the linear functional \(\langle v_j,\cdot\rangle\): its value on \(v_j\) is two and on every other listed generator is at most one.

**2. The unique smallest enclosing ball.** Each centered vertex has squared length
\[
\|v_j-c\|^2=\|v_j\|^2-\|c\|^2=2-\frac4n=2-\frac2m.
\]
For any center \(c+t\), averaging the squared distances to the vertices gives
\[
\frac1n\sum_j\|v_j-(c+t)\|^2=2-\frac2m+\|t\|^2.
\]
Hence every enclosing ball has squared radius at least \(2-2/m+\|t\|^2\); the ball displayed above attains that lower bound with center \(c\), and the center is necessarily unique. Since the Euclidean norm is strictly convex and all vertices have the same positive radius, a nonvertex convex combination of the \(v_j\) cannot touch this sphere.

**3. The covariance eigenvalues.** On \(H\), the constant part of the centered covariance vanishes, so \(C=AA^{\mathsf T}/n\). For the cyclic Fourier mode \(k\), the eigenvalue of \(AA^{\mathsf T}=2I+S+S^{\mathsf T}\) is
\[
2+2\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n}=4\cos^2\frac{\pi k}{n}.
\]
The modes \(k=0\) (constant) and \(k=m\) (alternating) are orthogonal to \(H\) and must be removed. Thus the positive eigenvalues of \(C\) are
\[
\lambda_k(C)=\frac4n\cos^2\frac{\pi k}{n},\qquad
1\le k\le n-1,\quad k\ne m.
\]
The conjugate frequencies \(k\) and \(n-k\) give the claimed doubled semiaxis family upon multiplying \(C\) by \(d=n-2\). All eigenvalues are strictly positive, proving that \(C\) is invertible on \(H\).

**4. A global certificate for the Löwner ellipsoid.** Cyclic rotation is an orthogonal symmetry preserving the center and covariance. Therefore all the squared Mahalanobis lengths \(q_j=\langle v_j-c,C^{-1}(v_j-c)\rangle\) are equal. Their mean is
\[
\frac1n\sum_j q_j=\operatorname{tr}_H(C^{-1}C)=d,
\]
so every \(q_j=d\). The displayed ellipsoid \(\mathcal E_m\) contains all \(v_j\) with equality and therefore contains their convex hull \(P_m\).

To verify **global** minimum volume, consider any ellipsoid containing \(P_m\), with center \(z\in c+H\) and positive-definite squared-shape operator \(Q\) on \(H\). Averaging the \(n\) vertex-containment inequalities yields
\[
\operatorname{tr}_H(Q^{-1}C)+\langle z-c,Q^{-1}(z-c)\rangle\le1.
\]
The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality applied to the \(d\) eigenvalues of \(Q^{-1/2}CQ^{-1/2}\) implies
\[
\left(\frac{\det_H C}{\det_H Q}\right)^{1/d}
\le\frac1d\operatorname{tr}_H(Q^{-1}C)\le\frac1d.
\]
Hence \(\det_H Q\ge d^d\det_H C\), with equality only when \(z=c\) and \(Q=dC\). Intrinsic ellipsoid volume is \(\kappa_d\sqrt{\det_H Q}\), so \(\mathcal E_m\) is the **unique** global minimizer, not merely a symmetry-restricted candidate. A strict convex combination of distinct vertices has Mahalanobis norm strictly below \(\sqrt d\), so exactly the vertices are contact points.

**5. Exact volume and conditioning.** The classical product identity
\[
\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\cos\frac{\pi k}{2m}=\frac{\sqrt m}{2^{m-1}}
\]
follows by pairing sine factors in \(\prod_{j=1}^{2m-1}\sin(\pi j/(2m))=2m/2^{2m-1}\). Using the twofold multiplicity of each \(a_k\),
\[
\sqrt{\det_H(dC)}=\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}a_k^2
=\left[4\frac{m-1}{m}\right]^{m-1}
\frac{m}{4^{m-1}}
=m\left(1-\frac1m\right)^{m-1}.
\]
The ball's volume factor is \((2-2/m)^{d/2}\), and division gives \(m/2^{m-1}\). Since \(a_1\) is the largest and \(a_{m-1}\) the smallest positive semiaxis, their ratio is
\[
\frac{a_1}{a_{m-1}}=\cot\frac{\pi}{2m}\sim\frac{2m}{\pi}.
\]
Finally, \((m+1)/2^m < m/2^{m-1}\) for every \(m\ge2\), proving strict decay. This establishes all claims, including the \(m=2\) boundary.

## Relationship to prior work
Kálmán and Tóthmérész introduce and analyze the root polytopes of semi-balanced directed graphs, including standard bipartite orientations, via dissections, shellability and Ehrhart information [1]. The congruent even-cycle model used here belongs to their geometric setting, but its Euclidean minimum-volume outer ellipsoid and the exact volume ratio above are not consequences stated in their studied results. An existing exact support-plane and incenter classification for the *same* bipartite-cycle root polytopes concerns the largest **inscribed ball**, rather than the Löwner ellipsoid [2]. Related explicit outer ellipsoid analyses for ordinary **odd-prism** and **wheel** edge polytopes treat different graphs with distinct edge orbits and different optimization parameters [3,4]. The general correspondence between minimum-volume ellipsoids and D-optimal designs is classical and is not claimed as new; for an account in a graph setting see [5].

## Limitations
The result fixes the unit-weight, unscaled ordinary Euclidean realization of the even-cycle graph-edge polytope. The metric statements are not affine-invariant and do not extend automatically to weighted cycles, arbitrary graph orientations or the centrally symmetric edge polytope. The fact that the mean covariance provides an optimum is a consequence of cyclic vertex symmetry, and the proof does not assert a new general ellipsoid optimization principle. The potentially novel geometric content is the explicit Fourier semiaxis spectrum, exact closed volume ratio and quantitative anisotropy for this natural family. As with any literature comparison, unindexed equivalent formulas are a historical-priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
