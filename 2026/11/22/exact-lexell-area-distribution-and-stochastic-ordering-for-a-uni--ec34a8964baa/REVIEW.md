# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact sphere-triangle solid-angle formula reduces the area sublevel set to the intersection of a spherical cap and a hemisphere. Two corner angles and a signed small-circle geodesic-curvature integral yield its area by Gauss–Bonnet. The derivative of the closed CDF with respect to the fixed base cosine is strictly positive at every interior threshold. Both singular-base limits and both endpoint-density coefficients follow by explicit expansion. An independently implemented conditional-arcsine quadrature passed 35 comparisons; finite numerical checks are corroboration, not infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

The fixed-area small-circle locus is classical (Euler–Lexell), and Papadopoulos, *On the works of Euler and his followers on spherical geometry*, arXiv:1409.4736 (2014), Sections 5–6, was inspected in full. The closest published random spherical-triangle area result, *Exact equatorial bifurcation and universal marked area law for pole-anchored spherical triangles*, has a different two-random-latitude-point sampling law; its complete public theorem and proof were compared and do not imply the whole-sphere uniform-apex CDF. published-finding corpus searches for fixed-base uniform-apex spherical-excess laws and their aliases found no more dominating exact law. Maehara–Martini, *On Lexell’s Theorem* (2017), was inspected only at abstract level; its full text was not obtained and historical overlap remains a risk. No claim is made that failed search alone establishes novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural probability law induced by uniform surface sampling turns Lexell’s level-circle geometry into an exact identifiable stochastic invariant for the base length. It is strictly ordered by the base and has sharply distinct shrinking-edge and antipodal-edge scaling limits. This supplies a motivated geometric distribution and a useful boundary of applicability for fixed-latitude and Euclidean-area studies.

## Limits

Exactly one fixed nonantipodal base and whole-sphere uniform apex; no arbitrary density, ambient curvature, or uniform convergence rate. Historical priority is provisional and independent verification is outstanding.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
