# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is local to the smooth, labelled product-sphere chamber of the orthogonal regular-pentagon pair. The existing twenty-five facets persist under small perturbations, and their oriented determinant sum gives the exact volume. The second variation is derived using normalized tangent paths, planar area components, first-order mixed bivectors, and the four-dimensional Hodge star. It is then diagonalized by the five-point discrete Fourier basis and the four-dimensional matrix-pair decomposition. The resulting five eigenvalues have multiplicities totaling 30; six zeros are exactly infinitesimal orthogonal rotations. The least positive eigenvalue produces the sharp metric-normal orbit-distance limit. The standalone script independently checks direct determinant finite differences in six directions. It is supplementary numerical testing, not a replacement for the symbolic proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
Medgyes's ten-vertex theorem establishes the unique global maximizer and its volume but does not state its full constrained Hessian, multiplicities or orbit-distance constant. The companion nine-vertex theorem proves the polygon-join factorization and a global Fourier inequality, neither of which provides the exact second variation without further analysis. The closest mathematical-record result is a local Hessian for a distinct seven-vertex maximizer, a triangle joined to two diameters. Targeted searches for the ten-vertex Hessian, regular-pentagon pair, second variation and the rational coefficient found no equivalent published result. A precursor derived implicitly from general Fourier inequalities or unindexed computations is a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The ten-vertex maximizer is a natural, newly determined extremal convex body. The exact five-level spectrum distinguishes three different nonrotational stiffness mechanisms, proves that all infinitesimal flat directions are isometries, and identifies the least stable deformation as a first angular Fourier mode. Its sharp local constant gives a quantitative rigidity refinement of the known uniqueness theorem, with an exact rational coefficient. This is not a mere alternative proof of the known maximum-volume value or a minor numerical improvement.
