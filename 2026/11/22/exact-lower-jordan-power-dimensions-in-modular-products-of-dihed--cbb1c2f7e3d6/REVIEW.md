# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In characteristic two, xy+yx is the Lie bracket. For A trivial, center Z is C2^r and K=kZ has the squarefree basis t_T with t_i=1+z_i. The raw spans split by quotient coset and are K-stable by moving any central coefficient into the first entry of a left-normed bracket. A bracket reaching v from u acquires the factor 1+∏z_i^{B_i(u_i,v_i)}, up to a central unit. Its support is in the ideal I_{S(v)} generated by nonzero coordinates of v, and degree is at least one. At level two each such t_i is achieved by choosing one coordinate of the commutator vector. Thus the induction gives only degree at least n−1 at level n, and only monomials meeting S(v). In the converse step for target support T of size at least n, take i in T∩S(v) and j in T other than i. Choose commutator vector supported exactly on i, yet u_j≠0: use u_j=v_j when v_j≠0, and otherwise any nonzero vector. Then t_{T−{i}}h_u is available at level n, and the next bracket gives t_T h_v after removal of its central unit. This proves the coset formula, including the zero coset and empty sums. For support size j there are 4^r−4^{r−j} eligible cosets; the last nonzero degree consists of 4^r−1 top monomials. Tensoring with kA multiplies dimensions by |A| even when A has even order: the other tensor factors may all be taken equal to 1 in the reverse inclusion. The independent binary group-table and elimination replay checks every coset rank for r=1,2,3 and every mixture up to permutation, including (512,387,243,63,0) for rank three. The proof, not the finite computation, covers arbitrary r and all characteristic-two fields.

## value

The support formula gives actual raw homogeneous subspaces in a natural class of modular group algebras. It shows precisely how the vanishing of commutator coordinates affects every intermediate layer and why dihedral and quaternion squares do not alter these ranks. This is useful for distinguishing lower central structure from nilpotency indices or generated-ideal dimensions, already inequivalent in the first nontrivial example.
