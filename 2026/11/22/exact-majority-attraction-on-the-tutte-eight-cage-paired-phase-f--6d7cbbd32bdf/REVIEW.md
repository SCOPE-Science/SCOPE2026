# Scientific assessment of the Tutte eight-cage majority census

## Correctness

**PASS.** The graph is defined without an external table as fifteen duads and fifteen synthemes; each vertex has degree three and uses strict open-neighbor majority. Both submitted verification programs exhaust all 32,768 inputs on each shore with different incidence constructions and Boolean implementations, establishing \(P^3=P^2\) and \(Q^3=Q^2\) as complete finite identities. The periodic states are proved to be a phase-label swap, giving exactly 2,386 fixed states and 2,845,305 two-cycle orbits. The nine paired weights, orbit-basin polynomial, exhaustive state-mass identity and first-entry histogram agree in both checkers. The 106/0 witness proves the four-update bound sharp. The result does not claim a general graph theorem or replace finite exhaustion with sampling.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** Prior period-two and general bipartite-expansion theorems are acknowledged. Coxeter's original graph paper provides the incidence structure but no dynamics. The published Heawood majority work supplies the phase-swap idea but has a different, seven-by-seven incidence structure and cannot determine the fifteen-by-fifteen paired fiber table. The unequal paired weights, precise basin polynomial and sharp four-update clock were not found in the searched primary literature or mathematical-result records. Novelty remains subject to possible unindexed equivalent enumerations.

## Value

**PASS.** The graph is the unique cubic girth-eight cage and the smallest nontrivial generalized quadrangle's incidence graph, not an arbitrary sample. The exact attraction census distinguishes a three-dimensional incidence geometry's recurrent states from their highly uneven incoming basins. The paired asymmetry \((22,38)\) and \((38,22)\) is structurally meaningful because self-duality does not force the initial-state fibers matched by the one-step map to have equal sizes; earlier balanced Heawood phases do not exhibit this phenomenon. A complete orbit-basin generating polynomial and sharp settling-time histogram provide precise benchmarks for network threshold dynamics.

## Closest work and limits

The inspected sources include Coxeter's graph construction (1958); the general threshold-network papers of Kuhlman et al. (2011) and Adam et al. (2012); and the complete published Heawood majority classification. Goles–Olivos (1980) was checked via its publisher-indexed abstract, not its full article. The result is only for one finite graph and one synchronous update rule; publication-priority risk remains. The included checkers verify all finite cases, while external validation has not been conducted.
