# Scientific review: marked first-excursion and terminal-capture origins

## Correctness — PASS

The proof derives the killed Brownian two-boundary probabilities \(A_m(x)\) and \(B_m(x)\) directly from their Dirichlet problems. Geometric renewal sums conditional first-exceedance marks. A second independent renewal calculation identifies the starting-site mark of the eventually absorbing cycle. At the *first* crossing of the high barrier, strong Markov and exponential memorylessness split the eventual capture into a same-excursion diagonal event with probability \(e^{-\kappa m}\), or a fresh-reset event with independent absorption mark. These cases are exhaustive and give the advertised joint formula, including the exact covariance. The monotone-likelihood-ratio comparison is algebraic, and the endpoint Gamma limits follow from positive endpoint density asymptotics by Laplace's method, with an exponentially vanishing diagonal mixture. The proof states all quantifiers, support restrictions, the identity of each mark, and the limits of the theorem.

An executable standalone standard-library checker reproduces a two-atom Brownian example and independently enumerates all transient states of a reflected discrete random walk with resetting and marked first threshold crossing; it agrees with the predicted discrete regenerative splitting. The checker also verifies finite-mesh convergence of killed first-exit probabilities and the two boundary-layer means for a Beta-type density. These tests support, but do not replace, the analytical argument. The checker passed 26 declared assertions.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Toledo-Marín and Boyer (2022; arXiv:2206.14387) analyze reset-location distributions, mean first passage, reset counts and entropy. Guo, Yan and Chen (2023; arXiv:2306.15929) derive preabsorptive maximum statistics for fixed reset locations. Méndez, Flaquer-Galmés and Campos (2024; arXiv:2401.01125) derive first-passage-time laws and mean-equivalent reset locations. The published *Sharp excursion-risk envelope at fixed Brownian search time under distributed resetting* (2026) is especially close: it explicitly gives an unmarked threshold-exceedance formula for the same spatial resetting process and uses the same basic killed-excursion calculation. That scalar formula, and its fixed-mean optimization, are **prior coverage**, not findings asserted here.

The present claim instead specifies two distinct random origin marks on a **single preabsorptive path** and proves their full conditional joint measure, threshold invariance of the first origin, exact covariance, strict mark ordering, and opposite-endpoint independent Gamma layers. The inspected unmarked maximum paper and first-passage references do not state these joint path-mark relationships. The displayed joint measure cannot be recovered as a logical statement from knowledge of the scalar maximum CDF or an absorption-time transform without further modeling of the marked path and a separate Markov calculation. General path-decomposition principles make this theorem a plausible extension rather than an unrelated invention; equivalent unindexed published marked-renewal statements remain a significant publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Resetting to a distribution creates two competing spatial mechanisms: sites far from the target make extreme excursions more likely, whereas sites near the target make absorption before resetting more likely. The exact conditional joint law identifies **which reset sites drive each mechanism**; it shows that the origin of a threshold-reaching event is insensitive to the chosen upper threshold outside the reset region, while the association between excursion and capture origins has an explicit exponential strength. Strict ordering is global over positive reset rates and all non-point reset measures. The independent Gamma boundary layers quantify both endpoint neighborhoods for physically natural continuous reset distributions, rather than merely estimating a scalar excursion probability. These are meaningful, measurable mark statistics not supplied by published mean completion times or unmarked maximum distributions.

## Limits and priority comparison

The initial location must share the reset law, the support must lie strictly away from the target, and the threshold must lie above the whole support. The marked event is the **first** excursion to reach that threshold, not the excursion attaining the eventual exact global maximum. The endpoint limit assumes the stated endpoint density asymptotics. No claims are made for non-Poisson clocks, state-dependent reset rates, target crossing by reset jumps or unbounded reset distributions. The general two-boundary ODE and unmarked exceedance formulas are established ingredients; originality is claimed only for the marked joint/ordering/layer theorem. Independent priority review may uncover broader formulas that imply it.
