In the edge-by-edge matching-saturation game, the icosahedral and dodecahedral graphs both have a strict second-player advantage: their (Max-first, Min-first) values are \((5,6)\) and \((8,9)\), respectively. After any Min opening on the icosahedron, exactly one of the 21 possible second edges allows Max to force a perfect matching; an exact classification covers all five Platonic graph skeletons, with the classical octahedral case credited to earlier work.
