# Mathematical review

## Correctness
PASS. The Gram inverse is obtained by a reciprocal-quartic factorization, the kernel coefficient sequence has two geometric modes, the coefficient-ratio bounds prove a unique sign-changing root in each half-cell, and geometric summation bounds the infinite tail. The L-infinity duality and preservation of constants give equality and the exact positivity loss. The supplementary checker runs independently of the proof.

## Originality
PASS, with historical risk. Known spline projector boundedness and periodic spline inverse-Gram decay do not provide this exact midpoint evaluation norm. Published work on the quadratic cardinal kernel's remote superlocalization addresses decay at a different evaluation point, whereas existing exact periodic linear-spline results use degree one. An older equivalent cardinal-spline Lebesgue-function calculation may exist; this was not exhaustively excluded.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The half-cell evaluation is a canonical sampling location for quadratic cardinal splines. Its sharp noise amplification and positivity defect give exact benchmarks for pointwise stability and a rigorous lower benchmark for the global norm, without asserting a global maximizer. The distinction between a constructive exact series and a finite quadrature approximation is explicit.

## Limitations
The proof is specific to uniform quadratic cardinal splines and fixed midpoint evaluation. No global norm or closed elementary value is established. Relevant older interpolation literature may contain reformulations.
