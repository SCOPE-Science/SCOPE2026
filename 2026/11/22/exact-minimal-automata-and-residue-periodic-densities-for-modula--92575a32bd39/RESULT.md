# Exact minimal automata and residue-periodic densities for modular mixed-pair words

## Finding
Fix an alphabet \(\Sigma=\{1,\ldots,q\}\), with \(q\ge2\), and an integer modulus \(m\ge2\). For letters \(i\ne j\), let \(\binom{w}{ij}\) be the number of ordered pairs of positions of \(w\) whose letters are \(i\) followed by \(j\) (not necessarily adjacent). Set
\[
L_{q,m}=\{w\in\Sigma^*: \binom{w}{ij}\equiv0\pmod m\text{ for every }i\ne j\}.
\]
The **minimal complete deterministic finite automaton** recognizing \(L_{q,m}\) has exactly
\[
\boxed{m^{q+\binom q2}=m^{q(q+1)/2}}
\]
states. Its transition group is precisely the finite class-two group of modular letter counts and one orientation of every mixed pair, defined below; in particular no distinct states of this explicit counting automaton merge under minimization.

There is also an exact residue-class-dependent asymptotic law. Put \(d=q(q+1)/2\), let \(W_n\) be a uniformly random length-\(n\) word, and define
\[
E_s(m,q)=\#\{(r_1,\ldots,r_q)\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^q:
\sum_i r_i=s,\ r_i r_j=0\ (i<j)\}.
\]
Then for each \(s\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), as \(n\to\infty\) along \(n\equiv s\pmod m\),
\[
\Pr(W_n\in L_{q,m})\longrightarrow\frac{E_s(m,q)}{m^{d-1}},
\]
with exponential convergence for fixed \(q,m\). When \(m=p\) is prime the limit simplifies to
\[
\boxed{\frac1{p^{d-1}}\ \text{if }n\equiv0\pmod p,\qquad
\frac q{p^{d-1}}\ \text{if }n\not\equiv0\pmod p.}
\]
Thus the long-word acceptance density alternates between two distinct values for every \(q\ge2\) and prime \(p\), rather than converging to a single density.

## Assumptions and scope
Subsequence occurrences are counted by distinct embeddings, not by distinct resulting strings. Only ordered patterns formed by **two different letters** are constrained; same-letter pairs \(ii\), which equal \(\binom{|w|_i}{2}\), are deliberately excluded. Words can be empty; empty and one-letter words are accepted. The automaton is deterministic, complete, and has no end marker. The state-count assertion holds for all integers \(q,m\ge2\), including composite moduli, with no probabilistic assumption. The density assertion assumes independently uniformly sampled input letters and takes a limit along each fixed length residue class. The group conclusion concerns the transition group of the displayed minimal DFA, not the set of entire words.

## Proof
**1. Finite counting group.** Work over \(R=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). For a word \(w\) record \(r_i=|w|_i\bmod m\) for each letter and \(c_{ij}=\binom{w}{ij}\bmod m\) for \(i<j\). A word with every ordered pair included would have redundant counters, since
\[
\binom{w}{ij}+\binom{w}{ji}=|w|_i|w|_j\quad(i\ne j).
\]
Define \(G=R^q\times R^{\binom q2}\) with multiplication
\[
(r,c)\cdot(t,u)=(r+t,\ (c_{ij}+u_{ij}+r_i t_j)_{i<j}).
\]
This is associative: in a product of three factors the cross-term for an ordered pair is \(r_i t_j+r_i v_j+t_i v_j\), independently of parenthesization. The identity is zero, and the inverse of \((r,c)\) is \((-r,(-c_{ij}+r_i r_j)_{i<j})\). Hence this is a group, whose order is \(m^d\). All letter maps are right multiplications by \(g_i=(e_i,0)\); they increment the letter count and any pair count whose second letter has just arrived.

**2. All \(m^d\) states are reached by positive words.** Each \(g_i^m=e\), since one-letter words create no different-letter pairs. Thus the submonoid generated by the letter images is also a group (inverse generators are positive powers). The commutator \(g_i g_j g_i^{-1}g_j^{-1}\), for \(i<j\), has zero letter coordinates and has pair coordinate \(c_{ij}=1\), with every other pair coordinate zero. These central elements generate the entire pair-coordinate subgroup. The \(g_i\) generate all letter-coordinate vectors. Every element of \(G\) is therefore a product of positive letter images. There are exactly \(m^d\) reachable states.

**3. Accepting set and minimality.** The acceptance condition is equivalent to
\[
F=\{(r,c)\in G:c_{ij}=0\ \text{and}\ r_i r_j=0\text{ for all }i<j\},
\]
because the reverse pair count is \(r_i r_j-c_{ij}\). From group state \(g\), reading a suffix with group image \(t\) is accepting exactly when \(gt\in F\). Thus two states \(g,g'\) have identical right languages exactly when \(hF=F\) for \(h=g'g^{-1}\). We show this **left setwise stabilizer** is trivial.

If \(hF=F\), since the identity is in \(F\), we have \(h\in F\). Write \(h=(b,0)\), where \(b_i b_j=0\) for all \(i<j\). Each single-letter group element \(g_j\) also belongs to \(F\), so \(h g_q\in F\). For each \(i<q\), the \((i,q)\) pair coordinate of \(h g_q\) is \(b_i\), and therefore \(b_i=0\). Finally \(h g_1\in F\) has letter counts \(1\) in coordinate \(1\) and \(b_q\) in coordinate \(q\); the product condition forces \(b_q=0\). Hence \(h=e\), so all reachable states are pairwise Myhill–Nerode distinguishable. This proves the exact minimum and the assertion about the full transition group.

**4. Residue-periodic limiting density.** At length \(n\), every group image satisfies \(\sum_i r_i\equiv n\pmod m\). Write \(G_s\) for this fiber. It has \(m^{d-1}\) elements. The right random walk obtained by choosing an input letter uniformly is irreducible, since the positive generators reach all of \(G\), and is doubly stochastic since each letter acts by a permutation. Every return walk has length divisible by \(m\), while the word consisting of \(m\) copies of any single letter returns to the identity. Therefore the Markov chain has period exactly \(m\). The standard finite irreducible periodic Markov-chain theorem gives convergence, exponentially fast along each residue class, to the uniform distribution on \(G_s\). The accepting points of \(G_s\) have \(c=0\) and precisely \(E_s(m,q)\) possible letter vectors. This proves the general formula.

When \(m=p\) is prime, \(r_i r_j=0\) for all unequal indices means at most one letter coordinate is nonzero. For \(s=0\), only the all-zero vector has total sum \(s\); for \(s\ne0\), exactly the \(q\) vectors having one coordinate equal to \(s\) qualify. This yields the displayed two-valued prime-modulus density. \(\square\)

## Verification
The included standalone `check_group.py` constructs the complete reachable counting automaton and independently minimizes it by output-partition refinement for \((q,m)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,2),(3,3),(4,2)\), agreeing with \(m^{q(q+1)/2}\). It separately enumerates all small words and directly counts every distinct-letter scattered pair to verify acceptance against the automaton for word lengths up to eight. `check_stabilizer.py` explicitly multiplies every pair of group elements and checks that the left stabilizer of \(F\) consists only of the identity for the same parameter cases. `check_asymptotics.py` independently propagates counts of length-\(n\) words for representative prime-modulus cases and prints their approach to the predicted residue-class limits through length forty. The recorded outputs accompany the scripts. These finite checks validate implementations and boundaries; they do not replace the all-parameter argument in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Fazekas, Huang, and Mercaş, *Subsequence Analysis Problems for Binary Parikh Matrices* (2026), investigate arithmetic language fibers specified by exact letter and scattered-pair counts, with universality and fixed-pattern containment questions. The present language instead imposes simultaneous **modular** zero constraints on every directed mixed pair and determines its exact deterministic state complexity and long-length density. The inspected full text, including theorem statements, contains neither conclusion.

Lejeune, Rigo, and Rosenfeld, *On the binomial equivalence classes of finite words* (2020), identify two-binomial equivalence with the positive part of a free nilpotent class-two group. Their structural group-theoretic description is directly relevant background, not claimed here. Passing to a finite modular quotient explains why the counting coordinates form a finite group but does not by itself show that a Boolean language testing all mixed-pair residues has the **full group** as its syntactic transition group. That requires the separate stabilizer proof above. The earlier work studies exact binomial classes and their growth, rather than the specific modular language or its periodic acceptance probabilities.

Other published calculations concerning a single scattered pattern such as \(0^d1\), \(010\), or \(0011\) give different single-pattern output or acceptance complexities. They do not imply the simultaneous all-ordered-mixed-pairs assertion: the reverse-orientation constraints change the final set, and the alphabet-general left-stabilizer proof is essential.

## Limitations
The classification excludes same-letter pairs \(ii\), higher-order patterns, and nonuniform or dependent input distributions. For composite \(m\), the limiting density is given by the exact finite arithmetic count \(E_s(m,q)\), not by a closed prime-style two-level expression. The theorem makes no claim about the minimum number of states of a nondeterministic automaton, communication complexity, or an unbounded-modulus limit. Literature overlap cannot be ruled out absolutely, especially for unindexed treatments of finite nilpotent-group languages; the conclusion is originality relative to the explicitly inspected sources.
