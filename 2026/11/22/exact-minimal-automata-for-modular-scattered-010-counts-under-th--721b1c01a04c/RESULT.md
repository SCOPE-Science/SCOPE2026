# Exact minimal automata for modular scattered \(010\) counts under the Fibonacci constraint

## Finding

For a finite binary word \(w\), let \(\binom{w}{010}\) be the number of strictly increasing triples of positions spelling `010`, counted with multiplicity. For every integer \(m\ge 2\), define
\[
\mathcal L_m=\{w\in\{0,1\}^{*}:\text{`11' is not a contiguous factor of }w,\quad\binom{w}{010}\equiv0\pmod m\}.
\]
The minimal **complete deterministic finite automaton** recognizing \(\mathcal L_m\) has exactly
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{sc}(\mathcal L_m)=\frac{2m^3}{\gcd(m,2)}+1.}
\]
Thus its size is \(2m^3+1\) for odd moduli and \(m^3+1\) for even moduli. The formula applies to composite as well as prime moduli, includes the rejecting sink, and counts distinct Boolean right languages rather than only distinct modular outputs.

The proof establishes a *safe simulation* property: every transformation of the three modular subsequence counters that is obtainable by an unrestricted word is also obtainable by a word beginning in `0` and containing no `11`. Consequently, forbidding adjacent ones preserves the entire unconstrained modular counter orbit, yet doubles its distinguishable legal boundary states.

## Assumptions and scope

The input alphabet is exactly \(\{0,1\}\). The empty word is legal and is accepted. `11` is excluded as a **contiguous** factor, whereas `010` is counted as a **scattered** subsequence, with index selections counted separately even when they produce identical patterns. The modulus is an arbitrary integer \(m\ge2\). A *complete* DFA has a transition on either letter from every state, including a nonaccepting sink reached after reading `11`. Distinguishability means the usual Myhill–Nerode equivalence of accepted continuations.

Let \(a,b,c\) denote, modulo \(m\), the number of zeros, the number of scattered `01` subsequences and the number of scattered `010` subsequences, respectively. Let \(\delta=\gcd(m,2)\). Define the residue set
\[
\mathcal R_m=\{(a,b,c)\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^3:c\equiv(a-1)b\pmod\delta\}.
\]
Congruence modulo \(\delta=1\) is vacuous. Its cardinality is \(|\mathcal R_m|=m^3/\delta\).

## Proof

**1. Modular counter dynamics.** Appending a zero creates a new zero and extends every previous `01` subsequence into a `010`; appending a one extends every previous zero into `01`. Hence the exact residue transitions are
\[
T_0(a,b,c)=(a+1,b,c+b),\qquad T_1(a,b,c)=(a,b+a,c).
\]
All components are modulo \(m\). Both are permutations of the finite residue cube, with
\[
T_0^m=T_1^m=\operatorname{id}.
\]
The run constraint is captured by a last-symbol flag \(t\in\{0,1\}\), with an attempted `1` from flag \(1\) sent to the sink. The empty prefix can use flag \(0\): the empty prefix and a genuine trailing-zero prefix carrying the zero counter triple have identical future behavior.

**2. The exact residue orbit.** Write \(A\) for the integer number of zeros of a word. For each one, let \(k_j\) be the number of zeros preceding that occurrence. Directly counting triples with the one in the middle gives
\[
\binom{w}{01}=\sum_j k_j,\qquad
\binom{w}{010}=A\sum_j k_j-\sum_j k_j^2.
\]
Since every \(k_j^2-k_j\) is even, every reachable counter triple belongs to \(\mathcal R_m\).

Conversely, the subgroup of \((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^2\) generated by \((1,1)\) and \((2,4)\) is precisely
\[
\mathcal H_m=\{(B,Q):Q\equiv B\pmod\delta\}.
\]
Indeed \((2,4)-2(1,1)=(0,2)\), so arbitrary first coordinate and an arbitrary even adjustment of the second are possible; all coefficients may be represented by nonnegative integers modulo \(m\).

Fix \((a,b,c)\in\mathcal R_m\). Put \(Q=ab-c\) modulo \(m\). Then \((b,Q)\in\mathcal H_m\). Choose nonnegative numbers of one-markers whose preceding-zero counts are congruent to \(1\) or \(2\) modulo \(m\), so their sum and sum of squares are \(b\) and \(Q\). Realize their counts as strictly increasing positive integers \(k_j\) by adding large multiples of \(m\). Choose a total number of zeros \(A\) larger than every \(k_j\), with \(A\equiv a\pmod m\), and append the remaining zeros. This constructs an `11`-avoiding word **ending in zero** with the prescribed triple. Empty marker collections are allowed, and padding can always make the total positive.

To obtain the same triple **ending in one**, instead reserve the last marker at \(k=A\equiv a\pmod m\). Subtract its contributions \((a,a^2)\) from the required \((B,Q)\). Because \(a^2-a\) is even, the remainder lies in \(\mathcal H_m\). Realize its markers at strictly increasing positive counts below a sufficiently large \(A\equiv a\pmod m\), and place one final `1` after the \(A\)-th zero. The resulting word has no adjacent ones and has the desired triple. This proves that both last-symbol classes have exactly \(|\mathcal R_m|\) reachable states.

**3. Safe simulation of arbitrary counter transformations.** For any binary word \(v\), construct \(\sigma_m(v)\) by replacing each `1` with the block \(0^m1\), leaving each `0` unchanged, and prepend \(0^m\). The resulting word begins in `0` and avoids `11`. Since \(T_0^m\) is the identity, its action on **every** counter triple is identical to that of \(v\). This works even when \(v\) contains adjacent ones. In particular, the finite group generated by \(T_0,T_1\) can be simulated by suffixes legal after **either** last-symbol flag.

Since both letter maps are permutations of a finite set, for every reachable triple \(x\) there exists an unrestricted transformation taking \(x\) back to \((0,0,0)\): reverse the transformations of a word reaching \(x\), using positive powers to express inverses. Applying \(\sigma_m\) to such a word gives a legal suffix beginning with `0` and taking \(x\) to the base triple.

**4. Distinguishing all legal states as Boolean right languages.** Consider two reachable non-sink states with different counter triples \(x\ne y\), regardless of their last-symbol flags. By the preceding step, append a legal suffix \(g\) beginning with `0` that takes \(x\) to \((0,0,0)\). The same permutation takes \(y\) to some \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\ne(0,0,0)\).

If \(\gamma\ne0\), suffix \(g\) already distinguishes acceptance. If \(\gamma=0\) and \(\beta\ne0\), append one more zero: the first counter triple still has last coordinate zero, but the second now has last coordinate \(\beta\ne0\). If \(\gamma=\beta=0\) and \(\alpha\ne0\), append \(0^m10\) after \(g\). This suffix contributes zero to the third coordinate of the base triple but contributes \(\alpha\ne0\) to that of the other state. Every distinguishing suffix used here avoids adjacent ones and is valid from either original last-symbol flag.

Now consider two legal states with the **same** triple but opposite last-symbol flags. From the trailing-zero state append `1`, which is legal, and then a zero-starting suffix taking the resulting counter triple back to the base. This extension is accepted. From the trailing-one state the first `1` immediately reaches the rejecting sink. Thus the two flags are distinct. Every legal state is distinguishable from the rejecting sink because it has an accepted continuation sending its triple to the base. Finally, the empty-prefix state is equivalent to the zero-flag state with base counters and creates no extra class.

We have explicitly constructed \(2|\mathcal R_m|+1=2m^3/\delta+1\) reachable pairwise distinguishable states, completing both the upper and lower bounds. \(\square\)

## Verification

`checker.py` independently builds the complete residue automaton for each modulus from \(2\) through \(14\), enumerates the reachable states, verifies their exact parity-restricted description, and minimizes each automaton by iterative right-congruence refinement. It obtains the predicted exact state counts for all thirteen moduli, totalling 15,789 checked reachable states. It independently enumerates all 32,767 binary words of lengths at most 14 and directly counts scattered `010` triples by position selection, producing 262,136 comparisons of acceptance with modular automaton transitions across eight moduli. Every check passes. Run `python3 checker.py`; no external dependency is required. The finite tests are not a proof for arbitrary \(m\); the four-step argument above is.

## Relationship to prior work

Kovačević and Vukobratović (arXiv:2105.04617, first public 2021-05-10) analyze constrained binary sequences and their asymptotic capacities, motivating the run-limited input model. Their all-length characterization concerns weight and run counts, not the minimal-state complexity of modular scattered `010` recognition. Černý (2010) develops the algebraic machinery of scattered-subword counts; it supplies a broader foundational framework rather than the present exact restricted Boolean quotient. Colbourn, Dougherty, Lidbetter and Shallit (arXiv:1804.11175) consider comparisons of contiguous-factor occurrence counts, not modular scattered-triple recognition under `11` avoidance.

The published *Exact output-state complexity for modular alternating scattered triples* (2026-11-08, identifier `f8d46c1509db`, https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/tree/main/2026/11/8/SCOPE-exact-output-state-complexity-for-modular-alternating-scattered--f8d46c1509db) proves that the **unrestricted** residue-valued Moore machine for `010` has \(m^3/\gcd(m,2)\) states. Its inspected theorem explicitly does not claim minimality for individual Boolean residue languages. The present statement is neither a restriction of its Moore output theorem nor a direct count of its states: it establishes an exact Boolean Myhill–Nerode quotient under a forbidden-factor constraint, including two independently necessary legal boundary classes and a sink. The new zero-padding simulation and zero-target distinguishing suffixes establish those missing implications.

Alazemi and Černý's *Counting Subwords Using a Trie Automaton* (2011) discusses a general subword-counting construction. Its accessible abstract and bibliographic material did not reveal a corresponding restricted-language minimality theorem, but the full text was not accessible for a definitive comparison. This is a documented residual priority risk.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to scattered pattern `010`, alphabet \(\{0,1\}\), the forbidden adjacent-one factor `11`, and target residue zero. By letter complementation an equivalent formula holds for scattered `101` with the forbidden factor `00`; that equivalent is **not** a separate finding. No assertion is made for every other target residue, longer alternating patterns, arbitrary run caps, or state complexity under other automaton models. The research does not claim independent validation. Historical priority beyond the inspected material cannot be guaranteed.
