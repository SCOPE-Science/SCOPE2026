# Scientific review: minimal hybrid observations for pulse and sampler kinetics

## Correctness — PASS
The model is a specified linear initial-value problem, not a fitted environmental model. The passive pulse response is derived directly by variation of constants, including the coincident-rate limit. For calibrated gain, the two identities \(P_2/P_1=a+b\) and \(P_1/(k\Delta C_1)=L(b/a)\) invert uniquely because the logarithmic-mean function is strictly increasing on all positive inputs; \(L'(1)=1/2\) proves regularity at coincidence. For uncalibrated gain, two active observations identify the ambient exponential; subtracting its survival factor from the passive ratio gives the sampler factor. A literal rate-swap transformation preserves one active observation and the entire passive trace if gain is free, establishing the method-specific lower bound. With two active samples but one passive sample, loss and gain remain continuously confounded. The generic three-versus-four measurement bounds are thus proved, not inferred from numerical tests. The supplemental `verify.py` passed 128,008 standard-library assertions including coincident/near-coincident positive rates, inversion, swap and quadratic sensitivity.

Risks and domain: proof assumes exact, contemporaneous, non-destructive measurements from a nondepleting first-order linear model and positive interior parameters. It provides neither noisy statistical confidence intervals nor identification under field-realistic variable shedding and sampler saturation. The generic necessity statement allows special-parameter exceptions.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with acknowledged residual historical risk
The passive-only rate-swap ambiguity is well known in first-order absorption/elimination kinetics; no novelty is assigned to that identity. General input-output identifiability of linear compartment models, first-order passive uptake kinetics, and the use of paired detection modalities are likewise known. The claimed distinction is more specific: **finite exact scalar measurement budgets, explicit inversion for separately known and unknown uptake gain, and a regular collided-rate formula in the natural active/passive sampling semantics**. In the materials inspected, none explicitly implied these two exact minimal experimental protocols or their combined calibration boundary. Searches were conducted on the model and its aliases, including two-exponential Bateman, flip-flop identifiability, logarithmic-mean inversion and active/passive eDNA. Relevant primary full-text sections were read where accessible. The strong general theory raises a genuine possibility that equivalent specialized formulas occur elsewhere; the review does not assert universal historical priority.

This **covers the kinetic forward law and a broader passive-response motivation**, but not an exact minimal fixed-count active/passive scalar sampling theorem in the inspected material. This is the closest primary prior.

Originality is a bounded evidence-based judgement, not independent literature certification. ## Value — PASS
A central limitation of passive eDNA quantification is the unknown relationship between captured material and ambient concentration; this model isolates exactly what separate uptake calibration buys in measurement count. It also states how many truly active versus accumulated readings are essential when that gain is unknown, rather than reporting only a generic count of unknown parameters. The coincident-rate branch prevents a misleading apparent singularity in naive divided-exponential reconstruction. These are motivated, complete and quantitatively constructive identification boundaries in a minimal environmental-monitoring model. The result deliberately makes no empirical calibration claim or forecast of field performance.
