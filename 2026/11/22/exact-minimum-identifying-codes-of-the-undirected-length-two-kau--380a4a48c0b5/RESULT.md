# Exact minimum identifying codes of the undirected length-two Kautz graphs on three through six symbols

## Finding
Let \(U_q\) be the undirected length-two Kautz graph on the \(q(q-1)\) ordered pairs \((a,b)\) of different symbols from \(\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\). A radius-one identifying code is a subset \(C\) such that the sets \(N[v]\cap C\) are nonempty and pairwise different over all vertices \(v\), with \(N[v]\) denoting the closed neighborhood. For \(q=3,4,5,6\), the exact minimum code sizes are respectively \(4,4,5,7\), and the numbers of distinct labeled minimum codes are respectively \(9,168,24,4320\). Under simultaneous alphabet relabeling by the symmetric group \(S_q\), these minima form respectively \(3,10,1,6\) orbits. The accompanying machine-readable table gives a representative and exact orbit size for **every** orbit. The first two nontrivial interior cases admit a code of size \(q\), but the six-symbol graph requires \(q+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set is \(V_q=\{(a,b):0\le a,b<q,\ a\ne b\}\). Distinct vertices \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\) are adjacent if and only if \(b=c\) or \(d=a\). All edges are undirected, with no loops, and \(N[v]\) includes \(v\) itself. The code is labeled: permutations of alphabet symbols generally produce different codebooks. The orbit action applies the *same* symbol permutation to both coordinates, and no assertion is made about the full automorphism group. The finding concerns **only** the four specified alphabet sizes; it does not claim a formula for larger alphabets.

## Proof
For each \(q\) in \(\{3,4,5,6\}\), the finite algorithm in `verification/enumerate_codes.cpp` enumerates all \(\binom{q(q-1)}k\) subsets of exactly \(k\) vertices without repetition, using a two-branch inclusion/exclusion recursion with a remaining-cardinality counter. Each vertex is encoded as a bit position. The program constructs each closed-neighborhood mask directly from the displayed adjacency predicate, intersects it with the chosen-code mask, and accepts the subset exactly when each of the \(q(q-1)\) resulting signatures is nonzero and all signatures are different. This is precisely the definition of identification, not a relaxation or sampled test.

For \(q=3\), the tested sizes \(k=2,3,4\) yield respectively \(0,0,9\) accepted codebooks; for \(q=4\), sizes \(3,4\) yield \(0,168\); for \(q=5\), sizes \(4,5\) yield \(0,24\); for \(q=6\), sizes \(5,6,7\) yield \(0,0,4320\). An identifying set remains identifying when additional vertices are added, since old distinct signatures remain distinct after restriction to the old selected vertices and old nonempty signatures remain nonempty. Thus absence at a tested size excludes **all smaller sizes**. The first nonzero layer in each case is therefore the global minimum, and its exhaustive enumeration is the complete labeled count.

For the orbit statement, `verification/orbit_representatives.json` contains exactly \(3,10,1,6\) proposed representatives in the four cases. The independent set-based verifier `verification/orbit_check.py` verifies each representative against the graph definition, generates its images under **every** permutation in \(S_q\), confirms the declared orbit cardinality, verifies all images are identifying codes, and proves distinct representative orbits disjoint. Their union has respectively \(9,168,24,4320\) elements, equal to the already exhaustively established counts of all optimal codebooks. Consequently every optimum lies in precisely one displayed orbit, and the orbit classification is complete.

## Verification
From the directory containing `verification/`, run `c++ -O3 -std=c++17 verification/enumerate_codes.cpp -o codes && ./codes`, `python verification/orbit_check.py`, and `python verification/crosscheck.py` (the last requires SciPy and NumPy). Actual runs matched the exact counts above. The independent MILP checker represents each nonempty-signature condition as a neighborhood hitting constraint and each distinct-signature condition as a symmetric-difference hitting constraint; it returned integral optimal sizes \(4,4,5,7\). Its purpose is crosschecking, not replacing the exhaustive proof. The orbit JSON permits replay without depending on any other dataset.

## Relationship to prior work
The undirected Kautz graph is established in Fan, Xu, and Lü's work on *restricted edge connectivity*. In their graph notation the present \(U_q\) is the length-two graph with parameter \(q-1\). Their restricted-edge-connectivity results concern edge cuts, not the minimum identifying code or orbit counts. Horan studies identifying codes of **undirected de Bruijn graphs**, where repeated-letter vertices are allowed, a different graph family; those results do not determine these minima. Foucaud and collaborators analyze identifying codes of **line graphs**, but the present graphs have three mutually nonadjacent forward neighbors when \(q\ge4\) (a claw) and hence are not line graphs. Published results on **directed** Kautz identifying codes have a different neighborhood definition and cannot be transferred as a counting identity. This is a finite classification of a different graph, not a claimed solution to a general Kautz identification problem.

## Limitations
Only alphabet sizes three through six are settled. Enumeration is exhaustive for those finite graphs, not a proof of a formula beyond them. All orbit statements concern simultaneous alphabet relabeling, not arbitrary graph isomorphisms. Historical priority is not independently guaranteed: an older classification under alternative terminology or in an unindexed table could exist. Computational reproducibility and an independently formulated checker do not constitute external mathematical validation.
