# Scientific assessment: exact mixed-prime residue images of a Klarner affine orbit

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem's quantifiers cover every \(b\geq1\) and every \(u\geq1\) coprime to \(6\). The key infinite steps are: permutation-group generation of translations modulo \(u\); lifting full coverage of odd \(N\) to \(3N\); the parity-based final-letter decomposition of odd orbit elements; and exact solution of the resulting congruences using full odd-modulus coverage. The disjointness and cardinality follow from the exact \(3\)-adic valuation of \(x+1\). Compatible images give the ternary closure and Haar measure by an absolutely convergent geometric series. An executable standard-library verifier agrees with exact orbit closure on 70 modular cases but is not used to infer the universal conclusion.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations

Shamazov and Talambutsa's 2025 full preprint treats the same free affine semigroup family and orbit growth, not exact images of the orbit modulo \(2\cdot3^bu\). Klarner and Rado's 1974 foundational work is much broader on recursively defined sets but does not state this all-depth mixed-prime image census in the relevant inspected results. A known earlier theorem for exactly this triple determines **which moduli are surjectively attained** and identifies the obstruction at \(6\); it does not determine the precise omitted classes, exact counts, or inverse-limit Haar measure, so the present statement is not a corollary of the earlier yes/no theorem alone. Search coverage cannot rule out an unindexed or implicit equivalent derivation, particularly in the general literature on profinite affine semigroups.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The motivating density and congruence-surjectivity questions for Klarner's concrete affine orbit make the mixed-prime local image a canonical, nonarbitrary invariant. In the first regime where a simultaneous \(2\)- and \(3\)-power obstruction occurs, the theorem gives a **complete** image classification at every ternary depth and every coprime odd cofactor, identifies a stable limiting Haar mass of \(3/4\), and explains its structure through forced terminal words. This is more information than a single forbidden residue, one small-modulus computation, or a restatement of the pre-existing full/non-full criterion. It does not address ordinary natural density or higher dyadic valuations.

## Literature and limitations

Sources inspected: Shamazov–Talambutsa, *On orbit sets generated by semigroups of one-dimensional affine functions* (arXiv:2507.06875v1, 9 July 2025), especially Sections 1, 3, and 4; Klarner–Rado, *Arithmetic properties of certain recursively defined sets* (Pacific Journal of Mathematics 53 (1974), 445–463), relevant arithmetic-congruence results. Residual originality risk: an uncatalogued general affine-action argument may imply the same residue classification. Residual scope limitation: moduli with \(4\mid m\) are not classified here.
