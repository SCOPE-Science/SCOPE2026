# Exact modulo-four prime-factor fibers and a prime-counting law for Dombos partitions

## Finding
Let \(d(N)\) count partitions of \(N\) into positive parts either divisible by \(4\) or congruent to \(1\) or \(5\pmod6\), with unrestricted repetition. For \(n\ge0\), write \(m=8n+5\). Let \(\chi_{-24}\) denote the primitive real quadratic Dirichlet character modulo \(24\), with \(\chi_{-24}(t)=0\) if \(\gcd(t,6)>1\), equal to \(1\) for \(t\equiv1,5,7,11\pmod{24}\), and equal to \(-1\) for \(t\equiv13,17,19,23\pmod{24}\).

Then, for all \(n\ge0\),
\[
d(6n+4)\equiv \sum_{t\mid m}\chi_{-24}(t)\pmod4.
\]
This residue equals \(2\pmod4\) precisely when
\[
m=3^a p^{4h+1}s^2,\qquad a,h\ge0,\quad s\ge1,\quad\gcd(s,6p)=1,
\]
where \(p\) is prime, \(p\equiv5\pmod{24}\) if \(a\) is even, and \(p\equiv7\pmod{24}\) if \(a\) is odd. In every other case it is \(0\pmod4\). In particular, the exceptional indices obey the asymptotic law
\[
\#\{0\le n\le X:d(6n+4)\equiv2\pmod4\}\sim\frac{\zeta(2)X}{\log X}\quad(X\to\infty).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All variables are ordinary integers unless explicitly marked real. The asymptotic is for real \(X\to\infty\), with the residue class \(6n+4\) fixed. The character is the Kronecker character \(\chi_{-24}(t)=\left(\frac{-24}{t}\right)\) for positive integers \(t\); its values on odd integers are determined by the residue classes given above. No statement is made about coefficients outside this progression or about higher powers of two.

## Proof
**Step 1 (published dissection).** The full-text proof of da Silva–Sellers, Section 4, formula (4.2) and its next display, establishes the following coefficientwise congruence in the formal power-series ring \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)[[q]]\):
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}d(6n+4)q^n\equiv 2\sum_{j,k\ge0}q^{j(j+1)+3k(k+1)/2}\pmod4.
\]
Multiplying the exponent equation by \(8\) and adding \(5\), the equation \(j(j+1)+3k(k+1)/2=n\) becomes \(m=2x^2+3y^2\), where \(x=2j+1\) and \(y=2k+1\) are positive odd integers. If \(R(m)\) is the number of such positive pairs, then \(d(6n+4)\equiv2R(m)\pmod4\).

**Step 2 (ideal-count conversion).** The two reduced positive-definite primitive forms of discriminant \(-24\) are \(x^2+6y^2\) and \(2x^2+3y^2\). They are the two proper classes, corresponding to the principal and nonprincipal classes of the maximal order \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-6}]\); its class number is two. Indeed, the Minkowski bound is \((2/\pi)\sqrt{24}<4\). Its nonprincipal ideal classes are represented by the ramified ideals of norm \(2\) and \(3\), which are equivalent since their product is generated by \(\sqrt{-6}\). Neither is principal, as neither \(2\) nor \(3\) is a norm \(x^2+6y^2\). The usual ideal–form correspondence therefore gives, for every positive integer \(m\),
\[
r_{x^2+6y^2}(m)+r_{2x^2+3y^2}(m)
=2\,a(m),\quad a(m)=\#\{\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-6}]:N\mathfrak a=m\}.
\]
Representations here use all \((x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2\), including zero, with each ordered sign choice counted. The Euler factors of the Dedekind zeta function of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6})\) yield
\[
a(m)=\sum_{t\mid m}\chi_{-24}(t).
\]
For \(m\equiv5\pmod8\), the first form has no representations: squares modulo \(8\) are \(0,1,4\), so \(x^2+6y^2\not\equiv5\pmod8\). Every representation of \(m\) by \(2x^2+3y^2\) has both coordinates odd and nonzero, again by modulo-\(8\) inspection; thus its integer representation count is exactly \(4R(m)\). Consequently \(4R(m)=2a(m)\), so \(d(6n+4)\equiv a(m)\pmod4\) as required. In particular \(a(m)\) is even.

**Step 3 (prime-factor classification).** Factor the odd integer \(m=3^a\prod_{p\ne3}p^{e_p}\). The ideal-count factors are \(1\) at the ramified prime \(3\); \(e_p+1\) at a split prime (\(\chi_{-24}(p)=1\)); and \(1\) if \(e_p\) is even, or \(0\) if odd, at an inert prime. Hence \(a(m)\equiv2\pmod4\) precisely when all inert exponents are even, exactly one split exponent is \(1\pmod4\), and all other split exponents are even. This is exactly \(m=3^a p^{4h+1}s^2\), with \(p\) split and \(\gcd(s,6p)=1\). Because \(m\equiv5\pmod8\), the distinguished prime satisfies \(p\equiv5\pmod8\) if \(a\) is even and \(p\equiv7\pmod8\) if \(a\) is odd. Intersecting with the split classes \(1,5,7,11\pmod{24}\) gives exactly \(5\) and \(7\pmod{24}\), respectively. Every other case gives \(a(m)\equiv0\pmod4\).

**Step 4 (sharp prime-counting asymptotic).** Set \(Y=8X+5\). Each exceptional \(m\le Y\) has a unique triple \((a,p,s)\) and an exponent \(4h+1\). First count the \(h=0\) contribution. Removing the coprimality condition \(p\nmid s\) changes this count by at most
\[
O\!\left(\sqrt Y\sum_{a\ge0}3^{-a/2}\sum_{p\ge5}p^{-3/2}\right)=O(\sqrt Y),
\]
by writing \(s=pt\). With \(r_a=5\) or \(7\pmod{24}\) depending on the parity of \(a\), the main count is
\[
\sum_{a\ge0}\sum_{\substack{s\ge1\\(s,6)=1}}\pi\!\left(\frac{Y}{3^as^2};24,r_a\right).
\]
For fixed \(a,s\), the prime-number theorem in the reduced arithmetic progressions \(5,7\pmod{24}\) gives \(\pi(Y/(3^as^2);24,r_a)\sim Y/(8\,3^as^2\log Y)\). This limit may be summed: restrict first to \(3^as^2\le Y^{1/2}\), where the standard uniform upper bound \(\pi(t;24,r)\ll t/\log t\) provides a summable majorant after division by \(Y/\log Y\). The complementary range contributes \(o(Y/\log Y)\), since \(\sum_{3^as^2>Y^{1/2}}(3^as^2)^{-1}=O(Y^{-1/4}\log Y)\), and elementary counting bounds handle bounded prime arguments. Consequently the main count is
\[
\frac{Y}{8\log Y}\sum_{a\ge0}3^{-a}\sum_{(s,6)=1}s^{-2}
\sim\frac{Y}{8\log Y}\cdot\frac32\cdot\frac{2\zeta(2)}3
=\frac{Y\zeta(2)}{8\log Y}.
\]
For every \(h\ge1\), \(p^{4h+1}\ge p^5\); the number of possibilities is \(O(\sqrt Y\log Y)\): sum \((Y/(3^as^2))^{1/5}\) over \(a\ge0\), \(s\le\sqrt Y\), then allow at most \(O(\log Y)\) choices of \(h\). This is negligible relative to \(Y/\log Y\). Substituting \(Y=8X+5\) proves the stated constant \(\zeta(2)\).

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python checker `check.py` independently computes restricted-partition coefficients modulo \(4\), the quadratic-character divisor sum, the prime-factor criterion, and positive quadratic-form representation counts for a finite range. These are reproducibility checks, not substitutes for the cited dissection, the ideal–form identity, or the prime-number theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Da Silva and Sellers derive the coefficientwise theta-series congruence quoted above and use nonrepresentation at specified prime factors to obtain progressional congruences. The present claim uses that congruence as an explicit premise; it adds a complete global modulo-four prime-factor fiber and its precise asymptotic counting constant. A related published study of the **different** \(16\)-regular partition function uses a prime-times-square criterion but does not imply this result for \(d(N)\).

## Limitations
The proof depends on the published dissection identity and standard quadratic-ideal counting and prime-number-theorem inputs. The residue classification does not determine the full integer coefficients or their valuations beyond modulo \(4\). The priority comparison cannot exclude an unindexed equivalent result. Independent validation has not been performed.
