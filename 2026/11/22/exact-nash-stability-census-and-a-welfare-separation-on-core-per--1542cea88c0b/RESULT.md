# Exact Nash-stability census and a welfare separation on core–periphery friendship graphs

## Finding
Consider the symmetric aversion-to-enemies hedonic game on the **complete split friendship graph** \(G_{r,s}=K_r\vee\overline{K_s}\), where \(r,s\) are arbitrary positive integers. Every core agent is friends with every other agent; peripheral agents are pairwise enemies. Let \(N=r+s\). Symmetric valuations are \(+1\) for friends and \(-N\) for enemies, and a player's utility is the sum of the valuations of members of her coalition other than herself. Nash welfare is the geometric mean of all nonnegative coalition utilities, taken over individually rational partitions. A Nash-stable partition permits no strictly profitable move by one agent to an existing coalition or to a singleton.

**Theorem (complete classification).** For every \(r,s\ge1\):

1. **Every Nash-stable partition** comprises all \(r\) core agents together with exactly one peripheral agent in a single coalition, and the other \(s-1\) peripheral agents as singletons. Conversely, each such partition is Nash stable. Thus there are **exactly \(s\)** Nash-stable partitions on the labeled graph.
2. The maximum Nash welfare is
   \[
   \operatorname{NW}^{\star}(r,s)=
   \begin{cases}
     0,&r<s,\\
     (r-s+1)^{(r-s+2)/(r+s)},&r\ge s.
   \end{cases}
   \]
   If \(r>s\), every maximizing partition has one coalition containing \(r-s+1\) core agents and one peripheral agent, together with \(s-1\) core–peripheral pairs. There are exactly \(s!\binom{r}{s-1}\) labeled maximizers. If \(r=s\), all maximizers are perfect core–peripheral pairings, in exactly \(r!\) ways. No optimizer count is asserted when \(r<s\).
3. For every \(r\ge s\ge2\), the maximum Nash welfare is strictly positive, **yet all Nash-stable partitions have welfare zero**. Consequently no Nash-stable partition attains any finite multiplicative approximation ratio to the optimum on this infinite family. The core–periphery separation persists with arbitrarily large populations and all-positive optimum welfare.

## Assumptions and scope
The network has \(N=r+s\) individually labeled players, divided into \(r\) universal core players and \(s\) peripheral players without mutual friendship ties. All coalition partitions are unordered collections of nonempty disjoint blocks covering the population. The empty singleton alternative is always available to a deviator and has utility zero. A Nash deviation is strictly beneficial to its mover regardless of whether it harms a destination member; there is no coalition admission veto. Utility zero is allowed in individually rational partitions. If a coalition contains an enemy of any of its members, that member has utility at most \((N-2)-N=-2\); hence every individually rational coalition is a clique in the friendship graph. For this graph each clique contains at most one peripheral player, with any number of core players.

All strict stability claims use the ordinary single-player Nash notion, not contractual Nash stability. The welfare classification concerns global optimization over individually rational partitions; zero-optimum cases may contain multiple zero-product optimizers and are intentionally not counted.

## Proof
**1. Positive welfare and its feasibility boundary.** A positive product requires every player to have positive utility. Each peripheral player must therefore share a coalition with at least one core player. Since distinct peripherals cannot share an individually rational coalition, there must be at least \(s\) different core agents, proving necessity of \(r\ge s\). When \(r\ge s\), assign one distinct core agent to each peripheral and distribute any remaining core agents among those coalitions. All players then have positive utility, proving sufficiency.

**2. Structure of a positive optimum.** If a positive-product partition contains a coalition made entirely of core players, it cannot be a singleton (which would give zero utility). Merge that core-only coalition with any peripheral-containing coalition. Their union remains a clique because every core player is friendly to everybody; all members of the merged coalition strictly increase their utility from their former coalition sizes minus one to their new coalition size minus one. Hence the Nash product strictly increases, contradicting optimality. Thus all \(r\) core agents are distributed among exactly \(s\) peripheral-containing coalitions. Write \(k_i\ge1\) for the number of core agents with peripheral \(i\), with \(\sum_{i=1}^s k_i=r\). This coalition contains \(k_i+1\) agents, each with utility \(k_i\). The Nash product is
\[
P(k_1,\dots,k_s)=\prod_{i=1}^s k_i^{k_i+1}.
\]
For real \(x\ge1\), \(f(x)=(x+1)\log x\) is strictly convex because \(f''(x)=(x-1)/x^2\ge0\), with strictly positive curvature beyond one. If two integer coordinates are greater than one, transferring one core agent from the smaller coordinate to the larger strictly increases \(\sum f(k_i)\), by increasing differences of \(f\). Repeating this transfer forces all but one coordinate to equal one. Thus the maximum occurs exactly at a permutation of \((r-s+1,1,\ldots,1)\) when \(r>s\), or at all ones when \(r=s\). Its Nash product is \((r-s+1)^{r-s+2}\); taking the \((r+s)\)-th root establishes the welfare formula. For \(r>s\), choose the peripheral belonging to the large coalition in \(s\) ways, choose the \(s-1\) core agents in the pairs in \(\binom{r}{s-1}\) ways, and biject those chosen cores to the remaining peripherals in \((s-1)!\) ways. This yields \(s!\binom{r}{s-1}\). For \(r=s\), the only positive-product configuration consists of \(s\) disjoint mixed pairs, counted by \(s!\).

**3. Exact Nash-stability structure.** In any Nash-stable partition, no agent can have negative utility, because forming a singleton gives zero. Thus every coalition is a friendship clique. Suppose core agents occupy two distinct coalitions of sizes \(p\le q\). A core agent from the smaller coalition receives utility \(p-1\) but would receive \(q\) by joining the larger, a strict improvement. This contradicts Nash stability. Consequently all core agents belong to one coalition; all remaining blocks are peripheral singletons. The core coalition can contain at most one peripheral agent by the clique property. If it contains none, an unassigned peripheral agent would gain \(r>0\) by joining; hence it contains exactly one.

Conversely, in such a partition each core player receives \(r\) utility. Moving to an outside peripheral singleton yields at most one, and forming a singleton yields zero; neither is strictly profitable. The peripheral inside the core coalition receives \(r\) and cannot improve by becoming a singleton or joining a peripheral singleton. Each outside peripheral receives zero; joining the core coalition creates an enemy and gives utility \(r-N=-s<0\), while joining another outside peripheral gives \(-N\). No Nash deviation exists. The selected peripheral has \(s\) possible identities and completely determines the partition, proving the census. For \(s\ge2\), every Nash-stable partition contains at least one singleton and therefore has Nash product zero, completing the separation in the positive-optimum regime.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library program `check_split.py` independently enumerates all set partitions for the finite labeled ranges \(1\le r\le5\), \(1\le s\le4\), \(r+s\le9\). Using the literal \(+1/-N\) payoff rule, it checks the Nash-product optimum, the number of optimizers when positive, the Nash-stable partition count, and the exact stability structure. A successful run prints `ALL ENUMERATED INSTANCES PASS`. Enumeration is finite supporting evidence, **not** the all-size proof; the latter is given in the preceding three steps.

For example, \((r,s)=(4,2)\) gives \(N=6\), maximum product \(3^4=81\), maximum welfare \(81^{1/6}\) and eight optimal partitions, but exactly two Nash-stable partitions, each with zero welfare. For \((r,s)=(4,4)\), the maximum product is one with \(4!=24\) optimal mixed-pair partitions, and again every Nash-stable partition has zero welfare.

## Relationship to prior work
Pagano and Schlenga (2026) introduce the Nash-welfare objective for additively separable hedonic games, establish the clique characterization of individually rational aversion-to-enemies partitions, and show that positive-Nash-welfare solutions are contractually Nash stable, but not necessarily Nash or individually stable. Their Section 7, Remark 1 gives a four-player counterexample with two mixed pairs versus a deviating three-person coalition and a singleton. **Neither the clique characterization nor the existence of a four-person Nash-stability conflict is new here.** The result here is the exact all-size core–periphery optimum, its optimizer census and the complete Nash-stability census together with the persistence of zero welfare at every Nash equilibrium.

The already published exact Nash-welfare theorems for complete three-role and four-role friendship graphs cover low-core specializations of this split family: in particular, our \(r=2\) and \(r=3\) Nash-welfare formulas are consequences of those results and are not claimed as independent discoveries. Those records do not classify Nash stability or establish the infinite universal-core family at unbounded \(r\). The published matching-edge-perturbed bipartite **enemy** graph formula treats a different hostility network and proves a product/census result, not this complete Nash-stability classification. The published complete-split binary anti-coordination classification is a different game with different actions and utility functions, so does not imply the coalition theorem above.

## Limitations
All statements assume the exact symmetric unit-friend/large-enemy additive payoff rule, an unbounded number of coalitions, a complete core and totally disconnected periphery, individual rationality for welfare, and unrestricted one-player Nash deviations for stability. They do not determine stability when players veto entrants, nor general friendship graphs with missing core–periphery edges, or arbitrary weighted valuations. Special low-core welfare cases and the four-player instability example are known; priority for the general census remains subject to independent examination, and other unindexed literature may exist. Finite enumeration is not treated as infinite proof. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
