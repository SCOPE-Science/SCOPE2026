# Scientific review: adjacent Catalan valuation drift and covariance

## Correctness
**PASS.** The theorem applies to all odd primes and all positive prime-power block depths. Its ratio identity, two exact root cylinders, carry expectation, finite summation, and prime-three coalescence are independently reconstructed and cover all boundary cases, including the successor beyond the final block index. An independent Legendre-factorial checker passed 33 parameter cases with 108,479 indices and 505 actual integer Catalan pairs. Only the displayed equations are asserted as infinite results; exhaustive checking was not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS on examined evidence, with priority risk.** The complete three-adic marginal distribution in an earlier publication does not determine correlations with a successor; similarly a prior adjacent *central-binomial* joint law yields a coincident jump marginal for primes at least five but not the Catalan regression covariance. A more general joint valuation framework for simple-root polynomials covers the ratio's two linear valuations, including the prime-three drift split, but not their covariance with a central-binomial carry count. Sobolewski (2023) gives valuation regularity, not this explicit covariance. Searches and full-text examinations found no theorem implying the claimed regression formula. It remains possible that unindexed digital-function literature contains an equivalent computation; that risk is not treated as proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The current-index valuation versus successor-drift covariance is an intrinsic, natural two-point statistic for the classical Catalan sequence, quantifying whether divisibility tends to rise or fall after a high-valuation term. The all-odd-prime finite-depth evaluation proves a sharp universal negative-regression law, a prime-three exception to the regression formula, and two different limiting regimes. This is distinct from a routine independent marginal histogram; the additional carry conditioning is necessary.

## Limits and closest literature
The theorem does not give the full joint law of consecutive Catalan valuations, says nothing about the prime two, and does not claim historical uniqueness. The elementary drift marginals themselves are acknowledged as covered by linear-root data, and the central-binomial drift marginal is also explicitly acknowledged as covered. Principal comparisons: Sobolewski (2023), Barat–Grabner (2001), OEIS A067397, the published three-adic Catalan marginal law (2026), and the pre-existing adjacent central-binomial and ternary binomial–Catalan valuation results.
