# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS (self-review).** For nonzero iterates the ordering of residual magnitudes depends only on the sign: the number of cached observations whose sign agrees with the iterate is binomial. Conditioned on that count the reused sampled update is an iid signed average; computing its conditional second moment gives the stated exact formula, and the re-query update consists of independent centered Rademacher observations. An odd batch maintains nonzero iterates. The all-row sign flip is exactly one binomial lower-tail event, and its exponential rate follows from bounding the binomial sum by its final summand and a polynomial multiple of it, then applying Stirling. The floor follows from bounded convergence and the binomial law of large numbers. The included `checker.py` reproduces 867 finite exact/approximate assertions; no finite enumeration is invoked as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified (self-review).** The underlying averaging and quantile screening algorithms and the qualitative possibility of selection-induced bias and fresh-query recovery are known; no novelty is claimed for these. In the inspected main theorems, algorithms, and numerical sections of the closest sources, the exact Rademacher risk as a function of the separate screening and update-batch sizes, the \(q=1/2\) limiting floor transition, and the exponentially persistent *all-row sign* chain are not stated. The 2025 quantile-subsampling study concerns fixed corruption and finite-horizon QRK, the 2022 averaging paper concerns fixed corrupted equations, and the 2026 AQRK study provides general error bounds and qualitative noise-bias comparisons, not this binomial law. This is a statement-level comparison, not a proof of global historical novelty. Unindexed or unpublished equivalent scalar calculations remain a risk.

**Value — PASS (self-review).** This exact testbed isolates a practical algorithmic distinction: additional averaging of *selected cached* measurements cannot by itself remove their conditional selection bias, while new independent measurements of the very same labels do. The nonzero limiting risk for any fixed fraction strictly below all rows, and an exponential sign-dwell time despite equal stationary sign weights, both supply motivated sharp stress tests beyond a generic warning about biased selection. These are neither a universal guarantee nor a proposed superior solver; the structured testbed provides reproducible boundary evidence for two-stage robust sampling designs.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Cai–Chen–Ma–Wu (arXiv:2507.15185v1), Cheng–Jarman–Needell–Rebrova (arXiv:2206.12554), Coria–Haddock–Pacheco (arXiv:2403.19874), and Shvaiko–Rebrova (arXiv:2611.21926v1; published after the eligibility anchor) are the closest inspected numerical-analysis literature. Conditions of generic convergence theorems may exclude identical rows or persistent cached-selection bias. Other special cases and more general selection lemmas may exist outside indexed results. Nothing here establishes behavior for high-dimensional matrices, nonunit relaxation, fixed errors, or typical data.
