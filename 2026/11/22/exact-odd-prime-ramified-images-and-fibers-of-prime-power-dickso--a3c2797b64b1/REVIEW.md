# Scientific review: ramified prime-power Dickson images

## Correctness — PASS
The claim specifies the complete domain \(p\ge5\), \(r,k\ge1\), normalization \(D_0=2,D_1=X\), and all input residues. The polynomial congruence \(D_p\equiv X^p\pmod p\), derivative reduction \(D_p'/p\equiv(X^2-4)^{(p-1)/2}\pmod p\), and the exact derivative values at \(\pm2\) give one-step and two-step branch contractions. An integral-polynomial perturbation of a unit affine map is a bijective \(p\)-adic isometry, so all asserted balls, fibers, counts and least periods follow for arbitrary iteration. Direct exact computations for four primes and multiple powers and iterations agree, but finite checking is not substituted for proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
The closest explicit primary source is Bremser–Gomez-Calderon (1991). Its Theorem 5 gives noncritical fibers, while Lemmas 9–10 and Theorem 11 leave only critical image bounds. Therefore the novel component is full critical branch surjectivity at exact depth \(2r+1\), uniform at every iteration, plus complete all-residue fibers, collision counts and least periods. Gassert (2012) treats finite-field Chebyshev functional graphs; Panraksa–Tangboonduangjit (2026) treats periodic points, not all-input values. Existing nearby classifications for the prime two and the Frobenius-minus-linear polynomial do not imply the present odd-prime Dickson theorem. A general older implicit identity not located remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Closing the exceptional-critical branch gap in a directly relevant 1991 paper turns value-set bounds into a complete classification for every prime-power degree \(p^r\) at the ramified prime \(p\ge5\). Exact local isometries yield independent applications: integer-image criteria at all precisions, exact all-output fiber multiplicities, sharp least additive periods, Haar measure and collision enumerators. This is not a renamed noncritical special case; both exceptional branches are essential.

## Limitations and sources
The theorem does not cover \(p=2,3\), arbitrary Dickson parameters or mixed degrees. Historical equivalence risk has not been independently eliminated.
