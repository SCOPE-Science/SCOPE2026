# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness

**PASS.** The plaquette product on each periodic coordinate plane is exactly one. For odd area, the product condition forces a total absolute angular displacement of at least \(\pi\) from \(-1\); a strict pairwise-averaging argument shows that an equal displacement of \(\pi/A\) is the unique energy minimizer on that plane, up to its global sign. An explicit periodic seam-gauge link construction realizes all three plane bounds together. The cube identity and periodicity force all optimal transverse sign choices to be constant, and flat connections modulo gauge leave three holonomies. The cubic energy expansion follows from Taylor's theorem. Code exercises 5,892 plaquettes on different rectangular tori and all branch choices; the proof, not the finite tests, covers arbitrary permitted sizes.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual risk.** Bonati–Vicari (arXiv:2611.03837v1, 2026-11-02) explicitly construct the fully frustrated even-periodic background, but their relevant sections give neither an odd-torus minimum nor an exhaustive rectangular parity and branch classification. Standard periodic \(U(1)\) flux quantization and periodic seam gauges are known and not claimed as new. Li–Meurice's negative-coupling work is for non-Abelian \(SU(2)\)/\(SU(3)\), not this problem. Targeted scholarly web and indexed-result searches did not identify an equivalent full theorem; this does not rule out unpublished or unindexed implicit coverage. The negative-coupling core is derived independently here rather than extrapolated from source snippets.

## Value

**PASS.** The exact minimum is a motivated finite-volume boundary of a contemporary negative-coupling lattice-gauge model. For the same model the even-periodic full-frustration ansatz is known, but odd systems have an unavoidable gauge-invariant obstruction, an explicit \(L^{-1}\) rather than extensive excess, and discrete branch multiplicity. The result determines the finite-size baseline for both even and odd lattices and distinguishes gauge-only constraints from the matter-coupled theory. It is not an arbitrary finite table or a routine computation of known coefficients.
