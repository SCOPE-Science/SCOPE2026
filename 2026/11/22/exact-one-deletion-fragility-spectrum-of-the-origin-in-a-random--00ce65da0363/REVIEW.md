# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

With probability one, circular direction spacings form a uniform Dirichlet vector and the open-semicircle criterion is exact. Direct deletion coalesces precisely two adjacent spacings. A pivotal event therefore corresponds to a two-spacing sum exceeding one half while all individual spacings remain below one half. Disjoint two-spacing sums cannot both exceed one half; consequently at most two pivotal events occur. A marginal beta integral gives each individual pivotal probability, and an explicit residual-simplex integral gives the probability of each adjacent simultaneous pivotal pair. No triple events exist, so the first two factorial moments specify the entire law. The independent Wendel one-time identity yields conditional robustness. A standalone exhaustive-grid and rational-identity checker supports the finite algebra; it is not used to establish the continuous integral identities.

## Originality — PASS with specified historical-priority risk

The closest inspected primary text is Wendel (1962), whose exact theorem gives only the origin-containment probability and not the multideletion fragility count. The online full text of Fodor, Montenegro Pinzón and Vígh (2022/2023) studies spindle-convex absorption probabilities and not deletion of individual ordinary convex-hull vertices. A 2015 arXiv research article of Kabluchko, Vysotsky and Zaporozhets proves random-walk absorption identities and recovers Wendel but does not, in its available abstract-level material, resolve the stated exact pivotal spectrum; no whole-document noncoverage claim is made about that paper. Published research-record searches for pivotality, Wendel equivalences and origin deletion did not reveal an exact distribution. No negative-search assertion of uniqueness is made. Historical semicircle-coverage extensions may still imply the result and require human verification.

## Value — PASS

Single-point failure of center containment is a natural robustness question in random directional sampling, depth and randomized feasibility. The work gives a complete finite-size fragility classification (including the sharp deterministic limit of two individually pivotal vertices) and an exact conditional one-deletion survival probability, rather than merely recalculating Wendel's marginal probability. It is a closed structural answer to a specific natural stability question; no broader robustness or higher-dimensional conclusion is claimed.
