# Exact one-error sphere spectrum of Fibonacci-constrained swap-and-replace words

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal F_n\) be the binary words of length \(n\ge 1\) with no adjacent occurrence of `11`. A *permitted one-error output* of \(w\in\mathcal F_n\) is a **different** word in \(\mathcal F_n\) formed either by replacing exactly one bit or by swapping exactly two consecutive unequal bits. Positions are linear, not cyclic. Write \(d(w)\) for the number of distinct permitted outputs, and \(B_1(w)\) for those outputs together with \(w\) itself.

Define \(t(w)\) to be the number of (possibly overlapping) contiguous occurrences of `101` in \(w\). Then for every \(n\ge1\) and every \(w\in\mathcal F_n\),

\[
\boxed{d(w)=n-t(w),\qquad |B_1(w)|=n+1-t(w).}
\]

More precisely, for every integer \(c\ge0\), the number \(N_{n,c}\) of allowed words with \(d(w)=n-c\) is

\[
\boxed{N_{n,c}=\mathbf1_{\{c=0\}}+\sum_{k=c+1}^{n}\binom{k-1}{c}\binom{n-2k+2}{k-c},}
\]

where a binomial coefficient with a negative top argument or an out-of-range bottom argument is defined to be zero. This is also a complete, composition-refined spectrum: the summand with given \(k\) counts exactly the words with \(k\) ones.

The rational bivariate enumerator, including the empty word at length zero, is

\[
\boxed{H(z,u)=\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{w\in\mathcal F_n}z^n u^{t(w)}
=\frac1{1-z}+\frac{z}{(1-z)[1-z-uz^2+(u-1)z^3]}.}
\]

The attained values of \(d\) at length \(n\) are *every* integer between \(\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil\) and \(n\), inclusive. For \(n\ge3\), the minimum-degree words are exactly the unique alternating word beginning and ending in `1` when \(n\) is odd, and the two alternating words when \(n\) is even. At \(n=1\) the two words both have degree one, and at \(n=2\) the three allowed words all have degree two. The number of words of maximum degree \(n\) has the ordinary generating function

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}N_{n,0}z^n=\frac{1+z+z^2}{1-z-z^3}.
\]

For uniformly chosen \(w\in\mathcal F_n\), the mean and variance of the one-error output count satisfy

\[
\mathbb E[d(w)]=\frac{2}{\sqrt5}\,n+O(1),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(d(w))=\left(\frac{46\sqrt5}{25}-4\right)n+O(1).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is exactly \(\{0,1\}\). The only source-and-target restriction is absence of adjacent `11`. A replacement changes one coordinate; a swap exchanges two adjacent unequal coordinates. Both operations have unit cost, and the ball is measured **inside** the constrained language, rather than in the full binary cube. A repeated output, if it could be generated by different operations, is counted only once; in fact, the permitted one-step operations here have distinct outputs because replacements change one position and unequal-bit swaps change two, and distinct swap positions change different position pairs. All results apply to finite *linear* words, with no wraparound edge.

## Proof

**Local degree identity.** Fix an admissible word with \(k\) ones. Let \(e\) be the number of ends occupied by `1` (both ends counted separately when \(n\ge2\); for \(n=1\), the unique position is an end but does not create any adjacent pairs). For \(n\ge2\), the number of adjacent `01` or `10` pairs is \(b=2k-e\). Let \(a\) be the number of distinct zeros adjacent to a `1`. An interior zero can be adjacent to two ones precisely when it is the center of `101`. Hence \(a=b-t(w)\). Exactly \(k\) replacements from one to zero and \(n-k-a\) replacements from zero to one are permitted, a total of \(n-a\). Of the \(b\) potential unequal-adjacent-pair swaps, precisely the two incident swaps around each `101` create `11`; these pairs are disjoint as potential swap positions even if patterns `101` overlap. The number of permitted swaps is therefore \(b-2t(w)\). Consequently

\[
d(w)=(n-a)+(b-2t(w))=n-t(w).
\]

The same identity follows directly for \(n=1\), when there is one legal replacement and no swap.

**Exact spectrum.** Suppose \(w\) contains \(k\ge1\) ones. It has \(k-1\) interior zero gaps, all of positive length, and arbitrary nonnegative leading/trailing zero lengths. Exactly \(c=t(w)\) interior gaps are length one. Choose these gaps in \(\binom{k-1}{c}\) ways. The other \(k-1-c\) gaps have base length two. After placing the \(k\) ones and minimum required interior zeros, there remain \(n-3k+2+c\) zeros, distributed among the two exterior gaps and the \(k-1-c\) longer interior gaps. Stars and bars gives \(\binom{n-2k+2}{k-c}\) allocations. The all-zero word adds the indicator term for \(c=0\), proving the binomial sum for every \(n\ge1\).

**Generating function.** Sum the same decomposition over lengths: each exterior gap has series \((1-z)^{-1}\), a one has weight \(z\), and each gap followed by a new one has weight \(z(uz+z^2/(1-z))\). The geometric series over one counts gives

\[
H(z,u)=\frac1{1-z}+\frac{z}{(1-z)^2}\sum_{j\ge0}\left[uz^2+\frac{z^3}{1-z}\right]^j,
\]

which simplifies to the stated rational form. In particular, setting \(u=1\) gives \(H(z,1)=(1+z)/(1-z-z^2)\), the standard enumeration \(|\mathcal F_n|=F_{n+2}\) with \(F_0=0,F_1=1\).

**Sharp endpoints and no gaps.** Two starts of `101` must be at least two positions apart, so \(0\le t(w)\le\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\). For every such integer \(c\), the word \((10)^c1\,0^{n-2c-1}\) is admissible and has exactly \(c\) starts, proving the contiguous degree support. Equality at the upper endpoint uses exactly the possible alternating three-letter starts; the gap decomposition (or endpoint count in the binomial formula) shows that for odd \(n\ge3\) this is attained only by the alternating word starting and ending with `1`, and for even \(n\ge4\) by the two alternating words. The \(n=1,2\) cases are direct. The maximal-degree words are exactly those avoiding both `11` and `101`, equivalently those with at least two zeros between successive ones. Substitution \(u=0\) in \(H\) gives the displayed generating function.

**Mean and variance.** Let \(D(z,u)=1-z-uz^2+(u-1)z^3\). Near \(u=1\), its unique strictly smallest-modulus zero is an analytic simple root \(\rho(u)\), with \(\rho(1)=(\sqrt5-1)/2\). By partial fractions, uniformly for real/complex \(u\) close to one, \([z^n]H(z,u)=C(u)\rho(u)^{-n}(1+O(\eta^n))\), with analytic nonzero \(C\) and \(\eta<1\). The cumulant generating function for \(t(w)\) is consequently \(n\log[\rho(1)/\rho(e^s)]+O(1)\). Implicit differentiation of \(D(\rho(u),u)=0\) gives its first two derivatives at zero as \(1-2/\sqrt5\) and \(46\sqrt5/25-4\), respectively. Since \(d=n-t\), its mean equals \(n\) minus the first cumulant, and its variance equals the second cumulant. This proves the two limits, without relying on finite experimental fitting.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses standard Python only. It directly lists all admissible words through length 20, enumerates and deduplicates every legal one-replacement and one-swap output, and tests the local identity. Independently it uses a two-symbol-prefix transfer recurrence to count `101` patterns, compares that recurrence against the closed binomial formula through length 100, checks exact support and extremizer counts, and checks that half the summed vertex degrees equals the previously published total edge count at every length through 100. Run `python verify.py` from the directory containing this document and the script. The finite checks support the algebra; they are not substituted for its all-length proof.

For example, at length ten the degree histogram (degree : number of vertices) is `6:2, 7:8, 8:24, 9:50, 10:60`, totaling 144 Fibonacci-constrained words.

## Relationship to prior work

Anselmo, Castiglione, Flores, Giammarresi, Madonia and Mantaci (2023) define the swap-and-replacement tilde-Fibonacci cube and determine its **total** number of edges, but do not give its vertex-by-vertex degree law or its complete one-error ball distribution. The 2025 open-access overview adds distance, radius and diameter information but likewise does not state this degree law or histogram. The original Fibonacci-cube degree polynomial of Klavžar, Mollard and Petkovšek (2011) refers to replacement-only adjacency and is not the same graph. As a consistency check, the first moment of our degree spectrum recovers the published tilde-edge formula

\[
|E_n|=\frac{(n+1)F_{n+3}+(n-2)F_{n+1}}5.
\]

The 2022 coding study of Gabrys, Guruswami, Ribeiro and Wu demonstrates independent motivation for substitution/transposition channels, but addresses code constructions involving deletions rather than the exact constrained one-error sphere of the present theorem.

## Limitations

These are constrained **linear** words and a channel with at most one replacement or adjacent unequal-bit swap; no cyclic boundary swaps, insertions, deletions, two-edit errors, or outputs violating the source constraint are included. The exact sphere distribution is not by itself a new packing bound, a code construction, or a statement about optimal code size. The analytical asymptotics are for the uniform distribution on the finite Fibonacci language. Literature priority cannot be established solely by negative search results; an equivalent unindexed or differently formulated degree distribution may exist.
