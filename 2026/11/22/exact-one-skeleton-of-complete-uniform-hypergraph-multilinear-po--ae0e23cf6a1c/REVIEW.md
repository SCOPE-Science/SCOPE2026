# Self-review

**Correctness (PASS).** The proof uses a midpoint extremality criterion in a finite polytope. For every high support the r-monomials force all high-side differing coordinates to share one Bernoulli variable, and mixed monomials force all opposite-side variables to be its complement. Thus only the two endpoints can represent the midpoint. When both supports are small, the zero-monomial face is precisely the rank-\((r-1)\) uniform-matroid independence polytope, whose active inequalities classify every small-small edge. The counting identities then follow without experimental extrapolation. The exact-integer test program independently checks 22 finite parameter instances, and all formulas include boundary parameters.

**Originality (PASS, with priority risk).** Full text of Del Pia–Xu (2026), especially its general adjacency results, Appendix A midpoint criterion, and diameter statements, was read and compared. The source studies the broader general hypergraph framework, but does not state the complete uniform exact-degree-\(r\) graph or enumerator. Del Pia–Khajavirad (2015/2017) treats full rank-\(r\) hypergraphs that include all intermediate degrees, which is not the same polytope. Padberg's graph case covers \(r=2\), not the full higher-degree statement. Searches of mathematical records and online literature did not identify stronger coverage; this is evidence against known overlap rather than proof of unique priority. The principal residual risk is older literature using different names for complete uniform multilinear lifts.

**Value (PASS).** The new theorem replaces a general at-most-\(n\) diameter guarantee by a sharp diameter-two formula for a natural dense high-degree family of binary-polynomial optimization polytopes. It provides every pair's adjacency, vertex-degree distribution, and complete edge count for all dimensions; the sharp structural distinction between the monomial-positive universal clique and the matroid face supplies a reusable explanation, not just a small finite enumeration. It concerns the actual polytope rather than its easier standard relaxation.

**Limitations.** Neither sparse hypergraphs, mixed-degree lifts, nor the standard relaxation are classified. Existing quadratic results are credited. Exhaustive computer testing at small sizes is supplementary, and priority remains provisional without external audit.
