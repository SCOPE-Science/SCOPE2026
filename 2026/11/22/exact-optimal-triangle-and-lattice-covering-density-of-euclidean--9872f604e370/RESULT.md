# Exact optimal triangle and lattice covering density of Euclidean stadiums

## Finding
Let \(a>0\), \(r>0\), and let the Euclidean stadium be the planar convex body
\[
K_{a,r}=([-a,a]\times\{0\})+rB_2,
\]
where \(B_2\) is the closed unit disk. Set \(t=a/r\), and define
\[
c(t)=\frac{2}{1+2t+\sqrt{(1+2t)^2+8}},\qquad s(t)=\sqrt{1-c(t)^2}.
\]
The greatest area of any Euclidean triangle contained in \(K_{a,r}\) is exactly
\[
\boxed{M_3(a,r)=r^2s(t)\bigl(2t+1+c(t)\bigr).}
\]
For \(a>0\), **exactly two geometric triangles** maximize area. They are the reflection pair whose respective vertices are
\[
(-a-rc,rs),\quad(-a-rc,-rs),\quad(a+r,0)
\]
and their reflections across the vertical axis (here \(c=c(t)\), \(s=s(t)\)). No orientation or vertex-position restrictions are imposed on candidate triangles.

Let \(\Delta(K)\) denote the largest determinant of a planar lattice \(\Lambda\) whose translates \(K+\Lambda\) cover all of \(\mathbb R^2\). By the classical Bambah–Rogers planar covering-triangle theorem, independently recalled in Kallus [1],
\[
\boxed{\Delta(K_{a,r})=2M_3(a,r).}
\]
Consequently the exact **minimum lattice-covering density** is
\[
\boxed{\vartheta_L(K_{a,r})
=\frac{|K_{a,r}|}{\Delta(K_{a,r})}
=\frac{\pi+4t}{2s(t)(2t+1+c(t))}.}
\]
A covering lattice achieving this bound is generated by
\[
(0,2rs),\qquad (2a+r+rc,rs).
\]
The optimal triangle-area fraction \(\eta(t)=M_3(a,r)/|K_{a,r}|\) is strictly increasing in \(t\ge0\), and the optimal lattice-covering density is strictly decreasing. Their sharp endpoints are
\[
\boxed{\frac{3\sqrt3}{4\pi}\le\eta(t)<\frac12,\qquad
1<\vartheta_L(K_{a,r})\le\frac{2\pi}{3\sqrt3}\quad(t>0),}
\]
where the left equality for \(\eta\) and the right equality for \(\vartheta_L\) belong only to the disk limit \(t=0\). Both other endpoint values are nonattained limits as \(t\to\infty\). In particular the covering density determines the stadium's aspect ratio \(a/r\) uniquely. The disk endpoint \(a=0\) is included in the area/density formulas by continuity, but has a continuous family of rotated maximizing equilateral triangles, rather than two maximizers.

## Assumptions and scope
Every triangle is an ordinary closed, nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, without restriction on its position or orientation. Its containment in the convex stadium is equivalent to containment of all three vertices. Lattices are full-rank translation lattices of \(\mathbb R^2\), with covering density \(|K|/\det\Lambda\); no rotations of separate copies are allowed. The maximum determinant is over covering lattices, and the infimum lattice density uses the same body in every translate. The stadium has area \(\pi r^2+4ar\). All assertions are exact for each fixed \(a>0,r>0\). The only external geometric theorem invoked is the established planar Bambah–Rogers equality for centrally symmetric convex disks; an explicit achieving lattice is nevertheless constructed here.

## Proof
**1. Reduce the unrestricted area optimization to two translated disks.** Write \(D_{-}=(-a,0)+rB_2\), \(D_{+}=(a,0)+rB_2\). Their convex hull is exactly \(K_{a,r}\). For any ordered triple of points in this hull, choose its orientation with nonnegative signed double area. With two vertices fixed, that signed double area is an affine functional of the third vertex. Maximizing it over the convex hull of two disks amounts to maximizing it on at least one of the two disks. Repeating for all three vertices produces an area-maximizing triangle with every vertex in \(D_-\cup D_+\). By the pigeonhole principle at least two vertices are in the same disk. If all three are in one disk their area is at most \(3\sqrt3 r^2/4\), the classical sharp disk bound.

**2. Sharp two-disk chord calculation.** Take two vertices in \(D_-\) and the third in \(D_+\). Let the line of the first two vertices be a distance \(d\in[0,r]\) from the left disk's center and let \(n\) be a unit normal to it. Its chord length is at most \(2\sqrt{r^2-d^2}\). The absolute perpendicular distance from a point of \(D_+\) to that line is at most
\[
2a|n_x|+r+d\le2a+r+d.
\]
Thus the triangle area is at most
\[
F(d)=\sqrt{r^2-d^2}\,(2a+r+d).
\]
For each \(d\in[0,r]\), equality is **achievable**: take a vertical chord of the left circular cap at \(x=-a-d\), with endpoints \((-a-d,\pm\sqrt{r^2-d^2})\), and apex \((a+r,0)\). The right-cap-base case follows by reflection. Therefore the global candidate maximum over different disks is \(\max_{0\le d\le r}F(d)\).

**3. Exact optimizer and rigidity.** Put \(c=d/r\), \(t=a/r\). The positive factor of \(F'(d)\) has the sign of
\[
r^2-2d^2-(2a+r)d,
\]
which decreases strictly from positive to negative on \([0,r]\). The unique maximizing normalized distance \(c\in(0,1/2)\) solves
\[
2c^2+(2t+1)c-1=0,
\]
and is precisely the displayed \(c(t)\). Hence \(F(rc)=M_3(a,r)\). For any \(a>0\), its value is strictly greater than \(M_3(0,r)=3\sqrt3r^2/4\), so the all-one-disk case cannot tie. Equality in the chord and distance bounds forces a full circular chord, a horizontal chord normal, the chord on the exterior side of one disk, and the opposite disk's unique outward axial endpoint. These conditions yield exactly the two displayed triangles among triples chosen on the generating disks.

To exclude additional maximizers with vertices on the stadium's straight sections, represent each arbitrary vertex of the hull as a convex combination of a point in each of the two disks. Signed triangle area is affine in each vertex separately and hence is a convex combination of at most eight signed areas of pure disk-vertex triples, all bounded by the global maximum. Equality forces **every** pure triple with positive combination weight to maximize area. If any vertex had a nontrivial disk-mixture, two such pure triples would differ in that vertex alone, contrary to the fact that the only two maximizing pure triangles have disjoint vertex sets when \(a>0\). Therefore a global maximum has no additional flat-edge or interior representatives, and the two displayed geometric triangles exhaust all possibilities.

**4. Explicit achieving covering lattice.** Let \(w=a+rc\), \(u=a+r\), \(h=rs\). The six vertices of the centrally symmetric hexagon
\[
H=\operatorname{conv}\{(\pm u,0),(\pm w,\pm h)\}
\]
are the vertices of the maximizing triangle and their negatives. Therefore \(H\subset K_{a,r}\). The vectors \((0,2h)\) and \((u+w,h)\) generate a lattice of determinant \(2h(u+w)=2M_3(a,r)\). This lattice tiles the plane by copies of \(H\). More explicitly, at a height \(0\le y\le h\) relative to the even rows, the half-width of the hexagon centered at height zero is \(u-(u-w)y/h\); the half-width of a neighboring one centered at height \(h\) is \(w+(u-w)y/h\). Their sum is \(u+w\), exactly the horizontal spacing of the two rows' centers. Translation by \((0,2h)\) repeats this no-gap, no-overlap pattern at every height. This is also the standard opposite-edge pairing of a centrally symmetric hexagon. Hence translates of \(K_{a,r}\), which contains \(H\), cover the plane at the claimed lattice determinant.

For optimality, the classical Bambah–Rogers theorem for centrally symmetric planar convex bodies [1, §1] states that their **greatest covering-lattice determinant equals twice the area of their largest inscribed triangle**. The hypothesis holds for \(K_{a,r}\), so no covering lattice has larger determinant. The construction gives the announced exact density.

**5. Sharp monotonicity and shape recovery.** Put \(m(t)=M_3(a,r)/r^2=\max_{0\le c\le1}\sqrt{1-c^2}(2t+1+c)\). Uniqueness of the maximizing \(c\) and differentiation of this smooth envelope give
\[
m'(t)=2\sqrt{1-c(t)^2}.
\]
With \(\eta(t)=m(t)/(\pi+4t)\), exact differentiation yields
\[
\boxed{\eta'(t)=\frac{2\sqrt{1-c(t)^2}\,[\pi-2-2c(t)]}{(\pi+4t)^2}>0.}
\]
Indeed \(0<c(t)\le1/2\) and \(\pi-2-2c(t)\ge\pi-3>0\). At \(t=0\), \(c=1/2\) and \(m=3\sqrt3/4\); as \(t\to\infty\), \(c\to0\) and \(m(t)/(\pi+4t)\to1/2\). The covering density is \(1/[2\eta(t)]\), hence decreases strictly from \(2\pi/(3\sqrt3)\) to \(1\). Strict monotonicity gives the unique inverse aspect ratio from either statistic.

## Verification
The standalone, dependency-free `checker.py` was run with Python 3 and printed `STADIUM_TRIANGLE_COVERING_CHECKS_OK cases=10 random_triangles=60000 hex_samples=9000`. It checks the quadratic optimum, witness triangle areas, exact circle normalization, cover-density end conditions, the envelope derivative, monotonicity, and 60,000 independent random contained triangles. Separately, 9,000 random points are checked for coverage by the displayed hexagon lattice. Those finite diagnostics are **not** an exhaustive proof: the all-parameter optimization and covering determinant are derived analytically in the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Kallus [1], first publicly posted on January 24, 2013, recalls the general Bambah–Rogers correspondence between a centrally symmetric planar convex body, its maximum-area inscribed triangles, and the maximal covering-lattice determinant. That general equivalence is **not** new. The 1982 extremal-polygon algorithms [2] concern arbitrary finite polygon vertex sets, not an exact analytic stadium formula.

The published result *Sharp rectangle capacity and perimeter extremizer of Euclidean stadiums* [3] computes the maximum-area **rectangle** in the same stadium family, including a different perimeter-normalized optimization. Its complete statement and proof do not determine the maximum-area triangle or this covering determinant. The published result *Exact maximal triangle area and equality configurations in every symmetric circular lens* [4] treats the **intersection** of two congruent disks, whereas the stadium is the convex hull of two separated disks. These are different objects, and neither formula implies the other. The disk fourth dual-deviation result [5] optimizes a radial fourth moment rather than ordinary area. No equivalent stadium covering-density profile or triangle equality classification was identified in the consulted records or targeted literature searches; priority beyond these comparisons is not certified.

## Limitations
The theorem addresses translative *lattice* coverings, not all nonlattice coverings, and centrally symmetric planar stadiums with a circular radius and straight central segment. It does not classify every optimal lattice up to equivalence, prove stability under perturbations, or give a closed-form inverse of its density function. The upper bound on covering determinant uses the classical Bambah–Rogers theorem; it is not an independent reproof of that general theorem. A full historical-priority search is impossible to guarantee, and independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
