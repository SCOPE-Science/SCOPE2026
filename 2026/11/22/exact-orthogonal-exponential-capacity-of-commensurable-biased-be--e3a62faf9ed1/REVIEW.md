# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact Fourier transform is a product of Bernoulli two-point factors, each with real zeros precisely for fair bits. Their 2-adic valuations lie in a set of size \(r\). The two-class residue splitting of the 2-adic integer lattice proves an upper bound of \(2^r\) by induction, and the explicitly constructed frequency cube gives exactly \(2^r\) orthogonal exponentials. The completeness corollary uses distinctness of subset sums at different valuation levels and a finite positive-translation support-growth argument. The exact rational checker enumerated the full candidate zero-frequency graphs for 816 finite cases; general correctness rests on the proof, not this enumeration. The commensurable scale is removed by rescaling the spatial and dual variables.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked evidence
The 2024 Cao–Deng–Li–Wu theorem treats qualitative spectrality for a broad Moran-type Bernoulli family, with an explicit basis on finite distinct-valuation subfamilies; those already known claims are not offered as new. The new comparison is to *incomplete orthogonal families* when the factors have repeated dyadic valuations and arbitrary biases. Their exact maximum size is not asserted by that source's theorem or its finite-basis lemma. Zhou (arXiv:2610.33987, first public 25 October 2026) characterizes convolution spectra under a subgroup hypothesis that does not hold for these finite integer-step measures. A public result on three irrational Bernoulli factors has different hypotheses and allows non-power-of-two maxima. Published examples and orthogonal-family results were searched by direct quantitative implication rather than title. Risk: a known elementary 2-adic packing lemma in earlier spectral-measure literature could imply the quantitative finite formula even if the searched sources do not state it verbatim.

## Value — PASS
For a nonspectral finite positive measure, the dimension of its full \(L^2\) space does not tell how many Fourier characters can be used in a *mutually orthogonal* system. The exact maximal size is therefore a natural invariant, not a restatement of the yes/no spectrality condition. It remains completely determined by the number of distinct balanced dyadic step scales, independent of odd step parts, other factors and bias values; this classifies an arbitrarily large family of overlapping atomic distributions, gives constructive optimal frequencies, and sharply quantifies lost orthogonal Fourier dimensions. It also identifies the discontinuity when a fair bias is perturbed. This is a complete arithmetic packing law, rather than a finite table or an arbitrary parameter substitution.
