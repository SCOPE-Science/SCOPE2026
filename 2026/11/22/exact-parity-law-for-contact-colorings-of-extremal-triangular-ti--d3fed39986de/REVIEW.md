The contact relation reduces exactly to the line graph of the multigraph with two parallel edges over every edge of the triangular torus graph: each subdivided triangular cell is associated with one side of one original lattice edge; contacts at old face centers are already accounted for by incidence at old vertices. The graph has 6mn cells, each with 21 contacts, and a 12-clique at each old vertex. For even mn, the connected six-regular Cayley graph of an even-order finite abelian group has a six-factor 1-factorization by Abdollahi's published theorem; doubling the six color classes gives twelve. For odd mn, the full set provides strict matching density above twelve. Every odd induced vertex subset has doubled-edge density at most thirteen: sizes 3 and 5 by simplicity; size 7 by the local neighbor hexagon; sizes 9 and 11 by a nonempty cut; and sizes at least 13 by degree. The crucial assumptions are simple six-regular triangular torus graphs and a six-cycle induced on each lattice vertex's neighborhood, valid at periods at least five. Both cited graph-coloring theorems apply as stated. The finite program is only a reproducibility supplement; the all-order proof is analytical and theorem-dependent.

The rigid twenty-one-neighbor monohedral tiling is a canonical extremal geometric object. Its closed-tile point-contact coloring is naturally a simultaneous separation constraint, and the complete parity law for all rectangular compactifications identifies exactly when the planar twelve-color optimum fails and exactly how much it fails.
