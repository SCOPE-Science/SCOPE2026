# Review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem applies to all prime powers and matrix sizes \(n\ge3\), including characteristic two. The upper bound follows from the radical filtration, the odd count from disjoint pairs of first-superdiagonal vectors, and the even count from a conditional uniformity dichotomy and a precise \(q^{-4}\) exceptional-event correction. Independent full matrix and reduced-coordinate enumerations agree with the formulas on eight tests. Enumeration alone does not prove the general theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit residual risk

The strongest accessible earlier full texts study coadjoint invariants for triangular nilradicals (Boyko–Patera–Popovych, 2007) or uniformly sampled single-matrix Jordan types (Fuchs–Kirillov, 2022). Neither implies the pushforward measure under a random bracket. Indexed published studies of \(4\times4\) bracket fibers and maximum nilpotency in ordinary matrix products do not assert the all-dimensional distribution in their accessible records. Their linked full texts could not be fully inspected; an unindexed broader theorem could subsume the result. Originality assessment is a substantive best-knowledge comparison, not a certification of global priority.

## Value — PASS

The highest possible Jordan-block size of a random Lie bracket is a natural extremal statistic for the complete triangular nilpotent Lie family. The exact even/odd ratio and the single long-step path correction identify a structural change in the bracket image distribution; the result is more than a routine filtration bound or small-field count.

## Scientific limitations

The result does not classify all Jordan types, group commutators, or iterated Lie brackets. The computations test prime fields and finitely many dimensions; extension fields are covered by the field-independent proof. Historical publication priority remains subject to specialist validation.
