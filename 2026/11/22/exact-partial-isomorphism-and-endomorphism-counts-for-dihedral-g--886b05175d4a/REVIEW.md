# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The subgroup-type partition is exhaustive and disjoint for odd-order kernels; counting subgroup multiplicities and their automorphisms produces the exact partial-isomorphism sum. Mapping the generating reflection and the odd-order kernel produces all endomorphisms. The separate parity treatment checks the coincidence of cyclic and reflection order-two subgroups, and the exceptional Klein-four automorphism count, for even dihedral orders. Standalone direct enumerations verify all dihedral widths 3 through 16 and the noncyclic kernel \(C_3\times C_3\). The mathematical proof covers all stated sizes.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority limitations.** Cameron's published full text states the general nonabelian inequality as a question and proves only the abelian equality, used here as a lemma. Relevant literature on dihedral automorphisms and on partial automorphisms of semigroups does not state the exact subgroup-isomorphism count or the new infinite-family inequality. Searches found no covering published computation. Full priority is limited by possible unindexed subgroup-count work; the algebraic automorphism and subgroup classifications themselves are established background.

**Value — PASS.** This is an explicit infinite-family affirmative result toward a named open finite-group counting question, not a renamed known theorem: its subgroup-type formula handles arbitrary odd abelian kernels, the even-dihedral correction exposes a nontrivial Klein-four exception, and the power-of-two family shows the ratio can grow without bound. It is a finite-group structural enumeration with reproducible independent small-instance checks, not a claim about all nonabelian groups.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Cameron (2025), DOI 10.1007/s00233-025-10514-5, is the primary comparison. Goberstein's partial semigroup automorphisms and a 2018 dihedral automorphism calculation provide background of different scope. The result does not resolve Cameron's question for all finite groups, and no external audit has been performed.
