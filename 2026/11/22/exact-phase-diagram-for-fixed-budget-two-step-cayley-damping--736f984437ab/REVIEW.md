# Scientific review of fixed-budget two-step Cayley damping

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem quantifies over every \(a>0\), \(b\geq0\), \(H>0\) and every split \(0\leq t\leq1\). The two-step matrix is a complex scalar multiplication because the oscillator is normal. The exact Cayley product depends on the split only through \(q=t(1-t)\). After rewriting the squared modulus as a monotone function of \(U(w)=w+(R+4\sigma^2)/w-4\sigma^2\), strict convexity gives a unique clipped optimizer, all phase boundaries, and the large-budget limit. The standalone symbolic tests pass, and the numerical comparisons are supplementary rather than substitutes for proof. Degenerate cases \(b=0\), zero-length steps, positive-step nonattainment, and equality boundaries are stated explicitly.

**Originality — PASS with residual historical risk.** Full-text HTML comparisons with the 2020 composite-method work of Zhang, Zhang, Xing and Masarati establish different last-stage equations and different optimization goals (accuracy, frequency-dependent dissipation and stability), not the exact fixed-budget minimization for two identical Cayley factors. The 2020/2021 work of Zhang, Zhang and Masarati instead optimizes equivalent multistep/single-step families. Layton, Pei, Qin and Trenchea analyze the variable-step DLN method, not this two-factor product. The older Dharmaraja–Wang–Strang article treats a trapezoidal–BDF split; its repository manuscript could not be fetched, so only its abstract was read. A coincident older formulation remains possible. Search coverage and mathematical distinction, not a failed search alone, support the assessment.

**Value — PASS.** The exact small-budget transition at \(b=a/\sqrt3\), the nontrivial intermediate equal-step window in a low-oscillation regime, and the asymptotic fixed-duration short substep form a complete, motivated numerical damping phase diagram. The result gives an exact way to tune a familiar two-solve implicit integrator in a single normal-mode setting and demonstrates the strict damping weakness of equal splitting for very large total durations. It does not establish solution accuracy, efficiency or full-spectrum optimality.

**Closest literature and limits.** See the references and explicit comparisons in `RESULT.md`. Most closely related literature studies general composite or variable-step implicit methods with different objectives. Residual risk is an undiscovered equivalent scalar optimization or other unindexed classical result; independent publication-priority examination has not occurred.
