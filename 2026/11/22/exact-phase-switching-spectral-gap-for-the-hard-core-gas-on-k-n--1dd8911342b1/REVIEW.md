# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The state space is the union of two binary hypercubes joined at the empty independent set. In the antisymmetric sector, the negative generator is the one-side Dirichlet restriction; in the symmetric sector, Cauchy interlacing bounds the first nonstationary eigenvalue below by the Dirichlet ground eigenvalue. The cofactor resolvent of the reversible independent-site hypercube yields a unique scalar root below \(1+\lambda\), so the *full*, not merely lumped, gap is determined. Strict descent follows from a positive Dirichlet eigenvector and a nonprincipal lifted trial function. The binomial inverse-moment expansion establishes the sharp prefactor and its first two corrections at fixed activity. These arguments include \(n=1\), all \(\lambda>0\), and the stated rate normalization. The rational cofactor test passed 108 cases; the full-state symmetric eigenvalue test passed 21 cases. These checks corroborate, but do not substitute for, the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to documented historical uncertainty
Den Hollander, Nardi and Taati (2017) explicitly treat complete bipartite hard-core dynamics and compute an effective-resistance and high-activity *hitting-time* asymptotic in Example 4.1; this is a closer precursor, but it does not report the finite-activity *spectral gap*, its strict size descent, or the growing-size expansion. Galvin and Tetali derive general slow-mixing bounds for hard-core dynamics on certain bipartite expanders, not an exact spectral equation, sharp prefactor, or strict finite-size descent on the balanced complete bipartite graph; its stated degree-size condition fails for large balanced bicliques. The classical Ehrenfest spectrum is background, not claimed as new. Searches of indexed research claims and relevant public primary literature found no equivalent statement. Bingham's 1991 Ehrenfest urn first-passage work remains a plausible partial precursor; only its abstract was accessible. This prevents a claim of certified worldwide novelty, but the inspected evidence does not dominate the complete hard-core-chain result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For a canonical finite two-phase hard-core system, the exact *full-chain* relaxation eigenvalue and strict size descent quantify metastability sharply rather than bounding it only exponentially. The explicit \(n\lambda/(1+\lambda)^n\) prefactor and two corrections are useful for finite-size scaling and for distinguishing the relaxation gap from an occupancy-count approximation. The result is restricted to the specified continuous-time, balanced, equal-activity model; no mixing-time cutoff or general-graph extension is claimed.
