# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact normalized quadratic zonal kernel is \((mt^2-1)/(m-1)\), and the centered permutation dot product is \((f-1)/(n-1)\). Direct substitution gives a central kernel \(f(f-2)/(n(n-2))\), whose explicit decomposition into three inequivalent Specht characters is proved from point and pair permutation modules and the exterior square of the standard representation. Character orthogonality yields the entire spectrum and multiplicities. The harmonic-domain nullity, design claim, and sharp positive conditioning follow without numerical extrapolation. Independent exact permutation-character checks for dimensions 4–8 and full Gram diagonalizations for dimensions 4–6 passed.

## Originality — PASS with residual historical risk
The motivating design/framelet papers provide general constructions, not an evaluated spectrum for normalized centered permutation matrices. A related published calculation gives an \(\ell^1/\ell^\infty\) endpoint central projection norm for the \((n-2,2)\) Specht block, which is a distinct operator and does not imply the three-sector sampling spectrum, quadratic nullity, or positive singular-value condition number without the new orbit-kernel calculation. Classical character identities are credited rather than claimed as discoveries. Searches for orbit aliases and a stronger containing theorem found no statement establishing this exact all-parameter conclusion, but incomplete indexing remains a real priority risk.

The Birkhoff orbit is a canonical doubly stochastic-polytope vertex configuration, and second-moment tightness raises the concrete question of recovery at the first nonlinear harmonic degree. This result answers the natural complete spectral question, quantifies a universal cubic-dimensional invisible quadratic space despite spherical \(2\)-design isotropy, and gives exact optimal inversion stability on the visible space. Neither mere design tightness nor a generic dimension bound gives these exact three levels or the sharp linear-in-parameter condition number.

## Limits
The proof is self-contained except for standard finite-group representation facts and the standard spherical addition theorem. It does not address higher degrees or arbitrary weights and cannot establish bibliographic uniqueness. Numerical checks are consistency tests, not infinite certificates.
