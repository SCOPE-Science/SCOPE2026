# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The model, vertex/edge labels, arrival order, and terminal observable are explicit. Conditional exchangeability of still-available edges gives the terminating transfer equation. Two directionally different exact-rational implementations on the full finite state graph agree on all five coefficients and normalization. An independent geometric argument proves the minimum six-dimer event has probability \(5(1/5)^6\); the sharp support follows from the independence number eight and the ten-spoke perfect matching. The finite computation proves the stated finite result, not any infinite-volume conclusion.

## Originality
PASS on examined evidence, subject to historical-priority risk. Penrose–Sudbury formalize graph dimer RSA and present Bethe-lattice, correlation, and general forward-equation results, but do not give these exact five dodecahedral terminal probabilities. Pippenger's earlier general paper is a plausible background source, but only its abstract was accessible. Indexed exact Petersen-graph dimer results use another graph, while prior work on the five inscribed cubes establishes the geometry but not the kinetic rational probabilities. The complete specific distribution and its cubic-compound race interpretation were not found in the examined literature or relevant indexed records. These search limits do not establish uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The dodecahedral graph is the smallest all-pentagon fullerene cage and a natural twenty-site cubic, non-bipartite finite substrate. The exact complete jam law quantifies the irreversible kinetic loss of perfect coverage (only about 7.39% even though perfect matchings exist), provides a reproducible finite-network adsorption benchmark, and connects a rare kinetic endpoint exactly to the five inscribed cubes. The precise natural invariant, rather than a renamed general recurrence, is the contribution.

## Risks and limits
The model is a one-shot uniform edge-clock idealization, not a chemistry-level model of carbon adsorption. A full historical comparison of Pippenger (1989) could not be completed from its accessible abstract; further unindexed dodecahedral adsorption tables may exist. Only self-review is claimed, not external confirmation.
