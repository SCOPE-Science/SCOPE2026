# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is an exact operator norm on real periodic BV modulo constants, with distributional variation including endpoint jumps. The normalized convolution and zero-mass Jordan-measure coupling prove that it suffices to optimize the \(L^1\) distance of two translates of the five-mode Dirichlet kernel. Every distance is realized by an arc indicator. The product \(-4\sin(\delta/2)\sin u(1+4\cos(\delta/2)\cos u)\) gives the exact piecewise integral, and the critical polynomial factors over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\). The global maximum, endpoint comparison, and unique arc separation are explicitly proved; the accompanying executable verifies the radical algebra and integrates the oscillatory kernel by partitioning at every zero.

No conclusion relies on a random finite sample or an unfinished enumeration. General real BV equality cases are deliberately not classified.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
A literature comparison covered the full material of the 2012 Candès–Fernandez-Granda low-pass super-resolution work (arXiv:1203.5871), Poon's 2014 discrete Fourier/gradient-sparsity work (arXiv:1407.5339), and three closely related published Fourier projection norm records. The former prove recovery from Fourier measurements and the latter compute finite-cycle Lipschitz, finite-group endpoint, or large-band Lebesgue norms. None of their inspected claims implies the exact fixed-cutoff **real BV** variation amplification or the interior maximizing arc angle. The kernel-distance reduction is acknowledged as a classical general mechanism; no ownership is claimed over it. Searches for aliases, equivalent norm formulations, tabulated constants and stronger coverage found no decisive precedent. An older unindexed special-case calculation remains a risk; search failure is not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is the natural sharp real-BV stability question for the first low-pass Fourier projection with two distinct nonzero harmonic pairs. The norm is appreciably larger than the antipodal gain, and its maximizer is at a unique nonantipodal separation, an informative geometric threshold for the variation of bandlimited approximations to jump data. It supplies a motivated exact finite-band benchmark without asserting a broader theorem.

## Scientific limitations
The result is for real, unweighted circular BV and the five-mode projector only. It neither solves the complex-scalar problem nor classifies all general BV equality measures or higher Fourier bandwidths. Historical independence from all pre-existing literature is not certified; independent external mathematical audit remains unperformed.
