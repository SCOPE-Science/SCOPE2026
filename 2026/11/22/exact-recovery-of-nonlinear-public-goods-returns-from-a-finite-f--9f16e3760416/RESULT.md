# Exact recovery of nonlinear public-goods returns from a finite fixation profile

## Finding
Fix a well-mixed population of \(N\) individuals playing an \(n\)-person public-goods game with \(2\le n<N\). The group return after exactly \(j\) contributions is \(r_j>0\), for \(j=1,\ldots,n\). Under mutation-free Fermi pairwise imitation with known cost \(c>0\) and known selection intensity \(\beta>0\), let \(H_i\) denote the probability of eventual full cooperation starting with exactly \(i\) cooperators. **The initial segment \(H_0,H_1,\ldots,H_{n+1}\) uniquely and constructively determines all \(n\) group-return values.** It also gives an exact criterion for whether any increasing full fixation profile is generated by this game. The group-size restriction is sharp: at \(n=N\), *every* return vector generates precisely the same fixation profile. Recovery has a quantified small-signal limitation: after lower return values are fixed, the sensitivity of the \(n\)-th local fixation log-curvature to \(r_n\) equals \(\binom{N-1}{n-1}^{-1}\), which is of order \(N^{-(n-1)}\) for fixed \(n\).

This is an exact inverse theorem for a *known-cost and known-selection* model, not a general solution of the inverse evolutionary-game problem. It identifies parameters from exact fixation probabilities, without a finite-sample estimation guarantee.

## Assumptions and scope
The population is unstructured, of constant finite size \(N\ge3\); interaction groups of size \(n\) are sampled uniformly without replacement, including the focal individual. Each cooperator pays \(c\), each defector pays nothing, and every participant in a group containing \(j\) cooperators receives \(jc r_j/n\). For \(j=0\) that shared benefit vanishes, so no \(r_0\) is defined. Individual payoffs are their expected interaction payoffs conditional on the current number of cooperators. One individual compares its payoff with one other and imitates by the Fermi rule with parameter \(\beta>0\); there is no mutation. Only the two homogeneous strategy states are absorbing. The parameters \(N,n,c,\beta\) and the labeled indexing \(H_i\) are assumed known. Define the binomial coefficient \(\binom{a}{b}=0\) whenever \(b<0\), \(b>a\), or \(a<0\).

For \(1\le i<N\), let \(\Pi_C(i)\) and \(\Pi_D(i)\) denote expected payoffs to the two strategies. Write
\[
L_i=-\frac{1}{\beta c}\log\frac{H_{i+1}-H_i}{H_i-H_{i-1}}.
\]
These quantities exist because \(0=H_0<H_1<\cdots<H_N=1\) for positive finite Fermi rates. Let \(B=\binom{N-1}{n-1}\), and define
\[
A_{ij}=\frac{j}{nB}\left[\binom{i-1}{j-1}\binom{N-i}{n-j}-\binom{i}{j}\binom{N-i-1}{n-1-j}\right]
\quad(1\le i<N,\ 1\le j\le n).
\]

## Proof
**Step 1: Recover each payoff difference from the fixation profile.** For \(1\le i<N\), write \(T_i^+\) and \(T_i^-\) for the probabilities that the next update increases or decreases the cooperator count. The common encounter factor cancels under the Fermi rule, giving
\[
\frac{T_i^-}{T_i^+}=\exp[-\beta(\Pi_C(i)-\Pi_D(i))].
\]
Harmonicity of the absorption probability implies
\[
T_i^+(H_{i+1}-H_i)=T_i^-(H_i-H_{i-1}),
\]
so \(L_i=(\Pi_C(i)-\Pi_D(i))/c\) exactly, at any positive selection intensity, without a weak-selection expansion.

**Step 2: The group-return map is triangular.** Hypergeometric group sampling gives
\[
\frac{\Pi_C(i)}c=-1+\frac1{nB}\sum_{j=1}^n j r_j\binom{i-1}{j-1}\binom{N-i}{n-j},
\]
\[
\frac{\Pi_D(i)}c=\frac1{nB}\sum_{j=1}^n j r_j\binom{i}{j}\binom{N-i-1}{n-1-j}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
L_i=-1+\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}r_j.
\]
When \(i\le n\) and \(j>i\), both binomial terms vanish. On the diagonal, Pascal's identity gives
\[
A_{ii}=\frac{i}{nB}\binom{N-i-1}{n-i}>0\qquad(1\le i\le n<N).
\]
Thus \((A_{ij})_{1\le i,j\le n}\) is lower triangular and nonsingular. Recover the returns in order from the observed \(L_1,\ldots,L_n\):
\[
r_i=\frac{L_i+1-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}A_{ij}r_j}{A_{ii}}\quad(i=1,\ldots,n).
\]
The inputs use only \(H_0,\ldots,H_{n+1}\). This proves constructive uniqueness. Conversely, a candidate strictly increasing profile with the prescribed endpoints is realizable precisely when the recovered values are all positive and each remaining \(L_i\), \(n<i<N\), satisfies the same \(L_i=-1+\sum_jA_{ij}r_j\). Necessity was proved; for sufficiency the candidate profile obeys the corresponding birth-death harmonic equations and boundary conditions, which have a unique solution.

**Step 3: Sharp group-size boundary.** If \(n=N\), each strategy sees the whole population and hence receives the same shared public return; their payoff difference is \(\Pi_C(i)-\Pi_D(i)=-c\), independently of every \(r_j\). Setting \(a=e^{\beta c}\), the entire fixation profile becomes
\[
H_i=\frac{a^i-1}{a^N-1},\qquad 0\le i\le N,
\]
for every positive return vector. Thus not one return coordinate is identifiable from these observations at \(n=N\), whereas every coordinate is identifiable for \(n<N\).

**Step 4: Exact sensitivity at the top return.** Lower triangularity gives \(A_{in}=0\) for \(i<n\). At \(i=n\),
\[
A_{nn}=\frac{1}{\binom{N-1}{n-1}}.
\]
Holding \(r_1,\ldots,r_{n-1}\) fixed, \(\partial L_n/\partial r_n=A_{nn}\). Inverting this last step amplifies perturbations in \(L_n\) by \(\binom{N-1}{n-1}\); for fixed \(n\) that factor grows as \(N^{n-1}/(n-1)!\). This is an algebraic observation-sensitivity statement, not a finite-data statistical variance bound.

**Example distinguishing a full fixation profile from endpoint abundance.** For \(N=4\), \(n=3\), \(c=1\), \(\beta=\log2\), the positive returns \((r_1,r_2,r_3)=(18,9,9)\) give \((\Pi_C-\Pi_D)/c=(1,-1,0)\) and exactly
\[
(H_0,H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4)=(0,2/7,3/7,5/7,1).
\]
The distinct returns \((9,9,9)\) give constant zero payoff difference and \((H_0,\ldots,H_4)=(0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1)\). Both have the same full-contribution return \(r_3=9\) and the same symmetric rare-mutation stationary cooperation abundance \(1/2\). Thus the mean-abundance observation alone cannot distinguish them, although the full fixation profile does. The endpoint formula is background, not claimed as novel.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python checker `verify_inverse_public_goods.py` computes direct hypergeometric payoffs independently of the stated coefficient matrix. It verifies exact rational identities, triangular reconstruction and endpoint selection sums for 198 parameter instances with \(3\le N\le13\) and \(2\le n<N\), and tests the associated floating-point fixation profiles. It separately verifies the four-person example with exact fractions and the \(n=N\) degeneracy in 36 instances. It uses only Python's standard library. Run `python3 verify_inverse_public_goods.py` from the extracted file directory. The all-size result and sharp boundary follow from the analytic proof above, not from finite testing.

## Relationship to prior work
Wang, Liu, Chen and Szolnoki (2026) supply the general frequency-dependent public-goods model and analyze which return parameters determine rare-mutation cooperation; the present statement concerns the *entire labeled fixation profile* and its exact inverse. Hauert and McAvoy (2024) develop nonlinear public-goods payoffs, finite-population evolutionary dynamics and fixation probabilities, but do not state the specified full-profile triangular inverse theorem. Traulsen, Pacheco and Nowak (2007) supply the classical Fermi pairwise-comparison and birth-death fixation apparatus; the probabilistic ratio identity is not asserted to be new. Most importantly, Chalub and Souza (2018/2019) explicitly study a general inverse problem from fixation probabilities to multiplayer games, using a Wright–Fisher model and payoff approximations; the *generic inverse question is established prior work*. Their result does not directly supply the exact known-cost, positive-return Fermi-game triangular reconstruction, its sharp \(n=N\) failure, or the \(\binom{N-1}{n-1}\) sensitivity of the last coordinate. These distinctions are structural, not title-based. Broader or unindexed inverse results may still cover the present theorem.

## Limitations
Full fixation probabilities for every initial count are assumed precisely known; empirical estimation may be difficult, especially as \(N\) grows. The cost and Fermi selection parameter must be known separately: without them, additional scale identifiability issues arise. The result does not cover structured interaction networks, mutation, noisy or mislabelled initial counts, arbitrary payoff matrices, or unknown group sizes. The exact observation-sensitivity coefficient is not a Cramér–Rao bound or a guarantee of recoverability from a finite number of trials. Historical originality is bounded by accessible primary texts and database searches; independent assessment has not been performed.
