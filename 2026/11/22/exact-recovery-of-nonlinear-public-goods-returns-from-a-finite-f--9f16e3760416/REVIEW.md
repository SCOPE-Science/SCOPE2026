# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The mutation-free Fermi birth–death harmonic equation recovers each payoff gap divided by cost from neighboring fixation increments and the known product of cost and selection intensity. Direct hypergeometric conditioning yields an exact lower-triangular matrix on the first \(n\) transient states with strictly positive diagonal \(A_{ii}=\frac{i}{n\binom{N-1}{n-1}}\binom{N-i-1}{n-i}\); hence the recursive inverse is valid for every \(2\le n<N\). At \(n=N\) both strategies receive the same group benefit and differ only by cost, so their fixation probabilities are independent of all returns. The original verification reports 198 rational and numerical cases, an exact four-player certificate and 36 boundary cases. Finite experiments corroborate rather than establish the theorem.

## Originality — PASS, literature-bounded
The inverse fixation-to-game problem was already formulated by Chalub and Souza (arXiv:1801.02550). The general inverse question, hypergeometric expected-payoff formulas, and standard fixation identities are prior work. The Wright–Fisher construction recovers a relative fitness function from a full profile and considers its representation or approximation by a multiplayer game. The forward public-goods work uses the same nonlinear payoff and Fermi update model. The compared statements do not establish the exact positive-return Fermi inversion using the first \(n+2\) labeled fixation values, the sharp group-size boundary, or the last-coordinate precision scaling. Search absence does not prove uniqueness; an earlier equivalent or broader theorem remains a possibility.

## Value — PASS
Frequency-dependent public-goods returns can have equal full-contributor payoff and rare-mutation abundance while producing distinct fixation outcomes at intermediate cooperator counts. The theorem shows which extra labeled absorption probabilities suffice to identify the return schedule and gives the exact algebraic resolution penalty in the last reconstruction step. This is a structural parameter-identification result for a natural evolutionary-game model, not merely a new numerical example.

## Scope and limitations
An exact labeled fixation prefix, known positive product \(\beta c\), well-mixed hypergeometric sampling and mutation-free Fermi imitation are assumed for inversion. Checking whether a supplied full profile is generated by the recovered game also uses its remaining entries. No finite-sample reconstruction guarantee is provided. The 2026 initiating work addresses forward and rare-mutation behavior; the 2018 inverse literature and broader generalizations are the principal priority concern.
