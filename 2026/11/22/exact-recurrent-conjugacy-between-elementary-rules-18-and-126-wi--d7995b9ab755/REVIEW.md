# Scientific review: recurrent equivalence of Rules 18 and 126

## Correctness — PASS
The exact eight-neighborhood identities yield Rule 126 \(=HD\) and Rule 18 \(=DH\) for cyclic XOR-difference \(D\) and adjacent-wall OR \(H\). For each temporal return \(t\), the explicitly constructed inverse \(H S^{t-1}\) of \(D\) on \(t\)-fixed states proves a bijection commuting with both time and spatial rotation. Two-element domain-wall fibers prove the basin-slice formula. A separate sixteen-state enumeration supplies the unequal basin example; two standalone programs, with distinct state evolutions, passed when run from the delivered package. The claim does **not** include equality of all transient behavior.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The most relevant earlier literature already connects statistical behavior of these rules and describes their matching *maximal* cycle-length envelope at selected sizes. Those statements do not imply an orbit-by-orbit recurrence conjugacy for all ring sizes, all temporal periods and all spatial rotations, nor the exact per-orbit basin parity transfer. The existing Rule 18 two- and eleven-period and Rule 126 four-period results cover selected periods, not this general equivalence. The explicit \(HD/DH\) technique is a standard algebraic device, and older unindexed formulations might have contained the same factorization; historical priority remains qualified. Relevant primary passages and complete earlier result statements were inspected.

## Value — PASS
This identifies a natural exact equivalence between two **different nonlinear elementary cellular automata** that are normally classified together numerically, explaining their identical complete recurrence and spatiotemporal symmetry spectra while also quantifying why their basin statistics are not identical. It transfers any future exact orbit theorem from one rule to the other and demonstrates a concrete, nontrivial distinction at the smallest four-site oscillator. The object and invariant are canonical, and the result is uniform rather than an isolated computation.
