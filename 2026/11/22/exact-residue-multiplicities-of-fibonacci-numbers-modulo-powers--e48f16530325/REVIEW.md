# Review of exact eleven-adic Fibonacci residue multiplicities

## Correctness — PASS

For each exponent, the theorem counts indices in the least period rather than counting attained values. The period is justified by the exact order of the Fibonacci companion matrix. The nine regular branches each have unit eleven-adic derivative after normalization. The tenth branch has an explicit Hensel-lifted square normal form, with a unit-slope change of index parameter. The square-root count converts into the stated factor eleven and accounts for every index exactly once. The special exponent-one case, zero square roots and the exponent-zero ring are treated separately. Independent exact-integer enumeration through exponent six matches the formula target by target. The proof is infinite; the computation is only corroborative.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

The closest papers of Rowland–Yassawi (2016/2017) and Bragman–Rowland (2022/2025) establish the image of Fibonacci numbers modulo powers of eleven and its limiting size, not the complete index-fiber distribution. In particular, they do not state the exact multiplicities of all attained individual residues. Carlip–Somer (2007) establish bounds and conditional frequency laws but not the specialization here; their 1999 general-bounds chapter was not available in full and is a genuine priority risk. Complete exact frequencies at prime three and for Lucas numbers at prime seven were also inspected as neighboring, non-covering work. A mathematical deduction from the earlier eleven-adic image analysis is possible, but the present full fiber law requires a separate exact local parameter count; absence from searches is not proof of historical uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

Frequency per period is a classical natural statistic for second-order recurrences, and different fibers are not determined by the known set of attained values. The complete distribution exposes constant multiplicities \(1,2,3\) on the regular disks, higher and unbounded singular fibers, and gives every finite precision without recurrence-table enumeration. It resolves a motivated quantitative gap in the established eleven-adic residue-tree analysis; it does not claim a new general theorem for other primes.
