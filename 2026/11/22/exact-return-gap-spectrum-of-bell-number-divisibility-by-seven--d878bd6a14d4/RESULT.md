# Exact return-gap spectrum of Bell-number divisibility by seven

## Finding

Let \(B_n\) denote the number of set partitions of an \(n\)-element labelled set, with \(B_0=1\). Write the increasing sequence of nonnegative indices at which \(7\mid B_n\) as \(z_1<z_2<\cdots\). For each \(j\), define the return gap \(g_j=z_{j+1}-z_j\).

**Theorem.** The divisibility indicator \(\mathbf1_{7\mid B_n}\) has least period \(P=137257=29\cdot4733\), with precisely \(19608\) divisible indices per period. Exactly fifty distinct return gaps occur, and their complete multiplicities in one cyclic period are given in `gap_histogram.csv` (also reproduced below). In particular, the greatest gap is \(65\), attained exactly once per least period, starting at the zero index \(n=85736\pmod P\), and gap \(7\) never occurs.

Hence every interval of \(65\) consecutive nonnegative indices contains an index \(n\) with \(7\mid B_n\), and \(65\) is sharp: the interval of \(64\) indices from \(85737\) to \(85800\) has no such index. The limiting frequency of a gap \(d\) among consecutive divisible indices is exactly \(c_d/19608\), where \(c_d\) is the second column of the table.

## Assumptions and scope

The statement is exact for every nonnegative integer index, modulo the fixed prime \(7\). A *cyclic period gap* includes the interval from the last divisible index in \([0,P)\) to the first divisible index in the next translated period. Its multiplicity is counted once. The density of divisible indices is \(19608/137257\). No statement about divisibility by higher powers of seven, other primes, or growth in the prime modulus is asserted.

## Proof

**1. Recurrence.** Touchard's congruence says that for every prime \(p\), \(B_{n+p}\equiv B_n+B_{n+1}\pmod p\). The first seven Bell numbers are \(1,1,2,5,15,52,203\); their residues modulo seven form the seed vector \((1,1,2,5,1,3,0)\). Thus the entire sequence modulo seven is exactly generated by

\[
a_{n+7}=(a_n+a_{n+1})\bmod 7,\qquad (a_0,\ldots,a_6)=(1,1,2,5,1,3,0).
\]

**2. Exact period.** In \(R=(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z)[X]/(X^7-X-1)\), repeated-squaring using \(X^7=X+1\) gives \(X^{137257}=1\), while \(X^{4733}=5+2X+4X^2+5X^3+2X^4+5X^5+X^6\) and \(X^{29}=X+4X^2+6X^3+4X^4+X^5\), neither equal to one. The first relation makes \(P\) a period of every solution of the recurrence. The first seven values of the specific solution at shifts \(29\) and \(4733\) are respectively \((4,5,1,3,6,4,0)\) and \((1,0,3,2,5,2,2)\), both different from the initial vector. As \(P=29\cdot4733\) with both factors prime, a proper period dividing \(P\) would divide \(29\) or \(4733\), contrary to these two explicit checks. Hence \(P\) is least.

**3. Exhaustive finite census.** The supplied standalone script `check_bell7.py` computes the \(P\) recurrence residues using exact integer arithmetic, retains exactly those \(n\) for which \(a_n=0\), and increments the histogram of successive differences, including the wraparound difference \(P+z_1-z_{19608}\). The loop is exhaustive over all \(0\le n<P\), not a sample. The script recomputes the same zero indices by an independent rolling-state traversal, confirms the period and two proper-divisor obstructions, and compares all fifty computed histogram entries against the standalone `gap_histogram.csv`. It verifies that the count of gaps is \(19608\) and their weighted sum is exactly \(137257\). The largest gap is \(65\) with unique initial index \(85736\); by periodicity these are all possible gaps and all their exact frequencies for the infinite sequence.

**4. Structural missing gap.** If two zero indices were exactly seven apart, Touchard's recurrence would force the immediately following index to be zero as well: \(a_{n+7}=a_n+a_{n+1}=0\) and \(a_n=0\) imply \(a_{n+1}=0\). Thus seven cannot be the gap between *successive* zeros. This also provides a separate symbolic check on the computed gap table.

## Verification

Run `python3 check_bell7.py` with `gap_histogram.csv` in the same directory, using Python 3 and no third-party dependencies. Expected terminal result: `CHECK_OK period= 137257 zeros= 19608 gap_types= 50 max_gap= 65 unique_max_start= 85736 gap_total= 19608 weighted_gap_total= 137257`. Exact polynomial residues, complete cyclic enumeration and independent rolling-state scan are included. The proof of the infinite-index statement uses the verified full period; a partial search alone would not justify it.

## Relationship to prior work

Touchard's prime-modulus congruence underlies the calculation. Levine and Dalton (1962) proved that \(137257\) is the least Bell period modulo seven; Montgomery, Nahm and Wagstaff (2010) obtained general least-period results. OEIS A146097 records Bell residues modulo seven, and A054767 records the known period. These sources establish the recurrence and period, not the fifty-entry cyclic return-gap census or its sharp worst-case spacing; their data and methods permit computation but do not supply the completed new histogram. Related arithmetic-progression zero-incidence results constrain simultaneous zeros but are not equivalent to the distribution of intervening nonzero runs.

## Limitations

The result is a finite exact evaluation for one naturally specified prime, not a uniform theorem for variable primes. It is proved by exhaustive, reproducible enumeration of a *known finite period*, rather than by a closed formula for individual histogram entries. This distinction limits extrapolation to other primes. The 1979 study by Lunnon, Pleasants and Stephens could not be inspected in full; possible implicit earlier discussion of return gaps remains a priority risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
