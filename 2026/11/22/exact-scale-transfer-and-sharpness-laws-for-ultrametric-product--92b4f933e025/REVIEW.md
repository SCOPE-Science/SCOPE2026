# Review of exact ultrametric-product covering laws

**Correctness — PASS.** The closed-ball equivalence relation provides a disjoint rectangle decomposition. Any radius-\(r\) product ball has only one rectangle. Every ultrametric ball has center-independent eccentricity equal to its diameter, proving the exact equality criterion. For each equilateral cluster, a missing-row lower bound and two explicit covers prove the complete scale-transfer minimum; the proof includes singleton and closed-radius endpoints. Exact finite set-cover tests reproduce both statements without replacing the analytic argument.

**Originality — PASS (provisional priority).** The motivating 2026 paper studies the general covering-product conjecture but has no equivalent finite-ultrametric equality and scale-transfer classification. Dovgoshey–Martio establish maximum-metric multiplicativity, which is strictly weaker than a finite-\(p\) criterion, while elementary rook-graph domination is only the two-equilateral-factor special case. The searches and full-text comparisons listed in the audit found no stronger encompassing claim. Historical terminology and unindexed calculations remain risks.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem provides an all-size exact criterion for equality and, under a natural cluster hypothesis, an explicit covering number of hierarchical products. It gives exact phase transitions with respect to metric exponent and supports arbitrarily large strictness examples, beyond reciting the known product lower bound.

No claims are made for non-ultrametric factors, general exact values with two arbitrary internal hierarchies, or independent verification.
