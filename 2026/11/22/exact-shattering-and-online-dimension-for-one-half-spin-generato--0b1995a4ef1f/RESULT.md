# Exact Shattering and Online Dimension for One Half-Spin Generator Family of Hyperbolic Quadrics

## Finding
Let \(q\) be any prime power and \(n\ge 2\). In the split quadratic space \(V=\mathbb F_q^n\oplus\mathbb F_q^n\) with \(Q(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i\), let \(X\) be the set of **projective singular points** \([v]\), \(v\ne0\), \(Q(v)=0\). Fix either of the two half-spin (ruling) families \(\mathcal G^+\) of maximal totally singular \(n\)-dimensional vector subspaces. Each generator \(G\) defines a Boolean concept \(C_G=\{[v]\in X:v\in G\}\).

**Theorem.** A query support \(S\subseteq X\) is shattered by \(\{C_G:G\in\mathcal G^+\}\) if and only if \(|S|\le n-1\) and any representatives of its points are linearly independent and pairwise orthogonal for the polar form \(B(u,v)=Q(u+v)-Q(u)-Q(v)\). This description is independent of which of the two families is selected. Consequently
\[
\Phi_{q,n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{q^{\binom{k}{2}}z^k}{k!}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{(q^{n-j-1}+1)(q^{n-j}-1)}{q-1},
\qquad \operatorname{VCdim}=\operatorname{Ldim}=n-1.
\]
The \(k=0\) term is one. The shattering complex is pure of dimension \(n-2\); for \(n\ge3\) it is neither a matroid independence complex nor a flag complex.

For rank three, the shattering complex is the connected point-collinearity graph of \(Q^+(5,q)\), isomorphic under the Klein correspondence to the intersection graph of lines in \(\operatorname{PG}(3,q)\). It is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of
\[
1+(q^2+1)(q^2+q+1)\left(\frac{q(q+1)^2}{2}-1\right)
\]
circles. The binary cases \(n=2,3,4\) have polynomials \(1+9z\), \(1+35z+315z^2\) and \(1+135z+4725z^2+56700z^3\), respectively. For \(q=3,n=3\), the polynomial is \(1+130z+3120z^2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The finite field has cardinality \(q\), including characteristic two; the quadratic form is **hyperbolic** (Witt index \(n\)). Points are one-dimensional projective subspaces, NOT all nonzero vectors. All subspace dimensions in the theorem are algebraic. A generator is a maximal totally singular vector subspace of dimension \(n\). Fix a reference generator \(E\) **in the chosen system**. A generator \(G\) is in that same system exactly when \(n-\dim(G\cap E)\) is even; the opposite parity characterizes the other system. The theorem is asserted for all \(q\) and \(n\ge2\), and for no elliptic or parabolic polar space.

A support is shattered if every map \(S\to\{0,1\}\) is obtained by restricting membership in some generator of the **same chosen system**. Littlestone dimension means complete adaptive binary decision-tree depth with individual singular-point membership queries.

## Proof
**Necessary conditions.** If \(S\) is shattered, its all-positive labeling is realized by some totally singular generator. Therefore the points are mutually polar-orthogonal. If a representative of one point lies in the linear span of the other representatives, the label assigning this point zero and all others one cannot be realized by any vector subspace. Thus the representatives are linearly independent. In particular \(|S|\le n\). If \(|S|=n\), all-positive membership forces the unique generator \(G=\langle S\rangle\), which belongs to the chosen system. Any \(n-1\) of these independent points span a codimension-one totally singular subspace lying in **exactly one** generator of the chosen half-spin system (the other of the two maximal extensions belongs to the opposite system). Therefore the labeling that excludes the remaining point while including the other \(n-1\) is impossible. Hence \(|S|\le n-1\).

**Sufficiency and the avoidance bound.** Assume \(S\) consists of \(s\le n-1\) linearly independent, pairwise orthogonal singular points. Choose an arbitrary positive subset \(T\subseteq S\), of cardinality \(k\), and let \(U=\langle T\rangle\), a totally singular vector subspace of dimension \(k\). Every generator of either family containing \(U\) corresponds to a generator of the split orthogonal quotient \(U^\perp/U\), which has rank \(r=n-k\); the selected half-spin system induces one of the two quotient systems.

Any negative point not in \(U^\perp\) is excluded automatically. All other negative points have **nonzero singular images** in the quotient, since linear independence of all the representatives rules out membership in \(U\). For \(r\ge2\), a uniformly sampled generator in a fixed system of a rank-\(r\) hyperbolic quadric contains any specified singular projective point with probability exactly \(1/(q^{r-1}+1)\). This follows by double counting incidences: there are \((q^{r-1}+1)(q^r-1)/(q-1)\) singular points, each generator contains \((q^r-1)/(q-1)\) of them, and the system is transitive on points. There are at most \(s-k\le r-1\) forbidden quotient points. By the union bound, the probability of meeting any of them is at most \((r-1)/(q^{r-1}+1)<1\). Thus a generator in the fixed system contains all positive and none of the negative points. If \(r=1\), the restriction \(s\le n-1\) forces \(s=k\), and the single generator of the chosen system through \(U\) realizes the all-positive labeling. Every labeling is realized, proving sufficiency.

**Face count.** A split quadratic space of rank \(m\ge1\) has \(P_m=(q^{m-1}+1)(q^m-1)/(q-1)\) singular projective points. After selecting \(j\) independent mutually orthogonal singular points spanning \(U\), the number of further singular projective points in \(U^\perp\setminus U\) is \(q^j P_{n-j}\): each point of the quotient lifts to \(q^j\) projective points. Count ordered tuples, divide by \(k!\), and sum for \(k\le n-1\) to obtain the stated polynomial. This count uses independence, not just pairwise orthogonality. Every face extends to size \(n-1\) inside a totally singular generator, hence the complex is pure.

**Online upper and lower bounds.** Shattering provides a depth-\(n-1\) complete tree. Conversely, follow the all-positive path in any putative complete tree of depth \(n\). At every internal node, the queried point must lie outside the linear span of earlier positively queried points; otherwise every surviving subspace would answer positive and there could be no negative child. After \(n-1\) positives, that span has rank \(n-1\), so exactly one generator of the fixed system survives. It cannot split at the next query. Therefore no depth-\(n\) complete tree exists.

**Structure and rank-three topology.** For \(n\ge3\), take \(a=[e_1]\), \(b=[f_1]\), \(c=[e_1+e_2]\), where \(e_i,f_i\) are hyperbolic pairs. Both \(a,b\) and \(b,c\) are nonfaces of size two, whereas \(a,c\) is a face. In a matroid of rank at least two, size-two nonfaces among nonloops are parallel pairs and form an equivalence relation, so this is not a matroid. Three different projective points on the singular line \(\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\) have all pairs shattered but their triple is not (by dependence when \(n\ge4\), and by the rank cutoff when \(n=3\)); hence the complex is not flag. For \(n=3\) its vertices number \((q^2+1)(q^2+q+1)\), each has degree \(q(q+1)^2\). Nonadjacent singular points have a common singular neighbor in the hyperbolic orthogonal complement of their span, proving connectivity. The Euler-characteristic formula for a connected graph gives the claimed wedge-of-circles count.

## Verification
Three standalone programs are included: `code/verify_binary.py`, `code/verify_binary.cpp` and `code/verify_ternary.py`. Run `python3 code/verify_binary.py`, compile the C++ file using `g++ -O2 -std=c++17 code/verify_binary.cpp -o check_binary` and run `./check_binary`, and run `python3 code/verify_ternary.py`. The binary programs enumerate generators independently (iterative subspace growth versus unordered-basis enumeration), classify the same half-spin family by intersection parity, check exact rank-three shattering and rank-four facet counts, and verify samples of rank-four Boolean traces. The ternary implementation separately constructs projective generator spaces, checking the point, generator and two-face counts in ranks two and three. The all-rank assertion is established by the proof above, not inferred from finite computations.

## Relationship to prior work
D'haeseleer, Ihringer and Schmidt (arXiv:2401.10523v1, first posted 19 January 2024) study generalized ovoids in classical polar spaces; their Section 2 provides the classical generator-incidence counts and Section 4.4 explicitly distinguishes the two generator systems of the hyperbolic quadric. De Boeck, D'haeseleer and Rodgers (arXiv:2310.14739v1, first posted 23 October 2023) study Cameron–Liebler generator sets and incidence/association schemes, including hyperbolic quadrics. Both are important structural input, but their investigated conclusions concern ovoid and generator-set structures rather than Boolean trace completeness of one **fixed** generator system, exact face counts, or adaptive query depth. The full-rank symplectic Lagrangian incidence problem is not a proof of this theorem: in characteristic two, restricting to singular points and to one of the two orthogonal generator systems changes the terminal extension behavior.

## Limitations
Historical priority has not been independently certified; an equivalent incidence theorem may be present in literature indexed under polar-building or half-spin terminology. Computational verification is restricted to \(q=2\) in ranks two through four and \(q=3\) in ranks two and three, with rank-four triple traces sampled rather than exhaustively tested. The general result rests on the argument above. The rank-three wedge description is a one-dimensional homotopy result, not a statement about homology in all ranks. No classification of integral homology in larger rank is claimed.
