# Exact six-gap law for ternary zeros of Stern's diatomic sequence

## Finding
Let \(s(0)=0\), \(s(1)=1\), and for every integer \(n\geq1\) define Stern's sequence by
\[
s(2n)=s(n),\qquad s(2n+1)=s(n)+s(n+1).
\]
Write \(0=z_0<z_1<\cdots\) for **all** nonnegative integers with \(3\mid s(z_j)\), and put \(g_j=z_{j+1}-z_j\). Then
\[
\{g_j:j\geq0\}=\{2,3,4,5,6,8\}.
\]
Every gap type has a limiting frequency among the successive gaps, exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
d&2&3&4&5&6&8\\\hline
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\#\{0\leq j<m:g_j=d\}}m
&\frac27&\frac17&\frac17&\frac27&\frac1{14}&\frac1{14}
\end{array}.
\]
These frequencies imply the exact limiting gap mean \(4\) and variance \(3\). In particular, the largest consecutive gap is \(8\), with positive asymptotic frequency \(1/14\), and gaps \(1\) and \(7\) never occur.

## Assumptions and scope
All congruences in the proof are modulo \(3\); all indices are nonnegative integers. Gaps are differences between successive indices of zeros in the residue sequence, **not** differences between successive Stern values. The result concerns actual consecutive zeros, not a cyclic end-to-start gap in a finite prefix. No independence of successive gaps is asserted. The density is with respect to the number of successive gaps (equivalently by their starting indices in \([0,X)\), as \(X\to\infty\)).

## Proof
**1. A fixed eight-symbol state substitution.** Set \(T(n)=(s(n),s(n+1))\pmod3\). Since consecutive Stern values are coprime, the possible states are the eight vectors in \(\mathbb F_3^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\). Define
\[
L(a,b)=(a,a+b),\qquad R(a,b)=(a+b,b)
\]
over \(\mathbb F_3\). The defining recurrence gives \(T(2n)=L(T(n))\), \(T(2n+1)=R(T(n))\). Thus the infinite state word \(T(0)T(1)\cdots\) is fixed under the length-two substitution \(\sigma(u)=L(u)R(u)\), beginning at \(T(0)=(0,1)\).

**2. All zeros inside an eight-index block.** The standard Stern block identity, easily proved by induction on \(r\), is
\[
s(2^rn+j)=s(2^r-j)s(n)+s(j)s(n+1),\quad0\leq j\leq 2^r.
\]
At \(r=3\), the integer values \((s(0),\ldots,s(8))=(0,1,1,2,1,3,2,3,1)\) give the following *complete* table. The right column contains precisely those \(j\in\{0,\ldots,7\}\) for which \(3\mid s(8n+j)\), conditional on \(T(n)\).

| \(T(n)\) | zero offsets in its block |
|---|---|
| \((0,1),(0,2)\) | \(0,5,7\) |
| \((1,0),(2,0)\) | \(1,3\) |
| \((1,1),(2,2)\) | \(2,6\) |
| \((1,2),(2,1)\) | \(4\) |

Every block has at least one zero. All gaps are either between consecutive offsets in one block or between the final offset of block \(n\) and the first offset of block \(n+1\). If these offset lists are \(Z(T(n))\) and \(Z(T(n+1))\), respectively, the latter gap is
\[
8+\min Z(T(n+1))-\max Z(T(n)).
\]

**3. Finite adjacent-state transition.** Take \(W(n)=(T(n),T(n+1))\). Its two children are
\[
W(2n)=(L(T(n)),R(T(n))),\qquad
W(2n+1)=(R(T(n)),L(T(n+1))).
\]
Starting from \(W(0)=((0,1),(1,1))\), repeated application of these two maps gives **exactly 20** distinct reachable ordered pairs; the accompanying `VERIFY.py` regenerates the 20 pairs using breadth-first closure. All 20 communicate with the initial pair, whose first child is itself. Hence the associated column-sum-two transition matrix \(M\) defines an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain \(M/2\). Its unique stationary vector \(\pi\) solves \(M\pi=2\pi\), \(\sum\pi=1\). The exact rational solution (in the deterministic breadth-first order generated by `VERIFY.py`) is
\[
\pi=\tfrac1{56}(4,2,4,1,7,2,7,4,4,1,4,2,2,4,2,1,1,2,1,1).
\]
It is positive, sums to one, and satisfies every stationary equation; these are checked using rational arithmetic in the standalone certificate. The 20-pair closure likewise establishes that only the six asserted differences occur across adjacent blocks. Since every reachable pair occurs, each listed cross-block gap is realized and there can be no unlisted gap.

**4. From dyadic block frequencies to natural frequencies.** A pair \(W(n)\) at a given position produces, after \(r\) binary expansions, the \(2^r\) pairs \(W(2^rn+j)\), \(0\leq j<2^r\), with frequencies given by \((M/2)^r\) applied to its basis vector. The primitive finite chain converges uniformly over all starting states to \(\pi\). Any sufficiently long interval can be partitioned into aligned dyadic blocks with all but an arbitrarily small proportion lying in blocks longer than a chosen threshold. It follows that \(W(n)\) has natural-frequency vector \(\pi\), not merely a dyadic-subsequence limit.

Each state pair \(W(n)=(a,b)\) contributes one cross-block gap and the gaps between successive elements of \(Z(a)\). Summing these contributions against \(\pi\), the **number of gaps of each length per eight-index block** is exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
d&2&3&4&5&6&8\\\hline
\gamma_d&4/7&2/7&2/7&4/7&1/7&1/7.
\end{array}
\]
There are exactly \(\sum_d\gamma_d=2\) gaps per block on average (equivalently two zeros per block). Dividing by two yields the asserted limiting probabilities. Direct calculation gives \(\sum d\Pr(g=d)=4\), \(\sum d^2\Pr(g=d)=19\), and variance \(19-4^2=3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Reznick (2006, §§4–5) proves the primitive-pair distribution modulo arbitrary moduli, and in Theorem 5.1 gives a recursive characterization of the entire zero-index set modulo three, along with its density. This previous characterization is the essential starting point and of course determines the underlying set; it does not itself compute this six-gap spectrum or the relative frequencies and variance of consecutive gaps. Dijkstra's earlier binary-language characterization, reproduced by Reznick and in OEIS A071911, likewise recognizes individual zero positions. Coons and Shallit (2011) study an exact binary pattern representation of Stern values, not this adjacent-zero return distribution. The new result is the explicit twenty-pair block-correlation calculation and its gap-frequency consequence, not a new characterization of the zero set or a new one-point density theorem.

## Limitations
The claim is specific to divisibility of Stern values by three, not all primes or higher prime powers. The exact zero-index set and its automaton were known. No novelty is claimed for Stern's recurrence, the underlying pair-distribution method, or the indexed zero sequence. The finite-state frequencies are same-author calculations and have not undergone independent mathematical audit. An unpublished earlier gap computation, including one implicit in older descriptions of the zero-index automaton, could pre-empt the exact numerical law. The proof does not establish independence or mixing rates for successive gaps.
