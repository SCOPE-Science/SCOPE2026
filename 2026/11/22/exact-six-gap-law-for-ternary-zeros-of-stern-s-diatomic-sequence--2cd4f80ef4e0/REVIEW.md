# Scientific review: exact six-gap law for ternary Stern zeros

## Correctness — PASS
The recursion on primitive consecutive Stern residues is exact. The eight-position zero-offset table is computed directly from the standard Stern block identity and exhausts every possible state. Twenty ordered neighboring states form a closed strongly connected binary substitution graph with a self-loop. Its stationary equations have a unique positive exact rational solution; each candidate gap and its weighted frequency can be independently regenerated by `VERIFY.py`. The standard primitive-substitution argument upgrades dyadic block frequencies to natural densities. The finite enumeration is a check, not a substitute for this proof. The limiting probabilities sum to one and yield mean four and variance three.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Reznick's 2006 work already completely describes the ternary-zero index set and proves its density; that is not being claimed as new. The relevant sections give neither the explicit six successive-zero gaps nor their rational limiting distribution. OEIS A071911 and the related Dijkstra language describe the index set, while the pattern-sequence formulas of Coons–Shallit do not state its first-return statistics. Existing published three- and four-term Stern-residue results have different finite-dimensional objects. A possible implicit previously published or unpublished six-gap calculation is the main historical-priority risk, so this is a qualified originality assessment, not a uniqueness certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The gaps are intrinsic first-return times to the natural divisibility set of Stern's sequence, rather than an arbitrary subsequence or chosen modulus slice. The exact complete six-valued law, a forbidden gap, a sharp maximum of eight and rational relative frequencies describe its spatial structure beyond the known one-point density. The twenty-state calculation gives a reproducible route to a natural arithmetic statistic, and the variance quantifies clustering beyond average spacing.

## Limits and references
The proof is for divisibility by three only; it neither treats every prime nor higher prime powers, and does not assert independence of successive gaps. Historical priority remains subject to wider literature and expert review. Relevant sources are Reznick, arXiv:math/0610601; Coons–Shallit, arXiv:1105.0086; and OEIS A071911. The code's rational identities have been executed; independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
