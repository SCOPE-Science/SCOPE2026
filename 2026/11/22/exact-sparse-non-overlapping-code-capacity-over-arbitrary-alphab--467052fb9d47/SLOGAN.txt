For every alphabet size \(q\ge2\) and word length \(n\ge2\), the largest non-overlapping code with exactly two nonzero positions has size \(\frac{n-1}{2}(q-1)^2\) for odd \(n\), and \(\frac{n-2}{2}(q-1)^2+\lfloor(q-1)^2/4\rfloor\) for even \(n\). Odd lengths saturate primitive-necklace packing, while even lengths have a sharp bipartition obstruction that first produces a strict deficit at alphabet size four.
