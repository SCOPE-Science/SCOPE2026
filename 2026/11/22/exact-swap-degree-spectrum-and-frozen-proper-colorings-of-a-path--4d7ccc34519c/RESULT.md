# Exact swap-degree spectrum and frozen proper colorings of a path

## Finding
Let \(q\ge2\) and \(n\ge2\) be integers, and let \(\mathcal W_{q,n}\) be the proper \(q\)-colorings of the labeled path on \(n\) vertices, viewed as strings of named colors in which consecutive letters differ. Two such colorings are adjacent if one is obtained from the other by interchanging the colors on a single adjacent pair while remaining proper. Write \(d(w)\) for the number of distinct adjacent colorings and \(H_{q,n}(u)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W_{q,n}}u^{d(w)}\).

For every integer \(q\ge2\), the **entire degree distribution** of this reconfiguration graph is determined by
\[
\sum_{n\ge2}H_{q,n}(u)z^{n-2}=
\frac{q(q-1)[u+(1-u)z]}{1-[1+(q-2)u]z+(q-2)(u-1)z^2}.
\]
Thus \(H_{q,2}(u)=q(q-1)u\), \(H_{q,3}(u)=q(q-1)[1+(q-2)u^2]\), and the higher coefficients follow from a two-term polynomial recurrence.

Put \(a=q-2\). The number of **isolated** (no admissible adjacent swap) proper colorings is zero for \(n=2\), equals \(q(q-1)\) for \(n=3\), and for all \(n\ge4\) equals \(q(q-1)f_{n-4}\), where \(f_0=1\), \(f_1=1+a\), and \(f_r=f_{r-1}+a f_{r-2}\) for \(r\ge2\). In particular, for three colors this equals \(6F_{n-2}\) for \(n\ge4\), using \(F_0=0,F_1=1\). For \(q=2\), both alternating colorings are isolated for every \(n\ge3\). For \(q\ge3\), the largest possible degree is exactly \(n-1\), achieved by \(q(q-1)(q-2)^{n-2}\) colorings (for \(n\ge3\)).

Finally, let \(W\) be uniform on \(\mathcal W_{q,n}\), where \(q\ge3\) is fixed. Put \(s=(q-2)/(q-1)\). For \(n\ge3\),
\[
\mathbb E d(W)=2s+(n-3)s^2.
\]
For \(n\ge4\),
\[
\operatorname{Var} d(W)=2s(1-s)+(n-3)s^2(1-s^2)
+2(n-4)s^3(1-s)+4s^2(1-s).
\]
For \(n=3\), the variance is \(4s(1-s)\), and at \(n=2\), the degree is identically one. The centered degree obeys the Gaussian limit
\[
\frac{d(W)-\mathbb E d(W)}{\sqrt n}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal N\!\left(0,s^2(1-s)(1+3s)\right).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All colors are named, all path vertices are distinguishable and linearly ordered, and there are no wraparound swaps. Exactly one adjacent *interchange* is permitted for the degree graph, and every intermediate and resulting coloring must be proper. A legal swap never fixes the word because adjacent symbols are distinct; distinct swap indices give distinct neighbors. All counting statements are exact for every \(q,n\) in the specified ranges. The limiting law has \(q\ge3\) fixed while \(n\to\infty\).

## Proof
**1. Local characterization.** For \(n\ge3\), define \(X_i=\mathbf1_{\{w_i\ne w_{i+2}\}}\) for \(1\le i\le n-2\) and put \(m=n-2\). Swapping positions 1 and 2 is legal precisely when \(X_1=1\); the last two positions are legal precisely when \(X_m=1\). An interior swap at \(i\) is legal precisely when both new exterior neighbor inequalities hold, namely \(X_{i-1}=X_i=1\). It follows that
\[
 d(w)=X_1+X_m+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}X_iX_{i+1}.
\]
The two endpoint terms are deliberately both present when \(m=1\); swapping the first and last pair then gives two different proper colorings.

**2. Independent Bernoulli representation.** Identify the named colors with the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), without changing what equality means. A uniform proper word is generated by choosing its first color uniformly and then choosing each nonzero difference \(Y_i=w_{i+1}-w_i\) independently uniformly from the \(q-1\) nonzero residues. Now \(X_i=1\) precisely when \(Y_{i+1}\ne-Y_i\). Conditional on all earlier differences, \(X_i\) equals one with constant probability \(s=(q-2)/(q-1)\), independently of the past indicators. Induction establishes that the \(X_i\) are mutually independent Bernoulli variables with parameter \(s\). Equivalently, once the first two colors have been fixed, each extension with \(X_i=1\) has \(a=q-2\) choices and each extension with \(X_i=0\) has one choice.

**3. Exact transfer polynomial.** When a fresh indicator is appended, its cross-term with the preceding indicator contributes one to the swap degree if and only if both are one. In the state order \((1,0)\), the initial weighted row is \((au,1)\), the transition matrix and terminal weight are
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}au&1\\a&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
v=\begin{pmatrix}u\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus for \(n\ge3\), \(H_{q,n}(u)=q(q-1)(au,1)T^{n-3}v\). Its characteristic polynomial is \(\lambda^2-(1+au)\lambda+a(u-1)\), and with the separately verified \(n=2\) base value, the recurrence gives the displayed bivariate rational generating function. The complete polynomial is therefore computed by a linear number of polynomial-recurrence steps in \(n\).

**4. Frozen and maximum-degree cases.** For \(n\ge4\) the local formula vanishes if and only if the first and last indicators are zero and the interior indicators have no consecutive pair of ones. An internal string of \(r=n-4\) indicators with a choice weight \(a\) for each one and no adjacent ones has enumerator \(f_r\), satisfying \(f_0=1, f_1=1+a, f_r=f_{r-1}+a f_{r-2}\). Multiplying by \(q(q-1)\) proves the frozen count, including all boundary cases separately. Conversely every one of the \(n-1\) path edges is swappable if and only if all \(m\) indicators equal one; this has \(q(q-1)a^{n-2}\) realizations for \(q\ge3\).

**5. Finite moments and limiting law.** The independence of the indicators and the local-degree identity yield the mean. For \(m\ge2\), the variance consists of \(2s(1-s)\) from the endpoints, \((m-1)s^2(1-s^2)\) from the interior products, \(2(m-2)s^3(1-s)\) from neighboring products, and \(4s^2(1-s)\) from the two endpoint/product covariances; all other covariances vanish. This gives the stated finite formula. To prove the limit, the stationary interior sum \(\sum X_iX_{i+1}\) has a bounded one-dependent sequence of summands, each of variance \(s^2(1-s^2)\) and successive covariance \(s^3(1-s)\). A block-and-gap decomposition into blocks of length \(\lfloor n^{1/5}\rfloor\) produces independent blocks after omitting one term per boundary; the omitted sum has \(o(n)\) variance, and Lyapunov's third-moment bound for the blocks is \(O(n^{7/5})=o(n^{3/2})\). The two endpoint contributions are uniformly bounded. The resulting normal variance density is \(s^2(1-s^2)+2s^3(1-s)=s^2(1-s)(1+3s)>0\), proving the Gaussian assertion.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` constructs all proper words for small alphabet/length parameters, computes their legal swap neighbors by modifying the word and rechecking every adjacent inequality, and compares the observed degree histogram with the independent two-state transfer calculation. It additionally checks the frozen count, full-degree count, and rational exact mean/variance against direct enumeration. The checker reported `VERIFY_OK exact_strata=31 randomized_long_words=2000`; the random tests compare direct swaps with the local formula for long proper words. The finite checks corroborate but do not establish the all-parameter statements, which follow from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Yamanaka et al. (2015) investigate general colored-token swapping on graphs without a requirement that each intermediate token configuration be a proper coloring. Fuchs et al. (2025) explicitly introduce the proper-coloring-preserving adjacent swap rule, give the path reachability algorithm for three colors, and state the local ternary swappability criterion (Observation 9 in the conference version). We do **not** claim these operations, local ternary swappability, or the concept of rigid colorings as new. The contribution here is the exact degree-distribution rational generating function for every named palette, the closed frozen and maximal-degree counts, and the finite-moment and Gaussian asymptotic laws. The earlier feasibility, local, and reachability statements do not imply these quantitative enumerations without additional enumeration and probabilistic arguments.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns path graphs, not cycles or arbitrary host graphs, and fixed unrestricted named palettes, not fixed color content. Its Gaussian law assumes fixed \(q\ge3\), not joint growth of \(q\) and \(n\). It describes degrees but neither component sizes nor global mixing or shortest-path distances. No independent mathematical audit has been performed; unindexed or differently phrased earlier quantitative work remains a novelty risk. The 2015 source motivates the adjacent-swap operation; the properness-preserving variant was explicitly introduced in the cited 2025 work.
