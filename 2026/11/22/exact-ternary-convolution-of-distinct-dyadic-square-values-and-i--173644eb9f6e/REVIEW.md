# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Square values split disjointly as an odd residue cylinder plus four times the smaller-depth square set. At depth at least five, even square values have zero and four modulo eight subfamilies with exact cardinalities. Partitioning ordered triples by their number of odd square values exhausts every target and proves the five numerical classes and the recursive class; base cases are enumerated explicitly. The inequality \(A\le B=u/4\), together with \(s_{k-2}<u\), proves the unique maximizing residue class for all \(k\ge6\). Energy follows by disjoint target counts and Parseval. The limiting rational constant follows by a convergent geometric recurrence. All 8190 targets at depths one through twelve were checked independently by a standalone enumerator; finite checks are not the infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** Harrington–Jones–Lamarche (arXiv:1404.0187) studies representation of residues by input squares; Arias–Borja–Rubio (arXiv:1812.11599) describes support cardinalities for three-square sums. Neither supplies multiplicities in the distinct square-value model. The closest same-object work, *Sharp Fourier-algebra norms of all dyadic square residues*, gives the square-set Fourier-transform recurrence and its absolute-first norm, which would permit indirect computation of all higher convolutions. It does **not** state or derive the present complete explicit triple-fiber recurrence, exact sharp maximizing targets, or evaluated sixth Fourier moment. The mathematical evaluation, not the underlying square-set decomposition or an unqualified claim of entirely new ingredients, is the contribution. The distinct parabola-graph triple-convolution problem lives in a different ambient group. General Fourier identities and a possible unstated prior equivalent remain priority risks.

## Value

**PASS.** The threefold additive distribution of square **values**, rather than roots, is a natural local invariant: it refines the classical three-square support problem, measures biases between represented residue classes, and gives an exact sixth Fourier moment. The sharp all-depth maximum and explicit moment constant are not determined merely by representability or by square counts. No global number-theoretic applications are asserted.

## Limitations

The work concerns the image indicator convolution on rings modulo powers of two, with repeats permitted. It does not evaluate root-weighted representation functions, integer three-square representations, or other prime moduli. Historical priority is qualified by the general Fourier-set result, and independent review remains outstanding.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
