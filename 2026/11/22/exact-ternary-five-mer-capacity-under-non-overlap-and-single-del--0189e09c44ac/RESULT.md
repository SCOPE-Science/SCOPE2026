# Exact ternary five-mer capacity under non-overlap and single-deletion correction

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma=\{0,1,2\}\). A non-overlapping length-\(n\) code corrects one deletion if no nonempty proper prefix of any codeword equals a suffix of any (possibly the same) codeword, and if no two distinct codewords have a common length-\(n-1\) deletion descendant. Write \(T_n\) for its largest size and \(E_n\) for the number of labeled maximum codes. Then, for \(2\le n\le5\),

\[
(T_2,T_3,T_4,T_5)=(1,2,3,6),\qquad
(E_2,E_3,E_4,E_5)=(6,18,60,72).
\]

The \(72\) maximum five-symbol codes fall into exactly seven orbits under simultaneous alphabet permutation and word reversal, of sizes \(12,12,12,6,12,12,6\). The seven representative codebooks appear in `optima.json`. In contrast, without deletion correction the largest ternary non-overlapping five-symbol code has \(17\) words. Thus requiring one-deletion correction reduces this exact synchronization-code capacity from \(17\) to \(6\). Since at length one the deletion shadow of every word is the empty word, \(T_1=1\); therefore five is also the shortest ternary blocklength that can carry at least four messages while meeting both conditions.

## Assumptions and scope

All words have exactly length \(n\) and belong to \(\Sigma^n\). A *non-overlap* condition means, for every \(u,v\) in the code, including \(u=v\), and each integer \(1\le k<n\),

\[
u_1\cdots u_k\ne v_{n-k+1}\cdots v_n.
\]

The single-deletion shadow \(D(u)\) is the **set**, not multiset, of distinct words produced by deleting one symbol of \(u\). Unique correction means \(D(u)\cap D(v)=\varnothing\) for distinct \(u,v\). The two constraints are simultaneous. No restriction is placed on received words. The group identifying codes consists of all six alphabet relabelings and a global reversal; neither operation changes either constraint.

## Proof

**1. Normal form.** In any nonempty non-overlapping code, the set \(P\) of first symbols and set \(S\) of last symbols are disjoint, by the overlap prohibition with \(k=1\). Since the ternary alphabet has three symbols, either \(|P|\le2,|S|=1\) or \(|P|=1,|S|\le2\). Reversing every word if necessary and relabeling the symbols therefore places every code inside the self-unbordered words whose first symbol belongs to \(\{0,2\}\) and whose last symbol is \(1\). There are exactly \(48\) such words of length five.

**2. Exact rational upper certificate.** Two distinct normal-form words are called conflicting if they exhibit a forbidden prefix-suffix overlap in either direction, or share any single-deletion descendant. In `clique_cover.json` are exactly \(28\) groups of these \(48\) words, with positive integer weights and common denominator \(16\). Every group is pairwise conflicting, every possible normal-form word is covered with total numerator at least \(16\), and the sum of all group numerators equals \(98\). Because a legal code meets each group in at most one word,

\[
16|C|\le\sum_{G}w_G|C\cap G|\le\sum_Gw_G=98.
\]

Hence \(|C|\le\lfloor98/16\rfloor=6\) at length five. All of these are exact integer checks performed by `verify.py`; neither an optimizer nor floating-point arithmetic is necessary to replay the proof.

**3. Attainment.** The following six codewords are non-overlapping and have mutually disjoint single-deletion shadows:

`00001`, `00221`, `02101`, `20111`, `22001`, `22211`.

They prove \(T_5=6\).

**4. Complete census.** The `verify.py` program independently builds every self-unbordered ternary source word at lengths two through five, evaluates prefix-suffix conflicts and deletion shadows directly, and enumerates all maximal compatible sets by a pivoted Bron–Kerbosch search. The resulting maximum sizes and counts are exactly those above. At length five, the \(72\) optimal sets are compared against the disjoint union of the seven complete symmetry orbits generated by `optima.json`; equality of the two *sets of codebooks*, not merely their counts, is checked. The independent `alternate_verify.py` program uses longest-common-subsequence dynamic programming in place of deletion shadows and enumerates all six-element compatible subsets of the \(48\)-word normal form, finding exactly \(12\). The three choices of the distinguished end symbol, plus reversal, give \(6\) disjoint orientations, hence \(6\cdot12=72\). These orientations are disjoint for maximum codes because every optimum uses two first symbols and one last symbol, or the reverse (verified by the exhaustive census).

**5. Uncorrected comparison.** The same exhaustive five-word computation omitting only the deletion-shadow conflicts gives maximum size \(17\). This is also the independently published five-symbol non-overlap optimum and is used as a comparison, not as part of the six-word upper proof.

## Verification

Use Python 3.10 or later, from the directory containing all ten supplied files:

```bash
python3 verify.py
python3 alternate_verify.py
```

The programs use only the Python standard library and print `VERIFY_OK` and `ALTERNATE_OK` upon success. The certificate is checked by integer arithmetic and complete normal-form coverage. Both programs reconstruct source objects from their definitions; no external solver, downloaded database, or secret file is needed.

## Relationship to prior work

Blackburn's *Non-overlapping codes* [1] proves general cardinality bounds and constructions for synchronization codes, and handles the non-overlap objective without the added deletion-correction constraint. The published exact ternary length-five non-overlapping size \(17\) [2] similarly does not determine the constrained optimum. A separate ternary length-five study of non-overlapping codes detecting a substitution [3] reports a maximum \(9\), but a single substitution *detection* requirement is weaker than one-deletion *correction*: the latter distinguishes deletion descendants, not merely words at Hamming distance one. The indexed binary ten-bit non-overlapping deletion-code calculation [4] addresses a different alphabet and block length. Wang, Fang and Feng [5] obtain bounds for balanced or Hamming-error-correcting partially overlap-free codes; the publicly accessible Section 4 explicitly uses Hamming spheres rather than deletion descendants. None of these reported statements implies the seven-class, 72-optimum, six-codeword result here. The separate length-five **two**-deletion maximum \(6\) [6] has the same numerical value but no upper-bound implication: a one-deletion-correcting code need not correct two deletions. The six-word witness here shares two-deletion descendants among different codewords. Full text for [3] and [5] was not completely accessible for comparison; this limits historical-priority certainty.

## Limitations

This is an exact finite classification only for ternary lengths two through five. It does not determine the full length-six capacity, any asymptotic rate, or the performance of an actual streaming decoder after an uncorrected deletion spanning codeword boundaries. Non-overlap is a strong framing condition, but the stated channel deletes exactly one symbol *inside one identified source word*; no claim about multiple-word edit propagation is made. The originality assessment concerns the identified literature and research records, not a guarantee that no equivalent unpublished or unindexed result exists. Independent external mathematical validation has not been performed.
