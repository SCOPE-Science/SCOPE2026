# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The upper variety reduces every word in three variables to one of 15 normal forms; all 32767 nonempty polynomials are therefore covered, not sampled. The complete absorption criterion provides exact equivalence signatures; an independent construction evaluates the same polynomials in finite variety generators and computes the congruence join needed for the intersection variety. Both independently give the seven numbers and reproduce the previously published ranks one and two. Proof of the cited normal-form and join theorems is used as a premise, rather than claimed to be reconstructed.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The motivating 2026 paper explicitly lists all two-generator orders and both endpoint three-generator orders, but its text does not tabulate the five intermediate three-generator orders. No equivalent tabulation was located in targeted searches of the mathematical literature and indexed research records. The paper's stronger complete absorption criterion makes these numbers computable, a substantive limit on priority: the new contribution is the fully evaluated natural first-separating-rank census, not a new classification or algorithmic decidability theorem. Unindexed or later computations remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The rank-three free algebras are mathematically singled out by the published basis-rank theorem: four of the varieties cannot be generated at free rank two, whereas all seven are generated by their rank-three free algebras. The five exact missing orders distinguish all seven varieties quantitatively at their first separating rank. This is a natural finite invariant answering a gap in the source's enumerative table, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a known table recomputation.
