# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Inclusion–exclusion separates the sixth Stirling column into analytic parity branches after the vanishing of prime-divisible bases. Coefficient valuations show that the even branch is a 3-adic isometry. The odd branch has one unique critical point, and its analytic square-root coordinate change is also an isometry, giving the exact global quadratic normal form and every finite-level fiber multiplicity. The standalone Python checker verified all two-branch histograms through \(3^7\), the critical lifts, and 726 exact Stirling comparisons. The proof rather than the finite checks supports universal quantification.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Kwong's 1989 paper gives the exact period and is explicitly acknowledged as prior. Sun (2005), Davis (2008, 2013), the full fifth-column binary fiber theorem, and the full sixth-binomial ternary image theorem were compared at the level of claims and their implications. None of the inspected statements yields the evaluated sixth-Stirling ternary fiber law, including its odd quadratic image. More general unindexed historical literature might contain a dominating theorem; this remains a priority risk rather than a claim of exhaustive novelty certification.

**Value — PASS.** A fixed Stirling column is a canonical sequence of set-partition counts; understanding its exact prime-power residues goes well beyond period length and pointwise divisibility. The unusual coexistence of complete ternary residue support with a quadratic, nonuniform half of the index fibers gives a natural and reusable structural classification of its modular behavior.
