# Exact three-seed freezing and geometric nucleation on the four-cube

## Finding

**Theorem (fair-tie zero-temperature Glauber dynamics on the four-cube).** Let \(Q_4\) have sixteen vertices \(\{0,1\}^{4}\), with nearest-neighbor edges. Initially assign positive spin to exactly three vertices \(S\), and negative spin to the other thirteen. Every vertex has an independent rate-one clock; when its clock rings, it flips if this strictly lowers the ferromagnetic Ising energy, flips with probability one-half if this leaves the energy unchanged, and otherwise retains its spin. All infinite-time outcomes are either consensus or one of eight frozen half-cube partitions.

Let \(D(S)\) denote the nondecreasing triple of the three pairwise Hamming distances in \(S\). The exact probability of final nonconsensus freezing depends only on \(D(S)\), with the complete table:

| \(D(S)\) | Three-site placements | Exact nonconsensus-freezing probability |
|:--:|--:|:--|
| \((1,1,2)\) | 96 | \(0\) |
| \((1,2,3)\) | 192 | \(\frac{1}{201}\) |
| \((1,3,4)\) | 64 | \(0\) |
| \((2,2,2)\) | 64 | \(\frac{12218}{844401}\) |
| \((2,2,4)\) | 48 | \(\frac{4874793853808}{908406262473057}\) |
| \((2,3,3)\) | 96 | \(\frac{58500002907638}{22710156561826425}\) |

These six rows partition all \(\binom{16}{3}=560\) three-site placements. Averaging uniformly over them, the exact nonconsensus-freezing probability is

\[
\boxed{\frac{1580907136642813192}{371020874452016488875}\approx0.004260965475265379.}
\]

This is a **sharp three-spin nucleation boundary**: every configuration with zero, one or two minority spins reaches consensus with probability one, while four of the six three-spin geometries have strictly positive probability of reaching a frozen half-cube. The most favorable three-spin geometry is the equidistant triple \((2,2,2)\), with probability \(12218/844401\). A three-site path \((1,1,2)\) never nucleates a frozen half-cube, even though some more dispersed triples do.

## Assumptions and scope

Write \(\sigma_v\in\{-1,+1\}\) for each vertex. The energy is \(H(\sigma)=-J\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E(Q_4)}\sigma_u\sigma_v\), with \(J>0\). A fair coin is tossed independently at each exactly energy-neutral update. No external magnetic field, disorder, or simultaneous update is allowed. Spins can flip more than once; in particular, neutral flips are permitted, unlike strict-descent dynamics.

To encode a state, label vertices by four-bit integers \(0,\ldots,15\) and let bit \(v\) of a sixteen-bit state mask be one precisely when the spin at vertex \(v\) is positive. The initial three-spin set is an arbitrary unordered three-subset of these sixteen labeled vertices, not a random walk in a four-site chain. The absorbing half-cubes are the eight masks obtained by choosing a coordinate and fixing its value to zero or one for the positive spin set; both monochromatic ground states are the other two absorbing states.

## Proof

**1. Exact transition rates.** Let \(d_v(s)\) be the number of the four neighbors of vertex \(v\) with spin opposite to that at \(v\) in state \(s\). Twice the accepted-flip rate is the integer

\[
w_v(s)=\begin{cases}2,&d_v(s)\ge3,\\1,&d_v(s)=2,\\0,&d_v(s)\le1.\end{cases}
\]

A possible move replaces \(s\) by \(s\mathbin{\oplus}2^v\). If \(c(s)\) denotes the number of disagreeing edges, then the move changes \(c\) by \(4-2d_v(s)\); thus \(c\) never increases. In particular, one cannot use a strictly decreasing-energy recurrence alone: ties require solving finite within-energy communicating classes.

**2. Exhaustive recurrent-class certificate.** The `verify_full_states.py` program independently enumerates all \(2^{16}=65{,}536\) states and all \(720{,}896\) positive-rate directed transitions. Strong-component decomposition of the complete directed graph gives exactly ten closed components, all singletons: the two consensus states and eight three-cube half-space partitions. No other closed component, including a neutral-flip blinker class, exists. In a finite continuous-time chain, absorption into one of these ten singleton classes therefore occurs almost surely. The accompanying certificate and checker verify each of these finite assertions by exhaustive iteration, not by sampling.

**3. Exactly lumped symmetry and rational harmonic equations.** The symmetry group generated by four vertex-coordinate reflections, three adjacent coordinate swaps and global spin reversal preserves the generator. It partitions the \(65{,}536\) spin configurations into exactly 222 orbits. Let \(p_i\) be the probability of ending frozen from orbit \(i\). At every transient orbit representative \(s_i\) its exact first-step equation is

\[
\left(\sum_{v=0}^{15}w_v(s_i)\right)p_i
=\sum_{v=0}^{15}w_v(s_i)\,p_{\operatorname{orb}(s_i\oplus 2^v)}.
\]

Boundary values are \(p_i=0\) for the consensus orbit and \(p_i=1\) for the frozen half-cube orbit. Because the disagreeing-edge count never increases, these equations split into blocks indexed by \(c\). Solving the blocks in ascending \(c\) by exact rational linear algebra gives the 222 fractions in `q4_certificate.json`. Uniqueness follows from the terminal-component computation: a finite absorbing Markov chain has a unique bounded solution with the specified boundary values. `verify_orbit_exact.py` reconstructs the entire orbit partition, all exact linear equations, and the complete certificate without reading any intermediate calculation.

**4. Full-state, independent proof check and geometries.** `verify_full_states.py` reconstructs the cube graph with a separate neighbor calculation, reconstructs the symmetry action, and checks the weighted harmonic identity independently for *every* one of the \(65{,}536\) original states using exact fractions. It also checks the ten terminal classes by a full-state strongly connected component calculation. Finally, it enumerates all 560 three-vertex subsets and sorts their three pairwise distances. The resulting six classes are exactly the table above, with the displayed counts and rational probabilities; there are no unexamined source geometries or approximated probabilities.

For additional direct witnesses, the representative triples with vertex sets \(\{0,3,4\}\) and \(\{1,2,4\}\) reach the half-cube with positive probability in five flips, while \(\{1,7,8\}\) and \(\{3,4,8\}\) do so in seven flips. Every specified flip is one of the admissible fair-tie or energy-lowering moves. Representatives of the two zero-probability geometries have no directed path to a frozen half-cube, as certified by the exact harmonic solution and exhaustive full-state verification. With at most two positive spins the certified probability is zero in every state, whereas a three-spin configuration with distances \((2,2,2)\) has strictly positive probability. This proves sharpness.

## Verification

The three self-contained reproducibility artifacts are `q4_certificate.json`, `verify_orbit_exact.py`, and `verify_full_states.py`. From the same directory, using Python 3.13, SymPy 1.14, NumPy 2.3, and SciPy 1.17 (or compatible versions), run:

```sh
python3 verify_orbit_exact.py q4_certificate.json
python3 verify_full_states.py q4_certificate.json
```

The exact solver reconstructs the 222 orbit-level absorbing probabilities and compares every rational to the recorded certificate; the independent full-state checker verifies every positive-rate transition, full-state harmonic equation, closed communicating class, orbit representative, and three-seed classification. Successful recorded local executions were obtained from these exact source files. The certificate also records optional exact ensemble-average freezing probabilities for uniform independent fair spins (approximately \(0.111877443903\)) and exactly eight positive spins (approximately \(0.154089067540\)); these values are checkable by the same full-state harmonic identity but are not needed for the three-seed theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Camia, De Santis and Newman (initial public arXiv submission 2001-03-07) develop the same zero-temperature fair-tie Glauber mechanism on the infinite square lattice; their results concern infinite-volume clusters and recurrence rather than exact finite-hypercube seed-nucleation probabilities. Jovanovski (initial public arXiv version 2015-08-31) studies low but positive-temperature crossover barriers between homogeneous states on hypercubes, not a zero-temperature three-seed absorbing-probability census.

Most relevant, Chen, Machta, Newman and Stein (arXiv v1 2025-06-24) study precisely zero-temperature Glauber dynamics on hypercubes, including numerical ground-state probabilities from *balanced* random initial configurations and a proof that persistent blinkers cannot exist below dimension eight. Their examined main paper does not state this exact four-cube three-seed classification, the six rational basin probabilities or the sharp one/two/three-seed boundary. That work supplies closely related context and limits the originality claim: the absence of blinkers as a general theorem is already known and is not claimed here.

A published exact four-cube synchronous-majority classification counts 702 tie-retaining fixed states and 885 primitive synchronous two-cycles. It concerns simultaneous deterministic updates and a different tie rule; its 702 fixed states do not imply the ten absorbing states, continuous-time first-step equations or three-seed probabilities here. A published exact bipartite zero-temperature consensus race applies to odd-by-odd complete bipartite graphs without ties, not to the four-cube. General absorbing-Markov-chain linear algebra is standard; the claim is the sharp, explicitly certified four-cube geometric nucleation law, not the linear-algebra method itself.

## Limitations

This is an exhaustive finite theorem at dimension four only. It is not an asymptotic nucleation law as dimension grows, an equilibrium result, or a statement for energy-neutral moves rejected or for simultaneous majority updating. The algorithmic proof is finite and exact, but its numerical certificate and source code have not received independent external mathematical validation. Published numerical curves for four-cube ground-state probabilities and unindexed earlier enumerations could contain overlapping finite data; definitive historical priority is not established.
