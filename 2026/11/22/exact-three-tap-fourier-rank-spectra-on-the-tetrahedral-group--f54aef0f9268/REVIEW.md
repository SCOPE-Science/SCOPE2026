# Scientific review: tetrahedral three-tap Fourier ranks

## Correctness — PASS
The quantified claim is over every exact three-element support in \(A_4\) and every nonzero complex coefficient triple. The four support types exhaust \(220\) subsets by direct Klein-coset counting and exhaustive verification of the four translation/conjugation orbits. The proof reduces regular convolution to the three degree-one Fourier blocks and the standard degree-three block; the two subgroup cases follow from the subgroup Fourier transforms. In both remaining types the displayed cubic determinant and explicit nonzero two-by-two minors show that the degree-three block loses at most one rank, and the substitution identity \(P=-4Y(Y+1)\) excludes simultaneous losses in scalar and degree-three blocks. Rational coefficient witnesses establish every claimed rank on every support. Two standalone exact scripts independently verify symbolic identities and all \(696\) transported, twelve-by-twelve regular-matrix witness ranks.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
The pre-2007 finite-group uncertainty principle establishes the lower bound \(R(f)\ge4\) for three-point support, not an attainable-rank diagram. The 2013 Cayley characteristic-polynomial paper supplies general representation decomposition but not the four geometry-sensitive coefficient rank sets. The published \(A_4\) rank-three Fourier-support result addresses the reverse quantifier (possible time-support sizes when Fourier rank is fixed to three), not this exact three-point time-support classification. The published odd-prime dihedral weighted three-tap result concerns distinct groups with only one- and two-dimensional irreducibles. The published binary-tetrahedral and icosahedral three-tap classifications use unweighted coefficients and different groups. A hidden equivalent weighted-tetrahedral classification in graph spectra or finite group algebras remains possible; the documented searches and inspected full scientific texts did not locate one.

## Value — PASS
\(A_4\) is the smallest even-permutation group with a genuine three-dimensional irreducible representation. The theorem completely determines which Fourier recovery ranks a three-sparse convolution can attain at every fixed support; it isolates a noncyclic transversal geometry where rank \(10\) is possible, distinct from both subgroup supports and mixed-coset triples. Such structural rank gaps are not implied by support-cardinality uncertainty bounds. It is a complete natural finite classification, not a numerical conditioning statement or an extrapolation to larger alternating groups.
