# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The Hamiltonian and bosonic double-occupancy normalization are explicit. Complete \(S_m\times S_n\) decomposition has symmetric dimension five, \(m-1\) and \(n-1\) standard blocks of dimensions three (or two on a size-two shore), and all residual zero-mode kernels. The incidence identity \(RR^{\mathsf T}=(m-2)I+J\) independently reconstructs the critical couplings. Its dimension sum matches the entire Hilbert space. Exact subtraction and the strict root intervals produce the unique positive crossing, precise multiplicity and order reversal for \(U>0\). Reproducibility tests use exact integer and rational computations with an independent full-matrix construction. Residual risk: subtle nonstandard representation bookkeeping, mitigated by the dimension check and exact determinant tests, remains subject to independent scrutiny.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence

Valiente–Petrosyan [1] solve the two-boson periodic one-dimensional chain, not a bipartite all-to-all network. Childs–Gosset–Webb [2] prove general-graph hardness, not a special-graph spectrum. The closest network-specific integrable constructions, Ymai et al. [3] and Santos Filho [4], include intersite density interactions that cannot specialize to strictly positive onsite-only repulsion with both shores nontrivial. The exact quadratic-versus-cubic boundary, all-size multiplicities and \(m>3n\) or \(n>3m\) resonance threshold are not entailed by the statements inspected. The comparison checked equivalent formulations, potential stronger coverage and database-like results. An unindexed equivalent theorem or unavailable full paper cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The classification turns an interacting bosonic Hamiltonian of quadratically growing Hilbert-space dimension into explicit low-degree factors for an infinite family. The sharp \(3:1\) imbalance condition and tunable high-multiplicity crossing locate a nontrivial symmetry-controlled spectral phenomenon. This is distinct from simply renaming a star-graph computation, because both shores have standard sectors and a size-dependent competition between them.
