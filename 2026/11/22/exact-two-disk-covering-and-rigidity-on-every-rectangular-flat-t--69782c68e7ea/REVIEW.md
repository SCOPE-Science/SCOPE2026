# Review — exact two-disk covering of rectangular flat tori

## Correctness
**PASS.** For every real \(a\ge b>0\), the transverse fiber at \(y=b/2\) gives the sharp arbitrary-center lower bound \(r\ge(a^2+b^2)/(4a)\). The half-diagonal centers achieve equality because their two horizontal arc half-widths sum to at least \(a/2\), by strict concavity of \(u\mapsto\sqrt{R^2-u^2}\). Saturation on the critical fiber forces the second center's vertical coordinate and, if \(a>b\), its horizontal coordinate; at \(a=b\), a neighboring-fiber limit fixes the horizontal coordinate. Strict interior concavity and endpoint equality establish the exact four-point (respectively two-point) deepest-hole locus. Algebraic differentiation proves unique density-optimal aspect ratio \(\sqrt3\). The accompanying checker corroborates the exact identities, contact points and a rational grid on nine shapes, but is not the universal proof.

## Originality
**PASS, provisional historical priority.** Joós (2019) proves the square endpoint only; the full 2026 covering-numbers article treats metric product coverings but not this exact Euclidean rectangular-torus two-center invariant. The separately inspected published result on well-rounded flat tori proves \(L/2\) for well-rounded lattices, which excludes *all* strict nonsquare rectangles: their systolic vectors span one dimension, so the former theorem cannot imply this radius, which strictly exceeds \(b/2\). Classical optimal planar triangular covering density gives one optimal value but neither the full profile nor uniqueness of centers. published-finding corpus index and targeted literature comparisons identified no equivalent all-aspect formula. Inaccessible or unindexed historical two-site periodic covering sources remain a real priority risk, not a claim of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The result completes a natural continuous 2-center metric covering problem on an entire anisotropic deformation family, with an unrestricted global lower bound, complete center rigidity and a sharp fixed-area aspect-ratio optimum. It extends the known square-only case and complements results for the well-rounded torus family. The exact \(\sqrt3\) minimizer connects the rectangular two-site torus lift to the classical triangular plane covering; the full exact profile and rigidity, not that classical planar constant, are the contribution.

## Main limitations
No assertion about arbitrary nonrectangular tori, three or more covering sites, finite-grid exhaustiveness, or independently confirmed historical priority. The published papers do not constitute an independent audit of this new proof.
