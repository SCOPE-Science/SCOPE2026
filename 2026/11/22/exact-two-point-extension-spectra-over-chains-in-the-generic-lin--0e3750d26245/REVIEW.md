# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns complete injective two-point types over a pointwise named chain in the homogeneous Fraïssé limit of finite posets with a distinguished linear extension. The proof separately classifies two possible partial-order relationships for a fixed orientation of the distinguished linear order. Cut monotonicity gives the comparable channel; exclusion of an old-vertex path gives the incomparable channel. Each is an exact finite double sum. Both sums are polynomials of degree at most six, so verification of their factorisations at seven exact integer arguments establishes every nonnegative size. The direct transitivity enumerator independently checks all insertion orders through seven named points, while the symbolic finite sums are checked through sixteen. The calculations and standalone reproducibility instructions are in `RESULT.md`, `VERIFICATION.md`, and `verification/check_lo_poset.py`.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
Dolinka–Mašulović identify the generic linearly ordered poset and the homogeneous linear extension, but do not give this finite-base pair-type enumeration. Sokić studies Ramsey/ordering properties, not pair-type spectra. The published chain-base order-polynomial enumeration concerns the *reduct without a named linear order* and is degree four; its polynomial does not determine the present degree-six two-channel counts. Earlier work on the ordered-poset expansion over *named antichains* has exponential dependence on base size and limiting comparable proportion \(3/7\), not a formula over chains. The public published-finding corpus antichain calculation in the unexpanded random poset also concerns a different language. The comparable channel's numerical values agree with the previously known OEIS A006542; no novelty of that integer sequence is asserted. Searches of published records, own available previous claims, and indexed literature did not uncover the joint linearly ordered chain-base formula and the monotone \(5/14\) law. A prior equivalent count under order-polynomial or linear-extension terminology remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The named-chain base is the elementary test configuration for finite extensions of the homogeneous linear extension of the random poset. The result gives the exact distribution between two distinct partial-order channels for all chain lengths; the \(5/14\) limit differs from the \(1/2\) limit for unexpanded random-poset chain extensions and the \(3/7\) limit for named antichains in the same ordered expansion. The strict finite-size monotonicity and asymptotic pair-to-squared-unary ratio \(7/5\) are structural rather than arbitrary table entries. The result is useful for testing and calibrating further finite-base orbit counting in ordered homogeneous posets.
