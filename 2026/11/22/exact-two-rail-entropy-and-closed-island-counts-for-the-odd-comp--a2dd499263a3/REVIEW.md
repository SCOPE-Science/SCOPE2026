# Scientific review: two-rail odd-component entropy

**Correctness — PASS.** The seven frontier states encode exactly the at-most-one component meeting a width-two vertical cut, including parity of its accumulated occupied sites. Closing a component is allowed only at odd parity, and the transfer matrix gives finite-ladder counts with zero boundaries. Row-interchange symmetry yields the five-state matrix and exact determinant/resolvent. Primitivity gives strict Perron growth. The inequalities \(a_n\le |\mathcal L_n(X_2)|\le a_{n+2}\) follow by adding at most one boundary column on either side to resolve any open components, and give the entropy formula without equating finite-end counts with actual shift words. The complete independent enumeration at lengths zero through eight matched the exact count matrix.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** Callard's 2025 thesis gives the odd-component definition and planar soficity, not its two-row entropy or its rational closed-island generating function. The subsequent high-dimensional nonsoficity theorem does not cover this finite-width count. The closest other indexed results involve different local constraints on ladders. Full historical priority remains uncertain because Cassaigne's 2010 unpublished source was inaccessible and a differently named older transfer table might exist.

**Value — PASS.** The first nontrivial transverse strip is the most natural quantitative boundary case between one-dimensional odd-run constraints and the full geometric odd-island problem. Both its exact entropy and its all-length finite-island counts are obtained in one theorem, supplying a reproducible algebraic benchmark for finite-width symbolic dynamics. No full-plane entropy or broad classification is asserted.

The verified public-defense date of the motivating 2025 thesis is June 24; its accessible document's later version date is August 16. The theorem's claim is independent of when that manuscript was uploaded. All analytic statements are proved; finite checks are additional evidence, not infinite certification.
