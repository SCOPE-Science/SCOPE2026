# Scientific review: exact two-shell Fourier energy and trace cancellation

## Correctness — PASS

The statement is one integrated all-even-dimensional theorem: the full ordered-pair convolution for two nonzero quadratic levels, its exact fourth Fourier moment and its average over the second radius. The proof gives the quadratic form's determinant-sign normalization, includes \(d=2\), both Witt classes, odd extension fields, the isotropic and zero-vector cases, and the exceptional singular quartic ratios. The key independent calculations are (i) the odd-dimensional quadratic level count after orthogonal projection for nonisotropic sum vectors, (ii) the hyperbolic-plane decomposition at nonzero isotropic sum vectors, (iii) the three-variable quadratic-character-square identity, and (iv) the character-sum interchange that makes the elliptic trace total independent of the size of the field except through \(\chi(-3)\). Exact direct-convolution checks in 17 cases and the radius-family trace checks agree. Enumeration alone is not the all-field proof; the complete symbolic argument appears in RESULT.md.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

The 2026 Pham–Xue single-sphere sharp extension theorem studies a different fourth-moment optimization, not the exact two-radius indicator energy and trace sum. Brittenham–Pakianathan and their predecessors develop general Euclidean association-scheme intersection numbers, which imply the foundational mixed two-sphere convolution; this underlying component is **not** claimed as new. A prior odd-dimensional two-radius fourth-moment classification does not cover even-dimensional determinant signs or a fixed-radius elliptic-trace sum. Searches of published mathematical records for two shells, fourth moments, equivalent character sums, genus-one trace averaging and association-scheme tables found no matching averaged two-radius law. Crucially, the proposed main contribution is the exact mean-energy and three-valued elliptic trace cancellation, not the title or existence of an association scheme. Incomplete indexing and possible hidden consequences in general intersection-number tables remain real originality risks.

## Value — PASS

A uniform two-radius sphere sampler is a natural finite-field Fourier aperture and a symmetric random-walk step distribution. Its exact fourth moment quantifies four-step return probability and constant-coefficient extension energy. Individual radius pairs have nontrivial elliptic trace fluctuations; averaging over an entire nonzero second-radius family annihilates the growing part of those fluctuations and leaves only three arithmetic possibilities governed by \(\chi(-3)\). This is a structural average law valid simultaneously in every even dimension, not an arbitrary small-parameter tabulation or a cosmetic rewriting of a single-radius estimate.
