# Self-review

## Correctness (PASS)
For every prime-power field and dimension at least two, the proof classifies all possible transvection pairs by the scalar interactions \(\varphi(v)\) and \(\psi(u)\). Nonzero product of these scalars gives a unique cyclic degree-two companion class per parameter and has exactly \(q^{2n-3}\) second factors. Zero product separates the identity, rank-one transvection, rank-two index-three unipotent, and rank-two square-zero unipotent cases, with explicit disjoint enumerations. The numerators sum algebraically to the full transvection class size. The prescribed-target formula follows from conjugation invariance and standard centralizer formulas; its quantifiers and the characteristic-two and smallest-dimension boundaries are specified. The independent finite-field checker exhaustively passed fifteen fixed-first-factor cases, and a second complete all-pairs target-fiber checker passed five cases, including extension fields and the boundary \((q,n)=(2,2)\). The proof, rather than this enumeration, establishes the unbounded statement.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)
The closest full-text primary comparison is Adan-Bante–Harris (2009, arXiv:0904.2152), which proves only lower bounds on the number of classes in a product of arbitrary noncentral classes. Hildebrand (1992) develops random-transvection mixing and character methods without the evaluated two-step, GL-conjugacy table. Diaconis–Ram–Simper (2022) establish projected Bruhat-double-coset transition laws rather than the original group conjugacy distribution. A closely related braid-identity count for transvection pairs imposes \(XYX=YXY\), whereas an unrestricted product census requires all ordered pairs; the restricted count cannot imply these weights. A semantic mathematical-record search found no direct equivalent, but nonretrieval does not prove novelty. Risk: the exact elementary two-factor coefficient table might occur in an older unindexed character-table or random-walk calculation; other unindexed comparisons may remain.

## Value (PASS)
This is a natural, complete multiplication structure-constant problem for the generating transvection conjugacy class of \(\mathrm{SL}_n(q)\), not an arbitrary parametric slice. It resolves all similarity types and prescribed-output factorization multiplicities at the first nontrivial convolution time, across all field characteristics and dimensions. The support count is sharper than the general published class-product lower bound and the constant nonzero-trace weights and high-dimensional limiting type masses expose concrete features not specified by mixing-time estimates.
