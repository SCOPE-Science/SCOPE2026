# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The quantification is over every integer \(n\ge1\), with one well-defined complete multiset observation. The unrestricted uniqueness cases are the published theorems of Acharya et al., verified in the complete 2014 public manuscript. The three genuinely extra constrained cases are finite and exhausted twice, with independently derived observational keys: \(55\), \(233\), and \(1597\) words, all partitioned into singleton or reversal-pair classes. The arithmetic factor lemma and explicit zero-boundary interleaving cover every other length; the interleaving identity follows from a substrings-by-endpoint-type bijection. Two independent scripts and an independent family sanity check were executed from the supplied sources. Residual risk: correctness of the stated foundational unrestricted theorem is used, not reproved.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The unrestricted prime/twice-prime/length-seven result, algebraic formulation, and interleaving construction are existing literature and explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. The closest published full binary census through length \(23\) would yield the three extra finite cases after filtering, and the length-fourteen census overlaps a single positive example, but neither supplies an all-length criterion for the adjacency-constrained source or the boundary-safe factorization dichotomy. Direct statement and implication comparisons with the complete primary 2014 exposition and these closest published expositions did not reveal prior coverage of the full constrained classification. Residual priority risks include unindexed work and easy derivability when combining general interleaving with a finite census; novelty is qualified rather than certified as globally unique.

## Value — PASS
The criterion gives an exact, natural all-length universality phase diagram for reconstruction from full substring compositions under the standard Fibonacci-source constraint. It identifies the precisely three lengths where the constraint changes a worst-case information-theoretic capability, and proves a concrete counterexample in every other length. This is stronger than a small finite census and directly separates a constraint-enabled reconstruction gain from asymptotically unchanged universal behavior. It does not claim a new asymptotic channel capacity or a fast reconstruction algorithm.

## Scientific limits
The proof uses the existing unrestricted reconstructability theorem as a premise. The finite exceptional checks are exhaustive within their stated domains, while the all-length negative side is symbolic; no experimental upper bound is promoted to an infinite proof. Historical originality remains subject to prior literature beyond the inspected sources.
