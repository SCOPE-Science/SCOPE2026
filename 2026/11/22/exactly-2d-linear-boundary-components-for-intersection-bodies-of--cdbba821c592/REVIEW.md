# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is for every integer \(d\ge2\), with Euclidean central-section normalization. The coarea formula identifies the radial boundary with a density of independent uniform sums, while an exact truncated-power formula produces rational boundary patches in every generic chamber. Polynomial unique factorization forces a planar patch to have a coordinate normal. Positivity of the remaining sum's density prevents any non-coordinate flat patch. The semialgebraic dimension/irreducible rational-image step ensures that the argument classifies algebraic **components**, not merely visible facets. Facet equality and affine covariance are explicit in the proof. Finite diagnostic checks passed, but the all-dimensional statement depends on the symbolic proof.

## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)

Berlow, Brandenburg, Meroni and Shankar, *Intersection Bodies of Polytopes* (arXiv:2110.05996, Section 6), established at least \(2d\) linear components and expressly conjectured exactly \(2d\). Their 2022 correction fixes normalization, not the gap. Brandenburg–Meroni's later translation paper concerns convexity after shifting the center rather than the all-dimensional component count. Related indexed results on smooth-prism axial facets and central cube-section zonotope criteria do not give this exact cube theorem. Full relevant text of the leading primary articles was compared, while some indexed findings were available only in summary form. No unindexed equivalent theorem can be categorically excluded.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem settles an explicitly stated geometric tomography conjecture uniformly in dimension and classifies every Euclidean flat facet as a coordinate crosspolytope. The method provides a structural reason no other hyperplane component is possible, whereas finite enumerations only gave evidence through dimension five. The centered-parallelotope consequence follows naturally by affine covariance, rather than being a separate unrelated claim.
