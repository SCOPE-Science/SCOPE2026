# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof defines the exact allowed-window set, constructs its 36-edge overlap graph, and exhibits a reversible 18-vertex degree-one suppression into the directed triangular prism. Kirchhoff's eigenvalue calculation yields 75 spanning trees; BEST supplies the factor \(2^6\), with rotations normalized by a fixed unique arc. Independent exact scripts check all 4,800 reconstructed tours and all 126 possible spanning-tree subsets. The proof handles every admissible cycle; it is not an empirical extrapolation.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Champlin–Godbole–Tomlinson's 2013 theorem proves universal-cycle *existence* for the broader non-injective four-letter language; it does not determine the exact count for words with exactly three distinct symbols. The 2020 study of random word deletions does not settle this content-specific problem. The closest indexed studies count four-letter cycles on exactly two distinct symbols or on the strict adjacent-double subset, neither of which implies the three-distinct-symbol total. Focused searches for 36-window ternary cycles, triangular-prism reductions and the number 4,800 yielded no exact counterpart. Some related indexed full texts could not be inspected, so an earlier equivalent derivation remains a real priority risk, not a proof of uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** Restricting length-four windows by the number of distinct letters gives a natural exact-content version of the universal-cycle problem. The 36-word ternary case is the smallest nontrivial mixed equality-pattern class; unlike the larger non-injective class it has a compact prism quotient whose spanning-tree count determines all rotational cycles. The result provides an exact benchmark for cyclic constrained sequence generation and demonstrates the effect of forced overlap corridors.

**Limitations.** No larger-alphabet enumeration, equivalence under alphabet relabeling or reversal, or exhaustive independent literature priority check is claimed.
