# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem quantifies over every real \(\beta>0\). Its large-parameter proof uses an exact positive theta-product logarithmic derivative, the strictly decreasing Gaussian-derivative envelope on \([1/2,\infty)\), an explicit uniformly dominated trigonometric tail at the symmetry line, and exact Zak reflection. Fourier duality reduces all smaller parameters to the proven regime and permutes the three parity zeros. The tail is bounded by a convergent geometric-moment sum below one, not by truncated numerical approximation. Boundary fibers and the half-open fundamental domain are handled explicitly. A standalone script probes identities on a finite grid but does not replace any analytic step.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature
Faulhuber (arXiv:2502.09510, Sections 3–4, Conjecture 4.1) explicitly asks whether generalized first-Hermite Zak transforms have only three zeros. That conjecture includes dilution/dilation and chirp. The new theorem establishes the full unchirped dilation slice \(s=0\), which is not stated as proven in the source. Other related indexed results treat the quadratic B-spline, Gaussian/first-Hermite mixtures, or common Zak-channel zeros—not the global zero set of pure first-Hermite dilates. A historical unindexed proof remains the principal residual originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Exact global zero enumeration over a continuous scale family answers a naturally isolated case of an explicit recent open question. Its significance is not a toy computation: Zak zeros control non-frame obstructions for periodically structured Gabor sampling, and exact absence of further zeros is stronger than identifying symmetry-forced zeros or one prescribed lattice failure. The full chirped conjecture remains unresolved.
