# Mathematical review of the exceptional three-cycle quandle second-homology dimensions

## Correctness

PASS. The finite chain complex is completely defined by the normalized quandle differentials. The attached standalone certificate rebuilds the conjugation tables and enumerates every normalized triple, checking chain identities and exact modular ranks. In both exceptional degrees, the transitive conjugation action proves the exact rank of the second differential. The universal coefficient theorem, together with torsion-free first quandle homology for connected quandles, gives precisely the asserted integral primary-generator counts. No assertion about prime-power exponents is made.

## Originality

PASS, subject to the priority limitations below. The 11 November 2026 primary paper explicitly excludes odd-cycle quandles on six and seven letters from its classification, and its representation-group argument is not applicable as stated there. An extrapolation of its formula is not a theorem in the excluded cases. The 2018 Schur-multiplier paper and the Rig small-quandle database concern relevant general machinery but the inspected materials do not identify the exact four exceptional dimensions. Dedicated searches for these statements and conjugacy/dual descriptions found no covering indexed result. This does not prove worldwide uniqueness, and full text of every secondary source was not available.

## Mathematical value

PASS. These are naturally singled-out exceptional cases in an actively developed second-quandle-homology classification, not arbitrarily chosen sizes. The two independent characteristic-three classes in each case and the characteristic-two distinction give exact obstruction/cocycle dimensions and primary-generator information in the excluded range; either could inform explicit finite-quandle knot invariants or extensions. A complete integral classification would be stronger, but is not required for the precise finite coefficient invariant.

## Closest literature and limits

Maassarani (2026), Section 2.3, handles odd-cycle conjugacy quandles outside six and seven letters and excludes both degrees explicitly. Bakshi et al. (2018) studies quandle homology and Schur multipliers, particularly Alexander cases, without an identified matching exceptional table in inspected material. Rig lists connected quandles below 48 and provides homology procedures; the possibility of an unpublished 40-element calculation or a pre-existing but unindexed computation remains. The full invariant-factor groups, other prime characteristics, torsion orders and higher degrees remain unproved.
