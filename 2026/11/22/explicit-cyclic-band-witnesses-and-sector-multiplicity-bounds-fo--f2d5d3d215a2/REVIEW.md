# Review of an explicit cyclic-band matching construction

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem's domain is all rectangular toroidal honeycomb matchings with both periods at least three and the indicated cell map. The mod-\(MN\) homomorphism has constant fibers of size \(g\). The mutually inverse predecessor rules preserve each band and make the global map bijective; exact fiber counts prove the two directional totals. Choosing nonoverlapping bands or shifting a proper arc gives injective families of configurations and establishes both lower bounds. The nearest-neighbor spin conclusion uses the published parity-reconstruction correspondence, not an additional physical assumption. The embedded checker verifies 60,438 constructed sectors and 16,900 independent determinant coefficients, including boundary cases. Finite verification supplements but does not replace the argument.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Jiang and Hou's 2026 Appendix A.3 provides the general exact coefficient algorithm and boundary counts but explicitly does not settle positive support at all interior slopes. Kenyon–Okounkov–Sheffield (2006) establishes the Newton polygon of allowed *translation-invariant measure slopes*, not a prescribed integer-sector matching on a fixed finite torus. Bao–He–Zahabi (2022) broadly associate Newton-polygon lattice points to perfect-matching fields, so bare existential saturation may be an established consequence in general toric-dimer theory. The present claim therefore specifically includes a direct \(O(MN)\) integer band algorithm and two quantitative multiplicity lower bounds, neither located in the inspected primary statements. The possibility of equivalent earlier constructive methods remains a real limitation.

**Value — PASS.** The result makes the exact coefficient nonvanishing question highlighted in the 2026 finite-torus dimer study mechanically decidable without determinant evaluation, produces witnesses for all admissible rectangular size/occupation parameters, yields nontrivial guaranteed sector degeneracy and directly identifies all realizable physical Ising ground-state slope sectors. This is not a mere renaming of known boundary cases or a finite table. It does not purport to count all interior configurations or determine finite-temperature properties.
