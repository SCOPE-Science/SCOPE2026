# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact grading follows from the ultrametric extension formula \(f(a)=\max\{j,d(a,b)\}\), bijective with radius-\(j\) balls. The counting construction distinguishes isometry types by a set of \(m\) different partitions of a common radius-two ball size, and the complete graded invariant records only the globally aggregated radius-one sizes once radius-two sizes are fixed. The counting inequalities \(p(t)\ge2^k\ge n^2\), \(\binom{p(t)}m\ge n^m\), and \(p(n)\le\exp(\pi\sqrt{2n/3})\) are each proved, giving the claimed lower rate. Direct distance-vector enumeration verifies the formula through seven old points. The finite checks are ancillary and not invoked to prove the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty
The nearest-ball extension dictionary, dendrogram formalism, ungraded singleton-type counts, higher-arity count dependence on levelwise cluster numbers, and a five-point hidden-nesting collision are established background. The published one-round colour-refinement result uses a strictly richer per-vertex invariant and has an eight-point first collision, rather than a bound for graded nearest-ball marginals. The published verbose Rips barcode five-point example also does not give an all-size fiber rate. The new retained claim is the explicit \(\exp(\Omega(n/\log n))\) lower bound for fibers of the complete graded singleton-type polynomial, not the earlier polynomial formula or finite collisions. Indexed result searches and relevant primary/source documents identified no dominating quantitative statement. Unindexed literature on tree-profile multiplicities remains a genuine risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem resolves a quantitative logical-spectrum reconstruction question: retaining all radius-wise ball cardinalities still admits rapidly increasing non-isometry fibers. It separates the strength of a natural parameter-type distribution from the full nested dendrogram structure. The bound has an explicit construction, a computable all-size inequality and a self-contained partition-estimate argument; it is substantially more informative than one isolated collision.

## Verification and limitations
The standalone checker and output verify all enumerated instances but not mathematical priority or the asymptotic inference. The proof of the asymptotic rate is independent of the computations. The bound is one-sided; no exact fiber maximum or matching exponent is claimed. The setting is the fixed three-distance universal homogeneous ultrametric.
