# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The exact finite-rank Riesz projection comes from a compact factorization, algebraically simple triangular eigenvalues, and a biorthogonal left/right eigenvector pair. The crucial nontrivial norm identity is proved by Hardy Parseval and a unit-circle automorphism; it yields an exact finite binomial expression without numerical interpolation. A nondegenerate unique Laplace maximum gives the fixed-positive-coupling expansion with its full prefactor and an \(O_t(m^{-1})\) remainder. Dominated convergence for the binomial series yields the \(I_0\) boundary layer, while the completeness argument uses analytic derivative evaluation at \(t\). Fixed-parameter eigenvector and coefficient identities were also tested by 2035 supplementary assertions. There is no inference of an infinite theorem from finite tests.

## Originality: PASS, with residual priority risk
The defining matrix, compactness, simple spectrum and right eigenvector formulas are established by Rhaly (1984) and Curbera–Ricker (2023); Albanese–Bonet–Ricker (2023) explicitly give the finite-support left eigenvectors too. Those are correctly cited and **not** presented as discoveries. The inspected primary articles establish spectral sets, eigenspaces and operator dynamics, but do not state this exact Hilbert-space Riesz projection norm, its sharp exponential prefactor, or the \(I_0\) transition. Targeted searches for projection norms, biorthogonal bases and spectral similarity, plus public-record comparisons, found no theorem that implies this full claim. A possible equivalent general binomial-transform/spectral-conditioning theorem in unindexed literature remains the principal priority uncertainty; lack of a search hit is not treated as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value: PASS
Compactness plus an unchanged simple positive spectrum can conceal severe, exponentially growing spectral-instability constants. The exact projection norms provide those intrinsic constants for every eigenvalue and coupling; the Bessel boundary identifies the natural scaling \(m t\) at which stability is restored. The complete-minimal-but-never-Schauder conclusion is a qualitative obstruction to spectral decompositions and normal similarity. These facts sharpen a natural established spectral classification, rather than evaluating an arbitrary test case.

## Related literature and limits
Curbera–Ricker, arXiv:2302.08750, and Albanese–Bonet–Ricker, DOI:10.1007/s13398-023-01470-2, are the closest known exact eigenvector sources. Rhaly, DOI:10.4153/CMB-1984-064-5, treats the same matrices but not the stated Riesz norm asymptotics. The fixed-parameter asymptotic is not claimed uniform as coupling vanishes; the zero-coupling and Bessel regimes are separate. Independence and external review are not claimed.
