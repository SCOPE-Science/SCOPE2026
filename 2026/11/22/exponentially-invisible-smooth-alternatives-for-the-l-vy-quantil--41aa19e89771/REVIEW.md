# Scientific assessment

## Correctness (C): PASS

A global density maximum supplies a strict lower bound on the reference quantile derivative, while a trigonometric derivative extremum bounds the smooth perturbation. The ordered coupling is exact because both quantile maps are increasing; both rank-band sums coincide on a two-binomial-count event. Binomial large deviations establish the sharp exponent for the explicit coupling's disagreement probability, using the lower-excursion event to match the union-bound rate. The alternative CDF differs at a known interior point, enabling an independent consistent empirical-CDF test. The complete checker passed 24,271 supplementary assertions; the proof, not numerical experimentation, establishes the infinite-sample claims.

## Originality (O): PASS, with priority risk

The parent paper, Pączek et al., arXiv:2311.15359v1 (26 November 2023), and substantial indexed full-text sections of its 2024 journal version were checked for its test definition, null asymptotics, power tables and discussion. Its fixed-band statistic and scale-free calibration are pre-existing; the new result concerns the smooth nonnull deformation class, exponentially close *entire statistic laws*, and the exact rate of disagreement under an explicit rank-preserving coupling. The authors' earlier arXiv:2212.13502 concerns a different conditional-variance estimator. Related research records on quantiles, trimmed losses, extropy and signed-statistic blind sets address different objects and do not dominate this claim.

Classical Chernoff/Hoeffding/Stirling methods are pre-existing and are not offered as novel. An older general theory of unselected rank-band perturbations might cover some of the mechanism; priority cannot be proved from finite literature searches. A direct browser challenge prevented a second complete inspection of the 2024 journal rendering, although its major passages were available and the 2023 preprint was inspected throughout.

## Value (V): PASS

The source intentionally uses separated tail intervals to test a heavy-tailed null. The finding shows that this leaves an interior region where a whole class of smooth and fully detectable alternatives is invisible to *every* test using this single statistic, with an explicit finite-sample total-variation error and an exact information-theoretic error exponent for the constructed coupling. This supplies a concrete coverage limitation rather than a mere algebraic cancellation. It suggests combining the statistic with central-distribution summaries when consistency against all fixed alternatives is required; such combination is not analyzed here.

## Limits of review

The theorem does not challenge the source's reported null CLT or its empirical powers for the particular alternatives studied. No lower bound on the total-variation distance itself is proved, and the result does not apply to the authors' ensemble statistic or location-invariant procedure. The 2024 journal source was only partially inspectable directly, and historical priority remains provisional.
