# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The new weak-binding law follows from an exact \(\sqrt n\) ground-state transform, a proved massive capacity asymptotic \(C_m(\lambda)\log(1/\lambda)\to2\), convergence of every fixed finite-support defect profile to its common ground-state amplitude, and Birman–Schwinger inversion. Finite-support Cauchy–Schwarz and min–max establish weak-coupling uniqueness of the bound state. Finite numeric checks are supplementary; the logarithmic limit and spectral cardinality do not depend on numerical extrapolation.

The known optimal Hardy weight and criticality are explicitly credited to Keller–Pinchover–Pogorzelski. Krejčiřík–Štampach's remainder identity is not new here. Gerhat–Krejčiřík–Štampach treat different powers and localized bilaplacian perturbations; Damanik–Teschl treat supercritical inverse-square eigenvalue counting. The checked primary texts and relevant indexed record summaries did not yield the explicit logarithmic coefficient for arbitrary finite defects of this canonical critical operator or the eventual single-eigenvalue statement. An implicit consequence of broader weak-coupling theory may exist and remains an acknowledged residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The optimal Hardy weight sits precisely at loss of nonnegative-form robustness; the result quantifies how a compact perturbation creates a negative mode despite arbitrarily small coupling. The exact exponential coefficient depends only on the ground-state-weighted total defect mass rather than its spatial arrangement. The bound-state-count conclusion distinguishes this critical regime from supercritical inverse-square accumulation, giving a complete natural weak-coupling classification for finite nonnegative defects.
