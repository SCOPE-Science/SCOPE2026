# Extremal anticanonical torus sections on the degree-seven del Pezzo surface

## Finding
Let \(q\ge 5\) be any prime power, put \(k=\mathbb F_q\) and \(t=q-1\), and blow up two distinct torus-fixed points of \(\mathbb P^2_k\). The result is the split smooth degree-seven toric del Pezzo surface \(X\), anticanonically embedded in \(\mathbb P^7\). On its dense torus, choose coordinates in which the eight anticanonical monomials have exponent set
\[
E=\{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1)\}.
\]
Let \(C_q\) be their evaluation code on \(T=(k^\times)^2\). Its complete generalized Hamming-weight hierarchy is
\[
\bigl(d_1,\ldots,d_8\bigr)=
\bigl(t^2-3t+2,\ t^2-2t,\ t^2-2t+1,\ t^2-t-1,\ t^2-t,\ t^2-2,\ t^2-1,\ t^2\bigr).
\]
More particularly, **every** four-dimensional subcode attaining \(d_4\) has as its common-zero set a complete coordinate torus fiber together with precisely one point outside that fiber; **every** five-dimensional subcode attaining \(d_5\) has as its common-zero set precisely one complete coordinate torus fiber. Conversely, each such point set determines one optimal subcode. Consequently the exact numbers of minimizing subcodes are
\[
N_4=2t^2(t-1),\qquad N_5=2t.
\]
These are two distinct families of ruling fibers: the coordinate equations \(x=a\) and \(y=b\), with \(a,b\in k^\times\). In terms of the anticanonical embedding, the conclusion classifies the rational projective \(3\)-spaces containing the maximum \(t+1\) torus points and the rational projective \(2\)-planes containing the maximum \(t\) torus points. The originality asserted here is the **all-field equality-case geometry and exact extremizer multiplicities**, not the general footprint method or a new theory of generalized weights.

## Assumptions and scope
The surface is the blowup of \(\mathbb P^2\) at \([1:0:0]\) and \([0:1:0]\). Its anticanonical sections are degree-three homogeneous forms vanishing at those points; on the chart \(Z=1\) their torus restrictions have exponent set \(E\), namely the degree-at-most-three triangle minus the pure cubics \(x^3\) and \(y^3\). This convex lattice pentagon has anticanonical self-intersection \(7\). Its monomial space is
\[
V=\langle1,x,x^2,y,xy,x^2y,y^2,xy^2\rangle_k,
\qquad \dim_k V=8.
\]
All zeros in this statement are distinct \(k\)-rational **dense-torus** points, not toric-boundary points or geometric intersection multiplicities. Since both coordinate degrees are at most \(2<t\), evaluation on \(T\) is injective. For \(1\le r\le8\), \(d_r=t^2-z_r\), where \(z_r\) is the largest number of common torus zeros of an \(r\)-dimensional subspace of \(V\). An optimal subcode is counted once as a vector subspace, not once per basis.

## Proof
**1. Generalized-weight bounds and attainment.** Write \(B_t=\{0,\ldots,t-1\}^2\). For any \(r\)-dimensional subspace \(W\subset V\), row-reduce a basis with a fixed monomial order. Its \(r\) distinct leading exponent vectors form a subset \(S\subset E\) of size \(r\). The footprint bound for the ideal generated by those polynomials and \(x^t-1,y^t-1\) gives
\[
|Z_T(W)|\le t^2-|\nabla_t(S)|,\qquad
\nabla_t(S)=\{(i,j)\in B_t:\exists(a,b)\in S,\ i\ge a,\ j\ge b\}.
\]
Conversely, order the \(t\) nonzero field elements independently in both coordinates. For every \((a,b)\in E\), set
\[
F_{a,b}(x,y)=\prod_{i=1}^{a}(x-\alpha_i)
                         \prod_{j=1}^{b}(y-\beta_j).
\]
Because \(E\) is downward closed, all these polynomials belong to \(V\). For any fixed \(S\subset E\), the \(F_{a,b}\) with \((a,b)\in S\) are independent (distinct leading monomials); their common-zero set has exactly \(t^2-|\nabla_t(S)|\) points. Therefore the footprint optimization is sharp, for **every** prime power in scope.

Inclusion-exclusion gives the exact polynomial
\[
|\nabla_t(S)|=
\sum_{\varnothing\ne U\subseteq S}(-1)^{|U|+1}
 (t-\max_{(a,b)\in U}a)(t-\max_{(a,b)\in U}b).
\]
The finite set \(E\) has eight elements, so there are precisely \(2^8-1=255\) nonempty candidate sets. The standalone exact-integer checker `check.py` evaluates the three coefficients of each such polynomial, verifies a nonnegative-coefficient certificate after substituting \(t=s+4\), \(s\ge0\), against the claimed minimum for its cardinality, and supplies an attaining set in every cardinality. The resulting minimum shadow polynomials for \(r=1,\ldots,8\) are, respectively,
\[
t^2-3t+2,\quad t^2-2t,\quad t^2-2t+1,\quad
t^2-t-1,\quad t^2-t,\quad t^2-2,\quad t^2-1,\quad t^2.
\]
These are the displayed generalized weights, and in particular \(z_4=t+1\) and \(z_5=t\). The integer classification is exhaustive by the direct reduction from an arbitrary \(r\)-subspace to an \(r\)-element leading-exponent subset. The finite checker is a certificate for the only finite combinatorial optimization; the preceding footprint argument proves the claim for all prime powers.

**2. Low-rank interpolation lemmas.** For \(P=(a,b)\in T\), let \(e(P)\in V^*\) be evaluation at \(P\), represented by the vector of the eight monomials. The subspace \(Q=\langle1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2\rangle\subset V\) is the quadratic Veronese space. Four distinct affine points have dependent evaluations on \(Q\) only when all four are collinear. Similarly, five distinct affine points have dependent evaluations on \(Q\) only if at least four are collinear. For completeness: if no four of five are collinear, then for any distinguished point \(P\), pair the other four into two pairs whose joining lines avoid \(P\); this is possible because no direction from \(P\) contains three of the other four. The product of the two line equations is a quadratic vanishing at the other four but not \(P\). These five separating quadratics prove independence. The analogous product-of-lines construction for four points proves the first assertion.

If four points lie on an affine line \(L\) on which both \(x\) and \(y\) vary, parameterize it as \(x=a+us\), \(y=b+vs\) with \(uv\ne0\). The restrictions of \(Q\) span all polynomials of degree at most two in \(s\). Moreover \(x^2y\in V\) restricts to a cubic with leading coefficient \(u^2v\ne0\). Hence the eight sections separate the four points independently by a degree-three Vandermonde matrix: their evaluations on \(V\) have rank four. An additional point off \(L\) increases rank, since the linear equation of \(L\), a member of \(V\), vanishes at the four but not at the extra point.

**3. All optimal five-dimensional subcodes.** Let \(S\subset T\) have \(|S|\ge t\ge4\) and evaluation rank at most three. Any four of its points must be collinear by the quadratic lemma. Such a line cannot have both coordinates varying, by the cubic restriction. Therefore four points lie on an axis-parallel line \(L\), one of \(x=a\) or \(y=b\). Their evaluation span has rank exactly three, because the restriction of \(V\) to \(L\) is the full degree-at-most-two univariate space. Every off-line point increases rank (the linear equation of \(L\) separates it). Hence \(S\subset L\) and \(|S|\le t\). Equality forces \(S=L\cap T\). Conversely \(V\) restricted to a complete axis fiber has rank three, so the kernel of restriction is a unique five-dimensional subspace with exactly that common-zero set. There are \(t\) fibers in each of the two directions, hence \(N_5=2t\).

**4. All optimal four-dimensional subcodes.** Let \(S\subset T\) have \(|S|\ge t+1\ge5\) and evaluation rank at most four. Any five points have dependent quadratic evaluations, hence four lie on an affine line \(L\). If both coordinates vary on \(L\), those four already have rank four on \(V\), and every point off \(L\) increases rank. Since \(|L\cap T|\le t\), this cannot account for \(|S|\ge t+1\). Thus \(L\) is an axis-parallel fiber. Four points on it have rank three on \(V\).

Suppose \(L\) is \(y=b\) (the other case is symmetric). The sections vanishing on those four points are exactly the sections divisible by \(y-b\): the restriction is a polynomial in \(x\) of degree at most two, and four distinct roots force it to vanish identically. Division gives a five-dimensional residual polynomial space whose exponent set is
\[
E\cap(E-(0,1))=\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}.
\]
It contains \(1,x,y\). Thus evaluation at two different points outside \(L\) imposes **two independent** conditions on this residual space: after division by the nonzero factors \(y-b\), the two evaluation vectors cannot be proportional, because the coordinates \(1,x,y\) distinguish them. Consequently at most one point of \(S\) lies outside \(L\). Our size bound now forces \(S\) to be the complete fiber and exactly one off-fiber point.

Conversely any \(L\cap T\) and any \(P\in T\setminus L\) impose rank \(3+1=4\) on \(V\), and their annihilator is a unique four-dimensional subspace. Their common zeros cannot contain additional points, by the two-off-fiber independence just proved. The fiber is uniquely determined from the set (two distinct axis fibers meet in at most one point), so counting choices gives
\[
N_4=(2t)(t^2-t)=2t^2(t-1).
\]
This concludes both extremizer classifications and the supporting weight hierarchy. \(\square\)

## Verification
Run `python3 check.py` with Python 3 and no third-party packages. The program certifies all 255 nonempty exponent subsets by exact integer inclusion-exclusion and a coefficientwise nonnegativity test for every integer \(t\ge4\). It independently enumerates all rank-three and rank-four evaluation flats over \(\mathbb F_5\) and \(\mathbb F_7\), using exact modular Gauss-Jordan reduction. The observed optimizer counts are \((N_4,N_5)=(96,8)\) over \(\mathbb F_5\) and \((360,12)\) over \(\mathbb F_7\). The output is preserved as `check_output.txt`. Field checks are corroboration; the interpolation argument proves equality-case classification for every prime power \(q\ge5\).

## Relationship to prior work
Little–Schwarz (2005) provides the toric monomial evaluation framework and lattice-equivalence results; Little–Schenck (2005) gives minimum-distance techniques using Minkowski sums. Neither determines the high-rank minimizing sections of this degree-seven anticanonical pentagon. Boguslavskii (1998) studies generalized weights of codes evaluated on **all** rational points of complete split del Pezzo surfaces: the degree-seven block length there is \(q^2+3q+1\), whereas here it is \((q-1)^2\). Beelen–Datta (2017) treats complete total-degree-bounded Cartesian spaces, whereas \(E\) omits two degree-three corner monomials. Camps–López–Matthews–Sarmiento (2020) defines the more general decreasing monomial-Cartesian class and determines minimum distance, not the present optimizer multiplicities. General footprint and hyperbolic-code work provides methods for weights but does not directly assert the **classification of every equality case** for these projective torus sections.

A related explicit cubic-scroll computation determines \(t\) optimal ruling lines and \(t^2(t-1)\) optimal larger linear sections for the five-monomial scroll \(S(1,2)\). This is not the degree-seven del Pezzo surface: its embedded point set has one ruling family rather than the two independent axis families arising here. Related degree-six del Pezzo work treats a seven-monomial *hexagon* and its second generalized weight; quartic-scroll work treats a six-monomial *trapezoid*. Neither covers the present eight-monomial pentagon or its two-family classification. The generic footprint principle and previously studied generalized weights are not presented as independent new theorems.

## Limitations
The classified objects are subcodes of the **dense-torus** anticanonical evaluation code; the numbers are not the weights or extremizer counts of the code evaluating at every rational point of the del Pezzo surface. No claim is made about all minimizing subcodes of dimensions one, two, three, six, seven, or eight, beyond the displayed generalized weights. No complete support enumerator is provided. The distinct-extremizer counts use subspaces over \(\mathbb F_q\), not geometric equivalence classes under surface automorphisms. The exact claims are proved internally but have not received independent specialist validation. Literature priority risk remains for unindexed or unpublished classifications of maximizing torus sections.
