# Facet-adjacency components classify first integral path homology of cyclic Hasse digraphs

## Finding

Let \(K\) be any finite, nonempty, pure two-dimensional simplicial complex with connected one-skeleton. Define \(\mathcal F(K)\) to be its set of triangular facets. Let \(\Gamma(K)\) be the graph on \(\mathcal F(K)\) in which two distinct triangles are adjacent exactly when they share an edge. Write \(c(K)=\pi_0(\Gamma(K))\) for its number of connected components. The cyclic Hasse digraph \(G_K\), defined below, satisfies

\[
H_1^{\mathrm{path}}(G_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{c(K)},\qquad H_0^{\mathrm{path}}(G_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

In fact, for **every abelian group** \(A\), the degree-one regular GLMY path cohomology has a canonical component-indexed description \(H^1_{\mathrm{path}}(G_K;A)\cong A^{\pi_0(\Gamma(K))}\). The component labels are canonical, although this description uses the specific cyclic-Hasse construction. The result is integral and has no first-homology torsion.

For \(r\ge1\), let \(K_r\) consist of \(r\) filled triangles with only a single common vertex and otherwise pairwise disjoint vertices. Then \(|K_r|\) is contractible while

\[
H_1^{\mathrm{path}}(G_{K_r};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^r.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

A pure two-dimensional finite simplicial complex has triangles as all maximal faces; thus every vertex and edge lies in at least one triangle. Its one-skeleton is assumed connected. For the vertices, edges and triangles of \(K\), use disjoint rank-labelled sets \(V,E,F\). The vertices of the directed graph \(G_K\) are \(V\sqcup E\sqcup F\), and its arrows are **exactly** \(v\to e\) for \(v\in e\), \(e\to f\) for \(e\subset f\), and \(f\to v\) for **every** pair \((f,v)\in F\times V\). There are no other arrows or loops. Thus every allowed two-path has one of the rank patterns \(F\to V\to E\), \(V\to E\to F\), or \(E\to F\to V\), and no direct shortcut from its starting vertex to its ending vertex.

Regular GLMY path chains have \(\Omega_0\) freely generated by digraph vertices, \(\Omega_1\) freely generated by arrows, and \(\Omega_2\) consisting of integral combinations of allowed two-paths whose alternating boundary is a combination of arrows. All paths in degrees zero, one and two used here have no consecutive repeated vertices. For coefficients in any abelian group \(A\), cochains are \(\operatorname{Hom}(\Omega_\bullet,A)\). All assertions concern **degree zero and degree one only**.

## Proof

**1. Describe degree-two relations integrally.** A directed two-path \((x,y,z)\) has formal boundary \((y,z)-(x,z)+(x,y)\). Since the endpoint shortcut \(x\to z\) is absent, a linear combination belongs to \(\Omega_2\) precisely when the sum of its coefficients is zero **separately for each ordered endpoint pair** \((x,z)\). Hence \(\Omega_2\) has a free integral basis of differences of two-paths having the same endpoints. An arrow-weight assignment is a first cocycle if and only if sums of its weights along any two allowed two-paths with the same ordered endpoints agree. This description holds over every abelian coefficient group, without divisions.

**2. Normalize all return-arrow weights.** Let \(A\) be an arbitrary abelian group. Name the weights of the three types of arcs \(a_{v,e}\) for \(v\to e\), \(b_{e,f}\) for \(e\to f\), and \(d_{f,v}\) for \(f\to v\). Fix any reference triangle \(f_0\). By adding a vertex-potential coboundary, arrange \(d_{f_0,v}=0\) for every vertex \(v\); this is possible because \(f_0\to v\) exists for **all** vertices.

For any edge \(e=\{u,w\}\), compare the two paths \(f_0\to u\to e\) and \(f_0\to w\to e\). Their cocycle sums imply \(a_{u,e}=a_{w,e}\); denote the common weight by \(a_e\). Now compare \(f\to u\to e\) and \(f\to w\to e\) for arbitrary \(f\). Their sums imply \(d_{f,u}=d_{f,w}\). Since the one-skeleton is connected, \(d_{f,v}\) is independent of \(v\); write it \(t_f\). We have \(t_{f_0}=0\).

**3. Identify precisely the remaining cocycle parameters.** For a vertex \(v\) of a triangle \(f\), compare the two paths \(v\to e\to f\) using the two triangle edges incident to \(v\). They give \(a_e+b_{e,f}\) equal for these two edges. Because the three edges of a triangle are linked by these comparisons, there exists \(q_f\in A\) such that \(b_{e,f}=q_f-a_e\) for all three edges \(e\subset f\).

If triangles \(f,g\) share an edge \(e\), compare the allowed two-paths \(e\to f\to v\) and \(e\to g\to v\) for any vertex \(v\). Since both facets have return arrows to every vertex, equality of sums yields \(q_f+t_f=q_g+t_g\). Consequently \(s_f:=q_f+t_f\) is constant on each connected component of \(\Gamma(K)\).

**4. Prove surjectivity and uniqueness, not merely an upper bound.** Add the potential whose values are zero on vertices, \(-a_e\) on edges, and \(t_f\) on triangles. Under the convention that a coboundary changes the weight of \(x\to y\) by \(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\), the normalized cocycle has zero weights on all vertex-to-edge arrows and facet-to-vertex arrows, and weight exactly \(s_f\) on \(e\to f\). Conversely, for any arbitrary assignment of elements of \(A\) to the connected components of \(\Gamma(K)\), setting \(a=d=0\) and \(b_{e,f}=s_{[f]}\) satisfies all three types of two-path comparisons. No nontrivial such assignment is a coboundary: if a potential preserves zero weights on vertex-to-edge arrows, connectedness of the one-skeleton forces all vertices and edges to have the same potential; preserving zero weights on every facet-to-vertex arrow forces all triangle potentials to have that same value, leaving all \(b\)-weights unchanged. Therefore \(H^1_{\mathrm{path}}(G_K;A)\cong A^{\pi_0(\Gamma(K))}\) for every abelian \(A\), and the identification is functorial in \(A\).

**5. Deduce integral path homology.** The graph is weakly connected, so \(H_0(G_K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\). The integral path chain groups are finite-rank free abelian groups (each is a subgroup of a finitely generated free abelian group). The universal coefficient sequence for this free chain complex, using freeness of \(H_0\), gives \(H^1(G_K;A)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(G_K;\mathbb Z),A)\) for every abelian \(A\). Since its left side is functorially \(A^{c(K)}\), the finitely generated abelian group \(H_1(G_K;\mathbb Z)\) has rank \(c(K)\) and no torsion (for example take \(A=\mathbb Q\) and all finite cyclic groups). This proves \(H_1(G_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{c(K)}\).

For a vertex-wedge of \(r\) filled triangles, \(\Gamma(K_r)\) has \(r\) isolated vertices and \(|K_r|\) is a wedge of disks, hence contractible. The stated arbitrary-rank separation follows.

## Verification

A standalone Python 3 consistency checker, `verify_hasse_h1.py`, constructs the complete directed graph for finite sample complexes, builds the integral length-two relations as differences of two-paths sharing endpoints, and independently computes the first homology dimension modulo primes \(2\), \(3\), and \(1000003\). It tests 57 connected pure two-complexes including a single triangle, triangles glued along an edge, triangles glued only at a vertex, three vertex-glued triangles, and a tetrahedron boundary. It reports 171 consistent checks. These tests are not an exhaustive classification and do not establish torsion-freeness; the preceding all-coefficient integral proof establishes the theorem.

Reproduction: `python3 verify_hasse_h1.py` (Python 3 standard library only). The code file is included in this package and does not need data downloads.

## Relationship to prior work

Ivanov and Zhang, *Cyclic Hasse diagrams of manifolds and noncommutative path cohomology* (arXiv:2611.19813v1, publicly posted 15 November 2026), introduce the same return-arrows-to-all-zero-cells construction. Their Theorem 6.3 assumes a Poincaré-duality pair of ideals, and Corollary 6.4 classifies cohomology of connected **closed oriented manifolds**. For a connected closed triangulated surface, its triangle-adjacency graph is connected, so the present degree-one formula agrees with their degree-one consequence. The new result addresses arbitrary connected **pure two-complexes**, including vertex-glued nonmanifolds whose facet-adjacency graph is disconnected and whose first path homology is unbounded despite contractible realization. The cited theorem does not give that extension without its additional hypotheses.

Chaplin, *First Betti number of the path homology of random directed graphs* (2022), supplies general two-path-difference relations, but studies first path homology for random digraphs; it does not state the closed formula in terms of connected components of the facet-adjacency graph. A result on multipath complexes of bidirected triangles is about a distinct chain complex, not the GLMY path homology of this cyclic face-poset digraph. No claim of exhaustive literature novelty is made.

## Limitations

The theorem requires finite, nonempty, pure dimension-two simplicial complexes with **connected** one-skeleton; the graph includes all return arrows from each triangle to every original vertex. It does not classify ordinary Hasse digraphs without returns, rank-nonpure face posets, disconnected complexes, higher path homology, or the graded path-cohomology multiplication. The computational tests cover only small complexes. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed, and unindexed equivalent results remain a publication-priority risk.
