# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact finite-dimensional semigroup kernel reduces the input-output response to a finite triangularly weighted Poisson sum. Below arrival, a uniform geometric-ratio argument, Stirling asymptotics, and endpoint Laplace integration produce the claimed \(h^4\) prefactor and rate. At arrival, a centered Poisson positive-part limit and integrable left-tail bound produce the \(h^{3/2}\) critical law; Gaussian integration verifies its explicit constant. Direct semigroup comparisons and asymptotic numerical checks independently test the algebra. The nonstandard limits are supported by arguments in RESULT.md rather than inferred from finite numerics.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with residual historical risk).** Inoue and Sato (arXiv:2610.28205), Remark 2.3, give the separated right-shift reachability example; Sections 5.2 and 5.4 show qualitatively that upwind diffusion can give false controllability. Their result does not supply the exact mesh-refinement Gramian exponent, prefactor, or critical Gaussian layer. Delarue and Lagoutière (arXiv:0712.3217) derive an upwind Markov-chain representation and diffusion-scale error analysis, but do not analyze these scalar target Gramians or their minimum-energy divergence. Searches across published mathematical records, control/numerics literature, and relevant prior results found no result implying all of the stated constants; the possibility of an overlooked equivalent estimate remains.

**Value — PASS.** The example directly quantifies the source paper's identified false-controllability mechanism: the discrete controllability rank test is positive for every mesh before physical arrival, while the control energy required to exploit that fictitious channel grows at an exactly determined exponential rate. The sharp critical layer connects this unreachable regime to actual controllability at the characteristic travel time. The leading law's independence from positive interval width makes the effect structural, not a tuned numerical curiosity.

The claim is limited to constant-speed one-dimensional transport, equal-length indicator intervals, endpoint-aligned first-order semidiscrete upwind grids, and a single target output. It does not characterize all actuator choices or general higher-dimensional upwind schemes.
