# Scientific review of the false-negative identifying-code census

## Correctness

PASS. The claimed graph definitions reproduce the nonisomorphic strongly regular \((16,6,2,2)\) pair. The fault model matches Jean–Seo's two-domination and asymmetric \(2^{\#}\)-separation test. The obstruction argument proves that for up to seven omitted vertices, only the adjacent-pair forbidden-four-set hypergraph and two-domination need be tested; for larger omissions, monotonicity closes the range. Exact enumeration gives all coefficients. Two independently constructed full-subset programs and a separate obstruction-based verifier have been executed and agree. The structural exchange and orbit assertions have complete finite permutation and adjacency certificates in the included code.

## Originality

PASS under the available evidence, with historical priority provisional. The 2022 primary paper introduces the false-negative criterion but does not enumerate these two graphs. The 2015 strongly regular graph paper and a related published note on the same pair study ordinary identifying codes, whose weaker criterion does not imply the present robust polynomial or mobility result. A similarly themed prior study of simultaneous total domination and ordinary identification uses a different condition and does not imply the asymmetric two-sharp counts. Searches for the named pair, the exact coefficients, equivalent fault terminology and optimum exchange graphs found no covering result. Complete access to all related unpublished or unindexed tables was not established.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a fault-tolerance comparison between the canonical nonisomorphic strongly regular pair with identical parameters: the ordinary identifying-code minima differ, but the false-negative-resistant minima coincide. The polynomial multiplicities and drastically different exchange connectivity distinguish systems of equal optimal detector cost; the forbidden-four-set obstructions and embedded rook exchange components give geometric structure beyond a single computed minimum. It is a motivated exact finite benchmark, not an arbitrary subcase or a trivial consequence of the shared parameters.

## Scope and risk

The result is finite rather than a general theorem for all strongly regular graphs. The sixteen-component rook isomorphism is certified by a map for one component and group images for the remaining five. Reproducibility does not establish historical priority or independent validation. Search-index omissions and inaccessible related full texts remain residual originality risks.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
