# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The parabolic coset decomposition identifies every Walsh coefficient with the standard parabolic Eulerian evaluation at \(-1\). Classical even/odd-rank evaluation and the displayed exhaustive list of finite Coxeter diagram types establish the iff criterion. A tree-boundary bijection and two-state Fibonacci recursion prove the all-rank count. The included integer verifier independently checks all type-\(B\) and type-\(D\) frequencies at ranks four through seven, plus labelled subcubic trees of sizes up to five. These finite checks are corroboration, not the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with explicit priority risk.** Reiner (1995) supplies the classical parabolic descent enumeration, and Cohen (2008) supplies the Coxeter Eulerian polynomial identities. They are not claimed as discoveries. The new component is their complete all-character nonzero criterion joined to the induced-even-component subcubic-tree Fibonacci theorem, yielding the universal rank. Searches and inspected primary full texts yielded no dominating statement for this claim, but an unindexed equivalence could exist.

**Value — PASS.** The result is a complete infinite-family Fourier-rank classification for the canonical simple-descent statistics of finite irreducible Coxeter groups, with explicit support and an exact product law for reducibles. It is motivated by recoverability of convolution on the binary descent-profile cube. The theorem does not determine full spectral magnitudes, inverse norms, or infinite Coxeter groups.

**Closest literature:** Victor Reiner (1995), *The distribution of descents and length in a Coxeter group*; Arjeh M. Cohen (2008), *Eulerian polynomials of spherical type*. Both are credited as substantial established prerequisites, not contradicted or superseded.
