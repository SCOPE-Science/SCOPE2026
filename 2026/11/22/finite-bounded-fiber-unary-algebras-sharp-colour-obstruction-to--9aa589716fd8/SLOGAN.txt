For finite total unary functions with at most \(k\) preimages per point and \(q\) independent named colours, ordinary amalgamation holds exactly when \(q=1\) or \(k=1\), while strong amalgamation holds exactly when \(k=1\). The positive cases admit explicit countable homogeneous limits, and a single pair of differently coloured new predecessors is the sharp obstruction to ordinary amalgamation.
