## Correctness — PASS
The theorem explicitly quantifies over every global fixed-iid sampling optimizer and every fixed finite leaf count/horizon. The projection identity is exact. Uniform convergence to the uncoupled diagonal risk localizes every optimizer on the coupling's square-root scale. A finite matrix Taylor recurrence produces the full asymmetric leading perturbation, whose simple Perron eigenvalue is strictly convex in diagonal probabilities under their zero-sum constraint. Thus all global minimizers have the same unique leading coefficient. Substitution gives the reported optimal retention and strict horizon law. The included checker exercises separate energy-space and original-coordinate recurrences with exact rational matrices and numerical small-coupling examples; numerical evidence is not used as a substitute for the proof.

## Value — PASS
Nonuniform importance sampling is usually calibrated to per-step contraction, while practical runs end after a finite count of updates. This finding determines the leading *globally optimal* sampling probabilities and sharp risk for every fixed horizon on a natural strictly convex star family. The strict, explicit geometric-average horizon law proves that optimizing one iteration and then composing that sampler incurs a strictly positive finite-horizon performance gap. The example is not an arbitrary isolated numerical instance. The gain tends to zero with coupling, and no full-coupling or time-to-solution advantage is asserted.
