# Finite-horizon reactivation of costly matching edges in complete diffusion networks

## Finding
Fix an integer \(m\ge2\), a conductance budget \(B>0\), and a relative price \(r>1\). Partition the \(n=2m\) vertices of the complete graph into \(m\) prescribed pairs. Each within-pair edge costs \(r\) per unit conductance; every edge between different pairs costs one. Independently select nonnegative conductances \(w_e\) satisfying \(\sum_{e\notin M}w_e+r\sum_{e\in M}w_e=B\). For every finite observation time \(t>0\), the globally unique minimizer of
\[
 H_t(w)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(e^{-tL(w)}\bigr)
\]
uses one conductance \(a_*>0\) on every nonmatching edge and one conductance \(b_*\ge0\) on every marked edge.

Set \(d=n-2\), \(c=B/m\). For \(r\ge2\) the exact optimizer is \(b_*=0\), \(a_*=c/d\), **at every finite horizon**. For \(1<r<2\), put
\[
 q_r=\log\frac{r}{2-r},\qquad
 T_*(n,B,r)=\frac{d}{2c}q_r=\frac{n(n-2)}{4B}\log\frac{r}{2-r}.
\]
Then the unique unrestricted optimizer is exactly
\[
 \boxed{\quad b_*(t)=\max\left\{0,\frac{c}{d+r}-\frac{d\,q_r}{2(d+r)t}\right\},\qquad
 a_*(t)=\frac{c-rb_*(t)}d.\quad}
\]
Thus all marked edges are deleted if and only if \(0<t\le T_*\), and all become strictly positive if and only if \(t>T_*\). In the active regime \(0<b_*(t)<a_*(t)\); they both tend to \(c/(d+r)\) as \(t\to\infty\).

The activation has a nontrivial large-network boundary. For fixed \(1<r<2\), \(B>0\), and \(\theta>0\), choose \(t=\theta n^2/B\) along even \(n\to\infty\). Then
\[
 \boxed{\qquad\frac{b_*(\theta n^2/B)}{a_*(\theta n^2/B)}
 \longrightarrow\left(1-\frac{1}{4\theta}\log\frac{r}{2-r}\right)_+,\qquad}
\]
where \(z_+=\max\{z,0\}\). The active fraction of matching-edge spending nevertheless vanishes: \(mr b_*/B=O(n^{-1})\) at this scale. The exact transition time is of order \(n^2/B\), while at prices \(r\ge2\) no positive time activates the matching.

## Assumptions and scope
The Laplacian is the unnormalized combinatorial weighted Laplacian \(L(w)=\sum_{\{i,j\}}w_{ij}(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^{\mathsf T}\). The marked edges form a fixed perfect matching \(M\) in the complete simple graph on \(n=2m\ge4\) labeled vertices. All edge variables are independent nonnegative real numbers, no degree constraints are imposed, every nonmatching price is one, every matching price is the same fixed \(r>1\), and expenditure is exactly \(B\). The observation time is any real \(t>0\). The trace includes the constant-mode contribution one, which does not affect optimization. Optimality and uniqueness hold in the entire feasible budget simplex, not only among pair-symmetric designs.

## Proof
**Strict convexity and unrestricted uniqueness.** For a symmetric matrix \(A\) and a nonzero symmetric direction \(K\), the second derivative of \(\operatorname{tr}e^{A+sK}\) at \(s=0\), in an orthonormal eigenbasis of \(A\), is
\[
 \sum_{i,j}|K_{ij}|^2\frac{e^{\alpha_i}-e^{\alpha_j}}{\alpha_i-\alpha_j}>0,
\]
with the coincident-eigenvalue quotient interpreted as \(e^{\alpha_i}\). Every quotient is strictly positive. Therefore \(w\mapsto\operatorname{tr}e^{-tL(w)}\) is strictly convex because different edge-conductance vectors give distinct Laplacians (their off-diagonal entries recover each edge weight), and \(t>0\). Compactness of the budget simplex yields a minimizer; strict convexity yields exactly one. The group generated by permuting the \(m\) pairs and swapping their two vertices preserves the objective and costs and acts transitively on each of the two edge classes. The unique minimizer must be invariant, hence has uniform conductances \(a\) off the matching and \(b\) on it.

**Spectrum and exact scalar objective.** There are \(m d\) nonmatching and \(m\) matching edges, so the budget is \(m(da+rb)=B\), or \(da+rb=c\). The \(m\)-dimensional subspace antisymmetric within the pairs has Laplacian eigenvalue \(\lambda_A=da+2b\), while pair-constant zero-sum vectors have \(\lambda_S=na\) with multiplicity \(m-1\). The all-ones vector has eigenvalue zero. Hence
\[
 H_t(a,b)=1+m e^{-t(da+2b)}+(m-1)e^{-tna}.
\]
Eliminating \(a=(c-rb)/d\) on \(0\le b\le c/r\) gives
\[
 F_t(b)=1+m\exp[-t(c+(2-r)b)]
 +(m-1)\exp[-t(nc/d-(nr/d)b)].
\]
Its second derivative is a sum of strictly positive terms for \(t>0\) (the coefficient \(nr/d\) is nonzero), so it has exactly one constrained minimum.

**Price barrier and single finite-time activation.** Differentiation and the identity \((m-1)n/d=m\) give
\[
 F_t'(b)=mt\left[r e^{-t(nc/d-(nr/d)b)}-(2-r)e^{-t(c+(2-r)b)}\right].
\]
For \(r\ge2\), the derivative is strictly positive everywhere, so \(b_*=0\). For \(1<r<2\), the derivative at zero is nonnegative precisely when
\[
 e^{-2tc/d}\ge\frac{2-r}r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\le\frac{d}{2c}\log\frac r{2-r}=T_*.
\]
The derivative is strictly increasing as a function of \(b\) because \(F_t''>0\). For \(t>T_*\) it has its unique zero at
\[
 b=\frac{c}{d+r}-\frac{d}{2(d+r)t}\log\frac r{2-r}.
\]
That value lies strictly between zero and \(c/(d+r)<c/r\), proving feasibility and an interior optimum. Substitution gives the stated \(a_*\); since \(b_*<c/(d+r)\), one has \(b_*<a_*\). At \(t=T_*\) strict convexity still makes the boundary solution unique. The formula shows \(a_*,b_*\to c/(d+r)\) as \(t\to\infty\).

**Diffusive scaling.** For fixed \(r\in(1,2)\), set \(q_r=\log(r/(2-r))>0\), \(c=2B/n\), \(d=n-2\), and \(t=\theta n^2/B\). Then
\[
 b_*=\frac{2B}{n(d+r)}\left(1-\frac{d\,q_r}{4\theta n}\right)_+.
\] Consequently \(n^2b_*/(2B)\to(1-q_r/(4\theta))_+\) and \(n^2a_*/(2B)\to1\), proving the ratio law. Finally, \(mr b_*/B\le mr[c/(d+r)]/B=r/(d+r)=O(n^{-1})\).

## Verification
The standalone `verify_thermal_matching.py` reconstructs exact characteristic polynomials of the weighted Laplacian using symbolic arithmetic for even graph orders 4 through 14, independently compares eigenvalue-formula heat traces with matrix exponentials, tests budget constraints, activation sides and analytic scaling, and samples unrestricted nonuniform feasible conductances. Run `python3 verify_thermal_matching.py` with Python 3, NumPy, SciPy and SymPy. It completed 17,202 finite checks in the described environment. Numerical checks are not used to justify infinite quantifiers; the derivations above are the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Sun, Boyd, Xiao and Diaconis [1] introduced the linear-cost continuous-time fastest-mixing problem based on maximizing the smallest positive Laplacian eigenvalue; their published examples treat small graphs, paths and cycles, not this finite-time trace objective. Boyd [2] surveys convex spectral design and symmetry reductions, which are acknowledged as general antecedents and do not supply the explicit heat-trace activation formula. Prior exact work [3–6] on matching-priced complete networks establishes the price-two *spectral-gap* boundary, optimizer faces and distinct total- and worst-pair-resistance thresholds. These earlier findings are not claimed as new: they neither characterize the unique finite-time heat-trace optimum nor imply its observation-horizon activation threshold and macroscopic diffusive crossover. In particular, maximum spectral gap alone does not rank the two nonzero eigenvalue classes at finite time. The result extends those comparisons to a strictly convex whole-spectrum diffusion criterion.

## Limitations
The price classes must be a perfect matching versus all remaining edges on a complete graph, with homogeneous within-class prices and fixed total linear budget. The result does not cover partial matchings, arbitrary edge costs, nonlinear budgets, degree-normalized dynamics, or stochastic discrete-time transitions. The limit fixes \(r\) and \(\theta\); it does not claim uniformity in prices tending to two with graph size. Although close source statements and full texts were compared, the possibility of an unindexed equivalent theorem remains. No independent external mathematical validation is represented.
