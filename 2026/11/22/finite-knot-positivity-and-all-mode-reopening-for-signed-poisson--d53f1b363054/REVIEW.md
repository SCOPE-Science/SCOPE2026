# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS (same-model).** Fourier normalization is fixed consistently in the theorem. The reciprocal of a positive Poisson mixture is a complete Bernstein function of the discrete-Laplacian symbol. Its nonconstant Fourier coefficients are negative, strictly so for a non-point mixing law; the key uniqueness argument is stated. Two applications of the nonlocal discrete maximum principle control respectively the Fourier skeleton at multiples of \(k\) and the intervening spectral frequencies. The invertible Gram map proves the exact cone. Cofinite positive spectrum proves distinct-site Gram strictness and the Fourier character criterion proves universality. Exact-rational tests check concrete nontrivial cases. Finite tests are not an infinite proof.

## Value
**PASS.** An infinite set of Fourier positivity constraints collapses to exactly \(d+1\) irredundant inequalities for a motivated family of stationary covariance kernels. Unlike a pure Poisson scale, any non-point mixture makes every nonzero admissible extreme ray strictly positive definite on all finite distinct configurations, though some rays omit finite frequencies and fail uniform RKHS density. This sharp geometric and spectral dichotomy matters when signed spectral masks are designed under covariance and interpolation constraints.

## Limits
The proof requires positive compactly supported mixing measures in \((0,1)\), real even masks, and standard complete Bernstein reciprocity. The degree starts at two to avoid repeating special lower-degree classifications. Priority claims are best-knowledge only; an independent audit has not been performed.
