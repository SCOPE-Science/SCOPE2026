# Review of the eleven-point prime-cycle horizon theorem

## Correctness
PASS. Canonical representation by a cyclic word beginning at the least label is bijective, so enumerating all \(10!\) suffix permutations is exhaustive. The two constant extreme-rank direction bits can be dropped safely. Every six-step and seven-step fingerprint fits injectively in a 64-bit integer. Exact sorted-key fiber counts are checked against the total of \(3,628,800\) maps and independently regenerated by a different phase-based evaluator. The explicit six-step collision and its seventh-step resolution were independently replayed. Risk: the finite proof is executable and should be recompiled by any reader; it is not an analytic classification of arbitrarily large primes.

## Originality
PASS within inspected sources. Fuhrmann–Gröger–Passeggi (arXiv:2610.07435v1), Section 2 and Remark 2.4, prove uniqueness only for complete time signatures. The publicly available quadratic-time reconstruction note also takes the full table as input. The composite-cycle ambiguity note instead assumes composite periods, where even full-table uniqueness can fail. Neither implies the exact prime-specific observation minima, the sharp six-to-seven threshold at eleven, or its full six-step fiber distribution. Actual searches for shortened prime-cycle direction signatures found no exact-table precursor. Risk: unpublished or unindexed enumeration work might contain these numbers; search silence is not proof of global novelty.

## Value
PASS. The source's rigidity mechanism makes recovering a prime cycle from ordinal motion data a natural inverse-dynamics problem. Minimizing the number of time observations measures how much of the complete signature is genuinely needed. The first three odd primes permit the majority-length heuristic, but eleven disproves it sharply; the six-step fiber census quantifies residual ambiguity and its possible multiplicity, rather than merely producing an isolated example. This is a natural complete finite cutoff and structural boundary, not a claim of broad unproved asymptotics. Risk: the full general prime-length threshold function remains unknown.

## Limits
All counts are for fully labeled ordered cycles; neither noisy observations nor arbitrary global interval maps are covered. No independent scientific audit was undertaken.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
