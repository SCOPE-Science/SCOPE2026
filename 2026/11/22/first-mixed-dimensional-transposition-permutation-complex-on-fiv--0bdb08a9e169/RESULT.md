# First mixed-dimensional transposition permutation complex on five letters

## Finding

For each integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(T_n\subset\mathfrak S_n\) be the group-theoretic conjugacy class of single transpositions, written in one-line notation. Let \(X_n\) be the Boolean cell complex with one \(k\)-cell for every *distinct ordered subsequence of length \(k+1\)* of a word in \(T_n\); the cellular boundary is signed deletion. Then the first five-letter case has exact integral homotopy type

\[
\boxed{|X_5|\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^3.}
\]

It is the smallest member of this transposition sequence with nonzero reduced integral homology in two different positive dimensions. Indeed,
\[
|X_2|\simeq\mathrm{pt},\qquad |X_3|\simeq S^1,\qquad
|X_4|\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^2.
\]

The five-letter complex is pure of dimension four (ten facets) but not shellable as a pure Boolean cell complex: it has nonzero reduced homology in dimensions two and three and none in dimension four.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground alphabet is \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\). The generators are precisely the \(\binom n2\) words obtained by swapping two entries of \((1,2,\ldots,n)\); there is no identity generator. A face is a not-necessarily-consecutive subsequence of a generator. Distinct orders of the same letters define different cells (for example \((1,2)\) and \((2,1)\)); these are **not** abstract simplicial faces with order forgotten. The realization is the standard regular semisimplicial/Boolean cell realization, homotopy equivalent to the order complex of the nonempty word face poset. All homotopy and homology statements are integral and nonequivariant.

## Proof

**Exact enumeration.** For \(n=5\), the ten length-five generators yield exactly \((5,20,48,41,10)\) cells in dimensions \(0,1,2,3,4\), respectively, for a total of \(124\) cells. The chain groups are freely generated by these ordered words, with
\[
\partial(v_0v_1\cdots v_k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j(v_0\cdots\widehat{v_j}\cdots v_k).
\]
Enumeration is exhaustive: every generator and every nonempty subset of its five ordered positions is visited, with identical resulting words deduplicated.

**An acyclic matching with four critical cells.** Sort all cells first by length and then lexicographically, assigning increasing indices. On the remaining face poset, choose the *largest-index* lower cell with exactly one remaining immediate coface, pair it with that coface, and remove both. If no such lower cell exists, choose the largest-index remaining maximal cell and remove it as critical. Continue until none remain. This completely specified finite algorithm produces \(60\) matched pairs and exactly four critical words:
\[
(1),\quad (4,1,5),\quad (5,2,3),\quad (2,1,4,5).
\]
Their dimensions are \(0,2,2,3\), respectively. Every selected pair is a valid elementary free-face pair in the residual cell complex. Moreover, the directed Hasse graph, with matched incidences oriented upward and unmatched incidences downward, has no directed cycles (the attached code reconstructs and verifies all edges). Thus Forman's discrete Morse theorem gives a CW complex with one zero-cell, two two-cells, and one three-cell, homotopy equivalent to \(|X_5|\).

**The remaining attaching map is null.** With the displayed signed-deletion differential, eliminate the \(60\) pairs in their precise chosen order by integral unit-pivot chain cancellation. Every pivot is \(+1\) or \(-1\), and the remaining boundary on the four critical generators is identically zero. In particular, the two two-cells attach to the unique zero-cell, so their two-skeleton is \(S^2\vee S^2\). Its second homotopy group is canonically its integral second homology \(\mathbb Z^2\), by the Hurewicz theorem. The attaching class of the sole three-cell is therefore determined by its two integer degrees. Both degrees vanish by the exact integral reduced differential, so the attaching class is zero; hence \(|X_5|\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^3\).

**Minimality and non-shellability.** Applying the same exhaustive procedure for \(n=2,3,4\) gives critical dimensions \((0)\), \((0,1)\), and \((0,2,2,2)\), with identically zero reduced differentials in each case. In these dimensions the Morse complexes are respectively contractible, a circle, and three two-spheres. Therefore five is the first mixed-positive-degree case. Finally, a pure shellable four-dimensional Boolean cell complex has the homotopy type of a wedge of four-spheres, contradicting the computed nonzero second and third homology; thus \(X_5\) is not shellable.

## Verification

The standalone Python file `transposition_morse.py` reconstructs every cell from the ten five-letter transposition generators, the signed deletion boundary, the deterministic matching, and the complete directed Hasse graph. It verifies acyclicity and performs all \(60\) integral unit-pivot cancellations directly; it checks that the final differential is zero and that the critical cells are exactly the four displayed words. It independently applies the same procedure for \(n=2,3,4\) to verify the minimality statement. It requires only the Python standard library. Run:

```bash
python3 transposition_morse.py
```

A second independent standard-library program, `rational_rank_check.py`, rebuilds all boundary matrices and directly computes their exact rational ranks \((4,16,30,10)\) without using the Morse checker. Those ranks corroborate the Betti numbers but are **not** used as a substitute for integral attachment verification.

The accompanying `MORSE_MATCHING.json` provides the complete list of the \(60\) paired lower and upper words and the four critical words, with one-based labels. The checker regenerates these data without reading the certificate and matches the recorded counts. This is an exhaustive finite mathematical calculation, not a sample extrapolated to an unproven infinite-family formula.

## Relationship to prior work

Elizalde, Hanlon and Hersh (2026) study subcomplexes of injective words selected by avoidance of a fixed permutation pattern and prove shellability in broad cases. The present family instead selects the entire **single-transposition conjugacy class** of full-length words. Its first mixed-degree, nonshellable case is not determined by their shellable pattern-avoidance results. Chacholski, Levi and Meshulam (2019) establish the general framework and classify complexes generated by **two permutations**, as well as realizability of stable homotopy types; these results do not evaluate the natural family of ten transpositions on five letters.

Libman (2023) proves contractibility for the much larger generator set of **all permutations with a fixed point**. For \(n\ge3\), the transposition family is a proper subset of this generator set, but contractibility of the larger complex does not determine the homotopy of the present subcomplex; the five-letter result demonstrates precisely that distinction.

Related public finite computations concern the twenty-element affine permutation group on five letters (with different face numbers \((5,20,60,80,20)\) and homotopy \(S^2\vee\bigvee^{17} S^3\)) and the dihedral family generated by cyclic and reversed cyclic words (with the five-letter type \(\bigvee^{14}S^2\)). These generating sets, their face posets and homotopy calculations differ; no implication to the transposition conjugacy-class computation follows from either theorem. No existing indexed exact transposition computation was located, but this is not a proof of global publication priority.

## Limitations

This theorem classifies the first four sizes of the **specified** transposition-generated word complexes and proves the five-letter threshold. It does not assert an all-\(n\) homotopy formula, an equivariant action on the integral homology, or shellability classifications for other conjugacy classes. The computational certificate is exhaustively checked but the calculation has not received an independent mathematical audit. Unindexed equivalent calculations remain possible.
