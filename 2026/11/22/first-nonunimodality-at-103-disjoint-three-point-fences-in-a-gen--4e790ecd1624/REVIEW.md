# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The one-point extension classification is a direct finite poset construction and gives the exact all-size formula. Two mathematically distinct coefficient formulas (integer polynomial convolution and an independent binomial-sum identity) exhaust all degrees at each of the \(102\) lower indices and evaluate the strict central valley at \(103\). Direct assignment enumeration confirms the extension-to-type bijection for \(k=1,2,3\). The eventual result uses independent concentration inequalities; no finite calculation is extrapolated to the infinite range.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical-access risk.** Conflitti's work counts order ideals in fences, not graded singleton extensions of disconnected fence unions or the first shape transition at \(103\). The generic-poset background and ungraded ideal/filter correspondence are established, and no novelty is asserted for them. Publicly indexed exact generic-poset type-counting results concern total counts, connected tree-cover bases or other families; neither a covering threshold nor an equivalent reciprocal-ideal-sum theorem was identified. Some closely relevant repository sources were available only through summaries, leaving a genuine possibility of unindexed equivalent results. The conclusion is a reasoned provisional originality assessment, not proof of absolute priority.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The comparable-parameter grading is an intrinsic natural statistic on logical extension types. Its coefficient shape displays a new finite-size phenomenon even though the component ideal polynomial is simple: a sharp cutoff at \(103\) and a general explanation via competition between distinct ideal-size means. This illustrates a structural boundary that disappears in ungraded enumeration. The classification does not claim universal significance for arbitrary posets.

## Scientific limitations

The sharp finite cutoff concerns only repeated three-element \(V\)-components. Nonunimodality is separately proved for all \(k\ge10000\), with the interval \(104\le k<10000\) not fully classified. Reproducing integer programs check the finite certificate but are not an independent mathematical audit. Unindexed literature may contain prior equivalent formulations.
