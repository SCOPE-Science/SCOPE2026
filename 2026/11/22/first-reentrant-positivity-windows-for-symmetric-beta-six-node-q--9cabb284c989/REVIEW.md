# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The single assessed claim is the exact symmetric-moment positivity region for fixed six-node interpolation and the induced first reentrant symmetric-Beta phase diagram. The proof constructs the Lagrange cardinals explicitly, reduces their even parts to second and fourth moments, proves the full feasible moment range by an explicit measure construction, substitutes Beta moments, and resolves all real-parameter signs by exact quadratic factorization and positive denominators. Formulas for every lower panel count prove the stated finite minimality. The packaged verifier independently checked exact even-cardinal identities, symbolic denominator-cleared coefficients, 280 exact-rational monomial reproductions, and rational examples from every phase. The finite tests are not substitutes for the analytic universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

The 2013 general weighted Newton–Cotes theorem is direct prior coverage of the **coefficient-computation method**; the 2005 Jacobi–Newton–Cotes paper covers the weighted family as a general construction. The 2019 positive-weight interpolation article provides a general Vandermonde/node-replacement framework and explicit symmetric Beta examples with moving nodes. The present claim instead fixes six equispaced sites and exactly classifies their full symmetric-moment positivity region, both disjoint Beta parameter windows, the first panel count exhibiting reentrance, and sharp negative outputs. None of these full phase or minimality statements appeared in the inspected material. The specialization is algebraically derivable from known general formulas; that is an explicit originality risk, as are a potentially unlocated prior specialization and incomplete 2005 full-text access. No independent priority audit has occurred.

## Value — PASS

Positivity of fixed polynomial-exact quadrature is a natural stability property: it is equivalent to preserving nonnegativity of every nonnegative nodal input and attaining maximal-norm one. The fact that stronger central concentration can destroy positivity, restore it, and then destroy it again through a different weight pair is a meaningful sharp boundary phenomenon rather than an arbitrary isolated number. The general symmetric-moment inequalities allow the criterion to be applied to non-Beta distributions as well. Nothing is claimed for movable nodes, nonsymmetric densities, panel counts above five, or numerical accuracy improvements.
