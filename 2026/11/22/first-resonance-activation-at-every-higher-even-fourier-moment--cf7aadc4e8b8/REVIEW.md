# Scientific assessment: first-resonance activation at higher Fourier moments

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is local in the supercritical dilation parameter and covers every integer moment half-order \(k\ge3\), with \(n=km\) or \(n>km\). Its critical norm bound follows from an explicit multinomial Cauchy--Schwarz argument and a strict occupancy-count bound, not from numerical extrapolation. The exact \(k\)-fold convolution has one extra collision precisely at \(n=km\). A compactness argument localizes all maximizers; the signed normal-form quadratic term controls the low-mode branch, and a negative transverse second derivative plus the implicit-function theorem controls the unique high-mode branch. The claimed gap coefficient follows from an explicit quadratic completion. Rational checker assertions verify the finite convolution identities and local expansion coefficients, but do not prove universal quantifiers. Independent audit has not been performed.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)

We compared both a sharp analytic Hardy hypercontractivity reference (Bayart et al., arXiv:1701.06897, introducing the standard dilation cutoff) and a Hardy coefficient-norm reference (Brevig et al., arXiv:1706.00738), as well as the additive-frequency Dirichlet-series hypercontractivity framework of Carando et al. (arXiv:2401.09996). These address critical contraction, coefficient estimates, or infinite-series frequency structure, not the optimizer's exact near-critical spectral support and \(\delta^{k/2}\) activation. Adjacent known special cases concern fourth moments or undamped sharp even moments; neither supplies the all-integer \(2k\ge6\) local law by direct implication. An equivalent unpublished or unindexed theorem remains a priority risk. Search absence alone was not treated as a novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first arithmetic collision in the \(k\)-fold Fourier convolution changes optimizer support from an exactly missing third mode to mandatory three-mode mixing. The coefficient and gain scales are sharp for *every* higher even moment, isolating the nonlinear spectral response just above a natural and independently motivated hypercontractivity threshold. This is a genuine spectral-selection statement, not merely a recomputation of the known sharp contraction radius or an isolated finite numeral. It may be useful when designing sparse analytic spectral multipliers or comparing moment-order sensitivity to additive structure.

## Limitations and residual risks

Only sufficiently small positive supercritical dilations are classified, and the result is not a global-radii theorem. Gaps below \(km\) and general multi-frequency bands are unaddressed. Relevant historical literature may contain equivalent bifurcation analysis that is not readily indexed; originality is a best-knowledge judgment. The finite exact checker is consistency evidence only, not independent proof or review.
