# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** All word domains, palindrome constraints, and scattered-index definitions are explicit. Two standalone exhaustive implementations—one summing increasing index tuples and another using prefix-update dynamic programming—independently enumerate all binary palindromes of each length from one through fourteen. Both produce the complete injectivity table, all six collision pairs, and the exact \(116+6\) profile distribution. The proof's finite scope is explicit and does not extrapolate from finite tests.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Source-level reading of Freydenberger et al. (arXiv:1509.00622) shows general equality-testing algorithms, not palindrome-limited enumeration. Lejeune, Rigo and Rosenfeld (arXiv:2001.11732) analyze finite-word equivalence classes, focusing particularly on second-order equivalence; the published statement does not give these third-order palindrome fibers. Rigo and Salimov's (2013) accessible abstract describes infinite-word binomial complexity rather than this finite cutoff. Searches for third-order binomial palindrome collisions and related reconstruction spectra returned no implying equivalent result. Relevant unrestricted three-deck, fixed-weight three-deck, and one-gap-deck records do not specify the palindrome-restricted equality layer. Remaining risk is historical unpublished or unindexed enumeration, as well as full text of the original 2013 source not inspected.

**Value — PASS.** The first strict failure of an established third-order scattered-subsequence fingerprint on the natural symmetric language of all binary palindromes is a meaningful, sharp reconstruction boundary. Exhaustively identifying its entire first ambiguity layer—not merely an isolated example—gives a precise minimal blocklength and six complete confusability pairs for use as finite reconstruction benchmarks. No general large-length theorem or code-design advance is asserted.
