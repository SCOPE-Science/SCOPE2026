# Scientific review: Narayana three-adic residue isometries

**Correctness — PASS.** The state transition matrix is an exact integer matrix, and the displayed eighth power and first-order matrix are independently reproduced. Binomial valuation lifting proves an exact first nonzero three-adic term of the difference for all distinct index parameters. Modular branch bijections, least periods, and exact singleton fibres follow directly. A standalone checker confirms eight complete prime-power orbits, ten lifting levels and 7,425 exact-integer difference pairs. The numerical tests corroborate, but do not replace, the universal argument.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** The two closest Narayana primary papers give valuations of \(a_n\) and of \(a_n\pm1\), but not arbitrary two-index isometries for all five index branches or the exact all-power singleton-fibre law. The period function is partially tabulated in OEIS and is not itself claimed as a new invariant. The second-order Pell residue-completeness theorem applies to a different recurrence. Research-record comparisons returned related Perrin and Padovan residue results, but neither implies the required Narayana branch coefficients. An older Narayana-modulo-primes paper was accessible only at abstract level; a stronger uninspected formulation remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The object is a classical integer sequence with existing number-theoretic valuation literature, and residue completeness and multiplicities are natural invariants of its modular orbit. The claim establishes a sharp contrast: one modular congruence class has singleton fibres across every prime power while the other two have at least double fibres. This is an infinite structural classification with constructive witnesses, not a finite catalogue or restatement of a valuation-at-zero formula.

**Limits.** No full fibre distribution is established in the other two residue classes, and no claim of new general theory for other recurrences is made. Sources not available in full and implicit consequences of earlier matrix treatments create residual originality risk.
