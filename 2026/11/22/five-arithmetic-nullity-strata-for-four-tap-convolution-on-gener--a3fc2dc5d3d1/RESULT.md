# Five arithmetic nullity strata for four-tap convolution on generalized dihedral groups

## Finding
Let \(H\) be any finite abelian group, let \(G=H\rtimes\langle s\rangle\) with \(s^2=e\) and \(shs=h^{-1}\) for all \(h\in H\), and set \(N=|H|\). Choose \(x,h\in H\) with \(h\) of order \(m\ge3\), and choose any \(y\in Hs\). On the exact four-point support
\[
S=\{xh^{-1},x,xh,y\},
\]
allow every complex coefficient to be nonzero. For such \(f\), let \(T_f:\mathbb C[G]\to\mathbb C[G]\) be left convolution and \(\nu(f)=\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker T_f\). Put \(q=N/m\). Then the **complete attainable complex nullity spectrum on each fixed support \(S\)** is
\[
\boxed{\{\nu(f):\operatorname{supp}f=S\}=
\begin{cases}
\{0,q,2q,3q\},&m\in\{3,4\},\\
\{0,q,2q,3q,4q\},&m\ge5.
\end{cases}}
\]
Equivalently, subtract these values from \(2N\) to obtain every attainable regular-convolution rank; every unlisted rank is impossible. The statement includes even-order and noncyclic \(H\). Thus the kernel loss is forced into packets of size \(N/m\), while the maximal packet count jumps from three to four precisely at subgroup order five.

There is also an exact support census. If \(e_H(m)=|\{h\in H:\operatorname{ord}(h)=m\}|\), the number of distinct four-element supports of the displayed form with step order \(m\) is
\[
\boxed{C_m(H)=\begin{cases}
N^2e_H(3)/6,&m=3,\\
N^2e_H(m)/2,&m\ge4.
\end{cases}}
\]
Different step orders give disjoint support families. For a cyclic \(H\), the number \(e_H(m)\) is \(\varphi(m)\) when \(m\mid N\), and zero otherwise.

## Assumptions and scope
The semidirect product has the *inversion* action; the cyclic subgroup generated by \(h\) has exact order \(m\ge3\). The four support points are distinct because the rotation triple has three points and the final point lies in the other coset. Coefficients range over \((\mathbb C^*)^4\), without positivity, equal-modulus or integrality restrictions. Ranks and kernels are over \(\mathbb C\) with ordinary (unnormalized) counting convolution. We claim every listed rank on **each** fixed support, not just somewhere in the group. The theorem does not classify supports whose three rotation points fail to form an arithmetic progression.

## Proof
**1. Reduce to an ordinary dihedral subgroup and account for the index.** Convolution by \(\delta_{x^{-1}}\) is invertible, so translating the coefficients does not alter rank or nullity. After this translation the support is \(\{h^{-1},e,h,y'\}\), with \(y'\in Hs\). Left translating once more by \(h\) moves the three rotational points to \(\{e,h,h^2\}\) and replaces \(y'\) by another reflection \(s'\in Hs\). The subgroup \(K=\langle h,s'\rangle\) is isomorphic to the dihedral group of order \(2m\). Left multiplication by an element of \(\mathbb C[K]\) preserves each of the \(q=[G:K]\) right coset spaces \(\mathbb C[K]g\), on which it acts as an identical left-regular \(K\)-operator. Consequently, its kernel dimension on \(G\) is exactly \(q\) times the kernel dimension on \(K\). No assumption about oddness or cyclicity of \(H\) enters this reduction.

**2. Compute the exact root-weighted nullity formula.** Write the four nonzero coefficients after normalization as \(a,b,c,d\), so that the convolution element in \(\mathbb C[K]\) is \(a e+b h+c h^2+d s'\). Put \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/m)\) and \(P(z)=a+bz+cz^2\). For each two-dimensional dihedral representation indexed by \(1\le j<m/2\), the Fourier block is
\[
M_j=\begin{pmatrix}P(\zeta^j)&d\\d&P(\zeta^{-j})\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Set \(t=z+z^{-1}\). A direct multiplication gives
\[
P(z)P(z^{-1})-d^2
=Q(t),\qquad
Q(t)=ac\,t^2+b(a+c)t+(a-c)^2+b^2-d^2.
\]
Since \(ac\ne0\), \(Q\) is a genuine quadratic, never the zero polynomial. Since \(d\ne0\), a singular block \(M_j\) has rank exactly one, contributing two to the regular-representation nullity. For \(t=2\), and also \(t=-2\) when \(m\) is even, there are two one-dimensional characters with eigenvalues \(P(\pm1)+d\) and \(P(\pm1)-d\); at \(Q(\pm2)=0\) exactly one eigenvalue vanishes because \(d\ne0\). Thus each endpoint contributes one to the nullity.

Write
\[
E_m=\{2\}\cup\bigl(\{-2\}\text{ if }2\mid m\bigr),\qquad
I_m=\{2\cos(2\pi j/m):1\le j<m/2\}.
\]
The distinct real numbers in \(I_m\) lie strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\). The complete formula is
\[
\boxed{\nu(f)=q\left(\sum_{t\in E_m}\mathbf1_{Q(t)=0}
+2\sum_{t\in I_m}\mathbf1_{Q(t)=0}\right).}
\]
This formula includes every one-dimensional and two-dimensional block with its correct regular multiplicity.

**3. Derive the upper bounds and attain every level.** Because \(Q\) has at most two distinct roots, the displayed weighted sum is at most four. For \(m=3\), there is one endpoint and one interior point, so the maximum is three. For \(m=4\), there are two endpoints and one interior point; two distinct roots can contribute at most three. For every \(m\ge5\), there are at least two interior points, so four is not excluded.

All listed values actually occur with exact nonzero coefficients. To prescribe any distinct real roots \(u,v\) with \(u+v\ne0\), take
\[
a=c=1,\qquad b=-(u+v)/2,\qquad d=(u-v)/2.
\]
Then every coefficient is nonzero and \(Q(t)=(t-u)(t-v)\). If the two desired roots are opposite, say \(v=-u\) with \(|u|\le2\), instead take
\[
a=1,\quad c=-1,\quad b=2,\quad d=\sqrt{8-u^2},
\]
which has all coefficients nonzero and \(Q(t)=-(t-u)(t+u)\). For zero nullity take the two off-grid roots \(3,4\). For packet counts one and two, respectively prescribe \(2,3\) and \(t_1,3\), where \(t_1\in I_m\). For packet count three prescribe \(2,t_1\). For packet count four, available precisely when \(m\ge5\), prescribe any two distinct \(t_1,t_2\in I_m\). Opposite pairs use the second construction. Hence all possible packet counts are attained, with no accidental additional roots. Undoing the initial translations establishes attainability on each original fixed support.

**4. Count the supports without duplication.** For a given \(m\), there are \(N\) choices of center \(x\), \(e_H(m)\) ordered choices of step \(h\), and \(N\) choices of reflection \(y\). If \(m\ge4\), the unordered rotation triple has a unique middle point and two possible oriented steps, so each support has exactly two such descriptions. Indeed, two distinct middle points would force three times the difference of two rotation points to vanish, forcing step order three. If \(m=3\), each of the three rotation points can be the middle point, and there are two choices of oriented step at each, so each support has six descriptions. Division by these exact multiplicities proves the support count. This also proves that the step order is intrinsic to the support and different orders cannot overlap.

## Verification
The standalone `check_symbolic.py` verifies the Laurent-polynomial identity symbolically, both nonzero-coefficient two-root constructions, and exact root weights at orders \(3,4,5,6\), including the opposite-root exception. The separate `check_regular.py` constructs the group multiplication and full unnormalized regular-convolution matrices directly, without using Fourier block matrices. It checks all attainable levels on ten different cyclic and product-abelian examples, including index factors greater than one, totaling 44 numerical matrix tests. These finite checks corroborate the algebraic identities but are not substituted for the all-group proof above. The third standalone checker `check_support_count.py` exhaustively confirms the support-incidence formula for eleven small cyclic/product groups. Run `python3 check_symbolic.py` (requires SymPy), `python3 check_regular.py` (requires NumPy), and `python3 check_support_count.py` (standard Python).

## Relationship to prior work
The dihedral irreducible decomposition and group-determinant factorization are classical. Alagic–Russell's rank-weighted uncertainty principle supplies a general lower bound, not the attainable-rank set on each fixed arithmetic-progression support. Yamaguchi's dihedral group-determinant factorization gives a general algebraic framework, but the source does not state the sharp packet classification, realizability of each packet count with all nonzero coefficients, or the exact support census here. Arezoomand–Taeri provide a general Cayley characteristic-polynomial decomposition rather than this rank diagram. A published balanced four-tap generalized-dihedral theorem concerns **two** nonzero points in **each** coset with unit coefficients, and a published prime-dihedral three-tap classification concerns **three** nonzero points in total; neither contains the present three-plus-one four-tap family. In the cyclic group of order five the current rank set agrees with the known finite three-plus-one calculation; the added conclusion is uniformity over arbitrary finite abelian kernels and every step order, with a sharp order-three/four boundary, full weights and support counts.

## Limitations
This result concerns a four-tap support consisting of a three-term progression in the rotation coset and one reflection. It does not classify arbitrary non-progression three-plus-one supports or balanced four-point supports, nor does it optimize nonzero singular values or conditioning. A sufficiently general unpublished or unindexed group-determinant specialization could reproduce the formulas, so historical priority should be reviewed independently. The explicit root witnesses use arbitrary complex coefficients; they should not be interpreted as equal-weight or phase-only witnesses.
