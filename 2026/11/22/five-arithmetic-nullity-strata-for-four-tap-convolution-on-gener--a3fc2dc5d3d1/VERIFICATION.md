The argument is symbolic and applies to all finite abelian kernels. It first reduces the convolution operator to copies of the regular operator on the dihedral subgroup generated by the step and the reflection; second, it factors the two-dimensional Fourier-block determinant as an exact quadratic in a real cyclotomic trace; third, it checks the one-dimensional endpoint multiplicities and constructs nonzero coefficients for every attainable root pattern.

Run `python3 check_symbolic.py` with SymPy and `python3 check_regular.py` with NumPy, and `python3 check_support_count.py` with standard Python. The first program tests the polynomial identity and both root constructions, plus 18 exact small-order root-weight configurations. The second independently builds ten sets of full group-convolution matrices and tests all 44 predicted index-scaled kernel dimensions. The third, `check_support_count.py`, exhaustively enumerates the progression-support families in eleven small cyclic or product-abelian groups, matching the count and disjointness formulas. These finite calculations are consistency checks, not substitutes for the general proof. No formal proof assistant or independent audit was used.

Unproved limits: the theorem does not address arbitrary non-progression four-point supports, finite-field rank, or optimal singular-value gaps. Exhaustive support counting is proved combinatorially, not inferred from sampled groups.
