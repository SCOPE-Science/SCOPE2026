# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Since every E-class is represented, a new point has one definite old class and arbitrary adjacency bits only outside that class. Each allowable diagram is realized infinitely often, and homogeneity makes each diagram a single complete nonalgebraic type. Substituting z=w−1 in the graded polynomial recovers the exponent multiset directly, hence n and the part sizes. For scalar comparison at fixed n, the exponent sum (q−1)n and number q of summands are also fixed. Every representation of a binary integer comes from splitting its canonical bits by reversing carries. With at most four summands, split configurations with equal exponent-sum changes either agree or coincide as multisets (the only nontrivial two-source tie has consecutive source exponents). With five summands, one-bit split trees have depth sums 12, 13 and 14; two-bit trees have changes, after subtracting 3B, of 3d−8, 3d−9, 2d−7, d−7, −8 and −9, where d=A−B>0. All ties occur at d=1 or 2 and give the same leaf multisets. Four or five canonical bits give at most one representation per change. For three canonical bits, the only distinct tie is splitting B twice versus splitting A and C once when A+C=2B−1. Only the first multiset retains A. Their exponent sum is 5B−6=4n, yielding B=4t+2, n=5t+1. Subtraction from n gives exactly the two frozen profiles, positive precisely for 1≤d≤t−2; the canonical binary integer distinguishes all d, so every nonsingleton fibre has size exactly two. The unique first pair at n=16 gives T=53248. The cross-size example has T=320 and does not meet the fixed-n hypothesis.
