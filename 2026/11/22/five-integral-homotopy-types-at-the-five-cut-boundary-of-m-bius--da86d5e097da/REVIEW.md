# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem is a finite classification of precisely five Möbius ladder graphs, not a sampled pattern extrapolated to an infinite family. The complete standalone program constructs graph adjacencies, independently checks every possible face against the definition, generates the full lexicographic matching on nonempty simplices, checks that every matched incidence is a cover and each simplex is used at most once, topologically sorts every oriented cover relation, and confirms that the only critical cells are one vertex and the stated \(d\)-cells. Thus Forman's discrete Morse theorem gives the claimed integral wedge types. Independent binary boundary reductions and Euler characteristic calculations agree. The largest case has 62,850 nonempty simplices and 491,376 cover relations; neither enumeration is partial.

**Originality — PASS with explicitly residual risks.** The motivating August 2026 preprint poses Möbius-ladder cut complexes as a direction, but does not calculate ordinary five-cuts. Full-text examination of the foundational cut-complex paper and published, directly relevant ordinary-four-cut and total-four-cut result statements found no implication covering these five ordinary-five-cut complexes. Ordinary three-cut results concern a different missing-set parameter, while total cuts use independent sets rather than disconnected sets. Multiple actual database and literature queries, including the five numerical ranks and alternative nomenclature, found no matching result. These checks are evidence for an original result under accessible literature, not proof that no unindexed or inaccessible prior result exists.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This addresses the next unclassified natural ordinary cut parameter after existing uniform three- and four-cut theorems for the same canonical cubic graph family. It resolves a complete consecutive five-size interval with exact integral homotopy types, exposing an exceptional pure two-dimensional complex with only one-dimensional homotopy at the Wagner graph, followed by four high-rank top-degree sphere wedges. The transition distinguishes the five-cut topology from the adjacent parameters and yields concrete data and a reproducible finite method for future structural conjectures; the result is not represented as an all-family theorem.
