# Five-layer square-cylinder perfect-matching complex: 88 four-spheres and the full symmetry character

## Finding
Let \(G=C_4\mathbin{\square}P_5\cong P_2\mathbin{\square}P_2\mathbin{\square}P_5\), with vertices \((a,b,t)\), where \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\) and \(t\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). Adjacency means that exactly one coordinate changes by one. Let \(K=\mathcal P(G)\) be the simplicial complex on the edges of \(G\) whose facets are its perfect matchings (equivalently, all partial matchings extendible to a perfect matching).

Then \(K\) is a pure nine-dimensional simplicial complex and
\[
|K|\simeq\bigvee^{88}S^4.
\]
In particular, this pure complex is not shellable.

The natural action of \(D_4\times C_2\), generated by quarter-turn \(r\) and reflection \(s\) of the square cross-section and reflection \(h\) of the five-layer direction, acts on \(\widetilde H_4(K;\mathbb Q)\) with the following complete irreducible multiplicities:

| \(D_4\) irrep | \(h=+1\) | \(h=-1\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(A_1\) | 7 | 6 |
| \(A_2\) | 6 | 3 |
| \(B_1\) | 9 | 4 |
| \(B_2\) | 4 | 5 |
| \(E\) (dimension two) | 10 | 12 |

Here \(A_1(r)=A_1(s)=1\), \(A_2(r)=1,A_2(s)=-1\), \(B_1(r)=-1,B_1(s)=1\), \(B_2(r)=B_2(s)=-1\), and \(E\) is the standard two-dimensional square representation. The dimension check is \(7+6+6+3+9+4+4+5+2(10+12)=88\). The invariant subspace for the whole natural group has rational dimension **seven**; this does not assert the integral homology or homotopy type of the geometric quotient.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected and labelled by the coordinates above. The notation \(C_4\) means the ordinary simple four-cycle, which is isomorphic to \(P_2\square P_2\). Perfect matchings cover all twenty graph vertices by ten disjoint edges. The simplicial complex has 36 eligible edge-vertices, and its maximal simplices have ten vertices. All homology and Smith calculations use exact integers. The symmetry claim is *rational*, not an integral lattice classification, and uses the sixteen stated graph automorphisms.

## Proof
**1. Enumeration and elementary collapses.** Number the twenty vertices lexicographically in \((a,b,t)\). Number graph edges by scanning each vertex, then the three positive coordinate directions in order. Enumerate all perfect matchings by selecting the least unused graph vertex and branching over its unused neighbors, and take all subfaces of each resulting ten-edge matching. This finite exhaustive recursion produces exactly 450 perfect matchings and 183,544 simplices, counting the empty face. The original face vector in dimensions zero through nine is
\[
(36,534,4104,17392,41104,54592,42072,18759,4500,450).
\]

For every current nonempty face, maintain the number of *immediate* cofaces. Repeatedly select the highest-dimensional face with exactly one immediate coface (ties broken by the edge-bit mask), delete that face and its unique coface, and update all affected immediate-coface counts. The reproducible procedure in `code/checker.py` asserts actual freeness at each step, making the operation an elementary simplicial collapse rather than a formal face cancellation. Exactly 88,171 pairs are removed. The remaining subcomplex has face vector
\[
(36,427,1850,3129,1759)
\]
in dimensions zero through four. The last collection of pairs is itself the exact computational certificate; a reproducible canonical encoding of the pair sequence has SHA-256 `4c419d1d85e24ce6f8ee53dfe566a82a565d6fca41099685d8a4aecc00764e4e`.

**2. The fundamental group.** In the remaining core's one-skeleton, construct a spanning tree by taking edges in increasing bit-mask order and retaining those joining distinct components. This gives 35 tree edges on 36 vertices. Since tree edges are nullhomotopic after contracting the tree, search the 1,850 triangular faces repeatedly. Whenever two sides are already known nullhomotopic, the third side also is. This verifies 392 further edge-relator eliminations and exhausts all 427 one-skeleton edges. Consequently \(\pi_1(K)=0\). The computation requires only elementary triangular relations and provides explicit generator elimination rather than treating vanishing \(H_1\) as proof of simple connectivity.

**3. Integral homology.** Form the full *oriented* boundary matrices of the collapsed core, orienting simplices by increasing edge indices. Verify \(\partial^2=0\) for every two-, three- and four-simplex. Apply exact elementary integer column operations to each boundary separately, pivoting only at coefficients \(+1\) or \(-1\), eliminating an entire pivot row and column. The resulting Smith decompositions have respectively 392, 1,458 and 1,671 unit diagonal entries for \(\partial_2,\partial_3,\partial_4\), with zero residual matrices; no torsion invariant factor remains. With \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1=35\), the Betti ranks are
\[
\beta_1=427-35-392=0,\qquad
\beta_2=1850-392-1458=0,
\]
\[
\beta_3=3129-1458-1671=0,\qquad
\beta_4=1759-1671=88.
\]
Since each nonzero Smith factor is a unit, this is a statement about *integral* groups, not merely mod-prime Betti numbers. Thus \(\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{88}\) for \(i=4\), and zero otherwise. As \(K\) is simply connected, successive applications of Hurewicz identify the first possible nonzero homotopy group in degree four with \(H_4\). Choose 88 representing maps from \(S^4\); their wedge induces an integral homology isomorphism. The homology Whitehead theorem makes that wedge map a homotopy equivalence. Since a pure nine-dimensional shellable complex has reduced homology only in its top dimension, nontrivial fourth homology excludes shellability.

**4. Rational symmetry character.** Let \(r(a,b,t)=(1-b,a,t)\), \(s(a,b,t)=(1-a,b,t)\) and \(h(a,b,t)=(a,b,4-t)\). These induce permutations of the 36 edge-vertices. For each of the sixteen elements \(r^ks^\varepsilon h^\delta\), enumerate all 183,543 nonempty simplices. A fixed simplex contributes \((-1)^{\dim\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_\sigma)\) to the simplicial Lefschetz number; all other simplices contribute zero. Because the only reduced rational homology is \(H_4\), subtract one for the degree-zero homology trace. The resulting character pairs (without and with \(h\)) are:

| \((k,\varepsilon)\) | \(\chi(r^ks^\varepsilon)\) | \(\chi(r^ks^\varepsilon h)\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| (0,0) | 88 | 4 |
| (1,0) | 0 | 0 |
| (2,0) | 0 | 12 |
| (3,0) | 0 | 0 |
| (0,1) | 8 | 4 |
| (1,1) | 0 | -8 |
| (2,1) | 8 | 4 |
| (3,1) | 0 | -8 |

Inner products with the five standard \(D_4\) irreducible characters, each tensored with the even/odd character of \(C_2\), produce exactly the multiplicity table in the Finding. Orthogonality gives nonnegative integer multiplicities, and the sum of character values divided by sixteen is seven. The argument applies to the action on homology, and does not require the collapses themselves to be equivariant.

## Verification
The self-contained program `code/checker.py` uses only the Python 3 standard library. Run it from the folder containing `code/` with `python3 code/checker.py`; it generates the graph, perfect matchings and all faces afresh, checks elementary collapse freeness, verifies the fundamental-group elimination and integer boundary identities, then recomputes all sixteen signed Lefschetz traces and character inner products. The exact output from a clean run is in `evidence/checker_output.json`, and the digest of the deterministically generated sequence of free-face pairs is recorded there. No external input tables or precomputed rank assertions are used in place of reconstruction; the assertions check the independently derived numerical expectations.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, *The Homotopy Types of the Independence and Perfect Matching Complexes of Möbius and Circular Ladder Graphs*, arXiv:2608.30601 (first publicly posted 31 August 2026), classify perfect-matching complexes of cubic circular ladders \(C_n\square P_2\) and Möbius ladders. Their full nineteen-page article, including the circle-ladder result in Theorem 25 and future directions in Problem 27, was compared. Our twenty-vertex graph \(C_4\square P_5\) is a five-layer square cylinder with interior vertices of degree four, not a cubic circular ladder; Theorem 25 does not imply our claim.

Chandrakar, Chau, Goyal, Karki and Subhash, *Perfect Matching Complexes of 3×2n Grid Graph*, arXiv:2611.18058 (first public 13 November 2026), concern open three-row rectangular grids, not this square cylinder. The accessible abstract was compared; its complete manuscript was not inspected, so no whole-document absence claim is made.

Closest publicly indexed finite calculations concern the ordinary matching complex of a four-by-four square cylinder, the perfect-matching complex of a planar four-by-four square grid, and a \(D_4\) character of that planar graph's ordinary matching complex. Those have different graphs or different definitions, and their recorded claims do not imply the present 88-sphere equivariant classification. This distinction was checked at the level of graph, allowed simplices and homology action rather than only titles.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite classification of one canonical graph, not a recurrence or prediction for longer square cylinders. The rational character does not identify the full integral symmetry lattice. The result has undergone only self-review; independent audit has not yet been performed. The closest external source bodies were not all accessible and an unindexed equivalent calculation is possible. All arithmetic and enumeration statements can be replayed with the embedded standalone checker; no assertion about an infinite family follows from the finite enumeration.
