# Scientific review of the seven-antihole extremal finite-space classification

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem's scope is any finite topological space containing an induced \(\overline{C_7}\) in its inseparability graph, with an exact classification only when it has fourteen points. Closed-twin quotienting reduces the lower bound to \(T_0\); an induced seven-antihole has no closed twins. Every edge at cyclic distance three forces a separate minimal lower-bound witness, because the relevant seven cliques contain at most one such edge. At equality, any comparability between two designated nonminimal vertices would make their closed neighborhoods nested, impossible since each has size five and the neighborhoods are distinct. The only optional order relations correspond to a vertex cover of a step-two \(C_7\). The proof gives both necessity and sufficiency. A direct checker exhausts all 128 optional subsets, confirms 29 valid labeled cases, five dihedral orbits and the ranks of the connected order-complex graphs.

The order-complex calculation gives **weak homotopy equivalence** for the finite spaces, by McCord's theorem; it is not misreported as an ordinary homotopy equivalence. The independent symbolic reduction and exact small-graph calculations support correctness. No unproved infinite extension is used.

## Originality — PASS (classification claim only)

P. J. Cameron and V. Kumar P. B., *Graphs on Topological Spaces* (2026), arXiv:2610.14783, Theorem 7.3 provides the general edge-cover characterization and Proposition 7.6 treats odd-antihole lower bounds, showing only a coarse \(n+5\) bound in that argument. Earlier S.-R. Kim, B. Park and Y. Sano, *Cycles and p-competition graphs* (2009), arXiv:0905.3942, Theorem 2.4 **already proves** that the edge clique cover number of \(\overline{C_7}\) is seven. Thus neither this numerical graph invariant nor the ensuing fourteen-point minimum is claimed as independently new. The examined texts do not give all equality-case incidence posets, their five dihedral isomorphism classes or their four weak homotopy types. K. Chen, J. DeLeo and O. Henderschedt (2025), arXiv:2503.22490, prove a general finite-topology graph recognition theorem and clique-cover descriptions, not these extremal orbits. Equivalent prior classifications outside the inspected texts remain a possibility.

## Value — PASS

The smallest genuine odd antihole \(\overline{C_7}\) is the first case not already isomorphic to an odd hole. Its extremal finite-space realizations are a natural boundary case for the graph/topology relationship. The result completely classifies the minimum-order spaces, not just the known edge clique cover number: five nonhomeomorphic spaces, with weak types containing five through eight circles, and two distinct spaces with the same six-circle weak type. This separates homeomorphism classification from weak homotopy classification and supplies explicit incidence models, a natural finite extremal classification rather than an arbitrary computation.

## Closest literature and limitations

The 2009 edge-clique-cover theorem is decisive prior coverage of the numerical bound, and the 2026 finite-space realization theorem supplies the general connection. The contribution is confined to the **complete equality classification and weak types**. The result is for the first seven-vertex odd antihole, not the entire odd-antihole family; the count of 29 presupposes a fixed cyclic labeling. Verification of the dihedral orbits is exhaustive, but publication-priority searches do not constitute proof of uniqueness in all literature.
