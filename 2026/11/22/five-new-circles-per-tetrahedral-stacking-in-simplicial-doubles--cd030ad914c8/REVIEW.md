# Scientific review: tetrahedral stacking and simplicial doubles

## Correctness — PASS
The proof is a complete graph-level description: a tetrahedral facet gives three opposite-pair edges in the double, while its subdivision deletes those three and inserts twelve edges through four new pair-vertices. These twelve form a connected subdivision of \(K_4\) joining the six original pair-vertices. The resulting exact changes \(\Delta V=4\), \(\Delta E=9\), and \(\Delta c=1-k\) imply \(\Delta\beta_1=6-k\). The base double is \(KG(5,2)\), a connected graph with first Betti number six. Induction proves the stacked-sphere wedge claim for every \(n\ge5\). The packaged verifier independently checks 6,646 distinct stacked facet sets through nine vertices and 1,002 other local examples; enumeration is supportive, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limits
The foundational 2025 Lesh--Schreiner--Wahl article introduces doubling and proves connectivity bounds; it does not compute the move-by-move topological effect. The nearest fully inspected published mathematical record gives a parity-dependent classification for cyclic four-polytope boundaries, not stacked boundaries or the local facet move. Searches under opposite-edge partition graphs, tetrahedral stacking, facet stellar subdivision, exact circle ranks and broader \(r\)-tupling did not identify an implication covering this theorem. An unindexed equivalent lemma remains possible. The complete compared results and source material are recorded in `AUDIT.json`.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a structural description of a basic refinement operation on a recent simplicial construction. It determines component fusion and fundamental-group rank, and supplies an infinite exact integral homotopy classification, not merely a numerical test. The contrast between connected stacked four-polytope doubles and sometimes-disconnected cyclic four-polytope doubles clarifies triangulation sensitivity.

## Limitations
The result applies to pure three-dimensional complexes and tetrahedral facet subdivisions, and to boundaries generated by stacked four-polytopes. It does not classify other bistellar moves or higher-dimensional doubles. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
