# Five omitted incidence gates restore maximal bipartite entanglement on the Tutte–Coxeter graph

## Finding

**Theorem (sharp five-gate restoration, with integral robustness).** Let \(S=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\). Let \(P\) be the set of fifteen unordered two-element subsets (duads) of \(S\), and let \(L\) be the set of fifteen decompositions of \(S\) into three unordered disjoint pairs (synthemes). Let \(G\) be their incidence graph, joining \(p\in P\) to \(\ell\in L\) precisely when \(p\) is a pair in \(\ell\). This is the thirty-vertex Tutte–Coxeter graph, the Levi graph of the generalized quadrangle \(GQ(2,2)\).

Prepare the standard graph state on its thirty vertices, by applying a controlled-phase \(CZ_d\) gate to every incidence edge, with \(d\ge2\) the local qudit dimension. Partition the register as all fifteen duad qudits against all fifteen syntheme qudits. Let the five edges in the following table be *omitted* from state preparation, with entries in each syntheme separated by semicolons:

| Omitted duad | Omitted syntheme |
|---|---|
| \(\{2,3\}\) | \(\{0,1\};\{2,3\};\{4,5\}\) |
| \(\{0,1\}\) | \(\{0,1\};\{2,5\};\{3,4\}\) |
| \(\{1,2\}\) | \(\{0,3\};\{1,2\};\{4,5\}\) |
| \(\{1,5\}\) | \(\{0,2\};\{1,5\};\{3,4\}\) |
| \(\{1,3\}\) | \(\{0,4\};\{1,3\};\{2,5\}\) |

For **every subset of \(k\) of these five edges**, \(0\le k\le5\), the nonzero Schmidt coefficients across the duad/syntheme partition are all equal, and their number is exactly \(d^{10+k}\). Consequently all positive-order Rényi and von Neumann entropies are exactly

\[
S_k=(10+k)\log_2 d\ \text{bits}.
\]

In particular, removing all five incidence gates changes the qubit state from \(10\) to **\(15\)** ebits, the maximal possible entropy of a fifteen-qubit subsystem. **Five gate omissions are necessary and sufficient** to obtain maximal cross-partition entanglement from this original graph state. In addition, the modified fifteen-by-fifteen integral incidence matrix has determinant \(-1\), so the result holds uniformly for *all integer qudit dimensions*, including composite dimensions.

## Assumptions and scope

The duads are listed in lexicographic order as `itertools.combinations(range(6),2)`. The synthemes are listed in the recursive order generated by `pair_partitions((0,1,2,3,4,5))` in `VERIFY_RANK.py`. Write \(B\in\{0,1\}^{15\times15}\) for the incidence matrix. The omitted edges are its entries with zero-based coordinates \((9,0),(0,2),(5,6),(8,5),(6,10)\); all are ones in \(B\), and their row endpoints and column endpoints are each distinct. The qudit graph state has phase \(\exp(2\pi i ab/d)\) on each bond for local basis values \(a,b\in\mathbb Z_d\), and has no other interactions or losses. Every original incidence gate not listed remains intact. The statement is about the full duad-versus-syntheme cut, not arbitrary smaller cuts.

## Proof

**1. Integral baseline rank is exactly ten.** For each \(a\in S\), let \(s_a\) be the length-fifteen row vector indicating the duads containing \(a\). Every syntheme contains exactly one pair containing \(a\), so \(s_a B=\mathbf 1\). Hence all five differences \((s_a-s_5)B=0\) for \(a=0,\ldots,4\). These five rows are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\): if \(\sum_{a\in S}c_as_a=0\), looking at the coordinate \(\{i,j\}\) gives \(c_i+c_j=0\) for every distinct \(i,j\), forcing all \(c_i=0\) over \(\mathbb Q\). Consequently \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q} B\le10\), and every eleven-by-eleven minor of \(B\) vanishes identically over the integers.

**2. A unit ten-by-ten minor.** Remove from the rows of \(B\) the indices \(9,0,5,8,6\) and from its columns the indices \(0,2,6,5,10\). With the surviving row indices \((1,2,3,4,7,10,11,12,13,14)\) and column indices \((1,3,4,7,8,9,11,12,13,14)\) in increasing order, direct integer Bareiss elimination gives determinant \(-1\). The complete independently reproducible finite computation is `VERIFY_RANK.py`; it builds all incidence entries from the definition and checks exact integer divisibility at every elimination step. This specific integer certificate proves that \(B\) has rank exactly ten over every field, and ten unit Smith factors (five zeros).

**3. Every selected omission adds precisely one Smith unit.** Fix any subset \(F'\) of \(k\) of the five displayed incidences and form \(B_{F'}\) by replacing these entries with zero. Take the square submatrix on the original ten retained rows and columns from Step 2, together with the \(k\) row endpoints and \(k\) column endpoints of \(F'\). Its size is \(10+k\). Regard each altered entry as an indeterminate subtraction from \(B\) and expand the determinant multilinearly in those \(k\) entries. Every coefficient of degree strictly less than \(k\) is zero, because the corresponding determinant term includes an unperturbed minor of size greater than ten, while \(B\) has rational rank ten. The unique degree-\(k\) coefficient is, up to sign, the determinant of the ten-by-ten complement in Step 2, and hence is \(\pm1\). Evaluating all omitted entries therefore produces a \((10+k)\)-minor of absolute determinant one. Conversely each omitted entry is a rank-one perturbation, so \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}B_{F'}\le10+k\). It follows that every nonzero Smith invariant factor of \(B_{F'}\) is \(1\), and precisely \(10+k\) such factors occur. Direct elimination for all \(2^5=32\) subsets in `VERIFY_RANK.py` checks the argument independently of this algebraic expansion; the full fifteen-by-fifteen determinant, with the specified order, is \(-1\).

**4. Schmidt spectrum for every local dimension.** Write \(C=B_{F'}\) and \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/d}\). The phase matrix of the bipartite graph state's amplitudes, after removing local normalization, is \(H_{x,y}=\omega^{x^{\mathsf T}Cy}\) for \(x,y\in\mathbb Z_d^{15}\). The product \(HH^*\) has entry \(d^{15}\) when \(C^{\mathsf T}(x-x')=0\) modulo \(d\), and zero otherwise, by character orthogonality. Thus the reduced density matrix has \(|\operatorname{im} C|\) equal nonzero eigenvalues, each \(|\operatorname{im}C|^{-1}\). The integer Smith factors from Step 3 give \(|\operatorname{im} C|=d^{10+k}\) for **every** \(d\ge2\), proving the uniform spectrum and entropies.

**5. Sharpness.** The initial entropy is \(10\log_2 d\). Altering (omitting) one gate changes a single matrix entry and can increase its rank over \(\mathbb F_p\) by at most one. Therefore four or fewer omitted gates cannot produce a full-rank cut matrix for qubits, or maximal \(15\log_2 d\) entropy for any qudit dimension. The five omissions in the table achieve full integral determinant \(-1\), so five is optimal.

## Verification

Run `python3 VERIFY_RANK.py` and `python3 VERIFY_BINARY.py` with Python 3 and no dependencies. The first constructs the 15 synthemes recursively and proves the specified unit complement minor and all 32 selected-fault maximal unit minors using fraction-free integer elimination; its final matrix determinant is \(-1\). The second independently generates synthemes by unordered-partition enumeration and computes binary ranks using bit-vector elimination. It confirms the baseline rank \(10\), the full subset rank law, and rank \(11\) after deleting any one of the 45 original incidence gates. Both passed in their original packaged versions; the finite tests verify the arithmetic certificates, while the arguments in the Proof establish the all-dimension theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Hein, Eisert and Briegel [1] give the stabilizer graph-state cut-rank framework and one-edge entanglement-change bounds, which are **prior methods, not new claims**. The duad/syntheme realization of \(GQ(2,2)\), and hence the thirty-vertex Tutte–Coxeter graph, is classical [2,3]. A published theorem on omission matchings for even-order *projective-plane* incidence graph states [4] yields a different sharp geometric threshold and cannot be applied to this \(GQ(2,2)\) matrix, which has fifteen points and fifteen lines rather than the seven-and-seven geometry of the projective plane of order two. The new claim here is the explicit **five-omission optimality, every-subset unit-minor restoration, and dimension-uniform maximal bipartite entropy** for the Tutte–Coxeter graph. Searches of nearby graph-state bond-deletion work identified other graph families but no result implying this exact finite-geometry certificate; undiscovered equivalent matrix or coding-theory computations remain possible.

## Limitations

The result assumes pure ideal graph-state preparation, exact omission of chosen controlled-phase gates, and the full natural incidence bipartition. It makes no statement about arbitrary five-edge failure patterns, other bipartitions, noisy gates, generalizations to larger generalized quadrangles, or operational distillation costs. A prior publication of the same integral unit-minor pattern, even without graph-state terminology, would defeat the claimed historical originality; none is established here. No independent audit is claimed.
