# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For rank-one planar Kaczmarz projectors, the operator product norm is an exact product of adjacent-row cosines. Splitting the iid adjacent transitions into even/odd independent blocks gives the almost-sure ergodic quadratic objective. The elementary identity \(\cos^3(\pi/5)-\cos(2\pi/5)=(4-\sqrt5)/8>0\) implies \(b>3a\). A positive four-coordinate second variation rules out all supports of size four or five. Classification of the two kinds of three-vertex support, concavity of the one surviving scalar quadratic, and the exact gap \(ab/[2(4b-a)]\) prove the global five-vector equality set. The trace bound plus doubled-angle sum proves the one-step comparison. The standalone script additionally passed 10,829 finite verification assertions; it is not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual publication-priority risk

The general rank-one Lyapunov quadratic formula is **already known** from Altschuler–Parrilo (2019). The claim is only the exact optimization and all five equality cases for the naturally equiangular five-direction row frame, together with a demonstrably strict typical-versus-mean-square sampler separation. The early Strohmer–Vershynin source proves annealed convergence under row-norm sampling, and Agaskar–Wang–Lu (2014) concern exact *annealed* mean-square propagation. The most relevant indexed planar published-finding corpus theorem (2026-10-13) proves an annealed/one-step coincidence and expressly excludes quenched rates; its full `RESULT.md` was inspected. Searches for regular-pentagon optimized row selection, Lyapunov exponents, and weighted five-cycle quadratic equivalents produced no statement implying the particular sharp five-optimum classification. The absence of an equivalent in unindexed combinatorial quadratic programming literature cannot be excluded and remains a concrete risk.

## Value — PASS

The pentagonal five-line frame is a canonical oversampled, tight, equal-row-norm test family. The complete nonconcave iid sampling-design classification demonstrates a non-obvious choice: exactly three of five rows must be retained, in five equivalent configurations, whereas the uniform law optimizes the classical one-step worst-direction expected error. Exact rate gaps show why the competing objectives matter for solver design. The result is a motivated complete family classification rather than a generic restatement of the known quadratic formula.

## Scope and limitations

The theorem applies only to real planar unrelaxed iid Kaczmarz on five specified directions and the almost-sure operator norm. General Lyapunov quadratic forms, annealed rate formulas, and literature priority are not newly claimed. The numerical checker is finite evidence; the mathematical proof is algebraic and probabilistic. No independent verification has been undertaken.
