## Originality — PASS, qualified
The nonstar minimum-power-domination triple classification is already established in the earlier two-subset Kneser power-domination-polynomial research and is not claimed as new. The closest isolating-set result uses a different forbidden triple family and unrestricted swaps; the same twelve-ground-label technique does not supply distances of this graph. Fischer's full-token-graph diameter theorem concerns all three-subsets (including forbidden star states), not shortest routes avoiding forbidden configurations. Bjorkman et al. treat general power-domination exchange/reconfiguration; Bartier et al. treat independent token slides, and Cornet–Torres static domination. None of these comparisons yielded the exact radius-four/diameter-five optimum graph or a necessary-and-sufficient extremal matching obstruction. The residual risk of inaccessible or unindexed equivalent historical results is acknowledged, not treated as proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The two-subset Kneser family is a canonical highly symmetric benchmark; the theorem explains how minimum sensor monitoring can be maintained while physically relocating sensors. It gives exact longest and shortest maximum relocation costs, complete vertex degrees by orbit, and a structural matching criterion characterizing **all** extremal pairs in an unbounded family. These properties are not implied by the previously known catalogue of successful sensor sets.
