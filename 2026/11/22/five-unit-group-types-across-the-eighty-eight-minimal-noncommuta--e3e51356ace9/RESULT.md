# Five unit-group types across the eighty-eight minimal noncommutative centrally essential rings

## Finding

Let \(R\) range over the **88 isomorphism classes of noncommutative centrally essential associative unital rings of order \(64\)** classified by Abyzov, Lyubimtsev and Tuganbaev [1]. Write \(G(R)=R^{\times}\) for its unit group. Then \(G(R)\) has order \(32\), and precisely **five abstract group isomorphism types** occur. Their frequencies, separated by ring characteristic, are:

| Type | \(\#\{g\in G:g^{2}=1\}\) | \(\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\Phi(G)\) | Characteristic \(2\) | Characteristic \(4\) | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(H_1\) | 24 | 1 | 3 | 10 | 13 |
| \(H_2\) | 16 | 2 | 6 | 22 | 28 |
| \(H_3\) | 8 | 1 | 2 | 5 | 7 |
| \(H_4\) | 8 | 2 | 5 | 21 | 26 |
| \(H_5\) | 8 | 3 | 3 | 11 | 14 |
| **Total** | | | **19** | **69** | **88** |

Here the involution count **includes the identity**, and \(\Phi(G)\) is the Frattini subgroup. All five types have a center isomorphic to \(C_2^3\), quotient by center \(C_2^2\), derived group \(C_2\), nilpotency class two and exponent four. In particular, \(H_1\cong D_8\times C_2^2\) and \(H_3\cong Q_8\times C_2^2\).

A canonical presentation defines each type. Let \(E=\langle w,z,t\rangle\cong C_2^3\), with \(w,z,t\) central involutions. Adjoin \(A,B\) subject to \([A,B]=w\) and the following prescribed squares:

| Type | \(A^2\) | \(B^2\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(H_1\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| \(H_2\) | \(1\) | \(z\) |
| \(H_3\) | \(w\) | \(w\) |
| \(H_4\) | \(w\) | \(z\) |
| \(H_5\) | \(z\) | \(t\) |

All other relations are consequences of these, centrality, and the order-two conditions on the generators of \(E\). Each presentation has order \(32\).

## Assumptions and scope

We take the completeness and pairwise nonisomorphism of the 88 ring representatives in [1, Tables 1–3 and Propositions 4.1–4.3] as **published input**, not as a theorem re-proved here. The result applies to all and only those minimal-order noncommutative centrally essential rings. No finite field beyond \(\mathbb F_2\) is used in the central-group calculation.

For every such ring, let \(J=J(R)\), \(Z_0=J\cap Z(R)\). The structural theorems in [1] yield \(R/J\cong\mathbb F_2\), \(|J|=32\), \(Z_0\cong C_2^3\), \(J/Z_0\cong C_2^2\), \(J^2\subseteq Z_0\), \(Z_0^2=0\), \(2Z_0=0\), and the nonzero additive commutator subgroup \([J,J]=\langle w\rangle\) of order two annihilates \(J\). Thus \(R^\times=1+J\) and \(1+Z_0\cong C_2^3\).

## Proof

**Step 1: a quadratic group invariant.** Choose \(a,b\in J\) whose classes form a basis of \(J/Z_0\), and put \(A=1+a\), \(B=1+b\), \(E=1+Z_0\). Since \([a,b]=w\), \(wJ=0\) and \(Z_0^2=0\), the commutator is \([A,B]=1+w\). The induced commutator form on \(G/E\cong\mathbb F_2^2\) is nondegenerate. Hence \(E=Z(G)\), and the group is completely specified by \(u=A^2=1+(2a+a^2)\in E\), \(v=B^2=1+(2b+b^2)\in E\) and the fixed nonzero commutator \(1+w\in E\). We use additive binary notation for these three central coordinates, retaining \(w\) for their commutator vector.

The square of a coset \((x,y)\in\mathbb F_2^2\) is the quadratic map
\[
q(x,y)=xu+yv+xyw\in E\cong\mathbb F_2^3.
\]
Replacing the quotient basis acts by \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\), while changing the basis of \(E\) acts by the stabilizer of \(w\) in \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\). Two presentations define isomorphic groups **if and only if** their pairs \((u,v)\) lie in the same orbit: the center and derived subgroup are characteristic, and an isomorphism must preserve the commutator pairing; conversely these changes of generators extend to isomorphisms between the presentations.

**Step 2: exactly five orbits.** Consider the linear map \(\overline q:G/E\to E/\langle w\rangle\), obtained by reducing \(q\) modulo \(w\). If its rank is zero, the remaining binary quadratic form with nondegenerate polarization has exactly the two usual Arf types: representatives \((0,0)\) and \((w,w)\). If its rank is one, the unique nonzero kernel vector has square either \(0\) or \(w\), and these give representatives \((0,z)\) and \((w,z)\). If its rank is two, all choices are equivalent to \((z,t)\). This proves precisely five abstract groups without relying on enumeration of rings.

For any such group, each coset with \(q(x,y)=0\) contains exactly eight elements squaring to one, since the central group has exponent two. The number of solutions to \(g^2=1\) is therefore \(8|q^{-1}(0)|\). The Frattini subgroup of a finite two-group is generated by its squares and commutators, so
\[
\Phi(G)=\langle u,v,w\rangle\subseteq E.
\]
These give five distinct signatures \((24,1),(16,2),(8,1),(8,2),(8,3)\) displayed above, establishing nonisomorphism and distinguishing every type.

**Step 3: transport each ring representative to its group.** For each ring representative, read \(a^2,b^2\) and the additive doubles \(2a,2b\) from [1, Tables 1–3 and Propositions 4.1–4.3]. Compute \(u=2a+a^2\), \(v=2b+b^2\) in \(Z_0\), classify the pair using Step 2, and count the rings. For clarity, the source's seven disjoint parameter subfamilies produce the following independent subtotals:

| Ring subfamily from [1] | \(H_1\) | \(H_2\) | \(H_3\) | \(H_4\) | \(H_5\) | Sum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Char. 2, Table 1 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 3 | 0 | 10 |
| Char. 2, Table 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 9 |
| Char. 4, \(2\cdot1\in[J,J]\), Table 1 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 3 | 0 | 10 |
| Char. 4, \(2\cdot1\in W_0\setminus[J,J]\) | 3 | 6 | 1 | 6 | 0 | 16 |
| Char. 4, \(2\cdot1\in W_0\), Table 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 9 |
| Char. 4, \(2\cdot1\in Z_0\setminus W_0\), \(\dim W_0=2\) | 2 | 4 | 1 | 3 | 0 | 10 |
| Char. 4, \(2\cdot1\in Z_0\setminus W_0\), \(\dim W_0=1\), Table 3 | 2 | 6 | 1 | 7 | 8 | 24 |

The rows sum to the displayed 88-class distribution. For characteristic four with \(2\cdot1\in W_0\), the radical's additive doubles vanish, so the parameters agree with the characteristic-two representatives. For \(2\cdot1\in Z_0\setminus W_0\), the nonzero doubles are supplied by [1, (4.2)–(4.3)] and are included in the calculation.

## Verification

The standalone `verify_unit_groups.py` reproduces all five quadratic-map orbits by independent \(24\times6\)-element linear-group actions, evaluates the signature of all \(64\) possible pairs of central square values, transcribes each of the 88 published ring parameter representatives, and checks the 7-by-5 and characteristic totals. Separately, it constructs each order-32 group from its multiplication law and exhaustively checks associativity, the size of its center, every square, and exponent four. Run with `python3 verify_unit_groups.py`; the success line is `CENTRALLY_ESSENTIAL_UNIT_GROUP_CENSUS_OK`.

The calculations verify the finite parameter census; the proof of the five-type bound is the rank-and-Arf argument in Step 2, and the completeness of the ring list remains the cited input [1].

## Relationship to prior work

Abyzov–Lyubimtsev–Tuganbaev [1] classify the 88 rings, their additive-group forms, radical multiplication tables and changes of parameters, but do **not** identify their unit groups as abstract groups or count the fibers of this invariant. Their earlier paper [2] proves minimality and supplies partial structure, likewise without this census. General classifications of groups of order 32, insofar as they cover these presentations, do not imply the stated **distribution among 88 ring isomorphism classes** without applying the ring square/double parameters. A related computation of zero-product pair counts across the same ring list concerns a different invariant and does not imply these group-type frequencies.

## Limitations

The finding depends on the validity and completeness of the published 88-ring classification [1]; it does not independently reconstruct that classification. The five types are given by explicit presentations, not by identifiers in an external group database. A finite checker cannot replace the structural orbit argument. While searches identified no covering exact unit-group census, the literature search cannot exclude unindexed or inaccessible earlier work. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
