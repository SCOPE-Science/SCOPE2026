These are reconstructed mathematical arguments and executable calculations, not merely log claims.

Liang (arXiv:1902.02914) studies invariants and bitangents, not the Weil spectrum. Published mathematical-record searches for precise equations, equivalent curve models, Weil polynomials, isogeny collisions, and larger covering classifications did not locate an implication of the five-row classification. Relevant previously published mathematical records on the same geometric curve and Picard Pryms concern hyperflexes or Cartier ranks, not these characteristic-seventeen quadratic-extension counts. Historical records and unindexed tables remain a genuine residual priority risk; unsuccessful search is not proof of absolute novelty.

Two pairs of nonisomorphic geometric curves have isogenous three-dimensional Jacobians, while one maximizer has an elliptic-cube Jacobian and all remaining members have four explicitly irreducible surface Prym factors. The arithmetic data determines counts over every extension and makes the two isogeny collisions explicit. The family restriction is substantive, not arbitrary coefficient slicing.
