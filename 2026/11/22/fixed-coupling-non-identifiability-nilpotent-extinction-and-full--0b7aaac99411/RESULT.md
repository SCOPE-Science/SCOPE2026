# Fixed-coupling non-identifiability: nilpotent extinction and full chaos in a rank-one matrix logistic map

## Finding
Let \(N\ge2\), let \(\mathcal K_N=\{H\in\mathbb C^{N\times N}:H=H^*,\ 0\preceq H\preceq I\}\), and define \(\Phi_B(H)=BH(I-H)B^*\). For unit vectors \(u,v\in\mathbb C^N\) and \(0<\sigma\le2\), put \(B=\sigma uv^*\), \(P=uu^*\), and \(\lambda=\sigma^2|v^*u|^2=|\operatorname{tr}B|^2\).

**Rank-one reduction and full invariant-measure classification.** The image \(\Phi_B(\mathcal K_N)\) is exactly \(J_\sigma=\{sP:0\le s\le\sigma^2/4\}\). For every initial \(H_0\in\mathcal K_N\), the subsequent orbit is \(H_n=s_nP\), \(n\ge1\), with \(s_{n+1}=\lambda s_n(1-s_n)\) and \(s_1=\sigma^2v^*H_0(I-H_0)v\). Every invariant Borel probability for \(\Phi_B\) is supported on \(J_\sigma\); the map \(s\mapsto sP\) gives a bijection between all invariant Borel probabilities of the ordinary logistic map \(s\mapsto\lambda s(1-s)\) on \([0,1]\) and those of \(\Phi_B\).

**Sharp failure of identification by the positive coupling matrix.** For every unit \(u\) and fixed \(A=\sigma^2P=BB^*\), as \(v\) ranges over unit vectors, the effective scalar parameter \(\lambda\) fills the complete interval \([0,\sigma^2]\), without any change in \(A\). In particular, at \(\sigma=2\), the two couplings \(B_\mathrm{chaos}=2uu^*\) and \(B_\mathrm{ext}=2uw^*\), where \(w\perp u\), have the **identical matrix** \(BB^*=4uu^*\). The first restricts to the fully chaotic logistic map \(s\mapsto4s(1-s)\) on \([0,1]P\), with topological entropy \(\log2\) and its familiar arcsine invariant density \(ds/(\pi\sqrt{s(1-s)})\); the second satisfies \(\Phi_{B_\mathrm{ext}}^2\equiv0\) on \(\mathcal K_N\), has only the invariant probability \(\delta_0\), and has topological entropy zero. Both are globally valid, not statements based on numerical experiments.

## Assumptions and scope
Matrices are complex Hermitian positive semidefinite with all eigenvalues in \([0,1]\); \(B\) may be nonnormal, is rank one, and has singular value \(\sigma\le2\). The two extreme entropy claims concern \(N\ge2\) and the *same* rank-one positive semidefinite matrix \(A=4uu^*\). Here \(\delta_0\) denotes the Dirac mass at the zero matrix. The matrix map is the positive-semidefinite map \(H\mapsto BH(I-H)B^*\), **not** the distinct non-Hermitian singular-value map used in some formulations of matrix logistic dynamics.

## Proof
1. **Invariant state space and image.** By spectral calculus, for each \(H\in\mathcal K_N\), \(0\preceq H(I-H)\preceq I/4\). Therefore \(\Phi_B(H)=\sigma^2[v^*H(I-H)v]P\), a positive multiple of \(P\) not exceeding \(\sigma^2/4\le1\). Conversely, taking \(H=tI\), with \(0\le t\le1\), produces coefficient \(\sigma^2t(1-t)\), which ranges continuously over the whole \([0,\sigma^2/4]\). Thus the image is exactly \(J_\sigma\).
2. **Exact scalar recurrence.** Since \(P^2=P\) and \((sP)(I-sP)=s(1-s)P\), \(\Phi_B(sP)=\sigma^2|v^*u|^2s(1-s)P\). This proves the recurrence for all \(n\ge1\), with no limiting argument. The trace identity follows from \(\operatorname{tr}(uv^*)=v^*u\).
3. **All invariant probabilities.** If \(\nu\) is invariant for \(\Phi_B\), then \(\nu(J_\sigma)=\nu(\Phi_B^{-1}(J_\sigma))=1\); the restriction to the segment is conjugate to the scalar logistic map there. Conversely, an invariant probability \(\mu\) for \(f_\lambda\) on \([0,1]\) is supported on \(f_\lambda([0,1])=[0,\lambda/4]\subset[0,\sigma^2/4]\). Pushing \(\mu\) forward under \(s\mapsto sP\) gives an invariant matrix probability. The two operations are inverse.
4. **Full fixed-\(A\) fiber and endpoints.** Fixing \(u\) and \(\sigma\) fixes \(BB^*=\sigma^2uu^*\) for every unit \(v\). Because \(N\ge2\), choose a unit \(w\perp u\) and \(v=\cos\theta\,u+\sin\theta\,w\) for \(0\le\theta\le\pi/2\). Then \(\lambda=\sigma^2\cos^2\theta\) fills \([0,\sigma^2]\). At \(\sigma=2\), \(\theta=\pi/2\), the induced map on \(J_2\) is identically zero; every point reaches zero in at most two iterations. At \(\theta=0\), \(J_2=[0,1]P\) carries the standard parameter-four logistic map. Its conjugacy to the full tent map (apart from the standard endpoint coding) establishes entropy \(\log2\) and the arcsine invariant probability. A continuous map on a compact space whose first iterate lies in a closed invariant subsystem has the same topological entropy as its restriction to that subsystem: finite initial transients cannot create exponential orbit separation. This yields the stated entropy values on \(\mathcal K_N\), not merely on the invariant line.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in a Python 3 environment. The self-contained exact-rational script checks equal coupling products for four rank-one orientations, verifies 336 direct matrix/scalar recurrence equalities (seven initial scalar states, twelve subsequent transitions, four choices), and separately checks two-step extinction on representative positive-semidefinite non-diagonal matrices. No floating-point tolerance enters these checks. The proof above is valid for every finite \(N\) and every admissible real or complex matrix input; finite tests are diagnostics, not its proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Pawela and Życzkowski introduced \(\Phi_B(H)=BH(I-H)B^*\) and studied numerical transfer of chaos in matrix-coupled systems, including the significance of \(A=BB^*\); the same map also appears in an earlier public presentation. Those works establish the *model*, not the classification of all rank-one coupling factors with an identical \(A\). The scalar parameter-four logistic invariant density, tent conjugacy, and entropy are classical and are only invoked, not claimed as new. Related two-node matrix-logistic analyses consider a neutral nonnormal Jordan boundary or transverse fluctuations along a chaotic curve, not rank-one fibers with fixed \(BB^*\) and opposite entropy outcomes. The result is an identifiability obstruction for using \(A\) alone, not a contradiction of results that specify \(B\) itself.

## Limitations
Only rank-one coupling factors are classified. The exact invariant-measure correspondence does not assert uniqueness of invariant measures in chaotic scalar regimes. The positive-entropic extreme has zero as well as many other invariant measures; the arcsine measure is not unique. A nonnormal factor generally yields a logistic parameter below \(\sigma^2\), and complex eigenvalue spectra beyond rank one are not classified. No new entropy theorem for general logistic parameters is claimed. Publication priority has not undergone independent review; closest public results were compared but unindexed equivalents are possible.
