# Fixed-maximum jump-set fibers are Boolean, with a sharp ordinary-semigroup cutoff

## Finding

For a numerical semigroup \(T\subseteq\mathbb N_0\), let \(A(T)\) be its unique finite minimal set of positive generators. Fix a positive integer \(M\) and define
\[
P_M(T)=\{s\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}:M-s\in T,\ M+s\in T\},
\qquad R_M(T)=\{|M-a|:a\in A(T),\ a\ne M\}.
\]
For a finite nonempty positive jump set \(S\) with \(\max S=M\), define its defect semigroup by
\[
\Sigma_S=\langle M,\ M-s,\ M+s:s\in S\rangle.
\]
Here \(\langle\cdot\rangle\) is the additive submonoid of nonnegative integers generated by the listed elements. Require the original primitive-jump condition \(\gcd(S)=1\).

**Fixed-maximum fiber theorem.** The fiber \(\mathcal F_M(T)=\{S:\max S=M,\gcd(S)=1,\Sigma_S=T\}\) is empty exactly when \(M\notin T\) or \(R_M(T)\not\subseteq P_M(T)\). Otherwise
\[
\mathcal F_M(T)=\left\{\{M\}\cup R_M(T)\cup E:E\subseteq P_M(T)\setminus R_M(T)\right\},
\qquad |\mathcal F_M(T)|=2^{|P_M(T)|-|R_M(T)|}.
\]
Its inclusion poset is a Boolean lattice. In particular, the number of jump sets of cardinality \(1+|R_M(T)|+j\) as jump sets equals \(\binom{|P_M(T)|-|R_M(T)|}{j}\), for \(0\le j\le |P_M(T)|-|R_M(T)|\).

For the ordinary numerical semigroup \(H_c=\{0\}\cup[c,\infty)\), where \(c\ge1\), the smallest realizable maximum jump is exactly \(\lceil(3c-1)/2\rceil\). More precisely,
\[
|\mathcal F_M(H_c)|=\begin{cases}
0,&M<\lceil(3c-1)/2\rceil,\\
1,&\lceil(3c-1)/2\rceil\le M\le2c,\\
2^{M-2c},&M\ge2c.
\end{cases}
\]
The two displayed nonzero branches agree at \(M=2c\). Thus, for each \(M\ge2c\), there are exactly \(2^{M-2c}\) distinct primitive jump sets with maximum \(M\) and the same defect semigroup \(H_c\). Their eventual unrestricted and congruence-restricted filtered spectral dimensions are all equal, by the published reconstruction formulas [1]. The assertion is only about eventual dimensions, not necessarily every finite weight.

## Assumptions and scope

A numerical semigroup is a cofinite additive submonoid of \(\mathbb N_0\); it may equal \(\mathbb N_0\). The positive integer \(M\) is the *maximum jump*, not necessarily the multiplicity of \(T\). Jump sets are finite sets, rather than multisets, and include \(M\). If \(M=1\) and \(T=\mathbb N_0\), the unique jump set is \(\{1\}\). If \(M\notin T\), there is no fiber. The ordinary family allows \(c=1\).

The target \(T\) is required to be numerical; gcd-one primitiveness is then automatic for any constructed jump set that generates \(T\). No assertion is made about reconstructing a unique jump set from all-degree spectral measurements. The fixed-maximum fiber problem, rather than existential variable-maximum realizability, is the central claim.

## Proof

**1. Necessary membership.** Suppose \(\Sigma_S=T\) with \(\max S=M\). The generator \(M\) lies in \(T\). For every \(s\in S\setminus\{M\}\), both \(M-s\) and \(M+s\) are positive members of \(T\); consequently \(s\in P_M(T)\). Every positive irreducible element \(a\in A(T)\) is one of the positive members of *any* generating family of \(T\): a representation involving two or more positive generators would contradict irreducibility. Thus \(a=M\) or \(a=M\pm s\) for some \(s\in S\setminus\{M\}\), forcing \(|M-a|=s\in S\). Hence \(R_M(T)\subseteq S\setminus\{M\}\subseteq P_M(T)\).

**2. Sufficiency and complete fibers.** Conversely, assume \(M\in T\) and \(R_M(T)\subseteq P_M(T)\). For any \(E\subseteq P_M(T)\setminus R_M(T)\), put \(S=\{M\}\cup R_M(T)\cup E\). Every generator in the definition of \(\Sigma_S\) is in \(T\), so \(\Sigma_S\subseteq T\). Each \(a\in A(T)\) equals \(M\) or one of the two generators \(M\pm|M-a|\), so \(T\subseteq\Sigma_S\). Therefore equality holds. Moreover
\[
\gcd(S)=\gcd\bigl(M,\ M-s,\ M+s:s\in S\bigr)=\gcd(\Sigma_S)=\gcd(T)=1.
\]
The middle notation means the greatest common divisor of all positive elements of the semigroup. Consequently the primitive condition imposes no additional restriction. Each optional jump occurs in exactly one of the described subsets \(E\), proving the Boolean lattice, its cardinality, and binomial rank counts.

**3. Sharp ordinary threshold.** The minimal generators of \(H_c\) are precisely \(c,c+1,\ldots,2c-1\), including the one-element set \(\{1\}\) at \(c=1\). Necessarily \(M\ge c\). For \(c\ge2\) and \(c\le M<\lceil(3c-1)/2\rceil\), the atom \(a=2c-1\ne M\) has reflected partner \(2M-a<c\) and therefore fails the necessary condition. Conversely, for \(M\ge\lceil(3c-1)/2\rceil\), every atom \(a\in[c,2c-1]\), other than possibly \(a=M\), has \(0<a<2M\) and \(2M-a\ge c\); both reflected generators are in \(H_c\). At \(c=1\) the same sufficiency holds for every \(M\ge1\). This proves the exact threshold.

**4. Sharp ordinary fiber count.** For \(M\ge c\), the admissible optional index set is
\[
P_M(H_c)=\{1,2,\ldots,M-c\},\qquad |P_M(H_c)|=M-c.
\]
When \(M\le2c\) and the fiber is nonempty, every positive lower reflected generator \(M-s\in[c,M-1]\) is an atom, forcing every \(s\in P_M(H_c)\) into \(R_M(H_c)\). Thus the unique jump set is \(\{M\}\cup P_M(H_c)\). For \(M\ge2c\), all \(c\) atoms are strictly below \(M\), their distinct mandatory jumps form
\[
R_M(H_c)=\{M-2c+1,\ldots,M-c\},
\]
which has cardinality \(c\). Therefore the free jumps are precisely \(1,\ldots,M-2c\), giving \(2^{M-2c}\) distinct choices. This also covers \(c=1,M=1\) through the unique-fiber branch and \(M=2c\) through the matching formula.

**5. Spectral consequence.** Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 of [1] show that at all sufficiently large filtration weights the unrestricted dimensions depend only on the fixed maximum jump and the genus of \(\Sigma_S\), and the restrictions modulo any positive divisor of \(M\) depend only on the gaps of \(\Sigma_S\) in the specified congruence class. Every member of \(\mathcal F_M(H_c)\) has the same \(M\) and the same semigroup \(H_c\). Since this fiber is finite for each \(M\), all its members share these eventually identical dimensions beyond a common sufficiently large weight. This yields the exact exponential ambiguity, without claiming equality of the underlying moments or of all finite-degree dimensions.

## Relationship to prior work

Vélez Caicedo and Cadavid [1], first publicly posted 10 November 2026, introduced the semigroup \(\Sigma_S\), proved eventual spectral-dimension reconstruction formulas and gave an example of nonuniqueness. Their Section 5 explicitly proposes intrinsic characterization of possible defect semigroups and Section 3 describes reconstruction up to \(\Sigma_S\), not complete fixed-maximum fibers. Separately, the public theorem [2] already proves that **every** numerical semigroup is realized by *some* maximum jump; no existential surjectivity is claimed here as new. That theorem itself states that classification at a prescribed maximum remains unresolved within its scope. The present fixed-maximum theorem gives an intrinsic minimal-generator test and the **entire** fiber, rather than only existence. The ordinary-family corollary quantifies spectral ambiguity exponentially. Familiar facts about irreducible semigroup generators underlie the argument; the novelty claim is the precise full-fiber identification in this specific spectral inverse construction, not those classical facts.

## Limitations

Historical priority is provisional, particularly outside indexed numerical-semigroup literature and among recent informal research reports. The broader variable-maximum realization theorem is covered by [2]. The formulas reconstruct fibers from a known target semigroup; they do not promise recovery of the hidden jumps from the **full** finite-weight spectral data. The equality of spectral dimensions is eventual. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
