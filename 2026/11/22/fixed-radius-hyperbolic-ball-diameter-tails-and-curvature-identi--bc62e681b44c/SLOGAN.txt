For two volume-uniform points in a fixed-dimensional hyperbolic ball, the probability of geodesic distance within \(\varepsilon\) of its diameter has the exact exponent \((n+3)/2\) and an explicit coefficient strictly increasing with negative-curvature magnitude. This coefficient uniquely identifies curvature when the radius is known; near-diametral pairs have a Dirichlet defect law, and in dimension three the entire upper-half distance tail has a one-dimensional exact integral.
