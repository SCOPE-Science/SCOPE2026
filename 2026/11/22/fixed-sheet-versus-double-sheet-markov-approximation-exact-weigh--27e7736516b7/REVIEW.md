# Review: fixed-sheet versus double-sheet Markov approximation

## Correctness — PASS
The norm is exactly twice the missing fiber mass by Riesz total variation. For an arbitrary-weight mixture, the two component disjointness and triangle inequality reduce the lower bound to the published sharp connected two-sheet distance, and the matching construction uses one identity section with the remaining optimal maps. The all-one-component cases and the one-map boundary are treated separately. For equal weights, one nonsection map must miss a fiber somewhere; balanced integer occupancies give the sharp lower bound. Residue-dependent identity-sheet and double-sheet allocations, implemented by labelled single-atom transports from the cited covering article, achieve it globally. Both formulas hold for every integer budget; no finite test is used as an infinite proof. The reproducible exact-rational diagnostic passed 2,513 assertions.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic qualification
The motivating Duru–İlter–Bilgin article proves the connected-covering laws. Its connected degree-three equal-weight formula matches the present numerical values. The earlier two-map and three-map disconnected-cover results cover both coefficient choices at those budgets. In particular, at three maps the known weighted optimum uses three coefficients of 1/3. The extension relative to these prior results starts at m>=4 and includes the asymptotic contrast between coefficient constraints. Brown (1966) studies different approximation settings, and Kim (1972), Theorems 3–4, gives sequential approximation under different measure-theoretic or Hilbert–Schmidt hypotheses, not exact fixed-budget laws for these covers.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A three-sheet monodromy with one fixed sheet and a nontrivial double component is the minimal example with both a section and a topological obstruction to finite convex representation. The classification shows a universal equal-weight numerical law but a quantitatively different free-weight law, giving precise limiting restriction penalties. This resolves a natural missing case, rather than a parameter substitution into the connected-cover result.

## Scope and risks
The theorem is specific to one identity sheet, one connected double sheet, equal target fiber masses, finite connected base graphs, globally constant convex coefficients, and real continuous-function spaces. The argument imports already-published topological atom transport and does not claim a classification of arbitrary disconnected coverings. The cited comparisons do not certify global priority.
