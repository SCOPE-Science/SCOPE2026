# Review of the palindromic two-read deletion forest theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The statement covers every labeled alphabet size \(q\ge2\) and every positive length, separating the exceptional one-letter case. A self-contained deletion-set argument establishes that a common output of different outer-letter palindromes forces opposite alternating phases and that they then share exactly two distinct outputs. Identical outer letters strip off exactly, preserving the *number* of common descendants. Induction therefore gives the precise centered alternating-edge criterion. The nearest letter outside the central unordered pair is an invariant; each associated component has \(2^r\) vertices and recursively splits into two trees with one connecting edge, proving acyclicity and the exact counts. A disjoint matching of central-triple flips proves the capacity upper bound; selecting palindromes with ordered center and neighbor proves the matching lower bound. The boundary cases and letter multiplicity arguments are explicit. Direct and structural implementations, independently constructed from different specifications, passed their stated ranges from the same package paths.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The earliest historical anchor is the public 2012-11-13 constrained-source single-deletion work of Kulkarni and Kiyavash (arXiv:1211.3128v1). Cai et al. (arXiv:2001.01376v1) and Chrisnata et al. (arXiv:2004.06032v1) establish the multi-read framework and the alternating Type-A phenomenon but do not supply the restricted palindromic independence number. The published one-read palindromic graph gives \(q^m\) codewords at odd length and counts edges under a *one-output* intersection threshold. The published unrestricted two-read graph counts alternating-interval edges but has no independence-number result for the palindrome sublanguage. Neither listed statement determines the newly classified component spectrum without a further argument distinguishing one from two common outputs. Accessible previous claims concern other deletion channels, 3-Abelian palindrome profiles, and finite two-deletion palindrome graphs, not this all-length one-deletion two-read theorem. Searches do not prove global uniqueness; earlier unindexed equivalent derivations remain possible.

## Value — PASS
The exact optimum \(q^m(q+1)/2\) at odd length \(2m+1\), its simple constructive code, and the complete component spectrum solve a natural zero-error problem for symmetric encoded words. The result quantifies the gain from a second distinct read: relative to the one-read optimal size \(q^m\), the optimal codebook expands by the factor \((q+1)/2\). The forest decomposition also explains why a simple matching attains the exact obstruction bound and how conflicts partition into a hierarchy of finite trees. This gives a complete structural classification for all alphabet sizes and lengths, not a routine finite table or a single numerical improvement.

## Closest sources and limitations
Key full-text scientific comparisons are the 2012 constrained-source deletion paper (https://arxiv.org/html/1211.3128v1), the 2020 reconstruction-code paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.06032), the published one-read palindrome graph (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/blob/main/2026/11/8/SCOPE-exact-one-deletion-confusability-and-capacity-of-palindromic-cod--9646637bbf2e/RESULT.md), and the published unrestricted two-read edge census (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/blob/main/2026/10/4/SCOPE-exact-edge-count-of-the-finite-alphabet-two-read-single-deletion--737fed325e91/RESULT.md). The theorem does not extend to general nonpalindromes, arbitrary multiple deletions, or two identical output copies. Unindexed historical precedence and independent proof review remain open.
