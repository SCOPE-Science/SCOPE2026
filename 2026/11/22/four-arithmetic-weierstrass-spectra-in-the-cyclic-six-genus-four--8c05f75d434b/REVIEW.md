# Scientific review: Four arithmetic Weierstrass spectra in the cyclic-six genus-four hyperelliptic family

## Correctness — PASS

In odd characteristic with \(q\equiv2\pmod3\), the separable cubic \(h(t)=t^3+t^2+at+b\) controls the ten branch points of \(xh(x^3)\). A rational cubic root lifts to one fixed point plus a 2-cycle; a quadratic Frobenius pair lifts to either three 2-cycles or one 6-cycle depending on cubic residuosity in \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\); an irreducible cubic lifts to one 3-cycle and one 6-cycle. The discriminant-zero count gives \(q^2-2q+2\) smooth marked parameters. Exact inclusion-exclusion counts split cubics, and the trace-of-cubes polynomial \(2u(u^2+3dv^2)\), with three rational linear factors, gives cubic-residue counts \((q-1)/2\) for zero trace and \((q-5)/6\) otherwise; the quadratic trace is \(-(1+r)\), so its zero case is \(r=-1\). These establish all four frequency polynomials, not merely examples. Because the zero branch point is rational, Frobenius-invariant even subsets modulo complementation give \(\lvert J[2](\mathbb F_q)\rvert=2^{c-2}\), proving 32,16,4,2. Two standalone exact arithmetic programs independently enumerated branch cycles and trace fibers for primes 5,11,17,23,29,41; exhaustive finite tests corroborate the proof but are not used as infinite certification.

The scheme is finite étale because the branch polynomial is squarefree. In characteristic different from two, the even-subset representation of hyperelliptic Jacobian two-torsion is valid. The proof gives the exact smooth-locus count and all four cycle frequencies for every odd prime power with \(q\equiv2\pmod3\); exact finite tests independently reproduce the predictions but are not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge priority)

The initiating Kudo–Nakagawa–Takagi work gives this curve family, automorphisms and superspeciality algorithms, not the four Frobenius spectra or parameter frequency formulae. Detailed characteristic-seven, -eleven and -seventeen arithmetic studies of this family concern Hasse/Cartier ranks and Jacobian Weil polynomials. A closely related result for \(y^2=x^{10}+ux^7+vx^4+x\) over \(q\equiv1\pmod3\) classifies five branch types in a disjoint field regime; it does not imply the present cubic-trace treatment for \(q\equiv2\pmod3\). The distinct Bruin–Kulkarni Jacobian-square family has a four-type Frobenius classification based on quadratic lifting but is not the current family. The 2025 work on paired quadratic/cubic irreducibility counts a different parametrized polynomial pair, and its cubic counting techniques are not claimed novel. Searches for the explicit displayed polynomial, parameter frequencies and cubic-residue formulation revealed no statement dominating the claimed theorem. This is a best-knowledge priority judgment, not a proof of uniqueness of publication.

The closest sources are Kudo–Nakagawa–Takagi (arXiv:2210.14822v1); the genus-four cyclic-six analysis in the disjoint congruence class \(q\equiv1\pmod3\); and Bruin–Kulkarni (arXiv:2309.01959v1, different family). An unindexed exact count might still exist. A separate earlier source was inaccessible; no novelty claim was inferred from that omission.

## Value — PASS

The geometric hyperelliptic branch locus and Jacobian two-torsion are canonical arithmetic structures central to genus-four descent and isogeny questions. This theorem solves the complete finite-field factorization-frequency problem for a natural entire smooth cyclic-six two-parameter family, instead of computing sporadic instances: it gives the four exact types, their nonzero frequencies, minimal splitting degrees and all rational \(2\)-torsion orders. A genuine cubic-residue trace dichotomy beyond the \(q\equiv1\pmod3\) case is needed. Coefficient-marked counts, rather than claims about moduli classes, are deliberately and naturally specified. No downstream application beyond these exact arithmetic invariants is asserted.
