# Review of the four-atom diamond count law

**Correctness: PASS.** The proof identifies the exact same-parity image lattice, enumerates three possible extra/deficit patterns and establishes the equal joint-parity areas by two disjoint radius-\(s\) or radius-\(t\) taxicab diamonds in the unit torus. It verifies means by area and derives the limiting binomial law with bounded parity remainder. Exact-rational and finite geometric checks in `checker.py` corroborate the argument but are not substituted for its general proof.

**Originality: PASS within inspected material.** The independent published papers of Rosen (1989) and Holgate (1990) already address the variance of rationally inclined rectangles, including the present shape; the variance is **not** claimed new. Holgate's complete two-page paper gives interval-count and covariance formulas but not the four-atom complete diamond PMF. Schlie's 2026 survey motivates distribution questions, and the complete 1965 de Bruijn paper addresses *random-angle thin rectangles*, not fixed-angle expanding diamonds. Multiple published-finding corpus aliases and nearby geometry findings were searched. Rosen's full paper was not accessed; historical implicit coverage remains a concrete priority risk.

**Value: PASS.** The result provides a complete, nontrivial finite-random-variable classification of a canonical rational-edge polygon under random translation, plus the phase-dependent non-Gaussian limiting fluctuation spectrum and the exceptional integer/half-integer phases. These statements are stronger than the existing moment results without suggesting unsupported general applicability.

Scientific limitations: The orientation is fixed at \(45^\circ\); no random rotation or smooth convex body is treated. The published variance method and classical exact mean are credited. The scope does not establish absolute literature uniqueness.
