# Review: Four centralizer strata and a saturation law over square-zero local rings

## Correctness

**PASS.** The four-row spectrum follows from a complete nonscalar/scalar residue partition. Over the residue field, every nonscalar \(2\times2\) matrix has a two-dimensional commutant; distinct such algebras intersect exactly in scalars. The frequency counts therefore follow from a disjoint partition of matrix tuples. The ordered-pair formula also has an independent lifting proof, giving \(q^{8d}\) commuting lifts for scalar-scalar residue pairs and \(q^{6d}\) for all other commuting residue pairs. Exhaustive direct matrix multiplication agreed with every claimed centralizer multiplicity and pair count in a genuine nonprincipal ring, as well as two principal boundary rings.

## Originality

**PASS, with a specified historical-priority risk.** Prasad, Singla and Spallone [1], Section 10, give a highly relevant general similarity framework for the nonprincipal radical-square-zero case. This is credited. They leave the simultaneous matrix-tuple orbit problem unresolved and do not give the four-row full-matrix centralizer spectrum or its saturation limit. The published chain-ring commuting-graph result [2] covers the known principal boundary and cannot be used as novelty for it. The affine-monoid [3] and matrix-square-zero [4] results count different objects. The explicit nonprincipal formulas were not located by the stated searches; this does not establish absolute historical uniqueness.

## Value

**PASS.** The invariant is the *entire* distribution of ring centralizers for an unbounded natural family; the new fourth size arises exactly at nonprincipal embedding dimension \(d\ge2\). As the embedding dimension grows at fixed residue field, a positive limiting commuting density and conditional concentration on scalar reductions emerge. This makes a substantive structural comparison with chain rings and avoids attempting a classification of the underlying wild tuple orbits.

## Checked literature and limits

The closest structural precursor is [1], Sections 8–10, in particular Theorem 10.3/Corollary 10.4. The closest existing numerical result is [2], which is confined to principal chain rings. Further overlapping themes [3]–[5] are separated by their mathematical objects or coefficient hypotheses. The proof is elementary but exact; the finite computations do not replace it. The result makes no claims about noncommutative coefficient rings, higher matrix sizes, or the conjugacy-class decomposition. A broader historical equivalent remains a residual risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
