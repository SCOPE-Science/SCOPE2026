# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The resultant vanishes because the middle ordered eigenvalues differ by the same positive constant at every angle. In four dimensions, each specialized common-divisor root lies at one of three discrete offsets from the lower middle eigenvalue. Averaging the roots recovers that eigenvalue as a rational function on an open arc. Monicity forces a Laurent polynomial, a degree comparison forces angular Fourier order at most one, and the gap excludes switching to a different root branch anywhere on the circle. Antipodal symmetry gives the disk support function. The Li–Sze half-plane formula and a polynomial division finish the theorem. All multiplicities and isolated degeneracies are covered. No computation is substituted for an infinite identity.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest motivating work is Jiang's 2026 theorem for the extreme-eigenvalue width of ordinary numerical ranges; the present claim concerns the interior pair in a four-dimensional Hermitian pencil and needs a new discrete-offset and global branch argument. Chien–Li–Nakazato (2021) explicitly distinguish raw ordered eigenvalue gap from the actual width of a higher-rank numerical range, and give a six-dimensional constant-gap counterexample to unqualified extension. Their inspected discussion does not state the four-dimensional rigidity result. Wu (2011) concerns ordinary numerical ranges, not the middle-pair spectral condition. Indexed related findings on special four-dimensional numerical ranges do not imply a universal four-dimensional middle-gap theorem. A differently formulated algebraic-pencil theorem not found by these searches is the residual literature risk, not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Four is the first matrix dimension in which every rank-two numerical range is guaranteed nonempty, making it the natural first higher-rank case. The result identifies a genuine rigidity threshold: a completely direction-independent raw middle gap forces a disk and a circular quadratic factor, whereas a published six-dimensional example has constant analogous gap but a noncircular rank-two range. This resolves a structural boundary case of a motivated higher-rank numerical-range problem rather than a numerical instance or a restatement of the extreme-eigenvalue theorem.

The theorem does not assert a converse, a zero-gap result, or higher-dimensional rigidity; an older equivalent reformulation may remain unknown.
