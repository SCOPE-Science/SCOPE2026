# Four geometric four-cycle wave types in cuboctahedral excitation

## Finding

Let \(G=L(Q_3)\) be the twelve-vertex cuboctahedral graph, the line graph of the three-dimensional cube. Under synchronous three-state, threshold-one Greenberg–Hastings excitation \(F:\{0,1,2\}^{V(G)}\to\{0,1,2\}^{V(G)}\), the only possible eventual least periods are \(1\), \(3\), and \(4\). There is one fixed configuration, \(13{,}580\) primitive three-cycle orbits, and \(48\) primitive four-cycle orbits. The least uniform first-periodic-entry bound is exactly ten updates.

Under spatial graph automorphisms (with temporal phase changes within an orbit identified), the primitive four-cycle orbits split into exactly four classes of sizes \(6,6,12,24\); the primitive three-cycle orbits split into exactly \(319\) classes. In particular, among the four-cycle classes there are two inequivalent spatial patterns with three excited vertices at every update.

The complete joint census of first periodic-entry time \(e\) and eventual least period \(p\), counting labelled initial configurations, is:

These entries sum to \(3^{12}=531{,}441\) configurations. Thus there are no other attractor types or later transient layers.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertices of \(Q_3\) are the integers \(0,\ldots,7\), interpreted as three-bit strings. Its twelve edges are ordered lexicographically by generating \((a,a\oplus2^k)\), where \(0\leq a<8\), \(0\leq k<3\), and retaining \(a<a\oplus2^k\); here \(\oplus\) denotes bitwise exclusive-or. These twelve cube edges are the vertices of \(G\). Two vertices of \(G\) are adjacent if the original cube edges share an endpoint. The resulting simple graph has degree four, twelve vertices and twenty-four edges.

The state \(x_v\in\{0,1,2\}\) at vertex \(v\) changes by
\[
 F(x)_v=\begin{cases}
 1&x_v=0\text{ and some neighbor has state }1,\\
 0&x_v=0\text{ and no neighbor has state }1,\\
 2&x_v=1,\\
 0&x_v=2.
 \end{cases}
\]
No random initialization, external stimulation, periodic boundary condition, or symmetry quotient is imposed on the labelled-state count. A configuration is periodic when \(F^p(x)=x\) for some \(p>0\); its least such \(p\) is its period. First entry \(e(x)\) is the smallest nonnegative integer such that \(F^{e(x)}(x)\) is periodic. A *temporal orbit* identifies the \(p\) phase states of a primitive period-\(p\) cycle; a *geometric orbit class* also identifies cycles related by an automorphism of \(G\).

## Proof

**The exact finite-state reduction.** Encode every colouring by
\[
 s(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{11}x_i3^i,\qquad 0\leq s(x)<3^{12}.
\]
For each of these \(531{,}441\) integers, construct the exact successor integer by the displayed vertex rule. This is the entire map, not a sample. The program `cubo_ghm.cpp` generates the graph using the cube-edge construction above and constructs the full successor array. It then computes indegrees, repeatedly deletes states of indegree zero, and identifies the remaining directed cycles. Every deleted vertex leads to one of those cycles; reversing the deletion order gives its exact distance and eventual period. As the state space is finite and each state has exactly one successor, this procedure exhausts all trajectories and establishes the joint table.

**The sharp transient and period certificate.** A separate all-state implementation `verify_cubo.py` constructs the successor array by coordinate-vector operations rather than the C++ trajectory traversal. It checks \(F^{22}=F^{10}\) on *every* state, but \(F^{21}\neq F^9\) on at least one state. Its exact temporal-period tests show that every state at time ten is fixed by at least one of \(F\), \(F^3\), or \(F^4\), and that the recurrent fixed-power populations are respectively \(1\), \(40{,}741\), and \(193\). These checks independently constrain the global attractor set to periods \(1,3,4\) and confirm the cycle population \((1,40{,}740,192)\). The least integer witness whose first entry is ten is \(s=3856\).

**The spatial symmetry classification.** The eight maximal triangular cliques of \(G\) correspond exactly to the stars of the eight cube vertices. Their intersection graph recovers \(Q_3\), so every automorphism of \(G\) comes from an automorphism of \(Q_3\). All such automorphisms are generated by three-coordinate permutations and the eight binary translations, yielding \(|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|=6\cdot8=48\). Each induces a permutation of the twelve encoded cube edges and commutes with \(F\). For a periodic orbit \(C\), canonicalize first by taking its least state integer, then under the 48 induced permutations. Direct exhaustive orbit enumeration in `verify_cubo.py` yields the following exact histograms, where the left column is the number of temporal orbits in one geometric class:

The two equal-spike-population classes are inequivalent under all 48 graph automorphisms and temporal shifts. The enumeration of the 48-element group and the equality tests are exact integer calculations. Together these arguments give the entire claim.

## Verification

Compile and execute the C++17 exhaustive enumerator from a directory containing the package files:

```sh
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 cubo_ghm.cpp -o cubo_ghm
./cubo_ghm
```

Run the separate full-state NumPy implementation with Python 3 and the dependency from `requirements.txt`:

```sh
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python verify_cubo.py
```

The second program checks the equality of the two time-shifted maps at all \(531{,}441\) states, the sharp bound, complete entry shells, fixed powers, and automorphism classes; it reports `SECOND-IMPLEMENTATION-CHECK-PASSED`. The C++ program prints the complete entry-period table for cross-checking. Both programs construct graph incidence from cube edges independently. Neither relies on a random run, a timeout, numerical root approximation, or an external precomputed table.

## Relationship to prior work

Greenberg–Hastings excitation and its graph formulation are classical. Gravner, Lyu and Sivakoff (first public arXiv version, 24 October 2016) give the vertex rule and a qualitative cycle-integral characterization of fixation and activity on arbitrary finite graphs, in particular their equation (1) and Theorem 3. That theorem does **not**, by itself, evaluate the labelled attractor populations, transient-entry table, or spatial-orbit partition for this particular graph. The cuboctahedral graph's identity as \(L(Q_3)\) and its 48 automorphisms are standard graph facts. Other exact excitation censuses on the icosahedron and the truncated tetrahedron concern different graphs and do not give these populations; a two-contact result on cubic line graphs uses a different local threshold from the single-contact rule here.

The contribution claimed here is only the exact cuboctahedral three-state single-contact temporal and symmetry-resolved census, including the sharp ten-step bound. The general excitation rule and the graph representation are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

This is a **finite** exact classification for one natural twelve-vertex graph, not a theorem for all Archimedean graphs, all cube line graphs, higher excitation thresholds, or arbitrary graph families. The proof is exhaustive and reproducible but relies on correct execution and inspection of the supplied algorithms; finite enumeration here is complete because it covers the entire fixed state space, not because a sample suggests a universal pattern. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed. An unindexed earlier equivalent finite census remains a priority risk.
