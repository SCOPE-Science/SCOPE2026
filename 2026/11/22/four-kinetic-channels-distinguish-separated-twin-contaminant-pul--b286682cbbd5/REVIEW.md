# Scientific review: four-channel aquatic pulse discriminator

## Correctness
PASS. The normalized first-order ODE response is an exponential moment of concentration input. Substitution yields a sparse integer-polynomial identity for the discriminant, confirmed symbolically with the embedded standard-library checker. Every signed factor is controlled on the whole admissible domain. A feasible exact radical three-channel alias proves minimality for worst-case classification. Strict log-concavity proves the unique intermediate-gap maximum. The exact proof establishes universal statements; 1312 checker assertions are supplemental.

## Originality
PASS relative to inspected evidence, subject to material historical uncertainty. Schäfer–Paschke–Liess (2008), Shaw–Mueller (2009), and Hawker (2009) model physically relevant pollutant pulses and first-order sampler responses. Accessible abstracts do not identify a matching four-harmonic-channel sign theorem, but their full texts were not all accessible and are not claimed not to cover it. Kunis–Peter–Römer–von der Ohe (2015) provide general exponential-sum/moment reconstruction, read in full open HTML; their sufficient general-data results do not directly imply minimal four-channel discrimination within the constrained equal-twin class. Targeted records for stormwater sequence ordering, tracer cumulants, finite-rate spectral recovery, and Bateman pulse peaks do not dominate this statement. Historical priority needs further review.

## Value
PASS. Distinguishing one polluting event from two separated events despite time integration is a motivated monitoring inverse problem. The exact four-versus-three threshold and unique sensitivity maximum are structural features, not merely recomputation of one table. The analysis does not claim noise-robust detectability, kinetic calibration, unknown backgrounds, unequal pulse heights, arbitrary pulse counts or practical instrument construction.
