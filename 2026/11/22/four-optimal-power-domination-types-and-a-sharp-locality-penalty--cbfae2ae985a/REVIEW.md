# Scientific review of the Folkman-graph power-domination classification

## Correctness — PASS

The graph is specified independently by a doubled-vertex incidence construction on five labels. The observation process starts with selected vertices and their neighbors and then applies the unique-unobserved-neighbor rule to a fixed point. Full enumeration of all subsets of cardinality at most four establishes minimum size four and precisely 560 optima; an independent enumeration establishes an exact four-family description. Orbit enumeration over a generating set of the graph's natural automorphisms yields 40 orbits at size four, of which only four are successful. Every legal one-token move is generated by the exact three-point-intersection test and, for slides, the original graph edge test. Independent implementations recompute all pairwise shortest distances, the degree and eccentricity distributions, and the sharp four-move distance penalty. Two complete standard-library programs were executed without error from their finalized sources. The result is fixed and finite; no empirical extrapolation is asserted.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority

The inspected primary paper *Power domination reconfiguration* (arXiv:2201.01798, originally 2022) defines token jumping and sliding and proves general results for different graph families, but gives neither the Folkman graph's minimum-set classification nor the two exact movement metrics. Its threefold edge-subdivision construction is not equivalent to the Folkman graph's twin-vertex construction. The inspected 2022 reconfiguration survey treats independent, ordinary dominating and connected dominating sets, not the present exact power-domination problem. Specific queries targeting Folkman, its incidence description, the 560-set count, four orbit sizes, and the 4,680/1,920 transition counts yielded no matching covering source. Related indexed computations for other named graphs do not imply this statement. An unindexed earlier computation remains a possible but currently unsupported priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The Folkman graph is the smallest regular edge-transitive graph that is not vertex-transitive, making it a particularly natural test of whether optimal monitoring placements inherit hidden symmetries and whether locality matters. The answer is not a bare single-number census: four concrete placement geometries partition every optimum, each is operationally mobile, all optimal states communicate under restricted sliding, the slide centers occupy exactly one placement type, and the sharp penalty occurs at 1,320 pairs. These are reproducible, structurally interpretable benchmarks for sensor-network reconfiguration and give a finite test case for studying locality effects beyond vertex-transitive networks.

## Boundaries and closest sources

Only the specified twenty-vertex Folkman graph is covered. The investigation did not establish a general theorem for every semisymmetric graph. The closest primary source is Bjorkman et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01798 , Sections 1, 4 and 5; a complementary general survey is https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10526 . Historical-priority uncertainty remains for inaccessible or unindexed works. The computation and proof were checked here, but external independent validation has not occurred.
