# Four orbit types and exact enumeration of minimum identifying codes in even square rook graphs

## Finding

For each integer \(k\ge3\), let \(n=2k\). All minimum radius-one closed-neighborhood identifying codes of the square rook graph \(R_n=K_n\square K_n\) have cardinality \(3k\) and split into **exactly four** orbits under the full graph automorphism group, generated by independent row/column permutations and board transposition. Represent a code \(C\) as a spanning bipartite graph \(H_C\) on \(n\) labeled row vertices and \(n\) labeled column vertices, with one edge for each selected cell. The four component decompositions and labeled counts are:

| Type | Components of \(H_C\) | Number of labeled codes |
|---|---|---|
| I | \(kP_4\) | \(((2k)!)^2/k!\) |
| IIa | \(K_2\sqcup P_6\sqcup(k-2)P_4\) | \(((2k)!)^2/(k-2)!\) |
| IIb | \(K_2\sqcup T_6\sqcup(k-2)P_4\) | \(((2k)!)^2/(k-2)!\) |
| IIc | \(K_2\sqcup P_5\sqcup P_5\sqcup(k-3)P_4\), opposite path orientations | \(((2k)!)^2/[4(k-3)!]\) |

Here \(T_6\) is the unique six-vertex tree formed by three arms of lengths \(1,2,2\) attached to one center. All six-vertex components are balanced between bipartition sides. The two five-vertex paths in type IIc have respective bipartition sizes \((3,2)\) and \((2,3)\).

Consequently the total number of minimum codes is

\[
N_{2k}=((2k)!)^2\left(\frac1{k!}+\frac2{(k-2)!}+\frac1{4(k-3)!}\right).
\]

The proportion of type IIc codes equals

\[
\frac{k(k-1)(k-2)}{4+8k(k-1)+k(k-1)(k-2)},
\]

and tends to \(1\). Thus, although all four classes persist, asymptotically almost every labeled minimum code has an isolated selected edge and two oppositely oriented five-vertex path components.

## Assumptions and scope

A *radius-one identifying code* in a graph is a vertex set \(C\) such that for every vertex \(v\) the intersection \(N[v]\cap C\) is nonempty, and all these intersections are pairwise distinct. In \(R_n\), two cells are adjacent when they share a row or column. In \(H_C\), each cell \((i,j)\) becomes the edge joining the row node \(r_i\) and column node \(c_j\). Both node sets have \(n\) distinct labels. Paths and trees are considered as bipartite graphs with their sides distinguished in the enumeration. The complete graph automorphism group additionally allows board transposition. Under the subgroup preserving the two sides, type IIb splits according to whether the degree-three center lies on the row or column side, giving five orbits; transposition identifies these two, leaving four orbits under the full group. The theorem covers every even \(n\ge6\), with no bound on \(k\); it does not classify \(n=4\), odd \(n\), or general rectangular boards.

## Proof

**1. A signature criterion.** For every square cell \((i,j)\), its signature is exactly

\[
I(i,j)=\{e\in E(H_C): e\text{ is incident with }r_i\text{ or }c_j\}.
\]

Assume every row and column node has positive selected degree. All signatures are then nonempty. Two distinct cells in one row \((i,j),(i,j')\) have equal signatures precisely when both columns \(c_j,c_{j'}\) are leaves adjacent to the same node \(r_i\). Indeed, after subtracting their common selected row edges, the two residual sets are disjoint, so both must be empty; positive column degree then forces the two respective edges to that row. The analogous statement applies to cells in the same column.

For cells in different rows and different columns, \((i,j)\) and \((i',j')\), the two incident-edge sets can coincide only if they consist of the two cross edges \(r_ic_{j'}\) and \(r_{i'}c_j\). Positive degrees force both edges to exist and to form distinct isolated \(K_2\) components. Conversely, those two isolated edges produce equal signatures for the two cross-corner cells. Therefore, under positive row and column degrees, \(C\) is identifying **if and only if** (a) no vertex has two adjacent degree-one neighbors and (b) at most one isolated \(K_2\) component occurs in \(H_C\).

**2. Every optimum supports all rows and columns.** If row \(r_i\) contains no code cell, a second empty row would create identical signatures. Moreover, if any other row had just one selected cell \((i',j)\), then \(I(i,j)=I(i',j)\), again a contradiction. Hence at least \(2(n-1)\) cells must be chosen. For even \(n\ge6\), this exceeds \(3n/2\). The same argument applies to an empty column. Since the explicit type-I construction below uses \(3n/2\) cells, every optimal code has all row and column degrees positive.

**3. Bound and defect.** In a spanning selected graph satisfying criterion (a), every connected component other than an isolated \(K_2\) contains at least four vertices. In fact, components of order three would be stars with two leaves adjacent to the same center, contrary to (a). For any connected component of size \(v\ge4\) and edge count \(e\ge v-1\), its *defect* satisfies

\[
4e-3v\ge v-4\ge0.
\]

Equality holds only for a four-vertex tree, and criterion (a) then forces that tree to be \(P_4\). If the whole graph has no isolated \(K_2\), summing gives \(4|C|-3(2n)\ge0\). If one isolated \(K_2\) occurs, it contributes defect \(-2\), while all other components have nonnegative defect. Thus \(4|C|-6n\ge-2\), which for even \(n\) and integral \(|C|\) also yields \(|C|\ge3n/2=3k\). Type I exists: partition the \(2k\) rows and columns into \(k\) pairs, pair the row-pairs with column-pairs, and choose a \(P_4\) on each resulting \(2+2\) block. Criterion (a),(b) makes it an identifying code, proving optimality.

**4. Equality classification.** In any optimal code, \(2n=4k\) and \(|C|=3k\), so the total defect is zero. With no isolated \(K_2\), every component must have defect zero, hence consists of four-vertex paths: type I.

With one \(K_2\), the sum of the defects of all other components must be \(2\). The bound \(4e-3v\ge v-4\) shows that every remaining component is a tree and that there are just two size possibilities: one tree of order six, or two trees of order five, with the other components all \(P_4\). A five-vertex tree satisfying criterion (a) is necessarily \(P_5\); its bipartition sizes are \((3,2)\) or \((2,3)\), so the two five-vertex paths must have opposite orientations to balance the entire row and column sets. A six-vertex tree satisfying (a) must be \(P_6\) or the one-branch tree with arm lengths \((1,2,2)\): a tree of order six with a degree-four vertex has at least two adjacent leaves, and a tree with two degree-three vertices also has two leaves adjacent to each such vertex. With exactly one degree-three vertex, its three arms must have lengths \((1,2,2)\) rather than \((1,1,3)\), the latter again having two adjacent leaves. Both eligible six-vertex trees have three row and three column nodes. This gives IIa, IIb and IIc. Conversely each listed decomposition obeys (a),(b) and covers all row/column vertices, so each produces an identifying code. The different component shapes show that the four types are disjoint. Within types I, IIa and IIc, the bipartite component shape determines a single orbit under independent row/column permutations. In type IIb, a degree-three center on a row is not carried to a column center by those permutations, so there are two such subgroup orbits. Board transposition interchanges them and is an automorphism. All full graph automorphisms arise from row/column permutations and, optionally, transposition: the maximal cliques are exactly rows and columns, and their two families must be preserved or exchanged. Hence the four displayed types are precisely the four orbits under the full automorphism group.

**5. Exact labeled enumeration.** For specified two rows and two columns, the number of paths \(P_4\) is \(4\). For specified three rows and two columns, the number of paths \(P_5\) is \(6\). For specified three rows and three columns, \(P_6\) and \(T_6\) each have \(36\) possibilities; of the latter, \(18\) have the central degree-three vertex in the row shore and \(18\) in the column shore. Hence partitioning labels into equal-sized components yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm I}&=\frac{(n!)^2}{k!},\\
N_{\mathrm{IIa}}&=n^2\binom{n-1}{3}^2\cdot36\cdot\frac{((n-4)!)^2}{(k-2)!}=\frac{(n!)^2}{(k-2)!},\\
N_{\mathrm{IIb}}&=\frac{(n!)^2}{(k-2)!},\\
N_{\mathrm{IIc}}&=n^2\left[\binom{n-1}{3}\binom{n-4}{2}\right]^2\cdot 6^2\cdot\frac{((n-6)!)^2}{(k-3)!}=\frac{(n!)^2}{4(k-3)!}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first identity uses the \(k!\) matching of row-pairs to column-pairs and one four-path per paired block. In the other cases, choose the isolated edge, the distinguished larger component(s), and then unordered pairs of the remaining row and column labels. For IIc the two paths are distinguished by which side contains three vertices; no extra division by two occurs. Their sum is the displayed formula. Dividing IIc by the sum and canceling \((n!)^2/k!\) gives the displayed proportion, which tends to one.

## Verification

A direct exhaustive program inspects all \(\binom{36}{9}=94,143,280\) nine-cell subsets of \(R_6\), testing actual closed-neighborhood signatures, and classifies every successful subset by the connected-component degree patterns of \(H_C\). It finds \(1,252,800\) valid codes, split as type I \(86,400\), IIa \(518,400\), IIb \(518,400\), and IIc \(129,600\), exactly matching the formula. An independently structured program based on the signature criterion reproduces those four counts. At \(n=4\), a separate exhaustive script finds \(2,208\) minimum six-cell codes, including configurations with an empty row or column; this confirms why the small order is excluded. These checks validate finite examples, not the universal proof, which is given above. Sources are provided in the standalone verification files.

## Relationship to prior work

Gravier, Moncel and Semri proved \(\gamma^{\mathrm{ID}}(K_n\square K_n)=\lfloor3n/2\rfloor\) and proved one orbit for odd \(n\). For even \(n\), their Lemma 2 describes the no-isolated-edge equality case and their closing discussion exhibits another minimum construction with an isolated edge. The present theorem does **not** claim their cardinality bound, their no-isolated-edge class, or their example as new; it classifies and counts all three isolated-edge classes and gives the aggregate four-type enumerator. Foucaud et al. proved related pendant-free sufficient criteria and a cardinality bound for identifying codes in line graphs, but their statement does not count the even square-rook optimizers. Earlier enumerations at the exceptional \(4\times4\) board likewise do not imply the all-order formula.

## Limitations

The conclusion is for even \(n\ge6\) and ordinary radius-one closed-neighborhood identification. The symmetric group on rows and the symmetric group on columns preserve the classification; transposition of the whole board adds no new component type. No claim is made for other sensors, noisy identification, odd orders, general rectangles, or sharp reconfiguration metrics. The bibliographic comparison inspected the closest full primary article and relevant line-graph theorem; undiscovered or unindexed earlier complete enumerations remain a priority risk. Numerical verification through the six-by-six board alone cannot prove the all-order theorem.
