# Scientific review: minimum identifying codes on even square rook graphs

## Correctness — PASS

For every even board side \(n\ge6\), all optimal code signatures reduce to edge-incidence unions in a selected spanning bipartite graph. The proof checks domination, same-row and cross-row separation, the empty-row boundary, integral defect inequalities, equality component classification, bipartite balance, labeled enumeration and the limiting fraction. The four full-automorphism type counts match an exhaustive signature-level computation on every nine-element subset of the \(6\times6\) board. The independent structural test agrees. Full all-order validity is established by the symbolic proof rather than inferred from finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS with stated residual risk

Gravier–Moncel–Semri (2008) prove the minimum \(\lfloor3n/2\rfloor\), classify optimal even configurations **without** isolated selected edges, and give only an example **with** one; they do not enumerate all isolated-edge forms or their number. Foucaud et al. (2013), Proposition 10 and Lemma 7, give an edge-identification criterion and minimum size for complete bipartite line graphs; their statements also do not yield the four full-automorphism orbit types or full minimum-code census. Goddard–Wash (2013) develop minimum sizes on rectangular rook graphs, rather than these even square equality classifications. Existing exact \(4\times4\) code enumeration is a boundary instance (with extra degenerate configurations), not the all-even formula. Overlap checks included published mathematical records, literature titles, aliases and implication comparisons. Priority is not certified against every unpublished/unindexed enumeration.

## Value — PASS

The pre-existing even-order work explicitly leaves multiple optimal constructions without a complete optimizer classification. The theorem closes this natural all-order classification with exactly four full-automorphism orbit types (five under side-preserving permutations), an exact factorial enumerator and a striking asymptotic: one type becomes almost sure under uniform sampling of optimal codes. Such exact optimizer geometry is useful for uniform random code generation, degeneracy analysis and optimal sensor arrangement counting, not merely for the already known scalar optimum.

## Limitations and closest sources

Only even \(n\ge6\), ordinary closed-neighborhood identifying codes. Full source text of the original 2008 paper and relevant results of the later line-graph paper were inspected; the complete ten-page rectangular-code paper was also inspected and does not enumerate these optimum configurations. Independent scholarly evaluation is outstanding.
