# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The precise graph and admissibility definition are fixed. Step 1 derives an endpoint-toggle recurrence on path two-independence complexes with four-vertex reduction and separately checked small bases. Step 2 partitions every face excluded by a triangle-vertex toggle into three disjoint shifted path families. Step 3 identifies a single global sequence of vertex toggles, hence an acyclic matching for every cycle length, and counts its critical cells in all four congruence classes. Step 4 constructs a CW model with one zero-cell and only equally dimensional positive cells, so the wedge assertion is an integral homotopy statement. Complete finite face-poset checks for cycle lengths three through sixteen reproduce the predicted counts and acyclicity, but are not substituted for the induction.

## Originality
PASS on the checked literature, with residual priority uncertainty. Sawant's 2026 tree theorem does not apply to the bicyclic graph. Deshpande–Singh's path/cycle and other explicitly classified families do not include a one-vertex gluing of triangle and cycle. Daundkar–Panja–Prasad's graph-wedge study is for ordinary independence, not the degree-at-most-one induced-subgraph complex here. General chordal existence results do not cover the induced long cycle and do not give the exact ranks. A published record concerning the *strong discrete-Morse matching complex* of the same underlying graph addresses a different functor; its homotopy types do not imply those of two-independence complexes. Search failures alone are not priority proofs; equivalence under an unindexed name remains possible.

## Value
PASS. This is a natural first genuinely bicyclic cactus family beyond the recent tree higher-independence results. It gives a complete all-length homotopy calculation, not an arbitrary finite table or a small parameter increment. The shared vertex couples the two petals in the selected-component constraint, and the proof resolves that coupling into three separate path fibers. Its alternating three-, two-, one-sphere and contractible cases give an exact boundary-condition effect that is not obtained by merely taking a join of known cycle complexes. The theorem is not claimed to classify all cactus graphs.
