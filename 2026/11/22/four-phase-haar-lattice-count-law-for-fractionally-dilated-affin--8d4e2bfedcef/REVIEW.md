# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The count is reduced exactly to a two-dimensional sum of lattice pairs with a diagonal window. Inclusion-exclusion gives identity (1) with integer-valued step variables and no estimates. Its eight lines exhaust all possible changes of these variables. Their internal triple intersections have only three parameter values, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, so cell-area masses have at most quadratic parameter dependence within each phase. The exact polygon-clipping check provides three distinct parameter values in each phase, matching the stated formula and testing six independent integer scales; together with the proved polynomial bound, this verifies all real t. All endpoint and normalization formulas agree; a finite check alone would not establish the theorem.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
Freyer–Hofstätter (2026) develop the general translation-count/Ehrhart framework, and Tokmachev (2025) gives constant counts of centrally symmetric **integer** polygons, so the n-integral endpoints are known. The current family is nonintegral at fractional radii; the exact four-phase law and all of its quadratic mass polynomials are not given in the inspected material. Related records address nearest-site quantization, covering radii and different random area statistics; none implies the same occupancy law. Full-text access to some older related articles was incomplete, and an unindexed earlier tabulation is possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem supplies a complete, sharp and uniform-in-scale classification of a natural translated-lattice statistic for a central six-sided polytope. It identifies the exact critical fractions 1/3, 1/2 and 2/3, all support values and masses, and a nonvanishing scaled fluctuation law. These are geometrically motivated consequences of three interacting lattice strip constraints, not arbitrary isolated coefficient calculations.

## Limitations and validation
The classification is specific to the root-type affine hexagon with fixed lattice orientation; it makes no claim for arbitrary Euclidean rotations, other hexagons or small radii below one. Boundary shifts have probability zero. Same-model algebraic and checker verification do not substitute for independent mathematical review.
