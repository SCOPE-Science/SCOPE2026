# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The forward-projection dynamics is a monotone period-one finite-phase map. The phase squeeze proves initial-phase independence; possible cycles have frequencies only \(1,3/2,2,3\); the earliest-feasible dominance argument and three explicit feasible cyclic schedules prove each exact threshold with equality cases. The five interval laws and variances are direct exact cycle calculations. The standalone rational checker executed successfully, but finite checks are not used as a proof for all phase offsets.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Kaymakçalan–Selçuk prove the recurrence and only the uniform-lattice firing rate, explicitly pointing to periodic clocks as an outstanding arithmetic question. Older forced-neuron mode-locking papers do not describe constant-input on-clock forward projection with three opportunity phases and these sharp formulas. A two-phase opportunity classification and phase-inhomogeneous clocks are related but do not imply the sharp three-gap all-offset classification and the hidden equal-rate interspike transition as stated. A broadly formulated periodic event-scheduling theorem might imply the rate classification; this remains a historical priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The smallest nonuniform clock capable of all four rational rates gives a complete phase diagram and a significant warning about rate-only inference: variance jumps fourfold at a parameter where mean firing rate is unchanged. This directly resolves the motivating paper's explicit periodic-clock arithmetic question in a natural three-phase family. The exact thresholds and interval laws, not only their numerical demonstration, provide reusable benchmark quantities.

## Limits
This addresses the on-clock constant-input model and not arbitrarily forced neurons, more than three clock phases, or interspike transients; independent validation and broader literature priority audit are not performed.
