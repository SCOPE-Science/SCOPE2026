# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim has a universal planar component and a sharp polygonal boundary. The first-moment Steiner formula is an established starting point; differentiating it yields the explicit stationary equation with two positive coefficients and a strictly increasing coefficient ratio. This proves uniqueness of any isolated coincidence, with the required collinearity condition included. For triangles, the determinant of the three barycentric center-vectors is nonzero for scalene shapes by strict concavity of sine; the isosceles apex coordinates are strictly ordered except in the equilateral case. For trapezoids, independent chord, edge, and turning-angle integrals give the three center formulas. At the height where the unrounded area and perimeter centroids meet, the curvature centroid lies **strictly below** them; the key strict inequality follows from monotonicity of \(\arcsin z/z\) on \((0,1/2)\). This provides a nonempty interval of genuine positive-radius reversals for every unequal base ratio. First-order expansion of the root equation yields the positive, dimensionless linear onset coefficient. Strict rational enclosures of \(\pi\) and \(\arctan(1/3)\) certify the four-vertex witness. The standalone `verify.py` passes 256 deterministic tests, including independent edge-strip and vertex-sector moment reconstructions; finite tests supplement, rather than replace, these general proofs.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The general curvature-centroid polynomial comes from Gollapudi et al. (2021). The indexed *Hyperbolic curvature paths and quantitative triangle rigidity* already proves the triangle behavior and describes the hyperbolic/collinear dichotomy, but explicitly does not classify general collinear-center bodies; these are **prior**, not rebranded findings. The indexed *A universal axial surface–volume centroid transition for tangential frusta* already gives the unrounded trapezoid static height at which the two centroids meet, as the planar case of its formula; this height is **not** claimed original. Neither inspected result identifies a **strictly positive** outer-rounding radius of temporary equality for an open family of unequal-base trapezoids, establishes the minimum four-vertex complexity, or calculates the new one-sided radius-onset slope. The November 2026 sharp centroid-distance theorem and Nazarov–Ryabogin–Yaskin's earlier planar version concern separation bounds for one body, not equality after arbitrary offsets. Direct claim/alias/parameter searches in indexed mathematical records produced no stronger theorem covering the new onset and vertex minimum. That absence is not a guarantee of historical novelty; unindexed classical geometric-center literature remains a specific risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural question is whether rounding can *erase and subsequently recreate* a difference between two canonically defined centers of the same convex body. The sharp vertex threshold distinguishes a rigid three-vertex regime from the first possible nonmonotone regime. The witness is not a one-off tuning: for every strictly unequal pair of trapezoid bases, a nonempty open interval of heights realizes the effect, and its birth near a canonical geometric threshold has a universal explicit linear law. This is a structural geometric boundary result about a natural invariant, not merely a regression table or a renamed form of the pre-existing centroid polynomial. It does not claim a full global phase diagram or independent novelty certification.
