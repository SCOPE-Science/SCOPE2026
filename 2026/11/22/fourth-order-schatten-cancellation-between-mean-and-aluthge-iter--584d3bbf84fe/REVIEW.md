# Scientific review — fourth-order Schatten cancellation

## Correctness — PASS
The weighted-shift calculation identifies the iterates with the binomial arithmetic mean of weights and the exponential of the binomial mean of their logarithms. Strict Jensen inequality gives a positive, nonzero gap; the explicit fixed-iteration expansion gives exactly \(d_{n,k}\sim k/(32n^4)\) and hence the sharp \(p>1/4\) condition. The centered-binomial variance calculation gives the uniform \(k^{-3}\) profile. Efron–Stein and a binomial lower-tail bound provide a summable domination, allowing the exact Schatten integrals and operator-norm maximum. The accompanying finite tests confirm rational identities and selected constants; they do not replace the analytic argument.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Chabbabi–Ostermann (arXiv:2203.15658, initial preprint 2022-03-29) analyze \(m\)-isometries and preservation under transforms, not the difference of iterates in a trace ideal. Chabbabi–Curto–Mbekhta (arXiv:1812.03499) establish mean-iteration and weighted-shift formulas, which are explicitly used as prior inputs. Altwaijry–Conde–Feki–Stanković (DOI:10.3934/cpaa.2024078) study one-step Schatten norm inequalities for Schatten operators, not the noncompact-shift orbit difference, its \(1/4\)-threshold or its full constants. Zhou–Yang (arXiv:2606.17556) investigate power mean transforms but not this comparison. Equivalent reformulations as binomial Jensen gaps and as compact differences were checked. The inspected literature and mathematical record search gave no stronger implication covering the reported theorem; negative searches do not rule out unindexed literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The two naturally defined nonlinear orbits are individually only harmonic-order compact perturbations of the unweighted shift, yet their difference has fourth-order singular decay, and is trace class with a sharp Schatten membership transition. The complete degree profile and all-p trace-mass constants distinguish operator-norm and trace-ideal relaxation and quantify genuine cancellation beyond a routine first-step inequality.

## Limits and independent assessment
The result is for classical transforms on a positive one-parameter family of non-isometric 2-isometric shifts, and the bounds fix the parameter before allowing the iteration count to grow.
