# Scientific assessment: fractional cut covers and two-probe trace minimax risk

## Correctness — PASS

The argument quantifies over **all joint couplings** of two marginally full-cube-uniform Rademacher probes, including couplings not expressible by a fixed coordinate sign flip. For each coupling the product signing \(D_i=Z_iW_i\) induces a cut distribution. Selecting a matrix supported on a single edge gives variance exactly four times that edge's non-crossing probability. This proves a universal lower bound of four times the maximum non-crossing probability, without assuming independent probes. Conversely, independently sampling full-cube signs and an optimal random signing makes the two probes marginally uniform; distinct edge sign-products are orthogonal, so the entire error covariance is diagonal. That construction attains the lower bound for all normalized edge coefficient vectors, establishing equality. The conversion from normalized cut probabilities to a fractional cut cover uses scaling in both directions and has no limiting approximation. Bipartiteness, edge-transitive reduction, complete-graph parities and the pendant-triangle witness follow from elementary exact cut counts. The diagonal can be shifted to produce positive-definite matrices without changing variance. A standalone executable finite verifier returned `PASS 594 finite LP, covariance, and exact combinatorial assertions`; finite checks are not used to infer infinite-graph generality.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit priority limits

The fractional cut-cover parameter and its LP are established mathematical objects, not new inventions. The stochastic-probing variance identity for a **given partition** is established in Frommer, Rinelli and Schweitzer (2023, §§2.2,3.1–3.2). Their probes have zeros outside assigned color classes, unlike two individually full-cube-uniform probes, and their optimization changes sample allocations after fixing a coloring. Neto and Ben-Ameur (2019) and Proença and coauthors (2023) cover the fractional cut-covering problem and graph duality, not the two-query trace-minimax conclusion. Public fixed-matrix coupling results on complete graphs and weighted five-cycles allow couplings adapted to the **numerical matrix entries**; they neither assert nor imply the order-reversed, matrix-blind worst-case theorem. A known shared-sign Hadamard scheme already realizes the complete-graph coefficient at dyadic orders, so no priority is asserted for that special numerical value. The claimed contribution is the all-graph, **all-couplings** minimax identity and its universal attainment/lower bound, including randomized-cut necessity on nonbipartite graphs. The 2019 fractional-cover article could not be read in full, and a specialist earlier trace-minimax derivation could exist; independent historical-priority review is outstanding.

## Value — PASS

Known graph sparsity but unknown edge magnitudes is a natural robust regime for matrix-free trace computation. The result solves precisely whether dependence between two *fully uniform* sign probes can be designed to control the worst case, despite the much wider space of nonlinear couplings. The sharp answer is a pre-existing graph invariant, giving an implementable optimal cut distribution in finite graphs, zero-variance classification by bipartiteness, and an exact separation between graph-robust and matrix-specific coupling. The dramatic odd-cycle law \(4/(2k+1)\) and the factor-of-two robust improvement over every deterministic flip on nonbipartite graphs show that the result is not a merely cosmetic parameter substitution. It also warns that a single uniform-edge maximum cut does not determine minimax risk on nontransitive graphs. No claim is made about large-graph LP runtime, adaptive queries, other marginal families, or universal realized-error dominance.
