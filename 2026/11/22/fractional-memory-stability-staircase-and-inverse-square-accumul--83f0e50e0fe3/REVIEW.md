# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The full real stationary set is obtained from the exact equations rather than a truncated inverse-sine branch: for \(c>1\), the positive-determinant branches are exactly the two symmetric states \(\pm P_m\), for every integer \(m\ge0\). Their Jacobian trace is \(-1\) and determinant is \(D_m=2\pi\sqrt{c-1}(m+\delta)\), while all other nonzero branches have a positive real eigenvalue and are linearly unstable. The scalar Caputo-delta generating function and the necessary-and-sufficient eigenvalue stability domain reduce stability to \(D_m<D_\alpha\). A strict monotonicity argument proves the uniqueness of \(D_\alpha\), hence exact integer counting and the inverse-square threshold. The standalone checker reproduced the equilibrium, Jacobian, spectral and count identities. Mathematical risks: the claim concerns **linearized** stability only, not nonlinear asymptotic attraction; boundary equality is excluded. Finite diagnostics are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality: PASS within examined evidence
Primary full text of Liu–Tang–Hong (2022) and Bi–Zhang–Xiang–Wang (2013) describes the model and selected principal equilibria, not the all-branch fractional-order stability staircase or the inverse-square accumulation. Busłowicz–Ruszewski (2013) supplies the general eigenvalue condition that this proof applies, and is not claimed as new. The earlier integer-order map was fully credited; only the order-dependent fractional census is the new contribution. Published-result semantic searches and independent literature queries found no direct dominating theorem for this exact map; no search establishes absolute uniqueness. Risk: unindexed, translated, or subsequent studies could have equivalent branch classifications. The text of three primary papers was inspected; PDF graphical screenshot retrieval was partly inaccessible, with source text available.

## Value: PASS
For a natural model where a prior study emphasizes the two principal fixed points, the full real stationary spectrum yields a sharp parameter-dependent count of linearly stable equilibria. In particular, fractional memory gives stable spectral branches when the integer map has none, and the exact accumulation exponent and prefactor constrain interpretation of increasingly remote branches near the degenerate parameter. This is a structural stability classification, not an arbitrary small-parameter computation. No claims are made about biological positivity, finite physical windows, nonlinear basins or experimental observability.
