# Low-rank free spectrum and basis rigidity of Ma–Yue’s five-element semiring

## Finding

Let \(S\) be Ma and Yue’s five-element additively idempotent semiring, with the tables below. For a positive finite integer \(r\), let \(F_r\) be the relatively free algebra on \(r\) generators in the variety generated by \(S\), in the signature \(\{+,\cdot\}\) without constants.

1. Every generating ordered \(r\)-tuple of \(F_r\) is a permutation of its free generators. Consequently \(\operatorname{Aut}(F_r)\cong\mathfrak S_r\).
2. The orders in ranks \(1,2,3\) are \(2,21,778\), respectively. The numbers of term functions with content equal to the entire generator set are \(2,17,721\), respectively. In rank three the numbers with content sizes \(1,2,3\) are \(6,51,721\).

The rank-three calculation gives the exact size and content distribution of the free algebra in which Ma and Yue’s Lemma 4.11 studies specific elements for its subvariety criterion. The all-rank conclusion concerns the generating tuples of the full algebra of sums of words, not merely its multiplicative words.

Identify \(F_r\) with the term functions on \(S^r\), with the operations applied pointwise. Two terms induce the same function precisely when their equality is an identity of \(S\) and hence of its generated variety. A word is a nonempty product of variables. By distributivity every term is equivalent to a nonempty sum of words. The content of a term is its nonempty set of variables; the proof below shows this descends to term functions.

## Proof

**Content and rigidity.** Set \(x_i=2\) and all other variables to \(3\). The multiplication table makes each word equal to \(2\) if it contains \(x_i\), and \(3\) otherwise. On these values the addition table makes the sum equal to \(2\) exactly when at least one summand contains \(x_i\). Thus content is an invariant of the function; content under either operation is the union of the operand contents.

If \((t_1,\ldots,t_r)\) generates \(F_r\), each projection \(x_j\) must be a term in the \(t_i\). Since content takes unions and is never empty, this requires at least one \(t_i\) with content exactly \(\{x_j\}\). There are \(r\) tuple entries, so after reordering, \(t_j\) has content \(\{x_j\}\) for every \(j\). The unary functions are exactly \(x_j,x_j^2\): the tables give \(x_j^k=x_j^2\) for \(k\ge2\) and \(x_j+x_j^2=x_j^2\). The two functions differ at input \(1\), and \(x_j^2\) alone generates only itself because it is idempotent under both operations. A term representing \(x_j\) cannot involve any \(t_i\) with \(i\ne j\) by content, so \(t_j=x_j\). A permutation of the projections extends to an automorphism by freeness, and any automorphism takes them to a generating tuple. This proves the group isomorphism.

**Published word reduction used for the census.** Ma and Yue [1, Lemma 4.1] prove that a word reduces to \(u\) or \(u^2\), where \(u\) lists its distinct variables in order of first occurrence. The choice is \(u\) if the final letter is new and \(u^2\) otherwise. The supplied certificate uses \(u x_i\) in place of \(u^2\), where \(x_i\) is the first letter of \(u\); both are in the second case of the lemma. These forms give distinct functions: assigning \(1\) to the last letter of \(u\) and \(3\) elsewhere separates the two flags, membership in the content is detected by the test above, and a pair of letters with their relative order reversed is separated by putting \(0\) and \(4\) at those letters and \(3\) elsewhere. In the last test, the first factor outside \(\{2,3\}\) fixes the output, by [1, Lemma 3.2]. In particular the preparatory word-function counts are \(2\sum_{k=1}^r r!/(r-k)!\), or \(2,8,30\) at the three ranks needed here. This count is a direct consequence of the published word reduction and product rule.

**Exact additive closure.** By the word reduction and distributivity, evaluating all nonempty sums of the distinct word functions exhausts \(F_r\). Compute their vectors on all \(5^r\) assignments, using lexicographic order with \(0<1<2<3<4\). For each successive word vector \(w\), replace the current set \(D\) by
\[
D\;\cup\;\{w\}\;\cup\;\{d+w:d\in D\},
\]
starting at \(D=\varnothing\), where the addition is coordinatewise. By induction this processes every nonempty subset of the word vectors without an arbitrary term-length cutoff; keeping a set of value vectors removes duplicates. The membership test for each variable then gives the content distribution. The exact counts are:

| Rank | All term functions | Content size 1 | Content size 2 | Content size 3 | Full content |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2 | 2 | — | — | 2 |
| 2 | 21 | 4 | 17 | — | 17 |
| 3 | 778 | 6 | 51 | 721 | 721 |

In rank three, `free3_certificate.json` supplies a nonempty sum of the thirty representative words for each of the 778 distinct vectors, encoded by a bit mask. The included `verify.py` checks the word vectors, exhausts the additive closure in each rank, and checks that the rank-three witnesses are pairwise distinct and their vectors form a set containing the projections and closed under both operations. This provides matching lower and upper bounds by explicit witnesses and closure. Each vector is compared on the complete 125-input cube in rank three, with no sampling.

## Relationship to prior work and limitations

Ma and Yue [1] introduced the semiring, proved its minimal-order limit-variety property, and gave a product rule (Lemma 3.2), word normal forms (Lemma 4.1), and term-identity criteria. The normal-form mechanism and its immediate word count are used as background, not advanced as new contributions. Lemma 4.11 works with three particular elements of a three-generated free algebra in a subvariety analysis; it does not compute the full free spectrum or content profile. The results here determine the full algebra’s exact order only in ranks one through three, and the generating-tuple rigidity in every positive finite rank. They do not make a claim about the full free spectrum at ranks \(r\ge4\) or about free algebras with named constants.
