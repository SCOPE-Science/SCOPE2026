# Frobenius descent and zeta spectra of Zariski chambers on degree-five del Pezzo surfaces

## Finding

Let \(S\) be any smooth degree-five del Pezzo surface over an arbitrary finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), and let \(F\) be geometric Frobenius acting on its ten geometric exceptional curves. The resulting permutation belongs to \(S_5\), acting on the ten two-element subsets of a five-element set. Write \(\mathscr Z(S_{\overline{\mathbb F}_q})\) for the finite set of geometric Zariski chambers of the big cone, identified by their negative-part supports (including the nef chamber, with empty support). Then \(\#\mathscr Z=76\), a previously known geometric count. The new determination is the **complete arithmetic Frobenius permutation** of these 76 chambers, the number of chambers realized by divisors defined over the ground field, and all finite-extension counts.

The notation \(d^m\) in the orbit column means \(m\) distinct Frobenius orbits of length \(d\). The five coefficients in the last column count Frobenius-fixed negative supports with respectively \(0,1,2,3,4\) geometric irreducible curves.

| Frobenius cycle type on five letters | Frobenius orbit profile on 76 chambers | Rational chambers | Fixed support-size coefficients (degree 0 through 4) |
|:--|:--|--:|:--|
| \((1,1,1,1,1)\) | \(1^{76}\) | 76 | \((1,10,30,30,5)\) |
| \((2,1,1,1)\) | \(1^{24}2^{26}\) | 24 | \((1,4,6,10,3)\) |
| \((2,2,1)\) | \(1^{8}2^{34}\) | 8 | \((1,2,2,2,1)\) |
| \((3,1,1)\) | \(1^{7}3^{23}\) | 7 | \((1,1,0,3,2)\) |
| \((3,2)\) | \(1^3 2^2 3^7 6^8\) | 3 | \((1,1,0,1,0)\) |
| \((4,1)\) | \(1^2 2^3 4^{17}\) | 2 | \((1,0,0,0,1)\) |
| \((5)\) | \(1^1 5^{15}\) | 1 | \((1,0,0,0,0)\) |

For the orbit multiplicities \(m_d\) in any row, the finite étale chamber scheme has zeta function
\[
Z_{\mathscr Z(S)}(T)=\prod_{d\geq1}(1-T^d)^{-m_d},\qquad
\#\mathscr Z(S)(\mathbb F_{q^n})=\sum_{d\mid n}d\,m_d\quad(n\geq1).
\]
This is the zeta function of the **finite set of chambers with Frobenius descent**, not the Hasse–Weil zeta function of the surface itself. All seven rows are realizable over every finite field. For a four-cycle the unique non-nef rational chamber has a support of four conjugate disjoint geometric exceptional curves even though no exceptional curve is individually rational. For a five-cycle only the nef chamber descends.

## Assumptions and scope

A *degree-five del Pezzo surface* is a smooth projective surface with ample anticanonical divisor and anticanonical self-intersection \(5\). Its geometric Picard lattice is the blowup lattice of four points of \(\mathbb P^2\) in general position. A *Zariski chamber* here means a chamber on the geometric big cone distinguished by the support of the negative part in the unique Zariski decomposition \(D=P+N\), where \(P\) is nef, \(N\) is effective with negative-definite intersection matrix, and \(P\cdot E=0\) for every component \(E\) of \(N\). The empty negative support denotes the nef chamber. An arithmetic/rational chamber is a geometric chamber fixed by Frobenius; below we prove that this is equivalent to existence of a divisor defined over \(\mathbb F_q\) in its interior. We count **geometric irreducible curves** in the last-column supports, not closed points or irreducible divisors over the base field.

## Proof

**The geometric support complex.** Work in the basis \(H,E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4\) with intersection matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1,-1)\). The ten exceptional curve classes are \(E_i\) for \(1\leq i\leq4\) and \(H-E_i-E_j\) for \(1\leq i<j\leq4\). The anticanonical class is \(-K_S=3H-E_1-E_2-E_3-E_4\). Identify these ten curves with two-element subsets of \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\) via \(E_i\leftrightarrow\{i,5\}\) and \(H-E_i-E_j\leftrightarrow\{1,2,3,4\}\setminus\{i,j\}\). A direct intersection computation shows that two distinct exceptional curves intersect in one point if and only if their corresponding pairs of labels are disjoint; otherwise they are disjoint. This is the Petersen (Kneser) intersection graph. The cone of effective curves is generated by the ten exceptional curves.

All negative curves on a smooth degree-five del Pezzo surface are exceptional \((-1)\)-curves; indeed if \(C^2<0\) for irreducible \(C\), adjunction and ampleness of \(-K_S\) give \(C^2=-1\), \((-K_S)\cdot C=1\), and \(C\) is rational. A collection of distinct exceptional curves has negative-definite intersection matrix precisely when its curves are pairwise disjoint. For the nontrivial direction, a subset of at least two distinct intersecting exceptional curves has principal matrix \(\begin{psmallmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{psmallmatrix}\), which is not negative definite. Conversely pairwise disjoint curves give negative identity. By the Zariski chamber support criterion, supports are exactly independent sets of the Petersen graph. Such a support is a family of mutually intersecting edges of the complete graph on five labels.

There are only six mutually exclusive combinatorial types: empty; a single edge; a two-edge star; a triangle; a three-edge star; a four-edge star. The size polynomial is
\[
1+10u+30u^2+(20+10)u^3+5u^4
=1+10u+30u^2+30u^3+5u^4,
\]
and its value at \(u=1\) is \(76\). The geometric total is earlier known; the classification is included here to make the arithmetic calculation self-contained.

**Arithmetic descent of actual chambers.** Uniqueness of Zariski decomposition makes the geometric negative support of any divisor defined over \(\mathbb F_q\) Frobenius stable. Conversely, let \(\mathcal E\) be any Frobenius-stable set of \(r\) pairwise disjoint exceptional curves. The geometric sum \(B=\sum_{E\in\mathcal E}E\) is defined over \(\mathbb F_q\) as an effective divisor (it is a union of full Galois orbits). Define the arithmetic integral divisor \(D=-K_S+2B\). We claim its Zariski decomposition is \(D=P+N\), with \(P=-K_S+B\) and \(N=B\). For \(E\in\mathcal E\), \(P\cdot E=1-1=0\). For any other exceptional curve \(F\), \(P\cdot F=1+\sum_{E\in\mathcal E} E\cdot F\geq1\). Since the Mori cone is generated by exceptional curves, \(P\) is nef. Also \(P^2=5+r>0\), so \(P\) is big; \(N\) is effective with intersection matrix \(-I_r\). The Zariski-decomposition characterization gives \(N=B\), so the support is exactly \(\mathcal E\). The chamber contains the rational class \(D\), proving the claimed arithmetic interpretation without assuming all ten curves are individually rational.

**The seven Frobenius types and an exact closed count.** The full geometric automorphism group is \(S_5\), and the Frobenius permutation of the five labels has one of the seven partitions \(\lambda\vdash5\). For \(r\ge0\) write
\[
a_r(\lambda)=[t^r]\prod_{\ell\in\lambda}(1+t^\ell),\qquad
f=\#\{\ell\in\lambda:\ell=1\}.
\]
If \(f>0\), set \(b_2=[t^2](1+t)^{f-1}\prod_{\ell\in\lambda,\ell>1}(1+t^\ell)\), and put \(b_2=0\) if \(f=0\). Fixed empty supports contribute \(1\), fixed singleton edges contribute \(a_2\), fixed triangles contribute \(a_3\), and two-edge stars contribute \(f b_2\). Three-edge stars contribute \(f(f-1)\): the center and omitted leaf must both be fixed. Four-edge stars contribute \(f\). Therefore the number of fixed chambers is exactly
\[
\#\mathscr Z(S)^{F}=1+a_2(\lambda)+a_3(\lambda)+f b_2+f^2.
\]
For full orbit profiles rather than fixed counts, the five-label permutation acts on a support by permuting its constituent two-subsets; applying this action to all 76 explicitly classified supports and following complete orbits gives exactly the seven table rows. The enclosed dependency-free verifier checks every support and all \(120\) permutations, not just one representative per partition; it also checks the displayed closed formula and all extension counts. Each row sums to \(76\) when orbit multiplicities are weighted by lengths. The zeta products follow from the standard cycle-index identity for a finite étale scheme; these are identities for all positive extension degrees, not extrapolations from finitely many checked degrees.

**No missing arithmetic types.** Zaitsev's classification of finite-field forms of degree-five del Pezzo surfaces shows that every cyclic subgroup of \(S_5\) can occur as geometric Frobenius image over any finite field. Each cyclic subgroup may be generated by any prescribed cycle partition in its conjugacy class, and the fixed-set/orbit-profile statements are constant on that class. This realizes all seven rows over every \(\mathbb F_q\), independently of a finite-field sample.

## Relationship to prior work

Bauer–Funke–Neumann establish the general negative-definite-support algorithm and calculate the **geometric 76 chambers** for degree-five del Pezzo surfaces; that total and support criterion are prior work, not claimed as new here. Derenthal–Joyce–Teitler study nef cones and arithmetic Galois actions of degree-five del Pezzo surfaces and calculate nef-cone volumes, but do not state this seven-row Zariski-chamber orbit or rational-support table. Ren–Shaw–Sturmfels exhibit the Petersen configuration in the degree-five setting. Zaitsev and Boitrel classify finite-field/perfect-field forms and their \(S_5\) actions, providing a stronger geometric classification from which the present permutation computation is in principle possible. An earlier published arithmetic Picard trace-lattice and Mori/nef extremal-ray calculation likewise concerns **different invariants** (integral saturation and cones, not Zariski-negative-part support or its Frobenius zeta spectrum). Thus the contribution is the explicitly proved complete **arithmetic chamber** classification and its base-field divisor realization, not the already known geometric count, surface classification, or Petersen graph.

## Limitations

The table is about the finite Galois set of **geometric Zariski chambers**. It is not a claimed classification of higher-dimensional Mori chamber decompositions, the Hasse–Weil zeta function of \(S\), or the number of rational divisor classes inside chambers. Absolute historical priority is not certified: the seven-row data could exist in an earlier combinatorial enumeration of the Petersen graph with \(S_5\)-action or be recorded implicitly in a general Weyl-group cycle-index computation. The scientific checks here are same-model only; independent mathematical audit has not occurred. Exact computation corroborates finite group calculations but is not used as a substitute for the geometric descent argument or the classical classification of surface forms.
