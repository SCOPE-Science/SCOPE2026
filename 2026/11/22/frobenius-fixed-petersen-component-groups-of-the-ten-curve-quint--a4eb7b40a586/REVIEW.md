# Mathematical review: Frobenius-fixed Petersen component groups

## Correctness — PASS
The proof reconstructs the ten-line Petersen graph and its complete reduced Laplacian. The exact determinant is 2000 and the explicit integer inverse certificate gives exponent at most ten; mod-prime ranks show the primary parts are elementary. Crucially, moving the deleted vertex is accounted for by the exact integral Frobenius action matrix on degree-zero divisors. Fourteen augmented rank calculations produce the seven fixed subgroups. A separate 2000-class Hermite enumeration independently checks their fixed orders and annihilators; both standalone scripts replay successfully. The geometric statement and conditional semistable interpretation have clear hypotheses.

## Originality — PASS, with historical risk
The closest published broad result, Ducey–Hill–Sin, *The critical group of the Kneser graph on 2-subsets of an n-element set* (arXiv:1707.09115), already implies the abstract Petersen critical group, so this is excluded from novelty. González-Sánchez et al. (arXiv:1009.4044) give the S5 Frobenius twists. The public Petersen integral cycle-lattice result concerns a connected generalized Jacobian, not a finite monodromy discriminant group. A classification of connected-Picard-torus rational point groups would address a different arithmetic invariant. The six nonidentity fixed critical groups were not found in targeted searches or materially inspected closest texts. An older unindexed equivalent calculation remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The complete boundary is a natural anticanonical-cycle divisor on every quintic del Pezzo surface, not a selected artificial subgraph. The full seven-class arithmetic classification distinguishes twists with geometric critical group of order 2000 but rational fixed groups of order 2000, 100, 20, 5, 1 or 2, which is genuinely finer degeneration information than the nonequivariant Smith form. The mixed three-plus-two Frobenius type gives the sharp vanishing case.

## Scope
The Néron interpretation requires a proper regular semistable smoothing with unit node thicknesses, and no existence theorem for such smoothings over every finite field is asserted. The classification neither recovers connected generalized-Jacobian point groups nor covers selected partial divisors. Independent mathematical and historical-priority validation remains outstanding.
