# Frobenius-fixed Zariski chambers of the nine-line two-nodal quartic del Pezzo resolution

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) have characteristic at least five. Let \(X\) be the blowup of \(\mathbb P^2_k\) in a reduced, geometrically five-point Galois-stable subscheme whose geometric points \(p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4\) have exactly two collinear triples, \(\{p_0,p_1,p_2\}\) and \(\{p_0,p_3,p_4\}\), lying on distinct lines. Let \(z_F(r)\) count Frobenius-stable negative-definite supports of size \(r\) among the eleven geometric negative curves; these correspond to the Zariski chambers of the arithmetic big cone by Galois descent.

Frobenius fixes \(p_0\) and preserves the unordered pair \(\{\{p_1,p_2\},\{p_3,p_4\}\}\). Its action therefore belongs to the eight-element group \(G=(S_2\times S_2)\rtimes S_2\cong D_8\), with precisely five conjugacy classes. For the indicated representative permutations of the four outer point labels, the complete invariant-support distributions (coefficients in increasing support size \(r=0,1,2,3,4,5\)) are:

| Frobenius representative | Conjugacy-class size | \((z_F(0),\ldots,z_F(5))\) | Total |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| identity | 1 | \((1,11,45,84,67,13)\) | 221 |
| \((1\,2)\) | 2 | \((1,5,13,18,19,5)\) | 61 |
| \((1\,2)(3\,4)\) | 1 | \((1,3,5,4,3,1)\) | 17 |
| \((1\,3)(2\,4)\) | 2 | \((1,3,5,6,5,3)\) | 23 |
| \((1\,3\,2\,4)\) | 2 | \((1,1,1,0,1,1)\) | 5 |

The geometric 221-chamber total is prior work, not claimed as new. The **new** information is the five-class arithmetic descent table, its refined counts by number of selected \((-2)\)-curves, and the full-group orbit classification. There are exactly \(52\) orbits of geometric Zariski chambers under \(G\), with orbit-size multiplicities \(5,6,31,10\) for sizes \(1,2,4,8\), respectively. Moreover, the two involutions \((1\,2)(3\,4)\) and \((1\,3)(2\,4)\) induce **identical** negative-curve permutation cycle lengths \((1,1,1,2,2,2,2)\), but their fixed-chamber totals differ, \(17\) versus \(23\). Thus the negative-curve splitting partition alone is insufficient to determine rational chamber descent. **Every one of these five Frobenius types is realizable for every finite field of characteristic at least five.**

The complete bivariate distribution \(z_F(r,s)\), with \(s\in\{0,1,2\}\) the number of selected \((-2)\)-curves, is reproduced by the standalone `verification/verify_incidence.py` and documented in its deterministic output `verification/expected_output.txt`. In particular the counts with no \((-2)\)-curve, one, or both are, respectively, \((80,108,33)\), \((24,30,7)\), \((8,8,1)\), \((16,0,7)\), \((4,0,1)\) in table order.

## Assumptions and scope
The five geometric points are distinct, with ordinary blowups and no further triple collinearity, including in positive characteristic. The anticanonical model has two \(A_1\) singularities and nine geometric lines, distinguishing this configuration from the other quartic \(2A_1\) type with eight lines. Zariski chambers are determined by the negative supports of big \(\mathbb Q\)-divisors. A chamber *descends* when its negative support is Frobenius-stable, equivalently when that chamber contains a divisor defined over \(k\). No identification with a chamber count on a singular Weil-divisor space is made.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive negative-curve list.** Write \(H\) for the line pullback and \(E_0,\ldots,E_4\) for exceptional classes, with \(H^2=1\), \(E_i^2=-1\), and other pairings zero. The canonical class is \(K_X=-3H+\sum E_i\). Two distinct root curves are \(A=H-E_0-E_1-E_2\) and \(B=H-E_0-E_3-E_4\), with squares \(-2\), zero mutual intersection, and zero anticanonical degree. The five exceptional curves and the four irreducible crossing lines \(L_{ij}=H-E_i-E_j\), \(i\in\{1,2\}\), \(j\in\{3,4\}\), have square \(-1\).

For an irreducible curve \(C\) other than exceptional curves and \(A,B\), let \(C=dH-\sum m_iE_i\) with \(d\ge1\), \(m_i\ge0\). Bézout with the two triple-point lines gives \(m_0+m_1+m_2\le d\) and \(m_0+m_3+m_4\le d\), so \((-K_X)\cdot C=3d-\sum m_i\ge d+m_0\ge1\). Thus \(-K_X\) is nef and big with square \(4\). If \(C^2<0\), arithmetic adjunction \(C^2+K_X\cdot C=2p_a(C)-2\ge-2\) forces \((-K_X)\cdot C=1\), \(C^2=-1\). The Bézout inequality then forces \(d=1,m_0=0,\sum m_i=2\), leaving exactly the four listed crossing-line curves. Therefore **all** eleven geometric negative curves are known.

**2. Integral negative-definiteness criterion.** For a chosen support, let \(S\) be its \((-1)\)-curves and \(R\subseteq\{A,B\}\) its roots. Distinct selected \((-1)\)-curves must be disjoint: otherwise their \(2\times2\) principal matrix is not negative definite. When they are pairwise disjoint, the entire intersection matrix, with \((-1)\)-curves first, equals
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-I & M^\mathsf T\\ M & -2I\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(M\) is the root-to-exceptional incidence matrix. The Schur complement is negative definite exactly when \(2I-MM^\mathsf T\) is positive definite. The only nonzero root incidences are \(A\cdot E_i=1\) for \(i=0,1,2\) and \(B\cdot E_i=1\) for \(i=0,3,4\). Therefore a support is valid exactly when (i) its selected \((-1)\)-curves are pairwise disjoint, (ii) if \(A\) is selected, at most one of \(E_0,E_1,E_2\) is selected, (iii) if \(B\) is selected, at most one of \(E_0,E_3,E_4\) is selected, and (iv) if both roots are selected, \(E_0\) is absent. This criterion is exact for every support, including empty and singleton supports.

**3. Descending chambers.** The negative-support correspondence of Bauer–Funke–Neumann identifies geometric chambers with negative-definite curve supports and the empty support with the nef chamber. A geometric chamber is stable under Frobenius precisely when its support is. For a stable support, choose an ample \(k\)-divisor and, as in the cited correspondence, add a sufficiently large positive multiple of the **sum of the selected curves**. This sum is defined over \(k\), and the resulting big \(k\)-divisor lies inside precisely the desired chamber. Conversely a \(k\)-divisor's negative part is Frobenius invariant by uniqueness of Zariski decomposition. Hence the fixed-support counts are exactly the rational-chamber counts claimed.

**4. Finite complete classification.** The two independent verifiers use different predicates: `verify_matrix.py` constructs the integral intersection matrix and tests every one of \(2^{11}=2048\) supports by all exact leading principal determinants; `verify_incidence.py` uses only the independent-set and Schur-complement criterion above. Both then enumerate every element of the full eight-element permutation group and its five conjugacy classes. The matrix verifier obtains the five support-size polynomials in the table and the two equal \((1^3 2^4)\) curve-cycle partitions. The incidence verifier independently obtains every bivariate \(z_F(r,s)\). Direct group-orbit enumeration gives \(5+6+31+10=52\) orbits, while Burnside's lemma independently gives \((221+2\cdot61+17+2\cdot23+2\cdot5)/8=52\). All operations involve finite, explicitly enumerated sets and exact integers.

**5. Realization of every Frobenius type.** In affine coordinates let the common point be \(p_0=(0,0)\). For the first three types, choose the two rational coordinate axes and put two points on each; select either a pair of distinct nonzero \(k\)-coordinates or a pair \(\{u,u^q\}\subset\mathbb F_{q^2}\setminus k\) independently on each axis. For the exchange involution, choose \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_{q^2}\setminus k\), take lines \(y=\alpha x\) and \(y=\alpha^q x\), and on the first take \((1,\alpha)\), \((2,2\alpha)\), together with their conjugates on the second. For an order-four action choose \(u\in\mathbb F_{q^4}\setminus\mathbb F_{q^2}\), take \((u,\alpha u)\) and its successive Frobenius iterates, alternating between the two conjugate lines. The four points on the two distinct lines never produce a third collinear triple with their common point, because each line has only its designated two noncentral points and a cross-line does not contain the common point. The constructed sets have exactly the required Galois cycle types for every \(q\) of characteristic at least five.


For a concrete pair over \(\mathbb F_5\), choose \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_{25}\) with \(\alpha^2=2\), so \(\alpha^5=-\alpha\). One double-inner-swap configuration has the common point \((0,0)\) and four other points \((\alpha,0),(-\alpha,0),(0,\alpha),(0,-\alpha)\). One exchange-pairs configuration has common point \((0,0)\) and other points \((1,\alpha),(2,2\alpha),(1,-\alpha),(2,-2\alpha)\). These two explicit reductions realize the two involutions with the same negative-curve cycle lengths but with \(17\) and \(23\) rational chambers, respectively.

## Verification
Using Python 3 and SymPy, run `python3 verification/verify_matrix.py`; using Python 3 alone, run `python3 verification/verify_incidence.py`. Both scripts are self-contained, deterministic, and start from explicit geometric or incidence data. Their terminal markers are `TWO_NODAL_QUARTIC_MATRIX_OK` and `TWO_NODAL_QUARTIC_INCIDENCE_OK`. The exact claimed counts, including group class sizes, orbit sizes, curve-cycle equivalence, and bivariate coefficients, are checked by equality assertions in the code. The saved outputs from these scripts are included as `verification/expected_output.txt`. The underlying geometric exhaustion and the Galois-descent argument are proved above rather than inferred from a finite experiment.

## Relationship to prior work
Derenthal's classification (arXiv:math/0604194, first publicly submitted April 10, 2006; the inspected later revision contains Table 6) distinguishes the nine-line and eight-line quartic \(2A_1\) surfaces. Bauer–Funke–Neumann (arXiv:0912.0958, first publicly submitted December 4, 2009) prove the chamber/negative-definite-support bijection, while Bauer–Schmitz (arXiv:1205.3817, first publicly submitted May 16, 2012) study chamber volumes. An earlier published exact computation of the specific nine-line two-nodal quartic already gives its \(221\) geometric chambers and the geometric bivariate support polynomial, including \(80\) root-free chambers. Those objects and values are excluded from the present novelty claim.

The present result instead computes the **full five-class Frobenius-equivariant refinement**, records the group-orbit classification, and shows that identical Frobenius cycle types on individual negative curves can yield different rational chamber counts. Neither the published geometric support polynomial nor the cited general theory supplies that equivariant table automatically; it requires retaining the incidence of the nontrivial permutations on *sets* of disjoint curves. Exact and alias-based literature/database comparison found no equivalent complete table in the inspected sources, though this is not proof of historical priority.

## Limitations
This is conditional on an ordinary five-point plane blowup with the specified two triple lines and characteristic at least five. It does not classify every arithmetic form of each anticanonical surface, the distinct eight-line quartic \(2A_1\) configuration, or Zariski chambers on the singular model's Weil-divisor class space. The eight-element group is the abstract label-incidence action, not necessarily the geometric automorphism group of any individual surface. It provides counts of rational chamber supports, not explicit chamber volume integrals or rational points on the surface. Exact calculations establish all finite combinatorics; unlocated historical computations remain an originality risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
