# Scientific review: Frobenius-fixed chambers of the nine-line two-nodal quartic del Pezzo resolution

## Correctness — PASS
The blowup is specified by five distinct geometric points with exactly two triple collinearities. Bézout bounds and adjunction establish exhaustively that there are exactly five exceptional curves, four cross-lines, and two disjoint \((-2)\)-root curves; no computation substitutes for this geometric argument. The support intersection matrix is negative definite precisely under the explicit disjointness and root-incidence conditions. For Galois-stable supports, the classical Zariski-chamber construction with a rational ample divisor supplies a rational big divisor realizing the chamber. The full eight-element action is the stabilizer of the two-line partition of the outer four points. Two independent algorithms enumerate all \(2^{11}\) subsets and all group elements: direct integral Sylvester tests and independent combinatorial Schur-complement tests agree on \(221\) supports and all five fixed-support counts \(221,61,17,23,5\). Independent direct orbits and Burnside's lemma both yield \(52\). The two order-two actions have identical individual curve-cycle partitions but different fixed-support totals. Explicit finite-field point configurations establish realization in every characteristic at least five. All provided standalone checks pass when run from the recorded package paths.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
The exact geometric \(221\)-chamber count and its negative-support bivariate polynomial are existing published results, and neither is claimed here. The closest result, *The 221 Zariski chambers of a nine-line two-nodal quartic del Pezzo resolution*, was inspected in full: it classifies the geometric negative supports and their volumes, not the action of arithmetic Frobenius. Derenthal's Table 6 classifies the nine-line quartic \(2A_1\) configuration, not fixed chamber counts. Bauer–Funke–Neumann provides the universal support correspondence, not this specific equivariant distribution. Bauer–Schmitz addresses volumes, not Frobenius. Targeted published-result searches including synonymous surface names, parameters, chamber orbit counts, and the two-involution distinction produced no closer equivalence. The new theorem refines the geometric count by five group-conjugacy types and a structural non-determination statement. An inaccessible or unindexed earlier specialized table remains a possible priority risk; neither failed search nor database partition is construed as proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The two-nodal quartic is a naturally distinguished degeneration in the geometric classification of degree-four del Pezzo surfaces, and arithmetic descent of negative supports gives a direct invariant of rational big linear series. This is the complete finite-field Frobenius classification for the specified blowup family, not an arbitrary finite slice. Each of the five cases can occur over every finite field of the stated characteristic. Particularly, two different rational forms with exactly the same negative-curve Frobenius cycle partition have \(17\) and \(23\) chambers. The full \(52\)-orbit decomposition and bivariate root counts explain how component splitting fails to encode the incidence of negative-definite supports. These exact results do not follow from the earlier geometric polynomial and provide a motivated, reproducible arithmetic refinement without claiming a new general theorem.
