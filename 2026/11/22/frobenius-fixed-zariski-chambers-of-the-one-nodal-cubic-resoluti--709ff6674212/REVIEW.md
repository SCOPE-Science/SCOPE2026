# Scientific review: Frobenius-fixed Zariski chambers of the one-nodal cubic resolution

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claim requires the complete twenty-two-curve list, the intersection calculation, the negative-definite-support criterion, arithmetic descent and exact orbit counts. The primary proof reconstructs those steps. The first executable checker enumerates every eligible support and confirms its Gram negative definiteness, every permutation action, both orbit-count methods and all eleven polynomial profiles. The second checker independently uses Frobenius-invariant orbit blocks, and its eleven outputs agree identically. Computer verification is finite and exact, not a proof of geometric completeness by itself; the geometric classification and descent are separately argued.

## Originality

**PASS, historical priority provisional.** The geometric \(1898\)-chamber count, \(648\) conic-support count, and allowed-support family are in the closest prior public result (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/blob/main/2026/11/8/SCOPE-zariski-chamber-spectrum-of-the-one-nodal-cubic-s-minimal-resolu--4b531d234d53/RESULT.md). The fixed-support and orbit tables are distinct statements, absent from that inspected full report. Bauer–Funke–Neumann supply the general criterion, and Derenthal–Joyce–Teitler classify relevant geometric frameworks and nef volumes. Targeted published-record and previously accessible claim comparisons did not locate an equivalent eleven-class arithmetic result. An unindexed earlier table remains possible; searches do not certify uniqueness.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Frobenius descent changes the number of rational big-cone chambers from \(1898\) to as few as \(3\), for a natural one-nodal cubic with six points on a conic. All eleven possible partitions actually occur for every finite field in the stated characteristic range. Full orbit-cycle data and the \(39\)-orbit quotient refine the prior nonequivariant polynomial.

## Limitations

The \(S_6\) action is an abstract labeling symmetry, not an asserted geometric automorphism action on every individual surface. The conclusions do not cover arbitrary six-point blowups or compute volumes. Independent mathematical validation remains outstanding.
