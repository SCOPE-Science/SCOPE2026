# Frobenius plateaux in finite-time cylinder covering for additive cellular automata

## Finding
Let \(p\) be any prime, \(X=\mathbb F_p^{\mathbb Z}\), \((Sx)_i=x_{i+1}\), and \(F=I-S\). For any finite \(E\subset\mathbb Z\), set
\[
r_E(t)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\{x|_E:\ F^t x=0\},\qquad t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]
The space of attainable restrictions of \(\ker F^t\) has exactly \(p^{r_E(t)}\) members. Fix an integer \(a\ge1\), put \(N=p^a\), and choose \(2\le L,R\le N\). For the separated two-block observation set
\[
E=\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\cup\{N,\ldots,N+R-1\},
\quad H=\max(L,R),\quad h=\min(L,R),
\]
the entire finite-time restriction-rank profile is
\[
\boxed{r_E(t)=\begin{cases}
\min(t,H),&0\le t\le N,\\
H+\min(t-N,h),&t>N.
\end{cases}}
\]
Consequently for *every* assignment \(v:E\to\mathbb F_p\), the image of its cylinder
\(C_E(v)=\{x\in X:x|_E=v\}\) satisfies \(F^t(C_E(v))=X\) if and only if \(t\ge N+h\). Thus a gap of prime-power length produces a rank plateau of length \(N-H\) (when \(H<N\)) and an exact, pattern-independent cylinder-covering delay.

## Assumptions and scope
All configurations are **bilateral** sequences over the prime field; no finite cyclic boundary conditions are imposed. The shift is \((Sx)_i=x_{i+1}\), and \(F=I-S\) is applied \(t\) times for a nonnegative integer \(t\). The two-block theorem assumes \(N=p^a\), \(a\ge1\), and both lengths between \(2\) and \(N\). Cases with singleton blocks, as well as arbitrary-spaced arithmetic-progression probes, are deliberately excluded from the novelty claim because related predecessor-trace formulas already cover important such cases. No claim is made for other local rules or for arbitrary disconnected sets beyond those expressly quantified.

## Proof
**1. A finite-dimensional kernel and its coordinate functionals.** For \(t\ge1\), the homogeneous recurrence \((I-S)^t x=0\) has nonzero endpoint coefficients at offsets \(0\) and \(t\). Consequently the freely chosen coordinates \(x_0,\ldots,x_{t-1}\) extend uniquely in both directions to an element of \(\ker F^t\); hence this kernel has dimension \(t\). The assertion at \(t=0\) holds because \(\ker F^0=\{0\}\). Put \(Y=S-I\). On \(\ker F^t\), \(Y^t=0\). The coordinate functional \(x\mapsto x_e\), for \(e\ge0\), is \(x\mapsto ((I+Y)^e x)_0\). Since \(x\mapsto((Y^j x)_0)_{0\le j<t}\) is an invertible triangular coordinate change, the dimension of the restriction to \(E\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) is exactly the rank of the polynomials \((1+y)^e\), \(e\in E\), in \(\mathbb F_p[y]/(y^t)\). This proves the basic polynomial-rank correspondence without an infinite-dimensional linear-algebra assumption.

**2. Two prime-power-separated blocks.** Let \(v_j=(1+y)^j\). Frobenius gives \((1+y)^N=1+y^N\) in \(\mathbb F_p[y]\). The two sets of polynomials in the rank calculation are therefore \(v_j\) for \(0\le j<L\), and \(v_j+y^Nv_j\) for \(0\le j<R\). If \(t\le N\), the \(y^N\) terms disappear and the rank is \(\min(t,H)\): consecutive \(v_j\) are a triangular basis for polynomials of degree below \(H\). If \(t>N\), projection to degree below \(N\) has image of dimension \(H\). Its kernel within the span of the two sets is generated by \(y^Nv_j\), \(0\le j<h\), because the only relations between the two initial spans have the common indices \(0,\ldots,h-1\). The latter vectors have rank \(\min(t-N,h)\) modulo \(y^t\). Rank-nullity gives the boxed profile for every \(t\ge0\).

**3. Exact cylinder filling.** The map \(F^t:X\to X\) is surjective for every \(t\): to solve the inhomogeneous recurrence \((I-S)^t x=z\), choose any \(t\) consecutive initial coordinates and continue uniquely in both directions using the unit endpoint coefficients (for \(t=0\), use the identity map). Fix any output \(z\) and one preimage \(x\). The full preimage fiber is \(x+\ker F^t\), so it meets every cylinder with domain \(E\) precisely when the restrictions of \(\ker F^t\) fill \(\mathbb F_p^E\), i.e., \(r_E(t)=|E|\). By step 2 this first occurs at \(t=N+h\) and persists at all later times. This also proves that the first filling time does not depend on the prescribed pattern.

## Verification
A dependency-free integer checker (`verification_check.py`) independently compares predicted ranks with Gaussian-elimination ranks of the binomial polynomial rows over \(\mathbb F_p\). For small cases it separately constructs all \(p^t\) order-\(t\) recurrence solutions, counts distinct observed patterns, and verifies that the count is \(p^{r_E(t)}\). Checked data include primes \(2,3\), multiple block lengths and prime-power separations. It reports **510** two-block rank comparisons, **68,279** enumerated initial words used for the independent pattern check. The finite checks support the proof but do not constitute an exhaustive verification over all parameters.

Run `python3 verification_check.py` in the directory containing the standalone checker. The proof above, not finite sampling, establishes the infinite collection of cases.

## Relationship to prior work
Castronuovo, Dennunzio and Margara (2025) develop group-cellular-automaton decomposition and discuss qualitative strong transitivity, including the obstruction for nonabelian alphabets. Their cited earlier work concerns deciding qualitative behavior. Dennunzio, Formenti, Grinberg and Margara (2019), Lemma 18, give a qualitative kernel-intersection criterion for strong transitivity. For the specific abelian automaton here, those criteria are consistent with eventual cylinder covering; neither inspected source states the exact two-block restriction-rank plateau. A separate quantitative predecessor-trace calculation for arithmetic-progression observation sets already covers the isolated-pair instance (\(L=R=1\)); the present claim explicitly excludes singleton blocks. That earlier progression formula does not determine the two contiguous-block rank profile. The polynomial representation of additive rules and the Frobenius binomial identity are standard ingredients and are not independently claimed as new results. The asserted contribution is their exact, all-parameter **finite-time cylinder-covering profile** in the stated geometries.

## Limitations
This is an exact result for a fixed difference automaton, not a universal covering bound for all additive or group cellular automata. The history comparison does not prove novelty; an equivalent, potentially broader cylinder-rank calculation in older literature may exist. In particular, the 1999 Manzini–Margara full article on topological classification could not be fully inspected; its abstract reports qualitative classification, but its proofs or examples may contain stronger quantitative information. No independent audit has been performed. The code checks only finite cases and does not replace the analytical proof.
