# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The formula rests on a direct Frobenius-power image calculation for the finite standard-monomial basis followed by the finite abelian \(p\)-group elementary-divisor identity. The collision family has matching explicit Frobenius shadow counts \((2p(p-1)-1,1,0)\), hence identical cyclic multiplicities. Their distinct Loewy lengths \(3p-2\) and \(3p-3\) give a ring-isomorphism obstruction. Direct multiplication tests, full small-instance enumeration, and four symbolic collision-prime checks agree. The infinite theorem does not rely on computational extrapolation.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence.** Chebolu–Corry–Grimm–Hatfield, *Rings with an elementary abelian \(p\)-group of units* (arXiv:2112.03853, Theorems 1.1/1.3 and Section 3), studies the markedly narrower problem of unit groups of exponent one. Angeltveit–Gerhardt–Hill–Lindenstrauss, *On the algebraic \(K\)-theory of truncated polynomial algebras in several variables* (arXiv:1206.0247, Sections 1–2 and 4–5), studies multivariate Witt structures and multi-relative algebraic \(K\)-theory, not the explicit abstract-unit-group collision asserted here. Searches for equivalent finite-ring unit groups, truncated-polynomial principal units, monomial complete intersections and Loewy length did not locate a directly dominating theorem or table. General Witt and unit-group literature may contain an equivalent implication; research priority remains provisional.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Abstract unit groups are a natural invariant in ring reconstruction. The theorem calculates their full invariant-factor structure for an entire natural monomial class, then demonstrates a uniform reconstruction obstruction among same-dimension, same-embedding-dimension, local Gorenstein complete intersections in every odd characteristic. The Loewy distinction is intrinsic, and the collision has a non-elementary principal-unit group rather than exploiting the trivial elementary-abelian regime.

**Scientific limitations and residual risk.** This is not an assertion that arbitrary Artinian algebras have these unit groups or that the abstract unit-group isomorphism is canonical. The source search cannot exclude all older formulations; independent review has not been performed.
