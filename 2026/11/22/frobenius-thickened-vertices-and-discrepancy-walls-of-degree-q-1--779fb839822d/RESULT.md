# Frobenius-thickened vertices and discrepancy walls of degree-\(q+1\) space-filling cones

## Finding
Fix a prime power \(q=p^h\), an integer \(N\ge5\), and a nonzero homogeneous degree-\(q+1\) form \(F\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) vanishing at every point of \(\mathbb P^{N-1}(\mathbb F_q)\). It has a unique expansion
\[
F=\sum_{1\le i<j\le N}c_{ij}(X_i^qX_j-X_iX_j^q),\quad c_{ij}\in\mathbb F_q.
\]
Write \(C\) for the associated alternating matrix, and assume \(\operatorname{rank}C=2r\ge4\) and \(c=N-2r\ge1\). Over \(\mathbb F_q\), a projective change of variables puts the equation in the normal form
\[
F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(U_i^qV_i-U_iV_i^q).
\]
Let \(V=\{U_1=V_1=\cdots=U_r=V_r=0\}\cong\mathbb P^{c-1}\), and \(Y_r\subset\mathbb P^{2r-1}\) be the smooth hypersurface with the displayed equation. Then:

1. \(X=V(F)\) is geometrically integral and normal, and its full Jacobian singular scheme has **homogeneous ideal**
   \[
   J_X=(U_1^q,V_1^q,\ldots,U_r^q,V_r^q)=I_V^{[q]},
   \]
   not merely radical ideal \(I_V\). Its Hilbert series is \(\frac{(1-t^q)^{2r}}{(1-t)^N}\), its degree is \(q^{2r}\), and its reduced support is exactly \(V\).
2. Blowing up \(V\) once gives a smooth birational morphism \(\pi:\widetilde X\to X\). Its exceptional divisor is the geometrically integral smooth product \(E\cong Y_r\times\mathbb P^{c-1}\), and
   \[
   K_{\widetilde X}=\pi^*K_X+(2r-q-2)E.
   \]
   Hence the singularities are log canonical iff \(q\le2r-1\), klt and canonical iff \(q\le2r-2\), and terminal iff \(q\le2r-3\).
3. At **every** point of \(V\), the local hypersurface ring fails Fedder's \(F\)-purity test: the defining equation belongs to the Frobenius power \(\mathfrak m^{[p]}\) of the local maximal ideal.
4. The number of distinct projective hypersurfaces of the stated rank (identifying proportional equations over \(\mathbb F_q\), without identifying coordinate changes) is exactly
   \[
   M_{N,r}(q)=\binom{N}{N-2r}_q\frac{q^{r(r-1)}\prod_{j=1}^r(q^{2j-1}-1)}{q-1}.
   \]

For example, among degree-three hypersurfaces through every rational point of \(\mathbb P^4_{\mathbb F_2}\), exactly \(868\) equations have rank four (one scalar per equation); each has an isolated length-\(16\) Jacobian vertex and a crepant blow-up, but is not \(F\)-pure at that vertex. For \(q=3\), exactly \(28{,}314\) scalar classes of degree-four hypersurfaces of rank four have a length-\(81\) Jacobian vertex with discrepancy \(-1\) (log canonical, not klt). For \(q\ge4\) at rank four, the vertex is not log canonical.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is the perfect finite field \(\mathbb F_q\); all geometric assertions hold after passing to its algebraic closure. \(q=p^h\) is any prime power; \(N\ge5\), \(2\le r\le\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor\), and \(c=N-2r\). A space-filling equation has minimum degree \(q+1\). The rank-two locus, whose equations split into rational hyperplanes, is excluded from normality and discrepancy statements. The zero form and non-space-filling equations are excluded. The Jacobian singular scheme means the projective subscheme defined by \(F\) and its first partial derivatives, not the reduced singular set. \(I_V^{[q]}\) denotes the ideal generated by the \(q\)-th powers of generators of \(I_V\). The Gaussian binomial coefficient counts \(c\)-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^N\). Discrepancies are for the normal Gorenstein hypersurface with its canonical divisor; no assumption of characteristic zero is used in the explicit canonical-divisor computation.

## Proof
**(1) Alternating form and normal form.** The degree-\(q+1\) homogeneous part of the ideal of all \(\mathbb P^{N-1}(\mathbb F_q)\) is spanned by \(X_i^qX_j-X_iX_j^q\). They are linearly independent, so the coefficient matrix is unique. Alternating bilinear forms over every finite field, including characteristic two, admit the symplectic normal form \(r\) hyperbolic blocks plus a \(c\)-dimensional radical. A change of variables defined over \(\mathbb F_q\) commutes with taking \(q\)-th powers, yielding the displayed equation. Its derivatives, up to signs, are the \(q\)-th powers of all \(2r\) transversal variables: \(\partial F/\partial U_i=-V_i^q\) and \(\partial F/\partial V_i=U_i^q\). Because the degree \(q+1\) is a unit modulo \(p\), Euler's identity gives \(F=\sum X_j(\partial F/\partial X_j)\). Hence the full homogeneous Jacobian ideal is \((U_1^q,V_1^q,\ldots,U_r^q,V_r^q)\). It is a regular sequence of pure powers, so its Hilbert series and degree are the stated complete-intersection formulas. Saturation does not alter this ideal because \(c\ge1\) gives at least one independent vertex coordinate: multiplication by that coordinate is injective on the quotient, so there is no irrelevant torsion.

**(2) Geometric integrity and normality.** On \(\mathbb P^{2r-1}\), the transversal base hypersurface \(Y_r\) has no geometric singular point: all its partial derivatives vanish only at the zero vector, which is not projective. A smooth hypersurface of positive dimension in projective space \(\mathbb P^{2r-1}\), \(r\ge2\), is geometrically integral (different components would intersect, contradicting smoothness). Thus \(X\) is the geometrically integral projective cone over \(Y_r\) with linear vertex \(V\). Its singular support is exactly \(V\), of codimension \((N-2)-(c-1)=2r-1\ge3\) in \(X\). As a hypersurface, \(X\) is Cohen--Macaulay and satisfies \(S_2\); smoothness off \(V\) supplies \(R_1\). Serre's criterion gives normality.

**(3) One blow-up and discrepancy.** The ambient blow-up of \(\mathbb P^{N-1}\) along smooth linear \(V\), of codimension \(2r\), has exceptional divisor \(\mathbb P(N_{V/\mathbb P^{N-1}})\cong \mathbb P^{2r-1}\times V\). The equation has order exactly \(q+1\) along \(V\), and its projectivized normal cone is \(Y_r\times V\). In every blow-up chart, dividing the equation by the appropriate exceptional coordinate to power \(q+1\) leaves the same smooth equation for \(Y_r\) in the slope variables, independent of the chart's exceptional and vertex parameters; therefore the strict transform \(\widetilde X\) is smooth and its exceptional divisor is \(E=Y_r\times V\). The ambient discrepancy is \(2r-1\), while the strict transform subtracts \(q+1\) copies of the exceptional divisor. Adjunction gives \(a(E;X)=2r-1-(q+1)=2r-q-2\). Because the displayed morphism is already a log resolution with one smooth, simple-normal-crossing exceptional component, the usual discrepancy inequalities give the sharp log canonical, klt, canonical and terminal ranges. The equality walls \(a=-1\), \(a=0\) occur at \(q=2r-1\) and \(q=2r-2\), respectively.

**(4) Failure of \(F\)-purity.** On each affine chart meeting \(V\), the local equation is a sum of monomials each divisible by a \(q\)-th power of one transversal local parameter. Since \(p\mid q\), it lies in \(\mathfrak m^{[p]}\). Consequently \(F^{p-1}\in\mathfrak m^{[p]}\), so Fedder's hypersurface criterion excludes \(F\)-purity at every vertex point. This holds even in the discrepancy-zero characteristic-two cubic-cone case; it is a statement of failure of Frobenius purity, not of geometric nonnormality.

**(5) Counting coefficient classes.** To obtain an alternating form of rank \(2r\), first select its \(c\)-dimensional radical: \(\binom Nc_q\) choices. For each radical, select a nondegenerate alternating form on the quotient of dimension \(2r\). The symplectic group acts transitively on such forms, so their number equals \(|\mathrm{GL}_{2r}(\mathbb F_q)|/|\mathrm{Sp}_{2r}(\mathbb F_q)|=q^{r(r-1)}\prod_{j=1}^r(q^{2j-1}-1)\). The degree-\(q+1\) generators are independent, so proportional equations give the same projective hypersurface and there are exactly \(q-1\) nonzero scalars per hypersurface. Division proves the formula. All counts are embedded equation classes, not isomorphism classes.

## Verification
The standalone `checker.py` uses exact integer and rational arithmetic and direct sparse-polynomial differentiation modulo \(p\). It confirms the Gaussian rank-partition identity \(\sum_r\binom{N}{N-2r}_q q^{r(r-1)}\prod_{j=1}^r(q^{2j-1}-1)=q^{\binom N2}\) for \(N=5,6,7,8\) and \(q=2,3,4,5,8,9\). It also exhaustively enumerates all \(2^{10}\) binary alternating 5-by-5 matrices (counts of ranks 0,2,4: \(1,155,868\)), and checks the polar identities and the wall inequalities for all covered normal rank strata, as well as the \(N=5\) examples. These finite checks are not an infinite proof; steps (1)--(5) above prove the quantified theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Bourgeois-Conti, Moon, Perez and Warren (2026), Section 3, prove that minimum-degree space-filling equations are alternating Frobenius forms and that smoothness is equivalent to invertibility of their coefficient matrix. The rank-dependent *nonreduced Jacobian scheme*, its complete-intersection Hilbert series, the all-rank embedded census, the blow-up resolution, the discrepancy wall and the Frobenius-purity obstruction are not stated in that source. Tironi (2014/2017) classifies hypersurfaces achieving the Homma--Kim bound, including their alternating presentation, but not these singularity invariants. Homma and Kim (2016/2017) treat the nonsingular extremal case, not the singular rank strata. Generic finite-field hyperplane-arrangement Jacobian thickenings concern a different polynomial and singular support. Particular small-field singular-length instances are known and are not independently claimed as new; the theorem addresses the full quantified normal-rank family and its discrepancy phases.

## Limitations
The formula does not classify the reducible rank-two members or positive-characteristic finer invariants such as local \(F\)-pure thresholds. It does not enumerate isomorphism classes under all projective transformations; it enumerates scalar-distinct equations. The log-singularity terms refer to the explicitly resolved normal Gorenstein models. Novelty is relative to the identified published results and accessible mathematical records; independently published but unindexed global statements remain a literature-priority risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been carried out.
