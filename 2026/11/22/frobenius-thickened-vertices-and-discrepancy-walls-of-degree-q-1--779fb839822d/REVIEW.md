# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The coefficient normal form follows from the minimum-degree Frobenius generators and finite-field symplectic reduction. Derivatives yield the exact Frobenius-power Jacobian ideal; regular-sequence algebra gives Hilbert series and degree. Smoothness of the transverse base, Serre normality, explicit single blow-up with adjunction, and Fedder's criterion prove the stated geometric and arithmetic thresholds. Group-index counting proves the embedded rank census. The checker confirms finite arithmetic and polar examples, not the general theorem by enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest source is Bourgeois-Conti–Moon–Perez–Warren, arXiv:2611.15768v1, §3: it supplies the alternating presentation and smoothness criterion, from which the reduced linear singular support is immediate; this part is credited rather than claimed original. Tironi arXiv:1410.7320 and Homma–Kim arXiv:1611.02371 cover space-filling equations and the smooth case. The quantified nonreduced Jacobian ideal, embedded rank census, blow-up discrepancy threshold, and non-F-purity contrast are a combination not supplied by those theorems. Other mathematical records contain individual singular vertex-length instances but do not subsume all fields and ranks. Novelty could be limited by unindexed equivalent singularity calculations and should be independently checked before publication.

## Value — PASS
The source asks which minimal-degree space-filling hypersurfaces are smooth. The complementary singular locus is not uniform as a scheme: its length grows as \(q^{2r}\), while normality, discrepancies, Frobenius purity and sharp birational singularity type are controlled by the same rank. The complete formula counts precisely how often each stratum occurs in the embedded minimum-degree linear system. This gives a natural, all-parameter classification of previously unenumerated local geometry, not a sample of small-field equations.

## Limits
Rank-two reduced arrangements are excluded from the geometric theorem. The census is of scalar-equivalence classes, not projective isomorphism classes. The checker is supplementary; the proof is mathematical, and publication priority retains an unindexed-literature risk.
