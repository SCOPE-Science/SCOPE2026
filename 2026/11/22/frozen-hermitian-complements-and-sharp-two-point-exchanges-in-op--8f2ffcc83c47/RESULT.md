# Frozen Hermitian complements and sharp two-point exchanges in \(\operatorname{PG}(2,4)\)

## Finding

Let \(\Pi=\operatorname{PG}(2,4)\) be the Desarguesian projective plane of order four. Its 21 points lie on 21 lines, each of size five. A **two-fold blocking set** is a set of points meeting each line at least twice. It is known that the minimum has 12 points and that the minimum sets fall into three geometric types (Govaerts, 2003); those static facts are credited to prior work.

Let \(\mathcal B\) be the family of all minimum two-fold blocking sets. For a positive integer \(t\), join two distinct members \(A,B\in\mathcal B\) in \(\mathcal R_t\) if \(|A\setminus B|=|B\setminus A|\le t\). Thus \(t=1\) is optimum-preserving one-point replacement, whereas \(t=2\) allows either one or two simultaneous replacements. The following is the new dynamic classification:

- The known three geometric types, identified by their line-intersection profiles \((h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4,h_5)\), have sizes \(1120\), \(10080\), and \(280\). Their profiles are respectively \((0,0,9,9,0,3)\), \((0,0,10,6,3,2)\), and \((0,0,12,0,9,0)\).
- \(\mathcal R_1\) has 280 isolated vertices, exactly the complements of the nine-point Hermitian unitals. The remaining 11200 vertices form one connected component with 90720 edges, radius eight, and diameter eight. Its 1120 triangle-type vertices have degree 27, each adjacent only to the two-full-line type; its 10080 two-full-line-type vertices have degree 15, with three neighbors of triangle type and twelve of their own type.
- \(\mathcal R_2\) is connected on all 11480 configurations. It has exactly 907200 edges, radius four, and diameter five. Hence the first exchange budget permitting global reconfiguration is two.

A graph-theoretic consequence concerns the 42-vertex point–line incidence graph \(G\) of \(\Pi\). A *double total dominating set* \(D\) requires every vertex to have at least two neighbors in \(D\). Since the two bipartition shores constrain one another independently, \(\gamma_{\times2,t}(G)=24\) and the 131790400 minimum sets are pairs of members of \(\mathcal B\) in the primal and dual planes. Their one-vertex-replacement graph is \(\mathcal R_1\square\mathcal R_1\): one connected 125440000-vertex component of diameter 16, 560 components of size 11200, and 78400 isolated vertices, a total of 78961 components.

## Assumptions and scope

The claim is for the *specific finite Desarguesian plane* \(\operatorname{PG}(2,4)\), not all projective planes of order four with unverified structure, nor all field orders. All point sets are labeled as subsets of the 21 explicitly indexed points. Two states are different whenever their actual point subsets differ; no symmetry quotient is taken when counting configurations. A simultaneous exchange of budget \(t\) means replacing at most \(t\) selected points while maintaining cardinality 12 and feasibility at both endpoints; intermediate configurations need not be feasible during a single simultaneous step. The one-vertex-replacement graph on the incidence graph always preserves both shore sizes at their minimum 12.

## Proof

Use \(\mathbf F_4=\mathbf F_2[\omega]/(\omega^2+\omega+1)\), with elements coded \(0,1,\omega,\omega+1\). Index the 21 projective points by the unique first-nonzero-coordinate-one representative of a nonzero triple. Index the 21 projective lines similarly by nonzero coefficient triples, and declare incidence by the zero dot product. Enumerating nonzero triples gives exactly 21 distinct projective point and line representatives; their incidence masks all have five set bits and are pairwise distinct.

For a 21-bit point mask \(s\), two-fold blocking is exactly the integer condition \(\operatorname{popcount}(s\mathbin{\&}\ell)\ge2\) for every one of the 21 line masks \(\ell\). The two independent standalone verifiers in this package enumerate all \(2^{21}\) masks and test that no feasible mask has fewer than twelve bits and precisely 11480 masks have exactly twelve. Recording the 21 line intersections for each mask partitions these 11480 masks into the three profiles and multiplicities stated above. This reproduces the *previously known* static classification as a check, not a new priority claim.

The following five explicit transformations of projective triples are incidence-preserving: \((a,b,c)\mapsto(b,a,c)\), \((a,b,c)\mapsto(a+b,b,c)\), \((a,b,c)\mapsto(\omega a,b,c)\), field Frobenius \((a,b,c)\mapsto(a^2,b^2,c^2)\), and \((a,b,c)\mapsto(a,c,b)\). They act on line coefficients by inverse transpose (and the corresponding field automorphism). The Python verifier checks line-set preservation explicitly and performs orbit closure on the three representative states. Its orbit sizes are precisely 1120, 10080, and 280, and the orbits exhaust the respective profile classes. Consequently, any graph distance or degree statistic calculated at one state in each class holds at all states in that class.

To construct \(\mathcal R_1\), for each of the 11480 masks replace each of its twelve selected positions by each of its nine unselected positions and retain only valid target masks. This finite construction is exhaustive because two size-12 subsets differ by a single replacement if and only if their symmetric difference consists of exactly these two positions. The computed degree-by-type vectors (in order: triangle type, two-full-line type, unital-complement type) are \((0,27,0)\), \((3,12,0)\), and \((0,0,0)\). Double-counting edges gives \([1120\cdot27+10080\cdot15]/2=90720\). Breadth-first search from one state of each transitive profile produces a reached set of size 11200 with maximum distance eight for each nonfrozen profile, and a singleton for the unital profile. Their complete BFS layer counts are \((1,27,135,711,1863,3972,3249,1188,54)\), \((1,15,132,619,1979,3759,3465,1146,84)\), and \((1)\). Since the first two reached sets both have size 11200, they are the same connected component; transitivity proves its radius and diameter are both eight.

For \(\mathcal R_2\), additionally choose any two of the twelve selected and any two of the nine unselected positions and exchange them at once. The degree-by-type vectors become \((27,135,0)\), \((15,141,3)\), and \((0,108,0)\). Thus the graph has \([1120\cdot162+10080\cdot159+280\cdot108]/2=907200\) edges. Breadth-first search from the three representatives reaches all 11480 states, with respective maximum distances five, four, and five, and complete distance layers \((1,162,2817,7230,1269,1)\), \((1,159,2901,7159,1260)\), and \((1,108,3078,6984,1305,4)\). Orbit transitivity makes these exact eccentricities for every state of each profile; therefore \(\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal R_2)=4\) and \(\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal R_2)=5\). Since \(\mathcal R_1\) is disconnected, this is the sharp connectivity threshold.

For the incidence-graph corollary, one shore of a double total dominating set must meet each five-element line neighborhood in at least two chosen points, and the dual shore satisfies the identical condition. Each shore therefore contributes at least twelve vertices, independently. Equality gives pairs of members of \(\mathcal B\), totaling \(11480^2=131790400\). At equality, one cannot replace a point-side vertex by a line-side vertex, since one shore would have only eleven chosen vertices and violate minimality. Therefore optimum-preserving single replacements change exactly one coordinate of the pair, yielding the Cartesian product \(\mathcal R_1\square\mathcal R_1\). Its connected components and diameters follow by multiplying the component factors and adding component diameters.

## Verification

The embedded standalone Python 3 program `verify.py` constructs \(\mathbf F_4\) by an explicit multiplication table, exhausts all 21-bit masks, verifies the five incidence-preserving permutations and their three state orbits, and executes exact BFS for both exchange budgets. The independent C++17 program `verify.cpp` constructs field multiplication from the cyclic multiplicative group and exhausts all 21-bit masks afresh. It independently checks all three classes, all one-exchange degrees, the 281 connected components, and BFS eccentricities for both budgets. Both programs were compiled/executed from the actual packaged paths, not inferred from previous computations; their complete executable source is embedded. No external libraries, downloads, randomization, or hidden data are required. The scripts prove finite completeness through explicit bounded loops; no extrapolation beyond this plane is made.

## Relationship to prior work

Govaerts's 2003 thesis, Appendix A.2, already classifies the twelve-point double blocking sets of \(\operatorname{PG}(2,4)\) up to isomorphism into three forms and identifies the unital-complement form. Its associated secant profiles were previously considered by Laskar and Sherk, as acknowledged in the thesis. These results are **not** presented as new. Henning and Yeo (2010) study double total domination and strong transversals in general graphs and hypergraphs, including the Heawood equality graph, not the optimum exchange network of the order-four plane. Suzuki, Mouawad and Nishimura (2014) study reconfiguration under vertex additions/deletions for ordinary dominating sets of size at most \(k\), rather than minimum two-fold blocking sets with fixed-cardinality simultaneous point exchanges. De Beule, Héger, Szőnyi and Van de Voorde (2016) construct blocking sets and double blocking sets in wider families of finite planes but do not give this exact order-four exchange graph. The new result concerns the complete dynamic graph, sharp budget threshold and exact distance metrics; it is not the familiar static classification in different wording.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the unique specified Desarguesian order-four projective plane and fixed-size minimum configurations; it does not assert analogous geometry for higher orders or other domination variants. The proof is a transparent finite exhaustive certificate, not an all-order symbolic classification. Searches and full-text comparisons do not establish absolute priority against unindexed or inaccessible historical work. The original 2003 thesis is catalogued with record creation on 11 December 2003; that date supports archival eligibility but is not asserted to be its first appearance on the internet. Independent scholarly validation has not been performed.
