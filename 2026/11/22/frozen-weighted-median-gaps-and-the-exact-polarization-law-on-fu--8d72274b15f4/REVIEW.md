# Mathematical assessment: common-influence median-gap law

## Correctness — PASS

The response rule is the projection onto the full weighted-median minimizer interval, not an arbitrary endpoint; this is critical at weight one half. Positive common row weights ensure either one weighted-median value or a genuine gap separating two exactly half-weight groups. The endpoint agents stay fixed, all other agents stay on their side and contract toward the endpoint, so the interval is invariant for every finite iterate. The exact coordinate formula follows without invoking convergence of an unrelated model. The probability formula follows from a disjoint partition of rank permutations and does not count complementary balanced sets twice: each ordered split corresponds to exactly one lower-ranked balanced subset. For uniform initial opinions, each spacing is Beta with parameters one and population size, and its distribution is independent of the rank permutation. Binary-encoded integer weights reduce the probability-positivity decision exactly to PARTITION, with a subset certificate giving NP membership. The all-size proofs do not rely on the finite checker. Verification source is supplied and the 3,267 and 1,089 exact-rational tests passed.

The closest 2026 primary source proves convergence, equilibrium characterization and the no-disjoint-cohesive-sets criterion for a broader mixed class. It already covers the mere existence of dissensus for the rank-one subclass and, via a short specialization, the effect of adding one averaging agent. Those statements are explicitly credited, not presented as the novel claim. Its inspected main text and supplement do not derive the immutable median interval, closed-form full orbit for arbitrary heterogeneous adjustment, or the exact random initial-condition law and conditional Beta gap. The 2024 asynchronous weighted-median work establishes unrelated generic NP-hardness for almost-sure dissensus, while the 2024–25 continuous-time median work provides a close but nonidentical flow and general theory; neither inspected text contained the full proposed discrete-time probability law. Closest publicly indexed research records concern continuous-time bipartite medians, weighted-median stars, and directional ring consensus, not a rank-one common-influence frozen interval or balanced-subset gap distribution. Residual risk: a past specialization or unpublished equivalent might be overlooked, and the common-median argument is elementary once the subclass is recognized. No independence or comprehensive-priority claim is made.

## Value — PASS

Identical all-to-all influence rows form a natural well-mixed limit of social aggregation rather than an arbitrary parametrization. The finding resolves the actual limiting opinion vector from every initial state, not only whether an equilibrium exists. For random opinions it makes the risk of polarization a computable exact balanced-coalition probability and gives a universal nonzero gap law independent of the detailed influence weights. The sharp even/odd split for equal weights and arithmetic NP-completeness for unequal integer weights show distinct structural and computational consequences, while a single averaging agent contrasts the all-median phenomenon with the broader model. This is relevant to quantifying polarization under random initialization and designing minimal behavioral interventions. The general cohesive-set criterion, classical PARTITION hardness and classical uniform-spacing law are credited and not themselves offered as novel discoveries.
