# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The declared channel is a single indexed deletion followed by rotation-equivalence, not an unmarked deletion followed by reindexing. A repeated cyclic adjacent symbol gives a genuine collision. A unique third-symbol marker transfers all other deletion outputs bijectively to distinct linear deletions of an alternating word, realizing the maximum \(n\). To bound the proper-word exceptions, each collision alignment determines a permutation of original sites with its deleted source site mapped to the other deleted site. A fixed point would let the identical circular outputs be rooted at a common surviving position, contradicting the distinctness of two linear deletions of a proper source. All compatible permutations are therefore fixed-point-free and have at most \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) cycles. A direct union bound over \(\binom n2(n-1)\) alignments proves the finite bound and exponential asymptotic. The cycle chromatic polynomial is independently reconstructed by a transfer matrix. Literal enumeration verifies all reported finite values. The exact asymptotic constant and finite counts beyond the tested range are not asserted.

## Originality — PASS against accessible literature
The closest result on exact one-deletion output counts concerns binary necklaces and proves smaller maximal shadows. The restricted repeated-permutation theorem has periodic inputs and in fact predicts collisions between deletion sites a period apart. The 2024 cyclic-subword study treats reconstruction of binary source necklaces from other scattered-subword lengths. The 2025 circular trace paper concerns stochastic sample complexity, and the 2026 necklace enumeration paper concerns generating words in colexicographic order. None of their inspected statements implies that almost every proper nonbinary circle has all single-deletion output necklaces distinct, with an explicit exponential bound. Searches for circular deletion shadows, ternary necklaces, proper colorings, and equivalent cyclic trace terminology did not reveal a dominating theorem; this is evidence of a gap, not proof of priority.

## Value — PASS
For an unrooted circular one-deletion channel, rotation can merge otherwise different read outputs. The theorem establishes a sharp alphabet threshold: three colors allow the information-theoretic maximum \(n\) for every length, whereas the known binary result has a linear but strictly smaller maximum for lengths at least four. The full-shadow property is not merely realized by specially marked words: it is exponentially typical under the natural proper-cycle constraint, with a quantitative all-length upper bound on exceptional populations. This separates local repeated-symbol loss from genuinely global rotational collisions in circular trace support analysis.

## Risks and scope
An older unindexed necklace-shadow or cyclic-deletion-graph theorem could be equivalent; no independence or external certification is claimed. The upper bound is not claimed to be sharp for each finite length, and the typicality claim is conditioned on cyclic properness. Exactly one deleted symbol, labeled letters, and rotation-only output equivalence are essential.
