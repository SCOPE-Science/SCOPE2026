# Assessment of the complete-bipartite enharmonic monodromy theorem

## Correctness

**PASS.** For pairwise distinct boundary values, evaluation at the boundary points is an invertible linear map from the degree-at-most-\(n-1\) polynomial coefficients to the \(n\) row energies. The row transformations are independent. This realizes \(m\) independent generic degree-\(n-1\) polynomials, and the symmetric-invariant-field construction proves their joint Galois group is the entire direct product, over both \(\mathbb Q\) and its algebraic closure. Each generic discriminant is irreducible and its generic double-root point supplies transposition inertia. Monic finite-flatness and the tensor-product trace pairing yield the exact trace discriminant with exponent \((n-1)^{m-1}\). An exact rational example with two independent cubic splitting fields and a dependency-free checker corroborate the computations. None of the all-parameter claims rests on a finite test.

## Originality

**PASS, with priority limitations.** Finkel, *Galois Actions on Energy-Harmonic Functions*, arXiv:2611.00990v1, Section 5.1, Proposition 15 proves only an upper inclusion for the generic group on \(K_{m,n}\); Example 16 gives \(K_{2,3}\). The new claim supplies equality for every \(m\ge2,n\ge3\) and the complete branch/trace-discriminant formula, not merely the known decoupling or its smallest example. The related paper *Graphical Scattering Equations*, arXiv:2511.07316v1, assumes momentum-conservation equations and does not give these results. Abrams–Kenyon's foundational fixed-energy work supplies the underlying physical and combinatorial setting; its accessible abstract does not state the new classification, although its full text was not available for an exhaustive comparison. Standard generic-polynomial Galois theory is an input rather than a claim of novelty. A previously unindexed equivalent calculation remains a risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The generic graph-configuration problem asks for the actual Galois permutations of solutions, not only that the solutions decouple. The exact product action distinguishes these covers from full symmetric covers on \((n-1)^m\) solutions, computes their Galois-closure degree, and resolves every reduced codimension-one branch component and its inertia for the entire two-parameter infinite family. The exact trace-discriminant multiplicities also quantify simultaneous permutation of solution tuples. Although the proof uses classical polynomial theory, the coefficient-independence reduction and complete branch classification address a natural outstanding sharpness question left by the initiating paper.

## Closest sources and limitations

1. B. Finkel, arXiv:2611.00990v1 (2026), Section 5.1, Proposition 15 and Example 16: identical graph and equations; only an upper bound and one small equality case.
2. A. Abrams and R. Kenyon, arXiv:1505.05785 (2015): general enharmonic functions and totally real Galois actions, not a full product formula in the accessible abstract; inaccessible full text is a remaining comparison risk.
3. B. Betti et al., arXiv:2511.07316v1 (2025): graph scattering equations with momentum constraints, so not identical independent-energy parameter space.

The theorem applies in characteristic zero with independent generic energies, distinct boundary values and nonzero row sums; it does not classify all rational specializations or degree-drop compactifications. It is not an independently validated priority or correctness certificate.
