# Scientific review: symmetric singular-fiber monodromy on index-three K3 pencils

## Correctness — PASS

The degree-12 ternary-cubic discriminant pulled back along the quadratic Veronese parameter gives degree 24. For a general triple the 24 intersections are transverse nodal cubics, and Bertini makes the bidegree-\((2,3)\) surface smooth. The marked-node incidence is a rank-27 vector bundle over \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\); its ordered-distinct-pair incidence is rank 24 over the configuration of two base parameters and two node points because evaluating binary quadratics at distinct parameters is surjective. Hence geometric monodromy is doubly transitive. A boundary triple with a nodal cubic at one parameter and vanishing first parameter derivative at its node has local discriminant \(\varepsilon+\kappa\tau^2\), \(\kappa\ne0\), and produces a transposition. This forces \(S_{24}\). The arithmetic Galois group contains the geometric group and has the same degree. The Picard-lattice basis \(e,h\) gives fiber divisibilities \(3\) and the covering involution conjugates the two fibrations. The auxiliary symbolic test is supplementary and does not prove monodromy.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority limitations

Galluzzi–van Geemen (arXiv:2611.15908v1, §3.3) construct the exact bidegree-\((2,3)\) surface and its two primitive genus-one pencils, but the paper does not establish the permutation group of their 24 singular parameters. Zhan (arXiv:2004.08719, Theorem 1.1 and §2) proves full \(S_{24}\) for a different universal **sectioned Weierstrass** family. That result does not logically imply monodromy for this rank-two **index-three, non-sectioned** subfamily, where the 24-root discriminant is constrained to the quadratic ternary-cubic form. Sottile–Yahl (arXiv:2108.07905v2, §1) survey the classical double-transitivity/transposition mechanism, which is used rather than claimed as novel. public mathematical-record comparisons returned no equivalent or dominating exact claim. The number 24 and the index lattice are prior or routine ingredients; the restricted-family full permutation monodromy is the claimed new contribution. An older unindexed monodromy calculation may still exist.

## Value — PASS

The claim determines a natural, exact global arithmetic/geometric invariant of a recently studied rank-two K3 family arising in the geometry of cubic and Verra fourfolds. It shows there is no hidden partition or smaller permutation group on singular fibers despite the special quadratic ternary-cubic presentation and period-three genus-one torsor structure. It simultaneously controls both involution-related pencils and gives the full generic splitting-field group. The answer is not implied by a generic singular-fiber count and has uses in base-change and arithmetic specialization questions.

## Closest prior literature and limitations

- Galluzzi–van Geemen, *Some families of birational cubic and Verra fourfolds*, 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15908v1, §§3.3, 3.13; full relevant construction and proof context checked.
- S. Zhan, *Monodromy of rational curves on K3 surfaces of low genus*, 2020/2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08719, Theorem 1.1 and §2; family has sections, unlike the one here.
- Sottile–Yahl, *Galois Groups in Enumerative Geometry and Applications*, https://arxiv.org/html/2108.07905v2, §1; method, not the specific bidegree-\((2,3)\) family.
- J. Baltes, *Singular rational curves on elliptic K3 surfaces*, https://doi.org/10.1002/mana.202200228, §2.3; concerns local homological monodromy and rational curves, not the 24-point permutation cover.

The exact Picard lattice requires very general coefficients. The global group is over parameter space, not the fiberwise \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\) local system. No integral torsor Brauer class or unconditional arithmetic specialization claim is included. Historical unindexed sources could contain a specific predecessor.
