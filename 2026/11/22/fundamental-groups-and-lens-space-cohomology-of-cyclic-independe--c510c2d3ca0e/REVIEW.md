# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof applies the precisely stated classical cycle independence-complex sphere/wedge classification at \(n\ge8\), proves by support-orbit analysis exactly which rotations and reflections fix points, and then applies Armstrong's orbit fundamental-group theorem. In the prime case, it establishes a free action on an odd-dimensional homotopy sphere; the integral Serre spectral sequence and finite-dimensionality force the nonzero transgression, yielding the complete ring without appealing to finite experiments. The standalone finite checker reproduced the expected Smith invariants for prime lengths eleven, thirteen and seventeen.

**Originality — PASS relative to identified sources.** Živaljević's reflection-equivariant results and the nonequivariant cycle sphere classification do not give the ordinary all-subgroup quotient groups, and the closest recorded prime-power multipath result concerns a distinct graph complex. Records about two-independence, nonmatching and Vietoris–Rips complexes do not dominate the present statement. Searches under independence-complex, flag-complex, anti-Rips, cyclic-rotation and lens-space aliases did not uncover an equivalent statement. Search incompleteness, inaccessible earlier journal text and unindexed equivalences remain publication-priority risks; this is not a claim of proven global novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The prime-power fundamental-group dichotomy classifies a natural complete lattice of geometric symmetry quotients. The integral ring exhibits unbounded numbers of prime-torsion degrees arising from cyclic symmetry even though the original cycle independence complexes are torsion-free homotopy spheres. It explains the mechanism using the geometric free action and transgression, not arbitrary finite sample selection.

The main limitation is that cohomology-ring agreement does not imply an actual lens-space homotopy equivalence. Computational samples are checks rather than proofs of the all-integer statement. The literature comparison does not rule out unindexed equivalent results.
