# Review: rank-conditioned fibers in increasing partial injections

## Correctness — PASS
Each increasing partial map is uniquely determined by its ordered domain/range, and the proof identifies an explicit bijection between factorizations with intermediate survivor set \(I\) and choices of four disjoint extra-coordinate sets in corresponding gaps. The product of binomial coefficients exhausts these choices without duplication; coefficient extraction enforces both factor ranks. Boundaries and the empty map are included. In corank one, the four choices of extra outer coordinates produce an exact two-multiset incidence law. The small fiber classification and class sizes are derived algebraically, not extrapolated from enumeration. Direct enumeration of all ordered products for \(n\le4\) and near-unit products for \(2\le n\le12\) agreed with every stated formula. Computational checks do not prove the infinite cases; the gap bijection does.

## Originality — PASS with historical risk
The primary inspected works are Fernandes (2001), on the monoid's structure and presentation; Dimitrova–Koppitz (2009), on maximal subsemigroups; Laradji–Umar (2013), on lattice-path counts for related order-preserving maps; and Lopez (2019), on generating sets and the rank-\(n-1\) composition criterion. None of their inspected claims gives the rank-conditioned prescribed-fiber formula or the complete near-unit \(4,5,6,8\) spectrum. Related indexed results for unrestricted symmetric inverse monoids determine rank-uniform convolution, and for finite-chain partial translations determine aggregate rank probabilities. These statements do not imply the nonuniform prescribed fibers in the larger order-preserving partial-injection monoid. The 2003 Ganyushkin–Mazorchuk paper and the full text of the indexed unrestricted inverse-monoid result were not accessible; an older equivalent counting theorem remains possible, so historical priority is not independently established.

## Value — PASS
Rank-conditioned multiplication fibers are natural structure constants for a finite inverse monoid and refine the widely studied Green-rank and generation data. In contrast to the rank-aggregate law (which coincides with the unrestricted inverse monoid), the actual fibers exhibit sharp gap dependence: among near-unit products \(7\) is excluded while \(8\) is attained at precisely described targets. The complete exact coefficient formula applies for all chain lengths, factor ranks and target maps, and the near-unit classification provides a concise nontrivial application rather than a single isolated low-order table.

## Limits
No all-rank spectrum of possible numerical fiber values beyond the formula is claimed. The inaccessible literature may contain an equivalent general theorem; full historical novelty remains a reasonable residual risk. The computations are finite cross-checks only.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
