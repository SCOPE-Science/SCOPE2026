# Scientific review of the Gaussian parking-stellohedron collision law

## Correctness — PASS
The parameter-independent formula follows from an exact vertex description and the identity that crossing a vertex-layer boundary changes one coordinate's contribution by \((1-s_{k+1})g_{(k+1)}\). This identifies the optimal layer with the negative Gaussian count. Conditional positive-coordinate orders are uniformly random, giving cone mass \(2^{-n}/(n-k)!\) at each of the \(n!/k!\) vertices. Summing squared masses gives the Laguerre polynomial exactly. Stirling's local expansion and monotone factorial-ratio tail bounds prove the \(n^{1/4}\)-scale conditional normal law and the \(e^{-1/2}\) correction. The standard star realization is independently counted by coordinate orders. Boundary layer coalescence is included. All quantifiers, metrics, and the limits are explicit. Exhaustive signed-order optimization was run for sixteen finite cases; numerical calculations were supplementary and never treated as infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations
The polytope, its vertex layers, and normal cones are known from Hanada–Lentfer–Vindas-Meléndez (2022), Bayer et al. (2024), and Liu–Thawinrak (2025). They allow the elementary individual angle count to be reconstructed, so that count alone is not asserted original. The full text of the relevant published results was compared for implication: the known classical exposed-layer binomial law does not determine the probability conditioned on equality of **two** independently exposed vertices; the published standard-star angular collision counts use a different Euclidean realization. The new finite Laguerre collision law, sharp asymptotic constant, Gaussian collision-conditioned active-coordinate limit, and exponential cross-realization ratio are evaluated and proved here as one integrated geometric statement. Source searches did not uncover a stronger prior result implying all these evaluated conclusions. Residual risk: an unindexed or overlooked derivation of these special-function collision asymptotics, particularly via general normal-fan enumerations.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result quantifies an extreme noninvariance of angular collision probabilities under combinatorial equivalence: two canonical stellohedra have collision rates separated by an exponential factor. It also explains a rare-event phenomenon—two independent directions selecting the same parking-polytope vertex forces the exposed non-unit rank down from linear to square-root order, with a sharp Gaussian limit. This is a motivated angular concentration statistic, not a random finite lookup or a mere rephrasing of the vertex count. The exact \(b_1=1\) boundary clarifies the limit under vertex-orbit coalescence.
