# Scientific review: Gaussian angular spectra and collision-orbit inversion for two-lineup products of simplices

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact Gaussian selection follows from the two alternatives for the runner-up of an additive grid and from uniform independent row/column rank permutations. The one-dimensional Gaussian gap survivor yields all individual cone masses and the squared-mass collision identity. A complete Poisson-exceedance proof supplies the exponential gap limits and the fixed-length ranked outer-sum process; the collision contribution ratio then proves orbit inversion. Explicit finite tests and quadrature of the new integral passed, and they are not substituted for the analytic proof.

**Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority.** The full specialized paper of Castillo and Labbé, arXiv:2306.00082 (2023-05-31), contains lineup-cone and additive-grid combinatorics, not Gaussian cone measures. The closest same-object published mathematical result at https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/tree/main/2026/10/11/SCOPE-two-lineup-products-of-two-simplices-have-an-exact-all-vertex-mi--8c763d514594 already classifies the two vertex families, and that classification is treated as prior. It determines their enclosing sphere, not their angular probability spectrum. Mallows–Vanderbei (2015) treats realizable order tables, not a Gaussian weighting of those tables. A different published two-orbit collision transition occurs for enriched-chain polytopes, and does not imply this product-simplex law. Search absence does not establish categorical novelty; a broader unindexed theorem may overlap.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact law resolves the normal-cone exposure probabilities of every vertex of a family with \(pq(p+q-2)\) vertices, gives the two-direction collision probability without numerical cone-volume integration, and isolates an asymptotic inversion of exposure-versus-collision typicality. The same additive-grid mechanism yields a genuine joint Poisson rank limit for all fixed lineup lengths, not merely a maximum-score limit.

**Limitations.** Orthogonal standard embedding; strictly decreasing positive lineup weights; angular claim for length two; fixed-rank Poisson limit under positive polynomial factor growth.
