In a dense random habitat with a fixed number \(s\) of absorbing sites, the principal invasion threshold fluctuates on the \(n^{-1/2}\) scale with variance coefficient \(sp(1-p)\), yielding an exact Gaussian crossover for population viability. Its gap below the observed average sink-contact count is asymptotically \(s(1-p)/n\), revealing a universal second-order relaxation correction.
