# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The reflected Neumann/Dirichlet heat kernels give a rigorously Hilbert–Schmidt commutator for every finite-measure indicator. Expanding the squared kernels yields a separable Gaussian cross term, hence the exact mass-only contrast and its sharp quarter bound. Two explicit Gaussian tail integrals give the individual interval profiles. The derivative signs follow from the strict Mills inequality and an explicit increasing auxiliary function, proving rather than merely numerically guessing the unique Neumann peak. Short-window constants follow from Taylor expansion and a dominated Gaussian integral. The standalone reproducibility script passed 10,053 assertions, supplementary to the proof.

## Originality
PASS subject to historical-priority risk. The closest papers checked concern fixed-set heat-content asymptotics (Schilling), commutators of boundary-condition Riesz transforms (Duong et al.), and Schatten estimates for covariance kernels (Kovács–Lang–Petersson). Their stated results do not supply the global Gaussian-mass-only Neumann-minus-Dirichlet Hilbert–Schmidt contrast, its sharp mask optimization, or the one-variable peak and cubic absorbing-wall law. Classical method-of-images kernels are acknowledged as an essential known tool, and a more specialized equivalent heat-leakage computation may exist.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The gap's invariance under arbitrary changes of mask geometry at fixed Gaussian boundary mass is a structural identifiability limitation, with an exactly attained optimal boundary-condition contrast. The interval profile reveals a genuine reflecting-wall overshoot and a sharp quadratic difference of energy-decay exponents for shrinking observation windows. These distinctions are natural for localized diffusion and operator-ideal diagnostics rather than a gratuitous parameter slice.

## Scope and limitations
The theorem is specific to one-dimensional heat semigroups on the half-line, characteristic-function masks, and squared Hilbert–Schmidt norms. It neither proves analogous laws in other dimensions nor determines individual singular-value distributions. Classical heat-content equivalences and incomplete historical coverage remain the primary originality risks.
