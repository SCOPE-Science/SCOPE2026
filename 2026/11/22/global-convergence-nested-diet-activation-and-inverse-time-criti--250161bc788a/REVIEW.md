# Scientific review: dynamic floral rewards and adaptive foraging

## Correctness — PASS

The equilibrium is determined by a unique decreasing scalar water-level equation; substitution proves all steady-state identities including zero-preference boundary cases. The composite resource-relative-entropy and preference-relative-entropy functional has an exact nonpositive derivative. Its only invariant zero-dissipation set is the stated equilibrium, which proves global attraction for initially positive rewards and full-support preferences in any finite dimension and for any positive adaptation speed. Strictly ordered activation thresholds follow by evaluating the water-level equation at each unconsumed reward level. For two sources at the first activation threshold, asymptotic convolution estimates turn the neutral invasion equation into an exact inverse-time coefficient. The supplied numerical checker tests formulae and trajectories; it is supplementary, not the proof.

Limitation: The all-dimensional convergence theorem needs constant, positive production, degradation, consumption, guild density, and behavioral adjustment; the inverse-time coefficient is proved for two floral sources only. Boundary initial conditions excluding a flower are outside the global full-support statement.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

Earlier coupled floral reward and adaptive-foraging dynamics are already present in Valdovinos et al. (2013), equations (6) and (7), in which empirical networks are investigated numerically; the basic mechanism is expressly credited as prior. The economic/biological equal-profit condition itself is old: Valdovinos and Marsland (2021), *Niche Theory for Mutualism*, equation (4), equilibrium equations (6)–(7), and Appendix A analyze adaptive-foraging stationarity; this is explicitly credited. Garay, Cressman and Varga (2003), *Optimal behaviour of honey bees based on imitation at fixed densities*, analyze an algebraic nectar-stock matrix game and prove local stability, not the full finite-dimensional dynamic-reward flow or its critical law. Yahaya, Rodger, Landi and Hui (2024), *Emergence of structure in plant–pollinator networks*, report that increasing animal abundance reduces network specialisation and examine a two-plant/one-animal toy model, but the inspected main text does not formulate the all-dimensional global-convergence, exact support-staircase, and inverse-time critical-recruitment combination.

Searching for ecological resource renewal, foraging replicator global stability, ideal free diet, and critical inverse-time relaxation yielded no inspected stronger result with the same assumptions and conclusion. The 2024 article's separate supplementary toy-model appendix could not be read, despite a retrieval attempt; it may contain additional boundary-local-stability facts. This limits confidence in historical priority, especially for the two-source corollary. Standard relative-entropy methods could provide an unlocated broader theorem; no assertion of absolute uniqueness in literature is made.

## Value — PASS

Floral rewards are depleted by visitors, so a larger consumer population can alter which floral species receive sustained attention even if floral production and degradation remain unchanged. This complete support classification converts a qualitative, simulation-supported specialization trend into a sharp and reproducible finite-dimensional ecological prediction. The global convergence theorem rules out sustained internally generated preference cycling under the restricted fixed-guild mechanism, identifying which extra mechanisms would be needed for cycling. The inverse-time boundary specifies a critical slowing-down mechanism precisely at the onset of diet expansion, relevant to interpreting long transient observations without mistaking them for stable specialization. No field calibration or full ecological-network extension is claimed.
