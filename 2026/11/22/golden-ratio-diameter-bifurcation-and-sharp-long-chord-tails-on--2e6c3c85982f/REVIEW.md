# Scientific assessment: truncated-parabola diameter and long-chord law

## Correctness — PASS
For independent uniform *parameters* on the compact arc \(P(t)=(t,t^2)\), the exact squared-chord identity reduces the global diameter to two one-variable edge maximizations, whose stationary equation is quadratic. The tie equation becomes \(w^4-w^2-1=0\); it has exactly one relevant positive root, yielding \(a_*=\sqrt{(11+5\sqrt5)/8}\). For the probability law, the unique corner maximum has a linear–linear local loss, whereas each endpoint–interior maximum has a linear–quadratic loss with strictly positive coefficients. Integration gives the exact constants of the \(\varepsilon^2\) and \(\varepsilon^{3/2}\) terms and the conditional limits. The endpoint contribution at equality has strictly lower order. Standalone quadrature agrees with both leading constants. Risk: the proof has not been assessed independently; no parameter-uniform transition window is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification
Bäsel (arXiv:1204.2707) calculates motion-invariant random line chords and interior distances of *regular polygons*; those distributions are not the same experiment as independently sampled parameters on a curved parabola. Mayer–Molchanov (arXiv:math/0605289) proves random-diameter limit theorems with examples on balls and spheres; the inspected text does not contain the parabolic-arc golden-ratio contact change or either coefficient. Related indexed exact results treat smooth ellipses, polygonal boundary sampling or other convex geometries, with different objects/contact regimes. Searches for parabolic-arc diameter threshold, reflected contact pairs and near-diameter parameter-uniform tails did not locate an implication establishing this result. Risk: a specialized, unindexed elementary calculation of the same parabola could exist. Search absence is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The diameter transition is a natural complete optimization of a classical conic arc, and the golden-ratio equality class separates two geometrically distinct extremal contact mechanisms. Their exact probabilistic \(2\)- versus \(3/2\)-power exponents show that the two mechanisms are distinguishable from near-diameter measurements; the conditional limit regions describe where rare long chords arise. These are motivated geometric invariants, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a numerical recomputation.

## Limits and closest literature
The model uses uniform parameter samples rather than arclength samples, and the result is for fixed arc width as the deficit vanishes. It does not assert global chord-density formulas or arbitrary-conic generalizations. Historical priority and correctness have not been independently certified.
