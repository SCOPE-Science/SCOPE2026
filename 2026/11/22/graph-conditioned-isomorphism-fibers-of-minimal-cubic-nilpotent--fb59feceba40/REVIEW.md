# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof reconstructs each minimal strict cubic-nilpotent semigroup as a two-valued multiplication table on its intrinsic noncentral vertices. Exactly one free bit remains for each diagonal position and unordered vertex pair. The proof that such tables have the prescribed center uses the no-universal-vertex assumption; without it, central vertices would invalidate the identification. Ordinary isomorphisms preserve both central elements and become precisely the automorphisms of the prescribed commuting graph acting on tables. The fixed-point argument explicitly addresses the only obstruction—an orientation-reversing nonedge orbit—through even-cycle antipodes. Burnside's lemma and cycle-pair gcd counts then prove the arbitrary-order formula. Exhaustive independent enumeration through four vertices and additional targeted calculations confirm small cases without replacing the general proof.

## Originality

PASS, subject to priority risk. Giudici–Kuzma (2015) already prove existence of an order-\(r+2\) realization, and this is credited. Distler–Mitchell (2012) already provide global isomorphism-class enumeration for all 3-nilpotent semigroups. Dolinka–FitzGerald–Mitchell (2024) already provide the general orbit action and the even-cycle antipodal description. The assessed, more specific claim is a closed graph-conditioned isomorphism-fiber count for *each prescribed* graph, with an edge-dependent fixed-point obstruction and corresponding exact rigid-fiber criterion. Neither the unrestricted totals nor the realization theorem alone gives the stated per-graph class count. A broader unpublished or differently phrased graph-conditioned census remains possible; independent historical certification is absent.

## Value

PASS. Counting all minimum-size semigroups realizing a prescribed commutation pattern is an intrinsic inverse classification problem beyond the classical existence theorem. The result gives a finite algorithm for all isomorphism types, detects precisely when every realization is rigid, explains which graph symmetries can lift to semigroup automorphisms, and provides a simple random-graph transfer law. The structural dependence on antipodal edge constraints is nontrivial and varies across graphs with the same number of vertices.

## Evidence and limits

Sources: Giudici–Kuzma, https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05397; Distler–Mitchell, https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3529; Dolinka–FitzGerald–Mitchell, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00466; Bauer–Greenfeld, https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05250. The verification program performs finite exhaustive and independent checks. The result assumes strict cubic nilpotency, minimum center size two, and a graph with no universal vertex; it does not classify larger centers or arbitrary nilpotency indices.
