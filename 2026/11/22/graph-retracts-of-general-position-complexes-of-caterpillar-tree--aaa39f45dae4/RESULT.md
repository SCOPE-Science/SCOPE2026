# Graph retracts of general-position complexes of caterpillar trees

## Finding
Let \(T\) be a finite caterpillar tree on at least three vertices. Its non-leaf vertices induce a path \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\), and \(\ell_i\) is the number of pendant leaves attached at \(v_i\). Set \(L=\sum_i\ell_i\). For \(m\ge 2\), both \(\ell_1\) and \(\ell_m\) are positive. List the indices with positive leaf multiplicity as \(t_1<\cdots<t_s\), and let \(g_j=t_{j+1}-t_j-1\) denote the number of leafless backbone vertices between successive leaf-bearing positions.

Let \(\mathcal{GP}(T)\) be the simplicial complex whose simplices are sets of vertices in *geodesic general position*: no three selected vertices lie on one path between two selected vertices. Then there is an explicit sequence of elementary simplicial collapses to a connected graph, and
\[
\boxed{\quad |\mathcal{GP}(T)|\simeq\bigvee^{B(T)}S^1,\qquad B(T)=L+2m-3+\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\binom{g_j+1}{2}.\quad}
\]
The empty sum is zero; the formula includes stars \(m=1\), for which \(B(T)=L-1\), and ordinary paths, for which \(B(T)=\binom{|V(T)|-1}{2}\). Hence \(\widetilde H_1(\mathcal{GP}(T);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{B(T)}\), all other reduced integral homology vanishes, and the fundamental group is free of rank \(B(T)\). The collapse is a simple-homotopy statement, stronger than a homology calculation.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, connected, simple and undirected, with unit edge lengths. The *backbone* is the path induced by all non-leaf vertices, uniquely defined for any tree on at least three vertices. A leaf attached at \(v_i\) is individually labelled; the formula counts the genuine simplicial complex on all vertices, not a quotient by leaf symmetries. A simplex is permitted to have any cardinality as long as it contains no three vertices on one geodesic. The convention for a graph's zero-dimensional complex includes its empty simplex, but all collapses below involve nonempty faces.

## Proof
**Face classification.** Denote by \(P_i\) the set of pendant leaves adjoining \(v_i\), and let \(\mathcal L=\bigsqcup_iP_i\) be the full leaf set. Every subset of \(\mathcal L\) is in general position, since a tree leaf cannot be internal to a path joining two other vertices. No simplex can contain three backbone vertices, since any three linearly ordered backbone vertices are geodesically collinear. With two distinct backbone vertices \(v_i,v_j\) and \(i<j\), the permissible chosen leaves are exactly any subset of \(\bigsqcup_{i<k<j}P_k\): leaves attached outside or at the endpoints make one of the chosen backbone vertices an internal path point. With exactly one backbone vertex \(v_i\), the leaf portion is an arbitrary subset of leaves attached strictly to the left, an arbitrary subset attached strictly to the right, or a single leaf in \(P_i\); one cannot mix these types, since any such pair makes \(v_i\) internal to the joining geodesic. These classifications are necessary and sufficient.

**Collapse I (two backbone vertices).** For each \(i<j\), write \(M_{ij}=\bigsqcup_{i<k<j}P_k\). If \(M_{ij}\ne\varnothing\), choose an anchor \(a_{ij}\in M_{ij}\). Pair each face \(\{v_i,v_j\}\cup S\), for \(S\subseteq M_{ij}\setminus\{a_{ij}\}\), with its coface obtained by adjoining \(a_{ij}\). Remove these pairs in decreasing order of \(|S|\). When a pair is removed, the smaller face is free: any larger coface not equal to its chosen partner has an extra interior leaf, and the corresponding pair was removed earlier. A coface cannot contain another backbone vertex or any exterior/endpoint leaf by the classification. Pairs with different \(i,j\) have disjoint supports as faces involving exactly those backbone vertices. Consequently all faces containing two backbone vertices and at least one leaf disappear, while the edges \(\{v_i,v_j\}\) survive exactly if \(M_{ij}=\varnothing\).

**Collapse II (one backbone vertex).** For each \(v_i\), separately consider the (possibly empty) set \(A_i^-\) of leaves strictly to its left and \(A_i^+\) strictly to its right. For every nonempty one of these sets choose one anchor leaf. Within the cone of faces consisting of \(v_i\) and nonempty subsets of that side, pair every face with at least one non-anchor leaf and without the anchor with the face obtained by adjoining the anchor; remove pairs in descending cardinality. As above, each is a free face at removal. Any two-backbone cofaces have already been eliminated in Collapse I, and there are no cofaces mixing left, right or own leaves. The sole remaining edges from \(v_i\) to leaves are: one anchor edge for each nonempty strict side and the \(\ell_i\) own-leaf edges. All faces of dimension at least two containing a backbone vertex are gone.

**Collapse III (the leaf simplex).** Choose an arbitrary leaf \(a\in\mathcal L\). For every nonempty subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal L\setminus\{a\}\) of cardinality at least two, pair the face \(S\) with \(S\cup\{a\}\), removing in decreasing \(|S|\). Each lower face is free because any other larger leaf face was removed earlier; no backbone-containing coface of size at least three survives. The leaf simplex thus collapses to a star graph with \(L-1\) leaf-leaf edges. Each operation above is an elementary collapse and leaves every original vertex.

**Graph and rank.** There are \(L\) backbone-to-own-leaf edges. For \(m\ge2\), every backbone vertex besides \(v_1\) has at least one leaf to its strict left, and every backbone vertex besides \(v_m\) has at least one leaf strictly to its right, giving exactly \(2m-2\) anchor edges; for \(m=1\), there are none. The surviving backbone-backbone edges are the pairs whose open backbone interval contains no leaf-bearing vertex. For each consecutive leaf-bearing pair \(t_j<t_{j+1}\), exactly \(\binom{g_j+2}{2}\) pairs have both endpoints in their intervening closed backbone segment; these sets of pairs are disjoint and exhaustive, so the number of such edges is
\[
 e_0=\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\binom{g_j+2}{2}=m-1+\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\binom{g_j+1}{2}\quad (m\ge2).
\]
For \(m=1\), set \(e_0=0\). The surviving connected graph has \(V=L+m\) vertices and \(E=(L-1)+L+(2m-2)+e_0\) edges. Its connectedness follows from the surviving leaf-star and all consecutive backbone edges, which attach every backbone vertex to the same component (or from the own-leaf edges when \(m=1\)). Therefore its first Betti number is \(E-V+1=L+m-2+e_0\), yielding the stated closed formula and its star boundary case. A connected graph collapses homotopically to a wedge of \(E-V+1\) circles. This proves the result for all specified parameters without extrapolation from finite cases.

## Verification
The standalone Python checker `verify_caterpillar_collapse.py` independently enumerates faces from all geodesic collinear triples, independently reconstructs the three collapses, and tests directly that *every* proposed smaller face has exactly two current cofaces (itself and the designated paired face), before removing them. It then verifies that the remainder is a connected graph with the claimed first Betti number. An exhaustive bounded profile census and selected larger families passed: 373 caterpillar profiles, 60,300 initial faces and 23,078 individual free-face pairs. The reproducible command is `python3 verify_caterpillar_collapse.py`; its captured output is in `verification_output.txt`. These checks corroborate but do not replace the proof for arbitrarily large trees.

## Relationship to prior work
Iršič, Klavžar, Rus and Tuite developed general-position polynomials, including combs and brooms, in *General Position Polynomials* (2024). Rather's 2026 treatment also obtains counting formulas for comb and corona graphs. A published caterpillar all-profile polynomial formula counts all general-position faces, and can determine Euler characteristic, but not the claimed graph collapse, fundamental group, vanishing of higher integral homology, or simple-homotopy type: Euler characteristic alone does not force any of these properties. Previously reported sphere-wedge homotopy classifications for certain two-hub general-position complexes address special shapes, not a theorem for arbitrary caterpillars. The explicit freeness and topology across all leaf profiles are the present claim.

## Limitations
Only caterpillar trees are covered; this theorem does not hold as stated for all trees with multiple branching levels or for non-tree graphs. The general-position complex uses vertex geodesics, not the different edge-general-position notion. The classification is nonequivariant: a specific automorphism representation on first homology is not claimed. The literature comparison is limited to identified accessible publications and public research records; independent external review and absolute priority certification have not occurred.
