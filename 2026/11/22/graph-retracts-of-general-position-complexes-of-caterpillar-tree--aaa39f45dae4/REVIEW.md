# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The face classification is exhaustive, by unique tree geodesics. All three collapse phases use explicit anchor-toggle pairs in decreasing face size. The lower face has only the paired larger coface at its deletion, with other potential cofaces either forbidden by the classification or already removed. The remaining graph is connected and has an explicit count of all four edge classes, including the star boundary case. The rank formula follows from the graph Euler characteristic. A separate geodesic-triple enumerator checked every free-face condition in 23,078 proposed collapses across 373 finite caterpillar profiles. A finite census is not a proof for all caterpillars; the symbolic collapse argument supplies that proof.

## Originality — PASS
The closest identified publication, *Complete general-position polynomials of arbitrary caterpillar trees* (2026-10-18), gives the face enumerator and thus Euler characteristic, but says nothing about homotopy, higher homology or explicit simplicial collapses. Its entire RESULT.md was compared. Iršič et al. (arXiv:2401.05696) and Rather (arXiv:2603.06930) give counting results for general-position sets and special graph classes, not the arbitrary-caterpillar simple-homotopy classification. Similar existing non-tree or two-hub homotopy results do not imply the all-profile theorem. Searches against indexed scientific findings and the primary literature did not identify a covering theorem; unindexed and inaccessible equivalent work remains a real residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This settles the full integral homotopy type and fundamental group of an infinite natural graph family. Existing complete face-count formulas do not establish whether the complex has higher-dimensional homology or torsion. The new theorem supplies an explicit family-uniform collapse, a nontrivial leaf-gap correction and closed fundamental-group rank, thus upgrades enumeration to a structural topological classification.

## Scope and limits
Finite simple connected caterpillar trees on at least three vertices only; ordinary vertex-geodesic general position, nonequivariant topology. No claim of a full classification for arbitrary trees or graph products. Bibliographic priority is assessed from accessible sources rather than certified exhaustively.
