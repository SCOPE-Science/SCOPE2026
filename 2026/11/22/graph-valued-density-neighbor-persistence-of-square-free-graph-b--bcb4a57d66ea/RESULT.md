# Graph-valued density-neighbor persistence of square-free graph blowups

## Finding
Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite simple graph with no four-cycle (triangles and disconnected graphs are allowed). Replace each vertex \(i\) by a nonempty independent fiber \(F_i\) of arbitrary size \(w_i\), and each edge by a complete bipartite link between its fibers. Call the resulting graph \(H[\mathbf w]\). For every integer \(m\ge1\), let \(\mathcal N_m\) be the complex of nonempty subsets of \(V(H[\mathbf w])\) that have at least \(m\) common *open* neighbors, with the empty face included.

Define \(s_j=\sum_{i\sim j}w_i\), and the bipartite weighted-incidence graph \(B_m\) with two kinds of vertices:
\[
\{v_j:s_j\ge m\}\ \sqcup\ \{e_i:w_i\ge m,\ \deg_H(i)>0\}.
\]
Its edges are \(e_iv_j\) precisely when \(i\sim j\) and \(w_i\ge m\). Then, **naturally across the entire decreasing-threshold filtration**, there are homotopy equivalences
\[
|\mathcal N_m(H[\mathbf w])|\simeq |B_m|,
\]
compatible with all inclusions as the threshold \(m\) decreases. In particular, the integral persistence modules are isomorphic; all groups in degrees at least two vanish; first homology is free abelian and every first-homology structure map is a primitive split injection. The full degree-zero barcode is the component-merger barcode of \(B_m\), and the degree-one barcode is the cycle-birth barcode of \(B_m\). Both are computable by ordinary graph algorithms with activation values \(s_j\) for \(v_j\) and \(w_i\) for the star at \(e_i\).

At each threshold, write \(c_m\) for the number of connected components of \(B_m\), and \(a_m=|\{j:s_j\ge m\}|\), \(b_m=|\{i:w_i\ge m,\deg_H(i)>0\}|\), and \(d_m=\sum_{i:w_i\ge m}\deg_H(i)\). For nonempty \(B_m\),
\[
\widetilde\beta_0=c_m-1,\qquad \beta_1=d_m-a_m-b_m+c_m,
\]
with all other nonnegative reduced Betti numbers zero. For empty \(B_m\), both realizations are empty and the conventional reduced degree-minus-one group is \(\mathbb Z\).

The no-four-cycle hypothesis is sharp for this general statement: for \(H=C_4\) and all \(w_i=1\), the ordinary neighborhood complex is two disjoint contractible edges, while \(B_1\) is two disjoint circles.

## Assumptions and scope
A four-cycle means a cyclic sequence of four **distinct** vertices joined by edges; chords do not matter. Equivalently, any two distinct vertices have at most one common neighbor. Isolated base vertices are permitted but contribute no vertices to these positive-threshold complexes. All weights \(w_i\) are strictly positive integers. Whenever a complex has no nonempty faces, its geometric realization is empty, in accordance with the graph model. The filtration is ordered by decreasing positive integer \(m\): \(\mathcal N_{m+1}\subseteq\mathcal N_m\).

The term 'natural' means a zigzag of levelwise homotopy equivalences commuting with threshold inclusions. It does not claim an inclusion of \(B_m\) as a simplicial subcomplex of the original multineighbor complex, nor a canonical direct simplicial collapse from the latter onto \(B_m\).

## Proof
**1. Common-witness structure.** A nonempty face \(\sigma\) meets fibers indexed by \(S\subseteq V\). If \(|S|=1\), say \(S=\{j\}\), the common-neighbor set consists of exactly all fibers \(F_i\) with \(i\sim j\), and has size \(s_j\). If \(|S|\ge2\), a common neighbor must belong to a fiber \(F_i\) whose center \(i\) is adjacent to all vertices in \(S\). The no-four-cycle hypothesis implies that there can be at most one such center, for two centers sharing two members of \(S\) would form a four-cycle. Thus the number of common neighbors is either zero or exactly \(w_i\), for a uniquely determined center \(i\).

**2. An exact fixed-simplex cover.** Let \(V_j=\Delta(F_j)\), activated if \(s_j\ge m\). Let \(E_i=\Delta(\bigcup_{j\sim i}F_j)\), activated if \(w_i\ge m\) and \(\deg(i)>0\). Step 1 shows that the activated simplices cover precisely \(\mathcal N_m\): any face in one fiber lies in its active \(V_j\); any face using at least two fibers lies in its unique active \(E_i\). Conversely, every face of an active \(V_j\) or \(E_i\) has the requisite witnesses. Every nonempty intersection of cover sets is a full simplex, hence contractible. If \(E_i\) is active and \(i\sim j\), then \(s_j\ge w_i\ge m\), so \(V_j\) is active as well.

**3. The nerve and a complete elementary collapse.** Label the nerve vertices by the active \(V_j\) and \(E_i\). Distinct \(V_j\)'s never meet. For any set \(S\) of at least two distinct active \(E\)-vertices, their intersection is either empty or exactly one fiber \(F_j\); the latter is possible only when every center in \(S\) is adjacent to \(j\), and then \(V_j\) is active. The common \(j\) is unique. Every such nerve face \(S\) without a \(V\)-vertex is paired with \(S\cup\{V_j\}\). Process these pairs in decreasing order of \(|S|\). Any larger coface has already been removed, and the only remaining coface of \(S\) is its paired face: uniqueness of \(j\) rules out competing \(V\)-vertices. Thus each pair is a valid elementary free-face collapse. Remaining faces are exactly the active \(V\)- and \(E\)-vertices and the edges \(E_iV_j\) for \(i\sim j\). These form precisely \(B_m\).

**4. Persistence compatibility.** At all thresholds, the generating simplices \(V_j\) and \(E_i\) are fixed sets and merely become active as \(m\) decreases. Hence their covers, intersections, and nerves form nested filtrations under the identity inclusions. Apply the functorial finite good-cover nerve theorem (equivalently, the poset of face–cover incidences and the contractible-fiber form of Quillen's Theorem A) to obtain a *natural zigzag* of levelwise homotopy equivalences from the original complexes to their nerves. At every threshold, the graph \(B_m\) is literally a simplicial subcomplex of the corresponding nerve, and these graph-to-nerve inclusions commute strictly with threshold inclusions. Step 3 shows each graph-to-nerve inclusion is a homotopy equivalence, regardless of the collapse order. This gives the required natural equivalence of filtrations. Consequently all integral persistence modules of \(\mathcal N_m\) and \(B_m\) are isomorphic.

**5. Algebraic consequences.** A graph has no homology above degree one, and its first integral homology is the subgroup of the free edge group consisting of cycles. Under graph inclusions that subgroup embeds integrally as a saturated, hence primitive, sublattice (the quotient of first-cycle groups is torsion-free, since both are kernels of incidence maps and saturation follows from vanishing on the larger edge set). Equivalently, extend a spanning forest of the earlier graph to one of the later graph, obtaining compatible fundamental-cycle bases. The graph identity \(\beta_1=|E|-|V|+c\) gives the displayed formulas; the standard union–find algorithm yields degree-zero births and mergers, and each independent cycle born upon adding edges persists for all later stages. The persistence conclusions then follow from Step 4 over \(\mathbb Z\), without any assumption on homology-field coefficients.

## Verification
The self-contained standard-library program `verify.py` directly enumerates nonempty subsets of the blown-up graph, counts common neighbors against the **actual graph adjacency**, obtains the resulting maximal facets, and computes reduced simplicial homology over \(\mathbb F_2\) by independent boundary-matrix reduction. It separately constructs \(B_m\) from the base graph and computes its graph cycle rank and components. A run of the included script checked **339** connected square-free graph instances and **1,481** threshold stages, including heterogeneous fiber weights, the Petersen graph, and the Heawood graph. It found no disagreement in reduced Betti numbers in degrees zero, one, or two; it independently confirms that the excluded square base fails. This is a finite sanity check, not a replacement for the universal integral nerve and collapse proof. It does not independently compute the persistence maps; these are proved by the natural-cover argument.

As one heterogeneous illustration, take two triangles sharing one vertex \(0\), with edge sets \(01,12,20,03,34,40\), and fiber weights \((3,2,2,1,1)\). Then \(\beta_1(B_m)\) is zero for \(m\ge3\), one for \(m=2\), and three for \(m=1\). This is genuine multi-stage cycle birth with no later death; the model is not just the ordinary \(m=1\) neighborhood classification.

## Relationship to prior work
Matysiak and Spaliński (arXiv:2302.09017, first public February 17, 2023, primary MSC 55N31) introduce the multineighbor filtration, prove asymptotic random-graph homology bounds, and discuss density-parameter persistence, but do not classify arbitrary heterogeneous graph blowups. Babson and Spaliński (arXiv:2309.05149) study random-graph multineighbor distributions and related probabilistic thresholds, not a deterministic graph-valued persistence model. Classical and published square-free graph neighborhood results cover \(m=1\) in related settings: the known open-neighborhood circle-wedge description, explicit bipartite graph spines, and a prism collapse. They do not determine \(m\ge2\) for arbitrary fiber weights, or the associated inclusion maps. In particular, blown-up graphs generally **contain** four-cycles and cannot themselves be inserted into a theorem requiring a four-cycle-free graph. The novelty assessed here is the entire *weighted density filtration* and its natural integral persistence model, not the already-known ordinary neighborhood graph-like consequence.

## Limitations
The no-four-cycle assumption is necessary for the unique-witness method and cannot be removed wholesale. We do not classify multineighbor filtrations for arbitrary base graphs containing overlapping common-neighbor pairs, nor claim a direct simplicial collapse of the original complexes onto the model. The verified finite tests establish only mod-two ranks, whereas the general integral and persistence conclusions rest on the complete proof above. Bibliographic coverage is limited to inspected indexed and accessible published sources; equivalent unindexed results remain possible.
