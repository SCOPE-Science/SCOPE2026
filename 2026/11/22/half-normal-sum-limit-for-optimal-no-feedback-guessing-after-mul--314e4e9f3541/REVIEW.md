# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The fixed-shuffle optimal guessing rule is quoted with its exact range of validity from Krityakierne–Aek Thanatipanonda, arXiv:2611.04339v1, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 4.1. GSR composition gives the independent \(2^k\)-packet label construction. On an exponentially likely event, guesses on the top and bottom halves are confined to the first and last packets respectively. Occurrence ranks then reduce the score, up to two boundary terms, to the sum of independent occupation counts of a centered aperiodic geometric-increment random walk. The local Fourier estimate and an explicit all-moment Dirichlet-simplex argument give the half-normal limit and moment convergence. Algebraic normal rotation gives the squared-error-function CDF, while half-normal second moments give the sharp variance coefficient. `CHECKER.py` independently checks finite mixture probabilities and the occupation identity; finite checks are not a proof of the limit.

## Originality
PASS against the examined evidence, with remaining historical-priority risk. The motivating 2026 paper states in Section 6 that the score distribution for fixed \(k\ge2\) remains a question, although its expectation and optimal strategy have been established. The 2024 Kuba–Panholzer limit law covers only one riffle. The 2025 Krityakierne–Aek Thanatipanonda framework gives one-position marginals, not the dependence needed to obtain a score law. The closest published separate result on fixed-shuffle guessing requires every label to be guessed exactly once and provides only a first-order optimal expected value; this does not imply the repeated-guess score distribution. A search of previously accessible research claims found no equivalent. The general local-time method is standard, and its applicability alone is not claimed original. A pre-existing but unlocated equivalent first-order multishuffle limit remains possible.

## Value
PASS. The current fixed-shuffle optimal-strategy literature explicitly identifies higher moments and the full score distribution as a missing dependence-level problem. The theorem supplies the entire limit distribution (not just the known mean), convergence of all fixed integer moments, a sharp previously unspecified variance constant, and a probabilistic explanation for why independent half-normal contributions arise. The dependence structure of the no-feedback score is statistically important, and the result is uniform across all fixed shuffle counts \(k\ge2\) without requiring a new special finite-deck optimizer.

## Limits and closest literature
The proof applies to standard independent GSR riffles with fixed \(k\); it does not claim the full finite-\(n\) score distribution, growing-\(k\) universality, or an independent audit. Closest sources: arXiv:2611.04339v1 (fixed-shuffle strategy and mean; explicitly open score distribution), Kuba–Panholzer (2024, one shuffle), AIMS Mathematics 10 (2025, marginal generating functions), and the published fixed-shuffle unique-label-matching expectation theorem. Literature searches do not establish universal priority.
