# Review of high-connectivity inversion-graph tails

**Correctness (PASS).** The graph-theoretic reduction to connected induced four-vertex subgraphs is bidirectional. Complementary disconnectedness produces exactly the two forbidden complete bipartite subgraphs. Maximum degree two plus the well-established no-induced-long-cycle property of inversion graphs forces path/triangle components. The independent direct-sum decomposition counts path permutations (two for each length at least three), singleton, edge, and triangle cases correctly, with the weight equal to the original noninversion count. The matching-only subcase supplies the Fibonacci tail. Exhaustive tests through order eight reproduce both the all-coefficient bivariate counts and the published table where available. Main limit: the argument does not classify smaller connectivity layers.

**Originality (PASS, historical priority provisional).** The closest direct source, Verkama (arXiv:2611.17237v1, §6/Table 1), supplies the full statistic and its seven-row data table, but no all-order high-tail formula or path–triangle characterization. Earlier increasing-oscillation sources provide precisely the path-component building blocks, not the connectivity reduction. Crucially, OEIS A180965 already contains the scalar sequence and its rational generating function (with shifted index), so these are NOT claimed as new in isolation. The specific identification as an inversion-graph connectivity tail, complete structural equivalence, exact highest-three coefficients, and weighted refinement were not covered by inspected sources or retrieved records. Relevant possible unidentified equivalent permutation classes remain a genuine priority risk; unsuccessful searches do not certify global novelty.

**Value (PASS).** The 2026 motivating source introduces connectivity distribution as a natural permutation statistic and gives only finite rows. Determining its highest three degrees uniformly is a motivated structural question, not an arbitrarily selected tiny example. The path–triangle characterization also gives all-order enumeration by noninversion count; this extra information is not recoverable from the matching univariate tatami sequence. A complete high-connectivity tail solves a natural extremal part of the distribution without implying the full distribution.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Verkama, *Permutation patterns and higher connectivity in inversion graphs*, arXiv:2611.17237v1; Albert–Brignall–Ruškuc–Vatter, *Rationality For Subclasses of 321-Avoiding Permutations*, §9; Albert–Jelínek–Opler, *Two examples of Wilf-collapse*, p. 8; OEIS A180965. Enumeration of the scalar count was known, and priority of the stronger identification and refined formula is provisional. The finite checker is not an infinite proof. An independent mathematical audit has not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
