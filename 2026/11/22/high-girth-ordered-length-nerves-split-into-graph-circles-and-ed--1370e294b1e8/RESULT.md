# High-girth ordered length nerves split into graph circles and edge spheres

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite connected simple unweighted graph containing a cycle, with \(v\) vertices, \(e\) edges, girth \(g(G)\), and cycle rank \(c=e-v+1\). For every integer \(L\ge3\) satisfying \(2L<g(G)\), let \(N_L(G)\) be its **ordered enriched nerve at inclusive total-length cutoff** \(L\), regarded as a simplicial set. Then

\[
|N_L(G)|\simeq \bigvee^{c} S^1\ \vee\ \bigvee^{e} S^L.
\]

In particular, \(\pi_1(|N_L(G)|)\cong F_c\), its reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^{c}\) in degree one and \(\mathbb Z^{e}\) in degree \(L\), and it vanishes in every other degree. Its universal cover has a primitive integral top-homology basis indexed by the edges of the universal covering tree, hence a free \(\mathbb Z[F_c]\)-module of rank \(e\).

The top integral homology downstairs has an explicit natural \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-lattice. Choose an orientation \(u\to v\) of each edge and write \(a_{uv}^{(L)}=(u,v,u,v,\ldots)\) and \(a_{vu}^{(L)}=(v,u,v,u,\ldots)\) for the two alternating \(L\)-simplices, each with \(L+1\) entries. The cycles

\[
 z_{\{u,v\},L}=a_{uv}^{(L)}+(-1)^{L+1}a_{vu}^{(L)}
\]

form a primitive basis of \(H_L(|N_L(G)|;\mathbb Z)\). Every graph automorphism permutes these edge classes, with an additional sign on a reversed edge exactly when \(L\) is even. For odd \(L\) the action is the ordinary permutation lattice on unoriented edges.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple, connected, finite and undirected, every edge has unit length, and the girth is the length of its shortest simple cycle. The theorem is asserted for graphs containing at least one cycle, and integer \(L\ge3\) satisfying \(g(G)\ge2L+1\). Tree calculations are used as a proof ingredient, not asserted as new findings. The ordered nerve is **not** an unordered diameter-controlled Vietoris–Rips or Čech complex.

Precisely, a \(q\)-simplex is an ordered tuple \((x_0,\ldots,x_q)\) with \(\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}d_G(x_j,x_{j+1})\le L\). Face maps delete entries and degeneracy maps repeat entries. In the normalized integral cellular complex, tuples with adjacent equal entries are degenerate, and deleting an entry that creates adjacent equality contributes zero. Every nondegenerate simplex has dimension at most \(L\). The realization is a finite CW complex.

## Proof
**1. An integral tree lemma, reconstructed from exact-length magnitude homology.** Let \(T\) be a finite tree with \(m\) edges, with the same nerve definition. Filter its normalized chains by integer total length. The associated graded chain complex at length \(p\) is the usual exact-length magnitude chain complex. For trees the published magnitude calculation states that for \(p\ge1\) its homology is concentrated in homological degree \(p\), freely generated by the \(2m\) alternating directed walks along individual edges; at length zero it consists of one degree-zero generator per vertex. The distinction between this **exact-length** fact and the **cumulative** nerve is essential.

The first length-decreasing differential is explicit. For an oriented edge write \(a_p=(u,v,u,\ldots)\) and \(b_p=(v,u,v,\ldots)\), with \(p+1\) entries. For every \(p\ge2\), deletion of the initial and final endpoints gives, on the diagonal magnitude generators,

\[
 d_1(a_p)=b_{p-1}+(-1)^p a_{p-1},\qquad
 d_1(b_p)=a_{p-1}+(-1)^p b_{p-1}.
\]

The intervening interior faces have consecutive repeated entries and vanish in normalized chains. On each edge the resulting matrix has rank one and primitive image. At \(p=1\), the differential is the oriented graph incidence map from two arcs per edge to vertices; for a tree it has rank \(m\) and primitive image. Consequently every intermediate length level has zero homology on the next page, the top length \(p=L\) has one primitive kernel generator \(a_L+(-1)^{L+1}b_L\) per edge, and the bottom level leaves one connected-component generator. All groups are free. Every higher spectral-sequence differential vanishes because the exact-length magnitude homology was supported only on its degree-equals-length diagonal. Thus \(\widetilde H_L(|N_L(T)|;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^m\) and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish.

For \(L\ge2\), the nerve of a tree is simply connected: a length-at-most-\(L\) directed edge corresponding to a nonadjacent vertex pair is homotopic, through geodesic two-simplices, to its uniquely determined unit-edge path; the backtracking two-simplices \((u,v,u)\) identify opposite directed arcs as inverse paths. Every loop therefore reduces to a loop in the tree itself. The nerve is \(L\)-dimensional and simply connected with only free top homology, so Hurewicz, followed by Whitehead's theorem, produces the homotopy equivalence \(|N_L(T)|\simeq\bigvee^m S^L\). The alternating top cycles are the explicit primitive basis. This argument independently establishes the needed cumulative tree lemma from the published exact-length theorem, not from finite numerical observations.

The same conclusion for a locally finite infinite tree follows by directed union over finite subtrees. Every finite chain and every compact loop is contained in a finite-subtree nerve. The integral top homology is therefore the direct sum \(\mathbb Z[E(T)]\), and the nerve remains simply connected. In particular, it applies to the universal covering tree of a finite graph.

**2. A short-length lifting lemma.** Write \(p:\widetilde G\to G\) for the ordinary graph universal cover, with deck group \(\Gamma=\pi_1(G)\cong F_c\). Since \(g(G)>2L\), any pair of geodesics between vertices at distance at most \(L\) is identical: two different choices would contain a simple cycle of length at most \(2L\). More generally, a walk of length at most \(L\) between two vertices and their shortest geodesic cannot lift to different endpoint lifts; if they did, their concatenation would contain a noncontractible reduced closed walk, hence a simple cycle of length at most \(2L\).

A nondegenerate tuple in \(N_L(G)\) thus lifts uniquely once the lift of its first vertex is fixed: lift each consecutive unique shortest geodesic. Its concatenated lift has the same total length, so defines a simplex in \(N_L(\widetilde G)\). Deleting any vertex agrees with lifting the shortened tuple by the preceding path-comparison argument. Degeneracies are also preserved. Conversely every simplex upstairs projects to one downstairs. These observations establish an isomorphism of simplicial sets

\[
 N_L(\widetilde G)/\Gamma\ \cong\ N_L(G).
\]

The deck action is free on the geometric realization because a nonidentity deck transformation fixes no vertex and therefore no ordered nondegenerate simplex. By the infinite-tree lemma, \(|N_L(\widetilde G)|\) is simply connected, so this is the **actual universal covering space** of \(|N_L(G)|\). It has reduced integral homology only in degree \(L\), where it is \(\mathbb Z[E(\widetilde G)]\cong\mathbb Z[\Gamma]^e\), generated by lifted alternating edge cycles.

**3. Upgrade from the universal-cover calculation to integral homotopy.** Choose one of the two directed length-one arcs for every undirected edge of \(G\). These arcs form a graph-shaped one-dimensional subcomplex \(G\hookrightarrow|N_L(G)|\), inducing an isomorphism of fundamental groups by the covering identification. For each edge \(e\), its two-vertex ordered nerve at cutoff \(L\) is \(S^L\) up to homotopy, with generator \(z_{e,L}\). After changing basepoint along paths in the graph, these give a map

\[
 f:\;|G|\vee\bigvee^{e} S^L\longrightarrow |N_L(G)|.
\]

The induced map on \(\pi_1\) is an isomorphism \(F_c\to F_c\). On universal covers, it maps the \(\Gamma\)-translates of each sphere generator bijectively onto the primitive edge-cycle basis \(\mathbb Z[E(\widetilde G)]\). Hence it is an integral homology isomorphism on universal covers in every degree. The simply connected Whitehead theorem applied upstairs, then descended through the \(\pi_1\)-isomorphism, makes \(f\) a homotopy equivalence. As a graph is a wedge of \(c\) circles, the displayed homotopy classification follows.

**4. Natural symmetry.** The chain cycle \(z_{e,L}\) is closed already in the full normalized chain complex, because the two endpoint boundaries cancel and all intermediate deletion faces are degenerate. Swapping its edge endpoints acts by \((-1)^{L+1}\); an automorphism mapping one oriented edge to another transports the cycles accordingly. Their integral primitivity follows from the universal-cover basis and the preceding equivalence, not merely from rational rank calculations.

## Verification
The file `checks/length_three_checker.py` (the filename retains the initial scale used in the computational checks) is a standalone standard-library Python verifier for arbitrary integer cutoff values in its sample suite. It enumerates **all** nondegenerate ordered simplices with total length at most the specified cutoff, verifies \(\partial^2=0\) with integer coefficients, checks the alternating top-degree edge cycles integrally, and independently computes full Betti vectors over the primes \(2\) and \(1000003\). Running it prints the recorded `checks/check_output.txt` output and ends with `ALL FINITE CHECKS PASSED`.

The verified high-girth cases include cycles on seven, eight and nine vertices at cutoff three, a figure-eight of two seven-cycles at cutoff three, the nine-cycle at cutoff four, and the eleven-cycle at cutoff five. The excluded boundary cases \(C_6\) at cutoff three, \(C_8\) at cutoff four and \(C_{10}\) at cutoff five have nonzero intermediate homology over both checked fields; the girth hypothesis cannot simply be omitted. The code is a finite corroboration, not an infinite-family proof. The general claim rests on the explicit integral spectral differential, the lifting lemma and the Whitehead comparison.

## Relationship to prior work
Livernet, Roff and Whitehouse, *Homotopy theories via the magnitude-path spectral sequence* (first public 8 June 2026), develop the filtered reachability complex and its magnitude-path spectral sequence. Their inspected introductory and spectral-sequence sections supply a relevant formal framework but do not formulate the graph-covering identification or the present high-girth homotopy classification. Melodia's August 2026 preprint explicitly connects magnitude homology to the associated graded of the length filtration, but its full manuscript could not be accessed in this review; the inspection of its abstract alone cannot establish whole-document noncoverage. Tajima's 2024 thesis, relying on earlier magnitude-homotopy work, establishes the exact-length magnitude calculation for trees, a critical **premise**, but its quotient at one exact length is not the cumulative ordered nerve of a graph with cycles.

Previously available descriptions for **trees at every cutoff** and for **quadrilateral-free graphs at cutoff two** are strictly narrower than the present statement for cyclic graphs and arbitrary higher cutoffs satisfying \(2L<g(G)\). Results about ordinary unordered graph Vietoris–Rips complexes and high-girth robust-clique complexes use different simplex conventions and do not imply the ordered total-length theorem. Statement-level searches of the mathematical literature and research records found no dominating classification; absence from these searches is not a proof of publication priority.

## Limitations
The proof requires a unit-edge, finite simple connected graph with a cycle and a strict bound \(2L<g(G)\), with integer \(L\ge3\). It does not classify the borderline or lower-girth cases, and does not determine the later filtration beyond the allowed girth window, cup products, or geometric homotopy types of symmetry quotients. Although all integral homology and the homotopy type are established internally, no independent mathematical audit has been performed. The exact-length magnitude premise is supported by published work; completeness of publication-priority searches is limited, particularly where a related full manuscript was inaccessible.
