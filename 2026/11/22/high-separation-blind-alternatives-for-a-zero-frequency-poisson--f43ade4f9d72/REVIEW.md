# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The four probabilities are positive and sum to one. Their zero mass, mean and variance exactly match those of a Poisson law, as shown algebraically and checked across 96 finite parameter pairs. This forces the same bivariate covariance and, by the i.i.d. central limit theorem and delta method, the same standard-normal limit of the exact published statistic. A Poisson local limit theorem gives the two outer overlap terms; a one-variable derivative sign argument proves the unique reciprocal optimizer. The finite checker does not replace the analytic infinite-limit arguments.

Neither the main accessible source, its tables and discussion, nor the searched published research records supplied the same quantitative statement. Generic moment-matching and normal local limits underlie parts of the argument; uninspected historical work remains a priority risk. The Poisson conditional Kolmogorov–Smirnov test concerns a different statistic; its abstract alone was available for this comparison.

## Value

**PASS.** A practical warning that a zero-frequency condition is restrictive is converted into a rigorous, quantitative extreme-separation example for the very test it constrains. Exact variance matching rules out the common escape that studentization will reveal the alternative. Positive mass at every count rules out support mismatch as a trivial explanation. The optimized local-overlap constant quantifies the separation in a structurally motivated low-support moment-matched family. This does not claim a new universal test or improved critical values.
