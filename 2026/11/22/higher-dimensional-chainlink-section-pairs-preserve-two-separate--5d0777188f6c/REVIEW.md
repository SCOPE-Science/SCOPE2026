# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The new theorem's domain is \(s\ge4\), \(a_i>0\), \(2\ell\le\min a_i\), and \(0<t<\sum a_i\). For integer data, the known equal Ehrhart quasipolynomials imply equality of lattice-normalized facet-area sums; the two facet types have exact and **different** lattice covolumes \(\sqrt{s-1}\) and \(\sqrt{2s}\). The known complementary volume identity extends to real parameters by rational scaling and continuity; differentiating in \(\ell\) gives the link-facet sum through the moving-support-hyperplane formula, so subtracting recovers the box sum. Boundary parameter cases follow by one-sided continuity. The rational checker independently verifies both sums in six four-coordinate cases and the nonisomorphic witness. Finite checks do not certify the general statement; the analytic argument does.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority
The 2022 work [1] proves general complementary Ehrhart and volume equalities but does not state the separated **Euclidean boundary-area** identity for \(s\ge4\). The 2026 public record [2] already establishes exactly the analogous result in \(s=3\), which is credited and excluded. The dimensional extension uses a lattice-facet normalization and a shape derivative, not a rephrasing of the planar explicit edge-length formula. Search comparisons were made by statement and implication; the key residual risk is that the all-dimensional boundary identity may have been derived elsewhere or appear in unavailable full text. The 2022 theorem is a powerful input, so the novelty is solely the Euclidean two-class consequence and nonisomorphic higher-dimensional witness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result provides an infinite-dimensional family of *Ehrhart-equivalent, generally nonisomorphic* polytope sections whose ordinary Euclidean boundary areas agree—something not implied by Ehrhart-equivalence in general. The equality separates two geometrically different facet-orbit classes, and an exact four-coordinate witness has different vertex counts despite the same two surface contributions. This is a natural structural boundary-invariant theorem, not a single numerical recomputation.
