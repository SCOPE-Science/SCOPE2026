# Scientific comparison

The witness in `RESULT.md` separates the face-lattice types of complementary sections, not just their metrics. The planar impossibility is an edge-count argument and covers exceptional heights as well as generic ones. The reported three-dimensional face vectors and metric totals are exactly reproducible using `check_witness.py`.

The complementary Ehrhart and volume theorem is due to Kantarcı Oğuz, Özel and Ravichandran, arXiv:2211.08382v1, Theorem 4.8. The separate metric-area equations follow from that theorem by first variation. Earlier examples of distinct planar metrics include Figure 2 of that paper and the noncongruent complementary hexagons in *Complementary three-chainlink sections preserve Euclidean perimeter and separate facet-length totals*. These are not examples of distinct polygon face lattices. The result here focuses on the first possible dimension of a genuine face-lattice distinction and the explicit witness; no universal priority claim is made.
