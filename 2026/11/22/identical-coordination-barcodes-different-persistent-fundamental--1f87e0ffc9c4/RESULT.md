# Identical coordination barcodes, different persistent fundamental groups on toroidal and Klein-bottle square grids

## Finding
For every integer \(L\ge 5\), there are explicit connected four-regular graphs \(T_L\) and \(K_L\), each with \(L^2\) vertices and \(2L^2\) edges, for which the complete mod-two *coordination-scale* persistence barcodes coincide, while their group-valued persistence systems differ at every scale \(4\le k<L\).

For the group \(A_k(G)=\pi_1(Y_k(G))\), where \(Y_k(G)\) is obtained by attaching a two-cell along every simple cycle of length at most \(k\), we have
\[
A_k(T_L)\cong
\begin{cases}
F_{L^2+1}, & 2\le k\le3,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&4\le k<L,\\
1,&k\ge L,
\end{cases}
\qquad
A_k(K_L)\cong
\begin{cases}
F_{L^2+1},&2\le k\le3,\\
\langle a,b\mid bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle,&4\le k<L,\\
1,&k\ge L.
\end{cases}
\]
The displayed groups occur with the natural surjective bonding homomorphisms induced by increasingly many relations. On the middle interval the maps are isomorphisms (indeed, they are quotient isomorphisms after identifying with the fundamental groups of the square-filled surfaces). At \(k=L\), the two standard generators are killed. Thus the groups are nonisomorphic throughout the middle interval, even though the entire mod-two persistence modules are isomorphic:
\[
\mathcal D(T_L)=\mathcal D(K_L)
=\big\{\underbrace{4,\ldots,4}_{L^2-1\text{ times}},L,L\big\}.
\]
Equivalently, both modules have \(L^2-1\) intervals \([2,4)\) and two intervals \([2,L)\). In particular, \(A_4(T_L)\) is abelian, but \(A_4(K_L)\) is nonabelian and has abelianization \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and have unit-length edges. Fix an integer \(L\ge5\). The common vertex set is \(\{(x,y):x,y\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\}\). Every graph has horizontal edges \((x,y)(x+1\bmod L,y)\) and vertical edges \((x,y)(x,y+1)\) for \(0\le y<L-1\). The final vertical seam is \((x,L-1)(x,0)\) in \(T_L\), and \((x,L-1)(-x\bmod L,0)\) in \(K_L\). These rules count each undirected edge once. In the formula \(F_r\) is the free group on \(r\) generators; \(\mathcal D\) is the multiset of death lengths in the mod-two first-homology persistence system \(H_1(Y_k(G);\mathbb F_2)\), indexed by integers \(k\ge 2\). This is the relation-length, not distance-threshold, filtration. The statement does not assert that the underlying two graphs are isomorphic.

## Proof
**1. Common covering-grid geometry and short cycles.** Take the square tiling of \(\mathbb R^2\) with vertices \(\mathbb Z^2\). Quotient its graph by the group generated by the horizontal translation \(h(x,y)=(x+L,y)\) and either \(v_+(x,y)=(x,y+L)\) or \(v_-(x,y)=(-x,y+L)\). The quotient graphs are exactly \(T_L\) and \(K_L\) with the edge descriptions above. These groups act freely, properly discontinuously, and preserve the unit-square cellulation. The two quotients of the whole plane are, respectively, a torus and a Klein bottle.

Every nonidentity deck transformation takes a lattice vertex to another lattice vertex at Manhattan distance at least \(L\). Indeed, a transformation with nonzero vertical exponent changes its \(y\)-coordinate by a nonzero multiple of \(L\); one with zero vertical exponent is a nontrivial horizontal translation by a multiple of \(L\). Any closed graph walk of length less than \(L\) therefore lifts to a closed grid walk. In particular, its image in the quotient surface is contractible. For \(L\ge5\), all triangles are absent and every simple four-cycle lifts to a simple four-cycle in the planar square grid. The latter must bound one unit square. Conversely every quotient unit square is an embedded four-cycle. There are exactly \(L^2\) such facial squares.

**2. The first two group stages.** Each graph is connected, has \(L^2\) vertices and \(2L^2\) edges, and has no triangles. Thus its graph fundamental group is free of rank \(2L^2-L^2+1=L^2+1\), and \(A_2=A_3\) has this rank. At \(k=4\), filling all squares and no other cycles produces precisely the cellulated torus or Klein bottle. The groups have presentations
\[
\pi_1(T^2)=\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle,
\quad \pi_1(\mathrm{Kl})=\langle a,b\mid bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle.
\]
By step 1 every simple cycle of length \(4<k<L\) is nullhomotopic in the filled surface. Attaching further disks along nullhomotopic cycles cannot change its fundamental group. Thus the groups and bonding maps are unchanged through \(k=L-1\).

**3. Final group stage.** A horizontal row loop has \(L\) edges and represents \(h\). The vertical column at \(x=0\) has \(L\) edges and represents \(v_+\) or \(v_-\), because the seam fixes \(x=0\). These two loops generate each surface group. Their inclusion among the relations at \(k=L\) kills every generator, making \(A_L\) trivial; all later groups remain trivial. The Klein-bottle group is not abelian: its normal infinite cyclic subgroup \(\langle a\rangle\) is inverted by \(b\). Abelianization forces \(2a=0\), giving \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). Thus the middle groups differ from the torus's \(\mathbb Z^2\).

**4. Entire mod-two barcode.** The binary cycle space of either graph has dimension \(L^2+1\). At scale four the \(L^2\) square-boundary vectors have rank exactly \(L^2-1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\): their sum vanishes, and if a subset sums to zero, every edge forces the two adjacent faces to occur together, so connectedness of the dual square grid forces that subset to be empty or all faces. Therefore the quotient after length-four relations has dimension two. Every cycle of length strictly less than \(L\) is contractible in the cellulated surface by step 1, hence is homologous to a binary sum of squares; no subsequent rank drop occurs before \(L\). At length \(L\), the horizontal and central vertical cycles represent independent generators of \(H_1(T^2;\mathbb F_2)\) and \(H_1(\mathrm{Kl};\mathbb F_2)\), both two-dimensional. Consequently these relations kill both surviving classes. All maps in this persistence system are quotients, so the rank-drop multiplicities give exactly \(L^2-1\) deaths at length four and two at length \(L\). This proves equality of the complete barcodes, not just of their dimension at length four. \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `checker.py` constructs both graphs using the seam rules and checks every three- and four-cycle by adjacency enumeration, the exact set of square faces, the binary rank \(L^2-1\) of their boundaries, and independence of the horizontal and fixed-column winding cycles. It passes for all \(L=5,6,7,8,9\). For \(L=5,6,7\), it additionally enumerates all simple cycles of length less than \(L\) and checks that their mod-two vectors lie in the facial span (25, 36, and 147 cycles for each respective graph). Run `python checker.py` with Python 3. No external data or packages are required. These finite computations do not prove the unrestricted result; the covering-space and cellular arguments above do.

## Relationship to prior work
Rajković, *Persistent A-homotopy of complex networks*, arXiv:2611.09967v1 (first public 2026-10-29), defines the coordination-scale group system, shows its binary barcode is the minimum-cycle-basis length multiset, and proves \(A_4(C_L\square C_L)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) for \(L\ge5\) (Propositions 3 and 6). Accordingly neither the definition nor the torus at scale four is claimed as new. The construction here additionally classifies the entire group systems for a Klein-bottle/toroidal pair and proves that **every** binary death length, not merely the scale-four Betti number, agrees. Horton and Berger (2005) study the binary-versus-rational difference in minimum cycle bases using a Klein-bottle triangulation; they do not identify this square-grid pair's common complete binary spectrum or the different group-valued coordination filtrations. Standard Klein-bottle covering descriptions are classical background. An equivalent result may exist outside indexed accessible literature.

## Limitations
The theorem treats the two explicit families for \(L\ge5\), unit edge-length coordination scale, and mod-two first-homology barcodes. It does not claim equal integral persistence, equal homotopy types, equal graph isomorphism types, or an optimal smallest counterexample. The integral abelianizations already distinguish the middle groups; the claim is a specifically binary loss of information. The proof uses topological covering spaces; the checker examines only finite sizes. Publication priority is not independently certified.
