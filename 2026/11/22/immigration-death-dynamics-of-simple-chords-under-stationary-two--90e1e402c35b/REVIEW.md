# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every present matching edge is removed with conditional probability \(2/n\), and every absent potential edge is created with conditional probability \(1/[n(n-1)]\). This proves the exact eigenfunction and covariance identity; the finite-size stationary moments follow from uniform perfect-matching probabilities. The stationary simple-chord Poisson limit follows from all fixed factorial moments. The process limit is justified by stationary bounds of order \(n^{-2}\) for two or more simultaneous count events, accelerated generator convergence in mean, compact containment, Aldous tightness and the unique immigration–death martingale problem. Exact rational enumeration of all matchings for sizes 2–6 corroborates the finite claims. Finite checks are not used as proof of infinite convergence.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations

The earliest selected source is Flajolet–Noy's 4 March 2000 chord-diagram analysis. Acan's 2015 work addresses marginal short-chord Poisson statistics; two published calculations give sharp static Poisson error and joint simple-chord/crossing enumeration. These overlap the stationary marginal and moment ingredients, which are credited as known. The 2021 Olesker-Taylor paper investigates the broader perfect-matching rewiring chain and its mixing time, with an additional lazy holding option; its results do not give the marked cyclic-adjacency count path limit, exact finite lag covariance or two-time void probability. Actual published-finding corpus semantic searches and own historical claim comparisons found no covering law. A more general existing local-observable theorem, or an unlocated prior temporal computation, remains a possible implication/priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The count of adjacent matching chords is a natural local defect observable with a known Poisson equilibrium law; understanding its *temporal persistence* under the standard local rewiring operation is genuinely different from static counting. The result supplies a complete limiting path law, explicit persistence times and simultaneous absence probability, and identifies a nontrivial exact eigenfunction of the full matching chain. Its purpose is monitoring local adjacency defects under random pairing changes, not a routine restatement of a stationary Poisson marginal.
