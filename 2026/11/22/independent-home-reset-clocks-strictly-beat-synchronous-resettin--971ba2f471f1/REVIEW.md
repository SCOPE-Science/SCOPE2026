# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every noncolliding configuration on a complete graph has the same ordinary collision hazard \(2/(n-1)\); a shared reset to distinct homes cannot kill. Under individual resets, the additional nonnegative state-dependent killing hazard and strictly positive transient probability of a capturable state prove strict survival ordering for every positive time. The exact five-orbit killed-generator backward equations produce the claimed rational mean, and differentiation gives its unique global optimum. Independent exact-rational full-generator calculations, quotient calculations and symbolic factorization verify the critical algebra. The finite tests do not substitute for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The 2026 Suarez-Jimenez–Riascos–Boyer full-text study and the 2025 Rubio-Gomez–Riascos–Mateos full-text study concern first encounters **at a prescribed node** in discrete-time systems, rather than absorption upon the first collision **anywhere** during continuous-time asynchronous jumps. Ohwa (2015) already proves the unreset exponential meeting law on complete graphs; that is credited, including the immediate shared-clock neutrality consequence. The published odd-cycle jointly resetting Fourier result covers first collision anywhere, but not independent continuous-time home resets or the sharp complete-graph optimizer. The contribution assessed is the strictly ordered all-time collision law in combination with its exact rational gain and universal optimum. Search-based novelty is not a proof of priority; an unindexed more general Markov-killing treatment could imply these formulas.

## Value — PASS

The result exhibits a mathematically sharp dependence of encounter efficiency on whether reset clocks are shared or independent *on a homogeneous network*, at matched total alarm rates and with the same initial home positions. It separates the fixed ordinary collision hazard from reset-created reaction channels, and derives a unique finite optimal rate and the nonzero, dimensionless large-graph gain \(1/(4+2\sqrt2)\). This is a precise, motivated counterpart to prescribed-target reset comparisons, not an arbitrary finite example or a routine normalization test.
