# Independent sets and false twins determine all graph-quandle retraction ranks

## Finding
For a connected finite simple graph \(G\) with \(n\ge2\) vertices, the graph quandle \(Q_G\), defined below, has the following complete image-cardinality enumerator of composition-idempotent quandle endomorphisms:
\[
\mathcal R_G(T)=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha(G)}i_j(G)\,2^j j^{n-j}T^j
+\prod_{C\in V(G)/{\sim}}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{|C|}\binom{|C|}{r}(2r)^{|C|-r}T^{2r}\right).
\]
Here \(i_j(G)\) counts independent vertex subsets of size \(j\), and \(v\sim w\) means equality of open neighborhoods \(N(v)=N(w)\). The first summand counts retractions that collapse every two-point quandle component to one point; the second counts those bijective on each two-point component. The formula gives every possible image cardinality and its exact multiplicity, with no automorphism-orbit division.

For a false-twin-free connected graph, the product reduces to \(T^{2n}\). Since \(\alpha(G)<n\), the retraction spectrum therefore recovers the entire nonempty independent-set polynomial:
\[
i_j(G)=\frac{[T^j]\mathcal R_G(T)}{2^j j^{n-j}}\qquad(1\le j\le n-1).
\]
In particular, for the path \(P_n\) with \(n\ge4\),
\[
\mathcal R_{P_n}(T)=T^{2n}+\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor}\binom{n-j+1}{j}2^j j^{n-j}T^j.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected loopless undirected graph with \(n=|V|\ge2\). Write \(a(v,w)\in\mathbb F_2\) for the adjacency indicator. The graph quandle is the set \(Q_G=V\times\mathbb F_2\), with point symmetry
\[
s_{(v,a)}(w,b)=(w,b+a(v,w)),
\]
where the first input's second coordinate does not affect the operation. Thus \((v,a)\triangleright(w,b)=s_{(v,a)}(w,b)\); the homomorphism identity is \(f(s_x(y))=s_{f(x)}(f(y))\). No linear or additive structure on the quandle is assumed. A retraction is a quandle endomorphism \(f\) with \(f\circ f=f\), and its rank is the number of points in its image. \(\alpha(G)\) denotes the independence number. Open-neighborhood equality is an equivalence relation; its classes are called false-twin classes. In a simple graph two distinct false twins cannot be adjacent.

## Proof
**Step 1: fibers and the complete homomorphism equation.** Since \(G\) is connected and each vertex has at least one neighbor, the orbit of \((v,a)\) under the inner permutations generated by point symmetries is exactly \(\{(v,0),(v,1)\}\). An endomorphism maps each such orbit into one orbit. Consequently every quandle endomorphism has a unique presentation
\[
f(v,a)=(h(v),\epsilon_v a+b_v),
\]
with \(h:V\to V\) and \(\epsilon_v,b_v\in\mathbb F_2\). Substitution into the homomorphism identity yields the necessary and sufficient condition
\[
\epsilon_w a(v,w)=a(h(v),h(w))\quad\text{for all }v,w\in V.
\]
The symmetry of adjacency implies \(\epsilon_v=\epsilon_w\) for each edge \(vw\). Connectivity therefore makes all \(\epsilon_v\) equal to a single \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\).

**Step 2: the collapsed-fiber branch.** If \(\epsilon=0\), the homomorphism equation is \(a(h(v),h(w))=0\) for all vertices, so the image \(I=h(V)\) is independent. Direct substitution shows \(f^2=f\) exactly when \(h^2=h\) and \(b_{h(v)}=b_v\). Such an \(h\) fixes every vertex in its image. Choosing an independent image \(I\) of cardinality \(j\) gives \(j^{n-j}\) choices for \(h\) off \(I\). The bit function \(b\) is any function constant on each fiber of \(h\), thus has \(2^j\) choices. Every resulting \(f\) has exactly \(j\) image points. Summing over \(I\) gives the first summand.

**Step 3: the full-fiber branch.** If \(\epsilon=1\), \(h\) must preserve adjacency *and nonadjacency*:
\[
a(h(v),h(w))=a(v,w)\quad(v,w\in V).
\]
The idempotence conditions are \(h^2=h\) and \(b_{h(v)}=0\). For any \(v,w\), apply adjacency preservation to \((h(v),w)\), and use \(h^2=h\), to obtain
\[
a(h(v),w)=a(h(h(v)),h(w))=a(h(v),h(w))=a(v,w).
\]
Hence \(N(h(v))=N(v)\). Conversely, if \(h\) maps each vertex within its own false-twin class, adjacency of all ordered pairs is preserved. Thus the admissible idempotent \(h\) are precisely the independent retractions of each false-twin class as a finite set.

For a class \(C\) of size \(m\), choose its fixed-point image of size \(r\) in \(\binom mr\) ways and map the remaining \(m-r\) vertices to one of those \(r\) points in \(r^{m-r}\) ways. The bits on the \(r\) fixed vertices must vanish; those on the remaining vertices are arbitrary, giving \(2^{m-r}\) choices. The image of \(f\) contains both points over each of the \(r\) retained vertices, hence has size \(2r\) on the class. The independent class choices multiply, giving the second summand. The branches cannot coincide because the connected graph has an edge, so both summands count disjoint families.

**Step 4: recovery and paths.** If all false-twin classes are singletons, the full-fiber branch has just the identity, with image size \(2n\). Every collapsed-fiber rank is at most \(n-1\). Its \(T^j\)-coefficient therefore recovers \(i_j(G)\). On a path of length \(n\ge4\), the elementary gap-selection bijection gives \(i_j(P_n)=\binom{n-j+1}{j}\), establishing the displayed specialization.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 script `verify_graph_quandle.py` uses the exact operation table. It enumerates **all functions** on the four- and six-element quandles for \(K_2\), \(P_3\), and \(K_3\), checking the quandle-homomorphism equation and composition-idempotence directly, with no use of the claimed normal forms. On eight-element quandles corresponding to \(P_4\), \(C_4\), \(K_4\), the paw graph and \(K_{1,3}\), it independently enumerates all possible fiber maps and tests the complete operation table. Every observed rank polynomial equals the formula. Concrete examples include
\[
\mathcal R_{P_4}(T)=8T+48T^2+T^8,\qquad
\mathcal R_{C_4}(T)=8T+32T^2+16T^4+8T^6+T^8.
\]
Run with `python3 verify_graph_quandle.py`; the success marker is `GRAPH_QUANDLE_RETRACTION_RANK_VERIFIED`. These finite tests are corroborative; the unlimited-graph assertion follows from the proof above, not from testing.

## Relationship to prior work
Furuki and Tamaru introduce this graph-quandle construction and investigate homogeneity and graph symmetry; the graph-to-quandle operation used here is theirs. Saito and Sugawara analyze the broader class of homogeneous quandles with abelian inner automorphism groups. Singh studies topological and finiteness properties of quandle endomorphism monoids. The cited graph-quandle and quandle-endomorphism works do not state the finite-graph retraction image-rank enumerator above in the portions inspected in full. A previously published classification of retractions of semisimple Alexander quandles applies a different, additional hypothesis and does not by itself yield this false-twin and independence-set factorization for graph quandles. This is a statement-and-hypothesis comparison, not a claim of exhaustive historical priority.

## Limitations
Only finite, simple, connected graphs with at least two vertices are covered. The disconnected and isolated-vertex cases can have additional component-wise fiber slopes and are not asserted. The polynomial counts actual retraction functions, not isomorphism classes, graph endomorphisms, or automorphism orbits. It does not describe the full quandle endomorphism monoid or its non-idempotent maps. Equivalences under wider universal-algebra literature remain a residual publication-priority risk; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
