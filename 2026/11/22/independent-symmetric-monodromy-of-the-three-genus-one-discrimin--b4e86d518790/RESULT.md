# Independent symmetric monodromy of the three genus-one discriminants of a triquadratic K3 surface

## Finding

Let \(V=H^0((\mathbb P^1)^3,\mathcal O(2,2,2))\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and let \(K=\mathbb Q(V)\) be the rational function field of the \(27\) independent coefficients of a universal trihomogeneous form \(F\). For each coordinate \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\), let \(\Delta_i\) denote the degree-\(24\) homogeneous discriminant on the corresponding \(\mathbb P^1\) whose roots are the parameters where the curve \(F=0\) in the other two \(\mathbb P^1\) factors is singular. Then each \(\Delta_i\) has \(24\) distinct roots over \(\overline K\), and their three splitting fields are jointly linearly disjoint. More precisely,

\[
\operatorname{Gal}\bigl(K(\operatorname{roots}\Delta_1,\operatorname{roots}\Delta_2,\operatorname{roots}\Delta_3)/K\bigr)
\cong S_{24}\times S_{24}\times S_{24},
\]

with the natural, separate actions on the three sets of \(24\) roots. This is geometric as well as arithmetic monodromy. Equivalently, the three coordinate degree-four del Pezzo fibrations of the generic Fano double cover branched over \(F=0\) have maximally independent singular-fiber parameters. The claim concerns variation of the triquadratic coefficients, not homological monodromy on an individual fiber.

## Assumptions and scope

Work in characteristic zero. The coefficient parameter space is \(P=\mathbb P(V)=\mathbb P^{26}\), which differs from the affine coefficient space only by a trivial scaling and does not change the splitting-field Galois groups. The generic branch divisor \(R=\{F=0\}\subset (\mathbb P^1)^3\) is smooth. Its three coordinate maps \(\rho_i:R\to\mathbb P^1\) are genus-one fibrations. The double cover \(X\to(\mathbb P^1)^3\) branched over \(R\) has degree-four del Pezzo surface fibrations in the same three directions, whose singular parameters coincide with those of \(\rho_i\). The theorem does not assert the same monodromy for any arbitrary special member of the family.

To define \(\Delta_i\) explicitly in a standard affine chart, write the chosen base coordinate as \(t\), and the remaining coordinates as \(u,v\). Regard \(F\) as a quadratic in \(v\):

\[
F=A(t,u)v^2+B(t,u)v+C(t,u).
\]

Write the degree-at-most-four polynomial \(B^2-4AC=a u^4+b u^3+c u^2+d u+e\), where \(a,b,c,d,e\) are polynomials in \(t\). Set \(I=12ae-3bd+c^2\) and \(J=72ace+9bcd-27ad^2-27b^2e-2c^3\). Up to a nonzero scalar, \(\Delta_i(t)=4I^3-J^2\), the discriminant of the binary quartic. This expression incorporates boundary points in both remaining projective coordinates after homogenization.

## Proof

**1. Degree and simple generic fibers.** A smooth triquadratic hypersurface \(R\) is a K3 surface by adjunction. Each coordinate fiber is a curve of bidegree \((2,2)\) and arithmetic genus one. The standard quartic discriminant construction just described produces a binary form of degree at most \(24\) in the base coordinates. The exact finite-field witness in the supplied `CHECK.py` proves that, for at least one integral coefficient vector, all three discriminants have degree exactly \(24\) and have coprime derivatives modulo \(101\). Because these are polynomial nonvanishing conditions on coefficients, there is a nonempty characteristic-zero Zariski open set on which the three discriminants have degree \(24\) and are squarefree. A simple point of the discriminant hypersurface of a \((2,2)\)-curve represents precisely one ordinary node; cusps, multiple nodes in one fiber, and nonreduced fibers lie in its singular locus and produce higher-order discriminant zeros. Hence for general \(F\) each projection has exactly \(24\) distinct one-nodal fibers. The same witness excludes a singularity of the total surface, by the standard local discriminant criterion (alternatively use Bertini and openness).

**2. The singly marked incidence space.** Fix a projection, say \(\rho_1\), and let \((t,u,v)\) be local coordinates on the ambient threefold. A critical point of \(\rho_1|_R\) on a smooth \(R\) satisfies \(F=F_u=F_v=0\) and \(F_t\ne0\). At any fixed point of the ambient product the three displayed equations are independent linear conditions on \(V\): the sections \(1,u,v\) provide independent values and vertical first derivatives in a local trivialization. Thus the incidence variety of a coefficient form and a marked critical point is an open subset of a \(\mathbb P^{23}\)-bundle over \((\mathbb P^1)^3\). It is irreducible of dimension \(26\). Over the squarefree locus it maps finitely étale of degree \(24\) to \(P\), so the single-root monodromy is transitive.

**3. Doubly marked incidence and two-transitivity.** For two distinct candidate critical points \(p,q\) whose first coordinates differ, the six equations \(F(p)=F_u(p)=F_v(p)=F(q)=F_u(q)=F_v(q)=0\) are independent. Indeed, multiply arbitrary vertical first-jet sections by a base-coordinate linear form vanishing at \(t_q\) but not \(t_p\) to prescribe the three jets at \(p\) while annihilating those at \(q\), and interchange \(p,q\) for the other three. The incidence over the open irreducible subset of ordered \((p,q)\) with \(t_p\ne t_q\) is consequently an open subset of a \(\mathbb P^{20}\)-bundle, and is irreducible. Over the generic squarefree locus the \(24\) critical values are pairwise distinct, so this incidence is precisely the degree-\(24\cdot23\) ordered-distinct-root cover. Its irreducibility proves that each marginal permutation group is two-transitive.

**4. An irreducible one-cusp boundary.** For fixed \(p\), impose the preceding three linear critical equations and require the vertical Hessian matrix \(H=\bigl(F_{uu},F_{uv};F_{uv},F_{vv}\bigr)\) to have rank one. The Hessian entries vary independently in the \((2,2,2)\) linear system, so \(\det H=0\) is an irreducible codimension-one condition in the three-dimensional Hessian coefficient space. On its open subset where \(F_t\ne0\) and the cubic term along the one-dimensional Hessian kernel is nonzero, \(\rho_1\) has an \(A_2\) critical point. The resulting cusp incidence is irreducible of dimension \(25\). Its image \(B_1\subset P\) is a divisor, and a general point of that divisor has a unique \(A_2\) point and no other degenerate critical points.

For the latter existence and separation assertions, the checker supplies a second integral coefficient vector whose restriction to the central fiber is exactly \(F(0,u,v)=(u-v)^2+u^2v+5u^2v^2\), and for which \(F_t(0,0,0)=1\). Here \(F_t(0)=1\), the vertical Hessian has rank one with kernel \(u=v\), and its cubic restriction is nonzero. Its three discriminants have degrees \(24,24,24\) and, modulo \(101\), derivative-GCD degrees \((1,0,0)\); the first has an exact double zero at \(t=0\). This proves in characteristic zero that the only repeated root of the first discriminant is the displayed cusp value and that both other discriminants are squarefree. Permuting the three coordinates supplies analogous points on \(B_2,B_3\). Consequently each \(B_i\) contains a smooth point where the other two root covers are unramified. These computations are only nonemptiness and separation certificates, not proofs of a Galois group.

**5. Pure transpositions and the product group.** Near an \(A_2\) point the smooth surface and its projection admit, in analytic coordinates, the familiar deformation model \(t=x^2+y^3+s y\), where \(s\) is a parameter transverse to \(B_i\). The two nearby critical points obey \(3y^2+s=0\), and a loop \(s\mapsto e^{2\pi i}s\) interchanges them. Thus a small loop around a general point of \(B_i\) acts by one transposition on the \(i\)-th set of \(24\) critical values. Because the other two discriminants are squarefree there, they admit holomorphic root sections near that point; the same loop acts identically on both other sets. Write \(G\le S_{24}^3\) for the joint monodromy. Its three projections are two-transitive, and it contains a pure transposition in each separate factor. For each \(i\), conjugates of that pure transposition under \(G\) give every transposition in its factor (by two-transitivity), while remaining trivial in the other factors. Hence \(G\) contains each full factor \(S_{24}\), and therefore \(G=S_{24}^3\). Because geometric monodromy already fills the maximal possible product, the arithmetic Galois group over \(\mathbb Q(V)\) is the same. This proves the theorem.

## Verification

Run `python CHECK.py` with Python 3.8 or later. The script uses only the standard library and evaluates each quartic-discriminant invariant and polynomial GCD exactly modulo \(101\), without floating point arithmetic. For the first fixed-seed coefficient vector it checks three squarefree degree-\(24\) discriminants. For a second vector with imposed \(A_2\) local jet it checks GCD degrees \((1,0,0)\), an exact double root in the first coordinate, and squarefreeness of the other two. These arithmetic statements prove the required generic open and isolated-boundary nonemptiness over characteristic zero. The incidence irreducibility, the analytic \(A_2\) local monodromy and the group-theoretic product argument are proofs, not finite computations.

## Relationship to prior work

Dvorsky, Kaliman and Zaidenberg introduce the three degree-four del Pezzo fibrations of the triquadratic double-cover threefold and prove algebraic Gromov ellipticity, but do not determine the joint Galois group of their three singular-parameter discriminants. Dao's Wehler K3 account states that a general triquadratic K3 has \(24\) nodal fibers for each coordinate projection. Zhan establishes full \(S_{24}\) monodromy for the nodal curves of the universal genus-one K3 Weierstrass family with a section; that does not address the thin triquadratic family or the *joint* three-way product group. An earlier specialized calculation of the moving sixteen-line scheme in the same threefold family gives eight-sheeted component covers and their local branching for each individual fibration, without asserting disjointness across the three coordinate discriminant splitting fields. The present claim is specifically the strong independence \(S_{24}^3\), not merely the three known counts or an individual symmetric group.

## Limitations

The result is generic in all independent triquadratic coefficients over characteristic zero. It does not describe the Galois groups of arbitrary special branch surfaces, positive-characteristic degenerations, or the Galois action on the sixteen lines of each degree-four del Pezzo surface. The finite-field witness does not by itself establish monodromy, and this review is not an independent mathematical audit. Publication priority cannot be deduced from unsuccessful literature searches; equivalent unindexed results may exist.
