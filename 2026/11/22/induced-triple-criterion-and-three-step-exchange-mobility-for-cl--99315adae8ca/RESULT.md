# Induced-triple criterion and three-step exchange mobility for Clebsch power domination

## Finding
Let \(C\) be the sixteen-vertex Clebsch graph and let \(S\subseteq V(C)\) be an initial sensor placement. Apply the standard power-domination process: observe \(N[S]\) initially, then repeatedly observe any currently unobserved vertex that is the unique unobserved neighbor of an observed vertex. A set is **successful** if all vertices are ultimately observed.

**Theorem.** An arbitrary set \(S\) is successful if and only if it contains a three-element subset inducing either three isolated vertices or a path on three vertices. Equivalently, the only unsuccessful placements are subsets of the sixty induced copies of two disjoint edges \(2K_2\). Every unsuccessful three-set extends to **exactly one** of those sixty maximal unsuccessful four-sets. The power-domination number is \(\gamma_P(C)=3\), and the complete successful-placement polynomial is
\[
\mathcal P_C(z)= (1+z)^{16}-1-16z-120z^2-240z^3-60z^4.
\]
Exactly \(320\) successful three-sets attain the minimum, split equally into \(160\) independent triples and \(160\) induced three-vertex paths. All successful placements complete observation at the initial stage or after one further simultaneous forcing wave.

Let \(\mathcal R\) have those \(320\) minimum successful triples as vertices, with an edge whenever one detector is replaced by one unselected vertex while maintaining minimum successful power domination. Then \(\mathcal R\) is connected and \(21\)-regular, has \(3360\) edges, and is **self-centered** with radius and diameter both \(3\). Every vertex has distance-shell profile \((1,21,126,172)\) at distances \(0,1,2,3\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
This is a finite labelled-graph result. For independent reproducibility, identify \(V(C)\) with \(\{0,1,\ldots,15\}\) and join distinct vertices \(u,v\) precisely when their bitwise XOR belongs to \(D=\{1,2,4,8,15\}\). Here the four low generator bits are the standard basis of \(\mathbb F_2^4\), and their XOR is the fifth generator. Equivalently, take the five-dimensional cube and identify opposite vertices. Its sixteen antipodal classes admit unique representatives of Hamming weight at most two, with adjacency when the symmetric difference of representatives has size one or four. The graph is five-regular and triangle-free. The group of all translations and permutations of the five generating directions acts by automorphisms.

The initial closed-neighborhood observation is not counted as a forcing wave. Replacements in \(\mathcal R\) need not follow edges of \(C\), and the three sensors are unlabelled subsets, not ordered triples. Statements on uniqueness and counts apply to the specified sixteen labelled vertices, with no quotient by symmetry.

## Proof
**Step 1: symmetry reduction for three-sets.** Translate a designated vertex to the empty subset of the five-coordinate model. Its five neighbors are singletons, and the ten nonneighbors are two-subsets. Two two-subsets are adjacent precisely when disjoint; a singleton and a two-subset are adjacent precisely when incident. Every three-vertex induced subgraph is therefore exactly one of: independent \(3K_1\), path \(P_3\), or \(K_2\sqcup K_1\). The translation-and-coordinate-permutation automorphism group is transitive on each of these three types: for an induced path choose the midpoint, for an independent triple translate any vertex to the empty set and take two intersecting two-subsets, and for a single-edge triple translate an endpoint to the empty set and take a singleton disjoint from the other two-subset. The representative XOR-labelled triples are respectively \(\{0,3,5\}\), \(\{0,1,2\}\), and \(\{0,1,6\}\).

**Step 2: exact representative observation.** With adjacency differences \(D\) and \(O_0=N[S]\), direct neighborhood unions give:

| \(S\) | Unobserved immediately after \(O_0\) | Applicable first-wave forces | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(\{0,3,5\}\) | \(\{6,9,14\}\) | \(2\to6,\ 8\to9,\ 10\to14\) | all observed |
| \(\{0,1,2\}\) | \(\{7,11,12\}\) | \(5\to7,\ 9\to11,\ 13\to12\) | all observed |
| \(\{0,1,6\}\) | \(\{10,11,12,13\}\) | none | fails |

For the failed representative, each observed vertex has either zero or two unobserved neighbors, hence no forcing step is possible. Automorphism transitivity proves the three-set criterion and shows that successful three-sets finish in one forcing wave. Every set of size zero, one or two extends to an unsuccessful three-set: for an edge take a nonneighbor of both endpoints, and for a nonedge choose a neighbor of exactly one endpoint (available directly in the five-coordinate model). Monotonicity of power domination then makes every smaller set unsuccessful.

**Step 3: classify four-sets and higher.** A four-set with no successful three-subset must have exactly one edge in each of its four triples. Each of its induced edges occurs in exactly two triples, so the set has exactly two induced edges. They cannot share a vertex, for that would create an induced \(P_3\), hence the four-set induces \(2K_2\). Conversely, every induced \(2K_2\) is unsuccessful. To see this, translate one endpoint of an edge to the empty set and its partner to a singleton \(\{i\}\). The other two endpoints are necessarily disjoint two-subsets partitioning the remaining four coordinates. The five-coordinate permutation group therefore makes all such configurations equivalent to the XOR-labelled \(\{0,1,6,7\}\). For this representative, \(O_0=V(C)\setminus\{10,11,12,13\}\), and each observed vertex again has zero or two unobserved neighbors. Thus it is stalled. For a five-set whose every triple would be unsuccessful, every triple would contain exactly one induced edge. Counting edges in triples would give \(3e=\binom53=10\), impossible. Therefore every five-set contains a successful triple; monotonicity covers all larger sets. Since every successful triple has completed by one forcing wave, the same latency bound applies to every successful superset.

**Step 4: exact counts and unique completion.** The graph has \(40\) edges. Each edge has six vertices adjacent to neither endpoint, so exactly \(40\cdot6=240\) triples induce \(K_2\sqcup K_1\). The number of paths is \(16\binom52=160\), and the remaining \(\binom{16}{3}-240-160=160\) triples are independent. To count induced \(2K_2\), choose its first edge (\(40\) options), then one of exactly three compatible disjoint parallel edges, and divide by two: \(40\cdot3/2=60\). Every one-edge triple is contained in at least one such induced matching: in the canonical representative \(\{0,1,6\}\), adjoining \(7\) does so, and symmetry covers all cases. Since each four-vertex matching contains exactly four one-edge triples and \(60\cdot4=240\), this extension is unique. This gives the polynomial and all minimum counts.

**Step 5: finite exchange certificate.** The only required remaining enumeration is the exchange-graph radius, diameter and full distance profile. Build all \(\binom{16}{3}=560\) three-subsets, retain those with zero or two induced edges, and connect two retained subsets exactly when their symmetric difference has cardinality two. The attached `verify_python.py` and `verify_cpp.cpp` independently construct the graph and run breadth-first search from **each of its 320 vertices**. Each finds degree \(21\), \(3360\) undirected edges, and distance shells \((1,21,126,172)\) for every root. The algorithms check all possible retained triples and all possible one-vertex replacements; hence this finite enumeration is exhaustive rather than a sample. Shells summing to \(320\) give connectedness, diameter three and equal eccentricity three at every vertex.

These are finite exact checks; no unbounded graph-family theorem is claimed.

## Relationship to prior work
Haynes, Hedetniemi, Hedetniemi and Henning introduced graph power domination in 2002. Dean, Ilic, Ramirez, Shen and Tian (2011) proved power-domination bounds and exact values for particular ordinary hypercubes, but **not** for the antipodally folded five-cube, nor a classification of all optimal sensor placements or their exchange graph. Benson and coauthors' 2015 public preprint (later published under a revised title) establishes general links between zero forcing and power domination and works on graph products; it does not cover the Clebsch placement criterion. Earlier exact Clebsch results on ordinary domination, zero forcing and determining sets concern different invariants, and their numerical findings do not imply the present closed-neighborhood propagation classification. A previously published census of power domination on the ordinary five-cube likewise does not pass to the antipodal quotient without additional argument.

## Limitations
This theorem is specific to the sixteen-vertex Clebsch graph. The symmetry arguments establish the finite classification; the exchange-graph metric relies on explicit, fully enumerated finite certificates rather than a closed-form infinite-family argument. The literature and earlier accessible mathematical claims were compared, but unindexed computations or documents not available for inspection could affect historical priority. No independent scholarly validation has been performed.
