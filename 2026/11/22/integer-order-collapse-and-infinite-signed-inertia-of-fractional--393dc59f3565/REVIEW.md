# Review of the fractional Volterra self-commutator theorem

## Correctness (PASS)
For each real positive order, compactness follows by an explicit small-lag truncation. Reflection about the interval midpoint conjugates the fractional integral with its adjoint, forcing signed spectral pairing. At noninteger order, fractional powers in a one-sided kernel expansion have nonzero coefficients and give independent kernel columns by a finite Vandermonde determinant for arbitrarily large column families. At integer order, the kernel is a polynomial with invertible anti-triangular coefficient matrix, yielding exact rank and, by the classical Hilbert Gram determinant, the asserted nonzero spectral product. The standalone exact-arithmetic checker verifies the intermediate polynomial and determinant identities at six integer orders and 28 sampled noninteger Vandermonde instances. Numerical evidence is not used to infer an infinite-rank conclusion.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)
The directly relevant 2022 study of first- and second-order Volterra self-commutator norms and the 2010 full-text study of polynomial Volterra singular numbers do not state the complete real-order rank/inertia theorem. Relevant indexed results concern a different negative Cesàro–Hardy self-commutator, stable rational convolution realizations or opposing-Volterra sum pencils. Their claims are not shown to imply the present general fractional statement. Searches included fractional Riemann–Liouville aliases, self-commutator rank, signed inertia, Hankel finite-rank criteria, and spectral determinant. A 2006 fractional Volterra orbit paper was only accessible at abstract level; an unindexed equivalent remains possible. The scientific comparison is recorded in AUDIT.json.

## Value (PASS)
The integer-versus-noninteger transition gives a complete, natural structural classification of the signed nonnormality defect on the familiar fractional Volterra family. The all-order factorial spectral product strengthens the finite-rank integer description without requiring arbitrary experimental cutoffs. It is a mathematically motivated invariant for compact integration operators; no broad theorem for unrelated kernels is asserted.
