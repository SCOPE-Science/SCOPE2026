# Review of integral ultrametric type-feasibility matrices

## Correctness — PASS
Every complete nonalgebraic type is uniquely \((j,C)\) with \(C\) an old \(E_j\)-class. The pairwise ultrametric criterion gives a rooted hierarchy merge value for distinct types and all distances up to \(j\) for equal types. For any prescribed distance level, this yields zero blocks, positive isolated units, and complete multipartite blocks with a universal looped hub. Explicit integer basis differences reduce each nonzero block to one positive and one negative unit per child, without denominators. A separate exhaustive script constructs all types directly from triangle inequalities and verifies the diagonal congruence with determinant-one-up-to-sign bases on 638 small labelled bases. The finite verification checks hypotheses; the argument proves the all-size theorem.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
Nguyen Van Thé (arXiv:0710.2347) gives the universal ultrametric and its tree description; Conant (arXiv:1504.02427) gives the generalized metric model theory, including relevant distance-type constraints; and Delhomme–Laflamme–Pouzet–Sauer (arXiv:1509.04346) treat homogeneous ultrametric ball trees. Relevant published ultrametric orbit-profile, finite-variable-theory and colour-refinement findings were also inspected. None of the accessible inspected results asserts the full arbitrary-named-base exact-distance matrix congruence, Smith form or recovery of cluster counts from inertia. Equivalent classical hierarchical-matrix results under other terminology remain a serious but nondecisive priority risk; a failed keyword search does not establish novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem is a natural complete classification over *all* finite named ultrametric bases and all distance levels. It provides a primitive integral arithmetic core and a direct inverse interpretation of negative matrix inertia as the number of old threshold clusters. It excludes finite-cokernel torsion uniformly and explains characteristic-independent rank, even when the matrix is singular. This is structurally motivated by logical extension feasibility and the ultrametric ball hierarchy rather than an arbitrary finite-size computation.

## Scope and limitations
The universal structure has a fixed finite chain of positive distances; the base is nonempty, finite and individually named. The theorem does not determine the ordinary eigenvalues, identify the full old clustering tree from the matrix inertias or solve higher-arity simultaneous-realization problems. Independent mathematical validation is not yet available. Coverage of older literature remains necessarily incomplete.
