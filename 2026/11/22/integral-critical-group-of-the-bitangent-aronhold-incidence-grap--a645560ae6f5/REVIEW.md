# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The canonical heptad graph follows from Cayley-octad bitangent parity. The explicit integral incidence matrix has \(28\) row sums \(72\), \(288\) column sums \(7\), and Gram \(56I+16J\), implying the exact spanning-tree count \(2^{160}7^{287}\). Modular local Smith reductions at primes \(2\) and \(7\) finish at precisions below \(p^{10}\), agree for four sink choices and give valuation totals matching the independent determinant. The \(2\)-part also has a direct small Schur-matrix argument.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The two classical \(8+280\) heptad orbits are explicitly known in Dolgachev and not claimed novel. The earlier incidence-cokernel computation identifies a quotient of the rectangular matrix, whereas the present theorem computes the reduced Laplacian of the bipartite graph. Searches for the latter Smith group found no covering formula in the inspected sources; an unindexed design-theoretic computation remains a genuine possibility.

## Value — PASS
The full integral chip-firing group of a canonical algebraic-geometric \(28\)-by-\(288\) configuration is an intrinsically motivated exact invariant. Its large \(7\)-primary extension structure, including \(26\) copies of order \(49\), is not determined by the familiar geometric counts, design Gram matrix, or spanning-tree number alone. This is not a claim about divisor groups of the quartic.
