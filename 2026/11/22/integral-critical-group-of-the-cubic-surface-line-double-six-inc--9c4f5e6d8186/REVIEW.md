# Scientific review: line–double-six graph critical group

## Correctness
PASS. Every geometric object is explicitly defined and the two exhaustive constructions produce the same 36 twelve-line supports from the standard 27 exceptional curves. The 63-by-63 Laplacian follows directly from 16/12 incidence degrees. An exact cubic annihilating polynomial for the 27-by-27 Gram matrix, its trace and row sums determine the rational spectrum and spanning-tree count. Prime-local Smith reductions at all determinant primes give invariant factors with precisely the independently derived determinant. Two different rooted minors agree valuation-by-valuation. Limit: this is an exact finite incidence computation extended through the classical uniform geometric classification, not a statement about special characteristic or actual surface Picard torsion.

## Originality
PASS under checked sources, provisional historical priority. Hunt's 1995 full text (pp. 4–5) and the 2015 root/double-six analysis give the classical geometry. The publicly inspected order-four reconstruction result supplies the Smith form of the 27-by-36 *incidence matrix*, not the 62-by-62 *reduced graph Laplacian*. The former quotient does not state the latter invariant. The earlier 27-line/45-tritangent incidence graph concerns a different bipartite graph. No covering direct graph-Jacobian calculation was located in searches under double-six, Schläfli, 27-weight/36-root incidence and sandpile/critical group terminology. Unindexed or unpublished equivalent calculations remain possible.

## Value
PASS. The unordered 36 double-sixes are a distinguished \(W(E_6)\)-homogeneous family on cubic surfaces, and integral chip-firing on the graph linking them to all 27 lines is a natural complete finite divisor-class invariant. Its 50 nontrivial cyclic factors capture 2-, 3- and 5-primary structure invisible to the rational incidence spectrum or tree count alone. The conclusion adds the full torsion structure to the previously known rational and rectangular-incidence data; it does not present a generic graph exercise or equate graph torsion with geometric Picard torsion.
