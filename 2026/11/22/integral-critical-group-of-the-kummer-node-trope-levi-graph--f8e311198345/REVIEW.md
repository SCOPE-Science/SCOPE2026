# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The Kummer node/trope incidence matrix is reconstructed explicitly by the classical \(4\times4\) biplane rule, giving \(NN^{\mathsf T}=4I+2J\). The exact reduced Laplacian determinant is \(3\cdot2^{72}\), both spectrally and by fraction-free integer elimination. A standalone Python 3 checker establishes local Smith valuations for primes two and three, in two separate precisions and for four removed vertices. These reproduce the claimed integral invariant factors exactly. The claimed group is expressly the graph Jacobian, not a K3 Picard discriminant group.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk

Kumar gives the classical geometric node/trope configuration, not its graph-critical-group decomposition. Gao–Marx-Kuo–McDonald–Yuen Proposition 2.12 determines only the mod-two rank for folded cube graphs; it implies nineteen nontrivial \(2\)-primary factors but not the group exponents, their multiplicities, or the order-three constituent. A published node–trope **incidence cokernel** has a different presentation and is not the graph Laplacian cokernel. Searches across graph aliases and complete integer factor patterns did not yield an exact previous complete Smith form. An unindexed earlier calculation is the principal remaining novelty risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Kummer \((16_6)\)-configuration is a canonical incidence structure attached to a Jacobian Kummer quartic, and its integral graph Jacobian is a natural discrete invariant refining its spectrum. The result resolves the complete integral structure, including a mixed prime cyclic factor of order 96, rather than merely the number of spanning trees. The value is that of a precise reusable invariant of classical algebraic-geometric incidence, not a claim to a new surface-theoretic Picard invariant.

## Limits

The configuration is taken over characteristic zero. The conclusion does not depend on a special quartic equation or establish new results about a genus-two Jacobian, its Picard lattice, or Brauer group. Full historical priority is not independently certified.
