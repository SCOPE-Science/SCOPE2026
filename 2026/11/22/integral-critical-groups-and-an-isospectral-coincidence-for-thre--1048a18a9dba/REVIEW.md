# Scientific review: integral component groups of three orchard boundaries

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem covers the exactly specified twelve-line C2, C6, and C7 configurations, blowing up all nineteen triple points and retaining nine ordinary nodes. Each resolved curve has thirty-one rational components, sixty-six nodes, and arithmetic genus thirty-six. The reproduced nineteen-triplet incidence lists uniquely determine each graph. Bareiss and Schur-complement determinants agree, and the complete prime-local Smith valuations recover each claimed cyclic factor. Three different rooted integer Smith computations, independent determinant factorization, and complete graph-isomorphism/spectrum tests also agree. The Néron interpretation has its explicit thickness-one semistability hypothesis.

**Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk.** Bokowski–Pokora (2016) provide exactly the three combinatorial incidence types; Farnik et al. (2018) study their distinct symbolic-power containment properties. Neither inspected source gives these integral Smith factors, and generic graph Jacobian theory alone cannot produce them without the numerical reduction. Relevant published published-finding corpus records concern different configurations, especially the three \((9_3)\) Levi graphs and other stable boundaries. The numerical tables were checked against aliases for graph Jacobians, sandpile groups, Smith forms, and Néron components. An earlier unindexed calculation remains possible, so this is not a historical exclusivity certificate.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The three boundaries have the same local incidence statistics and geometric genus, but one has an integral component group with \(61\)- and \(307\)-torsion while the other two have different prime support. Moreover the last two come from nonisomorphic line incidence structures yet have the same full critical group and Laplacian polynomial. This simultaneous separation and coincidence is a natural comparison of a classical configuration family, supplying exact degeneration information unavailable from the published incidence counts.

The complete statement is limited to the named complex arrangements and specified resolution. Independently verifying historic priority, extending to arithmetic forms or nonunit node thicknesses, and checking other pseudoline systems are outside the claim. The verifier is a finite exact calculation, not a symbolic formula for an arbitrary family of twelve-line arrangements.
