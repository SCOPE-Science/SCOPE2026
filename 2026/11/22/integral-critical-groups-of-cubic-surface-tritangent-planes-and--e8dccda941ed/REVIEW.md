# Scientific review: cubic-surface tritangent graph critical groups

## Correctness — PASS
The statement fixes the geometric graph using the 27 exceptional divisor classes and anticanonical tritangent triples. Exhaustive exact integer enumeration yields 45 vertices and strongly regular parameters (45,12,3,3). The integral Laplacian has determinant \(3^{62}5^{23}\), checked by separate spectral reasoning and fraction-free elimination, and local Smith reductions of both graphs at three different deleted vertices yield the claimed elementary divisors. A second catalogue adjacency matrix is embedded unmodified as 45 binary rows; it passes every symmetry, degree, common-neighbor, determinant and Smith check. The proof is finite and exact, not inferred from numerical eigenvalue approximations.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
The 2006 primary classification and Spence's complete graph catalogue supply the graph objects and spectra, not these two Laplacian critical-group decompositions. The 2012 thesis gives Smith forms for an adjacency matrix of a complementary graph, a different integral operator; neither its stated results nor the general strong-regular spectral formula implies our Smith factors. Searches by tritangent-plane graph, \(GQ(4,2)\) point graph, critical group, sandpile and Laplacian elementary divisors did not identify an equivalent full calculation. The closest other published mathematical records concern tritangent weight-lattice relations and critical groups of different graphs. A differently indexed or unpublished full Smith calculation remains a risk. Absence from a search index is not a proof of historical priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The adjacency among the 45 tritangent planes is a canonical incidence construction on every smooth cubic surface, and its integral graph Jacobian is a nontrivial natural invariant. The comparison with another graph having the same precise strongly regular parameters supplies a motivated discriminating use: both have equal spectral eigenvalues and spanning-tree count yet different 3-primary generator ranks. This distinguishes integral geometry not recovered from known spectral/classification statistics, rather than merely recomputing a familiar tree count.

## Scope and limitations
The computation applies to algebraically closed characteristic-zero cubic surfaces, and to exactly the second specified catalogue graph. It is not an arithmetic classification, not a classification of all 78 graphs, not a statement about surface Picard groups, and not independently externally validated. Historical priority is provisional.
