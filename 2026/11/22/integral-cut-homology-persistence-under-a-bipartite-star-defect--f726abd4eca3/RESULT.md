# Integral cut-homology persistence under a bipartite star defect

## Finding
Let \(a\ge4\), \(b\ge3\), and \(0\le t\le b-1\) be integers. From \(K_{a,b}\), delete \(t\) distinct edges incident with a single fixed vertex \(u\) in its \(a\)-vertex shore; call the resulting graph \(G_t\). Let \(\Delta_t\) be the ordinary three-cut complex whose facets are complements of three-vertex sets inducing **disconnected** subgraphs of \(G_t\). Write \(N=a+b\) and, with \(\binom{s}{2}=0\) for \(s<2\), put
\[
R_t=\binom{a-2}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}+(a-2)\binom{b-t-1}{2}.
\]
Then the **Alexander dual**, rather than the original cut complex, has the complete integral homotopy classification
\[
\Delta_t^\vee\simeq\bigvee^tS^1\vee\bigvee^{R_t}S^3.
\]
In all degrees the original cut complex has exactly
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_t;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{R_t},&j=N-6,\\
\mathbb Z^t,&j=N-4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
All these groups are torsion-free. Every \(\Delta_t\) is pure of dimension \(N-4\), and none is shellable.

There is a **map-level** refinement. Orient successive inclusions as \(\Delta_{t-1}\hookrightarrow\Delta_t\). In degree \(N-6\), they induce split surjections whose kernels have ranks \((a-2)(b-t-1)\); in degree \(N-4\), they induce split injections with free rank-one cokernel. These assertions hold integrally, for every \(1\le t\le b-1\). For instance at \(a=b=4\), the two Betti-number sequences across \(t=0,1,2,3\) are \((9,5,3,3)\) and \((0,1,2,3)\).

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite and simple. Let \(A=\{u\}\sqcup D\), with \(|D|=a-1\), and write \(B=T\sqcup B_t\), where \(|T|=t\) consists of the vertices **not** adjacent to \(u\), and \(|B_t|=b-t\ge1\) of its neighbors. Every vertex of \(D\) is adjacent to every vertex of \(B\). The set of three vertices called disconnected is defined by the graph **induced** on that set. The reduced homology conventions include degree zero and the empty face. All wedge and suspension statements mean homotopy equivalences of finite CW complexes, never equalities of abstract simplicial complexes.

For a vertex set \(V\), the Alexander dual of \(K\) is \(K^\vee=\{F\subseteq V:V\setminus F\notin K\}\). Define \(S_1(U)\) to be the one-skeleton of the simplex on \(U\), including its empty face. For \(|U|\ge1\) this is the complete graph on \(U\) vertices (a point at \(|U|=1\)).

## Proof
**1. An explicit dual.** Let \(Y_t=\Delta_t^\vee\). Because \(\Delta_t\) is generated by complements of disconnected triples, a set \(F\) is a face of \(Y_t\) precisely when it contains no disconnected triple. An induced connected triple in a bipartite graph has exactly two vertices on one shore and one on the other, and the singleton vertex is adjacent to **both** other vertices. It follows that faces not containing \(u\) form precisely
\[
X=S_1(D)*S_1(B).
\]
Faces containing \(u\) form a cone over the following link:
\[
L_t=(D_{\mathrm{disc}}*S_1(B_t))\ \sqcup\ T_{\mathrm{disc}},
\]
where \(D_{\mathrm{disc}}\) is the discrete complex on \(D\), and the vertices of \(T\) are isolated components (there are no cross-faces between them and the displayed join). Thus the equality of simplicial complexes is \(Y_t=X\cup(u*L_t)\), with intersection exactly \(L_t\). This equality also covers \(t=0\) and \(|B_t|=1\).

**2. Homotopy of the common subcomplex.** The complete graph \(S_1(D)\) has first Betti number \(\binom{a-2}{2}\), and \(S_1(B)\) has first Betti number \(\binom{b-1}{2}\). Both are connected, and each is a wedge of that many circles. The standard join identity for pointed, connected CW complexes yields
\[
X\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{a-2}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}}S^3.
\]
Because both binomial coefficients are positive in the stated range, \(X\) is simply connected and has vanishing \(\pi_2\). In particular every map from any CW complex of dimension at most two to \(X\) is nullhomotopic.

**3. Homotopy of the attaching link.** The discrete complex \(D_{\mathrm{disc}}\) is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{a-2}S^0\), while \(S_1(B_t)\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{b-t-1}{2}}S^1\), with the zero-circle case interpreted as contractible. Their join has homotopy type
\[
D_{\mathrm{disc}}*S_1(B_t)\simeq\bigvee^{(a-2)\binom{b-t-1}{2}}S^2.
\]
It is connected and has dimension at most two. The remaining \(t\) vertices are disjoint isolated components, so \(L_t\) has \(t+1\) connected components and no other reduced homology except the stated degree-two group.

**4. The cone attaches trivially in homotopy.** The inclusion \(L_t\to X\) is nullhomotopic: separately contract the maps of its two-dimensional connected components in the \(2\)-connected space \(X\), and join their endpoint images through paths in \(X\). Its mapping cone is therefore a wedge \(X\vee\Sigma L_t\), where reduced suspension of a complex with \(t+1\) connected components contributes exactly \(t\) circles. Combining Steps 2–3 gives the claimed complete homotopy type of \(Y_t\), including its integral homology and cohomology.

**5. Alexander duality and non-shellability.** Integral combinatorial Alexander duality on \(N\) vertices states
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_t;\mathbb Z)\cong
\widetilde H^{N-j-3}(Y_t;\mathbb Z).
\]
Dual cohomology is free and supported in degrees one and three, giving precisely the stated original homology. All disconnected triples yield facets of size \(N-3\), so \(\Delta_t\) is pure of dimension \(N-4\). The positive coefficient \(R_t\ge\binom{a-2}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}>0\) makes its degree-\(N-6\) homology nonzero, two below the facet dimension. This rules out pure shellability.

**6. Natural maps, not merely Betti numbers.** When one more edge at \(u\) is deleted, the dual complexes satisfy \(Y_t\hookrightarrow Y_{t-1}\), and the common subcomplex \(X\) remains fixed. The attaching links satisfy \(L_t\hookrightarrow L_{t-1}\). The induced map on reduced \(H_0\) sends \(\mathbb Z^t\) **onto** \(\mathbb Z^{t-1}\), with primitive kernel \(\mathbb Z\): exactly one isolated vertex joins the connected component when its formerly missing edge is restored. On \(H_2\), it is a primitive split inclusion
\[
\widetilde H_2(L_t;\mathbb Z)\hookrightarrow\widetilde H_2(L_{t-1};\mathbb Z).
\]
Indeed the latter identifies with the tensor product of the reduced \(H_0\) lattice of \(D_{\mathrm{disc}}\) and the cycle lattice of the complete graph on \(B_{t-1}\); the cycle lattice on \(B_t\) is a direct summand, by choosing a fixed reference vertex in \(B_t\) and triangle-cycle bases. Its rank increment is \((a-2)(b-t-1)\).

The relative homology sequence of the mapping-cone pair \((Y_t,X)\) now gives natural short exact sequences
\[
0\to H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\to H_3(Y_t;\mathbb Z)\to\widetilde H_2(L_t;\mathbb Z)\to0
\]
and natural isomorphisms \(H_1(Y_t;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H_0(L_t;\mathbb Z)\). The first diagram has the identity on \(H_3(X)\) and a primitive split inclusion on the quotients, hence its middle map is itself a primitive split injection; the second is a split surjection. By naturality of universal coefficients and combinatorial Alexander duality, the inclusion maps \(\Delta_{t-1}\to\Delta_t\) are respectively split surjections in degree \(N-6\), with the indicated kernel, and split injections in degree \(N-4\), with rank-one cokernel. This proves the complete integral persistence description.

## Verification
The standalone scripts `artifacts/check_star.py` and `artifacts/check_maps.py` use only the Python standard library. Execute each with Python 3. The first enumerates all graph-induced connected triples and full Alexander-dual face sets directly, checks the cone/link decomposition and reduced mod-two Betti numbers over \(27\) small parameter choices; the second constructs mod-two chain complexes and induced maps for all \(54\) nontrivial stage/degree checks. Both return `"status": "PASS"` on the supplied run. These are **finite mod-two checks**, not proofs of all-order homotopy, integral primitivity or torsion-freeness; Steps 1–6 provide those proofs. The embedded `artifacts/verification_output.txt` holds the actual outputs.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue [1] define ordinary graph cut complexes and give the complete bipartite case. The \(t=0\) integral Betti number specializes to their established rank \(\binom{a-1}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}\); this baseline is not claimed as new. The further results here require a fixed-endpoint **star deletion** rather than deletion of a matching. The existing crown-graph result [4] concerns disjoint deleted edges \(\{a_i,b_i\}\) and cannot imply the present filtration or its ranks by changing parameters: the deleted-edge degree patterns and intermediate graphs are different. The 2024 cut-complex sequel [2] addresses other graph families, while the total-cut variant [3] changes the definition by requiring independent instead of merely disconnected deleted triples. published-finding corpus searches of these aliases and the published crown statement did not reveal the present star-defect formula or its map-level refinement.

## Limitations
The statement does not cover deletion of **all** \(b\) incident edges, other non-star edge-fault patterns, or arbitrary nested graphs. It does not claim the original cut complex is itself a sphere wedge, nor does it determine its cup products or higher homotopy groups. The link-join method uses the two-dimensional input, so a naïve transfer to general higher cut parameters is not asserted. A semantically equivalent but unindexed earlier classification remains an originality risk; search nonmatches alone cannot establish global priority.
