# Scientific review: the Clebsch ordinary four-cut dual

**Correctness — PASS.** The face relation is the actual combinatorial Alexander dual: a set is admitted exactly when it contains no disconnected quadruple. The complete two-skeleton, 680 connected tetrahedra, 192 pentagon four-faces and absence of higher faces are independently enumerated and supported by the strongly regular parameters. Exact signed boundaries compose to zero. Integer unit elimination proves all 455 two-cycles are boundaries and the four-boundary has primitive rank 191; its one-column kernel is free. The dual is therefore a wedge of 34 three-spheres and one four-sphere by Hurewicz and Whitehead. Alexander duality gives the original cut complex's free homology in degrees nine and ten; neither finite-field extrapolation nor an unwarranted homotopy claim for the original complex is used.

**Originality — PASS against inspected evidence.** The primary 2023 ordinary cut-complex article and its 2024 sequel were read in accessible full HTML text, including definitions and their named graph families; neither computes this graph at cut parameter four. The closest public computations were read in full: the ordinary Clebsch three-cut complex has torsion, the Clebsch total four-cut complex has torsion but is defined using independent quadruples, and the ordinary four-cut complex of the four-cube has different homology. The new signed boundary reductions are not implied by any of those results. Semantic searches for the graph's folded-cube and strongly regular aliases, the four-cut threshold, dual homotopy, pentagon parameter and exact Betti ranks did not locate an equivalent. This remains a best-knowledge result, not an absolute claim against unindexed mathematics.

**Value — PASS.** The five-regular Clebsch graph is a canonical symmetric graph at the boundary between the four-cube and its antipodal-edge augmentation. Its four-cut Alexander dual has 192 pentagon faces that do not occur in the four-cube comparator; these contribute a genuine fourth-dimensional sphere alongside 34 three-dimensional spheres. The associated original pure eleven-dimensional complex has two different non-top integral homology degrees, in sharp contrast to the same graph's torsion-bearing ordinary three-cut complex. The exact torsion-free classification and its reason are useful natural finite test cases for the topological behavior of ordinary versus total cut constructions and for graph-edge augmentation.

**Residual risks and limits.** An independently unindexed computation might contain an equivalent result. The original cut complex's homotopy type, equivariant modules, and generalization to larger folded cubes have not been established. One published comparison is a public research record rather than a journal article; it was compared by implication, not treated as external certification.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
