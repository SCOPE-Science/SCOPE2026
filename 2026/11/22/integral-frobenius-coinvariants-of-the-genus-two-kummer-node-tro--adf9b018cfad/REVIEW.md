# Scientific assessment: Kummer node–trope integral Frobenius coinvariants

## Correctness — PASS

The six-Weierstrass-point labelling of sixteen nodes and sixteen tropes is standard and explicitly documented by Gonzalez-Hernandez (2024, Section 2.2) and Ohashi (2009, Section 4). The proof reduces the arithmetic group to the exact integer cokernel of the matrix \([A\mid P_\sigma-\operatorname{Id}]\). An identity \(A(3A^\mathsf T-J)=12\operatorname{Id}\) bounds the exponent, and separate exact Smith and Hermite computations establish every group structure. The executable verification enumerates every permutation of six letters and checks all eleven types. The two claimed matching orbit profiles are checked on both the node and trope actions; explicit squarefree sextics realize them over every odd finite field. Limits: no corresponding claim in characteristic two, and no geometric Picard/Brauer interpretation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Ohashi (2009) and Gonzalez-Hernandez (2024) give the geometric configuration and Galois permutation model but not the integral coinvariant table. The Ducey–Gerhard–Watson (2016) rook-graph Smith theorem is a nearby ordinary integer matrix calculation but does not specify the required \(S_6\)-equivariant Kummer module. The published Göpel-tetrad lattice has 60 support columns rather than 16 tropes; the published Segre-cubic coinvariant computation concerns a nonisomorphic 10-by-15 incidence map. Finite-étale orbit spectra determine permutations, not the Smith presentation or the equal-orbit/nonisomorphic-group distinction. Older unindexed biplane representation calculations could still be equivalent; no absolute priority claim is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sixteen Kummer nodes and sixteen tropes are canonical geometric markings of a genus-two Jacobian. Their signed geometric intersection theory is well established, but the unsigned integral cokernel retains subtle arithmetic two-primary data beyond the individual node and trope cycle profiles. The two realized patterns \((2,2,1,1)\) and \((2,2,2)\) have the same 16-point orbit spectrum in both configurations, and their nonisomorphic groups even have the same order. The eleven-case determination is a complete, natural arithmetic invariant rather than an arbitrary small-field enumeration. It is not promoted to a cohomological obstruction without further proof.
