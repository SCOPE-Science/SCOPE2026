# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The Fermat standard-line incidence pattern supplies \(6\ell\) ordinary \(\ell\)-fold crossings and \(3\ell^3\) nodes, with a connected normalization-incidence graph. For any nontrivial Frobenius order, its fixed graph is a unicyclic twelve-vertex graph; every other vertex and edge has the full \(d\)-orbit. Rooted lifting in the quotient constructs a Frobenius-invariant spanning tree and an integral fundamental-cycle basis, explicitly \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z[C_d]^{R/d}\). Local conductor/normalization theory, \(p>\ell\) truncated logarithms and Lang's theorem give the connected Picard group claim. Distinct finite exact programs check the geometry over \(\mathbb F_{11}\) and the integral spanning-tree mechanism in several degrees and Frobenius types. The genus also agrees with surface adjunction. The finite runs do not substitute for the uniform proof.

**Originality — PASS (qualified historical priority).** The published all-degree Fermat standard-line normalization formula already supplies singularity supports and point counts, and these are excluded from the novelty claim. Its full text does not evaluate the integral character lattice or a restriction-of-scalars torus. Schütt--Shioda--van Luijk study the ambient surface Néron--Severi lattice, not this singular-curve Picard torus. The Fermat quartic torus evaluation covers an even-degree different divisor, and the odd-degree Ceva divisor is a separate plane arrangement. General graph-Picard methods are known, but the evaluated induced integral lattice in this all-prime, all-residue family was not found in the inspected sources. An older unindexed equivalent evaluation remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Algebraic tori over finite fields are classified by integral Frobenius character lattices; merely knowing the curve zeta function or genus does not determine their isomorphism types. The all-prime-degree theorem supplies an exact integral structure and finite rational-point group for a canonical line arrangement on a classical surface. The three different torus types in degree five are natural and nontrivial arithmetic distinctions, and the uniform equivariant-tree argument is reusable for other singular geometric arrangements.
