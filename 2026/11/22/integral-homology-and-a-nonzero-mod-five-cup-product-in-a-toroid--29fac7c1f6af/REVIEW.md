# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The graph, action, face orientation, chain boundary and finite range are explicit. The fivefold action is free on the realization because a nonempty invariant independent face would contain an adjacent five-cycle. Exhaustive enumeration constructs 3,560 nonempty original faces and 712 quotient face orbits. Exact integral chain cancellations leave only multiplication by five between the residual degree-two and degree-one quotient generators, while the original complex has no homology below degree three beyond degree zero. Separate triangular-relator Tietze reductions give trivial original fundamental group and cyclic order-five quotient fundamental group. These imply that the cover is two-connected, and the Serre cohomology edge map proves the stated nonvanishing Bockstein cup. Modular ranks over four primes are independent supporting checks. The computational certificate is self-contained, not a sampling experiment.

## Originality

**PASS relative to inspected evidence.** Jonsson's general toroidal hard-square theorem yields the already known zero Euler characteristic at coprime widths but not the integral quotient topology or cup product. Bousquet-Mélou--Linusson--Nevo treat other boundary conditions; Adamaszek's nearby toroidal recurrence has three rows, not four. Related recorded computations concern other torus sizes or a nonisomorphic chessboard complex. Searches for the exact graph, translation quotient, equivalent descriptions, and the Bockstein product did not identify a statement implying the claimed classification. Full text of some comparators was not accessible, so independent priority verification remains necessary.

## Value

**PASS.** The \(4\)-by-\(5\) torus is a small coprime width-four periodic hard-square instance, and classical enumerative work gives only its Euler characteristic. The exact fifth-order fundamental-group torsion and the simultaneously surviving third- and fourth-degree free homology refine that coarse invariant. More significantly, the nonzero \(H^1\smile H^2\) product excludes an apparent Moore-space-and-spheres wedge decomposition, distinguishing this natural symmetry quotient at the level of multiplicative topology. The result is a complete computation of several motivated exact invariants of one natural finite object, not a claimed theorem for all toroidal grids.

The necessary integer calculations and low-dimensional presentation can be replayed using the attached standard-library checker; the cohomological consequence follows from the written covering-space proof rather than a separate cochain program.
