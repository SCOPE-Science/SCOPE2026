# Integral homology and the first mixed-degree threshold for long-cycle permutation complexes

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(C_n\subset S_n\) be the conjugacy class of permutations consisting of one cycle of length \(n\). Write every permutation in **one-line notation** \((\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(n))\), which differs from its disjoint-cycle notation. Let \(X_n\) be the permutation complex generated by these \((n-1)!\) words: its \(k\)-cells are the distinct ordered length-\(k+1\) subsequences of at least one word in \(C_n\), with the usual injective-word face maps.

The **complete reduced integral homology** for \(2\le n\le 6\) is

| \(n\) | Complete nonzero reduced integral homology |
| --- | --- |
| \(2\) | None |
| \(3\) | \(\widetilde H_1(X_3;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) |
| \(4\) | \(\widetilde H_2(X_4;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^3\) |
| \(5\) | \(\widetilde H_3(X_5;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{15}\) |
| \(6\) | \(\widetilde H_3(X_6;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), \(\widetilde H_4(X_6;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{88}\) |

Every unlisted reduced integral homology group in this range vanishes. All listed groups are torsion-free. Therefore \(n=6\) is the **first alphabet size** in this family with positive-degree reduced homology supported in more than one degree. In particular the size-six complex is not homology-equivalent to a wedge of spheres all in a common degree.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground alphabet is \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\); a permutation is a bijection of this alphabet. A permutation belongs to \(C_n\) exactly when iterating it from \(1\) visits every letter before returning to \(1\). Cells are *ordered words*: the words \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\) are distinct cells, and two identical subsequences coming from different generating permutations are identified as the same cell. The augmented chain group in degree \(-1\) has one generator \(\varnothing\). The result concerns the geometric realization of this finite semisimplicial complex, equivalently the barycentric order complex of the nonempty face-word poset. It makes no claim for \(n\ge7\).

## Proof
For \(-1\le k\le n-1\), form the free abelian group \(A_k\) on the distinct length-\(k+1\) generated words (and \(A_{-1}=\mathbb Z\)). For every \(k\ge0\), the actual cellular differential is
\[
\partial_k(a_0,\ldots,a_k)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^i(a_0,\ldots,\widehat a_i,\ldots,a_k).
\]
For \(k=0\), the expression is the empty word. This is the integral augmented complex for injective words, as justified by the standard cellular description in Chachólski–Levi–Meshulam. Since each word has distinct letters, deleting distinct positions produces the stated distinct faces, and the alternating deletion rule gives \(\partial_{k-1}\partial_k=0\).

Enumerate the entire symmetric group in one-line notation, select precisely the single \(n\)-cycles using the iterate-orbit test, and enumerate *every* ordered subsequence of each selected word. The resulting augmented cellular basis cardinalities in degrees \(-1,0,\ldots,n-1\) are:

| \(n\) | Cardinalities \((|A_{-1}|,|A_0|,\ldots,|A_{n-1}|)\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(2\) | \((1,2,1)\) |
| \(3\) | \((1,3,5,2)\) |
| \(4\) | \((1,4,12,18,6)\) |
| \(5\) | \((1,5,20,59,82,24)\) |
| \(6\) | \((1,6,30,120,348,460,120)\) |

An entry of \(+1\) or \(-1\) of any differential allows an exact **integral elementary chain cancellation**. Specifically, for a chosen unit entry \(u=\partial_k[r,c]\), eliminate generator \(c\) in degree \(k\) and generator \(r\) in degree \(k-1\), replacing each other entry in the same differential by the integral Schur-complement formula
\[
\partial'_k[s,d]=\partial_k[s,d]-\partial_k[r,d]\,\partial_k[s,c]/u.
\]
Delete the corresponding row and column in the adjacent differentials, in their induced integral bases. Because \(u=\pm1\), the resulting chain complex is integrally chain-homotopy equivalent to its predecessor, not just rationally equivalent. The code `check_homology.py` implements this full reduction with exact Python integers. It checks the complex identities before and after cancellation, and demands that *every residual differential be zero* (rather than interpreting uncanceled cells as homology without justification).

The number of canceled pairs for \(n=2,3,4,5,6\) is respectively \(2,5,19,88,498\). The full residual free-chain ranks in degrees \(-1,0,\ldots,n-1\) are respectively
\[
(0,0,0),\quad(0,0,1,0),\quad(0,0,0,3,0),\quad
(0,0,0,0,15,0),\quad(0,0,0,0,1,88,0).
\]
Every residual differential vanishes exactly. Thus these residual complexes have the claimed integral homology with no hidden torsion. The five cases exhaust all \(2\le n\le6\), proving the first-occurrence assertion.

## Verification
Run `python3 check_homology.py` from a directory containing the supplied standalone checker, using Python 3.10 or later and no third-party dependencies. The checker reconstructs each complex from scratch, checks all composite boundary maps are zero, executes the exact integer chain contractions, and verifies the listed ranks and numbers of canceled pairs. Separately, it computes all boundary ranks directly from the **original** cellular matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_{101}\) using sparse finite-field Gaussian elimination. The modular checks agree with all claimed ranks but **are not used to infer torsion-freeness**; the latter follows from the integral unit cancellations and the exactly zero residual differential. `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt` contains the replayed output. The finite search is exhaustive within the explicitly stated five values of \(n\), not an infinite-family argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Chachólski, Levi and Meshulam (2019) introduce permutation complexes, their integral injective-word cellular chains, the complete-word wedge-of-spheres theorem, and a homotopy classification for complexes generated by two permutations. Their theorems do not determine the homology of the whole long-cycle conjugacy class for \(3\le n\le6\); the complete symmetric-group generator set is a different complex. Govc (2026) proves that *every* permutation complex has free fundamental group; this does not imply the new higher-degree integral ranks or their first mixed-degree occurrence. Elizalde, Hanlon and Hersh (2026) establish shellability for several pattern-avoidance classes of injective words, a different condition on generating permutations. A two-orientation dihedral-word complex and alternating-group permutation complexes in related indexed work also have different generating sets and do not give the stated result as a formal specialization.

## Limitations
The theorem is a finite exact classification for \(2\le n\le6\) only. It does not supply a formula for \(n\ge7\), an explicit list of integral homology cycles, a homotopy-wedge classification, or an equivariant group-module structure. The value of the finite cutoff does not imply any monotonicity or asymptotic behavior in higher degrees. Priority assessment may miss unindexed or unpublished equivalent computations; no independent external mathematical audit is claimed.
