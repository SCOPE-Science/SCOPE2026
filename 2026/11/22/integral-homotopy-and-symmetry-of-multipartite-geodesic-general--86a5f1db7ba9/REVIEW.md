# Scientific review: multipartite geodesic general-position topology

## Correctness — PASS
The exact distance-two structure yields the full face criterion without invoking any unproved graph lemma. The complex is literally the join of the discrete part sets with each full part simplex attached along its entire discrete vertex set. Each such attachment is a cellular homotopy pushout of a contractible simplex along isolated points into a connected complex; homotoping the two attaching maps to constants adds precisely one circle for each attachment point beyond the first. Iterated joins of nonempty discrete complexes give the asserted spheres or a cone when a part is singleton. Thus the conclusion holds for every integer parameter, and is an **integral homotopy** equivalence rather than a finite inference. The equivariant first-homology isomorphism comes from the natural long exact sequence of the pair; the top module comes from signed join chains. Ten independent geodesic/enumeration/chain-reduction test cases and signed Lefschetz tests corroborate these arguments.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The family covers **every** connected complete multipartite graph with any number of parts and sizes, a natural central family for geodesic general-position polynomials. It determines the complete integral homotopy type (including both the free fundamental group and the higher homology), rather than only cardinalities or the Euler characteristic known from the polynomial. The simultaneous nonzero degrees \(1\) and \(t-1\) for \(t\ge3\) and all parts non-singleton give a nonpure mixed-dimensional phenomenon. The integral automorphism modules resolve which top cycles and fundamental cycles are permuted or sign-reversed, additional information not determined by Betti ranks or face counts. The derivation uses standard algebraic-topological constructions, but the complete naturally parameterized classification and integral symmetry refinement are substantive, specific topological invariants. No broad consequences outside this family are claimed.
