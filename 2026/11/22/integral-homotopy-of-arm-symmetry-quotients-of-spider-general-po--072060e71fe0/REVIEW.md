# Scientific review: integral homotopy of symmetric spider quotients

**Correctness — PASS.** The object is the ordinary geometric quotient, not a Borel quotient. The graph-geodesic face criterion gives the exact join-plus-free-edges decomposition. The quotient join is modelled as weighted multisets of depth labels; a continuous linear homotopy on the sorted-weight chamber merges repeated labels while fixing the depth vertices. The resulting simplex skeleton admits an explicit acyclic matching and a top-cell-only CW model. The remaining orbit edges contribute exactly \(\binom{L}{2}+L-1\) circles, yielding unrestricted integral homotopy and a free fundamental group. Independently enumerated barycentric quotient chain complexes have integer boundary-square zero and agree with predicted mod-\(2,3,5,7\) Betti vectors in ten exhaustive profiles. The checks do not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Rather (arXiv:2603.06930v1), Iršič–Klavžar–Rus–Tuite (2024), and the public spider-polynomial record treat general-position enumerators or labelled faces. The closest preceding spider equivariance result, item `82f2669d-efb7-42f8-9d07-3825da408f96`, already identifies the same quotient's **rational Betti ranks** and the original space's integral symmetry modules, but expressly leaves the quotient fundamental group, integral torsion and homotopy type undecided. The present statement does not reclaim those covered ranks and adds a concrete deformation-retraction homotopy proof. The published result on partwise-symmetric higher-independence quotients gives a related skeleton for a different group action and different complex; the clique-bouquet maximal-geodesic-avoidance result also concerns a different complex. Targeted literature and indexed-equivalence searches revealed no direct all-parameter integral quotient homotopy classification. A differently named symmetric-join equivalence remains possible; search noncoverage alone is not novelty proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Rational Betti information of a finite-group quotient cannot exclude new integral torsion or nonfree fundamental groups. The theorem resolves precisely these missing structural invariants for an infinite natural symmetry family, gives an explicit strong deformation retraction and a sharp high-sphere threshold \(L=r+1\), and separates the full-space exponentially many top classes from the quotient's binomial rank. This is a complete natural quotient classification, not a routine recalculation of known rational ranks or an arbitrary numerical cutoff.

**Limitations.** Only equal-length arms and the full group are covered; the proof does not classify arbitrary subgroup quotients. The checker assesses ten finite profiles and four characteristics; only the geometric argument proves the general claim. Publication priority is not independently certified.
