# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The face bound is exhaustive: any selected \((3,3)\) or \((2,4)\) bipartite subgraph still has at least six edges on six vertices after removing a matching, hence contains a cycle. Its relative chain complex has only new three- and four-cells. The projected integral four-boundary consists of primitive two-edge and signed six-cycle blocks, the latter carrying exactly one six-term kernel class per deleted-index triple. A tetrahedron basis anchored on any single deleted edge maps integrally onto the entire reference second homology. These facts yield the full integral homology and the consecutive-dimensional sphere wedge by Whitehead. The finite verification passes all 65 rectangular cases up to shore size six; mod-two ranks alone are not used to infer integer primitivity.

The 2023 forest-filtration paper covers the intact biclique and fully deleted balanced crown graph. ## Value — PASS
Partial matching deletion is the natural interpolation between the intact biclique and the crown graph, and acyclic induced vertex sets are a standard object in graph-topological filtrations. The formula gives every stage's full integral homotopy type, exhibits the birth of three-dimensional homology at the second deletion and four-dimensional homology at the third, and explains the integral primitive differential behind these transitions. Its all-parameter classification has mathematical value independently of any proposed downstream application.

## Scope and residual limits
The theorem assumes deleted cross-edges are pairwise disjoint. It does not calculate all natural inclusion maps, arbitrary missing-edge arrangements or equivariant homotopy splittings. The finite checker is over \(\mathbb F_2\); the integer proof, not that checker, establishes the infinite result. Independent mathematical auditing has not been carried out.
