# Integral homotopy of the full dihedral quotient of the Dürer matching complex

## Finding
Let \(G=GP(6,2)\) be the Dürer graph with vertices \(u_i,v_i\), \(i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\), and edges \(u_iu_{i+1}\), \(u_iv_i\), and \(v_iv_{i+2}\). Let \(K=\mathcal M(G)\) be its ordinary matching complex, and let \(D_6\) act by simultaneous rotations and reflections of the index. The **ordinary geometric quotient** has complete integral homotopy type
\[
|K|/D_6\simeq S^3\vee S^3.
\]
This is stronger than determining the two-dimensional invariant subspace of rational third homology: it rules out nontrivial fundamental group, integral torsion, and higher attaching obstructions.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. The twelve vertices are \(u_0,\ldots,u_5,v_0,\ldots,v_5\). Edge indices are the lexicographic order of the eighteen unordered pairs of integer vertices \(u_i=i\), \(v_i=6+i\). A simplex of \(K\) is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint graph edges, including the empty simplex by convention. The action has generators \(r(u_i)=u_{i+1}\), \(r(v_i)=v_{i+1}\), \(s(u_i)=u_{-i}\), and \(s(v_i)=v_{-i}\), with subscripts modulo six. These twelve automorphisms form the entire automorphism group: the inner vertices are exactly the vertices lying on triangles and the remaining six form an induced cycle.

Write \(\operatorname{sd}K\) for the barycentric subdivision. Every nonempty matching face gives a barycentric vertex; a strict chain of such faces gives a simplex. Distinct vertices in a chain have distinct face cardinalities. Thus every group element preserving a chain fixes all its barycentric vertices, so the quotient of \(\operatorname{sd}K\) is a regular cellular \(\Delta\)-complex with one cell for each \(D_6\)-orbit of strict chains, and realizes the genuine orbit space \(|K|/D_6\). We use its signed cellular boundary, not a naive vertex-orbit abstract complex.

## Proof
**Finite cellular model.** Exhaustive matching enumeration gives face counts \((18,117,334,402,156,10)\) in dimensions zero through five, a total of 1037 nonempty faces. The 166087 strict nonempty-face chains split under the twelve dihedral automorphisms into 14313 quotient cells, with counts \((117,1219,3949,5338,3090,600)\). An orbit cell represented by the chain \((F_0<\cdots<F_d)\) has oriented boundary given by deleting each \(F_j\) with coefficient \((-1)^j\); no two distinct deletions give the same orbit cell because they leave different sets of cardinalities.

**Actual homotopy collapses.** Repeatedly delete a cell whose set of currently present immediate cofaces consists of exactly one cell, together with that coface. The verifier also checks that the coface has no higher immediate coface, so each pair is a genuine free-face elementary collapse. Its deterministic queue removes exactly 6411 pairs; the remaining 1491 cells have dimension counts \((68,396,677,350)\), hence the quotient has the homotopy type of a three-dimensional CW complex.

**Fundamental group, independently of rational homology.** Contract a maximal tree of 67 edges in the remaining one-skeleton. The resulting fundamental-group presentation has 329 edge generators and 677 two-cell relators. Each triangular relator is the oriented product of its three edges, with tree edges suppressed. The verifier successively chooses a relator in which a generator occurs exactly once, solves for that generator, substitutes the resulting word in every remaining relator, freely reduces, and removes the defining relator. All 329 generators are eliminated and all residual relators are empty. Every step is a reversible Tietze move, proving \(\pi_1(|K|/D_6)=1\). An independent presentation of the unsubdivided original complex likewise eliminates all 100 non-tree generators; the quotient argument does not rely on that auxiliary calculation.

**Integral homology.** The signed chain complex of the collapsed quotient has ranks \((68,396,677,350)\). The verifier checks exact boundary-square identities. It then uses only pivots of coefficient \(\pm1\), with integral Schur-complement updates, to cancel 67 adjacent pairs in degrees one/zero, 329 in degrees two/one, and 348 in degrees three/two. These unimodular chain cancellations preserve integral homology, including its torsion. The final chain complex has precisely one zero-chain and two three-chains, both with zero differential. Therefore \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\), \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z^2\), and all other integral homology vanishes.

**Recognition of the full homotopy type.** Because the quotient is simply connected and has zero second homology, the Hurewicz theorem implies it is two-connected. Its degree-three Hurewicz map is therefore an isomorphism. Choose two maps from \(S^3\) representing an integral basis of third homology. Their wedge is an integral homology equivalence \(S^3\vee S^3\to |K|/D_6\), hence a homotopy equivalence by the simply connected homological Whitehead theorem. This is not an inference from rational Betti numbers or from finite-field ranks.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3.10 or later, using the standard library only. The script constructs the graph and all twelve transformations, all matching faces, all strict flags and orbit cells, and replays every free-face collapse. It independently verifies fundamental-group triviality by 329 reversible group-presentation eliminations, performs the 744 integral chain cancellations, and checks that the remaining differentials vanish. It also verifies the unsubdivided source fundamental-group presentation. The expected terminal line is `DURER_DIHEDRAL_ORBIT_HOMOTOPY_OK`. All critical counts are asserted in the script and its printed output is included in `verification_output.txt`. The finite enumerations are exhaustive because the graph has exactly eighteen edges, not extrapolations to an infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work
Shareshian and Wachs's 2004 matching-complex study gives general context and analyzes the complete-graph and complete-bipartite cases, not the Dürer graph's full geometric symmetry quotient [1]. Published work titled *The Dürer matching complex: an integral sphere wedge and its full dihedral character* establishes \(K\simeq\bigvee^{22}S^3\) and that \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_3(K;\mathbb Q)^{D_6}=2\) [2]. That character calculation predicts the **rational** quotient third Betti number but does not determine integral quotient homology or the quotient fundamental group. Another published Dürer computation classifies shortest octahedral carriers and integral sublattice obstructions in \(H_3(K;\mathbb Z)\); these are original-space classes rather than cellular chains of \(|K|/D_6\) [3]. A full homotopy calculation for a \(3\times3\) square-grid matching complex's dihedral quotient treats a different graph and symmetry group [4]. The present proof computes the exact geometric orbit complex for \(GP(6,2)\), ruling out hidden quotient torsion and recognizing the homotopy type.

## Limitations
Only the twelve-vertex Dürer graph and its full dihedral action are covered. The result does not classify quotients by proper subgroups or all generalized Petersen graphs, nor does it identify a genuine equivariant wedge decomposition. Searches and primary-source comparisons reduce but cannot eliminate the possibility of an unindexed equivalent statement. This exact finite computation has not received independent mathematical validation.
