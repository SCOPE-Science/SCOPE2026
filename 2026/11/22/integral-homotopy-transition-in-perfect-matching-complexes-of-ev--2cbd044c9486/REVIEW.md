# Scientific review of the even-bipyramid classification

## Correctness — PASS
An explicit enumeration of every perfect matching by the two apex endpoints gives \(2m^2\) facets for \(m=3,4,5\); an independent brute-force generator confirms the same facets. Every elementary simplicial collapse is certified against the complete evolving face set. The resulting graph and two- and three-dimensional cores have the stated connectivity. In the decagonal case, a signed integer boundary matrix has an explicit unimodular minor of order eight and two independent integer kernel vectors, yielding free integral homology. The homotopy upgrade uses the standard spanning-tree presentation, Hurewicz and Whitehead, with primitive attaching degrees. Exact finite computations, not a numeric trend, prove each stated size.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations
The principal comparison, Chandrakar et al. (arXiv:2611.18058, first public 13 November 2026), classifies three-row open rectangular grids; its graph definition does not include even bipyramids. Honeycomb graph results and cubic Möbius/circular ladder results have different graph domains. Indexed octagonal-bipyramid calculations concern the larger ordinary matching complex, not the perfect-matching extendibility complex; the known four-rim octahedron does not imply the three new cases. Search covered graph-suspension and bipyramid terminology, equivalent face definitions, parameters and potential dominating results. Unindexed equivalent results and incomplete access to some related manuscripts remain priority risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The first three natural even-bipyramid cases beyond the known octahedron are completely classified, and the decagonal case marks the first appearance of mixed-dimensional reduced homology. This is a motivated exact finite cutoff for a canonical graph family, with homotopy rather than only rational Betti numbers. The result makes no unsupported claim of a general recurrence.

## Scientific limitations
The classification applies only to rim sizes six, eight and ten and is not equivariant. The proof and verification are reproducible but have no independent mathematical validation.
