# Scientific review: Petersen matching-complex orbit homotopy

## Correctness (PASS)
The object is the ordinary matching complex of the ten-vertex Petersen graph, not the Petersen graph itself or its perfect-matching complex. All 331 nonempty matching faces and 12,121 barycentric flags are enumerated. All 120 permutations of five labels act on the flags. The two true quotient CW complexes have 125 and 238 cells, respectively. Elementary collapses exhaust the full-symmetric quotient and reduce the alternating quotient to a two-complex. The latter has an explicit nonabelian presentation allowing eleven homotopy-preserving one-/two-cell cancellations to a single two-sphere. The submitted standalone exact verifier reconstructs these steps directly from the definitions. The proof does not infer homotopy from rational chain ranks alone.

## Originality (PASS relative to inspected literature)
The closest published result, *Full symmetric-group homology of the Petersen ordinary matching complex* (2026-11-02), determines rational quotient homology, not the claimed integral homotopy types or elementary-collapse models. *The Petersen matching complex: two sphere dimensions and distinct symmetric-group modules* (2026-11-01) establishes the original complex's homotopy type and rational representations, not these geometric orbit-space attaching maps. Zaslavsky (2012), §3.2, classifies matchings up to symmetry but not the resulting flag orbit topology. Statement-level searches in published records and general literature found no dominating result; an unindexed equivalent theorem is a residual priority risk.

## Value (PASS)
The alternating subgroup and the full group are the canonical normal-subgroup pair in the complete symmetry group of the Petersen graph. Their quotient spaces exhibit a sharp **integral** homotopy contrast between a two-sphere and a contractible space, refining a previously published rational-homology contrast. The explicit cancellation certificate supplies the missing attaching-map and fundamental-group information, rather than a routine recomputation of known Betti numbers. The contribution is deliberately limited to the two canonical orbit spaces, not claimed as an infinite-family theorem.

## Scope and risks
No generality for other subgroups or matching complexes; a complete primary-literature index is not available, so independent priority review remains appropriate. The finite certificate has been replayed, but independent validation has not been performed.
