# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Blowing up an ordinary triple point separates its three strict transforms, each meeting the exceptional P¹ once. The other four nodes remain direct edges. A pair of points in F3² minus zero is either antipodal and joined directly, or lies on a unique affine triple avoiding zero and is joined through its exceptional vertex. Hence the graph is connected with 16 rational components and 28 nodes, and arithmetic genus 13. Its degrees 4 and 3 make the boundary stable. An independently written calculation enumerated distinct unordered triples with zero coordinate sum modulo 3 (exactly the affine collinear triples) and the four antipodal pairs. Integer Smith reduction of each of two rooted Laplacians gave nine units followed by 2,6,12,12,48,48. As a determinant cross-check, if P is the antipodal permutation, J is all ones, and N is the incidence block, then NNᵀ=2I+J−P. After eliminating the eight degree-three exceptional vertices the line block is (10I−2P−J)/3. The unscaled block has nonzero eigenvalues 8 with multiplicity 3 and 12 with multiplicity 4. A line-rooted cofactor therefore gives 3·8³·12⁴/8=3,981,312. Raynaud's theorem identifies the graph group with the Néron component group for the stated regular unit-thickness models; it does not identify the group of the original nonnodal line union.

## originality

PASS

The MacLane realizations and incidence, and the general component-group theorem, are known. The strongest graph alias inspected is the cubic Möbius–Kantor Levi graph, which lacks the four direct double-point edges. No distinct inspected statement covers the exact group of this augmented graph. The pass is limited to that meaningful calculation and its application to the specified boundary, not a priority claim for the incidence or the general theorem.

## value

PASS

Blowing up exactly the ordinary triples is motivated by obtaining a reduced nodal stable genus-13 boundary while retaining the ordinary double intersections. For its degenerating Jacobians the component group is a natural finite invariant. The exact Smith factors, including the exponent and the multiple 2-primary levels, are information the determinant and genus do not give; they prevent substituting the more familiar cubic Levi graph for this degeneration.
