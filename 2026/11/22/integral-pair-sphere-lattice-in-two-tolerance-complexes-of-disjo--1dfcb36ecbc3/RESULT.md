# Integral pair-sphere lattice in two-tolerance complexes of disjoint triangles

## Finding
Let \(r\ge 2\), let \(A_1,\ldots,A_r\) be pairwise disjoint sets of exactly three vertices each, and put
\[
K_r=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^r\Delta(A_i),\qquad
X_r=\mathcal T_2(K_r)
=\{\sigma\subseteq\bigsqcup_i A_i:\exists i,\ |\sigma\setminus A_i|\le2\}.
\]
Every \(X_r\) has dimension four. Choose an ordering of the three vertices in every \(A_i\). For \(i<j\), let \(z_{ij}\) be the oriented boundary of the abstract five-simplex on the ordered six vertices \((A_i,A_j)\). All six oriented facets belong to \(X_r\), although the six-vertex simplex itself does not.

**Theorem.** The classes of these explicit six-facet cycles are an integral basis:
\[
\widetilde H_4(X_r;\mathbb Z)
 =\bigoplus_{1\le i<j\le r}\mathbb Z[z_{ij}]
 \cong\mathbb Z^{\binom r2}.
\]
More canonically, let \(W_r\) be the rank-\(r\) lattice with basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_r\), on which a vertex permutation preserving the unordered partition into triples sends \(e_i\) to the sign of its within-triple bijection times \(e_{g(i)}\). The full wreath-product automorphism action on fourth homology is the natural exterior-square action under
\[
\widetilde H_4(X_r;\mathbb Z)\cong\bigwedge\nolimits^2 W_r,
\qquad [z_{ij}]\longleftrightarrow e_i\wedge e_j.
\]
Every nonzero fourth integral cycle occupies at least six oriented four-simplices and at least six vertices. The primitive classes attaining both minima are exactly \(\pm[z_{ij}]\), with vertex supports \(A_i\cup A_j\). Under the canonical inclusion \(X_r\hookrightarrow X_{r+1}\), fourth homology embeds as the summand generated by the old pairs, with free cokernel of rank \(r\).

## Assumptions and scope
The input is the **filled** clique complex of \(r\) vertex-disjoint copies of the triangle graph, not their unfilled one-dimensional edge complexes. Tolerance two means deleting at most two selected vertices to leave a face of this filled complex; it does not mean the two-independence, matching, or clique-free complex. The orientation of each triple is needed only to specify signs in the integral basis and symmetry module. All chain groups are ordinary finite simplicial chains over \(\mathbb Z\). A primitive class is one not divisible by an integer of absolute value greater than one.

## Proof
**(1) All top-dimensional faces.** A face of \(X_r\) has at most five vertices because each original facet has three vertices and tolerance permits two additional ones. A five-vertex face therefore consists of all three vertices of some block \(A_i\), together with two vertices outside that block. This \(A_i\) is unique. There are only two possibilities: the two additional vertices lie in the same block \(A_j\), yielding a *pair face* of distribution \((3,2)\), or they lie in two different other blocks \(A_j,A_k\), yielding a *mixed face* of distribution \((3,1,1)\). The latter occurs only when \(r\ge3\). No six-vertex face exists, so the chain group \(C_5(X_r;\mathbb Z)\) is zero.

**(2) Mixed faces cannot appear in a four-cycle.** Suppose a mixed four-simplex is \(F=A_i\cup\{v,w\}\), with \(v\in A_j\), \(w\in A_k\) and \(i,j,k\) distinct. Delete one vertex \(u\) of \(A_i\). The resulting three-simplex \(\tau=(A_i\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v,w\}\) is contained in **exactly one** four-simplex, namely \(F\): any five-vertex face containing \(\tau\) must contain a three-vertex block, and only the missing vertex \(u\) can complete such a block. Consequently, its coefficient in the boundary of a four-chain is, up to a unit sign, exactly the coefficient of \(F\). For a chain with zero boundary, the coefficient of every mixed \(F\) must vanish, over \(\mathbb Z\) without division.

**(3) Pair cycles decouple integrally.** The remaining four-simplices are partitioned by unordered block pairs \(\{i,j\}\). For a fixed pair they are exactly the six five-vertex facets of the boundary \(\partial\Delta(A_i\cup A_j)\) of the abstract five-simplex. A three-simplex in the boundary of one of these six facets has four vertices distributed as \((3,1)\) or \((2,2)\) among precisely the blocks \(i,j\). It cannot occur in the boundary of a pair face from any different block pair. Hence the zero-boundary equations for different pairs are completely disjoint, after the mixed coefficients have been eliminated.

Within each six-facet group, the ordinary simplicial boundary matrix of \(\partial\Delta^5\) has a rank-one integral kernel, generated by \(z_{ij}=\partial[A_i,A_j]\). One elementary way to see this is that every codimension-one face is shared by exactly two of the six facets, and the dual graph on these facets is connected. Its boundary equations force all coefficients to be a single common integer times the alternating boundary coefficients. Thus
\[
\ker(\partial_4:C_4(X_r;\mathbb Z)\to C_3(X_r;\mathbb Z))
=\bigoplus_{i<j}\mathbb Z z_{ij}.
\]
Since \(C_5=0\), there are no nonzero fourth boundaries. This gives the claimed **integral** top-homology basis, not just its rank over a field.

**(4) Symmetry.** An automorphism of the block partition maps each ordered triple \(A_i\) to \(A_{g(i)}\) with an orientation sign \(\epsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}\). Hence it maps the oriented six-vertex boundary \(z_{ij}\) to \(\epsilon_i\epsilon_j z_{g(i),g(j)}\), with an extra negative sign when the target block order must be swapped: interchanging two three-vertex blocks requires nine transpositions. This is exactly the action of \(g\) on \(e_i\wedge e_j\) in \(\bigwedge^2 W_r\).

**(5) Minimum support and inclusion.** Each generator \(z_{ij}\) contains six four-simplices; the supports of distinct generators are disjoint **as four-simplex sets**. Thus a nonzero chain \(\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}z_{ij}\) has exactly \(6\#\{(i,j):a_{ij}\ne0\}\) four-simplices in its support, and its vertex support contains every \(A_i\cup A_j\) with \(a_{ij}\ne0\). Six is the sharp minimum for both counts. If both minima hold, exactly one coefficient is nonzero; primitivity forces that coefficient to be \(\pm1\). Finally, adjoining a new disjoint triple preserves each old cycle and adds exactly the \(r\) new pairs involving that triple. The block-pair basis proves split injectivity and the stated cokernel without any inference from finite examples.

## Verification
The accompanying dependency-free `CHECKER.py` independently enumerates all five-vertex faces using the defining tolerance rule, checks the private-codimension-one face property for every mixed facet, and directly computes the signed boundaries of all pair generators. It also calculates the fourth boundary rank over \(\mathbb F_2\), and checks generators for block and within-block symmetry transformations, for \(2\le r\le8\). The seven runs produce respectively \(1,3,6,10,15,21,28\) pair cycles and mod-two nullities of the same sizes. The proof above is an exact infinite-family integral argument; the finite mod-two computations are independent cross-checks, not an assertion of infinite proof or an integral certificate by themselves.

## Relationship to prior work
Kim and Lew introduced and investigated the topology of tolerance complexes, including their standard example of **two** disjoint \((t+1)\)-vertex simplices with \(t\)-tolerance, which is a simplex boundary. In particular, their two-triangle, tolerance-two example supplies the rank-one base case \(r=2\). Selvaraja subsequently developed general collapsibility, Leray and squarefree-symbolic-power bounds for tolerance complexes, including complexes with many facets and clique complexes of chordal graphs. These results do not determine the **integral top-cycle kernel across arbitrary numbers of triangle components**, its primitive six-facet basis or the wreath-product action described here. The new statement is also distinct from results for one-tolerance of a union of filled simplices and higher tolerance of an **unfilled** graph complex. The prior two-component sphere is explicitly included as the known boundary case rather than claimed separately.

## Limitations
This theorem determines degree-four integral homology, its natural action and minimum chain/vertex supports, not the complete integral homotopy type, lower-degree homology, cohomology ring or generalization to unequal block sizes. It applies to filled disjoint triangles and tolerance exactly two. A targeted literature and database review found no covering theorem, but publication priority is not independently certified; an unindexed equivalent or a broader previously proved general theorem remains possible.
