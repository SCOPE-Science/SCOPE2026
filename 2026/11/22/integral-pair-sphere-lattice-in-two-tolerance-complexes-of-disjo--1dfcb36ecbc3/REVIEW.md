# Review of integral pair-sphere top homology

## Correctness
PASS. Every top simplex has exactly five vertices and a unique complete triangle. A mixed distribution \((3,1,1)\) has a tetrahedral face lying in no other top simplex, forcing its coefficient to vanish in every integral four-cycle. The surviving pair distributions have disjoint codimension-one boundary rows and each reproduce the six-facet boundary of one five-simplex, whose integral kernel is generated by its signed fundamental boundary. Because no five-simplices exist in the complex, this is the entire fourth integral homology. The minimal-support and integral exterior-square symmetry statements follow on the explicit basis, including the odd sign from swapping three-vertex blocks. The checker verifies the combinatorial premises and signed actions for seven finite ranks, without replacing the proof.

## Originality
PASS under checked-priority evidence, with residual uncertainty. Kim–Lew's two-disjoint-simplex extremal example establishes only the known single pair \(r=2\). Selvaraja's general 2026 bounds for multi-facet tolerance complexes give collapsibility/Leray estimates, not the exact top integral lattice, pairwise generator support or wreath-product action. The available indexed comparisons and earlier relevant mathematical statements about one-tolerance of **filled** disjoint cliques and higher tolerance of **unfilled** graphs do not imply this statement: neither has the same input dimension and tolerance parameter. The lack of a matching indexed theorem is not a proof of absolute novelty; unindexed equivalent formulations remain possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The two-triangle sphere from the classical sharp Helly/Leray example has a nontrivial multi-component extension: no new fourth-homology relations appear, and the complete primitive top lattice is supported on geometrically minimal pairs with a natural integral symmetry representation. This identifies the topological information retained under adding arbitrarily many disconnected blocks, an invariant not supplied by general upper bounds and not a mere recomputation of the two-component example.

## Limitations
Only integral degree-four homology and its equivariance, minimal supports and inclusions are classified. No lower homology, full homotopy equivalence or larger clique-block theorem is claimed. Formal publication priority and independent mathematical validation remain outstanding.
