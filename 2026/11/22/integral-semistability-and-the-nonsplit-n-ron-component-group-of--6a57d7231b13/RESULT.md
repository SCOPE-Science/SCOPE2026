# Integral semistability and the nonsplit Néron component group of Bring’s curve at two

## Finding

Let \(e_j\) be the \(j\)-th elementary symmetric polynomial in five homogeneous variables. The flat integral canonical Bring curve \(\mathcal B=V(e_1,e_2,e_3)\subset\mathbb P^4_{\mathbb Z_2}\) has **regular total space**. In particular, each of the five ordinary nodes of its characteristic-two special fibre has thickness exactly one. If \(J=\operatorname{Jac}(\mathcal B_{\mathbb Q_2})\), then the connected Néron special fibre and its component group are
\[
\mathcal J^0_{\mathbb F_2}\simeq\left(\operatorname{Res}^{1}_{\mathbb F_4/\mathbb F_2}\mathbb G_m\right)^4,\qquad
\Phi(\overline{\mathbb F}_2)\simeq\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z,
\]
with arithmetic Frobenius acting on \(\Phi\) by inversion. Consequently the local Tamagawa number is \(c_2=1\), while \(\#\Phi(\mathbb F_4)=5\). The semistable conductor exponent is \(f_2(J)=4\), and the local Euler factor on inertia-invariant first cohomology is \((1+T)^{-4}\).

## Assumptions and scope

These assertions concern the **elementary-symmetric** integral model over \(\mathbb Z_2\), not coefficientwise reduction of power sums. The generic fibre is the smooth genus-four Bring curve. The previously established special-fibre theorem states that over \(\mathbb F_4\) the special fibre is two conjugate twisted cubics meeting transversely in the five \(\mathbb F_2\)-rational points obtained by permuting \([0:1:1:1:1]\); the two components are exchanged by Frobenius and their geometric dual graph has two vertices joined by five edges. That geometric special-fibre classification and its zeta function are prior work, **not** claimed as new here. The new assertions require the integral thickness check and the induced integral Néron data.

## Proof

The special fibre is smooth away from its five ordinary nodes, so flatness of the family implies regularity of the total space at those smooth points. The permutation symmetry reduces the remaining regularity test to the node \([0:1:1:1:1]\). On the affine chart where the last coordinate equals one, solve \(e_1=0\) by writing the other four coordinates as
\[
 u,\quad 1+v,\quad 1+w,\quad -3-u-v-w.
\]
Let \(F_2\) and \(F_3\) be the resulting integral polynomials obtained from \(e_2,e_3\). At \((u,v,w)=(0,0,0)\), direct expansion gives
\[
F_2(0)=-6,\quad \nabla F_2(0)=(-3,-4,-4),\qquad F_3(0)=-8,\quad\nabla F_3(0)=(-9,-8,-8).
\]
In the completed ambient local ring, with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(2,u,v,w)\), the initial forms modulo \(\mathfrak m^2\) are exactly
\[
F_2\equiv 2+u,\qquad F_3\equiv u\pmod{\mathfrak m^2}.
\]
Thus the relative complete-intersection total space has cotangent space generated by the independent images of \(v,w\). Its local dimension is two (a flat family of curves over a DVR), so the embedding dimension equals the Krull dimension: **the total space is regular at this node**. Permutation symmetry proves this at all five nodes. For an ordinary double point in a regular semistable surface over a DVR, its completed strict-henselian local equation is \(xy=2\cdot\mathrm{unit}\), so the edge thickness is precisely one; no exceptional chains occur in the minimal regular model. An extra local check is that \(F_3-3F_2\) has constant term \(10\), linear terms \(4v+4w\), and, after eliminating \(u\) via \(F_2=0\), the special-fibre quadratic tangent cone in \(v,w\) is \(v^2+vw+w^2\), whose two lines are defined over \(\mathbb F_4\) and exchanged by Frobenius.

For a regular semistable curve, the geometric Néron component group is the cokernel of the degree-zero intersection pairing of special-fibre components. On the two conjugate components \(C_1,C_2\), each of the five transverse intersections contributes one; their intersection matrix is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-5&5\\5&-5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its degree-zero reduced presentation is multiplication by five, hence \(\Phi(\overline{\mathbb F}_2)\simeq\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\). Frobenius swaps \(C_1,C_2\), and therefore negates their degree-zero generator and acts by \(-1\) on this quotient. Its fixed subgroup over \(\mathbb F_2\) is trivial because five is odd, whereas the square of Frobenius fixes all five geometric elements; this gives the claimed Tamagawa numbers.

The connected special fibre is the generalized Jacobian torus of the nodal special curve, whose character lattice is the first integral homology of the two-vertex five-edge graph. Its rank is four. Since all five nodes are rational but Frobenius swaps the two components, Frobenius reverses every oriented edge, so acts as \(-I_4\) on this lattice. This is the character lattice of four copies of the nonsplit norm-one torus \(\operatorname{Res}^{1}_{\mathbb F_4/\mathbb F_2}\mathbb G_m\), proving the integral torus isomorphism. The standard semistable conductor formula gives \(f_2(J)=\operatorname{rank}(H_1)=4\) with Swan conductor zero. The Frobenius polynomial on inertia invariants is \((1+T)^4\), hence the displayed local Euler factor.

## Relationship to prior work

A published full-text account of the characteristic-two Bring special fibre already identifies the two Frobenius-conjugate twisted cubics, five ordinary nodes, stable reduction and its singular-fibre zeta function. It does **not** calculate the thickness in the integral total space, the geometric Néron component group, its Galois module, or the rational Tamagawa number. Those prior statements alone cannot distinguish regular nodal smoothings of thickness one from local deformations of thickness greater than one; the independent integral linearization above is essential. Classical publications on Bring's curve and its isogeny class describe the generic curve, periods and complex theta geometry but do not give these local integral component data. Known generic isogeny results can constrain the conductor, but an isogeny need not preserve the prime-five component group at the prime two. The present result supplies the missing integral invariant.

## Limitations

No global minimal model at other primes, global Néron group, integral isomorphism of the generic Jacobian with a product of elliptic curves, or historical priority beyond the checked sources is asserted. The indicated special-fibre node configuration is used from prior work, not rediscovered. Unindexed local-model calculations could contain an equivalent result.
