# Review of uncovered-site overlap Smith forms

**Correctness — PASS.** For every finite partial triple-system base, its induced one-point relational extension diagrams are exactly matchings in the leave graph. The indicator of unused old coordinates is a one-vector minus the sum of the endpoint vectors of the chosen matching. This makes the overlap matrix the Gram matrix of a lattice with integral generators \(\mathbf1\) and all edge-endpoint sums. The proof identifies the connected-edge lattice as either the even-coordinate-sum lattice or a signed-sum-zero lattice, and derives the full Smith factors from explicit discriminant presentations, including all low-order and balanced cases. Exact standalone checks on 772 labelled connected graphs through five vertices and 306 labelled partial triple systems through six vertices support the general proof; no finite run is used as an infinite certificate.

**Originality — PASS, provisional.** The 1997 Raines–Rodger paper, read in full, treats matchings and factors in partial-Steiner leaves, but not the overlap Gram or Smith invariants. A previously published one-point extension spectrum establishes the matching bijection and real-rooted enumeration, which is credited, not rediscovered. General signed-incidence and root-lattice facts are standard and are credited as tools; they do not by themselves state the site-overlap kernel's integral classification. The relevant previous result was read in full, and actual database and literature searches compared aliases and implication strength. An unindexed equivalent monomer-incidence Gram theorem remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact all-size closed classification for every connected leave depends only on bipartiteness, parity and class imbalance, not matching counts or fine graph structure. It supplies complete integral degeneracy data of a natural per-site availability observable on finite one-point extension diagrams, and distinguishes characteristic-dependent free-site identifiability invisible in the one-point matching polynomial. In particular, two disjoint old triples give a factor 6, whereas a Fano-plane base with one isolated old point produces factors 2 and 18. This is a structural lattice theorem, not a small numerical improvement or a claim of simultaneous pair realizability.

**Closest literature and remaining limits.** Raines–Rodger (1997) studies factor existence in leaves; the earlier published one-point extension polynomial gives the matching indexing but not the overlap kernel. Root-lattice discriminants are classical. The closed invariant-factor formulas require a connected leave, while the arbitrary-leave rank and constructive Gram reduction hold without connectivity. The kernel counts separately free sites; joint two-point feasibility would require additional edge-disjointness constraints and is not claimed. Historical priority and independent validation remain open.
