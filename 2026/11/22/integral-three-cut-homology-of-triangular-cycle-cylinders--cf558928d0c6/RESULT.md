# Integral three-cut homology of triangular cycle cylinders

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(G_n=K_3\square C_n\) be the Cartesian product of the triangle and the length-\(n\) cycle. Let \(\Delta_n=\Delta_3(G_n)\) be the **ordinary three-cut complex**, generated by complements of disconnected induced three-vertex subgraphs. Its complete reduced integral homology is
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{\,n},&j=3n-5,\\
\mathbb Z^{\,\binom{3n}{2}-15n+1},&j=3n-4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Both ranks are positive. The pure \((3n-4)\)-dimensional complex is therefore not shellable. More precisely, its combinatorial Alexander dual \(K_n=\Delta_n^\vee\), taken on all \(3n\) graph vertices, has the **integral homotopy type**
\[
K_n\simeq\bigvee^{\,\binom{3n}{2}-15n+1}S^1\vee\bigvee^n S^2.
\]
For \(n=5\) the cut-complex ranks are \(5\) and \(31\) in degrees \(10\) and \(11\); for \(n=6\) they are \(6\) and \(64\) in degrees \(13\) and \(14\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set is \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\times\{0,1,2\}\). Two vertices are adjacent precisely when their cycle coordinates agree and their colors differ, or their colors agree and their cycle coordinates differ by one. Throughout, \(n\ge5\); this restriction excludes exceptional short-cycle two-step coincidences. A three-cut facet is the complement of an induced **disconnected triple**, including triples with one induced edge, and is not the complement of only an independent triple. A simplicial collapse deletes a free face and its unique maximal coface. Homology has integer coefficients unless a field is specified.

## Proof
**1. Exact Alexander-dual faces.** By the definition of the cut complex, a subset \(S\) belongs to \(K_n\) precisely if each triple in \(S\) induces a connected graph. Every vertex and every unordered pair consequently belongs to \(K_n\). An allowed triple has at least two graph edges; it is either a within-column triangle, a straight two-edge path on a color cycle, or a two-edge corner across adjacent columns. There are respectively \(n\), \(3n\), and \(12n\) of these, giving exactly \(16n\) triangular faces.

Every four-vertex face must induce a graph in which every vertex has at least two neighbors among the other three (otherwise a triple omitting a suitable neighbor is disconnected). The graph \(G_n\) has no four-clique or diamond, and its only four-vertex subgraphs of minimum degree at least two are the \(3n\) rectangles on one edge in a triangle column and one edge of the base cycle. Conversely, all triples in any such rectangle are connected, so all \(3n\) rectangles produce tetrahedra. There are no five-vertex faces: any five-vertex face would contain a rectangle, but a fifth vertex would have to be adjacent to all four rectangle vertices to keep every triple containing two opposite corners connected; no vertex of \(G_n\) can do so. Thus the full dual has face counts \(f_0=3n\), \(f_1=\binom{3n}{2}\), \(f_2=16n\), \(f_3=3n\), with no higher faces.

**2. Integral elementary collapses.** For each rectangle on vertices \(x=(i,a)\), \(y=(i,b)\), \(z=(i+1,a)\), \(w=(i+1,b)\), with \(a<b\), first collapse its tetrahedron \(xyzw\) across triangle \(xyz\), which belongs to no other tetrahedron. Next collapse the remaining triangle \(yzw\) across the now-free diagonal edge \(yz\). These are actual elementary collapses: each distance-two diagonal has exactly its two corner triangles within its unique rectangle. Each straight two-edge path \((i-1,a),(i,a),(i+1,a)\) has its endpoint pair in exactly one allowed triple and in no tetrahedron, so collapse this triangle across its free endpoint edge. These operations are mutually compatible. There are exactly \(2(3n)+3n=9n\) free-face pairs.

The remaining two-dimensional complex \(T_n\) consists of the \(n\) within-column triangles and, for each rectangle, two triangles triangulating its square disk. In addition to original graph edges and one diagonal per rectangle, the only surviving edges are pairs of graph vertices at graph distance at least three, each appearing in no triangle. There are
\[
L_n=\binom{3n}{2}-6n-9n=\binom{3n}{2}-15n
\]
of these long edges: \(6n\) are original graph edges, and the \(9n\) other non-long pairs are precisely the \(3n\) straight-path endpoints and \(6n\) rectangle diagonals. Each long edge can be attached separately between points of the connected two-complex and adds a circle wedge summand. Consequently \(K_n\simeq T_n\vee\bigvee^{L_n}S^1\).

**3. A cyclic graph of two-spheres.** For each base-cycle edge \(\{i,i+1\}\), let \(P_i\) be the boundary of the triangular prism connecting the two triangle columns at \(i\) and \(i+1\), using its two triangular end disks and the three rectangular side disks. Thus \(P_i\cong S^2\) and \(T_n=\bigcup_iP_i\). Consecutive spheres \(P_i,P_{i+1}\) meet in exactly the full triangular disk at column \(i+1\); nonconsecutive spheres do not meet, as \(n\ge5\). There are no triple intersections. Glue the spheres successively along a spanning path of the cyclic intersection graph: attaching an \(S^2\) along a disk preserves the previous wedge and contributes one new \(S^2\). The final attachment closes the cycle of disk intersections and contributes one independent \(S^1\), while preserving the \(n\) sphere classes. Equivalently, contract the contractible intersection disks in the associated graph of spaces. Hence \(T_n\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^nS^2\), so
\[
K_n\simeq\bigvee^{L_n+1}S^1\vee\bigvee^nS^2.
\]
This argument is integral and determines homotopy, not only Betti numbers.

**4. Alexander duality and shellability.** The combinatorial Alexander-duality isomorphism on \(N=3n\) ground vertices is \(\widetilde H_j(\Delta_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{N-j-3}(K_n;\mathbb Z)\). Since the established wedge has torsion-free cohomology, concentrated in degrees one and two of ranks \(L_n+1\) and \(n\), the asserted cut-complex groups follow without torsion. The complements of disconnected triples are equicardinal facets of dimension \(N-4\); since \(\widetilde H_{N-5}(\Delta_n;\mathbb Z)\ne0\), the complex cannot be pure shellable.

## Verification
The included standalone program `verify.py`, requiring only the Python standard library, constructs the dual from the every-triple connectivity condition. For \(5\le n\le10\) it enumerates every triangle, tetrahedron, and potential five-vertex face; verifies all \(9n\) individual elementary free-face collapses against the **current** simplicial face sets; checks the exact residual triangular-prism surface decomposition; and independently computes both relevant mod-two boundary ranks of the original dual, matching the predicted Betti numbers. The accompanying `verify_output.txt` is the output of the supplied program. These finite cases are corroboration only; the universal claim follows from the explicit all-\(n\) proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer and collaborators [1] introduced ordinary graph cut complexes, and their sequel [2] studies path-grid and cycle cut complexes, among other classes. The grid results of [2] concern **rectangular path products**, rather than the periodic triangle–cycle cylinder. The previously published toroidal three-cut theorem [3] concerns \(C_m\square C_n\) with \(m,n\ge5\), which excludes the triangle factor and has a torus dual rather than the sphere-wedge dual proved here. The published triangular-cylinder result [4] studies the **Lovász open-neighborhood complex**, not any cut complex; its torus is a different simplicial invariant. The mixed-alphabet Hamming theorem [5] concerns Cartesian products of at least three **complete graphs**, not a cycle factor. The present classification does not restate any of these results, although it applies analogous geometric square-cell ideas to a different complex and a short-cycle boundary case.

## Limitations
The result assumes \(n\ge5\) and does not classify the exceptional \(n=3,4\) cases, the original complex's full homotopy type or its symmetry representation. The finite tests are not an infinite certificate. The inspected sources and semantic searches do not rule out an unindexed equivalent theorem, particularly one framed as a three-wise-connected simplicial complex. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
