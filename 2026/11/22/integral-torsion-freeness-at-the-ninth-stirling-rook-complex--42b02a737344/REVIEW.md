# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The 45 squares are indexed by 0≤i≤j≤8; the augmented chains include the empty placement. The frozen Morse certificate uses an exhaustive acyclicity check and integer unit eliminations. Its projection can be justified by quotienting out the acyclic subcomplex generated by the matched upper cells and their boundaries: on the lower matched cells the boundary coefficients form a triangular matrix with unit diagonal by acyclicity. I independently enumerated placements by selected rows and injective columns and eliminated the signed original differentials over Z, without any discrete Morse matching. At each pivot, subtract multiples of the pivot column to clear the other source vectors on that row; their remaining coordinates in an incoming chain are unchanged. The chain identity kills the pivot coordinate, so the next original differential is restricted to the surviving rows. In source degrees 0 through 8 this gave respectively 1, 44, 706, 5174, 17653, 24470, 8820, 510 and 1 unit pivots, with zero residual matrices. These primitive boundary images and the face counts give free reduced homology of ranks 402 and 815 in degrees 4 and 5 and zero otherwise. The same direct check for eight rows gives the published ranks 24, 292 and 1.

## originality

PASS

Both inspected versions of Clark–Zeckner stop their table at eight rows and explicitly ask about torsion in the nine-row complex. The strongest identified possible later overlap is Zeckner's dissertation: its abstract includes the family, but lawful full-text retrieval failed, and the abstract gives no result for this case. The distinct claim supported to the best of the inspected evidence is the complete nine-row integral homology, including the resolution of that torsion question.

## value

PASS

Nine rows is the published computational boundary and the exact first case singled out in a torsion question, not an arbitrary slice. A torsion-free certificate over all primes, together with the two precise free ranks, answers that question and supplies a useful benchmark for subsequent work on triangular Ferrers complexes.
