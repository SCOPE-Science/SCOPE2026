# Scientific review of the ninth Stirling rook complex

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is the nine-row strictly staircase Ferrers rook-placement complex with positions \((i,j)\), \(0\le i\le j\le8\). Exhaustive enumeration gives exactly 115975 augmented faces and the specified Stirling-number face counts. An acyclic matching is checked on the entire 597155-edge augmented Hasse diagram. The exact Morse chain groups have ranks 1096, 1728, 220 and 1 in dimensions 4–7. Their boundaries satisfy \(\partial^2=0\) over the integers and diagonalize with 694, 219 and 1 unit Smith pivots and no nonzero residual block. The ranks and lack of torsion follow, not merely from field computations. Published \(\operatorname{St}(8)\) Betti numbers are reproduced independently.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
Ehrenborg–Hetyei's earlier definition and Barmak's connectivity theorem do not give the nine-row integral homology. Clark–Zeckner (2010, Sections 5–6) explicitly report stopping at \(\operatorname{St}(8)\) and ask whether \(\operatorname{St}(9)\) has torsion. The computed answer of two torsion-free groups of ranks 402 and 815 is not a verbal reformulation of their partial Morse description. A later unrelated cubical complex also named Stirling complex is explicitly distinguished. Targeted indexed searches for the exact object, either numbering convention, exact ranks and torsion did not uncover a covering statement. A related 2011 dissertation was accessible only at abstract level and remains an originality risk, as does unindexed literature.

## Value — PASS
This is the first finite case expressly identified as beyond the published homology table and a direct answer to the concrete question of whether three-torsion emerges upon entering the five-by-five Durfee-square range. The absence of torsion is established integrally, not inferred from a few prime-field comparisons. The two independent positive-degree integral ranks also complete the missing natural census entry. A general theory of all staircase sizes is neither required nor claimed.

## Scientific limitations
The finding is an exact finite integral-homology classification, not a homotopy classification. Novelty is best-knowledge relative to inspected and indexed sources, and additional priority verification may be appropriate.
