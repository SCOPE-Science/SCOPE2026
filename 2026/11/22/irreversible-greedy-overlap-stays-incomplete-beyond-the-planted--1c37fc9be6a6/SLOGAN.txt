In the exponentially weighted planted-matching model on \(K_{n,n}\), irreversible first-feasible-edge selection retains a limiting fraction \(f(c)\) of the planted matching, determined by a two-equation fluid system, but loses at least \(1/[2(1+c)]\) for every fixed finite rate \(c>0\). In the identical edge-weight model, global minimum-total-weight matching achieves nearly perfect planted recovery for \(c\ge4\); greedy adsorption therefore has a provable macroscopic kinetic deficit even above that recovery threshold.
