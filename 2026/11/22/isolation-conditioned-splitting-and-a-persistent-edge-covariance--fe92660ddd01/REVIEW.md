# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The conditional law follows from a two-dimensional Dirichlet simplex integral and independent affine rescaling on each side. The factorial-weight ratio and its monotonicity give the two-sided probability, a direct indicator decomposition gives the exact edge-count covariance, and dominated convergence at the triangle's two corners gives both asymptotic constants. All hypotheses and the tagged-conditioning distinction are explicit. Exact-rational exhaustive verification through six intervals checks 168 assertions, not as a replacement for the all-size proof.

The primary 1988 Scheinerman article was not obtained in full and remains a historical-priority risk. Different rare-isolation covariance results concern random geometric circles and fixed-edge graph ensembles, not the random paired-interval model. No identified inspected source implies the tagged two-sided decomposition and its quantitative covariance as stated; originality is qualified, not certified uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

Rare isolation in a dense intersection graph is a natural connectivity defect; knowing the *complete conditional remaining-graph law*, not merely the isolation probability, supplies a reusable fragmentation identity for component and motif statistics. The \(O(n^{-1})\) total-variation coupling explains why the rest of the graph is almost unaffected, while the strictly negative finite edge covariance converging to a nonzero constant demonstrates that unconditional rare-event deletion cannot simply be ignored for unscaled joint moments. These are motivated structural features rather than a parameter substitution.
