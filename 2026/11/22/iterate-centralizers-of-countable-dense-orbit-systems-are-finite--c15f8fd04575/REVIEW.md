# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

Proposition 2.5 and Lemma 2.7 of Pérez-Remolina–Uzcátegui-Aylwin (arXiv:2611.17315v1) give the enumerated isolated orbit and the cyclic phase limits. The proof reconstructs every commuting map from its first \(m\) ray images and derives the necessary and sufficient congruences from sequential continuity. It separately establishes the surjectivity-permutation equivalence and the wreath-product order using the coprimality of \(m/d\) and \(k/d\). The checker exhaustively verifies the finite permutation condition and group cardinality for \(70\) pairs \((k,m)\), but proof of all parameters does not rely on that computation.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

The motivating dense-orbit paper establishes uniqueness up to conjugacy at finite derived rank, but does not compute all centralizers of the time iterates. Earlier papers of García-Ferreira and coauthors (arXiv:1404.6686 and Semigroup Forum 113 (2026)) investigate the Ellis semigroup, a smaller collection of limit iterates; they do not supply the enlarged groups of independently permuted time-sampling rays. Distinct published studies of centralizers in smooth dynamical systems and symbolic shift automorphism groups operate on different systems. Searches of bibliographic literature and related mathematical research records did not find a direct or dominating formula. Unindexed equivalences remain possible; absence of hits is not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Centralizers measure dynamical symmetry, and their change under sampling \(f\mapsto f^m\) is a natural invariant even when the underlying system is unique up to conjugacy. An entire class of previously classified rank-two countable systems acquires explicit finite wreath-product symmetries, while the original system has trivial homeomorphism centralizer. The result also classifies *all* continuous (including noninvertible) commuting maps rather than merely giving a group example.

## Boundaries and reproducibility

The result is not asserted for higher Cantor–Bendixson ranks or general maps without a forward dense orbit. The finitely checked cases are consistency checks. Historical priority remains subject to further specialist review.
