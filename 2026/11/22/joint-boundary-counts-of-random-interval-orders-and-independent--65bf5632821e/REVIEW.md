# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The endpoint-order matching proof counts exactly every feasible matching of three endpoint regions and normalizes by all matchings. The joint Rayleigh limit follows from corner-square avoidance for early right endpoints and late left endpoints, plus conditional binomial concentration. Subgaussian tails yield the claimed moment convergence. The included checker exhausts finite cases through seven intervals and performs rational cross-checks beyond; this is supporting computation, not the proof for all sample sizes.

## Originality: PASS (historical priority qualified)

The inspected open arXiv treatment *Interval graph limits* gives a general model, and *Random Interval Graphs* studies graph degree, clique, and independent-set observables. Neither inspected treatment states the specific bivariate precedence-poset boundary distribution. The 1990 *Random Intervals* paper explicitly addresses disjoint families and universal intersections in its abstract, but its full text was unavailable; this limits confidence in complete historical priority. A prior result for inclusion extrema would not by itself imply this different precedence-poset boundary law.

## Value: PASS

The theorem identifies the exact finite-sample joint law of both natural boundary antichains of an interval precedence order and independent Rayleigh extreme limits. The result describes observable constraints in random interval scheduling beyond marginal independence-number or graph-degree facts.

## Limits

The finite-sample boundary counts can be dependent; asymptotic decorrelation is weaker than an exact finite-size independence claim. No exact unscaled covariance limit is asserted. Results require atomless iid endpoints and are not verified under atoms, nonidentical marginals, or dependent pairs. Older unavailable primary papers may contain an equivalent statement; independent scholarly review is pending.
