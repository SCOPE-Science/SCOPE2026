# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The automorphism matrices follow by induction from \([e_1,e_j]=e_{j+1}\); the coefficient-polynomial group law is checked explicitly. Coprime-action norm splitting reduces group element order and conjugacy to multiplicative truncated-polynomial fixed units, whose \(p\)-power torsion is determined by Frobenius. Burnside's lemma gives the exact joint class-order census. Direct enumeration of group laws, element orders, centralizer-derived conjugacy class counts and selected matrix multiplication all agreed with the formulas. No finite computation is used as an infinite proof. The scope and endpoint conventions are explicit in RESULT.md.

The 2021 cyclic Leibniz automorphism paper supplies the semidirect group structure (Theorem 2.11), not a finite-field joint element-order/conjugacy-class census. The gradings paper supplies maximal-torus context rather than these class counts. Nearest published mathematical search records treat algebra-element orbits, derivation ranks, or different algebra automorphism groups. The new assertion is the evaluated all-order and all-conjugacy-class arithmetic, not a claim to a new abstract group classification. Priority risk: the explicit group structure logically enables derivation of these counts and a general finite-group enumeration theorem might imply them. An unresolved broad prior theorem could change the originality assessment.

## Value — PASS
Element-order and conjugacy-class spectra are intrinsic, natural finite-group invariants; their explicit joint census for an infinite family of nonassociative-algebra automorphism groups is stronger than an isolated small count. The theorem identifies precise cyclotomic/Frobenius divisibility transitions and shows that the group exponent can be unattained, which matters for representations and structural comparisons. The classification is reproducible and independent of the computational range.
