# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The one-interval edge projection has directly integrated mean \(2/3\), variance \(2/45\), and midpoint-coverage covariance \(1/12\). The degenerate order-two remainder has variance \(O(n^2)\). Uniform empirical-process concentration, Bernstein bounds on a mesh, and endpoint occupancy bounds prove \(\chi_n-C_n=o_{L^2}(\sqrt n)\). The resulting bivariate central limit theorem and Gaussian residual independence follow without invoking finite enumeration as an infinite proof. Exact enumeration checks 41,064 labelled endpoint orders for clique-versus-coverage equality, and 15 rational moment equalities for \(n=2,3,4\).

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The 1988 first-order interval-graph results, an analogous random-runs maximum CLT, 2011 interval graph limits, and 2015 thesis do not, in the accessible inspected material, directly give the simultaneous edge–chromatic Gaussian limit. The exact midpoint coverage/edge regression and its limiting squared correlation \(5/8\) are treated as prior material, not new. This finding concerns the nonlinear maximum, the joint weak limit, and asymptotically independent residual. The original 1988 paper could not be inspected in full; possible historical implicit derivations remain a genuine originality risk. Absence in searches is not proof of priority.

## Value — PASS
Simultaneous uncertainty about graph density and resource demand in the standard interval model needs the joint law; separate marginal CLTs and finite regressions do not provide it. The nonzero limiting determinant \(1/240\) and independent residual variance \(1/60\) quantitatively isolate edge-count fluctuation not explained by chromatic-number fluctuation. The object and invariant pair are canonical rather than arbitrarily chosen.

## Scope and limitations
Only independently paired, common-distribution atomless endpoints. The residual independence is asymptotic. The covering-median discrepancy bound is deliberately nonoptimal and does not give a full quantitative bivariate approximation rate. Inaccessible primary historical text leaves a named priority risk. Computation checks finite examples and integral identities only.
