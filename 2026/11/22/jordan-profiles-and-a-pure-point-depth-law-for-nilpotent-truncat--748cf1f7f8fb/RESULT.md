# Jordan profiles and a pure-point depth law for nilpotent truncated substitution endomorphisms

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), with arbitrary prime-power \(q\), and let \(A_N=k[t]/(t^N)\) for each integer \(N\ge3\). Write \(\mathfrak m=(t)\). Each unital algebra endomorphism is determined by \(t\mapsto f(t)\in\mathfrak m\). Denote the ideal of noninvertible endomorphisms by \(\mathcal N_N\), with the zero element meaning the augmentation substitution \(t\mapsto0\), not the zero unital map on all of \(A_N\).

For a nonzero \(f\in\mathfrak m^2\), write \(d=\operatorname{ord}_t(f)\). As \(f\) ranges through the \((q-1)q^{N-1-d}\) substitutions of order \(d\), the nilpotent linear operator \(T_f=\phi_f|_{\mathfrak m}\) has the same *entire Jordan partition*, given by
\[
\rho_r(d)=\operatorname{rank}(T_f^r)=\left\lfloor\frac{N-1}{d^r}\right\rfloor,\quad r\ge0,
\qquad
b_j(d)=\rho_{j-1}(d)-2\rho_j(d)+\rho_{j+1}(d).
\]
The zero substitution has type consisting of \(N-1\) blocks of size one. Consequently, if \(u_j\) marks a block of size \(j\), the complete finite-field profile enumerator is
\[
\mathcal J_{N,q}(u_1,\ldots,u_L)
=u_1^{N-1}+
\sum_{d=2}^{N-1}(q-1)q^{N-1-d}
\prod_{j=1}^L u_j^{b_j(d)},\qquad L=\lceil\log_2N\rceil.
\]
The summands are indexed by the first nonzero degree; if distinct degrees produce the same partition, their coefficients are added.

Under composition, \(\mathcal N_N\) is a nilpotent ideal with precisely \(q^{N-2}\) elements and sharp nilpotency class \(\lceil\log_2N\rceil\). For each nonzero substitution of order \(d\), the least \(r\ge1\) with \(T_f^r=0\) is \(\nu_N(d)=\lceil\log_dN\rceil\), while \(\nu_N(0)=1\). For a uniformly chosen element of \(\mathcal N_N\) and fixed \(q\),
\[
\frac{\nu_N}{\log N}\;\Longrightarrow\;Y,
\qquad
\Pr\!\left(Y=\frac1{\log d}\right)=(q-1)q^{1-d},\quad d=2,3,\ldots.
\]
In particular, \(Y\) is a nondegenerate, purely atomic law with infinitely many atoms accumulating at zero.

## Assumptions and scope

All endomorphisms preserve the \(k\)-algebra identity and the scalar field pointwise. \(N\ge3\) and \(q\) is an arbitrary prime power. A matrix rank is always over \(k\) on the \((N-1)\)-dimensional maximal ideal, not on all of \(A_N\) (where the unit contributes a fixed eigenvalue one). Jordan partitions are nilpotent Jordan types of \(T_f\). The endomorphism with \(f=0\) acts as zero on \(\mathfrak m\).

## Proof

**1. Endomorphism and ideal structure.** The unique maximal ideal is \(\mathfrak m\). Every nilpotent element of \(A_N\) lies in \(\mathfrak m\), so the image of \(t\) under a unital map lies there; conversely, any such image defines a well-defined endomorphism because its \(N\)-th power vanishes. The induced map on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) is multiplication by the linear coefficient of \(f\). Since \(A_N\) is generated by \(t\), this map is invertible exactly when the linear coefficient is nonzero; equivalently, its formal truncated compositional inverse exists. Hence the nonautomorphisms are precisely the \(q^{N-2}\) series in \(\mathfrak m^2\). Their composition with arbitrary endomorphisms on either side still has vanishing linear term, proving the two-sided ideal property.

**2. Multiplication of first nonzero degrees.** Suppose \(f=a_dt^d+O(t^{d+1})\) with \(a_d\ne0\), and similarly \(g=b_et^e+O(t^{e+1})\). Then \(f(g(t))\) has first nonzero term \(a_db_e^dt^{de}\) whenever \(de<N\); it is zero modulo \(t^N\) whenever \(de\ge N\). A product of \(r\) nonautomorphisms therefore vanishes once \(2^r\ge N\), and the repeated substitution \(f=t^2\) realizes a nonzero \(r\)-fold composite whenever \(2^r<N\). The least universally vanishing length is exactly \(\lceil\log_2N\rceil\).

**3. Rank and Jordan partition.** Iterated substitution of a fixed \(f\) with initial degree \(d\) has initial degree \(d^r\) until it vanishes. On the basis \(t,t^2,\ldots,t^{N-1}\) of \(\mathfrak m\), the images under \(T_f^r\) are powers of the iterated series; those with \(jd^r<N\) have distinct nonzero leading degrees \(jd^r\) and are linearly independent. No other images survive. Thus \(\rho_r(d)=\lfloor(N-1)/d^r\rfloor\). Each nilpotent Jordan block of length \(h\) contributes \(\max(h-r,0)\) to the rank of the \(r\)-th power. Taking two successive finite differences proves \(b_j(d)=\rho_{j-1}-2\rho_j+\rho_{j+1}\). The formula is nonnegative because it counts the blocks of an actual nilpotent operator. The zero substitution gives \(T_f=0\).

**4. Multiplicities and probability law.** Exactly \(q-1\) possibilities exist for the first coefficient \(a_d\), and the higher \(N-1-d\) coefficients are free, so the order \(d\) has multiplicity \((q-1)q^{N-1-d}\). Summing from \(d=2\) to \(N-1\), plus the zero series, gives \(q^{N-2}\). For the uniform model,
\[
\Pr(D_N=d)=(q-1)q^{1-d}\ (2\le d<N),\qquad \Pr(D_N=\infty)=q^{-(N-2)}.
\]
Here the symbol \(D_N=\infty\) is assigned to the zero substitution. The infinite discrete law \(\Pr(D=d)=(q-1)q^{1-d}\), \(d\ge2\), has total mass one, and the finite distribution differs from it only by truncating the tail of mass \(q^{-(N-2)}\). Since \(\nu_N(d)/\log N=\lceil\log_dN\rceil/\log N\to1/\log d\) for every fixed \(d\ge2\), controlling the exponentially small tail and then letting the finite cutoff increase proves weak convergence to the asserted law. Distinct positive integers \(d\ge2\) give distinct atoms, and the atom locations approach zero.

## Verification

The included `checker.py` constructs each substitution matrix directly from multiplication in \(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^N)\), computes ranks of its powers by independent finite-field Gaussian elimination, and compares the resulting Jordan block histogram with the formula. The actual `checker_output.txt` records exhaustive checks for \(q=2\) with \(3\le N\le8\), for \(q=3\) with \(5\le N\le8\), and for \((q,N)=(5,6)\), all passing. The checks include the exact order multiplicities and the total nonautomorphism count. These finite computations are corroboration; the preceding argument proves the claims uniformly for every allowed \(q,N\).

## Relationship to prior work

Enochs–Jenda–Ozbek study the untruncated formal-series composition monoid, its order function and support submonoids; their Theorem 2.1 classifies strongly closed support sets. The present finite-truncation Jordan partition and probabilistic limit are different invariants, not an assertion that the elementary order-multiplication principle is new. Bavula studies factorizations of injective polynomial substitutions over characteristic-zero fields and does not furnish this nonautomorphism finite-field census. Related finite-field work on nilpotent Jordan types of *multiplication* operators or on endomorphisms of chain evolution algebras concerns distinct operators or algebras. A general semigroup/representation theorem not inspected here could still subsume the result; mathematical priority is not independently certified.

## Limitations

No statement is made about automorphism (nonzero-linear-coefficient) Jordan types, the conjugacy classification of substitution maps, multivariate truncated rings, or arbitrary coefficient rings with zero divisors. The distribution is for fixed finite field size as \(N\to\infty\), and the proofs use the field property to prevent leading-coefficient cancellation. The classification is a finite-level result, not a classification of formal-series conjugacy classes.
