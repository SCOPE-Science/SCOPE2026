# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A finite monotone partial injection is nilpotent exactly when it has no fixed point: any surviving orbit creates a cycle, and a strictly order-preserving map has no nontrivial cycles. Fixed-point inclusion-exclusion yields the square-root bivariate generating function; its dominant positive singularity is \(\rho(y)=(1+\sqrt y)^{-2}\). Uniform singularity transfer supplies a differentiable logarithmic moment-generating function for every real tilt, and standard large-deviation and moment-generating-function theorems prove the complete rate law and central limit. Independent exhaustive two-subset enumeration over eleven chain sizes checks the finite input recurrence, not the asymptotics.

**Originality — PASS, with a material residual priority risk.** Garba's 1994 papers and Laradji–Umar (2015) already study the *same semigroup*, including fixed-point/height classes, finite nilpotent enumeration and total nilpotence asymptotics. Those are deliberately excluded from the new claim. The assessed contribution is the full conditional-rank large-deviation rate and Gaussian variance, neither of which is stated in the available descriptions or in the accessible corresponding historical thesis chapter. Searches of nearby published mathematical records found Gaussian ranks for *noninjective* partial monotone transformations, partial isometries, and other semigroups, but not this exact law. The full 2015 article was not accessible, leaving an explicit historical-priority risk; the abstract alone is not proof of whole-document noncoverage.

**Value — PASS.** The domain rank is the canonical Green-class parameter of increasing partial injections. Conditioning on nilpotence is a natural semigroup-statistical operation; its rank law distinguishes the sharp typical half-rank, precise Gaussian fluctuation scale and exponential cost of any macroscopic rank deviation. This is more information than the previously studied probability of nilpotence itself, and is meaningful independently of a new finite enumeration.

Closest sources: Garba (1994), Laradji–Umar (2015), OEIS A081696, and Kehinde–Umar (2010). Limitations: finite counting is known; one highly relevant full text remains inaccessible; asymptotic proof is not independently audited.
