# Linear-in-order entrywise instability of nearest correlation projection from a smooth elliptope face

## Finding
Let \(n\ge4\) and let \(\Pi_n\) be Frobenius nearest-point projection of a real symmetric unit-diagonal matrix onto the set \(\mathcal E_n\) of positive-semidefinite unit-diagonal matrices. The input and output perturbation norm is the largest absolute matrix entry. Write \(k=n-2\), \(b=k(k-1)/2\), and
\[
F_k(r)=\frac{2kr+b r^2-1}{1+2kr^2+b r^4}\qquad(r>0).
\]
There is, for **every** \(r>0\), an explicit rank-\((n-1)\) correlation matrix at which the tangent-plane derivative of \(\Pi_n\) has a specified output entry with exact row-\(\ell_1\) norm \(1+F_k(r)\). Hence the global two-input maximum-entry Lipschitz coefficient \(L_n\) obeys
\[
L_n\ge1+\max_{r>0}F_{n-2}(r),\qquad
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_n}{n}\ge\frac{2-\sqrt2}{4}=0.1464466094\ldots.
\]
Together with the universal estimate \(L_n\le(1+\sqrt{n(n-1)/2})/2\) from firm Euclidean nonexpansivity, this proves \(L_n=\Theta(n)\). More exactly, the one-parameter witness family has the asymptotically optimized coefficient
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\max_{r>0}F_k(r)=\frac{2-\sqrt2}{4},
\]
and every maximizing parameter satisfies \(\sqrt{k}\,r_k\to2^{1/4}\). These statements are **lower bounds**, not an assertion of the unrestricted global exact constant. Every claimed local gain is a limit of ratios at two distinct finite input matrices.

## Assumptions and scope
The domain is the affine Euclidean space \(\mathcal A_n=\{A\in\mathbb S^n:A_{ii}=1\}\), with \(\mathcal E_n=\{X\in\mathcal A_n:X\succeq0\}\). Define
\[
\Pi_n(A)=\operatorname*{argmin}_{X\in\mathcal E_n}\|X-A\|_F,
\quad
\|A\|_{\max}=\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}|,
\quad
L_n=\sup_{A\ne B\in\mathcal A_n}
\frac{\|\Pi_n(A)-\Pi_n(B)\|_{\max}}{\|A-B\|_{\max}}.
\]
A symmetric matrix with fixed diagonal is identified with its \(m=n(n-1)/2\) independent upper-triangular off-diagonal entries. Frobenius squared distance between such matrices is twice the squared Euclidean distance in these coordinates; thus \(\Pi_n\) is ordinary Euclidean projection in \(\mathbb R^m\). No restriction that the input be positive semidefinite, or that its entries stay within the correlation range, is imposed. The result concerns the metric projection operator, primary MSC2020 **47H09**.

## Proof
**1. A rank-one-kernel, smooth boundary family.** For arbitrary \(k\ge2\) and \(r>0\), put
\[
a=(1+k^2r^2)^{-1/2},\qquad c=\tfrac12 kr a.
\]
Define the symmetric \(n\)-by-\(n\) matrix \(C=C_{k,r}\) by \(C_{ii}=1\), and, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
C_{12}=2c^2-1,\qquad C_{1j}=C_{2j}=-ca\ (3\le j\le n),
\qquad C_{ij}=\frac{ka^2-1}{k-1}\ (3\le i<j\le n).
\]
The vector \(v=(1,1,r,\ldots,r)^\mathsf T\) is in the kernel, because \(2c=kra\). The invariant subspaces generated by the first-two-coordinate contrast, last-\(k\)-coordinate contrasts, and the two block-constant vectors give the nonzero eigenvalues
\[
2(1-c^2),\qquad \frac{k(1-a^2)}{k-1}\ \text{(multiplicity \(k-1\))},
\qquad 2c^2+ka^2.
\]
The fourth eigenvalue, in the block-constant sector, is zero. All displayed eigenvalues are positive. Therefore \(C\in\mathcal E_n\), \(\operatorname{rank}C=n-1\), and \(\ker C=\mathbb R v\).

**2. Tangent halfspace and its row norm.** The smallest eigenvalue of \(C\) is simple. In a neighborhood of its off-diagonal coordinate vector \(x\), the elliptope is given by the nonnegative smallest-eigenvalue inequality; the other eigenvalues remain strictly positive. The eigenvalue derivative on a symmetric zero-diagonal perturbation \(H\) is
\[
D\lambda_{\min}(C)[H]=\frac{v^\mathsf THv}{v^\mathsf Tv}
=\frac{2}{v^\mathsf Tv}\sum_{i<j}v_i v_jH_{ij}.
\]
Consequently, writing \(q_{ij}=v_iv_j\) for the independent off-diagonal coordinates, the tangent cone is the halfspace \(T=\{h:\langle q,h\rangle\ge0\}\). Let \(e=(1,2)\) be the distinguished coordinate. Here \(q_e=1\), exactly \(2k\) remaining entries equal \(r\), and exactly \(b\) entries equal \(r^2\). Thus
\[
\|q\|_2^2=1+2kr^2+br^4=:Q,\qquad
\sum_f q_f=1+2kr+br^2=:S.
\]
On the outside halfspace the Euclidean tangent projection has derivative \(H=I-qq^\mathsf T/Q\). The \(e\)-row has diagonal entry \(1-1/Q\) and remaining entries \(-q_f/Q\), so its exact absolute row sum is
\[
1-\frac1Q+\frac{S-1}{Q}
=1+\frac{S-2}{Q}=1+F_k(r).
\]

**3. Turning the tangent calculation into genuine two-input sensitivity.** For any nonempty closed convex \(E\) and \(x\in E\), scaled sets \((E-x)/t\) converge as \(t\downarrow0\) to the closed tangent cone \(T_E(x)\). Their Euclidean projections converge pointwise; scaling then gives
\[
P_E(x+th)=x+tP_{T_E(x)}h+o(t)
\quad\text{for every fixed }h.
\]
Let \(s_e=1\) and \(s_f=-1\) for all \(f\ne e\), and choose any \(M>0\) sufficiently large that both \(h_0=-Mq\) and \(h_1=h_0+s\) satisfy \(\langle q,h_i\rangle<0\). Projection onto \(T\) of either vector is its orthogonal projection to \(q^\perp\). Thus \(\|h_1-h_0\|_\infty=1\) and the distinguished output difference equals the sum of the absolute entries of the \(e\)-row of \(H\), namely \(1+F_k(r)\). Applying the preceding directional limit to \(x+th_0\) and \(x+th_1\) proves the asserted lower bound using distinct finite matrices for each sufficiently small \(t>0\).

**4. Exact asymptotic optimization of the witness family.** Put \(r=s/\sqrt{k}\). Then
\[
\frac{F_k(s/\sqrt{k})}{k}=
\frac{2s/\sqrt{k}+(k-1)s^2/(2k)-1/k}{1+2s^2+(k-1)s^4/(2k)}
\ \longrightarrow\ f(s)=\frac{s^2}{2+4s^2+s^4}.
\]
The convergence is uniform on compact positive \(s\)-intervals. For small \(s\), the numerator divided by \(k\) is bounded above by \(2s/\sqrt{k}+s^2/2\); for large \(s\), the denominator dominates a fixed positive multiple of \(s^4\) for all \(k\ge2\), while the numerator is at most \(2s/\sqrt{k}+s^2/2\). Hence no maximizing sequence escapes to zero or infinity after this scaling. Writing \(y=s^2\), the derivative of \(y/(2+4y+y^2)\) has the sign of \(2-y^2\). Its unique maximizer is \(y=\sqrt2\), so \(s=2^{1/4}\) and
\[
\max_{s>0}f(s)=\frac{\sqrt2}{4+4\sqrt2}=\frac{2-\sqrt2}{4}.
\]
This proves both the exact family asymptotic and the maximizing-scale statement. Since \(n=k+2\), the same constant lower-bounds \(\liminf L_n/n\).

**5. The matching order upper bound.** For any Euclidean metric projector, firm nonexpansivity gives \(\|g\|_2^2\le\langle g,h\rangle\), where \(h\) is the input difference and \(g\) is the projected-output difference. Equivalently, \(\|g-h/2\|_2\le\|h\|_2/2\). Thus for any coordinate \(e\), \( |g_e|\le(|h_e|+\|h\|_2)/2\). In the \(m=n(n-1)/2\) independent off-diagonal coordinates with \(\|h\|_\infty=1\), it follows that \(L_n\le(1+\sqrt m)/2\). Combining this with the constructed lower bound yields \(L_n=\Theta(n)\).

## Verification
The standalone file `check.py` reproduces the rational row identities and rank-\((n-1)\) Gram certificates for 161 positive rational parameter cases in orders \(4\) through \(26\), as well as supplementary asymptotic and derivative-sign checks. For illustration, at \(n=4\) and \(r=3/4\), the exact row gain is \(1+F_2(3/4)=1569/913\), approximately \(1.718510405\). A separate numerical nearest-correlation test at this point gave finite-pair ratios approaching this row gain as the perturbation scale decreased; this numerical observation is not needed for the infinite-family proof. The analytical eigenvalue decomposition, tangent-cone argument, and uniform asymptotic maximization are the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Higham and Strabić (2016; first public preprint 2015) analyze estimates of the **distance** from a matrix to its nearest correlation matrix; their problem formulation and error estimates do not themselves provide a two-input maximum-entry Lipschitz lower bound. Armijo, Bello-Cruz, and Haeser (first public arXiv version January 9, 2024) investigate semismooth Newton theory for general projection equations with the nearest correlation matrix as an application. Im, Jung, Moursi, Torregrosa-Belin, and Wolkowicz (first public arXiv version July 8, 2024) study projection, Jacobian conditioning, and degeneracy of spectrahedra, with an explicit elliptope application. The inspected sections of these two papers do not state this rank-one-kernel coordinate amplification construction. General firm nonexpansivity bounds this maximum-entry coefficient by \((1+\sqrt{n(n-1)/2})/2\), and is not contradicted by the lower bound. The proposed claim is the explicit **linear-in-matrix-order lower bound**, not a new general upper bound or a novel algorithm for nearest correlation matrices.

## Limitations
The exact unrestricted \(L_n\) is not determined, nor is the asymptotically best family among all elliptope boundary configurations. The explicit constant is optimal only in the stated one-parameter witness family. Infinitesimal boundary sensitivity yields arbitrarily close ratios for distinct finite inputs, but no finite-pair equality is claimed. The cited literature and database checks do not certify exhaustive historical priority, and independent verification has not been performed.
