# Scientific assessment

The uniform inequality and construction follow from the exact undirected closed-ball identity, the empty-row/column argument, and three explicit templates. The separation proof handles both orders of words spanning two blocks: a shared signature with the order reversed would force a row support to equal a column support inside a three-symbol template. The construction takes linear time in the alphabet size when listing pairs of labels.

Horan's undirected existence theorem leaves efficient construction and minimum size as questions. Horan, Adachi and Bak compute exact finite minima, including 4, 5 and 6 for the first three alphabets under discussion. The supplementary exclusions reproduce these prior table entries rather than establish new minima. The uniform bound and explicit construction do not follow from those finite computations or the directed-neighborhood results of Boutin, Horan Goliber and Pelto. Equivalences outside the inspected literature are not excluded.

The explicit family has at most four-thirds the optimum number of selected vertices on networks with quadratically many vertices in the alphabet size. The result concerns this design guarantee; it does not claim an exact all-alphabet optimum.
