# Scientific assessment: cyclic trinomial roughness variance

## Correctness — PASS

The variance coefficient is identified by the multinomial central limit theorem and fourth-moment uniform integrability. This works even at zero-probability boundary categories. In three categories the cyclic absolute-difference sum equals twice the Gaussian coordinate range. The reflection-symmetric probability line reduces exactly to two independent normal contrasts; polar integration supplies both first and second moments of the resulting range. Direct symbolic differentiation gives the uniform law's negative second derivative, and permutation symmetry lifts it to a negative-definite tangent Hessian on the entire two-dimensional simplex. The two-category boundary value and the three shape-theoretic consequences follow by explicit comparison. The packaged code passed 26 symbolic, quadrature and exact-trinomial-enumeration assertions, but finite numerical checks are not substituted for the Gaussian-limit and Hessian proofs.

## Originality — PASS against inspected sources, with residual priority risk

The motivating Part 5 by Banić and Elezović supplies cyclic matched-variance technology for fixed interior profiles, with Proposition 5.4 stated for at least five categories; it does not include the three-category Hessian or boundary-majorization classification. Part 4 instead treats the open chain (two comparisons in three categories), which is not equivalent to cyclic range variance. Bonetti–Cirillo–Ogay (2019) gives count order-statistic distributions, and its discussion of Corrado's (2011) arbitrary-probability matrix algorithm could be used in principle to calculate finite range moments, but neither inspected account proves this exact limiting curvature, competing boundary value, or non-quasi-concavity. Targeted searches for the explicit theorem, aliases involving trivariate Gaussian range variance, and majorization found no matching result. The original Corrado article was inaccessible and remains a material historical-priority risk. Absence of a retrieved duplicate is not a uniqueness or certification proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Matched-centered cyclic roughness is a natural histogram-noise statistic, whose leading variance calibrates stochastic fluctuations and goodness-of-fit behavior. The three-bin cycle is the smallest nontrivial closed histogram, in which all three centered contrasts are dependent. The finding identifies a non-obvious feature of this natural variance functional: uniformity is stable as a strict local maximum, yet an arbitrarily close approximation to a two-cell boundary has larger variance. Exact curvature and an explicit strict boundary contrast demonstrate failure of both directions of majorization monotonicity and of quasi-concavity. This is a structural obstruction relevant to optimizing or bounding roughness variance under unknown category probabilities, not merely a recalculation of a known point value.
