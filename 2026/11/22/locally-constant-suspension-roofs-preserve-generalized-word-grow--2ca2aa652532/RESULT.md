# Locally constant suspension roofs preserve generalized word-growth under bounded doubling

## Finding
Let \(\Lambda\subseteq A^{\mathbb Z}\) be a nonempty two-sided subshift over a finite alphabet. Write \(p_\Lambda(n)=|\mathcal L_n(\Lambda)|\), and let \(r:\Lambda\to(0,\infty)\) be **any positive locally constant roof**, with no arithmetic restriction on its values and no cohomology hypothesis. Let \(\Phi_r\) be the suspension flow. For every real \(t\ne0\), there are constants \(c,C>0\) for which the generalized entropy of the time map obeys
\[
\llbracket p_\Lambda(\lfloor cn\rfloor)\rrbracket\ \le\ o(\Phi_r^t)\ \le\ \llbracket p_\Lambda(\lceil Cn\rceil)\rrbracket.
\]
Here finitely many initial indices and fixed additive shifts in word length do not affect the growth orders. In particular, if \(\sup_{n\ge1}p_\Lambda(2n)/p_\Lambda(n)<\infty\), then
\[
\boxed{o(\Phi_r^t)=\llbracket p_\Lambda(n)\rrbracket\quad\text{for every }t\ne0.}
\]
This includes roofs not continuously cohomologous to a constant. For example, on the minimal binary substitution shift generated by \(0\mapsto0001\), \(1\mapsto0111\), the roof \(r(x)=1+(\sqrt2-1)x_0\) has noncommensurate heights and is not cohomologous to any constant, yet every nonzero time map has generalized entropy \(\llbracket n\rrbracket\).

## Assumptions and scope
The suspension is \(M_r=(\{(x,s):x\in\Lambda,0\le s\le r(x)\})/((x,r(x))\sim(\sigma x,0))\); translation along the real coordinate defines a continuous flow \(\Phi_r^u\) for \(u\in\mathbb R\). The roof is continuous, strictly positive, and depends on a finite coordinate window, but its values may be arbitrary positive real numbers. No minimality, mixing, or positive entropy is required for the general bounds. For a compact homeomorphism \(T\), its generalized entropy \(o(T)\) is the supremum of growth orders of its \((n,\varepsilon)\)-separated-set cardinalities as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), in the Dedekind–MacNeille completion of the multiplicative-comparability order of positive sequences (as in Corrêa–Lacerda). Denote by \(\llbracket a(n)\rrbracket\) the order of \(a\), with \(a\preceq b\) when \(a(n)\le K b(n)\) eventually for some \(K\).

## Proof
**1. Reduction to a one-symbol roof.** Because \(r\) is locally constant on the compact shift space, it depends on \(x_{-a},\ldots,x_b\) for fixed nonnegative integers \(a,b\). Replace \(x\) by the higher-block coding \(y_i=x_{[i-a,i+b]}\). This is a conjugacy of the subshifts that identifies their suspension flows with matching real-time parameters, and the new roof depends only on \(y_0\). If the new alphabet is \(B\), then \(p_{\widehat\Lambda}(N)=p_\Lambda(N+a+b)\). By replacing \(\Lambda\) with \(\widehat\Lambda\) we may therefore assume \(r(x)=R(x_0)\). Write \(0<m\le R(b)\le M<\infty\).

**2. Lower bound at a fixed separation scale.** For positive \(t\), choose one point \(x^w\in\Lambda\) extending each admissible length-\(N\) word \(w\), and set \(z_w=[x^w,0]\). If \(w\ne v\), let \(j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) be their first differing position. The roofs on positions \(0,\ldots,j-1\) agree. At the common physical time \(\tau=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}R(w_i)\le(N-1)M\), the respective images of \(z_w,z_v\) are \([\sigma^j x^w,0]\) and \([\sigma^j x^v,0]\), whose zeroth symbols differ. Since the cross-section \(x\mapsto[x,0]\) is a topological embedding of a compact space, there is a common \(\eta>0\) separating all such pairs (if the alphabet has only one occurring symbol, the lower bound is trivial). Uniform continuity of \((z,u)\mapsto\Phi_r^u z\) for \(0\le u\le t\) yields \(\varepsilon_0>0\): separation by \(\eta\) at any time \(\tau\in[kt,(k+1)t]\) forces separation by \(\varepsilon_0\) at time \(kt\). Take \(N=\max(1,\lfloor tn/(2M)\rfloor)\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\tau<(n-1)t\), so the \(p_\Lambda(N)\) points form an \((n,\varepsilon_0)\)-separated set for the time-\(t\) map. Thus
\[
\operatorname{sep}_{\Phi_r^t,\varepsilon_0}(n)\ge p_\Lambda(\lfloor tn/(2M)\rfloor).
\]

**3. Uniform upper bound.** Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). The map \(P(x,v)=\Phi_r^v[x,0]\) is uniformly continuous on \(\Lambda\times[-1,M+1]\). Hence there exist an integer \(L\ge1\) and \(\delta\in(0,1)\) such that \(D(P(x,v),P(y,v'))<\varepsilon\) whenever \(x_{[-L,L]}=y_{[-L,L]}\), \(|v-v'|<\delta\), and \(v,v'\in[-1,M+1]\). Fix
\[
N_n=\lceil tn/m\rceil+\lceil M/m\rceil+L+3.
\]
Cover the suspension by sets indexed by an admissible symbolic block on \([-L,N_n+L]\) and one of finitely many \(\delta/2\)-mesh phase cells in \([0,M]\) (using the letter at index zero to restrict the phase to \([0,R(x_0))\)). In each nonempty set choose a representative point. For two members with the same block and phases within \(\delta\), the symbol-wise roof values on the entire needed forward segment coincide. At every \(0\le u\le(n-1)t\), use the common roof sums to express both positions as \(P(\sigma^j x,v)\), \(P(\sigma^j y,v')\), where \(0\le j\le\lceil (M+(n-1)t)/m\rceil\), \(|v-v'|<\delta\), and the symbolic positions still agree on \([-L,L]\). They are therefore within \(\varepsilon\). This gives an \((n,\varepsilon)\)-spanning set with at most
\[
\operatorname{span}_{\Phi_r^t,\varepsilon}(n)\le K_\varepsilon\,p_\Lambda(\lceil tn/m\rceil+\lceil M/m\rceil+3L+4)
\]
representatives, where \(K_\varepsilon\) depends only on the fixed finite phase mesh. Enlarging the length to \(\lceil Cn\rceil\) for any \(C>t/m\) absorbs the additive constant for large \(n\).

**4. Growth orders.** Standard separated/spanning inequalities give the two growth-order bounds from steps 2 and 3. Any positive fixed rescaling of the argument preserves the order of \(p_\Lambda(n)\) when \(p_\Lambda\) is nondecreasing and has bounded doubling: for \(c>0\), iterating the doubling inequality bounds \(p_\Lambda(n)\) by a constant multiple of \(p_\Lambda(\lfloor cn\rfloor)\), and similarly in the reverse direction; integer-rounding changes nothing after sufficiently large \(n\). This proves the equality. For \(t<0\), generalized separated counts are unchanged by replacing a homeomorphism with its inverse (the orbit segments are the same in reverse order), so the proof applies with \(|t|\).

**5. A non-cohomologous irrational-height example.** Define the primitive constant-length substitution \(\vartheta(0)=0001\), \(\vartheta(1)=0111\). Its fixed point \(u=\lim_{k\to\infty}\vartheta^k(0)\) is uniformly recurrent, and the associated two-sided subshift is minimal. The substitution matrix has eigenvalues \(4,2\), and the word \(\vartheta^k(0)\) has length \(4^k\), with \((4^k-2^k)/2\) ones. Its limiting frequency of ones is \(1/2\), while its prefix discrepancy is \(-2^{k-1}\), which is unbounded. Thus \(u\) is aperiodic. The same substitution decomposition shows for \(4^k\le n<4^{k+1}\) that every factor of length \(n\) lies inside a pair of adjacent \(\vartheta^{k+1}\)-supertiles, giving \(p_\Lambda(n)\le4\cdot4^{k+1}\le16n\). Morse–Hedlund gives \(p_\Lambda(n)\ge n+1\). Therefore \(p_\Lambda(n)=\Theta(n)\). Choose a bi-infinite extension \(x\in\Lambda\) of the nonnegative coordinates of \(u\), which exists by compactness and uniform recurrence. For \(r(x)=1+(\sqrt2-1)x_0\), its Birkhoff sum along the above prefixes is
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{4^k-1}r(\sigma^i x)=\frac{1+\sqrt2}{2}\,4^k-\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2}\,2^k.
\]
If \(r=c+h-h\circ\sigma\) for a continuous \(h\), then every such sum differs from \(c4^k\) by at most \(2\|h\|_\infty\). Dividing by \(4^k\) forces \(c=(1+\sqrt2)/2\), after which the discrepancy diverges: contradiction. Hence this roof is not cohomologous to a constant. The main theorem yields \(o(\Phi_r^t)=\llbracket n\rrbracket\) for every \(t\ne0\).

## Verification
The finite checker `checks.py` verifies the substitution-count identities through depth eight and tests factor-complexity inequalities on several finite lengths. The infinite suspension theorem is established by the proof above, not by finite checks. The only outside standard inputs are compact uniform continuity, higher-block conjugacy, primitive-substitution uniform recurrence, Morse–Hedlund, and the separated/spanning definition of generalized entropy. The proof applies to arbitrary positive real-valued finite-window roofs; it neither computes an exact scalar topological entropy for exponential-complexity systems nor addresses arbitrary continuous roofs.

## Relationship to prior work
Corrêa and Lacerda, *Minimal dynamics with polynomial and intermediate complexity* (2026), Theorem C, establish the precise growth order for **constant** roof suspensions. Their Remark 5.8 notes the cohomologous-to-constant case and explicitly leaves the general variable-roof question unresolved. The present statement proves the bounded-doubling equality for every positive **locally constant** roof, including an explicit non-cohomologous roof with irrationally related heights. Classical symbol expansion/flow equivalence deals with recodings and, for commensurate roof lengths, can reduce to constant roof via finite symbol expansion; the real-valued noncommensurate theorem here requires the uniform physical-time argument. Earlier work on symbolic flow extensions (e.g. Gutman–Shi) treats existence of symbolic models rather than this precise generalized word-growth invariant. The method uses standard symbolic tools but does not claim those tools as new.

## Limitations
No conclusion is proved for general continuous roofs lacking finite-window dependence; errors in their Birkhoff sums need not be uniformly controllable from a fixed-length symbolic block. The non-cohomologous example is not evidence that **all** continuous roofs have the same growth order. The literature comparison did not exhaust all historical papers on suspension-growth invariants, and absence from targeted databases is not a proof of absolute priority. There has been no independent mathematical audit.
