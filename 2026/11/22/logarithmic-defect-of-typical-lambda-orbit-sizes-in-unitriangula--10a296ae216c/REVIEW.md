# Scientific review

### Correctness
PASS. The orbit is exactly an affine translate of the principal left ideal; its independent row-block dimensions sum the suffix ranks. Adjoining a random triangular row gives the exact rank-defect two-branch transfer, which gives a finite verification of the integrated-rank exponent. An independent group-action enumerator verifies all orbit classes over prime fields in the stated finite ranges. Geometric holding times supply uniform logarithmic-defect tails, proving the infinite asymptotic rather than inferring it from finite checks.

### Originality
PASS relative to inspected records, not an external priority certificate. The matrix left action and its row operations are already in Yan (2010); no novelty is claimed for these preliminaries. Properzi–Van Antwerpen (2023) define and study lambda-orbit graphs, but do not state the quantitative n-log-n typical-size law; their orbit-length framework motivates the statistic. Van Peski (2024) analyzes terminal ranks of random triangular matrices, not the integrated rank deficit controlling the typical lambda-orbit exponent. Indexed algebra findings on upper-triangular products, coadjoint orbits and brace nilpotency are not sufficient to imply the asserted quantitative typicality estimate directly. An older general orbit-counting or template theorem might give an equivalent specialization and is a residual priority risk.

### Value
PASS. The logarithm of a random brace lambda-orbit is a motivated statistic beyond the graph connectivity invariant. The result proves a quantitative nontrivial second-order \(n\log n\) typical-size correction obtained from stochastic rank defects.

### Closest sources and limits
