# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is for every depth and every common positive threshold. The piecewise-affine Jacobian argument applies on each strict shrinkage chamber and realizes every subset projector; the finite-chamber segment bound and local perturbation give exact equality of the two-input modulus with the largest projector norm. The elementary step decomposition reduces the path-variation operator seminorm to one jump; orthogonality leaves exactly one potentially relevant Haar detail at each dyadic scale. Its signed step response is computed directly from the Haar inner product. An exact, all-depth finite maximum follows. Triangle bounds give the \(2d\) upper bound; disjoint gradient supports at scales separated by four prove the \(\lfloor d/4\rfloor\) lower bound, including the odd-depth duplication argument. Integer enumeration checks all cuts and masks for depths one to eight, but is not used as a substitute for these all-depth proofs. Mathematical normalization is unweighted open-path variation, not circular BV or Euclidean norm.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The earlier fixed-Haar-mask BV criterion covers the binary question of which *individual* coefficient-suppression projectors are contractive, but neither computes the supremum over all masks and cuts nor proves a logarithmically divergent resolution law. A published exact signed Haar endpoint theorem is an \(L^\infty\) norm for signs \(\pm1\), not this BV seminorm or \(0/1\) shrinkage-Jacobian problem. The 2004 multiscale diffusion-equivalence article and the 2005 single-scale variation-diminishing study were inspected materially and their different quantified statements compared. Donoho–Johnstone's introductory soft-shrinkage article was only abstract-accessible, and Wojtaszczyk's BV approximation article was abstract-accessible; those access limitations and possible implicit earlier equivalents are residual historical priority risks. No finite or failed search is treated as a proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
Coefficientwise soft thresholding is the canonical nonlinear wavelet denoising map. Its precise two-input perturbation response in first-difference variation is independently useful from one-input oscillation diminution and standard Euclidean nonexpansivity. The result supplies a finite exact computable modulus at any depth, sharp low-depth benchmarks, the first expansive resolution, and the unbounded logarithmic growth mechanism. The growth law is structurally meaningful rather than an isolated parameter recomputation; it gives a quantitative warning for BV sensitivity of fine-resolution Haar reconstruction.

## Scope and limitations
The exact all-depth maximum is an algorithmic expression, not a simplified symbolic closed form. The finite integer enumeration establishes only the eight tabulated values, whereas the logarithmic bounds have analytical proofs. The result is limited to real equally spaced powers-of-two samples, open-path variation, common positive soft threshold, and the aligned Haar system. Priority remains contingent on broader archival verification.
