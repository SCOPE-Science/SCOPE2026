# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The stationary Gaussian conditional variance is trapped in the strict interval \([D/b,D/a]\); a deterministic stiff residence gives a uniform, positive bound on the variance gap at each soft-phase entrance. Stationary renewal age has an integrated-survival tail with index \(1-\beta\). This yields an exact leading small-gap distribution and a one-power-smaller stiff-phase contribution. The logarithmically slowly varying gap law transfers to a sharp spatial-density equivalent by a Gaussian-mixture Laplace argument. The included numerical quadrature and near-edge bounds support, but do not replace, the proof. The original regular-variation assumption is sufficient without a density for the sojourn law.

## Originality

PASS with priority qualification. The 2026 two-stiffness study gives general age-structured integral equations and second moments, but its explicit asymptotic spatial-tail theorem concerns zero soft stiffness, where the spatial tail is algebraic. The current positive/positive model instead gives a specific logarithmic correction to a Gaussian envelope with its full coefficient. The 2024 on/off Markov study is also outside the claim. A published heavy-tailed run-and-tumble boundary-concentration result has a related logarithmic mechanism, but its bounded active-particle support and lack of Brownian Gaussian mixing do not imply this unbounded Gaussian-density equivalent. Remaining risk includes equivalent unindexed general Gaussian-mixture/regular-variation statements and primary full texts not fully accessible.

## Value

PASS. Keeping even weak positive confinement changes the *type* of large-displacement tail relative to unconfined renewal phases. The exact logarithmic factor is not a decorative normalization: it preserves the soft-residence survival exponent in an otherwise Gaussian-looking rare-displacement law. This gives a motivated diagnostic for hidden residence-time memory and clarifies the nonuniform zero-confinement boundary. The result is limited to a concrete natural two-stiffness protocol, not claimed as a universal switching theorem.
