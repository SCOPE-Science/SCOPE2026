# Low-degree determinantal rigidity for Fermat-pullback singular surfaces

## Finding
Let \(S=\mathbb C[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\), and fix integers \(a\ge 2\), \(m\ge 5\). Let \(g_1,g_2,g_3\in S_a\) be a homogeneous regular sequence. Write \(J=(\partial g_i/\partial x_j)\) for its \(3\times4\) Jacobian matrix. Suppose the four signed maximal minors \(\Delta_j=(-1)^j\det J_{\widehat j}\) have greatest common divisor \(1\). Set
\[
F=g_1^m+g_2^m+g_3^m,\qquad
\Delta=(\Delta_0,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3).
\]
Define \(AR(F)_r\) as the vector space of homogeneous coefficient vectors \((A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3)\in S_r^4\) satisfying \(\sum A_j\partial F/\partial x_j=0\), with \(S_t=0\) for \(t<0\). Then **every** graded Jacobian syzygy through degree \((m-2)a\) is a polynomial multiple of this one determinantal syzygy:
\[
\boxed{AR(F)_r=S_{r-3a+3}\,\Delta\quad\text{for every integer }0\le r\le(m-2)a.}
\]
In particular, throughout this whole range,
\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb C} AR(F)_r=
\begin{cases}0,&r<3a-3,\\
\binom{r-3a+6}{3},&3a-3\le r\le(m-2)a.
\end{cases}}
\]
Thus the unique minimal-degree syzygy is the maximal-minor vector up to scalars and \(\operatorname{mdr}(F)=3a-3\). The **new structural claim** is the complete initial graded module and unique-generator assertion, not the bare minimum-degree equality, which also follows from the existing general Arnold-exponent bound.

For a general ordered triple of degree-\(a\) forms, the hypotheses hold, and \(V(F)\subset\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}\) is an integral surface with exactly \(a^3\) isolated singularities. Each singularity has analytic equation \(u^m+v^m+w^m=0\); its Milnor and Tjurina numbers both equal \((m-1)^3\). Consequently the total Tjurina number is \(a^3(m-1)^3\).

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over \(\mathbb C\) in characteristic zero. Regular sequence means \(\operatorname{ht}(g_1,g_2,g_3)=3\) in the four-variable polynomial ring. Coprime minors means there is no nonconstant polynomial dividing all four maximal minors, equivalently that \(\operatorname{rank}J\le2\) has no divisorial component. The initial-module statement holds for every triple with those two properties, not merely for general triples. Geometric integrality and the exact isolated-singularity census are asserted only for general triples in the parameter space \(S_a^3\). No claims are made about the full syzygy module in degrees larger than \((m-2)a\).

## Proof
**1. The determinantal syzygy.** By cofactor expansion, \(J\Delta^{\mathsf T}=0\). The partial derivatives of \(F\) satisfy
\[
\nabla F=m\bigl(g_1^{m-1}\nabla g_1+g_2^{m-1}\nabla g_2+g_3^{m-1}\nabla g_3\bigr).
\]
Thus \(\Delta\in AR(F)_{3a-3}\). The gcd hypothesis in particular guarantees \(\Delta\ne0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}J=3\) over the fraction field.

**2. No other early syzygies.** Fix \(0\le r\le(m-2)a\) and \(A\in AR(F)_r\). Define \(q_i=\sum_{j=0}^3 A_j\partial g_i/\partial x_j\), of degree \(r+a-1\). The chain rule gives \(\sum_{i=1}^3g_i^{m-1}q_i=0\). Powers of a homogeneous regular sequence are again a regular sequence; the first syzygies on \((g_1^{m-1},g_2^{m-1},g_3^{m-1})\) are generated by Koszul relations. Accordingly each \(q_i\) lies in the ideal \((g_1^{m-1},g_2^{m-1},g_3^{m-1})\). Because
\[
r+a-1\le(m-1)a-1<\deg(g_i^{m-1}),
\]
the degree-\((r+a-1)\) part of that ideal is zero. Hence \(q_1=q_2=q_3=0\), so \(JA^{\mathsf T}=0\).

Over the fraction field of \(S\), the kernel of the rank-three matrix \(J\) is the line generated by \(\Delta\), and hence \(A=H\Delta\) for some rational function \(H\). Since the four entries of \(\Delta\) are coprime in the UFD \(S\), a denominator of \(H\) would divide every entry; thus \(H\in S\). Homogeneity forces \(H\in S_{r-3a+3}\). This proves the module identity. Its dimension is the number of degree-\((r-3a+3)\) monomials in four variables. Since \(3a-3\le(m-2)a\) for \(m\ge5\), the same formula establishes minimal degree \(3a-3\) and uniqueness at that degree.

**3. Generic hypotheses.** For every fixed \(P\in\mathbb P^3\), evaluation of first jets of a degree-\(a\) form is surjective for \(a\ge1\). The incidence of triples for which the \(3\times4\) matrix \(J(P)\) has rank at most two has codimension two in the parameter-space fiber. Adding the three-dimensional parameter \(P\), the generic rank-drop locus in \(\mathbb P^3\) has dimension at most one, hence cannot contain a divisor. This proves the gcd condition generically. Three general degree-\(a\) hypersurfaces meet transversely in \(a^3\) reduced points by Bertini and Bézout; their equations form a regular sequence.

**4. Generic singularity census.** Every point of the complete intersection \(B=V(g_1,g_2,g_3)\) is singular on \(V(F)\). At these transverse points, \(g_1,g_2,g_3\) are independent local analytic coordinates \(u,v,w\), so the germ is exactly \(u^m+v^m+w^m\). The gradient ideal is \((u^{m-1},v^{m-1},w^{m-1})\); its colength, and also the Tjurina colength, equal \((m-1)^3\). Its projectivized tangent cone is a smooth Fermat plane curve, hence this is an ordinary isolated \(m\)-fold surface point.

There are no other singular points for a general triple. Indeed fix \(P\) outside \(B\). In the freely varying triple of first jets at \(P\), the nonzero values \((g_1(P),g_2(P),g_3(P))\) satisfy one independent equation \(F(P)=0\); the three tangent derivatives of \(F\) give three more independent linear constraints, because at least one coefficient \(g_i(P)^{m-1}\) is nonzero. Hence the incidence of singular points outside \(B\) has codimension four for fixed \(P\); allowing \(P\) to move in dimension three, its projection is a proper closed subset after taking the relevant projective incidence closure away from the transverse base locus. Thus the general surface is smooth off \(B\). A reducible or nonreduced projective surface in \(\mathbb P^3\) has a positive-dimensional singular set (intersection of components, or a repeated component), contrary to the established isolated singularities. Therefore \(F\) is irreducible.

## Verification
The accompanying `check.py` independently uses exact rational polynomial arithmetic and exact Gaussian elimination over the prime field of order \(101\) on the concrete degree-ten example
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_1&=x_0^2+x_1x_2+2x_3^2,\\
g_2&=x_1^2+x_0x_3+3x_2x_3,\\
g_3&=x_2^2+x_0x_1+5x_1x_3,\\
F&=g_1^5+g_2^5+g_3^5.
\end{aligned}
\]
It verifies that the four minors have gcd one, that both \(J\Delta^{\mathsf T}=0\) and \(\Delta\cdot\nabla F=0\) are exact polynomial identities, and that the eight complete-intersection base points are transverse by exact Gröbner calculations in the chart \(x_3=1\) (there are none on \(x_3=0\)). The \(\mathbb F_{101}\)-kernel dimensions of the homogeneous Jacobian map at coefficient degrees \(r=0,1,2,3,4,5,6\) are respectively \(0,0,0,1,4,10,20\), exactly as the formula predicts. These finite calculations illustrate the result; the proof for all degrees and all parameters in scope is algebraic and does not rely on extrapolation from tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Dimca and Sticlaru, *On the degree of the singular subscheme of hypersurfaces in projective space* (arXiv:2604.24308, first public April 27, 2026), determine general relationships between graded Betti data, singular-scheme dimensions, and degrees. The present result gives a concrete exact initial graded Jacobian-syzygy module for a natural infinite family of integral singular surfaces; those general identities alone do not specify its graded vector spaces or its determinantal generator.

The classical lower bound involving the minimal Arnold exponent, as discussed by Dimca (arXiv:1408.2244), already yields \(\operatorname{mdr}(F)\ge3a-3\) for the generic members, and the minors give the reverse inequality. The equality itself is therefore not claimed as independently new. The finer all-degrees-through-\((m-2)a\) statement is different. Dimca–Sticlaru (arXiv:1407.0168) characterize Koszul relations via the singular subscheme; Andrade, Beorchia, Dimca and Miró-Roig (arXiv:2502.06290) give a qualitative Jacobian-syzygy criterion for quasihomogeneity. Neither states this concrete initial module. Sticlaru's later plane-curve study (arXiv:2611.25767) handles two-form pencil compositions in \(\mathbb P^2\) and asks about higher-dimensional versions; it does not give the three-form \(\mathbb P^3\) graded module formula.

## Limitations
The structural conclusion stops at coefficient degree \((m-2)a\). This endpoint is the last degree where Koszul exactness immediately forces the three intermediary coefficients to vanish; no assertion is made that a new syzygy occurs in the next degree. The generic surface result is over \(\mathbb C\), with \(a\ge2\) and \(m\ge5\), and it is not a classification of all nongeneric triples. The exact example is a computational cross-check, not an independent proof of genericity. A mathematically equivalent calculation under alternate terminology could exist in unindexed literature; independent mathematical validation is not asserted.
