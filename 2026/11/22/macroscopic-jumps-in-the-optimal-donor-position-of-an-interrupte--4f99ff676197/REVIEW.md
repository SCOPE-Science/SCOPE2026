# Scientific review: macroscopic donor-position jumps

## Correctness (C): PASS
The objective follows directly from an explicitly defined finite visitation law. Imported foreign pollen is deposited at precisely the female flowers before the one donor, and exported pollen is the donor-origin load left on departure. The closed form and adjacent differences were reconstructed by finite geometric sums and checked with exact rational enumeration. The sign factor is strictly convex for every real flower index because it is the sum of two positive nonconstant exponentials and a constant. At the entire parameter window of the asymptotic theorem, the factor is negative at the stated logarithmic cutoff; its strictly convex shape then excludes any intermediate maximizer. Uniform upper/lower endpoint bounds establish that the early block strictly beats the last flower at one endpoint, while the last flower strictly beats every early or intermediate position at the other. A continuous maximum of finitely many objective functions supplies the tie. Boundaries at the first and last flowers, all positive weights and all retention rates in the stated open ranges are included. The finite ten-flower witness uses exact rational endpoint signs and a derivative bound. Risks: the conclusions are conditional on the complete-load-replacement and geometric-departure assumptions; no claim of a unique crossover is made.

## Originality (O): PASS, with residual historical-priority risk
The central comparison is between the exact theorem for an interrupted one-donor, weighted-receipt-versus-export finite sequence and earlier empirical/ESS studies of floral sex segregation, not merely between titles. Kudo, Maeda and Narita (2001) model upwards pollination and reported differences in pollen import and export by position, but did not in inspected full text give a one-donor global optimum or the large-separation tie theorem. Jersáková and Johnson (2007) measured how protandry and pollen discounting affect male pollination in orchids, without the present fixed-sequence closed form or asymptotic donor-position jump. Harder, Jordan, Gross and Routley (2004) explicitly explain how initial female versus terminal male flowers can promote outcrossing; their synthesis of display architecture and pollen discounting has no parameterized global one-donor placement theorem. Devaux, Lepers and Porcher (2014) discuss effects of pollen carryover and visitor behavior at the broader plant-mating-system level, but not the exact reduction and tie. Brunet and Charlesworth (1995) analyze ESS allocation among sequential blooms; the available abstract does not state this theorem, but their full article could not be inspected, so stronger implicit coverage remains a residual risk. Harder, Barrett and Cole (2000) is similarly only abstract-inspected. The analysis does not claim that geometric pollen loss, flower-position differences or benefits of protandry are novel. A search that failed to find an equivalent theorem is not itself proof of priority, and future comparison of unavailable primary text could change this assessment.

## Value (V): PASS
The problem isolates a natural biological tradeoff: foreign-pollen reception favors donor placement after several receptive flowers, while male export rewards a short remaining visit and early departure; placing the donor terminally also avoids downstream self pollen loss. The theorem finds a qualitative limitation of local movement heuristics: when continued visits become common, a globally optimal donor position can jump by nearly the entire length of the inflorescence even though the continuation probability varies continuously. The strict-convexity reduction gives a tractable global candidate set, and the exact ten-flower witness demonstrates that the behavior does not require astronomically large systems. This result is useful as a reproducible benchmark for more realistic models of movement, sex allocation and pollen transport, without requiring a biological calibration or an evolutionary-equilibrium interpretation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The nearest substantive published works are Brunet and Charlesworth (1995, sequential floral sex allocation), Kudo, Maeda and Narita (2001, spatially varying pollen transfer), Harder and colleagues (2004, inflorescence pollination), Jersáková and Johnson (2007, pollen discounting and protandry), and Devaux, Lepers and Porcher (2014, visitor-behavior constraints). The complete 1995 and 2000 full texts were not accessible for definitive priority comparison. The theorem covers a one-donor, one-visit-path, fixed-retention model with a weighted reproductive proxy; departures, pollen-load replacement and effect of donor identity are idealized. The asymptotic transition is existential and not asserted unique. Computational checks verify finite instances, not the infinite theorem.
