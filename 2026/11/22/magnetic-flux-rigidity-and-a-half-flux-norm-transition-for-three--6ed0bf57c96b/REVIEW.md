# Scientific review: three-mode quantum-torus flux rigidity

## Correctness

**PASS.** The statement is restricted to common, affinely independent frequencies on a two-dimensional quantum torus. The Weyl multiplication law determines the triangle flux \(q\); independent gauge phases reduce the problem exactly to \(aI+bU+cV\). The squared-norm defect identity gives uniform strictness for three positive weights when \(q\ne1\), and the full joint torus spectrum yields equality when \(q=1\). Orthogonal Fourier generators and Cauchy–Schwarz give necessary and sufficient equality for the nonzero input pair. At \(q=-1\), central squares reduce the universal norm to all two-dimensional Pauli fibers, and a one-variable concave maximization yields the claimed two regimes. The zero-mode and transition boundaries are included by continuity. The standalone numerical checker corroborates \(634556\) deterministic tests; it is not substituted for the infinite-dimensional proof.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The newly introduced convolution has a sharp endpoint inequality but its equality geometry on small genuine noncommutative Fourier packets is not given by the global constant. Three noncollinear characters are the first support size detecting the two-torus cocycle through the area determinant, whereas any two modes always attain the coefficient norm. The flux-dependent equality obstruction is structurally robust for all nonintegral fluxes; the closed half-flux norm identifies a nontrivial squared-weight triangle transition and its boundary and equal-coefficient cases. These facts quantify how noncommutativity destroys sparse endpoint extremizers, without imposing artificial parameter slices.
