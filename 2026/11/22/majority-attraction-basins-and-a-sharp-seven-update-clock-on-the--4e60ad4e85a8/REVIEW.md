# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

A precisely specified 11-vertex, 20-edge graph and open-neighborhood majority rule yield 2048 deterministic transitions. Two independent full-state algorithms were executed: successor path following with a constructed Mycielskian edge set, and indegree elimination with an independent literal edge list and signed neighbor sum. Both reproduced the identical eleven-cell first-entry-time/period histogram, the seven basin-size classes and a sharp seventh entry layer. The first additionally checked all twenty dihedral/complement transformations against the transition rule and classified the 57 recurrent orbits; the second checked the strict identities \(F^9=F^7\) and \(F^8\ne F^6\). Both scripts are self-contained and include their full exhaustive loops and fail-on-disagreement assertions. Limits: only the specified finite graph and update rule are established.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

An actual semantic search of the published mathematical findings index for the graph and aliases, majority basins, exact populations and equivalences found no corresponding claim. The closest indexed finite majority censuses concern different graphs (Petersen, Heawood, Kneser, icosahedral, Chvátal). Full accessible primary text for the 2015 deterministic-majority time-bound analysis supplies at-most-two eventual periods but not the graph-specific 57-orbit or 2048-state populations. No general result inspected numerically implies the new Grötzsch basin table. Residual risk: older or unindexed exhaustive tabulations might duplicate the same finite census; no inference of novelty is based only on a failed search.

## Value — PASS

The Grötzsch graph is the smallest triangle-free four-chromatic graph and the first nontrivial four-chromatic Mycielski example. Complete dynamical classification on it is a natural benchmark for the effect of chromatic obstruction on majority attraction. The exact coexistence of 57 attractors with a uniform-start consensus mass of \(221/256\), including a nontrivial seven-update last-entry layer feeding only unanimity, distinguishes attractor counts from basin weight and provides a reproducible reference for comparisons with larger Mycielski graphs. This is a motivated finite benchmark; no unproved generalization is asserted.

## Closest literature and residual risk

Kaaser–Mallmann-Trenn–Natale (2015, arXiv:1508.03519) provides a common update framework and general two-period limit, not a graph-specific exact census. Established graph descriptions of the Grötzsch–Mycielski construction fix the intended 11-vertex object. Indexed majority classifications on other graphs do not have the same labelled transition map or a stronger all-graph enumeration formula. An obscure preexisting tabulation could reduce priority, and this self-review is not independent external certification.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
