## Originality (O) — PASS, qualified
General Gravner–Lyu–Sivakoff results establish synchronization and activity criteria, not exact least periods for each initialization. A closer previously published all-graph result gives eventual-period divisibility \(p_H\mid p_C\), not equality; indeed its strictness is realized on Petersen and Heawood examples. Published pointwise equality for complete bipartite graphs is not applicable to the irregular Herschel graph. The Herschel graph reference supplies its topology, not a dynamical census. The exact Herschel counts, pointwise equivalence of period basins, 8/10 entrance clock difference, and inclusion of recurrent sets were absent from the inspected relevant full-text material and targeted searches. Claims of global historical priority remain inappropriate; inaccessible or unindexed equivalent computations remain possible. `AUDIT.json` provides actual query strings, implication checks, closest IDs and source inspections.

## Value (V) — PASS
The Herschel graph is a canonical extremal non-Hamiltonian polyhedron, not a freely selected graph instance. Exact matching of the two different automata's eventual period selection for every start, despite large inequalities in their attractor counts and transient clocks, is an interesting structural boundary in the general period-divisibility theory. Its counterpart fails on Petersen and Heawood systems. The complete finite classification, including basin partition and sharp first-entry laws, is substantively stronger than an isolated periodic witness.
