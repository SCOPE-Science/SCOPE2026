# Scientific review of matroidal skew-forcing bases

**Correctness — PASS.** The universal factorization follows from independence of the two white-neighbor processes on bipartite shores. For three of the graphs, finite complete checks on at most nine vertices per shore determine precisely the forbidden triples; their linear-triple geometry proves the general higher-cardinality formula. For the Desargues graph, the triangle-completion operation is exactly graph-component clique completion on the edges of \(K_5\), giving the all-cardinality connected-graph polynomial and spanning-tree minimum. The direct-sum basis interpretation proves geodesic exchange and forbids all adjacent-shore slides. Python 3 and C++17 implementations were executed from the supplied paths, with agreement on all coefficients and exchange metrics. The C++ implementation independently enumerates up to \(2^{20}\) complete initial sets. Checks are finite and exhaustive in the explicitly stated graph domain.

**Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty.** DeAlba (arXiv:1404.1618, sections 4 and 6) develops skew forcing and a conditional bipartite matroid theorem, but its uniquely restricted maximum-matching hypothesis is not satisfied by these regular cubic incidence graphs. Bong et al. (arXiv:2501.03642, sections 2 and 5) establish general skew-forcing reconfiguration results for other source graphs, not the four incidence geometries, matroid-base identifications, square enumerators, or exact mobility metrics. Kutnar–Marušič's 2009 paper selects the four graphs by cubic symmetry and girth, not skew forcing. Relevant indexed claims concerning ordinary zero forcing of these graphs and skew forcing of crown, book, prism, and cactus families have different objects or forcing rules and do not imply this classification. The absence of indexed matches is not proof of absolute priority, and unseen or unavailable detailed sources remain a risk.

**Value — PASS.** This is a natural complete answer for the four exceptional cubic symmetric graphs of girth six, with the full forcing-set polynomials, minimum-set counts and reconfiguration distances. More importantly, the forcing dynamics recover four concrete matroids: the missing fourth affine parallel class in the Pappus configuration and the \(K_5\) spanning-tree matroid on the Desargues configuration give structural explanations rather than unmotivated tables. The sharp jump-versus-slide distinction and the non-self-centered Desargues exchange space are meaningful comparisons in forcing reconfiguration.

The result is restricted to the four named graphs. Independent publication-priority verification and external mathematical audit are still outstanding.
