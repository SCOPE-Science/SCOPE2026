# Self-review

## Correctness (C): PASS
The claim is a classification of every inclusion-maximal commutative subsemigroup of \(B_n(G)\), not just an extremal inequality. The key elementary premise is explicit Brandt multiplication: any two commuting off-diagonal elements must have zero products in both orders, forcing disjoint source and target sets. Saturating the permitted rectangle and choosing inclusion-maximal abelian subgroups on exactly the unused indices is both necessary and sufficient for maximality. This proves the unique parameterization. Its independent weighted count follows from the exact family sizes, and the total comes from a two-missing-color inclusion-exclusion. The maximum follows from the balanced-product inequality with the small-index boundary cases checked separately. The Gaussian theorem follows from an exact uniform colored-word encoding, an exponentially negligible conditioning event, the multinomial CLT and the delta method. All quantified ranges are specified, including the one-index case and nonabelian structure groups.

The standalone direct-multiplication checker gives `VERIFY_OK`, with exact maximal-commuting-subset lists on ten cases including \(S_3\), but these finite tests are corroborative only. The proof does not use extrapolation from enumeration.

## Originality (O): PASS, with residual historical-priority risk
Kumar and Krishna, *The Large Rank of a Finite Semigroup using Prime Subsets*, arXiv:1308.5382 (first public 2013-08-25), discuss maximal *proper* Brandt subsemigroups and large rank, not commutative maximal families. Margolis--Rhodes--Silva, *On the subsemigroup complex of an aperiodic Brandt semigroup*, arXiv:1705.04956, give a more general description of subsemigroups for the trivial structure group and characterize maximal nilpotent families; the present specialization imposes commuting, treats arbitrary fixed \(G\), and proves a weighted spectrum and limit law. Ballester-Bolinches--Pérez-Calabuig, *The Abelian Kernel of an Inverse Semigroup*, Mathematics 8 (2020), concern relational morphism kernels, not these embedded maximal subsemigroups.

The especially close published results *Commutative Brandt divisors coincide with embeddings, with the sharp matrix index* (2026-11-02), and *Commutative divisors of finite completely zero-simple semigroups collapse to Brandt embeddings* (2026-11-08), already provide the broad **abstract** group-plus-annihilator decomposition for commutative divisors. Those general facts are acknowledged, and the claim is **not** to rediscover that decomposition. They do not give a classification of maximal commutative subsets of a *fixed* Brandt host with specified \(n,G\), do not index by actual maximal abelian subgroups, and do not imply the exact size polynomial or typical-size Gaussian law without additional reasoning. The accessible abstract/result text of *Commuting graphs of completely simple semigroups* (2025) identifies maximal commutative pieces in semigroups without the absorbing-zero mechanism; the full publisher article was blocked, so it is an explicit residual comparison risk. Searches for Brandt commuting-clique spectra, maximal commutative subsemigroups, equivalent closed polynomials, and Gaussian laws found no exact covering statement. Search absence is not a proof of absolute historical novelty.

## Value (V): PASS
The objects are the natural inclusion-maximal commutative subsemigroups of a fundamental finite inverse semigroup. The entire size distribution, including nonabelian structure groups, is an intrinsic invariant that the ordinary largest-proper-subsemigroup and abstract-divisor classifications do not determine. The formula reveals precisely how the maximal abelian subgroup census of \(G\) controls the spectrum, while the distinct Gaussian limit shows a typical quadratic size separated from the sharp balanced-rectangle maximum. These are substantive complete structural, finite enumerative, and asymptotic conclusions, rather than a small arbitrary case or computation-only observation.

## Limitations and provenance of checks
The formula is restricted to identity-sandwich Brandt semigroups; it does not classify arbitrary completely \(0\)-simple Rees matrices. The asymptotic requires fixed \(G\), and exact evaluation for a given \(G\) requires its maximal abelian subgroup list. Direct tests cover ten small cases. A plausible but inaccessible full publisher text and unidentified older commuting-clique literature remain historical-priority risks.
