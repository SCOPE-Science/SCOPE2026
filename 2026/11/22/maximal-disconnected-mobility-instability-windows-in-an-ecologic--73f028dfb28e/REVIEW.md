# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The reaction law is reconstructed from the displayed public-goods payoffs. Existence of a strictly positive coexistence root, its Jacobian signs, and the modal determinant's four rational endpoint signs are certified by standalone exact-rational interval arithmetic. The remaining infinite-family proof is analytical: an incidence-matrix Loewner comparison shows at least tenfold spectral separation on the normalized weighted star, while the fixed-ratio two-species modal determinant is a quadratic that has only one possible negative interval per eigenvalue. This proves both the constructive \(m\)-window lower bound and the general \(m\)-window upper bound without finite-spectrum extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority limits

Roy et al.'s equations and Appendix E already provide the one-mode Turing mechanism and note that finite graph spectra can prevent instability. Those are prior results, not new claims. Their paper's inspected methods, appendix and conclusions do not state the sharp count of arbitrarily many disconnected mobility-scale windows on a single fixed weighted graph, or the normalized geometric-star construction. The full relevant methods of Hütt–Armbruster–Lesne (2022) concern pattern diversity and topology perturbations; Yang et al. (2022) treats random-network predator-prey stability. The new statement follows from the published modal law plus a new, explicitly verified ecological parameter and spectral-extremal construction, rather than from a mere change of variable. Indexed neighboring research on graph spectral support and on edge-addition stability reversals was compared by implication; neither available summary claims the same fixed-graph and fixed-ratio extremal result. The inability to inspect all potentially applicable broad spectral theorems leaves a bounded priority risk, not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

The result gives a sharp *maximum possible number* of discrete mobility-scale stability reversals at fixed kinetics and fixed corridor network. It separates the instability of one continuous spatial frequency band from the very different multiple-window phenomenon obtained by sampling that band at a graph's widely spaced spectral modes. The family is valid for any habitat count and has unit total corridor conductance, so the result is a natural structural boundary in weighted habitat-network cooperation dynamics. The value is classification of linear risk, not a claim about realized spatial patterns.

## Important limitations

Only local linear stability is classified; no nonlinear pattern existence or global basin is proved. Endpoints are nonhyperbolic. Very uneven conductances and a wide mobility sweep are required by this construction. Broad historical spectral results could cover the conclusion in more abstract terms and require an independent priority review.
