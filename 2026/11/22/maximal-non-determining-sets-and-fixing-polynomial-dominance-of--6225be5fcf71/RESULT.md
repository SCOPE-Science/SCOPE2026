# Maximal non-determining sets and fixing-polynomial dominance of the Shrikhande–rook pair

## Finding

Let \(S\) be the Shrikhande graph with vertex set \(\mathbb Z_4^2\), where \(x,y\) are adjacent exactly when \(x-y\in\{\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1),\pm(1,1)\}\), and let \(R=K_4\square K_4\) be the four-by-four rook graph. Both are strongly regular with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\). A subset \(B\) of vertices is *determining* (or a *fixing set*) when the only automorphism fixing each vertex of \(B\) individually is the identity. Put \(F_G(z)=\sum_{B\text{ determining}}z^{|B|}\) and \(U_G(z)=(1+z)^{16}-F_G(z)\).

**Theorem.** For \(S\), the inclusion-maximal non-determining sets are exactly the \(28\) cosets of the seven order-four subgroups of \(\mathbb Z_4^2\). For \(R\), they are exactly the twelve two-row/two-column slabs (each of size eight) and the twenty-four permutation transversals (each of size four). Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_S(z)&=1+16z+120z^2+112z^3+28z^4,\\
U_R(z)&=1+16z+120z^2+560z^3+812z^4+672z^5+336z^6+96z^7+12z^8,\\
F_S(z)-F_R(z)&=448z^3+784z^4+672z^5+336z^6+96z^7+12z^8.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, the determining numbers are \(3\) and \(4\), every vertex set of size at least \(5\) determines \(S\), and every vertex set of size at least \(9\) determines \(R\). If every vertex is selected independently with probability \(p\), then \(\Pr(B_p\text{ determines }S)>\Pr(B_p\text{ determines }R)\) for every \(0<p<1\), since the probability difference is \(\sum_{k=3}^8d_kp^k(1-p)^{16-k}>0\) with \((d_3,\ldots,d_8)=(448,784,672,336,96,12)\).

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are simple and vertex-labelled by the displayed coordinates. Determination concerns **pointwise** stabilizers, not ordinary resolving sets, dominating sets, distinguishing two-colourings, or setwise stabilizers. Counts treat vertex subsets as unordered, without quotienting by graph automorphisms. The probability comparison uses independent identically distributed Bernoulli selections with common parameter \(p\); it asserts nothing for heterogeneous selection probabilities. Claims concern precisely the two named 16-vertex graphs, not every graph having their strongly regular parameters.

## Proof

**Fixed-point reduction.** For any finite graph, \(B\) fails to determine it exactly when some nonidentity automorphism fixes every point of \(B\). Thus its family of nonbases is the down-closure of the inclusion-maximal pointwise fixed sets of nonidentity automorphisms.

**Shrikhande automorphisms.** The six neighbors of \((0,0)\) induce a six-cycle. Once an automorphism fixes \((0,0)\) and those six neighbors, it fixes every other vertex, since the nine remaining vertices have nine different neighbor-intersection signatures with that cycle. Consequently \(|\operatorname{Aut}(S)|\le16\cdot12=192\). Independently, all translations of \(\mathbb Z_4^2\) preserve adjacency. Enumerating the \(4^4=256\) two-by-two matrices over \(\mathbb Z_4\) shows exactly twelve linear maps preserve the six connection steps, each necessarily invertible because it permutes a generating connection set. They are, writing row entries \((a,b,c,d)\):

\[
\begin{gathered}
(0,1,1,0),\ (0,1,3,1),\ (0,3,1,3),\ (0,3,3,0),\\
(1,0,0,1),\ (1,0,1,3),\ (1,3,0,3),\ (1,3,1,0),\\
(3,0,0,3),\ (3,0,3,1),\ (3,1,0,1),\ (3,1,3,0).
\end{gathered}
\]

Thus the \(192\) affine maps \(x\mapsto Ax+t\) are **all** automorphisms. For \(A\ne I\), the solutions to \(Ax+t=x\) form either the empty set or one coset of \(\ker(A-I)\). Among the eleven nonidentity linear maps, seven have a four-element kernel, precisely one for each of the seven order-four subgroups; four have a one-element kernel. Every coset of each four-element kernel is realized as the fixed set of some affine map (choose \(t=(I-A)x_0\)). Since every singleton is contained in one of these cosets and distinct order-four cosets cannot contain one another, these 28 cosets are exactly the maximal nonbases. They yield 28 four-subsets and \(28\binom43=112\) three-subsets. A triple cannot belong to two distinct four-element subgroup cosets: its pairwise differences generate the same order-four subgroup; otherwise the two cosets would coincide. Every singleton and pair lies in some coset (for a pair, extend the subgroup generated by its difference to order four). Therefore the displayed \(U_S\) follows, with no nonbases of size five or more.

**Rook automorphisms.** The maximal four-cliques of \(R\) are exactly its four rows and four columns. An automorphism must preserve the two clique families or exchange them and acts as \((i,j)\mapsto(\pi(i),\sigma(j))\) or \((i,j)\mapsto(\pi(j),\sigma(i))\), with \(\pi,\sigma\in S_4\). Thus the full group has \(2(4!)^2=1152\) elements. A nontrivial family-preserving element can fix points in at most two rows, or at most two columns (a nontrivial permutation of four letters fixes at most two). Its fixed set is therefore contained in a two-row or two-column slab. Every slab is itself fixed pointwise by transposing the two unused rows or columns. A family-exchanging element has fixed cells lying in the graph of a permutation \(\tau\), i.e. a permutation transversal, and each transversal is itself fixed pointwise by the coordinate-exchange involution \((i,j)\mapsto(\tau^{-1}(j),\tau(i))\). The twelve slabs and twenty-four transversals are pairwise incomparable under inclusion and hence are all maximal nonbases.

**Counting the rook nonbases.** All sets of size at most two are non-determining. Among three-subsets, the twelve slabs initially contribute \(12\binom83=672\); subtract \(2(8\binom43)=64\) for triples in a single full row or column counted in three slabs, and subtract \(\binom42^2\binom43=144\) for triples forming three cells of a two-by-two rectangle, counted in two slabs. This leaves \(464\) slab-contained triples. The other failing triples are three nonattacking rooks, \(\binom43^2\cdot3!=96\), each extendible to a permutation transversal, giving \(u_3=560\). At size four the twelve slabs contribute \(12\binom84-2\cdot8-\binom42^2=788\), where full rows/columns (eight sets, each in three slabs) and complete two-by-two rectangles (36 sets, each in two slabs) cause the indicated overcounts. Adding the twenty-four transversals yields \(u_4=812\). For sizes \(5\le k\le8\), only slabs are large enough, and a subset of that size cannot lie in two distinct slabs (any two distinct slabs intersect in at most four cells). Thus \(u_k=12\binom8k\), and \(u_k=0\) for \(k>8\). These yield the displayed \(U_R\).

Subtracting from \((1+z)^{16}\) and comparing coefficients proves the polynomial and probability statements. All combinatorial counts concern the complete relevant families, not statistical samples.

## Verification

Two standalone source files accompany this result. `verification/check_affine.py` constructs the full 192-element affine group and 1152-element row/column group from formulas, computes every nonidentity fixed set, verifies all 28 and 36 maximal nonbases, and exhausts all \(2^{16}=65536\) vertex subsets per graph. `verification/check_graph.py` independently constructs each graph from its edge relation, discovers every automorphism using a generic graph-isomorphism search, and exhausts every subset without using the affine or rook group formula. Both programs were executed with Python 3 and passed all asserted counts. The second needs `networkx` version 2.8 or later. Both output exact successful-subset coefficient lists.

For reproducing: run `python3 verification/check_affine.py` and `python3 verification/check_graph.py` from the extracted package directory. The first has no third-party dependencies. The second is a separately implemented structural check; agreement does not constitute independent expert review.

## Relationship to prior work

Boutin (2006) introduced determining-set tools and pointwise-stabilizer methods and classified selected Kneser graph cases; it does not give these two full fixing polynomials. Boutin (2009), Lemma 1, gives a general determining-set criterion for Cartesian products, so the rook half of the nonbase characterization is an instance of a stronger established criterion, **not** a claim to have invented the product test. Its specialization, and the two-graph polynomial comparison, require additional finite structural enumeration. The Shrikhande coset classification uses a different affine automorphism-group analysis and is not implied by the Cartesian-product theorem. Fixing-polynomial terminology was further developed in later research; the novel claim here is the exact pair-specific geometry and strict coefficientwise comparison, not the concept of such a polynomial. An earlier exact centroidal-locating census for the same two graphs reports twelve failing rook eight-sensor sets, also the two-row/two-column slabs, and proves that all rook nine-sensor sets are centroidal locating; the latter prior statement **already implies** that rook nine-sets are determining because pointwise fixing automorphisms preserve distance profiles. These overlapping consequences are not new. Centroidal sensing, identifying codes, and paired domination have different success predicates from pointwise automorphism fixing. The new residual result is the complete pointwise-fixed-set classification, especially the Shrikhande subgroup cosets, and the all-size fixing-polynomial difference.

## Limitations

Historical priority is not certified; mathematical database searches and accessible full texts may miss earlier unindexed fixing-set enumerations. A later paper on fixing parameters had accessible abstract information but its full article was not inspected, leaving a bounded priority risk. The product-graph half has a general published antecedent, and all novelty claims are explicitly restricted to the exact combined classification and coefficientwise inequality. Finite exhaustive verification supplements, but does not replace, the structural proof. No independently conducted scholarly audit has been performed.
