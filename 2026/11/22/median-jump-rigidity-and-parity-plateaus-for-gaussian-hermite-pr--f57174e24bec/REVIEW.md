# Review of the Gaussian-Hermite BV projection claim

**Correctness — PASS.** For every integer degree and every real full-line function with finite ordinary variation, Stieltjes decomposition reduces the norm to one-jump residuals. The exact Hermite coefficient formula and the telescoping polynomial recurrence give a positive integral representation for the derivative of the squared residual on the positive half-line. This establishes unique median-jump maximization, not just a finite-degree observation. Parity yields the precise finite sums; a positive central-binomial series and Stirling's formula yield the sharp asymptotic constant. Exact symbolic and finite numerical checks in `verify.py` corroborate the derivation. No assertion about weighted BV or other dimensions is made.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest published Gaussian-Hermite coefficient result determines the sharp norm of a **single** coefficient and its parity-dependent worst jump. Another determines a sharp **two-consecutive-coefficient** norm. Neither implies the sharp norm of the infinite coefficient tail: their scalar and two-mode estimates do not establish a center extremum for every tail. Jaming et al.'s 2014 and 2015 Hermite-function approximation papers treat almost time-/band-limited input classes and different estimates; Davis–Hagelstein's Gibbs theorem controls pointwise overshoot, not the full Gaussian-weighted residual norm. Prior Legendre/Chebyshev BV projection theorems use different orthogonal systems. Accessible published records and literature searches did not identify the same all-degree theorem, but absence from an index does not rule out an unindexed equivalent. A relevant recent coefficient-bound preprint could not be read in full; its stated subject does not amount to a demonstrated covering theorem.

**Value — PASS.** Polynomial-chaos truncation of bounded-variation Gaussian inputs is a natural discontinuous approximation problem. The result gives an exact worst-case error for every degree, a unique extremizer classification, an integral-square global monotonicity certificate, a plateau effect, and a sharp quarter-power convergence constant; these features are not obtainable by separately maximizing coefficient magnitudes. It is distinct from changing the degree in an existing table, and the center-vs-off-center discrepancy provides useful structural information for Gaussian spectral approximation.

**Remaining limits:** The proof is restricted to one-dimensional standard Gaussian measure and ordinary full-line variation. The historical-priority assessment is provisional, and formal or independent validation is not claimed.
