# Mersenne rigidity of one-point types over clustered diameter-three metric bases

## Finding

Let \(\mathbb U_3\) be the countable universal homogeneous metric structure with positive distances \(\{1,2,3\}\). Fix a finite nonempty parameter metric \(A\) partitioned into nonempty clusters with sizes \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\), where distinct points within a cluster have distance \(1\) and points in different clusters have distance \(2\). All parameters are named individually. Denote by \(N(A)\) the number of complete nonalgebraic one-point types over \(A\), equivalently the number of admissible new-point distance vectors in \(\{1,2,3\}^{|A|}\).

**Exact product and inverse theorem.** For every such profile,
\[
\Theta_A(x,y,z)=\prod_{i=1}^k\bigl((x+y)^{a_i}+(y+z)^{a_i}-y^{a_i}\bigr),\qquad
N(A)=\prod_{i=1}^k(2^{a_i+1}-1),
\]
where \(\Theta_A\) weights a type by the counts of distances \(1,2,3\) to named parameters. For any two finite nonempty cluster profiles \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b\), equality \(N(\mathbf a)=N(\mathbf b)\) holds **if and only if**, after canceling common cluster sizes, one side consists of \(t\) clusters of size \(5\), and the other consists of \(2t\) clusters of size \(1\) and \(t\) clusters of size \(2\), for some integer \(t\geq0\), with either orientation. In the zero case the two profiles are identical.

Consequently, \((|A|,N(A))\) uniquely determines the unordered cluster-size multiset for **all** finite sizes, and so does \((k,N(A))\). The single scalar \(N(A)\) alone is not complete: the smallest possible collision value is \(63\), realized by cluster profiles \((5)\) and \((1,1,2)\), containing five and four named points, respectively. The complete ambiguity is generated by the unique Mersenne identity
\[
2^6-1=(2^2-1)^2(2^3-1)=63.
\]

The inverse classification, rather than the elementary counting factorization, is the claimed new conclusion.

## Assumptions and scope

The ambient distance set is exactly \(\{0,1,2,3\}\). The parameter base has distance \(1\) within each equivalence class of its distance-one graph and distance \(2\) between distinct classes. Such parameter metrics are ultrametric, but the *ambient* generic metric space is not the ultrametric Fraïssé limit. Types are complete over the pointwise-named parameters, with the new element required distinct from all named elements; algebraic equality types are not counted. Profiles are finite multisets of **positive** integers; neither their order nor a prescribed number of clusters is required for the basic classification.

## Proof

**1. Exact admissibility.** A vector \(f:A\to\{1,2,3\}\) is a one-point metric extension precisely when, for every pair \(u,v\in A\),
\[
|f(u)-f(v)|\leq d(u,v)\leq f(u)+f(v).
\]
When \(d(u,v)=2\), all nine assignments are allowed. When \(d(u,v)=1\), the sole forbidden unordered assignment is \(\{1,3\}\). Thus values from \(\{1,3\}\) cannot coexist in a single old cluster; any choices on different clusters are independent. On a cluster of size \(a\), admissible assignments form the union of the \(\{1,2\}\)-valued and \(\{2,3\}\)-valued assignments, with the all-two vector counted twice. Its weight polynomial is exactly \((x+y)^a+(y+z)^a-y^a\). Multiplying over clusters proves the polynomial and scalar formulas. The universal homogeneous extension property guarantees that each and only each admissible vector corresponds to a realized complete nonalgebraic singleton type.

**2. Arithmetic rigidity.** Put \(M_j=2^j-1\) for integers \(j\geq2\). Consider any equality of two finite products of the \(M_j\)'s. Move all terms to one side and write \(e_j\in\mathbb Z\) for the difference of multiplicities, so \(\prod_j M_j^{e_j}=1\), with finite support. The Bang–Zsigmondy primitive-divisor theorem states that for every \(j\geq2\) other than \(j=6\), there is a prime \(p_j\mid M_j\) not dividing any \(M_i\) with \(2\leq i<j\). If the greatest index \(J\) with \(e_J\ne0\) were different from \(6\), taking \(p_J\)-adic valuations would give \(e_J v_{p_J}(M_J)=0\), a contradiction. Therefore the largest differing index is \(6\), if any. In particular, the exponents at indices \(4\) and \(5\) must be zero, by primitive divisors \(5\) and \(31\), and the only remaining indices are \(2,3,6\). Since \(M_2=3\), \(M_3=7\), and \(M_6=3^2\cdot7\), valuation at \(3\) and \(7\) yields
\[
e_2=-2e_6,\qquad e_3=-e_6.
\]
This proves that **every** multiplicative relation is a power of \(M_6=M_2^2M_3\), and that no other relations exist.

**3. Recovering the metric.** A factor \(M_j\) corresponds exactly to a cluster of cardinality \(j-1\). Under the sole primitive relation, the difference in numbers of named points on the two sides is
\[
(6-1)-2(2-1)-(3-1)=1,
\]
and the difference in cluster counts is \(1-2-1=-2\). Therefore equality of \(N\) together with either total parameter cardinality or cluster number forces the relation exponent to vanish. The cluster-size multisets are identical, so the bases are isometric. Without either extra datum, canceling shared factors yields precisely the stated \(t\)-fold substitutions. Every nontrivial collision contains the common value \(63^t\), so the smallest collision has value \(63\); its reduced profiles are \((5)\) and \((1,1,2)\).

## Verification

The included `artifacts/check.py` checks the formulas against all literal metric-distance assignments for every integer-partition profile on up to seven named vertices (44 profiles), comparing the complete trivariate grade polynomial rather than only totals. It also checks pairwise scalar injectivity for all 28,628 profiles on one through thirty vertices and verifies that all profile collisions with value at most one million obey the sole substitution law. It prints `VERIFY_OK`. The finite tests are checks of normalization and small cases, **not** a substitute for the Bang–Zsigmondy theorem or the all-size proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Sauer's study of homogeneous finite-distance metric spaces provides the extension/Katětov setting and the exact admissibility inequalities. Conant's generalized metric Fraïssé framework identifies the unrestricted finite-distance Urysohn structures. An earlier published treatment of one-point metric types, *Widom–Rowlinson partition functions as 1-type polynomials of the generic diameter-3 metric space*, proves a more general graph-metric generating-function identity for arbitrary distance-one graphs. Its formula **already implies the present product factorization** by specializing to a disjoint union of cliques; that counting identity is not claimed as original. The Bang–Zsigmondy primitive-divisor theorem and its exceptional identity are also classical. What is retained here is the exact **inverse** statement: the complete family of multiplicative ambiguities and the all-size rigidity once either natural base-size statistic is supplied.

An earlier investigation of bases having distance \(1\) within clusters but distance \(3\) between clusters gives a different scalar polynomial and actual fixed-size collisions. It does not cover the current distance-two inverse rigidity. The published primary sources and related indexed results checked did not state the inverse classification for these finite metric types. Whether a prior unindexed source applies the classical primitive-divisor theorem to exactly this invariant remains a residual priority risk.

## Limitations

The inverse result is restricted to distance-one clique clusters separated uniformly by distance \(2\) in the unrestricted diameter-three homogeneous metric space. It does not imply injectivity of the scalar type count for arbitrary distance-one graphs, arbitrary \(\{1,2,3\}\)-metrics, or other distance alphabets. It uses the classical Bang–Zsigmondy theorem and does not give a computationally efficient factorization-free inversion algorithm. The proof is mathematical review within the same research package, not independent certification.
