# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The conditional rising-factorial transform is obtained from the exact one-step parent-and-deletion recursion and then coefficient-extracted. It is checked independently through exhaustive parent-and-mask enumeration. The relative deconditioning step follows from an explicitly proved Poisson-binomial local central limit with a logarithmic mean shift and matched linear-order variance; gamma asymptotics, induction on moments, Carleman's test, and tightness then give the distributional limit. The 3,359 exact-rational assertions only check finite identities and do not establish the limit.

**Originality — PASS under inspected evidence.** Zheng studies the canonical Bernoulli growth process and eternal root leadership; Desmarais–Holmgren–Wagner supply the canonical root-cluster Mittag--Leffler marginal; Kürsten supplies canonical moments; Baur addresses different independent-edge regimes. Full relevant material from the first three papers, plus the Baur model/results, was compared. Published root-degree/cluster and singleton-count/cluster findings concern different observables. The canonical moment sequence is known and credited; the target not covered by the inspected statements is the exact-conditioned, all-moment law under uniformly fixed deletions, uniformly along moving positive retention fractions. General ensemble equivalence or unindexed historical work is a residual priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** Fixing an extensive total number of removed edges introduces long-range dependence between edge states and conditions on a binomial event with probability of order \(n^{-1/2}\). Showing preservation of a non-Gaussian root-cluster law under this constraint is useful for random forests sampled with prescribed component counts, not merely for an unconstrained percolation model. The moving \(n^{p_n}\) scale covers arbitrary slow convergence of the retained fraction; the exact finite conditioned moments enable reproducible comparison across deletion budgets.

**Scientific limits.** The claim is for limiting fractions strictly between zero and one and individual root components only. The finite checks are not infinite certification. Possible historical coverage by a general local-conditioning theorem has not been excluded exhaustively.
