For any oriented graph, all maps into a strict three-level target are counted by an explicit weighted independence polynomial after removing the closed neighbourhood of its mixed in/out vertices; the count vanishes when two mixed vertices are adjacent. For any connected bipartite graph, the two source–sink orientations uniquely maximize the number of maps to the three-vertex transitive tournament, and every other orientation has strictly less than half as many, with \(1/2\) asymptotically sharp even for trees.
